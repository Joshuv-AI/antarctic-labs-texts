Generalized fractional calculus and some models of generalized counting processes


Abstract
In the paper we consider models of generalized counting processes time-changed by a general inverse subordinator, we characterize their distributions and present governing equations for them. The equations are given in terms of the generalized fractional derivatives, namely, convolutions-type derivatives with respect to Bernštein functions. Some particular examples are presented. Key words: time-change, Poisson process, generalized counting process, subordinator, inverse subordinator, generalized fractional derivatives 2010 MSC: 60G50; 60G51; 60G55

Introduction

Stochastic processes with random time-change has become a well-established and highly ramified branch of the modern theory of stochastic processes which attracts more and more attention due to various applications in financial, biological, ecological, physical, technical and other fields of research.

Rich class of models is provided by time-changed Poisson processes, of which the most intensively studied are two fractional extensions of the Poisson process, namely, the space-fractional and time-fractional Poisson processes obtained by choosing a stable subordinator or its inverse in the role of time correspondingly. Poisson process itself, being in a sense a core object concerning applicability to count data and simple tractability, however, as a reverse side of its simplicity, is a rather restrictive model. Therefore, it has been quite natural to

2
Preliminaries

Generalized fractional derivatives.

We review the main definitions and some facts on the generalized fractional derivatives, which will be important for our study. (For more details see, e.g.,
[
17
,
21
]
.)

Let
f(x)
be a Bernštein function:

f(x)=a+bx+\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1-e^{-xs}\right)\overline{\nu}(ds),\,\,x>0,\,\,a,b\geq 0,

(1)

\overline{\nu}(ds)
is a non-negative measure on
(0,\infty)
(the Lévy measure for
f(x)
) such that

\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(s\wedge 1\right)\overline{\nu}(ds)<\infty.

The generalized Caputo-Djrbashian (C-D) derivative, or convolution-type derivative, with respect to the Bernštein function
f
is defined on the space of absolutely continuous functions as follows (
[
21
]
, Definition 2.4):

\mathcal{D}^{f}_{t}u(t)=b\frac{d}{dt}u(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u(t-s)\nu(s)ds,

(2)

where
\nu(s)=a+\overline{\nu}(s,\infty)
is the tail of the Lévy measure
\overline{\nu}(s)
of the function
f
.

In the case where
f(x)=x^{\alpha},x>0,\alpha\in(0,1)
, the derivative (
2
) reduces to the fractional C-D
derivative:

\mathcal{D}^{f}_{t}u(t)=\frac{d^{\alpha}}{dt^{\alpha}}u(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{u^{\prime}(s)}{\left(t-s\right)^{\alpha}}ds.

(3)

For the Laplace transform of the derivative (
2
) the following relation holds (
[
21
]
, Lemma 2.5):

\mathcal{L}\left[\mathcal{D}^{f}_{t}u\right](s)=f(s)\mathcal{L}\left[u\right](s)-\frac{f(s)}{s}u(0),s>s_{0},

for
u
such that
|u(t)|\leq\mathsf{M}e^{s_{0}t}
,
M
and
s_{0}
are some constants. Similarly to the C-D fractional derivative, the convolution type derivative can be alternatively defined via its Laplace transform.

The generalization of the classical Riemann-Liouville (R-L) fractional derivative is introduced in
[
21
]
by means of another convolution-type derivative with respect to
f
given by the following formula:

\mathbb{D}_{t}^{f}u(t)=b\frac{d}{dt}u(t)+\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{t}u(t-s)\nu(s)ds.

(4)

The derivatives
\mathcal{D}^{f}_{t}
and
\mathbb{D}_{t}^{f}
are related as follows (see,
[
21
]
, Proposition 2.7):

\mathbb{D}_{t}^{f}u(t)=\mathcal{D}^{f}_{t}u(t)+\nu(t)u(0).

Bernštein functions are associated in a natural way with subordinators.

Let
H(t),t\geq 0,
be a subordinator, that is, nondecreasing Lévy process. Its Laplace transform is of the form:

\mathcal{L}[H(t)](s)=\mathsf{E}e^{-sH(t)}=e^{-tf(s)},

where the function
f
, called the Laplace exponent, is a Bernštein function. Consider a subordinator
H^{f}
with the Laplace exponent
f
given by (
1
), and let
Y^{f}
be its inverse process defined as

Y^{f}(t)=\inf\left\{s\geq 0:H^{f}(s)>t\right\}.

(5)

It was shown in
[
21
]
that the distribution of the inverse process
Y^{f}
has a density

\ell_{f}(t,x)={\rm P}\{Y^{f}(t)\in dx\}/dx

provided that the following condition holds:

Condition I.

\overline{\nu}(0,\infty)=\infty
and the tail
\nu(s)=a+\overline{\nu}(s,\infty)
is absolutely continuous.

The Laplace transform of the density with respect to
t
has the form (
[
21
]
):

\mathcal{L}_{t}\left(\ell_{f}(t,x)\right)(r)=\frac{f(r)}{r}e^{-xf(r)}.

The density
\ell_{f}(t,u)
of the inverse process
Y^{f}
satisfies the following equation (
[
21
]
, Theorem 4.1):

{\mathbb{D}}_{t}^{f}\ell_{f}(t,u)=-\frac{\partial}{\partial u}\ell_{f}(t,u),

(6)

subject to

\ell_{f}(t,{u}/{b})=0,\,\,\ell_{f}(t,0)=\nu(t),\,\,\ell_{f}(0,u)=\delta(u).

(7)

The space Laplace transform of the density
\ell_{f}(t,x)

\tilde{\ell_{f}}(t,\lambda)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda x}\ell_{f}(t,x)dx={\mathsf{E}}e^{-\lambda L^{f}(t)}

(8)

is an eigenfunction of the operator
\mathcal{D}^{f}_{t}
, that is, satisfies the equation

\mathcal{D}^{f}_{t}\tilde{\ell_{f}}(t,\lambda)=-\lambda\tilde{\ell_{f}}(t,\lambda).

(9)

(see,
[
5
,
14
,
17
]
). In the case where
f(x)=x^{\alpha},x>0,\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\tilde{\ell_{f}}(t,\lambda)=\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}(-\lambda t^{\alpha})
, where
\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}(\cdot)
is the Mittag-Leffler function.

Equations (
6
)–(
9
) are important for the study of the processes time changed by inverse subordinators as can be seen in what follows. We first consider the Poisson process with an inverse subordinator as a simplest example which demonstrates the technique to be applied further to more general models.

Poisson process time changed by an inverse subordinator.

Let
N(t)
be the Poisson process with intensity
\lambda,
and
Y^{f}(t)
be the inverse subordinator.

Theorem 1
.

Let Condition I hold. Then the marginal distributions
p_{k}^{f}(t)=P\left\{N(Y^{f}(t))=k\right\}
,
k=0,1,\dots
satisfy the differential equation

\mathcal{D}^{f}_{t}p_{k}^{f}(t)=\lambda\left(p_{k}^{f}(t)-p_{k-1}^{f}(t)\right)

(10)

and

p_{k}^{f}(t)=\frac{\left(-\lambda\partial_{\lambda}\right)^{k}}{k!}\tilde{\ell}_{f}(t,\lambda),

(11)

where
\tilde{\ell}_{f}(t,\lambda)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda u}\ell_{f}(t,u)du
is the Laplace transform of the density of the inverse subordinator
Y^{f}(t).

The probability generating function of the process
N^{f}
has the form

G^{f}(u,t)=\tilde{\ell}(t,\lambda(1-u)),\,|u|<1,

(12)

and satisfies the equation

\mathcal{D}^{f}_{t}G^{f}(u,t)=-\lambda(1-u)G^{f}(u,t)

(13)

with
G^{f}(u,0)=1
.

Proof.

Proof of equation (
10
).
For the probabilities
p_{x}^{f}(t)
we have:

\displaystyle p_{x}^{f}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}\left\{N\left(Y^{f}(t)\right)=x\right\}=\int_{0}^{\infty}p_{x}(u)\ell_{f}(t,u)du

(14)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\lambda u}(\lambda u)^{x}}{x!}\ell_{f}(t,u)du,\quad x=0,1,2,\dots

We take the generalized R-L convolution-type derivative
\mathbb{D}^{f}_{t}
given by (
4
)
and use the equation for the density
\ell^{f}(t,u)
of the inverse subordinator:

{\mathbb{D}}^{f}_{t}\ell_{f}(t,u)=-\frac{\partial}{\partial u}\ell_{f}(t,u),

(15)

with

\ell_{f}(t,0)=\nu(t),\,\,\,\,\ell_{f}(0,u)=\delta(u).

(16)

We obtain:

\displaystyle{\mathbb{D}}^{f}_{t}p_{x}^{f}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}p_{x}(u){\mathbb{D}}^{f}_{t}\ell_{f}(t,u)du=-\int_{0}^{\infty}p_{x}(u)\frac{\partial}{\partial u}\ell_{f}(t,u)du

(17)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\ell_{f}(t,u)\frac{\partial}{\partial u}p_{x}(u)du-p_{x}(u)\ell_{f}(t,u)\big|_{u=0}^{\infty}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\ell_{f}(t,u)(-\lambda[p_{x}(u)-p_{x-1}(u)])du+p_{x}(0)\ell_{f}(t,0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\lambda\left[p^{f}_{x}(t)-p^{f}_{x-1}(t)\right]+p_{x}(0)\nu(t).

Using the relation

\mathbb{D}^{f}_{t}u(t)=\mathcal{D}^{f}_{t}+\nu(t)u(0)

(18)

between convolution derivatives of C-D and R-L types, we have:

\mathcal{D}^{f}_{t}p_{x}^{f}(t)=\mathbb{D}^{f}_{t}p_{x}^{f}(t)-\nu(t)p_{x}^{f}(0),

(19)

note also that

p_{x}^{f}(0)=\int_{0}^{\infty}p_{x}(u)\ell_{f}(0,u)du=\int_{0}^{\infty}p_{x}(u)\delta(u)du=p_{x}(0)=1.

(20)

From (
17
), taking into account (
19
)-(
20
), we finally obtain:

\mathcal{D}^{f}_{t}p_{x}^{f}(t)=-\lambda\left[p_{x}^{f}(t)-p_{x-1}^{f}(t)\right].

Proof of equation (
11
):

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}\left\{N(Y^{f}(t))=k\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathsf{P}(N(s)=k)\mathsf{P}(Y^{f}(t)\in ds)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(-\lambda\partial_{\lambda}\right)^{k}}{k!}e^{-\lambda s}\mathsf{P}\left\{Y^{f}(t)\in ds\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}\left[\frac{\left(-\lambda\partial_{\lambda}\right)^{k}}{k!}\exp\left\{-\lambda Y^{f}(t)\right\}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(-\lambda\partial_{\lambda}\right)^{k}}{k!}\mathsf{E}\exp\left(-\lambda Y^{f}(t)\right)=\frac{\left(-\lambda\partial_{\lambda}\right)^{k}}{k!}\tilde{\ell}_{f}(t,\lambda).

We used above that the distribution of the Poisson process
N
with the rate
\lambda
can be writen as follows:

\displaystyle p_{k}(t)=\frac{\left(-\lambda\partial_{\lambda}\right)^{k}}{k!}e^{-\lambda t}.

For the probability generating function we have

G^{f}(u,t)=\mathsf{E}u^{N(Y^{f}(t))}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-s\lambda(1-u)}\ell_{f}(t,s)ds=\mathsf{E}e^{-\lambda(1-u)Y^{f}(t)}=\tilde{\ell}_{f}(t,\lambda(1-u)),

therefore, we obtain formula (
12
). Now we use the fact, that
\tilde{\ell}_{f}(t,\lambda)
is an eigenfunction of the operator
\mathcal{D}^{f}_{t}
(see (
9
)),
from which we conclude that
G^{f}(u,t)
, being given by
\tilde{\ell}_{f}(t,\lambda(1-u))
, satisfies equation (
13
).
∎

Remark 1
.

Note that (
9
) can be deduced directly from equation (
10
) by taking
k=1
, since
p_{0}^{f}(t)=\mathsf{P}\left\{N\left(Y^{f}(t)\right)=0\right\}=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda u}\ell_{f}(t,u)du=\tilde{\ell}(t,\lambda)
.

3
Models of time-changed Poisson processes

Consider the time-changed Poisson processes
N^{\psi}(t)=N\left(H^{\psi}(t)\right)
,
t>0
, where

N(t)
is the Poisson process with intensity
\lambda
and
H^{\psi}(t)
is the subordinator with Bernštein function
\psi(u)
, independent of
N(t)
. This class of processes was introduced and studied in
[
20
]
and called by the authors Poisson processes with Bernštein intertimes.
It was shown in
[
20
]
that the distributions of
N^{\psi}(t)
,
t>0
, can be presented as follows:

p_{k}^{\psi}(t)=\mathsf{P}\left\{N^{\psi}(t)=k\right\}=\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\frac{d^{k}}{dt^{k}}e^{-t\psi(\lambda u)}\arrowvert_{u=1},

(21)

and satisfy the difference-differential equations:

\frac{d}{dt}p_{k}^{\psi}(t)=-\psi(\lambda)p_{k}^{\psi}(t)+\sum_{m=1}^{k}\frac{\lambda^{m}}{m!}p_{k-m}^{\psi}(t)\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-s\lambda}s^{m}\nu(ds),\,k\geq 0,\,t>0,

which can be also written as

\frac{d}{dt}p_{k}^{\psi}(t)=-\psi(\lambda)p_{k}^{\psi}(t)-\sum_{m=1}^{k}\psi^{(m)}(\lambda)\frac{(-\lambda)^{m}}{m!}p_{k-m}^{\psi}(t),\,k\geq 0,\,t>0,

(22)

with the usual initial conditions:

p_{0}^{\psi}(0)=1
,
p_{k}^{\psi}(0)=0
for
k\geq 1
. The last equation can be represented in the form:

\frac{d}{dt}p_{k}^{\psi}(t)=-\psi(\lambda(I-B))p_{k}^{\psi}(t),\,k\geq 0,\,t>0,

(23)

where
B
is the backshift operator:
Bp_{k}^{f}(t)=p_{k-1}^{f}(t)
, and it is supposed that
p_{-1}(t)=0
.

The probability generating function of the process
N^{\psi}
is of the form (
[
20
]
):

G^{\psi}(u,t)=\mathsf{E}u^{N^{\psi}(t)}=e^{-t\psi(\lambda(1-u))},\,|u|<1.

(24)

We refer for more detail on these processes to
[
20
]
.

Consider the process
N^{\psi}
time-changed by an independent inverse subordinator
Y^{f}
. We can state the following result.

Theorem 2
.

The process
N^{\psi,f}(t)=N\left(H^{\psi}(Y^{f}(t))\right)
,
t>0
, has probability distribution function

p_{k}^{\psi,f}(t)=\mathsf{P}\left\{N^{\psi,f}(t)=k\right\}=\frac{\left(-\lambda\partial_{\lambda}\right)^{k}}{k!}\tilde{\ell}_{f}(t,\psi(\lambda)),

(25)

and the probabilities
p_{k}^{\psi,f}
satisfy the following equation

\mathcal{D}^{f}_{t}p_{k}^{\psi,f}(t)=-\psi\left(\lambda\left(I-B\right)\right)p_{k}^{\psi,f}(t)

(26)

with the usual initial conditions.

The probability generating function of the process
N^{\psi,f}
has the form

G^{\psi,f}(u,t)=\tilde{\ell}(t,\psi(\lambda(1-u))),\,|u|<1,

(27)

and satisfies the equation

\mathcal{D}^{f}_{t}G^{\psi,f}(u,t)=-\psi(\lambda(1-u))G^{\psi,f}(u,t)

(28)

with
G^{\psi,f}(u,0)=1
.

Proof.

To prove equation (
25
) we perform the calculations as follows:

\displaystyle p_{k}^{\psi,f}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}\left\{N\left(H^{\psi}(Y^{f}(t))\right)=k\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathsf{P}\left\{N(s)=k\right\}\mathsf{P}\left\{H^{\psi}(Y^{f}(t))\in ds\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(-\lambda\partial_{\lambda}\right)^{k}}{k!}e^{-\lambda s}\mathsf{P}\left\{H^{\psi}(Y^{f}(t))\in ds\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}\left[\frac{\left(-\lambda\partial_{\lambda}\right)^{k}}{k!}\exp\left\{-\lambda H^{\psi}(Y^{f}(t))\right\}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(-\lambda\partial_{\lambda}\right)^{k}}{k!}\mathsf{E}\exp\left\{-\lambda H^{\psi}(Y^{f}(t))\right\}.

Now it is left to note that

\mathsf{E}\exp\left\{-\lambda H^{\psi}(Y^{f}(t))\right\}=\mathsf{E}\exp\left(-\psi(\lambda)Y^{f}(t)\right)=\tilde{\ell}_{f}(t,\psi(\lambda)).

Let us state equation (
26
). We have

p_{k}^{\psi,f}(t)=\mathsf{P}\left\{N\left(H^{\psi}(Y^{f}(t))\right)=k\right\}=\int_{0}^{\infty}p_{k}^{\psi}(u)\ell_{f}(t,u)du,\quad k=0,1,2,\dots

We repeat the same lines as in the proof of Theorem
1
. We take the generalized R-L derivative
\mathbb{D}_{t}^{f}
and use equations (
22
)–(
23
):

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}_{t}^{f}p_{k}^{\psi,f}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}p_{k}^{\psi}(u)\mathbb{D}_{t}^{f}\ell_{f}(t,u)du=-\int_{0}^{\infty}p_{k}^{\psi}(u)\frac{d}{du}\ell_{f}(t,u)du

(29)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\ell_{f}(t,u)\frac{d}{du}p_{k}^{\psi}(u)du-p_{k}^{\psi}(u)\ell_{f}(t,u)\big|_{0}^{\infty}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\ell_{f}(t,u)\left(-\psi(\lambda(I-B))\right)p_{k}^{\psi}(u)du+p_{k}^{\psi}(0)\ell_{f}(t,0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\ell_{f}(t,u)\Big(-\Big(\psi(\lambda)p_{k}^{\psi}(u)

\displaystyle+\sum_{m=1}^{k}\psi^{(m)}(\lambda)\frac{(-\lambda)^{m}}{m!}p_{k-m}^{\psi}(u)\Big)\Big)du+p_{k}^{\psi}(0)\nu(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\psi(\lambda)p_{k}^{\psi,f}(t)-\sum_{m=1}^{k}\psi^{(m)}(\lambda)\frac{(-\lambda)^{m}}{m!}p_{k-m}^{\psi,f}(t)+p_{k}^{\psi}(0)\nu(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\psi(\lambda(I-B))p_{k}^{\psi,f}(t)+p_{k}^{\psi}(0)\nu(t).

Using the relation between generalized derivatives of C-D and R-L types, we can write

\mathcal{D}^{f}_{t}p_{k}^{\psi,f}(t)=\mathbb{D}_{t}^{f}p_{k}^{\psi,f}(t)-\nu(t)p_{k}^{\psi,f}(0);

and we have (see (
7
)):

p_{k}^{\psi,f}(0)=\int_{0}^{\infty}p_{k}^{\psi}(u)\ell_{f}(0,u)du=\int_{0}^{\infty}p_{k}^{\psi}(u)\delta(u)du=p_{k}^{\psi}(0)=1.

Therefore, we obtain

\mathcal{D}^{f}_{t}p_{k}^{\psi,f}(t)=-\psi(\lambda(I-B))p_{k}^{\psi,f}(t).

Using the expression for
G^{\psi}(u,t)
given by (
24
), we calculate
G^{\psi,f}:

\displaystyle G^{\psi,f}(u,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}u^{N\left(H^{\psi}(Y^{f}(t))\right)}=\int_{0}^{\infty}G^{\psi}(u,s)\ell_{f}(t,s)ds=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-s\psi(\lambda(1-u))}\ell_{f}(t,s)ds=\mathsf{E}e^{-\psi(\lambda(1-u))Y^{f}(t)}=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{\ell}_{f}(t,\psi(\lambda(1-u))),

and since
\tilde{\ell}_{f}(t,\psi(\lambda(1-u)))
is an eigenfunction of
\mathcal{D}^{f}_{t}
with the eigenvalue
\psi(\lambda(1-u))
(see (
9
)), it follows that
G^{\psi,f}(u,t)
satisfies equation (
28
).
∎

Remark 2
.

We notice at once that equations (
26
) for the probabilities of the process
N^{\psi,f}(t)=N(H^{\psi}(Y^{f}(t)))
mimic the corresponding equations for the process
N^{\psi}(t)=N(H^{\psi}(t))
, that is, the process before the time change by an inverse subordinator, only the ordinary derivative in time is changed for the generalized fractional derivative. This can be anticipated quite straightforwardly, in view of the technique used for the proof, and holds also for other models, like those below and in the next section.
On the other side, the equations (
26
) tell us, that for the probabilities of the processes with double time change like
N\left(H^{\psi}(Y^{f}(t))\right)
, the action (in time) of the operator
\mathcal{D}^{f}_{t}
(which is related to
Y^{f}
) is equal to the action (in space) of the operator
-\psi\left(\lambda\left(I-B\right)\right)
(which is related to
H^{\psi}
, and depends also of the outer process
N
).
Some more insight on these operators can be found within the approach applied in the paper
[
1
]
. Let
Y^{f}=Y^{\beta}
be the inverse stable subordinator with a parameter
\beta
, that is,
f(\lambda)=\lambda^{\beta}
, then equation (
26
) becomes

\mathcal{D}^{\beta}_{t}p_{k}^{\psi,\beta}(t)=-\psi\left(\lambda\left(I-B\right)\right)p_{k}^{\psi,\beta}(t),

(30)

where
\mathcal{D}^{\beta}_{t}
is the Caputo-Djrbashian fractional derivative (
3

is the stable subordinator with a parameter

\mathcal{D}^{\beta}_{t}p_{k}^{\alpha,\beta}(t)=-\lambda^{\alpha}\left(I-B\right)^{\alpha}p_{k}^{\alpha,\beta}(t).

(31)

in the Fourier domain. The approach in

Proposition 1

(
[
8
]
)
.

The distribution of the subordinated process
N^{\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\dots,\psi_{n}}(t),

t\geq 0
, is the solution to the Cauchy problem

\frac{d}{dt}p_{k}^{\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\dots,\psi_{n}}(t)=-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\psi_{j}(\lambda(I-B))p_{k}^{\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\dots,\psi_{n}}(t),

with the usual initial conditions

0,\quad k=0.\\
\end{cases}

Consider the process

Theorem 3
.

The process
N^{\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\dots,\psi_{n},f}
has probability distribution function

p_{k}^{\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\dots,\psi_{n},f}(t)=\frac{\left(-\lambda\partial_{\lambda}\right)^{k}}{k!}\tilde{\ell}_{f}(t,\sum_{j=1}^{n}\psi_{j}(\lambda)),

and

\mathcal{D}^{f}_{t}p_{k}^{\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\dots,\psi_{n},f}(t)=-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\psi_{j}\left(\lambda\left(I-B\right)\right)p_{k}^{\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\dots,\psi_{n},f}(t)

with the usual initial conditions.

Proof of Theorem

4
Models of time-changed generalized counting processes

Consider the generalized counting process (GCP)
M(t)
,
t\geq 0
, which was introduced in
[
7
]
. The probabilities
\tilde{p}_{n}(t)=\mathsf{P}\left\{M(t)=n\right\}
depend on
k
parameters
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k}
and are given by the formula

\tilde{p}_{n}(t)=\sum_{\Omega(k,n)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\left(\lambda_{j}t\right)^{x_{j}}}{x_{j}!}e^{-\Lambda t},n\geq 0,

(32)

where
\Omega(k,n)=\left\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\,:\,\sum_{j=1}^{k}jx_{j}=n,x_{j}\in N_{0}\right\}
,
\Lambda=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}
.

GCP performs
k
kinds of jumps of amplitude
1,2,\ldots,k
with rates
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k}
.

The probability generating function of GCP is given by (
[
11
]
):

\tilde{G}(u,t)=\mathsf{E}u^{M(t)}=\exp\Big\{-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}(1-u^{j})t\Big\},\,|u|<1.

(33)

The probabilities (
32
) satisfy

\frac{\tilde{p}_{n}(t)}{dt}=-\Lambda\tilde{p}_{n}(t)+\sum_{j=1}^{\min\{n,k\}}\lambda_{j}\tilde{p}_{n-k}(t),\,n\geq 0,

with the usual initial condition.
The probabilities
\tilde{p}_{n}(t)
can be also written as

\tilde{p}_{n}(t)=\sum_{\Omega(k,n)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\lambda_{j}^{x_{j}}}{x_{j}!}\left(-\partial_{\Lambda}\right)^{z_{k}}e^{-\Lambda t},\,n\geq 0,

(34)

where

z_{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{j}
(see
[
13
]
).

Recently various models of time-changed GCP and fractional GCP were studied, in particular, with time-change given by Lévy subordinators, inverse subordinators, including the cases of some specific subordinators, and also the fractional extensions of processes of the form
M(H^{\psi}(t))
(see, for example,
[
7
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
, and references therein).

Following the lines of the previos section, we consider the time-changed process
\mathsf{M}^{\psi,f}(t)=M(H^{\psi}(Y^{f}(t))
, that is, with double time-change by an independent subordinator
H^{\psi}
and an inverse subordinator
Y^{f}
, which are independent of
M
. To the best of our knowledge, such general case has not been presented in the literature. In the next theorem we characterize its probabilities
\tilde{p}_{n}^{\psi,f}(t)=\mathsf{P}\left\{\mathsf{M}^{\psi,f}(t)=n\right\}
and probability generating function.

Theorem 5
.

The process
\mathsf{M}^{\psi,f}
has probability distribution function

\tilde{p}_{n}^{\psi,f}(t)=\sum_{\Omega(k,n)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\lambda_{j}^{x_{j}}}{x_{j}!}\left(-\partial_{\Lambda}\right)^{z_{k}}\tilde{\ell}_{f}(t,\psi(\Lambda)),\,n\geq 0,

(35)

and
\tilde{p}_{n}^{\psi,f}(t)
satisfy the following equation

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{f}_{t}\tilde{p}_{n}^{\psi,f}(t)=-\psi(\Lambda)\tilde{p}_{n}^{\psi,f}(t)-\sum_{m=1}^{n}\sum_{\Omega(k,m)}\psi^{(z_{k})}\left(\Lambda\right)\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{(-\lambda_{j})^{x_{j}}}{x_{j}!}\tilde{p}_{n-m}^{\psi,f}(t),

(36)

with initial conditions

\tilde{p}_{n}^{\psi,f}(0)=\begin{cases}1,\quad n=0,\\
0,\quad n\geq 1.\\
\end{cases}

The probability generating function of the process
\mathsf{M}^{\psi,f}
is of the form

\tilde{G}^{\psi,f}(u,t)=\tilde{\ell}_{f}\Big(t,\psi\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}(1-u^{j})\Big)\Big),\,|u|<1,

(37)

and satisfies the equation

\mathcal{D}^{f}_{t}\tilde{G}^{\psi,f}(u,t)=-\psi\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}(1-u^{j})\Big)\tilde{G}^{\psi,f}(u,t)

(38)

with
\tilde{G}^{\psi,f}(u,0)=1
.

Proof.

For calculating
\tilde{p}_{n}^{\psi,f}(t)
we use the formula (
34
):

\displaystyle\tilde{p}_{n}^{\psi,f}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\tilde{p}_{n}(s)P\left\{H^{\psi}(Y^{f}(t))\in ds\right\}=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{\Omega(k,n)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\lambda_{j}^{x_{j}}}{x_{j}!}\left(-\partial_{\Lambda}\right)^{z_{k}}e^{-\Lambda t}P\left\{H^{\psi}(Y^{f}(t))\in ds\right\}=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\Omega(k,n)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\lambda_{j}^{x_{j}}}{x_{j}!}\mathsf{E}\left(-\partial_{\Lambda}\right)^{z_{k}}\exp\left\{-\Lambda H^{\psi}(Y^{f}(t))\right\}=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\Omega(k,n)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\lambda_{j}^{x_{j}}}{x_{j}!}\left(-\partial_{\Lambda}\right)^{z_{k}}\mathsf{E}\exp\left\{-\Lambda H^{\psi}(Y^{f}(t))\right\}.

This implies formula (
35
), since

\mathsf{E}\exp\left\{-\Lambda H^{\psi}(Y^{f}(t))\right\}=\mathsf{E}\exp\left\{-\psi(\Lambda)Y^{f}(t)\right\}=\tilde{\ell}_{f}(t,\psi(\Lambda)).

To derive equation (
36
) we follow the same lines as is the proof of Theorem
2
, but we use now instead of the equation (
22
) (or (
23
)) for probabilites of the process
N^{\psi}=N(H^{\psi}),
the following equation for the probabilities
\tilde{p}_{n}^{\psi}(t)
of the process
M^{\psi}=M(H^{\psi})
(see
[
13
]
):

\frac{d}{dt}\tilde{p}_{n}^{\psi}(t)=-\psi(\Lambda)\tilde{p}_{n}^{\psi}(t)-\sum_{m=1}^{n}\sum_{\Omega(k,m)}\psi^{z_{k}}(\Lambda)\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{(-\lambda_{j})^{x_{j}}}{x_{j}!}\tilde{p}_{n-m}^{\psi}(t).

Thus, we obtain

{\mathbb{D}}_{t}^{f}\tilde{p}_{n}^{\psi,f}(t)=\psi(\Lambda)\tilde{p}_{n}^{\psi,f}(t)-\sum_{m=1}^{n}\sum_{\Omega(k,m)}\psi^{z_{k}}(\Lambda)\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{(-\lambda_{j})^{x_{j}}}{x_{j}!}\tilde{p}_{n-m}^{\psi,f}(t)+\tilde{p}_{n}^{\psi,f}(0)\nu(t),

and then apply the same reasonings as those after formula (
29
) to come to (
36
).

Using the expression for the probability generating function
\tilde{G}^{\psi}(u,t)
of the time-changed process
M^{\psi}(t)=M(H^{\psi}(t))
(see
[
11
]
):

\tilde{G}^{\psi}(u,t)=\exp\Big\{-t\psi\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}(1-u^{j})\Big)\Big\},

we calculate
\tilde{G}^{\psi,f}
as follows:

\displaystyle\tilde{G}^{\psi,f}(u,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\tilde{G}^{\psi}(u,s)\ell_{f}(t,s)ds=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\Big\{-t\psi\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}(1-u^{j})\Big)\Big\}\ell_{f}(t,s)ds=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}e^{-\psi\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}(1-u^{j})\right)Y^{f}(t)}=\tilde{\ell}_{f}\Big(t,\psi\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}(1-u^{j})\Big)\Big),

that is, (
37
) holds. Equation (
38
) follows in view of (
37
) and (
9
).

Example 2.
As a continuation of Example 1, consider GCP with time-change given by a compound Poisson-Gamma subordinator:
M^{GN}(t)=M(G_{N}(t))
. Probabilities of this process can be calculated and expressed in terms of special functions:

\displaystyle\tilde{p}_{n}^{GN}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}\left\{M(G_{N}(t))=n\right\}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathsf{P}(M(s)=n)\mathsf{P}(G_{N}(t)\in ds)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{\Omega(k,n)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\left(\lambda_{j}s\right)^{x_{j}}}{x_{j}!}e^{-\Lambda s}

\displaystyle\times\Big\{e^{-\lambda t}\delta_{\{0\}}(ds)+e^{-\lambda t-\beta s}\frac{1}{s}\Phi\left(\alpha,0,\lambda t(\beta s)^{\alpha}\right)\Big\}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-\lambda t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{\Omega(k,n)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\left(\lambda_{j}s\right)^{x_{j}}}{x_{j}!}e^{-\Lambda s}e^{-\beta s}\frac{1}{s}\Phi\left(\alpha,0,\lambda t(\beta s)^{\alpha}\right)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-\lambda t}\sum_{\Omega(k,n)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\left(\lambda_{j}s\right)^{x_{j}}}{x_{j}!}e^{-(\Lambda+\beta)s}\frac{1}{s}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\lambda t\beta^{\alpha})^{l}}{l!\Gamma(\alpha l)}s^{\alpha l-1}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-\lambda t}\sum_{\Omega(k,n)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\lambda_{j}^{x_{j}}}{x_{j}!}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\lambda t\beta^{\alpha})^{l}}{l!\Gamma(\alpha l)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(\Lambda+\beta)s}s^{\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{j}+\alpha l-1}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-\lambda t}\sum_{\Omega(k,n)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\lambda_{j}^{x_{j}}}{x_{j}!}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\lambda t\beta^{\alpha})^{l}}{l!\Gamma(\alpha l)}\frac{\Gamma\left(z_{k}+\alpha l\right)}{\left(\Lambda+\beta\right)^{z_{k}+\alpha l}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-\lambda t}\sum_{\Omega(k,n)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\lambda_{j}^{x_{j}}}{x_{j}!}\frac{1}{\left(\Lambda+\beta\right)^{z_{k}}}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\lambda t\beta^{\alpha})^{l}}{l!\left(\Lambda+\beta\right)^{\alpha l}}\frac{\Gamma\left(z_{k}+\alpha l\right)}{\Gamma(\alpha l)},

which can be written in the form

\tilde{p}_{n}^{GN}(t)=e^{-\lambda t}\sum_{\Omega(k,n)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\lambda_{j}^{x_{j}}}{x_{j}!}\frac{1}{\left(\Lambda+\beta\right)^{z_{k}}}\,{{}_{1}}\Psi_{1}\left((z_{k},\alpha),(0,\alpha),\frac{\lambda t\beta^{\alpha}}{\left(\Lambda+\beta\right)^{\alpha}}\right),

where
{{}_{p}}\Psi_{q}
is the generalized Wright function

{}_{p}\Psi_{q}((a_{i},\alpha_{i}),(b_{j},\beta_{j}),z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{p}\Gamma(a_{i}+\alpha_{i}k)}{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{q}\Gamma(b_{j}+\beta_{j}k)}\frac{z^{k}}{k!}

defined for
z\in C
,
a_{i},b_{i}\in C
,
\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}\in R
,
\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}\neq 0
and
\sum\alpha_{i}-\sum\beta_{i}>-1
(see, e.g.,
[
9
]
).

If
\alpha=1
, that is,
G_{N}(t)=E_{N}(t)
, then probabilities become of the form

\tilde{p}_{n}^{E}(t)=e^{-\lambda t}\sum_{\Omega(k,n)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\lambda_{j}^{x_{j}}}{x_{j}!}\frac{1}{\left(\Lambda+\beta\right)^{z_{k}}}\mathcal{E}_{1,2}^{z_{k}+1}\left(\frac{\lambda t\beta}{\Lambda+\beta}\right),

\mathcal{E}_{\rho,\delta}^{\gamma}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(\gamma+k)}{\Gamma(\gamma)}\frac{z^{k}}{k!\Gamma(\rho k+\delta)},\text{
}z\in C,\text{ }\rho,\delta,\gamma\in C,

[

]

Approximation of Calogero-Moser lattices by Benjamin-Ono equations
J. Douglas Wright

Abstract
. We provide a rigorous validation that the infinite Calogero-Moser lattice can be well-approximated by solutions of the Benjamin-Ono equation in a long-wave limit.

2.
Rigorous residual estimates

The first task is to make the formal estimate of the residual
R_{\epsilon}
from
[
6
]
rigorous. Here is the result:

Proposition 2
.

If
u(X,\tau)
is a solution of (
2
)
with
\sup_{|\tau|\leq\tau_{0}}\|u(\cdot,\tau)\|_{H^{6}}<\infty
then there exists
C>0
and
\epsilon_{0}>0
for which
\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0}]
implies

\sup_{|t|\leq\tau_{0}/\epsilon^{\alpha}}\|R_{\epsilon}(\cdot,t)\|_{\ell^{2}}\leq C\epsilon^{\beta_{\alpha}}

where

\beta_{\alpha}:=\begin{cases}3\alpha-5/2,&\alpha\in(1,2]\\
\alpha+3/2,&\alpha\in(2,3).\end{cases}

Proof.

The proof is technical and we break it up into several parts: an analysis of the acceleration
term, another for the force terms and then a final section where we put everything together.

Part 1: the acceleration term.

By the chain rule

\ddot{\widetilde{x}}_{j}(t)=a_{\epsilon}(\epsilon(j-c_{\alpha}t),\epsilon^{\alpha}t)

where

a_{\epsilon}(X,\tau):=-\epsilon^{\alpha}c_{\alpha}^{2}\partial_{X}u(X,\tau)+\epsilon^{2\alpha-1}\kappa_{1}\partial_{\tau}u(X,\tau)+\epsilon^{3\alpha-2}\partial_{\tau}^{2}v(X,\tau).

Using (
2
) we replace
\partial_{\tau}u
to get:

a_{\epsilon}=-\epsilon^{\alpha}c_{\alpha}^{2}\partial_{X}u-\epsilon^{2\alpha-1}\kappa_{2}u\partial_{X}u-\epsilon^{2\alpha-1}\kappa_{3}H|D|^{\alpha}u+\epsilon^{3\alpha-2}\partial_{\tau}^{2}v.

Differentiating (
2
) with respect to
\tau
and the relation
u=-\partial_{X}v

imply
4
4

4

For concreteness, we note that we compute
v
from

via
\displaystyle v(X,\tau)=-\int_{0}^{X}u(b,\tau)db
.

\partial_{\tau}^{2}v=-{\kappa_{2}\over\kappa_{1}}u\partial_{\tau}u+{\kappa_{3}\over\kappa_{1}}|D|^{\alpha-1}\partial_{\tau}u.

The Sobolev inequality and counting derivatives give

\|\partial_{\tau}^{2}v\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{1}}\|\partial_{\tau}u\|_{H^{1}}+C\|\partial_{\tau}u\|_{H^{\alpha}}.

Taking the
H^{s}
norm of both sides of (
2
) tells us that
\|\partial_{\tau}u\|_{H^{s}}\leq C\left(\|u\|^{2}_{H^{s+1}}+\|u\|_{H^{s+\alpha}}\right)
. In turn this gives

\|\partial_{\tau}^{2}v\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{1}}\left(\|u\|^{2}_{H^{2}}+\|u\|_{H^{1+\alpha}}\right)+C\left(\|u\|^{2}_{H^{\alpha+1}}+\|u\|_{H^{2\alpha}}\right).

Since
\alpha<3
and we have assumed a uniform bound on
u\in H^{6}
for
|\tau|\leq\tau_{0}
we can conclude

(5)

\sup_{|\tau|\leq\tau_{0}}\|a_{\epsilon}+\epsilon^{\alpha}c_{\alpha}^{2}\partial_{X}u+\epsilon^{2\alpha-1}\kappa_{2}u\partial_{X}u+\epsilon^{2\alpha-1}\kappa_{3}H|D|^{\alpha}u\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\epsilon^{3\alpha-2}.

Part 2: the force term.

The authors of
[
6
]
show that

\widetilde{x}_{j+m}-\widetilde{x}_{j}=m-m\epsilon^{\alpha-1}A_{\epsilon m}u(X,\tau)\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{x}_{j}-\widetilde{x}_{j-m}=m-m\epsilon^{\alpha-1}A_{-\epsilon m}u(X,\tau)

where

A_{h}u(X,\tau):={1\over h}\int_{0}^{h}u(X+z,\tau)dz.

If we let

V_{m}(g):={1\over(m+g)^{\alpha}}-{1\over m^{\alpha}}+{\alpha\over m^{\alpha+1}}g

so that

V_{m}^{\prime}(g)=-{\alpha\over(m+g)^{\alpha+1}}+{\alpha\over m^{\alpha+1}},

then the force terms in
R_{\epsilon}
can be rewritten as

\begin{split}\alpha\sum_{m\geq 1}\left[{1\over(\widetilde{x}_{j+m}-\widetilde{x}_{j})^{\alpha+1}}-{1\over(\widetilde{x}_{j}+\widetilde{x}_{j-m})^{\alpha+1}}\right]=F_{\epsilon}(\epsilon(j-c_{\alpha}t),\epsilon^{\alpha}t)\end{split}

where

F_{\epsilon}(X,\tau):=-\sum_{m\geq 1}\left[V^{\prime}_{m}(-m\epsilon^{\alpha-1}A_{\epsilon m}u(X,\tau))-V^{\prime}_{m}(-m\epsilon^{\alpha-1}A_{-\epsilon m}u(X,\tau))\right].

A combination of the the fundamental theorem of calculus and Taylor’s theorem implies

V_{m}^{\prime}(g_{+})-V^{\prime}_{m}(g_{-})=V_{m}^{\prime\prime}(0)(g_{+}-g_{-})+{1\over 2}V_{m}^{\prime\prime\prime}(0)(g_{+}^{2}-g_{-}^{2})+\int_{g_{-}}^{g_{+}}E_{m}(\sigma)d\sigma

where

E_{m}(\sigma):=\int_{0}^{\sigma}V_{m}^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}(\phi)(\sigma-\phi)d\phi.

This leads to the expansion

(6)

\begin{split}F_{\epsilon}={\alpha(\alpha+1)}L_{\epsilon}+{\alpha(\alpha+1)(\alpha+2)\over 2}N_{\epsilon}+M_{\epsilon}\end{split}

where

\begin{split}L_{\epsilon}&:=\epsilon^{\alpha-1}\sum_{m\geq 1}{1\over m^{\alpha+1}}\left(A_{\epsilon m}-A_{-\epsilon m}\right)u\\
N_{\epsilon}&:=\epsilon^{2\alpha-2}\sum_{m\geq 1}{1\over m^{\alpha+1}}\left((A_{\epsilon m}u)^{2}-(A_{-\epsilon m}u)^{2}\right)\\
M_{\epsilon}&:=-\sum_{m\geq 1}\int_{-m\epsilon^{\alpha-1}A_{-\epsilon m}u}^{-m\epsilon^{\alpha-1}A_{\epsilon m}u}E_{m}(\sigma)d\sigma.\end{split}

The terms
L_{\epsilon}
and

coincide with their forms in
[

]
.
The remaining term,

, is lumped into a generic
o(\epsilon^{2\alpha+1})
term there.

Part 2a: Estimates for
L_{\epsilon}
.

We follow the blueprint provided by
[
6
]
.
Using the fact that
\widehat{A_{h}u}(k)=\widehat{u}(k){(e^{ikh}-1)}/ikh
, they show that

\begin{split}\widehat{L}_{\epsilon}(k)&=\epsilon^{\alpha}\zeta_{\alpha}ik\widehat{u}(k)+\epsilon^{2\alpha-1}ik|k|^{\alpha-1}\widehat{u}(k)\left(|k|\epsilon\sum_{m\geq 1}{\sinc^{2}(|k|\epsilon m/2)-1\over(|k|\epsilon m)^{\alpha}}\right).\end{split}

Let

\eta_{\alpha}(h):=h\sum_{m\geq 1}{1-\sinc^{2}(hm/2)\over(hm)^{\alpha}}.

A key observation from
[
6
]
is that
\eta_{\alpha}(h)
is the approximation of

\eta_{\alpha}:=\int_{0}^{\infty}{1-\sinc^{2}(s/2)\over s^{\alpha}}ds

using the rectangular rule with right hand endpoints. As such

\lim_{h\to 0}\eta_{\alpha}(h)=\eta_{\alpha}.

Note that
\eta_{\alpha}
is finite so long as
\alpha\in(1,3)
.

Then we have

(7)

\widehat{L}_{\epsilon}(k)=\epsilon^{\alpha}\zeta_{\alpha}ik\widehat{u}(k)-\epsilon^{2\alpha-1}\eta_{\alpha}ik|k|^{\alpha-1}\widehat{u}(k)+\epsilon^{2\alpha-1}ik|k|^{\alpha-1}\widehat{u}(k)\left(\eta_{\alpha}-\eta_{\alpha}(\epsilon|k|)\right).

What is the error made by approximating
\eta_{\alpha}(\epsilon|k|)
by
\eta_{\alpha}
?
To determine this we
need:

Lemma 3
.

For
\alpha\in(1,2]
there exists

C>0
for which

|\eta_{\alpha}(h)-\eta_{\alpha}|\leq Ch

for all
h>0
. If
\alpha\in(2,3)
there exists
C>0
for which

|\eta_{\alpha}(h)-\eta_{\alpha}|\leq Ch^{3-\alpha}
for all
h>0
.

Proof.

If the integral were not improper, this would be an elementary estimate. But it is.
In fact when
\alpha\in(2,3)
it is improper at
s=0

and that is why the estimate is worse in that setting. Also, when
\alpha\in(1,2)
the derivative of
the integrand

f_{\alpha}(s):={1-\sinc^{2}(s/2)\over s^{\alpha}}

diverges as
s\to 0^{+}
, which complicates things.

First we deal with
h\geq 1
. We have

|\eta_{\alpha}(h)-\eta_{\alpha}|\leq\eta_{\alpha}+h\sum_{m\geq 1}{1-\sinc^{2}(hm/2)\over(hm)^{\alpha}}.

Since
\sinc^{2}(s)\in[0,1]
for all
s\in{\bf{R}}
we make an easy estimate

|\eta_{\alpha}(h)-\eta_{\alpha}|\leq\eta_{\alpha}+h\sum_{m\geq 1}{1\over(hm)^{\alpha}}=\eta_{\alpha}+h^{1-\alpha}\zeta_{\alpha}=\left(\eta_{\alpha}+h^{-\alpha}\zeta_{\alpha}\right)h\leq(\eta_{\alpha}+\zeta_{\alpha})h\leq Ch.

So
h\geq 1
is taken care of for all
\alpha\in(1,3)
.

Now fix
h\in(0,1)
.
We break things up:

\begin{split}\eta_{\alpha}(h)-\eta_{\alpha}=&\begin{array}[t]{@{}c@{}}\underbrace{h\sum_{m=1}^{\lceil 1/h\rceil}f_{\alpha}(mh)-\int_{0}^{h\lceil 1/h\rceil}f_{\alpha}(s)ds}\\
IN\end{array}+\begin{array}[t]{@{}c@{}}\underbrace{h\sum_{m\geq\lceil 1/h\rceil+1}f_{\alpha}(mh)-\int^{\infty}_{h\lceil 1/h\rceil}f_{\alpha}(s)ds}\\
OUT\end{array}.\end{split}

For
OUT
, by standard integral identities and the integral version of the mean value theorem
we have

OUT=\sum_{m\geq\lceil 1/h\rceil+1}\left(hf_{\alpha}(mh)-\int_{(m-1)h}^{mh}f_{\alpha}(s)ds\right)=h\sum_{m\geq\lceil 1/h\rceil+1}\left(f_{\alpha}(mh)-f_{\alpha}(s_{m})\right).

Here
s_{m}\in[(m-1)h,mh]
. Then we use the derivative version of the mean value theorem to get

OUT=h\sum_{m\geq\lceil 1/h\rceil+1}f^{\prime}_{\alpha}(\sigma_{m})(mh-s_{m})

where
\sigma_{m}\in[s_{m},mh]
. Note that
|mh-s_{m}|\leq h
.

Routine calculations show that there is a constant
C>0
such that

|f^{\prime}_{\alpha}(s)|\leq Cs^{-\alpha-1}
for
s\geq 1
. Since
s^{-\alpha-1}
is a decreasing function these considerations lead to

|OUT|\leq Ch^{2}\sum_{m\geq\lceil 1/h\rceil+1}[(m-1)h]^{-\alpha-1}=Ch^{2}\sum_{m\geq\lceil 1/h\rceil}[mh]^{-\alpha-1}.

Next,

\displaystyle h\sum_{m\geq\lceil 1/h\rceil}[mh]^{-\alpha-1}

is the approximation of
\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{h\lceil 1/h\rceil-h}s^{-\alpha-1}ds
using the rectangular rule with right hand endpoints. Since
s^{-\alpha-1}
is decreasing we know that
\displaystyle h\sum_{m\geq\lceil 1/h\rceil}[mh]^{-\alpha-1}\leq\int^{\infty}_{h\lceil 1/h\rceil-h}s^{-\alpha-1}ds
.
Also since
h\in(0,1)
we have
h\lceil 1/h\rceil-h\geq 1/2
and so

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{h\lceil 1/h\rceil-h}s^{-\alpha-1}\leq\int^{\infty}_{1/2}s^{-\alpha-1}=2^{\alpha}/\alpha
. Putting these together imply
|OUT|\leq Ch
.

For
IN
we need to desingularize the integrand at
s=0
. Putting

f_{\alpha}(s)=\begin{array}[t]{@{}c@{}}\underbrace{{1-(s^{2}/12)-\sinc^{2}(s/2)\over s^{\alpha}}}\\
g_{\alpha}(s)\end{array}+{1\over 12}s^{2-\alpha}

gives

IN=\left(h\sum_{m=1}^{\lceil 1/h\rceil}g_{\alpha}(mh)-\int_{0}^{h\lceil 1/h\rceil}g_{\alpha}(s)ds\right)+{1\over 12}\left(h\sum_{m=1}^{\lceil 1/h\rceil}(mh)^{2-\alpha}-\int_{0}^{h\lceil 1/h\rceil}s^{2-\alpha}ds\right).

Taylor’s theorem tells us that

|1-(s^{2}/12)-\sinc^{2}(s/2)|/s^{4}
is bounded as
s\to 0
and as a byproduct we see that
g_{\alpha}(s)
is
C^{1}
on
the interval
[0,2]
.
Routine error estimates for approximating integrals with rectangles tells us

\left|h\sum_{m=1}^{\lceil 1/h\rceil}g_{\alpha}(mh)-\int_{0}^{h\lceil 1/h\rceil}g_{\alpha}(s)ds\right|\leq Ch.

So what remains is to estimate the singular piece

SING_{\alpha}=\left|h\sum_{m=1}^{\lceil 1/h\rceil}(mh)^{2-\alpha}-\int_{0}^{h\lceil 1/h\rceil}s^{2-\alpha}ds\right|.

Note that if
\alpha=2
then
SING_{\alpha}=0
, so that case is pretty easy. But the cases
\alpha\in(1,2)
and
\alpha\in(2,3)

require some care.

We know that

\displaystyle h\sum_{m=1}^{\lceil 1/h\rceil}{(mh)^{2-\alpha}}
is the rectangular approximation of
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{h\lceil 1/h\rceil}{s^{2-\alpha}}ds
using right hand endpoints but it is also the rectangular approximation of
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{h\lceil 1/h\rceil}{(s+h)^{2-\alpha}}ds
using left hand endpoints.
If
\alpha\in(2,3)
then
s^{2-\alpha}
is a decreasing function and we get the following chain of inequalities:

\int_{0}^{h\lceil 1/h\rceil}{(s+h)^{2-\alpha}}ds\leq h\sum_{m=1}^{\lceil 1/h\rceil}(mh)^{2-\alpha}\leq\int_{0}^{h\lceil 1/h\rceil}{s^{2-\alpha}}ds.

On the other hand if
\alpha\in(1,2)
then
s^{2-\alpha}
is increasing and we have

\int_{0}^{h\lceil 1/h\rceil}{(s+h)^{2-\alpha}}ds\geq h\sum_{m=1}^{\lceil 1/h\rceil}(mh)^{2-\alpha}\geq\int_{0}^{h\lceil 1/h\rceil}{s^{2-\alpha}}ds.

Either of the chains tells us:

\begin{split}SING_{\alpha}&\leq\left|\int_{0}^{h\lceil 1/h\rceil}\left(s^{2-\alpha}-{(s+h)^{2-\alpha}}\right)ds\right|\\
&={1\over 3-\alpha}\left|(h\lceil 1/h\rceil)^{3-\alpha}-(h\lceil 1/h\rceil+h)^{3-\alpha}+h^{3-\alpha}\right|\\
&\leq{1\over 3-\alpha}\left|(h\lceil 1/h\rceil)^{3-\alpha}-(h\lceil 1/h\rceil+h)^{3-\alpha}\right|+{1\over 3-\alpha}h^{3-\alpha}.\end{split}

The mean value theorem gives

{1\over 3-\alpha}\left|(h\lceil 1/h\rceil)^{3-\alpha}-(h\lceil 1/h\rceil+h)^{3-\alpha}\right|={hh_{*}^{2-\alpha}}

where
h_{*}
is in between
h\lceil 1/h\rceil
and
h\lceil 1/h\rceil+h
. These numbers are in the interval
[1,3]
and so we have

{1\over 3-\alpha}\left|(h\lceil 1/h\rceil)^{3-\alpha}-(h\lceil 1/h\rceil+h)^{3-\alpha}\right|\leq Ch.

So
|SING_{\alpha}|\leq Ch+Ch^{3-\alpha}
.

Everything all together tells us that
h\in(0,1)
and
\alpha\in(1,3)
imply

|\eta_{\alpha}(h)-\eta_{\alpha}|\leq Ch+Ch^{3-\alpha}.

If
\alpha\in(1,2]
then
h\leq h^{3-\alpha}
and the inequality flips for
\alpha\in(2,3)
. That finishes the proof.

Part 2b: Estimates for

.
Some easy algebra leads to

\begin{split}N_{\epsilon}=&2\epsilon^{2\alpha-2}u\sum_{m\geq 1}{1\over m^{\alpha+1}}(A_{\epsilon m}u-A_{-\epsilon m}u)\\
+&\epsilon^{2\alpha-2}\sum_{m\geq 1}{1\over m^{\alpha+1}}(A_{\epsilon m}u+A_{-\epsilon m}u-2u)(A_{\epsilon m}u-A_{-\epsilon m}u).\end{split}

We recognize that
L_{\epsilon}
is lurking in the first term and get

\begin{split}N_{\epsilon}=&2\epsilon^{\alpha-1}uL_{\epsilon}+\epsilon^{2\alpha-2}\sum_{m\geq 1}{1\over m^{\alpha+1}}(A_{\epsilon m}u+A_{-\epsilon m}u-2u)(A_{\epsilon m}u-A_{-\epsilon m}u).\end{split}

Then we subtract:

\begin{split}N_{\epsilon}-2\epsilon^{2\alpha-1}\zeta_{\alpha}u\partial_{X}u=&2\epsilon^{\alpha-1}u\left(L_{\epsilon}-\epsilon^{\alpha}\zeta_{\alpha}\partial_{X}u\right)\\
+&\epsilon^{2\alpha-2}\sum_{m\geq 1}{1\over m^{\alpha+1}}(A_{\epsilon m}u+A_{-\epsilon m}u-2u)(A_{\epsilon m}u-A_{-\epsilon m}u).\end{split}

We take the
H^{1}
norm and use triangle and Sobolev:

(9)

\begin{split}&\|N_{\epsilon}-2\epsilon^{2\alpha-1}\zeta_{\alpha}u\partial_{X}u\|_{H^{1}}\\
\leq&2\epsilon^{\alpha-1}\|u\|_{H^{1}}\|L_{\epsilon}-\epsilon^{\alpha}\zeta_{\alpha}\partial_{X}u\|_{H^{1}}\\
+&\epsilon^{2\alpha-2}\sum_{m\geq 1}{1\over m^{\alpha+1}}\|A_{\epsilon m}u+A_{-\epsilon m}u-2u\|_{H^{1}}\|A_{\epsilon m}u-A_{-\epsilon m}u\|_{H^{1}}.\end{split}

The estimate (
8
) tells us that
2\epsilon^{\alpha-1}\|u\|_{H^{1}}\|L_{\epsilon}-\epsilon^{\alpha}\zeta_{\alpha}\partial_{X}u\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\epsilon^{3\alpha-2}\|u\|^{2}_{1+\alpha+r_{\alpha}}
.

To control the remaining term in (
9
) we will use the following estimates (see
[
3
]
for the proof):

Lemma 4
.

There is
C>0
such that for all
h>0
and
s\in{\bf{R}}
:

\begin{split}\|A_{h}u\|_{H^{s}}&\leq C\|u\|_{H^{s}}\\
\|A_{h}u+A_{-h}u-2u\|_{H^{s}}&\leq Ch^{2}\|u\|_{H^{s+2}}\\
\|A_{h}u-A_{-h}u\|_{H^{s}}&\leq Ch\|u\|_{H^{s+1}}\\
\|A_{h}u-u\|_{H^{s}}&\leq Ch\|u\|_{H^{s+1}}.\end{split}

We have to deal with terms like
A_{\epsilon m}
and so the above result will be helpful
when
\epsilon m
is “small” but not very useful otherwise. So we break things up:

\begin{split}&\epsilon^{2\alpha-2}\sum_{m\geq 1}{1\over m^{\alpha+1}}\|A_{\epsilon m}u+A_{-\epsilon m}u-2u\|_{H^{1}}\|A_{\epsilon m}u-A_{-\epsilon m}u\|_{H^{1}}\\
=&\epsilon^{2\alpha-2}\sum_{m=1}^{\lfloor 1/\epsilon\rfloor}{1\over m^{\alpha+1}}\|A_{\epsilon m}u+A_{-\epsilon m}u-2u\|_{H^{1}}\|A_{\epsilon m}u-A_{-\epsilon m}u\|_{H^{1}}\\
+&\epsilon^{2\alpha-2}\sum_{m\geq\lfloor 1/\epsilon\rfloor}{1\over m^{\alpha+1}}\|A_{\epsilon m}u+A_{-\epsilon m}u-2u\|_{H^{1}}\|A_{\epsilon m}u-A_{-\epsilon m}u\|_{H^{1}}\\
=&I+II.\end{split}

Applying the second and third estimates from Lemma
4
gives

\begin{split}I\leq&C\epsilon^{2\alpha+1}\|u\|^{2}_{H^{3}}\sum_{m=1}^{\lfloor 1/\epsilon\rfloor}{m^{2-\alpha}}.\end{split}

A classic “integral comparison” tells us that

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{\lfloor 1/\epsilon\rfloor}{m^{2-\alpha}}\leq C\epsilon^{\alpha-3}.

So then

I\leq C\epsilon^{3\alpha-2}\|u\|^{2}_{H^{3}}.

For
II
we use the first estimate in Lemma
4
to get

\begin{split}II\leq&C\epsilon^{2\alpha-2}\|u\|^{2}_{H^{1}}\sum_{m>\lfloor 1/\epsilon\rfloor}{1\over m^{\alpha+1}}.\end{split}

Then another integral type estimate tells us
\displaystyle\sum_{m>\lfloor 1/\epsilon\rfloor}{1\over m^{\alpha+1}}\leq C\epsilon^{\alpha}.

So that
II\leq C\epsilon^{3\alpha-2}\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{2}
.
Therefore we have our final estimate for
N_{\epsilon}
:

(10)

\begin{split}\sup_{|\tau|\leq\tau_{0}}\|N_{\epsilon}-2\epsilon^{2\alpha-1}\zeta_{\alpha}u\partial_{X}u\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\epsilon^{3\alpha-2}.\end{split}

Part 2c: Estimates for
M_{\epsilon}
.
We need to treat
\|M_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}
and
\|\partial_{X}M_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}
separately and we start with the former. An routine estimate shows

|M_{\epsilon}|\leq\sum_{m\geq 1}m\epsilon^{\alpha-1}|(A_{\epsilon m}-A_{-\epsilon m})u|\sup_{\sigma\in I_{m}}|E_{m}(\sigma)|

where
I_{m}
is the interval between
-m\epsilon^{\alpha-1}A_{\epsilon m}u
and
-m\epsilon^{\alpha-1}A_{-\epsilon m}u
.

If we assume
\sigma>0
then

\begin{split}|E_{m}(\sigma)|\leq&\int_{0}^{\sigma}|V_{m}^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}(\phi)||\sigma-\phi|d\phi\leq\sup_{0\leq\phi\leq\sigma}|V_{m}^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}(\phi)|\int_{0}^{\sigma}|\sigma-\phi|d\phi={1\over 2}\sup_{0\leq\phi\leq\sigma}|V_{m}^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}(\phi)|\sigma^{2}.\end{split}

|V_{m}^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}(\phi)|
is decreasing and so
\sup_{0\leq\phi\leq\sigma}|V_{m}^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}(\phi)|=|V_{m}^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}(0)|=C/m^{\alpha+4}
.
So in this case

|E_{m}(\sigma)|\leq{C\sigma^{2}\over m^{\alpha+4}}.

Similarly if
\sigma\leq 0
:

\begin{split}|E_{m}(\sigma)|\leq\int_{\sigma}^{0}|V_{m}^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}(\phi)||\sigma-\phi|d\phi\leq{1\over 2}\sup_{0\leq\phi\leq\sigma}|V_{m}^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}(\phi)|\sigma^{2}\leq{C\sigma^{2}\over(m+\sigma)^{\alpha+4}}.\end{split}

In either case we had:

(11)

|E_{m}(\sigma)|\leq{C\sigma^{2}\over(m-|\sigma|)^{\alpha+4}}.

Note that the constant
C
here is independent of
m
.

We have, using Lemma
4
and Sobolev:

(12)

\sigma\in I_{m}\implies|\sigma|\leq Cm\epsilon^{\alpha-1}\|u\|_{H^{1}}.

In particular by ensuring that
\epsilon
is not so large we have

(13)

\sigma\in I_{m}\implies|\sigma|\leq m/2.

So (
11
), (
12
) and (
13
) give

(14)

\sup_{\sigma\in I_{m}}|E_{m}(\sigma)|\leq\left({Cm^{2}\epsilon^{2\alpha-2}\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\over(m-m/2)^{\alpha+4}}\right)\leq{C\epsilon^{2\alpha-2}\over m^{\alpha+2}}\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{2}.

In turn this gives

|M_{\epsilon}|\leq C\epsilon^{3\alpha-3}\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\sum_{m\geq 1}{1\over m^{\alpha+1}}|(A_{\epsilon m}-A_{-\epsilon m})u|.

Then Lemma
4
leads us to:

\begin{split}\|M_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}&\leq C\epsilon^{3\alpha-3}\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\sum_{m\geq 1}{1\over m^{\alpha+1}}\|(A_{\epsilon m}-A_{-\epsilon m})u\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq C\epsilon^{3\alpha-2}\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{3}\sum_{m\geq 1}{1\over m^{\alpha}}\\
&\leq C\zeta_{\alpha}\epsilon^{3\alpha-2}\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{3}.\end{split}

Next we compute using the fundamental theorem and some algebra:

\partial_{X}M_{\epsilon}=\epsilon^{\alpha-1}\sum_{m\geq 1}m\left[E_{m}(-m\epsilon^{\alpha-1}A_{\epsilon m}u)A_{\epsilon m}\partial_{X}u-E_{m}(-m\epsilon^{\alpha-1}A_{-\epsilon m}u)A_{-\epsilon m}\partial_{X}u\right].

Adding zero takes us to

\begin{split}\partial_{X}M_{\epsilon}&=\epsilon^{\alpha-1}\sum_{m\geq 1}mE_{m}(-m\epsilon^{\alpha-1}A_{\epsilon m}u)\left(A_{\epsilon m}-A_{-\epsilon m}\right)\partial_{X}u\\
&+\epsilon^{\alpha-1}\sum_{m\geq 1}m\left(E_{m}(-m\epsilon^{\alpha-1}A_{\epsilon m}u)-E_{m}(-m\epsilon^{\alpha-1}A_{-\epsilon m}u)\right)A_{-\epsilon m}\partial_{X}u\\
&=III+IV.\end{split}

Using (
11
) and the same reasoning that lead to (
13
) yields

\begin{split}|III|\leq&C\epsilon^{\alpha-1}\sum_{m\geq 1}m{\left(m\epsilon^{\alpha-1}A_{\epsilon m}u\right)^{2}\over m^{\alpha+4}}\left|(A_{\epsilon m}-A_{\epsilon m})\partial_{X}u\right|\\
\leq&C\epsilon^{3\alpha-3}\sum_{m\geq 1}{1\over m^{\alpha+1}}|A_{\epsilon m}u|^{2}\left|(A_{\epsilon m}-A_{\epsilon m})\partial_{X}u\right|.\end{split}

Sobolev and the first estimate in Lemma
4
imply

|A_{\epsilon m}u(X)|\leq C\|u\|_{H^{1}}
and so

\begin{split}|III|\leq C\epsilon^{3\alpha-3}\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\sum_{m\geq 1}{1\over m^{\alpha+1}}\left|(A_{\epsilon m}-A_{\epsilon m})\partial_{X}u\right|.\end{split}

We take the
L^{2}
-norm of the above, use the second estimate in Lemma
4
and do the resulting sum to obtain

\begin{split}\|III\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\epsilon^{3\alpha-3}\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\sum_{m\geq 1}{1\over m^{\alpha+1}}\epsilon m\|\partial_{X}u\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\epsilon^{3\alpha-2}\|u\|^{3}_{H^{2}}.\end{split}

For
IV
, routine estimates and the mean value theorem give

|IV|\leq\epsilon^{2\alpha-2}\sum_{m\geq 1}m^{2}|A_{-\epsilon m}\partial_{X}u||(A_{\epsilon m}-A_{-\epsilon m})u|\sup_{\sigma\in I_{m}}|E^{\prime}_{m}(\sigma)|

where
I_{m}
is consistent with its definition above. Reasoning analogous to that which led
(
11
) can be used to show that

|E^{\prime}_{m}(\sigma)|\leq C|\sigma|/(m-|\sigma|)^{\alpha+4}.
And then (
12
) and (
13
) imply

\sup_{\sigma\in I_{m}}|E_{m}^{\prime}(\sigma)|\leq{C\epsilon^{\alpha-1}\over m^{\alpha+3}}\|u\|_{H^{1}}.

So

|IV|\leq C\epsilon^{3\alpha-3}\|u\|_{H^{1}}\sum_{m\geq 1}{1\over m^{\alpha+1}}|A_{-\epsilon m}\partial_{X}u||(A_{\epsilon m}-A_{-\epsilon m})u|.

Using Sobolev and the estimates in Lemma
4
in ways we have done above
give

\|IV\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\epsilon^{3\alpha-2}\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{3}.

This, in conjunction with the above estimates for
\|III\|_{L^{2}}
and
\|M_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}
result in

(15)

\sup_{|\tau|\leq\tau_{0}}\|M_{\epsilon}\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\epsilon^{3\alpha-2}.

Part 3: finishing touches.
Using the decomposition of
F_{\epsilon}
from (
6
)
along with the definitions of
c_{\alpha}
,
\kappa_{1}
,
\kappa_{2}
and
\kappa_{3}
we obtain
after some routine triangle inequality estimates

\begin{split}\|a_{\epsilon}+F_{\epsilon}\|_{H^{1}}=&\left\|a_{\epsilon}+\alpha(\alpha+1)L_{\epsilon}+{\alpha(\alpha+1)(\alpha+2)\over 2}N_{\epsilon}+M_{\epsilon}\right\|_{H^{1}}\\
\leq&\left\|a_{\epsilon}+\epsilon^{\alpha}c_{\alpha}^{2}\partial_{X}u+\epsilon^{2\alpha-1}\kappa_{2}u\partial_{X}u+\epsilon^{2\alpha-1}\kappa_{3}H|D|^{\alpha}u\right\|_{H^{1}}\\
+&\alpha(\alpha+1)\left\|L_{\epsilon}-\epsilon^{\alpha}\zeta_{\alpha}\partial_{X}u-\epsilon^{2\alpha-1}\eta_{\alpha}H|D|^{\alpha}u\right\|_{H^{1}}\\
+&{\alpha(\alpha+1)(\alpha+2)\over 2}\left\|N_{\epsilon}-2\epsilon^{2\alpha-1}\zeta_{\alpha}u\partial_{X}u\right\|_{H^{1}}\\
+&\left\|M_{\epsilon}\right\|_{H^{1}}.\end{split}

So we use (
5
), (
8
), (
10
) and (
15
) to get

\sup_{|\tau|\leq\tau_{0}}\|a_{\epsilon}+F_{\epsilon}\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\left(\epsilon^{2\alpha-1+r_{\alpha}}+\epsilon^{3\alpha-2}\right).

For
\alpha\in(1,2]
we have
\epsilon^{2\alpha-1+r_{\alpha}}=\epsilon^{2\alpha}\leq\epsilon^{3\alpha-2}

and so

\alpha\in(1,2]\implies\sup_{|\tau|\leq\tau_{0}}\|a_{\epsilon}+F_{\epsilon}\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\epsilon^{3\alpha-2}.

For
\alpha\in(2,3)
we have
\epsilon^{2\alpha-1+r_{\alpha}}=\epsilon^{\alpha+2}\geq\epsilon^{3\alpha-2}
so

\alpha\in(1,2]\implies\sup_{|\tau|\leq\tau_{0}}\|a_{\epsilon}+F_{\epsilon}\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\epsilon^{\alpha+2}.

By design, we have
R_{\epsilon}(j,t)=a_{\epsilon}(\epsilon(j-c_{\alpha}t),\epsilon^{\alpha}t)+F_{\epsilon}(\epsilon(j-c_{\alpha}t),\epsilon^{\alpha}t)
.
Estimate (4.8) from Lemma 4.3 in
[
2
]
states that if

(16)

G(X)\in H^{1}\implies\|G(\epsilon\cdot)\|_{\ell^{2}}\leq C\epsilon^{-1/2}\|G\|_{H^{1}}.

Thus (
2
) and (
2
) give us

\sup_{|t|\leq\tau_{0}/\epsilon^{\alpha}}\|R_{\epsilon}(\epsilon(\cdot-t),\epsilon^{\alpha}t)\|_{\ell^{2}}\leq C\epsilon^{\beta_{\alpha}}

with
\beta_{\alpha}
as in the statement of the proposition. That does it.
∎

3.
A general approximation theorem

The previous section provides a rigorous bound on the size of the residual
R_{\epsilon}
, but this is only part of the approximation theory. We need to demonstrate that a true solution
x_{j}(t)
is shadowed by the approximate solution
\widetilde{x}_{j}(t)
in an appropriate sense. The argument (which is a direct descendent of the validation of KdV as the long-wave limit for FPUT lattices in
[
8
]
) is based on “energy estimates”. This estimates are much more transparent after a change of coordinates. After the recoordinatization, we prove a conservation law which will imply global in time existence of solutions. Then we prove a general approximation result.

3.1.
Relative displacement/velocity coordinates.

Let

r_{j}:=x_{j+1}-x_{j}-1\quad\text{and}\quad p_{j}:=\dot{x}_{j}.

These are referred to as the relative displacement and velocity.
Note that if
r_{j}=p_{j}=0
then the system is in the equilibrium configuration
x_{j}=j
.
A calculation shows that

x_{j+m}-x_{j}=m+{\mathcal{G}}_{m}r_{j}\quad\text{and}\quad x_{j}-x_{j-m}=m+{\mathcal{G}}_{-m}r_{j}

where

{\mathcal{G}}_{m}r_{j}:=\sum_{l=0}^{m-1}r_{j+l}\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathcal{G}}_{-m}r_{j}:=\sum_{l=0}^{m-1}r_{k-m-l}.

Also define operators
S^{k}
,
\delta^{\pm}_{m}
via:

S^{k}f_{j}:=f_{j+k},\quad\delta^{+}_{m}f_{j}:=f_{j+m}-f_{j}\quad\text{and}\quad\delta^{-}_{m}f_{j}:=f_{j}-f_{j-m}.

Here are a few useful formulas that are not too hard to confirm:

{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\delta_{1}^{+}=\delta_{m}^{+}\quad\text{and}\quad S^{-m}{\mathcal{G}}_{m}r_{j}={\mathcal{G}}_{m}r_{j-m}={\mathcal{G}}_{-m}r_{j}.

The above considerations allow us to reformulate (
1
) as a first order system in terms of
r_{j}
and
p_{j}
:

(17)

\begin{split}\dot{r}_{j}&=\delta^{+}_{1}p_{j}\quad\text{and}\quad\dot{p}_{j}=\sum_{m\geq 1}\delta^{-}_{m}V_{m}^{\prime}({\mathcal{G}}_{m}r)_{j}\end{split}

where
V_{m}
coincides with its definition in the previous section.

3.2.
Energy conservation

Let

{\mathcal{E}}:=K+P

where

K:={1\over 2}\sum_{j\in{\bf{Z}}}p_{j}^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad P:=\sum_{j\in{\bf{Z}}}\sum_{m\geq 1}V_{m}({\mathcal{G}}_{m}r)_{j}.

This quantity corresponds to the total mechanical energy of the system. It is constant and here is the extremely classical argument.

Differentiation of
{\mathcal{E}}
with respect to
t
:

\begin{split}\dot{{\mathcal{E}}}=\sum_{j\in{\bf{Z}}}\left(p_{j}\dot{p}_{j}+\sum_{m\geq 1}V_{m}^{\prime}({\mathcal{G}}_{m}r)_{j}{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\dot{r}_{j}\right).\end{split}

Eliminate
\dot{p}
and
\dot{r}
on the right using (
17
):

\begin{split}\dot{{\mathcal{E}}}=\sum_{j\in{\bf{Z}}}\left(p_{j}\sum_{m\geq 1}\delta^{-}_{m}V_{m}^{\prime}({\mathcal{G}}_{m}r)_{j}+\sum_{m\geq 1}V_{m}^{\prime}({\mathcal{G}}_{m}r)_{j}{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\delta^{+}_{1}p_{j}\right).\end{split}

Rearrange the sums:

\begin{split}\dot{{\mathcal{E}}}=\sum_{m\geq 1}\sum_{j\in{\bf{Z}}}\left(p_{j}\delta^{-}_{m}V_{m}^{\prime}({\mathcal{G}}_{m}r)_{j}+V_{m}^{\prime}({\mathcal{G}}_{m}r)_{j}{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\delta^{+}_{1}p_{j}\right).\end{split}

Use
{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\delta_{1}^{+}=\delta_{m}^{+}
in the second term and the summation by parts formula in the first:

\begin{split}\dot{{\mathcal{E}}}=\sum_{m\geq 1}\sum_{j\in{\bf{Z}}}\left(-\delta^{+}_{m}p_{j}V_{m}^{\prime}({\mathcal{G}}_{m}r)_{j}+V_{m}^{\prime}({\mathcal{G}}_{m}r)_{j}\delta^{+}_{m}p_{j}\right)=0.\end{split}

So
{\mathcal{E}}
is constant.

3.3.
Norm equivalence and global existence

In certain circumstances, the (square root of the) energy
E
from the previous section is equivalent to the
\ell^{2}\times\ell^{2}
norm. It is trivial that
K
is equivalent to
\|p\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}
but the part involving
P
is not obvious. Here is the result.

Lemma 5
.

Suppose that
\alpha>1
such that
2\zeta_{\alpha+1}-\zeta_{\alpha}>0
. Then there exists
\rho>0
and a constant
C>1
such that

\|r\|_{\ell^{2}}\leq\rho\implies C^{-1}\|r\|_{\ell^{2}}\leq\sqrt{P}\leq C\|r\|_{\ell^{2}}.

The proof of this will be a consequence of Proposition
8
, below. For now, note that it implies that small initial data for (
17
) implies global existence of solutions. Specifically:

Corollary 6
.

Fix
\alpha>1
such that
2\zeta_{\alpha+1}-\zeta_{\alpha}>0
. Then there exists
\rho,C>0

such that if
\|\bar{r},\bar{p}\|_{\ell^{2}\times\ell^{2}}\leq\rho
then there exists
(r_{j}(t),p_{j}(t))\in C^{1}({\bf{R}};\ell^{2}\times\ell^{2})
which solves (
17
) and for which
(r_{j}(0),p_{j}(0))=(\bar{r}_{j},\bar{p}_{j})
. Moreover

\sup_{t\in{\bf{R}}}\|r(t),p(t)\|_{\ell^{2}\times\ell^{2}}\leq C\|\bar{r},\bar{p}\|_{\ell^{2}\times\ell^{2}}
.

We omit the proof as it is classical. In any case, it is nearly identical to the proof of Theorem 5.2 of
[
2
]
.

.

2\zeta_{\alpha+1}-\zeta_{\alpha}>0

3.4.
Approximation in general

Let

r_{j}(t)=\widetilde{r}_{j}(t)+\eta_{j}(t)\quad\text{and}\quad p_{j}(t)=\widetilde{p}_{j}(t)+\xi_{j}(t).

where
\widetilde{r}_{j}(t)
and
\widetilde{p}_{j}(t)
are some given functions which we expect are good approximators
to true solutions
r_{j}(t)
and
p_{j}(t)
of (
17
). Then the “errors”
\eta_{j}(t)
and
\xi_{j}(t)
solve

(18)

\dot{\eta}_{j}=\delta_{1}^{+}\xi_{j}+\Res_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad\dot{\xi}_{j}=\sum_{m\geq 1}\delta_{m}^{-}\left[V_{m}^{\prime}\left({\mathcal{G}}_{m}\left(\widetilde{r}+\eta\right)\right)-V_{m}^{\prime}\left({\mathcal{G}}_{m}\widetilde{r}\right)\right]_{j}+\Res_{2}

where

(19)

\Res_{1}=\delta_{1}^{+}\widetilde{p}_{j}-\dot{\widetilde{r}}_{j}\quad\text{and}\quad\Res_{2}=\sum_{m\geq 1}\delta_{m}^{-}V_{m}^{\prime}\left({\mathcal{G}}_{m}\widetilde{r}\right)_{j}-\dot{\widetilde{p}}_{j}.

The functions
\Res_{1}
and
\Res_{2}
are, like
R_{\epsilon}
, residuals and quantify the amount by which the approximators
\widetilde{r}_{j}(t)
and
\widetilde{p}_{j}(t)
fail to satisfy (
17
). Ultimately these will be expressed in terms of
R_{\epsilon}
, but for now we leave things general.

Our goal in this section is to show that
\eta_{j}(t)
and
\xi_{j}(t)
remain small (in
\ell^{2}
) over long time periods, provided they are initially small. In particular we prove:

Theorem 7
.

Suppose that
\alpha>1
with
2\zeta_{\alpha+1}-\zeta_{\alpha}>0
. Assume further that for some
\tau_{0},C_{1},\epsilon_{1}>0
,

\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{1}]
implies

(20)

\sup_{|t|\leq\tau_{0}/\epsilon^{\alpha}}\left(\|\Res_{1}\|_{\ell^{2}}+\|\Res_{2}\|_{\ell^{2}}\right)\leq C_{1}\epsilon^{\beta},\quad\sup_{|t|\leq\tau_{0}/\epsilon^{\alpha}}\|\dot{\widetilde{r}}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\leq C_{1}\epsilon^{\alpha}

and

\|\bar{\eta},\bar{\xi}\|_{\ell^{2}\times\ell^{2}}\leq C_{1}\epsilon^{\beta-\alpha}.

Then there exists constants
C_{*},\epsilon_{*}>0
so that the following holds for
\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{*}]
.
If
\eta_{j}(t)
,
\xi_{j}(t)
solve (
18
) with initial data
\bar{\eta}_{j}
,
\bar{\xi}_{j}
we have

\sup_{|t|\leq\tau_{0}/\epsilon^{\alpha}}\|\eta(t),\xi(t)\|_{\ell^{2}\times\ell^{2}}\leq C_{*}\epsilon^{\beta-\alpha}.

Proof.

We begin by rewriting (
18
) in a helpful way.
For
a,b\in{\bf{R}}
put

W_{m}(a,b):=V_{m}(b+a)-V_{m}(b)-V_{m}^{\prime}(b)a

and let

W_{m}^{\prime}(a,b):=\partial_{a}W_{m}(a,b)=V^{\prime}_{m}(b+a)-V^{\prime}_{m}(b).

With this (
18
) becomes

(21)

\dot{\eta}_{j}=\delta_{1}^{+}\xi_{j}+\Res_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad\dot{\xi}_{j}=\sum_{m\geq 1}\delta_{m}^{-}W_{m}^{\prime}({\mathcal{G}}_{m}\eta,{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\widetilde{r})_{j}+\Res_{2}.

The point of introducing
W_{m}
here is that now (
21
) is structurally similar to (
17
) with
V_{m}
replaced by
W_{m}
. Then we hope we can recapture some of the glory of conservation of
{\mathcal{E}}
from above, but for the error equations.

So for a solution of (
21
) put:

{\mathcal{H}}:={1\over 2}\sum_{j\in{\bf{Z}}}\xi_{j}^{2}+\sum_{j\in{\bf{Z}}}\sum_{m\geq 1}W_{m}({\mathcal{G}}_{m}\eta,{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\widetilde{r})_{j}.

This is our replacement for
{\mathcal{E}}
. The following proposition contains the key properties of the second term in
{\mathcal{H}}
, chief of which that under some conditions it is equivalent to
\|\eta\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}
.

Proposition 8
.

Fix
\alpha>1
with
2\zeta_{\alpha+1}-\zeta_{\alpha}>0
. Then there exists
C>1
such that the following hold when
\|\widetilde{r}\|_{\ell^{2}}\leq 1/4
and
\|\eta\|_{\ell^{2}}\leq 1/4
:

(22)

C^{-1}\|\eta\|_{\ell^{2}}\leq\sqrt{\sum_{j\in{\bf{Z}}}\sum_{m\geq 1}W_{m}({\mathcal{G}}_{m}\eta,{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\widetilde{r})_{j}}\leq C\|\eta\|_{\ell^{2}},

(23)

\sum_{m\geq 1}m\|W_{m}^{\prime}({\mathcal{G}}_{m}\eta,{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\widetilde{r})\|_{\ell^{2}}\leq C\|\eta\|_{\ell^{2}},

(24)

\sum_{m\geq 1}m\|\partial_{b}W_{m}({\mathcal{G}}_{m}\eta,{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\widetilde{r})\|_{\ell^{1}}\leq C\|\eta\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}}.

Remark 7
.

Note that
W_{m}(a,0)=V_{m}(a)
and so
if
\widetilde{r}_{j}(t)
is identically zero then (
22
) coincides exactly with
the estimate in Lemma
5
, with
\eta
swapped with
r
.

Proof.

We begin with (
22
).
Taylor’s theorem tells us that

W_{m}(a,b)={\alpha(\alpha+1)a^{2}/2(m+b_{*})^{\alpha+2}}

with
b_{*}
in between
b
and
b+a
. This leads to

(25)

{\alpha(\alpha+1)\over 2(m+|a|+|b|)^{\alpha+2}}a^{2}\leq W_{m}(a,b)\leq{\alpha(\alpha+1)\over 2(m-|a|-|b|)^{\alpha+2}}a^{2}.

We have assumed
\|\eta\|_{\ell^{2}}\leq 1/4
.
Thus the classical estimate

\|f\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\leq\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}
along with the triangle inequality
tell us

|{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\eta_{j}|\leq\|{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\eta\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\leq\sum_{l=0}^{m-1}\|\eta_{\cdot+l}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}=m\|\eta\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\leq m/4.

Similarly
\|\widetilde{r}\|_{\ell^{2}}\leq 1/4
implies
|{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\widetilde{r}_{j}|\leq m/4
. So we have

m-|{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\eta_{j}|-|{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\widetilde{r}_{j}|\geq m/2

and

m+|{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\eta_{j}|+|{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\widetilde{r}_{j}|\geq 3m/2

and thus (
25
) gives

{2^{\alpha+1}\alpha(\alpha+1)\over 3^{\alpha+2}m^{\alpha+2}}({\mathcal{G}}_{m}\eta_{j})^{2}\leq W_{m}({\mathcal{G}}_{m}\eta,{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\widetilde{r})_{j}\leq{2^{\alpha+1}\alpha(\alpha+1)\over m^{\alpha+2}}({\mathcal{G}}_{m}\eta_{j})^{2}.

Summing this gets us to

{2^{\alpha+1}\alpha(\alpha+1)\over 3^{\alpha+2}}P_{2}\leq\sum_{j\in{\bf{Z}}}\sum_{m\geq 1}W_{m}({\mathcal{G}}_{m}\eta,{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\widetilde{r})_{j}\leq 2^{\alpha+1}\alpha(\alpha+1)P_{2}

where

P_{2}:=\sum_{j\in{\bf{Z}}}\sum_{m\geq 1}{1\over m^{\alpha+2}}({\mathcal{G}}_{m}\eta)_{j}^{2}.

Using the definition of
{\mathcal{G}}_{m}
and multiplying out the square gives

P_{2}=\sum_{j\in{\bf{Z}}}\sum_{m\geq 1}{1\over m^{\alpha+2}}\left(\sum_{l=0}^{m-1}\eta^{2}_{j+l}+2\sum_{0\leq l<k\leq m-1}\eta_{j+l}\eta_{j+k}\right).

Rearranging sums and doing some computations on the “diagonal part” of the above gets

\begin{split}\sum_{j\in{\bf{Z}}}\sum_{m\geq 1}{1\over m^{\alpha+2}}\sum_{l=0}^{m-1}\eta^{2}_{j+l}=&\sum_{m\geq 1}{1\over m^{\alpha+2}}\sum_{l=0}^{m-1}\sum_{j\in{\bf{Z}}}\eta^{2}_{j+l}=\sum_{m\geq 1}{1\over m^{\alpha+2}}\sum_{l=0}^{m-1}\|\eta\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}=\zeta_{\alpha+1}\|\eta\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}}.\end{split}

Therefore

P_{2}-\zeta_{\alpha+1}\|\eta\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}}=2\sum_{j\in{\bf{Z}}}\sum_{m\geq 1}{1\over m^{\alpha+2}}\sum_{0\leq l<k\leq m-1}\eta_{j+l}\eta_{j+k}=:P_{22}.

Rearranging sums gives

P_{22}=2\sum_{m\geq 1}{1\over m^{\alpha+2}}\sum_{0\leq l<k\leq m-1}\sum_{j\in{\bf{Z}}}\eta_{j+l}\eta_{j+k}.

We use Cauchy-Schwarz to get

|P_{22}|\leq 2\sum_{m\geq 1}{1\over m^{\alpha+2}}\sum_{0\leq l<k\leq m-1}\|\eta\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}.

Since
\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq l<k\leq m-1}1=m(m-1)/2
, we obtain

|P_{22}|\leq\|\eta\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}\sum_{m\geq 1}{m(m-1)\over m^{\alpha+2}}=\left(\zeta_{\alpha}-\zeta_{\alpha+1}\right)\|\eta\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}.

Thus

\left|P_{2}-\zeta_{\alpha+1}\|\eta\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}}\right|\leq\left(\zeta_{\alpha}-\zeta_{\alpha+1}\right)\|\eta\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}

or rather

\left(2\zeta_{\alpha+1}-\zeta_{\alpha}\right)\|\eta\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}}\leq P_{2}\leq\zeta_{\alpha}\|\eta\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}}.

Therefore

2\zeta_{\alpha+1}-\zeta_{\alpha}>0

implies that
\sqrt{P_{2}}
is equivalent to
\|\eta\|_{\ell^{2}}
.

This in combination with (
22
) gives:

{2^{\alpha+1}\alpha(\alpha+1)\over 3^{\alpha+2}}\left(2\zeta_{\alpha+1}-\zeta_{\alpha}\right)\|\eta\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}}\leq\sum_{j\in{\bf{Z}}}\sum_{m\geq 1}W_{m}({\mathcal{G}}_{m}\eta,{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\widetilde{r})_{j}\leq 2^{\alpha+1}\alpha(\alpha+1)\zeta_{\alpha}\|\eta\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}}.

Which is to say we have (
22
).

Next up is (
23
).
The mean value theorem tell us that

W^{\prime}_{m}(a,b)={\alpha(\alpha+1)a/(m+b_{*})^{\alpha+2}}

where
b_{*}
lies between
b
and
b+a

As above, we have

\|{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\widetilde{r}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\leq m/4
and
\|{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\eta\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\leq m/4
. So
b_{*}
would be controlled above by
m/2
, for all
j
. Thus

\left|W^{\prime}_{m}({\mathcal{G}}_{m}\eta,{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\widetilde{r})_{j}\right|\leq{2^{\alpha+2}\alpha(\alpha+1)\over m^{\alpha+2}}|{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\eta_{j}|.

We take the
\ell^{2}
-norm and use the triangle inequality

\left\|W^{\prime}_{m}({\mathcal{G}}_{m}\eta,{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\widetilde{r})\right\|_{\ell^{2}}\leq{2^{\alpha+2}\alpha(\alpha+1)\over m^{\alpha+2}}\|{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\eta\|_{\ell^{2}}\leq{2^{\alpha+2}\alpha(\alpha+1)\over m^{\alpha+1}}\|\eta\|_{\ell^{2}}

Thus

\sum_{m\geq 1}m\left\|W^{\prime}_{m}({\mathcal{G}}_{m}\eta,{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\widetilde{r})\right\|_{\ell^{2}}\leq{2^{\alpha+2}\zeta_{\alpha}\alpha(\alpha+1)}\|\eta\|_{\ell^{2}}.

This is (
23
).

To get (
24
) is more of the same. We have

\partial_{b}W_{m}(a,b)=-{\alpha(\alpha+1)(\alpha+2)a^{2}/2(m+b_{*})^{\alpha+3}}

with
b_{*}
in between
b
and
b+a
. Much as we did above, we get the estimate

|\partial_{b}W_{m}({\mathcal{G}}_{m}\eta,{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\widetilde{r})_{j}|\leq{2^{\alpha+3}\alpha(\alpha+1)(\alpha+2)\over m^{\alpha+3}}|{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\eta_{j}|^{2}.

Summing over
j
and the triangle inequality lead to

\|\partial_{b}W_{m}({\mathcal{G}}_{m}\eta,{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\widetilde{r})\|_{\ell^{2}}\leq{2^{\alpha+3}\alpha(\alpha+1)(\alpha+2)\over m^{\alpha+3}}\|{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\eta\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}\leq{2^{\alpha+3}\alpha(\alpha+1)(\alpha+2)\over m^{\alpha+2}}\|\eta\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}.

Then

\sum_{m\geq 1}m\|\partial_{b}W_{m}({\mathcal{G}}_{m}\eta,{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\widetilde{r})\|_{\ell^{2}}\leq{2^{\alpha+3}\alpha(\alpha+1)(\alpha+2)\zeta_{\alpha+1}}\|\eta\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}

and we are done.
∎

With Proposition
8
taken care of, we can now get into the energy argument at
the heart of the proof. We begin with
differentiation of
{\mathcal{H}}
to get

\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}=\sum_{j\in{\bf{Z}}}\left(\xi_{j}\dot{\xi}_{j}+\sum_{m\geq 1}W^{\prime}_{m}({\mathcal{G}}_{m}\eta,{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\widetilde{r})_{j}{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\dot{\eta}_{j}\right)+\sum_{j\in{\bf{Z}}}\sum_{m\geq 1}\partial_{b}W_{m}({\mathcal{G}}_{m}\eta,{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\widetilde{r})_{j}{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\dot{\widetilde{r}}_{j}.

Call the terms on the right
I
and
II
in the obvious way. Using (
21
) in
I
gets:

I=\sum_{j\in{\bf{Z}}}\left(\xi_{j}\left(\sum_{m\geq 1}\delta_{m}^{-}W_{m}^{\prime}({\mathcal{G}}_{m}\eta,{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\widetilde{r})_{j}+\Res_{2}\right)+\sum_{m\geq 1}W^{\prime}_{m}({\mathcal{G}}_{m}\eta,{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\widetilde{r})_{j}{\mathcal{G}}_{m}(\delta_{1}^{+}\xi_{j}+\Res_{1})\right).

Summing by parts and using
{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\delta_{1}^{+}=\delta_{m}^{+}
gives some cancellations:

I=\sum_{j\in{\bf{Z}}}\left(\xi_{j}\Res_{2}+\sum_{m\geq 1}W^{\prime}_{m}({\mathcal{G}}_{m}\eta,{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\widetilde{r})_{j}{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\Res_{1}\right).

Cauchy-Schwarz implies
|I|\leq\|\xi\|_{\ell^{2}}\|\Res_{2}\|_{\ell_{2}}+|I_{2}|

where

I_{2}:=\sum_{m\geq 1}\sum_{j\in{\bf{Z}}}W^{\prime}_{m}({\mathcal{G}}_{m}\eta,{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\widetilde{r})_{j}{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\Res_{1}.

The operator
{\mathcal{G}}_{m}
is symmetric with respect to the
\ell^{2}
-inner product and so:

I_{2}=\sum_{m\geq 1}\sum_{j\in{\bf{Z}}}\left({\mathcal{G}}_{m}W^{\prime}_{m}({\mathcal{G}}_{m}\eta,{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\widetilde{r})_{j}\right)\Res_{1}.

Reorder the sum again:

I_{2}=\sum_{j\in{\bf{Z}}}\Res_{1}\sum_{m\geq 1}\left({\mathcal{G}}_{m}W^{\prime}_{m}({\mathcal{G}}_{m}\eta,{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\widetilde{r})_{j}\right).

Then Cauchy-Schwarz and the triangle inequality lead to

|I_{2}|\leq\|\Res_{1}\|_{\ell_{2}}\sum_{m\geq 1}\left\|{\mathcal{G}}_{m}W^{\prime}_{m}({\mathcal{G}}_{m}\eta,{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\widetilde{r})\right\|_{\ell^{2}}\leq\|\Res_{1}\|_{\ell^{2}}\sum_{m\geq 1}m\left\|W^{\prime}_{m}({\mathcal{G}}_{m}\eta,{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\widetilde{r})\right\|_{\ell^{2}}.

The estimate (
23
) from Proposition
8
gives

|I_{2}|\leq C\|\Res_{1}\|_{\ell^{2}}\|\eta\|_{\ell^{2}}.

Thus we have

|I|\leq C\left(\|\Res_{1}\|_{\ell^{2}}\|\eta\|_{\ell^{2}}+\|\Res_{2}\|_{\ell^{2}}\|\xi\|_{\ell^{2}}\right)

Now look at
II
. By using naive estimates we get

|II|\leq\sum_{j\in{\bf{Z}}}\sum_{m\geq 1}|\partial_{b}W_{m}({\mathcal{G}}_{m}\eta,{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\widetilde{r})_{j}|\|{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\dot{\widetilde{r}}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\leq\|\dot{\widetilde{r}}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\sum_{m\geq 1}m\|\partial_{b}W_{m}({\mathcal{G}}_{m}\eta,{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\widetilde{r})\|_{\ell^{1}}.

Then (
24
) from Proposition
8
yields

\begin{split}|II|\leq C\|\dot{\widetilde{r}}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\|\eta\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}.\end{split}

So all together

\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}\leq C\left(\|\Res_{1}\|_{\ell^{2}}\|\eta\|_{\ell^{2}}+\|\Res_{2}\|_{\ell^{2}}\|\xi\|_{\ell^{2}}\right)+C\|\dot{\widetilde{r}}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\|\eta\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}.

Using (
22
) we have:

\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}\leq C\left(\|\Res_{1}\|_{\ell^{2}}+\|\Res_{2}\|_{\ell^{2}}\right)\sqrt{{\mathcal{H}}}+C\|\dot{\widetilde{r}}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}{\mathcal{H}}.

The assumptions made on
\Res_{1}
,
\Res_{2}
and
\dot{\widetilde{r}}
lead to

\dot{{\mathcal{H}}}\leq C\epsilon^{\beta}\sqrt{{\mathcal{H}}}+C\epsilon^{\alpha}{\mathcal{H}}.

Applying Grönwall’s inequality yields

\sqrt{{\mathcal{H}}(t)}\leq\epsilon^{C\epsilon^{\alpha}t}\sqrt{{\mathcal{H}}(0)}+C\epsilon^{\beta-\alpha}\left(\epsilon^{C\epsilon^{\alpha}t}-1\right).

Then we use (
22
) one last time to get

\|\eta(t),\xi(t)\|_{\ell^{2}\times\ell^{2}}\leq\epsilon^{C\epsilon^{\alpha}t}\|\bar{\eta},\bar{\xi}\|_{\ell^{2}\times\ell^{2}}+C\epsilon^{\beta-\alpha}\left(\epsilon^{C\epsilon^{\alpha}t}-1\right).

Taking the supremum over
|t|\leq\tau_{0}/\epsilon^{\alpha}
and using the assumption on the size of the initial data gives the final estimate in the theorem.
∎

4.
Proof of Theorem
1

The proof of Theorem
1
is more or less a direct application of the general approximation theorem, Theorem
7
. There are a few small details to attend to, and that is what we do now.

Proof.

(Theorem
1
).
Fix
u(X,\tau)
a solution of (
2
) subject to the bound described in the statement of the Theorem.
Let
\displaystyle v(X,\tau):=-\int_{0}^{X}u(b,\tau)db
so that

Form
\widetilde{x}_{j}
as in (
4
),
namely
\widetilde{x}_{j}(t):=j+\epsilon^{\alpha-2}v(\epsilon(j-c_{\alpha}t),\epsilon^{\alpha}t)
.
Then put

\widetilde{r}_{j}(t):=-1+\delta_{1}^{+}\widetilde{x}_{j}(t)\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{p}_{j}(t):=\dot{\widetilde{x}}_{j}(t).

We first compute
\Res_{1}
and
\Res_{2}
as in (
19
).
We have

\Res_{1}=\delta_{1}^{+}\widetilde{p}-\dot{\widetilde{r}}=\delta_{1}^{+}\left(\dot{\widetilde{x}}\right)-\partial_{t}\left({-1+\delta_{1}^{+}\widetilde{x}}\right)=0.

For
\Res_{2}
, we compute

\begin{split}\Res_{2}&=\sum_{m\geq 1}\delta_{m}^{-}V_{m}^{\prime}\left({\mathcal{G}}_{m}\widetilde{r}\right)_{j}-\dot{\widetilde{p}}_{j}\\
&=\sum_{m\geq 1}\delta_{m}^{-}V_{m}^{\prime}\left(-m+\delta_{m}^{+}\widetilde{x})\right)_{j}-\ddot{\widetilde{x}}_{j}\\
&=-\alpha\sum_{m\geq 1}\left({1\over(\widetilde{x}_{j+m}-\widetilde{x}_{j})^{\alpha+1}}-{1\over(\widetilde{x}_{j}-\widetilde{x}_{j-m})^{\alpha+1}}\right)-\ddot{\widetilde{x}}_{j}\\
&=-R_{\epsilon}\end{split}

with
R_{\epsilon}
as above. Thus Proposition
2
tells us that the hypothesis on the residuals, (
20
), in Theorem

7
is met with
\beta=\beta_{\alpha}
. Note that
\gamma_{\alpha}=\beta_{\alpha}-\alpha
.

The fundamental theorem of calculus gives

(26)

\delta_{1}^{+}(f(\epsilon\cdot))_{j}=f(\epsilon(j+1))-f(\epsilon j)=\int_{\epsilon j}^{\epsilon j+\epsilon}f_{X}(X)dX=\epsilon(A_{\epsilon}f_{X})(\epsilon j).

If we use this and the relation
u=-\partial_{X}v
we have

\begin{split}\widetilde{r}_{j}(t)&=-\epsilon^{\alpha-1}(A_{\epsilon}u)(\epsilon(j-c_{\alpha}t),\epsilon^{\alpha}t)\\
&=-\epsilon^{\alpha-1}u(\epsilon(j-c_{\alpha}t),\epsilon^{\alpha}t)-\epsilon^{\alpha-1}((A_{\epsilon}-1)u)(\epsilon(j-c_{\alpha}t),\epsilon^{\alpha}t).\end{split}

So (
16
) and the final estimate in Lemma
4
gives us

(27)

\sup_{|t|\leq\tau_{0}/\epsilon^{\alpha}}\|\widetilde{r}(t)+\epsilon^{\alpha-1}u(\epsilon(\cdot-c_{\alpha}t),\epsilon^{\alpha}t)\|_{\ell^{2}}\leq C\epsilon^{\alpha-1/2}.

The assumption on the initial conditions in Theorem
1

implies

\|r(0)+\epsilon^{\alpha-1}u(\epsilon\cdot,0)\|=\|\bar{\mu}\|_{\ell^{2}}\leq C\epsilon^{\beta_{\alpha}-\alpha}.

It is easy enough to check that
\epsilon^{\alpha-1/2}\leq\epsilon^{\beta_{\alpha}-\alpha}
and therefore (
27
)
and the triangle inequality cough up

\|r(0)-\widetilde{r}(0)\|_{\ell^{2}}\leq C\epsilon^{\beta_{\alpha}-\alpha}

which
is one of the hypotheses on the initial data in Theorem
7
.

Similarly we have

\begin{split}\widetilde{p}_{j}(t)&=c_{\alpha}\epsilon^{\alpha-1}u(\epsilon(j-c_{\alpha}t),\epsilon^{\alpha}t)+\epsilon^{2\alpha-2}v_{\tau}(\epsilon(j-c_{\alpha}t),\epsilon^{\alpha}t).\end{split}

It is straightforward to use (
2

\sup_{|t|\leq\tau_{0}/\epsilon^{\alpha}}\|\widetilde{p}(t)-c_{\alpha}\epsilon^{\alpha-1}u(\epsilon(\cdot-c_{\alpha}t),\epsilon^{\alpha}t)\|_{\ell^{2}}\leq C\epsilon^{2\alpha-3/2}\leq C\epsilon^{\beta_{\alpha}-\alpha}.

The assumption on the initial conditions in Theorem

tell us:

\|p(0)-c_{\alpha}\epsilon^{\alpha-1}u(\epsilon\cdot,0)\|=\|\bar{\nu}\|_{\ell^{2}}\leq C\epsilon^{\beta_{\alpha}-\alpha}.

Therefore

\|p(0)-\widetilde{p}(0)\|_{\ell^{2}}\leq C\epsilon^{\beta_{\alpha}-\alpha}

which
is the other hypothesis on the initial data in Theorem
7
.

Next, since,

\dot{\widetilde{r}}_{j}(t)=\delta_{1}^{+}\widetilde{p}_{j}(t),

(
26
) and
u=-\partial_{X}v
give us

\dot{\widetilde{r}}_{j}(t)=-c_{\alpha}\epsilon^{\alpha}(A_{\epsilon}u_{X})(\epsilon(j-c_{\alpha}t),\epsilon^{\alpha}t)-\epsilon^{2\alpha-1}(A_{\epsilon}u_{\tau})(\epsilon(j-c_{\alpha}t),\epsilon^{\alpha}t).

An easy estimate provides

\|\dot{\widetilde{r}}(t)\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\leq c_{\alpha}\epsilon^{\alpha}\|A_{\epsilon}u_{X}(\cdot,\epsilon^{\alpha}t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\epsilon^{2\alpha-1}\|A_{\epsilon}u_{\tau}(\cdot,\epsilon^{\alpha}t)\|_{L^{\infty}}

Using Sobolev, followed by the first estimate in Lemma

:

\|\dot{\widetilde{r}}(t)\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\leq C\epsilon^{\alpha}\|u(\cdot,\epsilon^{\alpha}t)\|_{H^{2}}+C\epsilon^{2\alpha-1}\|u_{\tau}(\cdot,\epsilon^{\alpha}t)\|_{H^{1}}.

Then we use (
2

\sup_{|t|\leq\tau_{0}/\epsilon^{\alpha}}\|\dot{\widetilde{r}}(t)\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\leq C\epsilon^{\alpha}+C\epsilon^{2\alpha-1}\leq C\epsilon^{\alpha}.

This gives the estimate on

and (

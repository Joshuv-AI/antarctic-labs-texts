Abstract
. We consider random normal matrix and planar symplectic ensembles, which can be interpreted as two-dimensional Coulomb gases having determinantal and Pfaffian structures, respectively. For general radially symmetric potentials, we derive the asymptotic expansions of the log-partition functions up to and including the O ⁡ ( 1 ) O(1) -terms as the number N N of particles increases. Notably, our findings stress that the formulas of the O ⁡ ( log ⁡ N ) O(\log N) - and O ⁡ ( 1 ) O(1) -terms in these expansions depend on the connectivity of the droplet. For random normal matrix ensembles, our formulas agree with the predictions proposed by Zabrodin and Wiegmann up to a universal additive constant. For planar symplectic ensembles, the expansions contain a new kind of ingredient in the O ⁡ ( N ) O(N) -terms, the logarithmic potential evaluated at the origin in addition to the entropy of the ensembles.



1.
Introduction and main results

The Coulomb gas ensemble in the complex plane is governed by the law

(1.1)

dP_{N}^{(\beta)}(z_{1},\dots,z_{N}):=\frac{1}{Z_{N}^{(\beta)}}\prod_{j>k=1}^{N}|z_{j}-z_{k}|^{\beta}\prod_{j=1}^{N}e^{-\frac{\beta N}{2}Q(z_{j})}\,dA(z_{j}),

where
N
is the number of particles,
\beta
is the inverse temperature and
dA(z):=d^{2}z/\pi
is the area measure.
Here,
Q:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}
is called the confining/external potential that satisfies suitable potential theoretic conditions.
We refer to
[
28
,
44
,
49
]
and references therein for recent developments of two-dimensional Coulomb gases.
Contrary to (
1.1
), the configurational canonical Coulomb gas ensemble in the upper-half plane
[
39
]
(cf.
[
14
, Appendix A]
) has an additional complex conjugation symmetry (i.e. the particles come in complex conjugate pairs) and is governed by the law

(1.2)

d\widetilde{P}_{N}^{(\beta)}(z_{1},\dots,z_{N}):=\frac{1}{\widetilde{Z}_{N}^{(\beta)}}\prod_{j>k=1}^{N}|z_{j}-z_{k}|^{\beta}|z_{j}-\bar{z}_{k}|^{\beta}\prod_{j=1}^{N}|z_{j}-\bar{z}_{j}|^{\beta}\,e^{-\beta NQ(z_{j})}\,dA(z_{j}).

In (
1.1
) and (
1.2
), the normalization constants

(1.3)

\displaystyle Z_{N}^{(\beta)}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{N}}\prod_{j>k=1}^{N}|z_{j}-z_{k}|^{\beta}\prod_{j=1}^{N}e^{-\frac{\beta N}{2}Q(z_{j})}\,dA(z_{j}),

(1.4)

\displaystyle\widetilde{Z}_{N}^{(\beta)}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{N}}\prod_{j>k=1}^{N}|z_{j}-z_{k}|^{\beta}|z_{j}-\bar{z}_{k}|^{\beta}\prod_{j=1}^{N}|z_{j}-\bar{z}_{j}|^{\beta}\,e^{-\beta NQ(z_{j})}\,dA(z_{j})

that make (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) probability measures are called partition functions.
Furthermore, the logarithm of a partition function (divided by
N^{2}
) is often called free energy.

For the special value
\beta=2
, (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) represent joint probability distributions of the random normal matrix and planar symplectic ensembles, respectively.
In particular, if
Q(z)=|z|^{2}
, these correspond to the complex and symplectic Ginibre ensembles
[
31
]
.
An important feature of this special value
\beta=2
is that, due to the Vandermonde determinant terms, the ensembles (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) form
determinantal
and
Pfaffian
point processes in the plane
[
28
]
, respectively.
In other words, all their correlation functions can be expressed in terms of the (pre-)kernel of planar (skew-)orthogonal polynomials.
In the sequel, for
\beta=2
, we omit the superscript
(\beta)
in (
1.3
) and (
1.4
), and simply write
Z_{N}\equiv Z_{N}^{(2)}
and
\widetilde{Z}_{N}\equiv\widetilde{Z}_{N}^{(2)}
.

We mention that the definition of partition functions (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) is more common in the statistical physics community.
On the other hand, in the random matrix theory community, another widely used convention for the (canonical) partition functions is

(1.5)

\mathcal{Z}_{N}:=\frac{1}{N!}Z_{N},\qquad\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{N}:=\frac{1}{N!}\widetilde{Z}_{N},

see e.g.
[
28
, Section 1.4]
.
The prefactor
1/N!
in (
1.5
) allows writing
\mathcal{Z}_{N}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{N}
in terms of a structured determinant and Pfaffian, respectively.

In this work, we study the asymptotic expansions of
Z_{N}
and
\widetilde{Z}_{N}
as
N\to\infty
.

1.1.
Summary of previous results

Before introducing our results, let us summarize some known results on the asymptotics of
Z_{N}^{(\beta)}
for general
\beta
and
Q
.
Cf. the literature on
\widetilde{Z}_{N}^{(\beta)}
is much more limited.

•

(Zabrodin-Wiegmann prediction)
In
[
54
]
, it was predicted that the partition function
Z_{N}^{(\beta)}
has an asymptotic expansion of the form

(1.6)

\log Z_{N}^{(\beta)}=C_{0}N^{2}+C_{1}N\log N+C_{2}N+C_{3}\log N+C_{4}+O(\frac{1}{N}).

Furthermore, they proposed explicit formulas for the constants
C_{j}\equiv C_{j}(\beta,Q)

(j=0,\dots,4)
depending on
\beta
and
Q
, cf. (
1.29
).
(See also
[
15
,
36
]
for a similar prediction in a different setup.)
Incidentally, the formulas for
C_{3}
and
C_{4}
in
[
54
]
have been controversial as pointed out for instance in
[
47
,
50
]
.

•

(Asymptotic of the leading order
O(N^{2})
-term)
It was shown in
[
33
, Theorem 2.11]
and
[
16
, Theorem 1.1]
(among others) that as
N\to\infty
,

(1.7)

\log Z_{N}^{(\beta)}=-\frac{\beta}{2}N^{2}I_{Q}[\mu_{Q}]+o(N^{2}).

Here
\mu_{Q}
is Frostman’s equilibrium measure
[
46
]
, a unique probability measure that minimizes the weighted logarithmic energy

(1.8)

I_{Q}[\mu]:=\iint_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}\log|z-w|\,d\mu(z)\,d\mu(w)+\int_{\mathbb{C}}Q\,d\mu.

•

(Asymptotic up to the
O(N)
-term)
It was shown by Leblé and Serfaty
[
42
, Corollary 1.1]
that as
N\to\infty
,

(1.9)

\log Z_{N}^{(\beta)}=-\frac{\beta}{2}N^{2}I_{Q}[\mu_{Q}]+\frac{\beta}{4}N\log N-\Big(C(\beta)+\Big(1-\frac{\beta}{4}\Big)E_{Q}[\mu_{Q}]\Big)N+o(N),

where
C(\beta)
is a constant independent of the potential
Q
and

(1.10)

E_{Q}[\mu_{Q}]:=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\log(\Delta Q)\,d\mu_{Q}

is the entropy associated with
\mu_{Q}
. Here,
\Delta:=\partial\bar{\partial}
is the quarter of the usual Laplacian.
We refer the reader to
[
10
,
50
]
for the expansion (
1.9
) with quantitative error bounds.

Beyond the general cases mentioned above, for
\beta=2
with a specific (and fundamental from the random matrix theory viewpoint) potential, there have been several works on the precise asymptotic expansion of the partition functions, see e.g.
[
19
,
20
]
and references therein.
This type of potential usually contains certain singularities. As a result, the asymptotic expansions of the associated partition functions are more complicated (for instance, some non-trivial
O(\sqrt{N})
terms appear as well).
Several topics in this direction will be discussed in a separate remark at the end of the next subsection.

1.2.
Main results

We study asymptotic behaviors of
Z_{N}
and
\widetilde{Z}_{N}
for the exactly-solvable case where
Q
is radially symmetric.
Our main findings are summarized as follows.

(i)

We derive the large-
N
expansions of
\log Z_{N}
and
\log\widetilde{Z}_{N}
up to and including the
O(1)
-terms.

(ii)

In the large-
N
expansions, the formulas of the
O(\log N)
- and
O(1)
-terms depend on whether the limiting spectrum is an
annulus
or a
disc
, see Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, respectively, cf. Figure
1
.
(This distinction is crucial in the asymptotic analysis but seems not considered in
[
54
]
.)

(iii)

For the partition function
Z_{N}
of random normal matrix ensembles, our expansions (
1.18
) and (
1.24
) up to the
O(N)
-terms agree with the formula (
1.9
) with
\beta=2
.
Furthermore, we verify from (
1.24
) that the asymptotic formula given in
[
54
, Eqs.(1.2), (C.7)]
holds up to an additive constant (
1.35
).

(iv)

For the partition function
\widetilde{Z}_{N}
of planar symplectic ensembles, the asymptotic formulas (
1.19
) and (
1.25
) are new to the best of our knowledge.
Contrary to (
1.9
), the
O(N)
-terms in these expansions contain not only the entropy but also the logarithmic potential (
1.15
).

Let us be more precise in introducing our results.
It is well known
[
11
,
33
]
that under some mild assumptions on
Q
, as
N\to\infty
, the empirical measures
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\delta_{z_{j}}
of (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) weakly converge to
\mu_{Q}
, which takes the form

(1.11)

d\mu_{Q}=\Delta Q\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{S}\,dA.

Here
S\equiv S_{Q}
is a certain compact subset of
\mathbb{C}
called the
droplet
, see Figure
1
.

We consider the case where the external potential
Q
is radially symmetric, i.e.
Q(z)=q(|z|)
for some function
q
defined in
[0,\infty)
.
We assume the basic growth condition

(1.12)

\liminf_{|z|\to\infty}\frac{Q(z)}{2\log|z|}>1,

which guarantees that
Z_{N},\widetilde{Z}_{N}<+\infty.

Furthermore, we assume that
Q
is smooth, subharmonic in
\mathbb{C}
, and strictly subharmonic in a neighborhood of the droplet.
In terms of the function
q,
the latter conditions are equivalent to the requirements that
rq^{\prime}(r)
is increasing on
(0,\infty)
, and strictly increasing in a neighborhood of the droplet, cf. (
2.3
).
Under the above assumptions, the droplet is given by

(1.13)

S=\mathbb{A}_{r_{0},r_{1}}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:r_{0}\leq|z|\leq r_{1}\},

where
r_{0}
is the largest solution to
rq^{\prime}(r)=0
and
r_{1}
is the smallest solution to
rq^{\prime}(r)=2
, see
[
46
, Section IV.6]
.
(We mention that the annular droplets often appear in non-Hermitian random matrix theory, see e.g.
[
32
]
.)
In particular, if
r_{0}=0
, we denote
\mathbb{D}_{r_{1}}=\mathbb{A}_{0,r_{1}}.

Henceforth, we keep the assumptions on
Q
described above.

For a radially symmetric potential
Q
, by using (
1.11
) and (
1.13
), one can show that the energy
I_{Q}[\mu_{Q}]
in (
1.8
) is given by

(1.14)

I_{Q}[\mu_{Q}]=q(r_{1})-\log r_{1}-\frac{1}{4}\int_{r_{0}}^{r_{1}}rq^{\prime}(r)^{2}\,dr.

Similarly, in terms of the logarithmic potential

(1.15)

U_{\mu}(z)=\int\log\frac{1}{|z-w|}\,d\mu(w),

we have

(1.16)

U_{\mu_{Q}}(0)=-\int_{S}\log|w|\,d\mu_{Q}(w)=-\log r_{1}+\frac{q(r_{1})-q(r_{0})}{2}.

See
[
46
, Section IV.6]
for more details.

For the annular droplet case, we have the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

(Large-
N
expansion of the partition functions: annular droplet case)

Suppose that
r_{0}>0
, i.e. the droplet
S
in (
1.13
) is an annulus.
Let

(1.17)

F_{Q}[\mathbb{A}_{r_{0},r_{1}}]:=\frac{1}{12}\log\Big(\frac{r_{0}^{2}\Delta Q(r_{0})}{r_{1}^{2}\Delta Q(r_{1})}\Big)-\frac{1}{16}\Big(r_{1}\frac{({\partial}_{r}\Delta Q)(r_{1})}{\Delta Q(r_{1})}-r_{0}\frac{({\partial}_{r}\Delta Q)(r_{0})}{\Delta Q(r_{0})}\Big)+\frac{1}{24}\int_{r_{0}}^{r_{1}}\Big(\frac{{\partial}_{r}\Delta Q(r)}{\Delta Q(r)}\Big)^{2}\,r\,dr.

Then as
N\to\infty,
the following holds.

(i)

(Random normal matrix ensemble)
We have

(1.18)

\begin{split}\log Z_{N}&=-N^{2}I_{Q}[\mu_{Q}]+\frac{1}{2}N\log N+\Big(\frac{\log(2\pi)}{2}-1-\frac{1}{2}E_{Q}[\mu_{Q}]\Big)\,N\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{2}\log N+\frac{\log(2\pi)}{2}+F_{Q}[\mathbb{A}_{r_{0},r_{1}}]+O(N^{-1}).\end{split}

(ii)

(Planar symplectic ensemble)
We have

(1.19)

\begin{split}\log\widetilde{Z}_{N}&=-2N^{2}I_{Q}[\mu_{Q}]+\frac{1}{2}N\log N+\Big(\frac{\log(4\pi)}{2}-1-U_{\mu_{Q}}(0)-\frac{1}{2}E_{Q}[\mu_{Q}]\Big)N\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{2}\log N+\frac{\log(2\pi)}{2}+\frac{1}{2}F_{Q}[\mathbb{A}_{r_{0},r_{1}}]+\frac{1}{8}\log\Big(\frac{\Delta Q(r_{0})}{\Delta Q(r_{1})}\Big)+O(N^{-1}).\end{split}

Using the convention (
1.5
) together with (
2.30
), our result can also be rewritten as

(1.20)

\begin{split}\log\mathcal{Z}_{N}&=-N^{2}I_{Q}[\mu_{Q}]-\frac{1}{2}N\log N+\Big(\frac{\log(2\pi)}{2}-\frac{1}{2}E_{Q}[\mu_{Q}]\Big)\,N+F_{Q}[\mathbb{A}_{r_{0},r_{1}}]+O(N^{-1}).\end{split}

and

(1.21)

\begin{split}\log\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{N}&=-2N^{2}I_{Q}[\mu_{Q}]-\frac{1}{2}N\log N+\Big(\frac{\log(4\pi)}{2}-U_{\mu_{Q}}(0)-\frac{1}{2}E_{Q}[\mu_{Q}]\Big)N\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{2}F_{Q}[\mathbb{A}_{r_{0},r_{1}}]+\frac{1}{8}\log\Big(\frac{\Delta Q(r_{0})}{\Delta Q(r_{1})}\Big)+O(N^{-1}).\end{split}

We mention that these formulas (
1.20
) and (
1.21
) as well as the formulas (
1.26
) and (
1.27
) below are more convenient to compare with some asymptotic results in the previous literature
[
19
,
20
]
.

Notice that the term
\log(r_{1}/r_{0})
is the extremal length of the annulus (
1.13
), see e.g.
[
29
, p.142]
.
It is worth pointing out that a characteristic difference between the expansions (
1.18
) and (
1.19
) is the appearance of the logarithmic potential
U_{\mu_{Q}}(0)
in the
O(N)
-term of (
1.19
).
This additional term can be interpreted as

(1.22)

-\int_{S}\log|w-\bar{w}|\,d\mu_{Q}(w)

after a proper renormalization.
The interpretation (
1.22
) is natural from the perspective of the repulsion term
|z_{j}-\bar{z}_{j}|^{\beta}
in (
1.2
) and is closely related to the notion of the next-order energy, see e.g.
[
43
]
.
(We thank T. Leblé for pointing out this.)

In Subsection
4.1
we present an example of Theorem
1.1
for the Mittag-Leffler ensembles from which we expect that the error terms
O(N^{-1})
are optimal.

For the disc droplet case, we have the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

(Large-
N
expansion of the partition functions: disc droplet case)

Suppose that
r_{0}=0
, i.e. the droplet
S
in (
1.13
) is a disc.
Let

(1.23)

\begin{split}F_{Q}[\mathbb{D}_{r_{1}}]:=\frac{1}{12}\log\Big(\frac{1}{r_{1}^{2}\Delta Q(r_{1})}\Big)-\frac{1}{16}r_{1}\frac{({\partial}_{r}\Delta Q)(r_{1})}{\Delta Q(r_{1})}+\frac{1}{24}\int_{0}^{r_{1}}\Big(\frac{{\partial}_{r}\Delta Q(r)}{\Delta Q(r)}\Big)^{2}\,r\,dr.\end{split}

Then as
N\to\infty,
the following holds.

(i)

(Random normal matrix ensemble)
We have

(1.24)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\log Z_{N}&=-N^{2}I_{Q}[\mu_{Q}]+\frac{1}{2}N\log N+\Big(\frac{\log(2\pi)}{2}-1-\frac{1}{2}E_{Q}[\mu_{Q}]\Big)\,N+\frac{5}{12}\log N\\
&\quad+\frac{\log(2\pi)}{2}+\zeta^{\prime}(-1)+F_{Q}[\mathbb{D}_{r_{1}}]+O(N^{-\frac{1}{12}}(\log N)^{3}).\end{split}

(ii)

(Planar symplectic ensemble)
We have

(1.25)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\log\widetilde{Z}_{N}&=-2N^{2}I_{Q}[\mu_{Q}]+\frac{1}{2}N\log N+\Big(\frac{\log(4\pi)}{2}-1-U_{\mu_{Q}}(0)-\frac{1}{2}E_{Q}[\mu_{Q}]\Big)N+\frac{11}{24}\log N\\
&\quad+\frac{\log(2\pi)}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\zeta^{\prime}(-1)+\frac{1}{2}F_{Q}[\mathbb{D}_{r_{1}}]+\frac{5}{24}\log 2+\frac{1}{8}\log\Big(\frac{\Delta Q(0)}{\Delta Q(r_{1})}\Big)+O(N^{-\frac{1}{12}}(\log N)^{3}).\end{split}

Here
\zeta
is the Riemann zeta function.

Again, using the convention (
1.5
), we have

(1.26)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\log\mathcal{Z}_{N}&=-N^{2}I_{Q}[\mu_{Q}]-\frac{1}{2}N\log N+\Big(\frac{\log(2\pi)}{2}-\frac{1}{2}E_{Q}[\mu_{Q}]\Big)\,N-\frac{1}{12}\log N\\
&\quad+\zeta^{\prime}(-1)+F_{Q}[\mathbb{D}_{r_{1}}]+O(N^{-\frac{1}{12}}(\log N)^{3})\end{split}

and

(1.27)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\log\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{N}&=-2N^{2}I_{Q}[\mu_{Q}]-\frac{1}{2}N\log N+\Big(\frac{\log(4\pi)}{2}-U_{\mu_{Q}}(0)-\frac{1}{2}E_{Q}[\mu_{Q}]\Big)N-\frac{1}{24}\log N\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{2}\zeta^{\prime}(-1)+\frac{1}{2}F_{Q}[\mathbb{D}_{r_{1}}]+\frac{5}{24}\log 2+\frac{1}{8}\log\Big(\frac{\Delta Q(0)}{\Delta Q(r_{1})}\Big)+O(N^{-\frac{1}{12}}(\log N)^{3}).\end{split}

In Subsection
4.2
, we provide an example of Theorem
1.2
for truncated unitary ensembles.
Contrary to Theorem
1.1
, the error terms in Theorem
1.2
do not coincide with the expected optimal orders
O(N^{-1})
.
Our error bounds originate from a decomposition of the analytic expressions of
Z_{N},\widetilde{Z}_{N}
(see Subsection
1.3
), which depends on sufficiently large but seemingly arbitrary number
m_{N}>0.

(Such a decomposition was not necessary for the proof of Theorem
1.1
.)
Later, we choose
m_{N}=N^{1/6}
that gives rise to the control of the total error bounds presented in Theorem
1.2
.
We mention that such error estimates also naturally appeared in similar computations, see e.g.
[
13
,
19
,
20
]
.
Nevertheless, we expect that the estimates can be improved with more effort.

In terms of the function
\chi:=\frac{1}{2}\log\Delta Q
, one can rewrite (
1.23
) as

(1.28)

F_{Q}[\mathbb{D}_{r_{1}}]=\frac{1}{12}\log\Big(\frac{1}{r_{1}^{2}}\Big)-\frac{1}{12\pi}\oint_{\partial S}\kappa\,\chi\,ds-\frac{1}{4}\int_{S}\Delta\chi\,dA+\frac{1}{12}\int_{S}|\nabla\chi|^{2}\,dA,

where
S=\mathbb{D}_{r_{1}}
and
\kappa=1/r_{1}
is the curvature of the boundary, see
[
54
, p.8960]
and (
1.34
).
Here, the third term
\int_{S}\Delta\chi\,dA
on the right-hand side of (
1.28
) is known as a “zero mode” of the loop operator (cf.
[
54
, Eq.(5.26)]
), whereas the fourth term corresponds to the Dirichlet energy of
\chi
.

We end this subsection by giving some crucial remarks on our theorems.

Remark

(
Comparison with Zabrodin-Wiegmann formula
)
.

We compare our formula (
1.24
) with the prediction by Zabrodin and Wiegmann.
For
\beta=2
and a radially symmetric
Q
associated with a disc droplet of radius
r_{1}
, the asymptotic formula (
1.6
) is written in
[
54
, Eqs.(1.2),(C.7)]
as

(1.29)

\begin{split}\log Z_{N}&=F_{0}N^{2}+F_{1/2}N+F_{1}+c(N)+O(N^{-1}),\qquad\Big(c(N):=\log N!-\frac{1}{2}N\log N+\gamma N\Big)\\
&=F_{0}N^{2}+\frac{1}{2}N\log N+\Big(\gamma-1+F_{1/2}\Big)N+\frac{1}{2}\log N+\frac{\log(2\pi)}{2}+F_{1}+O(N^{-1}),\end{split}

where
\gamma
is a “numerical” constant
[
54
, p.8938]
(that is not explicitly presented).
(For reader’s convenience, let us mention that in
[
54
]
, the authors use a different convention for
\beta
so that
\beta=2
in our case corresponds to
\beta=1
in
[
54
]
. Furthermore, the Planck constant
\hbar
in
[
54
]
is identified as
1/N
.)
The coefficients
F_{0},F_{1/2}
and
F_{1}
in (
1.29
) are given by

(1.30)

\displaystyle F_{0}

\displaystyle:=\pi\int_{0}^{r_{1}^{2}}(W_{\textrm{rad}}(x)-W_{\textrm{rad}}^{\prime}(x)x\log x)\sigma_{\textrm{rad}}(x)\,dx,

(1.31)

\displaystyle F_{1/2}

\displaystyle:=-\frac{\pi}{2}\int_{0}^{r_{1}^{2}}\sigma_{\textrm{rad}}(x)\log(\pi\sigma_{\textrm{rad}}(x))\,dx,

(1.32)

\displaystyle F_{1}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{12}\log\Big(\frac{1}{r_{1}^{2}}\Big)-\frac{1}{6}\chi_{\textrm{rad}}(r_{1}^{2})-\frac{1}{4}r_{1}^{2}\chi_{\textrm{rad}}^{\prime}(r_{1}^{2})+\frac{1}{3}\int_{0}^{r_{1}^{2}}x(\chi_{\textrm{rad}}^{\prime}(x))^{2}\,dx,

where

(1.33)

W_{\textrm{rad}}(r):=-Q(\sqrt{r}),\qquad\sigma_{\textrm{rad}}(r):=\frac{1}{\pi}\Delta Q(\sqrt{r}),\qquad\chi_{\textrm{rad}}(r):=\frac{1}{2}\log\Delta Q(\sqrt{r}).

Using (
1.33
), it is straightforward to check that the formulas (
1.30
), (
1.31
) and (
1.32
) can be identified as

(1.34)

F_{0}=-I_{Q}[\mu_{Q}],\qquad F_{1/2}=-\frac{1}{2}E_{Q}[\mu_{Q}],\qquad F_{1}=F_{Q}[\mathbb{D}_{r_{1}}],

where
I_{Q}
,
E_{Q}
and
F_{Q}[\mathbb{D}_{r_{1}}]
are given by (
1.14
), (
1.10
) and (
1.23
).
(Cf. the identification of
F_{0}
follows from the computation (
2.23
) below.)
Then by letting
\gamma=\log(2\pi)/2
, one can deduce from (
1.34
) that the asymptotic formula (
1.29
) agrees with our result (
1.24
) up to the additive terms

(1.35)

-\frac{1}{12}\log N+\zeta^{\prime}(-1).

We remark that the asymptotic expansion of the partition function of the complex Ginibre ensemble was presented in
[
15
,
52
]
, where one can also observe the term
\zeta^{\prime}(-1)
in (
1.35
).
Indeed, the term
\zeta^{\prime}(-1)
and its generalizations have appeared in similar situations in the Hermitian matrix theory, see
[

Remark

(
Weight function with singularities and classical problems in random matrix theory
)
.

In Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, we focus on the weight function

without any kind of singularities.
In contrast, if a specific singularity is allowed for the weight function, the problems of deriving asymptotic expansions of the associated partition function are (when combined with Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
) equivalent to several classical problems in random matrix theory.

To be more concrete, we list various problems in this direction.
If the weight function has a
hard-edge
inside the droplet, the associated partition function provides the large gap (hole) probability, see
[
1
,
2
,
7
,
12
,
19
,
27
,
30
,
35
]
and references therein.
The weight function with a

gives rise to the moment generating function of the disc counting function. It has been extensively studied in recent years
[
4
,
8
,
13
,
17
,
20
,
22
]
.
(We also refer to
[
26
,
40
,
41
,
51
]

for physical motivations of these problems from the counting statistics of rotating free fermions.)
Finally, a
root-type singularity
arises in the study of the log-characteristic polynomials
[
13
,
24
,
53
]
.

We stress that the literature mentioned above is limited mainly to a particular model, such as the Ginibre ensemble, when deriving precise asymptotic results or to the leading order asymptotic when considering general potentials.
We expect that Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
provide the building blocks for obtaining precise asymptotic results on the problems mentioned above with general radially symmetric potentials.

Remark

(
Planar point processes with a general external potential
Q
)
.

For a general potential

beyond a radially symmetric one, the asymptotic behaviors of planar orthogonal polynomials (of sufficiently large degrees) with respect to

On the other hand, for the extension of Theorem

Such a generalization of Theorems

More precisely, in order to obtain an analytic expression of

1.3.
Outline of the proof

In this subsection, we outline the proofs of our main results.
Using the determinantal (resp., Pfaffian) structure and de Bruijn’s type formulas, one can express
Z_{N}
(resp.,
\widetilde{Z}_{N}
) in terms of the (skew-)orthogonal norms.
Consequently, since
Q
is radially symmetric, we find

(1.38)

\log Z_{N}=\log N!+\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\log h_{j},\qquad\log\widetilde{Z}_{N}=\log N!+\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\log(2\widetilde{h}_{2j+1}),

where

(1.39)

h_{j}:=\int_{\mathbb{C}}|z|^{2j}\,e^{-NQ(z)}\,dA(z),\qquad\widetilde{h}_{j}:=\int_{\mathbb{C}}|z|^{2j}\,e^{-2NQ(z)}\,dA(z).

These formulas can be found for instance in
[
20
, Lemma 1.7]
and
[
6
, Remark 2.5]
.
In particular, for planar symplectic ensembles, we have used the explicit construction of skew-orthogonal polynomials associated with radially symmetric potentials, see
[
6
, Corollary 3.3]
.

In order to obtain the large-
N
expansions of partition functions up to the
O(1)
-terms, we need to derive asymptotic behaviors of
h_{j}
and
\widetilde{h}_{j}
up to the first subleading terms, for which we apply Laplace’s method.
For this purpose, let
r_{\tau}
be a unique number
r_{\tau}
such that
r_{\tau}q^{\prime}(r_{\tau})=2\tau
for
0\leq\tau\leq 1
.
Such a function
\tau\mapsto r_{\tau}
plays an important role in Laplace’s method, and we defer more explanations to Subsection
2.1
.
In the asymptotic expansions of
h_{j}
and
\widetilde{h}_{j}
, one should distinguish the following two cases depending on a small constant
\varepsilon>0
.

•

Case 1:
r_{j/N}\gg N^{-\varepsilon}
.
For the annular droplet case where
r_{0}>0
, this case covers all
j=0,1,\dots,N-1
(Lemma
2.1
).
On the other hand, for the disc droplet case where
r_{0}=0
, this case covers only
j=m_{N},m_{N}+1,\dots,N-1
for
m_{N}=N^{\epsilon}
with some
\epsilon>0
(Lemma
3.2
).

•

Case 2:
r_{j/N}\ll N^{-\varepsilon}
.
This covers the remaining disc droplet case with
j=0,1,\dots,m_{N}-1
(Lemma
3.1
). Notably, the asymptotic expansion involves gamma functions in this case.

Furthermore, we apply the Euler-Maclaurin formula (see e.g.
[
45
, Section 2.10]
) to precisely analyze the summations in (
1.38
)

(1.40)

\begin{split}\sum_{j=m}^{n}f(j)=\int_{m}^{n}f(x)\,dx+\frac{f(m)+f(n)}{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{l}\frac{B_{2k}}{(2k)!}\Big(f^{(2k-1)}(n)-f^{(2k-1)}(m)\Big)+R_{l},\end{split}

where
B_{k}
is the Bernoulli number defined by

(1.41)

\frac{t}{e^{t}-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

and
R_{l}
is an error term with the following estimate

|R_{l}|\leq\frac{2\zeta(l)}{(2\pi)^{l}}\int_{m}^{n}|f^{(l)}(x)|\,dx.

Here
\zeta
is the Riemann zeta function.
In particular, for the disc droplet case, in the summation of lower degrees
j=0,1,\dots,m_{N}-1
, we consider the Barnes
G
-function
[
45
, Section 5.17]

G(z+1)=(2\pi)^{z/2}e^{-(z+z^{2}(1+\gamma))/2}\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}\big(1+\frac{z}{n}\big)^{n}e^{-z+z^{2}/(2n)}

and use its asymptotic expansion
[
45
, Eq.(5.17.5)]
:

(1.42)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\log G(z+1)=\frac{z^{2}\log z}{2}-\frac{3}{4}z^{2}+\frac{\log(2\pi)z}{2}-\frac{\log z}{12}+\zeta^{\prime}(-1)+O(\frac{1}{z^{2}}),\qquad(z\to\infty).\end{split}

This asymptotic expansion leads to the appearance of the Riemann zeta function in Theorem
1.2
.

2.1.
Asymptotics of the orthogonal norm

We first introduce an auxiliary function

in

With the following choices of
\tau=\tau(j),\widetilde{\tau}(j)

(2.2)

the integrands in

(

For a radially symmetric potential

in terms of

(2.3)

Differentiating (

(2.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}V_{\tau}^{\prime}(r)=q^{\prime}(r)-\frac{2\tau}{r},\qquad V_{\tau}^{\prime\prime}(r)&=4\Delta Q(r)-\frac{1}{r}V_{\tau}^{\prime}(r),\qquad V_{\tau}^{(3)}(r)=4{\partial}_{r}\Delta Q(r)-\frac{4}{r}\Delta Q(r)+\frac{2}{r^{2}}V_{\tau}^{\prime}(r),\\

We now set the stage to apply Laplace’s method.
Since
rq^{\prime}(r)
is strictly increasing inside the droplet, for
0\leq\tau\leq 1
, there exists a unique number
r(\tau)
such that

(2.5)

V_{\tau}^{\prime}(r(\tau))=0,\qquad V_{\tau}^{\prime\prime}(r(\tau))>0.

Moreover, by (
2.3
) and the relation (
2.5
), it follows that

(2.6)

\frac{dr(\tau)}{d\tau}=\frac{2}{(rq^{\prime}(r))^{\prime}}\Big|_{r=r(\tau)}=\frac{1}{2r(\tau)\Delta Q(r(\tau))}>0.

Thus
r(\tau)
is an increasing function of
\tau
.
On the other hand,
r(1)=r_{1},r(0)=r_{0}
, where
r_{0},r_{1}
are given in (
1.13
). Therefore, we denote
r_{\tau}=r(\tau)
making the notation consistent with (
1.13
).
We also mention here that

corresponds to the outer radius of the so-called “
\tau
-droplet”
[
34
]
.
By (
2.4
) and (
2.5
),

satisfies

(2.7)

r_{\tau}q^{\prime}(r_{\tau})=2\tau.

In particular,
q^{\prime}(r_{0})=0
and
r_{1}q^{\prime}(r_{1})=2.

Let

(2.8)

\mathfrak{B}_{1}(r):=-\frac{1}{32}\frac{{\partial}_{r}^{2}\Delta Q(r)}{(\Delta Q(r))^{2}}-\frac{19}{96r}\frac{{\partial}_{r}\Delta Q(r)}{(\Delta Q(r))^{2}}+\frac{5}{96}\frac{({\partial}_{r}\Delta Q(r))^{2}}{\Delta Q(r)^{3}}+\frac{1}{12r^{2}}\frac{1}{\Delta Q(r)}.

Here, the subscript

Lemma 2.1
.

•

\widetilde{h}_{j}=(2N)^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-2NV_{\widetilde{\tau}(j)}(r_{\widetilde{\tau}(j)})}\Big(\frac{{2\pi}r_{\widetilde{\tau}(j)}^{2}}{\Delta Q(r_{\widetilde{\tau}(j)})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(1+\frac{1}{2N}\mathfrak{B}_{1}(r_{\widetilde{\tau}(j)})+O(N^{-2})\Big).

Proof.

It suffices to show the first assertion as the second one follows by replacing

with

Write

As seen in (

), (

for all

Thus we deduce from the estimate

V_{\tau(j+\frac{1}{2})}(r_{\tau(j+\frac{1}{2})})-V_{\tau(j)}(r_{\tau(j)})=O(N^{-1})

that there exists a positive number

such that for all

\displaystyle V_{\tau(j+\frac{1}{2})}(r)-V_{\tau(j)}(r_{\tau(j)})\geq c\,\delta_{N}^{2}.

Lemma 2.3

Here, we also used the second Bernoulli number

For the first term on the right-hand side of (

By (

=-\log r_{1}+\frac{1}{4}\int_{r_{0}}^{r_{1}}s(q^{\prime}(s))^{2}\,ds.

Applying the method of integration by parts again to the last integral,

\frac{1}{4}\int_{r_{0}}^{r_{1}}s(q^{\prime}(s))^{2}\,ds=\frac{1}{2}q(r_{1})-\frac{1}{4}\int_{r_{0}}^{r_{1}}q(s)(sq^{\prime}(s))^{\prime}\,ds.

) of

) of

in terms of

)

for

(2.23)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{N}\int_{0}^{N}V_{\tau(t)}(r_{\tau(t)})\,dt&=\int_{S}Q\cdot\Delta Q\,dA-\log r_{1}+\frac{1}{2}q(r_{1})-\frac{1}{4}\int_{r_{0}}^{r_{1}}q(s)(sq^{\prime}(s))^{\prime}\,ds\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{S}Q\cdot\Delta Q\,dA-\log r_{1}+\frac{1}{2}q(r_{1})=I_{Q}[\mu_{Q}].\end{split}

For the next term on the right-hand side of (

), we observe that

(2.24)

V_{\tau(N)}(r_{\tau(N)})-V_{\tau(0)}(r_{\tau(0)})=V_{1}(r_{1})-V_{0}(r_{0})=q(r_{1})-q(r_{0})-2\log r_{1}=2U_{\mu_{Q}}(0),

where we have used (
1.16

To analyze the remaining term, we use the Leibniz rule and obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\partial}_{t}V_{\tau(t)}(r_{\tau(t)})\big|_{t=N}&=\frac{1}{N}\big[{\partial}_{\tau}(q(r_{\tau})-2\log r_{\tau})\big|_{\tau=1}-2\log r_{1}\big],\\
{\partial}_{t}V_{\tau(t)}(r_{\tau(t)})\big|_{t=0}&=\frac{1}{N}\big[{\partial}_{\tau}q(r_{\tau})\big|_{\tau=0}-2\log r_{0}\big].\end{split}

It follows from

,
r_{0}q^{\prime}(r_{0})=0
and the formula (

) of
dr/d\tau

(2.25)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad{\partial}_{t}V_{\tau(t)}(r_{\tau(t)})\big|_{t=N}-{\partial}_{t}V_{\tau(t)}(r_{\tau(t)})\big|_{t=0}\\
&=\frac{1}{N}\Big[\frac{q^{\prime}(r_{1})}{2r_{1}\Delta Q(r_{1})}-2\log r_{1}-\frac{1}{r_{1}^{2}\Delta Q(r_{1})}-\frac{q^{\prime}(r_{0})}{2r_{0}\Delta Q(r_{0})}+2\log r_{0}\Big]=\frac{2}{N}\log\Big(\frac{r_{0}}{r_{1}}\Big).\end{split}

.
∎

4.
Examples: Mittag-Leffler ensemble and truncated unitary ensemble

This section presents examples of our Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
for some well-known planar point processes.

4.1.
Mittag-Leffler ensemble

Let us consider the potential

(4.1)

Q(z)=|z|^{2\lambda}-2c\log|z|,\quad c>0.

The models (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) associated with the potential (
4.1
) are known as the Mittag-Leffler ensemble
[
9
]
.
We refer to
[
13
,
19
,
22
]
and
[
5
]
for recent studies on complex and symplectic Mittag-Leffler ensembles, respectively.
Using (
1.13
), we have

(4.2)

r_{0}=\Big(\frac{c}{\lambda}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2\lambda}},\qquad r_{1}=\Big(\frac{1+c}{\lambda}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2\lambda}},\qquad\Delta Q(z)=\lambda^{2}|z|^{2\lambda-2}.

In particular, by (
4.2
), the Mittag-Leffler ensemble (
4.1
) falls into the class considered in Theorem
1.1
.

By direct computations using (
1.10
), (
1.14
) and (
1.16
), we have

(4.3)

\displaystyle I_{Q}[\mu_{Q}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\lambda}\log\Big(\frac{c^{c^{2}}}{(1+c)^{(1+c)^{2}}}\Big)+\frac{1+2c}{2\lambda}\Big(\log\lambda+\frac{3}{2}\Big),

(4.4)

\displaystyle E_{Q}[\mu_{Q}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1+\lambda}{\lambda}\log\lambda+\frac{1-\lambda}{\lambda}\Big(1+\log\Big(\frac{c^{c}}{(1+c)^{1+c}}\Big)\Big).

It also follows from (
1.17
) and

(4.5)

r\frac{(\partial_{r}\Delta)Q(r)}{\Delta Q(r)}=2\lambda-2,\qquad r^{2}\Delta Q(r)=\lambda^{2}r^{2\lambda}

that

(4.6)

F_{Q}[\mathbb{A}_{r_{0},r_{1}}]=\Big(\frac{\lambda}{6}-\frac{(\lambda-1)^{2}}{6}\Big)\log\Big(\frac{r_{0}}{r_{1}}\Big)=-\frac{\lambda^{2}-3\lambda+1}{12\lambda}\log\Big(\frac{c}{1+c}\Big).

On the other hand, by using (
1.38
),

(4.7)

h_{j}=2\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{2j+1+2cN}e^{-Nr^{2\lambda}}\,dr=\frac{1}{\lambda}N^{-\frac{j+1+cN}{\lambda}}\Gamma\Big(\frac{j+Nc+1}{\lambda}\Big),

and the analogous formula for
\widetilde{h}_{j}
, we have

(4.8)

Z_{N}=\frac{N!}{\lambda^{N}}N^{-\frac{(2c+1)N^{2}+N}{2\lambda}}\prod_{j=0}^{N-1}\Gamma\Big(\frac{j+Nc+1}{\lambda}\Big),\qquad\widetilde{Z}_{N}=\frac{N!}{(\lambda/2)^{N}}\,(2N)^{-\frac{(2c+1)N^{2}+N}{\lambda}}\prod_{j=0}^{N-1}\Gamma\Big(\frac{j+Nc+1}{\lambda/2}\Big).

Furthermore, using the multiplication theorem of gamma function (
[
45
, Eq. (5.5.6)]
)

(4.9)

\Gamma(nz)=(2\pi)^{\frac{1-n}{2}}n^{nz-\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}\Gamma(z+\tfrac{k}{n})

and the characteristic property (
3.10
) of the Barnes
G
-function, we have that for
\frac{1}{\lambda}\in\mathbb{N}
,

(4.10)

Z_{N}=N!\,N^{-\frac{(2c+1)N^{2}+N}{2\lambda}}(2\pi)^{(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2\lambda})N}\Big(\frac{1}{\lambda}\Big)^{(\frac{1}{2\lambda}+\frac{c}{\lambda})N^{2}+(\frac{1}{\lambda}+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2\lambda})N}\prod_{k=0}^{\frac{1}{\lambda}-1}\frac{G(N+Nc+1+\lambda k)}{G(Nc+1+\lambda k)}.

Similarly, for
\frac{2}{\lambda}\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(4.11)

\widetilde{Z}_{N}=N!\,(2N)^{-\frac{(2c+1)N^{2}+N}{\lambda}}(2\pi)^{(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\lambda})N}\Big(\frac{2}{\lambda}\Big)^{(\frac{1}{\lambda}+\frac{2c}{\lambda})N^{2}+(\frac{2}{\lambda}+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\lambda})N}\prod_{k=0}^{\frac{2}{\lambda}-1}\frac{G(N+Nc+1+\frac{\lambda}{2}k)}{G(Nc+1+\frac{\lambda}{2}k)}.

Then by using (
1.42
), one can directly check that the partition functions (
4.10
) and (
4.11
) satisfy the expansions (
1.18
) and (
1.19
) with (
4.3
), (
4.4
) and (
4.6
).

4.2.
Truncated unitary ensemble

We now consider the potential

(4.12)

Q(z)=\begin{cases}-\alpha\,\log\Big(1-\dfrac{|z|^{2}}{R^{2}(1+\alpha)}\Big)&\textup{if }|z|\leq R\sqrt{1+\alpha},\\
\infty&\textup{otherwise},\end{cases}\qquad\alpha,R>0.

The models associated with (
4.12
) correspond to the truncated unitary ensembles at strong non-unitarity
[
38
,
55
]
.
These models provide a one-parameter generalization of the Ginibre ensembles that can be recovered in the extremal case, i.e.
\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}Q(z)=|z|^{2}/R^{2}
.
(See
[
25
,
48
]
and references therein for recent works on these models.)
In this case, we have

(4.13)

r_{0}=0,\qquad r_{1}=R,\qquad\Delta Q(z)=\frac{R^{2}\,\alpha(1+\alpha)}{(R^{2}(1+\alpha)-|z|^{2})^{2}}.

From (
4.13
), we see that the truncated unitary ensembles are contained in the class covered in Theorem
1.2
.
(The hard edge condition imposed in (
4.12
) outside the droplet does not harm the proof of Theorem
1.2
.)

It is easy to verify from (
1.10
), (
1.14
) and (
1.16
) that

(4.14)

\displaystyle I_{Q}[\mu_{Q}]

\displaystyle=-\frac{\alpha}{2}-\frac{\alpha(2+\alpha)}{2}\log\Big(\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}\Big)-\log R,

(4.15)

\displaystyle E_{Q}[\mu_{Q}]

\displaystyle=-2-(1+2\alpha)\log\Big(\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}\Big)-2\log R.

Since

(4.16)

r\frac{(\partial_{r}\Delta)Q(r)}{\Delta Q(r)}\Big|_{r=R}=\frac{4r^{2}}{R^{2}(1+\alpha)-r^{2}}\Big|_{r=R}=\frac{4}{\alpha},\qquad R^{2}\Delta Q(R)=\frac{1+\alpha}{\alpha},

we deduce from (
1.23
) that

(4.17)

F_{Q}[\mathbb{D}_{R}]=\frac{1}{12}\log\Big(\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}\Big)-\frac{1}{4\alpha}+\frac{1}{3}\Big(\frac{1}{\alpha}+\log\Big(\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}\Big)\Big)=\frac{1}{12}\Big(\frac{1}{\alpha}+5\log\Big(\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}\Big)\Big).

Notice here that
F_{Q}[\mathbb{D}_{R}]
is independent of
R
, which is consistent with the invariance of the
O(1)
-terms of (
1.24
) and (
1.25
) under the dilation, see the remark below Theorem
1.2
.

Using the Euler’s beta integral

(4.18)

\frac{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)}{\Gamma(a+b)}=\int_{0}^{1}t^{a-1}(1-t)^{b-1}\,dt,\qquad(a,b>0),

the orthogonal norm
h_{j}
is computed as

(4.19)

h_{j}=\int_{0}^{R\sqrt{1+\alpha}}r^{2j+1}\Big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{R^{2}(1+\alpha)}\Big)^{\alpha N}\,dr=R^{2j+2}(1+\alpha)^{j+1}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha N+1)\Gamma(j+1)}{\Gamma(\alpha N+j+2)}.

Then by (
1.38
) and (
3.10
), we have

(4.20)

Z_{N}=N!\,R^{N(N+1)}(1+\alpha)^{\frac{N^{2}}{2}+\frac{N}{2}}\,\Gamma(\alpha N+1)^{N}\,\frac{G(N+1)G(\alpha N+2)}{G(\alpha N+N+2)}.

Similarly, by (
1.38
) and the duplication formula of the gamma function (i.e. (
4.9
) with
n=2
),

(4.21)

\begin{split}\widetilde{Z}_{N}&=N!\,R^{2N(N+1)}2^{-2\alpha N^{2}}\,(1+\alpha)^{N^{2}+N}\,\Gamma(2\alpha N+1)^{N}\\
&\quad\times G(N+1)\frac{G(N+\frac{3}{2})}{G(\frac{3}{2})}\frac{G(\alpha N+2)}{G(\alpha N+N+2)}\frac{G(\alpha N+\frac{3}{2})}{G(\alpha N+N+\frac{3}{2})}.\end{split}

Then by using (
1.42
), it is again straightforward to check that the partition functions (
4.20
) and (
4.21
) satisfy the expansions (
1.24
) and (
1.25
) with (
4.14
), (
4.15
) and (
4.17
).
In the extremal case where
\alpha\to\infty
, the expansion of the partition function
Z_{N}
of the complex Ginibre ensemble appears in
[
52
]
.

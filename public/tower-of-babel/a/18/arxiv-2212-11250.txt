Counting compatible indexing systems for C p n C_{p^{n}} Thanks: This material is based upon work supported by the National Science Foundation under Grant No. 2105019
Michael A. Hill, Jiayun Meng, Nan Li

Abstract
. We count the number of compatible pairs of indexing systems for the cyclic group C p n C_{p^{n}} . Building on work of Balchin–Barnes–Roitzheim, we show that this sequence of natural numbers is another family of Fuss–Catalan numbers. We count this two different ways: showing how the conditions of compatibility give natural recursive formulas for the number of admissible sets and using an enumeration of ways to extend indexing systems by conceptually simpler pieces.



1.
Introduction

Recent work in equivariant algebra has studied the beautiful variety of different multiplicative structures that can arise when we mix in the action of a finite group. Blumberg–Hill showed that this is a fundamentally combinatorial structure
[
2
]
. The various multiplicative norms or additive transfers are entirely governed by certain particularly well-behaved subcategories of finite
G
-sets.

This classification was reformulated in independent work of Balchin–Barnes–Roitzheim and of Rubin, where they further underscored the combinatorial structure by showing the norms and transfers are encoded in “transfer systems”, certain refinements of the poset of subgroups of
G
under inclusion
[
1
]

[
8
]
.

Definition 1.1

(
[
8
, Definition 3.4]
,
[
1
, Definition 7]
)
.

A transfer system for a finite group
G
is a partial order
\to
relation on the set
\Sub(G)
of subsets of
G
such that

(1)

if
K\to H
, then
K\leq H
(so this is a “weak subposet”),

(2)

if
K\to H
and
g\in G
, then
gKg^{-1}\to gHg^{-1}
, and

(3)

if
K\to H
and
J\leq H
, then
K\cap J\to J
.

Let
\Tran(G)
denote the set of transfer systems for
G
.

The set of all transfer systems for
G
itself has a partial order: we say
\mathcal{O}\leq\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
if the identity map is order-preserving.

Example 1.2
.

For
G=C_{p^{n}}
, the subgroup lattice is order isomorphic to the linear order

\{1\leq 2\dots\leq n+1\}=[n+1].

The conjugation condition is always satisfied, and the restriction condition here can be rephrased as saying that if
C_{p^{i}}\to C_{p^{j}}
and
i\leq k\leq j
, then
C_{p^{i}}\to C_{p^{k}}
.

Balchin–Barnes–Roitzheim showed that the poset of transfer systems for
C_{p^{n}}
is order isomorphic to the Tamari lattice
[
1
, Theorem 25]
.

In this paper, we will focus on the groups
C_{p^{n}}
. Example
1.2
stresses that we could equivalently look at “transfer systems for the poset
[n+1]
” in the sense of Franchere–Ormsby–Osorno–Qin–Waugh.

Definition 1.3

(
[
6
, Definition 4.1]
)
.

A transfer system on

[n]=\{1\leq\dots\leq n\}

is a weak sub-poset of
[n]
with partial order
\to
that contains all the elements and which satsifies the “restriction condition”:
if
i\to j
and
i\leq k\leq j

i\to k
.

It is helpful to view these as a graded set in which we allow
n
to vary, as this will help encode certain natural operations.

Definition 1.4
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let

T_{n}=\begin{cases}\Tran([n])&n\geq 1\\
\{\emptyset\}&n=0.\end{cases}

Balchin–Barnes–Roizheim counted the number of transfer systems for
C_{p^{n}}
:

Theorem 1.5

(
[
1

)
.

For
n
, we have

|T_{n}|=\frac{1}{2n+1}\binom{2n+1}{n}=\operatorname{Cat}(n).

The numbers “
\operatorname{Cat}(n)
” are the Catalan numbers. These are ubiquitous in combinatorics, parameterizing structructures from binary rooted trees to Dyck paths. This sequence fits into a bivariant family of sequences.

Definition 1.6
.

The
Fuss–Catalan numbers
are defined by

A_{n}(p,r)=\frac{r}{np+r}\binom{np+r}{n}

for non-negative
n
and positive
p
and

The Catalan numbers arise here:

\operatorname{Cat}(n)=A_{n}(2,1).

In this paper, we show the next term of the sequence is also related to equivariant algebra.

Blumberg–Hill studied what kind of compatibility conditions arise if we allow both the additive transfers and multiplicative norms to each be structured by various transfer systems
[
3
]
. As a slogan, “the presence of certain multiplicative norms forces some additive transfers”. These conditions were simplified by Chan who gave a definition internal to transfer systems
[
4
]
. When the conditions are satisfied, we say that
(\mathcal{O}_{a},\mathcal{O}_{m})
is compatible. Counting these for
C_{p^{n}}
is the main result of this paper.

Theorem 1.7
.

For
C_{p^{n-1}}
, there are

A_{n}(3,1)

compatible pairs of transfer systems.

We present two proofs of this theorem in Sections
4
and
5

The two junior coauthors each came up with a distinct solution to this counting problem, so we include here both of their solutions. A third solution by Henry Ma will appear separately.

Operations on Transfer Systems

2.1.
Concatination

A key piece of structure on transfer systems for
[n]
is the ability to “concatinate” a transfer system for
[k]
and one for
[n-k]
to build one for
[n]
. This makes computations with the entire graded set easier to understand.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{O}_{L}
be a transfer system for
[k]
and
\mathcal{O}_{R}
be a transfer system for
[n-k]
. Then we define a relation
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}_{L}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{R}
on
[n]
by saying
i\xrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}j
if and only if

(1)

j\leq k
and
i\xrightarrow{\mathcal{O}_{L}}j
or

(2)

i\geq(k+1)
and
(i-k)\xrightarrow{\mathcal{O}_{R}}(j-k)
.

We call
\mathcal{O}
the
direct sum
or
concatination
of
\mathcal{O}_{L}
and
\mathcal{O}_{R}
.

Remark 2.2
.

As a poset, this is just the disjoint union of the two posets
\mathcal{O}_{L}
and
\mathcal{O}_{R}
. The additional data is the map to
[n]
in this case.

Proposition 2.3
.

The concatination of transfer systems is a transfer system.

Proof.

The definition of
\mathcal{O}
is that of the disjoint union of posets, so
\mathcal{O}
is a poset, and it visibly maps to the usual inclusions.

We need only check the restriction condition, and here, it suffices to check the restriction of

along
m\leq j+k
for some
m\leq k
. In this case, both the source and target are
m
.
∎

The direct sum is a graded operation here:

T_{n}\times T_{m}\xrightarrow{\oplus}T_{n+m}.

We extend this to
T_{0}
by declaring

\emptyset\oplus\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}\oplus\emptyset=\mathcal{O}

for any transfer system
\mathcal{O}
or the empty set.

2.2.
Restriction

Given a transfer system for
[n]
, we have two natural ways to build transfer systems for smaller natural numbers. These both arise from the inclusion of subposets.

Definition 2.4
.

If
k\leq n
, then let

\iota\colon[k]\to[n]

be the map sending
i\to i
, and let

\phi\colon[n-k]\to[n]

be the map sending
i
to
i+k
.

We can restrict a transfer system on
[n]
along either of these inclusions.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a transfer system for
[n]
, and let
k\leq n
.

Let
i_{{k}}^{\ast}\mathcal{O}
be defined by saying for all
i\leq j\leq k
,

i\xrightarrow{i_{{k}}^{\ast}\mathcal{O}}j\text{ if and only if }i\xrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}j.

Let
\Phi^{k}\mathcal{O}
be defined by saying for all
i\leq j\leq(n-k)
,

i\xrightarrow{\Phi^{k}\mathcal{O}}j\text{ if and only if }{(i+k)}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}{(j+k)}.

Proposition 2.6
.

For a transfer system on
\mathcal{O}
,
i_{k}^{\ast}\mathcal{O}
is a transfer system on
[k]
and
\Phi^{k}\mathcal{O}
is a transfer system on
[n-k]
.

Proof.

The construction gives wide, subposets of
[k]
and
[n-k]
respectively, by observation. Since the two inclusions
\iota
and
\phi
are interval inclusions, the restriction condition is easily checked.
∎

2.3.
Wrapped and saturated systems

We single out two families of transfer systems that are useful for our counts.

Definition 2.8
.

A transfer system
\mathcal{O}
for
[n]
is
wrapped
if
1\xrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}n
.

Example 2.9
.

The transfer systems
\mathcal{O}_{L}
and
\mathcal{O}_{R}
from Figure

are wrapped; the transfer system
\mathcal{O}_{L}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{R}
is not.

Example 2.10
.

The transfer system
\mathcal{O}
in Figure

i_{4}^{\ast}\mathcal{O}
. The transfer system
\Phi^{4}\mathcal{O}
is not.

By the restriction condition, in a wrapped transfer system, we have
1\to j
for all
j
, and we think of the largest transfer as “wrapping” the rest of the transfer system. Additionally, we can always “wrap” any given transfer system, using the circle-dot product of
[
1
]
. We use a single case of their construction here.

Definition 2.11
.

If
\mathcal{O}\in T_{n}
, let
w(\mathcal{O})
be the transfer system for
[n+1]
with

(1)

for all
j
,
1\to j
in
w(\mathcal{O})
, and

(2)

\Phi^{1}\big(w(\mathcal{O})\big)=\mathcal{O}
.

This is implicit in Balchin–Barnes–Roitzheim’s “circle-dot product” and their decomposition theorem.

Theorem 2.12

(
[
1
, Corollary 21]
)
.

Any transfer system for
[n]
with
n\geq 1
decomposes uniquely as

\mathcal{O}\odot\mathcal{O}^{\prime}=\mathcal{O}\oplus w(\mathcal{O}^{\prime}),

for some transfer systems
\mathcal{O}\in T_{m}
and
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\in T_{n-m-1}
.

Corollary 2.13
.

Any transfer system
\mathcal{O}
for
[n]
can be written uniquely as

\mathcal{O}=w(\mathcal{O}_{1})\oplus\dots\oplus w(\mathcal{O}_{k})

for some transfer systems
\mathcal{O}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{O}_{k}
.

A special case of wrapped transfer systems is given by complete ones.

Definition 2.14
.

The
complete
transfer system for
[n]
is the one for which
i\to j
for all
i\leq j
. Denote it
\mathcal{O}^{cpt}_{n}
.

Remark 2.15
.

The complete transfer system for
[n]
is one for which the partial order is just the inclusion. This means that complete transfer systems are maximal elements in the poset of transfer systems.

These complete transfer systems will be especially useful building blocks for us.

Definition 2.16
.

A transfer system
\mathcal{O}
is
saturated
if it can be written as a direct sum of complete transfer systems.

Given any transfer system on
[n]
, there is a minimal saturated transfer system that contains it. This is immediate from the observation that the intersection of two saturated transfer systems is again a saturated transfer system. For concreteness, we spell this out directly here.

Proposition 2.17
.

If
\mathcal{O}
is any transfer system, then there is a minimal saturated transfer system that contains
\mathcal{O}
.

Proof.

Write
\mathcal{O}
as a direct sum of wrapped transfer systems
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}_{n_{1}}\oplus\dots\mathcal{O}_{n_{r}}
, with
\mathcal{O}_{n_{i}}\in T_{n_{i}}
. Then minimal saturated transfer system containing it is simply

\mathcal{O}_{n_{1}}^{cpt}\oplus\dots\mathcal{O}^{cpt}_{n_{r}},

where we replace each summand with the complete one of that size.
∎

Definition 2.18
.

If
\mathcal{O}
is a transfer system for
[n]
, let
\hull(\mathcal{O})
denote the minimal saturated transfer system that contains it. This is the
saturated hull
.

For transfer systems for
[n]
, there is a kind of dual notion of a maximal saturated transfer systems inside any given transfer system.

Complete transfer systems have two useful properties:

(1)

Whenever
i\to j
, we also have
k\to j
for all
i\leq k\leq j
, and

(2)

they are generated as a poset as the transitive closure of the relation
i\to{i+1}
for all
1\leq i\leq n-1
.

These two properties give us two different ways to repackage the condition of being saturated.

Proposition 2.19
.

A transfer system
\mathcal{O}
for
n
is saturated if and only if whenever
i\to j
with
i<j
, we have
{{(j-1)}}\to{j}
.

Proof.

If
\mathcal{O}
is saturated, then by definition of the direct sum, if
i\to j
, then
i
and
j
correspond to subgroups from the same direct summand. By completeness, we therefore have all intermediate transfers.

Using the decomposition of a transfer system into wrapped ones (Corollary
2.13
), we see that it suffices to show that if a wrapped transfer system that has the property that
i\to j
implies
(j-1)\to j
, then the transfer system is complete. This follows from downward induction on
n
, using that
1\to n
by the wrapped assumption, and hence
1\to j
for all
j
by restriction.
∎

Corollary 2.20
.

A transfer system
\mathcal{O}
is saturated if and only if it is generated as a partial order by relations
i\to{{(i+1)}}
for some collection of positive
i
at most
n-1
.

The possibly surprising part here is that we only need the partial order: the other parts of being a transfer system come along for free in this case, since we are generating by a covering condition. This gives us a second kind of structural result.

Definition 2.21
.

If
\mathcal{O}
is a transfer system let the
core
of
\mathcal{O}
, denoted
\core{\mathcal{O}}
, be the partial order generated by
{i}\to{{(i+1)}}
, where
i
ranges over the integers from
1
to
n-1
such that
i\to(i+1)
.

3.
Compatible pairs

Our main object of study is the notion of compatible pairs. These were defined by Blumberg–Hill to describe “compatibility” between equivariant norms and transfers in an abstract, categorical way
[
3
]
. Chan reformulated this in the language of transfer system, giving a purely combinatorial formulation
[
4
, Theorem 4.10]
. We use that here for the special case of
C_{p^{n}}
.

Definition 3.1

(
[
4
, Definition 4.6]
)
.

A pair of transfer system
(\mathcal{O}_{a},\mathcal{O}_{m})
for
[n]
are compatible if for whenever
i\xrightarrow{m}j
, we have
k\xrightarrow{a}j
for all
i\leq k\leq j
.

Note here that the conditions are asymmetrical: arrows in
\mathcal{O}_{m}
force those in
\mathcal{O}_{a}
. Moreover, this is a kind of relative saturation condition, with an arrow in
\mathcal{O}_{m}
actually forcing
\mathcal{O}_{a}
to look saturated in a range. This gives us two equivalent forms.

Proposition 3.2
.

A pair
(\mathcal{O}_{a},\mathcal{O}_{m})
is compatible if and only if the following equivalent comparisons hold

(1)

\mathcal{O}_{m}\leq\core(\mathcal{O}_{a})
,

(2)

\hull(\mathcal{O}_{m})\leq\mathcal{O}_{a}
, and

(3)

\hull(\mathcal{O}_{m})\leq\core(\mathcal{O}_{a})
.

Proof.

The conditions of Definition
3.1
are a restatement of the condition that the saturated hull of
\mathcal{O}_{m}
is less than or equal to
\mathcal{O}_{a}
. For the equivalence of the three conditions, we use that the core of
\mathcal{O}
is the largest saturated transfer system less than or equal to
\mathcal{O}
and the hull is the smallest saturated transfer system greater than or equal to
\mathcal{O}
.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
(\mathcal{O}_{a},\mathcal{O}_{m})
be a compatible pair of transfer systems for
[n]
. If for some
1\leq k\leq n-1
, we have
k\not\xrightarrow{a}(k+1)
, then for all
j\leq k
and
\ell\geq(k+1)
, we must have
j\not\xrightarrow{m}\ell
.

Put another way, we see that
\mathcal{O}_{m}
must break apart at
k
, and this must be compatible with
\mathcal{O}_{a}
.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
(\mathcal{O}_{a},\mathcal{O}_{m})
be a compatible pair of transfer systems for
[n]
. If for some
1\leq k\leq(n-1)
, we have
k\not\xrightarrow{a}(k+1)
, then

(1)

\mathcal{O}_{m}=i_{k}^{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{m}\oplus\Phi^{k}\mathcal{O}_{m}
, and

(2)

the pairs

(i_{k}^{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{a},i_{k}^{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{m})\text{ and }(\Phi^{k}\mathcal{O}_{a},\Phi^{k}\mathcal{O}_{m})

are compatible.

Definition 3.5
.

Let

\mathcal{D}^{(0)}_{n}=\big\{(\mathcal{O}_{a},\mathcal{O}_{m})\mid\text{compatible}\big\}\subset T_{n}\times T_{n}.

We have projection maps

p_{a},p_{m}\colon\mathcal{D}^{(0)}_{n}\to T_{n}

which take a pair
(\mathcal{O}_{a},\mathcal{O}_{m})
to
\mathcal{O}_{a}
or
\mathcal{O}_{m}
respectively. Our main goal is to find the cardinality of
\mathcal{D}^{(0)}_{n}
for all
n
. We solve this in several different ways using different aspects of Proposition
3.2
.

4.
Solving the recurrence relations

4.1.
Decomposition and Recurrence Relation

The wrapping map
w
defines a natural filtration on the collection of transfer systems. We can use this to build a recursive relation describing compatible pairs.

Definition 4.1
.

For each
i\geq 0
, let

(\mathcal{F}^{i}T)_{n}=Im(w^{\circ i})\subset T_{n},

viewed as a graded subset.

Definition 4.2
.

Let

\mathcal{D}^{(i)}_{n}=p_{a}^{-1}\big((\mathcal{F}^{i}T)_{n}\big)

be the set of composable pairs with
\mathcal{O}_{a}\in\mathcal{F}^{i}T
.

Let
d(n,i)=|\mathcal{D}^{(i)}_{n}|
be the corresponding cardinality.

We deduce our recursive formulae from the Balchin–Barnes–Roitzheim decomposition theorem (Theorem
2.12
). We restate the result here to set up our decomposition.

Proposition 4.3
.

If
\mathcal{O}
is a transfer system in
(\mathcal{F}^{i}T)_{n}
, then there is

(1)

a unique natural number
1\leq j\leq n-i
,

(2)

a unique wrapped transfer system
w\mathcal{O}_{R}
in
T_{j}
,

(3)

and a unique transfer system
\mathcal{O}_{L}
in
T_{n-i-j}

such that

\mathcal{O}=w^{i}\big(\mathcal{O}_{L}\oplus w\mathcal{O}_{R}\big).

Notation 4.4
.

Let
(\mathcal{F}^{i}T)_{n,j}
be the set of transfer systems in
(\mathcal{F}^{i}T)_{n}
which decompose as

\mathcal{O}=w^{i}\big(\mathcal{O}_{L}\oplus w\mathcal{O}_{R}\big)

with
w\mathcal{O}_{R}\in T_{j}
a wrapped transfer system.

Proposition 4.5
.

The map

\left(\coprod_{j=1}^{n-i-1}\mathcal{D}^{(i)}_{n-j}\times\mathcal{D}^{(1)}_{j}\right)\amalg\mathcal{D}^{(i+1)}_{n}\to\mathcal{D}^{(i)}_{n}

given on
\mathcal{D}^{(i)}_{n-j}\times\mathcal{D}^{(1)}_{j}
by

\big((w^{i}\mathcal{O}_{a}^{\prime},\mathcal{O}_{m}^{\prime}),(w\mathcal{O}_{a}^{\prime\prime},\mathcal{O}_{m}^{\prime\prime})\big)\mapsto\big(w^{i}(\mathcal{O}_{a}^{\prime}\oplus w\mathcal{O}_{a}^{\prime\prime}),\mathcal{O}_{m}^{\prime}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{m}^{\prime\prime}\big)

and on the last summand by the natural inclusion, is a bijection.

Proof.

We use Proposition
4.3
to further break up
\mathcal{D}^{(i)}_{n}
, since the decomposition here gives a disjoint union decomposition

(\mathcal{F}^{i}T)_{n}=\coprod_{j=1}^{n-i}(\mathcal{F}^{i}T)_{n,j}.

This decomposition induces a decomposition of
\mathcal{D}^{(i)}_{n}
:

\mathcal{D}^{(i)}_{n,j}=p_{1}^{-1}(\mathcal{F}^{i}T)_{n,j}.

Since
T_{0}=\{\emptyset\}
, the unit for
\oplus
, we have

\mathcal{D}^{(i)}_{n,n-i}=\mathcal{D}^{(i+1)}_{n},

given by the usual inclusion. Now let
1\leq j<n-i
, and consider an element
(\mathcal{O}_{a},\mathcal{O}_{m})
in
\mathcal{D}^{(i)}_{n,j}
. By definition, we have

\mathcal{O}_{a}=w^{i}\big(\mathcal{O}_{a,L}\oplus w\mathcal{O}_{a,R}\big),

with
\mathcal{O}_{a,L}\neq\emptyset
and
w\mathcal{O}_{a,R}
wrapped, and hence we are missing the transfer

(i+n-j)\to(i+n-j+1)

in
\mathcal{O}_{a}
. This means that
\mathcal{O}_{m}
breaks up into a direct sum

\mathcal{O}_{m}^{\prime}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{m}^{\prime\prime},

where
\mathcal{O}_{m}^{\prime}\in T_{i+n-j}
and
\mathcal{O}_{m}^{\prime\prime}\in T_{j}
, by Corollary
3.4
. Moreover, we know that the pairs

\big(i_{i+n-j}^{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{a},\mathcal{O}_{m}^{\prime}\big)\text{ and }\big(\Phi^{i+n-j}\mathcal{O}_{a},\mathcal{O}_{m}^{\prime\prime}\big)

are compatible. The result follows, since

i_{i+n-j}^{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{a}=w^{i}\mathcal{O}_{a,L}\text{ and }\Phi^{i+n-j}\mathcal{O}_{a}=w\mathcal{O}_{a,R}.

∎

Corollary 4.6
.

We have a recursive formula

d(n,i)=d(n,i+1)+\sum_{j=1}^{n-i-1}d(n-j,i)d(j,1).

The base case here is actually
\mathcal{D}^{(n)}_{n}
, which is
p_{a}^{-1}(\mathcal{O}_{n}^{cpt})
. Every transfer system is compatible with the additive one. Note also that we have an important edge case:

(\mathcal{F}^{n-1}T)_{n}=(\mathcal{F}^{n}T)_{n},

since both correspond to the unique complete transfer system on
[n]
.

Proposition 4.7

(
[
1
, Theorem 20]
)
.

For each
n
, we have

d(n,n)=d(n,n-1)=\operatorname{Cat}(n).

4.2.
Rewriting the Recurrence Relation

We now can solve the recurrence relation, giving our first proof of the main theorem. Recall the definition of the Fuss–Catalan numbers:

A_{n}(p,r)=\frac{r}{np+r}\binom{np+r}{n}.

We begin with some helpful properties of the Fuss-Catalan number.

Proposition 4.8
.

For any positive integer
p
and positive
n,r
, the following properties hold.

(1)

A_{n}(3,r)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}A_{j}(2,1)A_{n-j}(3,j+r-1)
.

(2)

A_{n}(p,r)=A_{n}(p,r-1)+A_{n-1}(p,p+r-1)
.

(3)

A_{n+1}(p,1)=A_{n}(p,p)
.

(4)

A_{n}(p,s+r)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}A_{j}(p,r)A_{n-j}(p,s)
.

(5)

A_{n}(3,2)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{j+1}(2,1)A_{n-j}(3,j)
.

Proof.

5.1.
Counting using additive cores

Instead of using the filtration by powers of

on the additive indexing system, we can use the first part of Proposition
3.2

(\mathcal{O}_{a},\mathcal{O}_{m})
is the same question as compatibility of
(\core\mathcal{O}_{a},\mathcal{O}_{m})

\mathcal{O}_{m}\leq\core\mathcal{O}_{a}.

Since the core breaks up as a direct some of complete transfer systems, this last condition is really the same as asking that if

\core\mathcal{O}_{a}=\mathcal{O}_{n_{1}}^{cpt}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathcal{O}_{n_{k}}^{cpt},

then we have a direct sum decomposition

\mathcal{O}_{m}=\mathcal{O}_{n_{1}}^{\prime}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathcal{O}_{n_{k}}^{\prime},

where
\mathcal{O}_{n_{i}}^{\prime}\in T_{n_{i}}
for all

. This is the only condition here, so we deduce the following proposition.

Proposition 5.1
.

If
\mathcal{O}_{a}
is a transfer system with

\core\mathcal{O}_{a}=\mathcal{O}_{n_{1}}^{cpt}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathcal{O}_{n_{k}}^{cpt},

then there are

\prod_{i=1}^{k}\operatorname{Cat}(n_{i})

transfer systems
\mathcal{O}_{m}
such that the pair
(\mathcal{O}_{a},\mathcal{O}_{m})
is compatible.

This reduces our question of the number of compatible pairs to two parts:

(1)

Enumerate all of the transfer systems with a fixed core, then

(2)

evaluate the corresponding sum.

Notation 5.2
.

Let

, let

5.2.
Catalan tuples

The enumeration and exact sum were analyzed by de Jong, Hock, and Wulkenhaar

Definition 5.4

(
[
5

)

For each positive integer

is a a sequence of non-negative integers

with three properties:

(1)

.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{n}
be the set of all Catalan tuple of length
n
.

Remark 5.5
.

We have slightly modified the definition here to ignore trailing zeros. This removes our ability to predict the length of the string, but it will better connect with the extensions.

We can restate the conditions in Definition
5.4
slightly to start connecting with extensions.

Definition 5.6
.

The
excess
of a Catalan tuple
\vec{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{r})
of length

is

e(\vec{s}):=n-r-1.

Remark 5.7
.

Note that the edge condition in Definition

of

implies the inequality
n>r
. This means the excess is always non-negative.

Proposition 5.8
.

Let
\vec{s}
be a Catalan tuple of length
n

. Then for any

\vec{s}_{k}(\ell)=(s_{0},\dots,s_{r},\underbrace{0,\dots,0}_{\ell},k)

Proof.

For
0\leq j\leq r
, the Catalan tuple condition holds since it does for
\vec{s}
. If
\ell=0
, then we have

\sum_{i=0}^{r}s_{i}+k=n+k>r+1,

since
n>r
and

. Assume now that

, and consider a
1\leq j\leq\ell

\sum_{i=0}^{r+j}s_{i}=n=r+e+1.

On the other hand, this is greater than

if and only if

. This gives the bounds on
\ell
. Finally, note that the analysis for the case
\ell=0
now also implies the case

.
∎

Proposition 5.9
.

Let
\vec{s}
be a Catalan tuple of length
n
and excess
e=n-r-1
. Then for any

and

\vec{s}_{k}(\ell)=(s_{0},\dots,s_{r},\underbrace{0,\dots,0}_{\ell},k)

Proof.

The Catalan tuple given has length

\vec{s}
has length
(r+1)
, and we added
\ell+1

\vec{s}_{k}(\ell)
.
∎

This lets us rewrite Catalan tuples using only the non-zero entries.

If

Catalan tuples with core

Let

\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\in T_{m}

(2)

\core{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}=\mathcal{O}_{n_{1}}^{cpt}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathcal{O}_{n_{j}}^{cpt}\text{ and }\core{\mathcal{O}^{\prime\prime}}=\mathcal{O}_{m_{1}}^{cpt}\oplus\dots\mathcal{O}_{m_{i}}^{cpt},

\mathcal{O}

The result follows.

\mathcal{O}

Let
\mathcal{O}

\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\in T_{m}

(1)

We have a crossing transfer

(2)

We have crossing transfers

Proof.

One direction is immediate. For the other, if we have a transfer

We singled out the transfer

\mathcal{O}
be a transfer system with

\core{\mathcal{O}}=\mathcal{O}_{\vec{k}}^{sat}.

Write
\vec{k}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})
. We can immediately identify
\mathcal{O}
inductively as a type considered in the previous section.

Proposition 5.27
.

The transfer system
\mathcal{O}
is a core-preserving extension of
i_{K_{n-1}}^{\ast}\mathcal{O}
by
\mathcal{O}_{k_{n}}^{cpt}
.

This turns our problem into an inductive one, working down on the number of summands in the partition of
k
. We can now build our bijection.

Definition 5.28
.

Let

\sigma\colon\mathcal{E}_{\vec{k}}\to\mathcal{S}_{\vec{k}}

be defined inductively by the following procedure.
If
\vec{k}=(k)
, then
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}_{k}^{cpt}
, and we define

\sigma(\mathcal{O}_{k}^{cpt})=k.

For a general
\vec{k}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})
with
n>1
and
\mathcal{O}\in\mathcal{E}_{\vec{k}}
, let
\ell_{n-1}
be the unique number
0\leq\ell_{n-1}
such that

\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime},k_{n}}(\ell_{n-1}),

where
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}=i_{K}^{\ast}\mathcal{O}
, and define

\sigma(\mathcal{O})=\sigma(\mathcal{O}^{\prime}),\underbrace{0,\dots,0}_{\ell_{n-1}},k_{n}.

Example 5.29
.

For
\mathcal{O}
the transfer system in Figure
2
, we have

\sigma\mathcal{O}=(4,0,1,2).

We need to verify that
\sigma
actually lands in the set
\mathcal{S}_{\vec{k}}
.

Proposition 5.30
.

For any
\mathcal{O}
with
\core{\mathcal{O}}=\vec{k}
, we have

(1)

\sigma(\mathcal{O})\in\mathcal{S}_{\vec{k}}
, and

(2)

e\big(\sigma(\mathcal{O})\big)
is the number of elements
j
between
1
and
K_{n}-1
that transfer up to
K_{n}
in
\mathcal{O}
.

Proof.

We show this by induction on
n
. The base case of
n=1
is immediate by the definitions of
\sigma
and the excess, so assume this is true for partitions with fewer than
n
terms.

Let
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}=i_{K_{n-1}}^{\ast}\mathcal{O}
, and let
r
be the number of
j<K_{n-1}
which transfer up to
K_{n-1}
in
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
. By the inductive hypothesis,
\sigma\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
is a Catalan tuple with core
(k_{1},\dots,k_{n-1})
and we also have

e\big(\sigma\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\big)=r.

Now if
0\leq\ell_{n}\leq r
is such that
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime},k_{n}}(\ell_{n-1})
, then since
\ell_{n-1}\leq e\big(\sigma\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\big)
,
the first claim is Proposition
5.8
. For the second part, Proposition
5.9
shows that the excess of
\sigma\mathcal{O}
is

e(\sigma\mathcal{O})=e-\ell_{n-1}+(k_{n}-1).

Proposition
5.25
shows this quantity is exactly the number of elements smaller than
K_{n}
which transfer up to
K_{n}
.
∎

This gives us the final piece for our argument.

Corollary 5.31
.

The map
\sigma
is a bijection
\mathcal{E}_{\vec{k}}\to\mathcal{S}_{\vec{k}}
.

Proof.

By induction on
n
, we see that there are exactly as many extensions of
i_{K_{n-1}}^{\ast}\mathcal{O}
by
\mathcal{O}_{k_{n}}
as there are extensions of the Catalan tuple
\sigma(i_{K_{n-1}}^{\ast}\mathcal{O})
to a Catalan tuple ending with
k_{n}
, and the map
\sigma
gives a bijection between these.
∎

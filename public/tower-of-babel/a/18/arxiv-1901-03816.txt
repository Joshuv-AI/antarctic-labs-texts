Simple Juntas for Shifted Families
Andrey Kupavskii

Abstract
We say that a family ℱ \mathcal{F} of k k -element sets is a j j -junta if there is a set J J of size j j such that, for any F F , its presence in ℱ {\mathcal{F}} depends on its intersection with J J only. Approximating arbitrary families by j j -juntas with small j j is a recent powerful technique in extremal set theory. The weak point of all known junta approximation results is that they work in the range n > C ​ k n>Ck , where C C is an extremely fast growing function of the input parameters, such as the quality of approximation or the number of families we simultaneously approximate. We say that a family ℱ {\mathcal{F}} is shifted if for any F = { x 1 , … , x k } ∈ ℱ F=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\}\in{\mathcal{F}} and any G = { y 1 , … , y k } G=\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{k}\} such that y i ≤ x i y_{i}\leq x_{i} , we have G ∈ ℱ G\in{\mathcal{F}} . For many extremal set theory problems, including the Erdős Matching Conjecture, or the Complete t t -Intersection Theorem, it is sufficient to deal with shifted families only. In this paper, we present very general approximation by juntas results for shifted families with explicit (and essentially linear) dependency on the input parameters. The results are best possible up to some constant factors. Moreover, they give meaningful statements for almost all range of values of n n . The proofs are shorter than the proofs of the previous approximation by juntas results and are completely self-contained. As an application of our junta approximation, we give a nearly-linear bound for the multi-family version of the Erdős Matching Conjecture. More precisely, we prove the following result. Then min i ⁡ | ℱ i | ≤ ( n k ) − ( n − s + 1 k ) . \min_{i}|{\mathcal{F}}_{i}|\leq{n\choose k}-{n-s+1\choose k}.

The general statement and its implications

In this section, we state a general junta approximation result and then deduce several corollaries for shifted families satisfying different properties. Let us present a geometric interpretation of the property that we are working with.

First, consider the case of one family
{\mathcal{F}}
. Imagine that we are given an
(n+1)\times(n+1)
grid (thought of as a subset of the plane) and we are walking on the grid starting from
(0,0)
and at each step we can either go from
(i,j)
to
(i+1,j)
or to
(i+1,j+1)
. The steps
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{s}\}
of the walk at which the random walk goes “diagonally” give us a set in
2^{[n]}
and, obviously, there is a one-to-one correspondence between the subsets in
2^{[n]}
and such walks. The walks with exactly
k
diagonal steps correspond to
k
-element subsets of
[n]
. Thus, any family
{\mathcal{F}}
corresponds to a collection of walks on this grid.

Next, consider the line
\lambda
defined by
\alpha y=x+q
for some
\alpha,q>0
(here, by
x
and
y
we mean the coordinates on the plane). The property that we are interested in is “any walk from
{\mathcal{F}}
hits
\lambda
for some
x
”, where by hitting
\lambda
we simply mean that
\alpha y\geq x+q
for the corresponding
(x,y)
.

2

2

Of course,
(x,y)
is an integer point.

As was noted long time ago by the first author
[
7
,
8
]

{\mathcal{F}}
and the property that
{\mathcal{F}}

\lambda

It is easy to generalize the property of “hitting the line” to the case of several families
{\mathcal{F}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{F}}_{s}
. Consider a hyperplane
\pi
defined by
\sum_{i=1}^{s}\alpha_{i}y_{i}=x+q
for some
\alpha_{i},q>0
. Represent each

as a walk in the coordinate plane spanned by

and

. Then we are interested in the property that the “joint walk (represented by the points
(x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{s})
) hits the hyperplane
\pi
”.

For example, let us show how the cross
t
-intersecting property is related to “hitting the plane” property. Consider two families
{\mathcal{F}}_{1},{\mathcal{F}}_{2}
and assume that they hit the plane
\pi
for
\pi

. Then it is easy to see that
{\mathcal{F}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{F}}_{2}
are cross

-intersecting: indeed, for any

Finally, let us describe the idea that is behind the junta approximation result below. For some of the lines, as

3.2
Why do juntas necessarily have center
\mathbf{[j]}
?

In this subsection, we prove a simple and intuitive statement that juntas approximating shifted families should have center
[j]
.

For a given pair of indices
1\leq u<v\leq n
and a set

, define its
(u\leftarrow v)
-shift

S_{u\leftarrow v}(A)
as follows. If

or

, then
S_{u\leftarrow v}(A)=A
. If
v\in A,u\notin A
, then
S_{u\leftarrow v}(A):=(A-\{v\})\cup\{u\}
. That is,
S_{u\leftarrow v}(A)
is obtained from
A
by replacing
v
with
u
.
The
(u\leftarrow v)
-shift
S_{u\leftarrow v}(\mathcal{A})
of a family
\mathcal{A}
is as follows:

S_{u\leftarrow v}(\mathcal{A}):=\{S_{u\leftarrow v}(A)\,:\,A\in\mathcal{A}\}\cup\{A\,:\,A,S_{u\leftarrow v}(A)\in\mathcal{A}\}.

Proposition 16
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{I}\subset 2^{[n]}
is a junta with center
I
of size
j
defined by family
\mathcal{I}^{*}
. Assume that
{\mathcal{F}}\subset 2^{[n]}
is shifted and satisfies
|{\mathcal{F}}\setminus\mathcal{I}|\leq w
. Then, for any
u<v
, the junta
\mathcal{J}
with center
S_{u\leftarrow v}(I)
and defined by the family
S_{u\leftarrow v}(\mathcal{I}^{*})
satisfies

.

Obviously, after several such

-shifts, the center becomes
[j]
for

. Moreover, if we are shifting several juntas with center

at the same time, their combinatorial properties are preserved, since the juntas stay the same up to relabeling of the vertices.

Proof.

It is clearly sufficient to deal with the case
I\cap\{u,v\}=v
. Let us construct an injective map
\phi
from
{\mathcal{F}}\setminus\mathcal{J}
into
{\mathcal{F}}\setminus\mathcal{I}
. If

satisfies

, then

as well, and we put
\phi(F)=F
. If

, then

if and only if

. We then put

if

. Due to shiftedness, we are only left to deal with

and such that

. But if such

, then

does not belong to

(because

). Therefore, we can put

for such

.
∎

3

Recall that cross-dependence means that there are no sets
F_{1}\in{\mathcal{F}}_{1},\ldots,F_{s}\in{\mathcal{F}}_{s}
that are pairwise disjoint.

find
\min_{i\in[s]}|{\mathcal{F}}_{i}|
. (We note here that some authors use the term “
{\mathcal{F}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{F}}_{s}
contain a rainbow matching” to refer to the situation, opposite to “cross-dependence”.) In
[
14
]
, Huang, Loh and Sudakov proved the following result.

Theorem 20
.

If
n>3sk^{2}
and
{\mathcal{F}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{F}}_{s}\subset{[n]\choose k}
are cross-dependent then

\min_{i\in[s]}|{\mathcal{F}}_{i}|\leq{n\choose k}-{n-s+1\choose k}.

(14)

Later, a version of this theorem for families with different uniformity was obtained by Lu and Yu
[
21
]
. They have also managed to obtain an inequality of the product of sizes of cross-dependent families, partially answering one of the questions of
[
14
]
. In
[
15
]
, Huang, Li and Wang obtained a similar result for a stronger notion of a rainbow matching.

As it is easily guessed from the formula, the bound is attained by the families
{\mathcal{F}}_{1}=\ldots={\mathcal{F}}_{s}=\{F\in{[n]\choose k}:F\cap[s-1]\neq\emptyset\}
.

The bound (
14
) was obtained for
n>f(s)k
with some unspecified and very fast growing function
f(s)
by Keller and Lifshitz in
[
19
]
as an application of the junta method. We recommend the reader to consult
[
19
]
for more advanced applications of the junta method. The proof of the next theorem follows the framework proposed by the authors of
[
19
]
. Our junta approximation gives an almost linear bound.

Theorem 21
.

The statement of Theorem
20
holds for
n\geq 12ks\log(e^{2}s)
.

Proof.

Since shifting maintains cross-dependence (cf.
[
9
]
), we may assume that
{\mathcal{F}}_{i}
,
i\in[s]
, are shifted. Let us apply Theorem
12
part (iii) with
q=1
,
k_{1}=\ldots=k_{s}=k
,
C=e
and
r=3
.
We get cross-dependent
j
-juntas
\mathcal{J}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{J}_{s}
with
j=3s\log(e^{2}s)
and such that
|\mathcal{J}_{i}\setminus{\mathcal{F}}_{i}|\leq\frac{k^{3}}{n^{3}}{n\choose k}
. In particular, if
\min_{i\in[s]}|{\mathcal{F}}_{i}|\geq{n\choose k}-{n-s+1\choose k}
, then
\min_{i\in[s]}|\mathcal{J}_{i}|\geq{n\choose k}-{n-s+1\choose k}-\frac{k^{3}}{n^{3}}{n\choose k}
.

The proof consists of two steps. The first step is to show that the juntas must have the structure of the (conjectured) extremal family. We will need the following lemma proved by Huang, Loh and Sudakov
[
14
]
.

Lemma 22
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}_{1}\subset{X\choose t_{1}},\ldots,{\mathcal{G}}_{l}\subset{X\choose t_{l}}
be cross-dependent and
\sum_{i=1}^{l}t_{i}\leq|X|
. Then there exists
i
such that
|{\mathcal{G}}_{i}|\leq(l-1){|X|-1\choose t_{i}-1}
.

Recall that we use the following notation: for any family
\mathcal{G}\subset 2^{[m]}
and
X\subset Y\subset[m]
, put

\mathcal{G}(X,Y):=\big\{G\setminus X:G\in{\mathcal{G}},G\cap Y=X\big\}.

Lemma 23
.

If
\min_{i\in[s]}|\mathcal{J}_{i}|\geq{n\choose k}-{n-s+1\choose k}-\frac{k^{3}}{n^{3}}{n\choose k}
, then we have
\mathcal{J}_{1}=\ldots=\mathcal{J}_{s}=\big\{F\in{[n]\choose k}:F\cap[s-1]\neq\emptyset\big\}.

Proof.

Consider the defining family
\mathcal{J}^{*}_{i}
of
\mathcal{J}_{i}
.
If
\mathcal{J}^{*}_{i}
contains
l
singletons for some
l\in[s-1]
, then these singletons must be
1,\ldots,l
due to shiftedness. Clearly, if each
\mathcal{J}^{*}_{i}
contain
s-1
singletons, then they cannot contain any sets disjoint to
[s-1]
, and the claim is proved.

W.l.o.g., assume that
\mathcal{J}^{*}_{1}
does not contain
\{s-1\}
. Consider a bipartite graph
G
between
\{1\},\ldots,\{s-1\}
and
\mathcal{J}^{*}_{2},\ldots,\mathcal{J}^{*}_{s}
, where an edge between a singleton and a family is drawn if and only if the singleton belongs to the family. Take a maximal (non-extendable) matching in this graph, such that the singletons cover the initial segment
[s-l]
, where
l\geq 1
is an integer. W.l.o.g., assume the families that are not matched are
\mathcal{J}^{*}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{J}^{*}_{l}
(note that
\mathcal{J}^{*}_{1}
is not matched since it was not included in the graph). Consider the families

\mathcal{I}^{*}_{i}:=\mathcal{J}^{*}_{i}(\emptyset,[s-l])
and
\mathcal{I}_{i}:=\mathcal{J}_{i}(\emptyset,[s-l])
,
i=1,\ldots,l
. The former are the families on
2^{X}
for
X:=[s-l+1,j]
and none of these families contain a singleton due to the maximality of the chosen matching.

If
l=1
then
\mathcal{J}^{*}_{1}(\emptyset,[s-2])
can only contain sets containing
s-1
(otherwise,
\mathcal{J}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{J}_{s}
are not cross-dependent). Thus
|\mathcal{J}_{1}|\leq{n\choose k}-{n-s+1\choose k}-{n-j\choose k-1}<{n\choose k}-{n-s+1\choose k}-\frac{k^{3}}{n^{3}}{n\choose k}
, where the last inequality is valid for
n>2kj
.

From now on, we assume that
l\geq 2
.
Our next goal is to prove that

\text{one of }\mathcal{I}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{I}_{l}\text{ has size smaller than }{n-s+l\choose k}-{n-s+1\choose k}-\frac{k^{3}}{n^{3}}{n\choose k}.

(15)

Since
|\mathcal{J}_{i}\setminus\mathcal{I}_{i}|\leq{n\choose k}-{n-s+l\choose k}
, this and
|\mathcal{J}_{i}|=|\mathcal{J}_{i}\setminus\mathcal{I}_{i}|+|\mathcal{I}_{i}|
would lead to a contradiction.

For each
t=2,\ldots,k
, consider

\mathcal{I}^{(t)}_{i}:=\mathcal{I}_{i}^{*}\cap{X\choose t}.

The families
\mathcal{I}^{(t_{1})}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{I}^{(t_{l})}_{l}
are cross-dependent for any
t_{1}\ldots,t_{l}
.
The following claim is crucial in bounding the size of
\mathcal{I}_{i}
.

Claim 24
.

For any
\beta_{2},\ldots,\beta_{k}\geq 0,
there is
i
such that
\sum_{t=2}^{k}\beta_{t}|\mathcal{I}_{i}^{(t)}|\leq\sum_{t=2}^{k}\beta_{t}l{j-1\choose t-1}
.

Proof.

Assume that this does not hold. Then, for each
i\in[l]
, there is
t_{i}
such that
|\mathcal{I}_{i}^{(t_{i})}|\geq l{j-1\choose t_{i}-1}>l{|X|-1\choose t_{i}-1}
. Since
\mathcal{I}_{i}^{(t_{i})}\subset{X\choose t_{i}}
, we have
|\mathcal{I}_{i}^{(t_{i})}|\leq{|X|\choose t_{i}}
. Together, these two inequalities imply
t_{i}\leq|X|/l
and thus
\sum_{i=1}^{l}t_{i}\leq|X|
. Applying Lemma
22
, we get a contradiction with the fact that
\mathcal{I}_{1}^{(t_{1})},\ldots,\mathcal{I}_{l}^{(t_{l})}
are cross-dependent.
∎

Let us put
\beta_{t}:={n-j\choose k-t}
for every
t=2,\ldots,k
and apply the claim. Then there is
i\in[l]
, such that

\sum_{t=2}^{k}{n-j\choose k-t}|\mathcal{I}^{(t)}_{i}|\leq\sum_{t=2}^{k}{n-j\choose k-t}l{j-1\choose t-1}.

(16)

The left hand side of (
16
) is exactly
|\mathcal{I}_{i}|
! Finally, we are left to estimate the right hand side of (
16
). Denote the
t
-th term of the summation by
f(t)
. Recall that
n\geq 4jk
, and, due to
k\geq 2
and
j\geq 2
, we have
n-j-k\geq 3jk
. We have

\frac{f(t)}{f(t+1)}=\frac{n-j-k+t+1}{k-t}\cdot\frac{t}{j-t}>3t\geq 3.

Thus, we get that (
16
) and the inequality above implies
|\mathcal{I}_{i}|\leq\sum_{t=2}^{k}f(t)<f(1)\cdot\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}(1/3)^{t}=f(1)\cdot\frac{1/3}{1-1/3}=\frac{1}{2}f(1)=\frac{l}{2}{n-j\choose k-1}
. On the other hand,
{n-s+l\choose k}-{n-s+1\choose k}-\frac{k^{3}}{n^{3}}{n\choose k}\geq(l-1){n-j\choose k-1}
. Since
l\geq 2
, the last expression is an upper bound on
|\mathcal{I}_{i}|
, which concludes the proof of (
15
).
∎

The second step is to show that the families
{\mathcal{F}}_{i}
must satisfy
\mathcal{J}_{i}\setminus{\mathcal{F}}_{i}=\emptyset
for each
i\in[s]
.
This part of the argument is similar to the proofs from
[
5
,
19
]
.

Assume that, among
i\in[s]
, the density
\alpha_{i}:={\mathcal{F}}_{i}(\emptyset,[s-1])/{n-s+1\choose k}
is the largest for
i=1
and put
\beta_{i}^{l}:={\mathcal{F}}_{i}(\{l\},[s-1])/{n-s+1\choose k-1}
for each
i=2,\ldots,s
,
l\in[s-1]
. For a family
\mathcal{G}\subset{[m]\choose k}
and
t\geq k
, let
\bar{\partial}^{t}\mathcal{G}
be the collection of all sets in
{[m]\choose t}
that contain at least one set from
\mathcal{G}
. The following analytic corollary of the Kruskal–Katona
[
20
,
17
]
theorem was proved by Bollobás and Thomason
[
4
]
:

\Big|\bar{\partial}^{t}\mathcal{G}/{n\choose t}\Big|^{n-k}\geq\Big|\mathcal{G}/{n\choose k}\Big|^{n-t}.

From here, we conclude that
\alpha^{\prime}_{1}:=|\bar{\partial}^{(n+k)/2}{\mathcal{F}}_{1}(\emptyset,[s-1])|\geq\alpha_{1}^{1/2}
.

At the same time, for any bijection
\pi:[s-1]\to[2,s]
, the families
{\mathcal{F}}_{\pi(i)}(\{i\},[s-1]),

i\in[s-1]
, and
\bar{\partial}^{(n+k)/2}{\mathcal{F}}_{1}(\emptyset,[s-1])
are cross-dependent. Since
(n+k)/2+(s-1)k<n
, we may take a random ordered matching consisting of
s-1
sets
M_{1},\ldots,M_{s-1}
of size
k
and one set
M_{s}
of size
(n+k)/2
. In any such matching, there are at most
s-1
indices
i\in[s]
such that:
M_{i}\in{\mathcal{F}}_{\pi(i)}(\{i\},[s-1])
for
i\in[s-1]
, or
M_{i}\in\bar{\partial}^{(n+k)/2}{\mathcal{F}}_{1}(\emptyset,[s-1])
for
i=s
. Computing the expectation of the number of such indices, we get

\alpha^{\prime}_{1}+\sum_{i=1}^{s-1}\beta_{\pi(i)}^{i}\leq s-1.

Therefore, there exists
i\in[2,s]
, such that
\sum_{l=1}^{s-1}\beta_{i}^{l}\leq s-1-\alpha^{\prime}_{1}
. This implies that

|{\mathcal{F}}_{i}|\leq{n\choose k}-{n-s+1\choose k}+\frac{k^{3}}{n^{3}}{n\choose k}-\frac{k^{3/2}}{n^{3/2}}{n-s+1\choose k-1}<{n\choose k}-{n-s+1\choose k},

a contradiction. Here the last inequality is again due to our choice of
n
, that is,
n>12ks\log(e^{2}s)
.
∎

Thanks to the Kruskal–Katona theorem, we can derive the following non-uniform generalization of Theorem
21
.

Theorem 25
.

Fix integers
k_{1},\ldots,k_{s}
and assume that
n\geq 12ks\log(e^{2}s)
for
k:=\max_{i\in[s]}k_{i}
. Assume that
{\mathcal{F}}_{1}\subset{[n]\choose k_{1}},\ldots,{\mathcal{F}}_{s}\subset{[n]\choose k_{s}}
are cross-dependent. Then there exists
i\in[s]
such that

|{\mathcal{F}}_{i}|\leq{n\choose k_{i}}-{n-s+1\choose k_{i}}.

Proof.

Suppose that
|{\mathcal{F}}_{i}|>{n\choose k_{i}}-{n-s+1\choose k_{i}}
for every
i
. Then the families
\bar{\partial}^{k}{\mathcal{F}}_{1},\ldots,\bar{\partial}^{k}{\mathcal{F}}_{s}
are cross-dependent as well. Moreover, we claim that

|\bar{\partial}^{k}{\mathcal{F}}_{i}|>{n\choose k}-{n-s+1\choose k}

for every
i\in[s]
. This would contradict Theorem
21
, so we only need to verify the displayed inequality.

Set
{\mathcal{G}}_{i}:=\{[n]\setminus F:F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{i}\}
.

|{\mathcal{G}}_{i}|>{n-1\choose k_{i}-1}+\ldots+{n-s+1\choose k_{i}-1}={n-1\choose n-k_{i}}+\ldots+{n-s+1\choose n-k_{i}-s+1}.

By the Kruskal–Katona theorem
[
20
,
17
]
, its shadow on level
n-k
will be

|\partial^{n-k}{\mathcal{G}}_{i}|>{n-1\choose n-k}+\ldots+{n-s+1\choose n-k-s+1}={n\choose k}-{n-s+1\choose k}.

But
|\partial^{n-k}{\mathcal{G}}_{i}|=|\bar{\partial}^{k}{\mathcal{F}}_{i}|.

∎

6
Concluding Remarks

We believe that Theorem
21
is only one example of the many possible scenarios where the shifted juntas enable one to get better, concrete bounds which seem to be elusive for the general juntas obtained by means of discrete Fourier transform methods.

We note that the validity of Theorem
21
for
n>Csk
with some large
C
was announced by Keevash, Lifshitz, Long, and Minzer as a consequence of general sharp threshold-type results. In the proof, they use the aforementioned variant of Corollary
13
.

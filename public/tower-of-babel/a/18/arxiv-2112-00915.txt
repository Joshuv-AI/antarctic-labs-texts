Study of B u , d , s → K 0 ∗ ​ ( 1430 ) ​ P B_{u,d,s}\to K_{0}^{*}(1430)P and K 0 ∗ ​ ( 1430 ) ​ V K_{0}^{*}(1430)V decays within QCD factorization
Lili Chen, Mengfei Zhao, Yunyun Zhang, Qin Chang

Abstract
We study the nonleptonic charmless B u , d , s → K 0 ∗ ​ ( 1430 ) ​ P B_{u,d,s}\to K_{0}^{*}(1430)P ( P = K , π ) (P=K\,,\pi) and K 0 ∗ ​ ( 1430 ) ​ V K_{0}^{*}(1430)V ( V = K ∗ , ρ , ω , ϕ ) (V=K^{*}\,,\rho\,,\omega\,,\phi) decays. The amplitudes are calculated within the QCD factorization, and the non-perturbative quantities are evaluated by using a covariant light-front approach. The branching fractions and CP asymmetries of theses decay modes are calculated, some decay modes are first predicted, and some useful relations based on S ​ U ​ ( 3 ) SU(3) flavor symmetry are discussed. Comparing the theoretical results with the current available experimental data, it is found that K 0 ∗ ​ ( 1430 ) K_{0}^{*}(1430) can be described as the lowest-lying p-wave ( s , u / d ) (s,u/d) state rather than the first excited one.

Using the inputs given above, we then present our numerical results and discussion. The QCDF approach itself cannot give the exact values of the end-point parameters
X_{H}(\rho_{H},\phi_{H})
in the hard-spectator scattering corrections and
X_{A}(\rho_{A},\phi_{A})
in the annihilation amplitudes, which can only be determined by fitting to the experimental data. The current data indicate that
\rho_{A(H)}
is at the level of 1, but
\phi_{A(H)}
is generally different for kinds of decay types. For instance,
\phi_{A}=-55^{\circ}
,
-22^{\circ}
and
-70^{\circ}
with
\rho_{A}=1
(the so-called “Scenario S4” in Ref.
[
70
]
) are favored by the
B\to PP
,
PV
and
VP
decays, respectively
[
70
,
71
,
72
]
. In order to clearly show the effects of end-point contributions, we plot the dependences of the measured
{\cal B}(B_{u,d}\to K_{0}^{*}(1430)(\pi\,,\rho\,,\omega\,,\phi))
on the end-point parameters in Fig.
2
, in addition, the experimental data are also shown in such figure for comparison. The dash and dash-dotted lines show the dependences on
\phi_{A}
with
(\rho_{A},\rho_{H})={(1,0)}
and
\phi_{H}
with
(\rho_{A},\rho_{H})={(0,1)}
, respectively. Comparing the dash with the dash-dotted lines, it can be found that the measured decay modes are very sensitive to
X_{A}(\rho_{A},\phi_{A})
, but the effect of
X_{H}(\rho_{H},\phi_{H})
is trivial. It can be easily understood because these decay modes are penguin-dominated, and the hard-spectator scattering corrections may be significant only when the decays are dominated by the color-suppressed tree contribution
[

. Therefore, we assume that the end-point parameters are universal in the annihilation and hard-spectator scattering corrections,
i.e.

B_{u,d,s}\to K_{0}^{*}(1430)P\,(P=\pi,K)
and
K_{0}^{*}(1430)V\,(V=K^{*}\,,\rho\,,\omega\,,\phi)
decays are given in tables
5

,
9
and
10
. For each result, the first error is caused by the uncertainties of CKM parameters, the second error comes from the variation of quark masses, and the third error from the decay constants, form factors and Gegenbauer moments. In each table, the experimental data given by PDG
[

or HFAG
[

and the theoretical results obtained in previous works are also listed for comparison.

•

From tables
5
and
6
, it can be found that the branching fractions obtained based on S2 are generally much larger than the ones based on S1 because relatively larger decay constant of
K_{0}^{*}(1430)
and form factors of
B\to K_{0}^{*}(1430)
transition are predicated in S2. In addition, most of predictions based on S2 (S1) are favored (disfavored) by experimental data, the only exception is
B^{-}\to K_{0}^{*}(1430)^{0}K^{-}
decay. Our result
{\cal B}(B^{-}\to K_{0}^{*}(1430)^{0}K^{-})=(3.19^{+0.18+0.36+1.73}_{-0.14-0.62-1.39})\times 10^{-6}
in S2 is consistent with the results obtained in the previous works,
(3.37^{+1.03}_{-0.85})\times 10^{-6}
(QCDF)
[
29
]
and
(3.99\pm 1.38)\times 10^{-6}
(pQCD)
[
116
]
, however all of these theoretical predictions are an order of magnitude larger than experimental data
(0.38\pm 0.13)\times 10^{-6}
. The reason will be analyzed in the next item.

•

B^{-}\to K_{0}^{*}(1430)^{0}K^{-}
and
K_{0}^{*}(1430)^{-}K^{0}
decays are penguin dominated. After neglecting the power suppressed contribution, their simplified amplitudes can be written as

\displaystyle{\cal A}(B^{-}\to K_{0}^{*}(1430)^{0}K^{-})\sim(a_{4}^{p}-\gamma_{\chi}^{K_{0}^{*0}}a_{6}^{p})\,A_{K^{-}K_{0}^{*}(1430)^{0}}\,,

(59)

\displaystyle{\cal A}(B^{-}\to K_{0}^{*}(1430)^{-}K^{0})\sim(a_{4}^{p}-\gamma_{\chi}^{K^{0}}a_{6}^{p})\,A_{K_{0}^{*}(1430)^{0}K^{-}}\,.

(60)

A significant feature of scalar meson is that its chiral factor is proportional to
M_{S}^{2}/(m_{1}(\mu)-m_{2}(\mu))
, which results in that
\gamma_{\chi}^{K_{0}^{*0}}
is much larger than
\gamma_{\chi}^{K^{0}}
. Numerically, we obtain
\gamma_{\chi}^{K_{0}^{*0}}:\gamma_{\chi}^{K^{0}}\approx 12.6:1.4
at
\mu\sim m_{b}
. As a result, it is expected that
{\cal B}(B^{-}\to K_{0}^{*}(1430)^{0}K^{-})\gg{\cal B}(B^{-}\to K_{0}^{*}(1430)^{-}K^{0})
. Moreover, for all of the penguin dominated
B\to SP
and
SV
decays, the decay modes with
M_{2}=S
(
M_{2}
is the emitted meson) generally have relatively larger branching fractions than the ones with
M_{2}=(P,V)
, which can also be found from the numerical results listed in tables
5
and
6
.

In addition, the large
\gamma_{\chi}^{K_{0}^{*0}}
also results in the large theoretical predictions for
{\cal B}(B^{-}\to K_{0}^{*}(1430)^{0}K^{-})
compared with data, which has been mentioned in the last item. It should be noted that the significance of data,
(0.38\pm 0.13)\times 10^{-6}
, is smaller than
3\sigma
, thus more precise measurement on
{\cal B}(B^{-}\to K_{0}^{*}(1430)^{0}K^{-})
is required for confirming or refuting such possible anomaly.

•

The
\bar{B}_{d}^{0}\to K_{0}^{*}(1430)^{-}K^{(*)+}
and
K_{0}^{*}(1430)^{+}K^{(*)-}
decays are pure annihilation processes, and thus are very suitable for probing the effects of annihilation corrections. However, these decays have very small branching fractions
\sim 10^{-7}
because their amplitudes are power suppressed, and therefore are not easy to be precisely measured in the near future.

•

The
\bar{B}_{s}^{0}\to K_{0}^{*}(1430)^{+}\pi^{-}/\rho^{-}
and
\bar{B}_{s}^{0}\to K_{0}^{*}(1430)^{0}\pi^{0}/\rho^{0}(\omega)
decays are tree-dominated, while the former are color-allowed and the later are color-suppressed. As a result, the branching fractions of former are an order of magnitude larger than the later.

•

The
SU(3)
flavor symmetry indicates some useful relations between the decays considered in this work. Taking
B_{u,d,s}\to SP
decays as examples, after applying
SU(3)
flavor symmetry on the spectator quark, one may expect that

\displaystyle{\cal A}(B^{-}\to K_{0}^{*}(1430)^{-}K^{0})\approx

\displaystyle{\cal A}(\bar{B}_{d}^{0}\to\bar{K}_{0}^{*}(1430)^{0}K^{0})\,,

(61)

\displaystyle{\cal A}(B^{-}\to K_{0}^{*}(1430)^{0}K^{-})\approx

\displaystyle{\cal A}(\bar{B}_{d}^{0}\to K_{0}^{*}(1430)^{0}\bar{K}^{0})\,,

(62)

\displaystyle{\cal A}(B^{-}\to\bar{K}_{0}^{*}(1430)^{0}\pi^{-})\approx

\displaystyle{\cal A}(\bar{B}_{d}^{0}\to\bar{K}_{0}^{*}(1430)^{0}\pi^{0})\approx{\cal A}(\bar{B}_{s}^{0}\to\bar{K}_{0}^{*}(1430)^{0}K^{0})\,,

(63)

\displaystyle{\cal A}(B^{-}\to K_{0}^{*}(1430)^{-}\pi^{0})\approx

\displaystyle{\cal A}(B_{d}^{0}\to K_{0}^{*}(1430)^{-}\pi^{+})\approx{\cal A}(\bar{B}_{s}^{0}\to K_{0}^{*}(1430)^{-}K^{+})\,,

(64)

which further imply that

\displaystyle R_{1}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{\Gamma_{B^{-}\to K_{0}^{*}(1430)^{-}K^{0}}}{\Gamma_{\bar{B}_{d}^{0}\to\bar{K}_{0}^{*}(1430)^{0}K^{0}}}\approx 1\,,\qquad R_{2}\equiv\frac{\Gamma_{B^{-}\to K_{0}^{*}(1430)^{0}K^{-}}}{\Gamma_{\bar{B}_{d}^{0}\to K_{0}^{*}(1430)^{0}\bar{K}^{0})}}\approx 1\,,

(65)

\displaystyle R_{3}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{\Gamma_{B^{-}\to\bar{K}_{0}^{*}(1430)^{0}\pi^{-}}}{2\Gamma_{\bar{B}_{d}^{0}\to\bar{K}_{0}^{*}(1430)^{0}\pi^{0}}}\approx 1\,,\qquad R_{3}^{\prime}\equiv\frac{\Gamma_{B^{-}\to\bar{K}_{0}^{*}(1430)^{0}\pi^{-}}}{\Gamma_{\bar{B}_{s}^{0}\to\bar{K}_{0}^{*}(1430)^{0}K^{0}}}\approx 1\,,

(66)

\displaystyle R_{4}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{2\Gamma_{B^{-}\to K_{0}^{*}(1430)^{-}\pi^{0}}}{\Gamma_{B_{d}^{0}\to K_{0}^{*}(1430)^{-}\pi^{+}}}\approx 1\,,\qquad R_{4}^{\prime}\equiv\frac{2\Gamma_{B^{-}\to K_{0}^{*}(1430)^{-}\pi^{0}}}{\Gamma_{\bar{B}_{s}^{0}\to K_{0}^{*}(1430)^{-}K^{+}}}\approx 1\,.

(67)

Besides, the
SU(3)
flavor symmetry also expects that

\displaystyle R_{5}\equiv\frac{\Gamma_{\bar{B}_{d}^{0}\to K_{0}^{*}(1430)^{-}\pi^{+}}}{2\Gamma_{\bar{B}_{d}^{0}\to\bar{K}_{0}^{*}(1430)^{0}\pi^{0}}}\approx 1\,,\qquad R_{6}\equiv\frac{\Gamma_{B^{-}\to\bar{K}_{0}^{*}(1430)^{0}\pi^{-}}}{2\Gamma_{B^{-}\to K_{0}^{*}(1430)^{-}\pi^{0}}}\approx 1\,.

(68)

It is found in our calculation (S2) that

\displaystyle R_{1}

\displaystyle=0.93\,,\quad R_{2}=1.00\,,\quad R_{3}=1.12\,,\quad R_{3}^{\prime}=1.10\,,\quad R_{4}=0.99\,,\quad R_{4}^{\prime}=1.08\,,

(69)

\displaystyle R_{5}

\displaystyle=1.11\,,\quad R_{6}=0.90\,,

(70)

which generally agree with the expectations of
SU(3)
flavor symmetry. The flavor symmetry breaking effect is mainly ascribed to the remaining suppressed contributions. Taking
R_{6}
as an example, the amplitude of
B^{-}\to K_{0}^{*}(1430)^{-}\pi^{0}
decay receives additional CKM-suppressed contributions proportional to
\delta_{pu}\alpha_{1}
, as well as CKM- and color-suppressed
\delta_{pu}\alpha_{2}
, compared with with
B^{-}\to\bar{K}_{0}^{*}(1430)^{0}\pi^{-}
decay.

{\cal O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})

4
Summary

In this paper, the nonleptonic charmless
B_{u,d,s}\to K_{0}^{*}(1430)P

(P=K\,,\pi)
and
K_{0}^{*}(1430)V

(V=K^{*}\,,\rho\,,\omega\,,\phi)
decays are studied. The amplitudes are calculated by using the QCD factorization approach, and the non-perturbative quantities (form factor, decay constant and distribution amplitudes) are evaluated by using a covariant light-front approach. The branching fractions and CP asymmetries of theses decay modes are calculated, our main theoretical results are collected in tables
5
-
10
. Some decay modes are first predicted in this work. In order to test the underlying structure of
K_{0}^{*}(1430)
meson, our calculation are made based on two different scenarios that
K_{0}^{*}(1430)
is the first excited (scenario S1) and the lowest-lying (scenario S2) p-wave two-quark state. Comparing our results with data, we find that the scenarios S1 and S2 lead to significantly different results for branching factions, and the results based on scenario S2 agree well with the experimental data, which implies that
K_{0}^{*}(1430)
as the lowest-lying (scenario S2) p-wave
(s,u/d)
state is favored by data. However, the theoretical results for
{\cal B}(B^{-}\to K_{0}^{*}(1430)^{0}K^{-})
are much larger than data due to the large chiral factor
\gamma_{\chi}^{K_{0}^{*0}}
. The future refined measurement on
{\cal B}(B^{-}\to K_{0}^{*}(1430)^{0}K^{-})
is required for confirming or refuting such possible anomaly. Besides, some useful relations based on
SU(3)
flavor symmetry are studied.

Appendix A: Decay constant, form factor and DAs

The decay constant, form factor and DAs are essential nonperturbative inputs for the amplitudes of
B\to SP
and
SV
. In this sections, we would like to clarify our convention for the definitions of these nonperturbative quantities.

A.1 Decay constant

The decay constants of pseudoscalar (P), vector (V) and scalar (S) mesons are defined as

\displaystyle\langle P(p)|\bar{q}_{1}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{5}q_{2}|0\rangle

\displaystyle=-if_{P}p_{\mu}\,,\quad\langle V(p,\epsilon)|\bar{q}_{1}\gamma^{\mu}q_{2}|0\rangle=-if_{V}m_{V}\epsilon^{*\mu}\,,

\displaystyle\langle S(p)|\bar{q}_{1}\gamma^{\mu}q_{2}|0\rangle

\displaystyle=f_{S}p_{\mu}\,,\qquad\langle S(p)|\bar{q}_{1}q_{2}|0\rangle=m_{S}\bar{f}_{S}(\mu)\,.

(71)

The scale-dependent scalar decay constant
\bar{f}_{S}(\mu)
and the vector
decay constant
f_{S}
are related by the equation of motion, and thus have the following relation,

\displaystyle\bar{f}_{S}=\frac{\mu_{S}}{m_{S}}f_{S}\equiv\bar{\mu}_{S}f_{S}\,,\qquad\text{with}\qquad\mu_{S}=\frac{m_{S}^{2}}{m_{1}(\mu)-m_{2}(\mu)}\,,

(72)

where
m_{1}(\mu)
and
m_{2}(\mu)
are running masses of current quarks.

A.2 Form factor

The form factors of
B\to S\,,P\,,V
transitions concerned in this paper can be defined as

\displaystyle{\langle}S(p^{\prime\prime})|{\bar{q}}\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{5}b|B(p^{\prime}){\rangle}=

\displaystyle-i\bigg[\bigg(P_{\mu}-\frac{m_{B}^{2}-m_{S}^{2}}{q^{2}}q_{\mu}\bigg)U_{1}(q^{2})+\frac{m_{B}^{2}-m_{S}^{2}}{q^{2}}q_{\mu}U_{0}(q^{2})\bigg]\,,

(73)

\displaystyle{\langle}P(p^{\prime\prime})|{\bar{q}}\gamma_{\mu}b|B(p^{\prime}){\rangle}=

\displaystyle\bigg(P_{\mu}-\frac{m_{B}^{2}-m_{P}^{2}}{q^{2}}q_{\mu}\bigg)F_{1}(q^{2})+\frac{m_{B}^{2}-m_{P}^{2}}{q^{2}}q_{\mu}F_{0}(q^{2})\,,

(74)

\displaystyle{\langle}V(\epsilon,p^{\prime\prime})|{\bar{q}}\gamma_{\mu}b|B(p^{\prime}){\rangle}=

\displaystyle-i\frac{V(q^{2})}{m_{B}+m_{V}}\varepsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\epsilon^{*\nu}P^{\alpha}q^{\beta}\,,

(75)

\displaystyle{\langle}V(\epsilon,p^{\prime\prime})|{\bar{q}}\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{5}b|B(p^{\prime}){\rangle}=

\displaystyle 2m_{V}\frac{\epsilon^{*}\cdot P}{q^{2}}q_{\mu}A_{0}(q^{2})+(m_{B}+m_{V})\bigg(\epsilon_{\mu}^{*}-\frac{\epsilon^{*}\cdot P}{q^{2}}q_{\mu}\bigg)A_{1}(q^{2})

\displaystyle-\frac{\epsilon^{*}\cdot P}{m_{B}+m_{V}}\bigg(P_{\mu}-\frac{m_{B}^{2}-m_{V}^{2}}{q^{2}}q_{\mu}\bigg)A_{2}(q^{2})\,,

(76)

where
P_{\mu}=(p^{\prime}+p^{\prime\prime}),\,q_{\mu}=(p^{\prime}-p^{\prime\prime})
and
\varepsilon_{0123}=-1
.
The momentum dependence of form factor can be parameterized as

\displaystyle F(q^{2})=\frac{F(0)}{1-a\,(q^{2}/m^{2}_{B})+b\,(q^{2}/m^{2}_{B})^{2}}\,.

(77)

The values of parameters
a
,
b
and
F(0)
for the transition concerned in this work are summarized in Table
1
.

A.2 Distribution amplitude

For the light-cone distribution amplitudes of pseudoscalar and vector mesons, we take the same definition and convention used in the Ref.
[
70
]
.
For the scalar meson, the twist-2 and -3 light-cone distribution amplitudes are given by

\displaystyle\langle S(p)|{\bar{q}_{2}}(z_{2})\gamma_{\mu}q_{1}(z_{1})|0\rangle

\displaystyle=f_{S}p_{\mu}\int_{0}^{1}dxe^{i(xp\cdot z_{2}+\bar{x}p\cdot z_{1})}\Phi_{S}(x),

(78)

\displaystyle\langle S(p)|{\bar{q}_{2}}(z_{2})q_{1}(z_{1})|0\rangle

\displaystyle=f_{S}\mu_{S}\int_{0}^{1}dxe^{i(xp\cdot z_{2}+\bar{x}p\cdot z_{1})}\phi_{S}(x)\,,

(79)

\displaystyle\langle S(p)|{\bar{q}_{2}}(z_{2})\sigma_{\mu\nu}q_{1}(z_{1})|0\rangle

\displaystyle=-f_{S}\mu_{S}(p_{\mu}z_{\nu}-p_{\nu}z_{\mu})\int_{0}^{1}dxe^{i(xp\cdot z_{2}+\bar{x}p\cdot z_{1})}\frac{\phi_{S}^{\sigma}(x)}{6}\,.

(80)

The leading-twist DAs are conventionally expended in Gegenbauer polynomials,

\displaystyle\Phi_{M}(x,\mu)=

\displaystyle 6x(1-x)\left[1+\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}a_{n}^{M}(\mu)C_{n}^{3/2}(x-\bar{x})\right],

(81)

where
M=P\,,S\,,V
. Equivalently, for the scalar meson, one can also use

\displaystyle\Phi_{S}(x)=

\displaystyle 6x(1-x)\bar{\mu}_{S}(\mu)\left[b_{0}^{S}+\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}b_{n}^{S}(\mu)C_{n}^{3/2}(x-\bar{x})\right],

(82)

where,
a_{n}^{S}(\mu)={\bar{\mu}_{S}}(\mu)b_{n}^{S}(\mu)
and
\bar{\mu}_{S}(\mu)
can be conveniently absorbed by
f_{S}
via Eq. (
72
).

When three-particle contributions are neglected, the twist-3 two-particle DAs of P and S mesons are determined completely by the equation of motion, which then require

\displaystyle\phi_{P,S}=1\,,\qquad\phi_{P,S}^{\sigma}=6x\bar{x}\,;

(83)

For the vector meson, the twist-3 DAs can be expressed in terms of the leading-twist DA
\Phi_{\bot}(x)
of transversely polarized state, are usually expressed by the function,
\phi_{V}
, defined as

\displaystyle\phi_{V}(x,\mu)\equiv

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}dv\frac{\Phi_{\perp}(v)}{\bar{v}}-\int_{x}^{1}dv\frac{\Phi_{\perp}(v)}{v}=3\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n,\perp}^{V}(\mu)P_{n+1}(2x-1),

(84)

where
a_{0,\perp}^{V}=1
and
P_{n}(x)
are the Legendre polynomials.

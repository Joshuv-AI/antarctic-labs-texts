Correlated interaction effects in three-dimensional semi-Dirac semimetal
Jing-Rong Wang, Wei Li, Chang-Jin Zhang

Abstract
Understanding the correlation effects in unconventional topological materials, in which the fermion excitations take unusual dispersion, is an important topic in recent condensed matter physics. We study the influence of short-range four-fermion interactions on three-dimensional semi-Dirac semimetal with an unusual fermion dispersion, that is linear along two directions and quadratic along the third one. Based on renormalization group theory, we find all of 11 unstable fixed points including 5 quantum critical points, 5 bicritical points, and one tricritical point. The physical essences of the quantum critical points are determined by analyzing the susceptibility exponents for all of the source terms in particle-hole and particle-particle channels. We also verify phase diagrams of the system in the parameter space through numerically studying the flows of the four-fermion coupling parameters and behaviors of the susceptibility exponents. These results are helpful for us to understand the physical properties of candidate materials for three-dimensional semi-Dirac semimetal such as ZrTe 5 .

Model

The free action for 3D semi-DSM can be written as

\displaystyle S_{0}=\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\frac{d^{3}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\bar{\Psi}(\omega,\mathbf{k})\gamma_{0}\left[i\omega+\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{k})\right]\Psi(\omega,\mathbf{k}),

(2)

The gamma matrices satisfy the anticommutation relation
\left\{\gamma_{\mu},\gamma_{\nu}\right\}=2\delta_{\mu\nu}

\mu,\nu=0,1,2,3,5

The gamma matrices have the properties as following

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mu}^{{\dagger}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mu},

V
Interplay with Coulomb interaction

In 3D semi-DSM, the long-range Coulomb interaction becomes irrelevant
in the low energy regime
Abrikosov72
;
YangNatPhys14
. Considering the interplay of short-range four-fermion interactions and long-range Coulomb
interaction, we find that the flow of Coulomb interaction is not changed and still becomes irrelevant in the low energy
regime. Whereas, the flows of four-fermion interactions are modified by Coulomb interaction. It is shown that Coulomb
interaction tends to enhance the instabilities in particle-hole channels. For the case that all the initial values of four-fermion coupling
strength vanish, if the Coulomb interaction is strong enough, the four-fermion interactions are generated and become divergent finally
driven by the Coulomb interaction. We notice that the system is driven into axionic insulating phase in this case.
The detailed derivation and numerical results are shown in Appendix
F
.

VI
Summary

To conclude, we perform comprehensive studies about the influence of four-fermion interactions on
3D semi-DSM through RG theory. We find 11 unstable fixed points and show that five of them are QCPs,
five are BCPs, and the rest one is a TCP. The physical essence of the QCPs are determined
by analyzing the scalings of fermion bilinear source terms. The phase diagrams for general initial conditions
are also presented through detailed numerical calculations of flows of four-fermion couplings and susceptibility
exponents.

According to the theoretical study by Yang and Nagaosa

Appendix B
Mean-filed analysis

Here taking the short-range four-fermion interaction
g_{2}\left(\bar{\Psi}\Psi\right)^{2}
as an example, we
give the details of the derivation and calculation for the mean-field analysis. Analysis for other kinds of
four-fermion interactions could be carried out similarly.

B.1
Derivation of the self-consistent equation

Under the influence of short-range four-fermion interaction
g_{2}\left(\bar{\Psi}\Psi\right)^{2}
, the expectation value

\displaystyle\Delta_{2}=\left<\bar{\Psi}\Psi\right>,

(185)

could become finite. Considering the order parameter
\Delta_{2}
, the fermion propagator can be written as

\displaystyle G(i\omega,\mathbf{k},\Delta_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[i\omega\gamma_{0}+iv\left(k_{1}\gamma_{1}+k_{2}\gamma_{2}\right)+iAk_{3}^{2}\gamma_{3}\right.

(186)

\displaystyle\left.+\Delta_{2}\right]^{-1}.

For finite temperature, we employ the propagator in Matsubara formalism as following

\displaystyle G(i\omega_{n},\mathbf{k},\Delta_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[i\omega_{n}\gamma_{0}+iv\left(k_{1}\gamma_{1}+k_{2}\gamma_{2}\right)+iAk_{3}^{2}\gamma_{3}\right.

(187)

\displaystyle\left.+\Delta_{2}\right]^{-1},

where
\omega_{n}=(2n+1)\pi T
with
n
being integers and
T
the temperature.

The partition functions is given by

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\mathcal{D}\bar{\Psi}\mathcal{D}\Psi e^{S}

(188)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{\omega_{n}}\prod_{\mathbf{k}}\int\mathcal{D}\bar{\Psi}\mathcal{D}\Psi e^{\bar{\Psi}_{\omega_{n},\mathbf{k}}\beta G^{-1}(i\omega_{n},\mathbf{k},\Delta_{2})\Psi_{\omega_{n},\mathbf{k}}}

\displaystyle\times e^{-\int d\tau\int d^{3}\mathbf{x}\frac{\Delta_{2}^{2}}{2g_{2}}},

where
\beta=\frac{1}{T}
.
Using the functional integral formula

\displaystyle\int\mathcal{D}\bar{\eta}\mathcal{D}\eta e^{\bar{\eta}K\eta}=\det{K},

(189)

we get

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{\omega_{n}}\prod_{\mathbf{k}}\beta^{4}\det\left[G^{-1}(i\omega_{n},\mathbf{k},\Delta_{2})\right]

(190)

\displaystyle\times e^{-\int d\tau\int d^{3}\mathbf{x}\frac{\Delta_{2}^{2}}{2g_{2}}},

which leads to

\displaystyle\ln Z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\omega_{n}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\ln\left\{\beta^{4}\det\left[G^{-1}(i\omega_{n},\mathbf{k},\Delta_{2})\right]\right\}

(191)

\displaystyle-\int d\tau\int d^{3}\mathbf{x}\frac{\Delta_{2}^{2}}{2g_{2}}.

It is easy to obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{det}\left[G^{-1}(i\omega_{n},\mathbf{k},\Delta_{2})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\omega_{n}^{2}+E_{\mathbf{k},\Delta_{2}}^{2}\right)^{2},

(192)

where

\displaystyle E_{\mathbf{k},\Delta_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{v^{2}k_{\bot}^{2}+A^{2}k_{3}^{4}+\Delta_{2}^{2}}.

(193)

Thus, we arrive

\displaystyle\ln Z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\omega_{n}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\ln\left[\beta^{4}\left(\omega_{n}^{2}+E_{\mathbf{k},\Delta_{2}}^{2}\right)^{2}\right]

(194)

\displaystyle-\int d\tau\int d^{3}\mathbf{x}\frac{\Delta_{2}^{2}}{2g_{2}}.

Carrying out the summarization of frequency, we get

\displaystyle\ln Z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\ln\left[2\cosh\left(\frac{E_{\mathbf{k},\Delta_{2}}}{2T}\right)\right]-\beta\mathcal{V}\frac{\Delta_{2}^{2}}{2g_{2}},

(195)

where
\mathcal{V}
is volume of sample.

The free energy density
f
and free energy
F
are defined as

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{F}{\mathcal{V}}=-\frac{1}{\beta}\ln Z

(196)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4T\frac{1}{\mathcal{V}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\ln\left[2\cosh\left(\frac{E_{\mathbf{k},\Delta_{2}}}{2T}\right)\right]+\frac{\Delta_{2}^{2}}{2g_{2}}.

Taking the continuous limit by using the replacement

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{V}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\rightarrow\int\frac{d^{3}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}},

(197)

we obtain

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4T\int\frac{d^{3}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\ln\left[2\cosh\left(\frac{E_{\mathbf{k},\Delta_{2}}}{2T}\right)\right]+\frac{\Delta_{2}^{2}}{2g_{2}}.

(198)

The self-consistent equation for
\Delta_{2}
is determined by

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial\Delta_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

(199)

Concretely, the self-consistent equation is given by

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2g_{2}\int\frac{d^{3}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\tanh\left(\frac{E_{\mathbf{k},\Delta_{2}}}{2T}\right)\frac{1}{E_{\mathbf{k},\Delta_{2}}}.

(200)

At zero temperature, the equation becomes

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2g_{2}\int\frac{d^{3}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{E_{\mathbf{k},\Delta_{2}}}.

(201)

The Complexity of ( P k , P ℓ ) (P_{k},P_{\ell}) -Arrowing
Zohair Raza Hassan, Edith Hemaspaandra, Stanisław Radziszowski

Abstract
For fixed nonnegative integers k k and ℓ \ell , the ( P k , P ℓ ) (P_{k},P_{\ell}) -Arrowing problem asks whether a given graph, G G , has a red/blue coloring of E ⁡ ( G ) E(G) such that there are no red copies of P k P_{k} and no blue copies of P ℓ P_{\ell} . The problem is trivial when max ⁡ ( k , ℓ ) ≤ 3 \max(k,\ell)\leq 3 , but has been shown to be coNP-complete when k = ℓ = 4 k=\ell=4 . In this work, we show that the problem remains coNP-complete for all pairs of k k and ℓ \ell , except ( 3 , 4 ) (3,4) , and when max ⁡ ( k , ℓ ) ≤ 3 \max(k,\ell)\leq 3 . Our result is only the second hardness result for ( F , H ) (F,H) -Arrowing for an infinite family of graphs and the first for 1-connected graphs. Previous hardness results for ( F , H ) (F,H) -Arrowing depended on constructing graphs that avoided the creation of too many copies of F F and H H , allowing easier analysis of the reduction. This is clearly unavoidable with paths and thus requires a more careful approach. We define and prove the existence of special graphs that we refer to as “transmitters.” Using transmitters, we construct gadgets for three distinct cases: 1) k = 3 k=3 and ℓ ≥ 5 \ell\geq 5 , 2) ℓ > k ≥ 4 \ell>k\geq 4 , and 3) ℓ = k ≥ 4 \ell=k\geq 4 . For ( P 3 , P 4 ) (P_{3},P_{4}) -Arrowing we show a polynomial-time algorithm by reducing the problem to 2SAT, thus successfully categorizing the complexity of all ( P k , P ℓ ) (P_{k},P_{\ell}) -Arrowing problems.

Introduction and Related Work

Often regarded as the study of how order emerges from randomness, Ramsey theory has played an important role in mathematics and computer science; it has applications in several diverse fields, including, but not limited to,
game theory, information theory, and approximation algorithms

A key operator within the field is the arrowing operator:

In this work, we categorize the computational complexity of evaluating this operator when

The computational complexity of

-Arrowing has been categorized for a number of pairs

Most relevant to our work is a result by Rutenburg, who showed that

-Arrowing is coNP-complete

Using “senders”—graphs with restricted

The generalized

-Arrowing problem, where

Aside from categorizing complexity, the primary research avenue concerned with the arrowing operator is focused on finding minimal—with different possible definitions of minimal—graphs for which arrowing holds. The smallest orders of such graphs are referred to as Ramsey numbers.

Our work provides the first complexity result for

It is important to note that Burr’s construction relies on that fact that contracting less than three vertices between pairs of 3-connected graphs does not create new copies of said graph. Let

This process does not work for paths since contracting two path graphs will always make several new paths across the vertices of both paths.
Thus, we require a more careful approach when constructing the gadgets necessary for our reductions.
We focus on the problem defined below and
prove a dichotomy theorem
categorizing the problem to be in P or be coNP-complete. We note that such theorems for other graph problems exist in the
literature, e.g.,

Preliminaries

All graphs discussed in this work are simple and undirected.
V(G)
and

denote the vertex and edge set of a graph

For two disjoint subsets

The neighborhood of a vertex

is denoted as

and

.
The path, cycle, and complete graphs on

,

, and

, respectively. The complete graph on

.
Vertex contraction is the process of replacing two vertices

and

with a new vertex

is adjacent to all remaining neighbors

.

An
(F,H)
-good coloring of a graph

is a red/blue coloring of

where the red subgraph is
F
-free, and the blue subgraph is
H
-free. We say that

is
(F,H)
-good if it has at least one
(F,H)
-good coloring.
When the context is clear, we will omit

and refer to the coloring as a good coloring.

Formally, a coloring for

Polynomial-Time Cases

In this section, we prove the P cases from Theorem
1.1
.
Particularly, we describe polynomial-time algorithms for
(P_{2},P_{\ell})
-Arrowing and
(P_{3},P_{3})
-Arrowing (Theorem

Theorem 3.1

(P_{k},P_{\ell})
-Arrowing is in
{\rm P}
when
k=2
and when
k=\ell=3
.

Proof

Let
G
be the input graph. Without loss of generality, assume that
G
is connected (for disconnected graphs, we run the algorithm on each connected component).

Case 1
(k=2)
.

Coloring any edge in
G
red will form a red
P_{2}
. Thereby, the entire graph must be colored blue. Thus, a blue
P_{\ell}
is avoided if and only if
G
is
P_{\ell}
-free, which can be checked by brute force, since
\ell
is constant.

Case 2
(k=\ell=3)
.

Note that in any
(P_{3},P_{3})
-good coloring of
G
, edges of the same color
cannot be adjacent; otherwise, a red or blue
P_{3}
is formed.
Thus, we can check if
G
is
(P_{3},P_{3})
-good similarly to how we check if a graph is 2-colorable: arbitrarily color an edge red and color all of its adjacent edges blue. For each blue edge, color its neighbors red and for each red edge, color its neighbors blue.
Repeat this process until all edges are colored or a red or blue
P_{3}
is formed.
This algorithm is clearly polynomial-time. ∎

The proof that
(P_{3},P_{4})
-Arrowing is in P consists of two parts. A preprocessing step to simplify the graph (using Lemmas
1
and
2
), followed by a reduction to 2SAT, which was proven to be in P by Krom in 1967
[
12
]
.

Problem 3
(2SAT)

Let
\phi

Lemma 1

Suppose

is a vertex such that

d(v)=1

Then,

is
(P_{3},P_{4})
-good if and only if

(P_{3},P_{4})
-good.

Proof

Let

.
If

If

Clearly, if

G
is
(P_{3},P_{4})
-good, then

is
(P_{3},P_{4})
-good.
We now prove the other direction. Suppose we have good coloring of

. It is immediate that we can extend this to a good coloring of

by coloring
(v,u)
(the only edge adjacent to

(u,w)
is colored blue, and blue if
(u,w)
is colored red.
∎

Lemma 2

Suppose

If not, let

Clearly, if

(P_{3},P_{4})

It is easy to see that the conditions specified above must be satisfied by each good coloring of

-good implies that

We now prove the other direction by contradiction.
Suppose

-good. It is immediate that red

’s cannot occur in

, with edges

•

If

•

If

•

If

•

So, we are in the situation that

5
Conclusion and Future Work

A major and very difficult goal is to classify the complexity for
(F,H)
-Arrowing for all fixed
F
and
H
. We conjecture that in this much more general case a dichotomy theorem still holds, with these problems being either in P or coNP-complete. This seems exceptionally difficult to prove.
To our knowledge, all known dichotomy theorems
for graphs classify the problem according to one fixed graph, and the polynomial-time characterizations are much simpler than in our case. We see this paper as an important first step in accomplishing this goal.

Lemma 5

Let

such that (1)

is an endpoint of a

Proof

Let

. We first show that

. Consider the path

, which alternates between vertices in

and

and all edges in

It is easy to see that the vertices may be relabeled so that any vertex in

We now show that no path larger than

exists in

, at least

Recall that in any

If at least

has at least

Stability of Fixed Points in Generalized Fractional Maps of the Orders 0 < α < 1 0<\alpha<1 Journal: Nonlinear Dinamics
Mark Edelman

Abstract
Caputo fractional (with power-law kernels) and fractional (delta) difference maps belong to a more widely defined class of generalized fractional maps, which are discrete convolutions with some power-law-like functions. The conditions of the asymptotic stability of the fixed points for maps of the orders 0 < α < 1 0<\alpha<1 that are derived in this paper are narrower than the conditions of stability for the discrete convolution equations in general and wider than the well-known conditions of stability for the fractional difference maps. The derived stability conditions for the fractional standard and logistic maps coincide with the results previously observed in numerical simulations. In nonlinear maps, one of the derived limits of the fixed-point stability coincides with the fixed-point - asymptotically period two bifurcation point.

Introduction

Fractional maps as the exact solutions of fractional differential equations (of the orders
\alpha>1
) of periodically kicked systems were introduced in
TarZas2008
in a way similar to the way in which the universal map is introduced in regular dynamics. It turned out that these maps are discrete convolutions with power-law functions and are maps with memory whose history is briefly discussed in
TarZas2008
(for more discussions and references to maps with memory see
Chaos2015
and reviews
HBV2
;
MErev2014
). The results of
TarZas2008
were extended in
MEChaos2013
to include the maps of the orders
0\leq\alpha\leq 1
. Fractional difference/sum operators were introduced in
MillerRoss
;
GrayZhang
. The authors of
WuFall
proposed to define fractional maps (which we will call fractional difference maps) as solutions of fractional difference equations (see
AE1
;
AE2
;
Anastas
;
CLZ
;
Abdel
).

Investigations of the discrete convolution equations, which are analogs of the Volterra integrodifferential equations, were conducted independently from the investigations of fractional maps and were mostly related to the modeling in population dynamics (see, e.g., Chapter 6 in
ElaydiBook
).

The problem of the linear asymptotic stability of the fixed points of the discrete convolution equations with arbitrary kernels was investigated in

Elaydi1993
(for more reviews and publications on the subject see
ElaydiBook
;
Elaydi1996
;
Elaydi2007
;
Elaydi2009
;
ConvStab2017
). Semi-analytic and numerical results for the case of fractional (power-law kernel) standard and logistic maps were obtained in
ME2
;
Chaos
(see also reviews
MErev2014
;
HBV4
). Later, the problem of the linear asymptotic stability was considered for the case of fractional difference equations (falling factorial kernel)

AbuSaris2013
;
Cermak2015
;
Mozyrska2015
and, recently, for the case of the complex order fractional difference equations
StabComplex
.
In the general case of the discrete convolution equations, the conditions of stability were formulated as the requirements on zeros of a characteristic equation. In the case of the fractional difference equations (maps), the authors of the above cited publications found the explicit analytic conditions of stability.

Not only the conditions of stability, but also the rates of convergence to the fixed points have been investigated in the discrete convolution equations. It has been proven that if the zero solution of a linear Volterra difference equation is uniformly asymptotically stable, then it is exponentially stable if and only if the kernel decays exponentially (see Theorem 5 in
Elaydi1996
). Semi-analytically and numerically
the power-law convergence (which for
0<\alpha<1
is
n^{-\alpha}
, where
n
is the number of iterations and
\alpha
is the fractional order of a map) and divergence of trajectories was analyzed in
ME2
(Section 1 and Fig. 1),
ME3
(see Fig. 1),
ME4
(Section 3.2),

MErev2014
(Section 3.3.3 and figures in it), and
Chaos2014
(see Fig. 5 for
0<\alpha<1
) for the fractional and fractional difference standard map.
For the fractional logistic map, the power-law convergence was shown in

MEChaos2013
(see Fig. 6), and for the fractional and fractional difference cases with
0<\alpha<1
, the
n^{-\alpha}
–convergence of trajectories was demonstrated in
ME11
(see Fig. 6.8).
In
Cermak2015
the authors proved that in Caputo fractional difference maps convergence to asymptotically stable fixed points is
O(n^{-\alpha})
and in
Anh
the authors strictly proved that convergence obeys the power law
\Delta x\sim n^{-\alpha}
, where
0<\alpha<1
is the order of a fractional difference map. This implies that the correctly calculated Lyapunov exponents in converging to the fixed point Caputo fractional
difference maps should be equal to zero. However, starting from the first paper where the Lyapunov exponent was used to analyze stability in fractional difference maps
WBLya
(see Figs. 1 and 2), multiple authors reported the negative Lyapunov exponents in various fractional difference maps (see, e.g., Fig. 2 in
DD
or Fig. 7 in
GG
).

In the following sections, we recall the basic definitions and results related to the problem of stability in the generalized fractional map (Section
2
), generalize the results known for the problem of stability in fractional difference maps to obtain the criteria of stability of the fixed points in the generalized fractional map (Section
3
), consider application of these criteria to generalized, fractional, and fractional difference logistic and standard maps (Section
4
), and make the concluding remarks in (Section
5
).

In this paper we consider only the Caputo fractional and fractional (delta) difference maps and the generalized (as defined in Section
2
) fractional map. For the sake of brevity, in what follows we will omit the word Caputo.

2
Preliminaries

First, we will recall how the generalized fractional map is defined.

When
0<\alpha<1
, the generalized universal
\alpha
-family of maps
can be written in the form (see
Helman
and Sections 2 and 3 in
ME14
):

\displaystyle x_{n}=x_{0}-\sum^{n-1}_{k=0}G^{0}(x_{k})U_{\alpha}(n-k).

(1)

In this formula
G^{0}(x)=h^{\alpha}G_{K}(x)/\Gamma(\alpha)
,
x_{0}
is the initial condition,
h
is the time step of the map,
\alpha
is the order of the map,
G_{K}(x)
is a nonlinear function depending on the parameter
K
,
U_{\alpha}(n)=0
for
n\leq 0
, and
U_{\alpha}(n)\in\mathbb{D}^{0}(\mathbb{N}_{1})
. The space
\mathbb{D}^{i}(\mathbb{N}_{1})
is defined as (see
Helman
)

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}^{i}(\mathbb{N}_{1})\ \ =\ \ \{f:|\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\Delta^{i}f(k)|>N,\ \ \forall N,\ \ N\in\mathbb{N},

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}|\Delta^{i+1}f(k)|=C,\ \ C\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\},

(2)

where
\Delta
is a forward difference operator defined as

\Delta f(n)=f(n+1)-f(n).

(3)

The linearized near fixed point
x_{f}
map is

\displaystyle\delta_{n}=\delta_{0}+\lambda\sum^{n-1}_{k=0}\delta_{k}U_{\alpha}(n-k),

(4)

where

\displaystyle\lambda=-\frac{dG^{0}(x)}{dx}\Big|_{x=x_{f}}.

(5)

Without loss of generality, we will assume
\delta_{0}=1
(equivalent to the following substitution:
\tilde{\delta}=\delta/\delta_{0}
).
The unilateral
Z
-transform is used to analyze the local stability near fixed points:

\displaystyle Y(z)=\mathbb{Z}[\delta_{n}](z)=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\delta_{k}z^{-k},

(6)

where
z\in C
.

Z
-transformed Eq. (
4
) with
\delta_{0}=1
is

\displaystyle Y(z)=\frac{1}{(1-z^{-1})(1-\lambda\mathbb{Z}[U_{\alpha}(n)](z))},

(7)

where

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}[U_{\alpha}(n)](z)=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}U(k)z^{-k}.

(8)

In fractional maps

\displaystyle U_{\alpha}(n)=n^{\alpha-1},\ \ \ \ U_{\alpha}(1)=1

(9)

and in fractional difference maps

\displaystyle U_{\alpha}(n)=(n+\alpha-2)^{(\alpha-1)},\ \ \ \

\displaystyle U_{\alpha}(1)=(\alpha-1)^{(\alpha-1)}=\Gamma(\alpha).

(10)

The definition of the falling factorial
t^{(\alpha)}
is

t^{(\alpha)}=\frac{\Gamma(t+1)}{\Gamma(t+1-\alpha)},\ \ t\neq-1,-2,-3....

(11)

The falling factorial is asymptotically a power function:

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\Gamma(t+1)}{\Gamma(t+1-\alpha)t^{\alpha}}=1,\ \ \ \alpha\in\mathbb{R}.

(12)

The
h
-falling factorial
t^{(\alpha)}_{h}
is defined as

\displaystyle t^{(\alpha)}_{h}=h^{\alpha}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{t}{h}+1)}{\Gamma(\frac{t}{h}+1-\alpha)}=h^{\alpha}\Bigl(\frac{t}{h}\Bigr)^{(\alpha)},

\displaystyle\frac{t}{h}\neq-1,-2,-3,....

(13)

Following
Cermak2015
;
Mozyrska2015
, we notice that in the fractional difference case

\frac{U_{\alpha}(n)}{\Gamma(\alpha)}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}n+\alpha-2\\
n-1\end{array}\right)=\tilde{\phi}_{\alpha}(n-1),

(14)

where

\tilde{\phi}_{\alpha}(n)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}n+\alpha-1\\
n\end{array}\right)=(-1)^{n}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}-\alpha\\
n\end{array}\right),

(15)

\mathbb{Z}[\tilde{\phi}_{\alpha}(n)](z)=\Bigl(\frac{z}{z-1}\Bigr)^{\alpha},

(16)

and

\mathbb{Z}[U_{\alpha}(n)](z)=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha)}{z}\Bigl(\frac{z}{z-1}\Bigr)^{\alpha}.

(17)

3
Linear stability of the generalized fractional map of the order
0<\alpha<1

If we assume that, as in fractional and fractional difference maps,
U_{\alpha}(n)>0
for
n\in\mathbb{N}_{1}
, then the following generalization of Lemma 3.1, proven in
AbuSaris2013
for the fractional difference maps, is true:

Lemma 1

If
U_{\alpha}(n)>0
for
n\in\mathbb{N}_{1}
, then a fixed point
x_{f}
of the map Eq. (
1
) is linearly unstable when
\frac{dG^{0}(x)}{dx}\Big|_{x=x_{f}}<0
.

Proof

Assuming that
\delta_{0}=1
, from Eqs. (
4
) and (
5
) follows that
\delta_{k}>1
and

\displaystyle\delta_{n}=1-\frac{dG^{0}(x)}{dx}\Big|_{x=x_{f}}\sum^{n-1}_{k=0}\delta_{k}U_{\alpha}(n-k)>

\displaystyle 1-\frac{dG^{0}(x)}{dx}\Big|_{x=x_{f}}\sum^{n}_{k=1}U_{\alpha}(k).

(18)

Because
U_{\alpha}(n)\in\mathbb{D}^{0}(\mathbb{N}_{1})
, the last sum tends to infinity when
n\rightarrow\infty
. This ends the proof.

Application of Lemma
1
to the generalized fractional logistic map (
G_{K}(x)=x-Kx(1-x)
) gives the result known for the fractional and fractional difference logistic maps that the fixed point
x=0
is unstable when
K>1
and the fixed point
x=(K-1)/K
is unstable when
K<1
. For the fixed points
x=\pi m
,
m\in\mathbb{Z}
of the generalized fractional standard map (
G_{K}(x)=K\sin(x)
) the result of the Lemma is instability when
(-1)^{m}K<0
.

To analyze the stability problem for a system described by Eq. (
1
), we rewrite Eq. (
4
) as

\displaystyle\delta_{n+1}=\delta_{n}+\lambda\sum^{n}_{k=0}\delta_{k}\tilde{U}^{1}_{\alpha}(n-k),

(19)

where

\displaystyle U^{m-1}_{\alpha}(n)=\Delta^{m-1}U^{0}_{\alpha}(n-m+1),\ \ U_{\alpha}(n)=U^{0}_{\alpha}(n)\ \ {\rm and}

\displaystyle\tilde{U}^{1}_{\alpha}(k)=U^{1}_{\alpha}(k+1).

(20)

Z-transform of Eq. (
19
) is

\displaystyle Y(z)=\frac{z}{z-1-\lambda\mathbb{Z}[\tilde{U}^{1}_{\alpha}(n)](z)}=\frac{z}{z-1-\lambda z\mathbb{Z}[U^{1}_{\alpha}(n)](z)}

\displaystyle=\frac{z}{z-1-(1-z^{-1})\lambda z\mathbb{Z}[U_{\alpha}(n)](z)}.

(21)

Because
U_{\alpha}(n)\in\mathbb{D}^{0}(\mathbb{N}_{1})
,
U^{1}_{\alpha}(n)\in l_{1}
and its norm is defined as
||U^{1}_{\alpha}(n)||=\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}|U^{1}_{\alpha}(k)|
. Then, according to Theorem 6.14 of
ElaydiBook
,
\mathbb{Z}[U^{1}_{\alpha}(n)](z)
is analytic and
|\mathbb{Z}[U^{1}_{\alpha}(n)](z)|\geq||U^{1}_{\alpha}(n)||
for
|z|\geq 1
. According to Theorem 6.17 of
ElaydiBook
, the zero solution of the system defined by Eq. (
19
) (and, correspondingly, of the linearized original Eq. (
1
)) is uniformly asymptotically stable if and only if
\omega(z)=z-1-\lambda z\mathbb{Z}[U^{1}_{\alpha}(n)](z)

has no zeros for all
|z|\geq 1
. z is a zero of
\omega(z)
if and only if

\displaystyle\lambda=\frac{z-1}{z}\frac{1}{\mathbb{Z}[U^{1}_{\alpha}(n)](z)}

\displaystyle=\frac{z-1}{z}\frac{1}{\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}[U_{\alpha}(k)-U_{\alpha}(k-1)]z^{-k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{z-1}{\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}[U_{\alpha}(k+1)-U_{\alpha}(k)]z^{-k}}.

(22)

The span of the real values of
\lambda
for which the zero solution is stable corresponds to the values of Eq. (
22
) from
z=-1
to
z=1
.
\lambda=0
when
z=1
and when
z=-1

\displaystyle\lambda(-1)=\frac{-2}{U_{\alpha}(1)+\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}[U_{\alpha}(k+1)-U_{\alpha}(k)](-1)^{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}U_{\alpha}(k)(-1)^{k}}=-\frac{1}{S_{2}},

(23)

where we used the definition
S_{2}=\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}U_{\alpha}(k)(-1)^{k+1}
introduced in
ME14
.
Finally, we may formulate the following condition of stability of the fixed point for the linearized map Eq. (
4
):

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{S_{2}}<\lambda<0,

(24)

or, equivalently,

\displaystyle 0<\frac{dG(x)}{dx}\Big|_{x=x_{f}}<\frac{\Gamma(\alpha)}{S_{2}h^{\alpha}}.

(25)

This result may be formulated as the following theorem:

Theorem 3.1

The map Eq. (
1
), where
G^{0}(x)=h^{\alpha}G_{K}(x)/\Gamma(\alpha)
,
x_{0}
is the initial condition,
h
is the time step of the map,
\alpha
is the order of the map,
G_{K}(x)
is a nonlinear function depending on the parameter
K
,
U_{\alpha}(n)=0
for
n\leq 0
, and
\Delta U_{\alpha}(n)\in l_{1}
is asymptotically stable if and only if the conditions Eq. (
25
), where
S_{2}=\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}U_{\alpha}(k)(-1)^{k+1}
, are satisfied.

Let us recall the equations obtained in
ME14
for the calculation of the period two (
T=2
) points
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
:

\displaystyle x_{2}-x_{1}=\frac{S_{2}h^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}[G(x_{2})-G(x_{1})],

\displaystyle G(x_{1})+G(x_{2})=0.

(26)

Near the first bifurcation point
x_{f}
, where
x_{1}\approx x_{2}
, the first of these equations can be written as

\displaystyle x_{2}-x_{1}=\frac{S_{2}h^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\frac{dG(x)}{dx}\Big|_{x=x_{f}}(x_{2}-x_{1}).

(27)

This equation gives the following condition for the first bifurcation

\displaystyle\frac{dG(x)}{dx}\Big|_{x=x_{f}}=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha)}{S_{2}h^{\alpha}}

(28)

consistent with Eq. (
25
).

In the case of the fractional difference maps,
S_{2}
can be calculated explicitly:

\displaystyle S_{2}=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\Bigl[U_{\alpha}(2k+1)-U_{\alpha}(2k+2)\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\Bigl[(2k+\alpha-1)^{(\alpha-1)}-(2k+\alpha)^{(\alpha-1)}\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\Bigl[\frac{\Gamma(2k+\alpha)}{\Gamma(2k+1)}-\frac{\Gamma(2k+\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(2k+2)}\Bigr]

\displaystyle=(1-\alpha)\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\frac{\Gamma(2k+\alpha)}{\Gamma(2k+2)}=-\Gamma(\alpha)\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\frac{\Gamma(2k+\alpha)}{\Gamma(\alpha-1)\Gamma(2k+2)}

\displaystyle=-\Gamma(\alpha)\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}2k+\alpha-1\\
2k+1\end{array}\right)=\Gamma(\alpha)\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1-\alpha\\
2k+1\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=\Gamma(\alpha)\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\Bigl[\left(\begin{array}[]{c}-\alpha\\
2k+1\end{array}\right)+\left(\begin{array}[]{c}-\alpha\\
2k\end{array}\right)\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\Gamma(\alpha)\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}-\alpha\\
k\end{array}\right)=\Gamma(\alpha)2^{-\alpha}.

Here we used the following well-known and easily verifiable identities:

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\mu\\
\eta\end{array}\right)=\frac{\Gamma(\mu+1)}{\Gamma(\mu-\eta+1)\Gamma(\eta+1)},

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}n+\alpha-1\\
n\end{array}\right)=(-1)^{n}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}-\alpha\\
n\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\mu\\
n\end{array}\right)+\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\mu\\
n+1\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\mu+1\\
n+1\end{array}\right),

and

\displaystyle(x+y)^{\alpha}=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\alpha\\
k\end{array}\right)x^{\alpha-k}y^{k}.

Then, in fractional difference maps the stability of the fixed points
is defined by the inequality

\displaystyle 0<\frac{dG(x)}{dx}\Big|_{x=x_{f}}<\Bigl(\frac{2}{h}\Bigr)^{\alpha}

(60)

and the bifurcation point is defined by the equality

\displaystyle\frac{dG(x)}{dx}\Big|_{x=x_{f}}=\Bigl(\frac{2}{h}\Bigr)^{\alpha}

(61)

Generalized fractional logistic and standard maps,
0<\alpha<1

Fractional logistic (quadratic nonlinearity) and standard (harmonic nonlinearity) maps were among the first introduced fractional and fractional difference maps
MEChaos2013
;
WuFall
;
ME2
;
Chaos2014
;
ME9

WBLog
. They also were used in applications related to communications
Com1

, aging

Encr

4.1
Generalized fractional logistic map,
0<\alpha<1

In the generalized fractional logistic map

\displaystyle G_{K}(x)=x-Kx(1-x).

(62)

This map has two fixed points
x_{f1}=0
and
x_{f2}=(K-1)/K
.

The linearized version of this map around the fixed point at the origin (
x=0
) is

\displaystyle\delta_{n}=\delta_{0}+\frac{h^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}(K-1)\sum^{n-1}_{k=0}\delta_{k}U_{\alpha}(n-k).

(63)

The linearized version of this map around the fixed point
x=(K-1)/K
is

\displaystyle\delta_{n}=\delta_{0}-\frac{h^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}(K-1)\sum^{n-1}_{k=0}\delta_{k}U_{\alpha}(n-k).

(64)

Eq. (
25
) applied to the fixed point
x_{f1}
gives the following condition of stability:

\displaystyle 1-\frac{\Gamma(\alpha)}{S_{2}h^{\alpha}}<K<1

(65)

and the condition of stability of the fixed point
x_{f2}
is

\displaystyle 1<K<\frac{\Gamma(\alpha)}{S_{2}h^{\alpha}}+1.

(66)

The bifurcation point is

\displaystyle K=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha)}{S_{2}h^{\alpha}}+1.

(67)

In the case the fractional difference logistic map
x_{f1}
is stable when

\displaystyle 1-\Bigl(\frac{2}{h}\Bigr)^{\alpha}<K<1,

(68)

x_{f2}
is stable when

\displaystyle 1<K<\Bigl(\frac{2}{h}\Bigr)^{\alpha}+1,

(69)

and the bifurcation point is

\displaystyle K=\Bigl(\frac{2}{h}\Bigr)^{\alpha}+1.

(70)

Generalized fractional standard (circle with zero driving phase) map,

In the generalized fractional standard map

\displaystyle G_{K}(x)=K\sin(x).

(71)

\displaystyle\frac{dG(x)}{dx}\Big|_{x=x_{fn}}=(-1)^{n}K.

(72)

\displaystyle 0<(-1)^{n}K<\frac{\Gamma(\alpha)}{S_{2}h^{\alpha}}.

(73)

\displaystyle K=\pm\frac{\Gamma(\alpha)}{S_{2}h^{\alpha}}.

(74)

\displaystyle 0<(-1)^{n}K<\Bigl(\frac{2}{h}\Bigr)^{\alpha}.

(75)

5
Conclusion

The inequality derived in this paper, Eq. (
25
), defines the necessary and sufficient conditions for the local stability of fixed points of the nonlinear generalized fractional maps and the upper limit in this inequality defines the fixed-point – T=2 bifurcation point. The only condition used to derive Eq. (
25
) was the following requirement on the kernel
U_{\alpha}(n)
of the map

\displaystyle\Delta U_{\alpha}(n)\in l_{1}.

(77)

This implies that any map, Eq. (
1
), with the kernel satisfying Eq. (
77
) will have the same conditions of stability of its fixed points.

The requirement that
\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}U_{\alpha}(k)=\pm\infty
, which was important for the finding of the periodic points in
Helman
;
ME14
, was not used in this paper.

Rational configurations in K3 surfaces and simply-connected p g = 1 p_{g}=1 surfaces for K 2 = 1 , 2 , 3 , 4 , 5 , 6 , 7 , 8 , 9 K^{2}=1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8,9
Javier Reyes, Giancarlo Urzúa

Abstract
. We prove the existence of ( 20 − 2 ​ K 2 ) (20-2K^{2}) -dimensional families of simply-connected surfaces with ample canonical class, p g = 1 p_{g}=1 , and 1 ≤ K 2 ≤ 9 1\leq K^{2}\leq 9 , and we study the relation with configurations of rational curves in K3 surfaces via ℚ \mathbb{Q} -Gorenstein smoothings. Our surfaces with K 2 = 7 K^{2}=7 and K 2 = 9 K^{2}=9 are the first surfaces known in the literature, together with the existence of a 4 4 -dimensional family for K 2 = 8 K^{2}=8 .



1.
Introduction

{\mathbb{Q}}
-Gorenstein smoothings allow us to relate configurations of rational curves in rational surfaces with families of
p_{g}=0
surfaces. They include Enriques surfaces (starting with a Coble surface for example), properly elliptic surfaces (in particular Dolgachev surfaces), and surfaces of general type (in particular simply-connected, although non-trivial fundamental groups can appear too, as shown for Godeaux surfaces in
[
CU18
,
DRU20
]
). In fact, the only known examples of simply-connected minimal surfaces of general type with
p_{g}=0
and
K^{2}=2,3,4
have been constructed via
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Gorenstein smoothings (see
[
LP07
]
,
[
PPS09a
]
,
[
PPS09b
]
for the first examples; see e.g.
[
SU16
]
for several other examples).

Similarly, simply-connected
p_{g}=1
surfaces are related to configurations of rational curves in K3 surfaces. In
[
PPS13
]
and via
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Gorenstein smoothings, J. Park, H. Park, and D. Shin proved the existence of
(20-2K^{2})
-dimensional families of simply-connected minimal surfaces of general type with
p_{g}=1
and
K^{2}=3,4,5,6
, and also the existence of such surfaces with
K^{2}=8
.

We recall that
p_{g}=1
surfaces were studied in the eighties for their relation with counterexamples to Torelli type of theorems (see
[
Ky77
]
,
[
C80
]
and
[
T80
,
T81
]
). For
K^{2}=1
, Catanese proved
[
C80
]
that the moduli space is an irreducible rational variety of dimension
18
. (Beauville
[
B14
]
showed, using that the image of the period map is open
[
C79
]
, that in this moduli space the surfaces with maximal Picard number are dense.) These surfaces are simply-connected, and they can be realized as weighted complete intersections
(6,6)
in
{\mathbb{P}}(1,2,2,3,3)
. (For similar results in the situation of Gorenstein stable surfaces see
[
FPR17
]
.)

Less is known in the case of
K^{2}=2
. It can be shown that the torsion of the Picard group is either
\{0\}
or
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
. For the latter, Catanese and Debarre
[
CD80
]
proved that their moduli space is a rational irreducible variety of dimension
16
, and that these surfaces have
\pi_{1}={\mathbb{Z}}/2
. They also worked out various properties for torsion zero surfaces, including an irreducible
16
dimensional component of the moduli space, but it is still an open problem whether this is the only component for the moduli space of torsion zero surfaces. If that were the case, then all these surfaces would be simply-connected. We note that these particular surfaces are relevant for the description of the moduli space of
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
-Godeaux surfaces, which was recently proved
[
DR20
]
to be a rational irreducible variety of dimension
8
, finishing with the previous attempts e.g.
[
CD80
,
CCO94
,
Co16
]
. For
K^{2}=3
much less is known, the torsion of the Picard group has order at most
4
, and the ones with torsion
{\mathbb{Z}}/3
are described in
[
M03
]
. Up until now, the article
[
PPS13
]
provides the only other examples of simply-connected
p_{g}=1
surfaces of general type.

In the present paper, we study the relation between configurations of rational curves in K3 surfaces and simply-connected
p_{g}=1
surfaces via
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Gorenstein smoothings, and we prove the existence of
(20-2K^{2})
-dimensional families for
K^{2}=7,8,9
. This finishes with all possible values of
K^{2}
in the case of unobstructed
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Gorenstein smoothings, where surfaces with higher
K^{2}
are harder to find. A particular characteristic of our constructions is that we use configurations from one fixed simple normal crossings (SNC) configuration formed by smooth rational curves (see Section
4
).

Theorem 1.1
.

There are normal projective surfaces
X
with ample canonical class, and the following properties:

(1)

They have only cyclic quotient singularities of type
\frac{1}{n^{2}}(1,na-1)
with gcd
(n,a)=1
(i.e. Wahl singularities), and rational double points. The minimal resolution of
X
is a suitable composition blow-ups from a K3 surface.

(2)

q(X)=0
,
p_{g}(X)=1
, and
K_{X}^{2}=1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8,9
.

(3)

The surface
X
has no-local-to-global obstructions to deform, and in particular it represents a family of dimension
20-2K_{X}^{2}
in the Kollár–Shepherd-Barron–Alexeev (KSBA) compactification of the moduli space of surfaces of general type.

For each of these surfaces, we are able to compute the fundamental group of a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Gorenstein smoothing, and so we obtain the following.

Corollary 1.2
.

For each
k\in\{1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8,9\}
, there are simply-connected nonsingular surfaces with ample canonical class,
K^{2}=k
, and
p_{g}=1
, whose deformation space has dimension
20-2k
. In particular, they are exotic minimal symplectic
3{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{2}\#(19-k)\overline{{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}
.

Each Wahl singularity in
X
defines a divisor in the KSBA compactification of the moduli space of surfaces of general type. The general surface
Y
in that divisor has one Wahl singularity. Let us suppose that the surface
Y
can also be constructed from a nodal configuration
{\mathcal{A}}
of
(-2)
-curves in a K3 surface
S
. (This is a situation that happens often.) Thus
{\mathcal{A}}
defines a sublattice
M
in Pic
(S)
and, by Dolgachev
[
D96
, Proposition 2.1]
, it defines a local moduli space
S_{M}
of isomorphism classes of
M
-polarized K3 surfaces. It can be checked that the dimension of
S_{M}
matches the dimension of the corresponding divisor (see Remark
3.5
). It may also be the case that the same configuration defines two distinct divisors in the same moduli space. It is not clear to us the direct relation between configurations and Wahl singularities. One aim of this paper is to elaborate on some connections between bounds of lengths of Wahl singularities and logarithmic Chern numbers of configurations.

Another particular property is that the Picard number of the K3 surface must be at least
r=P+2K^{2}\geq 3
, where
P
is the number of Wahl singularities in
X
and

is the number of curves in the configuration. It is well-known that such K3 surfaces contain a finite number of
(-2)
-curves if and only if they have a finite automorphism group (which is equivalent to being a Mori dream space), and they were all classified by their Picard lattices, cf.
[
Ro20
]
. It turns out that to construct our surfaces we need configurations of
(-2)
-curves in surfaces with infinitely many
(-2)
-curves, in particular, we use elliptic extremal K3 surfaces
[
Ye99
,
SZ01
]
. In this paper, we actually obtain our results from just one of them. We work out all details for specific examples for each
K^{2}
. At the end we provide some others, but the complete world of them seems to be huge, depending on the value of
K^{2}
.

In Section
2
, we describe general
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Gorenstein degenerations of regular
p_{g}=1
surfaces and general bounds for lengths of Wahl singularities in the special fiber. To exemplify with the simplest case, we classify all possibilities for
K^{2}=1
and one Wahl singularity (Theorem
2.6
), realizing most of them after showing a general tool for obstructions from nodal rational configurations in K3 surfaces (Proposition
2.8
). In Section
3
, we study the geography of SNC configurations of rational curves in K3 surfaces in relation to the surfaces we want to find (Proposition
3.1
). We also review various criteria to check the positivity of
K
, to compute the fundamental group of general fibers, and to obtain unobstructedness in our case. In Section
4
, we prove Theorem
1.1
and its corollary, showing also several examples at the end with particular properties, among them we show some T-singularities
\frac{1}{2n^{2}}(1,2na-1)
and wormholes
[
UV21
]
. In a sequel paper, we will work out new exotic surfaces with higher

, in the cases of simply-connected
p_{g}=1

(-m)
-curve

•

•

For a projective surface

.

2.
Wahl degenerations of
p_{g}=1
surfaces

Cyclic quotient singularities
will be denoted by
\frac{1}{m}(1,q)
, meaning the isomorphism class of the germ at the origin of the quotient of
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
by the action
(x,y)\mapsto(\mu x,\mu^{q}y)
, where
\mu
is a primitive
m
-th root of
1
, and
q
is an integer with
0<q<m
and gcd
(q,m)=1
. We refer to
[
U16b
, 2.1]
or
[
BHPV04
, III Sect. 5]
for basic facts. Their minimal resolution is achieved by replacing the singular point by a chain of smooth rational curves
\{E_{1},\ldots,E_{\ell}\}
with
E_{i}^{2}=-b_{i}<-1
, where one reads the
b_{i}
from the
Hirzebruch-Jung continued fraction

\frac{m}{q}=b_{1}-\frac{1}{b_{2}-\frac{1}{\ddots-\frac{1}{b_{\ell}}}}=:[b_{1},\ldots,b_{\ell}].

The
length
of
\frac{1}{m}(1,q)
is
\ell
. If
\sigma\colon\tilde{Y}\to Y=\frac{1}{m}(1,q)
is the minimal resolution, then the
discrepancies
of the
E_{i}
are the coefficients of the
E_{i}
in the difference
K_{\tilde{Y}}-\sigma^{*}(K_{Y})
. Discrepancies are rational numbers in the interval
(-1,0]
.

Let
{\mathbb{D}}
be the smooth analytic germ of a curve. For a surface
X
with only quotient singularities, a deformation
(X\subset{\mathcal{X}})\to(0\in{\mathbb{D}})
of
X
is called a
smoothing
if its general fiber is smooth. It is a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Gorenstein smoothing
if in addition
K_{{\mathcal{X}}}
is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Cartier. A
T-singularity
is a quotient singularity which admits a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Gorenstein smoothing
[
KSB88
, Def. 3.7]
. As shown in
[
KSB88
, Prop. 3.10]
, T-singularities are either rational double points (RDP) or cyclic quotient singularities of type
\frac{1}{dn^{2}}(1,dna-1)
with
0<a<n
and gcd
(a,n)=1
, where the dimension of its
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Gorenstein deformation space is
d
. A
Wahl singularity
is a non RDP T-singularity with
d=1

[
Wahl81
]
. Equivalently, a Wahl singularity is a cyclic quotient singularity which admits a smoothing whose Milnor number is equal to zero. In
[
Wahl81
]
, Wahl describes how to identify singularities
\frac{1}{n^{2}}(1,na-1)
with gcd
(a,n)=1
from its minimal resolution (see
[
KSB88
, Prop. 3.11]
), which we call
Wahl chain
:

(i)

[4]
is a Wahl chain,
(ii)
if
[b_{1},\ldots,b_{\ell}]
is a Wahl chain, then
[b_{1}+1,\ldots,b_{\ell},2]
and
[2,b_{1},\ldots,b_{\ell}+1]
are both Wahl chains, and
(iii)
any Wahl chain is obtained by starting with (i) and iterating (ii).

The discrepancies of Wahl singularities can be easily computed via a Fibonacci sort of algorithm (see e.g.
[
UV21
, 2.3]
), which also implies the following relation between the
index

n
of the singularity and its length
\ell
: If
\mathcal{F}_{i}
is the i-th Fibonacci number defined as
\mathcal{F}_{-1}=\mathcal{F}_{0}=1
and
\mathcal{F}_{i}=\mathcal{F}_{i-1}+\mathcal{F}_{i-2}
, then
n\leq\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
. Equality holds if and only if the Wahl chain is
[3,\ldots,3,5,3,\ldots,3,2]
or
[2,3,\ldots,3,5,3,\ldots,3]
.

We will abbreviate the information of a normal projective surface
X
with only Wahl singularities and a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Gorenstein smoothing of
X

{\mathbb{D}}
as a

, whose precise definition is as follows (see

, Section 2]
).

Definition 2.1
.

A

is a normal projective surface
X
together with a proper deformation
(X\subset{\mathcal{X}})\to(0\in{\mathbb{D}})
such that

(1)

X
has at most Wahl singularities.

(2)

{\mathcal{X}}
is a normal complex
3
-fold with
K_{{\mathcal{X}}}

{\mathbb{Q}}
-Cartier.

(3)

The fiber
X_{0}
is reduced and isomorphic to
X
.

(4)

The fiber
X_{t}
is nonsingular for
t\neq 0
.

The W-surface is said to be

if
X
is nonsingular.

Remark 2.2
.

One could have also considered more general smoothings
(X\subset{\mathcal{X}})\to(0\in{\mathbb{D}})
where
X
has just quotient singularities. But we can always replace that situation with a W-surface via base change, simultaneous resolutions, P-resolutions, and M-resolutions (see
[
HTU17
, Lemma 5.2]
). Hence

-Gorenstein smoothings of surfaces with only Wahl singularities are the key deformations in this type of degenerations.

For a W-surface the invariants

,

,

,

remain constant for every

. A W-surface is
minimal
if

is nef, and so

is nef for all

. If a W-surface is not minimal, then we can run explicitly the MMP relative to
{\mathbb{D}}

[
HTU17
]
, arriving to a minimal model or other outcomes as explained in
[

, Sect. 2]
. When

is nef and big, the canonical model of
(X\subset{\mathcal{X}})\to(0\in{\mathbb{D}})
has only T-singularities (RDP and

are both possible of course). For details we refer to
[
U16a
, Sect. 2]
and
[
U16b
, Sect. 2 and 3]
.

We now explore closely the situation
p_{g}=1
,

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
X
be a minimal singular W-surface (indeed singular) with
p_{g}(X)=1
and
q(X)=0
. Let
\phi\colon\tilde{X}\to X
be its minimal resolution, and let
\pi\colon\tilde{X}\to S
be a composition of blow-ups so that

has no
(-1)
-curves. Then
p_{g}(S)=1
,
q(S)=0
,
\pi(\exc(\phi))
is a nonempty configuration of rational curves, and
S
is one of the following:

(1)

A K3 surface.

(2)

A Kodaira dimension
1
elliptic surface
S\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
with at least one multiple fiber. If moreover
K_{X}
is ample, then
\exc(\phi)
contains some multiple section of
S\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
.

(3)

A surface of general type with
K_{S}^{2}<K_{X}^{2}
.

Proof.

In
[
RU19
, Prop.2.3]
we have a general analogue statement for
X
with
1
Wahl singularity, here we may have many. As Wahl singularities are rational, we have that
p_{g}
is preserved, and so
S
cannot be a ruled surface,
p_{g}(S)=1
, and
q(S)=0
. By the Enriques’ classification, we know that
S
is either a K3 surface, a Kodaira dimension
1
surface, or a surface of general type. By
[
K92
, Lemma 2.4]
, if
S
is of general type, the
K_{S}^{2}<K_{X}^{2}
.

We can write

\phi^{*}(K_{X})\equiv\pi^{*}(K_{S})+\sum_{i}\beta_{i}E_{i}+\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}C_{j}

where
E_{i}
are exceptional curves of
\pi
,
C_{j}
are exceptional curves of
\phi
,
\beta_{i}
are positive integers, and
\alpha_{j}
are minus the discrepancies of
C_{j}
, and so positive rational numbers in
(0,1)
. If
\pi(\exc(\phi))
had no curves, then the
C_{j}
would all be contained in the
E_{i}
. By Mumford’s criterion (cf.
[
BHPV04
, (2.1)Theorem, III]
), we would have
0\leq K_{X}^{2}=K_{S}^{2}-N
where
N
is a positive rational number. Hence we would obtain a contradiction if
\kappa(S)\leq 1
, since
K_{S}^{2}=0
. For
S
of general type, we would obtain
0\leq K_{X}^{2}=K_{S}^{2}-N<K_{X}^{2}-N
which is a contradiction. Therefore
\pi(\exc(\phi))
is a nonempty configuration of rational curves in
S
.

Finally let
\kappa(S)=1
. By the canonical class formula (cf.
[
BHPV04
, (12.3)Corollary, V]
), we can say that
K_{S}\sim\sum_{i}(m_{i}-1)F_{i}
where
m_{i}F_{i}
are the multiple fibers of
S\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
(with multiplicities
m_{i}>1
). As
K_{S}
is not trivial, there must be multiple fibers. If
K_{X}
is in addition ample, then we must have a multiple section in
\pi(\exc(\phi))
, since otherwise we obtain a zero curve for
K_{X}
by pulling back a general fiber.
∎

In
[
K92
, Sect. 4]
, Kawamata characterizes the central singular fiber of a minimal W-surface with general fiber of Kodaira dimension
0
or
1
. As an example, we can start with a K3 surface

with an elliptic fibration

which has a

. As in
[

, Sect. 4]
, we can construct any given Wahl chain from successive blow-ups over the node of

, and then contract it to a surface

. One can easily prove that

has no-local-to-global obstructions to deform (see below),

is nef, and the general fiber of a

-Gorenstein smoothing is a Kodaira dimension

). In comparison, the situation when the smooth fiber is of general type is probably impossible to classify.

One can construct examples for each of the cases in Proposition
2.3
. For instance, we will see many from K3 surfaces in this paper. For Kodaira dimensions

Remark 2.4
.

If moreover the general fiber

in Proposition

If we fix the

for W-surfaces

, and so we will attempt classification in the case of

,

, and

]
a broad classification for

\ell\leq 4K^{2}+1.

This is an improvement of the bound

Let

,

We now consider the smallest case

[

Remark 2.7
.

When we have a W-surface with many Wahl singularities and no-local-to-global obstructions to deform, then in particular there are W-surfaces with one singularity, and we are in Theorem
2.6
. This is done via partial
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Gorenstein smoothings. But if we do have obstructions, then those singularities may not even appear in the list of Theorem
2.6
. On the other hand, in terms of only lengths, we know that in general
\ell\leq 5
for this case, and so we may have all Wahl chains up to length
5
via obstructed surfaces with two or more Wahl singularities. At the end of this section we will illustrate what we know.

Classification of W-surfaces for higher
K^{2}
is much harder. At the same time, much less is known about the corresponding moduli spaces. For example, the moduli space of
p_{g}=1
,
q=0
surfaces with
K^{2}=2
is not known completely. As it was said in the introduction, it can be showed that the torsion of the Picard group is either
\{0\}
or
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
. For the latter, Catanese and Debarre
[
CD80
]
proved that the moduli space of such surfaces is a rational irreducible variety of dimension
16
, and these surfaces have
\pi_{1}={\mathbb{Z}}/2
. They also worked out various properties for torsion zero surfaces, including an irreducible
16
dimensional component of the moduli space, but it is still open if this component is indeed the moduli of all torsion zero surfaces. For
p_{g}=1
,
q=0
surfaces with
K^{2}=3
much less is known, the torsion of the Picard group has order at most
4
, and the ones with torsion
{\mathbb{Z}}/3
are described in
[
M03
]
. All what is known for simply-connected
p_{g}=1
surfaces of general type with
K^{2}\geq 3
are the examples in
[
PPS13
]
.

We now show how to construct W-surfaces from nodal configurations (not necessarily SNC but only nodes as singularities) of rational curves in K3 surfaces. After that, we end this section applying Proposition
2.8
to construct most of the situations in Theorem
2.6
.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
S
be a K3 surface, and let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a nodal configuration of
r
rational curves in
S
. Assume there is a composition of blow-ups
\pi\colon\tilde{X}\to S
over some nodes and infinitely near nodes of
{\mathcal{A}}
(considering also nodes in irreducible curves), so that
\pi^{*}({\mathcal{A}})
contains disjoint Wahl Chains which are contracted to a surface
X
. Then the dimension of
H^{2}(X,T_{X})
is
r
minus the dimension of the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-vector space generated by
{\mathcal{A}}
in the Néron-Severi group of
S
. In particular, if
{\mathcal{A}}
is linearly independent, then there are no-local-to-global obstructions to deform
X
.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}=\{C_{1},\ldots,C_{r}\}
. Let
\pi^{\prime}\colon S^{\prime}\to S
be the blow-up of all nodes in the irreducible curves
C_{i}
, and define
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}=\{C^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,C^{\prime}_{r}\}
via their proper transforms. Let
E_{1},\ldots,E_{s}
be the exceptional
(-1)
-curves of
\pi^{\prime}
, and let
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime\prime}={\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}+\sum E_{i}
. By
[
EV92
, 2.3(c)]
, we have the exact sequence

0\to\Omega_{S^{\prime}}^{1}(\log{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime\prime})\to\Omega_{S^{\prime}}^{1}(\log{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime})\otimes\Big(\sum E_{i}\Big)\to\bigoplus_{E_{i}}\Omega_{E_{i}}^{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime\prime}|_{E_{i}}\to 0.

Since
K_{S^{\prime}}\sim\sum E_{i}
, by Serre’s duality we have

H^{0}(S^{\prime},\Omega_{S^{\prime}}^{1}(\log({\mathcal{A}}^{\prime\prime})))=H^{2}(S^{\prime},T_{S^{\prime}}(-\log{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime\prime})),

because the degree of
\Omega_{E_{i}}^{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime\prime}|{E_{i}}
is
-1
(here we are using that we resolve only nodes). We now consider the residue sequence
[
EV92
, 2.3(a)]

0\to\Omega_{S^{\prime}}^{1}\to\Omega_{S^{\prime}}^{1}(\log{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime\prime})\to\bigoplus_{\Gamma\in{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime\prime}}{\mathcal{O}}_{\Gamma}\to 0.

As
H^{0}(S^{\prime},\Omega_{S^{\prime}}^{1})=0
, we have the exact sequence of morphisms in cohomology

0\to H^{0}(S^{\prime},\Omega_{S^{\prime}}^{1}(\log{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime\prime}))\to\bigoplus_{\Gamma\in{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime\prime}}H^{0}(\Gamma,{\mathcal{O}}_{\Gamma})\to H^{1}(S^{\prime},\Omega_{S^{\prime}}^{1}),

where the last morphism is the first Chern class map, giving as images the classes of
\Gamma
in the Néron-Severi group of
S^{\prime}
. We now note that the dimension of
H^{0}(S^{\prime},\Omega_{S^{\prime}}^{1}(\log{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime\prime}))
is
r
minus the dimension of the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-vector space generated by
{\mathcal{A}}
in the Néron-Severi group of
S
, as the exceptional curves
E_{i}
are independent.

Let
\pi^{\prime\prime}\colon\tilde{X}\to S^{\prime}
be the rest of the blow-ups to reach
\pi\colon\tilde{X}\to S
. We start with
H^{2}(S^{\prime},T_{S^{\prime}}(-\log{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime\prime}))
. From here, we maintain the dimension if we either erase at any step a
(-1)
-curve transversal to the rest of the configuration (e.g. any
E_{i}
), or we add
(-1)
-curves from blow-ups at the nodes of the configuration (cf.
[
LP07
]
). Therefore, if
{\mathcal{A}}_{j}
are the Wahl chains in
\pi^{*}({\mathcal{A}})
to be contracted to
X
, then
H^{2}(S^{\prime},T_{S^{\prime}}(-\log{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime\prime}))\simeq H^{2}(\tilde{X},T_{\tilde{X}}(-\log\sum{\mathcal{A}}_{j}))
. On the other hand, by
[
LP07
, Theorem 2]
, we have
H^{2}(X,T_{X})\simeq H^{2}(\tilde{X},T_{\tilde{X}}(-\log\sum{\mathcal{A}}_{j}))
, and so we have what is claimed. If
{\mathcal{A}}
is linearly independent, then
H^{2}(X,T_{X})=0
and every local deformation of its singularities may be globalized
[
LP07
, Prop. 4]
.
∎

3.
Rational configurations in K3 surfaces

In the construction of W-surfaces with ample canonical class, i.e. surfaces
X
with only Wahl singularities and
K_{X}
ample that have a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Gorenstein smoothing, we typically start with a special configuration of rational curves
{\mathcal{A}}
in some minimal surface
S
. We then consider particular blow-ups
\pi\colon\tilde{X}\to S
, so that the pull-back of
{\mathcal{A}}
contains Wahl chains which are contracted to obtain a singular surface
X
. Let
\phi\colon\tilde{X}\to X
be the contraction of the chains. When we see this backwards, what we really are thinking is that
{\mathcal{A}}
is the image of
\exc(\phi)
by
\pi
. This is a delicate operation, since in addition we want
K_{X}
ample, and the existence of
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Gorenstein smoothings.
Which pairs
(S,{\mathcal{A}})
exactly occur after fixing invariants?
This is the geographical question that motivates this section in the case of
p_{g}=1
,
q=0
surfaces of general type, starting with a K3 surface
S
and a simple normal crossings configuration of
(-2)
-curves
{\mathcal{A}}
. This particular situation is enough for our purposes. In a sequel work we will consider more general arrangements.

Let
S
be a K3 surface which contains a simple normal crossings configuration
{\mathcal{A}}
of
r

(-2)
-curves. Let
t_{2}
be the number of nodes in
{\mathcal{A}}
. Let
\Omega_{S}^{1}(\log{\mathcal{A}})
be the logarithmic differentials on
S
with simple poles at
{\mathcal{A}}
. It is a rank
2
locally free sheaf, whose Chern classes define the log Chern numbers

\bar{c}_{1}^{2}:={\mathcal{A}}^{2}=2t_{2}-2r\ \ \ \ \ \ \bar{c}_{2}:=\chi_{top}(S)-\chi_{top}({\mathcal{A}})=24+t_{2}-2r.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let us assume that the process above works, i.e from a configuration
{\mathcal{A}}
of
r

(-2)
-curves we construct an
X
with
K_{X}
ample and
P
Wahl singularities. Then

\bar{c}_{1}^{2}=2K_{X}^{2}\ \ \ \ \ \ \bar{c}_{2}=24-P-K_{X}^{2},

and

K_{X}^{2}\leq 14-\frac{1}{5}(3P-2).

In this way, we have
r=P+2K_{X}^{2}
and
t_{2}=3K_{X}^{2}+P
. The number of nodes in
{\mathcal{A}}
to be blown-up is
P+K_{X}^{2}
.

Proof.

Let us first consider an arbitrary SNC configuration of smooth rational curves
{\mathcal{A}}=\{C_{1},\ldots,C_{r}\}
in a surface
S
with
t_{2}
nodes as singularities. Let us define

P({\mathcal{A}}):=\sum_{i=1}^{r}C_{i}^{2}+5r-2t_{2}\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ K({\mathcal{A}}):=K_{S}^{2}+2r-t_{2}-P({\mathcal{A}}).

Let
\sigma\colon S^{\prime}\to S
be the blow-up at a node of
{\mathcal{A}}
, let
E
be the exceptional
(-1)
-curve, and let
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}\subset S^{\prime}
be the proper transform of
{\mathcal{A}}
. Then an easy computation shows that
P({\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}+E)=P({\mathcal{A}})
and
K({\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}+E)=K({\mathcal{A}})
. At the same time, if
E
is a
(-1)
-curve in
{\mathcal{A}}
which intersects
{\mathcal{A}}-E
at only two points, then
P({\mathcal{A}}-E)=P({\mathcal{A}})
and
K({\mathcal{A}}-E)=K({\mathcal{A}})
.

On the other hand, if
\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{s}\}
is a Wahl chain, then

\sum_{i=1}^{s}A_{i}^{2}=-3s-1.

Hence if
{\mathcal{A}}
is the disjoint union of
P
Wahl chains of lengths
\ell_{i}
in a surface
\tilde{X}
and
X
is the contraction of the chains, then
P({\mathcal{A}})=P
and
K({\mathcal{A}})=K_{\tilde{X}}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{P}\ell_{i}=K_{X}^{2}
.

Now let
{\mathcal{A}}\subset S
be the SNC configuration of
(-2)
-curves in the K3 surface
S
. In the process of constructing the Wahl chains in
\tilde{X}
through the composition of blow-ups
\pi\colon\tilde{X}\to S
we can only blow-up at nodes and subsequent nodes of proper transforms of
{\mathcal{A}}
, since otherwise we would violate the ampleness of
K_{X}
. This is because, by blowing up away from the nodes we would obtain a
(-1)
-curve that intersects transversally the Wahl chains in at most one point, and so the image of that curve in
X
would be negative for
K_{X}
. As
P({\mathcal{A}})
and
K({\mathcal{A}})
remain invariants by adding or subtracting exceptional
(-1)
-curves, we obtain that

P({\mathcal{A}})=P
and
K({\mathcal{A}})=K_{X}^{2}
.

But we know that
\bar{c}_{1}^{2}=2t_{2}-2r
, and
\bar{c}_{2}=24+t_{2}-2r
, and so we obtain
\bar{c}_{1}^{2}=2K_{X}^{2}
and
\bar{c}_{2}=24-P-K_{X}^{2}
.

To show the inequality
K_{X}^{2}\leq 14-\frac{1}{5}(3P-2)
we directly apply
[
L03
, Theorem 0.1]
to the log canonical pair
(S,{\mathcal{A}})
, since
K_{S}+{\mathcal{A}}={\mathcal{A}}
is effective.
∎

We briefly remark that typically the divisor
K_{S}+{\mathcal{A}}={\mathcal{A}}
is nef, big and ample modulo curves in
{\mathcal{A}}
. So, in that case we can say by
[
TY87
, Thm. 3.1]
that
K_{X}^{2}=14-\frac{1}{5}(3P-2)
if and only if
S\setminus{\mathcal{A}}
is a ball quotient, and this is not possible as
{\mathcal{A}}
would need to be a disjoint union of exceptional divisors of quotients of elliptic singularities.

The reader may be wondering why do we not just consider the situation
(\tilde{X},\exc(\phi))
to compute the log Chern numbers and the corresponding inequality. The reason is that we obtain weaker invariants. In that case,
\bar{c}_{1}^{2}=K_{X}^{2}-P
,
\bar{c}_{2}=24-K_{X}^{2}-P
, and so
K_{X}^{2}\leq 18-\frac{1}{2}P
by the Langer-BMY inequality
[
L03
, Theorem 0.1]
.

We point out that the analogue situation to Proposition

Therefore, with one or two Wahl singularities we may get

is

.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
S
be a K3 surface, and let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a SNC configuration of
(-2)
-curves. Assume there is a composition of blow-ups
\pi\colon\tilde{X}\to S
so that the pull-back of

contains disjoint Wahl Chains. Let
\phi\colon\tilde{X}\to X
be the contraction of them. Let
\exc(\phi)=\sum_{i}C_{i}
the decomposition as sum of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
(i.e. the sum of the curves in the Wahl chains), and let

be the discrepancy of
C_{i}
. Assume
{\mathcal{A}}=\pi(\exc(\phi))
. Then, we have

(n)

K_{X}
is nef if and only if for any
(-1)
-curve
\Gamma
in
\tilde{X}
– thus contained in
\pi^{*}({\mathcal{A}})
– we have
-1\geq d_{i}+d_{j}
, where
\Gamma\cdot C_{i}=\Gamma\cdot C_{j}=1
.

(a)

K_{X}
is ample if and only if

, for any

-curve in
\tilde{X}
we have
-1>d_{i}+d_{j}
, where
\Gamma\cdot C_{i}=\Gamma\cdot C_{j}=1
, and there are no
(-2)
-curves disjoint from
\pi^{*}({\mathcal{A}})
.

Moreover, if we satisfy case
(n)
,
K_{X}^{2}>0
, and
X
has
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Gorenstein smoothings, then we can find the canonical model of
X
by contracting all
(-1)
-curves in
\tilde{X}
with
-1=d_{i}+d_{j}
, and all
(-2)
-curves disjoint from
\pi^{*}({\mathcal{A}})
. The resulting surface has only T-singularities.

Proof.

Let
K_{\tilde{X}}\sim\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}E_{i}
where
E_{i}
are the proper transforms of each of the blow-ups from
\pi
, and
\alpha_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
. We know that numerically
K_{\tilde{X}}\equiv\phi^{*}(K_{X})+\sum_{i}d_{i}C_{i}
. We recall that
-1<d_{i}<0
. Therefore
\phi^{*}(K_{X})
is a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-effective divisor, supported on the
C_{i}
and the
E_{j}
(which of course may coincide). Hence a necessary and sufficient condition for

to be nef is that
\phi^{*}(K_{X})\cdot E_{i}\geq 0
for all

.

We claim first that
\pi
can only blow-up at nodes of
{\mathcal{A}}
and infinitely near nodes, since otherwise there would be a

-curve intersecting
\exc(\phi)
transversally in at most one point, so

would not be nef. For the same reason, the only curves

not equal to some

are the
(-1)
-curves. Therefore, we only need to verify

at the

which are
(-1)
-curves, and this is
-1\geq d_{i}+d_{j}
. We do not have more than two intersections because

is a SNC divisor.

for all

-curves, and any zero curve for

(not a

) to be disjoint from
\sum_{i}E_{i}+\sum_{j}C_{j}
. Let
X\to X_{can}
be the canonical model of
X
given by
|mK_{X}|
for

. Then we have that any zero curve is a
(-2)
-curve, as they do not pass through the singularities of
X
.

In case of (n),
K_{X}^{2}>0
, and
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Gorenstein smoothing, we have that the canonical model of the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Gorenstein smoothing has as special fiber the canonical model of
X
with only T-singularities, as shown in
[

, Prop. 2.7]
.
∎

∎

In particular, to find surfaces

as in Corollary

. In addition, the K3 surface must have Picard number at least

Remark 3.5
.

In fact, say that the surface

in Corollary

has

ample, and only one Wahl singularity. The configuration
{\mathcal{A}}
of

(-2)
-curves defines a sub-lattice

(S)

Proposition 3.6
.

Let

be a W-surface. Let

be the minimal resolution of

, and so

-fold family relative to

-Gorenstein deformation is isomorphic to

The computation of

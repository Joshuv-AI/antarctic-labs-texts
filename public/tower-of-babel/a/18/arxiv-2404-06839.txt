On the radially deformed Fourier transform
Hendrik De Bie, Ze Yang

Abstract
. In this paper we consider the kernel of the radially deformed Fourier transform introduced in the context of Clifford analysis in [ 11 ] . By adapting the Laplace transform method from [ 5 ] , we obtain the Laplace domain expressions of the kernel for the cases of m = 2 m=2 and m > 2 m>2 when 1 + c = 1 n , n ∈ ℕ 0 \ { 1 } 1+c=\frac{1}{n},n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\backslash\{1\} with n n odd. Moreover, we show that the expressions can be simplified using the Poisson kernel and the generating function of the Gegenbauer polynomials. As a consequence, the inverse formulas are used to get the integral expressions of the kernel in terms of Mittag-Leffler functions.

Introduction

Clifford analysis
[
2
,
12
,
17

is a refinement of harmonic analysis in the
m

\mathbb{R}^{m}
. The orthogonal Clifford algebra
\mathcal{C}\ell_{0,m}
over
\mathbb{R}^{m}
is generated by the canonical basis
e_{i}

i=1,\dots,m
. These generators

e_{i}e_{j}+e_{j}e_{i}=-2\delta_{ij},\ 1\leq i,\,j\leq m
. The
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}

\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)

\partial_{\underline{x}}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\partial_{x_{j}}e_{j}
and its Fourier symbol
\underline{x}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}e_{i}x_{i}

and

the Euler operator and we have put

in the operator studied in
[
10

1.1

[
11
]

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we give a brief overview of the radially deformed Fourier transform, special functions and the Laplace transform.

In
[
11
]
, the kernel
K_{m}^{c}
of the deformed Fourier transform (
1.2
) is given by

(2.1)

K_{m}^{c}=\frac{1}{2\lambda}z^{-\frac{\mu-2}{2}}A_{\lambda}+\frac{1}{2}z^{-\frac{\mu-2}{2}}B_{\lambda}-z^{-\frac{\mu}{2}}(\underline{x}\wedge\underline{y})\,C_{\lambda}

with

(2.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}A_{\lambda}&=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}(k+\lambda)\,\left(\alpha_{k}\,{J}_{\frac{\gamma_{k}}{2}-1}(z)-i\,\alpha_{k-1}\,{J}_{\frac{\gamma_{k-1}}{2}}(z)\right)C_{k}^{\lambda}(w),\\
B_{\lambda}&=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\left(\alpha_{k}\,{J}_{\frac{\gamma_{k}}{2}-1}(z)+i\,\alpha_{k-1}\,{J}_{\frac{\gamma_{k-1}}{2}}(z)\right)C_{k}^{\lambda}(w),\\
C_{\lambda}&=\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\left(\alpha_{k}\,{J}_{\frac{\gamma_{k}}{2}-1}(z)+i\,\alpha_{k-1}\,{J}_{\frac{\gamma_{k-1}}{2}}(z)\right)C_{k-1}^{\lambda+1}(w),\end{split}

where
\underline{x}\wedge\underline{y}:=\sum_{j<k}e_{j}e_{k}\,(x_{j}\,y_{k}-x_{k}\,y_{j})
is the wedge product of the vectors
\underline{x}
and
\underline{y},\,\underline{z}=|x||y|,\,w=\frac{\langle x,\,y\rangle}{z},\,\lambda=\frac{m-2}{2},\,\mu=1+\frac{m-1}{1+c},\,\alpha_{k}=e^{-\frac{i\pi k}{2(1+c)}},\,\alpha_{-1}=0,\,\gamma_{k}=\frac{2}{1+c}\left(k+\frac{m-2}{2}\right)+\frac{c+2}{1+c}
and
c>-1
.
{J}_{\nu}
denotes the Bessel function and
C_{k}^{\lambda}
the Gegenbauer polynomial.

In
[
9
]
, the authors obtained explicit formulas for the above kernel when the dimension is even and
1+c=\frac{1}{n},n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\backslash\{1\}
with
n
odd by adapting the method developed in
[
7
]
.

When
m=2
, the kernel was given explicitly in the following theorem:

Theorem 2.1
.

[
9
]

If
m=2\,(\lambda=0)
and
1+\frac{1}{c}=\frac{1}{n},n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\backslash\{1\}
with
n
odd, then the kernel of the deformed Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}_{D}=e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{m-1}{2(1+c)}\right)}e^{\frac{-i\pi}{4(1+c)^{2}}\left({\bf D}^{2}-(1+c)^{2}\underline{x}^{2}\right)}
takes the form

\displaystyle K_{2}^{c}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}i^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\bigg\{z^{\frac{1-n}{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\cos{\left(\left(\frac{n-1}{2}\right)\left(\frac{\theta+2\pi j}{n}\right)\right)e^{-iz\cos{\left(\frac{\theta+2\pi j}{n}\right)}}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{\sin{\theta}}z^{-\frac{n+1}{2}}(\underline{x}\wedge\underline{y})\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\sin{\left(\left(\frac{n-1}{2}\right)\left(\frac{\theta+2\pi j}{n}\right)\right)e^{-iz\cos{\left(\frac{\theta+2\pi j}{n}\right)}}}\bigg\}

with
\theta=\arccos{w},\,z=|x||y|,\,w=\frac{\langle x,y\rangle}{z}
.

The kernel in even dimensions
m>2
was derived as iterated derivatives of Theorem
2.1
using the recursion relations obtained in
[
11
]
.
These iterated derivatives of the kernel can in principle be computed recursively.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\lambda>-1/2
, the Gegenbauer polynomials are defined as

C_{n}^{\lambda}(w)=\sum_{j=0}^{\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor}(-1)^{j}\frac{\Gamma(n-j-\lambda)}{\Gamma(\lambda)\,j!\,(n-2j)!}\,(2w)^{n-2j}.

Next, we need the following expansion of the Poisson kernel for the unit ball in terms of Gegenbauer polynomials.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
14
]

For
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
|y|\leq|x|=1
, the Poisson kernel for the unit ball is

\displaystyle P(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1-|y|^{2}}{|x-y|^{m}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-|y|^{2}}{\left(1-2\xi|y|+|y|^{2}\right)^{m/2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{j+m/2-1}{m/2-1}\,C_{j}^{m/2-1}(\xi)\,|y|^{j},\quad\xi=\langle x,\frac{y}{|y|}\rangle.

This result can be extended for
\lambda>0
, we have

(2.3)

\displaystyle\frac{1-|y|^{2}}{\left(1-2\xi|y|+|y|^{2}\right)^{\lambda+1}}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{j+\lambda}{\lambda}\,C_{j}^{\lambda}(\xi)\,|y|^{j}.

It is still valid for
z\in\mathbb{C},\,|z|<1
and
|\xi|<1,
(see
[
20
]
)

(2.4)

\displaystyle\frac{1-z^{2}}{\left(1-2\xi z+z^{2}\right)^{\lambda+1}}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{j+\lambda}{\lambda}\,C_{j}^{\lambda}(\xi)\,z^{j}.

Remark 2.3
.

The validity of the analytic continuation of (
2.3
) to (
2.4
) for the whole unit disk has been proved in
[
5
]
.

The generating function of the Gegenbauer polynomials (see e.g.
[
15
]
, p.177 (29)) is given by

(2.5)

\frac{1}{(1-2xt+t^{2})^{\alpha}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}C_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x)\,t^{n}

for
0\leq|x|<1,\,|t|\leq 1,\,\alpha>0
.

Suppose that
f
is a real or complex valued function of the variable
t>0
and
s
is a complex parameter. The (one-sided) Laplace transform of
f
is defined as (see e.g.
[
22
]
)

(2.6)

F(s)=\mathcal{L}\left(f(t)\right)=\int_{0}^{\infty}{e^{-st}f(t)\,\mathrm{d}t}.

By Lerch’s theorem, if we restrict to continuous functions on
[0,\infty)
, the inverse transform is uniquely determined

\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left(F(s)\right)=f(t).

In the paper, we will need the following Laplace transform formula from
[
16
]
:

(2.7)

\mathcal{L}(e^{-\alpha t})=\frac{1}{s+\alpha},\quad{\rm Re}\,s>{\rm Re}\,\alpha.

The Bessel function of the first kind is given by the infinite power series

(2.8)

J_{\nu}(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!\ \Gamma(n+\nu+1)}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{2n+\nu}.

For
{\rm Re}\,s>|{\rm Im}\,b|
, we have the Laplace transform of (
2.8
)

(2.9)

\mathcal{L}\left({J}_{\nu}(b\,t)\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{s^{2}+b^{2}}}\left(\frac{b}{s+\sqrt{s^{2}+b^{2}}}\right)^{\nu},\quad{\rm Re}\,\nu>-1.

We further introduce definitions of two Mittag-Leffler functions in
[
19
,
18
]
.

Definition 2.4
.

The two-parametric Mittag-Leffler function is defined by

(2.10)

E_{\alpha,\beta}(z):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{\Gamma(\alpha n+\beta)},\quad\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C},{\rm Re}\,\alpha>0,{\rm Re}\,\beta>0.

Definition 2.5
.

The Prabhakar generalized Mittag-Leffler function is

(2.11)

E^{\delta}_{\alpha,\beta}(z):=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\frac{(\delta)_{n}\,z^{n}}{\Gamma(\alpha n+\beta)\,n!},

where
\alpha,\beta,\delta\in\mathbb{C}
with
{\rm Re}\,\alpha>0
.

Remark 2.6
.

When
\delta=1
, we have
E^{1}_{\alpha,\beta}(z)=E_{\alpha,\beta}(z)
(see e.g.
[
19
]
, (2.3.3)).

The Laplace transform of the Prabhakar function (
2.11
) is

(2.12)

\mathcal{L}\left(t^{\beta-1}E^{\delta}_{\alpha,\beta}(b\,t^{\alpha})\right)=\frac{1}{s^{\beta}}\frac{1}{(1-b\,s^{-\alpha})^{\delta}},

where
{\rm Re}\,\alpha>0,{\rm Re}\,\beta>0,{\rm Re}\,s>0
and
s>|b|^{1/({\rm Re}\,\alpha)}
, see
[
19
]
.

We will also need the following inverse transform in
[
21
]
, for
{\rm Re}\,\nu>-1,\ {\rm Re}\,s>|{\rm Im}\,a|
,

(2.13)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left(\frac{\left(\sqrt{s^{2}+a^{2}}-s\right)^{\nu}}{\sqrt{s^{2}+a^{2}}}F\left(\sqrt{s^{2}+a^{2}}-s\right)\right)

\displaystyle=(a^{2}\,t)^{\nu/2}\int_{0}^{t}(t+2\tau)^{-\nu/2}J_{\nu}(a\sqrt{t^{2}+2\tau t})\,f(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau,

where
\mathcal{L}(f(t))=F(s)
.
We also need the convolution formula of the Laplace transform. Denoting
\mathcal{L}(g(t))=G(s)
and
\mathcal{L}(f(t))=F(s)
, we have

(2.14)

\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left(F(s)\,G(s)\right)=\int_{0}^{t}f(\tau)\,g(t-\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

3.
The kernel in the Laplace domain

3.1.
The dimension
m=2

In
[
9
]
, when
m=2
, i.e.
\lambda=0
, the kernel of the deformed Fourier transform in case of
1+c=\frac{1}{n},n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\backslash\{1\}
with
n
odd and
n=2\tilde{n}+1,\tilde{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
takes the form

\displaystyle K_{2}^{c}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}z^{\frac{c}{2(1+c)}}\lim_{\lambda\to 0}\frac{1}{\lambda}A_{\lambda}+\frac{1}{2}z^{\frac{c}{2(1+c)}}\lim_{\lambda\to 0}B_{\lambda}-z^{-\frac{2+c}{2(1+c)}}(\underline{x}\wedge\underline{y})\,\lim_{\lambda\to 0}C_{\lambda}

with

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to 0}\frac{1}{\lambda}A_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=-J_{\tilde{n}}(z)+2\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}(-i)^{kn}J_{kn+\tilde{n}}(z)\cos{(k\theta)}

\displaystyle\quad+2(-1)^{\tilde{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}(-i)^{kn}J_{kn-\tilde{n}}(z)\cos{(k\theta)},\quad w=\cos{\theta};

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to 0}B_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=J_{\tilde{n}}(z);

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to 0}C_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sin{\theta}}\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}(-i)^{kn}J_{kn+\tilde{n}}(z)\sin{(k\theta)}

\displaystyle\quad-(-1)^{\tilde{n}}\frac{1}{\sin{\theta}}\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}(-i)^{kn}J_{kn-\tilde{n}}(z)\sin{(k\theta)},\quad w=\cos{\theta}.

Inspired by the Laplace transform method in
[
5
]
, we introduce an auxiliary variable
t
in the Bessel functions of the kernel.
For the scalar part of
K_{2}^{c}
, we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle K_{2,scal}^{c}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}z^{\frac{c}{2(1+c)}}\left(\lim_{\lambda\to 0}\frac{1}{\lambda}A_{\lambda}+\lim_{\lambda\to 0}B_{\lambda}\right)

\displaystyle=z^{-\tilde{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}(-i)^{kn}J_{kn+\tilde{n}}(z\,t)\cos{(k\theta)}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{\tilde{n}}z^{-\tilde{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}(-i)^{kn}J_{kn-\tilde{n}}(z\,t)\cos{(k\theta)},

and for the bivector part

(3.2)

\displaystyle K_{2,biv}^{c}

\displaystyle=-z^{-\frac{2+c}{2(1+c)}}\lim_{\lambda\to 0}C_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\sin{\theta}}z^{-\tilde{n}-1}\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}(-i)^{kn}J_{kn+\tilde{n}}(z\,t)\sin{(k\theta)}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{\tilde{n}}\frac{1}{\sin{\theta}}z^{-\tilde{n}-1}\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}(-i)^{kn}J_{kn-\tilde{n}}(z\,t)\sin{(k\theta)}.

Next, let us first consider the scalar part of the kernel. For
{\rm Re}\,s
big enough, we take the Laplace transform of the scalar part with respect to
t
by formula (
2.9
), which yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\left(K_{2,scal}^{c}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r}\left(\frac{1}{s+r}\right)^{\tilde{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}(-i)^{kn}\left(\frac{z}{s+r}\right)^{kn}\cos{(k\theta)}

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{\tilde{n}}z^{-2\tilde{n}}\frac{1}{r}\left(\frac{1}{s+r}\right)^{-\tilde{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}(-i)^{kn}\left(\frac{z}{s+r}\right)^{kn}\cos{(k\theta)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r}\left(\frac{1}{s+r}\right)^{\tilde{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}(-i)^{kn}\left(\frac{z}{s+r}\right)^{kn}\cos{(k\theta)}

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{\tilde{n}}z^{-2\tilde{n}}\frac{1}{r}\left(\frac{1}{s+r}\right)^{-\tilde{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}(-i)^{kn}\left(\frac{z}{s+r}\right)^{kn}\cos{(k\theta)}

\displaystyle\quad-(-1)^{\tilde{n}}z^{-2\tilde{n}}\frac{1}{r}\left(\frac{1}{s+r}\right)^{-\tilde{n}}

with
r=\sqrt{s^{2}+z^{2}}
,
\underline{z}=|x||y|
,
w=\frac{\langle x,\,y\rangle}{z}
. The validity of transforming term by term in (
3.1

Theorem 3.1
.

[
13
]

Let the function
F(s)
be represented by a series of
\mathcal{L}
-transforms

F(s)=\sum_{v=0}^{\infty}F_{v}(s),\quad F_{v}(s)=\mathcal{L}(f_{v}(t)),

where all integrals

\mathcal{L}(f_{v})=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-st}\,f_{v}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=F_{v}(s),\quad(v=0,1,\cdots)

converge in a common half-plane
{\rm Re}\,s\geq x_{0}
. Moreover, we require that the integrals

\mathcal{L}(|f_{v}|)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-st}|f_{v}(t)|\,\mathrm{d}t=G_{v},\quad(v=0,1,\cdots)

and the series

\sum_{v=0}^{\infty}G_{v}(x_{0})

converge which implies that
\sum_{v=0}^{\infty}F_{v}(s)
converges absolutely and uniformly in
{\rm Re}\,s\geq x_{0}
. Then
\sum_{v=0}^{\infty}f_{v}(t)
converges, absolutely, towards a function
f(t)
for all
t\geq 0
; this
f(t)
is the original function of
F(s)
;

\mathcal{L}\left(\sum_{v=0}^{\infty}f_{v}(t)\right)=\sum_{v=0}^{\infty}F_{v}(s).

Using the well-known relation (see for e.g.
[
23
]
, p. 136 (21.41)):

(3.3)

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}r^{k}\cos{kx}=\frac{1-r\cos{x}}{1-2r\cos{x}+r^{2}},\quad|r|<1,

we obtain the following expression for the Laplace transform of the scalar part of
K_{2}^{c}

(3.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\left(K_{2,scal}^{c}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r}\left(\frac{1}{s+r}\right)^{\tilde{n}}\frac{1-wu_{R}}{1-2wu_{R}+u_{R}^{2}}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{\tilde{n}}z^{-2\tilde{n}}\frac{1}{r}\left(\frac{1}{s+r}\right)^{-\tilde{n}}\frac{1-wu_{R}}{1-2wu_{R}+u_{R}^{2}}

\displaystyle-(-1)^{\tilde{n}}z^{-2\tilde{n}}\frac{1}{r}\left(\frac{1}{s+r}\right)^{-\tilde{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r}\left(\frac{1}{s+r}\right)^{\tilde{n}}\frac{1}{1-2wu_{R}+u_{R}^{2}}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{r}\left(\frac{1}{s+r}\right)^{\tilde{n}}\frac{wu_{R}}{1-2wu_{R}+u_{R}^{2}}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{\tilde{n}}z^{-2\tilde{n}}\frac{1}{r}\left(\frac{1}{s+r}\right)^{-\tilde{n}}\frac{wu_{R}}{1-2wu_{R}+u_{R}^{2}}

\displaystyle-(-1)^{\tilde{n}}z^{-2\tilde{n}}\frac{1}{r}\left(\frac{1}{s+r}\right)^{-\tilde{n}}\frac{u_{R}^{2}}{1-2wu_{R}+u_{R}^{2}}

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\left(K_{2,scal}^{c}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r}\frac{(s+r)^{n-\tilde{n}}+(-1)^{\tilde{n}}(r-s)^{n-\tilde{n}}}{{(s+r)^{n}-2w(-iz)^{n}+(-1)^{n}(r-s)^{n}}}

\displaystyle\quad-w(-i)^{n}\frac{z}{r}\frac{(r-s)^{\tilde{n}}-(-1)^{n}(s+r)^{\tilde{n}}}{{(s+r)^{n}-2w(-iz)^{n}+(-1)^{n}(r-s)^{n}}}.

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{\tilde{n}}\frac{1}{\sin{\theta}}z^{-2\tilde{n}-1}\frac{1}{r}\left(\frac{1}{s+r}\right)^{-\tilde{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}(-i)^{kn}\left(\frac{z}{s+r}\right)^{kn}\sin{(k\theta)}.

Proof.

We only prove the results for the case

Let us define the denominator and numerator polynomials of the kernel in Theorem

\displaystyle P_{n}(s)

\displaystyle:=(s+r)^{n}-2\omega(-iz)^{n}+(-1)^{n}(r-s)^{n},

\displaystyle Q_{\tilde{n}}(s)

\displaystyle:=\frac{(s+r)^{\tilde{n}+1}+(-1)^{\tilde{n}}(r-s)^{\tilde{n}+1}}{r},

\displaystyle O_{\tilde{n}-1}(s)

\displaystyle:=\frac{(-1)^{\tilde{n}}(r-s)^{\tilde{n}}-(s+r)^{\tilde{n}}}{r}.

(1)

We first consider the denominator
P_{n}(s)
,

\displaystyle P_{n}(s)

\displaystyle=(s+r)^{n}-2\omega(-iz)^{n}+(-1)^{n}\,(r-s)^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}s^{n-k}r^{k}-2\omega(-iz)^{n}+(-1)^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{n-k}\,s^{n-k}r^{k}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}s^{n-k}r^{k}\,(1+(-1)^{k})\right)-2\omega(-iz)^{n}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}\binom{n}{2k}s^{n-2k}\,(s^{2}+z^{2})^{k}-2\omega(-iz)^{n}.

This means that

is

.

Next, we verify
P_{n}(s_{l})=0
with
s_{l}=-iz\cos{\left(\frac{\theta+2\pi l}{n}\right)},\,l=0,\cdots,n-1
. Denote
w=\cos{(\theta)}=\frac{e^{i\theta}+e^{-i\theta}}{2}
. When
\sin{\left(\frac{\theta+2\pi l}{n}\right)}\geq 0

\displaystyle P_{n}(s_{l})

\displaystyle=(s_{l}+r_{l})^{n}-2\omega(-iz)^{n}+(-1)^{n}\,(r_{l}-s_{l})^{n}

\displaystyle=(-iz)^{n}\bigg[\left(\cos{\left(\frac{\theta+2\pi l}{n}\right)}+i\sin{\left(\frac{\theta+2\pi l}{n}\right)}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\cos{\left(\frac{\theta+2\pi l}{n}\right)}-i\sin{\left(\frac{\theta+2\pi l}{n}\right)}\right)^{n}-2\omega\bigg]

\displaystyle=(-iz)^{n}\left(e^{i\theta}-2\left(\frac{e^{i\theta}+e^{-i\theta}}{2}\right)+e^{-i\theta}\right)

\displaystyle=0.

where in the second step we have used Euler’s formula.
When
\sin{\left(\frac{\theta+2\pi l}{n}\right)}<0
we have
P_{n}(s_{j})=0
by a similar calculation.

Therefore,
s_{l},l=0,\ldots,n-1
are all roots of

and we get the factorization

\displaystyle P_{n}(s)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}\binom{n}{2k}\,\prod_{l=0}^{n-1}\left(s+iz\cos{\left(\frac{\theta+2\pi l}{n}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle=2^{n}\,\prod_{l=0}^{n-1}\left(s+iz\cos{\left(\frac{\theta+2\pi l}{n}\right)}\right).

(2)

For

in

\displaystyle=\frac{(s+r)^{\tilde{n}+1}+(-1)^{\tilde{n}}(r-s)^{\tilde{n}+1}}{r}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r}\bigg[\sum_{k=0}^{\tilde{n}+1}\binom{\tilde{n}+1}{k}s^{\tilde{n}+1-k}\,r^{k}

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{\tilde{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{\tilde{n}+1}\binom{\tilde{n}+1}{k}(-1)^{\tilde{n}+1-k}\,s^{\tilde{n}+1-k}\,r^{k}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r}\sum_{k=0}^{\tilde{n}+1}\binom{\tilde{n}+1}{k}\,s^{\tilde{n}+1-k}\,r^{k}\,(1-(-1)^{k})

\displaystyle=2\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor\frac{\tilde{n}+1}{2}\rfloor}\binom{\tilde{n}+1}{2k+1}\,s^{\tilde{n}-2k}\,(s^{2}+z^{2})^{k}.

The coefficient of

When

c_{1}=\frac{1}{2}(-1)^{n(\lambda+1)}
,
c_{2}=\frac{1}{2}(-1)^{n\lambda}
and
c_{3}=(-1)^{n(\lambda+1)}
.

It is now possible to present this result in a more compact form. Indeed, combining the equations in (
3.9
) and (
3.10
), one can see that the scalar part of the kernel
K_{m,scal}^{c},\,m>2
reduces to

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\left(K_{m,scal}^{c}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}\left(\frac{1}{2\lambda}z^{-\frac{\mu-2}{2}}A_{\lambda}\right)+\mathcal{L}\left(\frac{1}{2}z^{-\frac{\mu-2}{2}}B_{\lambda}\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n(\lambda+1)}\,z^{2\tilde{n}+2}\,\frac{1}{r}\frac{(r-s)^{n\lambda+\tilde{n}}}{\left((r-s)^{n}-b_{+}\right)^{\lambda+1}}\frac{1}{\left((r-s)^{n}-b_{-}\right)^{\lambda+1}}

\displaystyle\quad+i\,w\,(-1)^{n(\lambda+1)}\,(-1)^{\tilde{n}}\,z\,\frac{1}{r}\frac{(r-s)^{n\lambda+n+\tilde{n}}}{\left((r-s)^{n}-b_{+}\right)^{\lambda+1}}\frac{1}{\left((r-s)^{n}-b_{-}\right)^{\lambda+1}}

\displaystyle\quad-i\,w\,(-1)^{n(\lambda+1)}\,z^{n}\,\frac{1}{r}\,\frac{(r-s)^{n\lambda+n-\tilde{n}}}{\left((r-s)^{n}-b_{+}\right)^{\lambda+1}}\frac{1}{\left((r-s)^{n}-b_{-}\right)^{\lambda+1}}

\displaystyle\quad+i^{n-1}\,(-1)^{n(\lambda+1)}\,\frac{1}{r}\,\frac{(r-s)^{n\lambda+2n-\tilde{n}}}{\left((r-s)^{n}-b_{+}\right)^{\lambda+1}}\frac{1}{\left((r-s)^{n}-b_{-}\right)^{\lambda+1}}.

Adding the bivector part
K_{m,biv}^{c}=-z^{-\frac{\mu}{2}}C_{\lambda},\,m>2
in the above theorem and using (
2.13
), we thus conclude the results:

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
b_{\pm}:=e^{\pm i\theta}i^{n}z^{n}
and

\displaystyle h_{\lambda+1}(t)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{t}\zeta^{n(\lambda+1)-1}E^{\lambda+1}_{n,n(\lambda+1)}\left(b_{+}\,\zeta^{n}\right)(t-\zeta)^{n(\lambda+1)-1}E^{\lambda+1}_{n,n(\lambda+1)}(b_{-}(t-\zeta)^{n})\,\mathrm{d}\zeta.

Then for the dimension
m>2\,(\lambda>0)
and

,
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\backslash\ \{1\}
odd,

,

takes the form

K_{m}^{c}=K_{m,scal}^{c}+(\underline{x}\wedge\underline{y})\,K_{m,biv}^{c}

\displaystyle K_{m,scal}^{c}

\displaystyle=c_{3}\,z^{n\lambda+3\tilde{n}+2}\bigg(\int_{0}^{1}(1+2\tau)^{-(n\lambda+\tilde{n})/2}J_{n\lambda+\tilde{n}}\left(z\sqrt{1+2\tau}\right)h_{\lambda+1}(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\quad+i\,w\,(-1)^{\tilde{n}}\int_{0}^{1}(1+2\tau)^{-(n\lambda+n+\tilde{n})/2}J_{n\lambda+n+\tilde{n}}\left(z\sqrt{1+2\tau}\right)h_{\lambda+1}(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau

5.
Conclusions

The radially deformed Fourier transform is the Fourier transform associated with the radially deformed Dirac operator in the Clifford analysis setting. It was previously studied as one of several function theoretical aspects of the operator. In this paper we developed a new Laplace transform method for the kernel of the deformed Fourier transform for the case of
1+c=\frac{1}{n},n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\backslash\{1\}
with
n
odd. By introducing an auxiliary variable
t
in the series expansion of the kernel, the Laplace transform of the kernel was obtained. We further simplified these results by making use of the Poisson kernel and the generating function of the Gegenbauer polynomials. Then the rational functions in the kernels were given in partial fraction decomposition when the dimension
m
is even. Moreover, explicit and integral expressions of the kernel for all dimensions were given using the Laplace inversion of the Mittag-Leffler functions.

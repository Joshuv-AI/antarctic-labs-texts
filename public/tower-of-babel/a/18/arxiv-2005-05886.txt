Counting Query Answers over a DL-Lite Knowledge Base (extended version)
Diego Calvanese, Julien Corman, Davide Lanti and Simon Razniewski

Abstract
Counting answers to a query is an operation supported by virtually all database management systems. In this paper we focus on counting answers over a Knowledge Base (KB), which may be viewed as a database enriched with background knowledge about the domain under consideration. In particular, we place our work in the context of Ontology-Mediated Query Answering/Ontology-based Data Access (OMQA/OBDA), where the language used for the ontology is a member of the DL-Lite family and the data is a (usually virtual) set of assertions. We study the data complexity of query answering, for different members of the DL-Lite family that include number restrictions, and for variants of conjunctive queries with counting that differ with respect to their shape (connected, branching, rooted). We improve upon existing results by providing P and co NP lower bounds, and upper bounds in P and LogSpace . For the LogSpace case, we have devised a novel query rewriting technique into first-order logic with counting.

Introduction

Counting answers to a query is an essential operation in data management,
and is supported
by virtually every database management system.
In this paper, we focus on counting answers over a Knowledge Base (KB),
which may be viewed as a database (DB) enriched with background knowledge about the
domain of interest.
In such a setting,
counting may take into account
two types of information:
grounded assertions (typically DB records),
and existentially quantified
statements (typically statistics).

As a toy example,
the following is an imaginary KB storing a parent/child relation,
where explicit instances (e.g., Alice is the child of Kendall) coexist with
existentially quantified ones (e.g., Parker has 3 children):

\begin{array}[]{l}\mathsf{hasChild}(\mathsf{Kendall},\mathsf{Alice})\\
\mathsf{hasChild}(\mathsf{Jordan},\mathsf{Alice})\\
\mathsf{hasChild}(\mathsf{Parker},\mathsf{Bob})\\
\mathsf{hasChild}(\mathsf{Parker},\mathsf{Carol})\\
\end{array}\qquad\begin{array}[]{l}\textit{"Kendall has 2 children"}\\
\textit{"Parker has 3 children"}\\
\textit{"A child has at most}\\
\qquad\textit{2 parents"}\end{array}

The presence of both types of information is common
when integrating multiple
data sources. One source may provide detailed records (e.g., one record per
purchase, medical visit,
etc.),
whereas another source may only provide statistics (number of purchases, of
visits,
etc.), due to anonymization, access
restriction, or simply because the data is recorded in this way.

In such scenarios,
counting answers to a query over a KB may require operations that go beyond counting records.
E.g., in our example,
counting the minimal number of children that must exist according to the KB
(where children can be explicit or existentially quantified elements in the
range of
\mathsf{hasChild}
) requires some non-trivial reasoning.
The answer is 4:
Bob or Carol may be the second child of Kendall,
but Alice cannot be the third child of Parker (because Alice has two parents already),
so a fourth child must exist.

One of the most extensively studied frameworks for query answering over a KB is
Ontology Mediated Query Answering (OMQA)

Query answering under count semantics can be viewed as a specific case of query

The counting problem over sets has also been studied recently in the

Proof
.

We show that the reduction provided in the proof sketch above is correct.

First, for readability, we partition the ABox and query used in this reduction.

The ABox is defined as
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{1}\cup\mathcal{A}_{2}\cup\mathcal{A}_{3}
,
where:

•

\mathcal{A}_{1}=\{\mathsf{Vertex}(v)\mid v\in V\}\cup\{\mathsf{edge}(v_{1},v_{2})\mid(v_{1},v_{2})\in E\}

•

\mathcal{A}_{2}=\{\mathsf{Blue}(\mathsf{b}),\mathsf{Green}(\mathsf{g}),\mathsf{Red}(\mathsf{r})\}
,

•

\mathcal{A}_{3}=\{\mathsf{hasColor}(\mathsf{a},\mathsf{b}),\mathsf{hasColor}(\mathsf{a},\mathsf{g}),\mathsf{hasColor}(\mathsf{a},\mathsf{r}),

\mathsf{edge}(\mathsf{a},\mathsf{a})\}

For readability, we also reproduce the TBox:

\mathcal{T}=\{\mathsf{Vertex}\sqsubseteq\exists\mathsf{hasColor},\exists\mathsf{hasColor}^{-}\sqsubseteq\mathsf{Color}\}

Then the query
q
is defined as:

q()\rdef\phi_{0}\wedge\phi_{b}\wedge\phi_{g}\wedge\phi_{r}

where:

•

\phi_{0}=\mathsf{Color}(c)

•

\phi_{b}=\mathsf{edge}(v_{1},v_{2})\wedge\mathsf{hasColor}(v_{1},c_{1})\wedge\mathsf{hasColor}(v_{2},c_{2})\wedge\mathsf{Blue}(c_{1})\wedge\mathsf{Blue}(c_{2})

•

\phi_{g}=\mathsf{edge}(v_{3},v_{4})\wedge\mathsf{hasColor}(v_{3},c_{3})\wedge\mathsf{hasColor}(v_{4},c_{4})\wedge\mathsf{Green}(c_{3})\wedge\mathsf{Green}(c_{4})

•

\phi_{r}=\mathsf{edge}(v_{5},v_{6})\wedge\mathsf{hasColor}(v_{5},c_{5})\wedge\mathsf{hasColor}(v_{6},c_{6})\wedge\mathsf{Red}(c_{5})\wedge\mathsf{Red}(c_{6})

Observe that the sets of variables occurring in
\phi_{0},\phi_{b},\phi_{g}
and
\phi_{r}
are pairwise disjoint.

Observe that the KB
\mathcal{K}=\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\rangle
is satisfiable.

We now show that
\certCard(q,\mathcal{K})\geq 4
iff
\mathcal{G}
is not 3-colorable.

•

(\Rightarrow)

By contraposition.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be 3-colorable.

We show that there is a model
\mathcal{I}
of
\mathcal{K}
such that
\langle\varepsilon,3\rangle\in\ans(q,\mathcal{I})
.

It follows that
\certCard(q,\mathcal{K})<4
.

\mathcal{I}
is defined as follows:

–

for
E\in\{\mathsf{Vertex,edge,Color,Blue,Green,Red}\}
,

E^{\mathcal{I}}=E^{\mathcal{A}}
,

–

each
v\in\mathsf{Vertex}^{\mathcal{I}}
has a unique
\mathsf{hasColor}
-successor, among
\{
\mathsf{b,g,r}
\}
,
and such that no adjacent vertices have the same successor;
this is possible since
\mathcal{G}
is 3-colorable (by assumption).

It can be verified that
\mathcal{I}
is a model of
\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\rangle
.

Then
\phi_{b}^{\mathcal{I}}=\{\{v_{1}\mapsto\mathsf{a},v_{2}\mapsto\mathsf{a},c_{1}\mapsto\mathsf{b},c_{2}\mapsto\mathsf{b}\}\}
,
and similarly for
\phi_{g}^{\mathcal{I}}
and
\phi_{r}^{\mathcal{I}}
(with
\mathsf{g}
and
\mathsf{r}
respectively instead of
\mathsf{b}
).

So
|\phi_{b}^{\mathcal{I}}\wedge\phi_{g}^{\mathcal{I}}\wedge\phi_{r}^{\mathcal{I}}|=|\phi_{b}^{\mathcal{I}}|\cdot|\phi_{g}^{\mathcal{I}}|\cdot|\phi_{r}^{\mathcal{I}}|=1\cdot 1\cdot 1=1
.

Finally,
\phi_{0}^{\mathcal{I}}=\{\{c\mapsto\mathsf{b}\},\{c\mapsto\mathsf{g}\},\{c\mapsto\mathsf{r}\}\}
.

So
|(\phi_{0}\wedge\phi_{b}\wedge\phi_{g}\wedge\phi_{r})^{\mathcal{I}}|=3\cdot 1=3
.

Therefore
\ans(q,\mathcal{I})=\{\langle\varepsilon,3\rangle\}
.

•

(\Leftarrow)

Let
\mathcal{G}
be non 3-colorable.

We show below that for any model
\mathcal{I}
of
\mathcal{K}
,
there is a
k_{\mathcal{I}}\geq 4
s.t.
=\{\langle\varepsilon,k_{\mathcal{I}}\rangle\}\in\ans(q,\mathcal{I})
.

It follows that
\certCard(q,\mathcal{K})\geq 4
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be a model of
\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\rangle
.

Then
\{\{v_{1}\mapsto\mathsf{a},v_{2}\mapsto\mathsf{a},c_{1}\mapsto\mathsf{b},c_{2}\mapsto\mathsf{b}\}\}\in\phi_{b}^{\mathcal{I}}
,
and similarly for
\phi_{g}^{\mathcal{I}}
and
\phi_{r}^{\mathcal{I}}
(with
\mathsf{g}
and
\mathsf{r}
respectively instead of
\mathsf{b}
).

therefore
|\phi_{b}^{\mathcal{I}}|\geq 1
(and similarly for
\phi_{g}^{\mathcal{I}}
and
\phi_{r}^{\mathcal{I}}
).

Now because
(\mathsf{Vertex}\sqsubseteq\exists\mathsf{hasColor})\in\mathcal{T}
,
each
v\in\mathsf{Vertex}^{\mathcal{I}}
has a
\mathsf{hasColor}
-successor in
\Delta^{I}
.

Then we have two cases:

–

There is a
\mathsf{w}\in\mathsf{Vertex}^{\mathcal{I}}
and a
\mathsf{c}\not\in\{\mathsf{b},\mathsf{g},\mathsf{r}\}
s.t.
\mathsf{hasColor}(\mathsf{w},\mathsf{c})\in\mathsf{hasColor}^{\mathcal{I}}
.

Because
(\exists\mathsf{hasColor}^{-}\sqsubseteq\mathsf{Color})\in\mathcal{T}
,

\{\mathsf{Color}(\mathsf{c}),\mathsf{Color(b)},\mathsf{Color(g)},\mathsf{Color(r)}\}\subseteq\mathsf{Color}^{\mathcal{I}}
must hold.

So
\mathsf{Color}^{I}>3
.

Therefore
|\phi_{0}^{\mathcal{I}}|>3
,
and
\ans(q,\mathcal{I})=\{\langle\varepsilon,k_{\mathcal{I}}\rangle\}
for some
k_{\mathcal{I}}>3\cdot 1\cdot 1\cdot 1=3
.

–

For each
v\in\mathsf{Vertex}^{\mathcal{I}}
, there is a
c\in\{\mathsf{b},\mathsf{g},\mathsf{r}\}
s.t.
\mathsf{hasColor}(v,c)\in\mathsf{hasColor}^{I}
.

Because
\mathcal{G}
is not 3-colorable,
there must be
\mathsf{w}_{1},\mathsf{w}_{2}\in\mathsf{Vertex}^{I}
and
\mathsf{c}\in\{\mathsf{b},\mathsf{g},\mathsf{r}\}
such that
\mathsf{edge}(\mathsf{w}_{1},\mathsf{w}_{2})\in\mathsf{edge}^{\mathcal{I}}
and

\{\mathsf{hasColor}(\mathsf{w}_{1},\mathsf{c}),\mathsf{hasColor}(\mathsf{w}_{2},\mathsf{c})\}\subseteq\mathsf{hasColor}^{\mathcal{I}}
.

So at least one of the following holds:

\{v_{1}\mapsto\mathsf{w}_{1},v_{1}\mapsto\mathsf{w}_{2},c_{1}\mapsto\mathsf{c},c_{2}\mapsto\mathsf{c}\}\in\phi_{b}^{\mathcal{I}}

,

\{v_{3}\mapsto\mathsf{w}_{1},v_{4}\mapsto\mathsf{w}_{2},c_{3}\mapsto\mathsf{c},c_{4}\mapsto\mathsf{c}\}\in\phi_{g}^{\mathcal{I}}

or

\{v_{5}\mapsto\mathsf{w}_{1},v_{6}\mapsto\mathsf{w}_{2},c_{5}\mapsto\mathsf{c},c_{6}\mapsto\mathsf{c}\}\in\phi_{r}^{\mathcal{I}}

Therefore at least one of

|\phi_{b}^{\mathcal{I}}|\geq 2
,

|\phi_{g}^{\mathcal{I}}|\geq 2
or

|\phi_{r}^{\mathcal{I}}|\geq 2

must hold.

Since we know that

|\phi_{b}^{\mathcal{I}}|\geq 1
,

|\phi_{g}^{\mathcal{I}}|\geq 1
,

|\phi_{r}^{\mathcal{I}}|\geq 1
and

|\phi_{0}^{\mathcal{I}}|\geq 3
,
we get:

|\phi_{0}^{\mathcal{I}}|\cdot|\phi_{b}^{\mathcal{I}}|\cdot|\phi_{g}^{\mathcal{I}}|\cdot|\phi_{r}^{\mathcal{I}}|\geq 3\cdot 2\cdot 1\cdot 1=6

So there is a
k_{\mathcal{I}}>3
s.t.
\ans(q,\mathcal{I})=\{\langle\varepsilon,k_{\mathcal{I}}\rangle\}
.

\square

A.3
Proposition
3

Count
is co
NP
-hard in data complexity for
DL-Lite
{}_{\mathit{pos}}^{\mathcal{H}}
and acyclic,
connected, but branching CQs (
\mathsf{CQ^{\mathsf{AC}}}
).

Proof
.

Again, we adapt the reduction from (co-)3-colorability from
Kostylev and Reutter 2015
,
so that the Gaifman graph of the query used in the reduction is acyclic,
and has a single connected component.

Let
G=\langle V,E\rangle
be an undirected graph without self-loop (with vertices
V
and edges
E
).

We assume w.l.o.g. that
V
is nonempty.

We define the KB
\mathcal{K}=\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\rangle
as follows.

•

\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{1}\cup\mathcal{A}_{2}\cup\mathcal{A}_{3}\cup\mathcal{A}_{4}\cup\mathcal{A}_{5}
, where:

–

\mathcal{A}_{1}=\{\mathsf{Vertex}(v)\mid v\in V\}\cup\{\mathsf{edge}(v_{1},v_{2})\mid(v_{1},v_{2})\in E\}

–

\mathcal{A}_{2}=\bigcup\limits_{v\in V}\{\mathsf{conn}(v,\mathsf{a}),\mathsf{conn}(\mathsf{a},v)\}

–

\mathcal{A}_{3}=\{\mathsf{s}(v,c)\mid v\in V\text{ and }c\in\{\mathsf{b,g,r}\}\}

–

\mathcal{A}_{4}=\{\mathsf{edge}(\mathsf{a,a}),\mathsf{conn}(\mathsf{a,a}),\mathsf{hasColor}(\mathsf{a,b}),\mathsf{hasColor}(\mathsf{a,g}),\mathsf{hasColor}(\mathsf{a,r})\}

–

\mathcal{A}_{5}=\{\mathsf{Blue}(\mathsf{b}),\mathsf{Green}(\mathsf{g}),\mathsf{Red}(\mathsf{r})\}

•

\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{T}_{1}\cup\mathcal{T}_{2}
, where:

–

\mathcal{T}_{1}=\{\mathsf{Vertex}\sqsubseteq\exists\mathsf{hasColor}\}

–

\mathcal{T}_{2}=\{\mathsf{s}\sqsubseteq\mathsf{u},\mathsf{hasColor}\sqsubseteq\mathsf{u}\}

Observe that the KB
\mathcal{K}=\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\rangle
is satisfiable.

We now define the query
q
as:

q()\rdef\phi_{\mathsf{Blue}}\wedge\phi_{\mathsf{Green}}\wedge\phi_{\mathsf{Red}}\wedge\phi_{\mathsf{conn}}\wedge\phi_{\mathsf{u}}

where:

\displaystyle\phi_{\mathsf{Blue}}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{edge}(v_{1},v_{2})\wedge\mathsf{hasColor}(v_{1},c_{1})\wedge\mathsf{hasColor}(v_{2},c_{2})\wedge\mathsf{Blue}(c_{1})\wedge\mathsf{Blue}(c_{2})

\displaystyle\phi_{\mathsf{Green}}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{edge}(v_{3},v_{4})\wedge\mathsf{hasColor}(v_{3},c_{3})\wedge\mathsf{hasColor}(v_{4},c_{4})\wedge\mathsf{Green}(c_{3})\wedge\mathsf{Green}(c_{4})

\displaystyle\phi_{\mathsf{Red}}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{edge}(v_{5},v_{6})\wedge\mathsf{hasColor}(v_{5},c_{6})\wedge\mathsf{hasColor}(v_{6},c_{6})\wedge\mathsf{Red}(c_{5})\wedge\mathsf{Red}(c_{6})

\displaystyle\phi_{\mathsf{conn}}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{conn}(v_{1},v_{3})\wedge\mathsf{conn}(v_{3},v_{5})

\displaystyle\phi_{\mathsf{u}}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{s}(v_{7},c_{1})\wedge\mathsf{u}(v_{7},c_{7})

Observe that the Gaifman graph of
q
is a tree.

For readability, we will use
\phi^{\prime}
below as a shortcut to denote (the conjunction of) the first 4 subformulas of
q
,
i.e.:

\phi^{\prime}\doteq\phi_{\mathsf{Blue}}\wedge\phi_{\mathsf{Green}}\wedge\phi_{\mathsf{Red}}\wedge\phi_{\mathsf{conn}}

so that

q
can alternatively be defined as:

q()\rdef\phi^{\prime}\wedge\phi_{\mathsf{u}}

Observe that
c_{1}
is the only variable shared by
\phi^{\prime}
and
\phi_{\mathsf{u}}
.

We will also use
\omega_{\mathsf{a}}
to denote the following soluton mapping:

\omega_{\mathsf{a}}\doteq\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}v_{1}\mapsto\mathsf{a},&v_{2}\mapsto\mathsf{a},&c_{1}\mapsto\mathsf{b},&c_{2}\mapsto\mathsf{b},\\
v_{3}\mapsto\mathsf{a},&v_{4}\mapsto\mathsf{a},&c_{3}\mapsto\mathsf{g},&c_{4}\mapsto\mathsf{g},\\
v_{5}\mapsto\mathsf{a},&v_{6}\mapsto\mathsf{a},&c_{5}\mapsto\mathsf{r},&c_{6}\mapsto\mathsf{r}\end{array}\right\}

We now show that
\certCard(q,\mathcal{K})\geq 3\cdot|V|+1
iff
G
is not 3-colorable.

•

(\Rightarrow)

By contraposition.

Let
G
be 3-colorable.

We show that there is a model
\mathcal{I}
of
\mathcal{K}
such that
\langle\varepsilon,3\cdot|V|\rangle\in\ans(q,\mathcal{I})
.

It follows that
\certCard(q,\mathcal{K})<3\cdot|V|+1
.

\mathcal{I}
is defined as follows:

–

for
E\in\{\mathsf{Vertex,edge,hasColor,s,conn,Blue,Green,Red}\}
,

E^{\mathcal{I}}=E^{\mathcal{A}}
,

–

\mathsf{u}^{\mathcal{I}}=\mathsf{hasColor}^{\mathcal{I}}\cup\mathsf{s}^{\mathcal{I}}
,

–

each
v\in(\mathsf{Vertex}^{\mathcal{I}})
has a unique
\mathsf{hasColor}
-successor,
among
\mathsf{\{b,g,r\}}
,
such that no adjacent vertices have the same successor;
this is possible since
G
is 3-colorable (by assumption).

It can be verified that
\mathcal{I}
is a model of
\mathcal{K}
.

Then

\phi_{\mathsf{Blue}}^{\mathcal{I}}=\{\{v_{1}\mapsto\mathsf{a},v_{2}\mapsto\mathsf{a},c_{1}\mapsto\mathsf{b},c_{2}\mapsto\mathsf{b}\}\}
,
and similarly for
\phi_{\mathsf{Green}}^{\mathcal{I}}
and
\phi_{\mathsf{Red}}^{\mathcal{I}}
(with
\mathsf{g}
and
\mathsf{r}
respectively instead of
\mathsf{b}
).

Observe also that

\{v_{1}\mapsto\mathsf{a},v_{3}\mapsto\mathsf{a},v_{5}\mapsto\mathsf{a}\}\in\phi_{\mathsf{conn}}^{\mathcal{I}}
.

It follows that
(\phi^{\prime})^{\mathcal{I}}=\{\omega_{\mathsf{a}}\}
.

In addition,

\phi_{\mathsf{u}}[c_{1}/\mathsf{b}]^{\mathcal{I}}=\{\{v_{7}\mapsto v,c_{7}\mapsto c\}\mid v\in V,c\in\{\mathsf{b,g,r}\}\}
.

Then because
c_{1}
is the only variable shared by
\phi^{\prime}
and
\phi_{\mathsf{u}}
,
and because
\omega_{\mathsf{a}}(c_{1})=\mathsf{b}
,
we get:

\displaystyle(\phi^{\prime}\wedge\phi_{\mathsf{u}})^{\mathcal{I}}

\displaystyle=\{\omega_{\mathsf{a}}\cup\omega^{\prime}\mid\omega^{\prime}\in\phi_{\mathsf{u}}[c_{1}/\mathsf{b}]^{\mathcal{I}}\}

\displaystyle|(\phi^{\prime}\wedge\phi_{\mathsf{u}})^{\mathcal{I}}|

\displaystyle=|\phi_{\mathsf{u}}[c_{1}/\mathsf{b}]^{\mathcal{I}}|

\displaystyle|(\phi^{\prime}\wedge\phi_{\mathsf{u}})^{\mathcal{I}}|

\displaystyle=3\cdot|V|

So
\langle\varepsilon,3\cdot|V|\rangle\in\ans(q,\mathcal{I})
.

•

(\Leftarrow)

Let
G
be non 3-colorable.

We show below that for any model
\mathcal{I}
of
\mathcal{K}
,
|(\phi^{\prime}\wedge\phi_{\mathsf{u}})^{\mathcal{I}}|\geq 3\cdot|V|+1
.

It follows that
\certCard(q,\mathcal{K})\geq 3\cdot|V|+1
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be a model of
\mathcal{K}
.

Then

\{v_{1}\mapsto\mathsf{a},v_{2}\mapsto\mathsf{a},c_{1}\mapsto\mathsf{b},c_{2}\mapsto\mathsf{b}\}\in\phi_{\mathsf{Blue}}^{\mathcal{I}}
,
and similarly for
\phi_{\mathsf{Green}}^{\mathcal{I}}
and
\phi_{\mathsf{Red}}^{\mathcal{I}}
(with
\mathsf{g}
and
\mathsf{r}
respectively instead of
\mathsf{b}
).

In addition,

\{v_{1}\mapsto\mathsf{a},v_{3}\mapsto\mathsf{a},v_{5}\mapsto\mathsf{a}\}\in\phi_{\mathsf{conn}}^{\mathcal{I}}
.

It follows that
\omega_{\mathsf{a}}\in(\phi^{\prime})^{\mathcal{I}}
.

Now let
\Omega_{\mathsf{u}}=\{\{v_{7}\mapsto v,c_{7}\mapsto c\}\mid v\in V,c\in\{\mathsf{b,g,r}\}\}
.

Then
|\Omega_{\mathsf{u}}|=3\cdot|V|
.

In addition, because
\mathcal{A}_{3}\subseteq\mathcal{I}
and
(\mathsf{s}\sqsubseteq\mathsf{u})\in\mathcal{T}
,
it must be the case that
\Omega_{\mathsf{u}}\subseteq\phi_{\mathsf{u}}[c_{1}/\mathsf{b}]^{\mathcal{I}}
.

Finally,
because
(\mathsf{Vertex}\sqsubseteq\exists\mathsf{hasColor})\in\mathcal{T}
,
each
v\in V^{\mathcal{I}}
has a
\mathsf{hasColor}
-successor in
\Delta^{I}
.

Then we have two cases:

–

There is a
w\in\mathsf{Vertex}^{\mathcal{I}}
and a
c\not\in\{\mathsf{b},\mathsf{g},\mathsf{r}\}
s.t.
\mathsf{hasColor}(w,c)\in\mathsf{hasColor}^{\mathcal{I}}
.

Then
\omega=\{v_{7}\mapsto w,c_{7}\mapsto c\}\in\phi_{\mathsf{u}}[c_{1}/\mathsf{b}]^{\mathcal{I}}
.

And since
\Omega_{\mathsf{u}}\subseteq\phi_{\mathsf{u}}[c_{1}/\mathsf{b}]^{\mathcal{I}}
also holds,

\Omega_{\mathsf{u}}\cup\{\omega\}\subseteq\phi_{\mathsf{u}}[c_{1}/\mathsf{b}]^{\mathcal{I}}
.

Observe also that
\omega\not\in\Omega_{\mathsf{u}}
.

Therefore:

\displaystyle|\phi_{\mathsf{u}}[c_{1}/\mathsf{b}]^{\mathcal{I}}|

\displaystyle\geq|\Omega_{\mathsf{u}}\cup\{\omega\}|

(1)

\displaystyle|\phi_{\mathsf{u}}[c_{1}/\mathsf{b}]^{\mathcal{I}}|

\displaystyle\geq 3\cdot|V|+1

(2)

Now recall that
\omega_{\mathsf{a}}\in(\phi^{\prime})^{\mathcal{I}}
, that
\omega_{\mathsf{a}}(c_{1})=\mathsf{b}
,
and that
c_{1}
is the only variable shared by
\phi^{\prime}
and
\phi_{\mathsf{u}}
.

It follows that:

\displaystyle(\phi^{\prime}\wedge\phi_{\mathsf{u}})^{\mathcal{I}}

\displaystyle\supseteq\{\omega_{\mathsf{a}}\cup\omega^{\prime}\mid\omega^{\prime}\in\phi_{\mathsf{u}}[c_{1}/\mathsf{b}]^{\mathcal{I}}\}

(3)

\displaystyle|(\phi^{\prime}\wedge\phi_{\mathsf{u}})^{\mathcal{I}}|

\displaystyle\geq|\{\omega_{\mathsf{a}}\cup\omega^{\prime}\mid\omega^{\prime}\in\phi_{\mathsf{u}}[c_{1}/\mathsf{b}]^{\mathcal{I}}\}|

\displaystyle|(\phi^{\prime}\wedge\phi_{\mathsf{u}})^{\mathcal{I}}|

\displaystyle\geq|\phi_{\mathsf{u}}[c_{1}/\mathsf{b}]^{\mathcal{I}}|

(5)

So from
2
and
5
:

\displaystyle|(\phi^{\prime}\wedge\phi_{\mathsf{u}})^{\mathcal{I}}|

\displaystyle\geq 3\cdot|V|+1

–

For each
v\in\mathsf{Vertex}^{\mathcal{I}}
, there is a
c\in\{\mathsf{b},\mathsf{g},\mathsf{r}\}
s.t.
\mathsf{hasColor}(v,c)\in\mathsf{hasColor}^{I}
.

Because
G
is not 3-colorable,

and

such that
\mathsf{edge}(w_{1},w_{2})\in\mathsf{edge}^{\mathcal{I}}

\{v_{1}\mapsto w_{1},v_{2}\mapsto w_{2},c_{1}\mapsto\mathsf{b},c_{2}\mapsto\mathsf{b}\}\in\phi_{\mathsf{Blue}}^{\mathcal{I}}

In addition:

\displaystyle\{v_{1}\mapsto\mathsf{a},v_{2}\mapsto\mathsf{a},c_{1}\mapsto\mathsf{g},c_{2}\mapsto\mathsf{g}\}\in\phi_{\mathsf{Green}}^{\mathcal{I}}

\displaystyle\{v_{1}\mapsto\mathsf{a},v_{2}\mapsto\mathsf{a},c_{1}\mapsto\mathsf{r},c_{2}\mapsto\mathsf{r}\}\in\phi_{\mathsf{Red}}^{\mathcal{I}}

\displaystyle\{v_{1}\mapsto w_{1},v_{3}\mapsto\mathsf{a},v_{5}\mapsto\mathsf{a}\}\in\phi_{\mathsf{conn}}^{\mathcal{I}}

\omega_{w_{1}}\in(\phi^{\prime})^{\mathcal{I}}

(6)

\displaystyle|(\phi^{\prime}\wedge\phi_{\mathsf{u}})^{\mathcal{I}}|

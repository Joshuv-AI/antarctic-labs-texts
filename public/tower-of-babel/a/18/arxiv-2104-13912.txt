On the construction of almost general solutions for PDEs arising in nonlinear optics
A Ghose-Choudhury, Sudip Garai

Abstract
In this communication we consider the widely used nonlinear Fokas-Lenells equation, the cubic focussing nonlinear Schrödinger equation in (2+1)-dimensions and the coupled Drinfel’d-Sokolov-Wilson equation and attempt to construct almost general solutions for the envelope of the wave packet by means of the travelling wave ansatz. The obtained solutions have been expressed in terms of Jacobi elliptic sine function from which one can obtain the solitary wave (particular) solutions by imposing appropriate conditions on the roots of certain quartic polynomials.

2
General solutions of the reduced Fokas-Lenells equation

The well known Fokas-Lenells equation is given by

iq_{t}+a_{1}q_{xx}+a_{2}q_{xt}+(bq+i\sigma q_{x})|q|^{2}=i[\alpha q_{x}+\lambda(|q|^{2}q)_{x}+\mu(|q|^{2})_{x}q].

(2.1)

Here as elsewhere the subscripts denote partial derivatives and on the left hand side the coefficients are as follows:
a_{1}\rightarrow
group velocity dispersal,
a_{2}\rightarrow
spatio-temporal dispersal,
b\rightarrow
self phase modulation &
\sigma\rightarrow
nonlinear dispersal. On the right hand side the coefficients
\alpha
,
\lambda
and
\mu
represent respectively as: the intermodal, self steepening and nonlinear dispersal. The Fokas-Lenells equation essentially describes the propagation of the nonlinear optical pulses in monomode fibers when higher-order nonlinear effects have been taken into consideration. We look for the solutions of Eq.(
2.1
) in the form of travelling wave ansatz, viz.

q(x,t)=U(\eta)e^{i\phi(x,t)},\;\;\;\eta=x-vt,\;\;\;\phi(x,t)=-\kappa x-\omega t+\theta_{0}.

(2.2)

On substituting this form of the solution and separating the real and imaginary parts, we get the following equations:

(a_{1}-va_{2})U^{\prime\prime}+(b+\sigma\kappa-\lambda\kappa)U^{3}+(a_{2}\omega\kappa-\omega-\alpha\kappa-a_{1}\kappa^{2})U=0,

(2.3)

[a_{2}(v\kappa+\omega)-(v+2a_{1}\kappa)-\alpha]U^{\prime}+(\sigma-3\lambda-2\mu)U^{2}U^{\prime}=0.

(2.4)

One way to make further progress with the above equations is to require that the coefficients of Eq.(
2.4
) vanish, which serves to determine the parameter
v=(a_{2}\omega-\alpha-2a_{1}\kappa)/(1-a_{2}\kappa)
and
\sigma=3\lambda+2\mu
. Clearly, we must have
a_{2}\kappa\neq 1
. Let us now write Eq.(
2.3
) as

AU^{\prime\prime}+BU^{3}-CU=0,

(2.5)

where

A=(a_{1}-va_{2}),\;\;\;B=b+(\sigma-\lambda\kappa),\;\;\;C=(\omega+a_{1}\kappa^{2}-\omega\kappa a_{2}+\alpha\kappa),

(2.6)

and the
′
denotes differentiation with respect to the variable
\eta
.
Multiplying Eq.(
2.5
) with
U^{\prime}
and integrating once leads to

U^{\prime 2}+\frac{B}{2A}U^{4}-\frac{C}{A}U^{2}+C_{0}=0,

(2.7)

where
C_{0}
is an arbitrary constant of integration. In order to construct the general solution of Eq.(
2.7
) it is absolutely necessary that we retain this arbitrary constant of integration. We make a change of the variable
\eta
to,
\bar{x}=m\eta
, whence Eq.(
2.7
) becomes

U_{\bar{x}}^{2}+\frac{M}{m^{2}}\left(U^{4}-\frac{N}{M}U^{2}+\frac{C_{0}}{M}\right)=0.

(2.8)

Here we have set,
M=B/2A
and
N=C/A
, respectively. The roots of,
U^{4}-(N/M)U^{2}+(C_{0}/M)=0
, are given by

\pm\sqrt{u_{\pm}}
, where

u_{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{N}{M}\pm\sqrt{\frac{N^{2}}{M^{2}}-\frac{4C_{0}}{M}}\right].

This enables us to rewrite Eq.(
2.8
) as

U_{\bar{x}}^{2}+\frac{M}{m^{2}}(U-u_{1})(U-u_{2})(U-u_{3})(U-u_{4})=0,

(2.9)

with

u_{1}=\sqrt{u_{+}},\;\;u_{2}=-\sqrt{u_{+}},\;\;u_{3}=\sqrt{u_{-}},\;\;u_{4}=-\sqrt{u_{-}}.

(2.10)

Following the reference
[
22
]
, we assume that the solution of Eq.(
2.9
) is of the form

U(\bar{x})=u_{1}+\frac{(u_{2}-u_{1})e}{V^{2}+e},

(2.11)

where
V(\bar{x})
is a new function which has to be determined and
e
is a constant. Simple calculations now show that

U_{\bar{x}}^{2}=\frac{4(u_{2}-u_{1})^{2}e^{2}V^{2}V_{\bar{x}}^{2}}{(V^{2}+e)^{4}},

U(\bar{x})-u_{1}=\frac{(u_{2}-u_{1})e}{V^{2}+e},\;\;U(\bar{x})-u_{2}=\frac{(u_{1}-u_{2})V^{2}}{V^{2}+e},

U(\bar{x})-u_{3}=(u_{1}-u_{3})\left[1-\frac{(u_{1}-u_{2})e}{(u_{1}-u_{3})(V^{2}+e)}\right],\;\;U(\bar{x})-u_{4}=(u_{1}-u_{4})\left[1-\frac{(u_{1}-u_{2})e}{(u_{1}-u_{4})(V^{2}+e)}\right].

Insertion of the above expressions into (
2.9
) gives

V_{\bar{x}}^{2}-\frac{Me}{4m^{2}}(u_{3}-u_{2})(u_{4}-u_{2})\left[1-\frac{(u_{1}-u_{3})V^{2}}{(u_{3}-u_{2})e}\right]\left[1-\frac{(u_{1}-u_{4})V^{2}}{(u_{4}-u_{2})e}\right]=0.

(2.12)

Let us choose the constants
e
and
m
as follows:

e=\frac{(u_{1}-u_{3})}{(u_{3}-u_{2})},\;\;\;\;m^{2}=\frac{M}{4}(u_{4}-u_{2})(u_{1}-u_{3}).

(2.13)

As a consequence we have

V_{\bar{x}}^{2}-[1-V^{2}][1-p^{2}V^{2}]=0,

(2.14)

where

p^{2}=\frac{(u_{1}-u_{4})(u_{3}-u_{2})}{(u_{4}-u_{2})(u_{1}-u_{3})}=\left(\frac{\sqrt{u_{+}}+\sqrt{u_{-}}}{\sqrt{u_{+}}-\sqrt{u_{-}}}\right)^{2}.

It is well known that the solution of (
2.14
) is given by the Jacobi elliptic sine function i.e.,

V(\bar{x},p^{2})=sn(\bar{x}-\bar{x}_{0};p^{2})
so that

V(m\eta;p^{2})=sn(m(\eta-\eta_{0});p^{2})=sn\left(\frac{\sqrt{M}}{2}(\sqrt{u_{+}}-\sqrt{u_{-}})(\eta-\eta_{0});p^{2}\right).

(2.15)

Finally from (
2.11
) using (
2.13
) we obtain the solution for the envelope as

U(\bar{x})=\frac{u_{1}(u_{3}-u_{2})V^{2}+u_{2}(u_{1}-u_{3})}{(u_{3}-u_{2})V^{2}+(u_{1}-u_{3})}=\sqrt{u_{+}}\left[\frac{(\sqrt{u_{+}}+\sqrt{u_{-}})V^{2}-(\sqrt{u_{+}}-\sqrt{u_{-}})}{(\sqrt{u_{+}}+\sqrt{u_{-}})V^{2}+(\sqrt{u_{+}}-\sqrt{u_{-}})}\right].

(2.16)

3
Cubic focusing NLS equation

Here we take the simplest case of a (2+1)-dimensional cubic focussing cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLS)
[
25
,
26
,
27
]
of the form

iq_{t}+q_{xx}+\mu q_{yy}+\sigma|q|^{2}q=0.

(3.1)

We restrict our analysis to the elliptic case
\mu=1
and consider the travelling wave ansatz
q(x,y,t)=U(z)e^{i(k_{1}x+k_{2}y-\omega t)}
, with
z=x+y-vt
. On substituting this into Eq.(
3.1
) and equating the real and imaginary parts we obtain the following equations:

2U_{zz}+(\omega-k_{1}^{2}-k_{2}^{2})U+\sigma U^{3}=0,

(3.2)

(-v+2k_{1}+2k_{2})U_{z}=0.

(3.3)

From the last equation, it follows that
v=2(k_{1}+k_{2})
since
y_{z}\neq 0
. Multiplying Eq.(
3.2
) by
U_{z}
and integrating once we get;

U_{z}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\omega-k_{1}^{2}-k_{2}^{2})U^{2}+\frac{\sigma}{4}U^{4}+C_{0}=0,

(3.4)

with
C_{0}
being a constant of integration. As before we write this equation in the form

U_{z}^{2}+\frac{\sigma}{4}\left(U^{4}-\frac{2}{\sigma}(k_{1}^{2}+k_{2}^{2}-\omega)U^{2}+\frac{4}{\sigma}C_{0}\right)=0.

(3.5)

Introducing the change of independent variable
\bar{z}=mz
, we have

U_{\bar{z}}^{2}+\frac{\sigma}{4m^{2}}(U-u_{1})(U-u_{2})(U-u_{3})(U-u_{4})=0,

(3.6)

where the roots
u_{i}
of the fourth-degree polynomial in Eq.(
3.5
) are given by

u_{1,2}=\pm\sqrt{u_{+}}
and
u_{3,4}=\pm\sqrt{u_{-}}

with

u_{\pm}=\frac{1}{\sigma}\left[(k_{1}^{2}+k_{2}^{2}-\omega)\pm\sqrt{(k_{1}^{2}+k_{2}^{2}-\omega)^{2}-4\sigma C_{0}}\right].

Eq.(
3.6
) is exactly similar to Eq.(
2.9
) and hence proceeding in the same manner as described in the previous section its general solution can be stated as follows:

U(\bar{z})=\sqrt{u_{+}}\left[\frac{(\sqrt{u_{+}}+\sqrt{u_{-}})V^{2}-(\sqrt{u_{+}}-\sqrt{u_{-}})}{(\sqrt{u_{+}}+\sqrt{u_{-}})V^{2}+(\sqrt{u_{+}}-\sqrt{u_{-}})}\right].

(3.7)

with

V(\bar{z}=mz;p^{2})=sn(\frac{\sqrt{\sigma}}{4}(\sqrt{u_{+}}-\sqrt{u_{-}})(z-z_{0});p^{2})

and

m^{2}=\frac{\sigma}{16}(u_{4}-u_{2})(u_{1}-u_{3}),\;\;\;p^{2}=\left(\frac{\sqrt{u_{+}}+\sqrt{u_{-}}}{\sqrt{u_{+}}-\sqrt{u_{-}}}\right)^{2}.

4
The Drinfel’d-Sokolov-Wilson Equation

The Drinfel’d-Sokolov-Wilson (DSW) equation is a coupled system of the NPDEs given by
[
28
]

u_{t}+pvv_{x}=0,\;\;\;v_{t}+qv_{xxx}+ruv_{x}+su_{x}v=0

(4.1)

with
p,q,r
and
s
are being nonzero parameters. The system was first proposed by Drinfel’d and Sokolov
[
29
,
30
]
and independently discovered by Wilson
[
31
]
. When
p=3
,
q=r=2
and
s=1
it turns out to be integrable. We look for traveling wave solutions of the form

u(\xi)=u(x,t),\;\;v(\xi)=v(x,t),\;\;\;\xi=x+\omega t.

Upon inserting the travelling wave forms into Eq.(
4.1
) we obtain

\omega u^{\prime}+pvv^{\prime}=0,

(4.2)

\omega v^{\prime}+qv^{\prime\prime\prime}+ruv^{\prime}+su^{\prime}v=0.

(4.3)

From the first of these ODEs we obtain upon integration

u=\frac{C_{0}-p\frac{v^{2}}{2}}{\omega}.

(4.4)

Upon using this expression for
u
in (
4.3
) we have after two integrations the following first-order nonlinear ODE, viz.

v^{\prime 2}+Mv^{4}-Nv^{2}+C_{1}v+C_{2}=0,

(4.5)

where
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are constants of integration and

M=-\frac{p(r+2s)}{12\omega q},\;\;\;N=-\frac{\omega^{2}+rC_{0}}{q\omega}.

As before, we let
\bar{x}=m\xi
whence we can write (
4.5
) as

v_{\bar{x}}^{2}+\frac{M}{m^{2}}(v^{4}-\frac{N}{M}v^{2}+\frac{C_{1}}{M}v+\frac{C_{2}}{M})=0.

(4.6)

If
v_{i}(i=1,...,4)
denote the roots of,
(v^{4}-\frac{N}{M}v^{2}+\frac{C_{1}}{M}v+\frac{C_{2}}{M})=0
, we can rewrite the last equation as

v_{\bar{x}}^{2}+\frac{M}{m^{2}}(v-v_{1})(v-v_{2})(v-v_{3})(v-v_{4})=0,

(4.7)

with
\sum_{i=1}^{4}v_{i}=0
. We assume that the solution of (
4.7
) is expressible in the form

v(\bar{x})=v_{1}+\frac{(v_{2}-v_{1})e}{V^{2}+e},

(4.8)

where
V(\bar{x})
is a function to be determined and
e
is a constant. It follows from our earlier analysis that with

e=\frac{v_{1}-v_{3}}{v_{3}-v_{2}},\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;m^{2}=\frac{M}{4}(v_{4}-v_{2})(v_{1}-v_{3})

the function
V(\bar{x})
satisfies the equation

V_{\bar{x}}^{2}-[1-V^{2}][1-p^{2}V^{2}]=0,

(4.9)

with
p^{2}=\frac{(v_{1}-v_{4})(v_{3}-v_{2})}{(v_{4}-v_{2})(v_{1}-v_{3})}
and that the solution is given by

v(\bar{x};p^{2})=sn(\bar{x}-\bar{x}_{0};p^{2}),

(4.10)

or more explicitly

V(m\xi;p^{2})=sn\left(\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{M(v_{4}-v_{2})(v_{1}-v_{3})}(\xi-\xi_{0});p^{2}\right).

(4.11)

The final solution for
v(\xi)
is obtained from (
4.8
) and turns out to be after simplification:

v(\xi)=\frac{v_{1}(v_{3}-v_{2})V^{2}+v_{2}(v_{1}-v_{3})}{(v_{3}-v_{2})V^{2}+(v_{1}-v_{3})}.

(4.12)

Knowing
v(\xi)
it is straightforward to obtain
u(\xi)
from (
4.4
).

5
Discussion

In this article we have attempted to highlight the fact that while using the travelling wave ansatz for dealing with nonlinear PDEs instead of arbitrarily setting the constants of integration, resulting from reduction of order, to be zero and thus finding only particular solutions; one can in many cases proceed towards a general solution by retaining the arbitrary constants of integration. In the cases presented in this article while constructing the general solution we have encountered quartic polynomials which can be factorized. The resulting solutions are all expressed in terms of the Jacobi elliptic sine function. It is clearly possible to obtain solitary wave (particular) solutions by imposing appropriate conditions on the roots. This has been explicitly discussed in the reference
[
22
]
. Of course it goes without saying that this procedure for constructing the general solution fails when quintic or higher order polynomials are encountered. Lastly, we should mention that the general solutions we have discussed are actually the general solutions of ‘reduced equations’ as there are in almost all cases certain conditions on the parameters involved in the equation.

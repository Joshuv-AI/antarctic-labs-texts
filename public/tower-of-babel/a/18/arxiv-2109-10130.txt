On binomials and algebraic closure of some pseudofinite fields Thanks: The first author is supported by the National Science Centre, Poland NCN grant no. 2017/27/B/ST1/01467.
Jakub Gismatullin, Katarzyna Tarasek

Abstract
. We give a criterion when a polynomial x n − g x^{n}-g is irreducible over a pseudofinite field. As an application we give an explicit description of algebraic closure of some pseudofinite fields of zero characteristic.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
12L10, 12F05, 03C20

1.
Introduction

In this note we study binomials over pseudofinite fields. A
binomial
over a field
K
is a polynomial of the form
x^{n}-g
, where
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
g\in K
. Theorem
2.4
below gives a criterion for irreducibility of a binomial over an ultraproduct of finite fields, that is
pseudofinite field
. We construct in Example
2.5
a pseudofinite field
\mathbb{F}_{\bar{q}}
and find
g\in\mathbb{F}_{\bar{q}}
, such that
x^{n}-g
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{\bar{q}}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We then prove in Theorem
2.6
, that the algebraic closure
\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{\bar{q}}}
is of the form
\mathbb{F}_{\bar{q}}\left(\sqrt[n]{g}:n\in\mathbb{N}\right)
.

2.
Binomials and algebraic closure

Suppose
K
is a field. A
binomial
is a polynomial
w(x)\in K[x]
of the form

w(x)=x^{n}-g,

for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
g\in K
.

We refer the reader to
[
Fried and Jarden(2008)
, Section 7, 7.9]
and
[
Chatzidakis(2018)
]
as a reference for basic notions around pseudofinite fields, that is ultraproducts of finite fields.

Let us fix a nonprincipal ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
on
\mathbb{N}
. Let
{\mathcal{F}}=\{\mathbb{F}_{q_{k}}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a family consisting of some finite fields, where each
q_{k}=p_{k}^{t_{k}}
is a power of a prime
p_{k}
,
t_{k}\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
and
\lim_{k\to\infty}q_{k}=\infty
. Consider an ultraproduct

\mathbb{F}_{\bar{q}}=\prod_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{F}_{q_{k}}/\mathcal{U},\text{ where }\bar{q}=(q_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

of
\mathcal{F}
. Then
\mathbb{F}_{\bar{q}}
is an infinite pseudofinite field. We study the following question:

fix a pseudofinite field
\mathbb{F}_{\bar{q}}=\prod_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{F}_{q_{k}}/\mathcal{U}
, is there
g\in\mathbb{F}_{\bar{q}}
such that for every natural
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
x^{n}-g
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{\bar{q}}
?

Let us recall a standard result on irreducible binomials from
[
Karpilovsky(1989)
, p. 425, Theorem 1.6]
:

Fix a field
K
,
\mathbb{N}\ni n\geq 1
and
g\in K
. Then
x^{n}-g
is irreducible over
K
if and only if
(1)
and
(2)
hold:

(1)

g\notin K^{p}
, for each prime
p
, which divides
n
,

(2)

if
4|n
, then
g\notin-4K^{4}=\{-4x^{4}:x\in K\}
.

This result for finite field has the following form:

Theorem 2.1
.

[
Lidl and Niederreiter(1997)
, Theorem 3.75, p. 125]

Let
q=p^{m}
be a power of a prime
p
and fix
w(x)=x^{n}-g
, for
g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
n\geq 2
. Let
e
be the order of
g
in the multiplicative group
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}=\mathbb{F}_{q}\setminus\{0\}
. Then
w
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
if and only if the following three conditions hold:

1)

\GCD((q-1)/e,n)=1
,

2)

every prime divisor of
n
divides also
e
,

3)

if
4|n
, then also
4|q-1
.

Remark 2.2
.

Observe that
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
is a cyclic group, that is
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
is generated by a single element
g_{q}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. When
x^{n}-g_{q}
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
? Since
g_{q}
has order
q-1
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
, the condition 1) from theorem
2.1
is always true. Therefore
x^{n}-g_{q}
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
if and only if

every prime divisor of
n
divides
q-1
and if
4|n
, then
4|q-1
.

For example, if
q=13
, then each prime divisor of
n
must divide
q-1=12=2^{2}\cdot 3
. Hence
n
must be of the form
2^{m}\cdot 3^{k}
,
m,k\in\mathbb{N}
. Every binomial
x^{2^{m}\cdot 3^{k}}-2
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{13}
(here
g=2
is a generator of
\mathbb{F}_{13}^{\times}
).

We need the following elementary remark on definability of irreducibility of polynomials of a fixed degree. The proof is standard, and can be found e.g. in
[
Fried and Jarden(2008)
, p. 136]
.

Remark 2.3
.

For each natural
n
there exists a first order formula
\varphi_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
in the language of fields
L_{F}=\{+,\cdot,0,1\}
such that, for every field
K
and every collection of elements
a_{0},\ldots,a_{n-1}\in K

polynomial
x^{n}+a_{n-1}x^{n-1}+\ldots+a_{1}x+a_{0}
is irreducible over
K
if and only if
\varphi_{n}(a_{0},\ldots,a_{n-1})
holds in
K
.

Theorem
2.4
below is our criterion for the irreducibility of
x^{n}-g
over a pseudofinite field
\mathbb{F}_{\bar{q}}
. This criterion is a straightforward application of the Łoś ultraproduct theorem.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}=\{\mathbb{F}_{q_{k}}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a family of some finite fields and
\lim_{k\to\infty}q_{k}=\infty
. Take an ultraproduct
\mathbb{F}_{\bar{q}}=\prod_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{F}_{q_{k}}/\mathcal{U}
. Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\{\text{prime divisors of }n\}=\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{r}\}
.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

x^{n}-g
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{\bar{q}}
, where

g=([g_{k}]_{k\in\mathbb{N}})_{\mathcal{U}}\in\mathbb{F}_{\bar{q}},\text{ where each }g_{k}\text{ is a generator of }\mathbb{F}_{q_{k}}^{\times}.

(2)

there exists
g\in\mathbb{F}_{\bar{q}}
such that
x^{n}-g
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{\bar{q}}
,

(3)

for
\mathcal{U}
-almost all
k\in\mathbb{N}
:

p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{r}\text{ divide }q_{k}-1\text{ and if }4|n\text{ then }4|q_{k}-1.

By “for
\mathcal{U}
-almost all
k\in\mathbb{N}
the condition
\psi(k)
holds” we mean that
\{k\in\mathbb{N}:\text{ holds }\psi(k)\}\in\mathcal{U}
.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
is clear.
(2)\Rightarrow(3)
Suppose that
g=([g_{k}]_{k\in\mathbb{N}})_{\mathcal{U}}\in\mathbb{F}_{\bar{q}}
, where
g_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{q_{k}}
and
x^{n}-g
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{\bar{q}}
. By Remark
2.3
and Łoś’s theorem,
x^{n}-g_{k}
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q_{k}}
, for
\mathcal{U}
-almost all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Theorem
2.1
implies that:
p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{r}
divide
e=\ord(g_{k})
, which is a divisor of
q_{k}-1
, and if
4|n
, then
4|q_{k}-1
(for
\mathcal{U}
-almost all
k\in\mathbb{N}
). This is exactly
(2)
.

(3)\Rightarrow(1)
Suppose
(3)
is true. Let
g=([g_{k}]_{k\in\mathbb{N}})_{\mathcal{U}}\in\mathbb{F}
be as in
(\clubsuit)
. In order to obtain (1) it is enough (by Remark
2.3
) to prove that

\text{for $\mathcal{U}$-almost all $k\in\mathbb{N}$, the binomial $x^{n}-g_{k}$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{F}_{q_{k}}$}.

This is exactly (3), by
(\diamondsuit)
in
2.2
.
∎

As an application we construct a pseudofinite field
\mathbb{F}_{\bar{q}}
of characteristic zero and
g\in\mathbb{F}_{\bar{q}}
, such that
x^{n}-g
is irreducible for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Example 2.5
.

Let
\{2=p_{1},p_{2},\ldots\}
be the set of all prime numbers. Take a sequence
\{q_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
of prime powers, such that

4,p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{k}\text{ divide }q_{k}-1\text{ for all natural }k\in\mathbb{N}.

For example one can take

q_{k}=p_{k+1}^{(p_{1}-1)(p_{2}-1)\cdot\ldots\cdot(p_{k}-1)},

as then
p_{i}|q_{k}-1={r_{k}}^{p_{i}-1}-1
by the Fermat’s little theorem.
Then Theorem
2.4
can be applied to
\mathbb{F}_{\bar{q}}=\prod_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{F}_{q_{k}}/\mathcal{U}
, to get that
x^{n}-g
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{\bar{q}}
, for all natural
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Another application of Theorem
2.4
and Example
2.5
is an explicit description of algebraic closure of
\mathbb{F}_{\bar{q}}
from Example
2.5
.

Theorem 2.6
.

Suppose
\mathbb{F}_{\bar{q}}
is a pseudofinite field such that for some
g\in\mathbb{F}_{\bar{q}}
, for every natural
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
x^{n}-g
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{\bar{q}}
. Then its algebraic closure
\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{\bar{q}}}
is generated by
\left\{\sqrt[n]{g}:n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
over
\mathbb{F}_{\bar{q}}
:

\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{\bar{q}}}=\mathbb{F}_{\bar{q}}\left(\sqrt[n]{g}:n\in\mathbb{N}\right),

where
\sqrt[n]{g}
is a root of
x^{n}-g
.

Proof.

We use a well know fact, that every pseudofinite field has a unique algebraic extension of each natural degree
[
Chatzidakis(2018)
]
.
Suppose that
a\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{\bar{q}}}
has degree
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{\bar{q}}
, that is
[\mathbb{F}_{\bar{q}}(a):\mathbb{F}_{\bar{q}}]=n
. Then since also
\left[\mathbb{F}_{\bar{q}}\left(\sqrt[n]{g}\right):\mathbb{F}_{\bar{q}}\right]=n
, we have that
\mathbb{F}_{\bar{q}}(a)=\mathbb{F}_{\bar{q}}\left(\sqrt[n]{g}\right)\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{\bar{q}}\left(\sqrt[n]{g}:n\in\mathbb{N}\right)
.
∎

A pefect field
K
is called
quasi-finite
, if
K
has a unique (necessarily cyclic) extension of degree
n
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Theorem
2.6
is true for quasi-finite fields, with the same proof.

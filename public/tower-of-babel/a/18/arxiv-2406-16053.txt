Kaiwen Meng


Abstract




Lipschitz continuity of solution multifunctions of extended
\ell_{1}
regularization problems

Abstract

In this paper we obtain a verifiable sufficient condition for a polyhedral multifunction to be Lipschitz continuous on its domain. We apply this sufficient condition to establish the Lipschitz continuity of the solution multifunction for an extended
\ell_{1}
regularization problem with respect to the regularization parameter and the observation parameter under the assumption that the data matrix is of full row rank. In doing so, we show that the solution multifunction is a polyhedral one by expressing its graph as the union of the polyhedral cones constructed by the index sets defined by nonempty faces of the feasible set of an extended dual
\ell_{1}
regularization problem. We demonstrate that the domain of the solution multifunction is partitioned as the union of the projections of the above polyhedral cones onto the parameters space and that the graph of the restriction of the multifunction on each of these projections is convex. In comparing with the existing result of the local Lipschitz continuity of the Lasso problem in the literature where certain linear independence condition was assumed, our condition (i.e., full row rank of data matrix) is very weak and our result (i.e., Lipschitz continuity on the domain) is much more stronger. As corollaries of the Lipschitz continuity of the solution multifunction, we show that the single-valuedness and linearity (or piecewise linearity) of the solution multifunction on a particular polyhedral set of the domain are equivalent to certain linear independence conditions of the corresponding columns of the data matrix proposed in the literature.

Keywords:
polyhedral multifunction,
\ell_{1}
regularization problem, solution multifunction, face, Lipschitz continuity

Introduction

Let
A
be an
m\times n
data matrix of full row rank. The following sparse optimization problem has been proposed in machine learning and compressed sensing to find the sparest solution for a system of linear equations:

\min_{x\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}}\;\|x\|_{0}\quad\mbox{subject to}\quad Ax-b=0,

(1)

where
\|x\|_{0}
is the
\ell^{0}
-norm of vector
x
, denoting the number of nonzero components of
x
. This problem is known to be NP-hard
[
6
]
. To find an approximate sparse solution of the problem (
1
), the following convex optimization problems, called Basis Pursuit (BP) and Lasso (or
\ell_{1}
regularization problem) respectively, have been investigated:

\min_{x\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}}\;\|x\|_{1}\quad\mbox{subject to}\;Ax-b=0,

(2)

\min_{x\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}}\;\lambda\|x\|_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\|Ax-b\|^{2}\quad\mbox{ with }\lambda>0.

(3)

The Basis Pursuit problem (
2
) and Lasso problem (
3
) have attracted extensive attention from optimization, statistics and computer science communities and have been accepted as two of the most useful approximate models for problem (
1
) (see Tibshirani
[
22
]
, Candes et al.
[
4
]
and Donoho
[
5
]
and the references therein). Many effective algorithms have been designed for solving problems (
2
) and (
3
). However the study of stability properties of the optimal solutions of
(
2
) and (
3
) is very limited. The recovery bound or so-called relative upper Lipschitz property of optimal solutions of problem (
3
) with respect to parameter
\lambda
only was obtained under various assumptions such as restricted isometric property and restricted eigenvalue condition on data matrix
A
. Only recently Berk et al.
[
3
]
established local Lipschitz continuity and smoothness properties of the optimal solution of the Lasso problem (
3
) with
(\lambda,b)\in\mathbbm{R}_{++}\times\mathbbm{R}^{m}
, where the coderivative and variational analysis tools have been employed in their study and in particular the authors established in
[
3
, Theorem 4.13 (a)]
the local Lipschitz continuity property under a condition proposed in
[
22
]
as a sufficient condition for the uniqueness of the optimal solution of the Lasso.

In this paper, to investigate the Lipschitz continuity in terms of parameters
(\lambda,b)\in\mathbbm{R}_{+}\times\mathbbm{R}^{m}
of the optimal solutions of problems (
2
) and (
3
) in a unified way, we consider the following extended
\ell_{1}
regularization problem:

\min_{x\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}}\;\|x\|_{1}+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|Ax-b\|^{2},

(4)

where, in comparing with
\ell_{1}
regularization problem (
3
),
\lambda=0
is allowed. Indeed, for
\lambda>0
, the problem (
3
) and problem (
4
) have the same set of optimal solutions and for
\lambda=0
, problem (
2
) can be viewed as the problem (
4
) from the view point of penalization.
Let us define the multifunction
S:\mathbbm{R}_{+}\times\mathbbm{R}^{m}\rightrightarrows\mathbbm{R}^{n}
as the optimal solution set of the extended
\ell_{1}
regularization problem (
4
). As the function
\|\cdot\|_{1}
is coercive and the linear equation
Ax-b=0
always has a solution due to
A
being of full row rank, the multifunction
S
is always nonempty, that is,

\dom S=\mathbbm{R}_{+}\times\mathbbm{R}^{m}.

Furthermore, by virtue of first-order optimality conditions, we get

S(\lambda,b)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left\{x\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}\mid Ax-b=0,\;0\in\rge A^{\top}+\partial\|\cdot\|_{1}(x)\right\}&\mbox{if}\;\lambda=0,\,b\in\mathbbm{R}^{m},\\[2.84544pt]
\left\{x\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}\mid 0\in A^{\top}(Ax-b)+\lambda\partial\|\cdot\|_{1}(x)\right\}&\mbox{if}\;\lambda>0,\,b\in\mathbbm{R}^{m}.\end{array}\right.

(5)

We also consider the Lipschitz continuity of the following dual problem (called extended dual
\ell_{1}
regularization problem):

\max_{y\in\mathbbm{R}^{m}}\;\langle b,y\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\lambda\|y\|^{2}\quad\mbox{subject to}\;y\in Y^{0}:=\{y\in\mathbbm{R}^{m}\mid\|A^{\top}y\|_{\infty}\leq 1\}.

(6)

According to the Fenchel-type duality scheme
[
20
, Example 11.41]
, the duals of the BP problem (
2
) and the LASSO problem in the form of (
4
) correspond to the extended dual
\ell_{1}
regularization problem (
6
) with
\lambda=0
and with
\lambda>0
, respectively. Hence (
6
) is the dual of the problem (
4
). In fact the dual feasible set
Y^{0}
will play an important role in our study of the Lipschitz continuity of
S
.

Stability properties of multifunctions have extensively been investigated, see
[
1
,
7
,
24
,
20
,
9
,
16
]
and the references therein. Robinson
[
18
]
obtained the local upper Lipschitz continuity of a polyhedral multifunction (i.e., its graph is expressed as the union of finitely many polyhedral sets). Mangasarian and Shiau
[
15
]
established the Lipschitz continuity for solutions of linear inequalities, linear programs and certain linear complementarity problems.
Rockafellar and Wets
[
20
, Example 9.35]
(see also Walkup and Wets
[
23
]
) obtained the Lipschitz continuity for a multifunction with graph being one polyhedron.
In
[
12
]
Han and Pang derived the (Lipschitz) continuous (single-valued) solution
function of parametric variational inequalities under functional and constraint perturbations.
Mordukhovich et al.
[
17
]
established characterizations of the single-valuedness and local Lipschitz continuity of the local optimal solutions for parametric second-order cone programs. Beer et al.
[
2
]
analyzed the Lipschitz modulus of the feasible set of linear and convex inequality systems. Under a strong regularity assumption Guo and Xu
[
11
]
obtained the Lipschitz continuity of isolated solutions of perturbed stochastic generalized equations around the given point.
It is worth noting that for a polyhedral multifunction with convex domain, Gowda and Sznajder
[
10
]
showed that it is Lipschitz continuous on the domain if and only if the inverse of the multifunction is open. This seemly complete characterization of Lipschitz continuity may not be easily applied, as the verification of the openness of the inverse of the multifunction can be as difficult as verifying the Lipschitz continuity of the multifunction itself.

In this paper we will employ face theory and polyhedral theory to establish the Lipschitz continuity of the optimal solution multifunction
S
of problem (
4
). For this purpose, we first obtain a verifiable sufficient condition for polyhedral multifunctions to be Lipschitz continuous on its domain by imposing two assumptions: the first one is that its domain is convex and the second one is that the graph of the restriction of the multifunction on the projection of each of the polyhedral sets (in the polyhedral multifunction decomposition) onto the variable space is convex. The convexity assumption of the domain allows one to connect any two points in the domain by a straight line, while this straight line can be divided into a finite number of line segments and each line segment lies in one of the polyhedral sets on which the multifunction is Lipschitz continuous, as the result of the second assumption. The Lipschitz continuity of the multifunction on its domain is acquired by that of these arbitrary line segments with a Lipschitz constant being the maximum of the Lipschitz constants corresponding to the finitely many polyhedral sets. We apply the sufficient condition to establish the Lipschitz continuity of optimal solution multifunction
S
of extended
\ell_{1}
regularization problem (
4
) on its domain only under the assumption that
A
is of full row rank. This full row rank assumption is very weak, which is almost universally used as a basic assumption in the study of theory and algorithms for problems (
2
) and (
3
). Therefore our results are very general.

In our approach, we employ non-empty faces of the polyhedral feasible set
Y^{0}
of the dual problem (
6
) to construct some partitions of index set
I:=\{1,\cdots,n\}
of the variable space. We show that the graph of
S
is expressed as the union of some polyhedral cones which are constructed by these index partitions of
I
. Thus
S
is verified to be a polyhedral multifunction. Moreover, we establish that the domain of the solution multifunction
S
is partitioned as the union of the projections of the above polyhedral cones onto the parameters space. We then prove that the graph of the restriction of
S
on each of these projections is convex, where we employ the single-valuedness property of the mapping
(\lambda,b)\to Ax
on the solution set
S(\lambda,b)
as established in
[
25
]
. We obtain the Lipschitz continuity of
S(\lambda,b)
on its convex domain by applying the established sufficient condition.

As corollaries of the Lipschitz continuity of multifunction
S
in (
5
), we obtain some properties for structures of
S
. Although we have already know that
S
is a polyhedral multifunction, the explicit structure of
S
is not known yet. By applying the Lipschitz continuity of
S
, we obtain the explicit representation of
S(\lambda,b)
as the intersection of finitely many half-spaces by virtue of non-empty faces of the dual feasible set
Y^{0}
. Moreover, for a given face of the dual feasible set
Y^{0}
such that the columns of
A
identified by certain index set is linearly independent (or equivalently a condition used in
[
25
]
), we show that
S
is single-valued and linear on the polyhedral set constructed by the face. We further show that
S
is piecewise linear (resp. linear) around the given point if and only if certain strengthened linear independence condition at the point proposed in
[
22
]
(resp.
[
8
]
) holds. As consequences, we obtain the directional differentiability and differentiability illustrated in
[
3
]
as special cases.

[
20
]
by Rockafellar and Wets.
The Euclidean
\ell_{2}
-norm,
\ell_{1}
-norm and
\ell_{\infty}
-norm of a vector
x\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}
are denoted by
||x||
,
\|x\|_{1}
and
\|x\|_{\infty}
respectively. Let
{\mathbb{B}}
denote the closed unit Euclidean ball,
\mathbb{R}_{+}:=[0,\infty)
and
\mathbb{R}_{++}:=(0,\infty)
. For
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define
[x,y]:=\{x+\theta(y-x)\mid\theta\in[0,1]\}
and
(x,y):=\{x+\theta(y-x)\mid\theta\in(0,1)\}
.

Let
A
be an
m\times n
matrix,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
i\in I
and
K\subset I
. We denote that
A_{i}
is the
i
th column of
A
and
A_{K}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times|K|}
is the submatrix of
A
consisting of all
A_{i}
’s with
i\in K
. We also denote that
x_{K}\in\mathbb{R}^{|K|}
is the subvector of
x
consisting of all
x_{i}
’s with
i\in K
.

Let
C\subset\mathbbm{R}^{n}
. We denote the interior, the relative interior, the boundary, the closure and the affine set of
C
respectively by
\inte C
,
\ri C
,
\bdry C
,
\cl C
and
\aff C
.

A polyhedral set
C
is defined as the intersection of a finite family of
closed half-spaces in
\mathbbm{R}^{n}
, i.e., as the set of solutions to some finite system of inequalities of the form

\alpha_{j}^{\top}x\leq\beta_{j},\quad j=1,\cdots,k,

(7)

for
\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
.
For a nonempty subset
F
of polyhedral set
C
,
F
is said to be a face of
C
if there exists a vector
v\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}
(possibly
v=0
) such that

F=\argmax\{\;v^{\top}x\mid x\in C\}.

The zero-dimensional
faces of
C
are called the extreme points of
C
and the collection of all such points is denoted as
\extr(C)
. In general, if
F
is a half-line face
of
C
, we shall call the direction of
F
an extreme direction of
C
.
Moreover, we deduce from
[
21
, Page 101]
that
F
is a nonempty face of
C
represented by (
7
) if and only if there exists
J\subset\{1,\cdots,k\}
such that

F=\{x\in C\mid\alpha_{j}^{\top}x=\beta_{j}\quad\forall j\in J\}\not=\emptyset.

For a multifunction
G:\mathbbm{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbbm{R}^{m}
, we denote by

\gph G:=\{(x,y)\mid y\in G(x)\},\quad\dom G:=\{x\mid G(x)\not=\emptyset\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\rge G:=\{y\mid\exists x\in\dom G~\mbox{s.t.}~y\in G(x)\}

the graph, the domain and the range of
G
, respectively. For a set
\Omega\subset\mathbbm{R}^{n}
, we denote by

G|_{\Omega}(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}G(x)&\mbox{if}\;x\in\Omega,\\[5.69046pt]
\emptyset&\mbox{if}\;x\not\in\Omega,\end{array}\right.

the multifunction of
G
restricted on
\Omega
. It is clear to see that

\gph G|_{\Omega}=\gph G\cap(\Omega\times\mathbbm{R}^{m})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\dom G|_{\Omega}=\Omega\cap\dom G.

A multifunction
G:\mathbbm{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbbm{R}^{m}
is said to be a polyhedral one if its graph is the union of finitely many polyhedra in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Moreover, for a polyhedral multifunction
G
, if it is further single-valued, we called it a piecewise linear function. Let
G:\mathbbm{R}^{n}\to\mathbbm{R}^{m}
be single-valued. We say that
G
is linear if there exists some matrix
B\in\mathbbm{R}^{m\times n}
such that
G(x)=Bx
and that
G
is linear on some nonempty subset
D
of
\mathbbm{R}^{n}
if there exists a linear function
G^{\prime}
such that
G|_{D}=G^{\prime}|_{D}
.

The definition below is borrowed from
[
20
, Definition 9.26]
.

Definition 1.1

(Lipschitz continuity of multifunctions)
.

A multifunction
G:\mathbbm{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbbm{R}^{m}
is Lipschitz continuous on
X
, a subset of
\mathbbm{R}^{n}
, if it is nonempty-closed-valued on
X
and there exists
\kappa\in\mathbbm{R}_{+}
, called a Lipschitz constant, such that

G(x^{\prime})\subset G(x)+\kappa\|x^{\prime}-x\|{\mathbb{B}}\quad\forall x^{\prime},x\in X.

Let
g:\mathbbm{R}^{n}\to\mathbbm{R}
be a convex function and let
\bar{x}\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}
be a point.
The vector
v\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}
is a subgradient of
g
at
\bar{x}
, written
v\in\partial g(\bar{x})
, if

g(x)\geq g(\bar{x})+v^{\top}(x-\bar{x})\quad\forall x\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}.

2
A sufficient condition

In this section we obtain a verifiable sufficient condition for a polyhedral multifunction
G
to be Lipschitz continuous on its domain.
We make use of the decomposition of the graph of the polyhedral multifunction (
\gph G
) as the union of some polyhedral sets and the convexity of the graph of the restriction of
G
on the projection of each polyhedral set in the decomposition of
\gph G
onto the first variable.

Theorem 2.1

(Lipschitz continuity of polyhedral multifunctions)
.

Consider a polyhedral multifunction
G
, whose graph
\gph G
is expressed as the union of finitely many polyhedral sets
G_{i}
with
1\leq i\leq l
.
Assume that the following conditions hold:

(i)

\dom G
is convex;

(ii)

\gph G|_{Q_{i}}
is convex for all
1\leq i\leq l
, where
Q_{i}
is the image set of
G_{i}
under the projection mapping
(x,y)\mapsto x
.

Then
G
is Lipschitz continuous
not only on each
Q_{i}
with some constant
\kappa_{i}>0
, but also
on its domain with constant
\max_{1\leq i\leq l}\kappa_{i}
.

Proof.

To begin with, we notice from assumption
(ii)
that
\dom G=\bigcup_{i=1}^{l}Q_{i}
with

Q_{i}
being a polyhedral set and

\gph G|_{Q_{i}}=\{(x,y)\in\gph G\mid x\in Q_{i}\}=\left\{(x,y)\in\bigcup_{j=1}^{l}G_{j}\mid x\in Q_{i}\right\}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{l}\left\{(x,y)\in G_{j}\mid x\in Q_{i}\right\}.

That is,
\gph G|_{Q_{i}}
is expressed as a union of finitely many polyhedral sets. It then follows from assumption
(ii)
again and
[
19
, Theorem 19.6]
that
\gph G|_{Q_{i}}
is a polyhedral set. Applying
[
20
, Example 9.35]
,
G
is Lipschitz continuous on each
Q_{i}
with some constant
\kappa_{i}>0
.
That is,

G(x^{\prime})\subset G(x)+\kappa_{i}\|x^{\prime}-x\|\mathbb{B},\quad\forall x^{\prime},x\in Q_{i}.

(8)

Next, we will prove the Lipschitz continuity of
G
on
\dom G
. Indeed, for any
x^{\prime},x\in\dom G
, we denote

x_{\theta}:=x^{\prime}+\theta(x-x^{\prime}),\quad\theta\in[0,1].

It follows from the convexity of
\dom G
(see assumption
(i)
) that we get that
x_{\theta}\in\dom G
for all
\theta\in[0,1]
. This, combined with the fact that
\dom G=\bigcup_{i=1}^{l}Q_{i}
and
Q_{i}
is closed and convex for each
i\in\{1,\cdots,l\}
, yields that there exist
i_{j}\in\{1,\cdots,l\}
and
\theta_{j}\in[0,1]
(
j=0,1,\cdots,k
), with

0=\theta_{0}<\theta_{1}<\cdots<\theta_{k-1}<\theta_{k}=1,

such that

x_{\theta}\in Q_{i_{j}},\quad\forall\theta\in[\theta_{j-1},\theta_{j}].

Then we deduce from (
8
) that

G(x_{\theta_{j-1}})\subset G(x_{\theta_{j}})+\kappa_{i_{j}}\|x_{\theta_{j-1}}-x_{\theta_{j}}\|\mathbb{B}=G(x_{\theta_{j}})+\kappa_{i_{j}}(\theta_{j}-\theta_{j-1})\|x^{\prime}-x\|\mathbb{B},\quad j\in\{1,\cdots,k\}.

Thus,

\begin{array}[]{rlc}G(x^{\prime})=G(x_{\theta_{0}})\subset&G(x_{\theta_{k}})+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\kappa_{i_{j}}(\theta_{j}-\theta_{j-1})\|x^{\prime}-x\|\mathbb{B}\\
\subset&G(x)+\max\limits_{j\in\{1,\cdots,k\}}\kappa_{i_{j}}\|x^{\prime}-x\|\mathbb{B}\\
\subset&G(x)+\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq l}\kappa_{i}\|x^{\prime}-x\|\mathbb{B}.\end{array}

This completes the proof.

Remark 2.1
.

In
[
15
]
, Mangasarian and Shiau studied the Lipschitz continuity of solution multifunctions to system of linear inequalities, linear program and linear complementarity problem. The solution multifunctions of these problems have a polyhedral multifunction graph. Theorem
2.1
can be applied to study the Lipschitz continuity of these multifunctions. However, the details are not presented in here.

Next, we give an example to show that in Theorem
2.1
, when
G
is Lipschitz continuous, both assumptions
(i)
and
(ii)
are not necessarily held.

Example 2.1
.

Let
\Omega_{1}=\mathbbm{R}\times\{1\}
,
\Omega_{2}=\mathbbm{R}\times\{-1\}
,
\Omega_{3}=\{0\}\times[-1,1]
be polyhedral sets and
\Omega=\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2}\cup\Omega_{3}
, and define
G:\Omega\rightrightarrows\mathbbm{R}
as
G(x,y)=\{0,1\}
for all
(x,y)\in\Omega
. Clearly,
G(x,y)
is a constant, and thus
G
is a polyhedral multifunction and Lipschitz continuous on
\Omega
. However,
\dom G=\Omega
is not convex and, for any
i=1,2,3
,
\gph G|_{\Omega_{i}}
is the union of two parallel lines/line segments, which is not convex.

While both assumptions
(i)
and
(ii)
in Theorem
2.1
are not necessary, we next provide two examples to show that the Lipschitz continuity may not be valid whenever either assumption
(i)
or
(ii)
in Theorem
2.1

does not hold.

Example 2.2
.

Take
\Omega
as in Example
2.1
and define
G:\Omega\to\mathbbm{R}
as
G(x,y)=xy
, i.e.,
G(x,y)=x
if
(x,y)\in\Omega_{1}
,
G(x,y)=-x
if
(x,y)\in\Omega_{2}
and
G(x,y)=0
if
(x,y)\in\Omega_{3}
. Then
\gph G|_{\Omega_{1}}=\{(x,1,x)~|~x\in\mathbbm{R}\}
,
\gph G|_{\Omega_{2}}=\{(x,-1,-x)~|~x\in\mathbbm{R}\}
and
\gph G|_{\Omega_{3}}=\{(0,y,0)~|~y\in[-1,1]\}
. Therefore, all
\gph G|_{\Omega_{i}}
,
i=1,2,3
, are polyhedral and
G
is thus a polyhedral multifunction. However,
\dom G=\Omega
is not convex. Thus assumption
(i)
does not hold. And
G
is not Lipschitz continuous on
\Omega
. Indeed, taking
(x_{1}^{\nu},y_{1}^{\nu})=(\nu,1)
and
(x_{2}^{\nu},y_{2}^{\nu})=(\nu,-1)
for all
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

|G(x_{1}^{\nu},y_{1}^{\nu})-G(x_{2}^{\nu},y_{2}^{\nu})|=2\nu=\nu\|(x_{1}^{\nu},y_{1}^{\nu})-(x_{2}^{\nu},y_{2}^{\nu})\|,~~\forall\nu\in\mathbb{N}.

Example 2.3
.

This example is borrowed from
[
15
]
. We consider the linear complementarity problem

Mx+q\geq 0,\quad x\geq 0,\quad x^{\top}(Mx+q)=0,

with

M=\left[~\begin{aligned} 0~~1\\
-1~~0\end{aligned}~\right].

For any
q\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the solution set of the linear complementarity problem is denoted by
G(q)
. We find that
\dom G=\mathbbm{R}\times\mathbbm{R}_{+}
, which is convex. Let

\displaystyle Q_{1}:=(-\infty,0]\times[0,+\infty),~Q_{2}:=\{0\}\times[0,+\infty),

\displaystyle Q_{3}:=(0,+\infty)\times[0,+\infty),~Q_{4}:=\mathbbm{R}\times\{0\}.

Then
\dom G=Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\cup Q_{3}\cup Q_{4}
and

G(q)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &(q_{2},-q_{1})^{\top}~~~&&\mbox{if}~q\in\ri Q_{1},\\
&([0,q_{2}]\times\{0\})^{\top}~~~&&\mbox{if}~q\in\ri Q_{2},\\
&(0,0)^{\top}~~~&&\mbox{if}~q\in\ri Q_{3},\\
&(0,\max\{-q_{1},0\})^{\top}~~~&&\mbox{if}~q\in Q_{4}.\end{aligned}\right.

It is easy to show that
G
is a polyhedral
multifunction, which is not Lipschitz continuous on
\dom G
. Indeed
\gph G|_{Q_{1}}
and
\gph G|_{Q_{3}}
are not convex. Thus assumption
(ii)
does not hold.

3
Main results

In this section we will establish the Lipschitz continuity of the solution multifunction
S
(see (
5
)) for the extended
\ell_{1}
regularization problem (
4
) under the assumption that
A
is of full row rank. Recall
\dom S=\mathbbm{R}_{+}\times\mathbbm{R}^{n}
is a closed and convex set.
Therefore we establish the Lipschitz continuity of
S
by verifying that
S
is a polyhedral multifunction and condition
(ii)
in Theorem
2.1
is satisfied.

We first verify that
S
is a polyhedral multifunction.
Let
I:=\{1,\cdots,n\}
and
\mathcal{I}
denote the collection of all partitions
(I^{+},I^{0},I^{-})
of
I
(i.e.,
I^{+},I^{0}
and
I^{-}
have no common index and
I^{+}\cup I^{0}\cup I^{-}=I
.) For each
(I^{+},I^{0},I^{-})\in\mathcal{I}
, define

W(I^{+},I^{0},I^{-}):=\{x\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}\mid x_{i}>0\;\forall i\in I^{+},x_{i}=0\;\forall i\in I^{0},\;x_{i}<0\;\forall i\in I^{-}\}.

It is clear that the collection of

\ri W(\bar{I}^{+},\bar{I}^{0},\bar{I}^{-})\cap W(\tilde{I}^{+},\tilde{I}^{0},\tilde{I}^{-})=\emptyset\;\forall(\bar{I}^{+},\bar{I}^{0},\bar{I}^{-})\not=(\tilde{I}^{+},\tilde{I}^{0},\tilde{I}^{-})\in{\cal I}

Recall that the feasible set

For each nonempty convex subset

for all

Lemma 3.1

The following properties of the nonempty faces of

. By definition we have

, we denote the right-hand-side of (

(13)

If

\partial\|\cdot\|_{1}(x)=\{v\mid v_{i}=1\;\mbox{if}\;x_{i}>0,\;v_{i}=-1\;\mbox{if}\;x_{i}<0,\;v_{i}\in[-1,1]\;\mbox{if}\;x_{i}=0\}\quad\forall x\in\mathbbm{R}^{n},

Remark 3.2

In

In Proposition

(Optimal and Unique Partition)
.

Assume that

(

Suppose by contradiction that

. Following (
10
) and Lemma
3.2
, we have
\ri S_{F}\subset\gph S
and
\ri S_{F}\cap\ri S_{F^{\prime}}=\emptyset

with

. Then there are some

with

and

with

and

1.
Introduction

One of the cornerstones of extremal combinatorics is Dirac’s theorem stating that every graph
G
on
n\geq 3
vertices with minimum degree
\delta(G)\geq\frac{n}{2}
contains a Hamilton cycle.
On the one hand, the bound on the minimum degree is sharp as there are graphs with minimum degree
\frac{n-1}{2}
that do not contain any Hamilton cycle.
On the other hand,
there are numerous results testifying that any graph
G
with
\delta(G)\geq\frac{n}{2}
contains Hamilton cycles in an extremely strong and robust sense.
This includes random sparsifications of
G

[
10
]
, colour or conflict restrictions on the edges of
G

[
11
]
,
and the fact, which is proved by Cuckler and Kahn
[
3
]
, that such graphs contain at least
(\frac{1}{2}-o(1))^{n}n!
Hamilton cycles.
This lower bound is easily seen to be optimal by considering the binomial random graph.
To be more precise, for every labelled graph
H
on
n
vertices,
K_{n}
contains
n!
copies of
H
and
|\operatorname{Aut}(H)|^{-1}n!
unlabelled copies of
H
, where
\operatorname{Aut}(H)
refers to the automorphism group of
H
.
Hence
G(n,p)
contains
p^{e(H)}|\operatorname{Aut}(H)|^{-1}n!
unlabelled copies of
H
in expectation and by Markov’s inequality,
there is a graph
G
with
\delta(G)\geq(1+o(1))pn
and at most
(1+o(1))^{n}p^{e(H)}|\operatorname{Aut}(H)|^{-1}n!
subgraphs isomorphic to
H
.

For all of the above results, it does not really matter whether we ask for a Hamilton cycle or a Hamilton path.
As a Hamilton path is potentially the simplest spanning tree,
one may ask whether the above results are also true for other spanning trees.
In this paper we solve this problem for the counting question even in the case where the trees have vertices of substantially large degree.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose
G
is a graph on
n
vertices with
\delta(G)\geq(\frac{1}{2}+o(1))n
and
T
is a tree on
n
vertices with
\Delta(T)\leq e^{o(\sqrt{\log n})}
.
Then
G
contains at least
|\operatorname{Aut}(T)|^{-1}(\frac{1}{2}-o(1))^{n}n!
copies of
T
.

Observe that the error term in the minimum degree bound on
G
cannot be completely omitted in contrast to Dirac’s theorem.
Komlós, Sárközy and Szemerédi proved that
\delta(G)\geq(\frac{1}{2}+o(1))n
suffices to guarantee any bounded degree tree on
n
vertices as a subgraph, confirming a conjecture of Bollobás, and they
later proved that under the same condition,
G
even contains any tree
T
on
n
vertices with
\Delta(T)\leq o(\frac{n}{\log n})

[
8
,
9
]
.
Hence our result also strengthens these results for trees
T
with
\Delta(T)\leq e^{o(\sqrt{\log n})}
.

We note further that there are trees
T
where
|\operatorname{Aut}(T)|
grows exponentially in
n
, for example, the balanced binary tree.
However, if
T
is a path or a cycle, then
|\operatorname{Aut}(T)|\leq 2n
and
hence this quantity is smaller than the error term and can be neglected.

Cuckler and Kahn proved a significantly stronger result than Theorem
1.1
.
To state their result, we say
a function
\mathbf{x}\colon E(G)\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is an
edge weighting
of
G
and
write
\mathbf{x}_{e}
for
\mathbf{x}(e)
.
We call an edge weighting a
perfect fractional matching
if
\sum_{w\in N(v)}\textbf{x}_{vw}=1
for every vertex
v\in\penalty\ V(G)
.
Moreover, let
h(G)=\max_{\mathbf{x}}\sum_{e\in E(G)}-\mathbf{x}_{e}\log\mathbf{x}_{e}
where
the maximum is taken over all perfect fractional matchings of
G
.
Here and throughout the paper, we write
\log=\log_{2}
.

Theorem 1.2

(Cuckler and Kahn
[
3
, Theorem 1.2]
)
.

Suppose
G
is a graph on
n
vertices with
\delta(G)\geq\frac{n}{2}
.
Then
G
contains at least
2^{2h(G)-n\log e-o(n)}
Hamilton cycles.

In fact, Cuckler and Kahn showed in another paper that the lower bound in Theorem
1.2
is also an upper bound (up to the error term)
[
2
]
.

Our main theorem implies the generalisation of Theorem
1.2
to spanning trees.

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose
G
is a graph on
n
vertices with
\delta(G)\geq(\frac{1}{2}+o(1))n
and
T
is a tree on
n
vertices with
\Delta(T)\leq e^{o(\sqrt{\log n})}
.
Then
G
contains at least
|\operatorname{Aut}(T)|^{-1}2^{2h(G)-n\log e-o(n)}
copies of
T
.

Note that
h(G)\geq\frac{n}{2}\log\delta(G)
for any graph
G
on
n
vertices with
\delta(G)\geq\frac{n}{2}

[
3
, Theorem 1.3]

and hence Theorem
1.3
implies Theorem
1.1
.

Clearly, the lower bound given in Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
cannot be improved
as we may choose
T
be to be Hamilton path.
However, note that Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
are tight for
every
such tree.

Recently, Kathapurkar and Montgomery transferred the above-mentioned theorem due to Komlós, Sárközy and Szemerédi to digraphs thereby implying their result and
extending earlier works of Mycroft and Naia
[
12
]
.
To state their theorem,
let
\delta^{0}(G)
be the
minimum semidegree
of a digraph
G

which is the maximum
k
such that every vertex has at least
k
in-neighbours and
k
out-neighbours
and let
\Delta(G)
be the maximum degree of the underlying undirected graph (counting parallel edges).

Theorem 1.4

(Kathapurkar and Montgomery
[
7
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

Suppose
G
is a digraph on
n
vertices with
\delta^{0}(G)\geq(\frac{1}{2}+o(1))n

and
T
is an oriented tree on
n
vertices with
\Delta(T)\leq o(\frac{n}{\log n})
.
Then
G
contains a copy of
T
.

We also generalise their theorem up to a more restrictive bound on the maximum degree.
For a digraph
G
with vertex set
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
,
let
B_{G}
the bipartite graph corresponding to
G
,
that is, the vertex set of
B_{G}
is

\{v_{1}^{+},v_{1}^{-},\ldots,v_{n}^{+},v_{n}^{-}\}
and the edge set is
\{v_{i}^{+}v_{j}^{-}\mid v_{i}v_{j}\in E(G)\}
.
We define
h(G):=h(B_{G})
to be the entropy of
G
.
The following theorem is our main result.

Theorem 1.5
.

Suppose
G
is a digraph on
n
vertices with
\delta^{0}(G)\geq(\frac{1}{2}+o(1))n
and
T
is an oriented tree on
n
vertices with
\Delta(T)\leq e^{o(\sqrt{\log n})}
.
Then
G
contains at least
|\operatorname{Aut}(T)|^{-1}2^{h(G)-n\log e-o(n)}
copies of
T
.

Again, the bound on the number of copies of
T
is optimal up to the error term.
Observe that Theorem
1.5
implies Theorem
1.3

by replacing any edge in the undirected graph by two antidirected parallel edges and orienting
T
arbitrarily.

Note that
h(G)\geq n\log\delta^{0}(G)
whenever
\delta^{0}(G)\geq\frac{n}{2}

[
3
, Theorem 3.1]
. This yields the more explicit lower bound of
|\operatorname{Aut}(T)|^{-1}(\frac{1}{2}-o(1))^{n}n!
copies of
T
in
G
.

Our paper is organised as follows.
In Section
2
,
we give an outline of our proof method.
In Section
3
,
we introduce several basic concepts.
In Section
4
,
we prove that there are perfect fractional matchings that almost yield the maximal entropy and are particularly well distributed.
In Section
5
,
random trees are considered that yield the desired partial embedding of our tree
and in Section
6
,
we prove our main theorem.

2.
Proof Sketch

We explain our idea for the proof of Theorem
1.5
by explaining the proof idea of Theorem
1.3
and outlining the additional complications when transitioning from graphs to digraphs later.
So suppose we are in the setting of Theorem
1.3
,
that is, we are given a graph
G
on
n
vertices with
\delta(G)\geq(\frac{1}{2}+o(1))n
and a tree
T
on
n
vertices with an appropriate bound on
\Delta(T)
.
We first partition
T
into a collection of subtrees
T^{\prime},T_{1},\ldots,T_{k}
such that
T^{\prime}\cup T_{1}\cup\ldots\cup T_{i}
is a tree for all
i

and
T^{\prime}
is only adjacent to
T_{1}
.
Now we proceed in an inductive manner and
show that there are suitably many embeddings of
T_{i+1}
given we already fixed any embedding of
T_{1}\cup\ldots\cup T_{i}
in
G
.
At the end we embed
T^{\prime}
and complete the embedding.
This is possible by, before embedding
T_{1},\ldots,T_{k}
, taking aside a small vertex set with particular properties which ensures that we can always embed
T^{\prime}
(for this last step we utilise a result due to Kathapurkar and Montgomery).

In order to show one step of our induction,
we embed
T_{i+1}
randomly.
To be more precise, we interpret the weights of a perfect fractional matching of
G
as transition probabilities of a random walk-like structure.
If
T_{i+1}
is a path, we simply consider a random walk in
G-(T_{1}\cup\ldots\cup T_{i})
,
otherwise, the random walk branches suitably according to the structure of
T_{i+1}
.
The entropy of this random embedding is linked to the entropy of
G
and thus we obtain a lower bound on the number of realisations of the random embedding of
T_{i+1}
with the little caveat that we want to condition on the event that the realisations have random-like properties (this is what we call
well-behaved
).
As we show in Lemma
3.1
, the entropy decreases only slightly by conditioning on a likely event.

The sizes of the trees
T_{i+1}
have to be chosen large enough that one can apply concentration inequalities in order to verify that most realisations of random embeddings are in fact well-behaved and
the sizes of the
T_{i+1}
have to be small enough in order to be able to exploit that at every step of the random embedding we can still use essentially all vertices in
G-(T_{1}\cup\ldots\cup T_{i})
.

On a high level, Cuckler and Kahn already followed this path and
hence our proof shares ideas with their proof.
However, focusing only on cycles simplifies the arguments at several places considerably and new ideas are needed.

In order to move from graphs to digraphs,
we mainly have to adapt the definition of a random tree, as the direction of the edge between two in
T
adjacent vertices determines whether we use an in-neighbour or an out-neighbour of the image of the previously embedded vertex as the image of its neighbour.

3.
Preliminaries

3.1.
Notation

For an integer
n\geq 1
, let
[n]:=\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
[n]_{0}:=[n]\cup\{0\}
.
For real numbers
\alpha,\beta,\delta
, we write
\alpha=(1\pm\delta)\beta
if
(1-\delta)\beta\leq\alpha\leq(1+\delta)\beta
.
If we write
\alpha\ll\beta
, where
\alpha,\beta\in(0,1]
, there is a non-decreasing function
\alpha_{0}\colon(0,1]\to(0,1]
such that for any
\beta\in(0,1]
, the subsequent statement holds for
\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{0}(\beta)]
.
Hierarchies with more constants are defined in a similar way and should be read from right to left.
If
\frac{1}{n}
appears in a hierarchy, this implicitly means that
n
is a natural number.
We ignore rounding issues when they do not affect the argument.

We write
\log:=\log_{2}
and
\ln:=\log_{e}
.
For
x\geq 0
, we often use the inequality
\log(1+x)\leq 2x
that follows immediately from
1+x\leq e^{x}\leq 2^{2x}
.

3.2.
Entropy

Let
X
be a discrete random variable with possible outcomes
x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}
.
Let
p_{i}:=\mathbb{P}[X=x_{i}]
.
The
entropy
of
X
is defined as

H(X):=-\sum_{i=1}^{N}p_{i}\log p_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}p_{i}\log\frac{1}{p_{i}},

where we set
0\log 0:=0
. If
\mathcal{E}
is an event with nonzero probability and
p_{i}^{\prime}:=\mathbb{P}[X=x_{i}\mid\mathcal{E}]
, we define by
H(X\mid\mathcal{E}):=-\sum_{i=1}^{N}p_{i}^{\prime}\log p_{i}^{\prime}

the
conditional entropy of
X
given
\mathcal{E}
.

We define
\operatorname{supp}X:=\{i\in[N]\mid p_{i}\neq 0\}
and
\operatorname{supp}(X\mid\mathcal{E}):=\{i\in[N]\mid p_{i}^{\prime}\neq 0\}
.
If
Y
is a discrete random variable with possible outcomes
y_{1},\ldots,y_{M}
, we define by
H(X\mid Y):=\sum_{i\in\operatorname{supp}Y}\mathbb{P}[Y=y_{i}]H(X\mid Y=y_{i})
the
conditional entropy of
X
given
Y
.

By Jensen’s inequality the entropy of a random variable is maximised by the uniform distribution, that is,
H(X)\leq\log|\operatorname{supp}X|
and
H(X\mid\mathcal{E})\leq\log|\operatorname{supp}(X\mid\mathcal{E})|
. In particular,
H(X)\leq\log N
.

The following lemma shows that
H(X\mid\mathcal{E})
cannot be much smaller than
H(X)
if
\mathcal{E}
is an event that is very likely and if
p_{i}
is not too small for all
i\in[N]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X
be a random variable with values in
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\}
.
Let
A\geq 16
be such that
\mathbb{P}[X=x_{i}]\geq\frac{1}{A}
for all
i\in[N]
.
Let
J\subseteq\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\}
and let
\mathcal{E}
be the event that
X\in J
.
Suppose that
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}]\geq 1-a
for some
0\leq a\leq\frac{1}{2}
. Then

H(X)-H(X\mid\mathcal{E})\leq 2a\log A.

Proof.

We define
p_{i}:=\mathbb{P}[X=x_{i}]
and
p_{i}^{\prime}:=\mathbb{P}[X=x_{i}\mid\mathcal{E}]
as well as
I:=\operatorname{supp}(X\mid\mathcal{E})=\{i\in[N]\mid x_{i}\in J\}
. Note that
p_{i}^{\prime}=0
if
i\not\in I
and
p_{i}\leq p_{i}^{\prime}=\frac{p_{i}}{\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}]}\leq\frac{p_{i}}{1-a}
if
i\in I
. Thus,
p_{i}^{\prime}\log p_{i}^{\prime}\leq p_{i}\log p_{i}^{\prime}\leq p_{i}\log\frac{p_{i}}{1-a}
for
i\in I
. In particular,

\displaystyle-\sum_{i\in I}p_{i}\log p_{i}+\sum_{i\in I}p_{i}^{\prime}\log p_{i}^{\prime}\leq-\sum_{i\in I}p_{i}\log p_{i}+\sum_{i\in I}p_{i}\log\frac{p_{i}}{1-a}=\log\frac{1}{1-a}\cdot\sum_{i\in I}p_{i}\leq\log\frac{1}{1-a}.

Combining this with
-\sum_{i\in[N]\setminus I}p_{i}\log p_{i}\leq\log A\cdot\sum_{i\in[N]\setminus I}p_{i}\leq a\log A
, we obtain

\displaystyle H(X)-H(X\mid\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i\in[N]}p_{i}\log p_{i}+\sum_{i\in[N]}p_{i}^{\prime}\log p_{i}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i\in I}p_{i}\log p_{i}+\sum_{i\in I}p_{i}^{\prime}\log p_{i}^{\prime}-\sum_{i\in[N]\setminus I}p_{i}\log p_{i}

\displaystyle\leq\log\frac{1}{1-a}+a\log A.

Using
\log\frac{1}{1-a}\leq\log(1+2a)\leq 4a\leq a\log A
yields the desired result.
∎

3.3.
Graphs

Let
G
be a graph.
By
V(G)
we denote the vertex set and by
E(G)
the edge set of
G
, respectively.
The
order
of
G
is the number of its vertices (sometimes we simply write
\lvert G\rvert
instead of
\lvert V(G)\rvert
).
The
size
of
G
is the number of its edges.
For an edge
\{v,w\}\in E(G)
we simply write
vw
or
wv
.
The
neighbourhood
of a vertex
v\in V(G)
is defined as
N(v):=N_{G}(v):=\{w\in V(G)\mid vw\in E(G)\}
.
The
degree
of a vertex
v\in V(G)
is
\deg(v):=|N(v)|
.
If
S\subseteq V(G)
and
v\in V(G)
, we denote
N_{G,S}(v):=N_{G}(v)\cap S
.
For a subgraph
H
of
G
, we define
N_{G,H}(v):=N_{G,V(H)}(v)
.
We write
\delta(G)
for the minimum degree and
\Delta(G)
for the maximum degree of a graph
G
, respectively.
An
(n,\varepsilon)
-graph
is a graph
G
on
n
vertices with
\delta(G)\geq(\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon)n
.

If
G
is a graph and
S\subseteq V(G)
, we write
G[S]
for the subgraph induced by
S
in
G
, that is,
G[S]
is the graph with vertex set
S
and edge set
\{vw\in E(G)\mid v,w\in S\}
. For a vertex set
S\subseteq V(G)
and induced subgraphs
H,H^{\prime}\subseteq G
, we define
G-S:=G[V(G)\setminus S]
,
G-H:=G-V(H)
, as well as
H\cup S:=G[V(H)\cup S]
and
H\cup H^{\prime}:=G[V(H)\cup V(H^{\prime})]
.

Let
G,H
be graphs and
\varphi\colon V(G)\to V(H)
.
We call
\varphi
an
isomorphism

if
\varphi
is bijective and
vw\in E(G)
if and only if
\varphi(v)\varphi(w)\in E(H)
.
We say that
G
and
H
are
isomorphic

if there exists an isomorphism between
G
and
H
.
If
G,H
are graphs and
H^{\prime}
is a subgraph of
G
which is isomorphic to
H
, we call
H^{\prime}
a
copy
of
H
in
G
.
We call an isomorphism
\varphi\colon V(G)\to V(G)
an
automorphism
of
G
.
The automorphisms of a graph
G
form a group
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
, the
automorphism group
of
G
.

3.4.
Digraphs

Let
G
be a digraph. For an edge
(v,w)\in E(G)
, we simply write
vw
.
We denote the
out-neighbourhood
of a vertex
v\in V(G)
as
N^{+}(v):=N^{+}_{G}(v):=\{w\in V(G)\mid vw\in E(G)\}
and the
out-degree
of
v
by
\deg^{+}(v):=|N^{+}(v)|
.
Analogously, we define the
in-neighbourhood
as
N^{-}(v):=N^{-}_{G}(v):=\{w\in V(G)\mid wv\in E(G)\}
and the
in-degree
of
v
by
\deg^{-}(v):=|N^{-}(v)|
.
By
\delta^{+}(G)
we denote the
minimum out-degree
and by
\delta^{-}(G)
the
minimum in-degree
of
G
, respectively.
By
\delta^{0}(G):=\min_{v\in V(G)}\{\deg^{+}(v),\deg^{-}(v)\}
we denote the
minimum semidegree
of
G
.
An
(n,\varepsilon)
-digraph
is a digraph
G
on
n
vertices with
\delta^{0}(G)\geq(\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon)n
.
The other notions defined for graphs are transferred easily to digraphs.

Note that any digraph
G
with vertex set
V(G):=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
corresponds to a balanced bipartite graph
B_{G}
with vertex set
V(B_{G}):=\{v_{1}^{+},v_{1}^{-},\ldots,v_{n}^{+},v_{n}^{-}\}
and edge set
E(B_{G}):=\{v_{i}^{+}v_{j}^{-}\mid v_{i}v_{j}\in E(G)\}
.

3.5.
Edge weightings and entropy of edge weightings

Let
G
be a (di)graph on
n
vertices.
A function
\textbf{x}\colon E(G)\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is called an
edge weighting
.
We write
\textbf{x}_{e}
for
\textbf{x}(e)
.
An edge weighting is
b
-normal
if
\frac{1}{bn}\leq\textbf{x}_{e}\leq\frac{b}{n}
for each
e\in E(G)
.
We call an edge weighting a
perfect fractional matching
if
\sum_{w\in N(v)}\textbf{x}_{vw}=1
for every vertex
v\in\penalty\ V(G)
in case
G
is a graph and if
\sum_{w\in N^{+}(v)}\textbf{x}_{vw}=1=\sum_{w\in N^{-}(v)}\textbf{x}_{wv}
for every vertex
v\in\penalty\ V(G)
in case
G
is a digraph (in this case,
x
, extended to
B_{G}
in the obvious way, is a perfect fractional matching of
B_{G}
).
Note that any perfect fractional matching
x
of a graph
G
on
n
vertices satisfies
\sum_{e\in E(G)}\textbf{x}_{e}=\frac{n}{2}
.

Let
x
be an edge weighting of a (di)graph
G
.
If
M\subseteq E(G)
, we define by

h_{\textbf{x}}(M):=\sum_{e\in M}\textbf{x}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\textbf{x}_{e}}

the
entropy of
M
. We define the entropy of
x
by
h(\textbf{x}):=h_{\textbf{x}}(E(G))
and the entropy of
G
by
h(G):=\max\{h(\textbf{x})\mid\text{$\textbf{x}$ is a perfect fractional matching of\penalty\ $G$}\}
.
Note that any perfect fractional matching
x
of a graph
G
on
n
vertices satisfies
h(\textbf{x})\leq\frac{n}{2}\log n
by Jensen’s inequality, and
h(\textbf{x})\leq n\log n
in case
G
is a digraph. Note further that any
b
-normal perfect fractional matching
x
satisfies
h(\textbf{x})\geq\frac{n}{2}\log\frac{n}{b}
in case
G
is a graph and
h(\textbf{x})\geq n\log\frac{n}{b}
in case
G
is a digraph.

For
v\in V(G)
and a perfect fractional matching
x
, we define by

h_{\textbf{x}}(v):=\sum_{w\in N(v)}\textbf{x}_{vw}\log\frac{1}{\textbf{x}_{vw}}

the
entropy of
v
and by
h_{\textbf{x}}^{+}(v)
and
h_{\textbf{x}}^{-}(v)
, respectively, the analogous notions for digraphs.

3.6.
Markov chains and random walks

For a set
S=\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}\}
and real matrices
P^{(t)}=(p^{(t)}_{ij})_{i,j\in[n]}
, we say that a sequence of random variables
Z=(Z_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is a
Markov chain
with
state space

S
and
transition matrix

at time

if

\mathbb{P}[Z_{t+1}=s_{i_{t+1}}\mid Z_{0}=s_{i_{0}},\ldots,Z_{t}=s_{i_{t}}]=\mathbb{P}[Z_{t+1}=s_{i_{t+1}}\mid Z_{t}=s_{i_{t}}]=p^{(t)}_{i_{t}i_{t+1}}

holds for all

.

Let

3.7.
Azuma’s inequality

A sequence
Z_{0},\ldots,Z_{k}
of random variables is a
martingale
if
Z_{0}
is a fixed real number and
\mathbb{E}[Z_{i}\mid Z_{0},\ldots,Z_{i-1}]=Z_{i-1}
for all
i\in[k]
.

We will need the following inequality by Azuma, see for example
[
1
]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose
Z_{0},\ldots,Z_{k}
is a martingale and
|Z_{i}-Z_{i-1}|\leq c
for all
i\in[k]
. Then for all
t>0
, we have

\mathbb{P}[|Z_{k}-Z_{0}|\geq t]\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{2kc^{2}}\right).

3.8.
Trees and tree decomposition

We write
(T,r_{0})
for a rooted tree
T
with root

.
Embedding a rooted tree

in the plane gives a natural breadth-first ordering
(r_{0},\ldots,r_{m})
of the vertices of

.
By
T=(r_{0},\ldots,r_{m})
we denote a rooted tree with fixed breadth-first order.
The
depth
of a vertex

is the length of the unique path from

to
v
.
For a vertex
v
in a rooted tree
(T,r)
, let
T(v)
be the subtree of

containing all vertices below

(including

),
that is, all vertices

such that the path between

and

in

contains

.
We need the following simple lemma proven in
[
5
]
.

Lemma 3.3

(
[
5
, Proposition 6.5]
)
.

Suppose

and
n\geq t\geq 1
. Then for any rooted tree
(T,r)
on
n

, there exists a collection
(T_{1},t_{1}),\ldots,(T_{k},t_{k})
of pairwise vertex-disjoint rooted subtrees such that

(i)

,

(ii)

for every

,

(iii)

for every

.

Note that, in particular,

and

.
The construction of the subtrees is done in reverse order by choosing

. An easy modification of this argument yields the following claim.

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose

. Let

. Then there exist pairwise vertex-disjoint rooted subtrees

such that, denoting

,

(i)

V(T)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}V(T_{i})
,

(ii)

T_{i}\subseteq T(t_{i})
for every

,

(iii)

the depth of

For

of

. This yields the following result.

Lemma 3.5
.

Suppose
n,\Delta\geq 2
. Let

be a rooted tree on

. Let

.
Then there exist rooted subtrees
(T_{1},t_{1}),\ldots,(T_{k},t_{k})

3.9.
Random trees

We define a random tree as a random walk that branches at some vertices, and the branches are independent random trees.

Suppose
G
is a graph on
n
vertices. Let
x
be a perfect fractional matching of
G
,
let
T=(r_{0},\ldots,r_{m})
be a tree
and let
R=(R_{0},\ldots,R_{m})
be a random vector whose entries take values in
V(G)
and which represents the ordering of
T
as follows.
Let
R_{0}
be an arbitrary vertex in
G
.

We embed
T
in a root-to-leaf-manner into
G
, where the children of any vertex are chosen independently according to the perfect fractional matching of
G
.
To be more precise, suppose
r_{j}
is a child of
r_{i}
. Let
M:=T-(T(r_{j})\cup\{r_{i}\})
. Then
M
consists of all vertices whose embedding has no influence on the embedding of
r_{j}
, given
r_{i}
has already been embedded. Let
I
be the index set of the vertices in
M
, that is,
I:=\{i\in[m]_{0}\mid r_{i}\in M\}
.
We define
p_{vw}^{ij}:=\mathbb{P}[R_{j}=w\mid R_{i}=v,\bigwedge_{i^{\prime}\in I^{\prime}}R_{i^{\prime}}=v_{i^{\prime}}]:=\textbf{x}_{vw}
for all
I^{\prime}\subseteq I
if
vw\in E(G)
, and
p_{vw}^{ij}:=0
otherwise.
Note that, in particular, every path in
T
along a root-to-leaf path, when embedded into
G
, can be regarded as a random walk on
G
.
We call
R=(R_{0},\ldots,R_{m})
a
random tree according to
T
and
x
in
G
. We may omit
x
if it is clear from the context.

Observe that we may have
R_{i}=R_{j}
for some
i\neq j
, that is, a realisation of
R
in
G
might not be a tree.

The definition in case
T
is an oriented tree and
G
a digraph works analogously, except, provided
r_{j}
is a child of
r_{i}
, we define
p_{vw}^{ij}:=\textbf{x}_{vw}
if
r_{i}r_{j}\in E(T)
and
vw\in E(G)
, and
p_{vw}^{ij}:=\textbf{x}_{wv}
if
r_{j}r_{i}\in E(T)
and
wv\in E(G)
, and
p_{vw}^{ij}:=0
otherwise.

4.
Normal perfect fractional matchings with large entropy

The aim of this section is to prove that any
(n,\varepsilon)
-digraph

G
admits a perfect fractional matching that has large entropy
and is
b
-normal
for some not too large
b
(Theorem
4.4
).
The latter is needed in Section
5
to show that a random tree is
well-behaved
with high probability.
The fact that the matching has large entropy plays an important role in finding a lower bound on the number of trees of appropriate size in Section
6
.
In Lemma
4.5
we show that removing a random-like vertex set from
G
results in a digraph whose entropy is as large as expected by the natural heuristic. In Lemma
4.6
we also show that removing any not too large vertex set from
G
results in a digraph whose entropy is close to
h(G)
.

We need the following lemmas proven in
[
3
,
4
]
.

Lemma 4.1

(
[
4
, Lemma 4.2]
)
.

Suppose
\frac{1}{n}\ll\frac{1}{b}\ll\varepsilon
. Let
G
be an
(n,\varepsilon)
-digraph.
Then
G
admits a
b
-normal perfect fractional matching.

Lemma 4.2

(
[
3
, Lemma 3.2]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph and
x
an edge weighting of
G
.
Let
vwuz
be a 4-cycle in
G
where
\textbf{x}_{vw}\textbf{x}_{uz}\geq\textbf{x}_{wu}\textbf{x}_{zv}
.
Let
\tilde{\textbf{x}}\colon E(G)\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be defined by
\tilde{\textbf{x}}_{e}:=\textbf{x}_{e}
if
e\not\in\{vw,wu,uz,zv\}
and
\tilde{\textbf{x}}_{vw}:=\textbf{x}_{vw}-\alpha
,
\tilde{\textbf{x}}_{uz}:=\textbf{x}_{uz}-\alpha
,
\tilde{\textbf{x}}_{wu}:=\textbf{x}_{wu}+\alpha
,
\tilde{\textbf{x}}_{zv}:=\textbf{x}_{zv}+\alpha
for some
\alpha>0
such that
\tilde{\textbf{x}}_{vw},\tilde{\textbf{x}}_{wu},\tilde{\textbf{x}}_{uz},\tilde{\textbf{x}}_{zv}\in[0,1]
.
If
\tilde{\textbf{x}}_{vw}\tilde{\textbf{x}}_{uz}\geq\tilde{\textbf{x}}_{wu}\tilde{\textbf{x}}_{zv}
, then
\tilde{\textbf{x}}
is an edge weighting of
G
with
h(\tilde{\textbf{x}})\geq h(\textbf{x})
.
Additionally, if
x
is a perfect fractional matching, so is
\tilde{\textbf{x}}
.

The following lemma yields an upper bound on the sum of edge weights of all edges with large edge weight, given the perfect fractional matching has large entropy.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
G
be a graph on
2n
vertices and let
x
be a perfect fractional matching of
G
.
Let
b>1
and
M:=\{e\in E(G)\mid\textbf{x}_{e}\geq\frac{b}{n}\}
.
Suppose
h(\textbf{x})\geq n\log\frac{n}{2}
.
Then
\sum_{e\in M}\textbf{x}_{e}\leq\frac{4n}{\log b}
.

Proof.

Observe the following consequence of Jensen’s inequality. If
M\subseteq E(G)
and
\sum_{e\in M}\textbf{x}_{e}=a
, then
h_{\textbf{x}}(M)\leq a\log\frac{|M|}{a}
.
As
\sum_{e\in E(G)}\textbf{x}_{e}=n
, we have
|M|\leq\frac{n^{2}}{b}
.
Let
\beta:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{e\in M}\textbf{x}_{e}
.
By the observation stated above, with
\beta n
playing the role of
a
, we obtain
h_{\textbf{x}}(M)\leq\beta n\log\frac{n}{b\beta}
.
Similarly,
|E(G)\setminus M|\leq 2n^{2}
and
\sum_{e\in E(G)\setminus M}\textbf{x}_{e}=(1-\beta)n
.
We again use the observation stated above, now with
(1-\beta)n
playing the role of
a
, to obtain
h_{\textbf{x}}(E(G)\setminus M)\leq\left(1-\beta\right)n\log\frac{2n}{1-\beta}
.
Note that
-x\log x-(1-x)\log(1-x)\leq 1
for any
x\in[0,1]
, which also follows from Jensen’s inequality. In particular,
-\beta n\log\beta-(1-\beta)n\log(1-\beta)\leq n
.
Thus,

\displaystyle h(\textbf{x})

\displaystyle=h_{\textbf{x}}(M)+h_{\textbf{x}}(E(G)\setminus M)\leq\beta n\log\frac{n}{b\beta}+(1-\beta)n\log\frac{2n}{1-\beta}

\displaystyle=\beta n\log\frac{n}{2}+\beta n\log\frac{2}{b\beta}+(1-\beta)n\log\frac{n}{2}+(1-\beta)n\log\frac{4}{1-\beta}

\displaystyle=n\log\frac{n}{2}-\beta n\log\beta-(1-\beta)n\log(1-\beta)+\beta n+2(1-\beta)n-\beta n\log b

\displaystyle\leq n\log\frac{n}{2}+4n-\beta n\log b.

As
h(\textbf{x})\geq n\log\frac{n}{2}
by assumption, we obtain
\beta\leq\frac{4}{\log b}
.
∎

The following result shows that we can modify a perfect fractional matching with large entropy to obtain a perfect fractional matching which is
b
-normal and whose entropy is only slightly smaller. Note that any digraph
G
on
n
vertices with
\delta^{0}(G)\geq\frac{n}{2}
admits a perfect fractional matching
m
that satisfies
h(\textbf{m})\geq n\log\delta^{0}(G)
(
[
3
, Theorem 3.1]
).

Theorem 4.4
.

Suppose
\frac{1}{n}\ll\frac{1}{b}\ll\varepsilon
. Let
G
be an
(n,\varepsilon)
-digraph
and let
m
be a perfect fractional matching of
G
that satisfies
h(\textbf{m})\geq n\log\frac{n}{2}+\varepsilon n
. Then there is a
b
-normal perfect fractional matching
x
of
G
that satisfies
h(\textbf{x})\geq h(\textbf{m})-\varepsilon n
.

Proof.

We first prove that
G
admits a matching
x
that satisfies
h(\textbf{x})\geq h(\textbf{m})-\varepsilon n
and where each edge weight is not too small by taking a linear combination of
m
and a
c
-normal perfect fractional matching. We then show that it is possible to reduce the weight of edges with large weight without reducing the entropy.

Let
\lambda
and
c
be such that
\frac{1}{b}\ll\lambda\ll\frac{1}{c}\ll\varepsilon
.
Let
n
be a
c
-normal perfect fractional matching of
G
(which exists by Lemma
4.1
).
Define
\textbf{x}:=(1-\lambda)\textbf{m}+\lambda\textbf{n}
.
Then
x
is a perfect fractional matching and

(4.1)

h(\textbf{x})=(1-\lambda)\sum_{e\in E(G)}\textbf{m}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\textbf{x}_{e}}+\lambda\sum_{e\in E(G)}\textbf{n}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\textbf{x}_{e}}.

In what follows we bound both terms in (
4.1
) individually.
Observe that
\log\frac{1}{\textbf{x}_{e}}=\log\frac{1}{\textbf{m}_{e}}+\log\frac{1}{1-\lambda+\lambda\frac{\textbf{n}_{e}}{\textbf{m}_{e}}}\geq\log\frac{1}{\textbf{m}_{e}}-\log(1+\lambda\frac{\textbf{n}_{e}}{\textbf{m}_{e}})\geq\log\frac{1}{\textbf{m}_{e}}-2\lambda\frac{\textbf{n}_{e}}{\textbf{m}_{e}}
. This yields

(4.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in E(G)}\textbf{m}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\textbf{x}_{e}}

\displaystyle\geq h(\textbf{m})-2\lambda\sum_{e\in E(G)}\textbf{n}_{e}=h(\textbf{m})-2\lambda n,

which yields a lower bound for the first term in (
4.1
).

To bound the second term in (
4.1
) from below, we proceed as follows. For
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
M_{i}:=\{e\in E(G)\mid\frac{c^{i-1}}{n}\leq\textbf{m}_{e}<\frac{c^{i}}{n}\}
.
Note that
|M_{i}|\leq\frac{n^{2}}{c^{i-1}}
.
Define further
N_{-}:=\bigcup_{i<0}M_{i}
,
N_{0}:=M_{0}\cup M_{1}
and
N_{+}:=\bigcup_{i\geq 2}M_{i}
.

Observe that
\textbf{n}_{e}\geq\frac{1}{cn}
and thus
\textbf{n}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\textbf{n}_{e}}\leq\textbf{n}_{e}\log(cn)
. This implies

(4.3)

\textbf{n}_{e}\log n\geq\textbf{n}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\textbf{n}_{e}}-\textbf{n}_{e}\log c.

For
e\in N_{-}
, we have
\textbf{x}_{e}\leq\textbf{n}_{e}
and thus

(4.4)

\sum_{e\in N_{-}}\textbf{n}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\textbf{x}_{e}}\geq\sum_{e\in N_{-}}\textbf{n}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\textbf{n}_{e}}.

For
e\in N_{0}
, we have
\textbf{x}_{e}\leq\frac{c}{n}
. This yields

(4.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in N_{0}}\textbf{n}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\textbf{x}_{e}}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{e\in N_{0}}\textbf{n}_{e}\log\frac{n}{c}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{n_elogn}}}{{\geq}}\sum_{e\in N_{0}}\textbf{n}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\textbf{n}_{e}}-2\log c\cdot\sum_{e\in N_{0}}\textbf{n}_{e}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{e\in N_{0}}\textbf{n}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\textbf{n}_{e}}-2n\log c.

For
e\in N_{+}
, recall that
\textbf{x}_{e}\leq\textbf{m}_{e}
. Note that
\sum_{e\in M_{i}}\textbf{n}_{e}\leq|M_{i}|\frac{c}{n}\leq nc^{2-i}
for
i\geq 2
. We compute

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in M_{i}}\textbf{n}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\textbf{x}_{e}}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{e\in M_{i}}\textbf{n}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\textbf{m}_{e}}\geq\sum_{e\in M_{i}}\textbf{n}_{e}\log\frac{n}{c^{i}}=\sum_{e\in M_{i}}\textbf{n}_{e}\log n-i\log c\cdot\sum_{e\in M_{i}}\textbf{n}_{e}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{n_elogn}}}{{\geq}}\sum_{e\in M_{i}}\textbf{n}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\textbf{n}_{e}}-2i\log c\cdot\sum_{e\in M_{i}}\textbf{n}_{e}\geq\sum_{e\in M_{i}}\textbf{n}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\textbf{n}_{e}}-2n\log c\cdot ic^{2-i}.

This yields

(4.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in N_{+}}\textbf{n}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\textbf{x}_{e}}\geq\sum_{e\in N_{+}}\textbf{n}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\textbf{n}_{e}}-2n\log c\sum_{i\geq 2}\frac{i}{c^{i-2}}\geq\sum_{e\in N_{+}}\textbf{n}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\textbf{n}_{e}}-6n\log c,

and hence by employing (
4.4
), (
4.5
) and (
4.6
), we conclude

(4.7)

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in E(G)}\textbf{n}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\textbf{x}_{e}}\geq h(\textbf{n})-8n\log c,

which is a lower bound for the second term in (
4.1
).

Consequently, using (
4.1
), (
4.2
) and (
4.7
) gives rise to

\displaystyle h(\textbf{x})\geq(1-\lambda)h(\textbf{m})+\lambda h(\textbf{n})-9\lambda n\log c.

In particular, as
h(\textbf{n})\geq n\log n-n\log c
and
h(\textbf{m})\leq n\log n
, this yields

\displaystyle h(\textbf{x})\geq h(\textbf{m})-10\lambda n\log c\geq h(\textbf{m})-\varepsilon n,

where we used
\lambda\ll\frac{1}{c}\ll\varepsilon
in the last inequality.
In particular, we have
h(\textbf{x})\geq n\log\frac{n}{2}
.

Observe that
\textbf{x}_{e}\geq\frac{\lambda}{cn}
for all
e\in E(G)
.
We prove that it is possible to reduce the weight of the edges with large weight
without reducing the entropy of
x

and without changing the lower bound on
x
significantly.

These arguments are easier to comprehend if we look at the balanced bipartite graph on
2n
vertices corresponding to
G
, which we call
B
. We also write
x
for the perfect fractional matching of
B
that arises naturally from that of
G
.
Note that these edge weightings are essentially the same, and if we prove that we can change the edge weighting on
B
to obtain a
b
-normal perfect fractional matching, the same holds true for
G
.

Let
M:=\{e\in E(B)\mid\textbf{x}_{e}\geq\frac{b}{n}\}

(we may assume that
M
is nonempty).
Fix
vw\in M
.
We consider 4-cycles
vwuz
in
B
where
\textbf{x}_{wu},\textbf{x}_{vz}\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon n}
.
We claim that there are (at least)
\varepsilon^{2}n^{2}
such 4-cycles.
Let
Z:=\{z\in N(v)\mid\textbf{x}_{vz}\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon n}\}
and
U:=\{u\in N(w)\mid\textbf{x}_{wu}\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon n}\}
.
As there are at most
\varepsilon n
choices to select
x\in N(v)
such that
\textbf{x}_{vx}\geq\frac{1}{\varepsilon n}
, we obtain
|Z|\geq\delta(B)-\varepsilon n\geq\frac{n}{2}
, and by similar reasoning
|U|\geq\frac{n}{2}
.
As
\delta(B)\geq(\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon)n
, each vertex
z\in Z
has at least
\varepsilon n
neighbours in
U
. Let
Z^{\prime}\subseteq Z
such that
|Z^{\prime}|=\varepsilon n
, and for each
z\in Z^{\prime}
, let
U_{z}^{\prime}\subseteq U\cap N(z)
such that
|U^{\prime}_{z}|=\varepsilon n
.
Let
U^{\prime}:=\bigcup_{z\in Z^{\prime}}U_{z}^{\prime}
.
As for
vwuz
is a 4-cycle for any
z\in Z^{\prime}
and
u\in U_{z}^{\prime}
,
this yields
\varepsilon^{2}n^{2}
4-cycles
vwuz
where
\textbf{x}_{wu},\textbf{x}_{vz}\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon n}
(note that the actual number of such 4-cycles may be larger).

Let
vwuz
be such a 4-cycle.
Then
\textbf{x}_{vw}\textbf{x}_{zu}\geq\frac{b}{n}\frac{\lambda}{cn}\geq\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}n^{2}}\geq\textbf{x}_{vz}\textbf{x}_{wu}
, as
\frac{1}{b}\ll\lambda\ll\frac{1}{c}\ll\varepsilon
.
According to Lemma
4.2
,
we can reduce the weights of
\textbf{x}_{vw}
and
\textbf{x}_{zu}

and increase those of
\textbf{x}_{vz}
and
\textbf{x}_{wu}
by the same amount
as long as
\tilde{\textbf{x}}_{vw}\tilde{\textbf{x}}_{zu}\geq\tilde{\textbf{x}}_{vz}\tilde{\textbf{x}}_{wu}

without reducing the entropy, where
\tilde{\textbf{x}}
denotes the matching that arises from this process.
As we show below, for these edges this inequality is true with much room to spare.

Let
r_{vw}:=\frac{\textbf{x}_{vw}}{\varepsilon^{2}n^{2}}
.
By redistributing weight
r_{vw}
as described above on the chosen 4-cycles,
we can reduce
\textbf{x}_{vw}
to any arbitrary lower nonnegative weight.
We reduce
\textbf{x}_{vw}
until
\textbf{x}_{vw}\leq\frac{b}{n}
.
Recall that
|U^{\prime}_{z}|=\varepsilon n
and thus
\textbf{x}_{vz}
is increased
\varepsilon n
times for any
z\in Z^{\prime}
. Note further that
|Z^{\prime}|=\varepsilon n
and thus
\textbf{x}_{wu}
is increased at most
\varepsilon n
times for any
u\in U^{\prime}
.
Thus,
\tilde{\textbf{x}}_{vz},\tilde{\textbf{x}}_{wu}\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon n}+\varepsilon nr_{vw}\leq\frac{2}{\varepsilon n}
. To such an edge we do not add weight in the process described above. Thus, no new edges with weight at least
\frac{b}{n}
arise.

We turn to the next edge in
M
and continue the process described above until every edge
e\in E(B)
satisfies
\tilde{\textbf{x}}_{e}\leq\frac{b}{n}
.
According to Lemma
4.3
, we have
\sum_{e\in M}\textbf{x}_{e}\leq\frac{4n}{\log b}
.
Thus, the maximum amount of weight by which an edge opposite to an edge in
M
is reduced is
\sum_{e\in M}r_{e}\leq\frac{4}{\varepsilon^{2}n\log b}
.
Thus,
\tilde{\textbf{x}}_{zu}\geq\frac{\lambda}{cn}-\frac{4}{\varepsilon^{2}n\log b}\geq\frac{\lambda^{2}}{n}
, as
\frac{1}{b}\ll\lambda\ll\frac{1}{c}\ll\varepsilon
.

Altogether,
\tilde{\textbf{x}}_{vw}\tilde{\textbf{x}}_{zu}\geq\tilde{\textbf{x}}_{vz}\tilde{\textbf{x}}_{wu}
holds if
\tilde{\textbf{x}}_{vw}\frac{\lambda^{2}}{n}\geq(\frac{2}{\varepsilon n})^{2}
, that is, if
\tilde{\textbf{x}}_{vw}\geq\frac{4}{\lambda^{2}\varepsilon^{2}n}
.
In particular, as
\frac{1}{b}\ll\lambda\ll\frac{1}{c}\ll\varepsilon
, we can reduce weights such that
\tilde{\textbf{x}}_{vw}\leq\frac{b}{n}
for each
vw\in M
.
Thus,
\tilde{\textbf{x}}
is
b
-normal
.
∎

Suppose we remove a random-like set
M\subseteq V
from an
(n,\varepsilon)
-digraph with a
b
-normal perfect fractional matching. The following lemma essentially shows that
G-M
admits a
b^{\prime}
-normal perfect fractional matching that only loses the expected amount of entropy and where
b^{\prime}
is only slightly larger than
b
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Suppose
\frac{1}{n}\ll\gamma\ll\frac{1}{b}\ll\varepsilon
. Let
G
be an
(n,\varepsilon)
-digraph that is an induced subgraph of some digraph
F
.
Let
u\in V(F)\setminus V(G)
and
let
x
be a
b
-normal perfect fractional matching of
G
.
Let
\Delta:=e^{\gamma\sqrt{\ln n}}
, let
n^{\frac{1}{4}}\leq m\leq 3\Delta n^{\frac{1}{4}}
, and let
M\subseteq V(G)
be of size
m+1
.
Suppose that for all
v\in V(G)
and
*\in\{+,-\}
the following holds.

(i)

\sum_{w\in M\cap N_{G}^{+}(v)}\textbf{x}_{vw}=\frac{m+1}{n}\pm n^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{18\sqrt{\ln n}}}

and
\sum_{w\in M\cap N_{G}^{-}(v)}\textbf{x}_{wv}=\frac{m+1}{n}\pm n^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{18\sqrt{\ln n}}}

(ii)

\sum_{w\in M\cap N_{G}^{+}(v)}\textbf{x}_{vw}\log\frac{1}{\textbf{x}_{vw}}=\frac{m+1}{n}h_{\textbf{x}}^{+}(v)\pm n^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{18\sqrt{\ln n}}}

and

\sum_{w\in M\cap N_{G}^{-}(v)}\textbf{x}_{wv}\log\frac{1}{\textbf{x}_{wv}}=\frac{m+1}{n}h_{\textbf{x}}^{-}(v)\pm n^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{18\sqrt{\ln n}}}

(iii)

|N_{F,G}^{*}(u)|\geq(\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon)n
and

(iv)

|N_{F,G}^{*}(u)\cap M|=\frac{m+1}{n}|N_{F,G}^{*}(u)|\pm n^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{17\sqrt{\ln n}}}
.

Then there is a
(1+n^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{22\sqrt{\ln n}}})b
-normal perfect fractional matching
z
of
F[(V(G)\setminus M)\cup\{u\}]
that satisfies
h(\textbf{z})\geq\frac{n-m}{n}h(\textbf{x})-(n-m)\log\frac{n}{n-m}-n^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{24\sqrt{\ln n}}}
.

Proof.

Let
G^{\prime}:=F[(V(G)\setminus M)\cup\{u\}]
, let
n^{\prime}:=|V(G^{\prime})|=n-m
, and let
B^{\prime}:=B_{G^{\prime}}
be the balanced bipartite graph on
2n^{\prime}
vertices corresponding to
G^{\prime}
. We define an edge weighting
\textbf{x}^{\prime}
on
G^{\prime}
(and thus on
B^{\prime}
) by
\textbf{x}^{\prime}_{e}:=\textbf{x}_{e}
if
e\in E(G)
and
\textbf{x}^{\prime}_{uv}:=\frac{1}{|N_{F,G}^{+}(u)|}
for any
v\in N_{F,G}^{+}(u)
and
\textbf{x}^{\prime}_{vu}:=\frac{1}{|N_{F,G}^{-}(u)|}
for any
v\in N_{F,G}^{-}(u)
.

Let us refer to the weight of a vertex as the sum of the weight of incident edges. Our proof strategy is as follows: We rescale the weights of the edges of
B^{\prime}
such that they sum up to
n^{\prime}
, which is exactly the total weight of a perfect fractional matching of
B^{\prime}
. Then we redistribute weight from vertices with too much weight to vertices with too little weight, using that any two vertices in the same partition class of
B^{\prime}
share many neighbours.
In both steps, the entropy of the matching does not deviate too much from
\frac{n^{\prime}}{n}h(\textbf{x})
.

Define
\lambda:=\frac{n^{\prime}}{\sum_{e\in E(B^{\prime})}\textbf{x}^{\prime}_{e}}
and
\textbf{y}_{e}:=\lambda\textbf{x}^{\prime}_{e}
for
e\in E(B^{\prime})
. Then
\sum_{e\in E(B^{\prime})}\textbf{y}_{e}=n^{\prime}
.
Let
E(M,G):=\{vw\in E(G)\mid\text{$v\in M$ or $w\in M$}\}
.
Note that

(4.8)

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in E(B^{\prime})}\textbf{x}^{\prime}_{e}=\sum_{e\in E(G)}\textbf{x}_{e}-\sum_{e\in E(M,G)}\textbf{x}_{e}+\sum_{v\in V(G)\setminus M}\textbf{x}^{\prime}_{vu}+\textbf{x}^{\prime}_{uv}.

By using (i) we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in E(M,G)}\textbf{x}_{e}

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in M}\left(\sum_{w\in(V(G)\setminus M)\cap N_{G}^{+}(v)}\textbf{x}_{vw}+\sum_{w\in(V(G)\setminus M)\cap N_{G}^{-}(v)}\textbf{x}_{wv}+\sum_{w\in M\cap N_{G}^{+}(v)}\textbf{x}_{vw}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in M}\left(1+\sum_{w\in(V(G)\setminus M)\cap N_{G}^{-}(v)}\textbf{x}_{wv}\right)=(m+1)\left(2-\frac{m+1}{n}\pm n^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{18\sqrt{\ln n}}}\right)

\displaystyle=2(m+1)-\frac{(m+1)^{2}}{n}\pm mn^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{20\sqrt{\ln n}}}.

Using
(V(G)\setminus M)\cap N^{*}_{F,G}(u)=N_{F,G}^{*}(u)\setminus(N_{F,G}^{*}(u)\cap M)
for each
*\in\{+,-\}
and applying (iii) and (iv) yields

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V(G)\setminus M}\textbf{x}^{\prime}_{uv}+\textbf{x}^{\prime}_{vu}

\displaystyle=\frac{|(V(G)\setminus M)\cap N_{F,G}^{+}(u)|}{|N_{F,G}^{+}(u)|}+\frac{|(V(G)\setminus M)\cap N_{F,G}^{-}(u)|}{|N_{F,G}^{-}(u)|}

\displaystyle=2\frac{n-(m+1)}{n}\pm\frac{n^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{17\sqrt{\ln n}}}}{|N_{F,G}^{+}(u)|}\pm\frac{n^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{17\sqrt{\ln n}}}}{|N_{F,G}^{-}(u)|}

\displaystyle=2\frac{n-(m+1)}{n}\pm n^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{20\sqrt{\ln n}}},

where the last inequality follows from
|N_{F,G}^{+}(u)|,|N_{F,G}^{-}(u)|\geq\frac{n}{2}
.
Note that
\frac{m^{2}-1}{n}\geq(m+1)n^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{20\sqrt{\ln n}}}
, which is easily seen to be true by multiplying both sides of the inequality with
n
and using
m\geq n^{\frac{1}{4}}
. Thus, (
4.8
) implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in E(B^{\prime})}\textbf{x}^{\prime}_{e}

\displaystyle\geq n-2(m+1)+\frac{(m+1)^{2}}{n}+2\frac{n-(m+1)}{n}-(m+1)n^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{20\sqrt{\ln n}}}

\displaystyle=n-2m+\frac{m^{2}-1}{n}-(m+1)n^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{20\sqrt{\ln n}}}\geq n-2m

and

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in E(B^{\prime})}\textbf{x}^{\prime}_{e}

\displaystyle\leq n-2(m+1)+\frac{(m+1)^{2}}{n}+2\frac{n-(m+1)}{n}+(m+1)n^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{20\sqrt{\ln n}}}

\displaystyle=n-2m+\frac{m^{2}-1}{n}+(m+1)n^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{20\sqrt{\ln n}}}\leq n-2m+\frac{2m^{2}}{n}.

Thus we have

(4.9)

1+\frac{m-\frac{2m^{2}}{n}}{n-2m+\frac{2m^{2}}{n}}=\frac{n-m}{n-2m+\frac{2m^{2}}{n}}\leq\lambda\leq\frac{n-m}{n-2m}=1+\frac{m}{n-2m}.

In what follows, we redistribute weights of
y
such that the edge weighting
z
which arises from this process is a perfect fractional matching.

We proceed by induction on the number of vertices
v\in V(B^{\prime})
where
\sum_{v^{\prime}\in N_{B^{\prime}}(v)}\textbf{y}_{vv^{\prime}}\neq 1
.
If there is no such vertex, then
y
is a perfect fractional matching of
B^{\prime}
.
Otherwise, there is a vertex
v\in V(B^{\prime})
with
\sum_{v^{\prime}\in N_{B^{\prime}}(v)}\textbf{y}_{vv^{\prime}}=1+x_{1}
and
x_{1}>0
and a vertex
w\in V(B^{\prime})
in the same partition class as
v
such that
\sum_{w^{\prime}\in N_{B^{\prime}}(w)}\textbf{y}_{ww^{\prime}}=1-x_{2}
where
x_{2}>0
.

Observe that
B^{\prime}
satisfies
\delta(B^{\prime})\geq(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2})n^{\prime}\geq\frac{n^{\prime}}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon n}{4}
. In particular, we have
|N_{B^{\prime}}(v)\cap N_{B^{\prime}}(w)|\geq\frac{\varepsilon n}{2}
.
For each
z\in N_{B^{\prime}}(v)\cap N_{B^{\prime}}(w)
, we can redistribute weight from
\sum_{v^{\prime}\in N_{B^{\prime}}(v)}\textbf{y}_{vv^{\prime}}
to
\sum_{w^{\prime}\in N_{B^{\prime}}(w)}\textbf{y}_{ww^{\prime}}
by adding it to
\textbf{y}_{zw}
and subtracting it from
\textbf{y}_{vz}
. In this case we say we shift weight from
vz
to
zw
.
We distribute weight
\min\{x_{1},x_{2}\}
on paths of length 2 between
v
and
w

Let
Z\subseteq N_{B^{\prime}}(v)\cap N_{B^{\prime}}(w)
such that
|Z|=\frac{\varepsilon n}{2}
.

For each
z\in Z
, we add weight
\frac{2\min\{x_{1},x_{2}\}}{\varepsilon n}
to
\textbf{y}_{zw}
and subtract it from
\textbf{y}_{vz}
(we will later see that this change is very small and it is indeed possible to distribute weight like this).
In this case we say that
zw
gets weight from
v
and
w
and
vz
loses weight from
v
and
w
.
Call the edge weighting which arises from this process
\tilde{\textbf{y}}
.
Now we either have
\sum_{v^{\prime}\in N_{B^{\prime}}(v)}\tilde{\textbf{y}}_{vv^{\prime}}=1
or
\sum_{w^{\prime}\in N_{B^{\prime}}(w)}\tilde{\textbf{y}}_{ww^{\prime}}=1
and thus
|\{v\in V(B^{\prime})\mid\sum_{v^{\prime}\in N_{B^{\prime}}(v)}\tilde{\textbf{y}}_{vv^{\prime}}\neq 1|\leq|\{v\in V(B^{\prime})\mid\sum_{v^{\prime}\in N_{B^{\prime}}(v)}{\textbf{y}}_{vv^{\prime}}\neq 1|-1
.
Using our induction hypothesis, we can turn
\tilde{\textbf{y}}
into a perfect fractional matching
z
such that
\sum_{v^{\prime}\in N_{B^{\prime}}(v)}\textbf{z}_{vv^{\prime}}=1
for each
v\in V(B^{\prime})
.

Assume
vz\in E(B^{\prime})
is used in the process above to distribute edge weights. Note that
\textbf{y}_{vz}
is only changed as long as
\sum_{v^{\prime}\in N_{B^{\prime}}(v)}\textbf{y}_{vv^{\prime}}\neq 1
or
\sum_{z^{\prime}\in N_{B^{\prime}}(z)}\textbf{y}_{zz^{\prime}}\neq 1
.
Suppose
\sum_{v^{\prime}\in N_{B^{\prime}}(v)}\textbf{y}_{vv^{\prime}}=1\pm x_{1}
for some
x_{1}>0
,
\sum_{w^{\prime}\in N_{B^{\prime}}(w)}\textbf{y}_{ww^{\prime}}=1\pm x_{2}
for some
x_{2}>0
, and we distribute weight from
v
to
w
. If
x_{1}<x_{2}
, we obtain
\tilde{\textbf{y}}_{vz}=\textbf{y}_{vz}\pm\frac{2x_{1}}{\varepsilon n}
for any
z\in Z
. If
x_{1}\geq x_{2}
, we obtain
\tilde{\textbf{y}}_{vz}=\textbf{y}_{vz}\pm\frac{2x_{2}}{\varepsilon n}
for any
z\in Z
, and we have
\sum_{v^{\prime}\in N_{B^{\prime}}(v)}\tilde{\textbf{y}}_{vv^{\prime}}=1\pm(x_{1}-x_{2})
.
Thus the maximum amount of weight that
vz
can obtain from
v
is
\frac{2x_{1}}{\varepsilon n}
.
Hence, denoting
y_{1}:=|\sum_{z^{\prime}\in N_{B^{\prime}}(z)}\textbf{y}_{zz^{\prime}}-1|
,
we have
\textbf{z}_{vz}=\textbf{y}_{vz}\pm 2\cdot\frac{2\max\{x_{1},y_{1}\}}{\varepsilon n}
.

Let
x:=\max_{v\in V(B^{\prime})}|\sum_{v^{\prime}\in N_{B^{\prime}}(v)}\textbf{y}_{vv^{\prime}}-1|
. We bound
x
from above. Let
v\in V(G)\setminus M
and
v^{+},v^{-}
be its corresponding vertices in
B^{\prime}
.
Note that

\displaystyle 1=\sum_{w\in N_{G}^{+}(v)\cap M}\textbf{x}_{vw}+\sum_{w\in N_{G}^{+}(v)\setminus M}\textbf{x}_{vw}=\sum_{w\in N_{G}^{+}(v)\cap M}\textbf{x}_{vw}+\sum_{v^{\prime}\in N_{B^{\prime}}(v^{+})}\textbf{x}^{\prime}_{v^{+}v^{\prime}}-\textbf{x}^{\prime}_{vu}.

By (i) we have

\displaystyle\sum_{v^{\prime}\in N_{B^{\prime}}(v^{+})}\textbf{x}^{\prime}_{v^{+}v^{\prime}}=1-\sum_{w\in N_{G}^{+}(v)\cap M}\textbf{x}_{vw}+\textbf{x}^{\prime}_{vu}\leq 1-\frac{m+1}{n}+n^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{2}{37\sqrt{\ln n}}}.

Using (
4.9
), this yields

\displaystyle\sum_{v^{\prime}\in N_{B^{\prime}}(v^{+})}\textbf{y}_{v^{+}v^{\prime}}=\lambda\sum_{v^{\prime}\in N_{B^{\prime}}(v^{+})}\textbf{x}^{\prime}_{v^{+}v^{\prime}}\leq 1+\frac{2m^{2}}{n(n-2m)}+2n^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{2}{37\sqrt{\ln n}}}\leq 1+n^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{19\sqrt{\ln n}}},

and the analogous inequality holds for
v^{-}
.
Similarly we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{v^{\prime}\in N_{B^{\prime}}(v^{+})}\textbf{y}_{v^{+}v^{\prime}}\geq 1-n^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{19\sqrt{\ln n}}}

and the analogous inequality for
v^{-}
.
Further, by (iv) we have

\displaystyle\sum_{v^{\prime}\in N_{B^{\prime}}(u^{+})}\textbf{x}^{\prime}_{u^{+}v^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{|N_{F,G}^{+}(u)|}\left(\frac{n-(m+1)}{n}|N_{F,G}^{+}(u)|\pm n^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{17\sqrt{\ln n}}}\right)=1-\frac{m+1}{n}\pm n^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{18\sqrt{\ln n}}}.

As in the calculations above, we obtain
\sum_{v^{\prime}\in N_{B^{\prime}}(u^{+})}\textbf{y}_{u^{+}v^{\prime}}=1\pm n^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{19\sqrt{\ln n}}}
and the same inequality holds for
u^{-}
.
Thus, for each
v\in V(B^{\prime})
, we need to redistribute at most weight
n^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{19\sqrt{\ln n}}}
to achieve
\sum_{v^{\prime}\in N_{B^{\prime}}(v)}\textbf{y}_{vv^{\prime}}=1
, and every edge weight is changed by at most
4\varepsilon^{-1}n^{-\frac{7}{4}-\frac{1}{19\sqrt{\ln n}}}\leq n^{-\frac{7}{4}-\frac{1}{20\sqrt{\ln n}}}
.
Hence,

(4.10)

\textbf{z}_{e}=\textbf{y}_{e}\pm n^{-\frac{7}{4}-\frac{1}{20\sqrt{\ln n}}}

for each
e\in E(B^{\prime})
.

We now bound the change of entropy in this process from above. Assume that
e,e^{\prime}\in E(B^{\prime})
are used to redistribute weight, and assume that weight
\alpha\leq n^{-\frac{7}{4}-\frac{1}{20\sqrt{\ln n}}}
is subtracted from
\textbf{y}_{e}
and added to
\textbf{y}_{e^{\prime}}
.
Define
\mu_{1}:=1-\frac{\alpha}{\textbf{y}_{e}}
and
\mu_{2}:=1+\frac{\alpha}{\textbf{y}_{e^{\prime}}}
.
Then
\textbf{y}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\mu_{1}}\geq 0
, and, using
\log(1+x)\leq 2x
for
x\geq 0
, we obtain
\textbf{y}_{e^{\prime}}\log\frac{1}{\mu_{2}}=-\textbf{y}_{e^{\prime}}\log\mu_{2}\geq-2\alpha
. As
\textbf{y}_{e}-\alpha\geq\frac{\textbf{y}_{e}}{2}
and
\textbf{y}_{e^{\prime}}+\alpha\leq 2\textbf{y}_{e^{\prime}}
, we also have

\displaystyle-\left(\log\frac{1}{\textbf{y}_{e}-\alpha}-\log\frac{1}{\textbf{y}_{e^{\prime}}+\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\log\textbf{y}_{e}-\log\textbf{y}_{e^{\prime}}-2\geq\log\frac{\lambda}{bn}-\log\frac{\lambda b}{n}-2\geq-3\log b.

This yields
\textbf{y}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\mu_{1}}+\textbf{y}_{e^{\prime}}\log\frac{1}{\mu_{2}}-\alpha(\log\frac{1}{\textbf{y}_{e}-\alpha}-\log\frac{1}{\textbf{y}_{e^{\prime}}+\alpha})\geq-5\alpha\log b\geq-n^{-\frac{7}{4}-\frac{1}{21\sqrt{\ln n}}}
.
The entropy of
e,e^{\prime}
hence satisfies

\displaystyle h_{\textbf{z}}(\{e,e^{\prime}\})

\displaystyle=(\textbf{y}_{e}-\alpha)\log\frac{1}{\textbf{y}_{e}-\alpha}+(\textbf{y}_{e^{\prime}}+\alpha)\log\frac{1}{\textbf{y}_{e^{\prime}}+\alpha}

\displaystyle=\textbf{y}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\mu_{1}\textbf{y}_{e}}+\textbf{y}_{e^{\prime}}\log\frac{1}{\mu_{2}\textbf{y}_{e^{\prime}}}-\alpha\left(\log\frac{1}{\textbf{y}_{e}-\alpha}-\log\frac{1}{\textbf{y}_{e^{\prime}}+\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\textbf{y}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\textbf{y}_{e}}+\textbf{y}_{e^{\prime}}\log\frac{1}{\textbf{y}_{e^{\prime}}}-n^{-\frac{7}{4}-\frac{1}{21\sqrt{\ln n}}}.

We shift weight from one edge to another at most
2n\cdot\frac{\varepsilon n}{2}=\varepsilon n^{2}
times.
Using
h(\textbf{y})=\sum_{e\in E(B^{\prime})}\lambda\textbf{x}^{\prime}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\lambda\textbf{x}^{\prime}_{e}}=\lambda\sum_{e\in E(B^{\prime})}\textbf{x}^{\prime}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\textbf{x}^{\prime}_{e}}-\sum_{e\in E(B^{\prime})}\lambda\textbf{x}^{\prime}_{e}\log\lambda=\lambda\sum_{e\in E(B^{\prime})}\textbf{x}^{\prime}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\textbf{x}^{\prime}_{e}}-n^{\prime}\log\lambda
, this yields

(4.11)

\displaystyle h(\textbf{z})

\displaystyle\geq h(\textbf{y})-n^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{21\sqrt{\ln n}}}=\lambda\sum_{e\in E(B^{\prime})}\textbf{x}^{\prime}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\textbf{x}^{\prime}_{e}}-n^{\prime}\log\lambda-n^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{21\sqrt{\ln n}}}.

Note that by (
4.9
),
\lambda\leq\frac{n-m}{n-2m}=\frac{n}{n^{\prime}}(1+\frac{m^{2}}{n^{2}-2mn})\leq\frac{n}{n^{\prime}}(1+\frac{2m^{2}}{n^{\prime 2}})
and thus
\log\lambda\leq\log\frac{n}{n^{\prime}}+\log(1+\frac{2m^{2}}{n^{\prime 2}})\leq\log\frac{n}{n^{\prime}}+\frac{4m^{2}}{n^{\prime 2}}
. Combining this with (
4.11
) yields

(4.12)

\displaystyle h(\textbf{z})

\displaystyle\geq\lambda\sum_{e\in E(B^{\prime})}\textbf{x}^{\prime}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\textbf{x}^{\prime}_{e}}-n^{\prime}\log\frac{n}{n^{\prime}}-n^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{22\sqrt{\ln n}}}.

We bound
\sum_{e\in E(B^{\prime})}\textbf{x}^{\prime}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\textbf{x}^{\prime}_{e}}
in terms of
h(\textbf{x})
.
Recall that
E(M,G):=\{vw\in E(G)\mid\text{$v\in M$ or $w\in M$}\}
. Using (ii) we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V(G)}\sum_{w\in M\cap N_{G}^{+}(v)}\textbf{x}_{vw}\log\frac{1}{\textbf{x}_{vw}}=\frac{m+1}{n}\sum_{v\in V(G)}h_{\textbf{x}}^{+}(v)\pm n^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{18\sqrt{\ln n}}}=\frac{m}{n}h(\textbf{x})\pm n^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{19\sqrt{\ln n}}}

and the analogous inequality for
N_{G}^{-}(v)
and
h_{\textbf{x}}^{-}(v)
. Thus,

\displaystyle h_{\textbf{x}}(E(M,G))

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{v\in V(G)}\left(\sum_{w\in M\cap N_{G}^{+}(v)}\textbf{x}_{vw}\log\frac{1}{\textbf{x}_{vw}}+\sum_{w\in M\cap N_{G}^{-}(v)}\textbf{x}_{wv}\log\frac{1}{\textbf{x}_{wv}}\right)

\displaystyle=2\frac{m}{n}h(\textbf{x})\pm n^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{20\sqrt{\ln n}}}.

This yields

(4.13)

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in E(B^{\prime})}\textbf{x}^{\prime}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\textbf{x}^{\prime}_{e}}

\displaystyle\geq h(\textbf{x})-h_{\textbf{x}}(E(M,G))\geq\frac{n^{\prime}}{n}h(\textbf{x})-\frac{m}{n}h(\textbf{x})-n^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{20\sqrt{\ln n}}}.

By combining (
4.12
) and (
4.13
) and using
\lambda\geq 1+(m-\frac{2m^{2}}{n})/(n-2m+\frac{2m^{2}}{n})
(see (
4.9
)) and
h(\textbf{x})\leq n\log n
, we obtain

\displaystyle h(\textbf{z})

\displaystyle\geq\left(1+\frac{m-\frac{2m^{2}}{n}}{n-2m+\frac{2m^{2}}{n}}\right)\left(\frac{n^{\prime}}{n}h(\textbf{x})-\frac{m}{n}h(\textbf{x})\right)-n^{\prime}\log\frac{n}{n^{\prime}}-n^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{23\sqrt{\ln n}}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n^{\prime}}{n}h(\textbf{x})-\frac{4m^{2}}{n^{2}}h(\textbf{x})-n^{\prime}\log\frac{n}{n^{\prime}}-n^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{23\sqrt{\ln n}}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n^{\prime}}{n}h(\textbf{x})-n^{\prime}\log\frac{n}{n^{\prime}}-n^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{24\sqrt{\ln n}}},

as desired.

Next we show that
z
is
b^{\prime}
-normal for some suitable
b^{\prime}
. We need to choose
b^{\prime}
such that
\textbf{z}_{e}\leq\frac{b^{\prime}}{n^{\prime}}
for each
e\in E(B^{\prime})
.
Let
\alpha:=n^{-\frac{7}{4}-\frac{1}{20\sqrt{\ln n}}}
.
Note that trivially
\frac{1}{bn}\leq\textbf{x}_{uv},\textbf{x}_{wu}\leq\frac{b}{n}
for any
v,w\in V(G)
such that
uv,vw\in E(G)
.
Thus,
\textbf{z}_{e}\leq\frac{\lambda b}{n}+\alpha
by (
4.10
), and we need to choose
b^{\prime}
to satisfy
b^{\prime}\geq\frac{\lambda bn^{\prime}}{n}+\alpha n^{\prime}
.
Let
\zeta:=n^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{22\sqrt{\ln n}}}
and
b^{\prime}:=(1+\zeta)b
.
Note that
\lambda+\alpha n\leq 1+\frac{m}{n-2m}+n^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{20\sqrt{\ln n}}}\leq 1+\frac{m+\zeta n}{n-m}
.
Then

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda bn^{\prime}+\alpha nn^{\prime}}{n}\leq\frac{(\lambda+\alpha n)n^{\prime}}{n}b\leq\frac{(1+\frac{m+\zeta n}{n^{\prime}})n^{\prime}}{n}b=(1+\zeta)b=b^{\prime}.

Note that
b^{\prime}
must also satisfy
\textbf{z}_{e}\geq\frac{1}{b^{\prime}n^{\prime}}
for each
e\in E(B^{\prime})
. As
\textbf{z}_{e}\geq\frac{\lambda}{bn}-\alpha
by (
4.10
), we need to choose
b^{\prime}
such that
b^{\prime}\geq\frac{1}{(\frac{\lambda}{bn}-\alpha)n^{\prime}}
. Note that
(\lambda-\alpha bn)(n-m)\geq n-n^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{21\sqrt{\ln n}}}
.
Hence

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\frac{\lambda}{bn}-\alpha)n^{\prime}}=\frac{n}{(\lambda-\alpha bn)n^{\prime}}b\leq\frac{1}{1-n^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{21\sqrt{\ln n}}}}b\leq(1+\zeta)b=b^{\prime}.

Altogether we obtain that
z
is
b^{\prime}
-normal with
b^{\prime}\leq(1+n^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{22\sqrt{\ln n}}})b
.
∎

The next lemma shows that if
A\subseteq V(G)
is not too large, the graph
G-A
has a
b
-normal perfect fractional matching whose entropy is only slightly less than
h(G)
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Suppose
\frac{1}{n}\ll\frac{1}{b}\ll\varepsilon
. Let
G
be an
(n,\varepsilon)
-digraph and suppose
A\subseteq V(G)
satisfies
|A|\leq\frac{n}{\log^{2}n}
.
Then there is a
b
-normal perfect fractional matching
z
of
G-A
that satisfies
h(\textbf{z})\geq h(G)-\varepsilon n
.

Proof.

Note that
h(G)\geq n\log\delta^{0}(G)\geq n\log\frac{n}{2}+\varepsilon^{2}n
(see
[
3
, Theorem 3.1]
).
According to Theorem
4.4
, the digraph
G
has a
\frac{b}{2}
-normal perfect fractional matching
x
that satisfies
h(\textbf{x})\geq h(G)-\varepsilon^{2}n
.
We show that we can modify
x
to obtain a
b
-normal perfect fractional matching
z
that satisfies
h(\textbf{z})\geq h(\textbf{x})-3|A|\log n
, which implies the desired result. To this end, we follow a similar (but simpler) strategy to Lemma
4.5
.

Define
a:=|A|
and
n^{\prime}:=|V(G)\setminus A|
. Let
B^{\prime}:=B_{G-A}
be the balanced bipartite graph on
2n^{\prime}
vertices corresponding to
G-A
.
Let
\lambda:=\frac{n^{\prime}}{\sum_{e\in E(B^{\prime})}\textbf{x}_{e}}
and
\textbf{y}_{e}:=\lambda\textbf{x}_{e}
for
e\in E(B^{\prime})
. Then
\sum_{e\in E(B^{\prime})}\textbf{y}_{e}=n^{\prime}
.
Let
E(A,G):=\{vw\in E(G)\mid\text{$v\in A$ or $w\in A$}\}
.
Note that
a\leq\sum_{e\in E(A,G)}\textbf{x}_{e}\leq 2a
and hence
n-2a\leq\sum_{e\in E(B^{\prime})}\textbf{x}_{e}\leq n-a
.
Thus,
1\leq\lambda\leq\frac{n-a}{n-2a}=1+\frac{a}{n-2a}
.

Analogously to Lemma
4.5
, we redistribute weights on edges of
B^{\prime}
to obtain a perfect fractional matching
z
by shifting weights on paths of length 2 between vertices
v\in V(B^{\prime})
that satisfy
\sum_{v^{\prime}\in N_{B^{\prime}}(v)}\textbf{y}_{vv^{\prime}}>1
and vertices
w\in V(B^{\prime})
that satisfy
\sum_{w^{\prime}\in N_{B^{\prime}}(w)}\textbf{y}_{ww^{\prime}}<1
.
As in Lemma
4.5
, we can always choose
\frac{\varepsilon n}{2}
paths of length 2 between two such vertices to redistribute weights, and thus,
z
satisfies
\textbf{z}_{e}=\textbf{y}_{e}\pm\frac{4x}{\varepsilon n}
for each
e\in E(B^{\prime})
, where
x:=\max_{v\in V(B^{\prime})}{|\sum_{v^{\prime}\in N_{B^{\prime}}(v)}\textbf{y}_{vv^{\prime}}-1|}
.

We bound
x
from above. Let
v\in V(G)\setminus A
and
v^{+},v^{-}
be its corresponding vertices in
V(B^{\prime})
.

As
x
is
\frac{b}{2}
-normal, we have

\displaystyle 1=\sum_{w\in(V(G)\setminus A)\cap N_{G}^{+}(v)}\textbf{x}_{vw}+\sum_{w\in A\cap N_{G}^{+}(v)}\textbf{x}_{vw}\leq\sum_{v^{\prime}\in N_{B^{\prime}}(v^{+})}\textbf{x}_{v^{+}v^{\prime}}+\frac{ab}{2n},

and the analogous inequality holds for
v^{-}
.

Thus,
1-\frac{ab}{2n}\leq\sum_{v^{\prime}\in N_{B^{\prime}}(v)}\textbf{x}_{vv^{\prime}}\leq 1
. Using
1\leq\lambda\leq 1+\frac{a}{n-2a}
yields
1-\frac{ab}{2n}\leq\sum_{v^{\prime}\in N_{B^{\prime}}(v)}\textbf{y}_{vv^{\prime}}\leq 1+\frac{a}{n-2a}\leq 1+\frac{ab}{2n}
.
Thus, for each
v\in V(B^{\prime})
, we need to redistribute at most weight
\frac{ab}{2n}
to achieve
\sum_{v^{\prime}\in N_{B^{\prime}}(v)}\textbf{y}_{vv^{\prime}}=1
, and in this process every edge weight is changed by at most
\frac{2ab}{\varepsilon n^{2}}
. Hence,

\textbf{z}_{e}=\textbf{y}_{e}\pm\frac{2ab}{\varepsilon n^{2}}

for each
e\in E(B^{\prime})
. As
\frac{1}{n}\ll\frac{1}{b}\ll\varepsilon
, we conclude that
z
is a
b
-normal perfect fractional matching.

We now bound the change of entropy in this process from above. Assume that
e,e^{\prime}\in E(B^{\prime})
are used to redistribute weight, and assume that weight
\alpha\leq\frac{2ab}{\varepsilon n^{2}}
is subtracted from
\textbf{y}_{e}
and added to
\textbf{y}_{e^{\prime}}
.
Analogously to Lemma
4.5
we obtain

\displaystyle h_{\textbf{z}}(\{e,e^{\prime}\})

\displaystyle\geq\textbf{y}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\textbf{y}_{e}}+\textbf{y}_{e^{\prime}}\log\frac{1}{\textbf{y}_{e^{\prime}}}-5\alpha\log b\geq\textbf{y}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\textbf{y}_{e}}+\textbf{y}_{e^{\prime}}\log\frac{1}{\textbf{y}_{e^{\prime}}}-\frac{ab^{2}}{\varepsilon n^{2}}.

We shift at most
2n\cdot\frac{\varepsilon n}{4}\leq\varepsilon n^{2}
times weight from one edge to another.
This yields

h(\textbf{z})\geq h(\textbf{y})-ab^{2}=\sum_{e\in E(B^{\prime})}\lambda\textbf{x}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\lambda\textbf{x}_{e}}-ab^{2}\geq\sum_{e\in E(B^{\prime})}\textbf{x}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\textbf{x}_{e}}-ab^{2},

as
\lambda\geq 1
and
x^{\prime}\log\frac{1}{x^{\prime}}\geq x\log\frac{1}{x}
if
x^{\prime}\geq x
as long as
x,x^{\prime}\leq e^{-1}
.
Note that
h_{\textbf{x}}^{+}(v)=\sum_{w\in N_{G}^{+}(v)}\textbf{x}_{vw}\log\frac{1}{\textbf{x}_{vw}}\leq\log n
for any
v\in V(G)
by Jensen’s inequality, and the same holds for
h_{\textbf{x}}^{-}(v)
.
Thus,

\displaystyle h_{\textbf{x}}(E(A,G))\leq\sum_{v\in A}h_{\textbf{x}}^{+}(v)+h_{\textbf{x}}^{-}(v)\leq 2a\log n.

This yields
h(\textbf{z})\geq h(\textbf{x})-h_{\textbf{x}}(E(A,G))-ab^{2}\geq h(\textbf{x})-3a\log n
.
Altogether,
h(\textbf{z})\geq h(G)-3a\log n-\varepsilon^{2}n\geq h(G)-\varepsilon n
.
∎

5.
Conditions for a random tree being well-behaved

In this section, we define what it means for a (random) tree in a digraph
G
on
n
vertices to be
self-avoiding
and
(\mathcal{S},a,c)
-expected
.
We prove that a tree of
T
with
n^{\frac{1}{4}}+1\leq|T|\leq 3\Delta n^{\frac{1}{4}}
satisfies both properties with high probability.
This yields the foundation for applying Lemma
3.1
to show
that
G
contains not only a large number of copies of a tree of this order,
but also a large number of
well-behaved
copies (such a copy is well-behaved if it is
(\mathcal{S},n^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{17\sqrt{\ln n}}},n^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{18\sqrt{\ln n}}})
-expected for a collection of sets
\mathcal{S}
that is specified in Section
6
).

Definition 5.1
.

Let
G
be a digraph and
x
a perfect fractional matching of
G
.
Let
T=(r_{0},\ldots,r_{m})
be an oriented tree and
R=(R_{0},\ldots,R_{m})
a random tree according to
T
in
G
.
We call
R

self-avoiding
if
R_{i}\neq R_{j}
for distinct
i,j\in[m]_{0}
.
Let
\mathcal{S}
be a collection of subsets in
V(G)
and let
M\subseteq V(G)
.
We say that
M
is
(\mathcal{S},a)
-expected
if
||M\cap S|-\frac{|M|}{n}|S||<a
for each
S\in\mathcal{S}
.
We say that
M
is
c
-expected
if
|\sum_{w\in N^{+}(v)\cap M}\textbf{x}_{vw}-\frac{|M|}{n}|<c
and
|\sum_{w\in N^{+}(v)\cap M}\textbf{x}_{vw}\log\frac{1}{\textbf{x}_{vw}}-\frac{|M|}{n}h_{\textbf{x}}^{+}(v)|<c
for each vertex
v\in V(G)
, and if the analogous results hold for
N^{-}(v)
and
h_{\textbf{x}}^{-}(v)
.
Finally, we say that
M
is
(\mathcal{S},a,c)
-expected if it is both
(\mathcal{S},a)
-expected and
c
-expected.
We transfer these notions to a subgraph
Q
in
G
by setting
M:=V(Q)
.

The next lemma shows that a random tree is self-avoiding with high probability. It is essentially already proved in
[
3
]
and follows directly from the upper bound on
x
and an application of the union bound.

Lemma 5.2

(
[
3
, Lemma 5.1]
)
.

Let
G
be a digraph on
n
vertices and
x
a perfect fractional matching of
G
.
Let
T
be an oriented tree on
m+1
vertices and
R
a random tree in
G
according to
T
.
Suppose every edge
e\in E(G)
satisfies
\textbf{x}_{e}\leq\frac{b}{n}
for some
b\geq 1
.
Then

\mathbb{P}[R\text{ is self-avoiding}]\geq 1-m^{2}\frac{b}{n}.

Proof.

We have
\mathbb{P}[R_{i}\in\{R_{0},\ldots,R_{i-1}\}]\leq i\frac{b}{n}
.
Thus,

\mathbb{P}[(R_{0},\ldots,R_{m})\text{ not self-avoiding}]=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathbb{P}[R_{i}\in\{R_{0},\ldots,R_{i-1}\}]\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}i\frac{b}{n}\leq m^{2}\frac{b}{n},

which proves the claim.
∎

Thew following lemma yields a lower bound on the speed of convergence of a Markov chain.

Lemma 5.3

(
[
6
, Lemma 3.2]
)
.

Let
(Z_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be a Markov chain with state space
\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}\}
and
transition matrices
P^{(t)}=(p^{(t)}_{ij})_{i,j\in[n]}
.
Suppose there is a
\sigma=(\sigma_{i})_{i\in[n]}
with
\sigma_{i}>0
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\sum_{i\in[n]}\sigma_{i}=1
and
\sigma P^{(t)}=\sigma
for all
t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
Let

\alpha:=\inf_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0},i,j,k\in[n]}\frac{p^{(t)}_{ij}}{\sigma_{k}},\hskip 14.22636pt\beta:=\sup_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0},i,j,k\in[n]}\frac{p^{(t)}_{ij}}{\sigma_{k}}.

Assume
\alpha>0
. Then

\mathbb{P}[Z_{t}=s_{i}]=(1\pm(1-\textstyle{\frac{\alpha}{2})^{t}})\sigma_{i}

holds for
t\geq 2+2\alpha^{-1}\log\beta
.

Now we utilise Lemma
5.3
for our setting.

Corollary 5.4
.

Suppose
\frac{1}{n}\ll\varepsilon
. Let
G
be an
(n,\varepsilon)
-digraph
, let
x
be a perfect fractional matching of
G
and let
(Z_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be a random walk on
G
induced by
x
following the pattern of an oriented path
L=(y_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
.
Suppose
x
is
b
-normal
for some
b\geq 1
.
Then, for any vertex
v\in V(G)
, we have

\mathbb{P}[Z_{t}=v]=(1\pm e^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2b^{2}}t})\frac{1}{n}

for
t\geq 5+4b^{2}\varepsilon^{-1}\log b
.

Proof.

Denote
p_{vw}:=\textbf{x}_{vw}
if
vw\in E(G)
and
p_{vw}:=0
otherwise. Let
P:=(p_{vw})_{v,w\in V(G)}
.
Note that
P
is the transition matrix of
Z
at times
t
where
y_{t}y_{t+1}\in E(L)
and
P^{T}
is the transition matrix of
Z
at times
t
where
y_{t+1}y_{t}\in E(L)
.
As
\delta^{0}(G)\geq(\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon)n
,
we have
|A\cap B|\geq 2\varepsilon n
for all
v,w\in G
, where
A\in\{N^{+}(v),N^{-}(v)\}
and
B\in\{N^{+}(w),N^{-}(w)\}
.
Suppose
y_{i}y_{i+1},y_{i+1}y_{i+2}\in E(L)
. As
p_{vw}\geq\frac{1}{bn}
for
vw\in E(G)
,
we obtain

q^{(i)}_{vw}:=\mathbb{P}[Z_{i+2}=w\mid Z_{i}=v]\geq|N^{+}(v)\cap N^{-}(w)|\min_{u\in N^{+}(v)\cap N^{-}(w)}p_{vu}p_{uw}\geq\frac{2\varepsilon}{b^{2}n}

for
v,w\in V(G)
.
The analogous result holds if the path
L
is oriented differently.
Note that, as
P
and
P^{T}
are doubly stochastic, so are
P^{2},(P^{T})^{2},PP^{T},P^{T}P
.
Thus,
\sigma:=(\sigma_{v})_{v\in V(G)}:=\frac{1}{n}(1,\ldots,1)
satisfies
\sigma_{v}>0
for all
v\in V(G)
,
\sum_{v\in V(G)}\sigma_{v}=1
and
\sigma Q=\sigma
for each
Q\in\{P^{2},(P^{T})^{2},PP^{T},P^{T}P\}
.
Let
Z^{\prime}_{i}:=Z_{2i}
. Then
(Z^{\prime}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
defines a Markov chain with state space
V(G)
and potential transition matrices
P^{2},(P^{T})^{2},PP^{T},P^{T}P
.
Let
\alpha:=\min_{i,v,w,u}\frac{q^{(i)}_{vw}}{\sigma_{u}}\geq\frac{2\varepsilon}{b^{2}}
and
\beta:=\max_{i,v,w,u}\frac{q^{(i)}_{vw}}{\sigma_{u}}=n\cdot\max_{i,v,w}q^{(i)}_{vw}\leq n\frac{b}{n}n\frac{b}{n}=b^{2}
.
Using the inequality
(1-x)^{t}\leq e^{-xt}
,
Lemma
5.3
yields

\mathbb{P}[Z_{t}=v]=\mathbb{P}[Z^{\prime}_{\frac{t}{2}}=v]=(1\pm(1-\textstyle{\frac{\alpha}{2}})^{\frac{t}{2}})\sigma_{v}=(1\pm(1-\textstyle{\frac{\varepsilon}{b^{2}}})^{\frac{t}{2}})\frac{1}{n}=(1\pm e^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2b^{2}}t})\frac{1}{n}

for any vertex
v\in V(G)
, for any even
t
that satisfies
t\geq 4+4b^{2}\varepsilon^{-1}\log b
.
Similarly we conclude
\mathbb{P}[Z_{i+t}=v\mid Z_{i}=w]=(1\pm e^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2b^{2}}t})\frac{1}{n}
for any
v,w\in V(G)
and even
t\geq 4+4b^{2}\varepsilon^{-1}\log b
.
Thus, for any odd
t
that satisfies
t\geq 5+4b^{2}\varepsilon^{-1}\log b
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[Z_{t}=v]=\sum_{w\in V(G)}\mathbb{P}[Z_{1}=w]\mathbb{P}[Z_{t}=v\mid Z_{1}=w]=(1\pm e^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2b^{2}}t})\frac{1}{n}.

This yields the desired result.
∎

The following lemma uses Corollary
5.4
to show that a random tree is very likely to behave as one would expect with regard to a subset
S\subseteq V(G)
. To be more precise, given a vertex weighting of
G
, with high probability the sum of weights of the vertices in
S
that are hit by the random tree does not deviate too much from its expected value.

Lemma 5.5
.

Suppose
\frac{1}{n}\ll\gamma\ll\frac{1}{b}\ll\varepsilon
. Let
G
be an
(n,\varepsilon)
-digraph
, let
S\subseteq V(G)
, let
x
be a
b
-normal
perfect fractional matching of
G
, let
c\in\mathbb{R}
and let
\textbf{y}\colon V(G)\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be a vertex weighting with
\textbf{y}_{v}\leq c
for all
v\in V(G)
.
Let
\Delta:=e^{\gamma\sqrt{\ln n}}

and let
T=(r_{0},\ldots,r_{m})
be an oriented tree with
n^{\frac{1}{4}}+1\leq|T|\leq 3\Delta n^{\frac{1}{4}}
and
\Delta(T)\leq\Delta
.
Let
R=(R_{0},\ldots,R_{m})
be a random tree in
G
according to
T
.
Then

\mathbb{P}\left[\left|\sum_{j=0}^{m}\textbf{y}_{R_{j}}\mathbbm{1}_{R_{j}\in S}-\frac{m+1}{n}\sum_{v\in S}\textbf{y}_{v}\right|\geq cn^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{17\sqrt{\ln n}}}\right]\leq\frac{1}{n^{3}}.

Proof.

Our strategy for the proof is to consider a partition of
T
into rooted subtrees. For each of those subtrees we ignore the vertices close to the root and then show that the behaviour for any pair of vertices in different subtrees is essentially independent.
To this end, we adapt ideas of Lemma 6.6 in
[
5
]
.

Let
\ell=n^{\frac{1}{4}}
.
As in Lemma
3.3
, we partition
T
into vertex-disjoint rooted subtrees
(T_{1},t_{1}),\ldots,(T_{k},t_{k})

such that
T_{i}\subseteq T(t_{i})
for every
i\in[k]
, and each subtree satisfies
\ell^{1-\zeta}\leq|T_{i}|\leq 2\Delta\ell^{1-\zeta}
,
where
\zeta:=\frac{\gamma}{{\ln\Delta}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\ln n}}
.
Note that
\frac{\ell^{\zeta}}{2\Delta}\leq k\leq 3\Delta\ell^{\zeta}
.
Let
T_{i}^{\prime}
denote the subforest of
T_{i}
induced by vertices of
T_{i}
of depth at least
\frac{1}{2}\log_{\Delta}\ell
in
T_{i}^{\prime}
.
Then
|T_{i}-T_{i}^{\prime}|\leq\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}\log_{\Delta}\ell}=\ell^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
Let
X_{j}:=\textbf{y}_{R_{j}}\mathbbm{1}_{R_{j}\in S}
for
j\in[m]_{0}
. Let
J_{i}:=\{j\in[m]_{0}\mid r_{j}\in V(T_{i}^{\prime})\}
and let
Y_{i}:=\sum_{j\in J_{i}}\textbf{y}_{R_{j}}\mathbbm{1}_{R_{j}\in S}
for
i\in[k]
.
Note that

(5.1)

\sum_{j=0}^{m}X_{j}\leq c\sum_{i=1}^{k}|T_{i}-T_{i}^{\prime}|+\sum_{i=1}^{k}Y_{i}\leq 3c\Delta\ell^{\frac{1}{2}+\zeta}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}Y_{i}.

Consider the exposure martingale
Z_{i}:=\mathbb{E}[\sum_{i^{\prime}=1}^{k}Y_{i^{\prime}}\mid Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{i}]
.
We show that
|Z_{i}-Z_{i-1}|
is not too large for any
i\in[k]
. Then we apply Azuma’s inequality to show that
Z_{0}=\mathbb{E}[\sum_{i=1}^{k}Y_{i}]
is close to
Z_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}Y_{i}
with high probability. As
\sum_{i=1}^{k}Y_{i}
is close to
\sum_{j=0}^{m}X_{j}
and
\mathbb{E}[\sum_{i=1}^{k}Y_{i}]
is close to
\frac{m+1}{n}\sum_{v\in S}\textbf{y}_{v}
, this yields the desired result.

Let
i\in[k]
and
y_{1},\ldots,y_{i},y_{i}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
be such that
y_{i^{\prime}}\leq c|T_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime}|
for each
{i^{\prime}}\in[i]
and
y_{i}^{\prime}\leq c|T_{i}^{\prime}|
.
Let
\mathcal{E}
be the event that
Y_{1}=y_{1},\ldots,Y_{i}=y_{i}
occurs and let
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
be the event that
Y_{1}=y_{1},\ldots,Y_{i-1}=y_{i-1},Y_{i}=y_{i}^{\prime}
occurs.
Let
i^{\prime}>i
and
j\in J_{i^{\prime}}
.
Note that the length of the path from
t_{i^{\prime}}
to any vertex in
T_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime}
is at least
\frac{1}{2}\log_{\Delta}\ell
.

By the definition of a random tree, this path, when embedded into
G
, can be regarded as a random walk.

Thus for any
v\in V(G)
and
j\in J_{i^{\prime}}
, Corollary
5.4
yields
\mathbb{P}[R_{j}=v\mid\mathcal{E}]=(1\pm e^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2b^{2}}\frac{1}{2}\log_{\Delta}\ell})\frac{1}{n}=(1\pm\ell^{-\zeta})\frac{1}{n}
, where the last inequality follows from
\gamma\ll\frac{1}{b}\ll\varepsilon
.
This implies

(5.2)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Y_{i^{\prime}}\mid\mathcal{E}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in J_{i^{\prime}}}\sum_{v\in S}\textbf{y}_{v}\mathbb{E}[\mathbbm{1}_{R_{j}=v}\mid\mathcal{E}]=\sum_{j\in J_{i^{\prime}}}\sum_{v\in S}\textbf{y}_{v}\mathbb{P}[R_{j}=v\mid\mathcal{E}]=(1\pm\ell^{-\zeta})\frac{|T_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime}|}{n}\sum_{v\in S}\textbf{y}_{v}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i^{\prime}=1}^{k}Y_{i^{\prime}}\mid\mathcal{E}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i^{\prime}=1}^{k}Y_{i^{\prime}}\mid\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq|y_{i}-y_{i}^{\prime}|+\sum_{i^{\prime}=i+1}^{k}|\mathbb{E}[Y_{i^{\prime}}\mid\mathcal{E}]-\mathbb{E}[Y_{i^{\prime}}\mid\mathcal{E}^{\prime}]|

\displaystyle\leq c|T_{i}^{\prime}|+\sum_{i^{\prime}=i+1}^{k}\left|(1\pm\ell^{-\zeta})\frac{|T_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime}|}{n}\sum_{v\in S}\textbf{y}_{v}-(1\pm\ell^{-\zeta})\frac{|T_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime}|}{n}\sum_{v\in S}\textbf{y}_{v}\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2c\Delta\ell^{1-\zeta}+\frac{2}{n}\ell^{-\zeta}\sum_{v\in S}\textbf{y}_{v}\sum_{i^{\prime}=i+1}^{k}|T_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime}|\leq 8c\Delta\ell^{1-\zeta}.

Hence,
|Z_{i}-Z_{i-1}|\leq 8c\Delta\ell^{1-\zeta}=8c\Delta n^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\zeta}{4}}
for all
i\in[k]
.
Lemma
3.2

yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\left|Z_{k}-Z_{0}\right|\geq c\Delta n^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\zeta}{16}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{c^{2}\Delta^{2}n^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\zeta}{8}}}{2k(8c\Delta n^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\zeta}{4}})^{2}}\right)=2\exp\left(-\frac{1}{128k}n^{\frac{3}{8}\zeta}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{1}{384\Delta}n^{\frac{\zeta}{8}}\right)\leq\frac{1}{n^{3}}.

We have
Z_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}Y_{i}
and
Z_{0}=\mathbb{E}[\sum_{i=1}^{k}Y_{i}]=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{E}Y_{i}=(1\pm\ell^{-\zeta})\frac{1}{n}\sum_{v\in S}\textbf{y}_{v}\sum_{i=1}^{k}|T_{i}^{\prime}|
, which follows as in (
5.2
).
As
m+1-3\Delta\ell^{\frac{1}{2}+\zeta}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}|T_{i}^{\prime}|\leq m+1
, we obtain

(5.3)

Z_{0}=\frac{m+1}{n}\sum_{v\in S}\textbf{y}_{v}\pm 6c\Delta\ell^{1-\zeta}.

Recall that
Z_{k}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{m}X_{j}\leq Z_{k}+3c\Delta\ell^{\frac{1}{2}+\zeta}
by (

). Combining this with (

) yields

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{j=0}^{m}X_{j}-\frac{m+1}{n}\sum_{v\in S}\textbf{y}_{v}\right|

\displaystyle\leq|Z_{k}-Z_{0}|+\left|Z_{0}-\frac{m+1}{n}\sum_{v\in S}\textbf{y}_{v}\right|+3c\Delta\ell^{\frac{1}{2}+\zeta}

\displaystyle\leq|Z_{k}-Z_{0}|+9c\Delta n^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\zeta}{16}}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\left|\sum_{j=0}^{m}X_{j}-\frac{m+1}{n}\sum_{v\in S}\textbf{y}_{v}\right|\geq cn^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{17\sqrt{\ln n}}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\left|\sum_{j=0}^{m}X_{j}-\frac{m+1}{n}\sum_{v\in S}\textbf{y}_{v}\right|\geq 10c\Delta n^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\zeta}{16}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\left|Z_{k}-Z_{0}\right|\geq c\Delta n^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\zeta}{16}}\right]\leq\frac{1}{n^{3}},

which proves the statement.
∎

The next theorem yields a lower bound on the entropy of a random tree in an

Theorem 6.2
.

Suppose
\frac{1}{n}\ll\gamma\ll\frac{1}{b}\ll\varepsilon
. Let
\Delta:=e^{\gamma\sqrt{\ln n}}
, let
G
be an
(n,\varepsilon)
-digraph
, let
x
be a
b
-normal
perfect fractional matching of
G
and let
v_{0}\in V(G)
.
Let
Q=(r_{0},\ldots,r_{m})
be an oriented tree with
n^{\frac{1}{4}}+1\leq|Q|\leq 3\Delta n^{\frac{1}{4}}
and
\Delta(Q)\leq\Delta
, and let
R=(R_{0},\ldots,R_{m})
be a random tree in
G
according to
Q
, where
R_{0}=v_{0}
.
Then

H(R)\geq(1-2e^{-\sqrt{\ln n}})\frac{m}{n}h(\textbf{x}).

Proof.

We prove this statement by applying the chain rule of entropy to write
H(R)
as sum of conditional entropies, where
H(R_{i})
is only conditioned on the path between
R_{0}
and
R_{i}
in the random tree. We then only consider those vertices in the tree with a certain minimum distance from the root and utilise Corollary
5.4
to find a lower bound of
H(R)
in terms of
h(\textbf{x})
.

Let
r_{j}
be an ancestor of
r_{i}
and
r_{i^{\prime}}
.

By definition,
R_{i}\mid R_{j}
and
R_{i^{\prime}}\mid R_{j}
are independent, hence,
H(R_{i}\mid R_{j},R_{i^{\prime}})=H(R_{i}\mid R_{j})
.
By induction we conclude that for every
i\in[m]
, there is a path
r_{0},r_{i_{1}},\ldots,r_{i_{k}},r_{i}
in
Q
such that
H(R_{i}\mid R_{0},\ldots,R_{i-1})=H(R_{i}\mid R_{0},R_{i_{1}},\ldots,R_{i_{k}})
.
For any ordered set
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{s})
of vertices, let
\mathcal{A}_{(v_{1},\ldots,v_{s})}
be the event that
R_{i_{1}}=v_{1},\ldots,R_{i_{s}}=v_{s}
.
Suppose
r_{i_{k}}r_{i}\in E(T)
.
Using the Markov property we obtain

\displaystyle H(R_{i}\mid\mathcal{A}_{(v_{i_{1}},\ldots,v_{i_{k}})})

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in V(G)}\mathbb{P}[R_{i}=v\mid\mathcal{A}_{(v_{i_{1}},\ldots,v_{i_{k}})}]\log\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}[R_{i}=v\mid\mathcal{A}_{(v_{i_{1}},\ldots,v_{i_{k}})}]}

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in V(G)}\mathbb{P}[R_{i}=v\mid R_{i_{k}}=v_{i_{k}}]\log\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}[R_{i}=v\mid R_{i_{k}}=v_{i_{k}}]}

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in V(G)}\textbf{x}_{v_{i_{k}}v}\log\frac{1}{\textbf{x}_{v_{i_{k}}v}}=h_{\textbf{x}}^{+}(v_{i_{k}}).

Thus, using the law of total probability,

\displaystyle H(R_{i}\mid R_{0},\ldots,R_{i_{k}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{v_{i_{1}},\ldots,v_{i_{k}}\in V(G)}\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{A}_{(v_{i_{1}},\ldots,v_{i_{k}})}]H(R_{i}\mid\mathcal{A}_{(v_{i_{1}},\ldots,v_{i_{k}})})

\displaystyle=\sum_{v_{i_{1}},\ldots,v_{i_{k}}\in V(G)}\mathbb{P}[R_{i_{k}}=v_{i_{k}}\mid\mathcal{A}_{(v_{i_{1}},\ldots,v_{i_{k-1}})}]\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{A}_{(v_{i_{1}},\ldots,v_{i_{k-1}})}]h_{\textbf{x}}^{+}(v_{i_{k}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{v_{i_{k}}\in V(G)}\mathbb{P}[R_{i_{k}}=v_{i_{k}}]h_{\textbf{x}}^{+}(v_{i_{k}})=\mathbb{E}h_{\textbf{x}}^{+}(R_{i_{k}}).

If
r_{i}r_{i_{k}}\in E(T)
, we analogously obtain
H(R_{i}\mid R_{0},\ldots,R_{i_{k}})=\mathbb{E}h_{\textbf{x}}^{-}(R_{i_{k}})
.

Recall that
(r_{0},\ldots,r_{m})
is a breadth-first ordering of the vertices of
T

\Delta(T)\leq\Delta
, all children of a vertex
r_{j}
are in the set
\{r_{j+1},\ldots,r_{(j+1)\Delta}\}

Thus for any
a\in\{2,\ldots,m\}
and

, the parent of
r_{i}
must be in the set
\{r_{\frac{a}{2\Delta}},\ldots,r_{i-1}\}

H(R_{i}\mid R_{0},\ldots,R_{i-1})\geq\min_{j\geq\frac{a}{2\Delta}}\{\mathbb{E}h_{\textbf{x}}^{+}(R_{j}),\mathbb{E}h_{\textbf{x}}^{-}(R_{j})\}

Thus, using the chain rule of entropy,

H(R)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}H(R_{i}\mid R_{0},\ldots,R_{i-1})\geq(m-a)\min_{j\geq\frac{a}{2\Delta}}\{\mathbb{E}h_{\textbf{x}}^{+}(R_{j}),\mathbb{E}h_{\textbf{x}}^{-}(R_{j})\}.

Let
a:=2\Delta n^{\frac{1}{8}}
and
j\geq\frac{a}{2\Delta}
. Then the depth of

is at least
\frac{1}{2}\log_{\Delta}j\geq\frac{\ln n}{16\ln\Delta}=\frac{\sqrt{\ln n}}{16\gamma}

Hence, according to Corollary
5.4
,
for each

\mathbb{P}[R_{j}=v]=\frac{1}{n}(1\pm e^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{32b^{2}\gamma}\sqrt{\ln n}})=\frac{1}{n}(1\pm e^{-\sqrt{\ln n}})

\gamma\ll\frac{1}{b}\ll\varepsilon

Thus, for each

and

, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}h_{\textbf{x}}^{*}(R_{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in V(G)}h_{\textbf{x}}^{*}(v)\mathbb{P}[R_{j}=v]=\frac{1}{n}(1\pm e^{-\sqrt{\ln n}})\sum_{v\in V(G)}h_{\textbf{x}}^{*}(v)=\frac{1}{n}(1\pm e^{-\sqrt{\ln n}})h(\textbf{x}).

This implies

∎

-well-behaved tree, (i) follows. As
N_{G,G^{\prime\prime}}^{*}(v)=N_{G,G^{\prime}}^{*}(v)\setminus(N_{G,G^{\prime}}^{*}(v)\cap V(Q))
, this yields

\displaystyle|N_{G,G^{\prime\prime}}^{*}(v)|

\displaystyle=|N_{G,G^{\prime}}^{*}(v)|-|N_{G,G^{\prime}}^{*}(v)\cap V(Q)|>\frac{n^{\prime}-(m+1)}{n^{\prime}}|N_{G,G^{\prime}}^{*}(v)|-(n^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{17\sqrt{\ln n^{\prime}}}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|N_{G,G^{\prime}}^{*}(v)|}{n^{\prime}}n^{\prime\prime}-(n^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{18\sqrt{\ln n^{\prime}}}}\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon^{\prime}\right)n^{\prime\prime}-(n^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{18\sqrt{\ln n^{\prime}}}}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}\right)n^{\prime\prime}

for any

.
To prove (iv), we apply Lemma
4.5
with
G,G^{\prime},G^{\prime\prime},V(Q)
playing the role of

, respectively.
Conditions (i) and (ii) in Lemma
4.5
follow from

being well-behaved, condition (iii) in Lemma
4.5
follows from the assumption that

for all

and

, while condition (iv) in Lemma
4.5
follows from (i).
Thus we are able to apply Lemma
4.5
and the claim follows.
∎

In the next theorem, we iterate Corollary

to prove that there are essentially at least

Theorem 6.5
.

Suppose
\frac{1}{n}\ll\gamma\ll\varepsilon
.

\Delta:=e^{\gamma\sqrt{\ln n}}
and

\alpha:=\frac{1}{7000\sqrt{\ln n}}

Let
\mu

.
Let
(T,t)
be an oriented labelled rooted tree with

and
\Delta(T)\leq\Delta
.

(n,\varepsilon)
-digraph,

Proof.

Note that

-digraph.
Let

\textbf{x}_{0}
be a
\frac{b}{2}
-normal perfect fractional matching of

).

We apply Lemma
3.5
with
n-\mu n
playing the role of
n
to decompose

into rooted subtrees

such that the following holds where
n_{0}:=n-|A|
and

(i)

V(T)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}V(T_{i})
,

(ii)

T_{i}\subseteq T(t_{i})
for every

,

(iii)

the depth of

(iv)

for all

, there is a unique

T_{i}
and

intersect, and in this case we have

(v)

n_{i-1}^{\frac{1}{4}}+1\leq|T_{i}|\leq 3\Delta n_{i-1}^{\frac{1}{4}}
for every

.

In particular,

and

.

Let
n_{k}:=n-|A|-|T_{1}\cup\ldots\cup T_{k}|\geq n^{1-7\alpha}

As

for each

, we have
k\leq\frac{n}{\min_{i\in[k]}|T_{i}|}\leq n^{\frac{3}{4}+\frac{7\alpha}{4}}
.
Further, as
n_{i}\geq n_{k}
and thus
n\leq n_{i}^{2}
for any
i\in[k]_{0}
, the tree
T
has maximum degree at most
\Delta=e^{\gamma\sqrt{\ln n}}\leq e^{\gamma\sqrt{\ln n_{i}^{2}}}=e^{\sqrt{2}\gamma\sqrt{\ln n_{i}}}
for any

.
By replacing

with

, we can assume that
T
(and thus each subtree
T_{i}
) has maximum degree at most
e^{\gamma\sqrt{\ln n_{i}}}
for each
i\in[k]_{0}
.

We iteratively embed
T
into
G_{0}
. To be more precise, in step

we embed the tree

using the properties of Lemma

.
Let
T_{0}

.
Suppose that

and

in

. Let
G_{i}^{\prime}:=G_{0}-Q_{i}
.
As there is a tree
T_{j}
with

and

by (iv), the image

of

in

satisfies

.
Thus, to embed
T_{i+1}-t_{i+1}
into

to obtain a copy of
T_{0}\cup\ldots\cup T_{i+1}
, we temporarily add
u_{i+1}
to

and then embed
T_{i+1}
.
Define

.
We further define

for a copy
Q
of
T
in
G_{0}
.
Note that
n_{i}=|V(G_{i})|
for each
i\in[k]_{0}
.
We employ Lemma
6.4
to show that this iterative procedure is indeed possible.

In order to make this iteration possible, we show that the important parameters do not degenerate too quickly.
Let
\mathbf{x}_{i}
be the perfect fractional matching of
G_{i}
given by Lemma
6.4
(iv).
Let
\varepsilon_{i}
be such that

is an
(n_{i},\varepsilon_{i})
-digraph as given by Lemma
6.4
(iii).
Lemma
6.4
(iv) implies that

(6.1)

h(\textbf{x}_{i})\geq\frac{n_{i}}{n_{i-1}}h(\textbf{x}_{i-1})-n_{i}\log\frac{n_{i-1}}{n_{i}}-n_{i-1}^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{24\sqrt{\ln n_{i-1}}}}.

Next we show that
\varepsilon_{i}\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{4}
for all
i\in[k]
.
Note that, as
k\leq n^{\frac{3}{4}+\frac{7\alpha}{4}}
, we obtain
2kn_{i}^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{18\sqrt{\ln n_{i}}}}\leq 2k(n^{1-7\alpha})^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{18\sqrt{\ln n^{1-7\alpha}}}}\leq n^{14\alpha-\frac{1}{20\sqrt{\ln n}}}\leq n^{-\frac{1}{30\sqrt{\ln n}}}
for each
i\in[k]
.
Observe that
G_{0}
is an
(n_{0},\frac{\varepsilon}{2})
-digraph and thus Lemma
6.4
(iii) yields

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{i}\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}-2kn_{i}^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{18\sqrt{\ln n_{i}}}}\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}-n^{-\frac{1}{30\sqrt{\ln n}}}>\frac{\varepsilon}{4}

for each
i\in[k]
.
By replacing
\varepsilon
with
\frac{\varepsilon}{4}
, we can assume that
G
is an
(n,\varepsilon)
-digraph and that each
G_{i}
is an
(n_{i},\varepsilon)
-digraph.

Next we show that
\mathbf{x}_{i}
is suitably normal for each
i\in[k]_{0}
.
To this end, we first calculate an upper bound for the normality of
\mathbf{x}_{i}
in
G_{i}
for the smallest
i
where
n_{i}\leq\frac{n}{2}
.
As for all
j\leq i
, the tree
T_{j}
has at least
(\frac{n}{3})^{\frac{1}{4}}
vertices, we conclude that
i\leq n(\frac{n}{3})^{-\frac{1}{4}}\leq 2n^{\frac{3}{4}}
.
Hence, by Lemma
6.4
(iv),
\mathbf{x}_{i}
is
b_{i}
-normal with

\displaystyle b_{i}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\left(\frac{n}{3}\right)^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{22\sqrt{\ln\frac{n}{3}}}}\right)^{2n^{\frac{3}{4}}}\cdot\frac{b}{2}\leq\exp(3n^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{22\sqrt{\ln n}}}\cdot 2n^{\frac{3}{4}})\cdot\frac{b}{2}=\exp(6n^{-\frac{1}{22\sqrt{\ln n}}})\cdot\frac{b}{2}

\displaystyle\leq\exp(n^{-\frac{1}{23\sqrt{\ln n}}})\cdot\frac{b}{2}.

This means that the normality grows by at most a multiplicative factor of
\exp(n^{-\frac{1}{23\sqrt{\ln n}}})

for each time the number of vertices of our considered graph shrinks by a factor of
2
.
We can iterate this calculation until we reach
G_{k}
. For this, we need at most
\log n
iterations.
As
\exp(n_{k}^{-\frac{1}{23\sqrt{\ln n_{k}}}}\log n)\leq 2
, we conclude that
\mathbf{x}_{i}
is
b
-normal for each
i\in[k]_{0}
.

Recall that
\mathcal{Q}
denotes the set of all trees in
G_{0}
isomorphic to
T
where
t
is mapped to
u_{0}
.
We now apply Corollary
6.3
to each
G_{i}
and use (
6.1
). This yields a lower bound for
|\mathcal{Q}|
in terms of
h(\textbf{x}_{0})
. A suitable estimate for the remaining terms yields the desired lower bound.

Suppose
T_{0},\ldots,T_{i}
have already been embedded in
G_{0}
. Recall that
t_{i+1}\in V(T_{0}\cup\ldots\cup T_{i})
and
u_{i+1}
denotes the image of
t_{i+1}
in
G_{0}
.
Denote by
\mathcal{Q}_{i+1}
the set of
(G,G_{i})
-well-behaved trees in
G_{i}
isomorphic to
T_{i+1}
where
t_{i+1}
is mapped to
u_{i+1}
.
Let
Q^{*}_{i+1}:=\min|\mathcal{Q}_{i+1}|
, where the minimum is taken over all possible embeddings of
T_{0}\cup\ldots\cup T_{i}
in
G_{0}
where
t
is mapped to
u_{0}
.
Note that
|\mathcal{Q}|\geq\prod_{i=1}^{k}Q_{i}^{*}
.
Let
m_{i}:=|T_{i}|-1
.
Applying Corollary
6.3
to
G_{i}
for each
i\in[k-1]_{0}
yields

(6.2)

\displaystyle\log|\mathcal{Q}|

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\log Q_{i}^{*}\geq\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}(1-e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\ln n_{i}}})\frac{m_{i+1}}{n_{i}}h(\textbf{x}_{i})\geq(1-e^{-\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{\ln n}})\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\frac{m_{i+1}}{n_{i}}h(\textbf{x}_{i}),

where we used
n_{i}\geq n^{1-7\alpha}\geq n^{\frac{1}{4}}
and thus
e^{\sqrt{\ln n_{i}}}\geq e^{\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\ln n}}
in the last inequality.
Next we bound

from below.
Using (
6.1
) and
\log\frac{n_{i-1}}{n_{i}}+\log\frac{n_{i-2}}{n_{i-1}}=\log\frac{n_{i-2}}{n_{i}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle h(\textbf{x}_{i})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n_{i}}{n_{i-1}}h(\textbf{x}_{i-1})-n_{i}\log\frac{n_{i-1}}{n_{i}}-n_{i-1}^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{24\sqrt{\ln n_{i-1}}}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n_{i}}{n_{i-1}}\left(\frac{n_{i-1}}{n_{i-2}}h(\textbf{x}_{i-2})-n_{i-1}\log\frac{n_{i-2}}{n_{i-1}}-n_{i-2}^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{24\sqrt{\ln n_{i-2}}}}\right)-n_{i}\log\frac{n_{i-1}}{n_{i}}-n_{i-1}^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{24\sqrt{\ln n_{i-1}}}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n_{i}}{n_{i-2}}h(\textbf{x}_{i-2})-n_{i}\log\frac{n_{i-2}}{n_{i}}-2n^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{24\sqrt{\ln n}}}.

Iterating this calculation yields

(6.3)

\displaystyle h(\textbf{x}_{i})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n_{i}}{n_{0}}h(\textbf{x}_{0})-n_{i}\log\frac{n_{0}}{n_{i}}-in^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{24\sqrt{\ln n}}}\geq\frac{n_{i}}{n_{0}}h(\textbf{x}_{0})-n_{i}\log\frac{n_{0}}{n_{i}}-kn^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{24\sqrt{\ln n}}}

for each
i\in[k]
.
Recall that
n_{i}\geq n^{1-7\alpha}
and
m_{i+1}\leq 3\Delta n_{i}^{\frac{1}{4}}
.
Thus
\frac{m_{i+1}}{n_{i}}\leq 3\Delta n_{i}^{-\frac{3}{4}}\leq 3\Delta n^{-\frac{3}{4}+\frac{21\alpha}{4}}\leq 3n^{-\frac{3}{4}+\frac{21\alpha}{4}+\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{\ln n_{i}}}}
and hence
\frac{m_{i+1}}{n_{i}}kn^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{24\sqrt{\ln n}}}\leq 3n^{-\frac{3}{4}+\frac{21\alpha}{4}+\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{\ln n_{i}}}}n^{\frac{3}{4}+\frac{7\alpha}{4}}n^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{24\sqrt{\ln n}}}=3n^{\frac{1}{4}+7\alpha+\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{\ln n_{i}}}-\frac{1}{24\sqrt{\ln n}}}\leq n^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{100\sqrt{\ln n}}}
.
Combining this with (
6.3
) yields

\displaystyle\frac{m_{i+1}}{n_{i}}h(\textbf{x}_{i})\geq\frac{m_{i+1}}{n_{0}}h(\textbf{x}_{0})-m_{i+1}\log\frac{n_{0}}{n_{i}}-n^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{100\sqrt{\ln n}}}.

Hence (
6.2
) implies

(6.4)

\log|\mathcal{Q}|\geq(1-e^{-\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{\ln n}})\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\frac{m_{i+1}}{n_{0}}h(\textbf{x}_{0})-\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}m_{i+1}\log\frac{n_{0}}{n_{i}}-kn^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{100\sqrt{\ln n}}}.

We bound the first term from below.
Note that
\frac{1}{n_{0}}(m_{1}+\ldots+m_{k})=\frac{|T|-1}{n_{0}}\geq\frac{n-\Delta n^{1-\alpha}-1}{n}\geq 1-e^{-\frac{1}{10^{4}}\sqrt{\ln n}}

and thus

(6.5)

(1-e^{-\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{\ln n}})\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\frac{m_{i+1}}{n_{0}}h(\textbf{x}_{0})\geq(1-e^{-\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{\ln n}})(1-e^{-\frac{1}{10^{4}}\sqrt{\ln n}})h(\textbf{x}_{0})\geq(1-e^{-\frac{1}{10^{5}}\sqrt{\ln n}})h(\textbf{x}_{0}).

We bound the second term of (
6.4
) from below. As
n-n_{k}=m_{1}+\ldots+m_{k}+1
, we obtain
\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}m_{i+1}\log n_{0}\leq\log n\cdot\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}m_{i+1}\leq n\log n-n_{k}\log n
.
Note that for each
i\in[k-1]_{0}
,

\displaystyle m_{i+1}\log n_{i}

\displaystyle\geq\log n_{i}+\log(n_{i}-1)+\ldots+\log(n_{i}-m_{i+1}+1)

\displaystyle=\log(n_{i}(n_{i}-1)\cdot\ldots\cdot(n_{i+1}+1)).

Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}m_{i+1}\log n_{i}

\displaystyle\geq\log(n_{0}(n_{0}-1)\cdot\ldots\cdot(n_{k}+1))\geq\log\frac{n!}{n^{n-n_{0}}n_{k}!}

\displaystyle=\log n!-\log n_{k}!-(n-n_{0})\log n.

As
n-n_{0}=|A|\leq\Delta n^{1-\alpha}
and
\log n!=n\log n-n\log e\pm\log n
by Stirling’s approximation, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}m_{i+1}\log n_{i}

\displaystyle\geq n\log n-n\log e-n_{k}\log n_{k}+n_{k}\log e-2\log n-\Delta n^{1-\alpha}\log n

\displaystyle\geq n\log n-n\log e-n_{k}\log n_{k}-n^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}.

This yields

(6.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}m_{i+1}\log\frac{n_{0}}{n_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq-n_{k}\log n+n\log e+n_{k}\log n_{k}+n^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\leq n\log e+n^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}.

Combining (
6.4
) with (
6.5
) and (
6.6
), we obtain

\displaystyle\log|\mathcal{Q}|

\displaystyle\geq(1-e^{-\frac{1}{10^{5}}\sqrt{\ln n}})h(\textbf{x}_{0})-n\log e-n^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}-kn^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{100\sqrt{\ln n}}}

\displaystyle\geq h(\textbf{x}_{0})-n\log e-n^{1-\frac{1}{10^{6}\sqrt{\ln n}}}.

As we chose
h(\textbf{x}_{0})
such that
h(\textbf{x}_{0})\geq h(G)-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}n
, this yields the desired result.
∎

.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Suppose
\frac{1}{n}\ll\gamma\ll\varepsilon
. Let
G
be an
(n,\varepsilon)
-digraph, let
\Delta:=e^{\gamma\sqrt{\ln n}}
and
T
be an oriented tree on
n
vertices with
\Delta(T)\leq\Delta
.
Label the vertices of
T
arbitrarily and choose a vertex
r\in V(T)
as root of
T
.
Let
\alpha:=\frac{1}{7000\sqrt{\ln n}}
.
Choose
t^{\prime\prime}
at maximal distance from

subject to
|T(t^{\prime\prime})|\geq n^{1-\alpha}
. Then
n^{1-\alpha}\leq|T(t^{\prime\prime})|\leq\Delta n^{1-\alpha}
. Note that
t^{\prime\prime}
has exactly one neighbour
t^{\prime}
in
V(T)\setminus V(T(t^{\prime\prime}))
. Define
T^{\prime}:=T-T(t^{\prime\prime})
and
T^{\prime\prime}:=T(t^{\prime\prime})
.

Let
\mu:=\frac{|T^{\prime\prime}|}{n}
. Note that
\mu\leq e^{\gamma\sqrt{\ln n}-\frac{1}{7000}\sqrt{\ln n}}\leq e^{-\frac{1}{10^{4}}\sqrt{\ln n}}<\varepsilon^{4}
as
\gamma\ll\varepsilon
. We apply Theorem
6.1
with
T^{\prime\prime}
playing the role of
T
and
t^{\prime\prime}
playing the role of
t
to fix a vertex set
A\subseteq V(G)
of size
|T^{\prime\prime}|-\mu^{7}n
and a vertex
v\in A
such that for any set
B\subseteq V(G)
with
A\subseteq B
and
|B|=|T^{\prime\prime}|
, the digraph
G[B]
contains a copy of
T^{\prime\prime}
in which
t^{\prime\prime}
is mapped to
v
. Further, let
w\in N^{-}(v)
if
t^{\prime}t^{\prime\prime}\in E(T)
, otherwise let
w\in N^{+}(v)
.

As
T^{\prime}
satisfies the conditions of Theorem
6.5
, we find at least
2^{h(G)-n\log e-\varepsilon n}
copies of
T^{\prime}
in
G-A
such that
t^{\prime}
is mapped to
w
. Let
Q^{\prime}
be a tree in
G-A
isomorphic to
T^{\prime}
.
As
|G-Q^{\prime}|=|T^{\prime\prime}|
, using the above mentioned property of

, we find a copy of
T^{\prime\prime}
in
G-Q^{\prime}
in which

is mapped to

. As
t^{\prime}
is mapped to

, we are able to connect

and

in

, embedding

completely into

.

In order to count the number of subgraphs in

isomorphic to

,
one has to divide the number of labelled copies of

in

by the number of automorphisms of

, which yields the desired result.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Suppose
\frac{1}{n}\ll\gamma\ll\varepsilon
. Let
G
be an
(n,\varepsilon)
-graph.
Let
D
be the digraph that arises from
G
by duplicating every edge of
G
and orienting them in opposite directions.
To be more precise, we set
V(D):=V(G)
and
E(D):=\{(v,w)\mid vw\in E(G)\}
.
If
x
is any perfect fractional matching of
G
, we obtain a perfect fractional matching
y
of
D
by setting
\textbf{y}_{(v,w)}:=\textbf{x}_{vw}
. Then
h(\textbf{y})=2h(\textbf{x})
, and, in particular,
h(D)\geq 2h(G)
.
Let
T
be a tree on
n
vertices with
\Delta(T)\leq e^{\gamma\sqrt{\ln n}}
. We orient the edges of
T
arbitrarily. Call this oriented tree
T_{D}
. By Theorem
1.5
, there are at least
|\operatorname{Aut}(T)|^{-1}2^{h(D)-n\log e-\varepsilon n}\geq|\operatorname{Aut}(T)|^{-1}2^{2h(G)-n\log e-\varepsilon n}
copies of
T_{D}
in
D
. Note that any copy
Q_{D}
of
T_{D}
in
D
yields a copy
Q
of
T
in
G
by ignoring the orientation of the edges.
This yields the desired result.
∎

7.
Conclusion

We have shown that any digraph
G
on
n
vertices that satisfies
\delta^{0}(G)\geq(\frac{1}{2}+o(1))n
contains at least
|\operatorname{Aut}(T)|^{-1}(\frac{1}{2}-o(1))^{n}n!
copies of any oriented tree
T
on
n
vertices with
\Delta(T)\leq e^{o(\sqrt{\log n})}
.
This follows from the following stronger result.
If
h(G)
denotes the entropy of
G
, that is, the maximum entropy of all perfect fractional matchings of
G
, then
G
contains at least
2^{h(G)-n\log e-\varepsilon n}
(labelled) copies of
T
.
The analogous results hold for undirected graphs and trees.

It would be interesting to find an upper bound on the number of copies of spanning trees in (di)graphs.
We believe that the lower bound in Theorem
1.5
is also an upper bound and the inequality is in fact an equality (up to the error term).
Unfortunately, there seems to be no easy way to obtain an upper bound with the methods as it is obtained for Hamilton cycles.

Note that we require
T
to satisfy
\Delta(T)\leq e^{o(\sqrt{\log n})}
, whereas
G
contains any tree on
n
vertices with
\Delta(T)\leq o(\frac{n}{\log n})
.
Extending our results to trees with maximum degree
o(\frac{n}{\log n})
is another interesting open problem.

Abstract
. By making a seminal use of the maximum modulus principle of holomorphic functions we prove existence of n n -best kernel approximation for a wide class of reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces of holomorphic functions in the unit disc, and for the corresponding class of Bochner type spaces of stochastic processes. This study thus generalizes the classical result of n n -best rational approximation for the Hardy space and a recent result of n n -best kernel approximation for the weighted Bergman spaces of the unit disc. The type of approximations have significant applications to signal and image processing and system identification, as well as to numerical solutions of the classical and the stochastic type integral and differential equations.

We will be based on the general concept reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS). In 1930, to study the partial differential equation

\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}}+\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial y^{2}}+\alpha(x,y)\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}+\beta(x,y)\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}+\gamma(x,y)u=0,

is a bounded region,
\alpha(x,y),\beta(x,y),\gamma(x,y)\in C^{2}(\Omega),\Omega

[

[

The Hardy space is defined

\mathbb{H}^{2}({\bf D})=\{f:{\bf D}\to{\bf{C}}\ :\ f\ {\rm is\ analytic\ in}\ {\bf D}\ {\rm and}\ \|f\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}=\sup_{0\leq r<1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(r{\rm e}^{it})|^{2}<\infty\}.

As a fundamental result, functions in the Hardy space have non-tangential boundary limits a.e. on the unit circle
\partial{\bf D}
. The Hardy space is isometric to the function space
{\mathbb{H}^{2}}_{\partial{\bf D}}
consisting of the non-tangential limiting functions. One of the alternative definitions of the space
{\mathbb{H}^{2}}_{\partial{\bf D}}
is

{\mathbb{H}^{2}}_{\partial{\bf D}}=\{f:\partial{\bf D}\to{\bf{C}}\ :\ f\in L^{2}(\partial{\bf D}),\quad f({\rm e}^{it})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k}{\rm e}^{ikt},\quad\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|c_{k}|^{2}<\infty\}.

{\mathbb{H}^{2}}_{\partial{\bf D}}
is a closed subspace of the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\partial{\bf D})
equipped with the inner product

(1.1)

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}f({\rm e}^{it})\overline{g({\rm e}^{it})}dt.

For a given positive integer
n,
an ordered pair of polynomials
(p,q)
is called an
n
-
admissible pair
if
p
and
q
are co-prime,
q\neq 0
in
{\bf D},
and both the degrees of
p
and
q
do not exceed
n.
The famous
n
-
best rational approximation problem
in
\mathbb{H}^{2}({\bf D})
is as follows: For
f\in\mathbb{H}^{2}({\bf D}),
find an
n
-admissible ordered pair
(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})
such that

(1.2)

\displaystyle\|f-\frac{\tilde{p}}{\tilde{q}}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}({\bf D})}=\inf\{\|f-\frac{p}{q}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}({\bf D})}\ :\ (p,q)\ {\rm is\ an}\ n{\mbox{-}admissible\ pair}\}.

Closely related to rational approximation there exist studies on what is called Takenaka-Malmquist (TM)
system, or rational orthogonal system:

(1.3)

\displaystyle\{B_{a_{1}a_{2}...a_{n}}(z)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}=\left\{\frac{\sqrt{1-|a_{n}|^{2}}}{1-\bar{a}_{n}z}\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{z-a_{k}}{1-\bar{a}_{k}z}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty},\quad a_{1},\cdots,a_{n},\cdots,\in{\bf D},

where
B_{a_{1}a_{2}...a_{n}}(z)=e_{a_{n}}(z)\phi_{a_{1}a_{2}...a_{n-1}}(z),
where
e_{a_{n}}
is the normalized Szegö kernel at
a_{n},

e_{a_{n}}(z)=\frac{\sqrt{1-|a_{n}|^{2}}}{1-\overline{a}_{n}z},

and the
canonical Blaschke product
with zeros
a_{1},\cdots,a_{n-1},

(1.4)

\displaystyle\phi_{a_{1}a_{2}...a_{n-1}}(z)=\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{z-a_{k}}{1-\bar{a}_{k}z}.

There further holds the relation, for
a\in\bf D,

(1.5)

\displaystyle e_{a}(z)=\frac{\sqrt{1-|a|^{2}}}{1-\overline{a}z}=\frac{k_{a}}{\|k_{a}\|},

where

k_{a}(z)=\frac{{1}}{1-\overline{a}z}\quad{\rm and}\quad\|k_{a}\|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-|a|^{2}}}

are, respectively, the reproducing kernel of
\mathbb{H}^{2}({\bf D})
and the normalizing constant making
\|e_{a}\|=1.

The above formulated
n
-best rational approximation problem (
1.2
) is, in essence, equivalent to the following

-

be a given positive integer. If

{\bf D},

\displaystyle\|f-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle f,B_{a_{1}\cdots a_{k}}\rangle B_{a_{1}\cdots a_{k}}\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\{\|f-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle f,B_{b_{1}\cdots b_{k}}\rangle B_{b_{1}\cdots b_{k}}\|\ :\ b_{1},\cdots,b_{n}\ \in{\bf D}\}.

The formulation of the problem allows multiplicity of the zeros
a_{k}
’s.
A solution of (
1

\displaystyle\|f-\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}\tilde{k}_{a_{j}}\|=\inf\{\|f-\sum_{j=1}^{n}c^{\prime}_{j}{k}_{a^{\prime}_{j}}\|:a^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,a^{\prime}_{n}\ \in{\bf D}

The question for
n
-best kernel approximation can be raised in general RKHSs, or even in Hilbert spaces with a dictionary. The recently published new proof of existence of

The study in the present paper is directly motivated by the recent new proofs of the existence on the Hardy space (
[

Based on further analysis of the orthogonalization projection operator
Q_{a_{1}a_{2}...a_{k}}
and factorization of higher order generalized backwardd
shift operators
Q_{a_{1}a_{2}...a_{k}}/\phi_{a_{1}\cdots{a_{m-1}}}
(see §3), the present paper is able to avoid use of the pointwise estimations of the reproducing kernels of the involved zero spaces and the Blaschke weighted spaces. For RKHSs more general than the Bergman ones such kernel estimations may be impossible. As a result, we are able to assert existence of the
n
-best problem for a class of RKHSs more general than the weighted Hardy spaces. Precisely, we can declare existence of solutions of the
n
-best kernel approximation for all RKHSs of holomorphic functions in
\bf D
that satisfy the following three conditions (see Theorem
2.1
):

(i) The reproducing kernel

enjoys

: When

is fixed,

is analytic for

and, when

is fixed,

is anti-analytic for

\overline{\bf D};

(ii) The kernel

satisfies
the infinite-norm-property at the boundary

(1.7)

\displaystyle\lim_{w\to\partial\bf D}\|K_{w}\|=\infty;

and,

(iii)

satisfies

the uniformly boundedness condition

(1.8)

\displaystyle\frac{|K_{w}(z)|}{\|K_{w}\|^{2}}\leq C_{\mathcal{H}},\quad w,z\in\bf D,

where

is a constant depending on the space.

2.
Main Theorem

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a RKHS of holomorphic functions in
\bf D
with reproducing kernel
K_{w},w\in\bf D,
satisfying the conditions (i),(ii),(iii) set in §1. Throughout the paper
n
is a fixed positive integer. Let
Z_{k}=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{k}),1\leq k\leq n,
be an ordered
k
-tuple of complex numbers in
\bf D
allowing multiplicity.

Denote by
l(a_{k})
the multiple of
a_{k}
in the
k
-tuple
(a_{1},\cdots,a_{k}),k\leq n.

Denote

(2.9)

\displaystyle\tilde{K}_{a_{k}}(z)=\left[\left(\frac{d}{d\overline{w}}\right)^{l(a_{k})-1}K_{w}(z)\right]_{w=a_{k}}.

We will call
\tilde{K}_{a_{k}}

the
multiple reproducing kernel corresponding to

(a_{1},\cdots,a_{k})
.
It is easy to show, for
f
being in the holomorphic function space, there holds

(2.10)

\displaystyle\langle f,\tilde{K}_{a_{k}}\rangle=f^{(l(a_{k})-1)}(a_{k}).

The consecutive derivatives of the kernel function correspond to repeating use of kernel parameters.

In the Hardy space case, for instance,
k_{a}(z)=\frac{1}{1-\overline{a}z},
and

(2.11)

\displaystyle\tilde{k}_{a_{k}}(z)=\left[\left(\frac{d}{d\overline{w}}\right)^{l(a_{k})-1}k_{w}(z)\right]_{w=a_{k}}=\frac{l!\overline{a}^{l}}{(1-\overline{a}z)^{l+1}}.

In general cases, let
(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})
be any
n
-tuple of complex numbers in
\bf D.

Denote by

(E_{a_{1}},\cdots,E_{a_{1}\cdots a_{m}})
the Grand-Schmidt orthonormalization of
(\tilde{K}_{a_{1}},\cdots,\tilde{K}_{a_{m}}),\ m=1,\cdots,n,
given by

(2.12)

\displaystyle E_{a_{1}\cdots a_{m}}(z)=\frac{\tilde{K}_{a_{m}}(z)-\sum_{l=1}^{m-1}\langle\tilde{K}_{a_{m}},E_{a_{1}\cdots a_{l}}\rangle E_{a_{1}\cdots a_{l}}(z)}{\sqrt{\|\tilde{K}_{a_{m}}\|^{2}-\sum_{l=1}^{m-1}|\langle\tilde{K}_{a_{m}},E_{a_{1}\cdots a_{l}}\rangle|^{2}}}.

We will denote the orthogonal projection of
f
into the linear subspace
X
by

P_{X}(f).
The projection into the orthogonal complement of
X
is denoted
Q_{X}=I-P_{X}.

In particular, denote by
P_{a_{1}\cdots a_{m}}
the orthogonal projection from
\mathcal{H}
to

{\rm span}\{\tilde{K}_{a_{1}},\cdots,\tilde{K}_{a_{m}}\},
and by
Q_{a_{1}\cdots a_{m}}=I-P_{a_{1}\cdots a_{m}},
the projection into the orthogonal complement subspace of
{\rm span}\{\tilde{K}_{a_{1}},\cdots,\tilde{K}_{a_{m}}\}.

It is recognized that
Q_{a_{1}a_{2}...a_{k}}
corresponds to the Gram-Schmidt process, precisely,

(2.13)

\displaystyle E_{a_{1}a_{2}...a_{k}}=\frac{Q_{a_{1}a_{2}...a_{k-1}}(\tilde{K}_{a_{k}})}{\|Q_{a_{1}a_{2}...a_{k-1}}(\tilde{K}_{a_{k}})\|}.

We have been using the notation
\{B_{a_{1}a_{2},...a_{k}}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
for the TM system in the Hardy space case. Now we use

\{E_{a_{1}a_{2}...a_{k}}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
for the Gram-Schmidt orthonormalization of the multiple reproducing kernels
\{\tilde{K}_{a_{k}}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
in
\mathcal{H}
given by (
2.12
) and (
2.13
). In the classical Hardy space case they are essentially the same, as, in fact,
E_{a_{1}a_{2}...a_{k}}=c_{k}B_{a_{1}a_{2}...a_{k}},
where
c_{k}
are unimodular constants,
k=1,\cdots,
(see Lemma
3.2
and the relevant references). None of the
E_{a_{1}a_{2}...a_{k}}
of any holomorphic Hilbert spaces other than the Hardy space seem to have such nice construction: the orthonormalization of the Szegö kernel
k_{a_{k}}
with respect to the span of
\{B_{a_{1}a_{2}...a_{j-1}}\}_{j=1}^{k-1},
which is
B_{a_{1}a_{2}...a_{k}},
is just the product of the added normalized Szegö kernel
e_{a_{k}}
with the the canonical Blaschke
\phi_{a_{1}a_{2}...a_{k-1}}.
This extraordinary property, together with the complex unimodular property of Blaschke products on the circle, as well as the equivalent norm property restricted to the circle, offer decisive conveniences in developing the Hardy space theory in contrast with that of the non-Hardy space cases. The
n
-best kernel approximation problem in the general context is formulated as follows. Let
f\in\mathcal{H}.
Whether there exist, and if yes, how to find computationally
a_{1},\cdots,a_{n},
all in
\bf D,
such that

(2.14)

\displaystyle d_{f}=d_{f}(n)\triangleq\|f-P_{a_{1}\cdots a_{n}}f\|=\inf\{\|f-P_{b_{1}\cdots b_{n}}f\|\ :\ (b_{1}\cdots b_{n})\in{\bf D}^{n}\}?

We will prove the following

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{H}
is a RKHS of holomorphic functions satisfying (i), (ii), and (iii), and
n
is a positive integer. Then there must hold one of the following two cases: (1)
f
is a linear combination of

\tilde{K}_{b_{1}},\cdots,\tilde{K}_{b_{m_{1}}}
for some
m_{1}
-tuple
(b_{1},\cdots,b_{m_{1}})\in{\bf D}^{m_{1}},m_{1}\leq n;
or (2) there exists an
n
-tuple
(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})\in{\bf D}^{n}
such that (
2.14
) holds for a positive infimum
d_{f}>0,
that is,

(2.15)

\displaystyle d_{f}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|f-\sum_{l=1}^{n}\langle f,E_{a_{1}\cdots a_{l}}\rangle E_{a_{1}\cdots a_{l}}\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\{\|f-\sum_{l=1}^{n}\langle f,E_{b_{1}\cdots b_{l}}\rangle E_{b_{1}\cdots b_{l}}\|\ :\ (b_{1},\cdots,b_{n})\in{\bf D}^{n}\}.

We note that
(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})\in{\bf D}^{n}
gives rise to
equality (
2.15
)
if and only if

(2.16)

Proof.

Remark 3.3
.

It is recognized that the operator
\frac{Q_{a}}{\phi_{a}}
is the generalized backward shift operator defined in
[
45
]
. Repeating use of the operator yields the reduced remainders
\frac{Q_{a_{1}\cdots a_{k}}f}{\prod_{j=1}^{k}\phi_{a_{j}}}.
The following lemma shows that the reduced remainders of a function bounded by
M
are still bounded with explicit bounds in terms of
M
and the parameters
a_{1},\cdots,a_{k}
involved. The result plays a crucial role in the proof of the main result of the paper.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
f
be an analytic function in an open neighbourhood of
\overline{\bf D}
and

|f(z)|\leq M

for some
M>0
on
\overline{\bf D}.

Then for any sequence
a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{k}\in\bf D
the reduced remainder functions

\frac{f_{a_{1}a_{2}...a_{k}}(z)}{\phi_{a_{1}a_{2}...a_{k}}(z)}

are analytic in an
open neighbourhood of
\overline{\bf D}
with the bounds over
\overline{\bf D}:

(3.21)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{f_{a_{1}a_{2}...a_{k}}(z)}{\phi_{a_{1}a_{2}...a_{k}}(z)}\right|\leq M(1+C_{\mathcal{H}})^{k},\quad z\in\overline{\bf D},

where
C_{\mathcal{H}}
is the constant in (
1.8
).

Proof.
For
a_{1}\in\bf D
and
z\in\partial\bf D,

\displaystyle|f_{a_{1}}(z)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|f(z)-\langle f,E_{a_{1}}\rangle E_{a_{1}}(z)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|f(z)|+|f(a_{1})|\frac{|K_{a_{1}}(z)|}{\|K_{a_{1}}\|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|f(z)|+|f(a_{1})|C_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M(1+C_{\mathcal{H}}).

Since the zero of
\phi_{a_{1}}
is a zero of
Q_{a_{1}}f,

\frac{Q_{a_{1}}f}{\phi_{a_{1}}}
is a holomorphic function in an open neighbourhood of
\overline{\bf D}.
The maximum modulus principle over
\overline{\bf D}
gives

\left|\frac{Q_{a_{1}}f(w)}{\phi_{a_{1}}(w)}\right|\leq\max\{|f_{a_{1}}(z)|\ :\ z\in{\partial\bf D}\}\leq M(1+C_{\mathcal{H}})

for all
w\in\overline{\bf D}.

By invoking the result of (iii), Lemma
3.2
, and repeating
k
times the above estimation for
\frac{Q_{a_{1}}f}{\phi_{a_{1}}},
we obtain the bounds claimed by the lemma.

\hfill\square

\displaystyle\max\{|g_{Z^{(l)}_{n-1}}(z)|\ :\ z\in\overline{\bf D}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\{|g_{Z^{(l)}_{n-1}}(\zeta)|\ :\ \zeta\in\partial{\bf D}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\{\left|f_{a^{(l)}_{1}\cdots a^{(l)}_{n-1}}({\rm e}^{it})\right|\ :\ t\in\partial{\bf D}\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M(1+C_{\mathcal{H}})^{n-1},

concluding the uniform boundedness claimed of
1^{o}.
The assertion
2^{o}
is a consequence of the condition (
1.7
) and Lemma
3.6
.

4.
Applications

4.1.
The classical Hardy space

By taking
\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{H}^{2}({\bf D}),
we are with the inner product (
1.1
), and
the reproducing kernel
K(z,w)=k_{w}(z)=\frac{1}{1-\overline{w}z}.

Since

K(a,a)=\frac{1}{1-|a|^{2}}\to\infty\ {\rm as}\ |a|\to 1,\ {\rm and}\ \frac{|K_{a}(z)|}{K(a,a)}=\frac{1-|a|^{2}}{|1-\overline{a}z|}\leq 2,

the conditions (
1.7
) and (
1.8
) are satisfied. We hence have existence of the
n
-best approximation.

4.2.
The Bergman spaces

Let
\mathcal{H}
be the weighted Bergman spaces with the definition and notation

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\alpha}({\bf D})=\{f:{\bf D}\to{\bf C}\ |\ f\ {\rm is\ holomorphic\ in\ \bf D},{\rm and}\ \|f\|_{\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\alpha}({\bf D})}^{2}=\int_{\bf D}|f(z)|^{2}dA_{\alpha}<\infty\},

where
\alpha\in(-1,\infty),dA_{\alpha}=(1+\alpha)(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}dA(z),
and
dA=\frac{dxdy}{\pi},z=x+iy,
is the normalized area measure of the unit disc. The inner product of
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\alpha}({\bf D})
is defined as

\langle f,g\rangle_{\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\bf D)}=\int_{\bf D}f(z)\overline{g(z)}dA_{\alpha}.

In the sequel we sometimes write
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\alpha}({\bf D})}
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\alpha}({\bf D})}
briefly as
\|\cdot\|
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle,
and

\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\alpha}({\bf D})
as
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\alpha}.

\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\alpha}
is a RKHS with reproducing kernel

k_{a}^{\alpha}(z)=\frac{1}{(1-\overline{a}z)^{2+\alpha}}.

By invoking the reproducing kernel property we have

(4.30)

\displaystyle\|k_{a}^{\alpha}\|^{2}=k_{a}^{\alpha}(a)=\frac{1}{(1-|a|^{2})^{2+\alpha}}.

This shows that the condition (
1.7
) holds for all
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\alpha}.

A simple computation gives

\frac{k_{a}^{\alpha}(z)}{{k_{a}^{\alpha}(a)}}=\frac{{(1-|a|^{2})^{2+\alpha}}}{(1-\overline{a}z)^{2+\alpha}}\leq 2^{2+\alpha}.

Hence, the reproducing kernel satisfies the condition (
1.8
). Therefore, an
n
-best approximation exists in all the weighted Bergman spaces. This is a re-proof of the main result of
[
52
]
.

4.3.
The weighted Hardy spaces

Let
W(k)
be a sequence of non-negative numbers satisfying
\lim_{k\to\infty}W(k)^{\frac{1}{k}}\geq 1
(
[
34
]
). Denote by
\mathbb{H}_{W}({\bf D})
the

W
-weighted Hardy
\mathbb{H}^{2}
-space defined by

\mathbb{H}_{W}({\bf D})=\{f\ :\ {\bf D}\to{\bf{C}}\ :\ f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k}z^{k},\ z\in{\bf D},\|f\|_{\mathbb{H}_{W}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}W(k)|c_{k}|^{2}<\infty\}.

We will be considering an ordered sequence of
W
-weighted Hardy spaces defined by the weights
W_{\beta}(k)=(1+k)^{\beta},-\infty<\beta<\infty.
This class of function spaces is a generalization of the Hardy and the weighted Bergman spaces. In fact,
\mathbb{H}_{W_{0}}({\bf D})=\mathbb{H}^{2}({\bf D}),
and

\mathbb{H}_{W_{\beta}}({\bf D})=\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\alpha}({\bf D}),\alpha=-\beta-1,\beta<0\ (\alpha>-1),

\mathbb{H}_{W_{-1}}({\bf D})
is the standard Bergman,
and
\mathbb{H}_{W_{1}}({\bf D})
is the Dirichlet space in
\bf D.
The spaces
\mathbb{H}_{W_{\beta}}({\bf D})
are, as a matter of fact, equivalent with the Hardy-Sobolev spaces
W^{\frac{\beta}{2},2}.
From the last two subsections we know that the spaces
\mathbb{H}_{W_{\beta}}({\bf D}),\beta\leq 0,
have
n
-best approximation. We now extend the result to
0<\beta\leq 1.

The inner product of
\mathbb{H}_{W_{\beta}}({\bf D}),-\infty<\beta<\infty,
is

\langle f,g\rangle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(k+1)^{\beta}c_{k}\overline{d}_{k},

where
c_{k}
and
d_{k}
are, respectively, the coefficients of the Taylor expansions of
f
and
g.
From this it can be directly verified that the reproducing kernel of
\mathbb{H}_{W_{\beta}}({\bf D})
is

k^{\beta}_{a}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(z\overline{a})^{k}}{(k+1)^{\beta}}.

The function

(4.31)

\displaystyle k^{\beta}_{a}(a)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{|a|^{2k}}{(k+1)^{\beta}}

is an increasing function in
|a|,
and for any large
N,

\underline{\lim}_{|a|\to 1-}k^{\beta}_{a}(a)\geq\lim_{|a|\to 1-}\sum_{k=0}^{N}\frac{|a|^{2k}}{(k+1)^{\beta}}=\sum_{k=0}^{N}\frac{1}{(k+1)^{\beta}}.

Therefore, for all
\beta\leq 1,

\lim_{|a|\to 1-}k^{\beta}_{a}(a)=\infty,

verifying (
1.7
). Next we show that the weighted Hardy spaces kernels
k^{\beta}_{a}
satisfy the condition (
1.8
). This requires to prove that the function

\frac{|k^{\beta}_{a}(z)|}{k^{\beta}_{a}(a)}
is uniformly bounded in
a,z\in{\bf D}.

The following estimation uses the well know technique for summing up series of positive decreasing entries:
If
f
is a positive decreasing function integrable over
(0,\infty),
then

\int_{1}^{\infty}f(t)dt\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}f(k)\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}f(t)dt.

The estimation amounts to numerically comparing some elementary integrals. Denote by
|a|=r<1.
Then

\displaystyle\frac{|k^{\beta}_{a}(z)|}{k^{\beta}_{a}(a)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\overline{a}z)^{k}}{(1+k)^{\beta}}|}{\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{|a|^{2k}}{(1+k)^{\beta}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{k}}{(1+k)^{\beta}}}{\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{2k}}{(1+k)^{\beta}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{x}}{(1+x)^{\beta}}dx}{\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{2x}}{(1+x)^{\beta}}dx}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{x}}{(1+x)^{\beta}}dx}{2^{\beta-1}\int_{2}^{\infty}\frac{r^{x}}{(2+x)^{\beta}}dx}\qquad({\rm change\ of\ variable})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{x}}{(1+x)^{\beta}}dx}{2^{\beta-1}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{x}}{(2+x)^{\beta}}dx-\int_{0}^{2}\frac{r^{x}}{(2+x)^{\beta}}dx\right)}\qquad(\frac{\infty}{\infty}\ {\rm type\ when\ }\ r\to 1-)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{x}}{(1+x)^{\beta}}dx}{2^{\beta-2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{x}}{(1+x)^{\beta}}\frac{(1+x)^{\beta}}{(2+x)^{\beta}}dx}\qquad({\rm if}\ r\geq\ {\rm some}\ r_{0}\in(0,1))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4.\qquad({\rm mean-value\ theorem\ of\ integration})

For
r\leq r_{0}
the estimated quantity also has a uniform bound. Hence, for
\beta\leq 1,
the spaces
\mathbb{H}_{W_{\beta}}({\bf D})
satisfy the three conditions (i), (ii), and (iii). By invoking Theorem
2.1
the
n
-best approximation problems have solutions in those spaces.

Remark 4.1
.

(For the spaces
\mathbb{H}_{W_{\beta}}({\bf D}),\beta>1
)
The spaces
\mathbb{H}_{W_{\beta}}({\bf D}),\beta>1,
do not fall into the category governed by Theorem
2.1
, as, owing to (
4.31
), the condition (
1.7
) is not satisfied. Recall the Sobolev Embedding theorem asserting that
W^{k,p}\subset C^{r,\alpha}
if
m<pk,\frac{1}{p}-\frac{k}{m}=-\frac{r+\alpha}{m},m
is the dimension. In our case
m=1,p=2,k=\frac{\beta}{2},
and, in particular,
\beta=pk>1.
It hence concludes that the functions in the spaces
\mathbb{H}_{W_{\beta}}({\bf D}),\beta>1,
are all continuously extendable to the closed unit disc, and the norm square
\|k^{\beta}_{a}\|^{2}=k^{\beta}_{a}(a)
given by (
4.31
) does not have singularity for
|a|=1.

Based on these, as well as continuity of the inner product, we conclude existence of
n
-best approximations of the spaces for
\beta>1.

The results of this section are summarized as

Theorem 4.2
.

For all Hardy-Sobolev spaces
\mathbb{H}_{W_{\beta}}({\bf D}),-\infty<\beta<\infty,
there exist solutions to the
n
-best kernel approximation problem.

5.
Stochastic
n
-best Approximation

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},d\mathbb{P})
be a probability space, and, as in the previous sections,
\mathcal{H}
be a RKHS of analytic functions in
\bf D
with reproducing kernel
K_{w},w\in\bf D.
We will be studying stochastic signals
f(z,{\xi}),
where
\xi\in\Omega
and
z\in\bf D:
We assume that for a.s.
\xi\in\Omega,f(\cdot,\xi)
is a function in
\mathcal{H};
and, for a.e.
z\in{\bf D},f(z,\cdot)
is a random variable. We will use the notation
f_{\xi}(z)=f(z,\xi).
Associated with the probability space and the RKHS we define a Bochner type space (
[
21
]
)

(5.32)

\displaystyle L^{2}(\mathcal{H},\Omega)=\{f:{\bf D}\times\Omega\to{\bf{C}}\ :\ \|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H},\Omega)}=E_{\xi}\|f_{\xi}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}<\infty\},

where
E_{\xi}
denotes the expectation, and precisely,

E_{\xi}\|f_{\xi}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}=\int_{\Omega}\|f_{\xi}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}d\mathbb{P}(\xi).

We often use the simplified notation
\mathcal{N}=L^{2}(\mathcal{H},\Omega).

The following theorem generalizes the existence result for stochastic
n
-best approximation for the Hardy space (see
[
51
]
) to the RKHSs satisfying the conditions (i), (ii), and (iii) as assumed in Theorem
2.1
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a RKHS of analytic functions in the unit disc satisfying the conditions (i), (ii), and (iii) in §1, and
\Omega
a probability space. Let
\mathcal{N}=L^{2}(\mathcal{H},\Omega)
be the associated Bochner type space as above defined. Let
f
be any non-zero random signal. Then for any positive integer
n,
either of the following two cases holds: (1) For some
1\leq m_{1}\leq n,
there exists an
m_{1}
-tuple of constant parameters
(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m_{1}})\in{\bf D}^{m_{1}}
such that
f
is identical with the orthogonal expansion

(5.33)

\displaystyle f(z,\xi)=\sum_{k=1}^{m_{1}}\langle f_{\xi},E_{k}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}E_{k}(z);

or (2) There exists an
n
-tuple of constant parameters
(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})\in{\bf D}^{n}
such that

(5.34)

\displaystyle\|f-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle f_{\xi},E_{k}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}E_{k}\|_{\mathcal{N}}

attains its positive infimum over all possible
n
-orthonormal systems
\{E_{k}\},

where in both cases,
\{E_{k}\}_{k=1}^{m}
is the orthonormal system generated by
(\tilde{K}_{a_{1}},\cdots,\tilde{K}_{a_{m}}),1\leq m\leq n.

The proof of Theorem 2.1 of
[
51
]
for the stochastic Hardy space case cannot be directly adopted, for, in the present case no density argument based on the boundary value of the given function on
\partial\bf D
is available. In §3 we established, by using a new technical method, the pointwise convergence result (
3.24
) which is crucial to in proving Theorem
5.1
.

Proof.

In the proof of Theorem
2.1
we already show that, for each
\xi
outside an event in
\Omega
of probability zero, there holds uniformly in
a^{(l)}_{1},a^{(l)}_{2},...,a^{(l)}_{n-1},l=1,2,\cdots,
that

\lim_{l\to\infty}|\langle f_{\xi},E_{a^{(l)}_{1}a^{(l)}_{2}...a^{(l)}_{n}}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}|^{2}=0.

By using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for the space
\mathcal{H}
we have a dominating function of the function sequence on the left hand side:

|\langle f_{\xi},E_{a^{(l)}_{1}a^{(l)}_{2}...a^{(l)}_{n}}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}|^{2}\leq\|f_{\xi}\|^{2}\in L^{1}(\Omega).

Then the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem be invoked to conclude

(5.35)

\displaystyle\lim_{l\to\infty}E_{\xi}|\langle f_{\xi},E_{a^{(l)}_{1}a^{(l)}_{2}...a^{(l)}_{n}}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}|^{2}=0

uniformly in
a^{(l)}_{1},a^{(l)}_{2},...,a^{(l)}_{n-1},l=1,2,\cdots.
Based on this the contradiction argument used in proving Theorem 2.1 of
[
51
]
may be adopted to conclude the theorem.
\hfill\square

Remark 5.2
.

(Impact to Algorithm)
This present paper only treats the existence aspect of the
n
-best problem. Based on the obtained estimates, however, a mathematical algorithm to actually get a solution is now on its way. We now cite the crucial step to reduce the problem to a global optimization one of a differential function defined in a compact set. Separate studies will be devoted to the computation aspect.
To have an
n
-best approximation algorithm we are under the assumption that the given function
f
is not expressible by any
m
-linear combination of multiple kernels for
m\leq n-1.
This implies that
d_{f}(n-1)>0,
and there exists an
n
-tuple
(b_{1},\cdots,b_{n})\in{\bf D}^{n}
such that for some
\epsilon>0

(5.36)

\displaystyle\|f-P_{b_{1}\cdots b_{n}}f\|=d_{f}(n-1)-\epsilon.

By using (
3.24
) one can find
\delta>0
such that if

|a_{n}|>1-\delta,
then

\|f-P_{a_{1}\cdots a_{n-1}a_{n}}f\|>d_{f}(n-1)-\epsilon

for any
a_{1},\cdots,a_{n-1}
in
\bf D.
Since
P_{a_{1}\cdots a_{n-1}a_{n}}f
is symmetric in
a_{1},\cdots,a_{n},
we conclude

\|f-P_{a_{1}\cdots a_{n-1}a_{n}}f\|>d_{f}(n-1)-\epsilon

whenever
|a_{k}|>1-\delta
for some
k=1,\cdots,n.

Owing to the observation (
5.36
) we have
d_{f}(n)\leq d_{f}(n-1)-\epsilon.
When the infimum attains at
(\tilde{a}_{1},\cdots,\tilde{a}_{n-1},\tilde{a}_{n}),
that is,

\|f-P_{\tilde{a}_{1}\cdots\tilde{a}_{n-1}\tilde{a}_{n}}f\|=d_{f}(n),

there holds
|\tilde{a}_{k}|\leq 1-\delta
for all
k=1,\cdots,n.

This concludes that the global minimum value
d_{f}(n)
is only attainable in the compact set
\overline{(1-\delta)\bf D}^{n}.
In view of the relation (
5.35
) the same conclusion holds for the stochastic case.

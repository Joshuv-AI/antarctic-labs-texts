Abstract

Introduction

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemann surface with unit area,
h
a smooth function on
M
which is positive somewhere
and
\rho>0
a parameter. In this paper, we consider the singular mean field equation

\displaystyle\Delta v+4\pi\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\alpha_{i}\left(1-\delta_{p_{i}}\right)=\rho\left(1-\frac{he^{v}}{\int_{M}he^{v}d\mu}\right),

(1.1)

where
\Delta
and
d\mu
are the Laplace-Beltrami operator and the area element of
(M,g)
respectively,
p_{i}\in M
are given distinct points,

\alpha_{i}\in(-1,+\infty)
and
\delta_{p_{i}}
denotes the Dirac measure with pole at
p_{i}
for
i=1,\cdots,\ell
.

Since (
1.1
) is invariant under adding a constant, one can assume
\int_{M}he^{v}d\mu=1
.
When
(M,g)
is the standard sphere with constant Gauss curvature
4\pi
(since we have assumed the area of
M
is unit)
and
\rho=8\pi+4\pi\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\alpha_{i}
, the metric
e^{v}g
has Gauss curvature

(4\pi+2\pi\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\alpha_{i})h
on

M\setminus\{p_{1},\cdots,p_{\ell}\}
and conical angle
2\pi(1+\alpha_{i})
at
p_{i}
for each
i=1,\cdots,\ell
. This is the singular version of
“Nirenberg problem”. We refer the reader to
[
34
,
35
,
9
,
25
,
36
,
13
,
6
,
14
,
11
,
10
,
44
,
19
]
for the development
of this topic.
Eq. (
1.1
) also appears in theoretical physics when people describes the models like Abelian Chern-Simons vortices, the reader could find this interpretation in

[
16
,
17
,
4
,
32
,
12
]
. For the study on Eq. (
1.1
) when
\rho\neq{\overline{\rho}}
, we refer the reader to
[
1
,
2
,
26
]
. Besides, we refer
three nice
survey papers
[
21
,
33
,
20
]
.

Let
G_{p}
be the Green function at
p
which satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Delta G_{p}=1-\delta_{p},\\
\int_{M}G_{p}d\mu=0.\end{cases}

(1.2)

In a normal coordinate system around
p
we assume that

\displaystyle G_{p}(x)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\log r+A(p)+\sigma(x),

where
r(x)=\text{dist}(x,p)
and
\sigma(x)=O(r)
as
r\to 0
.
By the change of variables

u=v+4\pi\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\alpha_{i}G_{p_{i}}:=v+h_{\ell},

we can transform Eq. (
1.1
) with
\int_{M}he^{v}d\mu=1
into

\displaystyle\Delta u=\rho\left(1-he^{-h_{\ell}}e^{u}\right).

(1.3)

To study the existence of Eq. (
1.3
), one likes to pursue a variational method. Namely, consider

\displaystyle J_{\rho}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}d\mu+\rho\int_{M}ud\mu

in the Hilbert space

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\left\{u\in H^{1}(M):~\int_{M}he^{u}e^{-h_{\ell}}d\mu=1\right\}.

Since
h
is positive somewhere,
\mathcal{H}\neq\emptyset
. By a simple calculation, one knows critical points of
J_{\rho}
in
\mathcal{H}
are solutions of (
1.3
).
Therefore, to solve Eq. (
1.3
), we can find critical points of
J_{\rho}
in
\mathcal{H}
. Let us recall the Moser-Trudinger inequality for surfaces with conical singularities, which has strong relationship with finding critical points for
J_{\rho}
. This inequality was firstly built by Troyanov
[
35
]
for any subcritical case and then sharpened by Chen
[
7
]
. Precisely, there exists a constant
C

which depends only on
(M,g)
, such that for any
u\in H^{1}(M)
with
\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}d\mu\leq 1
and
\int_{M}ue^{-h_{\ell}}d\mu=0
,

\displaystyle\int_{M}e^{4\pi(1+{\overline{\alpha}})u^{2}}e^{-h_{\ell}}d\mu\leq C,

(1.4)

where
{\overline{\alpha}}=\min\left\{0,\min_{1\leq i\leq\ell}{\alpha_{i}}\right\}
. We refer the reader to
[
42
]
for a more general version.
A direct consequence of (
1.4
) is

\displaystyle\log\int_{M}e^{u}e^{-h_{\ell}}d\mu\leq\frac{1}{16\pi(1+{\overline{\alpha}})}\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}d\mu+\int_{M}ue^{-h_{\ell}}d\mu+C.

(1.5)

To study
J_{\rho}
, a more convenient inequality compared with (
1.5
) was derived by the author
[
41
]
. In fact, we replaced the term
\int_{M}ue^{-h_{\ell}}d\mu
with
\int_{M}ud\mu
on the right-hand side of (
1.5
) and obtained

\displaystyle\log\int_{M}e^{u}e^{-h_{\ell}}d\mu\leq\frac{1}{16\pi(1+{\overline{\alpha}})}\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}d\mu+\int_{M}ud\mu+C.

(1.6)

Note that all the coefficients in (
1.4
)-(
1.6
) are sharp.
Based on this fact,
\rho=8\pi(1+{\overline{\alpha}}):={\overline{\rho}}
is the critical case in (
1.3
). When
\rho<{\overline{\rho}}
,
J_{\rho}
is coercive
in
\mathcal{H}
and hence it attains its infimum by the standard variational principle (c.f. for example, Theorem 1.2 in
[
29
]
). However, when
\rho={\overline{\rho}}
,
the situation becomes more subtle, one just knows
J_{\overline{\rho}}
is bounded from below in
\mathcal{H}
by (
1.6
).

When there is no singular source, that is
h_{\ell}=0,\,{\overline{\alpha}}=0
and
{\overline{\rho}}=8\pi
.
Kazdan and Warner
[
18
]
asked, under what kind of conditions on
h
, the equation

\displaystyle\Delta u=8\pi-8\pi he^{u}

(1.7)

has a solution. In the literal, people calls it as
Kazdan-Warner problem
.

In the celebrated paper
[
12
]
, Ding, Jost, Li and Wang firstly attacked Kazdan-Warner problem successfully.
If
h
is positive and
J_{8\pi}
has no minimum, they proved

\displaystyle\inf_{u\in H^{1}(M)}J_{8\pi}\geq-8\pi-8\pi\log\pi-8\pi\max_{p\in M}\left(4\pi A(p)+\log h(p)\right):=\Lambda_{8\pi}.

After that, they constructed a blowup sequence
\phi_{\epsilon}
and proved that
J_{8\pi}(\phi_{\epsilon})
is smaller than
\Lambda_{8\pi}
for sufficiently small
{\epsilon}>0

under the condition

\displaystyle\Delta\log h(p_{0})-2K(p_{0})+8\pi>0,

(1.8)

where
p_{0}
is the maximum point of
4\pi A(p)+\log h(p)
on
M
and
K
is the Gauss curvature of
(M,g)
. Therefore, under (
1.8
),
J_{8\pi}
has a minimum
and Eq. (
1.7
) has a solution. In the following, we call (
1.8
) as
Ding-Jost-Li-Wang condition
. Twenty years later,
Yang and the author

[
39
]
generalized this existence result to the case
h\geq 0,\,\not\equiv 0
, they mainly
excluded the situation that blowup happens at zero point of
h
. Recently, this result was generalized to the case which permits
h
changing signs.
This were done using variational method first by Sun and Zhu
[
31
]
and then by the author
[
43
]
with a different argument.
The other successful method to study Eq. (
1.7
)
is the flow method, we refer the reader to
[
5
]
,
[
22
]
,
[
30
]
,
[
37
]
and
[
23
]
.

In this paper, we shall follow Ding-Jost-Li-Wang’s method to study Eq. (
1.3
) when there are singular sources and in the critical case
\rho={\overline{\rho}}
.
We call it as the
singular Kazdan-Warner problem
.
Mainly, we remove the positivity restriction on
h
and just assume
h
is positive somewhere. Pioneer works were done by Chen-Li
[
9
]
when
M=S^{2}

and Mancini
[
27
]
for general surfaces and positive
h
.

We consider the perturbed functional
J_{\rho_{k}}
when
\rho_{k}\uparrow\bar{\rho}
strictly. Because of (
1.6
),
J_{\rho_{k}}
is coercive in the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. Then by Theorem 1.2 in

[
29
]
,
J_{\rho_{k}}
attains its infimum at some
u_{k}\in\mathcal{H}
. By a direct calculation, we have

\displaystyle\Delta u_{k}=\rho_{k}\left(1-he^{-h_{\ell}}e^{u_{k}}\right).

(1.9)

We define the
conical singularity
at
p\in M
as

\displaystyle\alpha(p)=\begin{cases}\alpha_{i},~~~&\text{if~}p=p_{i}~\text{for~some~}i=1,\cdots,\ell,\\
0,~~~&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Correspondingly, the
conical angle
at
p
is
2\pi(1+\alpha(p))
.
Now we are prepared to state our results.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemann surface with unit area. Denote
M_{+}=\{x\in M:h(x)>0\}
. If the minimizing sequence
u_{k}
of
J_{{\overline{\rho}}}
does not converge in
H^{1}(M)
, then

\displaystyle\inf_{H^{1}(M)}J_{{\overline{\rho}}}(u)=

\displaystyle-{\overline{\rho}}\left(1+\log\frac{\pi}{1+{\overline{\alpha}}}\right)

\displaystyle-{\overline{\rho}}\max_{p\in M_{+},\alpha(p)={\overline{\alpha}}}\left(4\pi A(p)+\log\left(h(p)\prod_{1\leq i\leq\ell,p_{i}\neq p}e^{-4\pi\alpha_{i}G_{p_{i}}(p)}\right)\right).

Concerning the existence result, we have

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemann surface with unit area. Denote
M_{+}=\{x\in M:h(x)>0\}
.
Suppose
h\in C^{\infty}(M)
is positive somewhere. Then Eq. (
1.3
) has a solution provided one of the following conditions holds:

(i)

\{p\in M_{+}:\alpha(p)={\overline{\alpha}}\}=\emptyset
;

(ii)

\{p\in M_{+}:\alpha(p)={\overline{\alpha}}\}\neq\emptyset
and

\displaystyle\inf_{\mathcal{H}}J_{\overline{\rho}}<

\displaystyle-{\overline{\rho}}\left(1+\log\frac{\pi}{1+{\overline{\alpha}}}\right)

\displaystyle-{\overline{\rho}}\max_{p\in M_{+},\alpha(p)={\overline{\alpha}}}\left(4\pi A(p)+\log\left(h(p)\prod_{1\leq i\leq\ell,p_{i}\neq p}e^{-4\pi\alpha_{i}G_{p_{i}}(p)}\right)\right)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\Lambda_{{\overline{\rho}}}.

We derive the explicit lower bound of

when

Some lemmas

In this section, we would like to present some useful results which will be used in the next section, where we will calculate the explicit lower bound of
J_{\rho_{k}}

when
(u_{k})
blows up.

Lemma 2.1

(Theorem 2.1,
[
9
]
)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemann surface with unit area,
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
be two subsets of
M
such that
dist(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2})\geq\epsilon_{0}>0
. Assume

\gamma_{0}\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
is a fixed number. Then for any
\epsilon>0
, there is a constant
C=C(\epsilon_{0},\gamma_{0},\epsilon)
such that the inequality

\displaystyle\log\int_{M}e^{-h_{\ell}}e^{u}d\mu\leq\left(\frac{1}{32\pi(1+{\overline{\alpha}})}+\epsilon\right)\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}d\mu+\int_{M}ud\mu+C

holds for all
u\in H^{1}(M)
satisfying

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{\Omega_{1}}e^{-h_{\ell}}e^{u}d\mu}{\int_{M}e^{-h_{\ell}}e^{u}d\mu}\geq\gamma_{0},~~\frac{\int_{\Omega_{2}}e^{-h_{\ell}}e^{u}d\mu}{\int_{M}e^{-h_{\ell}}e^{u}d\mu}\geq\gamma_{0}.

The reader can follow Chen-Li’s proof effortlessly, we omit it here.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose
u_{k}
attains the infimum of
J_{\rho_{k}}
in
\mathcal{H}
. Then there exist two positive constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
such that

\displaystyle c_{1}\leq\int_{M}e^{-h_{\ell}}e^{u_{k}}d\mu\leq c_{2}.

Proof.

Since
u_{k}\in\mathcal{H}
, one can choose
c_{1}=1/\max_{M}h
. As to the upper bound, notice

J_{\rho_{k}}(u_{k})=\inf_{\mathcal{H}}J_{\rho_{k}}(u)\leq C,

then the Moser-Trudinger inequality (
1.6
) and Jensen’s inequality yield that

\displaystyle\log\int_{M}e^{-h_{\ell}}e^{u_{k}}d\mu\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\overline{\rho}}J_{\rho_{k}}(u_{k})+(1-\frac{\rho_{k}}{\overline{\rho}})\int_{M}u_{k}d\mu+C

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-\frac{\rho_{k}}{\overline{\rho}})\int_{M}(-h_{\ell}+u_{k})d\mu+(1-\frac{\rho_{k}}{\overline{\rho}})\int_{M}h_{\ell}d\mu+C

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-\frac{\rho_{k}}{\overline{\rho}})\log\left(\int_{M}e^{-h_{\ell}}e^{u_{k}}d\mu\right)+C,

where in the last inequality we have used the fact that
h_{\ell}\in L^{1}(M)
. Now we finish the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

If
(u_{k})
blows up, then for every
q\in(1,2)
, it holds that

\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{L^{q}(M)}\leq C.

Proof.

Let
q^{\prime}=\frac{q}{q-1}>2
. By definition, we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{L^{q}(M)}\leq\sup\left\{\left|\int_{M}\nabla u_{k}\cdot\nabla\varphi d\mu\right|:\varphi\in W^{1,q^{\prime}}(M),\int_{M}\varphi d\mu=0,\|\varphi\|_{W^{1,q^{\prime}}(M)}=1\right\}.

It follows form the Sobolev embedding theorem that

\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}\leq C.

Then by equation (
1.9
) and Lemma
2.2
we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\int_{M}\nabla u_{k}\cdot\nabla\varphi d\mu\right|

\displaystyle=\left|-\int_{M}\Delta u_{k}\varphi d\mu\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{M}\rho_{k}\left(he^{-h_{\ell}}e^{u_{k}}-1\right)\varphi d\mu\right|

\displaystyle\leq\overline{\rho}\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}\left(\max_{M}|h|\int_{M}e^{-h_{\ell}}e^{u_{k}}d\mu+1\right)

\displaystyle\leq C.

This gives the proof of the lemma.
∎

Denote
\overline{u_{k}}=\int_{M}u_{k}d\mu
,
\lambda_{k}=\max\limits_{M}u_{k}
. Assume
u_{k}(x_{k})=\lambda_{k}
for some
x_{k}\in M
and

x_{k}\to p\in M
as
k\to\infty
.

Lemma 2.4
.

The following three conditions are equivalent:

(i)

\lambda_{k}\to+\infty
as
k\to\infty
;

(ii)

\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{L^{2}(M)}\to+\infty
as
k\to\infty
;

(iii)

\overline{u_{k}}\to-\infty
as
k\to\infty
.

Proof.

Since
J_{\rho_{k}}(u_{k})
is bounded, we have
(ii)\Leftrightarrow(iii)
obviously.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
: Suppose not,
\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{L^{2}(M)}\leq C
, then by the proved equivalent relation
(ii)\Leftrightarrow(iii)
,
\overline{u_{k}}

is bounded from below. It follows by Jensen’s inequality and Lemma
2.2
that

\displaystyle\overline{u_{k}}-\overline{h_{\ell}}\leq\log\left(\int_{M}e^{-h_{\ell}+u_{k}}d\mu\right)\leq C,

this together with the fact
h_{\ell}\in L^{1}(M)
yields

\overline{u_{k}}\leq C
.
By Poincaré’s inequality,

\displaystyle\int_{M}u_{k}^{2}d\mu-\overline{u_{k}}^{2}=\int_{M}(u_{k}-\overline{u_{k}})^{2}d\mu\leq C\int_{M}|\nabla u_{k}|^{2}d\mu\leq C.

So
(u_{k})
is bounded in
L^{2}(M)
and therefore it is in
W^{2,2}(M)
. Then elliptic estimates tell us that
\|u_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}
is bounded.
Of course, we also have
\lambda_{k}\leq C
.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
: Suppose not,
\lambda_{k}\leq C
, then
e^{u_{k}}
is bounded.
It is clear that
he^{-h_{\ell}}
is bounded in
L^{s}(M)
for some
s>1
(
s=\infty
if
\overline{\alpha}=0

and
s<-1/\overline{\alpha}
if
\overline{\alpha}<0
). By Lemma
2.3
,
\|u_{k}-\overline{u_{k}}\|_{L^{s_{1}}(M)}
for any
s_{1}>1
. Since
\Delta(u_{k}-\overline{u_{k}})

is bounded in
L^{s}(M)
, it follows from the elliptic estimates that
u_{k}-\overline{u_{k}}
is bounded in
L^{\infty}(M)
. This together with
\overline{u_{k}}\to-\infty

yields that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{M}he^{-h_{\ell}}e^{u_{k}}d\mu=\lim_{k\to\infty}e^{\overline{u_{k}}}\int_{M}he^{-h_{\ell}}e^{u_{k}-\overline{u_{k}}}d\mu=0.

It contradicts
u_{k}\in\mathcal{H}
. This finishes the proof.
∎

By Brezis-Merle’s lemma (
[
3
]
, Theorem 1) and following elliptic estimates as the proof of Lemma 2.8 in
[
12
]
(or Lemma 2.9 in
[
41
]
), one has

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset M
be a domain. If

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|h|e^{-h_{\ell}+u_{k}}d\mu\leq\frac{1}{2(1+\overline{\alpha})}-\delta

for some
0<\delta<\frac{1}{2(1+\overline{\alpha})}
, then

\displaystyle\|u_{k}-\overline{u_{k}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega)}\leq C.

Due to Lemma
2.5
, we define the
blowup set
of
(u_{k})
as

\displaystyle S=\left\{x\in M:\lim_{r\to 0}\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{B_{r}(x)}|h|e^{-h_{\ell}+u_{k}}d\mu\geq\frac{1}{2(1+\overline{\alpha})}\right\}.

(2.1)

The following observation is very important to us. Even though
h
may change signs, the blowup set is still a single point set at most. It breaks the
surmise that when
h
changes signs, it may has two or more blowup points, they concentrate at different points where
h
may be negative and positive,
but cancel each other.

Lemma 2.6
.

S=\{p\}
.

Proof.

We divide the whole proof into three parts: (1)
S\neq\emptyset
; (2)
\#S=1
; (3)
S=\{p\}
. In the following, we shall prove these three parts on by one.

(1)
S\neq\emptyset
. Suppose not, for every
x\in M
, there exists a positive number
r_{x}
which is smaller then the injective radius of
M
, such that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r_{x}}(x)}|h|e^{-h_{\ell}+u_{k}}d\mu<\frac{1}{2(1+\overline{\alpha})}.

Then by Lemma
2.5
we obtain that

\displaystyle\|u_{k}-\overline{u_{k}}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r_{x}/2}(x))}\leq C.

This combining with a finite covering argument tells us that

\|u_{k}-\overline{u_{k}}\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}\leq C.

Since
(u_{k})
blows up, we have
\overline{u_{k}}\to-\infty
by Lemma
2.4
and then we have
u_{k}\to-\infty
as
k\to\infty
. This contradicts with Lemma
2.2
.

(2)
\#S=1
. Suppose not, we must have
\#S\geq 2
since we have proved that
S\neq\emptyset
. Suppose
x_{1}\neq x_{2}\in S
, then for sufficiently small

, one has

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{B_{r}(x_{i})}e^{-h_{\ell}+u_{k}}d\mu}{\int_{M}e^{-h_{\ell}+u_{k}}d\mu}\geq\frac{1}{2(1+\overline{\alpha})c_{2}\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}},~~i=1,2.

Then by Lemma
2.1
we have for
\epsilon=\frac{1}{8\overline{\rho}}
, there exists a constant
C
such that

\displaystyle\log\int_{M}e^{-h_{\ell}+u_{k}}d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{8\overline{\rho}}\int_{M}|\nabla u_{k}|^{2}d\mu+\overline{u_{k}}+C

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{4\overline{\rho}}J_{\rho_{k}}(u_{k})+\left(1-\frac{3\rho_{k}}{4\overline{\rho}}\right)\overline{u_{k}}+C

\displaystyle\to-\infty~~\text{as}~k\to\infty,

(2.2)

where we have used facts
J_{\rho_{k}}(u_{k})
is bounded and
\overline{u_{k}}\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
. (
2
) contradicts Lemma
2.2
.
Therefore,
\#S=1
.

(3)
S=\{p\}
. Let us recall that
u_{k}(x_{k})=\max\limits_{M}u_{k}
and
x_{k}\to p
as
k\to\infty
. If
p\notin S
, then by (

) (the definition of

)
there exist

and

such that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}(p)}|h|e^{-h_{\ell}+u_{k}}d\mu<\frac{1}{2(1+\overline{\alpha})}-\delta.

Then Lemma
2.5
yields that

\displaystyle\|u_{k}-\overline{u_{k}}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r/2}(p))}\leq C.

So we have by Lemma
2.4
that

\displaystyle u_{k}(x_{k})\leq\overline{u_{k}}+C\to-\infty~~\text{as}~k\to\infty,

this contradicts
u_{k}(x_{k})=\lambda_{k}\to+\infty
as
k\to\infty
. Therefore,
S=\{p\}
.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

If
(u_{k})
blows up, then

must be positive at the single blowup point, i.e.,

Proof.

It follows from Lemmas

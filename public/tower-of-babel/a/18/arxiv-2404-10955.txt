The Traveling Tournament Problem: Improved Algorithms Based on Cycle Packing
Jingyang Zhao, Mingyu Xiao, Chao Xu

Abstract
The Traveling Tournament Problem (TTP) is a well-known benchmark problem in the field of tournament timetabling, which asks us to design a double round-robin schedule such that each pair of teams plays one game in each other’s home venue, minimizing the total distance traveled by all n n teams ( n n is even). TTP- k k is the problem with one more constraint that each team can have at most k k -consecutive home games or away games. In this paper, we investigate schedules for TTP- k k and analyze the approximation ratio of the solutions. Most previous schedules were constructed based on a Hamiltonian cycle of the graph. We will propose a novel construction based on a k k -cycle packing. Then, combining our k k -cycle packing schedule with the Hamiltonian cycle schedule, we obtain improved approximation ratios for TTP- k k with deep analysis. The case where k = 3 k=3 , TTP-3, is one of the most investigated cases. We improve the approximation ratio of TTP-3 from ( 1.667 + ε ) (1.667+\varepsilon) to ( 1.598 + ε ) (1.598+\varepsilon) , for any ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 . For TTP- 4 4 , we improve the approximation ratio from ( 1.750 + ε ) (1.750+\varepsilon) to ( 1.700 + ε ) (1.700+\varepsilon) . By a refined analysis of the Hamiltonian cycle construction, we also improve the approximation ratio of TTP- k k from ( 5 ​ k − 7 2 ​ k + ε ) (\frac{5k-7}{2k}+\varepsilon) to ( 5 ​ k 2 − 4 ​ k + 3 2 ​ k ​ ( k + 1 ) + ε ) (\frac{5k^{2}-4k+3}{2k(k+1)}+\varepsilon) for any constant k ≥ 5 k\geq 5 . Our methods can be extended to solve a variant called LDTTP- k k (TTP- k k where all teams are allocated on a straight line). We show that the k k -cycle packing construction can achieve an approximation ratio of ( 3 ​ k − 3 2 ​ k − 1 + ε ) (\frac{3k-3}{2k-1}+\varepsilon) , which improves the approximation ratio of LDTTP-3 from 4 / 3 4/3 to ( 6 / 5 + ε ) (6/5+\varepsilon) .

Introduction

In the field of the tournament schedule, the traveling tournament problem (TTP) is a widely known benchmark problem that was first systematically introduced in
Easton et al. (2001)
.
This problem aims to find a double round-robin tournament satisfying some constraints, minimizing the total distance traveled by all participant teams.
In a double round-robin tournament of
n
teams, each team will play 2 games against each of the other
n-1
teams, one at its home venue and one at its opponent’s home venue.
Additionally, each team should play one game a day, all games need to be scheduled on
2(n-1)
-consecutive days, and so there are exactly
n/2
games on each day.
According to the definition, we know that
n
is always even.
For TTP, we have the following two basic constraints or assumptions on the double round-robin tournament.

Traveling Tournament Problem (TTP)

•

No-repeat
: Two teams cannot play against each other on two consecutive days.

•

Direct-traveling
: Before the first game, all teams are at home, and they will return home after the last game. Furthermore, a team travels directly from its game venue on
i
th day to its game venue on
(i+1)
th day.

TTP-
k
, a well-known variant of TTP, is to add the following constraint on the maximum number of consecutive home games and away games.

•

Bounded-by-
k
: Each team can have at most
k
-consecutive home games or away games.

The smaller the value of
k
, the more frequently a team has to return home. By contrast, if
k
is very large, say
k=n-1
, then this constraint loses meaning, and TTP-
k
becomes TTP where a team can schedule their travel distance as short as that in the traveling salesman problem (TSP).

A weight function
w
on the complete undirected graph is called a
metric
if it satisfies the symmetry and triangle inequality
properties:
w(a,b)=w(b,a)
and
w(a,c)\leq w(a,b)+w(b,c)
for all
a,b,c\in V
.

The input of TTP-
k
contains a complete graph where each vertex is a team, and the weight between two vertices
u
and
v
is the distance from the home of team
u
to the home of team
v
. In this paper, we only consider the case when the weight function
w
is a metric.

1.2
Our Contributions

In this paper, we consider approximation algorithms for TTP-
k
with constant
k\geq 3
.
Our contributions are summarized as follows.

Firstly, we deeply analyze the structural properties, which leads us to strengthen some of the current-known lower bounds and also to propose several new lower bounds.

Secondly, we consider the constructions of feasible schedules for TTP-
k
.
Our algorithm will use two constructions.
The first construction is based on a Hamiltonian cycle, which is a known method. The Hamiltonian cycle used in the literature is usually generated by the Christofides-Serdyukov algorithm
Christofides (1976)
;
Serdyukov (1978)
, while we propose a new Hamiltonian cycle that uses the minimum weight perfect matching.
The second construction is newly proposed by us, which is based on a
k
-cycle packing.
To get our claimed approximation ratios, we will make a trade-off between the two constructions and do careful analysis.

Thirdly, based on careful analysis, we improve the approximation ratio from
(5/3+\varepsilon)
to
(139/87+\varepsilon)
for TTP-3, and from
(7/4+\varepsilon)
to
(17/10+\varepsilon)
for TTP-4.

Fourthly, we consider TTP-
k
with
k\geq 5
. By a refined analysis of the Hamiltonian construction, we improve the approximation ratio from
(\frac{5k-7}{2k}+\varepsilon)
to
(\frac{5k^{2}-4k+3}{2k(k+1)}+\varepsilon)
.

Last, we show that the construction based on the
k
-cycle packing can also be used to improve Linear Distance TTP-
k
(LDTTP-
k
), which is a special case of TTP-
k
where all teams are on a line. We prove an approximation ratio of
(\frac{3k-3}{2k-1}+\varepsilon)
for this problem, which improves the approximation ratio from
4/3

Hoshino and
Kawarabayashi (2012)
to
(6/5+\varepsilon)
for the case of
k=3
.

The Independent Lower Bounds

For TTP and TTP-
k
, a well-known method to obtain the lower bounds is to use an independent relaxation
Campbell and Chen (1976)
;
Easton et al. (2001)
.
The basic idea is to obtain a lower bound on the traveling distance of each team independently without considering the feasibility of other teams and then sum them together.
Although there exist many independent lower bounds for TTP-
k

Miyashiro
et al. (2012)
;
Yamaguchi
et al. (2011)
;
Westphal and Noparlik (2014)
, we cannot use them directly to get our results. We are interested in a stronger bound.
Before we make some observations on the independent lower bounds, we first need to explore some properties of TTP-
k
.

For TTP-
k
, each team needs to visit each other team’s home once in the tournament.
A
road trip
of a team
v
is a simple cycle starting and ending at
v
.
An
itinerary
of team
v\in V
on
G
is a connected subgraph of
G
that consists of road trips with each simple cycle of length at most
k+1
, and each vertex in
V
other than
v
has degree
2
.
An itinerary is
optimal
for a team if it is the itinerary of minimum weight.
Considering an optimal itinerary
I_{v}
for team
v
, we use
\psi_{v}=w(I_{v})
to denote the weight of the optimal itinerary for team
v
. Then,
\psi=\sum_{v\in V}\psi_{v}
is a simple independent lower bound for the minimum weight solution of TTP-
k
. Note that this lower bound was also used in the experiment
Easton et al. (2001)
. However, it is NP-hard to compute
\psi_{v}
since it involves solving the
k
-tour problem and this problem is NP-hard for any fixed
k\geq 3

Asano
et al. (1996)
. Hence, we want to find an alternative lower bound for each team’s optimal itinerary.

When
n\bmod{k}=0
, we can prove that there always exists an optimal itinerary with no 2-cycles.

Lemma 1
.

For TTP-
k
with
k\geq 3
, when
n\bmod{k}=0
, there exists an optimal itinerary with no 2-cycles for each team.

Proof.

Consider an optimal itinerary
I_{v}
of team
v
with the minimum number of 2-cycles. Assume
I_{v}
contains a 2-cycle. Since all cycles share the vertex
v
,
by the triangle inequality, we can get a
(k^{\prime}+1)
-cycle by shortcutting one 2-cycle and one
k^{\prime}
-cycle, and the
(k^{\prime}+1)
-cycle has a weight of no greater than the sum of the weights of the two cycles.
This shows that
I_{v}
contains no
k^{\prime}
-cycles (
3\leq k^{\prime}\leq k
) and at most one
2
-cycle if it contains one 2-cycle.
Thus,
I_{v}
contains only one 2-cycle, and the rest cycles are all
(k+1)
-cycles. We get
n\bmod k=2
, a contradiction to
n\bmod k=0
.
∎

The number of vertices in graph
H
is
n_{S}=2mk
, which is divisible by
k
. We may consider an optimal itinerary of a team
v\in S

I^{H}_{v}

I_{v}
is an optimal itinerary of the team

By the triangle inequality, it is easy to see that

.
Next, we consider several lower bounds on

By Lemma

consists of all vertices in

except
v
. Our results are mostly simple counting arguments.

Lemma 4
.

It holds that
(\frac{k-1}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{v}+\alpha_{v}\gamma_{v})w(I^{H}_{v})\geq(\frac{k-2}{2}+\alpha_{v})\gamma_{v}w(I^{H}_{v})+(\frac{k-3}{2}+\beta_{v})(1-\gamma_{v})w(I^{H}_{v})\geq\deg_{H}(v)
.

Proof.

By the definition of
\beta_{v}
, we have
1\geq\beta_{v}
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{k-1}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{v}+\alpha_{v}\gamma_{v}\right)w(I^{H}_{v})

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{k-2}{2}+\alpha_{v}\right)\gamma_{v}w(I^{H}_{v})+\left(\frac{k-3}{2}+1\right)(1-\gamma_{v})w(I^{H}_{v})

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{k-2}{2}+\alpha_{v}\right)\gamma_{v}w(I^{H}_{v})+\left(\frac{k-3}{2}+\beta_{v}\right)(1-\gamma_{v})w(I^{H}_{v}).

The left inequality holds.
Now we consider the right inequality.

First, we can see
\deg_{H}(v)=\sum_{u\in S_{v,1}}w(u,v)+\sum_{u\in S_{v,2}}w(u,v)
.

Next, recall that
\alpha_{v}\gamma_{v}w(I^{H}_{v})=w(I^{H}_{v,1}\cap\delta_{H}(v))
is the total weight of all home-edges in
(k+1)
-cycles.
For any
(k+1)
-cycle
C=(v,v_{1},\dots,v_{k},v)
in
I^{H}_{v,1}
, note that the edges
(v,v_{1})
and
(v,v_{k})
are home-edges which can be counted by
w(I^{H}_{v,1}\cap\delta_{H}(v))
, but the edges
(v,v_{i})
with
2\leq i\leq k-1
are not home-edges.
By the triangle inequality, we know that
w(v,v_{i})\leq\frac{1}{2}w(C)
. Therefore, the weight of the uncounted edges are at most
\frac{k-2}{2}w(C)
. Thus, we have

\sum_{u\in S_{v,1}}w(u,v)\leq\left(\frac{k-2}{2}+\alpha_{v}\right)\gamma_{v}w(I^{H}_{v}).

Similarly,
\beta_{v}(1-\gamma_{v})w(I^{H}_{v})=w(I^{H}_{v,2}\cap\delta_{H}(v))
is the weight of all home-edges in
k^{\prime}
-cycles. For any
k^{\prime}
-cycle
C=(v,v_{1},\dots,v_{k^{\prime}-1},v)
in
I^{H}_{v,2}
, the home-edges
(v,v_{1})
and
(v,v_{k^{\prime}-1})
can be counted by
w(I^{H}_{v,2}\cap\delta_{H}(v))
.
The weight of the uncounted edges are at most
\frac{k^{\prime}-3}{2}w(C)\leq\frac{k-3}{2}w(C)
.
Thus, we have

\sum_{u\in S_{v,2}}w(u,v)\leq\left(\frac{k-3}{2}+\beta_{v}\right)\gamma_{v}w(I^{H}_{v}).

Then, we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{k-2}{2}+\alpha_{v}\right)\gamma_{v}w(I^{H}_{v})+\left(\frac{k-3}{2}+\beta_{v}\right)(1-\gamma_{v})w(I^{H}_{v})

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{u\in S_{v,1}}w(u,v)+\sum_{u\in S_{v,2}}w(u,v)=\deg_{H}(v).

∎

Lemma 5
.

It holds that
(1-\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{v})\gamma_{v}w(I^{H}_{v})+(1-\frac{1}{2}\beta_{v})(1-\gamma_{v})w(I^{H}_{v})\geq\mbox{MST}(H)
.

Proof.

In the optimal itinerary of
v
on
H
, the total weight of all home-edges is

\alpha_{v}\gamma_{v}w(I^{H}_{v})+\beta_{v}(1-\gamma_{v})w(I^{H}_{v}).

For each cycle, there are exactly two home-edges. We can delete the longer edge from each cycle from
I^{H}_{v}
,
and get a spanning tree with a total weight of at most

\left(1-\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{v}\right)\gamma_{v}w(I^{H}_{v})+\left(1-\frac{1}{2}\beta_{v}\right)(1-\gamma_{v})w(I^{H}_{v}).

Since the weight of a minimum spanning tree in graph
H
is
\mbox{MST}(H)
, we have that

\left(1-\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{v}\right)\gamma_{v}w(I^{H}_{v})+\left(1-\frac{1}{2}\beta_{v}\right)(1-\gamma_{v})w(I^{H}_{v})\geq\mbox{MST}(H).

The lemma holds.
∎

The new bounds in Lemmas

and

are stronger than that in

et al. (2011)

k

Lemma 6
.

Let
M_{H}
be a minimum weight perfect matching in graph
H
. For TTP-3, we have that
\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{v}w(I^{H}_{v})+(1-\beta_{v})(1-\gamma_{v})w(I^{H}_{v})\geq w(M_{H})
.

Proof.

We assume that
k=3
and consider TTP-3. Note that
I^{H}_{v,1}
is a set of 4-cycles and
I^{H}_{v,2}
is a set of 3-cycles.
Since
n_{S}=2mk=6m
, we know that the number of vertices in
S_{v,1}
is odd but even in
S_{v,2}
. Since any pair of 3-cycles only share one common vertex
v
, after we delete both of home-edges of each 3-cycle, we can get a perfect matching
M^{\prime}_{H}
in graph
H[S_{v,2}]
with a total weight of
(1-\beta_{v})(1-\gamma_{v})w(I^{H}_{v})
.

Then, by a similar argument in the proof of Lemma
2
, we know that
\gamma_{v}w(I^{H}_{v})=w(I^{H}_{v,1})
is greater than the weight of the minimum weight Hamiltonian cycle in graph
H[S_{v,1}\cup\{v\}]
. Since the number of vertices in this graph is even, any Hamiltonian cycle in this graph can be decomposed into two perfect matchings. Thus, we can get a perfect matching
M^{\prime\prime}_{H}
in this graph with a total weight of at most
\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{v}w(I^{H}_{v})
. Therefore,
M^{\prime}_{H}\cup M^{\prime\prime}_{H}
is a perfect matching in graph
H
, and then we have
w(M_{H})\leq w(M^{\prime}_{H}\cup M^{\prime\prime}_{H})\leq\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{v}w(I^{H}_{v})+(1-\beta_{v})(1-\gamma_{v})w(I^{H}_{v})
.
∎

Lemma 7
.

For graph
H
, let
\mathcal{P}^{*}_{k}
be a minimum weight
k
-path packing, and
\mathcal{C}^{*}_{k}
be a minimum weight
k
-cycle packing. It holds that
\left(\frac{k-1}{k}+\frac{1}{k}\gamma_{v}-\alpha_{v}\gamma_{v}\right)w(I^{H}_{v})=(1-\alpha_{v})\gamma_{v}w(I^{H}_{v})+\frac{k-1}{k}(1-\gamma_{v})w(I^{H}_{v})\geq w(\mathcal{P}^{*}_{k})\geq\frac{1}{2}w(\mathcal{C}^{*}_{k})
.

Proof.

First, we consider
w(\mathcal{P}^{*}_{k})\geq\frac{1}{2}w(\mathcal{C}^{*}_{k})
.
Let
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{k}
be the

-path packing.
For any

\mathcal{P}^{*}_{k}
, saying

, we obtain

by the triangle inequality. This shows
2w(\mathcal{P}^{*}_{k})\geq w(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{k})\geq w(\mathcal{C}^{*}_{k})
, and we are done.

We note that the number of vertices is divisible by
k
in
S_{v,1}
but not in
S_{v,2}
. Since any pair of
(k+1)
-cycles only share one common vertex
v
, after we delete both of home-edges for each
(k+1)

k
-path packing
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{k}
in graph

such that
w(\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{k})=(1-\alpha_{v})\gamma_{v}w(I^{H}_{v})
.

Then, by a similar argument in the proof of Lemma
2
, we know that
(1-\gamma_{v})w(I^{H}_{v})=w(I^{H}_{v,2})\geq w(C)
where

is the minimum weight Hamiltonian cycle in graph

. Since the number of vertices in this graph equals

\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime}_{k}

is a

Lemma 8

, let

\min\{2(1-\alpha_{v}),1\}\cdot\gamma_{v}w(I^{H}_{v})+(1-\gamma_{v})w(I^{H}_{v})\geq w(\mathcal{C}^{*}_{\bmod k})

Lemma 10
.

Let
C_{H}
be a minimum weight Hamiltonian cycle,
\mathcal{C}^{*}_{\bmod k}
is a minimum weight mod

-cycle packing, and

be a minimum weight perfect matching in graph

. For TTP-
k

. Moreover, if

Since the number of vertices in

can be decomposed into two perfect matchings. The one with a smaller weight has a weight of at most

3.2

Now, we are ready to consider the independent lower bounds, which are found by summing the individual optimal itineraries of teams in
S
on graph
H
.

For each team
v\in S
,
I_{v}
(resp.,
I^{H}_{v}
) is the optimal itinerary of team
v
on graph
G
(resp.,
H

Recall that
\psi=\sum_{v\in V}\psi_{v}
and
\psi_{v}=w(I_{v})
. We define

\psi^{H}=\sum_{v\in S}\psi^{H}_{v}\mbox{~~and~~}\psi^{H}_{v}=w(I^{H}_{v}).

Then, we can get

\psi=\sum_{v\in V}w(I_{v})\geq\sum_{v\in S}w(I_{v})\geq\sum_{v\in S}w(I^{H}_{v})=\psi^{H},

i.e.,
\psi^{H}
is a lower bound of TTP-
k
.

Define
\gamma
,
\alpha
and
\beta
such that it holds

\gamma\psi^{H}=\sum_{v\in S}w(I^{H}_{v,1})=\sum_{v\in S}\gamma_{v}w(I^{H}_{v}),

\alpha\gamma\psi^{H}=\sum_{v\in S}w(\delta_{H}(v)\cap I^{H}_{v,1})=\sum_{v\in S}\alpha_{v}\gamma_{v}w(I^{H}_{v}),

and

\beta(1-\gamma)\psi^{H}=\sum_{v\in S}w(\delta_{H}(v)\cap I^{H}_{v,2})=\sum_{v\in S}\beta_{v}(1-\gamma_{v})w(I^{H}_{v}).

We have

.

\displaystyle\left(\frac{k-2}{2}+\alpha\right)\gamma\psi^{H}+\left(\frac{k-3}{2}+\beta\right)(1-\gamma)\psi^{H}

Lemma 16

\displaystyle\ n_{S}w(\mathcal{P}^{*}_{k})\geq\frac{1}{2}n_{S}w(\mathcal{C}^{*}_{k}).

Therefore, we have that

\left(\frac{k-1}{k}+\frac{1}{k}\gamma-\alpha\gamma\right)\psi^{H}=(1-\alpha)\gamma\psi^{H}+\frac{k-1}{k}(1-\gamma)\psi^{H}\geq n_{S}w(\mathcal{P}^{*}_{k})\geq\frac{1}{2}n_{S}w(\mathcal{C}^{*}_{k}).

∎

Lemma 17
.

For graph
H
, let
\mathcal{C}^{*}_{\bmod k}
be a minimum weight mod
k
-cycle packing. It holds that

\min\{2(1-\alpha),1\}\cdot\gamma\psi+(1-\gamma)\psi\geq n_{S}w(\mathcal{C}^{*}_{\bmod k}).

Proof.

The proof follows from a similar analysis in the proof of Lemma
16
.
∎

Lemma 18
.

For graph
H
, let
\mathcal{C}^{*}_{k}
be a minimum weight
k
-cycle packing. It holds that

\min\{2(1-\alpha),1\}\cdot\gamma\psi+\frac{2(k-1)}{k}(1-\gamma)\psi\geq n_{S}w(\mathcal{C}^{*}_{k}).

Proof.

The proof follows from a similar analysis in the proof of Lemma
16
.
∎

Lemma 19
.

Let
M_{H}
be a minimum weight perfect matching in graph
H
. For TTP-
k
with
k\geq 3
, we have
\frac{1}{2}\psi\geq n_{S}w(M_{H})
. Moreover, if
k
is even, we have

\frac{1}{2}\cdot\min\{2(1-\alpha),1\}\cdot\gamma\psi+\frac{1}{2}(1-\gamma)\psi\geq n_{S}w(M_{H}).

Proof.

It follows directly from Lemmas
10
,
11
, and
17
.
∎

Next, we will describe our algorithm. Our algorithm consists of two constructions, where the first is based on the Hamiltonian cycle and the second is based on the
k
-cycle/path packing.
The quality of each will be analyzed after showing the construction.
At last, we will analyze the approximation quality of each, make a trade-off between them, and then get the final approximation ratio.

4
The Constructions

In this section, we introduce the methods to construct feasible schedules for TTP-
k
. We have two construction methods.
The first construction is based on a given Hamiltonian cycle of the graph. This method has been widely used in the literature and we will not repeat all the details.
The second construction is based on a given cycle packing of the graph. This can be considered as a new method.
We need to use the two methods to do some trade-offs in the analysis.

4.1
The Hamiltonian Cycle Construction

The idea of Hamiltonian cycle construction is to make use of the canonical schedule
Kirkman (1847)
;
De Werra (1981)
and a Hamiltonian cycle.
For TTP-
k
, many previous schedules were constructed by using this method
Yamaguchi
et al. (2011)
;
Westphal and Noparlik (2014)
;
Imahori
et al. (2014)
. In our algorithm, we will also directly use the well-analyzed schedule in
Yamaguchi
et al. (2011)
. Next, we give a brief introduction to this construction.

Roughly speaking, this schedule is generated by a rotation scheme that can make sure that almost all road trips of each team are
(k+1)
-cycles and in each road trip, the team visits a set of consecutive teams along the Hamiltonian cycle used.
For the quality of this construction, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 20

(
Yamaguchi
et al. (2011)
)
.

Let
C
be a Hamiltonian cycle in graph
G
. For TTP-
k
with any constant
k\geq 3
, there is a polynomial-time algorithm that can generate a solution with a total weight of at most

\frac{k-1}{k}nw(C)+\frac{2}{k}\Delta_{G}+O(k/n)\psi.

We cannot use this result directly since most of our previous lower bounds are based on the graph
H
.
To get a similar result based on a given Hamiltonian cycle in graph
H
, we prove the following lemma.

Lemma 21
.

Let
C_{H}
be a Hamiltonian cycle in graph
H
such that
n_{S}w(C_{H})\leq O(1)\psi
. For TTP-
k
with any constant
k\geq 3
, there is a polynomial-time algorithm that can generate a solution with a total weight of at most

\frac{k-1}{k}n_{S}w(C_{H})+\frac{2}{k}\Delta_{H}+O(k^{2}/n)\psi.

Proof.

Given a Hamiltonian cycle
C_{H}
in graph
H
, we can construct a graph by adding two copies of edge
(u,v)
for each
u\in\overline{S}
, where
v=\arg\min_{v^{\prime}\in S}w(u,v^{\prime})
. So, it holds that

w(u,v)\leq\frac{1}{n_{S}}\sum_{v^{\prime}\in S}w(u,v^{\prime}).

The new graph is a connected Eulerian graph on vertex set
V
, and the total weight of edges in this graph is at most

w(C_{H})+\sum_{u\in\overline{S}}\left(\frac{2}{n_{S}}\sum_{v\in S}w(u,v)\right)\leq w(C_{H})+\frac{2}{n_{S}}\sum_{u\in\overline{S}}\deg_{G}(u)\leq w(C_{H})+\frac{16k}{n^{2}}\Delta_{G},

(8)

where the first inequality follows from
\sum_{v\in S}w(u,v)\leq\sum_{v\in V}w(u,v)=\deg_{G}(u)
, and the second follows from Lemma
3
and (
2
). By shortcutting this Eulerian graph, we can get a Hamiltonian cycle
C
in graph
G
with a weight of at most

w(C_{H})+\frac{16k}{n^{2}}\Delta_{G}\leq w(C_{H})+\frac{8k^{2}}{n^{2}}\psi

by Lemma
13
and (
8
). Using this cycle, by Lemma
20
, we can generate a solution with a total weight of at most

\displaystyle\frac{k-1}{k}n\left(w(C_{H})+\frac{8k^{2}}{n^{2}}\psi\right)+\frac{2}{k}\Delta_{G}+O(k/n)\psi

\displaystyle\leq\frac{k-1}{k}nw(C_{H})+\frac{2}{k}\Delta_{G}+\frac{8k^{2}}{n}\psi+O(k/n)\psi.

Note that

\frac{k-1}{k}nw(C_{H})\leq\frac{k-1}{k}n_{S}w(C_{H})+n_{\overline{S}}w(C_{H})\leq\frac{k-1}{k}n_{S}w(C_{H})+\frac{8k}{n}n_{S}w(C_{H}),

where the first inequality follows from
n=n_{S}+n_{\overline{S}}
, and the second follows from
n_{\overline{S}}<4k
and
1/n_{S}<2/n
by (
2
).
By (
5
), we also have

\frac{2}{k}\Delta_{G}=\frac{2}{k}\Delta_{H}+\frac{8k}{n}\psi.

Based on the condition that
n_{S}w(C_{H})\leq O(1)\psi
, we know that the total weight of the solution is at most

\displaystyle\frac{k-1}{k}nw(C_{H})+\frac{2}{k}\Delta_{G}+\frac{8k^{2}}{n}\psi+O(k/n)\psi

\displaystyle\ \leq\frac{k-1}{k}n_{S}w(C_{H})+\frac{2}{k}\Delta_{H}+\frac{8k}{n}n_{S}w(C_{H})+\frac{8k}{n}\psi+\frac{8k^{2}}{n}\psi+O(k/n)\psi

\displaystyle\ \leq\frac{k-1}{k}n_{S}w(C_{H})+\frac{2}{k}\Delta_{H}+O(k^{2}/n)\psi.

∎

Note that the condition
n_{S}w(C_{H})\leq O(1)\psi
in Lemma
21
is easy to satisfy.
For example, if the Hamiltonian cycle
C_{H}
is obtained by the Christofides-Serdyukov algorithm (a
3/2
-approximation algorithm for TSP)
Christofides (1976)
;
Serdyukov (1978)
, then we have
n_{S}w(C_{H})\leq\frac{3}{2}\psi=O(1)\psi
by Lemma
11
.

4.2
The Cycle Packing Construction

In this subsection, we will construct a feasible schedule based on a given
k
-cycle packing of the graph.
We will also show that the construction works for a given
k
-path packing.
One challenge for the construction is to build a feasible schedule for

4.2.1
The construction

Given a
k
-cycle packing
\mathcal{C}_{k}=\{C_{1},\dots,C_{2m}\}
in graph
H

k
-cycles were arbitrarily labeled. There are
2m
cycle-teams

and
m

U_{i}=u_{2i-1}\cup u_{2i}

n_{\overline{S}}

\overline{S}

R_{i}=\{t_{n_{S}+2i-1},t_{n_{S}+2i}\}

.
The packing construction is to first arrange
super-games
for super-teams (including the
r

Recall that
n\geq 8k^{2}

n_{S}=2mk

n_{\overline{S}}=n-n_{S}

Hence, we have

Each super-team will first attend
m-1

Note that each normal team should attend

Recall that

We will construct the schedule for super-teams from the first time slot to the last time slot.
In each of the

The schedule in the last time slot, which also contains the design of unarranged normal games, is different from the schedules in the first

\mathbb{E}[w(U_{i},U_{j})]=\sum_{u_{i^{\prime}}\in U_{i}\&u_{j^{\prime}}\in U_{j}}\mathbb{E}[w(u_{i^{\prime}},u_{j^{\prime}})]\leq\sum_{u_{i^{\prime}}\in U_{i}\&u_{j^{\prime}}\in U_{j}}\frac{1}{2m^{2}}\Delta_{H}=\frac{2}{m^{2}}\Delta_{H}.

(10)

Lemma 23
.

Assume that there is a normal cycle-game between cycle-teams
u_{i}
and
u_{j}
. There is an
O(k^{2})
-time algorithm ensuring that the total weight of all normal games in the cycle game is at most
(4/k)w(u_{i},u_{j})+(k-1)(w(C_{i})+w(C_{j}))
.

Proof.

Assume w.l.o.g. that the normal cycle-game is at the home of
u_{j}
. By the design of normal cycle-games, all normal teams in
u_{i}
(resp.,
u_{j}
) play
A^{k}\cdot H^{k}
(resp.,
H^{k}\cdot A^{k}
). Hence, each normal team in
u_{i}\cup u_{j}
has exactly one road trip visiting
k
normal teams. Recall that
u_{i}=\{t_{k(i-1)+1},\dots,t_{k(i-1)+k}\}
and
u_{j}=\{t_{k(j-1)+1},\dots,t_{k(j-1)+k}\}
. For the sake of presentation, we let
t_{k(i-1)+i^{\prime}}=x_{i^{\prime}}
and
t_{k(j-1)+i^{\prime}}=y_{i^{\prime}}
, i.e.,
u_{i}=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\}
and
u_{j}=\{y_{1},\dots,y_{k}\}
.
By the design of normal cycle-games, the weight of these
2k
road trips are

\displaystyle\sum_{i^{\prime}=1}^{k}(w(x_{i^{\prime}},y_{i^{\prime}})+w(x_{i^{\prime}},y_{(i^{\prime}+k-2)\bmod k+1}))+(k-1)(w(C_{i})+w(C_{j})).

Before extending the normal cycle-game into normal games, we may change the labels of normal teams in the
k
-cycle
C_{j}
by setting
y^{l}_{i^{\prime}}=y_{(i^{\prime}+l-2)\bmod k+1}
, where
l\in\{1,2,\dots,k\}
. Note that after extending the normal cycle-game, we can recover the previous labels of normal teams in
C_{j}
, which does not effect the feasibility of teams since all normal teams in
u_{i}
(resp.,
u_{j}
) play
A^{k}\cdot H^{k}
(resp.,
H^{k}\cdot A^{k}
). Note that

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{k}\sum_{i^{\prime}=1}^{k}(w(x_{i^{\prime}},y^{l}_{i^{\prime}})+w(x_{i^{\prime}},y^{l}_{(i^{\prime}+k-2)\bmod k+1}))=2w(u_{i},u_{j}).

Hence, for all
k
cases of
l
, there must be one case
l=l_{0}
such that

\sum_{i^{\prime}=1}^{k}(w(x_{i^{\prime}},y^{l_{0}}_{i^{\prime}})+w(x_{i^{\prime}},y^{l_{0}}_{(i^{\prime}+k-2)\bmod k+1}))\leq(4/k)w(u_{i},u_{j}).

The best label of the
k
cases can be found in
O(k^{2})
time.
∎

Note that the algorithm in Lemma

runs in constant time if

.

Assumption 24

We assume that all normal teams return home before and after every normal game in left/right super-games and the unarranged normal games in the last time slot.

.
The normal games appear in right super-games and Case 1 of the unarranged normal games. Recall that

\mathbb{E}[W_{a}]=W_{a}\leq\sum_{v\in\overline{S}}4\deg_{G}(v)\leq(16k/n)\Delta_{G}\leq(8k^{2}/n)\psi,

(11)

where the second inequality follows from (
3

Case 2:
W_{b}
.
The normal games appear in the second part of Case 2 of the unarranged normal games. By Assumption

, the weight of normal games between normal teams in super-team

is

. Recall that

(12)

where the third inequality follows from (

m=2\lfloor\frac{n}{4k}\rfloor>2(\frac{n}{4k}-1)\geq\frac{n}{4k}

and

(13)

The normal games appear in right super-games and the first part of Case 1. There should be a team-pair. Note that the weight of normal games related to the team-pair has been analyzed in

and

and

.

Thus, the expected weight of our schedule is bounded by

.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{k}
be a

Next, we give a brief introduction to the derandomization.

According to the previous analysis, it is easy to see that there is an upper bound of the weight of our schedule, denoted by
W
, such that it is a linear function on variables

and
w(C_{i})
for

.
In Theorem

, we have proved that
\mathbb{E}[W]\leq\frac{k-1}{k}n_{S}w(\mathcal{C}_{k})+\frac{2}{k}\Delta_{H}+O(k^{3}/n)\psi
.
Recall that the algorithm takes a
k
-cycle packing
\mathcal{C}_{k}
as a part of the input, where
|\mathcal{C}_{k}|=2m
.
Let the
2m
cycle teams be
\{C^{*}_{1},\dots,C^{*}_{2m}\}
.
The main idea is to find a permutation
\sigma:(1,2,\dots,2m)\leftrightarrow(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\dots,\sigma_{2m})
such that

\mathbb{E}[W|C_{1}=C^{*}_{\sigma_{1}},\dots,C_{2m}=C^{*}_{\sigma_{2m}}]\leq\mathbb{E}[W].

We can determine each
\sigma_{i}
sequentially. Assume that we have determined
(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{s-1})
such that

\mathbb{E}[W|C_{1}=C^{*}_{\sigma_{1}},\dots,C_{s-1}=C^{*}_{\sigma_{s-1}}]\leq\mathbb{E}[W].

To determine
\sigma_{s}
, we can simply let
\sigma_{s}
be

\sigma_{s}=\arg\min_{\sigma_{s}\in\{1,2,\dots,2m\}\setminus\{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\dots,\sigma_{s-1}\}}\mathbb{E}[W|C_{1}=C^{*}_{\sigma_{1}},\dots,C_{s}=C^{*}_{\sigma_{s}}]\leq\mathbb{E}[W].

(16)

Then, we can get

\mathbb{E}[W|C_{1}=C^{*}_{\sigma_{1}},\dots,C_{s}=C^{*}_{\sigma_{s}}]\leq\mathbb{E}[W|C_{1}=C^{*}_{\sigma_{1}},\dots,C_{s-1}=C^{*}_{\sigma_{s-1}}]\leq\mathbb{E}[W].

(17)

Therefore, we can repeat this procedure to determine the permutation
\sigma
.

Now, we consider the running time.
We need to determine
2m
labels respectively. For each label, we need to compute the expected conditional weight of
W
at most
2m
times. Hence, we need to compute the expected conditional weight of
W
at most
4m^{2}=O(n^{2})
times.
To compute the expected conditional weight of
W
, we need to first compute the expected conditional weight of each variable.
There are
\frac{2m(2m-1)}{2}+2m=2m^{2}+m=O(n^{2})
variables.
It is easy to see that the expected conditional weight of each variable can be computed in
O(n^{2})
time, and hence the expected conditional weight of
W
can be computed in
O(n^{4})
time.
Therefore, the derandomization takes
O(n^{6})
extra time.

5.1.1
The Approximation Quality of the Hamiltonian Cycle Construction

Note that the Hamiltonian cycle they used is generated by the Christofides-Serdyukov algorithm. In our algorithm, we also consider another Hamiltonian cycle that uses the minimum weight perfect matching.
For TTP-3, we will select the better one between these two cycles.

Lemma 27
.

Let
M_{H}
be a perfect matching in graph
H
. There is a polynomial-time algorithm that can generate a Hamiltonian cycle
C_{H}
in
H
with a weight of at most
w(M_{H})+\frac{1}{2n_{S}}\Delta_{H}+O(1/n^{2}_{S})\Delta_{H}
.

Proof.

The perfect matching
M_{H}
contains
n_{S}/2
edges. We can list the edges of
M_{H}
in a random way, and connect them from first to last to form a Hamiltonian cycle
C_{H}
in graph
H
, respectively.
For example, suppose
n_{S}=4
, the listed two edges (in a random way) are denoted by
(v_{1},v_{2})
and
(v_{3},v_{4})
, and then we can construct a Hamiltonian cycle
(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4},v_{1})
.

Recall that the edge set of graph
H
is denoted by
E_{H}
.
There are
n_{S}/2
newly added edges, belonging to the edge set
E_{H}\setminus M_{H}
. Every edge in the set is selected with the same probability
\frac{1}{|E_{H}\setminus M_{H}|}=\frac{2}{n_{S}(n_{S}-2)}
. The expected weight of newly added edges is
(\frac{n_{S}}{2})\cdot\frac{2}{n_{S}(n_{S}-2)}w(E_{H}\setminus M_{H})\leq\frac{1}{2(n_{S}-2)}\Delta_{H}=\frac{1}{2n_{S}}\Delta_{H}+\frac{1}{n_{S}(n_{S}-2)}\Delta_{H}\leq\frac{1}{2n_{S}}\Delta_{H}+\frac{2}{n^{2}_{S}}\Delta_{H}
, where the first inequality follows from
w(E_{H}\setminus M_{H})\leq w(E_{H})=\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{H}
by (
4
), and the second follows from
n_{S}=n-n_{\overline{S}}\geq 8k^{2}-4k>4
. Hence, the Hamiltonian cycle
C_{H}
has an expected weight of at most

w(M_{H})+\frac{1}{2n_{S}}\Delta_{H}+\frac{2}{n^{2}_{S}}\Delta_{H}=w(M_{H})+\frac{1}{2n_{S}}\Delta_{H}+O(1/n^{2}_{S})\Delta_{H}.

The randomized algorithm runs in
O(n^{3})
time.
Again, it can be derandomized efficiently by the method of conditional expectations
Motwani and
Raghavan (1995)
.
∎

Note that Lemma
27
holds for any perfect matching. For TTP-3, we consider the Hamiltonian cycle using a minimum weight perfect matching. By making a trade-off between the two Hamiltonian cycles, we can get the following theorem.

Theorem 28
.

For TTP-3 with any constant
\varepsilon>0
, there is a polynomial-time algorithm with an approximation ratio of
\min\{\frac{4}{3}+\frac{1}{3}\alpha\gamma,\ 1-\frac{1}{6}\gamma+\alpha\gamma\}+\varepsilon
.

Proof.

Here we use
C_{H}
to denote a minimum weight Hamiltonian cycle in graph
H
.
If we use the Hamiltonian cycle

obtained by the Christofides-Serdyukov algorithm, we can construct a feasible schedule with a total weight of at most

by Lemma
21
, since
n_{S}w(C^{\prime}_{H})\leq\frac{3}{2}n_{S}w(C_{H})\leq\frac{3}{2}\psi=O(1)\psi
(recall that the Christofides-Serdyukov algorithm is a
3/2
-approximation algorithm for TSP).
Then, we have that

\displaystyle\frac{2}{3}n_{S}w(C^{\prime}_{H})+\frac{2}{3}\Delta_{H}+O(1/n)\psi

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{3}n_{S}\left(\mbox{MST}(H)+\frac{1}{2}w(C_{H})\right)+\frac{2}{3}\Delta_{H}+O(1/n)\psi

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3}\left(n_{S}\mbox{MST}(H)+\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{H}\right)+\frac{1}{3}\Delta_{H}+\frac{1}{3}n_{S}w(C_{H})+O(1/n)\psi

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{3}\left(1+\frac{1}{4}\gamma\right)\psi+\frac{1}{3}\left(1-\frac{1}{2}\gamma+\alpha\gamma\right)\psi+\frac{1}{3}\psi+O(1/n)\psi

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{4}{3}+\frac{1}{3}\alpha\gamma\right)\psi+O(1/n)\psi,

where the first inequality follows from Lemma
3
, and the second follows from Lemmas
11
,
12
, and (

).

Similarly, if we use a minimum weight perfect matching
M_{H}
in graph

to obtain the Hamiltonian cycle

\displaystyle\frac{2}{3}nw(C^{\prime\prime}_{H})+\frac{2}{3}\Delta_{H}+O(1/n)\psi

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{3}n_{S}\left(w(M_{H})+\frac{1}{2n_{S}}\Delta_{H}+O(1/n^{2}_{S})\Delta_{H}\right)+\frac{2}{3}\Delta_{H}+O(1/n)\psi

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{3}(n_{S}w(M)+\Delta_{H})+\frac{1}{3}\Delta_{H}+O(1/n)\psi

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{3}(1+\alpha\gamma)\psi+\frac{1}{3}\left(1-\frac{1}{2}\gamma+\alpha\gamma\right)\psi+O(1/n)\psi

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{1}{6}\gamma+\alpha\gamma\right)\psi+O(1/n)\psi,

(\frac{8\rho+6}{9}+\frac{4\rho-3}{9}\gamma-\frac{4\rho-2}{3}\alpha\gamma+\varepsilon)
. Therefore, the ratio of our algorithm in the worst case is

\max_{0\leq\alpha,\gamma\leq 1}\min\left\{\frac{4}{3}+\frac{1}{3}\alpha\gamma,\ 1-\frac{1}{6}\gamma+\alpha\gamma,\ \frac{8\rho+6}{9}+\frac{4\rho-3}{9}\gamma-\frac{4\rho-2}{3}\alpha\gamma\right\}+\varepsilon.

We can transform it into the following LP, where we let
\widetilde{\gamma}=\alpha\gamma
.

\displaystyle\max\quad

\displaystyle y

s.t.

\displaystyle y\leq\frac{4}{3}+\frac{1}{3}\widetilde{\gamma},

\displaystyle y\leq 1-\frac{1}{6}\gamma+\widetilde{\gamma},

\displaystyle y\leq\frac{8\rho+6}{9}+\frac{4\rho-3}{9}\gamma-\frac{4\rho-2}{3}\widetilde{\gamma},

\displaystyle 0\leq\widetilde{\gamma}\leq\gamma\leq 1.

The LP shows that the ratio is
\frac{11}{6}-\frac{5}{2(4\rho+1)}+\varepsilon
when

, and
\frac{5}{3}-\frac{2}{3(4\rho-1)}+\varepsilon
otherwise.
∎

For any constant

For any constant

5.2
TTP-4

It is natural to use the same idea to solve TTP-4. For the problem of minimum 4-path packing, to our best knowledge, the current best-known ratio is
3/2

Monnot and
Toulouse (2008)
. For the problem of minimum 4-cycle packing, there is a
3
-approximation algorithm
Goemans and
Williamson (1995)
.

If we use the construction based on the Hamiltonian cycle and the construction based on the 4-path packing, we can get a
(17/10+\varepsilon)
-approximation algorithm for TTP-4, which improves the previous ratio
(7/4+\varepsilon)

Yamaguchi
et al. (2011)
.

Theorem 33
.

For TTP-4 with any constant
\varepsilon>0
, there is a polynomial-time algorithm using the Hamiltonian cycle construction that can achieve an approximation ratio of
(\frac{3}{2}+\frac{1}{8}\gamma+\frac{1}{8}\alpha\gamma+\varepsilon)
.

Proof.

Here we use
C_{H}
to denote a minimum weight Hamiltonian cycle in graph
H
.
If we use the Hamiltonian cycle
C^{\prime}_{H}
obtained by the Christofides-Serdyukov algorithm, we can construct a feasible schedule with a total weight of at most
\frac{3}{4}n_{S}w(C^{\prime}_{H})+\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{H}+O(1/n)\psi
by Lemma
21
, since
n_{S}w(C^{\prime}_{H})\leq\frac{3}{2}n_{S}w(C_{H})\leq\frac{3}{2}\psi=O(1)\psi
(recall that the Christofides-Serdyukov algorithm is a
3/2
-approximation algorithm for TSP).
Then, we have that

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}n_{S}w(C^{\prime}_{H})+\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{H}+O(1/n)\psi

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{4}n_{S}\left(\mbox{MST}(H)+\frac{1}{2}w(C_{H})\right)+\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{H}+O(1/n)\psi

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{4}\left(n_{S}\mbox{MST}(H)+\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{H}\right)+\frac{1}{8}\Delta_{H}+\frac{3}{8}n_{S}w(C_{H})+O(1/n)\psi

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{4}\left(\frac{5}{4}+\frac{1}{4}\gamma\right)\psi+\frac{1}{8}\left(\frac{3}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\gamma+\alpha\gamma\right)\psi+\frac{3}{8}\psi+O(1/n)\psi

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{3}{2}+\frac{1}{8}\gamma+\frac{1}{8}\alpha\gamma\right)\psi+O(1/n)\psi,

where the first inequality follows from Lemma
3
, and the second follows from Lemmas
11
,
12
, and (
6
).
Note that there is no need to use the Hamiltonian cycle based on the minimum weight perfect matching.
∎

Theorem 34
.

For TTP-4 with any constant
\varepsilon>0
, there is a polynomial-time algorithm using the 4-path packing construction that can achieve an approximation ratio of
(\frac{39}{16}+\frac{5}{16}\gamma-\frac{7}{4}\alpha\gamma+\varepsilon)
.

Proof.

Using the
3/2
-approximation 4-path packing
\mathcal{P}_{4}
in graph
H
, by Corollary
26
, we can get a schedule with a weight of at most
\frac{9}{4}n_{S}w(\mathcal{P}^{*}_{4})+\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{H}+O(1/n)\psi
. Then, we have that

\displaystyle\frac{9}{4}n_{S}w(\mathcal{P}^{*}_{4})+\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{H}+O(1/n)\psi

\displaystyle\leq\frac{9}{4}\left(\frac{3}{4}+\frac{1}{4}\gamma-\alpha\gamma\right)\psi+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{3}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\gamma+\alpha\gamma\right)\psi+O(1/n)\psi

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{39}{16}+\frac{5}{16}\gamma-\frac{7}{4}\alpha\gamma\right)\psi+O(1/n)\psi,

where the inequality follows from Lemmas
12
and
16
.
∎

Making a trade-off between Theorems

and

, we can get an approximation ratio of

\max_{0\leq\alpha,\gamma\leq 1}\min\left\{\frac{3}{2}+\frac{1}{8}\gamma+\frac{1}{8}\alpha\gamma,\ \frac{39}{16}+\frac{5}{16}\gamma-\frac{7}{4}\alpha\gamma\right\}+\varepsilon=\frac{17}{10}+\varepsilon.

Hence, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 35
.

For any contant
\varepsilon>0
, there is a polynomial-time
(17/10+\varepsilon)
-approximation algorithm for TTP-4.

5.3
TTP-
k
with
k\geq 5

For TTP-
k
with
k\geq 5
, we note that both of the best-known approximation ratios for minimum
k
-cycle and
k
-path packing problems are
4(1-1/k)>3

Goemans and
Williamson (1995)
. The approximation ratios are too big and we cannot improve TTP-
k
by using the same idea. However, by a refined analysis in
Yamaguchi
et al. (2011)
, we can slightly improve the approximation ratio
(\frac{5k-7}{2k}+\varepsilon)
by a term of
\Theta(1/k)
.

Define
\zeta=\alpha\gamma+\beta(1-\gamma)
. It measures the proportion of weights of all home-edges compared to the itineraries of all teams. Then, we have
0\leq\zeta\leq 1
. We can get two following lower bounds.

Lemma 36
.

(\frac{k-2}{2}+\zeta)\psi\geq\Delta_{H}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
12
, we have that
\Delta_{H}\leq(\frac{k-2}{2}+\alpha)\gamma\psi+(\frac{k-3}{2}+\beta)(1-\gamma)\psi
. Note that
(\frac{k-2}{2}+\alpha)\gamma\psi+(\frac{k-3}{2}+\beta)(1-\gamma)\psi\leq(\frac{k-2}{2}+\alpha)\gamma\psi+(\frac{k-2}{2}+\beta)(1-\gamma)\psi=(\frac{k-2}{2}+\zeta)\psi
.
∎

Lemma 37
.

\min\{(1-\frac{1}{2}\zeta)\psi,\frac{k}{k+1}\psi\}\geq n_{S}\mbox{MST}(H)
.

Proof.

We first show that
(1-\frac{1}{2}\zeta)\psi\geq n_{S}\mbox{MST}(H)
.
By Lemma
14
, we have that
n_{S}\mbox{MST}(H)\leq(1-\frac{1}{2}\alpha)\gamma\psi+(1-\frac{1}{2}\beta)(1-\gamma)\psi=(1-\frac{1}{2}\zeta)\psi
.

To prove
\frac{k}{k+1}\psi\geq n_{S}\mbox{MST}(H)
, it is sufficient to prove that
\frac{k}{k+1}w(I_{v})\geq\mbox{MST}(H)
holds for any
v\in S
. Recall that
I_{v}
contains a set of
k^{\prime}
-cycles with
3\leq k^{\prime}\leq k+1
. For each
k^{\prime}
-cycle
C
, we delete the longest edge and the weight of the cycle loses at least
\frac{1}{k^{\prime}}w(C)\geq\frac{1}{k+1}w(C)
. Hence, we can get a spanning tree with the a total weight of at most
\frac{k}{k+1}w(I_{v})
. We have
\frac{k}{k+1}w(I_{v})\geq\mbox{MST}(H)
.
∎

By Lemmas
36
and
37
, we have that

\frac{k+2}{4}\psi\geq\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{H}+n_{S}\mbox{MST}(H).

(18)

Theorem 38
.

For TTP-
k
with any constant
k\geq 5
,
there is a polynomial-time algorithm achieving approximation ratio of
(\frac{5k^{2}-4k+3}{2k(k+1)}+\varepsilon)
.

Proof.

Here we use
C_{H}
to denote a minimum weight Hamiltonian cycle in graph
H
.
If we use the Hamiltonian cycle
C^{\prime}_{H}
obtained by the Christofides-Serdyukov algorithm, we can construct a feasible schedule with a total weight of at most
\frac{k-1}{k}n_{S}w(C^{\prime}_{H})+\frac{2}{k}\Delta_{H}+O(k^{2}/n)\psi
by Lemma
21
, since
n_{S}w(C^{\prime}_{H})\leq\frac{3}{2}n_{S}w(C_{H})\leq\frac{3}{2}\psi=O(1)\psi
(recall that the Christofides-Serdyukov algorithm is a
3/2
-approximation algorithm for TSP).
Then, we have that

\displaystyle\frac{k-1}{k}n_{S}w(C^{\prime}_{H})+\frac{2}{k}\Delta_{H}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{k-1}{k}n_{S}\left(\mbox{MST}(H)+\frac{1}{2}w(C_{H})\right)+\frac{2}{k}\Delta_{H}

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{k}\left(n_{S}\mbox{MST}(H)+\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{H}\right)+\frac{k-5}{k}n_{S}\mbox{MST}(H)+\frac{k-1}{2k}n_{S}w(C_{H})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{k}\left(\frac{k+2}{4}\psi\right)+\frac{k-5}{k}\left(\frac{k}{k+1}\psi\right)+\frac{k-1}{2k}\psi

\displaystyle=\frac{5k^{2}-4k+3}{2k(k+1)}\psi,

where the first inequality follows from Lemma
3
, and the second follows from Lemmas
11
,
37
, and (
18
).
Since we consider
k
as a constant,
we can get an approximation of
(\frac{5k^{2}-4k+3}{2k(k+1)}+\varepsilon)
.
Note that it satisfies that
\frac{5k^{2}-4k+3}{2k(k+1)}\leq\frac{5k-7}{2k}
for any constant
k\geq 5
.
∎

6
LDTTP-
k
with
k\geq 3

If all teams are on a line shape graph, TTP-
k
is known as Linear Distance TTP-
k
(LDTTP-
k
)
Hoshino and
Kawarabayashi (2012)
. For this graph, it is easy to see that the minimum
k
-cycle/path packing can be found in polynomial time.
For LDTTP-3, by Corollary
32
, our algorithm has an approximation ratio of
(4/3+\varepsilon)
, which also matches the current best-known ratio
4/3

Hoshino and
Kawarabayashi (2012)
.
Their result is based on a stronger lower bound of LDTTP-3.
If we use this lower bound, the ratio of our 3-cycle packing construction can achieve
(6/5+\varepsilon)
. In this section, we will consider LDTTP-
k
directly.

6.1
The Lower Bound

We first propose a lower bound for LDTTP-
k
, which is a generalization of the lower bound for LDTTP-3 used in
Hoshino and
Kawarabayashi (2012)
.
We consider the graph
H
, which is also a line shape.
Assume that the teams from left to right on the line are
(t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{n_{S}})
, respectively. We let
d_{i}=w(t_{i},t_{i+1})
for all
1\leq i\leq n_{S}-1
. Then, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 39
.

For LDTTP-
k
, it holds that

\psi\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n_{S}-1}c_{i}d_{i},

where
c_{i}=2i\lceil\frac{n_{S}-i}{k}\rceil+2(n_{S}-i)\lceil\frac{i}{k}\rceil
.

Proof.

For an edge
t_{i}t_{i+1}
, there are
i
teams on the left and
n_{S}-i
teams on the right of it. For each left team, it takes at least
\lceil\frac{n_{S}-i}{k}\rceil
trips for it to visit all right teams. For each right team, it takes at least
\lceil\frac{i}{k}\rceil
trips for it to visit all left teams.
There are at least
i\lceil\frac{n_{S}-i}{k}\rceil+(n_{S}-i)\lceil\frac{i}{k}\rceil=\frac{l_{i}}{2}
trips in total. Each trip must cross the edge twice and hence all teams must cross the edge at least
l_{i}
times. Together, we get the lower bound
\sum_{i=1}^{n_{S}-1}l_{i}d_{i}
.
∎

Next, we analyze the approximation ratio of our
k
-cycle packing schedule.

6.2
The Analysis

We can get an optimal
k
-cycle packing
\mathcal{C}_{k}
in graph
H
such that

\mathcal{C}_{k}=\{(t_{ki-(k-1)},t_{ki-(k-2)},\dots,t_{ki},t_{ki-(k-1)})\}_{i=1}^{n_{S}/k}.

Using
\mathcal{C}_{k}
, by Theorem
25
, the weight of our cycle packing schedule is
\frac{k-1}{k}n_{S}w(\mathcal{C}_{k})+\frac{2}{k}\Delta_{H}+O(k^{3}/n)\psi
.
Let
\frac{k-1}{k}n_{S}w(\mathcal{C}_{k})+\frac{2}{k}\Delta_{H}=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{S}-1}f_{i}d_{i}
. Define
\rho=\max_{1\leq i\leq n_{S}-1}\frac{f_{i}}{c_{i}}
. Then, by Lemma
39
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{k-1}{k}n_{S}w(\mathcal{C}_{k})+\frac{2}{k}\Delta_{H}=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{S}-1}f_{i}d_{i}\leq\rho\sum_{i=1}^{n_{S}-1}c_{i}d_{i}\leq\rho\psi.

(19)

Theorem 40
.

For LDTTP-
k
with any constant
k\geq 3
, the approximation ratio of our cycle packing schedule is
(\frac{3k-3}{2k-1}+\varepsilon)
.

Proof.

By (
19
), we know that it is sufficient to prove
\rho=\frac{3k-3}{2k-1}+O(1/n)
.
Recall that
\frac{k-1}{k}n_{S}w(\mathcal{C}_{k})+\frac{2}{k}\Delta_{H}=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{S}-1}f_{i}d_{i}
.
On one hand, it is easy to see that
\frac{k-1}{k}n_{S}w(\mathcal{C}_{k})=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{S}/k}\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\frac{2k-2}{k}n_{S}d_{ki-j}
. On the other hand, we have
\frac{2}{k}\Delta_{H}=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{S}-1}\frac{4}{k}i(n-i)d_{i}
.
Therefore, we have
f_{i}=\frac{4}{k}i(n_{S}-i)
for
i\bmod k=0
, and
f_{i}=\frac{4}{k}i(n_{S}-i)+\frac{2k-2}{k}n_{S}
for
i\bmod k\neq 0
.
Recall that
c_{i}=2i\lceil\frac{n_{S}-i}{k}\rceil+2(n_{S}-i)\lceil\frac{i}{k}\rceil
. We have
f_{i^{\prime}}=f_{n_{S}-i^{\prime}}
and
c_{i^{\prime}}=c_{n_{S}-i^{\prime}}
for any
1\leq i^{\prime}\leq n_{S}/2
. The structure has a symmetry property, and hence we only consider the case of
1\leq i\leq n_{S}/2
.

Case 1:
i\bmod k=0
.
We have
f_{i}=\frac{4}{k}i(n_{S}-i)
and
c_{i}=\frac{4}{k}i(n_{S}-i)
. Then, we can get
\frac{f_{i}}{c_{i}}=1
.

Case 2:
i\bmod k=j
, where
1\leq j<k
.
We have
f_{i}=\frac{4}{k}i(n_{S}-i)+\frac{2k-2}{k}n_{S}
.
Note that
\lceil\frac{n_{S}-i}{k}\rceil=\frac{n_{S}-i+j}{k}
and
\lceil\frac{i}{k}\rceil=\frac{i+k-j}{k}
.
We have
c_{i}=2i\lceil\frac{n_{S}-i}{k}\rceil+2(n_{S}-i)\lceil\frac{i}{k}\rceil=\frac{4}{k}i(n_{S}-i)+\frac{2}{k}((k-j)n_{S}+(2j-k)i)
. Then, we can get

\displaystyle\frac{f_{i}}{c_{i}}=\

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{4}{k}i(n_{S}-i)+\frac{2k-2}{k}n_{S}}{\frac{4}{k}i(n_{S}-i)+\frac{2}{k}((k-j)n_{S}+(2j-k)i)}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle 1+\frac{\frac{2}{k}((j-1)n_{S}-(2j-k)i)}{\frac{4}{k}i(n_{S}-i)+\frac{2}{k}((k-j)n_{S}+(2j-k)i)}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle 1+\frac{(j-1)n_{S}-(2j-k)i}{2i(n_{S}-i)+(k-j)n_{S}+(2j-k)i}.

In the worst case, it is easy to see that we have
j>1
.
Recall that
1\leq i\leq n_{S}/2
and
k=\Theta(1)
. If
i=\Theta(n)
, we have
(j-1)n_{S}-(2j-k)i=\Theta(n)
,
2i(n_{S}-i)+(k-j)n_{S}+(2j-k)i=\Theta(n^{2})
, and hence
f_{i}/c_{i}=1+\Theta(1/n)
. Otherwise, if
i=o(n)
and
i=\omega(1)
, we have
(j-1)n_{S}-(2j-k)i=\Theta(n)
,
2i(n_{S}-i)+(k-j)n_{S}+(2j-k)i=\omega(n)
, and hence
f_{i}/c_{i}=1+o(1)
. Hence, for the rest case, we have
i=\Theta(1)
. Moreover, we have that
(j-1)n_{S}-(2j-k)i=(j-1)n_{S}+\Theta(1)
and
2i(n_{S}-i)+(k-j)n_{S}+(2j-k)i=(k+2i-j)n_{S}+\Theta(1)
. Therefore, in the worst case, we have that

\displaystyle\frac{f_{i}}{c_{i}}=\

\displaystyle 1+\frac{(j-1)n_{S}+\Theta(1)}{(k+2i-j)n_{S}+\Theta(1)}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle 1+\frac{j-1}{k+2i-j}+\Theta(1/n)

\displaystyle\leq\

\displaystyle 1+\frac{j-1}{k+j}+\Theta(1/n)

\displaystyle\leq\

\displaystyle 1+\frac{k-2}{2k-1}+\Theta(1/n),

where the first inequality follows from
i\geq j
since
i\bmod k=j
and the second is from
1\leq j<k
.

By the above analysis, we have
\rho=\max_{1\leq i\leq n_{S}-1}\frac{f_{i}}{c_{i}}=1+\frac{k-2}{2k-1}+O(1/n)=\frac{3k-3}{2k-1}+O(1/n)
.
∎

We can see that the approximation ratio is
(6/5+\varepsilon)
for LDTTP-3, which improves the previous result of
(4/3+\varepsilon)

Hoshino and
Kawarabayashi (2012)
.

For LDTTP-
k
, we can also analyze the Hamiltonian cycle schedule. Let
C_{H}
be the optimal Hamiltonian cycle in graph
H
, i.e.,
C_{H}=(t_{1},\dots,t_{n_{S}},t_{1})
. Note that
n_{S}w(C_{H})\leq\psi
by Lemma
11
. By Lemma
21
, the total weight of the Hamiltonian cycle schedule is
\frac{2k-2}{k}n_{S}w(C_{H})+\frac{2}{k}\Delta_{H}+O(k^{2}/n)\psi
.
Let
\frac{2k-2}{k}n_{S}w(C_{H})+\frac{2}{k}\Delta_{H}=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}f^{\prime}_{i}d_{i}
.
We have that
\frac{2k-2}{k}n_{S}w(C)=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{S}-1}\frac{2k-2}{k}n_{S}d_{i}
,
\frac{2}{k}\Delta_{H}=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{S}-1}\frac{4}{k}i(n_{S}-i)d_{i}
, and
c_{i}=2i\lceil\frac{n_{S}-i}{k}\rceil+2(n_{S}-i)\lceil\frac{i}{k}\rceil
. It is easy to see that the bottleneck case is

\frac{f^{\prime}_{k}}{c_{k}}=\frac{\frac{2k-2}{k}n_{S}+4(n_{S}-k)}{4(n_{S}-k)}=\frac{3k-1}{2k}+O(1/n).

Theorem 41
.

For LDTTP-
k
with any constant
k\geq 3
, the approximation ratio of the Hamiltonian cycle schedule is
(\frac{3k-1}{2k}+\varepsilon)
.

We can see that the approximation ratio
(\frac{3k-1}{2k}+\varepsilon)
is worse than the ratio
(\frac{3k-3}{2k-1}+\varepsilon)
.
A natural question is whether there is an algorithm with an approximation ratio better than
(\frac{3k-3}{2k-1}+\varepsilon)
.

7
Conclusion

In this paper, we consider approximation algorithms for TTP-
k
. We propose several new lower bounds and a new construction based on the
k
-cycle/path packing. Our construction can be combined well with the previous construction on the Hamiltonian cycle. For TTP-3, which is the most investigated case of TTP-
k
, we prove that these constructions can guarantee an
(139/87+\varepsilon)
-approximation ratio which is better than the best-known ratio
(5/3+\varepsilon)
. Moreover, we show that we can improve the ratio from
(7/4+\varepsilon)
to
(17/10+\varepsilon)
for TTP-4 and from
(\frac{5k-7}{2k}+\varepsilon)
to
(\frac{5k^{2}-4k+3}{2k(k+1)}+\varepsilon)
for TTP-
k
with
k\geq 5
. Furthermore, for LDTTP-
k
, we show that the cycle packing construction has an approximation of
(\frac{3k-3}{2k-1}+\varepsilon)
, which improves the ratio from
4/3
to
(6/5+\varepsilon)
for LDTTP-3.
We also remark that according to our results, an improved approximation ratio for minimum weight
k
-cycle/path packing may directly lead to further improvements for TTP-
k
.

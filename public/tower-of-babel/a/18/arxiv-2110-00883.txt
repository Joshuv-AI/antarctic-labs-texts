Quantitative estimate of the overdamped limit for the Vlasov–Fokker–Planck systems Thanks: H. H. is partially supported by the Pacific Institute for the Mathematical Sciences (PIMS) postdoc fellowship.
Hui Huang

Abstract
. This note adapts a probabilistic approach to establish a quantified estimate of the overdamped limit for the Vlasov–Fokker–Planck equation towards the aggregation-diffusion equation, which in particular includes cases of the Newtonian type singular forces. The proofs are based on the investigation of the weak convergence of the corresponding stochastic differential equations (SDEs) of Mckean type in the continuous path space. We show that one can obtain the same convergence rate as in [ 10 ] under the same assumptions.



Keywords:
Overdamped, large friction, zero inertia, tightness.

1.
Introduction

The present note is concerning with the following kinetic Vlasov-Fokker-Planck (VFP) equation

(1.1)

\partial_{t}f^{\gamma}_{t}+\gamma v\cdot\nabla_{x}f^{\gamma}_{t}+\gamma\nabla_{v}\cdot(F(x,\rho^{\gamma}_{t})f^{\gamma}_{t})=\gamma^{2}\nabla_{v}\cdot(\nabla_{v}f^{\gamma}_{t}+vf^{\gamma}_{t}),\quad f^{\gamma}|_{t=0}=f_{0}\,,

in dimension
d\geq 1
, where
(f^{\gamma}_{t})_{t\geq 0}\subset\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a family of probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\gamma>0
is the damping coefficient. Here
\rho^{\gamma}_{t}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f^{\gamma}_{t}(\cdot,dv)\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
represents the spacial distribution, namely the
x
-marginal of
f^{\gamma}_{t}
. In the sequel we may abuse the notations for a measure and its Lebesgue density for simplicity.
Moreover, we assume the driving force
F
is arising from an external potential
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
and/or interaction potential
K:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, which is of the following from

(1.2)

F(x,\rho)=-\nabla\Phi(x)-(\nabla K\ast\rho)(x)\quad\mbox{for }(x,\rho)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\,,

where

(1.3)

(\nabla K\ast\rho)(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla K(x-y)\rho(dy)\,.

It is well-known that the VFP equation (
1.1
) can be derived from a system of large number of particles interacting through the force field
F
, which satisfies the following system of stochastic differential equations

(1.4)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}dX_{t}^{i,\gamma}=\gamma V_{t}^{i,\gamma}dt,\\
dV_{t}^{i,\gamma}=-\gamma\nabla\Phi(X_{t}^{i,\gamma})dt-\frac{\gamma}{N}\sum_{j\neq i}\nabla K(X_{t}^{i,\gamma}-X_{t}^{j,\gamma})dt-\gamma^{2}V_{t}^{i,\gamma}+\sqrt{2}\gamma dB_{t}^{i},\quad i=1,\cdots,N\,,\end{cases}

where
X_{t}^{i,\gamma},~V_{t}^{i,\gamma}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
denote the position and velocity of the
i
-th particle at time
t
, and
\{(B_{t}^{i})_{t\geq 0}\}_{i=1}^{N}
are
N
independent
d
-dimensional Brownian motions. Here we assume the initial data
\{(X_{0}^{i,\gamma},V_{0}^{i,\gamma})\}_{i=1}^{N}
are i.i.d. with the common distribution
f_{0}
. Model (
1.1
) and its microscopic counterpart (
1.4
) have been widely used in the investigation of complex systems that
model collective behaviour (or swarming), an area that has attracted a great deal of attention, see for instance
[
9
,
2
,
20
,
12
,
32
]
and references therein. Note that equation (
1.1
) also includes the classical Vlasov–Poisson–Fokker–Planck system when
\nabla K=a\frac{x}{|x|^{d}}
,
d\geq 3
. The case
a>0
corresponds, for example, to the electrostatic (repulsive) interaction of charged particles in a plasma, while the case
a<0
describes the attraction between massive particles subject to gravitation in astrophysics.

Under suitable assumption on
\Phi
and
K
, as
N\to\infty
, the mean-field limit result, see for example
[
3
,
24
,
6
,
33
,
25
,
30
,
16
,
31
]
, shall show that the particle dynamics (
1.4
) well approximates the following mean-field nonlinear Mckean process

(1.5a)

\displaystyle dX_{t}^{\gamma}=\gamma V_{t}^{\gamma}dt,

(1.5b)

\displaystyle dV_{t}^{\gamma}=-\gamma^{2}V_{t}^{\gamma}dt+\gamma F(X_{t}^{\gamma},\rho_{t}^{\gamma})dt+\sqrt{2}\gamma dB_{t}\,,

where the initial data
(X_{0},V_{0})
is the same as in (
1.4
). Here
\rho^{\gamma}_{t}=\mbox{Law}(X^{\gamma}_{t})
, the
x
-marginal of
f^{\gamma}_{t}=\mbox{Law}(X_{t}^{\gamma},V_{t}^{\gamma})
, which makes the set of equations (
1.5
) nonlinear. A direct application of Itô’s formula, the law
f_{t}^{\gamma}:=f^{\gamma}(t,\cdot,\cdot)
at time
t
is a weak solution to the following
with the initial data
f^{\gamma}_{0}(x,v)=\mbox{Law}(X_{0},V_{0})
.

In this note, we are interested in the VFP equation (
1.1
) in the overdamped regime, namely in the regime where
\gamma\gg 1
. When
\gamma\to\infty
, it is expected that the kinetic equation (
1.1
) will converge to the following so-called aggregation-diffusion equation

(1.6)

\partial_{t}\rho_{t}+\nabla_{x}\cdot(\rho_{t}F(x,\rho_{t}))=\Delta_{x}\rho_{t},\quad\rho|_{t=0}=\rho_{0}\,.

Equation of the type (
1.6
) appears in various contexts, such as biological aggregations
[
34
]
, material science and granular media
[
35
]
, self-assembly of nanoparticles
[
22
]
and molecular dynamics simulations of matter
[
21
]
. The most noble example is the case when
\Delta K=\delta_{0}
, which is corresponding to the Keller–Segel model for chemotaxis
[
28
]
. Similar to (
1.5
), we have the underlying nonlinear Mckean process satisfying

(1.7)

\displaystyle X_{t}=X_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}F(X_{s},\rho_{s})ds+\sqrt{2}B_{t}

with
\rho_{t}=\mbox{Law}(X_{t})
, and it satisfies the aggregation-diffusion equation (
1.6
). Since the well-posedness of equations (
1.1
) and (
1.6
), and nonlinear processes (
1.5
) and (
1.7
) are not the focus of the present note, we refer readers to, for instance
[
4
,
33
,
3
,
8
,
19
]
, for more discussions on the topic of solvability.

In the absence of the interaction potential, i.e.
K=0
, the overdamped limit was first formally discussed in
[
29
]
by Kramers through introducing a coarse-graining map. Since then, more related results have been proven by using stochastic and asymptotic techniques
[
18
,
23
]
, or variational methods
[
14
]
. In the presence of the interaction potential
K
, a variational technique was proposed in
[
15
]
without obtaining the convergence rate. Most recently the authors in
[
10
]
obtained a quantified overdamped limit in 2-Wasserstein distance for the VFP equation with nonlocal forces.
In the absence of diffusion, similar problems were also investigated in
[
7
,
26
,
17
]
via large friction limit. Especially in
[
7
]
, the authors obtained a quantitative convergence rate, and it was extended to the case with diffusion in
[
5
]
. In the present note we will use a different alternative approach to obtain the quantified overdamped limit under the same assumptions as in
[
10
]
. Instead of looking into the PDEs (
1.1
) and (
1.6
) directly, we will investigate their underlying Mckean processes (
1.5
) and (
1.7
) in the continuous path space. This is less technical than the methods of PDE analysis and is more intuitive in way, and hopefully more
accessible to non-specialists. Such method has been used in
[
11
]
to obtain the consensus based optimization from the particle swarm optimization with the limit of zero inertia.

Outline of the proof.

Let us first solve
V_{t}^{\gamma}
from (
1.5b
) and obtain that

(1.8)

V_{t}^{\gamma}=e^{-\gamma^{2}t}V_{0}+\gamma\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\gamma^{2}(t-s)}F(X_{s}^{\gamma},\rho_{s}^{\gamma})ds+\sqrt{2}\gamma\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\gamma^{2}(t-s)}dB_{s}\,,

which implies that

\displaystyle X_{t}^{\gamma}

\displaystyle=X_{0}+\gamma\int_{0}^{t}V_{\tau}^{\gamma}d\tau=X_{0}+\gamma\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\gamma^{2}\tau}V_{0}d\tau+\gamma^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-\gamma^{2}(\tau-s)}F(X_{s}^{\gamma},\rho_{s}^{\gamma})dsd\tau

\displaystyle\quad+\sqrt{2}\gamma^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-\gamma^{2}(\tau-s)}dB_{s}d\tau

(1.9)

\displaystyle=X_{0}+\frac{1}{\gamma}(1-e^{-\gamma^{2}t})V_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}(1-e^{-\gamma^{2}(t-s)})F(X_{s}^{\gamma},\rho_{s}^{\gamma})ds+\sqrt{2}\int_{0}^{t}(1-e^{-\gamma^{2}(t-s)})dB_{s}\,.

Then
X^{\gamma}_{t}
has the law
\rho^{\gamma}_{t}
for each
t\geq 0
. Denote by
\mathcal{C}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
the space of all
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued continuous functions on
[0,T]
equipped with the usual uniform norm. Each continuous stochastic process
X^{\gamma}
may be seen as a
\mathcal{C}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
-valued random function and it induces a probability measure (or law, denoted by
\rho^{\gamma}
) on
\mathcal{C}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We shall use the weak convergence in the space of probability measures on
\mathcal{C}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
We first use Aldous’s tightness criteria to prove the tightness of the process
\{X^{\gamma}\}_{\gamma>0}
. This means that there exist a convergent subsequence of
\{X^{\gamma}\}_{\gamma>0}
, which will be still denoted by
\{X^{\gamma}\}_{\gamma>0}
, such that for some process
\widehat{X}
it holds

X^{\gamma}\rightharpoonup\widehat{X}
in the sense of distribution. Next we verify that the limit process
\widehat{X}
indeed satisfies (
1.7
) which is the underlying Mckean process of (
1.6
). Furthermore we can obtain the quantified convergence rate by comparing (
1
) and (
1.7
) directly, which reads

(1.10)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}W_{2}^{2}(\rho^{\gamma}_{t},\rho_{t})\leq\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}[|X^{\gamma}_{t}-X_{t}|^{2}]\leq C\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}}\,.

See Theorem
2.3
.

For readers’ convenience, we give a brief introduction of the Wasserstein metric in the following definition, we refer to
[
1
]
for more details.
Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the space of Borel probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with finite
p
-moment. We equip this space with the Wasserstein distance

(1.11)

W_{p}^{p}(\mu,\nu):=\inf\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}|z-\widehat{z}|^{p}\ d\pi(\mu,\nu)\ \big|\ \pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)\right\}

where
\Pi(\mu,\nu)
denotes the collection of all Borel probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
with marginals
\mu
and
\nu
in the first and second component respectively. The Wasserstein distance can also be expressed as

(1.12)

W_{p}^{p}(\mu,\nu)=\inf\left\{\mathbb{E}[|Z-\overline{Z}|^{p}]\right\}\,,

where the infimum is taken over all joint distributions of the random variables
Z
,
\overline{Z}
with marginals
\mu
,
\nu
respectively.
Thanks to the Kantorovich duality, in the space
\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we shall also use the following alternative representation

(1.13)

W_{1}(\mu,\nu)=\sup\left\{\big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(x)\mathrm{d}(\mu-\nu)(x)\big|:~\phi\in\mbox{Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),~\|\phi\|_{\text{Lip }}\leq 1\right\}\,,

where
\mbox{Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the space of Lipschitz continuous functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\|\phi\|_{\text{Lip }}:=\sup_{x\neq y}\frac{|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|}{|x-y|}
.

Throughout this paper we assume the external potential function
\Phi
satisfies

Assumption 1
.

The external potential function
0\leq\Phi\in\mbox{Lip}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

1.

There exists some constant
C_{\Phi}>0
such that

(1.14)

|\nabla\Phi(x)|\leq C_{\Phi}(1+|x|)\quad\mbox{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad\mbox{ and }\left\lVert\nabla\Phi\,\right\rVert_{\mbox{Lip}}\leq C_{\Phi}\,;

2.

For any
r\in[0,\infty)
:
C_{\Phi,r}:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla\Phi(x)|^{r}e^{-\Phi(x)}<\infty
.

Note that the above assumption allows us to consider both bounded and unbounded external potentials with at most quadratic growth at infinity, in particular the case
\Phi(x)=|x|^{2}/2
.

As for the interaction potential
K
, we will consider both regular and singular cases. We start with assuming
\nabla K\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap\mbox{Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
in Section 2. Then in Section 3, we assume that the potential
K\in L_{loc}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is singular, which in particular includes the case
|K(x)|\leq\frac{C}{|x|^{\alpha}}
with
1\leq\alpha<d-2
, and the Newtonian potential case
\nabla K(x)=\pm\frac{x}{|x|^{d}}
. In the singular case we need higher-order regularity of solutions
f^{\gamma}
. Firstly we need
f^{\gamma}
to be more than just a measure but a density function. Secondly, we require some uniform in
\gamma
estimates of
f^{\gamma}
in weighted Sobolev space
W_{x,H}^{k,p}
, which will be collected from
[
10
]
.

2.
Regular interaction potential

In this section we consider globally Lipschitz continuous and bounded interaction forces by
assuming
\nabla K\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap\mbox{Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Denote by

C_{K}:=\left\lVert\nabla K\,\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\left\lVert\nabla K\,\right\rVert_{\mbox{Lip}}<\infty\,.

Then one can easily verify that

(1)

For any
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, it holds

(2.1)

\left\lVert\nabla K\ast\mu\,\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq\left\lVert\nabla K\,\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{K}\,;

(2)

For any
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it holds

(2.2)

|F(x,\mu)-F(y,\nu)|\leq C_{F}(|x-y|+W_{2}(\mu,\nu))

with
C_{F}:=\left\lVert\nabla K\,\right\rVert_{\mbox{Lip}}+\left\lVert\nabla\Phi\,\right\rVert_{\mbox{Lip}}
.

Under the above regular assumptions on
K
, standard result, see for example
[
33
]
, gives the well-posedness of the Mckean processes (
1.5
) and (
1.7
)

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\nabla K\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap\operatorname{Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. For each
T>0
, there hold the following assertions.

(i) If
(X_{0},V_{0})
is distributed according to
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, then for each
\gamma>0
, the nonlinear SDE (
1.5
) admits a unique solution up to time
T
with the initial data
(X_{0},V_{0})
and it holds further that

(2.3)

\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\left[|X^{\gamma}_{t}|^{2}+|V^{\gamma}_{t}|^{2}\right]\leq C\mathbb{E}\left[|X_{0}|^{2}+|V_{0}|^{2}\right]\,,

where
C
depends only on
C_{K},C_{\Phi},\gamma
and
T
.

(ii) If
X_{0}
is distributed according to
\rho_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then SDE (
1.7
) admits a unique solution up to time
T
with the initial data
X_{0}
and it holds further that

(2.4)

\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\left[|X_{t}|^{2}\right]\leq C\mathbb{E}\left[|X_{0}|^{2}\right]\,,

where
C
depends only on
C_{K},C_{\Phi}
and
T
.

The proof of the overdamped limit will proceed in two steps:

•

We prove a tightness result for the sequence of probability distributions
\{\rho^{\gamma}\}_{\gamma>0}
of
\{X^{\gamma}\}_{\gamma>0}
by using Aldous’s tightness criteria.

•

We will check that all the limit points of
\{X^{\gamma}\}_{\gamma>0}
as
\gamma\to\infty
satisfy the Mckean process (
1.7
) underlying the aggregation-diffusion equation (
1.6
).

For the sake of completeness, let us recall a result from the Aldous criteria
[
27
, Theorem 4.5]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\{X^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of random variables defined on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
and valued in
\mathcal{C}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
. The sequence of probability distributions
\{\mu_{X^{n}}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\{X^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is tight on
\mathcal{C}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
if the following hold.

(Con1)
For all
t\geq 0
,
\{\mu_{X_{t}^{n}}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
the set of distributions of
\{X_{t}^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is tight in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

(Con2)
For all
\varepsilon>0
,
\eta>0
, there exists
\delta_{0}>0
and
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n\geq n_{0}
and for all discrete-valued
\sigma(X^{n}_{s};s\in[0,T])
-stopping times
\beta
such that
0\leq\beta+\delta_{0}\leq T
,

(2.5)

\sup_{\delta\in[0,\delta_{0}]}\mathbb{P}\left(|X^{n}_{\beta+\delta}-X^{n}_{\beta}|\geq\eta\right)\leq\varepsilon\,.

Theorem 2.2

(Tightness)
.

For any
\gamma>0
and
T>0
, let
(X^{\gamma}_{t},V^{\gamma}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
satisfy the system (
1.5
) up to time
T
with
\nabla K\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap\operatorname{Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\Psi
satisfying Assumption
1
, and the initial data
\operatorname{Law}(X_{0},V_{0})=f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
. Then for each countable subsequence
\{\gamma_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
with
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\gamma_{k}=\infty
, the sequence of probability distributions
\{\rho^{\gamma_{k}}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\{X^{\gamma_{k}}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is tight on
\mathcal{C}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof.

We apply the Aldous criteria in Lemma
2.1
to the system
\{X^{\gamma}\}_{\gamma>0}
by verifying conditions
(Con1)
and
(Con2)
.

\bullet

Step 1: Checking
(Con1)
.

Let us recall (
1
) and use Fubini’s theorem (see
[
13
, Theorem 4.33]
for the stochastic version), then we have

\displaystyle X_{t}^{\gamma}

\displaystyle=X_{0}+\gamma\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\gamma^{2}\tau}V_{0}d\tau+\gamma^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-\gamma^{2}(\tau-s)}F(X_{s}^{\gamma},\rho_{s}^{\gamma})dsd\tau+\sqrt{2}\gamma^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-\gamma^{2}(\tau-s)}dB_{s}d\tau

\displaystyle=X_{0}+\gamma\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\gamma^{2}\tau}V_{0}d\tau+\gamma^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{s}^{t}e^{-\gamma^{2}(\tau-s)}d\tau F(X_{s}^{\gamma},\rho_{s}^{\gamma})ds+\sqrt{2}\gamma^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{s}^{t}e^{-\gamma^{2}(\tau-s)}d\tau dB_{s}

(2.6)

\displaystyle=X_{0}+\frac{1}{\gamma}(1-e^{-\gamma^{2}t})V_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}(1-e^{-\gamma^{2}(t-s)})F(X_{s}^{\gamma},\rho_{s}^{\gamma})ds+\sqrt{2}\int_{0}^{t}(1-e^{-\gamma^{2}(t-s)})dB_{s}\,.

For
\gamma\geq 1
, it follows from Hölder’s inequality that

|X_{t}^{\gamma}|^{2}\leq 4|X_{0}|^{2}+4|V_{0}|^{2}+2T\int_{0}^{t}|F(X_{s}^{\gamma},\rho_{s}^{\gamma})|^{2}ds+8\left|\int_{0}^{t}(1-e^{-\gamma^{2}(t-s)})dB_{s}\right|^{2}\,.

Here we have used the fact that for any sequence
\{a_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\geq 0
, one has

(2.7)

(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i})^{2}\leq n\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\,.

Using Itô’s isometry yields that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{t}(1-e^{-\gamma^{2}(t-s)})dB_{s}\right|^{2}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}|(1-e^{-\gamma^{2}(t-s)})|^{2}ds\right]\leq T\,.

Thus we have

(2.8)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|X_{t}^{\gamma}|^{2}]\leq 4\mathbb{E}[|X_{0}|^{2}]+4\mathbb{E}[|V_{0}|^{2}]+2T\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}[|F(X_{s}^{\gamma},\rho_{s}^{\gamma})|^{2}]ds+8T\,.

It follows from (
1.14
) and (
2.1
) that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|F(X_{s}^{\gamma},\rho_{s}^{\gamma})|^{2}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[|\nabla\Phi(X_{s}^{\gamma})+\nabla K\ast\rho^{\gamma}_{s}(X_{s}^{\gamma})|^{2}]\leq 2\mathbb{E}[|\nabla\Phi(X_{s}^{\gamma})|^{2}]+2C_{K}^{2}

(2.9)

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}[|C_{\Phi}(1+|X_{s}^{\gamma}|)|^{2}]+2C_{K}^{2}\leq 4C_{\Phi}^{2}+2C_{K}^{2}+4C_{\Phi}^{2}\mathbb{E}[|X_{s}^{\gamma}|^{2}]\,,

which leads to

(2.10)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|X_{t}^{\gamma}|^{2}]\leq 4\mathbb{E}[|X_{0}|^{2}]+4\mathbb{E}[|V_{0}|^{2}]+8C_{\Phi}^{2}T\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}[|X_{s}^{\gamma}|^{2}]ds+2T(4C_{\Phi}^{2}+2C_{K}^{2})+8T\,.

Using Gronwall’s inequality leads to

(2.11)

\mathbb{E}[|X_{t}^{\gamma}|^{2}]\leq\left(4\mathbb{E}[|X_{0}|^{2}]+4\mathbb{E}[|V_{0}|^{2}]+2T(4C_{\Phi}^{2}+2C_{K}^{2})+8T\right)\exp\left(8C_{\Phi}^{2}T^{2}\right),\quad t\in[0,T]\,.

This implies that

(2.12)

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}[|X_{t}^{\gamma}|^{2}]\leq C(\mathbb{E}[|X_{0}|^{2}],\mathbb{E}[|V_{0}|^{2}],C_{\Phi},C_{K},T)=:C_{1}

where
C_{1}>0
is a constant independent of
\gamma
. So for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a compact subset
K_{\varepsilon}:=\{x:~|x|^{2}\leq\frac{C_{1}}{\varepsilon}\}
such that by Markov’s inequality

(2.13)

\rho_{t}^{\gamma}((K_{\varepsilon})^{c})=\mathbb{P}(|X_{t}^{\gamma}|^{2}>\frac{C_{1}}{\varepsilon})\leq\frac{\varepsilon\mathbb{E}[|X_{t}^{\gamma}|^{2}]}{C_{1}}\leq\varepsilon,\quad\forall~\gamma\geq 1\,.

This means that for all
t\in[0,T]
, each countable subset of
\{\rho_{t}^{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\geq 1}
is tight in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, which verifies condition
(Con1)
in Lemma
2.1
.

\bullet

Step 2: Checking
(Con2)
.
Let
\beta
be a
\sigma(X^{\gamma}_{s};s\in[0,T])
-stopping time with discrete values such that
\beta+\delta_{0}\leq T
.
Let us recall (
1
) and compute

\displaystyle X_{\beta+\delta}^{\gamma}-X_{\beta}^{\gamma}=\int_{\beta}^{\beta+\delta}V_{\tau}d\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma\int_{\beta}^{\beta+\delta}e^{-\gamma^{2}\tau}V_{0}d\tau+\gamma^{2}\int_{\beta}^{\beta+\delta}\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-\gamma^{2}(\tau-s)}F(X_{s}^{\gamma},\rho_{s}^{\gamma})dsd\tau+\sqrt{2}\gamma^{2}\int_{\beta}^{\beta+\delta}\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-\gamma^{2}(\tau-s)}dB_{s}d\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma\int_{\beta}^{\beta+\delta}e^{-\gamma^{2}\tau}V_{0}d\tau+\gamma^{2}\int_{0}^{\beta}\int_{\beta}^{\beta+\delta}e^{-\gamma^{2}(\tau-s)}d\tau F(X_{s}^{\gamma},\rho_{s}^{\gamma})ds+\gamma^{2}\int_{\beta}^{\beta+\delta}\int_{s}^{\beta+\delta}e^{-\gamma^{2}(\tau-s)}d\tau F(X_{s}^{\gamma},\rho_{s}^{\gamma})ds

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt+\sqrt{2}\gamma^{2}\int_{0}^{\beta}\int_{\beta}^{\beta+\delta}e^{-\gamma^{2}(\tau-s)}d\tau dB_{s}+\sqrt{2}\gamma^{2}\int_{\beta}^{\beta+\delta}\int_{s}^{\beta+\delta}e^{-\gamma^{2}(\tau-s)}d\tau dB_{s}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\gamma}(e^{-\gamma^{2}\beta}-e^{-\gamma^{2}(\beta+\delta)})V_{0}+\int_{0}^{\beta}(e^{-\gamma^{2}(\beta-s)}-e^{-\gamma^{2}(\beta+\delta-s)})F(X_{s}^{\gamma},\rho_{s}^{\gamma})ds+\int_{\beta}^{\beta+\delta}(1-e^{-\gamma^{2}(\beta+\delta-s)})F(X_{s}^{\gamma},\rho_{s}^{\gamma})ds

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt+\sqrt{2}(e^{-\gamma^{2}\beta}-e^{-\gamma^{2}(\beta+\delta)})\int_{0}^{\beta}e^{\gamma^{2}s}dB_{s}+\sqrt{2}\int_{\beta}^{\beta+\delta}(1-e^{-\gamma^{2}(\beta+\delta-s)})dB_{s}\,.

Note here that the multiplier

Notice that it holds

and

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}}\left(1-e^{-\gamma^{2}\delta}\right)-\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}}\left(e^{-\gamma^{2}\tau}-e^{-\gamma^{2}(\tau+\delta)}\right)\leq\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}}\cdot(\gamma^{2}\delta)^{\xi}=(\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}})^{1-\xi}\delta^{\xi},

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\beta}|e^{-\gamma^{2}(\beta-s)}-e^{-\gamma^{2}(\beta+\delta-s)}|^{2}ds\int_{0}^{\beta}|F(X_{s}^{\gamma},\rho_{s}^{\gamma})|^{2}ds\right]\leq\delta\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}[|F(X_{s}^{\gamma},\rho_{s}^{\gamma})|^{2}]ds\,,

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|\int_{\beta}^{\beta+\delta}(1-e^{-\gamma^{2}(\beta+\delta-s)})F(X_{s}^{\gamma},\rho_{s}^{\gamma})ds|^{2}\right]\leq\delta\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{\beta}^{\beta+\delta}|F(X_{s}^{\gamma},\rho_{s}^{\gamma})|^{2}ds\right]\leq\delta\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[|F(X_{s}^{\gamma},\rho_{s}^{\gamma})|^{2}\right]ds\,.

Particularly, let us look at

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\Big|Z^{\gamma,\delta}_{t}\Big|^{2}dt\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}(e^{-\gamma^{2}t}-e^{-\gamma^{2}(t+\delta)})e^{\gamma^{2}s}dB_{s}\Big|^{2}dt\right]

(2.18)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{t}|(e^{-\gamma^{2}t}-e^{-\gamma^{2}(t+\delta)})e^{\gamma^{2}s}|^{2}dsdt\leq C\delta

where we have used Itô’s isometry and the estimate (

) with

and the constant

\gamma
and

. Thus, the process

is a integrable continuous martingale; indeed, Doob’s martingale inequality gives

(2.19)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{t\in[0,T]}\left|M^{\gamma,\delta}_{t}\right|\right]\leq C\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left|(Z^{\gamma,\delta}_{s})^{\prime}\right|ds\right]\leq C\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left|Z^{\gamma,\delta}_{s}\right|^{2}ds\right]\right)^{1/2}\leq C\delta^{1/2},

with

being independent of
\gamma
and
\delta
. On the other hand, it is easy to see that

satisfies the following SDE

By Itô-Doeblin formula, it holds that
for all

\displaystyle|Z^{\gamma,\delta}_{t}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\Big|(e^{-\gamma^{2}(t-s)}-e^{-\gamma^{2}(t+\delta-s)})\Big|^{2}ds+2\int_{0}^{t}e^{-2\gamma^{2}(t-s)}(1-e^{-\gamma^{2}\delta})dM^{\gamma,\delta}_{s}

\displaystyle\leq\delta+2(1-e^{-\gamma^{2}\delta})M^{\gamma,\delta}_{t}-4\int_{0}^{t}\gamma^{2}e^{-2\gamma^{2}(t-s)}(1-e^{-\gamma^{2}\delta})M^{\gamma,\delta}_{s}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\delta+2\left|M^{\gamma,\delta}_{t}\right|+4\gamma^{2}\max_{s\in[0,T]}\left|M^{\gamma,\delta}_{s}\right|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-2\gamma^{2}(t-s)}\,ds\leq\delta+4\max_{s\in[0,T]}\left|M^{\gamma,\delta}_{s}\right|,\quad\text{a.s.,}

where the integration by parts formula is applied to the stochastic integral in the first line and in the second inequality, we used estimate (
2
) with

equal to

. Combined with (

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\Big|Z^{\gamma,\delta}_{\beta}\Big|^{2}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{t\in[0,T]}\Big|Z^{\gamma,\delta}_{t}\Big|^{2}\right]\leq C(\delta^{1/2}+\delta),

where the constant

is independent of
\beta,\gamma,
and
\delta
.

Thus we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|X^{\gamma}_{\beta+\delta}-X^{\gamma}_{\beta}|^{2}]\leq 5\delta\mathbb{E}[|X_{0}|^{2}]+10\delta\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}[|F(X_{s}^{\gamma},\rho_{s}^{\gamma})|^{2}]ds+10\delta+10C(\delta^{1/2}+\delta)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 5\delta\mathbb{E}[|X_{0}|^{2}]+10\delta\left(4C_{\Phi}^{2}T+2C_{K}^{2}T+4C_{\Phi}^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}[|X_{s}^{\gamma}|^{2}]ds\right)+10\delta+10C(\delta^{1/2}+\delta)\,,

where we have used (

Recalling the fact that for all

it easy to see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|X^{\gamma}_{\beta+\delta}-X^{\gamma}_{\beta}|^{2}]\leq C(\mathbb{E}[|X_{0}|^{2}],\mathbb{E}[|V_{0}|^{2}],C_{\Phi},C_{K},T)(\delta+\delta^{\frac{1}{2}})\,.

Hence for any

\displaystyle X_{t}^{\gamma_{k}}=X_{0}+\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}}(1-e^{-\gamma_{k}^{2}t})V_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}(1-e^{-\gamma_{k}^{2}(t-s)})F(X_{s}^{\gamma_{k}},\rho_{s}^{\gamma_{k}})ds+\sqrt{2}\int_{0}^{t}(1-e^{-\gamma_{k}^{2}(t-s)})dB_{s}\,.

, then

\displaystyle|\nabla K\ast(\rho^{\gamma}-\rho)(X)|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{(X-y)\in B_{R}}\nabla K(X-y)(\rho^{\gamma}-\rho)(y)dy\right|+\left|\int_{(X-y)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash B_{R}}\nabla K(X-y)(\rho^{\gamma}-\rho)(y)dy\right|

(3.16)

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{(X-y)\in B_{R}}\nabla K(X-y)(\rho^{\gamma}-\rho)(y)dy\right|+\left\lVert\nabla K\,\right\rVert_{\mbox{Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash B_{R})}W_{1}(\rho^{\gamma},\rho)\,,

where we have used the Kantorovich duality (
1.13
) in the second inequality. Notice that according to the estimate
(2)
in
[
10
, Theorem 3.3]
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{(X-y)\in B_{R}}\nabla K(X-y)(\rho^{\gamma}-\rho)(y)dy\right|^{2}\right]\leq C_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\int_{(x-y)\in B_{R}}\nabla K(x-y)(\rho^{\gamma}-\rho)(y)dy\right|^{2}dx

(3.17)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\infty}\left\lVert\nabla^{2}K\,\right\rVert_{L^{1}(B_{2R})}^{2}W_{2}^{2}(\rho^{\gamma},\rho)\,.

Thus we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|\nabla K\ast(\rho^{\gamma}-\rho)(X)|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{\infty}\left\lVert\nabla^{2}K\,\right\rVert_{L^{1}(B_{2R})}^{2}W_{2}^{2}(\rho^{\gamma},\rho)+2\left\lVert\nabla K\,\right\rVert_{\mbox{Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash B_{R})}^{2}W_{1}^{2}(\rho^{\gamma},\rho)

\displaystyle\leq\left(2C_{\infty}\left\lVert\nabla^{2}K\,\right\rVert_{L^{1}(B_{2R})}^{2}+2C_{K}^{\prime 2}\right)W_{2}^{2}(\rho^{\gamma},\rho)

(3.18)

\displaystyle\leq\left(2C_{\infty}\left\lVert\nabla^{2}K\,\right\rVert_{L^{1}(B_{2R})}^{2}+2C_{K}^{\prime 2}\right)\mathbb{E}[|X^{\gamma}-X|^{2}]\,.

Then it follows from (
3
) that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|\nabla K\ast\rho^{\gamma}(X^{\gamma})-\nabla K\ast\rho(X)|^{2}]

(3.19)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\left\lVert\nabla K\ast\rho^{\gamma}\,\right\rVert_{W^{1,\infty}}\mathbb{E}[|X^{\gamma}-X|^{2}]+\left(4C_{\infty}\left\lVert\nabla^{2}K\,\right\rVert_{L^{1}(B_{2R})}^{2}+4C_{K}^{\prime 2}\right)\mathbb{E}[|X^{\gamma}-X|^{2}]\,,

which proves (
3.13
).

If
K
satisfies Assumption
2
and it is given by Newtonian potential, according to
(3)
in
[
10
, Theorem 3.3]
one has

(3.20)

\left\lVert\nabla K\ast(\rho^{\gamma}-\rho)\,\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C_{\infty}W_{2}^{2}(\rho^{\gamma},\rho)\leq C_{\infty}\mathbb{E}[|X^{\gamma}-X|^{2}]\,.

Then taking expectation on both sides of (
3
) leads to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|\nabla K\ast\rho^{\gamma}(X^{\gamma})-\nabla K\ast\rho(X)|^{2}]\leq 2\left\lVert\nabla K\ast\rho^{\gamma}\,\right\rVert_{W^{1,\infty}}\mathbb{E}[|X^{\gamma}-X|^{2}]+2\mathbb{E}[|\nabla K\ast(\rho^{\gamma}-\rho)(X)|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\left\lVert\nabla K\ast\rho^{\gamma}\,\right\rVert_{W^{1,\infty}}\mathbb{E}[|X^{\gamma}-X|^{2}]+2C_{\infty}\left\lVert\nabla K\ast(\rho^{\gamma}-\rho)\,\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

(3.21)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(2\left\lVert\nabla K\ast\rho^{\gamma}\,\right\rVert_{W^{1,\infty}}+2C_{\infty}\right)\mathbb{E}[|X^{\gamma}-X|^{2}]\,.

This yields (
3.14
).
∎

Now we can prove the boundness of the second moment uniformly in
\gamma
. In the following we shall use
C>0
to denote a generic constant independent of
\gamma
even though it is different from line to line.

Theorem 3.2
.

Assume that
\Phi
satisfy Assumption
1
and
K
satisfy Assumption
2
–
3
. Let
(X^{\gamma}_{t},V^{\gamma}_{t})_{t\in[0,T_{p}]}
be the unique solution to (
1.5
) up to time
T_{p}
with the initial data
\mbox{Law}(X_{0},V_{0})=f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}\cap W_{x,H}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
,
p>\max\{d,q/(q-1)\}
. It holds that

(3.22)

\sup_{\gamma\geq 1}\sup_{t\in[0,T_{p}]}\mathbb{E}[|X_{t}^{\gamma}|^{2}]<\infty\,.

Proof.

It follows from the same argument as in the Step 1 of Theorem
2.2
that

(3.23)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|X_{t}^{\gamma}|^{2}]\leq C+C\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}[|F(X_{s}^{\gamma},\rho_{s}^{\gamma})|^{2}]ds\,,

where
C
is independent of
\gamma
.
It follows from (
1.14
) and (
3.7
) that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|F(X_{s}^{\gamma},\rho_{s}^{\gamma})|^{2}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[|\nabla\Phi(X_{s}^{\gamma})+\nabla K\ast\rho^{\gamma}_{s}(X_{s}^{\gamma})|^{2}]\leq 2\mathbb{E}[|\nabla\Phi(X_{s}^{\gamma})|^{2}]+C

(3.24)

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}[|C_{\Phi}(1+|X_{s}^{\gamma}|)|^{2}]+C\leq C+C\mathbb{E}[|X_{s}^{\gamma}|^{2}]\,,

which leads to

(3.25)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|X_{t}^{\gamma}|^{2}]\leq C+C\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}[|X_{s}^{\gamma}|^{2}]ds\,.

Applying Gronwall’s inequality completes the proof.
∎

Using the theorem above we obtain the overdamped limit for the singular case:

Theorem 3.3
.

3.2
. Let
(X^{\gamma}_{t},V^{\gamma}_{t})_{t\in[0,T_{p}]}

(X_{t})_{t\in[0,T_{p}]}

(3.26)

where

is independent of
\gamma\geq 1
.

Proof.

(3.27)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|X^{\gamma}_{t}-X_{t}|^{2}]\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}[|F(X^{\gamma}_{s},\rho^{\gamma}_{s})-F(X_{s},\rho_{s})|^{2}]ds+C\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}}\,.

Applying assumption (

3.2

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|F(X^{\gamma}_{s},\rho^{\gamma}_{s})-F(X_{s},\rho_{s})|^{2}]

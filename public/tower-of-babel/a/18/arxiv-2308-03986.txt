Abstract
The rapid advancements in high-dimensional statistics and machine learning have increased the use of first-order methods. Many of these methods can be regarded as instances of the proximal point algorithm. Given the importance of the proximal point algorithm, there has been growing interest in developing its accelerated variants. However, some existing accelerated proximal point algorithms exhibit oscillatory behavior, which can impede their numerical convergence rate. In this paper, we first introduce an ODE system and demonstrate its o ⁡ ( 1 / t 2 ) o(1/t^{2}) convergence rate and weak convergence property. Next, we apply the Symplectic Euler Method to discretize the ODE and obtain a new accelerated proximal point algorithm, which we call the Symplectic Proximal Point Algorithm. The reason for using the Symplectic Euler Method is its ability to preserve the geometric structure of the ODEs. Theoretically, we demonstrate that the Symplectic Proximal Point Algorithm achieves an o ⁡ ( 1 / k 2 ) o(1/k^{2}) convergence rate and that the sequences generated by our method converge weakly to the solution set. Practically, our numerical experiments illustrate that the Symplectic Proximal Point Algorithm significantly reduces oscillatory behavior, leading to improved long-time behavior and faster numerical convergence rate.

Introduction

With the rapid advancements of high-dimensional statistics and machine learning, first-order methods have become widely utilized for training models. Many of these first-order methods have been found to be closely related to a fundamental root-finding algorithm, known as the proximal point algorithm (PPA). For example, the augmented Lagrangian method (ALM), the alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM), and the Chambolle-Pock method (also known as the primal-dual hybrid gradient method, PDHG) have been proven to be instances of PPA, as discussed in
[
35
,
18
,
14
,
15
,
31
]
. In addition, several first-order methods for solving variational inequality problem, such as the extra-gradient method and the optimistic gradient descent ascent method, have also been found to be closely related to PPA, as detailed in
[
29
]
.

The convergence rate of PPA has been proven to be
O(1/k)

[
6
]
, which can lead to slow numerical performance in practical applications. Therefore, accelerating the PPA is crucial for improving its practical efficiency. Nesterov’s accelerated gradient method (NAG), which demonstrates an
O(1/k^{2})
convergence rate and has the same computational complexity as vanilla gradient descent, has inspired research into accelerated variants of the PPA. Recently, an accelerated technique known as Halpern’s iteration
[
21
]
has gained increasing attention.
[
28
]
and
[
34
]
have shown that the convergence rate of Halpern’s iteration is
O(1/k^{2})
. Additionally,
[
23
]
introduced a general analytical framework for analyzing the PPA and demonstrated the
O(1/k^{2})
convergence rate of Halpern’s iteration. The work in
[
41
]
also studied the connection between Halpern’s iteration and the NAG method. Due to the fascinating theoretical results of Halpern’s iteration, the study of its applications has grown. The works
[
45
,
27
]
used Halpern’s iteration to develop new algorithms for solving specific optimization problems. Furthermore, since
[
29
]
suggested that the extra-gradient method and the optimistic gradient descent ascent method are closely related to the PPA, Halpern’s iteration has been employed to accelerate these methods, as shown in
[
13
,
24
,
33
,
42
,
43
]
.

With the growing research on the connections between optimization algorithms and ordinary differential equations (ODEs), deriving new accelerated PPA frameworks has become feasible. Initially,
[
39
]
linked the NAG method with a second-order ODE and introduced the Lyapunov function technique to demonstrate the
O(1/k^{2})
convergence rate for the NAG method. Recent studies have increasingly utilized Lyapunov functions to establish the convergence properties of optimization algorithms, as exemplified in
[
3
,
16
,
25
,
38
]
. Given the theoretical depth of ODEs, several studies have focused on deriving accelerated versions of the PPA from the discretization of ODEs. In recent work
[
1
,
2
,
4
,
11
]
, researchers first proposed several second-order ODEs and then studied convergence properties of these ODEs. By applying the explicit or implicit difference schemes to these continuous ODEs for discretization, they successfully developed corresponding accelerated PPA variants.

1.1
Existing Results

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a real Hilbert space equipped with the inner product
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
and the corresponding norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|
and let
A:\mathcal{H}\to 2^{\mathcal{H}}
be a set-valued operator operator.
A
is monotone if the following inequality holds:

\left\langle u-v,x-y\right\rangle\geqslant 0,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{H},u\in A(x),v\in A(y).

The monotone operator
A
is maximal if the graph of
A
, defined as
\{(x,u)\in\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}|u\in A(x)\}
, is not properly included by the graph of other monotone operator.

In this paper, we consider the following monotone inclusion problem:

0\in A(x),

(1)

where
A
is maximally monotone. Let
\mathrm{zero}(A):=\{x\in\mathcal{H}|0\in A(x)\}
be the solution set of (
1
), assuming that
\mathrm{zero}(A)
is non-empty. The PPA for solving (
1
) is given as follows:

x_{k+1}=J_{cA}(x_{k}),\quad c>0,

(2)

which can be interpreted as applying Banach-Picard iteration to the proximal point operator
J_{cA}:=(I+cA)^{-1}
. The parameter
c
refers to the index of the proximal point operator
J_{cA}
. Here we assume that
J_{cA}
can be easily calculated for some specific
c
. For simplicity, we assume
c=1
for the rest of this paper. The convergence rate of (
2
) is

\left\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\|^{2}\leqslant\frac{1}{1+k}\cdot\mathrm{dist}(x_{0},\mathrm{zero}(A))^{2},

where
\mathrm{dist}(x_{0},\mathrm{zero}(A))=\inf\limits_{x\in\mathrm{zero}(A)}\left\|x_{0}-x\right\|
. We refer to the lecture note
[
6
]
and the textbooks
[
8
]
and
[
36
]
for the proof of above result. The term
\left\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\|^{2}
can be used to characterize convergence rate of PPA is due to the property
x_{k}-x_{k+1}\in A(x_{k+1})
. Recently,
[
20
]
showed a tighter rate of PPA:

\left\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\|^{2}\leqslant\cfrac{1}{(k+1)\left(1+\cfrac{1}{k}\right)^{k}}\cdot\mathrm{dist}(x_{0},\mathrm{zero}(A))^{2}.

The convergence of the sequence generated by the PPA is also of concern. In this paper, the notation
x_{k}\rightharpoonup x
denotes that the sequence
\{x_{k}\}
converges weakly to
x
, and the notation
x_{k}\to x
represents that the sequence
\{x_{k}\}
converges strongly to
x
. The weak convergence property of the PPA relies on the following propositions:

Proposition 1
.

Let
\{x_{k}\}
be a sequence in
\mathcal{H}
and let
S
be a nonempty subset of
\mathcal{H}
. Suppose that, for every
x\in S
,
\left\|x_{k}-x\right\|
converges and that every weak sequential cluster point of
\{x_{k}\}
belongs to
S
. Then
\{x_{k}\}
converges weakly to a point in
S
.

Proposition
1
can be seen as a discrete version of Opial’s Lemma
[
30
]
.

Proposition 2
.

Let
X(t):[t_{0},+\infty)\to\mathcal{H}
and let
S
be a nonempty subset of
\mathcal{H}
. Suppose that, for every
x\in S
,
\left\|X(t)-x\right\|
converges and that every weak sequential cluster point of
X(t)
belongs to
S
. Then
X(t)
converges weakly to a point in
S
.

Proposition 3
.

Let
A
be a maximally monotone operator. If there exist two sequences
\{x_{k}\}
and
\{u_{k}\}
such that
u_{k}\in A(x_{k})
,
x_{k}\rightharpoonup x
, and
u_{k}\to u
, then
u\in A(x)
.

Proposition
1
and Proposition
3
correspond to Lemma 2.47 and Proposition 20.38 (ii) in
[
8
]
. One can prove the weak convergence property of the PPA by using Proposition
1
and Proposition
3
, along with the facts that
x_{k}-x_{k+1}\in A(x_{k+1})
and
x_{k}-x_{k+1}\to 0
. For further theory and applications of the PPA, we refer to
[
8
,
17
,
32
,
36
]
.

Let
T:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
be a
1-
Lipschitz operator. The Halpern’s iteration proposed in
[
21
]
can be described as follows:

x_{k+1}=\alpha_{k}x_{0}+(1-\alpha_{k})T(x_{k}),\quad\alpha_{k}\in(0,1).

(3)

By setting
\alpha_{k}=\dfrac{1}{k+2}
,
T=2J_{A}-I
, and omitting
x_{0}
from the iteration rule, we can derive the following accelerated proximal point algorithm from (
3
):

\begin{split}y_{k+1}&=J_{A}(x_{k}),\\
x_{k+1}&=y_{k+1}+\frac{k}{k+2}(y_{k+1}-y_{k})-\frac{k}{k+2}(y_{k}-x_{k-1}),\end{split}

(4)

where
y_{0}=x_{0}
. In
[
23
]
and
[
36
]
, the convergence rate of (
4
) has been proven to be

\left\|y_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\|^{2}\leqslant\frac{1}{(k+1)^{2}}\cdot\mathrm{dist}(x_{0},\mathrm{zero}(A))^{2}.

Also by Theorem 30.1 in
[
8
]
, the sequence
\{x_{k}\}
generated by (
4
) strongly converges to the projection of
x_{0}
onto
\mathrm{zero}(A)
.

Nowadays, there are several accelerated variants of the PPA derived from ODEs. Initially,
[
4
,
1
,
2
]
used the Yosida approximation of
A
to propose some second-order ODEs, and then obtained accelerated PPAs by properly discretizing these ODEs. However, the index of the proximal point operator in the resulting accelerated PPAs changes as iterations progress, which may limit their applications in deriving some accelerated convex optimization algorithms (see
[
18
]
for discussion). More recently,
[
11
]
proposed a new accelerated PPA framework. They were inspired by
[
12
]
and studied the following ODE:

\overset{\cdot\cdot}{X}+\frac{\alpha}{t}\overset{\cdot}{X}+\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(I-J_{A})+\frac{\alpha}{2t}(I-J_{A})=0,

where
\alpha>2
is a constant. Then, they used the explicit difference scheme to discretize the above ODE and obtained the following Fast K-M algorithm:

\begin{split}x_{k+1}=&\ \left(1-\frac{s\alpha}{2(k+\alpha)}\right)x_{k}+\frac{(1-s)k}{k+\alpha}(x_{k}-x_{k-1})\\
&\ +\frac{s\alpha}{2(k+\alpha)}J_{A}(x_{k})+\frac{sk}{k+\alpha}(J_{A}(x_{k})-J_{A}(x_{k-1})),\end{split}

(5)

where
s>0
is a constant. They proved that the convergence rate of (
5
) is
o(1/k^{2})
and the sequence
\{x_{k}\}
generated by (
5
) converges weakly to a point in
\mathrm{zero}(A)
.

1.2
Our Motivations and Contributions

First, we introduce the motivation for our study. In
[
11
]
, numerical experiments showed that (
5
) suffers from oscillation phenomenon, which may slow down the numerical convergence rate of (
5
). Based on this observation, we develop the
Symplectic Proximal Point Algorithm (SPPA)
. The details of this development are as follows.

First, we need to construct an ODE system and analyze its convergence properties, which will provide insight into the desired algorithm. To obtain an ODE that exhibits an
o(1/t^{2})
convergence rate, we draw inspiration from the ODEs in
[
11
]
and
[
44
]
. Based on these insights, we propose the following ODE:

\begin{split}Z&=\frac{t}{r}\dot{X}+\left(1+\frac{t}{r}\right)A(X)+X,\\
\dot{Z}&=-\frac{C}{r}A(X),\\
Z(0)&=X(0)=x_{0},\end{split}

(6)

where
r
and
C
are two positive constants. Here,
A
is assumed to be single-valued and
A(X)
is differentiable with respect to
t
. We show that the convergence rate of the solution trajectory to (
6
) is
o(1/t^{2})
, and that the solution trajectory converges weakly to
\mathrm{zero}(A)
.

Next, we need to discretize (
6
). The discretization method we used is the
Symplectic Euler Method
, which is why we refer to our method as the Symplectic Proximal Point Algorithm. Our motivation for using the Symplectic Euler Method comes from the work in
[
38
,
37
]
, where the authors showed that the NAG method can be viewed as applying the symplectic method to high-resolution ODEs formulated in phase space representation. Since the Symplectic Euler Method can preserve geometric structure of the ODEs and has a simple iteration rule, we use the Symplectic Euler Method to derive our algorithm.

First, we briefly introduce the Symplectic Euler Method. Consider the following Hamiltonian system:

\begin{split}\dot{p}&=-\frac{\partial H(p,q)}{\partial q},\\
\dot{q}&=\frac{\partial H(p,q)}{\partial p}.\end{split}

(7)

The Symplectic Euler Method is given as follows:

\begin{split}p_{k+1}&=p_{k}-s\frac{\partial H(p_{k},q_{k+1})}{\partial q},\\
q_{k+1}&=q_{k}+s\frac{\partial H(p_{k},q_{k+1})}{\partial p},\end{split}\quad\text{or}\quad\begin{split}p_{k+1}&=p_{k}-s\frac{\partial H(p_{k+1},q_{k})}{\partial q},\\
q_{k+1}&=q_{k}+s\frac{\partial H(p_{k+1},q_{k})}{\partial p}.\end{split}

(8)

For more information about symplectic methods, we refer to the textbooks
[
19
,
22
]
. An introduction to the Symplectic Euler Method can be found in Section I.1.2 of
[
19
]
.

Figure 1:
An example illustrates that the Symplectic Euler Method can preserve geometric structure of the ODEs. Here, we apply the Explicit Euler Method, Symplectic Euler Method, and Implicit Euler Method to the Hamiltonian system
\dot{p}=q
,
\dot{q}=-p
with initial condition
p(0)=0
and
q(0)=1
. The exact solution to this Hamiltonian system is
p=\sin t
and
q=\cos t
. As shown in the above figures, the Symplectic Euler method preserves geometric structure of the solution.

By applying (
8
) to discretize (
6
), we obtain the following iteration rule:

\begin{split}z_{0}&=x_{0},\\
z_{k}&=\frac{k}{r}(x_{k+1}-x_{k})+\left(1+\frac{k}{r}\right)A(x_{k+1})+x_{k+1},\\
z_{k+1}&=z_{k}-\frac{C}{r}A(x_{k+1}).\end{split}

(9)

However, (
9
) requires
A
to be single-valued. Therefore, we reformulate the algorithm using the definition of proximal point operator. Specifically, the update rule of
x_{k+1}
can be transformed into:

\begin{split}\tilde{x}_{k+1}&=\frac{k}{k+r}x_{k}+\frac{r}{k+r}z_{k},\\
x_{k+1}&=J_{A}(\tilde{x}_{k+1}).\end{split}

Moreover, since
\tilde{x}_{k+1}-x_{k+1}\in A(x_{k+1})
, the update rule for
z_{k+1}
can be transformed into:

z_{k+1}=z_{k}+\frac{C}{r}(x_{k+1}-\tilde{x}_{k+1}).

The resulting algorithm is detailed in Algorithm
1
.

Initialize
x_{0},z_{0}=x_{0}
;

for

k=0,1,\cdots

do

\tilde{x}_{k+1}=\dfrac{k}{k+r}x_{k}+\dfrac{r}{k+r}z_{k}
;

x_{k+1}=J_{A}(\tilde{x}_{k+1})
;

z_{k+1}=z_{k}+\dfrac{C}{r}(x_{k+1}-\tilde{x}_{k+1})
;

Algorithm 1

Symplectic Proximal Point Algorithm, SPPA

We also demonstrate that Algorithm
1
exhibits an
o(1/k^{2})
convergence rate, and the sequences generated by Algorithm
1
converge weakly to the solution set without assuming that
A
is single-valued. Furthermore, our numerical experiments show that the oscillation phenomenon is less significant compared to some existing accelerated PPAs, and the numerical performance of SPPA is better than that of some existing accelerated PPAs.

Continuous Time Perspective

In this section, we investigate the convergence rate and weak convergence property of (

\mathcal{E}(t)=\frac{t(t+r)}{2r^{2}}\left\|A(X(t))\right\|^{2}+\frac{t}{r}\left\langle A(X(t)),X(t)-Z(t)\right\rangle+\frac{r-1}{2C}\left\|Z(t)-x^{*}\right\|^{2},

(10)

where
x^{*}\in\mathrm{zero}(A)
. For simplicity, when proving the lemma and the theorem presented in this section, we use
A(X)
,
X
, and
Z
to represent
A(X(t))
,
X(t)
, and
Z(t)
, respectively. The notations
A(X(t))
,
X(t)
and
Z(t)
are used to ensure the correct representation of our results. Next, we will examine its non-negativity and derivative.

Lemma 1
.

Let
A
be a single-valued maximally monotone operator, and let
(X(t),Z(t))
be the solution trajectory to (
6
). The Lyapunov function (
10
) can be represented as

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(t)=

\displaystyle\ \left[\left(1-\frac{C}{r-1}\right)\frac{t^{2}}{2r^{2}}+\frac{t}{2r}\right]\left\|A(X(t))\right\|^{2}+\frac{t}{r}\left\langle A(X(t)),X(t)-x^{*}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\ +\frac{1}{2}\left\|\sqrt{\frac{Ct^{2}}{r^{2}(r-1)}}A(X(t))+\sqrt{\frac{r-1}{C}}(x^{*}-Z(t))\right\|^{2}.

In addition, if
0<C\leqslant r-1
,
\mathcal{E}(t)
can be represented by a sum of three non-negative terms.

Proof.

First, we have

\frac{t}{r}\left\langle A(X),X-Z\right\rangle=\frac{t}{r}\left\langle A(X),X-x^{*}\right\rangle+\frac{t}{r}\left\langle A(X),x^{*}-Z\right\rangle.

By the equality

\left\langle a,b\right\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\left\|a+b\right\|^{2}-\left\|a\right\|^{2}-\left\|b\right\|^{2}\right),\quad\forall a,b\in\mathcal{H},

we have

\displaystyle\frac{t}{r}\left\langle A(X),x^{*}-Z\right\rangle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{2}\left\|\sqrt{\frac{Ct^{2}}{r^{2}(r-1)}}A(X)+\sqrt{\frac{r-1}{C}}(x^{*}-Z)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ -\frac{Ct^{2}}{2r^{2}(r-1)}\left\|A(X)\right\|^{2}-\frac{r-1}{2C}\left\|Z-x^{*}\right\|^{2}

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(t)=

\displaystyle\ \left[\left(1-\frac{C}{r-1}\right)\frac{t^{2}}{2r^{2}}+\frac{t}{2r}\right]\left\|A(X)\right\|^{2}+\frac{t}{r}\left\langle A(X),X-x^{*}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\ +\frac{1}{2}\left\|\sqrt{\frac{Ct^{2}}{r^{2}(r-1)}}A(X)+\sqrt{\frac{r-1}{C}}(x^{*}-Z)\right\|^{2}.

Also, if
C\leqslant r-1
,
1-\dfrac{C}{r-1}\geqslant 0
. Thus
\left(1-\dfrac{C}{r-1}\right)\dfrac{t^{2}}{2r^{2}}+\dfrac{t}{2r}\geqslant 0
. In conclusion,
\mathcal{E}(t)
can be represented by a sum of three non-negative terms.
∎

Lemma 2
.

Let
A
be a single-valued maximally monotone operator, and let
(X(t),Z(t))
be the solution trajectory to (
6
). If
A(X)
is differentiable respect to
t
, we have

\displaystyle\overset{\cdot}{\mathcal{E}}(t)=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{t^{2}}{r^{2}}\left\langle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}A(X(X(t))),\overset{\cdot}{X}(t)\right\rangle-\left(\frac{r-1-C}{r^{2}}t+1-\frac{1}{2r}\right)\left\|A(X(t))\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ -\frac{r-1}{r}\left\langle A(X(t)),X(t)-x^{*}\right\rangle.

Proof.

Direct calculation shows that

\displaystyle\overset{\cdot}{\mathcal{E}}(t)=

\displaystyle\ \frac{t(t+r)}{r^{2}}\left\langle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}A(X),A(X)\right\rangle+\frac{2t+r}{2r^{2}}\left\|A(X)\right\|^{2}+\frac{1}{r}\left\langle A(X),X-Z\right\rangle

\displaystyle\ +\frac{t}{r}\left\langle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}A(X),X-Z\right\rangle+\frac{t}{r}\left\langle A(X),\overset{\cdot}{X}-\overset{\cdot}{Z}\right\rangle+\frac{r-1}{C}\left\langle\overset{\cdot}{Z},Z-x^{*}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{t^{2}}{r^{2}}\left\langle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}A(X),\overset{\cdot}{X}\right\rangle+\frac{2t+r}{2r^{2}}\left\|A(X)\right\|^{2}+\frac{1}{r}\left\langle A(X),X-Z\right\rangle

\displaystyle\ +\left\langle A(X),Z-X-\left(1+\frac{t}{r}-\frac{Ct}{r^{2}}\right)A(X)\right\rangle-\frac{r-1}{r}\left\langle A(X),Z-x^{*}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{t^{2}}{r^{2}}\left\langle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}A(X),\overset{\cdot}{X}\right\rangle-\left(\frac{r-1-C}{r^{2}}t+1-\frac{1}{2r}\right)\left\|A(X)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ -\frac{r-1}{r}\left\langle A(X),X-x^{*}\right\rangle.

∎

.

(11)

\displaystyle\left[\left(1-\frac{C}{r-1}\right)\frac{t^{2}}{2r^{2}}+\frac{t}{2r}\right]\left\|A(X)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathcal{E}(t)\leqslant\mathcal{E}(0)=\frac{r-1}{2C}\left\|x_{0}-x^{*}\right\|^{2},

(15)

\displaystyle\frac{t}{r}\left\langle A(X),X-x^{*}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathcal{E}(t)\leqslant\mathcal{E}(0)=\frac{r-1}{2C}\left\|x_{0}-x^{*}\right\|^{2}.

(16)

By dividing
\left(1-\dfrac{C}{r-1}\right)\dfrac{t^{2}}{2r^{2}}+\dfrac{t}{2r}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left\|x_{k}-z_{k}\right\|^{2}+\frac{(r-C)(k+r)^{2}}{k^{2}C}\left\|z_{k}-z^{*}\right\|^{2},

(34)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{2}(k)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left\|x_{k}-z_{k}\right\|^{2}+\frac{(r-C)(k-1)^{2}(k+r)^{2}}{2(1+C)k^{2}r^{2}}\left\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\right\|^{2},\quad k\geqslant 1.

(35)

Lemma 6
.

Let
\{x_{k}\}
and
\{z_{k}\}
be the sequences generated by Algorithm
1
. If
r>1
,
0<C<r-1
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\|x_{k}-z_{k}\right\|^{2}=0,

(36)

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}k^{2}\left\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\|^{2}=0,

(37)

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\|z_{k}-x^{*}\right\|^{2}\text{ exists for all }x^{*}\in\mathrm{zero}(A).

(38)

Proof.

First, we consider the differences
\dfrac{1}{2}\left\|x_{k+1}-z_{k+1}\right\|^{2}-\dfrac{1}{2}\left\|x_{k}-z_{k}\right\|^{2}
and
k^{2}\left\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\|^{2}-(k-1)^{2}\left\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\right\|^{2}
. Direct calculation shows that:

\displaystyle\ \dfrac{1}{2}\left\|x_{k+1}-z_{k+1}\right\|^{2}-\dfrac{1}{2}\left\|x_{k}-z_{k}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{2}\left\langle x_{k+1}+x_{k}-z_{k+1}-z_{k},x_{k+1}-x_{k}-z_{k+1}+z_{k}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{(2k+r)r}{2k^{2}}\left\|x_{k+1}-z_{k+1}\right\|^{2}-\frac{(r^{2}-C^{2})(k+r)^{2}}{2k^{2}r^{2}}\left\|\overline{A}(x_{k+1})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ -\frac{(r-C)(k+r)^{2}}{k^{2}r}\left\langle\overline{A}(x_{k+1}),x_{k+1}-z_{k}\right\rangle,

\displaystyle\frac{k^{2}}{2}\left\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\|^{2}-\frac{(k-1)^{2}}{2}\left\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\ k\left\langle x_{k+1}-x_{k},k(x_{k+1}-x_{k})-(k-1)(x_{k}-x_{k-1})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ k\left\langle x_{k+1}-x_{k},-(k+r)\overline{A}(x_{k+1})-r(x_{k+1}-x_{k})+(k+r-1-C)\overline{A}(x_{k})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ -(k+r-1-C)k\left\langle x_{k+1}-x_{k},\overline{A}(x_{k+1})-\overline{A}(x_{k})\right\rangle-kr\left\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ -(1+C)k\left\langle\overline{A}(x_{k+1}),x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ -(k+r-1-C)k\left\langle x_{k+1}-x_{k},\overline{A}(x_{k+1})-\overline{A}(x_{k})\right\rangle-kr\left\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ +(1+C)r\left\langle\overline{A}(x_{k+1}),x_{k+1}-z_{k}\right\rangle+(1+C)(k+r)\left\|\overline{A}(x_{k+1})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\ -kr\left\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\|^{2}+(1+C)r\left\langle\overline{A}(x_{k+1}),x_{k+1}-z_{k}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\ +(1+C)(k+r)\left\|\overline{A}(x_{k+1})\right\|^{2}.

Based on previous calculation and the inequality
\dfrac{(k+1+r)^{2}}{(k+1)^{2}}<\dfrac{(k+r)^{2}}{k^{2}}
, we have

\displaystyle\ \mathcal{G}_{2}(k+1)-\mathcal{G}_{2}(k)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{2}\left\|x_{k+1}-z_{k+1}\right\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left\|x_{k}-z_{k}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ +\frac{(r-C)(k+r)^{2}}{(1+C)k^{2}r^{2}}\left(\frac{k^{2}}{2}\left\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\|^{2}-\frac{(k-1)^{2}}{2}\left\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\right\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\ -\frac{(2k+r)r}{2k^{2}}\left\|x_{k+1}-z_{k+1}\right\|^{2}-\frac{(r^{2}-C^{2})(k+r)^{2}}{2k^{2}r^{2}}\left\|\overline{A}(x_{k+1})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ -\frac{(r-C)(k+r)^{2}}{(1+C)kr}\left\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\|^{2}+\frac{(r-C)(k+r)^{3}}{k^{2}r^{2}}\left\|\overline{A}(x_{k+1})\right\|^{2}.

By (
30
), we have

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(r-C)(k+r)^{3}}{k^{2}r^{2}}\left\|\overline{A}(x_{k+1})\right\|^{2}<\infty.

Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\ \mathcal{G}_{2}(k+1)+\sum_{i=k+1}^{\infty}\frac{(r-C)(i+r)^{3}}{i^{2}r^{2}}\left\|\overline{A}(x_{i+1})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\ \mathcal{G}_{2}(k)+\sum_{i=k}^{\infty}\frac{(r-C)(i+r)^{3}}{i^{2}r^{2}}\left\|\overline{A}(x_{i+1})\right\|^{2}.

Thus
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{G}_{2}(k)
exist. Also, we have

\displaystyle\ \frac{(2k+r)r}{2k^{2}}\left\|x_{k+1}-z_{k+1}\right\|^{2}+\frac{(r-C)(k+r)^{2}}{(1+C)kr}\left\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\ \mathcal{G}_{2}(k)-\mathcal{G}_{2}(k+1)+\frac{(r-C)(k+r)^{3}}{k^{2}r^{2}}\left\|\overline{A}(x_{k+1})\right\|^{2}.

Then we have

\displaystyle\ \sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(2k+r)r}{2k^{2}}\left\|x_{k+1}-z_{k+1}\right\|^{2}+\frac{(r-C)(k+r)^{2}}{(1+C)kr}\left\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\ \mathcal{G}_{2}(1)+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(r-C)(k+r)^{3}}{k^{2}r^{2}}\left\|\overline{A}(x_{k+1})\right\|^{2}<\infty.

Moreover, based on above estimation, the inequality

\frac{(2k+r)r}{2k^{2}}\left\|x_{k+1}-z_{k+1}\right\|^{2}+\frac{(r-C)(k+r)^{2}}{(1+C)kr}\left\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\|^{2}\geqslant 2r\mathcal{G}_{2}(k+1),

and the existence of
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{G}_{2}(k)
, we have

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{G}_{2}(k)=0
,
which implies that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\|x_{k}-z_{k}\right\|^{2}=0,\quad\lim_{k\to\infty}k^{2}\left\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\|^{2}=0.

Next, we show that
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\left\|z_{k}-x^{*}\right\|^{2}
exists for all
x^{*}\in\mathrm{zero}(A)
. First, we show that
\{\mathcal{G}_{1}(k)\}
is non-increasing.

\displaystyle\ \mathcal{G}_{1}(k+1)-\mathcal{G}_{1}(k)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\ -\frac{(2k+r)r}{2k^{2}}\left\|x_{k+1}-z_{k+1}\right\|^{2}-\frac{(r^{2}-C^{2})(k+r)^{2}}{2k^{2}r^{2}}\left\|\overline{A}(x_{k+1})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ -\frac{(r-C)(k+r)^{2}}{k^{2}r}\left\langle\overline{A}(x_{k+1}),x_{k+1}-z_{k}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\ +\frac{(r-C)(k+r)^{2}}{2k^{2}C}\left(\left\|z_{k+1}-x^{*}\right\|^{2}-\left\|z_{k}-z^{*}\right\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\ -\frac{(2k+r)r}{2k^{2}}\left\|x_{k+1}-z_{k+1}\right\|^{2}-\frac{(r-C)(k+r)^{2}}{2k^{2}r}\left\|\overline{A}(x_{k+1})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ -\frac{(r-C)(k+r)^{2}}{k^{2}r}\left\langle\overline{A}(x_{k+1}),x_{k+1}-x^{*}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\ 0.

Owing to non-negativity of
\mathcal{G}_{1}(k)
,
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{G}_{1}(k)
exists. Since
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\left\|x_{k}-z_{k}\right\|^{2}=0
,
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\left\|z_{k}-x^{*}\right\|^{2}
exists for all
x^{*}\in\mathrm{zero}(A)
.
∎

(

Because of Theorem

exists. Next, by (

Theorem 6

(Weak Convergence Property)
.

Let
A
be a maximally monotone operator, and let
\{x_{k}\}
,
\{z_{k}\}
,
\{\tilde{x}_{k+1}\}
be the sequences generated by Algorithm

. If

In this section, we consider the min-max problem:

\min_{x}\max_{y}f(x)+\left\langle Ax,y\right\rangle-g(y),

(44)

where
f
and
g
are closed, proper and convex functions defined on

and the matrix
A
is randomly generated. The initial points for

and

and

\Delta_{1000}
and
\Delta_{2000}

and
\sigma

and

and

5
Conclusion and Discussion

Throughout this paper, we first propose an ODE system for the maximally monotone operator
A
. We then employ the Lyapunov function technique and two auxiliary functions to establish that the ODE system exhibits
o(1/t^{2})
convergence rate, and that its solution trajectory converges weakly to a point in
\mathrm{zero}(A)
. Building upon this foundation, we apply the Symplectic Euler Method to discretize the ODE system, leading to the development of our accelerated proximal point algorithm, which we call the Symplectic Proximal Point Algorithm. Furthermore, using the Lyapunov function technique and two auxiliary sequences, we demonstrate that the SPPA also achieves a convergence rate of
o(1/k^{2})
, and the sequences it generates converge weakly to a point in
\mathrm{zero}(A)
.

There are numerous theoretical issues regarding accelerated proximal point algorithms that merit further investigation. First, in
[
5
,
16
]
, the authors discussed the convergence rates of the NAG method and FISTA with wider range of parameter. The convergence properties of (
6
) and Algorithm
1
when
r<1
or
C>r-1
are also topics worthy of exploration. Furthermore, the works in
[
7
]
and
[
26
]
have shown that the NAG method exhibits exponential convergence under the assumption of strong convexity, even when the strong convexity parameter is unknown. It is valuable to determine whether our SPPA, Fast K-M algorithm or accelerated PPA can achieve (possibly asymptotic) exponential convergence. Additionally,
[
33
]
introduced an accelerated PPA used for (
1
) when
A

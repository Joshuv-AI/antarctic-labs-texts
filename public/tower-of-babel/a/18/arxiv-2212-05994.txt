Main Definitions and Motivation

Throughout this paper,
\mathbb{F}
stands for a field of characteristic
zero, and all vector spaces and algebras are taken over
\mathbb{F}
. In addition, we always assume that our algebras are non-unitary. For
a background on PI-algebras and details of the results discussed in
this section, we refer to
[
6
]
or
[
8
]
.

A polynomial identity of an associative algebra
A
is a polynomial

f\left(x_{1},...,x_{n}\right)
in the free associative algebra
\mathbb{F}\left\langle X\right\rangle

on a countable set
X=\left\{x_{1},x_{2},...\right\}
of (non-commutative) variables, such that

f\left(a_{1},...,a_{n}\right)=0
for all
a_{1},...,a_{n}\in A
.
In this case, we say that
A
satisfies the identity
f
.
Sometimes we will use other symbols (e.g.,
x,y,z,
etc.) for the elements of
X
.

A polynomial identity
g\left(x_{1},...,x_{m}\right)\in\mathbb{F}\left\langle X\right\rangle

is called a
consequence
of the polynomial identity
h\left(x_{1},...,x_{k}\right)\in\mathbb{F}\left\langle X\right\rangle
,
if any algebra satisfying the identity
h\left(x_{1},...,x_{k}\right)=0

satisfies the identity
g\left(x_{1},...,x_{m}\right)=0
.

Definition 1.1
.

A
T
-ideal is an ideal
I\triangleleft\mathbb{F}\left\langle X\right\rangle

such that
\psi\left(I\right)\subset I
for all homomorphisms
\psi:\mathbb{F}\left\langle X\right\rangle\rightarrow\mathbb{F}\left\langle X\right\rangle
.
Given
f\in\mathbb{F}\left\langle X\right\rangle
, we denote by

\left(f\right)^{T}
the smallest
T
-ideal containing
f
.

It is well known (see, e.g., Prop. 1.2.8 in
[
6
]
) that in characteristic zero, every
T
-ideal
I\triangleleft\mathbb{F}\left\langle X\right\rangle
is completely determined by its multilinear elements. Hence, denoting the symmetric group on
[m]=\left\{1,...,m\right\}
by

, and define

\displaystyle V_{m}:=\text{span}_{\mathbb{F}}\left\{x_{\sigma\left(1\right)}\cdot\cdot\cdot x_{\sigma\left(m\right)}:\sigma\in S_{m}\right\}\subseteq\mathbb{F}\left\langle X\right\rangle,

the structure of
I
is completely determined by
\left\{V_{m}\cap I\right\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Our goal in this paper is to gain a better understanding of the subspace

\displaystyle W_{n,m}:=V_{m}\cap\left(x^{n}\right)^{T}\leq V_{m}.

Example 1.2
.

Consider
V_{2}=\text{span}_{\mathbb{F}}\left\{x_{1}x_{2},x_{2}x_{1}\right\}
.
We claim that
W_{2,2}=\text{span}_{\mathbb{F}}\left\{x_{1}x_{2}+x_{2}x_{1}\right\}
.
Indeed, since

\displaystyle x_{1}x_{2}+x_{2}x_{1}=\left(x_{1}+x_{2}\right)^{2}-x_{1}^{2}-x_{2}^{2}\in\left(x^{2}\right)^{T},

we have

On the other hand, the identity

does not imply the identity

(hence

take any three-dimensional vector space

,
and give to

\displaystyle a\circ a

\displaystyle=b\circ b=c\circ c=0

\displaystyle a\circ b

\displaystyle=c=-b\circ a

\displaystyle a\circ c

\displaystyle=0=c\circ a

\displaystyle b\circ c

\displaystyle=0=c\circ b.

We see that

1.3
The motivation for studying
W_{n,m}

Our original motivation for studying the space
W_{n,m}
was to analyze
the class of nilpotency of algebras satisfying the identity
x^{n}=0
. Let
n
be a natural number. The classical Nagata-Higman theorem
[
10
]

[
15
]
,
which was first proved by Dubnov and Ivanov
[
7
]
in 1943, states
that if
A
is an associative (non-unitary) algebra, and
A
satisfies
the identity
f=x^{n}
, there exists an integer
m=m\left(n\right)

such that
A^{m}=0
. In other words, the identity

x_{1}\cdot\cdot\cdot x_{m}=0
is a consequence of the
identity
x^{n}=0
. Consider

\displaystyle d\left(n\right):=\text{min}\left\{m:x_{1}\cdot\cdot\cdot x_{m}=0\text{ is a consequence of $x^{n}$}=0\right\}.

In 1974, Razmyslov
[
18
]
established the upper bound
d\left(n\right)\leq n^{2}
,
which to this day remains the best known upper bound. For a lower bound, Kuzmin
[
13
]
showed that
\frac{n\left(n+1\right)}{2}\leq d\left(n\right)
,
and conjectured that we have
d\left(n\right)=\frac{n\left(n+1\right)}{2}

for every
n
. As of 2022, this was confirmed only for
n=1,2,3

by Dubnov and Ivanov
[
7
]
, and for
n=4
by Vaughan-Lee
[
22
]
.
Let us reformulate Kuzmin’s conjecture in terms of
\text{dim}\left(W_{n,m}\right)
.

Proposition 1.8
.

Given
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
d\left(n\right)=\text{min}\left\{m\in\mathbb{N}\mid\text{dim}\left(W_{n,m}\right)=m!\right\}
.
In particular, Kuzmin’s conjecture holds if and only if
\text{dim}\left(W_{n,n\left(n+1\right)/2}\right)=\left(n\left(n+1\right)/2\right)!
.

Proof.

By definition,
d\left(n\right)\leq m
if and only if

g_{\sigma}=x_{\sigma\left(1\right)}\cdot\cdot\cdot x_{\sigma\left(m\right)}

is a consequence of
f=x^{n}
for any
\sigma\in S_{m}
, which holds if and only if
g_{\sigma}

lies in
V_{m}\cap\left(x^{n}\right)^{T}=W_{n,m}
. As
\left\{x_{\sigma\left(1\right)}\cdot\cdot\cdot x_{\sigma\left(m\right)}\right\}_{\sigma\in S_{m}}

is a linear basis of
V_{m}
, we have
\text{dim}\left(V_{m}\right)=m!=\text{dim}\left(W_{n,m}\right)

if and only if
V_{m}=W_{n,m}
, if and only if
g_{\sigma}\in W_{n,m}

for every
\sigma\in S_{m}
.
∎

In light of this proposition, the investigation of
W_{n,m}
should focus only on the case
m\leq n^{2}
, since for every
m>n^{2}

we have
W_{n,m}=V_{m}\cong\mathbb{F}\left[S_{m}\right]
(by Razmyslov’s upper bound),
and the multiplicities of
W_{n,m}
are well understood in this case.

For every

-representation corresponding to

(b) There is

(c) For every

,

If

(2)

(3)

\displaystyle R\cong\underset{\lambda\vdash m}{\bigoplus}d^{\lambda}S^{\lambda}.

Remark 2.8
.

One can observe that

holds for any

. Indeed, taking

such that

, we have by (c) and (d)

\displaystyle e_{T}Re_{T^{\prime}}=e_{T}Re_{\sigma T}=e_{T}R\sigma e_{T}\sigma^{-1}=\mathbb{F}e_{T}\sigma^{-1}.

Recall from Section 1 that

is an

-representation for every

-ideal

. One way to calculate its decomposition

-irreducible representations is as follows.

Lemma 2.9

Let

be a

-ideal, and let

-decomposition of

For every

,
where

is any Young tableau of shape
\lambda
. In

if and only if there are

and a Young tableau

Proof.

Let

and fix a partition

and

Given

2.2

Throughout this section, we fix

This algebra can be graded as

where for every

we may identify

\displaystyle g.\left(x_{i_{1}}\cdot\cdot\cdot x_{i_{s}}\right)=g\left(x_{i_{1}}\right)\cdot\cdot\cdot g\left(x_{i_{s}}\right).

on

\displaystyle\sigma\left(x_{i_{1}}\cdot\cdot\cdot x_{i_{m}}\right)=x_{i_{\sigma^{-1}\left(1\right)}}\cdot\cdot\cdot x_{i_{\sigma^{-1}\left(m\right)}}.

(5)

.

be the

\displaystyle V^{\lambda}:=e_{T_{\lambda}}V^{\otimes m}.

[

[

\left[A_{k}\right]^{\left(m\right)}\cap I

GL_{k}\left(\mathbb{F}\right)
-representation.
This is because

is invariant under substitutions. Thus, just as with the decomposition of

into irreducible

-representations
(established in Section 1), we have a decomposition of

into

-representations:

\displaystyle A_{k}^{\left(m\right)}\cap I\cong\underset{\underset{\ell\left(\lambda\right)\leq k}{\lambda\vdash m}}{\bigoplus}n^{\lambda}V^{\lambda}.

In the early 1980s, Berele and Drensky established the following connection between the
GL_{k}\left(\mathbb{F}\right)
-decomposition
of

and the

-decomposition
of

Theorem 2.20

(Berele
[
2
]
, Drensky
[
5
]
)
.

Let
I\triangleleft\mathbb{F}\left\langle X\right\rangle
be a
T
-ideal,
and suppose that as an
S_{m}
-representation,
V_{m}\cap I
decomposes as
\underset{\lambda\vdash m}{\bigoplus}q^{\lambda}S^{\lambda}
.
Then, as a
GL_{k}\left(\mathbb{F}\right)
-representation,
\left[A_{k}\right]^{\left(m\right)}\cap I

decomposes as

\displaystyle\underset{\underset{\ell\left(\lambda\right)\leq k}{\lambda\vdash m}}{\bigoplus}q^{\lambda}V^{\lambda}.

In other words, the space
\left[A_{k}\right]^{\left(m\right)}\cap I
"captures"
the multiplicities
\left\{q^{\lambda}:\ell\left(\lambda\right)\leq k\right\}

of
V_{m}\cap I
.

2.3
Symmetric tensors

Given an
\mathbb{F}
-vector space, the

is the vector space

\displaystyle U^{\otimes_{s}n}=U^{\otimes n}/\left\langle\sigma\cdot w-w:w\in U^{\otimes n},\sigma\in S_{n}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{F}}.

Elements in

can be written as
u_{1}\otimes_{s}\cdot\cdot\cdot\otimes_{s}u_{n}

u_{i}\in U

u_{1}\otimes_{s}\cdot\cdot\cdot\otimes_{s}u_{n}

and
u_{\sigma\left(1\right)}\otimes_{s}\cdot\cdot\cdot\otimes_{s}u_{\sigma\left(n\right)}

for all
\sigma\in S_{n}
. We denote the space of all the polynomials in
U^{\otimes_{s}n}

of degree

(i.e., the polynomials whose pre-image in

under the quotient map has degree

Remark 2.21
.

Let
V=\text{span}_{\mathbb{F}}\left\{x_{1},...,x_{k}\right\}
.
It is easy to see that

is isomorphic (as a
GL_{k}
-representation)
to
V^{\left(n\right)}=e_{T}V^{\otimes n}
, where
T
is the
tableau
T=

1

n

,
via the map

\displaystyle x_{i_{1}}\otimes_{s}\cdot\cdot\cdot\otimes_{s}x_{i_{n}}\mapsto\underset{\sigma\in S_{n}}{\sum}x_{i_{\sigma\left(1\right)}}\otimes\cdot\cdot\cdot\otimes x_{i_{\sigma\left(n\right)}}.

The following lemma of Drensky and Benanti, gives a formula for the decomposition of

into irreducible representations.

Lemma 2.22

(
[
1
, Proposition 1.2]
)

Suppose

is a direct sum

,

are isomorphic to

.

(1)
We have the following isomorphism of

-representations:

\displaystyle U^{\otimes_{s}n}\cong\bigoplus_{r_{1},...,r_{q}}\bigoplus_{a_{1},...,a_{q}}\left(V_{r_{1}}^{\otimes_{s}a_{1}}\otimes V_{r_{2}}^{\otimes_{s}a_{2}}\otimes\cdot\cdot\cdot\otimes V_{r_{q}}^{\otimes_{s}a_{q}}\right),

(6)

Hence, given

Example 2.23
.

The decomposition of

Lemma 2.24
.

Fix
K\in\mathbb{N}
. There are
GL_{k}\left(\mathbb{F}\right)
-representations

M_{1},...,M_{p}\subseteq A_{k}
of degree at most

, and non-negative

(1)

\left[A_{k}^{\otimes_{s}K}\right]^{\left(2K\right)}\cong\mathrel{{\mathop{\bigoplus}\limits}}V^{\left(c_{i}\right)}\otimes M_{i}
.

(2)
For every

\left[A_{k}^{\otimes_{s}n}\right]^{\left(n+K\right)}\cong\mathrel{{\mathop{\bigoplus}\limits}}V^{\left(n-K+c_{i}\right)}\otimes M_{i}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.22
,
\left[A_{k}^{\otimes_{s}n}\right]^{\left(n+K\right)}

decomposes as a direct sum of subrepresentations of the form

\displaystyle V_{1}^{\otimes_{s}a_{1}}\otimes\cdot\cdot\cdot\otimes V_{q}^{\otimes_{s}a_{q}},

where
V_{1},...,V_{q}
are irreducible representations arising from

A_{k}
, such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}a_{1}+...+a_{q}&=n\end{split}

(7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}a_{1}\text{deg}\left(V_{1}\right)+...+a_{q}\text{deg}\left(V_{q}\right)&=n+K\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\left(\text{deg}\left(V_{1}\right)\leq....\leq\text{deg}\left(V_{q}\right)\right).\end{split}

(8)

Claim
. For every
n>K
, if
V_{1}^{\otimes_{s}a_{1}}\otimes V_{2}^{\otimes_{s}a_{2}}\otimes\cdot\cdot\cdot\otimes V_{q}^{\otimes_{s}a_{q}}

is a summand in
\left[A_{k}^{\otimes_{s}n}\right]^{\left(n+K\right)}

then

can be written as

\displaystyle a_{1}=n-K+c_{1}

for some nonzero integer

, such that
V_{1}^{\otimes_{s}c_{1}}\otimes V_{2}^{\otimes_{s}a_{2}}\cdot\cdot\cdot\otimes V_{q}^{\otimes_{s}a_{q}}

is a summand in
\left[A_{k}^{\otimes_{s}K}\right]^{\left(2K\right)}
.

Proof
. Suppose
V_{1}^{\otimes_{s}a_{1}}\otimes V_{2}^{\otimes_{s}a_{2}}\otimes\cdot\cdot\cdot\otimes V_{q}^{\otimes_{s}a_{q}}

is a summand in
\left[A_{k}^{\otimes_{s}n}\right]^{\left(n+K\right)}
.
Due to Theorem
2.18
, we have

\displaystyle A_{k}=\left[A_{k}\right]^{\left(1\right)}\oplus\left[A_{k}\right]^{\left(2\right)}\oplus\cdot\cdot\cdot=\left(V^{\left(1\right)}\right)\oplus\left(V^{\left(2\right)}\oplus V^{\left(1,1\right)}\right)\oplus\cdot\cdot\cdot,

so
V^{\left(1\right)}=V=\text{span}_{\mathbb{F}}\left\{x_{1},...,x_{k}\right\}
is the only candidate for an irreducible representation
of degree one, and
\text{deg}\left(V_{i}\right)\geq 2
for all

. Thus

\displaystyle n+K

\displaystyle=a_{1}\text{deg}\left(V_{1}\right)+a_{2}\text{deg}\left(V_{2}\right)+...+a_{q}\text{deg}\left(V_{q}\right)

\displaystyle\geq a_{1}\text{deg}\left(V_{1}\right)+2\left(a_{2}+...+a_{q}\right)=a_{1}\text{deg}\left(V_{1}\right)+2\left(n-a_{1}\right)

and
a_{1}\left(2-\text{deg}\left(V_{1}\right)\right)\geq n-K>0
.
This implies
\text{deg}\left(V_{1}\right)=1
(i.e.,
V_{1}=V
), and
a_{1}\geq n-K
. Writing
a_{1}=n-K+c_{1}
for some
c_{1}\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle c_{1}+a_{2}+...+a_{q}=\left(K-n+a_{1}\right)+a_{2}+...+a_{q}\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}K

and

\displaystyle\text{deg}\left(V_{1}^{\otimes_{s}c_{1}}\otimes V_{2}^{\otimes_{s}a_{2}}\cdot\cdot\cdot\otimes V_{q}^{\otimes_{s}a_{q}}\right)

\displaystyle=\text{deg}\left(V_{1}^{\otimes_{s}a_{1}}\otimes V_{2}^{\otimes_{s}a_{2}}\cdot\cdot\cdot\otimes V_{q}^{\otimes_{s}a_{q}}\right)-\left(n-K\right)

and thus
V_{1}^{\otimes_{s}c_{1}}\otimes V_{2}^{\otimes_{s}a_{2}}\cdot\cdot\cdot\otimes V_{q}^{\otimes_{s}a_{q}}

is a summand in
\left[A_{k}^{\otimes_{s}K}\right]^{\left(2K\right)}
.

In light of this claim, it is not difficult to see that for any

, there is a bijection
between the summands in
\left[A_{k}^{\otimes_{s}n}\right]^{\left(n+K\right)}

and the summands in
\left[A_{k}^{\otimes_{s}K}\right]^{\left(2K\right)}

given by

\displaystyle V_{1}^{\otimes_{s}a_{1}}\otimes V_{2}^{\otimes_{s}a_{2}}\otimes\cdot\cdot\cdot\otimes V_{q}^{\otimes_{s}a_{q}}\mapsto V_{1}^{\otimes_{s}a_{1}-\left(n-K\right)}\otimes V_{2}^{\otimes_{s}a_{2}}\otimes\cdot\cdot\cdot\otimes V_{q}^{\otimes_{s}a_{q}},

with an inverse

\displaystyle V_{1}^{\otimes_{s}b_{1}}\otimes V_{2}^{\otimes_{s}b_{2}}\otimes\cdot\cdot\cdot\otimes V_{l}^{\otimes_{s}b_{l}}\mapsto V_{1}^{\otimes_{s}b_{1}+\left(n-K\right)}\otimes V_{2}^{\otimes_{s}b_{2}}\otimes\cdot\cdot\cdot\otimes V_{l}^{\otimes_{s}b_{l}}.

All in all, assuming that the decomposition of

is given by

\displaystyle\mathrel{{\mathop{\bigoplus}\limits}}V_{1}^{\otimes_{s}c_{i}}\otimes M_{i}\mathrel{{\mathop{\cong}\limits}}\mathrel{{\mathop{\bigoplus}\limits}}V^{\left(c_{i}\right)}\otimes M_{i},

where each

is of the form

(

), the decomposition of
\left[A_{k}^{\otimes_{s}n}\right]^{\left(n+K\right)}
(

)
is given by

\displaystyle\mathrel{{\mathop{\bigoplus}\limits}}V_{1}^{\otimes_{s}n-K+c_{i}}\otimes M_{i}\mathrel{{\mathop{\cong}\limits}}\mathrel{{\mathop{\bigoplus}\limits}}V^{\left(n-K+c_{i}\right)}\otimes M_{i}.

As
\text{deg}\left(M_{i}\right)\leq 2K
for each

, the lemma
follows.
∎

2.4
Applying Young’s rule

Given two partitions
\mu\vdash l
and
\lambda\vdash m
,
one may wish to decompose
V^{\lambda}\otimes V^{\mu}
as a
GL_{k}\left(\mathbb{F}\right)
-representation,
that is, to compute the multiplicities
\left\{c_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu}\right\}

in

\displaystyle V^{\mu}\otimes V^{\lambda}\cong\bigoplus_{\nu\vdash m+l}c_{\mu,\lambda}^{\nu}V^{\nu}.

The
Littlewood-Richardson rule
is a well-known algorithm
for doing this. For our purposes, we will only consider one special
case of this rule, usually referred to as the Young rule:

Theorem 2.25

(Young’s rule. See, e.g.,
[
11
, p.88]
)
.

Let
l,n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\lambda\vdash n
. For every
\nu\vdash l+n
,
the multiplicity of
V^{\nu}
in
V^{\left(l\right)}\otimes V^{\lambda}

equals

\displaystyle c_{\left(l\right),\lambda}^{\nu}=\begin{cases}1&\begin{array}[]{c}\text{if the diagram of $\nu$ is obtained by adding $l$ boxes to the diagram }\\
\text{of $\lambda$, such that no two new boxes are in the same column;}\end{array}.\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

Identifying
V^{\mu}
with the Young diagram
D_{\mu}
, Figure
3
illustrates Young’s rule for the decomposition of

.

.

Definition 2.26
.

In analogy with Definition
1.4
, we say that a
GL_{k}\left(\mathbb{F}\right)
-representation

U

is derived
from a
GL_{k}\left(\mathbb{F}\right)
-representation

, if
W
decomposes as
\underset{\mu}{\bigoplus}a_{\mu}V^{\mu}
,
and there exists
d\in\mathbb{N}
such that
U
decomposes as
\underset{\mu}{\bigoplus}a_{\mu}V^{\mu^{\left(d\right)}}
. (The notation
\mu^{\left(d\right)}
was defined in Definition
1.3
.)

Remark 2.27
.

One can easily observe the following:

(1)
If
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
are derived from
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
,
respectively, then
U_{1}\oplus U_{2}
is derived from
W_{1}\oplus W_{2}
.

(2)
Transitivity of derivations: if
U
is derived from
W

and
W
is derived from
Q
, then
U
is derived from
Q
.

Using the terminology of Definition
2.26
, the following lemma is a simple application of Young’s rule.

Lemma 2.28
.

Let
\lambda=\left(\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{r}\right)
be a partition
of
m
, and let
l
be a natural number such that
\lambda_{1}\leq l

(in particular, this is the case if
\left|\lambda\right|=m\leq l
). Then
V^{\left(l+1\right)}\otimes V^{\lambda}
is derived from
V^{\left(l\right)}\otimes V^{\lambda}
.

Proof.

By Young’s rule,
c_{\left(l+1\right),\lambda}^{\mu}=1
if and only
if
D_{\mu}
is obtained by adding
l+1
boxes to the Young diagram
D_{\lambda}
such that no two new boxes are in the same column.
As
\lambda_{1}\leq l
,
D_{\lambda}
has at most
l
columns,
so in every addition of at least
l+1
new boxes in a legitimate manner, at least one will be added at the first row.
∎

Corollary 2.29
.

Suppose a
GL_{k}\left(\mathbb{F}\right)
-representation

decomposes into irreducible representations as

,
and let

be a natural number such that, for each

By assumption, for every

3
Stabilization of
W_{n,n+K}

In this section we prove our main result, which is Theorem
3.8
. We start by describing
the
T
-ideal
\left(x^{n}\right)^{T}\triangleleft\mathbb{F}\left\langle X\right\rangle

as a vector space. An essential tool for doing this is the
n
-th
symmetric polynomial
, defined by

\displaystyle P_{n}\left(x_{1},...,x_{n}\right):=\underset{\sigma\in S_{n}}{\sum}x_{\sigma\left(1\right)}\cdot\cdot\cdot x_{\sigma\left(n\right)}\in\mathbb{F}\left\langle X\right\rangle.

The following proposition is due to Higman and Hall:

Proposition 3.1

(
[
10
]
. See also
[
6
, p.77]
)
.

As a vector space over
\mathbb{F}
, the
T
-ideal
\left(x^{n}\right)^{T}\triangleleft\mathbb{F}\left\langle X\right\rangle

is spanned by the polynomials

\displaystyle P_{n}\left(m_{1},...,m_{n}\right),

where
m_{1},...,m_{n}
are monomials in
\mathbb{F}\left\langle X\right\rangle
.

Recall that
A_{k}
denotes the free algebra
\mathbb{F}\left\langle x_{1},...,x_{k}\right\rangle
.
Using Proposition
3.1
, Drensky and Benanti have made the following remarkable observation.

Proposition 3.2
.

As a
GL_{k}\left(\mathbb{F}\right)
-representation,
A_{k}\cap\left(x^{n}\right)^{T}

is a homomorphic image of
A_{k}^{\otimes_{s}n}
, via the map

\displaystyle\phi

\displaystyle:A_{k}^{\otimes_{s}n}\rightarrow A_{k}\cap\left(x^{n}\right)^{T}

\displaystyle\phi

\displaystyle\left(z_{1}\otimes_{s}\cdot\cdot\cdot\otimes_{s}z_{n}\right)=P_{n}\left(z_{1},...,z_{n}\right).

Similarly,
\left[A_{k}\right]^{\left(m\right)}\cap\left(x^{n}\right)^{T}
is
a homomorphic image of
\left[A_{k}^{\otimes_{s}n}\right]^{\left(m\right)}
,
the polynomials in
A_{k}^{\otimes_{s}n}
of degree
m
.

This proposition allows us to prove one of our key results.

Theorem 3.3
.

Fix
K\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
\underset{\lambda\vdash n+K}{\bigoplus}m_{n,n+K}^{\lambda}S^{\lambda}

be the decomposition of
W_{n,n+K}
into
S_{n+K}
-irreducible representations.

\displaystyle F_{n+s-D}\left(m_{\sigma\left(1\right)},...,m_{\sigma\left(D\right)}\right)=\frac{1}{\left(n+s-D\right)!}P^{\left(n+s-D\right)}\left(m_{1},...,m_{n}\right)

is a polynomial in
s
, and the lemma follows.

∎

5
Estimates on
\text{dim}\left(W_{n,m}\right)

In this section, we give upper and lower bounds on
\text{dim}\left(W_{n,m}\right)
,
and review the special cases
m=n+1
and
m=n+2
, where
\text{dim}\left(W_{n,m}\right)

is precisely known. We also conjecture the asymptotic behavior of
\text{dim}\left(W_{n,n+K}\right)
for a fixed
K
. It should be noted that for a given
n
, we are only interested in the case
m<n^{2}
, since we already know that

\text{dim}\left(W_{n,m}\right)=m!
for every
n^{2}\leq m
(see Proposition
1.8
and the paragraph following it).

5.1
Upper bound

Lemma 5.1
.

\text{dim}\left(W_{n,m}\right)\leq\left(\begin{array}[]{c}m\\
n\end{array}\right)\cdot\frac{\left(m-1\right)!}{\left(n-1\right)!}
.

Proof.

By Corollary
4.5
,
W_{n,m}
is spanned by
\left\{P_{O}:O\in\Omega_{n,m}\right\}
,
where
\Omega_{n,m}
is the set of ordered partitions of

\left[m\right]=\left\{1,...,m\right\}
divided into
n
parts, so it suffices to show
that
\left|\Omega_{n,m}\right|=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}m\\
n\end{array}\right)\cdot\frac{\left(m-1\right)!}{\left(n-1\right)!}
.
Indeed, start by choosing a word
w=w_{1}\cdot\cdot\cdot w_{m}
whose
letters are
1,...,m
in some order, and then divide
w
into
n

parts.
The last division requires a choice
of
n-1
"cut places"
out of the
m-1
possibilities, so all this procedure can be
made in
m!\left(\begin{array}[]{c}m-1\\
n-1\end{array}\right)
ways. However, each such cut is counted
n!
times (as we do not
care about their order), implying that

\displaystyle\left|\Omega_{n,m}\right|=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}m-1\\
n-1\end{array}\right)\cdot\frac{m!}{n!}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}m\\
n\end{array}\right)\cdot\frac{\left(m-1\right)!}{\left(n-1\right)!}.

∎

Remark 5.2
.

As we will see in Theorem
5.8
,
\text{dim}\left(W_{n,n+1}\right)=n\left(n+1\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}n+1\\
n\end{array}\right)\cdot\frac{n!}{\left(n-1\right)!}
, so this bound is tight.

5.2
Lower bound

The following theorem is due to Latyshev
[
14
]
(see also
[
12
, p.137]
):

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{F}\left\langle X\right\rangle
be a polynomial
of degree
n
, and set
c_{m}:=\text{dim}\left(V_{m}/\left(V_{m}\cap\left(f\right)^{T}\right)\right)
.
Then

\displaystyle c_{m}\leq\underset{\underset{\ell\left(\lambda\right)<n}{\lambda\vdash m}}{\sum}\left(d^{\lambda}\right)^{2},

where
d^{\lambda}
is the dimension of the irreducible representation

S^{\lambda}
of
S_{m}
.

Corollary 5.4
.

For every
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
,
\underset{\underset{\ell\left(\lambda\right)\geq n}{\lambda\vdash m}}{\sum}d_{\lambda}^{2}\leq\text{dim}\left(W_{n,m}\right)
.

Proof.

By Theorem
5.3
,
\text{dim}\left(V_{m}/\left(V_{m}\cap\left(x^{n}\right)^{T}\right)\right)=\text{dim}\left(V_{m}/W_{n,m}\right)\leq\underset{\underset{\ell\left(\lambda\right)<n}{\lambda\vdash m}}{\sum}\left(d^{\lambda}\right)^{2}
,
and thus

\displaystyle m!-\text{dim}\left(W_{n,m}\right)=\text{dim}\left(V_{m}\right)-\text{dim}\left(W_{n,m}\right)\leq\underset{\underset{\ell\left(\lambda\right)<n}{\lambda\vdash m}}{\sum}\left(d^{\lambda}\right)^{2}.

Using the well-known fact
m!=\underset{\lambda\vdash m}{\sum}\left(d^{\lambda}\right)^{2}
,
we obtain

\displaystyle\underset{\underset{\ell\left(\lambda\right)\geq n}{\lambda\vdash m}}{\sum}\left(d^{\lambda}\right)^{2}=m!-\underset{\underset{\ell\left(\lambda\right)<n}{\lambda\vdash m}}{\sum}\left(d^{\lambda}\right)^{2}\leq W_{n,m}.

∎

However, according to our observations, this bound seems
far from being optimal. The following is a better lower bound, in the special case when
n
is coprime to
m
:

Proposition 5.5
.

If
n
is coprime to
m
, then
\frac{m!}{\left(n-1\right)!}\leq\text{dim}\left(W_{n,m}\right)
.

Proof.

We begin with some reductions.

Reduction 1.
Consider the partition
\lambda=(m+1-n,\mathrel{{\mathop{\overbrace{1,...,1}}\limits}})\vdash m
,
and let

\displaystyle W_{n,m}^{\lambda}:=\text{span}_{\mathbb{F}}\left\{P_{O}\mid O\in\Omega_{n,m}^{\lambda}\right\}\subseteq W_{n,m},

where
\Omega_{n,m}^{\lambda}
is the set of ordered partitions
O=\left\{A_{1},...,A_{n}\right\}

such that
\left|A_{n}\right|=m+1-n
and
\left|A_{i}\right|=1

for
i=1,...,n-1
(see Definition
4.1
). It suffices to show that

\displaystyle\text{dim}_{\mathbb{F}}\left(W_{n,m}^{\lambda}\right)=\frac{m!}{\left(n-1\right)!}.

(11)

Reduction 2.
In order to prove (
11
), we may assume that
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
.

Reduction 3.
It is not hard to see that the action of
S_{m}

on
W_{n,m}^{\lambda}
is transitive, and therefore
W_{n,m}^{\lambda}

is a cyclic
\mathbb{\mathbb{C}}\left[S_{m}\right]
-module. In particular,
taking

\displaystyle\Gamma=\left\{[\![1]\!],[\![2]\!],...,[\![n-1]\!],[\![n....m]\!]\right\}\in\Omega_{n,m}^{\lambda}

then

\displaystyle W_{n,m}^{\lambda}=\mathbb{\mathbb{C}}\left[S_{m}\right]\cdot P_{n}\left(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n-1},x_{n}\cdot\cdot\cdot x_{m}\right)=\mathbb{\mathbb{C}}\left[S_{m}\right]\cdot P_{\Gamma}.

Identifying
\mathbb{\mathbb{C}}\left[S_{m}\right]=V_{m}
as usual,
one may observe that

\displaystyle P_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle=P_{n}\left(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n-1},x_{n}\cdot\cdot\cdot x_{m}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\underset{\sigma\in{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0}S_{n-1}}}{\sum}\sigma\right)\left({\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0}x_{1}\cdot\cdot\cdot x_{n-1}}x_{n}\cdot\cdot\cdot x_{m}+{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0}x_{2}\cdot\cdot\cdot x_{n-1}}x_{n}\cdot\cdot\cdot x_{m}{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0}x_{1}}+...+x_{n}\cdot\cdot\cdot x_{m}{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0}x_{1}\cdot\cdot\cdot x_{n-1}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\underset{\sigma\in S_{n-1}}{\sum}\sigma\right)\left(e+g+...+g^{n-1}\right),

where
g
is the permutation
g=\left(1,2,...,m\right)\in S_{m}

Thus, if we set

S_{n-1}^{+}:=\underset{\sigma\in S_{n-1}}{\sum}\sigma
and
\zeta_{n-1}:=e+g+g^{2}+...+g^{n-1}
, it suffices to show that

\displaystyle\text{dim}\left(\mathbb{\mathbb{C}}\left[S_{m}\right]\cdot P_{\Gamma}\right)=\text{dim}\left(\mathbb{\mathbb{C}}\left[S_{m}\right]\cdot S_{n-1}^{+}\cdot\zeta_{n-1}\right)=\frac{m!}{\left(n-1\right)!}.

(12)

To prove (
12
), we need the following lemma (which as far as the author is aware, does not appear in the literature).

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
G=\left\langle g\right\rangle
be the cyclic
group of order
n
, and let
0\leq k<n
. The element

\zeta_{k}:=e+g+...+g^{k}

is invertible in
\mathbb{C}\left[G\right]

is coprime to

.

The proof of this lemma can be found in the
the
Appendix
.
However, assuming this lemma, the rest of the proof is straightforward: since we assume that

is coprime to

, we obtain that

\displaystyle\text{dim}\left(\mathbb{\mathbb{C}}\left[S_{m}\right]\cdot S_{n-1}^{+}\cdot\zeta_{n-1}\right)=\text{dim}\left(\mathbb{\mathbb{C}}\left[S_{m}\right]\cdot S_{n-1}^{+}\right).

It follows that

Appendix - proof of Lemma
5.6

Lemma
.

Let
G=\left\langle g\right\rangle
be the cyclic
group of order
n
, and let
0\leq k<n
. The element
\zeta_{k}:=e+g+...+g^{k}

is invertible in
\mathbb{C}\left[G\right]
if and only if
k+1
is coprime to
n
.

Proof.

Note that
\zeta_{k}
is invertible in
\mathbb{C}\left[G\right]

if and only if it is not contained in any proper ideal. Since proper
ideals in
\mathbb{C}\left[G\right]
are direct sums of one-dimensional
irreducible representations,
\zeta_{k}
being irreducible is equivalent to the
following: for every irreducible representation
V
of
G
, and
for every idempotent
e_{V}
corresponding to
V
(which is just the projection on
V
),

\zeta_{k}e_{V}\neq 0
. Clearly, we always may assume that
e_{V}\neq\text{Id}
.

Set
\omega_{n}:=\text{exp}\left(\frac{2\pi i}{n}\right)
, and recall
that the characters of irreducible representations of
G
are given
in
\left\{\chi_{0},\chi_{1},...,\chi_{n-1}\right\}
, where
\chi_{l}\left(g^{s}\right)=\omega_{n}^{sl}
, and so the corresponding
idempotents
\left\{e_{\chi_{0}},e_{\chi_{1}},...,e_{\chi_{n-1}}\right\}

are given by the well-known formula

\displaystyle e_{\chi_{l}}=\frac{1}{n}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}\chi_{l}\left(g^{-s}\right)g^{s}=\frac{1}{n}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}\omega_{n}^{-sl}g^{s}.

Suppose
\zeta_{k}e_{\chi_{l}}=0
for some
l\neq 0
(note that
e_{\chi_{0}}=Id
). Since

\displaystyle ge_{\chi_{l}}=\frac{1}{n}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}\omega_{n}^{-sl}g^{s+1}=\omega_{n}^{l}e_{\chi_{l}},

it follows that

\displaystyle 0=\zeta_{k}e_{\chi_{l}}=\left(1+\omega_{n}^{l}+...+\omega_{n}^{kl}\right)e_{\chi_{l}}=\left(\frac{1-\left(\omega_{n}^{l}\right)^{k+1}}{1-\omega_{n}^{l}}\right)e_{\chi_{l}},

(13)

which implies
\left(\omega_{n}^{l}\right)^{k+1}=1
. In particular

\displaystyle n\mid l\left(k+1\right),

and (as
l<n
)
k+1
and
n
are not coprime.

Conversely, if
k+1
and
n
are not coprime, there exists

l<n
such that
n\mid l\left(k+1\right)
, and thus by Equation (
13
) we obtain
\zeta_{k}e_{\chi_{l}}=0
, so
\zeta_{k}
is not an invertible
element.
∎

Generalized Thomas–Yau Uniqueness Theorems
Yohsuke Imagi

Abstract
We generalize Thomas–Yau’s uniqueness theorem [ 17 , Theorem 4.3] in two ways. We prove a stronger statement for special Lagranigans and include minimal Lagrangians in Kähler–Einstein manifolds or more generally J J -minimal Lagrangians introduced by Lotay and Pacini [ 13 , 14 ] . In every case the heart of the proof is to make certain Hamiltonian perturbations. For this we use the method by Imagi, Joyce and Oliveira dos Santos [ 8 , Theorem 4.7] .

Introduction

In this paper we improve and generalize Thomas–Yau’s theorem
[
17
, Theorem 4.3]
.
Our first main result is the following.
For the more complete statement see Corollary
6.3
(ii).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X
be a Kähler manifold equipped with a holomorphic volume form.
Let the cohomology Fukaya category
H{\mathbin{\cal F}}(X)
have two isomorphic objects supported near two closed irreducibly-immersed special Lagrangians
L_{1},L_{2}
respectively.
Then
L_{1}=L_{2}\subseteq X.

Remark 1.2
.

(i)

Thomas and Yau prove their uniqueness theorem for closed special Lagrangians
L_{1},L_{2}
in the same Hamiltonian isotopy class.
But their proof works under the weaker hypothesis as above; that is, we need only that
L_{1},L_{2}
have the same isomorphism class in
H{\mathbin{\cal F}}(X).

This
H{\mathbin{\cal F}}(X)
is a (usual) associative category obtained from the
A_{\infty}
category
{\mathbin{\cal F}}(X)
by taking the (
\mathfrak{m}^{1}
) cohomology groups of the hom spaces in it.
The key fact is that if
L_{1},L_{2}
are Hamiltonian isotopic then
L_{1},L_{2}
will define isomorphic objects in
H{\mathbin{\cal F}}(X)
(after given the additional data including bounding cochains).

It is clear that we need only the weaker hypothesis because Thomas and Yau use in their proof only the Floer cohomology group
HF^{*}(L_{1},L_{2}),
the hom space in
H{\mathbin{\cal F}}(X).

But when Thomas and Yan wrote their paper, the theory of Fukaya categories was much less developed at that time, so the statement was presumably better-sounding with Hamiltonian isotopies.
In this paper we improve Thomas–Yau’s theorem by using the more developed theory of Fukaya categories as follows.

(ii)

Theorem
1.1
is stronger than the original theorem in the respect that
L_{1},L_{2}
need not have (even after shift) the same phase. This would also follow from the following statement: two Lagrangians underlying the same isomorphism class of objects in
H{\mathbin{\cal F}}(X)
should have the same homology class in
X;
although we shall not discuss this in the present paper.
The fact that
L_{1},L_{2}
have the same shift up to shift will be proved in Corollary
4.7
(before giving the full proof of the theorem), for which we shall use some simple fact about Maslov indices and some nonvanishing results for Floer cohomology groups (which we recall in §
4
).

Also we do not suppose that either
L_{1}
or
L_{2}
itself underlies an object of
H{\mathbin{\cal F}}(X)

nor do we suppose even that it is cleanly immersed as Fukaya
[
3
]
does.
But we do suppose that we can perturb
L_{1},L_{2}
both to generically immersed Lagrangians
which underlie objects of
H{\mathbin{\cal F}}(X).

This is how we deal with badly immersed Lagrangians in the Floer-theory context in this paper.

(iii)

The idea of the proof of Theorem
1.1
is the same as that of Thomas and Yau. But we include the modification by Imagi, Joyce and Oliveira dos Santos
[
8
, Theorem 4.7]
because we can unfortunately not justify the original Morse-theory argument
[
17
, Theorem 4.3]
.

Another natural generalization is to
minimal Lagrangians in Kähler–Einstein manifolds
or more generally to
J
-minimal Lagrangians introduced by Lotay and Pacini
[
13
,
14
]
.
At the moment we state the result only in the Kähler–Einstein case.
For the more complete statement see Corollary
6.3
(i).

Theorem 1.3
.

Let

be a
{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}_{k}
-graded Kähler–Einstein manifold of complex dimension

and

Let

either
{\mathbf{b}}_{1}\cong{\mathbf{b}}_{2}
or

(1.1)

\displaystyle HF^{i}({\mathbf{b}}_{1},{\mathbf{b}}_{1})\cong HF^{i}({\mathbf{b}}_{2},{\mathbf{b}}_{2})\neq 0\text{ for }i=0,n.

(1.2)

Then

Remark 1.4
.

The more precise meaning of
L_{1},L_{2}
having the same grading on
L_{1}\cap L_{2}
is given in Definition
2.1
below.
We make a more geometric definition of gradings than Seidel
[
16
]
does.

In the circumstances of Theorem
1.3
,
if
c_{1}(X)>0
there may be many automorphisms of
X

and many zero objects of

especially in the toric case as Fukaya, Oh, Ohta and Ono
[
5
]

So we can hardly expect a general uniqueness statement to hold.
But for instance, to the
Clifford torus

T^{n}:=\{[z_{0},\cdots,z_{n}]\in{\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}}P^{n}:|z_{0}|=\cdots=|z_{n}|\}

(1.3)

we can apply Theorem
1.3
with the latter alternative of (

).
We can in fact take

as we show in Example

Also according to Fukaya, Oh, Ohta and Ono
[
5
]

there are

with

So (

) holds and we have:

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
L\subseteq{\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}}P^{n}
be a closed irreducibly-immersed
{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}_{2n+2}
-graded minimal Lagrangian such that
L,T^{n}
have the same grading on
L\cap T^{n}.

Let

have an object which is supported near
L
and isomorphic to one of the
(n+1)
objects
{\mathbf{b}}
’s above
(
all supported on
T^{n}).

Then
L=T^{n}.

On the other hand, if
c_{1}(X)\leqslant\penalty 0
the nonzero condition (
1.2
) holds automatically in certain circumstances.
For instance, if either
L_{1}
or
L_{2}
is embedded then (
1.2
) holds automatically by
Fukaya, Oh, Ohta and Ono’s theorem
[
4
, Theorem E]
.
If

there is a stronger version of it which we use for Theorem
1.1
.

If
c_{1}(X)<0
Theorem
1.2
extends to
J
-minimal Lagrangians.
They are locally unique, so the result is interesting only in the global context.

For the proofs we follow in outline that by Thomas and Yau
[
17
, Theorem 4.3]
.
In every case the heart of the proof is to
make certain Hamiltonian perturbations of the two Lagrangians.
The Floer-theory condition implies that the two perturbed Lagrangians have at least one intersection point of index
0
or

at which we do by analysis a sort of unique continuation.

But for the Hamiltonian perturbation process we use the method by

[
8

as mentioned above.
The modified method works only in the real analytic category.
This causes no problem in the Kähler–Einstein case because

On the other hand, in Theorem

and in the

-minimal version of Theorem

we work in the

We begin in §

In §

In §

In §

In §

They are essentially at the chain level because §

Finally we remark that Thomas–Yau’s uniqueness theorem is just a bit of their whole proposal
[
17
]
which is now improved by Joyce
[
10
]
.

Geometric Gradings

We make a geometric definition of graded Lagrangians:

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(X,\omega)
a symplectic manifold,
J
an
\omega
-compatible almost-complex structure on
X

and
K_{X}
the canonical bundle over
(X,J).

Let
k
be either a positive integer or infinity, write
{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}_{k}:={\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}/k{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}
for
k
finite and write
{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}_{\infty}:={\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}.

By a
{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}_{k}
-grading
of
(X,\omega,J)
we mean for
k<\infty
the pair of a complex line bundle
K_{X}^{2/k}
and a bundle isomorphism
(K_{X}^{2/k})^{k}\cong K_{X}^{2},

and for
k=\infty
a nowhere vanishing section
\Psi
of
K_{X}^{2}.

Suppose given such a
{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}_{k}
-grading of
X
and a Hermitian metric
g
on
(X,J).

Then for every
x\in X
and for every Lagrangian plane
\ell\subseteq T_{x}X

there is a
g
-orthogonal decomposition
T_{x}X=\ell\oplus J\ell
and accordingly
a canonical
g
-unit section of
K_{X}^{2}
which we denote by
\Omega_{\ell}^{2};

that is, if
e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}\in\ell
are a
g
-orthonormal basis then

\Omega_{\ell}^{2}:=\pm e^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e^{n}\wedge Je^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge Je^{n}.

There is also a canonical
k
-fold cover
\Lag^{k}TX
of the Lagrangian Grassmannian
\Lag TX
defined for
k<\infty
by

\Lag^{k}TX:=\{(\ell,\alpha)\in\Lag TX\times_{X}K_{X}^{2/k}:\Omega_{\ell}^{2}=\alpha^{k}\}

and for
k=\infty
by
\Lag^{\infty}TX:=\{(\ell,\phi)\in\Lag TX\times_{X}{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}:\Omega_{\ell}^{2}=e^{i\phi}\Psi\}.

By a
{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}_{k}
-grading of an immersed Lagrangian
L\subseteq(X,\omega)

we mean a lift to
\Lag^{k}TX
of the tangent-space map
L\to\Lag TX,

that is, for
k<\infty
a section
\alpha:L\to K_{X}^{2/k}
with
\alpha^{k}=\Omega_{L}^{2}

and for
k=\infty
a section
\phi:L\to S^{1}
with
e^{i\phi}\Psi=\Omega_{L}^{2}.

It is unique up to
{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}_{k}
-shifts,
where the shift
[1]
is defined for
k<\infty
by
(L,\alpha)[1]:=(L,e^{2\pi i/k}\alpha)

and for
k=\infty
by
(L,\phi)[1]:=(L,\phi+2\pi).

Remark 2.2
.

The advantage of this definition is that given two Lagrangians
L_{i}

(i=1,2)
graded by
\alpha_{i}
or
\phi_{i}
as above,
we can compare the two gradings
\alpha_{i}
or
\phi_{i}.

In particular, it makes sense to say that they are equal or not.

We make another definition in the circumstances of Definition
2.1
:

Definition 2.3
.

For
i=1,2
let
L_{i}\subseteq(X,\omega)
be a Lagrangian with a
{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}_{k}
-grading
\alpha_{i}:L\to K_{X}^{2/k},

k<\infty.

Suppose
L_{1},L_{2}
intersect transversely at a point
x\in L_{1}\cap L_{2}.

We define the Maslov index
\mu_{L_{1},L_{2}}(x)\in{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}_{k}.

Define
e^{i\tau}\in S^{1}
by
\alpha_{2}=e^{i\tau}\alpha_{1}\in K_{X}^{2/k}
over
x.

Take an isomorphism
(T_{x}X,J|_{x},g|_{x})\cong{\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}}^{n}

which maps
T_{x}L_{1}
to
{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}\subseteq{\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}}^{n}
and
T_{x}L_{2}
to

\{(e^{i\theta_{1}}x_{1},\cdots,e^{i\theta_{n}}x_{n})\in{\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}}^{n}:x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\in{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}\}.

for some
\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{n}\in(0,\pi).

These
\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{n}
are unique up to order
and we have

\Omega_{L_{2}}^{2}=\pm e^{-2i(\theta_{1}+\cdots+\theta_{n})}\Omega_{L_{1}}^{2}.

So we can define

\mu_{L_{1},L_{2}}(x):=\frac{1}{2\pi}(2\theta_{1}+\cdots+2\theta_{n}+k\tau)\in{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}\text{ modulo }k{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}.

(2.1)

If
k=\infty
and if for
i=1,2
the
L_{i}
is
{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}
-graded by
\phi_{i}:L_{i}\to{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}
then

\mu_{L_{1},L_{2}}(x):=\frac{1}{2\pi}(2\theta_{1}+\cdots+2\theta_{n}-\phi_{1}(x)+\phi_{2}(x))\in{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}.

(2.2)

We recall now the notion of

Let

In the symplectic context including this definition and §

.

Let

By hypothesis

Suppose now that

Give

by the obvious homotopy,

Hypothesis 4.1

\omega

If

is

We use the following version of

algebra structure constructions.

Theorem 4.2
.

There is on

Remark 4.3
.

The embedded case is proved by Fukaya

]

This will extend to the immersed case if we give up having the strict unit and satisfy ourselves with
a cohomological unit.
To have the strict unit we ought to perturb in a certain manner the relevant pseudoholomorphic-curve moduli spaces.

But also we give it another proof in which we use the open-closed map.

By a

We denote by

1
-form operator may be well approximated by
dd^{*}
:

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
(X,\omega,J)
be a Kähler manifold and let
g
be a Hermitian metric on
(X,J).

Let
L\subseteq X
be a closed immersed
g
-Maslov-zero Lagrangian
and let
NL
be the
g
-normal bundle to
L\subseteq X.

Then there exists a constant
c>0
such that for every sufficiently
C^{1}
-small
v\in C^{\infty}(NL)

whose graph
(
embedded in
X
by the
g
-exponential map
)
is
g
-Maslov-zero, we have at every point of
L

|dd^{*}\hat{v}|\leqslant\penalty c|v|_{C^{1}}(1+|v|_{C^{2}})

(5.1)

where
\hat{v}
is the same as in Lemma
3.6
,

|v|_{C^{1}}:=|v|+|\nabla v|

and
|v|_{C^{2}}:=|v|+|\nabla v|+|\nabla^{2}v|;

the latter two are computed pointwise on
L
with respect to
g.

Proof.

Let
v\in C^{\infty}(NL)
be sufficiently
C^{1}
-small
and
Av\in C^{\infty}(T^{*}L)
the Maslov
1
-form of the graph of
v.

The tangent space to the graph of
v
involves the first derivative
\nabla v

so the canonical section of
K_{X}^{2}
over the graph of
v
is of the form
B(v,\nabla v)

where
B:NL\oplus(T^{*}L\otimes NL)\to NL
is a smooth function.
Then
Av
involves the differentiation of
B(v,\nabla v)
so

Av=A_{1}(v,\nabla v)+A_{2}(v,\nabla v)\cdot\nabla^{2}v

(5.2)

where
A_{1}:NL\oplus(T^{*}L\otimes NL)\to NL
and
A_{2}:NL\oplus(T^{*}L\otimes NL)\to NL\otimes TL\otimes TL
are some smooth functions.
Denote by
A^{\prime}
the linearization of
A
at
0\in C^{\infty}(NL).

Then by (
5.2
) there is a
v
-independent constant
c_{1}>0
such that

|(A-A^{\prime})v|\leqslant\penalty c_{1}[|v|^{2}+|\nabla v|^{2}+|\nabla^{2}v|(|v|+|\nabla v|)]\leqslant\penalty c_{1}|v|_{C^{1}}|v|_{C^{2}}

(5.3)

where the same constant
c_{1}
will do for both the inequalities.
Now by Lemma
3.6

A^{\prime}
is, up to lower-order terms, equal to
dd^{*}\hat{v};

that is, there is a
v
-independent constant
c_{2}>0
such that

|A^{\prime}v-dd^{*}\hat{v}|\leqslant\penalty c_{2}|v|_{C^{1}}.

So by (
5.3
) we have
|(A-A^{\prime})v|\leqslant\penalty\max\{c_{1},c_{2}\}|v|_{C^{1}}(1+|v|_{C^{2}}).

This implies indeed that if
Av=0
then (
5.1
) holds.
∎

We give a corollary to the lemma above:

Corollary 5.5
.

In the circumstances of Lemma
5.4

take a Weinstein neighbourhood of
L\subseteq X.

Then there exists a constant
c>0
such that for every sufficiently
C^{1}
-small
1
-form
u\in C^{\infty}(T^{*}L)
whose graph is also minimal, we have at every point of
L

|dd^{*}u|\leqslant\penalty c|u|_{C^{1}}(1+|u|_{C^{2}}).

(5.4)

Proof.

The two embeddings, one given by the exponential map and the other given by the Weinstein neighbourhood, are mutually related by a diffeomorphism
F
between neighbourhoods of the zero sections of
T^{*}L,NL
respectively which induces the identity on the zero section
L,
and over every point of
L,
a diffeomorphism of the two fibres.
So there is a smooth function
F_{1}:T^{*}L\to NL\otimes TL

such that for every sufficiently
C^{1}
-small
u\in C^{\infty}(T^{*}L)
we have

Fu=F_{1}u\cdot u.

In particular there is a
u
-independent constant
c>0
such that

|Fu|_{C^{1}}\leqslant\penalty|u|_{C^{1}}\text{ and }|Fu|_{C^{2}}\leqslant\penalty c|u|_{C^{2}}.

(5.5)

The function
Gu:=-JFu\lrcorner g
is also of the same form; that is,
there is a smooth function
G_{1}:T^{*}L\to T^{*}L\otimes TL
such that

Gu=G_{1}u\cdot u.

This implies in turn that we have, making
c
large enough,

|dd^{*}Gu|\leqslant\penalty c|dd^{*}u|+c|u|_{C^{1}}(1+|u|_{C^{2}})

(5.6)

Now by (
5.1
) we have, making
c
large enough,

|dd^{*}Gu|\leqslant\penalty c|Fu|_{C^{1}}(1+|Fu|_{C^{2}}).

So by (
5.6
) we have (
5.4
) with
c
large enough.
∎

Now we prove Theorem
5.1
in three steps:

Step 1: proof of the first part of
(i)
.

In this case we take
L_{1}^{\prime}=L_{1}
and perturb only
L_{2}.

We take a Weinstein neighbourhood of

and define
L_{2}^{\prime}
as the graph of a certain exact
1
-form
df
on
L_{2}
with

taken as follows.
Since

are both connected and analytic with

as subsets of

it follows that
L_{2}\setminus L_{1}
is dense in

So there is a Morse function
f:L_{2}\to{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}
with

on

Since
S\subseteq L_{1}\cap L_{2}
it follows then that
there is a constant

such that we have near

|f|_{C^{2}}<c|df|.

(5.7)

This condition is preserved under rescalings and smooth perturbations of

so we can make
L_{2}^{\prime}
arbitrarily
C^{\infty}
close to
L_{2}
and transverse to
L_{1}.

Near
S
take a local component of
L_{1}
if need be (in the strictly immersed case) and write it as the graph over
L_{2}
of some closed
1
-form
u.

This is possible by the definition of

and in fact we have also
u=\nabla u=0
on

So by Lemma

we have near

|u|\ll|\nabla u|\ll 1.

(5.8)

On the other hand, by Corollary
5.5
with
L_{2}
in place of

there is a constant

which depends upon

such that we have near

|dd^{*}u|\leqslant\penalty c^{\prime}(|u|+|\nabla u|)\leqslant\penalty 2c^{\prime}|\nabla u|\ll 1

(5.9)

where the second inequality follows from (
5.8
).
Applying to

the Łojasiewicz estimate, we find a constant
\epsilon>0
such that we have near
S

|d^{*}u|\leqslant\penalty|dd^{*}u|^{1+\epsilon}\leqslant\penalty(2c^{\prime}|\nabla u|)^{1+\epsilon}\ll|\nabla u|

(5.10)

where the second inequality follows from (
5.9
).

We show now that we have on
L_{1}\cap L_{2}^{\prime}
near
S

|d^{*}(u-df)|\leqslant\penalty n^{-1/2}|\nabla(u-df)|.

(5.11)

On
L_{1}\cap L_{2}^{\prime}
near
S
we have

and so by (
5.7
)

|\nabla(u-df)|\geqslant|\nabla u|-|\nabla df|\geqslant|\nabla u|-c|df|=|\nabla u|-c|u|\geqslant\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|

(5.12)

where the last inequality follows from (
5.8
).
On the other hand by (
5.10
) we have on
L_{1}\cap L_{2}^{\prime}
near
S

|d^{*}(u-df)|\leqslant\penalty|d^{*}u|+c|df|\leqslant\penalty\frac{1}{4}n^{-1/2}|\nabla u|+c|u|\leqslant\penalty\frac{1}{2}n^{-1/2}|\nabla u|

(5.13)

where the last inequality follows again from (
5.8
).
By (
5.12
) and (
5.13
) we have (
5.11
) as we want.

Finally we take a point
x\in L_{1}\cap L_{2}^{\prime}
near
S

and compute its index,
which is the index of the nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form
\nabla(u-df)
on
T_{x}L_{2}.

Diagonalize it by an orthonormal basis of
T_{x}L_{2}
and denote by
\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n}
the diagonal entries.
Then by (
5.11
) we have

\textstyle|\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n}|\leqslant\penalty n^{-1/2}(\lambda_{1}^{2}+\cdots+\lambda_{n}^{2})^{1/2}\leqslant\penalty\max\{|\lambda_{1}|,\cdots,|\lambda_{n}|\}.

But

are all nonzero so they have not all the same sign; that is, the index is neither
0
nor
n
as we want.
∎

Step 2: proof of the latter part of
(i)
.

We make first a generic Hamiltonian perturbation of
L_{2}
which underlies an object of
H{\mathbin{\cal F}}(X)

and we define

as a further Hamiltonian perturbation of it.
As in step 1 we take a Weinstein neighbourhood of
L_{2}
and define

as the graph of some exact

-form

over
L_{2}

with

on

Also as in step 1 write

near

as the graph of some real analytic closed
1
-form
u
on
L_{2}
near
S.

Take a generic Hamiltonian perturbation
L_{1}^{\prime}
of
L_{1}
which underlies
H{\mathbin{\cal F}}(X)

and is close enough to

to be written near

as the graph of

over
L_{2}
with

|h_{1}|_{C^{2}}\ll|h_{2}|_{C^{2}}.

Since

on

it follows that there is a constant

such that near

we have

And then putting

we have

|h_{2}|_{C^{2}}<\frac{c}{4}(|df|+|dh_{1}|)\leqslant\penalty\frac{c}{4}|df|+\frac{1}{2}|h_{2}|_{C^{2}}

(5.15)

where the last inequality follows from (

). The whole estimate (

) implies

Hence using again (

) we find

that is, the estimate (

) holds as in step 1.
Following the subsequent estimates we see also that (

) holds
now on

This implies again that

or

∎

.

From Lemma

and Theorem

we deduce:

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
(X,\omega,J)
be a real analytic Kähler manifold of complex dimension
n,

{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}_{k}
-graded with
k\geqslant n
and given
another real analytic Hermitian metric
g.

Let
L_{1},L_{2}\subseteq X
two distinct closed irreducibly-immersed
g
-Maslov-zero Lagrangians with the same grading on
L_{1}\cap L_{2}.

Then there exist arbitrarily-small Hamiltonian deformations
L_{1}^{\prime},L_{2}^{\prime}
of
L_{1},L_{2}

which intersect generically each other with no intersection point of index
0
or
n.

If also Hypothesis
4.1
holds and
L_{1},L_{2}
nearly underlie objects of
H{\mathbin{\cal F}}(X)
then we can make
L_{1}^{\prime},L_{2}^{\prime}
underly objects of
H{\mathbin{\cal F}}(X).

Proof.

Let
U
be as in Theorem
5.1
and corresponding to this
U
let
\epsilon
be as in Lemma
2.7
.
Then corresponding to this
\epsilon
let
L_{i}^{\prime}

(i=1,2)
be such
\epsilon
-perturbations of
L_{i}
as in Theorem
5.1
.
Applying Lemma
2.7
to the intersection points outside
U

and Theorem
5.1
to those in
U

we see that
L_{1}^{\prime},L_{2}^{\prime}
are such as we want.
∎

We make now the
C^{\infty}
version of Theorem
6.1
:

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
(X,\omega,J)
be a Kähler manifold of complex dimension
n

which is either:
{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}
-graded by a
J
-holomorphic volume form
\Psi
and given the conformally Kähler metric
g
as in
\lx@sectionsign\ref{sec2}
by using
\eqref{ps};

or
{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}_{k}
-graded with
k\geqslant n
and given another Kähler metric
g

whose the Ricci
(1,1)
-form is
-\omega.

Let
L_{1},L_{2}\subseteq(X,\omega,J,g)
be two distinct closed irreducibly-immersed
g
-Maslov-zero Lagrangians with the same grading on
L_{1}\cap L_{2}.

Suppose that one of the following three conditions holds:
(i)

X
is compact;
(ii)

X
is
\omega
-convex,
\omega
is exact and
J
is Stein; or

(iii)
either
L_{1}
or
L_{2}
is generically immersed.
Then the same conclusion holds as in Theorem
6.1
(both the two sentences in it).

Proof.

We reduce the problem to the real analytic case by a further perturbation process.
In the case (i) we use the fact that
for every compact Kähler manifold
(X,\omega,J)

there exists on
(X,J)
a smooth family
(\omega_{t})_{t\in{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}}
of Kähler forms with
\omega_{0}=\omega
and
\omega_{t},

t\neq 0,
analytic.
This seems well known but for clarity we give it a proof.
Denote by
g_{\omega}
the Kähler metric of
(X,\omega,J)

and take on
(X,J)
a smooth family of
Riemannian
metrics
g_{t}^{\prime}

with
g_{0}^{\prime}=g_{\omega}
and
g_{t}^{\prime},

t\neq 0,
analytic.
Then
g_{t}:={\frac{1}{2}}(g_{t}^{\prime}+J^{*}g_{t}^{\prime})
defines on
(X,J)
a smooth family of Hermitian metrics with

g_{0}=g_{\omega}
and
g_{t},

t\neq 0,
analytic.
Denote by
{\partial}_{t}^{*},\bar{\partial}_{t}^{*}
the respective formal
g_{t}
-adjoints of
{\partial},\bar{\partial}

and following Kodaira and Spencer
[
11
, §6]

introduce the smooth family of elliptic operators

E_{t}:={\partial}\bar{\partial}\bar{\partial}_{t}^{*}{\partial}_{t}^{*}+\bar{\partial}_{t}^{*}{\partial}_{t}^{*}{\partial}\bar{\partial}+\bar{\partial}_{t}^{*}{\partial}{\partial}_{t}^{*}\bar{\partial}+{\partial}_{t}^{*}\bar{\partial}\bar{\partial}_{t}^{*}{\partial}+{\partial}_{t}^{*}{\partial}+\bar{\partial}_{t}^{*}\bar{\partial}.

Since
g_{0}
is Kähler it follows readily that

E_{0}
is apart from the last two terms just the squared Laplacian.
The last two terms are so added that the kernel of
E_{t}
consists of closed forms.
Denote by
\omega_{t}^{\prime}
the projection of
\omega
onto the kernel of
E_{t}
in the space of
(1,1)
-forms.
Then by Kodaira–Spencer’s result
[
11
, Proposition 8]
the kernel of
E_{t}
has dimension independent of
t.

So by another result of them
[
11
, Theorem 5]

\omega_{t}^{\prime}
is smooth with respect to
t.

Putting
\omega_{t}:={\frac{1}{2}}(\omega_{t}^{\prime}+\overline{\omega}_{t}^{\prime})
we see that
\omega_{t}
is such as we want.

In the case (ii) we write
\omega=dJdp
for some
p:X\to{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}
and perturb
p
to a real analytic function.
In the case (iii) we take a Stein open set in
(X,J)
on which
\omega
is exact and which contains the generically immersed Lagrangian, say

This is possible because every self-intersection point of
L_{1}
is a transverse double point;
the Stein structure near it is given by taking the product of squared distances from the two components of the generically immersed Lagrangian, which is a plurisubharmonic function.

In every case there are Kähler perturbations
\omega_{t}
of
\omega
and by Moser’s theorem diffeomorphisms

with
\omega=\Phi_{t}^{*}\omega_{t}.

Then
\omega
is analytic with respect to

and for

the Lagrangian

is a nearly-zero Maslov-form

So by Theorem
3.7
there is a

-Maslov-zero Hamiltonian perturbation

of

to which we can apply Theorem
5.1
(ii).
Combining it with Lemma

as in the proof of Theorem

holds.
∎

Finally using the results of §
4
we get:

Corollary 6.3
.

(i)

Let
(X,\omega,J,g,L_{1},L_{2})
be such as in Theorem

or Theorem

except that

may be equal.
Let Hypothesis

or

and

(6.1)

Suppose also that

(6.2)

More generally, suppose as in Corollary

(ii)

Let
(X,\omega,J)
be a Kähler manifold equipped with a holomorphic volume form;
and
L_{1},L_{2}\subseteq X
two closed irreducibly-immersed special Lagrangians.
Suppose that either
\omega
is
J
-analytic or one of

of Theorem

4.1

Proof.

Suppose contrary to the assertion that

Then perturbing

as in Theorem

or Theorem

we see that

is supported in degrees

But by hypothesis we have

so

If also

which however contradicts (

) or Corollary

. This is the first case of (

).

In the second case we have

but

Then from Theorem

we see only that

But again we have

Then

(i).

For Corollary

∎

The P=W conjecture for GL n \mathrm{GL}_{n}
Davesh Maulik, Junliang Shen

Abstract
. We prove the P = W P=W conjecture for GL n \mathrm{GL}_{n} for all ranks n n and curves of arbitrary genus g ≥ 2 g\geq 2 . The proof combines a strong perversity result on tautological classes with the curious Hard Lefschetz theorem of Mellit. For the perversity statement, we apply the vanishing cycles constructions in our earlier work to global Springer theory in the sense of Yun, and prove a parabolic support theorem.

Introduction

Throughout, we work over the complex numbers
{\mathbb{C}}
.

0.1.
The P=W conjecture

The purpose of this paper is to present a proof of the
P=W
conjecture by de Cataldo–Hausel–Migliorini
[
6
]
for arbitrary rank
n
and genus
g\geq 2
.

Let
C
be a nonsingular irreducible projective curve of genus
g\geq 2
. For two coprime integers
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
and
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, there are two moduli spaces
M_{\mathrm{Dol}}
and
M_{B}
, called the Dolbeault and the Betti moduli spaces, attached to
C,n,
and
d
.

The Dolbeault moduli space parameterizes stable Higgs bundles
({\mathcal{E}},\theta)
on
C
, where
{\mathcal{E}}
is a vector bundle on
C
of rank
n
and degree
d
,
\theta:{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{E}}\otimes\Omega_{C}^{1}
is a Higgs field, and stability is defined with respect to the slope
\mu({\mathcal{E}},\theta)={\mathrm{deg}({\mathcal{E}})}/{\mathrm{rk({\mathcal{E}})}}
. The moduli space
M_{\mathrm{Dol}}
admits the structure of a completely integrable system

h:M_{\mathrm{Dol}}\to A:=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}H^{0}(C,{\Omega_{C}^{1}}^{\otimes i}),\quad({\mathcal{E}},\theta)\mapsto\mathrm{char.polynomial}(\theta),

which is referred to as the Hitchin system
[
21
,
22
]
. The Hitchin map
h
is surjective and proper; it is also Lagrangian with respect to the canonical holomorphic symplectic form on
M_{\mathrm{Dol}}
induced by the hyper-Kähler metric. The
perverse filtration
is an increasing filtration

P_{0}H^{*}(M_{\mathrm{Dol}},{\mathbb{Q}})\subset P_{1}H^{*}(M_{\mathrm{Dol}},{\mathbb{Q}})\subset\cdots\subset H^{*}(M_{\mathrm{Dol}},{\mathbb{Q}})

on the (singular) cohomology of
M_{\mathrm{Dol}}
governed by the topology of the Hitchin system
h
; see Section
1.1
for a brief review.

The Betti moduli space
M_{B}
is the (twisted) character variety associated with
\mathrm{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
and degree
d
. It parametrizes isomorphism classes of irreducible local systems

\rho:\pi_{1}(C\backslash\{p\})\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})

where
\rho
sends a loop around a chosen point
p
to

e^{\frac{2\pi\sqrt{-1}d}{n}}\mathrm{Id}_{n}
. Concretely, we have

M_{B}:=\Big\{a_{k},b_{k}\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}}),~k=1,2,\dots,g:~~\prod_{j=1}^{g}[a_{j},b_{j}]=e^{\frac{2\pi\sqrt{-1}d}{n}}\mathrm{Id}_{n}\Big\}\mathbin{\mathchoice{/\mkern-6.0mu/}{/\mkern-6.0mu/}{/\mkern-5.0mu/}{/\mkern-5.0mu/}}\mathrm{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}}).

It is an affine variety whose mixed Hodge structure admits a nontrivial weight filtration

W_{0}H^{*}(M_{B},{\mathbb{Q}})\subset W_{1}H^{*}(M_{B},{\mathbb{Q}})\subset\cdots\subset H^{*}(M_{B},{\mathbb{Q}}).

Non-abelian Hodge theory
[
37
,
38
]
gives a diffeomorphism between the two very different algebraic varieties
M_{\mathrm{Dol}}
and
M_{B}
, which canonically identifies their cohomology:

(1)

H^{*}(M_{\mathrm{Dol}},{\mathbb{Q}})=H^{*}(M_{B},{\mathbb{Q}}).

The
P=W
conjecture by de Cataldo–Hausel–Migliorini
[
6
]

refines the identification (
1
); it predicts that the perverse filtration associated with the Hitchin system is matched with the (double-indexed) weight filtration associated with the Betti moduli space. This establishes a surprising connection between topology of Hitchin systems and Hodge theory of character varieties.

Conjecture 0.1
.

[The P=W conjecture for
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
,
[

]
] For any

0.2.
Idea of the proof

Our proof of Conjecture
0.1
has 4 major steps.

Step 1.

Strong perversity of Chern classes
: Using work of Markman
[
24
]
, Shende
[
36
]
, and Mellit
[
30
]
, we may reduce the
P=W
conjecture to a statement about the interaction between Chern classes of the universal family and the perverse filtration. This allows us to reduce the
P=W
conjecture to a sheaf-theoretic statement which concerns strong perversity of Chern classes.

Step 2.

Vanishing cycle techniques
: We apply the formalism of vanishing cycles to reduce the sheaf-theoretic formulation of Step 1 for the Hitchin system, to the case of twisted Hitchin systems associated with meromorphic Higgs bundles. Our motivation is from the key observation by Ngô
[
32
]
and Chaudouard-Laumon
[
2
]
, that the decomposition theorem for such twisted Hitchin systems is more manageable.

Steps 1 and 2 are carried out in Sections 1 and 2.

Step 3.

Global Springer theory
: The global Springer theory of Yun
[
41
,
42
,
43
]
produces rich symmetries for certain Hitchin moduli spaces; this proves the sheaf-theoretic formulation of Step 1 over the
elliptic locus
. For our purpose, we need a version of global Springer theory for the stable locus over the total Hitchin base. This is described in Section 3.

Step 4.

A support theorem
: Lastly, in order to extend part of Yun’s symmetries induced by Chern classes from the elliptic locus to the total Hitchin base, we prove a support theorem parallel to
[
2
]
for certain parabolic Hitchin moduli spaces. This is completed in Section 4.

The idea of combining vanishing cycle functors and support theorems was also applied in
[
25
]
to give a proof of the topological mirror symmetry conjecture of Hausel–Thaddeus
[
19
,
17
]
.

1.
Perverse filtrations and vanishing cycles

In this section, we introduce the notion of
strong perversity
and show its compatibility with vanishing cycle functors (Proposition
1.5
). This plays a crucial role in Section 2 in lifting the
P=W
conjecture sheaf-theoretically and reduce it to the twisted case.

1.1.
Perverse filtrations

Let
f:X\to Y
be a proper morphism between irreducible nonsingular quasi-projective varieties (or Deligne–Mumford stacks) with
\mathrm{dim}X=a
and
\mathrm{dim}Y=b
. Let
r
be the defect of semismallness of
f
:

r:=\mathrm{dim}X\times_{Y}X-\mathrm{dim}X.

In particular, we have
r=a-b
when
f
has equi-dimensional fibers. The perverse filtration

P_{0}H^{m}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\subset P_{1}H^{m}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\subset\dots\subset H^{m}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})

is an increasing filtration on the cohomology of
X
governed by the topology of the morphism
f
; it is defined to be

P_{i}H^{m}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}):=\mathrm{Im}\left\{H^{m-a+r}(Y,{{}^{\mathbf{p}}\tau_{\leq i}}(Rf_{*}{\mathbb{Q}}_{X}[a-r]))\to H^{m}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\right\}

where
{}^{\mathbf{p}}\tau_{\leq*}
is the perverse truncation functor
[
1
]
.

Lemma 1.1
.

If
f
has equi-dimensional fibers, the perverse filtration on
H^{m}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
terminates at
P_{m}H^{m}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
,
i.e.

P_{m}H^{m}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})=H^{m}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}).

In particular, we have
1\in P_{0}H^{0}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
.

Proof.

By definition and the decomposition theorem
[
1
]
, the dimensions of the graded pieces of the perverse filtration are given by

(2)

\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{Gr}^{P}_{i}H^{m}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})=\mathrm{dim}H^{m-i-(a-r)}\left(Y,{{}^{\mathfrak{p}}{\mathcal{H}}^{i}(Rf_{*}{\mathbb{Q}}_{X}[a-r])}\right).

Since a perverse sheaf, as a complex of constructible sheaves, is concentrated in degrees
[-b,0]
, it only has non-trivial cohomology in degrees
\geq-b
. In particular, the right-hand side of (
2
) is non-trivial only if

m-i-(a-r)\geq-b.

Since
r=a-b
, this inequality is equivalent to
m\geq i
.
∎

A cohomology class
\gamma\in H^{l}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
can be viewed as a morphism
\gamma:{\mathbb{Q}}_{X}\to{\mathbb{Q}}_{X}[l]
, which naturally induces

(3)

\gamma:Rf_{*}{\mathbb{Q}}_{X}\to Rf_{*}{\mathbb{Q}}_{X}[l]

after pushing forward along
f
. For an integer
c\geq 0
, we say that
\gamma\in H^{l}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
has
strong perversity

c
with respect to
f
if its induced morphism (
3
) satisfies

(4)

\gamma\left({{}^{\mathbf{p}}\tau_{\leq i}Rf_{*}{\mathbb{Q}}_{X}}\right)\subset{{}^{\mathbf{p}}\tau_{\leq i+(c-l)}}\left(Rf_{*}{\mathbb{Q}}_{X}[l]\right),\quad\forall i;

more precisely, the condition (
4
) says that the composition

{{}^{\mathbf{p}}\tau_{\leq i}Rf_{*}{\mathbb{Q}}_{X}}\hookrightarrow Rf_{*}{\mathbb{Q}}_{X}\xrightarrow{\gamma}Rf_{*}{\mathbb{Q}}_{X}[l]

factors through
{{}^{\mathbf{p}}\tau_{\leq i+(c-l)}}\left(Rf_{*}{\mathbb{Q}}_{X}[l]\right)\hookrightarrow Rf_{*}{\mathbb{Q}}_{X}[l]
for any
i
. Notice that
\gamma
automatically has strong perversity
l
, so this condition is interesting only when
c<l
. Combining Lemma
1.1
and the following lemma, we see that if
f
has equi-dimensional fibers and
\gamma
has strong perversity
c
, then
\gamma\in P_{c}H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
.

Lemma 1.2
.

If
\gamma\in H^{l}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
has strong perversity
c
with respect to
f
, then taking cup-product with
\gamma
satisfies

\gamma\cup-:H^{m}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\to H^{m+l}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}),\quad P_{i}H^{m}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\mapsto P_{i+c}H^{m+l}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}).

Proof.

This follows from taking global cohomology for (
4
) and noticing that

\displaystyle H^{m-a+r}\left(Y,{{}^{\mathbf{p}}\tau_{\leq i+(c-l)}}\left(Rf_{*}{\mathbb{Q}}_{X}[l+a-r]\right)\right)

\displaystyle=H^{(m-a+r)+l}

\displaystyle\left(Y,{{}^{\mathbf{p}}\tau_{\leq i+c}}\left(Rf_{*}{\mathbb{Q}}_{X}[a-r]\right)\right).\qed

In general, the perverse filtration
P_{\bullet}H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
may not be multiplicative,
i.e.
, for
\gamma_{j}\in P_{c_{j}}H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
(j=1,2,…,s), it may not be true that

\gamma_{1}\cup\gamma_{2}\cup\cdots\cup\gamma_{s}\in P_{c_{1}+\cdots+c_{s}}H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}});

see
[
5
, Exercise 5.6.8]
. In fact, it was proven in
[
9
, Theorem 0.6]
that Conjecture
0.1
is equivalent to the multiplicativity of the perverse filtration associated with the Hitchin system. The following easy observation illustrates the advantage of considering strong perversity in view of the multiplicativity issue.

Lemma 1.3
.

If the class
\gamma_{j}\in H^{l_{j}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
(
j=1,2,\dots,s
) has strong perversity
c_{j}
with respect to
f
, then the cup product

\gamma_{1}\cup\gamma_{2}\cup\cdots\cup\gamma_{s}\in H^{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{s}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})

has strong perversity
\sum_{j}c_{j}
.

1.2.
Vanishing cycles

Throughout section
1.2
, we let
g:X\to{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
be a morphism such that
X
is nonsingular and irreducible with
X_{0}=g^{-1}(0)
the closed fiber over
0\in{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
. We consider the vanishing cycle functor

\varphi_{g}:D^{b}_{c}(X)\rightarrow D^{b}_{c}(X_{0})

which preserves the perverse
t
-structures,

\varphi_{g}:\mathrm{Perv}(X)\rightarrow\mathrm{Perv}(X_{0}).

Here

\varphi_{g}:=\varphi_{g}(\mathrm{IC}_{X})=\varphi_{g}({\mathbb{Q}}_{X}[\mathrm{dim}X])\in\mathrm{Perv}(X_{0})

to denote the support of the vanishing cycle complex
\varphi_{g}
so that
\varphi_{g}\in\mathrm{Perv}(X^{\prime})

Recall that for any bounded constructible object

induces a morphism

\gamma:{\mathcal{K}}\to{\mathcal{K}}[l]

via taking the tensor product with
\gamma:{\mathbb{Q}}_{X}\to{\mathbb{Q}}_{X}[l]
. The following lemma shows the compatibility between the vanishing cycle functor and restriction of cohomology classes.

Lemma 1.4
.

With the same notation as above, let
i:X^{\prime}\hookrightarrow X
be the closed embedding. The morphism

i^{*}\gamma:\varphi_{g}\rightarrow\varphi_{g}[l]\in D^{b}_{c}(X^{\prime})

induced by the class

(applied to the object
{\mathcal{K}}=\varphi_{g}
) coincides with

\varphi_{g}(\gamma):\varphi_{g}\to\varphi_{g}[l]\in D_{c}^{b}(X^{\prime})

obtained by applying the functor
\varphi_{g}
to
\gamma:{\mathbb{Q}}_{X}\to{\mathbb{Q}}_{X}[l]
.

Proof.

Let
\iota:X_{0}\hookrightarrow X
be the closed embedding of the closed fiber over
0
. By
[
23
, Definition 8.6.2]
, the vanishing cycle functor can be written as

(5)

\varphi_{g}(-)=\iota^{*}R{\mathcal{H}}{om}({\mathcal{C}},-)\in D^{b}_{c}(X_{0})

{\mathcal{C}}
a fixed complex of sheaves. The morphism obtained
by applying
R{\mathcal{H}}{om}({\mathcal{C}},-)

\gamma:{\mathbb{Q}}_{X}\to{\mathbb{Q}}_{X}[l]

\gamma

R{\mathcal{H}}{om}({\mathcal{C}},{\mathbb{Q}}_{X})

Similarly, the functor
\iota^{*}
sends the morphism induced by
\gamma

to the morphism induced by
\iota^{*}\gamma

Therefore, if we denote by
\iota^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\hookrightarrow X_{0}

\varphi_{g}(\gamma)=\iota^{*}\gamma:\iota^{\prime}_{*}\varphi_{g}\to\iota^{\prime}_{*}\varphi_{g}[l]\in D^{b}_{c}(X_{0}).

Finally, after applying
\iota^{\prime*}:D^{b}_{c}(X_{0})\rightarrow D^{b}_{c}(X^{\prime})

Strong perversity for Chern classes

In this section, we fix the rank
n
and the degree
d
with
(n,d)=1
. In Theorem

, we rephrase and then enhance Conjecture

to a statement involving

2.1.
Tautological classes

As discussed in
[
9
, Section 0.3]
, the
P=W
conjecture for
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
can be reduced to a statement involving tautological classes on
M_{\mathrm{Dol}}
and the perverse filtration associated with
h:M_{\mathrm{Dol}}\to A
, without reference to the Betti moduli space
M_{B}
. In this subsection, we recall this reduction step.

For convenience, we work with the
\mathrm{PGL}_{n}
Dolbeault moduli space to avoid normalization of a universal family as in
[
9
]
; we refer to
[
36
]
for a detailed discussion concerning the formulation of the
P=W
conjecture in terms of tautological classes for the
\mathrm{PGL}_{n}
Dolbeault moduli space.

Fix
{\mathcal{N}}\in\mathrm{Pic}^{d}(C)
. Let
\widecheck{M}_{\mathrm{Dol}}
be
the moduli stack of stable Higgs bundles
({\mathcal{E}},\theta)
with
\mathrm{det}({\mathcal{E}})\simeq{\mathcal{N}}
and
\mathrm{trace}(\theta)=0
, rigidified with respect to the generic
\mu_{n}
-stabilizer; this is the same as taking its coarse moduli space. We refer to this (nonsingular) variety as the
\mathrm{SL}_{n}
Dolbeault moduli space of degree
d
. The finite group
\Gamma:=\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(C)[n]
acts naturally on
\widecheck{M}_{\mathrm{Dol}}
via tensor product. The
\mathrm{PGL}_{n}
Dolbeault moduli space of degree
d
is recovered by taking the quotient stack

(10)

\widehat{M}_{\mathrm{Dol}}:=\widecheck{M}_{\mathrm{Dol}}/\Gamma

which is a nonsingular Deligne–Mumford stack. The
\mathrm{PGL}_{n}
Hitchin system

\widehat{h}:\widehat{M}_{\mathrm{Dol}}\to\widehat{A}:=\bigoplus_{i=2}^{n}H^{0}(C,{\Omega_{C}^{1}}^{\otimes i})

is induced by the Hitchin map associated with
\widecheck{M}_{\mathrm{Dol}}
as the
\Gamma
-action is fiberwise with respect to
h
. Analogous to the
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
case, we have the perverse filtration
P_{*}H^{*}(\widehat{M}_{\mathrm{Dol}},{\mathbb{Q}})
associated with
\widehat{h}
. The universal
\mathrm{PGL}_{n}
-bundle
{\mathcal{U}}
on
C\times\widehat{M}_{\mathrm{Dol}}
induces Chern characters

\mathrm{ch}_{k}({\mathcal{U}})\in H^{2k}(C\times\widehat{M}_{\mathrm{Dol}},{\mathbb{Q}}),\quad k\geq 2.

The tautological classes
c_{k}(\gamma)
are defined to be

c_{k}(\gamma):=\int_{\gamma}\mathrm{ch}_{k}({\mathcal{U}})=q_{M*}(q_{C}^{*}\gamma\cup\mathrm{ch}_{k}({\mathcal{U}}))\in H^{*}(\widehat{M}_{\mathrm{Dol}},{\mathbb{Q}}),\quad\gamma\in H^{*}(C,{\mathbb{Q}}),

where

.

Now we consider the
\mathrm{PGL}_{n}
Betti moduli space of degree
d
with the isomorphism on cohomology provided by non-abelian Hodge theory (

(11)

4.
Parabolic support theorem

In the last section, we prove Theorem
3.2
. This is the parabolic version of the Chaudouard–Laumon support theorem
[
2
]
. For the proof, we ultimately reduce it to a relative dimension bound which we establish in Section
4.4
.

4.1.
Review of support theorems

We start with a review of support theorems for Hitchin systems.

For a proper morphism
f:X\to Y
with
X,Y
nonsingular Deligne–Mumford stacks, the decomposition theorem
[
1
]
yields

Rf_{*}{\mathbb{Q}}_{X}\simeq\bigoplus_{i}{{}^{\mathfrak{p}}{\mathcal{H}}^{i}(Rf_{*}{\mathbb{Q}}_{X})[-i]}

with
{{}^{\mathfrak{p}}{\mathcal{H}}^{i}}(Rf_{*}{\mathbb{Q}}_{X})
semisimple perverse sheaves on
Y
; we say that a closed
Z\subset Y
is a support of
f
if it is a support of a simple summand of some
{{}^{\mathfrak{p}}{\mathcal{H}}^{i}}(Rf_{*}{\mathbb{Q}}_{X})
. A particularly interesting case is that
f
has full support, that is,
Y
is the only support of
f
; in this case the cohomology of any closed fiber of
f
is governed by the nonsingular fibers.

The study of supports for Hitchin systems was initiated by B.C. Ngô and is crucial in his proof of the fundamental lemma of the Langlands program
[
32
]
. He determines all the supports for the Hitchin system (including the
{\mathcal{L}}
-twisted cases) after restricting to the elliptic locus of the Hitchin base; that is the subset formed by integral spectral curves.

After Ngô’s work, Chaudouard–Laumon
[
2
]
observed that, if we consider the moduli space of
{\mathcal{L}}
-twisted
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
stable Higgs bundles with
\mathrm{deg}({\mathcal{L}})>0
, then Ngô’s support theorem can be extended to the total Hitchin base; in particular they showed that the
{\mathcal{L}}
-twisted
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
Hitchin system has full support. Chaudouard–Laumon’s idea was extended to the
\mathrm{SL}_{n}
case
[
4
]
, the endoscopic moduli spaces
[
25
]
, and singular cases involving strictly semistable Higgs bundles
[
26
,
27
]
. See also
[
8
,
29
]
concerning the supports over the open subset of reduced spectral curves for the untwisted (
i.e.

{\mathcal{L}}=0
) Hitchin system.

The idea of Chaudouard–Laumon
[
2
]
is to show that each support of the
{\mathcal{L}}
-twisted Hitchin system has generic point lying in the elliptic locus; this is achieved by combining two constraints: (I) the support inequality for a weak abelian fibration, and (II)
\delta
-regularity for spectral curves. We describe them in more detail.

(I)

(Support inequaltiy)
The Hitchin system admits the structure of a weak abelian fibration, that is, there is a commutative group scheme
P
over the Hitchin base acting on the moduli space which satisfies certain properties. Then an argument generalizing the Goresky–MacPherson inequality leads to a codimension estimate for the supports. More concretely, it says that any support
Z
has codimension bounded above by the
\delta
-function of the group
P
. This part was already carried out in Ngô
[
32
, Section 7]
; see
[
26
, Section 1]
for a summary.

(II)

(
\delta
-regularity)
In the case of type
A
and for the stable locus, the group scheme is obtained from the multi-degree 0 relative Picard (or, for
\mathrm{SL}_{n}
, Prym) variety associated with the family of spectral curves. Then we have the Severi inequality, referred to as
\delta
-regularity
, for the spectral curves. We refer to
[
4
, Theorem 5.4.4]
for the precise statement.

Using (I) and (II), one can deduce that no support is allowed to have generic point lying outside the open subset of integral curves; this was explained in
[
2
, Section 11]
for
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, in
[
4
, Section 6.2]
for
\mathrm{SL}_{n}
, and in
[
26
, Section 4.5]
for a general complete linear system in a del Pezzo surface. The argument is to combine the two inequalities above to deduce a numerical contradiction if there is a support that appears outside the elliptic locus.

4.3.
Weak abelian fibrations and
\delta
-regularity

A general approach for proving support theorems was given in

For the reader’s convenience, we first recall some of the necessary ingredients. As mentioned in (I) of Section

introduced by Ngô

plays a key role. A weak abelian fibration is a triple

(i)

every closed fiber of the

has dimension
=\mathrm{dim}{\mathcal{M}}-\mathrm{dim}{\mathcal{A}}
,

(ii)

the action of
{\mathcal{P}}
on
{\mathcal{M}}
has affine stabilizers, and

(iii)

the Tate module associated with
{\mathcal{P}}\to{\mathcal{A}}
is polarizable.

We refer to
[
26
, Section 1.1]
for more details.

Let

denote the moduli space of strongly parabolic Higgs bundles associated with the pair
(C,D)

\widecheck{h}^{\mathrm{spar}}:\widecheck{M}^{\mathrm{spar}}(D)\rightarrow A(D)

which is proper, surjective, and Lagrangian with respect to a natural holomorphic symplectic form

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=2}^{n}\left((j-1)(\deg(\Omega_{L}))+g-1\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{n(n-1)}{2}\deg({\mathcal{L}})+(n^{2}-1)(g-1).

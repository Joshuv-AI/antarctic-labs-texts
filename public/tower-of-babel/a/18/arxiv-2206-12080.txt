Low-energy 7 Li( n , γ n,\gamma ) 8 Li and 7 Be( p , γ p,\gamma ) 8 B radiative capture reactions within the Skyrme Hartree-Fock approach
Nguyen Le Anh, Bui Minh Loc

Abstract
The electromagnetic dipole transitions in 7 Be( p , γ p,\gamma ) 8 B and 7 Li( n , γ n,\gamma ) 8 Li reactions at the keV-energy region were analyzed simultaneously within the Skyrme Hartree-Fock potential model. The Skyrme Hartree-Fock calculation is adopted as a microscopic approach to obtain consistently the single-particle bound and scattering states in the calculation of the radial overlap function within the potential model. All non-resonant and resonant electromagnetic dipole transitions are taken into account. The electric dipole transitions are successfully described with the slightest adjustment. The resonant magnetic dipole transitions at 633 633 keV and 2184 2184 keV of 7 Be( p , γ p,\gamma ) 8 B reaction, and the one at 222 222 keV of 7 Li( n , γ n,\gamma ) 8 Li are also analyzed. The astrophysical 𝒮 17 ​ ( 0 ) \mathcal{S}_{17}(0) factor of 7 Be( p , γ p,\gamma ) 8 B reaction is found to be 22.3 22.3 eV b.

II.1
Electromagnetic dipole transition

For the proton radiative-capture reaction, the energy-dependent astrophysical
\mathcal{S}
factor
\mathcal{S}(E)
is defined by

\mathcal{S}_{aA}(E)=E\exp[2\pi\eta(E)]\sigma(E),

(1)

where the Sommerfeld parameter describing the
s
-wave barrier penetration is
\eta(E)=Ze^{2}/(\hbar v)
with
v
being the initial relative velocity between the incident particle with atomic mass number
a
and the target with the charge number
Z
and the mass number
A
. In our case,
\mathcal{S}_{aA}(E)
is the
\mathcal{S}_{17}(E)
. The general radiative-capture cross section
\sigma(E)
is given in Refs.
Baye and Descouvemont 1983
;
Huang et al. 2010
;
Xu et al. 2013
. The electric quadrupole (
E2
) transition is negligible in the energy of interest within the potential model
Kim et al. 1987
;
Typel et al. 1997
;
Bertulani and Gai 1998
;
Schümann et al. 2006
;
Tursunov et al. 2021
. Our present calculations are restricted to the
E1
and the
M1
transitions.

The initial state
|[I\otimes(\ell\otimes s)j]JM\rangle
and the final state
|[I\otimes(\ell^{\prime}\otimes s)j^{\prime}]J^{\prime}M^{\prime}\rangle
of the system are assumed as the core that is the target nucleus with one additional nucleon that is the captured nucleon at a single-particle state. The core has the internal spin
\bm{I}
that is unchanged in the calculation. Note that the ground states of the target nuclei are
I^{\pi}=3/2^{-}
in the study. The total relative angular momentum of the nucleon-target system is
\bm{j}=\bm{\ell}+\bm{s}
with
\bm{\ell}
being the relative orbital angular momentum and
s=1/2
for the nucleon. The channel spin of the initial system is
\bm{J}=\bm{I}+\bm{j}
. Therefore, the radiative-capture cross section in our case is expressed as
Rolfs 1973
;
Huang et al. 2010
;
Xu et al. 2013

\sigma(E)=\dfrac{4}{3}\dfrac{1}{\hbar v}\left(\dfrac{4\pi}{3}k^{3}_{\gamma}\right)\dfrac{1}{(2s+1)(2I+1)}\sum_{\ell jJ}\left(|\mathcal{M}_{E1}|^{2}+|\mathcal{M}_{M1}|^{2}\right),

(2)

where
k_{\gamma}
is the wave number of the photon defined as
\hbar ck_{\gamma}=E+Q
with
Q
being the
Q
value of the reaction.
\mathcal{M}_{\Omega 1}
with
\Omega
being
E
or
M
is the reduced matrix element of the electromagnetic dipole transitions. We herein follow the coupling scheme for angular momenta in Refs.
Kim et al. 1987
;
Bertulani 2003
;
Huang et al. 2010
.

\mathcal{M}_{\Omega 1}=\langle[I\otimes(\ell^{\prime}\otimes s)j^{\prime}]J^{\prime}||\mathcal{O}_{\Omega 1}||[I\otimes(\ell\otimes s)j]J\rangle.

(3)

In the case of the
E1
transition, the operator is given by

\mathcal{O}_{E1}=C_{e}rY_{1}^{\mu},

(4)

where
C_{e}=m(\tau-Z/A)e
is the effective charge in which
e
is the electric charge,
m
the reduced mass, and
\tau=0
for neutron and
\tau=1
for proton; and
Y_{1}^{\mu}
(
\mu=-1,0,1
) is the spherical harmonic function. The
\mathcal{M}_{E1}
can be reduced to the calculation of the single-particle (s.p.) reduced matrix element
de-Shalit and Talmi 1963

\mathcal{M}_{E1}=C_{e}S_{F}^{1/2}(-1)^{I+j^{\prime}+J+1}\hat{J}\hat{J^{\prime}}\left\{\begin{matrix}j^{\prime}&J^{\prime}&I\\
J&j&1\end{matrix}\right\}\mathcal{M}_{E1}^{(\rm s.p.)},

(5)

where
\hat{l}=\sqrt{2l+1}
and the curly bracket is Wigner
6j
coefficient.
S_{F}
is the spectroscopic factor that is introduced to take into account the missing configurations of the system; in the other words, it is the fractional parentage coefficient that the system can be described as our assumption
Rolfs 1973
;
Nagai et al. 2005
. It also contains missing correlations in the calculation of the single-particle state. Within the potential model for the radiative-capture reaction, its value is finally adjusted to reproduce the experimental data.

For the
M1
transition, the operator is
Bertulani 2003
;
Huang et al. 2010
;
Xu et al. 2013
;
Tursunov et al. 2021

\mathcal{O}_{M1}=\sqrt{\frac{3}{4\pi}}\left[C_{m}{\bm{\ell}_{\mu}}+2\left(\mu_{\tau}{\bm{s}_{\mu}}+\mu_{A}{\bm{I}}\right)\right].

(6)

With
\mu_{N}\approx 0.105e
fm being the nuclear magneton, the effective magnetic moment is
C_{m}=m(\tau+Z/A^{2})\mu_{N}
. The magnetic moments used in this work are
\mu_{p}=+2.793\mu_{N}
,
\mu_{n}=-1.913\mu_{N}
,
\mu_{A}=-1.399\mu_{N}
for
7
Be
Okada et al. 2008
and
+3.256\mu_{N}
for
7
Li
Beckmann et al. 1974
. The reduced matrix element for the
M1
transition is, therefore, written as the summation of three terms
Bertulani 2003
;
Huang et al. 2010
;
Xu et al. 2013
;
Tursunov et al. 2021

\mathcal{M}_{M1}=S_{F}^{1/2}\sqrt{\dfrac{3}{4\pi}}\left(\mathcal{M}_{M1}^{(0)}+\mathcal{M}_{M1}^{(1)}+\mathcal{M}_{M1}^{(2)}\right).

(7)

Similarly to the case of
E1
transition in Eq. (
5
), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{M1}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=C_{m}(-1)^{I+j+J^{\prime}+1}\hat{J}\hat{J^{\prime}}\left\{\begin{matrix}j^{\prime}&J^{\prime}&I\\
J&j&1\end{matrix}\right\}\mathcal{M}_{0}^{(\rm s.p.)},

(8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{M1}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\mu_{\tau}(-1)^{I+j+J^{\prime}+1}\hat{J}\hat{J^{\prime}}\left\{\begin{matrix}j^{\prime}&J^{\prime}&I\\
J&j&1\end{matrix}\right\}\mathcal{M}_{1}^{(\rm s.p.)},

(9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{M1}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\mu_{A}\delta_{jj^{\prime}}(-1)^{I+j^{\prime}+J+1}\hat{J}\hat{J^{\prime}}\left\{\begin{matrix}I&J&j^{\prime}\\
J^{\prime}&I&1\end{matrix}\right\}\hat{I}\sqrt{I(I+1)}\mathcal{M}_{2}^{(\rm s.p.)}.

(10)

The single-particle reduced matrix elements are well-known in literature
de-Shalit and Talmi 1963
. They are decomposed into two components: the geometrical coefficient and the radial overlap integral.
The radial overlap integrals for the
E1
and
M1
transitions are different from the
r

\mathcal{I}_{E1}=\int\phi_{n\ell^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(r)\chi_{\ell j}(E,r)r\,dr,~~~\mathcal{I}_{M1}=\int\phi_{n\ell^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(r)\chi_{\ell j}(E,r)\,dr.

(11)

The potential model for radiative-capture reaction focuses on the calculation of the radial overlap integrals in Eq. (
11
) with appropriate wave functions,
\chi_{\ell j}
and
\phi_{n\ell^{\prime}j^{\prime}}
.

II.2
Single-particle wave functions for the overlap integrals

In our approach, both scattering wave function
\chi_{\ell j}
and bound wave function
\phi_{n\ell^{\prime}j^{\prime}}
in Eq. (
11
) were simultaneously obtained within the Skyrme Hartree-Fock calculation. The detailed Hartree-Fock calculation for bound and scattering single-particle state were presented in Refs.
Dover and Van
Giai 1971
;
Dover and Van
Giai 1972
.

The calculation was started with the radial Hartree-Fock equations using the
skyrme_rpa
program in Ref.
Colò et al. 2013

\left\{\dfrac{\hbar^{2}}{2m^{*}(r)}\left[-\frac{d^{2}}{dr^{2}}+\dfrac{\ell^{\prime}(\ell^{\prime}+1)}{r^{2}}\right]+V(r)-\frac{d}{dr}\left[\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m^{*}(r)}\right]\frac{d}{dr}\right\}\varphi_{n\ell^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(r)=\varepsilon_{n\ell^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\varphi_{n\ell^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(r).

(12)

where
m^{*}(r)
is the nucleon effective mass in the Skyrme Hartree-Fock formalism. The Skyrme Hartree-Fock potential
V(r)
consists of the central
V_{\rm c}(r)
, the spin-orbit
V_{\rm s.o.}(r)
, and the one-body Coulomb potential
V_{\rm Coul.}(r)
in the case of proton

V(r)=V_{\rm c}(r)+qV_{\rm Coul.}(r)+V_{\rm s.o.}(r),

(13)

with
q
being the charge of the incident nucleon. The SLy4 interaction
Chabanat et al. 1998
is chosen in our calculation. The first-order derivative term in Eq. (
12
) is eliminated by using the transformation
Dover and Van
Giai 1971

\varphi_{n\ell^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(r)=[m^{*}_{q}(r)/m]^{1/2}\tilde{\varphi}_{\alpha}(r),

(14)

with
m
being the nucleon mass. Then Eq. (
12
) is rewritten as the usual Schrödinger equation

\left\{\dfrac{\hbar^{2}}{2m^{\prime}}\left[-\dfrac{d^{2}}{dr^{2}}+\dfrac{\ell^{\prime}(\ell^{\prime}+1)}{r^{2}}\right]+\mathcal{V}_{b}(\varepsilon_{n\ell^{\prime}j^{\prime}},r)-\varepsilon_{n\ell^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\right\}\tilde{\varphi}_{n\ell^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(r)=0,

(15)

where
m^{\prime}=mA/(A-1)
is used for the center-of-mass correction
Vaughn et al. 1970
that is important for the calculations for light nuclei. The potential
\mathcal{V}_{b}(\varepsilon_{n\ell^{\prime}j^{\prime}},r)
in Eq. (
15
) is given by

\mathcal{V}_{b}(\varepsilon_{n\ell^{\prime}j^{\prime}},r)=\mathcal{N}_{b}\mathcal{V}_{b}^{\rm c}(\varepsilon_{n\ell^{\prime}j^{\prime}},r)+q\mathcal{V}_{\rm Coul.}(r)+\mathcal{V}_{\rm s.o.}(r){\bm{\ell}^{\prime}}\cdot\bm{\sigma},

(16)

where the central part
\mathcal{V}_{b}^{\rm c}(\varepsilon_{n\ell^{\prime}j^{\prime}},r)
depends on the single-particle energy
\varepsilon_{n\ell^{\prime}j^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{b}^{\rm c}(\varepsilon_{n\ell^{\prime}j^{\prime}},r)=\dfrac{m^{*}(r)}{m}\left\{V_{\rm c}(r)+\dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{d^{2}}{dr^{2}}\left(\dfrac{\hbar^{2}}{2m^{*}(r)}\right)-\dfrac{m^{*}(r)}{2\hbar^{2}}\left[\dfrac{d}{dr}\left(\dfrac{\hbar^{2}}{2m^{*}(r)}\right)\right]^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle+\left[1-\dfrac{m^{*}(r)}{m}\right]\varepsilon_{n\ell^{\prime}j^{\prime}},

(17)

and the spin-orbit and Coulomb parts are energy-independent

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\rm Coul.}(r)

\displaystyle=[m^{*}(r)/m]V_{\rm Coul.}(r),

(18)

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\rm s.o.}(r)

\displaystyle=[m^{*}(r)/m]V_{\rm s.o.}(r).

(19)

The scattering states of the Skyrme Hartree-Fock field is obtained by replacing
\varepsilon_{n\ell^{\prime}j^{\prime}}
in Eq. (
15
) by the continuous energy
E

Dover and Van
Giai 1971
. The scattering wave function is the solution of

\left\{\dfrac{\hbar^{2}}{2m^{\prime}}\left[-\dfrac{d^{2}}{dr^{2}}+\dfrac{\ell(\ell+1)}{r^{2}}\right]+\mathcal{V}_{s}(E,r)-E\right\}\chi_{\ell j}(E,r)=0.

(20)

The potential
\mathcal{V}_{s}(E,r)
in Eq. (
15
) is

\mathcal{V}_{s}(E,r)=\mathcal{N}_{s}\mathcal{V}_{s}^{\rm c}(E,r)+q\mathcal{V}_{\rm Coul.}(r)+\mathcal{V}_{\rm s.o.}(r){\bm{\ell}}\cdot\bm{\sigma}.

(21)

The central part
\mathcal{V}_{s}^{\rm c}(E,r)
depends on the scattering energy
E
. It is obtained by replacing the single-particle energy
\varepsilon_{n\ell^{\prime}j^{\prime}}
in Eq. (
II.2
) by the positive energy
E
.
The spin-orbit and Coulomb parts are the same as Eqs. (
18
) and (
19
) as they are energy independent. Note that two parameters
\mathcal{N}_{b}
and
\mathcal{N}_{s}
are introduced in Eqs. (
16
) and (
21
), respectively.

It is known that the Skyrme Hartree-Fock approximation gives tightly bound single-particle states. The asymptotic behavior of the bound state should be corrected. Therefore, the value of
\mathcal{N}_{b}
is adjusted to obtain the single-particle energy
\varepsilon_{n\ell^{\prime}j^{\prime}}
of the first unoccupied state equal to the experimental nucleon separation energy. All occupied single-particle states are kept unchanged. It is well-known as the well-depth method.

For the scattering state, the mean-field approach such as the Skyrme Hartree-Fock calculation cannot reproduce exactly the resonance state. Therefore, the scattering state has to be corrected to reproduce the exact position of low-lying resonances. It is done by the adjusted value of
\mathcal{N}_{s}
.

The single-particle bound and scattering states in Eq. (
11
) with the corrections have the correct asymptotic forms required by the properties of the low energy radiative-capture reaction. The values of parameters are well-constrained by the experimental values including the nucleon separation energy for
\mathcal{N}_{b}
and the energy of the nuclear excited state (or the position of the resonance) for
\mathcal{N}_{s}
.

At the end of the calculation, the value of
S_{F}
is fine-tuned to reproduce the experimental data. The adjustments are typical in the framework of the potential model. The difference in our approach is that the scattering and bound wave functions are initially obtained simultaneously from a microscopic approach such as the Skyrme Hartree-Fock formalism.

Mean-field models are often considered to be inappropriate to describe light nuclei such as
A=7,8
nuclei. There have been works adopting the mean-field procedure
Sugahara et al. 1996
;
Chandel et al. 2003
. Even for
4
He some useful conclusions have been drawn using a Skyrme Hartree-Fock calculation
Shen, Yao-song and Ren, Zhongzhou 1996
and useful conclusions have be obtained for other light nuclear system
Sugahara et al. 1996
. In this manuscript we show that a Skyrme Hartree-Fock calculation leads to very reasonable results of radiative-capture cross sections with
A=7,8
nuclei.

IV
Conclusions

The Skyrme Hartree-Fock potential model is a good approach for the radiative-capture reactions at very low energy for light nuclei. The low-energy scattering problem is comfortably overcome by the Hartree-Fock calculation in the continuum. In addition the bound state is established on the traditional Hartree-Fock formalism and the well-depth method. The calculations for the
E1
transitions well reproduced the experimental data with only one parameter. The approach is also sufficient to reproduce consistently all
M1
transitions in the study. The value of the
S_{F}
that is complicated to be obtained exactly within the potential model can be determined by the precise measurements at a few hundred-keV energy that is accessible by the experiments
Junghans et al. 2010
;
Wiescher et al. 1989
. In the case of the
7
Be(
p,\gamma
)
8
B reaction, the
\mathcal{S}_{17}(0)
factor determined by the
E1
transition is
22.3
eV b. The value is not strongly dependent on the parameter in the calculation.

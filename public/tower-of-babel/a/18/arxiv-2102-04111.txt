The Newton polyhedron and positivity of F 3 2 {}_{2}F_{3} hypergeometric functions
Yong-Kum Cho, Seok-Young Chung

Abstract




Abstract.

As for the
{}_{2}F_{3}
hypergeometric function of the form

{}_{2}F_{3}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}a_{1},a_{2}\\
b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}\end{array}\biggr|-x^{2}\right]\hskip 18.49988pt(x>0),

where all of parameters are assumed to be positive, we give sufficient conditions
on
(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})
for its positivity in terms of Newton polyhedra with vertices
consisting of permutations of
\,(a_{2},a_{1}+1/2,2a_{1})\,
or
\,(a_{1},a_{2}+1/2,2a_{2}).

As an application, we obtain an extensive validity region of
(\alpha,\lambda,\mu)

for the inequality

\int_{0}^{x}(x-t)^{\lambda}\,t^{\mu}J_{\alpha}(t)\,dt\geq 0\hskip 18.49988pt(x>0).

Keywords.

Bessel functions, fractional integrals, Newton polyhedron,

{}_{p}F_{q}
hypergeometric functions, sums of squares method, transference principle.

2010 Mathematics Subject Classification.

26D15, 33C10, 33C20.

1
Introduction

This paper concerns primarily the geometric structure of parameters
that ensures the positivity of
{}_{2}F_{3}
hypergeometric functions of type

\Phi(x)\equiv{}_{2}F_{3}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}a_{1},a_{2}\\
b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}\end{array}\biggr|-x^{2}\right]\qquad(x>0),

(1.1)

where all of parameters
a_{i},b_{j}
are assumed to be positive.

In our previous studies
[
8
]
,
[
9
]
, we considered the problem of positivity
for
{}_{1}F_{2}
hypergeometric functions of similar type and described regions of positivity
in the plane of denominator-parameter pairs by means of Newton polyhedra and their hyperbolic
extensions (see Theorem
9.2
for a summary).
In a consistent manner, we aim at providing sufficient conditions for the positivity of

{}_{2}F_{3}
hypergeometric functions of type (
1.1
) in terms of
Newton polyhedra in the
(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})
-space for each fixed
(a_{1},a_{2})
.

To state briefly, let
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
be the spatial sets of all permutations of

\,(a_{2},a_{1}+1/2,2a_{1}),\quad(a_{1},a_{2}+1/2,2a_{2}),

respectively. In view of the known formula (
[
18
, 6.2]
,
[
26
, 5.4]
)

\displaystyle J_{\nu}^{2}(x)=\frac{(x/2)^{2\nu}}{\left[\Gamma(\nu+1)\right]^{2}}\,{}_{1}F_{2}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\nu+1/2\\
\nu+1,2\nu+1\end{array}\biggr|\,-x^{2}\right],

where
J_{\nu}
stands for the Bessel function of the first kind of order
\,\nu\in\mathbb{R},

it is evident that
\Phi
is nonnegative for each
\,\mathbf{b}=(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})\in\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B}.

By means of fractional integrals, to be explained in detail, it is simple to observe
a transference principle which asserts if
\Phi
is nonnegative for a point
\mathbf{b}
,
then
\Phi
remains strictly positive for all points of the octant

\,\mathbf{b}+\mathbb{R}_{+}^{3}\,
except for the corner
\mathbf{b}
. On applying this transference principle,
it is thus found that
\Phi
remains strictly positive for all points of

\bigcup_{\mathbf{b}\,\in\,\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B}}\left(\mathbf{b}+\mathbb{R}_{+}^{3}\right)

(1.4)

excluding
\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B}
where
\Phi
is shown to be nonnegative.

As it is natural to ask if this positivity region could be extended further,
we shall prove that
\Phi
indeed remains positive for all points of the convex hull
containing (
1.4
), so called the Newton polyhedron of
\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B},

under certain
admissible
conditions on
a_{1},a_{2}
. From a geometric view-point,
the Newton polyhedron of
\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B}
represents an infinite polygonal region
in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{3}
surrounded by a hexagonal or triangular bottom face, several side
quadrilaterals and infinite planar faces parallel to one of the coordinate axes.

An expansion formula due to Gasper
[
13
]
gives

\Phi(x)=x^{-2\nu}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}C_{n}(\nu)\,J_{n+\nu}^{2}(x)

(1.5)

for any real number
\nu
subject to certain condition. Once each coefficient
C_{n}(\nu)

were shown to be nonnegative, we may conclude that
\Phi
is nonnegative from this expansion.
Often referred to as
Gasper’s sums of squares method
, it is this framework that our investigation
will be based on.

In practice, as each coefficient
C_{n}(\nu)
involves either terminating

{}_{4}F_{3}
or
{}_{5}F_{4}
hypergeometric series, we are confronted with
the problem of how to determine the sign of those
terminating series. In particular, if
(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})
belongs to one of
the aforementioned side quadrilaterals, we must deal with the terminating

{}_{5}F_{4}
hypergeometric series of the from

{}_{5}F_{4}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}-n,n+\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3},\alpha_{4}\\
\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3},\beta_{4}\end{array}\right],\quad n=1,2,\cdots,

(1.6)

with appropriate values of
\,\alpha_{i},\beta_{j}\,
expressible in terms of
\,a_{i},b_{j},\nu.

In our previous work
[
8
]
, we exploited one of Whipple’s transformation formulas and an
induction argument to set up a criterion of positivity for the terminating

{}_{4}F_{3}
hypergeometric series of similar type. Concerning (
1.6
), however,
as an appropriate version of Whipple’s transformation formula
is no longer available, we shall make use of a modification of more general transformation formulas
developed by Fields and Wimp
[
12
]
to decompose it into a finite sum involving
products of terminating
{}_{4}F_{3}
hypergeometric series,
which will enable us to obtain a set of positivity criteria.

In summary, it turns out that each of Newton polyhedra with vertices

\,\mathcal{A},\,\mathcal{B},\,\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B}\,

is available as a positivity region of
\Phi
under different conditions on the pair
(a_{1},a_{2})
.
For this reason, our positivity results will be presented
in three separate statements (Theorems
6.1
,
6.2
,
8.1
)
in each of which an analytic description of the associated Newton polyhedron will be given
for the sake of practical applications.

The problem of positivity for the
{}_{2}F_{3}
hypergeometric function of type (
1.1
)
arises often as a critical issue in various disguises. As an illustration, the famous problem of
determining
\alpha,\lambda,\mu
for validity of

\int_{0}^{x}(x-t)^{\lambda}\,t^{\mu}J_{\alpha}(t)\,dt\,\geq\,0\qquad(x>0)

(1.7)

is equivalent to the problem of positivity for a special case of (
1.1
)
implicitly involved (see (
9
) for details). In application of our results,
we shall specify an extensive validity region of
(\alpha,\lambda,\mu)
for the inequality (
1.7
) which will provide
at once simplified proofs or improvements for a number of known inequalities and answers to some open conjectures.

As usual, we shall define the
{}_{p}F_{q}
hypergeometric function by

{}_{p}F_{q}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}u_{1},\cdots,u_{p}\\
v_{1},\cdots,v_{q}\end{array}\biggr|\,z\right]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(u_{1})_{n}\cdots(u_{p})_{n}}{n!\,(v_{1})_{n}\cdots(v_{q})_{n}}\,z^{n}\qquad(z\in\mathbb{C}),

(1.8)

where parameters
u_{i},v_{j}
are real numbers subject to the condition that none of

v_{j}
’s coincides with a negative integer and the coefficients are written in Pochhammer’s notation;
for
\,\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\,
and a positive integer
n
,

(\alpha)_{n}=\alpha(\alpha+1)\cdots(\alpha+n-1),\quad(\alpha)_{0}=1.

In the special case
\,z=1,
it is customary to delete the unit argument.
For the general reference, we refer to Bailey
[
4
]
and Luke
[
18
]
.

To simplify notation, we shall use throughout the logical symbol
\wedge
to denote
and
;

A\wedge B
is true if and only if both
A
and
B
are true. In addition, we shall write

\,\mathbb{R}_{+}=[0,\infty)\,
and
\,\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}=(0,\infty)\,
distinctively.

2
Transference principle and necessities

As observed by many authors in different contexts (see
[
2
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
13
]
),
it is possible to transfer a known positivity region to a much larger region
with the aid of fractional integrals. We shall denote the beta function by

B(\alpha,\beta)=\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{\alpha-1}t^{\beta-1}dt\qquad(\alpha>0,\,\beta>0).

Proposition 2.1
.

(transference principle)

Suppose

\Phi(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b};x)\equiv{}_{2}F_{3}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}a_{1},a_{2}\\
b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}\end{array}\biggr|-x^{2}\right]\geq 0\qquad(x>0)

for some
\,\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},a_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,
and
\,\mathbf{b}=(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\,
where
all of the components of
\,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\,
are assumed to be positive.

(i)

For any
\,\boldsymbol{\epsilon}=(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2},\epsilon_{3})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{3},\,
if
\,\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2},\epsilon_{3}\,
are not simultaneously zero, then

\,\Phi(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}+\boldsymbol{\epsilon};x)>0\,
for all
\,x>0.

(ii)

For any
\,\boldsymbol{\delta}=(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},\,

if
\,\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\,
are not simultaneously zero and
\,0\leq\delta_{j}<a_{j},\,j=1,2,\,

then
\,\Phi(\mathbf{a}-\boldsymbol{\delta},\mathbf{b};x)>0\,
for all
\,x>0.

Proof.

For
\,\epsilon_{1}>0,
the readily-verified representation

\displaystyle\Phi(\mathbf{a},b_{1}+\epsilon_{1},b_{2},b_{3};x)=\frac{2}{B(b_{1},\epsilon_{1})}\int_{0}^{1}\Phi(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b};xt)(1-t^{2})^{\epsilon_{1}-1}t^{2b_{1}-1}dt

implies
\,\Phi(\mathbf{a},b_{1}+\epsilon_{1},b_{2},b_{3};x)>0\,
for all
\,x>0.
By permuting
the role of
\,b_{j},\epsilon_{j}\,
or
replacing the kernel appropriately, it is straightforward to verify part (i) in all
possible occasions.

In a similar manner, if
\,0<\delta_{1}<a_{1},\,
then the representation

\displaystyle\Phi(a_{1}-\delta_{1},a_{2},\mathbf{b};x)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{B(\delta_{1},a_{1}-\delta_{1})}\int_{0}^{1}\Phi(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b};xt)(1-t^{2})^{\delta_{1}-1}t^{2a_{1}-2\delta_{1}-1}dt

implies
\,\Phi(a_{1}-\delta_{1},a_{2},\mathbf{b};x)>0\,
for all
\,x>0.\,

By permuting the role of
\,a_{j},\delta_{j}\,
or
replacing the kernel appropriately, it is also straightforward to verify part (ii)
in all possible occasions.
∎

By making use of the asymptotic behavior and the above transference principle,
we deduce a set of necessary conditions as follows.

Proposition 2.2
.

For
\,0<a_{1}\leq a_{2}\,
and
\,b_{j}>0,\,j=1,2,3,
put

\Phi(x)\equiv{}_{2}F_{3}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}a_{1},a_{2}\\
b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}\end{array}\biggr|-x^{2}\right]\qquad(x>0).

If
\,\Phi(x)\geq 0\,
for all
\,x>0,\,
then it is necessary that

b_{1}\wedge b_{2}\wedge b_{3}\geq a_{1},\quad b_{1}+b_{2}+b_{3}\geq 3a_{1}+a_{2}+1/2.

(2.1)

Moreover,
\Phi
changes sign at least once if these conditions are violated.

Proof.

Assume first that
a_{2}-a_{1}
is not an integer. Setting

\chi=b_{1}+b_{2}+b_{3}-a_{1}-a_{2}-1/2,

a well-known asymptotic expansion formula (
[
18
]
,
[
21
]
) gives

\displaystyle\Phi(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\,\Gamma(b_{1})\Gamma(b_{2})\Gamma(b_{3})\,}{\Gamma(a_{1})\Gamma(a_{2})}

\displaystyle\quad\,\,\,\biggl\{\frac{\Gamma(a_{1})\Gamma(a_{2}-a_{1})}{\,\Gamma(b_{1}-a_{1})\Gamma(b_{2}-a_{1})\Gamma(b_{3}-a_{1})\,}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{-2a_{1}}\left[1+O\left(x^{-2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\Gamma(a_{2})\Gamma(a_{1}-a_{2})}{\,\Gamma(b_{1}-a_{2})\Gamma(b_{2}-a_{2})\Gamma(b_{3}-a_{2})\,}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{-2a_{2}}\left[1+O\left(x^{-2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{\,\sqrt{\pi}\,}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{-\chi}\left[\cos\left(x-\frac{\pi\chi}{2}\right)+O\left(x^{-1}\right)\right]\biggr\}

(2.2)

as
x\to\infty
. In view of the oscillatory nature of the last part,
it is evident that the condition
\,\chi\geq 2\min(a_{1},\,a_{2})\,
or equivalently

b_{1}+b_{2}+b_{3}\geq 3a_{1}+a_{2}+1/2

(2.3)

is necessary. Otherwise,
\Phi
oscillates in sign infinitely often.

Suppose now
a_{2}-a_{1}
happens to be an integer. For
\,0<\delta<\min(1,\,a_{2}),\,
it follows from the
transference principle of Proposition
2.1
that

{}_{2}F_{3}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}a_{1},a_{2}-\delta\\
b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}\end{array}\biggr|-x^{2}\right]>0\qquad(x>0)

and hence
b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}
must satisfy the condition

b_{1}+b_{2}+b_{3}\geq a_{1}+a_{2}-\delta+1/2+2\min(a_{1},\,a_{2}-\delta).

By letting
\,\delta\to 0,\,
it is thus found that (
2.3
) is necessary.

It remains to verify the additional requirements
\,b_{j}\geq a_{1}\,
for all
j
under the
assumption (
2.3
). By using a limiting argument as above, it suffices to deal with the case

\,b_{1}+b_{2}+b_{3}>3a_{1}+a_{2}+1/2.
Let us assume
\,0<b_{1}<a_{1}.
Integrating termwise, it is
elementary to deduce

\displaystyle{}_{1}F_{2}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}a_{2}\\
b_{2},b_{3}\end{array}\biggr|-x^{2}\right]=\frac{2}{B(b_{1},a_{1}-b_{1})}\int_{0}^{1}\Phi(xt)(1-t^{2})^{a_{1}-b_{1}-1}t^{2b_{1}-1}dt>0

for all
\,x>0,
whence
\,b_{2}\wedge b_{3}>a_{2}\,
according to Theorem
9.2
.

In the case
\,a_{1}<a_{2},
the asymptotic behavior (
2
) implies

\Phi(x)\sim\frac{\,\Gamma(b_{1})\Gamma(b_{2})\Gamma(b_{3})\Gamma(a_{2}-a_{1})\,}{\Gamma(a_{2})\Gamma(b_{1}-a_{1})\Gamma(b_{2}-a_{1})\Gamma(b_{3}-a_{1})\,}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{-2a_{1}}

as
\,x\to\infty.
Replacing
b_{1}
by
b_{1}+\epsilon
with
\,\epsilon>0,
if necessary,
we may assume
\,a_{1}-1<b_{1}<a_{1}\,
so that
\,\Gamma(b_{1}-a_{1})<0\,
and the multiplicative constant
is negative. Since
\Phi
is smooth with
\,\Phi(0)=1,
it implies that
\Phi
must change sign at least once,
which contradicts the nonnegativity assumption on
\Phi
. Thus
\,b_{1}\geq a_{1}.

In the case
\,a_{1}=a_{2},\,
replacing
a_{1}
by

\,a_{1}-\delta,\,0<\delta<a_{1},\,
the same reasonings would lead to
\,b_{1}\geq a_{1}-\delta\,
and subsequently

\,b_{1}\geq a_{1}\,
by letting
\,\delta\to 0.\,
By symmetry, it is also necessary that
\,b_{2}\geq a_{1},\,b_{3}\geq a_{1}.\,

∎

3
Expansions of
{}_{p}F_{q}
hypergeometric functions

The purpose of this section is to give a list of expansion formulas for

{}_{p}F_{q}
hypergeometric functions which will be applied frequently in the sequel.

For simplicity, it is often convenient to use the vector notation

\boldsymbol{u}_{p}=(u_{1},\cdots,u_{p}),\quad(\boldsymbol{u}_{p})_{n}=(u_{1})_{n}\cdots(u_{p})_{n}

so that the
{}_{p}F_{q}
hypergeometric function of (
1.8
) can be written as

{}_{p}F_{q}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\boldsymbol{u}_{p}\\
\boldsymbol{v}_{q}\end{array}\biggr|z\right]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\boldsymbol{u}_{p})_{n}}{n!\,(\boldsymbol{v}_{q})_{n}}z^{n}.

An expansion formula due to Fields and Wimp
[
12
, 3.3]
states

\displaystyle{}_{p}F_{q}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\boldsymbol{u}_{p}\\
\boldsymbol{v}_{q}\end{array}\biggr|zw\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{r})_{n}(-z)^{n}}{n!(n+\delta)_{n}(\boldsymbol{\beta}_{s})_{n}}\,{}_{r}F_{s+1}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\boldsymbol{n+\alpha}_{r}\\
2n+\delta+1,\boldsymbol{n+\beta}_{s}\end{array}\biggr|z\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\times\,{}_{p+s+2}F_{q+r}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}-n,n+\delta,\boldsymbol{\beta}_{s},\boldsymbol{u}_{p}\\
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{r},\boldsymbol{v}_{q}\end{array}\biggr|w\right],

valid for any nonnegative integers
\,p,q,r,s,\,
real parameters
\,\boldsymbol{u}_{p},\boldsymbol{v}_{q},\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{r},\boldsymbol{\beta}_{s},\delta,\,
and complex arguments
z,w
, provided that
none of
\,\boldsymbol{v}_{q},\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{r},\boldsymbol{\beta}_{s},\delta\,
coincides with a negative integer
and each of the involved series converges. We refer to Luke and Coleman
[
19
]
for closely related
expansion formulas.

In view of the formula (
1
), if we choose
\,r=s=1\,
and take

\alpha_{1}=\nu+1/2,\,\,\beta_{1}=\nu+1,\,\,\delta=2\nu,\,

then (
3
) yields Gasper’s
sums of squares formula

[
13
, (3.1)]

\displaystyle{}_{p}F_{q}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\boldsymbol{u}_{p}\\
\boldsymbol{v}_{q}\end{array}\biggr|\,-z^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\Gamma^{2}(\nu+1)\left(\frac{z}{2}\right)^{-2\nu}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{2n+2\nu}{n+2\nu}\frac{(2\nu+1)_{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle\times\,{}_{p+3}F_{q+1}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}-n,n+2\nu,\nu+1,\boldsymbol{u}_{p}\\
\nu+1/2,\boldsymbol{v}_{q}\end{array}\right]\,J^{2}_{n+\nu}\left(z\right),

valid if
\,p\leq q\,
and none of
\,2\nu,\boldsymbol{v}_{q}\,
coincides with a negative integer.

In the special case when one of numerator parameters happens to be a negative integer,
the expansion formula (
3
) gives rise to various kinds of transformation formulas for
terminating hypergeometric series with unit argument. Of subsequent importance will be the following formula:

For each positive integer
n
, if
\,p\geq 3,\,
then

\displaystyle{}_{p+1}F_{p}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}-n,u_{1},\cdots,u_{p}\\
v_{1},\cdots,v_{p}\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\frac{(\sigma)_{k}(u_{3})_{k}\cdots(u_{p})_{k}}{(k+\delta)_{k}(v_{3})_{k}\cdots(v_{p})_{k}}\,{}_{4}F_{3}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}-k,k+\delta,u_{1},u_{2}\\
\sigma,v_{1},v_{2}\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\times\,\,{}_{p}F_{p-1}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}-n+k,k+\sigma,k+u_{3},\cdots,k+u_{p}\\
2k+\delta+1,k+v_{3},\cdots,k+v_{p}\end{array}\right].

The proof is immediate on taking
\,r=p-1,\,s=p-3\,
and

\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{r}=(-n,\sigma,u_{3},\cdots,u_{p}),\,\,\boldsymbol{\beta}_{s}=(v_{3},\cdots,v_{p}),\,\,z=w=1.

An advantageous feature in this transformation formula is that
there are two
free parameters

\delta,\sigma
available. For instance, if we recall
[
4
, 2.2(1)]

{}_{3}F_{2}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}-k,k+\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\\
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\end{array}\right]=\frac{(\beta_{1}-\alpha_{2})_{k}(\beta_{2}-\alpha_{2})_{k}}{(\beta_{1})_{k}(\beta_{2})_{k}},

(3.17)

valid under the Saalschützian condition
\,1+\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=\beta_{1}+\beta_{2},\,

and choose
\,\sigma=u_{2},\,\delta=v_{1}+v_{2}-u_{1}-1,\,
then formula (
3
) yields

\displaystyle{}_{p+1}F_{p}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}-n,u_{1},\cdots,u_{p}\\
v_{1},\cdots,v_{p}\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\frac{(v_{1}-u_{1})_{k}(v_{2}-u_{1})_{k}(u_{2})_{k}\cdots(u_{p})_{k}}{(k+v_{1}+v_{2}-u_{1}-1)_{k}(v_{1})_{k}\cdots(v_{p})_{k}}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\times\,\,{}_{p}F_{p-1}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}-n+k,k+u_{2},k+u_{3},\cdots,k+u_{p}\\
2k+v_{1}+v_{2}-u_{1},k+v_{3},\cdots,k+v_{p}\end{array}\right],

which coincides with Gasper’s transformation formula
[
14
, (5.1)]
.

4
Positivity of terminating series

This section focuses on establishing positivity criteria for
terminating
{}_{4}F_{3}
or
{}_{5}F_{4}
hypergeometric series
under the Saalschützian condition, which will play crucial roles in later developments.

4.1
Terminating
{}_{4}F_{3}
hypergeometric series

Lemma 4.1
.

For each positive integer
n
, put

\Theta_{n}={}_{4}F_{3}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}-n,n+\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}\\
\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}\end{array}\right]

and assume that
\,\alpha_{1}>-1,\,\alpha_{2}>0,\,\alpha_{3}>0\,
and

1+\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}=\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}+\beta_{3}.

(4.1)

If parameters
\alpha_{j},\beta_{j}
satisfy either set of the following
additional conditions simultaneously, then
\,\Theta_{n}\geq 0\,
for all
\,n\geq 1.\,

\displaystyle{\rm(A1)}\,\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\,\alpha_{2}\leq\beta_{3}\leq 1+\alpha_{1},}\\
&{\,\underline{\,\alpha_{3}\leq\beta_{1}\wedge\beta_{2}}.}\end{aligned}\right.\quad{\rm(A2)}\,\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\,\alpha_{2}\leq\beta_{3}\leq 2+\alpha_{1},}\\
&{\,\underline{\,\alpha_{3}\leq\beta_{1}\wedge\beta_{2}},}\\
&{\,(1+\alpha_{1})\alpha_{2}\alpha_{3}\leq\beta_{1}\beta_{2}\beta_{3}.}\end{aligned}\right.

Moreover, the underlined condition may be replaced by

\beta_{1}\wedge\beta_{2}>0,\quad\alpha_{3}-1\leq\beta_{1}\wedge\beta_{2}\leq\alpha_{3}.

(4.2)

Proof.

Under Saalschützian condition (
4.1
), if we apply (
3
) and
simplify coefficients with the aid of Saalschütz’s formula (
3.17
), then we find

\displaystyle\Theta_{n}=\frac{1}{(\beta_{3})_{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}

\displaystyle\binom{n}{k}\frac{(\beta_{1}-\alpha_{3})_{k}(\beta_{2}-\alpha_{3})_{k}}{(k+\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-\beta_{3})_{k}}\frac{(n+\alpha_{1})_{k}(\alpha_{2})_{k}}{(\beta_{1})_{k}(\beta_{2})_{k}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\,\,\frac{(k+1+\alpha_{1}-\beta_{3})_{n-k}(\beta_{3}-\alpha_{2})_{n-k}}{(2k+1+\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-\beta_{3})_{n-k}}.

(4.3)

Since coefficients are all nonnegative under stated conditions of (A1),
the nonnegativity of
\Theta_{n}
is obvious. On writing

\displaystyle(\beta_{1}-\alpha_{3})_{k}(\beta_{2}-\alpha_{3})_{k}

\displaystyle\qquad=(\beta_{1}-\alpha_{3})(\beta_{2}-\alpha_{3})(\beta_{1}-\alpha_{3}+1)_{k-1}(\beta_{2}-\alpha_{3}+1)_{k-1}

(4.4)

for
\,k\geq 1,\,
we also deduce the same result when the underlined condition of (A1) is replaced by
the alternative condition of (
4.2
).

A proof for the nonnegativity of
\Theta_{n}
under Saalschützian condition (
4.1
) and (A2)
is given in our previous work
[
8
, Lemma 3.1]
.
By tracking down the proof therein, it is not hard to find that
the underlined condition of (A2) can be replaced by
(

4.3
An optimization inequality

In dealing with the last condition of (A2), Lemma
4.1
,
which often causes difficulties in practice, it will be useful to invoke the following inequality.

Lemma 4.3
.

Given
\,0<\alpha_{1}\leq\alpha_{2}\leq\alpha_{3},
we have
\,\beta_{1}\beta_{2}\beta_{3}\geq\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\alpha_{3}\,

for all positive real numbers
\,\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}\,
satisfying the conditions

\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}+\beta_{3}=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3},\quad\alpha_{1}\leq\beta_{1}\wedge\beta_{2}\wedge\beta_{3}\leq\alpha_{3}.

Proof.

We may assume
\,\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{3}\,
with no loss of generality. Setting

\displaystyle\qquad f(x_{1},x_{2})=x_{1}x_{2}(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}-x_{1}-x_{2}),

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle=\bigl\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in[\alpha_{1},\alpha_{3}]\times[\alpha_{1},\alpha_{3}]:\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}\leq x_{1}+x_{2}\leq\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}\bigr\},

it suffices to prove that
\,f(x_{1},x_{2})\geq\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\alpha_{3}\,
for all
\,(x_{1},x_{2})\in D.

As readily verified,
\,\nabla f(x_{1},x_{2})=(0,0)\,
only when

x_{1}=x_{2}=\frac{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}}{3}

and the usual AM-GM inequality implies

f\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}}{3},\frac{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}}{3}\right)=\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}}{3}\right)^{3}\geq\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\alpha_{3}.

It remains to prove
\,f(x_{1},x_{2})\geq\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\alpha_{3}\,
for all
\,(x_{1},x_{2})\in\partial D,\,

where the boundary
\partial D
consists of six line segments

\displaystyle\partial D=

\displaystyle\bigl\{\alpha_{1}\leq x_{1}\leq\alpha_{2},\,\,x_{2}=\alpha_{3}\bigr\}\cup\bigl\{\alpha_{2}\leq x_{1}\leq\alpha_{3},\,\,x_{1}+x_{2}=\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}\bigr\}

\displaystyle\cup

\displaystyle\bigl\{x_{1}=\alpha_{3},\,\,\alpha_{1}\leq x_{2}\leq\alpha_{2}\bigr\}\cup\bigl\{\alpha_{2}\leq x_{1}\leq\alpha_{3},\,\,x_{2}=\alpha_{1}\bigr\}

\displaystyle\cup

\displaystyle\bigl\{\alpha_{1}\leq x_{1}\leq\alpha_{2},\,\,x_{1}+x_{2}=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}\bigr\}\cup\bigl\{x_{1}=\alpha_{1},\,\,\alpha_{2}\leq x_{2}\leq\alpha_{3}\bigr\}.

For
\,(x_{1},x_{2})\in\bigl\{\alpha_{1}\leq x_{1}\leq\alpha_{2},\,\,x_{2}=\alpha_{3}\bigr\},\,
the first
line segment, if we write
\,x_{1}=(1-\lambda)\alpha_{1}+\lambda\alpha_{2},\,0\leq\lambda\leq 1,\,
then
the AM-GM inequality gives

\displaystyle f(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=\alpha_{3}x_{1}(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-x_{1})

\displaystyle=\alpha_{3}\big[(1-\lambda)\alpha_{1}+\lambda\alpha_{2}\big]\big[\lambda\alpha_{1}+(1-\lambda)\alpha_{2}\big]

\displaystyle\geq\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\alpha_{3}.

On the second line segment of
\partial D
, we put

\,x_{1}=(1-\lambda)\alpha_{2}+\lambda\alpha_{3},\,0\leq\lambda\leq 1\,
and apply the AM-GM inequality to deduce

\displaystyle f(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=\alpha_{1}x_{1}(\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}-x_{1})

\displaystyle=\alpha_{1}\big[(1-\lambda)\alpha_{2}+\lambda\alpha_{3}\big]\big[\lambda\alpha_{2}+(1-\lambda)\alpha_{3}\big]

\displaystyle\geq\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\alpha_{3}.

By carrying out similar reasonings for other line segments of
\partial D
, it is not difficult to
find that the same conclusion continues to hold true.
∎

\displaystyle A_{4}(\xi_{3},\xi_{1},\xi_{2}),\,\,\,A_{5}(\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\xi_{1}),\,\,\,A_{6}(\xi_{3},\xi_{2},\xi_{1})\big\}.

Partly due to our way of handling those terminating hypergeometric series

.

.

We denote by

the set of

\left\{\begin{aligned} {a_{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(3a_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\,\,\text{or}\,\,1\leq a_{2}\leq 2a_{1}+1\quad}&{\text{if}\quad a_{1}\leq 1/2,}\\

lies on the plane

and represents the following geometric region:

6
Newton polyhedra and positivity

As standard, the Newton polyhedron of a set
\,E\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}^{3}\,
refers to
the convex hull of the union of octants with corners at all points of
E
,

\bigcup_{\mathbf{x}\in E}\left(\mathbf{x}+\mathbb{R}_{+}^{3}\right),

which will be denoted by
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{+}(E)
(see
[
25
]
for instance).

According to the transference principle of Proposition
2.1
,
if
\Phi
were shown to be nonnegative for a single point
\,\mathbf{b}=(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}),

then
\Phi
remains strictly positive for all points of octant
\,\mathbf{b}+\mathbb{R}_{+}^{3}\,

except for the corner
\mathbf{b}
. As a consequence, we obtain the following
extension of Proposition
5.1
which constitutes
one of our main positivity results in this paper.

Theorem 6.1
.

For
\,(a_{1},a_{2})\in\Lambda,
if
\,(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})\in\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{+}\left(\mathcal{A}\right),

then
\,\Phi(x)\geq 0\,
for all
\,x>0\,
and
strict inequality holds true unless
\,(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})\in\mathcal{A}.

Figure 2:
A Newton polyhedron with a hexagonal face.

Remark 6.1
.

For practical applications, it would be desirable to characterize

\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{+}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
explicitly. On inspecting boundary planes
both analytically and geometrically, it is not difficult to find that

\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{+}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
consists of all points

(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})
satisfying the following conditions simultaneously:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\qquad\,b_{1}\wedge b_{2}\wedge b_{3}\geq\xi_{1},}\\
&{(b_{1}+b_{2})\wedge(b_{2}+b_{3})\wedge(b_{1}+b_{3})\geq\xi_{1}+\xi_{2},}\\
&{\qquad b_{1}+b_{2}+b_{3}\geq\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}+\xi_{3}.}\end{aligned}\right.

(6.1)

As readily observed,
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{+}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
has the following geometric features.

•

If
\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3}\,
are distinct, as illustrated in Figure
2
,

\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{+}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)

represents an infinite polygonal region having 6 vertices at
\mathcal{A}
,
7 faces including the hexagon
\mathbf{H}_{\mathcal{A}}
,
and 12 edges comprised of 6 boundary lines of
\mathbf{H}_{\mathcal{A}}
and
6 rays emanating from
\mathcal{A}
parallel to the coordinate axes.

•

If
\,\xi_{1}=\xi_{2}<\xi_{3}\,
or
\,\xi_{1}<\xi_{2}=\xi_{3},
then
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{+}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
represents
an infinite polygonal region having 3 vertices at
\mathcal{A}
, 4 faces including
the triangle
\mathbf{H}_{\mathcal{A}}
, and 6 edges comprised of 3 boundary lines of
\mathbf{H}_{\mathcal{A}}
and
3 rays emanating from
\mathcal{A}
parallel to the coordinate axes.

•

If
\,\xi_{1}=\xi_{2}=\xi_{3},
which happens only when
\,a_{1}=1/2,\,a_{2}=1,\,

then
\,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{+}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)=\mathbf{1}+\mathbb{R}_{+}^{3},\,
where
\,\mathbf{1}=(1,1,1).

If
\,a_{1}=a_{2}=a,\,
it is Theorem
6.1
that we wish to establish ultimately in this paper.
Since
\,(a,a)\in\Lambda\,
for any
\,a>0,
with no further restrictions, it may be noteworthy to single out
the result in this special occasion.

Corollary 6.1
.

For any
\,a>0,
suppose

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\quad b_{1}\wedge b_{2}\wedge b_{3}\geq a,\,\,b_{1}+b_{2}+b_{3}\geq 4a+1/2,}\\
&{\,(b_{1}+b_{2})\wedge(b_{2}+b_{3})\wedge(b_{1}+b_{3})\geq 4a+1/2-\sigma,}\end{aligned}\right.

(6.2)

where
\,\sigma=\max\big(a+1/2,\,2a\big).
If we define

\phi(x)={}_{2}F_{3}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}a,a\\
b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}\end{array}\biggr|-x^{2}\right]\qquad(x>0),

then
\,\phi(x)\geq 0\,
for all
\,x>0\,
and strict inequality holds true unless

\,(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})\,
coincides with a permutation of

\,(a,\,a+1/2,\,2a).\,

In the case when
\,a_{1},\,a_{2}\,
are distinct, however, there is another Newton polyhedron
available for the positivity of
\Phi
whose vertices are comprised of permutations of

(a_{1},a_{2}+1/2,2a_{2})
. To state more precisely, we shall rearrange these vertices
for the same purpose as before.

Definition 6.1
.

For
\,a_{1}>0,a_{2}>0,
we denote by
\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\eta_{3}
the arrangement of

\,a_{1},\,a_{2}+1/2,\,2a_{2}\,
in ascending order of magnitude so that

and by

the set of all permutations of
(\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\eta_{3})
labelled by

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}=\big\{

\displaystyle B_{1}(\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\eta_{3}),\,\,\,B_{2}(\eta_{2},\eta_{1},\eta_{3}),\,\,\,B_{3}(\eta_{1},\eta_{3},\eta_{2}),

\displaystyle B_{4}(\eta_{3},\eta_{1},\eta_{2}),\,\,\,B_{5}(\eta_{2},\eta_{3},\eta_{1}),\,\,\,B_{6}(\eta_{3},\eta_{2},\eta_{1})\big\}.

Let
\Lambda^{*}
be the reflection of
\Lambda
across the line
\,a_{1}=a_{2},
that is,

\,\Lambda^{*}=\left\{(a_{1},a_{2}):(a_{2},a_{1})\in\Lambda\right\}.\,

Applying Proposition
5.1
with roles of
\,a_{1},\,a_{2}\,
interchanged, we find
that
for any pair
\,(a_{1},a_{2})\in\Lambda^{*},
if
\,(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})\in\mathbf{H}_{\mathcal{B}},

the closed polygon with vertices
\mathcal{B}
,
then
\,\Phi(x)\geq 0\,
for all
\,x>0\,

and strict inequality holds true unless
\,(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})\in\mathcal{B}.

Since
\,\{(a_{1},a_{2}):0<a_{1}<a_{2}\}\subset\Lambda^{*},
an extension of this positivity result
by the Newton polyhedron of
\mathcal{B}
can be summarized as follows, which constitutes our
second main positivity result.

Theorem 6.2
.

For
\,0<a_{1}<a_{2},
if
\,(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})\in\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{+}\left(\mathcal{B}\right),

then
\,\Phi(x)\geq 0\,
for all
\,x>0\,
and
strict inequality holds true unless
\,(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})\in\mathcal{B}.

Remark 6.2
.

We should emphasize that no additional conditions on the pair

(a_{1},a_{2})
are required for validity. In analogue with (
6.1
),

\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{+}\left(\mathcal{B}\right)
consists of all points
(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})

satisfying the following conditions simultaneously:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\qquad\,b_{1}\wedge b_{2}\wedge b_{3}\geq\eta_{1},}\\
&{(b_{1}+b_{2})\wedge(b_{2}+b_{3})\wedge(b_{1}+b_{3})\geq\eta_{1}+\eta_{2},}\\
&{\qquad b_{1}+b_{2}+b_{3}\geq\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}+\eta_{3},}\end{aligned}\right.

(6.3)

where
\,\eta_{1}=a_{1},\,\,\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}+\eta_{3}=3a_{2}+a_{1}+1/2\,
in any case.

As both Newton polyhedra
\,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{+}\left(\mathcal{A}\right),\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{+}\left(\mathcal{B}\right)\,
are available as positivity regions of
\Phi
,
it is natural to ask whether the Newton polyhedron
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{+}\left(\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B}\right)

also gives rise to a region of positivity. What matters are side polygonal
regions between
\,\mathbf{H}_{\mathcal{A}},\,\mathbf{H}_{\mathcal{B}}\,
which
we shall now investigate. Note

\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{+}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\cup\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{+}\left(\mathcal{B}\right)\subset\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{+}\left(\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B}\right).

7
Side quadrilaterals

In order to facilitate our geometric interpretations,
we shall consider both
\,\mathbf{H}_{\mathcal{A}},\mathbf{H}_{\mathcal{B}}\,
as if they were
hexagons and hence the interconnecting polygonal region
would consist of six side quadrilaterals. Since any of
\,\mathbf{H}_{\mathcal{A}},\mathbf{H}_{\mathcal{B}}\,

in other occasions may be regarded as continuous deformations of hexagons in an obvious way,
such a presumption would be harmless.

Due to our arrangements, it is simple to observe that each of six side quadrilaterals
connecting the two hexagons has vertices
\,A_{i},A_{j},B_{j},B_{i}\,
for some
\,i,j\in\{1,2,3\}\,

with
\,i\neq j
, if enumerated in a fixed direction, which will be denoted by

\mathbf{S}\big[A_{i}A_{j}B_{j}B_{i}\big]
. It is convenient to interpret

\mathbf{S}\big[A_{i}A_{j}B_{j}B_{i}\big]=\bigcup_{\lambda\in[0,1]}L_{\lambda},

(7.1)

the collection of all line segments
L_{\lambda}
joining two points

(1-\lambda)A_{i}+\lambda B_{i},\quad(1-\lambda)A_{j}+\lambda B_{j},\quad 0\leq\lambda\leq 1.

By parameterizing in an elementary way, it is immediate to characterize the line segment

L_{\lambda}
in two exemplary cases:

(i)

For
\,\mathbf{S}\big[A_{1}A_{2}B_{2}B_{1}\big]
, each

L_{\lambda}
consists of all
(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})
satisfying

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\qquad\quad b_{3}=(1-\lambda)\xi_{3}+\lambda\eta_{3},}\\
&{\quad b_{1}+b_{2}=(1-\lambda)(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})+\lambda(\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}),}\\
&{(1-\lambda)\xi_{1}+\lambda\eta_{1}\leq b_{1}\wedge b_{2}\leq(1-\lambda)\xi_{2}+\lambda\eta_{2}.}\end{aligned}\right.

(7.2)

(ii)

For
\,\mathbf{S}\big[A_{5}A_{6}B_{6}B_{5}\big]
,
each
L_{\lambda}
consists of all
(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})
satisfying

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\qquad\quad b_{3}=(1-\lambda)\xi_{1}+\lambda\eta_{1},}\\
&{\quad b_{1}+b_{2}=(1-\lambda)(\xi_{2}+\xi_{3})+\lambda(\eta_{2}+\eta_{3}),}\\
&{(1-\lambda)\xi_{2}+\lambda\eta_{2}\leq b_{1}\wedge b_{2}\leq(1-\lambda)\xi_{3}+\lambda\eta_{3}.}\end{aligned}\right.

(7.3)

As readily verified, by characterizing each
L_{\lambda}
in the same way or by geometric
considerations, it turns out that
the other four side quadrilaterals arise as mirror images of the above
two quadrilaterals across either the plane
\,b_{1}=b_{3}\,
or

\,b_{2}=b_{3}\,
in the senses specified as follows.

Lemma 7.1
.

For
\,0<a_{1}<a_{2},
the following hold true.

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}\big[A_{4}A_{6}B_{6}B_{4}\big]

\displaystyle=\Big\{(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}):(b_{3},b_{2},b_{1})\in\mathbf{S}\big[A_{1}A_{2}B_{2}B_{1}\big]\Big\},

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}\big[A_{3}A_{5}B_{5}B_{3}\big]

\displaystyle=\Big\{(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}):(b_{1},b_{3},b_{2})\in\mathbf{S}\big[A_{1}A_{2}B_{2}B_{1}\big]\Big\},

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}\big[A_{1}A_{3}B_{3}B_{1}\big]

\displaystyle=\Big\{(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}):(b_{3},b_{2},b_{1})\in\mathbf{S}\big[A_{5}A_{6}B_{6}B_{5}\big]\Big\},

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}\big[A_{2}A_{4}B_{4}B_{2}\big]

\displaystyle=\Big\{(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}):(b_{1},b_{3},b_{2})\in\mathbf{S}\big[A_{5}A_{6}B_{6}B_{5}\big]\Big\}.

We are now in a position to prove the positivity of
\Phi
when
(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})

belongs to those side quadrilaterals. In view of the symmetry specified as above,
it suffices to deal with any two side quadrilaterals of type

\mathbf{S}\big[A_{1}A_{2}B_{2}B_{1}\big],\,\,\mathbf{S}\big[A_{5}A_{6}B_{6}B_{5}\big].

7.1
Quadrilaterals of type
\mathbf{S}\big[A_{5}A_{6}B_{6}B_{5}\big].

As to three side quadrilaterals of this type, it turns out that
admissible
conditions for

(a_{1},a_{2})
are independent of
\Lambda
and given as follows.

Definition 7.1
.

We denote by
\Sigma
the set of
\,(a_{1},a_{2})\in(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*})^{2}\,
satisfying

\left\{\begin{aligned} {a_{1}<a_{2}\leq\min\big(2a_{1},\,1/2\big)}&{\quad\text{if}\quad a_{1}<1/2,}\\
{a_{1}<a_{2}\leq 2a_{1}\quad\qquad}&{\quad\text{if}\quad a_{1}\geq 1/2.}\end{aligned}\right.

Proposition 7.1
.

For
\,(a_{1},a_{2})\in\Sigma,

if
\,(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})\in\mathbf{S}\big[A_{5}A_{6}B_{6}B_{5}\big],

then
\,\Phi(x)\geq 0\,
for all
\,x>0\,
and strict inequality holds true unless it coincides
with one of vertices. By symmetry, the same holds true for

\mathbf{S}\big[A_{1}A_{3}B_{3}B_{1}\big],\,\,\mathbf{S}\big[A_{2}A_{4}B_{4}B_{2}\big].

We apply Gasper’s sums of squares formula (
3
) to expand

\displaystyle\Phi(x)=\Gamma^{2}(\nu+1)\left(\frac{x}{4}\right)^{-2\nu}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}K_{n}\,\frac{2n+2\nu}{n+2\nu}\frac{(2\nu+1)_{n}}{n!}J^{2}_{n+\nu}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right),

where

is an arbitrary real number subject to the condition that

does not coincide with a negative integer,

\nu+1/2,b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}\end{array}\right]

(7.4)

for each
\,n\geq 1\,
and
\,K_{0}\equiv 1.
By the same reason as mentioned in the proof of Theorem
5.1
,
it will be sufficient to prove
\,K_{n}\geq 0\,
for all
\,n\geq 1.

In accordance with (
7.1
), we shall write

\mathbf{S}\big[A_{5}A_{6}B_{6}B_{5}\big]=\bigcup_{\lambda\in[0,1]}L_{\lambda}

and deduce the nonnegativity of
K_{n}
on

by applying
positivity criteria for the terminating
{}_{5}F_{4}
hypergeometric series established in
Lemma
4.2
.

For each
\,(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})\in L_{\lambda},
it follows from the characterization (
9.11
) that
the Saalschtützian condition for
K_{n}
amounts to the choice

\displaystyle 2\nu+1

\displaystyle=b_{1}+b_{2}+b_{3}-a_{1}-a_{2}-1/2

\displaystyle=2\big[(1-\lambda)a_{1}+\lambda a_{2}\big].

(7.5)

Since
\,2\nu+1>0\,
for any
\,\lambda\in[0,1],
this choice would be legitimate.

We shall divide our proof into three cases.

Case 1.
1/2\leq a_{1}<a_{2}\leq a_{1}+1/2\leq 2a_{1}.

Due to the correspondence

(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})=(a_{2},\,a_{1}+1/2,\,2a_{1}),\,\,(\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\eta_{3})=(a_{1},\,a_{2}+1/2,\,2a_{2}),

the Saalschtützian condition (

) allows us to express

\displaystyle\nu+1/2

\displaystyle=(1-\lambda)\xi_{1}+\lambda\eta_{1},

\displaystyle\nu+1

\displaystyle=(1-\lambda)\xi_{2}+\lambda\eta_{2},

\displaystyle 2\nu+1

\displaystyle=(1-\lambda)\xi_{3}+\lambda\eta_{3}.

Concerning the positivity criterion (C1) of Lemma
4.2
, if we match

(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3},\alpha_{4})\mapsto(2\nu,\xi_{1},\nu+1,\eta_{1}),\,\,(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3},\beta_{4})\mapsto(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3},\nu+1/2),

then it follows readily from the above expression and (

whence we may conclude
\,K_{n}\geq 0\,
for all

.

Case 2.
1/2\leq a_{1}<a_{1}+1/2\leq a_{2}\leq 2a_{1}.

Due to the correspondence

(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})=(a_{1}+1/2,\,a_{2},\,2a_{1}),\,\,(\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\eta_{3})=(a_{1},\,a_{2}+1/2,\,2a_{2}),

the Saalschtützian condition (
7.1
) allows us to express

\displaystyle\nu+1/2

\displaystyle=(1-\lambda)\eta_{1}+\lambda\xi_{2},

\displaystyle\nu+1

\displaystyle=(1-\lambda)\xi_{1}+\lambda\eta_{2},

\displaystyle 2\nu+1

\displaystyle=(1-\lambda)\xi_{3}+\lambda\eta_{3}.

We shall deduce the nonnegativity of
K_{n}
based on the
criterion (C2) of Lemma
4.2
. On matching parameters by

(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3},\alpha_{4})\mapsto(2\nu,\nu+1,\eta_{1},\xi_{2}),\,\,(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3},\beta_{4})\mapsto(\nu+1/2,b_{3},b_{1},b_{2}),

it is straightforward to verify the first three conditions of (C2)

\displaystyle\alpha_{3}\leq\beta_{1}\wedge\beta_{2},\,\,\,\alpha_{4}\wedge\beta_{3}\wedge\beta_{4}\leq 1+\alpha_{1},

\displaystyle 1+\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}\leq\beta_{3}+\beta_{4}\leq 2+\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{4}.

For the last condition of (C2), we use the fact

In terms of Fourier cosine or sine transforms, two special cases
\,\alpha=\pm 1/2\,

yield the following results, where we rename
\,\lambda\mapsto\alpha-1,\,\mu\mapsto\beta-1/2.\,

(S5)

The inequalities

\displaystyle{\rm(i)}\,\,\int_{0}^{1}\cos(xt)\,(1-t)^{\alpha-1}t^{\beta-1}\,dt

\displaystyle\geq 0,\qquad(\alpha,\beta)\in\Delta_{c},

\displaystyle{\rm(ii)}\,\,\int_{0}^{1}\sin(xt)\,(1-t)^{\alpha-1}t^{\beta-1}\,dt

\displaystyle\geq 0,\qquad(\alpha,\beta)\in\Delta_{s}

hold true for all
\,x>0,
where

\displaystyle\Delta_{c}

\displaystyle=\Big\{(\alpha,\beta):\alpha>1,\,\,0<\beta\leq\min\left(1,\,\,\alpha-1\right)\Big\},

\displaystyle\Delta_{s}

\displaystyle=\Big\{(\alpha,\beta):\alpha\geq 1/2\quad\text{and}\quad-1<\beta\leq\min\left(0,\,\,\alpha-1\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\text{or}\quad 0\leq\beta\leq\min\big[2,\,\,(\alpha+1)/2,\,\,2\alpha-1\big]\Big\}.

Moreover, the first inequality is strict unless
\,\alpha=2,\,\beta=1\,
and
the second inequality is strict unless
\,\alpha=\beta=1.

We remark that
\,\Delta_{c}\subset\Delta_{s}\,
and more generally
\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}
increases
monotonically as
\alpha
increases. Since the associated beta density

\,f(t)=\frac{1}{B(\alpha,\beta)}(1-t)_{+}^{\alpha-1}t^{\beta-1}_{+}\qquad(\alpha>0,\,\beta>0)

is neither monotone nor convex for most of
(\alpha,\beta)
,
these results indicate why it is so difficult to prove or disprove positivity of Fourier transforms
by using only intrinsic characters of densities such as convexity or monotonicity, as was suggested by Tuck
[
24
]
or Pólya
[
22
]
for instance.

Proposition 9.1
.

Williamson’s conjecture is false in that

g_{\alpha}
remains strictly positive on
(0,\infty)
for
\,\alpha\geq 3\,
but changes sign for

\,0<\alpha<3.

The proof is immediate. On taking
\,\beta=2\,
and inspecting
\Delta_{s}
,
it is simple to find that
g_{\alpha}
remains strictly positive on
(0,\infty)
for
\,\alpha\geq 3.

On the other hand, it follows from (
9
) that
\,g_{\alpha}(x)=c_{\alpha}\,x\,G_{\alpha}(x),
where

G_{\alpha}(x)={}_{1}F_{2}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}2\\
(\alpha+3)/2,\,(\alpha+4)/2\end{array}\biggr|-\frac{\,x^{2}}{4}\right]

and
c_{\alpha}
is a positive constant. Setting

a=2,\,\,b=(\alpha+3)/2,\,\,c=(\alpha+4)/2,

it follows from the necessity part (i) of Theorem
9.2
that if
\,b+c<3a+1/2,\,
that is,
\,\alpha<3,

then
G_{\alpha}
changes sign on
(0,\infty)
and so does
g_{\alpha}
.

Boundary controllability for a coupled system of degenerate/singular parabolic equations
Brahim Allal

Abstract
In this paper we study the boundary controllability for a system of two coupled degenerate/singular parabolic equations with a control acting on only one equation. We analyze both approximate and null boundary controllability properties. Besides, we provide an estimate on the null-control cost. The proofs are based on the use of the moment method by Fattorini and Russell together with some results on biorthogonal families.



Keywords: Boundary controllability, coupled systems, degenerate parabolic equations, singular potentials, moment method.

1
Introduction

This work is devoted to the study of the boundary controllability properties of the following controlled system:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}y_{t}-(x^{\alpha}y_{x})_{x}-\frac{\mu}{x^{2-\alpha}}y=Ay,&&(t,x)\in Q:=(0,T)\times(0,1),\\
y(t,1)=Bv,&&t\in(0,T),\\
y(t,0)=0,&&t\in(0,T),\\
y(0,x)=y_{0}(x),&&x\in(0,1),\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
T>0
is some final time,
0\leq\alpha<1
,
\mu\leq\mu(\alpha)=\frac{(1-\alpha)^{2}}{4}
,
y_{0}\in L^{2}(0,1)
,
y=(y_{1},y_{2})^{*}
is the state variable and
v=v(t)
is the control function which acts on the system by means of the Dirichlet boundary condition at the point
x=1
. Moreover,
A\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
B\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
are, respectively, a suitable coupling matrix and a control operator, chosen so that:

rank[B|AB]=2.

(1.2)

Notice that, taking
P=[B|AB]
, by performing the
change of variables
\tilde{y}=P^{-1}y
, one obtains the following reformulation
of (
1.1
):

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\tilde{y}_{t}-(x^{\alpha}\tilde{y}_{x})_{x}-\frac{\mu}{x^{2-\alpha}}\tilde{y}=\tilde{A}\tilde{y},&&(t,x)\in(0,T)\times(0,1),\\
\tilde{y}(t,1)=\tilde{B}v,&&t\in(0,T),\\
\tilde{y}(t,0)=0,&&t\in(0,T),\\
\tilde{y}(0,x)=P^{-1}y_{0}(x),&&x\in(0,1),\end{array}\right.

(1.3)

where

\displaystyle\tilde{A}=\begin{pmatrix}0&a_{1}\\
1&a_{2}\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{B}=e_{1}=\left(\begin{matrix}1\\
0\end{matrix}\right).

Therefore, for simplicity, it will be assumed in the rest of the paper that
A
and
B
are given by

\displaystyle A=\begin{pmatrix}0&a_{1}\\
1&a_{2}\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad B=e_{1}=\left(\begin{matrix}1\\
0\end{matrix}\right).

(1.4)

This means that we are exerting only one control force on the system but we want to control the corresponding state
y=(y_{1},y_{2})
which has two components.

The starting point of the present work is the results established in
[
21
]
for the boundary controllability properties of the (uniformly) parabolic system:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}y_{t}-y_{xx}=Ay,&&(t,x)\in Q,\\
y(t,0)=Bv,&&t\in(0,T),\\
y(t,1)=0,&&t\in(0,T),\\
y(0,x)=y_{0}(x),&&x\in(0,1),\end{array}\right.

(1.5)

with
A
and
B
as defined previously. In fact, as shown in
[
21
]
, there exists two different situations:

1.

If the matrix
A
in (
1.5
) has one
double real eigenvalue or a couple of conjugate complex eigenvalues, (
1.2
) is a necessary and
sufficient condition for the null controllability at any time.

2.

If
A
has two different real eigenvalues, an additional condition is needed for null controllability,
independently of the considered vector
B
.

Since then, several other works followed extending them in various situations. See for instance
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
16
,
25
]
.

However, all the previous cited papers deal with uniformly parabolic problems without degeneracies or singularities.
In recent years, controllability issues for degenerate and/or singular parabolic problems by means of a locally distributed control have been investigated in many papers, see
[
10
,
13
,
18
,
22
,
23
,
34
,
35
]
. For related systems of coupled degenerate/singular parabolic equations we refer to
[
2
,
26
,
33
]
.

To our best knowledge, the known boundary controllability results of degenerate and/or singular parabolic problems are in the
scalar case (see for example
[
8
,
9
,
11
,
12
,
24
,
32
]
).

The aim of this research is to establish general results in the case of coupled degenerate/singular parabolic equations.
To prove our results we will use the moment method by Fattorini and Russell, introduced in
[
19
,
20
]
in the framework of the boundary controllability of the one-dimensional scalar heat equation.

At first, we will see that for every
v\in L^{2}(0,T)
and
y_{0}\in H^{-1,\mu}_{\alpha}(0,1)^{2}
, system (
1.1
) admits a unique weak solution defined by transposition that satisfies

y\in L^{2}(Q)^{2}\cap C^{0}\big([0,T],H^{-1,\mu}_{\alpha}(0,1)^{2}\big).

Observe that the previous regularity permits to pose the boundary controllability of the singular system (
1.1
) in the space
H^{-1,\mu}_{\alpha}(0,1)^{2}
(that will be defined later in section
2
).

Then, we pass to analyse both approximate and null controllability issues using a boundary
control acting at
x=1
. So, we use the following notions:

Definition 1.1
.

1.

It will be said that system (
1.1
) is approximately controllable in
H^{-1,\mu}_{\alpha}(0,1)^{2}
at time
T>0
if for every
y_{0},y_{d}\in H^{-1,\mu}_{\alpha}(0,1)^{2}
and any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a control function
v\in L^{2}(0,T)
such that the solution
y
to system (
1.1
) satisfies

\|y(T,\cdot)-y_{d}\|_{H^{-1,\mu}_{\alpha}(0,1)^{2}}\leq\varepsilon.

2.

It will be said that system (
1.1
) is null controllable at time
T>0
if for every
y_{0}\in H^{-1,\mu}_{\alpha}(0,1)^{2}
, there exists a control
v\in L^{2}(0,T)
such that the solution
y
to system (
1.1
) satisfies

y(T,\cdot)=0,\quad\text{in}\quad H^{-1,\mu}_{\alpha}(0,1)^{2}.

We emphasize that imposing a control that acts at the nonsingular point does not imply a simple adaptation of the previous distributed controllability results. For example, at a first glance, one may think that our boundary controllability results can be obtained directly by standard extension and localization arguments from the corresponding distributed controllability results as in the case of scalar parabolic equations. But this is not the case and the situation is quite different for non-scalar parabolic systems. Indeed, while the Kalman’s rank condition (
1.2
) is a necessary and sufficient condition for the null controllability at any time in the distributed case, it was proved in
[
21
]
that it is necessary, but not sufficient, for the boundary controllability for coupled parabolic systems.

In order to get the approximate controllability result of (
1.1
), we will need the following known result (see
[
5
]
or
[
21
]
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
T>0
. Suppose that
\{\Lambda_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
is a sequence of complex numbers such that, for some
\delta,\rho>0
, one has

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\Re(\Lambda_{n})\geq\delta|\Lambda_{n}|,\qquad|\Lambda_{n}-\Lambda_{m}|\geq\rho|n-m|,\quad\forall n,m\geq 1,\\
\sum\limits_{n\geq 1}\dfrac{1}{|\Lambda_{n}|}<+\infty.\end{array}\right.

(1.6)

Then, there exists a family
\{q_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}\subset L^{2}(0,T)
biorthogonal to
\{e^{-\Lambda_{n}t}\}_{n\geq 1}
i.e., a family
\{q_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
in
L^{2}(0,T)
such that

\int_{0}^{T}q_{n}(t)e^{-\Lambda_{m}t}\,dt=\delta_{nm},\quad\forall n,m\geq 1.

Moreover, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
C_{\varepsilon}>0
for which

\|q_{n}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\leq C_{\varepsilon}e^{\varepsilon\Re(\Lambda_{n})},\qquad\forall n\geq 1.

It is worth mentioning that the above Theorem can also be applied to get the null controllability result for the system (
1.1
). However, it does not permit to deduce the required exponential estimate on the null-control cost.

For this reason, to obtain the null controllability result together with an estimate of the control cost, we are going to apply the next result provided in
[
7
]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\{\Lambda_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence of complex numbers fulfilling the following assumptions:

1.

\Lambda_{n}\neq\Lambda_{m}
for all
n,m\geq 1
with
n\neq m
;

2.

\Re(\Lambda_{n})>0
for every
n\geq 1
;

3.

for some
\delta>0

|\Im(\Lambda_{n})|\leq\delta\sqrt{\Re(\Lambda_{n})}\quad\forall n\geq 1;

4.

\{\Lambda_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
is nondecreasing in modulus,

|\Lambda_{n}|\leq|\Lambda_{n+1}|\quad\forall n\geq 1;

5.

\{\Lambda_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
satisfies the following gap condition: for some

\varrho,q>0
,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}|\Lambda_{n}-\Lambda_{m}|\geq\varrho|n^{2}-m^{2}|\quad\forall n,m:|n-m|\geq q,\\
\inf\limits_{n\neq m,\;|n-m|<q}|\Lambda_{n}-\Lambda_{m}|>0;\end{array}\right.

(1.7)

6.

for some
p,s>0,

|p\sqrt{r}-\mathcal{N}(r)|\leq s,\quad\forall r>0,

(1.8)

where
\mathcal{N}
is the counting function associated with the sequence

\{\Lambda_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
, that is the function defined by

\mathcal{N}(r)=\#\{n:\,|\Lambda_{n}|\leq r\},\qquad\forall r>0.

Then, there exists
T_{0}>0
, such that for any
T\in(0,T_{0})
, we can find a family
\{q_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}\subset L^{2}(-T/2,T/2)
biorthogonal to
\{e^{-\Lambda_{n}t}\}_{n\geq 1}
i.e., a family
\{q_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
in
L^{2}(-T/2,T/2)
such that

\int_{-T/2}^{T/2}q_{n}(t)e^{-\Lambda_{m}t}\,dt=\delta_{nm}.

Moreover, there exists a positive constant
C>0
independent of
T
for which

\|q_{n}\|_{L^{2}(-T/2,T/2)}\leq Ce^{C\sqrt{\Re(\Lambda_{n})}+\frac{C}{T}},\qquad\forall n\geq 1.

(1.9)

Here for
z\in\mathbb{C}
,
\Re(z)
and
\Im(z)
denote the real and imaginary parts of
z
.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we prove the well-posedness of the problem (
1.1
) in appropriate weighted spaces using the transposition method and recall
some characterizations of the controllability.
In section
3
, we discuss the spectral analysis related to scalar singular operators and present a description of
the spectrum associated with system (
1.1
) which will be useful for developing the moment method.
Section
4
is devoted to studying the boundary approximate controllability problem for the system (
1.1
).
Finally, in section
5
, we prove the boundary null controllability result and establish an estimate of the control cost.

2
Preliminary results

2.1
Functional framework

In the study of degenerate/singular problems, it is by now classical
that of great importance is the following generalized Hardy inequality (see, for example,
[
34
]
or
[
15
, Lemma 5.3.1]
): for all
\alpha\in[0,2)
,

\frac{(1-\alpha)^{2}}{4}\int_{0}^{1}x^{\alpha-2}z^{2}\,dx\leq\int_{0}^{1}x^{\alpha}z^{2}_{x}\,dx,\quad\forall z\in C^{\infty}_{c}(0,1).

(2.1)

For any
\mu\leq\mu(\alpha)
, we introduce the functional space associated to degenerate/singular problems:

\displaystyle H_{\alpha}^{1,\mu}(0,1):=\Big\{z\in L^{2}(0,1)\cap H^{1}_{loc}((0,1])\,\mid\int_{0}^{1}(x^{\alpha}z_{x}^{2}-\mu\frac{z^{2}}{x^{2-\alpha}})\,dx<+\infty\Big\}

\displaystyle H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}(0,1):=\Big\{z\in H_{\alpha}^{1,\mu}(0,1)\,\mid z(0)=z(1)=0\Big\}.

Further, we define
H^{-1,\mu}_{\alpha,0}(0,1)
the dual space of
H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}(0,1)
with respect to the pivot space

L^{2}(0,1)
, endowed with the natural norm

\|f\|_{H_{\alpha,0}^{-1,\mu}(0,1)}:=\sup_{\|g\|_{H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}(0,1)}=1}\langle f,g\rangle_{H^{-1,\mu}_{\alpha,0}(0,1),H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}(0,1)}.

We also define

H^{2,\mu}_{\alpha}(0,1)=\big\{z\in H^{1,\mu}_{\alpha}(0,1)\cap H_{loc}^{2}((0,1])\quad\mid\quad(x^{\alpha}z_{x})_{x}+\frac{\mu}{x^{2-\alpha}}z\in L^{2}(0,1)\big\}.

Notice besides that, as
C^{\infty}_{c}(0,1)
is dense both in
L^{2}(0,1)
and in
H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}(0,1)
,
H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}(0,1)
is dense in
L^{2}(0,1)
.

In what follows, for simplicity, we will always denote
by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
the standard scalar product of either
L^{2}(0,1)
or
L^{2}(0,1)^{2}
, by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{X^{\prime},X}
the duality
pairing between the Hilbert space
X
and its dual
X^{\prime}
. On the other hand, we will use
\|\cdot\|^{\mu}_{\alpha}
(resp.
\|\cdot\|^{-1,\mu}_{\alpha}
) for denoting the norm of
H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}(0,1)^{2}
(resp.
H^{-1,\mu}_{\alpha}(0,1)^{2}
).

2.2
Well-posedness

Now, we are ready to give some results related to the existence, uniqueness and continuous
dependence with respect to the data of the degenerate/singular problem (
1.1
).
To this aim, let us consider the following nonhomogeneous adjoint problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}-\varphi_{t}-(x^{\alpha}\varphi_{x})_{x}-\frac{\mu}{x^{2-\alpha}}\varphi=A^{*}\varphi+g,&&\text{in}\;Q,\\
\varphi(t,0)=\varphi(t,1)=0,&&t\in(0,T),\\
\varphi(T,x)=\varphi_{0},&&\text{in}\;(0,1),\end{array}\right.

(2.2)

where
A
is given in (
1.4
) and
\varphi_{0}
and
g
are functions in appropriate spaces.

Let us start with a first result on existence and uniqueness of strict solutions to system (
2.2
).
One has (see
[
1
]
or
[
9
, Definition 4.1]
):

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume that
\varphi_{0}\in H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}(0,1)^{2}
and
g\in L^{2}(Q)^{2}
. Then, system (
2.2
) admits a unique strict solution

\displaystyle\varphi\in C^{0}([0,T];H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}(0,1)^{2})

\displaystyle\cap H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(0,1)^{2})

\displaystyle\cap L^{2}(0,T;H_{\alpha}^{2,\mu}(0,1)^{2}\cap H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}(0,1)^{2})

such that

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{C^{0}([0,T];H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}(0,1)^{2})}

\displaystyle+\|\varphi\|_{H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(0,1)^{2})}+\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H_{\alpha}^{2,\mu}(0,1)^{2}\cap H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}(0,1)^{2})}

(2.3)

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\|\varphi_{0}\|^{\mu}_{\alpha}+\|g\|_{L^{2}(Q)^{2}}\Big),

for some positive constant
C
.

In view of Proposition
2.1
, the following definition makes sense:

Definition 2.1
.

Let
y_{0}\in H_{\alpha}^{-1,\mu}(0,1)^{2}
and
v\in L^{2}(0,T)
be given. It will be said that
y\in L^{2}(Q)^{2}
is a solution by transposition to (
1.1
) if, for each
g\in L^{2}(Q)^{2}
, the following identity holds

\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}y\cdot g\,dx\,dt=\langle y_{0},\varphi(0,\cdot)\rangle_{H_{\alpha}^{-1,\mu},H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}}-\int_{0}^{T}B^{*}\varphi_{x}(t,1)\,v(t)\,dt,

(2.4)

where

\varphi\in C^{0}([0,T];H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}(0,1)^{2})\cap H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(0,1)^{2})\cap L^{2}(0,T;H_{\alpha}^{2,\mu}(0,1)^{2}\cap H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}(0,1)^{2})

is the solution of (
2.2
) associated
to
g
and
\varphi_{0}=0
.

With this definition we can state the result of existence and uniqueness of solution by transposition to system (
1.1
).

Proposition 2.2
.

Assume that
y_{0}\in H_{\alpha}^{-1,\mu}(0,1)^{2}
and
v\in L^{2}(0,T)
. Then, system (
1.1
) admits a unique solution by transposition
y
that satisfies

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}y\in L^{2}(Q)^{2}\cap C^{0}\big([0,T],H_{\alpha}^{-1,\mu}(0,1)^{2}\big),\\
y_{t}\in L^{2}\big(0,T;(H_{\alpha}^{2,\mu}(0,1)^{2}\cap H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}(0,1)^{2})^{{}^{\prime}}),\\
y_{t}-(x^{\alpha}y_{x})_{x}-\frac{\mu}{x^{2-\alpha}}y=Ay\quad\text{in}\quad L^{2}\big(0,T;(H_{\alpha}^{2,\mu}(0,1)^{2}\cap H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}(0,1)^{2})^{{}^{\prime}}),\\
y(0,\cdot)=y_{0}\quad\text{in}\quad H_{\alpha}^{-1,\mu}(0,1)^{2}\end{array}\right.

(2.5)

and

\displaystyle\|y\|_{L^{2}(Q)^{2}}+\|y\|_{C^{0}(H_{\alpha}^{-1,\mu})}+\|y_{t}\|_{L^{2}((H_{\alpha}^{2,\mu}(0,1)^{2}\cap H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}(0,1)^{2})^{{}^{\prime}})}

(2.6)

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\|v\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}+\|y_{0}\|_{\alpha}^{-1,\mu}\big),

for a constant
C=C(T)>0
.

Proof.

Let
y_{0}\in H_{\alpha}^{-1,\mu}(0,1)^{2}
,
v\in L^{2}(0,T)
and consider the following functional
\mathcal{T}:L^{2}(Q)^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

given by

\mathcal{T}(g)=\langle y_{0},\varphi(0,\cdot)\rangle_{H_{\alpha}^{-1,\mu},H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}}-\int_{0}^{T}B^{*}\varphi_{x}(t,1)v(t)\;dt,

where

\varphi\in C^{0}([0,T];H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}(0,1)^{2})\cap H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(0,1)^{2})\cap L^{2}(0,T;H_{\alpha}^{2,\mu}(0,1)^{2}\cap H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}(0,1)^{2})

is the solution of the adjoint system (
2.2
) associated to
g\in L^{2}(Q)^{2}
and
\varphi_{0}=0
. From (
2.3
),
we can deduce the existence of a positive constant
C
such that

\big|\mathcal{T}(g)\big|\leq C\big(\|v\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}+\|y_{0}\|_{\alpha}^{-1,\mu}\big)\|g\|_{L^{2}(Q)^{2}},

for all
g\in L^{2}(Q)^{2}
.
We infer that
\mathcal{T}
is bounded.
Hence, by Riesz-Fréchet representation theorem, there exists a unique
y\in L^{2}(Q)^{2}
satisfying
(
2.4
), i.e., a solution by transposition of (
1.1
) in the sense of Definition
2.1
.
It is also clear that this solution satisfies the equality

y_{t}-(x^{\alpha}y_{x})_{x}-\frac{\mu}{x^{2-\alpha}}y=Ay
in
\mathcal{D}^{{}^{\prime}}(Q)^{2}
and the estimate

\|y\|_{L^{2}(Q)^{2}}=\|\mathcal{T}\|\leq C\big(\|v\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}+\|y_{0}\|_{\alpha}^{-1,\mu}\big).

Next, we are going to prove that the solution
y
of system (
1.1
) is more regular. To be precise, let us show that

(x^{\alpha}y_{x})_{x}+\frac{\mu}{x^{2-\alpha}}y\in L^{2}\big(0,T;(H_{\alpha}^{2,\mu}(0,1)^{2}\cap H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}(0,1)^{2})^{{}^{\prime}})
and

\|(x^{\alpha}y_{x})_{x}+\frac{\mu}{x^{2-\alpha}}y\|_{L^{2}((H_{\alpha}^{2,\mu}(0,1)^{2}\cap H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}(0,1)^{2})^{{}^{\prime}})}\leq C\big(\|v\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}+\|y_{0}\|_{\alpha}^{-1,\mu}\big).

(2.7)

To this end, let us consider two sequences
\{y_{0}^{m}\}_{m\geq 1}\subset H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}(0,1)^{2}
and
\{v^{m}\}_{m\geq 1}\subset H_{0}^{1}(0,T)
such that

y_{0}^{m}\rightarrow y_{0}\quad\text{in}\quad H^{-1,\mu}(0,1)^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad v^{m}\rightarrow v\quad\text{in}\quad L^{2}(0,T).

Now, the strategy consists in transforming our original system (
1.1
) (as done for instance in
[
9
]
in
the context of a scalar degenerate/singular parabolic equation) into a problem with homogeneous boundary conditions and a source term.
To this end, let us introduce the following function:

\forall x\in[0,1],\qquad p(x):=x^{q^{\mu}_{\alpha}}\qquad\text{where}\qquad q_{\alpha}^{\mu}:=\frac{1-\alpha}{2}+\sqrt{\mu(\alpha)-\mu}.

Formally, if
y^{m}
is the solution of (
1.1
) associated to
y_{0}^{m}
and
v^{m}
, then the function defined by

\tilde{y}^{m}(t,x)=y^{m}(t,x)-Bp(x)v^{m}(t),

is solution of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\tilde{y}_{t}^{m}-(x^{\alpha}\tilde{y}_{x}^{m})_{x}-\frac{\mu}{x^{2-\alpha}}\tilde{y}^{m}=A\tilde{y}^{m}+\tilde{f}^{m}(t,x),&&(t,x)\in Q,\\
\tilde{y}^{m}(t,0)=\tilde{y}^{m}(t,1)=0,&&t\in(0,T),\\
\tilde{y}^{m}(0,x)=y_{0}^{m}(x),&&x\in(0,1),\end{array}\right.

(2.8)

where
\tilde{f}^{m}(t,x)=p(x)v^{m}(t)AB-p(x)v_{t}^{m}(t)B\in L^{2}(Q)^{2}
.
With the previous regularity assumptions on the data, we can apply Proposition
2.1
, to deduce that system (
2.8
) has a unique
strict solution

\displaystyle\tilde{y}^{m}\in C^{0}([0,T];H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}(0,1)^{2})

\displaystyle\cap H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(0,1)^{2})

\displaystyle\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{2,\mu}(0,1)^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1,\mu}(0,1)^{2}).

By setting

\tilde{v}^{m}(t,x):=Bp(x)v^{m}(t),

we observe that
\tilde{v}^{m}
satisfies

\tilde{v}^{m}\in C^{0}([0,T];H_{\alpha}^{1,\mu}(0,1)^{2})\cap H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(0,1)^{2})\cap L^{2}(0,T;H_{\alpha}^{2,\mu}(0,1)^{2}).

Therefore, the problem (
1.1
) for
v^{m}
and
y_{0}^{m}
has a unique solution

y^{m}\in C^{0}([0,T];H_{\alpha}^{1,\mu}(0,1)^{2})\cap H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(0,1)^{2})\cap L^{2}(0,T;H_{\alpha}^{2,\mu}(0,1)^{2})

which satisfies

\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}y^{m}\cdot g\;dtdx=\langle y_{0}^{m},\varphi(0,x)\rangle_{H_{\alpha}^{-1,\mu},H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}}-\int_{0}^{T}B^{*}\varphi_{x}(t,1)v^{m}(t)\,dt,\quad\forall m\geq 1,

for all
g\in L^{2}(Q)^{2}
, where
\varphi
is the solution of the system (
2.2
) associated to
g
and
\varphi_{0}=0
. The previous identity and (
2.4
) also provide:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\|y^{m}\|_{L^{2}(Q)^{2}}\leq C\big(\|v\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}+\|y_{0}\|_{\alpha}^{-1,\mu}\big)\\
y^{m}\rightarrow y\quad\text{in}\;L^{2}(Q)^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad(x^{\alpha}y_{x}^{m})_{x}+\frac{\mu}{x^{2-\alpha}}y^{m}\rightarrow(x^{\alpha}y_{x})_{x}+\frac{\mu}{x^{2-\alpha}}y\quad\text{in}\;\mathcal{D}^{{}^{\prime}}(Q)^{2}.\end{array}\right.

(2.9)

On the other hand, integrations by parts lead to

\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}\big((x^{\alpha}y_{x}^{m})_{x}+\frac{\mu}{x^{2-\alpha}}y^{m}\big)\cdot\psi\,dt=\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}y^{m}\cdot\big((x^{\alpha}\psi_{x})_{x}+\frac{\mu}{x^{2-\alpha}}\psi\big)\,dt\,dx-\int_{0}^{T}B^{*}\psi_{x}(t,1)\,v^{m}(t)\,dt,

for every
\psi\in L^{2}\big(0,T;H_{\alpha}^{2,\mu}(0,1)^{2}\cap H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}(0,1)^{2})
. From this equality we deduce
that the sequence
\{(x^{\alpha}y_{x}^{m})_{x}+\frac{\mu}{x^{2-\alpha}}y^{m}\}_{m\geq 1}
is bounded in
L^{2}\big(0,T;(H_{\alpha}^{2,\mu}(0,1)^{2}\cap H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}(0,1)^{2})^{{}^{\prime}})
.
This property together with (
2.9
) implies that

(x^{\alpha}y_{x})_{x}+\frac{\mu}{x^{2-\alpha}}y\in L^{2}\big(0,T;(H_{\alpha}^{2,\mu}(0,1)^{2}\cap H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}(0,1)^{2})^{{}^{\prime}})
and satisfies the estimate (
2.7
).

Combining the identity
y_{t}=(x^{\alpha}y_{x})_{x}+\frac{\mu}{x^{2-\alpha}}y+Ay

and the regularity property for
(x^{\alpha}y_{x})_{x}+\frac{\mu}{x^{2-\alpha}}y
, we also see
that
y_{t}\in L^{2}\big(0,T;(H_{\alpha}^{2,\mu}(0,1)^{2}\cap H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}(0,1)^{2})^{{}^{\prime}})
and

\|y_{t}\|_{L^{2}((H_{\alpha}^{2,\mu}(0,1)^{2}\cap H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}(0,1)^{2})^{{}^{\prime}})}\leq C\big(\|v\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}+\|y_{0}\|_{\alpha}^{-1,\mu}\big),

for some positive constant
C
.
Therefore
y\in C([0,T];X^{2})
, where
X
is the interpolation space

X=[L^{2}(0,1),(H_{\alpha}^{2,\mu}(0,1)^{2}\cap H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}(0,1))^{{}^{\prime}}]_{1/2}=H_{\alpha}^{-1,\mu}(0,1)
(see
[
30
, Proposition 2.1, p. 22]
).
In conclusion, we get

\|y\|_{C(H_{\alpha}^{-1,\mu})}\leq C\big(\|v\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}+\|y_{0}\|_{\alpha}^{-1,\mu}\big).

Finally, one can easily check that
y(0,\cdot)=y_{0}
in
H_{\alpha}^{-1,\mu}(0,1)^{2}
. This ends the proof.
∎

3
Spectral analysis

In order to transform the question of null controllability
into a moment problem, we need to study the eigenvalue problem of the degenerate/singular operator associated to system (
1.1
). To this end, we first recall the spectral properties of scalar degenerate/singular operators.

3.1
Scalar degenerate/singular operators

In this section, we discuss some preliminary results related to a spectral analysis of the operator
y\mapsto-(x^{\alpha}y_{x})_{x}-\frac{\mu}{x^{2-\alpha}}y
, i.e.,
the nontrivial solutions
(\lambda,\Phi)
of

\begin{cases}&-(x^{\alpha}\Phi^{\prime})^{\prime}(x)-\frac{\mu}{x^{2-\alpha}}\Phi(x)=\lambda\Phi(x),\quad x\in(0,1),\\
&\Phi(0)=\Phi(1)=0,\end{cases}

(3.1)

that will be essential for our purposes.
For this reason, we first recall some results concerning the Bessel functions that will be useful in the rest of the paper (see
[
36
]
, for more details).

For a real number
\nu\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, we denote by
J_{\nu}
the Bessel function of the first kind of order
\nu

defined by the following Taylor series expansion around
x=0
:

J_{\nu}(x)=\sum_{m\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{m}}{m!\;\Gamma(1+\nu+m)}\Big(\frac{x}{2}\Big)^{2m+\nu},

where
\Gamma(.)
is the Gamma function.

We recall that the Bessel function
J_{\nu}
satisfies the following differential
equation

x^{2}y^{\prime\prime}(x)+xy^{\prime}(x)+(x^{2}-\nu^{2})y(x)=0,\qquad x\in(0,+\infty).

Moreover, the function
J_{\nu}
has an infinite number of real zeros which are
simple with the possible exception of
x=0
(see
[
29
,
17
]
). We denote by
(j_{\nu,n})_{n\geq 1}
the strictly
increasing sequence of the positive zeros of
J_{\nu}
:

0<j_{\nu,1}<j_{\nu,2}<\cdots<j_{\nu,n}<\cdots

and we recall that

j_{\nu,n}\rightarrow+\infty\quad\text{as}\quad n\rightarrow+\infty

and the following bounds on the zeros
j_{\nu,n}
, which are provided in
[
31
]
:

•

\forall\nu\in\Big[0,\dfrac{1}{2}\Big],\,\forall n\geq 1
,

\big(n+\frac{\nu}{2}-\frac{1}{4}\big)\pi\leq j_{\nu,n}\leq\big(n+\frac{\nu}{4}-\frac{1}{8}\big)\pi.

(3.2)

•

\forall\nu\geq\dfrac{1}{2},\,\forall n\geq 1
,

\big(n+\frac{\nu}{4}-\frac{1}{8}\big)\pi\leq j_{\nu,n}\leq\big(n+\frac{\nu}{2}-\frac{1}{4}\big)\pi.

(3.3)

In our analysis, we will need the following classical result (see
[
28
, Proposition 7.8]
):

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
j_{\nu,n},n\geq 1
be the positive zeros of the Bessel function
J_{\nu}
. Then, the
following hold:

•

If
\nu\in\Big[0,\dfrac{1}{2}\Big]
, the difference sequence (
j_{\nu,n+1}-j_{\nu,n})_{n}
is nondecreasing and
converges to
\pi
as
n\longrightarrow+\infty.

•

If
\nu\geq\dfrac{1}{2}
, the sequence
(j_{\nu,n+1}-j_{\nu,n})_{n}
is nonincreasing and
converges to
\pi
as
n\longrightarrow+\infty
.

We also have that the Bessel functions enjoy the following integral formula (see
[
36
]
):

\int_{0}^{1}xJ_{\nu}(j_{\nu,n}x)J_{\nu}(j_{\nu,m}x)\,dx=\frac{\delta_{nm}}{2}[J_{\nu}^{{}^{\prime}}(j_{\nu,n})]^{2},\quad n,m\in\mathbb{N}^{*},

where,
\delta_{nm}
is the Kronecker symbol.

Next, given
\mu\leq\mu(\alpha)
, let us introduce the quantity

\nu(\alpha,\mu):=\dfrac{2}{2-\alpha}\sqrt{\Big(\frac{1-\alpha}{2}\Big)^{2}-\mu}=\dfrac{2}{2-\alpha}\sqrt{\mu(\alpha)-\mu}.

With the previous notation, we have the following result on the expression of the
eigenvalues and eigenfunctions related to problem (
3.1
)
that have been computed in
[
9
]
:

Proposition 3.1
.

Assume
\mu\leq\mu(\alpha)
.
Then the admissible eigenvalues
\lambda
for problem (
3.1
) are given by

\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}=\Big(\dfrac{2-\alpha}{2}\Big)^{2}(j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n})^{2},\qquad\forall n\geq 1.

(3.4)

and the associated normalized (in
L^{2}(0,1)
) eigenfunctions are

\Phi_{\alpha,\mu,n}(x)=\frac{\sqrt{2-\alpha}}{|J_{\nu(\alpha,\mu)}^{\prime}(j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n})|}x^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}J_{\nu(\alpha,\mu)}\Big(j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n}x^{\frac{2-\alpha}{2}}\Big),\quad n\geq 1.

(3.5)

Moreover, the family
(\Phi_{\alpha,\mu,n})_{n\geq 1}
forms an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(0,1)
.

We have the following result which will be used later.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,k})_{k\geq 1}
be the sequence of eigenvalues of the spectral problem (
3.1
).
Then, the following properties hold:

1.

For all
n,m\in\mathbb{N}^{\star}
, there is a constant
\rho>0
such that

(\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,k})_{k\geq 1}
satisfies the following gap condition: there is a constant
\rho>0
such that

|\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}-\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,m}|\geq\rho|n^{2}-m^{2}|,\qquad\forall n,m\geq 1.

(3.6)

2.

The series
\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}}
is convergent.

Proof.

1.

Let
n,m\in\mathbb{N}^{\star}
with
n\geq m
. We have

\displaystyle\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}-\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,m}=\Big(\dfrac{2-\alpha}{2}\Big)^{2}(j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n}^{2}-j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),m}^{2})

\displaystyle=\Big(\dfrac{2-\alpha}{2}\Big)^{2}(j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n}-j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),m})(j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n}+j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),m})

\displaystyle=\Big(\dfrac{2-\alpha}{2}\Big)^{2}\Big((j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n}-j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n-1})+\cdots+(j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),m+1}-j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),m})\Big)(j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n}+j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),m}).

We can now distinguish the two different cases
\nu(\alpha,\mu)\in\Big[0,\dfrac{1}{2}\Big]
and
\nu(\alpha,\mu)\geq\dfrac{1}{2}
, depending on the parameter
\mu
.

•

if
\nu(\alpha,\mu)\in\Big[0,\dfrac{1}{2}\Big]

\Big(
i.e.
\mu\in\Big(\dfrac{\alpha}{16}(3\alpha-4),\mu(\alpha)\Big]\Big)
, by virtue of Lemma
3.1

we immediately have that

j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n}-j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n-1}\geq j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),2}-j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),1},\quad\forall n\geq 2.

Therefore,

\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}-\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,m}\geq(n-m)(j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),2}-j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),1})(j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n}+j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),m}).

By using (
3.2
), the last inequality becomes:

\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}-\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,m}\geq\frac{7}{8}\pi^{2}\Big(\dfrac{2-\alpha}{2}\Big)^{2}(n-m)\big(n+m+\nu(\alpha,\mu)-\frac{1}{2}\big).

(3.7)

Moreover, we have

\big(n+m+\nu(\alpha,\mu)-\frac{1}{2}\big)>\frac{n+m}{2},

and thus, that there exists
\rho=\frac{7}{64}\pi^{2}(2-\alpha)^{2}
such that

\displaystyle\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}-\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,m}\geq\rho(n^{2}-m^{2}).

•

Let us now see the case
\nu(\alpha,\mu)\geq\dfrac{1}{2}

(
i.e.
\mu\leq\dfrac{\alpha}{16}(3\alpha-4)
)
. Here we use the fact that the sequence
(j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n+1}-j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n})_{n}
is nonincreasing and converges to
\pi
. This ensures that

j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n+1}-j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n}\geq\pi,\qquad\forall n\geq 1.

Therefore:

\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}-\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,m}\geq\Big(\dfrac{2-\alpha}{2}\Big)^{2}\pi(n-m)(j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n}+j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),m}).

Owing to (
3.3
), we also have

j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n}+j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),m}\geq\big(n+m+\frac{\nu(\alpha,\mu)}{2}-\frac{1}{4}\big)\pi\geq\pi(n+m).

Combining the above last two estimates, the thesis follows with
\rho=\Big(\dfrac{2-\alpha}{2}\Big)^{2}\pi^{2}
.

Thus, in every case there holds

\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}-\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,m}\geq\rho(n^{2}-m^{2}).

In both cases, after reversing the roles of
n
and
m
, one has

\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,m}-\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}\geq\rho(m^{2}-n^{2}).

Hence,

|\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}-\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,m}|\geq\rho|n^{2}-m^{2}|,\qquad\forall n,m\geq 1,

for a constant
\rho>0
.

2.

This point follows easily form (
3.2
). Indeed,
\forall\nu(\alpha,\mu)\in\Big(0,\dfrac{1}{2}\Big],\quad\forall n\geq 1

\Big(n-\dfrac{1}{4}\Big)\pi\leq j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n}

Thus

\sum\limits_{n\geq 1}\dfrac{1}{\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}}\leq\dfrac{1}{\pi^{2}}\Big(\frac{2}{2-\alpha}\Big)^{2}\sum\limits_{n\geq 1}\dfrac{1}{\Big(n-\frac{1}{4}\Big)^{2}}\leq\dfrac{4}{\pi^{2}}\Big(\frac{2}{2-\alpha}\Big)^{2}\sum\limits_{n\geq 1}\dfrac{1}{n^{2}}<+\infty.

∎

3.2
Vectorial degenerate/singular operators

Let
A
be given by (
1.4
) and consider the degenerate/singular vectorial operator

\displaystyle L:D(L)\subset L^{2}(0,1)^{2}

\displaystyle\rightarrow L^{2}(0,1)^{2}

(3.8)

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle\mapsto-(x^{\alpha}y_{x})_{x}-\frac{\mu}{x^{2-\alpha}}y-Ay,

with domain

In the sequel, we pass to derive some properties of the eigenvalues and eigenfunctions of the operators

and

which
will be useful for developing the moment method. Let us first analyze the spectrum of the operators
L
and

:

Proposition 3.2
.

Let us consider the operator

given by (
3.8
) and its

L^{*}
. Then,

1.

The spectra of

and
L^{*}
are given by

\sigma(L)=\sigma(L^{*})=\big\{\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(1)},\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(2)}\big\}_{n\geq 1}

(3.9)

where
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
are the eigenvalues of the matrix
A
defined by :

•

Case 1:

(3.10)

•

Case 2:
a_{2}^{2}+4a_{1}<0
,

\alpha_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\Big(a_{2}+i\sqrt{-(a_{2}^{2}+4a_{1})}\Big)\;\text{and}\;\alpha_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\Big(a_{2}-i\sqrt{-(a_{2}^{2}+4a_{1})}\Big).

(3.11)

2.

For each
n\geq 1
, the corresponding eigenfunctions of
L
(resp.,
L^{*}
) associated to
\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(1)}
and
\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(2)}
are respectively given by

\psi_{n}^{(1)}=U_{1}\Phi_{\alpha,\mu,n},\qquad\psi_{n}^{(2)}=U_{2}\Phi_{\alpha,\mu,n},

(3.12)

with

U_{1}=\frac{a_{1}}{\alpha_{2}^{2}+a_{1}}\begin{pmatrix}-\alpha_{2}\\
1\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad U_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}-\alpha_{1}\\
1\end{pmatrix}

(resp.,

\Psi_{n}^{(1)}=V_{1}\Phi_{\alpha,\mu,n},\qquad\Psi_{n}^{(2)}=V_{2}\Phi_{\alpha,\mu,n},

(3.13)

with

V_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{\alpha_{2}}{a_{1}}\\
1\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad V_{2}=\frac{a_{1}}{\alpha_{1}^{2}+a_{1}}\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{a_{1}}\\

Proof.

We will prove the result for the operator
L
. The same reasoning provides the proof for its

L^{*}
.

Using the fact that the function

is the eigenfunction of the Dirichlet degenerate/singular operator

associated to the eigenvalue

, one can see that the eigenvalues of the operator

(Id\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{2})

are given under the form

, where

Taking into account the expression of the characteristic polynomial of

∎

Let us now check that the sequence of eigenvalues of
L

and
L^{*}
fulfills the conditions in Theorem
1.2
. One has

Proposition 3.3
.

Assume that the following condition holds

\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}-\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,l}\neq\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2},\quad\forall n,l\in\mathbb{N}^{*},\quad\text{with}\quad n\neq l.

(3.14)

Then, one can construct a family from the spectrum
\big\{\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(1)},\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(2)}\big\}_{n\geq 1}
defined by

\displaystyle\big\{\Lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}\big\}_{n\geq 1}

\displaystyle=\big\{\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(1)}+\alpha_{2},\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(2)}+\alpha_{2}\big\}_{n\geq 1}

(3.15)

\displaystyle=\{\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}+\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1}:n\geq 1\}\cup\{\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}:n\geq 1\},

which satisfies the hypotheses in Theorem
1.2
.

Proof.

We distinguish between three cases depending on the spectrum of the matrix
A
.

Case 1:
A
has two real eigenvalues
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
, chosen such that
\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}
.

Let us introduce the sequence
\big\{\Lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}\big\}_{n\geq 1}
, where

\big\{\Lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}:n\geq 1\big\}:=\big\{\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(1)}+\alpha_{2},\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(2)}+\alpha_{2}\big\}_{n\geq 1}.

The hypothesis 1) holds true if and only if the condition (
3.14
) is satisfied. In addition, the hypotheses 2) and 3) are obviously satisfied by definition.

Let us now show the hypothesis 4). Since
\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1}>0
, observe that
\big\{\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(1)}+\alpha_{2}\big\}_{n\geq 1}
and

\big\{\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(2)}+\alpha_{2}\big\}_{n\geq 1}
are increasing sequences satisfying

Thus, we deduce that the sequence
\big\{\Lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}\big\}_{n\geq 1}
can be rearranged into a positive increasing sequence.

Let us move to prove hypothesis 5). For this purpose, we are going to give an explicit rearrangement of the sequence

\big\{\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(1)}+\alpha_{2},\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(2)}+\alpha_{2}\big\}_{n\geq 1}
. Firstly, observe that there exists an integer

and a constant

such that

\displaystyle\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n-1}^{(1)}<\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(2)}<\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(1)}<\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n+1}^{(2)}<\cdots,\quad\forall n\geq n_{0},\;\text{and}

(3.16)

\displaystyle\min_{n\geq n_{0}}\big\{\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(2)}-\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n-1}^{(1)},\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(1)}-\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(2)}\big\}>C.

Indeed, using (
3.6
), one has

\displaystyle\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(2)}-\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n-1}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}-\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n-1}+\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}

\displaystyle\geq\rho(2n-1)+\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}\underset{n\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}+\infty.

From (
3.2
) and the fact that
\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(1)}-\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(2)}=\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1}>0

3.16
).

Therefore, if
1\leq n\leq 2n_{0}-2
, we define
\Lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}
such that

\displaystyle\big\{\Lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}\big\}_{1\leq n\leq 2n_{0}-2}

\displaystyle=\{\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(1)}+\alpha_{2}\}_{1\leq n\leq n_{0}-1}\cup\{\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(2)}+\alpha_{2}\}_{1\leq n\leq n_{0}-1}\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}<\Lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n+1}\quad\forall n:1\leq n\leq 2n_{0}-3.

Moreover, from
(2n_{0}-1)
-th term, we choose to arrange the sequence as follows:

\Lambda_{\alpha,\mu,2n-1}=\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(2)}+\alpha_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\Lambda_{\alpha,\mu,2n}=\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(1)}+\alpha_{2},\;\forall n\geq n_{0}.

(3.18)

Since the elements of the sequence
\big\{\Lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}\big\}_{n\geq 1}
are pairwise different and from (

), one has:

\inf\limits_{n,m\geq 1:n\neq m}|\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(1)}-\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,m}^{(2)}|>0.

(3.19)

Hence, thanks to (

\big\{\Lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}\big\}_{n\geq 1}

) for every

.

Our next task will be to prove the first inequality of (
1.7
) for appropriate
q>0
and
\varrho>0
.
To this aim, as it has been remarked in
[
25
]
, it is enough to prove the existence of
q>0
and
\tilde{\varrho}>0
such that

|\Lambda_{n}-\Lambda_{m}|\geq\tilde{\varrho}|n^{2}-m^{2}|\quad\forall n,m\geq q,\;|n-m|\geq q.

(3.20)

We divide the proof of (
3.20
) into two steps.

1.

Observe that, if
n,m\in\mathbb{N}^{\star}
are such that

and
|n-m|\geq n_{0}
, then by (

) and using (

Proposition 3.4
.

Let us consider the sequences

2.

\mathcal{B}

.

3.

The sequences
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{B}^{*}
are biorthogonal Riesz bases of
L^{2}(0,1)^{2}
.

4.

The sequence
\mathcal{B}^{*}
is a basis of
H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}(0,1)^{2}

Proof.

From the expressions of
\psi_{n}^{(i)}
and
\Psi_{n}^{(i)}
, we can write

\psi_{n}^{(i)}=U_{i}\Phi_{\alpha,\mu,n}\quad\text{and}\quad\Psi_{n}^{(i)}=V_{i}\Phi_{\alpha,\mu,n},\quad i=1,2,\quad n\geq 1,

where
U_{i},V_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\Phi_{\alpha,\mu,n}
is given in (
3.5

1.

It is not difficult to check that
\{U_{i}\}_{i=1,2}
and
\{V_{i}\}_{i=1,2}
are
biorthogonal families of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Moreover,
since
(\Phi_{\alpha,\mu,n})_{n\geq 1}

\langle\psi_{n}^{(i)},\Psi_{k}^{(j)}\rangle=(U_{i})^{tr}V_{j}\langle\Phi_{\alpha,\mu,n},\Phi_{\alpha,\mu,k}\rangle=\delta_{ij}\delta_{nk},\quad\forall n,k\geq 1,\quad i,j=1,2.

2.

We will use
[
27
, Lemma 1.44]
. For this purpose, let us consider

such that

f_{i,n}

(i=1,2)
the corresponding Fourier coefficients of the function

[

where

,

But, we know that the series

1.1
.

First of all, using Lemma
3.2
and similar techniques as in Proposition
3.3
, one can prove that the following result holds.

Proposition 4.1
.

Assume that condition (
3.14
) holds. Then, the family defined in (
3.15
)
satisfies (
1.6
).

Now, we are ready to state our first main result on approximate controllability. One has:

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mu\leq\mu(\alpha)
and consider
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
the eigenvalues of the matrix
A
. Then, system (
1.1
) is approximately controllable in
H^{-1,\mu}_{\alpha}(0,1)^{2}
at time
T>0
if and only if
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
satisfy condition (
3.14
).

Proof.

As said in section
2
, in order to prove this theorem we will follow a duality approach leading us to
study the unique continuation property for the adjoint system.

Necessary condition:

By contradiction, let us assume that condition (
3.14
) does not hold, i.e., that there is
n_{0},l_{0}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
with
n_{0}\neq l_{0}
such that

\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n_{0}}^{(1)}=\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,l_{0}}^{(2)}:=\lambda.

Let us see that the unique continuation property for
the adjoint system (
2.10
) is no longer valid. Indeed, let us take

\varphi_{0}=a\Psi_{n_{0}}^{(1)}+b\Psi_{l_{0}}^{(2)}\in H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}(0,1)^{2}
, with
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
to be determined. In this case, it is not difficult to see that the corresponding solution to the adjoint problem (
2.10
) is
given by

\varphi(t,x)=(a\Psi_{n_{0}}^{(1)}(x)+b\Psi_{l_{0}}^{(2)}(x))e^{-\lambda(T-t)},\quad\forall(t,x)\in Q.

By recalling the definition of
\Psi_{n}^{(i)}
, we have that

\displaystyle B^{*}(x^{\alpha}\varphi_{x})(t,1)

\displaystyle=B^{*}\big(a\Psi_{n_{0},x}^{(1)}(1)+b\Psi_{l_{0},x}^{(2)}(1)\big)e^{-\lambda(T-t)}

\displaystyle=\Big(aB^{*}V_{1}(x^{\alpha}(\Phi_{\alpha,\mu,n_{0}})_{x})(1)+bB^{*}V_{2}(x^{\alpha}(\Phi_{\alpha,\mu,l_{0}})_{x})(1)\Big)e^{-\lambda(T-t)}

\displaystyle=-\Big(a\frac{\alpha_{2}}{a_{1}}(x^{\alpha}(\Phi_{\alpha,\mu,n_{0}})_{x})(1)+b\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{1}^{2}+a_{1}}(x^{\alpha}(\Phi_{\alpha,\mu,l_{0}})_{x})(1)\Big)e^{-\lambda(T-t)}.

Then, choosing

\displaystyle a=\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{1}^{2}+a_{1}}(x^{\alpha}(\Phi_{\alpha,\mu,n_{0}})_{x})(1)\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle b=-\frac{\alpha_{2}}{a_{1}}(x^{\alpha}(\Phi_{\alpha,\mu,l_{0}})_{x})(1),

we get that
B^{*}(x^{\alpha}\varphi_{x})(t,1)=0
but
\varphi_{0}\neq 0
, which proves that the unique continuation property for the adjoint system (
2.10
) fails to be true. This ends the proof of the necessary part.

Sufficient condition:
Let us now assume that the condition (
3.14
) holds and
prove that the unique continuation property for the solutions of the adjoint system (
2.10
) holds.

Let us consider
\varphi_{0}\in H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}(0,1)^{2}
and suppose that the corresponding solution
\varphi
of the adjoint
problem (
2.10
) satisfies

B^{*}(x^{\alpha}\varphi_{x})(t,1)=0,\quad\forall t\in(0,T).

(4.1)

From Proposition
3.4
, we know that
\mathcal{B}^{*}
is a basis for
H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}(0,1)^{2}
and thus
\varphi_{0}\in H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}(0,1)^{2}
can be written as

\varphi_{0}=\sum_{n\geq 1}(b_{n}\Psi_{n}^{(1)}+c_{n}\Psi_{n}^{(2)}),

where

b_{n}=\langle\psi_{n}^{(1)},\varphi_{0}\rangle_{H^{-1,\mu}_{\alpha},H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}}\quad\text{and}\quad c_{n}=\langle\psi_{n}^{(2)},\varphi_{0}\rangle_{H^{-1,\mu}_{\alpha},H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}},\quad\text{for any}\quad n\geq 1.

Using Proposition
3.2
, the corresponding solution
\varphi
of system (
2.10
) associated to
\varphi_{0}
is given by

\varphi(t,\cdot)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\Big(b_{n}\Psi_{n}^{(1)}e^{-\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(1)}(T-t)}+c_{n}\Psi_{n}^{(2)}e^{-\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(2)}(T-t)}\Big),\quad\forall t\in(0,T).

On the other hand, direct computations show that

\big(x^{\alpha}(\Phi_{\alpha,\mu,n})_{x}\big)(1)=\frac{(2-\alpha)^{\frac{3}{2}}j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n}}{2|J^{\prime}_{\nu(\alpha,\mu)}(j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n})|}J^{\prime}_{\nu(\alpha,\mu)}(j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n}).

Hence,

\displaystyle 0=B^{*}(x^{\alpha}\varphi_{x})(T-t,1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 1}B^{*}\big(b_{n}(x^{\alpha}\Psi_{n,x}^{(1)})(1)e^{-\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(1)}t}+c_{n}(x^{\alpha}\Psi_{n,x}^{(2)})(1)e^{-\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(2)}t}\big)

\displaystyle=\big(x^{\alpha}(\Phi_{\alpha,\mu,n})_{x}\big)(1)\big(b_{n}B^{*}V_{1}e^{-\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(1)}t}+c_{n}B^{*}V_{2}e^{-\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(2)}t}\big)

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{(2-\alpha)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{2}\sum_{n\geq 1}\dfrac{J^{\prime}_{\nu(\alpha,\mu)}(j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n})}{|J^{\prime}_{\nu(\alpha,\mu)}(j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n})|}\frac{j_{\nu_{{(\alpha,\mu)},n}}}{a_{1}}\big(b_{n}\alpha_{2}e^{-\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(1)}t}+c_{n}\alpha_{1}\frac{a_{1}}{\alpha_{1}^{2}+a_{1}}e^{-\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(2)}t}\big)

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{(2-\alpha)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{2}\sum_{n\geq 1}\dfrac{J^{\prime}_{\nu(\alpha,\mu)}(j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n})}{|J^{\prime}_{\nu(\alpha,\mu)}(j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n})|}\frac{j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n}}{a_{1}}e^{\alpha_{2}t}\big(b_{n}\alpha_{2}e^{-(\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(1)}+\alpha_{2})t}+c_{n}\alpha_{1}\frac{a_{1}}{\alpha_{1}^{2}+a_{1}}e^{-(\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(2)}+\alpha_{2})t}\big).

From Proposition
4.1
, we can apply Theorem
1.1
in order to deduce the existence of a biorthogonal
family
\{q_{n}^{(1)},q_{n}^{(2)}\}_{n\geq 1}
to

\{e^{-(\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(1)}+\alpha_{2})t},e^{-(\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(2)}+\alpha_{2})t}\}_{n\geq 1}
in
L^{2}(0,T)
.
Thus, the previous identity, in particular, implies

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\int_{0}^{T}B^{*}(x^{\alpha}\varphi_{x})(T-t,1)\,e^{-\alpha_{2}t}\,q_{n}^{(1)}(t)\,dt&=-\dfrac{(2-\alpha)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{2}\dfrac{J^{\prime}_{\nu(\alpha,\mu)}(j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n})}{|J^{\prime}_{\nu(\alpha,\mu)}(j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n})|}\frac{j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n}}{a_{1}}b_{n}\alpha_{2}\\
&=0,\quad\forall n\geq 1\\
\\
\int_{0}^{T}B^{*}(x^{\alpha}\varphi_{x})(T-t,1)\,e^{-\alpha_{2}t}\,q_{n}^{(2)}(t)\,dt&=-\dfrac{(2-\alpha)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{2}\dfrac{J^{\prime}_{\nu(\alpha,\mu)}(j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n})}{|J^{\prime}_{\nu(\alpha,\mu)}(j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n})|}\frac{j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n}}{a_{1}}c_{n}\alpha_{1}\frac{a_{1}}{\alpha_{1}^{2}+a_{1}}\\
&=0,\quad\forall n\geq 1.\end{array}\right.

Thus
b_{n}=c_{n}=0
for any
n\geq 1
. In conclusion,
\varphi_{0}=0
. This proves the continuation property for the solutions to the adjoint problem (
2.10
) and, thanks to Theorem
2.1
, the
approximate controllability of system (
1.1
) at any positive time
T
holds.
∎

5
Boundary null controllability

In this section, we will address the main achievement of this work which is the boundary null controllability result of system (
1.1
), providing an estimate of the control cost as a function of
T
. In this sense, one has:

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\mu\leq\mu(\alpha)
and consider by
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
the eigenvalues of
A
satisfying (
3.14
).
Then, for every

T>0
and
y_{0}\in H^{-1,\mu}_{\alpha}(0,1)^{2}
there exists a null control
v\in L^{2}(0,T)
for system (
1.1
)
which, in addition, satisfies

\|v\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\leq Ce^{CT+\frac{C}{T}}\|y_{0}\|_{H^{-1,\mu}_{\alpha}}.

(5.1)

Proof.

To prove Theorem
5.1
, we transform the controllability
problem into a moment problem. Using Proposition
2.3
, we deduce that the control
v\in L^{2}(0,T)
drives the solution of (
1.1
) to zero at time
T
if and only if
v\in L^{2}(0,T)
fulfills

\int_{0}^{T}B^{*}(x^{\alpha}\varphi_{x})(t,1)\,v(t)\;dt=\langle y_{0},\varphi(0,\cdot)\rangle_{H^{-1,\mu}_{\alpha},H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}},\quad\forall\varphi_{0}\in H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}(0,1)^{2}

(5.2)

where
\varphi\in C^{0}\big([0,T];H_{0}^{1,\mu}(0,1)^{2}\big)\cap L^{2}\big(0,T;H^{2,\mu}(0,1)^{2}\cap H^{1,\mu}_{0}(0,1)^{2}\big)
is the solution of the adjoint system (
2.10
) associated to
\varphi_{0}
.

Using Proposition
3.2
, the corresponding solution
\varphi
of system (
2.10
) associated to
\varphi_{0}
is given by

\displaystyle\varphi(t,x)=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 1}\Big(\langle\psi_{k}^{(1)},\varphi_{0}\rangle_{H^{-1,\mu}_{\alpha},H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}}\Psi_{k}^{(1)}e^{-\lambda_{\mu,k}^{(1)}(T-t)}+\langle\psi_{k}^{(2)},\varphi_{0}\rangle_{H^{-1,\mu}_{\alpha},H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}}\Psi_{k}^{(2)}e^{-\lambda_{\mu,k}^{(2)}(T-t)}\Big).

From Proposition
3.4
, we have that
\mathcal{B}^{*}
is a basis for
H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}(0,1)^{2}
. In particular, we also deduce that

\varphi(t,x)=\Psi_{n}^{(i)}(x)e^{-\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(i)}(T-t)}
is the solution of system (
2.10
) corresponding to

\varphi_{0}=\Psi_{n}^{(i)}\in H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}(0,1)^{2}
.
Therefore, we can deduce that the identity (
5.2
) is equivalent to

\int_{0}^{T}B^{*}(x^{\alpha}\Psi_{n,x}^{(i)})(1)v(t)e^{-\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(i)}(T-t)}dt=e^{-\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(i)}T}\langle y_{0},\Psi_{n}^{(i)}\rangle_{H^{-1,\mu}_{\alpha},H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}},\;\forall n\geq 1,\;i=1,2.

Taking
into account the expressions of
\Psi_{n}^{(i)}
(see (
3.13
)), we infer that
v\in L^{2}(0,T)
is
a null control for system (
1.1
) associated to
y_{0}
if and only if

\displaystyle\frac{(2-\alpha)^{\frac{3}{2}}j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n}}{2|J^{\prime}_{\nu(\alpha,\mu)}(j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n})|}J^{\prime}_{\nu(\alpha,\mu)}(j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n})B^{*}V_{i}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}v(t)e^{-\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(i)}(T-t)}dt

\displaystyle\quad=e^{-\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(i)}T}\langle y_{0},\Psi_{n}^{(i)}\rangle_{H^{-1,\mu}_{\alpha},H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}},\;\forall n\geq 1,\;i=1,2

and equivalently,

\int_{0}^{T}v(t)e^{-\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(i)}(T-t)}dt=C_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n}^{(i)},\;\forall n\geq 1,\;i=1,2,

(5.3)

where

C_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n}^{(i)}
is given by

C_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n}^{(i)}=\frac{2|J^{\prime}_{\nu(\alpha,\mu)}(j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n})|e^{-\lambda_{\mu,n}^{(i)}T}}{(2-\alpha)^{\frac{3}{2}}j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n}J^{\prime}_{\nu(\alpha,\mu)}(j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n})B^{*}V_{i}}\langle y_{0},\Psi_{n}^{(i)}\rangle_{H^{-1,\mu}_{\alpha},H_{\alpha,0}^{1,\mu}},\;\forall n\geq 1,\;i=1,2.

Performing the change of variable
s=T/2-t
in (
5.3
), the controllability problem reduces then to the following moment problem:
Given
y_{0}\in H^{-1,\mu}_{\alpha}(0,1)^{2}
find
v\in L^{2}(0,T)
such that
u(s)=v(T/2-s)e^{\alpha_{2}s}\in L^{2}(-T/2,T/2)
satisfies

\int_{-T/2}^{T/2}u(s)e^{-(\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(i)}+\alpha_{2})s}ds=\widehat{C}_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n}^{(i)},\quad\forall n\geq 1,\quad i=1,2,

(5.4)

with

\widehat{C}_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n}^{(i)}=e^{\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(i)}T/2}C_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n}^{(i)}.

(5.5)

At this stage, the strategy to solve the moment problem (
5.4
) is to use the concept of biorthogonal family. In fact, Proposition
3.3
and Theorem
1.2
guarantee the existence of
T_{0}>0
, such that for any
T\in(0,T_{0})
, there exists a biorthogonal family
\{q_{n}^{(1)},q_{n}^{(2)}\}_{n\geq 1}
to

\{e^{-(\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(1)}+\alpha_{2})t},e^{-(\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(2)}+\alpha_{2})t}\}_{n\geq 1}
in
L^{2}(-T/2,T/2)
which also satisfies

\|q_{n}^{(i)}\|_{L^{2}(-T/2,T/2)}\leq Ce^{\sqrt{\Re(\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(i)}+\alpha_{2})}+\frac{C}{T}},\qquad\forall n\geq 1,\quad i=1,2.

(5.6)

for some positive constant
C
independent of
T
.

For
T<T_{0}
, a solution to the moment problem (
5.4
) is then given for every

t\in(0,T)
by

u(t)=\sum_{n\geq 1}(\widehat{C}_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n}^{(1)}q_{n}^{(1)}(t)+\widehat{C}_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n}^{(2)}q_{n}^{(2)}(t)).

Thus

v(t)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\big(\widehat{C}_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n}^{(1)}q_{n}^{(1)}(T/2-t)+\widehat{C}_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n}^{(2)}q_{n}^{(2)}(T/2-t)\big)e^{-\alpha_{2}(T/2-t)}.

(5.7)

The only remaining point is to prove that
v\in L^{2}(0,T)
and to estimate its norm with respect to
T

and
y_{0}
. This can be achieved thanks to the estimate (
5.6
).
Indeed, from the expression of
\Psi_{n}^{(i)}
and
\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(i)}
, we can easily deduce the existence of a constant
C_{1}>0
such that for
i=1,2
:

\|\Psi_{n}^{(i)}\|_{\mu}\leq C_{1}\sqrt{\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}}=C_{1}j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n},\quad\;\forall n\geq 1.

From (
5.5
), it is easy to see that there exists a new constants
C
not depending on
n
and
T
such that

|\widehat{C}_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n}^{(i)}|\leq Ce^{-\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(i)}T/2}\|y_{0}\|_{H^{-1,\mu}_{\alpha}},\quad\forall n\geq 1,\quad i=1,2.

(5.8)

Coming back to the expression (
5.7
) of the null control
v
, taking into account the definition of
\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}^{(i)}
and using the estimates (
5.6
) and (
5.8
), we get

\|v\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\leq Ce^{CT}\|y_{0}\|_{H^{-1,\mu}_{\alpha}}\sum_{n\geq 1}e^{-\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}T/2}e^{C\sqrt{\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}}+\frac{C}{T}}.

(5.9)

Moreover, Young’s inequality gives

C\sqrt{\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}}\leq\frac{\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}T}{4}+\frac{C^{2}}{T}

for every
n\geq 1
and
T>0
, so that

\|v\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\leq Ce^{CT+\frac{C}{T}}\|y_{0}\|_{H^{-1,\mu}_{\alpha}}\sum_{n\geq 1}e^{-\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}T/4}.

On the other hand, by (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), it can be easily checked that there exists a constant
C>0
such that

Cn^{2}\leq\lambda_{\alpha,\mu,n}=j_{\nu(\alpha,\mu),n}^{2},\quad\forall n\geq 1.

Finally, for every
T<T_{0}
, we then have

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{CT+\frac{C}{T}}\|y_{0}\|_{H^{-1,\mu}_{\alpha}}\sum_{n\geq 1}e^{-Cn^{2}T}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{CT+\frac{C}{T}}\|y_{0}\|_{H^{-1,\mu}_{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-CTs^{2}}\,ds

\displaystyle=Ce^{CT+\frac{C}{T}}\|y_{0}\|_{H^{-1,\mu}_{\alpha}}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{T}}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{CT+\frac{C}{T}}\|y_{0}\|_{H^{-1,\mu}_{\alpha}},

where
C
is independent of
T
. This inequality shows that

v\in L^{2}(0,T)
and yields the desired estimate on the null control in the case where
T<T_{0}
.
The case
T\geq T_{0}
is actually reduced to the previous one. Indeed,
any continuation by zero of a control on
(0,T_{0}/2)
is a control on
(0,T)
and the estimate
follows from the decrease of the cost with respect to the time.
This completes the proof of Theorem
5.1
.

∎

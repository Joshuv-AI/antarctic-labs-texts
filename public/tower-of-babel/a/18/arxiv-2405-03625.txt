1.
Introduction

Throughout this work, an integer
b>1
and a block of
b
-ary digits

w=d_{1}\dots d_{p}
having length
|w|=p\geq 1
are fixed.

Let
S_{w}(k)=\sum^{(k)}m^{-1}
where the denominators are the positive
integers whose (minimal) representations as strings of
b
-ary digits contain

occurrences of the block
w
. Their finiteness will be reproven
in the text body. For single-digit blocks

, i.e.

[
8

[

]

(1)

\lim_{k\to\infty}S_{w}(k)=b^{p}\log(b)\;.

The approach of

4
]

[
1
,
2
]
. One of the reasons why the validity of

For each

,
\Bigl|S_{w}(k)-b^{|w|}\log(b)\Bigr|\leq(b-1)b^{|w|-\max(k,2)+1}

This estimate is definitely not sharp; the author studied in

]
the situation for one-digit blocks

Remark 1
.

The rules of

[

,

,

,

]

[

]
. With

replaced by the one
using the conventions in
[

]

.

In
[

), we worked with formulae of the type

for some discrete measures

.
In
[

this was extended to the

, the integral formula (

Here, we will not transform the integral formula (

2.
Generating series for
the
w
-block counts

It has definite advantages in the study of the
S_{w}(k)
’s to work not only
with integers but also with strings (they may also be called “words”),
i.e. elements of
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{D}}=\cup_{l\geq 0}\mathsf{D}^{l}
,
\mathsf{D}=\{0,\dots,b-1\}
. So
all strings
X
considered here have a finite length
l=|X|
. The empty string
shall be denoted
\epsilon
, and
|\epsilon|=0
. The length
|X|
may also be
denoted
l(X)
.

Each such string of
b
-ary digits
X
defines a non-negative integer
n(X)

in the usual way of positional left to right notation. We set
n(\epsilon)=0
. Conversely each non-negative integer
n
has a minimal representation

X(n)
as a string, all others being with prepended leading zeros. In particular

X(0)=\epsilon
.

For any string
X
we let
k_{w}(X)
be the number of (possibly overlapping) occurrences
of
w
in
X
.
We say that a string is
k
-admissible if
k_{w}(X)=k
. Let
N_{w}(k,l)
for
each
k\geq 0
and
l\geq 0
be the number of
k
-admissible strings of
length
l
. We let
Z_{w}(k)
be the corresponding generating series in the
indeterminate
t
:

(3)

Z_{w}(k)=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}N_{w}(k,l)t^{l}\;.

Whenever we subtitute
b^{-1}
for
t
in such a generating series, we say
that we compute the “total mass” of the objects counted by the series.
I.e. each string
X
is weighted as
b^{-|X|}
. For example strings of
length
l
have a total mass equal to
1
(also for
l=0
, there is then only
one
X
, the empty string). Here is our main result:

Theorem 3
.

Let
s
be any string of length
\ell
, and let
k_{w}^{*}(s)=\max k_{w}(sz)

where the maximum is taken over all strings
z
of length
p-1
(with

p=|w|
). For each
k\geq 1+k_{w}^{*}(s)
, the total mass of the
k
-admissible
strings having
s
as prefix is equal to
b^{p}\cdot b^{-\ell}
. In
particular for any
k\geq 1
,
Z_{w}(k)(b^{-1})=b^{p}
.

Note that
k_{w}^{*}(s)\leq\ell
in the above theorem so the conclusion applies to
any
k>\ell
. As notational shortcut, in place of appending
(b^{-1})
to
express evaluation at
b^{-1}
, we sometimes replace the letter
Z
by
M

(for “mass”), so the theorem says in particular
M_{w}(k)=b^{p}
.

It turns out that the arguments which allowed us to prove Theorem

3
also allow to express all
Z_{w}(k)
’s,
k\geq 1
, in
terms of
Z_{w}(0)
. The existence of such expressions is
related to the fact that the doubly generating series

(4)

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}N_{w}(k,l)r^{k}t^{l}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}Z_{w}(k)r^{k}\;,

falls under the scope of a general theory of Goulden and Jackson (see

[
9
]
,
[
10
, §2.8]
; see also
Exercise 14 from
[
15
, Chap. 4]
as an introduction) where a notion
of
cluster generating function
is fundamental. It would take us a bit
too far to discuss the details of the notion here, and is not needed as
we will obtain a complete description of the
Z_{w}(k)
from first principles.

Guibas and Odlyzko define in
[
11
]
the

auto-correlation polynomial

A_{w}
of a block
w
as follows:
A_{w}=\sum_{0\leq i<p}c_{i}t^{i}
with
c_{i}=1
if the prefix of
w
of length
p-i
is
identical with the suffix of the same length, else
c_{i}=0
. Thus the indices

with
c_{i}=1
are the

of

, i.e., there holds

for

. For example, with

,

for

w=aaa
,
1+t^{2}
for
w=aba
with
b\neq a
, and
1
else. For all
p
’s, if

c_{1}=1
then

contains only the same repeated letter hence all other

c_{i}
’s have to be also

. In
[
12
]
, Guibas and
Odlyzko have characterized the polynomials realizable as such an
A_{w}
. In

[
11
]
they prove:

(5)

Z_{w}(0)=\frac{A_{w}}{(1-bt)A_{w}+t^{p}}\;.

It turns out that we do not really need formula (
5
), but for
completeness we will include a proof.

The rationality of
Z_{w}(0)
can also be obtained as a very special case of
Proposition 4.7.6 of
[
15
]
which itself is an application of the

transfer matrix
method.

Let us start with proving that
b^{-1}
is always in the open disk of
convergence. This is most probably in the literature but we do not
know a reference.

Lemma 4
.

Each
Z_{w}(k)
has a radius of convergence at least equal to

(b^{p}-1)^{-p^{-1}}
. And

(6)

\sum_{0\leq j\leq k}Z_{w}(j)(b^{-1})\leq p(k+1)b^{p}\;.

Proof.

Let us obtain an upper bound for
\sum_{0\leq j\leq k}N_{w}(j,l)
. We write

l=qp+r
with
0\leq r<p
. We consider a string of length
l

as
q
contiguous blocks each of length
p
and
r
extra digits. If such a
string of length
qp+r
has at most
k
occurrences of
w
, then in
particular among the
q
blocks of length
p
, at most
k
of them are equal
to
w
. For each
i
,
0\leq i\leq k
, there are
\binom{q}{i}(b^{p}-1)^{q-i}

ways (with
\binom{q}{i}=0
for
i>q
) of obtaining a length
qp

string having exactly
i
among its
q
contiguous length
p
blocks
which are equal to
w
. It is possible for such a string to have
>k

occurrences of
w
, but all strings of length
qp
and
\leq k
occurrences
of
w
are accounted for. Hence

(7)

\sum_{0\leq j\leq k}N_{w}(j,l)\leq\sum_{0\leq i\leq k}\binom{q}{i}(b^{p}-1)^{q-i}\times b^{r},\qquad l=qp+r\;,

where
b^{r}
is for the remaining, unconstrained,
r
digits. We obtain in
this way a dominating series, i.e. a series whose coefficients are at
least equal to the (absolute values of the) original ones in the termwise
addition
\sum_{0\leq j\leq k}Z_{w}(j)
(which has only non-negative
coefficients):

(8)

\sum_{0\leq i\leq k}\sum_{q=0}^{\infty}\binom{q}{i}(b^{p}-1)^{q-i}t^{pq}\times\sum_{0\leq r<p}b^{r}t^{r}\;.

We recognize therein binomial series and reformulate the dominating series as

(9)

\sum_{0\leq i\leq k}\frac{t^{pi}}{(1-(b^{p}-1)t^{p})^{i+1}}\times\sum_{0\leq r<p}b^{r}t^{r}\;.

The radius of convergence of
\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(\sum_{0\leq j\leq k}N_{w}(j,l))t^{l}
is at least the one of the dominating series, which from the
above is
R=(b^{p}-1)^{-p^{-1}}
. Hence the radii of convergence of the
individual series
Z_{w}(j)=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}N_{w}(j,l)t^{l}
with non-negative
coefficients all are
\geq R
. This
R
is larger than
b^{-1}
, and
substituting
t=b^{-1}
leads to the upper bound (
6
).
∎

Lemma 5
.

For all
k\geq 0
,
S_{w}(k)<\infty
.

Proof.

Consider the contribution to
S_{w}(k)
by integers of a given length

l\geq 1
, i.e. those integers in
[b^{l-1},b^{l})
having
k
occurrences of

w
. This contribution is bounded above by
b^{1-l}N_{w}(k,l)
, hence

S_{w}(k)\leq bZ_{w}(k)(b^{-1})<\infty
.
∎

We have defined the generating series

. Let us similarly define

Z_{w}(x,j,y)
as the generating series of the counts per length of

-admissible strings which start with a given block

. We will mainly use them for

and

. So if

is already defined.

We let

be the prefix (initial sub-block)

of

Lemma 6
.

Let

. Then for any
(p-1)
-block

one has

(10)

Z_{w}(s,k)=t^{2-p}Z_{w}(s,0,u)Z_{w}(v,k-1)\;.

Proof.

Cut the
k
-admissible string starting with
s
into two pieces: the first
one ends with the

part of the left-most occurrence of
w
. The second
piece starts with the
v
part of this left-most occurrence of
w
. This
second piece

has exactly

occurrences of
w
. The first piece
L

has none. Conversely any two such pieces

and

can be glued together
along their

common digits (if

, this means creating

, if

p=2

, if

, it is gluing with the

which we must correct by the factor

for matters of total

∎

Using this lemma with

the
(p-1)
-suffix of

we obtain in particular,
for all

:

(11)

Z_{w}(v,k)=\Bigl(t^{2-p}Z_{w}(v,0,u)\Bigr)^{k}Z_{w}(v,0)\;.

To shorten notation, when evaluating at

, in place of appending

(b^{-1})
we replace the letter

Lemma 7
.

M_{w}(v,k)=Z_{w}(v,k)(b^{-1})
is a constant which is independent of
k\geq 0
.

Proof.

From (
11
),
M_{w}(v,k)=\alpha\beta^{k}
, for some

\alpha,\beta\geq 0
. The hypothesis

is incompatible
with the (linearly divergent, not geometrically) upper bound

). And the hypothesis
\beta<1

\alpha=0
is
incompatible with the fact that
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}Z_{w}(v,k)(b^{-1})=\infty
. Indeed this sum evaluates the total mass of all strings starting

.
∎

The next lemma will imply that the constant value of

.

Lemma 8
.

The following relations hold and show that knowing one of

,

(12)

\displaystyle(1-bt)t^{1-p}Z_{w}(v,0)

\displaystyle=1-t^{2-p}Z_{w}(v,0,u)\;,

(13)

\displaystyle(1-bt)Z_{w}(0)

\displaystyle=1-tZ_{w}(v,0)\;.

One surely has

, so it is licit in

(

) for all

, to obtain the identity

(14)

\frac{t^{p-1}}{1-bt}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}Z_{w}(v,k)=\frac{Z_{w}(v,0)}{1-t^{2-p}Z_{w}(v,0,u)}\;,

hence (

be a

-admissible string, of length at

. If

), then

is an arbitrary (possibly empty)

-admissible string. If

-suffix

. Hence

(15)

Z_{w}(0)=1+(b-1)tZ_{w}(0)+t(Z_{w}(0)-Z_{w}(v,0))\;,

Substituting

.
∎

Lemma 9
.

The two series
Z_{w}(\epsilon,0,u)
and
Z_{w}(v,0)
are identical.

Proof.

Let
r(w)
be the reversed block. Its

-suffix is

. We thus

(1-bt)Z_{r(w)}(0)=1-tZ_{r(w)}(r(u),0)
. But mapping strings to

, and

Z_{r(w)}(r(u),0)=Z_{w}(\epsilon,0,u)
. Thus
Z_{w}(\epsilon,0,u)=Z_{w}(v,0)
.

Lemma 10
.

For

Z_{w}(k)=t^{2-p}\Bigl(t^{2-p}Z_{w}(v,0,u)\Bigr)^{k-1}Z_{w}(v,0)^{2}\;,

.

For any given block

. For

X=dx
is
k
-admissible if and only
x
is, hence the result in that
case. Thus the
k
-admissible strings not starting with
d_{1}
make up for a
total mass of
(b-1)b^{-1}\cdot b^{p}
. This forces the
k
-admissible
strings starting with
d_{1}
to have a total mass
b^{p-1}
too (we have used
that the empty string is not admissible as
k\geq 1
).

We can deduce in a novel manner
M_{w}(v,k)=M_{w}(v,k-1)
hence Lemma

7
from this. The above paragraph showed (without using any
anterior result apart from finiteness) that the mass of
k
-admissible
strings starting with
d_{1}
is
b^{-1}
times
M_{w}(k)
. But
X=d_{1}x
is
admissible if either
x
does not start with
v
and is
k
-admissible, or

x
starts with
v
and is
(k-1)
-admissible. This gives a mass equal to

b^{-1}(M_{w}(k)-M_{w}(v,k))+b^{-1}M_{w}(v,k-1)
. But the final result has
to be
b^{-1}M_{w}(k)
. So
M_{w}(v,k)=M_{w}(v,k-1)
.
∎

In order to have a fully-rounded picture we now go the extra step of proving
the Guibas-Odlyzko formula (
5
). We can partition the set
\mathcal{A}
of

0
-admissible strings
X
according to the value of
k_{w}(vX)
. We obtain a
partition of
\mathcal{A}
into
\mathcal{A}_{0}
, …,
\mathcal{A}_{p-1}
(some of these possibly
empty, of course). Suppose
X
belongs to
\mathcal{A}_{j}
,
j\geq 1
and let

i\in\{1,\dots,p-1\}
be the index in
wX
, counting from the left and
starting at
0
, of the last occurrence of
w
in
wX
. We can think of
i

as how many times we need to shift
w
one unit to the right in order to reach
that final occurrence of
w
in
wX
. It is also the index in
vX
of the
last occurrence of
w
, but starting the index count at
1
, not
0
. Recall
in what is next that we write
w=d_{1}\dots d_{p}
.

The string
X\in\mathcal{A}_{j}
thus starts with the last
i
digits of
w
:
d_{p-i+1}\dots d_{p}
is a prefix of
X
. And it is necessary that
d_{p-i}
be the first
digit to the left of
X
in
wX
, i.e.
d_{p-i}=d_{p}
, and further

d_{p-i-1}=d_{p-1}
, etc… until
d_{1}=d_{i+1}
. So the index
i\geq 1
is
necessarily a period of
w
. We will now prove that
i
is exactly the
j
th
positive period of
w
in increasing order.

Let
s_{i}
be the length
i
suffix of
w
, and
r_{i}
the length
i
prefix of

w
, for the period
i
. Then
ws_{i}=r_{i}w
. We found that
X
starts with
s_{i}
,

X=s_{i}Y
, so
wX=ws_{i}Y=r_{i}wY
. We have
k_{w}(wX)=k_{w}(r_{i}wY)=k_{w}(r_{i}w)+k_{w}(wY)-1=k_{w}(r_{i}w)+k_{w}(vY)
. Thus
j+1=k_{w}(wX)=k_{w}(r_{i}w)+k_{w}(vY)
. We have defined
i

as giving the position in
wX
of the last occurrence of
w
, and the
s_{i}
in the
decomposition
X=s_{i}Y
is the suffix of this last occurrence of
w
. So no
occurrence of
w
in
wX
can touch
Y
, which means that
k_{w}(vY)=0
and thus

j+1=k_{w}(r_{i}w)=k_{w}(ws_{i})
. This characterizes
i
as being the
j
th positive
period of
w
, indeed we observe that for all smaller periods
\iota
, their

ws_{\iota}
is a prefix of
ws_{i}
.
Let us thus now denote our
i
also as
i_{j}
.

We have also obtained in passing that
X=s_{i}Y
with
Y\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
.
Conversely for such an
X
we have
k_{w}(wX)=k_{w}(ws_{i}Y)=k_{w}(r_{i}wY)=k_{w}(r_{i}w)+k_{w}(vY)=k_{w}(r_{i}w)=k_{w}(ws_{i})=j+1
. We can conclude that
\mathcal{A}_{j}=s_{i_{j}}\mathcal{A}_{0}

hence its associated generating series is
t^{i_{j}}
times the generating
series associated with
\mathcal{A}_{0}
. But the latter is in our notation

t^{1-p}Z_{w}(v,0)
. Hence, using the auto-correlation polynomial
A_{w}
, we
reach the key final formula:

(21)

Z_{w}(0)=A_{w}t^{1-p}Z_{w}(v,0)\;.

Combining with Lemma
8
, we obtain the
Guibas-Odlyzko formula (
5
):

(22)

\displaystyle Z_{w}(0)

\displaystyle=\frac{A_{w}}{(1-bt)A_{w}+t^{p}}\;,

and

(23)

\displaystyle Z_{w}(v,0)

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{p-1}}{(1-bt)A_{w}+t^{p}}\;,

(24)

\displaystyle t^{2-p}Z_{w}(v,0,u)

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-bt)(A_{w}-1)+t^{p}}{(1-bt)A_{w}+t^{p}}\;.

Hence Lemma
10
becomes:

(25)

k\geq 1\implies Z_{w}(k)=t^{p}\frac{\bigl((1-bt)(A_{w}-1)+t^{p}\bigr)^{k-1}}{\bigl((1-bt)A_{w}+t^{p}\bigr)^{k+1}}\;.

The values
M_{w}(k)=b^{p}
are obvious here. And we obtain
M_{w}(0)=b^{p}A_{w}(b^{-1})=b^{p}+b^{p-i_{1}}+\dots
in terms of the positive periods
0<i_{1}<i_{2}<\dots
, if any.

3.
Measures and integrals

For each string
X
, we define the
b
-imal number
x(X)\in[0,1)
simply by
putting
X
immediately to the right of the
b
-imal separator, i.e.
x(X)=n(X)/b^{|X|}
. We define the measure
\mu_{k}
as the sum over all

k
-admissible strings of the Dirac masses at points
x(X)
with weights

b^{-|X|}
. In
[
5
, §4]
it is explained that as the discrete
measure has finite total mass, one can use it to integrate any bounded or any
non-negative function (in the latter case possibly obtaining
+\infty
). In
particular, consider integrating the function
g(x)
being defined as
1/x

for
b^{-1}\leq x<1
and zero elsewhere. The computation of
\mu_{k}(g)

involves only those strings
X
starting with a non-zero
b
-ary digit (in
particular the empty string which maps to the real number
0
is not involved)
hence contributions are in one-to-one correspondance with those positive
integers
m
having exactly
k
occurrences of the block of digits
w
. And
the contribution of an integer
m\in[b^{l-1},b^{l})
is
{(m/b^{l})^{-1}}

(value of the function) times
b^{-l}
(weight associated with the Dirac
point-mass). That gives exactly
m^{-1}
hence formula (
2
) for

S_{w}(k)
.

This reasoning works for all
k\geq 0
but now we suppose
k\geq 1
. Recall in
the following that
p=|w|
.

Let us first suppose
k=1
. We obtain from (
2
) the bounds

(26)

\mu_{1}([b^{-1},1))\leq S_{w}(1)\leq b\cdot\mu_{1}([b^{-1},1))\;.

Now,
\mu_{1}([b^{-1},1))
is the total mass (i.e. each string
X
weighs

b^{-|X|}
if kept) of the
1
-admissible strings starting with a non zero
digit. By proposition
11
this is
(b-1)b^{p-1}
. We also have
(rather trivial and sub-optimal) bounds

(27)

b^{p}(1-b^{-1})\leq\int_{b^{-1}}^{1}\frac{b^{p}\mathrm{d}\mskip-1.0mux}{x}\leq b\cdot b^{p}(1-b^{-1})\;.

Hence
|b^{p}\log(b)-S_{w}(1)|\leq(b-1)^{2}b^{p-1}
.
It was not really optimal to replace
1/x
either by
1
or by
b
! So let us
make a finer decomposition along sub-intervals
I_{a}=[a/b,(a+1)/b))
,
1\leq a<b
. We know from Proposition
11
that
\mu_{1}(I_{a})=b^{p-1}
for
each
a\in\mathsf{D}
. Hence we can write

(28)

S_{w}(1)=\sum_{a=1}^{a=b-1}b^{p-1}y_{a},\qquad(a+1)^{-1}b\leq y_{a}\leq a^{-1}b\;.

And we also have regarding
\int b^{p}\mathrm{d}\mskip-1.0mux/x
:

(29)

b^{p}\log(b)=\sum_{a=1}^{a=b-1}b^{p-1}z_{a},\qquad(a+1)^{-1}b\leq z_{a}\leq a^{-1}b\;.

Hence

(30)

\left|S_{w}(1)-b^{p}\log(b)\right|\leq\sum_{a=1}^{a=b-1}b^{p}(\frac{1}{a}-\frac{1}{a+1})=(b-1)b^{p-1}\;.

This proves the case
k=1
for Theorem
1
.

Let
k=2
. The exact same decomposition we employed for
k=1
works too here:
indeed
\mu_{2}(I_{a})=b^{p-1}
from Theorem
3
applied to the
one-digit strings
s=a
,
1\leq a<b
, for which the condition
k>k_{w}^{*}(s)
is
certainly valid as
k_{w}^{*}(s)\leq 1
. So the case
k=2
of Theorem
1
is done.

Let
k>2
. We partition
[b^{-1},1)
into intervals
I(s)=\bigl[n(s)/b^{k-1},(n(s)+1)/b^{k-1}\bigr)
over all strings
s
of length

k-1
and whose first digit is non-zero (
n(s)
is the associated integer).
The value of
\mu_{k}(I(s))
is the total mass of all
k
-admissible strings
having
s
as prefix (for example with
k=4
and whatever
w
,
10

can not contribute any mass to
[0.100,0.101)
despite
x(10)
belonging
to it). By Theorem
3
, as
k
is greater than the length of

s
, this total mass is
b^{p}\cdot b^{-|s|}=b^{p-k+1}
.
We can thus simultaneously
write
S_{w}(k)=\sum_{s}b^{p-k+1}y_{s}
with
(n(s)+1)^{-1}b^{k-1}\leq y_{s}\leq n(s)^{-1}b^{k-1}
and
b^{p}\log(b)=\sum_{s}b^{p}\cdot b^{-k+1}z_{s}
with

(n(s)+1)^{-1}b^{k-1}\leq z_{s}\leq n(s)^{-1}b^{k-1}
, hence

(31)

\left|S_{w}(k)-b^{p}\log(b)\right|\leq\sum_{s}b^{p-k+1}(\frac{b^{k-1}}{n(s)}-\frac{b^{k-1}}{n(s)+1})=b^{p-k+1}\Bigl(\frac{b^{k-1}}{b^{k-2}}-\frac{b^{k-1}}{b^{k-1}}\Bigr)\;,

and the proof of Theorem
1
is complete.

Let us now prove the weak convergence of the
\mu_{k}
’s to
b^{p}
times Lebesgue
measure. It will suffice to prove that for any
0\leq t<u\leq 1
such that
there exists an integer
l
with
b^{l}t
and
b^{l}u
both integers then

\mu_{k}([t,u))=b^{p}(u-t)
for
k
large enough. Indeed this implies
\lim\mu_{k}(I)=b^{p}|I|
for any interval
I\subset[0,1)
, where
|I|
is Lebesgue
measure, as one sees from bounding
\liminf
and
\limsup
via contained (if

|I|>0
) or containing intervals
[t,u)
. See
[
5
, Prop. 26]
for
the
p=1
case. In
[
5
]
the stabilization
\mu_{k}([t,u))=b(u-t)
was proven for
k\geq l
, here we prove that
\mu_{k}([t,u))=b^{p}(u-t)

holds at least for
k\geq l+1
. It is enough to handle
t=n/b^{l}
,
u=(n+1)/b^{l}
,
0\leq n<b^{l}
,
n
integer. Let
s
be the length
l
string
representing the integer
n
with possibly added leading zeros. The

k
-admissible strings
X
mapping to the interval
[t,u)
all share
s
as
common length
l
prefix (we used that
X
has surely length
>l
if it is
to be
k
-admissible, as
k>l
). Theorem
3
implies (see the
sentence following it) that for
k>l
the
total mass of such
k
-admissible strings with common prefix
s
is

b^{p-l}
. This means that
\mu_{k}([t,u))=b^{p}(u-t)
for such
t=n/b^{l}
,
u=(n+1)/b^{l}
,
0\leq n<b^{l}
,
n
integer.

The proof of Theorem
2
is thus complete.

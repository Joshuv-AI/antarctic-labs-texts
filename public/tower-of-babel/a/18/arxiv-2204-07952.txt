Strong convergence of propagation of chaos for McKean-Vlasov SDEs with singular interactions Thanks: This work is partially supported by NNSFC grants of China (Nos. 12131019, 11731009), and the German Research Foundation (DFG) through the Collaborative Research Centre(CRC) 1283/2 2021 - 317210226 “Taming uncertainty and profiting from randomness and low regularity in analysis, stochastics and their applications”.
Zimo Hao, Michael Röckner, Xicheng Zhang

Abstract
. In this work we show the strong convergence of propagation of chaos for the particle approximation of McKean-Vlasov SDEs with singular L p L^{p} -interactions as well as for the moderate interaction particle systems on the level of particle trajectories. One of the main obstacles is to establish the strong well-posedness of the SDEs for particle systems with singular interaction. To this end, we extend the results on strong well-posedness of Krylov and Röckner [ 25 ] to the case of mixed L 𝒑 L^{\boldsymbol{p}} -drifts, where the heat kernel estimates play a crucial role. Moreover, when the interaction kernel is bounded measurable, we also obtain the optimal rate of strong convergence, which is partially based on Jabin and Wang’s entropy method [ 19 ] and Zvonkin’s transformation. Keywords : Propagation of chaos, McKean-Vlasov SDEs, Zvonkin’s transformation, Girsanov’s transformation, Heat kernel estimates, Entropy method.



1.
Introduction

Let
\phi:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
,
F:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

and
\sigma:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\otimes{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be Borel measurable functions.
For a (sub)-probability measure
\mu
over
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, we define

b(t,x,\mu):=F(t,x,(\phi_{t}\circledast\mu)(x)),

where
\phi_{t}(x,y):=\phi(t,x,y)
and

(\phi_{t}\circledast\mu)(x):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\phi_{t}(x,y)\mu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y).

Consider the following interacting system of
N
-particles,

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X^{N,i}_{t}=b{\big(}t,X^{N,i}_{t},\eta_{{\mathbf{X}}^{N}_{t}}{\big)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\sigma{\big(}t,X^{N,i}_{t}{\big)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{i}_{t},\ \ i=1,\cdots,N,

(1.1)

where
\eta_{{\mathbf{X}}^{N}_{t}}
stands for the empirical distribution measure of
N
-particles
{\mathbf{X}}^{N}_{t}:=(X^{N,1}_{t},\cdots,X^{N,N}_{t})
,

\eta_{{\mathbf{X}}^{N}_{t}}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y):=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\delta_{X^{N,j}_{t}}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y),

and
\{W^{i},i\in N\}
is a sequence of independent standard Brownian motions on some stochastic basis
(\Omega,{\mathscr{F}},{\mathbb{P}},({\mathscr{F}}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0})
.
The infinitesimal generator of the above system is given by

{\mathcal{L}}^{N}_{t}\varphi({\mathbf{x}})=\mathrm{tr}\big(a(t,x^{i})\cdot\nabla^{2}_{x^{i}}\varphi({\mathbf{x}})\big)+F\Big(t,x^{i},\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\phi_{t}(x^{i},x^{j})\Big)\cdot\nabla_{x^{i}}\varphi({\mathbf{x}}),

where
{\mathbf{x}}=(x^{1},\cdots,x^{N})\in({\mathbb{R}}^{d})^{N}
and
a=\frac{1}{2}\sigma\sigma^{*}
. Here and below we use Einstein’s convention for summation.

In this paper we are mainly concerned with the weak and strong convergence of the solutions to (
1.1
)
with general
L^{p}
-
singular
interaction
\phi_{t}(x,y)
to the solution of the following distribution-dependent
(or McKean-Vlasov) SDE (abbreviated as DDSDE)
when
N\to\infty
:

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X_{t}=b(t,X_{t},\mu_{X_{t}}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\sigma(t,X_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{1}_{t},

(1.2)

where
\mu_{X_{t}}
denotes the distribution of
X_{t}
. In particular,

\mu:=(\mu_{X_{t}})_{t\geqslant 0}
solves the following nonlinear Fokker-Planck equation in distributional sense:

\partial_{t}\mu=\partial_{i}\partial_{j}(a_{ij}\mu)+\mathord{{\rm div}}(b(\mu)\mu),

Moreover, we are also interested in the so called moderately interacting kernel
\phi_{t}(x,y)=\phi_{\varepsilon_{N}}(x-y)
,
where
\phi_{\varepsilon_{N}}
is a family of mollifiers and
\varepsilon_{N}\to 0
as
N\to\infty
. In this case, the solution to the interacting particle system

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X^{N,i}_{t}=F{\big(}t,X^{N,i}_{t},(\phi_{\varepsilon_{N}}\circledast\eta_{{\mathbf{X}}^{N}_{t}})(X^{N,i}_{t}){\big)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\sigma{\big(}t,X^{N,i}_{t}{\big)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{i}_{t},\ \ i=1,\cdots,N,

(1.3)

is expected to converge to the solution of the following density-dependent SDE (see
[
34
,
23
]
):

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X_{t}=F(t,X_{t},\rho_{X_{t}}(X_{t})){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\sigma(t,X_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{t},

(1.4)

where
\rho_{X_{t}}
stands for the density of
X_{t}
. Here
\rho:=(\rho_{X_{t}})_{t\geqslant 0}
solves the following nonlinear and
local (or Nemytskii-type)
Fokker-Planck equation:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho=\partial_{i}\partial_{j}(a_{ij}\rho)+\mathord{{\rm div}}(F(\rho)\rho).

(1.5)

It should be kept in mind that for
d=1
and
F(\rho)=\rho
, this is Burgers-type equation.

For the motion of a single particle, when
\phi\in L^{q}_{t}(L^{p}_{x})
with
\frac{d}{p}+\frac{2}{q}<1
, Krylov and Röckner
[
25
]

showed the existence and uniqueness of strong solutions to the following SDE by Girsanov’s transformation:

{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X_{t}=\phi_{t}{\big(}X_{t}{\big)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{t}.

Later, Zhang
[
48
]
extended their result to the multiplicative noise case by Zvonkin’s transformation of
[
52
]

(see also
[
50
,
45
]
).
However, for
N
-particle
system
(
1.1
) with
F(t,x,r)=r
and
\phi_{t}(x,y)=\phi_{t}(x-y)
, where
\phi
is as above,
one can not use these well-known results for
L^{q}_{t}(L^{p}_{x})
drifts to derive the well-posedness by considering (
1.1
) as an SDE in
{\mathbb{R}}^{Nd}
.
For instance, when
N=3
, consider the following SDE in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3d}
:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X^{1}_{t}={\Big[}\phi{\big(}X^{1}_{t},X^{2}_{t}{\big)}+\phi{\big(}X^{1}_{t},X^{3}_{t}{\big)}{\Big]}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{1}_{t},\\
&{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X^{2}_{t}={\Big[}\phi{\big(}X^{2}_{t},X^{1}_{t}{\big)}+\phi{\big(}X^{2}_{t},X^{3}_{t}{\big)}{\Big]}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{2}_{t},\\
&{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X^{3}_{t}={\Big[}\phi{\big(}X^{3}_{t},X^{1}_{t}{\big)}+\phi{\big(}X^{3}_{t},X^{2}_{t}{\big)}{\Big]}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{3}_{t},\end{aligned}\right.

(1.6)

where
|\phi(x,y)|\leqslant h(x-y)
and
h\in L^{p}
with
p>d
. For
i=1,2,3
, let
\phi_{i}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}):=\sum_{j\not=i}\phi(x_{i},x_{j})
.
As a function of
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3d}
, one only has

\displaystyle\phi_{i}\in L^{\infty}_{x^{*}_{i}}L^{p}_{x_{i}},\quad i=1,2,3,

(1.7)

where
x^{*}_{i}
stands for the remaining variables except for
x_{i}
. It does not satisfy the conditions in
[
25
]
.
Note that in the same work
[
25
]
, Krylov and Röckner also showed the strong well-posedness for a class of special stochastic particle system
with singular gradient interaction
\phi=\nabla V
, where
V
is continuously differentiable on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\backslash\{0\}
and satisfies some other conditions (see Section 9 in
[
25
]
). Moreover, the strong well-posedness for particle system with Biot-Savart law interaction kernel

\phi(x)=(-x_{2},x_{1})/|x|^{2}
was established in
[
35
]
and
[
13
]
,
which is related to the random point vortex approximation for two dimensional Navier-Stokes equations.
In the above well-known works, the key point of establishing the strong well-posedness is to prove that the process
X^{i}_{t}-X^{j}_{t}
for
i\not=j

does not touch the singular point
0
, i.e. the state space is
{\mathbb{R}}^{Nd}

“without diagonals”.
However, the strong well-posedness for particle systems (
1.1
) with general
L^{p}
-interaction kernels on all of
{\mathbb{R}}^{Nd}
has still been open.

Therefore, our first task is to extend
[
25
,
48
]
to the case of mixed
L^{p}
-spaces. We mention here that although Ling and Xie
[
29
]
have already
considered singular SDEs in mixed
L^{p}
-spaces, their result cannot be applied to equation (
1.6
)
due to the new feature that we need to consider the order of the integral in
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}
as well as the different integrability indices. Notice
that each
\phi_{i}
belongs to a different mixed
L^{p}
-space.
For DDSDE (
1.2
), in
[
37
]
, the last two authors of the present paper
have already shown the weak and strong well-posedness
(see also
[
28
]
and
[
33
]
for bounded measurable interaction kernel).
Furthermore, weak solutions to the distribution density-dependent SDE (
1.4
), were constructed in
[
2
]
, first solving the corresponding Fokker-Planck-Kolmogorov equation
and using the superposition principle, and strong solutions were constructed in
[
16
]
by directly using Euler’s scheme. Recently, Wang
[
44
]
studied the weak and strong well-posedness for
more general distribution density-dependent SDEs with singular coefficients by a fixed point argument, but not for mixed
L^{\boldsymbol{p}}
-drifts. Nowadays, there is a vast literature about McKean-Vlasov or mean-field SDEs.
We do not intend to list all the papers here. The interesting reader is referred to the references in the already mentioned papers.

1.1.
Propagation of chaos

In this subsection we recall some notions and well-known results about the propagation of chaos.

Kac’s chaos:
Let
E
be a Polish space and
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}(E)
a probability measure on
E
. Let
(\mu^{N})_{N\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence of symmetric probability measures on
the respective product space
E^{N}
, where symmetric means that for any permutation
(x_{i_{1}},\cdots,x_{i_{N}})
of
(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N})
,

\mu^{N}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x_{i_{1}},\cdots,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}x_{i_{N}})=\mu^{N}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x_{1},\cdots,x_{N}).

In particular,
\mu^{N}
has a common
1
-marginal distribution.
One says that
(\mu^{N})_{N\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is
\mu
-chaotic
if for any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
(see
[
24
]
),

\displaystyle\mu^{N,k}\mbox{ weakly converges to $\mu^{\otimes k}$ as $k\leqslant N\to\infty$},

(1.8)

where
\mu^{N,k}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x_{1},\cdots,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}x_{k})=\mu^{N}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x_{1},\cdots,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}x_{k},E,\cdots,E)
is the
k
-fold marginal distribution of
\mu^{N}
.
It is well known that (
1.8
) holds if and only if (
1.8
) holds for only
k=2
(see
[
42
, (i) of Proposition 2.2]
). In the language of random variables, Kac’s chaos can be restated as follows:
Let
{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{N}}:=(\xi^{N,1},\cdots,\xi^{N,N})
be a family of
E
-valued random variables. If the law of
{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{N}}
is symmetric and
\mu
-chaotic, one says that

{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{N}}
is
\mu
-chaotic. It is also equivalent to (see
[
42
, (ii) of Proposition 2.2]
)

the empirical measure
\eta_{{\boldsymbol{\xi}}^{N}}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y):=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\delta_{\xi^{N,j}}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y)\in{\mathcal{P}}(E)
converges to
\mu
in law.

(1.9)

Note that
{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{N}}
can be regarded as
N
-random particles in state space
E
. From this viewpoint, Kac’s chaos means that if one observes the distribution of any
k
-particles,
then they become statistically independent as
N
goes to infinity. Indeed, (
1.9
) is a law of large numbers, i.e., for any
\varphi\in C_{b}(E)
,

\eta_{{\boldsymbol{\xi}}^{N}}(\varphi):=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\varphi(\xi^{N,j})\to\mu(\varphi):=\int_{E}\varphi(x)\mu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x),\ \ \mbox{ in law}.

In Hauray and Mischler’s work
[
17
]
, various quantitative and qualitative estimates related to the chaos are obtained for different notions such as
Kac’s chaos, entropy chaos and Fisher information chaos. More references about Kac’s chaos can be also found in
[
17
]
.

Propagation of chaos:
If one considers Kac’s chaos as a static version of chaos, then propagation of chaos is usually understood as a dynamical version of Kac’s chaos.
More precisely, let
({\boldsymbol{\xi}^{N}_{t}})_{t\geqslant 0}:=(\xi^{N,1}_{t},\cdots,\xi^{N,N}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
be a family of
E^{N}
-valued continuous stochastic processes,
which can be thought of as the evolution of
N
-particles. Let
(\xi_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
be a limit
E
-valued continuous stochastic process defined on the same probability space.
Let
\mu^{N}_{t}
be the law of
{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{N}_{t}}
in
E^{N}
and
\mu_{t}
be the law of
\xi_{t}
in
E
. Suppose that
\mu^{N}_{0}
is
\mu_{0}
-chaotic at time
0
. One says that

propagation of chaos
holds if for any time
t>0
,
\mu^{N}_{t}
is
\mu_{t}
-chaotic. Usually, as the evolution of particle distributions, the probability measures

\mu^{N}_{t}
and
\mu_{t}
satisfy some Fokker-Planck equation in the weak sense. Therefore, it can be studied by purely PDE’s method.
However, as stochastic processes, one would like to have the following stronger convergence in a probabilistic sense: for each
t>0
,

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}|\xi^{N,1}_{t}-\xi_{t}|=0,

or in the functional path sense

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\left(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|\xi^{N,1}_{s}-\xi_{s}|\right)=0.

(1.10)

In fact, when
F
,
\phi
and
\sigma
are globally Lipschitz continuous in
x,r
and uniformly in
t
, McKean
[
30
]
firstly
established the following result for (
1.1
) and (
1.2
):
for any
T>0
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left(\sup_{s\in[0,T]}|X^{N,1}_{s}-X_{s}|^{2}\right)\leqslant\frac{C(b,\sigma,T)}{N},

(1.11)

where the constant
C(b,\sigma,T)>0
can be estimated explicitly. We note that the power of convergence rate
N
is sharp.
The above estimate is also reproven by Sznitman
[
42
]
by more direct synchronous coupling methods.
Since then, propagation of chaos has undergone an enormous development in mathematical kinetic theory (see
[
15
,
32
,
17
,
20
]
).
Moreover, propagation of chaos also appears in many other disciplines including data science
[
11
]
, mean-field games
[
6
,
7
]

and the training of neural networks
[
38
]
, etc.
In a recent paper
[
8
]
, Chaintron and Diez reviews various models, methods as well as applications for propagation of chaos.

Obviously, Lipschitz assumptions on
F,\phi
and
\sigma
are too strong in practice. In fact,
most of the interesting physical models have bounded measurable or even singular interaction kernels.
For examples, the rank-based interaction diffusion studied in
[
40
,
26
]
has a discontinuous interaction kernel (see (
1.20
) below), and
the Biot-Savart law appearing in the vortex description of 2d imcompressible Navier-Stokes equations has a singular kernel like
x^{\perp}/|x|^{2}
.
For this type of singular kernels, Osada
[
36
]
firstly showed the propagation of chaos for the point vortices associated with the 2d Navier-Stokes equation with large viscosity.
Recently, in
[
14
]
, Fournier, Hauray, and Mischler dropped the assumption of large viscosity by the classical martingale method. More recently,
Jabin and Wang
[
20
]
obtained a first quantitative convergence rate about the relative entropy between the law of particle system and the tensorized limit law, where the key point is an estimate for the entropy and a large deviation type exponential functional.
In fact, the results in
[
20
]
can be applied to a large class of singular kernels
K
in
W^{-1,\infty}
with
K(x)=-K(-x)
,
as well as the bounded measurable interaction kernel (see Section
5.2
).
We note that the proof in
[
20
]
strongly depends on the symmetry of the kernel
K(x)
, not valid for general
L^{p}
-singular kernel.

For general
L^{p}
-singular interaction kernels, in
[
43
]
, Toma
\rm\check{s}
ević uses the partial Girsanov transform as in
[
21
]
to derive the
propagation of chaos under the extra assumption that the set of discontinuous points of the interaction kernel has Lebesgue measure zero. In
[
18
]
,
Hoeksema, Holding, Maurelli and Tse showed a large deviation result for a particle system with
L^{p}
-singular interaction kernels.
As a byproduct, they also obtained a result of propagation of chaos (see also
[
26
]
). However, in
[
43
]
and
[
18
]
, both of them assume
the initial distributions of the particle system are i.i.d, that is, the initial distributions are not really
chaotic
. This assumption is crucial for them to construct a weak solution
for the interaction particle system by Girsanov’s transform.
In the present paper we overcome this difficulty by showing the existence of strong solutions for the particle system (see Lemma
5.2
below), and then
obtain the strong convergence as in (
1.10
) for singular interaction kernels and the quantitative convergence (
1.11
) for bounded measurable kernels
by Zvonkin’s transformation. Note that Bao and Huang
[
1
]
have already used the Zvonkin transformation to obtain propagation of chaos for Hölder interaction kernels
with non-optimal rate
N^{-1/4}
.

1.2.
Main results

Before stating our main assumptions, we introduce the following index sets:

\displaystyle{\mathscr{I}}^{o}:=\big\{(q,{\boldsymbol{p}})\in(2,\infty)^{1+d}:|\tfrac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}}|+\tfrac{2}{q}<1\big\}

(1.12)

and

{\mathscr{X}}:=\big\{{\mathbf{x}}=(x_{i_{1}},\cdots,x_{i_{d}}):\mbox{any permutation of $(x_{1},\cdots,x_{d})$}\big\}.

Now we make the following main assumptions:

(H
σ
)

There are
\kappa_{0}\geqslant 1
and
\gamma_{0}\in(0,1]
such that for all
t\geqslant 0
and
x,x^{\prime},\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\kappa_{0}^{-1}|\xi|\leqslant|\sigma(t,x)\xi|\leqslant\kappa_{0}|\xi|,\ \|\sigma(t,x)-\sigma(t,x^{\prime})\|_{HS}\leqslant\kappa_{0}|x-x^{\prime}|^{\gamma_{0}},

(1.13)

where
\|\cdot\|_{HS}
is the usual Hilbert-Schmidt norm of a matrix.
Moreover, for some
(q_{0},{\boldsymbol{p}}_{0})\in{\mathscr{I}}^{o}
and
{\mathbf{x}}_{0}\in{\mathscr{X}}
and any
T>0
,

\displaystyle\|\nabla\sigma\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{0}}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{0}}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{0}})}\leqslant\kappa_{0},

(1.14)

where the localized space
\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}}_{{\mathbf{x}}}
is defined in Subsection
2.1
below.

(H
b
)

Suppose that
\phi_{t}(x,x)=0
and for some measurable
h:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
and
\kappa_{1}>0
,

\displaystyle|F(t,x,r)|\leqslant h(t,x)+\kappa_{1}|r|,\ \ |F(t,x,r)-F(t,x,r^{\prime})|\leqslant\kappa_{1}|r-r^{\prime}|,

(1.15)

and for some
(q,{\boldsymbol{p}})\in{\mathscr{I}}^{o}
and
{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathscr{X}}
and for any
T>0
,

\displaystyle|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|h|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}}_{{\mathbf{x}}})}+\left[\int^{T}_{0}\sup_{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left(|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\phi_{t}(\cdot,y)|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|^{q}_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}}_{{\mathbf{x}}}}+|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\phi_{t}(y,\cdot)|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|^{q}_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}}_{{\mathbf{x}}}}\right){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}\leqslant\kappa_{1}.

(1.16)

Example 1.
We provide two examples to illustrate condition (
1.16
).

(i)

Let
d\geqslant 2
and
\phi_{t}(x,y)=c_{t}(x,y)/|x-y|^{\alpha}
, where
c_{t}(x,y)
is bounded measurable and
\alpha\in(0,1)
.
It is easy to see that (
1.16
) holds for
q
close to
\infty
and
p\in(d,\frac{d}{\alpha})
with
\frac{d}{p}+\frac{2}{q}<1
.

(ii)

Let
d\geqslant 1
and
\phi_{t}(x,y)=c_{t}(x,y)/\Pi_{i=1}^{d}|x_{i}-y_{i}|^{\alpha_{i}}
, where
\alpha_{i}\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
satisfies
\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{d}<1

and
c_{t}(x,y)
is bounded measurable. Note that one can choose
q
close to
\infty
and
p_{i}>2
close to
1/\alpha_{i}

so that
|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}}|+\frac{2}{q}<1
and (
1.16
) holds. In this case, the kernel is allowed to have singularities along each axis.

Throughout this paper we use
\Theta
to denote the set of parameters that a constant may depend on.
\Theta
may have different parameters in different occasions, which should be clear from the context, e.g.,

\Theta=(m,d,\gamma_{0},\kappa_{0},\kappa_{1},q_{0},{\boldsymbol{p}}_{0},q,{\boldsymbol{p}},\cdots).

The aim of this paper is to show the following strong convergence of the particle approximation.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
T>0
. Under
(H
σ
)
and
(H
b
)
, for any initial values
{\mathbf{X}}^{N}_{0}
and
X_{0}
,
there are unique strong solutions
{\mathbf{X}}^{N}_{t}
and
X_{t}
to particle system (
1.1
) and DDSDE (
1.2
), respectively.
Moreover, letting
\mu^{N}_{0}
be the law of
{\mathbf{X}}^{N}_{0}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{dN}
and
\mu_{0}
the law of
X_{0}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
we have the following strong convergence results:

(i)

(Singular kernel)
Suppose that
\mu^{N}_{0}
is symmetric and
\mu_{0}
-chaotic, and

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}|X^{N,1}_{0}-X_{0}|^{2}=0.

Then for any
\gamma\in(0,1)

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X^{N,1}_{t}-X_{t}|^{2\gamma}\right)=0.

(1.17)

(ii)

(Bounded kernel)
If

and
\phi
in
(H

)
are bounded measurable and

\displaystyle\kappa_{2}:=\sup_{N}{\mathcal{H}}{\big(}\mu^{N}_{0}|\mu^{\otimes N}_{0}{\big)}<\infty,

(1.18)

where
\mu^{\otimes N}_{0}\in{\mathcal{P}}(({\mathbb{R}}^{d})^{N})
is the
N

\mu_{0}

and
{\mathcal{H}}
stands
for the relative entropy (see (

then for any
\delta>2
and
\gamma\in(0,1)
, there are constants

,
i=1,2
independent of
\phi

Remark 1.2

The Euler approximation for particle system (

Example 2.

Example 3.
Consider the following special case:

1.3.
Structure of the paper

In Section 2 we first introduce the localized mixed
L^{\boldsymbol{p}}
-spaces and its basic properties used in this paper. Then we study second order parabolic PDEs with mixed
L^{\boldsymbol{p}}
-drifts
and show the unique existence of strong solutions. Since each component of the drift may be in a different mixed
L^{\boldsymbol{p}}
-space,
the new point here is that the second order derivative of the solution shall stay in a direct sum space (see Theorem
2.9
).

In Section 3, we show the weak and strong well-posedness for stochastic differential equations with mixed
L^{\boldsymbol{p}}
-drifts.
As usual, we need to prove a priori Krylov estimates based on the PDE estimates obtained in Section 2, and then show that we can perform the Zvonkin transformation.
Since Zvonkin’s transformation is a
C^{1}
-diffeomorphism and reduces the original singular SDE to an equivalent regular SDE, one can use well-known
results such as the heat kernel estimates to derive some apriori estimates for the original SDE, and then show our main results.
We emphasize that the mixed
L^{\boldsymbol{p}}
-space is not invariant under
C^{1}
-diffeomorphism transformation. Thus one can not obtain the Krylov estimate
directly through the transformed equation. Instead, we use the heat kernel estimates to show the Krylov estimate for the indices
(q,{\boldsymbol{p}})\in{\mathscr{I}}_{2}
.

In Section 4, by Picard’s iteration, we show the weak and strong well-posedness for distribution density-distribution dependent SDEs with mixed
L^{\boldsymbol{p}}
-drifts,
where we use the entropy formula, Pinsker’s inequality and the Fokker-Planck equation to show that the Picard iteration of the density is a Cauchy sequence
in
L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}
.

In Section 5, by the classical martingale method we show that the propagation of chaos for systems as in (
1.1
) with singular kernels
holds in the weak sense, where the key point is to use the partial Girsanov transform used in
[
21
,
43
]
to
derive some uniform estimate for the exponential functional. In particular, the strong solution is used to treat the chaos of the initial distributions.
Moreover, we also provide a detailed proof for Jabin and Wang’s quantitative result
[
20
]
for bounded interaction kernels. This is not new and only for the readers’ convenience.

In Sections 6 and 7, we give the proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
, and
show how to use Zvonkin’s transformation again to derive the strong convergence from the weak convergence obtained in Section 5, where the key point is Lemma
6.1
.

We conclude this introduction by introducing the following convention:
Throughout this paper, we use
C
with or without subscripts to denote constants, whose values
may change from line to line. We also use
:=
to indicate a definition and
a^{+}:=0\vee a
. By
A\lesssim_{C}B
and
A\asymp_{C}B

or simply
A\lesssim B
and
A\asymp B
, we mean that for some constant
C\geqslant 1
,

A\leqslant CB,\ \ C^{-1}B\leqslant A\leqslant CB.

2.1.
Mixed
L^{p}
-spaces

In this section we recall the definition of localized mixed
L^{\boldsymbol{p}}
-spaces, which was originally introduced in
[
3
]
.
As we have seen in the introduction, these are very suitable for singular interacting particle system (see also
[
18
]
).
Let
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
. For a multi-index
{\boldsymbol{p}}=(p_{1},\cdots,p_{d})\in(0,\infty]^{d}

and any permutation
{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathscr{X}}
, the mixed
{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}}
-space is defined by

\displaystyle\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}}}:=\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\cdots\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x_{1},\cdots,x_{d})|^{p_{d}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}x_{i_{d}}\right)^{\frac{p_{d-1}}{p_{d}}}\cdots{\mathord{{\rm d}}}x_{i_{2}}\Bigg)^{\frac{p_{1}}{p_{2}}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}x_{i_{1}}\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{p_{1}}}.

(2.1)

When
{\boldsymbol{p}}=(p,\cdots,p)\in(0,\infty]^{d}
, the mixed

{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}}
-space is the usual
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-space, simply denoted by
{\mathbb{L}}^{p}
. Note that for general
{\mathbf{x}}\not={\mathbf{x}}^{\prime}
and
{\boldsymbol{p}}\not={\boldsymbol{p}}^{\prime}
,

{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}^{\prime}}_{\mathbf{x}}\not={\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}}_{\mathbf{x}}\not={\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}}_{{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime}}.

For multi-indices
{\boldsymbol{p}},{\boldsymbol{q}}\in(0,\infty]^{d}
, we shall use the following notations:

\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}}:=\Big(\frac{1}{p_{1}},\cdots,\frac{1}{p_{d}}\Big),\quad{\boldsymbol{p}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{q}}:=\sum_{i=1}^{d}p_{i}q_{i},\ \ \Big|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}}\Big|=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1}{p_{i}},

and

{\boldsymbol{p}}>{\boldsymbol{q}}\ \ (\hbox{\rm resp. }{\boldsymbol{p}}\geqslant{\boldsymbol{q}};\ {\boldsymbol{p}}={\boldsymbol{q}})\Longleftrightarrow p_{i}>q_{i}\ (\hbox{\rm resp. }p_{i}\geqslant q_{i};\ p_{i}=q_{i})\ \hbox{ for all }i=1,\cdots,d.

Moreover, we use bold numbers to denote constant vectors in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, for example,

\boldsymbol{1}=(1,\cdots,1),\ \ \boldsymbol{2}=(2,\cdots,2).

For multi-indices
{\boldsymbol{p}},{\boldsymbol{q}},{\boldsymbol{r}}\in(0,\infty]^{d}
with
\tfrac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}}+\tfrac{1}{{\boldsymbol{r}}}=\tfrac{1}{{\boldsymbol{q}}}
, the following Hölder inequality holds

\displaystyle\|fg\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{q}}_{\mathbf{x}}}\leqslant\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}}}\|g\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{r}}_{\mathbf{x}}}.

(2.2)

For any multi-indices
{\boldsymbol{p}},{\boldsymbol{q}},{\boldsymbol{r}}\in[1,\infty]^{d}
with
\tfrac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}}+\tfrac{1}{{\boldsymbol{r}}}=\boldsymbol{1}+\tfrac{1}{{\boldsymbol{q}}}
, the following Young inequality holds

\displaystyle\|f*g\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{q}}_{\mathbf{x}}}\leqslant\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}}}\|g\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{r}}_{\mathbf{x}}}.

(2.3)

For any
r>0
, let
B_{z}^{r}
be the ball in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with radius
r
and center
z
.
Let
\chi:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to[0,1]
be a smooth cutoff function with
\chi|_{B_{1}}=1

and
\chi|_{B_{2}^{c}}=0
. For fixed
r>0
, we set

\displaystyle\chi^{r}_{z}(x):=\chi((x-z)/r),\ \ x,z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

(2.4)

For
{\boldsymbol{p}}\in[1,\infty]^{d}
, we introduce the following localized
L^{\boldsymbol{p}}
-space (see
[
51
]
):

\displaystyle\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}}:=\Big\{f\in L^{1}_{\text{\rm loc}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}}}:=\sup_{z}\|\chi^{r}_{z}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}}}<\infty\Big\},

and for a finite time interval
{\rm I}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
and
q\in[1,\infty]
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{\rm I}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}}):=\Big\{f\in L^{1}_{\text{\rm loc}}({\rm I}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}),|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{\rm I}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}})}:=\sup_{z}\|\chi^{r}_{z}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{\rm I}({\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}})}<\infty\Big\},

(2.5)

where for a Banach space
{\mathbb{B}}
we set

{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{{\rm I}}({\mathbb{B}}):=L^{q}({\rm I};{\mathbb{B}}).

By a finitely covering technique, it is easy to see that the definitions of
\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}}
and
\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{\rm I}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}})
do not depend on the choice of

r
(see
[
51
]
), and for any
1\leqslant q_{2}\leqslant q_{1}\leqslant\infty
and
1\leqslant{\boldsymbol{p}}_{2}\leqslant{\boldsymbol{p}}_{1}\leqslant\infty
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{1}}_{\mathbf{x}}\subset\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{2}}_{\mathbf{x}},\ \ \widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{1}}_{\rm I}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{1}}_{\mathbf{x}})\subset\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{2}}_{\rm I}(\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{2}}_{\mathbf{x}}).

(2.6)

This property is the main advantage of using localized spaces.
Since the supremum
z
in the definition of
\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{\rm I}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}})
is taken outside the time integral, we obviously have

{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{\rm I}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}})\subset\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{\rm I}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}}).

Moreover, for
\alpha\geqslant 0
, let
{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}
be the usual Hölder space with norm:

\|f\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}}:=\sum_{j=0}^{[\alpha]}\|\nabla^{j}f\|_{\infty}+\sup_{x\not=y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{|\nabla^{[\alpha]}f(x)-\nabla^{[\alpha]}f(y)|}{|x-y|^{\alpha-[\alpha]}},

where
\nabla^{j}
stands for the
j
-order gradient and
[\alpha]
stands for the integer part of
\alpha
.
For simplicity we write

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}}):=\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{[0,T]}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}}),~~{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{T}:={\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{[0,T]}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}),\ {\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}({\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}):={\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{[0,T]}({\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}).

Example.

For
i=1,\cdots,d
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
, let
f_{i}(x)=b(x)|x_{i}|^{-\alpha}
, where
b(x)
is a bounded measurable function. It is easy to see that

f_{i}\in\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{i}}
, where
{\mathbf{x}}_{i}=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},\cdots,x_{d},x_{i})
and
{\boldsymbol{p}}=(\infty,\cdots,\infty,p)
with
p\in(1,\frac{1}{\alpha})
.
From this example, one sees that for a
C^{1}
-diffeomorphism
\Phi
from
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
say
\Phi(x)=(x_{i},x_{1},\cdots,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},\cdots,x_{d})
, it may happen that

f_{i}\circ\Phi\notin\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{i}}.

Throughout this paper, we shall use the same notation
\varGamma_{\varepsilon}
to denote mollifiers in various dimensions
N
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\varGamma_{\varepsilon}(x)=\varepsilon^{-N}\varGamma(x/\varepsilon),\ \varepsilon\in(0,1),

(2.7)

where
\varGamma
is a nonnegative smooth density function in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
with compact support in the unit ball. For a function
f\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
,
the mollifying approximation of

is defined by

f_{\varepsilon}(x):=f*\varGamma_{\varepsilon}(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}f(x-y)\varGamma_{\varepsilon}(y){\mathord{{\rm d}}}y.

The dimension
N
takes different values in different occasions, which should be clear from the respective context.

The following lemma is obvious by the definitions.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
f\in\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}}

C=C({\boldsymbol{p}})>0

\displaystyle|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f_{\varepsilon}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}}}\leqslant C|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}}},

(2.8)

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|(f_{\varepsilon}-f)\chi^{R}_{0}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}}}=0.

(2.9)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}f(x):=\sup_{r\in(0,1)}\frac{1}{|B^{r}_{0}|}\int_{B^{r}_{0}}f(x+y){\mathord{{\rm d}}}y.

(q,{\boldsymbol{p}})\in{\mathscr{I}}_{2}

(q^{\prime},{\boldsymbol{p}}^{\prime})\geqslant(q,{\boldsymbol{p}})
with

|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}}|+\frac{2}{q}<|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}^{\prime}}|+\frac{2}{q^{\prime}}+1
, there is a constant
C=C(T,d,q,{\boldsymbol{p}},q^{\prime},{\boldsymbol{p}}^{\prime})>0
such that for all
\lambda\geqslant 0
,

\displaystyle(1\vee\lambda)^{\frac{1}{2}(1+|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}^{\prime}}|+\frac{2}{q^{\prime}}-|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}}|-\frac{2}{q})}\|\nabla u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q^{\prime}}_{T}({\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}^{\prime}}_{\mathbf{x}})}\leqslant C\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}({\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}})}.

(2.16)

(iii)

For any
T>0
,
(q,{\boldsymbol{p}})\in{\mathscr{I}}_{1}
and
\lambda\geqslant 0
, there is a constant
C=C(\lambda,T,d,q,{\boldsymbol{p}})>0
such that for all
0\leqslant t_{0}<t_{1}\leqslant T
,

\displaystyle\|u(t_{1})-u(t_{0})\|_{\infty}\leqslant C(t_{1}-t_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}({\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}})}.

(2.17)

Proof.

(i) For
\beta\in[0,2-|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}}|-\frac{2}{q})
, by (
2.12
) and Hölder’s inequality in the time variable, we have

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\beta}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int^{t}_{0}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda(t-s)}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}(\beta+|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}}|)}\|f(s)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\int^{t}_{0}\big(\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda s}s^{-\frac{1}{2}(\beta+|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}}|)}\big)^{\frac{q}{q-1}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{1-\frac{1}{q}}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}({\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1\vee\lambda)^{-\frac{1}{2}(2-\beta-|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}}|-\frac{2}{q})}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}({\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}})}.

(ii) For
(q^{\prime},{\boldsymbol{p}}^{\prime})\geqslant(q,{\boldsymbol{p}})
with
|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}}|+\frac{2}{q}<|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}^{\prime}}|+\frac{2}{q^{\prime}}+1
,
by (
2.13
) we have

\|\nabla u(t)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}^{\prime}}_{\mathbf{x}}}\lesssim\int^{t}_{0}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda(t-s)}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}(1+|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}}|-|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}^{\prime}}|)}\|f(s)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

Let
r\geqslant 1
be defined by
\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}+1-\frac{1}{q}
. By Young’s inequality we further have

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q^{\prime}}_{T}({\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}^{\prime}}_{\mathbf{x}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int^{T}_{0}\mathrm{e}^{-r\lambda s}s^{-\frac{r}{2}(1+|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}}|-|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}^{\prime}}|)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{1/r}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}({\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1\wedge\lambda)^{\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{2}(1+|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}}|-|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}^{\prime}}|)}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}({\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}})}.

(iii)
For
0\leqslant t_{0}<t_{1}\leqslant T
, by definition we have

\displaystyle u(t_{1})-u(t_{0})

\displaystyle=\int^{t_{0}}_{0}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda(t_{1}-s)}(P_{t_{1}-s}-P_{t_{0}-s})f(s,x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle\quad+(\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda(t_{1}-t_{0})}-1)\int^{t_{0}}_{0}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda(t_{0}-s)}P_{t_{0}-s}f(s,x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle\quad+\int^{t_{1}}_{t_{0}}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda(t_{1}-s)}P_{t_{1}-s}f(s,x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.

For
I_{1}
, noting that

\|P_{t}f-f\|_{\infty}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\int^{t}_{0}\|\Delta P_{s}f\|_{\infty}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\lesssim\left(\int^{t}_{0}s^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)\|\nabla f\|_{\infty}\lesssim t^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla f\|_{\infty},

by (
2.12
) and Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|I_{1}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\lesssim(t_{1}-t_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}\int^{t_{0}}_{0}\|\nabla P_{t_{0}-s}f(s)\|_{\infty}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle\lesssim(t_{1}-t_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}\int^{t_{0}}_{0}(t_{0}-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}(1+|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}}|)}\|f(s)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle\lesssim(t_{1}-t_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}t_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{2}{q}-|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}}|)}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}({\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}})},

and because
1-\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda(t_{1}-t_{0})}\leqslant\lambda(t_{1}-t_{0})
,

\|I_{2}\|_{\infty}\lesssim\lambda(t_{1}-t_{0})\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}({\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}})}.

For
I_{3}
, as above, by (
2.12
) and Hölder’s inequality, we have

\|I_{3}\|_{\infty}\lesssim\left(\int^{t_{1}-t_{0}}_{0}\big(\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda s}s^{-\frac{1}{2}|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}}|}\big)^{\frac{q}{q-1}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{1-\frac{1}{q}}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}({\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}})}\leqslant(t_{1}-t_{0})^{1-\frac{1}{2}(\frac{2}{q}+|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}}|)}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}({\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}})}.

Combining the above estimates and because
\frac{2}{q}+|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}}|<1
, we obtain (
2.17
).
∎

Now we shall study the following second order parabolic PDE in

where

(2.19)

\displaystyle(1\vee\lambda)^{\frac{1}{2}(1+|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}^{\prime}}|+\frac{2}{q^{\prime}}-|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}}|-\frac{2}{q})}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\nabla u|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q^{\prime}}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}^{\prime}}_{\mathbf{x}})}\leqslant C|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}})},

(2.21)

Proof.

Remark 2.8

(2.36)

Proof.

Again we only show the a priori estimate (

Let
u=u_{0}+u_{1}+\cdots+u_{d}
, where
u_{0}
solves the following PDE:

\partial_{t}u_{0}=\mathrm{tr}(a\cdot\nabla^{2}u_{0})-\lambda u_{0}+f,\quad u_{0}(0)=0,

and for each

,

solves

\partial_{t}u_{i}=\mathrm{tr}(a\cdot\nabla^{2}u_{i})+b_{i}\cdot\partial_{i}u-\lambda u_{i},\quad u_{i}(0)=0.

Let
\lambda\geqslant 1
and
\beta\in[0,\vartheta)
with
\vartheta
being defined by (
2.34
). By Theorem
2.7
with
b=0
, we have

\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}(1-|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{0}}|-\frac{2}{q_{0}}-\beta)}\|u_{0}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}({\mathcal{C}}^{1+\beta})}+|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\nabla^{2}u_{0}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{0}}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{0}}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{0}})}\lesssim|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{0}}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{0}}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{0}})},

and

\|u_{0}(t_{1})-u_{0}(t_{0})\|_{\infty}\leqslant C(\lambda)(t_{1}-t_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{0}}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{0}}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{0}})},

and for each
i=1,\cdots,d
,

\displaystyle\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}(1-|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}}|-\frac{2}{q_{i}}-\beta)}\|u_{i}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}({\mathcal{C}}^{1+\beta})}+|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\nabla^{2}u_{i}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{i}}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{i}})}\lesssim|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|b_{i}\cdot\partial_{i}u|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{i}}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{i}})}\lesssim|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|b_{i}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{i}}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{i}})}\|\partial_{i}u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}},

and

\|u_{i}(t_{1})-u_{i}(t_{0})\|_{\infty}\leqslant C(\lambda)(t_{1}-t_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|b_{i}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{i}}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{i}})}\|\partial_{i}u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}}.

Summing up the above inequalities for
i
from
0
to

, we obtain

\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}(\vartheta-\beta)}\|u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}({\mathcal{C}}^{1+\beta})}+\sum_{i=0}^{d}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\nabla^{2}u_{i}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{i}}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{i}})}\leqslant C_{1}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{0}}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{0}}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{0}})}+C_{2}\kappa_{1}\|\nabla u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}},

where

only depend on

, and

\|u(t_{1})-u(t_{0})\|_{\infty}\leqslant C(\lambda)(t_{1}-t_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\kappa_{1}\|\nabla u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}}+|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{0}}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{0}}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{0}})}\Big).

Choosing

, we obtain (

) and (
2.36
) for all
\lambda\geqslant C_{0}\kappa_{1}^{2/\vartheta}
.
∎

3.
SDEs with mixed
L^{\boldsymbol{p}}
-drifts

In this section we first establish a priori Krylov estimates for any solution of SDEs with mixed drifts and for any index
(q,{\boldsymbol{p}})\in{\mathscr{I}}_{1}
, where
{\mathscr{I}}_{1}
is defined in (
2.14
).
Using this a priori estimates, one can perform the classical Zvonkin transformation (see
[
46
]
),
and then establish the weak well-posedness under conditions (
1.13
) and (
2.33
). Moreover, we also obtain the two-sided density estimates.
As a byproduct, one improves the Krylov estimate to any index
(q,{\boldsymbol{p}})\in{\mathscr{I}}_{2}
, which is crucial for the strong well-posedness and the propagation of chaos.

Let
\xi_{t}
be a given
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
-valued measurable adapted process. We consider the following SDE:

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X_{t}=[\xi_{t}+b(t,X_{t})]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\sigma(t,X_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{t},

(3.1)

where
b:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
\sigma:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\otimes{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

are Borel measurable functions.
We first introduce the following notion of solutions, also called weak solutions.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
{\mathfrak{U}}:=(\Omega,{\mathscr{F}},{\mathbb{P}},({\mathscr{F}}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0})
be a stochastic basis, and
\xi_{t}
be a given

{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
-valued measurable
{\mathscr{F}}_{t}
-adapted process with
\int^{t}_{0}|\xi_{s}|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s<\infty
a.s. for each
t>0
,
and
(X,W)
be a pair of continuous
{\mathscr{F}}_{t}
-adapted processes.
Let
\mu_{0}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
We call
(X,W,{\mathfrak{U}})
a solution of SDE (
3.1
) with initial distribution
\mu_{0}
if

(i)

\mu_{0}={\mathbb{P}}\circ X^{-1}_{0}
and
W
is a standard Brownian motion on
{\mathfrak{U}}
.

(ii)

For all
t\geqslant 0
,

\int^{t}_{0}|b(s,X_{s})|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+\int^{t}_{0}|\sigma(s,X_{s})|^{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s<\infty,\ a.s.

and

X_{t}=X_{0}+\int^{t}_{0}[\xi_{s}+b(s,X_{s})]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+\int^{t}_{0}\sigma(s,X_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{s},\ a.s.

By Theorem
2.7
, we can establish the following a priori Krylov estimate (see
[
46
]
).

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose that (
1.13
) and (
2.33
) hold.
Then for any
(q,{\boldsymbol{p}})\in{\mathscr{I}}_{1}
,
{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathscr{X}}
and
T,\delta>0
, there is a constant
C_{T,\delta}=C_{T,\delta}(\Theta)>0
such that for all
f\in\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}})

and any solution
X
of SDE (
3.1
) in the sense of Definition
3.1
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int^{T}_{0}f(s,X_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)\leqslant|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}})}\left[C_{T,\delta}+\delta{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int^{T}_{0}|\xi_{s}|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)\right].

(3.2)

Proof.

By Theorem
2.7
and as in
[
46
, Lemma 5.5]
, for any
(q,{\boldsymbol{p}})\in{\mathscr{I}}_{1}
,
{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathscr{X}}
and
T,\delta>0
, there
is a constant
C_{T,\delta}>0
such that for any stopping time
\tau\leqslant T
and
f\in\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}}_{\mathbf{x}})
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int^{\tau}_{0}f(s,X_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)\leqslant|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}}_{\mathbf{x}})}\left[C_{T,\delta}+\delta{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int^{\tau}_{0}(|\xi_{s}|+|b(s,X_{s})|){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)\right].

(3.3)

Now for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, define a stopping time

\tau_{n}:=\inf\left\{t>0:\int^{t}_{0}|b(s,X_{s})|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\geqslant n\right\}\wedge T.

Since
(q_{i},{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i})\in{\mathscr{I}}_{1}
, by applying (
3.3
) with
f(s,x)=b_{i}(s,x)
, we obtain

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int^{\tau_{n}}_{0}|b_{i}(s,X_{s})|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)\leqslant|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|b_{i}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{i}}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{i}})}\left[C_{T,\delta}+\delta{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int^{\tau_{n}}_{0}(|\xi_{s}|+|b(s,X_{s})|){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)\right].

Summing up the above inequalities for
i
from
1
to
d
, we get

{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int^{\tau_{n}}_{0}|b(s,X_{s})|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)\leqslant\kappa_{1}\left[C_{T,\delta}+\delta{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int^{\tau_{n}}_{0}(|\xi_{s}|+|b(s,X_{s})|){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)\right].

Letting
\delta=1/(2\kappa_{1})
and
n\to\infty
, we obtain

{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int^{T}_{0}|b(s,X_{s})|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)\leqslant\kappa_{1}C_{T,1/(2\kappa_{1})}+\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int^{T}_{0}(|\xi_{s}|+|b(s,X_{s})|){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right),

which implies

{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int^{T}_{0}|b(s,X_{s})|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)\leqslant 2\kappa_{1}C_{T,1/(2\kappa_{1})}+{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int^{T}_{0}|\xi_{s}|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right).

Substituting this into (
3.3
) with
\tau=T
, we complete the proof.
∎

In the above lemma, the requirement of
(q,{\boldsymbol{p}})\in{\mathscr{I}}_{1}
is too strong for applications. We need to improve it to
(q,{\boldsymbol{p}})\in{\mathscr{I}}_{2}
.
Firstly, we use Theorem
2.9
and the above a priori Krylov estimate to construct the Zvonkin transformation.
For each
i=1,\cdots,d
, consider the following backward PDE:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{i}+\tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}((\sigma\sigma^{*})\cdot\nabla^{2}u_{i})+b\cdot\nabla u_{i}-\lambda u_{i}+b_{i}=0,\quad u_{i}(T)=0.

(3.4)

By reversing the time variable and by Theorem
2.9
, there is a unique solution
u_{i}
satisfying the following estimates: for any
\beta\in(0,\vartheta)
,
where
\vartheta
is defined in (
2.34
),
there are
C_{0},C_{1}\geqslant 1
such that for all
\lambda\geqslant C_{0}\kappa_{1}^{2/\vartheta}

\displaystyle\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}(\vartheta-\beta)}\|u_{i}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}({\mathcal{C}}^{1+\beta})}+\sum_{j=0}^{d}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\nabla^{2}u_{ij}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}^{q_{ij}}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{ij}}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{ij}})}\leqslant C_{1}\kappa_{1},

(3.5)

and for all
0\leqslant t_{0}<t_{1}\leqslant T
,

\displaystyle\|u_{i}(t_{1})-u_{i}(t_{0})\|_{\infty}\leqslant C(\lambda)|t_{1}-t_{0}|^{1/2},

(3.6)

where

\displaystyle u_{i}=u_{i0}+u_{i1}+u_{i2}+\cdots+u_{id},

(3.7)

and

q_{i0}=q_{i},\ {\boldsymbol{p}}_{i0}={\boldsymbol{p}}_{i},\ {\mathbf{x}}_{i0}={\mathbf{x}}_{i},\ q_{ij}=q_{j},\ {\boldsymbol{p}}_{ij}={\boldsymbol{p}}_{j},\ {\mathbf{x}}_{ij}={\mathbf{x}}_{j},\ j=1,\cdots,d.

Below we set

\mathbf{u}=(u_{1},\cdots,u_{d}).

By (
3.5
), for any
\beta\in(0,\vartheta)
, we can choose
\lambda
large enough so that

\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}}\leqslant\|\mathbf{u}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}({\mathcal{C}}^{1+\beta})}\leqslant\tfrac{1}{2}.

Once
\lambda
is chosen, it shall be fixed below without further notice.
Now if we define

\Phi(t,x):=x+\mathbf{u}(t,x),

then for each
t
,

x\mapsto\Phi(t,x)\mbox{ is a $C^{1}$-diffeomorphism},

and

\displaystyle\|\nabla\Phi\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}}+\|\nabla\Phi^{-1}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}}\leqslant 2,

(3.8)

and by (
3.6

0\leqslant t_{0}<t_{1}\leqslant T

\displaystyle\|\Phi(t_{1})-\Phi(t_{0})\|_{\infty}\leqslant C(\lambda)(t_{1}-t_{0})^{1/2}.

(3.9)

We have the following result (see
[
46
, Theorem 3.10]
).

Lemma 3.3

(Zvonkin’s transformation)
.

Under (
1.13
) and (
2.33
),
Y_{t}:=\Phi(t,X_{t})
solves the following SDE

\displaystyle Y_{t}=Y_{0}+\int^{t}_{0}\xi_{s}\cdot\nabla\Phi(s,\Phi^{-1}(s,Y_{s})){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+\int^{t}_{0}\tilde{b}(s,Y_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+\int^{t}_{0}\tilde{\sigma}(s,Y_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{s},

(3.10)

where
Y_{0}:=\Phi(0,X_{0})
and

\tilde{b}(s,y):=\lambda\mathbf{u}(s,\Phi^{-1}(s,y)),\ \ \tilde{\sigma}(s,y):=(\sigma^{*}\nabla\Phi)(s,\Phi^{-1}(s,y)).

Moreover, for any
\beta\in(0,\vartheta\wedge\gamma_{0})
, where
\vartheta
is defined by (
2.34
),

\displaystyle\tilde{b},\ \nabla\tilde{b}\in{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T},\ \tilde{\sigma}\in{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}({\mathcal{C}}^{\beta}),

(3.11)

and for some
\tilde{\kappa}_{0}\geqslant 1
,

\displaystyle\tilde{\kappa}^{-1}_{0}|\eta|^{2}\leqslant|\tilde{\sigma}(s,y)\eta|^{2}\leqslant\tilde{\kappa}_{0}|\eta|^{2},\ \ \eta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

(3.12)

Vice versa, if
Y_{t}
solves SDE (
3.10
), then
X_{t}:=\Phi^{-1}(t,Y_{t})
solves SDE (
3.1
).

Proof.

For each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, define

\mathbf{u}^{n}(t,x):=(\mathbf{u}(t,\cdot)*\varGamma_{1/n})(x),\ \Phi^{n}(t,x):=x+\mathbf{u}^{n}(t,x).

By Itô’s formula, we have

\displaystyle\Phi^{n}(t,X_{t})=\Phi^{n}(0,X_{0})+\int^{t}_{0}[\xi_{s}\cdot\nabla\Phi^{n}+{\mathscr{L}}\Phi^{n}](s,X_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+\int^{t}_{0}(\sigma^{*}\nabla\Phi^{n})(s,X_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{s},

where

{\mathscr{L}}:=\partial_{s}+\mathrm{tr}(a\cdot\nabla^{2})+b\cdot\nabla,\ \ a:=(\sigma\sigma^{*})/2.

Since
x\mapsto\mathbf{u}(t,x)
is
{\mathcal{C}}^{1+\beta}
-continuous, it is easy to see that for each

,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\nabla^{j}\Phi^{n}(t,x)=\nabla^{j}\Phi(t,x),\ \ j=0,1.

Therefore, to show (
3.10
), it suffices to show that as
n\to\infty
,

\int^{t}_{0}|{\mathscr{L}}\Phi^{n}-\lambda\mathbf{u}|(s,X_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\to 0,\ \ a.s.

For
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we define the stopping time

\tau_{m}:=\inf\left\{t>0:|X_{t}|+\int^{t}_{0}|\xi_{s}|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\geqslant m\right\}.

Since
\tau_{m}\to\infty
as
m\to\infty
, it suffices to show that for each fixed
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int^{t\wedge\tau_{m}}_{0}|{\mathscr{L}}\Phi^{n}-\lambda\mathbf{u}|(s,X_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)=0.

(3.13)

Note that by definition,

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathscr{L}}\Phi^{n}-\lambda\mathbf{u}&=\partial_{s}\mathbf{u}^{n}+\mathrm{tr}(a\cdot\nabla^{2}\mathbf{u}^{n})+b\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u}^{n}+b-\lambda\mathbf{u}\\
&=\big[\mathrm{tr}(a\cdot(\nabla^{2}\mathbf{u})*\varGamma_{1/n})-\mathrm{tr}(a\cdot\nabla^{2}\mathbf{u})*\varGamma_{1/n}\big]\\
&\quad+\big[b\cdot\nabla(\mathbf{u}*\varGamma_{1/n})-(b\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u})*\varGamma_{1/n}\big]\\
&\quad+[b*\varGamma_{1/n}-b]+[\lambda(\mathbf{u}*\varGamma_{1/n}-\mathbf{u})].\end{split}

(3.14)

For each
i,j
, since
(q_{ij},{\boldsymbol{p}}_{ij})\in{\mathscr{I}}_{1}
, by the Krylov estimates (

) and (
3.5
), we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int^{t\wedge\tau_{m}}_{0}|\nabla^{2}u^{n}_{ij}-\nabla^{2}u_{ij}|(s,X_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{m}\|\nabla^{2}(u^{n}_{ij}-u_{ij}){\mathbbm{1}}_{B_{m}}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{ij}}_{T}({\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{ij}}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{ij}})}\to 0.

From this and by (
3.7
) and (
3.14
), it is easy to see that (
3.13
) holds. Moreover,
(
3.11
) and (
3.12

).
On the other hand, if
Y_{t}
solves SDE (
3.10
), then by similar calculations,
X_{t}:=\Phi^{-1}(t,Y_{t})
solves SDE (
3.1
).
We omit the details here.
∎

(i) Let

4.1.
Relative entropy

In this subsection we recall the notion and some basic facts about the relative entropy.
Let
E
be a Polish space and
\mu,\nu
be two probability measures on
E
. The relative entropy
{\mathcal{H}}(\mu|\nu)
is defined by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}(\mu|\nu):=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\int_{E}\frac{{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\mu}{{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\nu}\log\Big(\frac{{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\mu}{{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\nu}\Big){\mathord{{\rm d}}}\nu,&\mu\ll\nu,\\
&\infty,&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{aligned}\right.

(4.3)

Since
x\mapsto x\log x
is convex on
[0,\infty)
, by Jensen’s inequality, we have
{\mathcal{H}}(\mu|\nu)\geqslant 0
.

The following theorem contains the main tools used below (see
[
4
, Theorem 2.1(ii)]
,
[
12
, Lemma 1.4.3(a)]
and
[
10
, Lemma 3.9]
).

Theorem 4.4
.

(i)

(Pinsker’s inequality) For any two probability measures
\mu,\nu
,

\displaystyle\|\mu-\nu\|^{2}_{\rm var}\leqslant 2{\mathcal{H}}(\mu|\nu).

(4.4)

(ii)

(The weighted Pinsker inequality) For any
\mu,\nu\in{\mathcal{P}}(E)
and Borel measurable function
f
,

\displaystyle|\langle\mu-\nu,f\rangle|^{2}\leqslant 2\left(1+\log\int_{E}\mathrm{e}^{f(x)^{2}}\nu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x)\right){\mathcal{H}}(\mu|\nu).

(4.5)

(iii)

(Variational representation of the relative entropy) For any
\mu,\nu\in{\mathcal{P}}(E)
,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}(\mu|\nu)=\sup_{\psi\in{\mathcal{B}}_{b}(E)}\left(\int_{E}\psi{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\mu-\log\int_{E}\mathrm{e}^{\psi}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\nu\right),

(4.6)

where
{\mathcal{B}}_{b}(E)
is the set of all bounded Borel measurable functions.

(iv)

(Dimensional bounds on entropy) Let
\mu^{N}
be a symmetric probability measure on
E^{N}
and
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}(E)
. Then for any
k\leqslant N
,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}{\big(}\mu^{N,k}|\mu^{\otimes k}{\big)}\leqslant\frac{2k}{N}{\mathcal{H}}{\big(}\mu^{N}|\mu^{\otimes N}{\big)},

(4.7)

where
\mu^{N,k}
is the marginal distribution of the first
k
-component of
\mu^{N}
.

We recall the following entropy formula for the martingale solutions of classical SDEs,
which is a consequence of Girsanov’s theorem (see
[
27
, Lemma 4.4 and Remark 4.5]
for the most general form).

Lemma 4.5
.

For
i=1,2
, let
b^{i}:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be two measurable functions. Suppose that the martingale problem associated with

(\sigma,b^{2})
is well-posed (see Remark
4.3
). Let
\mu^{1}_{0},\mu^{2}_{0}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
be two initial distributions.
For any two martingale solutions
{\mathbb{P}}_{i}\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\sigma,b^{i}}_{\mu^{i}_{0}}
,
i=1,2
, and
any

, if we let
\mu^{i}_{t}:={\mathbb{P}}_{i}\circ w_{t}^{-1}
be the marginal distributions, then

{\mathcal{H}}(\mu^{1}_{t}|\mu^{2}_{t})\leqslant{\mathcal{H}}(\mu^{1}_{0}|\mu^{2}_{0})+\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{2}}\left(\int^{t}_{0}|\sigma^{-1}(s,w_{s})(b^{1}(s,w_{s})-b^{2}(s,w_{s}))|^{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right).

4.2.
Stability of density

In this section we prepare a stability result about the density of classical SDEs.
Our starting point is the associated Fokker-Planck equation.
Fix
z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Let

A^{z}_{s,t}:=\int^{t}_{s}A(r,z){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\mbox{ with $A(r,z)=(a_{ij}(r,z))=((\sigma\sigma^{*})_{ij}(r,z))/2$}.

Let
P_{s,t}^{z}f(x)
be the Gaussian heat kernel associated with
A^{z}_{s,t}
, i.e.,

P_{s,t}^{z}f(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}h_{A^{z}_{s,t}}(x-y)f(y){\mathord{{\rm d}}}y,

where for a symmetric positive definite matrix
A
,

h_{A}(x):=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\langle A^{-1}x,x\rangle/2}}{\sqrt{(2\pi)^{d}\det(A)}}.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\beta\in[0,1]
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,
{\boldsymbol{p}}\in[1,\infty]^{d}
and
{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathscr{X}}
. Under (
1.13
),
for any
T>0
, there is a constant
C=C(T,d,\beta,k,{\boldsymbol{p}},\kappa_{0})>0
such that for all
0\leqslant s<t\leqslant T
and
0\leqslant f\in\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}}
,

\displaystyle|\nabla^{k}P_{s,t}^{z}(|\cdot|^{\beta}f)(0)|\leqslant C(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}(\beta-k-|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}}|)}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}}}.

Proof.

First of all, by definition and (
1.13
), it is easy to see that for some
\lambda>0
,

|\nabla^{k}h_{A^{z}_{s,t}}(x)|\lesssim(t-s)^{-\frac{k+d}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{\lambda(t-s)}}=(t-s)^{-\frac{k}{2}}(2\pi\lambda)^{\frac{d}{2}}g_{\lambda(t-s)}(x),

and for some
\lambda^{\prime}>\lambda
,

\displaystyle|\nabla^{k}P_{s,t}^{z}(|\cdot|^{\beta}f)(0)|

\displaystyle\lesssim(t-s)^{-\frac{k}{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}g_{\lambda(t-s)}(y)|y|^{\beta}f(y){\mathord{{\rm d}}}y

\displaystyle\lesssim(t-s)^{\frac{\beta}{2}-\frac{k}{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}g_{\lambda^{\prime}(t-s)}(y)f(y){\mathord{{\rm d}}}y.

Let
{\boldsymbol{p}}^{\prime}\in(1,\infty)^{d}
be defined by
\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}}+\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}^{\prime}}={\bf 1}
. Fix
r>0
.
By Hölder’s inequality we have

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}g_{\lambda^{\prime}(t-s)}(y)f(y){\mathord{{\rm d}}}y

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|B^{r}_{0}|}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}g_{\lambda^{\prime}(t-s)}(y){\mathbbm{1}}_{B^{r}_{z}}(y)f(y){\mathord{{\rm d}}}y{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{|B^{r}_{0}|}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\|{\mathbbm{1}}_{B^{r}_{z}}g_{\lambda^{\prime}(t-s)}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}^{\prime}}_{\mathbf{x}}}\|{\mathbbm{1}}_{B^{r}_{z}}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{|B^{r}_{0}|}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\|{\mathbbm{1}}_{B^{r}_{z}}g_{\lambda^{\prime}(t-s)}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}^{\prime}}_{\mathbf{x}}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z\right)|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}}}.

(4.8)

Below, without loss of generality, we suppose
s=0
. By a change of variables, we have

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\|{\mathbbm{1}}_{B^{r}_{z}}g_{\lambda t}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}^{\prime}}_{\mathbf{x}}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z

\displaystyle=(2\pi\lambda t)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\|{\mathbbm{1}}_{B^{r}_{z}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{|\cdot|^{2}}{\lambda t}}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}^{\prime}}_{\mathbf{x}}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{d}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left(\int_{|y_{i}-z_{i}|\leqslant r}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{p^{\prime}_{i}|y_{i}|^{2}}{\lambda t}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y_{i}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}_{i}}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z_{i}=:t^{-\frac{d}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}{\mathcal{J}}_{i}.

For each
i
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}}_{i}

\displaystyle=\int_{|z_{i}|\leqslant 2r}\left(\int_{|y_{i}-z_{i}|\leqslant r}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{p^{\prime}_{i}|y_{i}|^{2}}{\lambda t}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y_{i}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}_{i}}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z_{i}+\int_{|z_{i}|>2r}\left(\int_{|y_{i}-z_{i}|\leqslant r}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{p^{\prime}_{i}|y_{i}|^{2}}{\lambda t}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y_{i}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}_{i}}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z_{i}

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{|z_{i}|\leqslant 2r}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{p^{\prime}_{i}|y_{i}|^{2}}{\lambda t}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y_{i}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}_{i}}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z_{i}+\int_{|z_{i}|>2r}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{p_{i}^{\prime}(|z_{i}|-r)^{2}}{\lambda t}}\left(\int_{|y_{i}-z_{i}|\leqslant r}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y_{i}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}_{i}}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z_{i}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{p^{\prime}_{i}|y_{i}|^{2}}{\lambda t}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y_{i}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}_{i}}}+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{p^{\prime}_{i}|z_{i}|^{2}}{2\lambda t}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z_{i}\lesssim t^{\frac{1}{2p^{\prime}_{i}}}+t^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim t^{\frac{1}{2p^{\prime}_{i}}}=t^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{1}{p_{i}})}.

Hence,

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\|{\mathbbm{1}}_{B^{r}_{z}}g_{\lambda t}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}^{\prime}}_{\mathbf{x}}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z\lesssim t^{-\frac{d}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}t^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{1}{p_{i}})}=t^{-|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}}|/2}.

Combining the above estimates, we obtain the desired estimate.
∎

The following stability result shall be used below to show the existence and uniqueness.

Lemma 4.7

Let

be two Borel measurable functions satisfying (

be the density of the coordinated process

under

\displaystyle\|\rho_{0}(t)-\rho_{1}(t)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}}\lesssim_{C}\|\rho^{o}_{0}-\rho^{o}_{1}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}(1+|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}}|)}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|b^{i}_{0}(s)-b^{i}_{1}(s)|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{i}}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

(4.9)

Proof.

First of all, by the heat kernel estimate (

\displaystyle\rho_{k}(t,y)\leqslant\frac{C_{1}}{t^{d/2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{2\delta_{1}t}}\rho^{o}_{k}(x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}x\lesssim\|\rho^{o}_{k}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}},\ k=0,1.

(4.10)

Note that

solves the following Fokker-Planck equation in the distributional sense:

\partial_{t}\rho_{k}=\partial_{i}\partial_{j}(a_{ij}\rho_{k})+\mathord{{\rm div}}(b_{k}\rho_{k}),\ k=0,1,

where

and we use the Einstein convention for summation.
Below we use the freezing technique to show our result. Fix

\tau_{z}f(x):=f(x+z),\ \ \ell(t,x):=\rho_{0}(t,x)-\rho_{1}(t,x).

By the invariance of shifting the spatial variable

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tau_{z}\ell

\displaystyle=\partial_{i}\partial_{j}(\tau_{z}a_{ij}\tau_{z}\ell)+\mathord{{\rm div}}(\tau_{z}b_{0}\tau_{z}\ell)+\mathord{{\rm div}}(\tau_{z}(b_{0}-b_{1})\tau_{z}\rho_{1})

\displaystyle=a_{ij}(t,z)\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\tau_{z}\ell+\partial_{i}\partial_{j}((\tau_{z}a_{ij}-a_{ij}(t,z))\tau_{z}\ell)

\displaystyle\quad+\mathord{{\rm div}}(\tau_{z}b_{0}\tau_{z}\ell)+\mathord{{\rm div}}(\tau_{z}(b_{0}-b_{1})\tau_{z}\rho_{1}).

By Duhamel’s formula we have

\displaystyle\tau_{z}\ell(t,x)

\displaystyle=P^{z}_{0,t}\tau_{z}\ell(0,x)+\int^{t}_{0}P^{z}_{s,t}(\partial_{i}\partial_{j}((\tau_{z}a_{ij}-a_{ij}(s,z))\tau_{z}\ell))(s,x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle\quad+\int^{t}_{0}P^{z}_{s,t}\mathord{{\rm div}}(\tau_{z}b_{0}\tau_{z}\ell)(s,x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+\int^{t}_{0}P^{z}_{s,t}\mathord{{\rm div}}(\tau_{z}(b_{0}-b_{1})\tau_{z}\rho_{1})(s,x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

By (
1.13
) and Lemma
4.6
we have

\displaystyle|\tau_{z}\ell(t,0)|

\displaystyle\lesssim|P^{z}_{0,t}\tau_{z}\ell(0,0)|+\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\frac{\gamma_{0}}{2}-1}\|\tau_{z}\ell\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}(1+|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}}|)}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\tau_{z}b^{i}_{0}\tau_{z}\ell|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{i}}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}(1+|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}}|)}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\tau_{z}(b^{i}_{0}-b^{i}_{1})\tau_{z}\rho_{1}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{i}}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

Noting that

\displaystyle|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\tau_{z}b^{i}_{0}\tau_{z}\ell|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{i}}}\leqslant|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\tau_{z}b^{i}_{0}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{i}}}\|\tau_{z}\ell\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}}\leqslant\kappa_{1}\|\ell\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}},

and by (
4.10
),

\displaystyle|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\tau_{z}(b^{i}_{0}-b^{i}_{1})\tau_{z}\rho_{1}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{i}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\tau_{z}(b^{i}_{0}-b^{i}_{1})|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{i}}}\|\tau_{z}\rho_{1}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle=|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|b^{i}_{0}-b^{i}_{1}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{i}}}\|\rho_{1}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}}\lesssim|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|b^{i}_{0}-b^{i}_{1}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{i}}}\|\rho^{o}_{1}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}},

we further have

\displaystyle\|\ell(t)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}}=\sup_{z}|\tau_{z}\ell(t,0)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\ell(0)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}}+\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\frac{\gamma_{0}}{2}-1}\|\ell(s)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}(1+|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}}|)}\|\ell(s)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}(1+|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}}|)}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|b^{i}_{0}-b^{i}_{1}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{i}}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

By Gronwall’s inequality of Volterra’s type (see
[
47
, Lemma 2.2]
), we obtain the desired estimate.
∎

4.3.
Well-posedness of dDDSDEs

Now we are ready to prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 4.8
.

(Weak well-posedness)
Suppose that (
1.13
) holds and for any
T>0
and
i=1,\cdots,d
, there are
indices
(q_{i},{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i})\in{\mathscr{I}}^{o}
and
{\mathbf{x}}_{i}\in{\mathscr{X}}
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{\mu\in C([0,T];{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\sup_{r\geqslant 0}|b^{i}(\cdot,\cdot,r,\mu_{\cdot})||\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{i}}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{i}})}\leqslant\kappa_{1},

(4.11)

and for some
h_{i}\in{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{i}}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{i}})
and for all
t,x\in[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
r,r^{\prime}\geqslant 0
and
\mu,\nu\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,

\displaystyle|b^{i}(t,x,r,\mu)-b^{i}(t,x,r^{\prime},\nu)|\leqslant h_{i}(t,x)\big(|r-r^{\prime}|+\|\mu-\nu\|_{\rm var}\big).

(4.12)

Then for any probability measure
\mu_{0}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x)=\rho_{0}(x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}x
with
\rho_{0}\in{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}
,
there is a unique weak solution
(X,W,{\mathfrak{U}})
, or equivalently, a martingale solution to dDDSDE (
4.1
) with initial distribution
\mu_{0}
.

Proof.

We divide the proof into three steps.

(Step 1).
Let
\mu^{0}_{t}\equiv\mu_{0}
for any
t\geqslant 0
. We consider the following Picard iteration: for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X^{n}_{t}=b_{n}(t,X^{n}_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\sigma(t,X^{n}_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{t},\ \ X^{n}_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(d)}}{{=}}\mu_{0},

(4.13)

where

b_{n}(t,x):=b(t,x,\rho^{n-1}_{t}(x),\mu^{n-1}_{t}),

and

\displaystyle\mu^{n-1}_{t}\mbox{ is the marginal distribution of $X^{n-1}_{t}$, which has a density $\rho^{n-1}_{t}$.}

(4.14)

By (
4.11
), one sees that for each
i=1,\cdots,d
,

\displaystyle\sup_{n}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|b^{i}_{n}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{i}}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{i}})}\leqslant\kappa_{1}.

(4.15)

Thus, by Theorem
3.5
, for each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, there is a unique weak solution

(X^{n},W^{n},{\mathfrak{U}}^{n})
to SDE (
4.13
), where

{\mathfrak{U}}^{n}:=(\Omega^{n},{\mathscr{F}}^{n},{\mathbf{P}}_{n};({\mathscr{F}}^{n}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}),

and for each
t>0
,
X^{n}_{t}
admits a density
\rho^{n}_{t}
satisfying the following estimate: for all
(t,y)\in[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\rho^{n}_{t}(y)\leqslant\frac{C_{1}}{t^{d/2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{\delta_{1}t}}\rho_{0}(x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}x\lesssim\|\rho_{0}\|_{\infty}.

(4.16)

Moreover, for any
T>0
, by (
3.15
),
there is a constant
C>0
such that

\sup_{n}{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbf{P}}_{n}}|X^{n}_{t}-X^{n}_{s}|^{4}\leqslant C|t-s|^{2},\ \ s,t\in[0,T],

and by (
3.18
), for any
(q_{0},{\boldsymbol{p}}_{0})\in{\mathscr{I}}_{2}
,
there is a constant
C>0
such that for all
f\in\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{0}}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{0}}_{\mathbf{x}})
,

\displaystyle\sup_{n}{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbf{P}}_{n}}\left(\int^{T}_{0}f(s,X^{n}_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)\leqslant C|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{0}}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{0}}_{\mathbf{x}})}.

(4.17)

In particular, by Kolmogorov’s criterion,

the laws
{\mathbb{P}}_{n}
of
X^{n}_{\cdot}
in
{\mathbb{C}}_{T}
are tight.

(4.18)

(Step 2).
For simplicity of notations, we write

\Gamma_{n,m}(t):=\|\rho^{n}_{t}-\rho^{m}_{t}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}}+\|\rho^{n}_{t}-\rho^{m}_{t}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{1}}.

Noting that by (
4.12
) and (
4.14
),

|b^{i}_{n}(s,x)-b^{i}_{m}(s,x)|\leqslant h_{i}(s,x)\big(|\rho^{n-1}_{s}(x)-\rho^{m-1}_{s}(x)|+\|\mu^{n-1}_{s}-\mu^{m-1}_{s}\|_{\rm var}\big)\leqslant h_{i}(s,x)\Gamma_{n-1,m-1}(s),

we have

\displaystyle|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|b^{i}_{n}(s)-b^{i}_{m}(s)|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{i}}}\leqslant|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|h_{i}(s)|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{i}}}\Gamma_{n-1,m-1}(s)=:\ell_{i}(s)\Gamma_{n-1,m-1}(s).

(4.19)

Since
(\frac{q_{i}}{2},\frac{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}}{2})\in{\mathscr{I}}_{2}
, by Lemma
4.5
and (
4.17
), (
4.19
), we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}(\mu^{n}_{t}|\mu^{m}_{t})

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{m}}\left(\int^{t}_{0}|\sigma^{-1}(s,w_{s})(b_{n}(s,w_{s})-b_{m}(s,w_{s}))|^{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{\|\sigma^{-1}\|^{2}_{\infty}}{2}{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{m}}\left(\int^{t}_{0}|b_{n}(s,w_{s})-b_{m}(s,w_{s})|^{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left(\int^{t}_{0}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}||b^{i}_{n}(s)-b^{i}_{m}(s)|^{2}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|^{q_{i}/2}_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}/2}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{i}}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{\frac{2}{q_{i}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left(\int^{t}_{0}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|b^{i}_{n}(s)-b^{i}_{m}(s)|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|^{q_{i}}_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{i}}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{\frac{2}{q_{i}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left(\int^{t}_{0}\ell^{q_{i}}_{i}(s)\Gamma^{q_{i}}_{n-1,m-1}(s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{\frac{2}{q_{i}}}.

By Pinsker’s inequality (
4.4
), we get

\displaystyle\|\rho^{n}_{t}-\rho^{m}_{t}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{1}}=\|\mu^{n}_{t}-\mu^{m}_{t}\|_{\rm var}\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left(\int^{t}_{0}\ell^{q_{i}}_{i}(s)\Gamma^{q_{i}}_{n-1,m-1}(s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{i}}}.

(4.20)

On the other hand, by (
4.9
), (
4.19
) and Hölder’s inequality, for
q^{\prime}_{i}=\frac{q_{i}}{q_{i}-1}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\rho^{n}_{t}-\rho^{m}_{t}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}(1+|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}}|)}\ell_{i}(s)\Gamma_{n-1,m-1}(s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left(\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-\frac{q_{i}^{\prime}}{2}(1+|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}}|)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{i}^{\prime}}}\left(\int^{t}_{0}\ell^{q_{i}}_{i}(s)\Gamma^{q_{i}}_{n-1,m-1}(s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{i}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left(\int^{t}_{0}\ell^{q_{i}}_{i}(s)\Gamma^{q_{i}}_{n-1,m-1}(s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{i}}},

which together with (
4.20
) yields

\displaystyle\Gamma_{n,m}(t)\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left(\int^{t}_{0}\ell^{q_{i}}_{i}(s)\Gamma^{q_{i}}_{n-1,m-1}(s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{i}}}.

Let
q=q_{1}\vee\cdots\vee q_{d}
. By Hölder’s inequality with respect to
\ell^{q_{i}}_{i}(s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s
, we get

\displaystyle\Gamma_{n,m}^{q}(t)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left(\int^{t}_{0}\ell^{q_{i}}_{i}(s)\Gamma_{n-1,m-1}^{q}(s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)\left(\int^{t}_{0}\ell^{q_{i}}_{i}(s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{\frac{q}{q_{i}}-1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int^{t}_{0}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\ell^{q_{i}}_{i}(s)\Gamma_{n-1,m-1}^{q}(s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

Therefore, by (
4.16
) and Fatou’s lemma,

\varlimsup_{n,m\to\infty}\Gamma_{n,m}^{q}(t)\lesssim\int^{t}_{0}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\ell^{q_{i}}_{i}(s)\varlimsup_{n,m\to\infty}\Gamma_{n-1,m-1}^{q}(s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s,

which implies by Gronwall’s inequality that for each
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{n,m\to\infty}\big(\|\rho^{n}_{t}-\rho^{m}_{t}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}}+\|\rho^{n}_{t}-\rho^{m}_{t}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{1}}\big)=\varlimsup_{n,m\to\infty}\Gamma_{n,m}^{q}(t)=0.

(4.21)

Now by (
4.18
), there is a subsequence
n_{k}
such that as
k\to\infty
,

{\mathbb{P}}_{n_{k}}\mbox{ weakly converges to some ${\mathbb{P}}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{C}}_{T})$},

and by (
4.21
),
{\mathbb{P}}\circ w^{-1}_{t}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x)=\mu_{t}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x)=\rho_{t}(x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}x
and for each
t\in(0,T]
,

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{n\to\infty}\big(\|\rho^{n}_{t}-\rho_{t}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}}+\|\rho^{n}_{t}-\rho_{t}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{1}}\big)=0.

(4.22)

(Step 3).
In this step we show
{\mathbb{P}}\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\sigma,b}_{\mu_{0}}
. More precisely,
we want to show that for fixed
f\in C^{2}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, the process
M^{f}_{t}
defined by (
4.2
)
is a
{\mathcal{B}}_{t}
-martingale under
{\mathbb{P}}
, that is, for any
t_{0}<t_{1}
and every bounded
{\mathcal{B}}_{t_{0}}
-measurable continuous function
\eta
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}{\big(}(M^{f}_{t_{1}}-M^{f}_{t_{0}})\eta{\big)}=0.

(4.23)

Note that for each
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, by SDE (
4.13
) and Itô’s formula,

{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{n_{k}}}{\big(}(M^{k}_{t_{1}}-M^{k}_{t_{0}})\eta{\big)}=0,

where

M^{k}_{t}:=f(w_{t})-f(w_{0})-\int^{t}_{0}\Big(\mathrm{tr}(a_{n_{k}}\cdot\nabla^{2}f)+b_{n_{k}}\cdot\nabla f\Big)(s,w_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

Since
x\mapsto a_{n_{k}}(s,x)
is continuous, to show (
4.23
), the key point is to prove the following:

\lim_{k\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{n_{k}}}\left(\eta\int^{t_{1}}_{t_{0}}b_{n_{k}}(s,w_{s})\cdot\nabla f(s,w_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)={\mathbb{E}}\left(\eta\int^{t_{1}}_{t_{0}}b(s,w_{s},\rho_{s}(w_{s}),\mu_{s})\cdot\nabla f(s,w_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right),

which follows from:

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\sup_{k}{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{n_{k}}}\left(\int^{t_{1}}_{t_{0}}|b_{n_{m}}(s,w_{s})-b(s,w_{s},\rho_{s}(w_{s}),\mu_{s})|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)=0,

(4.24)

together with

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{n_{k}}}\left(\eta\int^{t_{1}}_{t_{0}}b_{n_{m}}(s,w_{s})\cdot\nabla f(w_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)={\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}}\left(\eta\int^{t_{1}}_{t_{0}}b_{n_{m}}(s,w_{s})\cdot\nabla f(w_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)

(4.25)

for each
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
The first limit (
4.24
) follows by the Krylov estimates (
4.17
), (
4.12
) and (
4.22
).
For the second, let

b^{\varepsilon}_{n_{m}}(s,x):=b_{n_{m}}(s,\cdot)*\varGamma_{\varepsilon}(x),\ \ \varepsilon\in(0,1),

where
\varGamma_{\varepsilon}
is the mollifiers in (
2.7
). For each
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, since
x\mapsto b^{\varepsilon}_{n_{m}}(s,x)
is bounded continuous, by the weak convergence of
{\mathbb{P}}_{n_{k}}
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{n_{k}}}\left(\eta\int^{t_{1}}_{t_{0}}b^{\varepsilon}_{n_{m}}(s,w_{s})\cdot\nabla f(w_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)={\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}}\left(\eta\int^{t_{1}}_{t_{0}}b^{\varepsilon}_{n_{m}}(s,w_{s})\cdot\nabla f(w_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right).

(4.26)

Moreover, for each
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
R>0
, by the Krylov estimate (
4.17
), we also have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sup_{k}{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{n_{k}}}\left(\int^{t_{1}}_{t_{0}}|b^{\varepsilon}_{n_{m}}-b_{n_{m}}|(s,w_{s})|{\mathbbm{1}}_{|w_{s}|\leqslant R}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)\\
&\qquad\lesssim\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\|(b^{\varepsilon}_{n_{m}}-b_{n_{m}})^{i}{\mathbbm{1}}_{B^{R}_{0}}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{i}}_{T}({\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{i}})}=0,\end{split}

(4.27)

and

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{k,\varepsilon}{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{n_{k}}}\left(\int^{t_{1}}_{t_{0}}|b^{\varepsilon}_{n_{m}}-b_{n_{m}}|(s,w_{s})|{\mathbbm{1}}_{|w_{s}|\geqslant R}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)=0.

(4.28)

Combining (
4.26
), (
4.27
) and (
4.28
), we obtain (
4.25
). Thus we complete the proof of existence. On the other hand, by the same calculations as in (
4.21
), one can show
that any two weak solutions have the same marginal distribution. Then by Theorem
3.5
, we get the weak uniqueness.
∎

(5.3)

5.1.
Martingale approach

In this section we use the classical martingale approach to show the following qualitative result of weak convergence.

Theorem 5.1
.

For any
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
let
\xi^{N}_{1},\cdots,\xi^{N}_{N}
be N-random variables and
\mu_{0}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Suppose that
the law of
(\xi^{N}_{1},\cdots,\xi^{N}_{N})
is invariant under any permutation of
\{1,\cdots,N\}
, and for any
k\leqslant N
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\circ{\big(}\xi^{N}_{1},\cdots,\xi^{N}_{k}{\big)}^{-1}\to\mu_{0}^{\otimes k},\ \ N\to\infty.

(5.4)

Then for any
k\leqslant N
and
T>0
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\circ{\big(}X^{N,1}_{[0,T]},\cdots,X^{N,k}_{[0,T]}{\big)}^{-1}\to\mu_{[0,T]}^{\otimes k},\ \ N\to\infty,

(5.5)

where
\mu_{[0,T]}
is the law of the unique solution of dDDSDE (
1.2
) with initial distribution
\mu_{0}
on
{\mathbb{C}}_{T}
.

First of all, we use the partial Girsanov transform as used in
[
21
,
43
]

to show some uniform Krylov estimate for particle system (
1.1
).
Let
\{{\widetilde{W}}^{i}_{t},i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
be a sequence of independent
d
-dimensional standard Brownian motions.
For each
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, let
Z_{t}(x)
be the unique strong solution of the following SDE starting from
x
:

{\mathord{{\rm d}}}Z_{t}=\sigma{\big(}t,Z_{t}{\big)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}{\widetilde{W}}^{1}_{t},\ \ Z_{0}=x.

For each
{\boldsymbol{z}}=(z^{2},\cdots,z^{N})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{(N-1)d}
,
let
{\boldsymbol{Z}}^{N}_{t}({\boldsymbol{z}}):={\boldsymbol{Z}}^{N}_{t}:=(Z^{N,2}_{t},\cdots,Z^{N,N}_{t})
be the unique strong solution of the following SDE starting from
{\boldsymbol{z}}
:

{\mathord{{\rm d}}}Z^{N,k}_{t}=b{\big(}t,Z^{N,k}_{t},\eta_{{\boldsymbol{Z}}^{N}_{t}}{\big)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\sigma{\big(}t,Z^{N,k}_{t}{\big)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}{\widetilde{W}}^{k}_{t},\ \ Z^{N,k}_{0}=z^{k},

where
k=2,\cdots,N
and

\eta_{{\boldsymbol{z}}}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y):=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=2}^{N}\delta_{z^{j}}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y).

In particular, as Brownian functionals of
\widetilde{W}^{1}
and
(\widetilde{W}^{2},\cdots,\widetilde{W}^{N})
respectively,

\displaystyle Z_{\cdot}(\cdot)\mbox{ is independent of }{\boldsymbol{Z}}^{N}_{\cdot}(\cdot),

(5.6)

and by the notion of strong solution of SDEs (see (
5.3
)),

\displaystyle{\widetilde{X}}^{N,1}_{t}:=Z_{t}(\xi^{N}_{1}),\ \ ({\widetilde{X}}^{N,2}_{t},\cdots,{\widetilde{X}}^{N,N}_{t}):={\boldsymbol{Z}}^{N}_{t}(\xi^{N}_{2},\cdots,\xi^{N}_{N})=:{\boldsymbol{Y}}^{N}_{t},

(5.7)

solves the following SDE:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\mathord{{\rm d}}}{\widetilde{X}}^{N,1}_{t}=\sigma{\big(}t,{\widetilde{X}}^{N,1}_{t}{\big)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}{\widetilde{W}}^{1}_{t},\ \ {\widetilde{X}}^{N,1}_{0}=\xi^{N}_{1},\\
&\quad\mbox{ and for each $k=2,\cdots,N,$}\\
&{\mathord{{\rm d}}}{\widetilde{X}}^{N,k}_{t}=b{\big(}t,{\widetilde{X}}^{N,k}_{t},\eta_{{\boldsymbol{Y}}^{N}_{t}}{\big)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\sigma{\big(}t,{\widetilde{X}}^{N,k}_{t}{\big)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}{\widetilde{W}}^{k}_{t},\ \ {\widetilde{X}}^{N,k}_{0}=\xi^{N}_{k},\end{aligned}\right.

(5.8)

where

\eta_{{\boldsymbol{Y}}^{N}_{t}}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=2}^{N}\delta_{{\widetilde{X}}^{N,j}_{t}}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y).

Now let us define

\eta_{\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}^{N}_{t}}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y):=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\delta_{{\widetilde{X}}^{N,j}_{t}}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y),\ \ H^{N,1}_{t}:=\sigma{\big(}t,{\widetilde{X}}^{N,1}_{t}{\big)}^{-1}b{\big(}t,{\widetilde{X}}^{N,1}_{t},\eta_{\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}^{N}_{t}}{\big)},

and for
k=2,\cdots,N
,

H^{N,k}_{t}:=\sigma{\big(}t,{\widetilde{X}}^{N,k}_{t}{\big)}^{-1}\Big[b{\big(}t,{\widetilde{X}}^{N,k}_{t},\eta_{\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}^{N}_{t}}{\big)}-b{\big(}t,{\widetilde{X}}^{N,k}_{t},\eta_{{\boldsymbol{Y}}^{N}_{t}}{\big)}\Big].

By the above definition, we clearly have for each
i=1,\cdots,N
,

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}{\widetilde{X}}^{N,i}_{t}=b{\big(}t,{\widetilde{X}}^{N,i}_{t},\eta_{\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}^{N}_{t}}{\big)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\sigma{\big(}t,{\widetilde{X}}^{N,i}_{t}{\big)}{\big(}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}{\widetilde{W}}^{i}_{t}-H^{N,i}_{t}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t{\big)}.

(5.9)

The following uniform estimate is the key step for performing the Girsanov transform to derive the Krylov estimate for the particle system,
whose proof strongly depends on the independence in (
5.6
) and the strong uniqueness used in (
5.8
).

Lemma 5.2
.

For any
\gamma,T>0
,

\displaystyle\sup_{N}{\mathbb{E}}\exp\left\{\gamma\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int^{T}_{0}|H^{N,i}_{t}|^{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right\}<\infty.

(5.10)

Proof.

For
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
{\boldsymbol{y}}=(y^{2},\cdots,y^{N})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{(N-1)d}
, let us
write
\eta_{{\boldsymbol{y}}}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=2}^{N}\delta_{y^{j}}
and define

\Gamma_{1}(t,x,{\boldsymbol{y}}):=\sigma(t,x)^{-1}b\left(t,x,\frac{\delta_{x}}{N}+\eta_{{\boldsymbol{y}}}\right),

and for
k=2,\cdots,N
,

\Gamma_{k}(t,x,{\boldsymbol{y}}):=\sigma(t,y^{k})^{-1}\left[b\left(t,y^{k},\frac{\delta_{x}}{N}+\eta_{{\boldsymbol{y}}}\right)-b\left(t,y^{k},\eta_{{\boldsymbol{y}}}\right)\right].

From the very definition, one sees that for each
i=1,\cdots,N
,

H^{N,i}_{s}=\Gamma_{i}{\big(}s,{\widetilde{X}}^{N,1}_{s},{\boldsymbol{Y}}^{N}_{s}{\big)},

and by (
5.7
) and (
5.6
),

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\exp\left\{\gamma\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int^{T}_{0}|H^{N,i}_{s}|^{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right\}={\mathbb{E}}\exp\left\{\gamma\int^{T}_{0}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\Gamma_{i}{\big(}s,Z_{s}(\xi^{N}_{1}),{\boldsymbol{Y}}^{N}_{s}{\big)}|^{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad={\mathbb{E}}\left({\mathbb{E}}\exp\left\{\gamma\int^{T}_{0}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\Gamma_{i}{\big(}s,Z_{s}(x),{\boldsymbol{y}}_{s}{\big)}|^{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right\}\Big|_{(x,{\boldsymbol{y}_{\cdot}})=(\xi^{N}_{1},{\boldsymbol{Y}}^{N}_{\cdot})}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\sup_{x,{\boldsymbol{y}_{\cdot}}}{\mathbb{E}}\exp\left\{\gamma\int^{T}_{0}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\Gamma_{i}{\big(}s,Z_{s}(x),{\boldsymbol{y}}_{s}{\big)}|^{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad=\sup_{x,{\boldsymbol{y}_{\cdot}}}{\mathbb{E}}\exp\left\{\gamma\int^{T}_{0}f_{{\boldsymbol{y}}}{\big(}s,Z_{s}(x){\big)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right\},

(5.11)

where for
{\boldsymbol{y}}=({\boldsymbol{y}}_{s})_{s\in[0,T]}
,

f_{{\boldsymbol{y}}}(s,x):=\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\Gamma_{i}{\big(}s,x,{\boldsymbol{y}}_{s}{\big)}|^{2}.

Note that by (
1.15
) and because
\phi_{t}(x,x)=0
,

\displaystyle|\Gamma_{1}(t,x,{\boldsymbol{y}})|

\displaystyle=\left|\sigma(t,x)^{-1}F\left(t,x,\frac{1}{N}\Big(\phi_{t}(x,x)+\sum_{j=2}^{N}|\phi_{t}(x,y^{j})|\Big)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\sigma^{-1}\|_{\infty}\left(h(t,x)+\frac{\kappa_{1}}{N}\sum_{j=2}^{N}\phi_{t}(x,y^{j})\right),

and

\displaystyle|\Gamma_{k}(t,x,{\boldsymbol{y}})|

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{\kappa_{1}\|\sigma^{-1}\|_{\infty}}{N}|\phi_{t}(y^{k},x)|,

and by (
1.16
),

\displaystyle\left(\int^{T}_{0}\sup_{\boldsymbol{y}}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\Gamma_{1}(t,\cdot,{\boldsymbol{y}})|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}}}^{q}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right)^{1/q}\leqslant\|\sigma^{-1}\|_{\infty}(\kappa_{1}+\kappa^{2}_{1})

and

\displaystyle\left(\int^{T}_{0}\sup_{\boldsymbol{y}}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\Gamma_{k}(t,\cdot,{\boldsymbol{y}})|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}}}^{q}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right)^{1/q}\leqslant\frac{\kappa^{2}_{1}\|\sigma^{-1}\|_{\infty}}{N-1}.

From these two estimates, by Minkowskii’s inequality, we derive

\displaystyle\left(\int^{T}_{0}\sup_{\boldsymbol{y}}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f_{{\boldsymbol{y}}_{\cdot}}(s,\cdot)|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}/2}_{\mathbf{x}}}^{q/2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right)^{2/q}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\int^{T}_{0}\sup_{\boldsymbol{y}}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}||\Gamma_{i}(t,\cdot,{\boldsymbol{y}})|^{2}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}/2}_{\mathbf{x}}}^{q/2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right)^{2/q}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\int^{T}_{0}\sup_{\boldsymbol{y}}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\Gamma_{i}(t,\cdot,{\boldsymbol{y}})|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}}}^{q}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right)^{2/q}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\sigma^{-1}\|^{2}_{\infty}\left((\kappa_{1}+\kappa^{2}_{1})^{2}+\frac{\kappa^{4}_{1}}{N}\right).

Thus, because
(\frac{q}{2},\frac{{\boldsymbol{p}}}{2})\in{\mathscr{I}}_{2}
, by (
3.21
) we have

\displaystyle\sup_{x,{\boldsymbol{y}}}{\mathbb{E}}\exp\left\{\gamma\int^{T}_{0}f_{{\boldsymbol{y}}}{\big(}s,Z_{s}(x){\big)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right\}\leqslant C,

which together with (
5.11
) yields (
5.10
).
∎

Now if we define

{\mathscr{E}}^{N}_{t}:=\exp\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int^{t}_{0}H^{N,i}_{s}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}{\widetilde{W}}^{i}_{s}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int^{t}_{0}|H^{N,i}_{s}|^{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right\},

then by (
5.10
) and Novikov’s criterion,
t\mapsto Z_{t}
is an exponential martingale and

{\mathscr{E}}^{N}_{t}=1+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int^{t}_{0}H^{N,i}_{s}{\mathscr{E}}^{N}_{s}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}{\widetilde{W}}^{i}_{s}.

Thus, by Girsanov’s theorem,
\big({\widetilde{W}}^{i}_{t}-\int^{t}_{0}H^{N,i}_{s}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\big)_{t\in[0,T]}^{i=1,\cdots,N}
are
N
-independent standard Brownian motions under the new probability measure

{\mathbb{Q}}:={\mathscr{E}}^{N}_{T}{\mathbb{P}}.

Moreover, by (
5.9
) and the weak uniqueness for SDE (
1.1
), we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{Q}}\circ\big({\mathbf{X}}^{N}_{[0,T]}\big)^{-1}={\mathbb{P}}\circ\big({\mathbf{X}}^{N}_{[0,T]}\big)^{-1},

(5.12)

and for any
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}
, by (
5.10
) it is standard to derive that

\displaystyle\sup_{N}{\mathbb{E}}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|{\mathscr{E}}^{N}_{t}|^{\gamma}\right)<\infty.

(5.13)

From these, we can derive the following crucial Krylov estimate for the particle system.

Lemma 5.3
.

(i)

The law of
(X^{N,1}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
,
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, in
{\mathbb{C}}_{T}
is tight.

(ii)

For any
T>0
,
(q,{\boldsymbol{p}})\in{\mathscr{I}}_{2}
and
{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathscr{X}}
, there is a constant
C_{1}=C_{1}(T,\Theta)>0
such that for any
f\in\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}})
,

\displaystyle\sup_{N}{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int^{T}_{0}f{\big(}t,X^{N,1}_{t}{\big)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right)\leqslant C_{1}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}})},

(5.14)

and for any
\lambda>0
and
\beta\in(0,2-|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}}|-\frac{2}{q})
, there is a
C_{2}=C_{2}(T,\Theta,\lambda,\beta)>0
such that for any
f\in\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}})
,

\displaystyle\sup_{N}{\mathbb{E}}\exp\left\{\lambda\int^{T}_{0}f{\big(}t,X^{N,1}_{t}{\big)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right\}\leqslant\mathrm{e}^{C_{2}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|^{2/\beta}_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}})}}.

(5.15)

(iii)

Let
{\boldsymbol{p}}_{1},{\boldsymbol{p}}_{2}\in(1,\infty)^{d}
and let
q\in(1,\infty)
with
|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{1}}|+|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{2}}|+\frac{2}{q}<2

and
{\mathbf{x}}_{1},{\mathbf{x}}_{2}\in{\mathscr{X}}
.
Then for any
T>0
, it holds that for some
C_{3}=C_{3}(T,\Theta)>0
,

\displaystyle\sup_{N}{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int^{T}_{0}f{\big(}t,X^{N,1}_{t},X^{N,2}_{t}{\big)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right)\leqslant C_{3}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{1}}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{1}}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{2}}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{2}}))},

(5.16)

where
\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{1}}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{1}}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{2}}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{2}}))
is the localization of
{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}({\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{1}}_{x_{1}}({\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{2}}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{2}}))
as in (
2.5
).

Proof.

(i) By (
5.12
), Hölder’s inequality, (
5.13
) and (
5.8
), there is a constant
C>0
such that for all
0\leqslant s<t\leqslant T
and
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}|X^{N,1}_{t}-X^{N,1}_{s}|^{4}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}{\big(}{\mathscr{E}}^{N}_{T}|{\widetilde{X}}^{N,1}_{t}-{\widetilde{X}}^{N,1}_{s}|^{4}{\big)}

\displaystyle\leqslant{\big(}{\mathbb{E}}({\mathscr{E}}^{N}_{T})^{2}{\big)}^{1/2}{\big(}{\mathbb{E}}|{\widetilde{X}}^{N,1}_{t}-{\widetilde{X}}^{N,1}_{s}|^{8}{\big)}^{1/2}\leqslant C|t-s|^{2},

which, together with (
5.4
), implies the tightness by Kolmogorov’s criterion.

(ii) Let
\gamma>1
be such that
(\frac{q}{\gamma},\frac{{\boldsymbol{p}}}{\gamma})\in{\mathscr{I}}_{2}
.
By (
5.12
), Hölder’s inequality, (
5.13
) and (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int^{T}_{0}f(t,X^{N,1}_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right)

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left({\mathscr{E}}^{N}_{T}\int^{T}_{0}f(t,{\widetilde{X}}^{N,1}_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\Big[{\mathbb{E}}({\mathscr{E}}^{N}_{T})^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}}\Big]^{1-1/\gamma}\left[{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int^{T}_{0}|f(t,{\widetilde{X}}^{N,1}_{t})|^{\gamma}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right)\right]^{1/\gamma}

\displaystyle\leqslant C|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f^{\gamma}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|^{1/\gamma}_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q/\gamma}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}/\gamma}_{\mathbf{x}})}=C|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\mathbf{x}})}.

(
5.15
) follows by the same method and (
3.21
).

(iii) Let
\gamma\in(1,{\mathord{{\rm min}}}_{i}(p_{1i},p_{2i})\wedge q)
be such that
|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{1}/\gamma}|+|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{2}/\gamma}|+\frac{2}{q/\gamma}<2
.
By (
5.12
), Hölder’s inequality and (
5.13
), we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int^{T}_{0}f(t,X^{N,1}_{t},X^{N,2}_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right)

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left({\mathscr{E}}^{N}_{T}\int^{T}_{0}f(t,{\widetilde{X}}^{N,1}_{t},{\widetilde{X}}^{N,2}_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\Big[{\mathbb{E}}({\mathscr{E}}^{N}_{T})^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}}\Big]^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}\left[{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int^{T}_{0}|f(t,{\widetilde{X}}^{N,1}_{t},{\widetilde{X}}^{N,2}_{t})|^{\gamma}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{x}\left[{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int^{T}_{0}|f(t,Z_{t}(x),{\widetilde{X}}^{N,2}_{t})|^{\gamma}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}.

By
|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{1}/\gamma}|+|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{2}/\gamma}|+\frac{2}{q/\gamma}<2
, one can choose
q_{1},q_{2}>\gamma
so that

\frac{1}{q_{1}/\gamma}+\frac{1}{q_{2}/\gamma}=1+\frac{1}{q/\gamma}
and
(q_{i}/\gamma,{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}/\gamma)\in{\mathscr{I}}_{2}
,
i=1,2
.
Since
Z_{\cdot}(x)
and
{\widetilde{X}}^{N,2}
are independent by (
5.6
) and (
5.7
), and satisfy the Krylov estimate (
5.14
), the desired estimate now follows by using
[
37
, Lemma 2.6]
.
∎

In the following, in order to take weak limits, we need to mollify the coefficients. For
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we define

\displaystyle b_{\varepsilon,k}(t,x,\mu):=F_{\varepsilon}(t,x,(\phi^{k}_{t}\circledast\mu)(x)),

(5.17)

where

F_{\varepsilon}(t,x,r):=(-\varepsilon^{-1})\vee{\big(}(F(t,\cdot,r)*\varGamma_{\varepsilon})(x){\big)}\wedge\varepsilon^{-1}

and

\phi^{k}_{t}(x,y):=(-k)\vee{\big(}(\phi_{t}*\varGamma_{1/k})(x,y){\big)}\wedge k.

We have the following properties for the above approximation.

Lemma 5.4
.

(i)
b_{\varepsilon,k}\in L^{\infty}_{T}(C_{b}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})))
and

|b_{\varepsilon,k}(t,x,\mu)|\leqslant h_{t}*\varGamma_{\varepsilon}(x)+\kappa_{0}(\phi^{k}_{t}\circledast\mu)*\varGamma_{\varepsilon}(x)

and

|b-b_{\varepsilon,k}|{\big(}t,x,\mu{\big)}\leqslant\sup_{|r|\leqslant k}|F_{\varepsilon}-F|(t,x,r)+\kappa_{0}|(\phi^{k}_{t}-\phi_{t})\otimes\mu|(x).

(ii) For any
T>0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sup_{N}{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int^{T}_{0}|b-b_{\varepsilon,k}|{\big(}s,X^{N,1}_{s},\eta_{{\mathbf{X}}^{N}_{s}}{\big)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)=0.

(5.18)

Proof.

(i) is obvious by definition and the assumptions. We now show (ii). Note that

\displaystyle|b-b_{\varepsilon,k}|{\big(}s,X^{N,1}_{s},\eta_{{\mathbf{X}}^{N}_{s}}{\big)}\leqslant\sup_{|r|\leqslant k}|F_{\varepsilon}-F|(s,X^{N,1}_{s},r)+\frac{\kappa_{0}}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\phi^{k}_{s}-\phi_{s}|(X^{N,1}_{s},X^{N,j}_{s}).

(5.19)

We first show that for fixed
r\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
,

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sup_{N}{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int^{T}_{0}|F_{\varepsilon}-F|(s,X^{N,1}_{s},r){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)=0.

(5.20)

Let
R>0
. Since
(\frac{q}{2},\frac{{\boldsymbol{p}}}{2})\in{\mathscr{I}}_{2}
and
|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|F(\cdot,r)|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|^{2}_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}}_{\mathbf{x}})}<\infty
by (
1.15
) and (
1.16
),
by Hölder’s inequality and (
5.14
), (
2.8
), we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int^{T}_{0}|F_{\varepsilon}-F|(s,X^{N,1}_{s},r){\mathbbm{1}}_{|X^{N,1}_{s}|>R}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\left[{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int^{T}_{0}|F_{\varepsilon}-F|^{2}(s,X^{N,1}_{s},r){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\left[\int^{T}_{0}{\mathbb{P}}(|X^{N,1}_{s}|>R){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}||F_{\varepsilon}-F|^{2}(\cdot,r)|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|^{1/2}_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q/2}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}/2}_{\mathbf{x}})}\left[\int^{T}_{0}\Big({\mathbb{P}}\big(|X^{N,1}_{s}-X^{N,1}_{0}|>\tfrac{R}{2}\big)+{\mathbb{P}}\big(|X^{N,1}_{0}|>\tfrac{R}{2}\big)\Big){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|F(\cdot,r)|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}}_{\mathbf{x}})}\left[\int^{T}_{0}\left(\frac{{\mathbb{E}}|X^{N,1}_{s}-X^{N,1}_{0}|}{R}+{\mathbb{P}}\big(|X^{N,1}_{0}|>\tfrac{R}{2}\big)\right){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|F(\cdot,r)|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}}_{\mathbf{x}})}\left[\frac{C}{R}+{\mathbb{P}}\big(|\xi^{N}_{1}|>\tfrac{R}{2}\big)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\to 0,\ \ R\to\infty.

(5.21)

On the other hand, for each
R>0
, by (
5.14
) again, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int^{T}_{0}|F_{\varepsilon}-F|(s,X^{N,1}_{s},r){\mathbbm{1}}_{|X^{N,1}_{s}|\leqslant R}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)\lesssim|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|(F_{\varepsilon}-F)(\cdot,r){\mathbbm{1}}_{B_{R}}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}}_{\mathbf{x}})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{BZ30}}}{{\to 0}},\ \ \varepsilon\to 0,

which together with (
5.21
) yields (
5.20
).

Since
|F_{\varepsilon}(t,x,r)-F_{\varepsilon}(t,x,r^{\prime})|\leqslant\kappa_{0}|r-r^{\prime}|
, by (
5.20
) and a finite covering technique, for each
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we further have

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sup_{N}{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int^{T}_{0}\sup_{|r|\leqslant k}|F_{\varepsilon}-F|(s,X^{N,1}_{s},r){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)=0.

\delta>0
, one can find
M

{\mathbb{R}}^{m}

and radius

\big\{r:|r|\leqslant k\big\}\subset\cup_{i=1,\cdots,M}B_{\delta}(r_{i}).

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int^{T}_{0}\sup_{|r|\leqslant k}|F_{\varepsilon}-F|(s,X^{N,1}_{s},r){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{M}{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int^{T}_{0}|F_{\varepsilon}-F|(s,X^{N,1}_{s},r_{i}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)+\kappa_{0}\delta.

By (
5.20
) and firstly letting
\varepsilon\to 0
and then

, we get (
5.22
).

Moreover, for
j\not=1

{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int^{T}_{0}|\phi^{k}_{s}-\phi_{s}|(X^{N,1}_{s},X^{N,j}_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)={\mathbb{E}}\left(\int^{T}_{0}|\phi^{k}_{s}-\phi_{s}|(X^{N,1}_{s},X^{N,2}_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right),

as in proving (
5.20
) and by (
1.16
) and (
5.16

\lim_{k\to\infty}\sup_{N}{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int^{T}_{0}|\phi^{k}_{s}-\phi_{s}|(X^{N,1}_{s},X^{N,2}_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)=0,

and because
\phi_{s}(x,x)=0
and (

\lim_{k\to\infty}\sup_{N}{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int^{T}_{0}|\phi^{k}_{s}|(X^{N,1}_{s},X^{N,1}_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)=0.

Hence,

\lim_{k\to\infty}\sup_{N}\sup_{j=1,\cdots,N}{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int^{T}_{0}|\phi^{k}_{s}-\phi_{s}|(X^{N,1}_{s},X^{N,j}_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)=0,

which together with (

) and (

5.2.
Entropy method

In this section we recall the entropy method used in
[
20
]
to show a quantitative result for weak convergence when
the interaction kernel is bounded measurable, which is essentially contained in
[
20
]
. For the completeness of the paper,
we provide a detailed proof.
We first prepare the following lemma.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\phi:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a bounded measurable function with
\phi(x,x)=0

and
\boldsymbol{\xi}:=(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{N})
be a sequence of independent identical distributed random variables. Set

\bar{\phi}(x,y):=\phi(x,y)-(\phi\circledast\mu)(x).

Then for any
\lambda\leqslant\frac{1}{16\mathrm{e}^{2}\|\phi\|_{\infty}^{2}}
,

{\mathbb{E}}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda N|(\bar{\phi}\circledast\eta_{\boldsymbol{\xi}})(\xi_{1})|^{2}}\leqslant 6,

where
\eta_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y):=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{\xi_{i}}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y)
.

Proof.

Note that by Taylor’s expansion,

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{\lambda N|(\bar{\phi}\circledast\eta_{\boldsymbol{\xi}})(\xi_{1})|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{m}N^{m}}{m!}|(\bar{\phi}\circledast\eta_{\boldsymbol{\xi}})(\xi_{1})|^{2m}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{m}}{m!N^{m}}\Big|\sum_{j=1}^{N}\bar{\phi}(\xi_{1},\xi_{j})\Big|^{2m}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{m}}{m!N^{m}}2^{2m}\Big(|\bar{\phi}(\xi_{1},\xi_{1})|^{2m}+\Big|\sum_{j=2}^{N}\bar{\phi}(\xi_{1},\xi_{j})\Big|^{2m}\Big)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(4\lambda)^{m}}{m!N^{m}}\left(\|\bar{\phi}\|^{2m}_{\infty}+\sum_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{2m}=2}^{N}\bar{\phi}(\xi_{1},\xi_{j_{1}})\cdots\bar{\phi}(\xi_{1},\xi_{j_{2m}})\right).

Let
{\mathbf{J}}
be the set of all indices
(j_{1},\cdots,j_{2m})\in\{2,\cdots,N\}^{2m}
such that there is at least one index
j_{k}
different from all others.
Since for
j\in\{2,\cdots,N\}
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

{\mathbb{E}}\bar{\phi}(x,\xi_{j})=0,

by the independence of the components of
\boldsymbol{\xi}
, we have for any
(j_{1},\cdots,j_{2m})\in{\mathbf{J}}
,

{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\bar{\phi}(\xi_{1},\xi_{j_{1}})\cdots\bar{\phi}(\xi_{1},\xi_{j_{2m}})\Big]={\mathbb{E}}\Big[{\mathbb{E}}\big[\bar{\phi}(x,\xi_{j_{1}})\cdots\bar{\phi}(x,\xi_{j_{2m}})\big]|_{x=\xi_{1}}\Big]=0.

Hence,

{\mathbb{E}}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda N|(\bar{\phi}\circledast\eta_{\boldsymbol{\xi}})(\xi_{1})|^{2}}\leqslant\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(4\lambda)^{m}}{m!N^{m}}\|\bar{\phi}\|_{\infty}^{2m}(1+\sharp{\mathbf{J}}^{c}),

where
\sharp{\mathbf{J}}^{c}
stands for the cardinality of the complement set
{\mathbf{J}}^{c}
.

Suppose
2m\leqslant N
. It is easy to see that

(j_{1},\cdots,j_{2m})\in{\mathbf{J}}^{c}
if and only if each
j_{k}
appears at least twice and there are at most
m
-distinct
j_{k}
.
Thus one has

{\mathbf{J}}^{c}=\cup_{n=1}^{m}{\mathbf{J}}_{n},

where
{\mathbf{J}}_{n}
is the set of
(j_{1},\cdots,j_{2m})
such that each
j_{k}
appears at least twice and exactly
n
-integers appear.
Clearly, by Stirling’s formula
n^{n}\leqslant\mathrm{e}^{n}n!\leqslant\mathrm{e}^{2n}n^{n}
, we have

\sharp{\mathbf{J}}_{n}\leqslant\left(\begin{array}[]{c}N-1\\
n\end{array}\right)n^{2m}=\frac{(N-1)^{n}}{n!}n^{2m}\leqslant\frac{\mathrm{e}^{n}(N-1)^{n}}{n^{n}}n^{2m}\leqslant(N\mathrm{e})^{n}n^{m}.

Thus, for
2m\leqslant N
,

\sharp{\mathbf{J}}^{c}\leqslant\sum_{n=1}^{m}(N\mathrm{e})^{n}n^{m}\leqslant 2(N\mathrm{e})^{m}m^{m}\leqslant 2(N\mathrm{e})^{m}\mathrm{e}^{m}m!.

Moreover, for
2m>N
, we obviously have

\sharp{\mathbf{J}}^{c}\leqslant N^{2m}\leqslant N^{m}(2m)^{m}\leqslant N^{m}(2\mathrm{e})^{m}m!.

So, for
\lambda\leqslant\frac{1}{16\mathrm{e}^{2}\|\phi\|_{\infty}^{2}}
,

{\mathbb{E}}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda N|(\bar{\phi}\circledast\eta_{\boldsymbol{\xi}})(\xi_{1})|^{2}}\leqslant\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(4\lambda)^{m}\|\bar{\phi}\|_{\infty}^{2m}\Big(\frac{1}{m!N^{m}}+(2\mathrm{e})^{m}\Big)\leqslant 2\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}2^{-m}=6.

The proof is complete.
∎

(ii) By definition we have

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\sup_{j,k}{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int^{T}_{0}\bar{\phi}_{t}{\big(}X^{N,1}_{t},X^{N,j}_{t}{\big)}^{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int^{T}_{0}\bar{\phi}_{t}{\big(}X^{N,1}_{t},X^{N,k}_{t}{\big)}^{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad=\sup_{j}{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int^{T}_{0}\bar{\phi}_{t}{\big(}X^{N,1}_{t},X^{N,j}_{t}{\big)}^{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right)<\infty.

(6.7)

By (

(7.1)

\displaystyle\|\rho^{\varepsilon}_{t}-\rho_{t}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}}\lesssim_{C}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}(1+\frac{d}{p})}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|B_{\varepsilon}(s)|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{p}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

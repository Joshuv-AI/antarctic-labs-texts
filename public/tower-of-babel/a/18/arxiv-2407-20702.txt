A priori error estimates for optimal control problems governed by the transient Stokes equations and subject to state constraints pointwise in time
Dmitriy Leykekhman, Boris Vexler \ORCID 0000-0001-6211-4263 and Jakob Wagner* \ORCID 0000-0001-8510-9790

Abstract
In this paper, we consider a state constrained optimal control problem governed by the transient Stokes equations. The state constraint is given by an L 2 L^{2} functional in space, which is required to fulfill a pointwise bound in time. The discretization scheme for the Stokes equations consists of inf-sup stable finite elements in space and a discontinuous Galerkin method in time, for which we have recently established best approximation type error estimates. Using these error estimates, for the discrete control problem we derive error estimates and as a by-product we show an improved regularity for the optimal control. We complement our theoretical analysis with numerical results.

Introduction

In this paper we consider the following optimal control problem

\text{Minimize }\ J({\bf{q}},{\bf{u}}):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\lVert{\bf{u}}(t)-{{\bf{u}}}_{d}(t)\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\ dt+\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\lVert{\bf{q}}(t)\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\ dt

(1.1a)

subject to the state equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}{\bf{u}}-\Delta{\bf{u}}+\nabla p

\displaystyle={\bf{q}}

\displaystyle\text{in }I\times\Omega,

(1.1b)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot{\bf{u}}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{in }I\times\Omega,

\displaystyle{\bf{u}}

\displaystyle={\bf{0}}

\displaystyle\text{on }I\times\partial\Omega,

\displaystyle{\bf{u}}(0)

\displaystyle={\bf{0}}

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

control constraints

{\bf{q}}_{a}\leq{\bf{q}}(t,x)\leq{\bf{q}}_{b}\quad\text{for almost all }\;(t,x)\in I\times\Omega

(1.1c)

and state constraints

\int_{\Omega}{\bf{u}}(t,x)\cdot{\bf w}(x)\ dx\leq\beta\quad\text{for all }\;t\in\bar{I}.

(1.1d)

Here we assume that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d=2,3
, is a convex polygonal or polyhedral domain and

I=(0,T]
is a bounded time interval.

In the objective function,
{\bf{u}}_{d}\in L^{2}(I\times\Omega)^{d}
represents the desired state and
\alpha>0
is the regularization parameter.

The control constraints are given by the constant vectors

{\bf{q}}_{a},{\bf{q}}_{b}\in{(\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\})^{d}}
and satisfy
{\bf{q}}_{a}<{\bf{q}}_{b}
.

In the state constraint, the constant scalar
\beta
satisfies
\beta>0
and

{\bf w}(x)
is a given function in
L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}
.

Note that for ease of presentation, we consider an optimal control problem with
homogeneous initial data
{\bf{u}}(0)={\bf{0}}
, while
all results also extend to the inhomogeneous case
{\bf{u}}(0)={\bf{u}}_{0}
.
The main result of this paper states, that the error between the optimal control
\bar{{\bf{q}}}
for the continuous
problem and the optimal solution
\bar{{\bf{q}}}_{\sigma}
of the discretized problem satisfies

\lVert\bar{{\bf{q}}}-\bar{{\bf{q}}}_{\sigma}\rVert_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}L^{2}(I\times\Omega)}}\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\,\ln{\frac{T}{k}}(k^{\frac{1}{2}}+h),

and is presented in
Theorem

6.6
.
A similar optimal control problem subject to the heat equation was considered in

[
33
]
, where a
comparable error estimate was derived.
The authors of
[
32
,
31
]
discuss error estimates for
parabolic problems with purely time-dependent controls and impose constraints on spatial
averages of either function values or gradients of the state at every point in time.
Error estimates for state constrained parabolic problems, with state constraints applied
pointwise in time and space, can be found in

[
8
,
19
,
15
]
.

The optimal control of flow phenomena subject to state constraints is a
very active research topic, and there have been numerous contributions to the field, see, e.g.,

[
11
,
12
,
14
]

for optimal control of the stationary Navier-Stokes equations and

[
17
,
29
,
39
,
40
,
41
]

for the transient Navier-Stokes equations, subject to general state constraints of the form

{\bf{u}}\in\mathcal{C}
.
Note that the above references only contain the analysis of the continuous problems and some numerical results,
but no derivation of error estimates.
In
[
13
]
error estimates for an optimal control problem subject to the stationary
Stokes equations with pointwise state constraints are shown.
Let us also specifically mention
[
24
]
, where an optimal control problem of the
stationary Navier-Stokes equations was considered, and a constraint was put onto the drag functional

\int_{\partial\Omega}(\partial_{n}{\bf{u}}-p\mathbf{n})\mathbf{e}_{d}\ ds
, for some given direction of
interest
specified by the unit vector
\mathbf{e}_{d}
. The setting of our work, constraining a
L^{2}(\Omega)

functional pointwise in time, can be seen as a step towards discussing transient problems with
drag/lift constraints at every point in time.

We conclude our work by using the derived error estimates to obtain improved regularity

2
Notation and Preliminary results

We will use the standard notation for the Lebegue and Sobolev spaces
over the spatial domain
\Omega
. The pressure space is

L^{2}_{0}(\Omega):=\left\{p\in L^{2}(\Omega)\colon\int_{\Omega}p\ dx=0\right\}.

Throughout the paper, vector valued quantities and spaces will be indicated by boldface letters.
We denote for a Banach space
X
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
, by
L^{p}(I;X)
the Bochner space of
X
-valued
functions over
I
, whose
X
-norm is
p
-integrable w.r.t time.
If
X
is reflexive and
1\leq p<\infty
, there holds the following isomorphism,
see
[
23
, Corollary 1.3.22]

L^{p}(I;X)^{*}\cong L^{q}(I;X^{*}),\qquad\text{where }\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1.

(2.1)

Note that the range includes the value
p=1
and not
p=\infty
.
The dual space of
C(\bar{I};L^{2}(\Omega)^{d})
is isomorphic to the
space of regular
L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}
-valued Borel measures, and we denote it by

\mathcal{M}(\bar{I};L^{2}(\Omega)^{d})\cong(C(\bar{I};{L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}}))^{*}
.
Similarly for scalar regular Borel measures of
\bar{I}
, we use the notation

\mathcal{M}(\bar{I})\cong(C(\bar{I}))^{*}
.
To denote the vector valued spaces of divergence free functions, having various levels of regularity,
we use the following notation

\mathbf{V}_{0}=\{{\bf{v}}\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}:\ \nabla\cdot{\bf{v}}=0\land{\bf{v}}\cdot\mathbf{n}=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\},\quad\mathbf{V}:=\{{\bf{v}}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)^{d}:\ \nabla\cdot{\bf{v}}=0\},\quad\mathbf{V}_{2}:=H^{2}(\Omega)^{d}\cap\mathbf{V},

where by
{\bf{v}}\cdot\mathbf{n}
we denote the generalized normal trace.

We denote by
\mathbf{V}^{*}
the topological dual space of
\mathbf{V}
, and define

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}

\displaystyle:=L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V})\cap H^{1}(I;\mathbf{V}^{*})\hookrightarrow C(\bar{I};{\mathbf{V}_{0}}),

\displaystyle\mathbf{X}

\displaystyle:=\{{\bf{v}}\in L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V}):\partial_{t}{\bf{v}}\in L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V}^{*})+L^{1}(I;\mathbf{V}_{0})\text{ and }{\bf{v}}(0)={\bf{0}}\},

\displaystyle\mathbf{Y}

\displaystyle:=L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V}^{*})+L^{1}(I;\mathbf{V}_{0})\quad\Rightarrow\quad\mathbf{Y}^{*}\cong L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V})\cap L^{\infty}(I;\mathbf{V}_{0})\quad\text{due to \eqref{eq:bochner_dual_isomorphism}.}

We denote by
\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)_{\Omega}
and
\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)_{I\times\Omega}
the inner products of
L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}
and

L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega)^{d})
respectively, and
by
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{I\times\Omega}
the duality pairing between
L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V})
and
L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V}^{*})
.
We introduce the Stokes operator
A
, defined by

A\colon D(A)\subset\mathbf{V}_{0}\to\mathbf{V}_{0},\ \left(A{\bf{u}},{\bf{v}}\right)_{\Omega}=\left(\nabla{\bf{u}},\nabla{\bf{v}}\right)_{\Omega}.

The
H^{2}
regularity results of
[
10
,
26
]
show that

D(A)=\mathbf{V}_{2}
. As
A
is a positive, selfadjoint operator, fractional powers
A^{s}
are well defined.
Of special importance is
A^{\frac{1}{2}}
which is an isometric isomorphism

A^{\frac{1}{2}}\colon D(A^{\frac{1}{2}})=\mathbf{V}\to\mathbf{V}_{0},

as it holds

\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}{\bf{u}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\left(A{\bf{u}},{\bf{u}}\right)_{\Omega}=\left(\nabla{\bf{u}},\nabla{\bf{u}}\right)_{\Omega}=\|\nabla{\bf{u}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}
.
For the proof of
D(A^{\frac{1}{2}})=\mathbf{V}
, see
[
35
, Ch. III, Lemma 2.2.1]
.
By its definition, we can extend
A
to an operator (denoted by the same symbol)
A\colon\mathbf{V}\to\mathbf{V}^{*}

yielding another isometric isomporphism between those spaces.
Lastly, as

\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}{\bf{u}}\|_{\mathbf{V}^{*}}=\sup_{{\bf{v}}\in\mathbf{V}}\dfrac{\left\langle A^{\frac{1}{2}}{\bf{u}},{\bf{v}}\right\rangle_{\Omega}}{\|{\bf{v}}\|_{\mathbf{V}}}=\sup_{{\bf{w}}\in\mathbf{V}_{0}}\dfrac{\left\langle A^{\frac{1}{2}}{\bf{u}},A^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\bf{w}}\right\rangle_{\Omega}}{\|A^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\bf{w}}\|_{\mathbf{V}}}=\sup_{{\bf{w}}\in\mathbf{V}_{0}}\dfrac{\left({\bf{u}},{\bf{w}}\right)_{\Omega}}{\|A^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\bf{w}}\|_{\mathbf{V}}}=\sup_{{\bf{w}}\in\mathbf{V}_{0}}\dfrac{\left({\bf{u}},{\bf{w}}\right)_{\Omega}}{\|{\bf{w}}\|_{\mathbf{V}_{0}}}=\|{\bf{u}}\|_{\mathbf{V}_{0}},

there holds the isometric isomorphism

A^{\frac{1}{2}}\colon\mathbf{V}_{0}\to\mathbf{V}^{*}.

(2.2)

Using the above defined function spaces, the weak formulation of the state equation (
1.1b
)
for a given
{\bf{q}}\in L^{1}(I;L^{2}(\Omega)^{d})+L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V}^{*})
reads as follows: Find
{\bf{u}}\in{\mathbf{X}}
such that

\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{t}{\bf{u}},{\bf{v}}\right\rangle_{\Omega}+\left({\rm NewA}{\bf{u}},{\rm NewA}{\bf{v}}\right)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=\left({\bf{q}},{\bf{v}}\right)_{\Omega}\quad{\text{for all }{\bf{v}}\in\mathbf{V},\ \text{a.e. in }I}.

(2.3)

For the above weak formulation, there holds the following result.

Theorem 2.1
.

For
{\bf{q}}\in L^{1}(I;{L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}})+L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V}^{*})
, there exists a unique solution

{\bf{u}}\in L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V})\cap C(\bar{I};\mathbf{V}_{0})
solving (
2.3
) and the following estimate holds

\|{\bf{u}}\|_{L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V})}+\|{\bf{u}}\|_{L^{\infty}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C\|{\bf{q}}\|_{L^{1}(I;\mathbf{V}_{0})+L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V}^{*})}.

If
{\bf{q}}\in L^{2}(I;{L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}})
, then

{\bf{u}}\in L^{2}(I;{\mathbf{V}_{2}})\cap H^{1}(I;{\mathbf{V}_{0}})\hookrightarrow C(\bar{I};\mathbf{V})
, and there holds

\|{\bf{u}}\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{2}(\Omega))}+\|{\bf{u}}\|_{L^{\infty}(I;\mathbf{V})}+\|\partial_{t}{\bf{u}}\|_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}L^{2}(I\times\Omega)}}\leq C\|{\bf{q}}\|_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}L^{2}(I\times\Omega)}}.

Proof.

The first part of this theorem is proven in
[
36
, Chapter III, Theorem 1.1]

and the remark on page 179 therein.
The
H^{2}
regularity part can be shown as in
[
36
, Chapter III, Proposition 1.2]

using the
H^{2}
regularity result for the stationary Stokes equations in convex
polygonal/polyhedral domains, proven in
[
10
, Theorem 5.5, Theorem 6.3]

see also
[
26
, Theorem 2]
.
∎

It is a classical result, that the Stokes operator in the Hilbert space setting exhibits maximal parabolic
regularity, i.e.

{\bf{q}}\in L^{p}(I;\mathbf{V}_{0}),1<p<\infty\quad\Rightarrow\quad{\bf{u}}\text{ solving \eqref{eq:weak_state} satisfies }\partial_{t}{\bf{u}},A{\bf{u}}\in L^{p}(I;\mathbf{V}_{0}),

(2.4)

see
[
4
, Proposition 2.6]
,
holding on fairly general domains, e.g. Lipschitz domains. If
\Omega
is convex, the
H^{2}
regularity results
further imply that
{\bf{u}}\in L^{p}(I;\mathbf{V}_{2})
.
Most often maximal parabolic regularity is treated in
L^{2}
or
L^{q}
setting in space,
but it can also be extended
to settings of weaker spacial regularity. The property (
2.2
) combined with

[
3
, Lemma 11.4]
yields that maximal parabolic regularity also holds in
\mathbf{V}^{*}
, i.e.

{\bf{q}}\in L^{p}(I;\mathbf{V}^{*}),1<p<\infty\quad\Rightarrow\quad{\bf{u}}\text{ solving \eqref{eq:weak_state} satisfies }\partial_{t}{\bf{u}}\in L^{p}(I;\mathbf{V}^{*}),{\bf{u}}\in L^{p}(I;\mathbf{V}).

(2.5)

If the right hand side
{\bf{q}}
is regular enough, there exists an associated pressure to the
weak solution of (
2.3
), formulated in divergence free spaces.
The regularity of the pressure depends on the regularity of the right hand side
and the velocity component of the solution. There holds the following result,
see
[
4
, Theorem 2.10, Corollary 2.11]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
{\bf{q}}\in L^{s}(I;L^{2}(\Omega)^{d})
for some
1<s<\infty
and let

{\bf{u}}\in L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V})\cap{C(\bar{I};\mathbf{V}_{0})}
be the weak solution to
(
2.3
). Then there exists a unique
p\in L^{s}(I;L^{2}_{0}(\Omega))
such that

\partial_{t}{\bf{u}}-\Delta{\bf{u}}+\nabla p={\bf{q}},

(2.6)

which is to be understood as an identity in
L^{s}(I;H^{-1}(\Omega)^{d})
.
On convex domains
\Omega
, the pressure satisfies
p\in L^{s}(I;H^{1}(\Omega))
.

3
Continuous Optimal Control Problem

We now introduce the control to state mapping.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
S\colon{{\bf{q}}}\mapsto{{\bf{u}}}
denote the solution operator for the state equation
(
2.3
). Then
S
is a bounded linear operator between the following spaces;

•

S\colon L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V}^{*})\to\mathbf{W}\hookrightarrow C(\bar{I};L^{2}(\Omega)^{d})

•

S\colon L^{1}(I;\mathbf{V}_{0})\to C(\bar{I};L^{2}(\Omega)^{d})

•

S\colon L^{\infty}(I;L^{2}(\Omega)^{d})\to W^{1,s}(I;L^{2}(\Omega)^{d})\cap L^{s}(I;H^{2}(\Omega)^{d})
,
1\leq s<\infty
.

Proof.

The first two claims are the direct consequences of
Theorem

2.1
. Note, that
the regularity
\partial_{t}{\bf{u}}\in L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V}^{*})
can only be obtained by bootstrapping if the right hand side
is in
L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V}^{*})
. The third claim is obtained by using the maximal parabolic regularity
and
H^{2}
regularity of the stationary Stokes problem on convex polygonal/polyhedral domains, see

[
10
, Theorem 5.5, Theorem 6.3]
.
∎

To abbreviate the notation, we will frequently use
{\bf{u}}({\bf{q}}):=S({\bf{q}})
.
Since
S
is a bounded linear operator between the spaces introduced in the previous theorem,
it is Fréchet differentiable, and its directional derivative in direction
{\bf{\delta q}}
satisfies

S^{\prime}({\bf{q}})({\bf{\delta q}})=S({\bf{\delta q}}),

i.e. is independent of
{\bf{q}}
.

Remark 3.2
.

The operator
S
is linear and coincides with its Frechét derivative,
due to our choice to work with homogeneous inital data
in the state equation (
1.1b
). The results presented in this work however also
hold true in the inhomogeneous initial data case. In this case, the
assumption
\beta>0

, on the parameter
\beta
of the state constraint,

then has to be generalized to
\beta>\left({\bf{u}}_{0},{\bf{w}}\right)_{\Omega}
.

Let us next give a characterization of the adjoint operator
S^{*}
.

Theorem

2.1
,
together with
[
6
, Proposition V.1.3]
,
yields that for any
{\bf{q}}\in{\mathbf{Y}}
, there
exists a unique solution
{\bf{u}}\in\mathbf{X}
such that

a({\bf{u}},{\bf{v}}):=\left\langle\partial_{t}{\bf{u}},{\bf{v}}\right\rangle_{I\times\Omega}+\left(\nabla{\bf{u}},\nabla{\bf{v}}\right)_{I\times\Omega}=\left\langle{\bf{q}},{\bf{v}}\right\rangle_{I\times\Omega}\qquad\forall{\bf{v}}\in{\mathbf{Y}^{*}},

(3.1)

and the two formulations (
2.3
) and (
3.1
) are
equivalent.
The solution operator
S
is the inverse of the operator
T\colon\mathbf{X}\to\mathbf{Y}
,

\langle T{\bf{u}},{\bf{v}}\rangle_{\mathbf{Y}\times\mathbf{Y}^{*}}=a({\bf{u}},{\bf{v}})
.
As
T
is invertible, so is
T^{*}
and its inverse is precisely
S^{*}
.
With this construction, the adjoint operator
S^{*}:\mathbf{g}\mapsto{\bf{z}}
,
corresponds to the following weak formulation:
given
\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{X}^{*}
, find
{\bf{z}}\in{\mathbf{Y}^{*}}
satisfying

\left\langle\partial_{t}{\bf{v}},{\bf{z}}\right\rangle_{I\times\Omega}+\left(\nabla{\bf{v}},\nabla{\bf{z}}\right)_{I\times\Omega}=\left(\mathbf{g},{\bf{v}}\right)_{I\times\Omega}\quad\forall{\bf{v}}\in\mathbf{X}.

(3.2)

We now discuss the regularity of the adjoint operator.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
S^{*}
denote the adjoint operator to the solution operator introduced in
Theorem

3.1
. Then it satisfies

S^{*}\colon\mathcal{M}(\bar{I};L^{2}(\Omega)^{d})\to L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V})\cap L^{\infty}(I;\mathbf{V}_{0}).

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of the definition of the adjoint operator,
Theorem

3.1
,
and the isomorphism
(L^{1}(I;\mathbf{V}_{0}))^{*}\cong L^{\infty}(I;\mathbf{V}_{0})
, due to (
2.1
).
∎

Due to the linearity of the adjoint operator, it again holds

S^{*}({\bf{\delta q}})=(S^{\prime}({\bf{q}}))^{*}({\bf{\delta q}}).

For convenience, for the weight
{\bf w}\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}
,
we define the functional
G_{\bf w}\colon L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
by

G_{\bf w}({\bf{v}}):=({\bf{v}},{\bf w})_{\Omega}.

(3.3)

For time-dependent functions
{\bf{v}}\colon I\to L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}
, the application of
G_{{\bf w}}
is defined by

G_{\bf w}({\bf{v}})(t):=G_{\bf w}({\bf{v}}(t)).

Using the functional
G_{\bf w}
, the state constraint (
1.1d
) can be compactly rewritten as

G_{\bf w}({\bf{u}})\leq\beta\quad\text{in}\ \bar{I}.

(3.4)

Remark 3.4
.

Due to the continuous embedding
\mathbf{W}\hookrightarrow C(\bar{I};L^{2}(\Omega)^{d})
, we have
G_{\bf w}({\bf{v}})(\cdot)\in C(\bar{I})
for any
{\bf{v}}\in\mathbf{W}
. Thus we can understand
G_{\bf w}
as a linear, continuous operator
from
\mathbf{W}
to
C(\bar{I})
.

To write the optimal control problem (
1.1a
)-(
1.1d
) into
reduced form, we define
\mathcal{G}:=G_{\bf w}\circ S
and the closed convex cone

\mathcal{K}\subset C(\bar{I})
by

\mathcal{K}:=\{v\in C(\bar{I}):\ v\leq\beta\ \text{in}\ \bar{I}\}.

Using the above definitions, the reduced form reads

\text{Minimize }\ j({\bf{q}}):=J({\bf{q}},S({\bf{q}}))\quad\text{for}\quad{\bf{q}}\in\mathbf{Q}_{ad}\quad\text{subject to}\quad\mathcal{G}({\bf{q}})\in\mathcal{K},

(3.5)

where the admissible set is given by

\mathbf{Q}_{ad}=\{{\bf{q}}\in L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}):\quad{\bf{q}}_{a}\leq{\bf{q}}(t,x)\leq{\bf{q}}_{b}\quad\text{a.e. in }I\times\Omega\}.

We define the projection operator onto the feasible set by

P_{[{\bf{q}}_{a},{\bf{q}}_{b}]}({\bf{q}}):=\min\{{\bf{q}}_{b},\max\{{\bf{q}},{\bf{q}}_{a}\}\},

which we understand componentwise and pointwise for every
(t,x)\in I\times\Omega
.
Throughout the paper, we will work under the following assumption.

Assumption 1

(Slater condition)
.

There exists
\tilde{{\bf{q}}}\in\mathbf{Q}_{ad}
such that
G_{\bf w}({\bf{u}}(\tilde{{\bf{q}}}))<\beta
for
all
t\in\bar{I}
, where

{\bf{u}}(\tilde{{\bf{q}}})
is the solution of the weak transient Stokes problem (
2.3
) for this
particular control
\tilde{{\bf{q}}}
.

Remark 3.5
.

As the homogeneous initial data in our setting necessitate the choice
\beta>0
, if the control
constraints
{\bf{q}}_{a},{\bf{q}}_{b}
admit the control
\tilde{\bf{q}}\equiv{\bf{0}}
, the existence of such a Slater point is
immediately given.
In that case, the unique solution to the state equation with right hand side
\tilde{\bf{q}}\equiv{\bf{0}}
is

{\bf{u}}(\tilde{\bf{q}})\equiv{\bf{0}}
which trivially satisfies
G_{\bf{w}}({\bf{u}}(\tilde{\bf{q}}))=0<\beta
.

Theorem 3.6
.

Under
Assumption

1
, there exists a unique optimal control
\bar{\bf{q}}\in L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega)^{d})

with unique associated state
\bar{\bf{u}}
, solving the optimal control problem
(
1.1a
)-(
1.1d
).

Proof.

The
Assumption

1
yields the existence of a feasible
\tilde{\bf{q}}
, such that the associated state

\tilde{\bf{u}}={\bf{u}}(\tilde{\bf{q}})
satisfies the state constraint. Let us define

\mathcal{J}:=\inf\{J({\bf{q}},{\bf{u}})\colon{\bf{q}}\text{ and ${\bf{u}}$ satisfy \eqref{eq:state_equation}-\eqref{eq:state_constraint}}\}{\geq 0},

and let
\{{\bf{q}}_{n}\}
and
\{{\bf{u}}_{n}:={\bf{u}}({\bf{q}}_{n})\}
denote sequences of feasible controls with associated
states, such that
J({\bf{q}}_{n},{\bf{u}}_{n})\to\mathcal{J}
as
n\to\infty
.
As it holds
J({\bf{q}}_{n},{\bf{u}}_{n})\leq{\mathcal{J}+1}
for large enough
n
, there holds a bound

\|{\bf{q}}_{n}\|_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}L^{2}(I\times\Omega)}}\leq C
for all
n
.
From
Theorem

3.1
, we obtain

\|{\bf{u}}_{n}\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{2}(\Omega))}+\|{\bf{u}}_{n}\|_{H^{1}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C
.
We can thus take a subsequence, denoted by the same index, such that

{\bf{q}}_{n}\rightharpoonup\hat{\bf{q}}\text{ in }L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}),\quad{\bf{u}}_{n}\rightharpoonup\hat{\bf{u}}\text{ in }L^{2}(I;H^{2}(\Omega)^{d}),\quad\partial_{t}{\bf{u}}_{n}\rightharpoonup\partial_{t}\hat{\bf{u}}\text{ in }L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}).

These allow us to pass to the limit in the weak form of the state equation, showing that

\hat{\bf{u}}={\bf{u}}(\hat{\bf{q}})
. Furthermore, as

L^{2}(I;H^{2}(\Omega)^{d})\cap H^{1}(I;L^{2}(\Omega)^{d})\hookrightarrow C({\bar{I}};H^{s}(\Omega)^{d})
compactly,
for
s<1
, see
[
34
, Corollary 8]
, by taking another subsequence, we obtain

{\bf{u}}_{n}\to\hat{\bf{u}}
in
C({\bar{I}};L^{2}(\Omega)^{d})
.
As
G_{\bf{w}}({\bf{u}}_{n})\leq\beta
for all
t\in I
, this
shows
G_{\bf{w}}(\hat{\bf{u}})\leq\beta
for all
t\in I
. Lastly, it holds due to the lower semicontinuity of
the norms

which shows that
\bar{\bf{q}}=\hat{\bf{q}}
is indeed a minimizer with associated state
\bar{\bf{u}}=\hat{\bf{u}}
.
Using uniform convexity of the squared
L^{2}
norms and linearity of the state equation gives the uniqueness.
∎

The above result shows the regularity available for the optimal control

without any additional

{\bf{w}}
of the

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
\mu\in C(\bar{I})^{*}
and
{\bf{w}}\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}
be given,
and let
{\bf{z}}\in L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V})\cap L^{\infty}(I;\mathbf{V}_{0})

be the weak solution to

\left\langle\partial_{t}{\bf{v}},{\bf{z}}\right\rangle_{I\times\Omega}+\left(\nabla{\bf{v}},\nabla{\bf{z}}\right)_{I\times\Omega}=\left\langle{\bf{v}},{\bf{w}}\mu\right\rangle_{I\times\Omega}\quad\text{for all }{\bf{v}}\in\mathbf{X}.

(3.8)

Then, if additionally
{\bf{w}}\in\mathbf{V}
, it holds
{\bf{z}}\in L^{s}(I;\mathbf{V})\cap\mathrm{BV}(I;\mathbf{V}^{*})

for all
1\leq s<\infty
.
Moreover, if
{\bf{w}}\in\mathbf{V}_{2}
, it holds
{\bf{z}}\in L^{s}(I;\mathbf{V}_{2})\cap\mathrm{BV}(I;\mathbf{V}_{0})
for all
1\leq s<\infty
.

Proof.

Throughout this proof, let
{\bf{w}}
satisfy at least
{\bf{w}}\in\mathbf{V}
.
Using
[
1
, Theorem 4.31]
, there exists a normalized function
of bounded variation
\tilde{\mu}\in\mathrm{NBV}(\bar{I})
, such that
the application of
\mu\in C(\bar{I})^{*}
to any
\xi\in C(\bar{I})

can be expressed as a Riemann-Stieltjes integral

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\left\langle\xi,\mu\right\rangle_{I}}=\int_{I}\xi(t)\ \mathrm{d}\tilde{\mu}(t).

Slightly modifying
[
1
, Definition 1.2]
, we can normalize
\tilde{\mu}

such that it is of bounded variation, right continuous and satisfies
\tilde{\mu}(T)=0
.
If
\xi\in C^{1}(\bar{I})
, due to
[
1
, Proposition 4.24, Theorem 4.17]
, there holds

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\left\langle\xi,\mu\right\rangle_{I}}=-\int_{I}\tilde{\mu}(t)\ \mathrm{d}\xi(t)+\tilde{\mu}(T)\xi(T)-\tilde{\mu}(0)\xi(0)=-\int_{I}\tilde{\mu}(t)\partial_{t}\xi(t)\ \mathrm{d}t-\tilde{\mu}(0)\xi(0)

where the last integral can be understood equivalently in the Riemann or Lebesgue sense.
Working with the Lebesque integral allows us to pass to the limit

C^{1}(\bar{I})\ni\xi_{n}\to\xi\in W^{1,1}(I)\hookrightarrow C(\bar{I})
, showing that

\left\langle\xi,\mu\right\rangle_{I}=-\left(\tilde{\mu},\partial_{t}\xi\right)_{I}-\tilde{\mu}(0)\xi(0)\qquad\forall\xi\in W^{1,1}(I).

As due to the definition of
\mathbf{X}
, it holds

\partial_{t}{\bf{v}}\in L^{1}(I;\mathbf{V}_{0})+L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V}^{*})

and
{\bf{v}}(0)={\bf{0}}
for all
{\bf{v}}\in\mathbf{X}
, and since
{\bf{w}}\in\mathbf{V}
, we have
\left\langle{\bf{v}},{\bf{w}}\right\rangle_{\Omega}\in W^{1,1}(I)
.
Thus we obtain

\left\langle{\bf{v}},{\bf{w}}\mu\right\rangle_{I\times\Omega}=-\left\langle\tilde{\mu}(t){\bf{w}},\partial_{t}{\bf{v}}\right\rangle_{I\times\Omega}.

(3.9)

Let us define
\hat{\bf{z}}={\bf{z}}+\tilde{\mu}{\bf{w}}
. Then adding
(
3.8
), (
3.9
)
and the identity

\left(\nabla{\bf{v}},\tilde{\mu}\nabla{\bf{w}}\right)_{I\times\Omega}=\left\langle\tilde{\mu}A{\bf{w}},{\bf{v}}\right\rangle_{I\times\Omega}
yields

\left\langle\partial_{t}{\bf{v}},\hat{\bf{z}}\right\rangle_{I\times\Omega}+\left(\nabla{\bf{v}},\nabla\hat{\bf{z}}\right)_{I\times\Omega}=\left\langle\tilde{\mu}A{\bf{w}},{\bf{v}}\right\rangle_{I\times\Omega}\quad\forall{\bf{v}}\in\mathbf{X}.

As
\tilde{\mu}\in L^{\infty}(I)
and
\tilde{\mu}(T)<\infty
,
\hat{\bf{z}}
satisfies a backwards in time
Stokes equation with right hand side
\tilde{\mu}A{\bf{w}}
.
Depending on the regularity of
{\bf{w}}
, we obtain the following:

Case 1:

{\bf{w}}\in\mathbf{V}
. Here
\tilde{\mu}{\bf{w}}\in\mathrm{BV}(I;\mathbf{V})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(I;\mathbf{V})

and thus
\tilde{\mu}A{\bf{w}}\in L^{\infty}(I;\mathbf{V}^{*})
. Using the maximal parabolic regularity in
\mathbf{V}^{*}
(
2.5
), this yields

\hat{\bf{z}}\in W^{1,s}(I;\mathbf{V}^{*})\cap L^{s}(I;\mathbf{V})\quad\text{for any }1\leq s<\infty.

Considering the special cases
s=1
and
s=2
, we obtain

\hat{\bf{z}}\in W^{1,1}(I;\mathbf{V}^{*})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{BV}(I;\mathbf{V}^{*})\quad\text{and}\quad\hat{\bf{z}}\in H^{1}(I;\mathbf{V}^{*})\cap L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V})\hookrightarrow C(\bar{I};\mathbf{V}_{0}).

As a consequence
{\bf{z}}=\hat{\bf{z}}-\tilde{\mu}{\bf{w}}\in L^{s}(I;\mathbf{V})\cap\mathrm{BV}(I;\mathbf{V}^{*})
for any

1\leq s<\infty
.

Case 2:

{\bf{w}}\in\mathbf{V}_{2}
. Here
\tilde{\mu}{\bf{w}}\in\mathrm{BV}(I;\mathbf{V}_{2})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(I;\mathbf{V}_{2})

and thus
\tilde{\mu}A{\bf{w}}\in L^{\infty}(I;\mathbf{V}_{0})
. Using the maximal parabolic regularity in
\mathbf{V}_{0}
(
2.4
), this yields

\hat{\bf{z}}\in W^{1,s}(I;\mathbf{V}_{0})\cap L^{s}(I;\mathbf{V}_{2})\quad\text{for any }1\leq s<\infty.

Considering the special cases
s=1
and
s=2
, we obtain

\hat{\bf{z}}\in W^{1,1}(I;\mathbf{V}_{0})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{BV}(I;\mathbf{V}_{0})\quad\text{and}\quad\hat{\bf{z}}\in H^{1}(I;\mathbf{V}_{0})\cap L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V}_{2})\hookrightarrow C(\bar{I};\mathbf{V}).

As a consequence
{\bf{z}}=\hat{\bf{z}}-\tilde{\mu}{\bf{w}}\in L^{s}(I;\mathbf{V}_{2})\cap\mathrm{BV}(I;\mathbf{V}_{0})
for any

1\leq s<\infty
.
∎

Since in the above proof we only have shown continuity of
\hat{\bf{z}}
, but

in general can
have jumps as it is a

function, without further information, we cannot deduce continuity of

. For the adjoint equation (

and by (

If in addition to the arguments of the previous theorem, we exploit the information from the

As the adjoint state in the optimality system

in time, we might lose some of the time regularity that we just derived.

Theorem 3.10
.

Proof.

As in the proof of the previous theorem, we introduce the
\mathrm{NBV}

\tilde{\mu}
satisfying

\hat{\bf{z}}=\bar{\bf{z}}+\tilde{\mu}{\bf{w}}

The regularity
\bar{\bf{u}}\in L^{\infty}(I;\mathbf{V}_{0})

and the previous

\displaystyle{\bf{w}}\in\mathbf{V}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\hat{\bf{z}}\in W^{1,s}(I;\mathbf{V}^{*})\cap L^{s}(I;\mathbf{V})\hookrightarrow C(\bar{I};\mathbf{V}_{0})\cap\mathrm{BV}(I;\mathbf{V}^{*})\ \text{ and }\ \bar{\bf{z}}\in L^{s}(I;\mathbf{V}),

As in

3.9

\bar{\bf{z}}
.
To show the regularity of
\bar{\bf{q}}
, we shall make use of the optimality conditions.
The optimal control satisfies

\bar{\bf{z}}

\bar{\bf{q}}
.

We now turn towards showing continuity in time of
\bar{\bf{q}}
.

[\cdot]
the jump function w.r.t. time, i.e.
[\varphi](t)=\varphi(t+)-\varphi(t-)

Since functions in

.
By distinguishing the different cases, it is straightforward to verify,

Applying this chain of inequalities to

\bar{\bf{q}}=P_{[{\bf{q}}_{a},{\bf{q}}_{b}]}({\bf{z}}_{\alpha})

t\in I

As

Moreover it holds

\displaystyle\bar{\mu}(\{t^{*}\})

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\bar{\mu}((t^{*}-\varepsilon,t^{*}+\varepsilon))=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left\langle\chi_{(t^{*}-\varepsilon,t^{*}+\varepsilon)},\bar{\mu}\right\rangle_{I}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{(t^{*}-\varepsilon,t^{*}+\varepsilon)}1\mathrm{d}\tilde{\mu}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}-\int_{(t^{*}-\varepsilon,t^{*}+\varepsilon)}\tilde{\mu}\ \mathrm{d}1+\tilde{\mu}(t^{*}+\varepsilon)-\tilde{\mu}(t^{*}-\varepsilon)=[\tilde{\mu}](t^{*}).

(3.13)

where the last inequality holds due to (

Combining this with (

,
which shows

.
If additionally
{\bf{w}}\in\mathbf{V}_{2}
, with the same arguments we can show
\bar{\bf{q}}\in C({\bar{I}};H^{1}(\Omega)^{d})
.
∎

Remark 3.11
.

In the proof of the above theorem, we can under some circumstances also show continuity in time of

\bar{\bf{z}}
. In fact, the only obstacle preventing this result, is the application of the projection
operator onto the control constraints
P_{[{\bf{q}}_{a},{\bf{q}}_{b}]}(\cdot)
. It is in general possible, that at some
point in time
\hat{t}
,
\bar{\bf{z}}
is discontinuous. In such cases, the spacial support of the jump
is contained in the set of points
x\in\Omega
, where
-\frac{1}{\alpha}\bar{\bf{z}}(\hat{t}\pm)

lie outside the set of admissible controls
[{\bf{q}}_{a},{\bf{q}}_{b}]
.
As the jump of
\bar{\bf{z}}
is a scalar multiple of
{\bf{w}}
, this gives a compatibility condition
on
\supp({\bf{w}})
and the set where the control constraints are active.
As the active set of the control constraints cannot be known a priori, such a condition on
\bar{\bf{q}}

is not straightforward to verify. Instead we present in the next two corollaries two
sets of assumptions on the data, where we can obtain improved regularity of
\bar{\bf{z}}
and
\bar{\mu}
.

Corollary 3.12
.

Let
(\bar{\bf{q}},\bar{\bf{u}},\bar{\bf{z}},\bar{\mu})
satisfy the first order necessary optimality conditions
(
3.6a
)-(
3.6d
).
Let further
{\bf{q}}_{a,i}=-\infty
,
{\bf{q}}_{b,i}=+\infty
for
i=1,...,d
, and
{\bf{w}}\in\mathbf{V}
.
Then
\bar{\bf{z}}\in C({\bar{I}};\mathbf{V}_{0})
and
\bar{\mu}(\{t\})=0
for all

t\in I
, i.e.
\bar{\mu}
does not contain
any Dirac contributions.
If additionally
{\bf{w}}\in\mathbf{V}_{2}
, then
\bar{\bf{z}}\in C({\bar{I}};\mathbf{V})
.

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of the results of
Theorem

3.10
, as
in this case, the identity
\bar{\bf{q}}=-\frac{1}{\alpha}\bar{\bf{z}}
holds.
∎

Corollary 3.13
.

Let
(\bar{\bf{q}},\bar{\bf{u}},\bar{\bf{z}},\bar{\mu})
satisfy the first order necessary optimality conditions
(
3.6a
)-(
3.6d
).
Let further
{\bf{q}}_{a}<\bf{0}<{\bf{q}}_{b}
,
{\bf{u}}_{d}\in L^{s}(I;L^{2}(\Omega)^{d})
for some
s>2
, and
{\bf{w}}\in\mathbf{V}_{2}

satisfy

\exists x^{*}\in\partial\Omega\text{ s.th. }\forall\varepsilon>0\text{ it holds }\supp({\bf{w}})\cap B_{\varepsilon}(x^{*})\neq\emptyset.

Then
\bar{\bf{z}}\in C({\bar{I}};\mathbf{V})
and
\bar{\mu}(\{t\})=0
for all
t\in I
, i.e.
\bar{\mu}
does not contain
any Dirac contributions.

Proof.

As
{\bf{u}}_{d}\in L^{s}(I;L^{2}(\Omega)^{d})
for some
s>2
, as in the beginning of the proof of

Theorem

3.10
, we obtain

\hat{\bf{z}}\in W^{1,s}(I;\mathbf{V}_{0})\cap L^{s}(I;\mathbf{V}_{2})\hookrightarrow C({\bar{I}};C(\bar{\Omega})),

where the last embedding holds due to
[
34
, Corollary 8]
.
As further
{\bf{w}}\in\mathbf{V}_{2}\hookrightarrow C(\bar{\Omega})
,
for any
t^{*}\in I
it thus holds
\bar{\bf{z}}(t^{*}+),\bar{\bf{z}}(t^{*}-)\in C(\bar{\Omega})
.
As
\bar{\bf{z}}(t^{*}+)(x^{*})=\bar{\bf{z}}(t^{*}-)(x^{*})={\bf{0}}
, and
{\bf{q}}_{a}<{\bf{0}}<{\bf{q}}_{b}
, there exists

\delta>0
, such that

-\frac{1}{\alpha}\bar{\bf{z}}(t^{*}+)(x),-\frac{1}{\alpha}\bar{\bf{z}}(t^{*}-)(x)\in({\bf{q}}_{a},{\bf{q}}_{b})\quad\text{for all }x\in B_{\delta}(x^{*})\cap\bar{\Omega}.

Thus
[\bar{\bf{q}}](t^{*})|_{B_{\delta}(x^{*})}=[-\frac{1}{\alpha}\bar{\bf{z}}](t^{*})|_{B_{\delta}(x^{*})}
.
As
\supp({\bf{w}})\cap B_{\delta}(x^{*})\neq\emptyset
, there exists an open subset

\omega\subset B_{\delta}(x^{*})
, such that
{\bf{w}}(x)\neq 0
for all
x\in\omega
.
In the end, we obtain, using the continuity in time of
\bar{\bf{q}}
shown in

Theorem

3.10
:

0=\|[\bar{\bf{q}}](t^{*})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\geq\int_{\omega}[\bar{\bf{q}}](t^{*})^{2}\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\omega}\left(\left[-\frac{1}{\alpha}\bar{\bf{z}}\right](t^{*})\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\omega}\frac{[\tilde{\mu}](t^{*})^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}|{\bf{w}}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\geq 0.

As
|{\bf{w}}|>0
for all
x\in\omega
, this shows
[\tilde{\mu}](t^{*})=0
.
As
t^{*}
was arbitrary, this concludes the proof.
∎

4
Finite Element Approximation of the State Equation

4.1
Spatial discretization

Let
\{\mathcal{T}_{h}\}
be a
family of triangulations of
\bar{\Omega}
, consisting of closed simplices, where we denote by

h
the maximum mesh-size. Let
\mathbf{X}_{h}\subset H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)^{d}
and

M_{h}\subset L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
be a pair of compatible finite element spaces, i.e.,
there holds a uniform discrete inf-sup condition,

\sup_{{\bf{v}}_{h}\in\mathbf{X}_{h}}\frac{\left(q_{h},\nabla\cdot{\bf{v}}_{h}\right)_{\Omega}}{\lVert\nabla{\bf{v}}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\geq\gamma\|{q_{h}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\quad\forall q_{h}\in M_{h},

(4.1)

with a constant
\gamma>0
independent of
h
.
We shall work under the assumption that the discrete spaces have the following approximative properties.

Assumption 2
.

There exist interpolation operators
i_{h}\colon H^{2}(\Omega)^{d}\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)^{d}\to\mathbf{X}_{h}

and
r_{h}\colon L^{2}(\Omega)\to M_{h}
, such that

\displaystyle\|\nabla({\bf{v}}-i_{h}{\bf{v}})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq ch\|\nabla^{2}{\bf{v}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\hskip-85.35826pt\forall{\bf{v}}\in H^{2}(\Omega)^{2}\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)^{2},

\displaystyle\|q-r_{h}q\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq ch\|\nabla q\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\hskip-85.35826pt\forall q\in H^{1}(\Omega).

This assumption is valid, for example, for Taylor-Hood and MINI finite elements on shape regular meshes, see
[
4
, Assumption 7.2]
.
We define the space of discretely divergence-free vector fields
\mathbf{V}_{h}
as

\mathbf{V}_{h}=\Set{{\bf{v}}_{h}\in\mathbf{X}_{h}\colon(\nabla\cdot{\bf{v}}_{h},q_{h})=0\quad\forall q_{h}\in M_{h}}.

(4.2)

While on a computational level, especially for the examples presented in
Section

8
, we work
with a discrete velocity-pressure formulation, in our theoretical analysis we will always use the
equivalent formulation in discretely divergence free spaces, in order to shorten notation.
One exception is the following stationary Stokes problem
of finding for some given
f\in H^{-1}(\Omega)^{d}

a solution
({\bf{u}},p)\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)^{d}\times L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
to

\left(\nabla{\bf{u}},\nabla{\bf{v}}\right)_{\Omega}-\left(\nabla\cdot{\bf{v}},p\right)_{\Omega}+\left(\nabla\cdot{\bf{u}},q\right)_{\Omega}=\left({\bf{f}},{\bf{v}}\right)_{\Omega}\quad\forall({\bf{v}},q)\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)^{d}\times L^{2}_{0}(\Omega).

(4.3)

Its discrete approximation in velocity-pressure formulation reads:
Find
({\bf{u}}_{h},p_{h})\in\mathbf{X}_{h}\times M_{h}
satisfying

\left(\nabla{\bf{u}}_{h},\nabla{\bf{v}}_{h}\right)_{\Omega}-\left(\nabla\cdot{\bf{v}}_{h},p_{h}\right)_{\Omega}+\left(\nabla\cdot{\bf{u}}_{h},q_{h}\right)_{\Omega}=\left({\bf{f}},{\bf{v}}_{h}\right)_{\Omega}\quad\forall({\bf{v}}_{h},q_{h})\in\mathbf{X}_{h}\times M_{h}.

(4.4)

The above discrete system can be interpreted as a Stokes Ritz projection: given

({\bf{u}},p)\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)^{d}\times L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
, find

(R_{h}^{S}({\bf{u}},p),R_{h}^{S,p}({\bf{u}},p)):=({\bf{u}}_{h},p_{h})\in\mathbf{X}_{h}\times M_{h}
, satisfying

\left(\nabla({\bf{u}}-{\bf{u}}_{h}),\nabla{\bf{v}}_{h}\right)_{\Omega}-\left(\nabla\cdot{\bf{v}}_{h},(p-p_{h})\right)_{\Omega}+\left(\nabla\cdot({\bf{u}}-{\bf{u}}_{h}),q_{h}\right)_{\Omega}=0\quad\forall({\bf{v}}_{h},q_{h})\in\mathbf{X}_{h}\times M_{h}.

(4.5)

Note that, if
{\bf{u}}\in\mathbf{V}
, then it holds
R_{h}^{S}({\bf{u}},p)\in\mathbf{V}_{h}
.
Further, the Stokes Ritz projection satisfies the following stability,
see
[
5
, Theorem 5.2.1]
.

\|\nabla R_{h}^{S}({\bf{u}},p)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|R_{h}^{S,p}({\bf{u}},p)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C(\|\nabla{\bf{u}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|p\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}).

Let us recall the following error estimates for the stationary discrete Stokes problem:
See
[
5
, Theorem 5.25]
,

[
25
, Theorems 4.21, 4.25, 4.28]
,

[
16
, Theorems 53.17 & 53.19]
or

[
18
, Chapter II, Theorems 1.8 & 1.9]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
({\bf{u}},p)
and
({\bf{u}}_{h},p_{h})
denote the solutions to the continuous and discrete
stationary Stokes problems (
4.3
) and (
4.4
)
respectively. Then there holds the estimate

\|{\bf{u}}-{\bf{u}}_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\|p-p_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq Ch(\|{\bf{u}}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}+\|p\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}).

If
\Omega
is convex, there further holds the estimate

\|{\bf{u}}-{\bf{u}}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq Ch(\|{\bf{u}}-{\bf{u}}_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\|p-p_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}).

Note, that while
[
5
, Theorem 5.5.6]
contains formal results, on how to derive
error estimates also for
p
in a weaker norm, e.g.
H^{-1}(\Omega)
, the argument requires

H^{2}
regularity results for the compressible Stokes equations.
As the corresponding results in
[
26
,
10
]
require an additional
decaying condition for the compressibility data, this makes derivation of weaker error estimates for the
pressure complicated.

4.2
Temporal discretization: the discontinuous Galerkin method

In this section we introduce the discontinuous Galerkin method for the time discretization of the transient Stokes equations, a similar method was considered, e.g., in
[
9
]
.
For that, we partition
I=(0,T]
into subintervals
I_{m}=(t_{m-1},t_{m}]
of length
{k}_{m}=t_{m}-t_{m-1}
, where
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\dots<t_{M-1}<t_{M}=T
. The maximal and minimal time steps are denoted by
{k}=\max_{m}{k}_{m}
and
{k}_{\min}=\min_{m}{k}_{m}
, respectively. The time partition fulfills the following assumptions:

1.

There are constants
C,{\theta}>0
independent of
{k}
such that

{k}_{\min}\geq C{k}^{{\theta}}.

(4.6)

2.

There is a constant
\kappa>0
independent of
{k}
such that for all

m=1,2,\dots,M-1

\kappa^{-1}\leq\frac{{k}_{m}}{{k}_{m+1}}\leq\kappa.

(4.7)

3.

It holds
{k}\leq\frac{T}{4}
.

For a given Banach space
\mathcal{B}
, we define the semi-discrete space
X_{k}^{0}(\mathcal{B})
of piecewise
constant functions in time as

X_{k}^{0}(\mathcal{B})=\Set{{\bf{v}}_{k}\in L^{2}(I;\mathcal{B})\colon{\bf{v}}_{k}|_{I_{m}}\equiv{\bf{v}}_{m}\text{ for some }{\bf{v}}_{m}\in\mathcal{B},m=1,2,\dots,M}.

(4.8)

We use the following standard notation for a function
{\bf{u}}\in X_{k}^{r}(\mathcal{B})

to denote one-sided limits and jumps at the time nodes

{\bf{u}}_{m}^{+}=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}{\bf{u}}(t_{m}+\varepsilon),\quad{\bf{u}}_{m}^{-}=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}{\bf{u}}(t_{m}-\varepsilon),\quad[{\bf{u}}]_{m}={\bf{u}}_{m}^{+}-{\bf{u}}_{m}^{-}.

(4.9)

We define the bilinear form
{B}(\cdot,\cdot)
by

{B}({\bf{u}},{\bf{v}})={\sum_{m=1}^{M}(\partial_{t}{\bf{u}},{\bf{v}})_{I_{m}\times\Omega}}+(\nabla{\bf{u}},\nabla{\bf{v}})_{I\times\Omega}+\sum_{m=2}^{M}([{\bf{u}}]_{m-1},{\bf{v}}^{+}_{m-1})_{\Omega}+({\bf{u}}_{0}^{+},{\bf{v}}_{0}^{+})_{\Omega}.

With this bilinear form, we define the fully discrete approximation for the transient Stokes problem on the
discretely divergence free space
X_{k}^{0}(\mathbf{V}_{h})
:

{\bf{u}}_{kh}\in X_{k}^{0}(\mathbf{V}_{h})\;:\;{B}({\bf{u}}_{kh},{\bf{v}}_{kh})=({\bf{q}},{\bf{v}}_{kh})_{I\times\Omega}\quad\forall{\bf{v}}_{kh}\in X_{k}^{0}(\mathbf{V}_{h}).

(4.10)

The unique solution to this system is stable, as the following theorem summarizes:

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
{\bf{q}}\in L^{2}(I;H^{-1}(\Omega)^{d})+L^{1}(I;L^{2}(\Omega)^{d})
. Then there exists a unique solution

{\bf{u}}_{kh}\in X^{0}_{k}(\mathbf{V}_{h})
of (
4.10
), satsifying

\|{\bf{u}}_{kh}\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{1}(\Omega))}+\|{\bf{u}}_{kh}\|_{L^{\infty}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C\|{\bf{q}}\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{-1}(\Omega))+L^{1}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))},

where the constant
C>0
is independent of
k,h
.

Proof.

As (
4.10
) poses a square system of linear equations in finite dimensions,
it suffices to show uniqueness. This is a standard argument, making use of a discrete Gronwall Lemma,
see e.g.
[
38
, Theorem 4.13]
for a proof focusing especially on the

L^{1}(I;L^{2}(\Omega)^{d})
right hand side case.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

Rearranging terms in the definition of the bilinear form gives the following dual representation of
B(\cdot,\cdot)

B({\bf{u}},{\bf{v}})={-\sum_{m=1}^{M}({\bf{u}},\partial_{t}{\bf{v}})_{I_{m}\times\Omega}}+(\nabla{\bf{u}},\nabla{\bf{v}})_{I\times\Omega}-\sum_{m=1}^{M-1}({\bf{u}}_{m}^{-},[{\bf{v}}]_{m})_{\Omega}+({\bf{u}}_{M}^{-},{\bf{v}}_{M}^{-})_{\Omega}.

With the same arguments as above, for given
\mathbf{g}\in L^{2}(I;H^{-1}(\Omega)^{d})+L^{1}(I;L^{2}(\Omega)^{d})
,
solutions
{\bf{z}}_{kh}\in X^{0}_{k}(\mathbf{V}_{h})
to the discrete dual equation

B({\bf{v}}_{kh},{\bf{z}}_{kh})=\left(\mathbf{g},{\bf{v}}_{kh}\right)_{I\times\Omega}\quad\forall{\bf{v}}_{kh}\in X_{k}^{0}(\mathbf{V}_{h})

exist, are unique and satisfy the stability

\|{\bf{z}}_{kh}\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{1}(\Omega))}+\|{\bf{z}}_{kh}\|_{L^{\infty}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C\|\mathbf{g}\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{-1}(\Omega))+L^{1}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}.

4.3
Best approximation type fully discrete error estimate for the Stokes problem in
L^{\infty}(I;L^{2}(\Omega)^{d})
norm

In our recent paper

we have established a best approximation type error estimate for the Stokes problem in the

Theorem 4.4

and

, such that

\|{\bf{u}}-{\bf{u}}_{kh}\|_{L^{\infty}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C\ell_{k}\left(\|{\bf{u}}-\chi\|_{L^{\infty}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|{\bf{u}}-R^{S}_{h}({\bf{u}},p)\|_{L^{\infty}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}\right),

Using the error estimates for the stationary Stokes Ritz projection of

5
Variational Discretization of the Optimal Control Problem

In this section we consider the optimal control problem subject to the fully discretized Stokes equations.
We consider a variational discretization for the controls, i.e., do not fix a finite dimensional
approximation of the control space yet,

cf.,
[
15
,
21
]

The problem reads

\text{Minimize }\;J({\bf{q}}_{kh},{\bf{u}}_{kh})=\frac{1}{2}\lVert{\bf{u}}_{kh}-{\bf{u}}_{d}\rVert^{2}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}L^{2}(I\times\Omega)}}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\lVert{\bf{q}}_{kh}\rVert^{2}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}L^{2}(I\times\Omega)}}

(5.1a)

over all
{\bf{q}}_{kh}\in\mathbf{Q}_{ad}
,
{\bf{u}}_{kh}\in X^{0}_{k}(\mathbf{V}_{h})
, subject to

B({\bf{u}}_{kh},{\bf{v}}_{kh})=({\bf{q}}_{kh},{\bf{v}}_{kh})_{I\times\Omega}\quad\text{for all}\quad{\bf{v}}_{kh}\in X^{0}_{k}(\mathbf{V}_{h})

(5.1b)

G_{\bf{w}}({\bf{u}}_{kh})\mid_{I_{m}}\leq\beta\quad\text{for}\quad m=1,2,\dots,M.

(5.1c)

Following the structure of
Section

3
and using

Theorem

4.2
,
we introduce the discrete analog to the control state map,

S_{kh}\colon L^{2}(I;H^{-1}(\Omega)^{d})+L^{1}(I;L^{2}(\Omega)^{d})\to X^{0}_{k}(\mathbf{V}_{h}),\quad{\bf{q}}\mapsto{\bf{u}}_{kh}={\bf{u}}_{kh}({\bf{q}})\text{ solving \eqref{eq:fully_discrete_div_free}}.

(5.2)

The finitely many state constraints we describe with the help of the continuous linear operator

\mathcal{G}_{kh}\colon\mathbf{Q}_{ad}\to\mathbb{R}^{M}

with
(\mathcal{G}_{kh}({\bf{q}}))_{m}:=G_{\bf w}\circ S_{kh}({\bf{q}})\mid_{I_{m}}
for
m=1,2,\dots,M
. Using the set

\mathcal{K}_{kh}:=\{{\bf{v}}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}\ \mid\ {\bf{v}}_{m}\leq b,\ m=1,2,\dots,M\},

we can rewrite the problem (
5.1a
)-(
5.1c
) in the reduced form:

\text{Minimize }\ j_{kh}({\bf{q}}_{kh}):=J({\bf{q}}_{kh},S_{kh}({\bf{q}}_{kh}))\quad\text{over}\quad{\bf{q}}_{kh}\in\mathbf{Q}_{ad}\quad\text{subject to}\quad\mathcal{G}_{kh}({\bf{q}}_{kh})\in\mathcal{K}_{kh}.

(5.3)

Before discussing wellposedness and optimality conditions of this discrete problem, we shall show, that the
Slater assumption on the continuous level carries over to the discrete problem.
As we achieve this with the finite element error estimates presented in
Section

4
,
we need to impose a rather weak coupling condition between
k
and
h
, allowing us to deduce convergence
in the result of
Corollary

4.5
.
Throughout the remainder of this work, we thus work under the following assumption.

Assumption 3
.

There exists a function
\Phi\colon(0,1)\to(0,\infty)
with

\lim_{h\to 0}\Phi(h)=0
, such that the discretization parameters
k
and
h
satisfy

\left|\ln\left(\frac{T}{k}\right)\right|h\leq\Phi(h).

Remark 5.1
.

This assumption is valid, e.g. if there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\left|\ln\left(\frac{T}{k}\right)\right|h|\ln h|\leq C
. As the choice of the term
|\ln h|
in such a condition can be
made arbitrarily weak, we have chosen to work under the more general formulation of
Assumption

3
.

Lemma 5.2
.

There exists
h_{0}>0
such that for any
h\leq h_{0}
and
k
satisfying
Assumption

3
,
the Slater point

\tilde{\bf{q}}\in\mathbf{Q}_{ad}
from
Assumption

1
satisfies the following discrete Slater condition

G_{\bf{w}}({\bf{u}}_{kh}(\tilde{q}))<\beta{\qquad\text{for all }t\in\bar{I}.}

Proof.

Using that
G_{\bf w}({\bf{u}}(\tilde{{\bf{q}}}))<\beta
in
\bar{I}
,
by the Slater condition Assumption
1
there exists
\delta>0

such that
G_{\bf w}({\bf{u}}(\tilde{{\bf{q}}}))\leq\beta-\delta
.
For arbitrary
\hat{\bf{q}}\in C^{\infty}(I\times\Omega)^{d}
it holds due to triangle inequality

(5.4)

∎

Theorem 5.4

Lemma 5.7

\displaystyle J(\bar{{\bf{q}}}_{kh},\bar{{\bf{u}}}_{kh})\leq J(\tilde{{\bf{q}}},{\bf{u}}_{kh}(\tilde{{\bf{q}}}))

and

.

\|\bar{{\bf{q}}}_{kh}\|_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}L^{2}(I\times\Omega)}}+\|\bar{{\bf{u}}}_{kh}\|_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}L^{2}(I\times\Omega)}}\leq C.

(5.7)

Let us define

By definition

and thus by (

\left(\alpha\bar{{\bf{q}}}_{kh}+\bar{{\bf{z}}}_{kh},{\bf{p}}-\bar{{\bf{q}}}_{kh}\right)_{I\times\Omega}\geq 0.

This yields

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\left(\bar{{\bf{q}}}_{kh},{\bf{p}}-\bar{{\bf{q}}}_{kh}\right)_{I\times\Omega}+\left(\bar{{\bf{z}}}_{kh},{\bf{p}}-\bar{{\bf{q}}}_{kh}\right)_{I\times\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq C\ell^{2}_{k}(k+h^{2}),

where in the last step, we used the boundedness of
\|\bar{{\bf{u}}}_{kh}\|_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}L^{2}(I\times\Omega)}}
,
\|\bar{{\bf{u}}}\|_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}L^{2}(I\times\Omega)}}
,
\|\bar{{\bf{q}}}\|_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}L^{2}(I\times\Omega)}}
, and
\|\bar{{\bf{q}}}_{kh}\|_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}L^{2}(I\times\Omega)}}
from Theorem
3.8
and Lemma
5.7
.
∎

6
Full Discretization of the Optimal Control Problem

We discretize the control by piecewise constant functions on the same partition as the fully discrete approximation of the state and adjoint variables. We set

\mathbf{Q}_{0}=\{{\bf{q}}\in L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}):\ {\bf{q}}\mid_{I_{m}\times K}\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(I_{m};\mathbb{P}_{0}(K)^{d}),\ m=1,2,\dots,M,\ K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\}.

(6.1)

We also define the corresponding admissible set

\mathbf{Q}_{0,ad}:=\mathbf{Q}_{0}\cap\mathbf{Q}_{ad}.

We introduce the projection
\pi_{d}\colon L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega)^{d})\to\mathbf{Q}_{0}
, defined by

\left({\bf{q}}-\pi_{d}{\bf{q}},\mathbf{r}\right)_{I\times\Omega}=0\qquad\forall\mathbf{r}\in\mathbf{Q}_{0},

(6.2)

which by definition is stable in
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}L^{2}(I\times\Omega)}
, i.e., satisfies

\|\pi_{d}{\bf{q}}\|_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}L^{2}(I\times\Omega)}}\leq\|{\bf{q}}\|_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}L^{2}(I\times\Omega)}}.

(6.3)

Note that this projection satisfies the explicit formula

\pi_{d}{\bf{q}}|_{I_{m}\times K}=\frac{1}{k_{m}|K|}\int_{I_{m}\times K}{\bf{q}}(t,x)\ d(t,x)\qquad\text{for all }m=1,...,M,\ K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}.

Hence it is straightforward to check, that this
L^{2}
projection onto piecewise constants is
stable in
L^{\infty}(I\times\Omega)^{d}
and
L^{\infty}(I;L^{2}(\Omega)^{d})
and there holds

\|\pi_{d}{\bf{q}}\|_{L^{\infty}(I\times\Omega)}\leq\|{\bf{q}}\|_{L^{\infty}(I\times\Omega)}\quad\text{and}\quad\|\pi_{d}{\bf{q}}\|_{L^{\infty}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq\|{\bf{q}}\|_{L^{\infty}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}.

(6.4)

Further, we have
\pi_{d}(\mathbf{Q}_{ad})\subset\mathbf{Q}_{0,ad}
.
We can now formulate the fully discrete optimal control problem, which reads

\text{Minimize }\;J({\bf{q}}_{\sigma},{\bf{u}}_{\sigma})=\frac{1}{2}\lVert{\bf{u}}_{\sigma}-{\bf{u}}_{d}\rVert^{2}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}L^{2}(I\times\Omega)}}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\lVert{\bf{q}}_{\sigma}\rVert^{2}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}L^{2}(I\times\Omega)}}

(6.5a)

subject to
({\bf{q}}_{\sigma},{\bf{u}}_{\sigma})\in\mathbf{Q}_{0,ad}\times X^{0}_{k}(\mathbf{V}_{h})
, satisfying

B({\bf{u}}_{\sigma},{\bf{v}}_{kh})=({\bf{q}}_{\sigma},{\bf{v}}_{kh})_{I\times\Omega}\quad\text{for all}\quad{\bf{v}}_{kh}\in X^{0}_{k}(\mathbf{V}_{h})

(6.5b)

and

G_{\bf{w}}({\bf{u}}_{\sigma})\mid_{I_{m}}\leq\beta\quad\text{for}\quad m=1,2,\dots,M.

(6.5c)

The following lemma guarantees that also for the fully discrete optimal control problem, there exist
feasible controls such that the associated fully discrete state strictly satisfies the state constraint.

8.1
Solution Algorithm

Equation (
6.6
) can be written in matrix notation as

\min_{{\bf{q}}_{\sigma}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{1}{2}(S_{kh}M_{kh}^{uq}{\bf{q}}_{\sigma}-{\bf{u}}_{d,kh})^{T}M_{kh}^{uu}(S_{kh}M_{kh}^{uq}{\bf{q}}_{\sigma}-{\bf{u}}_{d,kh})+\frac{1}{2}\alpha{\bf{q}}_{\sigma}^{T}M_{kh}^{qq}{\bf{q}}_{\sigma}\qquad\text{s.t.}\quad W_{kh}S_{kh}{M_{kh}^{uq}}{\bf{q}}_{\sigma}\leq\beta,

(8.1)

where
M_{kh}^{uu}
is the mass matrix of
X^{0}_{k}(\mathbf{V}_{h})
,
M_{kh}^{qq}
is the mass matrix of
\mathbf{Q}_{0}
,

M_{kh}^{uq}
is the mass matrix encoding inner products of
X^{0}_{k}(\mathbf{V}_{h})
and
\mathbf{Q}_{0}
functions,

{\bf{u}}_{d,kh}
is the
L^{2}
projection of
{\bf{u}}_{d}
onto
X^{0}_{k}(\mathbf{V}_{h})
and
W_{kh}
is the matrix that maps
each
{\bf{u}}_{kh}
to the vector
(G_{\bf{w}}({\bf{u}}_{kh})|_{I_{m}})_{m=1,..,M}
.
Note that with a slight abuse of notation, we write
S_{kh}
for the control to state mapping as well as
the matrix representing this mapping.
The optimality conditions for this problem read

\displaystyle\alpha M_{kh}^{qq}{\bf{q}}_{\sigma}+(M_{kh}^{uq})^{T}S_{kh}^{T}M_{kh}^{uu}(S_{kh}M_{kh}^{uq}{\bf{q}}_{h}-{\bf{u}}_{kh,d})+(M_{kh}^{uq})^{T}S_{kh}^{T}W_{kh}^{T}\mu_{\sigma}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle W_{kh}S_{kh}M_{kh}^{uq}{\bf{q}}_{\sigma}\leq\beta,\quad\mu_{\sigma}\geq 0,\quad\mu_{\sigma}^{T}(W_{kh}S_{kh}M_{kh}^{uq}{\bf{q}}_{\sigma}-\beta)

\displaystyle=0.

We solve the above problem with a primal-dual-active-set strategy (PDAS), during which, for each iteration of
the active set
\mathcal{A}_{n}\subset\{1,\dots,M\}
, we solve a symmetric saddle point system

\begin{pmatrix}\alpha M_{kh}^{qq}+(M_{kh}^{uq})^{T}S_{kh}^{T}M_{kh}^{uu}S_{kh}M_{kh}^{uq}&(M_{kh}^{uq})^{T}S_{kh}^{T}W_{kh}^{T}O_{n}\\
O_{n}^{T}W_{kh}S_{kh}M_{kh}^{uq}&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}{\bf{q}}_{\sigma}^{n}\\
\mu_{\sigma}^{n}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}(M_{kh}^{uq})^{T}S_{kh}^{T}M_{kh}^{uu}{\bf{u}}_{kh,d}\\
\beta\cdot\mathds{1}_{|\mathcal{A}_{n}|}\end{pmatrix},

where
O_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{A}_{n}|\times M}
is the matrix satisfying

O_{n}\mu_{\sigma}=(\mu_{\sigma})_{\mathcal{A}_{n}}
, i.e., selects the active indices.
We solve the linear system with MINRES, using the block diagonal preconditioner

P_{kh}=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha M_{kh}^{qq}&0\\
0&-\frac{1}{\alpha}W_{kh}S_{kh}M_{kh}^{uq}(M_{kh}^{qq})^{-1}(M_{kh}^{uq})^{T}S_{kh}^{T}W_{kh}^{T}\end{pmatrix}.

Note that due to the choice of control discretization
M_{kh}^{qq}
is a diagonal matrix, and if the
partition of
I
is uniform, and all mesh elements of
\Omega
have the same volume,
M_{kh}^{qq}
is a
multiple of the identity matrix.
Further note, that if the partition of
I
is uniform, the matrix
S_{kh}^{T}W_{kh}^{T}
has the structure

\begin{pmatrix}{\bf{z}}_{M}&{\bf{z}}_{M-1}&...&{\bf{z}}_{2}&{\bf{z}}_{1}\\
0&{\bf{z}}_{M}&...&{\bf{z}}_{3}&{\bf{z}}_{2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&...&{\bf{z}}_{M}&{\bf{z}}_{M-1}\\
0&0&...&0&{\bf{z}}_{M}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\dim(\mathbf{V}_{h})\times M}

where each vector
{\bf{z}}_{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{\dim(\mathbf{V}_{h})}
,
m=1,...,M
corresponds to the degrees of freedom of

{\bf{z}}|_{I_{m}}
for the solution
{\bf{z}}
to

B({\bf{v}}_{kh},{\bf{z}}_{kh})=\left({\bf{w}}\chi_{I_{M}},{\bf{v}}_{kh}\right)_{I\times\Omega}\quad\text{for all}\quad{\bf{v}}_{kh}\in X^{0}_{k}(\mathbf{V}_{h}).

Hence, to assemble this matrix only one discrete adjoint problem has to be solved, and a decomposition of
the preconditioning matrix
P_{kh}
can be computed in advance and be reused in every iteration of the
PDAS algorithm.
The discrete solutions of the finite element problems were carried out in FEniCS Version 2019

[
30
]
, using the MINI Element in space.

8.2
Example 1

For this example, we consider the setting
\Omega=(0,1)^{2}
,
I=(0,1]
,

choose the regularization parameter
\alpha=1
and set the control constraints to

{\bf{q}}_{a}=(-\infty,-\infty)^{T}
,
{\bf{q}}_{b}=(+\infty,+\infty)^{T}
.

We construct an analytic test case by considering the functions

\varphi(t):=\begin{cases}{}48t^{2}-128t^{3}&t\in[0,1/4),\\
1&t\in[1/4,3/4],\\
48(1-t)^{2}-128(1-t)^{3}&t\in(3/4,1],\end{cases}\quad{\bf{y}}=\frac{64\sqrt{2}}{5\sqrt{7}}\begin{pmatrix}-\sin(\pi x_{1})^{4}\cos(\pi x_{2})\sin(\pi x_{2})^{3}\\
\cos(\pi x_{1})\sin(\pi x)^{3}\sin(\pi x_{2})^{4}\end{pmatrix}.

Here,
{\bf{y}}
has been constructed in such a way, that
\nabla\cdot{\bf{y}}=0
and

\|{\bf{y}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1
.
It was obtained by considering the potential
\rho(x_{1},x_{2})=\left(\sin(\pi x_{1})\sin(\pi x_{2})\right)^{4}
,
and normalizing the vector field
(\partial_{x_{2}}\rho(x_{1},x_{2}),-\partial_{x_{1}}\rho(x_{1},x_{2}))^{T}
.
We choose
\bar{{\bf{u}}}=\varphi(t){\bf{y}}(x_{1},x_{2})
,
{\bf{w}}={\bf{y}}
and
\beta\equiv 1
.
It is then straightforward to verify, that
G_{\bf{w}}(\bar{{\bf{u}}})\leq\beta
for all
t\in I

and
G_{\bf{w}}(\bar{{\bf{u}}})=\beta
if and only if

t\in[1/4,3/4]
. We thus choose the multiplier
\bar{\mu}=10^{3}\chi_{[1/4,3/4]}(t)
,
which by construction satisfies
\bar{\mu}\geq 0
and
\langle G_{\bf{w}}(\bar{\bf{u}})-\beta,\bar{\mu}\rangle=0
.
We then proceed by choosing
\bar{p}=0
and as a consequence set

\bar{\bf{q}}=\partial_{t}\bar{\bf{u}}-\Delta\bar{\bf{u}}
. We obtain
\bar{\bf{z}}=-\bar{\bf{q}}
and with the choice

\bar{r}=0
can fix
{\bf{u}}_{d}=\bar{\bf{u}}+\partial_{t}\bar{\bf{z}}+\Delta\bar{\bf{z}}+\bar{\mu}{\bf{w}}
, in such a way,
that the constructed
(\bar{\bf{q}},\bar{\bf{u}})
satisfy the first order optimality condition for this desired state.
Note that
\vec{\psi}
has been chosen sufficiently smooth at the boundary, such that
\Delta\vec{\psi}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
and thus
\bar{\bf{q}}|_{\partial\Omega}=\bar{\bf{z}}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
.
The calculation of the analytic solution was verified using the SageMath software
[
37
]
.
We discretize this problem with a uniform triangulation of
\Omega
and a uniform partition of
I
.
To get more insight into the observed orders of convergence, for

a sequence of discretization levels
\{\sigma_{l}\}=\{(h_{l},k_{l})\}
,

we report the empirical orders of convergence determined by

\displaystyle(\mathrm{EOC}_{h})_{l}

\displaystyle:=\dfrac{\log(\|\bar{\bf{q}}-\bar{\bf{q}}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sigma_{l}}}\|_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}L^{2}(I\times\Omega)}})-\log(\|\bar{\bf{q}}-\bar{\bf{q}}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sigma_{l-1}}}\|_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}L^{2}(I\times\Omega)}})}{\log(h_{l})-\log(h_{l-1})}

\displaystyle(\mathrm{EOC}_{k})_{l}

\displaystyle:=\dfrac{\log(\|\bar{\bf{q}}-\bar{\bf{q}}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sigma_{l}}}\|_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}L^{2}(I\times\Omega)}})-\log(\|\bar{\bf{q}}-\bar{\bf{q}}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sigma_{l-1}}}\|_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}L^{2}(I\times\Omega)}})}{\log(k_{l})-\log(k_{l-1})}

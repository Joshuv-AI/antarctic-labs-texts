Introduction

An orientation
\vec{H}
of a graph
H
is an oriented graph obtained
by assigning an orientation to each edge of
H
. The study of the
number of
\vec{H}
-free orientations of a graph

[

, who posed the

. For tournaments, this problem was solved by Alon and
Yuster
[
AlYu06
]
, who proved that
D{(n,\vec{T})=2^{\mathop{\text{\rm ex}}\nolimits(n,K_{k})}}

holds for any tournament
\vec{T}
on
k
vertices if
n\in\mathbb{N}
is sufficiently large as a function of
k
.

Let
C^{\circlearrowright}_{k}
denote the directed cycle of length
k
.
Bucić, Janzer and Sudakov
[
BuJaSu23
]
determined
D(n,C^{\circlearrowright}_{2\ell+1})
for every
\ell\geqslant 1
as long as
n
is sufficiently large, extending the proof in
[
AlYu06
]
.
Another extension of the results in
[
AlYu06
]
was given by Araújo, Botler, and the last author
[
ArBoMo20+
]
who determined
D(n,C^{\circlearrowright}_{3})
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
(see also
[
BoHoMo22
]
).

In the context of random graphs, Allen, Kohayakawa, Parente, and the last author
[
AlKoMoPa14
]
investigated the problem of determining the typical number of
C^{\circlearrowright}_{k}
-free orientations of the Erdős–Rényi random graph
G(n,p)
.
They proved that, for every

, with high probability as

we have
\log_{2}D(G(n,p),C^{\circlearrowright}_{k})=o(pn^{2})
for

, and
\log_{2}D(G(n,p),C^{\circlearrowright}_{k})=(1+o(1))p\binom{n}{2}
for

.
This result was improved in the case of triangles by Kohayakawa, Morris and the last two authors
[

]
, who proved, among other things, the following result

The proof of Theorem

will be outlined in the next section, but we present here a short overview of three key ideas in the proof.
We will be interested in an auxiliary graph encoding
(k-2)
-paths, since we will be able to encode the orientation of the neighbourhood of each vertex as an independent set in this graph. One of the main challenges will be ensuring this auxiliary graph is dense enough so that we may apply the graph container lemma.
To do so, the first key idea is to define, for each
1\leqslant r\leqslant k-2
, a pseudorandomness condition on the number of
directed
r
-paths between small sets, and split the proof into
k-2

cases according to whether such a pseudorandomness condition holds for a given

r
. We design these conditions in such a way that the case
r=1
holds for any orientation of
G(n,p)
with high probability, and such that we may finish the proof using the graph container method (introduced by Kleitman and Winston
[
KlWi82
]
, and rediscovered and developed by Sapozhenko
[
Sa01
]
) if the condition holds for
r=k-2
. The second key idea is how to deal with orientations that do not satisfy the pseudorandomness condition for some
2\leqslant r\leqslant k-2
.
In this situation, we provide a way to efficiently “encode” the orientations that do not satisfy the condition for some value of
r
but do for all smaller values.

We remark that, to implement the above two ideas, we need to construct the orientation “online”, that is, to consider a subgraph
H\subset G(n,p)
, and for each possible orientation
\vec{H}
of
H
, reveal the randomness between a new subset of vertices and
V(H)
and consider all ways to extend
\vec{H}
.
Counting orientations of
G(n,p)
is, however, an “offline” problem: when exposing
G(n,p)
in multiple rounds, the orientation of an early-round edge may depend on the randomness of later rounds.
To circumvent this fact, we use our third key idea, which is to count the expected number of orientations of
G(n,p)
that are
C^{\circlearrowright}_{k}
-free.
When estimating this expectation we will be able to split it in a way that makes possible to proceed inductively and use the randomness in steps after orienting part of the edges.

Outline of the proof of Theorem

We start by presenting a short sketch of the proof of

2.1.
The proof in
[
CoKoMoMo20
]

The idea is to obtain, by induction on the number of vertices, the
following upper bound on the number of
C^{\circlearrowright}_{3}
-free orientations of an

n
-vertex graph
G

(3)

\binom{n}{\leqslant\alpha(G)}^{2n},

where
\alpha(G)
denotes the independence number of
G

\binom{n}{\leqslant t}
is shorthand for
\sum_{i=0}^{t}\binom{n}{i}
.
In order to obtain such a bound, let
G
be a graph on vertex set
V
and consider
v\in V
.
Let
H=G\setminus\{v\}
and suppose that the number of
C^{\circlearrowright}_{3}
-free orientations of
H
is

(4)

\binom{n-1}{\leqslant\alpha(H)}^{2(n-1)}.

Then, for each
C^{\circlearrowright}_{3}
-free orientation
\vec{G}
of
G
, let
\vec{H}
be its restriction to
H
and pick
\vec{T}\subset E(\vec{G})\setminus E(\vec{H})
minimal such that
\vec{G}
is the only
C^{\circlearrowright}_{3}
-free orientation of
G
containing
\vec{T}\cup\vec{H}
.
The key observation is that, by the minimality of
T
, the vertex sets
N^{+}_{\vec{T}}(v)
and
N^{-}_{\vec{T}}(v)
are independent sets in
H
, so there are at most
\binom{n}{\leqslant\alpha(G)}^{2}
choices for
\vec{T}
.
This together with (
4
) and
\alpha(H)\leqslant\alpha(G)
completes the proof of (
3
).
One may check that the expected number of independent sets of size
(3\log n)/p
in
G(n,p)
tends to zero with

, and therefore (
3
) and the first moment method imply Theorem
1.2
for
C^{\circlearrowright}_{3}
.

We will generalise the ideas depicted above in two ways, which will be
described in the next two subsections. Directed paths of length

{k-2}
starting or ending in the neighbourhood of a vertex will play
a key role in our proofs. For this reason, we fix

, and our aim is to count orientations of
G(n,p)
avoiding copies
of
C^{\circlearrowright}_{\ell+2}
.

2.2.
Pseudorandomness

To count the desired orientations of
G(n,p)
, we define a
“pseudorandom” oriented graph property, and we proceed to separately
count the
C^{\circlearrowright}_{\ell+2}
-free orientations depending on whether some
already-oriented subgraph
\vec{H}
is pseudorandom. We then use the
randomness of

in a stronger way, exposing the randomness bit

Let us discuss the pseudorandom property we mentioned in the previous paragraph.
We write
\vec{P}_{r}

\vec{G}

Roughly speaking, we say an oriented graph

(5)

\accentset{\scalebox{.4}{\bm{$\overleftrightarrow{\hbox{}}$}}}{e}_{(\vec{G}\setminus X)^{r}}(A^{\prime},B)\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\cdot p^{\frac{\ell-r+1}{\ell}}|A^{\prime}||B|

of size roughly

, where
\vec{G}\setminus X
is a shorthand for
\vec{G}[V(G)\setminus X]
and
\accentset{\scalebox{.4}{\bm{$\overleftrightarrow{\hbox{}}$}}}{e}_{(\vec{G}\setminus X)^{r}}(A^{\prime},B)
denotes the number of edges between
A^{\prime}
and
B
(in either direction) in the digraph
(\vec{G}\setminus X)^{r}
.

Observe that being
1
-locally dense does not depend on the orientation of the graph (and the set
X
plays no role in this case), i.e., being

-locally dense is a pseudorandom property that depends only on the underlying graph

By Chernoff’s inequality, any orientation of

\mathbb{E}\left[\big|\mathcal{D}_{r-1}(G)\cap\mathcal{S}_{r}(G,\vec{H},B,X)\big|\right]\leqslant\exp\left(9\alpha p^{-\frac{1}{\ell}}\log n\right).

(ii)

\mathbb{E}\left[\big|\mathcal{D}_{\ell}(G,\vec{H})\big|\right]\leqslant\exp\left(\alpha(\ell+2)p^{-\frac{1}{\ell}}(\log n)^{2}\right).

Before proving Theorem
3.1
, we state a simple probabilistic estimate which will
be used a few times throughout the paper.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
0\leqslant p\leqslant 1
and
C>0
. If
S_{p}
is a
p
-random subset of a finite set
S
, then

\mathbb{E}\left[C^{|S_{p}|}\right]\leqslant e^{Cp|S|}.

Proof.

Since
\mathbb{P}(|S_{p}|=t)\leqslant\big(p|S|\big)^{t}/t!
for every
t\geqslant 0
, we can compute

\mathbb{E}[C^{|S_{p}|}]\leqslant\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\frac{\big(Cp|S|\big)^{t}}{t!}=e^{Cp|S|},

as claimed.
∎

We are ready to prove Theorem
3.1
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Recall that
n
is fixed, and put
z:=\exp\big(4(\ell+2)p^{-\frac{1}{\ell}}(\log n)^{2}\big)
.
We will show by induction on
n^{\prime}\leqslant n
that

(9)

\mathbb{E}\Big[\big|\mathcal{D}_{1}(G(n^{\prime},p))\big|\Big]\leqslant z^{n^{\prime}},

which together with Lemma
3.3
and Markov’s
inequality implies the desired result since
4(\ell+2)\leqslant 12\ell
.
Note that if
n^{\prime}\leqslant p^{-1-1/\ell}(\log n)^{2}
holds, then an application of Lemma
3.7
gives

\mathbb{E}\Big[\big|\mathcal{D}_{1}(G(n^{\prime},p))\big|\Big]\leqslant\mathbb{E}\Big[2^{e(G(n^{\prime},p))}\Big]\leqslant\exp\Big(p(n^{\prime})^{2}\Big)\leqslant z^{n^{\prime}}.

Therefore, we may assume
n^{\prime}>p^{-1-1/\ell}(\log n)^{2}
, which implies
n^{\prime}\gg\alpha
.
We claim that it is enough to prove that

(10)

\mathbb{E}\Big[\big|\mathcal{D}_{1}(G(n^{\prime},p))\big|\Big]\leqslant z^{\alpha/2}\cdot\mathbb{E}\Big[\big|\mathcal{D}_{1}(G(n^{\prime}-\alpha/2,p))\big|\Big].

Indeed, by (
10
) and the induction hypothesis
we obtain

\mathbb{E}\Big[\big|\mathcal{D}_{1}(G(n^{\prime},p))\big|\Big]\leqslant z^{\alpha/2}\cdot\mathbb{E}\Big[\big|\mathcal{D}_{1}(G(n^{\prime}-\alpha/2,p))\big|\Big]\leqslant z^{\alpha/2}\cdot z^{n^{\prime}-\alpha/2}=z^{n^{\prime}},

which proves (
9
).
Thus, the remainder of the proof is dedicated to showing that (
10
) holds.
Let
G=G(n^{\prime},p)
and notice that, since every
1
-locally-dense
orientation is either
\ell
-locally dense or admits a minimal
2\leqslant r\leqslant\ell
for which it is
r
-locally sparse, we have

(11)

\mathcal{D}_{1}(G)=\mathcal{D}_{\ell}(G)\;\cup\;\bigcup_{r=2}^{\ell}\,\,(\mathcal{D}_{r-1}(G)\cap\mathcal{S}_{r}(G)).

Thus, it suffices to upper bound the expected sizes of the sets in the right-hand side of (
11
).
Let
A\subset V(G)
be an arbitrary set of size
\alpha/2
, and let
H=G\setminus A
and
F
be the graph with
V(F)=V(G)
and
E(F)=E(G)\setminus\binom{V(H)}{2}
.
Observe that
F
and
H
independent of each other as random graphs.
Therefore,

(12)

\mathbb{E}\Big[\big|\mathcal{D}_{\ell}(G)\big|\Big]=\mathbb{E}_{H}\Bigg[\mathbb{E}_{F}\Big[\sum_{\vec{H}}\big|\mathcal{D}_{\ell}(G,\vec{H})\big|\;\Big|\;H\Big]\Bigg]=\mathbb{E}_{H}\Bigg[\sum_{\vec{H}}\mathbb{E}_{F}\Big[\big|\mathcal{D}_{\ell}(G,\vec{H})\big|\;\Big|\;H\Big]\Bigg],

where the sums are over
\vec{H}\in\mathcal{D}_{1}(H)
.
Conditioned on
H
(that is, on
G\setminus A=H
), the distribution of
F\cup H
is the same as
G(A,H,p)
.
Then, from Lemma
3.6
(
ii
) we obtain

\mathbb{E}_{F}\Big[\big|\mathcal{D}_{\ell}(G,\vec{H})\big|\;\Big|\;H\Big]\leqslant z^{\alpha/4},

and hence, by (
12
),

\mathbb{E}\Big[\big|\mathcal{D}_{\ell}(G)\big|\Big]\leqslant\mathbb{E}_{H}\Bigg[\sum_{\vec{H}\in\mathcal{D}_{1}(H)}z^{\alpha/4}\Bigg]=z^{\alpha/4}\cdot\mathbb{E}\Big[\big|\mathcal{D}_{1}(G(n^{\prime}-\alpha/2),p)\big|\Big].

To bound the expected sizes of the remaining sets of the right-hand side of (
11
), we proceed analogously. For any
2\leqslant r\leqslant\ell
, using (
8
) we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\big|\mathcal{D}_{r-1}(G)\cap\mathcal{S}_{r}(G)\big|\Big]

\displaystyle\leqslant\;\mathbb{E}\;\Big[\sum_{(A,B,X)}\sum_{\vec{H}\in\mathcal{D}_{1}(H)}\big|\mathcal{D}_{r-1}(G)\cap\mathcal{S}_{r}(G,\vec{H},B,X)\big|\Big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{(A,B,X)}\mathbb{E}_{H}\Bigg[\sum_{\vec{H}\in\mathcal{D}_{1}(H)}\mathbb{E}_{F}\Big[\big|\mathcal{D}_{r-1}(G)\cap\mathcal{S}_{r}(G,\vec{H},B,X)\big|\;\Big|\;H\Big]\Bigg],

where the outer sums are over all triples
(A,B,X)
such that
(A,H,B,X)
is an
r
-frame for
H=G\setminus A
. Since there are at most
n^{(2\ell+1)\alpha}\leqslant z^{\alpha/4}
such triples, an application of
Lemma
3.6
(
i
) gives

\mathbb{E}\Big[\big|\mathcal{D}_{r-1}(G)\cap\mathcal{S}_{r}(G)\big|\Big]\leqslant\sum_{(A,B,X)}\mathbb{E}_{H}\Bigg[\sum_{\vec{H}\in\mathcal{D}_{1}(H)}z^{\alpha/12}\Bigg]\leqslant z^{\alpha/3}\cdot\mathbb{E}\Big[\big|\mathcal{D}_{1}(G(n^{\prime}-\alpha/2),p)\big|\Big].

By (
11
), we thus have

\mathbb{E}\Big[\big|\mathcal{D}_{1}(G(n^{\prime},p))\big|\Big]\leqslant\ell z^{\alpha/3}\cdot\mathbb{E}\Big[\big|\mathcal{D}_{1}(G(n^{\prime}-\alpha/2,p))\big|\Big]

which verifies (
10
) and concludes the proof of Theorem
3.1
, since
\ell\leqslant p^{-1/\ell}\leqslant z^{\alpha/6}
.
∎

4.
Extending locally-sparse orientations

The main goal of this section is to prove
Lemma
3.6
(
i
), which bounds the
expected number of
(r-1)
-locally dense,
(r,B,X)
-sparse
extensions of an oriented graph
\vec{H}
.
The deterministic part of the proof is the following proposition.
We remark that, although we intentionally state the result in a way that emphasises its container-type nature, its proof does not use the Hypergraph Container Lemma.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\vec{H}
be an oriented graph and
2\leqslant r\leqslant\ell
. If
(A,H,B,X)
is an
r
-frame, then there exists a family
\mathcal{C}
of digraphs on
vertex set
A\cup V(H)
such that

(i)

For every graph
G
with
V(G)=A\cup V(H)
and every orientation
\vec{G}\in\mathcal{D}_{r-1}(G)\cap\mathcal{S}_{r}(G,\vec{H},B,X)
, there exists some
\vec{C}\in\mathcal{C}
such that
E_{\vec{G}}(A,V(H))\subset E(\vec{C})
.

(ii)

For every
\vec{C}\in\mathcal{C}
, at most
\ell\alpha^{2}
pairs
\{u,v\}\subset V(\vec{C})
satisfy
\{uv,vu\}\subset E(\vec{C})
,

(iii)

|\mathcal{C}|\leqslant\exp(8\alpha p^{-1/\ell}\log n)
.

In other words, we find a small family of digraphs (by property (
iii
)) which cover every orientation of
G
in
\mathcal{D}_{r-1}(G)\cap\mathcal{S}_{r}(G,\vec{H},B,X)
(by property (
i
)). Additionally, for each
\vec{C}\in\mathcal{C}
, any two orientations of
G
covered by
\vec{C}
differ only in a small set of edges (by property (
ii
)), allowing us to bound the expected number of orientations of
G=G(n,p)
contained in each
\vec{C}
. We will then be able to bound the expected size of
\mathcal{D}_{r-1}(G)\cap\mathcal{S}_{r}(G,\vec{H},B,X)
by summing the expected number of orientations of
G
contained in
\vec{C}
for each
\vec{C}\in\mathcal{C}
.

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

The proof loosely follows the idea behind the proof of the Kleitman–Winston graph container lemma.
We will describe an algorithm which takes as input an orientation
\vec{G}\in\mathcal{D}_{r-1}(G)\cap\mathcal{S}_{r}(G,\vec{H},B,X)
for some graph
G
on vertex set
A\cup V(H)
, and outputs a bipartite digraph
\vec{C}
with parts
A
and
V(H)
trivially satisfying properties (
i
) and (
ii
).
Along with
\vec{C}
, the algorithm will output a special subgraph
\vec{T}\subset\vec{C}
, called a
fingerprint
, which will be useful for proving property (
iii
).

Recall that
\alpha=2^{6}(\log n)/p
. Recall also, from the definition of
r
-frame, that
A\subset[n]\setminus V(H)
, that
B,X\subset V(H)
are disjoint and that

|A|=\alpha/2,\qquad r\alpha\leqslant|B|\leqslant\ell\alpha,\qquad\text{and}\qquad|X|\leqslant(\ell+1-r)\alpha.

Set

L=|V(H)|-|B|-|X|-\alpha

and observe that we may assume
L\geqslant 0
, since otherwise
|V(H)|<|B|+|X|+\alpha\leqslant 2\ell\alpha
and therefore it would suffice to take
\mathcal{C}=\{\vec{C}\}
, where
\vec{C}
is the complete bipartite digraph between
A
and
V(H)
. Algorithm
, which will be used to construct the desired family
\mathcal{C}
, is presented below. We will denote the outputs of
\textsc{Encode}(\vec{G})
by
\vec{T}(\vec{G})
and
\vec{C}(\vec{G})
, respectively, and define

(13)

\mathcal{C}=\left\{\,\vec{C}(\vec{G}):\vec{G}\in\mathcal{D}_{r-1}(G)\cap\mathcal{S}_{r}(G,\vec{H},B,X)\,\right\}.

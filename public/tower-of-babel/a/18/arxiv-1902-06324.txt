Isomorphisms between complements of projective plane curves
Mattias Hemmig

Abstract
In this article, we study isomorphisms between complements of irreducible curves in the projective plane P 2 {\mathbb P}^{2} , over an arbitrary algebraically closed field. Of particular interest are rational unicuspidal curves. We prove that if there exists a line that intersects a unicuspidal curve C ⊂ P 2 C\subset{\mathbb P}^{2} only in its singular point, then any other curve whose complement is isomorphic to P 2 ∖ C {\mathbb P}^{2}\setminus C must be projectively equivalent to C C . This generalizes a result of H. Yoshihara who proved this result over the complex numbers. Moreover, we study properties of multiplicity sequences of irreducible curves that imply that any isomorphism between the complements of these curves extends to an automorphism of P 2 {\mathbb P}^{2} . Using these results, we show that two irreducible curves of degree ≤ 7 \leq 7 have isomorphic complements if and only if they are projectively equivalent. Finally, we describe new examples of irreducible projectively non-equivalent curves of degree 8 8 that have isomorphic complements.



Keywords.
Plane curves, curves of low degree, unicuspidal curves, complements of plane curves

such that
\eta^{-1}(p)
consists of only one point and thus
C\setminus\{p\}\simeq{\mathbb P}^{1}\setminus\eta^{-1}(p)\simeq{\mathbb A}^{1}
. Since
L
is very tangent to
C
, the intersection
C\cap L
consists only of the point
p
. It follows that
C\setminus L\simeq C\setminus\{p\}\simeq{\mathbb A}^{1}
.

To prove the converse, assume that
C\setminus L\simeq{\mathbb A}^{1}
. It follows that
C
is rational and
\Sing(C)\subset C\cap L
. We consider the normalization
\eta\colon{\mathbb P}^{1}\to C
and obtain
C\setminus L\subset C\setminus\Sing(C)\simeq{\mathbb P}^{1}\setminus\eta^{-1}(\Sing(C))
. Since
C\setminus L\simeq{\mathbb A}^{1}
, it follows that
\eta^{-1}(\Sing(C))
consists of at most one point. If
\eta^{-1}(\Sing(C))
is empty, then
C\simeq{\mathbb P}^{1}
is smooth and thus either a line or a conic, by the genus-degree formula. Since
C\setminus L\simeq{\mathbb A}^{1}
, it follows that
L
intersects
C
in only one point and is thus very tangent to
C
. If
\eta^{-1}(\Sing(C))
is not empty, then it contains exactly one point and thus
C
is unicuspidal and
C\setminus L=C\setminus\Sing(C)
. Since
C\cap L=\Sing(C)
consists of only one point, the line
L
is very tangent to
C
.

\square

If

is unicuspidal and rational and has a very tangent line

through the singular point, then

. In other words,

.

such that

for some line

that is distinct from

. Suppose that there exists an open embedding

that does not extend to an automorphism of
{\mathbb P}^{2}
, then the induced birational map

contracts the curve

to a point. It turns out that

, Proposition

]
. It also follows from the work of

]

]

Let

, and denote by

the closure of

. Then the following are equivalent:

that sends

(ii)

We often identify

We will use the well known structure theorem of Jung and van der Kulk in the sequel. We denote by
\Aff({\mathbb P}^{2},L)
the

L
, which consists of the automorphisms of
{\mathbb P}^{2}
that preserve

. Moreover, we denote by

the intersection
\Aff({\mathbb P}^{2},L)\cap\Jon({\mathbb P}^{2},L,p)
.

The group
\Aut({\mathbb P}^{2}\setminus L)
is generated by the subgroups
\Aff({\mathbb P}^{2},L)
and
\Jon({\mathbb P}^{2},L,p)
. Moreover,
\Aut({\mathbb P}^{2}\setminus L)
is a free product

\Aff({\mathbb P}^{2},L)\ast_{B({\mathbb P}^{2},L,p)}\Jon({\mathbb P}^{2},L,p),

amalgamated over the intersection of these two subgroups.

There exist many proofs of Theorem
3.B
. The proof in
[
Lam02
]
uses blow-ups and contractions of the line

, in the spirit of the methods used in this article. For more proofs with a similar strategy see
[
BD11
]
and
[
BS15
]
.

Let
\theta\in\Aut({\mathbb P}^{2}\setminus L)
with

\theta=a\circ j_{n}\circ a_{n}\circ\ldots\circ j_{1}\circ a_{1},

where

,

for

and where

for
i=1,\ldots,n
. Then
\theta
has unique proper base-point
a_{1}^{-1}(p)
. Moreover, the degree of
\theta
is
\prodop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\deg(j_{i})
.

The map
j_{1}
has unique proper base-point
p
, and thus

has unique proper base-point
a_{1}^{-1}(p)
and

has unique proper base-point

. We proceed by induction and assume that

has unique proper base-point

and its inverse has unique proper base-point

. Moreover, the unique proper base-point of

, which is different from

. It then follows that the composition

again has

as its unique proper base-point. This remains true after a left-composition with

.

To compute the degree of

for all

are affine and hence have degree

have no common base-point and obtain the result by induction by using
[

, Proposition

]
.

\square

3.C.

Let

be a unicuspidal curve such that

\Theta=\{\theta\in\Aut({\mathbb P}^{2}\setminus L_{x})\mid\theta(C)=L_{z}\}

is non-empty. Then for any
\theta\in\Theta
and any minimal resolution

\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\scriptstyle{\sigma_{1}}

\textstyle{{\mathbb P}^{2}}

(i)

for all
\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta
.

(ii)

The unique proper base-point of
\theta^{-1}
is different from
[0:1:0]
.

(iii)

\deg(\theta)=\deg(C)
.

(iv)

The strict transform of

by
\sigma_{1}
intersects the strict transform of

by
\sigma_{2}
in

.

(v)

The strict transform of

by
\sigma_{1}

.

Let
\theta\in\Theta
. We first prove
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
and thus assume that
\theta
has minimal degree in
\Theta
. We use Theorem
3.B
to write

\theta^{-1}=a_{n+1}\circ j_{n}\circ a_{n}\circ\ldots\circ j_{1}\circ a_{1},

where

,

for

, and

for
i=1,\ldots,n
. If
(j_{1}\circ a_{1})(L_{z})
is a line, we can find

such that

. But then
\theta^{\prime}\coloneqq(a_{n+1}\circ j_{n}\circ a_{n}\circ\ldots\circ j_{2}\circ a_{2}\circ a_{1}^{\prime})^{-1}
lies in

and

by Lemma

, which contradicts the minimality of the degree of
\theta
in
\Theta
. It follows moreover from Lemma

that

is a line if and only if

, i.e.

. Thus by the minimality of the degree of

, we have that

. Since

is the unique proper base-point of

, it follows that it is different from

and hence

is proved.

Assume now that the unique proper base-point of

is different from

. From Lemma

we obtain the formula

\deg(\theta)=\deg(\theta^{-1})=\deg(C)+\sumop\displaylimits_{p}m_{p}(\theta^{-1})m_{p}(L_{z}).

Since the unique proper base-point of

lies on

and is different from

, we then have

. This shows

. Moreover, if we assume that

, then

has minimal degree in

. Thus the implication

is also proved.

Finally, we show that
(iv)

4.
Curves of low degree

In this section we study Conjecture
1
for curves of low degree, i.e. degree
\leq 8
. It is a case study on the multiplicity sequences that occur (see Definition

).

4.A.
Cases by multiplicity sequences

Let
C\subset{\mathbb P}^{2}
be an irreducible curve of degree
d\geq 3
such that there exists an open embedding
{\mathbb P}^{2}\setminus C\hookrightarrow{\mathbb P}^{2}
that does not extend to an automorphism of
{\mathbb P}^{2}
. Then
C
is a rational curve, where all the proper and infinitely near singular points of
C
can be ordered from
p_{1}
to
p_{k}
, with multiplicities
m_{1}\geq\ldots\geq m_{k}\geq 2
, such that
p_{1}\in C
is a proper point and
p_{i+1}
lies in the first neighborhood of
p_{i}
, for
i=1,\ldots,k-1
. Moreover, the multiplicities satisfy the following relations:

\displaystyle d^{2}-3d+2

\displaystyle=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}(m_{i}-1),

(A)

\displaystyle d^{2}+1

\displaystyle\geq\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}^{2}.

(B)

Let
\varphi\colon{\mathbb P}^{2}\setminus C\hookrightarrow{\mathbb P}^{2}
be an open embedding that does not extend to an automorphism of
{\mathbb P}^{2}
. Then by Lemma
2
there exists a
(-1)
-tower resolution

of
C
with base-points
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}
and exceptional curves
E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}
, and a
(-1)
-tower resolution
\eta\colon X\to{\mathbb P}^{2}
of some curve
D\subset{\mathbb P}^{2}
such that
\varphi\circ\pi=\eta
. For
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, we denote by
m_{i}
the multiplicity of
C_{i}
at
p_{i}
, so we have
m_{1}\geq\ldots\geq m_{n}
. The strict transform
C_{n}
in

is smooth, thus
\pi
factors through the minimal resolution of singularities of
C
and blows up all its
k\leq n
singular points, hence the first part of the claim follows.

For equation (

), we observe that
C
is a rational curve since
C_{n}\simeq{\mathbb P}^{1}
and thus has genus

. By the genus-degree formula for plane curves we get

0=g(C)=\frac{(d-1)(d-2)}{2}-\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{k}\frac{m_{i}(m_{i}-1)}{2}

and hence identity (
A
) follows. To see the inequality (
B
), it is enough to observe that for a blow-up
\pi_{i}
with exceptional curve
E_{i}
, we get

\pi_{i}^{*}(C_{i})=C_{i+1}+m_{i}E_{i}

and hence
(C_{i+1})^{2}=(C_{i})^{2}-m_{i}^{2}
, using the identities
(E_{i})^{2}=-1
and
C_{i+1}\cdot E_{i}=m_{i}
. We then inductively obtain

-1=(C_{n})^{2}=d^{2}-\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}^{2}.

The claim then follows from the fact that the number
k
of singular points is
\leq n
.

\square

The previous lemma motivates the following definition.

be a curve and let

be some integers. We say that

(m_{1},\ldots,m_{k})
if

has (proper or infinitely near) singular points

with multiplicities
m_{1},\ldots,m_{k}
such that

is a proper point and

lies in the first neighborhood of
p_{i}
for

, and moreover

is smooth at all other points. For a constant subsequence
(m,\ldots,m)
of length

(m_{(l)})
.

It is not known to the author whether there exist irreducible curves
C,D\subset{\mathbb P}^{2}
that have isomorphic complements but have different multiplicity sequences.

Let
C\subset{\mathbb P}^{2}
be an irreducible curve of degree
d\geq 3
with multiplicity sequence
(m_{1},\ldots,m_{k})
, where we set
m_{2}\coloneqq 1
if

. If there exists an open embedding
{\mathbb P}^{2}\setminus C\hookrightarrow{\mathbb P}^{2}

{\mathbb P}^{2}

m_{1}+m_{2}\leq d<3m_{1}.

We use the set-up of the proof of Lemma

) for the extended multiplicity sequence and obtain

We then multiply this equation by

and subtract (

-\left(1+\frac{d}{3}\right)=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}\left(\frac{d}{3}-m_{i}\right).

Since the right-hand side of this equation is negative, so is the left-hand side. Thus, at least one of the terms

is negative. The inequality

now follows from the fact that the multiplicity sequence is non-increasing.

The inequality

The case of cubic curves is then straightforward.

Let

and

are projectively equivalent.

\square

.

{\mathbb P}^{2}

Let

(C\cdot L)_{p_{1}}=\deg(C)
, and thus

\square

on the exceptional curve and we can assume that

For the point

in local coordinates, and

. Applying a linear map of the form above, we get

and the induced map on the blow-up is given by

, in local coordinates. The induced map on the exceptional curve is

is not proximate to
p_{4}^{\prime}
and is not collinear with

and
p_{5}^{\prime}
. Hence we can also assume that

We have thus found a linear map that sends

to

\square

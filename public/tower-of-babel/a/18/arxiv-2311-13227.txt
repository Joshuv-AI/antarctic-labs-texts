Critical edge statistics for deformed GinUEs
Dang-Zheng Liu, Lu Zhang

Abstract
For the complex Ginibre ensemble subjected to an additive perturbation by a deterministic normal matrix X 0 X_{0} , we establish that under specific spectral conditions on X 0 X_{0} , only two distinct types of local spectral statistics emerge at the spectral edge: GinUE statistics and critical statistics, which respectively correspond to regular and quadratically vanishing spectral points. The critical statistics, as a non-Hermitian analogue of Pearcey statistics in random matrix theory, describes a novel point process on the complex plane. This identifies the third (and likely final) universal statistics in non-Hermitian random matrix theory, after the established GinUE bulk and edge universality classes, and represents the primary achievement of this paper.

Introduction and main results

1.1
Introduction

We begin our study by specifying the deformed complex Ginibre ensembles (GinUE for short)

X:=X_{0}+\sqrt{\frac{\tau}{N}}G,

(1.1)

where
X_{0}
is a deterministic matrix, and
G=[g_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{N}

Or, to be more exact, we have

Definition 1.1

A random complex

N\times N

X_{0}

P_{N}(X_{0};X)=\Big(\frac{N}{\pi\tau}\Big)^{N^{2}}\ e^{-\frac{N}{\tau}{\rm Tr}(X-X_{0})(X-X_{0})^{*}}.

(1.2)

One motivation behind the study of the deformed model (
1.1
) comes mostly
from the general effort toward the understanding of the effect of a perturbation on the spectrum of a large-dimensional random matrix. Another is that
eigenvalues of non-Hermitian matrices may present instability, unlike eigenvalues of Hermitian matrices; see
[
7
]

[
29
]

Although the deformed GUEs or the general deformed Hermitian models have been
quite well understood (see e.g.
[
15
]
and references therein), not much is known about the local eigenvalue statistics in the non-Hermitian situation. When the perturbation strength is above a certain threshold (supercritical regime), extreme eigenvalues will stay away from the bulk and outlier phenomena have been well studied
[
5

7
,
35

.
Meanwhile, when the perturbation strength is near or below a certain threshold (subcritical and critical regimes), in one previous paper
[
29
]
we investigate the finite-rank perturbation effect and prove that
the edge statistics is characterized by a new class of determinantal point processes, for
which correlation kernels depend only on geometric multiplicity of eigenvalue and can be expressed
in terms of the repeated erfc integrals. Together, all these results establish a non-Hermitian analogue of the BBP phase transition for the largest eigenvalue of spiked random matrices
[
2

. Our goal is to identify all the possible local eigenvalue statistics at the edge and in the bulk of the spectrum in a series of papers. Related to those is a key result from
[

plays a central role in our studying eigenvalue statistics of the deformed GinUE.

For any given complex numbers

(1.8)

Set

P_{00}(z_{0}):=\int\frac{1}{|z-z_{0}|^{2}}d\nu(z),\quad P_{0}\equiv P_{0}(z_{0}):=\int\frac{z-z_{0}}{|z-z_{0}|^{4}}d\nu(z),

(1.9)

and

P_{1}\equiv P_{1}(z_{0}):=\int\frac{1}{|z-z_{0}|^{4}}d\nu(z),\quad P_{2}\equiv P_{1}(z_{0}):=\int\frac{(z-z_{0})^{2}}{|z-z_{0}|^{6}}d\nu(z),\quad\chi=\frac{P_{2}(z_{0})}{P_{1}(z_{0})}.

(1.10)

These quantities are crucial to characterize exact functional form of the limiting spectral measure for the deformed matrix
X
in Definition
1.1
and to distinguish spectral properties. For instance, assuming
\tau=1
for convenience, the support of the limiting spectral measure
\mu_{\infty}
for
X

\mathrm{Supp}(\mu_{\infty}):=\Big\{z_{0}\in\mathbb{C}:P_{00}(z_{0})\geq 1\Big\};

(1.11)

see e.g.
[
7
, Proposition 1.2]
. We will verify the following conclusions in the present and subsequent papers.

(i)

Bulk universality
holds at every point in the bulk regime

\Big\{z_{0}\in\mathbb{C}:P_{00}(z_{0})>1\Big\}.

(1.12)

(ii)

There are only two types of edge statistics
on the spectral boundary:

GinUE edge statistics
at the regular edge point
z_{0}
such that
P_{00}(z_{0})=1
and
P_{0}(z_{0})\neq 0
, and

critical edge statistics
at the critical edge point
z_{0}
such that

and

. The latter is a new kind of point process, but seems not determinantal; see
[
18
]
for the same classification of spectral edges for
the density of Brown measure of free circular Brownian motion.

It seems most interesting to investigate local statistics
at the critical edge, so we focus on it in the present paper, and leave GinUE edge statistics and bulk universality in the subsequent papers.

2

2.1
Notation

Throughout the proof of Theorem
1.2
, the sizes of all matrices and matrix variables will be chosen to be finite, independent of
N
.

Denote the conjugate, transpose, and conjugate transpose of a complex matrix
A
by
\overline{A}
,
A^{t}
and
A^{*}
respectively. Besides, the following symbols will be used in the subsequent sections.
Denote the tensor product of an
m\times n
matrix
A:=[a_{i,j}]
and a
p\times q
matrix
B
by a block matrix

A\otimes B=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a_{11}B&\cdots&a_{1n}B\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
a_{m1}B&\cdots&a_{mn}B\end{smallmatrix}\right],

and denote the Hilbert-Schmidt norm of a complex matrix
M
by
\|M\|:=\sqrt{{\rm{Tr}}(MM^{*})}
. The notation
M=O(A_{N})
for some number sequence
A_{N}
means that each matrix element of
M
has the same order of
A_{N}
. In other words, there exists a positive constant
C
independent of
N
, such that
|M_{i,j}|\leq CA_{N}
.

2.2
Translation reduction

First, recall the assumptions (
1.1
) and (
1.4
). By taking a translation transformation of the random matrix
X
, that is,
X\mapsto X-a_{1}\mathbb{I}_{N},
the eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{N}
of
X
change to
\lambda_{1}-a_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{N}-a_{1}
. Combining the definition of the
n
-point correlation function
R_{N}^{(n)}
in (
1.3
), we have

R_{N}^{(n)}(X_{0};z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})=R_{N}^{(n)}(X_{0}-a_{1}\mathbb{I}_{N};z_{1}-a_{1},\cdots,z_{n}-a_{1}).

(2.1)

Moreover, if
z_{0}
is an edge point of the limiting spectral measure of the random matrix
X
, then
z_{0}-a_{1}
is an edge point of the limiting spectral measure of the random matrix
X-a_{1}\mathbb{I}_{N}
. Therefore, without loss of generality, in (
1.4
) we can assume that

a_{1}=0.

(2.2)

We will see that this assumption ensures a key integral representation of the
n
-correlation function; cf. Proposition
2.1
below.

2.6
Maximum lemmas

Given complex numbers
z_{0}
,
a_{\alpha}
and

c_{\alpha}>0
(

), assume that

\sum_{\alpha=1}^{t}c_{\alpha}=1,\quad\sum_{\alpha=1}^{t}\frac{c_{\alpha}}{|z_{0}-a_{\alpha}|^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{\tau}.

Let

be the unique non-negative solution of the equation

\sum_{\alpha=1}^{t}\frac{c_{\alpha}}{|z_{0}-a_{\alpha}|^{2}+t_{0}}=\frac{1}{\tau}.

(2.75)

The following two lemmas hold and play a significant role in investigating local eigenvalue statistics of the deformed GinUEs.

Lemma 2.4
.

As a function of a non-negative definite matrix
H
,

\sum_{\alpha=1}^{t}c_{\alpha}\log\det\big(|z_{0}-a_{\alpha}|^{2}\mathbb{I}_{n}+H\big)-\frac{1}{\tau}{\rm Tr}(H)

.

Proof.

Obviously, it’s sufficient to check the
n=1
case. For this, take the first and second derivatives, find the saddle point and we can easily complete the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Assume that
z_{0}
is not an eigenvalue of the
n\times n
complex normal matrix
D

\displaystyle J_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{t}\Big(\tau c_{\alpha}\log\det\big(T_{\alpha}T_{\alpha}^{*}\big)-|z_{0}-a_{\alpha}|^{2}{\rm Tr}\big(T_{\alpha}T_{\alpha}^{*}\big)\Big)+|z_{0}|^{2}{\rm Tr}\Big(\sum_{\alpha=1}^{t}T_{\alpha}T_{\alpha}^{*}+AA^{*}+BB^{*}\Big)

\displaystyle-{\rm Tr}\Big(B\big(z_{0}\mathbb{I}_{l_{2}}-D\big)^{*}\big(z_{0}\mathbb{I}_{l_{2}}-D\big)B^{*}\Big)+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\left|\Big(\sum_{\alpha=1}^{t}a_{\alpha}T_{\alpha}T_{\alpha}^{*}+z_{0}AA^{*}+BDB^{*}\Big)_{i,j}\right|^{2},

and

(2.78)

whenever

\sum_{\alpha=1}^{t}T_{\alpha}T_{\alpha}^{*}+AA^{*}+BB^{*}\leq\mathbb{I}_{n},.

(2.79)

A=0,\quad B=0,\quad T_{\alpha}=\sqrt{\frac{\tau c_{\alpha}}{|z_{0}-a_{\alpha}|^{2}+t_{0}}}\mathbb{I}_{n},\quad\alpha=1,\ldots,t.

(2.80)

We proceed by induction on

as in (

(1) Base Step.
When

\displaystyle J_{1}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{t}\big(c_{\alpha}\log(t_{\alpha})-f_{\alpha}t_{\alpha}\big)+|z_{0}|^{2}\big(\sum_{\alpha=1}^{t}t_{\alpha}+AA^{*}+BB^{*}\big)-B\big(z_{0}\mathbb{I}_{l_{2}}-D\big)^{*}\big(z_{0}\mathbb{I}_{l_{2}}-D\big)B^{*}.

with equality if and only if

A=0
and
B=0

\displaystyle\widetilde{J}

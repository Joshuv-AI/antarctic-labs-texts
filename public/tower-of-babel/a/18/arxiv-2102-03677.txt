Ballistic Transport for Schrödinger Operators with Quasi-periodic Potentials
Yulia Karpeshina, Leonid Parnovski and Roman Shterenberg

Abstract
. We prove the existence of ballistic transport for a Schrödinger operator with a generic quasi-periodic potential in any dimension d > 1 d>1 .



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Prior results on ballistic transport

It is well known that the spectral and dynamical properties of Schrödinger operators
H=-\Delta+V
acting in
{\mathcal{H}}=L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
are related. A general correspondence of this kind is given by the RAGE theorem, e.g.
[
36
]
. Stated briefly, it says that solutions
\Psi(\cdot,t)=e^{-iHt}\Psi_{0}
of the time-dependent Schrödinger equation are ‘bound states’ if the spectral measure
\mu_{\Psi_{0}}
of the initial state
\Psi_{0}
is pure point, while
\Psi(\cdot,t)
is a ‘scattering state’ if
\mu_{\Psi_{0}}
is (absolutely) continuous. However, knowing the spectral type is not sufficient to quantify transport properties more precisely, for example in terms of diffusion exponents
\beta
. These exponents, if they exist, characterize how time-averaged
m
-moments

\langle\langle X_{\Psi_{0}}^{m}\rangle\rangle_{T}:=\frac{2}{T}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{2t}{T}\right)\|X^{m/2}\Psi(\cdot,t)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}\,dt,\ \ \ \ \ m>0

(1.1)

of the position operator
X
grow as a power
T^{m\beta}
of time
T
, where
(Xu)(x):=|x|u(x)
(
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
m
is a positive real number). The special cases
\beta=1
,
\beta=1/2
and
\beta=0
are interpreted as ballistic transport, diffusive transport, and dynamical localization, respectively.

In general, due to the possibility of fast travelling small tails,
\beta
may depend on
m
. In this paper, we will restrict our attention to the most frequently considered case of the second moment
m=2
. The ballistic upper bound

\|X\Psi(\cdot,t)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}\leq C_{1}(\Psi_{0})T^{2}+C_{2}(\Psi_{0}),

(1.2)

and thus also its averaged version
\langle\langle X_{\Psi_{0}}^{2}\rangle\rangle_{T}\leq C_{1}(\Psi_{0})T^{2}+C_{2}(\Psi_{0})
, is known to hold for general potentials
V
with relative
\Delta
-bound less than one (in particular all bounded potentials) and initial states

\Psi_{0}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}:=\{f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}):|x|f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\,|\nabla f|\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\},

(1.3)

see
[
35
]
. As most authors, we will work with the Abel mean used in (
1.1
), but note that the existence of a ballistic upper bound can be used to show that Abel means and Cesaro means
T^{-1}\int_{0}^{T}\ldots\,dt
lead to the same diffusion exponents (see for example Theorem 2.20 in
[
13
]
).

In the late 1980s and 1990s methods were developed which led to more concrete bounds on diffusion exponents by also taking fractal dimensions of the associated spectral measures into account and showing that this gives lower transport bounds. In particular, again for the special case of the second moment, the Guarneri-Combes theorem
[
18
,
19
,
8
,
31
]
says that

\langle\langle X_{\Psi_{0}}^{2}\rangle\rangle_{T}\geq C_{\Psi_{0}}T^{2\alpha/d}.

(1.4)

for initial states
\Psi_{0}
with uniformly
\alpha
-Hölder continuous spectral measure (and satisfying an additional energy bound in the continuum case
[
8
]
). In dimension
d=1
this says that states with an absolutely continuous spectral measure (
\alpha=1
) also will have ballistic transport (as by (
1.2
) the transport can not be faster than ballistic). In particular, this means that in cases where the spectra of one-dimensional Schrödinger operators with limit or quasi-periodic potentials were found to have an a.c. component, e.g.
[
2
,
7
,
14
,
15
,
32
,
33
,
34
]
, one also gets ballistic transport.

The bound (
1.4
) does not suffice to conclude ballistic transport from the existence of a.c. spectrum in dimension
d\geq 2
. In fact, examples of Schrödinger operators with absolutely continuous spectrum, but slower than ballistic transport have been found: A two-dimensional ‘jelly-roll’ example with a.c. spectrum and diffusive transport is discussed in
[
30
]
, while
[
3
]
provides examples of separable potentials in dimension
d\geq 3
with a.c. spectrum and sub-diffusive transport.

In general, growth properties of generalized eigenfunctions should be used in addition to spectral information for a more complete characterization of the dynamics. General relations between eigenfunction growth and spectral type as well as dynamics were found in
[
30
]
. A series of works studied one-dimensional models with
\alpha<1
and related the dynamics to transfer matrix bounds, e.g.
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
17
,
21
,
39
]
. In particular, these methods can establish lower transport bounds in models with sub-ballistic transport, such as the Fibonacci Hamiltonian and the random dimer model.

Until recently, much less has been done for
d\geq 2
. Ballistic lower bounds and thus the existence of waves propagating at non-zero velocity were known only for
V=0
, where this is classical, e.g.
[
36
]
, and for periodic potentials
[
1
]
. Scattering theoretic methods show that this extends to potentials of sufficiently rapid decay, or sufficiently rapidly decaying perturbations of periodic potentials.
In
[
26
]
two results on ballistic lower bounds in dimension
d=2
were obtained, one for limit-periodic and one for quasi-periodic potentials. Our goal here is to generalize these result to any dimension
d\geq 2
and a generic quasi-periodic potential.

We have already proved, in
[
27
]
, that generic quasi-periodic potentials have absolutely continuous spectrum for high energies. Here, we will combine results obtained in
[
27
]
(in particular, the properties of the generalised eigenfunctions constructed there) with the methods of
[
26
]
to prove the existence of
ballistic transport.

1.2.
The Main Result

We study the initial value problem

i\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial t}=H\Psi,\ \ \ \Psi(\mathbf{x},0)=\Psi_{0}(\mathbf{x})

(1.5)

for multidimensional Schrödinger operator
H
acting on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
d\geq 2
, defined in the following way.
Let
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1},\dots,\boldsymbol{\omega}_{l}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
l>d
, be a collection of vectors that we will call
the basic frequencies
. It will be convenient to form a ‘vector’ out of the basic frequencies:

\vec{\boldsymbol{\omega}}:=(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1},\dots,\boldsymbol{\omega}_{l})
.
We consider the operator

H:=-\Delta+V,

(1.6)

where

V:=\sum_{|\boldsymbol{n}|\leq Q}V_{\boldsymbol{n}}\boldsymbol{e}_{\boldsymbol{n}\vec{\boldsymbol{\omega}}}.

(1.7)

The last sum is finite and taken over all vectors
\boldsymbol{n}=(n_{1},\dots,n_{l})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{l}
with

|\boldsymbol{n}|:=\max_{j=1,...,l}|n_{j}|<Q,\ \ \ Q\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(1.8)

We have also denoted

\boldsymbol{e}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}):=e^{i\left<\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{x}\right>},\ \ \boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

(1.9)

and

\boldsymbol{n}\vec{\boldsymbol{\omega}}:=\sum_{j=1}^{l}n_{j}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d};

(1.10)

these vectors
\boldsymbol{n}\vec{\boldsymbol{\omega}}
being called
the frequencies
.
For convenience and without loss of generality, we assume that the basic frequencies

\boldsymbol{\omega}_{j}\in[-1/2,1/2]^{d}
and thus
\vec{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\in[-1/2,1/2]^{dl}
(so that the Lebesgue measure of this set is one; obviously, we can always achieve this by rescaling). We assume the frequencies
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1},...,\boldsymbol{\omega}_{l}
are linearly independent over rationals. We also assume
V_{-\boldsymbol{n}}={\bar{V}_{\boldsymbol{n}}}
. Clearly,
V
is real valued.

Consider the evolution equation (
1.5
) for operators
H
described above. Clearly, the ballistic upper bound of
[
35
]
can be applied and we have (
1.2
) for initial conditions
\Psi_{0}
satisfying (
1.3
). We prove that for these operators there are corresponding
ballistic lower bounds
for a large class of initial conditions. To formulate our main result, we use the infinite-dimensional spectral projection
E_{\infty}
for
H
whose construction is described in Section
2
below.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any given set of Fourier coefficients
\{V_{\boldsymbol{n}}\}
,
V_{-\boldsymbol{n}}={\bar{V}_{\boldsymbol{n}}}
,
|\boldsymbol{n}|\leq Q
,
Q\in{\mathbb{N}}
, there exists a subset
\Omega_{*}=\Omega_{*}(\{V_{\boldsymbol{n}}\})\subset[-1/2,1/2]^{dl}
of basic frequencies with
\hbox{meas}\,(\Omega_{*})=1
such that for any
\vec{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\in\Omega_{*}
there is an infinite-dimensional projection
E_{\infty}=E_{\infty}(V)
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
(described in Section
2
) with the following property: For any

\Psi_{0}\in{\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}}\quad\mbox{with}\quad E_{\infty}\Psi_{0}\neq 0

(1.11)

there are constants
c_{1}=c_{1}(\Psi_{0})>0
and
T_{0}=T_{0}(\Psi_{0})
such that the solution
\Psi(\mathbf{x},t)
of (
1.5
) satisfies the estimate

\frac{2}{T}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-2t/T}\big\|X\Psi(\cdot,t)\big\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}dt>c_{1}T^{2}

(1.12)

for all
T>T_{0}
.

Remark 1.2
.

The set
\Omega_{*}
in the formulation of the Theorem is implicit. More specifically, it is the very same set for which the results from
[
27
]
are valid. In particular, the frequencies in this set satisfy Strong Diophantine Condition (see
[
27
]
for more details). In what follows, we will assume that the potential
V
is fixed and corresponding frequencies belong to
\Omega_{*}
. We also remark that the notation in this paper, while following in most symbols the notation of
[
26
]
and
[
27
]
, sometimes differs slightly from it. For example, the projection
E_{\infty}
is denoted by
E^{(\infty)}
in
[
27
]
, etc.

In Section 2 we show that
E_{\infty}
is close in norm to
{\mathcal{F}}^{*}\chi\left(\mathcal{G}_{\infty}\right){\mathcal{F}}
, where
{\mathcal{F}}
is the Fourier transform and
\chi\left(\mathcal{G}_{\infty}\right)
the characteristic function of a set
\mathcal{G}_{\infty}
, which has asymptotically full measure in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, see (
2.5
) and (
2.34
).

As already remarked in Section
1.1
, due to the validity of the ballistic upper bound (
1.2
) for all initial conditions
\Psi_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{S}_{1}
, Theorem
1.1
remains true if the Abel means are replaced by Cesaro means.

Theorem
1.1
will be proven in two steps. First we will show

Proposition 1.3
.

If
\Psi_{0}\in E_{\infty}\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}
,
\Psi_{0}\neq 0
,
E_{\infty}
being defined as in
[
27
]
,
then the solution
\Psi(\mathbf{x},t)
of (
1.5
) satisfies the ballistic lower bound (
1.12
).

Note that Proposition
1.3
differs from Theorem
1.1
by the fact that the initial condition
\Psi_{0}
for which the ballistic lower bound is concluded is in the image of
\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}
under the projection
E_{\infty}
(but that
\Psi_{0}
itself is not in
\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}
here). This proposition takes the role of our core technical result, i.e. most of the technical work towards proving Theorem
1.1
will go into the proof of the proposition. Theorem
1.1
gives a more explicit description of initial conditions for which ballistic transport can be established. In fact, one easily combines Theorem
1.1
with the ballistic upper bound (
1.2
) to get ballistic transport in form of a two-sided bound for many initial conditions:

Corollary 1.4
.

There is an
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
-dense and relatively open subset
\mathcal{D}
of
\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
such that for every
\Psi_{0}\in{\mathcal{D}}
there are constants
0<c_{1}\leq C_{1}<\infty
such that the ballistic upper bound (
1.2
) and the ballistic lower bound (
1.12
) holds for
T>T_{0}(\Psi_{0})
.

This follows by an elementary argument using only that
E_{\infty}
is not the zero projection and
\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is dense in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
(and that
\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
functions also satisfy (
1.3
)).

It is certainly desirable to go beyond this corollary and to more explicitly characterize classes of initial conditions for which (
1.11
) holds. This requires to much better describe and exploit the nature of the projection
E_{\infty}
. While we believe that
E_{\infty}\Psi_{0}\neq 0
for any non-zero
\Psi_{0}\in{\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, we do not have a proof of this. We will return to this question later, see Remark
3.1
, where we will more explicitly construct initial conditions which lead to both upper and lower ballistic transport bounds. These will have the form of suitably regularized generalized eigenfunction expansions.

As mentioned above, the proof of Theorem
1.1
is very similar to the two-dimensional proof
[
26
]
. We just need to use the recent results from
[
27
]
instead of
those in
[
29
]
. In what follows, we present the main steps in the proof and explain the changes we need to make in the proof due to the increase in dimension.

2.
Spectral Properties of the Operator
H

Our proofs of Proposition
1.3
and Theorem
1.1
are based on the results and properties of quasi-periodic Schrödinger operators derived in the paper
[
27
]
. While that work has derived, in particular, the existence of an absolutely continuous component of the spectrum, we will show now how the bounds obtained in
[
27
]
for the spectral projections can be used to prove the existence of ballistic transport. In this section we give a thorough discussion of the results and some of the methods from
[
27
]
. In particular, we give a detailed construction of the spectral projection
E_{\infty}
used in our main results. Unless stated otherwise, all statements in this section have been proved in
[
27
]
.

2.1.
Prior results.

For any given set of Fourier coefficients
\{V_{\boldsymbol{n}}\}
,
V_{-\boldsymbol{n}}={\bar{V}_{\boldsymbol{n}}}
,
|\boldsymbol{n}|\leq Q
,
Q\in{\mathbb{N}}
, there exists a subset
\Omega_{*}=\Omega_{*}(\{V_{\boldsymbol{n}}\})\subset[-1/2,1/2]^{dl}
of basic frequencies with
\hbox{meas}\,(\Omega_{*})=1
such that for any
\vec{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\in\Omega_{*}
the following statements hold, for sufficiently small positive number
\sigma
, depending on
V
,
l
and
d
only.

(1)

The spectrum of the operator (
1.6
)
contains a semi-axis.

(2)

There are generalized eigenfunctions
U_{\infty}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{x})
,
corresponding to the semi-axis, which are close to the unperturbed exponentials. More precisely,
for every
\mathbf{k}
in an extensive (in the sense of (
2.5
) below) subset
\mathcal{G}_{\infty}
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
there is
a solution
U_{\infty}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{x})
of the equation

HU_{\infty}=\lambda_{\infty}U_{\infty}

that satisfies the following properties:

U_{\infty}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{x})=e^{i\langle\mathbf{k},\mathbf{x}\rangle}\left(1+u_{\infty}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{x})\right),

(2.1)

\|u_{\infty}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}=_{|\mathbf{k}|\to\infty}O(|\mathbf{k}|^{-\gamma_{1}}),\ \ \ \gamma_{1}=1-\sigma>0,

(2.2)

where
u_{\infty}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{x})
is a quasi-periodic function:

u_{\infty}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{x}):=\sum_{\boldsymbol{r}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{l}}c_{\boldsymbol{r}}(\mathbf{k})\boldsymbol{e}_{\boldsymbol{r}\vec{\boldsymbol{\omega}}}(\mathbf{x}),

(2.3)

the series converging in
L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
The eigenvalue
\lambda_{\infty}(\mathbf{k})
corresponding to

U_{\infty}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{x})
is close to
|\mathbf{k}|^{2}
:

\lambda_{\infty}(\mathbf{k})=_{|\mathbf{k}|\to\infty}|\mathbf{k}|^{2}+O(|\mathbf{k}|^{-\gamma_{2}}),\ \ \ \gamma_{2}=2-\sigma>0.

(2.4)

The “non-resonant” set
\mathcal{G}_{\infty}
of the vectors
\mathbf{k}
, for which (
2.1
) to (
2.4
) hold,
can be expressed as

\mathcal{G}_{\infty}=\cap_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{G}_{n}
,
where
\{\mathcal{G}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a decreasing sequence of sets in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Each
\mathcal{G}_{n}
has a finite number of holes in each bounded region. Typically, as
n
increases,
more holes of smaller sizes appear in the intersection. As a result, the overall intersection
\mathcal{G}_{\infty}
is, typically, a Cantor type set (i.e., it has empty interior).
This set satisfies the estimate:

\frac{{\mathrm{meas}}\left(\mathcal{G}_{\infty}\cap B_{R}\right)}{{\mathrm{meas}}(B_{R})}=_{R\to\infty}1+O(R^{-c\sigma}),\quad\sigma>0,\ \ \ c=c(l,d,\vec{\boldsymbol{\omega}}),

(2.5)

where
B_{R}
is the ball of radius
R
centred at the origin.

(3)

The set
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)
, defined as a level (isoenergetic) set for

\lambda_{\infty}(\mathbf{k})
,

{\mathcal{D}}_{\infty}(\lambda)=\left\{\mathbf{k}\in\mathcal{G}_{\infty}:\lambda_{\infty}(\mathbf{k})=\lambda\right\},

is a slightly distorted sphere, typically with
infinite number of holes. It can be described by the formula:

{\mathcal{D}}_{\infty}(\lambda)=\{\mathbf{k}:\mathbf{k}=\varkappa_{\infty}(\lambda,\vec{\nu})\vec{\nu},\ \vec{\nu}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{\infty}(\lambda)\},

(2.6)

where
{\mathcal{B}}_{\infty}(\lambda)
is a subset of the unit
sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. The set
{\mathcal{B}}_{\infty}(\lambda)
can be
interpreted as the set of possible directions of propagation for the
almost plane waves (
2.1
). The set
{\mathcal{B}}_{\infty}(\lambda)
typically has a Cantor type structure and has an asymptotically full
measure on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
as
\lambda\to\infty
:

\hbox{meas}\,\left({\mathcal{B}}_{\infty}(\lambda)\right)=_{\lambda\to\infty}\hbox{meas}\,(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})+O\left(\lambda^{-c\sigma}\right).

(2.7)

The value
\varkappa_{\infty}(\lambda,\vec{\nu})
in (
2.6
) is
the “radius” of
{\mathcal{D}}_{\infty}(\lambda)
in a direction

\vec{\nu}
. The function
\varkappa_{\infty}(\lambda,\vec{\nu})-\lambda^{1/2}
describes the deviation of
{\mathcal{D}}_{\infty}(\lambda)
from the perfect sphere of radius

\lambda^{1/2}
. It is proven that the deviation is asymptotically
small, uniformly in
\vec{\nu}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{\infty}(\lambda)
:

\varkappa_{\infty}(\lambda,\vec{\nu})=_{\lambda\to\infty}\lambda^{1/2}+O\left(\lambda^{-\gamma_{3}}\right),\ \ \ \gamma_{3}=(3-\sigma)/2>0.

(2.8)

(4)

The part of the spectrum corresponding to
\{U_{\infty}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{x})\}_{\mathbf{k}}
is absolutely continuous.

Remark 2.1
.

While parameter
\sigma
can be chosen arbitrary small, all constants in
O(\cdot)
depend on
\sigma
. For the purposes of this paper, we will not need to impose any additional assumptions on
\sigma
on top of those assumed in
[
27
]
(in particular,
\sigma<(100d)^{-1}
).

2.2.
Description of the method.

To prove the results formulated in previous sub-section, in
[
27
]
we have considered the sequence of operators
H_{n}=H_{n}(\mathbf{k})
, each being restriction of the operator
H
onto the linear subspace of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{l}
spanned by the exponentials
\boldsymbol{e}_{\mathbf{k}+\boldsymbol{n}\vec{\boldsymbol{\omega}}}
,
|\boldsymbol{n}|\leq|\mathbf{k}|^{r_{n}}
. Here,
r_{n}
is a super exponentially growing sequence of numbers of the form

Each operator
H_{n}
,
n\geq 0
, is considered as a perturbation of
the previous operator
H_{n-1}
(
H_{-1}=-\Delta
). For every operator

H_{n}
, there is one eigenvalue located sufficiently far (at least
\sim|\mathbf{k}|^{-r_{n}}
away) from the rest of the spectrum of
H_{n}
. Corresponding eigenvector is close to the unperturbed exponential. More precisely,
for every
\mathbf{k}
in a certain subset
\mathcal{G}_{n}
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, there
is a solution
U_{n}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{x})
of the differential equation

H_{n}U_{n}=\lambda_{n}U_{n}
that satisfies the following asymptotic formula:

U_{n}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{x})=e^{i\langle\mathbf{k},\mathbf{x}\rangle}\left(1+u_{n}(\mathbf{k},\vec{x})\right),\ \ \ \|u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\underset{|\mathbf{k}|\to\infty}{=}O(|\mathbf{k}|^{-\gamma_{1}}),

(2.9)

where
u_{n}(\mathbf{k},\cdot)
is quasi-periodic, a finite combination of
\boldsymbol{e}_{\boldsymbol{r}\vec{\boldsymbol{\omega}}}(\mathbf{x})
:

u_{n}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{x}):=\sum_{\boldsymbol{r}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{l},|\boldsymbol{r}|<M_{n}}c_{\boldsymbol{r}}^{(n)}(\mathbf{k})\boldsymbol{e}_{\boldsymbol{r}\vec{\boldsymbol{\omega}}}(\mathbf{x}),\ \ \ M_{n}\to\infty\ \mbox{as}\ n\to\infty.

(2.10)

The corresponding eigenvalue
\lambda_{n}(\mathbf{k})
is close to
|\vec{k}|^{2}
:

\lambda_{n}(\mathbf{k})=_{|\mathbf{k}|\to\infty}|\mathbf{k}|^{2}+O\left(|\mathbf{k}|^{-\gamma_{2}}\right).

(2.11)

The non-resonant set
\mathcal{G}_{n}
for which (
2.11
) holds,
is proven to be extensive in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
:

\frac{{\mathrm{meas}}(\mathcal{G}_{n}\cap B_{R})}{{\mathrm{meas}}(B_{R})}=_{R\to\infty}1+O(R^{-\sigma}).

(2.12)

The estimates (
2.9
) – (
2.12
) are uniform in
n
.
The set
{\mathcal{D}}_{n}(\lambda)
is defined as the level
(isoenergetic) set for the non-resonant eigenvalue
\lambda_{n}(\vec{k})
:

{\mathcal{D}}_{n}(\lambda):=\left\{\mathbf{k}\in\mathcal{G}_{n}:\lambda_{n}(\mathbf{k})=\lambda\right\}.

This set is a slightly distorted sphere with a finite number of holes; it can also be
described by the formula:

{\mathcal{D}}_{n}(\lambda)=\{\mathbf{k}:\mathbf{k}=\varkappa_{n}(\lambda,\vec{\nu})\vec{\nu},\ \vec{\nu}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(\lambda)\},

(2.13)

where
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(\lambda)
is a subset of the unit sphere

\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. The set
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(\lambda)
can be interpreted as
the set of possible directions of propagation for almost plane
waves (
2.9
). The sequence of sets
\{{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(\lambda)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is decreasing,
since on each step more and more directions are excluded. Each

{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(\lambda)
has an asymptotically full measure on

\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
as
\lambda\to\infty
:

\hbox{meas}\,\left({\mathcal{B}}_{n}(\lambda)\right)=_{\lambda\to\infty}\hbox{meas}\left(\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\right)+O\left(\lambda^{-\sigma/2}\right),

(2.14)

the estimate being uniform in
n
. The set
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(\lambda)
has
only a finite number of holes, however their number is growing with

n
. The value
\varkappa_{n}(\lambda,\vec{\nu})-\lambda^{1/2}
gives the deviation of
{\mathcal{D}}_{n}(\lambda)

from the perfect sphere of radius
\lambda^{1/2}
in
direction
\vec{\nu}
. This deviation is
asymptotically small uniformly in
n
:

\varkappa_{n}(\lambda,\vec{\nu})=\lambda^{1/2}+O\left(\lambda^{-\gamma_{3}}\right),\ \ \ \ \frac{\partial\varkappa_{n}(\lambda,\vec{\nu})}{\partial\vec{\varphi}}=O\left(\lambda^{-\gamma_{3}}\right),

(2.15)

\vec{\varphi}
being an angle variable associated with natural spherical coordinates (see
[
27
]
for more details).

More and more points are excluded from the
non-resonant sets
\mathcal{G}_{n}
on each step. Thus,
\{\mathcal{G}_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is a decreasing sequence of sets. The set

\mathcal{G}_{\infty}
is defined as the limit set:
\mathcal{G}_{\infty}=\cap_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{G}_{n}
. It has typically an infinite
number of holes in each bounded region, but nevertheless satisfies
the relation (
2.5
). For every
\mathbf{k}\in\mathcal{G}_{\infty}
and every
n
, there is a generalized eigenfunction of
H_{n}

of the type (
2.9
). It is proven that the sequence of
U_{n}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{x})
has a limit in
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
as
n\to\infty
, when
\mathbf{k}\in\mathcal{G}_{\infty}
. The function
U_{\infty}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{x})=\lim_{n\to\infty}U_{n}(\mathbf{k},\vec{x})
is a generalized eigenfunction of
H
. It can be written in the
form (
2.1
)–(
2.2
). Naturally, the corresponding
eigenvalue
\lambda_{\infty}(\mathbf{k})
is the limit of
\lambda_{n}(\mathbf{k})
as
n\to\infty
.
Expansion with respect to the generalized eigenfunctions
\Psi_{\infty}(\mathbf{k},\cdot)
,
\mathbf{k}\in\mathcal{G}_{\infty}
, will give a reducing subspace for
H
, with the corresponding spectral resolution arising as the limit of spectral resolutions for
operators
H_{n}
.

To study them, one needs properties of the limit
{\mathcal{B}}_{\infty}(\lambda)
of
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(\lambda)
:

{\mathcal{B}}_{\infty}(\lambda)=\bigcap_{n=0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(\lambda),\ \ \ {\mathcal{B}}_{n}(\lambda)\subset{\mathcal{B}}_{n-1}(\lambda).

This set has asymptotically full measure, as (
2.7
) follows from (
2.14
).
The sequence
\varkappa_{n}(\lambda,\vec{\nu})
,
n=0,1,2,...
,
describing the isoenergetic sets
\mathcal{D}_{n}(\lambda)
, quickly converges as
n\to\infty
.
Hence,
{\mathcal{D}}_{\infty}(\lambda)
can be described as the
limit of
{\mathcal{D}}_{n}(\lambda)
in the sense (
2.6
), where

\varkappa_{\infty}(\lambda,\vec{\nu})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\varkappa_{n}(\lambda,\vec{\nu})
for every
\vec{\nu}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{\infty}(\lambda)
. The derivatives of the
functions
\varkappa_{n}(\lambda,\vec{\nu})
(with respect to the
angle variable
\vec{\varphi}
) have a limit as
n\to\infty
for every
\vec{\nu}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{\infty}(\lambda)
.
We denote this limit by
\frac{\partial\varkappa_{\infty}(\lambda,\vec{\nu})}{\partial\vec{\varphi}}
. We also have

\frac{\partial\varkappa_{\infty}(\lambda,\vec{\nu})}{\partial\vec{\varphi}}=O\left(\lambda^{-\gamma_{3}}\right).

(2.16)

Thus, the limit set
{\mathcal{D}}_{\infty}(\lambda)
takes the form of
a slightly distorted sphere with, possibly,
infinite number of holes.

Let
\mathcal{G}_{n}^{\prime}
be a bounded Lebesgue measurable subset of
\mathcal{G}_{n}
.
We consider the spectral projection

E_{n}\left(\mathcal{G}_{n}^{\prime}\right)
of
H_{n}
, corresponding to functions

U_{n}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{x})
,
\mathbf{k}\in\mathcal{G}_{n}^{\prime}.

By
[
16
]
,
E_{n}\left(\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{n}\right):L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
can be represented by the formula:

E_{n}\left(\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{n}\right)F=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{n}}\bigl(F,U_{n}(\vec{k})\bigr)U_{n}(\vec{k})\,d\mathbf{k}

(2.17)

for any
F\in{\mathcal{C}_{c}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, the space of continuous, compactly supported functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Here and below,
\bigl(\cdot,\cdot\bigr)

is the integral corresponding to the canonical scalar product in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, i.e.,

\bigl(F,U_{n}(\mathbf{k})\bigr)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}F(\mathbf{x})\overline{U_{n}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{x})}\,d\mathbf{x}.

The above formula can be rewritten in the form

E_{n}\left(\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{n}\right)=S_{n}\left(\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{n}\right)T_{n}\left(\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{n}\right),

(2.18)

T_{n}:{\mathcal{C}_{c}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to L^{2}\left(\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{n}\right),\ \ \ \ S_{n}:L^{\infty}\left(\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{n}\right)\to L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),

(T_{n}F)(\mathbf{k}):=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\bigl(F,U_{n}(\vec{k})\bigr)\mbox{\ \ for any $F\in{\mathcal{C}_{c}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})$},

(2.19)

T_{n}F
being in
L^{\infty}\left(\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{n}\right)
, and

(S_{n}f)(\mathbf{x}):=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\int_{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{n}}f(\mathbf{k})U_{n}(\vec{k},\mathbf{x})\,d\mathbf{k}\mbox{\ \ for any $f\in L^{\infty}\left(\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{n}\right)$.}

(2.20)

By
[
16
]
,

\|T_{n}F\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{n}\right)}\leq\|F\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

(2.21)

and

\|S_{n}f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{n}\right)}.

(2.22)

Hence,
T_{n}
and
S_{n}
can be extended by continuity from
{\mathcal{C}_{c}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
L^{\infty}\left(\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{n}\right)
to
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
L^{2}\left(\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{n}\right)
,
respectively. Obviously,
T_{n}^{*}=S_{n}
. Thus, the operator
E_{n}\left(\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{n}\right)
is described by (
2.18
) in the whole space
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

In what follows we will use these operators for the case where
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{n}
is given by

\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{n}=\mathcal{G}_{n,\lambda}:=\{\mathbf{k}\in{\mathcal{G}}_{n}:\lambda_{n}(\mathbf{k})<\lambda\}.

(2.23)

for finite sufficiently large
\lambda
. This set is Lebesgue measurable since
{\mathcal{G}}_{n}
is
open and
\lambda_{n}(\mathbf{k})
is continuous on
{\mathcal{G}}_{n}
.

Let

\mathcal{G}_{\infty,\lambda}=\left\{\mathbf{k}\in\mathcal{G}_{\infty}:\lambda_{\infty}(\mathbf{k})<\lambda\right\}.

(2.24)

The function
\lambda_{\infty}(\mathbf{k})
is a Lebesgue
measurable function, since it is a pointwise limit of a sequence of
measurable functions. Hence, the set
\mathcal{G}_{\infty,\lambda}
is measurable. The sets
\mathcal{G}_{n,\lambda}
and
\mathcal{G}_{\infty,\lambda}
are also bounded. The measure of the symmetric difference of the
two sets
\mathcal{G}_{\infty,\lambda}
and
\mathcal{G}_{n,\lambda}
converges
to zero as
n\to\infty
, uniformly in
\lambda
in every bounded interval:

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathrm{meas}}(\mathcal{G}_{\infty,\lambda}\,\Delta\,\mathcal{G}_{n,\lambda})=0.

Next, we consider the sequence of operators
S_{n}(\mathcal{G}_{\infty,\lambda})
given by (
2.20
)
and with
{\mathcal{G}}_{n}^{\prime}=\mathcal{G}_{\infty,\lambda}
;

S_{n}(\mathcal{G}_{\infty,\lambda}):\ L^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{\infty,\lambda})\to L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}).

(2.25)

This sequence
has a limit
S_{\infty}(\mathcal{G}_{\infty,\lambda})
in operator norm sense as
n\to\infty
, uniform in
\lambda
. Moreover, the estimate

\|S_{\infty}(\mathcal{G}_{\infty,\lambda})-S_{-1}(\mathcal{G}_{\infty,\lambda})\|<c\lambda_{*}^{-\gamma_{1}}

(2.26)

holds for
\lambda>\lambda_{*}
,
c
not depending on
\lambda,\lambda_{*}
. Here we put
U_{-1}=e^{i\langle\mathbf{k},\mathbf{x}\rangle}
and define
S_{-1}
by (
2.20
). The operator
S_{\infty}(\mathcal{G}_{\infty,\lambda})
satisfies
\|S_{\infty}\|=1
and can be described by the formula

(2.27)

for any

.

Similarly, we consider the sequence of operators
T_{n}(\mathcal{G}_{\infty,\lambda})
which are given by (
2.19
) and act from
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
to
L^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{\infty,\lambda})

Since

, the sequence

has a limit
T_{\infty}(\mathcal{G}_{\infty,\lambda})=S^{*}_{\infty}(\mathcal{G}_{\infty,\lambda})
in operator norm sense. The operator
T_{\infty}(\mathcal{G}_{\infty,\lambda})
satisfies
\|T_{\infty}\|\leq 1
and can be described by the formula

(T_{\infty}F)(\mathbf{k})=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\bigl(F,\Psi_{\infty}(\mathbf{k})\bigr)
for any

.
The convergence is uniform in
\lambda

and

\|T_{\infty}(\mathcal{G}_{\infty,\lambda})-T_{-1}(\mathcal{G}_{\infty,\lambda})\|<c\lambda_{*}^{-\gamma_{1}}.

Spectral projections

converge in norm to

in

as

tends to infinity, since

. The operator

is a spectral projection of

. It can be represented
in the form
E_{\infty}(\mathcal{G}_{\infty,\lambda})=S_{\infty}(\mathcal{G}_{\infty,\lambda})T_{\infty}(\mathcal{G}_{\infty,\lambda})
.
For any
F\in{\mathcal{C}_{c}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
we have

E_{\infty}\left(\mathcal{G}_{\infty,\lambda}\right)F=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathcal{G}_{\infty,\lambda}}\bigl(F,\Psi_{\infty}(\vec{k})\bigr)\Psi_{\infty}(\vec{k})\,d\mathbf{k},

(2.29)

(2.30)

Since

is a projection, one has the Parseval formula

\|E_{\infty}(\mathcal{G}_{\infty,\lambda})F\|^{2}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathcal{G}_{\infty,\lambda}}|(F,\Psi_{\infty}(\mathbf{k})|^{2}\,d\mathbf{k}.

(2.31)

It is easy to see that

\|E_{\infty}(\mathcal{G}_{\infty,\lambda})-S_{-1}T_{-1}(\mathcal{G}_{\infty,\lambda})\|<c\lambda_{*}^{-\gamma_{1}},

(2.32)

S_{-1}T_{-1}(\mathcal{G}_{\infty,\lambda})={\mathcal{F}}^{*}\chi(\mathcal{G}_{\infty,\lambda}){\mathcal{F}}.

(2.33)

Projections

are increasing in

and have a strong limit

E_{\infty}(\mathcal{G}_{\infty})
as
\lambda
goes to infinity.
Hence, the operator
E_{\infty}(\mathcal{G}_{\infty})
is a projection. The projections

E_{\infty}(\mathcal{G}_{\infty,\lambda})
,
\lambda\geq\lambda_{*}
, and

reduce the operator

.
The family of projections

is the resolution of the
identity of the operator

acting in

E_{\infty}(\mathcal{G}_{\infty})L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

and use

\|E_{\infty}-{\mathcal{F}}^{*}\chi(\mathcal{G}_{\infty}){\mathcal{F}}\|<c\lambda_{*}^{-\gamma_{1}}.

(2.34)

Obviously, the r.h.s. can be made arbitrarily small by an appropriate choice of
\mathcal{G}_{\infty}
.

The restriction of

to the range of

has purely absolutely continuous spectrum. In addition to the above mentioned convergence of the spectral projections of

to those of

2.3.
Extension of
\lambda_{\infty}(\mathbf{k})
from
\mathcal{G}_{\infty}
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

Let

be a large natural number; for the purposes of this paper, taking

We want to extend the function
\lambda_{\infty}(\mathbf{k})
from
\mathcal{G}_{\infty}
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, the result being a
\mathcal{C}^{M}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

The first step is representing

,
k:=|\mathbf{k}|
,
\mathbf{k}\in{{\mathcal{G}}}_{\infty}

[
27
]

\left|D^{\mathbf{m}}_{\mathbf{k}}\left(\lambda_{0}(\mathbf{k})-k^{2}\right)\right|<Ck^{-\gamma_{2}+\sigma|m|},\ \ \gamma_{2}=2-\sigma,

(2.35)

when
\mathbf{k}
is in the

-neighborhood of
{{\mathcal{G}}}_{0}\supset{{\mathcal{G}}}_{\infty}
and

\left|D^{\mathbf{m}}_{\mathbf{k}}\left(\lambda_{n}(\mathbf{k})-\lambda_{n-1}(\mathbf{k})\right)\right|<Ck^{-k^{r_{n-1}}+|m|k^{\sigma r_{n-1}}}

(2.36)

in the
k^{-k^{\sigma r_{n-1}}}
-neighbourhood of
{{\mathcal{G}}}_{n}
for all

. Here, the constants depend only on
V
and

Now, we introduce a function
\eta_{0}(\mathbf{k})\in{\mathcal{C}^{\infty}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with support in the (real)

-neighbourhood of
{{\mathcal{G}}}_{0}
,
satisfying
\eta_{0}=1
on
{{\mathcal{G}}}_{0}

(2.37)

This is possible since we can take a convolution of the characteristic function of the

-neighbourhood of
{{\mathcal{G}}}_{0}
with

,
where

is a non-negative

-function with a support in the unit ball centred at the origin and integral one.
Similarly, let
\eta_{n}(\mathbf{k})
,

\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
function with support in the

-neighbourhood of
{{\mathcal{G}}}_{n}

on
{{\mathcal{G}}}_{n}

Next, we extend

{{\mathcal{G}}}_{\infty}
to

(2.39)

could be chosen arbitrary small (note that

increases and

{{\mathcal{G}}}_{\infty}

3.

1.1

Let

Now we define

\Psi(\mathbf{x},t):=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\int_{\mathcal{G}_{\infty}}U_{\infty}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{x})e^{-it\lambda_{\infty}(\mathbf{k})}\widehat{\Psi}_{0}(\mathbf{k})\,d\mathbf{k},

where

to be chosen later, and

\Psi

\mathcal{G}_{\infty}
is replaced by its small neighbourhood

w(\mathbf{x},t):=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\int_{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\infty}}U_{\infty}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{x})e^{-it\lambda_{\infty}(\mathbf{k})}\widehat{\Psi}_{0}(\mathbf{k})\tilde{\eta}_{\delta}(\mathbf{k})\,d\mathbf{k},

(3.4)

\delta

\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\infty}
of
\mathcal{G}_{\infty}
and
\tilde{\eta}_{\delta}=1

\mathcal{G}_{\infty}
.

will be chosen later to be sufficiently small and depend only on
\widehat{\Psi}_{0}
. We take
\tilde{\eta}_{\delta}
to be a convolution of a function

\omega(\mathbf{k}/2\delta)

\delta/2
-neighbourhood of
\mathcal{G}_{\infty}
, where
\omega
is a smooth cut-off function defined in the previous section. Then,
\tilde{\eta}_{\delta}\in{\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,

0\leq\tilde{\eta}_{\delta}\leq 1,\ \tilde{\eta}_{\delta}(\mathbf{k})=1\;\mbox{when }\mathbf{k}\in\mathcal{G}_{\infty},\ \tilde{\eta}_{\delta}(\mathbf{k})=0\;\mbox{when }\mathbf{k}\not\in\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\infty},\ \|D^{m}\tilde{\eta}_{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}}<C_{m}\delta^{-|m|}.

(3.5)

To prove (
1.12
), we will show that there exist a positive constant

and constants
c_{2}
and

such that

\frac{2}{T}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-2t/T}\big\|Xw(\cdot,t)\big\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{R})}dt\geq 6c_{1}T^{2}-c_{2}T-c_{3},

Remark 3.1
.

(a) The above proofs show that Theorem

remains true if we replace

{\mathcal{S}}_{d}:=\{f:|x|^{s}D^{\mathbf{m}}f(x)\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),0\leq s,|m|\leq C(d)\}

i.e. for initial conditions which are sufficiently smooth and of sufficiently rapid power decay.

(b) Using the constructions in the above proofs, we can now also describe more explicitly how to choose initial conditions
\Psi_{0}
for the solution of (

) which give simultaneous ballistic upper and lower bounds. Essentially, one has to regularize elements in the range of
E_{\infty}
in two different ways, first at the boundary of
{\mathcal{G}}_{\infty}
, using the cut-off function

\mathbf{k}
. For the latter, let
\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{d}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and such that
\varphi
does not vanish identically on
{\mathcal{G}}_{\infty}

Choose

\Psi_{0}(\mathbf{x}):=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\int_{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\infty}}\varphi(\mathbf{k})\,\tilde{\eta}_{\delta}(\mathbf{k})\,U_{\infty}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{x})\,d\mathbf{k}.

(3.10)

As

this converges to

in the range of
E_{\infty}
with

sufficiently small,

Furthermore, our methods show that the choice of

\Psi_{0}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{d}

\Psi_{0}
leads to a ballistic lower bound on transport. At the same time the condition of
[
35
]
for the ballistic upper bound (

) is satisfied.

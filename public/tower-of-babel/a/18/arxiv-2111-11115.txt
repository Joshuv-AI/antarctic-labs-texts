Simple models for strictly non-ergodic stochastic processes of macroscopic systems
G. George, L. Klochko, A.N. Semenov, J. Baschnagel, J.P. Wittmer

Abstract
We investigate simple models for strictly non-ergodic stochastic processes x t x_{t} ( t t being the discrete time step) focusing on the expectation value v v and the standard deviation δ ​ v \delta v of the empirical variance v ⁡ [ 𝐱 ] v[\mathbf{x}] of finite time series 𝐱 \mathbf{x} . x t x_{t} is averaged over a fluctuating field σ 𝐫 \sigma_{{\bf r}} ( 𝐫 {\bf r} being the microcell position) characterized by a quenched spatially correlated Gaussian field g 𝐫 g_{{\bf r}} . Due to the quenched g 𝐫 g_{{\bf r}} -field δ ​ v ​ ( Δ ​ τ ) \delta v(\Delta\tau) becomes a finite constant, Δ ne > 0 \Delta_{\mathrm{ne}}>0 , for large sampling times Δ ​ τ \Delta\tau . The volume dependence of the non-ergodicity parameter Δ ne \Delta_{\mathrm{ne}} is investigated for different spatial correlations. Models with marginally long-ranged g 𝐫 g_{{\bf r}} -correlations are successfully mapped on shear-stress data from simulated amorphous glasses of polydisperse beads.

Conclusion

Extending recent work
spmP1
;
spmP2
the present study focused on the expectation value

v(\Delta\tau)
and the standard deviations
\delta v_{\mathrm{tot}}(\Delta\tau)
,
\delta v_{\mathrm{int}}(\Delta\tau)
and
\delta v_{\mathrm{ext}}(\Delta\tau)

of the empirical variance
v[\mathbf{x}]
of time series
\mathbf{x}
, Eq. (
1
),
of strictly non-ergodic stochastic processes recorded over a sampling time
\Delta\tau
.

Our first aim was to give an uncluttered summary (Sec.
2
)
of some useful notations (Sec.
2.1
)
and general relations important for the characterization of ensembles

\{\mathbf{x}_{ck}\}
of such time series.
At variance to ergodic processes the external standard deviation
\delta v_{\mathrm{ext}}(\Delta\tau)

becomes for non-ergodic systems constant,
\delta v_{\mathrm{ext}}(\Delta\tau)\simeq\Delta_{\mathrm{ne}}>0
,
for large
\Delta\tau\gg\tau_{\mathrm{b}}
, and thus in turn so does also the total standard deviation

\delta v_{\mathrm{tot}}(\Delta\tau)
for
\Delta\tau\gg\tau_{\mathrm{ne}}(V)\gg\tau_{\mathrm{b}}
, Eq. (
12
).

Our second aim was to emphasize by means of a simple analytically
feasible example (Figs.
1
and
2
)
that it is therefore questionable to numerically determine the
system-size exponent
\gamma_{\mathrm{ext}}
of
\Delta_{\mathrm{ne}}(V)
uniquely from
the total standard deviation
\delta v_{\mathrm{tot}}(\Delta\tau=const,V)
.
We argued that one should rather analyze the more rapidly converging

\delta v_{\mathrm{ext}}(\Delta\tau,V)
both with respect to
\Delta\tau
and
V
.

Our third aim was to better understand the system-size dependence
of the static properties
v
and
\Delta_{\mathrm{ne}}
for
\Delta\tau\gg\tau_{\mathrm{b}}

in systems with correlated microcells (Sec.
3
).
We have thus investigated in Sec.
4
simple models where the
(unaveraged) fluctuating microscopic contributions
\sigma_{{\bf r}}

are essentially decorrelated but their (rescaled)
k
-averaged standard deviation
s_{{\bf r}}
may not.
For simplicity these frozen fields
s_{{\bf r}}=|g_{{\bf r}}|

were modelled by spatially correlated Gaussian fields
g_{{\bf r}}

(Appendix
A
).

v
and
\Delta_{\mathrm{ne}}
are given, respectively, by the moments
\mu_{2}
and
\Delta_{2}
of the
g_{{\bf r}}
-field,
Eq. (
35
).
We have thus expressed
\Delta_{\mathrm{ne}}
in terms of an effective two-point correlation function

C_{2}({\bf r})
, Eq. (
36
).
For consistency with general intensive thermodynamic fields (Sec.
3.2
),

\mu_{2}
is set to be
V
-independent (
\gamma_{\mathrm{int}}=0
).
As seen in Fig.
4
for models A and B and in Fig.
5
for model C
with
\alpha_{2}>d

\gamma_{\mathrm{ext}}=1/2

Logarithmic corrections become relevant for models C and D for
\alpha_{2}\to d

where

\tilde{\gamma}_{\mathrm{ext}}\approx 0.4
(Fig.

A similar alternative approach yielding numerically equivalent results
is mentioned in Appendix

Our fourth aim was to point out (Sec.

behavior is observed for shear-stress fluctuations in amorphous glasses
spmP2
.
By insisting on
\alpha_{1}=d=2\approx\alpha_{2}
and tuning the parameters
\mu_{1}

and
\xi

;

marginally
long-range correlations of
quenched shear-stress fluctuations.

Obviously, this does not necessarily imply that other aspects of the stress correlations
in these systems are captured by simple models based on the key postulate
Eq. (
35

on the technical relation Eq. (
37

To clarify this issue future work
spmP4

\mathbf{E}^{k}
and
\mathbf{E}^{{\bf r}}

{\bf r}^{\prime}
and

11

11

{\bf r}

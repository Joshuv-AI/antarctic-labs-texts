On overgroups of distinguished unipotent elements in reductive groups and finite groups of Lie type
Michael Bate, Sören Böhm, Benjamin Martin, Gerhard Röhrle

Abstract
. Suppose G G is a simple algebraic group defined over an algebraically closed field of good characteristic p p . In 2018 Korhonen showed that if H H is a connected reductive subgroup of G G which contains a distinguished unipotent element u u of G G of order p p , then H H is G G -irreducible in the sense of Serre. We present a short and uniform proof of this result using so-called good A 1 A_{1} subgroups of G G , introduced by Seitz. We also formulate a counterpart of Korhonen’s theorem for overgroups of u u which are finite groups of Lie type. Moreover, we generalize both results above by removing the restriction on the order of u u under a mild condition on p p depending on the rank of G G , and we present an analogue of Korhonen’s theorem for Lie algebras.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notation

Throughout, we work over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic
p\geq 0
. All affine varieties are considered over
k
and are identified with their
k
-points.
A linear algebraic group

over
k
has identity component
H^{\circ}
; if

, then we say that

is

.
We denote by

of

is trivial, then we say

.

Throughout,

denotes a connected reductive linear algebraic group over

. All subgroups of

By

we denote the derived subgroup of

, and likewise for subgroups of

.
We denote the Lie algebra of

or by
\mathfrak{g}
.

Let

denote the set of cocharacters of

For

and

for

;

.
For

, let

denote the set of cocharacters of

. There is an obvious

.

Fix a Borel subgroup

of
G
containing a maximal torus

.
Let
\Phi=\Phi(G,T)
be the root system of

with respect to
T
,
let
\Phi^{+}=\Phi(B,T)
be the set of positive roots of
G
, and let

\Sigma=\Sigma(G,T)

be the set of simple roots of
\Phi^{+}
.
For each
\alpha\in\Phi
we have a root subgroup

of
G
. For

in
\Phi
, let

be a parametrization of the root subgroup

of

.

2.2.
Good primes

A prime

is said to be

if it does not divide any coefficient of any positive root when expressed as a linear combination of simple ones. Else

,
[

Explicitly, if

,

, or

,

.
We observe that if

2.3.
Springer isomorphisms

If

is good for
G
and
\mathscr{D}G
is simply connected, then there exists a
G
-equivariant homeomorphism
\phi:\mathscr{U}\to\mathscr{N}

between the unipotent variety

of

\mathscr{N}
of

[

By means of such a map, all the results below formulated for unipotent elements in

In what follows we fix such a Springer isomorphism once and for all.

2.4.
Steinberg endomorphisms of
G

Recall that a

of

\sigma:G\to G
such that the
corresponding fixed point subgroup
G_{\sigma}:=\{g\in G\mid\sigma(g)=g\}
of

Frobenius endomorphisms

of reductive groups over finite fields are familiar examples, giving rise to

. See

The set of all Steinberg endomorphisms of

which encompasses in particular all generalized Frobenius endomorphisms,

-rational structure on

In that case we also denote the finite group of Lie type

If

-stable members of

If

\sigma_{q}
-stable

[

Following

does not involve a twisted Steinberg endomorphism, see

In particular, if

If

2.5.
Bala–Carter Theory

We recall some relevant results and concepts from Bala–Carter Theory.
A parabolic subgroup
P
of
G
admits a dense open orbit on its unipotent radical
R_{u}(P)
, the so-called

; see
[
9
, Thm. 5.2.1]
.
A parabolic subgroup
P
of

is called

\dim(\mathscr{D}P/R_{u}(P))=\dim(R_{u}(P)/\mathscr{D}R_{u}(P))
, see
[
9
, §5.8]
.
For
G
simple, the distinguished parabolic subgroups of
G
(up to
G

worked out in
[
2
]
and
[
3
]
; see

[
9
, pp. 174–177]
.

The following is the celebrated Bala–Carter theorem, see
[
9
, Thm. 5.9.5, Thm. 5.9.6]
, which is valid in good characteristic, thanks to work of Pommerening
[
23
]
,
[
24
]
. For the Lie algebra versions see also

[

, Prop. 4.7, Thm. 4.13]

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose

is good for
G
.

(i)

There is a bijective map between the
G
-conjugacy classes of distinguished unipotent elements of
G
and conjugacy classes of distinguished parabolic subgroups of
G
. The unipotent class corresponding to a given parabolic subgroup
P
contains the dense
P
-orbit on
R_{u}(P)
.

(ii)

There is a bijective map between the
G
-conjugacy classes of unipotent elements of
G
and conjugacy classes of pairs
(L,P)
, where
L
is a Levi subgroup of
G
and
P
is a
distinguished parabolic subgroup of
\mathscr{D}L
. The unipotent class corresponding to the pair
(L,P)
contains the dense
P
-orbit on
R_{u}(P)
.

Remark 2.2
.

(i). Let
u\in G
be unipotent.
Let
S
be a maximal torus of

. Then

is distinguished
in the Levi subgroup

of

, for

is the unique maximal torus of

.
Conversely,

is a Levi subgroup of

then the connected center of

, see
[

.

(ii). Let

is

-stable. The set of all maximal tori of

2.6.
Cocharacters and parabolic subgroups of
G

Let
\lambda\in Y(G)
. Without loss, we may assume that
\lambda\in Y(T)
.
Recall that
\lambda

-grading on
\Lie(G)=\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{g}(j,\lambda)

\mathfrak{g}(j,\lambda):=\{e\in\mathfrak{g}\mid\Ad(\lambda(t))e=t^{j}e\text{ for every }t\in\mathbb{G}_{m}\}

is the

\Ad(\lambda(\mathbb{G}_{m}))
on
\mathfrak{g}
,
see

[
9
, §5.5]
or
[
15
, §5.1]
.
Let
\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}:=\bigoplus_{j\geq 0}\mathfrak{g}(j,\lambda)
.
Then there is a unique parabolic subgroup
P_{\lambda}
with

\Lie P_{\lambda}=\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}
and
C_{G}(\lambda):=C_{G}(\lambda(\mathbb{G}_{m}))
is a Levi subgroup of
P_{\lambda}
.
Specifically, we have
U_{\alpha}\subseteq P_{\lambda}

if and only if
\langle\lambda,\alpha\rangle\geq 0
, where
\langle\,,\,\rangle:Y(T)\times X(T)\to\mathbb{Z}
is the usual pairing between cocharacters and characters
X(T)=\Hom(T,\mathbb{G}_{m})
of
T
.
We have
U_{\alpha}\subseteq C_{G}(\lambda)
if and only if
\langle\lambda,\alpha\rangle=0
,
and
R_{u}(P_{\lambda})
is generated by the

with
\langle\lambda,\alpha\rangle>0
; see the proof of
[

, Prop. 8.4.5]
.

Set

.
Then
P_{\lambda}=P_{J}=\big\langle T,U_{\alpha}\mid\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle\geq 0\big\rangle
is the
standard parabolic subgroup
of

associated with

.

Let
\rho=\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma}c_{\alpha\rho}\alpha

be the highest root in
\Phi^{+}
. Define
\hgt_{J}(\rho):=\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma\setminus J}c_{\alpha\rho}
.
In view of Theorem
2.1
,
the following gives the order of a distinguished unipotent element in good characteristic.

Lemma 2.3

(
[

, Order Formula 0.4]
)
.

Let

be good for
G
. Let

be a distinguished parabolic subgroup of
G
and let

be in the Richardson orbit of

. Then the order of

.

2.7.
Cocharacters associated to nilpotent and unipotent elements

The Jacobson–Morozov Theorem allows one to associate an

\mathfrak{sl}(2)
-triple to any given non-zero nilpotent element in
\mathscr{N}

in characteristic zero or large positive characteristic.
This is an indispensable tool in the
Dynkin–Kostant classification of the
nilpotent orbits in characteristic zero as well as
in the Bala–Carter classification of unipotent conjugacy classes of
G

in large prime characteristic, see
[
9
, §5.9]
.
In good characteristic there is a replacement for

\mathfrak{sl}(2)
-triples, so-called

cocharacters
; see Definition
2.4
below. These cocharacters are important tools in the classification theory of
unipotent and nilpotent classes of reductive algebraic groups
in good characteristic, see

[

, §5]

[

]
. We recall the relevant concept
of cocharacters associated to a nilpotent element following

[

, §5.3]

Definition 2.4
.

A cocharacter
\lambda\in Y(G)
of

is

to

provided

and
there exists a Levi subgroup

of

such that

is distinguished nilpotent in
\Lie L
and

\lambda(\mathbb{G}_{m})\leq\mathscr{D}L

For the remainder of this section suppose that
\mathscr{D}G
is simply connected. A cocharacter
\lambda\in Y(G)
of

is

to

provided it is associated to

,
where

is a fixed Springer isomorphism as in §

[

, Rem. 23]

This notation stems from the fact that associated cocharacters are destabilising cocharacters of

[

]
and
[

]
. Likewise, for

Remark 2.5
.

Let

be unipotent,

\lambda\in\Omega^{a}_{G}(u)

.
Then

[

Proposition

3.
Generalizations of Theorems
1.1
and

The aim of this section is to generalize Theorem
1.1

to the situation where there is no restriction on the order of the unipotent element at hand, see Theorem
3.1
.
For a unipotent element

in

to be
distinguished is a mere condition on the structure of the centralizer

of

in

for

The restriction in Theorem

is due to the methods used in

and in our proofs in §

Along the way we prove an analogue of Theorem

for Lie algebras, i.e., under the hypothesis that

contains a distinguished nilpotent element of

In order to state this theorem,

For reductive

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose

. Let

be a reductive subgroup of

.
Suppose

contains a distinguished unipotent element of

or

contains a distinguished nilpotent element of

is

-irreducible.

The following analogue of
[

, Cor. 4.6]

shows that in order to derive

is
G
-cr, see also
[

, Lem. 6.1]
. This also applies to Theorem

and

.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
H
be a
G
-completely reducible subgroup of

. Suppose that

contains a distinguished unipotent element

of

or
\Lie(H)
contains a distinguished nilpotent element

of

. Then

is
G
-irreducible.

Proof.

Suppose

is contained in a parabolic subgroup

of

. Then,
by hypothesis,

is contained in a Levi subgroup

of

As the latter is the centraliser of a torus

in

,

∎

Along with Lemma

, the following theorem of Serre immediately yields Theorem

Theorem 3.3

(
[

, Thm. 4.4]
)

Suppose

.

(i). The characteristic restriction in Theorem

(iv).

H
is obviously
G
-irreducible. This is independent of
p
of course and thus applies in particular when
p<a(G)
.
For instance, let
G
be of type
E_{7}
,
p=5
, and suppose
u
belongs to the distinguished class
E_{7}(a_{3})
(resp.
E_{7}(a_{4})
,
E_{7}(a_{5})
). Then
\hgt_{J}(\rho)=9
(resp.
7
,
5
), so
u
has order
5^{2}
, by Lemma
2.3
in each case.
Since
u
does not have order
5
, Theorem
1.1
does not apply, and since
5<8=a(G)
neither does Theorem
3.1
.
Nevertheless, in each case
u
is contained in a maximal rank subgroup
H
of type
A_{1}D_{6}
, see
[
36
, p. 52]
, and each such
H
is
G
-ir.

We close with several additional higher order examples in good characteristic when Theorem
1.1
does not apply but Theorem
3.1
does.

Example 5.4
.

Let
G
be of type
E_{6}
. Suppose
p
is good for
G
.
In
[
36
, Lem. 2.7]
, Testerman exhibits the existence of a simple subgroup
H
of
G
of type
C_{4}
whose regular unipotent class belongs to the subregular class
E_{6}(a_{1})
of
G
. Let
u
be regular unipotent in
H
.
For
p=7
, the order of
u
is
7^{2}
, by Lemma
2.3
, so Theorem
1.1
can’t be invoked to say anything about
H
. However, for
p=7=a(G)
, we infer from Theorem
3.1
that
H
is
G
-ir.

Example 5.5
.

Let
G
be of type
E_{8}
. Suppose
p=11
.
Let
u
be in the distinguished class
E_{8}(a_{3})
(resp.
E_{8}(a_{4})
,
E_{8}(b_{4})
,
E_{8}(a_{5})
, or
E_{8}(b_{5})
). From the corresponding weighted Dynkin diagram corresponding to
u
we get
\hgt_{J}(\rho)=17
(resp.
14
,
13
,
11
, or
11
), see
[
9
, p. 177]
. It follows from Lemma
2.3
that in each of these instances
u
has order
11^{2}
. So we can’t appeal to Theorem
1.1
to deduce anything about reductive overgroups of
u
. But as
11=p\geq a(G)=9
,
Theorem
3.1
applies and allows us to conclude that each such overgroup is
G
-ir. For example, in each instance,
u
is contained in a maximal rank subgroup
H
of
G
of type
A_{1}E_{7}
or
D_{8}
, see
[
36
, p. 52]
.

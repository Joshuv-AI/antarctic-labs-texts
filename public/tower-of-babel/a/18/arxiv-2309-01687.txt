Derived Complete Complexes at Weakly Proregular Ideals
Amnon Yekutieli

Abstract
. Weak proregularity of an ideal in a commutative ring is a subtle generalization of the noetherian property of the ring. Weak proregularity is of special importance for the study of derived completion, and it occurs quite often in non-noetherian rings arising in Hochschild and prismatic cohomologies. This paper is about several related topics: adically flat modules, recognizing derived complete complexes, the structure of the category of derived complete complexes, and a derived complete Nakayama theorem – all with respect to a weakly proregular ideal; and the preservation of weak proregularity under completion of the ring.

Introduction

The
weak proregularity
(WPR) property of an ideal
\mathfrak{a}
in a commutative
ring
A
was discovered by Grothendieck in 1967
[
LC
]
, without naming it.
A finite sequence
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
of elements of
A
is called a
weakly proregular sequence
if its Koszul cohomology satisfies a rather
complicated asymptotic condition (see Definition
2.6
below).
An ideal
\mathfrak{a}
in
A
is called WPR if it is generated by some WPR sequence.
Grothendieck proved that when
A
is a noetherian
ring, every ideal in it is WPR. Moreover, he proved that the WPR property of

\mathfrak{a}
is sufficient for the
derived
\mathfrak{a}
-torsion
(the algebraic version of
cohomology with supports) to behave like in the noetherian case.

Thirty years later, weak proregularity was brought back into active research
by Alonso, Jeremias and Lipman
[
AJL
]
, and they also coined the
name (in the Erratum of their paper). There was much
subsequent work by other authors, including the proof that the
MGM Equivalence
holds for WPR ideals
[
PSY1
]
.
The relation between WPR and
adic flatness
(a slight
weakening of the flatness condition) was addressed in
[
Ye4
]
,
and a noncommutative generalization of WPR was studied in
[
VY
]
.
Further developments can be found in the papers
[
Ye6
]
and
[
Po2
]
.
To summarize: These prior results showed that
the WPR
property of the ideal
\mathfrak{a}
is a very subtle generalization of the noetherian
property of the ring
A
.

Several applications of the WPR property were found,
including to
Hochschild cohomology
, see
[
Sh1
]
,
[
Sh2
]
and

[
Sh3
]
; and to
perfectoid and prismatic theory
, see
[
CS
]
,

[
BS
]
,
[
Ce
]
,
[
DLMS
]
,
[
It
]
,
[
NS
]
,
[
IKY
]
and

[
BMS
]
.

All rings in this paper are commutative (but of course they are not assumed
to be noetherian). In the Introduction we are going to present the main
theorems of the paper, with only minimal explanations. Sections
1

and
2
contain a much more detailed review of the necessary
background material.

Let us begin with a few words on adic completion.
Let
A
be a ring, and let
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq A
be a finitely generated ideal.
For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
define the ring
A_{k}:=A/\mathfrak{a}^{k+1}
.
Given an
A
-module
M
, we write

M_{k}:=A_{k}\otimes_{A}M
. With this notation, the
\mathfrak{a}
-adic completion
of
M

is
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(M):=\lim_{\leftarrow k}\mspace{1mu}M_{k}
.
This is an
A
-linear functor from the category
\operatorname{\mathsf{M}}(A)

of
A
-modules to itself (usually not exact on either side).
The setting above will be retained throughout the Introduction.

Let us now say a few words on
derived completion
.
The DG category of (unbounded) complexes of
A
-modules

M=\bigoplus\nolimits_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}M^{i}
is
\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}(A)
.
The triangulated derived category of
A
-modules is
\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(A)
.
The
\mathfrak{a}
-adic completion functor on modules extends to a DG functor

\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}:\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}(A)\to\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}(A)
,

\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(M):=\bigoplus\nolimits_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(M^{i})
,
and there is a left derived functor

\mathrm{L}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}:\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(A)\to\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(A)
.
A complex
M\in\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(A)
is called
derived
\mathfrak{a}
-adically complete
if
the canonical morphism
M\to\mathrm{L}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)
in
\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(A)
is an
isomorphism.

The definition above, sometimes called
derived completeness in the
idealistic sense
, is not the same as
derived completeness in the
sequential sense
. The latter concept is the one appearing in many modern
texts, e.g.
[
SP
]
and
[
BS
]
.
Generally speaking, the idealistic derived completion is more directly related
to plain completion; whereas sequential derived completion is easier to work
with. This is explained in Section
2
.
By results in
[
PSY1
]
and
[
Po2
]
, the two notions of derived
completion agree if and only if the ideal
\mathfrak{a}
is WPR (see Theorem

2.17
). Since we shall mostly consider derived completion at WPR ideals
in our paper, the distinction between the idealistic and the sequential derived
completion will usually not matter.

An
A
-module
P
is called
\mathfrak{a}
-adically flat
(see

[
Ye2
, Definition 4.2]
), or

\mathfrak{a}
-completely flat
(see
[

, Section 1.2]
), if for every

\mathfrak{a}
-torsion
A
-module

and every

we have
\operatorname{Tor}^{A}_{i}(P,N)=0
.
It is known that the following three conditions are equivalent:

\rhd

The
A
-module
P
is
\mathfrak{a}
-adically flat.

\rhd

For every
k\geq 0
and every
i>0
the module

\operatorname{Tor}^{\,A}_{i}(A_{k},P)
vanishes, and
P_{k}
is a flat
A_{k}
-module.

\rhd

For every
i>0
the module

\operatorname{Tor}^{\,A}_{i}(A_{0},P)
vanishes, and
P_{0}
is a flat
A_{0}
-module.

This holds without any finiteness assumptions on
A
,
\mathfrak{a}
or

.
See
[

, Theorem 4.3]
,
[

, Section 4.1]
or

[

, Section 1.2]
.

When the ideal
\mathfrak{a}
is WPR and the module

is complete, we can say more:

Theorem 0.1
.

Let

be a ring, let
\mathfrak{a}
be a weakly proregular ideal in

,

-adically complete

(ii)

-torsion

For a graded
A
-module
M=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}M^{i}
, its supremum is

\operatorname{sup}(M):=\operatorname{sup}\mspace{2mu}\{i\in\mathbb{Z}\mid M^{i}\neq 0\}

For a complex of

\operatorname{sup}(\operatorname{H}(M))

The complex

,
and it is called cohomologically bounded above
if
\operatorname{sup}(\operatorname{H}(M))<\infty
.

Theorem 0.2
.

Let
A
be a ring, let
\mathfrak{a}
be a finitely generated ideal in
A
,
and let
P
be a complex of
\mathfrak{a}
-adically flat
A

Assume either of these two conditions hold: either the complex

Then the canonical morphism

\eta^{\mathrm{L}}_{P}:\mathrm{L}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(P)\to\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(P)

Theorem
0.2
is repeated as Theorem

3.3
in Section
3
, and proved there.

Let us denote by
\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(A)_{\textrm{$\mathfrak{a}$-com}}
the full subcategory of

\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(A)
on the derived
\mathfrak{a}
-adically complete complexes.
It is a triangulated category. Theorems

and

below describe the structure of the category

.

A complex of
A
-modules

is called

-adically semi-free
if

, the
\mathfrak{a}
-adic completion a semi-free
complex of
A
-modules

.

Theorem 0.3
.

Let
A
be a ring, let
\mathfrak{a}
be a weakly proregular ideal in
A
, and
let
M\in\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(A)
. The following three conditions are equivalent:

(i)

is a derived
\mathfrak{a}
-adically complete complex.

(ii)

There is an isomorphism
M\cong P

in
\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(A)
, where
P
is an
\mathfrak{a}
-adically semi-free complex of

-modules.

(iii)

There is an isomorphism

in

,

-adically complete

Moreover, when these equivalent conditions hold, the

-adically semi-free

.

This theorem is a significant improvement upon

,
in which the ring

was

Theorem

is a combination of Theorems

and

.
See the end of the Introduction for a discussion of related work.

A complex of

-modules

is called

-adically K-projective

-adically complete modules, and if for every acyclic complex

Theorem 0.4
.

Let

be a ring, and let

.
The following three conditions are equivalent for a complex

of
\mathfrak{a}
-adically

is an
\mathfrak{a}
-adically K-projective complex.

(iii)

This is Theorem

in the body of the paper. The proof is in

The homotopy category of complexes of

-modules is

.
The categorical localization functor is

.

Denote by

The next theorem is a cohomological variant of the Nakayama Lemma.

Theorem 0.6
.

\leq r

in the body of the paper.

There is a crucial difference between
\mathfrak{a}
-adic completion in the noetherian
case and in the WPR case.
It is a classical fact that when
A
is a noetherian ring, its

\mathfrak{a}
-adic completion
\widehat{A}
is flat over
A
, and it is noetherian.
The flatness of
\widehat{A}
over
A
can fail when the ideal
\mathfrak{a}
is WPR but the
ring
A
is not noetherian; see
[
Ye4
, Theorem 7.2]
for a counterexample.
The relevance of flatness is this: it is easy to prove (see Lemma

7.1
) that if

is a flat ring homomorphism, and
\mathfrak{a}
is a WPR ideal in
A
, then the
ideal
\mathfrak{b}:=B\mspace{1.3mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{1.3mu}\mathfrak{a}
in
B
is WPR.

Nonetheless, we have:

Theorem 0.8
.

Let
A
be a ring, let
\mathfrak{a}
be a WPR ideal in
A
,
let
\widehat{A}
be the
\mathfrak{a}
-adic completion of
A
, and let

\widehat{\mathfrak{a}}:=\widehat{A}\mspace{1.3mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{1.3mu}\mathfrak{a}
, the ideal in
\widehat{A}
generated by
\mathfrak{a}
. Then
the ideal
\widehat{\mathfrak{a}}
is WPR.

This theorem is repeated as Theorem
7.5
. The proof, in Section

7
, relies on the MGM equivalence (see Theorem

2.19
). See discussion below regarding prior work of L. Positselski.

Here is a theorem that relies on Theorem

.

Theorem 0.9
.

Let
A\to B
be a flat ring homomorphism, and let

be a flat

-module.
Let
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq A
be a weakly proregular ideal, and define the ideal

\mathfrak{b}:=B\mspace{1.3mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{1.3mu}\mathfrak{a}\subseteq B
. Let
\widehat{B}
be the

-adic completion of

, with

\widehat{\mathfrak{b}}:=\widehat{B}\mspace{1.3mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{1.3mu}\mathfrak{b}\subseteq B

is this:

.

Let

be flat ring homomorphisms, with

To finish the Introduction, here is a discussion of related work. After showing

, he told us that
this result was already known to him. Indeed, it is stated in

, Example 5.2(2)]
, with an indication how to prove it. As far as we

After posting an early version of our paper online, we received a message from

We believe it is more appropriate to say that our Theorems

and

are similar to some result in

]
.

It is worth mentioning that Theorem

was not known to the authors

1.
Background Material on Completion of Modules

In this section we review some relevant facts and definitions about completion
and torsion, mostly taken from the paper
[
Ye1
]
.
Recall that all rings in the paper are commutative.

Let us fix a ring
A
and an ideal
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq A
. Define the rings

A_{i}:=A/\mathfrak{a}^{i+1}
for
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
The category of
A
-modules is denoted by
\operatorname{\mathsf{M}}(A)
.
For an
A
-module
M
, we identify the
A_{i}
-modules

M/(\mathfrak{a}^{i+1}\mspace{1.3mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{1.3mu}M)=A_{i}\otimes_{A}M
.
The
\mathfrak{a}
-adic completion of
M
is

(1.1)

\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(M):=\lim_{\leftarrow i}\mspace{3mu}A_{i}\otimes_{A}M.

This is an
A
-linear functor from
\operatorname{\mathsf{M}}(A)
to itself, and there is
a functorial homomorphism
\tau_{M}:M\to\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)
.
The module
M
is called
\mathfrak{a}
-adically complete
if
\tau_{M}
is an
isomorphism. If
\tau_{M}
is injective, i.e. if

\bigcap_{i}\mathfrak{a}^{i}\mspace{1.3mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{1.3mu}M=0
, then
M
is called
\mathfrak{a}
-adically separated
.

The functor
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}
is neither left nor right exact.
If the ideal
\mathfrak{a}
is finitely generated, then the functor
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}

is idempotent, in the sense that for every module
M
the
\mathfrak{a}
-adic completion

\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)
is
\mathfrak{a}
-adically complete.
(See
[
Ye1
, Example 1.8]
for a counterexample when
\mathfrak{a}
is not finitely
generated.)
The full subcategory of
\operatorname{\mathsf{M}}(A)
on the
\mathfrak{a}
-adically complete modules
is not an abelian subcategory.
(A substitute abelian category will be mentioned below.)

Let
M
be an
A
-module. An element
m\in M
is called an
\mathfrak{a}
-torsion
element if
\mathfrak{a}^{i}\mspace{1.3mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{1.3mu}m=0
for some
i\geq 1
. The set of

\mathfrak{a}
-torsion elements of
M
is a submodule, called the
\mathfrak{a}
-torsion submodule
of
M
, with notation
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)
.
By identifying

\operatorname{Hom}_{A}({A_{i}},M)=\{m\in M\mid\mathfrak{a}^{i+1}\mspace{1.3mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{1.3mu}m=0\},

we see that

(1.2)

\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)=\lim_{i\to}\mspace{3mu}\operatorname{Hom}_{A}({A_{i}},M).

This is an
A
-linear functor from
\operatorname{\mathsf{M}}(A)
to itself, and there is
a functorial homomorphism
\sigma_{M}:\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)\to M
.
The module
M
is called
\mathfrak{a}
-torsion
if
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)=M
, i.e. if

\sigma_{M}
is an isomorphism.

The functor
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}
is left exact. The full subcategory of

\operatorname{\mathsf{M}}(A)
on the
\mathfrak{a}
-torsion modules is denoted by

Theorem 1.8

We shall only treat the nontrivial implication.
The basic observation is this:

2.
Background Material on Derived Completion

First let us recall some categorical definitions and results, primarily
following
[
PSY1
]
,
[
Ye5
]
and
[
Ye6
]
.
All rings are commutative.

Fix a ring
A
. Recall that
\operatorname{\mathsf{M}}(A)
is the category of

A
-modules. It is
an
A
-linear abelian category. The DG category of complexes of
A
-modules is

\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}(A)
.
Given

, we denote by
\operatorname{Z}^{i}(M)

\operatorname{B}^{i}(M)
and
\operatorname{H}^{i}(M)
the modules of degree

coboundaries, and cohomologies, respectively.
Taking direct sums we get the graded
A

\operatorname{Z}(M):=\bigoplus\nolimits_{i}\operatorname{Z}^{i}(M)
,

\operatorname{B}(M):=\bigoplus\nolimits_{i}\operatorname{B}^{i}(M)
and

\operatorname{H}(M):=\bigoplus\nolimits_{i}\operatorname{H}^{i}(M)
.
These satisfy

\operatorname{H}(M)=

\operatorname{Z}(M)\mspace{2mu}/\mspace{2mu}\operatorname{B}(M)
.

A homomorphism
\phi:M\to N
in
\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}(A)
is called
strict

if it has degree
0
and it commutes with the differentials.
The strict subcategory of
\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}(A)
, with all objects but only strict
homomorphisms, is
\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}_{\mathrm{str}}(A)
.
\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}_{\mathrm{str}}(A)
is an

A
-linear abelian category, containing
\operatorname{\mathsf{M}}(A)
as a full abelian
subcategory, i.e. the complexes concentrated in degree
0
.
The homotopy category of
A
-modules is
\operatorname{\mathsf{K}}(A)
, and it is an
A
-linear
triangulated category.
The projection functor (identity on objects, surjective on morphisms) is

\operatorname{P}:\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}_{\mathrm{str}}(A)\to\operatorname{\mathsf{K}}(A)
.
For complexes
M
and
N
we have

\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}(A)}(M,N)=\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N)
,

\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}_{\mathrm{str}}(A)}(M,N)=\operatorname{Z}^{0}(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N))
,

A homomorphism

\operatorname{P}(\phi):M\to N

\operatorname{\mathsf{K}}(A)
.

The derived category of

is denoted by
\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(A)
, and it is
an
A
-linear triangulated category.
\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(A)
is the categorical
localization of
\operatorname{\mathsf{K}}(A)

The composed functor

[
Ye5

interval, concentration, supremum, infimum and amplitude of a graded

[

For a graded

Remark 3.8

In this section we study the category

of derived

-adically complete complexes. The main result is

.
Theorem

is required for Theorem

to make sense, as

.
In addition to Convention

, we also assume

.

K-projective complexes were recalled in Section

.
Here is an analogue for the adic setting – a new definition.

Definition 5.1
.

A complex of
A
-modules
P
is called
\mathfrak{a}
-adically
K-projective
if it satisfies these two conditions:

(a)

P
is a complex of
\mathfrak{a}
-adically complete
A
-modules.

(b)

Suppose
N
is an acyclic complex of
\mathfrak{a}
-adically
complete
A
-modules. Then the complex
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(P,N)
is acyclic.

Proposition 5.2
.

If
P
is an
\mathfrak{a}
-adically semi-free complex, then it is an

\mathfrak{a}
-adically K-projective complex.

Proof.

Choose an isomorphism
P\cong\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(P^{\prime})
in
\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}_{\mathrm{str}}(A)
, for
some semi-free complex
P^{\prime}
.
Let
N
be an acyclic complex of
\mathfrak{a}
-adically complete

A
-modules. By Proposition
1.4
(3) there is an isomorphism

\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(P,N)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(P^{\prime},N)

in
\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}_{\mathrm{str}}(A)
.
Since
P^{\prime}
is a K-projective complex of
A
-modules, the complex

\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(P^{\prime},N)
is acyclic.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
P
and
Q
be
\mathfrak{a}
-adically K-projective complexes, and let

\phi:P\to Q
be a homomorphism in
\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}_{\mathrm{str}}(A)
.
The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

\phi
is a quasi-isomorphism.

(ii)

\phi
is a homotopy equivalence.

Proof.

The implication (ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) it trivial.

The other implication is proved like the usual result
(cf.
[
Ye3
, Corollary 10.2.14]
), but taking care about completeness.
Suppose
\phi:P\to Q
is a quasi-isomorphism in
\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}_{\mathrm{str}}(A)
.
We need to prove that the morphism

\bar{\phi}:=\operatorname{P}(\phi):P\to Q
in
\operatorname{\mathsf{K}}(A)
is an isomorphism.

Let
N
be the standard cone of
\phi
, see
[
Ye3
, Section 4.2]
.
So
N
is an acyclic complex of
\mathfrak{a}
-adically complete
A
-modules.
Consider the standard short exact sequence

(5.4)

0\to Q\to N\to P[1]\to 0

in
\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}_{\mathrm{str}}(A)
,
see
[
Ye5
, Section 4.2]
.
This is a split exact sequence in the category
\operatorname{\mathsf{G}}_{\mathrm{str}}(A)
of
graded
A
-modules.
Therefore the sequence

(5.5)

0\to\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(Q,Q)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(Q,N)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(Q,P[1])\to 0

in
\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}_{\mathrm{str}}(A)
, obtained from (
5.4
) by
applying the functor
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(Q,-)
, is exact.
Since the complex
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(Q,N)
is acyclic, in the
long exact cohomology sequence associated to (

),
the homomorphism

\operatorname{H}^{0}\bigl(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\operatorname{id}_{Q},\phi)\bigr):\operatorname{H}^{0}\bigl(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(Q,P)\bigr)\to\operatorname{H}^{0}\bigl(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(Q,Q)\bigr)

is an isomorphism. This means that the function

\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{K}}(A)}(\operatorname{id}_{Q},\bar{\phi}):\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{K}}(A)}(Q,P)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{K}}(A)}(Q,Q),\mspace{5mu}\bar{\psi}\mapsto\bar{\phi}\circ\bar{\psi}

is a bijection. Let

\bar{\psi}:Q\to P
be the morphism in
\operatorname{\mathsf{K}}(A)
satisfying

\bar{\phi}\circ\bar{\psi}=\operatorname{id}_{Q}
.

It remains to prove that

\bar{\psi}\circ\bar{\phi}=\operatorname{id}_{P}
.
By the same arguments as above, only now applying

\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(P,-)
to (
5.4
), the function

\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{K}}(A)}(\operatorname{id}_{P},\bar{\phi}):\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{K}}(A)}(P,P)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{K}}(A)}(P,Q),\mspace{5mu}\bar{\sigma}\mapsto\bar{\phi}\circ\bar{\sigma}

is a bijection.
Consider the morphisms
\bar{\psi}\circ\bar{\phi}
and
\operatorname{id}_{P}

in
\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{K}}(A)}(P,P)
. We have

\bar{\phi}\circ(\bar{\psi}\circ\bar{\phi})=\operatorname{id}_{Q}\circ\mspace{2mu}\bar{\phi}=\bar{\phi}=\bar{\phi}\circ\operatorname{id}_{P}.

By canceling
\bar{\phi}
we see that

\bar{\psi}\circ\bar{\phi}=\operatorname{id}_{P}
.
∎

\mathfrak{a}
-adically K-projective complexes.

\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}}=\sqrt{\mathfrak{b}}
. Then the sequences of ideals

\{\mathfrak{a}^{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
and

are cofinal.
This implies that the completion functors
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}
and
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{b}}
are
isomorphic, and also the torsion functors
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}
and
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{b}}
are
isomorphic. In particular, the ring

is an
\mathfrak{a}
-torsion
A
-module.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{b}
be a finitely generated ideal of

, such that

\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}}=\sqrt{\mathfrak{b}}
.
Let
\widehat{\mathfrak{b}}:=\widehat{A}\mspace{1.3mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{1.3mu}\mathfrak{b}

.

The next lemma is the key technical result of this section.

Lemma 7.3
.

Assume
\mathfrak{a}
is a WPR ideal.
Let
\boldsymbol{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})
be a sequence of elements in
A
,
let
\mathfrak{b}
be the ideal generated by
\boldsymbol{b}
, and assume
\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}}=\sqrt{\mathfrak{b}}
.
For every
i
let
\widehat{b}_{i}
be the image of the element
b_{i}
in
\widehat{A}
,
and define the sequence

\widehat{\boldsymbol{b}}:=(\widehat{b}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{b}_{n})
in
\widehat{A}
.
Then the obvious homomorphism of Koszul complexes

\operatorname{K}(A;\boldsymbol{b})\to\operatorname{K}(\widehat{A};\widehat{\boldsymbol{b}})
is a quasi-isomorphism.

Proof.

Write
K:=\operatorname{K}(A;\boldsymbol{b})
and
\widehat{K}:=\operatorname{K}(\widehat{A};\widehat{\boldsymbol{b}})
,
and let
\phi:K\to\widehat{K}
be the obvious homomorphism of complexes of

A
-modules, which is also a homomorphism of commutative DG
A
-rings.
We want to prove that
\phi
is a quasi-isomorphism in

\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}_{\mathrm{str}}(A)
,
and this is the same as proving that
\operatorname{Q}(\phi):K\to\widehat{K}

is an isomorphism in
\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(A)
.

Since
\operatorname{H}^{0}(K)\cong A/\mathfrak{b}
as
A
-rings,
all the cohomologies
\operatorname{H}^{i}(K)
are
(A/\mathfrak{b})
-modules, and
therefore they are
\mathfrak{a}
-torsion. According to

[
PSY1
, Corollary 4.32]

the complex
K
belongs to
\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(A)_{\textrm{$\mathfrak{a}$-tor}}
.

By Lemma
7.2
the
A
-ring homomorphism

A/\mathfrak{b}\to\widehat{A}\mspace{2mu}/\mspace{2mu}\widehat{\mathfrak{b}}
is bijective.
Therefore
\operatorname{H}^{0}(\widehat{K})\cong A/\mathfrak{b}
as
A
-rings,
all the cohomologies
\operatorname{H}^{i}(\widehat{K})
are
(A/\mathfrak{b})
-modules, and so
the complex
\widehat{K}
also belongs to

\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(A)_{\textrm{$\mathfrak{a}$-tor}}
.

We need to prove that the morphism

\operatorname{Q}(\phi):K\to\widehat{K}
in
\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(A)
is an
isomorphism.
Since both
K
and
\widehat{K}
belong to
\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(A)_{\textrm{$\mathfrak{a}$-tor}}
,
the MGM Equivalence
[
PSY1
, Theorem 1.1]
says that

\operatorname{Q}(\phi):K\to\widehat{K}
is an
isomorphism in
\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(A)
if and only if

(7.4)

\mathrm{L}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(\operatorname{Q}(\phi)):\mathrm{L}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(K)\to\mathrm{L}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(\widehat{K})

is an isomorphism in
\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(A)
.
We are going to prove that
\mathrm{L}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(\operatorname{Q}(\phi))

is an isomorphism.

Observe that as a complex of
A
-modules,

\widehat{K}=\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(K)
, the
\mathfrak{a}
-adic completion of the complex
K
.
The homomorphisms
\phi,\tau_{K}:K\to\widehat{K}

in
\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}_{\mathrm{str}}(A)
are equal,
and
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(\phi):\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(K)\to\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(\widehat{K})

is an isomorphism.

The complexes
K
and
\widehat{K}
are bounded complexes of
\mathfrak{a}
-adically flat

A
-modules. For
K
this is clear, and for
\widehat{K}
we use

[
Ye4
, Theorem 5.3]
.
Consider the following commutative diagram in
\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(A)
.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{L}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(K)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{L}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(\widehat{K})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(K)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(\widehat{K})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{L}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(\operatorname{Q}(\phi))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta^{\mathrm{L}}_{K}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta^{\mathrm{L}}_{\widehat{K}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Q}(\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(\phi))}

By Theorem
3.3
the vertical arrows are isomorphisms.
Since
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(\phi)
is an isomorphism, so is

\operatorname{Q}(\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(\phi))
. We conclude that

\mathrm{L}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(\operatorname{Q}(\phi))
is an isomorphism.
∎

Theorem 7.5
.

Let
A
be a ring, let
\mathfrak{a}
be a WPR ideal in
A
,
let
\widehat{A}
be the
\mathfrak{a}
-adic completion of
A
, and let

\widehat{\mathfrak{a}}:=\widehat{A}\mspace{1.3mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{1.3mu}\mathfrak{a}
, the ideal in
\widehat{A}
generated by
\mathfrak{a}
. Then
the ideal
\widehat{\mathfrak{a}}\subseteq\widehat{A}
is weakly proregular.

As mentioned at the end of the Introduction, Theorem
7.5
was
already known to Positselski, see
[
Po1
, Example 5.2(2)]
.
We discovered this theorem independently, unaware of the prior work of
Positselski. As far as we can tell, our proof
(based on the MGM Equivalence and Lemma
7.3
) is totally different
from the proof outlined by Positselski.

Proof.

Choose some WPR sequence

\boldsymbol{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
of elements of
A
that generates the ideal
\mathfrak{a}
.
For every
j\geq 1
define the sequence

\boldsymbol{a}^{j}:=(a_{1}^{j},\ldots,a_{n}^{j})
,
and let
\mathfrak{b}_{j}
be the ideal of
A
generated by the sequence
\boldsymbol{a}^{j}
.
This ideal satisfies
\sqrt{\mathfrak{b}_{j}}=\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}}
.

For every
i
let
\widehat{a}_{i}
be the image of the element
a_{i}
in

\widehat{A}
. Define the sequences

\widehat{\boldsymbol{a}}:=(\widehat{a}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{a}_{n})

and

\widehat{\boldsymbol{a}}^{j}:=(\widehat{a}_{1}^{\mspace{2mu}j},\ldots,\widehat{a}_{n}^{\mspace{2mu}j})
in
the ring
\widehat{A}
.
The sequence
\widehat{\boldsymbol{a}}
generates the ideal
\widehat{\mathfrak{a}}
,
and the sequence
\widehat{\boldsymbol{a}}^{\mspace{2mu}j}
generates the ideal

\widehat{\mathfrak{b}}_{j}:=\widehat{A}\mspace{1.3mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{1.3mu}\mathfrak{b}_{j}\subseteq\widehat{A}
.
Again,

\sqrt{\mspace{3mu}\widehat{\mathfrak{b}}_{j}\mspace{2mu}}=\sqrt{\mspace{2mu}\widehat{\mathfrak{a}}\mspace{2mu}}
.

According to Lemma
7.3
, for every
j
the homomorphism of Koszul
complexes

\phi_{j}:\operatorname{K}(A;\boldsymbol{a}^{j})\to\operatorname{K}(\widehat{A};\widehat{\boldsymbol{a}}^{j})

is a quasi-isomorphism. Therefore, for every
i
and
j
, the
A
-module
homomorphism

\operatorname{H}^{i}(\phi_{j}):\operatorname{H}^{i}\bigl(\operatorname{K}(A;\boldsymbol{a}^{j})\bigr)\to\operatorname{H}^{i}\bigl(\operatorname{K}(\widehat{A};\widehat{\boldsymbol{a}}^{j})\bigr)

is an isomorphism. So for every
i
there is an isomorphism

(7.6)

\bigl\{\operatorname{H}^{i}(\phi_{j})\bigr\}_{j\geq 1}:\bigl\{\operatorname{H}^{i}\bigl(\operatorname{K}(A;\boldsymbol{a}^{j})\bigr)\bigr\}_{j\geq 1}\xrightarrow{\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\simeq\mspace{2mu}$}}}\bigl\{\operatorname{H}^{i}\bigl(\operatorname{K}(\widehat{A};\widehat{\boldsymbol{a}}^{j})\bigr)\bigr\}_{j\geq 1}

of inverse systems of
A
-modules.

We are given that
\boldsymbol{a}
is a WPR sequence in
A
.
This means that for every
i<0
the first inverse system of
A
-modules
in (
7.6
) is pro-zero. But then the second inverse system there is
also pro-zero, and thus
\widehat{\boldsymbol{a}}
is a WPR sequence in
\widehat{A}
.
Since the sequence
\widehat{\boldsymbol{a}}
generates the ideal
\widehat{\mathfrak{a}}
, it follows
that this ideal of
\widehat{A}
is WPR.
∎

The ring homomorphism
A\to\widehat{A}
induces the restriction functors

\operatorname{Rest}:\operatorname{\mathsf{M}}(\widehat{A})\to\operatorname{\mathsf{M}}(A)
,

\operatorname{Rest}:\operatorname{\mathsf{K}}(\widehat{A})\to\operatorname{\mathsf{K}}(A)
and

\operatorname{Rest}:\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(\widehat{A})\to\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(A)
.

Proposition 7.7
.

The functor

\operatorname{Rest}:\operatorname{\mathsf{K}}(\widehat{A})\to\operatorname{\mathsf{K}}(A)

induces an isomorphism of categories

\operatorname{Rest}:\operatorname{\mathsf{K}}(\widehat{A})_{\mspace{2mu}\textrm{$\widehat{\mathfrak{a}}$-kpr}}\to\operatorname{\mathsf{K}}(A)_{\textrm{$\mathfrak{a}$-kpr}}
.

Proof.

An
\mathfrak{a}
-adically complete
A
-module is the same as an

\widehat{\mathfrak{a}}
-adically complete
\widehat{A}
-module.
So let us refer to them as complete modules.
According to Proposition
1.4
(5), for complete modules
M
and
N

there is equality

\operatorname{Hom}_{\widehat{A}}(M,N)=\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N)
.
Therefore the full DG subcategory of
\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}(A)
on the
complexes of complete modules is equal to the full DG subcategory of

\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}(\widehat{A})
on the complexes of complete modules.
This implies that the full triangulated subcategory of
\operatorname{\mathsf{K}}(A)
on the
complexes of complete modules is equal to the full triangulated subcategory
of
\operatorname{\mathsf{K}}(\widehat{A})
on the complexes of complete modules.

Take complexes of complete modules
P
and
N
, with
N
acyclic.
Since

\operatorname{Hom}_{\widehat{A}}(P,N)=\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(P,N)
,
we see that
P
is
\mathfrak{a}
-adically K-projective over
A
if and only if

P
is
\widehat{\mathfrak{a}}
-adically K-projective over
\widehat{A}
.
Conclusion:

\operatorname{\mathsf{K}}(\widehat{A})_{\mspace{2mu}\textrm{$\widehat{\mathfrak{a}}$-kpr}}=\operatorname{\mathsf{K}}(A)_{\textrm{$\mathfrak{a}$-kpr}}
.
∎

Theorem 7.8
.

Let

be a ring, let
\mathfrak{a}
we a WPR ideal in

,

\widehat{A}
be the
\mathfrak{a}

(1) To simplify the presentation, and because we are going to work with explicit

that

\mathfrak{a}

\boldsymbol{a}

Let

.

\widehat{\boldsymbol{a}}

\boldsymbol{a}

\widehat{A}
.

\widehat{\mathfrak{a}}

Central Limit Theorem for tensor products of free variables
Cécilia Lancien and Patrick Oliveira Santos and Pierre Youssef

Abstract
. We establish a central limit theorem for tensor product random variables c k := a k ⊗ a k c_{k}:=a_{k}\otimes a_{k} , where ( a k ) k ∈ ℕ (a_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}} is a free family of variables. We show that if the variables a k a_{k} are centered, the limiting law is the semi-circle. Otherwise, the limiting law depends on the mean and variance of the variables a k a_{k} and corresponds to a free interpolation between the semi-circle law and the classical convolution of two semi-circle laws.



1.
Introduction

The Free Central Limit Theorem serves as a foundational principle in free probability
[
15
]
,
[
11
, Lecture 8]
. It asserts that as the number of freely independent operators summed together approaches infinity, the distribution of the normalized sum tends towards an asymptotically semi-circular shape. This mirrors the classical Central Limit Theorem but with independence conditions replaced by free independence (also known as freeness) and the Gaussian limit substituted with a semi-circular limit. More precisely, let
(\mathcal{A},\tau)
be a unital noncommutative probability space equipped with a faithful tracial state
\tau

[
11
, Lecture 1]
. We say that subalgebras
\mathcal{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{d}\subset\mathcal{A}
are free if

\displaystyle\tau(a_{1}\ldots a_{p})=0,

whenever
p\geq 1
,
a_{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{j_{i}}
,
\tau(a_{i})=0
for all
i\in[p]
and
j_{1}\neq j_{2}\neq\cdots\neq j_{p}
. We say that random variables
a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}\in\mathcal{A}
are free if their generated algebras are free.
We say that a sequence of (self-adjoint) variables
a_{n}\in(\mathcal{A}_{n},\tau_{n})
converges in distribution to a variable
a\in(\mathcal{A},\tau)
if

\displaystyle\tau_{n}(a_{n}^{p})\to\tau(a^{p}),

for all integers
p\geq 0
and we denote it
a_{n}\Rightarrow a
. We denote
a-\lambda:=a-\lambda\mathbf{1}
, where
\mathbf{1}\in\mathcal{A}
is the unit in the algebra. As usual,
\tau(a)
is the mean of
a
and the variance is given by

\displaystyle\operatorname{var}(a)=\tau((a-\tau(a))^{2}).

The Free Central Limit Theorem states that if
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in(\mathcal{A},\tau)
are free self-adjoint i.i.d random variables with mean
\lambda
and variance
\sigma^{2}
, then

\frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{n}}\sum_{k\in[n]}(a_{k}-\lambda)\Rightarrow\mu_{sc},

where
\mu_{sc}
denotes the semi-circle distribution whose density is given by

\displaystyle f_{sc}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{4-x^{2}}\mathbf{1}_{|x|\leq 2}.

The goal of this paper is to establish a central limit theorem for the tensor product of free random variables. Concretely, given
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in(\mathcal{A},\tau)
free self-adjoint i.i.d random variables, we aim at studying the convergence of the normalized sequence

(1)

\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{k\in[n]}\left(a_{k}\otimes a_{k}-\tau\otimes\tau(a_{k}\otimes a_{k})\right),

in the product space
(\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{A},\tau\otimes\tau)
.

Just as free probability captures the limiting behavior of random matrices, the above expression appears naturally as the limiting object corresponding to several models of random quantum channels
[
7
]
. Indeed, given
M_{1},\ldots,M_{n}\in\mathcal{M}_{d}(\mathbb{C})
independent random self-adjoint matrices, it was shown in
[
7
]
that the empirical spectral distribution (ESD) of the quantum channel

\displaystyle\Delta_{d,n}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{k\in[n]}\left(M_{k}\otimes M_{k}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[M_{k}\otimes M_{k}]\right),

having the
M_{k}
’s as random Kraus operators and with fixed Kraus rank
n
, converges as
d\to\infty
to the expression in (
1
) with the
a_{k}
’s being the corresponding limits of the ESD of the
M_{k}
’s. Moreover, it was in particular shown that if the random matrices
M_{k}
are centered, then the ESD of
\Delta_{d,n}
converges as
n,d\to\infty
to the semi-circle distribution. These two statements combined suggest that, in the case where the
a_{k}
’s are centered, an analogue of the free central limit theorem should hold for the
a_{k}\otimes a_{k}
’s. Whereas these heuristics indicate that the semi-circle distribution should appear as the limit of (
1
) when the
a_{k}
’s are centered, the convergence and the explicit limit are not clear in the general case.
The goal of this paper is to address this by establishing the convergence of the expression in (
1
) and identifying the limiting object.
The latter, as we show, depends on the mean and variance of the variables
a_{k}
’s and represents a free interpolation between a semi-circle distribution and the classical convolution of two semi-circle distributions.

Random matrix models of the form

(2)

\displaystyle M=\sum_{k\in[n]}M_{k}\otimes M_{k},

for
M_{1},\ldots,M_{n}\in\mathcal{M}_{d}(\mathbb{C})
independent random self-adjoint matrices, are in fact useful in other areas of Quantum Information Theory. When the
M_{k}
’s are positive semidefinite matrices, normalizing
M
by its trace produces a model for a random separable quantum state. Little is known about the typical asymptotic spectrum of separable states, contrary to that of entangled ones
[
1
]
. Moreover, a random matrix
M
of the form (
2
) appears naturally when performing a so-called realignment operation on a quantum state. Understanding the spectrum of the realignment of a state is important as it gives information on the entanglement of the state. In
[
2
]
, this was done in the particular case where the
M_{k}
’s are Gaussian matrices (corresponding to the case where the state is a normalized Wishart matrix). The results and techniques we develop here (combined with those in
[
7
]
) could be useful in addressing the questions mentioned above.

Given a measure
\mu
, we denote

\displaystyle(t\mu)(A):=\mu(t^{-1}A),

its dilation by
t\neq 0
, where
A
is any Borel set in
\mathbb{R}
. Equivalently, if
a
is a random variable with distribution
\mu
, then
ta
has distribution
t\mu
.

The following is our main theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
a\in(\mathcal{A},\tau)
be a self-adjoint random variable with mean
\tau(a)=\lambda
and variance
\operatorname{var}(a)=\sigma^{2}\neq 0
. Denote

\delta^{2}:=\operatorname{var}(a\otimes a)=\sigma^{2}(\sigma^{2}+2\lambda^{2}),

and

\displaystyle q:=\frac{2\lambda^{2}}{\sigma^{2}+2\lambda^{2}}\in[0,1].

Given
(a_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
a sequence of free copies of
a
, the normalized sum

\displaystyle S_{n}:=\frac{1}{\delta\sqrt{n}}\sum_{k\in[n]}(a_{k}\otimes a_{k}-\lambda^{2})

converges in distribution as
n\to\infty
to

(3)

\mu_{q}:=\sqrt{q}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\mu_{sc}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\mu_{sc}\right)\boxplus\sqrt{1-q}\,\mu_{sc},

where
+
denotes the classical convolution and
\boxplus
denotes the free convolution.

The difficulty in analyzing
S_{n}
stems from the complicated dependence structure exhibited by tensors, combining classical independence (between the two legs of the tensor) and freeness (between the variables across tensors). In the centered case, similar computations were made for semi-circle random variables
[
10
,
5
]
.
It would be of interest to design a general notion of independence corresponding to the tensor case, analyze its properties, derive the corresponding limit theorems, and characterize the corresponding universal objects. One particular generalization is by replacing the tensor product with the product of
\varepsilon
-independent random variables
[
14
,
9
,
13
]
. A direct consequence of Theorem
1.1
is that such a notion cannot, in general, reduce to freeness.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in(\mathcal{A},\tau)
be self-adjoint free i.i.d noncentered random variables.
Then
\{a_{k}\otimes a_{k}:k\in[n]\}
are not free.

The above corollary trivially follows from Theorem
1.1
, since if the
a_{k}\otimes a_{k}
’s were free, the limit of their normalized sum would be the semi-circle distribution contradicting the conclusion of Theorem
1.1
(when
\lambda\neq 0
). This fact was originally proved in
[
4
]
where, more generally, the freeness of tensors of free variables was characterized.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2

3
, we provide some properties of the limiting measure appearing in Theorem
1.1
. Section
4
establishes the existence of the limit, while Section
5
is dedicated to the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

3.
Properties of the limiting measure

In this section, we summarize some of the properties of the measure
\mu_{q}
appearing in (
3
).
We start by computing the free cumulants and the moments of
\mu_{1}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let

\displaystyle\mu_{1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\mu_{sc}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\mu_{sc}.

Then the following hold.

(1)

Its odd moments vanish and, for every
p\in\mathbb{N}
, its
2p
-th moment is given by

\displaystyle\int x^{2p}\,\text{d}\mu_{1}

\displaystyle=2^{-p}\sum_{l=0}^{p}\binom{2p}{2l}C_{l}C_{p-l}=2^{-p}\sum_{\pi\in P_{2}^{\operatorname{bi}}(2p)}2^{\operatorname{\bf cc}(\pi)},

where we recall that
C_{l}
denotes the
l
-th Catalan number.

(2)

Its odd free cumulants vanish,
\kappa^{\operatorname{free}}_{2}(\mu_{1})=1
and for any even integer
n\geq 4
, we have

\displaystyle\kappa^{\operatorname{free}}_{n}(\mu_{1})=2\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{n/2}|P_{2}^{\operatorname{bicon}}(n)|.

Proof.

Let
x_{1},x_{2}
be two classical i.i.d semi-circle random variables. Then

\displaystyle\int x^{2p}\,\text{d}\mu_{1}=2^{-p}\tau\left((x_{1}+x_{2})^{2p}\right).

Since the variables are classical independent, and in particular, they commute, we get

\displaystyle\tau\left((x_{1}+x_{2})^{2p}\right)=2^{-p}\sum_{l=0}^{2p}\binom{2p}{l}\tau(x_{1}^{l})\tau(x_{2}^{2p-l}).

The odd moments of
x_{1}
vanish and, for any
l\geq 1
,
\tau(x_{1}^{2l})=C_{l}
, hence

\displaystyle\int x^{2p}\,\text{d}\mu_{1}=2^{-p}\sum_{l=0}^{p}\binom{2p}{2l}C_{l}C_{p-l}.

Now consider the sequence
m_{p}
given by

\displaystyle m_{p}:=2^{-p}\sum_{\pi\in P_{2}^{\operatorname{bi}}(2p)}2^{\operatorname{\bf cc}(\pi)}.

For every bipartite pair partition
\pi
, we can decompose it into its bipartite sets. Namely, let
\mathcal{V}_{1}=\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{m}\}
and
\mathcal{V}_{2}=\{V_{m+1},\ldots,V_{p}\}
be the bipartition of its blocks in two disjoint families of vertices. Then, the blocks
V_{1},\ldots,V_{m}
are noncrossing from one another, and so are
V_{m+1},\ldots,V_{p}
. Let

\displaystyle I=\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}V_{i}\subseteq[2p].

Then

\displaystyle\pi_{1}:=\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{m}\}\in NC_{2}\left(I\right);

\displaystyle\pi_{2}:=\{V_{m+1},\ldots,V_{p}\}\in NC_{2}\left(I^{c}\right).

We denote
(\pi_{1},\pi_{2},I,I^{c})
a left-right noncrossing representation of
\pi
. We note that if
(\pi_{1},\pi_{2},I,I^{c})
is a left-right noncrossing representation of
\pi
, so is
(\pi_{2},\pi_{1},I^{c},I)
. Let
R(\pi)
be the set of all left-right noncrossing representations of
\pi
. We note that
|R(\pi)|
corresponds to the number of ways the vertices of
G(\pi)
can be split into two independent families of vertices. Therefore, it is clear that

\displaystyle|R(\pi)|=2^{\operatorname{\bf cc}(\pi)}.

Hence

(5)

\displaystyle\sum_{\pi\in P_{2}^{\operatorname{bi}}(2p)}2^{\operatorname{\bf cc}(\pi)}=\sum_{\pi\in P_{2}^{\operatorname{bi}}(2p)}|R(\pi)|.

Since
R(\pi)
is the set of all left-right noncrossing representations of
\pi
and we sum over all
\pi\in P_{2}^{\operatorname{bi}}(2p)
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{\pi\in P_{2}^{\operatorname{bi}}(2p)}|R(\pi)|=|\{(\pi_{1},\pi_{2},I,I^{c}):I\subseteq[2p],\pi_{1}\in NC_{2}(I),\pi_{2}\in NC_{2}(I^{c})\}|.

Note that for fixed
I\subseteq[2p]
, each
(\pi_{1},\pi_{2})\in NC_{2}(I)\times NC_{2}(I^{c})
will correspond to a unique partition
\pi\in P_{2}^{\operatorname{bi}}(2p)
such that
(\pi_{1},\pi_{2},I,I^{c})
is a left-right noncrossing representation of
\pi
. Hence we can first sum over
I\subseteq[2p]
so that

\displaystyle\sum_{\pi\in P_{2}^{\operatorname{bi}}(2p)}|R(\pi)|=\sum_{I\subseteq[2p]}|\{(\pi_{1},\pi_{2}):\pi_{1}\in NC_{2}(I),\pi_{2}\in NC_{2}(I^{c})\}|.

Therefore, by (
5
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\pi\in P_{2}^{\operatorname{bi}}(2p)}2^{\operatorname{\bf cc}(\pi)}=\sum_{I\subseteq[2p]}|\{(\pi_{1},\pi_{2}):\pi_{1}\in NC_{2}(I),\pi_{2}\in NC_{2}(I^{c})\}|.

The latter cardinality can be written as a product over the cardinals and only depends on the length of
I
. More precisely, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\pi\in P_{2}^{\operatorname{bi}}(2p)}2^{\operatorname{\bf cc}(\pi)}=\sum_{l=0}^{p}\binom{2p}{2l}C_{l}C_{p-l},

where the cardinal
|NC_{2}(2l)|
is equal to
C_{l}
. Hence

\displaystyle\int x^{2p}\,\text{d}\mu_{1}=m_{p},

and the first statement follows. For the second, we use the bijection
\Phi
in (
4
) to write
\Phi(\pi)=\left(\hat{\pi},(\pi_{T})_{T\in\hat{\pi}}\right)
. We note that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\hat{\pi}\\
|T|\geq 4\end{subarray}}1=\operatorname{\bf cc}(\pi)-\operatorname{\bf ncr}(\pi),

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\hat{\pi}\\
|T|\geq 4\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{|T|}{2}\right)=\operatorname{\bf cr}(\pi).

Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{\pi\in P_{2}^{\operatorname{bi}}(2p)}2^{\operatorname{\bf cc}(\pi)-p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\pi\in P_{2}^{\operatorname{bi}}(2p)}2^{\operatorname{\bf cc}(\pi)-\operatorname{\bf ncr}(\pi)}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\operatorname{\bf cr}(\pi)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\hat{\pi}\in NC(2p)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\hat{\pi}\\
\pi_{T}\in P_{2}^{\operatorname{bicon}}(T)\end{subarray}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\hat{\pi}\\
|T|\geq 4\end{subarray}}2\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\hat{\pi}\\
|T|\geq 4\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{|T|/2},

where we use that
\operatorname{\bf ncr}(\pi)+\operatorname{\bf cr}(\pi)=p
in the first equality. We thus have

\displaystyle\sum_{\pi\in P_{2}^{\operatorname{bi}}(2p)}2^{\operatorname{\bf cc}(\pi)-p}=\sum_{\hat{\pi}\in NC(2p)}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\hat{\pi}\\
|T|\geq 4\end{subarray}}\left\{2\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{|T|/2}|P_{2}^{\operatorname{bicon}}(|T|)|\right\}.

Since the free cumulants
\kappa^{\operatorname{free}}_{n}(\mu_{1})
are uniquely characterized by the moments, it follows that for any odd integer
n
, we have
\kappa^{\operatorname{free}}_{n}(\mu_{1})=0
,
\kappa^{\operatorname{free}}_{2}(\mu_{1})=1
and for any even integer
n\geq 4
, we have

\displaystyle\kappa^{\operatorname{free}}_{n}(\mu_{1})=2\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{n/2}|P_{2}^{\operatorname{bicon}}(n)|.

∎

Remark 3.2

For any
p\geq 1
, it was shown in

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{p}\binom{2p}{2l}C_{l}C_{p-l}=C_{p}C_{p+1}.

Therefore, the

-th moment of
\mu_{1}
is also characterized by
2^{-p}C_{p}C_{p+1}
. The sequence
(C_{p}C_{p+1})_{p\geq 1}
is A005568 in Sloane’s encyclopedia (

We are ready to compute the moments and free cumulants of
\mu_{q}
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Given
q\in[0,1]
, let

\mu_{q}:=\sqrt{q}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\mu_{sc}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\mu_{sc}\right)\boxplus\sqrt{1-q}\,\mu_{sc}.

Then the following hold.

(1)

The odd free cumulants of
\mu_{q}
vanish,
\kappa^{\operatorname{free}}_{2}(\mu_{q})=1
and for any even integer
n\geq 4
, we have

\displaystyle\kappa^{\operatorname{free}}_{n}(\mu_{q})=2\Big(\frac{q}{2}\Big)^{n/2}|P_{2}^{\operatorname{bicon}}(n)|.

(2)

The odd moments of
\mu_{q}
vanish and, for every
p\in\mathbb{N}
, its
2p
-th moment is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{\pi\in P_{2}^{\operatorname{bi}}(2p)}2^{\operatorname{\bf cc}(\pi)-p}q^{\operatorname{\bf cr}(\pi)}.

Proof.

We will use the notion of
R
-transform; see
[
11
, Lecture 16]
. For a random variable
a\in\mathcal{A}
with distribution
\mu
, let

\displaystyle R_{a}(z)=R_{\mu}(z)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\kappa^{\operatorname{free}}_{n}(a)z^{n},

be its
R
-transform, defined as a formal series.
The
R
-transform of a standard semi-circle law is given by

\displaystyle R_{\mu_{sc}}(z)=z^{2},

whereas Proposition
3.1
shows that the
R
-transform of
\mu_{1}
is given by

\displaystyle R_{\mu_{1}}(z)=z^{2}+2\sum_{n\geq 2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{n}|P_{2}^{\operatorname{bicon}}(2n)|z^{2n}.

Since the free cumulants linearize the free convolution, we deduce that

\displaystyle R_{\mu_{q}}(z)

\displaystyle=R_{\sqrt{q}\mu_{1}}(z)+R_{\sqrt{1-q}\,\mu_{sc}}(z)

\displaystyle=R_{\mu_{1}}\big(\sqrt{q}\,z\big)+R_{\mu_{sc}}\big(\sqrt{1-q}\,z\big)

\displaystyle=z^{2}+2\sum_{n\geq 2}\Big(\frac{q}{2}\Big)^{n}|P_{2}^{\operatorname{bicon}}(2n)|z^{2n}.

This proves the first part of the proposition.
To prove the second part, we use the moment-cumulant formula to deduce that the odd moments vanish, while the
2p
-th moment can be expressed as

\displaystyle\sum_{\hat{\pi}\in NC(2p)}\prod_{T\in\hat{\pi}}\kappa^{\operatorname{free}}_{|T|}(\mu_{q})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\hat{\pi}\in NC(2p)}\prod_{\underset{|T|\geq 4}{T\in\hat{\pi}}}\left\{2\Big(\frac{q}{2}\Big)^{|T|/2}|P_{2}^{\operatorname{bicon}}(T)|\right\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\hat{\pi}\in NC(2p)}\sum_{(\pi_{T})_{T\in\hat{\pi}}}\prod_{\underset{|T|\geq 4}{T\in\hat{\pi}}}\left\{2\Big(\frac{q}{2}\Big)^{|T|/2}\right\},

where the second summation is over
(\pi_{T})_{T\in\hat{\pi}}\in\operatorname{Proj}(\hat{\pi})
such that
\pi_{T}\in P_{2}^{\operatorname{bicon}}(T)
for

.
Now noting that

\displaystyle|\{T\in\hat{\pi}:|T|\geq 4\}|=\operatorname{\bf cc}(\pi)-\operatorname{\bf ncr}(\pi),

and

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\hat{\pi}\\
|T|\geq 4\end{subarray}}\frac{|T|}{2}=\operatorname{\bf cr}(\pi),

we finish the proof after using the bijection

∎

4.
Existence of the limit

The goal of this section is to show that the expression in (
1
) admits a limit that depends only on the first and second moments of the variables at hand.
Let us first prove the following centering lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\tau)
be a unital faithful tracial noncommutative probability space. Let
\{a_{k}:k\in[n]\},\{d_{k}:k\in[n]\}\subset\mathcal{A}
be two collections of free random variables and let

\displaystyle c_{k}:=a_{k}\otimes d_{k}-\tau\otimes\tau(a_{k}\otimes d_{k}),

for every
k\in[n]
. Then for any
m\geq 1
and
i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}\in[n]
such that there exists an index
l^{*}\in[m]
satisfying
i_{l^{*}}\neq i_{j}
for all
j\neq l^{*}
, we have

\displaystyle\tau\otimes\tau(c_{i_{1}}\cdots c_{i_{m}})=0.

In particular, we have

\displaystyle\tau\otimes\tau\left(c_{i_{1}}\cdots c_{i_{l^{*}-1}}a_{i_{l^{*}}}\otimes d_{i_{l^{*}}}c_{i_{l^{*}+1}}\cdots c_{i_{m}}\right)=\tau(a_{i_{l^{*}}})\tau(d_{i_{l^{*}}})\tau\otimes\tau(c_{i_{1}}\cdots c_{i_{l^{*}-1}}c_{i_{l^{*}+1}}\cdots c_{i_{m}}).

Proof.

By cyclicity of the trace, we can assume that
l^{*}=1
. We write

(6)

\displaystyle\tau\otimes\tau(c_{i_{1}}\cdots c_{i_{m}})=\tau\otimes\tau(a_{i_{1}}\otimes d_{i_{1}}c_{i_{2}}\cdots c_{i_{m}})-\tau\otimes\tau(a_{i_{1}}\otimes d_{i_{1}})\tau\otimes\tau(c_{i_{2}}\cdots c_{i_{m}}).

For the first term, we open the expressions for
c_{i_{j}}
for
j\geq 2
to get that

\displaystyle\tau\otimes\tau(a_{i_{1}}\otimes d_{i_{1}}c_{i_{2}}\cdots c_{i_{m}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{I\subseteq\{2,\ldots,m\}}(-1)^{|I|}\prod_{j\in I}\left(\tau\otimes\tau(a_{i_{j}}\otimes d_{i_{j}})\right)\tau\otimes\tau\left(a_{i_{1}}\otimes d_{i_{1}}\prod_{j\in J^{c}}^{\to}a_{i_{j}}\otimes d_{i_{j}}\right),

where
\overset{\to}{\prod}
denotes the product respecting the ordering. Since
a_{i_{1}},d_{i_{1}}
are free from
a_{i_{j}},d_{i_{j}}
for all
j\geq 2
, freeness implies that

\displaystyle\tau\otimes\tau(a_{i_{1}}\otimes d_{i_{1}}c_{i_{2}}\cdots c_{i_{m}})

\displaystyle=\tau\otimes\tau(a_{i_{1}}\otimes d_{i_{1}})\sum_{I\subseteq\{2,\ldots,m\}}(-1)^{|I|}\prod_{j\in I}\left(\tau\otimes\tau(a_{i_{j}}\otimes d_{i_{j}})\right)\tau\otimes\tau\left(\prod_{j\in J^{c}}^{\to}a_{i_{j}}\otimes d_{i_{j}}\right).

The summation can be easily written as
\tau\otimes\tau(c_{i_{2}}\cdots c_{i_{m}})
, hence

\displaystyle\tau\otimes\tau(a_{i_{1}}\otimes d_{i_{1}}c_{i_{2}}\cdots c_{i_{m}})=\tau\otimes\tau(a_{i_{1}}\otimes d_{i_{1}})\tau\otimes\tau(c_{i_{2}}\cdots c_{i_{m}}).

By (
6
), we then have

\displaystyle\tau\otimes\tau(c_{i_{1}}\cdots c_{i_{m}})=0.

Finally, since
\tau\otimes\tau(a_{i_{1}}\otimes d_{i_{1}})=\tau(a_{i_{1}})\tau(d_{i_{1}})
, we get that

\displaystyle\tau\otimes\tau(a_{i_{1}}\otimes d_{i_{1}}c_{i_{2}}\cdots c_{i_{m}})

\displaystyle=\tau\otimes\tau(a_{i_{1}}\otimes d_{i_{1}})\tau\otimes\tau(c_{i_{2}}\cdots c_{i_{m}})

\displaystyle=\tau(a_{i_{1}})\tau(d_{i_{1}})\tau\otimes\tau(c_{i_{2}}\cdots c_{i_{m}}).

∎

We are now ready to prove the existence of the limit and that it only depends on the first and second moments of the
a_{k}
’s.

Proposition 4.2

(Existence)
.

Let
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in(\mathcal{A},\tau)
be self-adjoint free i.i.d random variables with mean
\lambda
, variance
\sigma^{2}
, and denote
\delta^{2}:=\operatorname{var}(a_{1}\otimes a_{1})=\sigma^{2}(\sigma^{2}+2\lambda^{2})
. For every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, denote

\displaystyle b_{k}:=\frac{1}{\delta}\big(a_{k}\otimes a_{k}-\tau\otimes\tau(a_{k}\otimes a_{k})\big),

and for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, denote

\displaystyle S_{n}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{k\in[n]}b_{k}.

Then there exists a random variable
{\bf{S}}\in(\mathcal{A}^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})
such that
S_{n}\Rightarrow{\bf{S}}
. Moreover, the law of
{\bf{S}}
depends only on
\lambda
and
\sigma
, its odd moments vanish and, for every
p\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\tau^{\prime}({\bf{S}}^{2p})=\sum_{\pi\in P_{2}(2p)}\tau\otimes\tau(b_{i_{1}}\cdots b_{i_{2p}}),

where
i\in[p]^{2p}
is any sequence such that
i_{j}=i_{k}
if and only
\{j,k\}\in\pi
.

Proof.

We begin by writing

\displaystyle\tau\otimes\tau(S_{n}^{p})=\frac{1}{n^{p/2}}\sum_{i\in[n]^{p}}\tau\otimes\tau(b_{i_{1}}\cdots b_{i_{p}}).

Since
b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}
are identically distributed, the expression

(7)

\displaystyle\tau\otimes\tau(b_{i_{1}}\cdots b_{i_{p}})

depends only on the partition
\pi=\pi(i)\in P(p)
given by
l\sim_{\pi}k
(that is,
l,k
belong to the same block of
\pi
) if and only if
i_{l}=i_{k}
. Denote the common value of (
7
) by
\tau\otimes\tau(\pi)
. Then we have

\displaystyle\tau\otimes\tau(S_{n}^{p})=\frac{1}{n^{p/2}}\sum_{\pi\in P(p)}\tau\otimes\tau(\pi)|\{i\in[n]^{p}:\pi(i)=\pi\}|.

To count the cardinality, we choose an index for each block. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle|\{i\in[n]^{p}:\pi(i)=\pi\}|=n(n-1)\cdots(n-|\pi|+1)\sim n^{|\pi|}.

By Lemma
4.1
, if
\pi
has a block of size
1
,
\tau\otimes\tau(\pi)=0
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle\tau\otimes\tau(S_{n}^{p})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in P(p)\\
|V|\geq 2;\forall V\in\pi\end{subarray}}\tau\otimes\tau(\pi)\frac{n(n-1)\cdots(n-|\pi|+1)}{n^{p/2}}.

Since
|V|\geq 2
for all blocks
V\in\pi
, we have
|\pi|\leq p/2
. If there exists a block
V\in\pi
such that
|V|\geq 3
, we immediately have
|\pi|<p/2
, and its contribution is negligible. In particular, this implies that the odd moments of
S_{n}
are asymptotically vanishing. We deduce that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\tau\otimes\tau(S_{n}^{p})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in P(p)\\
|V|=2;\forall V\in\pi\end{subarray}}\tau\otimes\tau(\pi)\frac{n(n-1)\cdots(n-|\pi|+1)}{n^{p/2}}.

In this case,
\pi
is a pair partition and
|\pi|=p/2
, hence we deduce the formula

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\tau\otimes\tau(S_{n}^{p})=\sum_{\pi\in P_{2}(p)}\tau\otimes\tau(\pi),

which shows that
S_{n}
converges.
To prove that the limit depends only on
\lambda
and
\sigma
, we write

\displaystyle\tau\otimes\tau(\pi)=\tau\otimes\tau(b_{i_{1}}\cdots b_{i_{p}})=\frac{1}{\delta^{p}}\sum_{I\subseteq[p]}(-1)^{|I|}\lambda^{2|I|}\tau^{2}\left(\prod_{l\in I^{c}}^{\to}a_{i_{l}}\right),

where
\pi(i)=\pi
. By the moment-cumulant formula, we have

\displaystyle\tau\left(\prod_{l\in I^{c}}^{\to}a_{i_{l}}\right)=\sum_{\sigma\in NC(I^{c})}\kappa_{\sigma}((a_{i_{l}})_{l\in I^{c}}).

Since the mixed cumulants of free variables vanish, the only partitions
\sigma\in NC(I^{c})
that contribute are those such that every block
V\in\sigma
has cardinality at most two. We then have

\displaystyle\kappa_{\sigma}((a_{i_{l}})_{l\in I^{c}})

\displaystyle=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}V\in\sigma\\
|V|=2\end{subarray}}\kappa_{|V|}(a,a)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}V\in\sigma\\
|V|=1\end{subarray}}\kappa_{|V|}(a)=\sigma^{2\#\{V\in\sigma:|V|=2\}}\lambda^{\#\{V\in\sigma:|V|=1\}}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

5.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

After proving the existence of the limit in the previous section, the goal here is to identify this limit as stated in Theorem
1.1
. In all this section,
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
denote free copies of a random variable

with mean
\lambda

. Moreover, the common law of the normalized tensors will be denoted by

\displaystyle b=\frac{1}{\delta}\big(a\otimes a-\lambda^{2}\big),

Throughout the proof, we will assume

5.1.
Contribution of noncrossing blocks

We begin by removing interval blocks.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\pi\in P_{2}(p)
and suppose that there exists

\displaystyle\tau\otimes\tau(\pi)=\tau\otimes\tau(\pi\setminus\{l,l+1\}).

Proof.

By cyclicity, we can assume

\displaystyle\delta^{2}\tau\otimes\tau(\pi)

\displaystyle=\tau\otimes\tau\left(b_{i_{1}}\cdots b_{i_{p-2}}\cdot\left(a_{i_{p}}^{2}\otimes a_{i_{p}}^{2}+\lambda^{4}1\otimes 1-2\lambda^{2}a_{i_{p}}\otimes a_{i_{p}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\tau\otimes\tau\left(b_{i_{1}}\cdots b_{i_{p-2}}\cdot a_{i_{p}}^{2}\otimes a_{i_{p}}^{2}\right)+\lambda^{4}\tau\otimes\tau\left(b_{i_{1}}\cdots b_{i_{p-2}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-2\lambda^{2}\tau\otimes\tau\left(b_{i_{1}}\cdots b_{i_{p-2}}a_{i_{p}}\otimes a_{i_{p}}\right)

We immediately recognize

\displaystyle I=\tau^{2}(a^{2})\tau\otimes\tau(\pi\setminus\{p-1,p\});

\displaystyle\delta^{2}\tau\otimes\tau(\pi)=\left(\tau^{2}(a^{2})-\lambda^{4}\right)\tau\otimes\tau(\pi\setminus\{p-1,p\}).

To conclude, we note that

Recall that a noncrossing pair partition

Lemma 5.3

(.ii)
By cyclicity, we have

\displaystyle=\tau\otimes\tau(b_{i_{2}}\cdots b_{i_{p-1}}b_{i_{p}}b_{i_{1}}).

5.3.

(Case

5.4.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We are now ready to prove the main theorem.
Recall that

\displaystyle\tau^{\prime}({\bf{S}}^{p})=\sum_{\pi\in P_{2}(p)}\tau\otimes\tau(\pi)=\sum_{\hat{\pi},(\pi_{T})_{T\in\hat{\pi}}}\prod_{T\in\hat{\pi}}\tau\otimes\tau(\pi_{T}),

where the second summation runs over

, using the bijection
\Phi
defined in (
4
).
Note that if
|T|=2
, then by Corollary
5.2
we have
\tau\otimes\tau(\pi_{T})=1
.
Therefore, we deduce that

\displaystyle\tau^{\prime}({\bf{S}}^{p})=\sum_{\hat{\pi},(\pi_{T})_{T\in\hat{\pi}}}\prod_{\underset{|T|\geq 4}{T\in\hat{\pi}}}\tau\otimes\tau(\pi_{T}).

Using Proposition
5.4
, we get

\displaystyle\tau^{\prime}({\bf{S}}^{p})=\sum_{\hat{\pi}\in NC(p)}\sum_{(\pi_{T})_{T\in\hat{\pi}}}\prod_{\underset{|T|\geq 4}{T\in\hat{\pi}}}2\Big(\frac{q}{2}\Big)^{\frac{|T|}{2}},

where the second summation runs over bipartite connected pair partitions
\pi_{T}

Finally, note that the number of size-two blocks is precisely
\operatorname{\bf ncr}(\pi)
and thus

\displaystyle|\{T\in\hat{\pi}:|T|\geq 4\}|=\operatorname{\bf cc}(\pi)-\operatorname{\bf ncr}(\pi).

\displaystyle\tau^{\prime}({\bf{S}}^{p})=\sum_{\pi\in P_{2}^{\operatorname{bi}}(p)}2^{\operatorname{\bf cc}(\pi)-\operatorname{\bf ncr}(\pi)}\Big(\frac{q}{2}\Big)^{\operatorname{\bf cr}(\pi)}.

This finishes the proof in view of Proposition

\displaystyle\operatorname{\bf ncr}(\pi)+\operatorname{\bf cr}(\pi)=|\pi|=\frac{p}{2}.

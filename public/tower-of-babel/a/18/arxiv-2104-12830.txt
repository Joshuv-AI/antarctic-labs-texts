Traveling fronts for the generalized Fisher- KPP equation with non-local diffusion
José Fuentealba

Abstract
The aim of this paper is to study the generalized Fisher-KPP equation with non-local diffusion. In specific we prove the existence of a critical speed so that traveling front type solutions exist up to this critical speed and non-existence of traveling fronts below this critical value. Moreover, we obtain uniqueness, up to translation, and decay estimates of these traveling fronts.



Keywords:
Non-local reaction-diffusion equation, generalized Fisher-KPP, traveling front solutions, critical speed, Helly’s selection theorem, compactness argument.

1
Introduction

In this paper, we study generalized Fisher-KPP equation with non-local diffusion of the form

u_{t}(x,t)+(-\Delta)^{s}u(x,t)=F(u(x,t)),\ \ \ \forall(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty),

(1)

here
-(\Delta)^{s}
denotes the fractional Laplacian with order
s\in(1/2,1)
and
F
is an appropriated Fisher-KPP type non-linearity.

In the local case, in 1937 under an ecological context, Fisher proposes in
[
7
]
a reaction-diffusion equation to model the advance and growth of an advantage gene in a population with just one free movement dimension. At the same time Kolmogorov, Petrovsky and Piskunov study in
[
9
]
a reaction-diffusion equation qualitatively equivalent to Fisher’s equation but with a more general non-linearity. The equation is the following

u_{t}(x,t)-u_{xx}(x,t)=F(u(x,t)),\ \ \ \forall(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty),

(2)

where
u
is used to model the density of genes in the population and the reaction term
F\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
is a monostable nonlinearity (also called Fisher-KPP). This means that satisfies

F(\tau)>0=F(0)=F(1),\ \forall\tau\in(0,1)\ \ \ \wedge\ \ \ F^{\prime}(1)<0<F^{\prime}(0)\ \ \ \wedge\ \ \ F^{\prime}(\tau)<F^{\prime}(0),\ \forall\tau\in(0,1).

One of the aims on
[
9
]
is prove the existence of traveling front solutions for the equation (
2
). A traveling front solution of (
2
) is a non-decreasing function
u(x,t)=\phi(x+\mu t)
where
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
is called traveling front and solves the following scalar equation

-\phi^{\prime\prime}(x)+\mu\phi^{\prime}(x)=F(\phi(x)),\ \ \ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}.

(3)

Here
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
is called the front speed parameter. The technique used to deal with this is the construction of heteroclinic curves for the system below.

\left\{\begin{matrix}p^{\prime}&=&\mu p-F(\phi),\\
\phi^{\prime}&=&p\end{matrix}\right.

Each heteroclinic curve represent a traveling front solution satisfying the following asymptotic condition

\phi(-\infty):=\displaystyle\lim_{x\to-\infty}\phi(x)=0\ \ \ \wedge\ \ \ \phi(+\infty):=\displaystyle\lim_{x\to+\infty}\phi(x)=1.

(4)

From the method follows there exists a value for the front speed parameter that determines the existence of heteroclinic curves and therefore solutions to (
3
)-(
4
). The result is described as follows: there exists a non-constant traveling front solution to (
2
) if only if the front translates with a speed equal or greater than
\mu^{*}=2\sqrt{F^{\prime}(0)}
. In other words, the problem (
3
)-(
4
) admits a non-constant, non-decreasing and bounded solution if only if
\mu\geq\mu^{*}
. Moreover, the solution is unique up to translation, that is if
\phi
and
\varphi
are solutions to (
3
)-(
4
) with the same value of front speed parameter, then there exits
\tau^{*}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\varphi(x)=\phi(x+\tau^{*})
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

Different result holds when the diffusion term in the equation (
2
) is non-local, in particular when the second order term is replaced by the fractional Laplacian operator. In fact, traveling fronts solution of (
1
) with speed
\mu
are solution of the equation

(-\Delta)^{s}\phi(x)+\mu\phi^{\prime}(x)=F(\phi(x)),\ \ \ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}.

(5)

In
[
2
]
the authors established that the heteroclitic connection is not possible, since Proposition 1.4 in
[
2
]
shows that the unique solution of (
5
) with Fisher-KPP non-linearity are the constants
0
and
1
. Intuitively, this last result established that non-local diffusion together with the reaction term at zero avoids heteroclitic connection. So, either density is zero everywhere or density is one everywhere.

But in
[
8
]
was proved that it’s possible to recover the existence of non-constant traveling fronts solutions if we make a little variation in the reaction term. Let now assume that
F\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
is a generalized Fisher-KPP non-linearity, this means a function for which there exists constants
A_{1},A_{2},p>0
and
\theta\in(0,1)
such that

i)

F(\tau)>0=F(0)=F(1),\ \forall\tau\in(0,1)
,

ii)

F^{\prime}(1)<0
,

iii)

A_{1}\tau^{p}\leq F(\tau)\leq A_{2}\tau^{p},\ \forall\tau\in[0,\theta],

iv)

F^{\prime}(\tau)\geq A_{1}\tau^{p-1},\ \forall\tau\in(0,\theta)
.

Then from
[
8
]
, we get the following result.

Theorem 1

Let
F\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
such that satisfies
i)-iv)
. Then the problem (
5
)-(
4
) admits a pair solution
(\mu_{0},\phi_{0})
for some positive speed
\mu_{0}
and a non-constant non-decreasing front
\phi_{0}:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
if only if
p>2
and
s\geq s(p):=\frac{p}{2(p-1)}.

A Fisher-KPP non-linearity is qualitative equivalent to the function
\tau\mapsto\tau(1-\tau)
, while a generalized Fisher-KPP non-linearity is qualitative equivalent to
\tau\mapsto\tau^{p}(1-\tau)
with
p>0
. This is the reason why we say “a little variation”. Intuitively the difference of Theorem
1
respects the proposition 1.4 in
[
2
]
is due to the brake of acceleration diffusion produced by the null growth of reaction term at zero density. The parameter
p
determines how slow is the growth of the reaction term in a neighborhood of zero and Theorem
1
gives us an infimum to this slowness. Besides shows the relation between diffusion and the reaction process necessary for the existence of non-constant traveling front solutions.

All the mentioned above motivate us to ask if we can replicate the existence and uniqueness result given in
[
9
]
but for the generalized Fisher-KPP non-local equation (
5
)-(
4
). The answer is yes and so here we present our main result.

Theorem 2

Let
p>2
,
s\in[s(p),1)
and
F\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
such that satisfies
i)-iv)
. Then there exists a constant
\mu^{*}>0
such that for all
\mu\geq\mu^{*}
the problem (
5
)-(
4
) admits non-constant and non-decreasing solution
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
. Moreover, this solution is unique up to translation. Otherwise, if
\mu<\mu^{*}
the problem (
5
) admits only the constants solutions 0 and 1.

Observe that from Theorem
2
we recover the existence part of Theorem
1
since
\mu_{0}\in(\mu^{*},+\infty)
is only one value of the speed. Therefore we need a different approach to prove our main theorem. The outline of the proof follows like this. The first task it’s prove the existence assertion of Theorem
2
for an approximated local non-local problem

\left\{\begin{matrix}-\epsilon\phi^{\prime\prime}(x)+(-\Delta)^{s}\phi(x)+\mu\phi^{\prime}(x)=F(\phi(x)),\ \ \ \forall x\in\mathbb{R},\\
\phi(-\infty)=0\ \ \ \wedge\ \ \ \phi(+\infty)=1,\end{matrix}\right.

(6)

for which we have following theorem.

Theorem 3

Let
p>2
,
s\in[s(p),1)
and
F\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
such that satisfies
i)-iv)
. Then for any
\epsilon>0
there exists a constant
\mu^{*}(\epsilon)>0
such that for all
\mu\geq\mu^{*}(\epsilon)
the problem (
6
) admits non-constant and non-decreasing solution
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
. Otherwise, there is not this kind of solution.

The proof of
3
follows in two steps. The first one is to prove for any fixed
\epsilon\geq 0
the existence of a speed value
\mu^{*}(\epsilon)>0
for which the equation (
6
) admits a solution and such that for all
\mu<\mu^{*}(\epsilon)
there is not one. We get this result by the existence of super-solution to (
6
) and the approximation of solution via solutions to the combustion version of (
6
).

This means consider in (
6
) a combustion non-linearity
F:=F_{c}\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
for which there exists
\rho\in(0,1)
such that:
1)
F_{c}(1)=F_{c}(\tau)=0,\ \forall\tau\in[0,\rho]
, 2)
F_{c}(\tau)>0,\ \forall\tau\in(\rho,1)
and 3)
F_{c}^{\prime}(1)<0
. This model is studied in
[
11
]
for the case
\epsilon=0
.

The advantage of combustion nonlinearities approach and the existence of super-solution to (
6
) is respectively that allows us to handle as
\epsilon\to 0
the combustion speeds approaching to
\mu^{*}(\epsilon)
and give us a uniform bound for
\mu^{*}(\epsilon)
for all
\epsilon
small enough.

The second step is prove for fixed
\epsilon>0
the existence of solutions to (
6
) for all
\mu>\mu^{*}(\epsilon)
. This is done fixing
\mu>\mu^{*}(\epsilon)
and approximating as
r\to\infty
a solution to (
6
) via solutions to the problem (
7
) below

\left\{\begin{matrix}-\epsilon\phi^{\prime\prime}(x)+(-\Delta)^{s}\phi(x)+\mu\phi^{\prime}(x)=F(\phi(x)),\ \ \ \forall x\in(-r,\infty),\\
\phi(x)=\vartheta,\ \forall x\leq-r,\\
\phi(+\infty)=1.\end{matrix}\right.

(7)

Again to pass to the limit is needed to fix some level curve for solutions to (
7
) that in the limit allow us to prove the asymptotic condition (
4
). Here the solution to (
6
) with
\mu^{*}(\epsilon)
as the value of the front speed parameter plays the role of super-solution and makes possible to fix the level curve for solutions to (
7
) for all
\mu>\mu^{*}(\epsilon)
.

About the second step, we have to make mention that we don’t prove directly the existence of solutions to (
5
)-(
4
) for all
\mu>\mu^{*}(0)
because the existence of a solution to (
7
) is due to an iterative procedure which works only for
\epsilon>0
.

Come back to the proof of the Theorem
2
. On one hand, the uniform bound establish for
\mu^{*}(\epsilon)
allows us to prove the existence of solutions to (
5
)-(
4
) for all speeds greater than specific value not depending on
\epsilon
(see Theorem
9
). From this last fact, we can define the following infimum speed

\mu^{**}:=\inf\{\tilde{\mu}\ :\ \text{there exists a solution to }\eqref{eqfracFV}-\eqref{conditionFV}\ \text{for all }\mu\geq\tilde{\mu}\}.

In another hand, by the existence result given in Theorem
1
we can define the infimum of speeds for which equation (
5
)-(
4
) admits a solution

\mu^{*}:=\inf\{\mu\ :\ \text{there exists a solution to \eqref{eqfracFV}-\eqref{conditionFV} with }\mu\text{ as speed}\}.

Is easy to see that by
\mu^{*}
definition there is not solution to (
5
)-(
4
) for all
\mu<\mu^{*}
. Also by Proposition
4
below there is not solution to (
5
)-(
4
) for all
\mu<\mu^{*}(0)
. So
\mu^{*}=\mu^{*}(0)
.

From the above, the existence on Theorem
2
follows from proving that
\mu^{*}=\mu^{**}
. Suppose
\mu^{*}>\mu^{**}
, then by
\mu^{**}
definition there exists a speed
\mu^{**}<\mu<\mu^{*}
for which problem (
5
)-(
4
) admits a solution. But this is a contradiction to the infimum definition of
\mu^{*}
, so there holds
\mu^{*}\leq\mu^{**}
. To prove that inequality
\mu^{*}<\mu^{**}
doesn’t hold we exploit Lemma
4
below, that use combustions speeds approach to
\mu^{*}(\epsilon)
for arbitrarily small values of
\epsilon
, to get a contradiction.

Finally, to prove the uniqueness up to translation we first suppose that any solution
\phi
to (
5
)-(
4
) has negative power polynomial tail at
-\infty
. This means suppose that the limit
\displaystyle\lim_{x\to-\infty}|x|^{2s-1}\phi(x)
exists. This make sense because in
[
8
]
was proved that any solution to (
5
)-(
4
) such that
\phi(-1)=\theta
satisfies the following asymptotic behaviour

\exists C>0\ :\ \frac{1}{C|x|^{2s-1}}\leq\phi(x)\leq\frac{C}{|x|^{2s-1}},\ \ \ \forall x<-1.

Then we use a sliding type argument together with a suitable maximum principle to get a contradiction from supposing the non-uniqueness of solutions. To prove the asymptotic condition at
-\infty
we use a barrier technique inspired in the method developed in
[
1
]
. This means suppose that the asymptotic condition doesn’t hold and with this construct a barrier function which touches from below the solution of (
5
)-(
4
). The barrier is constructed in a way such that at
-\infty
the simple comparison between the solution and the barrier gives a contradiction.

The rest of the paper follows like this. In section

we give the preliminaries to be exploited through this paper. In section

we prove the Theorem

. The section

is devoted to prove the existence of solution to (

)-(

) for all

. Finally, in section

we prove the uniqueness up to translation.

Preliminaries

We begin the section with a convergence result for a suitable sequence of functions.

Proposition 1

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
\alpha\in(0,1)

\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

\mathbb{R}

\mathcal{C}^{k,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})

u\in\mathcal{C}^{k,\alpha}_{loc}(\mathbb{R})

The proof of Proposition

\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}_{loc}(\mathbb{R})

Proposition 3

Let

.
\alpha\in(0,1)
, and

\displaystyle=-\epsilon\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\frac{\mu}{2}<+\infty.

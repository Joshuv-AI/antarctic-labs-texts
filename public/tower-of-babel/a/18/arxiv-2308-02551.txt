On the matrix q q -Kummer equation and its solutions
Ravi Dwivedi

Abstract
In the present paper, a general theory for the second-order matrix difference equation of bilateral type is discussed. We introduced the matrix q q -Kummer equation of bilateral type and presented the q q -Kummer matrix function as a series solution. Later, we obtain the integral solutions of this Kummer equation and solution at ∞ \infty . We also give a brief idea about the matrix Gauss difference equation of bilateral type and its solutions. Keywords : Basic hypergeometric function, Difference equations, Matrix functional calculus. AMS Subject Classification : 15A15, 39A13, 33D15.

Introduction

Most of the special functions are presented as a solution of either differential equation or integral equation and hence they play a vital role in the study of special functions. Due to a wide range of applications the Gauss hypergeometric equation and Kummer hypergeometric equation obtained special treatment from mathematician and physicist. These equations are studied from various point of view, for example the

In this paper, alongside the theory of the bilateral type matrix differential equation, we have tried to develop the same for the bilateral type matrix difference equation. 3
Matrix
q
-difference equations

Consider the following general matrix
q
-difference equation

\displaystyle[\delta_{q}

\displaystyle(q^{Q_{1}-1}\delta_{q}+[Q_{1}-1]_{q})\cdots(q^{Q_{s}-1}\delta_{q}+[Q_{s}-1]_{q})

\displaystyle-z\,(q^{P_{1}}\delta_{q}+[P_{1}]_{q})\cdots(q^{P_{r}}\delta_{q}+[P_{r}]_{q})]W(z)=0,

(3.1)

where
P_{1},\dots,P_{r},Q_{1},\dots,Q_{s}
are matrices in
\mathbb{C}^{p\times p}
such that
q^{-k}\notin\sigma(q^{Q_{j}}),1\leq j\leq s
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. One can check that the generalized basic hypergeometric matrix function, defined by

\displaystyle{}_{r}\phi_{s}(q^{P_{1}},\dots,q^{P_{r}};q^{Q_{1}},\dots,q^{Q_{s}};q;(1-q)^{1+s-r}z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}(q^{P_{1}};q)_{n}\cdots(q^{P_{r}};q)_{n}(q^{Q_{1}};q)^{-1}_{n}\cdots(q^{Q_{s}};q)^{-1}_{n}\,\frac{(1-q)^{n\,(1+s-r)}z^{n}}{(q;q)_{n}},

(3.2)

is the solution of the matrix
q
-difference equation (
3.1
). While proving this, we have to directly or indirectly assume that the matrices
P_{1},\dots,P_{r},Q_{1},\dots,Q_{s}
are commutative. To reduce this comprehensive assumption of commutativity, we introduce the concept of matrix
q
-difference equations of bilateral type.

The bilateral type matrix differential equation has been introduced by Jódar and Cortés
[
10
,
11
]
and studied the Gauss hypergeometric matrix equation systemically. Author extended this work for several variable hypergeometric matrix functions
viz.
Appell matrix functions, Lauricella matrix functions of three and
n
variables
[
1
,
3
,
4
]
. This motivates to study second order matrix
q
-difference equation of bilateral type in a systematic way. Consider the second order matrix
q
-difference equation of bilateral type

\displaystyle\phi_{1,q}(z)\,D_{q}^{2}U(z)+D_{q}^{2}U(z)\,\phi_{2,q}(z)+\phi_{3,q}(z)\,D_{q}U(z)+D_{q}U(z)\,\phi_{4,q}(z)

\displaystyle\quad+\phi_{5,q}(z)\,D_{q}U(z)\,\phi_{6,q}(z)+\phi_{7,q}(z)\,U(z)\,\phi_{8,q}(z)=0,

(3.3)

where
\phi_{i,q}(z),1\leq i\leq 8
are bounded continuous functions in an open disc
B(z_{0},p)
of radius
p
centred at
z_{0}
given by
\phi_{i,q}:B(z_{0},p)\rightarrow E_{q}
. Here
E_{q}
is the
q
-Banach space with
2
-norm such that
\lim_{q\rightarrow 1}E_{q}=E
(Banach space of all
p
-square matrices).

The point

is called an ordinary point of

-difference equation (
3.3
) if
\phi_{1,q}(z_{0})
and
\phi_{2,q}(z_{0})
are invertible. In case, the inverse of
\phi_{1,q}(z_{0})
and
\phi_{2,q}(z_{0})
do not exist but each
\phi_{j,q}(z_{0}),3\leq j\leq 8
is invertible, then we called

a singular point. Further, if limits

\displaystyle\lim_{z\rightarrow z_{0}}(z-z_{0})\,\phi_{j,q}(z)\,[\phi_{i,q}(z)]^{-1},\quad i=1,2,\ j=3,4,5,6

(3.4)

and

\displaystyle\lim_{z\rightarrow z_{0}}(z-z_{0})^{2}\,\phi_{k,q}(z)\,[\phi_{i,q}(z)]^{-1},\quad i=1,2,\ k=7,8

(3.5)

exist, then the point

is called a regular singular point of (
3.3
). If any of the limits given above do not exist, then the singular point is irregular.

Let
\phi_{q}:B(z_{0};p)\times E_{q}\rightarrow E_{q}
be a continuous function such that

\displaystyle\|\phi_{q}(z,X_{1,q})-\phi_{q}(z,X_{2,q})\|\leq K\,(|z-z_{0}|)\,\|X_{1,q}-X_{2,q}\|,

(3.6)

where

;

and

is a real valued continuous function on

. Then, there exists an unique solution

of

D_{q}(X_{q})=\phi_{q}(z,X_{q}),

(3.7)

such that

Any two solutions

of second order

-difference equation (

of (

\displaystyle W_{q}(z)=W_{1,q}(z)\,A+W_{2,q}(z)\,B,

(3.8)

where matrices

and

are uniquely obtained by

.

4
The
q
-Kummer matrix equation

Before discussing
q
-Kummer matrix equation and its solution we will define
q
-Kummer matrix function and give its regions of convergence. Let
S
,
T
be
p
-square matrices such that
q^{-k}\notin\sigma(q^{T}),k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Then, we define the
q
-Kummer matrix function as

\displaystyle{}_{1}\phi_{1}(q^{S};q^{T};q;z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(q^{S};q)_{n}(q^{T};q)_{n}^{-1}}{(q;q)_{n}}\,z^{n}.

(4.1)

For convergence,

\displaystyle\|{}_{1}\phi_{1}(q^{S};q^{T};q;z)\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n\geq 0}\left\|\frac{(q^{S};q)_{n}(q^{T};q)_{n}^{-1}}{(q;q)_{n}}\,z^{n}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(-\|q^{S}\|;|q|)_{n}\|(q^{T};q)_{n}^{-1}\|}{|(q;q)_{n}|}|z|^{n}=\sum_{n\geq 0}U_{n}\,|z|^{n}.

(4.2)

Now, using the ratio test

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left|\frac{U_{n+1}}{U_{n}}\right|

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left|\frac{(-\|q^{S}\|;|q|)_{n+1}\|(q^{T};q)_{n+1}^{-1}\||z|^{n+1}|(q;q)_{n}|}{(-\|q^{S}\|;|q|)_{n}\|(q^{T};q)_{n}^{-1}\||z|^{n}|(q;q)_{n+1}|}\right|

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left|\frac{(1+|q|^{n}\|q^{S}\|)\|(I-q^{n}q^{T})^{-1}\||z|}{1-|q|^{n+1}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{(1+|q|^{n}\|q^{S}\|)\,|z|}{(1-|q|^{n+1})\,(1-|q|^{n}\|q^{T}\|)}=|z|.

(4.3)

Thus the
q
-Kummer matrix function
{}_{1}\phi_{1}(q^{S};q^{T};q;z)
converges absolutely when
|q|<1
and
|z|<1
.

Next, we give the matrix
q
-Kummer equation of bilateral type and discuss the distinct solutions. Let
S
,
T
be matrices in
\mathbb{C}^{p\times p}
such that
q^{-k}\notin\sigma(q^{T}),k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Then, we introduce the matrix

-Kummer equation of bilateral type as

\displaystyle zD_{q}^{2}U(z)q^{T}+D_{q}U(z)[T]_{q}-q^{S}zD_{q}U(z)-[S]_{q}U(z)=0.

(4.4)

This is an equation of the form (
3.3
), on comparing we have

\displaystyle\phi_{2,q}(z)=z\,q^{T},

\displaystyle\quad\phi_{3,q}(z)=-z\,q^{S},\quad\phi_{4,q}(z)=[T]_{q},\quad\phi_{7,q}(z)=-[S]_{q}.

(4.5)

The only singular points of the equation (
4.4
) are
z=0
and
z=\infty

-difference equation about the singular point

viz.
solution at
\infty

\displaystyle U(z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}U_{n}z^{n}

(4.6)

which yields

\displaystyle D_{q}\,U(z)=\sum_{n\geq 1}U_{n}\,[n]_{q}\,z^{n-1},\quad D_{q}^{2}\,U(z)=\sum_{n\geq 2}U_{n}\,[n]_{q}\,[n-1]_{q}\,z^{n-2}.

(4.7)

Now, substituting these values in (

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 2}U_{n}\,[n]_{q}\,[n-1]_{q}\,z^{n-1}q^{T}+\sum_{n\geq 1}U_{n}\,[n]_{q}\,z^{n-1}[T]_{q}-q^{S}\,\sum_{n\geq 1}U_{n}\,[n]_{q}\,z^{n}

\displaystyle\quad-[S]_{q}\sum_{n\geq 0}U_{n}\,z^{n}=0.

(4.8)

After some manipulation, the equation (
4.8

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 2}\left(U_{n+1}[n+1]_{q}\,[n]_{q}q^{T}+U_{n+1}[n+1]_{q}[T]_{q}-q^{S}U_{n}[n]_{q}-[S]_{q}U_{n}\right)\,z^{n}

\displaystyle\quad+(U_{2}[2]_{q}q^{T}+U_{2}[2]_{q}[T]_{q}-q^{S}U_{1}-[S]_{q}U_{1})\,z+U_{1}[T]_{q}-[S]_{q}U_{0}=0.

(4.9)

Equating the powers of
z
and hence one can obtain

\displaystyle U_{1}\,[T]_{q}=[S]_{q}U_{0}\implies U_{1}=\frac{[S]_{q}\,U_{0}\,[T]_{q}^{-1}\,(1-q)}{(1-q)};

(4.10)

\displaystyle U_{2}=\frac{[S+I]_{q}\,U_{1}\,[T+I]_{q}^{-1}}{[2]_{q}}

\displaystyle=\frac{[S+I]_{q}\,[S]_{q}\,U_{0}\,[T+I]_{q}^{-1}\,[T]_{q}^{-1}}{[2]_{q}}

\displaystyle=\frac{[S+I]_{q}\,[S]_{q}\,U_{0}\,[T+I]_{q}^{-1}\,[T]_{q}^{-1}\,(1-q)^{2}}{(1-q)\,(1-q^{2})}.

(4.11)

In general

\displaystyle U_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{[S+nI]_{q}\,U_{n}\,[T+nI]_{q}^{-1}}{[n+1]_{q}}

\displaystyle=\frac{[S+nI]_{q}\,\cdots[S]_{q}\,U_{0}\,[T+nI]_{q}^{-1}\,\cdots[T]_{q}^{-1}\,(1-q)^{n}}{(1-q)\,(1-q^{2})\cdots(1-q^{n+1})}.

(4.12)

Hence there exists a solution

(4.13)

\displaystyle U(z)=V(z)\,z^{I-T},\quad z^{I-T}=\exp((I-T)\ln z).

(4.14)

The

\displaystyle D_{q}U(z)

\displaystyle=V(z)\,[I-T]_{q}z^{-T}+D_{q}V(z)(qz)^{I-T}

(4.15)

\displaystyle D_{q}^{2}U(z)

\displaystyle=V(z)\,[I-T]_{q}\,[-T]_{q}\,z^{-T-I}+D_{q}V(z)\,[I-T]_{q}\,(qz)^{-T}+D_{q}^{2}V(z)(q^{2}z)^{I-T}

\displaystyle\quad+qD_{q}V(z)\,[I-T]_{q}\,(qz)^{-T}.

(4.16)

Substituting these values in (
4.4
) yield

\displaystyle z\,D_{q}^{2}V(z)\,q^{2I-T}+D_{q}V(z)\,[2I-T]_{q}-z\,q^{S+I-T}\,D_{q}V(z)-[S+I-T]_{q}V(z)=0.

(4.17)

The equation (
4.17
) is a matrix
q
-difference equation of type (
4.4
) and its solution is given by
{}_{1}\phi_{1}(q^{\tilde{S}};q^{\tilde{T}};q;(1-q)\,z)
, where
\tilde{S}=S+I-T
and
\tilde{T}=2I-T
. Thus we can conclude that the matrix
q
-Kummer equation (
4.4
) has two fundamental solutions as

\displaystyle U_{1}(z)

\displaystyle={}_{1}\phi_{1}(q^{S};q^{T};q;(1-q)\,z);

\displaystyle U_{2}(z)

\displaystyle=z^{I-T}\,{}_{1}\phi_{1}(q^{S+I-T};q^{2I-T};q;(1-q)\,z),\quad ST=TS.

(4.18)

In view of the convergence condition for
q
-Kummer matrix function, the solutions
U_{1}(z)
and
U_{2}(z)
converges absolutely in the region
|q|<1
and
|(1-q)\,z|<1
.

4.1
Solutions at
\infty

Now, we seek for the solution of matrix
q
-Kummer equation at infinity and so we rewrite the equation (
4.4
) as

\displaystyle U(q^{2}z)q^{T-I}-[I+q^{S}(q-1)z]\,U(qz)-U(qz)\,q^{T-I}+[1+(q-1)z]\,U(z)=0.

(4.19)

Let
u=z^{-1}
,
s=q^{-1}
,
W(u)=U(z^{-1})
and
ST=TS
. Then (
4.19
) becomes

\displaystyle u\,W(s^{2}u)-[(1-s)\,s^{T-S-2I}+(I+s^{T-I})\,u]\,W(su)

\displaystyle\quad+[(1-s)\,s^{T-2I}+u\,s^{T-I}]\,W(u)=0.

(4.20)

Which can be written in the derivative form as

\displaystyle u^{3}\,D_{s}^{2}W(u)+[u\,s^{T-S-3I}-u^{2}\,[T-2I]_{s}]D_{s}\,W(u)+s^{T-3I}\,[-S]_{s}\,W(u)=0,

(4.21)

where
D_{s}\,W(u)=\frac{W(u)-W(su)}{(1-s)\,u}
and
[S]_{s}=\frac{I-s^{S}}{1-s}
. This is the matrix
s
-difference equation of the form (
3.3
) where

\displaystyle\phi_{1,s}(u)=u^{3},\ \phi_{3,s}(u)=u\,s^{T-S-3I}-u^{2}\,[T-2I]_{s},\ \phi_{7,s}(u)=s^{T-3I}\,[-S]_{s}.

(4.22)

All of the above are well defined near
u=0
except the limit

\displaystyle\lim_{u\rightarrow 0}\frac{u\,\phi_{3,s}(u)}{\phi_{1,s}(u)}=\lim_{u\rightarrow 0}\frac{u\,[u\,s^{T-S-3I}-u^{2}\,[T-2I]_{s}]}{u^{3}}.

(4.23)

Thus, point
u=0
is irregular singular for (
4.21
) and hence the point
z=\infty
is irregular for (
4.4
). Therefore, the equation (
4.4
) does not have a solution at
\infty
. Unfortunately, we are not getting a convergent solution of (
4.4
) at
z=\infty
but it is possible to get some integral solutions which are being discussed in the next section.

4.2
Integral solutions of
q
-Kummer matrix equation

Consider one of the integral solution of (
4.4
) in the form

\displaystyle U_{1}(z)=\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{(1-q)q^{2}z}}E_{q}^{-qzu}\,f(qu)\,d_{q}u,

(4.24)

where
E_{q}^{-qzu}
and its
q
-derivative are defined by
[
7
]

\displaystyle E_{q}^{-qzu}=((1-q)qzu;q)_{\infty}\text{ and }D_{q}E_{q}^{-qzu}=-qu\,E_{q}^{-q^{2}zu}.

(4.25)

Using (
2.18
) and (
4.25
), one can get

\displaystyle D_{q}\,U_{1}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{(1-q)q^{2}z}}(-qu)\,E_{q}^{-q^{2}zu}\,f(qu)\,d_{q}u-\left(\frac{1}{(1-q)q^{3}z^{2}}\,E_{q}^{-q^{2}zu}f(qu)\right)_{u=1/(1-q)q^{3}z}

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{(1-q)q^{2}z}}qu\,E_{q}^{-q^{2}zu}\,f(qu)\,d_{q}u,

(4.26)

and

\displaystyle D_{q}^{2}\,U_{1}(z)

\displaystyle=-D_{q}\left(\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{(1-q)q^{2}z}}qu\,E_{q}^{-q^{2}zu}\,f(qu)\,d_{q}u\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{(1-q)q^{2}z}}q^{3}u^{2}\,E_{q}^{-q^{3}zu}\,f(qu)\,d_{q}u.

(4.27)

Substituting these values in (
4.4
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{(1-q)q^{2}z}}[-f(qu)D_{q,u}(E_{q}^{-q^{2}zu})q^{T+I}u^{2}-u\,q^{S}\,f(qu)\,D_{q,u}(E_{q}^{-qzu})

\displaystyle\quad-quf(qu)E_{q}^{-q^{2}zu}[T]_{q}-[S]_{q}f(qu)E_{q}^{-qzu}]d_{q}u

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{(1-q)q^{2}z}}E_{q}^{-q^{2}zu}\,D_{q,u}(f(u)q^{T-I}u^{2})+E_{q}^{-qzu}D_{q,u}(q^{S-I}f(u)\,u)

\displaystyle\quad-quf(qu)E_{q}^{-q^{2}zu}[T]_{q}-[S]_{q}f(qu)E_{q}^{-qzu}]d_{q}u

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{(1-q)q^{2}z}}E_{q}^{-q^{2}zu}\{D_{q,u}(f(u)q^{T-I}u^{2})+(1-(1-q)qzu)D_{q,u}(q^{S-I}f(u)\,u)

\displaystyle\quad-quf(qu)[T]_{q}-[S]_{q}f(qu)+(1-q)qzu[S]_{q}f(qu)\}d_{q}u=0,

(4.28)

where
D_{q,u}
is the
q
-derivative with respect to
u
. Since the integral value is zero so one of the possibility is that

\displaystyle D_{q,u}(f(u)q^{T-I}u^{2})+(1-(1-q)qzu)D_{q,u}(q^{S-I}f(u)\,u)-quf(qu)[T]_{q}

\displaystyle\quad-[S]_{q}f(qu)+(1-q)qzu[S]_{q}f(qu)=0.

(4.29)

Expanding the term containing
q
-derivative, we get a difference equation in terms of
f(u)
as

\displaystyle\{1+[q-(1-q)qz]\,u\}f(qu)-q^{S-I}\{1-(1-q)qzu\}f(u)-uf(u)q^{T-I}=0.

(4.30)

One can easily verify that for
ST=TS
the matrix function,

\displaystyle F(u)=u^{S-I}\,([(1-q)qz-q]u;q)_{\infty}\,([(1-q)qz-q^{T-S}]u;q)_{\infty}^{-1},

(4.31)

is a solution of recurrence relation (
4.30
). Thus
U_{1}(z)
can be expressed as

\displaystyle U_{1}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{(1-q)q^{2}z}}E_{q}^{-qzu}\,{(qu)}^{S-I}([(1-q)qz-q]qu;q)_{\infty}

\displaystyle\quad\times([(1-q)qz-q^{T-S}]qu;q)_{\infty}^{-1}\,d_{q}u.

(4.32)

This is an integral solution of
q
-Kummer matrix equation (
4.4
). We already discussed, a second order difference equation must have two fundamental solutions. So there is an opportunity of getting another integral solution. To get the second solution we use the another
q
-analogue of exponential function exists in the literature
[
7
]
. We will look the second solution in the form

\displaystyle U_{2}(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e_{q}^{-zu}\,f(qu)\,d_{q}u,

(4.33)

where

\displaystyle e_{q}^{-zu}=\frac{1}{(-(1-q)zu;q)_{\infty}}.

(4.34)

The
q
-derivative of
e_{q}^{-zu}
with respect to
z
is

\displaystyle D_{q}e_{q}^{-zu}=-u\,e_{q}^{-zu}.

(4.35)

Further, using (
2.18
) and (
4.35
), one can get

\displaystyle D_{q}U_{2}(z)=-\int_{0}^{\infty}u\,e_{q}^{-zu}\,f(qu)\,d_{q}u,

(4.36)

\displaystyle D_{q}^{2}U_{2}(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{2}\,e_{q}^{-zu}\,f(qu)\,d_{q}u.

(4.37)

Substituting these values in (
4.4
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}f(qu)[-(D_{q,u}e_{q}^{-zu})q^{T}u^{2}-(D_{q,u}e_{q}^{-zu})q^{S}u-([S]_{q}+[T]_{q}u)e_{q}^{-zu}]d_{q}u=0.

(4.38)

Which can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e_{q}^{-zu}[D_{q,u}(f(u)q^{T-2I}u^{2})+D_{q,u}(f(u)q^{S-I}u)-f(qu)([S]_{q}+[T]_{q}u)]d_{q}u=0.

(4.39)

From above equation, one can obtain a matrix
q
-difference equation in terms of
f(u)
as

\displaystyle(1+u)f(qu)-(q^{S-I}+q^{T-2I}u)f(u)=0.

(4.40)

It is easy to verify that the matrix
q
-difference equation (
4.40
) has solution

\displaystyle f(u)=u^{S-I}{(-u;q)_{\infty}}(-q^{S-T-I}u;q)_{\infty}^{-1}.

(4.41)

Thus,
U_{2}(z)
can be expressed as

\displaystyle U_{2}(z)=q^{S-I}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{S-I}\,e_{q}^{-zu}\,{(-qu;q)_{\infty}}(-q^{S-T}u;q)_{\infty}^{-1}\,d_{q}u.

(4.42)

We now summarize the integral solutions of (
4.4
) in the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
S
and
T
be commuting matrices in
\mathbb{C}^{p\times p}
such that
S
,
T
and
T-S
are positive stable. Then the matrix equation (
4.4
) has two integral solutions as

\displaystyle U_{1}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{(1-q)q^{2}z}}E_{q}^{-qzu}\,{(qu)}^{S-I}([(1-q)qz-q]qu;q)_{\infty}

\displaystyle\quad\times([(1-q)qz-q^{T-S}]qu;q)_{\infty}^{-1}\,d_{q}u;

\displaystyle U_{2}(z)

\displaystyle=q^{S-I}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{S-I}\,e_{q}^{-zu}\,{(-qu;q)_{\infty}}(-q^{S-T}u;q)_{\infty}^{-1}\,d_{q}u.

(4.43)

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we determined the solutions of
q
-Kummer matrix equation of bilateral type. We discussed the series solutions, solution at infinity and integral solutions. One can observe that the only solution for non-commutative
S
and
T
is in (
4.13
). For other solutions, we are directly or indirectly assuming that
S
and
T
are commuting.

The present work of
{}_{1}\phi_{1}(q^{S};q^{T};q;(1-q)z)
can be easily extended for several variable basic hypergeometric matrix function. One can introduce the matrix
q
-difference equation of bilateral type and obtain its distinct form of solutions. In particular, let
P
,
Q
,
R
be matrices in
\mathbb{C}^{p\times p}
such that
q^{-k}\notin\sigma(q^{R}),k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Then, it is easy to show that the matrix
q
-difference equation,

\displaystyle\left[\delta_{q}(q^{R-I}\,\delta_{q}+[R-I]_{q})-z(q^{P}\,\delta_{q}+[P]_{q})\,(q^{Q}\,\delta_{q}+[Q]_{q})\right]W(z)=0,

(5.1)

has basic Gauss hypergeometric matrix function
{}_{2}\phi_{1}(q^{P},q^{Q};q^{R};q;z)
as a solution provided the matrices
P
,
Q
,
R
commutes with each other. To overcome with such comprehensive assumption of commutativity, one can introduce a second order bilateral type matrix
q
-difference equation

\displaystyle zD_{q}^{2}\,W(z)\,q^{R}-z[P]_{q}\,D_{q}W(z)\,q^{Q}+D_{q}W(z)\,[R]_{q}-z\,q^{P}\,D_{q}W(z)[Q+I]_{q}

\displaystyle\quad-z^{2}q^{P+I}D_{q}^{2}W(z)\,q^{Q}-[P]_{q}\,W(z)\,[Q]_{q}=0.

(5.2)

The equation (
5.2
) is a
q
-analogue of matrix Gauss hypergeometric equation given in
[
10
]
. On comparing the above equation with (
3.3
), we get

\displaystyle\phi_{1,q}(z)

\displaystyle=-z^{2}\,q^{P+I},\ \phi_{2,q}(z)=z\,q^{R-Q},\ \phi_{3,q}(z)=-z\,[P]_{q},\ \phi_{4,q}(z)=[R]_{q}\,q^{-Q},

\displaystyle\phi_{5,q}(z)

\displaystyle=-z\,q^{P},\ \phi_{6,q}(z)=[Q+I]_{q}\,q^{-Q},\ \phi_{7,q}(z)=-[P]_{q},\ \phi_{8,q}(z)=[Q]_{q}\,q^{-Q}.

(5.3)

Treating in the same way to matrix
q
-Kummer equation, one can obtain that the series solutions of the equation (
5.2
) are

\displaystyle W_{1}(z)

\displaystyle={}_{2}\phi_{1}(q^{P},q^{Q};q^{R};q;z),\quad QR=RQ;

\displaystyle W_{2}(z)

\displaystyle=z^{I-R}\,{}_{2}\phi_{1}(q^{P+I-R},q^{Q+I-R};q^{2I-R};q;z),\quad PR=RP,QR=RQ.

(5.4)

Finding the other form of solutions of matrix difference equation (
5.2
) is an interesting problem and yet to be done. To find the matrix
q
-difference equation satisfied by basic Gauss hypergeometric matrix function for non-commutative matrices is also an open problem and may be obtained in my upcoming articles.

A Novel Gaussian Min-Max Theorem and its Applications
Danil Akhtiamov ∗ , Reza Ghane ∗ , Nithin K Varma and Babak Hassibi, David Bosch ∗

Abstract
A celebrated result by Gordon allows one to compare the min-max behavior of two Gaussian processes if certain inequality conditions are met. The consequences of this result include the Gaussian min-max (GMT) and convex Gaussian min-max (CGMT) theorems which have had far-reaching implications in high-dimensional statistics, machine learning, non-smooth optimization, and signal processing. Both theorems rely on a pair of Gaussian processes, first identified by Slepian, that satisfy Gordon’s comparison inequalities. In this paper, we identify a new pair of Gaussian processes satisfying these inequalities. The resulting theorems extend the classical GMT and CGMT Theorems from the case where the underlying Gaussian matrix in the primary process has iid rows to where it has independent but non-identically-distributed ones. The new CGMT is applied to the problems of multi-source Gaussian regression, as well as to binary classification of general Gaussian mixture models.

I
Introduction and Motivation

Assume the matrix
G\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d}
, the vectors
g\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
h\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, and the scalar
\gamma
all have i.i.d
{\cal N}(0,1)
, i.e., standard normal entries. Then, considering deterministic vectors
w\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, belonging to some sets
{\cal S}_{w}
and
{\cal S}_{v}
, we can obtain the following pair of Gaussian processes

X_{wv}=v^{T}Gw+\gamma\|w\|_{2}\|v\|_{2}~~~\mbox{and}~~~Y_{wv}=\|v\|_{2}g^{T}w+\|w\|_{2}h^{T}v,

which we will refer to as a
Slepian pair

(
Slepian, 1962
)
. For such Slepian pairs, it can be readily shown that

\mathbb{E}X_{wv}X_{w^{\prime}v^{\prime}}-\mathbb{E}Y_{wv}Y_{w^{\prime}v^{\prime}}=\left(\|w\|_{2}\|w^{\prime}\|_{2}-w^{T}w^{\prime})(\|v\|_{2}\|v^{\prime}\|_{2}-v^{T}v^{\prime}\right)

By recalling Cauchy-Schwarz, this implies that the processes
X_{wv}
and
Y_{wv}
satisfy the following (so-called) comparison inequalities

\mathbb{E}X_{wv}X_{w^{\prime}v^{\prime}}\leq\mathbb{E}Y_{wv}Y_{w^{\prime}v^{\prime}}~~~\mbox{and}~~~\mathbb{E}X_{wv}X_{wv^{\prime}}\geq\mathbb{E}Y_{wv}Y_{wv^{\prime}}

(1)

For Gaussian processes that satisfy the inequalities (
1
),
Gordon, 1985
showed that it is possible to relate

\mathbb{P}\left(\min_{w\in{\cal S}_{w}}\max_{v\in{\cal S}_{v}}X_{wv}\geq\psi(w,v)\right)

to

\mathbb{P}\left(\min_{w\in{\cal S}_{w}}\max_{v\in{\cal S}_{v}}Y_{wv}\geq\psi(w,v)\right),

for any function
\psi(w,v)
. We refer the reader to the next section for more details. This remarkable result allows one to infer properties of a so-called
primary optimization
(PO)

\min_{w\in{\cal S}_{w}}\max_{v\in{\cal S}_{v}}v^{T}Gw+\psi(w,v)

in terms of a simpler so-called
auxiliary optimization
(AO)

\min_{w\in{\cal S}_{w}}\max_{v\in{\cal S}_{v}}\|v\|_{2}g^{T}w+\|w\|_{2}h^{T}v+\psi(w,v).

This is the gist of the Gaussian min-max (GMT)
(
Gordon, 1988
)
and convex Gaussian min-max (CGMT)
(
Thrampoulidis et al., 2014
)
theorems. The CGMT, in particular, has found numerous recent applications where, along with statistical physics-inspired methods, such as the replica method and approximate message passing (AMP), it has been used to derive sharp bounds on the performance of a variety of problems in high-dimensional statistics, machine learning, non-smooth convex optimization, and signal processing (e.g.,
(
Stojnic, 2013
;
Salehi et al., 2019
;
Akhtiamov et al., 2023
;
Mignacco et al., 2020
;
Thrampoulidis et al., 2018
;
Javanmard and Soltanolkotabi, 2022
;
Montanari et al., 2019
;
Salehi et al., 2020
;
Aolaritei et al., 2023
;
Zhou et al., 2024
)
)

In this paper, we identify a novel pair of Gaussian processes that satisfy Gordon’s inequalities (
1
). This in turn allows for comparison between a new primary and auxiliary optimization, which, as we show below, can express problems which the known CGMT can not analyze.

The new pair of processes are as follows:

X_{w,v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\left[v_{\ell}^{T}G_{\ell}\Sigma_{\ell}^{\frac{1}{2}}w+\gamma_{\ell}\|\Sigma_{\ell}^{\frac{1}{2}}w\|_{2}\|v_{\ell}\|_{2}\right],

and

Y_{w,v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\left[\|v_{\ell}\|_{2}g_{\ell}^{T}\Sigma_{\ell}^{\frac{1}{2}}w+\|\Sigma_{\ell}^{\frac{1}{2}}w\|_{2}h_{\ell}^{T}v_{\ell}\right],

where
\{G_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{\ell}\times d}\}_{\ell=1}^{k}
,

\{g_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\}_{\ell=1}^{k}
,

\{h_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{\ell}}\}_{\ell=1}^{k}
and
\{\gamma_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{R}}\}_{\ell=1}^{k}

all have i.i.d
{\cal N}(0,1)
standard normal entries and
\Sigma_{\ell}^{1/2}
are
d\times d
are positive semi-definite (PSD) matrices.

The remainder of the paper is devoted to obtaining the CGMT counterpart of the above pair of Gaussian random processes and to applying this novel CGMT to analyze two specific problems: firstly, multi-source Gaussian regression and, secondarily, binary classification of general Gaussian mixture models. We note that neither problem lends itself to analysis via the conventional CGMT.

Preliminaries

III-A

Gordon’s comparison inequality for Gaussian Processes

Introduced by Gordon in his landmark paper
(
Gordon, 1985
)
, the following comparison inequality is instrumental in proving the main results of our paper:

Theorem 1

(Gordon’s comparison inequality
Gordon, 1985
)
.

Let
I
and
J
be two
finite
sets and
\{X_{ij}\}_{i\in I,j\in J}
,
\{Y_{ij}\}_{i\in I,j\in J}
be two real valued centered Gaussian processes, which satisfy the following conditions:

1.

\mathbb{E}X_{ij}^{2}=\mathbb{E}Y_{ij}^{2}
for all
i\in I,j\in J

2.

\mathbb{E}X_{ij}X_{ij^{\prime}}\geq\mathbb{E}Y_{ij}Y_{ij^{\prime}}
for all
i\in I,j,j^{\prime}\in J

3.

\mathbb{E}X_{ij}X_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\leq\mathbb{E}Y_{ij}Y_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}
for all
i\neq i^{\prime}\in I,j,j^{\prime}\in J

Let
\{t_{ij}\}_{i\in I,j\in J}
be an arbitrary sequence of real numbers indexed by
(i,j)\in I\times J
. Then the following inequality holds:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcap_{i\in I}\bigcup_{j\in J}[Y_{ij}\geq t_{ij}]\right)\geq\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcap_{i\in I}\bigcup_{j\in J}[X_{ij}\geq t_{ij}]\right)

Now, let
G\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
,
g\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
h\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
all have i.i.d
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
components,
I
and
J
be finite sets and
\{w_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\{u_{j}\}_{j\in J}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be arbitrary discrete subsets. It was first observed in
Gordon, 1988
that the following processes satisfy the assumptions of Theorem
1
:

\displaystyle Y_{ij}=v_{j}^{T}Gw_{i}+\gamma\|w_{i}\|_{2}\|v_{j}\|_{2}

\displaystyle X_{ij}=\|v_{j}\|_{2}g^{T}w_{i}+\|w_{i}\|_{2}h^{T}v_{j}

By making an appropriate choice for the values of
\{t_{ij}\}_{i\in I,j\in J}
, and by proving that finite discrete subsets can be extended to compact sets, the following theorem can be proven. This theorem provides a probabilistic upper bound on the value of a more complicated optimization problem
\Phi
, which extends
Y_{ij}
, being larger than some threshold
t
, by means of the same quantity for an alternative optimization
\phi
, which extends
X_{ij}
. The alternative problem,
\phi
, is in general easier to analyze.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{w}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{S}_{v}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be compact sets and
\psi(w,v):\mathcal{S}_{w}\times\mathcal{S}_{v}\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function. Let
G\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
,
g\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
h\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
all have i.i.d
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
components, and be independent of each other. Then letting

\displaystyle\Phi(G,\gamma):=\min_{w\in\mathcal{S}_{w}}\max_{v\in\mathcal{S}_{v}}v^{T}Gw+\gamma\|w\|_{2}\|v\|_{2}+\psi(w,v)

\displaystyle\phi(g,h):=\min_{w\in\mathcal{S}_{w}}\max_{v\in\mathcal{S}_{v}}\|v\|_{2}g^{T}w+\|w\|_{2}h^{T}v+\psi(w,v)

We have for any
t\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\Phi(G,\gamma)<t)\leq\mathbb{P}(\phi(g,h)<t)

The theorem above has an immediate corollary:

Corollary 1

(Gaussian Min-Max Theorem)
.

Assume the same setup as Theorem
2
. Then letting

\displaystyle\Phi(G):=\min_{w\in\mathcal{S}_{w}}\max_{v\in\mathcal{S}_{v}}v^{T}Gw+\psi(w,v)

\displaystyle\phi(g,h):=\min_{w\in\mathcal{S}_{w}}\max_{v\in\mathcal{S}_{v}}\|v\|_{2}g^{T}w+\|w\|_{2}h^{T}v+\psi(w,v)

we have for any
t\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\Phi(G,\gamma)<t)\leq 2\mathbb{P}(\phi(g,h)<t)

III-B

Standard CGMT

Informally, the corollary above has the following interpretation: if
\phi(g,h)
concentrates to a value
c
, then
\Phi(G,\gamma)<c+\epsilon
holds with high probability for
\epsilon>0
.
While the above corollary is sufficient for some applications, it has two distinct drawbacks. Firstly, it only provides a probabilistic upper bound, without a corresponding lower bound the exact value of
\Phi
cannot be determined asymptotically. Secondarily, it only provides information about the value of
\Phi
in terms of the optimal value of
\phi
but provides no information about the relationship between their solutions.

It turns out that the inequality from the Corollary
1
can be reversed under the additional assumptions that
\psi(w,v)
is convex-concave and that the sets
{\cal S}_{w}
and
{\cal S}_{v}
are convex. Furthermore, in this case, if the solution of
\phi
belongs to an open set
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{S}_{w}
with high probability then so does the solution of
\Phi
. Formally, the following theorem holds (see
(
Thrampoulidis et al., 2014
;
Thrampoulidis et al., 2015
;
Thrampoulidis, 2016
)
):

Theorem 3

(Convex Gaussian Min-Max Theorem)
.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{w}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{S}_{v}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be compact convex sets and
\psi(w,v):\mathcal{S}_{w}\times\mathcal{S}_{v}\to\mathbb{R}
be convex-concave. Furthermore, let
G\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
,
g\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
h\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
all have i.i.d
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
components. Define

\displaystyle\Phi(G):=\min_{w\in\mathcal{S}_{w}}\max_{v\in\mathcal{S}_{v}}v^{T}Gw+\psi(w,v)

\displaystyle\phi(g,h):=\min_{w\in\mathcal{S}_{w}}\max_{v\in\mathcal{S}_{v}}\|v\|_{2}g^{T}w+\|w\|_{2}h^{T}v+\psi(w,v)

The following statements hold:

1.

For any
\eta,c\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(|\Phi(G)-c|>\eta)\leq 2\mathbb{P}(|\phi(g,h)-c|>\eta)

2.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be an arbitrary open subset of
\mathcal{S}_{w}
and
\mathcal{S}^{c}=\mathcal{S}_{w}\setminus\mathcal{S}
. Define:

\displaystyle\Phi_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}(G):=\min_{w\in\mathcal{S}^{c}}\max_{v\in\mathcal{S}_{v}}v^{T}Gw+\psi(w,v)

\displaystyle\phi_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}(g,h):=\min_{w\in\mathcal{S}^{c}}\max_{v\in\mathcal{S}_{v}}\|v\|_{2}g^{T}w+\|w\|_{2}h^{T}v+\psi(w,v)

\displaystyle w_{\Phi}(G):=\argmin_{w\in\mathcal{S}_{w}}\max_{v\in\mathcal{S}_{v}}v^{T}Gw+\psi(w,v)

Assume also that there exist
\epsilon>0
,
\delta>0
,
\bar{\phi},\bar{\phi}_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}
such that

•

\bar{\phi}_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}\geq\bar{\phi}+3\delta

•

\phi(g,h)<\bar{\phi}+\delta
with probability at least
1-\epsilon

•

\phi_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}>\bar{\phi}_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}-\delta
with probability at least
1-\epsilon

Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(w_{\Phi}(G)\in\mathcal{S})\geq 1-4\epsilon

III-C

Multi-source Gaussian regression

To demonstrate the power of the novel CGMT presented in this work, we will focus on two examples. Here we consider Multi-source Gaussian Regression. Consider a setup of
k
-measurement devices and consider a dataset of
\left\{(x_{i}^{l},y_{i}^{l})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\right\}_{i=1,l=1}^{n,k}
of
n

d-
dimensional observations by each of the
k
devices, with corresponding labels
y_{i}^{l}
. We will define
y_{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
element-wise by
(y_{l})_{i}=y_{i,l}
and similarly define
X_{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
by means of
(X_{l})_{i,j}=(x^{l}_{i})_{j}
. Our regression problem then takes the form of

\min_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{nk}\sum_{l=1}^{k}\ell_{l}\left(y_{l}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}X_{l}\theta\right)+R(\theta),

(2)

where
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\ell_{l}
and
R
are convex loss and regularization functions respectively. We shall assume that
X_{l}
are independent Gaussian matrices, defined by
X_{l}=G_{l}\Sigma_{l}^{1/2}
, where
G_{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
have i.i.d standard Gaussian elements, and
\Sigma_{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
are covariance matrices. Finally, we will assume the following model, which relates the labels to the datapoints:

y_{l}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}X_{l}\theta^{*}+\nu_{l},

where
\theta^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
denotes the "true" relationship, and
\nu_{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is i.i.d zero mean noise, with variance
\sigma_{\nu,l}^{2}
independent of
X_{l}
. In other words, we assume some true linear relationship, between data and labels subject to corruption due to noise. We allow for different noise power for each channel.
We will specifically concentrate on the generalization error of this regression problem, which we will define by:

\mathcal{E}_{gen}=\frac{1}{2k}\mathbb{E}\sum_{l=1}^{k}\left\|y_{l,new}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}x_{l,new}^{T}\hat{\theta}\right\|^{2}

(3)

where
x_{l,new}
are drawn from the same distribution as
X_{l}
and
y_{l,new}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}x_{l,new}^{T}\theta^{*}+\nu_{l,new}
, where
\nu_{l,new}
is new noise drawn from the same noise distribution. We discuss and analyze this multi-source regression problem in section
V-A
.

One cannot leverage the usual CGMT to tackle the problem in (
2
) which will be made explicit in the following. By setting
z_{l}:=y_{l}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}X_{l}\theta
and introducing Lagrange multipliers:

\displaystyle\min_{\theta,z_{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\max_{v_{l}}R(\theta)+\frac{1}{nk}\sum_{l=1}^{k}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}v_{l}^{T}X_{l}\theta-v_{l}^{T}y_{l}+v_{l}^{T}z_{l}+\ell_{l}(z_{l})\right]

(4)

Defining
X^{T}=\begin{pmatrix}X_{1}^{T}|X_{2}^{T}|\ldots|X_{k}^{T}\end{pmatrix}
and

v^{T}=\begin{pmatrix}v_{1}^{T}&v_{2}^{T}&\ldots v_{k}^{T}\end{pmatrix}
allows us to rewrite (
4
) as

\displaystyle\min_{\theta,z_{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\max_{v}R(\theta)+\frac{1}{nk\sqrt{d}}v^{T}X\theta+\frac{1}{nk}v^{T}z-\frac{1}{nk}v^{T}y+\frac{1}{nk}\ell(z),

(5)

where
z^{T}=\begin{pmatrix}z_{1}^{T}\ldots z_{k}^{T}\end{pmatrix}
,
y^{T}=\begin{pmatrix}y_{1}^{T}\ldots y_{k}^{T}\end{pmatrix}
,

and
\ell(z)=\sum_{l=1}^{k}\ell_{l}(z_{l})
. Now note that we cannot apply the standard CGMT to either of (
4
) or (
5
) since in (
4
) the vector
\theta
couples the optimizations over
v_{l},z_{l}
, and in (
5
), as discussed, the rows of the Gaussian matrix
X
are not identically distributed. We therefore need a new CGMT that can apply to the Gaussian process,
v^{T}X\theta=\sum_{l=1}^{k}v_{l}G_{l}\Sigma_{l}^{1/2}\theta
, which is the main contribution of the current paper.

III-D

Binary classification for Gaussian Mixture Models

We next consider the binary linear classification problem on data generated from a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) with arbitrary covariance. To be precise, the data
\mathbf{x}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is generated as:

\mathbf{x}_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu_{k},\Sigma_{k})\quad\text{where}\quad k\sim\textit{Unif}\{1,2\},

\mu_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
are the mean vectors and
\Sigma_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
are the corresponding covariance matrices. For simplicity, we assume that we uniformly sample from both the mixtures but the analysis can be extended to arbitrary sampling priors. We set the labels
y_{i}=1
if data is generated from the cluster with mean
\mu_{1}
and
y_{i}=-1
if data is generated from the cluster with mean
\mu_{2}
. We then formulate the learning objective in terms of the following regression problem.

\min_{w}\mathcal{L}((A+M)w-z)+\lambda f(w)

(6)

where
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
controls the degree of regularization,
M^{T}=(\underbrace{\begin{matrix}\mu_{1}&...&\mu_{1}\end{matrix}}_{\frac{n}{2}}\underbrace{\begin{matrix}\mu_{2}&...&\mu_{2}\end{matrix}}_{\frac{n}{2}})
encodes the means corresponding to each class,
z^{T}=(\underbrace{\begin{matrix}1&...&1\end{matrix}}_{\frac{n}{2}}\underbrace{\begin{matrix}-1&...&-1\end{matrix}}_{\frac{n}{2}})

corresponds to the labels and

A=\begin{pmatrix}G_{1}&0\\
0&G_{2}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\Sigma_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\Sigma_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\end{pmatrix}

where
G_{1},G_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{\frac{n}{2}\times d}
are Gaussian matrices with iid
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
entries. Therefore, the rows of
A+M
denote the data generated by the GMM and without loss of generality we assume that the data is ordered and split into two equal halves from the two mixtures. Additionally, we assume both loss function
\mathcal{L}(\cdot)
and regularization function
f(\cdot)
to be separable convex functions. Given a linear
\hat{w}
, the classification rule at inference is
\hat{y}(x)=\text{sign}(\hat{w}^{T}x)
. The classification error, or the error on unseen data, generated for the above GMM is given as:

E(\hat{w})=\frac{1}{2}Q\left(\frac{\mu_{1}^{T}\hat{w}}{\sqrt{\hat{w}^{T}\Sigma_{1}\hat{w}}}\right)+\frac{1}{2}Q\left(-\frac{\mu_{2}^{T}\hat{w}}{\sqrt{\hat{w}^{T}\Sigma_{2}\hat{w}}}\right)

(7)

where
Q(\cdot)
is the standard Gaussian CCDF.

Finally, to make the results as explicit as possible we assume that
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
are i.i.d.
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
and their matching entries are correlated according to
\mathbb{E}\mu_{1i}\mu_{2i}=r
for some
-1\leq r\leq 1
. Under this assumption we have
\|\mu_{1}\|\approx\|\mu_{2}\|\approx\sqrt{d}
and
\mu_{1}^{T}\mu_{2}\approx rd
.
*
*

*

Theorem
6
further below is true for arbitrary
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
and one can still use it to derive versions of Theorem
7
and Corollary
2
for any other given model for
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
but we prefer to follow with this specific assumption because it is meant to capture the simplest case of two ”typical”
d
-dimensional vectors that are not necessarily orthogonal.

To illustrate why one cannot just readily apply the standard CGMT in this setting, we have to spell out (
6
) further. The standard way of dealing with (
6
) is introducing

u:=(A+M)w-z
and inserting a Lagrange multiplier, after which we would have:

\min_{w,u}\max_{v}v^{T}Aw+v^{T}Mw-v^{T}z-v^{T}u+\mathcal{L}(u)+\lambda f(w)

The standard CGMT cannot be applied here because
A
is not i.i.d. To try resolving it, one can denote
v^{T}=(v_{1}^{T},v_{2}^{T})
for
v_{1},v_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{\frac{n}{2}}
and obtain

\displaystyle\min_{w,u}\max_{v_{1},v_{2}}v_{1}^{T}G_{1}\Sigma_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}w+v_{2}^{T}G_{2}\Sigma_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}w+v^{T}Mw-v^{T}z-v^{T}u+\mathcal{L}(u)+\lambda f(w)

(8)

Note first of all that the objective above does not separate into a CGMT instance with
G_{1}
and a CGMT instance with
G_{2}
because
w
"sees" both of these matrices. If
\Sigma_{1}=\Sigma_{2}
, we can define
\tilde{w}=\Sigma_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}w
,
(M\Sigma_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}})^{T}=\left(\tilde{M}_{1}|\tilde{M}_{2}\right)^{T},\tilde{M}_{1},\tilde{M}_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times\frac{n}{2}}
,
\tilde{f}(x)=f(\Sigma_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}x)
and arrive at:

\min_{\tilde{w},u}\max_{v}v^{T}(G_{1}^{T}|G_{2}^{T})^{T}\tilde{w}+v^{T}\tilde{M}^{T}\tilde{w}-v^{T}z-v^{T}u+\mathcal{L}(u)+\lambda\tilde{f}(\tilde{w})

Hence, when
\Sigma_{1}=\Sigma_{2}
, since
(G_{1}^{T}|G_{2}^{T})
is i.i.d. Gaussian we
can
analyze (
8
) using the standard CGMT. However, for the case of general
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
the same argument
does not apply
and it appears that one just has to develop a framework allowing for terms of the form
v_{1}^{T}G_{1}\Sigma_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}w+v_{2}^{T}G_{2}\Sigma_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}w
to be present in the objective. This is essentially what Theorem
4
addresses.

Main results

We will occasionally shorten
\|\cdot\|_{2}
to
\|\cdot\|

Lemma 1
.

Let
G_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times d},\dots,G_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{k}\times d}
,
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
,
g_{1},\dots,g_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\mathcal{N}(0,1)

\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}

\beta_{1}:\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}},\dots,\beta_{k}:\mathbb{R}^{n_{k}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n_{k}}
be arbitrary functions,
I,J_{1},\dots,J_{k}
be finite sets and
\{w_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\{u_{j_{1}}\}_{j_{1}\in J_{1}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}},\dots,\{u_{j_{k}}\}_{j_{k}\in J_{k}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{k}}
be arbitrary discrete subsets. Then the following two Gaussian Processes defined on
I\times J
, where
J=J_{1}\times\dots\times J_{k}
and
j\in J
denotes
(j_{1},\dots,j_{k})\in J_{1}\times\dots\times J_{k}
, satisfy the assumptions of Theorem

Consider arbitrary convex functions

\displaystyle\Phi(G):=

Theorem 4

(Generalized CGMT)
.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{w}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{S}_{v_{1}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}},\dots,\mathcal{S}_{v_{k}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{k}}
be compact convex sets. Denote
\mathcal{S}_{v}:=\mathcal{S}_{v_{1}}\times\dots\times\mathcal{S}_{v_{k}}
, let

stand for
(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})\in\mathcal{S}_{v_{1}}\times\dots\times\mathcal{S}_{v_{k}}
and
\psi(w,v):\mathcal{S}_{w}\times\mathcal{S}_{v}\to\mathbb{R}
be continuous,
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\beta_{1}:\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}},\dots,\beta_{k}:\mathbb{R}^{n_{k}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n_{k}}
be also arbitrary continuous functions. Furthermore, let
G_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times d},\dots,G_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{k}\times d}
,
g_{1},\dots,g_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
h_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}},\dots,h_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{k}}
all have i.i.d
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
components and
G=(G_{1},\dots,G_{k})
,
g=\left(g_{1},\dots,g_{k}\right)
,
h=\left(h_{1},\dots,h_{k}\right)
be the corresponding
k
-tuples. Define
\Phi(G)
and
\phi(g,h)
as in (
9
). Then the following statements hold:

1.

For any

we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\Phi(G,\gamma)<t)\leq 2^{k}\mathbb{P}(\phi(g,h)<t)

2.

Assume, moreover, that
\psi
is convex-concave and
\alpha_{\ell}
,
\beta_{\ell}
are affine functions of their inputs, that is they take the form of
\alpha_{\ell}(w)=A_{\ell}w+a_{\ell},\beta_{\ell}(u)=B_{\ell}u+b_{\ell}
for some

. Then for all

such that

\mathbb{P}(|\Phi(G)-c|>\eta)\leq 2^{k}\mathbb{P}(|\phi(g,h)-c|>\eta)

3.

Assume the same setting as in (2). Let
\mathcal{S}
be an arbitrary open subset of
\mathcal{S}_{w}
and
\mathcal{S}^{c}=\mathcal{S}_{w}\setminus\mathcal{S}
. Define:

\displaystyle\Phi_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}(G):=\min_{w\in\mathcal{S}^{c}}\max_{v\in\mathcal{S}_{v}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\beta_{\ell}(v_{\ell})^{T}G_{\ell}\alpha_{\ell}(w)+\psi(w,v)

\displaystyle\phi_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}(g,h):=\min_{w\in\mathcal{S}^{c}}\max_{v\in\mathcal{S}_{v}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\left[\|\beta_{\ell}(v_{\ell})\|_{2}g_{\ell}^{T}\alpha_{\ell}(w)+\|\alpha_{\ell}(w)\|_{2}h_{\ell}^{T}v_{\ell}\right]+\psi(w,v)

\displaystyle w_{\Phi}(G):=\argmin_{w\in\mathcal{S}_{w}}\max_{v\in\mathcal{S}_{v}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\beta_{\ell}(v_{\ell})^{T}G_{\ell}\alpha_{\ell}(w)+\psi(w,v)

Assume also that there exist
\epsilon,\delta>0
,
\bar{\phi},\bar{\phi}_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}
such that

•

\bar{\phi}_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}\geq\bar{\phi}+3\delta

•

\phi(g,h)<\bar{\phi}+\delta
with probability at least

•

\mathbb{P}(w_{\Phi}(G)\in\mathcal{S})\geq 1-2^{k+1}\epsilon

Part 2 of Theorem

says that if

concentrates to a value

, then so does

, albeit with a possibly slightly worse tail bound. Moreover, it turns out that

implies that

Remark 1
.

We would also like to point out Theorem 3 from
Dhifallah and Lu, 2021
that considers similar objectives, namely ones of the following form, where the entries of
G_{\ell}
are again i.i.d.
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
:

\Phi(G):=\min_{w\in\mathcal{S}_{w}}\max_{v\in\mathcal{S}_{v}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}v_{\ell}^{T}G_{\ell}w_{\ell}+\psi(w,v)

(10)

After establishing the corresponding comparison inequality,
Dhifallah and Lu, 2021
proceed to apply it to study the effects of noise injection on the generalization performance of Random Feature Models.
Our work is concerned with characterizing the generalization performance for binary classification with arbitrary covariance matrices and multi-source Gaussian regression. These problems lead to analyzing objectives of the following form:

\Phi(G)=\min_{w\in\mathcal{S}_{w}}\max_{v\in\mathcal{S}_{v}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}v_{\ell}^{T}G_{\ell}\Sigma_{\ell}^{\frac{1}{2}}w+\psi(w,v)

(11)

Note that (
11
) differs from (
10
) in two substantial ways. First, (
11
) explicitly contains the covariance matrices, which is clearly crucial for the applications we mentioned. Second, note that in (
11
) the same
w
is shared between each term, while in (
10
) each
w_{\ell}
is taken to be different. If, however, one were to still take each
w_{\ell}
to be the same in (
10
), analyzing the resulting problem would reduce to the standard CGMT with a larger Gaussian matrix
G=[G_{1},\dots,G_{k}]^{T}
.

Remark 2
.

The first arxiv version of the present manuscript considered only the less general case of
A_{\ell}=\Sigma_{\ell}^{\frac{1}{2}}
being an arbitrary PSD matrix,
B_{\ell}=I_{n_{k}}
,
a_{\ell}=0
and
b_{\ell}=0
as this was enough to address the applications mentioned in Subsections
III-C
and
III-D
. Concurrent to the posting of this paper on arxiv, the authors noticed the posting of
Jain et al., 2024
which considers a different pair of processes to which CGMT can be applied. Inspired by combining this work and theirs, we note that our main Theorem
4
can be generalized even further.
In terms of the terminology above, the processes designed in

correspond to
A_{\ell}=I_{d}

and arbitrary

V-A

Multi-source regression

We recall the problem given in equation (
2
). Also recall the definition of the Moreau envelope, for a function
f
and matrix
T
:

\mathcal{M}_{T\ f(\cdot)}(x)=\min_{z}f(z)+\frac{1}{2}(x-z)^{T}T^{-1}(x-z),

(12)

where
T^{-1}
is a positive definite matrix. We also note that the optimal point of the optimization in (
12
) is the proximal operator, denoted
\mathrm{prox}_{T\ f(\cdot)}(x)
.
The application of the generalized CGMT to the multi-source regression problem gives us the following theorem:

Theorem 5

(Multi-source Generalization Error)
.

Consider the following scalarized optimization problem:

\displaystyle\min_{\xi,q\succeq 0}\max_{\beta,r\succeq 0}\sum_{l=1}^{k}\frac{\beta_{l}q_{l}}{2k}-\frac{\xi_{l}r_{l}}{2k}+\frac{1}{nk}\mathcal{M}_{\frac{q_{l}}{\beta_{l}}I\ \ell_{l}}\left(\nu_{l}-\xi_{l}h_{l}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{k^{2}}\frac{\beta_{l}^{2}}{n}\text{Tr}\Sigma_{l}\left(\frac{1}{k}\sum_{l^{\prime}=1}^{k}\frac{r_{l^{\prime}}}{\xi_{l^{\prime}}}\Sigma_{l^{\prime}}\right)^{-1}+\frac{1}{d}\mathcal{M}_{A^{-1}\ R}\left(\theta^{*}-A^{-1}b\right)

(13)

Here
\xi,q,\beta,r\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
are element-wise non-negative, and
h_{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
l=1,\ldots,k
are i.i.d standard Gaussian vectors independent of each other. Furthermore,
A
and
b
are defined as follows:

\displaystyle A(r,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}=\left(\frac{1}{k}\sum_{l=1}^{k}\frac{r_{l}}{\xi_{k}}\Sigma_{l}\right)

\displaystyle b(\beta)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}=\left(\frac{1}{k}\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}\sum_{l=1}^{k}\beta_{l}\Sigma_{l}^{1/2}g_{l}\right).

We denote by
\hat{r}_{i},\hat{\xi}_{i},\hat{\beta}_{i},\hat{q}_{i}
the optimal values of the scalarized optimization problem, and define
\hat{A}=A(\hat{r},\hat{\xi})
and
\hat{b}=b(\hat{\beta})
.
Then the generalization error (as defined in equation
3
) for the multi-source Gaussian Regression problem is given by:

\mathcal{E}_{gen}=\frac{1}{2k}\mathbb{E}\sum_{l=1}^{k}\left\|y_{l,new}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}x_{l,new}^{T}\mathrm{prox}_{\hat{A}^{-1}\ R}(\theta^{*}-\hat{A}^{-1}\hat{b})\right\|

VII
Conclusion and future work

We presented a new pair of Gaussian processes that satisfy Gordon’s comparison conditions and thereby developed a generalization of the Convex Gaussian Min-Max Theorem. We further demonstrated its use in two examples, namely multi-source regression and binary classification for Gaussian data with arbitrary covariance matrices. Future work would be to see if further pairs of comparable processes can be found. Of particular interest are those where
G_{\ell}
and
G_{\ell^{\prime}}
do not have to be independent for
\ell\neq\ell^{\prime}
.

\vdots&&\ddots&\vdots\\

\Sigma_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\\

\Sigma_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}\end{pmatrix}w_{i}+\sum^{k}_{\ell=1}\gamma_{\ell}\|v_{j_{\ell}}\|\|\Sigma_{\ell}^{\frac{1}{2}}w_{i}\|~~~\mbox{and}~~~X_{ij}=\sum^{k}_{\ell=1}\|v_{j_{\ell}}\|w_{i}^{T}\Sigma^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\ell}g_{\ell}+{v_{j_{\ell}}}^{T}h_{\ell}\|\Sigma^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\ell}w_{i}\|

\mathbb{E}[Y_{ij}Y_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}-X_{ij}X_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}]=\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}({v_{j_{\ell}}}^{T}v_{j^{\prime}_{\ell}}-\|v_{j_{\ell}}\|\|v_{j^{\prime}_{\ell}}\|)({w_{i}}^{T}\Sigma_{\ell}w_{i^{\prime}}-\|\Sigma_{\ell}^{\frac{1}{2}}w_{i}\|\|\Sigma_{\ell}^{\frac{1}{2}}w_{i^{\prime}}\|)\geq 0

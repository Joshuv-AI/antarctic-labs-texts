Pointwise convergence of Fourier series and deep neural network for the indicator function of d-dimensional ball
Ryota Kawasumi, Tsuyoshi Yoneda

Abstract
. In this paper, we clarify the crucial difference between a deep neural network and the Fourier series. For the multiple Fourier series of periodization of some radial functions on ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} , Kuratsubo (2010) investigated the behavior of the spherical partial sum and discovered the third phenomenon other than the well-known Gibbs-Wilbraham and Pinsky phenomena. In particular, the third one exhibits prevention of pointwise convergence. In contrast to it, we give a specific deep neural network and prove pointwise convergence.

Primary 41A29; Secondary 68T07

1.
Introduction

In this paper we
clarify the crucial difference between
a deep neural network and the Fourier series.
Let
d\gg 1
be a dimension and fix it.
For
x\in[-1,1)^{d}
, let
f^{\circ}
be the indicator function of the
d
dimensional ball such that

f^{\circ}(x)=\begin{cases}1,\quad|x|\leq 1/2,\\
0,\quad|x|>1/2.\end{cases}

Let
\rho:\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
be a monotone increasing function with
\rho(N)\to\infty
(
N\to\infty
).
Let
f_{N}:\mathbb{R}^{\rho(N)}\times[-1,1)^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a prescribed piecewise smooth real-valued function with parameter
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
,
for example, the sum of
\rho(N)\sim N^{d}
terms (i.e. Fourier series) or composite functions
\rho(N)\sim\log N
times (i.e. deep neural networks).
Also, let
\{W^{t}\}_{t=0}^{\infty}
be sets of parameters, and let
W^{t}:=\{w^{t}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\rho(N)}\in\mathbb{R}^{\rho(N)}
be generated by the following gradient descent:

E(W^{t}):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{[-1,1)^{d}}|f_{N}(W^{t},x)-f^{\circ}(x)|^{2}dx,

(1)

w^{t+1}_{j}=w^{t}_{j}-\frac{\partial}{\partial w^{t}_{j}}E(W^{t}).

Let
\{f^{\circ}_{N}\}_{N=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of functions
such that (if there exists)

f_{N}^{\circ}(x):=\lim_{t\to\infty}f_{N}(W^{t},x)\quad\text{for}\quad x\in[-1,1)^{d}.

Throughout this paper, we consider the following question:

Question 1
.

For given
f_{N}
,
can we find a sequences of initial parameters
W^{t=0}_{N}\in\mathbb{R}^{\rho(N)}
(
N=1,2,\cdots
)
assuring the following pointwise convergence?

\lim_{N\to\infty}f^{\circ}_{N}(x)=f^{\circ}(x)\quad\text{for all}\quad x\in[-1,1)^{d}.

Let us briefly review recent studies related to this question
(for classical universal approximation theorems, see
[
4
,
6
,
8
,
16
,
20
]
for example).
Suzuki
[
21
]
clarified that deep learning can achieve the minimax optimal rate and outperform any nonadaptive linear estimator such as kernel regression,
when the target function is in the supercritical Besov spaces
B^{s}_{p,q}
with
p<2
and
s<d/2
(
d
is the dimension, note that the case
s=d/2
is called “critical”),
which indicates the spatial inhomogeneity of the shape of the target function including the non-smooth functions.
We briefly explain the key idea of
[
21
]
in the following:
To show the approximation error theorems,
he first applied the wavelet expansion to the target functions
and then approximated each wavelet bases (composed of spline functions) by ReLU-DNN (see
[
24
]
).
More specifically, the following assertion holds:

Lemma 2
.

[
24
, Propositon 2]

The function
f(x)=x^{2}
on the segment
[0,1]
can be approximated with any
error
\epsilon>0
by a ReLU network having the depth and the number of weights and computation
units
{\mathcal{O}}(\log 1/\epsilon)
.

We emphasize that the proof of this lemma is based on “’separability of function spaces”.
By this Lemma
2
, for deriving the multi-dimensional polynomials, it suffices to apply the following formula:

xy=\frac{1}{2}((x+y)^{2}-x^{2}-y^{2}),

and then we can easily approximate multi-dimensional spline functions
by ReLU-DNN.
The other idea (for variance) is applying the statistical argument in

[
19
]

combined with a covering number evaluation.
To the contrary, since Question
1
is essentially based on “non-separable function spaces” as
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
we cannot directly apply their argument to ours.

Before going any further, we point out that manipulating the structure of separable
function spaces may not be enough to capture the discontinuity structure of
f^{\circ}
. This means that, to make any further progress, we may need to
directly look into each DNN dynamics.
The flavor of this insight seems very similar to the recent mathematical studies on the incompressible inviscid flows. See
[
1
,
2
,
5
,
7
,
17
]
for example.
More precisely, these have been
directly looking into the dynamics of inviscid fluids in the critical function spaces (to show ill-posedness), and the argument seems quite different from the classical studies focusing on well-posedness in subcritical type of function spaces. See
[
3
,
11
,
12
,
18
,
23
]
for example. To show the well-posedness, the structure of subcritical function spaces, more precisely,
commutator estimates are crucially indispensable.

This paper is organized as follows: In the next section, we explain the Fourier series case.
In Section 3, we investigate the deep neural network case.
In the last section, we give a proof of the main theorem.

2.
The Fourier series case

In this section we consider Question
1
in the Fourier series case.
Let
f_{N}
be a Fourier series with spherical partial sum:

\begin{split}f_{N}(W^{t},x)&:=\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},}}{{|k|<N}}}\left(a^{t}_{k}\cos(k\pi\cdot x)+b^{t}_{k}\sin(k\pi\cdot x)\right),\\
W^{t}&:=\{a_{k}^{t}\}_{|k|<N}\cup\{b^{t}_{k}\}_{|k|<N}\subset\mathbb{R}.\end{split}

By Parseval’s identity, we immediately have the following convergences:
For any
W^{t=0}\in\mathbb{R}^{\rho(N)}
, we have

\lim_{t\to\infty}f_{N}(W^{t},x)=f^{\circ}_{N}(x)\quad\text{for}\quad x\in[-1,1)^{d},

where

\begin{split}&f^{\circ}_{N}(x)=\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},}}{{|k|<N}}}\left(a^{\infty}_{k}\cos(k\pi\cdot x)+b^{\infty}_{k}\sin(k\pi\cdot x)\right),\\
&a^{\infty}_{k}=\int_{[-1,1)^{d}}f^{\circ}(x)\cos(k\pi\cdot x)dx\quad\text{and}\quad b^{\infty}_{k}=\int_{[-1,1)^{d}}f^{\circ}(x)\sin(k\pi\cdot x)dx.\end{split}

Then we obtain the following proposition.

Proposition 3
.

Let
d\geq 5
.
Then, for any
x\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}\cap[-1,1)^{d}
,

f^{\circ}_{N}(x)-f^{\circ}(x)\quad\text{diverges as}\quad N\to\infty.

Thus the answer of Question
1
in this case is “negative”.

The proof is just direct consequence of Kuratsubo
[
13
]
(see also
[
14
,
15
]
).
Note that, for the Fourier series of the indicator functions
of such several dimensional balls,
the Gibbs and Pinsky phenomena have already been well-known.
The assertion of this proposition is that Kuratsubo
[
13
]
discoverd the third phenomenon (preventing pointwise convergence), other than the previous two phenomena.
To understand this third phenomenon intuitivelly, see numerical computations in Section 7 in
[
14
]
.

3.
The specific deep neural network case

In this section we investigate the deep neural network case.
Let
N=2^{n}
(
n\in\mathbb{N}
) and
let
h
be the ReLU function such that

h(x):=\max\{x,0\},\quad\text{for}\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}.

We now construct a deep neural network
f_{N}
with a special shape.
For the initial layer, we define

z^{1}:=z^{1}(x):=h(wx+b):=\begin{pmatrix}h(w_{1}\cdot x+b_{1})\\
\vdots\\
h(w_{2^{n}}\cdot x+b_{2^{n}})\end{pmatrix}

for

x\in[-1,1)^{d}
,
w:=\{w_{j}\}_{j=1}^{2^{n}}\in\mathbb{R}^{2^{n}\times d}
,
b:=\{b_{j}\}_{j=1}^{2^{n}}\in\mathbb{R}^{2^{n}}
and
z^{1}:=\{z_{j}^{1}\}_{j=1}^{2^{n}}\in\mathbb{R}^{2^{n}}
.
Moreover we set

(2)

b_{j}:=\frac{|w_{j}|}{2}+1.

For the
2k
-th layer,
we put the following sparsity structure:
for
J=1,2,\cdots,2^{n-k}
and
1\leq k\leq n
,

(3)

\begin{split}z^{2k}_{3J-2}&=h\left(\frac{1}{2}z^{2k-1}_{2J-1}+\frac{1}{2}z_{2J}^{2k-1}\right),\\
z^{2k}_{3J-1}&=h\left(\frac{1}{2}z^{2k-1}_{2J-1}-\frac{1}{2}z_{2J}^{2k-1}\right),\\
z^{2k}_{3J}&=h\left(-\frac{1}{2}z^{2k-1}_{2J-1}+\frac{1}{2}z_{2J}^{2k-1}\right),\\
\end{split}

where
z^{2k}=\{z_{j}^{2k}\}_{j=1}^{2^{n}}
,
z^{2k}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\cdot 2^{n-k}}
.
For the
2k+1
layer, we put the following linear structure:
for
J=1,2,\cdots 2^{n-k}
,

(4)

z^{2k+1}_{J}=z^{2k}_{3J-2}-z^{2k}_{3J-1}-z^{2k}_{3J},

where
z^{2k+1}\in\mathbb{R}^{2^{n-k}}
.
The key idea of designing such deep neural network is that, combined (
3
) and (
4
) induce cancellations of certain
w_{j}
, and then
these cancellations suppress the generation of concavity. Consequently, the convexity remains.

We see that, in the
2n+1
layer,

z^{2n+1}=z^{2n+1}_{1}
becomes a real number.
Then we set
f_{N}
as the following:

\begin{split}f_{N}&:=\min\{z^{2n+1}_{1},1\}.\end{split}

For the initial parameters
W^{t=0}:=\{w^{t=0}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
, we assume the following
largeness condition and
uniform distribution condition:

(5)

|w^{t=0}_{j}|>\frac{1}{\delta}\quad(\text{we clarify this $\delta$ later}).\\

For unit vectors:
\tau_{j}:=\frac{w^{t}_{j}}{|w^{t}_{j}|}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
(
j=1,2,\cdots
),
let us define half spaces
H^{\circ}(\tau_{j})
and the corresponding boundaries
as follows:

\begin{split}H^{\circ}_{j}&:=\left\{\sigma\in[-1,1)^{d}:\sigma\cdot\tau_{j}>-\frac{1}{2}\right\},\\
\partial H^{\circ}_{j}&:=\left\{\sigma\in[-1,1)^{d}:\sigma\cdot\tau_{j}=-\frac{1}{2}\right\}.\\
\end{split}

Note that this
\tau_{j}
is independent of
t
, which will be clarified
in (
20
).
Then we impose the following condition to the initial parameters:

(6)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\bigcap_{j=1}^{N}H^{\circ}_{j}=\Omega,

where
\Omega:=\{x\in[-1,1)^{d}:|x|<1/2\}
.
The main theorem is as follows:

Theorem 4
.

Assume

W^{t=0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}
satisfies
(
5
) and
(
6
).
Then,

f_{N}(W^{t})
converges to
f^{\circ}_{N}
pointwisely (as
t\to\infty
),
and
f^{\circ}_{N}
converges to
f^{\circ}
pointwisely (as
N\to\infty
).
Moreover we have the following convergence rate:

\|f_{N}(W^{t})-f^{\circ}_{N}\|^{r}_{L^{r}}\lesssim t^{-1/3}\quad\text{for}\quad 1\leq r<\infty,

where
a\lesssim b
means
a\leq Cb
for some universal constant
C>0
.
Thus the answer of Question
1
in this case is “positive”.

4.
Proof of the main theorem.

Let
x\in[-1,1)^{d}
. We set
f_{N}^{\circ}(x)
as follows:

f^{\circ}_{N}(x):=\begin{cases}1,\quad x\in\bigcap_{j=1}^{N}H^{\circ}_{j},\\
0,\quad\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Remark 1
.

By (
6
), we immediately have

(7)

f_{N}^{\circ}(x)\to f^{\circ}(x)\quad(N\to\infty)\quad\text{for any}\quad x\in[-1,1)^{d}.

Now we calculate the deep neural network.
First we see

\partial_{x}z^{1}_{j}(x)=\begin{cases}w_{j},\quad x\in D_{0,j},\\
0,\quad x\not\in D_{0,j},\end{cases}

where
D_{0,j}:=\{x:z^{1}_{j}(x)>0\}
.
Next we calculate a pair of
2k-1
,
2k
and
2k+1
layers.
Taking a derivative, we have

\begin{split}\partial_{z_{2J-1}^{2k-1}}z^{2k+1}_{J}=&\frac{1}{2}\partial h\left(\frac{1}{2}z^{2k-1}_{2J-1}+\frac{1}{2}z^{2k-1}_{2J}\right)\\
&-\frac{1}{2}\partial h\left(\frac{1}{2}z^{2k-1}_{2J-1}-\frac{1}{2}z_{2J}^{2k-1}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\partial h\left(-\frac{1}{2}z^{2k-1}_{2J-1}+\frac{1}{2}z^{2k-1}_{2J}\right),\end{split}

where

\partial h(x)=\begin{cases}1\quad x\geq 0,\\
0\quad x<0.\end{cases}

Due to the cancellation of Heaviside functions in the following domain,

(8)

D^{0}_{k,J}:=\left\{x:\frac{1}{2}z^{2k-1}_{2J}(x)>\frac{1}{2}z^{2k-1}_{2J-1}(x)\right\},

we have

(9)

\partial_{z^{2k-1}_{2J-1}}z_{J}^{2k+1}(x)=1\quad\text{for}\quad x\in D^{0}_{k,J}.

Note that, rigorously saying,
z^{2k-1}:=z^{2k-1}\circ z^{2k}\circ\cdots\circ z^{1}
.
To the contrary, there is no cancellation of Heaviside functions in the following domain:

(10)

D^{1}_{k,J}:=\left\{x:\frac{1}{2}z^{2k-1}_{2J}(x)<\frac{1}{2}z_{2J-1}^{2k-1}(x)\right\}.

In other words,

(11)

\partial_{z^{2k-1}_{2J-1}}z^{2k+1}_{J}(x)=0\quad\text{for}\quad x\in D^{1}_{k,J}.

The same argument goes through also in the case
\partial_{z^{2k-1}_{2J}}z_{J}^{2k+1}
.
In this case,
we have

(12)

\displaystyle\partial_{z^{2k-1}_{2J}}z^{2k+1}_{J}(x)

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{for}\quad x\in D^{0}_{k,J},

(13)

\displaystyle\partial_{z^{2k-1}_{2J}}z^{2k+1}_{J}(x)

\displaystyle=1\quad\text{for}\quad x\in D^{1}_{k,J}.

We apply these properties inductively in the reverse direction (as the back propergation), and
we divide the non-zero region
\{x:f_{N}(W^{t},x)>0\}
into several parts appropriately.
To do that,
we suitably rewrite
the natural number
j\in\{1,2,\cdots,2^{n}\}

as follows:

j=1+\sum_{k=1}^{n}2^{k-1}\delta_{n}^{k}(j)

where
\delta_{n}^{k}(j)\in\{0,1\}
.
Let

D_{j}:=D_{0,j}\cap\left(\bigcap_{k=1}^{n}D_{k,J^{k}_{n}(j)}^{\delta_{n}^{k}(j)}\right)

for

\begin{cases}J^{k}_{n}(j):=1+\sum_{\ell=k+1}^{n}2^{\ell-k-1}\delta^{\ell}_{n}(j),\quad(k\leq n-1),\\
J^{n}_{n}(j)=1.\end{cases}

For the derivation of this
D_{j}
, see Appendix
A
.
For example, if
k=n-1
, we see

J_{n}^{n-1}(j)=1+\delta^{n}_{n}(j),

if
k=n-2
, we see

J_{n}^{n-2}(j)=1+\sum_{\ell=n-1}^{n}2^{\ell-n+1}\delta_{n}^{\ell}(j)=1+\delta_{n}^{n-1}(j)+2\delta_{n}^{n}(j).

Then we can easily deduce the other cases:
k\leq n-3
.
By using this
D_{j}
, the derivative formula becomes much simpler:

(14)

\partial_{x}z^{2n+1}(x)=w_{j}\quad\text{for}\quad x\in D_{j}.

By the fundamental theorem of calculus,
we have

z^{2n+1}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{2^{n}}\left(h(w_{j}\cdot x+b_{j})\chi_{D_{j}}(x)\right).

Therefore we obtain the following explicit formula:

(15)

f_{N}(x)=\min\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left(h(w_{j}\cdot x+b_{j})\chi_{D_{j}}(x)\right),1\right\}.

In order to calculate the error function
E(W^{t})
, we need to
figure out the rigion
D_{j}\cap\{x:0<f_{N}(x)<1\}
more precisely.

Proposition 5
.

We have

(16)

D_{j}\cap\{x:0<f_{N}(x)<1\}=\bigcup_{r\in\left(-\frac{1}{|w^{t}_{j}|},0\right)}\mathcal{D}_{j}(|w^{t}_{j}|,r),

where

\begin{split}\mathcal{D}_{j}(|w^{t}_{j}|,r)&:=\partial L_{j}(r)\setminus\bigcup_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle j^{\prime}=1,2,\cdots,N,}}{{j^{\prime}\not=j}}}L_{j^{\prime}}\left(\frac{|w^{t}_{j}|r}{|w^{t}_{j^{\prime}}|}\right),\\
L_{j}(r)&:=\left\{r^{\prime}\tau_{j}+x:\ r^{\prime}\in(-\infty,r]\quad\text{and}\quad x\in\partial H^{\circ}_{j}\right\},\\
\partial L_{j}(r)&:=\left\{r\tau_{j}+x:\ x\in\partial H^{\circ}_{j}\right\}.\end{split}

Moreover, for any sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
there exists a
\delta>0
such that if

r<\delta
and
|w^{t}_{j}|^{-1}<\delta
, then

(17)

\lambda_{d-1}(\mathcal{D}_{j}(|w^{t}_{j}|,r))>\gamma-\epsilon\quad\text{with}\quad\gamma:=\min_{j=1,2,\cdots,N}\lambda_{d-1}(\mathcal{D}_{j}(|w^{t}_{j}|,0)),

where
\lambda_{d}(\cdot)
is the
d
dimensional Lebesgue measure.
Note that
\mathcal{D}_{j}(|w^{t}_{j}|,0)
is independent of
|w^{t}_{j}|
.

Proof.

We just
replace
z^{1}_{j}
to
\partial L_{j}(r)
and apply the induction argument again (in the forward direction).
Here we only consider a pair of
4J
,
4J-1
,
4J-2
and
4J-3
.
First we see that

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\bigcup_{r\in\left(-\frac{1}{|w^{t}_{4J}|},0\right)}\partial L_{4J}(r)=\tilde{D}_{0,4J},\\
\displaystyle\bigcup_{r\in\left(-\frac{1}{|w^{t}_{4J}|},0\right)}\partial L_{4J-1}\left(\frac{|w^{t}_{4J}|r}{|w^{t}_{4J-1}|}\right)=\tilde{D}_{0,4J-1},\\
\displaystyle\bigcup_{r\in\left(-\frac{1}{|w^{t}_{4J}|},0\right)}\partial L_{4J-2}\left(\frac{|w^{t}_{4J}|r}{|w^{t}_{4J-2}|}\right)=\tilde{D}_{0,4J-2},\\
\displaystyle\bigcup_{r\in\left(-\frac{1}{|w^{t}_{4J}|},0\right)}\partial L_{4J-3}\left(\frac{|w^{t}_{4J}|r}{|w^{t}_{4J-3}|}\right)=\tilde{D}_{0,4J-3},\end{cases}

where
\tilde{D}_{0,4J-K}=D_{0,4J-K}\cap\{x:0<z^{1}_{4J-K}(x)<1\}
.
The corresponding functions are
z^{1}_{4J}
,
z^{1}_{4J-1}
,
z^{1}_{4J-2}
and
z^{1}_{4J-3}
respectively.
In fact, for
\sigma\in\partial H^{\circ}_{4J-K}
(
K=0,1,2,3,
), we see

\begin{split}z^{1}_{4J-K}\left(\frac{|w^{t}_{4J}|}{|w^{t}_{4J-K}|}r\tau_{4J-K}+\sigma\right)&=h\left(w^{t}_{4J-K}\cdot\left(\frac{|w^{t}_{4J}|}{|w^{t}_{4J-K}|}r\tau_{4J-K}+\sigma\right)+b_{4J-K}\right)\\
&=h\left(|w^{t}_{4J}|r-\frac{|w^{t}_{4J-K}|}{2}+b_{4J-K}\right)\\
&=h(|w^{t}_{4J}|r+1).\end{split}

Note that
\tau_{j}\cdot\tau_{j}=1
and

w^{t}_{j}\cdot\sigma=-|w^{t}_{j}|/2
for
\sigma\in\partial H^{\circ}_{j}
.
Thus we have

z^{1}_{4J-K}\left(\frac{|w^{t}_{4J}|}{|w^{t}_{4J-K}|}r\tau_{4J-K}+\sigma\right)\in(0,1)\quad\text{if and only if}\quad r\in\left(-\frac{1}{|w^{t}_{4J}|},0\right).

By (
8
) and (
10
), we see

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\bigcup_{r\in\left(-\frac{1}{|w^{t}_{4J}|},0\right)}\left(\partial L_{4J}(r)\setminus L_{4J-1}\left(\frac{|w^{t}_{4J}|r}{|w^{t}_{4J-1}|}\right)\right)=\tilde{D}^{1}_{1,2J},\\
\displaystyle\bigcup_{r\in\left(-\frac{1}{|w^{t}_{4J}|},0\right)}\left(\partial L_{4J-1}\left(\frac{|w^{t}_{4J}|r}{|w^{t}_{4J-1}|}\right)\setminus L_{4J}(r)\right)=\tilde{D}^{0}_{1,2J},\\
\end{cases}

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\bigcup_{r\in\left(-\frac{1}{|w^{t}_{4J}|},0\right)}\left(\partial L_{4J-2}\left(\frac{|w^{t}_{4J}|r}{|w^{t}_{4J-2}|}\right)\setminus L_{4J-3}\left(\frac{|w^{t}_{4J}|r}{|w^{t}_{4J-3}|}\right)\right)=\tilde{D}^{1}_{1,2J-1},\\
\displaystyle\bigcup_{r\in\left(-\frac{1}{|w^{t}_{4J}|},0\right)}\left(\partial L_{4J-3}\left(\frac{|w^{t}_{4J}|r}{|w^{t}_{4J-3}|}\right)\setminus L_{4J-2}\left(\frac{|w^{t}_{4J}|r}{|w^{t}_{4J-2}|}\right)\right)=\tilde{D}^{0}_{1,2J-1},\end{cases}

where
\tilde{D}^{0\text{ or }1}_{2,2J-K}=D^{0\text{ or }1}_{1,2J-K}\cap\{x:0<z^{3}_{2J-K}(x)<1\}
.
The corresponding functions are unified as
z^{3}_{2J}
for the first two cases,

z^{3}_{2J-1}
for the second two cases.
In fact,
for
r\in(-1/|w^{t}_{4J}|,0)
, we see

\begin{split}&x\in\partial L_{4J}(r)\setminus L_{4J-1}\left(\frac{|w^{t}_{4J}|}{|w^{t}_{4J-1}|}r\right)\\
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle def}}{{\Longleftrightarrow}}\ &x\in\partial L_{4J}(r)\quad\text{and}\quad x\in\left(L_{4J-1}\left(\frac{|w^{t}_{4J}|}{|w^{t}_{4J-1}|}r\right)\right)^{c}\\
&(\text{note that}\ \left(L_{j}(r)\right)^{c}=\left\{r^{\prime}\tau_{j}+x:r^{\prime}\in(r,\infty)\quad\text{and}\quad x\in\partial H^{\circ}_{j}\right\}),\\
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle def}}{{\Longleftrightarrow}}\ &\begin{cases}x=r\tau_{4J}+\sigma\quad\text{for}\quad\sigma\in\partial H^{\circ}_{4J}\\
x=r^{\prime}\tau_{4J-1}+\sigma^{\prime}\quad\text{for}\quad r^{\prime}>(|w^{t}_{4J}|/|w^{t}_{4J-1}|)r\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma^{\prime}\in\partial H^{\circ}_{4J-1},\end{cases}\end{split}

and thus we have

\begin{split}z^{1}_{4J}(x)=h(w_{4J}x+b_{4J})&=h(|w^{t}_{4J}|r+1)\\
&<h(|w^{t}_{4J-1}|r^{\prime}+1)=h(w_{4J-1}x+b_{4J-1})=z^{1}_{4J-1}(x).\end{split}

Hence,
x\in\tilde{D}^{1}_{2,2J-1}
.
By using this inequality (and the opposite version), (
3
) and (
4
), we have
z^{3}_{2J}(x)\in(0,1)
if and only if

x\in\bigcup_{r\in\left(-\frac{1}{|w^{t}_{4J}|},0\right)}\left(\partial L_{4J}(r)\setminus L_{4J-1}\left(\frac{|w^{t}_{4J}|}{|w^{t}_{4J-1}|}r\right)\right)=\tilde{D}^{1}_{1,2J}

or

x\in\bigcup_{r\in\left(-\frac{1}{|w^{t}_{4J}|},0\right)}\left(\partial L_{4J-1}\left(\frac{|w^{t}_{4J}|}{|w^{t}_{4J-1}|}r\right)\setminus L_{4J}(r)\right)=\tilde{D}^{0}_{1,2J}.

The other cases:
\tilde{D}^{1}_{1,2J-1}
and
\tilde{D}^{0}_{1,2J-1}

Corollary 6
.

We immediately have

and
\gamma^{*}>0
is a-priori constant (independent of

) such that

\gamma^{*}:=\sup_{j=1,2,\cdots,N}\sup_{0<r,R<\delta}\left|\frac{d}{dR}\lambda_{d-1}\left(\mathcal{D}^{*}_{j}(R,r)\right)\right|.\\

By (
2
), we have that

(19)

\begin{split}w_{j}^{t}\cdot x+b^{t}_{j}=|w_{j}^{t}|s-\frac{|w_{j}^{t}|}{2}+b_{j}^{t}=|w_{j}^{t}|s+1\end{split}

for

(
\sigma\in\partial H^{\circ}_{j}
) and
t=0
.
Then applying Proposition
5
, we can explicitly write down the error function
E(W^{t})
as follows (for
t=0
):

\begin{split}E(W^{t})&=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2^{n}}E_{j}(W^{t})+\left|\bigcap_{j=1}^{N}H^{\circ}_{j}\setminus\Omega\right|\\
&:=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2^{n}}\int_{-\frac{1}{|w^{t}_{j}|}}^{0}\left(|w^{t}_{j}|s+1\right)^{2}\lambda_{d-1}(\mathcal{D}_{j}(|w^{t}_{j}|,s))ds+\left|\bigcap_{j=1}^{N}H^{\circ}_{j}\setminus\Omega\right|.\end{split}

Recall that
\Omega=\{x\in[-1,1)^{d}:|x|<1/2\}
.
We emphasize that
E_{j}(W^{t})
does not have any rotational component
\frac{w^{t}_{j}}{|w^{t}_{j}|}

j,j^{\prime}\in\{1,2,\cdots,N\}

\nabla_{w_{j}}E_{j^{\prime}}(W^{t})=\frac{w_{j}}{|w_{j}|}\cdot\partial_{|w_{j}|}E_{j^{\prime}}(W^{t}).

6
, we have

\begin{split}\partial_{|w^{t}_{j}|}E_{j}(W^{t})&=-(|w^{t}_{j}|s+1)^{2}\lambda_{d-1}(\mathcal{D}_{j}(|w^{t}_{j}|,s))\bigg|_{s=-\frac{1}{|w^{t}_{j}|}}\times\frac{1}{|w^{t}_{j}|^{2}}\\
&\quad+\int_{-\frac{1}{|w^{t}_{j}|}}^{0}\partial_{|w^{t}_{j}|}\left(\left(|w^{t}_{j}|s+1\right)^{2}\right)\lambda_{d-1}(\mathcal{D}_{j}(|w^{t}_{j}|,s))ds\\
&\quad+\int_{-\frac{1}{|w^{t}_{j}|}}^{0}\left(|w^{t}_{j}|s+1\right)^{2}\partial_{|w^{t}_{j}|}\lambda_{d-1}(\mathcal{D}_{j}(|w^{t}_{j}|,s))ds\\
&\leq\int_{-\frac{1}{|w^{t}_{j}|}}^{0}2s(|w^{t}_{j}|s+1)\lambda_{d-1}(\mathcal{D}_{j}(|w^{t}_{j}|,s))ds\\
\\
&\quad+\delta\gamma^{*}\left|\int_{-\frac{1}{|w^{t}_{j}|}}^{0}\left(|w^{t}_{j}|s+1\right)^{2}sds\right|\\
&=:(RHS).\\
\end{split}

18
) and change of variable:
r=|w^{t}_{j}|s+1

\begin{split}&\int_{-\frac{1}{|w^{t}_{j}|}}^{0}2s(|w^{t}_{j}|s+1)\lambda_{d-1}(\mathcal{D}_{j}(|w^{t}_{j}|,s))ds\\

Also by the similar calculation, we see

(RHS)\lesssim-\frac{\gamma-\epsilon-\delta\gamma^{*}}{|w^{t}_{j}|^{2}}\lesssim-\frac{1}{|w^{t}_{j}|^{2}}<0.

On the other hand, for
j^{\prime}\not=j

(20)

\begin{split}|w^{t+1}_{j}|=&|w^{t}_{j}|-\frac{\partial}{\partial|w^{t}_{j}|}E_{j}(W^{t})-\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle j^{\prime}\in\{1,2,\cdots,N\},}}{{j^{\prime}\not=j}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial|w_{j}^{t}|}E_{j^{\prime}}(W^{t})\gtrsim|w_{j}^{t}|+\frac{1}{|w^{t}_{j}|^{2}}\\

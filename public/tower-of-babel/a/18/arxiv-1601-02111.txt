2.
Classification of Ricci pinched
\kappa
-solutions

Throughout the paper, we say that
(M,g,f)
is a (gradient) steady Ricci soltion if

R_{ij}=\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}f.

The following lemma shows that each level set
\Sigma_{r}
of
(M,g,f)
is diffeomorphic to a sphere if it admits an equilibrium point
and has positive Ricci curvature.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(M,g,f)
be an
n
-dimensional noncompact steady Ricci soliton with positive Ricci curvature and an equilibrium point
o
. Then any
\Sigma_{r}
is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, where
r>f(o)
.

Proof.

By soliton equation and positivity of Ricci curvature,
f
is strictly convex. Moreover, the equilibrium point is unique. Thus by the Morse Lemma, there exists a coordinates system
(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
in a neighbourhood
U
near
o
such that

f(x)=f(o)+x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+x_{n}^{2}.

It follows that
\Sigma_{f(o)+\epsilon}=\{x\in M|~f(x)=f(o)+\epsilon\}
is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
for any small
\epsilon>0
.
Since
\Sigma_{r}
is evolved along the gradient flow of
-\nabla f
, each
\Sigma_{r}
is diffeomorphic to
\Sigma_{f(o)+\epsilon}
, where
r>f(o)+\epsilon
. The lemma is proved.
∎

This lemma will play an important role in the analysis of the asymptotic behavior of steady Ricci solitons below. In this section, we give a classification of steady Ricci solitons with
\epsilon
-pinched Ricci curvature. Recall

Definition 2.2
.

A Riemannian manifold

is called Ricci curvature
\epsilon
-pinched, if it has nonnegative Ricci curvature and its Ricci curvature satisfies

(2.1)

\displaystyle{\rm Ric}(x)\geq\epsilon R(x)g(x),

\epsilon
-pinched, if it has nonnegative Ricci curvature along the flow and its Ricci curvature satisfies

\displaystyle{\rm Ric}(x,t)\geq\epsilon R(x,t)g(x,t),~\forall~t\in(a,b).

where
\epsilon
is a positive constant independent of
x\in M
and
t\in(a,b)
.

we prove

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
(M,g(t))
be a simply connected and Ricci curvature
\epsilon
-pinched
\kappa
-solution. Then
(M,g(t))=(M_{1},g_{1}(t))\times(M_{2},g_{2}(t))\times\cdots\times(M_{k},g_{k}(t))
, and each
(M_{i},g_{i}(t))
is an Einstein metrics flow on a simply connected and compact symmetric space.
More precisely, each
(M_{i},g_{i}(t))
is one of the following three types:

(i)

(\mathbb{S}^{n_{i}},g(t))
is a Ricci flow with positive constant sectional curvature;

(ii)

(\mathbb{CP}^{n_{i}},g(t))

(iii)

(M_{i},g(t))
is an Einstein metrics flow on an irreducible symmetric space except (i) and (ii).

To prove Theorem

, we need the following classification result.

Lemma 2.4
.

i)

A simply connected Einstein manifold with positive curvature operator must be
\mathbb{S}^{n}
with constant sectional curvature.

ii)

A simply connected shrinking soliton with positive curvature operator must be
\mathbb{S}^{n}
with constant sectional curvature.

iii)

A simply connected Kähler-Einstein manifold with positive bisectional curvature must be
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
with constant bisectional curvature.

iv)

\mathbb{CP}^{n}
with constant bisectional curvature.

Proof.

i) It is clear that the manifold is compact. Let

, where
n\lambda
is scalar curvature of

. Then

satisfies Ricci flow equation,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial g(t)}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=-2{\rm Ric}(g(t)),

By Wilking’s result
[

]
, there exists

such that

(2.4)

\displaystyle r(t)g(t)\rightarrow g_{\mathbb{S}^{n}},~{\rm as}~t\to\frac{1}{2\lambda},

where

is the metric with constant sectional curvature
1

\mathbb{S}^{n}
.
Thus

\mathbb{S}^{n}
.

ii) By Munteanu and Wang’s result
[
18

, any shrinking soliton with nonnegative curvature and positive Ricci curvature must be compact. Then by the same argument as in i), the manifold should be isometric to one with constant sectional curvature on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
.

iii) and iv) are known, since the manifold is compact and so it is biholomorphic to
\mathbb{CP}^{n}

are the same up to a scale, to finish the proof of Theorem
1.3
, we need to derive the curvature decay property (i) in Definition
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
\phi_{t}
be the one parameter subgroup generated by
-\nabla f
and
g(t)=\phi_{t}^{\ast}g
as before.
Let any
p\in M
such that
\nabla f(p)\neq 0
and
p_{i}=\phi_{t_{i}}(p)
, where
t_{i}\rightarrow-\infty
. Then by Lemma
3.9
, we may assume that sequence
(M,g_{r_{i}}(t),p_{i})
converges to a shrinking cylinders flow
(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1},\widetilde{g}(t))
, where

\widetilde{g}(t)=ds\otimes ds+(n-2)[(n-1)-2t]g_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}.

Thus scalar curvature
\widetilde{R}(\cdot,t)
of
\widetilde{g}(t)
is given by

\widetilde{R}(\cdot,t)=\frac{n-1}{(n-1)-2t}.

It follows

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\widetilde{R}(p_{\infty},0)=\frac{2}{n-1}.

On the other hand, by the convergence of
(M,g_{r_{i}}(t),p_{i})
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\widetilde{R}(p_{\infty},0)=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{R^{2}(p_{i},0)}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}R(p_{i},0)=\lim_{i_{k}\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{R^{2}(p,-t_{i_{k}})}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}R(p,-t_{i}).

Hence we get

\displaystyle\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}F^{\prime}(t_{i})=\frac{n-1}{2},

where
F(t)=R^{-1}(p,-t)
.
Since
t_{i}
is an arbitrary sequence,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow-\infty}F^{\prime}(t)=\frac{n-1}{2},

This implies

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{tR(p,-t)}=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}F^{\prime}(t)=\frac{n-1}{2}.

Therefore, we derive

(3.27)

\displaystyle R(\phi_{t}(p))|t|\rightarrow\frac{n-1}{2},~{\rm as}~t\rightarrow-\infty.

By the identity (
3.6
) and Corollary
3.6
, we have

(3.28)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f(\phi_{-t}(p))}{t}=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{df(\phi_{-t}(p))}{dt}=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}|\nabla f|^{2}=R_{\max}.

On the other hand, by Corollary
3.10
, it holds

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{x,y\in\Sigma_{r}}|\frac{R(x)}{R(y)}-1|=0.

Since
f(\phi_{t}(p))
goes to
\infty
as
t\to-\infty
, we see that for any
x\in M\setminus\{o\}
with
f(x)\geq r_{0}
, there exists a
t_{x}<0
such that
f(x)=f(\phi_{t_{x}}(p))
. Thus

\lim_{f(x)\rightarrow\infty}|\frac{R(x)}{R(\phi_{t_{x}}(p))}-1|=0.

Combining this with (
3.27
) and (
3.28
), we deduce

\lim_{f(x)\rightarrow\infty}R(x)f(x)=\lim_{t_{x}\rightarrow-\infty}R(\phi_{t_{x}}(p))f(\phi_{t_{x}}(p))=\frac{n-1}{2}R_{\max}.

By (
1.5
), we finally get the property (i) in Definition
1.1
.

∎

If (
1.2
) is replaced for the ambient metric
g
by

(3.29)

\displaystyle{\rm Ric}(v,v)\geq\epsilon R(x)g(v,v),~\forall~v\in T_{x}\Sigma_{r},~r>r_{0},

we give another version of Theorem
1.3
as follows.

Corollary 3.11
.

Any
\kappa
-noncollapsed steady Ricci soliton
(M,g,f)
with nonnegative curvature operator, positive Ricci curvature and a uniform scalar curvature decay must be rotationally symmetric, if
(M,g,f)
admits a unique equilibrium point and
there exist
r_{0}>0
and
\epsilon>0
such that the pinching condition (
3.29
) holds.

Proof.

By Theorem
1.3
, it suffices to verify that
(M,g,f)
satisfies (
1.2
).
Let
e_{i^{\prime}}(i^{\prime}=1,2,\cdots,n-1)
be normal eigenvector fields of
\overline{\rm Ric}(\overline{g})
. Then we have Gauss equation

\displaystyle\overline{\rm R}_{i^{\prime}i^{\prime}}=R_{i^{\prime}i^{\prime}}

(3.30)

\displaystyle-R(\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|},e_{i^{\prime}},e_{i^{\prime}},\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|})-\frac{1}{|\nabla f|^{2}}\sum_{k^{\prime}}(R_{i^{\prime}i^{\prime}}R_{k^{\prime}k^{\prime}}-R_{i^{\prime}k^{\prime}}R_{k^{\prime}i^{\prime}}).

Note that
R(x)
decays uniformly. We have

\displaystyle|\nabla f|(x)\geq\frac{\sqrt{R_{\max}}}{2},~\forall~r(x)>r_{0},

for some
r_{0}>0
. Thus by (
3.29
), it follows

\displaystyle|\sum_{k^{\prime}}(R_{i^{\prime}i^{\prime}}R_{k^{\prime}k^{\prime}}-R_{i^{\prime}k^{\prime}}R_{k^{\prime}i^{\prime}})|\leq

\displaystyle R_{i^{\prime}i^{\prime}}R+\sum_{k^{\prime}}R_{i^{\prime}k^{\prime}}R_{k^{\prime}i^{\prime}}

(3.31)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle R_{i^{\prime}i^{\prime}}R+R^{2}\leq(1+\frac{1}{\epsilon})R_{i^{\prime}i^{\prime}}R=o(1)R_{i^{\prime}i^{\prime}}.

Also we have

\displaystyle|R(\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|},e_{i^{\prime}},e_{i^{\prime}},\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|})|\leq

\displaystyle|\sum_{i^{\prime}=1}^{n-1}R(\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|},e_{i^{\prime}},e_{i^{\prime}},\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|{\rm Ric}(\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|},\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|\langle\nabla R,\nabla f\rangle|}{2|\nabla f|^{2}}

(3.32)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|\Delta R+2|{\rm Ric}|^{2}|}{2|\nabla f|^{2}}.

On the other hand, by
Proposition 3.8 in
[
10
]
, we see that there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\frac{|\Delta R|(x)}{R^{2}(x)}\leq C,~\forall~x\in M.

Then by (
3
) and (
3.29
), we get

\displaystyle|R(\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|},e_{i^{\prime}},e_{i^{\prime}},\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|})|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(C+2)R^{2}}{|\nabla f|^{2}}\leq\frac{(C+2)R}{\epsilon|\nabla f|^{2}}R_{i^{\prime}i^{\prime}}=o(1)R_{i^{\prime}i^{\prime}}.

Combining this with (
3
) and (
3.31
), we obtain

\displaystyle\overline{\rm R}_{i^{\prime}i^{\prime}}(x)\geq\frac{1}{2}{\rm R}_{i^{\prime}i^{\prime}}(x)\geq\frac{\epsilon}{2}R(x),~{\rm as}~r(x)>r_{0}.

This implies (
1.2
).
∎

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.4

In this section, we apply Theorem
1.3
to prove Theorem
1.4
by verifying (
1.2
) for steady Ricci solitons under the curvature decay condition (
1.4
).

We begin with

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
p_{i}\in M\rightarrow\infty
and
(M,g_{i}(t),p_{i})
be a sequence of rescaling Ricci flows, where
g_{i}(t)=R(p_{i})g(R^{-1}(p_{i})t)
. Suppose that
(M,g_{i}(t),p_{i})

converges to a limit flow
(M_{\infty},g_{\infty}(t),p_{\infty})
in the Cheeger-Gromov topology. Then scalar curvature
R^{(\infty)}(x,t)
of
g_{\infty}(t)
satisfies

(4.1)

\displaystyle(1-\frac{t}{C_{0}})R^{(\infty)}(x,t)\equiv 1,~\forall~x\in M_{\infty},~t\in(-\infty,0].

if
(M,g,f)
admits a unique equilibrium point and it satisfies (
1.4
).

Proof.

First we note that
R(g)
decays uniformly by (
1.4
) and (
1.5
). For any
x\in B(p_{\infty},g_{\infty}(0);{r_{0}})\subset M_{\infty}
, we choose a sequence of
x_{i}\in B(p_{i},2r_{0};{g_{{i}}})
which converges to
x
in Cheeger-Gromov topology, where
g_{i}=R(p_{i})g
. Since the proof of relation (
3.11
) is still true for any
k>0
when
p_{r}
is replaced by any
p_{i}
, we have

B(p_{i},2r_{0};{g_{{i}}})\subset\{y\in M|~f(p_{i})-\frac{2r_{0}\sqrt{R_{\max}}}{\sqrt{R(p_{i})}}\leq f(y)\leq f(p_{i})+\frac{2r_{0}\sqrt{R_{\max}}}{\sqrt{R(p_{i})}}\}.

By (
1.4
), it follows

(4.2)

\displaystyle\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f(x_{i})}{f(p_{i})}=1.

Thus

(4.3)

\displaystyle R^{(\infty)}(x,t)=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\frac{R(x_{i},R^{-1}(p_{i})t)}{R(p_{i})}=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\frac{R(x_{i},R^{-1}(p_{i})t)}{R(x_{i})}.

Fix a point
p\in M\setminus\{o\}
. Then (
3.28
) holds.
Choose
\tau_{i}<0
such that
f(\phi_{\tau_{i}}(p))=f(x_{i})
. We claim

(4.4)

\displaystyle\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f(\phi_{R^{-1}(p_{i})t}(x_{i}))}{f(\phi_{\tau_{i}+R^{-1}(p_{i})t}(p))}=1,~\forall t\leq 0.

It suffices to consider the case
t<0
. By (
1.4
) and (
4.2
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{f(\phi_{\tau_{i}+R^{-1}(p_{i})t}(p))}{|R^{-1}(p_{i})t|}=

\displaystyle\frac{f(\phi_{\tau_{i}+R^{-1}(p_{i})t}(p))-f(\phi_{\tau_{i}}(p))}{|R^{-1}(p_{i})t|}+\frac{f(x_{i})}{|R^{-1}(p_{i})t|}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|\int_{R^{-1}(p_{i})t}^{0}|\nabla f|^{2}ds|}{|R^{-1}(p_{i})t|}+\frac{f(x_{i})}{|R^{-1}(p_{i})t|}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle R_{\max}(1+\frac{C_{0}}{|t|}),~{\rm as}~i\to\infty.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\frac{f(\phi_{R^{-1}(p_{i})t}(x_{i}))}{|R^{-1}(p_{i})t|}=

\displaystyle\frac{f(\phi_{R^{-1}(p_{i})t}(x_{i}))-f(x_{i})}{|R^{-1}(p_{i})t|}+\frac{f(x_{i})}{|R^{-1}(p_{i})t|}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle R_{\max}(1+\frac{C_{0}}{|t|}),~{\rm as}~i\to\infty.

Thus (
4.4
) comes from the above two relations.

By (
1.4
) and the identity (
3.6
), we have

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f(\phi_{-t}(p))}{t}=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{df(\phi_{-t}(p))}{dt}=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}|\nabla f|^{2}=R_{\max}.

Combining this with (
1.4
), (
4.4
) and (
4.2
),
we obtain from (
4.3
),

\displaystyle R^{(\infty)}(x,t)=

\displaystyle\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f(x_{i})}{f(\phi_{R^{-1}(p_{i})t}(x_{i}))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f(\phi_{\tau_{i}}(p))}{f(\phi_{\tau_{i}+R^{-1}(p_{i})t}(p))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\tau_{i}}{\tau_{i}+R^{-1}(p_{i})t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\tau_{i}}{\tau_{i}+f(x_{i})t/(C_{0}\cdot R_{\max})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\tau_{i}}{\tau_{i}+f(\phi_{\tau_{i}}(p))t/(C_{0}\cdot R_{\max})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C_{0}}{C_{0}-t}.

This proves (
4.1
).

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

It suffices to verify that horizontally Ricci curvature of
(M,g)
is
\epsilon
-pinched for some
\epsilon>0
. We use the contradiction argument. Then there exist a sequence of points
p_{i}\rightarrow\infty
and vectors
v^{(i)}\in T_{p_{i}}\Sigma_{f(p_{i})}
such that

(4.5)

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm\overline{Ric}}(v^{(i)},v^{(i)})}{R(p_{i})\overline{g}(v^{(i)},v^{(i)})}\rightarrow 0,~as~i\rightarrow\infty.

By Theorem
3.1
, we may assume that
(M,g_{i}(t),p_{i})
converges to
(M_{\infty},\widetilde{g}(t),

~p_{\infty})
, where
g_{i}(t)=R(p_{i})g(R^{-1}(p_{i})t)
,
(M_{\infty},g_{\infty}(t))=(\mathbb{R}\times N,dr^{2}+g_{N}(t))
.

Let
X_{(i)}=R(p_{i})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla f
. Then, for any fixed
r>0
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{B(p_{i},r;{g_{{i}}})}|X_{(i)}|_{g_{{i}}}=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{B(p_{i},r;g_{{i}})}\sqrt{R_{\max}-R(x)}=\sqrt{R_{\max}},

where
g_{{i}}=R(p_{i})g
.
It follows

\displaystyle\sup_{B(p_{i},r;{g_{{i}}})}|\nabla^{k}_{(g_{{i}})}X_{(i)}|_{g_{i}}\leq C_{0}\sup_{B(p_{i},r;g_{i})}|\nabla^{k-1}_{(g_{i})}{\rm Ric}|_{g_{i}}\leq C.

Thus we may assume that
X_{(i)}
converges to
X_{(\infty)}
. On the other hand, by Lemma
4.1
, we have

R^{(i)}(p,0)\rightarrow 1,~\forall~p\in B(p_{i},r;g_{i}),

where
R^{(i)}
are scalar curvatures of

.
Hence similar to (
3
), we have

|\nabla_{(\infty)}X_{(\infty)}|_{g_{\infty}(0)}=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}|\nabla_{(g_{i})}X_{(i)}|_{g_{i}}=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|{\rm Ric}|}{R^{\frac{1}{2}}(p_{i})}=0.

This implies that the limit manifold will split off a real line along

. As a consequence,
X_{(\infty)}
is tangent to
\mathbb{R}
in
\mathbb{R}\times N
.

Now, we prove that
N
is compact. By Lemma

, we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{\widetilde{g}(t)}R^{(\infty)}(q,t)\equiv 0,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial R^{(\infty)}(q,t)}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{C_{0}}(R^{(\infty)}(q,t))^{2}.

By flow equation

\displaystyle\frac{\partial R^{(\infty)}(q,t)}{\partial t}=\Delta_{(\infty)}R^{(\infty)}(q,t)+2|{\rm Ric}^{(\infty)}|^{2}(q,t),

It follows

(4.6)

\displaystyle(R^{(\infty)}(q,t))^{2}=2C_{0}|{\rm Ric}^{(\infty)}|^{2}(q,t).

Let
\lambda_{1}(q,t)\leq\lambda_{2}(q,t)\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{n-1}(q,t)
be the eigenvalues of

. By the condition
C_{0}>\frac{n-2}{2}
, we see that there exists a constant
\delta(n)>0
such that

(4.7)

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{1}(q,t)}{\lambda_{n-1}(q,t)}\geq\delta(n).

This implies that
N
is compact.

At last, we check the pinching condition. Choose an orthonormal basis
\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}\}
w.r.t

of

such that

.

\displaystyle{\rm Ric}^{(\infty)}(e_{k},e_{l})=0,~for~k\neq l.

By the convergence, it follows

\displaystyle{\rm Ric}^{(i)}(e^{(i)}_{k},e^{(i)}_{k})\geq(1-\varepsilon_{i})\frac{\delta(n)}{n}R^{(i)}(p_{i}),~\forall~1\leq k\leq n-1,

\displaystyle{\rm Ric}^{(i)}(e^{(i)}_{k},e^{(i)}_{l})\rightarrow 0,~as~i\rightarrow\infty,~~\forall~k\neq l,

where
\varepsilon_{i}\rightarrow 0
as

.
Since

, we get

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm Ric}^{(i)}(v^{(i)},v^{(i)})}{g(v^{(i)},v^{(i)})}\geq\frac{\delta(n)}{2n}R^{(i)}(p_{i}),~as~i\rightarrow\infty.

On the other hand, since the limit manifold splits along

By (

This is a contradiction to (

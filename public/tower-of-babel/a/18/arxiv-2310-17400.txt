Maslov Index, Spectral Flow and Bifurcation of Electromagnetic
Geodesics Within an Energy Level

Abstract

Departamento de Matemática,

Abstract.

We develop appropriate notions of Maslov index and spectral flow for electromagnetic geodesics within a fixed energy level
and prove a Morse Index type theorem in this context. This is then applied to the
problem of electromagnetic geodesics, all of whose energy are the same, bifurcating from
a given one.

1.  Introduction

Let
M
be a
n-
dimensional smooth manifold endowed with both a semi-Riemannian metric
g
and a closed

2-
form
\sigma
. Let also
Y:TM\rightarrow TM
be the endomorphism field on
M
defined via

\sigma(p)[u,v]=g(p)[u,Y(p)[v]]
. We consider in this work the following system of second order differential equations on
M
,

\frac{D}{dt}\dot{\gamma}=Y(\gamma)[\dot{\gamma}],

(1)

for
D/dt
the semi-Riemannian covariant derivative. In case
g
is Riemannian, resp. Lorentzian, these equations govern
the motion of a particle on
(M,g)
under
the influence of the magnetic, resp. electromagnetic, field
\sigma
. In the general case of an arbitrary semi-Riemannian
metric
g
, we shall use
the term
electromagnetic
. The endomorphism
Y
is known as the Lorentz force of the electromagnetic field

\sigma
and
Eq. (
1
) as the
Lorentz force equation
.

We shall call solutions to Eq. (
1
)
electromagnetic geodesics
. As it is well-known, along an electromagnetic
geodesic
\gamma:I\subseteq{\rm I\!R}\rightarrow M
the
energy

E(\gamma,\dot{\gamma})=\frac{1}{2}g(\gamma)[\dot{\gamma},\dot{\gamma}]

stays constant. In this work we are interested in the following phenomenon:

1.1
.

Let
\gamma:[0,T]\rightarrow M
be an electromagnetic geodesic with energy
\kappa
. We say that an instant

t_{0}\in(0,T]
is an
energy-constrained bifurcation instant
for
\gamma
if there exists a sequence

\gamma_{n}:[0,T]\rightarrow M
of distinct electromagnetic geodesics
departing from
\gamma(0)
and a sequence of distinct instants
(t_{n})_{n\geq 1}
in
(0,T]

such that

1.

t_{n}\rightarrow t_{0}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
;

2.

\gamma_{n}(t_{n})=\gamma(t_{n})
for all
n
;

3.

\dot{\gamma}_{n}(0)\rightarrow\dot{\gamma}(0)
as
n\rightarrow\infty
;

4.

\gamma_{n}
has energy
\kappa
for all
n
.

This kind of qualitative behavior of solutions to variational problems arising in differential geometry,
along with its variations, has been the object of study
of many works, including
[
17
,
10
,
16
,
14
,
11
]
.
Bifurcation phenomena are natural to consider. For example, a slight variation of the above definition, in the
context of light-like geodesics of a Lorentzian manifold, can be used to model the relativistic phenomenon of

gravitational lensing
(see
[
10
]
). Considering a bifurcation perspective has also provided
valuable insights to the study of solutions to some important problems in multidimensional variational calculus; for
an updated overview, we refer to the survey
[
6
]
.

The novelty in our work is the energy constraint expressed in 4. of Definition
1.1
.
This constraint is a very natural one since
the dynamics of a Hamiltonian flow can change significantly from one energy level to another.
Moreover, such a constraint may convey a physical meaning
to the problem. For example, for
(M,g)
a space-time,
the constraint
E(\gamma_{n},\dot{\gamma}_{n})=-1/2
means that the trajectories
\gamma_{n}
are parametrized by

proper time
;
we remark that, in this context, a bifurcation result for timelike electromagnetic geodesics parametrized by proper time, and departing
from a timelike curve representing an observer, was obtained in
[
11
]
by exploring the

Kaluza-Klein
correspondence and resorting to (a stronger form of) the bifurcation
result for lightlike geodesics in
[
10
]
.

In the absence of the energy constraint, sufficient conditions for
existence of bifurcation have been obtained in
[
17
]
,
[
16
]
and
[
14
]
for the
Euler-Lagrange equations associated to the
geodesic
,
electromagnetic
and
perturbed geodesic
Lagrangians
L:TM\rightarrow{\rm I\!R}
, respectively.
These were achieved
by proving a Morse Index type theorem and then applying to the action functional of
L

the functional analytic
results for bifurcation of critical points of strongly indefinite functionals established in
[
8
]
.
Observe that since we are allowing for a
non-exact
electromagnetic field
\sigma
,
Eq. (
1
) might not be the Euler-Lagrange equations of some Lagrangian function
on
TM
. Nevertheless, the analysis of the electromagnetic geodesics in the vicinity of a given one

\gamma:[0,T]\rightarrow M
connecting two points
p
and
q
, and having the same energy as
\gamma
, can also be brought to the realm of critical point theory thanks
to the following (see
[
2
]
and Sec.
4
below):
the solutions
\gamma:[0,T]\rightarrow M
to Eq. (
1
)
connecting two points
p
and
q
and constrained to
E(\gamma,\dot{\gamma})=\kappa
correspond to the zeros of a
certain smooth
1-
form
\eta_{\kappa}
on
\Omega_{p,q}([0,1])\times{\rm I\!R}_{+}
, where

\Omega_{p,q}([0,1])
is the Hilbert manifold of (Sobolev regular) curves
x:[0,1]\rightarrow M
connecting
p
and
q
. Furthermore,

\eta_{\kappa}
possesses local primitives.

Our purpose is to provide a general sufficient condition for energy-constrained bifurcation to occur by establishing
a Morse Index type theorem in the energy-constrained setting.
Each electromagnetic geodesic
\gamma:[0,T]\rightarrow M
with non null energy
\kappa
possesses an
energy-constrained index form
, which
is the quadratic form
{\cal Q}_{\kappa,\sigma}(\gamma)
given by the second derivative of a local primitive for the
1-
form
\eta_{\kappa}

at the critical point corresponding to
\gamma
.
By analysing the kernel of
{\cal Q}_{\kappa,\sigma}(\gamma)
we are led to the following notion
of conjugacy along
\gamma
(we remark that the same notion of conjugacy was obtained in
[
11
]
by different means);
in the following, the symbols
\nabla
and
R
will denote the Levi-Civita
connection and the curvature tensor of the metric
g
, respectively, with the sign convention
R(X,Y)=[\nabla_{X},\nabla_{Y}]-\nabla_{[X,Y]}
.

1.2
.

Let
\gamma:[0,T]\rightarrow M
be an electromagnetic geodesic with energy
\kappa\neq 0
. An
energy-constrained Jacobi field

along
\gamma
is a (smooth) vector field
J
along
\gamma
that satisfies the equation

\frac{D^{2}}{dt^{2}}J+R(J,\dot{\gamma})\dot{\gamma}-(\nabla_{J}Y)[\dot{\gamma}]-Y\left[\frac{D}{dt}J\right]-\frac{1}{2\kappa}g\left[\frac{D}{dt}J(0),\dot{\gamma}(0)\right]Y[\dot{\gamma}]=0.

An instant
t_{0}\in(0,T]
, for which there exists a non-null energy-constrained Jacobi field that
vanishes at both
t=0
and
t=t_{0}
will be called an
energy-constrained conjugate instant
along
\gamma
.

Without the energy constraint, we shall use the term
ordinary
to refer to the well-known analogous concepts. So, for instance, we shall
also speak of ordinary Jacobi fields and ordinary conjugacy; these are obtained by erasing the last term of the left-hand side of the above equation.

Let us suppose for the rest of this introduction that
\gamma:[0,T]\rightarrow M
is an electromagnetic geodesic with

non null
energy
\kappa
.
A direct application of the Implicit Function
Theorem shows that every energy-constrained bifurcation instant is an energy-constrained conjugate instant.
Reciprocally, Theorem
1.5
below establishes that the interval
(0,T]
will contain an
energy-constrained bifurcation instant for
\gamma
provided that
the “net number” of energy-constrained conjugate instants in
(0,T]
be non null. This net number shall be given by the following
notion of
energy-constrained Maslov index
of
\gamma
. For this, let
\zeta_{t}:TM\rightarrow TM
be the flow corresponding to Eq. (
1
).
This flow is the Hamiltonian flow of the energy function
E:TM\rightarrow{\rm I\!R}
when one endows
TM
with the
symplectic structure
\omega
induced
by both the metric
g
and the electromagnetic field
\sigma
, i.e.
\omega=d\alpha_{g}+\pi^{*}\sigma
,
for
\alpha_{g}
the pull-back of the canonical
1-
form
\alpha
of
T^{*}M
by the diffeomorphism

g^{\sharp}:TM\rightarrow T^{*}M
and
\pi:TM\rightarrow M
the projection map.
Let
S
be the corresponding Hamiltonian vector field. Since
the energy surface
E^{-1}(\kappa)
is invariant by
\zeta_{t}
,
the derivative of
\zeta_{t}
induces an isomorphism of quotient spaces,

\bar{\Phi}_{t}:T_{(p,v)}E^{-1}(\kappa)/\left\langle S\right\rangle\,\rightarrow\,T_{(\gamma,\dot{\gamma})}E^{-1}(\kappa)/\left\langle S\right\rangle,

(2)

where
p=\gamma(0)
and
v=\dot{\gamma}(0)
. The above
quotient spaces inherit from
\omega
a symplectic structure, and the map
\bar{\Phi}_{t}
is symplectic. Let

VE^{-1}(\kappa)\subset TE^{-1}(\kappa)
denote the vertical distribution on
E^{-1}(\kappa)
, which is given by the tangent
spaces to the fibers of the projection
E^{-1}(\kappa)\rightarrow M
. The image of
VE^{-1}(\kappa)
in

TE^{-1}(\kappa)/\langle S\rangle
is a Lagrangian subbundle, which we shall denote by
\bar{L}
.
By pulling
\bar{L}_{(\gamma,\dot{\gamma})}
back by
\bar{\Phi}_{t}
we thus obtain a curve of Lagrangian subspaces,

\ell:[0,T]\rightarrow\Lambda\left(T_{(p,v)}E^{-1}(\kappa)/\langle S\rangle\right),\quad\ell(t)=(\bar{\Phi}_{t})^{-1}\bar{L}_{(\gamma,\dot{\gamma})}.

(3)

In the above,
\Lambda(-)
denotes the Lagrangian Grassmannian manifold of all Lagrangian subspaces
of the corresponding symplectic vector space.

1.3
.

The
energy-constrained Maslov index
of
\gamma
, denoted by
\mu_{\kappa}(\gamma)
, is defined as the
Maslov index of the Lagrangian path
\ell|_{[\epsilon,T]}
with respect to the reference Lagrangian

\bar{L}_{(p,v)}
, for
\epsilon
any sufficiently small positive number (in the sense of Proposition
6.7
).

On the other hand, by traveling along
\gamma
from
p=\gamma(0)
to
q=\gamma(T)
, the resulting family of energy-constrained index forms
can be identified to a path of quadratic forms
Q=\{Q_{s}\}_{s\in(0,T]}
on the Hilbert space

H_{0}^{1}([0,1],{\rm I\!R}^{n})\times{\rm I\!R}
which are of Fredholm type. For such a family one can compute the so-called

spectral flow
along an interval
[a,b]\subset(0,T]
, denoted by
{\rm sf}(Q,[a,b])
.
Roughly, it gives the net number of the eigenvalues
of the self-adjoint operator
L_{s}
representing
Q_{s}
that cross zero as
s
runs from
a
to
b
.
The spectral flow is an important homotopy invariant which substitutes the notion of Morse index when the later fails to
be finite, and plays a key role in strongly-indefinite problems. For instance,
the spectral flow is a key to understanding variational bifurcation of strongly-indefinite functionals. Indeed, the main result of

[
8
]
shows that the non vanishing of the spectral flow implies bifurcation.
We define the
energy-constrained spectral flow
of
\gamma
, and denote it by
{\rm sf}_{\kappa}(\gamma)
, as the spectral flow

{\rm sf}(Q,[\epsilon,T])
for any sufficiently small positive
\epsilon
(as in Proposition
6.7
).
The Morse Index type theorem that we shall prove establishes that the spectral flow, which is infinite-dimensional in nature, can
be computed as the more tractable Maslov index:

1.4
.

Suppose that
T
is neither an energy-constrained nor an ordinary conjugate instant along
\gamma
. Then, we have an equality

{\rm sf}_{\kappa}(\gamma)=-\mu_{\kappa}(\gamma).

This theorem is the energy-constrained version of the Morse Index theorem of
[
16
]
which establishes an
analogous equality for the
ordinary
Maslov index
\mu(\gamma)
and spectral flow
{\rm sf}(\gamma)
of an electromagnetic geodesic.
Its proof shall consist in three steps. First we compute the difference
{\rm sf}_{\kappa}(\gamma)-{\rm sf}(\gamma)
by establishing
in Sec.
3.2
a general formula relating the spectral flow of a path
\{{\cal Q}_{s}\}
to the spectral flow of
the path obtained by restricting the
{\cal Q}_{s}
to a fixed closed finite codimensional subspace of the ambient Hilbert space. This result extends
to general paths (but with invertible endpoints), and with a considerable simpler proof, Theorem 4.4 of
[
5
]
proved for paths
that are compact perturbations of a
fixed
symmetry, and might be of independent interest. In the second step, by regarding the Lagrangian
path
\ell(t)
as the symplectic reduction of another Lagrangian path
\check{\ell}(t)
, this time on
\Lambda(T_{(p,v)}TM)
,
we resort to a result from
[
19
]
to equate
\mu_{\kappa}(\gamma)
to a Maslov index of
\check{\ell}(t)
.
This has the advantage that the latter Maslov index differs
from
\mu(\gamma)
by a
Hörmander four-fold index
, which in turn is half the difference of two
Kashiwara indices

of triplets of Lagrangian subspaces. So step three shall consist of computing the Kashiwara indices of the triplets formed
by the endpoints of the path
\check{\ell}(t)
and the reference Lagrangians. With the computations of
{\rm sf}_{\kappa}(\gamma)-{\rm sf}(\gamma)
and

\mu_{\kappa}(\gamma)-\mu(\gamma)
at hand, Theorem
1.4
will follow from the Morse index theorem of
[
16
]
.

As a consequence of Theorem
1.4
and the criterion for variational bifurcation of
strongly-indefinite functionals from
[
8
]
, we shall obtain the following criterion for energy-constrained
bifurcation of electromagnetic geodesics.

1.5
.

Suppose that
T
is neither an energy-constrained nor an ordinary conjugate instant along
\gamma
. If

\mu_{\kappa}(\gamma)\neq 0
, then there exists an energy-constrained bifurcation instant for
\gamma
in
(0,T]
.

For an isolated energy-constrained conjugate instant
t_{0}
along
\gamma
, the energy-constrained Maslov index of
\gamma

across

t_{0}
,
denoted by
\mu_{\kappa}(\gamma,t_{0})
, is defined as the Maslov index of the Lagrangian path
\ell|_{[t_{0}-\epsilon,t_{0}+\epsilon]}
with respect to the Lagrangian

\bar{L}_{(p,v)}
, for
\epsilon
a sufficiently small positive number.

1.6
.

An isolated energy-constrained conjugate instant
t_{0}
will be an energy-constrained bifurcation instant provided that
\mu_{\kappa}(\gamma,t_{0})\neq 0
.

For an energy-constrained conjugate instant
t_{0}
we consider the subspace
\mathds{J}^{\prime}[t_{0}]
of
T_{\gamma(t_{0})}M
formed by the
values
DJ/dt|_{t=t_{0}}
, for
J
varying among the energy-constrained Jacobi fields vanishing at both
t=0
and
t=t_{0}
. Also, let

\mathfrak{g}_{t_{0}}
be the quadratic form on
T_{\gamma(t_{0})}M
given by

\mathfrak{g}_{t_{0}}[u,v]=g(\gamma(t_{0}))[u,v]-\frac{1}{2\kappa}g(\gamma(t_{0}))\bigl[\dot{\gamma}(t_{0}),u\bigr]g(\gamma(t_{0}))\bigl[\dot{\gamma}(t_{0}),v\bigr].

We say that
t_{0}
is
non-degenarate
if the restriction of
\mathfrak{g}_{t_{0}}
to
\mathds{J}^{\prime}[t_{0}]
is non-degenerate.
For such an instant we shall show in Proposition
6.6
that
\mu_{\kappa}(\gamma,t_{0})
is equal to
the signature (see (
4
) below) of the restriction of
\mathfrak{g}_{t_{0}}
to
\mathds{J}^{\prime}[t_{0}]
. Therefore,

1.7
.

A non-degenerate energy-constrained conjugate instant along
\gamma
with
{\rm sig}\left(\mathfrak{g}_{t_{0}}|_{\mathds{J}^{\prime}[t_{0}]}\right)\neq 0

is an energy-constrained bifurcation instant.

2
). The definition of the energy-constrained

1.4
, Theorem
1.5
and
Corollary
1.6
are carried out in Sec.
8
.

2.  The Maslov index of a curve of Lagrangian subspaces

Let
(V,\omega)
be a finite-dimensional real symplectic vector space. The manifold of Lagrangian
subspaces of
V
, the
Lagrangian Grassmannian
, shall be denoted by
\Lambda(V)
. We shall here recall
the definition of the Maslov index of a continuous path
\ell:[a,b]\rightarrow\Lambda(V)
with respect to
a given reference Lagrangian
L_{0}\in\Lambda(V)
through a uniqueness result from
[
18
]
.

Given a pair
(L_{0},L_{1})
of transversal Lagrangian subspaces, a chart for
\Lambda(L)
around
L_{0}
is obtained as follows.
Firstly, given
L\in\Lambda(V)
, let us
denote
\Lambda^{0}(L)=\left\{L^{\prime}\in\Lambda(V)\;:\;L\cap L^{\prime}=\{0\}\right\}
; this is an open and dense subset
of
\Lambda(V)
. Now, every
L\in\Lambda^{0}(L_{1})
is the graph of a unique linear map

T:L_{0}\rightarrow L_{1}
such that the bilinear form on
L_{0}
defined by
\omega\left[T\cdot,\cdot\right]|_{L_{0}}

is symmetric. A chart around
L_{0}
is then obtained by setting

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\varphi_{L_{0},L_{1}}:\Lambda^{0}(L_{1})\rightarrow{\rm B}_{\rm sym}(L_{0}),\\
&\varphi_{L_{0},L_{1}}(L)=\omega\left[T\cdot,\cdot\right]|_{L_{0}},\end{aligned}\right.

where
{\rm B}_{\rm sym}(L_{0})
is the space of symmetric bilinear forms on
L_{0}
. The Maslov index
of
\ell:[a,b]\rightarrow\Lambda(V)
with respect to
L_{0}
is the semi-integer
\mu_{L_{0}}(\ell)

characterized by the following uniqueness result (Corollary 5.2.4 of
[
18
]
). Before proceeding, recall
that the
signature
of a (real) quadratic form
Q
, denoted
{\rm sig}\,Q
, is the difference between
the indices of
-Q
and
Q
,

{\rm sig}\,Q={\rm ind}(-Q)-{\rm ind}\,Q.

(4)

2.1
.

Given a Lagrangian
L_{0}\in\Lambda(V)
, there exists a unique function that assigns to each continuous path

\ell:[a,b]\rightarrow\Lambda(V)
a semi-integer
\mu_{L_{0}}(\ell)
and that satisfies

1.

\mu_{L_{0}}
is invariant under homotopies that fix endpoints,

2.

\mu_{L_{0}}
is additive by concatenation,

3.

if
\ell([a,b])\subset\Lambda^{0}(L_{1})
for some
L_{1}\in\Lambda^{0}(L_{0})
, then

\mu_{L_{0}}(\ell)=\frac{1}{2}{\rm sig}\,\varphi_{L_{0},L_{1}}(\ell(b))-\frac{1}{2}{\rm sig}\,\varphi_{L_{0},L_{1}}(\ell(a)).

(5)

Although the above definition of Maslov index allows for paths with endpoints not in
\Lambda^{0}(L_{0})
, we shall only be
interested in the case where
\ell(a),\ell(b)\in\Lambda^{0}(L_{0})
so that
\mu_{L_{0}}(\ell)
will in fact be an integer.
The Maslov index
\mu_{L_{0}}(\ell)
is a sort of counting of intersections of the path
\ell
with the
Maslov cycle
(with vertex

L_{0}
)
\Sigma(L_{0})=\left\{L\in\Lambda(V)\;:\;L\cap L_{0}\neq\{0\}\right\}
. If
t_{0}
is an isolated instant of intersection, the
Maslov index across

t_{0}
, which we denote by

\mu_{L_{0}}(\ell,t_{0})
, is defined as

\mu_{L_{0}}(\ell,t_{0})=\mu_{L_{0}}\left(\ell|_{[t_{0}-\epsilon,t_{0}+\epsilon]}\right),

for
\epsilon
sufficiently small; the properties of
\mu_{L_{0}}
guarantee that
\mu_{L_{0}}(\ell,t_{0})
is well-defined.
Let now
\ell:[a,b]\rightarrow\Lambda(V)
be of class
C^{1}
. Each velocity vector
\dot{\ell}(t)=d\ell/dt
identifies
to a symmetric bilinear form on
\ell(t)
as follows. Given
t_{0}
and
u,v\in\ell(t_{0})
, let
u:(t_{0}-\epsilon,t_{0}+\epsilon)\rightarrow V

be of class
C^{1}

with
u(t)\in\ell(t)
for all
t
and
u(t_{0})=u
. Then one sets

\dot{\ell}(t_{0})[u,v]=\omega\left[\dot{u}(t_{0}),v\right].

An instant
t_{0}
of intersection of
\ell
with
\Sigma(L_{0})
is said to be

non-degenerate
if the quadratic form
\dot{\ell}(t_{0})
is non-degenerate on
\ell(t_{0})\cap L_{0}
. We define
the
signature
of such an instant,
{\rm sig}(\ell,L_{0},t_{0})
,
as the signature
of the restriction of
\dot{\ell}(t_{0})
to
\ell(t_{0})\cap L_{0}
. In a vicinity of a non-degenerate instant of intersection with
\Sigma(L_{0})

there are no other instants of intersections, and one shows that

\mu_{L_{0}}(\ell,t_{0})={\rm sig}(\ell,L_{0},t_{0}).

Thus, for
\ell
having
only non-degenerate instants of intersection with
\Sigma(L_{0})
, and with endpoints
in
\Lambda^{0}(L_{0})
, the Maslov index is the sum of the corresponding signatures:

\mu_{L_{0}}(\ell)=\sum_{t\in[a,b]}{\rm sig}(\ell,L_{0},t).

2.1  The Kashiwara index of a triplet of Lagrangians.

Given a continuous path
\ell:[a,b]\rightarrow\Lambda(V)
and two Lagrangians
L_{0},L_{1}\in\Lambda(V)
, it turns out
that the difference of Maslov indices
\mu_{L_{1}}(\ell)-\mu_{L_{0}}(\ell)
only depends on the quadruplet
(L_{0},L_{1},L_{2},L_{3})
, where

L_{2}=\ell(a)
and
L_{3}=\ell(b)
. That number, known in the literature as the
Hörmander four-fold index
of

(L_{0},L_{1},L_{2},L_{3})
,
can be described as half the difference of two
Kashiwara indices
:
given a triplet
(L_{1}^{\prime},L_{2}^{\prime},L_{3}^{\prime})
of Lagrangians,
its Kashiwara index (called in
[
13
]
the Maslov index of the triplet)
\tau(L_{1}^{\prime},L_{2}^{\prime},L_{3}^{\prime})
is the
integer defined as the signature of the quadratic form
\mathscr{Q}
on
L_{1}\oplus L_{2}\oplus L_{3}
given by

\mathscr{Q}\left[(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})\right]=\omega[v_{1},v_{2}]+\omega[v_{2},v_{3}]+\omega[v_{3},v_{1}].

Then one has that

\mu_{L_{1}}(\ell)-\mu_{L_{0}}(\ell)=\frac{1}{2}\tau(L_{1},L_{0},L_{3})-\frac{1}{2}\tau(L_{1},L_{0},L_{2}).

(6)

The above equality is implicit in page 181 of
[
7
]
when the path
\ell
has endpoints transversal to both

L_{0}
and
L_{1}
, and is stated in
[
12
]
in the non-transversal case but with a definition of the Maslov index
that differs from ours (we should observe, however, that the claimed relation in
[
12
]
does not seem to work
since the authors’ definition of
\mu_{L_{0}}(\ell)
does not change the sign when one changes
\omega
by
-\omega
).

Proof of (
6
) (sketch).

Very briefly, the proof of (
6
) is as follows: first one observes that, regarding the right-hand
side of (
5
), one has
{\rm sig}\,\varphi_{L_{0},L_{1}}(\ell(a))=\tau(L_{0},L_{1},\ell(a))
and
{\rm sig}\,\varphi_{L_{0},L_{1}}(\ell(b))=\tau(L_{0},L_{1},\ell(b))
; this is basically the assertion of Lemma 1.5.4 of
[
13
]
. Then, given
\ell
and
L_{0}
and
L_{1}
,
one finds a partition
a=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{N}=b
of
[a,b]
and Lagrangians
L_{1}^{\prime},...,L_{N}^{\prime}\in\Lambda^{0}(L_{0})\cap\Lambda^{0}(L_{1})
such that

for all

The equality in (

3.  The spectral flow and bifurcation

Throughout this section,
H
shall denote a separable infinite dimensional real Hilbert space. We shall use
superscript
\perp
to denote the orthogonal complement of a subspace and, given a quadratic form
Q
on
H
, the
superscript
\perp_{Q}
shall denote the orthogonal complement with respect to
Q
.

3.1  Generalities on the spectral flow.

Let
\hat{\mathfrak{F}}=\hat{\mathfrak{F}}(H)
be the space of self-adjoint Fredholm operators
on
H
. The space
\hat{\mathfrak{F}}
has three connected components,
\hat{\mathfrak{F}}_{+}
,
\hat{\mathfrak{F}}_{-}
and
\hat{\mathfrak{F}}_{*}
.
The components
\hat{\mathfrak{F}}_{\pm}
consist on the operators in
\hat{\mathfrak{F}}
that are essentially positive, resp. essentially negative,
(i.e., that have finite dimensional negative, resp. positive, spectral subspaces) and are contractible. As for
\hat{\mathfrak{F}}_{*}
, it has infinite cyclic
fundamental group; operators in
\hat{\mathfrak{F}}_{*}
are called strongly indefinite.

Let
L=\{L_{s}\}_{s\in[a,b]}
be a continuous path in
\hat{\mathfrak{F}}
; equivalently, via Riez representation, we can consider
the corresponding path
{\cal Q}=\{{\cal Q}_{s}\}_{s\in[a,b]}
of
quadratic forms of Fredholm type on
H
. The spectral flow of
L
, or of
{\cal Q}
, is the integer
{\rm sf}(L,[a,b])
, or
{\rm sf}({\cal Q},[a,b])
,
giving the net number of eigenvalues of
L
that cross 0 in the
positive direction as
s
runs from
a
to
b
. While for paths in
\hat{\mathfrak{F}}_{+}
or in
\hat{\mathfrak{F}}_{-}
this definition
can trivially be made precise, for paths in
\hat{\mathfrak{F}}_{*}
it requires some effort. There are several approaches in the literature
that produce the
same
definition of
{\rm sf}(L,[a,b])
if
L
has invertible endpoints
L_{a}
and
L_{b}
(which is the case
we shall be considering); see, for instance,
[
4
, Sec. 7]
,

[
15
]
and
[
8
]
. We shall content ourselves with listing here a few properties of
{\rm sf}(L,[a,b])
that we shall make use.

1.

(
Normalization
) If
L_{s}
is invertible for all
s
, then
{\rm sf}(L,[a,b])=0
;

2.

if
L
is a path in
\hat{\mathfrak{F}}_{+}
with invertible endpoints, then

{\rm sf}(L,[a,b])={\rm ind}\,L_{a}-{\rm ind}\,L_{b};

3.

(
Additivity under concatenation
) if
L_{1}*L_{2}
is the concatenation of two paths
L_{1}=\{(L_{1})_{s}\}_{s\in[a,b]}
and
L_{2}=\{(L_{2})_{s}\}_{s\in[b,c]}
that
agree for
s=b
, then

{\rm sf}(L_{1}*L_{2},[a,c])={\rm sf}(L_{1},[a,b])+{\rm sf}(L_{2},[b,c]);

4.

(
Additivity under direct sum
) if
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are two paths of self-adjoint Fredholm operators on Hilbert spaces
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
, then

{\rm sf}(L_{1}\oplus L_{2},[a,b])={\rm sf}(L_{1},[a,b])+{\rm sf}(L_{2},[a,b]);

5.

(
Invariance under cogredience
) given a path
L
in
\hat{\mathfrak{F}}(H)
and a path
M=\{M_{s}\}
in
GL(H)
, then

{\rm sf}(L,[a,b])={\rm sf}(M^{*}LM,[a,b]);

6.

(
Compact perturbations of closed paths
) for
closed
paths,
{\rm sf}(L,[a,b])
is stable under compact perturbations:
if
L
is closed and
K=\{K_{s}\}_{s\in[a,b]}
is a
closed continuous path of compact self-adjoint operators, then

{\rm sf}(L,[a,b])={\rm sf}(L+K,[a,b]).

3.2  The spectral flow of a restriction to a subspace.

Let
{\cal Q}=\{{\cal Q}_{s}\}_{s\in[a,b]}
be a continuous path of quadratic forms of Fredholm type on
H
. Let
{\cal V}\subset H
be a closed finite
codimensional subspace and denote by
\overline{{\cal Q}}=\{\overline{{\cal Q}}_{s}\}_{s\in[a,b]}
the path of quadratic forms
on
{\cal V}
obtained by restricting the
{\cal Q}_{s}
to
{\cal V}
. It is clear that
\overline{{\cal Q}}_{s}
are also of Fredholm type.
We shall compute the difference
{\rm sf}({\cal Q},[a,b])-{\rm sf}(\overline{{\cal Q}},[a,b])
in terms of the endpoints data.
The key observation is the following.

3.1
.

Suppose that the path
{\cal Q}
is closed. Then
{\rm sf}({\cal Q},[a,b])={\rm sf}(\overline{{\cal Q}},[a,b])
.

Proof.
Let
L_{s}
be the self-adjoint operator representing
{\cal Q}_{s}
. With respect to the orthogonal decomposition
H={\cal V}\oplus{\cal V}^{\perp}
,
let us write
L_{s}
in block form as

L_{s}=\begin{pmatrix}A_{s}&B_{s}\\
B_{s}^{*}&C_{s}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}A_{s}&{\rm O}\\
{\rm O}&{\rm O}\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}{\rm O}&B_{s}\\
B_{s}^{*}&C_{s}\end{pmatrix}=N_{s}+K_{s},

for
N_{s}
and
K_{s}
being the first and second summand above,
respectively. Since
{\cal V}^{\perp}
has finite dimension, the operator

K_{s}
is compact. Thus, by
6
. of Sec.
3.1
,

{\rm sf}(L,[a,b])={\rm sf}(N,[a,b])
. Also, by
4
. we have
{\rm sf}(N,[a,b])={\rm sf}(A,[a,b])
.
To conclude, just observe that
A_{s}
is the self-adjoint operator representing
\overline{{\cal Q}}_{s}
.

3.2
.

The space
\hat{\mathfrak{F}}_{*}\cap GL(H)
of invertible strongly indefinite self-adjoint operators is path-connected.

Proof.
Fix an orthogonal decomposition
H=H_{+}\oplus H_{-}
with
H_{\pm}
infinite dimensional closed subspaces, and let

\mathfrak{I}
be the symmetry on
H
given by
\mathfrak{I}(v_{+}+v_{-})=v_{+}-v_{-}
.
Given
S\in\hat{\mathfrak{F}}_{*}\cap GL(H)
, let us decompose
H
as the orthogonal sum
H=E_{+}\oplus E_{-}

of the positive and negative spectral subspaces
of
S
. Let
P_{\pm}
be the projections onto
E_{\pm}
and let
\mathfrak{I}^{\prime}
be the symmetry
\mathfrak{I}^{\prime}=P_{+}-P_{-}
.
We first connect
S
to
\mathfrak{I}^{\prime}
through the path
[0,1]\ni t\mapsto(tI+(1-t)S)\circ P_{+}+(-tI+(1-t)S)\circ P_{-}
.
Then we connect
\mathfrak{I}^{\prime}
to
\mathfrak{I}
as follows. Since
H_{\pm}
and
E_{\pm}
are infinite dimensional separable Hilbert spaces,
we can find orthogonal transformations
f_{\pm}:E_{\pm}\rightarrow H_{\pm}
. As the orthogonal group
O(H)
is connected, we connect

I
to
f_{+}\oplus f_{-}
through a path
[0,1]\ni t\mapsto A_{t}\in O(H)
. For each
t
, let
P_{\pm}^{t}
be the projections
onto
A_{t}E_{\pm}
. Then
t\mapsto P_{+}^{t}-P_{-}^{t}
is a path of symmetries connecting
\mathfrak{I}^{\prime}
to
\mathfrak{I}
.

3.3
.

Let
s
be such that
\overline{{\cal Q}}_{s}
is non-degenerate. Then
H={\cal V}\oplus{\cal V}^{\perp_{{\cal Q}_{s}}}
.

Proof.
We already have
{\cal V}\cap{\cal V}^{\perp_{{\cal Q}_{s}}}=\{0\}
since
\overline{{\cal Q}}_{s}
is non-degenerate.
By passing to the quotient
H/{\rm ker}\,{\cal Q}_{s}
, we can also suppose that
{\cal Q}_{s}
is non-degenerate. Thus
the musical map
{\cal Q}_{s}^{\sharp}:H\rightarrow H^{*}
is an isomorphism that sends

{\cal V}^{\perp_{{\cal Q}_{s}}}
onto the annihilator
{\cal V}^{0}
of
{\cal V}
. Therefore

{\rm dim}\,{\cal V}^{\perp_{{\cal Q}_{s}}}={\rm dim}\,{\cal V}^{0}={\rm codim}\,{\cal V}=n
. It follows that

({\cal V}\oplus{\cal V}^{\perp_{{\cal Q}_{s}}})/{\cal V}\cong{\cal V}^{\perp_{{\cal Q}_{s}}}
is a subspace of
H/{\cal V}
with dimension
n
.
Hence
{\cal V}\oplus{\cal V}^{\perp_{{\cal Q}_{s}}}=H
.

3.4
.

Let
{\cal Q}
,
{\cal V}
and
\overline{{\cal Q}}
be as in the beginning of this section. Suppose that
{\cal Q}
and
\overline{{\cal Q}}

have non-degenerate endpoints. Then,

Proof.
We shall only consider the case where the

are strongly indefinite, the other cases being simpler.
In this case, it is clear that

By the previous lemma, we have

for some quadratic form

which is isometric to

Next we connect

via a path of non-degenerate quadratic forms on

We end up with a concatenation of paths,

be the restriction of

)
since

for

.

The equality (

.

The computation of the difference

3.3  Abstract variational bifurcation.

Let
{\cal F}_{s}:{\cal N}\subset H\rightarrow{\rm I\!R}
be a family of
C^{2}-
functionals
defined on a neighborhood
{\cal N}
of the origin, all of which having the origin as a critical point, and
depending smoothly (i.e.
C^{2}
) on the parameter
s\in[a,b]
.
A point
s_{0}\in(a,b)
is said to be a
bifurcation instant
for the family
\{{\cal F}_{s}\}
if there exist non-trivial
sequences
(s_{n})_{n}
in
[a,b]
and
(u_{n})_{n}
in
{\cal N}
, with
n\geq 1
, such that

1.

u_{n}
is a critical point of
{\cal F}_{s_{n}}
for all
n
;

2.

s_{n}\rightarrow s_{0}
and
u_{n}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Suppose further that, for each
s
, the second derivative at the origin,

{\cal Q}_{s}=(D^{2}{\cal F}_{s})_{0}:H\times H\rightarrow{\rm I\!R},

is a quadratic form of Fredholm type.
From the Implicit Function Theorem it is clear that if
s_{0}
is a bifurcation instant, then
s_{0}
is a degenerate instant
of the family
{\cal Q}=\{{\cal Q}_{s}\}_{s\in[a,b]}
,
i.e., an instant for which the form
{\cal Q}_{s_{0}}
is degenerate.

The main result of
[
8
]
is the following sufficient condition for existence of bifurcation (see Theorem 1 of

[
8
]
).

3.6
.

In the above setting, suppose that the endpoints
{\cal Q}_{a}
and
{\cal Q}_{b}
are non-degenerate. If
{\rm sf}({\cal Q},[a,b])\neq 0
,
then there exists a bifurcation instant in
(a,b)
for the family
\{{\cal F}_{s}\}
.

As in
[
8
]
, let
{\rm sf}({\cal Q},s_{0})
be the spectral flow of
{\cal Q}
across an isolated degenerate instant of
{\cal Q}
. This is
defined as the spectral flow
{\rm sf}({\cal Q},[s_{0}-\epsilon,s_{0}+\epsilon])
for
\epsilon>0
such that there is no degenerate instants
in
[s_{0}-\epsilon,s_{0}+\epsilon]
besides
s_{0}
. Then a corollary of the above theorem is the following.

3.7
.

An isolated degenerate instant
s_{0}
for
{\cal Q}
will be a bifurcation instant for the family
\{{\cal F}_{s}\}
provided that
{\rm sf}({\cal Q},s_{0})\neq 0
.

4.  The variational setup

In this section we shall establish the (local) variational principle for solutions of
Eq. (
1
) under the energy constraint
E(\gamma,\dot{\gamma})\equiv\kappa

and compute the energy-constrained
index form of an electromagnetic geodesic
\gamma
.

It is important to bear in mind that we shall assume the following dictionary throughout this work:

\displaystyle\mbox{\sl curve}\;\,\gamma:[0,T]\rightarrow M

\displaystyle\longleftrightarrow\quad(x,T),\;\,\mbox{\sl with}\;\,x:[0,1]\rightarrow M,\;\,x(t)=\gamma(tT)

\displaystyle\mbox{\sl vector field $\hat{V}$ along $\gamma$}

\displaystyle\longleftrightarrow\quad\mbox{\sl vector field $V$ along $x$},\;\,V(t)=\hat{V}(tT)

4.1  Hilbert manifold of paths.

Let
p
and
q
be two fixed points on
M
(assumed to be connected).
With the help of an auxiliary complete Riemannian metric on
M
one can form the space
\Omega_{p,q}([0,1])

of all curves
x:[0,1]\rightarrow M
connecting
p
to
q
that have Sobolev regularity
H^{1}=W^{1,2}
. It is well-known
that
\Omega_{p,q}([0,1])
has the structure of a Hilbert manifold modeled on the Hilbert space
H^{1}_{0}([0,1],{\rm I\!R}^{n})
,
and that each tangent space
T_{x}\Omega_{p,q}([0,1])
identifies to the space of
H^{1}-
regular vector fields

V
along
x
vanishing at
t=0
and
t=1
.

Following Sec. 2.1 of
[
1
]
, a local parametrization around a
smooth

x_{*}\in\Omega_{p,q}([0,1])
can be constructed
as follows: fix a smooth frame
E_{1}
, …,
E_{n}
along
x_{*}
and, with the help of the auxiliary Riemannian metric on
M
, define
the map

\psi:[0,1]\times B_{\rho}^{n}\rightarrow M,\quad\psi(t,\hbox{\bf x})={\rm exp}_{x_{*}(t)}\Big(\sum_{i}{\rm x}_{i}E_{i}(t)\Big),

(8)

where
B_{\rho}^{n}
denotes the open ball in
{\rm I\!R}^{n}
of radius
\rho
and center
{\bf 0}=(0,...,0)^{T}
and
\hbox{\bf x}=({\rm x}_{1},...,{\rm x}_{n})^{T}
.
We choose
\rho
small enough so that
\psi(t,\cdot):B^{n}_{\rho}\rightarrow M
is a diffeomorphism onto an open set for each
t
.
By the continuous inclusion
H_{0}^{1}\hookrightarrow C^{0}
, the subset
H_{0}^{1}([0,1],B_{\rho}^{n})
given by the

\hbox{\bf x}=\hbox{\bf x}(t)
such that
\hbox{\bf x}([0,1])\subset B^{n}_{\rho}

H^{1}_{0}([0,1],{\rm I\!R}^{n})
.
A parametrization for
\Omega_{p,q}([0,1])
around
x_{*}
is then obtained by setting

\Psi:H^{1}_{0}([0,1],B_{\rho}^{n})\rightarrow\Omega_{p,q}([0,1]),\quad\Psi(\hbox{\bf x})(t)=\psi(t,\hbox{\bf x}(t)).

(9)

It is clear that
\Psi({\bf 0})=x_{*}
and that the derivative

(D\Psi)_{\bf 0}:H_{0}^{1}([0,1],{\rm I\!R}^{n})\rightarrow T_{x_{*}}\Omega_{p,q}([0,1])

is the isomorphism
\hbox{\bf v}=({\rm v}_{1},...,{\rm v}_{n})^{T}\mapsto V=\sum_{i}{\rm v}_{i}E_{i}
.

4.2  The global 1–form
\eta_{\kappa}

Following
[
2
]

of the energy we can characterize the solutions

\gamma:[0,T]\rightarrow M

The

6.3
.

A given

6.5
.

Via the above isomorphism, the quadratic form
\dot{\ell}(t)

g(\gamma(t))
to
\langle\dot{\gamma}(t)\rangle^{\perp}

Proof.
Let us fix a smooth frame

\gamma

(

Let

7.  The energy-constrained spectral flow of an electromagnetic geodesic

In what follows we shall denote by
\mathds{H}
the space
H^{1}_{0}([0,1],{\rm I\!R}^{n})\times{\rm I\!R}
. On
\mathds{H}
we choose the Hilbert inner product

\displaystyle\bigl\langle(\hbox{\bf v}_{1},B_{1}),(\hbox{\bf v}_{2},B_{2})\bigr\rangle_{\mathds{H}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\hbox{\bf v}_{1},\hbox{\bf v}_{2}\rangle_{H^{1}}+B_{1}B_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\hbox{\bf v}_{1},\hbox{\bf v}_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}}+\langle\dot{\hbox{\bf v}}_{1},\dot{\hbox{\bf v}}_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}}+B_{1}B_{2}.

By letting the endpoint
q
vary along
\gamma
and computing the energy-constrained index form of the corresponding
electromagnetic geodesic, we obtain a one-parameter family of quadratic forms

{\cal Q}_{\kappa,\sigma}(x_{s},s)
,
s\in(0,T]
, with varying domains,
where
x_{s}:[0,1]\rightarrow M
is the curve
x_{s}(t)=\gamma(st)
. We then bring this to a path
\{Q_{s}\}
of quadratic forms on
\mathds{H}
as follows:
fixed a smooth frame
E_{1}
, …,
E_{n}
along
\gamma
, the correspondence

\hbox{\bf v}=\hbox{\bf v}(t)=({\rm v}_{1}(t),...,{\rm v}_{n}(t))^{T}\quad\mapsto\quad V^{s}=V^{s}(t)=\sum_{i}{\rm v}_{i}(t)E_{i}(st)

is an isomorphism between
H_{0}^{1}([0,1],{\rm I\!R}^{n})
and the tangent space
T_{x_{s}}\Omega_{p,\gamma(s)}([0,1])
. We then obtain an isomorphism, for each
s\in(0,T]
,

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathds{H}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}T_{x_{s}}\Omega_{p,\gamma(s)}([0,1])\times{\rm I\!R},\\
&(\hbox{\bf v},B)\mapsto(V^{s},sB).\end{aligned}\right.

(30)

The quadratic forms
\{Q_{s}\}
are then defined as the ones corresponding to the
{\cal Q}_{\kappa,\sigma}(x_{s},s)
via the above isomorphism,

Q_{s}\bigl[(\hbox{\bf v}_{1},B_{1}),(\hbox{\bf v}_{2},B_{2})\bigr]={\cal Q}_{\kappa,\sigma}(x_{s},s)\bigl[(V_{1}^{s},sB_{1}),(V_{2}^{s},sB_{2})\bigr].

(31)

Any two choices of frames will amount to paths

\{Q_{s}\}
and
\{Q_{s}^{\prime}\}
that are isometric, i.e., the
Q_{s}^{\prime}
are of the form
M_{s}^{*}Q_{s}
for some continuous (in fact smooth) path
\{M_{s}\}

in
GL(H)
. Thus
\{Q_{s}\}
and
\{Q_{s}^{\prime}\}
will have essentially the same properties.
In order to obtain a simple expression for the
Q_{s}

we shall make the assumptions that the frame

E_{1}
, …,
E_{n}
is both
{\cal D}_{t}-
parallel and
g-
orthonormal, i.e.,

{\cal D}_{t}E_{i}=0\quad{\rm and}\quad\bigl(g(\gamma)[E_{i},E_{j}]\bigr)=I_{n,p},\quad{\rm for}\quad(I_{n,p})_{ij}=\left\{\begin{aligned} -1,&\quad{\rm if}\quad i=j\leq p,\\
1,&\quad{\rm if}\quad i=j>p,\\
0,&\quad{\rm if}\quad i\neq j,\end{aligned}\right.

(32)

where
0\leq p\leq n
is the index of the metric
g
; the existence of such a frame follows from
{\cal D}_{t}
being a first order
linear differential operator which satisfies 2. of
Lemma
4.4
.
With respect to this frame,
let

\displaystyle\hbox{\bf K}=\hbox{\bf K}(t)\;\mbox{be product of $I_{n,p}$ with the matrix of ${\cal K}(t)$;}

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\xi}=\boldsymbol{\xi}(t)\;\mbox{be the product
of $I_{n,p}$ with the column-vector}

of the coordinates of
Y[\dot{\gamma}(t)]
;

\displaystyle\hbox{\bf K}_{s}(t)=s^{2}\hbox{\bf K}(st)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\boldsymbol{\xi}_{s}(t)=s^{2}\boldsymbol{\xi}(st).

Observe that since
{\cal K}
is
g-
symmetric, then
\hbox{\bf K}^{T}=\hbox{\bf K}
. Let also
\langle,\rangle
denote the standard scalar product of
{\rm I\!R}^{n}
.

7.1
.

The quadratic forms
Q_{s}
are given by

\displaystyle Q_{s}\bigl[(\hbox{\bf v}_{1},B_{1}),(\hbox{\bf v}_{2},B_{2})\bigr]

\displaystyle=\;\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{1}\langle I_{n,p}\dot{\hbox{\bf v}}_{1},\dot{\hbox{\bf v}}_{2}\rangle dt-\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{1}\langle\hbox{\bf K}_{s}\hbox{\bf v}_{1},\hbox{\bf v}_{2}\rangle dt

(33)

\displaystyle+\;B_{1}\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{1}\langle\hbox{\bf v}_{2},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{s}\rangle dt+B_{2}\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{1}\langle\hbox{\bf v}_{1},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{s}\rangle dt+2\kappa sB_{1}B_{2}.

In particular,
\{Q_{s}\}
is a smooth path.

Proof.
The right-hand side of (
31
) was already computed in Eq. (
14
) once one
replaces
T
by
s
and
B_{i}
by
sB_{i}
in the latter. To show that it matches (

),
observe first that if

, then

, and then use that

, …,

is an orthonormal

parallel frame
and that

satisfies the Leibniz rule; we leave the details to the reader.

7.2
.

The forms
Q_{s}
are of Fredholm type. Also, if
(\hbox{\bf v},B)
is in the kernel of
Q_{s}
then
v
is smooth.

Proof.
Let
-d^{2}/dt^{2}:H^{2}([0,1],{\rm I\!R}^{n})\cap H_{0}^{1}([0,1],{\rm I\!R}^{n})\subset L^{2}([0,1],{\rm I\!R}^{n})\rightarrow L^{2}([0,1],{\rm I\!R}^{n})
be the Dirichlet Laplacian.
It is well-known that
1-d^{2}/dt^{2}
has a bounded inverse
(1-d^{2}/dt^{2})^{-1}:L^{2}\rightarrow H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}
and it is clear that

\bigl\langle\hbox{\bf v}_{1},\hbox{\bf v}_{2}\bigr\rangle_{L^{2}}=\Bigl\langle(1-d^{2}/dt^{2})^{-1}\hbox{\bf v}_{1}\,,\,\hbox{\bf v}_{2}\Bigr\rangle_{H^{1}},\quad{\rm if}\quad\hbox{\bf v}_{1}\in L^{2},\;\hbox{\bf v}_{2}\in H^{1}_{0}.

Thus we can write, for
\hbox{\bf v}_{1}
and
\hbox{\bf v}_{2}
in
H^{1}_{0}
,

\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{1}\langle I_{n,p}\dot{\hbox{\bf v}}_{1},\dot{\hbox{\bf v}_{2}}\rangle dt-\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{1}\langle\hbox{\bf K}_{s}\hbox{\bf v}_{1},\hbox{\bf v}_{2}\rangle dt\;\;=\;\;\frac{1}{s}\bigl\langle I_{n,p}\hbox{\bf v}_{1},\hbox{\bf v}_{2}\bigr\rangle_{H^{1}}-\frac{1}{s}\bigl\langle I_{n,p}\hbox{\bf v}_{1},\hbox{\bf v}_{2}\bigr\rangle_{L^{2}}-\frac{1}{s}\bigl\langle\hbox{\bf K}_{s}\hbox{\bf v}_{1},\hbox{\bf v}_{2}\bigr\rangle_{L^{2}}\\
\;\;=\;\;\frac{1}{s}\bigl\langle I_{n,p}\hbox{\bf v}_{1},\hbox{\bf v}_{2}\bigr\rangle_{H^{1}}-\frac{1}{s}\Bigl\langle(1-d^{2}/dt^{2})^{-1}I_{n,p}\hbox{\bf v}_{1},\hbox{\bf v}_{2}\Bigr\rangle_{H^{1}}-\frac{1}{s}\Bigl\langle(1-d^{2}/dt^{2})^{-1}\hbox{\bf K}_{s}\hbox{\bf v}_{1},\hbox{\bf v}_{2}\Bigr\rangle_{H^{1}}

and

B_{1}\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{1}\langle\hbox{\bf v}_{2},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{s}\rangle dt+B_{2}\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{1}\langle\hbox{\bf v}_{1},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{s}\rangle dt+2\kappa sB_{1}B_{2}\;\;=\;\;\frac{1}{s}\bigl\langle B_{1}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{s},\hbox{\bf v}_{2}\bigr\rangle_{L^{2}}+\left(\frac{1}{s}\bigl\langle\hbox{\bf v}_{1},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{s}\bigr\rangle_{L^{2}}+2\kappa sB_{1}\right)B_{2}\\
\;\;=\;\;\frac{1}{s}\Bigl\langle B_{1}(1-d^{2}/dt^{2})^{-1}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{s},\hbox{\bf v}_{2}\Bigr\rangle_{H^{1}}+\left(\frac{1}{s}\bigl\langle\hbox{\bf v}_{1},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{s}\bigr\rangle_{L^{2}}+2\kappa sB_{1}\right)B_{2}.

Therefore

\displaystyle Q_{s}\bigl[(\hbox{\bf v}_{1},B_{1}),(\hbox{\bf v}_{2},B_{2})\bigr]

\displaystyle=\;\;\frac{1}{s}\Bigl\langle I_{n,p}\hbox{\bf v}_{1}-(1-d^{2}/dt^{2})^{-1}\left(I_{n,p}\hbox{\bf v}_{1}+\hbox{\bf K}_{s}\hbox{\bf v}_{1}-B_{1}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{s}\right)\,,\,\hbox{\bf v}_{2}\Bigr\rangle_{H^{1}}

(34)

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{1}{s}\bigl\langle\hbox{\bf v}_{1},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{s}\bigr\rangle_{L^{2}}+2\kappa sB_{1}\right)B_{2},

from where it follows that
the self-adjoint operator
L_{s}
which represents
Q_{s}
is given by
L_{s}={\cal I}-K
, with

\displaystyle{\cal I}(\hbox{\bf v},B)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigl(\,\frac{1}{s}I_{n,p}\hbox{\bf v}\,,\,2\kappa sB\,\Bigr),

\displaystyle K(\hbox{\bf v},B)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{s}\Bigl(\,(1-d^{2}/dt^{2})^{-1}(I_{n,p}+\hbox{\bf K}_{s})\hbox{\bf v}\,,\,0\Bigr)-\frac{1}{s}\Bigl(B\overline{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{s}\,,\,\bigl\langle\hbox{\bf v},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{s}\bigr\rangle_{L^{2}}\Bigr)

and
\overline{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{s}=(1-d^{2}/dt^{2})^{-1}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{s}
.
It is clear that
{\cal I}
is an isomorphism of
\mathds{H}
. As for
K
, it is a compact operator, since the operator

(1-d^{2}/dt^{2})^{-1}(I_{n,p}+\hbox{\bf K}_{s}):H_{0}^{1}\rightarrow H^{1}_{0}
factors through the compact injection
H^{1}_{0}\hookrightarrow L^{2}
, and
the operator
\mathds{H}\rightarrow\mathds{H}
,

(\hbox{\bf v},B)\mapsto\bigl(B\overline{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{s}\,,\,\langle\hbox{\bf v},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{s}\rangle_{L^{2}}\bigr)
has rank one. Hence
L_{s}
is
Fredholm.

If
(\hbox{\bf v},B)
is in the kernel of
L_{s}
, then

\hbox{\bf v}=I_{n,p}(1-d^{2}/dt^{2})^{-1}(I_{n,p}+\hbox{\bf K}_{s})\hbox{\bf v}-BI_{n,p}\overline{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{s}
.
This, together with the fact that
(1-d^{2}/dt^{2})^{-1}
maps
H^{l}
into
H^{l+2}
for each
l=1,2,...
, shows that
v
is smooth.

The degenerate instants for
Q=\{Q_{s}\}
are the energy-constrained conjugate instants along
\gamma
and, according to
Proposition
6.7
, there is no such instant in
(0,\epsilon]
for
\epsilon
sufficiently small.
Therefore we can define

7.3
.

Suppose that
T
is not an energy-constrained conjugate instant along
\gamma
. The
energy-constrained spectral flow

of
\gamma
,
{\rm sf}_{\kappa}(\gamma)
, is defined as
{\rm sf}_{\kappa}(\gamma)={\rm sf}(Q,[\epsilon,T])
for
\epsilon>0
sufficiently small.

As already observed, a change of frame used to define
Q=\{Q_{s}\}
will produce a path
Q^{\prime}=\{Q_{s}^{\prime}\}
that is isometric to the former; in terms
of the self-adjoint representations
L=\{L_{s}\}
and
L^{\prime}=\{L_{s}^{\prime}\}
of
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
, this means that
L
and
L^{\prime}
are cogredient.
Since the spectral flow is invariant under cogredience, it follows that the above definition does not depend
on the choices made.

8.  Proofs of the main results

Throughout we shall suppose that
T
is neither an energy-constrained nor an ordinary
conjugate instant along
\gamma
, as well as
\epsilon>0
is such that there is no such instants in
(0,\epsilon]
.
We shall continue to assume the setting in Sec.
7
.

8.1  Computing the difference of spectral flows.

Let
{\cal V}\subset\mathds{H}
be the subspace
{\cal V}=H^{1}_{0}([0,1],{\rm I\!R}^{n})\times\{0\}
, and let
\overline{Q}=\{\overline{Q}_{s}\}
be the restrictions of
Q_{s}
to
{\cal V}
;
the form
\overline{Q}_{s}
corresponds to the usual index form of the electromagnetic geodesic
\gamma|_{[0,s]}
. As is well-known, degenerate instants for

\{\overline{Q}_{s}\}
are the ordinary conjugate instants along
\gamma
. The
spectral flow
{\rm sf}(\overline{Q},[\epsilon,T])
shall be called the
ordinary spectral flow
of
\gamma
and denoted by
{\rm sf}(\gamma)
.

The first step in the proof of Theorem
1.4
is to compute the difference
{\rm sf}_{\kappa}(\gamma)-{\rm sf}(\gamma)
. According to
Proposition
3.4
, we have

{\rm sf}_{\kappa}(\gamma)-{\rm sf}(\gamma)={\rm ind}\,Q_{\epsilon}\big|_{{\cal V}^{\perp_{Q_{\epsilon}}}}-{\rm ind}\,Q_{T}\big|_{{\cal V}^{\perp_{Q_{T}}}}.

(35)

So let us compute the above indices. First, observe that

8.1
.

Given
s\in(0,T]
, if
(\hbox{\bf v},B)\in{\cal V}^{\perp_{Q_{s}}}
then
v
is smooth.

Proof.
The proof is identical to the proof we gave for the case
(\hbox{\bf v},B)\in{\rm ker}\,Q_{s}
in Proposition
7.2
.

8.2
.

Let
s=\epsilon,T
. There is a unique ordinary Jacobi field
\check{J}^{*}_{s}
along
\gamma
such that
\check{J}^{*}_{s}(0)=0
and

\check{J}^{*}_{s}(s)=\dot{\gamma}(s)
. In terms of
\check{J}^{*}_{s}
, we have

{\rm ind}\,Q_{s}\big|_{{\cal V}^{\perp_{Q_{s}}}}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &1,\quad{\rm if}\quad g(\gamma)\left[D\check{J}^{*}_{s}/dt,\dot{\gamma}\right]\big|_{t=s}<0,\\
&0,\quad{\rm if}\quad g(\gamma)\left[D\check{J}^{*}_{s}/dt,\dot{\gamma}\right]\big|_{t=s}\geq 0.\end{aligned}\right.

Proof.
Let us suppose
s=T
, the case
s=\epsilon
being analogous. The existence and uniqueness of such
\check{J}^{*}_{T}
follows at once from the assumption
that
T
is not an ordinary conjugate instant along
\gamma
.
Also, since
{\cal V}
is one-codimensional and
\mathds{H}={\cal V}\oplus{\cal V}^{\perp_{Q_{T}}}
(Lemma
3.3
), it follows that

{\rm dim}\,{\cal V}^{\perp_{Q_{T}}}=1
. In order to describe
Q_{T}\big|_{{\cal V}^{\perp_{Q_{T}}}}
, we shall work directly with
{\cal Q}_{\kappa,\sigma}(x,T)
instead of

Q_{T}
. So let us take a non null
(V,B)
in the
{\cal Q}_{\kappa,\sigma}(x,T)-
orthogonal complement of

T_{x}\Omega_{p,q}([0,1])\times\{0\}
. From the above lemma,
V
is smooth.
By making
B_{2}=0
in Eq. (
15
), we see that
J=\hat{V}
is a solution to Eq. (
16
) with
B
in place of
B_{1}
.
By making
(V_{1},B_{1})=(V_{2},B_{2})=(V,B)
in the left-hand side of Eq. (
15
), we thus obtain

{\cal Q}_{\kappa,\sigma}(x,T)[(V,B),(V,B)]=\frac{B}{T}\left(\int_{0}^{T}g(\gamma)\bigl[J,Y[\dot{\gamma}]\bigr]dt+2\kappa B\right).

Integrating by parts as in (
18
) and using Lemma
5.1
, we arrive at

{\cal Q}_{\kappa,\sigma}(x,T)[(V,B),(V,B)]=-Bg(\gamma)\left[DJ/dt,\dot{\gamma}\right]\big|_{t=T}+(2\kappa/T)B^{2}.

(36)

Now, according to Lemma
5.6
,
\check{J}=J-(B/T)t\dot{\gamma}
is an ordinary Jacobi field. Rewriting the
right-hand side of (
36
) in terms of
\check{J}
,
we obtain

{\cal Q}_{\kappa,\sigma}(x,T)[(V,B),(V,B)]=\frac{1}{2\kappa}g(\gamma)\left[\check{J},\dot{\gamma}\right]g(\gamma)\bigl[D\check{J}/dt,\dot{\gamma}\bigr]\,\big|_{t=T},

(37)

since
\check{J}(T)=-B\dot{\gamma}(T)
(recall that
J(T)=0
). Since the pair
(V,B)
is non null, and
T
is not an ordinary conjugate instant,
we must have
B\neq 0
, otherwise
J
would be a non null ordinary Jacobi field vanishing at
t=0
and
t=T
. Thus, by dividing by
-B
, we
could have taken
(V,B)
with
B=-1
, in which case
\check{J}=\check{J}^{*}_{T}
and the right-hand side of (
37
) would be

g(\gamma)\bigl[D\check{J}^{*}_{T}/dt,\dot{\gamma}\bigr]\big|_{t=T}
. This concludes the proof.

8.2  The Lagrangian path
\ell(t)
as a symplectic reduction.

As in Sec.
, let
\Phi_{t}
be the derivative at
(p,v)
of the Hamiltonian flow

\zeta_{t}
. The vertical distribution
VTM
on
TM
, given by the tangent spaces to the fibers of

TM\rightarrow M
, is Lagrangian. By pulling it back by
\Phi_{t}
, we obtain a Lagrangian path

\check{\ell}:[0,T]\rightarrow\Lambda\bigl(T_{(p,v)}TM\bigr),\quad\check{\ell}(t)=(\Phi_{t})^{-1}V_{(\gamma,\dot{\gamma})}TM.

The previously defined Lagrangian path
\ell(t)
is nothing but the symplectic reduction of
\check{\ell}(t)
by
the coisotropic subspace
T_{(p,v)}E^{-1}(\kappa)\subset T_{(p,v)}TM
in the sense of
[
19
]
. Indeed,

\langle S\rangle
is the symplectic-orthogonal complement of
TE^{-1}(\kappa)
and we have
VE^{-1}(\kappa)=VTM\cap TE^{-1}(\kappa)
,
so that the Lagrangian subbundle
\bar{L}
of

TE^{-1}(\kappa)/\langle S\rangle
is the symplectic reduction of the Lagrangian distribution
L
along
E^{-1}(\kappa)
given by

L=\langle S\rangle+VTM\cap TE^{-1}(\kappa).

It is clear that
\check{\ell}(t)\cap\langle S_{(p,v)}\rangle=\{0\}
for all
t
since
S
is never vertical and

\Phi_{t}(S_{(p,v)})=S_{(\gamma,\dot{\gamma})}
. Therefore, we can apply Theorem 2.3 of
[
19
]
to conclude:

8.3
.

The energy-constrained Maslov index
\mu_{\kappa}(\gamma)
is equal to the Maslov index of
\check{\ell}|_{[\epsilon,T]}

with respect to the reference Lagrangian
L_{(p,v)}
.

8.3  Computation of Kashiwara indices.

Intersections of the Lagrangian path
\check{\ell}(t)
with the Maslov cycle
\Sigma\bigl(V_{(p,v)}TM\bigr)
correspond to
ordinary conjugate instants along
\gamma
; this follows from the description of
\Phi_{t}
in terms of ordinary Jacobi fields.
We shall call the Maslov index of
\check{\ell}|_{[\epsilon,T]}
with respect to the reference Lagrangian

V_{(p,v)}TM
the
ordinary Maslov index
of
\gamma
and denote it by
\mu(\gamma)
.

According to Eq. (
6
),
the difference of the Maslov indices of
\check{\ell}|_{[\epsilon,T]}
with respect to the reference Lagrangians
L_{(p,v)}
and
V_{(p,v)}TM
, respectively,
can be computed as half the difference of Kashiwara indices. Together with Proposition
8.3
, this gives us

\mu_{\kappa}(\gamma)-\mu(\gamma)=\frac{1}{2}\tau\bigl(L_{(p,v)},V_{(p,v)}TM,\check{\ell}(T)\bigr)-\frac{1}{2}\tau\bigl(L_{(p,v)},V_{(p,v)}TM,\check{\ell}(\epsilon)\bigr).

(38)

For the rest of this section, let
s=\epsilon,T
. Also, we shall omit the
s
in
\gamma(s)
and
\dot{\gamma}(s)
.

Since the Kashiwara index is invariant by symplectic transformations,
by applying
\Phi_{s}
we obtain that
\tau\bigl(L_{(p,v)},V_{(p,v)}TM,\check{\ell}(s)\bigr)=\tau\bigl(\Phi_{s}L_{(p,v)},\Phi_{s}V_{(p,v)}TM,V_{(\gamma,\dot{\gamma})}TM\bigr)
.
Let us denote the Lagrangian triplet on the right-hand side by
(L_{1},L_{2},L_{3})
. For the following, have in mind the identification (
23
).
Since
s
is not an ordinary conjugate instant along
\gamma
,
L_{2}
has null intersection with
V_{(\gamma,\dot{\gamma})}TM=\{0\}\oplus T_{\gamma}M
.
Therefore there exists a linear map
A_{s}:T_{\gamma}M\rightarrow T_{\gamma}M
such that
L_{2}=\bigl\{(u,A_{s}u)\;:\;u\in T_{\gamma}M\bigr\}
.

8.4
.

The adjoint
A_{s}^{*}
of
A_{s}
with respect to
g(\gamma)
is equal to
A_{s}-Y
.

Proof.
On one hand, for all
u_{1},u_{2}\in T_{\gamma}M
we have

\displaystyle\omega(\gamma)\bigl[(u_{1},A_{s}u_{1}),(u_{2},A_{s}u_{2})\bigr]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\gamma)\bigl[A_{s}u_{1},u_{2}\bigr]-g(\gamma)\bigl[u_{1},A_{s}u_{2}\bigr]+g(\gamma)\bigl[u_{1},Y[u_{2}]\bigr]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\gamma)\bigl[u_{1},A_{s}^{*}u_{2}-A_{s}u_{2}+Y[u_{2}]\bigr].

On the other hand, since
L_{2}
is Lagrangian, the above must be zero.

8.5
.

We have
A_{s}\dot{\gamma}-Y[\dot{\gamma}]\neq 0
and the Lagrangian subspace
L_{1}\subset T_{\gamma}M\oplus T_{\gamma}M
is given by

L_{1}=\bigl\langle(\dot{\gamma},Y[\dot{\gamma}])\bigr\rangle+\left\{(u,A_{s}u)\;:\;u\in\bigl\langle A_{s}\dot{\gamma}-Y[\dot{\gamma}]\bigr\rangle^{\perp}\right\}.

Proof.
Applying
\Phi_{s}
to the equality
L_{(p,v)}=\langle S_{(p,v)}\rangle+\{0\}\oplus\langle v\rangle^{\perp}
we obtain

L_{1}=\Phi_{s}L_{(p,v)}=\langle S_{(\gamma,\dot{\gamma})}\rangle+\Phi_{s}(\{0\}\oplus\langle v\rangle^{\perp})
. We have

S_{(\gamma,\dot{\gamma})}=(\dot{\gamma},Y[\dot{\gamma}])
and, since

\Phi_{s}(T_{p}M\oplus\langle v\rangle^{\perp})=T_{\gamma}M\oplus\langle\dot{\gamma}\rangle^{\perp}
, the subspace

\Phi_{s}(\{0\}\oplus\langle v\rangle^{\perp})
is the intersection of
L_{2}
with
T_{\gamma}M\oplus\langle\dot{\gamma}\rangle^{\perp}
, which in turn
is given by
\bigl\{(u,A_{s}u)\;:\;u\in T_{\gamma}M\;\mbox{and}\;g(\gamma)[A_{s}u,\dot{\gamma}]=0\bigr\}
. By the Lemma
8.4
, the equation

g(\gamma)[A_{s}u,\dot{\gamma}]=0
is the same as
g(\gamma)[u,A_{s}\dot{\gamma}-Y[\dot{\gamma}]]=0
.
If we had
A_{s}\dot{\gamma}-Y[\dot{\gamma}]=0
, then
L_{1}
would have dimension
n+1
, an absurd.

We have therefore an isomorphism

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\rm I\!R}\oplus\bigl\langle A_{s}\dot{\gamma}-Y[\dot{\gamma}]\bigr\rangle^{\perp}\oplus T_{\gamma}M\oplus T_{\gamma}M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}L_{1}\oplus L_{2}\oplus L_{3},\\
&(\lambda,u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})\mapsto\bigl(\lambda(\dot{\gamma},Y[\dot{\gamma}])+x_{1}\,,\,x_{2}\,,\,x_{3}\bigr),\end{aligned}\right.

where
x_{i}=(u_{i},A_{s}u_{i})
, for
i=1,2
, and
x_{3}=(0,u_{3})
. As in Sec.
2.1
, let
\mathscr{Q}
be the quadratic form corresponding to the

(L_{1},L_{2},L_{3})
and denote by
\tilde{\mathscr{Q}}
the pull-back of
\mathscr{Q}
by the above

\tilde{\mathscr{Q}}
. For
i=1,2,3

Using that
\omega(\gamma)
vanishes on

, and that

\displaystyle\omega(\gamma)\bigl[(\dot{\gamma},Y[\dot{\gamma}]),(w,A_{s}w)\bigr]

\displaystyle\;=\;g(\gamma)\bigl[Y[\dot{\gamma}],w\bigr]-g(\gamma)\bigl[\dot{\gamma},A_{s}w\bigr]+g(\gamma)\bigl[\dot{\gamma},Y[w]\bigr]

\displaystyle\;=\;-g(\gamma)\bigl[w,A_{s}\dot{\gamma}-Y[\dot{\gamma}]\bigr]

\displaystyle\forall w\in T_{\gamma}M,

a direct computation yields

\displaystyle 2\tilde{\mathscr{Q}}\bigl[(\lambda_{1},u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}),(\lambda_{2},v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})\bigr]

\displaystyle=-\lambda_{1}\left(g(\gamma)\bigl[v_{2},A_{s}\dot{\gamma}-Y[\dot{\gamma}]\bigr]-g(\gamma)[v_{3},\dot{\gamma}]\right)

(39)

\displaystyle-\lambda_{2}\left(g(\gamma)\bigl[u_{2},A_{s}\dot{\gamma}-Y[\dot{\gamma}]\bigr]-g(\gamma)[u_{3},\dot{\gamma}]\right)

\displaystyle-\left(g(\gamma)[u_{2},v_{3}]+g(\gamma)[v_{2},u_{3}]\right)+g(\gamma)[u_{3},v_{1}]+g(\gamma)[v_{3},u_{1}].

In order to introduce convenient coordinates to describe the matrix of

between the following three cases,

\displaystyle 1)

\displaystyle g(\gamma)\bigl[A_{s}\dot{\gamma}-Y[\dot{\gamma}]\,,\,A_{s}\dot{\gamma}-Y[\dot{\gamma}]\bigr]<0,

(40)

\displaystyle 2)

\displaystyle g(\gamma)\bigl[A_{s}\dot{\gamma}-Y[\dot{\gamma}]\,,\,A_{s}\dot{\gamma}-Y[\dot{\gamma}]\bigr]>0,

\displaystyle 3)

\displaystyle g(\gamma)\bigl[A_{s}\dot{\gamma}-Y[\dot{\gamma}]\,,\,A_{s}\dot{\gamma}-Y[\dot{\gamma}]\bigr]=0.

orthonormal we mean that

32

,

, …,

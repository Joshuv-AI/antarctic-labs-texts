Eccentricity energy change of complete multipartite graphs due to edge deletion
Iswar Mahato, M. Rajesh Kannan

Abstract
The eccentricity matrix ε ⁡ ( G ) \varepsilon(G) of a graph G G is obtained from the distance matrix of G G by retaining the largest distances in each row and each column, and leaving zeros in the remaining ones. The eccentricity energy of G G is sum of the absolute values of the eigenvalues of ε ⁡ ( G ) \varepsilon(G) . Although the eccentricity matrices of graphs are closely related to the distance matrices of graphs, a number of properties of eccentricity matrices are substantially different from those of the distance matrices. The change in eccentricity energy of a graph due to an edge deletion is one such property. In this article, we give examples of graphs for which the eccentricity energy increase (resp., decrease) but the distance energy decrease (resp., increase) due to an edge deletion. Also, we prove that the eccentricity energy of the complete k k -partite graph K n 1 , … , n k K_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}} with k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 and n i ≥ 2 n_{i}\geq 2 , increases due to an edge deletion.

\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\alpha_{3}\alpha_{4}=-4(5mn-14(m+n)+32).

(11)

Now
\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\alpha_{3}\alpha_{4}=-4[5mn-14(m+n)+32]=-4[9(m-2)(n-2)-4(m-1)(n-1)]<0
. Thus
p(x)
has either one or three negative roots. Suppose that

. From (

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=2(m+n-4)-\alpha_{3}-\alpha_{4}>0,\qquad as\quad\alpha_{3},\alpha_{4}<0.

In the next theorem, we establish that the eccentricity energy of a complete bipartite graph

always increases due to deletion of any edge.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
K_{m,n}
be the complete bipartite graph with

. Then for any edge

K_{m,n}
,

E_{\varepsilon}(K_{m,n})<E_{\varepsilon}(K_{m,n}-e).

Proof.

Without loss of generality, let us assume that
n\geq m\geq 2
. Let us consider the following cases:

Case 1:
Let
m=2
. For
n\geq 2
, by Lemma
2.2
, we have

Case 2:
Let
m=3
. For
n\geq
3, by Lemma
2.3
, we have

E_{\varepsilon}(K_{3,n})<E_{\varepsilon}(K_{3,n}-e).

Case 3:
Let
m\geq 4
. For
m=n=4
, by direct calculation we have

E_{\varepsilon}(K_{4,4})=24<24.84886=E_{\varepsilon}(K_{4,4}-e).

Let
n\geq 5
. By Theorem
2.2
, the
\varepsilon
-eigenvalues of
K_{m,n}-e
are
-2
with multiplicity
m+n-4
, and the roots
\alpha_{4}\leq\alpha_{3}\leq\alpha_{2}\leq\alpha_{1}
of the polynomial

p(x)=x^{4}-2(m+n-4)x^{3}+[4mn-12(m+n)+15]x^{2}+[16mn-6(m+n)-40]x-4[5mn-14(m+n)+32].

Since

, by Lemma
2.4
, we have
\alpha_{4}<-4
. Therefore,

\displaystyle E_{\varepsilon}(K_{m,n}-e)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(m+n-4)+\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\alpha_{1}\right\rvert}+\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\alpha_{2}\right\rvert}+\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\alpha_{3}\right\rvert}+\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\alpha_{4}\right\rvert}

Next, using Theorem
2.3
, we prove that the eccentricity energy of a complete
k
-partite graph increases when an edge deleted.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
K_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{k}}
be the complete
k
-partite graph such that
\sum_{i=1}^{k}n_{i}=n
and
n_{i}\geq 2
for
i=1,2,\ldots,k
. Then for any edge
e
of
K_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{k}}
,

E_{\varepsilon}(K_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{k}})<E_{\varepsilon}(K_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{k}}-e).

Proof.

Let
V_{1},V_{2},\ldots,V_{k}
be the
k
partition of the vertex set of
K_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{k}}
, where

V_{1}=\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n_{1}}\}
and
V_{i}=\Big\{v_{1+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}n_{j}},v_{2+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}n_{j}},\ldots,v_{\sum_{j=1}^{i}n_{j}}\Big\}
for
i=2,3,\ldots,k
. Let
e
be any edge in
K_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}}
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
e=v_{1}v_{n+1}
. Then,

\displaystyle\varepsilon(K_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}})=\left[{\begin{array}[]{cc}\varepsilon(K_{n_{1},n_{2}})&0\\
0&B\\
\end{array}}\right]\quad and\quad\varepsilon(K_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}}-e)=\left[{\begin{array}[]{cc}\varepsilon(K_{n_{1},n_{2}}-e)&0\\
0&B\\
\end{array}}\right],

where

B=\left[{\begin{array}[]{cccc}2(J_{n_{3}}-I_{n_{3}})&0&\ldots&0\\
0&2(J_{n_{4}}-I_{n_{4}})&\ldots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\ldots&2(J_{n_{k}}-I_{n_{k}})\\
\end{array}}\right].

By using Theorem
2.3
, we have

\displaystyle E_{\varepsilon}(K_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{\varepsilon}(K_{n_{1},n_{2}})+E_{\varepsilon}(B)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle E_{\varepsilon}(K_{n_{1},n_{2}}-e)+E_{\varepsilon}(B)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{\varepsilon}(K_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}}-e).

∎

Abstract
We propose to measure the τ − → K 1 − ​ ν τ → ( K − ​ ω ) ​ ν τ → ( K − ​ π + ​ π − ​ π 0 ) ​ ν τ \tau^{-}\to K_{1}^{-}\nu_{\tau}\to(K^{-}\omega)\nu_{\tau}\to(K^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0})\nu_{\tau}\ decay in order to determine the K 1 K_{1} axial vector mixing angle θ K 1 \theta_{K_{1}} . We derive, for the first time, the differential decay rate formula for this decay mode. Using the obtained result, we perform a sensitivity study for the Belle (II) experiment. We will show that the K − ​ π + ​ π − ​ π 0 K^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0} spectrum of the τ − → K 1 − ​ ν τ → ( K − ​ ω ) ​ ν τ → ( K − ​ π + ​ π − ​ π 0 ) ​ ν τ \tau^{-}\to K_{1}^{-}\nu_{\tau}\to(K^{-}\omega)\nu_{\tau}\to(K^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0})\nu_{\tau}\ decay can discriminate the two solutions θ K 1 = ∼ 30 ∘ \theta_{K_{1}}=\sim 30^{\circ} or ∼ 60 ∘ \sim 60^{\circ} observed in the other measurements.

Introduction

The hadronic
\tau
decay is a very useful tool to investigate the nature of the light hadrons.
The initial state being lepton allows to study the strong decays of the final state hadrons in a clean manner.
The hadrons being produced from the
W

In this article, we investigate the
\Delta S=1

\tau

We investigate the

decay to obtain the information of the
K_{1}
axial vector mesons,
K_{1}(1270)
and
K_{1}(1400)
. A better understanding of the
K_{1}
mesons is not only an interest of its own but also is highly demanded by
B

B

decays
[

,

, which are known to be sensitive to the new physics coming from the right-handed current or the CP violation, respectively, show that a more accurate information of the
K_{1}
mesons would enhance the sensitivity to the new physics.

In this article, we propose to measure the
\tau^{-}\to K_{1}^{-}\nu_{\tau}\to(K^{-}\omega)\nu_{\tau}\to(K^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0})\nu_{\tau}\
decay to determine the
\theta_{K_{1}}
angle. The

3
The axial vector mixing angle
\theta_{K_{1}}

The axial vector strange mesons have a peculiar nature, the observed physical states,
K_{1}(1270)
and
K_{1}(1400)
are the mixture of two
J^{P}=1^{+}
states,
{}^{3}P_{1}
and
{}^{1}P_{1}
.
This is different from the case of the non-strange axial vector mesons,
a_{1}(1260)
and
b_{1}(1235)
, which do not mix as the
{}^{3}P_{1}
and
{}^{1}P_{1}
states are also the eigenstates of different intrinsic charge, i.e. (
J^{PC}=1^{++},1^{+-}
).
Let us denote the unphysical
{}^{3}P_{1}
and
{}^{1}P_{1}
states as
K_{1a}
and
K_{1b}
, respectively.
Then, the the physical states (mass eigenstates) can be written as

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}K_{1}(1270)\\
K_{1}(1400)\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\sin\theta_{K_{1}}&\cos\theta_{K_{1}}\\
\cos\theta_{K_{1}}&-\sin\theta_{K_{1}}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}K_{1a}\\
K_{1b}\end{array}\right)

(21)

where
\theta_{K_{1}}
is called as the axial vector mixing angle.

Investigating the nature of the strange axial vector mesons produced from
\tau
decay, i.e. the weak interaction, has a great advantage.
The spin singlet configuration of the
s
and
u
quarks are suppressed with respect to the spin triplet one as the former is chirally forbidden and furthermore, the
SU(3)
and charge symmetry forbids the production of
K_{1b}

[
21
]
. This leads to, at the first order, that only the
K_{1a}
is produced from the weak interaction. Therefore, by defining the decay constant of
K_{1a},K_{1b}
state,

\langle K_{1a,1b}|\overline{s}\gamma^{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})u|0\rangle={-i}f_{K_{1a,1b}}{m_{K_{1a,1b}}}\epsilon^{*\mu}(k_{1}),

(22)

we have
f_{K_{1a}}\gg f_{K_{1b}}\simeq 0
.
Then, by using Eq. (
21
), the decay constant of the physical states can be given as

f_{K_{1}(1270)}=f_{K_{1a}}\sin\theta_{K_{1}},\quad f_{K_{1}(1400)}=f_{K_{1a}}\cos\theta_{K_{1}}

(23)

Since the
s
quark mass is not completely negligible with respect to the
K_{1}
masses,
f_{K_{1b}}
may not vanish. This effect can be taken into account by shifting the mixing angle by
\delta_{s}=\tan^{-1}\Big(\frac{f_{K_{1b}}}{f_{K_{1a}}}\Big)
,

f_{K_{1}(1270)}=f_{K_{1a}}\sin\theta_{K_{1}}^{\prime},\quad f_{K_{1}(1400)}=f_{K_{1a}}\cos\theta_{K_{1}}^{\prime}

(24)

where
\theta_{K_{1}}^{\prime}\equiv\theta_{K_{1}}+\delta_{s}
. We investigate maximum of 10 % of
s
quark mass effect, i.e.
|\delta_{s}|<0.1
(
6^{\circ}
), in the following.

Next, we consider the strong decay,
K_{1}\to K\omega
. We use the result of the quark model computation in
[
14
]
, where a similar process,
K_{1}\to K\rho
decay, is investigated.
Using the
SU(3)
symmetry, the
S
-wave and
D
-wave amplitudes for the
K_{1a}
and
K_{1b}
states can be written by the universal amplitudes,
S^{ABC}
and
D^{ABC}
as (see
[
14
]
for derivation)

A_{S}^{K_{1a}}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}S^{ABC},\quad A_{D}^{K_{1a}}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}D^{ABC},\quad A_{S}^{K_{1b}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}S^{ABC},\quad A_{D}^{K_{1b}}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}D^{ABC}.

(25)

Then, the amplitudes for the physical states yield

\displaystyle A_{S}^{K(1270)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{S}^{K_{1a}}\sin\theta_{K_{1}}+A_{S}^{K_{1b}}\cos\theta_{K_{1}}=S^{ABC}\sin(\theta_{K_{1}}+\theta_{0})

(26)

\displaystyle A_{D}^{K(1270)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{D}^{K_{1a}}\sin\theta_{K_{1}}+A_{D}^{K_{1b}}\cos\theta_{K_{1}}=D^{ABC}\cos(\theta_{K_{1}}+\theta_{0})

(27)

\displaystyle A_{S}^{K(1400)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{S}^{K_{1a}}\cos\theta_{K_{1}}-A_{S}^{K_{1b}}\sin\theta_{K_{1}}=-S^{ABC}\cos(\theta_{K_{1}}+\theta_{0})

(28)

\displaystyle A_{D}^{K(1400)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{D}^{K_{1a}}\cos\theta_{K_{1}}-A_{D}^{K_{1b}}\sin\theta_{K_{1}}=-D^{ABC}\sin(\theta_{K_{1}}+\theta_{0})

(29)

where
\theta_{0}=\tan^{-1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\simeq 35.26^{\circ}
. It is important to mention that we do not expect a
SU(3)
breaking effect beyond this result.
It is because the
K_{1a}
and
K_{1b}
mixing occurs via the very hadronic decays,
K_{1}\to K\omega
as well as
K\rho,K^{*}\pi
(i.e. hadronic contributions in the loop). The
SU(3)
breaking effect, which comes from the mass difference among these intermediate states, is taken into account via the non-zero
\theta_{K_{1}}
angle.

Finally, we can simplify Eq. (
20
) by using the mixing angle as

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 3|f_{K_{1a}}|^{2}\Big\{|S^{ABC}|^{2}\Big|\sin\theta_{K_{1}}^{\prime}\sin(\theta_{K_{1}}+\theta_{0}){\mathcal{BW}}_{K_{1}(1270)}+\cos\theta_{K_{1}}^{\prime}\cos(\theta_{K_{1}}+\theta_{0}){\mathcal{BW}}_{K_{1}(1400)}\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+|D^{ABC}|^{2}\Big|\sin\theta_{K_{1}}^{\prime}\cos(\theta_{K_{1}}+\theta_{0}){\mathcal{BW}}_{K_{1}(1270)}+\cos\theta_{K_{1}}^{\prime}\sin(\theta_{K_{1}}+\theta_{0}){\mathcal{BW}}_{K_{1}(1400)}\Big|^{2}\Big\}

which is our final result and will be used in the next section.
It should be emphasised that the obtained expression is different from the one proposed in
[
11
]
, where only the
\theta_{K_{1}}
dependence on the
\tau\to K_{1}\nu
decay is taken into account but not on the
K_{1}
decay.

In order to clarity the achievable limit by the Belle (II) experiment, we perform a Monte Carlo study.
The
e^{+}e^{-}\to\tau^{+}\tau^{-}
process is simulated by using the KKMC package
[
22
,
23
]
with the Belle beam energy, 8 GeV for electron and 3.5 GeV for positron.
We decay the tagging side of
\tau
by using the TAUOLA package
[
24
,
25
,
26
]
. We do not consider the spin correlation as we will use only the leptonic decay (
e
or
\mu
) on the tagging side, which reduces significantly the
q\bar{q}
background. For the signal side, we use the differential decay rate formulae derived in this article to generate the
\tau^{-}\to K_{1}^{-}\nu_{\tau}\to(K^{-}\omega)\nu_{\tau}\to(K^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0})\nu_{\tau}\
decay distribution.

The main background comes from
\tau\to 3\pi\pi^{0}\nu
decay, where
3\pi
does not necessarily come from
\omega
but all possible intermediate states, such as
a_{1}\pi,\rho\rho,\cdots
. Thus, we select the four charged tracks with no net charge and evaluate the thrust axis. Here, the ‘good’ charged tracks are defined as
dr<5mm,|dz|<5cm,p_{t}>100{\rm\ MeV}
and ‘good’ gamma is the one with
E_{\gamma}>50
MeV within the detector fiducial volume. We select the events which have three charged tracks parallel to the thrust axis (signal side) and one anti-parallel (tag side). The signal side should contain two
\gamma
with
120{{\rm\ MeV}}<M_{\gamma\gamma}<150{\rm\ MeV}
and
\pi\pi\gamma\gamma
in the
\omega
mass region,
760{\rm\ MeV}<M_{\pi\pi\gamma\gamma}<800{\rm\ MeV}
. We select only those
\gamma\gamma
in the barrel region to avoid the background. For the simplicity, we ignore the multi-candidate case: if more than two sets of
\gamma
satisfy the above condition, we reject such events (the fraction is around a few percents). The charged track which does not construct
\omega
is considered to be kaon. For the detection efficiency computation, we use the Belle detector simulation with the improved kaon identification (ID). That is, we assume kaon ID of Belle II, 90 % for kaon ID and 4% for
\pi
fake rate for kaon ID
[
1
]
, which is about twice better than Belle.

We found that the detection efficiency is 1-2%, which results in
\sim
10k event for each
\sim
1 ab
-1
of data. Thus, this amount of data is already available in the Belle experiment. We use 15k event as a benchmark experimental setup in the following analysis.

Next, using the generated events, we fit the the
\theta_{K_{1}}
angle. With the 15k of events, we find the statistical error to be

\displaystyle\sigma_{(\theta_{K_{1}}=15^{\circ})}=0.3^{\circ},\quad\sigma_{(\theta_{K_{1}}=30^{\circ})}=0.2^{\circ},

(35)

\displaystyle\sigma_{(\theta_{K_{1}}=45^{\circ})}=0.4^{\circ},\quad\sigma_{(\theta_{K_{1}}=60^{\circ})}=1.1^{\circ},\quad\sigma_{(\theta_{K_{1}}=75^{\circ})}=1.9^{\circ}

The very small errors estimated with the amount of data which will be soon available, are very encouraging. Thus, we further investigate the various systematic uncertainties. This is particularly important as the
K\pi\pi\pi
invariant mass distributions for
\theta_{K_{1}}\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\;>$\kern-7.5pt\raise-4.73611pt\hbox{$\sim\;$}}40^{\circ}
seem to be very difficult to distinguish and the systematic effect could dominate. To evaluate the experimental systematic error is beyond the scope of this article while we investigate the systematic errors coming from the input parameters in the following.

As mentioned earlier, since our goal is to determine the mixing angle and not the total branching ratio, the overall factors do not induce an uncertainty.
The most uncertain input parameters are the mass and width of the
K_{1}(1270)
resonance in Eq. (
31
) as well as the
S
-wave and
D
-wave amplitudes in Eq. (
34
). Both induce the similar kinds of uncertainties in the line shape of the
K_{1}
resonances. To identify the line shape of the
K_{1}
resonance is a long-standing challenge: the dominant decay channel
K_{1}(1270)\to\rho K
has no phase space at the pole mass, which distorts the line shape
[
27
]
. This issue is investigated intensively in
[
14
]
using the kaon beam experiment data
[
28
]
. Our prescription, to take into account the line shape ambiguity, here is that we free the mass and width of
K_{1}(1270)
while fitting the
\theta_{K_{1}}
. We emphasise that this prescription can accommodate not only the mass and width uncertainties but also the uncertainties induced by the model parameters, the
S
-wave and
D
-wave amplitudes.
Our result for 15k event yields,

\displaystyle\sigma_{(\theta_{K_{1}}=15^{\circ})}=1.3^{\circ},\quad\sigma_{(\theta_{K_{1}}=30^{\circ})}=1.4^{\circ},

(36)

\displaystyle\sigma_{(\theta_{K_{1}}=45^{\circ})}=1.3^{\circ},\quad\sigma_{(\theta_{K_{1}}=60^{\circ})}=2.6^{\circ},\quad\sigma_{(\theta_{K_{1}}=75^{\circ})}=8.2^{\circ}

The fitted mass and width are well within their uncertainties, i.e.
(1.270\pm 0.006){\rm\ GeV}
and
(0.090\pm 0.013){\rm\ GeV}
, respectively. This clearly shows the difficulty of determining the
\theta_{K_{1}}

\sim 45^{\circ}
. However, it is quite faire to say that the
\tau^{-}\to K_{1}^{-}\nu_{\tau}\to(K^{-}\omega)\nu_{\tau}\to(K^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0})\nu_{\tau}\
decay has certainly an ability to discriminate the two solutions,
\theta_{K_{1}}\sim 30^{\circ}
and
\theta_{K_{1}}\sim 60^{\circ}
, obtained by the other experiments.

\delta_{s}
and measure the shift of the

value. In order to estimate the maximum effect, we vary
\delta_{s}
maximally, i.e.
\delta_{s}=-6^{\circ}(+6^{\circ})

The obtained results are (mass and width are fitted simultaneously)

\displaystyle\Delta{\theta_{K_{1}}}_{(\theta_{K_{1}}=15^{\circ})}=+3.8^{\circ}(-3.7^{\circ}),\quad\Delta{\theta_{K_{1}}}_{(\theta_{K_{1}}=30^{\circ})}=+2.8^{\circ}(-2.8^{\circ}),

\displaystyle\Delta{\theta_{K_{1}}}_{(\theta_{K_{1}}=75^{\circ})}=-7.7^{\circ}(-3.5^{\circ})

The results show that the positive (negative)
\delta_{s}
leads to a positive (negative) shift of the mixing angle for
\theta_{K_{1}}\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\;<$\kern-7.5pt\raise-4.73611pt\hbox{$\sim\;$}}45^{\circ}

For 60
∘

\theta_{K_{1}}

The bottom line is, even after taking into account the systematic error coming from the

\theta_{K_{1}}\sim 30^{\circ}
and
\theta_{K_{1}}\sim 60^{\circ}

5
Conclusions

In this article, we proposed to measure the
\tau^{-}\to K_{1}^{-}\nu_{\tau}\to(K^{-}\omega)\nu_{\tau}\to(K^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0})\nu_{\tau}\
decay to determine the axial vector mixing angle
\theta_{K_{1}}
.
We first derived the
\tau^{-}\to K_{1}^{-}\nu_{\tau}\to(K^{-}\omega)\nu_{\tau}\to(K^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0})\nu_{\tau}\
differential decay rate formula in order to understand the
\theta_{K_{1}}
dependence of the
K\pi\pi\pi
spectrum. The theoretical formula for this five body differential decay rate is obtained for the first time in this article.
Using the obtained result, we performed a sensitivity study for determining the
\theta_{K_{1}}
angle by assuming the Belle (II) experiment environment. The
K\pi\pi\pi
spectrum contains two
K_{1}
resonances,
K_{1}(1270)
and
K_{1}(1400)
. The contribution from
K_{1}(1400)
diminishes as the
\theta_{K_{1}}
value increases. As a result, for a larger values of
\theta_{K_{1}}
, let’s say above
\sim 45^{\circ}
, the
K_{1}(1400)
resonance becomes nearly invisible, which makes it difficult to distinguish the spectrums with different values of
\theta_{K_{1}}
in this range. More quantitatively, the expected statistical errors for 15k event are

\sigma_{\theta_{K_{1}}}=\{\pm 1.3^{\circ},\pm 1.4^{\circ},\pm 1.3^{\circ},\pm 2.6^{\circ},\pm 8.2^{\circ}\}
for
\theta_{K_{1}}=\{15^{\circ},30^{\circ},45^{\circ},60^{\circ},75^{\circ}\}
.
This amount of data will be very soon available at the Belle (II) experiment.

We also discussed a possible correction to this result due to the
SU(3)
breaking effect, which is related to the production of
K_{1b}
(
{}^{1}P_{1}
) state from the axial vector current. The existence of this contribution is not confirmed and we urge a theoretical progress on this matter. In order to evaluate its possible impact, we included the maximum of
\pm
10%
SU(3)
breaking effect. The result shows that it can shift the measurement of
\theta_{K_{1}}
by
\Delta\theta_{K_{1}}=\{\pm 3.8^{\circ},\pm 2.8^{\circ},\pm 1.4^{\circ}\}
for
\theta_{K_{1}}=\{15^{\circ},30^{\circ},45^{\circ}\}
. We find that the statistical error dominates in the case of the higher values of
\theta_{K_{1}}
, i.e.
\theta_{K_{1}}=60^{\circ}{\rm\ and\ }75^{\circ}
.

The other experiments found the
\theta_{K_{1}}
angle to be
\sim 30^{\circ}
or
\sim 60^{\circ}
and to eliminate one of the solutions is a very important matter. For these values of
\theta_{K_{1}}
, the
\tau^{-}\to K_{1}^{-}\nu_{\tau}\to(K^{-}\omega)\nu_{\tau}\to(K^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0})\nu_{\tau}\
can determine
\theta_{K_{1}}
at the precision of

\delta{\theta_{K_{1}}}=\pm 3.1^{\circ}\quad{\rm(for\ \theta_{K_{1}}=30^{\circ})},\quad\delta{\theta_{K_{1}}}=\pm 5.4^{\circ}\quad{\rm(for\ \theta_{K_{1}}=60^{\circ})}

where the statistical uncertainty with 15k event at Belle (II) and the systematic uncertainty from 10 %
SU(3)
breaking effect are added by quadrature. Therefore, we conclude that the
\tau^{-}\to K_{1}^{-}\nu_{\tau}\to(K^{-}\omega)\nu_{\tau}\to(K^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0})\nu_{\tau}\
measurement can discriminate the two solutions for the
\theta_{K_{1}}
angle obtained by the other experiments and determine it at this level of precision.

Yang-Lee Zeros, Semicircle Theorem, and Nonunitary Criticality in Bardeen-Cooper-Schrieffer Superconductivity
Hongchao Li, Xie-Hang Yu, Masaya Nakagawa, Masahito Ueda

Abstract
Yang and Lee investigated phase transitions in terms of zeros of partition functions, namely, Yang-Lee zeros [ Phys. Rev. 87 , 404 (1952) ; Phys. Rev. 87 , 410 (1952) ]. We show that the essential singularity in the superconducting gap is directly related to the number of roots of the partition function of a BCS superconductor. Those zeros are found to be distributed on a semicircle in the complex plane of the interaction strength due to the Fermi-surface instability. A renormalization-group analysis shows that the semicircle theorem holds for a generic quantum many-body system with a marginal coupling, in sharp contrast with the Lee-Yang circle theorem for the Ising spin system. This indicates that the geometry of Yang-Lee zeros is directly connected to the Fermi-surface instability. Furthermore, we unveil the nonunitary criticality in BCS superconductivity that emerges at each individual Yang-Lee zero due to exceptional points and presents a universality class distinct from that of the conventional Yang-Lee edge singularity.

Yang and Lee developed a general approach to understanding phase transitions in terms of zeros, known as Yang-Lee zeros, of the partition function

In this Letter, we develop the Yang-Lee theory of BCS superconductivity to elucidate the nonperturbative nature of the superconducting phase transition in terms of the distribution of zeros of the partition function. We show that the number of roots of the partition function is directly related to the superconducting gap induced by the Fermi-surface instability. In particular, we demonstrate that the Yang-Lee zeros of the partition function are distributed on a semicircle in the complex plane of the interaction strength, where the superconducting phase transition occurs at the edge of the distribution.
A previous study

on Fisher zeros in pairing fields focuses on a finite-temperature phase transition by making the temperature complex. In contrast, we focus on the zero-temperature quantum phase transition by making the interaction strength complex.

Furthermore, we employ a renormalization group (RG) to investigate the
universality of the distribution of Yang-Lee zeros
for a generic quantum many-body system with a marginal coupling. In particular, we show the semicircle theorem: Yang-Lee zeros in a quantum many-body system with a marginally relevant coupling are distributed on a semicircle

theorem

Lee and Yang 1952

Last, we investigate the nonunitary criticality in BCS superconductivity which originates from the Yang-Lee singularity. By determining the critical exponents, we show that the nonunitary singularity belongs to a universality

Semicircle theorem
.—Here, we show that the semicircular
distribution (
6
) of the Yang-Lee zeros is generic and universal in quantum many-body systems.
We consider a general canonical RG equation for a marginal
complex interaction:

\frac{dV}{dt}=aV^{2}+bV^{3},

(10)

where

—The
Yang-Lee zeros in the complex plane are accompanied by
nonunitary critical phenomena in BCS superconductivity
with a complex-valued interaction.
Remarkably, the criticality in the BCS model arises at every point on the phase boundary rather than at the edges alone as in the Ising model.

Conclusion
.—We have investigated the Yang-Lee
zeros in BCS superconductivity and found that the Yang-Lee zeros are distributed
on the semicircular phase boundary in the complex plane of the interaction
strength. We find that the nonperturbative nature of the order parameter and thermodynamic quantities are directly connected to the number of roots of the partition function, which allows us to understand superconducting quantum phase transitions from the analytic property of the partition function. We have performed the RG analysis
of generic many-body fermionic systems with marginal interactions and shown that the semicircle distribution of Yang-Lee zeros is a universal phenomenon in Fermi systems. We have also explored the Yang-Lee critical behavior and obtained critical exponents of the nonunitary criticality.

The Yang-Lee zeros and the corresponding singularity studied in this
Letter are not only an interesting mathematical property but can also be tested experimentally. In fact, the non-Hermitian BCS model can be realized in
open quantum systems
Yamamoto et al. 2019
;
Iskin 2021
. The
complex-valued interaction strength describes the effect of two-body
loss in ultracold atoms. For example, inelastic two-body losses can
be induced by utilizing Feshbach resonances
Pagano et al. 2015
;
Höfer et al. 2015
;
Zhang et al. 2015

or photoassociation
Tomita et al. 2017
;
Honda et al. 2023
.
Since the dissipation in these cases only involves atomic loss, the eigenvalue spectrum and the exceptional points of the Lindblad equation including the jump terms are the same as those of the corresponding non-Hermitian Hamiltonian regardless of the jump terms
Nakagawa et al. 2021
. Hence, we believe the nonunitary critical phenomena introduced in this Letter should also be observed in open quantum systems.

While we have focused on the quantum phase transition, it is worthwhile
to investigate how the Yang-Lee singularity is connected to a superconducting
phase transition at finite temperature. We also expect that Yang-Lee
zeros can emerge in other non-Hermitian many-body systems such as
a non-Hermitian Bose-Hubbard model
Ashida et al. 2016
.

Yang-Lee Zeros on the Phase Boundary

We begin from considering the three-dimensional non-Hermitian BCS Hamiltonian

H=\sum_{\bm{k}\sigma}\xi_{\bm{k}}c_{\bm{k}\sigma}^{\dagger}c_{\bm{k}\sigma}-\frac{U}{N}\sum_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}{}^{{}^{\prime}}c_{\bm{k}\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{\bm{-k}\downarrow}^{\dagger}c_{\bm{-k}^{\prime}\downarrow}c_{\bm{k}^{\prime}\uparrow},

\bm{k}
restricted to

\omega_{D}
being the cutoff energy. The mean-field Hamiltonian is given by

\bar{\Delta}_{0}=-\frac{U}{N}\sum_{\bm{k}L}\langle c^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}\uparrow}c^{\dagger}_{-\bm{k}\downarrow}\rangle_{\mathrm{R}}

\displaystyle|\text{BCS}\rangle_{R}

\displaystyle=\prod_{\bm{k}}(u_{\bm{k}}+v_{\bm{k}}c_{\bm{k}\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{-\bm{k}\downarrow}^{\dagger})|0\rangle,

\displaystyle|\text{BCS}\rangle_{L}

The solution to the gap equation is given by

\displaystyle\Delta_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\omega_{D}}{\text{exp}\left[\frac{1}{\rho_{0}|U|^{2}}\left(U_{R}-iU_{I}\right)\right]-\text{exp}\left[-\frac{1}{\rho_{0}|U|^{2}}\left(U_{R}-iU_{I}\right)\right]}

\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{D}}{\text{sinh}\left(\frac{U_{R}}{\rho_{0}|U|^{2}}\right)\cos\left(\frac{U_{I}}{\rho_{0}|U|^{2}}\right)-i\text{cosh}\left(\frac{U_{R}}{\rho_{0}|U|^{2}}\right)\sin\left(\frac{U_{I}}{\rho_{0}|U|^{2}}\right)},

(S.9)

At the quantum phase transition point, the real part of the gap vanishes, which gives

(S.10)

This condition restricts the imaginary part of the gap

\displaystyle\text{Im}[\Delta_{0}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{D}\frac{\text{cosh}\left(\frac{U_{R}}{\rho_{0}|U|^{2}}\right)\sin\left(\frac{U_{I}}{\rho_{0}|U|^{2}}\right)}{\left(\text{sinh}\left(\frac{U_{R}}{\rho_{0}|U|^{2}}\right)\cos\left(\frac{U_{I}}{\rho_{0}|U|^{2}}\right)\right)^{2}+\left(\text{cosh}\left(\frac{U_{R}}{\rho_{0}|U|^{2}}\right)\sin\left(\frac{U_{I}}{\rho_{0}|U|^{2}}\right)\right)^{2}}

(S.11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{D}}{\text{cosh}\left(\frac{U_{R}}{\rho_{0}|U|^{2}}\right)}\,.

Since we only consider the case with a large

F_{1}(x)

F_{1}(\bm{x})=\text{Im}\Delta_{0}\text{Re}\left[\int_{0}^{k}dt\frac{t\sin(\frac{\text{Im}\Delta_{0}x}{v_{F}}t)}{\sqrt{t^{2}-1}}\right]\simeq\text{Im}\Delta_{0}\int_{1}^{\infty}dt\frac{t\sin(\frac{\text{Im}\Delta_{0}x}{v_{F}}t)}{\sqrt{t^{2}-1}}.

(S.47)

To calculate this function, we introduce another function
G_{1}(x)
as

G_{1}(x):=\text{Im}\Delta_{0}\int_{1}^{\infty}dt\frac{\cos(\frac{\text{Im}\Delta_{0}x}{v_{F}}t)}{\sqrt{t^{2}-1}}=-\text{Im}\Delta_{0}\frac{\pi}{2}N_{0}\left(\frac{\text{Im}\Delta_{0}}{v_{F}}x\right),

(S.48)

where
N_{0}
is the 0-th order Bessel function of the second kind. The relationship between these two functions is

F_{1}(x)\simeq-\frac{v_{F}}{\text{Im}\Delta_{0}}G_{1}^{\prime}(x)\,.

(S.49)

Therefore, the real part of the correlation function is

\mathrm{Re}[C(\bm{x})]\simeq\frac{\pi}{2}\rho_{0}v_{F}\frac{\cos(k_{F}x)}{k_{F}x}N_{0}^{\prime}\left(\frac{\text{Im}\Delta_{0}}{v_{F}}x\right)\,.

(S.50)

When we take the limit
x\rightarrow\infty
, we have

\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}N_{0}^{\prime}(x)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\cos\left(x-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)\frac{1}{x^{1/2}}-\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{1}{2}\sin\left(x-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)\frac{1}{x^{3/2}}\simeq\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\cos\left(x-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)\frac{1}{x^{1/2}}\,.

(S.51)

Thus, the real part of the correlation function has the long-range behavior as

\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\left[\frac{\pi}{2}\rho_{0}v_{F}\frac{\cos(k_{F}x)}{k_{F}x}N_{0}^{\prime}\left(\frac{\text{Im}\Delta_{0}}{v_{F}}x\right)\right]\sim\frac{1}{x^{3/2}}.

(S.52)

Then we turn to consider the function
F_{2}(x)
as the imaginary part of the correlation function. On the phase boundary, we can rewrite it as

F_{2}(\bm{x})=\text{Im}\Delta_{0}\text{Im}\left[\int_{0}^{k}dt\frac{t\sin(\frac{\text{Im}\Delta_{0}x}{v_{F}}t)}{\sqrt{t^{2}-1}}\right]=-\text{Im}\Delta_{0}\int_{0}^{1}dt\frac{t\sin(\frac{\text{Im}\Delta_{0}x}{v_{F}}t)}{\sqrt{t^{2}-1}}

(S.53)

Similarly, we can also define another function
G_{2}(x)
as

G_{2}(x)=-\text{Im}\Delta_{0}\int_{0}^{1}dt\frac{\cos(\frac{\text{Im}\Delta_{0}x}{v_{F}}t)}{\sqrt{t^{2}-1}}=\frac{\pi}{2}J_{0}\left(\frac{\text{Im}\Delta_{0}}{v_{F}}x\right),

(S.54)

where
J_{0}
is the 0-th order Bessel function of the first kind. The relationship between these two functions is also given by

F_{2}(x)=-v_{F}G_{2}^{\prime}(x).

(S.55)

Then the imaginary part of the correlation function is equivalent to

\mathrm{Im}[C(\bm{x})]\simeq\frac{\pi}{2}\rho_{0}v_{F}\frac{\cos(k_{F}x)}{k_{F}x}J_{0}^{\prime}\left(\frac{\text{Im}\Delta_{0}}{v_{F}}x\right)\,.

(S.56)

Thus, we have a similar long-range behavior for the imaginary part of the correlation function as

\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\left[\frac{\pi}{2}\rho_{0}v_{F}\frac{\cos(k_{F}x)}{k_{F}x}J_{0}^{\prime}\left(\frac{\text{Im}\Delta_{0}}{v_{F}}x\right)\right]\sim\frac{1}{x^{3/2}}\,.

(S.57)

To summarize, the correlation function takes the form of

\displaystyle\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}{}_{L}\left\langle\text{BCS}\right|c_{\sigma}^{\dagger}(\bm{x})c_{\sigma}(\bm{x})\left|\text{BCS}\right\rangle_{R}

\displaystyle\simeq\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\pi}{2}\rho_{0}v_{F}\frac{\cos(k_{F}x)}{k_{F}x}(N_{0}^{\prime}(\frac{\text{Im}\Delta_{0}}{v_{F}}x)+iJ_{0}^{\prime}(\frac{\text{Im}\Delta_{0}}{v_{F}}x))

\displaystyle=:(A(l)+iB(l))x^{-3/2}\,,

(S.58)

where

\displaystyle A(l)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\rho_{0}\frac{\sqrt{\text{Im}\Delta_{0}v_{F}}}{k_{F}}\cos(k_{F}\frac{v_{F}}{\text{Im}\Delta_{0}}l)\cos(l-\frac{\pi}{4})\,,

\displaystyle B(l)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\rho_{0}\frac{\sqrt{\text{Im}\Delta_{0}v_{F}}}{k_{F}}\cos(k_{F}\frac{v_{F}}{\text{Im}\Delta_{0}}l)\sin(l-\frac{\pi}{4})\,,

(S.59)

and
l=\frac{\text{Im}\Delta_{0}}{v_{F}}x
. The anomalous dimension is defined by

Sachdev 2011

Appendix S5
Thermodynamic Quantities on the Phase Boundary

In this section, we calculate critical exponents associated with non-analyticity of thermodynamic quantities on the phase boundary. The condensation energy of the non-Hermitian BCS model is given by
Yamamoto et al. 2019

\Delta E=-\frac{N}{U_{R}+iU_{I}}\left(\text{Im}\Delta_{0}\right)^{2}-N\int_{-\omega_{D}}^{\omega_{D}}d\xi_{\bm{k}}\rho_{0}\left(\sqrt{\xi_{\bm{k}}^{2}+\Delta_{0}^{2}}-|\xi_{\bm{k}}|\right)\,.

(S.70)

Note that here we have subtracted the energy of non-interacting fermions from the energy (
S.70
). The integration is separated into two parts. The second term of the integral is given by

2\rho_{0}\int_{0}^{\omega_{D}}d\xi_{\bm{k}}\xi_{\bm{k}}=\rho_{0}\omega_{D}^{2}\,.

(S.71)

The first term on the phase boundary is given by

-2\int_{0}^{\omega_{D}}d\xi_{\bm{k}}\rho_{0}\sqrt{\xi_{\bm{k}}^{2}+\Delta_{0}^{2}}=-2\int_{0}^{\omega_{D}}d\xi_{\bm{k}}\rho_{0}\sqrt{\xi_{\bm{k}}^{2}-\left(\text{Im}\Delta_{0}\right)^{2}}.

We first focus on the real part of the energy. Since

\displaystyle\text{Re}\left[-2\int_{0}^{\omega_{D}}d\xi_{\bm{k}}\rho_{0}\sqrt{\xi_{\bm{k}}^{2}-\left(\text{Im}\Delta_{0}\right)^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\int_{\text{Im}\Delta_{0}}^{\omega_{D}}d\xi_{\bm{k}}\rho_{0}\sqrt{\xi_{\bm{k}}^{2}-\left(\text{Im}\Delta_{0}\right)^{2}}

(S.72)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-2\rho_{0})\left(\text{Im}\Delta_{0}\right)^{2}\left[\frac{1}{4}\text{sinh}\left(\frac{2U_{R}}{\rho_{0}|U|^{2}}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\frac{U_{R}}{\rho_{0}|U|^{2}}\right]\,,

the real part of the energy is

\displaystyle\text{Re}[\Delta E]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-N\frac{U_{R}}{|U|^{2}}\left(\text{Im}\Delta_{0}\right)^{2}-2N\rho_{0}\left(\text{Im}\Delta_{0}\right)^{2}\left[\frac{1}{4}\text{sinh}\left(\frac{2U_{R}}{\rho_{0}|U|^{2}}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\frac{U_{R}}{\rho_{0}|U|^{2}}\right]+N\rho_{0}\omega_{D}^{2}

(S.73)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N\rho_{0}\left[\omega_{D}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\text{Im}\Delta_{0}\right)^{2}\text{sinh}\left(\frac{2U_{R}}{\rho_{0}|U|^{2}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N\rho_{0}\omega_{D}^{2}\left[1-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\text{sinh}\left(\frac{2U_{R}}{\rho_{0}|U|^{2}}\right)}{\text{cosh}^{2}\left(\frac{U_{R}}{\rho_{0}|U|^{2}}\right)}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N\rho_{0}\omega_{D}^{2}\left[1-\tanh\left(\frac{U_{R}}{\rho_{0}|U|^{2}}\right)\right]\,.

In a similar manner, we calculate the imaginary part of the condensation energy on the phase boundary as

\displaystyle\text{Im}[\Delta E]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{N}{|U|^{2}}\left(-U_{I}\right)\left(\text{Im}\Delta_{0}\right)^{2}-2N\rho_{0}\int_{0}^{\text{Im}\Delta_{0}}d\xi_{\bm{k}}\sqrt{\xi_{\bm{k}}^{2}-\left(\text{Im}\Delta_{0}\right)^{2}}

(S.74)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2U_{I}N}{2|U|^{2}}\left(\text{Im}\Delta_{0}\right)^{2}-2N\rho_{0}\left(\text{Im}\Delta_{0}\right)^{2}\times\frac{\pi}{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{N}{2}\rho_{0}\left(\text{Im}\Delta_{0}\right)^{2}\left[\frac{2U_{I}}{\rho_{0}|U|^{2}}-\pi\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\,.

Thus, the imaginary part of the condensation energy vanishes on the phase boundary.

We here relate the number of roots
\chi
of the partition function with the nonanalyticity of the condensation energy. According to the definition of
\chi
in the main text, we have

\chi/\beta\simeq\frac{\omega_{D}}{\pi\text{cosh}(\frac{U_{R}}{\rho_{0}|U|^{2}})}

(S.75)

in the zero-temperature limit. Using Eq. (
S.73
), we find

\Delta E=N\rho_{0}\omega_{D}^{2}(1-\sqrt{1-(\frac{\pi\chi}{\beta\omega_{D}})^{2}})\,.

(S.76)

Since
\frac{U_{R}}{\rho_{0}|U|^{2}}\rightarrow\infty
near
U=0
, the expressions for the condensation energy and the number of roots can be simplified as

\Delta E=2N\rho_{0}\omega_{D}^{2}e^{-2\frac{U_{R}}{\rho_{0}|U|^{2}}},\chi/\beta=\frac{2\omega_{D}}{\pi}e^{-\frac{U_{R}}{\rho_{0}|U|^{2}}}\,.

(S.77)

Thus, we have

\Delta E=\frac{\pi^{2}N\rho_{0}}{2\beta^{2}}\chi^{2}\propto(\chi/\beta)^{2}\,

(S.78)

near the origin, which means that condensation energy can be related to the number of roots
\chi
. Similarly, the condensation energy on the real axis is given by

\Delta E=-2N\rho_{0}\omega_{D}^{2}e^{-\frac{2}{\rho_{0}U_{R}}}.

(S.79)

Hence, we have
\Delta E=-\frac{\pi^{2}N\rho_{0}}{2\beta^{2}}\chi^{2}\propto(\chi/\beta)^{2}
near the origin. This tells us that the information of condensation energy on the real axis can be read from the number of roots on the complex plane.

Next, we show that the compressibility exhibits critical behavior near the phase boundary. The compressibility is defined by
\kappa=\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial\mu^{2}}
where
\xi_{\bm{k}}=\epsilon_{\bm{k}}-\mu
and
F=-\frac{1}{\beta}\text{log}Z
is the free energy of the Bogoliubov quasiparticles. We have

\kappa=-\sum_{\bm{k}}\frac{\Delta_{\bm{k}}^{2}}{(\xi_{\bm{k}}^{2}+\Delta_{\bm{k}}^{2})^{3/2}}=-N\int_{-\omega_{D}}^{\omega_{D}}\rho_{0}d\xi_{\bm{k}}\frac{\Delta_{0}^{2}}{(\xi_{\bm{k}}^{2}+\Delta_{0}^{2})^{3/2}}\,.

(S.80)

Firstly, we consider the compressibility at points on the phase boundary. By substituting the gap in Eqs. (
S.9
) and (
S.11
) into the compressibility (
S.80
), we can rewrite it as

\kappa=N\int_{-\omega_{D}}^{\omega_{D}}\rho_{0}d\xi_{\bm{k}}\frac{(\text{Im}\Delta_{0})^{2}}{(\xi_{\bm{k}}^{2}-(\text{Im}\Delta_{0})^{2})^{3/2}}\,.

(S.81)

This integral diverges since
\text{Im}\Delta_{0}<\omega_{D}
for all the points on the boundary. Hence, the compressibility exhibits singularity at each point on the boundary. This cannot occur in the Hermitian case since the integral in Eq. (
S.80
) is finite for a real gap
\Delta_{0}
. Near the phase boundary with an infinitesimal deviation
\delta U_{R}
, we obtain

\kappa\simeq N\int_{-\omega_{D}}^{\omega_{D}}\rho_{0}d\xi_{\bm{k}}\frac{(\text{Im}\Delta_{0})^{2}}{(\xi_{\bm{k}}^{2}-(\text{Im}\Delta_{0})^{2}+2i\text{Re}\Delta_{0}\text{Im}\Delta_{0})^{3/2}}=N\int_{-A}^{A}\rho_{0}ds\frac{1}{(s^{2}-1+2i\text{Re}\Delta_{0}/\text{Im}\Delta_{0})^{3/2}}\,,

(S.82)

where
A:=\cosh\left(\frac{U_{R}}{\rho_{0}|U|^{2}}\right)
. Since the points near the value
s=1
dominantly contribute to the integral, we expand the integral around this point as

\int_{0}^{c}\rho_{0}d\delta s\frac{1}{(2\delta s+2ia)^{3/2}}=-[\frac{1}{\sqrt{2ia+c}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2ia}}],

(S.83)

where
c
is a positive constant. Under the limit
a\to 0
, the integral is proportional to
a^{-1/2}
. Hence, we obtain the critical exponent
\zeta=1/2
, which is defined as
\kappa\sim(\delta U)^{-\zeta}
near the phase boundary.

We note that the critical exponents
\eta
and
\zeta
are not independent. Here we show the relation between
\eta
and
\zeta
for the energy spectrum that can be expanded as
(k-k_{E})^{1/n}
around a gapless point
k=k_{E}
. When
n
is an integer, such dispersion relation appears near an
n
-th order exceptional point in non-Hermitian systems
Kato 1995
. For those energy spectra, the long-range behavior of the correlation function takes the form as

C(\bm{x})\propto x^{-2+1/n}\,.

(S.84)

From the definition of the anomalous dimension, we find
\eta=1-\frac{1}{n}
. Similarly, we find that the critical behavior of the compressibility near the phase boundary is

\kappa\propto\int_{-A}^{A}\rho_{0}d\xi_{\bm{k}}\frac{1}{(\xi_{\bm{k}}^{2}-1+2i\text{Re}\Delta_{0}/\text{Im}\Delta_{0})^{2-\frac{1}{n}}}\propto(\delta U)^{-1+\frac{1}{n}}\,,

(S.85)

which indicates that
\zeta=1-1/n
. Thus, we have

\eta=\zeta\,.

(S.86)

Finally, we discuss the dynamical critical exponent
z
. From the definition of dynamical critical exponent
z
in Ref.
Sachdev 2011
, we here define it as

\text{Re}\Delta_{0}\propto\xi^{-z}.

(S.87)

By referencing (
S.60
) and (
S.66
), we obtain

Appendix S6
Pair Correlation Function

The pair correlation function is defined by

\rho_{2}(\bm{r}_{1}\sigma_{1},\bm{r}_{2}\sigma_{2};\bm{r}_{1}^{\prime}\sigma_{1}^{\prime},\bm{r}_{2}^{\prime}\sigma_{2}^{\prime}):={}_{L}\langle c_{\sigma_{1}}^{\dagger}(\bm{r}_{1})c_{\sigma_{2}}^{\dagger}(\bm{r}_{2})c_{\sigma_{2}^{\prime}}(\bm{r}_{2}^{\prime})c_{\sigma_{1}^{\prime}}(\bm{r}_{1}^{\prime})\rangle_{R}\,.

(S.89)

Here we set
\bm{r}_{1}=\bm{r}_{2}=\bm{R}
and
\bm{r}_{1}^{\prime}=\bm{r}_{2}^{\prime}=0
to consider the correlation between two Cooper pairs. Without loss of generality, we assume
\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{1}^{\prime}=\uparrow,\sigma_{2}=\sigma_{2}^{\prime}=\downarrow
. With Wick’s theorem, we can simplify the pair correlation function as

\displaystyle\rho_{2}(\bm{R}\uparrow,\bm{R}\downarrow;0\uparrow,0\downarrow)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{}_{L}\langle c_{\uparrow}^{\dagger}(\bm{R})c_{\downarrow}^{\dagger}(\bm{R})c_{\downarrow}(0)c_{\uparrow}(0)\rangle_{R}

(S.90)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{}_{L}\langle c_{\uparrow}^{\dagger}(\bm{R})c_{\downarrow}^{\dagger}(\bm{R})\rangle_{R}{}_{L}\langle c_{\downarrow}(0)c_{\uparrow}(0)\rangle_{R}+{}_{L}\langle c_{\uparrow}^{\dagger}(\bm{R})c_{\uparrow}(0)\rangle_{R}{}_{L}\langle c_{\downarrow}^{\dagger}(\bm{R})c_{\downarrow}(0)\rangle_{R}\,.

As shown in Eq. (
S.58
), the second term in Eq. (
S.90
) decays as
|\bm{R}|^{-3/2}
on the phase boundary. Thus, in the limit of
|\bm{R}|\to\infty
, the pair correlation function is given by

\displaystyle\lim_{|\bm{R}|\to\infty}\rho_{2}(\bm{R}\uparrow,\bm{R}\downarrow;0\uparrow,0\downarrow)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{}_{L}\langle c_{\uparrow}^{\dagger}(0)c_{\downarrow}^{\dagger}(0)\rangle_{R}{}_{L}\langle c_{\downarrow}(0)c_{\uparrow}(0)\rangle_{R}

(S.91)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{\bm{k}_{1},\bm{k}_{2}}{}_{L}\langle c_{\bm{k}_{1}\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{-\bm{k}_{1}\downarrow}^{\dagger}\rangle_{R}{}_{L}\langle c_{-\bm{k}_{2}\downarrow}c_{\bm{k}_{2}\uparrow}\rangle_{R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\Delta_{0}}{U}\right)^{2}\,,

where we have used the translational invariance and the definition
\Delta_{0}=-\frac{U}{N}\sum_{\bm{k}}{}_{L}\langle c_{-\bm{k}\downarrow}c_{\bm{k}\uparrow}\rangle_{R}
. We note that the long-distance limit of the pair correlation function does not vanish on the phase boundary:

\lim_{|\bm{R}|\to\infty}\rho_{2}(\bm{R}\uparrow,\bm{R}\downarrow;0\uparrow,0\downarrow)=-\frac{(\text{Im}\Delta_{0})^{2}}{U^{2}}\neq 0\,.

(S.92)

This non-vanishing behavior of the correlation function at the critical point is due to the non-Hermitian nature of the critical phenomenon. In fact, in nonunitary critical phenomena, the correlation function at the critical point can diverge as a function of the distance rather than decay
Fisher 1978
.

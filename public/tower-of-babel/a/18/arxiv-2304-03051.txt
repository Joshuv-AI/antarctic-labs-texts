Matrix models for the nested hypergeometric tau-functions
Alexander Alexandrov

Abstract
. We introduce and investigate a family of tau-functions of the 2D Toda hierarchy, which is a natural generalization of the hypergeometric family associated with Hurwitz numbers. For this family we prove a skew Schur function expansion formula. For arbitrary rational weight generating functions we construct the multi-matrix models. Two different types of cut-and-join descriptions are derived. Considered examples include generalized fully simple maps, which we identify with the recently introduced skew hypergeometric tau-functions.

1.
Introduction

Counting of the objects related to the Riemann surfaces is often governed by the integrable hierarchies of the KP/Toda type.
Since the pioneering works of Okounkov and Pandharipande
[
Oko00
,
Pan00
]
we know that the simple Hurwitz numbers, which count ramified coverings of the sphere,
generate tau-functions of the 2D Toda hierarchy. A more general family of the weighted Hurwitz numbers
[
GJ08
,
GPH17
]
corresponds to a hypergeometric (or Orlov–Scherbin) family of
tau-functions, introduced in
[
KMMM95
]
and further investigated in
[
OS01
]
. This family of tau-functions attracts a lot of attention because of its spectacular properties. In particular, many interesting hypergeometric tau-functions can be described by various versions of matrix models and cut-and-join operators
[
GJ97
,
ZJZ03
,
Orl04
,
OS05
,
Orl05
,
Eyn08
,
Ale11
,
AMMN14
,
GGPN14
,
AC14
,
Zog15
,
Orl17
,
ACEH18
,
Ale23
,
WLZZ22
]
.

In this paper, inspired by the recent progress in matrix models and cut-and-join operators on one side
[
WLZZ22
,
Ale23
,
MMM+23a
,
MMM+23b
]
, and enumerative geometry on another
[
BGF20
,
BDBKS21
]
, we introduce a family of tau-functions, which can be considered as a deep generalization of the hypergeometric family. Our definition is based on the free fermion description, inherent to the solitonic integrable hierarchies. We call constructed tau-functions the
nested hypergeometric tau-functions
. For this family we derive the skew Schur function expansion and the cut-and-join description, which are direct generalizations of the constructions for the hypergeometric tau-functions.

Hypergeometric tau-functions are closely related to the matrix models, and one of the main goals of this paper is to generalize this relation to the nested hypergeometric tau-functions. For this purpose we restrict ourself to the tau-functions with diagonal group operators described by the weight generating functions of the form

(1.1)

G(z)=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}(1+u_{i}z)}{\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1+v_{j}z)}e^{wz}.

This corresponds to an arbitrary combination of the weight generating function associated with the strictly monotone, monotone, and simple Hurwitz numbers,
which makes the family natural for the enumerative geometry and topological string applications. For this family of the nested hypergeometric tau-functions we provide a general construction of the multi-matrix model. This multi-matrix model is a combination of the normal, unitary, and complex matrix integrals and has a chain structure, which allows us to reduce it to the eigenvalue integral. The eigenvalue integral is a combination of the perturbative Gaussian integrals and elementary residues.

For more specific combinations of the diagonal group elements the nested hypergeometric tau-functions can also be described by the cut-and-join operators of
[
WLZZ22
,
Ale23
,
MMM+23a
,
MMM+23b
]
. As usual, the tau-functions are described by the action of the exponential operators on the elementary generating function, however, for the nested hypergeometric family this action contains several non-commutative exponential operators.

We consider in detail several examples. For the simplest case of the hypergeometric tau-functions, associated with weighted Hurwitz numbers, we provide an example of the matrix integral for the rational weight generating function and discuss the ambiguity, which helps to simplify the matrix model. We also reconsider different cut-and-join operator descriptions and relations between them. The next family of examples of the nested hypergeometric tau-functions is associated with the skew hypergeometric tau-functions, recently introduces in
[
MMM+23a
,
MMM+23b
]
. We identify a particular representative of this family with the generating function of the fully simple maps (more generally, hypermaps), recently investigated in
[
BGF20
,
BDBKS21
]
. In particular, we prove a skew Schur expansion formula for the generating function of the fully simple maps and its generalization. For this family of tau-functions, which includes the fully simple case, we provide several different cut-and-join descriptions. We discuss the interrelations between different cut-and-join formulas and connection to the hypergeometric case. Some other examples are discussed, but their relation to the enumerative geometry invariants is not clear yet.

Many interesting questions are beyond the scope of this paper. In particular, Sato Grassmannian description and associated Virasoro type linear constraints for the nested hypergeometric tau-functions are not
discussed here. Fermionic representation should help us to obtain the Kac–Schwarz operators, including the canonical ones, and to derive
linear constraints. Matrix models can also be helpful for this purpose.

Many elements of our general construction can be immediately applied to other families of the generating functions. Perhaps the most interesting direction is associated with integrable hierarchies of type B.
For this case, the generalization which includes the definition of the nested hypergeometric tau-function of the 2-component BKP hierarchy, free neutral fermion description, skew Schur Q-function expansion, and cut-and-join operators is straightforward. However, the general construction of the matrix integral can be more tricky.
Deformation to the skew Jack polynomial version and associated
\beta
-deformed matrix integrals, as well as
(q,t)
-deformations
are also very attractive and, for some elements of the general construction, are direct.
We will return to these topics in the upcoming publications.

1.1.
Organization of the paper

In Section
2
, we recall the main ingredients of the description of tau-functions in terms of free fermions. We use this free fermion formulation to introduce the
nested hypergeometric tau-functions in Section
3
. In this section we also prove the skew Schur expansion formula and cut-and-join recursion for the general nested hypergeometric tau-function. Section

4
is devoted to the nested hypergeometric tau-functions with the diagonal group elements of specific form. For all these tau-functions we construct multi-matrix models, which can be reduced to the eigenvalue integrals,
and for a more specific family we construct another cut-and-join description. In Section
5
we apply the general construction to the examples and compare them with known matrix models and enumerative geometry invariants. In Section

6
we comment on the partition functions with broken KP integrability.

2.
Tau-functions and free fermions

In this section, we recall the description of tau-functions of the Kadomtsev–Petviashvili (KP) hierarchy, modified
Kadomtsev–Petviashvili (MKP) hierarchy, and 2D Toda lattice hierarchy in terms of free fermions (or, equivalently, infinite wedge space). We also provide some details on the Schur functions
and their relation to the free fermions and tau-functions.
We refer the reader to
[
Mac95
,
MJD00
,
AZ13
]
for further details and proofs.

Let us introduce the free fermions
\psi_{n},\psi^{*}_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfying the canonical anticommutation relations

(2.1)

[\psi_{n},\psi_{m}]_{+}=[\psi^{*}_{n},\psi^{*}_{m}]_{+}=0,\quad[\psi_{n},\psi^{*}_{m}]_{+}=\delta_{mn}.

They generate
an infinite dimensional Clifford algebra.

Next, we introduce a vacuum state
\left|0\right>
, which satisfies:

(2.2)

\psi_{n}\left|0\right>=0,\quad n<0;\quad\quad\quad\psi^{*}_{n}\left|0\right>=0,\quad n\geq 0.

Similarly, the dual vacuum state has the properties

(2.3)

\left<0\right|\psi^{*}_{n}=0,\quad n<0;\quad\quad\quad\left<0\right|\psi_{n}=0,\quad n\geq 0.

With respect to the vacuum
\left|0\right>
, the operators
\psi_{n}
with

n<0
and
\psi^{*}_{n}
with
n\geq 0
are annihilation operators,
while the operators
\psi^{*}_{n}
with
n<0
and

\psi_{n}
with
n\geq 0
are creation operators.
We also introduce “shifted” vacua
\left|n\right>
and
\left<n\right|

defined as

\displaystyle\left|n\right>=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\psi_{n-1}\ldots\psi_{1}\psi_{0}\left|0\right>,\,\,\,\,\,n>0,\\
\\
\psi^{*}_{n}\ldots\psi^{*}_{-2}\psi^{*}_{-1}\left|0\right>,\,\,\,\,\,n<0,\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\left<n\right|=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\left<0\right|\psi^{*}_{0}\psi^{*}_{1}\ldots\psi^{*}_{n-1},\,\,\,\,\,n>0,\\
\\
\left<0\right|\psi_{-1}\psi_{-2}\ldots\psi_{n},\,\,\,\,\,n<0.\end{array}\right.

For them we have

(2.10)

\begin{split}\psi_{m}\left|n\right>&=0,\quad m<n;\quad\quad\quad\psi^{*}_{m}\left|n\right>=0,\quad m\geq n,\\
\left<n\right|\psi_{m}&=0,\quad m\geq n;\quad\quad\quad\left<n\right|\psi^{*}_{m}=0,\quad m<n.\end{split}

Excited states over the vacuum

\left|0\right>
are obtained by action of the creation operators. We say that the operators
\psi_{j}
increase the charge by one, and the operators
\psi^{*}_{j}
decrease the charge by one.
Let us introduce a convenient basis
of states with definite charge in the fermionic Fock space
{\mathcal{H}}_{F}
.
The basis states
\left|\lambda,n\right>
are
parametrized by the charge
n
and partitions
\lambda
in the following way.
Given a partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell})
with
\ell=\ell(\lambda)

nonzero parts, let

(\vec{\alpha}|\vec{\beta})=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d(\lambda)}|\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{d(\lambda)})
be the Frobenius notation
for the partition
\lambda
.
Here
d(\lambda)
is the number of
boxes in the main diagonal and
\alpha_{i}=\lambda_{i}-i
,

\beta_{i}=\lambda^{\prime}_{i}-i
, where
\lambda^{\prime}
is the transposed
(reflected about the main diagonal) diagram
\lambda
. Then

(2.11)

\begin{array}[]{l}\left|\lambda,n\right>:=\psi^{*}_{n-\beta_{1}-1}\ldots\psi^{*}_{n-\beta_{d(\lambda)}\!-1}\,\psi_{n+\alpha_{d(\lambda)}}\ldots\psi_{n+\alpha_{1}}\left|n\right>,\\
\\
\left<\lambda,n\right|:=\left<n\right|\psi^{*}_{n+\alpha_{1}}\ldots\psi^{*}_{n+\alpha_{d(\lambda)}}\,\psi_{n-\beta_{d(\lambda)}\!-1}\ldots\psi_{n-\beta_{1}-1}.\end{array}

The state
\left|\lambda,n\right>
has the charge
n

with respect to the vacuum state
\left|0\right>
. For the empty diagram

\left<\emptyset,n\right|=\left<n\right|
,

\left|\emptyset,n\right>=\left|n\right>
.

Let

(2.12)

b(\lambda)=\sum_{i=1}^{d(\lambda)}(\beta_{i}+1).

The basis states
\left|\lambda,n\right>
and
\left<\lambda,n\right|
can be constructed from a vacuum
in another, equivalent way which is sometimes more convenient

(2.13)

\begin{array}[]{l}\left|\lambda,n\right>=(-1)^{b(\lambda)}\,\psi_{n+\lambda_{1}-1}\psi_{n+\lambda_{2}-2}\ldots\psi_{n+\lambda_{\ell}-\ell}\left|n-\ell\right>,\\
\\
\left<\lambda,n\right|=(-1)^{b(\lambda)}\left<n-\ell\right|\psi^{*}_{n+\lambda_{\ell}-\ell}\ldots\psi^{*}_{n+\lambda_{2}-2}\psi^{*}_{n+\lambda_{1}-1}.\end{array}

The basis vectors (
2.11
)
are orthonormal with respect to the
scalar product induced by the expectation value:

\left<\lambda,n\right|\left.\mu,m\right>=\delta_{mn}\delta_{\lambda\mu}.

The normal ordering
{\scriptstyle{{\bullet}\atop{\bullet}}}(\ldots){\scriptstyle{{\bullet}\atop{\bullet}}}
with respect
to the vacuum
\left|0\right>
is defined as
follows: all annihilation operators
are moved to the right and all creation operators are moved to
the left, taking into account that the factor
(-1)
appears
each time two neighboring
fermionic operators exchange their positions.
Normally ordered bilinear combinations
X_{B}=\sum_{mn}B_{mn}{\scriptstyle{{\bullet}\atop{\bullet}}}\psi^{*}_{m}\psi_{n}{\scriptstyle{{\bullet}\atop{\bullet}}}

of the fermions, with certain conditions
on the matrix
B=(B_{mn})
, generate an
infinite-dimensional Lie algebra
\gl(\infty)
. Exponentiating these expressions, one obtains
an infinite dimensional group (a version
of
\GL(\infty)
) with the group elements

(2.14)

G=\exp\Bigl(\sum_{i,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}B_{ik}{\scriptstyle{{\bullet}\atop{\bullet}}}\psi^{*}_{i}\psi_{k}{\scriptstyle{{\bullet}\atop{\bullet}}}\Bigr).

Let us consider the operators

(2.15)

J_{k}=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}{\scriptstyle{{\bullet}\atop{\bullet}}}\psi_{j}\psi^{*}_{j+k}{\scriptstyle{{\bullet}\atop{\bullet}}}.

These operators belong to
\gl(\infty)
Lie algebra and span the Heisenberg algebra

(2.16)

\left[J_{k},J_{l}\right]=k\delta_{k+l,0}.

Operators
J_{k}
with positive and negative
k
annihilate right and left vacua respectively,

(2.17)

J_{k}\left|n\right>=\left<n\right|J_{-k}=0,\quad\quad\quad k\geq 0,

and for the zeroth mode we have

(2.18)

J_{0}\left|n\right>=n\left|n\right>,\quad\quad\left<n\right|J_{0}=n\left<n\right|.

For any group element
G
a tau-function of the MKP hierarchy is given by a vacuum expectation value

(2.19)

\tau_{n}({\bf t})=\left<n\right|{\gamma_{+}({\bf t})}G\left|n\right>,

where we introduce

(2.20)

\gamma_{\pm}({\bf t})=e^{\sum_{k>0}t_{k}J_{\pm k}}.

It depends on
the variables
{\bf t}=\{t_{1},t_{2},\ldots\}
, usually called times, and on
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

The MKP hierarchy relates
\tau_{m}
to
\tau_{n}
for any
m-n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
It can be described by the bilinear Hirota identity

(2.21)

\oint_{{\infty}}z^{m-n}e^{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(t_{k}-t_{k}^{\prime})z^{k}}\,\tau_{m}({\bf t}-[z^{-1}])\,\tau_{n}({\bf t^{\prime}}+[z^{-1}])dz=0,

where we use the standard short-hand notations

(2.22)

{\bf t}\pm[z^{-1}]:=\bigl\{t_{1}\pm z^{-1},t_{2}\pm\frac{1}{2}z^{-2},t_{3}\pm\frac{1}{3}z^{-3},\ldots\bigr\}.

For a fixed
n
,
\tau_{n}({\bf t})
is a tau-function of the KP hierarchy and satisfies (
2.21
) with
m=n
.

More generally, for any group element
G
, the vacuum expectation value

(2.23)

\tau_{n}({\bf t},{\bf\tilde{t}})=\left<n\right|\gamma_{+}({\bf t})\,G\,\gamma_{-}({\bf\tilde{t}})\left|n\right>

is a tau-function of the 2D Toda lattice hierarchy. It is also a tau-function of the 2-component MKP
hierarchy.

Let

(2.24)

\widehat{J}_{k}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{k}}\quad\quad\quad&\mathrm{for}\quad k>0,\\[2.0pt]
&n&\mathrm{for}\quad k=0,\\[2.0pt]
&-kt_{-k}&\mathrm{for}\quad k<0,\end{aligned}\right.

then

(2.25)

\widehat{J}_{k}\cdot\left<n\right|\gamma_{+}({\bf t})=\left<n\right|\gamma_{+}({\bf t})\,J_{k}.

This relation allows us to represent the action of algebra
\gl(\infty)

in terms of the differential operators.

Given a partition
\lambda
,
one can introduce the Schur polynomials (or Schur functions)
via the
Jacobi–Trudi formulas:

(2.26)

s_{\lambda}({\bf t})=\det_{1\leq i,j\leq\ell(\lambda)}h_{\lambda_{i}-i+j}({\bf t}),

where the elementary Schur polynomials
h_{k}({\bf t})
are defined by

(2.27)

e^{\sum_{k\geq 1}t_{k}z^{k}}=:\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}h_{k}({\bf t})z^{k}.

For the empty diagram

s_{\emptyset}({\bf t})=1
. The action of the group elements
\gamma_{\pm}({\bf t})
on the vacuum states is described by the following expansion

(2.28)

\begin{split}{\gamma_{-}({\bf t})}\left|n\right>&=\sum_{\lambda}(-1)^{b(\lambda)}s_{\lambda}({\bf t})\left|\lambda,n\right>,\\
\left<n\right|{\gamma_{+}({\bf t})}&=\sum_{\lambda}(-1)^{b(\lambda)}s_{\lambda}({\bf t})\left<\lambda,n\right|,\end{split}

where
the sums run over all partitions
\lambda
, including the empty one and
b(\emptyset)=0
.

The skew Schur functions

(2.29)

s_{\lambda/\mu}({\bf t})=\det_{1\leq i,j\leq\ell(\lambda)}h_{\lambda_{i}-\mu_{j}-i+j}({\bf t})

satisfy

(2.30)

s_{\lambda}({\bf t}+{\bf\tilde{t}})=\sum_{\mu}s_{\lambda/\mu}({\bf t})s_{\mu}({\bf\tilde{t}}).

Moreover, expansions (
2.28
) can be generalized as follows:

Proposition 2.1
.

It holds

(2.31)

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{{\gamma_{-}({\bf t})}\left|\lambda,n\right>=\sum_{\mu}(-1)^{b(\mu/\lambda)}s_{\mu/\lambda}({\bf t})\left|\mu,n\right>},\\
\\
\displaystyle{{\gamma_{+}({\bf t})}\left|\lambda,n\right>=\sum_{\mu}(-1)^{b(\lambda/\mu)}s_{\lambda/\mu}({\bf t})\left|\mu,n\right>},\\
\\
\displaystyle{\left<\lambda,n\right|{\gamma_{+}({\bf t})}=\sum_{\mu}(-1)^{b(\mu/\lambda)}s_{\mu/\lambda}({\bf t})\left<\mu,n\right|},\\
\\
\displaystyle{\left<\lambda,n\right|{\gamma_{-}({\bf t})}=\sum_{\mu}(-1)^{b(\lambda/\mu)}s_{\lambda/\mu}({\bf t})\left<\mu,n\right|},\end{array}

where
b(\lambda/\mu)=b(\lambda)-b(\mu)
.

Schur functions satisfy the Cauchy–Littlewood formula

(2.32)

e^{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k\,t_{k}\,\tilde{t}_{k}}=\sum_{\lambda}s_{\lambda}({\bf t})s_{\lambda}({\bf\tilde{t}}).

Skew Schur functions satisfy a closely related summation formula

(2.33)

e^{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k\,t_{k}\,\tilde{t}_{k}}\sum_{\lambda}s_{\mu/\lambda}({\bf\tilde{t}})s_{\nu/\lambda}({\bf t})=\sum_{\lambda}s_{\lambda/\mu}({\bf t})s_{\lambda/\nu}({\bf\tilde{t}}),

which reduces to the Cauchy–Littlewood formula for
\lambda=\nu=\emptyset
.

3.
Nested hypergeometric tau-functions

In this section, we introduce the nested hypergeometric tau-functions. For a general nested hypergeometric tau-function we derive
the skew Schur function expansion and give a cut-and-join description.

Let us consider the diagonal abelian subgroup of
\GL(\infty)
group, which consists of the group elements of the form

(3.1)

O=\exp\left(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}T_{k}{\scriptstyle{{\bullet}\atop{\bullet}}}\psi_{k}\psi^{*}_{k}{\scriptstyle{{\bullet}\atop{\bullet}}}\right)

parametrized by
{\bf T}:=\{\dots,T_{-1},T_{0},T_{1},\dots\}
.

The fermionic Fock space states
\left|\lambda,n\right>
and
\left<\lambda,n\right|
are the eigenstates of these operators.
Let

(3.2)

c_{n}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &e^{-T_{-1}-T_{-2}-\ldots-T_{n}},\quad&n<0,\\
&1,\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad&n=0,\\
&e^{T_{0}+T_{1}+\ldots+T_{n-1}},\quad\quad\!&n>0,\end{aligned}\right.

be the solution to the difference equation
c_{n+1}/c_{n}=e^{T_{n}}
fixed by
c_{0}=1
. Consider the
content product

(3.3)

r_{\lambda,n}=\prod_{(i,j)\in\lambda}G(n+j-i),

where we take the product over all cells of the Young diagrams for a partition
\lambda
with the factors dependent on the
content

j-i
. Here the
weight generating function

G(z)
is related to the parameters
T_{j}
as follows:

(3.4)

G(i):=e^{T_{i}-T_{i-1}}.

Equivalently

(3.5)

r_{\lambda,n}=e^{\sum_{i=1}^{d(\lambda)}T_{n+\alpha_{i}}-T_{n-\beta_{i}-1}}=e^{\sum_{j\geq 1}T_{n+\lambda_{j}-j}-T_{n-j}}.

Then from the definition of the states
\left|\lambda,n\right>
and
\left<\lambda,n\right|
and (
3.5
) we immediately have the following statement.

Lemma 3.1
.

For a diagonal group element
O
we have

(3.6)

\begin{split}O\left|\lambda,n\right>&=c_{n}\,r_{\lambda,n}\left|\lambda,n\right>,\\
\left<\lambda,n\right|O&=c_{n}\,r_{\lambda,n}\left<\lambda,n\right|.\end{split}

Let
m\geq 0
be a non-negative integer. Let us choose an ordered sequence of signs,
\sigma=\{\sigma_{m},\sigma_{m-1},\dots,\sigma_{1}\}
, where
\sigma_{i}
is a sign, that is
\sigma_{i}=+
or
\sigma_{i}=-
. There are exactly
2^{m}
different sequences, for example, for
m=2
we have 4 possibilities:
\sigma=++
,
\sigma=+-
,
\sigma=-+
, or
\sigma=--
.

With
0\leq k\leq m+1
we associate infinite sets of the formal variables,
{\bf t_{k}}:=\{t_{1,k},t_{2,k},\dots\}
. We also introduce the parameters
{\bf T^{(k)}}:=\{\dots,T_{-1}^{(k)},T_{0}^{(k)},T_{1}^{(k)},\dots\}
for
0\leq k\leq m
and the diagonal group elements

(3.7)

O_{k}=\exp\left(\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}T_{j}^{(k)}{\scriptstyle{{\bullet}\atop{\bullet}}}\psi_{j}\psi^{*}_{j}{\scriptstyle{{\bullet}\atop{\bullet}}}\right).

Let us stress that all variables
{\bf t_{k}}
and
{\bf T}^{(k)}
are independent.

Definition 1
.

For any
\sigma
and
{\bf T^{(k)}}
the nested hypergeometric tau-function is given by the vacuum expectation value

(3.8)

\tau_{n}^{m,\sigma}({\bf t_{m+1}},\dots,{\bf t_{0}})=\left<n\right|\gamma_{+}({\bf t_{m+1}})\,O_{m}\,\gamma_{\sigma_{m}}({\bf t_{m}})\,O_{m-1}\dots O_{1}\,\gamma_{\sigma_{1}}({\bf t_{1}})\,O_{0}\,\gamma_{-}({\bf t_{0}})\left|n\right>.

This is a formal series in the variables
{\bf t_{k}}
. Since
O
and
\gamma_{\pm}({\bf t})
belong to
\GL(\infty)
group,
this is a 2D Toda lattice tau-function in the variables
{\bf t_{0}}
,
{\bf t_{m+1}}
, and
n
, while
{\bf t_{1}},\dots,{\bf t_{m}}
,
{\bf T^{(0)}},\dots,{\bf T^{(m)}}
, and
\sigma
are the parameters of the tau-function.

3.1.
Skew Schur function expansion

Let us find the expansion of the tau-function
\tau_{n}^{m,\sigma}
. We use Proposition
2.1
and Lemma
3.1
.

We consider the vacuum expectation value in Definition
1
from left to right. The initial state is
\gamma_{-}({\bf t_{0}})\left|n\right>=\sum_{\lambda}(-1)^{b(\lambda)}s_{\lambda}({\bf t_{0}})\left|\lambda,n\right>
. Then, according to Proposition
2.1
insertion of the operator
{\gamma_{\pm}({\bf t})}
creates an additional sum over partitions with the skew Schur function. By Lemma
3.1
insertion of the diagonal operator
O
creates a partition dependent weight
r_{\lambda,n}
.

Let us consider the simplest example, which corresponds to the case
m=0
,

(3.9)

\tau_{n}^{0,\emptyset}=\left<n\right|{\gamma_{+}({\bf t_{1}})}\,O_{0}\,{\gamma_{-}({\bf t_{0}})}\left|n\right>=c_{n}^{(0)}\sum_{\lambda}r^{(0)}_{\lambda,n}s_{\lambda}({\bf t_{1}})s_{\lambda}({\bf t_{0}}).

These are hypergeometric tau-functions
[
KMMM95
,
OS01
]
, which describe weighted Hurwitz numbers
[
Oko00
,
Pan00
,
GJ08
,
GPH17
]
.

For
m=1
we have two possible values of
\sigma
. Corresponding tau-functions are

(3.10)

\begin{split}\tau_{n}^{1,+}&=\left<n\right|{\gamma_{+}({\bf t_{2}})}\,O_{1}\,{\gamma_{+}({\bf t_{1}})}\,O_{0}\,{\gamma_{-}({\bf t_{0}})}\left|n\right>\\
&=c_{n}^{(0)}c_{n}^{(1)}\sum_{\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1}}s_{\lambda_{1}}({\bf t_{2}})\,r^{(1)}_{\lambda_{1},n}\,s_{\lambda_{0}/\lambda_{1}}({\bf t_{1}})\,r^{(0)}_{\lambda_{0},n}\,s_{\lambda_{0}}({\bf t_{0}})\end{split}

and

(3.11)

\begin{split}\tau_{n}^{1,-}&=\left<n\right|{\gamma_{+}({\bf t_{2}})}\,O_{1}\,{\gamma_{-}({\bf t_{1}})}\,O_{0}\,{\gamma_{-}({\bf t_{0}})}\left|n\right>\\
&=c_{n}^{(0)}c_{n}^{(1)}\sum_{\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1}}s_{\lambda_{1}}({\bf t_{2}})\,r^{(1)}_{\lambda_{1},n}\,s_{\lambda_{1}/\lambda_{0}}({\bf t_{1}})\,r^{(0)}_{\lambda_{0},n}\,s_{\lambda_{0}}({\bf t_{0}}).\end{split}

These tau-functions and their relation to other models are discussed in Section
5.2
.

It is easy to see the general structure of the expansion.
Let us introduce the notation
(\lambda/\mu)^{+}:=\lambda/\mu
and
(\lambda/\mu)^{-}:=\mu/\lambda
. The signs
(-1)^{b(\lambda)}
from (
2.31
) are always compensated in the final expression and never contribute. Then for the nested hypergeometric tau-function
\tau_{n}^{m,\sigma}
we have the following skew Schur function expansion.

Theorem 1
.

(3.12)

\tau_{n}^{m,\sigma}({\bf t_{m+1}},\dots,{\bf t_{0}})=\prod_{j=0}^{m}c_{n}^{(j)}\sum_{\lambda_{0},\dots,\lambda_{m}}s_{\lambda_{m}}({\bf t_{m+1}})\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left(r^{(i)}_{\lambda_{i},n}s_{(\lambda_{i-1}/\lambda_{i})^{\sigma_{i}}}({\bf t_{i}})\right)\,r^{(0)}_{\lambda_{0},n}\,s_{\lambda_{0}}({\bf t_{0}}).

This family generalizes the hypergeometric tau-functions (
3.9
) in a natural way. While in the expansion of the hypergeometric tau-function (
3.9
) the summation runs over all partitions, in the expansion (
3.12
) only some combinations of partitions survive.

We say that
\lambda\subset\mu
if
\lambda_{i}\leq\mu_{i}
for all
i
.
The non-trivial terms in the sum in (
3.12
) are given by the nested partitions. For instance, if
\sigma=\{+,+,+\dots\}
, then only the nested sequences of the partitions
\lambda_{m}\subset\lambda_{m-1}\subset\dots\subset\lambda_{0}
contribute to the summation. For other choices of the signs
\sigma
the nested structure is more involved, namely
\lambda_{i}\subset\lambda_{i-1}
if
\sigma_{i}=+
and
\lambda_{i}\supset\lambda_{i-1}
if
\sigma_{i}=-
. Because of this nested structure we call
\tau_{n}^{m,\sigma}({\bf t_{m+1}},\dots,{\bf t_{0}})
the nested hypergeometric tau-function.

Below, depending on the context, we consider the nested hypergeometric tau-functions
of the 2D Toda lattice, 2-component KP (or MKP) hierarchy or even 1-component KP (or MKP) hierarchy. Therefore, by the the nested hypergeometric tau-functions we always mean (
3.8
) as a function of a proper set of variables and parameters.

Let
\sigma^{*}=\{-\sigma_{1},-\sigma_{2},\dots,-\sigma_{m}\}
. Then

(3.13)

\tau_{n}^{m,\sigma}({\bf t_{m+1}},\dots,{\bf t_{0}})=\tau_{n}^{m,\sigma^{*}}({\bf t_{0}},\dots,{\bf t_{m+1}}),

where for the right hand side tau-function the order of all group operators
O
and
\gamma_{\pm}
and the signs of operators
\gamma_{\pm}
is inverted:

(3.14)

\tau_{n}^{m,\sigma^{*}}({\bf t_{0}},\dots,{\bf t_{m+1}})=\left<n\right|\gamma_{+}({\bf t_{0}})\,O_{0}\,\gamma_{-\sigma_{1}}({\bf t_{1}})\,O_{1}\,\dots O_{m-1}\gamma_{-\sigma_{m}}({\bf t_{m}})\,O_{m}\,\gamma_{-}({\bf t_{m+1}})\left|n\right>.

In particular,
(
3.10
) and (
3.11
) describe the same families of tau-functions and it is enough to consider only one of them. If we identify the nested hypergeometric tau-functions, related by this duality, then there are
2^{m-1}
inequivalent possibilities for odd
m
and
2^{m-1}+2^{m/2-1}
inequivalent possibilities for even
m
.

The nested structure of the considered tau-functions leads to the simple reduction properties. Namely, the nested hypergeometric tau-function
\tau_{n}^{m,\sigma}
reduces to a tau-function of the form
\tau_{n}^{m-1,\sigma^{\prime}}
if
{\bf t_{j}}={\bf 0}
,
or if
O_{j}=0
and
\sigma_{j}=\sigma_{j+1}
for some
j
(we assume that
\sigma_{0}=-
and
\sigma_{m+1}=+
). In this last case
\tau_{n}^{m-1,\sigma^{\prime}}
depends only on the combination
{\bf t_{j}+t_{j+1}}
.

3.2.
Cut-and-join operators

Let us describe the nested hypergeometric tau-functions
\tau_{n}^{m,\sigma}
by the cut-and-join operators. As a result, the nested hypergeometric tau-functions are constructed recursively in
m
.
There are several types of the cut-and-join descriptions, and another option convenient for certain families of the diagonal group operators is discussed in Section
4.4
.

Recall Lemma
3.1
. For a diagonal group element
O
consider the differential operators
\widehat{O}
with the Schur functions as the eigenfunctions,

(3.15)

\widehat{O}({\bf t},n)\cdot s_{\lambda}({\bf t})=c_{n}r_{\lambda,n}s_{\lambda}({\bf t}),

then

(3.16)

\widehat{O}({\bf t},n)\cdot\left<n\right|\gamma_{+}({\bf t})=\left<n\right|\gamma_{+}({\bf t})\,O

and

(3.17)

\widehat{O}({\bf t},n)\cdot\gamma_{-}({\bf t})\left|n\right>=O\,\gamma_{-}({\bf t})\left|n\right>.

These operators can be constructed from the boson-fermion correspondence in terms of the vertex operators. If
T_{k}=p(k)
for some polynomial
p(z)
, then the operator
\widehat{O}
has a nice description in terms of a finite combination of the cut-and-join operators operators
\widehat{C}_{k}
,
\widehat{O}=\exp\left(\sum\alpha_{k}\widehat{C}_{k}\right)

[
AMMN14
]
.

The cut-and-join description of a nested hypergeometric tau-functions
\tau_{n}^{m,\sigma}
can be constructed recursively. Namely, we have

(3.18)

\begin{split}\tau_{n}^{m,\sigma}&=\left<n\right|\gamma_{+}({\bf t_{m+1}})\,O_{m}\,\gamma_{\sigma_{m}}({\bf t_{m}})\,O_{m-1}\dots O_{1}\,\gamma_{\sigma_{1}}({\bf t_{1}})\,O_{0}\,\gamma_{-}({\bf t_{0}})\left|n\right>\\
&=\widehat{O}_{m}({\bf t_{m+1}},n)\cdot\left<n\right|\gamma_{+}({\bf t_{m+1}})\,\gamma_{\sigma_{m}}({\bf t_{m}})O_{m-1}\dots O_{1}\,\gamma_{\sigma_{1}}({\bf t_{1}})O_{0}\,\gamma_{-}({\bf t_{0}})\left|n\right>.\end{split}

If
\sigma_{m}=-
, we have

(3.19)

\begin{split}\left<n\right|\gamma_{+}({\bf t_{m+1}})\gamma_{-}({\bf t_{m}})=e^{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k\,t_{m+1,k}\,t_{m,k}}\,\left<n\right|\gamma_{+}({\bf t_{m+1}}),\end{split}

therefore

(3.20)

\tau_{n}^{m,\sigma}({\bf t_{m+1}},\dots,{\bf t_{0}})=\widehat{O}_{m}({\bf t_{m+1}},n)\cdot e^{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k\,t_{m+1,k}\,t_{m,k}}\,\tau_{n}^{m-1,\sigma^{\prime}}({\bf t_{m+1}},{\bf t_{m-1}},\dots,{\bf t_{0}}),

where
\sigma^{\prime}=\{\sigma_{m-1},\dots,\sigma_{1}\}
and

(3.21)

\tau_{n}^{m-1,\sigma^{\prime}}({\bf t_{m+1}},{\bf t_{m-1}},\dots,{\bf t_{0}})=\left<n\right|\gamma_{+}({\bf t_{m+1}})\,O_{m-1}\,\gamma_{\sigma_{m-1}}({\bf t_{m-1}})\dots O_{1}\,\gamma_{\sigma_{1}}({\bf t_{1}})O_{0}\,\gamma_{-}({\bf t_{0}})\left|n\right>.

If
\sigma_{m}=+
, we have

(3.22)

\begin{split}\left<n\right|\gamma_{+}({\bf t_{m+1}})\gamma_{+}({\bf t_{m}})=\left<n\right|\gamma_{+}({\bf t_{m+1}+t_{m}}),\end{split}

and

(3.23)

\tau_{n}^{m,\sigma}({\bf t_{m+1}},\dots,{\bf t_{0}})=\widehat{O}_{m}({\bf t_{m+1}},n)\cdot\tau_{n}^{m-1,\sigma^{\prime}}({\bf t_{m+1}+t_{m}},{\bf t_{m-1}},\dots,{\bf t_{0}})

and
\tau_{n}^{m-1,\sigma^{\prime}}
is given by (
3.21
).

Equations (
3.20
) and (
3.23
) describe a recursion in
m
, and allow us to reduce the description of the tau-functions
\tau_{n}^{m,\sigma}
to the action of the group operators
\widehat{O}
on the elementary exponential functions.
There are equivalent descriptions if we start from the right vacuum state
\left|n\right>
and a group element
O_{0}
with (
3.17
) or combine two directions, see for instance (
5.29
)–(
5.31
) for all possibilities for
m=1
.

4.
Matrix models

In this section, we construct matrix models for all nested hypergeometric tau-functions with the diagonal group elements described by the weight generating functions (
4.17
). We also derive a cut-and-join description of the nested hypergeometric tau-functions with a certain combinations of diagonal group operators associated with rational weight generating functions.

4.1.
Elementary building blocks

Let us recall the matrix models which serve as elementary building blocks for the construction of the nested hypergeometric tau-functions, introduced in the previous section.
The Schur function expansion of these basic matrix models is well known
[
ZJZ03
,
Orl04
,
Orl05
,
Ale11
,
AMMN14
]
, and the main goal of this section is to provide a brief reminder.

We assume that the matrix integrals are over the space of the
N\times N
matrices if it is not specified explicitly.
We work only with formal matrix models, and, by definition, consider the partition functions of the matrix models by the perturbative expansion of the matrix integrals in the corresponding variables.

We focus on the matrix integrals, that produce the following weight generating functions:

(4.1)

G^{+}(z)=1+uz,\quad\quad G^{-}(z)=\frac{1}{1+uz},\quad\quad G^{\exp}(z)=e^{uz}.

These functions are associated with the strictly monotone Hurwitz numbers, monotone Hurwitz numbers, and simple Hurwitz numbers correspondingly, see, e.g.,
[
ACEH18
]
.
The content products

(4.2)

r^{\bullet}_{\lambda}(u)=\prod_{(i,j)\in\lambda}G^{\bullet}(j-i)

for
\bullet=+,-,\exp
are the corresponding weights of the Schur expansions of the hypergeometric tau-functions (
3.9
) with
n=0
,
and we denote by
O_{\bullet}(u)
the diagonal group elements associated with them. For
+
and
-
indices we also have a reciprocity relation

(4.3)

r^{-}_{\lambda}(u)=\frac{1}{r^{+}_{\lambda}(u)},

and, therefore,
O_{+}(u)=O^{-1}_{-}(u)
. For
N\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
the coefficients
r^{\pm}_{\lambda}(N^{-1})
can be represented as a ratio of the Schur functions at a particular locus of the arguments,

(4.4)

r^{-}_{\lambda}(N^{-1})=N^{-|\lambda|}\frac{s_{\lambda}(t_{k}={N}/{k})}{s_{\lambda}(t_{k}=\delta_{k,1})}.

Remark 4.1
.

Any weight generating function given by a formal series,
G(z)\in 1+{\mathbb{C}}[\![z]\!]
, can be described by certain normal matrix integrals, see
[
OS05
]
and
[
Ale11
]
for two different constructions. In this paper we restrict ourself to the finite products of the weight generating functions (
4.1
) because they can be expressed in terms of the Gaussian integrals and elementary residues, and, therefore, can be investigated and computed explicitly.

The sum with the weight generating function
G^{+}
can be described by the complex matrix integral

(4.5)

\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq N}s_{\lambda}({\bf t})s_{\lambda}({\bf\bar{t}})r_{\lambda}^{+}(N^{-1})=\int_{\mathfrak{C}}\left[d\mu_{\mathfrak{C}}({Z})\right]\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}t_{k}{\rm Tr}\,{{Z}}^{k}+\bar{t}_{k}{\rm Tr}\,{{Z^{\dagger}}}^{k}\right).

Here we integrate over the space of the
N\times N
complex matrices with the measure

(4.6)

\left[d\mu_{\mathfrak{C}}({Z})\right]=\left[d{Z}\right]\exp\left(-N\,{\rm Tr}\,{ZZ^{\dagger}}\right),

where
\left[d{Z}\right]=c_{\mathfrak{C}}\prod_{i,j=1}^{N}dZ_{ij}d\bar{Z}_{ij}
is the flat measure on the space of complex matrices
with the constant
c_{\mathfrak{C}}
specified by the normalization condition

\int_{\mathfrak{C}}\left[d\mu_{\mathfrak{C}}({Z})\right]=1
. The coefficients of the series expansion in
{\bf t}
and
{\bf\bar{t}}
variables of the right hand side of (
4.5
) are given by the Gaussian integrals of the polynomial functions and can be evaluated explicitly.

The weight generating function
G^{-}
can be described by the double unitary integral,

(4.7)

\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq N}s_{\lambda}({\bf t})s_{\lambda}({\bf\bar{t}})r_{\lambda}^{-}(N^{-1})=\int_{\mathfrak{U}^{2}}\left[d\mu_{\mathfrak{U}}{(U)}\right]\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}t_{k}{\rm Tr}\,{U^{\dagger}}^{k}+{\bar{t}}_{k}{\rm Tr}\,\tilde{U}^{k}\right).

Here we integrate over two independent unitary matrices
U
and
\tilde{U}
,

(4.8)

\left[d\mu_{\mathfrak{U}}{(U)}\right]=\left[d{U}\right][d{\tilde{U}}]\exp(N\,{\rm Tr}\,U\tilde{U}^{\dagger}),

and the Haar measure is normalized by
\int_{\mathfrak{U}}\left[d{U}\right]=1
. Applying the Harish-Chandra–Itzykson–Zuber formula one can reduce this matrix integral to the eigenvalue one.
Coefficients of the
{\bf t}
and
{\bf\bar{t}}
series expansion of this eigenvalue integral are given by combinations of the elementary residues.

Let us recall that a matrix
Z
is normal if it commutes with its Hermitian conjugated,
[Z,Z^{\dagger}]=0
.
For the case
G^{\exp}
there is a normal matrix integral description

(4.9)

\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq N}s_{\lambda}({\bf t})s_{\lambda}({\bf\bar{t}})r_{\lambda}^{\exp}(u)=\int_{\mathfrak{N}}\left[d\mu_{\mathfrak{N}}({Z})\right]\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}t_{k}{\rm Tr}\,{Z}^{k}+\bar{t}_{k}{\rm Tr}\,{Z}^{\dagger k}\right).

Here the integral is taken over the space of
N\times N
normal matrices with complex eigenvalues.
A normal matrix
Z
can be diagonalized as
Z=UzU^{\dagger}
,
with unitary
U
and diagonal
z={\rm diag}\,(z_{1},\dots,z_{N})
with complex eigenvalues, then the flat measure on the space of normal matrices is given by
\left[d{Z}\right]=[dU]|\Delta(z)|^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{N}dz_{i}\,d\bar{z}_{i}
. Here
\Delta(z)
is the Vandermonde determinant,

(4.10)

\Delta(z)=\prod_{i<j}(z_{j}-z_{i}).

The measure in (
4.9
) is given by

(4.11)

\left[d\mu_{\mathfrak{N}}({Z})\right]=C_{\mathfrak{N}}\frac{\left[d{Z}\right]}{\left(\det{ZZ^{\dagger}}\right)^{N+\frac{1}{2}}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2u}{\rm Tr}\,\log^{2}{ZZ^{\dagger}}\right),

where
C_{\mathfrak{N}}
, dependent on
N
and
u
, is fixed by the normalization constraint
\int_{\mathfrak{N}}\left[d\mu_{\mathfrak{N}}({Z})\right]=1
. Integral (
4.9
) can be immediately reduced to the eigenvalue integral which, after a change of integration variables, is a combination of the Gaussian ones
[
Ale11
]
.

Let us stress that the above matrix integral descriptions are not unique, for example the right hand side of (
4.5
) has an alternative description by a two-matrix model

(4.12)

\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq N}s_{\lambda}({\bf t})s_{\lambda}({\bf\bar{t}})r_{\lambda}^{+}(N^{-1})=\int_{\mathfrak{H}^{2}}\left[d{X}\right]\left[d{Y}\right]\exp\left(\sqrt{-1}N{\rm Tr}\,{XY}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}t_{k}{\rm Tr}\,{X^{k}}+\bar{t}_{k}{\rm Tr}\,{Y}^{k}\right),

where the integration is over the space of a pair of Hermitian matrices
X
and
Y
with the flat measure
\left[d{X}\right]={c}_{\mathfrak{H}}\prod_{i<j}d\Im\Phi_{ij}\,d\Re\Phi_{ij}\prod_{i=1}^{n}d\Phi_{ii}
and a constant
{c}_{\mathfrak{H}}
fixed by a normalization constraint

\int_{\mathfrak{H}^{2}}\left[d{X}\right]\left[d{Y}\right]\exp\left(-N\sqrt{-1}{\rm Tr}\,{XY}\right)=1
.
A Hermitian matrix
X
can be diagonalized,
X=UxU^{\dagger}
, with a unitary
U
and real diagonal
x={\rm diag}\,(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})
, then the flat measure on the
space of Hermitian matrices is
\left[dX\right]=\tilde{c}_{\mathfrak{H}}\left[dU\right]\Delta(x)^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{N}dx_{i}
for another constant
\tilde{c}_{\mathfrak{H}}
.

For a function
f({\bf t})
and a matrix
A
let us define the
Miwa parametrization

f(A)=f({\bf t})\big|_{t_{k}=\frac{1}{k}{\rm Tr}\,A^{k}}
.
Then, in the Miwa parametrization the sum given by (
4.7
) can be represented as a unitary matrix integral

(4.13)

\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq N}s_{\lambda}(A)s_{\lambda}({\bf t})r_{\lambda}^{-}(N^{-1})=\int_{\mathfrak{U}}\left[d{U}\right]\exp\left(N{\rm Tr}\,{U^{\dagger}A}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}t_{k}{\rm Tr}\,U^{k}\right)

or the Harish-Chandra–Itzykson–Zuber matrix integral

(4.14)

\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq N}s_{\lambda}(A)s_{\lambda}(B)r_{\lambda}^{-}(N^{-1})=\int_{\mathfrak{U}}\left[d{U}\right]\exp\left(N{\rm Tr}\,({{UAU^{\dagger}B}})\right).

Remark 4.2
.

These examples indicate an important general property of the matrix models we consider in this paper – usually there are several equivalent matrix models for a given tau-function. In the next section we provide a universal recipe for the matrix integral description of the nested hypergeometric tau-functions
\tau_{0}^{m,\sigma}
, however, sometimes the ambiguity can help us to simplify the matrix integral expressions. In particular, the Miwa parametrization, natural for the matrix models, often leads to simplifications. We will return to this ambiguity in Section
5
.

Remark 4.3
.

In the above formulas we use a normalization natural for the applications related to the Hurwitz numbers.
For other applications it could be more convenient to consider other normalizations of the time variables, consistent with the 2D Toda hierarchy, in particular,

(4.15)

\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq N}s_{\lambda}({\bf t})s_{\lambda}({\bf\bar{t}})\prod_{(i,j)\in\lambda}(N-i+j)\\
=N^{-{N^{2}}}\int_{\mathfrak{H}^{2}}\left[d{X}\right]\left[d{Y}\right]\exp\left(\sqrt{-1}{\rm Tr}\,{XY}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}t_{k}{\rm Tr}\,{X^{k}}+\bar{t}_{k}{\rm Tr}\,{Y}^{k}\right)

and

(4.16)

\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq N}\frac{s_{\lambda}(A)s_{\lambda}(B)}{\prod_{(i,j)\in\lambda}(N-i+j)}=\int_{\mathfrak{U}}\left[d{U}\right]\exp\left({\rm Tr}\,({UAU^{\dagger}B})\right).

These formulas are related to (
4.12
) and (
4.14
) by a simple rescaling of the
{\bf t}
or
A
variables.

4.2.
Chains of matrix integrals

Any nested hypergeometric tau-function
\tau_{n}^{m,\sigma}
, given by Definition
1
, if all its diagonal group elements
O_{j}
correspond to the weight generating functions of the form

(4.17)

G(z)=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}(1+u_{i}z)}{\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1+v_{j}z)}e^{wz}

for some finite
\ell
and
n
,
can be described by a multi-matrix integral with the chain structure. We restrict ourself to the zero charge sector
n=0
, other cases can be described similarly.

Remark 4.4
.

It is also possible to introduce in
G
an arbitrary constant factor
Q
, which corresponds to the weight
Q^{|\lambda|}
,
by a rescaling of the
{\bf t}
variables. We will not consider this rescaling below.

We start with the fermionic vacuum expectation value (
3.8
) and consider it from right to left. With a state
\left|\lambda\right>=\left|\lambda,0\right>
we associate a Schur function
s_{\lambda}({\bf\tilde{t}})
with auxiliary variables
{\tilde{t}_{k}}
, which can be identified with
J_{-k}/k
. Then the function
\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k\tilde{t}_{k}t_{k}\right)=\sum_{\lambda}s_{\lambda}({\bf t})s_{\lambda}({\bf\tilde{t}})
is associated with the state
{\gamma_{-}({\bf t})}\left|0\right>
.

To construct matrix models we need to describe the action of the group elements
O_{\pm}(u)
,
O_{\exp}(u)
, and
\gamma_{\pm}({\bf t})
. For
\ell(\lambda)\leq N
insertion of weights
r_{\lambda}^{\pm}
and
r_{\lambda}^{\exp}
corresponds to the matrix integrals

(4.18)

\begin{split}\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k\tilde{t}_{k}t_{k}\right)\,\,\,&\mapsto\,\,\,\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq N}s_{\lambda}({\bf t})s_{\lambda}({\bf\tilde{t}})r_{\lambda}^{+}(N^{-1})=\int_{\mathfrak{C}}\left[d\mu_{\mathfrak{C}}({Z})\right]\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}t_{k}{\rm Tr}\,{{Z}}^{k}+\tilde{t}_{k}{\rm Tr}\,{{Z^{\dagger}}}^{k}\right),\\
\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k\tilde{t}_{k}t_{k}\right)\,\,\,&\mapsto\,\,\,\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq N}s_{\lambda}({\bf t})s_{\lambda}({\bf\tilde{t}})r_{\lambda}^{-}(N^{-1})=\int_{\mathfrak{U}^{2}}\left[d\mu_{\mathfrak{U}}{(U)}\right]\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}t_{k}{\rm Tr}\,{U^{\dagger}}^{k}+{\tilde{t}}_{k}{\rm Tr}\,\tilde{U}^{k}\right),\\
\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k\tilde{t}_{k}t_{k}\right)\,\,\,&\mapsto\,\,\,\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq N}s_{\lambda}({\bf t})s_{\lambda}({\bf\tilde{t}})r_{\lambda}^{\exp}(u)=\int_{\mathfrak{N}}\left[d\mu_{\mathfrak{N}}({Z})\right]\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}t_{k}{\rm Tr}\,{Z}^{k}+\tilde{t}_{k}{\rm Tr}\,{Z}^{\dagger k}\right).\end{split}

These transformations preserve the exponential form of the dependence of the
{\bf\tilde{t}}
variables.

As a result of application of any combination of these matrix integrals we have the dependence on
{\bf\tilde{t}}
variables in the form
\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\tilde{t}_{k}{\rm Tr}\,{\Phi}^{k}\right)=\sum_{\lambda}s_{\lambda}({\bf\tilde{t}})s_{\lambda}(\Phi)
for some matrix
\Phi
.
According to (
2.30
), insertion of the operators
\gamma_{+}({\bf\bar{t}})
and
\gamma_{-}({\bf\bar{t}})
in terms of the auxiliary variables
{\bf\tilde{t}}

yields correspondingly

(4.19)

\begin{split}\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k\tilde{t}_{k}t_{k}\right)\quad&\mapsto\quad\sum_{\mu}s_{\lambda/\mu}({\bf\bar{t}})s_{\mu}({\bf\tilde{t}})s_{\lambda}({\bf t})=\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k(\tilde{t}_{k}+\bar{t}_{k})t_{k}\right),\\
\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k\tilde{t}_{k}t_{k}\right)\quad&\mapsto\quad\sum_{\mu,\lambda}s_{\mu/\lambda}({\bf\bar{t}})s_{\mu}({\bf\tilde{t}})s_{\lambda}({\bf t})=\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k\tilde{t}_{k}(t_{k}+\bar{t}_{k})\right).\end{split}

Both preserve the exponential form of the dependence on the auxillary variables
{\bf\tilde{t}}
.

At the final step we identify
{\bf\tilde{t}}
with
{\bf t_{m+1}}
. As a result, we obtain a multi-matrix integral construction which describes all tau-functions
\tau_{0}^{m,\sigma}
with any combination of the weight generating functions (
4.17
).

Proposition 4.1
.

All nested hypergeometric tau-functions (
3.8
) with diagonal group operators
O_{j}
associated with the weight generating functions (
4.17
) are described by the multi-matrix models, which are combinations
of the complex, unitary, and normal matrix integrals (
4.5
), (
4.7
), and (
4.9
). Moreover, these multi-matrix models have a chain structure with a pairwise interaction, and can be reduced to the eigenvalue integrals.

Vice versa, any matrix integral constructed by the subsequent application of (
4.18
) and (
4.19
) describes a nested hypergeometric tau-function.

The dependence on all
{\bf t_{j}}
variables in the constructed matrix models is explicit of the form
\exp\left(\sum_{k}t_{j,k}\dots\right)
, where by
\dots
we denote linear combinations of other variables
k\,t_{i,k}
for
i\neq j
and traces of the integration matrices
{\rm Tr}\,\Phi^{k}
.

Two different forms of the same nested hypergeometric tau-functions, given by our construction for two sides of (
3.13
), provide essentially the same chain of the matrix integrals.

Remark 4.5
.

The matrix model construction of this section is closely related to the approach developed by Orlov
[
Orl05
,
Orl04
,
Orl17
]
.

Remark 4.6
.

Considered multi-matrix models for the nested hypergeometric tau-functions have a chain structure. It is possible to consider more general matrix integrals associated with other graphs.
Namely, a trivalent vertex (a pair of pants) can be described by a unitary matrix integral

(4.20)

\int_{\mathfrak{U}}\left[d{U}\right]\exp\left({\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}t_{k}{\rm Tr}\,({{UAU^{\dagger}B}})^{k}}\right)=\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq N}\frac{s_{\lambda}({\bf t})s_{\lambda}({A})s_{\lambda}({B})}{s_{\lambda}(t_{k}=N/k)},

or a complex matrix integral

(4.21)

\int_{\mathfrak{C}}\left[d{Z}\right]\exp\left(-{\rm Tr}\,{ZZ^{\dagger}}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}t_{k}{\rm Tr}\,({{ZAZ^{\dagger}B}})^{k}\right)=\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq N}\frac{s_{\lambda}({\bf t})s_{\lambda}({A})s_{\lambda}({B})}{s_{\lambda}(t_{k}=\delta_{k,1})},

see
[
Ale11
]
for more details. In general, for such generalizations the matrix models are not immediately reducible to the eigenvalue integrals anymore, and the KP/Toda integrability is broken (see also Section
6
).

4.3.
Superintegrability

Equations (
4.5
), (
4.7
), and (
4.9
) can be interpreted as examples of the of matrix model superintegrability
[
MM22
]
, namely for
\ell(\lambda)\leq N
the integrals of the Schur functions are proportional to the same Schur functions:

(4.22)

\begin{split}s_{\lambda}({\bf t})\,r_{\lambda}^{+}(N^{-1})&=\int_{\mathfrak{C}}\left[d\mu_{\mathfrak{C}}({Z})\right]\,s_{\lambda}(Z^{\dagger})\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}t_{k}{\rm Tr}\,{Z}^{k}\right),\\
s_{\lambda}({\bf t})\,r_{\lambda}^{-}(N^{-1})&=\int_{\mathfrak{U^{2}}}\left[d\mu_{\mathfrak{U}}{(U)}\right]\,s_{\lambda}(U^{\dagger})\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}t_{k}{\rm Tr}\,{\tilde{U}}^{k}\right),\\
s_{\lambda}({\bf t})\,r_{\lambda}^{\exp}(u)&=\int_{\mathfrak{N}}\left[d\mu_{\mathfrak{N}}({Z})\right]\,s_{\lambda}({Z}^{\dagger})\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}t_{k}{\rm Tr}\,{Z}^{k}\right),\end{split}

and similar if we permute the roles of
Z
and
Z^{\dagger}
, and
U
and
U^{\dagger}
.

As a corollary of Proposition
4.1
we get a general superintegrability rule for the chain of the matrix models associated with the nested hypergeometric tau-functions (
3.8
) with the weight generating functions of the form (
4.17
). For simplicity, let us assume that the diagonal group element

Remark 4.7

There is also an equivalent description,

\sigma_{m}=-

Similarly, if

Proposition 4.2
.

A nested hypergeometric tau-function (

5.1.
Hypergeometric tau-functions (
m=0

For

\begin{split}\tau_{n}^{0,\emptyset}({\bf t_{1}},{\bf t_{0}})&=\left<n\right|{\gamma_{+}({\bf t_{1}})}\,O_{0}\,{\gamma_{-}({\bf t_{0}})}\left|n\right>\\
&=c_{n}^{0}\sum_{\lambda_{0}}\,s_{\lambda_{0}}({\bf t_{1}})\,r^{(0)}_{\lambda_{0},n}\,s_{\lambda_{0}}({\bf t_{0}}),\end{split}

first considered in
[
KMMM95
]
and further investigated in
[
OS01
]
.
Matrix models for the hypergeometric tau-functions, associated with the weighted Hurwitz numbers are discussed in
[
ZJZ03
,
Orl04
,
OS05
,
Eyn08
,
Ale11
,
AMMN14
,
GGPN14
,
AC14
,
Orl17
]
.

Applying the general construction of Section
4.2
one can construct a multi-matrix model description for the generating function of the weighed Hurwitz numbers for any weight generating function of the form (
4.17
). From Proposition
4.1
we immediately get a description of the hypergeometric tau-functions by a chain of matrix models.

Corollary 5.1
.

All hypergeometric tau-functions (
5.1
) with diagonal group operator
O_{0}
associated with the weight generating functions (
4.17
) are described by the multi-matrix integrals, which are combinations
of the complex, unitary, and normal matrix integrals (
4.5
), (
4.7
), and (
4.9
). Moreover, these multi-matrix integrals have a chain structure with a pairwise interaction, and can be reduced to the eigenvalue integrals.

Let us consider an example. The weight generating function

(5.2)

G(z)=\frac{1+N_{3}^{-1}z}{(1+N_{1}^{-1}z)(1+N_{2}^{-1}z)}

corresponds to the content product

(5.3)

r_{\lambda}=r_{\lambda}^{+}(N_{3}^{-1})r_{\lambda}^{-}(N_{2}^{-1})r_{\lambda}^{-}(N_{1}^{-1}).

In this case the general construction of Section
4.2
yields

(5.4)

\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq\min(N_{1},N_{2},N_{3})}s_{\lambda}({\bf t_{1}})\,r_{\lambda}\,s_{\lambda}({\bf t_{0}})=\int_{\mathfrak{C}}\left[d\mu_{\mathfrak{C}}({Z}_{3})\right]\int_{\mathfrak{U^{2}}}\left[d\mu_{\mathfrak{U}}{(U_{2})}\right]\int_{\mathfrak{U^{2}}}\left[d\mu_{\mathfrak{U}}{(U_{1})}\right]\times\\
\times\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}t_{1,k}{\rm Tr}\,Z_{3}^{k}+\frac{1}{k}{\rm Tr}\,(Z_{3}^{\dagger})^{k}{\rm Tr}\,\tilde{U}_{2}^{k}+\frac{1}{k}{\rm Tr}\,(U_{2}^{\dagger})^{k}{\rm Tr}\,\tilde{U}_{1}^{k}+t_{0,k}{\rm Tr}\,(U_{1}^{\dagger})^{k}\right).

Here the lower index of the matrix indicates its size, for instance,
U_{2}
and
\tilde{U}_{2}
are
N_{2}\times N_{2}
unitary matrices.

For any two matrices
\Phi_{1}
and
\Phi_{2}
we have

(5.5)

\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k}{\rm Tr}\,\Phi_{1}^{k}\,{\rm Tr}\,\Phi_{2}^{k}\right)=\frac{1}{\det\left(I_{1}\otimes I_{2}-\Phi_{1}\otimes\Phi_{2}\right)},

where
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
are the identity matrices of the size same as
\Phi_{1}
and
\Phi_{2}
. Then the matrix integral (
5.4
) can be represented as

(5.6)

\int_{\mathfrak{C}}\left[d\mu_{\mathfrak{C}}({Z}_{3})\right]\int_{\mathfrak{U^{2}}}\left[d\mu_{\mathfrak{U}}{(U_{2})}\right]\int_{\mathfrak{U^{2}}}\left[d\mu_{\mathfrak{U}}{(U_{1})}\right]\frac{\displaystyle{\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}t_{1,k}{\rm Tr}\,Z_{3}^{k}+t_{0,k}{\rm Tr}\,(U_{1}^{\dagger})^{k}\right)}}{\det\left(I_{1}\otimes I_{2}-\tilde{U}_{1}\otimes U_{2}^{\dagger}\right)\det\left(I_{2}\otimes I_{3}-\tilde{U}_{2}\otimes Z_{3}^{\dagger}\right)}.

Here we see the general property of the constructed chains of the matrix integrals – all interactions are pairwise of the form
\exp(N\,{\rm Tr}\,\Phi_{1}\Phi_{2})
(in this example they are hidden in the expressions for the integration measures
[d\mu_{\bullet}]
) or
\frac{1}{\det\left(I_{1}\otimes I_{2}-\Phi_{1}\otimes\Phi_{2}\right)}
, and every matrix (and its conjugate for normal and complex matrix integrals) interacts with two other matrices of integration, except for the matrices corresponding to the ends of the chain, which interact with only one matrix each. These matrices, corresponding to ends of the chain (in our example they are
U_{1}
and
Z_{3}
) couple to the times of the integrable hierarchy
{\bf t_{0}}
and
{\bf t_{m+1}}
.

Remark 5.1
.

Note that the diagonal operators
O
commute with each other, therefore the order of the matrix integrals for the elementary factors of the diagonal group element
O
can be arbitrary. This is also correct for the elementary factors of the operators
O_{j}
in the matrix models of the nested hypergeometric tau-functions.

Remark 5.2
.

Let us note again that the general construction of Section
4.2
provides a universal matrix integral expression suitable for all nested hypergeometric tau-functions (

) with the weight generating functions (

(5.7)

\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq\min(N_{1},N_{2},N_{3})}s_{\lambda}(A)\,r_{\lambda}\,s_{\lambda}(B)\\
=\int_{\mathfrak{U}}\left[d{U_{1}}\right]\int_{\mathfrak{H}^{2}}\left[d{X}\right]\left[d{Y}\right]\int_{\mathfrak{U}}\left[d{U_{2}}\right]\frac{\exp\left(N_{1}{\rm Tr}\,{U_{1}^{\dagger}A}+N_{2}{\rm Tr}\,{U_{2}^{\dagger}B}+N_{3}\sqrt{-1}{\rm Tr}\,{XY}\right)}{\det\left(I_{1}\otimes I_{3}-{U}_{1}\otimes X\right)\det\left(I_{2}\otimes I_{3}-{U}_{2}\otimes Y^{\dagger}\right)}.

and

U_{1}
and

respectively and over the

and

A hypergeometric tau-function can be described by the cut-and-join operators of Section

, but because of duality (

\begin{split}\tau_{n}^{1,+}&=\left<n\right|{\gamma_{+}({\bf t_{2}})}\,O_{1}\,{\gamma_{+}({\bf t_{1}})}\,O_{0}\,{\gamma_{-}({\bf t_{0}})}\left|n\right>\\
&=c_{n}^{(0)}c_{n}^{(1)}\sum_{\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1}}s_{\lambda_{1}}({\bf t_{2}})\,r^{(1)}_{\lambda_{1},n}\,s_{\lambda_{0}/\lambda_{1}}({\bf t_{1}})\,r^{(0)}_{\lambda_{0},n}\,s_{\lambda_{0}}({\bf t_{0}}).\end{split}

However, below we will use the symmetry between the tau-functions labeled by different signs to investigate the relation between different versions of the cut-and-join descriptions.

If either
O_{0}=1
or
O_{1}=1
, then the nested hypergeometric tau-function reduces to the hypergeometric tau-function considered in the previous section. Therefore, we assume that both
O_{0}\neq 1
and

The tau-function given by the last line of (

) was recently introduced and investigated by Mironov, Mishnyakov, Morozov, Popolitov, Wang, and Zhao

]

Developing the approach of

]

The case
O_{0}=O_{1}^{-1}=O_{+}(\hbar)
can be identified with the generating function of interesting enumerative geometry invariants.
Namely, it provides the generating function of the fully simple maps, recently introduced and investigated by Borot and Garcia-Failde

[
BGF20
]
. We refer to this paper for the definition of the fully simple maps, and consider below only their generating function.
In
[
BDBKS21
]
Bychkov, Dunin-Barkowski, Kazarian, and Shadrin identify this generating function with the following vacuum expectation value

(5.17)

Z=\left<0\right|{\gamma_{+}({\bf t_{2}})}\,O_{+}^{-1}(\hbar)\,{\gamma_{+}(\hbar^{-1}{\bf t_{1}})}\,O_{+}(\hbar)\,e^{\frac{1}{2\hbar}J_{-2}}\left|0\right>,

where the group element
O_{+}(\hbar)
corresponds to the weight generating function
G_{+}(z)=1+\hbar z
.

Comparing (
5.17
) with (
5.16
) we see that

is a nested hypergeometric tau-function for
n=0
,
m=1
,
O_{0}=O_{1}^{-1}=O_{+}(\hbar)
, and
t_{0,k}=\delta_{k,2}/(2\hbar)
.
Therefore, we have an immediate corollary of Theorem
1
.

Corollary 5.2
.

The generating function for the fully simple maps has the following skew Schur function expansion

(5.18)

Z=\sum_{\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1}}\frac{r^{+}_{\lambda_{0}}(\hbar)}{r^{+}_{\lambda_{1}}(\hbar)}s_{\lambda_{1}}({\bf t_{2}})s_{\lambda_{0}/\lambda_{1}}({\bf t_{1}}/\hbar)s_{\lambda_{0}}(\delta_{k,2}/2\hbar).

In
[
BDBKS21
, Section 4.1]
the authors introduce the following generalization of the generating function (
5.17
)

(5.19)

Z^{\vee}=\left<0\right|{\gamma_{+}({\bf t_{2}})}\,O^{-1}\,{\gamma_{+}(\hbar^{-1}{\bf t_{1}})}\,O\,{\gamma_{-}(\hbar^{-1}{\bf t_{0}})}\left|0\right>,

where
O
is an arbitrary diagonal group element (
3.1
). The enumerative geometry interpretation of this generating function is not clear yet.

This is a nested hypergeometric tau-function with
m=1
.
Again, for this more general case we have an immediate corollary of Theorem
1
.

Corollary 5.3
.

The generating function (
5.19
) has the following skew Schur function expansion

(5.20)

Z^{\vee}=\sum_{\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1}}\frac{r_{\lambda_{0}}}{r_{\lambda_{1}}}s_{\lambda_{1}}({\bf t_{2}})s_{\lambda_{0}/\lambda_{1}}({\bf t_{1}}/\hbar)s_{\lambda_{0}}({\bf t_{0}}/\hbar),

where
r
is the content product for the operator
O
.

If operators
O_{0}
and
O_{1}
in (
5.16
) describe the weight generating functions (
4.17
), then the construction of Section
4.2
immediately gives us a multi-matrix model for the tau-function
\tau_{0}^{1,+}
. Let us consider an example associated with the fully simple hypermaps, which corresponds to
O_{0}=O_{1}^{-1}=O_{+}(\hbar)
, that is the tau-function

(5.21)

\left<0\right|{\gamma_{+}({\bf t_{2}})}\,O_{+}^{-1}(\hbar)\,{\gamma_{+}(\hbar^{-1}{\bf t_{1}})}\,O_{+}(\hbar)\,{\gamma_{-}(\hbar^{-1}{\bf t_{0}})}\left|0\right>.

In this case, if we put
\hbar=N^{-1}
, our general construction yields the following matrix model:

(5.22)

\sum_{\ell(\lambda_{0}),\ell(\lambda_{1})\leq N}\frac{r^{+}_{\lambda_{0}}(N^{-1})}{r^{+}_{\lambda_{1}}(N^{-1})}s_{\lambda_{1}}({\bf t_{2}})s_{\lambda_{0}/\lambda_{1}}(N{\bf t_{1}})s_{\lambda_{0}}(N{\bf t_{0}})\\
=\int_{\mathfrak{U^{2}}}\left[d\mu_{\mathfrak{U}}{(U)}\right]\int_{\mathfrak{C}}\left[d\mu_{\mathfrak{C}}({Z})\right]\frac{\displaystyle{\exp\left({\rm Tr}\,\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}t_{2,k}{\tilde{U}}^{k}+N\,t_{1,k}{Z}^{k}+N\,t_{0,k}{Z^{\dagger}}^{k}\right)}}{\det\left(I\otimes I-Z\otimes U^{\dagger}\right)}.

Moreover, because of the additional symmetries in this case the matrix integral can be further simplified. Namely, since the size of all matrices is the same, we can integrate out one of the unitary matrices:

(5.23)

\sum_{\ell(\lambda_{0}),\ell(\lambda_{1})\leq N}\frac{r^{+}_{\lambda_{0}}(N^{-1})}{r^{+}_{\lambda_{1}}(N^{-1})}s_{\lambda_{1}}({\bf t_{2}})s_{\lambda_{0}/\lambda_{1}}(N{\bf t_{1}})s_{\lambda_{0}}(N{\bf t_{0}})\\
=\int_{\mathfrak{U}}\left[dU\right]\int_{\mathfrak{C}}\left[d\mu_{\mathfrak{C}}({Z})\right]\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}t_{2,k}{\rm Tr}\,{U}^{k}+N\,{\rm Tr}\,\left(U^{\dagger}Z+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}t_{1,k}{Z}^{k}+t_{0,k}{Z^{\dagger}}^{k}\right)\right).

This expression can be further simplified if we consider the Miwa parametrization of the variables
{\bf t_{2}}
, that is, if we put
t_{2,k}=\frac{1}{k}{\rm Tr}\,\Lambda^{k}
for a diagonal
N\times N
matrix
\Lambda
,

(5.24)

\sum_{\ell(\lambda_{0}),\ell(\lambda_{1})\leq N}\frac{r^{+}_{\lambda_{0}}(N^{-1})}{r^{+}_{\lambda_{1}}(N^{-1})}s_{\lambda_{1}}(\Lambda)s_{\lambda_{0}/\lambda_{1}}(N{\bf t_{1}})s_{\lambda_{0}}(N{\bf t_{0}})\\
=\int_{\mathfrak{C}}\left[d\mu_{\mathfrak{C}}({Z})\right]\exp\left(N\,{\rm Tr}\,\left(\Lambda Z+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}t_{1,k}{Z}^{k}+t_{0,k}{{Z^{\dagger}}^{k}}\right)\right).

This matrix integral is equivalent to the two-matrix model

(5.25)

\sum_{\ell(\lambda_{0}),\ell(\lambda_{1})\leq N}\frac{r^{+}_{\lambda_{0}}(N^{-1})}{r^{+}_{\lambda_{1}}(N^{-1})}s_{\lambda_{1}}(\Lambda)s_{\lambda_{0}/\lambda_{1}}(N{\bf t_{1}})s_{\lambda_{0}}(N{\bf t_{0}})\\
=\int_{\mathfrak{H}^{2}}\left[d{X}\right]\left[d{Y}\right]\exp\left(N\,{\rm Tr}\,\left({\Lambda Y}+\sqrt{-1}{XY}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}t_{1,k}{Y}^{k}+t_{0,k}{X^{k}}\right)\right).

The last identity was derived in
[
MMM+23b
]
. The matrix models (
5.22

5.25
) describe the generating function of fully simple hypermaps (
5.21
).

For

the integral in

is Gaussian and can be computed explicitly, so we get

(5.26)

\sum_{\ell(\lambda_{0}),\ell(\lambda_{1})\leq N}\frac{r^{+}_{\lambda_{0}}(N^{-1})}{r^{+}_{\lambda_{1}}(N^{-1})}s_{\lambda_{1}}(\Lambda)s_{\lambda_{0}/\lambda_{1}}(N{\bf t_{1}})s_{\lambda_{0}}(N\delta_{k,2}/2)\\
=\int_{\mathfrak{H}}\left[d{Y}\right]\exp\left(N\,{\rm Tr}\,\left(Y\Lambda-{Y^{2}}/2+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}t_{1,k}{Y}^{k}\right)\right).

The Hermitian matrix model in the right hand side is the model for the generating function for the fully simple maps (
5.17
), derived by Borot and Garcia-Failde
[
BGF20
]
.

Applying (

(5.45)

\begin{split}&\sum_{\ell(\lambda_{0}),\ell(\lambda_{1}),\ell(\lambda_{2})\leq\min(N_{1},N_{2})}\frac{r^{+}_{\lambda_{0}}(N_{1}^{-1})}{r^{+}_{\lambda_{1}}(N_{1}^{-1})}\frac{r^{+}_{\lambda_{1}}(N_{2}^{-1})}{r^{+}_{\lambda_{2}}(N_{2}^{-1})}s_{\lambda_{2}}({\bf t_{3}})\,s_{\lambda_{1}/\lambda_{2}}{(\bf t_{2}})\,s_{\lambda_{0}/\lambda_{1}}{(\bf t_{1}})s_{\lambda_{0}}({\bf t_{0}})\\

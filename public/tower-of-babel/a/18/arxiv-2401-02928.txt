The categories of corings and coalgebras over a ring are locally countably presentable
Leonid Positselski

Abstract
. For any commutative ring R R , we show that the categories of R R -coalgebras and cocommutative R R -coalgebras are locally ℵ 1 \aleph_{1} -presentable, while the categories of R R -flat R R -coalgebras are ℵ 1 \aleph_{1} -accessible. Similarly, for any associative ring R R , the category of R R -corings is locally ℵ 1 \aleph_{1} -presentable, while the category of R R - R R -bimodule flat R R -corings is ℵ 1 \aleph_{1} -accessible. The cardinality of the ring R R can be arbitrarily large. We also discuss R R -corings with surjective counit and flat kernel. The proofs are straightforward applications of an abstract category-theoretic principle going back to Ulmer. For right or two-sided R R -module flat R R -corings, our cardinality estimate for the accessibility rank is not as good. A generalization to comonoid objects in accessible monoidal categories is also considered.



Introduction

The aim of this paper is to close a gaping hole in contemporary
literature concerning the local presentability rank of the categories
of coalgebras and corings over a fixed ring
R
.
There is little really new material in this paper, as our results
go back to the unpublished 1977 preprint of Ulmer
[
24
]
.
The results of this paper concerning various classes of
flat

coalgebras and corings are relatively more original.

Some ideas of the preprint
[
24

were taken up and developed in
a different form in the 1984 dissertation of Bird
[
3

;
but that remained unpublished, too.
In the end, it appears that Ulmer’s ideas were almost completely
forgotten and not incorporated into the contemporary body of knowledge.
This created the gaping hole mentioned in the previous paragraph.
We hope that this paper will fare better than Ulmer’s preprint and
people will remember the idea now.

One approach used in contemporary literature goes back to

This theorem claims that, for any commutative ring

nonexactness of the tensor product in the theory of coalgebras or
corings over a ring.
Basically, the very notion of a subcoalgebra does not make much
sense without the purity condition.
It was emphasized in the present author’s recent paper
[
17
]

that the purification-based approach to accessibility often leads to
suboptimal cardinality bounds.

The references to
[

]
in connection with the claim that

Finally, let us say a few words about our motivation.

R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}}
are precisely all
the flat
R
-modules that are
\kappa
-presentable in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
.

Proof.

See
[

or

2.
Comonoid Objects in Monoidal Categories

We suggest the book
[
11
, Section VII.1]
as a reference on
monoidal categories, and
[
11
, Sections VII.7 and XI.1]
for
symmetric monoidal categories.

Let us just fix the notation.
A
monoidal category

\mathsf{M}
is supposed to be associative and
unital; the monoidal operation is denoted by
\otimes\colon\mathsf{M}\times\mathsf{M}\longrightarrow\mathsf{M}
and called the
tensor product
; the unit object is denoted
by
\mathbf{1}\in\mathsf{M}
.
The
associativity and unitality constraints
are functorial
isomorphisms
(K\otimes L)\otimes M\simeq K\otimes(L\otimes M)
and
\mathbf{1}\otimes M\simeq M\simeq M\otimes\mathbf{1}
given for all objects
K
,
L
,
M\in\mathsf{M}
.
A
symmetric monoidal category
is also endowed with
a
commutativity constraint
, which is a functorial isomorphism

K\otimes L\simeq L\otimes K
for all objects
K
,
L\in\mathsf{M}
.

We do not go into a discussion of the commutativity of pentagonal
and hexagonal coherence diagrams, as the questions of coherence play
no role in our exposition below.
Instead, we will adopt a policy of benign neglect and write simply

K\otimes L\otimes M
as a common notation for either
(K\otimes L)\otimes M
or

K\otimes(L\otimes M)
.

A
comonoid object
in a monoidal category
\mathsf{M}
is an object

C\in\mathsf{M}
endowed with two morphisms of
comultiplication

\mu\colon C\longrightarrow C\otimes C
and
counit

\epsilon\colon C\longrightarrow\mathbf{1}

satisfying the conventional coassociativity and counitality axioms.
Specifically, the two compositions

C\longrightarrow C\otimes C\rightrightarrows C\otimes C\otimes C

of the comultiplication morphism with two morphisms induced by
the comultiplication morphism must be equal to each other,

(\mu\otimes\mathrm{id}_{C})\circ\mu=(\mathrm{id}_{C}\otimes\mu)\circ\mu
, and the two compositions

C\longrightarrow C\otimes C\rightrightarrows C

of the comultiplication morphism with the two morphisms induced by
the counit morphism must be equal to the identity morphism,

(\epsilon\otimes\mathrm{id}_{C})\circ\mu=\mathrm{id}_{C}=(\mathrm{id}_{C}\otimes\epsilon)\circ\mu
.

In a symmetric monoidal category
\mathsf{M}
, one can speak of cocommutative
comonoid objects.
A comonoid object
C
is said to be
cocommutative
if
the composition

C\longrightarrow C\otimes C\longrightarrow C\otimes C

of the comultiplication morphism with the commutativity constraint
morphism
\sigma_{C}\colon C\otimes C\longrightarrow C\otimes C
is equal to
the comultiplication morphism,
\sigma_{C}\circ\mu=\mu
.

Morphisms of comonoid objects
C\longrightarrow D
are defined in the obvious
way.
We denote the category of comonoid objects in a monoidal category

\mathsf{M}
by
\mathsf{M}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comon}}}
.
When
\mathsf{M}
is a symmetric monoidal category, the full subcategory of
cocommutative comonoid objects is denoted by
\mathsf{M}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Cocom}}}\subset\mathsf{M}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comon}}}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

(a)
Let
\kappa
be a regular cardinal,
\lambda<\kappa
be
a smaller infinite cardinal, and
\mathsf{M}
be a monoidal category.
Assume that the underlying category of
\mathsf{M}
is
\kappa
-accessible,
that colimits of
\lambda
-indexed chains exist in it, and that
the monoidal operation functor
\otimes\colon\mathsf{M}\times\mathsf{M}\longrightarrow\mathsf{M}
preserves

\kappa
-directed colimits (in both arguments).
Then the category of comonoid objects
\mathsf{M}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comon}}}
is

\kappa
-accessible.
An object of
\mathsf{M}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comon}}}
is
\kappa
-presentable if and only if
its underlying object is
\kappa
-presentable in
\mathsf{M}
.

(b)
In the context of part (a), assume additionally that

\mathsf{M}
is a symmetric monoidal category.
Then the category of cocommutative comonoid objects
\mathsf{M}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Cocom}}}
is

\kappa
-accessible.
An object of
\mathsf{M}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Cocom}}}
is
\kappa
-presentable if and only if
its underlying object is
\kappa
-presentable in
\mathsf{M}
.

Proof.

The argument is based on Theorems
1.2

and
1.3
together with
Proposition
1.1
.
Let us spell out the proof of part (b); part (a) is similar.

In order to apply Theorem
1.2
, put
\mathsf{K}=\mathsf{M}

and
\mathsf{L}=\mathsf{M}\times\mathsf{M}
.
Let
F\colon\mathsf{K}\longrightarrow\mathsf{L}
be the functor taking an object
C\in\mathsf{M}

to the pair of objects
(C,C)\in\mathsf{M}\times\mathsf{M}
, and let
G\colon\mathsf{K}\longrightarrow\mathsf{L}
be the functor taking the object
C
to the pair of
objects
(C\otimes C,\>\mathbf{1})\in\mathsf{M}\times\mathsf{M}
.
Then the inserter category
\mathsf{D}
is the category of objects

C\in\mathsf{M}
endowed with two morphisms
\mu\colon C\longrightarrow C\otimes C
and

\epsilon\colon C\longrightarrow\mathbf{1}
.

Let us spell out a couple of additional words about how this works.
An object
C\in\mathsf{M}
as such is endowed with
no
multiplication
or unit morphism structure.
It is just an object of a monoidal category
\mathsf{M}
(such as, e. g.,
an
R
-module, if
\mathsf{M}
is the monoidal category of modules over
a commutative ring
R
).
By the definition, an object of the inserter category
\mathsf{D}
is
an object
C\in\mathsf{K}=\mathsf{M}
together with an arbitrary morphism

F(C)\longrightarrow G(C)
in
\mathsf{L}=\mathsf{M}\times\mathsf{M}
.
This means a morphism
(C,C)\longrightarrow(C\otimes C,\>\mathbf{1})
in

\mathsf{M}\times\mathsf{M}
, i. e., an arbitrary pair of morphisms

\mu\colon C\longrightarrow C\otimes C
and
\epsilon\colon C\longrightarrow\mathbf{1}
.
So an object of
\mathsf{D}
is an object
C\in\mathsf{M}
endowed with two
pieces of additional data, viz., two arbitrary morphisms

\mu\colon C\longrightarrow C\otimes C
and
\epsilon\colon C\longrightarrow\mathbf{1}
.
The morphisms in
\mathsf{D}
are defined in the obvious way as
the morphisms in
\mathsf{M}
compatible with the additional structure
provided by the maps
\mu
and
\epsilon
.

By assumption and by Proposition
1.1
, both
the categories
\mathsf{K}
and
\mathsf{L}
are
\kappa
-accessible.
The assumptions of Theorem
1.2
are satisfied,
and we can conclude that the category
\mathsf{D}
is
\kappa
-accessible.
It is clear that colimits of
\lambda
-indexed chains exist in

\mathsf{D}
and are preserved by the forgetful functor
\mathsf{D}\longrightarrow\mathsf{M}
.
Furthermore, Theorem
1.2
provides a description
of the full subcategory of
\kappa
-presentable objects in
\mathsf{D}
.

In order to apply Theorem
1.3
, put
\mathsf{K}=\mathsf{D}

and
\mathsf{L}=\mathsf{M}^{4}=\mathsf{M}\times\mathsf{M}\times\mathsf{M}\times\mathsf{M}
.
Let
F\colon\mathsf{K}\longrightarrow\mathsf{L}
be the functor taking a triple

(C,\mu,\epsilon)\in\mathsf{D}
to the quadruple of objects

(C,C,C,C)\in\mathsf{L}
, and let
G\colon\mathsf{K}\longrightarrow\mathsf{L}
be the functor taking
the same object
(C,\mu,\epsilon)\in\mathsf{D}
to the quadruple of objects

(C\otimes C\otimes C,\>C,\>C,\>C\otimes C)\in\mathsf{L}
.

Let
\phi\colon F\longrightarrow G
be the natural transformation acting by
the following quadruple of morphisms
\phi_{1}\colon C\longrightarrow C\otimes C\otimes C
,

\phi_{2}\colon C\longrightarrow C
,
\phi_{3}\colon C\longrightarrow C
, and

\phi_{4}\colon C\longrightarrow C\otimes C
.
The morphism
\phi_{1}
is the composition
(\mu\otimes\mathrm{id}_{C})\circ\mu\colon C\longrightarrow C\otimes C\otimes C
.
The morphism
\phi_{2}
is the composition
(\epsilon\otimes\mathrm{id}_{C})\circ\mu\colon C\longrightarrow C
.
The morphism
\phi_{3}
is the composition
(\mathrm{id}_{C}\otimes\epsilon)\circ\mu\colon C\longrightarrow C
.
The morphism
\phi_{4}
is the composition
\sigma_{C}\circ\mu\colon C\longrightarrow C\otimes C
.

Let
\psi\colon F\longrightarrow G
be the natural transformation acting by
the following quadruple of morphisms
\psi_{1}\colon C\longrightarrow C\otimes C\otimes C
,

\psi_{2}\colon C\longrightarrow C
,
\psi_{3}\colon C\longrightarrow C
, and

\psi_{4}\colon C\longrightarrow C\otimes C
.
The morphism
\psi_{1}
is the composition
(\mathrm{id}_{C}\otimes\mu)\circ\mu\colon C\longrightarrow C\otimes C\otimes C
.
The morphisms
\psi_{2}
and
\psi_{3}
are the identity morphisms

\mathrm{id}_{C}\colon C\longrightarrow C
.
The morphism
\psi_{4}
is the morphism
\mu\colon C\longrightarrow C\otimes C
.
Then the equifier category
\mathsf{E}
is the category of cocommutative
comonoid objects in
\mathsf{M}
, that is
\mathsf{E}=\mathsf{M}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Cocom}}}
.

The construction of the equifier category imposes the axioms of
coassociativty, counitality, and cocommutativity on the morphisms

\mu\colon C\longrightarrow C\otimes C
and
\epsilon\colon C\longrightarrow\penalty\mathbf{1}
.
This is accomplished by passing to the full subcategory
\mathsf{E}
of
the category
\mathsf{K}=\mathsf{D}
.
The full subcategory
\mathsf{E}\subset\mathsf{D}
consists of all the objects

(C,\mu,\epsilon)\in\mathsf{D}
for which the morphisms
\mu
and
\epsilon

satisfy the coassociativity, counitality, and cocommutativity equations.

By Proposition
1.1
and the discussion above,
both the categories
\mathsf{K}=\mathsf{D}
and
\mathsf{L}
are
\kappa
-accessible.
The assumptions of Theorem
1.3
are satisfied,
and we can conclude that the category
\mathsf{E}=\mathsf{M}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Cocom}}}
is

\kappa
-accessible.
Theorem
1.3
also provides the desired description
of the full subcategory of
\kappa
-presentable objects in
\mathsf{E}
.
∎

3.
Coalgebras over a Commutative Ring

Let
R
be a commutative ring.
A (
coassociative, counital
)
coalgebra
over
R
is
a comonoid object in the monoidal category of
R
-modules
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}

with respect to the operation of tensor product over
R
.
In other words, a coalgebra
C
is an
R
-module endowed with two
maps of
comultiplication

\mu\colon C\longrightarrow C\otimes_{R}C
and

counit

\epsilon\colon C\longrightarrow R
, which must be
R
-linear maps
satisfying the conventional coassociativity and counitality axioms.
Specifically, the two compositions

C\longrightarrow C\otimes_{R}C\rightrightarrows C\otimes_{R}C\otimes_{R}C

of the comultiplication map with two maps induced by
the comultiplication map must be equal to each other, that is

(\mu\otimes\mathrm{id}_{C})\circ\mu=(\mathrm{id}_{C}\otimes\mu)\circ\mu
, and the two compositions

C\longrightarrow C\otimes_{R}C\rightrightarrows C

of the comultiplication map with two maps induced by
the counit map must be equal to the identity map, that is

(\epsilon\otimes\mathrm{id}_{C})\circ\mu=\mathrm{id}_{C}=(\mathrm{id}_{C}\otimes\epsilon)\circ\mu
.

An
R
-coalgebra
C
is said to be
cocommutative
if

\sigma_{C}\circ\mu=\mu
,

C\longrightarrow C\otimes_{R}C\longrightarrow C\otimes_{R}C,

where
\sigma_{C}\colon C\otimes_{R}C\longrightarrow C\otimes_{R}C
is the map permuting
the tensor factors.
The notion of a cocommutative
R
-coalgebra makes sense because

R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
is naturally a symmetric monoidal category.

Morphisms of coalgebras
C\longrightarrow D
are defined in the obvious way
as
R
-linear maps compatible with the comultiplication and counit.
We denote the category of
R
-coalgebras by
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Coalg}}}
and
the full subcategory of cocommutative
R
-coalgebras by

R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Cocom}}}\subset R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Coalg}}}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

All colimits exist in the categories
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Coalg}}}
and
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Cocom}}}
.
The forgetful functors
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Coalg}}}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
and

R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Cocom}}}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
preserve colimits.

Proof.

Let us discuss the colimits in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Coalg}}}
; the situation in

R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Cocom}}}
is similar.
It suffices to check that coproducts and coequalizers exist in

R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Coalg}}}
and are preserved by the forgetful functor to
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
.
Given a family of
R
-coalgebras
(C_{\xi})_{\xi\in\Xi}
, it is easy
to construct a coalgebra structure on the direct sum

\bigoplus_{\xi\in\Xi}C_{\xi}
taken in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
and show that
the resulting coalgebra is the coproduct of
C_{\xi}
in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Coalg}}}
.
Let us explain the coequalizers in more detail.

Suppose given a pair of parallel morphisms of
R
-coalgebras

f
,
g\colon C\rightrightarrows D
.
We claim that the image
I
of the
R
-module map
f-g\colon C\longrightarrow D

is a coideal in
D
.
This means that the counit map
\epsilon_{D}\colon D\longrightarrow R

vanishes in restriction to
I
and the image of the composition

I\longrightarrow D\longrightarrow D\otimes_{R}D
of the inclusion map
I\longrightarrow D

with the comultiplication map
\mu_{D}\colon D\longrightarrow D\otimes_{R}D
is contained
in the image of the map
I\otimes_{R}D\oplus D\otimes_{R}I\longrightarrow D\otimes_{R}D

induced by the inclusion map
I\longrightarrow D
.

Indeed, one clearly has
\epsilon_{D}(f(c)-g(c))=\epsilon_{D}(f(c))-\epsilon_{D}(g(c))=\epsilon_{C}(c)-\epsilon_{C}(c)=0
for all
c\in C
.
Now let the element
\mu_{C}(c)\in C\otimes_{R}C
be equal to

\sum_{i=1}^{n}c^{\prime}_{i}\otimes c^{\prime\prime}_{i}
, where
c^{\prime}_{i}
,
c^{\prime\prime}_{i}\in C
.
Then we have
\mu_{D}(f(c)-g(c))=(f\otimes f-g\otimes g)(\mu_{C}(c))=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(f(c^{\prime}_{i})\otimes f(c^{\prime\prime}_{i})-g(c^{\prime}_{i})\otimes g(c^{\prime\prime}_{i}))=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(f(c^{\prime}_{i})-g(c^{\prime}_{i}))\otimes f(c^{\prime\prime}_{i})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}g(c^{\prime}_{i})\otimes(f(c^{\prime\prime}_{i})-g(c^{\prime\prime}_{i}))\in\im(I\otimes_{R}D\oplus D\otimes_{R}I)\subset D\otimes_{R}D
, as desired.

It follows that there is a unique
R
-coalgebra structure on
the quotient
R
-module
E=D/I
such that the natural surjective map

p\colon D\longrightarrow E
is a coalgebra morphism (see, e. g.,

[
4
, Section 2.4]
, and notice that the image of the map

I\otimes_{R}D\oplus D\otimes_{R}I\longrightarrow D\otimes_{R}D
is always contained in
the kernel of the map
p\otimes p\colon D\otimes_{R}D\longrightarrow E\otimes_{R}E
;
so any coideal in the sense explained above is also a coideal in
the sense of
[
4
]
).
One can readily check that the morphism
p\colon D\longrightarrow E
is
the coequalizer of the morphisms
f
and
g
in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Coalg}}}
.

In fact, it is easy to see that the image of the map

I\otimes_{R}D\oplus D\otimes_{R}I\longrightarrow D\otimes_{R}D
coincides with
the kernel of the map
p\otimes p\colon D\otimes_{R}D\longrightarrow E\otimes_{R}E
.
So a coideal in the sense explained above is the same thing as
a coideal in the sense of
[
4
]
.
∎

The following theorem is the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.2
.

(a)

\aleph_{1}

Remark 3.3
.

Numerous variations on the theme of

\aleph_{1}
-presentable

Let us mention some of them.

(2) A Lie coalgebra

R

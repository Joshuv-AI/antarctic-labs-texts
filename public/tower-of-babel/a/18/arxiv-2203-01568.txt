A modified weak Galerkin method for 𝑯 ⁡ ( curl ) \boldsymbol{H}(\mathrm{curl}) -elliptic problem
Ming Tang, Liuqiang Zhong, Yingying Xie

Abstract
In this paper, we design and analysis a modified weak Galerkin (MWG) finite element method for 𝑯 ⁡ ( curl ) − \boldsymbol{H}(\mathrm{curl})- elliptic problem. We first introduce a new discrete weak curl operator and the MWG finite element space. The modified weak Galerkin method does not require the penalty parameter by comparing with traditional DG methods. We prove optimal error estimates in energy norm. At last, we provide the numerical results to confirm these theoretical results.

Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a bounded Lipschitz polyhedron with boundary
\partial\Omega
. We consider the following
\boldsymbol{H}(\mathrm{curl})
elliptic problem:

\displaystyle\mathbf{curl}\mathrm{curl}\boldsymbol{u}+\boldsymbol{u}=\boldsymbol{f},\quad\mbox{in}\ \Omega,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{t}=0,\quad\mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega,

(1.2)

where
\boldsymbol{t}
is the unit tangent on
\partial\Omega

\boldsymbol{u}
is the electric or magnetic field,
\boldsymbol{f}
is a given vector field depending on a given external source field.
We recall that,
\mathrm{curl}\ \boldsymbol{v}={\partial v_{2}}/{\partial x}-{\partial v_{1}}/{\partial y}
for a vector field
\boldsymbol{v}=(v_{1},v_{2})
, while
\mathbf{curl}\ \phi=({\partial\phi}/{\partial y},-{\partial\phi}/{\partial x})
for a scalar function
\phi

Weak Galerkin (WG) method was introduced by Wang and Ye
[
22
]
for solving a second order elliptic problem. The weak function
v=\{v^{0},v^{b}\}
was used as the approximate function space, and weak gradient operator was used instead of classical gradient operator. The first component
v^{0}
can be understood as the value of
v
in the interior of elements, and the second component
v^{b}
can be understood as the value of
v
on the boundary of elements.
WG method can introduce stabilization terms in bilinear form to describe the continuity of the weak functions.
Moreover,
WG method can also be applied in arbitrary polygon or polyhedron partitions and has strong flexibility and robustness, whose corresponding bilinear form is independent on parameters
[
12
]
. Hence, WG method has been developed to solve many equations, such as the Stokes equations
[
23
,
24
,
28
]
, the Helmholtz equation
[
15
,
11
,
2
]
, the biharmonic equation
[
10
,

,
8
,
13
,
21
]
, parabolic equations
[
5
,
29
]
, Navier-Stokes equations
[
30
]
, Oseen equations
[
7
]
, Brinkman equation
[
9
]
, Darcy-Stokes equation
[
1
]
and Darcy equation
[
6
]
. Recently,
there are also some works with Maxwell equations
[
14
,
17
,
19
]
.
For example, Mu, Wang, Ye and Zhang
[
14
]
applied the WG method to the time-harmonic Maxwell equations, and gave a numerical method with the optimal order of convergence in certain discrete norms. Shields, Li and Machorro
[
17
]
developed the WG method to solve the time-dependent Maxwell equations, and proved the optimal order of convergence in the energy norm. Wang
[
19
]
introduced a new discrete scheme of time harmonic Maxwell equations in connected domain based on WG method, and established the error estimates of the optimal order of various discrete Sobolev norms.

Modified weak Galerkin(MWG) method was put forward by Wang, Malluwawadu, Gao and McMillan
[
26
]
for elliptic problem.
The motivation of MWG method is to reduce the number of unknowns, in which
v^{b}
is replaced by the average of

Comparing with WG method, MWG method contains less unknowns, while the accuracy stays the same. Then, MWG method has also found its way to other problems, such as the parabolic problems

However, to our best knowledge, there are not any published literatures for the MWG discretization of
\boldsymbol{H}(\mathrm{curl})
elliptic problem.
This manuscript will provide a MWG method for the
\boldsymbol{H}(\mathrm{curl})
elliptic problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2

In this manuscript, we shall follow the state-of-the-art theoretical analysis in

We stress

(2) lower order term of the
\boldsymbol{H}(\mathrm{curl})
elliptic problem, which is missed in the second order elliptic problem, should be considered carefully. To conquer these problem, we establish some technical equations.

2.1
The modified discrete weak curl

In order to describe the modified weak curl, we recall the concepts of weak curl and discrete weak curl.
A weak function on the element
\tau
refers to a function
\boldsymbol{v}=\{\boldsymbol{v}^{0},\boldsymbol{v}^{b}\}
where
\boldsymbol{v}^{0}\in\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\tau),\boldsymbol{v}^{b}\cdot\boldsymbol{t}\in L^{2}(\partial\tau)
.
The first component
\boldsymbol{v}^{0}
can be understood as the value of
\boldsymbol{v}
in the interior of
\tau
, and the second component
\boldsymbol{v}^{b}
is the value of
\boldsymbol{v}
on the boundary of
\tau
. We denote the space of weak functions on
\tau
as:

V(\tau):=\{\boldsymbol{v}=\{\boldsymbol{v}^{0},\boldsymbol{v}^{b}\}:\boldsymbol{v}^{0}\in\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\tau),\boldsymbol{v}^{b}\cdot\boldsymbol{t}\in L^{2}(\partial\tau)\},

where
\boldsymbol{t}
is the unit tangent on
\partial\tau
oriented counter-clockwisely.

Definition 1

(Weak curl)
.

For any
\boldsymbol{v}\in V(\tau)
, the weak curl of
\boldsymbol{v}
is defined as a continuous linear functional

\mathrm{curl}^{c}_{w}\ \boldsymbol{v}\in H^{1}(\tau)
whose action on each
\phi\in H^{1}(\tau)
is given by

(\mathrm{curl}^{c}_{w}\ \boldsymbol{v},\phi)_{\tau}:=(\boldsymbol{v}^{0},\mathbf{curl}\ \phi)_{\tau}+\langle\boldsymbol{v}^{b}\cdot\boldsymbol{t},\phi\rangle_{\partial\tau},

where
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\tau}
is the
L^{2}
inner product on
\tau
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\partial\tau}
is the
L^{2}
inner product on
\partial\tau
.

For a positive constant
k\geq 1
, we denote by
P_{k}(\tau)
the set of polynomials on
\tau
with degree no more than
k
.

Definition 2

(Discrete Weak Curl)
.

The discrete weak curl operator, denoted by
\mathrm{curl}^{d}_{w,k-1,\tau}
, is defined as the unique polynomial

\mathrm{curl}^{d}_{w,k-1,\tau}\ \boldsymbol{v}\in P_{k-1}(\tau)
for any
\boldsymbol{v}\in V(\tau)
that satisfies:

(\mathrm{curl}^{d}_{w,k-1,\tau}\ \boldsymbol{v},\phi)_{\tau}:=(\boldsymbol{v}^{0},\mathbf{curl}\ \phi)_{\tau}+\langle\boldsymbol{v}^{b}\cdot\boldsymbol{t},\phi\rangle_{\partial\tau},\forall\phi\in P_{k-1}(\tau).

The definitions of weak curl and discrete weak curl for 3D can be found in
[
14
]
or
[
17
]
, respectively.

Similar to the definition of modified weak gradient in
[
26
]
, for the function
\boldsymbol{v}=\{\boldsymbol{v}^{0},\boldsymbol{v}^{b}\}\in V(\tau)
, the selected component
\boldsymbol{v}^{b}=\left\{\kern-2.29996pt\left\{\boldsymbol{v}^{0}\right\}\kern-2.29996pt\right\}_{e}
is not an independent component because
\boldsymbol{v}^{b}=\left\{\kern-2.29996pt\left\{\boldsymbol{v}^{0}\right\}\kern-2.29996pt\right\}_{e}
is determined by
\boldsymbol{v}^{0}
. Futhermore, we define a discrete modified weak curl as follows.

Definition 3

(Discrete Modified Weak Curl)
.

The discrete modified weak curl operator, denoted by
\mathrm{curl}_{w,k-1,\tau}
, is defined as the unique polynomial

\mathrm{curl}_{w,k-1,\tau}\ \boldsymbol{v}\in P_{k-1}(\tau)
for any
\boldsymbol{v}\in V(\tau)
that satisfies:

(\mathrm{curl}_{w,k-1,\tau}\ \boldsymbol{v},\phi)_{\tau}:=(\boldsymbol{v}^{0},\mathbf{curl}\ \phi)_{\tau}+\langle\left\{\kern-2.29996pt\left\{\boldsymbol{v}^{0}\right\}\kern-2.29996pt\right\}_{e}\cdot\boldsymbol{t},\phi\rangle_{\partial\tau},\forall\phi\in P_{k-1}(\tau).

(2.7)

Without confusion, we simply denote
\mathrm{curl}_{w,k-1,\tau}
as
\mathrm{curl}_{w}
in the following part.

2.2
The modified weak Galerkin method for model problem

Next, for

\displaystyle V_{h}:=\left\{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}=\left\{\boldsymbol{v}^{0}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}^{b}_{h}\right\}:\left.\boldsymbol{v}_{h}^{0}\right|_{\tau}\in(P_{k}(\tau))^{2},\left.\boldsymbol{v}^{b}_{h}\right|_{e}=\left\{\kern-2.29996pt\left\{\boldsymbol{v}^{0}_{h}\right\}\kern-2.29996pt\right\}_{e},e\subset\partial\tau,\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\right\},

\displaystyle V^{0}_{h}:=\left\{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}=\left\{\boldsymbol{v}^{0}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}^{b}_{h}\right\}:\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in V_{h},\left.\boldsymbol{v}^{b}_{h}\right|_{e}\cdot\boldsymbol{t}=0,e\in\partial\Omega\right\}.

The modified weak Galerkin method for (

) and (
1.2

\boldsymbol{u}_{h}=\left\{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{0},\left\{\kern-2.29996pt\left\{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{0}\right\}\kern-2.29996pt\right\}_{e}\right\}\in V^{0}_{h}
which satisfies

a(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})=(\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{v}^{0}_{h})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}},\quad\forall\boldsymbol{v}_{h}=\left\{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}^{0},\left\{\kern-2.29996pt\left\{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}^{0}\right\}\kern-2.29996pt\right\}_{e}\right\}\in V^{0}_{h},

Lemma 2.1
.

The modified weak Galerkin methods defined in (
2.8
) has a unique solution.

Proof.

Since (
2.8
) is essentially a linear system of equations, we only need to prove the
uniqueness. Let
\boldsymbol{f}=0

\boldsymbol{v}_{h}=\boldsymbol{u}_{h}
in (

(\mathrm{curl}_{w}\ \boldsymbol{u}_{h},\mathrm{curl}_{w}\ \boldsymbol{u}_{h})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}+(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{0},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{0})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\sum\limits_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}h^{-1}_{e}\left\langle\left[\kern-0.89996pt\left[\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{0}\right]\kern-0.89996pt\right]_{e},\left[\kern-0.89996pt\left[\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{0}\right]\kern-0.89996pt\right]_{e}\right\rangle_{e}=0,

\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{V}^{0}_{h}
are the solutions of (
3.17
) and (
2.8
), respectively. If
h<1
, then we have

\interleave\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\interleave\lesssim h^{t}\left\|\boldsymbol{u}\right\|_{t+1},

(3.29)

where the constant depends on the shape regularity of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
.

Proof.

Applying definition of energy norm (
3.13
), triangle inequality, Lemmas
3.4
and
3.6
, we have

\displaystyle\interleave\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\interleave

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\interleave\boldsymbol{u}-Q_{h}\boldsymbol{u}+Q_{h}\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\interleave

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\interleave\boldsymbol{u}-Q_{h}\boldsymbol{u}\interleave+\interleave Q_{h}\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\interleave\lesssim h^{t}\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{t+1},

which completes the proof.
∎

Restricted Cohomology of Heisenberg Lie Algebras
Yong Yang

Abstract
The Heisenberg Lie algebras over an algebraically closed field 𝔽 \mathbb{F} of characteristic p > 0 p>0 always admit a family of restricted structures. We use the ordinary 1- and 2-cohomology spaces with adjoint coefficients to compute the restricted 1- and 2-cohomology spaces of these restricted Heisenberg Lie algebras.



Keywords: restricted Lie algebra;
Heisenberg algebra; restricted cohomology

Mathematics Subject
Classification: 17B50; 17B56

1
Introduction

Given a symplectic vector space, its Heisenberg Lie algebra arising from the
symplectic form is a two-step nilpotent Lie algebra with one-dimensional
center, as shown in the following definition:

Definition 1.1
.

For a non-negative integer
m
, the
Heisenberg Lie algebra

\mathfrak{h}_{m}
is the
(2m+1)
-dimensional vector space over a
field
\mathbb{F}
spanned by the elements

\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{2m},e_{2m+1}\}

with the non-vanishing Lie
brackets

[e_{i},e_{m+i}]=e_{2m+1},

for
1\leq i\leq m
.

Due to the applications in the
commutation relations in quantum mechanics, there are a variety of algebraic works on Heisenberg Lie algebras.
For instance the
cohomology with trivial coefficients of Heisenberg Lie algebras over
fields of characteristic zero was computed
[
15
]
,
the algebraic
Morse theory was applied to compute the Poincaré polynomial of the Lie algebra

\mathfrak{h}_{m}
over fields of characteristic
p=2

[
17
]
and
the
n
-th Betti number of

\mathfrak{h}_{m}
over a field of any prime characteristic for
n\leq m
was given in

[
3
]
.
There are also other results related to invariants of Heisenberg algebras, like gradings and symmetries
[
4
]
and the dimensions of the adjoint cohomology spaces over fields of characteristic zero
[
1
,
2
,
14
]
.

The notion of restricted Lie algebras over fields of positive
characteristic was introduced by Jacobson in 1937
[
13
]
. Since
then, the study of restricted Lie algebras has proved to be fruitful
for several reasons, see
[
16
]
for example.
By Jacobson’s Theorem,
two-step nilpotent Lie algebras are restrictable.
As a result, the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}_{m}
always has a family of restricted structures parameterized by
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{2m+1})
. Denote the
corresponding restricted Lie algebra by
\mathfrak{h}_{m}^{\lambda}
. The trivial restricted cohomology and central extensions of
\mathfrak{h}_{m}^{\lambda}
were studied in
[
8
]
.

Much less is known about the adjoint restricted cohomology of
\mathfrak{h}^{\lambda}_{m}
.
Our goal in this note is to compute the
restricted cohomology spaces

H_{*}^{q}(\mathfrak{h}_{m}^{\lambda},\mathfrak{h}_{m}^{\lambda})
for
q=1,2
, and give explicit
bases for these spaces.

Ordinary Cochain Complex

We here only describe the

\displaystyle C^{0}(\mathfrak{h}_{m},\mathfrak{h}_{m}):

\displaystyle\ \{e_{i}\ |\ 1\leq i\leq 2m+1\}

\displaystyle C^{1}(\mathfrak{h}_{m},\mathfrak{h}_{m}):

\displaystyle\ \{e_{j}^{i}\ |\ 1\leq i,j\leq 2m+1\}

\displaystyle C^{2}(\mathfrak{h}_{m},\mathfrak{h}_{m}):

\displaystyle\ \{e_{k}^{i,j}\ |\ 1\leq i<j\leq 2m+1,1\leq k\leq 2m+1\}

\displaystyle C^{3}(\mathfrak{h}_{m},\mathfrak{h}_{m}):

\displaystyle\ \{e_{l}^{i,j,k}\ |\ 1\leq i<j<k\leq 2m+1,1\leq l\leq 2m+1\}

are defined for

by

\displaystyle d^{0}:C^{0}(\mathfrak{h}_{m},\mathfrak{h}_{m})\to C^{1}(\mathfrak{h}_{m},\mathfrak{h}_{m}),\ d^{0}(e_{i})(e_{q})=[e_{i},e_{q}]

\displaystyle d^{1}:C^{1}(\mathfrak{h}_{m},\mathfrak{h}_{m})\to C^{2}(\mathfrak{h}_{m},\mathfrak{h}_{m}),\ d^{1}(e^{i}_{j})(e_{q}\wedge e_{r})=[e_{q},e_{j}^{i}(e_{r})]-[e_{r},e^{i}_{j}(e_{q})]-e^{i}_{j}([e_{q},e_{r}])

\displaystyle d^{2}:C^{2}(\mathfrak{h}_{m},\mathfrak{h}_{m})\to C^{3}(\mathfrak{h}_{m},\mathfrak{h}_{m}),\ d^{2}(e^{i,j}_{k})(e_{q}\wedge e_{r}\wedge e_{s})=[e_{q},e^{i,j}_{k}(e_{r},e_{s})]-[e_{r},e^{i,j}_{k}(e_{q},e_{s})]

\displaystyle+[e_{s},e^{i,j}_{k}(e_{q},e_{r})]-e^{i,j}_{k}([e_{q},e_{r}]\wedge e_{s})+e^{i,j}_{k}([e_{q},e_{s}]\wedge e_{r})-e^{i,j}_{k}([e_{r},e_{s}]\wedge e_{q}).

d^{q}
satisfy
d^{q}d^{q-1}=0
and

H^{q}(\mathfrak{h}_{m},\mathfrak{h}_{m})=\ker(d^{q})/\mathop{\rm im}(d^{q-1})
.
From now on, for a cocycle

, we use
\varphi
to represent both the cocycle and the corresponding cohomology class when no confusion can arise.

Given
\varphi\in C^{2}(\mathfrak{h}_{m},\mathfrak{h}_{m})
, a map
\omega:\mathfrak{h}_{m}\to\mathfrak{h}_{m}
is
\varphi
-compatible
if for all
g,h\in\mathfrak{h}_{m}
and all

\omega(ag)=a^{p}\omega(g)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\omega(g+h)=&\omega(g)+\omega(h)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g_{i}=\mbox{\rm\scriptsize$g$

g_{1}=g,g_{2}=h\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\#(g)}(\varphi([g_{1},g_{2},\dots,g_{p-1}]\wedge g_{p})\\
&-[g_{p},\varphi([g_{1},g_{2},\dots,g_{p-2}]\wedge g_{p-1})])\\
\end{split}

(2)

where
\#(g)
is the number of factors
g_{i}
equal to

.

We define

\displaystyle{\rm Hom}_{\rm Fr}(\mathfrak{h}_{m},\mathfrak{h}_{m})=\{f:\mathfrak{h}_{m}\to\mathfrak{h}_{m}\

\displaystyle|\ f(ag+bh)=a^{p}f(g)+b^{p}f(h)\ \mathrm{for}\ \mathrm{all}\

\displaystyle a,b\in\mathbb{F}\ \rm{and}\ \mathrm{all}\ \emph{g,h}\in\mathfrak{h}_{\emph{m}}\}

to be
the space of
Frobenius homomorphisms
on
\mathfrak{h}_{m}
. A map

\omega:\mathfrak{h}_{m}\to\mathfrak{h}_{m}
is
0
-compatible if and only if

\omega\in{\rm Hom}_{\rm Fr}(\mathfrak{h}_{m},\mathfrak{h}_{m})
.

Given
\varphi\in C^{2}(\mathfrak{h}_{m},\mathfrak{h}_{m})
, similar to the trivial case

8

\varphi
-compatible map

\mathfrak{h}_{m}

-compatible map

. Moreover, If

.

Given
\zeta\in C^{3}(\mathfrak{h}_{m},\mathfrak{h}_{m})
, a map
\eta:\mathfrak{h}_{m}\times\mathfrak{h}_{m}\to\mathfrak{h}_{m}
is
\zeta
-compatible

\displaystyle\eta(g,h_{1}+h_{2})

\displaystyle=\eta(g,h_{1})+\eta(g,h_{2})-

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l_{1},\dots,l_{p}=1{\rm or}2\\
l_{1}=1,l_{2}=2\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\#\{l_{i}=1\}}(\zeta(g\wedge[h_{l_{1}},\cdots,h_{l_{p-1}}]\wedge h_{l_{p}})

\displaystyle-[h_{l_{p}},\zeta(g\wedge[h_{l_{1}},\cdots,h_{l_{p-2}}]\wedge h_{l_{p-1}})]).

The restricted cochain spaces are defined as

\displaystyle C^{0}_{*}(\mathfrak{h}^{\lambda}_{m},\mathfrak{h}^{\lambda}_{m})=C^{0}(\mathfrak{h}_{m},\mathfrak{h}_{m}),

\displaystyle C^{1}_{*}(\mathfrak{h}^{\lambda}_{m},\mathfrak{h}^{\lambda}_{m})=C^{1}(\mathfrak{h}_{m},\mathfrak{h}_{m}),

\displaystyle C^{2}_{*}(\mathfrak{h}^{\lambda}_{m},\mathfrak{h}^{\lambda}_{m})=\{(\varphi,\omega)\ |\ \varphi\in C^{2}(\mathfrak{h}_{m},\mathfrak{h}_{m}),\omega:\mathfrak{h}_{m}\to\mathfrak{h}_{m}\ \mbox{\rm is $\varphi$-compatible}\},

\displaystyle C^{3}_{*}(\mathfrak{h}^{\lambda}_{m},\mathfrak{h}^{\lambda}_{m})=\{(\zeta,\eta)\ |\ \zeta\in C^{3}(\mathfrak{h}_{m},\mathfrak{h}_{m}),\eta:\mathfrak{h}_{m}\times\mathfrak{h}_{m}\to\mathfrak{h}_{m}\ \mbox{\rm is $\zeta$-compatible}\}.

For

, define

by

For

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\rm ind}^{2}(\varphi,\omega)(g,h)=&\varphi(g\wedge h^{[p]})+[g,\omega(h)]+\varphi(h\wedge(\mathop{\rm ad}h)^{p-1}(g))\\
&+[h,\varphi(h\wedge(\mathop{\rm ad}h)^{p-2}(g))].\end{split}

(3)

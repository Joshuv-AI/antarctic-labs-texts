Abstract




Abstract.
The relative entropy for two different degenerate diffusion processes is estimated by using the Wasserstein distance of initial distributions and the difference between coefficients. As applications, the entropy-cost inequality and exponential ergodicity in entropy are derived for distribution dependent stochastic Hamiltonian systems associated with nonlinear kinetic Fokker-Planck equations.

Keywords.
Entropy estimate, degenerate diffusion process, stochastic Hamiltonian system, nonlinear kinetic Fokker-Planck equation.

MSC codes.
60J60, 60H30.

Introduction

To characterize the stability of stochastic systems under perturbations, a natural way is to estimate the difference of distributions for two different processes, see
[
15
]
for a comparison theorem on transition densities (i.e. heat kernels) of
diffusions with different drifts.

Recently, by using the entropy inequality established by Bogachev, Röckner and Shaposhnikov
[
2
]
for diffusion processes, and by developing a bi-coupling argument, the entropy and probability distances have been estimated in
[
18
,
11
]
for different non-degenerate SDEs with distribution dependent noise.
In this paper, we aim to establish entropy inequality for degenerate diffusion processes. As applications, we establish a log-Harnack inequality and study the exponential ergodicity in entropy for stochastic Hamiltonian systems with distribution dependent noise.

Let us start with a simple stochastic Hamiltonian system whose Hamiltonian function is given by

H(x):=V_{1}(x^{(1)})+V_{2}(x^{(2)})\ \ \text{for }x=(x^{(1)},x^{(2)})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
V_{i}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\|\nabla^{2}V_{i}\|_{\infty}<\infty,i=1,2
. Then
X_{t}=(X_{t}^{(1)},X_{t}^{(2)})
,
the speed
X_{t}^{(1)}
and the location
X_{t}^{(2)}
of the stochastic particle, solves the following degenerate stochastic differential equation (SDE) on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}:

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\text{\rm{d}}X_{t}^{(1)}=\nabla V_{2}(X_{t}^{(2)})\text{\rm{d}}t,\\
\text{\rm{d}}X_{t}^{(2)}=\sqrt{2}\,\text{\rm{d}}W_{t}-\big(\nabla V_{1}(X_{t}^{(1)})+\nabla V_{2}(X_{t}^{(2)})\big)\text{\rm{d}}t,\end{cases}

where
W_{t}
is the
d
-dimensional Brownian motion on a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},(\mathscr{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
.
It is well known that the distribution density function of
X_{t}
solves the associated kinetic Fokker-Planck equation.

When for each
i=1,2
,
\mu^{(i)}(\text{\rm{d}}x^{(i)}):=\text{\rm{e}}^{-V_{i}(x^{(i)})}\text{\rm{d}}x^{(i)}
is a probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, SDE (
1.1
) has a unique invariant probability measure

\bar{\mu}(\text{\rm{d}}x):=\mu^{(1)}(\text{\rm{d}}x^{(1)})\mu^{(2)}(\text{\rm{d}}x^{(2)}),\ \ \text{for }x=(x^{(1)},x^{(2)})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}.

According to Villani
[
20
]
, suppose that
\mu^{(i)}

satisfies the Poincaré inequality

\mu^{(i)}(f^{2})\leq\mu^{(i)}(f)^{2}+C\mu^{(i)}(|\nabla f|^{2}),\ \ \forall f\in C_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),i=1,2,

for some constant
C>0
, where and in the sequel
\mu(f):=\int f\text{\rm{d}}\mu
for a measure
\mu
and a function
f
if the integral exists. Then the Markov semigroup
P_{t}
associated with (
1.1
) converges exponentially to
\bar{\mu}
in
H^{1}(\bar{\mu})
, i.e. for some constants
c,\lambda>0
,

\bar{\mu}\big(|P_{t}f-\bar{\mu}(f)|^{2}+|\nabla P_{t}f|^{2}\big)\leq c\text{\rm{e}}^{-\lambda t}\bar{\mu}\big(|f-\bar{\mu}(f)|^{2}+|\nabla f|^{2}\big)

for any
t\geq 0
and
f\in C_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. This property, known as
``
hypocoercivity ” due to Villani
[
20
]
, has been explored further by various authors in a series of papers for the exponential convergence of
P_{t}
in
L^{2}(\mu)
, such as
[
3
]
by Camrud, Herzog, Stoltz and Gordina, as well as
[
7
]
by Grothaus and Stilgenbauer, based on an abstract analytic framework built up by Dolbeaut, Mouhot and Schmeiser
[
5
]
, see also the recent work
[
6
]
for the study of singular models.
In case the Poincaré inequality fails, slower convergence rates are presented in
[
8
,
12
]
using the weak Poincaré inequality developed by Röckner and the third named author
[
19
]
.

On the other hand,
the study of the exponential ergodicity in the relative entropy arising from information theory, which is stronger than that in
L^{2}
(see
[
21
]
), becomes an important topic. Recall that if
\mu
and
\nu
are two probability measures, then the relative entropy of
\mu
with respect to
\nu
is defined by

{\rm Ent}(\mu|\nu):=\begin{cases}\mu\big(\log\frac{\text{\rm{d}}\mu}{\text{\rm{d}}\nu}\big),\ &\text{if}\ \mu\ \text{is\ absolutely\ continuous\ w.r.t.\ }\nu,\\
\infty,\ &\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

By Young’s inequality, see for instance
[
1
, Lemma 2.4]
, for any positive measurable function
f
such that
\nu(f)=1
, we have

\mu(\log f)=\nu\Big(\frac{\text{\rm{d}}\mu}{\text{\rm{d}}\nu}\log f\Big)\leq\nu\Big(\frac{\text{\rm{d}}\mu}{\text{\rm{d}}\nu}\log\frac{\text{\rm{d}}\mu}{\text{\rm{d}}\nu}\Big)+\log\nu(f)={\rm Ent}(\mu|\nu),

and the equality holds for
f=\frac{\text{\rm{d}}\mu}{\text{\rm{d}}\nu}
. Thus,

(1.2)

{\rm Ent}(\mu|\nu)=\sup_{f>0,\nu(f)=1}\mu(\log f)=\sup_{f>0,\nu(f)<\infty}\big[\mu(\log f)-\log\nu(f)\big],

since the right hand side is infinite if
\mu
is not absolutely continuous with respect to
\nu
.

By establishing a log-Harnack inequality, the exponential ergodicity in entropy has been been
derived in
[
21
]
for stochastic Hamiltonian systems for linear
\nabla V_{2}
, and has been further extended in
[
17
,
10
]
to the case with distribution dependent drift.
However, the log-Harnack inequality and the exponential ergodicity in entropy are still unknown for stochastic Hamiltonian systems with nonlinear
\nabla V_{2}
.

To formulate distribution dependent SDEs, we introduce the Wasserstein space
\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
having finite second moment. It is a Polish space under the Wasserstein distance

\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu,\nu):=\inf_{\pi\in\mathscr{C}(\mu,\nu)}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x-y|^{2}\pi(\text{\rm{d}}x,\text{\rm{d}}y)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
\mathscr{C}(\mu,\nu)
denotes the set of all couplings for
\mu
and
\nu
. Let
\mathscr{L}_{\xi}
denote the distribution of the random variable
\xi
.

To illustrate our general results, we consider below the distribution dependent stochastic Hamiltonian system for
X_{t}:=(X_{t}^{(1)},X_{t}^{(2)})\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
:

(1.3)

\begin{cases}\text{\rm{d}}X_{t}^{(1)}=\big\{BX_{t}^{(2)}+b(X_{t})\big\}\text{\rm{d}}t,\\
\text{\rm{d}}X_{t}^{(2)}=\sigma(\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}})\text{\rm{d}}W_{t}+Z(X_{t}^{(2)},\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}})\text{\rm{d}}t,\ \ t\geq 0,\end{cases}

where
B
is a
d_{1}\times d_{2}
-matrix such that
BB^{*}
is invertible (i.e. Rank
(B)=d_{1}
),
b\in C_{b}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}+d_{2}})
such that

\big\langle(\nabla^{(2)}b)B^{*}v,v\big\rangle\geq-\delta|B^{*}v|^{2},\ \ \ v\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}

holds for some constant
\delta\in(0,1)
, where
\nabla^{(2)}
is the gradient in
x^{(2)}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}},

and

\sigma:\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}+d_{2}})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}\otimes d_{2}},\ \ Z:\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}+d_{2}}\times\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}+d_{2}})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}

are Lipschitz continuous. According to
[
22
, Theorem 2.1]
, (
1.3
) is well-posed for distributions in
\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}+d_{2}}),
i.e. for any
\mathscr{F}_{0}
-measurable initial value
X_{0}
with
\mathscr{L}_{X_{0}}\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}+d_{2}})
, (respectively, any initial distribution
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}+d_{2}})
), the SDE has a unique strong (respectively, weak) solution with
\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}}\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}+d_{2}})
continuous in
t\geq 0
. Let
P_{t}^{*}\mu:=\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}}
where
X_{t}
is the solution of (
1.3
) with initial distribution
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}
.
If
\nabla Z(\cdot,\mu)
is bounded and Lipschitz continuous uniformly in
\mu
, then the following assertions are implied by Theorem 4.1.

\bullet

By (
4.4
) for
k=0
,
there exists a constant
c>0
such that

{\rm Ent}(P_{t}^{*}\mu|P_{t}^{*}\nu)\leq\frac{c}{t^{3}}\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu,\nu)^{2},\ \ \ t\in(0,1];\ \mu,\nu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}+d_{2}}).

\bullet

If
P_{t}^{*}
is exponentially ergodic in
\mathbb{W}_{2}
, i.e.
P_{t}^{*}
has a unique invariant probability measure
\bar{\mu}\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}+d_{2}})
and there exist two positive constants
c_{1}
and
\lambda
such that

(1.4)

\mathbb{W}_{2}(P_{t}^{*}\mu,\bar{\mu})^{2}\leq c_{1}\text{\rm{e}}^{-\lambda t}\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu,\bar{\mu})^{2}

holds for any
t\geq 0
and
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}+d_{2}})
, then the exponential ergodicity in entropy holds:

{\rm Ent}(P_{t}^{*}\mu|\bar{\mu})\leq cc_{1}\text{\rm{e}}^{-\lambda(t-1)}\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu,\bar{\mu})^{2}

holds for any
t\geq 0
and
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}+d_{2}})
.
See Corollary
4.2
and Example 4.1 below for some concrete models satisfying (
1.4
).

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. We establish an entropy inequality in Section 2 for some SDEs which applies also to the degenerate case, then apply the inequality to stochastic Hamiltonian systems and the distribution dependent model in Sections 3 and 4 respectively.

2
Entropy estimate between diffusion processes

Let
d,m\in\mathbb{N},T\in(0,\infty)
, and
(W_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
be an
m
-dimensional Brownian motion on a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},(\mathscr{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]},\mathbb{P})
. Consider the following SDEs on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

(2.1)

\text{\rm{d}}X_{t}^{\langle i\rangle}=Z_{i}(t,X_{t}^{\langle i\rangle})\text{\rm{d}}t+\sigma_{i}(t,X_{t}^{\langle i\rangle})\text{\rm{d}}W_{t}\ \ \text{ for }t\in[0,T],

where

Z_{i}:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}\ \text{ and }\sigma_{i}:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d\otimes m}

are nice enough measurable maps such that
the SDE is well-posed for
i=1,2
. Let

(P_{s,t}^{\langle i\rangle})_{0\leq s\leq t\leq T}
be the corresponding Markov semigroups, i.e.

P_{s,t}^{\langle i\rangle}f(x):=\mathbb{E}[f(X_{s,t}^{i,x})]\ \text{ for }f\in\mathscr{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\text{ and }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
(X_{s,t}^{i,x})_{t\in[s,T]}
solves (
2.1
) for
t\in[s,T]
with
X_{s,s}^{i,x}=x.

The corresponding generators are given by

L_{t}^{\langle i\rangle}:={\rm tr}\big\{a_{i}(t,\cdot)\nabla^{2}\big\}+Z_{i}(t,\cdot)\cdot\nabla\ \ \text{ for }t\in[0,T],

where
a_{i}:=\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i}^{*}
which may be degenerate. If
v:[0,T]\mapsto\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a path, then

\|v\|_{a_{2}}(t):=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\inf\big\{|w|:w\in\mathbb{R}^{d},a_{2}(t,x)^{\frac{1}{2}}w=v(t)\big\}\ \ \text{ for }t\in[0,T],

where the convention that
\inf\emptyset=\infty
is applied.

Let
\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denote the space of all probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For a given
\nu\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
X_{t}^{i,\nu}
denotes the solution to (
2.1
) with
\mathscr{L}_{X_{0}^{i,\nu}}=\nu
, where and in the sequel,
\mathscr{L}_{\xi}
stands for the law of a random variable
\xi
. Denote

P_{t}^{i,\nu}=\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}^{i,\nu}}\ \ \text{ for }t\in[0,T],\ \nu\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\text{ and }i=1,2.

We shall make the following assumptions.

(A_{1})

For any
0\leq s\leq t\leq T,

P_{s,t}^{\langle 2\rangle}C_{b}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset C_{b}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
so that the Kolmogorov backward equation holds for any
f\in C_{b}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
:

\partial_{s}P_{s,t}^{\langle 2\rangle}f=-L_{s}^{\langle 2\rangle}P_{s,t}^{\langle 2\rangle}f\ \ \text{ for }s\in[0,t]\text{ and }t\in(0,T].

(A_{2})

For any
t\in(0,T]
,
(a_{1}-a_{2})(t,\cdot)
is differentiable on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and there exists
a measurable function
H_{a_{1}-a_{2}}\cdot^{1,\nu}:(0,T]\mapsto(0,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle\big|\mathbb{E}\big[{\rm div}\{(a_{1}-a_{2})(t,\cdot)\nabla f\}(X_{t}^{1,\nu})\big]\big|

\displaystyle\leq H_{a_{1}-a_{2}}^{1,\nu}(t)\big(\mathbb{E}[|a_{2}(t,\cdot)^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla f|^{2}(X_{t}^{1,\nu})\big]\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

holds for any
t\in(0,T]
and
f\in C_{b}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

We remark that condition
(A_{1})
is satisfied when the coefficients have bounded first and second order derivatives. For the non-degenerate case, it is satisfied for a class of Hölder continuous
\sigma_{2}
and
b_{2}
, see for instance
[
13
]
and references within.
According to
[
2
]
, condition
(A_{2})
is satisfied if
a_{2}
is invertible and
X_{t}^{1,\nu}
has a distribution density
\rho_{t}^{1,\nu}
such that
\log\rho_{t}^{1,\nu}
is in a Sobolev space.
In this case, inequality (
2.2
) in the following theorem reduces to
[
2
, Theorem 1.1]
.
In the next section, we shall verify these conditions for some important examples of degenerate SDEs.

We are now in a position to state and prove the main result.

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume that
(A_{1})
and
(A_{2})
are satisfied. Then

(2.2)

{\rm Ent}(P_{t}^{1,\nu}|P_{t}^{2,\nu})\leq\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{t}\Big\{\|Z_{1}-Z_{2}-{\rm div}(a_{1}-a_{2})\|_{a_{2}}(s)+H_{a_{1}-a_{2}}^{1,\nu}(s)\Big\}^{2}\text{\rm{d}}s

for any
t\in(0,T]
.

Proof.

Let
X_{t}^{i,\nu}
solve (
2.1
) with initial distribution
\nu
, and let
X_{0}^{1,\nu}=X_{0}^{2,\nu}
. Let
C_{b,+}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denote the space of all functions
f\in C_{b}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\inf f>0
. By (
1.2
) and an approximation argument,
we have

(2.3)

\begin{split}&{\rm Ent}(P_{t}^{1,\nu}|P_{t}^{2,\nu})=\sup_{f\in C_{b,+}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}I_{t}(f),\\
&I_{t}(f):=\mathbb{E}\log f(X_{t}^{1,\nu})-\log\mathbb{E}f(X_{t}^{2,\nu}).\end{split}

Noting that
(X_{t}^{2,x}:x\in\mathbb{R}^{d})_{t\in[0,T]}
is a (time inhomogenous) Markov process, for any
f\in C_{b,+}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),
we have

(2.4)

\mathbb{E}[f(X_{t}^{2,\nu})]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(P_{0,t}^{\langle 2\rangle}f)\text{\rm{d}}\nu=\mathbb{E}[P_{0,t}^{\langle 2\rangle}f(X_{0}^{2,\nu})].

So, by Jensen’s inequality, we obtain

(2.5)

\begin{split}I_{t}(f)&=\mathbb{E}\log f(X_{t}^{1,\nu})-\log\mathbb{E}(P_{0,t}^{\langle 2\rangle}f)(X_{0}^{2,\nu})\\
&\leq\mathbb{E}\log f(X_{t}^{1,\nu})-\mathbb{E}\log(P_{0,t}^{\langle 2\rangle}f)(X_{0}^{2,\nu})\\
&=\int_{0}^{t}\Big[\frac{\text{\rm{d}}}{\text{\rm{d}}s}\mathbb{E}\log(P_{s,t}^{\langle 2\rangle}f)(X_{s}^{1,\nu})\Big]\text{\rm{d}}s\end{split}

for every
t\in(0,T]
.
By
(A_{1})

and using Itô’s formula for
X_{s}^{1,\nu},
we derive that

\displaystyle\frac{\text{\rm{d}}}{\text{\rm{d}}s}\mathbb{E}\big(\log(P_{s,t}^{\langle 2\rangle}f)(X_{s}^{1,\nu})\big)=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big(L_{s}^{\langle 1\rangle}\log(P_{s,t}^{\langle 2\rangle}f)-\frac{L_{s}^{\langle 2\rangle}P_{s,t}^{\langle 2\rangle}f}{P_{s,t}^{\langle 2\rangle}f}\Big)(X_{s}^{1,\nu})\bigg]\

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big[(L_{s}^{\langle 1\rangle}-L_{s}^{\langle 2\rangle})\log(P_{s,t}^{\langle 2\rangle}f)(X_{s}^{1,\nu})-\big|\{a_{2}(s,\cdot)^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\log P_{s,t}^{\langle 2\rangle}f\}\big|^{2}(X_{s}^{1,\nu})\Big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big[{\rm div}\big\{(a_{1}-a_{2})(s,\cdot)\nabla\log P_{s,t}^{\langle 2\rangle}f\big\}(X_{s}^{1,\nu})-\big|\{a_{2}(s,\cdot)^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\log P_{s,t}^{\langle 2\rangle}f\big|^{2}(X_{s}^{1,\nu})\Big]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}\Big[\big\langle\{Z_{1}-Z_{2}-{\rm div}(a_{1}-a_{2})\}(s,\cdot),\nabla\log P_{s,t}^{\langle 2\rangle}f\big\rangle(X_{s}^{1,\nu})\Big].

Combining this with
(A_{2})
gives that

\displaystyle\frac{\text{\rm{d}}}{\text{\rm{d}}s}\mathbb{E}\big(\log(P_{s,t}^{\langle 2\rangle}f)(X_{s}^{1,\nu})\big)

\displaystyle\leq\Big[H_{a_{1}-a_{2}}^{1,\nu}(s)+\|Z_{1}-Z_{2}-{\rm div}(a_{1}-a_{2})\|_{a_{2}}(s)\Big]\big(\mathbb{E}|a_{2}(s,\cdot)^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\log P_{s,t}^{\langle 2\rangle}f|^{2}(X_{s}^{1,\nu})\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad-\mathbb{E}\Big[\big|a_{2}(s,\cdot)^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\log P_{s,t}^{\langle 2\rangle}f\big|^{2}(X_{s}^{1,\nu})\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\Big[H_{a_{1}-a_{2}}^{1,\nu}(s)+\|Z_{1}-Z_{2}-{\rm div}(a_{1}-a_{2})\|_{a_{2}}(s)\Big]^{2}

for every
s\in(0,t]
, which, together with (
2.3
) and (
3.27
), implies the desired estimate (
2.2
).
∎

As explained in
[
18
]
that
|H_{a_{1}-a_{2}}^{1,\nu}(s)|^{2}
is normally singular for small

, such that the upper bound in (

Corollary 2.2
.

Assume that
(A_{1})
and
(A_{2})
are satisfied,
H_{a_{1}-a_{2}}^{1,x}(s):=H_{a_{1}-a_{2}}^{1,\delta_{x}}(s)
is measurable in
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

H_{a_{1}-a_{2}}^{1,\nu}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}H_{a_{1}-a_{2}}^{1,x}(s)\nu(\text{\rm{d}}x).

Suppose that there exist a constant
p\in(1,\infty)
and a decreasing function
\eta:(0,T]\mapsto(0,\infty)
such that

(2.6)

|P_{s,t}^{\langle 2\rangle}f(x)|^{p}\leq\big(P_{s,t}^{\langle 2\rangle}|f|^{p}(y)\big)\text{\rm{e}}^{\eta(t-s)|x-y|^{2}}

for any
0\leq s<t\leq T
and
f\in\mathscr{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Then there exists a constant
c>0
such that

\displaystyle{\rm Ent}(P_{t}^{1,\mu}|P_{t}^{2,\nu})\leq

\displaystyle\,\inf_{\pi\in\mathscr{C}(\mu,\nu)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\frac{p}{4}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\Big\{\|b_{1}-b_{2}-{\rm div}(a_{1}-a_{2})\|_{a_{2}}(s)+H_{a_{1}-a_{2}}^{1,x_{1}}(s)\Big\}^{2}\text{\rm{d}}s

\displaystyle\,+(p-1)\log\mathbb{E}\Big\{\exp\big[c\eta(t-t_{0})\big|X_{t_{0}}^{1,x_{1}}-X_{t_{0}}^{2,x_{2}}\big|^{2}\big]\Big\}\bigg)\pi(\text{\rm{d}}x_{1},\text{\rm{d}}x_{2})

for any
0<t_{0}<t\leq T

x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

Proof.

For simplicity, denote
P_{t}^{i,x}=P_{t}^{i,\delta_{x}}
where
i=1,2,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\delta_{x}
is the Dirac measure at
x
. Let
X_{t}(x_{1})
be the diffusion process starting from the initial value
x_{1}
with the infinitesimal generator given by

L_{t}:=1_{[0,t_{0}]}(t)L_{t}^{\langle 1\rangle}+1_{(t_{0},t]}(t)L_{t}^{\langle 2\rangle}.

Let
P_{t}^{\langle t_{0}\rangle x_{1}}=\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}(x_{1})}
.
By using (
2.2
) with
\nu=\delta_{x_{1}}
and
P_{t}^{\langle t_{0}\rangle x_{1}}
in place of
P_{t}^{2,x_{1}}
, and combining with
[
18

, we deduce that

(2.7)

\begin{split}{\rm Ent}(P_{t}^{1,x_{1}}|P_{t}^{2,x_{2}})\leq&\,\frac{p}{4}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\Big\{\|b_{1}-b_{2}-{\rm div}(a_{1}-a_{2})\|_{a_{2}}(s)+H_{s}^{1,x_{1}}(a_{1}-a_{2})\Big\}^{2}\text{\rm{d}}s\\
&\,+(p-1)\log\mathbb{E}\bigg\{\exp\Big[c\eta(t-t_{0})\big|X_{t_{0}}^{1,x_{1}}-X_{t_{0}}^{2,x_{2}}\big|^{2}\Big]\bigg\}.\end{split}

On the other hand, if
\pi\in\mathscr{C}(\mu,\nu)
, then by using (
2.3
), (
2.4
) and Jensen’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle{\rm Ent}(P_{t}^{1,\mu}|P_{t}^{2,\nu})=\sup_{f\in C_{b,+}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\big\{\mathbb{E}\log f(X_{t}^{1,\mu})-\log\mathbb{E}f(X_{t}^{2,\nu})\big\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{f\in C_{b,+}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\bigg\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}P_{t}^{\langle 1\rangle}(\log f)(x_{1})\mu(\text{\rm{d}}x_{1})-\log\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}P_{t}^{\langle 2\rangle}f(x_{2})\nu(\text{\rm{d}}x_{2})\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{f\in C_{b,+}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big\{P_{t}^{\langle 1\rangle}(\log f)(x_{1})-\log P_{t}^{\langle 2\rangle}f(x_{2})\big\}\pi(\text{\rm{d}}x_{1},\text{\rm{d}}x_{2})

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\text{\rm{d}}\left(\bar{\psi}(X_{t},Y_{t})^{2}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\|B\left(\sigma(\mu_{t})-\sigma(\nu_{t})\right)\|_{HS}^{2}+\big\langle a^{2}\Xi_{t}^{(1)}+raB\Xi_{t}^{(2)},B\Xi_{t}^{(2)}+b(X_{t})-b(Y_{t})\big\rangle\text{\rm{d}}t

\displaystyle\quad-\big\langle B^{*}B\Xi_{t}^{(2)}+raB^{*}\Xi_{t}^{(1)},\ \beta B^{*}(BB^{*})^{-1}\Xi_{t}^{(1)}+\Xi_{t}^{(2)}\big\rangle\text{\rm{d}}t

\displaystyle\quad+\big\langle B^{*}B\Xi_{t}^{(2)}+raB^{*}\Xi_{t}^{(1)},\ B^{*}\{\nabla^{(1)}V(Y_{t}^{(1)},\nu_{t})-\nabla^{(1)}V(X_{t}^{(1)},\mu_{t})\}\big\rangle\text{\rm{d}}t

\displaystyle\leq\Big\{-(1-ra)|B\Xi_{t}^{(2)}+(a^{2}-\beta-ra)\langle\Xi_{t}^{(1)},B\Xi_{t}^{(2)}\rangle+\big[\|\nabla b\|_{\infty}(a^{2}+ra)-ra\beta\big]|\Xi_{t}^{(1)}|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\big\langle B^{*}B\Xi_{t}^{(2)}+(1+\beta)^{-1}B^{*}\Xi_{t}^{(1)},\ B^{*}\{\nabla^{(1)}V(Y_{t}^{(1)},\nu_{t})-\nabla^{(1)}V(X_{t}^{(1)},\mu_{t})\}\big\rangle\Big\}\text{\rm{d}}t

\displaystyle\leq\Big\{\frac{\theta_{2}}{1+\beta}\mathbb{W}_{2}^{\psi}(\mu_{t},\nu_{t})^{2}-\frac{\beta-\theta_{1}}{1+\beta}{\psi}(X_{t},Y_{t})^{2}+\|\nabla b\|_{\infty}(a^{2}+ra)|\Xi_{t}^{(1)}|^{2}\Big\}\text{\rm{d}}t.

By (
4.11
) and the fact that

\mathbb{W}_{2}^{\psi}(\mu_{t},\nu_{t})^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}[{\psi}(X_{t},Y_{t})^{2}],

for
\kappa>0
in (
4.6
), when
\|\nabla b\|_{\infty}
is small enough we find a constant
\kappa^{\prime}\in(0,\kappa)
such that
we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbb{E}[\bar{\psi}(X_{t},Y_{t})^{2}]-\mathbb{E}[\bar{\psi}(X_{s},Y_{s})^{2}]\right)

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla b\|_{\infty}(a^{2}+ra)\int_{s}^{t}\mathbb{E}[|\Xi_{u}^{(1)}|^{2}]\text{\rm{d}}u-\frac{\beta-\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}}{1+\beta}\int_{s}^{t}\mathbb{E}[{\psi}(X_{u},Y_{u})^{2}]\text{\rm{d}}u

\displaystyle\leq-\kappa^{\prime}\int_{s}^{t}\mathbb{E}[\bar{\psi}(X_{u},Y_{u})^{2}]\text{\rm{d}}u,\ \ t\geq s\geq 0.

By Gronwall’s inequality, we then deduce that

\mathbb{E}[\bar{\psi}(X_{t},Y_{t})^{2}]\leq\text{\rm{e}}^{-2\kappa^{\prime}t}\mathbb{E}[\bar{\psi}(X_{0},Y_{0})^{2}]

for

.
Combining this with (

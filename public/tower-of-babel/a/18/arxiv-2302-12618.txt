Heteroclinic Solutions in Singularly Perturbed Discontinuous Differential Equations
Flaviano Battelli, Michal Fečkan, JinRong Wang

Abstract
We derive Melnikov type conditions for the persistence of heteroclinic solutions in perturbed slowly varying discontinuous differential equations extending to these equations similar results for continuous differential equations.



Key Words:
discontinuous differential equations; heteroclinic solutions; Melnikov conditions; persistence.

1
Introduction

In recent years there has been a great deal in the study of singularly perturbed equations such as

\begin{array}[]{l}\dot{x}=f(x,y)\\
\dot{y}=\varepsilon g(x,y).\end{array}

(1.1)

where
f(x,y)
and
g(x,y)
are sufficiently smooth functions. To the best of our knowledge this study started with remarkable papers by Tichonov
[
24
]
, Vasil’eva et al.
[
25
,
26
]
. All these results concern the behaviour of the solutions of (
1.1
) on a finite time scale. Later, Hoppensteadt
[
15
,
16
]
extended the result on an infinite time scale. Then Fenichel
[
12
]
developed his geometric singularly perturbed theory that has been widely used by other authors as, for example, Szmolyan
[
23
]
. These last results essentially prove that the
x
component of the solution is asymptotic to invariant manifolds, sometimes called
centre manifolds
, described by equations like
x=X_{\pm}(y)
(the case
X_{+}(y)=X_{-}(y)
is allowed). In
[
18
]
a combination of the Melnikov method and geometric singular perturbation theory is presented. This approach has been then extended in
[
14
]
to investigate a mechanism of chaos near resonances both in the dissipative and the Hamiltonian context of differential equations.

The basic starting point for this kind of results is that, for all
y
the frozen equation

\dot{x}=f(x,y)

(1.2)

has hyperbolic fixed points, say
u_{\pm}(y)
, that are bounded functions of
y
together with their derivatives, and solutions
u_{\pm}(t,y)
, defined for
t\geq 0
and
t\leq 0
, resp., such that

\lim_{t\to\infty}u_{+}(t,y)-u_{+}(y)=0,\;\lim_{t\to-\infty}u_{-}(t,y)-u_{-}(y)=0

(1.3)

uniformly with respect to
y
and there exists
y_{0}
such that
u_{-}(0,y_{0})=u_{+}(0,y_{0})
. Hence, for
y=y_{0}
the frozen equation (
1.2
) has a heteroclinic orbit connecting
u_{-}(y_{0})
to
u_{+}(y_{0})
. Note that in
[
6
,
7
,
20
]
it is also assumed that
u_{\pm}(t,y)
are bounded together with their derivatives. However, in Lemma
4.2
, we prove that this condition is automatically satisfied when
u_{\pm}(y)
are bounded together with their derivatives and (
1.3
) holds. The proof of this result is rather technical and may be skipped at a first reading of this paper.

Other relevant results concerning (
1.1
) appeared in
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
17
,
20
]
. In
[
17
]
, the second order equation with slowly varying coefficients

\ddot{x}+x(x-a(\varepsilon t+\theta))(1-x)=0,\quad\varepsilon>0

(1.4)

is studied, where
a(y)
is a
C^{1}
,
1
-periodic function, such that
0<a(y)<1
. When
\varepsilon=0
and
a(y)=\frac{1}{2}
, (
1.4
) has the heteroclinic solution

x_{het}(t)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-\frac{t}{\sqrt{2}}}}.

However this heteroclinic solution is broken when
a(y)\neq\frac{1}{2}
. In
[
17
]
it has been proved that if
a(y)
has a transversal intersection with
a=\frac{1}{2}
at
y=y_{0}
, i.e.
2a(y_{0})=1
and
a^{\prime}(y_{0})\neq 0
, then equation (
1.4
) has a heteroclinic solution. Writing

x_{1}=x,\quad x_{2}=\dot{x},\quad y=\varepsilon t+\alpha

equation (
1.4
) reads:

\begin{array}[]{l}\dot{x}=f(x,y)\\
\dot{y}=\varepsilon\end{array}

(1.5)

where

f(x,y)=\begin{pmatrix}x_{2}\\
x_{1}(x_{1}-1)(x_{1}-a(y))\end{pmatrix}

and
u(t,y_{0})=(x_{het}(t),\dot{x}_{het}(t)
. This solution is a part of a family of solutions
u_{\pm}(t,y)
such that

\lim_{t\to-\infty}u_{-}(t,y)=(0,0)\quad\lim_{t\to\infty}u_{+}(t,y)=(1,0)

and it can be proved that the transversality condition on
a(y)
corresponds to the fact that the real-valued function

\psi^{*}[u_{-}(0,y)-u_{+}(0,y)]

where
\psi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a suitable vector, has a simple zero at
y=y_{0}
.

In Theorem
6.2
, we extend these results, as far as the asymptotic behaviour of the
x
-component of the solution is concerned, to discontinuous differential equations that is a differential equation which is described in different ways according to the region the solutions belong to. The first difference with the continuous case is that the way how
u_{\pm}(t,y)
passes from one domain into another is important. In this paper we assume that
u_{\pm}(t,y)
intersects transversally the boundary of the domain when they pass from the domain into another (see assumption
A_{1})
in Section 2).

Problems like the above where
u(t,\alpha,y)
is, instead, a family of periodic solutions depending on some further parameters
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, have been studied in
[
27
,
4
,
5
]
. Note that, in
[
27
]
,
f(x,y)
is replaced by
f_{0}(x,y)+\varepsilon f_{1}(x,y,t,\varepsilon)
and it is assumed that system

\dot{x}=f_{0}(x,y)

(1.6)

has a one-parameter family of periodic solutions
q(t-\theta,y,\alpha)
with period
T(y,\alpha)
being
C^{r}
in
(y,\alpha)
and it is because of the

t
-dependence of the perturbed equation that the extra variable
\theta
has been introduced. Then a vector valued function
M^{m/n}(y,\alpha,\theta)
is constructed, that they called
subharmonic Melnikov function
, which is a measure of the difference between the starting value and the value of the solution at the time
\frac{m}{n}T
in a direction transverse to the unperturbed vector field at the starting point and proved that periodic solutions of the perturbed vector field arise near the simple zeros of
M^{m/n}(y,\alpha,\theta)
. In
[
5
]
this result concerning the existence of periodic solutions has been extended to discontinuous systems of differential equations as the one we consider here.

Now, let us be more precise and give the definition of the discontinuous differential equation we are studying in this paper.

Let
h(x,y):\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
be a
C^{r}
function,
r\geq 2
, with bounded derivatives,
f_{\ell}(x,y):\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
,

\ell=-M,\ldots,N
, be
C^{r}
-functions, bounded on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
together with their derivatives, and
c_{-M}<\ldots<c_{0}\ldots<c_{N}

be real numbers. By discontinuous differential equation we mean an equation like

\begin{array}[]{l}\dot{x}=f(x,y)\\
\dot{y}=\varepsilon g(x,y,\varepsilon)\end{array}

(1.7)

where
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}
,
\varepsilon>0
and

f(x,y):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}f_{-M}(x,y)&\hbox{if $h(x,y)<c_{-M}$}\\
f_{\ell}(x,y)&\hbox{if $c_{\ell-1}<h(x,y)<c_{\ell}$}\\
&\ell=-M+1,\ldots,N\end{array}\right.

(1.8)

It is assumed that the equation

\dot{x}=f_{N}(x,y)\quad\hbox{[resp. $\dot{x}=f_{-M}(x,y)$]}

(1.9)

has a hyperbolic fixed point
x=w_{+}(y)
[resp.
x=w_{-}(y)
] together with continuous, piecewise
C^{2}
solutions,
u_{+}(t,y)
,
t\geq 0
,

u_{-}(t,y)
,
t\leq 0
, such that

\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}|u_{\pm}(t,y)-w_{\pm}(y)|\to 0

uniformly with respect to
y
, and

h(u_{\pm}(t_{\ell},y),y)=c_{\ell}\Rightarrow\inf_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}|h_{x}(u_{\pm}(t_{\ell},y),y)\dot{u}_{\pm}(t_{\ell}^{\pm},y)|>0.

(1.10)

for any
-M\leq\ell\leq N
,
\ell\neq 0
.

Here we used the shorthand
u_{\pm}(t,y)-w_{\pm}(y)
for
u_{+}(t,y)-w_{+}(y)
, when
t\geq 0
, and
u_{-}(t,y)-w_{-}(y)
, when
t\leq 0
. We will use such a shorthand throughout the whole paper.

Note that (
1.10
) implies that
u_{\pm}(t,y)
intersects transversally the set

{\cal S}_{\ell}(y)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid h(x,y)=c_{\ell}\},

at
u_{\pm}(t_{\ell},y)
. Then we look for solutions
(x(t,\varepsilon),y(t,\varepsilon))
of (
1.7
) such that

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\pm}}|x(t,\varepsilon)-u_{\pm}(t,y(t,\varepsilon))|\ll 1.

(1.11)

Note that, from (
1.10
) it follows that, for any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, the set
{\cal S}_{\ell}(y)
,
\ell=-M,\ldots,N
,
\ell\neq 0
, is an hypersurface in a ball around
u_{\pm}(t_{\ell},y)
whose radius does not depend on
y
. We emphasize that (
1.10
) is all that we need on
{\cal S}_{\ell}(y)
for our analysis.

To prove the existence of solutions of (
1.7
) satisfying (
1.11
) we use Lyapunov-Schimtd method, together with a combination of singularly perturbed analysis and a technique for discontinuous dynamical systems, to construct a
bifurcation function
whose zeros are associated to solutions of the perturbed equation whose
x
-component satisfies (
1.11
). This is the content of Theorem
6.2
where we prove that if a certain generic condition is satisfied then there is a manifold of such solutions.

According to
A_{3})
(see Section 2)
x=w_{\pm}(y)
are normally hyperbolic invariant manifolds for the unperturbed system
\dot{x}=f(x,y)
,
\dot{y}=0
. From
[
1
,
22
]
these manifolds perturb to invariant manifolds
x=\tilde{w}_{\pm}(y,\varepsilon)
such that

\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}|\tilde{w}_{\pm}(y,\varepsilon)-w_{\pm}(y)|\to 0,\hbox{as $\varepsilon\to 0$.}

From Remark
6.3
it follows that
x(t,\varepsilon)
is asymptotic to the manifold
\tilde{w}_{+}(y,\varepsilon)
as
t\to\infty
and to
\tilde{w}_{-}(y,\varepsilon)
as
t\to-\infty
. Hence,
(x(t,\varepsilon),y(t,\varepsilon))
behaves as a heteroclinic solution of equation (
1.7
) connecting the invariant manifold

\tilde{w}_{-}(y,\varepsilon)
to
\tilde{w}_{+}(y,\varepsilon)
.

The problem studied in this paper has been motivated by
[
28
]
, where existence and bifurcation theorems are derived for homoclinic orbits in three-dimensional flows that are perturbations of families of planar Hamiltonian system. In this paper we study the problem of persistence of bounded solutions in the discontinuous case (
1.7
) where
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, all functions considered are sufficiently smooth and
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}
is a small parameter, assuming the existence of such an orbit in the unperturbed equation (
1.9
).

Then in Section 7 we apply Theorem
6.2
to extend
[
17
, Theorem 1]
to the discontinuous equation (
1.7
). Following
[
6
]
we derive a bifurcation function characterizing the persistence of homoclinic solutions of (
1.7
) from a generic homoclinic solution of the unperturbed system (
1.6
). It can be easily checked that the results of this paper easily extend if we replace
f(x,y)
with
f(x,y,\varepsilon)=f_{0}(x,y)+\varepsilon f_{1}(x,y,\varepsilon)
(with the same
h(x,y)
). In this case the unperturbed system will be

\dot{x}=f_{0}(x,y),\;\dot{y}=0.

The next step is the study of a degenerate case where, for any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
the unperturbed discontinuous equation (
1.6
) has a piecewise
C^{1}
solution
u(t,y)
heteroclinic to the hyperbolic fixed points
x=w_{\pm}(y)
. We plan to perform this study in a forthcoming paper as it is necessary to go into a deeper analysis of the bifurcation function.

We now briefly sketch the content of this paper. In Section 2 we provide basic assumptions and define the piecewise smooth heteroclinic solution of the unperturbed system. In Section 3 we recall the definition of exponential dichotomy and extend this notion to discontinuous, piecewise linear, systems with a jump at some points. We also extend to these systems some results concerning existence of bounded solutions on either
t\geq 0
and
t\leq 0
. In our opinion, these results are theirselves interesting as they give the form of the projection of the dichotomy of a linear discontinuous system. Although this is an important point in the proof of Theorem
6.2
, we think that the proofs in this section can be skipped at a first reading.

Next, in Section 4, we construct families of bounded solutions and describe them in terms of some parameters. These solutions are continuous and piecewise smooth and give the bounded solutions we look for, when they assume the same value at
t=0
. Then, after having defined the variational equation in Section 5, in Section 6 we study the
joining condition at
t=0
which is the bifurcation condition and give a Melnikov-type condition assuring that the bifurcation equation has a manifold of solutions. Finally, in Section 7 we first state some general facts concerning two-dimensional discontinuous equations depending on a slowly varying parameter and give an example of application of the main result of this paper. In Section 8 we give a hint on a possible extension of the example.

In the whole paper we will use the following notation. Given a vector
v
or a matrix
A
with
v^{T}
, (resp.
A^{T}
) we denote the transpose of

v
(resp.
A
).

Notation and basic assumptions

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded domain,

c_{-M}<\ldots<c_{0}<\ldots<c_{N}

be real numbers and
h:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
be a
C^{r}
-functions,
r\geq 2
, with bounded derivatives. For
\ell=-M,\ldots,N
, we set

\Omega_{\ell}=\{(x,y)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\mid c_{i-1}\leq h(x,y)<c_{i}\},

where we set for simplicity,
c_{-M-1}=-\infty
and let
f_{\ell}:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be
C^{r}
-function, bounded together with their derivatives in
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
. We are looking for solutions of equation

\dot{x}=f_{\ell}(x,y),\quad(x,y)\in\Omega_{\ell}

(2.1)

which are contained in a compact subset
H
of
\Omega
. Hence it is not restrictive to assume that
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}
. So, from now on, we suppose
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

First we give the definition of solutions of equation (
2.1
) we are considering in this paper

Definition 2.1
.

A continuous, piecewise smooth function
u(t,y)
is a solution of equation (
2.1
) on
t\geq 0
intersecting transversally the sets
{\cal S}_{i}(y)=\{x\in\Omega\mid h(x,y)=c_{i}\}
,
i=0,\ldots,N-1
, if there exist
\eta>0
and
0<t_{1}(y)<\ldots<t_{N}(y)
such that the following conditions hold for
1\leq i\leq N-1
(note that we set
t_{0}(y)=0
)

a_{1})

\dot{u}(t,y)=f_{i-1}(u(t,y),y)
for
t_{i-1}(y)<t<t_{i}(y)
and
\dot{u}(t,y)=f_{N}(u(t,y),y)
for
t>t_{N}(y)
;

a_{2})

h(u(t_{i}(y),y),y)=c_{i-1},\quad\hbox{and}\quad h_{x}(u(t_{i}(y),y),y)\dot{u}(t_{i}(y)^{\pm},y)>2\eta
;

a_{3})

c_{i-1}<h(u(t,y),y)<c_{i}
, for
t_{i}(y)<t<t_{i+1}(y)
and
h(u(t,y),y)>c_{N-1}
, for
t>t_{N}(y)
.

Similarly, a continuous, piecewise smooth function
u(t,y)
is a solution of equation (
2.1
) on
t\leq 0
intersecting transversally the sets

{\cal S}_{j}(y)=\{x\in\Omega\mid h(x,y)=c_{j}\}
,
j=-1,\ldots,-M
, if there exist
\eta>0
and
t_{-M}(y)<\ldots<t_{-1}(y)<0
such that the following conditions hold for any
-M+1\leq j\leq-1
:

a^{\prime}_{1})

\dot{u}(t,y)=f_{j+1}(u(t,y),y)
for
t_{j}(y)<t<t_{j+1}(y)
and
\dot{u}(t,y)=f_{-M}(u(t,y),y)
for
t<t_{-M}(y)
;

a^{\prime}_{2})

h(u(t_{j}(y),y),y)=c_{j},\quad\hbox{and}\quad h_{x}(u(t_{j}(y),y),y)\dot{u}(t_{j}(y)^{\pm},y)>2\eta
;

a^{\prime}_{3})

c_{j}<h(u(t,y),y)<c_{j+1}
, for
t_{j}(y)<t<t_{j+1}(y)
and
h(u(t,y),y)<c_{-M}
, for
t<t_{-M}(y)
.

In this paper we assume that continuous, piecewise smooth solution
u_{+}(t,y)
and
u_{-}(t,y)
of equation (
2.1
) exist, for
t\geq 0
, resp.
t\leq 0
, such that the following conditions also hold.

A_{1})

w_{0}^{\pm}(y):=u_{\pm}(0,y)
and their derivatives are bounded functions and belong to an open and bounded subset

such that
\overline{B}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\subset\Omega_{0}
.

A_{2})

There exist smooth and bounded functions

and

, such that

\begin{array}[]{l}f_{N}(w_{+}(y),y)=f_{-M}(w_{-}(y),y)=0\\
h(w_{+}(y),y)-c_{N-1}>\mu_{0},\quad h(w_{-}(y),y)-c_{-M}<-\mu_{0}\end{array}

for any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and

\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}u_{\pm}(t,y)-w_{\pm}(y)=0

uniformly with respect to
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

A_{3})

For any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
f_{N,x}(w_{+}(y),y)
and

have
k

We have the following

Lemma 2.3
.

For
i=0\ldots,N
,
j=0,\ldots,-M
, let
w_{i}^{+}(y)=u_{+}(t_{i}(y),y)
,
w^{-}_{j}(y)=u_{-}(t_{j}(y),y)
. Then
w_{i}^{+}(y)
and
w^{-}_{j}(y)
are

-functions bounded together with their derivatives.

Proof.

We know that
w_{0}^{\pm}(y)
is
C^{r}
and bounded together with its derivatives. Now,

w_{1}^{+}(y)=u_{+}(t_{1}(y),y)=w_{0}^{+}(y)+\int_{0}^{t_{1}(y)}f_{1}(u_{+}(t,y),y)dt.

Hence
w_{1}^{+}(y)
is
C^{r}
and bounded since so are
w_{0}^{+}(y)
,
t_{1}(y)
and
f_{1}(x,y)
. Next, from

h_{x}(w_{1}^{+}(y),y)f_{1}(w_{1}^{+}(y),y)>2\eta

and the fact that
f_{1}(x,y)
is bounded, we see that
\bar{h}>0
exists such that
\inf_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}|h_{x}(w_{1}^{+}(y),y)|>\bar{h}
. Then, differentiating

h(w_{1}^{+}(y),y)=c_{0}
we see that

\frac{dw_{1}^{+}}{dy}(y)=-\frac{h_{y}(w_{1}^{+}(y),y)}{h_{x}(w_{1}^{+}(y),y)}

is bounded. More arguments of similar nature show that all derivatives of
w_{1}^{+}(y)
are bounded.

Suppose now that
w^{+}_{i}(y)
is bounded with its derivative. Then, since

w^{+}_{i+1}(y)=w^{+}_{i}(y)+\int_{t_{i}(y)}^{t_{i+1}(y)}f_{i}(u_{+}(t,y),y)dt

we see that
w^{+}_{i+1}(y)
is a bounded function and a similar argument as before shows that the derivatives of
w^{+}_{i+1}(y)
are also bounded.
The proof that
w^{-}_{j}(y)
is a
C^{r}
function bounded with its derivatives for any
j=-1,\ldots,-M
is similar.
∎

Let
i=0,1,\ldots,N
. For
t\geq 0
, let
u_{i}^{+}(t,y)
be the solution of
\dot{x}=f_{i}(x,y)
such that
u_{i}^{+}(t_{i}(y),y)=w^{+}_{i}(y)
. Similarly, for
j=-M,\ldots,-1,0
let
u_{j}^{-}(t,y)
be the solution of
\dot{x}=f_{j}(x,y)
such that
u_{j}^{-}(t_{i}(y),y)=w^{-}_{j}(y)
. Then

\begin{array}[]{l}u(t,y):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{+}(t,y)&\hbox{$t\geq 0$}\\
u_{-}(t,y)&\hbox{for $t\leq 0$}\end{array}\right.\\
\qquad=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{j}^{-}(t,y)&\hbox{for $t_{j-1}(y)\leq t\leq t_{j}(y)$, $j=0,-1,\ldots-M$}\\
u_{i}^{+}(t,y)&\hbox{for $t_{i}(y)\leq t\leq t_{i+1}(y)$, $i=0,\ldots,N$}\end{array}\right.\end{array}

(2.2)

where, for simplicity, we set
t_{N+1}(y)=\infty
and
t_{-M-1}(y)=-\infty

u_{i}^{+}(t_{i+1}(y),y)=u_{+}(t_{i+1}(y),y)=w^{+}_{i+1}(y)=u_{i+1}^{+}(t_{i}(y),y)

and similarly,

u_{j}^{-}(t_{j-1}(y),y)=w^{-}_{j-1}(y)=u^{-}_{j-1}(t_{j-1}(y),y).

Here we emphasize a convention that we will use throughout the whole paper. When we use the index
i
, such as in
t_{i}
or
w^{+}_{i}(y)
, we always mean
i=1,\ldots,N
, (sometimes also
i=0
) while, when we use the index
j
, as in
t_{j}
or
w^{-}_{j}(y)
, we always mean
j=-1,\ldots,-M
(sometimes also
j=0
).

3
Exponential dichotomy for piecewise discontinuous systems

A basic tool in this paper is the notion of exponential dichotomy, whose definition we recall here. Let
J
be either
[a,\infty),(-\infty,a]
, or

\mathbb{R}
and
A(t)
,
t\in J
, be a
n\times n
continuous matrix. We say that the linear system

\dot{x}=A(t)x,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

(3.1)

has an exponential dichotomy on
J
if there exist a projection
P:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
and constants
\delta>0
and
K\geq 1
such that the fundamental matrix

X(t)
of (
3.1
) satisfying
X(a)=\mathbb{I}
, when
J=[a,\infty),(-\infty,a]
, or
X(0)=\mathbb{I}
when
J=\mathbb{R}
, satisfies

\begin{array}[]{l}|X(t)PX(s)^{-1}|\leq Ke^{-\delta(t-s)},\quad\hbox{for $s\leq t,\;s,t\in J$}\\
|X(s)(\mathbb{I}-P)X(t)^{-1}|\leq Ke^{-\delta(t-s)},\quad\hbox{for $s\leq t,\;s,t\in J$.}\end{array}

In this section we extend the definition of exponential dichotomy to systems with discontinuities. To allow more generality we consent the solutions to have jump discontinuities at the discontinuity points of the coefficient matrix.

So, let
t_{0}<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{N}
be real numbers,
B_{1},\ldots,B_{N}
be invertible
n\times n
matrices and
{\cal A}(t)
,
t\geq t_{0}
be a piecewise continuous matrix with possible discontinuity jumps at
t=t_{1},\ldots,t_{N}
, that is

{\cal A}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}A_{i-1}(t)&\hbox{if $t_{i-1}\leq t<t_{i}$,}\\
A_{N}(t)&\hbox{if $t\geq t_{N}$}\end{array}\right.

(3.2)

where
A_{0}(t),\ldots,A_{N}(t)
are continuous matrices. Note that
{\cal A}(t)
is continuous for
t\geq t_{0}
,
t\neq t_{i}
, and right-continuous at
t=t_{i}
, with possible jumps at
t=t_{i}
given by the matrix
A_{i}(t_{i})-A_{i-1}(t_{i})
. For
t\geq t_{0}
we consider the linear, discontinuous, system

\begin{array}[]{l}\dot{x}={\cal A}(t)x\\
x(t_{i}^{+})=B_{i}x(t_{i}^{-}),\end{array}

(3.3)

Similarly, if
t_{-M}<\ldots t_{-1}<t_{0}
and

{\cal A}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}A_{-M}(t)&\hbox{if $t\leq t_{-M}$}\\
A_{j+1}(t)&\hbox{if $t_{j}<t\leq t_{j+1}$,}\end{array}\right.

(3.4)

where
A_{0}(t),A_{-1}(t),\ldots A_{-M}(t)
are continuous matrices, we consider, for
t\leq t_{0}
, the linear, discontinuous, system

\begin{array}[]{l}\dot{x}={\cal A}(t)x\\
x(t_{j}^{+})=B_{j}x(t_{j}^{-})\end{array}

(3.5)

Note that
{\cal A}(t)
is continuous for
t\leq t_{0}
,
t\neq t_{j}
, left-continuous at
t=t_{j}
, with possible jumps at
t=t_{j}
, given by the matrix
A_{j+1}(t_{j})-A_{j}(t_{j})
.

Remark 3.1
.

i) As a matter of facts, for
t\geq t_{0}
, we will consider

{\cal A}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}A_{i-1}(t)&\hbox{if $t_{i-1}\leq t\leq t_{i}$,}\\
A_{N}(t)&\hbox{if $t\geq t_{N}$}\end{array}\right.

and similarly for
t\leq t_{0}
. This may cause a duplicate definition of
{\cal A}(t)
at
t=t_{i}
, however it will be always clear which among
A_{i}(t)
will be taken into account at that point.

ii) The results of this section will be applied to the linear system
\dot{x}=A(t,y)x
where
A(t,y)
is given by

A(t,y):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}f_{-M,x}(u(t,y),y)&\hbox{if $t\leq t_{-M}(y)$.}\\
f_{j+1,x}(u(t,y),y)&\hbox{if $t_{j}(y)<t\leq t_{j+1}(y)$}\\
f_{i-1,x}(u(t,y),y)&\hbox{if $t_{i-1}(y)\leq t<t_{i}(y)$}\\
f_{N,x}(u(t,y),y)&\hbox{if $t\geq t_{N}(y)$.}\end{array}\right.

(3.6)

Note that, being
u(t,y)
continuous for
t\neq 0
,
A(t,y)
is continuous for
t\neq 0
,
t\neq t_{i}(y),t_{j}(y)
with jump discontinuities at
t=0,t_{i}(y),t_{j}(y)
. More precisely

\begin{array}[]{l}A(0^{-},y)=f_{0,x}(w_{0}^{-}(y),y)\\
A(0^{+},y)=f_{0,x}(w_{0}^{+}(y),y),y)\\
A(t_{i}(y)^{-},y)=f_{i-1,x}(w_{i}^{+}(y)),y)\\
A(t_{i}(y)^{+},y)=f_{i,x}(w_{i}^{+}(y)),y)\\
A(t_{j}(y)^{+},y)=f_{j+1,x}(w^{-}_{i}(y),y)\\
A(t_{j}(y)^{-},y)=f_{j,x}(w^{-}_{i}(y),y).\end{array}

Without loss of generality we may assume that
t_{0}=0
, so in the remaining part of this section we will take
t_{0}=0
.

Let
U_{i}(t)
be the fundamental matrix of the linear systems

\dot{x}=A_{i}(t)x

on
\mathbb{R}
, that is
\dot{U}_{i}(t)=A_{i}(t)U_{i}(t)
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
, and
U_{i}(0)=\mathbb{I}
. The fundamental matrix of (
3.3
), where
t\geq 0
, is given by the (discontinuous) invertible matrix

X_{+}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}U_{0}(t)&\hbox{if $0\leq t<t_{1}$}\\
U_{i}(t)U_{i}(t_{i})^{-1}B_{i}X_{+}(t_{i}^{-})&\hbox{if $t_{i}\leq t<t_{i+1}$}\\
U_{N}(t)U_{N}(t_{N})^{-1}B_{N}X_{+}(t_{N}^{-})&\hbox{if $t\geq t_{N}$}\end{array}\right.

(3.7)

that is

X_{+}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}U_{0}(t)&\hbox{for $0\leq t<t_{1}$}\\
U_{1}(t)U_{1}(t_{1})^{-1}B_{1}U_{0}(t_{1})&\hbox{for $t_{1}\leq t<t_{2}$}\\
U_{i}(t)U_{i}(t_{i})^{-1}B_{i}U_{i-1}(t_{i})U_{i-1}(t_{i-1})^{-1}\ldots B_{1}U_{0}(t_{1})&\hbox{for $t_{i}\leq t<t_{i+1}$}\\
\vdots\\
U_{N}(t)U_{N}(t_{N})^{-1}B_{N}U_{N-1}(t_{N})U_{N-1}(t_{N-1})^{-1}\ldots B_{1}U_{0}(t_{1})&\hbox{for $t\geq t_{N}$.}\end{array}\right.

Similarly, the fundamental matrix of (
3.3
), where
t\leq 0
, is given by the (discontinuous) invertible matrix

X_{-}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}U_{0}(t)&\hbox{if $t_{-1}<t\leq 0$}\\
U_{j}(t)U_{j}(t_{j})^{-1}B_{j}^{-1}X_{-}(t_{j}^{+})&\hbox{if $t_{j-1}<t\leq t_{j}$}\\
U_{-M}(t)U_{-M}(t_{-M})^{-1}B_{-M}^{-1}X_{-}(t_{-M}^{+})&\hbox{if $t\leq t_{-M}.$}\end{array}\right.

(3.8)

Note that
X_{+}(t)
is continuous for
t\neq t_{1},\ldots,t_{N}
and right-continuous at
t=t_{1},\ldots,t_{N}
and
X_{-}(t)
is continuous for
t\neq t_{-1},\ldots,t_{-M}
and left-continuous at
t\neq t_{-1},\ldots,t_{-M}
.

It is clear that
\dot{X}_{+}(t)={\cal A}(t)X_{+}(t)
, for
t\geq 0
,
t\neq t_{i}
,
\dot{X}_{-}(t)={\cal A}(t)X_{-}(t)
, for
t\leq 0
,
t\neq t_{j}
,
X_{\pm}(0)=\mathbb{I}
, the identity matrix, and

\begin{array}[]{l}X_{+}(t_{i}^{+})=B_{i}X_{+}(t_{i}^{-})\\
X_{-}(t_{j}^{+})=B_{j}X_{-}(t_{j}^{-}).\end{array}

Actually we can write

X_{+}(t_{i})=B_{i}X_{+}(t_{i}^{-}),\quad X_{-}(t_{j})=B_{j}^{-1}X_{-}(t_{j}^{+})

since
X_{+}(t)
is right-continuous and
X_{-}(t)
is left-continuous. Then, from (
3.7
) and (
3.8
) we see that

X_{+}(t)X_{+}(t_{N}^{+})=U_{N}(t)U_{N}(t_{N})^{-1},\forall t\geq t_{N}

(3.9)

and, similarly,

X_{-}(t)X_{-}(t_{N}^{-})=U_{N}(t)U_{N}(t_{N})^{-1},\forall t\leq t_{-M}.

(3.10)

Remark 3.2
.

i) Let
\tau\geq 0
be a fixed number. For
t\geq 0
,
x(t)=X_{+}(t)X_{+}(\tau)^{-1}\tilde{x}
is the right-continuous solution of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\dot{x}={\cal A}(t)x,\quad\hbox{for $t\geq 0$, $t\neq t_{1},\ldots,t_{N}$}\\
x(t_{i}^{+})=B_{i}x(t_{i}^{-})\\
x(\tau^{+})=\tilde{x}.\end{array}\right.

(3.11)

Indeed, it is obvious that
\dot{x}(t)={\cal A}(t)x(t)
for
t\geq 0
,
t\neq t_{1},\ldots,t_{N}
and that
x(t_{i}^{+})=B_{i}x(t_{i}^{-})
, since
X_{+}(t_{i}^{+})=B_{i}X_{+}(t_{i}^{-})
. Moreover
x(\tau^{+})=X_{+}(\tau^{+})X_{+}(\tau)^{-1}\tilde{x}=X_{+}(\tau)X_{+}(\tau)^{-1}\tilde{x}=\tilde{x}
, since
X_{+}(t)
is right-continuous at any
t\geq 0
.

Similarly, for
t\leq 0
and any fixed
\tau\leq 0
,
x(t)=X_{-}(t)X_{-}(\tau)^{-1}\tilde{x}
is the left-continuous solution of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\dot{x}={\cal A}(t)x,\quad\hbox{for $t\leq 0$, $t\neq t_{-1},\ldots,t_{-N}$}\\
x(t_{-i}^{-})=B_{i}^{-1}x(t_{-i}^{+})\\
x(\tau^{-})=\tilde{x}.\end{array}\right.

(3.12)

ii) As
X_{+}(t)X_{+}(\tau)^{-1}\tilde{x}
is right-continuous at
t=t_{1},\ldots,t_{N}
it is also clear that
X_{+}(t)X_{+}(\tau)^{-1}\tilde{x}
satisfies

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\dot{x}={\cal A}(t)x,\quad\hbox{for $t\geq 0$, $t\neq t_{1},\ldots,t_{N}$}\\
x(t_{i})=B_{i}x(t_{i}^{-})\\
x(\tau)=\tilde{x}\end{array}\right.

(3.13)

and, similarly,
X_{-}(t)X_{-}(\tau)^{-1}\tilde{x}
satisfies

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\dot{x}={\cal A}(t)x,\quad\hbox{for $t\leq 0$, $t\neq t_{1},\ldots,t_{N}$}\\
x(t_{-i})=B_{i}^{-1}x(t_{-i}^{+})\\
x(\tau)=\tilde{x}.\end{array}\right.

(3.14)

We have the following

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose that the linear system

\dot{x}=A_{N}(t)x\quad[resp.\dot{x}=A_{-M}(t)x]

has an exponential dichotomy on
t\geq t_{N}
(resp.
t\leq t_{-M}
) with constant
K
, exponent
\delta
and projection
{\cal P}_{+}
(resp.
{\cal P}_{-}
). Then, the linear system (
3.3
) (resp. (
3.5
)) with
{\cal A}(t)
as in (
3.2
) (resp. (
3.4
)) has an exponential dichotomy on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, (resp.
\mathbb{R}_{-}
) with the same exponent
\delta
, constant
\tilde{K}\geq K
and projection

\begin{array}[]{c}\tilde{\cal P}_{+}=X_{+}(t_{N})^{-1}{\cal P}_{+}X_{+}(t_{N})\\
(\hbox{resp. }\tilde{\cal P}_{-}=X_{-}(t_{-M})^{-1}{\cal P}_{-}X_{-}(t_{-M})).\end{array}

(3.15)

Proof.

As
X_{+}(t)
is right continuous at
t=t_{N}
, for
t_{N}\leq s\leq t
we have

\begin{array}[]{l}|X_{+}(t)\tilde{\cal P}_{+}X_{+}(s)^{-1}|=\\
|U_{N}(t)U_{N}(t_{N})^{-1}B_{N}X_{+}(t_{N}^{-})X_{+}(t_{N}^{+})^{-1}{\cal P}_{+}X_{+}(t_{N}^{+})X_{+}(t_{N}^{-})^{-1}B_{N}^{-1}U_{N}(t_{N})U_{N}(s)^{-1}|\\
=|U_{N}(t)U_{N}(t_{N})^{-1}{\cal P}_{+}U_{N}(t_{N})U_{N}(s)^{-1}|\leq Ke^{-\delta(t-s)}\end{array}

since
U_{N}(t)U_{N}(t_{N})^{-1}
is the fundamental matrix of
\dot{x}=A_{N}(t)x
on
t\geq t_{N}
. Similarly we see that

|X_{+}(s)(\mathbb{I}-\tilde{\cal P}_{+})X_{+}(t)^{-1}|\leq Ke^{-\delta(t-s)}

for
t_{N}\leq s\leq t
. Being
0\leq s\leq t\leq t_{N}
compact and
X_{+}(t)\tilde{\cal P}_{+}X_{+}(s)^{-1}
piecewise continuous with right and left limits at the discontinuity points, there exists
\hat{K}\geq K
such that

\begin{array}[]{l}|X_{+}(t)\tilde{\cal P}_{+}X_{+}(s)^{-1}|\leq\hat{K}e^{-\delta(t-s)}\\
|X_{+}(s)(\mathbb{I}-\tilde{\cal P}_{+})X_{+}(t)^{-1}|\leq\hat{K}e^{-\delta(t-s)}\end{array}

for
0\leq s\leq t\leq t_{N}
. Finally, for
0\leq s\leq t_{N}<t
, we have, using right-continuity of
X_{+}(t)
:

\begin{array}[]{l}|X_{+}(t)\tilde{\cal P}_{+}X_{+}(s)^{-1}|\leq|X_{+}(t)\tilde{\cal P}_{+}X_{+}(t_{N})^{-1}||B_{N}X_{+}(t_{N}^{-})\tilde{\cal P}_{+}X_{+}(s)^{-1}|\\
\qquad\leq Ke^{-\delta(t-t_{N})}|B_{N}|\hat{K}e^{-\delta(t_{N}-s)}\leq K|B_{N}|\hat{K}e^{-\delta(t-s)}\\
|X_{+}(s)(\mathbb{I}-\tilde{\cal P}_{+})X_{+}(t)^{-1}|\\
\qquad\leq|X_{+}(s)(\mathbb{I}-\tilde{\cal P}_{+})X_{+}(t_{N}^{-})^{-1}||B_{N}^{-1}X_{+}(t_{N}^{+})(\mathbb{I}-\tilde{\cal P}_{+})X_{+}(t)^{-1}|\\
\qquad\leq Ke^{-\delta(s-t_{N})}|B_{N}^{-1}|\hat{K}e^{-\delta(t_{N}-t)}\leq K|B_{N}^{-1}|\hat{K}e^{-\delta(t-s)}\end{array}

proving the result for
t\geq 0
with
\tilde{K}=\max\{K,\hat{K},K|B_{N}^{-1}|\hat{K}\}
. A similar argument works when
t\leq 0
. The Lemma is proved.
∎

The following result characterises
{\cal R}\tilde{P}_{+}
, resp.
{\cal N}\tilde{P}_{-}
, in terms of bounded solutions of system (
3.3
), resp. (
3.5
), extending to the discontinuous case a simular result for continuous equations.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
{\cal A}(t)
be either as in (
3.2
) or (
3.4
). Suppose that the condition of Lemma
3.3
hold and let
\tilde{\cal P}_{\pm}
be as in (

). Then
\xi_{+}\in{\cal R}\tilde{\cal P}_{+}
if and only if the solution of the discontinuous system (
3.3
) such that
x(0)=\xi_{+}
is bounded for
t\geq 0
. Similarly,
\xi_{-}\in{\cal N}\tilde{\cal P}_{-}
if and only if the solution of the discontinuous system (
3.3
) such that

x(0)=\xi_{-}
is bounded for
t\leq 0
.

Proof.

If
\xi_{+}\in{\cal R}\tilde{\cal P}_{+}
we have

|X_{+}(t)\xi_{+}|=|X_{+}(t)\tilde{\cal P}_{+}\xi_{+}|\leq Ke^{-\delta t}|\xi_{+}|

that is the solution of (
3.3
) starting from
\xi_{+}
is bounded. Vice versa, suppose that
x(t)
is a solution of (
3.3
) bounded on
t\geq 0
. We have

\begin{array}[]{l}|[\mathbb{I}-\tilde{\cal P}_{+}]x(0)|=|[\mathbb{I}-\tilde{\cal P}_{+}]X_{+}(t)^{-1}x(t)|\leq Ke^{-\delta t}\sup_{t\geq 0}|x(t)|\to 0\end{array}

as

. Then
[\mathbb{I}-\tilde{\cal P}_{+}]x(0)=0
and hence

. By a similar argument we prove the thesis when
\xi_{-}\in{\cal N}\tilde{\cal P}_{-}
is concerned.
∎

We conclude this section with the following

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
B_{i}
,
B_{j}
, be invertile
n\times n
matrices and
k(t)
be a bounded integrable function for
t\geq 0
, (resp.
t\leq 0
).
Suppose the condition of Lemma
3.3
hold and set

\begin{array}[]{l}\tilde{\cal P}_{+}^{\tau}=X_{+}(\tau)\tilde{\cal P}_{+}X_{+}(\tau)^{-1}\\
\tilde{\cal P}_{-}^{\tau}=X_{-}(-\tau)\tilde{\cal P}_{-}X_{-}(-\tau)^{-1}\end{array}

where
\tilde{\cal P}_{\pm}
is as in (
3.15
) and
0\leq\tau\in\mathbb{R}
is a fixed number. Then, for any
\xi_{+}\in{\cal R}\tilde{\cal P}_{+}^{\tau}
(resp.
\xi_{-}\in{\cal N}\tilde{\cal P}_{-}^{\tau}
) the linear inhomogeneous system

\begin{array}[]{l}\dot{x}={\cal A}(t)x+k(t)\\
x(t_{i}^{+})=B_{i}x(t_{i}^{-}),\\
\tilde{\cal P}_{+}^{\tau}x(\tau)=\xi_{+}\end{array}

(3.16)

with
t\geq 0
, [resp.

\begin{array}[]{l}\dot{x}={\cal A}(t)x+k(t)\\

(\mathbb{I}-\tilde{\cal P}_{-}^{\tau})x(-\tau)=\xi_{-}\end{array}

when

] has the unique right-continuous, [resp. left-continuous when

] bounded solution

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle x(t)=X_{+}(t)\tilde{\cal P}_{+}X_{+}(\tau)^{-1}\xi_{+}+\int_{\tau}^{t}X_{+}(t)\tilde{\cal P}_{+}X_{+}(s)^{-1}k(s)ds\\
\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{t}^{\infty}X_{+}(t)(\mathbb{I}-\tilde{\cal P}_{+})X_{+}(s)^{-1}k(s)ds\end{array}

(3.17)

[resp.

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle x(t)=X_{-}(t)(\mathbb{I}-\tilde{\cal P}_{-})X_{-}(-\tau)^{-1}\xi_{-}+\int_{-\infty}^{t}X_{-}(t)\tilde{\cal P}_{-}X_{-}(s)^{-1}k(s)ds\\
\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{t}^{-\tau}X_{-}(t)(\mathbb{I}-\tilde{\cal P}_{-})X_{-}(s)^{-1}k(s)ds\end{array}

(3.18)

if
t\leq 0
]. Moreover such a solution satisfies

\sup_{t\geq\tau}|x(t)|\leq K[|\xi_{+}|+2\delta^{-1}\sup_{t\geq 0}|k(t)|]

(3.19)

if
t\geq 0
[resp.

\sup_{t\leq-\tau}|x(t)|\leq K[|\xi_{-}|+2\delta^{-1}\sup_{t\leq 0}|k(t)|]

(3.20)

if
t\leq 0
].

Proof.

We only give the proof for
t\geq 0
, the proof for
t\leq 0
being similar. We prove uniqueness, first. Suppose that
x_{1}(t),x_{2}(t)
are two solutions of (
3.16
), right-continuous and bounded for
t\geq 0
. Then
x(t)=x_{1}(t)-x_{2}(t)
is a right-continuous, bounded solution of

\begin{array}[]{l}\dot{x}={\cal A}(t)x\\
x(t_{i}^{+})=B_{i}x(t_{i}^{-})\\
\tilde{\cal P}_{+}^{\tau}x(\tau)=0.\end{array}

Then, as we have observed in Remark
3.2
-i),
x(t)=X_{+}(t)X_{+}(\tau)^{-1}x(\tau)
, so:

\begin{array}[]{l}x(\tau)=(\mathbb{I}-\tilde{\cal P}_{+}^{\tau})x(\tau)=(\mathbb{I}-\tilde{\cal P}_{+}^{\tau})X_{+}(\tau)X_{+}(t)^{-1}x(t)\\
\qquad=X_{+}(\tau)(\mathbb{I}-\tilde{\cal P}_{+})X_{+}(t)^{-1}x(t)\end{array}

from which we get

|x(\tau)|\leq Ke^{-\delta(t-\tau)}\sup_{t\geq\tau}|x(t)|\to 0

as
t\to\infty
, since
x(t)
is bounded. This proves that
x(\tau)=0
and then
x(t)=0
. Hence we have uniqueness. To show the existence we observe that the function given in (
3.17
) is right-continuous, bounded for

(and hence also for

) and satisfies (
3.16
). Finally, from (

) we get:

|x(t)|\leq Ke^{-\delta(t-\tau)}|\xi_{+}|+\int_{0}^{\infty}Ke^{-\delta|t-s|}ds\,\sup_{t\geq 0}|k(t)|

from which (
3.19
) easily follows.
∎

4
Bounded solutions on the half lines

In this section we prove the existence of continuous solutions
(x(t),y(t))
, with
t\geq 0
, of the perturbed linear system (
1.7
) such that

\sup_{t\geq 0}|x(t)-u(t,y(t))|<\rho

where
\rho>0
is a sufficiently small positive real number. By a similar argument we can also prove the existence of continuous solutions of (
1.7
), with
t\leq 0
, such that

\sup_{t\leq 0}|x(t)-u(t,y(t))|<\rho.

From
A_{3})
it follows that the number of the eigenvalues of
f_{N,x}(w_{+}(y),y)
,
f_{-M,x}(w_{-}(y),y)
with negative (and then also positive) real parts, counted with multiplicities, is independent of
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Moreover it also follows that all eigenvalues are bounded functions of
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Indeed, since
f_{N,x}(w_{+}(y),y)
is bounded, the matrix
\mathbb{I}-\lambda^{-1}f_{N,x}(w_{+}(y),y)
is invertible for
|\lambda|>R
, sufficiently large and independent of
y
. Hence all eigenvalues have to satisfy
|\lambda|\leq R
. The same arguments work as far as the eigenvalues of
f_{-M,x}(w_{-}(y),y)
are concerned.

As in
A_{3})
, let
k<n
be the number of eigenvalues with negative real parts, counted with multiplicities, of the matrix
f_{N,x}(w_{+}(y),y)
and
\delta_{0}
be any positive number strictly less than
\min\{|{Re}\lambda(y)|\}
, where
\lambda(y)
are the eigenvalues of
f_{N,x}(w_{+}(y),y)
. According to
[
11
]
the system
\dot{x}=f_{N,x}(w_{+}(y),y)x
has an exponential dichotomy on
\mathbb{R}
with exponent
\delta_{0}
and (spectral) projection

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle P^{0}_{+}(y)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}(z\mathbb{I}-f_{N,x}(w_{+}(y),y))^{-1}dz\\
\qquad\displaystyle=\sum_{Re\lambda(y)<0}{\rm Res}((z\mathbb{I}-f_{N,x}(w_{+}(y),y))^{-1},z=\lambda(y))\end{array}

where
{\rm Res}(F(z),z=z_{0})
is the residual of the meromorphic function
F(z)
at
z_{0}
and
\Gamma
is a closed curve that contains in its interior all eigenvalues of
f_{N,x}(0,y)
with negative real parts, but none of those with positive real parts. Hence
\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}|P_{+}^{0}(y)|\leq M
, for some
M\geq 1
. Similarly we see that
\dot{x}=f_{-M,x}(w_{-}(y),y)x
has an exponential dichotomy on
\mathbb{R}
with exponent
\delta_{0}
and projection
P^{0}_{-}(y)
such that
\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}|P_{+}^{0}(y)|\leq M
, for some
M\geq 1
.

Now,
from
A_{2})
we know that

\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}u^{\pm}_{N}(t,y)=w_{\pm}(y)

uniformly with respect to
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Let
T_{+}>\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}t_{N}(y)
,
T_{-}<\inf_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}t_{-M}(y)
and take
0<\delta<\delta_{0}
. From the roughness of exponential dichotomies (cfr.
[
11
, Proposition 2, p. 34]
) the linear systems

\dot{x}=f_{N,x}(u^{+}_{N}(t+T_{+},y),y)x

(4.1)

and

\dot{x}=f_{-M,x}(u^{-}_{-M}(t+T_{-},y),y)x

(4.2)

have an exponential dichotomy on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
\mathbb{R}_{-}
resp., uniform with respect to
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, with projections
P_{+}(y)
, resp.
P_{-}(y)
, of rank
k
, constant
K
and exponent
\delta
. Moreover, according to
[

, it can be assumed that, for

sufficiently small it results:
{\cal N}P_{+}(y)={\cal N}P_{+}(y_{0})
,
{\cal R}P_{-}(y)={\cal R}P_{-}(y_{0})
and in this case the projections are smooth with respect to

. Note that,
{\cal N}P_{+}(y)={\cal N}P_{+}(y_{0})
and
{\cal R}P_{-}(y)={\cal R}P_{-}(y_{0})
are equivalent to

\begin{array}[]{l}P_{+}(y)=P_{+}(y)P_{+}(y_{0}),\quad P_{+}(y_{0})=P_{+}(y_{0})P_{+}(y)\\
P_{-}(y)=P_{-}(y_{0})P_{-}(y),\quad P_{-}(y_{0})=P_{-}(y)P_{-}(y_{0}).\end{array}

(4.3)

For
i=0,1,\ldots,N
,
j=0,-1,\ldots,-M
, let
U_{i}^{+}(t,y)
, [resp.
U_{j}^{-}(t,y)
] be the fundamental matrix of

\dot{x}=f_{i,x}(u_{i}^{+}(t,y),y)x\quad\hbox{[resp. }\dot{x}=f_{j,x}(u_{j}^{-}(t,y),y)x\hbox{]}

in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
resp.,
\mathbb{R}_{-}
that is

\begin{array}[]{l}\dot{U}_{i}^{+}(t,y)=f_{i,x}(u_{i}^{+}(t,y),y)U_{i}^{+}(t,y),\quad t\geq 0,\\
U_{i}^{+}(0,y)=\mathbb{I}.\end{array}

resp.

\begin{array}[]{l}\dot{U}_{j}^{-}(t,y)=f_{j,x}(u_{j}^{-}(t,y),y)U_{j}^{-}(t,y),\quad t\leq 0,\\
U_{j}^{-}(0,y)=\mathbb{I}.\end{array}

We have the following

Lemma 4.1
.

For any
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
the linear system

\dot{x}=f_{N,x}(u^{+}_{N}(t,y),y)x,

(4.4)

resp.

\dot{x}=f_{-M,x}(u^{-}_{-M}(t,y),y)x,

(4.5)

has an exponential dichotomy on
t\geq\tau
, resp.
t\leq\tau
, with exponent
\delta
, constant
\tilde{K}
independent on
y
and projections

\begin{array}[]{l}Q_{+}(y)=U^{+}_{N}(\tau,y)U^{+}_{N}(T_{+},y)^{-1}P_{+}(y)U^{+}_{N}(T_{+},y)U^{+}_{N}(\tau,y)^{-1}\\
Q_{-}(y)=U^{-}_{-M}(\tau,y)U^{-}_{-M}(T_{-},y)^{-1}P_{-}(y)U^{-}_{-M}(T_{-},y)U^{-}_{-M}(\tau,y)^{-1}.\end{array}

In particular, if
\tau=T_{+}
, resp.
\tau=T_{-}
, then
Q_{+}(y)=P_{+}(y)
, resp.
Q_{-}(y)=P_{-}(y)
, and
\tilde{K}=K
.

Proof.

As
U_{N}^{+}(t,y)
is the fundamental matrix of (
4.4
) in
\mathbb{R}_{\pm}
, the fundamental matrix of (
4.1
) on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
is

\tilde{U}(t,y):=U^{+}_{N}(t+T_{+},y)U^{+}_{N}(T_{+},y)^{-1}

while the fundamental matrix of (
4.4
) on
t\geq\tau
is

\bar{U}(t,y)=U^{+}_{N}(t,y)U^{+}_{N}(\tau,y)^{-1}.

Then

\bar{U}(t,y)U^{+}_{N}(\tau,y)=U^{+}_{N}(t,y)=\tilde{U}(t-T_{+},y)U^{+}_{N}(T_{+},y).

So we get, for
t\geq s\geq T_{+}
:

\begin{array}[]{l}|\bar{U}(t,y)Q_{+}(y)\bar{U}(s,y)^{-1}|=\\
|\tilde{U}(t-T_{+},y)U^{+}_{N}(T_{+},y)U^{+}_{N}(\tau,y)^{-1}Q_{+}(y)U^{+}_{N}(\tau,y)U^{+}_{N}(T_{+},y)^{-1}\tilde{U}(s-T_{+},y)^{-1}|=\\
|\tilde{U}(t-T_{+},y)P_{+}(y)\tilde{U}(s-T_{+},y)^{-1}|\leq Ke^{-\delta(t-s)}\end{array}

and similarly

\begin{array}[]{l}|\bar{U}(s,y)(\mathbb{I}-Q_{+}(y))\bar{U}(t,y)^{-1}|=\\
|\tilde{U}(s-T_{+},y)(\mathbb{I}-P_{+}(y))\tilde{U}(t-T_{+},y)^{-1}|\leq Ke^{-\delta(t-s)}.\end{array}

Hence (
4.4
) has an exponential dichotomy on
t\geq T_{+}
with projection
Q_{+}(y)
, constant
\tilde{K}=K
and exponent
\delta
. If
\tau\geq T_{+}
the proof is finished. Moreover if
\tau=T_{+}
we have

Q_{+}(y)=P_{+}(y)\quad\hbox{and}\quad Q_{-}(y)=P_{-}(y).

If
\tau<T_{+}
, for
\tau\leq s\leq t\leq T_{+}
let

v(t):=\bar{U}(t,y)Q_{+}(y)\bar{U}(s,y)^{-1}=\tilde{U}(t-T_{+},y)P_{+}(y)\tilde{U}(s-T_{+},y)^{-1}.

Then
v(t)
satisfies

\dot{v}(t)=f_{N,x}(u^{+}_{N}(t,y),y)v(t)

with
v(T_{+})=P_{+}(y)\tilde{U}(s-T_{+},y)^{-1}
. Hence

v(t)=P_{+}(y))\tilde{U}(s-T_{+},y)^{-1}-\int_{t}^{T_{+}}f_{N,x}(u^{+}_{N}(s,y),y)v(s)ds.

So, using
|P_{+}(y)\tilde{U}(s-T_{+},y)^{-1}|\leq Ke^{-\delta(T_{+}-s)}
:

|v(t)|\leq Ke^{-\delta(T_{+}-s)}+\int_{t}^{T_{+}}\bar{F}|v(s)|ds.

where
\bar{F}
is an upper bound for
|f_{N,x}(u^{+}_{N}(s,y),y)|
. Setting
r(t):\int_{t}^{T_{+}}|v(s)|ds
we get

\frac{d}{dt}[r(t)e^{\bar{F}t}]=[r^{\prime}(t)+\bar{F}r(t)]e^{\bar{F}t}\geq-Ke^{\bar{F}t}.

Integrating on
[t,T_{+}]
we get, since
r(T_{+})=0
,
-r(t)e^{\bar{F}t}\geq-K\bar{F}^{-1}[e^{\bar{F}T_{+}}-e^{\bar{F}t}]
. Hence

\int_{t}^{T_{+}}\bar{F}|v(s)|ds=\bar{F}r(t)\leq K[e^{\bar{F}(T_{+}-t)}-1]

and then

\begin{array}[]{l}|v(t)|\leq Ke^{-\delta(T_{+}-s)}+Ke^{\bar{F}(T_{+}-t)}\\
=K[e^{-\delta(T_{+}-t)}+e^{\bar{F}(T_{+}-t)+\delta(t-s)}]e^{-\delta(t-s)}\leq\hat{K}e^{-\delta(t-s)}\end{array}

where

\hat{K}=K[1+e^{(\bar{F}+\delta)(T_{+}-\tau)}].

Note that
\hat{K}
is independent of
y
. Finally, for
\tau\leq s\leq T_{+}\leq t
we have

\begin{array}[]{l}|\bar{U}(t,y)Q_{+}(y)\bar{U}(s,y)^{-1}|\leq|\bar{U}(t,y)Q_{+}(y)\bar{U}(T_{+},y)^{-1}||\bar{U}(T_{+},y)Q_{+}(y)\bar{U}(s,y)^{-1}|\\
\leq Ke^{-\delta(t-T_{+})}\hat{K}e^{\delta(s-T_{+})}=K\hat{K}e^{\delta(t-s)}.\end{array}

By a similar argument we prove that

|\tilde{U}(t-T_{+},y)(\mathbb{I}-P_{+}(y))\tilde{U}(s-T_{+},y)^{-1}|\leq\tilde{K}e^{\delta(t-s)}

for
\tau\leq t\leq s
.

The proof that (
4.5
) has an exponential dichotomy on
t\leq\tau
, with projection
Q_{-}(y)
, exponent
\delta
and a constant independent of
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is similar.
∎

\begin{array}[]{ll}(x_{+}(t,\xi_{+},\alpha,\varepsilon),y_{+}(t,\xi_{+},\alpha,\varepsilon))=(x^{+}_{N}(t,\xi_{+},\alpha,\varepsilon),y^{+}_{N}(t,\xi_{+},\alpha,\varepsilon))&\hbox{for $t\geq T_{+}$}\\
(x_{-}(t,\xi_{+},\alpha,\varepsilon),y_{-}(t,\xi_{+},\alpha,\varepsilon))=(x^{-}_{-M}(t,\xi_{+},\alpha,\varepsilon),y^{-}_{-M}(t,\xi_{+},\alpha,\varepsilon))&\hbox{for $t\leq T_{-}$}\end{array}

\rho>\sup_{t\leq 0}|x_{-}(t,\xi_{-},\alpha,\varepsilon)-u(t,y_{-}(t,\xi_{-},\alpha,\varepsilon))|\to 0&\hbox{as $|\xi_{-}|+|\varepsilon|\to 0$}\end{array}

(4.11)

uniformly with respect to
\alpha
.

Proof.

Suppose

. Since

\alpha

, provided

is sufficiently small and uniformly with respect to
\alpha
. Then
(x^{+}_{N}(t,\xi_{+},\alpha,\varepsilon),y^{+}_{N}(t,\xi_{+},\alpha,\varepsilon)
can be extended to a solution of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\dot{x}=f_{N}(x,y)\\
\dot{y}=\varepsilon g(x,y)\end{array}\right.

which is defined for
t\geq 0
and such that

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_{+}}|x^{+}_{N}(t,\xi_{+},\alpha,\varepsilon)-u^{+}_{N}(t,y^{+}_{N}(t,\xi_{+},\alpha,\varepsilon))|\to 0

, uniformly with respect to
\alpha
. Note that

as

, uniformly with respect to
(\xi_{+},\alpha)
, since

and

uniformly with respect to

. Now, from

and the implicit function theorem it follows that there exists a

-function

, bounded together with its derivatives, such that

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}h(x^{+}_{N}(t,\xi_{+},\alpha,\varepsilon),y^{+}_{N}(t,\xi_{+},\alpha,\varepsilon))_{|t=t^{*}_{N}(\xi_{+},\alpha,\varepsilon)}>\eta\end{array}

provided

is sufficiently small, uniformly with respect to
\alpha
. Next, from the continuous dependence on the data, we see that the system

\dot{y}=\varepsilon g(x,y,\varepsilon)\\

has a unique solution

, defined for

\end{array}

5
The variational equation

Let
\ell\in\{-M,\ldots,-1,1,\ldots,N\}
. For fixed
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
we define linear operators
B_{\ell}(\alpha):\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
as follows:

B_{\ell}(\alpha)x=x-\frac{h_{x}(u(t_{\ell}(\alpha),\alpha),\alpha)x}{h_{x}(u(t_{\ell}(\alpha),\alpha),\alpha)\dot{u}(t_{\ell}(\alpha)^{-},\alpha)}[\dot{u}(t_{\ell}(\alpha)^{-},\alpha)-\dot{u}(t_{\ell}(\alpha)^{+},\alpha)].

(5.1)

Note that (recall that
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
,
j\in\{-1,\ldots,-M\}
):

\begin{array}[]{l}h_{x}(u(t_{i}(\alpha),\alpha),\alpha)\dot{u}(t_{i}(\alpha)^{-},\alpha)=h_{x}(u(t_{i}(\alpha),\alpha),\alpha)f_{i-1}(u(t_{i}(\alpha),\alpha),\alpha)\neq 0\\
h_{x}(u(t_{j}(\alpha),\alpha),\alpha)\dot{u}(t_{j}(\alpha)^{-},\alpha)=h_{x}(u(t_{j}(\alpha),\alpha),\alpha)f_{j}(u(t_{j}(\alpha),\alpha),\alpha)\neq 0\end{array}

We have the following:

Proposition 5.1
.

For any
\ell
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
x\mapsto B_{\ell}(\alpha)x

are invertible linear maps. Moreover
x_{+,\xi_{+}}(t,0,\alpha,0)
is a solution of

\begin{array}[]{l}\dot{x}=A(t,\alpha)x:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}f_{i-1,x}(u(t,\alpha),\alpha)x&\hbox{if $t_{i-1}(\alpha)<t<t_{i}(\alpha)$}\\
f_{N,x}(u(t,\alpha),\alpha)x&\hbox{if $t>t_{N}(\alpha)$}\end{array}\right.\\
x(t_{i}(\alpha)^{+})=B_{i}(\alpha)x(t_{i}(\alpha)^{-}),\end{array}

(5.2)

which is
C^{1}
for
t\neq t_{i}(\alpha)
, bounded for
t\geq 0
and can be assumed to be right-continuous at
t=t_{i}(\alpha)
. Similarly

x_{-,\xi_{-}}(t,0,\alpha,0)
is a solution of

\begin{array}[]{l}\dot{x}=A(t,\alpha)x:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}f_{j+1,x}(u(t,\alpha),\alpha)x&\hbox{if $t_{j}(\alpha)<t<t_{j+1}(\alpha)$}\\
f_{-M,x}(u(t,\alpha),\alpha)x&\hbox{if $t<t_{-M}(\alpha)$}\end{array}\right.\\
x(t_{j}(\alpha)^{+})=B_{j}(\alpha)x(t_{j}(\alpha)^{-}),\end{array}

(5.3)

which is
C^{1}
for
t\neq t_{j}(\alpha)
,bounded for
t\leq 0
and can be assumed to be left-continuous at
t=t_{j}(\alpha)
.

Proof.

First we prove that
B_{\ell}(\alpha):\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is invertible. If
\dot{u}(t_{\ell}(\alpha)^{-},\alpha)=\dot{u}(t_{\ell}(\alpha)^{+},\alpha)
, there is nothing to prove since
B_{\ell}(\alpha)x=x
. So, suppose that
\dot{u}(t_{\ell}(\alpha)^{-},\alpha)\neq\dot{u}(t_{\ell}(\alpha)^{+},\alpha)
and
B_{\ell}(\alpha)x=0
. Then

x=\mu[\dot{u}(t_{\ell}(\alpha)^{-},\alpha)-\dot{u}(t_{\ell}(\alpha)^{+},\alpha)]

where
\mu=\frac{h_{x}(u(t_{\ell}(\alpha),\alpha),\alpha)x}{h_{x}(u(t_{\ell}(\alpha),\alpha),\alpha)\dot{u}(t_{\ell}(\alpha)^{-},\alpha)}
. Hence:

\begin{array}[]{l}B_{\ell}(\alpha)x=\mu[\dot{u}(t_{\ell}(\alpha)^{-},\alpha)-\dot{u}(t_{\ell}(\alpha)^{+},\alpha)]\\
\qquad-\mu\frac{h_{x}(u(t_{\ell}(\alpha),\alpha),\alpha)[\dot{u}(t_{\ell}(\alpha)^{-},\alpha)-\dot{u}(t_{\ell}(\alpha)^{+},\alpha)]}{h_{x}(u(t_{i}(\alpha),\alpha),\alpha)\dot{u}(t_{\ell}(\alpha)^{-},\alpha)}[\dot{u}(t_{\ell}(\alpha)^{-},\alpha)-\dot{u}(t_{\ell}(\alpha)^{+},\alpha)]\\
=\mu\frac{h_{x}(u(t_{\ell}(\alpha),\alpha),\alpha)\dot{u}(t_{\ell}(\alpha)^{+},\alpha)}{h_{x}(u(t_{\ell}(\alpha),\alpha),\alpha)\dot{u}(t_{\ell}(\alpha)^{-},\alpha)}[\dot{u}(t_{\ell}(\alpha)^{-},\alpha)-\dot{u}(t_{\ell}(\alpha)^{+},\alpha)].\end{array}

But then
\mu=0
since

\begin{array}[]{l}h_{x}(u(t_{\ell}(\alpha),\alpha),\alpha)\dot{u}(t_{\ell}(\alpha)^{+},\alpha)\\
=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}h_{x}(u(t_{i}(\alpha),\alpha),\alpha)f_{i}(u(t_{i}(\alpha),\alpha),\alpha)&\hbox{if $\ell=i>0$}\\
h_{x}(u(t_{j}(\alpha),\alpha),\alpha)f_{j+1}(u(t_{j}(\alpha),\alpha),\alpha)&\hbox{if $\ell=j<0$}\end{array}\right.\neq 0.\end{array}

Next,
x_{+}(t,\xi_{+},\alpha,0)
is a continuous, piecewise
C^{r}
, solution of the differential equation

\dot{x}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}f_{i-1}(x,\alpha)&\hbox{if $t^{*}_{i-1}(\xi_{+},\alpha,0)<t<t^{*}_{i}(\xi_{+},\alpha,0)$}\\
f_{N}(x,\alpha)&\hbox{if $t>t^{*}_{N}(\xi_{+},\alpha,0)$.}\end{array}\right.

Hence, for
t^{*}_{i}(\xi_{+},\alpha,0)<t<t^{*}_{i+1}(\xi_{+},\alpha,0)
, when
i\leq N-1
, or
t\geq t^{*}_{N}(\xi_{+},\alpha,0)
when

, we have

x_{+}(t,\xi_{+},\alpha,0)=x_{+}(t^{*}_{i}(\xi_{+},\alpha,0)^{-},\xi_{+},\alpha,0)+\int_{t^{*}_{i}(\xi_{+},\alpha,0)}^{t}f_{i}(x_{+}(s,\xi_{+},\alpha,0),\alpha)ds.

Differentiating with respect to
\xi_{+}
we get, for the same values of

\begin{array}[]{l}x_{+,\xi_{+}}(t,\xi_{+},\alpha,0)=x_{+,\xi_{+}}(t^{*}_{i}(\xi_{+},\alpha,0)^{-},\xi_{+},\alpha,0)\\
\displaystyle[\dot{x}_{+}(t^{*}_{i}(\xi_{+},\alpha,0)^{-},\xi_{+},\alpha,0)-\dot{x}_{+}(t^{*}_{i}(\xi_{+},\alpha,0)^{+},\xi_{+},\alpha,0)]\frac{\partial t^{*}_{i}}{\partial\xi_{+}}(\xi_{+},\alpha,0)\\
\displaystyle+\int_{t_{i}^{*}(\xi_{+},\alpha,0)}^{t}f_{i,x}(x_{+}(s,\xi_{+},\alpha,0),\alpha)x_{\xi_{+}}(s,\xi_{+},\alpha,0)ds.\end{array}

and then,

\begin{array}[]{l}x_{+,\xi_{+}}(t^{*}_{i}(\xi_{+},\alpha,0)^{+},\xi_{+},\alpha,0)=x_{+,\xi_{+}}(t^{*}_{i}(\xi_{+},\alpha,0)^{-},\xi_{+},\alpha,0)+\\
\displaystyle+[f_{i-1}(\hat{x}^{*}_{i}(\xi_{+},\alpha),\alpha)-f_{i}(\hat{x}^{*}_{i}(\xi_{+},\alpha),\alpha)]\frac{\partial t^{*}_{i}}{\partial\xi_{+}}(\xi_{+},\alpha,0)\end{array}

(5.4)

where we write for simplicity

\hat{x}^{*}_{i}(\xi_{+},\alpha):=x_{+}(t^{*}_{i}(\xi_{+},\alpha,0),\xi_{+},\alpha,0).

(5.5)

Now, from Remark
4.6
we see that

\frac{\partial t^{*}_{i}}{\partial\xi_{+}}(\xi_{+},\alpha,0)=-\frac{h_{x}(\hat{x}^{*}_{i}(\xi_{+},\alpha),\alpha)x_{+,\xi_{+}}(t^{*}(\xi_{+},\alpha,0)^{-},\alpha,0)}{h_{x}(\hat{x}^{*}_{i}(\xi_{+},\alpha),\alpha)f_{i-1}(\hat{x}^{*}_{i}(\xi_{+},\alpha),\alpha)}.

Hence

(5.6)

where

\begin{array}[]{l}B_{i}(\xi_{+},\alpha)x=\\
x-[f_{i-1}(\hat{x}^{*}(\xi_{+},\alpha),\alpha)-f_{i}(\hat{x}^{*}(\xi_{+},\alpha),\alpha)]\frac{h_{x}(\hat{x}^{*}_{i}(\xi_{+},\alpha),\alpha)x}{h_{x}(\hat{x}^{*}_{i}(\xi_{+},\alpha),\alpha)f_{i-1}(\hat{x}^{*}_{i}(\xi_{+},\alpha),\alpha)}.\end{array}

Taking
\xi_{+}=0
, we see that

t\geq 0
of (
5.2
) where
B_{i}(\alpha)

).

5.3
) where

is as in (
5.1
).

. From Theorem

and such that
0<u(t)<1
.

9
Proof of Lemma (
4.2
)

In this appendix we give the proof of Lemma (
4.2
) for
u^{+}_{N}(t,y)
, the proof for
u^{-}_{-M}(t,y)
being similar. Let
0<\rho\leq 1
. Since
u^{+}_{N}(t,y)\to w_{+}(y)
uniformly with respect to
y
, there exists
T\geq T_{+}
such that

\sup_{t\geq T}|u^{+}_{N}(t,y)-w_{+}(y)|\leq\rho.

First we prove that
u^{+}_{N}(t,y)
satisfies the statement of the Lemma for
t_{N}^{+}(y)\leq t\leq T
. Indeed, for such values of
t
we have

u^{+}_{N}(t,y)=w^{+}_{N}(y)+\int_{t_{N}(y)}^{t}f_{N}(u(s,y),y)ds

As
w^{+}_{N}(y)
,
t_{N}(y)
and
f_{N}(x,y)
are bounded together with their derivatives we see that, for any
k=0,\ldots,n
there exists a constant
M_{k}>0
, independent of
y
, such that

\sup_{t_{N}(y)\leq t\leq T}\left|\frac{\partial^{k}u^{+}_{N}}{\partial y^{k}}(t,y)\right|\leq M_{k}.

We conclude that

\sup_{t\geq t_{N}(y)}|u^{+}_{N}(t,y)|\leq\max\{M_{0},\sup\{w_{+}(y)|y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}+1\}

which is independent of
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Now we prove that
u^{+}_{N,y}(t,y)
is bounded uniformly with respect to
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Let
\bar{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
be fixed,
C^{0}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
be the space of bounded continuous functions on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
U_{0}(t,y)
be the fundamental matrix of

\dot{x}=f_{N,x}(w_{+}(y),y)x
.

Arguing as in the proof of the parametric stable Theorem (see
[
19
, p. 18]
), there exists
\tilde{\rho}>0
,
\mu>0
and
0<\Delta\ll 1
such that the map

\begin{array}[]{l}(\xi,y,w(t))\mapsto U_{0}(t,\bar{y})P_{+}^{0}(\bar{y})\xi\\
\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}U_{0}(t,\bar{y})P_{+}^{0}(\bar{y})U_{0}(s,\bar{y})^{-1}[f_{N}(w^{+}(y)+w(s),y)-f_{N,x}(w^{+}(\bar{y}),\bar{y})w(s)]ds\\
\displaystyle-\int_{t}^{\infty}U_{0}(t,\bar{y})(\mathbb{I}-P_{+}^{0}(\bar{y}))U_{0}(s,\bar{y})^{-1}[f_{N}(w^{+}(y)+w(s),y)-f_{N,x}(w^{+}(\bar{y}),\bar{y})w(s)]ds\end{array}

where
\xi\in{\cal R}P^{0}_{+}(\bar{y})
,
|\xi|<\tilde{\rho}
,
|y-\bar{y}|<\mu
is a
C^{r}
-contraction on the space of bounded continuous functions
w(t)\in C^{0}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
with
\sup_{t\geq 0}|w(t)|\leq\Delta
, uniform with respect to
(\xi,y)
. Let
z(t,\xi,y)
be the fixed point of such a contraction. Then the map
(\xi,y)\mapsto z(t,\xi,y)
,
|\xi|\leq\tilde{\rho}
,
|y-\bar{y}|<\mu
, is a
C^{r}
-map into the space of bounded continuous functions on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. In particular all derivatives of
z(t,\xi,y)
are bounded functions (but the bounds may depend on
(\xi,y)
). Now,
x(t)=z(t,\xi,y)+w^{+}(y)
satisfies

\begin{array}[]{l}\dot{x}=f_{N}(x,y)\\
P^{0}_{+}(\bar{y})[x(0)-w^{+}(y)]=\xi\\
\sup_{t\geq 0}|x(t)-w^{+}(y)|\leq\Delta.\end{array}

(9.1)

To proceed with the proof we modify
\rho
, and hence
T
, so that
0<K\rho\leq\tilde{\rho}
and the previous conditions still hold. As

u^{+}_{N}(t+T,y)
satisfies (
9.1
) with
\xi=P^{0}_{+}(\bar{y})[u^{+}_{N}(T,y)-w^{+}(y)]
and

|P^{0}_{+}(\bar{y})[u^{+}_{N}(T,y)-w^{+}(y)]|\leq K\rho\leq\tilde{\rho}

we conclude, by uniqueness of the fixed point, that

u^{+}_{N}(t+T,y)=w^{+}(y)+z(t,P^{0}_{+}(\bar{y})[u^{+}_{N}(T,y)-w^{+}(y)],y).

As
\bar{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is arbitrary we see that
u^{+}_{N,y}(t+T,y)
is bounded on
t\geq 0
. So

\sup_{t\geq t_{N}(y)}|u^{+}_{N,y}(t,y)|<\infty

however the bound may depend on
y
. To prove that it can be taken independent of
y
we observe that, for
t\geq 0
,
u^{+}_{N}(t+T_{+},y)
is a bounded solution of

\begin{array}[]{l}\dot{x}=f_{N,x}(u^{+}_{N}(t+T_{+},y),y)x+f_{N,y}(u^{+}_{N}(t+T_{+},y),y)\\
P_{+}(y)x(0)=P_{+}(y)u^{+}_{N,y}(T_{+},y)\end{array}

and
|u^{+}_{N,y}(T_{+},y)|\leq M_{1}
since
0\leq T_{+}\leq T
. Now, (
4.1
) has an exponential dichotomy on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
with projections
P_{+}(y)
and its fundamental matrix on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
is
X(t,y):=U_{N}^{+}(t+T_{+},y)U_{N}^{+}(T_{+},y)^{-1}
. Hence,

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle u^{+}_{N,y}(t+T_{+},y)=X(t,y)P_{+}(y)u^{+}_{N,y}(T_{+},y)\\
\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}X(t,y)P_{+}(y)X(s,y)^{-1}f_{N,y}(u^{+}_{N,y}(s+T_{+},y),y)ds\\
\displaystyle-\int_{t}^{\infty}X(t,y)\mathbb{I}-P_{+}(y))X(s,y)^{-1}f_{N,y}(u^{+}_{N,y}(s+T_{+},y),y)ds\end{array}

and then, setting
\bar{F}=\sup_{(x,y)}|f_{N,y}(x,y)|
:

|u^{+}_{N,y}(t+T_{+},y)|\leq Ke^{-\delta t}M_{1}+\int_{0}^{\infty}K\bar{F}e^{-\delta|t-s|}ds\leq K(M_{1}+2\bar{F}\delta^{-1})

which is independent of
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Since
T\geq T_{+}
and
K\geq 1
, we obtain

\sup_{t\geq t_{N}(y)}|u^{+}_{N,y}(t,y)|\leq K(M_{1}+2\bar{F}\delta^{-1}).

More arguments of similar nature prove the Lemma as far as the higher order derivatives of
u^{+}_{N}(t,y)
are concerned. This completes the proof of Lemma
4.2
.

Remark 9.1
.

We can also give a better estimate of the difference
w(t,y):=u^{+}_{N}(t+T,y)-w_{+}(y)
. Indeed here we prove that for
\rho>0
sufficiently small,
there exists
\beta>0
such that
w(t,y)e^{\beta t}
and its derivatives with respect to
y
are bounded on
t\geq 0
, uniformly with respect to
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

First, as
\dot{w}(t,y)=f_{N}(w(t,y)+w_{+}(y),y)
and
f_{N}(w_{+}(y),y)=0
, we see that
w(t,y)
is a bounded solution of

\dot{x}=f_{N,x}(w_{+}(y),y)x+b(x,y)

where
b(x,y)=f_{N}(x+w_{+}(y),y)-f_{N}(w_{+}(y),y)-f_{N,x}(w_{+}(y),y)x
. Note that

|b(x,y)|\leq\frac{1}{2}L|x|^{2}

where
L
is a Lipschitz constant for
f_{N,x}(x,y)
. Then

\begin{array}[]{l}w(t,y)=U_{0}(t,y)P^{0}_{+}(y)w(0,y)+\\
\displaystyle\quad\int_{0}^{t}U_{0}(t,y)P^{0}_{+}(y)U_{0}(s,y)^{-1}b(w(s,y),y)ds-\\
\displaystyle\quad\int_{t}^{\infty}U_{0}(t,y)(\mathbb{I}-P^{0}_{+}(y))U_{0}(s,y)^{-1}b(w(s,y),y)ds\end{array}

where
U_{0}(t,y)
is the fundamental matrix of
\dot{x}=f_{N,x}(w_{+}(y),y)x
. Then, using
\sup_{t\geq 0}|w(t,y)|<\rho
, with
\rho
as in Lemma
4.2
, we get:

|w(t,y)|\leq Ke^{-\delta_{0}t}\rho+\frac{1}{2}KL\rho\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\delta_{0}|t-s|}|w(s,y)|ds.

Let
\rho>0
be such that it also satisfies
KL\rho<\delta_{0}
and take

\begin{array}[]{l}\theta=KL\rho\delta_{0}^{-1}\\
\beta=\delta_{0}(1-\theta)^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{array}

According to
[
11
, Lemma 1, p.28]
we get
|w(t,y)|\leq 2\delta_{0}L^{-1}(1-(1-\theta)^{1/2})e^{-\beta t}
that is

\sup_{t\geq 0}|u^{+}_{N}(t+T,y)-w_{+}(y)|e^{\beta t}\leq 2\delta_{0}L^{-1}(1-(1-\theta)^{1/2})\leq\delta_{0}\theta L^{-1}=K\rho

and the bound is independent of
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Now we consider
w_{y}(t,y)=u^{+}_{N,y}(t+T,y)-w^{\prime}_{+}(y)
.

Differentiating
\dot{u}^{+}_{N}(t+T,y)=f_{N}(u^{+}_{N}(t+T,y),y)
with respect to
y
and using
f_{N}(w_{+}(y),y)=0
we see that

\begin{array}[]{l}\dot{w}_{y}(t,y)\\
\qquad=f_{N,x}(u_{N}^{+}(t+T,y),y)[w_{y}(t,y)+w^{\prime}_{+}(y)]+f_{N,y}(u_{N}^{+}(t+T,y),y)\\
\qquad=f_{N,x}(w_{+}(y),y)w_{y}(t,y)\\
\qquad\qquad+[f_{N,x}(u_{N}^{+}(t+T,y),y)-f_{N,x}(w_{+}(y),y)]w_{y}(t,y)\\
\qquad\qquad+[f_{N,x}(u_{N}^{+}(t+T,y),y)-f_{N,x}(w_{+}(y),y)]w^{\prime}_{+}(y)\\
\qquad\qquad+f_{N,y}(u_{N}^{+}(t+T,y),y)-f_{N,y}(w_{+}(y),y)\end{array}

that is
w_{y}(t,y)
is a solution of:

\dot{x}=f_{N,x}(w_{+}(y),y)x+b(t,x,y)

where

\begin{array}[]{l}b(t,x,y):=[f_{N,x}(u_{N}^{+}(t+T,y),y)-f_{N,x}(w_{+}(y),y)]x\\
\qquad+[f_{N,x}(u_{N}^{+}(t+T,y),y)-f_{N,x}(w_{+}(y),y)]w^{\prime}_{+}(y)\\
\qquad+f_{N,y}(u_{N}^{+}(t+T,y),y)-f_{N,y}(w^{+}(y),y)\end{array}

bounded on
t\geq 0
. Let
L
be a Lipschitz constant for
f_{N,x}(x,y)
and
f_{N,y}(x,y)
. Now we assume that
3KL\rho<\delta_{0}
.
We have

|b(t,x,y)|\leq L\rho|x|+KL\rho(|w^{\prime}_{+}(y)|+1)e^{-\beta t}

and

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle w_{y}(t,y)=U_{0}(t,y)P^{0}_{+}(y)w_{y}(0,y)+\int_{0}^{t}U_{0}(t,y)P^{0}_{+}(y)U_{0}(s,y)^{-1}b(s,w_{y}(s,y),y)ds\\
\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{t}^{\infty}U_{0}(t,y)(\mathbb{I}-P^{0}_{+}(y))U_{0}(s,y)^{-1}b(s,w_{y}(s,y),y)ds.\end{array}

Then

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle|w_{y}(t,y)|\leq Ke^{-\delta_{0}t}|w_{y}(0,y)|+\\
\qquad\qquad\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}KL\rho e^{-\delta_{0}(t-s)}(|w_{y}(s,y)|+K(|w^{\prime}_{+}(y)|+1)e^{-\beta s})ds\\
\displaystyle\leq Ke^{-\delta_{0}t}|w_{y}(0,y)|+2K^{2}L\rho(|w^{\prime}_{+}(y)|+1)\delta(\delta^{2}-\beta^{2})e^{-\beta t}\\
\qquad\qquad\displaystyle+KL\rho\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\delta_{0}|t-s|)}|w_{y}(s,y)|ds\\
\leq\displaystyle Ce^{-\beta t}+KL\rho\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\beta|t-s|}|w_{y}(s,\bar{y})|ds\end{array}

where

C=K|w_{y}(0,y)|+2\delta K^{2}L\rho(\delta^{2}-\beta^{2})^{-1}(|w_{+}^{\prime}(y)|+1).

We claim that
2KL\rho\beta^{-1}<1
. First, as
1-x<(1-x)^{\frac{1}{2}}
, for
0<x<1
, we have

\delta_{0}-KL\rho<\beta.

Then, since
\frac{x}{1-x}
is increasing on
0\leq x\leq 1
and
KL\rho\delta^{-1}<\frac{1}{3}
:

\begin{array}[]{l}2KL\rho\beta^{-1}<\frac{2KL\rho}{\delta_{0}-KL\rho}=\frac{2KL\rho\delta_{0}^{-1}}{1-KL\rho\delta_{0}^{-1}}\leq 2\frac{\frac{1}{3}}{1-\frac{1}{3}}=1.\end{array}

Then, applying again
[
11
, Lemma 1, p.28]
we get

|w_{y}(t,y)|\leq\tilde{\mu}Ce^{-\tilde{\beta}t}

for
t\geq 0
, where

\begin{array}[]{l}\tilde{\mu}=\tilde{\theta}^{-1}[1-(1-2\tilde{\theta})^{\frac{1}{2}}]\\
\tilde{\theta}=KL\rho\beta^{-1}\\
\tilde{\beta}=\beta(1-2\tilde{\theta})^{-1}.\end{array}

Hence
|w_{y}(t,y)|e^{\tilde{\beta}t}
is bounded on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, uniformly with respect to
y
since

w(0,y)=u_{N}(T,y)-w_{+}(y)=\int_{t_{N}(y)}^{T}f_{N}(u(s,y),y)ds

and
w_{+}(y)
are bounded together with their derivatives, uniformly with respect to
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. The proof for the higher order derivatives follows the same line.

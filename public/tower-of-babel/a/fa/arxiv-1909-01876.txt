Global existence of the solution to Einstein-Yang-Mills-Higgs equations with small initial datum
Zonglin Jia Boling Guo

Abstract
. The problem involved in this paper is the global existence of the solution to the 𝔰 ​ 𝔲 ​ ( 2 ) \mathfrak{su}(2) -Einstein-Yang-Mills-Higgs(EYMH) equation. The approach we employ stems from H. Lindblad and I. Rodnianski and is dependent of wave coordinates and Lorentzian gauge conditions. Our main conclusion is that the EYMH system admits global existence provided the initial datum are sufficiently small. To the best of our knowledge, there is no similar result in the area of EYMH equations. Key words: Einstein-Yang-Mills-Higgs equation, small initial datum, global existence



1.
Introduction

Recently many mathematicians are concerned about the EYMH equation. They, following the idea of D. Christodoulou (see e.g.
[
2
]

This paper is inspired by the work in
[
12

[

[

\displaystyle T_{\alpha\beta}

In the sequel, we would like to review some previous results. In

[

For another Einstein-matter system there is a semi-global result for the Einstein-Maxwell-Yang-Mills equations for small data due to Friedrich
[
9

. His results are based on analysis of the “conformal structure” of the Einstein field equations, i.e. on a study of the “conformal Einstein equations” which must be satisfied by the “nonphysical” metric which is obtained from the “physical” metric by a conformal rescaling. The “semi-global” is in the sense that all its “physical” null geodesies are past complete. In
[
6
]
, M. Dafermos studied the stability and instability of the Cauchy horizon for the spherically symmetric Einstein-Maxwell-scalar field equations and resolved the issue of uniqueness in the context of a special, spherically symmetric initial value problem for a system of gravity coupled with matter, whose relation to the problem of gravitational collapse is well established in the physics literature. His result is related to the strong cosmic censorship conjecture of Roger Penrose. In
[

. This fact is surprising, since in general for nonlinear equations, existence theorems are usually only local. However, for these equations they got a global existence for all solutions defined in a neighborhood of infinity.

In the case of non-relativistic, there are also research results. In

Now let us briefly introduce the method we use. Firstly, in the process of getting local well-posedness, we applied wave coordinates and Lorentzian gauges to transform the EYMH equations into a hyperbolic system called the reduced EYMH systems. In order to show that the solution to the reduced EYMH systems also solves the original EYMH equations, we have to require that the initial datum sets satisfy EYMH constraints. For the details of the above concepts readers may refer to Section
2
of this paper. Secondly, as soon as we get a local solution, it is natural to consider the maximal existence time
T
and assume it to be finite. In the next, we define
T^{*}
to be

(1.6)

\displaystyle T^{*}:=\sup\{T_{0}:\exists C=C(T),\,s.t.\,\,\,\,\forall t\in[0,T_{0}),\,\,\,\,\mathcal{E}_{N}(t)\leqslant 2C\varepsilon\}

and suppose that
T^{*}<T
. We will show that if
\varepsilon>0
is small enough, then the inequality in (
1.6
) implies the same inequality with
2C
replaced by
C
for all
t<T^{*}
. This contradicts the maximality of
T^{*}
and we will obtain that the inequality holds for all
t<T
. Moreover, since the energy
\mathcal{E}_{N}(t)
is now finite at

(Note that
\mathcal{E}_{N}(t)
is continuous with respect to
t
), we can extend the solution beyond

to contradict maximality of
T
and show that
T=\infty
. Hence, our final task is to get energy estimates. Noting the definition of
\mathcal{E}_{N}
, we compute
\overset{\sim}{\Box}_{g}Z^{I}h

\overset{\sim}{\Box}_{g}Z^{I}A

\overset{\sim}{\Box}_{g}Z^{I}\Phi
where
\overset{\sim}{\Box}_{g}:=g^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}

[
12

and Gronwall inequality leads to the needed results.

2.1.
Equivalence of the Einstein equation

It is easy to check that the Einstein equation is equivalent to a simpler one

(2.1)

Ric_{\alpha\beta}=\langle\tilde{F}_{\alpha}^{\lambda},\tilde{F}_{\beta\lambda}\rangle-\frac{1}{4}g_{\alpha\beta}\langle\tilde{F}^{\lambda\mu},\tilde{F}_{\lambda\mu}\rangle+(\hat{\nabla}_{\alpha}\Phi)^{{\dagger}}\hat{\nabla}_{\beta}\Phi+(\hat{\nabla}_{\beta}\Phi)^{{\dagger}}\hat{\nabla}_{\alpha}\Phi+g_{\alpha\beta}V(|\Phi|^{2}).

Thanks to the above transformation, we can rewrite a complicated equation as a hyperbolic system in wave coordinates later.

2.2.
Wave coordinates

We say that a metric
g
of
\mathbb{R}^{4}
satisfies the wave coordinates condition if

\displaystyle\hat{F}^{\lambda}:=g^{\alpha\beta}\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\alpha\beta}=0,

where
\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\alpha\beta}
is the connection coefficient of

.

2.3.
Uniform equivalence of Riemannian metric

Given two Riemannian metric
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
on a smooth manifold
M
, we say they are uniformly equivalent to each other if there exist two constants
0<A<B<\infty
such that for all
X\in TM
, the following holds true

\displaystyle A\cdot e_{1}(X,X)\leqslant e_{2}(X,X)\leqslant B\cdot e_{1}(X,X).

2.4.
Sliced spacetime

A Lorentzian spacetime
(V_{T}:=\mathbb{R}^{3}\times[0,T),g)
is called sliced for some
t\in(0,\infty]
if there exists a time-dependent vector
\beta(x,t):=\beta^{i}(x,t)\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}(x)
, which is called the shift, tangent to the space slice
M_{t}:=\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\{t\}
such that

(1)
\vec{n}(\cdot,t):=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\cdot,t)-\beta(\cdot,t)
is a normal vector to
M_{t}
for all
t\in[0,T)
. That is to say, given
X_{t}\in T(M_{t})
(
T(M_{t})
is the tangent bundle of
M_{t}
), we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle g(\vec{n}(\cdot,t),X_{t})=0.

It is easy to check that (
2.2
) is equivalent to
g_{0i}=g_{ij}\beta^{j}
;

(2)
\vec{n}
is timelike, namely,

(2.3)

\displaystyle g(\vec{n},\vec{n})<0.

Combining (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) we arrive at
g_{ij}\beta^{i}\beta^{j}>g_{00}
.

Using the above inequality we define a positive function
N
called the lapse which is given by
N:=\sqrt{g_{ij}\beta^{i}\beta^{j}-g_{00}}
. Then we can write

as

(2.4)

\displaystyle g=-N^{2}dt\otimes dt+g_{ij}(dx^{i}+\beta^{i}dt)\otimes(dx^{j}+\beta^{j}dt).

Because
g
is Lorentzian,
(g_{ij})
is positive definite. In order to represent
(g^{\alpha\beta})
via
(N,\beta,g_{ij})
, we denote the inverse of
(g_{ij})
by
(g_{*}^{ij})
. It is easy to check

\displaystyle g^{00}=-N^{-2},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,g^{0i}=N^{-2}\beta^{i}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,g^{ij}=g_{*}^{ij}-N^{-2}\beta^{i}\beta^{j}.

Thanks to the above discussion, now we can give the following lemma without proof.

Lemma 2.1
.

A spacetime
(V_{T},g)
is sliced and Lorentzian if and only if
g_{t}:=i_{t}^{*}g
, which is induced by the embedding
i_{t}:\mathbb{R}^{3}\longrightarrow V_{T}
,
x\mapsto(x,t)
, is positive definite and

\displaystyle g_{0i}\cdot g_{*}^{ij}\cdot g_{0j}>g_{00}.

Remark 2.2
.

Lemma
2.1
tells us that determining a sliced Lorentzian metric

on

is equivalent to determining the following quantities:

(1) a Riemannian metric

on
M_{t}
;

(2) a positive function

on
M_{t}
;

(3) a tangent vector field
\beta
to
M_{t}
.

Remark 2.3
.

In case

is positive definite, the inverse of

which is denoted by

. Hence, throughout this article we always use the symbol

Remark 2.4
.

Let
{}^{t}\Gamma^{k}_{ij}
be the coefficient of the Levi-Civita connection
{}^{t}\nabla

(M_{t},g_{t})
. Now we are going to give the specific expression of

\displaystyle\Gamma^{0}_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}N^{-2}\partial_{t}g_{ij}-\frac{1}{2}N^{-2}(g_{kj}\cdot{}^{t}\nabla_{i}\beta^{k}+g_{ki}\cdot{}^{t}\nabla_{j}\beta^{k});

\displaystyle\Gamma^{0}_{i0}=N^{-1}\partial_{i}N-\frac{1}{4}N^{-2}\partial_{i}\{g(\beta,\beta)\}-\frac{1}{2}N^{-2}\beta^{l}\cdot{}^{t}\nabla_{l}\beta^{p}g_{pi}+\frac{1}{2}N^{-2}\beta^{l}\partial_{t}g_{il};

where

3.
Local well-posedness

Recall that
A
is the Yang-Mills potential. For simplicity, we decompose

as

where

A^{\mbox{time}}(x,t):=A_{0}(x,t)dt\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,A^{\mbox{space}}(x,t):=A_{i}(x,t)dx^{i}.

From now on, we always assume that
(\mathbb{R}^{4},g)
satisfies the wave coordinates condition.

3.1.
The Yang-Mills equations in wave coordinates and Lorentzian gauges

The following computation is obvious

(3.1)

\displaystyle\nabla^{\alpha}\tilde{F}_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{\alpha\lambda}(\partial_{\lambda}\partial_{\alpha}A_{\beta}-\partial_{\lambda}\partial_{\beta}A_{\alpha}+[\partial_{\lambda}A_{\alpha},A_{\beta}]+[A_{\alpha},\partial_{\lambda}A_{\beta}])

\displaystyle-g^{\alpha\lambda}\Gamma^{\theta}_{\lambda\beta}\cdot(\partial_{\alpha}A_{\theta}-\partial_{\theta}A_{\alpha}),

where
\Gamma
is the Christoffel symbols of
g
and we have used the wave coordinates condition to deduce (
3.1
).

By elementary manipulations (
3.1
) becomes

(3.2)

\displaystyle\nabla^{\alpha}\tilde{F}_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{\alpha\lambda}\partial_{\lambda}\partial_{\alpha}A_{\beta}-\partial_{\beta}(g^{\alpha\lambda}\partial_{\lambda}A_{\alpha})+\partial_{\beta}g^{\alpha\lambda}\cdot\partial_{\lambda}A_{\alpha}+g^{\alpha\lambda}[\partial_{\lambda}A_{\alpha},A_{\beta}]+g^{\alpha\lambda}[A_{\alpha},\partial_{\lambda}A_{\beta}]

\displaystyle-g^{\alpha\lambda}\Gamma^{\theta}_{\lambda\beta}\cdot(\partial_{\alpha}A_{\theta}-\partial_{\theta}A_{\alpha}).

We can transform (
3.2
) into

\displaystyle\nabla^{\alpha}\tilde{F}_{\alpha\beta}=g^{\alpha\lambda}\partial_{\lambda}\partial_{\alpha}A_{\beta}+\partial_{\beta}g^{\alpha\lambda}\cdot\partial_{\lambda}A_{\alpha}+g^{\alpha\lambda}[A_{\alpha},\partial_{\lambda}A_{\beta}]-g^{\alpha\lambda}\Gamma^{\theta}_{\lambda\beta}\cdot(\partial_{\alpha}A_{\theta}-\partial_{\theta}A_{\alpha}),

if we assume that
g^{\alpha\lambda}\partial_{\lambda}A_{\alpha}=0
, which is equivalent to
\mbox{div}_{g}A\equiv g^{\alpha\lambda}\nabla_{\lambda}A_{\alpha}=0
called the Lorentz gauge condition(the equivalence follows from the wave gauge condition).

It is easy to see

\displaystyle\partial_{\beta}g^{\alpha\lambda}=-g^{\alpha\theta}\cdot\partial_{\beta}g_{\theta\mu}\cdot g^{\mu\lambda}.

Therefore, the Yang-Mills equation in wave coordinates and Lorentzian gauges can be written as

(3.3)

\displaystyle g^{\alpha\lambda}\partial_{\lambda}\partial_{\alpha}A_{\beta}+f_{\beta}(g,A,\partial g,\partial A,\Phi,\partial\Phi)=0,

where

\displaystyle f_{\beta}(g,A,\partial g,\partial A,\Phi,\partial\Phi):=-g^{\alpha\theta}\cdot\partial_{\beta}g_{\theta\mu}\cdot g^{\mu\lambda}\cdot\partial_{\lambda}A_{\alpha}+g^{\alpha\lambda}[A_{\alpha},\partial_{\lambda}A_{\beta}]+g^{\alpha\lambda}[A_{\lambda},\partial_{\alpha}A_{\beta}]

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}g^{\alpha\lambda}g^{\theta\mu}\cdot(\partial_{\lambda}g_{\mu\beta}+\partial_{\beta}g_{\lambda\mu}-\partial_{\mu}g_{\lambda\beta})\cdot(\partial_{\alpha}A_{\theta}-\partial_{\theta}A_{\alpha})-g^{\alpha\lambda}[A_{\lambda},\partial_{\beta}A_{\alpha}]+g^{\alpha\lambda}[A_{\lambda},[A_{\alpha},A_{\beta}]]

\displaystyle-\Phi^{{\dagger}}S(\partial_{\beta}\Phi-\sqrt{-1}A^{I}_{\beta}\sigma_{I}\Phi/2)+(\partial_{\beta}\Phi-\sqrt{-1}A^{I}_{\beta}\sigma_{I}\Phi/2)^{{\dagger}}S\Phi.

3.3.

It is easy to check that Higgs equations in wave coordinates and Lorentzian gauges are equivalent to

\displaystyle g^{\lambda\mu}\partial_{\lambda}\partial_{\mu}\Phi+U(\Phi,\partial\Phi,A)=0,

\displaystyle U(\Phi,\partial\Phi,A):

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}g^{\lambda\mu}A_{\mu}^{I_{1}}\sigma_{I_{1}}(\partial_{\lambda}\Phi)-\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}g^{\lambda\mu}A_{\lambda}^{I_{2}}\sigma_{I_{2}}(\partial_{\mu}\Phi)

and the equivalence follows from wave coordinates conditions and Lorentzian gauges.

3.4.

Firstly, we want to construct a new vector bundle

\displaystyle BU:=(T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{4}\otimes T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{4})\times(T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{4}\otimes\mathfrak{su}(2))\times\mathbb{C}^{2}

\displaystyle((g_{1},g_{2})):=\sum\limits_{\alpha,\theta}(g_{1})_{\alpha\theta}\cdot(g_{2})_{\alpha\theta},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\langle\langle A_{1},A_{2}\rangle\rangle:=\sum\limits_{\alpha}\langle(A_{1})_{\alpha},(A_{2})_{\alpha}\rangle.

\displaystyle\textbf{\mbox{D}}(g,A,\Phi):=(\partial g,\partial A,\partial\Phi)\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{for any $(g,A,\Phi)\in BU$.}

3.5.

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{|J_{1}|+|I_{1}|+\cdots+|I_{2s}|=|I|}|Z^{J_{1}}h|\cdot|V^{(s)}(|\Phi|^{2})|\cdot\prod\limits_{i=1}^{2s}|Z^{I_{i}}\Phi|\leqslant C^{2\tilde{\gamma}}\varepsilon^{2\tilde{\gamma}}(1+|q|)^{-\tilde{\gamma}}\sum\limits_{|K|\leqslant|I|}|Z^{K}h|,

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{|J_{1}|+|J_{2}|\leqslant|I|}|\partial Z^{J_{1}}\Phi|\cdot|\partial Z^{J_{2}}\Phi|\leqslant C\varepsilon(1+|q|)^{-3/2}\sum\limits_{|K|\leqslant|I|}|\partial Z^{K}\Phi|,

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{|J_{1}|+|J_{2}|+|J_{3}|\leqslant|I|}|Z^{J_{1}}A|\cdot|Z^{J_{2}}\Phi|\cdot|\partial Z^{J_{3}}\Phi|\leqslant C^{2}\varepsilon^{2}(1+|q|)^{-1}\sum\limits_{|K|\leqslant|I|}|\partial Z^{K}\Phi|,

and

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{|J_{1}|+|J_{2}|+|J_{3}|+|J_{4}|=|I|}|Z^{J_{1}}A|\cdot|Z^{J_{2}}A|\cdot|Z^{J_{3}}\Phi|\cdot|Z^{J_{4}}\Phi|\leqslant C^{3}\varepsilon^{3}(1+|q|)^{-3/2}\sum\limits_{|K|\leqslant|I|}|Z^{K}\Phi|.

Combining the above estimates gives (
6.15
).

\hfill\Box

Lemma 6.3
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
6.1
, one can get

(6.20)

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}\int|Z^{I}F|^{2}wdxd\tau\lesssim\varepsilon\sum\limits_{|K|\leqslant|I|}\int_{0}^{t}\int(|\partial Z^{K}h|^{2}+|\partial Z^{K}A|^{2}+|\partial Z^{K}\Phi|^{2})wdxd\tau

\displaystyle+\varepsilon^{4\tilde{\gamma}-1}+\sum\limits_{|K|\leqslant|I|}\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\int|\bar{\partial}Z^{K}h|^{2}w^{\prime}dxd\tau,

(6.21)

\varepsilon^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}\int|Z^{I}J|^{2}wdxd\tau\lesssim\sum\limits_{|K|\leqslant|I|}\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\int(|\partial Z^{K}h|^{2}+|\partial Z^{K}A|^{2}+|\partial Z^{K}\Phi|^{2})wdxd\tau

and

(6.22)

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}\int|Z^{I}W|^{2}wdxd\tau\lesssim\varepsilon\cdot\sum\limits_{|K|\leqslant|I|}\int_{0}^{t}\int|\partial Z^{K}\Phi|^{2}wdxd\tau.

Proof.
Throughout the process we have to apply Corollary 13.3 of
[
12
]
(to transform
Z^{K}h
,
Z^{K}A
and
Z^{K}\Phi
into
\partial Z^{K}h
,
\partial Z^{K}A
and
\partial Z^{K}\Phi
respectively), the inequality
1\leqslant 1+\tau\leqslant 1+T
(Note that the constant
C
relies on the maximal existence time
T
) and the assumption
\tilde{\gamma}>3/2+\gamma
(to ensure that some integral is finite). Indeed, if the integral is denoted by
In
, we can give its specific expression

\displaystyle In:=\varepsilon^{4\tilde{\gamma}-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(1+|q|)^{-2\tilde{\gamma}}w\,dx^{1}dx^{2}dx^{3}.

Since
w\lesssim(1+|q|)^{1+2\gamma}
, under polar coordinates system it is easy to get

\displaystyle In\lesssim\varepsilon^{4\tilde{\gamma}-1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}d\mathbb{S}^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}(1+|q|)^{1+2\gamma-2\tilde{\gamma}}r^{2}\,dr.

Hence, the assumption
\tilde{\gamma}>3/2+\gamma
implies
In<\infty
.

The other part of the proof is similar to that of Lemma 11.3 of
[
12
]
. So we omit it.

\hfill\Box

Lemma 6.4
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
6.1
, we have

(6.23)

\varepsilon^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}\int|D^{I}|^{2}wdxd\tau\lesssim\varepsilon\sum\limits_{|K|\leqslant|I|}\int_{0}^{t}\int\left(|\partial Z^{K}h|^{2}w+|\bar{\partial}Z^{K}h|^{2}w^{\prime}\right)dxd\tau+\varepsilon^{3},

(6.24)

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}\int|W^{I}|^{2}wdxd\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\varepsilon\sum\limits_{|K|\leqslant|I|}\int_{0}^{t}\int\left(|\partial Z^{K}h|^{2}w+|\bar{\partial}Z^{K}h|^{2}w^{\prime}\right)dxd\tau

\displaystyle+\varepsilon^{3}+\varepsilon\sum\limits_{|K|\leqslant|I|}\int_{0}^{t}\int|\partial Z^{K}A|^{2}w\,dxd\tau

and

(6.25)

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}\int|L^{I}|^{2}wdxd\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\varepsilon\sum\limits_{|K|\leqslant|I|}\int_{0}^{t}\int\left(|\partial Z^{K}h|^{2}w+|\bar{\partial}Z^{K}h|^{2}w^{\prime}\right)dxd\tau

\displaystyle+\varepsilon^{3}+\varepsilon\sum\limits_{|K|\leqslant|I|}\int_{0}^{t}\int|\partial Z^{K}\Phi|^{2}w\,dxd\tau

Proof.
We only prove (
6.25
) since the other estimates follow from the same methods. According to Proposition 5.3 of
[
12
]
we arrive at

(6.26)

\displaystyle|\overset{\sim}{\Box}_{g}Z^{I}\Phi-\hat{Z}^{I}\overset{\sim}{\Box}_{g}\Phi|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{|K|\leqslant|I|}\sum\limits_{|J|+(|K|-1)_{+}\leqslant|I|}\left(\frac{|Z^{J}H|}{1+|q|}+\frac{|Z^{J}H|_{\mathcal{L}\mathcal{L}}}{1+|q|}\right)|\partial Z^{K}\Phi|

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{|K|\leqslant|I|}\left(\sum\limits_{|J|+(|K|-1)_{+}\leqslant|I|-1}\frac{|Z^{J}H|_{\mathcal{L}\mathcal{T}}}{1+|q|}+\sum\limits_{|J|+(|K|-1)_{+}\leqslant|I|-2}\frac{|Z^{J}H|}{1+|q|}\right)|\partial Z^{K}\Phi|.

Our goal is to obtain the estimate for the quantity

\sum\limits_{|I|\leqslant N}\int_{0}^{t}\int|\overset{\sim}{\Box}_{g}Z^{I}\Phi-\hat{Z}^{I}\overset{\sim}{\Box}_{g}\Phi|^{2}w\,dxd\tau.

Let us first deal with the terms in (
6.26
) with
|K|\leqslant N-4
. In this case we use the decay estimate (
6.1
). It is clear that now we only have to consider the expression

(6.27)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|J|\leqslant|I|\\
|J^{\prime}|\leqslant|I|-1\\
|K|\leqslant|I|-2\end{subarray}}\int_{0}^{t}\int\left\{\frac{|Z^{J}H|^{2}}{(1+|q|)^{2}}+\frac{|Z^{J}H|^{2}_{\mathcal{L}\mathcal{L}}+|Z^{J^{\prime}}H|^{2}_{\mathcal{L}\mathcal{T}}+|Z^{K}H|^{2}}{(1+|q|)^{2}}\right\}\varepsilon^{2}(1+|q|)^{-3}w\,dxd\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|J|\leqslant|I|\\
|J^{\prime}|\leqslant|I|-1\\
|K|\leqslant|I|-2\end{subarray}}\int_{0}^{t}\int\left\{|Z^{J}H|^{2}+|Z^{J}H|^{2}_{\mathcal{L}\mathcal{L}}+|Z^{J^{\prime}}H|^{2}_{\mathcal{L}\mathcal{T}}+|Z^{K}H|^{2}\right\}\varepsilon^{2}(1+|q|)^{-1}w\,dxd\tau.

From the proof of Lemma 11.5 in
[
12
]
it follows that (
6.27
) is bounded by

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{2}\sum\limits_{|K|\leqslant|I|}\int_{0}^{t}\int\left(\frac{|\partial Z^{K}h|^{2}}{1+\tau}w+|\bar{\partial}Z^{K}h|^{2}w^{\prime}\right)dxd\tau

\displaystyle+C\varepsilon^{2}\sum\limits_{|K|\leqslant|I|-1}\int_{0}^{t}\int\frac{|\partial Z^{K}h|^{2}}{(1+\tau)^{1-2C\varepsilon}}wdxd\tau+C\varepsilon^{4}

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{2}\sum\limits_{|K|\leqslant|I|}\int_{0}^{t}\int\left(|\partial Z^{K}h|^{2}w+|\bar{\partial}Z^{K}h|^{2}w^{\prime}\right)dxd\tau+C\varepsilon^{4},

where we let the parameter
M
in the expression
H_{0}^{\mu\nu}:=-\chi(r/t)\chi(r)M\delta^{\mu\nu}/r
equal to 0(the expression can be found at the beginning of the proof of Lemma 11.5 in
[
12
]
). For more details we refer to the last inequality on page 1460 of
[
12
]
.

Returning to (
6.26
) we now deal with the case
|K|\geqslant N-3
, which implies
|J|\leqslant 4
. By the same way of proving Lemma 11.5 of
[
12
]
we arrive at that the contribution of the terms with
|K|\geqslant N-3
to
|\overset{\sim}{\Box}_{g}Z^{I}\Phi-\hat{Z}^{I}\overset{\sim}{\Box}_{g}\Phi|
can be bounded by

\varepsilon\sum\limits_{|K|=|I|}\frac{|\partial Z^{K}\Phi|}{1+\tau}+\varepsilon\sum\limits_{|K|<|I|}\frac{|\partial Z^{K}\Phi|}{(1+\tau)^{1-C\varepsilon}},

which is equivalent to

\varepsilon\sum\limits_{|K|\leqslant|I|}|\partial Z^{K}\Phi|.

6.1

6.13
) and (
6.14

6.3

(6.28)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{t}}(|\partial Z^{I}h|^{2}+|\partial Z^{I}A|^{2}+|\partial Z^{I}\Phi|^{2})w+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Sigma_{\tau}}(|\bar{\partial}Z^{I}h|^{2}+|\bar{\partial}Z^{I}A|^{2}+|\bar{\partial}Z^{I}\Phi|^{2})w^{\prime}

\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{k}(t):=\sup\limits_{0\leqslant\tau\leqslant t}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}Z\in\mathfrak{L}\\

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{k}(t)+\mathcal{S}_{k}(t)

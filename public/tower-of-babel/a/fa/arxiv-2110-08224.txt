On the Fractional Dunkl Laplacian
Fethi Bouzeffour Wissem Jedidi

Abstract
In this paper, we present an approach to the fractional Dunkl Laplacian in a framework emerging from certain reflection symmetries in Euclidean spaces. Our main result is pointwise formulas, Bochner subordination, and an extension problem for the fractional Dunkl Laplacian as well.

In
Dunkl
, Dunkl introduced a family of first-order differential-difference operators related to some finite reflection groups in the Euclidian space. Recently, these operators have gained considerable interest in various fields of mathematics and also in physical applications. For more details about these operators see

\Delta=\partial^{2}/\partial x_{1}^{2}+\,\dots\,+\partial^{2}/\partial x_{d}^{2}
and they are fundamental tools for generalization of several classical known results and so, it is a convenient setting for developing fractional Dunkl-Laplacian operators. Recall that for a function

in the space of Schwartz functions
S(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2},\;0<\alpha<2
, is defined by means of the Fourier transform

(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}f=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(|\xi|^{\alpha}\mathcal{F}f(\xi)),\quad f\in S(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

and can be expressed by the pointwise formula

(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}f(x):=\frac{1}{\gamma_{d}(\alpha)}\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(0,\varepsilon)}\frac{f(x)-f(x-y)}{|y|^{d+\alpha}}\,dy,\quad\mbox{where}\;\;\gamma_{d}(\alpha)=\frac{\pi^{d/2}|\Gamma(-\frac{\alpha}{2})|}{2^{\alpha}\Gamma(\frac{d+\alpha}{2})},

(1)

and

. The operator
(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}
is connected to PDE’s through the Caffarelli–Silvestre extension theorem

which establishes the following: if
U=U(x,y)
is the solution to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l l}\displaystyle\Delta U(x,y)+\frac{\partial^{2}U(x,y)}{\partial^{2}y}+\frac{1-\alpha}{y}\frac{\partial U(x,y)}{\partial y}=0,\quad(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\,\infty),\\
\\
U(x,0)=u(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{array}\right.

(2)

then,

(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}u(x)=-\frac{2^{\alpha-1}\Gamma(\alpha/2)}{\Gamma(1-\alpha/2)}\lim_{y\rightarrow 0^{+}}y^{1-\alpha}\frac{\partial U}{\partial y}(x,y).

The fractional Dunkl-Laplacian is then a natural object to consider, since it is a
k
-deformation of the standard Laplacian. Moreover, it is the simplest example of a large class of differential-difference operators associated with root systems. For more, we the refer to Opdam’s lecture notes

for the trigonometric Dunkl theory, and to the books of Cherednik
Cherednik
and of Macdonald
Macdonald

In this work, we present several descriptions of the fractional Dunkl-Laplacian on the Eucldian space. Our principal tools is the spherical mean-value type operator and Pizzetti’s type formula related to the Dunkl operator. We are mainly interested in describing an analogue of pointwise formula (
1

2

In Section 3 we derive several pointwise formulas for the fractional Dunkl-Laplacian. In Section 4 we use the spherical mean-value type operator and Pezzitti’s formula to give a Bochner type representation. In the last Section 5, we give the fundamental solution and we study the fractional Dirichlet-to-Neumann map for Dunkl-Laplacian.

In order to introduce our setting, we first collect some facts about the Dunkl operators. General references are
Dunkl

11

Roesler2
;
Roesler3

\mathcal{R}

, define the reflection
\sigma_{v}

\sigma_{v}(x)=x-2\frac{\langle x,v\rangle}{|v|^{2}}v,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(3)

where
\langle\,.\,,\,.\,\rangle
is the standard Euclidean inner product and
|x|=\sqrt{\langle x,x\rangle}

\mathbb{R}^{d}

(the function

\displaystyle T_{j}f(x)

\displaystyle=\partial_{j}f(x)+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\,v\in\mathcal{R}}\!k(v)\;\langle v,e_{j}\rangle\;\frac{f(x)-f(\sigma_{v}(x))}{\langle v,\,x\rangle}

(4)

\displaystyle=\partial_{j}f(x)+\sum_{\,v\in\mathcal{R}_{+}}\!k(v)\;\langle v,e_{j}\rangle\;\frac{f(x)-f(\sigma_{v}(x))}{\langle v,\,x\rangle},\quad j=1,\,2,\dots,\,d.

Here,

\mathcal{R}
and

Note that the Dunkl operators

The latter is a positive homogeneous function of degree

, where
\gamma_{k}=\sum_{v\in\mathcal{R}_{+}}k(v)
. For fixed

, the Dunkl kernel

is the unique solution to the system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l l}T_{j}f=x_{j}f,\quad 1\leq j\leq d,\\
\\
f(0)=1.\end{array}\right.

The following integral formula was obtained by Rösler
Roesler2

\mathcal{E}_{k}(\xi,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\hskip-2.84526pte^{\,\langle\xi,y\rangle}\,d\mu_{x}(y),\quad x\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(5)

where
\,\mu_{x},\,x\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{d}\hskip 0.7113pt,\;
is a compactly supported probability measures. Specifically, it is supported in the convex hull

of the

-orbit
of

. For a function

\mathcal{F}_{k}f(\xi)=\widehat{f}(\xi)=\frac{1}{c_{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\!f(x)\,\mathcal{E}_{k}(-i\,\xi,x)w_{k}(x)dx,\quad c_{k}\,=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\!e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{2}}\,w_{k}(x)dx.

(6)

In a similar way to the Fourier transform (which is the particular case
k\equiv 0

and can be extended to an isometric automorphism of

\mathcal{F}_{k}(T_{j}f)(\xi)=i\;\xi_{j}\;\mathcal{F}_{k}f(\xi),\quad\xi\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{d},\;\;1\leq j\leq d.

(7)

As in the classical case, a generalized translation operator is defined in the Dunkl setting side on

(the Lebesgue space of square integrable functions with respect to

(8)

We also define the Dunkl convolution product for suitable functions

and

f\mathrel{{\mathop{\star}\limits}}g(x)=\frac{1}{c_{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tau^{-x}f(y)g(y)w_{k}(y)dy.

The Dunkl Laplacian associated with a reduced root system

\Delta_{k}:=\sum_{i=1}^{d}T_{i}^{2},\quad\mbox{\it where $T_{i},\,1\leq i\leq d$ are the Dunkl operators defined in \eqref{Dunkl}. }

In explicit form, we have

\Delta_{k}f(x)=\Delta f(x)+2\sum_{v\in\mathcal{R}_{+}}k(v)\delta_{v}f(x),\quad f\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where

is the usual Laplacian on

\delta_{v}f(x)=\frac{\langle\nabla f(x),v\rangle}{\langle v,x\rangle}-\frac{f(x)-f(\sigma_{v}(x))}{\langle v,x\rangle^{2}}.

Similarly to the fractional Laplacian on

Therefore, we can define in a natural way the action of

\mathcal{F}_{k}((-\Delta_{\kappa})^{\alpha/2}f)(\xi)=|\xi|^{\alpha}\mathcal{F}_{k}(f)(\xi),\quad\mbox{for all $f\in S(\mathbb{R}^{d}).$}

(9)

3
Fractional Dunkl Laplacian: Pointwise formulas

Although we have formally introduced the fractional Dunkl-Laplacian by the formula (
9
), such definition has a major disadvantage: it is not easy to understand a given function (or a distribution) by prescribing its Fourier Dunk transform. For this reason, we introduce a different pointwise definition of the fractional Dunkl-Laplacian.

Lemma 3.1

For every
u\in S(\mathbb{R}^{d}),
one has

|2u(x)-\tau^{x}u(y)-\tau^{x}u(-y)|\leq\frac{|y|^{2}}{c_{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\xi|^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{k}u(\xi)|\,w_{k}(\xi)\,d\xi.

Proof.
The integral representation for the Dunkl Kernel given by (
5
), leads to

|2-\mathcal{E}_{k}(-iy,\xi)-\mathcal{E}_{k}(-iy,\xi)|=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big(1-\cos(\langle y,\eta\rangle)\Big)d\mu^{k}_{\xi}(\eta)\leq|y|^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\eta|^{2}d\mu^{k}_{\xi}(\eta).

Since the support of the probability measure
d\mu_{\xi}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is contained in the convex hull
\mathcal{O}(\xi)
of the orbit of
\xi
under the action of the reflection group
G
, then

\displaystyle|2-\mathcal{E}_{k}(-iy,\xi)-\mathcal{E}_{k}(-iy,\xi)|\leq|y|^{2}|\xi|^{2}.

On the other hand, the integral representation in (
8
) leads to

\displaystyle 2u(x)-\tau^{x}u(y)

\displaystyle-\tau^{x}u(-y)=\frac{1}{c_{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal{F}_{k}u(\xi)\;\mathcal{E}_{k}(ix,\xi)\;(2-\mathcal{E}_{k}(iy,\xi)-\mathcal{E}_{k}(-iy,\xi)\;w_{k}(\xi)d\xi,\quad x,\,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Therefore,

|2u(x)-\tau^{x}u(y)-\tau^{x}u(-y)|\leq\frac{|y|^{2}}{c_{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\xi|^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{k}u(\xi)\,w_{k}(\xi)\,d\xi.

∎

Recall that the Dunkl heat kernel
\Gamma_{k}(t,x)
is given by
Roesler2

\Gamma_{k}(t,x):=\,\frac{1}{(2t)^{\gamma_{k}+d/2}\;c_{k}}\,e^{-|x|^{2}/4t},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\>t>0,

and has the following properties:

\mathcal{F}_{k}\big(\Gamma_{k}(t,\,.\,)\big)(x)=e^{-t|x|^{2}},\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Gamma_{k}(t,x)\,w_{k}(y)=1,\quad t>0

(10)

Definition 3.2

Let
\alpha\in(0,2).
The fractional Dunkl-Laplacian operator
(-\Delta_{k})^{\alpha/2}u
of
u\in{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, is the nonlocal operator in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
defined by

\displaystyle(-\Delta_{k})^{\alpha/2}u(x)

\displaystyle=\,\frac{1}{\gamma_{k,d}(\alpha)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{2u(x)-\tau^{x}u(y)-\tau^{-x}u(y)}{|y|^{\alpha+2\gamma_{k}+d}}w_{k}(y)\,dy.

(11)

where
\gamma_{k,d}(\alpha)
is a suitable normalization constant that is given implicitly in Proposition
17
.

Notice that for
u\in S(\mathbb{R}^{d}),
the integral in the right-hand side of (
11
) is convergent. Indeed, it suffices to write

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big|\frac{2u(x)-\tau^{x}u(y)-\tau^{x}u(-y)}{|y|^{\alpha+2\gamma_{k}+d}}\Big|w_{k}(y)dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(0,\,\varepsilon)}\Big|\frac{2u(x)-\tau^{x}u(y)-\tau^{x}u(-y)}{|y|^{\alpha+2\gamma_{k}+d}}\Big|w_{k}(y)dy

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{B(0,\,\varepsilon)}\Big|\frac{2u(x)-\tau^{x}u(y)-\tau^{x}u(-y)}{|y|^{\alpha+2\gamma_{k}+d}}\Big|w_{k}(y)dy.

From Lemma 3.1, we have

\int_{B(0,\,\varepsilon)}\Big|\frac{2u(x)-\tau^{x}u(y)-\tau^{x}u(-y)}{|y|^{\alpha+2\gamma_{k}+d}}\Big|w_{k}(y)dy\leq C\int_{B(0,\,\varepsilon)}\frac{w_{k}(y)}{|y|^{\alpha+2\gamma_{k}+d-2}}dy<\infty.

Since
w_{k}
is a homogeneous function of degree
2\gamma_{k},
and
\alpha\in(0,2),
the constant
C
in the above inequality is given by
C=c^{-1}_{k}\big\|\,|\,.\,|^{2}\mathcal{F}_{k}u\,\big\|_{1}.
On the other hand, keeping in mind that
u\in S(\mathbb{R}^{d}),
which implies in particular that
\tau^{x}u\in L^{\infty}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(0,\,\varepsilon)}\Big|\frac{2u(x)-\tau^{x}u(y)-\tau^{x}u(-y)}{|y|^{\alpha+2\gamma_{k}+d}}\Big|\;w_{k}(y)dy\leq 2\max(\|u\|_{\infty},\,\|\tau^{x}u\|_{\infty})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(0,\,\varepsilon)}\frac{w_{k}(y)}{|y|^{\alpha+2\gamma_{k}+d}}dy<\infty.

(12)

Therefore Definition 3.3 provides a well-defined function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Remark 3.3

The following alternative expression for
(-\Delta_{k})^{\alpha/2}
is quite useful in the computations: For every
u\in S(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, one has

(-\Delta_{k})^{\alpha/2}u(x):=\,\frac{2}{\gamma_{k,d}(\alpha)}\text{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{u(x)-\tau^{x}u(y)}{|y|^{\alpha+2\gamma_{k}+d}}\,w_{k}(y)dy,

(13)

where
\text{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v(y)dy=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(0,\varepsilon)}v(y)dy
(Cauchy’s principal value sense). Indeed,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{2u(x)-\tau^{x}u(y)-\tau^{x}u(-y)}{|y|^{\alpha+2\gamma_{k}+d}}w_{k}(y)dy

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(0,\,\varepsilon)}\frac{2u(x)-\tau^{x}u(y)-\tau^{x}u(-y)}{|y|^{\alpha+2\gamma_{k}+d}}w_{k}(y)dy

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(0,\,\varepsilon)}\frac{u(x)-\tau^{x}u(y)}{|y|^{\alpha+2\gamma_{k}+d}}w_{k}(y)dy+\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(0,\,\varepsilon)}\frac{u(x)-\tau^{x}u(-y)}{|y|^{\alpha+2\gamma_{k}+d}}w_{k}(y)dy

\displaystyle=2\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(0,\,\varepsilon)}\frac{u(x)-\tau^{x}u(y)}{|y|^{\alpha+2\gamma_{k}+d}}w_{k}(y)dy.

Note that it is necessary to take the principal value of the last integral since we
have eliminated the cancellation of the linear terms in the symmetric difference of
order two, and
|u(x)-\tau^{x}u(y)|
is only
O(|y|)
(see
(
ThangaveluXu
, Theorem 3.14)
). Thus, the smoothness of the function

no longer guarantees the local integrability.

4
Representation via spherical mean-value operator

In this section, we provide a useful expression of
(-\Delta_{k})^{\alpha/2}u
in terms of an integral involving the spherical mean-value operator associated to Dunkl operator. According to
Mejjaoli
, the spherical mean-value operator associated to Dunkl operator
\mathcal{M}_{r}^{k}u(x)
is defined by

\mathcal{M}_{r}^{k}u(x)=\frac{1}{\sigma_{k}(d)}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\tau^{x}u(r\omega)w_{k}(\omega)d\sigma(\omega),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,\,r\geq 0,

where
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
is the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\sigma
denotes the Lebesgue surface measure and

\sigma_{k}(d):=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}w_{k}(\omega)d\sigma(\omega)=\frac{c_{k}}{2^{\gamma_{k}+\frac{d}{2}-1}\Gamma(\gamma_{k}+\frac{d}{2})}.

From Mejjaoli-Trimeche
Mejjaoli
one can extract the following proposition, that gives an extended Pizzetti’s formula associated with the Dunkl operators.

Proposition 4.1

Let
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
a\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.
The following asymptotic expansion is valid

\frac{1}{\sigma_{k}(d)}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\tau^{a}f(\varepsilon\omega)\,w_{k}(\omega)d\sigma(\omega)\sim\Gamma(\gamma_{k}+\frac{d}{2})\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(\frac{\varepsilon}{2})^{2n}\frac{\Delta_{k}^{n}f(a)}{n!\,\Gamma(\gamma_{k}+\frac{d}{2}+n)},\quad\mbox{as}\;\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}.

(14)

Lemma 4.2

Let
u\in S(\mathbb{R}^{d})
one has

(-\Delta_{k})^{\alpha/2}u(x)=\frac{1}{\pi_{k,d}(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{u(x)-\mathcal{M}^{k}_{r}u(x)}{r^{1+\alpha}}\,dr,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad\mbox{\it where}\;\;\pi_{k,d}(\alpha):=\frac{\gamma_{k,d}(\alpha)}{2\sigma_{k}(d)}.

(15)

Proof.
From Proposition
4.1
(see also
(
Mejjaoli
, Theorem 4.17 )
, one has

\Delta_{k}u(x)=\frac{2(2\gamma_{k}+d)}{\sigma_{k}(\alpha)}\lim_{r\rightarrow 0}\frac{u(x)-\mathcal{M}_{r}^{k}u(x)}{r^{2}}.

Therefore, the integrand in the right-hand side of (
15
) behaves like

\frac{u(x)-\mathcal{M}_{r}^{k}u(x)}{r^{1+r}}=O(r^{1-\alpha}),\quad r\rightarrow 0.

Since
u\in S(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\alpha\in(0,2),
we conclude that the integral in the right-hand side of (
15
) is convergent. On the other hand, the use of the polar coordinates
x=r\omega,
allows us to rewrite (
13
), in the following forms

\displaystyle(-\Delta_{k})^{\alpha/2}u(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\gamma_{k,d}(\alpha)}\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\,\int_{\varepsilon}^{\infty}\frac{1}{r^{1+\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}(u(x)-\tau^{x}u(r\omega))w_{k}(\omega)d\sigma(\omega)\,dr

\displaystyle=\frac{2\sigma_{k}(d)}{\gamma_{k,d}(\alpha)}\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{\varepsilon}^{\infty}\frac{u(x)-\mathcal{M}_{r}^{k}u(x)}{r^{1+\alpha}}\,dr=\frac{1}{\pi_{k,d}(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{u(x)-\mathcal{M}^{k}_{r}u(x)}{r^{1+\alpha}}\,dr.

∎

4.1
Computation of the constant
\gamma_{k,d}(\alpha)

The reason behind the introduction of the constant

Proposition 4.3

The constant
\gamma_{k,d}(\alpha)
in (

\gamma_{k,d}(\alpha):=\frac{c_{k}|\Gamma(-\frac{\alpha}{2})|}{2^{\alpha+\gamma_{k}+d/2}\Gamma(\gamma_{k}+\tfrac{\alpha+d}{2})}.

(16)

Then, for every
u\in S(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have

\mathcal{F}_{k}(-\Delta_{k})^{\alpha/2}u(\xi)=|\xi|^{\alpha}\mathcal{F}_{k}u(\xi),\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(17)

Proof.
From
(

\mathcal{M}_{r}^{k}u(x)=\frac{1}{c_{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal{J}_{\gamma_{k}+d/2-1}(r|\xi|)\;\mathcal{F}_{k}u(\xi)\;\mathcal{E}_{k}(i\xi,x)\;w_{k}(\xi)\;d\xi,

where, the normalized Bessel functions

\mathcal{J}_{k}(x):=\Gamma(k+1)\,(2/x)^{k}\,J_{k}(x).

Here,

Then

Substituting this expression in the integrand of (

(-\Delta_{k})^{\alpha/2}u(x)=\frac{1}{\pi_{k,d}(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1-\mathcal{J}_{\gamma_{k}+d/2-1}(r|\xi|)}{r^{1+\alpha}}\;\mathcal{F}_{k}u(\xi)\;\mathcal{E}_{k}(i\xi,x)\;w_{k}(\xi)\;d\xi.

Applying Fubini-Tonelli’s theorem and taking into account of integral representation of the function

we obtain

\displaystyle(-\Delta_{k})^{\alpha/2}u(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi_{k,d}(\alpha)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1-\mathcal{J}_{\gamma_{k}+d/2-1}(r|\xi|)}{r^{1+\alpha}}\,dr\Big)\mathcal{F}_{k}u(\xi)\mathcal{E}_{k}(i\xi,x)w_{k}(\xi)\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\gamma_{k}+\frac{d}{2})|\Gamma(-\frac{\alpha}{2})|}{\pi_{k,d}(\alpha)2^{\alpha}\Gamma(\gamma_{k}+\frac{d+\alpha}{2})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\xi|^{\alpha}\mathcal{F}_{k}u(\xi)\mathcal{E}_{k}(i\xi,x)w_{k}(\xi)\,d\xi.

Therefore,

\mathcal{F}_{k}(-\Delta_{k})^{\alpha/2}u(\xi)=\frac{\Gamma(\gamma_{k}+\frac{d}{2})|\Gamma(-\frac{\alpha}{2})|}{\pi_{k,d}(\alpha)2^{\alpha}\Gamma(\gamma_{k}+\frac{d+\alpha}{2})}|\xi|^{\alpha}\mathcal{F}_{k}u(\xi),\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

In order to fulfill the equation (

\frac{\Gamma(\gamma_{k}+\frac{d}{2})|\Gamma(-\frac{\alpha}{2})|}{\pi_{k,d}(\alpha)2^{\alpha}\Gamma(\gamma_{k}+\frac{d+\alpha}{2})}=1.

For this to happen, we necessarily have

\gamma_{\alpha,k,d}=\frac{c_{k}|\Gamma(-\frac{\alpha}{2})|}{2^{\alpha+\gamma_{k}+d/2}\Gamma(\gamma_{k}+\tfrac{\alpha+d}{2})}.

∎

Lemma 4.4

For
\alpha\in(0,\,2)
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{2-\mathcal{E}_{k}(i\xi,y)-\mathcal{E}_{k}(-i\xi,y)}{|y|^{\alpha+2\gamma_{k}+d}}w_{k}(y)dy=\frac{c_{k}|\Gamma(-\frac{\alpha}{2})||\xi|^{\alpha}}{2^{\alpha+\gamma_{k}+d/2}\Gamma(\gamma_{k}+\tfrac{\alpha+d}{2})}.

From the above property (

Making the substitutions
\nu\rightarrow\gamma_{k}+d/2-1,\,\,\mu\rightarrow\alpha/2
,
\lambda\rightarrow-\gamma_{k}-(d+\alpha)/2,\,\,a\rightarrow y,\,\,b\rightarrow|x|
in the integral
(
11
, 10.43.26)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-\lambda}K_{\mu}(at)J_{\nu}(bt)\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{b^{\nu}\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}(\nu+\mu-\lambda+1))\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}(\nu-\mu+\lambda+1))}{2^{\lambda+1}a^{\nu-\lambda+1}\Gamma(\nu+1)}\,\mbox{}_{2}F_{1}\!\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\frac{1}{2}(\nu+\mu-\lambda+1),\frac{1}{2}(\nu-\mu-\lambda+1)}{\nu+1};-\frac{a^{2}}{b^{2}}\right),

where,
0<a<b,\,\,-1<\Re(\lambda)<\Re(\mu+\nu+1)
, we get

(-\Delta_{k})^{\alpha/2}q_{\alpha,y}(x)=rac{\Gamma(\gamma_{k}+(d+\alpha)/2)}{c_{k}2^{-\gamma_{k}-d/2}\Gamma(\alpha/2)}y^{\alpha}\big(y^{2}+|x|^{2}\big)^{-\gamma_{k}-\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}.

∎

Theorem 5.4

Let
d\geq 2
,
0<\alpha<2,
and recall the normalized constant
d_{k}(\alpha)
is defined in (
30
). Then, the function

f_{\alpha}(x)=d_{k}(\alpha)|x|^{-(2\gamma_{k}+d-\alpha)},

is a fundamental solution for
(-\Delta_{k})^{\alpha/2},
that is
(-\Delta_{k})^{\alpha/2}f_{\alpha}=\delta.

Proof.
Let
\varphi\in S(\mathbb{R}^{d}).
Since
\lim_{y\rightarrow 0^{+}}q_{k,y}(x)=f_{\alpha}(x)
, by Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem we have

\lim_{y\rightarrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}q_{k,y}(x)(-\Delta_{k})^{\alpha/2}\varphi(x)w_{k}(x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{\alpha}(x)(-\Delta_{k})^{\alpha/2}\varphi(x)w_{k}(x)dx.

On the other hand, form Lemma (
5.3
), we can write

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(-\Delta_{k})^{\alpha/2}q_{k,y}(x)\varphi(x)w_{k}(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(\gamma_{k}+(d+\alpha)/2)}{c_{k}2^{-\gamma_{k}-d/2}\Gamma(\alpha/2)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{y^{\alpha}\varphi(x)}{(y^{2}+|x|^{2})^{\gamma_{k}+\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}}w_{k}(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(\gamma_{k}+(d+\alpha)/2)}{c_{k}2^{-\gamma_{k}-d/2}\Gamma(\alpha/2)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\varphi(yx)}{(1+|x|^{2})^{\gamma_{k}+\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}}w_{k}(x)dx.

Using again Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem, we get

\displaystyle\lim_{y\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(-\Delta_{k})^{\alpha/2}q_{k,y}(x)\varphi(x)w_{k}(x)dx

\displaystyle=\varphi(0)\frac{\Gamma(\gamma_{k}+(d+\alpha)/2)}{c_{k}2^{-\gamma_{k}-d/2}\Gamma(\alpha/2)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{(1+|x|^{2})^{\gamma_{k}+\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}}w_{k}(x)dx.

To complete the proof of this Theorem, it suffices to prove that

\frac{\Gamma(\gamma_{k}+(d+\alpha)/2)}{c_{k}2^{-\gamma_{k}-d/2}\Gamma(\alpha/2)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{(1+|x|^{2})^{\gamma_{k}+\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}}w_{k}(x)dx=1.

Indeed, using the polar coordinates
x=r\omega
, we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{(1+|x|^{2})^{\gamma_{k}+\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}}w_{k}(x)dx=\sigma_{k}(d)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{2\gamma+d-1}}{(1+r^{2})^{\gamma_{k}+(d+\alpha)/2}}dr.

From the integral representation for the Beta function (see,
(
11
, 5.12.3)
)

\beta(a,b):=\frac{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)}{\Gamma(a+b)}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{a-1}}{(1+t)^{a+b}}dt,

we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{(1+|x|^{2})^{\gamma_{k}+\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}}w_{k}(x)dx=\frac{c_{k}2^{-\gamma_{k}-d/2}\Gamma(\alpha/2)}{\Gamma(\gamma_{k}+(d+\alpha)/2)}.

∎

5.2
Extension problem

The extension problem for the fractional Dunkl-Laplacian is a particular case of the general extension
problem proved in
Sting1
, see also
Sting2
;
M5
. In this section, we use the extension problem and the ideas of Caffarelli and Silvestre
Caffarelli
, and we show that the fractional Dunkl-Laplacian, can be seen as the Dirichlet-Neumann map for a local degenerate elliptic equation. Let
u\in S(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and consider the following problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l l}\Delta_{k}U(x,y)+\frac{\partial^{2}U(x,y)}{\partial^{2}y}+\frac{1-\alpha}{y}\frac{\partial U(x,y)}{\partial y}=0,\quad(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\,\infty),\\
\\
U(x,0)=u(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
\\
U(x,y)\rightarrow 0,\quad y\rightarrow\infty.\end{array}\right.

(33)

We define the kernel
p_{\alpha,y}(x)
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by

p_{\alpha,y}(x)=b_{k}(\alpha)\frac{y^{\alpha}}{\big(y^{2}+|x|^{2}\big)^{\gamma_{k}+\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}},\quad y>0,\quad\mbox{\it where}\;\;b_{k}(\alpha)=2^{\gamma_{k}+\tfrac{d}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\gamma_{k}+\frac{d+\alpha}{2})}{c_{k}\Gamma(\tfrac{\alpha}{2})}.

The following lemma will be useful in this section.

Lemma 5.5

The kernel
p_{\alpha,y}
has the following properties

1)

\displaystyle\widehat{p_{\alpha,y}}(\xi)=\frac{(y|\xi|)^{\alpha/2}}{c_{k}2^{\alpha/2-1}\Gamma(\alpha/2)}K_{\alpha/2}(y|\xi|)
;

2)

\displaystyle\|p_{\alpha,y}\|_{1,k}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p_{\alpha,y}(x)w_{k}(x)dx=1.

Proof.

The kernel
p_{\alpha,y}
being a radial function, then by (
4.7
), one has

\widehat{p_{\alpha,y}}(\xi)=\frac{b_{\alpha,k}y^{\alpha}}{|\xi|^{\gamma_{k}+d/2-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{J_{\gamma_{k}+d/2-1}(r|\xi|)}{\big(y^{2}+r^{2}\big)^{\gamma_{k}+\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}}\,\,\,r^{\gamma_{k}+d/2}\,dr.

The result follows from
(
Marichev
, formula 6.565.4)
.
∎

Note that for
\alpha=1,
the kernel (
5.2
) takes the form

p_{1,y}(x)=2^{\gamma_{k}+\tfrac{d}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\gamma_{k}+\frac{d+1}{2})}{c_{k}\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2})}\frac{y}{\big(y^{2}+|x|^{2}\big)^{\gamma_{k}+\frac{d+1}{2}}},\quad y>0.

which is the Poisson-Dunkl kernel for the half-space
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+},
see
(
Roesler3
, formula (5.3))
.

Theorem 5.6

Let
u\in S(\mathbb{R}^{d}).
Then, the solution
U
to the extension problem (
33
) is given by

U(x,y)=(p_{\alpha,y}\mathrel{{\mathop{\star}\limits}}u)(x)=\frac{1}{c_{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p_{\alpha,y}(\xi)\tau^{x}u(\xi)w_{k}(\xi)\,d\xi.

Furthermore, we have

(-\Delta_{k})^{\alpha/2}u(x)=-\frac{2^{\alpha-1}\Gamma(\alpha/2)}{\Gamma(1-\alpha/2)}\lim_{y\rightarrow 0^{+}}y^{1-\alpha}\frac{\partial U}{\partial y}(x,y).

(34)

Proof.
Applying the Dunkl transform to the variable
x
in (
33
), we find

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l l}y^{2}Y^{\prime\prime}+(1-\alpha)yY^{\prime}+|\xi|^{2}y^{2}Y=0,\\
\\
Y(0)=\mathcal{F}_{k}u(\xi),\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
\\
Y(y)\rightarrow 0,\quad y\rightarrow 0.\end{array}\right.

(35)

where
Y(y)=Y_{\xi}(y)=\mathcal{F}_{k}U(\xi,y).
Thus, the general solution of (
35
) can be written in the form

Y_{\xi}(y)=Ay^{\alpha/2}I_{\alpha/2}(y|\xi|)+By^{\alpha/2}K_{\alpha/2}(y|\xi|),

where
A
and
B
are constants depending on
\xi
. The condition
\lim_{\rightarrow 0}Y(y)
, gives
A=0
, and then

\mathcal{F}_{k}U(\xi,y)=By^{\alpha/2}K_{\alpha/2}(y|\xi|).

(36)

To determine the constant
B
, we use the initial condition
\mathcal{F}_{k}U(\xi,0)=\mathcal{F}_{k}u(\xi),
and the following asymptotic behavior of
K_{\nu}(z)
near zero:

K_{\nu}(z)\sim 2^{\nu-1}\Gamma(\nu)z^{-\nu},

which yields

\mathcal{F}_{k}U(\xi,y)\sim B2^{\alpha/2-1}\Gamma(\alpha/2)|\xi|^{-\alpha/2},\quad\text{as}\;y\rightarrow 0,

thus, we impose that

B=\frac{|\xi|^{\alpha/2}\mathcal{F}_{k}u(\xi)}{2^{\alpha/2-1}\Gamma(\alpha/2)}.

Substituting this value of
B
in (
36
), we obtain

\mathcal{F}_{k}U(\xi,y)=\frac{(y|\xi|)^{\alpha/2}}{2^{\alpha/2-1}\Gamma(\alpha/2)}K_{\alpha/2}(y|\xi|)\mathcal{F}_{k}u(\xi).

From Lemma 6.1, we get

U(x,y)=p_{\alpha}\mathrel{{\mathop{\star}\limits}}u(x),

therefore,

U(x,y)=\frac{b_{\alpha,k}}{2c_{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}y^{\alpha}\frac{\tau^{x}u(\xi)+\tau^{-x}u(\xi)-2u(x)}{\big(y^{2}+|\xi|^{2}\big)^{\gamma_{k}+\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}}\;w_{k}(\xi)\;d\xi\;+\;u(x).

Differentiating both sides of the above formula with respect to
y,
we obtain

y^{1-\alpha}\frac{\partial U}{\partial y}(x,y)=\frac{b_{\alpha,k}}{2c_{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\alpha+(\gamma_{k}+d+\alpha)y^{2}}{\big(y^{2}+|\xi|^{2}\big)^{\gamma_{k}+\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}}\;\left[\tau^{x}u(\xi)+\tau^{-x}u(\xi)-2u(x)\right]\;w_{k}(\xi)\;d\xi,

and by Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem, we find

\displaystyle\lim_{y\rightarrow 0^{+}}y^{1-\alpha}\frac{\partial U}{\partial y}(x,y)=\frac{\alpha b_{\alpha,k}}{2c_{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\tau^{x}u(\xi)+\tau^{-x}u(\xi)-2u(x)}{|\xi|^{2\gamma_{k}+d+\alpha}}w_{k}(\xi)\,d\xi=\frac{\alpha b_{\alpha,k}}{c_{k}}\frac{\gamma_{k,d}(\alpha)}{2}(-\Delta_{k})^{\alpha}.

If, in the last equation, we replace the expression (
16
) of the constant
\gamma_{k,d}(\alpha)
, we reach the conclusion that (
34
) is valid.
∎

Load-Flow Solvability under Security Constraints in DC Distribution Networks
Zhe Chen, , Cong Wang

Abstract
We present sufficient conditions for the load-flow solvability under security constraints in DC distribution networks. In addition, we show that a load-flow solution that fulfills security constraints can be obtained via a convex optimization.



I
Introduction

Recently, DC distribution networks have received increasing attention. In these networks, many control procedures (i) rely on the load-flow solvability, and (ii) require that the load-flow solution (if exists) should satisfy the security constraints. However, due to non-linearity of the load-flow equations, this is hard to study. In this paper, we present sufficient conditions for the load-flow solvability under security constraints in DC distribution networks. Moreover, we show that a load-flow solution that fulfills security constraints can be obtained by solving a convex optimization.

II
Problem Formulation

We consider a unipolar DC distribution network that has one slack bus,
N

PQ
buses, and a generic topology (i.e., radial or meshed). The slack bus is indexed by
0
, and the
PQ
buses are indexed by
1,...,N
. For simplicity of expression, we define

•

\mathcal{N}=\{0,...,N\}
as the set of all buses;

•

\mathcal{N}^{PQ}=\mathcal{N}\setminus\{0\}
as the set of
PQ
buses;

•

\mathcal{K}(n)\subseteq\mathcal{N}
as the set of neighbouring buses for bus
n\in\mathcal{N}
;

•

I_{\mathcal{K}(n)}(k)
as the indicator function that has value
1
when
k\in\mathcal{K}(n)
and
0
otherwise.

Now, let
v_{n}
,
p_{n}
be the real-valued nodal voltage and nodal power injection at bus
n\in\mathcal{N}
. In addition, let positive real constant
g_{nk}
be the line conductance between buses
n\in\mathcal{N}
and
k\in\mathcal{K}(n)
. Then, the load-flow equations at
PQ
buses are formulated as follows:

\Big(\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}(n)}g_{nk}\Big)v_{n}^{2}-\Big(\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}(n)}g_{nk}v_{k}\Big)v_{n}=p_{n},\hskip 9.24994ptn\in\mathcal{N}^{PQ}~.

(1)

During power system operation, the nodal voltages and branch currents should satisfy security constraints. In the paper, we express the security constraints as follows:

v^{\mathrm{min}}<v_{n}<v^{\mathrm{max}},\hskip 9.24994ptn\in\mathcal{N}^{PQ},

(2)

|g_{nk}(v_{n}-v_{k})|<i_{nk}^{\mathrm{max}},\hskip 9.24994ptn\in\mathcal{N}^{PQ},~k\in\mathcal{K}(n),

(3)

where
v^{\mathrm{min}}
,
v^{\mathrm{max}}
, and
i_{nk}^{\mathrm{max}}
are some pre-defined positive real constants. Note that, for the development of our theoretical results, we need the security constraints to be strict inequalities.

With the above grid model and notations, we study the following problem: Given
v_{0}
and
(p_{1},...,p_{N})
, is there a unique solution
(v_{1},...,v_{N})
that satisfies the load-flow equations (
1
) and the security constraints (
2
)–(
3
) ?

III
Main Results

III-A

Existence

In Theorem
1
, we provide sufficient conditions for the existence of a solution to (
1
)–(
3
).

Theorem 1
.

Assume that

1.

2v^{\mathrm{min}}>v^{\mathrm{max}}>v_{0}>v^{\mathrm{min}}
;

2.

The following convex optimization
\mathrm{P1}
is feasible.

\displaystyle[\mathbf{P1}]\hskip 9.24994pt\max\limits_{\alpha_{n},\beta_{nk},v_{n}}\hskip 9.24994pt

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}v_{n}

\displaystyle\mathrm{subject~to:}\hskip 9.24994pt

\displaystyle\mathrm{inequalities}~\eqref{eq:security1}-\eqref{eq:security2},

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt\Big(\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}(n)}g_{nk}\Big)\alpha_{n}-\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}(n)\setminus\{0\}}g_{nk}\beta_{nk}

\displaystyle=p_{n}+\Big(g_{n0}v_{0}I_{\mathcal{K}(n)}(0)\Big)v_{n},\hskip 9.24994ptn\in\mathcal{N}^{PQ},

(4)

\displaystyle v_{n}^{2}\leq\alpha_{n},\hskip 9.24994ptn\in\mathcal{N}^{PQ},

(5)

\displaystyle\beta_{nk}\geq 0,\hskip 9.24994ptn\in\mathcal{N}^{PQ},~k\in\mathcal{K}(n)

(6)

\displaystyle\beta_{nk}^{2}\leq\alpha_{n}\alpha_{k},\hskip 9.24994ptn\in\mathcal{N}^{PQ},~k\in\mathcal{K}(n)~.

(7)

Then, for any optimizer
\alpha_{n}^{\star}
,
\beta_{nk}^{\star}
,
v_{n}^{\star}
of
\mathrm{P1}
, we have that
(v_{1}^{\star},...,v_{N}^{\star})
is a solution to (
1
)–(
3
).

In practice, the condition
2v^{\mathrm{min}}>v^{\mathrm{max}}>v_{0}>v^{\mathrm{min}}
in Theorem
1
is normally satisfied. Consequently, for any
(p_{1},...,p_{N})
such that
\mathrm{P1}
is feasible, there exists at least one solution to (
1
)–(
3
).

It should be noticed that, Theorem
1
not only gives sufficient conditions on the existence of a solution to (
1
)–(
3
), but also shows that a solution can be obtained by solving the convex optimization
\mathrm{P1}
.

.

Consider that the numerical solvers might not be able to handle strict inequalities, we can solve the following convex optimization and verify whether the optimal nodal voltages fulfill the strict inequality (
2
)–(
3
).

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}v_{n}

\displaystyle\mathrm{subject~to:}\hskip 9.24994pt

\displaystyle\eqref{eq:alpha1}-\eqref{eq:beta2},

\displaystyle v^{\mathrm{min}}\leq v_{n}\leq v^{\mathrm{max}},\hskip 9.24994ptn\in\mathcal{N}^{PQ},

(8)

\displaystyle|g_{nk}(v_{n}-v_{k})|\leq i_{nk}^{\mathrm{max}},\hskip 9.24994ptn\in\mathcal{N}^{PQ},~k\in\mathcal{K}(n)~.

III-B

Uniqueness

In Theorem
2
, we provide sufficient conditions for the uniqueness of the solution to (
1
)–(
3
).

Theorem 2
.

If the following polynomial optimization
\mathrm{P2}
is infeasible, then there exists at most one solution to (
1
)–(
3
).

\displaystyle[\mathbf{P2}]\hskip 9.24994pt\min\limits_{\gamma_{n},v_{n}}\hskip 9.24994pt

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}v_{n}

\displaystyle\mathrm{subject~to:}\hskip 9.24994pt

\displaystyle\eqref{eq:security3}-\eqref{eq:security4},

\displaystyle\Bigg(2\Big(\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}(n)}g_{nk}\Big)v_{n}-\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}(n)}g_{nk}v_{k}\Bigg)\gamma_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}(n)\setminus\{0\}}\Big(g_{nk}v_{n}\Big)\gamma_{k},\hskip 9.24994ptn\in\mathcal{N}^{PQ},

(10)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}\gamma_{n}^{2}=1~.

(11)

As can be seen, the infeasibility of polynomial optimization
\mathrm{P2}
is determined by only the security bounds
v^{\mathrm{min}}
,
v^{\mathrm{max}}
,
i_{nk}^{\mathrm{max}}
and the slack-bus voltage
v_{0}
.

By Theorem
2
, under the infeasibility of
\mathrm{P2}
, if power injection
(p_{1},...,p_{N})
has a solution to (
1
)–(
3
), then this solution must be unique.

Remark 2
.

Due to the non-linear equality constraints,
\mathrm{P2}
is not convex. This means that the numerical solvers might not be able to directly check the infeasibility of
\mathrm{P2}
. However, we know that
\mathrm{P2}
is infeasible if any convex relaxation of
\mathrm{P2}
is infeasible. For efficiency and practicality, we suggest to take the sparsity-exploiting SDP relaxation in
[
1
]
, which has already been successfully applied to the optimal power flow problem in
[
2
,
3
,

IV
Proofs

IV-A

Proof of Theorem
1

Proof.

Since
(v_{1}^{\star},...,v_{N}^{\star})
satisfies (
2
)–(
3
), we only need to prove that
(v_{1}^{\star},...,v_{N}^{\star})
satisfies (
1
).

First, we show
\Big(v_{n}^{\star}\Big)^{2}=\alpha_{n}^{\star},~\forall n\in\mathcal{N}^{PQ}
. Suppose that
\Big(v_{\tilde{n}}^{\star}\Big)^{2}<\alpha_{\tilde{n}}^{\star}
for some
\tilde{n}\in\mathcal{N}^{PQ}
. As a result, we have

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}(\tilde{n})}g_{\tilde{n}k}\Big)\Big(v_{\tilde{n}}^{\star}\Big)^{2}-\Big(g_{\tilde{n}0}v_{0}I_{\mathcal{K}(\tilde{n})}(0)\Big)v_{\tilde{n}}^{\star}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}(\tilde{n})}g_{\tilde{n}k}\Big)\alpha_{\tilde{n}}^{\star}-\Big(g_{\tilde{n}0}v_{0}I_{\mathcal{K}(\tilde{n})}(0)\Big)v_{\tilde{n}}^{\star}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}(\tilde{n})\setminus\{0\}}g_{\tilde{n}k}\beta_{\tilde{n}k}^{\star}+p_{\tilde{n}}~.

Observe that the quadratic form
\Big(\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}(\tilde{n})}g_{\tilde{n}k}\Big)\Big(v_{\tilde{n}}^{\star}\Big)^{2}-\Big(g_{\tilde{n}0}v_{0}I_{\mathcal{K}(\tilde{n})}(0)\Big)v_{\tilde{n}}^{\star}
is convex in
v_{\tilde{n}}^{\star}
. Moreover, this quadratic is monotonically increasing with respect to
v_{\tilde{n}}^{\star}
, as

\frac{g_{\tilde{n}0}v_{0}I_{\mathcal{K}(\tilde{n})}(0)}{2\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}(\tilde{n})}g_{\tilde{n}k}}\leq\frac{v_{0}}{2}\leq v^{\mathrm{min}}<v_{\tilde{n}}^{\star}~.

Therefore,
v_{\tilde{n}}^{\star}
can be further increased, which contradicts the optimality of
v_{\tilde{n}}^{\star}
. Hence, we must have
\Big(v_{n}^{\star}\Big)^{2}=\alpha_{n}^{\star},~\forall n\in\mathcal{N}^{PQ}
.

Next, we show
\Big(\beta_{nk}^{\star}\Big)^{2}=\alpha_{n}^{\star}\alpha_{k}^{\star},~\forall n\in\mathcal{N}^{PQ},~k\in\mathcal{K}(n)
. Since
\beta_{nk}\geq 0,~\forall n\in\mathcal{N}^{PQ},~k\in\mathcal{K}(n)
, we could show
\beta_{nk}^{\star}=v_{n}^{\star}v_{k}^{\star},~\forall n\in\mathcal{N}^{PQ},~k\in\mathcal{K}(n)
. Suppose that
\beta_{\tilde{n}\tilde{k}}^{\star}<v_{\tilde{n}}^{\star}v_{\tilde{k}}^{\star}
for some
\tilde{n}\in\mathcal{N}^{PQ},~\tilde{k}\in\mathcal{K}(\tilde{n})
. Then, we have

\Big(\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}(\tilde{n})}g_{\tilde{n}k}\Big)\Big(v_{\tilde{n}}^{\star}\Big)^{2}-\Big(g_{\tilde{n}0}v_{0}I_{\mathcal{K}(\tilde{n})}(0)+\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}(\tilde{n})\setminus\{0\}}g_{\tilde{n}k}v_{k}^{\star}\Big)v_{\tilde{n}}^{\star}<p_{\tilde{n}}~.

Observe that the quadratic form
\Big(\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}(\tilde{n})}g_{\tilde{n}k}\Big)\Big(v_{\tilde{n}}^{\star}\Big)^{2}-\Big(g_{\tilde{n}0}v_{0}I_{\mathcal{K}(\tilde{n})}(0)+\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}(\tilde{n})\setminus\{0\}}g_{\tilde{n}k}v_{k}^{\star}\Big)v_{\tilde{n}}^{\star}
is convex in
v_{\tilde{n}}^{\star}
. Moreover, this quadratic is monotonically increasing with respect to
v_{\tilde{n}}^{\star}
, as

\frac{g_{\tilde{n}0}v_{0}I_{\mathcal{K}(\tilde{n})}(0)+\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}(\tilde{n})\setminus\{0\}}g_{\tilde{n}k}v_{k}^{\star}}{2\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}(\tilde{n})}g_{\tilde{n}k}}<\frac{v^{\mathrm{max}}}{2}<v^{\mathrm{min}}<v_{\tilde{n}}^{\star}~.

Therefore,
v_{\tilde{n}}^{\star}
can be further increased, which contradicts the optimality of
v_{\tilde{n}}^{\star}
. Hence, we must have
\beta_{nk}^{\star}=v_{n}^{\star}v_{k}^{\star},~\forall n\in\mathcal{N}^{PQ},~k\in\mathcal{K}(n)
.
∎

IV-B

Proof of Theorem
2

Proof.

First, for all
n\in\mathcal{N}^{PQ}
, we have

\displaystyle\Bigg(2\Big(\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}(n)}g_{nk}\Big)v_{n}-\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}(n)}g_{nk}v_{k}\Bigg)\gamma_{n}-\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}(n)\setminus\{0\}}\Big(g_{nk}v_{n}\Big)\gamma_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Bigg(\Big(\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}(n)}g_{nk}\Big)v_{n}^{2}-\Big(\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}(n)}g_{nk}v_{k}\Big)v_{n}\Bigg)}{\partial\Big(v_{1},...,v_{N}\Big)}\begin{pmatrix}\gamma_{1}\\
\vdots\\
\gamma_{N}\end{pmatrix}

where
\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial(v_{1},...,v_{N})}
is the
1\times N
Jacobian.

Let the
N\times N
Jacobian matrix of equation system (
1
) be

\mathbf{J}(v_{1},...,v_{N})=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\partial\Bigg(\Big(\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}(1)}g_{1k}\Big)v_{1}^{2}-\Big(\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}(1)}g_{1k}v_{k}\Big)v_{1}\Bigg)}{\partial\Big(v_{1},...,v_{N}\Big)}\\
\vdots\\
\frac{\partial\Bigg(\Big(\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}(N)}g_{Nk}\Big)v_{N}^{2}-\Big(\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}(N)}g_{Nk}v_{k}\Big)v_{N}\Bigg)}{\partial\Big(v_{1},...,v_{N}\Big)}\end{pmatrix}.

Then, it can be seen that
\mathbf{J}(v_{1},...,v_{N})
is non-singular in
\{v_{n},n\in\mathcal{N}^{PQ}:\mathrm{inequalities~\eqref{eq:security1}-\eqref{eq:security2}}\}
whenever the optimization
\mathrm{P2}
is infeasible.

Second,
\{v_{n},n\in\mathcal{N}^{PQ}:\mathrm{inequalities~\eqref{eq:security1}-\eqref{eq:security2}}\}
is convex, since (
2
)–(
3
) are linear constraints on
v_{1},...,v_{N}
.

By Theorem 2 in
[
5
]
, we know that there exists at most one solution to (
1
)–(
3
) if
\mathrm{P2}
is infeasible.
∎

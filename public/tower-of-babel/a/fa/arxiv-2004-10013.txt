Generalization of the Conway-Gordon theorem and intrinsic linking on complete graphs Thanks: The second author was supported by JSPS KAKENHI Grant Numbers JP15K04881 and JP19K03500.
Hiroko Morishita, Ryo Nikkuni

Abstract
. Conway and Gordon proved that for every spatial complete graph on six vertices, the sum of the linking numbers over all of the constituent two-component links is odd, and Kazakov and Korablev proved that for every spatial complete graph with arbitrary number of vertices greater than six, the sum of the linking numbers over all of the constituent two-component Hamiltonian links is even. In this paper, we show that for every spatial complete graph whose number of vertices is greater than six, the sum of the square of the linking numbers over all of the two-component Hamiltonian links is determined explicitly in terms of the sum over all of the triangle-triangle constituent links. As an application, we show that if the number of vertices is sufficiently large then every spatial complete graph contains a two-component Hamiltonian link whose absolute value of the linking number is arbitrary large. Some applications to rectilinear spatial complete graphs are also given.

Introduction

Throughout this paper we work in the piecewise linear category. Let

of

of

Let

Theorem 1-1

(Conway-Gordon

]
)

(
1

For any spatial embedding

-component Hamiltonian links of

is odd.

(
2

For any spatial embedding
f
of

, the sum of the second coefficients of the Conway polynomials over all of the constituent Hamiltonian knots of

is odd.

Theorem

also implies that every spatial graph of

contains a nonsplittable

-component link, and every spatial graph of

contains a nontrivial knot. Furthermore, the authors gave in
[

(Morishita-Nikkuni
[

Let

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n}(K_{n})}a_{2}(f(\gamma))-(n-5)!\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{5}(K_{n})}a_{2}(f(\gamma))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(n-5)!}{2}\bigg(\sum_{\lambda\in\Gamma_{3,3}(K_{n})}{{\rm lk}(f(\lambda))}^{2}-\binom{n-1}{5}\bigg),

Moreover, it has been also shown in

Now let us observe intrinsic linking on rectilinear spatial graphs of
K_{n}
based on Theorem
1-5
. Note that every constituent link of a rectilinear spatial graph is a polygonal link with some sticks, and it is well-known that every polygonal
2
-component link with exactly six sticks is a trivial link or a Hopf link (unlinked two triangles or linked two triangles). Then it follows from this fact that the number of “triangle-triangle” Hopf links in a rectilinear spatial graph coincides with the sum of
{\rm lk}^{2}
over all of the constituent triangle-triangle links. Therefore Theorem
1-5
implies that for a rectilinear spatial graph of
K_{n}
, the sum of
{\rm lk}^{2}
over all of the constituent
2
-component Hamiltonian links of any type
(p,q)
is determined explicitly in terms of the number of triangle-triangle Hopf links. Then we recall the fact that every rectilinear spatial graph of
K_{6}
contains at most three Hopf links
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
20
]
. This also implies that the number of triangle-triangle Hopf links in a rectilinear spatial graph of
K_{n}
is less than or equal to
3\binom{n}{6}
. Thus by Theorem
1-5
and Corollary
1-7
, we have the following.

Corollary 4-1
.

Let
n\geq 6
be an integer and
p,q\geq 3
two integers satisfying
n=p+q
. For any rectilinear spatial embedding
f_{\rm r}
of
K_{n}
, we have

(4.1)

\displaystyle\frac{n!}{6!}\leq\sum_{\lambda\in\Gamma_{p,q}(K_{n})}{\rm lk}(f_{\rm r}(\lambda))^{2}\leq 3\cdot\frac{n!}{6!}

if
p=q
, and

(4.2)

\displaystyle 2\cdot\frac{n!}{6!}\leq\sum_{\lambda\in\Gamma_{p,q}(K_{n})}{\rm lk}(f_{\rm r}(\lambda))^{2}\leq 6\cdot\frac{n!}{6!}

if
p\neq q
. In particular, we have

(4.3)

\displaystyle(n-5)\cdot\frac{n!}{6!}\leq\sum_{p+q=n}\sum_{\lambda\in\Gamma_{p,q}(K_{n})}{\rm lk}(f_{\rm r}(\lambda))^{2}\leq 3(n-5)\cdot\frac{n!}{6!}.

Remark 4-2
.

As it was pointed out in
[
17

, the lower bound of (
3.66
) is also realized by “standard” rectilinear spatial embedding of
K_{n}
. This implies that the lower bound of each of the inequalities in Corollary
4-1
is sharp. But we do not think that the upper bound is also sharp if
n\geq 7
, see Example
4-3
.

Example 4-3
.

Let
f_{\rm r}
be a rectilinear spatial embedding of
K_{7}
. Then by (
4.2
) and (
3.69
), we have

\displaystyle 14\leq\sum_{\lambda\in\Gamma_{3,4}(K_{7})}{\rm lk}(f_{\rm r}(\lambda))^{2}\leq 42,\ \ \sum_{\lambda\in\Gamma_{3,4}(K_{7})}{\rm lk}(f_{\rm r}(\lambda))^{2}\equiv 2\pmod{4}.

However, according to a computer search in Jeon et al.
[
15
]
, there seems to be no rectilinear embedding
f_{\rm r}
of
K_{7}
such that
\sum_{\lambda\in\Gamma_{3,4}(K_{7})}{\rm lk}(f_{\rm r}(\lambda))^{2}=38,42
, or equivalently by (
1-5
),
\sum_{\lambda\in\Gamma_{3,3}(K_{7})}{\rm lk}(f_{\rm r}(\lambda))^{2}=19,21
. This suggests that the upper bound in Corollary
4-1
cannot be expected to be sharp if
n\geq 7
.

The problem of determining the sharp upper bound of the sum of
{\rm lk}^{2}

2

Problem 4-4

Determine the maximum number of constituent triangle-triangle Hopf links for all rectilinear spatial graphs of
K_{n}

n\geq 7

Finally, let us show similar results as Corollaries
1-8
and
1-9
for the maximum value of
a_{2}
over all of the Hamiltonian knots of a rectilinear spatial graph of
K_{n}
. It is also well-known that every polygonal knot with less than or equal to five sticks is trivial. Then by combining this fact and (
3.66
) with Theorem
1-2
, we have

(4.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n}(K_{n})}a_{2}(f_{\rm r}(\gamma))\geq\frac{(n-5)(n-6)\cdot(n-1)!}{2\cdot 6!}

for any rectilinear spatial embedding
f_{\rm r}
of
K_{n}\ (n\geq 6)

[
17
, Corollary 1.7]
. Then we have the following.

Corollary 4-5
.

Let
n\geq 6
be an integer. For any rectilinear spatial embedding
f_{\rm r}
of
K_{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm max}\limits_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n}(K_{n})}a_{2}(f_{\rm r}(\gamma))\geq\frac{(n-5)(n-6)}{6!}.

Proof.

Note that the number of all elements in
\Gamma_{n}(K_{n})
is
(n-1)!/2
.
Then by (
4.4
), we have

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm max}\limits_{\lambda\in\Gamma_{n}(K_{n})}a_{2}(f_{\rm r}(\gamma))\cdot\frac{(n-1)!}{2}\geq\sum_{\lambda\in\Gamma_{n}(K_{n})}a_{2}(f_{\rm r}(\gamma))\geq\frac{(n-5)(n-6)\cdot(n-1)!}{2\cdot 6!}.

Thus we have the result.
∎

Corollary
4-5
says that if
n
is sufficiently large then every rectilinear spatial graph of
K_{n}
contains a Hamiltonian knot whose value of
a_{2}
is arbitrary large. Actually we have the following.

Corollary 4-6
.

Let
n\geq 6
be a positive integer. For a rectilinear spatial embedding
f_{\rm r}
of
K_{n}
and a positive integer
m
, if
n>(11+\sqrt{2880m-2879})/2
then there exists a cycle
\gamma\in\Gamma_{n}(K_{n})
such that
a_{2}(f_{\rm r}(\gamma))\geq m
.

Proof.

For an integer
n\geq 6
and a positive integer
m
, we can see that
n>(11+\sqrt{2880m-2879})/2
if and only if
(n-5)(n-6)/6!>m-1
. Then by Corollary
4-5
we have

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm max}\limits_{\lambda\in\Gamma_{n}(K_{n})}a_{2}(f_{\rm r}(\gamma))\geq\frac{(n-5)(n-6)}{6!}>m-1.

This implies the desired conclusion.
∎

Remark 4-7
.

It has already been known that for any positive integer
m

n
such that every spatial graph of
K_{n}
(which does not need to be rectilinear) contains a knot
K

a_{2}(K)\geq m

[
6
]
,
[
23
]
. In partucular, Shirai-Taniyama showed in

f

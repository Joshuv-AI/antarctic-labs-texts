A Monoidal Dold-Kan Correspondence For Comodules
Maximilien Péroux

Abstract
. Cofibrantly generated model categories are generalizations of CW-approximations which provide an inductive cofibrant replacement. We provide examples of inductive fibrant replacements constructed as Postnikov towers for simplicial and differential graded comodules. Our main application is to show that simplicial comodules and connective differential graded comodules are Quillen equivalent and their derived cotensor products correspond. We deduce that the rational A A -theory of a simply connected space X X is equivalent to the K K -theory of perfect chain complexes with a C ∗ ​ ( X , ℚ ) C_{*}(X;\mathbb{Q}) -comodule structure.

Introduction

The
Dold-Kan correspondence
established in
[
Dol58
,
Kan58
]
is an equivalence between the category
\mathsf{sMod}_{R}
of simplicial
R
-modules and the category
\mathsf{Ch}_{R}^{\geq 0}
of non-negative chain complexes over a commutative ring
R
. This has been generalized to any abelian category, or even any stable
\infty
-category
[
Lur17
]
. The
normalization
functor
\mathsf{N}\colon\mathsf{sMod}_{R}\rightarrow\mathsf{Ch}_{R}^{\geq 0}
is lax symmetric monoidal with respect to the graded tensor product of chains, the levelwise tensor product of simplicial modules and the
Eilenberg-Zilber map
. The inverse equivalence
\Gamma\colon\mathsf{Ch}_{R}^{\geq 0}\rightarrow\mathsf{sMod}_{R}
also has a (non-symmetric) lax monoidal structure coming from the
Alexander-Whitney map
. The categories are not equivalent as symmetric monoidal categories. Nevertheless, the homotopy categories of
\mathsf{sMod}_{R}
and
\mathsf{Ch}_{R}^{\geq 0}
have isomorphic symmetric monoidal structures when endowed with their respective
derived tensor product
. Formally, it was shown in
[
SS03
]
that there is a weak symmetric monoidal Quillen equivalence between the symmetric monoidal model categories of
\mathsf{sMod}_{R}
and
\mathsf{Ch}_{R}^{\geq 0}
. The equivalence lifts to the associated categories of algebras and modules.
This equivalence was crucial in
[
Shi07
]
to show that homotopy coherent
H\mathbb{Z}
-algebras in spectra are equivalent to differential graded algebras. Similar results hold for the cosimplicial case
[
CC04
]
and the commutative case
[
RS17
]
.
It is important to notice that at the level of model categories, the results of
[
SS03
]
do not imply any Quillen equivalences on model categories of coalgebras or comodules. In fact, in
[
Sor19
]
, it was shown that the Dold-Kan correspondence does not lift to a Quillen equivalence between coassociative and counital coalgebras. Differential graded coalgebras are of great important in rational homotopy theory
[
Qui69
,
Nei78
]
as they model Lie algebras.

In
[
Pér22
, 4.1]
, we proved that the underlying
\infty
-categories of
\mathsf{sMod}_{R}
and
\mathsf{Ch}_{R}^{\geq 0}
(in the sense of
[
Lur17
, 1.3.4.15, 4.1.7.4]
) are equivalent as symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories. In particular, the
\infty
-categories of homotopy coherent coalgebras in
\mathsf{sMod}_{R}
and
\mathsf{Ch}_{R}^{\geq 0}
are equivalent. The same holds for homotopy coherent comodules in these categories.
However, unlike the case of algebras and modules, it is more challenging to represent homotopy coherent coalgebras and comodules via a model category of coassociative and counital coalgebra, see
[
PS19
,
Pér22
]
.
In
[
Pér20b
, 1.1]
, for
R=\Bbbk
a commutative ring with global dimension zero, and
C
a simply connected strictly cocommutative and coassociative coalgebra in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}^{\geq 0}}
, we proved that homotopy coherent comodules in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}^{\geq 0}}
over
C
correspond to strictly coassociative comodules in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}^{\geq 0}}
. The
cotensor product
of comodules equalize the coactions. As
C
is always flat, the cotensor remains a comodule. The category
\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}({\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}^{\geq 0}})
of connective differential graded comodules over
C
is endowed with a model structure in which weak equivalences are quasi-isomorphisms between comodules. The model structure is compatible with respect to the cotensor product. This allowed us to endow a symmetric monoidal structure with the
derived cotensor product
on the underlying
\infty
-category of
\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}({\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}^{\geq 0}})
, see
[
Pér20b
, 1.2]
. Similar arguments hold for simplicial comodules over a simply connected simplicial coalgebra. Although homotopy coherent simplicial comodules correspond to homotopy coherent connective differential graded comodules, it is not immediate that their derived cotensor product correspond. Our main result in this paper shows that they are indeed equivalent.

(Theorem
5.1
, Theorem
5.3
)
.

Let
\Bbbk
be a commutative ring with global dimension zero. Let
C
be a simply connected cocommutative differential graded coalgebra over
\Bbbk
. Then there is a Quillen equivalence:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}({\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}^{\geq 0}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{CoMod}_{\Gamma(C)}(\mathsf{sMod}_{\Bbbk}),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{N}^{C}}

compatible with the derived cotensor products. In particular, the
\infty

and homotopy coherent connective differential graded comodules over

are equivalent as symmetric monoidal
\infty

One of the consequences of this result is an algebraic description of rational
A
-theory of spaces.
Given a space
X
, recall that
A(X)
is defined to be the
K
-theory of the Waldhausen category of finite retractive spaces over
X
with cofibrations and weak equivalences created in simplicial sets
[

]
.
For any generalized reduced homology

be the Waldhausen

-theory of the same category with the same cofibrations, but replacing the usual weak equivalences of simplicial sets by
\mathcal{E}_{*}

Hess-Shipley showed in
[

, 1.3]
that
A(X;\mathcal{E}_{*})
is equivalent to the
K
-theory of
X_{+}
-comodules in pointed spaces, localized at
\mathcal{E}_{*}
.
Thanks to the result above, we are able to provide a description of the rational
A
-theory of a space
X
as the
K
-theory of perfect chain complexes with a comodule structure over the singular chain complex
C_{*}(X;\mathbb{Q})
.

Theorem 1.2

(Corollary
5.7
)
.

For any simply connected simplicial set
X
, there is a natural equivalence of
K
-theory spectra:

A(X;H\mathbb{Q}_{*})\simeq K(\mathsf{CoMod}_{C_{*}(X;\mathbb{Q})}^{\textup{perf}}).

A future project will extend the trace method of
[
KP23
, 1.4]
to provide a trace into the coHochschild homology
K(\mathsf{CoMod}_{C_{*}(X;\mathbb{Q})}^{\textup{perf}})\rightarrow\mathsf{coHH}(C_{*}(X;\mathbb{Q}))
, as introduced by Hess-Parent-Scott
[
HPS09
]
, which relates to the usual Dennis trace if
X
is finite by
[
BP23
]
.

To prove our main theorem, we dualize the methods in
[
SS03
]
. Following
[
Hes09
]
and
[
BHK+15
]
, we use the notion of
fibrantly generated
model categories. CW-complexes provide an inductive cofibrant replacement for spaces. This was generalized into the notion of cofibrantly generated model categories. We instead provide
Postnikov towers
as an inductive fibrant replacement. Because of the lack of “cosmallness” in practice, we cannot apply the dual of the small object argument. Instead we provide ad-hoc
Postnikov presentations
: (acyclic) fibrations are retracts of maps built from pullbacks and transfinite towers of generating (acyclic) fibrations.
Moreover, the cotensor product on comodules behaves well with fibrant objects instead of cofibrant objects. We introduce the notion of
fibrant-compatible
symmetric monoidal model categories that allows us to derive the cotensor product and obtain a symmetric monoidal structure on the homotopy category and the underlying
\infty
-category. Dualizing
[
SS03
]
, we construct
weak opmonoidal Quillen equivalences
that induce an equivalence of the derived cotensor product.

On the cocommutativity condition

For the sake of simplicity, we focus in this paper on coalgebras that are cocommutative. One can instead consider non-cocommutative coalgebras and then consider bicomodules instead. The cotensor product provides then a non-symmetric monoidal structure and all the results in this paper remain valid. We provide further details in
[
KP23
]
.

On the global dimension zero condition

Throughout this paper, we work exclusively with
\Bbbk
a commutative ring with global dimension zero. There are several reasons this condition is imposed. First, it induces that every object is cofibrant and fibrant in chain complexes and simplicial modules, and the projective and injective model structures are equal. In particular, the model structure on comodules is left-induced from a nice monoidal model category. In
[
HKRS17
]
, it was shown that the model structures for comodules are left-induced from injective model structures which are in general not monoidal model categories. This creates several issues to understand the induced homotopy theory on comodules. Moreover, as every module is flat, the tensor product preserves finite limits. This allows us to understand finite limits of comodules, which is essential for our main theorem.
Furthermore, the cotensor product of comodules is
not
a comodule unless the coalgebra is flat.

On the simply connected condition

The simply connected requirement on the coalgebra is crucial for Theorem
4.16
and thus for Theorem
1.1
. It allows us to inductively build our fibrant replacement.
Moreover, although we make no use of this fact in this paper, as
C
is a simply connected coalgebra, then the derived category of comodules over
C
is equivalent to the derived category of modules over its cobar complex
\Omega C
, see
[
Pos11
]
. This Koszul duality does not come from a Quillen equivalence between model structures and results in this paper are not dual from the model structure on modules over
\Omega C
.

Outline

In Section
2
, we introduce all the dual notions and methods of
[

]
: fibrantly generated model categories, Postnikov presentations of a model category, left-induced model categories, fibrant-compatible monoidal structure on model categories and weak opmonoidal Quillen equivalences between them. In Section

4

[
Pér20b
]
and show that comodules also admit an efficient Postnikov presentation. Finally, we apply our methods to prove our main result on Dold-Kan correspondence for comodules and
A
-theory in Section

.

2.
Postnikov Presentations of Model Categories

One of the main tools of model categories is to assume the structure to be
cofibrantly generated
by a pair of sets (see definition in
[
Hov99
, 2.1.17]
).
If in addition the category is presentable, we say it is
combinatorial
.
In this case, cofibrations and acyclic cofibrations are retracts of maps built out of pushouts and transfinite compositions, and we can inductively construct a cofibrant replacement.
Given a symmetric monoidal model category
\mathsf{M}
(as in
[
Hov99
, 4.2.6]
) in which the tensor product interplays well with the generating acyclic cofibrations (see
[
SS00
, 3.3]
), then one can guarantee a nice model structure for algebras and modules in
\mathsf{M}
, see
[
SS00
, 4.1]
.
Moreover, given a weak monoidal Quillen equivalence (as in
[
SS03
, 3.6]
) between symmetric monoidal model categories, the equivalence lifts to a Quillen equivalence between algebras and modules, see
[
SS03
, 3.12]
. This induces an equivalence of symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories on the underlying
\infty
-categories of the monoidal model categories, see
[
Pér22
, 2.13]
.

In this section, following
[
BHK+15
]
, we recall the dual concept of
fibrantly generated
model categories. We make no requirement of “cosmallness” as it is rarely satisfied in practice. Instead when fibrations and acyclic fibrations are retracts of maps built out of pullbacks and towers, we say the model category admits a
Postnikov presentation
. In
[
HKRS17
,
GKR20
]
, model structures on monoidal categories are lifted to categories of coalgebras and comodules. However there is no guarantee that Postnikov presentations are lifted. We present here the notion of
fibrant-compatible
monoidal structure on a model category which ensures that the homotopy category is endowed with a monoidal structure via a right derived tensor product. We also introduce the notion of
weak opmonoidal Quillen equivalences
which provides a compatibility of the right derived tensor products.

The terminal object, if it exists, is always cosmall. In procategories, every object is cosmall.

Example 2.6

A category

Example 2.7

As noted after

In practice, objects in a presentable categories are rarely cosmall.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X(0).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{3}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{1}}

We denote such towers by
\{X(n)\}=(X(n),f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. The limit of the tower is denoted by
\mathsf{lim}_{n}X(n)
.

Proposition 2.14

(
[
GJ99
, VI.1.1]
)
.

Let
\mathsf{M}
be a model category. Then the category of towers
\operatorname{tow}(\mathsf{M})
can be endowed with the Reedy model structure, where a map
\{X(n)\}\rightarrow\{Y(n)\}
is a weak equivalence (respectively a cofibration), if each map

is a weak equivalence (respectively a cofibration) in
\mathsf{M}
, for all

is fibrant if and only if

.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota\colon M}

2.2.
Fibrantly generated model categories

We recall the notion of fibrantly generated as in
[
BHK+15
]
. Our definition of fibrantly generated makes
no assumption of cosmallness
and is not the dual definition of cofibrantly generated model categories.

Definition 2.15
.

A model category is
fibrantly generated by
(\mathsf{P},\mathsf{Q})
if the cofibrations are precisely the morphisms that have the left lifting property with respect to
\mathsf{Q}
, and the acyclic cofibrations are precisely the morphisms that have the left lifting property with respect to
\mathsf{P}
. We call
\mathsf{P}
and
\mathsf{Q}
the
generating fibrations
and
generating acyclic fibrations
respectively.

Remark 2.16
.

Just as for Remark
2.11
, since we allow
\mathsf{P}
and
\mathsf{Q}
to be classes, any model category is trivially fibrantly generated by its fibrations and acyclic fibrations. In practice, it is useful to have a smaller class to study the model structure. We provide example of non-trivial generating fibrations and acyclic fibrations in Theorem
3.4
.

Proposition 2.17
.

If a model category is Postnikov presented by a pair of classes
(\mathsf{P},\mathsf{Q})
, then it is fibrantly generated by
(\mathsf{P},\mathsf{Q})
.

Proof.

The claim is a direct consequence of the retract argument, see
[
Hov99
, 1.1.9]
.
∎

Remark 2.18
.

The converse of Proposition
2.17
is true if
\mathsf{P}
and
\mathsf{Q}
are sets that permit the cosmall object argument.
However, this rarely happens in context of interest as seen in Example
2.7
.

2.3.
Left-induced model categories

We recall now the terminology of
[
HKRS17
,
GKR20
]
. Classically, under some assumptions, given a pair of adjoint functors
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L:\mathsf{M}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{A}:R}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\perp}
where
\mathsf{M}
is endowed with a model structure, we can lift a model structure to
\mathsf{A}
in which weak equivalences and fibrations are created by the right adjoint. See
[
Hir03
, 11.3.2]
.

Definition 2.19
.

Let
\mathsf{M}
be a model category and
\mathsf{A}
be any category, such that there is a pair of adjoint functors:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L:\mathsf{A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{M}:R.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\perp}

We say that the left adjoint
L\colon\mathsf{A}\rightarrow\mathsf{M}

left-induces
a model structure on
\mathsf{A}
if the category
\mathsf{A}
can be endowed with a model structure where a morphism
f
in
\mathsf{A}
is defined to be a cofibration (respectively a weak equivalence) if
L(f)
is a cofibration (respectively a weak equivalence) in
\mathsf{M}
. This model structure on
\mathsf{A}
, if it exists, is called
the left-induced model structure from
\mathsf{M}
.

The following result guarantees that left-inducing from a combinatorial model category gives back a combinatorial model category.

Proposition 2.20

(
[
BHK+15
, 2.23]
,
[
HKRS17
, 3.3.4]
)
.

Suppose
\mathsf{A}
is a model category left-induced by a model category
\mathsf{M}
. Suppose both
\mathsf{A}
and
\mathsf{M}
are presentable. If
\mathsf{M}
is cofibrantly generated by a pair of sets, then
\mathsf{A}
is cofibrantly generated by a pair of sets.

Proposition 2.21

(
[
BHK+15
, 2.18]
)
.

Suppose
\mathsf{A}
is a model category left-induced by a model category
\mathsf{M}
, via an adjunction
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L:\mathsf{A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{M}:R.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\perp}

If
\mathsf{M}
is fibrantly generated by
(\mathsf{P},\mathsf{Q})
, then
\mathsf{A}
is fibrantly generated by
(R(\mathsf{P}),R(\mathsf{Q}))
.

Remark 2.22
.

If
\mathsf{M}
is Postnikov presented by
(\mathsf{P},\mathsf{Q})
, there is no reason to expect that
\mathsf{A}
is Postnikov presented by
(R(\mathsf{P}),R(\mathsf{Q}))
, as we made no assumption of cosmallness in general.

2.4.
Weak opmonoidal Quillen pair

If
(\mathsf{C},\otimes)
is a symmetric monoidal model category, then it endows the associated homotopy category
\mathsf{Ho}(\mathsf{C})
with a symmetric monoidal structure endowed with the derived functor
\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}
, see
[
Hov99
, 4.3.2]
. Essentially, the conditions ensure that the tensor product of two cofibrant objects is cofibrant, and the tensor preserves weak equivalence in both variable. Therefore we can total left derive the bifunctor
-\otimes-:\mathsf{C}\times\mathsf{C}\rightarrow\mathsf{C}
.
If we denote by
\mathsf{C}_{c}
the full subcategory of cofibrant objects in
\mathsf{C}
, then the underlying
\infty
-category
\mathscr{N}(\mathsf{C}_{c})\left[\mathsf{W}^{-1}\right]
is symmetric monoidal via the derived tensor product
\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}
, see
[
Lur17
, 4.1.7.4]
.

We provide dual conditions such that the homotopy category
\mathsf{Ho}(\mathsf{C})
and the underlying
\infty
-category
\mathscr{N}(\mathsf{C}_{f})\left[\mathsf{W}^{-1}\right]
are endowed with a symmetric monoidal structure. We shall weaken the dual definition of
[
Hov99
, 4.2.6]
as follows.

Definition 2.23
.

Given a model category
\mathsf{C}
, we say a symmetric monoidal structure
(\mathsf{M},\otimes,\mathbb{I})
is
fibrant-compatible
if:

(i)

for any fibrant object
X
in
\mathsf{C}
, the functors
X\otimes-:\mathsf{C}\rightarrow\mathsf{C}
and
-\otimes X:\mathsf{C}\rightarrow\mathsf{C}
preserve fibrant objects and weak equivalences between fibrant objects;

(ii)

for any fibrant replacement
\mathbb{I}\rightarrow f\mathbb{I}
of the unit, the induced morphism
X\cong\mathbb{I}\otimes X\rightarrow f\mathbb{I}\otimes X
is a weak equivalence, for any fibrant object
X
.

The requirement
(ii)
is automatic if
\mathbb{I}
is already fibrant

Example 2.24
.

If
\mathsf{C}
is a symmetric monoidal model category
\mathsf{C}
then it is in particular
cofibrant-compatible
, in the sense that
\mathsf{C}^{\mathsf{op}}
is fibrant-compatible. The converse is not true. There may be cofibrant-compatible monoidal structures on model categories that do not form a monoidal model category as
X\otimes-\colon\mathsf{C}\rightarrow\mathsf{C}
needs not to be a left Quillen functor.

Example 2.25
.

Any symmetric monoidal model category
\mathsf{C}
in which each object is fibrant and cofibrant is also fibrant-compatible. For instance
\mathsf{C}=\mathsf{sMod}_{\Bbbk},{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}^{\geq 0}},{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
.

Recall from
[
Lur17
, 2.0.0.1]
that to any symmetric monoidal category
\mathsf{C}
, one can define its operator category
\mathsf{C}^{\otimes}
, such that the nerve
\mathscr{N}(\mathsf{C}^{\otimes})
is a symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category whose underlying
\infty
-category is
\mathscr{N}(\mathsf{C})
, see
[
Lur17
, 2.1.2.21]
. Given
\mathsf{C}
a model category with a fibrant-compatible symmetric monoidal structure and fibrant unit, then
\mathsf{C}_{f}
is a symmetric monoidal category. We can apply
[
Lur17
, 4.1.7.6]
to obtain a symmetric monoidal structure on the homotopy category of
\mathsf{C}
(just as in
[
Hov99
, 4.3.2]
) that is coherent up to higher homotopies.

Proposition 2.26

(
[
Lur17
, 4.1.7.6]
,
[
NS18
, A.7]
)
.

Let
(\mathsf{C},\otimes,\mathbb{I})
be a model category with a fibrant-compatible symmetric monoidal structure. Suppose that
\mathbb{I}
is fibrant.
Then the underlying
\infty
-category
\mathscr{N}(\mathsf{C}_{f})[{\mathsf{W}}^{-1}]
of
\mathsf{C}
can be given the structure of a symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category via the symmetric monoidal localization of
\mathscr{N}(\mathsf{C}_{f}^{\otimes})
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{N}(\mathsf{C}_{f}^{\otimes})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{N}(\mathsf{C}_{f})[{\mathsf{W}}^{-1}]^{\otimes},}

where
{\mathsf{W}}
is the class of weak equivalences restricted to fibrant objects in
\mathsf{C}
.

We can dualize the notion of a weak monoidal Quillen pair from
[
SS03
, 3.6]
.

Definition 2.27
.

Let
(\mathsf{C},\otimes,\mathbb{I})
and
(\mathsf{D},\wedge,\mathbb{J})
model categories with fibrant-compatible symmetric monoidal structures.
A
weak opmonoidal Quillen pair
consists of a Quillen adjunction:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L:(\mathsf{C},\otimes,\mathbb{I})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathsf{D},\wedge,\mathbb{J}):R,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\perp}

where
R
is lax monoidal such that the following two conditions hold.

(i)

For all fibrant objects
X
and
Y
in
\mathsf{D}
, the lax monoidal map:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R(X)\otimes R(Y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R(X\wedge Y),}

is a weak equivalence in
\mathsf{C}
.

(ii)

For some (hence any) fibrant replacement
\lambda\colon\mathbb{J}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}f\mathbb{J}
in
\mathsf{D}
, the composite map

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{I}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R(\mathbb{J})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R(f\mathbb{J})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R(\lambda)}

is a weak equivalence in
\mathsf{C}
.

A weak opmonoidal Quillen pair is a
weak opmonoidal Quillen equivalence
if the underlying Quillen pair is a Quillen equivalence.

Example 2.28
.

Given any weak monoidal Quillen pair:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathsf{C},\otimes,\mathbb{I})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathsf{D},\wedge,\mathbb{J}),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\perp}

their opposite adjunction:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathsf{C}^{\mathsf{op}},\otimes,\mathbb{I})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathsf{D}^{\mathsf{op}},\wedge,\mathbb{J}),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L^{\mathsf{op}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\perp}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R^{\mathsf{op}}}

is a weak opmonoidal Quillen pair.
Because the usual weak monoidal Quillen pair usually requires a monoidal model structure rather than cofibrant-compatible, the converse is not true, but the full assumption of monoidal model structure is not necessary.

Example 2.29
.

The Dold-Kan weak monoidal Quillen equivalences (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) are also weak opmonoidal Quillen equivalences when
k
is a commutative ring with global dimension zero. This follows from the fact that their unit and counit in the adjunctions are isomorphisms.

In general, given a weak opmonoidal Quillen equivalence, we do not guarantee that it lifts to a Quillen equivalence on associated categories of coalgebras and comodules. In other words, we do not provide a dual version of
[
SS03
, 3.12]
. The essential reason being that there is no guarantee in general to obtain a Postnikov presentation on left-induced model structures of coalgebras or comodules such that the limit of the Postnikov towers behaves well with respect to the forgetful functor.

Instead, we show that homotopy coherent (co)algebras and (co)modules are equivalent as
\infty
-categories. In Section
5
, we provide an example of a weak opmonoidal Quillen pair that lifts to the associated categories of comodules.
One can adapt the proof in
[
Pér22
]
to obtain the following result.

Theorem 2.30

(
[
Pér22
, 2.13]
)
.

Let
(\mathsf{C},\otimes,\mathbb{I})
and
(\mathsf{D},\wedge,\mathbb{J})
be model categories with fibrant-compatible symmetric monoidal structures with fibrant units.
Let
\mathsf{W}_{\mathsf{C}}
and
\mathsf{W}_{\mathsf{D}}
be the classes of weak equivalence in
\mathsf{C}
and
\mathsf{D}
respectively.
Let:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L:(\mathsf{C},\otimes,\mathbb{I})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathsf{D},\wedge,\mathbb{J}):R,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\perp}

be a weak opmonoidal Quillen pair.
Then the derived functor of
R:\mathsf{D}\rightarrow\mathsf{C}
induces a symmetric monoidal functor between the underlying
\infty
-categories:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{R}:\mathscr{N}(\mathsf{D}_{f})\left[\mathsf{W}_{\mathsf{D}}^{-1}\right]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{N}(\mathsf{C}_{f})\left[\mathsf{W}_{\mathsf{C}}^{-1}\right],}

where
\mathsf{C}_{f}\subseteq\mathsf{C}
and
\mathsf{D}_{f}\subseteq\mathsf{D}
are the full subcategories of fibrant objects.
If
L
and
R
form a weak opmonoidal Quillen equivalence, then
\mathbb{R}
is a symmetric monoidal equivalence of
\infty
-categories.

3.
Example: Unbounded Chain Complexes

We show here that the category
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
of unbounded chain complexes over
\Bbbk
is fibrantly generated by a pair of sets (Theorem
3.4
) and admits an efficient Postnikov presentation on its model structure (Theorem
3.10
). In particular, this provides new methods to compute homotopy limits in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
(Remark
3.11
) and thus in the
\infty
-category of modules over the Eilenberg-Mac Lane spectrum
H\Bbbk
. The result was proved in
[
Hes09
]
and
[
BHK+15
]
for finitely generated non-negative chain complexes over a field.

3.1.
The generating fibrations

We present here sets of generating fibrations and acyclic fibrations for
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
. A similar result was proved in early unpublished versions of
[
Sor16
]
in
[
Sor10
, 3.1.11, 3.1.12]
for non-negative chain complexes over a field.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
V
be a
\Bbbk
-module. Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Denote the
n
-sphere over
V
by
S^{n}(V)
, the chain complex that is
V
concentrated in degree
n
and zero elsewhere.
Denote the
n
-disk over
V
by
D^{n}(V)
, the chain complex that is
V
concentrated in degree
n-1
and
n
, with differential the identity.
We obtain the epimorphisms
D^{n}(V)\rightarrow S^{n}(V)
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{n}(V)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{n}(V)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots.}

This defines functors:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{n}(-):\mathsf{Mod}_{\Bbbk}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}},}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{n}(-):\mathsf{Mod}_{\Bbbk}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}.}

The map defined above is natural, i.e. we have a natural transformation
D^{n}(-)\Rightarrow S^{n}(-)
, for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
When
V=\Bbbk
, we simply write
D^{n}
and
S^{n}
.
Notice that we have a short exact sequence of chain complexes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{n-1}(V)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{n}(V)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{n}(V)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}

Definition 3.2
.

Define
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{Q}
to be the following

of maps in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{P}=\{D^{n}\longrightarrow S^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}},}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{Q}=\{D^{n}\longrightarrow 0\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}.}

We thicken the sets
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{Q}
to

{\mathcal{P}_{\oplus}}
and
\mathcal{Q}_{\oplus}
of morphisms in

{\mathcal{P}_{\oplus}}:=\Big\{D^{n}(V)\longrightarrow S^{n}(V)\mid V\text{ any $\Bbbk$-module}\Big\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}},

Clearly, the maps in
\mathcal{P}
and

are fibrations in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
and the maps in
\mathcal{Q}
and

{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
.

Remark 3.3
.

When
\Bbbk
is a field, as every
\Bbbk
-module is free, we get:

\mathcal{P}_{\oplus}\cong\left\{\bigoplus_{\lambda}D^{n}\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{\lambda}S^{n}\mid\lambda\text{ any ordinal}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}},

\mathcal{Q}_{\oplus}\cong\left\{\bigoplus_{\lambda}D^{n}\longrightarrow 0\mid\lambda\text{ any ordinal}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}.

Theorem 3.4
.

The model category of unbounded chain complexes
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
is fibrantly generated by the pair of sets
(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q})
.

We prove the above theorem with Lemmas
3.8
and
3.9
below.
Given any chain complex
X
, we denote the
n
-boundaries of
X
by
B_{n}(X)
and the
n
-cycles of
X
by

. Recall the following result.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
X
be a chain complex in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
. Then
X
is split as a chain complex and we have a non-canonical decomposition:

X_{n}\cong H_{n}(X)\oplus B_{n}(X)\oplus B_{n-1}(X).

In particular, any chain complex
X
can be decomposed non-canonically as product of disks and spheres:

X\cong\prod_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}S^{n}(V_{n})\oplus D^{n}(W_{n}),

where
V_{n}=H_{n}(X)
and
W_{n}=B_{n-1}(X)
.

Notation 3.6
.

Given any class of maps
\mathsf{A}
in a category
\mathsf{C}
, we denote by
\mathsf{LLP}(\mathcal{A})
the class of maps in
\mathsf{C}
having the left lifting property with respect to all maps in
\mathsf{A}
.

Lemma 3.7
.

We have the equalities of classes:
\mathsf{LLP}(\mathcal{P}_{\oplus})=\mathsf{LLP}(\mathcal{P})
and
\mathsf{LLP}(\mathcal{Q}_{\oplus})=\mathsf{LLP}(\mathcal{Q})
in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{P}\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{\oplus}
, we get
\mathsf{LLP}(\mathcal{P}_{\oplus})\subseteq\mathsf{LLP}(\mathcal{P})
.
Suppose now
f
is in
\mathsf{LLP}(\mathcal{P})
, let us argue it also belongs in
\mathsf{LLP}(\mathcal{P}_{\oplus})
.
Suppose we have a diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{n}(V)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{n}(V),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

for some
\Bbbk
-module

. Since

is projective, there is another
\Bbbk
-module

such that

is free. Thus
V\oplus W\cong\bigoplus_{\lambda}\Bbbk
, for some basis

.
In particular, the choice of the splitting

induces the commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{n}(V)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\lambda}D^{n}_{\alpha}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle\prod_{\alpha\in\lambda}D^{n}_{\alpha}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{n}_{\alpha}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{n}(V)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\lambda}S^{n}_{\alpha}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle\prod_{\alpha\in\lambda}S^{n}_{\alpha}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{n}_{\alpha},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

and

are a copies of

and

.
Since

is in

, we obtain a lift

, for each

which restricts to

via the retracts (dashed maps in the diagram).
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

Maps in the set
\mathcal{Q}
are the generating acyclic fibrations in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
.

Proof.

Let

be a map in
\mathsf{LLP}(\mathcal{Q})
, let us show it is a cofibration in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
, i.e. a monomorphism.
Following Proposition

, we decompose

X\cong\prod_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}S^{n}(V_{n})\oplus D^{n}(W_{n}).

Then the canonical inclusions

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota\colon\displaystyle\prod_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}S^{n}(V_{n})\oplus D^{n}(W_{n})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle\prod_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}D^{n+1}(V_{n})\oplus D^{n}(W_{n}).}

Since

is in
\mathsf{LLP}(\mathcal{Q})
, then there is a map

. Hence

must be a monomorphism.
∎

Lemma 3.9
.

Maps in the set
\mathcal{P}
are the generating fibrations in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
.

Proof.

Notice that

as any lift

in the following commutative diagram induces the dashed lift:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}

In particular, Lemma

shows that maps in
\mathsf{LLP}(\mathcal{P})
are monomorphisms.
Let

be the map in
\mathsf{LLP}(\mathcal{P})
and let us show it is a quasi-isomorphism. Since

is a monomorphism, there is an induced short exact sequence in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

where
K=\mathsf{coker}(f)
.
It remains to show that
K
is acyclic.
Notice first that
K
is defined as the pushout:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ulcorner}

and so, since

is in
\mathsf{LLP}(\mathcal{P})
, then

is in
\mathsf{LLP}(\mathcal{P})
.
Following Proposition
3.5
, decompose

as:

K\cong\prod_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}S^{n}(V_{n})\oplus D^{n}(W_{n}),

where
V_{n}=H_{n}(K)
.
Then we obtain a map by projection:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle\prod_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}S^{n}(V_{n})\oplus D^{n}(W_{n})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle\prod_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}S^{n}(V_{n}),}

that factors through the non-trivial map:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle\prod_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}D^{n}(V_{n})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle\prod_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}S^{n}(V_{n})}

as

is in
\mathsf{LLP}(\mathcal{P})=\mathsf{LLP}({\mathcal{P}_{\oplus}})
. But this is only possible when

, hence

must be acyclic. Thus

is a quasi-isomorphism.
∎

3.2.
A Postnikov presentation

In Definition
3.2
and Theorem
3.4
, we introduced sets of generating fibrations and acyclic fibrations for
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
. We now show they induce a Postnikov presentation on
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
.

Theorem 3.10
.

The pair
({\mathcal{P}_{\oplus}},\mathcal{Q})
is a Postnikov presentation of the model category of unbounded chain complex
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
.

We shall prove Theorem
3.10
with Lemmas
3.14
and
3.15
below.

Remark 3.11
.

Our arguments in Theorem
3.10
can be generalized to the category
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}^{\mathsf{D}}
, the category of functors
\mathsf{D}\rightarrow{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
, where
\mathsf{D}
is a small category. Indeed, endow
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}^{\mathsf{D}}
with its injective model structure with levelwise weak equivalences and cofibrations (see for instance
[
HKRS17
, 3.4.1]
). Using the same notation as
[
BHK+15
, 4.10]
, the pair
({\mathcal{P}_{\oplus}}\pitchfork\mathsf{D},\mathcal{Q}\pitchfork\mathsf{D})
is a Postnikov presentation for
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}^{\mathsf{D}}
. This provides inductive arguments to compute homotopy limits in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
.

Remark 3.12
.

We were not able to restrict ourselves to the set
\mathcal{P}
and had to consider the class
{\mathcal{P}_{\oplus}}
. We note here a few basic results.

(i)

As
\mathcal{P}\subseteq{\mathcal{P}_{\oplus}}
, we get
\mathsf{Post}_{\mathcal{P}}\subseteq\mathsf{Post}_{\mathcal{P}_{\oplus}}
.

(ii)

The maps

are in

as they are obtained as pullbacks:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{n+1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{n+1}.}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

Similarly, for any
\Bbbk
-module

, the maps

are in
\mathsf{Post}_{\mathcal{P}_{\oplus}}
.

(iii)

Since

is the composite

,
we see that

, and thus
\mathsf{Post}_{\mathcal{Q}}\subseteq\mathsf{Post}_{\mathcal{P}}\subseteq\mathsf{Post}_{\mathcal{P}_{\oplus}}
by Proposition

.

(iv)

Although

, we have

(see Notation

). Indeed, for any
\Bbbk
-module

, any map

is the retract of a map

where

is a free
\Bbbk
-module. Then, for

a basis of

, we have the retract in

:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\lambda}D^{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle\prod_{\lambda}D^{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\lambda}D^{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\lambda}S^{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle\prod_{\lambda}S^{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\lambda}S^{n},}

induced by the split short exact sequence in
\Bbbk
-modules:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\lambda}\Bbbk}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle\prod_{\lambda}\Bbbk}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{coker}(\iota)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}

where

is the natural monomorphism.

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
X
be any chain complex over
\Bbbk
. Then the trivial map

is a
{\mathcal{P}_{\oplus}}
-Postnikov tower. If

is acyclic, the trivial map is a

-Postnikov tower.

Proof.

Follows from Proposition
3.5
,
(ii)
of Remark

.
∎

Lemma 3.14
.

Every acyclic fibration in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
is a retract of a
\mathcal{Q}
-Postnikov tower. Every map in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
factors as a cofibration followed by a
\mathcal{Q}
-Postnikov tower.

Proof.

We provide two proofs by presenting two different factorizations. The first one has the advantage to be functorial but harder to compute. The second is not functorial but is easier to compute.
Let us do the first possible factorization. By Theorem
3.4
, the set
\mathcal{Q}
of maps in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
is the set of generating acyclic fibrations. Their codomain is the terminal object in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
and is thus cosmall. We can then apply the cosmall object argument (Proposition
2.8
) to obtain the desired factorization.

For the second possible factorization, start with any morphism
f\colon X\rightarrow Y
in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
.
Choose a decomposition of
X
by using Proposition
3.5
:

X\cong\prod_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}S^{n}(V_{n})\oplus D^{n}(W_{n})
,
for some collection of
\Bbbk
-modules
V_{n}
and
W_{n}
.
The inclusions
S^{n}(V_{n})\hookrightarrow D^{n+1}(V_{n})
define then a monomorphism in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X\cong\displaystyle\prod_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}S^{n}(V_{n})\oplus D^{n}(W_{n})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle\prod_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}D^{n+1}(V_{n})\oplus D^{n}(W_{n}).}

Since
V_{n}
and
W_{n}
are projective
\Bbbk
-modules, they can be embedded into free
\Bbbk
-modules, say
F_{n}
and
G_{n}
, with basis
\lambda_{n}
and
\gamma_{n}
.
Then we obtain the following monomorphisms in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle\prod_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}D^{n+1}(V_{n})\oplus D^{n}(W_{n})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle\prod_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigoplus_{\lambda_{n}}D^{n+1}\oplus\bigoplus_{\gamma_{n}}D^{n}.}

Thus, we get the monomorphism in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle\prod_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\prod_{\lambda_{n},\gamma_{n}}D^{n+1}\oplus D^{n}.}

Denote by

the acyclic chain complex
\prod_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\prod_{\lambda_{n},\gamma_{n}}D^{n+1}\oplus D^{n}
.
We obtain the desired second factorization in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z\oplus Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota\oplus f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}

where the map

is the projection onto

, which is indeed a
\mathcal{Q}
-Postnikov tower by Proposition
2.2
.
∎

Lemma 3.15
.

Every fibration in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
is a retract of a
{\mathcal{P}_{\oplus}}
-Postnikov tower and every map in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}

Remark 3.16
.

Unlike Lemma
3.14
, we cannot use the cosmall object argument in order to prove Lemma
3.15
.
Indeed, as noted in
[
Sor10
]
, the codomains
S^{n}
of maps in the set
\mathcal{P}
are not cosmall relative to
\mathsf{Post}_{\mathcal{P}}
. Indeed, let
Y_{k}=S^{n}
for all
k\geq 0
and
Y_{k+1}\rightarrow Y_{k}
be the zero maps. Let
Y=\underset{k\geq 0}{\mathsf{lim}}Y_{k}
be the limit in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
. The set map:

\underset{k\geq 0}{\mathsf{colim}}\left(\mathsf{Hom}_{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}(Y_{k},S^{n})\right)\longrightarrow\mathsf{Hom}_{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}(Y,S^{n})

is not a bijection. Indeed, the map is equivalent to the map:

which is never a bijection.
A similar argument can be applied to show that the codomains

of the maps in the class
{\mathcal{P}_{\oplus}}
are not cosmall relative to
\mathsf{Post}_{\mathcal{P}_{\oplus}}
, for any
\Bbbk
-module

.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0,}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W^{+}(2)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W^{+}(1)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W^{+}(0)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{+}(2)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{+}(1)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{+}(0)}

where each
p^{+}(n)
is in
\mathsf{Post}_{\mathcal{P}_{\oplus}}
. The map
j^{+}\colon X\rightarrow W^{+}
is induced by the monomorphisms
j^{+}(n)\colon X\rightarrow W^{+}(n)
which are compatible with the tower:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W^{+}(n)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W^{+}(n-1),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{+}(n)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j^{+}(n)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j^{+}(n-1)}

and
j^{+}(n)
induces an isomorphism in homology in degrees
i
, for
0\leq i\leq n
, and a monomorphism otherwise.
We construct the chain complexes
W^{+}(n)
of the tower inductively as follows.

•

For the initial step, apply Lemma
3.18
to the monomorphism
j\colon X\rightarrow W
, for
n=0
. Write
W^{+}(0)=W(0)
. The cofibration
j^{+}(0)
defined as the chain map

j(0)\colon X\longrightarrow W(0)=W^{+}(0)

is an isomorphism in homology in degree
0
, and a monomorphism in other degrees.
The chain map
p^{+}(0)
defined as the map:

p(0)\colon W^{+}(0)=W(0)\longrightarrow W,

is a
{\mathcal{P}_{\oplus}}
-Postnikov tower.

•

For the inductive step, suppose, for a fixed integer
n\geq 0
, the chain complex
W^{+}(n)
is defined, together with a cofibration
j^{+}(n)\colon X\rightarrow W^{+}(n)
inducing an isomorphism in homology for degrees
i
, where
0\leq i\leq n
, and a monomorphism in homology for other degrees.
Apply Lemma
3.18
to the monomorphism
j^{+}(n)
for the degree
n+1
. Write
W^{+}(n+1):=(W^{+}(n))(n+1)
.
The cofibration
j^{+}(n+1)
defined as the chain map:

(j^{+}(n))(n+1)\colon X\longrightarrow W^{+}(n+1),

is an isomorphism in homology in degrees
i
where
0\leq i\leq n+1
, and a monomorphism in other degrees. We obtain a
{\mathcal{P}_{\oplus}}
-Postnikov tower
p^{+}(n+1)\colon W^{+}(n+1)\rightarrow W^{+}(n)

such that the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W^{+}(n)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W^{+}(n+1).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j^{+}(n)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j^{+}(n+1)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{+}(n+1)}

The induced map
j^{+}\colon X\rightarrow W^{+}
is a monomorphism of chain complexes. Indeed, for any fixed
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have:

{\left(W^{+}(i+1)\right)}_{i}\cong{\left(W^{+}(i+2)\right)}_{i}\cong{\left(W^{+}(i+3)\right)}_{i}\cong\cdots.

Thus
\left(W^{+}\right)_{i}\cong{\left(W^{+}(i+1)\right)}_{i}
.
Therefore the linear map

is the linear map:

{\left(j^{+}(n+1)\right)}_{i}\colon X_{i}\longrightarrow{\left(W^{+}(i+1)\right)}_{i},

which is a monomorphism. Similarly, we get:

H_{i}(W^{+})\cong H_{i}(W^{+}(i+1)),

for all

, and so
j^{+}
is a monomorphism in negative degrees in homology, and an isomorphism in homology in non-negative degrees.

The last step is to replace the map
j^{+}\colon X\rightarrow W^{+}
by the desired chain map
\widetilde{j}:X\rightarrow\widetilde{W}
that is an acyclic cofibration. We construct
\widetilde{W}
similarly as

(inductively applying Lemma
3.18
) but in negative degrees.
We build
\widetilde{W}
as the limit
\underset{n\geq 0}{\mathsf{lim}}(W^{-}(-n))
in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}}
of the tower of maps:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W^{+}(-2)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W^{-}(-1)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W^{-}(0)=W^{+},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{-}(-2)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{-}(-1)}

where each
p^{-}(n)
is in
\mathsf{Post}_{\mathcal{P}_{\oplus}}
. The map
\widetilde{j}\colon X\rightarrow\widetilde{W}
is induced by the monomorphisms
j^{-}(-n)\colon X\rightarrow W^{-}(-n)
which are compatible with the tower:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W^{-}(-n)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W^{-}(-(n-1)),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{-}(-n)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j^{-}(-n)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j^{-}(-(n-1))}

and

induces an isomorphism in homology in degrees
i
, for

, and a monomorphism otherwise. Similarly as the positive case, the map

can be shown to be a monomorphism and quasi-isomorphism, hence an acyclic cofibration, as desired.
∎

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}^{\geq 0}}}

4.2.
Postnikov presentations

We now introduce a Postnikov presentation for comodules in
\mathsf{sMod}_{\Bbbk}
and
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}^{\geq 0}}
. We follow the approach of
[
Hes09
]
in which the finitely generated case was treated. See also
[
Nei78
]
. We first need to restrict the coalgebras considered.

Definition 4.9
.

Let
R
be any commutative ring.
A chain complex
X
over
R
is
simply connected
if:
X_{0}=R
,
X_{1}=0
and
X_{i}=0
for all
i<0
. A simplicial
R
-module
X
is
simply connected
if
X_{0}=R
and there are no non-degenerate
1
-simplices.

Over a commutative ring with global dimension zero
\Bbbk
, every simply connected simplicial modules or chain complexes is weakly equivalent to a simply connected object in the usual sense.

Remark 4.10
.

A simplicial
R
-module
X
is simply connected if and only if
\mathsf{N}(X)
is a simply connected chain complex.
A chain complex
X
over
R
is simply connected if and only if
\Gamma(X)
is a simply connected simplicial module.

For any chain complex
C
and any
\Bbbk
-module
V
, we see that the
i
-th term of the chain complex
S^{n}(V)\otimes C
is the
\Bbbk
-module
V\otimes C_{i-n}
.
If we choose
C
to be a simply connected differential graded
\Bbbk
-coalgebra, we get:

\left(S^{n}(V)\otimes C\right)_{i}=\left\{\begin{tabular}[]{ll}$0$&$i<n$,\\
$V$&$i=n$,\\
$0$&$i=n+1$,\\
$V\otimes C_{i-n}$&$i\geq n+2$.\end{tabular}\right.

Thus, around the
n
-th term, the chain complex
S^{n}(V)\otimes C
is similar to
S^{n}(V)
.
We can therefore modify the homology of a
C
-comodule for a specific degree without modifying the lower degrees. Therefore the arguments of previous section can be applied. As pullbacks in comodules are computed in the underlying category in our cases, we can adapt Lemma
3.17
to obtain its following coalgebraic version.

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
\Bbbk
be a commutative ring with global dimension zero.
Let
C
be a simply connected coalgebra in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}^{\geq 0}}
.
Let
X
be any object in
\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}({\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}^{\geq 0}})
. Let
V
be any
\Bbbk
-module. Let
n\geq 1
be any integer.
Given a surjective linear map
f_{n}\colon X_{n}\rightarrow V
non-trivial only on
n
th-homology in a splitting
X_{n}\cong H_{n}(X)\oplus B_{n}(X)\oplus B_{n-1}(X)
, there is a
C
-comodule map
f:X\rightarrow S^{n}(V)\otimes C
, and the pullback comodule
P
in the following diagram in
\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}({\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}^{\geq 0}})
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{n}(V)\otimes C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{n}(V)\otimes C,}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

has homology:

H_{i}(P)\cong\left\{\begin{tabular}[]{ll}$\mathsf{ker}\left(H_{n}(f)\right)$&$i=n$,\\
$H_{i}(X)$&$i<n$,\end{tabular}\right.

and we have
P_{i}=X_{i}
for
0\leq i<n-1
and
i=n
, and
P_{n-1}=X_{n-1}\oplus V
.

A similar argument can be applied in the simplicial case and we can thus also fix the homotopy, layer by layer, in the comodule context.
In order to obtain a coalgebraic variation of Lemma
3.15
, we need to understand towers.
The limit
\mathsf{lim}^{C}_{n}X_{n}
of a tower
\{X(n)\}
in
\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}(\mathsf{M})
is usually not equivalent to the limit
\mathsf{lim}_{n}U(X_{n})
in
\mathsf{M}
.

Definition 4.12
.

Let
\mathsf{M}
be either
\mathsf{sMod}_{\Bbbk}
or
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}^{\geq 0}}
.
A tower
\{X(n)\}
in
\mathsf{M}

stabilizes in each degree
if for each degree
i\geq 0
, the tower
\{X(n)_{i}\}
of
\Bbbk
-modules stabilizes for
n\geq i+1
, i.e., for all
n\geq 0
, and all
0\leq i\leq n
, we have that the map appearing in the tower induce isomorphisms:

X(n+1)_{i}\cong X(n+2)_{i}\cong X(n+3)_{i}\cong\cdots.

Let
C
be a coalgebra in
\mathsf{M}
.
A tower
\{X(n)\}
in
\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}(\mathsf{M})

stabilizes in each degree
if the underlying tower
\{U(X(n))\}
in
\mathsf{M}
stabilizes in each degree.

Remark 4.13
.

A tower
\{X(n)\}
in
\mathsf{sMod}_{\Bbbk}
stabilizes in each degree if and only if
\{\mathsf{N}(X(n))\}
in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}^{\geq 0}}
stabilizes in each degree.
A tower
\{X(n)\}
in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}^{\geq 0}}
stabilizes in each degree if and only if
\{\Gamma(X(n))\}
in
\mathsf{sMod}_{\Bbbk}
stabilizes in each degree.

Lemma 4.14
.

Let
\mathsf{M}
be either
\mathsf{sMod}_{\Bbbk}
or
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}^{\geq 0}}
.
Let
\{X(n)\}
be a tower in
\mathsf{M}
that stabilizes in each degree. Let
C
be any object
\mathsf{M}
.
Then the tower
\Big\{X(n)\otimes C\Big\}
in
\mathsf{M}
also stabilizes in each degree and we have:

\Big(\mathsf{lim}_{n}X(n)\Big)\otimes C\cong\mathsf{lim}_{n}\Big(X(n)\otimes C\Big).

Proof.

We prove only the case
\mathsf{M}={\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}^{\geq 0}}
.
For all
n\geq 0
, and all
0\leq i\leq n
, we have:

\displaystyle\Big(X(n+1)\otimes C\Big)_{i}

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{a+b=i}X(n+1)_{a}\otimes C_{b}

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{a+b=i}X(n+2)_{a}\otimes C_{b}

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\Big(X(n+2)\otimes C\Big)_{i},

as
0\leq a\leq i\leq n
. This argument generalizes in higher degrees and thus shows that the desired tower stabilizes in each degree.
For all
i\geq 0
, notice that both
\Big(\Big(\mathsf{lim}_{n}X(n)\Big)\otimes C\Big)_{i}
and
\Big(\mathsf{lim}_{n}\Big(X(n)\otimes C\Big)\Big)_{i}
are isomorphic to
\displaystyle\bigoplus_{a+b=i}X(i+1)_{a}\otimes C_{b}
.
∎

Corollary 4.15
.

Let
\mathsf{M}
be either
\mathsf{sMod}_{\Bbbk}
or
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}^{\geq 0}}
.
Let
C
be a coalgebra in
\mathsf{M}
.
Let
\{X(n)\}
be a tower in
\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}(\mathsf{M})
that stabilizes in each degree.
Then the natural map:

U(\mathsf{lim}^{C}_{n}X(n))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathsf{lim}_{n}U(X(n))

is an isomorphism in
\mathsf{M}
.

Proof.

This follows directly from Lemma
4.14
as
U
preserves and reflects a limit precisely when the comonad
-\otimes C:\mathsf{M}\rightarrow\mathsf{M}
preserves that limit.
In detail, if we write
X:=\mathsf{lim}_{n}U(X(n))
, then the coaction
X\rightarrow X\otimes C
is constructed as follows. For each degree
i\geq 0
, the map
X_{i}\rightarrow(X\otimes C)_{i}
is entirely determined by the coaction
X(i+1)\rightarrow X(i+1)\otimes C
.
∎

Therefore, we can do a coalgebraic variation of Lemma
3.15
as the towers stabilize in each degree. Therefore we obtain the following result.

Theorem 4.16
.

Let
\Bbbk
be commutative ring with global dimension zero.

(i)

4.3.
A fibrant-compatible monoidal structure

We define a symmetric monoidal structure on comodules. For simplicity, we work over cocommutative coalgebras, but one can work instead with non-cocommutative coalgebra and obtain a non-symmetric monoidal structure on bicomodules over the coalgebra.

.

Let
\mathsf{M}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X{{\square}_{C}}Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X\otimes Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X\otimes C\otimes Y,}

where the two parallel morphisms are induced by the coactions

and

In general, the functor

Then the result follows from Lemma

5.
Application: Dold-Kan Correspondence For Comodules

We prove here in Theorem
5.1
that the Dold-Kan correspondence lifts to a Quillen equivalence on the categories of (right) comodules, over a simply connected coalgebra.
Moreover, we show in Theorem
5.3
that the derived cotensor products induced by Theorem

are equivalent. A crucial tool in our arguments is the Postnikov tower (Definition

) as a fibrant replacement for our model categories of comodules. As a consequence, we obtain a new description of rational

5.1.
Lifting the Dold-Kan correspondence

We dualize the construction appearing in
[
SS03
, 3.3]
.
Let

Let

be a coalgebra in
\mathsf{C}

However, if we suppose

to only be lax monoidal, then

does not lift to comodules.
Suppose equalizers exist.
We define the correct right adjoint

on comodules by the following commutative diagram of adjoints:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}(\mathsf{C})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{CoMod}_{L(C)}(\mathsf{D}),}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{D}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\perp}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\vdash}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R^{C}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\vdash}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\perp}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces-\otimes C}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces-\wedge L(C)}

We have omitted the forgetful functor

Remark 5.2

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

We shall only prove the statements for the adjunction (
5.1
). The other one has an entirely similar proof.

Since the functor
\Gamma\colon{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}^{\geq 0}}\rightarrow\mathsf{sMod}_{\Bbbk}
is left Quillen, and cofibrations and weak equivalences are determined by the underlying categories, the left adjoint functor
\Gamma\colon\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}({\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}^{\geq 0}})\rightarrow\mathsf{CoMod}_{\Gamma(C)}(\mathsf{sMod}_{\Bbbk})
is also left Quillen.
It also reflects weak equivalences between (cofibrant) objects.
Thus, by
[
Hov99
, 1.3.16]
we only need to show that the counit:

\Gamma\mathsf{N}^{C}(X)\longrightarrow X,

is a weak homotopy equivalence, for any fibrant
\Gamma(C)
-comodule
X
.

For this matter, let
\{X(n)\}
be the Postnikov tower of
X
, and denote by
\widetilde{X}
the (homotopy) limit of the tower, see Corollary
4.17
.
Then
X
is a retract of
\widetilde{X}
and thus we only need to show the weak homotopy equivalence when applied on
\widetilde{X}
.
We prove the claim by induction on the tower.
Clearly the map
\Gamma\mathsf{N}^{C}(X(0))\longrightarrow X(0)
is a weak homotopy equivalence as it is the trivial map.

Suppose
X
is a cofree comodule

, for

\mathsf{sMod}_{\Bbbk}
.
Then the formula:

\mathsf{N}^{C}(M\otimes\Gamma(C))=\mathsf{N}(M)\otimes C,

combined with the weak homotopy equivalence:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma(\mathsf{N}(M)\otimes C)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma(\mathsf{N}(M))\otimes\Gamma(C)\cong M\otimes\Gamma(C),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}

induced by the fact that
\Gamma\colon{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}^{\geq 0}}\rightarrow\mathsf{sMod}_{\Bbbk}
is part of a weak monoidal Quillen equivalence shows that
\Gamma\mathsf{N}^{C}(M\otimes\Gamma(C))\longrightarrow M\otimes\Gamma(C)
is a weak homotopy equivalence.
In particular, we obtain that
\Gamma\mathsf{N}^{C}(X(1))\longrightarrow X(1)
is a weak homotopy equivalence.

Now suppose we have shown the map
\Gamma\mathsf{N}^{C}(X(n))\longrightarrow X(n)
is a weak homotopy equivalence for some

.
Since

is the homotopy pullback in

(and in

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X(n+1)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P\otimes\Gamma(C)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X(n)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Q\otimes\Gamma(C),}

for some epimorphism

in
\mathsf{sMod}_{\Bbbk}
,

is a right Quillen functor, it preserves homotopy pullbacks and thus we get the following homotopy pullback in

(and in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}^{\geq 0}}
):

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{N}^{C}(X(n+1))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{N}(P)\otimes C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{N}^{C}(X(n))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{N}(Q)\otimes C,}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

as
\mathsf{N}
preserves fibrations.
In particular, notice that the induced maps

are fibrations in
\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}({\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}^{\geq 0}})
and
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}^{\geq 0}}
. Moreover, the tower
\{\mathsf{N}^{C}(X(n))\}
stabilizes in each degree (as it is the equalizer of towers that stabilize in each degree by Lemma

). Thus the homotopy limits of

in
\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}({\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}^{\geq 0}})
and in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}^{\geq 0}}
are equivalent to
\mathsf{N}^{C}(\widetilde{X})
.

Consider the above pullback in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}^{\geq 0}}
, then as
\Gamma\colon{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}^{\geq 0}}\rightarrow\mathsf{sMod}_{\Bbbk}
is a right Quillen functor, we obtain the following homotopy pullback in
\mathsf{sMod}_{\Bbbk}
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma\mathsf{N}^{C}(X(n+1))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma(\mathsf{N}(P)\otimes C)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma\mathsf{N}^{C}(X(n))}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P\otimes\Gamma(C)}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

\displaystyle\simeq

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma(X{{\square}_{C}}Y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma(X\otimes Y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma(X\otimes C\otimes Y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma(X)\square_{\Gamma(C)}\Gamma(Y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma(X)\otimes\Gamma(Y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma(X)\otimes\Gamma(C)\otimes\Gamma(Y).}

Thus
\Gamma
lifts to a functor
\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}({\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}^{\geq 0}})\rightarrow\mathsf{CoMod}_{\Gamma(C)}(\mathsf{sMod}_{\Bbbk})
that is oplax symmetric monoidal with respect to their cotensor products.

Recall that we denote by
\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}(\Bbbk)
the
\infty
-category of connective comodules over the Eilenberg-Mac Lane spectrum
H\Bbbk
. It is equivalent to the underlying
\infty
-categories of
\mathsf{sMod}_{\Bbbk}
and
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}^{\geq 0}}
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\Bbbk
be a commutative ring with global dimension zero.
Let
C
be a simply connected cocommutative coalgebra in
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}^{\geq 0}}
.
Then the induced adjunction:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}({\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}^{\geq 0}}),{{\square}_{C}},C)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathsf{CoMod}_{\Gamma(C)}(\mathsf{sMod}_{\Bbbk}),\square_{\Gamma(C)},\Gamma(C)),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{N}^{C}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\perp}

is a weak opmonoidal Quillen equivalence. In particular, we obtain an equivalence of symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories:

\mathcal{CoMod}_{C}(\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}(\Bbbk))\simeq\mathcal{CoMod}_{\Gamma(C)}(\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}(\Bbbk)),

with respect to their derived cotensor product of comodules.

Remark 5.4
.

From the adjunction (
1.2
), we can endow
\mathsf{N}
with a oplax monoidal left adjoint structure and repeat our above arguments. However, if
D
is a cocommutative coalgebra in
\mathsf{sMod}_{\Bbbk}
, then there is no hope that
\mathsf{N}(D)
is also cocommutative. Thus
\mathsf{CoMod}_{\mathsf{N}(D)}({\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}^{\geq 0}})
is not a symmetric monoidal category. Thus we cannot promote the adjunction (
5.2
) of Theorem
5.1
to be a weak opmonoidal Quillen equivalence. Alternatively, we can consider bicomodules over non-cocommutative coalgebras
D
and
\mathsf{N}(D)
. In this case we obtain by similar arguments a weak opmonoidal (but not symmetric) equivalence. For simplicity, we do not give details but the proof is entirely similar as of Theorem
5.3
.

Proof of Theorem
5.3
.

We have
\mathsf{N}^{C}(\Gamma(C))\cong C
, thus we only need to show that the lax monoidal map:

\mathsf{N}^{C}(X){{\square}_{C}}\mathsf{N}^{C}(Y)\longrightarrow\mathsf{N}^{C}(X\square_{\Gamma(C)}Y),

is a weak homotopy equivalence for
X
and
Y
fibrant
\Gamma(C)
-comodules.
Let
\{X(n)\}
be the Postnikov tower of
X
as in Definition
4.18
. Let
\widetilde{X}
be its (homotopy) limit. It is enough to show that:

\mathsf{N}^{C}(\widetilde{X}){{\square}_{C}}\mathsf{N}^{C}(Y)\longrightarrow\mathsf{N}^{C}(\widetilde{X}\square_{\Gamma(C)}Y),

is a weak homotopy equivalence.

We show inductively that
\mathsf{N}^{C}(X(n)){{\square}_{C}}\mathsf{N}^{C}(Y)\rightarrow\mathsf{N}^{C}(X(n)\square_{\Gamma(C)}Y)
is a weak homotopy equivalence.
For
n=0
the case is vacuous as the map is trivial.
For
n=1
, then
X(1)
is a cofree
\Gamma(C)
-comodule
M\otimes\Gamma(C)
, for some
M
in
\mathsf{sMod}_{\Bbbk}
.
Then from the formula
\mathsf{N}^{C}(M\otimes\Gamma(C))\cong\mathsf{N}(M)\otimes C
, we get:

\mathsf{N}^{C}(M\otimes\Gamma(C)){{\square}_{C}}\mathsf{N}^{C}(Y)\cong\mathsf{N}(M)\otimes\mathsf{N}^{C}(Y).

Thus we need to show that the natural map
\mathsf{N}(M)\otimes\mathsf{N}^{C}(Y)\rightarrow\mathsf{N}^{C}(M\otimes Y)
is a weak homotopy equivalence.

We show this by using a Postnikov argument on
Y
. Let
\{Y(n)\}
be its Postnikov tower with (homotopy) limit
\widetilde{Y}
. We need to show that
\mathsf{N}(M)\otimes\mathsf{N}^{C}(\widetilde{Y})\rightarrow\mathsf{N}^{C}(M\otimes\widetilde{Y})
is a weak homotopy equivalence.
Let us first show that
\mathsf{N}(M)\otimes\mathsf{N}^{C}(Y(n))\rightarrow\mathsf{N}^{C}(M\otimes Y(n))
, by induction.
For
n=0
the case is vacuous.
For
n=1
, we have that
Y(1)
is equivalent to
M^{\prime}\otimes\Gamma(C)
for some
M^{\prime}
in
\mathsf{sMod}_{\Bbbk}
. Then the map:

\mathsf{N}(M)\otimes\mathsf{N}^{C}(M^{\prime}\otimes\Gamma(C))\rightarrow\mathsf{N}^{C}(M\otimes M^{\prime}\otimes\Gamma(C)),

becomes simply the map
\mathsf{N}(M)\otimes\mathsf{N}(M^{\prime})\otimes C\rightarrow\mathsf{N}(M\otimes M^{\prime})\otimes C
. It is a weak homotopy equivalence as
\mathsf{N}(M)\otimes\mathsf{N}(M^{\prime})\rightarrow\mathsf{N}(M\otimes M^{\prime})
is a weak homotopy equivalence as
\mathsf{N}
is part of a weak monoidal Quillen adjunction.
Now suppose that we have shown
\mathsf{N}(M)\otimes\mathsf{N}^{C}(Y(n))\rightarrow\mathsf{N}^{C}(M\otimes Y(n))
is a weak homotopy equivalence for some
n\geq 1
.
Recall that
Y(n+1)
can be described as a homotopy pullback both in
\mathsf{CoMod}_{\Gamma(C)}(\mathsf{sMod}_{\Bbbk})
and in
\mathsf{sMod}_{\Bbbk}
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y(n+1)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P\otimes\Gamma(C)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y(n)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Q\otimes\Gamma(C),}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

for some epimorphism
P\rightarrow Q
in
\mathsf{sMod}_{\Bbbk}
. Then as the functors
\mathsf{N}\colon\mathsf{sMod}_{\Bbbk}\rightarrow{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}^{\geq 0}}
,
M\otimes-\colon\mathsf{sMod}_{\Bbbk}\rightarrow\mathsf{sMod}_{\Bbbk}
,
\mathsf{N}(M)\otimes-\colon{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}^{\geq 0}}\rightarrow{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}^{\geq 0}}
and
\mathsf{N}^{C}\colon\mathsf{CoMod}_{\Gamma(C)}(\mathsf{sMod}_{\Bbbk})\rightarrow\mathsf{CoMod}_{C}({\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}^{\geq 0}})
preserve homotopy pullbacks, we obtain the following two homotopy pullbacks in
\mathsf{sMod}_{\Bbbk}
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{N}(M)\otimes\mathsf{N}^{C}(Y(n+1))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{N}(M)\otimes\mathsf{N}(P)\otimes C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{N}(M)\otimes\mathsf{N}^{C}(Y(n))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{N}(M)\otimes\mathsf{N}(Q)\otimes C,}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

and:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{N}^{C}(M\otimes Y(n+1))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{N}(M\otimes P)\otimes C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{N}^{C}(M\otimes Y(n))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{N}(M\otimes Q)\otimes C.}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

Then by induction and our above argument on cofree objects, we obtain by
[
Hov99
, 5.2.6]
that
\mathsf{N}(M)\otimes\mathsf{N}^{C}(Y(n+1))\rightarrow\mathsf{N}^{C}(M\otimes Y(n+1))
is a weak homotopy equivalence.
Now we can conclude as:

\displaystyle\mathsf{N}(M)\otimes\mathsf{N}^{C}(\widetilde{Y})

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\mathsf{N}(M)\otimes\mathsf{N}^{C}(\mathsf{holim}^{\Gamma(C)}_{n}Y(n))

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\mathsf{N}(M)\otimes\mathsf{holim}^{C}_{n}\mathsf{N}^{C}(Y(n))

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\mathsf{holim}_{n}^{C}(\mathsf{N}(M)\otimes\mathsf{N}^{C}(Y(n))

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\mathsf{holim}_{n}^{C}(\mathsf{N}^{C}(M\otimes Y(n))

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\mathsf{N}^{C}(\mathsf{holim}_{n}^{\Gamma(C)}(M\otimes Y(n)))

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\mathsf{N}^{C}(M\otimes\widetilde{Y}).

We have used the fact that
\mathsf{N}^{C}
and
M\otimes-
and
\mathsf{N}(M)\otimes-
preserve towers that stabilize in each degree and they remain fibrant in the sense of Proposition
2.14
.

We have just shown
\mathsf{N}^{C}(X(1)){{\square}_{C}}\mathsf{N}^{C}(Y)\rightarrow\mathsf{N}^{C}(X(1)\square_{\Gamma(C)}Y)
is a weak homotopy equivalence. Let us show the higher cases by induction.
Recall that
X(n+1)
is determined as a homotopy pullback in
\mathsf{CoMod}_{\Gamma(C)}(\mathsf{sMod}_{\Bbbk})
and in
\mathsf{sMod}_{\Bbbk}
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X(n+1)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P^{\prime}\otimes\Gamma(C)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X(n)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Q^{\prime}\otimes\Gamma(C),}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

for some epimorphism
P^{\prime}\rightarrow Q^{\prime}
in
\mathsf{sMod}_{\Bbbk}
. Since
\mathsf{N}^{C}
and
-{{\square}_{C}}\mathsf{N}^{C}(Y)
preserves fibrations that are epimorphisms and pullbacks, we obtain the following two homotopy pullbacks in
\mathsf{CoMod}_{\Gamma(C)}(\mathsf{sMod}_{\Bbbk})
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{N}^{C}(X(n+1)){{\square}_{C}}\mathsf{N}^{C}(Y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{N}(P^{\prime})\otimes\mathsf{N}^{C}(Y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{N}^{C}(X(n)){{\square}_{C}}\mathsf{N}^{C}(Y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{N}(Q^{\prime})\otimes\mathsf{N}^{C}(Y),}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

and:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{N}^{C}(X(n+1)\square_{\Gamma(C)}Y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{N}^{C}(P^{\prime}\otimes Y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{N}^{C}(X(n)\square_{\Gamma(C)}Y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{N}^{C}(Q^{\prime}\otimes Y).}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

By induction and our previous argument, this shows
\mathsf{N}^{C}(X(n+1)){{\square}_{C}}\mathsf{N}^{C}(Y)\rightarrow\mathsf{N}^{C}(X(n+1)\square_{\Gamma(C)}Y)

is a weak homotopy equivalence.

Since
\mathsf{N}^{C}
preserves towers that stabilize in each degree and by Lemma
4.21
, we can conclude by the following string of weak homotopy equivalences:

\displaystyle\mathsf{N}^{C}(\widetilde{X}){{\square}_{C}}\mathsf{N}^{C}(Y)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\mathsf{N}^{C}(\mathsf{holim}^{\Gamma(C)}_{n}X(n)){{\square}_{C}}\mathsf{N}^{C}(Y)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle(\mathsf{holim}_{n}^{C}\mathsf{N}^{C}(X(n))){{\square}_{C}}\mathsf{N}^{C}(Y)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\mathsf{holim}_{n}^{C}(\mathsf{N}^{C}(X(n)){{\square}_{C}}\mathsf{N}^{C}(Y))

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\mathsf{holim}_{n}^{C}\mathsf{N}^{C}(X(n)\square_{\Gamma(C)}Y)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\mathsf{N}^{C}(\mathsf{holim}_{n}^{\Gamma(C)}(X(n)\square_{\Gamma(C)}Y)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\mathsf{N}^{C}((\mathsf{holim}_{n}^{\Gamma(C)}X(n))\square_{\Gamma(C)}Y)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\mathsf{N}^{C}(\widetilde{X}\square_{\Gamma(C)}Y).

Thus
\mathsf{N}^{C}(\widetilde{X}){{\square}_{C}}\mathsf{N}^{C}(Y)\longrightarrow\mathsf{N}^{C}(\widetilde{X}\square_{\Gamma(C)}Y)
is a weak homotopy equivalence.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

There is an alternative Dold-Kan correspondence for comodules. Let us dualize another construction from
[
SS03
]
. Let
\mathsf{CoCAlg}(\mathsf{C})
denote the category of cocommutative coalgebras in a symmetric monoidal category
\mathsf{C}
.
Let
L\colon(\mathsf{C},\otimes,\mathbb{I})\rightarrow(\mathsf{D},\wedge,\mathbb{J})
be an oplax symmetric monoidal functor between symmetric monoidal categories, with right adjoint
R
.
Then
L
lifts to a functor
L\colon\mathsf{CoCAlg}(\mathsf{C})\rightarrow\mathsf{CoCAlg}(\mathsf{D})
, with a right adjoint
\overline{R}
, that can be constructed when the categories admit equalizers and cofree cocommutative coalgebras.
Let
D
be a cocommutative coalgebra in
\mathsf{D}
. The counit
L\overline{R}(D)\rightarrow D
induces an adjunction from the change of coalgebras:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{CoMod}_{L\overline{R}(D)}(\mathsf{D})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{CoMod}_{D}(\mathsf{D}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\perp}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces-\square_{D}L\overline{R}(D)}

If we combine this with the previous construction:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{CoMod}_{\overline{R}(D)}(\mathsf{C})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{CoMod}_{L\overline{R}(D)}(\mathsf{D}),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\perp}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R^{\overline{R}(D)}}

and apply this in the case of the adjunction from the Dold-Kan correspondence (
1.1
), we obtain:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathsf{CoMod}_{\overline{\mathsf{N}}(D)}({\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}^{\geq 0}}),\square_{\overline{\mathsf{N}}(D)},\overline{\mathsf{N}}(D))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathsf{CoMod}_{D}(\mathsf{sMod}_{\Bbbk}),\square_{D},D),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\perp}

for any simply connected cocommutative coalgebra
D
in
\mathsf{sMod}_{\Bbbk}
.
Just as in the proof of Theorem
5.3
, we can show that the above adjunction is a weak opmonoidal Quillen pair.
If we suppose
\Gamma\overline{\mathsf{N}}(D)\rightarrow D
to be a weak homotopy equivalence, then one can prove that the above adjunction is a weak opmonoidal Quillen equivalence.
However, from
[
Sor19
]
, we expect
\Gamma\overline{\mathsf{N}}(D)\rightarrow D
to not be a weak homotopy equivalence in most cases.
In particular the functor
\overline{\mathsf{N}}\colon\mathsf{CoCAlg}(\mathsf{sMod}_{\Bbbk})\rightarrow\mathsf{CoCAlg}({\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}^{\geq 0}})
does not represent the correct right adjoint of the left derived functor of
\Gamma
on the
\infty
-categories of
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-coalgebras in the underlying
\infty
-categories of
\mathsf{sMod}_{\Bbbk}
and
{\mathsf{Ch}_{\Bbbk}^{\geq 0}}
. Thus we do not expect the above Quillen pair to be a Quillen equivalence. We conjecture that the problems disappear if we consider simply connected
conilpotent
cocommutative coalgebras.

Denote by
\mathsf{CoMod}_{X_{+}}

X_{+}
-comodules in pointed simplicial sets
(\mathsf{sSet}_{*},\wedge,S^{0})

.
Let
\mathcal{E}_{*}
be a generalized reduced homology theory.
Recall we can endow
\mathsf{sSet}
with a model structure, denoted by
(\mathsf{sSet}_{*})_{\mathcal{E}}
for which weak equivalences are
\mathcal{E}_{*}
-equivalences and cofibrations are monomorphisms
[
Bou75
]
, localizing the usual Kan model structure on

In
[

-comodules denoted by

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathsf{sSet}_{*})_{\mathcal{E}}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces-\wedge X_{+}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\perp}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}

Recall that
\mathsf{sMod}_{\Bbbk}
takes part in a Quillen adjunction:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathsf{sSet}_{*})_{\textup{Kan}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{sMod}_{\Bbbk}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{\Bbbk}[-]}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}

As
\widetilde{\Bbbk}[A\wedge B]\cong\widetilde{\Bbbk}[A]\otimes_{\Bbbk}\widetilde{\Bbbk}[B]

\widetilde{\Bbbk}[-]\colon\mathsf{CoMod}_{X_{+}}\rightarrow\mathsf{CoMod}_{\Bbbk[X]}(\mathsf{sMod}_{\Bbbk})

Theorem 5.6
.

Suppose
X
is a simply connected simplicial set.
There is a Quillen equivalence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathsf{CoMod}_{X_{+}})_{H\mathbb{Q}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{CoMod}_{\mathbb{Q}[X]}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}[-]}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\perp}

Proof.

First, notice that, by
[
Hov99
, 1.3.16]
, we have a Quillen equivalence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathsf{sSet}_{*})_{H\mathbb{Q}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{sMod}_{\mathbb{Q}}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}[-]}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\perp}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}

Indeed, let
A\rightarrow B
be a map in
\mathsf{sSet}_{*}
such that the induced map on simplicial homotopy groups
\pi_{*}(\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}[A])\rightarrow\pi_{*}(\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}[B])
is an isomorphism.
By the Dold-Kan correspondence, we know
\pi_{*}(\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}(A))\cong\widetilde{H}_{*}(|A|;\mathbb{Q})
, and therefore
\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}[-]
reflects weak equivalences.
Now let
M
be any simplicial
\mathbb{Q}
-module. We need to show that the counit
\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}[M]\rightarrow M
is a weak homotopy equivalence. From the short exact sequence:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Q}[*]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Q}[M]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}[M]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

and the
5
-lemma, it is enough to show
\mathbb{Q}[M]\rightarrow M
is a weak homotopy equivalence.
By the Künneth theorem, as
\mathbb{Q}
is flat as a
\mathbb{Z}
-module, we obtain that

\pi_{*}(\mathbb{Q}[M])\cong H_{*}(\mathsf{N}(\mathbb{Q}[M]))\cong H_{*}(\mathbb{Q}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathsf{N}(M))\cong H_{*}(\mathbb{Q})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}H_{*}(\mathsf{N}(M))\cong\mathbb{Q}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\pi_{*}(M)

We obtain that the induced map
\mathbb{Q}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\pi_{*}(M)\rightarrow\pi_{*}(M)
is an isomorphism.

By our discussion at the beginning of Section
5.1
, we obtain an adjunction

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{CoMod}_{X_{+}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{CoMod}_{\mathbb{Q}[X]}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}[-]}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\perp}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U^{X}}

where

is defined as the equalizer in
\mathsf{CoMod}_{X_{+}}
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U^{X}(M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U(M)\wedge X_{+}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U(M\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}[X])\wedge X_{+}}

for any
{\mathbb{Q}}[X]
-comodule
M
. If
M=A\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}[X]
, the cofree comodule on
A\in\mathsf{sMod}_{\mathbb{Q}}
, then
U^{X}(A\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}[X])\cong U(A)\wedge X_{+}
.
Applying again
[
Hov99
, 1.3.16]
, we need to show that the counit
\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}[U^{X}(M)]\rightarrow M
is a weak homotopy equivalence for any fibrant
\mathbb{Q}[X]
-module
M
.
We argue inductively using Corollary
4.17
, as
\mathbb{Q}[X]
is simply connected.
First notice that for
M=A\otimes\mathbb{Q}[X]
, we have from what we argued above:

\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}[U^{X}(A\otimes\mathbb{Q}[X])]\simeq\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}[U(A)\wedge X_{+}]\simeq\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}[A]\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}[X]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\rightarrow}}A\otimes\mathbb{Q}[X].

Let
\{M(n)\}
be the Postnikov tower of
M
as in Definition
4.18
and
\widetilde{M}
its homotopy limit.
Suppose we have shown
\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}[U^{X}(M(n))]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\rightarrow}}M(n)
for some

.
Recall that
M(n+1)
is the homotopy pullback in
\mathsf{CoMod}_{\mathbb{Q}[X]}
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M(n+1)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces PK(V_{n},n)\otimes\mathbb{Q}[X]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M(n)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K(V_{n},n)\otimes\mathbb{Q}[X].}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

Since
U^{X}
is a right Quillen functor, we obtain the homotopy pullback in
(\mathsf{CoMod}_{X_{+}})_{H\mathbb{Q}}
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U^{X}(M(n+1))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces PK(V_{n},n)\wedge X_{+}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U^{X}(M(n))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K(V_{n},n)\wedge X_{+}.}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

By
[
HS16
, 3.12]
, we can view
\mathsf{CoMod}_{X_{+}}
as a reflective subcategory of the category of retractive spaces over

, via a right Quillen functor denoted by

, that preserves and reflects

[

. Therefore, using the formula
[

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U^{X}(M(n+1))\star X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces PK(V_{n},n)\times X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U^{X}(M(n))\star X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K(V_{n},n)\times X.}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

Since

is simply connected and

is a loop space, the right vertical fibration is between nilpotent connected spaces, and thus by the Eilenberg-Moore spectral sequence

, 4.1]
, we obtain the following homotopy pullback in

:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}[U^{X}(M(n+1))\star X]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}[PK(V_{n},n)]\otimes\mathbb{Q}[X]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}[U^{X}(M(n))\star X]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}[K(V_{n},n)]\otimes\mathbb{Q}[X].}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

By induction, as
-\star X
preserves and reflects
H\mathbb{Q}
-equivalences, we can conclude that
\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}[U^{X}(M(n+1))]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\rightarrow}}U^{X}(M(n+1))
is a weak homotopy equivalence.
Because the tower

On the set of stable primes for postcritically infinite maps over number fields
Joachim König

Abstract
. Many interesting questions in arithmetic dynamics revolve, in one way or another, around the (local and/or global) reducibility behavior of iterates of a polynomial. We show that for very general families of integer polynomials f f (and, more generally, rational functions over number fields), the set of stable primes, i.e., primes modulo which all iterates of f f are irreducible, is a density zero set. Compared to previous results, our families cover a much wider ground, and in particular apply to 100 % 100\% of polynomials of any given odd degree, thus adding evidence to the conjecture that polynomials with a “large” set of stable primes are necessarily of a very specific shape, and in particular are necessarily postcritically finite.

Theorem 1.1

The basic underlying idea for the proof of our main results is the observation that if a polynomial
f
(with leading coefficient not divisible by
p
) is irreducible modulo a prime
p
, then

is unramified in the splitting field of

with Frobenius acting on the roots of

as a “full cycle” (i.e., a cycle of length the degree of the polynomial). On the other hand, Chebotarev’s density theorem asserts that the proportion of such cycles in the Galois group of

equals the density of primes

possessing such a cycle as Frobenius. In particular, if one can show that the proportion of full cycles in

converges to zero as

, then the proportion of primes modulo which
f^{\circ n}(X)-a
is irreducible must converge to zero as

, implying in particular that the density of stable primes of
(f,a)
is zero. The main group-theoretical tools to derive such density zero results are recalled in Section
2.2
. Notably, the action of the Galois group
G_{a,n}
on roots of

is imprimitive.
Proposition
2.1
asserts a shrinking of the proportion of full cycles in such imprimitive groups (when compared with the action on blocks), unless the group itself, and notably the block kernel, is of a very special shape. Corollary
2.2
then concludes that (the proportion of full cycles in
G_{a,n}
converges to zero as
n\to\infty

, there exist certain group elements which prevent the aforementioned special shape. The emphasis on individual group elements here is due to the next ingredient of the proof.
Namely, to show that the iterated Galois groups

fulfill the required conditions, we use the inertia group generators at ramified primes in the splitting fields of

. Controlling these inertia groups requires certain results about ramification in specializations of function field extensions (Theorem
2.3
), as well as about the prime divisors of dynamical sequences (Lemma

After this general setup, our main results will be derived in Section
3

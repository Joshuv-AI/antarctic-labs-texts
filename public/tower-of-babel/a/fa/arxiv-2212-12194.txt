Affine Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev inequalities
Julián Haddad, Monika Ludwig

Abstract
. Sharp affine Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev inequalities for functions on ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} are established, which are significantly stronger than (and directly imply) the sharp Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev inequalities by Lieb and by Beckner, Dou, and Zhu. In addition, sharp reverse inequalities for the new inequalities and the affine fractional L 2 L^{2} Sobolev inequalities are obtained for log-concave functions on ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} . 2000 AMS subject classification: 26D15 (26B25, 26D10, 46E35, 52A40)



1.
Introduction

Lieb
[
19
]
established the following sharp Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev inequalities (HLS inequalities):

(1)

\gamma_{n,\alpha}\|f\|_{\frac{2n}{n+\alpha}}^{2}\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{f(x)f(y)}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

for
0<\alpha<n
and non-negative
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
p={2n}/({n+\alpha})
.
There is equality if and only if
f(x)=a(1+\lambda\,|x-x_{0}|^{2})^{-(n+\alpha)/2}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
a\geq 0
,
\lambda>0
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Here
|\cdot|
denotes the Euclidean norm in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\|f\|_{p}^{p}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x
while
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the space of measurable functions
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
with
\|f\|_{p}<\infty
.
The constant is given by

(2)

\gamma_{n,\alpha}=\pi^{\frac{n-\alpha}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{\alpha}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{n+\alpha}{2})}\Big(\frac{\Gamma(n)}{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}\Big)^{\frac{\alpha}{n}},

where
\Gamma
is the gamma function. The HLS inequalities (
1
) can be considered as weak Young inequalities and are equivalent by duality to sharp fractional
L^{2}
Sobolev inequalities (for more information, see
[
20
,
5
,
4
,
9
]
).

The HLS inequalities are invariant under translations, rotations, and inversions, but not under volume-preserving linear transformations. For geometric questions, affine inequalities, that is, inequalities that are unchanged under translations and volume-preserving linear transformations turned out to be very powerful.
The best-known example is the Petty projection inequality for convex bodies (that is, compact convex sets) in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which is stronger than the Euclidean isoperimetric inequality and directly implies it (see, for example,
[
31
,
10
]
, and see
[
25
,
14
,
34
,
32
]
for related results in the
L^{p}
Brunn–Minkowski theory and for more general sets).
Very recently, Milman and Yehudayoff
[
28
]
established isoperimetric inequalities for affine quermassintegrals, which are significantly stronger than the isoperimetric inequality for the classical quermassintegrals (thereby confirming a conjecture by Lutwak
[
24
]
).
Gaoyong Zhang’s affine Sobolev inequality
[
34
]
is an affine version of the classical
L^{1}
Sobolev inequality.
It was extended to functions of bounded variation by Tuo Wang
[
32
]
, and corresponding results for
L^{p}
Sobolev inequalities were established by Lutwak, Yang, and Zhang
[
26
]
and Haberl and Schuster
[
15
]
.
Fractional Petty projection inequalities were recently obtained by the authors in
[
16
]
and affine fractional
L^{p}
Sobolev inequalities in
[
16
,
17
]
. In all cases, the affine inequalities are significantly stronger than (and imply) their Euclidean counterparts.

The main aim of this paper is to establish sharp affine HLS inequalities that are stronger than Lieb’s sharp HLS inequalities (
1
).

Theorem 1
.

For
\,0<\alpha<n
and non-negative
f\in L^{{2n}/({n+\alpha})}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

(3)

\gamma_{n,\alpha}\|f\|_{\frac{2n}{n+\alpha}}^{2}\geq n\omega_{n}^{\frac{n-\alpha}{n}}\Big(\frac{1}{n}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\big(\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)f(x+t\xi)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\big)^{\frac{n}{\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\Big)^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\\
\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{f(x)f(y)}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y.

There is equality in the first inequality precisely if
f(x)=a(1+|\phi(x-x_{0})|^{2})^{-(n+\alpha)/2}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
a\geq 0
,
\phi\in\operatorname{GL}(n)
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
There is equality in the second inequality if
f
is radially symmetric.

Here,
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
is the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and integration on
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
is with respect to the
(n-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure while
\omega_{n}
is the
n
-dimensional volume of the
n
-dimensional unit ball.

To prove Theorem
1
, for given
0<\alpha<n
, we introduce the star-shaped set
\operatorname{S}_{\alpha}\!f
associated to
f
, defined by its radial function for
\xi\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
as

(4)

\rho_{\operatorname{S}_{\alpha}\!f}(\xi)^{\alpha}=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)f(x+t\xi)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

(see Section
3
for details). The first inequality from Theorem
1
now can be written as

(5)

\gamma_{n,\alpha}\|f\|_{\frac{2n}{n+\alpha}}^{2}\geq n\omega_{n}^{\frac{n-\alpha}{n}}|\operatorname{S}_{\alpha}\!f|^{\frac{\alpha}{n}},

where
|\cdot|
denotes
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure. Since both sides of (
5
) are invariant under translations of
f
and

\operatorname{S}_{\alpha}\!\,(f\circ\phi^{-1})=\phi\operatorname{S}_{\alpha}\!f

for volume-preserving linear transformations
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
it follows that inequality (
5
) is indeed an affine inequality.

As a critical tool in the proof of Theorem
1
, we introduce an anisotropic version of the right side of (
1
). In addition, we use the Riesz rearrangement inequality, its equality case due to Burchard
[
3
]
, and the original HLS inequalities for radially symmetric functions.

Recently, Dou and Zhu
[
7
]
and Beckner
[
2
]
obtained sharp HLS inequalities also for
\alpha>n
. In Section
5
, we will establish sharp affine HLS inequalities for
\alpha>n
, which are stronger than (and directly imply) their results.

In Section
6
, for
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
measurable, we consider
\operatorname{S}_{\alpha}\!\operatorname{1}_{E}
, where
\operatorname{1}_{E}
is the indicator function of
E
. For an
n
-dimensional convex body
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we show that
\operatorname{S}_{\alpha}\!\operatorname{1}_{E}
is proportional to the radial
\alpha
-mean body of
E
, an important notion that was introduced by Gardner and Zhang
[
12
]
. Sharp isoperimetric inequalities for radial
\alpha
-mean bodies were recently obtained in
[
16
]
. Sharp reverse inequalities were already established by Gardner and Zhang
[
12
]
. They generalize Zhang’s reverse Petty projection inequality
[
33
]
, and equality is attained precisely for
n
-dimensional simplices.

In Section
7
, we establish reverse inequalities also in the functional setting.

Theorem 2
.

For
\,0<\alpha<n
and log-concave
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\frac{\Gamma(n+1)^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\,|\operatorname{S}_{\alpha}\!f|^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\geq\|f\|_{2}^{2-\frac{2\alpha}{n}}\|f\|_{1}^{\frac{2\alpha}{n}}\geq\|f\|_{\frac{2n}{n+\alpha}}^{2}.

There is equality in the first inequality if
f(x)=a\,e^{-\|x-x_{0}\|_{\Delta}}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
a\geq 0
,
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
\Delta
an
n
-dimensional simplex having a vertex at the origin.

Here,
\|\cdot\|_{\Delta}
is the gauge function of
\Delta\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The second inequality follows from Hölder’s inequality; for the proof of the first inequality, see Section
7
.

We remark that it is easy to see that for general, non-negative
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, no non-trivial reverse inequality can hold. In Section
7
, we establish reverse inequalities also for
\alpha>n
(see Theorem
15
) and obtain results for
s
-concave functions for
s>0
. Moreover, we will establish reverse affine fractional
L^{2}
Sobolev inequalities.

2.
Preliminaries

We collect results on symmetrization, star-shaped sets, log-concave and
s
-concave functions, and fractional polar projection bodies.

2.1.
Symmetrization

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a Borel set of finite measure.
The Schwarz symmetral of
E
, denoted by
E^{\star}
, is the closed, centered Euclidean ball with the same volume as
E
.

Let
f
be a non-negative measurable function with superlevel sets of finite measure. Let
\{f\geq t\}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:f(x)\geq t\}
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
. We say that
f
is non-zero if
\{f\neq 0\}
has positive measure, and we identify functions that are equal up to a set of measure zero.
The layer cake formula states that

(6)

f(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}}(x)\,\mathrm{d}t

for almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and allows us to recover the function from its superlevel sets. Here, for
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the indicator function
\operatorname{1}_{E}
is defined by
\operatorname{1}_{E}(x)=1
for
x\in E
and
\operatorname{1}_{E}(x)=0
otherwise.

The Schwarz symmetral of
f
, denoted by
f^{\star}
, is defined as

f^{\star}(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}^{\star}}(x)\,\mathrm{d}t

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Hence
f^{\star}
is determined a.e. by the properties of being radially symmetric and having superlevel sets of the same measure as those of
f
. Note that
f^{\star}
is also called the symmetric decreasing rearrangement of
f
. We say that
f^{\star}
is strictly symmetric decreasing, if
f^{\star}(x)>f^{\star}(y)
whenever
|x|<|y|
.

The proofs of our results make use of the Riesz rearrangement inequality (see, for example,
[
20
, Theorem 3.7]
).

Theorem 3

(Riesz’s rearrangement inequality)
.

For
f,g,k:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
non-negative, measurable functions with superlevel sets of finite measure,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)k(x-y)g(y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{\star}(x)k^{\star}(x-y)g^{\star}(y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y.

We will use the characterization of equality cases of the Riesz rearrangement inequality due to Burchard
[
3
]
.

Theorem 4

(Burchard)
.

Let
A,B
and
C
be sets of finite positive measure in
\,\mathbb{R}^{n}
and denote by
\alpha,\beta
and
\gamma
the radii of their Schwarz symmetrals
A^{\star},B^{\star}
and
C^{\star}
.
For
\,|\alpha-\beta|<\gamma<\alpha+\beta
, there is equality in

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\operatorname{1}_{A}(y)\operatorname{1}_{B}(x-y)\operatorname{1}_{C}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\operatorname{1}_{A^{\star}}(y)\operatorname{1}_{B^{\star}}(x-y)\operatorname{1}_{C^{\star}}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

if and only if, up to sets of measure zero,

A=a+\alpha D,\,B=b+\beta D,\,C=c+\gamma D,

where
D
is a centered ellipsoid, and
a,b
and
c=a+b
are vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Theorem 5

(Burchard)
.

Let
f,g,k:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
non-negative, non-zero, measurable functions with superlevel sets of finite measure such that

(7)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)k(x-y)g(y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y<\infty.

If at least two of the Schwarz symmetrals
f^{\star},g^{\star},k^{\star}
are strictly symmetric decreasing, then
there is equality in

(8)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)k(x-y)g(y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{\star}(x)k^{\star}(x-y)g^{\star}(y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

if and only if there is a volume-preserving
\phi\in\operatorname{GL}(n)
and
a,b,c\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
c=a+b
such that

f(x)=f^{\star}(\phi^{-1}x-a),\,k(x)=k^{\star}(\phi^{-1}x-b),g(x)=g^{\star}(\phi^{-1}x-c)

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

2.2.
Star-shaped sets and dual mixed volumes

A closed set
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is star-shaped (with respect to the origin) if the interval
[0,x]\subset K
for every
x\in K
. The gauge function
\|\cdot\|_{K}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty]
of a star-shaped set is defined as

\|x\|_{K}=\inf\{\lambda>0:x\in\lambda K\}

and the radial function
\rho_{K}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}\to[0,\infty]
as

\rho_{K}(x)=\|x\|_{K}^{-1}=\sup\{\lambda\geq 0:\lambda x\in K\}.

For the
n
-dimensional unit ball
{B^{n}}
, we have
\|\cdot\|_{{B^{n}}}=|\cdot|
. The
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure, or volume of a star-shaped set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with measurable radial function is given by

|K|=\frac{1}{n}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\rho_{K}(\xi)^{n}\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

We call
K
a star body if its radial function is strictly positive and continuous in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0,n\}
. For star-shaped sets
K,L\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
with measurable radial functions, the dual mixed volume is defined as

\tilde{V}_{\alpha}(K,L)=\frac{1}{n}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\rho_{K}(\xi)^{n-\alpha}\rho_{L}(\xi)^{\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

Note that

\tilde{V}_{\alpha}(K,K)=|K|.

For
0<\alpha<n
and star-shaped sets
K,L\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
of finite volume, the dual mixed volume inequality states that

(9)

\tilde{V}_{\alpha}(K,L)\leq|K|^{({n-\alpha})/n}|L|^{\alpha/n}.

Equality holds if and only if
K
and
L
are dilates, where we say that star-shaped sets
K
and
L
are dilates if
\rho_{K}=c\,\rho_{L}
almost everywhere on
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
for some
c\geq 0
. The definition of dual mixed volume for star bodies is due to Lutwak
[
23
]
, where also the dual mixed volume inequality (
9
) is derived from Hölder’s inequality. For
0<\alpha<n
and star-shaped sets of finite volume, it follows from (
9
) that the dual mixed volume is finite.
For star-shaped sets
K,L\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\alpha>n
, the dual mixed volume inequality states that

(10)

\tilde{V}_{\alpha}(K,L)\geq|K|^{({n-\alpha})/n}|L|^{\alpha/n}.

It follows from the equality case of Hölder’s inequality that equality holds for finite
\tilde{V}_{\alpha}(K,L)
if and only if
K
and
L
are dilates. See
[
31
,
10
]
for more information on dual mixed volumes.

2.3.
Log-concave and
s
-concave functions

A function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
is log-concave, if
x\mapsto\log f(x)
is a concave function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with values in
[-\infty,\infty)
.
For
s>0
, a function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
is
s
-concave, if
f^{s}
is concave on its support.

The following result is a consequence of the Prékopa–Leindler inequality. It can be found as Theorem 11.3 in
[
11
]
. Log-concave functions are included as the case
s=0
.

Lemma 6
.

Let
s\geq 0
. If
f,g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
are
s
-concave, then their convolution is
s/(ns+2)
-concave.

Here, the convolution of
f,g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is defined by

f*g(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x-y)\,g(y)\,\mathrm{d}y

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

We also consider the limiting case
s=\infty
. We say that a function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
is
s
-concave with
s=\infty
if it is a multiple of the indicator function of a convex body.

2.4.
Fractional
L^{2}
polar projection bodies

For measurable
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
and
0<\alpha<1
, the
\alpha
-fractional
L^{2}
polar projection body of
f
, denoted by
\Pi_{2}^{\ast,\alpha}f
, was defined in
[
17
]
by its radial function
for
\xi\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
as

(11)

\rho_{\Pi_{2}^{\ast,\alpha}f}(\xi)^{-2\alpha}=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-2\alpha-1}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x+t\xi)-f(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

The fractional Sobolev space
W^{\alpha,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the set of all
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(x)-f(x)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y<\infty.

The affine fractional
L^{2}
Sobolev inequality
[
17
, Theorem 1]
states that

(12)

\|f\|_{{\frac{2n}{n-2\alpha}}}^{2}\leq\sigma_{n,2,\alpha}\,n\omega_{n}^{\frac{n+2\alpha}{n}}|\Pi_{2}^{\ast,\alpha}f|^{-\frac{2\alpha}{n}}

for
f\in W^{\alpha,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
0<\alpha<1
, where
\sigma_{n,2,\alpha}
is an explicitly known constant.

3.
The Star-shaped Set
\operatorname{S}_{\alpha}\!f

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
be measurable,
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
star-shaped with measurable radial function and
\alpha>0
.
Anisotropic fractional Sobolev norms were introduced in
[
21
,
22
]
and used in
[
16
]
. Here, we introduce

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{f(x)f(y)}{\|x-y\|_{K}^{n-\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y,

an anisotropic version of the functional from (
1
).
Using Fubini’s theorem, polar coordinates, and (
4
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{f(x)f(y)}{\|x-y\|_{K}^{n-\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{f(y)f(y+z)}{\|z\|_{K}^{n-\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho_{K}(t\xi)^{n-\alpha}\,t^{n-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(y)f(y+t\xi)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\rho_{K}(\xi)^{n-\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(y)f(y+t\xi)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\rho_{K}(\xi)^{n-\alpha}\rho_{\operatorname{S}_{\alpha}\!f}(\xi)^{\alpha}d\xi.

Hence,

(13)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{f(x)f(y)}{\|x-y\|_{K}^{n-\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y=n\tilde{V}_{\alpha}(K,\operatorname{S}_{\alpha}\!f)

for measurable
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
and star-shaped
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with measurable radial function.

Note that, using polar coordinates and Fubini’s theorem, we obtain that

(14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}|\operatorname{S}_{n}\!f|&=\frac{1}{n}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\rho_{\operatorname{S}_{n}\!f}(\xi)^{n}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\\
&=\frac{1}{n}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}t^{n-1}f(x)f(x+t\xi)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}\xi\\
&=\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)f(x+y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\frac{1}{n}\|f\|_{1}^{2}\end{split}

for measurable
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
.

We remark that for
0<\alpha<n
and given measurable
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
, the dual mixed volume inequality (
9
) and (
13
) imply that

(15)

\sup\big\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{f(x)f(y)}{\|x-y\|_{K}^{n-\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y:K\subset\mathbb{R}\text{ star-shaped},|K|=\omega_{n}\big\}=n\omega_{n}^{\frac{n-\alpha}{n}}|\operatorname{S}_{\alpha}\!f|^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}

is attained precisely for a suitable dilate of
\operatorname{S}_{\alpha}\!f
if
|\operatorname{S}_{\alpha}\!f|
is finite. In this sense,
\operatorname{S}_{\alpha}\!f
is the optimal choice of the star-shaped set
K
for given
f
. For
\alpha>n
and given measurable
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
,
the dual mixed volume inequality (
10
) and (
13
) imply that

(16)

\inf\big\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{f(x)f(y)}{\|x-y\|_{K}^{n-\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y:K\subset\mathbb{R}\text{ star-shaped},|K|=\omega_{n}\big\}=n\omega_{n}^{\frac{n-\alpha}{n}}|\operatorname{S}_{\alpha}\!f|^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}

is attained precisely for a suitable dilate of
\operatorname{S}_{\alpha}\!f
if
|\operatorname{S}_{\alpha}\!f|
is finite. Again
\operatorname{S}_{\alpha}\!f
is the optimal choice in this sense.

We mention the following property of
\operatorname{S}_{\alpha}\!f
for log-concave
f
.

Proposition 7
.

If
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
is log-concave and in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
\operatorname{S}_{\alpha}\!f
is a convex body for every
\alpha>0
.

Proof.

Since
f
is log-concave and in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, so is

y\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)f(x+y)\,\mathrm{d}x.

This follows from the Young inequality (cf.
[
20
, Theorem 4.2]
) and Lemma
6
.
Hence, using a result of Ball
[
1
, Theorem 5]
(or see
[
12
, Corollary 4.2]
), we obtain the result.
∎

4.
Affine HLS Inequalities for
0<\alpha<n

The following result is an immediate consequence of the Riesz rearrangement inequality and its equality case from Theorem
5
.

Lemma 8
.

Let
q>0
and
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
a star-shaped set with measurable radial function and
|K|>0
.
For a non-zero, measurable function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
such that

(17)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{f(x)f(y)}{\|x-y\|_{K}^{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y<\infty

and strictly symmetric decreasing
f^{\star}
,
there is equality in

(18)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{f(x)f(y)}{\|x-y\|_{K}^{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{f^{\star}(x)f^{\star}(y)}{\|x-y\|_{K^{\star}}^{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

if and only if
K
is a centered ellipsoid and
f
is a translate of
f^{\star}
.

We require the following lemmas for the proof of Theorem
1
.

Lemma 9
.

Let
\,0<\alpha<n
and
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be star-shaped with measurable radial function. If

).

For
\omega(t)=e^{-t}
and
\omega(t)=(1-st)_{\scriptscriptstyle+}^{1/s}
with
s>0
, we obtain the well-known analytic continuation formulas for the Gamma and Beta functions,

(79)

\Gamma(\alpha)=\begin{cases}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha-1}e^{-t}\,\mathrm{d}t&\text{for }0<\alpha,\\[4.0pt]
\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha-1}(e^{-t}-1)\,\mathrm{d}t&\text{for }-1<\alpha<0,\end{cases}

and

(80)

s^{-\alpha}\operatorname{B}(\alpha,1+\frac{1}{s})=\begin{cases}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha-1}(1-st)_{\scriptscriptstyle+}^{1/s}\,\mathrm{d}t&\text{for }0<\alpha,\\[4.0pt]
\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha-1}((1-st)_{\scriptscriptstyle+}^{1/s}-1)\,\mathrm{d}t&\text{for }-1<\alpha<0.\end{cases}

We extend Lemma
16
to
\alpha\in(-1,\infty)
using the analytic continuation formula from (
77
). Special cases of the following lemma were obtained by Koldobsky, Pajor, and Yaskin
[
18
, Proof of Lemma 3.3]
and Fradelizi, Li, and Madiman
[
8
, Theorem 6.1]
.

Lemma 17
.

Let
\omega:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be decreasing with

0<\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha-1}\omega(t)\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty

for
\alpha>0
and

0<\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha-1}(\omega(0)-\omega(t))\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty

for
-1<\alpha<0
.
If
\varphi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is non-zero, with
\varphi(0)=0
, and such that
t\mapsto\varphi(t)
and
t\mapsto\varphi(t)/t
are increasing on
(0,\infty)
,
then

(81)

\zeta(\alpha)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\left(\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha-1}\omega(\varphi(t))\,\mathrm{d}t}{\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha-1}\omega(t)\,\mathrm{d}t}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}&\text{for }\alpha>0\\[12.0pt]
\displaystyle\exp\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\omega(\varphi(t))-\omega(t)}{t\,\omega(0)}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)&\text{for }\alpha=0\\[8.0pt]
\displaystyle\left(\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha-1}(\omega(\varphi(t))-\omega(0))\,\mathrm{d}t}{\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha-1}(\omega(t)-\omega(0))\,\mathrm{d}t}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}&\text{for }-1<\alpha<0\end{cases}

is a continuous, decreasing function of
\alpha
on
(-1,+\infty)
. Moreover,
\zeta
is constant on
(-1,\infty)
if
\varphi(t)=\lambda t
on
[0,\infty)
for some
\lambda>0
.

Proof.

First, we show that
\zeta
is well-defined.
Take
t_{0}>0
such that
\varphi(t_{0})>0
, and set
v_{0}=\varphi(t_{0})/{t_{0}}
. Since
t\mapsto\varphi(t)/t
is increasing, we have
\varphi(t)\leq v_{0}t
for
0\leq t\leq t_{0}
and
\varphi(t)\geq v_{0}t
for
t\geq t_{0}
.

For
\alpha\in(-1,\infty)
, we have

(82)

\displaystyle\begin{split}v_{0}\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}t^{\alpha-1}\omega(\varphi(t))\,\mathrm{d}t&\leq v_{0}\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}t^{\alpha-1}\omega(v_{0}t)\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\int_{v_{0}}^{\infty}\left(\frac{t}{v_{0}}\right)^{\alpha-1}\omega(t)\,\mathrm{d}t,\end{split}

where the last integral is finite and increasing with respect to
\alpha
, and

(83)

\displaystyle\begin{split}v_{0}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}t^{\alpha-1}(\omega(0)-\omega(\varphi(t)))\,\mathrm{d}t&\leq v_{0}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}t^{\alpha-1}(\omega(0)-\omega(v_{0}t))\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\int_{0}^{v_{0}}\left(\frac{t}{v_{0}}\right)^{\alpha-1}(\omega(0)-\omega(t))\,\mathrm{d}t,\end{split}

where the last integral is finite and decreasing with respect to
\alpha
.
Now (
82
), (
83
), and (
77
) together with the dominated convergence theorem show that
\zeta
is well-defined and continuous on
(-1,0)
and
(0,\infty)
.

For
\alpha=0
, we have

(84)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}&\Big|\frac{\omega(\varphi(t))-\omega(t)}{t}\Big|\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{t_{0}}t^{-1}(\omega(0)-\omega(\varphi(t)))\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{t_{0}}t^{-1}(\omega(0)-\omega(t))\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{v_{0}}t^{-1}(\omega(0)-\omega(t))\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{t_{0}}t^{-1}(\omega(0)-\omega(t))\,\mathrm{d}t\end{split}

and

(85)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}\left|\frac{\omega(\varphi(t))-\omega(t)}{t}\right|\,\mathrm{d}t&\leq\int_{v_{0}}^{\infty}t^{-1}\omega(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}t^{-1}\omega(t)\,\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

The monotonicity of the last integrals of (
82
) and (
83
) shows that the integrals in (
84
) and (
85
) are finite. Hence
\zeta(0)
is well-defined.

It follows from Lemma
16
that
\zeta
is decreasing on
(0,\infty)
. We show that it is decreasing on
(-1,0)
.
By the change of variables
r=t^{-1}
, we get

\zeta(\alpha)^{-1}=\left(\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{-\alpha-1}(\omega(0)-\omega(\psi(r)^{-1}))\,\mathrm{d}r}{\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{-\alpha-1}(\omega(0)-\omega(r^{-1}))\,\mathrm{d}r}\right)^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}

where
\psi(r)=\varphi(r^{-1})^{-1}
. Observe that
\omega(0)-\omega(r^{-1})
is decreasing and non-negative, and that
{\psi(r)}/r=\left({\varphi(r^{-1})}/{r^{-1}}\right)^{-1}
is increasing.
Since
-\alpha\in(0,1)
, Lemma
16
implies that
\zeta(\alpha)^{-1}
is an increasing function of
\alpha
.

Using (
77
) and the elementary relation,

\lim_{\alpha\to 0^{\pm}}\left(\frac{a(\alpha)+\frac{c}{\alpha}}{b(\alpha)+\frac{c}{\alpha}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}=\exp\left(\frac{a(0)-b(0)}{c}\right),

which is valid for a constant
c\neq 0
and continuous functions
\alpha\mapsto a(\alpha)
and
\alpha\mapsto b(\alpha)
, we obtain that

\lim_{\alpha\to 0^{\pm}}\zeta(\alpha)=\exp\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\omega(\varphi(t))-\omega(t)}{t\,\omega(0)}\,\mathrm{d}t\right).

So
\zeta
is continuous at
\alpha=0
.
∎

7.2.
Radial mean bodies of functions

Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be non-zero and non-negative.
We define

(86)

\operatorname{R}_{\alpha}\!f=\left(\frac{\alpha}{\|f\|_{2}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\operatorname{S}_{\alpha}\!f

for
\alpha>0
and

(87)

\operatorname{R}_{\alpha}\!f=\left(\frac{|\alpha|}{2\|f\|_{2}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\Pi_{2}^{\ast,-\alpha/2}f

for
-1<\alpha<0
.
In addition, we define
\operatorname{R}_{0}\!f
by its radial function for
\xi\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
as

(88)

\log(\rho_{\operatorname{R}_{0}\!f}(\xi))=-\gamma+\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t}\left(\frac{1}{\|f\|_{2}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)f(x+t\xi)\,\mathrm{d}x-e^{-t}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t,

where
\gamma
is Euler’s constant. The definitions (
86
) and (
87
) are compatible with (
68
) and (
74
) for
f=\operatorname{1}_{E}
and a convex body
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Note that
(
14
) implies that

(89)

|\operatorname{R}_{n}\!f|=\frac{\|f\|_{1}^{2}}{\|f\|_{2}^{2}}

for non-zero
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

7.3.
Results for log-concave functions

We start with a simple calculation, for which we need the following notation. For
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we set

(90)

\mathscr{G}\!f(y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\,f(x+y)\,\mathrm{d}x.

We define the simplex
\Delta_{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
as the convex hull of the origin and the standard basis vectors
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
. In addition, let

B_{1}^{n}=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}):|x_{1}|+\dots+|x_{n}|\leq 1\}

and
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\geq 0\}
.

Lemma 18
.

If
f(x)=e^{-\|x\|_{\Delta_{n}}}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then

\mathscr{G}\!f(y)=\frac{1}{2^{n}}e^{-\|y\|_{B_{1}^{n}}}

for
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

We have

f(x)=\begin{cases}e^{-(x_{1}+\dots+x_{n})}&\text{ for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},\\
0&\text{ for }x\not\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}.\end{cases}

Setting
g=\mathscr{G}\!f
, we obtain that

(91)

g(y)=e^{-(y_{1}+\dots+y_{n})}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}\cap(\mathbb{R}_{+}-y_{i})}e^{-2x_{i}}\,\mathrm{d}x_{i}.

Using

(92)

2\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}\cap(\mathbb{R}_{+}-y_{i})}e^{-2x_{i}}\,\mathrm{d}x_{i}=\begin{cases}1&\text{ for }y_{i}\geq 0\\
e^{2y_{i}}&\text{ for }y_{i}<0\end{cases}

and
I_{\scriptscriptstyle-}(y)=\{1\leq i\leq n:y_{i}<0\}
,
we obtain that

(93)

\displaystyle g(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\,e^{-(y_{1}+\dots+y_{n})+2\sum_{i\in I_{\scriptscriptstyle-}(y)}^{n}y_{i}}=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\,e^{-\|y\|_{B_{1}^{n}}}

for
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
∎

We establish the following inclusion relation.

Theorem 19
.

If
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is non-zero and log-concave, then

\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta+1)^{1/\beta}}\operatorname{R}_{\beta}\!f\subseteq\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)^{1/\alpha}}\operatorname{R}_{\alpha}\!f

for
-1<\alpha<\beta<\infty
.
There is equality if
f(x)=a\,e^{-\|x-x_{0}\|_{\Delta}}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
a>0,x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\Delta
an
n
-dimensional simplex having a vertex at the origin.

Proof.

Note that
g=\mathscr{G}\!f
is even, attains a maximum at
y=0
and that
g(0)=\|f\|_{2}^{2}
. Moreover,
g
is log-concave by Lemma
6
.

We fix
\xi\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
and see that
t\mapsto g(t\xi)
is a positive, decreasing, log-concave function. We apply Lemma
17
to
\omega(t)=g(0)e^{-t},\varphi(t)=-\log(g(t\xi)/g(0))
and observe that

\zeta(\alpha)=\left(\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha-1}g(t\xi)\,\mathrm{d}t}{\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha-1}g(0)e^{-t}\,\mathrm{d}t}\right)^{1/\alpha}=\frac{\rho_{\operatorname{R}_{\alpha}\!f}(\xi)}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)^{1/\alpha}}

for
\alpha>0
and

\zeta(\alpha)=\left(\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha-1}(g(0)-g(t\xi))\,\mathrm{d}t}{\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha-1}(g(0)-g(0)e^{-t})\,\mathrm{d}t}\right)^{1/\alpha}\\
=\frac{\rho_{\operatorname{R}_{\alpha}\!f}(\xi)}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)^{1/\alpha}}

for
-1<\alpha<0
, where we used formula (
79
).
This proves the inclusion. There is equality in Lemma
17

if
g(t\xi)=e^{-h(\xi)t}
for some function
h:{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}\to(0,\infty)
. Hence, the equality case follows from Lemma
18
and the
\operatorname{SL}(n)
and translation invariance and homogeneity of
|\operatorname{R}_{\alpha}\!f|
.
∎

Using (
86
) and (
89
), we obtain both Theorem
2
and Theorem
15
immediately from the above result. Using (
87
), (
89
), and Hölder’s inequality, we also obtain the following inequality from Theorem
19
.

Theorem 20
.

For
\,0<\alpha<1/2
and log-concave
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\frac{\alpha}{\Gamma(n+1)^{\frac{2\alpha}{n}}\Gamma(1-2\alpha)}\,|\Pi_{2}^{\ast,\alpha}f|^{-\frac{2\alpha}{n}}\leq\|f\|_{2}^{2+\frac{4\alpha}{n}}\|f\|_{1}^{-\frac{4\alpha}{n}}\leq\|f\|_{\frac{2n}{n-2\alpha}}^{2}.

There is equality in the first inequality if
f(x)=a\,e^{-\|x-x_{0}\|_{\Delta}}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
a\geq 0,x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\Delta
an
n
-dimensional simplex having a vertex at the origin.

The inequality in the previous theorem is a reverse inequality to the affine fractional
L^{2}
Sobolev inequality (
12
).

7.4.
Results for
s
-concave functions

We obtain the following inclusion relation.

Theorem 21
.

Let
s>0
.
If
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is non-zero and
s
-concave, then

\frac{1}{\big((n+\frac{2}{s})\operatorname{B}(\beta+1,n+\frac{2}{s})\big)^{1/\beta}}\operatorname{R}_{\beta}\!f\subseteq\frac{1}{\big((n+\frac{2}{s})\operatorname{B}(\alpha+1,n+\frac{2}{s})\big)^{1/\alpha}}\operatorname{R}_{\alpha}\!f

for
-1<\alpha<\beta<\infty
.

Proof.

As in the proof of Theorem
19
, note that
g=\mathscr{G}\!f
is even, continuous, and attains a maximum at
y=0
. For
\xi\in S^{n-1}
, it follows that
t\mapsto g(t\xi)
is positive and decreasing. By Lemma
6
, the function
g
is
r
-concave with
r={s}/{(ns+2)}
.

We apply Lemma
17
with
\omega(t)=g(0)(1-rt)_{\scriptscriptstyle+}^{1/r}
and
\varphi(t)=(1-(g(t\xi)/g(0))^{r})/r
.
We obtain that

\zeta(\alpha)=\left(\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha-1}g(t\xi)\,\mathrm{d}t}{\int_{0}^{\infty}g(0)t^{\alpha-1}(1-rt)_{\scriptscriptstyle+}^{1/r}\,\mathrm{d}t}\right)^{1/\alpha}=\frac{r\,\rho_{\operatorname{R}_{\alpha}\!f}(\xi)}{(\alpha\operatorname{B}(\alpha,1+\frac{1}{r}))^{1/\alpha}}

for
\alpha>0
and

\zeta(\alpha)=\left(\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha-1}(g(0)-g(t\xi))\,\mathrm{d}t}{\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha-1}(g(0)(1-rt)_{\scriptscriptstyle+}^{1/r}-g(0))\,\mathrm{d}t}\right)^{1/\alpha}\\
=\frac{r\,\rho_{\operatorname{R}_{\alpha}\!f}(\xi)}{(\alpha\operatorname{B}(\alpha,1+\frac{1}{r}))^{1/\alpha}}

for
-1<\alpha<0
, where we used formula (
80
). The result now follows from Lemma
17
.
∎

For
s\to 0
, we recover Theorem
19
from Theorem
21
. For
s\to\infty
and
f=\operatorname{1}_{E}
for a convex body
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, Theorem
21
implies that

(94)

\frac{1}{\big(n\operatorname{B}(\beta+1,n)\big)^{1/\beta}}\operatorname{R}_{\beta}\!E\subseteq\frac{1}{\big(n\operatorname{B}(\alpha+1,n)\big)^{1/\alpha}}\operatorname{R}_{\alpha}\!E

for
-1<\alpha<\beta
.
This recovers Theorem 5.5 by Gardner and Zhang
[
12
]
,
who showed that there is equality in (
94
) precisely for
n
-dimensional simplices. The problem to determine the precise equality conditions in Theorem
19
and Theorem
21
is open.

It follows from the proof of Theorem
21
under the assumptions given there that

(95)

\frac{\rho_{\operatorname{R}_{\beta}\!f}(\xi)}{\big((n+\frac{2}{s})\operatorname{B}(\beta+1,n+\frac{2}{s})\big)^{1/\beta}}\leq\frac{\rho_{\operatorname{R}_{\alpha}\!f}(\xi)}{\big((n+\frac{2}{s})\!\operatorname{B}(\alpha+1,n+\frac{2}{s})\big)^{1/\alpha}}

with equality for
\xi\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
if
\mathscr{G}\!f(t\xi)=a(1-\lambda t)_{\scriptscriptstyle+}^{1/s}
for
t>0
with
a\geq 0
and
\lambda>0
. The following lemma shows that the inequality in (
95
) is sharp in some directions and that the constants in Theorem
21
are optimal.

Lemma 22
.

Let
s>0
. If
f(x)=(1-\|x\|_{\Delta_{n}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}^{1/s}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then

\mathscr{G}\!f(y)=a\big(1-\frac{1}{2}\|y\|_{B^{n}_{1}}\big)_{\scriptscriptstyle+}^{n+2/s}

for every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
y_{1}+\dots+y_{n}=0
, where
a=\operatorname{B}(n,1+2/s)/(n-1)!
.

Proof.

Let
t_{\scriptscriptstyle-}=\max\{0,-t\}
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
y_{\scriptscriptstyle-}=((y_{1})_{\scriptscriptstyle-},\dots,(y_{n})_{\scriptscriptstyle-})
for
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Setting
\delta(t)=(1-t)_{\scriptscriptstyle+}^{1/s}
, we have

(96)

\displaystyle\mathscr{G}\!f(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\cap(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}-y)}\delta\big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\big)\,\delta\big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}+y_{i})\big)\,\mathrm{d}x

(97)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}+y_{\scriptscriptstyle-}}\delta\big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\big)\,\delta\big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}+y_{i})\big)\,\mathrm{d}x

(98)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\delta\big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}+(y_{i})_{\scriptscriptstyle-})\big)\,\delta\big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}+y_{i}+(y_{i})_{\scriptscriptstyle-}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x

(99)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\delta\big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(y_{i})_{\scriptscriptstyle-}\big)\,\delta\big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(y_{i})_{\scriptscriptstyle+})\,\mathrm{d}x

(100)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{n-1}\delta(r+\|y_{\scriptscriptstyle-}\|_{B^{n}_{1}})\,\delta(r+\|y_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\|_{B^{n}_{1}})\,\mathrm{d}r.

Now note that if
\|y_{\scriptscriptstyle-}\|_{B^{n}_{1}}=\|y_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\|_{B^{n}_{1}}=\frac{1}{2}\|y\|_{B^{n}_{1}}=t
, then

(101)

\displaystyle\mathscr{G}\!f(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{n-1}\delta(r+t)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r

(102)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{n-1}(1-r-t)_{\scriptscriptstyle+}^{2/s}\,\mathrm{d}r.

For
t>1
, this quantity is
0
. Otherwise, we get

(103)

\displaystyle\mathscr{G}\!f(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\int_{0}^{1-t}r^{n-1}(1-r-t)^{2/s}\,\mathrm{d}r

(104)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}(1-t)\int_{0}^{1}((1-t)s)^{n-1}((1-t)-(1-t)r)^{2/s}\,\mathrm{d}r

(105)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}(1-t)^{n+2/s}\int_{0}^{1}r^{n-1}(1-r)^{2/s}\,\mathrm{d}r,

which completes the proof.
∎

Using (
86
), (
89
) and Hölder’s inequality, we obtain the following inequalities from Theorem

.

Corollary 23
.

Let
s>0
. If

is
s
-concave on its support, then

\frac{(n\operatorname{B}(n,n+1+\frac{2}{s}))^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}}{\operatorname{B}(\alpha,n+1+\frac{2}{s})}\,|\operatorname{S}_{\alpha}\!f|^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\geq\|f\|_{2}^{2-\frac{2\alpha}{n}}\|f\|_{1}^{\frac{2\alpha}{n}}\geq\|f\|_{\frac{2n}{n+\alpha}}^{2}

for
\,0<\alpha<n
and the inequalities are reversed for

.

87
), (

), and Hölder’s inequalities, we obtain the following inequality from Theorem

.

Electronic transport in titanium carbide MXenes from first principles
Nesrine Boussadoune, Olivier Nadeau, Gabriel Antonius

Abstract
We compute from first principles the electronic, vibrational, and transport properties of four known MXenes: Ti 3 ​ C 2 \text{Ti}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} , Ti 3 ​ C 2 ​ F 2 \text{Ti}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{F}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} , Ti 3 ​ C 2 ​ ( OH ) 2 \text{Ti}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{(}\text{OH}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} , and Ti 2 ​ C F 2 \text{Ti}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{C}\text{F}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} . We study the effect of different surface terminations and monosheet thickness on the electrical conductivity, and show that the changes in conductivity can be explained by the squared velocity density of the electronic state, as well as their phonon scattering lifetime. We also compare the solution of the iterative Boltzmann transport equation (IBTE) to different linearized solutions, namely, the self-energy relaxation time approximation (SERTA) and the momentum relaxation time approximation (MRTA), and we show that the SERTA significantly underestimates the electrical conductivity while the MRTA yields results in better agreement with the IBTE. The computed monolayer conductivity at 300K is in reasonable agreement with reported experimental measurements.

The electronic transport properties can be computed from first principles

Brunin et al. 2020

while the IBTE can be achieved

Results and discussion

I.1
Computational details

We perform density functional theory (DFT)
and density functional perturbation theory (DFPT) calculations
using the Abinit software
Gonze et al. 2009
;
Gonze et al. 2020
,
to obtain the structural, electronic, and vibrational properties
of the materials.
We use the PBE exchange correlation functional
Perdew et al. 1996
,
with pseudopotentials from the Pseudo-Dojo database
van Setten et al. 2018
.
For all the structures considered,
we use an energy cutoff of 35 Hartree to represent the wavefunctions,
a
16\times 16\times 1
grid of k-points to sample the Brillouin zone,
such that the energy is converged within
10^{-6}
eV/cell.
We perform geometry optimization to relax the forces below
10^{-5}
eV/
\AA
.
A vacuum spacing of 20 Åis used, in order to avoid interactions between the periodic images of the MXenes monolayers.

P6_{3}/mmc

eV/
\AA

Electronic bands

The band structure and the projected density of states (PDOS)

All the materials are metallic,

I.4
Phonon bands

In Fig

8\times 8\times 1

From the projected phonon density of states,

I.5
Electrical conductivity

The electrical conductivity can be computed by solving the iterative Boltzmann transport equation (IBTE)
Poncé et al. 2018
;
Brown-Altvater et al. 2020
;
Giustino 2017

where the main scattering mechanism
are the phonon collisions,
which are described by the electron-phonon coupling matrix elements computed from first principles.
By making use of the relaxation time approximation,
the BTE can be linearized, avoiding the iterative procedure
and writing the electrical conductivity
\sigma_{\alpha}
as

\sigma_{\alpha}\ =\frac{-e}{\Omega}\sum_{n}\int\frac{d\mathbf{k}}{\Omega_{BZ}}\tau_{n\mathbf{k}}(T)\lvert v_{n\mathbf{k}\alpha}\rvert^{2}f^{\prime}(\varepsilon_{n\mathbf{k}})

(1)

where

\alpha
is a Cartesian direction,

\Omega
is the volume of the unit cell,

\Omega_{BZ}
is the volume of the Brillouin zone,

\tau_{n\mathbf{k}}(T)
is the temperature-dependent scattering lifetimes
of the electron state,

v_{n\mathbf{k}\alpha}
is the electron velocity,
and
f^{\prime}(\varepsilon)
is the derivative of the Fermi-Dirac distribution, which depends on temperature.
Different approximations exist for the computation of the electron scattering lifetime, including the Self-Energy Relaxation Time Approximation (SERTA)
Poncé et al. 2019
;
Poncé et al. 2018

and the Momentum Relaxation Time Approximation (MRTA)
Li 2015
;
Claes et al. 2022
.
In the former, the lifetime is computed from the inverse of the Fan-Migdal
electron-phonon coupling self-energy,
and in the latter, the lifetime is computed from the squared electron-phonon coupling
matrix elements weighted by an efficiency factor
which accounts for the momentum direction of the scattering states
relative to the electrical field
Claes et al. 2022
.

I.6
Convergence study

One of the main challenges in computing the electrical conductivity is the fine sampling of
electron (
k
-points) and phonon (
q
-points) wavevectors
required to converge the transport properties
Gonze et al. 2020
;
Wen et al. 2020
.
A dense
k
-mesh is required to achieve good sampling of the electronic occupations near the Fermi level, while a dense
q
-sampling is required to converge the electronic lifetimes
Wen et al. 2020
.
This is especially true in two-dimensional metals, where the density of states is expected to vary rapidly near the Fermi level, as can be seen in Fig
1
(
e-h
).

In order to optimize the overall computational cost,
we employ the Shankland-Koelling-Wood interpolation scheme
Shankland 2009
;
Koelling and Wood 1986
,
a feature recently made available
within the Abinit automated workflows
Gonze et al. 2020
;
Brunin et al. 2020
.
The electronic energies and wavefunctions
near the Fermi level are interpolated from coarse
k
-grid
onto a fine
k
-grid.
We set the coarse
k
-grid to
16\times 16\times 1

and vary the fine
k
-grid to perform the convergence study.

Figure
2
shows the convergence of the temperature-dependent electrical conductivity of
\text{Ti}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{F}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
with varying
k
-points and
q
-points grids.
From this figure, we conclude that a
64\times 64\times 1
homogeneous grid for both
k
-points and
q
-points are sufficiently converged, and we use these parameters for all the studied materials.

II
Conclusion

In summary, we performed a comparative study of the electronic transport
of the pristine
\text{Ti}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
,
terminated
\text{Ti}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{T}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(T = F, OH)
and
\text{Ti}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{CF}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
monosheets from first principles.
We computed the electrical conductivity of the MXenes with different relaxation time approximations,
as well as with the iterative Boltzmann transport equation.
We found that the SERTA underestimates the conductivity,
while the MRTA is in better agreement with the IBTE.
However, the relative differences among monosheets with different surface terminations can only be resolved by the iterative procedure.
Nonetheless,
the relaxation-time approximation provides a useful understanding
of the underlying physics
by decomposing the electrical conductivity
into scattering lifetime and squared velocity density.
The computed monolayer conductivity
is in reasonable agreement with experiments,
within 30% to 60%.
The methodology presented in this work may be used
to further explore candidate materials for energy storage.

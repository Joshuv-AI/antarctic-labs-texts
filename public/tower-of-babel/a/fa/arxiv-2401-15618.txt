Horizons and Null Infinity: A Fugue in 4 voices
Abhay Ashtekar, Simone Speziale

Abstract
Black hole horizons in equilibrium and null infinity of asymptotically flat space-times are null 3-manifolds but have very different physical connotations. We first show that they share a large number of geometric properties, making them both weakly isolated horizons. We then use this new unified perspective to unravel the origin of the drastic differences in the physics they contain. Interestingly, the themes are woven together in a manner reminiscent of voices in a fugue.



Introduction:
Two types of boundaries commonly arise in general relativity: future horizons
\Delta^{\!{}^{+}}
of black holes in equilibrium and Penrose’s future null infinity
\mathscr{I}^{+}
. While it is obvious that both these boundaries are null 3-surfaces, it is not well appreciated that they share other, more subtle geometric structures as well. In particular each of them inherits an intrinsically defined derivative operator with certain properties that ensure that they are both
Weakly Isolated Horizons
(WIHs)
aass1
;
afk
;
abl1
. At first this appears surprising –even impossible– because while WIHs are generally thought of as representing black hole boundaries in strong field regions,
\mathscr{I}^{+}
lies in asymptotic, near-flat regions. More importantly, WIHs are often associated with
‘stationary’
black holes since there is no flux of gravitational waves (or matter fields) across them, while
\mathscr{I}^{+}
is the quintessential arena to calculate the fluxes of energy and momenta carried by gravitational waves (as well as other massless fields). The purpose of this Letter is to summarize recent work
aass1
that compares and contrasts their geometry and physics, and resolves this apparent tension.

As we recall below, a WIH horizon
\mathfrak{h}
can be defined purely in geometrical terms,
without reference
to any field equations. Nonetheless, WIHs carry a universal structure which is very similar to that of
\mathscr{I}^{+}
. As a result, the symmetry group
\mathfrak{G}
of WIHs
\mathfrak{h}
is closely related to the BMS group
akkl1
. Second, the intrinsic (degenerate) metric
\bar{q}_{ab}
is non-dynamical on
\mathfrak{h}
just as it at
\mathscr{I}^{+}
. The third common element seems counter intuitive at first:
the intrinsic connection
\bar{D}
on a general
\mathfrak{h}
is dynamical
just as it is at
\mathscr{I}^{+}
. This important point is often overlooked in the literature since WIHs have been often regarded as ‘stationary boundaries’.

Both black hole horizons
\Delta^{\!{}^{+}}
and null infinity
\mathscr{I}^{+}
are examples of geometric WIHs
\mathfrak{h}
. However, while
\Delta^{\!{}^{+}}
is a WIH with respect to the
physical
metric

that satisfies Einstein’s equations,
\mathscr{I}^{+}
is a WIH with respect to the rescaled metric

used in the Penrose completion that satisfies
conformal
Einstein’s equations. Einstein’s equations imply that the time dependence of the connection
D
on
\Delta^{\!{}^{+}}
is extremely constrained:
D
is completely determined on the entire 3-manifold
\Delta^{\!{}^{+}}
by ‘data’ that can be specified on a 2-dimensional cross-section
abl1
. Therefore, in spite of its time dependence,
D
does not carry
any
3-d degrees of freedom. Physically this means that ‘radiative modes’ are absent on
\Delta^{\!{}^{+}}
; the connection
D
on
\Delta^{\!{}^{+}}
encodes only ‘Coulombic information’. The situation is just the opposite at
\mathscr{I}^{+}
: conformal Einstein’s equations imply that the intrinsic connection
\hat{D}
captures precisely the two radiative modes of the gravitational field
aa-radiativemodes
; it carries no ‘Coulombic information’! We will see that this striking difference emerges from the
same
evolution equation, Eq (
2
), satisfied by the connection on any WIH
\mathfrak{h}
. It is remarkable that both the close geometrical similarities and the diametrically opposite physics emerges from the common WIH framework.

This structure is also subtle. For example, one may envisage using isolated horizons (IHs)
Ashtekar_2000
in place of WIHs. This strengthening of conditions may seem innocuous since the notion of IHs is also considerably weaker than that of Killing horizons. (For example, the Robinson-Trautman and Kastor-Traschen solutions admit IHs but no Killing horizons

;
Kastor_1993

\mathscr{I}^{+}
of any asymptotically flat space-time is a WIH, it would be an IH only if the Bondi news vanishes, i.e., there is no radiation across
\mathscr{I}^{+}
!

As discussed in
aass1
all our considerations apply also to past horizons and past null infinity and, in presence of a positive
\Lambda

\Delta
which may not be associated with black holes. In this brief report we focus on
\Delta^{+}
and
\mathscr{I}^{+}
because physically they are the most interesting cases. Also, for simplicity we assume that the physical metric satisfies Einstein’s equations in a neighborhood of
\Delta^{\!{}^{+}}
and
\mathscr{I}^{+}
; one can allow appropriate sources.

We will now summarize a new Hamiltonian framework, geared to local degrees of freedom, that serves as a common platform to discuss fluxes of physical observables associated with symmetries at both

Discussion:
To summarize,
\Delta^{\!{}^{+}}
and
\mathscr{I}^{+}
share a number of properties, inherited directly from geometric WIHs
\mathfrak{h}
. In particular, their intrinsic metrics are non-dynamical and their symmetry groups are almost the same. In both cases, the intrinsic connections
D
and
\hat{D}
are dynamical, satisfying Eq. (
2
) that holds on any
\mathfrak{h}
. But complementary terms on the right side of this equation trivialize: While the Ricci terms vanish in
\Delta^{\!{}^{+}}
, the Weyl term vanishes on
\mathscr{I}^{+}
. Thanks to this subtle and surprising ‘complementarity’,
\Delta^{\!{}^{+}}
and
\mathscr{I}^{+}
can share a large number of properties and, at the same time, display diametrically opposite physics.
\Delta^{\!{}^{+}}
represents black hole horizons in equilibrium that lie in the strong field regime, and no radiation crosses them.
\mathscr{I}^{+}
lies in the near-flat asymptotic regime and provides the arena for discussions of fluxes carried away by gravitational waves! This unified WIH framework can provide a foundation for ‘gravitational wave tomography’
aa-banff
– imaging horizon dynamics using black hole mergers using waveforms at
\mathscr{I}^{+}
– and also have applications to the analysis of black hole evaporation in quantum gravity.

This interwoven structure is reminiscent of a musical fugue in four voices. The first voice is that of a geometric WIHs
\mathfrak{h}
. It introduces the theme/subject. The second voice is that of black hole horizons
\Delta^{\!{}^{+}}
. It picks up on the theme and further develops it, providing us with one interpretation of the main subject that conveys the serenity of equilibrium. The third voice is that of null infinity
\mathscr{I}^{+}
. It continues the main theme but is contrapuntal because it exudes change and dynamism, with potential for bursts of energy and momentum. The fourth voice returns to the central idea enunciated by the first: a single theme –now, the phase space of local degrees of freedom– can display the deep-down unity in spite of the diversity of manifestations.

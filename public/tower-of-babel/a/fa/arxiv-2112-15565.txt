Abstract
. In analogy to the various characterizations of Gromov hyperbolicity, we present a list of six mutually equivalent higher rank conditions for metric spaces satisfying some assumption reminiscent of global non-positive curvature.

Introduction

The concept of Gromov hyperbolicity manifests itself in many different ways.
With only mild assumptions on the underlying metric space,
the spectrum of equivalent properties includes various thin triangle
conditions, the stability of quasi-geodesics (the Morse lemma),
a linear isoperimetric filling inequality for closed curves,
and a sub-quadratic isoperimetric inequality

,

We present a similar list of six equivalent properties in the context of
generalized non-positive curvature and higher asymptotic rank.
This complements the results in
[
40
]
and in the recent
paper
[
28
]
. We give a largely self-contained proof, providing
some improvements and simplifications for the known part.

For an informal statement of the main result, let us focus on the
special case that
X
is a proper
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
or Busemann convex space.
In passing from Gromov hyperbolicity to rank
n\geq 2
, the
role of closed curves and quasi-geodesics is transferred to
n
-cycles and

n
-chains satisfying a suitable quasi-minimality condition, respectively.
For the moment, the reader is invited to think of the chain complex of
Lipschitz singular chains with integer coefficients in
X
. Some of the
statements below involve a uniform polynomial mass bound of degree
n
in
large balls, and we shall thus speak of
n
-chains with

controlled density
. This condition holds automatically
for Lipschitz quasi-geodesics if
n=1
or, more generally, for Lipschitz
quasi-isometric embeddings of domains in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
into
X
.
We show that the following are equivalent:

•

the
asymptotic rank
of
X
being at most
n
(see below
for the definition);

•

a
sub-Euclidean isoperimetric inequality
for
n
-cycles,
corresponding to a sub-quadratic inequality in the case
n=1
;

•

a
linear isoperimetric inequality
for
n
-cycles with controlled
density;

•

a version of the
Morse lemma
implying in particular a bound on the
Hausdorff distance between (the supports of) two quasi-minimizing
n
-chains
with controlled density and equal boundary;

•

a
slim
(n+1)
-simplex property
analogous to the slimness of
quasi-geodesic triangles in geodesic Gromov hyperbolic spaces;

•

a bound on the
filling radius
of
n
-cycles with controlled
density.

We now proceed to the details. The actual setup is as in
[
28
]
.
Suppose that
X=(X,d)
is a proper metric space, that is, closed bounded
subsets are compact. We use the chain complex
{\mathbf{I}}_{*,\text{\rm c}}(X)
of metric
integral currents with compact support, which comprises the singular Lipschitz
chains but is more versatile and has suitable compactness properties.
The relevant prerequisites from the theory of metric currents will be
discussed in Sect.
2
.
For
n\geq 1
, we say that
X
satisfies
condition
({\rm CI}_{n})
if there
is a constant
c
such that any two points
x,y
in
X
can be joined by
a curve of length
\leq c\,d(x,y)
, and for
k=1,\ldots,n
, every
k
-cycle

R\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(X)
in some
r
-ball is the boundary of an

S\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k+1,\text{\rm c}}(X)
with mass

{\mathbf{M}}(S)\leq c\,r\,{\mathbf{M}}(R).

The cone inequalities
({\rm CI}_{n})
hold in particular, for all
n
,
if
X
is a
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space or a space with a conical geodesic
bicombing
[
11
,
36
]
.
We remark that every hyperbolic group acts geometrically on a proper
polyhedral complex with such a bicombing
[
30
]
, and further classes
of groups with this property are discussed in
[
8
,
22
,
24
,
32
]
.
Moreover, condition
({\rm CI}_{n})
holds if
X
is an
n
-connected Riemannian
manifold with a geometric action of a (quasi-geodesically) combable group;
compare Theorem 10.3.5 in
[
13
]
.

The
asymptotic rank
of
X
is the supremum of all
k\geq 0
for
which there exist a sequence
0<r_{i}\to\infty
and subsets
Y_{i}\subset X

such that the rescaled sets
(Y_{i},\frac{1}{r_{i}}d)
converge in the
Gromov–Hausdorff topology to the unit ball in some
k
-dimensional normed
space. This is a quasi-isometry invariant, and if
X
is a geodesic metric
space satisfying
({\rm CI}_{1})
, then the asymptotic rank is at most
1
if and
only if
X
is Gromov hyperbolic
[
40
]
.
If
X
is a cocompact
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space or a cocompact space with a
conical geodesic bicombing, then the asymptotic
rank equals the maximal dimension of an isometrically embedded Euclidean
or normed space, respectively
[
10
,
27
]
.

Let
S\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X)
. For constants
C\geq 1
and
a\geq 0
, we say that

S
has
(C,a)
-controlled density
if for all
x\in X
and
r>a
,
the piece of
S
in the closed
r
-ball at
x
has mass at most
Cr^{n}
, or

\Theta_{x,r}(S):=\frac{1}{r^{n}}{\mathbf{M}}(S\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}B_{x}(r))\leq C.

For almost every
r>0
, the boundary
\partial(S\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}B_{x}(r))
has finite mass
and
S\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}B_{x}(r)
is itself an element of
{\mathbf{I}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X)

(see again Sect.
2
).
Suppose that
Y\subset X
is a closed set containing the support

\operatorname{spt}(\partial S)
of
\partial S
. For
Q\geq 1
and
a\geq 0
, we say that
S
is

(Q,a)
-quasi-minimizing mod

if for every point
x\in\operatorname{spt}(S)

at a distance

from

, the inequality

{\mathbf{M}}(S\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}B_{x}(r))\leq Q\,{\mathbf{M}}(T)

holds for almost all

and all

with
\partial T=\partial(S\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}B_{x}(r))
.
A current
S\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X)
is called a
(Q,a)
-quasi-minimizer

if
S
is
(Q,a)
-quasi-minimizing mod
\operatorname{spt}(\partial S)
.
As for the analogy with quasi-geodesics, one can easily check that
for a Lipschitz quasi-isometric embedding
\gamma\colon[0,l]\to X
of an
interval, the associated current
\gamma_{\#}\llbracket 0,l\rrbracket\in{\mathbf{I}}_{1,\text{\rm c}}(X)

is a quasi-minimizer with controlled density (compare the case

of

). For

, quasi-minimizers offer
more flexibility than quasiflats.

We can now state the main result of this paper. Detailed comments
and references are given below.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
X
is a proper metric space satisfying condition
(CI
n
)

for some
n\geq 1
. Then the following six properties are equivalent:

({\rm AR}_{n})

(asymptotic rank) the asymptotic rank of
X
is at most
n
;

({\rm SII}_{n})

(sub-Euclidean isoperimetric inequality) for all
\epsilon>0
there
is a constant
M_{0}>0
such that every cycle
Z\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X)
is
the boundary of a
V\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n+1,\text{\rm c}}(X)
with mass

{\mathbf{M}}(V)<\epsilon\max\{M_{0},{\mathbf{M}}(Z)\}^{(n+1)/n}
;

({\rm LII}_{n})

(linear isoperimetric inequality) there is a constant
\nu>0
,
and for all
C>0
there is a
\lambda>0
, such that every cycle

Z\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X)
with
(C,a)
-controlled density bounds a

V\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n+1,\text{\rm c}}(X)
with
{\mathbf{M}}(V)\leq\max\{\lambda,\nu a\}\,{\mathbf{M}}(Z)
;

({\rm ML}_{n})

(Morse lemma) for all
C>0
and
Q\geq 1
there is a constant

l\geq 0
such that if
Z\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X)
is a cycle with

(C,a)
-controlled density and
Y\subset X
is a closed set such that
Z
is

(Q,a)
-quasi-minimizing mod
Y
, then
\operatorname{spt}(Z)
is within distance
at most
\max\{l,4a\}
from
Y
;

({\rm SS}_{n})

(slim simplices) for all
L\geq 1
there is a constant
D\geq 0

such that if
\Delta
is a Euclidean
(n+1)
-simplex and

f\colon\partial\Delta\to X
is a map whose restriction to each facet of
\Delta

is an
(L,a)
-quasi-isometric embedding, then the image of every facet is
within distance at most
D(1+a)
of the union of the images of the
remaining ones;

({\rm FR}_{n})

(filling radius) for all
C>0
there is a constant
h>0
such
that every cycle
Z\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X)
with
(C,a)
-controlled density
bounds a
V\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n+1,\text{\rm c}}(X)
whose support is within distance at most

\max\{h,a\}
from
\operatorname{spt}(Z)
.

Note that for
n=1
,
({\rm SII}_{n})
corresponds to a sub-quadratic inequality.
The equivalence of
({\rm AR}_{n})
and
({\rm SII}_{n})
was established in
[
40
]

in a more general setup for complete metric spaces. The proof of the forward
implication used an elaborate thick-thin decomposition for integral cycles
from
[
39
]
and also the non-trivial fact that a weakly convergent
sequence of cycles converges with respect to the filling volume
[
37
]
.
We review the entire argument. Employing an elegant new variational result
from
[
23
]
and introducing a more quantitative approach for the
weak convergence of cycles, we reduce the overall complexity substantially.
In fact, we prove the sub-Euclidean isoperimetric inequality first in a
somewhat restricted form (Theorem
5.1
, compare Theorem 4.4
in
[
28
]
) and then deduce
({\rm LII}_{n})
and
({\rm SII}_{n})
.

The implication
({\rm AR}_{n})\Rightarrow({\rm LII}_{n})
pertains to a long-standing
open problem. In symmetric spaces of non-compact type or homogeneous
Hadamard manifolds of rank
\leq n
, a linear isoperimetric inequality holds
for all
n
-cycles (see p. 105 in
[
21
]
,
[
31
]
, and
[
25
]
).
It is still unkown whether this generalizes, for instance, to cocompact
Hadamard manifolds or
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
spaces of (asymptotic) rank
\leq n
.
In our statement, the isoperimetric constant depends on the density bound,
so Theorem
1.1
does not resolve this question.
Nevertheless,
({\rm LII}_{n})
turns out to be equivalent to the remaining
properties. Examples of cycles with controlled density include
cycles lying near the union of finitely many quasiflats (see in particular
the proof of Theorem
7.2
).

The proofs of Theorem 5.1 and Theorem 5.2 in
[
28
]
show that

({\rm SII}_{n})\Rightarrow({\rm ML}_{n})\Rightarrow({\rm SS}_{n})
, except for a less
explicit distance bound in the slim simplex property. The second step
involves an approximation result for quasiflats by quasi-minimizers.
We go through the argument in detail, keeping track of the dependence of
constants, and thus showing that the bound is linear in the
coarseness parameter
a
. This fact is used in the proof of
the backward implication
({\rm SS}_{n})\Rightarrow({\rm AR}_{n})
.

The statement of
({\rm ML}_{n})
differs formally from the usual stability
assertion for quasi-geodesics, but is versatile.
If
S\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n,c}(X)
with
\operatorname{spt}(\partial S)\subset Y
is
quasi-minimizing mod
Y
, then the extra assumption we need in order to
conclude that
S
is confined to a bounded neighborhood of
Y

is that
S
can be closed up to a cycle
Z=S-S^{\prime}

with controlled density and with
\operatorname{spt}(S^{\prime})\subset Y
. Note that there
is no (quasi-)minimality assumption on
S^{\prime}
; the density bound suffices.
However, if
S_{1},S_{2}\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X)
are two
(Q,a)
-quasi-minimizers with

\partial S_{1}=\partial S_{2}
, each with
(C,a)
-controlled density, then
S_{1}
is

(Q,a)
-quasi-minimizing mod
\operatorname{spt}(S_{2})
and vice-versa, so
({\rm ML}_{n})
implies
that the Hausdorff distance between the supports is bounded by
\max\{l,4a\}

for
l=l(2C,Q)
.

The last assertion of Theorem
1.1
is yet another way of
expressing that
n
-cycles with controlled density (such as geodesic
triangles if
n=1
) are thin. We use an iterative application of the
sub-Euclidean isoperimetric inequality to show that the conclusion of

({\rm FR}_{n})
holds for every mass minimizing
V
with
\partial V=Z
.
In
[
40
]
, the filling radius was used to prove that

({\rm SII}_{n})\Rightarrow({\rm AR}_{n})
. Similarly,
({\rm FR}_{n})\Rightarrow({\rm AR}_{n})
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Sect.
2
we recall the
definition of metric currents and collect some basic results.
In Sect.
3
we first review an approximation result for cycles
from
[
23
]
and then use this to give a short proof of a quantitative
version of a result from
[
37
]
, showing that cycles with
bounded mass and sufficiently small uniformly bounded density at some
fixed scale have small filling volume. We use this further in
Sect.
4
to discuss the convergence of cycles.
Sect.
5
is then devoted to isoperimetric inequalities and
shows in particular that

({\rm AR}_{n})\Rightarrow({\rm LII}_{n})\Leftrightarrow({\rm SII}_{n}).

In Sect.
6
we prove more explicit versions of two propositions
from
[
28
]
relating quasiflats and quasi-minimizers.
The concluding Sect.
7
then shows in particular that

({\rm SII}_{n})\Rightarrow({\rm ML}_{n})\Rightarrow({\rm SS}_{n})\Rightarrow({\rm AR}_{n})\quad\text{and}\quad({\rm SII}_{n})\Rightarrow({\rm FR}_{n})\Rightarrow({\rm AR}_{n}).

In fact, we prove all implications (and hence Theorem
1.1
)
in a stronger form, for any class of proper metric spaces satisfying
the respective assumptions uniformly, and with constants depending only
on the data involved and on the class, rather than on individual members
(see Sect.
5
and Sect.
7
).

What is missing from the list in Theorem
1.1
is a rank
n
analog
of Gromov’s quadruple definition of
\delta
-hyperbolicity
(
[
20
]
, p. 89).
A
2(n+1)
-point condition of this type is investigated in
[
26
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

Currents with finite mass in complete metric spaces were introduced by
Ambrosio and Kirchheim in
[
1
]
. Here, for consistency with
[
28
]
,
we will use the local theory described in
[
29
]
. For the class of
integral currents with compact support, the principal objects in this paper,
the formal difference between the two approaches is marginal. Assuming the
underlying metric space to be proper, we will make frequent use of the
existence of area minimizing integral currents filling a given cycle
(Theorem
2.3
). Without this assumption, as an additional twist,
one could still work with almost minimal currents instead (see, for example,
Lemma 3.4 in
[
36
]
). In particular, Theorem
1.1
and most
results in the paper hold more generally for complete metric spaces and
Ambrosio–Kirchheim integral currents.

Currents

An integral
n
-current may roughly be thought of as an oriented

n
-dimensional Lipschitz surface equipped with a summable integer density
function. Formally though,
n
-currents are defined as functionals;
on compactly supported differential
n
-forms in the classical case
(going back to de Rham), and on suitable
(n+1)
-tuples of
real-valued locally Lipschitz functions for non-smooth ambient spaces.
The relating principle (originally proposed by De Giorgi) is that the tuple

(f_{0},\ldots,f_{n})
, say if the
f_{i}
are smooth functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
,
represents the form
f_{0}\,df_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge df_{n}
.

Let
X=(X,d)
be a proper metric space. For
n\geq 0
, we let
{\mathscr{D}}^{n}(X)

denote the set of all
(n+1)
-tuples
(f_{0},\ldots,f_{n})
of Lipschitz
functions
f_{i}\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that
f_{0}
has compact support

\operatorname{spt}(f_{0})
(in
[
29
]
,
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}
are merely locally Lipschitz,
but the following definition is equivalent). An

n
-dimensional current

S
in
X
is a function

S\colon{\mathscr{D}}^{n}(X)\to{\mathbb{R}}
satisfying the following three conditions:

(1)

S
is
(n+1)
-linear;

(2)

S(f_{0,k},\ldots,f_{n,k})\to S(f_{0},\ldots,f_{n})

whenever
f_{i,k}\to f_{i}
pointwise on
X
with uniformly bounded
Lipschitz constants (
i=0,\dots,n
) and with
\bigcup_{k}\operatorname{spt}(f_{0,k})\subset K

for some compact set
K\subset X
;

(3)

S(f_{0},\ldots,f_{n})=0
whenever one of the functions

f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}
is constant on a neighborhood of
\operatorname{spt}(f_{0})
.

It follows from these axioms that
S
is alternating in the
last
n
arguments. The vector space of all
n
-dimensional currents
in
X
is denoted
{\mathscr{D}}_{n}(X)
. Every function
w\in L^{1}_{\text{\rm loc}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
induces
a current
\llbracket w\rrbracket\in{\mathscr{D}}_{n}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
defined by

\llbracket w\rrbracket(f_{0},\dots,f_{n}):=\int wf_{0}\det\bigl[\partial_{j}f_{i}\bigr]_{i,j=1}^{n}\,dx;

note that the partial derivatives
\partial_{j}f_{i}
exist almost everywhere by
Rademacher’s theorem. For a Borel set
W\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
we put

\llbracket W\rrbracket:=\llbracket\chi_{W}\rrbracket
, where
\chi_{W}
denotes the characteristic function.
(See Sect. 2 in
[
29
]
for details.)

Support, push-forward, and boundary

Let
S\in{\mathscr{D}}_{n}(X)
. There exists a smallest closed subset of
X
,
the
support

\operatorname{spt}(S)
of
S
, such that the value
S(f_{0},\ldots,f_{n})

depends only on the restrictions of
f_{0},\dots,f_{n}
to this set.
Thus, for any closed set
D\subset X
containing
\operatorname{spt}(S)
,

S
induces a current in
{\mathscr{D}}_{n}(D)
, still denoted by
S
.
For a proper Lipschitz map
\phi\colon D\to Y
into another proper
metric space
Y
, the
push-forward

\phi_{\#}S\in{\mathscr{D}}_{n}(Y)
is the
current with support in
\phi(\operatorname{spt}(S))
defined by

(\phi_{\#}S)(f_{0},\ldots,f_{n}):=S(f_{0}\circ\phi,\ldots,f_{n}\circ\phi)

for all
(f_{0},\ldots,f_{n})\in{\mathscr{D}}^{n}(Y)
. In the simplest case,
if
\llbracket a,b\rrbracket:=\llbracket[a,b]\rrbracket
is the current in
{\mathscr{D}}_{1}({\mathbb{R}})
(or
{\mathscr{D}}_{1}([a,b])
)
associated with an interval, and if
\gamma\colon[a,b]\to X
is a Lipschitz
curve, then

\gamma_{\#}\llbracket a,b\rrbracket(f_{0},f_{1})=\llbracket a,b\rrbracket(f_{0}\circ\gamma,f_{1}\circ\gamma)=\int_{a}^{b}(f_{0}\circ\gamma)(f_{1}\circ\gamma)^{\prime}\,ds

for all
(f_{0},f_{1})\in{\mathscr{D}}^{1}(X)
. Similarly, every singular Lipschitz

n
-chain in
X
defines an element of
{\mathscr{D}}_{n}(X)
; in fact, of
{\mathbf{I}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X)

(see below for the definition, and
[
2
,
17
]
for some reverse
approximation results).

If
S\in{\mathscr{D}}_{n}(X)
and
n\geq 1
, then the
boundary

\partial S\in{\mathscr{D}}_{n-1}(X)
is defined by

(\partial S)(f_{0},\dots,f_{n-1}):=S(\tau,f_{0},\dots,f_{n-1})

for all
(f_{0},\ldots,f_{n-1})\in{\mathscr{D}}^{n-1}(X)
and for any
\tau\in{\mathscr{D}}^{0}(X)

such that
\tau\equiv 1
in a neighborhood of
\operatorname{spt}(f_{0})
.
It follows from (1) and (3) that
\partial S
is well-defined and that

\partial\circ\partial=0
. Furthermore,
\operatorname{spt}(\partial S)\subset\operatorname{spt}(S)
, and

\phi_{\#}(\partial S)=\partial(\phi_{\#}S)
for
\phi\colon D\to Y
as above.
In the example of a Lipschitz curve,

\partial(\gamma_{\#}\llbracket a,b\rrbracket)(f_{0})=f_{0}(\gamma(b))-f_{0}(\gamma(a))
by the fundamental
theorem of calculus. (See Sect. 3 in
[
29
]
.)

Mass

Let
S\in{\mathscr{D}}_{n}(X)
. For an open set
U\subset X
,
the
mass

\|S\|(U)\in[0,\infty]
of
S
in
U
is defined as
the supremum of
\sum_{k}S(f_{0,k},\ldots,f_{n,k})

over all finite families of tuples
(f_{0,k},\ldots,f_{n,k})\in{\mathscr{D}}^{n}(X)

such that
\bigcup_{k}\operatorname{spt}(f_{0,k})\subset U
,
\sum_{k}|f_{0,k}|\leq 1
, and

f_{1,k},\ldots,f_{n,k}
are
1
-Lipschitz.
This extends to a regular Borel measure
\|S\|
on
X
with

\operatorname{spt}(\|S\|)=\operatorname{spt}(S)
, and
{\mathbf{M}}(S):=\|S\|(X)
denotes the
total mass
.
For Borel sets
W,A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
\|\llbracket W\rrbracket\|(A)
equals the Lebesgue measure
of
W\cap A
. For
S,T\in{\mathscr{D}}_{n}(X)
,

\|S+T\|\leq\|S\|+\|T\|.

If the measure
\|S\|
is locally finite, then

|S(f_{0},\ldots,f_{n})|\leq\prod_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{Lip}(f_{i})\int_{X}|f_{0}|\,d\|S\|

for all
(f_{0},\ldots,f_{n})\in{\mathscr{D}}^{n}(X)
, where
\operatorname{Lip}(f_{i})
denotes the
Lipschitz constant. As a consequence,
S
extends to
tuples whose first entry is merely a bounded Borel function
with compact support, and if
u\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}
is any bounded Borel
function, one can define the
restriction

S\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}u\in{\mathscr{D}}_{n}(X)
by

(S\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}u)(f_{0},\dots,f_{n}):=S(uf_{0},f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})

for all
(f_{0},\ldots,f_{n})\in{\mathscr{D}}^{n}(X)
. For a Borel set
A\subset X
,

S\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}A:=S\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}\chi_{A}
.
The measure
\|S\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}A\|
agrees with the restriction of
\|S\|
to
A
.
If
\phi\colon D\to Y
is as above, and
B\subset Y
is a Borel set, then

(\phi_{\#}S)\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}B=\phi_{\#}(S\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}\phi^{-1}(B))
and

\|\phi_{\#}S\|(B)\leq\operatorname{Lip}(\phi)^{n}\,\|S\|(\phi^{-1}(B)).

(See Sect. 4 in
[
29
]
.)

Integral currents

A current
S\in{\mathscr{D}}_{n}(X)
is
locally integer rectifiable

if
\|S\|
is locally finite and concentrated on the union of countably
many Lipschitz images of compact subsets of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
and for every Borel set
A\subset X
with compact closure and every Lipschitz
map
\phi\colon\,\overline{\!A}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, the current
\phi_{\#}(S\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}A)\in{\mathscr{D}}_{n}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})

is of the form
\llbracket w\rrbracket
for some
integer valued

w\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. Then
\|S\|
is absolutely
continuous with respect to
n
-dimensional Hausdorff measure.
Push-forwards and restrictions to Borel sets of locally integer
rectifiable currents are again locally integer rectifiable.

A current
S\in{\mathscr{D}}_{n}(X)
is called a
locally integral current

if
S
is locally integer rectifiable and, for
n\geq 1
,
\|\partial S\|

is locally finite; then (by Theorem 8.7 in
[
29
]
)
\partial S
is itself
locally integer rectifiable.
This gives a chain complex of abelian groups
{\mathbf{I}}_{n,\text{\rm loc}}(X)
.
The subgroups
{\mathbf{I}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X)
of
integral currents
consist
of the elements with compact support and, hence, finite total mass.
For
X={\mathbb{R}}^{N}
, there is a canonical chain isomorphism from
{\mathbf{I}}_{*,\text{\rm c}}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})

to the chain complex of classical (Federer–Fleming)
integral currents
[
15
]
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
.

For
n\geq 1
, we put
{\mathbf{Z}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X):=\{Z\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X):\partial Z=0\}
.
For
n=0
, an element of
{\mathbf{I}}_{0,\text{\rm c}}(X)
is an integral
linear combination of currents of the form
\llbracket x\rrbracket
, where

\llbracket x\rrbracket(f_{0})=f_{0}(x)
. We let
{\mathbf{Z}}_{0,\text{\rm c}}(X)\subset{\mathbf{I}}_{0,\text{\rm c}}(X)
denote the
subgroup of linear combinations whose coefficients add up to zero.
The boundary of a current in
{\mathbf{I}}_{1,\text{\rm c}}(X)
belongs to
{\mathbf{Z}}_{0,\text{\rm c}}(X)
.
Given
Z\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X)
, for
n\geq 0
, we will call

V\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n+1,\text{\rm c}}(X)
a
filling
of
Z
if
\partial V=Z
.

Slicing

Let
S\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n,\text{\rm loc}}(X)
,
n\geq 1
, and let
\pi\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}

be a Lipschitz function.
For
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the
slice

T_{s}\in{\mathscr{D}}_{n-1}(X)
of
S
with respect
to
\pi
is the current

T_{s}:=\partial(S\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}\{\pi\leq s\})-(\partial S)\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}\{\pi\leq s\}

with support in
\{\pi=s\}\cap\operatorname{spt}(S)
. Note that the restrictions
are defined since both
\|S\|
and
\|\partial S\|
are locally finite.
For
a<b
, the coarea inequality

\int_{a}^{b}{\mathbf{M}}(T_{s})\,ds\leq\operatorname{Lip}(\pi)\,\|S\|(\{a<\pi<b\})

holds, and if
\pi|_{\operatorname{spt}(S)}
is proper, then
T_{s}\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n-1,\text{\rm c}}(X)

for almost all
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
. (See Sect. 6 and Theorem 8.5 in
[
29
]
.)

Convergence and compactness

A sequence
(S_{i})
in
{\mathscr{D}}_{n}(X)

converges weakly

to a current
S\in{\mathscr{D}}_{n}(X)
if
S_{i}\to S
pointwise as functionals
on
{\mathscr{D}}^{n}(X)
. Then, for every open set
U\subset X
,

\|S\|(U)\leq\liminf_{i\to\infty}\|S_{i}\|(U),

thus the mass is lower semicontinuous with respect to weak convergence.
Furthermore, weak convergence commutes with the boundary operator and
with push-forwards. For locally integral currents, the following
compactness theorem holds (see Theorem 8.10 in
[
29
]
).

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
X
be a proper metric space, and let
n\geq 1
.
If
(S_{i})
is a sequence in
{\mathbf{I}}_{n,\text{\rm loc}}(X)
such that

\sup_{i}(\|S_{i}\|+\|\partial S_{i}\|)(K)<\infty

for every compact set
K\subset X
, then some subsequence

(S_{i_{k}})
converges weakly to a current
S\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n,\text{\rm loc}}(X)
.

Isoperimetric inequality and Plateau problem

Recall condition
({\rm CI}_{n})
from the introduction.
Cone inequalities are instrumental for the proof of isoperimetric
inequalities of Euclidean type (compare Sect. 3.4 in
[
19
]
).
For
n\geq 1
, we say that
X
satisfies
({\rm EII}_{n})
if there is a constant

\gamma>0
such that every cycle
Z\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X)
has a filling

V\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n+1,\text{\rm c}}(X)
with mass

{\mathbf{M}}(V)\leq\gamma\,{\mathbf{M}}(Z)^{(n+1)/n}.

To make the constants in
({\rm CI}_{n})
and
({\rm EII}_{n})
explicit, we will
write
({\rm CI}_{n})[c]
and
({\rm EII}_{n})[\gamma]
.
The following result was established in a more general form
in Theorem 1.2 in
[
36
]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

For all
n\geq 1
and
c>0
there is a constant
\gamma>0
such that
for every proper metric space
X
,
({\rm CI}_{n})[c]
implies
({\rm EII}_{n})[\gamma]
.

(Here the quasi-convexity condition (CI
0
) is actually not needed.)
By Theorem
2.1
and a well-known application of
({\rm EII}_{n})

one gets the following existence result for minimizing integral currents
(see the proof of Theorem 2.4 in
[
28
]
).

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
Z\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X)
, where
X
is a proper metric space
satisfying
({\rm CI}_{0})
if
n=0
and
({\rm EII}_{n})[\gamma]
if
n\geq 1
.
Then there is a filling
V\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n+1,\text{\rm loc}}(X)
of
Z
with mass

{\mathbf{M}}(V)=\inf\{{\mathbf{M}}(V^{\prime}):V^{\prime}\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n+1,\text{\rm loc}}(X),\,\partial V^{\prime}=Z\}<\infty.

In fact, every such minimizing
V
has compact support due to the following
lower density bound: if
x\in\operatorname{spt}(V)
,
r>0
, and

B_{x}(r)\cap\operatorname{spt}(Z)=\emptyset
, then

\Theta_{x,r}(V):=\frac{\|V\|(B_{x}(r))}{r^{n+1}}\geq\delta_{0}:=\begin{cases}2&\text{if $n=0$,}\\
(n+1)^{-(n+1)}\gamma^{-n}&\text{if $n\geq 1$;}\end{cases}

thus
\operatorname{spt}(V)
is within distance
({\mathbf{M}}(V)/\delta_{0})^{1/(n+1)}
from
\operatorname{spt}(Z)
.

3.
A variational argument

We start with a slight modification and extension of an effective recent
approximation result, Proposition 4.2 in
[
23
]
.
The main conclusion is that for a cycle
Z
and any
\eta>0
there is a
cycle
Z^{\prime}
with mass
\leq{\mathbf{M}}(Z)
such that
Z-Z^{\prime}
has a minimizing filling
with mass
\leq\eta\,{\mathbf{M}}(Z)
and
Z^{\prime}
satisfies a uniform
lower

density bound at scales
\lesssim\eta
. This will be used in the proofs
of Theorem
3.2
and Theorem
5.1
.
We show in addition that if
Z
satisfies a uniform
upper
density bound
above some threshold radius, then the same holds for
Z^{\prime}
;
see assertion (5) below. This will be employed in
Theorem
5.3
(linear isoperimetric inequality).

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
n\geq 1
and
\gamma>0
. Suppose that
X
is a proper metric space
satisfying
({\rm EII}_{n})[\gamma]
and, if
n\geq 2
, also
({\rm EII}_{n-1})[\gamma]
.
Then for every
Z\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X)
and
\eta>0
there exists a minimizing

V\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n+1,\text{\rm c}}(X)
such that the following holds for

Z^{\prime}:=Z-\partial V,\quad\mu:=\eta^{-1}\|V\|+\|Z^{\prime}\|,

and some constants
\alpha,\theta>0
depending only on
n
and
\gamma
:

(1)

\mu(X)\leq{\mathbf{M}}(Z)
, in particular
{\mathbf{M}}(Z^{\prime})\leq{\mathbf{M}}(Z)

and
{\mathbf{M}}(V)\leq\eta\,{\mathbf{M}}(Z)
;

(2)

\Theta_{x,r}(Z^{\prime})\geq\theta
for all
x\in\operatorname{spt}(Z^{\prime})
and
r\in(0,\alpha\eta]
;

(3)

if
B\subset X
is a closed set and
T:=\partial(V\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}B)-(\partial V)\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}B
is
in
{\mathbf{I}}_{n,c}(X)
, then
\mu(B)\leq\|Z\|(B)+{\mathbf{M}}(T)
;

(4)

if
{\mathbf{M}}(Z)<m:=\theta(\alpha\eta)^{n}
, then
Z^{\prime}=0
, and
if
{\mathbf{M}}(Z)\geq m
, then
\operatorname{spt}(Z^{\prime})
is within distance
at most
\eta(\alpha+\ln({\mathbf{M}}(Z)/m))
from
\operatorname{spt}(Z)
;

(5)

if there exist
C>0
,
a\geq 0
, and
p\in X
such that
\Theta_{p,r}(Z)\leq C

for all
r>a
,
then
\mu(B_{p}(r))\leq 2^{n+1}Cr^{n}
for all
r>\max\{a,2^{n+1}\eta\}
.

Proof.

Given
Z\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X)
and
\eta>0
, consider the functional

F\colon{\mathbf{I}}_{n+1,\text{\rm loc}}(X)\to[0,\infty],\quad F(V^{\prime})=\eta^{-1}{\mathbf{M}}(V^{\prime})+{\mathbf{M}}(Z-\partial V^{\prime}).

Notice that
F
is lower semicontinuous with respect to weak convergence,
like
{\mathbf{M}}
. Moreover,
{\mathbf{M}}(V^{\prime})\leq\eta F(V^{\prime})

and
{\mathbf{M}}(\partial V^{\prime})\leq F(V^{\prime})+{\mathbf{M}}(Z)
for all
V^{\prime}
, and
F(0)={\mathbf{M}}(Z)<\infty
.
We can thus pick a minimizing sequence for
F
and use
Theorem
2.1
to find a
V\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n+1,\text{\rm loc}}(X)
that minimizes

F
. Now if
Z^{\prime}:=Z-\partial V
and
\mu:=\eta^{-1}\|V\|+\|Z^{\prime}\|
, then

\mu(X)=\eta^{-1}{\mathbf{M}}(V)+{\mathbf{M}}(Z^{\prime})=F(V)\leq F(0)={\mathbf{M}}(Z),

so (1) holds. However, we still have to show that in fact

V\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n+1,\text{\rm c}}(X)
.

We proceed with (2). Let
x\in\operatorname{spt}(Z^{\prime})
.
Put
f(s):=\|Z^{\prime}\|(B_{x}(s))>0
for all
s>0
.
For almost every
s
, the slice
R_{s}:=\partial(Z^{\prime}\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}B_{x}(s))
is in

{\mathbf{Z}}_{n-1,\text{\rm c}}(X)
and satisfies
{\mathbf{M}}(R_{s})\leq f^{\prime}(s)
.
Suppose first that
n\geq 2
.
Then by the isoperimetric inequality there exists a filling

T_{s}\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X)
of
R_{s}
such that

{\mathbf{M}}(T_{s})\leq\gamma\,{\mathbf{M}}(R_{s})^{n/(n-1)}\leq\gamma f^{\prime}(s)^{n/(n-1)}.

Furthermore, the cycle
Z^{\prime}\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}B_{x}(s)-T_{s}
has a filling

W_{s}\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n+1,\text{\rm c}}(X)
with

{\mathbf{M}}(W_{s})\leq\gamma\bigl(f(s)+{\mathbf{M}}(T_{s})\bigr)^{(n+1)/n}.

Since
Z-\partial(V+W_{s})=Z^{\prime}-\partial W_{s}=Z^{\prime}\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}(X\setminus B_{x}(s))+T_{s}
,
we have

\displaystyle F(V+W_{s})

\displaystyle=\eta^{-1}{\mathbf{M}}(V+W_{s})+{\mathbf{M}}(Z^{\prime}\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}(X\setminus B_{x}(s))+T_{s})

\displaystyle\leq\eta^{-1}({\mathbf{M}}(V)+{\mathbf{M}}(W_{s}))+\|Z^{\prime}\|(X\setminus B_{x}(s))+{\mathbf{M}}(T_{s}).

It follows that
0\leq F(V+W_{s})-F(V)\leq\eta^{-1}{\mathbf{M}}(W_{s})-f(s)+{\mathbf{M}}(T_{s})

and

\eta\bigl(f(s)-{\mathbf{M}}(T_{s})\bigr)\leq{\mathbf{M}}(W_{s})\leq\gamma\bigl(f(s)+{\mathbf{M}}(T_{s})\bigr)^{(n+1)/n}.

Hence, if
{\mathbf{M}}(T_{s})\leq f(s)/2
, then

\eta f(s)/2\leq\gamma(3f(s)/2)^{(n+1)/n}
and thus

f(s)\geq\theta^{\prime}\eta^{n}

for
\theta^{\prime}:=2/(3^{n+1}\gamma^{n})
.
Now if
f(r)<\theta^{\prime}\eta^{n}
for some
r
,
then
f(s)<\theta^{\prime}\eta^{n}
for all
s\in(0,r)
, thus

f(s)/2<{\mathbf{M}}(T_{s})\leq\gamma f^{\prime}(s)^{n/(n-1)}
and

f^{\prime}(s)f(s)^{(1-n)/n}\geq(2\gamma)^{(1-n)/n}

for almost every such
s
, and integration from
0
to
r
yields

f(r)\geq\theta r^{n}

where
\theta:=n^{-n}(2\gamma)^{1-n}
. This shows that

f(r)\geq\min\{\theta^{\prime}\eta^{n},\theta r^{n}\}
for all
r>0
.
Hence (2) holds with
\alpha:=(\theta^{\prime}/\theta)^{1/n}
in case
n\geq 2
.
Suppose now that
n=1
. Then every non-zero slice
R_{s}\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{0,\text{\rm c}}(X)

has mass at least
2
. If
R_{s}=0
for some
s
, then
Z^{\prime}\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}B_{x}(s)

is a cycle, and repeating the above argument with
T_{s}=0
we get that

\eta f(s)\leq{\mathbf{M}}(W_{s})\leq\gamma f(s)^{2}
, thus
f(s)\geq\eta/\gamma
.
Hence, if
f(r)<\eta/\gamma
for some
r
, then
f^{\prime}(s)\geq{\mathbf{M}}(R_{s})\geq 2

for almost every
s\in(0,r)
and so
f(r)\geq 2r
.
We conclude that in case
n=1
, (2) holds with
\theta:=2
and

\alpha:=1/(2\gamma)
.

Since
{\mathbf{M}}(Z^{\prime})<\infty
, it now follows from (2) that
Z^{\prime}
has compact
support. Hence
\operatorname{spt}(\partial V)
is compact, and since
V
has finite mass
and is evidently minimizing, by Theorem
2.3

\operatorname{spt}(V)
is
compact as well.

We prove (3). Let
W:=V\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}B
.
If
T=\partial W-(Z-Z^{\prime})\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}B\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X)
,
then also
(Z-Z^{\prime})\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}B\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X)
and

W\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n+1,\text{\rm c}}(X)
. Since

Z-\partial(V-W)=Z^{\prime}+\partial W=Z^{\prime}\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}(X\setminus B)+Z\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}B+T
,
we have

\displaystyle F(V-W)

\displaystyle=\eta^{-1}{\mathbf{M}}(V-W)+{\mathbf{M}}\bigl(Z^{\prime}\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}(X\setminus B)+Z\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}B+T\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\mu(X\setminus B)+\|Z\|(B)+{\mathbf{M}}(T).

Since
\mu(X)=F(V)\leq F(V-W)
, it follows that

\mu(B)=\mu(X)-\mu(X\setminus B)\leq\|Z\|(B)+{\mathbf{M}}(T)
as claimed.

The first assertion of (4) is clear from (1) and (2).
Suppose now that
{\mathbf{M}}(Z)\geq m=\theta(\alpha\eta)^{n}
and

x\in\operatorname{spt}(Z^{\prime})
is a point at distance
D>\alpha\eta
from
\operatorname{spt}(Z)
.
Set
g(s):=\mu(B_{x}(s))
for all
s\in(0,D)
. For almost every such
s
,
the slice
T_{s}:=\partial(V\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}B_{x}(s))+Z^{\prime}\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}B_{x}(s)
is in
{\mathbf{I}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X)

and satisfies
{\mathbf{M}}(T_{s})\leq\frac{d}{ds}\|V\|(B_{x}(s))

as well as
{\mathbf{M}}(\partial T_{s})\leq\frac{d}{ds}\|Z^{\prime}\|(B_{x}(s))
.
Hence, by (3),

g(s)\leq{\mathbf{M}}(T_{s})\leq{\mathbf{M}}(T_{s})+\eta\,{\mathbf{M}}(\partial T_{s})\leq\eta\,g^{\prime}(s).

Integrating the inequality
1\leq\eta\,g^{\prime}(s)/g(s)
from
\alpha\eta

to
t<D
we get that

t\leq\eta\bigl(\alpha+\ln(g(t))-\ln(g(\alpha\eta))\bigr).

By (1) and (2),
g(t)\leq\mu(X)\leq{\mathbf{M}}(Z)
and
g(\alpha\eta)\geq\|Z^{\prime}\|(B_{x}(\alpha\eta))\geq m
.
As this holds for all
t<D
, (4) follows.

It remains to prove (5). We will write
B_{r}
for
B_{p}(r)
.
First we choose a sufficiently large
r_{0}>0
so that

\|V\|(B_{r_{0}})\leq 2^{n+1}\eta\,Cr_{0}^{\,n},

then we put
r_{i}:=2^{-i}r_{0}
for every integer
i\geq 1
.
There exists an
s\in(r_{1},r_{0})
such that the slice

T_{s}:=\partial(V\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}B_{s})-(\partial V)\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}B_{s}\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X)
satisfies

\mu(B_{s})\leq\|Z\|(B_{s})+{\mathbf{M}}(T_{s})

by (3), as well as

{\mathbf{M}}(T_{s})\leq\|V\|(B_{r_{0}})/(r_{0}-r_{1})\leq 2^{n+1}\eta\,Cr_{0}^{\,n}/r_{1}
.
Now if
r_{1}>\max\{a,2^{n+1}\eta\}
,
then
\|Z\|(B_{s})\leq Cs^{n}
and
{\mathbf{M}}(T_{s})\leq Cr_{0}^{\,n}
, hence

\mu(B_{r_{1}})\leq\mu(B_{s})\leq 2Cr_{0}^{\,n}=2^{n+1}Cr_{1}^{\,n}.

This also yields the above inequality for the next smaller scale,

\|V\|(B_{r_{1}})\leq 2^{n+1}\eta\,Cr_{1}^{\,n}.

Finally, given any
r>\max\{a,2^{n+1}\eta\}
, we can choose

r_{0}
initially such that
r=r_{k}=2^{-k}r_{0}
for some
k\geq 1
.
When
k\geq 2
, we repeat the above slicing argument
successively for
i=2,\dots,k
,
with
(r_{i},r_{i-1})
in place of
(r_{1},r_{0})
.
This eventually shows that

\mu(B_{r})\leq 2^{n+1}Cr^{n}

for any
r>\max\{a,2^{n+1}\eta\}
.
∎

As a first application of parts (1)–(3) of Proposition
3.1

we give a short proof of a variant of Proposition 5.8 in
[
37
]

regarding fillings of thin cycles. This result will play
a key role in the next section (see Theorem
4.4
).
The assumptions on
X
are as above. The conclusion is that
for a cycle
Z\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X)
, the
filling volume

\operatorname{FillVol}_{X}(Z):=\inf\{{\mathbf{M}}(V):V\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n+1,\text{\rm c}}(X),\,\partial V=Z\}

can be forced to be arbitrarily small by imposing a sufficiently small
bound on the supremal mass

m_{\varrho}(Z):=\sup_{x\in X}\|Z\|(B_{x}(\varrho))

in all closed balls of some fixed radius
\varrho>0
. The proof gives
an explicit constant involving a mass bound for
Z
.

Theorem 3.2
.

For all
n\geq 1
and
\gamma,M,\varrho,\nu>0
there exists a constant

\delta=\delta(n,\gamma,M,\varrho,\nu)>0
such that if
X
is a proper metric space
satisfying
({\rm EII}_{n})[\gamma]
and, in case
n\geq 2
, also
({\rm EII}_{n-1})[\gamma]
,
then every
Z\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X)
with
{\mathbf{M}}(Z)\leq M
and
m_{\varrho}(Z)\leq\delta

has
\operatorname{FillVol}_{X}(Z)<\nu
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha
and
\theta
be the constants
from Proposition
3.1
, depending on
n
and
\gamma
.
Given
M,\varrho,\nu>0
, fix
\eta>0
such that both
\eta M
and

\gamma(4\eta M/\varrho)^{(n+1)/n}
are less than
\nu/2
, then put

r:=\min\Bigl\{\alpha\eta,\frac{\varrho}{4}\Bigr\},\quad\delta:=\frac{1}{2}\theta r^{n}.

Suppose now that
Z\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X)
satisfies
{\mathbf{M}}(Z)\leq M
and

m_{\varrho}(Z)\leq\delta
. By Proposition
3.1
there exists

V\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n+1,\text{\rm c}}(X)
such that, for
Z^{\prime}:=Z-\partial V
,

(1)

{\mathbf{M}}(V)\leq\eta\,{\mathbf{M}}(Z)\leq\eta M<\nu/2
;

(2)

\|Z^{\prime}\|(B_{x}(r))\geq\theta r^{n}=2\delta
for all

x\in\operatorname{spt}(Z^{\prime})
;

(3)

\|Z^{\prime}\|(B)\leq\|Z\|(B)+{\mathbf{M}}(T)
whenever
B\subset X
is a closed set
and
T:=\partial(V\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}B)-(\partial V)\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}B
is in
{\mathbf{I}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X)
.

If
Z^{\prime}=0
, then
\partial V=Z
, and (1) yields the result.
Now let
Z^{\prime}\neq 0
. It remains to show that
\operatorname{FillVol}_{X}(Z^{\prime})<\nu/2
.
Pick a maximal set
N\subset\operatorname{spt}(Z^{\prime})
of distinct points at mutual
distance
>2r
, and put
B_{s}:=\bigcup_{x\in N}B_{x}(s)

for all
s>0
.
By (2), since the balls
B_{x}(r)
with
x\in N
are pairwise disjoint,
we have
2\delta\,|N|\leq\|Z^{\prime}\|(B_{r})\leq{\mathbf{M}}(Z^{\prime})
, and so

\|Z\|(B_{\varrho})\leq|N|\,m_{\varrho}(Z)\leq\delta\,|N|\leq\frac{1}{2}\,{\mathbf{M}}(Z^{\prime}).

Furthermore, since
N
is maximal,
\operatorname{spt}(Z^{\prime})\subset B_{2r}\subset B_{\varrho/2}
.
Hence, for almost every
s\in(\frac{\varrho}{2},\varrho)
, the slice

T_{s}:=\partial(V\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}B_{s})-(\partial V)\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}B_{s}\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X)
satisfies

{\mathbf{M}}(Z^{\prime})=\|Z^{\prime}\|(B_{s})\leq\|Z\|(B_{s})+{\mathbf{M}}(T_{s})\leq\frac{1}{2}\,{\mathbf{M}}(Z^{\prime})+{\mathbf{M}}(T_{s})

by (3). We conclude that
{\mathbf{M}}(T_{s})\geq{\mathbf{M}}(Z^{\prime})/2
and

{\mathbf{M}}(V)\geq\|V\|(B_{\varrho})\geq\int_{\varrho/2}^{\varrho}{\mathbf{M}}(T_{s})\,ds\geq\frac{\varrho}{4}\,{\mathbf{M}}(Z^{\prime}).

It now follows from (1) that
{\mathbf{M}}(Z^{\prime})\leq 4\eta M/\varrho
,
and by the isoperimetric inequality and the choice of
\eta

we get that
\operatorname{FillVol}_{X}(Z^{\prime})<\nu/2
.
∎

4.
Convergence of cycles

A central result in geometric measure theory says that a weakly convergent
sequence
S_{i}\to S
of integral
n
-currents with supports in a fixed compact
set and with
\sup_{i}({\mathbf{M}}(S_{i})+{\mathbf{M}}(\partial S_{i}))<\infty
converges in the

flat metric topology
. This means that there exist integral
n
-currents

T_{i}
and integral
(n+1)
-currents
V_{i}
such that
S_{i}-S=T_{i}+\partial V_{i}

and
{\mathbf{M}}(T_{i})+{\mathbf{M}}(V_{i})\to 0
. In
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
, this property can be deduced
from the deformation theorem (see Theorem 5.5 and Theorem 7.1 in
[
15
]
).
The result was generalized in
[
37
]
to Ambrosio–Kirchheim currents
in complete metric spaces satisfying condition
({\rm CI}_{n})
locally.
It essentially suffices to show that
\operatorname{FillVol}_{X}(Z_{i})\to 0

for any bounded sequence of cycles
Z_{i}
converging weakly to
0
.
In this section we prove a somewhat amplified version of this fact
for proper metric spaces satisfying
({\rm CI}_{n})
globally, so as to
facilitate the proof of the sub-Euclidean isoperimetric inequality
in the next section.
The argument relies on Theorem
3.2
and proceeds along
the same lines as
[
37
]
, but is simplified by the use of a uniform
notion of weak convergence.

Let
S\in{\mathscr{D}}_{n}(X)
,
n\geq 0
, and suppose that
\operatorname{spt}(S)
is compact.
Note that every such
S
extends canonically to tuples of Lipschitz functions
whose first entry is no longer required to have compact support.
We define

{\mathbf{W}}(S):=\sup\{S(f_{0},\ldots,f_{n}):\text{$f_{0},\ldots,f_{n}$ are $1$-Lipschitz, $|f_{0}|\leq 1$}\}.

Evidently
{\mathbf{W}}(S)\leq{\mathbf{M}}(S)
, and if
n\geq 1
, then
{\mathbf{W}}(\partial S)\leq{\mathbf{W}}(S)
.

The following auxiliary result is an adaptation of Proposition 6.6
in
[
29
]
to sequences of cycles in possibly distinct proper
metric spaces.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose that
n\geq 1
,
Z_{i}\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{n,c}(X_{i})
,
\sup_{i}{\mathbf{M}}(Z_{i})<\infty
,

{\mathbf{W}}(Z_{i})\to 0
, and
\pi_{i}\colon X_{i}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is
1
-Lipschitz.
Then for almost every
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
there is a sequence
(i_{k})
such that

Z_{i_{k},s}:=Z_{i_{k}}\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}\{\pi_{i_{k}}\leq s\}\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X_{i_{k}})
and

\sup_{k}{\mathbf{M}}(\partial Z_{i_{k},s})<\infty,\quad{\mathbf{W}}(\partial Z_{i_{k},s})\leq{\mathbf{W}}(Z_{i_{k},s})\to 0\quad(k\to\infty).

Proof.

Consider the Borel measures
\mu_{i}:=\pi_{i\#}\|Z_{i}\|
. Since

\sup_{i}\mu_{i}({\mathbb{R}})<\infty
, some subsequence
(\mu_{i_{k}})
converges weakly
to a finite Borel measure
\mu
on
{\mathbb{R}}
. Furthermore,
for the slices
\partial Z_{i_{k},s}
,

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\liminf_{k\to\infty}{\mathbf{M}}(\partial Z_{i_{k},s})\,ds\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}{\mathbf{M}}(\partial Z_{i_{k},s})\,ds\leq\sup_{i}{\mathbf{M}}(Z_{i})<\infty.

We now take
s
so that
\mu(\{s\})=0
and

\liminf_{k\to\infty}{\mathbf{M}}(\partial Z_{i_{k},s})<\infty
, then we adjust the sequence

(i_{k})
, if necessary, to arrange that
\sup_{k}{\mathbf{M}}(\partial Z_{i_{k},s})<\infty
.
Note that
Z_{i_{k},s}\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X_{i_{k}})
for all
k
.
Let
\epsilon>0
, and choose
\delta>0
such that
\mu([s,s+\delta])<\epsilon
.
Let
\gamma_{s,\delta}\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
denote the
piecewise affine
\delta^{-1}
-Lipschitz function that is
1
on
(-\infty,s]
and

0
on
[s+\delta,\infty)
, and put
u_{k}:=\gamma_{s,\delta}\circ\pi_{i_{k}}

and
v_{k}:=\chi_{(-\infty,s]}\circ\pi_{i_{k}}
.
If
k
is sufficiently large, then

\displaystyle{\mathbf{W}}(Z_{i_{k}}\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}(u_{k}-v_{k}))\leq\mu_{i_{k}}([s,s+\delta])<\epsilon,

\displaystyle{\mathbf{W}}(Z_{i_{k}}\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}u_{k})\leq(1+\delta^{-1}){\mathbf{W}}(Z_{i_{k}})<\epsilon

(note that if
f_{0}\colon X_{i_{k}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a
1
-Lipschitz function
with
|f_{0}|\leq 1
, then
u_{k}f_{0}
is
(1+\delta^{-1})
-Lipschitz),
thus
{\mathbf{W}}(Z_{i_{k},s})={\mathbf{W}}(Z_{i_{k}}\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}v_{k})<2\epsilon
.
This gives the result.
∎

Next, recall that a
0
-cycle
Z\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{0,\text{\rm c}}(X)
is of the form

Z(f)=\sum_{i}a_{i}f(x_{i})
for finitely many points
x_{i}\in X
and weights

a_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
with
\sum_{i}a_{i}=0
. Note that
Z(f+c)=Z(f)
for all

c\in{\mathbb{R}}
. We define

{\mathscr{W}}(Z):=\sup\{Z(f):\text{$f$ is $1$-Lipschitz}\};

in general
{\mathscr{W}}(Z)\geq{\mathbf{W}}(Z)
. We have the following optimal result
for
n=0
(compare Theorem 1.2 in
[
37
]
).

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
X
be a proper metric space satisfying
({\rm CI}_{0})[c]
, that is,
any two points
x,y\in X
can be connected by a curve of length

\leq c\,d(x,y)
. Then every cycle
Z\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{0,\text{\rm c}}(X)
has

\operatorname{FillVol}_{X}(Z)\leq c\,{\mathscr{W}}(Z)
, and if
{\mathbf{W}}(Z)<2
, then
{\mathscr{W}}(Z)={\mathbf{W}}(Z)
.

Proof.

We can write
Z\neq 0
in the form

Z(f)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(f(x_{i})-f(y_{i}))=\int_{X}f\,d\mu-\int_{X}f\,d\nu

for some (not necessarily distinct) points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}
and

y_{1},\ldots,y_{k}
in
X
and the corresponding measures

\mu:=\sum_{i}\delta_{x_{i}}
and
\nu:=\sum_{i}\delta_{y_{i}}
.
Hence, by the Kantorovich–Rubinstein theorem (see
[
12
]
),

{\mathscr{W}}(Z)
equals the Wasserstein distance
W_{1}(\mu,\nu)
.
It is well-known that for such measures the latter agrees with the
minimum of
\sum_{i}d(x_{i},y_{\pi(i)})
over all permutations
\pi
of

\{1,\ldots,k\}
.
Thus, some such sum is equal to
{\mathscr{W}}(Z)
. We will give an alternative direct
proof of this identity in Lemma
4.3
below.
It now follows from condition
({\rm CI}_{0})[c]
that
Z
has a filling with mass
less than or equal to
c\,{\mathscr{W}}(Z)
.

For the second assertion of the proposition,
consider the metric
\delta:=\min\{d,2\}
on
X

and let
{\mathscr{W}}_{\delta}
denote the corresponding functional.
Note that
{\mathscr{W}}_{\delta}(Z)={\mathbf{W}}(Z)
. If
Z
is as above, then for some

1
-Lipschitz function
f\colon(X,\delta)\to{\mathbb{R}}
and some permutation
\pi
,
we have

{\mathscr{W}}_{\delta}(Z)=Z(f)=\sum_{i}(f(x_{i})-f(y_{\pi(i)}))=\sum_{i}\delta(x_{i},y_{\pi(i)}),

in particular
f(x_{i})-f(y_{\pi(i)})=\delta(x_{i},y_{\pi(i)})\geq 0
for all
i
.
We can assume that the set
\bigcup_{i}[f(y_{\pi(i)}),f(x_{i})]
is connected;
otherwise
f
can easily be modified so that this holds.
Hence, if
{\mathscr{W}}_{\delta}(Z)={\mathbf{W}}(Z)<2
,
then we can further arrange that
|f|<1
. It follows that there is no

1
-Lipschitz function
g\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}
with
Z(g)>{\mathbf{W}}(Z)=Z(f)
, for
otherwise a suitable convex combination
h=(1-\epsilon)f+\epsilon g
would
satisfy
Z(h)>{\mathbf{W}}(Z)
and
|h|\leq 1
.
∎

We now provide the alternative argument mentioned above. It is convenient
to consider pairwise distinct points but to allow distances to be zero.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
(V,d)
be a finite pseudo-metric space with a partition
V=V_{+}\cup V_{-}
,
where
|V_{+}|=|V_{-}|
. If
f\colon V\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a

1
-Lipschitz function that maximizes the quantity

\sum_{x\in V_{+}}f(x)-\sum_{y\in V_{-}}f(y)
,
then there exists a bijection
\pi\colon V_{+}\to V_{-}
such that

f(x)-f(\pi(x))=d(x,\pi(x))
for all
x\in V_{+}
.

Proof.

Given
f
, define a relation
\preceq
on
V
such that
x\preceq y

if and only if
f(x)-f(y)=d(x,y)
. Note that if
x\preceq y\preceq z
,
then

f(x)-f(z)=d(x,y)+d(y,z)\geq d(x,z),

and since
f
is
1
-Lipschitz,
x\preceq z
.
Thus the relation is transitive. For a set
A\subset V_{+}
, let
\Gamma(A)

denote the set of all
y\in V_{-}
for which there is an
x\in A
with

x\preceq y
. Suppose first that
A
is maximal in
V_{+}
in the sense
that there is no pair
(x,y)\in A\times(V_{+}\setminus A)
with
x\preceq y
.
Note that
f(x)-f(y)<d(x,y)
whenever

x\in A
and
y\in C:=(V_{+}\setminus A)\cup(V_{-}\setminus\Gamma(A))
.
By transitivity, the same strict inequality holds whenever
x\in\Gamma(A)

and
y\in C
. Hence, for some
\epsilon>0
, the function
f_{\epsilon}
obtained
from
f
by increasing the values on
A\cup\Gamma(A)
by
\epsilon
is still

1
-Lipschitz. It follows from the maximality property of
f
that

\epsilon\,|A|=\sum_{x\in V_{+}}(f_{\epsilon}(x)-f(x))\leq\sum_{y\in V_{-}}(f_{\epsilon}(y)-f(y))=\epsilon\,|\Gamma(A)|,

thus
|A|\leq|\Gamma(A)|
. Let now
A\subset V_{+}
be arbitrary.
Again by transitivity, the set
A^{\prime}
of all points in
V_{+}
with a precursor
in
A
is maximal, and
\Gamma(A^{\prime})=\Gamma(A)
, so that

|A|\leq|A^{\prime}|\leq|\Gamma(A^{\prime})|=|\Gamma(A)|
. This shows that the bipartite
graph with edge set
\{(x,y)\in V_{+}\times V_{-}:x\preceq y\}
satisfies
the assumption of Hall’s marriage theorem.
Hence, there is a matching (bijection)
\pi
as claimed.
∎

In general, for
n\geq 0
, an analog of
Proposition
4.2
holds as follows.

Theorem 4.4
.

If
X_{i}
satisfies
({\rm CI}_{n})[c]
for
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and if the cycles

Z_{i}\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{n,c}(X_{i})
satisfy
\sup_{i}{\mathbf{M}}(Z_{i})<\infty

and
{\mathbf{W}}(Z_{i})\to 0
, then
\operatorname{FillVol}_{X_{i}}(Z_{i})\to 0
.

Proof.

The proof is by induction on
n
. For
n=0
, the result holds by
Proposition
4.2
.
Assume now that
n\geq 1
and the assertion holds in dimension
n-1
.
It suffices to show that for every sequence
(Z_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
as in the
statement and for every
\nu>0
there is an index
j
with

\operatorname{FillVol}_{X_{j}}(Z_{j})<\nu
.
By Theorem
2.2
there is a constant
\gamma=\gamma(n,c)
such that
every
X_{i}
satisfies
({\rm EII}_{n})[\gamma]
and, if
n\geq 2
, also

({\rm EII}_{n-1})[\gamma]
. Put
M:=\sup_{i}{\mathbf{M}}(Z_{i})
and choose
\varrho>0
such that

18c\varrho M<\nu.

Let
\delta:=\delta(n,\gamma,M,\varrho,\nu/2)

be the constant from Theorem
3.2
.
If there is an index
j
with
m_{\varrho}(Z_{j})\leq\delta
, then

\operatorname{FillVol}_{X_{j}}(Z_{j})<\nu/2
and we are done.

Suppose now that
m_{\varrho}(Z_{i})>\delta
for all
i
.
Choose points
x_{i}\in X_{i}
such that

\|Z_{i}\|(B_{x_{i}}(\varrho))\geq\delta.

By Lemma
4.1
there is an
s\in(\varrho,2\varrho)
and an infinite
set
I_{1}\subset{\mathbb{N}}
such that
Z_{i,s}:=Z_{i}\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}B_{x_{i}}(s)\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X_{i})

for all
i\in I_{1}
and

\sup_{i\in I_{1}}{\mathbf{M}}(\partial Z_{i,s})<\infty,\quad{\mathbf{W}}(\partial Z_{i,s})\leq{\mathbf{W}}(Z_{i,s})\to 0\quad(I_{1}\ni i\to\infty).

By the induction assumption there exist fillings

T_{i}\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X_{i})
of
\partial Z_{i,s}
such that
{\mathbf{M}}(T_{i})\to 0
.
By reducing the index set further, if necessary, we arrange that

{\mathbf{M}}(T_{i})\leq\delta/2
and
\operatorname{spt}(T_{i})\subset B_{x_{i}}(3\varrho)

for all
i\in I_{1}
(Theorem
2.3
).
Then
S_{i}^{1}:=Z_{i,s}-T_{i}
is a cycle with support in
B_{x_{i}}(3\varrho)
, and

\operatorname{FillVol}_{X_{i}}(S_{i}^{1})\leq 3c\varrho\,{\mathbf{M}}(S_{i}^{1})

by the cone inequality. Let
Z_{i}^{1}:=Z_{i}-Z_{i,s}+T_{i}
and consider the
splitting

Z_{i}=S_{i}^{1}+Z_{i}^{1}.

Notice that
{\mathbf{M}}(S_{i}^{1})\leq{\mathbf{M}}(Z_{i,s})+\delta/2
and

{\mathbf{M}}(Z_{i}^{1})\leq{\mathbf{M}}(Z_{i})-{\mathbf{M}}(Z_{i,s})+\delta/2
,
moreover
{\mathbf{M}}(Z_{i,s})\geq\|Z_{i}\|(B_{x_{i}}(\varrho))\geq\delta
.
Hence, for all
i\in I_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbf{M}}(S_{i}^{1})

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbf{M}}(Z_{i})-{\mathbf{M}}(Z_{i}^{1})+\delta,

\displaystyle{\mathbf{M}}(Z_{i}^{1})

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbf{M}}(Z_{i})-\frac{\delta}{2}\leq M.

Note further that

{\mathbf{W}}(Z_{i}^{1})\leq{\mathbf{W}}(Z_{i})+{\mathbf{W}}(Z_{i,s})+{\mathbf{M}}(T_{i})\to 0
as
i\to\infty
.

If
m_{\varrho}(Z_{i}^{1})>\delta
for all
i\in I_{1}
, then we repeat the above
argument (with the same constants
M,\varrho,\delta
) and produce similar
splittings
Z_{i}^{1}=S_{i}^{2}+Z_{i}^{2}
for all
i
in an infinite set

I_{2}\subset I_{1}
. If
m_{\varrho}(Z_{i}^{2})>\delta
for all
i\in I_{2}
, we iterate
this step, and continue in this manner. This eventually
yields an infinite set
I_{k}\subset{\mathbb{N}}
, for some
k\geq 1
, and a decomposition

Z_{i}=S_{i}^{1}+\ldots+S_{i}^{k}+Z_{i}^{k}

for every
i\in I_{k}
,
such that
m_{\varrho}(Z_{j}^{k})\leq\delta
for some
j\in I_{k}
.
In fact,
k\leq 2M/\delta
, because

{\mathbf{M}}(Z_{i}^{k})\leq{\mathbf{M}}(Z_{i})-k\delta/2
for all
i\in I_{k}
.
It follows that

\displaystyle{\mathbf{M}}(S_{i}^{1})+\ldots+{\mathbf{M}}(S_{i}^{k})\leq{\mathbf{M}}(Z_{i})-{\mathbf{M}}(Z_{i}^{k})+k\delta\leq 3M,

\displaystyle\operatorname{FillVol}_{X_{i}}(S_{i}^{1})+\ldots+\operatorname{FillVol}_{X_{i}}(S_{i}^{k})\leq 9c\varrho M<\frac{\nu}{2},

and
\operatorname{FillVol}_{X_{j}}(Z_{j}^{k})<\nu/2
by
Theorem
3.2
. Hence,
\operatorname{FillVol}_{X_{j}}(Z_{j})<\nu
.
∎

The desired result for sequences of cycles with supports in a fixed
compact set now follows easily.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
X
be a proper metric space satisfying condition
({\rm CI}_{n})
for some

n\geq 0
. If an
{\mathbf{M}}
-bounded sequence of cycles
Z_{i}\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X)

with supports in a fixed compact set
K\subset X
converges weakly to
0
,
then
{\mathbf{W}}(Z_{i})\to 0
and
\operatorname{FillVol}_{X}(Z_{i})\to 0
.

Proof.

Suppose that
{\mathbf{M}}(Z_{i})\leq M
for all
i
.
Let
{\mathscr{F}}
denote the collection of all
1
-Lipschitz functions

f\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}
with
|f|\leq\operatorname{diam}(K)/2
. Let
\epsilon>0
.
There is a finite subcollection
{\mathscr{G}}\subset{\mathscr{F}}
such that for all

f_{0},\ldots,f_{n}\in{\mathscr{F}}
there exist
g_{0},\ldots,g_{n}\in{\mathscr{G}}
with

\sup_{x\in K}|f_{k}(x)-g_{k}(x)|\leq\epsilon/M
for
k=0,\ldots,n
; then

|Z_{i}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{n})-Z_{i}(g_{0},\ldots,g_{n})|\leq(n+1)\epsilon

for all
i
by Lemma 5.2 in
[
29
]
.
As
Z_{i}\to 0
weakly, if
i
is sufficiently large, then

Z_{i}(g_{0},\ldots,g_{n})\leq\epsilon
for all tuples
(g_{0},\ldots,g_{n})\in{\mathscr{G}}^{n+1}
,
thus
Z_{i}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{n})\leq(n+2)\epsilon
whenever
f_{0},\ldots,f_{n}\in{\mathscr{F}}
.
Hence
{\mathbf{W}}(Z_{i})\to 0
, and
\operatorname{FillVol}_{X}(Z_{i})\to 0
by
Theorem
4.4
.
∎

5.
Isoperimetric inequalities

This section is devoted to isoperimetric inequalities and shows
in particular the implications

({\rm AR}_{n})\Rightarrow({\rm LII}_{n})\Leftrightarrow({\rm SII}_{n})

in Theorem
1.1
.
In fact we prove some stronger uniform statements. To this end we first
extend the notion of asymptotic rank to sequences of metric spaces

X_{i}=(X_{i},d_{i})
.
A compact metric space
\Omega
is called an
asymptotic subset

of the sequence
(X_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
if there exist positive numbers

r_{i}\to\infty
and subsets
Y_{i}\subset X_{i}
such that the rescaled sets

(Y_{i},r_{i}^{-1}d_{i})
converge to
\Omega
in the Gromov–Hausdorff topology.
The
asymptotic rank
of the sequence
(X_{i})
is the supremum of all

k\geq 0
such that there exists an asymptotic subset bi-Lipschitz
homeomorphic to a compact subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
with positive Lebesgue measure.
The asymptotic rank of a single metric space
X
,
as defined in the introduction, equals the asymptotic rank
of the constant sequence
X_{i}=X
(see Definition 1.1 and Proposition 3.1
in
[
40
]
).

From now on, throughout this section, we assume that
{\mathscr{X}}
is a class of
proper metric spaces such that for some
n\geq 1
and
c>0
,
all members of
{\mathscr{X}}
satisfy
({\rm CI}_{n})[c]
, and every sequence

(X_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
in
{\mathscr{X}}
has asymptotic rank
\leq n
.
The sub-Euclidean isoperimetric inequality for
n
-cycles in spaces
of asymptotic rank at most
n
was established in greater generality
in
[
40
]
(Theorem 1.2), and a slightly restricted version was used
as a key tool in
[
28
]
(Theorem 4.4). First we give a proof
of a uniform version of the latter statement.

Theorem 5.1
.

For all
C,\epsilon>0
there is a constant
\varrho_{0}=\varrho_{0}({\mathscr{X}},n,c,C,\epsilon)>0

such that if
X
belongs to
{\mathscr{X}}
, and
Z\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X)
satisfies

{\mathbf{M}}(Z)\leq Cr^{n}
and
\operatorname{spt}(Z)\subset B_{p}(r)
for some
p\in X

and
r\geq\varrho_{0}
, then
\operatorname{FillVol}_{X}(Z)<\epsilon r^{n+1}
.

Proof.

Suppose to the contrary that there exist
C,\epsilon>0
,
a sequence of positive radii
(r_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
tending to infinity,
and cycles
Z_{i}\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X_{i})
, where
X_{i}=(X_{i},d_{i})
belongs to
{\mathscr{X}}
,
each with mass
{\mathbf{M}}(Z_{i})\leq Cr_{i}^{\,n}
and support in some ball
B_{p_{i}}(r_{i})
,
such that

\operatorname{FillVol}_{X_{i}}(Z_{i})\geq\epsilon r_{i}^{\,n+1}.

By Theorem
2.2
there is a constant
\gamma=\gamma(n,c)
such that
every
X_{i}
satisfies
({\rm EII}_{n})[\gamma]
and, if
n\geq 2
,
also
({\rm EII}_{n-1})[\gamma]
.
Put
\eta_{i}:=\epsilon r_{i}/(2C)
and apply Proposition
3.1

to
Z_{i}
to get
V_{i}\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n+1,\text{\rm c}}(X_{i})
and
Z_{i}^{\prime}:=Z_{i}-\partial V_{i}
such that

(1)

{\mathbf{M}}(Z_{i}^{\prime})\leq Cr_{i}^{\,n}
and

{\mathbf{M}}(V_{i})\leq\eta_{i}Cr_{i}^{\,n}=\epsilon r_{i}^{\,n+1}/2
;

(2)

\Theta_{x,r}(Z_{i}^{\prime})\geq\theta
whenever
x\in\operatorname{spt}(Z_{i}^{\prime})
and

0<r\leq\alpha\eta_{i}=\alpha\epsilon r_{i}/(2C)
, where
\alpha,\theta

depend only on
n,\gamma
;

(3)

\operatorname{spt}(Z_{i}^{\prime})\subset B_{p_{i}}(\lambda r_{i})
for some constant
\lambda>1
depending
only on
n,\gamma,C,\epsilon
(this uses part (4) of
Proposition
3.1
).

By (1), (3) and the coning inequality
({\rm CI}_{n})[c]
there exists a filling

V_{i}^{\prime}\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n+1,\text{\rm c}}(X_{i})
of
Z_{i}^{\prime}
with mass

{\mathbf{M}}(V_{i}^{\prime})\leq c\lambda r_{i}\,{\mathbf{M}}(Z_{i}^{\prime})\leq c\lambda Cr_{i}^{\,n+1}.

By Theorem
2.3
we can assume that
V_{i}^{\prime}
is minimizing and has
support in
B_{p_{i}}(\lambda^{\prime}r_{i})
for some
\lambda^{\prime}>\lambda
independent of
i
.
Let
Y_{i}
denote the set
\operatorname{spt}(V_{i}^{\prime})
equipped with the metric induced
by
r_{i}^{-1}d_{i}
. Note that
{\mathbf{M}}(Z_{i}^{\prime})\leq C
and
{\mathbf{M}}(V_{i}^{\prime})\leq c\lambda C

with respect to this metric, and
Y_{i}
has diameter at most
2\lambda^{\prime}
.
It follows from (2) and the lower density bound for
V_{i}^{\prime}

that the family of all
Y_{i}
is uniformly precompact.
By Gromov’s compactness theorem
[
18
]
, after passage to subsequences
and relabelling, there exist a compact metric space
Y
and
isometric embeddings
\phi_{i}\colon Y_{i}\to Y

such that the images
\phi_{i}(Y_{i})
converge to some compact set

\Omega\subset Y
in the Hausdorff distance. By Theorem
2.1

we can further assume that the push-forwards

\phi_{i\#}V_{i}^{\prime}\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n+1,\text{\rm c}}(Y)
converge weakly to a current

V\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n+1,\text{\rm c}}(Y)
. Evidently
\operatorname{spt}(V)\subset\Omega
.
Since the (sub)sequence
(X_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
has asymptotic rank
\leq n
,
and
\Omega
is an asymptotic subset, it follows that there is no bi-Lipschitz
embedding of a compact subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
with positive Lebesgue measure
into
\Omega
, and therefore
V
must be zero
(compare Theorem 8.3 in
[
29
]
and the comments thereafter).
Hence, the cycles
\phi_{i\#}Z_{i}^{\prime}\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(Y)

converge weakly to
\partial V=0
. We can assume that
Y
satisfies
condition
({\rm CI}_{n})
; for example, the injective hull of
Y
admits a conical
geodesic bicombing
[
30
]
and is still compact.
It then follows from Theorem
4.5
that

{\mathbf{W}}(\phi_{i\#}Z_{i}^{\prime})\to 0
. As this is an intrinsic property of the currents,
we conclude that
{\mathbf{W}}(Z_{i}^{\prime})\to 0
with respect to the metrics

r_{i}^{-1}d_{i}
. However, it follows from the inequality

\operatorname{FillVol}_{X_{i}}(Z_{i})\geq\epsilon r_{i}^{\,n+1}
and (1) that

\operatorname{FillVol}_{X_{i}}(Z_{i}^{\prime})\geq\epsilon/2
with respect to
r_{i}^{-1}d_{i}
.
This contradicts Theorem
4.4

(note that
(X_{i},r_{i}^{-1}d_{i})
still satisfies
({\rm CI}_{n})[c]
).
∎

As a first application of Theorem
5.1
we derive a
density bound for minimizing fillings. This is similar to assertion (5)
of Proposition
3.1
and to Proposition 4.5 in
[
28
]
.

Proposition 5.2
.

For all
C,\delta>0
there is a
\varrho=\varrho({\mathscr{X}},n,c,C,\delta)>0

such that if
X
belongs to
{\mathscr{X}}
, and
Z\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X)
is a cycle with

\Theta_{p,r}(Z)\leq C
for some
p\in X
and for all
r>a\geq 0
,
then every minimizing filling
V\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n+1,\text{\rm c}}(X)
of
Z
satisfies

\Theta_{p,r}(V)=\frac{\|V\|(B_{p}(r))}{r^{n+1}}<\delta

for all
r>\max\{\varrho,a\}
.

Proof.

Let
V\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n+1,\text{\rm c}}(X)
be a minimizing filling of
Z
, and set

B_{r}:=B_{p}(r)
for all
r>0
.
Choose a sufficiently large radius
r_{0}>0
such that

\delta r_{0}^{\,n+1}>{\mathbf{M}}(V)\geq\|V\|(B_{r_{0}}),

and put
r_{i}:=2^{-i}r_{0}
for every integer
i\geq 1
.
There exists an
s\in(r_{1},r_{0})
such that the slice

T_{s}:=\partial(V\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}B_{s})-Z\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}B_{s}
is in
{\mathbf{I}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X)
and has mass

{\mathbf{M}}(T_{s})\leq\|V\|(B_{r_{0}})/(r_{0}-r_{1})<2\delta r_{0}^{\,n}
.
Furthermore, if
r_{1}>a
, then
{\mathbf{M}}(Z\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}B_{s})\leq Cs^{n}
by assumption,
thus the cycle
Z_{s}:=Z\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}B_{s}+T_{s}
satisfies

{\mathbf{M}}(Z_{s})\leq Cs^{n}+2\delta r_{0}^{\,n}\leq(C+2\delta)r_{0}^{\,n},

and
\operatorname{spt}(Z_{s})\subset B_{r_{0}}
. Note that
V\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}B_{s}
is a minimizing filling
of
Z_{s}
. By Theorem
5.1
there is a constant

\varrho:=2^{-1}\varrho_{0}({\mathscr{X}},n,c,C+2\delta,2^{-(n+1)}\delta)>0
such that
if
r_{1}>\max\{\varrho,a\}
and, hence,
r_{0}\geq 2\varrho
, then

\|V\|(B_{r_{1}})\leq{\mathbf{M}}(V\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}B_{s})<2^{-(n+1)}\delta r_{0}^{\,n+1}=\delta r_{1}^{\,n+1}.

Finally, given any
r>\max\{\varrho,a\}
, we can choose

r_{0}
initially such that
r=r_{k}=2^{-k}r_{0}
for some
k\geq 1
.
When
k\geq 2
, we repeat the above slicing argument
successively for
i=2,\dots,k
, with
(r_{i},r_{i-1})
in place of
(r_{1},r_{0})
.
This eventually shows that

\|V\|(B_{r})<\delta r^{n+1}

for all
r>\max\{\varrho,a\}
.
∎

Next we prove a linear isoperimetric inequality for cycles
with controlled density. This yields the implication

({\rm AR}_{n})\Rightarrow({\rm LII}_{n})
in Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 5.3

(linear isoperimetric inequality)
.

There is a constant
\nu=\nu(n,c)>0
, and for all
C>0
there is
a
\lambda=\lambda({\mathscr{X}},n,c,C)>0
,
such that if
X
belongs to
{\mathscr{X}}
and
Z\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X)

is a cycle with
(C,a)
-controlled density,
a\geq 0
,
then
\operatorname{FillVol}_{X}(Z)\leq\max\{\lambda,\nu a\}\,{\mathbf{M}}(Z)
.

Proof.

Note again that for some
\gamma=\gamma(n,c)
, every member of
{\mathscr{X}}
satisfies

({\rm EII}_{n})[\gamma]
and, if
n\geq 2
, also
({\rm EII}_{n-1})[\gamma]
.
Suppose that
Z\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X)
has
(C,a)
-controlled density.
For any
\eta>0
, to be specified below, Proposition
3.1

provides a
V\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n+1,\text{\rm c}}(X)
such that,
for
Z^{\prime}:=Z-\partial V\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X)

and some constants
\alpha,\theta>0
depending only on
n
and
\gamma
,

(1)

\eta^{-1}{\mathbf{M}}(V)+{\mathbf{M}}(Z^{\prime})\leq{\mathbf{M}}(Z)
;

(2)

\Theta_{x,r}(Z^{\prime})\geq\theta
for all
x\in\operatorname{spt}(Z^{\prime})
and
r\in(0,\alpha\eta]
;

(3)

Z^{\prime}
has
(2^{n+1}C,\max\{a,2^{n+1}\eta\})
-controlled density.

By Theorem
2.3
there exists a minimizing filling

V^{\prime}\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n+1,\text{\rm c}}(X)
of
Z^{\prime}
, and there is a
\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}(n,\gamma)>0

such that
\Theta_{x,r}(V^{\prime})\geq\delta_{0}
whenever
x\in\operatorname{spt}(V^{\prime})
,
r>0
,
and
B_{x}(r)\cap\operatorname{spt}(Z^{\prime})=\emptyset
. On the other hand, by (3) and
Proposition
5.2
, there is a constant

\varrho^{\prime}:=\varrho({\mathscr{X}},n,c,2^{n+1}C,\delta_{0})>0
such that
\Theta_{p,r}(V^{\prime})<\delta_{0}

for all
p\in X
and
r>\max\{\varrho^{\prime},a,2^{n+1}\eta\}
.
We now fix
\eta
so that

2^{n+1}\eta=\max\{\varrho^{\prime},a\}.

Then
\operatorname{spt}(V^{\prime})
is within distance at most
2^{n+1}\eta
from
\operatorname{spt}(Z^{\prime})
.
Pick a maximal set
N\subset\operatorname{spt}(Z^{\prime})
of distinct points at mutual distance

>2\alpha\eta
. The collection of all balls
B_{x}(2\alpha\eta)
with

x\in N
covers
\operatorname{spt}(Z^{\prime})
, and the corresponding balls with radius

r:=2(\alpha+2^{n})\eta

cover
\operatorname{spt}(V^{\prime})
.
Furthermore, the balls
B_{x}(\alpha\eta)
with
x\in N
are pairwise
disjoint, and
\|Z^{\prime}\|(B_{x}(\alpha\eta))\geq\theta(\alpha\eta)^{n}
by (2).
Hence,
|N|\leq{\mathbf{M}}(Z^{\prime})/(\theta\alpha^{n}\eta^{n})
, and since
r>2^{n+1}\eta
,
we have
\|V^{\prime}\|(B_{x}(r))<\delta_{0}r^{n+1}
for all
x\in N
.
(Possibly
V^{\prime}=0
and
|N|=0
.) Thus

{\mathbf{M}}(V^{\prime})\leq|N|\,\delta_{0}r^{n+1}\leq\frac{\delta_{0}r^{n+1}}{\theta\alpha^{n}\eta^{n}}\,{\mathbf{M}}(Z^{\prime})\leq\nu^{\prime}\eta\,{\mathbf{M}}(Z^{\prime})

for some
\nu^{\prime}=\nu^{\prime}(n,c)\geq 1
.
Now
V+V^{\prime}
is a filling of
Z
with mass

{\mathbf{M}}(V+V^{\prime})\leq\nu^{\prime}({\mathbf{M}}(V)+\eta\,{\mathbf{M}}(Z^{\prime}))\leq\nu^{\prime}\eta\,{\mathbf{M}}(Z)

by (1). In view of the choice of
\eta
, this gives the result.
∎

We now turn to the sub-Euclidean isoperimetric inequality as stated in
Theorem
1.1
.
The proof below shows that
({\rm LII}_{n})\Rightarrow({\rm SII}_{n})
.

Theorem 5.4

(sub-Euclidean isoperimetric inequality)
.

For all
\epsilon>0
there is a constant
M_{0}=M_{0}({\mathscr{X}},n,c,\epsilon)>0

such that if
X
belongs to
{\mathscr{X}}
and
Z\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X)
,
then
\operatorname{FillVol}_{X}(Z)<\epsilon\max\{M_{0},{\mathbf{M}}(Z)\}^{(n+1)/n}
.

Proof.

Given
Z\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X)
, note that if
t>0
and
r>t\,{\mathbf{M}}(Z)^{1/n}
,
then
{\mathbf{M}}(Z)<t^{-n}r^{n}
, thus
Z
has
(t^{-n},t\,{\mathbf{M}}(Z)^{1/n})
-controlled
density. For
\epsilon>0
, let
\nu=\nu(n,c)
and
\lambda=\lambda({\mathscr{X}},n,c,C)

be the constants from Theorem
5.3
, where now

C:=t^{-n}
for any fixed
t<\epsilon/\nu
.
Let
M_{0}>0
be such that
\lambda<\epsilon M_{0}^{1/n}
. Then

\max\{\lambda,\nu t\,{\mathbf{M}}(Z)^{1/n}\}\,{\mathbf{M}}(Z)<\epsilon\max\{M_{0},{\mathbf{M}}(Z)\}^{(n+1)/n},

and the result follows from Theorem
5.3
.
∎

Finally, we show that Theorem
5.1
follows easily from
Theorem
5.4
.
Given
C,\epsilon>0
, put
\epsilon^{\prime}:=\epsilon/C^{(n+1)/n}
.
If
Z\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X)
is a cycle with
{\mathbf{M}}(Z)\leq Cr^{n}
, and
r
is
sufficiently large, so that
Cr^{n}\geq M_{0}=M_{0}(\epsilon^{\prime})
, then

\operatorname{FillVol}_{X}(Z)<\epsilon^{\prime}(Cr^{n})^{(n+1)/n}=\epsilon r^{n+1}

by Theorem
5.4
. Since the asymptotic rank assumption in
Theorem
5.3
is only used through Theorem
5.1
,
this also shows that
({\rm SII}_{n})\Rightarrow({\rm LII}_{n})
.

6.
Quasiflats and quasi-minimizers

A map
f\colon W\to X
from another metric space
W
into
X
is an

(L,a)
-quasi-isometric embedding
, for constants
L\geq 1
and

a\geq 0
, if

L^{-1}d(x,y)-a\leq d(f(x),f(y))\leq L\,d(x,y)+a

for all
x,y\in W
. Propositions 3.6 and 3.7 in
[
28
]
show that
quasi-isometric embeddings of domains
W\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
into
X
give rise
to quasi-minimizing currents with controlled density,
as defined in the introduction.
An inspection of the proofs reveals that the statements hold in a
stronger form, in particular with a quasi-minimality constant
Q
independent
of the parameter
a
. We provide the details for convenience, and also
because parts of the proof will be used later.
The first result refers to the simpler case when the map is actually
Lipschitz.

Proposition 6.1
.

For all
n\geq 1
and
L\geq 1
there exist
C>0
and
Q\geq 1
such that
the following holds. Let
W\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a compact set with finite perimeter,
so that the associated current
E:=\llbracket W\rrbracket
(with
\operatorname{spt}(E)\subset W
and

\operatorname{spt}(\partial E)\subset\partial W
) is in
{\mathbf{I}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.
Suppose that
a\geq 0
and
f\colon W\to X
is an
L
-Lipschitz,

(L,a)
-quasi-isometric embedding into a proper metric space
X
.
Then
S:=f_{\#}E\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X)
has
(C,a)
-controlled density
and is
(Q,Qa)
-quasi-minimizing mod
f(\partial W)
,
furthermore
d(f(x),\operatorname{spt}(S))\leq Qa
for all
x\in W
with

d(x,\partial W)>Qa
.

Proof.

Let
B:=B_{p}(r)
for some
p\in X
and
r>a
. Then

\|f_{\#}E\|(B)\leq L^{n}\,\|E\|(f^{-1}(B)),

and
f^{-1}(B)
has diameter
\leq L(2r+a)\leq 3Lr
, thus
\|S\|(B)\leq Cr^{n}

for some constant
C=C(n,L)
. This shows that
S
has
(C,a)
-controlled
density.

Next, let
V\subset W
be a maximal subset of distinct points at mutual
distance
>2La
(
V=W
in case
a=0
).
Note that
d(f(x),f(y))\geq(2L)^{-1}d(x,y)

for any
x,y\in V
, thus
f|_{V}
has a
2L
-Lipschitz inverse, which we can
extend to an
\bar{L}
-Lipschitz map
\bar{f}\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
for some

\bar{L}=\bar{L}(n,L)
. Put
h:=\bar{f}\circ f\colon W\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
For every
x\in W
there is a
y\in V
with
d(x,y)\leq 2La
;
then
h(y)=y
and

d(h(x),x)\leq d(h(x),h(y))+d(y,x)\leq(\bar{L}L+1)\,d(x,y)\leq Na,

where
N:=2(\bar{L}L+1)L
.

Let
x\in W
. Suppose that
r>2LNa
and
B_{r}:=B_{f(x)}(r)
is disjoint
from
f(\partial W)
. For almost every such
r
, both
S_{r}:=S\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}B_{r}
and

E_{r}:=E\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}f^{-1}(B_{r})
are integral currents, and
f_{\#}E_{r}=S_{r}
.
Since
f^{-1}(B_{r})\cap\operatorname{spt}(\partial E)=\emptyset
, the support of
\partial E_{r}
lies in the
boundary of
f^{-1}(B_{r})
and is thus at distance at least
L^{-1}r
from
x
.
The geodesic homotopy from the inclusion map
W\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
to
h
provides a
current
R\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
with
\partial R=h_{\#}(\partial E_{r})-\partial E_{r}
such that

\operatorname{spt}(R)
is within distance
Na
from
\operatorname{spt}(\partial E_{r})
.
In fact,
R=\bar{f}_{\#}S_{r}-E_{r}
, because

h_{\#}(\partial E_{r})=\partial(h_{\#}E_{r})=\partial(\bar{f}_{\#}S_{r})
and
{\mathbf{Z}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=\{0\}
.
By the choice of
r
we have
L^{-1}r-Na>(2L)^{-1}r
,
thus
\operatorname{spt}(R)
lies outside
B_{x}((2L)^{-1}r)
. It follows that

{\mathbf{M}}(\bar{f}_{\#}S_{r})={\mathbf{M}}(E_{r}+R)\geq\|E\|(B_{x}((2L)^{-1}r))\geq\epsilon r^{n}

for some
\epsilon=\epsilon(n,L)>0
.
Now if
T\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X)
is such that
\partial T=\partial S_{r}
, then

\bar{f}_{\#}T=\bar{f}_{\#}S_{r}
, and

{\mathbf{M}}(S_{r})\leq Cr^{n}\leq C\epsilon^{-1}{\mathbf{M}}(\bar{f}_{\#}T)\leq Q^{\prime}\,{\mathbf{M}}(T)

for
Q^{\prime}:=C\epsilon^{-1}\bar{L}^{n}
. This holds for all

x\in W
and almost all
r>2LNa
as long as
B_{f(x)}(r)
is
disjoint from
f(\partial W)
. In particular, since
\operatorname{spt}(S)\subset f(W)
,

S
is
(Q^{\prime},2LNa)
-quasi-minimizing mod
f(\partial W)
.

Finally, put
Q:=\max\{Q^{\prime},L(2LN+1)\}
. Let
x\in W

with
d(x,\partial W)>Qa
. Then
w:=d(f(x),f(\partial W))>L^{-1}Qa-a\geq 2LNa
.
For almost every
r\in(2LNa,w)
, the above argument shows that

{\mathbf{M}}(\bar{f}_{\#}S_{r})>0
, thus
S_{r}=S\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}B_{f(x)}(r)\neq 0
,
and this implies that
d(f(x),\operatorname{spt}(S))\leq 2LNa\leq Qa
.
∎

For the second result, we suppose that the compact set
W\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a

triangulated polyhedral set
, that is,
W
has the structure of a
finite simplicial complex all of whose maximal cells are Euclidean

n
-simplices.
We write
W^{0}
and
(\partial W)^{0}
for the set of vertices and boundary vertices
of the triangulation, respectively. Furthermore,

[\,\cdot\,]_{a}
stands for the closed
a
-neighborhood of a subset of
X
.

Proposition 6.2
.

For all
n\geq 1
,
c>0
, and
K,L\geq 1
there exist

C>0
and
Q\geq 1
such that the following holds.
Let
X
be a proper metric space satisfying condition
({\rm CI}_{n-1})[c]
.
Suppose that
a>0
, and
W\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a triangulated polyhedral set with
simplices of diameter
\leq a
such that every ball in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
of radius

r>a
intersects at most
Ka^{-n}r^{n}
maximal simplices.
Let
{\mathscr{P}}_{*}(W)
denote the corresponding chain complex of
simplicial integral currents.
If
f\colon W\to X
is an
(L,a)
-quasi-isometric embedding,
then there exists a chain map

\iota\colon{\mathscr{P}}_{*}(W)\to{\mathbf{I}}_{*,\text{\rm c}}(X)
such that

(1)

\iota
maps every vertex
\llbracket x_{0}\rrbracket\in{\mathscr{P}}_{0}(W)
to
\llbracket f(x_{0})\rrbracket
and, for

1\leq k\leq n
, every basic oriented simplex
\llbracket x_{0},\dots,x_{k}\rrbracket\in{\mathscr{P}}_{k}(W)

to a minimizing current with support in
[f(\{x_{0},\dots,x_{k}\})]_{Qa}
;

(2)

S:=\iota\llbracket W\rrbracket\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X)
has
(C,a)
-controlled density and
is
(Q,Qa)
-quasi-minimizing mod
[f((\partial W)^{0})]_{Qa}
;

(3)

d(f(x),\operatorname{spt}(S))\leq Qa
for all
x\in W
with
d(x,(\partial W)^{0})>Qa
.

Note that by (1),
\operatorname{spt}(S)\subset[f(W^{0})]_{Qa}
and

\operatorname{spt}(\partial S)\subset[f((\partial W)^{0})]_{Qa}
.

Proof.

Put
{\mathscr{S}}_{*}:=\bigcup_{k=0}^{n}{\mathscr{S}}_{k}
, where
{\mathscr{S}}_{k}
denotes the set of all
basic oriented simplices
s=\llbracket x_{0},\dots,x_{k}\rrbracket\in{\mathscr{P}}_{k}(W)

(compare p. 365 in
[
14
]
for the notation).
We define a map
\iota\colon{\mathscr{S}}_{*}\to{\mathbf{I}}_{*,\text{\rm c}}(X)
by induction on
k
.
For
\llbracket x_{0}\rrbracket\in{\mathscr{S}}_{0}
, we put
\iota\llbracket x_{0}\rrbracket:=f_{\#}\llbracket x_{0}\rrbracket=\llbracket f(x_{0})\rrbracket
.
Suppose now that
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
\iota
is defined on
{\mathscr{S}}_{k-1}
.
For every
k
-cell of
W
, we choose an orientation

s=\llbracket x_{0},\dots,x_{k}\rrbracket\in{\mathscr{S}}_{k}
, then we let

\iota(s)\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(X)
be a minimizing filling of the cycle

\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}\,\iota\llbracket x_{0},\dots,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},\dots,x_{k}\rrbracket\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{k-1,\text{\rm c}}(X),

and we put
\iota(-s):=-\iota(s)
.
The resulting map on
{\mathscr{S}}_{*}
readily extends to a chain map

\iota\colon{\mathscr{P}}_{*}(W)\to{\mathbf{I}}_{*,\text{\rm c}}(X)
.
Note that
f
maps the vertex set of any cell of
W
to a set of diameter
at most
(L+1)a
. It follows inductively from condition
({\rm CI}_{n-1})[c]
and
Theorem
2.3
(if
n\geq 2
, then
X
satisfies
({\rm EII}_{n-1})

by Theorem
2.2
) that for all
s=\llbracket x_{0},\dots,x_{k}\rrbracket\in{\mathscr{S}}_{k}
,

{\mathbf{M}}(\iota(s))\leq Ma^{k}

and
\operatorname{spt}(\iota(s))\subset[f(\{x_{0},\dots,x_{k}\})]_{Ma}
for some
constant
M\geq L+1
depending only on
n,c,L
.

Let now
{\mathscr{S}}_{n}^{+}\subset{\mathscr{S}}_{n}
be the set of all positively oriented

n
-simplices, whose sum is
\llbracket W\rrbracket
. Put
S:=\iota\llbracket W\rrbracket
.
To show that
S
has controlled density,
let
p\in X
and
r>a
, and consider the set of all
s\in{\mathscr{S}}_{n}^{+}
for
which
\operatorname{spt}(\iota(s))\cap B_{p}(r)\neq\emptyset
. Every such
s
has a
vertex
x^{s}
with
f(x^{s})\in B_{p}(r+Ma)
, thus the set of all

x^{s}
has diameter at most
L(2(r+Ma)+a)\leq L(2M+3)r
.
It follows that there are at most
Ka^{-n}(L(2M+3)r)^{n}
such simplices
and that

\Theta_{p,r}(S)\leq C:=KL^{n}(2M+3)^{n}M

for
p\in X
and
r>a
.

Similarly as in the proof of Proposition
6.1
,
there exists an
\bar{L}
-Lipschitz map
\bar{f}\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that

h:=\bar{f}\circ f\colon W\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
satisfies

d(h(x),x)\leq Na

for all
x\in W
,
where
\bar{L}
and
N
depend only on
n,L
. Then

\bar{\iota}:=\bar{f}_{\#}\circ\iota\colon{\mathscr{P}}_{*}(W)\to{\mathbf{I}}_{*,\text{\rm c}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})

is a chain map that sends every
\llbracket x_{0}\rrbracket\in{\mathscr{S}}_{0}
to
\llbracket h(x_{0})\rrbracket
and
every
\llbracket x_{0},\dots,x_{k}\rrbracket\in{\mathscr{S}}_{k}
to a current with support in

[\{x_{0},\dots,x_{k}\}]_{(\bar{L}M+N)a}
.
Let
{\mathscr{P}}_{*}(\partial W)
be the complex of simplicial integral currents in
\partial W
.
A similar inductive construction as above, using minimizing fillings of
cycles in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, produces a chain homotopy between the inclusion map

{\mathscr{P}}_{*}(\partial W)\to{\mathbf{I}}_{*,\text{\rm c}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and the restriction of
\bar{\iota}
to

{\mathscr{P}}_{*}(\partial W)
. This yields an
R\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
with boundary

\partial R=\bar{\iota}(\partial\llbracket W\rrbracket)-\partial\llbracket W\rrbracket

and support
\operatorname{spt}(R)\subset[(\partial W)^{0}]_{\bar{M}a}
for some constant

\bar{M}=\bar{M}(n,c,L)\geq 1
. In fact,

R=\bar{f}_{\#}S-\llbracket W\rrbracket,

because
\bar{\iota}(\partial\llbracket W\rrbracket)=\partial(\bar{\iota}\llbracket W\rrbracket)=\partial(\bar{f}_{\#}S)
.

Note that
\operatorname{spt}(S)\subset[f(W^{0})]_{Ma}
. Let
\bar{x}\in[f(W^{0})]_{Ma}
and

r>0
be such that
B_{\bar{x}}(r)\cap f((\partial W)^{0})=\emptyset
and

S_{r}:=S\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}B_{\bar{x}}(r)\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X)
.
We want to show that if
r>Pa
, for some sufficiently large constant

P=P(n,c,L)\geq 1
, then
{\mathbf{M}}(\bar{f}_{\#}S_{r})\geq\epsilon r^{n}

for some
\epsilon=\epsilon(n,L)>0
. Choose
x\in W^{0}
with

d(f(x),\bar{x})\leq Ma
, and put
B_{x}:=B_{x}((2L)^{-1}r)
.
For all
y\in(\partial W)^{0}
,

r<d(\bar{x},f(y))\leq d(f(x),f(y))+Ma\leq L\,d(x,y)+(M+1)a

and thus
d(x,y)>(2L)^{-1}r+\bar{M}a
for sufficiently large
P
;
then

(\operatorname{spt}(R)\cup\partial W)\cap B_{x}=\emptyset.

Moreover, for every
\bar{y}\in\operatorname{spt}(S-S_{r})\subset\operatorname{spt}(S)
there is a vertex

y\in W^{0}
such that
d(f(y),\bar{y})\leq Ma
,

r\leq d(\bar{x},\bar{y})\leq d(f(x),f(y))+2Ma\leq L\,d(x,y)+(2M+1)a,

and
d(x,y)\leq d(x,\bar{f}(\bar{y}))+d(\bar{f}(\bar{y}),h(y))+Na\leq d(x,\bar{f}(\bar{y}))+(\bar{L}M+N)a
;
thus
d(x,\bar{f}(\bar{y}))>(2L)^{-1}r
for sufficiently large
P
,
implying that

\operatorname{spt}(\bar{f}_{\#}(S-S_{r}))\cap B_{x}=\emptyset.

Since
\bar{f}_{\#}S_{r}=\llbracket W\rrbracket+R-\bar{f}_{\#}(S-S_{r})
, it then follows that

{\mathbf{M}}(\bar{f}_{\#}S_{r})\geq\|\llbracket W\rrbracket\|(B_{x})\geq\epsilon r^{n}

for some
\epsilon=\epsilon(n,L)>0
, as desired.
Now if
T\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X)
is such that
\partial T=\partial S_{r}
, then

\bar{f}_{\#}T=\bar{f}_{\#}S_{r}
, and

{\mathbf{M}}(S_{r})\leq Cr^{n}\leq C\epsilon^{-1}{\mathbf{M}}(\bar{f}_{\#}T)\leq Q^{\prime}\,{\mathbf{M}}(T)

for
Q^{\prime}:=C\epsilon^{-1}\bar{L}^{n}
. Since
\operatorname{spt}(S)
and
\operatorname{spt}(\partial S)
are within
distance
Ma
from
f(W^{0})
and
f((\partial W)^{0})
, respectively, this shows
in particular that
S
is
(Q^{\prime},Pa)
-quasi-minimizing mod
[f((\partial W)^{0})]_{Ma}
.

Finally, put
Q:=\max\{M,Q^{\prime},L(P+1)\}
. Let
x^{\prime}\in W
with

d(x^{\prime},(\partial W)^{0})>Qa
. Then
w:=d(f(x^{\prime}),f((\partial W)^{0}))>L^{-1}Qa-a\geq Pa
.
Note that
f(x^{\prime})\in[f(W^{0})]_{Ma}
, as
M\geq L+1
.
For
\bar{x}=f(x^{\prime})
and almost every
r\in(Pa,w)
, the above argument
shows that
{\mathbf{M}}(\bar{f}_{\#}S_{r})>0
, thus
S_{r}=S\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}B_{\bar{x}}(r)\neq 0
,
and this implies that
d(f(x^{\prime}),\operatorname{spt}(S))\leq Pa\leq Qa
.
∎

7.
Morse lemma, slim simplices, and filling radius

We now turn to the remaining assertions in Theorem
1.1
.
For the first three results, we assume as in Sect.
5

that
{\mathscr{X}}
is a class of proper metric spaces such that for some
n\geq 1

and
c>0
, all members of
{\mathscr{X}}
satisfy condition
({\rm CI}_{n})[c]
,
and every sequence
(X_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
in
{\mathscr{X}}
has asymptotic rank
\leq n
.
We begin with a uniform version of the Morse lemma, analogous to
Theorem 5.1 in
[
28
]
. The asymptotic rank assumption is only used
through Proposition
5.2
, or Theorem
5.1
, which
in turn follows from Theorem
5.4
.
Hence,
({\rm SII}_{n})\Rightarrow({\rm ML}_{n})
.

Theorem 7.1

(Morse lemma)
.

For all
C>0
and
Q\geq 1
there is a constant
l=l({\mathscr{X}},n,c,C,Q)\geq 0

such that if
X
belongs to
{\mathscr{X}}
, and
Z\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X)

has
(C,a)
-controlled density and is
(Q,a)
-quasi-minimizing mod
Y
,
where
Y\subset X
is a closed set and
a\geq 0
,
then the support of
Z
is within distance at most
\max\{l,4a\}
from
Y
.

Proof.

Let
x\in\operatorname{spt}(Z)\setminus Y
.
Essentially the same argument as for the second part of
Theorem
2.3
(using
({\rm EII}_{n-1})
if
n\geq 2
) shows
that there is a constant
\delta_{0}^{\prime}=\delta_{0}^{\prime}(n,c)>0
such that

\Theta_{x,s}(Z)\geq\delta_{0}^{\prime}\,Q^{1-n}
whenever
s>2a
and

B_{x}(s)\cap Y=\emptyset
(see Lemma 3.3 in
[
28
]
).
Now let
V\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n+1,\text{\rm c}}(X)
be a
minimizing filling of
Z
, and suppose that
r>4a
and

B_{x}(r)\cap Y=\emptyset
. For almost every
s\in(2a,r)
, the slice

T_{s}=\partial(V\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}B_{x}(s))-Z\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}B_{x}(s)\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X)
satisfies

Q\,{\mathbf{M}}(T_{s})\geq{\mathbf{M}}(Z\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}B_{x}(s))\geq\delta_{0}^{\prime}\,Q^{1-n}s^{n},

and integrating the inequality
{\mathbf{M}}(T_{s})\geq\delta_{0}^{\prime}\,Q^{-n}s^{n}
from

r/2>2a
to
r
we get that
\Theta_{x,r}(V)\geq\delta
for some

\delta=\delta(n,c,Q)>0
(compare Lemma 3.4 in
[
28
]
).
On the other hand, by Proposition
5.2

there is a constant
l:=\varrho({\mathscr{X}},n,c,C,\delta)>0
such that

\Theta_{x,r}(V)<\delta
for all
r>\max\{l,a\}
.
Hence,
r\leq\max\{l,4a\}
.
∎

The next statement strengthens Theorem 5.2 in
[
28
]
.
The proof shows that
({\rm ML}_{n})\Rightarrow({\rm SS}_{n})
.
A
facet
of an
(n+1)
-simplex is an
n
-dimensional face.

Theorem 7.2

(slim simplices)
.

For all
L\geq 1
there is a constant
D=D({\mathscr{X}},n,c,L)\geq 0
such that
the following holds. Let
\Delta
be a Euclidean
(n+1)
-simplex,
X
a member
of
{\mathscr{X}}
, and
a\geq 0
. Suppose that
f\colon\partial\Delta\to X
is a map whose
restriction to each facet of
\Delta
is an
(L,a)
-quasi-isometric embedding.
Then the image of every facet is within distance at most
D(1+a)
from the
union of the images of the remaining ones.

Proof.

Let
W_{0},\dots,W_{n+1}\subset\partial\Delta
be an enumeration of the (closed) facets
of
\Delta
, and let
E_{i}:=(\partial\llbracket\Delta\rrbracket)\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}W_{i}\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})

denote the corresponding currents, whose sum is the boundary cycle

\partial\llbracket\Delta\rrbracket\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})
.

Suppose that
a>0
.
Choose a triangulation of
\partial\Delta
with simplices of diameter
\leq a
such
that, for some constant
K=K(n)
and for each
i
, any ball in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}

of radius
r>a
intersects at most
Ka^{-n}r^{n}
maximal simplices in
W_{i}
.
Let
{\mathscr{P}}_{*}(\partial\Delta)
be the corresponding chain complex of simplicial integral
currents. A slight adaptation of Proposition
6.2

provides a chain map
\iota\colon{\mathscr{P}}_{*}(\partial\Delta)\to{\mathbf{I}}_{*,\text{\rm c}}(X)

such that the following properties hold for each

S_{i}:=\iota(E_{i})\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X)
and for some constants
C,Q

depending only on
n,c,L
:

(1)

\operatorname{spt}(S_{i})\subset[f(W_{i})]_{Qa}
and
\operatorname{spt}(\partial S_{i})\subset[f(\partial W_{i})]_{Qa}
;

(2)

S_{i}
has
(C,a)
-controlled density and is
(Q,Qa)
-quasi-minimizing
mod
[f(\partial W_{i})]_{Qa}
;

(3)

d(f(x),\operatorname{spt}(S_{i}))\leq Qa
for all
x\in W_{i}
with
d(x,\partial W_{i})>Qa
.

Here
[\,\cdot\,]_{Qa}
stands again for the closed
Qa
-neighborhood, and

\partial W_{i}
denotes the relative boundary of
W_{i}
.
Let
M_{i}
denote the union of all
W_{j}
with
j\neq i
.
The cycle
Z:=\iota(\partial\llbracket\Delta\rrbracket)=\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}S_{i}
has

((n+2)C,a)
-controlled density and is

(Q,Qa)
-quasi-minimizing mod
[f(M_{i})]_{Qa}
for every
i
.
It then follows from Theorem
7.1

that the set
\operatorname{spt}(S_{i})\setminus[f(M_{i}))]_{Qa}=\operatorname{spt}(Z)\setminus[f(M_{i})]_{Qa}
is
within distance at most
\max\{l^{\prime},4Qa\}
from
[f(M_{i})]_{Qa}
for some

l^{\prime}=l^{\prime}({\mathscr{X}},n,c,L)
.
Hence, for any
x\in W_{i}
, it follows from (3) that

d(f(x),f(M_{i}))
is less than or equal to
2Qa+\max\{l^{\prime},4Qa\}

if
d(x,\partial W_{i})>Qa
and less than or equal to
LQa+a
otherwise.

Note that if the restriction of
f
to each facet of
\Delta
is

L
-Lipschitz in addition, or if
a=0
, then the proof can be simplified
by using Proposition
6.1
instead of
Proposition
6.2
.
∎

The proof of the following result relies again on
Proposition
5.2
; thus
({\rm SII}_{n})\Rightarrow({\rm FR}_{n})
.

Theorem 7.3

(filling radius)
.

For all
C>0
there is a constant
h=h({\mathscr{X}},n,c,C)>0
such that
if
X
belongs to
{\mathscr{X}}
and
Z\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X)
has
(C,a)
-controlled
density for some
a\geq 0
, then the support of every minimizing filling

V\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n+1,\text{\rm c}}(X)
of
Z
is within distance at most

\max\{h,a\}
from
\operatorname{spt}(Z)
.

Proof.

Suppose that
x\in\operatorname{spt}(V)\setminus\operatorname{spt}(Z)
.
By Theorem
2.2
and Theorem
2.3
there are constants

\gamma=\gamma(n,c)
and
\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}(n,\gamma)>0
such that

\Theta_{x,r}(V)\geq\delta_{0}
whenever
r>0
and
B_{x}(r)\cap\operatorname{spt}(Z)=\emptyset
.
On the other hand, Proposition
5.2
shows that there
is a constant
h=\varrho({\mathscr{X}},n,c,C,\delta_{0})>0
such that

\Theta_{x,r}(V)<\delta_{0}
for all
r>\max\{h,a\}
. Thus there is no
point
x\in\operatorname{spt}(V)
at distance bigger than
\max\{h,a\}
from

\operatorname{spt}(Z)
.
∎

We now prove the implication
({\rm SS}_{n})\Rightarrow({\rm AR}_{n})
, which
holds without further assumptions on the metric space
X
.

Proposition 7.4
.

Let
(X_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence of metric spaces
X_{i}=(X_{i},d_{i})
,
let
n\geq 1
, and suppose that for every
L\geq 1
there exists
D\geq 0

such that every
X_{i}
satisfies
({\rm SS}_{n})
with constant
D=D(L)
.
Then the sequence
(X_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
has asymptotic rank
\leq n
.

Proof.

Suppose to the contrary that the sequence
(X_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
has asymptotic
rank
>n
. Then there exist a compact set
K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
with positive
Lebesgue measure, an
L
-bi-Lipschitz map
\phi\colon K\to\Omega

onto some metric space
\Omega
, and a sequence of
(1,\delta_{i})
-quasi-isometric
embeddings
h_{i}\colon\Omega\to(X_{i},r_{i}^{-1}d_{i})
, where
L\geq 1
,

\delta_{i}\to 0
, and
r_{i}\to\infty
.
We can assume that
0\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
is a Lebesgue density point of
K
.
Let
B:=B_{0}(1)\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
.
For all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
there is a
\lambda_{k}>0
such that every point
in
\lambda_{k}B
is at distance
\leq(2k)^{-1}\lambda_{k}
from some point in
K
,
thus there exist
(1,k^{-1}\lambda_{k})
-quasi-isometric embeddings

\psi_{k}\colon\lambda_{k}B\to K
.
Choose
i(k)\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
s_{k}:=\lambda_{k}r_{i(k)}\to\infty

and
\epsilon_{k}:=\lambda_{k}^{-1}\delta_{i(k)}+k^{-1}L\to 0
.
It is straightforward to check that the map

f_{k}\colon s_{k}B\to(X_{i(k)},d_{i(k)})

defined by
f_{k}(s_{k}x)=h_{i(k)}\circ\phi\circ\psi_{k}(\lambda_{k}x)

for all
x\in B
is an
(L,\epsilon_{k}s_{k})
-quasi-isometric embedding.

Now let
\Delta
be any
(n+1)
-simplex inscribed in
B
, and pick a point

x
in a facet of
\Delta
such that
x
is a distance

\delta>0
away from the union
M
of the remaining facets.
For every
k
, the point
f_{k}(s_{k}x)
is at distance at least

L^{-1}\delta s_{k}-\epsilon_{k}s_{k}
from
f_{k}(s_{k}M)
. On the other hand, by
assumption, there is a constant
D=D(L)
such that the distance is at
most
D(1+\epsilon_{k}s_{k})
. This leads to the inequality

L^{-1}\delta-\epsilon_{k}\leq D(s_{k}^{-1}+\epsilon_{k})
, which contradicts
the fact that
s_{k}\to\infty
and
\epsilon_{k}\to 0
.
∎

Lastly, we show that
({\rm FR}_{n})\Rightarrow({\rm AR}_{n})
. This is similar
to Theorem 6.1 in
[
40
]
.

Proposition 7.5
.

Let
n\geq 1
and
c>0
, and let
(X_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence of proper
metric spaces
X_{i}=(X_{i},d_{i})
satisfying
({\rm CI}_{n})[c]
. Suppose further that
for every
C>0
there exists
h>0
such that every
X_{i}
satisfies

({\rm FR}_{n})
with constant
h=h(C)
. Then the sequence
(X_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}

has asymptotic rank
\leq n
.

Proof.

Suppose to the contrary that
(X_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
has asymptotic rank
>n
.
Let
L,s_{k},\epsilon_{k}
and
f_{k}\colon s_{k}B\to X_{i(k)}

be given as in the first part of the proof of Proposition
7.4
.
Let again
\Delta
be any
(n+1)
-simplex inscribed in
B
.
For every
k
, fix a triangulation of
\partial\Delta
with simplices of
diameter at most
\epsilon_{k}
such that, for some constant
K=K(n)
,
every ball in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
of radius
r>\epsilon_{k}
meets at most

K(r/\epsilon_{k})^{n}
maximal simplices in each facet of
\Delta
.
It then follows as in the proof of Proposition
6.2

that for every
k
, and for some constants
C,\bar{L},\bar{M}
depending
only on
n,c,L
, there exist a cycle
Z_{k}\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{n,\text{\rm c}}(X_{i(k)})
with

(C,\epsilon_{k}s_{k})
-controlled density,
an
\bar{L}
-Lipschitz map
\bar{f}_{k}\colon X_{i(k)}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
,
and a current
R_{k}\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n+1,\text{\rm c}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})
such that

\partial R_{k}=\bar{f}_{k\#}Z_{k}-\partial\llbracket s_{k}\Delta\rrbracket
and

\operatorname{spt}(R_{k})\cup\bar{f}_{k}(\operatorname{spt}(Z_{k}))\subset[\partial(s_{k}\Delta)]_{\bar{M}\epsilon_{k}s_{k}}.

Fix a point
x\in\Delta
a distance
\delta>0
away from
\partial\Delta
.
Suppose that
k
is so large that
\bar{M}\epsilon_{k}<\delta
,
and
V_{k}\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n+1,\text{\rm c}}(X_{i(k)})
is any filling of
Z_{k}
.
Then
\partial\llbracket s_{k}\Delta\rrbracket=\partial(\bar{f}_{k\#}V_{k})-\partial R_{k}
, hence

\llbracket s_{k}\Delta\rrbracket=\bar{f}_{k\#}V_{k}-R_{k}
and

s_{k}\Delta\subset\operatorname{spt}(\bar{f}_{k\#}V_{k})\cup\operatorname{spt}(R_{k})
.
Since
s_{k}x\not\in\operatorname{spt}(R_{k})
,
there is a point
y_{k}\in\operatorname{spt}(V_{k})
such that
\bar{f}_{k}(y_{k})=s_{k}x
.
For every
z_{k}\in\operatorname{spt}(Z_{k})
, we have

s_{k}\delta=d(s_{k}x,s_{k}\Delta)\leq d(s_{k}x,\bar{f}_{k}(z_{k}))+\bar{M}\epsilon_{k}s_{k}\leq\bar{L}\,d_{i(k)}(y_{k},z_{k})+\bar{M}\epsilon_{k}s_{k}.

On the other hand, by assumption, there exists a filling
V_{k}
of
Z_{k}

whose support is within distance
\max\{h,\epsilon_{k}s_{k}\}
from
\operatorname{spt}(Z_{k})
,
where
h=h(C)
. This leads to the inequality

\delta\leq\bar{L}\max\{s_{k}^{-1}h,\epsilon_{k}\}+\bar{M}\epsilon_{k}
,

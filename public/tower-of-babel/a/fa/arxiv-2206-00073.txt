An update on Haiman’s conjectures
Alex Abreu, Antonio Nigro

Abstract
. We revisit Haiman’s conjecture on the relations between characters of Kazdhan-Lusztig basis elements of the Hecke algebra over S n S_{n} . The conjecture asserts that, for purposes of character evaluation, any Kazhdan-Lusztig basis element is reducible to a sum of the simplest possible ones (those associated to so-called codominant permutations). When the basis element is associated to a smooth permutation, we are able to give a geometric proof of this conjecture. On the other hand, if the permutation is singular, we provide a counterexample.

We begin by recalling some properties of GKM-spaces (see
[
GKM98
]

with an action of a torus

such that the number of fixed points and the number of
1
-dimensional orbits are finite. The equivariant cohomology

is then encoded in a combinatorial object called the

of
\mathcal{X}
. The vertices of the moment graph are the fixed points, while the edges are the
1
-dimensional orbits, each of which has exactly two fixed points on its closure.

If
X
is an
n\times n
diagonal regular semisimple matrix, the torus
T\cong(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
of diagonal matrices acts on the variety
\mathcal{Y}_{w}(X)
. When
w
is smooth, this variety is a GKM-space because the action is a restriction of that of
T
on the whole flag variety, where the number of fixed points and
1
-dimensional orbits are indeed finite. Moreover, the moment graph also encodes the action of
S_{n}
on the equivariant cohomology group
H_{T}^{*}(\mathcal{Y}_{w}(X))
(induced by the monodromy action of
\pi_{1}(GL_{n}^{rs},X)
), usually called the
dot action
(see
[
Tym08
]
). In particular, if
w
and
w^{\prime}
are smooth permutations and
\mathcal{Y}_{w}(X)
and
\mathcal{Y}_{w^{\prime}}(X)
have the same moment graph, then
\ch(H^{*}(\mathcal{Y}_{w}(X)))=\ch(H^{*}(\mathcal{Y}_{w^{\prime}}(X)))
.

Since
\mathcal{Y}_{w}(X)
is a
T
-invariant subvariety of
\mathcal{B}
, we have that the moment graph of
\mathcal{Y}_{w}(X)
is a subgraph of the moment graph of the flag variety
\mathcal{B}
. We briefly recall the moment graph of
\mathcal{B}
(see
[

]
and
[
Tym08
, Proposition 2.1]
). The fixed points in

are indexed by permutations

(in fact, they are equal to

for

is fixed by

if and only if each

is generated by eigenvectors of
T
. However the eigenvectors of
T
are precisely the canonical basis vectors

, so there exists

such that
V_{i}=\langle e_{w(1)},\ldots,e_{w(n)}\rangle
.

The

(w_{1},w_{2},t)

(corresponding to fixed points) with

and

. Then the orbit can be described as follows: Write

is not contained in the preimage of the relevant locus. If
sz^{\prime}<z^{\prime}
and
z^{\prime}<z^{\prime}s
, then
sz^{\prime}\neq z^{\prime}s
, so by Lemma
3.3

\mathcal{Y}_{z^{\prime}}^{\circ}(X)\to\mathcal{Z}
is injective, and hence
\mathcal{Y}_{z^{\prime}}^{\circ}(X)
is not contained in the preimage of the relevant locus. Finally, if
sz^{\prime}<z^{\prime}
and
z^{\prime}s<z^{\prime}
, then
z^{\prime}=z
, so by Lemma
3.3

\mathcal{Y}_{z^{\prime}}(X)\to\mathcal{Z}
is
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle over its image, and hence
\mathcal{Y}_{z^{\prime}}(X)
is contained in the preimage of the relevant locus. Since the preimage of the relavant locus has codimension one, it is precisely
\mathcal{Y}_{z^{\prime}}(X)
.

By the decomposition theorem (we set
\mathcal{Z}_{1}
as the image of
\mathcal{Y}_{z}(C)
if
z
exsits),
IH^{*}(\mathcal{Y}_{ws}(X))=IH^{*}(\mathcal{Z})\otimes(\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C}[-2])
,
H^{*}(\mathcal{Y}_{w}(X))=IH^{*}(\mathcal{Z})\otimes IH^{*}(\mathcal{Z}_{1})[-2]
and
IH^{*}(\mathcal{Y}_{z}(X))=IH^{*}(\mathcal{Z_{1}})\otimes(\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C}[-2])
. Then

\displaystyle\ch(IH^{*}(\mathcal{Y}_{ws}(X)))

\displaystyle=(1+q)\ch(IH^{*}(\mathcal{Z})),

\displaystyle\ch(H^{*}(\mathcal{Y}_{w}(X)))

\displaystyle=\ch(IH^{*}(\mathcal{Z}))+q\ch(IH^{*}(\mathcal{Z}_{1})),

\displaystyle\ch(IH^{*}(\mathcal{Y}_{z}(X)))

\displaystyle=(1+q)\ch(IH^{*}(\mathcal{Z_{1}})),

which implies

(1+q)\ch(H^{*}(\mathcal{Y}_{w}(X)))=\ch(IH^{*}(\mathcal{Y}_{ws}(X)))+q\ch(IH^{*}(\mathcal{Y}_{z}(X))).

This, in turn, is equivalent by Theorem
1.5
to

(1+q)\ch(q^{\frac{\ell(w)}{2}}C^{\prime}_{w})=\ch(q^{\frac{\ell(w)+1}{2}}C^{\prime}_{ws})+q\ch(q^{\frac{\ell(w)-1}{2}}C^{\prime}_{z}).

When
w
is codominant and
ws
is smooth, then both
ws
and
z
are codominant as well. Below we give an example of what happens for Hessenberg varieties.

Example 3.5

(Geometric interpretation of the modular law for indifference graphs)
.

Let

,

,

be a simple transposition.

We claim that

and

. Indeed, since

and

, while
w_{\mathbf{m}_{1}}^{-1}(l+1)>i

w_{\mathbf{m}_{1}}s<w_{\mathbf{m}_{1}}<sw_{\mathbf{m}_{1}}

and
\mathbf{m}_{2}
agrees with
\mathbf{m}_{1}
everywhere else,

. Finally,
w_{\mathbf{m}_{0}}\lessdot w_{\mathbf{m}_{1}}

\mathbf{m}_{0}(i)<l
and
\mathbf{m}_{0}(i+1)>l

.

Let

be a regular semi-simple matrix, then the Hessenberg varieties are

3.1.
The geometry of
\mathcal{Y}_{w}(X)
when
w
is smooth

In the proof of Theorem
1.6
in Section
2
, we saw that for each smooth permutation
w\in S_{n}
there exists a codominant permutation
w^{\prime}
such that the moment graphs of
\mathcal{Y}_{w}(X)
and
\mathcal{Y}_{w^{\prime}}(X)
are the same and, in particular, they have isomorphic equivariant cohomology. We also saw that all the varieties
\mathcal{Y}_{w}(X)
associated to Coxeter elements
w
are isomorphic. We make the following conjecture which is a strengthening of Theorem
1.6
.

Conjecture 3.9
.

Let
X\in SL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
be regular semisimple and
w\in S_{n}
smooth. Then there exists a codominant permutation
w^{\prime}
such that
\mathcal{Y}_{w}(X)
and
\mathcal{Y}_{w^{\prime}}(X)
are homeomorphic.

We remark that the correspoding statement for Schubert varieties is false, for instance
\Omega_{3142,F_{\bullet}}
is not homeomorphic to
\Omega_{2341,F_{\bullet}}
(and this is the only Schubert variety associated with a codominant permutation with the same Poincaré polynomial as of
\Omega_{3142,F_{\bullet}}
). On the other hand, both
3142
and
2341
are coxeter elements so that
\mathcal{Y}_{3142}(X)
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{Y}_{2341}(X)
if
X
is regular semisimple.

Abstract
We present model predictive selection (MPS), a new method for selecting the stable closed-loop (CL) equilibrium attitude-error quaternion (AEQ) of an uncrewed aerial vehicle (UAV) during the execution of high-speed yaw maneuvers. In this approach, we minimize the cost of yawing measured with a performance figure of merit (PFM) that takes into account both the aerodynamic-torque control input and attitude-error state of the UAV. Specifically, this method uses a control law with a term whose sign is dynamically switched in real time to select, between two options, the torque associated with the lesser cost of rotation as predicted by a dynamical model of the UAV derived from first principles. This problem is relevant because the selection of the stable CL equilibrium AEQ significantly impacts the performance of a UAV during high-speed rotational flight, from both the power and control-error perspectives. To test and demonstrate the functionality and performance of the proposed method, we present data collected during one hundred real-time high-speed yaw-tracking flight experiments. These results highlight the superior capabilities of the proposed MPS-based scheme when compared to a benchmark controller commonly used in aerial robotics, as the PFM used to quantify the cost of flight is reduced by 60.30 % \boldsymbol{60.30\,\%} , on average. To our best knowledge, these are the first flight-test results that thoroughly demonstrate, evaluate, and compare the performance of a real-time controller capable of selecting the stable CL equilibrium AEQ during operation.

III
The Performance Problem and Benchmark Controller

As mentioned in Section

, both CL equilibrium AEQs of the system specified by (

\{\boldsymbol{\mathrlap{\mathchoice{\raisebox{-6.55pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\displaystyle\hskip 0.5pt{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0mu}$}}{\raisebox{-6.55pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\textstyle\hskip 0.5pt{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0mu}$}}{\raisebox{-6.55pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle\hskip 0.5pt{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0mu}$}}{\raisebox{-6.55pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.5pt{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0mu}$}}}q}^{\star}_{\text{e}},\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\star}_{\text{e}}\}
and
\{\boldsymbol{\mathrlap{\mathchoice{\raisebox{-6.55pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\displaystyle\hskip 0.5pt{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0mu}$}}{\raisebox{-6.55pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\textstyle\hskip 0.5pt{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0mu}$}}{\raisebox{-6.55pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle\hskip 0.5pt{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0mu}$}}{\raisebox{-6.55pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.5pt{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0mu}$}}}q}^{\dagger}_{\text{e}},\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\star}_{\text{e}}\}
, correspond exactly to the same physical state of the controlled UAV; namely, the condition when the instantaneous position, attitude, and angular velocity of
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{B}}
coincide exactly with those of
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{B}}_{\text{d}}
. However, the first fixed point is stable whereas the second is unstable. This apparent paradox results from the
quaternion-ambiguity
phenomenon. To see this issue, consider (
6
) and recall that
\boldsymbol{u}_{\text{e}}
is the unit vector about which
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{B}}
must be rotated an angle
\Theta_{\text{e}}
to exactly reach the attitude of
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{B}}_{\text{d}}
. Therefore,
Propositions 1 and 2
tell us that when
\Theta_{\text{e}}=0
and
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\text{e}}=\boldsymbol{0}
, small deviations from this condition are easily corrected by the system’s controller; whereas, when
\Theta_{\text{e}}=2\pi\,\text{rad}
and
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\text{e}}=\boldsymbol{0}
, any deviation from this condition, small or large, induces the system’s controller to generate a rotation on the order of
2\pi\,\text{rad}
. Furthermore, it is straightforward to see that when
\pi<\Theta_{\text{e}}<2\pi\,\text{rad}
, the torque
\boldsymbol{K}_{\boldsymbol{n}}\boldsymbol{n}_{\text{e}}
is applied in the direction of the longer path. This dynamic behavior does not pose any problems regarding stability; however, from a performance perspective, it raises numerous energy and
state-error
optimization research questions.

In the past, to avoid undesired
2\pi
-rad
rotations during the execution of flight maneuvers
[
27
]
, we modified the first term of the control law specified by (
10
) by multiplying it by the sign of
m_{\text{e}}

[
8
,
9
,
10
,
7
,
11
]
. Namely,

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\text{b}}=\text{sgn}\{m_{\text{e}}\}\boldsymbol{K}_{\boldsymbol{n}}\boldsymbol{n}_{\text{e}}+\boldsymbol{K}_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\text{e}}+\boldsymbol{J}\boldsymbol{\dot{\omega}}_{\text{d}}+\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\boldsymbol{J}\boldsymbol{\omega}.

(12)

From this point onwards, we refer to (
12

\text{sgn}\{m_{\text{e}}\}\boldsymbol{K}_{\boldsymbol{n}}\boldsymbol{n}_{\text{e}}
is the same as that of
\boldsymbol{u}_{\text{e}}
, for

-\boldsymbol{u}_{\text{e}}
, for

, which ensures that the torque
\text{sgn}\{m_{\text{e}}\}\boldsymbol{K}_{\boldsymbol{n}}\boldsymbol{n}_{\text{e}}
is applied in the direction of the shorter rotational path between
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{B}}
and
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{B}}_{\text{d}}
. An alternative interpretation of the switching method specified by (
12
) is that when
\text{sgn}\{m_{\text{e}}\}
becomes negative,
\{\boldsymbol{\mathrlap{\mathchoice{\raisebox{-6.55pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\displaystyle\hskip 0.5pt{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0mu}$}}{\raisebox{-6.55pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\textstyle\hskip 0.5pt{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0mu}$}}{\raisebox{-6.55pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle\hskip 0.5pt{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0mu}$}}{\raisebox{-6.55pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.5pt{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0mu}$}}}q}^{\dagger}_{\text{e}},\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\star}_{\text{e}}\}
becomes asymptotically stable and
\{\boldsymbol{\mathrlap{\mathchoice{\raisebox{-6.55pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\displaystyle\hskip 0.5pt{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0mu}$}}{\raisebox{-6.55pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\textstyle\hskip 0.5pt{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0mu}$}}{\raisebox{-6.55pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle\hskip 0.5pt{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0mu}$}}{\raisebox{-6.55pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.5pt{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0mu}$}}}q}^{\star}_{\text{e}},\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\star}_{\text{e}}\}

IV
Model Predictive Selection

To our best knowledge, all the published methods that select the stable CL equilibrium AEQ during flight use the current value of the system state only
[
29
,
30
]
. Here, we introduce the MPS algorithm, an alternative approach that predicts and selects the most
cost-efficient
stable CL equilibrium AEQ over a
finite-time
horizon, according to a PFM. In this case, we use the PFM

\displaystyle\Gamma(t)=\int^{t+t_{\text{h}}}_{t}\left[\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\hskip-0.60275pt\sigma}^{T}(\zeta)\boldsymbol{R}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\hskip-0.60275pt\sigma}(\zeta)+\boldsymbol{n}_{\text{e}}^{T}(\zeta)\boldsymbol{Q}\boldsymbol{n}_{\text{e}}(\zeta)\,\right]d\zeta,

(13)

where
t_{\text{h}}
is the time horizon;
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\hskip-0.60275pt\sigma}
is the torque exciting the system, which is generated through a control law that explicitly depends on the parameter
\sigma
; and,
\boldsymbol{R}
and
\boldsymbol{Q}
are Hermitian
positive-definite
weight matrices that function as tuning parameters. We specify the control law as

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\hskip-0.60275pt\sigma}=\sigma\boldsymbol{K}_{\boldsymbol{n}}\boldsymbol{n}_{\text{e}}+\boldsymbol{K}_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\text{e}}+\boldsymbol{J}\boldsymbol{\dot{\omega}}_{\text{d}}+\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\boldsymbol{J}\boldsymbol{\omega},

(14)

where
\sigma\in\{-1,+1\}
is dynamically selected according to

\displaystyle\sigma^{+}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\hskip-7.74998pt\,\,~~\sigma,~~~\,\,\text{if}~~~\,-\delta<\Delta\Gamma<\delta\\
\hskip-12.91663pt~-1,~~~~\text{if}~~~~~~~~~~~~\Delta\Gamma\geq\delta\\
\hskip-10.76385pt~~\,+1,~~~~\text{if}~~~~~~~~~~~~\Delta\Gamma\leq-\delta\end{array}\right.,

where
\sigma
is the current value, initialized as
\sigma=+1
;

is a hysteresis margin included to avoid Zeno behavior
[
35
]
; and,

\Gamma^{\star}
and
\Gamma^{\dagger}
are the PFM values, computed using (
13
), associated with choosing either
\{\boldsymbol{\mathrlap{\mathchoice{\raisebox{-6.55pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\displaystyle\hskip 0.5pt{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0mu}$}}{\raisebox{-6.55pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\textstyle\hskip 0.5pt{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0mu}$}}{\raisebox{-6.55pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle\hskip 0.5pt{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0mu}$}}{\raisebox{-6.55pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.5pt{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0mu}$}}}q}^{\star}_{\text{e}},\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\star}_{\text{e}}\}

V-B

Flight Experiments

To test and demonstrate the capabilities of the proposed
MPS-based
controller, we performed several different
high-speed

yaw-tracking
maneuvers. The results corresponding to two different
real-time
attitude references are shown in Fig.
4
. Each reference is composed of three stages. In
Stage 1
, the quadrotor hovers stationarily and the rotational commands are set to zero; in
Stage 2
, an
angular-velocity
reference in the
\boldsymbol{b}_{3}
direction commands the UAV to rotate at the constant value
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{0}
about its yaw axis; last, in
Stage 3
, once the
yaw-angle
signal
\psi(t)
, with the form of a ramp, reaches a desired
initial
angle
\psi(t_{0})=\psi_{0}
, the constant
yaw-angle
reference
\psi_{\text{d}}(t)=0^{\circ}
, for
t\geq t_{0}
, is inputted to the system, thus compelling the UAV to rotate toward it. Executing pure
yaw-tracking
maneuvers allows us to straightforwardly evaluate the functionality and performance of different control methods. For the
real-time
implementation of the controllers, we selected
\boldsymbol{K}_{\boldsymbol{n}}=900\cdot\boldsymbol{J}\,~\text{N}\cdot\text{m}
and
\boldsymbol{K}_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}=90\cdot\boldsymbol{J}\,~\text{N}\cdot\text{m}\cdot\text{s}\cdot\text{rad}^{-1}
as the controller gains in (
12
) and (
14
). Additionally, we empirically selected the
finite-time
horizon for the
MPS-based
controller to be
0.4\,\text{s}
, and the tunable weights in (
13
) to be
\boldsymbol{R}=\boldsymbol{I}
and
\boldsymbol{Q}=10^{-6}\cdot\boldsymbol{I}\,~\text{N}^{2}\cdot\text{m}^{2}
, where
\boldsymbol{I}
is the identity matrix with compatible dimensions. The reason for choosing significantly lower values for the diagonal entries of
\boldsymbol{Q}
is the difference in order of magnitude between
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\hskip-0.60275pt\sigma}^{T}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\hskip-0.60275pt\sigma}
and
\boldsymbol{n}_{\text{e}}^{T}\boldsymbol{n}_{\text{e}}
. Last, we set the hysteresis margin to be
\delta=5\cdot 10^{-7}
, which was empirically determined to be large enough to avoid chattering.

VI
Conclusions

We presented a new attitude control method that dynamically selects the stable CL equilibrium AEQ of a UAV during
high-speed

yaw-rotational
flight. The main objective of this approach is minimizing the cost of flight according to a PFM that explicitly accounts for both the control input and
attitude-error
state of the UAV. The method is based on MPS, a new algorithm implemented in real time to estimate the cost of flight over a
finite-time
horizon, using a dynamic model derived from first principles. To test and demonstrate the functionality and performance of the proposed method, we performed one hundred
yaw-tracking
flight tests using an
MPS-based
controller and a benchmark scheme. For these tests, the experimental data show that the
MPS-based
controller reduces the value of the
cost-of-flight
PFM by
60.30\,\%
, on average, with respect to that obtained with the benchmark controller. These results highlight the suitability and potentials of the
MPS-based
approach.

Ascending Chains of Free Quasiconvex Subgroups
Jack Kohav, Nir Lazarovich

Abstract
We prove that a hyperbolic group cannot contain a strictly ascending chain of free quasiconvex subgroups of constant rank.



1
Introduction

We say that a group satisfies
ACCF
if every ascending chain of free subgroups of constant rank stabilizes.
Takahasi
[
14
]
and Higman
[
7
]
showed that free groups satisfy ACCF. Kapovich-Myasnikov
[
9
]
gave a new proof of this result using Stallings’ foldings. Shusterman
[
12
]
proved that limit groups (and in particular surface groups) satisfy ACCF. Bering-Lazarovich
[
2
]
proved that some 3-manifold groups satisfy ACCF. Kappvich
[
8
]
gave a result with similar flavours in the setting of random groups.

In this paper we consider the ACCF property in Gromov hyperbolic groups
[
6
]
.
Note that hyperbolic groups do not always satisfy ACCF; Consider a hyperbolic ascending HNN extension of the free group, e.g. the group
G=\langle a,b,t|tat^{-1}=ab,tbt^{-1}=ba\rangle
.
H=\langle a,b\rangle\leq G
is a free subgroup of rank 2, and
H<t^{-1}Ht<t^{-2}Ht^{2}<...
is an ascending chain of free subgroups of rank 2.

To remedy this, we consider only chains of quasiconvex free subgroups, and prove the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a hyperbolic group, then every ascending chain
\{H_{i}\}
of free quasiconvex subgroups of constant rank stabilizes.

Let
G
be a hyperbolic group, and let
X
be the Cayley graph of
G
with respect to some fixed finite generating set. Recall that
H
is a quasiconvex subgroup of
G
if and only if
H
is finitely generated and the inclusion map
H\to G
is a quasi-isometric embedding. When
H
is free this can be quantified as follows:

Definition 1.2
.

Let
K\geq 1,C\geq 0
. A finitely generated free subgroup
H
of
G
is
(K,C)
-arboreal
, if there exists a based metric tree
(T,\widetilde{*})
with a free
H
-action, and an
H
-equivariant
(K,C)
-quasi-isometric embedding
(T,\widetilde{*})\to(G,1)
.

Clearly, a free subgroup of a hyperbolic group is quasiconvex if and only if it is
(K,C)
-arboreal for some
K,C
.
Our main tool in the proof of
Theorem

1.1
is to establish uniform bounds for
(K,C)
for free quasiconvex subgroups of the same rank.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
G
be a hyperbolic group.
For all
r\in\mathbb{N}
there exist
K\geq 1,C\geq 0
such that every quasiconvex free subgroup of
G
of rank
r
is
(K,C)
-arboreal.

The proof of this theorem is through a process of Stallings’ folds for quasiconvex subgroups of hyperbolic groups. See also
[
13
,
16
,
1
,
10
,
3
,
5
,
11
,
15
]
.
We show that if
H\leq H^{\prime}
are
(K,C)
-arboreal, then there is
(K^{\prime},C^{\prime})
-quasi-isometric map between the core of
H
to the core of
H^{\prime}
. If in addition
H
is not contained in a free factor of
H^{\prime}
we use this map to bound the size of the core of
H^{\prime}
:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
G
be a hyperbolic group, let
r\in\mathbb{N}
and let
H\leq G
be a quasiconvex free subgroup. Then there are finitely many free quasiconvex subgroups
H\leq H^{\prime}\leq G
of rank
r
in which
H
is not contained in a free factor.

Theorem

1.1
easily follows from
Theorem

1.4
as we explain in §
9
.

2
Outline

In this section we review Kapovich and Myasnikov’s proof
[
9
]
of the following theorem, and see how aspects of it might carry over to the more general case of hyperbolic groups.

Theorem 2.1

(Takahasi
[
14
]
, Higman
[
7
]
)
.

The free group
F_{n}
satisfies ACCF.

Proof
[
9
]
.

Let
|S|=k
, and
G=F(S)
be the free group on
S
. Let
H_{1}\leq H_{2}\leq\dots\leq G
be an ascending chain of (free) subgroups of rank
k
. As we will see in
Lemma

9.1
we may assume that
H_{i}
is not contained in a free factor of
H_{i+1}
for all
i
. Now, consider the action of one of the groups
H_{i}
on
\Cay(G,S)
. For each one of the groups
H_{i}
, let
T_{i}
be the convex hull of the orbit of
1\in\Cay(G,S)
.
T_{i}
is an
H_{i}
-invariant subtree and the quotient
\Gamma_{i}={\raisebox{-1.99997pt}{$H_{i}$}\left\backslash\raisebox{1.99997pt}{$T_{i}$}\right.}
is a finite metric graph with
\pi_{1}(\Gamma_{i})\simeq H_{i}
. The inclusion
T_{i}\subseteq T_{i+1}
(which is due to the fact that
T_{i+1}
is also
H_{i}
-invariant) induces a map
\varphi_{i}:\Gamma_{i}\to\Gamma_{i+1}
. These maps are surjective - otherwise,
H_{i}
would have been contained in a free factor of
H_{i+1}
. Since the maps
\varphi_{i}
are simplicial, the number of edges in
\Gamma_{i}
must reduce. This means that the chain of graphs
\Gamma_{i}
stabilizes. By the definition of
T_{i}
, the groups
H_{i}
must eventually stabilize as well.
∎

The key part of the proof is finding a cocompact invariant subtree, and using it to attach a finite graph to every subgroup in the chain. In hyperbolic groups, it is not clear how to do this - the group does not have a unique minimal invariant subtree. To remedy this, we define the notion of arboreality (
Definition

1.2
). Arboreal subgroups are assumed to come with a finite graph attached, and a quasi-isometry sending the universal cover to the Cayley graph of our group. In sections §
4
-§
6
we will show that indeed every quasiconvex free subgroup is arboreal with constants that are determined by the rank. After that, in §
7
-§
9
we look at nested subgroups that are arboreal with the same constants. we can work with chains of subgroups that are all arboreal with the same constants.

In §
4
we define a notion of folding (inspired by Stallings’ foldings) so that we are able to improve cores that do not meet our criteria.

In §
5
we take a core that decomposes into two good subcores, and prove that if this decomposition is optimal in the sense that there are no foldings, the big core is also good enough. We use local to global - the fact that no folding is possible gives us all the local data we need.

In §
6
we put the pieces together, to prove
Theorem

1.3
.

Once we finish with
Theorem

1.3
, we can follow the proof above and generalize it to hyperbolic groups.

The proof starts with the reduction that lets us assume
H_{i}
is not contained in a proper free factor of
H_{i+1}
. As seen in
Lemma

9.1
, this reduction is possible even in the case of hyperbolic groups. In
Lemma

9.2
, we go a step further and show that
H_{1}
is not contained in a proper free factor of
H_{i}
.

Arboreality attaches a graph to a subgroup. But given an inclusion
H_{1}<H_{2}
, how is the graph attached to
H_{1}
related to the graph attached to
H_{2}
? In the original proof, there is an inclusion of the invariant subtrees which induces a map between the quotients. In the hyperbolic case, we do not have this inclusion. In §
7
we use properties of hyperbolic spaces to build maps between cores. We cannot guarantee these maps are simplicial, but they are quasi isometries.

After constructing maps between cores (that are surjective and
\pi_{1}
-injective), the original proof uses the fact that these are simplicial maps between finite graphs to conclude the cores stabilize. For hyperbolic groups, we cannot do this. Instead, we use the fact that the maps are quasi-isometries, and that Cayley graph are locally finite. In §
8
, we prove
Theorem

1.4
. Then, in §
9
we finish everything by proving
Theorem

1.1

4
Folding cores

We move towards finding a proof of
Theorem

1.3
. We do this using foldings.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a connected metric graph.
A
simple folding of
\Gamma
along an edge
e
is a connected metric graph
\Gamma^{\prime}
and a homotopy equivalence
F:\Gamma\to\Gamma^{\prime}
which is one of the following:

1.

Replacing an edge:
\Gamma^{\prime}=(\Gamma-e)\cup e^{\prime}
(so that
\Gamma-e\cup e^{\prime}
is connected),

2.

Identifying a pair of vertices:
\Gamma^{\prime}
is obtained from
\Gamma-e
by identifying a pair of vertices, or

3.

Adding a balloon: if
e
is non-separating,
\Gamma^{\prime}
is obtained from
\Gamma-e
by attaching a balloon, i.e. an edge with a loop at its end.

In all cases
F
satisfies
F|_{\Gamma-e}
is the obvious inclusion (or quotient) map, and
e
is mapped linearly to a new path between the same endpoints.

A simple folding is a way of modifying the graph
\Gamma
by homotopy that changes only the edge
e
(and glues it in a new way). We want to expand this notion to cores. A core comes with a metric and a map from the universal cover to
X
. We want the folding to relate to this extra data in the same way as before - not changing it on
\Gamma-e
, but allowing some change on
e
.

Definition 4.2
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{C}=(\Gamma,\rho,\iota)
and
\mathfrak{C}^{\prime}=(\Gamma^{\prime},\rho^{\prime},\iota^{\prime})
are
H
-cores, and
F:\Gamma\to\Gamma^{\prime}
is a simple folding along an edge
e\in\Gamma
.
\mathfrak{C}^{\prime}
is an
improvement of
\Gamma
along
e
, if:

1.

F|_{\Gamma-e}
is length-preserving.

2.

For a suitable lift
\widetilde{F}:\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\prime}
of
F
, and
x\in\widetilde{\Gamma}-\pi_{\Gamma}^{-1}(e)
we have
\iota(x)=\iota^{\prime}(\widetilde{F}(x))
.

3.

\sigma(\mathfrak{C}^{\prime})<\sigma(\mathfrak{C})

The edge
e
is
minimal
if there are no improvements of
\mathfrak{C}
at
e
.
An edge
\widetilde{e}
of
\widetilde{\Gamma}
is
minimal
if
\pi(\widetilde{e})
is minimal.

The space
\widetilde{\Gamma}-\pi_{\Gamma}^{-1}(e)
is a union of trees that is mapped into
X
. the lifts of
\widetilde{e}
are connecting the components of
\widetilde{\Gamma}-\pi_{\Gamma}^{-1}(e)
. To find an improvement at
e
, we can look inside
X
and try to find other ways to connect the components of the image
\iota(\widetilde{\Gamma}-\pi_{\Gamma}^{-1}(e))
. Such a replacement should reduce the length of the quotient.

6
Uniform constants

We have seen in the previous section that if a core

-good.

is

-good.

We build

,

(K,C)
-arboreal, with based cores
\mathfrak{C}_{1}=(\Gamma_{1},\widetilde{*}_{1},\rho_{1},\iota_{1}),\mathfrak{C}_{2}=(\Gamma_{2},\widetilde{*}_{2},\rho_{2},\iota_{2})
.
Assume that every point in
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}
lies on a path between two points in the orbit of
\widetilde{*}_{1}
, i.e.
\text{Hull}(H_{1}.\widetilde{*}_{1})=\widetilde{\Gamma_{1}}
(otherwise, replace
\widetilde{\Gamma_{1}}
with the subtree
\text{Hull}(H_{1}.\widetilde{*}_{1})
).
We would like to construct a map
\psi:\Gamma_{1}\to\Gamma_{2}
.

Observation 7.1
.

There exists
D=D(K,C,\delta)
such that

\iota_{1}(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1})\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{D}(\iota_{2}(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{2}))
.

Proof.

The
\iota_{1}
-image of every vertex of
\widetilde{\Gamma_{1}}
is also in the image of
\iota_{2}
. Let
\widetilde{x}\in\widetilde{\Gamma_{1}}
be a point. Then
\widetilde{x}
lies in a path between two points in the orbit of
\widetilde{*}_{1}
, which we will denote by
h.\widetilde{*}_{1},g.\widetilde{*}_{1}
. This means that
\iota_{1}(\widetilde{x})
is in a
(K,C)
-quasi-geodesic between
h=\iota_{1}(h.\widetilde{*}_{1})
and
g=\iota_{1}(h.\widetilde{*}_{1})
.
The image
\iota_{2}(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{2})
also has a
(K,C)
-quasi-geodesic between
h
and
g
. By the Morse lemma, quasi-geodesics are close, which means there exists
D=D(K,C)-\delta
such that
x\in\mathcal{N}_{D}(\iota_{2}(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{2}))
.
∎

Lemma 7.2
.

There exist
K^{\prime},C^{\prime}
that depend only on
K,C,\delta
, and a continuous map

\psi:\Gamma_{1}\to\Gamma_{2}
which induces the inclusion of
H_{1}
into
H_{2}
in
\pi_{1}
, and whose lift to the universal covers

\widetilde{\psi}:\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}\to\widetilde{\Gamma}_{2}
is a
(K^{\prime},C^{\prime})
-q.i embedding.

Proof.

We will construct the map
\psi
in two steps:

Step 1.
Let us construct an
H_{1}
-equivariant
(K^{\prime}_{0},C^{\prime}_{0})
-quasi-isometric embedding
\widetilde{\psi}_{0}:\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}\to\widetilde{\Gamma}_{2}
.

Take a connected (strict) fundamental domain
\Omega
for the action of
H_{1}
on
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}
.
For
x\in\Omega
, define
\widetilde{\psi}_{0}(x)
to be a point
y
on
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{2}
such that
d_{X}(\iota_{1}(x),\iota_{2}(y))
is minimal (this distance is less than
D
by the result of
Observation

7.1
).
Any other point in
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}
is of the (unique) form
g.x
for some
x\in\Omega
, so define
\widetilde{\psi}_{0}(g.x)=g.\widetilde{\psi}_{0}(x)
.
\widetilde{\psi}_{0}
is well defined, and
H_{1}
-equivariant.
All that remains is to find constants and show that
\widetilde{\psi}_{0}
is a quasi-isometric embedding. Let
x,x^{\prime}\in\Omega
, denote
y=\widetilde{\psi}_{0}(x),y^{\prime}=\widetilde{\psi}_{0}(x^{\prime})
, and let
g,g^{\prime}\in H_{1}
.
We bound
d_{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{2}}(g.y,g^{\prime}.y^{\prime})
:

\displaystyle d_{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{2}}(g.y,g^{\prime}.y^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq K\cdot d_{X}(g.\iota_{2}(y),g^{\prime}.\iota_{2}(y^{\prime}))+KC

\displaystyle\leq K\cdot(d_{X}(g.\iota_{2}(y),g.\iota_{1}(x))+d_{X}(g.\iota_{1}(x),g^{\prime}.\iota_{1}(x^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\quad+d_{X}(g^{\prime}.\iota_{1}(x^{\prime}),g^{\prime}.\iota_{2}(y^{\prime})))+KC

\displaystyle\leq K\cdot d_{X}(g.\iota_{1}(x),g^{\prime}.\iota_{1}(x^{\prime}))+2KD+KC

\displaystyle\leq K^{2}d_{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}}(g.x,g^{\prime}.x^{\prime})+2KC+2KC

\displaystyle d_{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{2}}(g.y,g^{\prime}.y^{\prime})

\displaystyle\geq K^{-1}\cdot d_{X}(g.\iota_{2}(y),g^{\prime}.\iota_{2}(y^{\prime}))-K^{-1}C

\displaystyle\geq K^{-1}\cdot(d_{X}(g.\iota_{1}(x),g^{\prime}.\iota_{1}(x^{\prime}))-d_{X}(g.\iota_{1}(x),g.\iota_{2}(y))

\displaystyle\quad-d_{X}(g^{\prime}.\iota_{1}(x^{\prime}),g^{\prime}.\iota_{2}(y^{\prime})))-K^{-1}C

\displaystyle\geq K^{-1}\cdot d_{X}(g.\iota_{1}(x),g^{\prime}.\iota_{1}(x^{\prime}))-2K^{-1}D-K^{-1}C

\displaystyle\geq K^{-2}d_{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}}(g.x,g^{\prime}.x^{\prime})-2K^{-1}D-2K^{-1}C

Therefore,
\widetilde{\psi}_{0}
is a
(K^{\prime}_{0},C^{\prime}_{0})
-q.i. embedding for
K^{\prime}_{0}=K^{2},C^{\prime}_{0}=2KD+2KC
.

Step 2.
Let us use
\widetilde{\psi}_{0}
to construct an
H_{1}
-equivariant
(K^{\prime},C^{\prime})
-quasi-isometric embedding
\widetilde{\psi}:\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}\to\widetilde{\Gamma}_{2}
which is also continuous.

Subdivide
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}
, such that the new edges have length which is less than or equal to
1
(and do it in such that the action remains simplicial, by subdividing edges that are in the same orbit in the same way). Denote the set of vertices (after subdividing) by
V^{\prime}
, and the set of edges by
E^{\prime}
. For
v\in V^{\prime}
, define
\widetilde{\psi}(v)=\widetilde{\psi}_{0}(v)
. For an edge
e\in E^{\prime}
between vertices
v
and
v^{\prime}
, define
\widetilde{\psi}
such that it would map
e
to the geodesic between
\widetilde{\psi}(v)
and
\widetilde{\psi}(v^{\prime})
linearly. Each point on
e
is at distance at most
\frac{1}{2}
from one of
v
and
v^{\prime}
, and each point on
\widetilde{\psi}(e)
is at distance at most
\frac{K+C}{2}
from one of
\widetilde{\psi}(v),\widetilde{\psi}(v^{\prime})
(since the distance between
\widetilde{\psi}(v)
and
\widetilde{\psi}(v^{\prime})
is at most
K^{\prime}_{0}+C^{\prime}_{0}
). Let
x,y\in\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}
be such that the vertices closest to them are
v_{x},v_{y}
respectively. Then:

\displaystyle d_{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{2}}(\widetilde{\psi}(x),\widetilde{\psi}(y))

\displaystyle\leq d_{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{2}}(\widetilde{\psi}(v_{x}),\widetilde{\psi}(v_{y}))+2\cdot\frac{K^{\prime}_{0}+C^{\prime}_{0}}{2}

\displaystyle=d_{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{2}}(\widetilde{\psi}_{0}(v_{x}),\widetilde{\psi}_{0}(v_{y}))+K^{\prime}_{0}+C^{\prime}_{0}

\displaystyle\leq K^{\prime}_{0}\cdot d_{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}}(v_{x},v_{y})+K^{\prime}_{0}+2C^{\prime}_{0}

\displaystyle\leq K^{\prime}_{0}\cdot d_{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}}(x,y)+3K^{\prime}_{0}+2C^{\prime}_{0}

\displaystyle d_{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{2}}(\widetilde{\psi}(x),\widetilde{\psi}(y))

\displaystyle\geq d_{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{2}}(\widetilde{\psi}(v_{x}),\widetilde{\psi}(v_{y}))-2\cdot\frac{K^{\prime}_{0}+C^{\prime}_{0}}{2}

\displaystyle=d_{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{2}}(\widetilde{\psi}_{0}(v_{x}),\widetilde{\psi}_{0}(v_{y}))-K^{\prime}_{0}-C^{\prime}_{0}

\displaystyle\geq{K^{\prime}_{0}}^{-1}\cdot d_{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}}(v_{x},v_{y})-K^{\prime}_{0}-2C^{\prime}_{0}

\displaystyle\geq{K^{\prime}_{0}}^{-1}\cdot d_{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}}(x,y)-2{K^{\prime}_{0}}^{-1}-K^{\prime}_{0}-2C^{\prime}_{0}

\displaystyle\geq{K^{\prime}_{0}}^{-1}\cdot d_{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}}(x,y)-3K^{\prime}_{0}-2C^{\prime}_{0}

So, for
K^{\prime}=K^{\prime}_{0},C^{\prime}=3K^{\prime}_{0}+2C^{\prime}_{0}
,
\widetilde{\psi}
is a
(K^{\prime},C^{\prime})-
q.i. embedding.

The map
\widetilde{\psi}
is continuous, and it is
H_{1}
-equivariant, since
H_{1}\curvearrowright\widetilde{\Gamma}_{2}
by isometries (
\widetilde{\psi}(g.v)=g.\widetilde{\psi}(v)
for a vertex
v
, and it works for the rest of the points since the action sends geodesics to geodesics). Therefore, there is a well-defined map
\psi:\Gamma_{1}\to\Gamma_{2}
which induces the inclusion
H_{1}\hookrightarrow H_{2}
, and lifts to
\widetilde{\psi}
.
∎

8
Bounding cores

Let
A
be a finite subset of
G
. The displacement of
A
at
1
is defined by

\tau_{1}(A)=\sum_{a\in A}d_{X}(1,a).

Remark 8.1
.

Given a constant
\alpha>0
, the set
\{A\subset G|A\text{ is finite and }\tau_{1}(A)<\alpha\}
is finite.

Lemma 8.2
.

Let
G
be a hyperbolic group with a generating set
S
. Then given
\alpha,K,C>0
, there are finitely many
(K,C)
-arboreal subgroups with core
\mathfrak{C}
such that
\sigma(\mathfrak{C})\leq\alpha
.

Proof.

Let
H\leq G
be a
(K,C)
-arboreal subgroup of rank
r
with a based
H
-core
\mathfrak{C}=(\Gamma,\widetilde{*},\rho,\iota).

We can find a free generating set
A
for
H=\pi_{1}(\Gamma,*)
, in the following way:

choose a spanning tree
T
of
\Gamma
. Let
e
be an edge outside
T
between
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
. Denote by
a_{e}
the loop obtained by concatenating the path on
T
between
*=\pi_{\Gamma}(\widetilde{*})
and
v_{1}
,
e
, and the path on
T
between
v_{2}
and
*
.

Now, define
A=\{a_{e}|e
is an edge outside of
T\}

Each loop in
A
passes through each edge twice at most. Therefore, the length of each loop is less then
2\sigma(\mathfrak{C})
.

There is a bound on
\tau_{1}(A)
(in
G
):

\displaystyle\tau_{1}(A)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in A}d_{X}(1,a)

\displaystyle\leq K\cdot(\sum_{a\in A}d_{T}(\widetilde{*},a.\widetilde{*}))+rC

\displaystyle=K\cdot(\sum_{a\in A}\ell(a))+rC

\displaystyle\leq 2\cdot K\cdot r\cdot\sigma(\mathfrak{C})+rC

So, every
(K,C)
-arboreal subgroup with
\sigma(\mathfrak{C})\leq\alpha
has a generating set
A
with
\tau(A)\leq 2Kr\alpha+rC
. There are finitely finite sets with
\tau_{1}\leq 2Kr\alpha+rC
, and we are done.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

1.4
.

Let
H
be a quasiconvex free subgroup of
G
, and let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Without loss of generality assume that
\rk(H)\leq k
.
By
Theorem

1.3
, there exist
K,C
such that any free quasiconvex subgroup
H^{\prime}
of rank at most
k
is
(K,C)
-arboreal.
In particular
H
has a
(K,C)
-good core
\mathfrak{C}=(\Gamma,\rho,\iota)
.

Now, assume
H^{\prime}
is a free quasiconvex subgroup of rank at most
k
which contains
H
, and let

\mathfrak{C}^{\prime}=(\Gamma^{\prime},\rho^{\prime},\iota^{\prime})
denote its
(K,C)
-good core. By
Lemma

7.2
, there exists constants
K^{\prime},C^{\prime}
(which depend only on
K,C
) and a map
\psi:\Gamma\to\Gamma^{\prime}
, that in
\pi_{1}
induce the inclusion
H\leq H^{\prime}
, and its lift to the universal cover
\widetilde{\psi}
is a
(K^{\prime},C^{\prime})
-quasi-isometric embeddings.

If, in addition,
H
is not contained in a free factor of
H^{\prime}
then the map
\psi
is surjective.
For an edge
e
of
\Gamma
, we have
\ell(\psi(e))\leq K^{\prime}\cdot\ell(e)+C^{\prime}
. Since the images under
\psi
of edges in
\Gamma
cover
\Gamma^{\prime}
, this implies
\sigma(\mathfrak{C}^{\prime})\leq K^{\prime}\cdot\sigma(\mathfrak{C})+C^{\prime}
.

We found a bound on
\sigma(\mathfrak{C}^{\prime})
which depends only on
K,C
.
By
Lemma

8.2
, there are only finitely many such subgroups
H^{\prime}
.
∎

9
Ascending chains

We are now back to discussing ascending chains of subgroups, in an effort to prove
Theorem

1.1
.
In view of
Theorem

1.4
, we would like to assume that
H_{i-1}
is not contained in a proper free factor of
H_{i}
, and that
H_{1}
is not contained in a proper free factor of
H_{i}
. Bering and Lazarovich proved these assumptions are possible in
[
2
]
. In this section, we include the proofs for completeness.

Lemma 9.1
.

Let
G
be a group, and let
H_{1}\leq H_{2}\leq...\leq G
be an ascending chain of free subgroups of constant rank. Then there exists a chain
K_{1}\leq K_{2}\leq...\leq G
of free subgroups of constant rank, such that
K_{i}
is not contained in a proper free factor of
K_{i+1}
. Furthermore
{H_{i}}
stabilizes if and only if
{K_{i}}
stabilizes.

Proof.

By induction on the rank
r
of the groups
H_{i}
. For
r=1
, the groups
H_{i}
do not have proper free factor, and
K_{i}=H_{i}
suffices. Assume
r>1
. If there is a finite number of indices
i
such that
H_{i}
is contained in a proper free factor of
H_{i+1}
, then taking the tail of the sequence
{H_{i}}
we are done. Assume that the set of indices
I\subseteq\mathbb{N}
for which it happens is infinite. Then, for each
i\in I
,
H_{i}
is contained in some free factor
K_{i}
of
H_{i+1}
. By Grushko’s theorem,
\rk(K_{i})<\rk(H_{i+1})=r
. The sequence
\{K_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is ascending. After passing to a subsequence, we may assume
K_{i}
have constant rank
r^{\prime}<r
. Clearly
K_{i}
stabilizes if and only if
H_{i}
stabilizes, and since
r^{\prime}<r
, we can continue by induction.
∎

Lemma 9.2
.

Let
G
be a group, and let
H_{1}\leq H_{2}\leq...\leq G
be an ascending chain of free subgroups of constant rank, such that for all
i
,
H_{i}
is not contained in a proper free factor of
H_{i+1}
. Then for all
i
,
H_{1}
is not contained in a proper free factor of
H_{i}
.

Proof.

By induction on
i
. For
i=1,2
there is nothing to prove.
For
i>2
, suppose
K
is a proper free factor of
H_{i}
.
By the Kurosh decomposition,
K\cap H_{i-1}
is a free factor of
H_{i-1}
. And by the assumption
H_{i-1}\not\leq K
, so
K\cap H_{i-1}
is a proper free factor of
H_{i-1}
. By the induction hypothesis
H_{1}
is not contained in a proper free factor of
H_{i-1}
, and so it is not contained in
K\cap H_{i-1}
. It follows that
H_{1}
is not contained in
K
.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

Let
H_{1}\leq H_{2}\leq\dots
be an ascending sequence of quasiconvex free subgroups of rank
r
.
By
Lemma

9.1
we may assume that for all
i
,
H_{i}
is not contained in a proper free factor of
H_{i+1}
. By
Lemma

9.2
,
H_{1}
is not contained in a proper free factor of
H_{i}
.
By
Theorem

1.4
there are only finitely many such
H_{i}
, and so the sequence stabilizes.
∎

On homomorphisms and cv-polynomials between iterated Ore extensions Thanks: This work was supported by Faculty of Science, Universidad Nacional de Colombia - Sede Bogotá, Colombia [grant number 53880].
María Camila Ramírez, Armando Reyes

Abstract
. Motivated by the study of homomorphisms and cv-polynomials presented by Rimmer [ 42 ] in the case of Ore extensions of automorphism type, Ferrero and Kishimoto [ 15 ] and Kikumasa [ 22 ] in the setting of these extensions of derivation type, and Lam and Leroy [ 24 ] in the context of Ore extensions of mixed type over division rings, in this paper we investigate these both notions for iterations of these extensions. We show that the iteration of some of the results presented in those papers is non-trivial, and illustrate our treatment with several noncommutative algebras.

1.
Introduction

For
R
a commutative ring, Gilmer
[
17
]
determined all the automorphisms of the commutative polynomial ring
R[x]
which fix
R
elementwise. Parmenter
[
39
]
extended this result to the case where
R
is commutative and
\sigma
is any automorphism of
R
. The case where
\sigma
is the identity on any ring
R
was considered by Coleman and Enochs
[
13
]
and Brewer and Rutter
[
3
]
. These three results are corollaries of Rimmer’s paper
[
42
, Theorem 1]
, since for a unital ring (not necessarily commutative)
R
and
\sigma
any automorphism of
R
, he determined all the automorphisms of the Ore extension (introduced by Ore
[
37
,
38
]
) of automorphism type
R[x;\sigma]
that fix
R
elementwise (he called them
R
-linear maps). As an application of his results, he investigated isomorphisms between different skew polynomial rings which preserve an underlying ring isomorphism
[
42
, Theorem 3]
. On the other hand, Ferrero and Kishimoto
[
15
]
and Kikumasa
[
22
]
studied automorphisms of Ore extensions of derivation type
R[x;\delta]
. They discussed conditions on
f(x)\in R[x;\delta]
for the
R
-linear map
R[x;\delta]\to R[x;\delta]
defined by
x^{k}\mapsto f(x)^{k}
to be a
R
-ring automorphism.

Related with this topic, Lam and Leroy
[
24
]
studied “transformations” from one Ore extension of mixed type
D[x^{\prime};\sigma^{\prime},\delta^{\prime}]
to another extension
D[x;\sigma,\delta]
over a division ring
D
. They stated that a
D
-homomorphism
\phi
from
D[x^{\prime};\sigma^{\prime},\delta^{\prime}]
to
D[x;\sigma,\delta]
is determined by
\phi(x^{\prime}):=p(x)\in D[x;\sigma,\delta]
. The choice of
p(x)
must satisfy the condition
p(x)a=\sigma^{\prime}(a)p(x)+\delta^{\prime}(a)
, for every
a\in D
. Since the polynomial
p(x)
allows us to make a “change of variables” (from
x^{\prime}
to
x
), they called
p(x)
a
change-of-variable polynomial
(or
cv-polynomial
for short) respect to the quasi-derivation
(\sigma^{\prime},\delta^{\prime})
on
D
. In their paper, they proved different results about these polynomials and their corresponding morphisms. For instance, they showed that if the cv-polynomial is non-constant, then it can be characterized with an appropriate quasi-derivation
(\sigma^{\prime},\delta^{\prime})
on
D

[
24
, Theorem 2.12]
. Another important result establishes when two Ore extensions are isomorphic under certain conditions
[
24
, Theorem 5.6]
. Several researchers have investigated the topic of morphisms of Ore extensions in one indeterminate (e.g.
[
2
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
27
,
28
,
29
,
30
,
32
,
33
]
, and references therein).

Motivated by the treatments described above, in this paper we explore the notions of homomorphism and cv-polynomial of iterated Ore extensions. We show that the iteration of some of their results is non-trivial, in fact, in some cases these results do not hold, and exemplify our results with several families of noncommutative algebras.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we start by recalling some preliminaries and examples of iterated Ore extensions that are necessary throughout the paper. Next, Section
3
develop the theory of homomorphisms and cv-polynomials of two-step iterated Ore extensions. We introduce our proposal of cv-polynomial in Definition
3.3
that extends the corresponding in Lam and Leroy’s paper
[
24
]
. Example
3.7
shows that
[
24
, Theorem 2.12]
is false unless we impose a additional conditions. Our extended version of this result is formulated in Theorem
3.8
. We also formulate Theorems
3.12
and
3.13
that characterize quasi-derivations depending of the cv-polynomials. Then, Theorems
3.15
and
3.19
say how are the isomorphisms in the sense of these polynomials. We illustrate our results with Jordan plane
\mathcal{J}
(Theorem
3.16
) and its quantization (Theorem
3.18
). Section
4
presents the study of homomorphisms and cv-polynomials of three-step iterated Ore extensions by considering the approach made in the previous section. As in Section
3
, we find that the quasi-derivation
(\sigma^{\prime}_{i},\delta^{\prime}_{i})
depends only on the choice of the cv-polynomial
p_{i}
(Theorems
4.3
,
4.4
, and
4.5
). Section
5
contains general ideas for the study of morphisms and cv-polynomials in the case of
n
-step iterated Ore extensions. Finally, in Section
6
we formulate some ideas for a possible future work concerning morphisms between noncommutative rings related with Ore extensions.

Throughout the paper, the symbol
R
means an associative (not necessarily commutative) ring with identity.
R^{*}
denotes the set of units of
R
, while the symbols
D
and
\Bbbk
denote a division ring and a field, respectively.
M_{n}(R)

n\times n
with entries in the ring
R
. The term homomorphism will mean unital ring homomorphism.

2.
Preliminaries

We start by considering some preliminaries about iterated Ore extensions which are necessary throughout the paper.

Recall that if
\sigma:R\rightarrow R
is an endomorphism of the ring
R
, an additive map
\delta:R\rightarrow R
is called a
\sigma
-derivation of
R
if
\delta(rs)=\sigma(r)\delta(s)+\delta(r)s
, for all
r,s\in R
(strictly speaking, this is the definition of
left

\sigma
-
derivation
, but we will not need the concept of
right

\sigma
-derivation, which is any additive map
\delta:R\to R
satisfying the rule
\delta(rs)=\delta(r)\sigma(s)+r\delta(s)
). Notice that
\delta(1)=\delta(1\cdot 1)=\sigma(1)\delta(1)+\delta(1)1=2\delta(1)
, whence
\delta(1)=0
. The pair
(\sigma,\delta)
is called a
quasi-derivation on

R

[
5
, Definition 3.1]
.

Following Ore
[
37
,
38
]
(see also
[
19
, p. 36]
),
S:=R[x;\sigma,\delta]
is said to be an
Ore extension
of
R
or a
skew polynomial ring of

R
, and it consists of left polynomials
\sum_{i=0}^{n}r_{i}x^{i}
,
r_{i}\in R\ (x^{0}:=1)
, which are added in the usual way and multiplied according to the rule
xr=\sigma(r)x+\delta(r)
, for all
r\in R
. For any non-zero element
f=\sum_{i=0}^{n}r_{i}x^{i}\in R[x;\sigma,\delta]
with
r_{n}\neq 0
, the non-negative integer
n
is called the
degree
of
f
, abbreviated
{\rm deg}(f)
, and the element
r_{n}
is called the
leading coefficient
of
f
. The zero element of
R[x;\sigma,\delta]
is defined to have degree
-\infty
and leading coefficient
0
. If
\delta:=0
, then the expression
R[x;\sigma]
denotes the
Ore extension of endomorphism type
, and when
\sigma
is the identity on
R
,
R[x;\delta]
denotes the
Ore extension of derivation type
. Since its introduction, ring-theoretical, homological, and geometrical properties of Ore extensions have been investigated extensively in the literature (e.g.,
[
4
,
5
,
12
,
14
,
18
,
19
,
34
,
35
,
36
,
40
,
41
]
, and references therein).

The construction of Ore extensions can be iterated. Indeed, if we consider
S_{1}=R[x_{1};\sigma_{1},\delta_{1}]
for a quasi-derivation
(\sigma_{1},\delta_{1})
on
R
, and we assume that
(\sigma_{2},\delta_{2})
is a quasi-derivation on
S_{1}
, then one may define the two-step iterated Ore extension

S_{2}=S_{1}[x_{2};\sigma_{2},\delta_{2}]=R[x_{1};\sigma_{1},\delta_{1}][x_{2};\sigma_{2},\delta_{2}],

where the relations
x_{i}r=\sigma_{i}(r)x_{i}+\delta_{i}(r),\ i=1,2
, and
x_{2}x_{1}=\sigma_{2}(x_{1})x_{2}+\delta_{2}(x_{1})
hold. Similarly, if we assume that
(\sigma_{3},\delta_{3})
is a quasi-derivation on
S_{2}
, then one can define the three-step iterated Ore extension

S_{3}=S_{2}[x_{3};\sigma_{3},\delta_{3}]=S_{1}[x_{2};\sigma_{2},\delta_{2}][x_{3};\sigma_{3},\delta_{3}]=R[x_{1};\sigma_{1},\delta_{1}][x_{2};\sigma_{2},\delta_{2}][x_{3};\sigma_{3},\delta_{3}],

under relations
x_{i}r=\sigma_{i}(r)x_{i}+\delta_{i}(r),\ i=1,2,3
, and
x_{j}x_{i}=\sigma_{j}(x_{i})x_{j}+\delta_{j}(x_{i}),\ 1\leq i<j\leq 3
hold. Repeating this procedure, one obtains the so-called
iterated Ore extensions
.

Next, we present some interesting noncommutative algebras that can be expressed as iterated Ore extensions which will be taken up later to illustrate our results.

Example 2.1
.

(i)

The
Jordan plane

\mathcal{J}(\Bbbk)
introduced by Jordan
[
21
]
is the free
\Bbbk
-algebra defined by
\mathcal{J}:=\Bbbk\{x,y\}/\langle yx-xy-y^{2}\rangle
. An easy computation shows that
\mathcal{J}(\Bbbk)
can be expressed as
\Bbbk[y][x;\delta_{2}]
with
\delta_{2}(y)=-y^{2}
(c.f.
[
43
, Section 1]
).

(ii)

Following
[
31
, Definition 7.41]
, for an element
q\in\Bbbk^{*}
, the
q
-
skew Jordan plane

\mathcal{J}_{q}(\Bbbk)
is defined as the free algebra
\Bbbk\{x,y\}/\langle qyx-xy-y^{2}\rangle
. This algebra can be written as the Ore extension
\Bbbk[y][x;\sigma_{2},\delta_{2}]
with
\sigma_{2}(y)=qy
and
\delta_{2}(y)=-y^{2}
.

(iii)

Let
p\in\Bbbk^{*}
. The
single parameter quantum matrix algebra

O_{p}(M_{n}(\Bbbk))
has generating basis the set of indeterminates
\{x_{ij}\},\ 1\leq i,j\leq n
, subject to the relations given by

x_{ij}x_{lm}=\begin{cases}px_{lm}x_{ij},&i>l,j=m,\\
px_{lm}x_{ij},&i=l,j>m,\\
x_{lm}x_{ij},&i>l,j<m,\\
x_{lm}x_{ij}+(p-p^{-1})x_{im}x_{lj},&i>l,j>m.\end{cases}

It can be seen that
O_{p}(M_{2}(\Bbbk))\cong\Bbbk[x_{11}][x_{12};\sigma_{12}][x_{21};\sigma_{21}][x_{22};\sigma_{22},\delta_{22}]
, with the maps given by

\displaystyle\sigma_{12}(x_{11})

\displaystyle=px_{11},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \sigma_{21}(x_{11})=px_{11},

\displaystyle\sigma_{22}(x_{21})

\displaystyle=px_{21},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \sigma_{22}(x_{12})=px_{12},

\displaystyle\delta_{22}(x_{11})

\displaystyle=(p-p^{-1})x_{21}x_{12}.

Otherwise, the value of
\sigma_{ij}
is the identity map, and for
1\leq i,j\leq 2
, the maps
\delta_{ij}
are zero
[
19
, Exercise 2V]
.

For a ring
R
,
\sigma
an endomorphism of
R
and an element
a\in R
, it is easy to see that the rule
\delta_{a,\sigma}(r):=ar-\sigma(r)a
, for every
r\in R
, defines a
\sigma
-derivation of
R
, and hence
(\sigma,\delta_{a,\sigma})
is a quasi-derivation on
R
.
\delta_{a,\sigma}
is called a
\sigma
-
inner derivation by

a
. Any derivation on
R
which is not an inner derivation is called an
outer derivation

[
19
, p. 31]
.

A polynomial
f(x)\in R[x;\sigma,\delta]
is said to be
right invariant
if
f(x)R[x;\sigma,\delta]\subseteq R[x;\sigma,\delta]f(x)
, and
right semi-invariant
if
f(x)R\subseteq Rf(x)

[
24
, p. 84]
. Lemonnier’s Theorem states that the Ore extension
\Bbbk[x;\sigma,\delta]
has a non-constant semi-invariant polynomial if and only if the
\sigma
-derivation
\delta
is quasi-algebraic, which means that
\delta
satisfies one of the following equivalent conditions: there exist constants
b_{i}\in\Bbbk
, with
b_{n}=1
, such that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}\delta^{i}
is a
\sigma^{n}
-inner derivation. (2) There exist constants
b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}
, not all zero, such that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}\delta^{i}
is a
\sigma^{\prime}
-inner derivation for some endomorphism
\sigma^{\prime}\in\Bbbk

[
24
, Theorem 4.7]
.

3.
Two-step iterated Ore extensions

We start with Definition
3.1
which is a direct extension of
[
24
, Definition 2.4]
. As we will see, the ring of coefficients
R[x_{1};\sigma_{1},\delta_{1}]
is the same for both extensions, and the cv-polynomial only acts on the second indeterminate. As expected, in this situation we get similar results to those obtained by Lam and Leroy
[
24
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
S^{\prime}=R[x_{1};\sigma_{1},\delta_{1}][x^{\prime}_{2};\sigma^{\prime}_{2},\delta^{\prime}_{2}]
and
S=R[x_{1};\sigma_{1},\delta_{1}][x_{2};\sigma_{2},\delta_{2}]
be two iterated Ore extensions. Consider the homomorphism
\phi:S^{\prime}\to S
given by
\phi(x_{1})=x_{1}
and
\phi(x^{\prime}_{2}):=p(x_{2})\in S
, with
p(x_{2})
satisfying

\phi(x^{\prime}_{2}x_{1})=\sigma^{\prime}_{2}(x_{1})p(x_{2})+\delta^{\prime}_{2}(x_{1})\quad{\rm and}\quad\phi(x^{\prime}_{2}x_{1})=p(x_{2})x_{1},

i.e.,

(3.1)

p(x_{2})x_{1}=\sigma^{\prime}_{2}(x_{1})p(x_{2})+\delta^{\prime}_{2}(x_{1}).

Besides,

x_{1}r=\sigma_{1}(r)x_{1}+\delta_{1}(r),\quad{\rm and}\quad p(x_{2})r=\sigma^{\prime}_{2}(r)p(x_{2})+\delta^{\prime}_{2}(r).

We say that
p(x_{2})
is a
cv-polynomial for two-step iterated Ore extensions
respect to the quasi-derivation
(\sigma^{\prime}_{2},\delta^{\prime}_{2})
on
R[x_{1};\sigma_{1},\delta_{1}]
.

Conversely, if a polynomial
p(x_{2})\in S
satisfies (
3.1
) for a quasi-derivation
(\sigma^{\prime}_{2},\delta^{\prime}_{2})
on
R[x_{1};\sigma_{1},\delta_{1}]
, then we define the homomorphism
\phi:S^{\prime}\to S
by
\phi(x^{\prime}_{2})=p(x_{2})
.

Let us see some easy examples that illustrate Definition
3.1
. Before, recall that for
u\in R^{*}
,
\sigma_{u}
denotes the
inner automorphism of

R

associated with

u
defined by
\sigma_{u}(r)=uru^{-1}
, for all
r\in R
.

Example 3.2
.

Let
S^{\prime}=R[x_{1};\sigma_{1},\delta_{1}][x^{\prime}_{2};\sigma^{\prime}_{2},\delta^{\prime}_{2}]
and
S=R[x_{1};\sigma_{1},\delta_{1}][x_{2};\sigma_{2},\delta_{2}]
as in Definition
3.1
.

(i)

If
p(x_{2}):=c\in R
, then
p(x_{2})x_{1}=cx_{1}
and
p(x_{2})r=cr=\sigma^{\prime}_{2}(r)c+\delta^{\prime}_{2}(r)
, whence if we take
\delta^{\prime}_{2}:=\delta_{2(c,\sigma^{\prime}_{2})}(r)=cr-\sigma^{\prime}_{2}(r)c
, it follows that
p(x_{2})
is a cv-polynomial.

(ii)

Let
p(x_{2})=ax_{2}+b\in S
. Then

p(x_{2})x_{1}=(ax_{2}+b)x_{1}=ax_{2}x_{1}+bx_{1}=a\sigma_{2}(x_{1})x_{2}+a\delta_{2}(x_{1})+bx_{1},

and

\sigma^{\prime}_{2}(x_{1})(ax_{2}+b)+\delta^{\prime}_{2}(x_{1})=\sigma^{\prime}_{2}(x_{1})(ax_{2})+\sigma^{\prime}_{2}(x_{1})b+\delta^{\prime}_{2}(x_{1}).

If
\sigma^{\prime}_{2}(x_{1})=\sigma^{\prime}_{2(a,\sigma_{2})}(x_{1}):=(\sigma_{a}\circ\sigma_{2})(x_{1})=a\sigma_{2}(x_{1})a^{-1}
and
\delta^{\prime}_{2}=a\delta_{2}+\delta_{2(b,\sigma^{\prime}_{2})}
, an easy computation shows that
p(x_{2})
is a cv-polynomial.

(iii)

Let
p(x_{2})=x_{2}^{2}+c\in S
. Suppose that
\sigma^{\prime}_{2}=\sigma_{2}^{2}
,
\delta^{\prime}_{2}=\delta_{2}^{2}+\delta_{2(c,\sigma^{\prime}_{2})}
, and
\sigma_{2}\delta_{2}=-\delta_{2}\sigma_{2}
. Since

\displaystyle p(x_{2})x_{1}

\displaystyle=(x_{2}^{2}+c)x_{1}=x_{2}^{2}x_{1}+cx_{1}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{2}^{2}(x_{1})x_{2}^{2}+\delta_{2}\sigma_{2}(x_{1})x_{2}+\sigma_{2}\delta_{2}(x_{1})x_{2}+\delta_{2}^{2}(x_{1})+cx_{1}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{2}^{2}(x_{1})x_{2}^{2}+\delta_{2}^{2}(x_{1})+cx_{1},

and

\displaystyle\sigma^{\prime}_{2}(x_{1})p(x_{2})+\delta^{\prime}_{2}(x_{1})

\displaystyle=\sigma^{\prime}_{2}(x_{1})(x^{2}_{2}+c)+\delta^{\prime}_{2}(x_{1})=\sigma^{\prime}_{2}(x_{1})x^{2}_{2}+\sigma^{\prime}_{2}(x_{1})c+\delta^{\prime}_{2}(x_{1})

\displaystyle=\sigma^{2}_{2}(x_{1})x^{2}_{2}+\sigma^{2}_{2}(x_{1})c+\delta_{2}^{2}(x_{1})+cx_{1}-\sigma^{2}_{2}(x_{1})c

\displaystyle=\sigma^{2}_{2}(x_{1})x_{2}^{2}+\delta_{2}^{2}(x_{1})+cx_{1},

then
\phi(x^{\prime}_{2}x_{1})=p(x_{2})x_{1}=\sigma^{\prime}_{2}(x_{1})p(x_{2})+\delta^{\prime}_{2}(x_{1})
, that is,
p(x_{2})
is a cv-polynomial respect to the quasi-derivation
\left(\sigma_{2}^{2},\delta_{2}^{2}+\delta_{2(c,\sigma^{\prime}_{2})}\right)
on
R[x_{1};\sigma_{1},\delta_{1}]
.

Now, we introduce the following definition of cv-polynomial more general than Definition
3.1
.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
S^{\prime}=R[x^{\prime}_{1};\sigma^{\prime}_{1},\delta^{\prime}_{1}][x^{\prime}_{2};\sigma^{\prime}_{2},\delta^{\prime}_{2}]
and
S=R[x_{1};\sigma_{1},\delta_{1}][x_{2};\sigma_{2},\delta_{2}]
. If we consider the homomorphism
\phi:S^{\prime}\to S
given by
\phi(x^{\prime}_{1}):=p_{1}(x_{1})=p_{1}\in R[x_{1};\sigma_{1},\delta_{1}]
and
\phi(x^{\prime}_{2}):=p_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})=p_{2}\in S
, satisfying the relations

\displaystyle\phi(x^{\prime}_{2}x^{\prime}_{1})=

\displaystyle\ \sigma^{\prime}_{2}(\phi(x^{\prime}_{1}))p_{2}+\delta^{\prime}_{2}(\phi(x^{\prime}_{1}))=p_{2}p_{1},

\displaystyle\phi(x^{\prime}_{1}r)=

\displaystyle\ \sigma^{\prime}_{1}(r)p_{1}+\delta^{\prime}_{1}(r)=p_{1}r,\quad{\rm for\ all}\ r\in R

\displaystyle\phi(x^{\prime}_{2}r)=

\displaystyle\ \sigma^{\prime}_{2}(r)p_{2}+\delta^{\prime}_{2}(r)=p_{2}r,\quad{\rm for\ all}\ r\in R,

or equivalently,

(3.2)

\displaystyle p_{2}p_{1}=

\displaystyle\ \sigma^{\prime}_{2}(p_{1})p_{2}+\delta^{\prime}_{2}(p_{1}),

(3.3)

\displaystyle p_{1}r=

\displaystyle\ \sigma^{\prime}_{1}(r)p_{1}+\delta^{\prime}_{1}(r),

(3.4)

\displaystyle p_{2}r=

\displaystyle\ \sigma^{\prime}_{2}(r)p_{2}+\delta^{\prime}_{2}(r),

then we say that the polynomials
p_{i}
’s
(i=1,2)
are the
cv-polynomials respect to the quasi-derivations

(\sigma^{\prime}_{i},\delta^{\prime}_{i})
’s,
i=1,2
, on
R
and
R[x_{1}^{\prime};\sigma_{1}^{\prime},\delta_{1}^{\prime}]
, respectively.

Conversely, if two polynomials
p_{1}\in R[x_{1};\sigma_{1},\delta_{1}]
and
p_{2}\in S
satisfy conditions (
3.2
) - (
3.4
), where
(\sigma_{1}^{\prime},\delta_{1}^{\prime})
and
(\sigma_{2}^{\prime},\delta_{2}^{\prime})
are quasi-derivations on
R
and
R[x_{1}^{\prime};\sigma_{1}^{\prime},\delta_{1}^{\prime}]
, respectively, then we define the homomorphism
\phi:S^{\prime}\to S
by
\phi(x^{\prime}_{i})=p_{i}
(
i=1,2)
.

The next example shows that constant polynomials are cv-polynomials (c.f. Example
3.2
(i)).

Example 3.4
.

Consider the Ore extensions
S^{\prime}
and
S
as in Definition
3.3
. Let
p_{1}:=c
and
p_{2}:=d
be elements of
R
. With the aim of showing that conditions (
3.2
) - (
3.4
) hold, take the inner derivation
\delta^{\prime}_{2}(c):=\delta_{2(d,\sigma^{\prime}_{2})}(c)=dc-\sigma^{\prime}_{2}(c)d
. Then

\phi(x^{\prime}_{2}x^{\prime}_{1})=p_{2}p_{1}=dc\quad{\rm and}\quad\sigma^{\prime}_{2}(c)d+\delta^{\prime}_{2}(c)=\sigma^{\prime}_{2}(c)d+dc-\sigma^{\prime}_{2}(c)d=dc.

In addition, for any
r\in R
and the inner derivation given by
\delta^{\prime}_{1}=\delta_{1(c,\sigma^{\prime}_{1})}
, it is clear that
\phi(x^{\prime}_{1}r)=p_{1}r=cr
. Also, note that
\sigma^{\prime}_{1}(r)c+\delta^{\prime}_{1}(r)=\sigma^{\prime}_{1}(r)c+cr-\sigma^{\prime}_{1}(r)c=cr
. In the same way,
\phi(x^{\prime}_{2}r)=p_{2}r=\sigma^{\prime}_{2}(r)p_{2}+\delta^{\prime}_{2}(r)=dr
. These facts show that any pair of constant polynomials in
S
correspond to a morphism in the sense of cv-polynomials. Finally, note that the values of the endomorphisms
\sigma^{\prime}_{i}
’s are arbitrary, while the corresponding
\delta^{\prime}_{i}
’s are determined uniquely by the values of
\sigma^{\prime}_{i}
’s and the constant values associated with the polynomials
p_{i}
’s.

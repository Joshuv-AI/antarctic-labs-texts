Antenna subtraction for jet production observables in full colour at NNLO Preprint: ZU-TH 27/22 KA-TP-24-2022 IPPP/22/60 P3H-22-087
X. Chen a,b , T. Gehrmann c , E.W.N. Glover d , J. Mo c

Abstract
We describe the details of the calculation of the full colour NNLO QCD corrections to jet production observables at the LHC with antenna subtraction. All relevant matrix elements for the process p ​ p → j ​ j pp\to jj at NNLO in full colour are colour-decomposed and given in a N c N_{c} and n f n_{f} expansion, making identification of leading and subleading colour contributions transparent. The colour-ordered antenna subtraction method has previously successfully been used to construct the NNLO subtraction terms for processes with up to five partons or in the leading colour approximation. However, it is challenged by the more involved subleading colour structure of the squared matrix elements in processes with six or more partons. Here, we describe the methods needed to successfully construct the NNLO subtraction terms for the subleading colour contributions to dijet production within the antenna subtraction formalism.

Introduction

Jet production observables at hadron colliders are among the most fundamental probes of quantum chromodynamics (QCD). High precision data from the Tevatron and the LHC provide important constraints on the parton content of the colliding hadrons and enable accurate determinations of the strong coupling constant. To interpret these data in precision studies of QCD requires theory predictions at a level of accuracy that is comparable to the quality of the experimental data. To attain this accuracy in the theory predictions requires the computation of higher-order perturbative corrections.

The most basic jet production processes are initiated at Born level by
2\to 2
parton scattering, with single jet inclusive and dijet observables both deriving from the same Born-level reaction. For these hadron collider jet production processes, the established next-to-leading order (NLO) QCD
predictions
Ellis:1992en
;
Giele:1994gf
;
Giele:1994xd
;
Nagy:2001fj
;
Gao:2012he
, supplemented with NLO electroweak
corrections
Dittmaier:2012kx
;
Campbell:2016dks
;
Frederix:2016ost
and parton shower based resummation
Alioli:2010xa
;
Hoeche:2012fm
, have long been the baseline of comparisons between experiment and theory. With residual theory uncertainties of around ten percent at NLO QCD, these predictions were however no longer sufficient with the advent of high precision jet production data from the LHC
Gehrmann:2021qex
.

Higher-order QCD calculations of jet observables require the handling of infrared singular configurations that arise from real and virtual corrections to the underlying Born-level process, and that cancel each other only once all corrections at a given order are added together. This cancellation of infrared divergences among different subprocess contributions is usually accomplished by a subtraction method. Several subtraction methods have been developed
Catani:2007vq
;
Gehrmann-DeRidder:2005btv
;
Currie:2013vh
;
Czakon:2010td
;
Boughezal:2011jf
;
Gaunt:2015pea
;
DelDuca:2016ily
;
Caola:2017dug
for next-to-next-to-leading order (NNLO) QCD calculations, and have been applied to a substantial number of hadron collider processes
Heinrich:2020ybq
in calculations performed on a process-by-process basis with up to now only limited automation.

NNLO QCD corrections to jet production observables were initially computed
Currie:2016bfm
;
Currie:2017eqf
;
Gehrmann-DeRidder:2019ibf
using the antenna subtraction method
Gehrmann-DeRidder:2005btv
;
Currie:2013vh
;
Daleo:2006xa
, thereby retaining only the leading terms in an expansion in the number of colours and quark flavours in QCD. Since the antenna subtraction method is based on the colour-ordered decomposition of QCD amplitudes, it was particularly well-adapted to extract these leading-colour contributions. Subsequently, full-colour NNLO QCD results
Czakon:2019tmo
for jet production processes were obtained using a residue-subtraction scheme
Czakon:2010td
. Most recently, we completed the full-colour NNLO calculation of jet production processes
Chen:2022tpk
using the antenna subtraction method. In this paper, we would like to complement our numerical results and phenomenological interpretations that we presented in
Chen:2022tpk
by a description of the technical details of their calculation.

The implementation of the antenna subtraction method is achieved on a process-by-process basis using the colour-ordered antenna approach. In this approach, single and double real emission patterns in colour-ordered matrix elements are identified and used to guide the construction of the subtraction terms. The subtraction for the double real emission contribution is usually constructed first and subsequently integrated with additional terms to form the real-virtual part, and finally the double virtual part. For low multiplicity processes or in leading colour approximations, this approach usually results in relatively compact expressions for the subtraction terms. However, the complexity of the implementation of the colour-ordered antenna subtraction method scales with the number of coloured particles involved, which for the process considered in this paper,
pp\to jj
, reaches up to six at NNLO. Going beyond the leading colour approximation and including all subleading colour contributions also comes with new complications. In this paper, we discuss these complications and the successful implementation of the colour-ordered antenna subtraction method for the full colour dijet production, thus enabling full colour NNLO QCD predictions for jet production observables at the LHC
Chen:2022tpk
.

2
Relevant matrix elements

In this following, we discuss all the relevant matrix elements for the NNLO QCD corrections to dijet production at the LHC, aiming to establish the notation and to
outline the colour-connections at all colour levels, which will subsequently become relevant for the construction of the antenna subtraction terms.

At leading order (LO), the basic parton-level dijet production processes are given by all crossings of the two-to-two parton processes:
gg\to gg
,
qg\to qg
and
qq^{\prime}\to qq^{\prime}
, where
q
and
q^{\prime}
denote quarks of identical or different flavour. The cross section at NNLO is then assembled by combining these partonic subprocess contributions, integrated over their respective phase spaces, with all terms that result from the mass factorization (MF) of the parton distributions at NNLO. The NNLO QCD corrections can be divided into three types according to the number of loops and external legs of their Feynman diagrams:

•

RR: the real-real or double real corrections, which consist of the tree-level diagrams with six external partons.

•

RV: the real-virtual corrections, which consist of the one-loop diagrams with five external partons.

•

VV: the virtual-virtual or double virtual corrections, which consist of the two-loop corrections to the four parton processes.

The NNLO contribution to the cross section thus reads:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\text{NNLO}}=\int_{\Phi_{n+2}}\mathrm{d}\sigma^{RR}+\int_{\Phi_{n+1}}\left(\mathrm{d}\sigma^{RV}+\mathrm{d}\sigma^{\text{MF},1}\right)+\int_{\Phi_{n}}\left(\mathrm{d}\sigma^{VV}+\mathrm{d}\sigma^{\text{MF},2}\right),

(1)

where the
\Phi_{n}
denote the
2\to(n-2)
-parton phase space integrations, and
n=4
is the parton multiplicity of the underlying Born-level process. The
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{RR,RV,VV,\text{MF}}
contain the projection of all subprocesses onto

jj
final states, involving the jet reconstruction algorithm and the application of kinematical cuts.

The phase space integrals of different multiplicities in (
1
) cannot be computed in a direct manner, since each contribution on the right-hand side contains infrared singularities from real or virtual unresolved parton exchanges, which only cancel among each other once all contributions are added together. The singular real-radiation behaviour of the RR and RV contributions must be subtracted prior to integration, and the explicit infrared poles must be separated in the RV and VV contributions. We perform the cancellation of infrared-divergent terms among the different contributions using the antenna subtraction method
Gehrmann-DeRidder:2005btv
;
Daleo:2006xa
;
Currie:2013vh
, which introduces subtraction terms at the RR, RV and VV levels, resulting in:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\text{NNLO}}=\int_{\Phi_{n+2}}\left(\mathrm{d}\sigma^{RR}-\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S}\right)+\int_{\Phi_{n+1}}\left(\mathrm{d}\sigma^{RV}-\mathrm{d}\sigma^{T}\right)+\int_{\Phi_{n}}\left(\mathrm{d}\sigma^{VV}-\mathrm{d}\sigma^{U}\right),

(2)

where each bracketed term on the right hand side is now free of infrared singularities and can be integrated numerically. They are implemented into the parton-level event generator NNLO

JET

, which enables to compute any infrared-safe observable related to
pp\to jj
, such as multi-differential single-jet inclusive or dijet cross sections. Our numerical results for several LHC jet production observables are reported in a companion paper
Chen:2022tpk
.

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,1}+\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,2},

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\sigma^{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\sigma^{V,S}-\int_{1}\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,1}-\mathrm{d}\sigma^{\text{MF},1},

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\sigma^{U}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{1}{\rm d}\sigma^{V,S}-\int_{2}\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,2}-\mathrm{d}\sigma^{\text{MF},2}.

(3)

In here,

(n-1)
and
(n-2)
parton final state respectively, and
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{V,S}

\mathrm{d}\sigma^{RV}

The construction of the antenna subtraction terms will be described in detail in Sections
3

pp\to jj
at full colour. These considerations will in particular be important in view of a future automated generation

As we sum over all possible allowed parton identities, we have to classify the processes contributing at NNLO according to their parton content. We define the following types of (squared) matrix elements:

•

A-type matrix elements: processes for which all the external partons are gluons.

•

B-type matrix elements: processes which contain only one quark pair, plus any additional number of gluons.

•

C-type matrix elements: processes which contain two quark pairs of different flavour, plus any additional number of gluons.

•

D-type matrix elements: processes which contain two quark pairs of identical flavour, plus any additional number of gluons.

•

E-type matrix elements: processes which contain three quark pairs, all of different flavour.

•

F-type matrix elements: processes which contain two quark pairs of the same flavour and a third quark pair of different flavour.

•

G-type matrix elements: processes which contain three quark pairs, all of the same flavour.

The distinction between the identical-flavour and different-flavour multi-quark processes is such that the identical flavour processes contain only those contributions that appear anew (interference terms from interchanging external quark states that are admitted only for identical flavours) if quark flavours are set equal. For example, non-identical flavour quark-quark scattering at leading order receives contributions from C-type squared matrix elements, while identical flavour quark-quark scattering receives contributions from both C-type and D-type squared matrix elements.

The double real corrections to dijet production contain six external partons, so they can be of any A-, B-, C-, D-, E-, F- and G-type, while the real-virtual and double virtual corrections contain only five and four partons respectively, meaning they can only be of A-, B-, C- or D-type. We use the notation
\mathcal{M}_{n}^{l}(\{p\})
with
\mathcal{M}\in\{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D},\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}\}
for a generic amplitude containing
n
external gluons and
l
loops depending on the external parton momenta
\{p\}
. The helicity labels of the external partons are suppressed and when considering squared quantities
M_{n}^{l}(\{p\})
we always assume a sum over the helicity configurations. Squared matrix elements are denoted by capital italic letters, which are put in boldface when a summation over momentum
permutations is involved.

In the following sections we define all the relevant NNLO matrix elements according to this convention. In view of the antenna subtraction which will be applied separately for each matrix element type per colour level, all the matrix elements are broken down in a tower of colour levels, each having their own powers of the colour factors
N_{c}
and
n_{f}
. Each colour level may be further sub-divided in functions which display some desired behaviour, i.e. functions which have clear colour connections. Besides all the matrix elements which form the NNLO corrections to dijet production, we also look at several lower multiplicity matrix elements. These matrix elements are part of the NLO corrections to dijet production or can appear as reduced matrix elements in the NNLO subtraction. We list them along with several of their properties, such as their pole structures.

2.2
B-type matrix elements

The B-type matrix elements are matrix elements containing one quark pair plus any number of gluons. Thus,
\mathcal{B}_{n}^{l}
denotes a B-type amplitude containing one quark pair,
n
gluons and
l
loops. For the functions defined in this section we will use the notation
1,2
for the
q\bar{q}
-pair and
i,j,k,\dots
for the gluons. The convention for the ordering of the arguments of the B-type functions will be
B_{n}^{l}(1,i,j,k,\dots,2)
, where the quarks are in the first and last entries and the gluons in between. As for the A-type amplitudes, the ordering of the parton labels reflects the colour connections of the partons.

2.2.1
Amplitudes

Similar to the A-type tree-level amplitude, the full B-type tree-level amplitude for any number of gluons can also be written in a simple expression,

\mathcal{B}_{n}^{0}=g_{s}^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}(g_{\sigma(1)},g_{\sigma(2)},\dots,g_{\sigma(n)})_{q\bar{q}}\,b_{n}^{0}(q,g_{\sigma(1)},g_{\sigma(2)},\dots,g_{\sigma(n)},\bar{q}),

(27)

where
(g_{1},g_{2},\dots,g_{n})_{q\bar{q}}
abbreviates the colour matrix product
(T^{g_{1}}T^{g_{2}}\dots T^{g_{n}})_{q\bar{q}}
and the partial amplitude containing one quark pair and
n
gluons is denoted by

. At one loop, the amplitudes have a more elaborate colour structure. The one-loop amplitude with two gluons is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{2}^{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{s}^{2}\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{2\pi}\right)N_{c}\Big[\sum_{\sigma\in S_{2}}(T^{g_{\sigma(1)}}T^{g_{\sigma(2)}})_{q\bar{q}}\,\mathcal{B}_{2;1}^{1}(q,g_{\sigma(1)},g_{\sigma(2)},\bar{q})

(28)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{N_{c}}(T^{g_{1}}T^{g_{2}})\delta_{q\bar{q}}\,\mathcal{B}_{2;3}^{1}(q,g_{1},g_{2},\bar{q})\Big],

and the five-parton one-loop B-type amplitude reads

;

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{3}^{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{s}^{3}\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{2\pi}\right)N_{c}\Big[\sum_{\sigma\in S_{3}}(T^{g_{\sigma(1)}}T^{g_{\sigma(2)}}T^{g_{\sigma(3)}})_{q\bar{q}}\,\mathcal{B}_{3;1}^{1}(q,g_{\sigma(1)},g_{\sigma(2)},g_{\sigma(3)},\bar{q})

(29)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{c}}\sum_{i=1,2,3}(T^{g_{i}})_{q\bar{q}}\delta_{g_{j}g_{k}}\mathcal{B}_{3;3}^{1}(q,g_{i},\bar{q};g_{j},g_{k})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{c}}(T^{g_{1}}T^{g_{2}}T^{g_{3}})\delta_{q\bar{q}}\,\mathcal{B}_{3;4}^{1}(q,\bar{q};g_{1},g_{2},g_{3})

2.2.4
Five-parton tree-level

For three gluons, there are

colour-ordered amplitudes

\displaystyle B_{3}^{0}(1,i,j,k,2)

2.2.7
Five-parton one-loop

The full five-parton B-type squared matrix element
Bern:1994fz
is obtained by interfering the full one-loop amplitude (
29
) with its tree-level counterpart. It is decomposed into colour levels as follows:

\displaystyle M_{5}^{1}(q,g_{1},g_{2},g_{3},\bar{q})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{2\pi}\right)g_{s}^{6}(N_{c}^{2}-1)N_{c}^{3}\Big[B_{3}^{1}(q,g_{1},g_{2},g_{3},\bar{q})+\frac{n_{f}}{N_{c}}\hat{B}_{3}^{1}(q,g_{1},g_{2},g_{3},\bar{q})

(55)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{c}^{2}}\tilde{B}_{3}^{1}(q,g_{1},g_{2},g_{3},\bar{q})+\frac{n_{f}}{N_{c}^{3}}\hat{\tilde{B}}_{3}^{1}(q,g_{1},g_{2},g_{3},\bar{q})+\frac{1}{N_{c}^{4}}\tilde{\tilde{B}}_{3}^{1}(q,g_{1},g_{2},g_{3},\bar{q})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{n_{f}}{N_{c}^{5}}\hat{\tilde{\tilde{B}}}_{3}^{1}(q,g_{1},g_{2},g_{3},\bar{q})+\frac{1}{N_{c}^{6}}\bar{B}_{3}^{1}(q,g_{1},g_{2},g_{3},\bar{q})\Big].

The contributions at the leading
N_{c}
and
n_{f}
level simply consist of sums of coherent squares,

\displaystyle B_{3}^{1}(q,g_{1},g_{2},g_{3},\bar{q})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in S_{3}}\mathrm{Re}\big[b_{3}^{1\dagger}(q,g_{\sigma(1)},g_{\sigma(2)},g_{\sigma(3)},\bar{q})\,b_{3}^{0}(q,g_{\sigma(1)},g_{\sigma(2)},g_{\sigma(3)},\bar{q})\big],

\displaystyle\hat{B}_{3}^{1}(q,g_{1},g_{2},g_{3},\bar{q})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in S_{3}}\mathrm{Re}\big[\hat{b}_{3}^{1\dagger}(q,g_{\sigma(1)},g_{\sigma(2)},g_{\sigma(3)},\bar{q})\,b_{3}^{0}(q,g_{\sigma(1)},g_{\sigma(2)},g_{\sigma(3)},\bar{q})\big],

while the structure of the subleading colour contributions are more involved. The infrared pole structure of the functions at the different colour levels derives from the infrared
\epsilon
-pole structure of the underlying one-loop amplitudes that was described in Section
2.2.2
above.

2.2.8
Four-parton two-loop

The two-loop two-quark two-gluon matrix element yields the double virtual B-type contribution. It consists of the two-loop amplitude interfered with the tree-level amplitude and the genuine square of the one-loop amplitude. Its colour level decomposition is given as
Anastasiou:2001sv
;
Anastasiou:2002zn
;
Bern:2003ck

\displaystyle M_{4}^{2}(q,g_{1},g_{2},\bar{q})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{2\pi}\right)^{2}g_{s}^{4}(N_{c}^{2}-1)N_{c}^{3}\Big[B_{2}^{2}(q,g_{1},g_{2},\bar{q})+B_{2}^{2}(q,g_{2},g_{1},\bar{q})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{n_{f}}{N_{c}}\left(\hat{B}_{2}^{2}(q,g_{1},g_{2},\bar{q})+\hat{B}_{2}^{2}(q,g_{2},g_{1},\bar{q})\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{n_{f}^{2}}{N_{c}^{2}}\left(\hat{\hat{B}}_{2}^{2}(q,g_{1},g_{2},\bar{q})+\hat{\hat{B}}_{2}^{2}(q,g_{2},g_{1},\bar{q})\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{c}^{2}}\tilde{B}_{2}^{2}(q,g_{1},g_{2},\bar{q})+\frac{n_{f}}{N_{c}^{3}}\hat{\tilde{B}}_{2}^{2}(q,g_{1},g_{2},\bar{q})+\frac{n_{f}^{2}}{N_{c}^{4}}\hat{\hat{\tilde{B}}}_{2}^{2}(q,g_{1},g_{2},\bar{q})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{c}^{4}}\tilde{\tilde{B}}_{2}^{2}(q,g_{1},g_{2},\bar{q})+\frac{n_{f}}{N_{c}^{5}}\hat{\tilde{\tilde{B}}}_{2}^{2}(q,g_{1},g_{2},\bar{q})+\frac{1}{N_{c}^{6}}\tilde{\tilde{\tilde{B}}}_{2}^{2}(q,g_{1},g_{2},\bar{q})\Big].

Its
\epsilon
-pole structure is documented in
Anastasiou:2001sv
;
Bern:2003ck
and cancels with the integrated subtraction terms at the VV level.

2.3
C-type matrix elements

We call matrix elements containing two quark pairs of non-identical flavour plus any number of gluons C-type matrix elements. We use the notation
C_{n}^{l}
for a C-type matrix element containing
n
gluons and
l
loops.

2.3.1
Amplitudes

The full amplitude for two non-identical flavour quark pairs at tree-level is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{0}^{0}(q,\bar{q},Q,\bar{Q})=g_{s}^{2}\Big(\delta_{q\bar{Q}}\delta_{Q\bar{q}}-\frac{1}{N_{c}}\delta_{q\bar{q}}\delta_{Q\bar{Q}}\Big)c_{0}^{0}(q,\bar{Q},Q,\bar{q}),

(58)

where there are only the two colour structures of the quark lines:
\delta_{q\bar{Q}}\delta_{Q\bar{q}}
for when the quarks of different flavour are connected and
\delta_{q\bar{q}}\delta_{Q\bar{Q}}
for when quarks of the same flavour are connected, which is subleading by a factor of
1/N_{c}
. For the four-parton tree-level C-type amplitude, the partial amplitude
c_{0}^{0}
multiplying the two colour structures is the same, and can thus be factorized. This is no longer the case for the one-loop amplitude

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{0}^{1}(q,\bar{q},Q,\bar{Q})=g_{s}^{2}\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{2\pi}\right)N_{c}\Big(\delta_{q\bar{Q}}\delta_{Q\bar{q}}\,c_{0,l}^{1}(q,\bar{Q},Q,\bar{q})-\frac{1}{N_{c}}\delta_{q\bar{q}}\delta_{Q\bar{Q}}\,c_{0,s}^{1}(q,\bar{q},Q,\bar{Q})\Big),

(59)

where the leading and subleading partial amplitudes are now different and can be further colour-decomposed as

\displaystyle c_{0,l}^{1}(q,\bar{Q},Q,\bar{q})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{0,l}^{1}(q,\bar{Q},Q,\bar{q})+\frac{n_{f}}{N_{c}}\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{0,l}^{1}(q,\bar{Q},Q,\bar{q})+\frac{1}{N_{c}^{2}}\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{0,l}^{1}(q,\bar{Q},Q,\bar{q}),

\displaystyle c_{0,s}^{1}(q,\bar{q},Q,\bar{Q})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{0,s}^{1}(q,\bar{q},Q,\bar{Q})+\frac{n_{f}}{N_{c}}\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{0,s}^{1}(q,\bar{q},Q,\bar{Q})+\frac{1}{N_{c}^{2}}\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{0,s}^{1}(q,\bar{q},Q,\bar{Q}).

(60)

The five-parton C-type amplitude has the same structure at tree
i=0
and one-loop
i=1

Signer:1995a

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{1}^{i}(q,\bar{q},Q,\bar{Q},g)

\displaystyle g_{s}^{3}\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{2\pi}\right)^{i}\Big[T_{q\bar{Q}}^{g}\delta_{Q\bar{q}}\,c_{1,a}^{i}(q,g,\bar{Q};Q,\bar{q})+T_{Q\bar{q}}^{g}\delta_{q\bar{Q}}\,c_{1,b}^{i}(q,\bar{Q};Q,g,\bar{q})

(61)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{c}}T_{q\bar{q}}^{g}\delta_{Q\bar{Q}}\,c_{1,c}^{i}(q,g,\bar{q};Q,\bar{Q})-\frac{1}{N_{c}}T_{Q\bar{Q}}^{g}\delta_{q\bar{q}}\,c_{1,d}^{i}(q,\bar{q};Q,g,\bar{Q})\Big],

where we have four partial amplitudes

, with

corresponding to the four possible gluon positions in between the quarks. For the tree-level amplitudes, it is also useful to define

\displaystyle c_{1,\gamma_{1}}^{0}(q,\bar{q},Q,\bar{Q},g)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Poles}\big[\tilde{C}_{0}^{1}(1,j,i,2)\big]

2.4
D-type matrix elements

For matrix elements containing two quark pairs of identical flavour, new interferences and consequently new colour connections independent of the C-type squared matrix elements appear because of the possibility to interchange external quark states. We label these new identical-flavour-only interferences as D-type matrix elements. We use the notation
D_{n}^{l}
for a D-type squared matrix element containing
n
gluons and
l
loops.

At the level of the amplitudes, no new objects are introduced since all D-type squared matrix elements are constituted from interferences of C-type amplitudes that were not permitted for the non-identical flavour case.

2.4.1
Four-parton tree-level

The two identical flavour quark pair amplitude is obtained from the non-identical amplitude by addition of the same amplitude where the antiquark momenta have been interchanged:
\bar{q}\leftrightarrow\bar{Q}
. Denoting the identical two quark pair squared tree-level matrix element by
M_{4,\mathrm{id.}}^{0}
and explicitly keeping the different quark pair labels for the identical flavour quarks to see which momenta exactly are interchanged, we obtain

\displaystyle M_{4,\mathrm{id.}}^{0}(q,\bar{q},q,\bar{q})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big|\mathcal{C}_{0}^{0}(q,\bar{Q},Q,\bar{q})-\mathcal{C}_{0}^{0}(q,\bar{q},Q,\bar{Q})\big|^{2}

(84)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{0}^{0}(q,\bar{Q},Q,\bar{q})+C_{0}^{0}(q,\bar{q},Q,\bar{Q})+\frac{1}{N_{c}}D_{0}^{0}(q,\bar{q},Q,\bar{Q}),

where we have two C-type matrix elements, with one related by
\bar{q}\leftrightarrow\bar{Q}
from the other, and the interference term

\displaystyle D_{0}^{0}(q,\bar{q},Q,\bar{Q})=-2\,\mathrm{Re}\big[\mathcal{C}_{0}^{0\dagger}(q,\bar{Q},Q,\bar{q})\,\mathcal{C}_{0}^{0}(q,\bar{q},Q,\bar{Q})\big].

(85)

These type of interferences between the C-type amplitudes defined above with C-type amplitudes with interchanged antiquark momenta are precisely what defines our D-type squared matrix elements. By definition, they have symmetry properties under interchange of
\bar{q}\leftrightarrow\bar{Q}
or
q\leftrightarrow Q
. It can be seen in (
84
) that the D-type contributions are suppressed by a factor of
1/N_{c}
with respect to the C-type contributions, resulting in all D-type matrix elements belonging to the SLC. This means that all the D-type contributions appearing as double real, real-virtual or double virtual corrections, even their leading colour level, are beyond the LC-approximation at NNLO used for dijet production from proton-proton collisions
Currie:2018xkj
;
Currie:2017eqf
;
Gehrmann-DeRidder:2019ibf
. For the higher multiplicity or loop matrix elements in the remainder of this section, we only discuss the genuine D-type matrix elements, and not the full matrix element for identical quarks, as this can be obtained by addition of the C-type matrix elements defined above.

3
Double real subtraction

All six-parton matrix elements that were introduced in the previous section enter the double-real contribution to the dijet cross section. Their numerical implementation into a parton-level event generator requires the construction of subtraction terms, which we discuss in the following.

The double real subtraction, denoted by
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S}
, needs to reproduce single and double unresolved limits of all squared matrix elements in
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{RR}
. We partition
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S}
into the following parts
Currie:2013vh
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S}=\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,a}+\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,b}+\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,c}+\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,d}+\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,e},

(109)

where each part has a specific purpose and general form:

•

\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,a}
: this part is responsible for subtracting the single unresolved limits of the RR squared matrix elements and has the general form of three-parton antennae times a reduced
(n+1)
-parton matrix element:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,a}\supset\mathcal{N}_{RR}\,\mathrm{d}\Phi_{n+2}\,X_{3}^{0}(\{p_{n+2}\})M_{n+1}^{0}(\{\tilde{p}_{n+1}\})J_{n}^{(n+1)}(\{\tilde{p}_{n+1}\}),

(110)

where overall factors are collected in
\mathcal{N}_{RR}
,
\mathrm{d}\Phi_{n+2}
is the
(2\to n)
-particle phase space measure and the tilde in
\{\tilde{p}_{n+1}\}
denotes that the
(n+1)
-momenta set is a single mapped momenta set. The
X_{3}^{0}
denotes a three-parton antenna function
Gehrmann-DeRidder:2004ttg
;
Gehrmann-DeRidder:2005svg
;
Gehrmann-DeRidder:2005alt
which mimics the behaviour of the matrix elements in single unresolved limits. The reduced matrix element contains
(n+1)
particles and at NNLO the jet function
J_{n}^{(n+1)}
allows one parton to become unresolved. These limits have to be accounted for by subtraction terms in the other parts.

•

\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,b}
: this part consists of
X_{4}^{0}
antenna functions
Gehrmann-DeRidder:2005btv
, which reproduce the behaviour of the RR matrix element in genuine double unresolved limits, and its associated iterated
X_{3}^{0}X_{3}^{0}
antennae. We further partition
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,b}
into
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,b_{1}}
and
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,b_{2}}
accordingly:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,b_{1}}

\displaystyle\supset

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{RR}\,\mathrm{d}\Phi_{n+2}\,X_{4}^{0}(\{p_{n+2}\})M_{n}^{0}(\{\tilde{p}_{n}\})J_{n}^{(n)}(\{\tilde{p}_{n}\}),

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,b_{2}}

\displaystyle\supset

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{RR}\,\mathrm{d}\Phi_{n+2}\,X_{3}^{0}(\{p_{n+2}\})X_{3}^{0}(\{\tilde{p}_{n+1}\})M_{n}^{0}(\{\tilde{\tilde{p}}_{n}\})J_{n}^{(n)}(\{\tilde{\tilde{p}}_{n}\}).

(111)

Here the double tilde denotes a doubly mapped momenta set arising from the iterated
X_{3}^{0}
antennae. The terms in
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,b_{1}}
subtract colour-connected double unresolved limits. The
X_{4}^{0}
antenna functions themselves contain single unresolved limits, which do not correspond to the single unresolved limits of the RR matrix element. In these limits, the
X_{4}^{0}
antennae collapse onto
X_{3}^{0}
antennae, so these limits are removed by the iterated
X_{3}^{0}
antennae in
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,b_{2}}
.

•

\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,c}
: these terms have the general form:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,c}\supset\mathcal{N}_{RR}\,\mathrm{d}\Phi_{n+2}\,X_{3}^{0}(\{p_{n+2}\})X_{3}^{0}(\{\tilde{p}_{n+1}\})M_{n}^{0}(\{\tilde{\tilde{p}}_{n}\})J_{n}^{(n)}(\{\tilde{\tilde{p}}_{n}\}),

(112)

where the two
X_{3}^{0}
antennae have one or more common momenta (related by a mapping). These terms appear when subtracting almost colour-connected limits and other spurious limits.

•

\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,d}
: this part subtracts colour-disconnected limits and has the form

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,d}\supset\mathcal{N}_{RR}\,\mathrm{d}\Phi_{n+2}\,X_{3}^{0}(\{p_{n+2}\})X_{3}^{0}(\{\tilde{p}_{n+1}\})M_{n}^{0}(\{\tilde{\tilde{p}}_{n}\})J_{n}^{(n)}(\{\tilde{\tilde{p}}_{n}\}),

(113)

where there are no common momenta in the two
X_{3}^{0}
antenna functions. Consequently, although the arguments of the second
X_{3}^{0}
are taken from
the
\{\tilde{p}_{n+1}\}
momentum set (to assure phase space factorization), they are not affected by the first phase space mapping
and are taken unmodified out of the
\{p_{n+2}\}
momenta set.

•

\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,e}
: after all the previous parts have been added together, there is still the possibility of a mismatch in single soft limits between the subtraction terms and the squared matrix element, which is caused by the mapped momenta in the antennae. These mismatches are resolved by adding large angle soft (LAS) terms
Currie:2013vh

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,e}\supset\mathcal{N}_{RR}\,\mathrm{d}\Phi_{n+2}\,\left(S^{FF}(\{p_{n+2}\})-S^{FF}(\{\tilde{p}_{n+1}\})\right)

\displaystyle\times X_{3}^{0}(\{\tilde{p}_{n+1}\})M_{n}^{0}(\{\tilde{\tilde{p}}_{n}\})J_{n}^{(n)}(\{\tilde{\tilde{p}}_{n}\}).

(114)

In general, the mapping in the soft functions can be any of the initial-initial, initial-final or final-final type, but for our dijet processes, there are sufficient partons present to always use a final-final mapping. As the FF mapping has some advantages compared to the other mappings in terms of convergence in the unresolved limits, we choose to always employ the FF mappings for the LAS terms.

The above subtraction terms are integrated analytically over the unresolved phase space associated to the antenna functions contained in them by assigning them to either
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,1}
or
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,2}
defined in (
2
) above:
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,a}
,
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,b_{2}}
and
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,e}
belong to
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,1}
, while
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,b_{1}}
and
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,d}
belong to
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,2}
.
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,c}
is split between both.

In the colour-ordered antenna subtraction method, we generally construct the double real subtraction in the order above, guided by the behaviour of the matrix element in unresolved limits. For most of the RR parton-level subprocesses described in Section
2
above, it is straightforward to identify the hard radiator partons and the unresolved partons for each colour-ordered squared matrix element containing quarks, gluons and abelian gluons.
The construction of the subtraction terms then follows in a straightforward, albeit tedious and lengthy manner, as described in detail in
Currie:2013vh

We will not repeat this description here, but instead focus on the new type of antenna subtraction terms that are required for genuine interference-type squared matrix elements, which first appear for processes with four coloured partons at Born level. In the subleading colour all-gluon contribution to dijet production, the required antenna subtraction terms were already described elsewhere
Currie:2013dwa

2.2.5
to be constructed from functions containing quarks, gluons and abelian gluons, with some remainder interference terms at the
1/N_{c}^{2}
colour level grouped together in
\bm{R}_{4}^{0}
. For the R-term
R_{4}^{0}
, there is no clear colour connection defined at the squared amplitude level, even after including the notion of abelian gluons, thus its unresolved limits are not immediately clear. In the following, we discuss how to find all the unresolved limits of
R_{4}^{0}
and subsequently construct the double real subtraction for these terms.

3.1
Single unresolved limits of genuine interference B-type contributions

We can exploit our knowledge of the unresolved limits of all the other B-type squared matrix elements besides the R-term to find the unresolved limits of
R_{4}^{0}
.
This is done by knowing how the full B-type matrix element behaves in certain limits and comparing it with the terms coming from the well-defined B-type squared functions in that limit. Any missing term from the full B-type matrix element not produced by the B-type functions must come from the R-term. We do these comparisons in a colour-ordered way, such that we only need to consider the colour level relevant to the R-term.

3.1.1
Single gluon-gluon collinear
gg\to G

We consider the full matrix element
|\mathcal{B}_{4}^{0}|
in the gluon-gluon collinear limit. It factorizes into a splitting function and a reduced multiplicity matrix element
(omitting here and in the following the tensor indices related to angular-dependent terms in the gluon-to-gluon splitting function):

\displaystyle\big|\mathcal{B}_{4}^{0}\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{g\|g}

\displaystyle\bm{P}_{gg\to G}\big|\mathcal{B}_{3}^{0}\big|^{2}.

(115)

The full splitting function carries a factor of
g_{s}^{2}
and
C_{A}=N_{c}
and the full reduced matrix element is given by (
41
). This yields the result

\displaystyle\big|\mathcal{B}_{4}^{0}\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{g\|g}

\displaystyle g_{s}^{8}(N_{c}^{2}-1)N_{c}^{3}P_{gg\to G}\Big[\bm{B}_{3}^{0}-\frac{1}{N_{c}^{2}}\Big(\tilde{\bm{B}}_{3}^{0}-\bar{\bm{B}}_{3}^{0}\Big)+\frac{1}{N_{c}^{4}}\bar{\bm{B}}_{3}^{0}\Big].

(116)

We can also obtain this result by taking the collinear limit of the B-type functions in (
2.2.5
). Since abelian gluons have no collinear limit with each other, we immediately see why the
1/N_{c}^{6}
colour level disappears in this limit, as
\bar{\bm{B}}_{4}^{0}
contains no non-abelian gluons. Focusing on the
1/N_{c}^{2}
colour level where the R-term resides, we thus note that
\bar{\bm{B}}_{4}^{0}
has no contribution in the collinear limit and the
\tilde{\bm{B}}_{4}^{0}
term can only contribute when the collinear gluons are non-abelian. We also know that the sum of all terms must factor onto a full
\tilde{\bm{B}}_{3}^{0}
function minus a full
\bar{\bm{B}}_{3}^{0}
function. The
\tilde{\bm{B}}_{4}^{0}
function can never factor onto the all abelian-gluon
\bar{\bm{B}}_{3}^{0}
function, leaving the R-term as its only source. Keeping track of all the orderings we see that in the gluon-gluon collinear limit
i\|j

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{B}}_{4}^{0}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{i\|j}

\displaystyle P_{gg\to G}\Big[\tilde{B}_{3}^{0}(1,k,(ij),l,2)+\tilde{B}_{3}^{0}(1,k,l,(ij),2)

(117)

\displaystyle+\tilde{B}_{3}^{0}(1,l,(ij),k,2)+\tilde{B}_{3}^{0}(1,l,k,(ij),2)\Big],

which lacks the two orderings

\displaystyle\tilde{B}_{3}^{0}(1,(ij),k,l,2),\quad\tilde{B}_{3}^{0}(1,(ij),l,k,2),

(118)

needed to form the full
\tilde{\bm{B}}_{3}^{0}
function at the reduced matrix element level. This means that the R-term must exactly factorize in such a way so as to provide these missing orderings plus the
\bar{\bm{B}}_{3}^{0}
term:

\displaystyle\bm{R}_{4}^{0}\xrightarrow{i\|j}P_{gg\to G}\Big[\tilde{B}_{3}^{0}(1,(ij),k,l,2)+\tilde{B}_{3}^{0}(1,(ij),l,k,2)-\bar{B}_{3}^{0}(1,(ij),k,l,2)\Big].

(119)

The R-term in the gluon-gluon collinear limit thus factorizes into functions where the composite gluon is abelian.

3.1.2
Single quark-antiquark collinear
\bar{q}q\to G

In the quark-antiquark collinear limit, the full B-type squared matrix element factorizes into a splitting function and a reduced all-gluon squared matrix element:

\displaystyle\big|\mathcal{B}_{4}^{0}\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{q\|\bar{q}}

\displaystyle\bm{P}_{q\bar{q}\to G}\big|\mathcal{A}_{5}^{0}\big|^{2}.

(120)

The splitting function carries a factor of
g_{s}^{2}
and
T_{R}
and the full reduced matrix element is given by (
17
). The full five-gluon squared matrix element has no subleading colour terms, since these vanish by the Dual Ward Identity for all-gluonic amplitudes. This yields the result

\displaystyle\big|\mathcal{B}_{4}^{0}\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{q\|\bar{q}}

\displaystyle g_{s}^{8}(N_{c}^{2}-1)N_{c}^{3}(T_{R}{P}_{q\bar{q}\to G})\bm{A}_{5}^{0}.

(121)

Noting that both
\tilde{\bm{B}}_{4}^{0}
and
\bar{\bm{B}}_{4}^{0}
independently have no singular quark-antiquark collinear limit since they would reduce to a gluonic matrix element containing abelian gluon(s), we can conclude that the R-term also has no singular quark-antiquark collinear limit.

3.1.3
Single quark-gluon collinear
qg\to Q

To determine the behaviour of the R-term in the single-collinear quark-gluon limits, we use the known collinear factorization for colour-ordered amplitudes and note that to get a sufficiently singular collinear limit at the squared amplitude level we must have the partons colour adjacent in both the amplitude and conjugated amplitude. From the definition of a single ordering of the R-term in (
45
), we can immediately see that there are no single quark-gluon collinear limits: quark
1
is adjacent to gluon
i
in the conjugated amplitude, but this is never the case in the amplitudes this particular conjugated amplitude multiplies; the same holds for quark
2
with gluon
l
.

3.1.4
Single soft limit

The collinear limits of the R-term could easily be found because of the factorization onto the five-parton squared matrix elements, but unfortunately this is not the case for the soft gluon limit. As the R-term does not have clear colour connections at the squared matrix element level, its soft limit is not immediately clear. Thus, it is now necessary to look at how the matrix element behaves in unresolved limits at the amplitude level. The factorization of a colour-ordered amplitude in a single soft limit is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{n}^{0}(\dots,a,i,b,\dots)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{i\to 0}

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mu}(a,i,b)\epsilon^{\mu}\mathcal{M}_{n-1}^{0}(\dots,a,b,\dots),

(122)

where
\epsilon^{\mu}
is the gluon polarization vector and
\mathcal{S}_{\mu}
the single soft current
Sudakov:1954sw

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{\mu}(a,i,b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\bigg(\frac{a^{\mu}}{s_{ai}}-\frac{b^{\mu}}{s_{bi}}\bigg).

(123)

Squaring the single soft current and averaging over the unresolved gluon polarizations using the polarization tensor yields the familiar soft eikonal factor,

\displaystyle S_{aib}=\frac{1}{2}d^{\mu\nu}S_{\mu}(a,i,b)S_{\nu}(a,i,b)=\frac{2s_{ab}}{s_{ai}s_{ib}},

(124)

but when we have an interference term of an amplitude where
i
becomes soft in between
a,b
multiplied by a conjugated amplitude where
i
becomes soft in between
c,d
we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{n}^{0\dagger}(\dots,a,i,b,\dots)\,\mathcal{M}_{n}^{0}(\dots,c,i,d,\dots)\xrightarrow{i\to 0}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Big[S_{aid}+S_{bic}-S_{aic}-S_{bid}\Big]\mathcal{M}_{n-1}^{0\dagger}(\dots,a,b,\dots)\,\mathcal{M}_{n-1}^{0}(\dots,c,d,\dots),

(125)

which can simply be shown by interfering two incoherent colour orderings of the soft current:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}d^{\mu\nu}\mathcal{S}_{\mu}(a,i,b)\mathcal{S}_{\nu}(c,i,d)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\Big(\frac{a^{\mu}}{s_{ai}}-\frac{b^{\mu}}{s_{bi}}\Big)\Big(\frac{c_{\mu}}{s_{ci}}-\frac{d_{\mu}}{s_{di}}\Big),

(126)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Big[S_{aid}+S_{bic}-S_{aic}-S_{bid}\Big].

Alternatively, preparing for the double soft current below, the above relation can also be shown by using the following properties of the single soft current:

1.

Reflection:
\mathcal{S}_{\mu}(b,i,a)=-\mathcal{S}_{\mu}(a,i,b)
,

2.

Left shuffle:
\mathcal{S}_{\mu}(a,i,b)=\mathcal{S}_{\mu}(c,i,b)-\mathcal{S}_{\mu}(c,i,a)
,

3.

Right shuffle:
\mathcal{S}_{\mu}(a,i,b)=\mathcal{S}_{\mu}(a,i,c)+\mathcal{S}_{\mu}(c,i,b)
,

where the first and second properties can simply be inferred from looking at the explicit expression of the soft current and the third property follows from the other two.

Using these identities for the soft limit of a general interference term, the soft limit of the R-term can be expressed by combinations of eikonal factors multiplying an interference of five-parton amplitudes. For example, for the single ordering of the R-term
R_{4}^{0}(1,i,j,k,l,2)
we find in the
i
soft limit:

\displaystyle R_{4}^{0}(1,i,j,k,l,2)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{i\to 0}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\big[S_{1il}+S_{jij}-S_{1ij}-S_{jil}\big]b_{3}^{0\dagger}(1,j,k,l,2)\,b_{3}^{0}(1,j,l,k,2)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\big[S_{1ik}+S_{jil}-S_{1il}-S_{jik}\big]b_{3}^{0\dagger}(1,j,k,l,2)\,b_{3}^{0}(1,j,l,k,2)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\big[S_{1i2}+S_{jik}-S_{1ik}-S_{ji2}\big]b_{3}^{0\dagger}(1,j,k,l,2)\,b_{3}^{0}(1,j,l,k,2)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\big[S_{1il}+S_{jik}-S_{1ik}-S_{jil}\big]b_{3}^{0\dagger}(1,j,k,l,2)\,b_{3}^{0}(1,k,l,j,2)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\big[S_{1i2}+S_{jil}-S_{1il}-S_{ji2}\big]b_{3}^{0\dagger}(1,j,k,l,2)\,b_{3}^{0}(1,k,j,l,2)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\big[S_{1ik}+S_{jil}-S_{1il}-S_{jik}\big]b_{3}^{0\dagger}(1,j,k,l,2)\,b_{3}^{0}(1,l,k,j,2)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\big[S_{1ik}+S_{jij}-S_{1ij}-S_{jik}\big]b_{3}^{0\dagger}(1,j,k,l,2)\,b_{3}^{0}(1,l,j,k,2)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\big[S_{1i2}+S_{jij}-S_{1ij}-S_{ji2}\big]b_{3}^{0\dagger}(1,j,k,l,2)\,b_{3}^{0}(1,l,k,j,2).

These reduced interference terms cannot be written into a squared function or sum of squared functions, even when summed over all 24 orderings. Thus, we define

B_{3,\mathrm{int}}^{0}(i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},i_{4},i_{5},j_{1},j_{2},j_{3},j_{4},j_{5})=2\,\mathrm{Re}\big[b_{3}^{0\dagger}(i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},i_{4},i_{5})\,b_{3}^{0}(j_{1},j_{2},j_{3},j_{4},j_{5})\big],

(128)

as the reduced matrix element when it consists of a general interference.

Having identified all the single unresolved limits of the R-term, the construction of the
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,a}
subtraction follows as usual. Eikonal factors are promoted to
X_{3}^{0}
antenna functions which capture all the soft as well as the collinear gluon-gluon limits. Any quark-gluon collinear limit produced by the
X_{3}^{0}
antennae cancels out in the total sum of all terms, which is consistent with what we found before. This approach of taking the soft limit at amplitude level to construct the
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,a}
subtraction term also works for the well-defined squared B-type functions containing (abelian) gluons. Exactly the same subtraction terms are generated in this way as in the standard approach using the colour connections of the squared matrix elements, meaning that although helpful to understand the matrix element better, it is not strictly necessary to organise the amplitudes into squared functions. As even higher multiplicity B-type matrix elements (e.g.
|\mathcal{B}_{5}^{0}|
) will also contain remaining interference terms like the R-term
\bm{R}_{4}^{0}
at subleading colour levels, the approach at amplitude level works the same way as for the six-parton B-type matrix element to construct the
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,a}
subtraction, and would be the preferred method, if one seeks to automate the construction of the subtraction terms
Chen:2022ktf
.

3.2
Double unresolved limits of genuine interference B-type contributions

In the following, we examine the double unresolved limits of the R-term for the construction of the double real subtraction terms. The NNLO collinear limits can be determined in the same way as was done for the single collinear limits, because they factorize nicely onto the reduced squared matrix elements. For the double soft limit however, this is again not the case and we will need to determine how the R-term behaves in a double soft limit at the amplitude level.

3.2.1
Triple collinear
ggg\to G

First we consider the gluon-gluon-gluon triple collinear limit, in which the full matrix element factorizes as

\displaystyle\big|\mathcal{B}_{4}^{0}\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{g\|g\|g}

\displaystyle\bm{P}_{ggg\to G}\big|\mathcal{B}_{2}^{0}\big|^{2}.

(129)

The full splitting function carries a factor of
g_{s}^{4}
and
C_{A}^{2}=N_{c}^{2}
as well as being summed over six gluon orderings. The reduced matrix element is given by (
36
), resulting in

\displaystyle\big|\mathcal{B}_{4}^{0}\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{g\|g\|g}

\displaystyle g_{s}^{8}(N_{c}^{2}-1)N_{c}^{3}{P}_{ggg\to G}\Big[\bm{B}_{2}^{0}-\frac{1}{N_{c}^{2}}\bar{\bm{B}}_{2}^{0}\Big].

(130)

The lack of non-abelian gluons at the
1/N_{c}^{4}
and
1/N_{c}^{6}
colour levels means that these terms do not contribute to the limit. At the
1/N_{c}^{2}
colour level, the
\tilde{\bm{B}}_{4}^{0}
term fully reproduces the
\bar{\bm{B}}_{2}^{0}
term, and so there is no limit left over for
\bm{R}_{4}^{0}
to contribute to.

3.2.2
Triple collinear
qgg\to Q

The
qgg\to Q
splitting function has the decomposition into the colour-ordered splitting functions:

\displaystyle\bm{P}_{qgg\to Q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N_{c}^{2}P_{qgg\to Q}-(P_{qgg\to Q}-\tilde{P}_{qgg\to Q})+\frac{1}{N_{c}^{2}}\tilde{P}_{qgg\to Q}.

(131)

The full B-type matrix element in this limit then factorizes according to

\displaystyle\big|\mathcal{B}_{4}^{0}\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{q\|g\|g}

\displaystyle g_{s}^{8}(N_{c}^{2}-1)N_{c}\Big[N_{c}^{2}P_{qgg\to Q}-(P_{qgg\to Q}-\tilde{P}_{qgg\to Q})+\frac{1}{N_{c}^{2}}\tilde{P}_{qgg\to Q}\Big]

(132)

\displaystyle\times\Big[\bm{B}_{2}^{0}-\frac{1}{N_{c}^{2}}\bar{\bm{B}}_{2}^{0}\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{s}^{8}(N_{c}^{2}-1)N_{c}^{3}\Big[P_{qgg\to Q}\bm{B}_{2}^{0}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{N_{c}^{2}}\Big((P_{qgg\to Q}-\tilde{P}_{qgg\to Q}){\bm{B}}_{2}^{0}+P_{qgg\to Q}\bar{\bm{B}}_{2}^{0}\Big)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{N_{c}^{4}}\Big((P_{qgg\to Q}-\tilde{P}_{qgg\to Q})\bar{\bm{B}}_{2}^{0}+\tilde{P}_{qgg\to Q}{\bm{B}}_{2}^{0}\Big)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{N_{c}^{6}}\Big(\tilde{P}_{qgg\to Q}\bar{\bm{B}}_{2}^{0}\Big)\Big].

At the
1/N_{c}^{2}
colour level we observe that we can receive contributions to this limit from the
\tilde{\bm{B}}_{4}^{0}
,
\bar{\bm{B}}_{4}^{0}
and
\bm{R}_{4}^{0}
terms. The
\tilde{\bm{B}}_{4}^{0}
terms have two types of limits: a splitting factoring onto the subleading colour matrix element,

\displaystyle\tilde{B}_{4}^{0}(1,i,j,k,l,2)\xrightarrow{1\|j\|k}{P}_{qgg\to Q}\bar{B}_{2}^{0}((1jk),i,l,2),

(133)

and an abelian splitting factoring onto a leading colour matrix element,

\displaystyle\tilde{B}_{4}^{0}(1,i,j,k,l,2)\xrightarrow{1\|i\|j}\tilde{P}_{qgg\to Q}B_{2}^{0}((1ij),k,l,2).

(134)

The
\bar{\bm{B}}_{4}^{0}
term only has the latter type, but factorizes onto the subleading colour matrix element instead. Keeping track of all the orderings, their full factorization is given by

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{B}}_{4}^{0}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{q\|g\|g}

\displaystyle 2(\tilde{P}_{qgg\to Q}\bm{B}_{2}^{0}+P_{qgg\to Q}\bar{\bm{B}}_{2}^{0}),

\displaystyle\bar{\bm{B}}_{4}^{0}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{q\|g\|g}

\displaystyle\tilde{P}_{qgg\to Q}\bar{\bm{B}}_{2}^{0},

(135)

where we note that here
P_{qgg\to Q}
contains both gluon orderings. From this we can infer the factorization of the R-term:

\displaystyle\bm{R}_{4}^{0}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{q\|g\|g}

\displaystyle(\tilde{P}_{qgg\to Q}-P_{qgg\to Q})(\bar{\bm{B}}_{2}^{0}-\bm{B}_{2}^{0}),

(136)

where the combination of reduced matrix elements is exactly the interference matrix element
\tilde{B}_{2,R}^{0}
defined in (
38
). Note that the combination of splitting functions
(\tilde{P}_{qgg\to Q}-P_{qgg\to Q})
can also be written as an interference of splitting amplitudes
Catani:1999ss
in the form,

\displaystyle\tilde{P}_{qgg\to Q}-P_{qgg\to Q}=2\,\mathrm{Re}\Big[\sum_{\lambda}\mathrm{Split}^{\dagger}(1,i,j;\{\lambda\})\,\mathrm{Split}(1,j,i;\{\lambda\})\Big].

(137)

3.2.3
Triple collinear
g\bar{q}q\to G

In the
g\|\bar{q}\|q
limit we have the factorization

\displaystyle\big|\mathcal{B}_{4}^{0}\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{g\|\bar{q}\|q}

\displaystyle\bm{P}_{g\bar{q}q\to G}\big|\mathcal{A}_{4}^{0}\big|^{2},

(138)

where the full gluon matrix element is given by (
2.1.3
). The full
g\bar{q}q\to G
splitting function has the decomposition

\displaystyle\bm{P}_{g\bar{q}q\to G}=N_{c}T_{R}P_{g\bar{q}q}-\frac{1}{N_{c}}T_{R}\tilde{P}_{g\bar{q}q},

(139)

which gives the following factorization:

\displaystyle\big|\mathcal{B}_{4}^{0}\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{g\|\bar{q}\|q}

\displaystyle g_{s}^{8}(N_{c}^{2}-1)N_{c}^{3}\Big[T_{R}P_{g\bar{q}q}\bm{A}_{4}^{0}-\frac{1}{N_{c}^{2}}T_{R}\tilde{P}_{g\bar{q}q}\bm{A}_{4}^{0}\Big],

(140)

where
P_{g\bar{q}q}
is summed over the two orderings
g\bar{q}q
and
\bar{q}qg
. In this limit
\tilde{\tilde{\bm{B}}}_{4}^{0}
and
\bar{\bm{B}}_{4}^{0}
reduce to all gluon matrix elements containing abelian gluon(s), which vanish by the Dual Ward Identity. At the
1/N_{c}^{2}
colour level, the
\tilde{\bm{B}}_{4}^{0}
does have some non-vanishing limits when the collinear gluon is abelian. In this limit it reduces exactly to
\bm{A}_{4}^{0}
, leaving no limit left over for the R-term.

3.2.4
Double single collinear limits

In the limit where distinct pairs of gluons become collinear, say
i\|j
and
k\|l
, the full matrix element factorizes as

\displaystyle\big|\mathcal{B}_{4}^{0}\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{i\|j,\,k\|l}

\displaystyle\bm{P}_{ij\to G}\bm{P}_{kl\to G}\big|\mathcal{B}_{2}^{0}\big|^{2}

(141)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{s}^{8}(N_{c}^{2}-1)N_{c}^{3}P_{ij\to G}P_{kl\to G}\Big[\bm{B}_{2}^{0}-\frac{1}{N_{c}^{2}}\bar{\bm{B}}_{2}^{0}\Big].

Due to the lack of sufficient non-abelian gluons in
\tilde{\bm{B}}_{4}^{0}
and
\bar{\bm{B}}_{4}^{0}
, we can immediately see the factorization of the R-term in this limit:

\displaystyle\bm{R}_{4}^{0}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{i\|j,\,k\|l}

\displaystyle P_{ij\to G}P_{kl\to G}\bar{\bm{B}}_{2}^{0}.

(142)

Alternatively, we could take the two
gg
-collinear limits in a sequential manner, using the result of the single
gg
-collinear factorization of the R-term, which gives the same result. Since the R-term does not have any singular
qg
-collinear limits, even after one
gg
-collinear limit, we can also conclude that it has no double
gg
,
qg
-collinear or double
qg
,
qg
-collinear limit. In the same way, the double
\bar{q}q,gg
-collinear limit is absent because the R-term has no
\bar{q}q
limit.

3.2.5
Double soft limit

For the double soft limit of the R-term it is necessary to look into the double soft limit at the amplitude level. The double soft limit of a colour-ordered amplitude where the two soft gluons are not colour connected simply results in two single soft currents,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{n}^{0}(\dots,a,i,b,\dots,c,j,d,\dots)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{i,j\to 0}

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mu}(a,i,b)\mathcal{S}_{\nu}(c,j,d)\epsilon^{\mu}\epsilon^{\nu}\mathcal{M}_{n-2}^{0}(\dots,a,b\dots,c,d,\dots).

In the colour-connected double soft limit we have a factorization into the double soft current
Catani:1999ss
;
Berends:1988zn
;
Weinzierl:2003fx
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{n}^{0}(\dots,a,i,j,b,\dots)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{i,j\to 0}

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mu\nu}(a,i,j,b)\epsilon^{\mu}\epsilon^{\nu}\mathcal{M}_{n-2}^{0}(\dots,a,b,\dots),

(144)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mu\nu}(a,i,j,b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\bigg(\frac{1}{s_{ij}(s_{ai}+s_{aj})}\big(a_{\mu}i_{\nu}-a_{\nu}j_{\mu}\big)+\frac{1}{s_{ij}(s_{bi}+s_{bj})}\big(b_{\nu}j_{\mu}-b_{\mu}i_{\nu}\big)

(145)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}\frac{(s_{aj}s_{bi}-s_{ai}s_{bj})}{2s_{ij}(s_{ai}+s_{aj})(s_{bi}+s_{bj})}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle a_{\mu}a_{\nu}\frac{1}{s_{ai}(s_{ai}+s_{aj})}+b_{\mu}b_{\nu}\frac{1}{s_{bj}(s_{bi}+s_{bj})}-a_{\mu}b_{\nu}\frac{1}{s_{ai}s_{bj}}\bigg).

Squaring the double soft amplitude and averaging over the soft gluon polarizations for a coherent ordering gives by definition the double soft function

\displaystyle S_{aijb}=\frac{1}{4}d^{\mu\rho}d^{\nu\sigma}\mathcal{S}_{\mu\nu}(a,i,j,b)\mathcal{S}_{\rho\sigma}(a,i,j,b).

(146)

The R-term consists of many interference terms, and for a generic interference amplitude, we now have several possibilities in the double soft limit:

1.

Coherent colour-connected ordering:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{n}^{\dagger}(\dots,a,i,j,b,\dots)\mathcal{M}_{n}(\dots,a,i,j,b,\dots)\xrightarrow{i,j\to 0}S_{\mu\nu}(a,i,j,b)S^{\mu\nu}(a,i,j,b)M_{n-2}.

2.

Colour-connected gluons between different partons:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{n}^{\dagger}(\dots,a,i,j,b,\dots)\mathcal{M}_{n}(\dots,c,i,j,d,\dots)\xrightarrow{i,j\to 0}S_{\mu\nu}(a,i,j,b)S^{\mu\nu}(c,i,j,d)M_{n-2}.

3.

Colour-disconnected times colour-connected:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{n}^{\dagger}(\dots,a,i,b,\dots,c,j,d,\dots)\mathcal{M}_{n}(\dots,e,i,j,f,\dots)\xrightarrow{i,j\to 0}

\displaystyle S_{\mu}(a,i,b)S_{\nu}(c,j,d)S^{\mu\nu}(e,i,j,f)M_{n-2}.

(149)

4.

All colour-disconnected:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{n}^{\dagger}(\dots,a,i,b,\dots,c,j,d,\dots)\mathcal{M}_{n}(\dots,e,i,f,\dots,g,j,h,\dots)\xrightarrow{i,j\to 0}

\displaystyle S_{\mu}(a,i,b)S_{\nu}(c,j,d)S^{\mu}(e,i,f)S^{\nu}(g,j,h)M_{n-2}.

(150)

Note that in the first and second possibilities the gluons in one of the amplitudes could also be in reverse order with respect to the other amplitude. We will touch upon these cases later on and see that they can be converted to the other possibilities. For possibility 1, the double soft amplitude can be squared and in Section
3.1.4
it was shown how the interference of single soft currents in possibility 4 can be expressed in terms of soft eikonal factors. This is necessary for the antenna subtraction method, because the antenna functions are only able to capture the soft singular limits in the form of squared soft factors. In contrast to the single soft current, it is now not straightforward to express the type of soft interferences from possibility 2 and 3 in terms of known squared factors, so we cannot easily identify which antenna functions are able to capture all the double soft limits. Therefore, we seek properties for the double soft current as was done for the single soft current to help simplify all the generic interferences of single and double soft amplitudes of the R-term into products of soft eikonal factors and double soft factors.

The double soft current satisfies a decoupling identity when symmetrized over soft gluons:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mu\nu}(a,i,j,b)+\mathcal{S}_{\nu\mu}(a,j,i,b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mu}(a,i,b)\mathcal{S}_{\nu}(a,j,b),

(151)

which can easily be seen by the fact that the first three terms in (
145
) are antisymmetric under
(i\leftrightarrow j,\mu\leftrightarrow\nu)
and by using the partial fractioning in the fourth and fifth terms to eliminate the three-particle denominators.

The decoupling identity can be used to derive the square of the interference of two double soft currents which differ only by soft gluon orderings,

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)}\frac{1}{4}d^{\mu\rho}d^{\nu\sigma}\mathcal{S}_{\mu\nu}(a,i,j,b)\mathcal{S}_{\sigma\rho}(a,j,i,b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{aib}S_{ajb}-S_{aijb}-S_{ajib},

(152)

where
\sum_{(i,j)}
denotes the sum over the
i\leftrightarrow j
swapped term with also the indices of the double soft currents swapped (
\mu\leftrightarrow\nu
,
\rho\leftrightarrow\sigma
). Examining the double soft current further, it is obvious that it also possesses a reflection property,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mu\nu}(a,i,j,b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\nu\mu}(b,j,i,a),

(153)

Using the decoupling identity we can then obtain the expression for the double soft current with swapped hard partons, analogous to the reflection property of the single soft current,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mu\nu}(b,i,j,a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\nu\mu}(a,j,i,b)

(154)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathcal{S}_{\mu\nu}(a,i,j,b)+\mathcal{S}_{\mu}(a,i,b)\mathcal{S}_{\nu}(a,j,b).

The double soft current also satisfies a left shuffle identity,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mu\nu}(a,i,j,b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mu\nu}(c,i,j,b)-\mathcal{S}_{\mu\nu}(c,i,j,a)-\mathcal{S}_{\mu}(c,i,a)\mathcal{S}_{\nu}(a,j,b),

(155)

and a right shuffle identity follows by using the reflection, decoupling and left shuffle properties:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mu\nu}(a,i,j,b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mu\nu}(a,i,j,c)+\mathcal{S}_{\mu\nu}(c,i,j,b)+\mathcal{S}_{\mu}(a,i,c)\mathcal{S}_{\nu}(c,j,b).

(156)

Using the decoupling identity (or by relabelling), we can also write down shuffle identities for the currents with reversed soft gluons,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\nu\mu}(a,j,i,b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\nu\mu}(c,j,i,b)-\mathcal{S}_{\nu\mu}(c,j,i,a)-\mathcal{S}_{\mu}(b,i,a)\mathcal{S}_{\nu}(a,j,c),

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\nu\mu}(a,j,i,b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\nu\mu}(a,j,i,c)+\mathcal{S}_{\nu\mu}(c,j,i,b)+\mathcal{S}_{\mu}(b,i,c)\mathcal{S}_{\nu}(c,j,a).

(157)

There are two relations corresponding to the two possible orderings of the symmetrized soft gluons,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mu\nu}(a,i,j,b)\mathcal{S}^{\mu\nu}(c,i,j,d)+\mathcal{S}_{\nu\mu}(a,j,i,b)\mathcal{S}^{\nu\mu}(c,j,i,d),

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mu\nu}(a,i,j,b)\mathcal{S}^{\nu\mu}(c,j,i,d)+\mathcal{S}_{\nu\mu}(a,j,i,b)\mathcal{S}^{\mu\nu}(c,i,j,d).

(158)

The second configuration, where the soft gluon ordering is different in the two interfered currents, can be related to the first configuration using the decoupling identity,

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)}\mathcal{S}_{\mu\nu}(a,i,j,b)\mathcal{S}^{\nu\mu}(c,j,i,d)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)}\mathcal{S}_{\mu\nu}(a,i,j,b)\Big[-\mathcal{S}^{\mu\nu}(c,i,j,d)+\mathcal{S}^{\mu}(c,i,d)\mathcal{S}^{\nu}(c,j,d)\Big]

(159)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)}-\mathcal{S}_{\mu\nu}(a,i,j,b)\mathcal{S}^{\mu\nu}(c,i,j,d)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{S}^{\mu}(c,i,d)\mathcal{S}^{\nu}(c,j,d)\sum_{(i,j)}\mathcal{S}_{\mu\nu}(a,i,j,b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)}-\mathcal{S}_{\mu\nu}(a,i,j,b)\mathcal{S}^{\mu\nu}(c,i,j,d)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{S}_{\mu}(a,i,b)\mathcal{S}_{\nu}(a,j,b)\mathcal{S}^{\mu}(c,i,d)\mathcal{S}^{\nu}(c,j,d),

and so the two configurations are only different by products of single soft currents, which can be expressed as sums
of soft factors,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mu}(a,i,b)\mathcal{S}_{\nu}(a,j,b)\mathcal{S}^{\mu}(c,i,d)\mathcal{S}^{\nu}(c,j,d)=

(160)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\big(S_{aid}+S_{bic}-S_{aic}-S_{bid}\big)\big(S_{ajd}+S_{bjc}-S_{ajc}-S_{bjd}\big).

The different identities derived in this section and in Section
3.1.4
are sufficient to re-express the double soft limit of the R-term into a suitable form for antenna subtraction:

\displaystyle\bm{R}_{4}^{0}\xrightarrow{k,l\to 0}\big(

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle S_{1k2}S_{1li}+S_{1k2}S_{1lj}+S_{1k2}S_{2li}-S_{1k2}S_{2lj}-2S_{1k2}S_{ilj}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle S_{1ki}S_{1l2}+S_{1ki}S_{1lj}+S_{1ki}S_{2lj}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle S_{1kj}S_{1l2}+S_{1kj}S_{1li}-2S_{1kj}S_{1lj}-S_{1kj}S_{2li}+2S_{1kj}S_{ilj}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle S_{2ki}S_{1l2}-S_{2ki}S_{1lj}-2S_{2ki}S_{2li}+S_{2ki}S_{2lj}+2S_{2ki}S_{ilj}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle S_{2kj}S_{1l2}+S_{2kj}S_{1li}+S_{2kj}S_{2li}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle 2S_{ikj}S_{1l2}+2S_{ikj}S_{1lj}+2S_{ikj}S_{2li}-2S_{ikj}S_{ilj}\big)B_{2}^{0}(1,i,j,2)+(i\leftrightarrow j)

\displaystyle+\big(

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle 2S_{1kl2}-2S_{1lk2}

(161)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 2S_{1k2}S_{1l2}-2S_{1k2}S_{ilj}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle S_{1ki}S_{2li}+S_{1ki}S_{2lj}+S_{1ki}S_{ilj}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle S_{1kj}S_{2li}-S_{1kj}S_{2lj}+S_{1kj}S_{ilj}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle S_{2ki}S_{1li}+S_{2ki}S_{1lj}+S_{2ki}S_{ilj}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle S_{2kj}S_{1li}-S_{2kj}S_{1lj}+S_{2kj}S_{ilj}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle 2S_{ikj}S_{1l2}+S_{ikj}S_{1li}+S_{ikj}S_{1lj}+S_{ikj}S_{2li}+S_{ikj}S_{2lj}-2S_{ikj}S_{ilj}\big)

\displaystyle\times\tilde{B}_{2,R}^{0}(1,i,j,2),

where we see that all the products of the single and double soft currents at amplitude level have been re-expressed in terms of only iterated single eikonal factors and double soft factors at the squared matrix element level. The reduced amplitudes have been squared to leading colour functions
B_{2}^{0}
or are present as the interference matrix element
\tilde{B}_{2,R}^{0}
. Each soft factor can now be promoted to a corresponding antenna function to obtain the first candidate subtraction for the R-term. Focusing only at the double soft factors, we see that we get the following
X_{4}^{0}
terms:

\displaystyle-2A_{4}^{0}(1,k,l,2)\tilde{B}_{2,R}^{0}([1],i,j,[2])\quad\mbox{and}\quad-2A_{4}^{0}(1,l,k,2)\tilde{B}_{2,R}^{0}([1],i,j,[2]).

(162)

If we also promote the
2\,S_{1k2}S_{1l2}\tilde{B}_{2,R}^{0}(1,i,j,2)
term to
2\,\tilde{A}_{4}^{0}(1,k,l,2)\tilde{B}_{2,R}^{0}([1],i,j,[2])
, we see that we end up with exactly the correct structure of
X_{4}^{0}
terms we needed to subtract all the triple collinear limits of the R-term. The construction of
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,b_{1}}
has been completed, and having found all the functions to account for the soft limits, the construction for the rest of
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S}
can continue in the same manner as for the other B-type matrix elements. Dressing the
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,b_{1}}
terms with
X_{3}^{0}X_{3}^{0}
, adding
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,a}
and the newly
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,c}
and
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,d}
terms obtained from (
3.2.5
), all the single collinear limits of the R-term are also correctly reproduced. Due to the cross talk of the several terms with mapped momenta, the soft limits are however not correctly subtracted anymore, but this is fixed by the usual process of adding LAS terms
Currie:2013vh
, thereby completing the construction of
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S}
for the R-term.

3.2.6
Tests of the RR subtraction term

To assess how well the subtraction term
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S}
approaches the matrix element
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{RR}
that it is supposed to subtract in unresolved limits, we take the double soft gluons and
q\|g\|g
triple collinear limits for the R-term as examples. The validity of the subtraction term is tested as described in
NigelGlover:2010kwr
by generating a number of phase space points approaching the considered IR limit at
\sqrt{s}=1000
GeV, and for each point the following ratio is calculated:

\displaystyle X_{RR}=\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma^{RR}}{\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S}}.

(163)

This ratio is expected to converge to unity as the points go deeper into the IR limit. The depth of the IR divergence is controlled by a variable
x
, which is defined in terms of momenta invariants in a suitable way for each considered unresolved limit. For the exemplary double soft
i,j\to 0
and triple collinear
1\|i\|j
limits in the
1,2\rightarrow i,j,k,l
scattering processes,
x
is defined as
x=1-s_{kl}/s
and
x=s_{1ij}/s
respectively. Three different values for
x
are considered for each limit, with smaller
x
representing larger IR depth. The results for
X_{RR}
are histogrammed and shown in Figure
1
where the number of points which fall outside the plot range are also denoted. For both limits we observe narrower spikes around unity and less outliers as
x
becomes smaller, meaning the subtraction term mimics the matrix element better as the points become closer to the IR singularity. After the full subtraction term
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S}
successfully produces sufficiently narrow spikes in the spike plots for each possible unresolved limit,
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S}
can be considered a working subtraction term.

:

\mathrm{d}\sigma^{\mathrm{RR}}/\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S}
verifying the correct behaviour of the subtraction terms for the R-term in the double soft gluons (left) and triple collinear
q\|g\|g
(right) limits.

4
Real-virtual subtraction

All five-parton one-loop matrix elements that were introduced in Section
2
enter the real-virtual contribution to the dijet cross section. The antenna subtraction terms that are required for their numerical implementation are discussed in the following, focusing in particular on novel aspects that first appear for processes with four external partons at Born level.

Since the RV corrections are
(n+1)
-parton matrix elements, we now only need to consider single unresolved limits. For the one-loop matrix elements we have the following factorization in soft limits
Bern:1999ry
;
Catani:2000pi
:

\displaystyle M_{n+1}^{1}(\dots,a,b,c,\dots)\xrightarrow{b\to 0}S_{abc}M_{n}^{1}(\dots,a,c,\dots)+S_{abc}^{(1)}(\epsilon)M_{n}^{0}(\dots,a,c,\dots),

(164)

and similarly for collinear limits
Kosower:1999rx
;
Bern:1999ry
:

\displaystyle M_{n+1}^{1}(\dots,i,j,\dots)\xrightarrow{i\|j}\frac{P_{ij\to K}}{s_{ij}}M_{n}^{1}(\dots,K,\dots)+\frac{P_{ij\to K}^{(1)}}{s_{ij}}M_{n}^{0}(\dots,K,\dots),

(165)

where we have the same unresolved factors from the tree-level factorization, but now factoring onto a reduced one-loop matrix element, and additional terms which have the form of a one-loop soft factor
Bern:1998sc
or one-loop splitting function
Bern:1999ry
;
Bern:1994zx
times a reduced tree-level matrix element. In the antenna subtraction method, these unresolved limits of the RV corrections are subtracted in the same way by “tree times loop” (
T\times L
)
X_{3}^{0}M_{n}^{1}
terms and “loop times tree” (
L\times T
)
X_{3}^{1}M_{n}^{0}
terms, where
X_{3}^{1}
is a one-loop antenna function
Gehrmann-DeRidder:2005btv
. Besides mimicking the behaviour of the matrix element in unresolved limits, the subtraction term also needs to cancel the explicit
\epsilon
-poles of the one-loop matrix element, which can be obtained from the relevant one-loop amplitude expressions in Section
2
.

Similar as for the RR level, we partition the whole subtraction term into the following parts
Currie:2013vh
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\sigma^{T}=\mathrm{d}\sigma^{T,a}+\mathrm{d}\sigma^{T,b}+\mathrm{d}\sigma^{T,c}+\mathrm{d}\sigma^{T,e},

(166)

and briefly describe each part:

•

\mathrm{d}\sigma^{T,a}
: this part consists of the integrated
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,a}
terms:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\sigma^{T,a}\supset\mathcal{N}_{RV}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{1}}{x_{1}}\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{2}}{x_{2}}\mathrm{d}\Phi_{n+1}\,\mathcal{X}_{3}^{0}(s_{ij})M_{n+1}^{0}(\{p_{n+1}\})J_{n}^{(n+1)}(\{p_{n+1}\}),

(167)

and removes the explicit
\epsilon
-poles of the RV squared matrix element. The calligraphic
\mathcal{X}_{3}^{0}
denotes an integrated antenna and the reduced matrix elements here are
(n+1)
-parton squared matrix elements which can
develop single unresolved limits. These limits, not associated with the RV matrix element, have to be taken care of by subtraction terms in the other parts.
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{T,a}
combines with
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{\text{MF},1}
to yield the single integrated dipole factors
\mathcal{J}_{2}^{(1)}
that are listed in Appendix
B
, multiplying the reduced squared matrix elements.

•

\mathrm{d}\sigma^{T,b}
: this part is responsible for subtracting the single unresolved limits of the RV matrix element. The one-loop antennae and reduced matrix elements can contain explicit
\epsilon
-poles which are not associated to the RV matrix element. These poles are removed by terms of the form
\mathcal{X}_{3}^{0}X_{3}^{0}M_{n}^{0}
. We thus further partition
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{T,b}
into
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{T,b_{1}}
and
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{T,b_{2}}
parts, corresponding to the
L\times T
,
T\times L
terms and the terms cancelling the poles of the one-loop antennae and reduced matrix elements:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\sigma^{T,b_{1}}

\displaystyle\supset

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{RV}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{1}}{x_{1}}\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{2}}{x_{2}}\mathrm{d}\Phi_{n+1}\,X_{3}^{1}(\{p_{n+1}\})\delta(1-x_{1})\delta(1-x_{2})M_{n}^{0}(\{\tilde{p}_{n}\})J_{n}^{(n)}(\{\tilde{p}_{n}\})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{RV}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{1}}{x_{1}}\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{2}}{x_{2}}\mathrm{d}\Phi_{n+1}\,X_{3}^{0}(\{p_{n+1}\})\delta(1-x_{1})\delta(1-x_{2})M_{n}^{1}(\{\tilde{p}_{n}\})J_{n}^{(n)}(\{\tilde{p}_{n}\}),

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\sigma^{T,b_{2}}

\displaystyle\supset

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{RV}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{1}}{x_{1}}\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{2}}{x_{2}}\mathrm{d}\Phi_{n+1}\,\mathcal{X}_{3}^{0}(s_{ij})X_{3}^{0}(\{p_{n+1}\})M_{n}^{0}(\{\tilde{p}_{n}\})J_{n}^{(n)}(\{\tilde{p}_{n}\}).

(168)

The one-loop antennae in the
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{T,b_{1}}
subtraction are renormalized at the scale of the invariant mass of their antenna partons
s_{abc}
, while the one-loop matrix elements are renormalized at the renormalization scale
\mu_{r}^{2}
. This mismatch is corrected by the following replacement:

\displaystyle X_{3}^{1}(a,b,c)\to X_{3}^{1}(a,b,c)+\frac{b_{0}}{\epsilon}X_{3}^{0}(a,b,c)\left(\left(\frac{|s_{abc}|}{\mu_{r}^{2}}\right)^{-\epsilon}-1\right).

(169)

•

\mathrm{d}\sigma^{T,c}
: this part cancels the single unresolved limits of the reduced squared matrix elements in
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{T,a}
and consists of terms of the form:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\sigma^{T,c}\supset\mathcal{N}_{RV}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{1}}{x_{1}}\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{2}}{x_{2}}\mathrm{d}\Phi_{n+1}\,\mathcal{X}_{3}^{0}(s_{ij})X_{3}^{0}(\{p_{n+1}\})M_{n}^{0}(\{\tilde{p}_{n}\})J_{n}^{(n)}(\{\tilde{p}_{n}\}).

(170)

These terms partly originate from integration of
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,c}
terms over the antenna phase space of the first
X_{3}^{0}
, and partly of terms newly introduced here, which will consequently be subtracted again at the double virtual level.

•

\mathrm{d}\sigma^{T,e}
: the soft functions in the LAS terms are integrated and added back at the RV level:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\sigma^{T,e}\supset\mathcal{N}_{RV}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{1}}{x_{1}}\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{2}}{x_{2}}\mathrm{d}\Phi_{n+1}\,\mathcal{S}^{FF}X_{3}^{0}(\{p_{n+1}\})M_{n}^{0}(\{\tilde{p}_{n}\})J_{n}^{(n)}(\{\tilde{p}_{n}\}).

(171)

Note the absence of a
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{T,d}
part corresponding to integration of
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,d}
, since the colour-disconnected terms are usually added back at the VV level.

Novel features of the RV subtraction terms for dijet processes are discussed on two examples in the following.

4.1
RV subtraction for the subsubleading colour C-type matrix element

We first discuss the antenna subtraction for the subsubleading colour (SSLC) C-type matrix element
\tilde{\tilde{C}}_{1}^{1}(q,\bar{q},Q,\bar{Q},g)
defined in (
2.3.7
), as this matrix element still admits a relatively compact form of its subtraction terms while
already exposing some of the key features of the RV subtraction. For definiteness, we take the crossing where
q,\bar{Q}
are in the initial state and use the notational abbreviation
(1,i,j,2,k)=(q,\bar{q},Q,\bar{Q},g)
in this section.

The first step is to cancel the explicit
\epsilon
-poles of the matrix element by
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{T,a}
. The pole structure for the full SSLC level is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{Poles}\big[\tilde{\tilde{C}}_{1}^{1}(1,i,j,2,k)\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\,\big[I_{q\bar{q}}^{(1)}(\epsilon,s_{1i})+I_{q\bar{q}}^{(1)}(\epsilon,s_{2j})+2I_{q\bar{q}}^{(1)}(\epsilon,s_{12})+2I_{q\bar{q}}^{(1)}(\epsilon,s_{ij})

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle 2I_{q\bar{q}}^{(1)}(\epsilon,s_{1j})-2I_{q\bar{q}}^{(1)}(\epsilon,s_{2i})\big]C_{1,\gamma}^{0}(1,i,j,2,k)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle 2\,\big[I_{q\bar{q}}^{(1)}(\epsilon,s_{1i})+I_{q\bar{q}}^{(1)}(\epsilon,s_{2j})+I_{q\bar{q}}^{(1)}(\epsilon,s_{12})+I_{q\bar{q}}^{(1)}(\epsilon,s_{ij})

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle I_{q\bar{q}}^{(1)}(\epsilon,s_{1j})-I_{q\bar{q}}^{(1)}(\epsilon,s_{2i})\big]\big(C_{1,c}^{0}(1,i,j,2,k)+C_{1,d}^{0}(1,i,j,2,k)\big).

This expression can directly be used to construct
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{T,a}
by using the known pole structures of the integrated three-parton antenna functions
Gehrmann-DeRidder:2005btv
and matching the poles. Taking also the mass factorization terms into account, we obtain the general expression:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\sigma^{T,a}=\sum_{i,j}c_{ij}\mathcal{J}_{2}^{(1)}(i,j)M_{5}^{0},

(173)

where
c_{ij}
denote numerical coefficients and
\mathcal{J}_{2}^{(1)}
are the single integrated dipoles given in Appendix
B
which contain integrated
X_{3}^{0}
antennae and mass factorization kernels. One could then also unintegrate
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{T,a}
to obtain
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{S,a}
which subtracts all single unresolved limits of the corresponding matrix element which forms the double real correction (
\tilde{\tilde{C}}_{2}^{0}
in this example). This approach from using the infrared singularity structure of the virtual corrections to construct the virtual subtraction term first, and subsequently unintegrating to obtain the real subtraction term
Chen:2022ktf
works well at NLO due to its relative simplicity.

After achieving the explicit pole cancellation, we look at the single unresolved limits of the RV matrix element. The single collinear quark-antiquark limits of
\tilde{\tilde{C}}_{1}^{1}(q,\bar{q},Q,\bar{Q},g)
are easily reproduced by the following terms:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\sigma^{T,b_{1}}\supset

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}E_{3,qq^{\prime}}^{0}(1,j,2)\tilde{\tilde{B}}_{2}^{1}([1],k,[2],i)

(174)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}E_{3,q^{\prime}}^{0}(i,j,2)\tilde{\tilde{B}}_{2}^{1}(1,k,[2],[i,j])

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}E_{3,q^{\prime}}^{0}(j,1,i)\tilde{\tilde{B}}_{2}^{1}([j,i],k,[1],2)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}E_{3,qq^{\prime}}^{0}(2,1,i)\tilde{\tilde{B}}_{2}^{1}(j,k,[1],[2])

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\tilde{E}_{3,qq^{\prime}}^{1}(1,j,2)\bar{B}_{2}^{0}([1],k,[2],i)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\tilde{E}_{3,q^{\prime}}^{1}(i,j,2)\bar{B}_{2}^{0}(1,k,[2],[i,j])

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\tilde{E}_{3,q^{\prime}}^{1}(j,1,i)\bar{B}_{2}^{0}([j,i],k,[1],2)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\tilde{E}_{3,qq^{\prime}}^{1}(2,1,i)\bar{B}_{2}^{0}(j,k,[1],[2]).

The unresolved limits involving the gluon, especially the soft limit, are less straightforward due to similar reasons as for the double real subtraction. Here we make use of the known pole structures of the antenna functions and matrix element to find the correct subtraction terms for the soft gluon limit. To construct these subtraction terms we take the soft gluon
k
limit of (
4.1
). For a generic five-parton one-loop colour-ordered matrix element, one then obtains an expression of a sum of products of infrared singularity operators
I^{(1)}
, eikonal factors
S
and four-parton reduced tree-level matrix elements:

\displaystyle M_{5}^{1}(1,i,j,2,k)\xrightarrow[k\,\mathrm{soft}]{\mathrm{sing.}}\sum_{a,b\in\{1,i,j,2\}}\sum_{c,d\in\{1,i,j,k,2\}}\sum_{e}C_{abcde}S_{akb}I_{pp^{\prime}\in\{q,g\}}^{(1)}(\epsilon,s_{cd})M^{0}_{4,e},

where
C_{abcde}
contains the relevant colour factors and integer multiplicity factors, depending on the involved partons and reduced matrix element. For the case of the SSLC C-type matrix element in this example, this expression can still be written in a relatively compact form:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Poles}\big[\tilde{\tilde{C}}_{1}^{1}(1,i,j,2,k)\big]\big|_{k\to 0}=

\displaystyle+\big[2I_{q\bar{q}}^{(1)}(\epsilon,s_{1i})+2I_{q\bar{q}}^{(1)}(\epsilon,s_{2j})+4I_{q\bar{q}}^{(1)}(\epsilon,s_{12})+4I_{q\bar{q}}^{(1)}(\epsilon,s_{ij})-4I_{q\bar{q}}^{(1)}(\epsilon,s_{1j})-4I_{q\bar{q}}^{(1)}(\epsilon,s_{2i})\big]

\displaystyle\times\big(S_{1ki}+S_{2kj}\big)C_{0}^{0}(1,2,j,i)

\displaystyle+\big[4I_{q\bar{q}}^{(1)}(\epsilon,s_{1i})+4I_{q\bar{q}}^{(1)}(\epsilon,s_{2j})+6I_{q\bar{q}}^{(1)}(\epsilon,s_{12})+6I_{q\bar{q}}^{(1)}(\epsilon,s_{ij})-6I_{q\bar{q}}^{(1)}(\epsilon,s_{1j})-6I_{q\bar{q}}^{(1)}(\epsilon,s_{2i})\big]

\displaystyle\times\big(S_{1k2}+S_{ikj}-S_{1kj}-S_{2ki}\big)C_{0}^{0}(1,2,j,i).

(176)

The above expression has to be reproduced by the
L\times T
and
T\times L
subtraction terms in the same soft limit: eikonal factors by the antenna functions and the five-parton reduced matrix elements and the
I^{(1)}
functions by the one-loop antennae in the
L\times T
terms as well as the one-loop matrix elements in the

In our approach of constructing the antenna subtraction terms from the known IR divergent behaviour of the RR and RV squared matrix elements, the analytic pole cancellation in the VV contribution provides a strong cross-check on the correctness of the subtraction terms at the RR and RV levels. This argument could be turned around by starting the construction of the subtraction terms at the VV level, by matching the known pole structure of two-loop squared matrix elements and mass factorization terms onto a combination of integrated dipole factors. A systematic unintegration of these dipole factors
Chen:2022ktf
could then yield the RV and RR antenna subtraction terms in a systematic and algorithmic manner.

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we documented the details of the NNLO QCD calculation in full colour for jet production observables at the LHC
Chen:2022tpk
using the antenna subtraction method. All the relevant matrix elements were listed in a colour-decomposed manner, along with several properties such as their colour connections and pole structures. The use of the traditional colour-ordered antenna subtraction method to construct the NNLO subtraction terms starting from the double real and ending at the double virtual level was summarized. Several complications within this approach that arise due to the more involved structure of the high parton multiplicity matrix elements at the subleading colour levels were highlighted and explicit examples of the RR, RV and VV subtractions were discussed to show how to subtract all unresolved limits and cancel all the
\epsilon
-poles without needing new antenna functions or performing new analytic integrals. Building upon earlier developments at leading colour
Currie:2013vh
, we introduced the full set of integrated dipole terms for full colour, which allow the infrared pole cancellation between integrated subtraction terms, mass factorization terms and virtual matrix elements to be structured in a compact and modular manner.

Although successful, with phenomenological results reported in
Chen:2022tpk
, this paper also illustrates the need for an automated method to apply the antenna subtraction at NNLO in full colour, considering both the more involved structure of subleading colour contributions and that the complexity only increases as the multiplicity of coloured particles in the process grows. This has motivated the development of the colourful antenna subtraction method, a reformulation of the antenna subtraction where the predictability of the pole structures of loop-amplitudes in colour space is used to generate the subtraction terms in an automated procedure starting at the double virtual and cascading to the double real level. Progress in this colourful antenna subtraction method has already been made and applied for gluonic three-jet production from proton-proton collisions and continues to be developed
Chen:2022ktf
.

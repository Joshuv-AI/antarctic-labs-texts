Primal-Proximity Spaces
Ahmad Al-Omari, Murad Özkoç, Santanu Acharjee, e-mail: omarimutah1@yahoo.com, murad.ozkoc@mu.edu.tr

Abstract
The main purpose of this paper is to introduce and study the primal-proximity spaces. Also, we define two new operators via primal proximity spaces and investigate some of their fundamental properties. In addition, we obtain a new topology, which is weaker than old one, via these new operators. Moreover, we not only discuss some of their properties but also enrich with some examples. 2010 AMS Classifications: 54E05, 54A05, 54A10, 54B99. Keywords: Primal, primal-proximity, Kuratowski closure operator, primal topological space

Definition 2.1
.

Let

Remark 3.3
.

Let
X
be a non-emptyset and
A\subseteq X
such that
\mathcal{P}=2^{X}\setminus\{X\}.

1.

If
x\in A
, then
\{x\}\hookrightarrow A.

2.

If
A\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}B

Suppose

(A\cap B)^{c}\in\mathcal{P}

It follows that

Example 3.7
.

Let
(X,\tau,\mathcal{P})
be a primal topological space such that
\mathcal{P}=2^{X}\setminus\{X\}.
Let
(X,\tau)
be a normal space and

. Define a binary relation

\hookrightarrow

A\hookrightarrow B\Leftrightarrow\left(cl(A)\cap cl(B)\right)^{c}\in\mathcal{P},

Proof.

(1)

A\hookrightarrow B\Leftrightarrow\left(cl(A)\cap cl(B)\right)^{c}\in\mathcal{P}\Leftrightarrow\left(cl(B)\cap cl(A)\right)^{c}\in\mathcal{P}\Leftrightarrow B\hookrightarrow A
.

(2)

Let

\begin{array}[]{rcl}A\hookrightarrow(B\cup C)&\Leftrightarrow&\left(cl(A)\cap cl(B\cup C)\right)^{c}\in\mathcal{P}\\
&\Leftrightarrow&\left(cl(A)\cap\left(cl(B)\cup cl(C)\right)\right)^{c}\in\mathcal{P}\\
&\Leftrightarrow&\left(\left(cl(A)\cap cl(B)\right)\cup\left(cl(A)\cap cl(C)\right)\right)^{c}\in\mathcal{P}\\
&\Leftrightarrow&\left(cl(A)\cap cl(B)\right)^{c}\cap\left(cl(A)\cap cl(C)\right)^{c}\in\mathcal{P}\\
&\Leftrightarrow&\left(cl(A)\cap cl(B)\right)^{c}\in\mathcal{P}\text{ or }\left(cl(A)\cap cl(C)\right)^{c}\in\mathcal{P}\\
&\Leftrightarrow&A\hookrightarrow B\text{ or }A\hookrightarrow C.\end{array}

(3)

Let
A\hookrightarrow B.

\left.\begin{array}[]{rr}A\hookrightarrow B\Leftrightarrow\left(cl(A)\cap cl(B)\right)^{c}\in\mathcal{P}\Rightarrow\left(cl(A)\right)^{c}\in\mathcal{P}\\
\mathcal{P}=2^{X}\setminus\{X\}\end{array}\right\}\Rightarrow\left(cl(A)\right)^{c}\neq X

\left.\begin{array}[]{rr}\Rightarrow cl(A)\neq\emptyset\Rightarrow A\neq\emptyset\Rightarrow A^{c}\neq X\\
\mathcal{P}=2^{X}\setminus\{X\}\end{array}\right\}\Rightarrow A^{c}\in\mathcal{P}.

(4)

Let
A\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}B.

\left.\begin{array}[]{rr}A\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}B\Rightarrow\left(cl(A)\cap cl(B)\right)^{c}\notin\mathcal{P}\\
\mathcal{P}=2^{X}\setminus\{X\}\end{array}\right\}\Rightarrow\left(cl(A)\cap cl(B)\right)^{c}=X

\left.\begin{array}[]{rr}\Rightarrow cl(A)\cap cl(B)=\emptyset\Rightarrow A\cap B=\emptyset\Rightarrow(A\cap B)^{c}=X\\
\mathcal{P}=2^{X}\setminus\{X\}\end{array}\right\}\Rightarrow(A\cap B)^{c}\notin\mathcal{P}.

cl(C)\cap cl(B)=\emptyset
and
\left(cl(C)\cap cl(B)\right)^{c}\notin\mathcal{P}.
Hence,
C\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}B
.
Let
E=C^{c}
. Then,
A\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}B
implies that there exists a subset
E
such that
A\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}E
and
E^{c}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}B
and
\left(E\cap E^{c}\right)^{c}\notin\mathcal{P}
.∎

4
Point-Primal Proximity Operator

This section introduces point-primal proximity operator. Here, we study several properties of a primal-proximity space using this operator.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(X,\hookrightarrow)
be a primal-proximity space. Then, the operator
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{(\cdot)}:2^{X}\to 2^{X}
defined by
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}:=\{x\in X|\{x\}\hookrightarrow A\}
is said to be point-primal proximity operator. Moreover,
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}
is said to be point-primal proximity of
A
.

We now provide the following lemma without the proof.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a primal on a non-emptyset
X.
If
A\hookrightarrow B,

A\subseteq C,
and
B\subseteq D,
then
C\hookrightarrow D.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
(X,\hookrightarrow,\mathcal{P})
be a primal-proximity space and
A,B\subseteq X
. If
B\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}A,
then
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}\subseteq B^{c}
.

Proof.

Suppose
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}\cap B\neq\emptyset
. Then there exists at least a point
x\in\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}\cap B
. So
x\in\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}
and
x\in B
, i.e.

\{x\}\hookrightarrow A
and
\{x\}\subseteq B
by Lemma
4.2
implies that
A\hookrightarrow B
, which is a contradiction. Hence,

\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}\subseteq B^{c}
.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
(X,\hookrightarrow,\mathcal{P})
be a primal-proximity space and
A,B\subseteq X
. If

B\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}A,
then
B\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}
.

Proof.

Let
B\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}A.
Then by (5) of Definition
3.1
there exist

C,D\subseteq X
such that
B\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}C^{c}
,
D^{c}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}A
and
(C\cap D)^{c}\notin\mathcal{P}
.
This result, combined with Lemma
4.3
, implies that
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}\subseteq D
. Now, we want
to prove that
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}\subseteq C^{c}
. Let
x\in\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}
, then
\{x\}\hookrightarrow A
. Suppose
x\in C
then implies that

x\in C\cap D
and
(C\cap D)^{c}\subseteq X\setminus\{x\}
, so
X\setminus\{x\}\notin\mathcal{P}
. Then, by Definition
3.1
(3), we have
\{x\}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}A
, which a contradiction. Hence,
x\in C^{c}
. So

\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}\subseteq C^{c}
. Now, we have by Lemma
4.2

B\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}
. Hence, the theorem is proven.
∎

Due to Theorem
4.4
and (1) of Definition
3.1
, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
(X,\hookrightarrow,\mathcal{P})
be a primal-proximity space and
A,B\subseteq X
. If

B\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}A,
then
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{B}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}
.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
(X,\hookrightarrow,\mathcal{P})
be a primal-proximity space and
A,B\subseteq X
. Then, the following properties hold:

1.

if
A\subseteq B,
then
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}\subseteq\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{B};

2.

\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{(A\cap B)}\subseteq\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}\cap\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{B};

3.

\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}\cup\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{B}=\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{(A\cup B)};

4.

\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}}\subseteq\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A};

5.

if
A^{c}\notin\mathcal{P},
then
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}=\emptyset;

6.

\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{\emptyset}=\emptyset;

7.

\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}\setminus\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{B}\subseteq\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{(A\setminus B)};

8.

if
B^{c}\notin\mathcal{P}
, then
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{(A\cup B)}=\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}=\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{(A\setminus B)};

9.

if
[(A\setminus B)\cup(B\setminus A)]^{c}\notin\mathcal{P}
, then
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}=\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{B}.

Proof.

1) Let
A\subseteq B
and
x\in\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}.
Then
\{x\}\hookrightarrow A.
Since
A\subseteq B,
by Lemma
4.2
, we have
\{x\}\hookrightarrow B.
Hence,
x\in\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{B}.

2) Let
A,B\subseteq X.
From
(1)
, it is not difficult to see that
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{(A\cap B)}\subseteq\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}
and
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{(A\cap B)}\subseteq\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{B}
. Thus, we get
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{(A\cap B)}\subseteq\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}\cap\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{B}.

3) Let
A,B\subseteq X.
From
(1)
, we can easily find that
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}\subseteq\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{(A\cup B)}
and
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{B}\subseteq\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{(A\cup B)}
. Thus, obviously
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}\cup\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{B}\subseteq\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{(A\cup B)}
.

Conversely, let
y\in\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{(A\cup B)}
. Then,
\{y\}\hookrightarrow A\cup B
. Due to Definition 3.1, either
\{y\}\hookrightarrow A
or
\{y\}\hookrightarrow B
. It indicates that either
y\in\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}
or
y\in\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{B}
. So, we can conclude that
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{(A\cup B)}\subseteq\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}\cup\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{B}
.

4) Let
A\subseteq X
and let
x\notin\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}
. Then,
\{x\}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}A
. So, by Theorem
4.4

we have
\{x\}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}
. Hence,
x\notin\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}}
. Thus, we get

\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}}\subseteq\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}
.

5) Let
A^{c}\notin\mathcal{P}.
Then, by (3) of Definition
3.1
,
A\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}B
for all subsets
B
of
X.
Therefore, we have
A\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}\{x\}
for all
x\in X.
Again, by (1) of Definition
3.1
,
\{x\}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}A
for all
x\in X.
This means
x\notin\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}
for all
x\in X.
Hence,
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}=\emptyset.

6) Let
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{\emptyset}\neq\emptyset.
Thus, we assume that
x\in\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{\emptyset}
. Hence,
\{x\}\hookrightarrow\emptyset
. By (1) of Definition 3.1,
\emptyset\hookrightarrow\{x\}
. Again, by (3) of Definition 3.1,
\emptyset^{c}=X\in\mathcal{P}
; which is a contradiction to the definition of primal
\mathcal{P}
. Hence,
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{\emptyset}=\emptyset.

7) For all
A,B\subseteq X
,
A=(A\setminus B)\cup(A\cap B)
, by (3) we have
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}=\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{(A\setminus B})\cup\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{(A\cap B)}\subseteq\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{(A\setminus B})\cup\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{B}
. Hence,
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}\setminus\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{B}\subseteq\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{(A\setminus B})
.

8) If
B^{c}\notin\mathcal{P}
, then
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{(A\cup B)}=\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}\cup\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{B}=\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}\cup\emptyset=\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}
. Also,
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}\setminus\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{B}\subseteq\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{(A\setminus B})
, then
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}\subseteq\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{(A\setminus B)}
. And
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{(A\setminus B})=\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{(A\cap B^{c})}\subseteq\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}\cap\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{B^{c}}\subseteq\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}
.
Hence,
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{(A\cup B)}=\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}=\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{(A\setminus B)}.

9) If
[(A\setminus B)\cup(B\setminus A)]^{c}\notin\mathcal{P}
, then
(A\setminus B)^{c}\notin\mathcal{P}
and
(B\setminus A)^{c}\notin\mathcal{P}
. Since
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}=\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{[(A\setminus B)\cup(A\cap B)]}

and
(A\setminus B)^{c}\notin\mathcal{P}
, by using (8)
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}=\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{(A\cap B)}\subseteq\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{B}.
It follows that
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}\subseteq\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{B}
.
Similarly, since
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{B}=\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{[(B\setminus A)\cup(B\cap A)]}

and
(B\setminus A)^{c}\notin\mathcal{P}
, by using (8)
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{B}=\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{(B\cap A)}\subseteq\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}.
It follows that
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{B}\subseteq\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}
.
Hence,
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}=\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{B}
.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

Let
(X,\hookrightarrow,\mathcal{P})
be a primal-proximity space and
A\subseteq X
. The inclusion
A\subseteq\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}
need not be true in general as shown by following example.

Example 4.8
.

Let
X=\{a,b,c\}
,
\mathcal{P}=\{\emptyset,\{b\},\{c\},\{b,c\}\}
and the binary relation
\hookrightarrow
on
2^{X}
defined as Example
3.6
. For the subset
A=\{b\},
we have
A=\{b\}\nsubseteq\emptyset=\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
(X,\hookrightarrow,\mathcal{P})
be a primal-proximity space and
A,B\subseteq X
. Then, the following statements hold:

1.

A\cap\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{B}=\emptyset
, for all
A^{c}\notin\mathcal{P}
and
B\subseteq X
,

2.

\{x\}\hookrightarrow X
for all
x\in X
if and only if
\mathcal{P}=2^{X}\setminus\{X\}
.

3.

if
\mathcal{P}=2^{X}\setminus\{X\},
then
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{X}=X.

Proof.

(1) Let
A^{c}\notin\mathcal{P}
and suppose
A\cap\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{B}\neq\emptyset.
It follows that
A\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}B
since
A^{c}\notin\mathcal{P}
and also
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{B}\nsubseteq A^{c}
. Hence, by Lemma
4.3
we have

A\hookrightarrow B
, which is a contradiction. Thus,
A\cap\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{B}=\emptyset
.

(2) If
\{x\}\hookrightarrow X
for all
x\in X
, then by (3) of Corollary
3.4
, we have

\{x\}^{c}\in\mathcal{P}
for all
x\in X
. Hence,
\mathcal{P}=2^{X}\setminus\{X\}
. Conversely, if

\mathcal{P}=2^{X}\setminus\{X\}
, then
(\{x\}\cap X)^{c}=(\{x\})^{c}\in\mathcal{P}
and by (4) of Definition
3.1
, we have
\{x\}\hookrightarrow X
for all
x\in X
.

(3) Let
x\in X.
Since
\mathcal{P}=2^{X}\setminus\{X\},
then
(\{x\})^{c}=(\{x\}\cap X)^{c}\in\mathcal{P}
and by (4) of Definition
3.1
, we get
\{x\}\hookrightarrow X
for all
x\in X
. Hence,
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{X}=X.

∎

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
(X,\hookrightarrow,\mathcal{P})
be a primal-proximity space. If
A,B,C\subseteq X
and
B\subsetneqq C
such that

A\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}B
but
A\hookrightarrow C
, then
A\hookrightarrow(C\setminus B)
.

Proof.

Let
A,B,C\subseteq X
and
B\subsetneqq C
.. We consider
D=C\setminus B
. Since

A\hookrightarrow C
, then
A\hookrightarrow B\cup(C\setminus B)=B\cup D
.
Then by Definition
3.1
,
A\hookrightarrow B
or
A\hookrightarrow D
. Now,
A\hookrightarrow B
is not possible since we consider
A\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}B
. Then obviously,
A\hookrightarrow(C\setminus B)
.
∎

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
(X,\hookrightarrow,\mathcal{P})
be a primal-proximity space and
A,B\subseteq X
. If
A\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}B
, then there exists
C\subseteq X
such that
A\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}C
and
B\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}C^{c}
.

Proof.

Since
A\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}B
, thus by (5) of Definition 3.1, there exist
M,N\subseteq X
such that
A\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}M^{c}
,
N^{c}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}B
and
(M\cap N)^{c}\notin\mathcal{P}
. Let
M=X\setminus C
and
N=C
. Then,
(M\cap N)^{c}=X\notin\mathcal{P}
. Also,
A\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}(X\setminus C)^{c}
,
C^{c}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}B
. It yields
A\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}C
,
B\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}C^{c}
. Hence, the proof is completed.
∎

A\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}B

5
Proximal Closed Sets and
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{\tau}
Topology

In this section, proximal closed sets are defined. Moreover, various results between a primal-proximity space and a primal topological space are obtained using proximal closed sets and related notions.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
(X,\hookrightarrow,\mathcal{P})
be a primal-proximity space. Then, a subset
F
of
X
is called proximity-closed if and only if
\{x\}\hookrightarrow F
implies
x\in F
.

Lemma 5.2
.

If there is a point
x\in X
such that
A\hookrightarrow\{x\}
and
\{x\}\hookrightarrow B
, then
A\hookrightarrow B
.

Proof.

Suppose
A\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}B
, by Theorem
4.11
, there exists a subset
C
such that

A\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}C
and
C^{c}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}B
. Now, either
x\in C
or
x\in C^{c}
.

Case (1): If
x\in C
, then
A\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}\{x\}
. For if
A\hookrightarrow\{x\}
, then
A\hookrightarrow C
, by Lemma
4.2
which is a contradiction.

Case (2): If
x\in C^{c}
, then
\{x\}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}B
. Therefore, if
A\hookrightarrow\{x\}
and
\{x\}\hookrightarrow B
, then
A\hookrightarrow B
.
∎

Theorem 5.3
.

The collection of complements of all proximity-closed sets of
(X,\hookrightarrow,\mathcal{P})
forms a topology on
X
. This topology is denoted by
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{\tau}
.

Proof.

Since
X
and
\emptyset
are proximity-closed in
(X,\hookrightarrow,\mathcal{P})
, their complements
\emptyset
and
X
are in
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{\tau}
.

Let
\{F_{i}:i\in I\}
be a collection of proximity-closed sets. If
\{x\}\hookrightarrow\bigcap\{F_{i}:i\in I\},
then

\{x\}\hookrightarrow F_{i}
for every
i\in I
by Lemma
4.2
. Since
F_{i}
is proximity-closed,
x\in F_{i}
for every
i\in I
. Hence,
x\in\bigcap\{F_{i}:i\in I\}
and
\bigcap\{F_{i}:i\in I\}
is proximity-closed. Therefore, if
(X\setminus F_{i})\in\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{\tau}
for every
i\in I
, then
\bigcup\{X\setminus F_{i}:i\in I\}
is the complement of

\bigcap\{F_{i}:i\in I\}
which belongs to
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{\tau}
.

Finally, let
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
be two proximity-closed sets. If
\{x\}\hookrightarrow F_{1}\cup F_{2},
then
\{x\}\hookrightarrow F_{1}
or
\{x\}\hookrightarrow F_{2}
. Thus,

x\in F_{1}
or
x\in F_{2}
since
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are proximity-closed. This implies
x\in F_{1}\cup F_{2}
. Thus,
F_{1}\cup F_{2}
is proximity-closed.
Therefore, if
X\setminus F_{1}\in\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{\tau}
and
X\setminus F_{2}\in\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{\tau},
then

(X\setminus F_{1})\cap(X\setminus F_{2})=X\setminus(F_{1}\cup F_{2})\in\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{\tau}
. Hence,
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{\tau}
is
a topology on
X
.
∎

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
(X,\hookrightarrow,\mathcal{P})
be a primal-proximity space. The set
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}
is the closure of
A
where the closure is taken with respect to the topology
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{\tau}
and denoted by
cl_{\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{\tau}}(A).

Proof.

Let
x\in\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}.
Then
\{x\}\hookrightarrow A
. By Lemma
4.2
,
\{x\}\hookrightarrow cl_{\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{\tau}}(A)

since
A\subseteq cl_{\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{\tau}}(A)
and
cl_{\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{\tau}}(A)
is proximity-closed in
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{\tau}.
Thus,
x\in cl_{\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{\tau}}(A).
Hence,
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}\subseteq cl_{\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{\tau}}(A)
.

Conversely, let
x\notin\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}.
Then
\{x\}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}A
. By Theorem
4.11
, there exists a subset
C
such that
\{x\}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}C
and
C^{c}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}A
. Since there is no point of
C^{c}
which is related to

A
, then
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}\subseteq C
. By Lemma
4.2
,
\{x\}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}cl_{\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{\tau}}(A)
.
Thus,
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}
is proximity-closed in
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{\tau}
. Therefore,
cl_{\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{\tau}}(A)\subseteq\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}
. Hence,
cl_{\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{\tau}}(A)=\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}
.
∎

Definition 5.5
.

[
15
]

The the operator
\Phi:2^{X}\to 2^{X}
is a Kuratowski closure operator provided:

(1)

\Phi(\emptyset)=\emptyset
;

(2)

A\subseteq\Phi(A)
for every
A\in 2^{X}
;

(3)

\Phi(A\cup B)=\Phi(A)\cup\Phi(B)
for any
A,B\in 2^{X}
;

(4)

\Phi(\Phi(A))=\Phi(A)
for every
A\in 2^{X}
.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
(X,\hookrightarrow,\mathcal{P})
be a primal-proximity space such that
\mathcal{P}=2^{X}\setminus\{X\}
. Then, the operator

\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}:=\{x\in X|\{x\}\hookrightarrow A\}
on a primal-proximity space
(X,\hookrightarrow,\mathcal{P})

is a Kuratowski closure operator.

Proof.

(1) By (6) of Theorem
4.6
,
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{\emptyset}=\emptyset
.

(2) If
x\in A
, then
\{x\}\hookrightarrow A
. Hence,
x\in\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}
. This shows that
A\subseteq\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}
.

(3) By (3) of Theorem
4.6
,
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{(A\cup B)}=\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}\cup\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{B}
.

(4) By (4) of Theorem
4.6
, we have always
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}}\subseteq\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}.

Now, let
x\notin\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}}.
Then
\{x\}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}}.
By (4) of Corollary

3.4
, we have
\left(\{x\}\cap{\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}}\right)^{c}\notin\mathcal{P}.
Since
\mathcal{P}=2^{X}\setminus\{X\},
we get
\left(\{x\}\cap{\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}}\right)^{c}=X
which means that
\{x\}\cap{\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}}=\emptyset.
Thus, we have
x\notin{\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}}.
Hence,
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}\subseteq\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}}
and
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}}=\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}

which completes the proof and this topology is denoted by
\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{\tau}
.
∎

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
(X,\hookrightarrow,\mathcal{P})
be a primal-proximity space.
Then, the operator
cl^{*}:2^{X}\to 2^{X}

defined by
cl^{*}(A):=A\cup\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}
satisfies Kuratowski closure
axioms and induces a topology on
X
called
\tau^{*}
is given by
\tau^{*}=\{A\subseteq X|cl^{*}(A^{c})=A^{c}\}.

Proof.

(1) By (6) of Theorem
4.6
, we have
cl^{*}(\emptyset)=\emptyset\cup\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{\emptyset}=\emptyset
.

(2) Let
A\subseteq X.
Since
cl^{*}(A):=A\cup\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A},
we have
A\subseteq cl^{*}(A).

(3) Let
A,B\subseteq X.
By (3) of Theorem
4.6
, we have

\begin{array}[]{rcl}cl^{*}(A\cup B)&=&(A\cup B)\cup\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{(A\cup B)}\\
&=&(A\cup B)\cup\left(\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}\cup\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{B}\right)\\
&=&\left(A\cup\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}\right)\cup\left(B\cup\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{B}\right)\\
&=&cl^{*}(A)\cup cl^{*}{(B)}.\end{array}

(4) Let
A\subseteq X.
By (4) of Theorem
4.6
, we have

\begin{array}[]{rcl}cl^{*}(cl^{*}(A))&=&cl^{*}(A)\cup\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{cl^{*}(A)}\\
&=&\left(A\cup\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}\right)\cup\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{\left(A\cup\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}\right)}\\
&=&\left(A\cup\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}\right)\cup\left(\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}\cup\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}}\right)\\
&=&\left(A\cup\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}\right)\cup\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}\\
&=&A\cup\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}\\
&=&cl^{*}(A).\qed\end{array}

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
(X,\hookrightarrow,\mathcal{P})
be a primal-proximity space. Then the following properties hold:

1.

B\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}A
if and only if
B\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}cl^{*}(A)
.

2.

cl^{*}\left(\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}\right)=\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}
.

3.

cl^{*}\left(\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}\right)=\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{cl^{*}(A)}
.

Proof.

(1) Let
B\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}A.
Then, by Theorem
4.4
, we have
B\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}
. Hence, by (2) of Definition
3.1
,

B\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}(A\cup\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A})=cl^{*}(A)
if and only if
B\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}A
and
B\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}
.

(2) Let
A\subseteq X.
By (4) of Theorem
4.6
, we have

cl^{*}\left(\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}\right)=\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}\cup\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}}=\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}.

(3) Let

. By (3) of Theorem
4.6
, we have

cl^{*}\left(\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}\right)=\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}\cup\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}}=\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{\left(A\cup\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}\right)}=\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{cl^{*}(A)}.\qed

.

Let
(X,\hookrightarrow,\mathcal{P})
be a primal-proximity space and

If

Case 2:

Example 5.10
.

Let
(X,\tau,\mathcal{P})
be a primal topological space and
\hookrightarrow
be a binary relation on
2^{X}

defined as
A\hookrightarrow B
if and only if
(A\cap cl(B))^{c}\in\mathcal{P}
. Then
``\hookrightarrow"
is not a primal-proximity relation on

but satisfes (2),(3),(4) and (5) of Definition

. Hence, in this case
\tau\subseteq\tau^{*}
.

Proof.

We want to show that
cl^{*}(A)\subseteq cl(A)
for all
A\subseteq X
. Let
x\in cl^{*}(A)=A\cup\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}
. Then,
x\in A
or
x\in\overset{\operatorname{\hookrightarrow}}{A}
. If
x\in A
, then

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we introduced a new type of proximity space called primal-proximity space. Later, we defined point-primal proximity operator and investigated some of its fundamental properties. We also proved that this operator is a Kuratowski closure operator under special condition. Moreover, one more operator via point-primal proximity operator was defined. Furthermore, we gave not only some relationships but also several examples.

∎

Chaotic Dynamics and Zero Distribution: Implications for Yitang Zhang’s Landau–Siegel Zero Theorem
Zeraoulia Rafik, Alvaro Humberto Salas

Abstract
This study delves into the realm of chaotic dynamics derived from Dirichlet L-functions, drawing inspiration from Yitang Zhang’s groundbreaking work on Landau–Siegel zeros. The dynamic behavior reveals a profound chaos, corroborated by the calculated Lyapunov exponents and entropy, which attest to the system’s inherent unpredictability. The examination of stability further uncovers the instability of fixed points. Most notably, these findings unveil a compelling connection between the studied dynamics and the intricate distribution of zeros, thereby offering indirect support for Zhang’s groundbreaking theorem concerning Landau–Siegel zeros.

Introduction

In recent years, the landscape of number theory has been reshaped by remarkable advancements, with Yitang Zhang’s pioneering work on Landau–Siegel zeros standing out as a testament to the field’s dynamic evolution. Zhang’s contributions have taken center stage in the mathematical community, culminating in virtual talks at Shandong University and Peking University, where he presented his latest findings. Additionally, Zhang’s preprint
[
8
]
, which we explore in this study, introduces a pivotal result that asserts the existence of Landau–Siegel zeros. Specifically, for a real primitive character
\chi
modulo
D
, Zhang’s work reveals an intriguing inequality:

L(1,\chi)>c_{1}(\log D)^{-2022}

where
c_{1}>0
is an absolute, effectively computable constant. This result, assuming its correctness, raises significant questions about its implications in number theory.

In this context, our study explores a novel avenue: the dynamics of discrete maps derived from Dirichlet L-functions. We investigate the chaotic behavior of these dynamics, compute Lyapunov exponents, and determine the system’s entropy. Additionally, we examine the stability of fixed points using two distinct methods.

The intriguing aspect of our findings is their potential connection to the distribution of zeros of the Dirichlet L-function. While chaotic dynamics do not inherently imply a uniform distribution of zeros, we present evidence that these dynamics may indirectly support the assumption of Landau–Siegel zeros, bolstering Zhang’s groundbreaking theorem.

Our results contribute to the growing body of research that seeks to bridge the gap between chaotic dynamics and the distribution of zeros in the captivating landscape of number theory.

Main Results

2.1
Chaotic Dynamics of Yitang Dynamics 1

The Yitang Dynamics 1, defined by the equation:

x_{n+1}=\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{x_{n}}}+c\log(x_{n})^{-\alpha},\quad\chi(-1)=-1

(1)

shows both stability and chaotic behavior. The derivative at fixed points remains positive and equal to 1, indicating stability. However, the eigenvalues of the Jacobian matrix have a real part of approximately 196 and an imaginary part of approximately -3.58, suggesting potential chaotic behavior. The calculated entropy for this system is 0.3365, indicating high unpredictability and complexity.

2.2
Chaotic Dynamics of Yitang Dynamics 2

The Yitang Dynamics 2, defined by the equation:

x_{n+1}=\frac{\beta\log(|\epsilon|)}{\pi\sqrt{x_{n}}}+c\log(x_{n})^{-\alpha},\quad\chi(-1)=1

(2)

also exhibits both stability and chaotic behavior. The derivative at fixed points remains positive and equal to 1, indicating stability. Similar to Yitang Dynamics 1, the eigenvalues of the Jacobian matrix suggest potential chaotic behavior. The calculated entropy for this system is consistent at 0.3365, indicating high unpredictability.

2.3
Unimodal Distribution and Entropy

The approximate solutions for both Yitang Dynamics 1 and Yitang Dynamics 2 show a unimodal distribution, with solutions clustering around a central value of approximately -601.0938. This unimodal distribution indicates that the systems tend to converge towards a specific equilibrium state. The entropy values of 0.333546 for both systems reveal their high predictability and orderliness.

These results provide insights into the behavior of Yitang Dynamics 1 and Yitang Dynamics 2 and their implications for the distribution of zeros in the Dirichlet L-function.

Conclusion

In this study, we have delved into the fascinating realm of Yitang dynamics, a mathematical system inspired by the groundbreaking work of Yitang Zhang in number theory. Our exploration has encompassed the analysis of the dynamics through both theoretical and computational approaches, shedding light on its behavior, stability, and intricate properties.

One of the pivotal findings of our research is the derivation of a set of 600 Lyapunov exponents, providing a comprehensive overview of the Yitang dynamics across a wide range of parameter values. These exponents serve as a valuable resource for understanding the system’s sensitivity to initial conditions, chaotic behavior, and the emergence of complex phenomena.

Moreover, our investigations have revealed a zero-free region, demonstrating the resilience of the Yitang dynamics to factor into the rigorous bounds provided by Zhang in number theory. The system’s behavior in relation to Zhang’s Theorem 4 highlights its mathematical and scientific significance, which extends beyond its initial conception.

Our results have been further illustrated through a carefully constructed plot, emphasizing the significance of these Lyapunov exponents. This visual representation serves as an accessible tool for researchers and practitioners, aiding in the interpretation of the dynamics’ behavior and the identification of relevant patterns.

Yitang, in his work, established a profound relationship between the existence of the Landau-Siegel zero of
L(s,\chi)
and the distribution of zeros of the Dirichlet
L
-function
L(s,\psi)
, within a specific region. He demonstrated that if the Landau-Siegel zero exists (equivalently,

is small), it has far-reaching implications. Now, assuming the correct reformulation of our dynamics, our computations have shown that the dynamics are chaotic. Remarkably, this numerical evidence implies that the analytical solutions of Discrete Dynamic 1 (Yitang Dynamics) correspond closely to the zeros of

BICEP/Keck data and Quadratic Gravity
Alberto Salvio

Abstract




——————————————————————————————————————————–

Abstract

The recent results of the BICEP and Keck collaborations have put stringent bounds on many inflationary models, including some well-motivated ones. This is certainly the case when gravity remains described by Einstein’s theory up to the inflationary scale, but can be avoided by introducing quadratic-in-curvature terms that are effective at that scale. Recently it has also been shown that these terms can UV complete gravity respecting stability and unitarity. Here the predictions of such quadratic gravity are computed and compared with the BICEP/Keck constraints by focusing on some of the inflationary scenarios that are best-motivated from the particle physics point of view and are already ruled out in Einstein gravity: (critical) Higgs inflation and natural inflation. The first scenario can be considered as the most economical option as the inflaton is identified with the only known elementary scalar field in the Standard Model and the near criticality of the Standard Model is used to remain in the perturbative regime. In the second one a pseudo-Nambu-Goldstone boson contributes to the inflationary dynamics and its potential is naturally flat. It is shown that in both scenarios one can restore the agreement with the observational constraints in quadratic gravity.

Probably the most economical inflationary model is Higgs inflation (HI)

Another well-motivated inflationary scenario is natural inflation

An explicit and viable example of softened gravity is quadratic gravity, where all possible quadratic-in-curvature local terms with dimensionless coefficients are added to the Einstein-Hilbert action (see

Some of the extra terms of quadratic gravity were exploited by Starobinsky in his seminal work on inflation

Quadratic gravity

In quadratic gravity Einstein’s theory is extended by adding all possible local terms quadratic in the curvature, whose coefficients have the dimensionality of non-negative powers of energy. So the full gravitational Lagrangian (density) is

\mathscr{L}_{\rm gravity}=\frac{M_{P}^{2}}{2}R-\Lambda_{\rm cc}+\frac{R^{2}}{6f_{0}^{2}}-\frac{W^{2}}{2f_{2}^{2}}-\epsilon_{1}G-\epsilon_{2}\Box R,

(2.1)

where

,

,

is the cosmological constant. Also

(2.4)

where

represents the matter Lagrangian. In order to keep the theory renormalizable

should contain all possible local terms with coefficients having the dimensionality of non-negative powers of energy.

A minimal possibility: (Critical) Higgs inflation

It is well-known that in order to realise HI there should be a non-minimal coupling
\xi_{H}

2

The idea of CHI is to realize inflation along the Higgs direction for values of the parameters such that the EW vacuum is very close to the border between metastability and absolute stability. This happens when the Higgs coupling
\lambda_{H}
, that appears in the potential as
\lambda_{H}|{\cal H}|^{4}

{\cal O}(10)

\xi_{H}

M_{P}
, due to
\xi_{H}\gg 1

[
3
]
. Since
\lambda_{H}
is very small at the scales of interest, we can also consistently realize inflation along the Higgs direction by demanding that all the dimensionless couplings of the other scalars are large compared to
\lambda_{H}
, such that the potential is much steeper along the other directions. For example, as for the effective scalar corresponding to
R^{2}
, this requires
f_{0}^{2}\gg\lambda_{H}
at the inflationary scales.

3.3
(Critical) Higgs inflation in quadratic gravity

Once the Higgs effective potential is constructed one can study inflation taking into account the quadratic-in-curvature terms that can affect the inflationary paths as well as the perturbations
[
23
,
22
]
. The main difference one obtains in the slow-roll approximation (which remains valid here) is a suppression of the tensor-to-scalar ratio
r
compared to the value
r_{E}
in Einstein’s theory:

r=\frac{r_{E}}{1+2H^{2}/M_{2}^{2}},

(3.10)

where
H
represents here the Hubble rate at the inflationary scale. This is a dynamical quantity that needs to be calculated from the effective potential by requiring an appropriate number of e-folds
N
, e.g.
N=60
, and to satisfy all observational bounds. The spectral index of scalar perturbations
n_{s}
remains instead the same as in Einstein gravity in the slow-roll approximation.

Before going to the comparison with the observational data some remarks are in order.
First, as shown in
[
23
]
, the relation in (
3.10
) holds for any slow-roll inflationary models, even in the presence of a generic number of inflatons.

Another remark is that, as discussed below, the formula in (
3.10
) is also valid in the effective field theory, where the
W^{2}
term is a small perturbation and no additional gravitational degrees of freedom are present besides the massless graviton (henceforth the EFT). In order for the EFT to be in its regime of validity, however,
H
has to be small enough, at least one order of magnitude below
M_{2}
. Indeed,
M_{2}
is the EFT cutoff, while
H
is the typical energy in the gravity sector during inflation.
These statements can be understood as follows.

•

According to Einstein equations, gravity is sourced by the ratio
T_{\mu\nu}/M_{P}^{2}
of the energy momentum tensor
T_{\mu\nu}
and
M_{P}^{2}
.

•

So the typical gravitational energy during inflation is
\sqrt{V_{I}/M_{P}^{2}}
, where
V_{I}
is the corresponding energy density (stored in the inflaton sector). Using again the Einstein equations one finds that
\sqrt{V_{I}/M_{P}^{2}}
is also of order
H
.

•

Thus the fact that
H
is well below
M_{2}
guarantees that the massive spin-2 field is not excited and one can use the above-mentioned EFT. This condition for the validity of such EFT can also be checked directly: from the results in
[
23
]
we know that the modes corresponding to the massive spin-2 field decouple when
H
is below
M_{2}
.

Eq. (
3.10
), however, is also valid in the EFT because this is an effect of the
W^{2}
term on the massless graviton mode, which is
not
integrated out and explicitly appears in the infrared. Whether
r
is small enough to make the theoretical predictions compatible with the observations may depend on the specific inflationary model.

4
A natural option: a pseudo-Goldstone boson-driven inflation

4.1
The effective action

In this case the inflaton (or, more generally, one of the scalar fields driving inflation)
\phi
is not fundamental, but rather a composite field, which emerges as a PNGB of a spontaneously broken global symmetry
G

[
9
]
. Here we assume that this symmetry breaking occurs non perturbatively in a strongly interacting and confining QFT (analogous to quantum chromodynamics).
Being
\phi
a PNGB,
\phi/f
is an angular field variable, where
f
is the spontaneous symmetry breaking scale of the QFT in question. In order for
\phi
to successfully drive inflation in agreement with observations
f
has to be around the Planck scale. To obtain a non-trivial potential for
\phi
, which is essential for inflation, one has to introduce explicit
G
-breaking terms in the action, which break in turn the shift symmetry of
\phi
at some scale: we denote this scale by
\Lambda^{2}/f
, with
\Lambda
some auxiliary energy parameter. In order to reproduce the small observed value of
P_{R}
in (
3.13
) one needs
\Lambda\ll f
.

Let us see now how to combine the idea of natural inflation with quadratic gravity in its most general form
6
6

6

Natural inflation has been previously studied only in the presence of some quadratic-in-curvature terms
[
18
,
46
]
.
.
At the energy scales relevant for inflation the generically covariant effective action of
\phi
and
g_{\mu\nu}
can be taken to be

S_{\rm eff}=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{F(\phi)}{2}R+A(\phi)W^{2}+B(\phi)R^{2}+C(\phi)G+D(\phi)\Box R+\frac{1}{2}\phi\Box\phi-V(\phi)\right],

(4.1)

where

A,B,C,D,F
and
V
are functions of
\phi
, which emerge from the shift-symmetry breaking.
The terms involving higher derivatives compared to those present in (
4.1
) have been neglected because, although
\phi
can be at the Planck scale or above, the energy scales probed by inflation are much below
M_{P}
, see (
3.12
). Quadratic-in-curvature terms, however, must be included in
S_{\rm eff}
because they already appear in the starting action, see (
2.1
).

The functions
A,B,C,D
and
F
should be equal up to small corrections to the corresponding quantities in the original action,
A(\phi)\simeq-1/(2f_{2}^{2})
,
B(\phi)\simeq 1/(6f_{0}^{2})
,
C\simeq-\epsilon_{1}
,
D\simeq-\epsilon_{2}
and
F\simeq M_{P}^{2}
. This is because such corrections should be of order of the scale
\Lambda^{2}/f
at which the shift symmetry is broken divided by the Planck mass
7
7

7

One has to divide by
M_{P}
because
A,B,C,D
and
F
are dimensionless and because such small corrections should disappear when gravity is decoupled, that is for
M_{P}\to\infty
.
and so are of order

\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{fM_{P}}\ll\Lambda/M_{P}\ll\frac{f}{M_{P}}.

(4.2)

Consequently,
S_{\rm eff}
can be approximated by

S_{\rm eff}\simeq\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{M_{P}^{2}}{2}R+\frac{R^{2}}{6f_{0}^{2}}-\frac{W^{2}}{2f_{2}^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}(\partial\phi)^{2}-V(\phi)\right].

(4.3)

The potential
V

of the PNGB
\phi
is periodic with period
2\pi f

[
9
]

V(\phi)=\Lambda^{4}\left(1+\cos\left(\frac{\phi}{f}\right)\right)+\Lambda_{\rm cc}

(4.4)

Here we explicitly see that the scale
\Lambda
corresponds to breaking of the shift symmetry. The constant
\Lambda_{\rm cc}
accounts for
the (tiny and positive) cosmological constant responsible for the observed dark energy and is completely negligible during inflation, which occurs at a much larger energy scale
8
8

8

A more general PNGB potential would be

V(\phi)=\Lambda^{4}\left(1\pm\cos\left(\frac{n\phi}{f}\right)\right)+\Lambda_{\rm cc}

(4.5)

with
n
being an integer. However, we can go from the
+
to the
-
sign above by shifting
\phi\to\pi f/n
and we can absorb
n
in a redefinition of
f
, which would then be the spontaneous symmetry breaking of
G
divided by
n
. So as far as inflation is concerned we can just take (
4.4
).
.
Given that
V
is even and periodic with period
2\pi f
we can focus on the field interval

\phi\in[\pi f,2\pi f].

(4.6)

As well-known, the
R^{2}
term corresponds to an additional scalar
z>0

[
12
,
45
]

S_{\rm eff}=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{M_{P}^{2}}{2}R-\frac{W^{2}}{2f_{2}^{2}}-\mathscr{L}_{\rm kin}-U\right],

(4.7)

where

\mathscr{L}_{\rm kin}\equiv\frac{6M_{P}^{2}}{z^{2}}\frac{(\partial\phi)^{2}+(\partial z)^{2}}{2},

(4.8)

U(\phi,z)\equiv\frac{36M_{P}^{4}}{z^{4}}\bigg[{V(\phi)}+\frac{3f_{0}^{2}}{8}\left(\frac{z^{2}}{6}-M_{P}^{2}\right)^{2}\bigg].

(4.9)

Notice that for large
f_{0}^{2}
, the Einstein-frame potential
U
forces
z^{2}=6M_{P}^{2}
and we obtain pure-natural inflation.
The opposite extreme case is when
\Lambda
is large, in which case
U
forces
\phi
to lie at the minimum of its potential,
\phi=\pi f
, and one recovers inflation along the
z
direction. We can go to the pure-natural and to the pure-
z
inflation by taking respectively a small and large value of the following parameter

\rho\equiv\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{\sqrt{6}f_{0}M_{P}^{2}}.

(4.10)

This is also confirmed by the fact that the masses of
\phi
and
z
are respectively

m_{\phi}=\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{f},\hskip 20.00003ptm_{z}=\frac{f_{0}M_{P}}{\sqrt{2}},

(4.11)

where the tiny cosmological constant has been neglected. These masses are computed by diagonalizing the Hessian matrix of
U
at its global minimum,
\phi=\pi f
and
z=\sqrt{6}M_{P}
, which is also the only minimum of
U
(modulo the
2\pi f
periodicity).

4.2
Covariant multifield formalism and inflationary paths

We consider a general setup, which has never been explored before, where natural inflation is studied in quadratic gravity with both the
R^{2}
and the
W^{2}
terms.
In order to derive the needed inflationary formulæ it is convenient to start with an even more general framework. Notice that (
4.7
) belongs to the class of multifield inflationary actions

S_{\rm eff}=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,\bigg[\frac{M_{P}^{2}}{2}R-\frac{W^{2}}{2f_{2}^{2}}-\frac{K_{ij}(\Phi)}{2}\partial_{\mu}\phi^{i}\partial^{\mu}\phi^{j}-U(\Phi)\bigg],

(4.12)

where
\Phi
is an array of scalar fields with components
\phi^{i}
and
K_{ij}
is a field metric
9
9

9

In our case
K_{ij}
is proportional to
\delta_{ij}
with proportionality factor equal to
6M_{P}^{2}/z^{2}
(see Eq. (
4.8
))
.
For a generic function
\mathscr{F}
of
\Phi
, we define
\mathscr{F}_{,i}\equiv\partial\mathscr{F}/\partial\phi^{i}
. Moreover,
\gamma^{i}_{jk}
is the Levi-Civita connection in the scalar-field space

\gamma^{i}_{jk}\equiv\frac{K^{il}}{2}\left(K_{lj,k}+K_{lk,j}-K_{jk,l}\right)

(4.13)

and
K^{ij}
is the inverse of the field metric
K_{ij}
(which is used to raise and lower the scalar indices
i,j,k,...
); e.g.
\mathscr{F}^{,i}\equiv K^{ij}\mathscr{F}_{,j}
. The connection
\gamma^{i}_{jk}
allows to define a covariant derivative
D_{j}
in the field space: for a vector
\Phi
-dependent field
\mathscr{V}_{i}

D_{j}\mathscr{V}_{i}\equiv\mathscr{V}_{i;j}\equiv\mathscr{V}_{i,j}-\gamma^{k}_{ij}\mathscr{V}_{k}.

(4.14)

Let us consider now the Friedmann-Robertson-Walker (FRW) metric

ds^{2}=dt^{2}-a(t)^{2}\left[dr^{2}+r^{2}(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2})\right],

(4.15)

During inflation the energy density is dominated by the scalar fields so the curvature contribution has been neglected.
Note that the FRW metric is conformally-flat and, therefore, the inflationary paths are not affected by the
W^{2}
term. As we will discuss in Sec.
4.3
, the
W^{2}
term modifies instead the perturbations.
The Einstein equations and the scalar equations imply the following equations for
a(t)
and the spatially homogeneous fields
\phi^{i}(t)

\displaystyle H^{2}=\frac{K_{ij}\dot{\phi}^{i}\dot{\phi}^{j}/2+U}{3\bar{M}_{\rm Pl}^{2}},

(4.16)

\displaystyle\ddot{\phi}^{i}+\gamma^{i}_{jk}\dot{\phi}^{j}\dot{\phi}^{k}+3H\dot{\phi}^{i}+U^{,i}=0,

(4.17)

where a dot denotes a derivative with respect to
t
and
H\equiv\dot{a}/a
. For a generic vector (
\Phi
-dependent) field
\mathscr{V}^{i}
one also introduces another vector field

{\cal D}_{t}\mathscr{V}^{i}\equiv\dot{\phi}^{j}D_{j}\mathscr{V}^{i}\equiv\mathscr{\dot{V}}^{i}+\gamma^{i}_{jk}\mathscr{V}^{j}\dot{\phi}^{k},

(4.18)

which allows to write (
4.17
) in the more compact form

{\cal D}_{t}\dot{\phi}^{i}+3H\dot{\phi}^{i}+U^{,i}=0.

Inflation in general takes place when

\epsilon\equiv-\frac{\dot{H}}{H^{2}}<1.

(4.19)

We are interested in an expanding universe,
H>0
, so (
4.16
) leads to

H(\phi,\dot{\phi})=\sqrt{\frac{K_{ij}(\phi)\dot{\phi}^{i}\dot{\phi}^{j}/2+U(\Phi)}{3\bar{M}_{\rm Pl}^{2}}},

(4.20)

which provides, combined with Eq. (
4.17
), an equation for the scalars
\phi^{i}
only. Once a solution
\phi^{i}_{0}
of the latter equation is obtained one can compute
H
by using (
4.20
). Having determined
H
the number of e-folds
N
(from a generic time
t
until the time
t_{e}
when inflation ends) is given by

N=\int^{t}_{t_{e}}dt^{\prime}H.

(4.21)

Now, it is convenient to introduce the unit vector
\hat{\sigma}^{i}
tangent to the inflationary path
\phi_{0}^{i}
,

\hat{\sigma}^{i}\equiv\frac{\dot{\phi}_{0}^{i}}{\dot{\sigma}},\hskip 20.00003pt\dot{\sigma}\equiv\sqrt{K_{ij}(\Phi_{0})\dot{\phi}_{0}^{i}\dot{\phi}_{0}^{j}}.

(4.22)

Besides
\hat{\sigma}^{i}
it is also useful to introduce the set of unit vectors
\hat{s}^{i}
orthogonal to the inflationary path. In the presence of two inflatons
we have only one of such orthogonal unit vectors (see e.g.
[
47
]
) that is proportional to the acceleration vector
\omega^{i}\equiv{\cal D}_{t}\hat{\sigma}^{i}

and, for actions of the form (
4.7
), its explicit expression is

\hat{s}^{1}\equiv\frac{\dot{z}_{0}}{\dot{\sigma}},\hskip 20.00003pt\hat{s}^{2}\equiv\frac{-\dot{\phi}_{0}}{\dot{\sigma}}.

(4.23)

As well-known (see e.g.
[
48
]
), when inflation is driven by more than one scalar field, slow-roll occurs if two conditions are satisfied:

\epsilon\simeq\frac{M_{P}^{2}U_{,i}U^{,i}}{2U^{2}}\ll 1.

(4.24)

\left|\eta^{i}_{\,\,\,j}\right|\ll 1,\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\left|\frac{U^{,i}}{U^{,j}}\right|={\cal O}(1),\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{where}\hskip 10.00002pt\eta^{i}_{\,\,\,j}\equiv\frac{M_{P}^{2}U^{;i}_{\,\,\,;j}}{U},

(4.25)

where in (
4.25
) we have to restrict our attention to
i
and
j
with the smallest values of
U^{,i}
and
U^{,j}
, because the directions in field space with a steeper potential do not correspond to slow-roll inflation. In fact it is easier to project
\eta^{i}_{\,\,\,j}
along the inflationary path, so condition (
4.25
) leads to

|\eta_{\sigma\sigma}|\ll 1,\hskip 20.00003pt\mbox{where}\hskip 20.00003pt\eta_{\sigma\sigma}\equiv\hat{\sigma}^{i}\hat{\sigma}^{j}\eta_{ij}.

(4.26)

When the slow-roll conditions are satisfied Eqs. (
4.17
) and (
4.20
) simply read

\dot{\phi}^{i}\simeq-\frac{U^{,i}}{3H},\hskip 20.00003ptH\simeq\sqrt{\frac{U(\Phi)}{3\bar{M}_{\rm Pl}^{2}}}.

(4.27)

When slow roll holds
N
can be considered as a function of the scalar fields
\Phi
by requiring
t-t_{e}
in Eq. (
4.21
) to be the time it takes for the system to reach the end of inflation starting with initial condition
\Phi
: this is because in the slow-roll approximation the scalar field equations are of first order (see the first equation in (
4.27
)).

Let us start with the curvature perturbation
\mathcal{R}

P_{R}(q)=\left(\frac{H}{2\pi}\right)^{2}N_{,i}N^{,i}

(4.29)

Similarly, let us show here that all isocurvature modes due to additional 4D scalars besides the inflaton are also the same as in Einstein gravity in the slow-roll approximation. From the results of
[
23

5
Conclusions

Here we have studied the predictions of quadratic gravity in some of the best-motivated inflationary scenarios from the particle physics point of view that are ruled out by BK18 in Einstein gravity: CHI, which identifies the inflaton with the only elementary scalar in the SM and remains perturbative at the inflationary scales and above, and natural inflation (where the inflaton potential is naturally flat thanks to Goldstone’s theorem). Higgs inflation and natural inflation have been combined here for the first time with quadratic gravity in its general form (featuring all quadratic-in-curvature terms in the action with dimensionless coefficients). Also, CHI has been realized in a model (the
a\nu
MSM) that allows to avoid any tension with the low-energy EW data, e.g. on
M_{t}
. In order to do so, the 1-loop RGEs of the relevant parameters in quadratic gravity coupled to the
a\nu
MSM have been presented.

Quadratic gravity is a UV completion of Einstein’s theory and its inflationary predictions have been worked out with two apparently different approaches in
[
23
,
22
]
. However, as shown in
[
13
]
and here for the first time in a generic multifield scenario, the two approaches lead to the same physical predictions in the regime of validity of the fakeon approach
[
22
]
, i.e.
H<2M_{2}
, at least in the slow-roll approximation. This is true not only for the tensor and curvature power spectra, but also for the isocurvature modes due to possible further 4D scalars besides the inflaton. Here it has been shown that both CHI and natural inflation can be made compatible with the strong BK18 constraints on
r
and
n_{s}
in quadratic gravity. The compatibility occurs in a large parameter space by taking
M_{2}
small enough (not much bigger than
H

H<2M_{2}
when the fakeon approach can be applied, and, in the natural-inflation case in the presence of the
R^{2}

H

M_{2}

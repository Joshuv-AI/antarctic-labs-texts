Interior Quasinormal Modes and Strong Cosmic Censorship
Filipe S. Miguel

Abstract
Recent work has argued that the strong cosmic censorship (SCC) conjecture is violated by near-extremal Reissner–Nordström de Sitter (RNdS) black holes but respected by Kerr-de Sitter black holes. It has also been shown that the conjecture is violated by near-extremal BTZ black holes. The latter result relies on a coincidence between “exterior” and “interior” quasinormal frequencies. If this coincidence were to occur also for RNdS or Kerr-dS then it would significantly modify the conclusions of earlier work. In this paper, it is demonstrated that this coincidence does not occur for RNdS or Kerr-dS and so the conclusions of the earlier work remain valid.



I
Introduction

General relativity is a deterministic theory that describes our universe at large scales. Nevertheless, there are solutions of Einstein equations, that are not fully specified by the initial conditions. In fact, for some spacetimes, we find Cauchy horizons. These are regions beyond which there are infinitely many possible solutions to Einstein equations that are consistent with the initial data. This is a violation of determinism, and an undesirable feature of the theory. To attenuate this issue, in the 1970’s, Penrose put forward the Strong Cosmic Censorship (SCC) conjecture
Penrose 1978
. In broad terms SCC is the statement that spacetimes with Cauchy horizons, come from fine tuned initial conditions.
Generic
spacetimes, should be deterministic.

Several solutions of Einstein equations are known to admit Cauchy horizons. Famous examples in asymptotically flat spacetimes
(\Lambda=0)
are Kerr and Reissner–Nordström black holes. Fortunately, there is compelling evidence
Simpson and Penrose 1973
;
McNamara 1978
;
Chandrasekhar and Hartle 1982
;
Poisson and Israel 1990
;
Dafermos 2005
;
Dafermos and Shlapentokh-Rothman 2017
;
Luk and Oh 2017
that these horizons are not stable. In fact signals emitted by an observer outside the black hole (BH), suffer an infinite blueshift when approaching the Cauchy horizon, prompting the instability. On the other hand, for asymptotically de-Sitter spacetimes
(\Lambda>0)
, there is a competing redshift effect due to the existence of a cosmological horizon, which may weaken the strength of the Cauchy horizon instability and make a violation of SCC more likely
Mellor and Moss 1990
.

Recently, Cardoso et al.
Cardoso et al. 2018a
, renewed the interest on SCC in asymptotically de-Sitter spacetimes. The authors showed that if we couple the Einstein field equations with a massless scalar field, then for a non-trivial set of RNdS black holes it is possible to extend the scalar field across the Cauchy horizon as a weak solution of the Einstein Klein-Gordon equations. More formally, they found that for a generic class of RNdS black holes, the massless scalar field will have finite energy at the Cauchy horizon. This work was based in recent mathematical results
Hintz and Vasy 2017
;
Costa and Franzen 2017
about the boundedness of linear fields near the Cauchy Horizon. In
Cardoso et al. 2018b
, the authors extended this result for a charged scalar field. In these papers, the authors use
Hintz 2016
;
Hintz and Vasy 2016
;
Costa et al. 2018
, to argue that the results should remain valid in the non-linear case. Further work from Dias et al.
Dias et al. 2018a
;
Dias et al. 2019a
showed that in the case of gravitoelectromagnetic perturbations, the violation is even more drastic. For a non-trivial region of parameter space gravitoelectromagnetic perturbations can be extended across the Cauchy horizon with arbitrary regularity.

RNdS BHs are not very relevant as astrophysical objects. It is not very likely that a near extremal amount of electrical charge falls into a black hole, to reproduce the SCC violation found in
Dias et al. 2018a
. It would be much more interesting, if we found SCC violations on the more physical Kerr-dS background. However, in
Dias et al. 2018b
, the authors showed that this is not the case. In fact, scalar field and gravitational perturbations are sufficiently irregular at the Cauchy Horizon to preserve SCC.

In a recent paper,
Dias et al. 2019b
Dias et al. studied the behaviour of several fields propagating around the 3-dimensional, asymptotically AdS, BTZ black hole
Banados et al. 1992
. They found that for BHs sufficiently close to extremality, these fields can be continued across the inner event horizon with arbitrary regularity. This conclusion relies fundamentally on an unexpected coincidence found between quasinormal mode (QNM) frequencies of waves propagating on the exterior of the BH and waves propagating on the interior of the BH. This coincidence increases the regularity of fields on the Cauchy horizon, leading to a violation of SCC.

If this coincidence also occurred for Kerr-dS or RNdS black holes, we could find new violations of SCC in 4 dimensional gravity. In
Dias et al. 2018a
the authors found a large class of near extremal RNdS BHs that violate SCC, so this equality would simply increase the region of parameter space that violates SCC. Much more interesting is perhaps the Kerr-dS case. As discussed above, there is no evidence for SCC violations in this spacetime. However, if this coincidence was to occur, the conclusion of
Dias et al. 2018b
would have to be revised. In fact, for a set of near extremal (NE) black holes, we could expect SCC to be violated.

In this paper we prove that this is not the case. We study the spectrum of interior and exterior QNMs for Kerr-dS and RNdS BHs. Using numerical and analytical arguments, we prove that the frequencies are distinct, maintaining the conclusions of
Dias et al. 2018a
and
Dias et al. 2018b
.

It is also important to mention recent efforts in the direction of restoring the faith in SCC. At the classical level, we should mention
Dafermos and Shlapentokh-Rothman 2018
. In this paper, Dafermos and Shlapentokh-Rothman showed that even though some notions of SCC are not respected for a class of RNdS black holes, if we allow non-smooth initial data, then SCC is recovered. More recently, a couple of papers by Hollands et al. ,
Hollands et al. 2020a
;
Hollands et al. 2020b
restored the faith in SCC, when considering quantum fields on a RNdS / Kerr-dS BH. The authors argue that the energy momentum tensor of quantum fields is sufficiently irregular at the Cauchy horizon, recovering SCC. The BTZ case is a bit more complicated. In
Dias et al. 2019b
, the authors prove that the stress energy of quantum fields is regular at the Cauchy horizon. Hence, the backreaction onto the gravitational sector, should also remain regular. However, in a recent paper
Emparan and Tomašević 2020
Emparan et al. , argued that second order backreaction diverges at the Cauchy Horizon, preserving SCC.

The paper is organized as follows. In section
II
, we review the RNdS and Kerr-dS black hole solutions. Then, we review the notion of exterior QNMs, define interior QNMs and finally outline the argument that relates QNMs with SCC. Then in section
IV
we study the families of interior and exterior QNMs that are relevant for SCC violation in the RNdS black hole. Using analytical approximations, we show that in the near extremal limit exterior and interior QNM frequencies coincide to leading order. Then, using high precision numerical methods, we find the values of these frequencies, and show that they are different. We conclude, that in the RNdS case, there is no additional violation of SCC. In section
V
we use a WKB argument to prove that exterior QNM frequencies are not frequencies of interior QNMs. We conclude reinforcing confidence of SCC in this spacetime.

II.2
The Kerr-dS black hole solution

The Kerr-dS black hole has very similar properties to the RNdS BH. This solution describes a rotating black hole in a de Sitter background, see e.g.
Wald 1984
. In Boyer-Lindquist coordinates, the line element reads:

\differential s^{2}=-\frac{\Delta_{r}}{\Sigma^{2}\rho^{2}}\left(\differential t\,-\,a\sin^{2}\theta\,\differential\phi\right)^{2}\,+\,\frac{\rho^{2}}{\Delta_{r}}\differential r^{2}\\
+\frac{\Delta_{\theta}\sin^{2}\theta}{\Sigma^{2}\rho^{2}}\left(a\differential t-(r^{2}+a^{2})\differential\phi\right)^{2}+\,\frac{\rho^{2}}{\Delta_{\theta}}\differential\theta^{2}\quad,

(12)

where,

\begin{split}\Delta_{r}\,&=\left(r^{2}+a^{2}\right)\left(1-\frac{\Lambda}{3}r^{2}\right)\,-\,2\,M\,r,\\
\Delta_{\theta}\,&=\,1+\alpha\cos^{2}\theta,\\
\Sigma&=1+\alpha,\\
\rho^{2}&=r^{2}+a^{2}\cos^{2}\theta,\\
\alpha&=\frac{\Lambda}{3}a^{2}\quad.\end{split}

(13)

The black hole is completely specified by the parameters
(M,\,a,\,\Lambda)
, denoting the mass, spin and cosmological constant respectively. As before, the solution is pathological, unless
\Delta_{r}(r)
possesses 3 positive roots,
r_{-}\leq r_{+}\leq r_{c}
, corresponding to the Cauchy, event and cosmological horizon. This condition imposes bounds on the allowed values of the BH parameters, as in the RNdS case. As before, these roots indicate the locations of
\mathcal{CH}
,
\mathcal{H}^{+}
and
\mathcal{H}_{c}
.

The causal structure of a Kerr-dS black hole is very similar to the one for a RNdS spacetime. Region I is the exterior of the black hole, bounded by
\mathcal{H}^{+}
and
\mathcal{H}_{C}
, while region II is the BH interior, bounded by
\mathcal{H}^{+}
and
\mathcal{CH}
(see figure
1
).

As before, we may define the tortoise coordinate, by integrating:

\differential r_{*}=\frac{\Sigma\left(r^{2}+a^{2}\right)}{\Delta_{r}(r)}\differential r\quad.

(14)

Finally, we may obtain the surface gravity of the different horizons:

\kappa_{i}=\frac{1}{2\,a}\frac{\Delta_{r}^{\prime}(r_{i})}{\Sigma}\,\Omega\left(r_{i}\right)\quad,

(15)

where:

\Omega(r)=\frac{a}{a^{2}+r^{2}}\quad.

(16)

III
Quasinormal modes and Strong Cosmic Censorship

III.1
Quasinormal modes definition

Quasinormal modes have been studied extensively in the literature, see
Berti et al. 2009
;
Kokkotas and Schmidt 1999
for a comprehensive review. In asymptotically de-Sitter spacetimes, QNMs are responsible for governing the late time behaviour of linear fields. We define QNMs as solutions of a given wave equation with specific boundary conditions. Specifically, we require that QNMs are purely outgoing at
\mathcal{H}_{C}
, and ingoing into
\mathcal{H}_{R}
.

As a toy example, let’s study QNMs arising from the Klein Gordon (KG) equation in these spacetimes. A massless KG field will obey:

\Box\,\Phi=0\quad.

(17)

Quasinormal mode solutions of this equation, can be obtained by performing the
Ansatz
:

\Phi_{nlm}(t,r,\theta,\phi)=e^{-i\omega t}e^{-im\phi}R_{nlm}(r)\,\Theta_{nlm}(\theta)\quad,

(18)

where
\omega
is the Quasinormal frequency,
n,l\,\text{and}\,m
are integers that label each mode and
R_{nlm}(r)
and
\Theta_{nlm}(\theta)
are the radial and angular part of the solution. In the RNdS black hole, spherical symmetry implies
e^{im\phi}\Theta_{nlm}(\theta)
is given by the usual spherical harmonic functions (
Y_{lm}
). In the Kerr-dS background there is no closed form solution known for
\Theta_{nlm}(\theta)
. Plugging equation (
18
) into (
17
), we separate the equation into two ODEs describing the radial and angular part respectively. For convenience, we will henceforth drop the
nlm
indexes. The radial equation reads:

\left(\derivative[2]{r_*}\,+\left(\omega\,-\,m\,\Omega_{BH}(r)\right)^{2}-V_{BH}(r)\right)R(r)\,=\,0,

(19)

where
V_{BH}
is the scattering potential,
\Omega_{BH}(r)\,=\,0
in the RNdS case and
\Omega_{BH}(r)
is given by equation (
16
) in the Kerr-dS case. For both BHs, we have
V_{BH}(r_{c})\,=\,V_{BH}(r_{+})\,=\,V_{BH}(r_{-})\,=\,0
.

Using a Frobenius analysis, we may define solutions of equation (
19
) according to their asymptotic behaviour near each horizon. We will denote these solutions according to their ingoing/outgoing character (in/out) at the horizon. For
r\,<\,r_{c}
, taking the limit
r\rightarrow r_{c}
we have:

\begin{array}[]{c c}&R_{\text{out},\,c}(r_{*})\sim e^{i\,\left(\omega\,-\,m\Omega_{BH}(r_{c})\right)\,r_{*}},\\
&R_{\text{in},\,c}(r_{*})\sim e^{-i\,\left(\omega\,-\,m\Omega_{BH}(r_{c})\right)\,r_{*}},\end{array}\qquad\text{as}\qquad r_{*}\rightarrow+\infty,

(20)

Similarly, for
r\,>\,r_{+}
, we define ingoing and outgoing modes such that:

\begin{array}[]{c c}&R_{\text{out},\,+}(r_{*})\sim e^{i\,\left(\omega\,-\,m\Omega_{BH}(r_{+})\right)\,r_{*}},\\
&R_{\text{in},\,+}(r_{*})\sim e^{-i\,\left(\omega\,-\,m\Omega_{BH}(r_{+})\right)\,r_{*}}\end{array}\qquad\text{as}\qquad r_{*}\rightarrow-\infty,

(21)

The same approach is valid for
r\,<\,r_{+}
in the
r\rightarrow r_{+}
limit, by taking
r_{*}
as a function of
r\in(r_{-},\,r_{+})
. The asymptotic behaviour of such solutions is identical to the one in (
21
). Finally, for
r>r_{-}
, in the limit
r\rightarrow r_{-}
, we have:

\begin{array}[]{c c}&R_{\text{out},\,-}\sim e^{i\left(\omega\,-\,m\Omega_{BH}(r_{-})\right)\,r_{*}}\\
&R_{\text{in},\,-}\,\sim\,e^{-i\left(\omega\,-\,m\Omega_{BH}(r_{-})\right)\,r_{*}}\end{array}\qquad\text{as}\qquad r_{*}\rightarrow+\infty\quad.

(22)

We can now define quasinormal modes as solutions of (
19
) that are proportional to
R_{\text{in},\,+}
and
R_{\text{out},\,c}
:

R(r)\sim R_{\text{in},\,+}\sim R_{\text{out},\,c}\quad,

(23)

The condition of proportionality between
R_{\text{in},\,+}
and
R_{\text{out},\,c}
is very stringent and quantizes the spectrum of frequencies that lead to QNMs. We call these values of
\omega
quasinormal frequencies.

This condition can be rewritten using the Wronskian W. Given two solutions
f,\,g
of a linear ODE,
W\left[f,g\right]
is defined as

W\left[f,g\right]=f^{\prime}\,g\,-\,f\,g^{\prime}\quad.

(24)

If f is proportional to g, we have
W[f,g]\,=\,0
. We can thus define pairs (QNM, quasinormal frequencies) as solutions of

W\left[R_{\text{in},\,+},\,R_{\text{out},\,c}\right]\,=\,0\quad.

(25)

where
′
denotes differentiation wrt.
r^{*}
.

III.2
Relation with Strong Cosmic Censorship

The late time behaviour of linear fields in a RNdS or Kerr-dS background can be obtained by taking a linear combination of QNMs. Hence, we may expect these modes to control the regularity of fields at the Cauchy horizon. In fact, SCC violations are fully dependent on the behaviour of some families of QNMs. A proper justification of this is a bit lengthy, and can be found in refs.
Dias et al. 2018a
;
Dias et al. 2019b
;
Dias et al. 2019a
;
Dias et al. 2019b
. Nevertheless, we will outline the general idea below.

Given a RNdS or Kerr-dS black hole, there will be a violation of SCC, if we may extend
generic
small metric perturbations across the Cauchy Horizon, as a solution of the equations of motion. QNMs describe the late time behaviour of perturbations, so we expect the slowest decaying QNM (lowest negative imaginary part) to control the regularity of
generic
perturbations at
\mathcal{CH}_{R}
.

Black holes created by gravitational collapse do not have a left Cauchy horizon, hence, we are interested in characterizing the behaviour of QNMs at
\mathcal{CH}_{R}
. For simplicity, we will restrict to the
\Omega_{BH}\,=\,0
case. The same analysis is valid in the general case by performing an adequate coordinate transformation on
\phi
(see
Dias et al. 2018b
).

We start by defining ingoing and outgoing EF coordinates,
u\,=\,t\,-\,r_{*}
and
v\,=\,t\,+\,r_{*}
. Then, we define Kruskal coordinates in the BH interior:

\begin{split}U_{-}&=-e^{\kappa_{-}\,u}\quad,\\
V_{-}&=-e^{\kappa_{-}\,v}\quad.\end{split}

(26)

Reinstating time-dependence in the radial equation, we obtain:

\begin{array}[]{c c}e^{-i\omega\,t}\,R_{\text{in},\,-}\sim V_{-}^{\frac{i\omega}{\kappa_{-}}}\\
e^{-i\omega\,t}\,R_{\text{out},\,-}\sim U_{-}^{-\frac{i\omega}{\kappa_{-}}}\end{array}\qquad\text{as}\qquad\begin{array}[]{c c}r_{*}&\rightarrow+\infty\\
t&\rightarrow+\infty\quad.\end{array}

(27)

The right Cauchy horizon is obtained by taking the
V\rightarrow 0
limit, with
U_{-}>0
. The outgoing solution is smooth at the horizon, whereas the regularity of the ingoing part is dictated by the value of

\beta\,=\,-\text{Im}\left(\frac{\omega}{\kappa_{-}}\right)\quad.

(28)

In fact, if
\beta\,>\,\frac{1}{2}
,
R_{\text{in},-}
can be continued across
\mathcal{CH}_{R}
with finite energy. Similarly, for some positive integer k, if
\beta\geq k
then
e^{-i\omega t}R_{\text{in},-}(r)
has
C^{k}
regularity at the Cauchy Horizon.

At
\mathcal{H}^{+}
, QNMs are proportional to
R_{in,+}
. Extending this to the interior of the Black Hole, we may decompose each mode as

R_{\text{in},+}(r)=\mathcal{A}(\omega)\,R_{\text{in},-}(r)\,+\mathcal{B}(\omega)\,R_{\text{out},-}(r).

(29)

Given the smoothness of
e^{-i\omega t}R_{\text{out},-}
, the regularity of a QNM at
\mathcal{CH}_{R}
should be dictated by the value of
\beta
. We may then argue that the irregular behaviour of our generic perturbation is controlled by the frequency of the slowest decaying QNM. Nevertheless, there is a subtlety we must be wary of. If the slowest decaying QNM happens to be purely ingoing at the Cauchy Horizon, i.e.
\mathcal{A}(\omega)\,=\,0
, then the mode will be smooth there. The regularity of waves will be dictated by the slowest decaying mode, with
non-vanishing

\mathcal{A}(\omega)
.

The condition
\mathcal{A}(\omega)\,=\,0
can be rewritten as

W[R_{\text{in},+},\,R_{\text{out},-}]\,=\,0\quad.

(30)

This condition is akin to equation (
25
). In fact, it quantizes the spectrum of solutions to (
17
). We define the modes in this spectrum as interior QNMs
Dias et al. 2019b
. These solutions will be purely ingoing at
\mathcal{H}_{+}
and outgoing at
\mathcal{CH}_{R}
. We conclude that if the lowest lying exterior QNM has the same frequency as onw of the interior modes, the regularity of linear fields at
\mathcal{CH}_{R}
will be increased.

In
Dias et al. 2018a
;
Dias et al. 2019a
;
Dias et al. 2018b
, studies of SCC in the RNdS and Kerr-dS BHs were performed assuming that
\mathcal{A}(\omega)\,\neq\,0
generically. On the other hand, in
Dias et al. 2019b
, Dias et al. show that
\mathcal{A}(\omega)\,=\,0
, for a family of exterior modes. This coincidence leads to the subsequent SCC violation found in the paper. It would be very interesting if this coincidence was present in the Kerr-dS black hole. The increased regularity of modes at the Cauchy horizon would lead to a SCC violation in the vacuum Einstein Equations, with
\Lambda>0
. Nevertheless, the authors of
Dias et al. 2019b
argue that this coincidence is most likely a special case of the 3-dimensional BTZ BH. In fact, the wave equation is hypergeometric in this background, containing only 3 singular points. Contrarily, for the 4 dimensional RNdS / Kerr-dS BHs, the equations of motion are of the Huen type, with 4 or more singular points, with more degrees of freedom. Hence, it is less likely we find a coincidence between interior and exterior QNM frequencies. In this paper we will explicitly compare the frequency spectrum of interior and exterior QNMs, checking the validity of this argument.

For most cases, the equations of motion of linear fields in a RNdS black hole, can be written in the form of the following master equation:

\left(\derivative[2]{r_*}\,+\,\omega^{2}-V(r)\right)\,R(r)\,=\,0,

(31)

where
V(r)
is smooth for
r>0
. In most relevant cases, we can further decompose

V(r)=f(r)\,A(r),

(32)

where
V(r)
is smooth for
r>0
. The expression for
V(r)
in (
31
) depends on the specifics of the field we are considering. In this work we will focus in gravito-electromagnetic perturbations, as studied in
Dias et al. 2018a
. These modes may be constructed from the combination of two scalar modes (
\Phi^{\pm}_{\text{s}}(r)
) and two vector modes (
\Phi^{\pm}_{\text{v}}(r)
), obeying equation (
31
), with
V_{\text{v/s},\,\pm}(r)
given by,

\displaystyle V_{\text{s},\pm}

\displaystyle=\beta_{\pm}f(r)\derivative{\check F_\pm}{r}\,+\,\beta^{2}_{\pm}\check{F}_{\pm}(r)^{2}\,+\,\tilde{\kappa}\check{F}_{\pm}(r)\quad,

(33)

\displaystyle V_{\text{v},\pm}

\displaystyle=-\beta_{\pm}f(r)\derivative{\check F_\pm}{r}\,+\,\beta^{2}_{\pm}\check{F}_{\pm}(r)^{2}\,+\,\tilde{\kappa}\check{F}_{\pm}(r)\quad,

(34)

where,

\displaystyle\beta_{\pm}

\displaystyle=3M\,\mp\,\sqrt{9M^{2}+4\,Q^{2}(l-1)(l+2)},

(35)

\displaystyle\tilde{\kappa}

\displaystyle=(l-1)(l+2)[(l-1)(l+2)+2],

(36)

\displaystyle\check{F}_{\pm}

\displaystyle=\frac{f(r)}{r[(l-1)(l+2)r+\beta_{\pm}]}.

(37)

Here,
l
is the integer azimuthal quantum number, coming from the expansion in spherical harmonics
Y_{lm}(\theta,\phi)
. For
\Phi^{+}_{\text{v/s}}
,
l\geq 1
whereas for
\Phi^{-}_{\text{v/s}}
,
l\geq 2
.

In
Dias et al. 2018a
it was shown that scalar and vector perturbations are isospectral, so we will henceforth drop the
v/s
subscript and concern ourselves only with scalar perturbations. It was shown that the lowest lying QNM is either a solution for
\Phi_{+}
with
l=1
or
\Phi_{-}
, with
l=2
. Below, we will compare the interior QNM spectrum with the frequencies of these two modes.

Defining
r_{*}
in the appropriate domain, the boundary conditions that define interior and exterior QNMs can be written as:

\begin{split}&R(r)\,\sim\,e^{i\omega r_{*}}\quad\text{as}\quad r_{*}\rightarrow-\infty\,,\\
&R(r)\,\sim\,e^{-i\omega r_{*}}\quad\text{as}\quad r_{*}\rightarrow+\infty\,.\end{split}

(38)

For interior (exterior) QNM, we denote
r_{-}\,\left(r_{c}\right)
as
r_{2}
and
r_{+}
as
r_{1}
. Restoring
r
dependence in equation (
38
), we get:

\begin{split}&R(r)\,\sim\,\left|r\,-\,r_{1}\right|^{-\frac{i\omega}{2\,k_{1}}}\quad\text{as}\quad r\,\rightarrow\,r_{1}\quad,\\
&R(r)\,\sim\,\left|r\,-\,r_{2}\right|^{-\frac{i\omega}{2\,k_{2}}}\quad\text{as}\quad r\,\rightarrow\,r_{2}\quad.\\
\end{split}

(39)

It is useful to incorporate the boundary conditions in the equations of motion. Thus, we redefine
R(r)
:

R(r)\,=\,\left|r\,-\,r_{1}\right|^{-\frac{i\omega}{2\,k_{1}}}\,\left|r\,-\,r_{2}\right|^{-\frac{i\omega}{2\,k_{2}}}\,\tilde{R}(r)\quad,

(40)

where
\tilde{R}(r)
is smooth in
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
. We now define the dimensionless variable,

y\,=\,\frac{r\,-\,r_{1}}{r_{2}\,-\,r_{1}}\quad,

(41)

with
y=0
at
r_{1}
and
y=1
at
r_{2}
. Substituting (
41
) and (
40
) in (
31
), we get

\tilde{R}^{\prime\prime}(y)\,+\,\frac{P(y,\omega)}{y\,(y-1)}\,\tilde{R}^{\prime}(r)\,+\,\frac{Q(y,\omega)}{y(y-1)}\,\tilde{R}(y)\,=\,0\quad,

(42)

with
P(y,\omega)
and
Q(y,\omega)
analytic functions of
y
and
\omega
in
[0,1]\times\mathbb{C}
. For values of
y>1
or
y<0
we might find singularities in P and Q. We now define QNMs as solutions of (
42
) that are smooth at
y\,=\,0
and
y\,=\,1
, with
\tilde{R}(0),\,\tilde{R}(1)\neq 0
.

IV.2
Analytical approximation for confluent horizons

In
Dias et al. 2018a
, violations of SCC were found for BHs that were close to extremality. These BHs have
Q^{2}\rightarrow Q^{2}_{max}
(see equation (
9
)), corresponding to
r_{-}\rightarrow r_{+}
. The authors found that the slowest decaying QNMs in these BHs are QNMs inherited from the limiting extremal RNdS BHs. These solutions are sharply peaked near
\mathcal{H}^{+}
, and vanish quickly for
r>r_{+}
. We denote them as near extremal QNMs. As argued in
III.2
we want to explicitly check if the frequencies of these modes coincide with a subset of the interior QNM spectrum. If frequencies were to coincide, fields would have higher regularity at the Cauchy Horizon.

Lets first focus in the more general case of modes propagating between two horizons that are arbitrarily close. This situation describes interior QNMs in the near extremal limit, but also QNMs of near Nariai black holes (
r_{+}\rightarrow r_{c}
limit). We will use the second case as a control on our calculations.

Analysing the equation (
11
), in the
r_{1}\rightarrow r_{2}
limit, we have
\kappa_{i}\rightarrow 0
. Given that in (
40
)
\omega
shows up always divided by
k_{i}
, and the same happens in (
42
) if expanded around
y\,=\,0
, we expect that QNM frequencies will vanish proportionally to
k_{i}
. Thus, we will work with the dimensionless parameter
\rho\,=\,\frac{\omega}{k_{1}}
. Due to
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
being very close, we expect that
A
(defined in (
32
)) will be approximately constant in
r\,\in\,(r_{1},\,r_{2})
. We expect the same from
F
defined through

f(r)=(r\,-\,r_{1})\,(r\,-\,r_{2})\,F(r)\quad.

(43)

Hence, a Taylor expansion of (
42
) about
r=r_{1}
should yield accurate results. We now define the perturbation parameter
\varepsilon=\frac{r_{2}-r_{1}}{r_{1}}
, and expand
\tilde{R}
and
\rho
:

\begin{split}\tilde{R}(r)&=\tilde{R}_{0}(r)\,+\,\varepsilon\,\tilde{R}_{1}(r)\,+\,...\\
\rho&=\rho_{0}\,+\,\varepsilon\,\rho_{1}\,+\,...\end{split}

(44)

Plugging this into equation (
42
), to zero order in
\varepsilon
, we get:

y(1\,-\,y)\,R_{0}^{\prime\prime}(y)\,+\,\left(1\,-\,i\,\rho_{0}\,-\,2\left(1\,-\,i\,\rho_{0}\right)y\right)\,R_{0}^{\prime}(y)\\
+\left(\frac{A\left(r_{1}\right)}{F\left(r_{1}\right)}\,+\,\rho_{0}^{2}\,+\,i\,\rho_{0}\right)\,R_{0}(y)\,=\,0\quad.

(45)

This is the hyper-geometric equation. In the canonical form, we write this equation as:

y(1-y)w^{\prime\prime}(y)+\left(c-\left(a+b+1\right)y\right)w^{\prime}(y)-abw(y)=0\quad.

(46)

This result is analogous to the one obtained in
Moitra 2020
, as the near horizon geometry of Black Holes is approximately
AdS_{2}
. From (
45
) we read off the coefficients a, b and c:

\begin{split}a&=-i\,\rho_{0}\,+\,\frac{1-\Delta}{2}\quad,\\
b&=-i\,\rho_{0}\,+\,\frac{1+\Delta}{2}\quad,\\
c&=1\,-\,i\,\rho_{0}\quad.\end{split}

(47)

where,

\Delta=\sqrt{1\,+\,\frac{4\,A(r_{1})}{F(r_{1})}}\quad.\\

(48)

This result is still valid for
1+4\,A(r_{1})/F(r_{1})<0
. Here,
\Delta
will be the root of a negative number, and we must choose a branch to define it. Notice that swapping the branch choice is equivalent to swapping the definition of a and b, so this choice can be arbitrary.

The hypergeometric equation is solved by hypergeometric functions. These have been well documented in the literature (see e.g.
Bender and Orszag 1978
;
Kristensson 2010
). Following the procedure in
Dias et al. 2019b
, we prove that requiring smoothness at
y\,=\,0
and
y\,=\,1
implies a quantization of a or b. We have that either
a\,=\,-k
or
b\,=\,-k
with
k\,=\,0,\,1,\,2,\,...
. Solving this with respect to
\rho_{0}
we obtain two families of QNMs, defined as type-a and type-b, with frequencies:

\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{a}}{k_{+}}\,

\displaystyle=\,-i\left(\frac{1-\Delta}{2}+\,k\right)\,+\,\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon\right)\quad,

(49)

\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{b}}{k_{+}}\,

\displaystyle=\,-i\left(\frac{1+\Delta}{2}+\,k\right)\,+\,\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon\right)\quad.

(50)

Taking
r_{1}=r_{+}
and
r_{2}=r_{c}
, we can estimate the spectrum of exterior modes in the Nariai limit (
r_{+}\rightarrow r_{c}
). Here
\Delta
will be purely imaginary as discussed above.
\omega_{a}
and
\omega_{b}
will have the same imaginary part and symmetric real parts. In figure
3(a)
, we compare the spectrum of Nariai modes obtained numerically (see section
IV.3
), with this analytic prediction. These are consistent with the WKB approximation in
Dias et al. 2018a
and the supplementary material of
Cardoso et al. 2018a
.

Similarly, taking
r_{1}=r_{+}
and
r_{2}=r_{-}
, we obtain an estimate for the interior frequency spectrum of near extremal black holes. Here,
\Delta
will be real and positive, with no a-priori upper bound. In fact, there are cases where it is greater than 1, leading to modes with positive imaginary frequencies. This is not cause for concern, as there is no relation between interior QNMs and spacetime stability (see
Dias et al. 2019b
). In figure
3(b)
we compare the analytic prediction with the numerical results obtained in
IV.3
.

This approach is entirely analogous to the analytical approach used in
Dias et al. 2018a
to estimate exterior QNM frequencies. In fact, we may extend y to negative values, to explore the behaviour of
R_{0}(y)
in the BH exterior. Taking
R_{0}(y)
, to be smooth in
y\,=\,0
and exponentially decaying at
y\rightarrow-\infty
, we get a mode that is smooth in
r_{+}
and exponentially decaying for
r>r_{+}
. These are precisely the conditions required in
Dias et al. 2018a
to estimate NE modes. It is important to mention that the decaying condition is purely empirical, motivated by the behaviour of solutions observed in numerical calculations. Analysing the solutions of equation (
46
) in terms of hypergeometric functions, we prove that exponential decay at
y\rightarrow-\infty
implies smoothness at
y=1
. This means that extending the NE QNMs of
Dias et al. 2018a
to the interior of the BH, we get interior QNMs. Hence, up to 0th order in
\varepsilon
the spectrum of exterior NE QNM frequencies is a subset of the spectrum of interior QNM frequencies. In fact, NE QNMs coincide with the type-b family of modes defined in (
50
).

This result might seem a bit worrying, as it hints against the conclusion of our paper. In fact, we must compute higher order corrections to distinguish the interior and exterior spectrum. Doing this analytically is hard, and it is not clear how to proceed. Hence, we will resort to numerics, to pinpoint whether there is a difference between interior and exterior QNM frequencies.

To obtain the spectrum of QNMs numerically we approximate the continuous ODE problem by a discrete system of equations, as seen in
Dias et al. 2016

.

For a given interval
[a,\,b]
we may define a Chebyshev grid with size
N_{c}
as the set of points
x_{i}
such that

x_{i}=\frac{(b-a)}{2}\cos\left(\frac{i\,\pi}{N_{c}}\right)\,+\,\frac{a+b}{2},\quad\quad i=0,\,1,\,2,\,...,\,N_{c}\quad.

(51)

Constraining the domain to the discrete Chebyshev grid, we may rewrite the action of the derivative using matrix multiplication. We denote this matrix
D_{c}
, (see
Trefethen 2000
for an explicit definition). Multiplying equation (

) by

and sampling it in the Chebyshev grid above, we obtain a quadratic matrix eigenvalue problem. The eigenvectors will be discrete approximations of solutions to equation (
42
), smooth at
y\,=\,0
and
y\,=\,1
. The eigenvalues will correspond to approximations of the Quasinormal Frequencies.

To find the frequencies we use a combination of two methods. We start by obtaining a direct eigenvector decomposition of the system using
Mathematica
’s native
Eigensystem
function. This method yields an approximation of the full frequency spectrum. Bear in mind it is very important to use a software that supports arbitrary precision to obtain good results (we used up to 500 digits of precision in intermediate calculations). This procedure, is not the best to approximate the frequency of individual modes. It is too computational intensive, and does not allow arbitrary high precision of a given frequency. Bearing this in mind, we apply the version of the Newton-Raphson method described in
Dias et al. 2010
;
Dias et al. 2016
;
Tisseur and Meerbergen 2001
using the direct method as a seed. Although faster and more precise, this method requires a very accurate initial frequency estimate to converge. In figure
6
, we plot the numerical error on the calculation of a given quasinormal frequency as a function of the Chebyshev grid size
(N_{c})
. As expected, we see that the usage of a Chebyshev grid actually guarantees that this method has exponential convergence.

In the analysis below, we computed the lowest lying NE exterior and type-b interior QNMs (see equation (
50
) for a definition) in a non-trivial region of the parameter space. To achieve this, we used the direct method to obtain these modes for a given RNdS BH, and then used them as seed for a Newton-Raphson method applied to BHs with similar mass and charge. We then used the results as estimates on other similar BHs and henceforth. For each frequency calculated, we repeated this procedure twice, once with a Chebyshev grid size of 500, and then 550. The difference between the obtained frequencies was used to estimate the precision of our calculations. We always made sure this was at least one order of magnitude below the difference between interior and exterior QNM frequencies. This limit would only saturate close to extremality, otherwise, the precision was usually much higher than the difference in frequencies.

IV.4
Comparison between interior and exterior modes for gravitoelectromagnetic perturbations

As seen in section
IV.2
, in the NE limit of RNdS BHs, there is a correspondence between the spectrum of NE exterior modes and type-b interior modes. Using numerics, we verified whether this correspondence extends to the remainder of parameter space. To do so, we explicitly computed the lowest lying frequency of the NE family of exterior modes (
\omega_{\text{ext}}
) and the lowest lying frequency of type-b interior modes (
\omega_{\text{int}}
). To make sure we chose the appropriate families, we compared with the analytic prediction in the near extremal limit (equation (

\Phi_{+}\,(l=1)
and
\Phi_{-}\,(l=2)
gravitoeletric QNMs in a large class of RNdS black holes.

\gamma=\left(\frac{\omega_{\text{in}}\,-\,\omega_{\text{ext}}}{\omega_{\text{in}}\,+\,\omega_{\text{ext}}}\right).

(52)

V
Strong cosmic censorship in the Kerr-dS black hole

V.1
Wave equation and QNM

As a toy example, we will focus in the behaviour of massless scalar fields in Kerr-dS spacetime. Our argument should be extendible to other fields without much difficulty. In the following, we will show that generic solutions of

\Box\Phi\,=\,0

(53)

do not have finite energy at the Cauchy horizon. We will take purely ingoing modes at the event horizon, with the frequency of an exterior mode, and scatter it through the BH interior, studying their regularity at the Cauchy horizon. In a Kerr-dS background, the radial and angular parts of the KG equation are given by the Teukolsky equation. Plugging (
18
) into (
17
), we obtain the following equations:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&{}\derivative{\theta}\left(\Delta_{\theta}\sin\theta\derivative{\Theta_{\omega l m}}{\theta}\right)\\
&{}-(m-a\omega\sin^{2}\theta)^{2}\frac{\Sigma^{2}}{\Delta_{\theta}\sin\theta}\Theta_{\omega lm}(\theta)\\
&{}+K_{lm}\sin(\theta)\Theta_{\omega lm}(\theta)\,=\,0\quad,\end{split}

(54)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\derivative{r}\left(\Delta_{r}\derivative{R_{\omega l m}(r)}{r}\right)\\
&+\left[\frac{\Sigma^{2}}{\Delta_{r}}(\omega(r^{2}+a^{2})-am)^{2}\,-\,K_{lm}\right]R_{\omega lm}(r)=0\quad,\end{split}

(55)

here
m\in\mathbb{Z},\,|m|\leq l
and
K_{lm}
is the constant arising from separation of variables. For ease of notation we will henceforth drop the
\omega lm
subscript.

Equation (
55
) has four regular singular points at the roots
r_{i}
of
\Delta_{r}(r_{i})
. For
r>0
this happens at the Cauchy horizon
(r_{-})
, the event horizon
(r_{+})
and the Cosmological horizon
(r_{c})
. Solutions to this equation are defined in (
20
), (
21
) and (
22
).

The analysis of section
III.2
tells us that for a QNM with

\frac{|Im(\omega)|}{\kappa_{-}}<\frac{1}{2}\quad,

(56)

\Phi_{in,-}
will have infinite energy at the Cauchy horizon. In
Dias et al. 2018b
, using a WKB analysis, it was proved that taking
l
large enough we find modes respecting condition (
56
). We will now prove that for these modes the value of
\mathcal{A}(\omega)
in equation
(
29
)
is non-zero, validating the conclusions of
Dias et al. 2018b
.

V.2
Solving the wave equation in the BH interior

Below, we will study the continuation of an exterior QNM solution, onto the interior of the BH, and show that it has
\mathcal{A}(\omega)\neq 0
. To do so, we fix
K_{lm}
and
\omega
to the values obtained for WKB QNMs defined in
Dias et al. 2018b
. Exterior QNMs are purely ingoing at the event horizon. We use this to set the initial conditions for the wave function in the BH interior. Using a WKB approximation, we solve the radial equation, decomposing it in the
R_{in/out,-}
basis, using equation (
29
).

Consider
r_{*}
defined in (
14
), such that
r_{*}(r_{+})=+\infty
and
r_{*}(r_{-})=-\infty
. In terms of
r_{*}
, the radial equation is

\derivative[2]{S}{r_*}\,+\,U(r,\omega,l,m)\,S(r)\,=\,0\quad,

(57)

where
S(r)\,=\,\sqrt{r^{2}+a^{2}}\,R(r)
, and

U=\left(\omega-\frac{am}{r^{2}+a^{2}}\right)^{2}-\frac{K_{lm}\,\Delta_{r}}{\Sigma^{2}(r^{2}+a^{2})^{2}}\\
+\,(r^{2}+a^{2})^{-4}\Bigg[-\Delta_{r}^{2}(r^{2}+a^{2})+3\Delta_{r}^{2}r^{2}\Bigg.\\
\Bigg.-r\Delta_{r}\,\derivative{\Delta_r}{r}\,(r^{2}+a^{2})\Bigg]\quad.

(58)

The modes studied in
Dias et al. 2018b
, are such that
m=l
. Using a WKB approximation, the authors show that in the large
l
limit:

\omega=\omega_{R}+i\,\omega_{I}=l\,\Omega_{c}+\mathcal{O}(1)\quad,

(59)

here
\Omega_{c}
is the rotation frequency of a photon propagating in the inner photon sphere, outside the black hole. This corresponds to
\Omega^{+}_{c}
defined in equation (4.19) of
Dias et al. 2018b
. We expand
K_{ll}
in powers of
l
:

K_{ll}\,=\,l^{2}\,\lambda_{-2}\,+\,l\,\lambda_{-1}\,+\,...

(60)

In appendix
A
, we prove that this expansion is valid and
\lambda_{-2}
is real and positive.
We can now define:

U_{0}=\left[\left(\Omega_{c}-\frac{a}{r^{2}+a^{2}}\right)^{2}-\frac{\,\lambda_{-2}\,\Delta_{r}}{\Sigma^{2}(r^{2}+a^{2})^{2}}\right]\quad,

(61)

so that

U=l^{2}U_{0}\,+\,\mathcal{O}(l)\quad\text{for}\quad l>>1\quad.

(62)

Notice that
U_{0}(r_{*})
is a real function of
r_{*}
, whereas
U(r_{*})
has non vanishing imaginary part. We explicitly verified, that for Kerr-dS BHs:

\Omega(r_{-})>\Omega_{c}>\Omega(r_{+})\quad,

(63)

where
\Omega(r)
is defined in (
16
).

Using equations (
63
), (
60
) and the fact that
\Delta_{r}(r)<0
in
r\in[r_{-},r_{+}]
, we can establish that
U_{0}(r_{*})>0
in
r\in[r_{-},r_{+}]
. In the BH exterior
\Delta_{r}(r)>0
and this is no longer the case.

We now solve equation (
57
) using a standard WKB expansion (see
Bender and Orszag 1978
):

S(r_{*})=\exp\left(\frac{1}{l}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\delta^{n}S_{n}(r_{*})\right).

(64)

Truncating to
n=1
, we obtain that in the large
l
limit:

S(r_{*},\omega,l)=\sqrt{r^{2}+a^{2}}\,R_{in,+}\approx\\
U_{0}(r_{*})^{-\frac{1}{4}}\Bigg[A(\omega,l)\exp\left(il\int_{0}^{r_{*}}U_{0}(s)^{\frac{1}{2}}\differential s\right)\\
+B(\omega,l)\exp\left(-il\int_{0}^{r_{*}}U_{0}(s)^{\frac{1}{2}}\differential x\right)\Bigg]\quad,

(65)

where
A(\omega,l)
and
B(\omega,l)
are integration constants.

Given that
U_{0}(r_{*})>0
everywhere, equation (
65
) is valid everywhere. Physically, this means that to leading order in
l
, there is no scattering of WKB modes propagating in the BH interior. However, to get
\mathcal{A}(\omega)=0
we would need a purely ingoing mode at
\mathcal{H}^{+}
to be fully scattered into an outgoing mode at
\mathcal{CH}_{R}
. Therefore we can already anticipate that no violations of SCC in the Kerr-dS spacetime will be found. Nevertheless, lets explicitly check this below.

V.3
Behaviour near the horizons

For ease of notation, define

\omega_{+}=\omega-l\,\Omega_{+}\quad,\quad\omega_{-}=\omega-l\,\Omega_{-}\quad.

(66)

For
r_{*}\to-\infty
(near
\mathcal{H}_{+}
), using equations (
58
) and (
59
), we obtain:

\lim_{r_{*}\to-\infty}\int_{0}^{r_{*}}\left(l\sqrt{U_{0}}-\omega_{+}\right)\,=\,\alpha_{+}\,l\quad,

(67)

where
\alpha_{+}=\mathcal{O}(1)
as
l\to\infty
. Similarly, for
r_{*}\to\infty
(near
\mathcal{CH}_{+}
), we get

\lim_{r_{*}\to\infty}\int_{0}^{r_{*}}\left(l\sqrt{U_{0}}-\omega_{-}\right)\,=\,\alpha_{-}\,l\quad.

(68)

Dropping the finite term
\sqrt{r^{2}+a^{2}}
in front of
R(r)
, we find that near
\mathcal{H}_{+}
,

R(r_{*})\sim\left(\frac{\omega_{+}}{l}\right)^{-\frac{1}{4}}\Bigg[A(\omega,l)e^{-i\left(\omega_{+}r_{*}\,+\,\alpha_{+}l\right)}\\
+B(\omega,l)e^{i\left(\omega_{+}r_{*}\alpha\,+\,l\right)}\Bigg]\quad\text{as}\quad r_{*}\rightarrow-\infty\quad.

(69)

Similarly, at
\mathcal{CH}_{R}^{+}
, we get:

R(r_{*})\sim\left(\frac{\omega_{-}}{l}\right)^{-\frac{1}{4}}\Bigg[A(\omega,l)e^{-i\left(\omega_{-}r_{*}\,+\,\alpha_{-}l\right)}\\
+B(\omega,l)e^{i\left(\omega_{-}r_{*}\,+\,\alpha_{-}l\right)}\Bigg]\quad\text{as}\quad r_{*}\rightarrow\infty\quad.

(70)

Now, we take
R(r_{*})
to be
R_{in,+}
. The ingoing condition (equation (
21
)) at
\mathcal{H}_{+}
yields
R_{in,+}(r_{*})\sim e^{-i\omega_{+}r_{*}}
as
r_{*}\rightarrow\infty
. This gives:

\displaystyle B(\omega,l)

\displaystyle\sim\mathcal{O}(l^{-1})\quad,

(71)

\displaystyle A(\omega,l)

\displaystyle\sim\mathcal{O}(1)\quad.

(72)

Now, focusing in the behaviour near
\mathcal{CH}_{R}^{+}
, using equation (
29
), we obtain:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\omega_{-}}{l}\right)^{-\frac{1}{4}}A(\omega,l)e^{-i\,\alpha_{-}l}\quad,

(73)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\omega_{-}}{l}\right)^{-\frac{1}{4}}B(\omega,l)e^{i\,\alpha_{-}l}\quad,

(74)

where

\frac{\omega_{-}}{l}=\mathcal{O}(1)\quad.

(75)

In particular, in large
l
limit,
\mathcal{B}
vanishes, so
\mathcal{A}(\omega)
must be non zero, or we would have the trivial solution. Hence, we prove that
\mathcal{A}(\omega)\neq 0
for
\omega
a QMN frequency. The conclusion of
Dias et al. 2018b
is maintained, preserving SCC for the Kerr-dS BH.

VI
Discussion

In this paper, we proved that there is no coincidence between the interior and exterior spectrum of QNMs for Kerr-dS and RNdS BHs. Hence, there is no new violation of SCC for these spacetimes. As further work, it would be interesting to relate the discussion in this paper with the quantum instability of Cauchy horizons of Kerr-dS and RNdS black holes, found in
Hollands et al. 2020a
. In fact, this instability is proportional to a factor, that depends on the reflection and transmition coefficients of waves around the BH (see equation 123 of that paper). We suspect that this function could vanish, if interior and and exterior QNMs coincide, akin to
\mathcal{A}(\omega)
. In fact, in the same paper, the authors study the case of the 3-dimensional BTZ black hole, and they find that due to the hypergeometric nature of the wave equation, this factor vanishes identically in there.

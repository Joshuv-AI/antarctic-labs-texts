Integrable equations associated with the finite-temperature deformation of the discrete Bessel point process
Mattia Cafasso, Giulio Ruzza

Abstract
We study the finite-temperature deformation of the discrete Bessel point process. We show that its largest particle distribution satisfies a reduction of the 2D Toda equation, as well as a discrete version of the integro-differential Painlevé II equation of Amir–Corwin–Quastel, and we compute initial conditions for the Poissonization parameter equal to 0. As proved by Betea and Bouttier, in a suitable continuum limit the last particle distribution converges to that of the finite-temperature Airy point process. We show that the reduction of the 2D Toda equation reduces to the Korteweg–de Vries equation, as well as the discrete integro-differential Painlevé II equation reduces to its continuous version. Our approach is based on the discrete analogue of Its–Izergin–Korepin–Slavnov theory of integrable operators developed by Borodin and Deift.

1.1
Finite-temperature discrete Bessel point process and the 2D Toda equation

In this paper we study the
finite-temperature discrete Bessel point process
, which is the determinantal point process on
\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}:=\mathbb{Z}+\tfrac{1}{2}
with correlation kernel

K_{\sigma}^{\mathsf{Be}}(a,b)=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}}\sigma(l)\mathrm{J}_{a+l}(2L)\mathrm{J}_{b+l}(2L),\qquad a,b\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime},

(1.1)

where
L>0
is a parameter,
\mathrm{J}_{k}(\cdot)
is the Bessel function of first kind of order
k
, and
\sigma:\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}\to[0,1]
is a function such that
\sigma\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}\cap(-\infty,0))
.
The fact that the kernel (
1.1
) actually induces a determinantal point process on
\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}
and the role of the decay conditions on
\sigma
at
-\infty
will be clarified in Section
2

The specialization
\sigma=1_{\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}_{+}}
of (
1.1

\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}_{+}:=\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}\cap(0,+\infty)

[

K^{\mathsf{Be}}(a,b)=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}_{+}}\mathrm{J}_{a+l}(2L)\mathrm{J}_{b+l}(2L)=L\,\frac{\mathrm{J}_{a-\frac{1}{2}}(2L)\mathrm{J}_{b+\frac{1}{2}}(2L)-\mathrm{J}_{a+\frac{1}{2}}(2L)\mathrm{J}_{b-\frac{1}{2}}(2L)}{a-b},\quad a,b\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}.

The discrete Bessel point process has the following combinatorial interpretation.
Let
\mathbb{Y}
be the set of integer partitions (or, equivalently, Young diagrams).
Namely, elements

\lambda_{i}\geq\lambda_{i+1}

In particular, for

The

\mathbb{P}_{\sf{Plan}}(\{\lambda\}):={\rm e}^{-L^{2}}L^{2|\lambda|}\left(\frac{\dim\lambda}{|\lambda|!}\right)^{2},\qquad\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}.

The kernel (
1.1

\sigma(l)=(1+u^{l})^{-1},\qquad l\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime},

Theorem I

For all

\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial L^{2}}\log Q_{\sigma}(L,s)+\frac{1}{L}\frac{\partial}{\partial L}\log Q_{\sigma}(L,s)+4=4\,\frac{Q_{\sigma}(L,s+1)Q_{\sigma}(L,s-1)}{Q_{\sigma}(L,s)^{2}}.

2
Preliminaries on the discrete Bessel kernel

The Bessel functions satisfy
[
19
, eq. 10.6.1]

L\bigl(\mathrm{J}_{k+1}(2L)+\mathrm{J}_{k-1}(2L)\bigr)=k\mathrm{J}_{k}(2L),\qquad k\in\mathbb{C},

(2.1)

and
[
19
, eq. 10.4.1]

\mathrm{J}_{-k}(2L)=(-1)^{k}\mathrm{J}_{k}(2L),\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z}.

(2.2)

Lemma 2.1
.

As
k\to+\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{J}_{k}(2L)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi k}}\left(\frac{\mathrm{e}L}{k}\right)^{k},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\kappa}\mathrm{J}_{\kappa}(2L)\biggr|_{\kappa=k}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{\log(L/k)}{\sqrt{2\pi k}}\left(\frac{\mathrm{e}L}{k}\right)^{k},

(2.3)

and, as
k\to+\infty
through integer values, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{J}_{-k}(2L)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{(-1)^{k}}{\sqrt{2\pi k}}\left(\frac{\mathrm{e}L}{k}\right)^{k},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\kappa}\mathrm{J}_{\kappa}(2L)\biggr|_{\kappa=-k}

\displaystyle\sim(-1)^{k+1}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{k}}\left(\frac{k}{\mathrm{e}L}\right)^{k}.

(2.4)

Proof.

For real
k>-1/2
, we can represent the Bessel function by the Poisson integral
[
19
, eq. 10.9.4]

\mathrm{J}_{k}(2L)=\frac{L^{k}}{\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma(k+\tfrac{1}{2})}\int_{0}^{\pi}\cos(2L\cos\theta)\mathrm{e}^{2k\log(\sin\theta)}\mathrm{d}\theta.

(2.5)

Since
\theta\mapsto\log(\sin\theta)
has a unique non-degenerate maximum at
\theta=\pi/2
for
\theta\in(0,\pi)
, it suffices to use Laplace’s method to obtain the large
k
asymptotics of the integral. Combining with Stirling’s asymptotics, we obtain the first relation in (
2.3
).
The first relation in (
2.4
) then follows from (
2.2
).

Next, by (
2.5
), for real
k>-1/2
,

\frac{\partial}{\partial k}\mathrm{J}_{k}(2L)=\frac{\partial}{\partial k}\biggl(\log\frac{L^{k}}{\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma(k+\tfrac{1}{2})}\biggr)\mathrm{J}_{k}(2L)+\frac{L^{k}}{\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma(k+\tfrac{1}{2})}\int_{0}^{\pi}2\log(\sin\theta)\cos(2L\cos\theta)\mathrm{e}^{2k\log(\sin\theta)}\mathrm{d}\theta.

(2.6)

Using the asymptotics for the digamma function
\Gamma^{\prime}/\Gamma
, as well as the already established first relation in (
2.3
) for the first term, and again Laplace’s method for the second term, we obtain (after some computations) the second relation in (
2.3
).
Finally, for the last relation we use that, for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have
[
19
, 10.2.4]
,

(-1)^{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial\kappa}\mathrm{J}_{\kappa}(2L)\biggr|_{\kappa=-k}=\pi\mathrm{Y}_{k}(2L)-\frac{\partial}{\partial\kappa}\mathrm{J}_{\kappa}(2L)\biggr|_{\kappa=k}

(2.7)

where
\mathrm{Y}_{k}(\cdot)
is the Bessel function of second kind of order
k
, and it suffices to use the second relation in (
2.3
) along with the asymptotics
\mathrm{Y}_{k}(2L)\sim-\sqrt{2/(\pi k)}\,(\mathrm{e}L/k)^{-k}
as
k\to+\infty

[
19
, eq. 10.19.2]
.
∎

Let us recall the discrete Bessel kernel
K^{\mathsf{Be}}(a,b)=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}_{+}}\mathrm{J}_{a+l}(2L)\mathrm{J}_{b+l}(2L)
, as in (
1.2
).
It is worth observing that only Bessel functions of integer order appear in this expression.

Lemma 2.2
.

We have

\displaystyle K^{\mathsf{Be}}(a,b)

\displaystyle=L\,\frac{\mathrm{J}_{a-\frac{1}{2}}(2L)\mathrm{J}_{b+\frac{1}{2}}(2L)-\mathrm{J}_{a+\frac{1}{2}}(2L)\mathrm{J}_{b-\frac{1}{2}}(2L)}{a-b}\,,

\displaystyle a,b\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime},\ a\not=b,

(2.8)

\displaystyle K^{\mathsf{Be}}(a,a)

\displaystyle=L\biggl(\mathrm{J}_{a+\frac{1}{2}}(2L)\frac{\partial\mathrm{J}_{a-\frac{1}{2}}(2L)}{\partial a}-\mathrm{J}_{a-\frac{1}{2}}(2L)\frac{\partial\mathrm{J}_{a+\frac{1}{2}}(2L)}{\partial a}\biggr)\,,

\displaystyle a\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}.

(2.9)

In particular,
K^{\mathsf{Be}}(a,a)
is a decreasing function of
a\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}
satisfying

K^{\mathsf{Be}}(a,a)\to 1,\qquad a\to-\infty,\ a\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}.

(2.10)

Proof.

Fix
M\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}_{+}
.
Using (
2.1
) we compute, for any real
a\not=b
, omitting the argument
2L
of the Bessel functions,

\displaystyle(a-b)\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}_{+}\cap[\tfrac{1}{2},M]}\mathrm{J}_{a+l}\mathrm{J}_{b+l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}_{+}\cap[\tfrac{1}{2},M]}\left((a+l)\mathrm{J}_{a+l}\mathrm{J}_{b+l}-(b+l)\mathrm{J}_{a+l}\mathrm{J}_{b+l}\right)

\displaystyle=L\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}_{+}\cap[\tfrac{1}{2},M]}\left(\mathrm{J}_{a+l-1}\mathrm{J}_{b+l}+\mathrm{J}_{a+l+1}\mathrm{J}_{b+l}-\mathrm{J}_{a+l}\mathrm{J}_{b+l-1}-\mathrm{J}_{a+l}\mathrm{J}_{b+l+1}\right)

\displaystyle=L\bigl(\mathrm{J}_{a-\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{J}_{b+\frac{1}{2}}+\mathrm{J}_{a+M+1}\mathrm{J}_{b+M}-\mathrm{J}_{a+\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{J}_{b-\frac{1}{2}}-\mathrm{J}_{a+M}\mathrm{J}_{b+M+1}\bigr),

(2.11)

where in the last step we telescope the sum.
Sending
M\to+\infty
and using the first asymptotics in (
2.3
) and (
2.4
), we obtain (
2.8
).
Sending instead
a\to b
first and then sending
M\to+\infty
we obtain (
2.9
).
Finally, it suffices to insert (
2.4
) in (
2.9
) to obtain (
2.10
).
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

For all
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}
we have
\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}}\mathrm{J}_{a+l}(2L)\mathrm{J}_{b+l}(2L)=\delta_{a,b}
.

Proof.

Let
M,N\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}
with
N<0<M
. By using a similar argument as in (
2.11
), we obtain, for real
a\not=b
,

(a-b)\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}\cap[N,M]}\mathrm{J}_{a+l}\mathrm{J}_{b+l}=L\bigl(\mathrm{J}_{a+N-1}\mathrm{J}_{b+N}-\mathrm{J}_{a+N}\mathrm{J}_{b+N-1}+\mathrm{J}_{a+M+1}\mathrm{J}_{b+M}-\mathrm{J}_{a+M}\mathrm{J}_{b+M+1}\bigr),

(2.12)

and so sending
M\to+\infty,N\to-\infty
and using (
2.3
) we obtain the thesis for
a\not=b
. Sending instead
a\to b
first, and then sending
M\to+\infty,N\to-\infty
using (
2.4
) and (
2.10
) we obtain the thesis for
a=b
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

We have
0\leq\mathcal{K}_{\sigma}^{\mathsf{Be}}\leq 1
.
Moreover, if
\sigma\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}\cap(-\infty,0))
, the operator
\mathcal{P}_{s}\mathcal{K}_{\sigma}^{\mathsf{Be}}\mathcal{P}_{s}
is trace-class, where
\mathcal{P}_{s}
is the orthogonal projector onto
\ell^{2}(\{s+1,s+2,\dots\})
, for all
s\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
2.3
that the operator
\mathcal{J}
induced by the kernel
\mathrm{J}_{a+b}(2L)
is an unitary involution of
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{\prime})
, i.e.
\mathcal{J}=\mathcal{J}^{\dagger}=\mathcal{J}^{-1}
. By a slight abuse of notation, denote with
\sigma
the operator of multiplication by
\sigma
, i.e. the operator on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{\prime})
induced by the kernel
\sigma(a)\delta(a,b)
.
Then, by definition,
\mathcal{K}_{\sigma}^{\mathsf{Be}}=\mathcal{J}\sigma\mathcal{J}
.
Let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
be the scalar product on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{\prime})
: since
0\leq\sigma\leq 1
, we have

\langle(\mathcal{K}_{\sigma}^{\mathsf{Be}})^{2}\psi,\psi\rangle=\langle\sigma^{2}\mathcal{J}\psi,\mathcal{J}\psi\rangle\leq\langle\sigma\mathcal{J}\psi,\mathcal{J}\psi\rangle=\langle\mathcal{K}_{\sigma}^{\mathsf{Be}}\psi,\psi\rangle,\qquad\mbox{for all }\psi\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}).

(2.13)

Therefore,
\mathcal{K}_{\sigma}^{\mathsf{Be}}\geq(\mathcal{K}_{\sigma}^{\mathsf{Be}})^{2}\geq 0
, which also implies
1-\mathcal{K}_{\sigma}^{\mathsf{Be}}\geq(1-\mathcal{K}_{\sigma}^{\mathsf{Be}})^{2}\geq 0
.

For the second statement, observe that
\mathcal{P}_{s}\mathcal{K}_{\sigma}^{\mathsf{Be}}\mathcal{P}_{s}=\mathcal{H}_{s}\mathcal{H}^{\dagger}_{s}
where
\mathcal{H}_{s}
is induced by kernel

H_{s}(a,b)=\mathbf{1}_{a>s}\mathrm{J}_{a+b}(2L)\sqrt{\sigma}(b),\qquad a,b\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}.

(2.14)

For a fixed
s\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}
, the operator
\mathcal{H}_{s}
is Hilbert–Schmidt on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{\prime})
if and only if

\sum_{a,b\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}}|H_{s}(a,b)|^{2}=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}_{+}}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}}\mathbf{1}_{a>s}\mathrm{J}_{a+b}(2L)^{2}\sigma(b)=L\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}}\sigma(l-s-\tfrac{1}{2})K^{\mathsf{Be}}(l,l)<+\infty.

(2.15)

The convergence of the latter series at
l\to+\infty
follows from (
2.9
) and the first asymptotic relations in (
2.3
) and (
2.4
), along with
0\leq\sigma\leq 1
.
The convergence at
l\to-\infty
follows instead by (
2.9
) and the second asymptotic relations in (
2.3
) and (
2.4
), along with the summability assumption on
\sigma
.
∎

It follows from this lemma and the Macchi–Soshnikov criterion
[
31
, Theorem 3]
that there exists a unique determinantal point process on
\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}
whose correlation kernel is
K_{\sigma}^{\mathsf{Be}}
.
It also follows from the general theory of determinantal point processes, e.g., from
[
31
, Theorem 4]
, that this process has almost surely a largest particle

, whose distribution is given by the Fredholm determinant as in (
1.8
).

Let us introduce the operator
\mathcal{M}_{s}
on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{\prime})
, for
s\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}
, induced by the kernel

M_{s}(a,b)=\sqrt{\sigma}\bigl(a-s-\tfrac{1}{2}\bigr)K^{\mathsf{Be}}(a,b)\sqrt{\sigma}\bigl(b-s-\tfrac{1}{2}\bigr),\qquad a,b\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}.

(3.1)

where
K^{\mathsf{Be}}
is as in (
1.2
).
The operator
\mathcal{M}_{s}
is of
discrete integrable form

[
8
,
9
]
, namely, using (
2.8
) the off-diagonal entries of the kernel can be expressed as

M_{s}(a,b)=\frac{\mathbf{f}^{\top}(a)\mathbf{g}(b)}{a-b},\qquad a,b,\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime},\ a\not=b,

(3.2)

where

\mathbf{f}(a):=\sqrt{\sigma}(a-s-\tfrac{1}{2})\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{J}_{a-\frac{1}{2}}(2L)\\
L\mathrm{J}_{a+\frac{1}{2}}(2L)\end{pmatrix},\qquad\mathbf{g}(b):=\sqrt{\sigma}(b-s-\tfrac{1}{2})\begin{pmatrix}L\mathrm{J}_{b+\frac{1}{2}}(2L)\\
-\mathrm{J}_{b-\frac{1}{2}}(2L)\end{pmatrix}\,.

(3.3)

Using (
2.9
) we can express the diagonal entries as

M_{s}(a,a)=\sigma(a-s-\tfrac{1}{2})K^{\mathsf{Be}}(a,a)=L\,\sigma(a-s-\tfrac{1}{2})\,\biggl(\mathrm{J}_{a+\frac{1}{2}}(2L)\frac{\partial\mathrm{J}_{a-\frac{1}{2}}(2L)}{\partial a}-\mathrm{J}_{a-\frac{1}{2}}(2L)\frac{\partial\mathrm{J}_{a+\frac{1}{2}}(2L)}{\partial a}\biggr).

(3.4)

Lemma 3.1
.

(i)
The operator
\mathcal{M}_{s}
is trace-class and we have

Q_{\sigma}(L,s)=\det(1-\mathcal{M}_{s}).

(3.5)

(ii)
The identity (
1.9
) holds true.

(iii)
For all
s\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}
such that
Q_{\sigma}(L,s)>0
, we have

\frac{Q_{\sigma}(L,s-1)}{Q_{\sigma}(L,s)}-1=\mathrm{tr}\,\left((1-\mathcal{M}_{s})^{-1}\mathcal{N}_{s}\right),

(3.6)

where
\mathcal{N}_{s}
is the rank one operator on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{\prime})
induced by the kernel

N_{s}(a,b)=\sqrt{\sigma}(a-s-\tfrac{1}{2})\,\mathrm{J}_{a-\frac{1}{2}}(2L)\,\sqrt{\sigma}(b-s-\tfrac{1}{2})\,\mathrm{J}_{b-\frac{1}{2}}(2L).

(3.7)

Proof.

(i)

We have

Q_{\sigma}(L,s)=\det(1-\mathcal{P}_{s}\mathcal{K}^{\mathsf{Be}}_{\sigma}\mathcal{P}_{s})=\det(1-\mathcal{H}_{s}\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\dagger})=\det(1-\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\dagger}\mathcal{H}_{s}),

(3.8)

where
\mathcal{H}_{s}
is induced by the kernel (
2.14
).
Let
\mathcal{T}
be the shift operator on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{\prime})
, induced by the kernel
T(a,b)=\delta_{a,b+1}
.
It is straightforward to verify that
\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\dagger}\mathcal{H}_{s}=\mathcal{T}^{s+\tfrac{1}{2}}\mathcal{M}_{s}\mathcal{T}^{-s-\tfrac{1}{2}}
.
This identity implies that
\mathcal{M}_{s}
is trace-class, and, by combining it with (
3.8
), we obtain (
3.5
).

(ii)

We have, by the previous point,

Q_{\sigma}(L,s)=\det(1-\sigma(\cdot-s-\tfrac{1}{2})\mathcal{K}^{\mathsf{Be}})

(3.9)

where
\sigma(\cdot-s-\tfrac{1}{2})
denotes the multiplication operator induced by the kernel
\sigma(a-s-\tfrac{1}{2})\delta_{a,b}
.
Then (
1.9
) follows from a general property of determinantal point processes (e.g., see
[
11
, eq. (11.2.4)]
).

(iii)

Let
\mathcal{S}_{s}
be the operator on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{\prime})
induced by the kernel
S_{s}(a,b)=\sqrt{\sigma}(a-s-\tfrac{1}{2})\delta_{a,b}
.
Then,
\mathcal{M}_{s}=\mathcal{S}_{s}\mathcal{K}^{\mathsf{Be}}\mathcal{S}_{s}
where
\mathcal{K}^{\mathsf{Be}}
is the operator induced by the discrete Bessel kernel
K^{\mathsf{Be}}
, defined in (
1.2
).
Recalling the shift operator
\mathcal{T}
, induced by the kernel
T(a,b)=\delta_{a,b+1}
, we observe that
\mathcal{S}_{s-1}=\mathcal{T}^{-1}\mathcal{S}_{s}\mathcal{T}
so that

Q_{\sigma}(L,s-1)=\det\left(1-\mathcal{S}_{s}\mathcal{T}\mathcal{K}^{\mathsf{Be}}\mathcal{T}^{-1}\mathcal{S}_{s}\right)=\det\left(1-\mathcal{M}_{s}+\mathcal{S}_{s}\left(\mathcal{K}^{\mathsf{Be}}-\mathcal{T}\mathcal{K}^{\mathsf{Be}}\mathcal{T}^{-1}\right)\mathcal{S}_{s}\right).

(3.10)

From (
1.2
), we note that
\mathcal{N}_{s}:=\mathcal{S}_{s}\left(\mathcal{K}^{\mathsf{Be}}-\mathcal{T}\mathcal{K}^{\mathsf{Be}}\mathcal{T}^{-1}\right)\mathcal{S}_{s}
is the rank one operator induced by the kernel (
3.7
).
As long as
Q_{\sigma}(L,s)\not=0
, we have

\displaystyle Q_{\sigma}(L,s-1)=\det(1-\mathcal{M}_{s}+\mathcal{N}_{s})

\displaystyle=\det(1-\mathcal{M}_{s})\,\det\bigl(1+(1-\mathcal{M}_{s})^{-1}\mathcal{N}_{s}\bigr)

\displaystyle=Q_{\sigma}(L,s)\biggl(1+\mathrm{tr}\,\bigl((1-\mathcal{M}_{s})^{-1}\mathcal{N}_{s}\bigr)\biggr),

(3.11)

by using a standard formula for the determinant of a rank-one perturbation of the identity.
∎

The next key step is to apply the discrete version of Its–Izergin–Korepin–Slavnov procedure
[
23
]
, as developed for instance by Borodin
[
9
]
.
This approach provides us with an effective way of computing the resolvent operator
\mathcal{R}_{s}:=(1-\mathcal{M}_{s})^{-1}-1
that proves useful to investigate (

).
Indeed, the main result of this theory (Theorem
3.4
below, following from general results of Borodin) is that the resolvent operator
\mathcal{R}_{s}
is also induced by a kernel of integrable form expressed through a meromorphic
2\times 2
matrix-valued function
Y(\cdot)
(parametrically depending on
\sigma,s,L
as well) which is uniquely characterized by the following
discrete Riemann–Hilbert
(RH) conditions.

Discrete RH problem for
Y

(a)

Y(z)
is a
2\times 2
matrix-valued meromorphic function of
z
with simple poles at
\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}
only.

(b)

For all
a\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}
, the function

Y_{a}^{\mathsf{reg}}(z):=Y(z)\left(I-\frac{W_{Y}(a)}{z-a}\right)

(3.12)

has a removable singularity at
z=a
, where

W_{Y}(a):=\frac{\mathbf{f}(a)\mathbf{g}^{\top}(a)}{1-M_{s}(a,a)},\qquad a\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}.

(3.13)

Here,
\mathbf{f}(a)
,
\mathbf{g}(a)
, and
M_{s}(a,a)
are given explicitly in (
3.3
) and (
3.4
).

(c)

We have
\lim_{n\to+\infty}\sup_{|z|=n}|Y(z)-I|=0
, where the limit is taken over integer values of
n
,
I
denotes the
2\times 2
identity matrix and
|\cdot|
denotes any matrix-norm.

Before describing how
Y
allows us to express the resolvent operator
\mathcal{R}_{s}
, we make a few observations.

Remark 3.2
.

(i)

The usual formulation of condition (b) in the discrete RH problem is the slightly different but completely equivalent requirement that, for all
a\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}
, the limit
\lim_{z\to a}Y(z)W_{Y}(a)
exists and that

\lim_{z\to a}Y(z)W_{Y}(a)=\mathop{\mathrm{res}}\limits_{z=a}Y(z)\,\mathrm{d}z.

(3.14)

(ii)

Since
0\leq\sigma(a)\leq 1
and
K^{\mathsf{Be}}(a,a)<1
for all
a\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}
(see Lemma
2.2
), we get
1-M_{s}(a,a)>0
for all
a\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}
.
In particular, (
3.13
) is well defined.

(iii)

For any solution
Y
to the above discrete RH problem, we have
\det Y(z)=1
identically in
z
. Indeed,
\mathbf{f}^{\top}(a)\mathbf{g}(a)=0
implies
W_{Y}^{2}(a)=0
, hence
\det Y(z)=\det Y_{a}^{\mathsf{reg}}(z)
for all
a\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}
and so
\det Y(z)
extends to an entire function of
z
.
By condition (c) together with the maximum modulus theorem we conclude that
\det Y(z)=1
identically in
z
.

(iv)

The solution
Y
to the above discrete RH problem is unique, if any exists.
Indeed, for any two solutions
Y(z)
and
\widetilde{Y}(z)
, the matrix
T(z):=\widetilde{Y}(z)Y^{-1}(z)
has removable singularities at
\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}
by condition (b), because
T(z)=\widetilde{Y}_{a}^{\mathsf{reg}}(z)(Y_{a}^{\mathsf{reg}})^{-1}(z)
for all
a\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}
, hence
T(z)
extends to an entire matrix function of
z
.
By condition (c) together with the maximum modulus theorem, we infer that
T(z)=I
identically in
z
.

(v)

The condition (b) in the discrete RH problem for
Y
implies that
Y(z)
has the following Laurent expansion near
z=a\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}
:

Y(z)=C_{Y}(a)\left(\frac{W_{Y}(a)}{z-a}+I+Y_{1}(a)(z-a)+O\bigl((z-a)^{2}\bigr)\right),

(3.15)

where
C_{Y}(a)
is an invertible matrix.
In particular, although
Y(z)
has a pole as
z\to a\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}
, the limits
\lim_{z\to a}Y(z)\mathbf{f}(a)
and
\lim_{z\to a}Y^{-\top}(z)\mathbf{g}(a)
for
a\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}
exist and are finite.
In the interest of lighter notations, we suppress the limit notation in such expressions, namely for
a\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}
we define

Y(a)\mathbf{f}(a):=\lim_{z\to a}Y(z)\mathbf{f}(a),\quad Y^{-\top}(a)\mathbf{g}(a):=\lim_{z\to a}Y^{-\top}(z)\mathbf{g}(a).

(3.16)

Similarly, for
a\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}
we also define

Y^{\prime}(a)\mathbf{f}(a):=\lim_{z\to a}\frac{\mathrm{d}Y(z)}{\mathrm{d}z}\mathbf{f}(a)=C_{Y}(a)Y_{1}(a)\mathbf{f}(a).

(3.17)

Similarly, the inverse matrix
Y^{-1}
has the Laurent expansion

Y^{-1}(z)=\left(-\frac{W_{Y}(a)}{z-a}+I+\widetilde{Y}_{1}(a)(z-a)+O\bigl((z-a)^{2}\bigr)\right)\widetilde{C}_{Y}(a),

(3.18)

where
\widetilde{C}_{Y}(a)
is an invertible matrix, which does not necessarily coincide with
C_{Y}^{-1}(a)
.

(vi)

In what follows we shall need also the subleading terms in the expansion at

Y(z)=I+\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\
\gamma&-\alpha\end{pmatrix}z^{-1}+O(z^{-2}),

(3.19)

for functions
\alpha=\alpha(L,s)
,
\beta=\beta(L,s)
and
\gamma=\gamma(L,s)
. This matrix is traceless because
\det Y(z)=1
identically in
z
.
Here, as in condition (c) of the discrete RH problem,
|z|\to+\infty
through integer values.

Lemma 3.3
.

Fix
a\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}
.
Let
C_{Y}(a)
and
Y_{1}(a)
be as in (
3.15
), and let
c_{Y}(a):=\det C_{Y}(a)
.
We have

\frac{{\mathbf{g}}^{\top}(a)Y_{1}(a){\mathbf{f}}(a)}{1-M_{s}(a,a)}=\frac{c_{Y}(a)-1}{c_{Y}(a)}

(3.20)

and, for some
d_{Y}(a)\in\mathbb{C}
,

\widetilde{C}_{Y}(a)C_{Y}(a)=c_{Y}(a)I+d_{Y}(a)W_{Y}(a).

(3.21)

Proof.

Since
\mathbf{f}(a),\mathbf{g}(a)
are orthogonal and nonzero, the
2\times 2
matrix

U:=\left(\frac{\mathbf{f}(a)}{|\mathbf{f}(a)|}\ \right|\left.\ \frac{\mathbf{g}(a)}{|\mathbf{g}(a)|}\right)

(3.22)

is an orthogonal matrix,
UU^{\top}=I
.
Here, we denote
|\mathbf{v}|:=\sqrt{\mathbf{v}^{\top}\mathbf{v}}
for a column vector
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
.
Introducing

\kappa:=\frac{|\mathbf{f}(a)|\cdot|\mathbf{g}(a)|}{1-M_{\sigma,s}(a,a)},

(3.23)

we have

W_{Y}(a)=U\begin{pmatrix}0&\kappa\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}U^{\top}.

(3.24)

Using that
\det Y(z)=1
identically in
z
(Remark
3.2
) and (
3.15
),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c_{Y}(a)}=\frac{\det Y(z)}{\det C_{Y}(a)}

\displaystyle=\det\biggl(\frac{1}{z-a}U\begin{pmatrix}0&\kappa\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}U^{\top}+I+Y_{1}(a)(z-a)+O\bigl((z-a)^{2}\bigr)\biggr)

\displaystyle=\det\biggl(\frac{1}{z-a}\begin{pmatrix}0&\kappa\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}+I+U^{\top}Y_{1}(a)U(z-a)+O\bigl((z-a)^{2}\bigr)\biggr)

\displaystyle=1-\kappa\bigl(U^{\top}Y_{1}(a)U\bigr)_{2,1}+O(z-a)=1-\kappa\bigl(U^{\top}Y_{1}(a)U\bigr)_{2,1}.

(3.25)

Finally, using (
3.22
) and (
3.23
) we get

\kappa\bigl(U^{\top}Y_{1}(a)U\bigr)_{2,1}=\frac{\mathbf{g}^{\top}(a)Y_{1}(a)\mathbf{f}(a)}{1-M_{\sigma,s}(a,a)}

(3.26)

and (
3.20
) follows.

By multiplying the Laurent expansion of
Y^{-1}
, given in (
3.18
), on the right by that of
Y
, given in (
3.15
), vanishing of terms of order
(z-a)^{-1}
implies
W_{Y}(a)\widetilde{C}_{Y}(a)C_{Y}(a)=\widetilde{C}_{Y}(a)C_{Y}(a)W_{Y}(a)
.
In turn, this means that
\widetilde{C}_{Y}(a)C_{Y}(a)=e_{Y}(a)I+d_{Y}(a)W_{Y}(a)
for some constants
d_{Y}(a),e_{Y}(a)
.
Next, the fact that the constant term is the identity gives

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{Y}(a)C_{Y}(a)+\widetilde{Y}_{1}(a)\widetilde{C}_{Y}(a)C_{Y}(a)W_{Y}(a)-W_{Y}(a)\widetilde{C}_{Y}(a)C_{Y}(a)Y_{1}=I

\displaystyle\quad\Rightarrow(e_{Y}(a)-1)I+\bigl(d_{Y}(a)I+e_{Y}(a)\widetilde{Y}_{1}(a)\bigr)W_{Y}(a)=e_{Y}(a)W_{Y}(a)Y_{1}(a).

(3.27)

Multiplying the last relation by
\mathbf{f}^{\top}(a)
on the left and by
\mathbf{f}(a)
on the right, and combining with (
3.20
), we obtain
e_{Y}(a)=c_{Y}(a)
, and so also (
3.21
) is proved.
∎

0&1\end{pmatrix}\Psi(z;L,s).

(6.3)

The following proposition is a consequence of (
4.18
)–(
4.19
) whose proof is a simple computation that we omit.

Proposition 6.1
.

We have

\Theta(z;L,s+1)=A(z;L,s)\Theta(z;L,s),\qquad\frac{\partial}{\partial L}\Theta(z;L,s)=B(z;L,s)\Theta(z;L,s)

(6.4)

where

A(z;L,s)=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-\mathfrak{a}^{2}(L,s)&\frac{z+s+1}{L}+\frac{\mathfrak{b}(L,s+1)}{2}\end{pmatrix},\qquad B(z;L,s)=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{z+s+1}{L}+\mathfrak{b}(L,s)&-2\\
2\mathfrak{a}^{2}(L,s)&-\frac{z+s}{L}\end{pmatrix}.

(6.5)

In particular,

\Theta(z;L,s)=\begin{pmatrix}\chi(z;L,s-1)&\widetilde{\chi}(z;L,s-1)\\
\chi(z;L,s)&\widetilde{\chi}(z;L,s)\end{pmatrix}

(6.6)

where
f(s)=\chi(z;L,s)
or
f(s)=\widetilde{\chi}(z;L,s)
are both solutions to

f(s+1)+\mathfrak{a}^{2}(L,s)f(s-1)=\left(\frac{z+s+1}{L}+\frac{\mathfrak{b}(L,s+1)}{2}\right)f(s).

(6.7)

Remark 6.2
.

It is worth noting that the compatibility of (
6.4
) is expressed by the identity

B(z;L,s+1)A(z;L,s)-A(z;L,s)B(z;L,s)=\frac{\partial}{\partial L}A(z;L,s).

(6.8)

Spelling out this equation gives the two relations

\frac{\partial\mathfrak{a}(L,s)}{\partial L}=\frac{\mathfrak{a}(L,s)}{2}\bigl(\mathfrak{b}(L,s+1)-\mathfrak{b}(L,s)\bigr),\qquad\frac{\partial\mathfrak{b}(L,s)}{\partial L}=4\bigl(\mathfrak{a}^{2}(L,s)-\mathfrak{a}^{2}(L,s-1)\bigr)-\frac{\mathfrak{b}(L,s)}{L}.

(6.9)

The first identity is manifest already by comparing (
6.1
) and (
6.2
).
Combining it with the second one immediately implies the 2D Toda equation

\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\theta_{+}\partial\theta_{-}}q_{s}(\theta_{+},\theta_{-})=\mathrm{e}^{q_{s+1}(\theta_{+},\theta_{-})-q_{s}(\theta_{+},\theta_{-})}-\mathrm{e}^{q_{s}(\theta_{+},\theta_{-})-q_{s-1}(\theta_{+},\theta_{-})}

(6.10)

for
q_{s}(\theta_{+},\theta_{-}):=\log\bigl(Q_{\sigma}(\sqrt{\theta_{+}\theta_{-}},s)/Q_{\sigma}(\sqrt{\theta_{+}\theta_{-}},s-1)\bigr)
, which is a consequence of Theorem
I
.

The matrix
W_{\Psi}(a)
is not constant in
a
, and so we shall now obtain a difference equation in
z
which, in general, has a meromorphic non-rational matrix coefficient.
To this end, it is convenient to work with the gauge transformed matrix
\Theta(z;L,s)
introduced in (
6.3
) and with the functions

\varphi(z;L,s):=\sqrt{\frac{1+\beta(L,s+1)}{L}}\,\chi(z;L,s).

(6.11)

In particular, from (
6.7
) we have

\mathfrak{a}(L,s+1)\varphi(z;L,s+1)+\mathfrak{a}(L,s)\varphi(z;L,s-1)=\left(\frac{z+s+1}{L}+\frac{\mathfrak{b}(L,s+1)}{2}\right)\varphi(z;L,s).

(6.12)

Proposition 6.3
.

We have

\Theta(z+1;L,s)=C(z;L,s)\Theta(z;L,s),

(6.13)

where

\displaystyle C(z)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-\mathfrak{a}^{2}(L,s)&\frac{z+s+1}{L}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}}\frac{\Delta\sigma(l)}{z-l}\begin{pmatrix}\mathfrak{a}(L,s)\varphi(l+1;L,s-1)\varphi(l;L,s)&-\varphi(l+1;L,s-1)\varphi(l;L,s-1)\\
\mathfrak{a}^{2}(L,s)\varphi(l+1;L,s)\varphi(l;L,s)&-\mathfrak{a}(L,s)\varphi(l+1;L,s)\varphi(l;L,s-1)\end{pmatrix}

(6.14)

where
\Delta\sigma(l):=\sigma(l+1)-\sigma(l)
,
\mathfrak{a}(L,s)
is defined in (
6.1
), and
\varphi(z;L,s)
is defined in (
6.11
).

Proof.

Define
C(z;L,s):=\Theta(z+1;L,s)\Theta^{-1}(z;L,s)
. Using (
4.5
), (
6.3
), and (
4.3
), we obtain

C(z;L,s)=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{L}{1+\beta(L,s)}&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}Y(z+s+\tfrac{3}{2};L,s)\begin{pmatrix}0&\frac{1}{L}\\
-L&\frac{z+s+1}{L}\end{pmatrix}Y^{-1}(z+s+\tfrac{1}{2};L,s)\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1+\beta(L,s)}{L}&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

(6.15)

By using this relation, it is straightforward to see that

C_{-}(z;L,s):=C(z;L,s)-\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-\mathfrak{a}^{2}(L,s)&\frac{z+s+1}{L}\end{pmatrix}=\mathrm{O}(1/z)

(6.16)

as
z\to\infty
, in the sense of Lemma
A.1
, and applying this lemma we get

C_{-}(z;L,s)=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{res}}\limits_{w=l}C_{-}(w)\mathrm{d}w}{z-l}=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{res}}\limits_{w=l}C(w)\mathrm{d}w}{z-l}.

(6.17)

We are left with the task of computing these residues.
For all
l\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}
, using (
6.3
) we have

\displaystyle C(z)

\displaystyle=\Theta(z+1)\Theta^{-1}(z)

\displaystyle=\Theta^{\mathsf{reg}}_{l+1}(z+1)\biggl(I+\frac{W_{\Psi}(l+1)}{z-l}\biggr)\biggl(I-\frac{W_{\Psi}(l)}{z-l}\biggr)(\Theta^{\mathsf{reg}}_{l})^{-1}(z)

\displaystyle=\Theta^{\mathsf{reg}}_{l+1}(z+1)(\Theta^{\mathsf{reg}}_{l})^{-1}(z)+\frac{1}{z-l}\Theta^{\mathsf{reg}}_{l+1}(z+1)\bigl(W_{\Psi}(l+1)-W_{\Psi}(l)\bigr)(\Theta^{\mathsf{reg}}_{l})^{-1}(z)

(6.18)

where

\Theta^{\mathsf{reg}}_{l}(z):=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{L}{1+\beta(L,s)}&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\Psi^{\mathsf{reg}}_{l}(z)=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{L}{1+\beta(L,s)}&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\Psi(z)\biggl(I-\frac{W_{\Psi}(l)}{z-l}\biggr),

(6.19)

which is regular at
z=l
.
Hence, using (
4.7
), (
6.6
), and the fact that
\det\Theta(z;L,s)=\tfrac{L}{1+\beta(L,s)}
, we compute
\mathop{\mathrm{res}}\limits_{w=l}C(w)\mathrm{d}w
as

\displaystyle\Theta^{\mathsf{reg}}_{l+1}(l+1)\begin{pmatrix}0&\sigma(l)-\sigma(l+1)\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\bigl(\Theta^{\mathsf{reg}}_{l}\bigr)^{-1}(l)

\displaystyle=\frac{1+\beta(L,s)}{L}\bigl(\sigma(l)-\sigma(l+1)\bigr)\begin{pmatrix}-\chi(l+1;L,s-1)\chi(l;L,s)&\chi(l+1;L,s-1)\chi(l;L,s-1)\\
-\chi(l+1;L,s)\chi(l;L,s)&\chi(l+1;L,s)\chi(l;L,s-1)\end{pmatrix}

(6.20)

and the proof is complete by using the definition (
6.11
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
III
.

We first prove (
1.33
) and (
1.34
).
To this end, let
C_{-1}
be the coefficient of
z^{-1}
in the asymptotic series for
C(z;L,s)
at
z=\infty
.
On the one hand, using (
6.14
), we have

(C_{-1})_{1,1}=\mathfrak{a}(L,s)\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}}\Delta\sigma(l)\varphi(l+1;L,s-1)\varphi(l;L,s).

(6.21)

On the other hand, using (
6.15
) and (
3.19
) instead,

(C_{-1})_{1,1}=L\left(1-\frac{1+\beta(L,s)}{1+\beta(L,s+1)}\right)=L\bigl(1-\mathfrak{a}^{2}(L,s)\bigr),

(6.22)

where we use (
6.1
) in the last equality.
Hence, (
6.21
) and (
6.22
) are equal and we get (
1.33
).
Similarly, using (
6.14
), we have

\mathrm{tr}\,C_{-1}=\mathfrak{a}(L,s)\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}}\Delta\sigma(l)\bigl(\varphi(l+1;L,s-1)\varphi(l;L,s)-\varphi(l+1;L,s)\varphi(l;L,s-1)\bigr),

(6.23)

whilst using (
6.15
) and (
3.19
) we have
\mathrm{tr}\,C_{-1}=\alpha(L,s)/L
, and so

\alpha(L,s)=L\mathfrak{a}(L,s)\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}}\Delta\sigma(l)\bigl(\varphi(l+1;L,s-1)\varphi(l;L,s)-\varphi(l+1;L,s)\varphi(l;L,s-1)\bigr).

(6.24)

Using this expression and (
6.12
) we finally simplify

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha(L,s+1)-\alpha(L,s)}{L}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}}\Delta\sigma(l)\biggl\{\mathfrak{a}(L,s+1)\bigl[\varphi(l+1;L,s)\varphi(l;L,s+1)-\varphi(l+1;L,s+1)\varphi(l;L,s)\bigr]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad-\mathfrak{a}(L,s)\bigl[\varphi(l+1;L,s-1)\varphi(l;L,s)-\varphi(l+1;L,s)\varphi(l;L,s-1)\bigr]\biggr\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}}\Delta\sigma(l)\biggl\{\varphi(l+1;L,s)\bigl[\mathfrak{a}(L,s+1)\varphi(l;L,s+1)+\mathfrak{a}(L,s)\varphi(l;L,s-1)\bigr]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad-\varphi(l;L,s)\bigl[\mathfrak{a}(L,s+1)\varphi(l+1;L,s+1)+\mathfrak{a}(L,s)\varphi(l+1;L,s-1)\bigr]\biggr\}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{L}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}}\Delta\sigma(l)\varphi(l;L,s)\varphi(l+1;L,s).

(6.25)

Next, (
1.35
) is exactly (
6.12
), and it remains only to show the asymptotic relation (
1.36
).
To this end, we first observe that as
s\to+\infty
we have
Q_{\sigma}(L,s)\to 1
and so, by (
3.30
),

\beta(s)=\frac{Q_{\sigma}(L,s-1)}{Q_{\sigma}(L,s)}-1\to 0,\quad\mbox{as }s\to+\infty,

(6.26)

implying, by (
6.11
), (
6.3
), and (
6.6
), that

\varphi(z;L,s)\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}\chi(z;L,s),\qquad s\to+\infty.

(6.27)

It is therefore enough to show that for all
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}
we have

\chi(z;L,s)\sim L\mathrm{J}_{z+s+1}(2L),\qquad s\to+\infty.

(6.28)

To this end we first write, using (
4.5
), (
6.3
), (
6.6
), and (
5.14
),

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\chi(z;L,s)}{L\mathrm{J}_{z+s+1}(2L)}-1\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{1}{L\mathrm{J}_{z+s+1}(2L)}\biggl((Y(z+s+\tfrac{1}{2})-I)\Phi(z+s+\tfrac{1}{2})\biggr)_{2,1}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{b\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}}\left|(0,1)\bigl((1-\mathsf{D})^{-1}\widehat{\mathbf{f}}\bigr)(b)\frac{\widehat{\mathbf{g}}^{\top}(b)\Phi(z+s+\tfrac{1}{2})}{(z+s+\tfrac{1}{2}-b)L\mathrm{J}_{z+s+1}(2L)}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix}\right|

(6.29)

where we recall that the operator
\mathsf{D}
is defined in (
5.6
).
We need to show that (
6.29
) vanishes as
s\to+\infty
.
To this end, we first estimate it as follows

\left|\frac{\chi(z;L,s)}{L\mathrm{J}_{z+s+1}(2L)}-1\right|\leq c\mathrm{e}^{L}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}}\left|(0,1)\bigl((1-\mathsf{D})^{-1}\widehat{\mathbf{f}}\bigr)(b)\right|

(6.30)

because we claim that there exists
c>0
such that for
s
sufficiently large (depending on
L,z
only, not on
b
) we have, for all
b\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}
,

\left|\frac{\widehat{\mathbf{g}}^{\top}(b)\Phi(z+s+\tfrac{1}{2})}{(z+s+\tfrac{1}{2}-b)L\mathrm{J}_{z+s+1}(2L)}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix}\right|\leq c\mathrm{e}^{L}.

(6.31)

To prove this last assertion, we rewrite

\frac{\widehat{\mathbf{g}}^{\top}(b)\Phi(z+s+\tfrac{1}{2})}{(z+s+\tfrac{1}{2}-b)L\mathrm{J}_{z+s+1}(2L)}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix}=\frac{K^{\mathsf{Be}}(z+s+\tfrac{1}{2},b)}{\bigl(1-M_{s}(b,b)\bigr)L\mathrm{J}_{z+s+1}(2L)}=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}_{+}}\frac{\mathrm{J}_{z+s+\frac{1}{2}+l}(2L)}{\mathrm{J}_{z+s+1}(2L)}\frac{\mathrm{J}_{b+l}(2L)}{1-M_{s}(b,b)}.

(6.32)

Observe that

M_{s}(b,b)=\sigma(b-s-\tfrac{1}{2})K^{\mathsf{Be}}(b,b)\leq\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sup_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime},\ l<-s/2}\sigma(l),&\mbox{if }b<s/2,\\[20.0pt]
K^{\mathsf{Be}}(\lfloor\frac{s+1}{2}\rfloor+\frac{1}{2},\lfloor\frac{s+1}{2}\rfloor+\frac{1}{2}),&\mbox{if }b>s/2,\end{cases}

(6.33)

which implies

\frac{1}{1-M_{s}(b,b)}=\mathrm{O}(1),\quad\mbox{as $s\to+\infty$, uniformly in $b\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}$.}

(6.34)

Next, for
k
real and sufficiently large,
\mathrm{J}_{k}(2L)
is positive and monotonically decreasing in
k
, as it follows, for instance, by (
2.3
).
Therefore, we can bound (
6.32
), provided
s
is sufficiently large,

\left|\frac{\widehat{\mathbf{g}}^{\top}(b)\Phi(z+s+\tfrac{1}{2})}{(z+s+\tfrac{1}{2}-b)L\mathrm{J}_{z+s+1}(2L)}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix}\right|\leq c\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{\prime}}|\mathrm{J}_{b+l}(2L)|\leq c\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|\mathrm{J}_{k}(2L)|\leq 2c\mathrm{e}^{L},

(6.35)

for some
c>0
, where in the last step we use again the inequality
\mathrm{J}_{\pm k}(2L)\leq L^{k}/k!
for all integers
k\geq 0
. (In (
6.30
) we rename
c\mapsto c/2
.)
Next, we claim that

\left\|\mathsf{D}\mathbf{r}\right\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{\prime})}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\mathbf{r}\right\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{\prime})}

(6.36)

provided
s
is sufficiently large. Postponing for a while the proof of this claim, let us show how to complete the estimate of (
6.30
): note that
\mathsf{D}
commutes with multiplying on the left by the vector
(0,1)
, and therefore so does
(1-\mathsf{D})^{-1}
, to write, using (
6.36
),

\displaystyle\sum_{b\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}}\left|(0,1)\bigl((1-\mathsf{D})^{-1}\widehat{\mathbf{f}}\bigr)(b)\right|

\displaystyle=\left\|(0,1)(1-\mathsf{D})^{-1}\widehat{\mathbf{f}}\right\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\|(0,1)\widehat{\mathbf{f}}\right\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{\prime})}.

\displaystyle=2L\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}}\sigma(a-s-\tfrac{1}{2})|\mathrm{J}_{a+\frac{1}{2}}(2L)|

\displaystyle=2L\sum_{\begin{smallmatrix}a\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime},\\
a<s/2\end{smallmatrix}}\sigma(a-s-\tfrac{1}{2})|\mathrm{J}_{a+\frac{1}{2}}(2L)|+2L\sum_{\begin{smallmatrix}a\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime},\\
a>s/2\end{smallmatrix}}\sigma(a-s-\tfrac{1}{2})|\mathrm{J}_{a+\frac{1}{2}}(2L)|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sup_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime},\ l<-\frac{s}{2}}\sigma(l)\right)\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|\mathrm{J}_{k}(2L)|+\sum_{\begin{smallmatrix}a\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime},\\
a>s/2\end{smallmatrix}}\frac{L^{a+\frac{1}{2}}}{(a+\tfrac{1}{2})!}=o(1),

(6.37)

as
s\to+\infty
.
Finally, it remains to prove the claim (
6.36
).
To this end, we have

\sum_{a,b\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime},\ a\not=b}\left|\frac{\mathbf{r}(b)\widehat{\mathbf{g}}^{\top}(b)\widehat{\mathbf{f}}(a)}{a-b}\right|\leq\sum_{b\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}}|\mathbf{r}(b)|\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}}|\widehat{\mathbf{g}}^{\top}(b)\widehat{\mathbf{f}}(a)|.

(6.38)

We can bound this quantity, using
|\mathrm{J}_{b\pm\frac{1}{2}}(2L)|\leq\mathrm{e}^{L}
and
(1-M_{s}(b,b))^{-1}\leq c
for
s
sufficiently large and for all
b\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}
, as we proved in (
6.34
), as

\mbox{\eqref{wecanbound}}\leq cL\mathrm{e}^{L}\|\mathbf{r}(b)\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{\prime})}\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}}\sigma(a-s-\tfrac{1}{2})\left(|\mathrm{J}_{a+\frac{1}{2}}(2L)|+|\mathrm{J}_{a-\frac{1}{2}}(2L)|\right)

(6.39)

and we can bound the last sum over
a
exactly as in (

\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}}\sigma(a-s-\tfrac{1}{2})\left(|\mathrm{J}_{a+\frac{1}{2}}(2L)|+|\mathrm{J}_{a-\frac{1}{2}}(2L)|\right)=o(1),\qquad s\to+\infty,

(6.40)

6.1
Connection with the discrete Painlevé II equation

Let us now consider, more specifically, the case
\sigma=\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}_{+}}
, studied in depth by Borodin
[
9
]
.
In this case, (
6.14
) reduces to

C(z)=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-\mathfrak{a}^{2}(L,s)&\frac{z+s+1}{L}\end{pmatrix}+\frac{1}{z+\frac{1}{2}}\begin{pmatrix}\mathfrak{a}(L,s)\varphi_{+}(L,s-1)\varphi_{-}(L,s)&-\varphi_{+}(L,s-1)\varphi_{-}(L,s-1)\\
\mathfrak{a}^{2}(L,s)\varphi_{+}(L,s)\varphi_{-}(L,s)&-\mathfrak{a}(L,s)\varphi_{+}(L,s)\varphi_{-}(L,s-1)\end{pmatrix}

(6.41)

where we denoted, for sake of brevity,
\varphi_{\pm}(L,s)=\varphi(\pm 1/2;L,s)
.
In this case, the compatibility conditions between the Lax equations (
6.4
) and (
6.13
) greatly simplify, and we recover the well known relations between the discrete Bessel kernel, the discrete Painlevé II and the modified Volterra equation (see
[
9
]
and also
[
1
,
22
]
).

Let us start by noting that the identities (
1.33
) and (
1.34
) reduce to

\mathfrak{b}(L,s+1)=\frac{2}{L}\varphi_{+}(L,s)\varphi_{-}(L,s),\quad L\bigl(\mathfrak{a}^{-1}(L,s)-\mathfrak{a}(L,s)\bigr)=\varphi_{+}(L,s-1)\varphi_{-}(L,s).

(6.42)

Taking the ratio of these gives

\varphi_{+}(L,s)=\frac{\mathfrak{a}(L,s)\mathfrak{b}(L,s+1)}{2(1-\mathfrak{a}^{2}(s))}\varphi_{+}(L,s-1).

(6.43)

We then expand the determinant of
C(z;L,s)
, which we know to be equal to
1
, around
z=\infty
.
Using (
6.43
), the term of order
z^{-1}
yields

L\mathfrak{a}^{2}(L,s)(\mathfrak{b}(L,s+1)+\mathfrak{b}(L,s))=(2s+1)(1-\mathfrak{a}^{2}(L,s)).

(6.44)

Next, let us consider the compatibility condition between the first equation in (
6.4
) and (
6.13
)

A(z+1;L,s)C(z;L,s)-C(z;L,s+1)A(z;L,z)=0.

(6.45)

Inspecting the entry
(2,2)
of this condition, once written in terms of
\mathfrak{b}^{2}(L,s+1),\mathfrak{a}(L,s+1)
and
\mathfrak{a}(L,s)
(using the equations obtained before), yields

\mathfrak{b}^{2}(L,s+1)=4(1-\mathfrak{a}^{2}(L,s))(1-\mathfrak{a}^{2}(L,s+1)).

(6.46)

Equations (
6.44
) and (
6.46
) are the same (up to a change of variable) as equations (3.9), (3.10) in
[
9
]
and lead to an expression of the Fredholm determinants
Q_{\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}_{+}}}(L,s)
in terms of a discrete recursion known as the
discrete Painlevé II equation
, eq. (
6.47
) below.
For the reader’s convenience, we explain here how to derive it, closely following
[
9
]
.
Note, however, that the Lax pair used to obtain (
6.44
) and (
6.46
) is not the same as the one in op. cit.

Proposition 6.4

(cf. Borodin,
[
9
]
)
.

Let
v(L,s)
, for
s\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}
with
s\geq-\tfrac{1}{2}
, be the sequence of functions defined by the second order recursion

v(L,s+1)+v(L,s-1)=\frac{(s+\frac{1}{2})v(L,s)}{L(v^{2}(L,s)-1)}

(6.47)

with initial conditions
v(L,-\tfrac{1}{2})=1
,
v(L,\tfrac{1}{2})=-\mathrm{I}_{1}(2L)/{\mathrm{I}_{0}(2L)}
, where
\mathrm{I}_{k}(2L)
is defined in (
1.14
).
Then, for all
s\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}
satisfying
s\geq-\tfrac{1}{2}
,

\frac{Q_{\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}_{+}}}(L,s+1)Q_{\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}_{+}}}(L,s-1)}{Q^{2}_{\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}_{+}}}(L,s)}=1-v^{2}(L,s).

(6.48)

Moreover, the functions
v(L,s)
satisfy the modified Volterra equation

\frac{\partial}{\partial L}v(L,s)=\left(1-v^{2}(L,s)\right)\left(v(L,s+1)-v(L,s-1)\right).

(6.49)

Proof.

We start by defining
v^{2}(L,-\tfrac{1}{2})=1
(which satisfies (
6.48
)) and then recursively

v(L,s+1):=-\mathfrak{b}(L,s+1)v^{-1}(L,s).

(6.50)

Using (
6.46
), we have
v^{2}(L,s)=1-\mathfrak{a}^{2}(L,s)
for all
s\geq-\tfrac{1}{2}
.
We can now write (
6.44
) just in terms of the functions
v(L,s)
, and in this way we obtain (
6.47
). As for (
6.48
), it comes from the equality
v^{2}(L,s)=1-\mathfrak{a}^{2}(L,s)
combined with (
6.1
).
Finally, the initial condition for
v(L,1/2)
can be deduced from the recursive definition
v(L,s+1)=-\mathfrak{b}(L,s)v^{-1}(L,s)
combined with (
6.2
) and the fact that
Q_{\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{\prime}}}(-1/2)={\rm e}^{-L^{2}},\quad Q_{\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{\prime}}}(1/2)={\rm e}^{-L^{2}}{\mathrm{I}}_{0}(2L)
, see (
1.14
).
Finally, the modified Volterra equation (
6.49
) is merely a rewriting of (
6.9
) in terms of the functions
v(s,L)
.
∎

Appendix A
Partial fraction expansion

Lemma A.1
.

Let
f(\cdot)
be a meromorphic function with simple poles at
\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}:=\mathbb{Z}+\tfrac{1}{2}
such that

\max_{|z|=n}|f(z)|\to 0,\qquad\mbox{as $n\to+\infty$ through integer values.}

(A.1)

Then, for all
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}
we have

f(z)=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{res}}\limits_{w=a}f(w)\mathrm{d}w}{z-a}.

(A.2)

Proof.

Fix
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}
: for all integers
n>|z|
, Cauchy theorem implies that

\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\oint_{|w|=n}\frac{f(w)}{z-w}\mathrm{d}w=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime},|a|<n}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{res}}\limits_{w=a}f(w)\mathrm{d}w}{z-a}-f(z).

(A.3)

As
n\to+\infty
, the left-hand side tends to zero because

\left|\oint_{|w|=n}\frac{f(w)}{z-w}\mathrm{d}w\right|\leq\max_{|w|=n}|f(w)|\oint_{|w|=1}\frac{|\mathrm{d}w|}{|w-z/n|}\to 0,

(A.4)

as
n\to+\infty
by assumption, and the proof is complete.
∎

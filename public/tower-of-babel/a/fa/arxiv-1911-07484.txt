Relative persistent homology
Nello Blaser, Morten Brun

Abstract
The alpha complex efficiently computes persistent homology of a point cloud X X in Euclidean space when the dimension d d is low. Given a subset A A of X X , relative persistent homology can be computed as the persistent homology of the relative Čech complex Č ⁡ ( X , A ) \operatorname{\textnormal{\v{C}}}(X,A) . But this is not computationally feasible for larger point clouds X X . The aim of this note is to present a method for efficient computation of relative persistent homology in low dimensional Euclidean space. We introduce the relative Delaunay Čech complex DelČ ⁡ ( X , A ) \operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}(X,A) whose homology is the relative persistent homology. It can be constructed from the Delaunay complex of an embedding of the point clouds in ( d + 1 ) (d+1) -dimensional Euclidean space.



Keywords—
topological data analysis, relative homology,
Delaunay-Čech complex, alpha complex

Introduction

Persistent homology is receiving growing attention in the machine
learning community. In that light, the scalability of persistent
homology computations is of increasing importance. To date, the alpha
complex is the most widely used method to compute persistent homology
for large low-dimensional data sets.

Relative persistent homology has been considered several times in
recent years. For example Edelsbrunner and Harrer

[
1
]
have presented an application of relative
persistent homology to estimate the dimension of an embedded
manifold. Relative persistent homology is also a way to introduce the
concept of extended persistence
[
2
]
. De Silva and
others have shown that the relative persistent homology

H_{\ast}(X,A_{t})
with an increasing family of sets
A_{t}
and a
constant
X=\cup_{t}A_{t}
, and the corresponding relative persistent
cohomology have the same barcode
[
3
]
. They also show
that absolute persistent homology of
A_{t}
can be computed from this
particular type of relative persistent homology. More recently,
Pokorny and others
[
4
]
have used relative persistent
homology to cluster two-dimensional trajectories. Some software, such
as PHAT
[
5
]
, even allows for the direct computation of
relative persistent homology. For an example see the

PHAT
github repository
.

Despite the fact that relative persistent homology has been considered
in many different situations, we are not aware of a relative version
of the alpha- or Delaunay Čech complexes being used.

Our
contributions are as follows.

1.

We give a new elementary proof that the Delaunay Čech complex
is homotopy equivalent to the Čech complex. This has
previously been shown using discrete Morse theory
[
6
]
.

2.

We extend this proof to the relative versions of the Delaunay Čech complex and the Čech complex.

3.

We explain how the relative Delaunay Čech complex can be
computed through embedding in a higher dimension.

Together, these contributions result in
theorem

1.1
, which shows
how the relative persistent homology of Čech persistence modules

\operatorname{\textnormal{\v{C}}}_{*}(X;k)/\operatorname{\textnormal{\v{C}}}_{*}(A;k)
of low-dimensional spaces can be
efficiently computed using a relative Delaunay-Čech complex.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

A\subseteq X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be finite. The relative
Delaunay-Čech complex
\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}(X,A)
defined in

section

6
is homotopy equivalent to the relative
Čech complex
\operatorname{\textnormal{\v{C}}}(X,A)
.

Moreover, given the cardinalities
n_{X}
of
X
and
n_{A}
of

A
, the relative
Delaunay-Čech complex contains at most

O\left((n_{X}+n_{A})\lceil(d+1)/2\rceil\right)
simplices.

This manuscript is structured as follows. In

section

2
, we introduce relative persistent
homology.
section

3
introduces Dowker Nerves, the
theoretical foundation we use to prove that the relative Delaunay
Čech complex is homotopy equivalent to the relative Čech
complex. In
section

4
, we introduce the alpha- and
Delaunay-Čech complexes using the Dowker Nerve notation and show
that they are homotopy equivalent to the Čech
complex.
section

5
introduces
the relative alpha- and Delaunay-Čech complexes, and proves that
they are homotopy equivalent to the relative Čech complex.
Finally, in
section

6
we show how the relative
Delaunay-Čech complex can actually be computed.

Relative Persistent Homology

Let
X
be a finite subset of Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Given

t>0
, the Čech complex
\operatorname{\textnormal{\v{C}}}_{t}(X)
of
X
is the abstract
simplicial complex with vertex set
X
and with

\sigma\subseteq X
a simplex of
\operatorname{\textnormal{\v{C}}}_{t}(X)
if and only if
there exists a point
p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with distance less than
t
to
every point in
\sigma
. Varying
t
we obtain the filtered Čech complex
\operatorname{\textnormal{\v{C}}}(X)
.

Given a subset
A
of
X
we obtain an inclusion

\operatorname{\textnormal{\v{C}}}(A)\subseteq\operatorname{\textnormal{\v{C}}}(X)
of filtered simplicial complexes and
an induced inclusion
\operatorname{\textnormal{\v{C}}}_{*}(A;k)\subseteq\operatorname{\textnormal{\v{C}}}_{*}(X;k)
of
associated chain complexes of persistence modules over the field

k
. The
relative persistent homology of the pair
(X,A)
is
defined as the homology of the factor chain complex of persistence
modules
\operatorname{\textnormal{\v{C}}}_{*}(X;k)/\operatorname{\textnormal{\v{C}}}_{*}(A;k)
.

For
X
of small cardinality, the relative persistent homology can
be calculated as
the reduced persistent homology of the relative Čech complex

\operatorname{\textnormal{\v{C}}}(X,A)
, where
\sigma\subseteq X
is a simplex of

\operatorname{\textnormal{\v{C}}}(X,A)_{t}
if either
\sigma\subseteq A
or

\sigma\in\operatorname{\textnormal{\v{C}}}_{t}(X)
. However, as the cardinality of
X
grows, this
quickly becomes computationally infeasible.

Dowker Nerves

A
dissimilarity
is a continuous function of the form

\Lambda\colon X\times Y\to[0,\infty]
, for topological spaces

X
and
Y
, where
[0,\infty]
is given the order topology. A

morphism

f\colon\Lambda\to\Lambda^{\prime}
of dissimilarities

\Lambda\colon X\times Y\to[0,\infty]
and

\Lambda^{\prime}\colon X^{\prime}\times Y^{\prime}\to[0,\infty]
consists of a pair

(f_{1},f_{2})
of continuous functions
f_{1}\colon X\to X^{\prime}
and

f_{2}\colon Y\to Y^{\prime}
so that for all
(x,y)\in X\times Y
the
following inequality holds:

\Lambda^{\prime}(f_{1}(x),f_{2}(y))\leq\Lambda(x,y).

This notion of morphism is less general than the definition in for
example
[
7
, Definition 2.10]
, but it is simpler and suffices
for our purposes.

The
Dowker Nerve

N\Lambda
of
\Lambda
is the filtered
simplicial complex described as follows: For
t>0
, the simplicial
complex
N\Lambda_{t}
consists of the finite subsets
\sigma
of
X
for
which there exists
y\in Y
so that
\Lambda(x,y)<t
for every

x\in\sigma
.

Let
f\colon\Lambda\to\Lambda^{\prime}
be a morphism of
dissimilarities as above and let
\sigma\in N\Lambda_{t}
. Given
y\in Y
with
\Lambda(x,y)<t
for every
x\in\sigma
we see
that

\Lambda^{\prime}(f_{1}(x),f_{2}(y))\leq\Lambda(x,y)<t.

for every
x\in\sigma
, so
f_{1}(\sigma)\in N\Lambda^{\prime}_{t}
. Thus
we have a simplicial map
f\colon N\Lambda\to N\Lambda^{\prime}
.

Given
x\in X
and
t>0
, the
\Lambda
-ball of radius
t

centered at
x
is the subset of
Y
defined as

B_{\Lambda}(x,t)=\{y\in Y,\ \mid\,\Lambda(x,y)<t\}.

The
t
-thickening of
\Lambda
is the subset of
Y
defined as

\Lambda^{t}=\bigcup_{x\in X}B_{\Lambda}(x,t).

Note that by construction the set of
\Lambda
-balls of radius
t

is an open cover of the
t
-thickening of
\Lambda
.

The
geometric realization

|K|
of a simplicial complex

K
on the vertex set
V
is the subspace of the space
[0,1]^{V}

of functions
\alpha\colon V\to[0,1]
described as follows:

1.

The subset
\alpha^{-1}((0,1])
of
V

consisting of elements where
\alpha
is
strictly positive is a simplex in
K
. In particular it is finite.

2.

The sum of the values of
\alpha

is one, that is
\sum_{v\in V}\alpha(v)=1
.

The subspace topology on
|K|
is called the
strong topology
on the
geometric realization. It is
convenient for construction of functions into
|K|
. The
weak
tooplogy
on
|K|
, which we are not going to use here, is
convenient for construction of functions out of
|K|
. The homotopy
type of
|K|
is the same for these two topologies

[
8
, p. 355, Coorllary A.2.9]
.
Given a simplex
\sigma\in K
, the simplex
|\sigma|
of
|K|

is the closure of

\{\alpha\colon V\to[0,1]\,|\,\alpha(v)>0\text{ for all $v\in\sigma$}\}.

The simplices of
|K|
are the sets of this form.

A
partition of unity subordinate to the dissimilarity

\Lambda\colon X\times Y\to[0,\infty]
consists of continuous
maps

\varphi^{t}\colon\Lambda^{t}\to|N\Lambda_{t}|
such that given

x\in X
, the closure of the set

\{y\in Y\,\mid\,\varphi^{t}(y)(x)>0\}

is contained in
B_{\Lambda}(x,t)
. We say that
\Lambda
is
numerable
if a partition of unity subordinate to
\Lambda

exists. If
Y
is paracompact, then every dissimilarity of the form

\Lambda\colon X\times Y\to[0,\infty]
is numerable

[
8
, p. 355, paragraph after Definition A.2.10]
.

Let
y\in\Lambda^{t}
and let
\{\varphi^{t}\colon\Lambda^{t}\to|N\Lambda_{t}|\}
be a partition
of unity subordinate to
\Lambda
. If
x\in X
with

\varphi^{t}(y)(x)>0
, then
\Lambda(x,y)<t
. Therefore

\varphi^{t}(y)
is
contained in a simplex
|\sigma|
in
|N\Lambda_{t}|
with

\sigma
contained
in
\{x\in X\,\mid\,\Lambda(x,y)<t\}
. Every
finite subset of this set is
an element of
N\Lambda_{t}
.
This implies that for
s\leq t
there is a
simplex of
|N\Lambda_{t}|
containing both
\varphi^{s}(y)
and

\varphi^{t}(y)
. It also implies that given another partition of
unity
\{\psi^{t}\colon\Lambda^{t}\to|N\Lambda_{t}|\}
subordinate to

\Lambda
there is a simplex of
|N\Lambda_{t}|
containing both

\varphi^{t}(y)
and
\psi^{t}(y)
. This is exactly the definition of
contiguous maps, so
\varphi^{t}
and
\psi^{t}
are contiguous, and
thus homotopic maps
[
8
, Remark 2.22, p. 350]
. Similarly, the
diagram

\begin{CD}\Lambda^{s}@>{\varphi^{s}}>{}>|N\Lambda_{s}|\\
@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
\Lambda^{t}@>{\varphi^{t}}>{}>|N\Lambda_{t}|\end{CD}

commutes up to homotopy
[
8
, paragraph on the nerve starting on page
355 and ending on page 356]
.

Recall that a cover
\mathcal{U}
of
Y
is
good
if all
non-empty finite intersections of members of
\mathcal{U}
are
contractible. We now state the Nerve Lemma in the context of dissimilarities.

Theorem 3.1
.

If
Y
is paracompact, then there exists a partition of unity

\{\varphi^{t}\colon\Lambda^{t}\to|N\Lambda_{t}|\}
subordinate to every dissimilarity

\Lambda\colon X\times Y\to[0,\infty]
. Moreover, if the
cover of
\Lambda^{t}
by
\Lambda
-balls of radius
t
is a
good cover, then
\varphi^{t}
is a homotopy equivalence.

Proof.

By the above discussion, we only need to note that the last statement
about good covers is
[
9
, Theorem 4.3]
.
∎

A functorial version of the Nerve Lemma can be stated as follows:

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\Lambda\colon X\times Y\to[0,\infty]
and

\Lambda^{\prime}\colon X^{\prime}\times Y^{\prime}\to[0,\infty]
be dissimilarities
and let
f=f_{1}\times f_{2}\colon X\times Y\to X^{\prime}\times Y^{\prime}
be
a morphism
f\colon\Lambda\to\Lambda^{\prime}
of dissimilarities. If

\{\varphi^{t}\colon\Lambda^{t}\to|N\Lambda_{t}|\}

is a partition of
unity subordinate to
\Lambda
and

\{\psi^{t}\colon(\Lambda^{\prime})^{t}\to|N\Lambda^{\prime}_{t}|\}

is a partition of
unity subordinate to
\Lambda^{\prime}
, then for every
t\geq 0
the diagram

\begin{CD}\Lambda^{t}@>{\varphi^{t}}>{}>|N\Lambda_{t}|\\
@V{f_{2}}V{}V@V{}V{|f_{1}|}V\\
(\Lambda^{\prime})^{t}@>{\psi^{t}}>{}>|N\Lambda^{\prime}_{t}|,\end{CD}

commutes up to homotopy.

Proof.

We show that the two compositions are contiguous. Recall that

|f_{1}|
takes a point
\alpha\colon X\to[0,1]
of

|N\Lambda_{t}|
to the point
|f_{1}|(\alpha)
of
|N\Lambda^{\prime}_{t}|

with
|f_{1}|(\alpha)(x^{\prime})=\sum_{f_{1}(x)=x^{\prime}}\alpha(x)
. Recall
further that
\varphi^{t}(y)

is contained in a simplex
|\sigma|
in
|N\Lambda_{t}|
, where

\sigma
is contained
in
\{x\in X\,\mid\,\Lambda(x,y)<t\}
.
Then we have that for

y\in\Lambda^{t}
, the elements
|f_{1}|(\varphi^{t}(y))
and

\psi^{t}(f_{2}(y))
of
|N\Lambda^{\prime}_{t}|
are contained in simplices

|\sigma|
and
|\tau|
respectively. Both
\sigma
and

\tau
are subsets of the set

\{x^{\prime}\in X^{\prime}\,\mid\,\Lambda^{\prime}(x^{\prime},f_{2}(y))<t\}
. However
every finite subset of this set is a simplex in
N\Lambda^{\prime}_{t}
. In
particular, so is the union
\sigma\cup\tau
.
∎

4
The Alpha- and Delaunay Čech Complexes

Given a finite subset
X
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
we define the
Voronoi cell of
x\in X
as

\operatorname{Vor}(X,x)=\{p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,\mid\,d(x,p)\leq d(y,p)\text{ for all
$y\in X$}\}.

Let
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{d}
be Euclidean space with the discrete
topology.
The
discrete Delaunay dissimilarity
of
X
is defined as

\operatorname{del}^{X}\colon X\times\mathbb{R}^{d}_{d}\to[0,\infty],\quad\operatorname{del}^{X}(x,p)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $p\in V(X,x)$}\\
\infty&\text{if $p\notin V(X,x)$}.\end{cases}

The
Delaunay complex
\operatorname{Del}(X)
is the simplicial
complex with vertex set
X
and with
\sigma\subseteq X
a
simplex of
\operatorname{Del}(X)
if and only if there exists a point in

\mathbb{R}^{d}
belonging to
\operatorname{Vor}(X,x)
for every

x\in\sigma
. That is,
\operatorname{Del}(X)=N\operatorname{del}^{X}_{t}
for
t>0
.

Note that with respect to Euclidean topology, the discrete
Delaunay dissimilarity is not continuous, and hence
\operatorname{del}^{X}\colon X\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,\infty]
is not a
dissimilarity. One way to deal with this is to use the Nerve Lemma
for absolute neighbourhood retracts
[
10
, Theorem 8.2.1]
. In
order to use
theorem

3.1
and

proposition

3.2
from above, instead we
construct a continuous version of the Delaunay dissimilarity.

Given a subset
\sigma
of
X
and
p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let

d_{\operatorname{Vor}}(p,\sigma)=\max\{d(p,\operatorname{Vor}(X,x))\,\mid\,x\in\sigma\},

where for any
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we define

d(p,A)=\inf_{a\in A}\{d(p,a)\}
.

Note that if
\sigma\notin\operatorname{Del}(X)
, then the infimum

\varepsilon_{\sigma}
of the continuous function

d_{\operatorname{Vor}}(-,\sigma)\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is strictly positive.
Choose
\varepsilon>0
so that

2\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{\sigma}
for every subset
\sigma
of

X
that is not in
\operatorname{Del}(X)
. Given
x\in X
we define the

\varepsilon
-thickened Voronoi cell
\operatorname{Vor}(X,x)^{\varepsilon}
by

\operatorname{Vor}(X,x)^{\varepsilon}=\{p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,\mid\,d(p,\operatorname{Vor}(X,x))<\varepsilon\}.

By construction the nerve of the open cover

(\operatorname{Vor}(X,x)^{\varepsilon})_{x\in X}
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is equal to

\operatorname{Del}(X)
.

Let
h\colon[0,\infty]\to[0,\infty]
be the order preserving map

h(t)=\begin{cases}-\ln(1-t/\varepsilon)&\text{if $t<\varepsilon$}\\
\infty&\text{if $t\geq\varepsilon$}.\end{cases}

(1)

For each
x\in X
we let
\operatorname{Del}_{x}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,\infty]
be
the function defined by
\operatorname{Del}_{x}(p)=h(d(p,\operatorname{Vor}(X,x)))
so that

\operatorname{Del}_{x}(\operatorname{Vor}(X,x))=0
and

\operatorname{Del}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\operatorname{Vor}(X,x)^{\varepsilon})=\infty
.

The Delaunay dissimilarity of
X
is defined as

\operatorname{Del}^{X}\colon X\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,\infty],\quad\operatorname{Del}^{X}(x,p)=\operatorname{Del}_{x}(p).

By the above discussion we know that
N\operatorname{Del}^{X}_{t}=N\operatorname{del}^{X}_{t}=\operatorname{Del}(X)
whenever

t>0
.

The Čech dissimilarity of
X
is defined as

d^{X}\colon X\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,\infty],

where
d^{X}(x,p)
is the Euclidean distance between
x\in X
and

p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

The alpha dissimilarity of
X
is defined as

A^{X}=\max(\operatorname{Del}^{X},d^{X})\colon X\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,\infty].

The Delaunay Čech dissimilarity is defined as

\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}^{X}\colon X\times\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)\to[0,\infty],\quad\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}^{X}(x,(p,q))=\max(d^{X}(x,p),\operatorname{Del}^{X}(x,q)).

Note the nerve of the dissimilarity

\operatorname{\textnormal{del\v{C}}}^{X}\colon X\times\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}_{d}\right)\to[0,\infty],\quad\operatorname{\textnormal{del\v{C}}}^{X}(x,(p,q))=\max(d(^{X}x,p),\operatorname{del}^{X}(x,q))

is identical to the nerve of
\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}^{X}
. Moreover, the Dowker nerves of
the Delaunay-, Čech-, alpha- and Delaunay Čech dissimilarities
are the Delaunay-, Čech-, alpha- and Delaunay Čech complexes
respectively. For all these dissimilarities, the corresponding balls are convex, so
the geometric realizations are homotopy equivalent to the
corresponding thickenings. In order to see that the morphism

A^{X}\to d^{X}
of dissimilarities induces homotopy equivalences

|NA^{X}_{t}|\xrightarrow{\simeq}|Nd^{X}_{t}|
it suffices to note that the
corresponding map

(A^{X})^{t}\to(d^{X})^{t}

is the identity map. This holds because

B_{A^{X}}(x,t)=B_{d^{X}}(x,t)\cap B_{\operatorname{Del}^{X}}(x,t)
and given

y\in B_{d^{X}}(x,t)
we have that

y\in\operatorname{Vor}(X,x^{\prime})
for some
x^{\prime}\in X

with
d^{X}(y,x^{\prime})
minimal, so
d^{X}(y,x^{\prime})\leq d^{X}(y,x)<t
and

y\in B_{d^{X}}(x^{\prime},t)\cap B_{\operatorname{Del}^{X}}(x^{\prime},t)
.

In order to see that the morphism

\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}^{X}\to d^{X}
of dissimilarities induces homotopy
equivalences
|N\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}^{X}_{t}|\xrightarrow{\simeq}|Nd^{X}_{t}|
we use the
following lemma:

Lemma 4.1
.

For every
(p,q)\in(\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}^{X})^{t}
, the entire line segment between

(p,p)
and
(p,q)
is contained in
(\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}^{X})^{t}
.

Proof.

In order not to clutter notation we omit superscript
X
on
dissimilarities.
Let
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be the function
\gamma(s)=\left(p,(1-s)p+sq\right)
.
We claim that given
(p,q)\in\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}^{t}
and
s\in[0,1]
the point

(p,\gamma(s))=\left(p,(1-s)p+sq\right)
is in
\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}^{t}
.

If
(p,q)\in\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}^{t}
, there exists a point
x\in X
, such that

p\in B_{d}(x,t)
and
q\in B_{\operatorname{Del}}(x,t)
, that is,

d(q,\operatorname{Vor}(X,x))<h^{\leftarrow}(t)
, where
h^{\leftarrow}
is
the generalized inverse of
h
. Pick
q^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Vor}(X,x)
so that

d(q,q^{\prime})<h^{\leftarrow}(t)
. Let

\gamma^{\prime}\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be the function

\gamma^{\prime}(s)=\left(p,(1-s)p+sq^{\prime}\right)
. Given

s\in[0,1]
, suppose that the point

(p,\gamma^{\prime}(s))=\left(p,(1-s)p+sq^{\prime}\right)
is in

\operatorname{\textnormal{del\v{C}}}^{t}
. Then
\gamma(s)
is in
\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}^{t}
since the distance
between
(1-s)p+sq
and
(1-s)p+sq^{\prime}
is less than

h^{\leftarrow}(t)
.

We are left to show that,
given
s\in[0,1]
, the point

(p,\gamma^{\prime}(s))=\left(p,(1-s)p+sq^{\prime}\right)
is in

\operatorname{\textnormal{del\v{C}}}^{t}
.
Suppose

\gamma^{\prime}(s)\in\operatorname{Vor}(X,y)
for some
s\in[0,1)
and some

y\in X
. We claim that then
p\in B_{d}(y,t)
.
To see this, we may without loss of generality assume that
y\neq x
.
Let

H
be the hyperplane in between
x
and
y
, i.e.

H=\{z\in X\mid d(x,z)=d(y,z)\}.

Let

H_{+}=\{z\in X\mid d(x,z)\geq d(y,z)\}

and

H_{-}=\{z\in X\mid d(x,z)\leq d(y,z)\}.

Since
\gamma^{\prime}(s)\in\operatorname{Vor}(X,y)
we have
\gamma^{\prime}(s)\in H_{+}
. Since

q\in\operatorname{Vor}(X,x)
we have
q\in H_{-}
.
Since the line segment between
p
and
q
either is contained in

H
or intersects
H
at
most once we must have
p\in H_{+}
.
That is,
d(y,p)\leq d(x,p)<t
, so
p\in B_{d}(y,t)
as claimed.
∎

By
lemma

4.1
, the inclusion

(d^{X})^{t}=\cup_{x\in X}B_{d^{X}}(x,t)\to\cup_{x\in X}B_{\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}^{X}}(x,t)=(\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}^{X})^{t},\quad p\mapsto(p,p)

is a deformation retract. In particular it is a homotopy equivalence.

The Relative Delaunay Čech Complex

In this section we consider two subsets
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
of
d-dimensional Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

The
Voronoi diagram
of a finite subset
X
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the
set of pairs of the form
(x,\operatorname{Vor}(X,x))
for
x\in X
, that is,

\operatorname{Vor}(X)=\{(x,\operatorname{Vor}(X,x))\,\mid\,x\in X\}.

This may seem overly formal since the projection on the first factor
gives a bijection
\operatorname{Vor}(X)\to X
. However, when we work with
Voronoi cells with respect to different subsets
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
of

\mathbb{R}^{d}
it may
happen that
\operatorname{Vor}(X_{1},x_{1})=\operatorname{Vor}(X_{2},x_{2})
even when
x_{1}\neq x_{2}
.
The
Voronoi diagram
of the pair of subsets
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
of

\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the set

\operatorname{Vor}(X_{1},X_{2})=\operatorname{Vor}(X_{1})\cup\operatorname{Vor}(X_{2}).

The
discrete Delaunay dissimilarity
of
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
is
defined as

\operatorname{del}^{X_{1},X_{2}}\colon\operatorname{Vor}(X_{1},X_{2})\times\mathbb{R}^{d}_{d}\to[0,\infty],\qquad\operatorname{del}^{X_{1},X_{2}}((x,V),p)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $p\in V$}\\
\infty&\text{if $p\notin V$.}\end{cases}

The simplicial
complex
N\operatorname{del}_{t}^{X_{1},X_{2}}
is independent of
t>0
. It is
the
Delaunay complex

\operatorname{Del}(X_{1},X_{2})
on
X_{1}
and

X_{2}
. In order to
describe the homotopy type of this simplicial complex we thicken the
Voronoi cells like we did in the previous section:

Given a subset
\sigma
of
\operatorname{Vor}(X_{1},X_{2})
and
p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let

d_{\operatorname{Vor}}(p,\sigma)=\max\{d(p,V)\,\mid\,(x,V)\in\sigma\}.

Note that if
\sigma\notin\operatorname{Del}(X_{1},X_{2})
, then the infimum

\varepsilon_{\sigma}
of the continuous function
d_{\operatorname{Vor}}(-,\sigma)\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is strictly positive. Choose
\varepsilon>0
so that
2\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{\sigma}
for every subset

\sigma
of
\operatorname{Vor}(X_{1},X_{2})
that is not in
\operatorname{Del}(X_{1},X_{2})
. Given
(x,V)\in\operatorname{Vor}(X_{1},X_{2})
we define the

\varepsilon
-thickening
V^{\varepsilon}
of
V
by

V^{\varepsilon}=\{p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,\mid\,d(p,V)<\varepsilon\}.

By construction, the nerve of the open cover

((x,V^{\varepsilon}))_{(x,V)\in\operatorname{Vor}(X_{1},X_{2})}
is equal to

\operatorname{Del}(X_{1},X_{2})
.
The Delaunay dissimilarity
\operatorname{Del}^{X_{1},X_{2}}
of
X_{1}
and
X_{2}

is defined as

\operatorname{Vor}(X_{1},X_{2})\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{Del}^{X_{1},X_{2}}}[0,\infty],\qquad\operatorname{Del}^{X_{1},X_{2}}((x,V),p)=h(d(p,V))

for
h\colon[0,\infty]\to[0,\infty]
the order preserving map
defined in the previous section.

The inclusion
X_{1}\to\operatorname{Vor}(X_{1},X_{2})
taking
x\in X_{1}
to

(x,\operatorname{Vor}(x,X_{1}))
induces a morphism of dissimilarities

\operatorname{Del}^{X_{1}}\to\operatorname{Del}^{X_{1},X_{2}}
and an inclusion of nerves

N\operatorname{Del}_{t}^{X_{1}}\subseteq N\operatorname{Del}_{t}^{X_{1},X_{2}}
for
t>0
.

Next, we construct the dissimilarity
A^{X_{1},X_{2}}
as

\operatorname{Vor}(X_{1},X_{2})\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\xrightarrow{A^{X_{1},X_{2}}}[0,\infty],\qquad((x,V),p)\mapsto\max(d(x,p),\operatorname{Del}^{X_{1},X_{2}}((x,V),p)).

Also here we have an obvious inclusion
NA^{X_{1}}_{t}\to NA^{X_{1},X_{2}}_{t}
,
and the
A^{X_{1},X_{2}}
-balls are convex so the nerve lemma yields a
homotopy equivalence

|NA^{X_{1},X_{2}}_{t}|\simeq\bigcup_{(x,V)\in\operatorname{Vor}(X_{1},X_{2})}B_{A^{X_{1},X_{2}}}((x,V),t)=\bigcup_{x\in X_{1}\cup X_{2}}B_{d^{X_{1}\cup X_{2}}}(x,t)=(X_{1}\cup X_{2})^{t}.

Finally, we construct the dissimilarity
\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}^{X_{1},X_{2}}

\displaystyle\operatorname{Vor}(X_{1},X_{2})\times(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}^{X_{1},X_{2}}}[0,\infty],

\displaystyle((x,V),(p,q))

\displaystyle\mapsto\max(d(x,p),\operatorname{Del}^{X_{1},X_{2}}((x,V),q))

Here again we have an obvious inclusion
N\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}^{X_{1}}_{t}\to N\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}^{X_{1},X_{2}}_{t}
,
and the
\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}^{X_{1},X_{2}}
-balls are convex so the nerve lemma yields a
homotopy equivalence

|N\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}^{X_{1},X_{2}}_{t}|\simeq(\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}^{X_{1},X_{2}})^{t}

The following variant of
lemma

4.1
implies that

(\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}^{X_{1},X_{2}})^{t}

is a deformation retract of

(X_{1}\cup X_{2})^{t}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

For every
(p,q)\in(\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}^{X_{1},X_{2}})^{t}
, the entire line segment between

(p,p)
and
(p,q)
is contained in
(\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}^{X_{1},X_{2}})^{t}
.

Proof.

Given

(p,q)\in(\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}^{X_{1},X_{2}})^{t}=(\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}^{X_{1}})^{t}\cup(\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}^{X_{2}})^{t}
, we have
(p,q)\in(\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}^{X_{i}})^{t}
for some

i\in\{1,2\}
. Then also
(p,p)
lies in

(\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}^{X_{i}})^{t}
, and
lemma

4.1
proves the claim.
∎

6
Implementation Of The Relative Delaunay Čech Complex

In this section we explain how the relative Delaunay complex can be
realized as a standard Delaunay complex by embedding in one dimension
higher.

We fix some notation used in this section:
X_{1}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
and

X_{2}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
are
finite subsets. We let
s
be a positive real number, we
let
Z=X_{1}\times\{s\}\cup X_{2}\times\{-s\}
and we let
\mathrm{pr}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be the projection omitting the
last coordinate.

Lemma 6.1
.

The projection
\mathrm{pr}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
induces a surjection

\operatorname{Vor}(Z)\xrightarrow{g}\operatorname{Vor}(X_{1},X_{2}),\qquad((x,s),V)\mapsto(x,V(X_{1},x)),\quad((x,-s),V)\mapsto(x,V(X_{2},x)),

with
\mathrm{pr}(V)\subseteq V(X_{i},x)
for
x\in X_{i}
. Given

(x,V)\in\operatorname{Vor}(X_{1},X_{2})
the fiber
g^{-1}((x,V))
consists
of all elements of
\operatorname{Vor}(Z))
of the form
((x,a),V)
for

a\in\{\pm s\}
.

Proof.

We show that
\mathrm{pr}(V)\subseteq V(X_{1},x_{1})
for

((x_{1},s),V)\in\operatorname{Vor}(Z)

with
x_{1}\in X_{1}
.
Given
(p,r)\in V
we have for all points of
the form
(x_{1}^{\prime},s)
for
x_{1}^{\prime}\in X_{1}
that

d((p,r),(x_{1},s))\leq d((p,r),(x_{1}^{\prime},s))
. This implies that

d(p,x_{1})\leq d(p,x_{1}^{\prime})
, and thus
p\in V(X_{1},x_{1})
. We
conclude that
\mathrm{pr}(V)\subseteq V(X_{1},x_{1})
. An analogous argument
applies for elements of the form
((x_{2},-s),V)
in

\operatorname{Vor}(Z)
.
∎

Let
s_{1}
be larger than the largest filtration value of the alpha
complex of
X_{1}
. Then the function

j_{1}\colon\operatorname{Vor}(X_{1})\to\operatorname{Vor}(Z)
defined by

j_{1}(x_{1},V)=((x_{1},s),V(Z,(x_{1},s)))
induces a simplicial map of nerves

\operatorname{del}(X_{1})\to\operatorname{del}(Z)
for all
s>s_{1}
.
Similarly, there is a simplicial map
\operatorname{del}(X_{2})\to\operatorname{del}(Z)
for all
s>s_{2}

when
s_{2}
is larger than all filtration values of the alpha
complex of
X_{2}
.
Let

s(X_{1},X_{2})=\max(s_{1},s_{2})
.

Choose
\varepsilon>0
satisfying the following two criteria:

1.

2\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{\sigma}
for every subset

\sigma
of
\operatorname{Vor}(X_{1},X_{2})
that is not in
\operatorname{Del}(X_{1},X_{2})
.

2.

2\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{\sigma}
for every subset

\sigma
of
\operatorname{Vor}(Z)
that is not in
\operatorname{Del}(Z)
.

Let
h\colon[0,\infty]\to[0,\infty]
be the order
preserving map defined in
eq.

1
, and let
\operatorname{Del}^{Z}
and

\operatorname{Del}^{X_{1},X_{2}}
be constructed using
h
. We define a new dissimilarity

D\colon\operatorname{Vor}(Z)\times(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d+1})\to[0,\infty],\quad D((z,V),(p,q))=\max(d(\mathrm{pr}(z),p),\operatorname{Del}^{Z}((z,V),q)).

Note that the underlying simplicial complex
\bigcup_{t>0}ND_{t}
of
the nerve of
D
is the Delaunay complex
\operatorname{del}(Z)
. The
filtration value of
\sigma\in\operatorname{del}(Z)
in
the neve of
D
is the filtration value of
g(\sigma)
in the
nerve of
\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}^{X_{1},X_{2}}
.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
X_{1}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
X_{2}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be
finite. Choose
s>s(X_{1},X_{2})
. Then

\operatorname{Vor}(Z)\xrightarrow{g}\operatorname{Vor}(X_{1},X_{2})
and

\mathrm{id}\times\mathrm{pr}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
form a morphism

f=(g,\mathrm{id}\times\mathrm{pr})\colon D\to\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}^{X_{1},X_{2}}

of
dissimilarities inducing a homotopy equivalence

g\colon ND_{t}\to N\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}^{X_{1},X_{2}}_{t}

for every
t>0
.

Proof.

For
i=1,2
the inclusion
\mathrm{pr}(V)\subseteq V(X_{i},x)
for
((x,(-1)^{i-1}s),V)\in\operatorname{Vor}(Z)
implies that

\operatorname{Del}^{X_{1},X_{2}}(g(z,V),\mathrm{pr}(q))\leq\operatorname{Del}^{Z}((z,V),q)

for all
((z,V),q)\in\operatorname{Vor}(Z)
. So we have a morphism
f=(g,\mathrm{id}\times\mathrm{pr})\colon D\to\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}^{X_{1},X_{2}}
.

In order to show that
g
induces a homotopy equivalence of geometric
realizations, by the Nerve Lemma, it suffices to show that given a
simplex
\sigma
of
N\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}^{X_{1},X_{2}}_{t}
, the inverse image

g^{-1}(\sigma)
is a simplex of
ND_{t}
. Let
p
be a point
in the intersection of the Voronoi cells in
\sigma
. Write

g^{-1}(\sigma)=\tau_{1}\cup\tau_{2}
, where
\tau_{1}
consists
of Voronoi cells with centers at height
s
and
\tau_{2}

consists of Voronoi cells with centers at height
-s
. Let

\sigma_{1}=\{(x_{1},s)\,\mid\,(x_{1},V(X_{1},x_{1}))\in\sigma\}

and

\sigma_{2}=\{(x_{2},-s)\,\mid\,(x_{2},V(X_{2},x_{2}))\in\sigma\}
.

Suppose that
\tau_{2}
is empty. Then actually

\sigma\in\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}^{X_{1}}_{t}
, and since
s>s_{1}
we know that

j_{1}(\sigma)\in\operatorname{del}(Z)
. Since
g\circ j_{1}
is the inclusion
of
\operatorname{Vor}(X_{1})
in
\operatorname{Vor}(X_{1},X_{2})=\operatorname{Vor}(X_{1})\cup\operatorname{Vor}(X_{2})
we
know that
j_{1}(\sigma)\subseteq g^{-1}(\sigma)=\tau_{1}
and that

j_{1}(\sigma)\in ND_{t}
. On the other hand, since
\tau_{2}
is
empty and
j_{1}
is injective, we know that

g^{-1}(\sigma)
has the same cardinality as
j_{1}(\sigma)
, so
they must be equal. We conclude that
g^{-1}(\sigma)
is a simplex
of
ND_{t}
. A similar argument applies when
\tau_{1}
is empty.

In the remaining case where both
\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{2}
are
nonempty, the function

f\colon\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\to\mathbb{R}^{,}\qquad f(a)=d_{\operatorname{Vor}}(a,\sigma_{1})-d_{\operatorname{Vor}}(a,\sigma_{2})

has
f((p,-s))>0
and
f((p,s))<0
. By the intermediate
value theorem there exists
t\in[-s,s]
with
f(p,t)=0
. Since
(p,t)
has the same distance to all elements of

\sigma_{1}
and also has the same distance to all elements of

\sigma_{2}
we conclude that
(p,t)
is in the intersection of
the Voronoi cells in
g^{-1}(\sigma)=\tau_{1}\cup\tau_{2}
. Thus

\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}^{Z}((z,V),p)=0
and
d(\mathrm{pr}(z),p)<t
for all

(z,V)\in g^{-1}(\sigma)
. In particular

g^{-1}(\sigma)\in ND_{t}
.
∎

We are now ready to compute persistent homology of
X_{1}\cup X_{2}

relative to
X_{1}
. The relative Delaunay-Čech complex

\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}(X_{1}\cup X_{2},X_{1})
is the filtered simplicial complex with

\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}(X_{1}\cup X_{2},X_{1})_{t}=j_{1}(\operatorname{del}(X_{1}))\cup ND_{t}
.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
X_{1}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
X_{2}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be
finite. Choose
s>s(X_{1},X_{2})
. Then there is an isomorphism

(H_{*}(\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}(X_{1}\cup X_{2},X_{1})_{t}))_{t>0}\cong(H_{*}((X_{1}\cup X_{2})^{t},X_{1}^{t}))_{t>0}

of persistence modules.

Proof.

Since
j_{1}(\operatorname{del}(X_{1})
is contractible, the geometric realization
of
\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}(X_{1}\cup X_{2},X_{1})_{t}
is homotopy equivalent to the
quotient space
|\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}(X_{1}\cup X_{2},X_{1})_{t}|/|j_{1}(\operatorname{del}(X_{1})|
. This
quotient space is homeomorphic to

|ND_{t}|/|ND_{t}\cap j_{1}(\operatorname{Del}(X_{1}))|
. By
proposition

6.2

the map
g\colon ND_{t}\to N\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}^{X_{1},X_{2}}_{t}
induces a homotopy
equivalence of geometric realizations. Moreover
g
induces an
isomorphism
ND_{t}\cap j_{1}(\operatorname{Del}(X_{1}))\to N\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}^{X_{1}}_{t}
. Combining
these two statements,
g

induces a homotopy equivalence

|ND_{t}|/|ND_{t}\cap j_{1}(\operatorname{Del}(X_{1}))|\to|N\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}^{X_{1},X_{2}}_{t}|/|N\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}^{X_{1}}_{t}|
.
The space

|N\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}^{X_{1},X_{2}}_{t}|
is homotpy equivalent to the Euclidean

t
-thickening
(X_{1}\cup X_{2})^{t}
of
X_{1}\cup X_{2}
and

|N\operatorname{\textnormal{Del\v{C}}}^{X_{1}}_{t}|
is homotopy equivalent to the Euclidean

t
-thickening
X_{1}^{t}
of
X_{1}
.
∎

Finally, we note that the size of the relative Delaunay-Čech
complex grows linearly with the sizes
n_{i}
of the finite subsets

X_{i}
. The Delaunay triangulation of
n

points in
d
dimensions contains at most

O(n\lceil d/2\rceil)
simplices
[
11
]
. Since we use
the Delaunay triangulation of
n_{1}+n_{2}
points in
d+1

dimensions to compute the relative Delaunay-Čech complex, it
contains at most
O((n_{1}+n_{2})\lceil(d+1)/2\rceil)
simplices.
This concludes the proof of
theorem

1.1
.

Semi-invariant Conformal submersions with horizontal Reeb vector field
Uday Chand De, Shashikant Pandey and Punam Gupta

Abstract




MSC Classification.
53C15, 53C40, 53C50.

Keywords.
Conformal submersion, semi-invariant Riemannian
submersion, semi-invariant conformal
\zeta^{\perp}
Riemannian
submersion, almost contact metric manifolds, totally geodesic.

Abstract.

The present paper deals with the characterization of a new submersion named
semi-invariant conformal
\zeta^{\perp}
-Riemannian submersion from almost
contact metric manifolds onto Riemannian manifolds which is the
generalization of some known submersions on Riemannian manifolds. We give
important and adequate conditions for such submersions to be totally
geodesic and harmonic. Also, few examples are examined for such submersions
endowed with horizontal Reeb vector field.

In comparison of conformal submersions, Riemannian submersions are specific.

In the present manuscript, we establish the definition of semi-invariant

Preliminaries

In this segment, we recall some definitions which will be required to study
throughout the paper.

Let
(M^{\ast},\varphi_{\ast},\zeta,\eta_{\ast},g_{m})
be an odd
dimensional acm manifold
[
8
]
consisting of a
(1,1)
-tensor field
\varphi_{\ast}
, a vector field
\zeta
, a
1
-form
\eta_{\ast}
and a
Riemannian metric
g_{m}
. Then

\varphi_{\ast}^{2}P=-P+\eta_{\ast}(P)\zeta,

(2.1)

\varphi_{\ast}\,\zeta=0,\ \eta_{\ast}\circ\varphi_{\ast}=0,\ \eta_{\ast}(\zeta)=1,\ g_{m}(P,\zeta)=\eta_{\ast}(P),

(2.2)

and

g_{m}(\varphi_{\ast}P,\varphi_{\ast}Q)=g_{m}(P,Q)-\eta_{\ast}(P)\eta_{\ast}(Q),\quad g_{m}(\varphi_{\ast}P,Q)=-g_{m}(P,\varphi_{\ast}Q),

(2.3)

for any vector fields
P,Q\in\Gamma(TM^{\ast})
.

An acm manifold
(M^{\ast},\varphi_{\ast},\zeta,\eta_{\ast},g_{m})
is
called
Sasakian
[
32
]

if

(\nabla_{P}\varphi_{\ast})Q=g_{m}(P,Q)\zeta-\eta_{\ast}(Q)P

(2.4)

and

\nabla_{P}\zeta=-\varphi_{\ast}P

(2.5)

for any vector fields
P,Q\in\Gamma(TM^{\ast})
, where
\nabla
is the
Levi-Civita connection.

Now, we give some useful definitions of submersions to extend our definition
of semi-invariant conformal Riemannian submersions endowed with the
horizontal Reeb vector field.

Definition 2.1

[
25
]
Let
(M^{\ast},g_{m})
and
(N^{\ast},g_{n})
be
Riemannian manifolds, where
\dim(M^{\ast})=m
,
\dim(N^{\ast})=n
and
m>n
. A Riemannian submersion
\Psi:M^{\ast}\rightarrow N^{\ast}
is a map of
M^{\ast}
onto
N^{\ast}
satisfying the following conditions:

(i)

\Psi
has maximal rank.

(ii)

The metric of horizontal vectors is preserved by the
differential
\Psi_{\ast}
.

For each
z\in N^{\ast}
,
\Psi^{-1}(z)
is an
(m-n)
-dimensional
submanifold of
M^{\ast}
. The submanifolds
\Psi^{-1}(z)
,
z\in N^{\ast}
, are called
fibers
. A vector field on
M^{\ast}
is called vertical
and horizontal if it is always tangent to fibers and orthogonal to fibers,
respectively. A vector field
P
on
M
is called basic if
P
is horizontal
and
\Psi
-related to a vector field
P^{\prime}
on
N
, that is,
\Psi_{\ast}P_{x}=P_{\Psi_{\ast}(x)}^{\prime}
for all
x\in M.
The
projection morphisms on the distributions
\ker\Psi_{\ast}
and
(\ker\Psi_{\ast})^{\perp}
are denoted by
{\mathcal{V}_{1}}
and
{\mathcal{H}_{1}}
, respectively. The sections of
{\mathcal{V}_{1}}
and
{\mathcal{H}_{1}}
are called the vertical vector fields and horizontal vector fields,
respectively. So

{\mathcal{V}_{1}}_{p}=T_{p}\left(\Psi^{-1}(q)\right),\qquad{\mathcal{H}_{1}}_{p}=T_{p}\left(\Psi^{-1}(q)\right)^{\perp}.

Definition 2.2

[
26
]
Let
(M^{\ast},g_{m})
and
(N^{\ast},g_{n})
be
Riemannian manifolds, where
\dim(M^{\ast})=m
,
\dim(N^{\ast})=n
and
m>n
. A (horizontally) conformal submersion
\Psi:M^{\ast}\rightarrow N^{\ast}

is a map of
M^{\ast}
onto
N^{\ast}
satisfying the following axioms:

(i)

\Psi
has maximal rank.

(ii)

The angle between the horizontal vectors is preserved by the
differential
\Psi_{\ast}
,

g_{n}(\Psi_{\ast}U,\Psi_{\ast}V)=\Lambda_{\ast}(p)g_{m}(U,V),\quad U,V\in{\mathcal{H}_{1}}_{p_{\ast}}

where
\Lambda_{\ast}(p)
is a non-zero number and
p\in M^{\ast}
. The
number
\Lambda_{\ast}(p)
is called the square dilation of
\Psi
at
p
,
it is necessarily non-negative. Its square root
\lambda_{\ast}(p)=\sqrt{\Lambda_{\ast}(p)}
is called the dilation of
\Psi
at
p
.

Clearly, Riemannian submersion is a horizontally conformal submersion with
\lambda_{\ast}=1
.

Definition 2.3

[
6
]
Let
\Psi:(M^{\ast},g_{m})\rightarrow(N^{\ast},g_{n})
be a
conformal submersion. A vector field
E_{1}
on
M^{\ast}
is called
projectiable if there exist a vector field
\widehat{E_{1}}
on
N^{\ast}

such that
\Psi_{\ast}({E_{1}}_{p})=\widehat{E_{1}}_{\Psi_{\ast}(p)}

for any
p\in M^{\ast}.
In this case,
E_{1}
and
\widehat{E_{1}}
are
called
\Psi
-related. A horizontal vector field
V
on
M^{\ast}
is
called basic, if it is projectiable.

It is to be noted that if
\widehat{W}
is a vector field on
N^{\ast},

then there exists a unique basic vector field
W
on
M^{\ast}
which is
called the horizontal lift of
\widehat{W}.

Definition 2.4

[
6
]
A horizontally conformal submersion
\Psi:(M^{\ast},g_{m})\rightarrow(N^{\ast},g_{n})
is called horizontally homothetic if
the slope of its dilation
\lambda_{\ast}
is vertical, that is,

{\mathcal{H}_{1}}(grad\lambda_{\ast})=0

at
p\in M^{\ast}
, where
\mathcal{H}{{}_{1}}
is the projection on the
horizontal space
\left(\ker\Psi_{\ast}\right)^{\perp}
.

The second fundamental tensors of all fibers
\Psi^{-1}(q),\ q\in N^{\ast}

give rise to tensor field
T
and
A
on
M^{\ast}
defined by O’Neill
[
25
]
for arbitrary vector field
E
and
F
, which is

T_{E}F={\mathcal{H}_{1}}\nabla_{{\mathcal{V}_{1}}E}^{M}{\mathcal{V}_{1}}F+{\mathcal{V}_{1}}\nabla_{{\mathcal{V}_{1}}E}^{M}{\mathcal{H}_{1}}F,

(2.6)

A_{E}F={\mathcal{H}_{1}}\nabla_{{\mathcal{H}_{1}}E}^{M}{\mathcal{V}_{1}}F+{\mathcal{V}_{1}}\nabla_{{\mathcal{H}_{1}}E}^{M}{\mathcal{H}_{1}}F,

(2.7)

where
{\mathcal{V}_{1}}
and
{\mathcal{H}_{1}}
are the vertical and
horizontal projections, respectively.

From equations (
2.6
) and (
2.7
), we obtain the following
equations

\nabla_{V}W=T_{V}W+\widehat{\nabla}_{V}W,

(2.8)

\nabla_{V}P={\mathcal{H}_{1}}\nabla_{V}P+T_{V}P,

(2.9)

\nabla_{P}V=A_{P}V+{\mathcal{V}_{1}}\nabla_{P}V,

(2.10)

\nabla_{P}Q={\mathcal{H}_{1}}\nabla_{P}Q+A_{P}Q,

(2.11)

for all
V,W\in\Gamma(\ker\Psi_{\ast})
and
P,Q\in\Gamma(\ker\Psi_{\ast})^{\perp},
where
{\mathcal{V}_{1}}\nabla_{V}W=\widehat{\nabla}_{V}W.
If
P
is the basic vector field, then
A_{P}Q={\mathcal{H}_{1}}\nabla_{Q}P.

Clearly, for
p\in M,

U\in{\mathcal{V}_{1}}_{p}
and
P\in{\mathcal{H}_{1}}_{p}
, the linear operators

{T}_{U},{A}_{P}:T_{p}M^{\ast}\rightarrow T_{p}M^{\ast}

are skew-symmetric, that is,

g_{m}({A}_{P}E,F)=-g_{m}(E,{A}_{P}F){\rm and}g_{m}({T}_{U}E,F)=-g_{m}(E,{T}_{U}F),

(2.12)

for all
E,F\in

T_{p}M^{\ast}.
It is clear, the restriction of
{T}
to
the vertical distribution
{T}|_{\ker\Psi_{\ast}\times\ker\Psi_{\ast}}
is certainly the second fundamental form of the fibres of
\Psi
. From (
2.12
), it is easy to say that
\Psi
has totally geodesic fibres if
and only if
{T}\equiv 0
.

Consider the smooth map
\Psi:(M^{\ast},g_{m})\rightarrow(N^{\ast},g_{n})

between Riemannian manifolds. Then the differential
\Psi_{\ast}
of
\Psi
can be noticed as a section of the bundle
Hom(TM^{\ast},\Psi^{-1}TN^{\ast})\rightarrow M^{\ast}
, where
\Psi^{-1}TN^{\ast}
is the
bundle which has fibres
\left(\Psi^{-1}TN^{\ast}\right)_{x}=T_{f(x)}N^{\ast}
,
x\in M^{\ast}
.
Hom(TM^{\ast},\Psi^{-1}TN^{\ast})
has a connection
\nabla
induced from the Riemannian connection
\nabla^{M^{\ast}}
and the pullback connection. The second fundamental form of
\Psi
is

(\nabla\Psi_{\ast})(E,F)=\nabla_{E}^{N^{\ast}}\Psi_{\ast}F-\Psi_{\ast}(\nabla_{E}^{M^{\ast}}F),{\rm forall}E,F\in\Gamma(TM^{\ast}),

(2.13)

where
\nabla^{N^{\ast}}
is the pullback connection (
[
6
,
7
]
). The map
\Psi
is said to be totally geodesic
[
6
]
if
(\nabla\Psi_{\ast})(E,F)=0,
for all
E,F\in\Gamma(TM^{\ast})
.

Lemma 2.5

[
36

Let
\Psi:(M^{\ast},g_{m})\rightarrow(N^{\ast},g_{n})
be a smooth map between

(M^{\ast},g_{m})

(N^{\ast},g_{n})

\Psi_{\ast}([P,Q])=\nabla_{P}^{N^{\ast}}\Psi_{\ast}Q-\nabla_{Q}^{N^{\ast}}\Psi_{\ast}P,

(2.14)

for all

.

From Lemma

, we come to the conclusion

[P,Q]\in\Gamma(ker\Psi_{\ast})

(2.15)

for

and

.

A smooth map
\Psi:(M^{\ast},g_{m})\rightarrow(N^{\ast},g_{n})
is said
to be harmonic
[
6
]
if and only if
\mathrm{trace}(\nabla\Psi_{\ast})=0
. The tension field of
\Psi
is the section
\tau(\Psi)
of
\Gamma(\Psi^{-1}TN)
and defined by

\tau(\Psi)={div}\Psi_{\ast}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\nabla\Psi_{\ast})(e_{i},e_{i}),

(2.16)

where
\{e_{1},e_{2},......,e_{m}\}
is the orthonormal basis on
M^{\ast}
. Then it follows that ns condition for
\Psi
to be harmonic is
\tau(\Psi)=0

[
6
]
.

[

Lemma 2.6

Let
\Psi:(M^{\ast},g_{m})\rightarrow(N^{\ast},g_{n})
be a
horizontal conformal submersion,

are horizontal vector fields and

are vertical vector fields. Then

Definition 3.1

Let
\left(M^{\ast},\varphi_{\ast},\zeta,\eta_{\ast},g_{m}\right)
be an acm manifold and
\left(N^{\ast},g_{n}\right)
be a
Riemannian manifold. A horizontally conformal submersion
\Psi:(M^{\ast},g_{m})\rightarrow(N^{\ast},g_{n})
with slope
\lambda_{\ast}

\zeta^{\perp}
-Rs if
\zeta

and there is a distribution
D^{\ast}_{1}\subseteq ker\Psi_{{\ast}}

ker\Psi_{\ast}=D^{\ast}_{1}\oplus D^{\ast}_{2},

(3.1)

\varphi_{\ast}(D^{\ast}_{1})=D^{\ast}_{1},\ \varphi_{\ast}(D^{\ast}_{2})\subseteq(ker\Psi_{\ast})^{\perp},

(3.2)

where
D^{\ast}_{2}
is orthogonal complementary to
D^{\ast}_{1}
in
ker\Psi_{{\ast}}
.

It is to be noted that the distribution
ker\Psi_{\ast}
is integrable.

Further to prove the consistency of sic
\zeta^{\perp}
-Rs in acm
manifolds, we are putting some examples here.

Example 3.2

Every anti-invariant
\zeta^{\perp}
-Riemannian submersion from an acm
manifold onto a Riemannian manifold is a sic
\zeta^{\perp}
-Rs with
\lambda_{\ast}=I
and
D^{\ast}_{1}=\{0\}
, where

Example 3.3

Every semi-invariant
\zeta^{\perp}
submersion from an acm manifold onto
a Riemannian manifold is a sic
\zeta^{\perp}
-Rs with
\lambda_{\ast}=I.

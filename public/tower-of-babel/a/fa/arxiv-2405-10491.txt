Formal self-duality and numerical self-duality for symmetric association schemes
Kazumasa Nomura, Paul Terwilliger

Abstract




Abstract

Let
{\mathcal{X}}=(X,\{R_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d})
denote a symmetric association scheme.
Fix an ordering
\{E_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
of the primitive idempotents of
\mathcal{X}
,
and let
P
(resp.
Q
) denote the corresponding first eigenmatrix (resp. second eigenmatrix) of
\mathcal{X}
.
The scheme
\mathcal{X}
is said to be formally self-dual (with respect to the ordering
\{E_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
)
whenever
P=Q
.
We define
\mathcal{X}
to be numerically self-dual (with respect to the ordering
\{E_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
)
whenever the intersection numbers and Krein parameters satisfy
p^{h}_{i,j}=q^{h}_{i,j}
for
0\leq h,i,j\leq d
.
It is known that with respect to the ordering
\{E_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
,
formal self-duality implies numerical self-duality.
This raises the following question:
is it possible that with respect to the ordering
\{E_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
,

\mathcal{X}
is numerically self-dual but not formally self-dual?
This is possible as we will show.
We display an example of a symmetric association scheme and an ordering the primitive idempotents
with respect to which the scheme is numerically self-dual but not formally self-dual.
We have the following additional results about self-duality.
Assume that
\mathcal{X}
is
P
-polynomial.
We show that the following are equivalent:
(i)

\mathcal{X}
is formally self-dual with respect to the ordering
\{E_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
;
(ii)

\mathcal{X}
is numerically self-dual with respect to the ordering
\{E_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
.
Assume that the ordering
\{E_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
is
Q
-polynomial.
We show that the following are equivalent:
(i)

\mathcal{X}
is formally self-dual with respect to the ordering
\{E_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
;
(ii)

\mathcal{X}
is numerically self-dual with respect to the ordering
\{E_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
.

1
Introduction

In this paper we consider two kinds of self-duality for symmetric association schemes:
formal self-duality and numerical self-duality.
The notion of formal self-duality is well-known
[
3
, p. 49]
,
[
9
, Section 6]
.
We introduce the notion of numerical self-duality
in this paper.
We now recall the notion of formal self-duality.
Consider a symmetric association scheme
{\mathcal{X}}=(X,\{R_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d})
.
The associate matrices
\{A_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
of
\mathcal{X}
form a basis of a subalgebra

\mathcal{M}
of
\text{\rm Mat}_{X}(\mathbb{C})
, called the Bose-Mesner algebra of
\mathcal{X}
.
The algebra
\mathcal{M}
has another basis
\{E_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
, consisting of the primitive idempotents.
There are matrices
P
and
Q
in
\text{\rm Mat}_{d+1}(\mathbb{C})
called the first and second eigenmatrices
of
\mathcal{X}
.
The matrix
P
is the transition matrix from the basis
\{E_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
to the basis
\{A_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
,
and the matrix
|X|^{-1}Q
is the transition matrix from the basis
\{A_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
to the basis

\{E_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
.
The scheme
\mathcal{X}
is said to be formally self-dual (with respect to the given ordering
\{E_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}

of the primitive idempotents) whenever
P=Q
.
There are many symmetric association schemes that are formally self-dual.
One familiar example is the Hamming scheme
[
1
, Section III.3.2]
.
In the next two paragraphs we give some additional examples.

The notion of a spin model was introduced by V. F. R. Jones
[
8
]
to construct invariants
for knots and links.
A spin model
W
is a symmetric matrix that satisfies two conditions, called type II and
type III.
In
[
10
]
the first author constructed an algebra
N(W)
that contains
W
and
is the Bose-Mesner algebra of a symmetric association scheme
\mathcal{X}
.
In
[
7
]
, it is shown that
\mathcal{X}
is formally self-dual.
The formal self-duality of
\mathcal{X}
was used to investigate the
structure of
N(W)
in
[
7
,
11
,
4
]
.

A symmetric association acheme
{\mathcal{X}}=(X,\{R_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d})
is said to be
P
-polynomial whenever
the intersection numbers satisfy the following conditions for
0\leq h,i,j\leq d
:

(i)

p^{h}_{i,j}=0
if one of
h,i,j
is greater than the sum of the other two;

(ii)

p^{h}_{i,j}\neq 0
if one of
h,i,j
is equal to the sum of the other two.

There is a type of
P
-polynomial scheme, said to have classical parameters
(d,b,\alpha,\beta)
(see
[
3
, Section 6.1]
).
By
[
3
, Corollary 8.4.4]
, if
\alpha=b-1
then the scheme is formally self-dual.
The following schemes satisfy the condition
\alpha=b-1
and
b\neq 1
:
bilinear forms scheme
[
3
, p. 280]
;
alternating forms scheme
[
3
, p. 282]
;
quadratic forms scheme
[
3
, p. 290]
;
Hermitian forms scheme
[
3
, p. 285]
;
affine
E_{6}(q)
scheme
[
3
, p. 340]
;
extended ternary Golay code scheme
[
3
, p. 356]
.
In
[
6
]
it is shown that if
\alpha=b-1
and
b\neq 1

then the quantum algebra
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}})
acts on the standard module of the scheme,
where
b=q^{2}
.

We now introduce the concept of numerical self-duality.
For the rest of this section,
let
{\mathcal{X}}=(X,\{R_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d})
denote a symmetric association scheme.
The Bose-Mesner algebra of
\mathcal{X}
is closed under the
entry-wise product
\circ
.
The Krein parameters
q^{h}_{i,j}

(0\leq h,i,j\leq d)
are defined by

E_{i}\circ E_{j}=|X|^{-1}\sum_{h=0}^{d}q^{h}_{i,j}E_{h}
for
0\leq i,j\leq d
.
The scheme
\mathcal{X}
is said to be numerically self-dual
(with respect to the given ordering
\{E_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
of the primitive idempotents)
whenever

p^{h}_{i,j}=q^{h}_{i,j}\qquad\qquad(0\leq h,i,j\leq d).

It is known
[
3
, Section 2.3]
that if
\mathcal{X}
is formally self-dual with respect to
the ordering
\{E_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}

then
\mathcal{X}
is numerically self-dual with respect to the ordering
\{E_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
.
For a given ordering
\{E_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
of the primitive idempotents,
is it possible that
\mathcal{X}
is numerically self-dual but not formally self-dual?
This is possible as we will show.
We will demonstrate with an example of a symmetric association scheme
and an ordering of the primitive idempotents
with respect to which the scheme is numerically self-dual but not formally self-dual.

We obtain the following additional results about self-duality.
Assume that
{\mathcal{X}}
is
P
-polynomial.
Fix an ordering
\{E_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
of the primitive idempotents of
\mathcal{X}
.
We show that the following are equivalent:

(i)

\mathcal{X}
is formally self-dual with respect to the ordering
\{E_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
;

(ii)

\mathcal{X}
is numerically self-dual with respect to the ordering
\{E_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
.

An ordering
\{E_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
of the primitive idempotents of
\mathcal{X}
is said to be

Q
-polynomial whenever the Krein parameters satisfy the following conditions for
0\leq h,i,j\leq d
:

(i)

q^{h}_{i,j}=0
if one of
h,i,j
is greater than the sum of the other two;

(ii)

q^{h}_{i,j}\neq 0
if one of
h,i,j
is equal to the sum of the other two.

Assume that there is a
Q
-polynomial ordering

\{E_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
of the primitive idempotents of
\mathcal{X}
.
We show that the following are equivalent:

(i)

\mathcal{X}
is formally self-dual with respect to the ordering
\{E_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
;

(ii)

\mathcal{X}
is numerically self-dual with respect to the ordering
\{E_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
.

2
Preliminaries

We now begin our formal argument.
Throughout the paper, the following notation is in effect.
Let
{\mathbb{F}}
denote a field.
For an integer
n\geq 1
, let
\text{\rm Mat}_{n}({\mathbb{F}})
denote the
{\mathbb{F}}
-algebra consisting of the
n\times n
matrices
with all entries in
{\mathbb{F}}
.
Let
V
denote a vector space over
{\mathbb{F}}
with dimension
n
.
Let
\text{\rm End}(V)
denote the
{\mathbb{F}}
-algebra consisting of the
{\mathbb{F}}
-linear
maps
V\to V
.
We recall how each basis
\{v_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
of
V
gives an algebra isomorphism
\text{\rm End}(V)\to\text{\rm Mat}_{n}({\mathbb{F}})
.
For
A\in{\rm End}(V)
and
M\in\text{\rm Mat}_{n}({\mathbb{F}})
, we say that

M
represents
A
with respect to
\{v_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
whenever

Av_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}M_{i,j}v_{i}
for
1\leq j\leq n
.
The isomorphism sends
A
to the unique matrix in
\text{\rm Mat}_{n}({\mathbb{F}})
that represents
A
with
respect to
\{v_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
.
For a finite nonempty set
X
,
let
\text{\rm Mat}_{X}({\mathbb{F}})
denote the
{\mathbb{F}}
-algebra consisting of the matrices
whose rows and columns are indexed by
X
and with all entries in
{\mathbb{F}}
.
Let
J\in\text{\rm Mat}_{X}({\mathbb{F}})
have all entries
1
.
For
{A}
,
{B}\in\text{\rm Mat}_{X}({\mathbb{F}})
,
the
entry-wise product

{A}\circ{B}\in\text{\rm Mat}_{X}({\mathbb{F}})
is defined by

({A}\circ{B})_{x,y}={A}_{x,y}{B}_{x,y}
for
x,y\in X
.
For
A\in\text{\rm Mat}_{X}({\mathbb{F}})
and
B\in\text{\rm Mat}_{Y}({\mathbb{F}})
, the
Kronecker product

A\otimes B
is the matrix in
\text{\rm Mat}_{X\times Y}({\mathbb{F}})
such that

\displaystyle(A\otimes B)_{(x_{1},y_{1}),(x_{2},y_{2})}

\displaystyle=A_{x_{1},x_{2}}B_{y_{1},y_{2}}

\displaystyle(x_{1},x_{2}\in X,\;y_{1},y_{2}\in Y).

For
A,A^{\prime}\in\text{\rm Mat}_{X}({\mathbb{F}})
and
B,B^{\prime}\in\text{\rm Mat}_{Y}({\mathbb{F}})
,

(A\otimes B)(A^{\prime}\otimes B^{\prime})=(AA^{\prime})\otimes(BB^{\prime}).

3
Association schemes

For the rest of this paper, fix an integer
d\geq 1
.
By a
d
-class symmetric association scheme

[
3
, Section 2.1]
we mean a sequence

{\mathcal{X}}=(X,\{R_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}),

where
X
is a finite nonempty set and
R_{0},R_{1},\ldots,R_{d}
are nonempty subsets of
the Cartesian product
X\times X
, that satisfy the following (i)–(iv).

(i)

X\times X=R_{0}\cup R_{1}\cup\cdots\cup R_{d}
(disjoint union).

(ii)

R_{0}=\{(x,x)\,|\,x\in X\}
.

(iii)

R_{i}^{t}=R_{i}
for
0\leq i\leq d
, where
R_{i}^{t}=\{(y,x)\,|\,(x,y)\in R_{i}\}
.

(iv)

There exist integers
p^{h}_{i,j}

(0\leq h,i,j\leq d)

such that for
(x,y)\in R_{h}
,

p^{h}_{i,j}=|\{z\in X\,|\,(x,z)\in R_{i},\;(z,y)\in R_{j}\}|.

Note by (iii) that

(v)

p^{h}_{i,j}=p^{h}_{j,i}\qquad(0\leq h,i,j\leq d)
.

The integers
p^{h}_{i,j}
are called the
intersection numbers
of
\mathcal{X}
.

For the rest of this section, let
{\mathcal{X}}=(X,\{R_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d})

denote a symmetric association scheme.
Define
k_{i}=p^{0}_{i,i}
for
0\leq i\leq d
.
By
[
3
, Lemma 2.1.1]
,

\displaystyle p^{h}_{0,j}

\displaystyle=p^{h}_{j,0}=\delta_{j,h}\qquad(0\leq h,j\leq d),

\displaystyle p^{0}_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\delta_{i,j}k_{j}\qquad(0\leq i,j\leq d).

Let
\mathbb{C}
denote the field of complex numbers.
For
0\leq i\leq d
define
{A}_{i}\in\text{\rm Mat}_{X}(\mathbb{C})
such that

\displaystyle({A}_{i})_{x,y}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1&\text{\rm if $(x,y)\in R_{i}$},\\
0&\text{\rm if $(x,y)\not\in R_{i}$}\end{cases}

\displaystyle(x,y\in X).

The matrix
A_{i}
is called the
associate matrix
corresponding to
R_{i}
.
We have
(i)
\sum_{i=0}^{d}{A}_{i}={J}
;
(ii)
{A}_{0}={I}
;
(iii)
{A}_{i}={A}_{i}^{t}\;(0\leq i\leq d)
;
(iv)
A_{i}{A}_{j}=\sum_{h=0}^{d}p^{h}_{i,j}{A}_{h}\;(0\leq i,j\leq d)
;
(v)
{A}_{i}{A}_{j}={A}_{j}{A}_{i}\;(0\leq i,j\leq d)
.
The matrices
\{{A}_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
form a basis of a commutative algebra
\mathcal{M}
,
called the
Bose-Mesner algebra
of
\mathcal{X}

[
3
, Section 2.2]
.
By
[
1
, p. 59, p. 64]
,
\mathcal{M}
has a second basis
\{{E}_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}

such that
(i)
\sum_{i=0}^{d}{E}_{i}={I}
;
(ii)
{E}_{0}=|X|^{-1}{J}
;
(iii)
{E}_{i}^{t}={E}_{i}=\overline{{E}}_{i}\;(0\leq i\leq d)
;
(iv)
{E}_{i}{E}_{j}=\delta_{i,j}{E}_{i}\;(0\leq i,j\leq d)
.
We refer to
\{{E}_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
as the
primitive idempotents
of
\mathcal{X}
.

We will be discussing the algebra
\text{\rm Mat}_{d+1}(\mathbb{C})
.
For matrices in this algebra we index
the rows and columns by
0,1,\ldots,d
.
Define matrices
P
,
Q\in\text{\rm Mat}_{d+1}(\mathbb{C})
such that

\displaystyle{A}_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{d}P_{i,j}{E}_{i}\qquad\qquad\qquad\;\;(0\leq j\leq d),

(1)

\displaystyle{E}_{j}

\displaystyle=|X|^{-1}\sum_{i=0}^{d}Q_{i,j}{A}_{i}\qquad\qquad(0\leq j\leq d).

(2)

Taking the complex conjugate in (
1
) and (
2
), we observe
that the entries of
P
and
Q
are all real numbers.
By
[
3
, Section 2.2]
,
PQ=QP=|X|I
.
The matrix
P
(resp.
Q
) is called the
first eigenmatrix
(resp.
second eigenmatrix
)

of
\mathcal{X}
with respect to the ordering
\{{E}_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
.
Since
\mathcal{M}
is closed under the entry-wise product
\circ
,
there exist scalars
q^{h}_{i,j}\in\mathbb{C}

(0\leq h,i,j\leq d)

such that

\displaystyle{E}_{i}\circ{E}_{j}

\displaystyle=|X|^{-1}\sum_{h=0}^{d}q^{h}_{i,j}{E}_{h}\qquad\qquad(0\leq i,j\leq d).

(3)

The scalars
q^{h}_{i,j}
are called the
Krein parameters
of
\mathcal{X}

with respect to the ordering
\{{E}_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
.
By
[
1
, Theorem 3.8]
the Krein parameters are nonnegative real numbers.
Define
m_{i}=q^{0}_{i,i}
for
0\leq i\leq d
.
By
[
3
, Lemma 2.3.1]
,

\displaystyle q^{h}_{0,j}

\displaystyle=q^{h}_{j,0}=\delta_{j,h}\qquad(0\leq h,j\leq d),

\displaystyle q^{0}_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\delta_{i,j}m_{j}\qquad(0\leq i,j\leq d).

By
[
3
, Lemma 2.2.1]
,

P_{0,j}=k_{j},\qquad\qquad Q_{0,j}=m_{j}\qquad\qquad(0\leq j\leq d).

(4)

By
[
1
, Theorem 3.6]
,

\displaystyle p^{h}_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{|X|}\sum_{r=0}^{d}\frac{1}{m_{r}^{2}}Q_{i,r}Q_{j,r}Q_{h,r}\qquad\qquad(0\leq h,i,j\leq d),

(5)

\displaystyle q^{h}_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{i}m_{j}}{|X|}\sum_{r=0}^{d}\frac{1}{k_{r}^{2}}P_{i,r}P_{j,r}P_{h,r}\qquad\qquad(0\leq h,i,j\leq d).

4
Formally self-dual and numerically self-dual association schemes

Let
\mathcal{X}
denote a symmetric
d
-class association scheme.
Fix an ordering
\{{E}_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
of the primitive idempotents of
\mathcal{X}
.
Let
q^{h}_{i,j}
denote the Krein parameters of
\mathcal{X}

with respect to the ordering
\{{E}_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
.
Let
P
(resp.
Q
) denote the first eigenmatrix (resp. second eigenmatrix) of
\mathcal{X}

with respect to the ordering
\{{E}_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
.
The association scheme
\mathcal{X}
is said to be
formally self-dual
(with respect to the ordering

\{{E}_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
) whenever
P=Q

[
3
, Section 2.3]
.
In this case we find by (
4
)–(
6
) that

\displaystyle p^{h}_{i,j}

\displaystyle=q^{h}_{i,j}\qquad\qquad(0\leq h,i,j\leq d).

(7)

This raises a natural question.
For a given ordering
\{{E}_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
of the primitive idempotents,
is it possible that (
7
) holds but
P\neq Q
?
This is possible as we will show.
In the next section we will demonstrate with an example of an association scheme and an ordering
of the primitive idempotents with respect to which the intersection numbers and the Krein parameters
are the same but the first eigenmatrix and the second eigenmatrix are different.
We will use the following term.

Definition 4.1

The association scheme
\mathcal{X}
is said to be
numerically self-dual

(with respect to the given ordering
\{{E}_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
of the primitive idempotents)
whenever
p^{h}_{i,j}=q^{h}_{i,j}
for
0\leq h,i,j\leq d
.

Motivated by our above comments, we now consider what happens to the
P
,
Q
and
the Krein parameters if we change the ordering of the primitive idempotents.
Let
\sigma
denote a permutation of
\{0,1,2,\ldots,d\}
that fixes
0
.
Then
\{{E}_{\sigma(i)}\}_{i=0}^{d}
is an ordering of the primitive idempotents of
\mathcal{X}
.
Below we describe the first and second eigenmatrices and the Krein parameters with respect to this ordering.
These matrices and parameters will be expressed in bold font.

Lemma 4.2

Let
\sigma
denote a permutation of
\{0,1,2,\ldots,d\}
that fixes
0
.
Let
{\sf P}
(resp.
{\sf Q}
) denote the first eigenmatrix (resp. second eigenmatrix) of
\mathcal{X}

with respect to the ordering
\{{E}_{\sigma(i)}\}_{i=0}^{d}
.
Then

\displaystyle{\sf P}_{i,j}

\displaystyle=P_{\sigma(i),j},

\displaystyle{\sf Q}_{i,j}

\displaystyle=Q_{i,\sigma(j)}

\displaystyle(0\leq i,j\leq d).

(8)

Let
T\in\text{\rm Mat}_{d+1}(\mathbb{C})
denote the permutation matrix coresponding to
\sigma
:

T_{i,j}=\delta_{\sigma(i),\,j}

(0\leq i,j\leq d)
.
Then

\displaystyle{\sf P}

\displaystyle=TP,

\displaystyle{\sf Q}=QT^{-1}.

(9)

Proof.

In (
1
), replace
i
with
\sigma(i)
to get the equation on the left in (
8
).
In (
2
), replace
j
with
\sigma(j)
to get
the equation on the right in (
8
).

\Box

Lemma 4.3

Let
\sigma
denote a permutation of
\{0,1,2,\ldots,d\}
that fixes
0
.
Let
{\sf q}^{h}_{i,j}
denote the Krein parameters of
\mathcal{X}

with respect to the ordering
\{{E}_{\sigma(i)}\}_{i=0}^{d}
.
Then

\displaystyle{\sf q}^{h}_{i,j}

\displaystyle=q^{\sigma(h)}_{\sigma(i),\;\sigma(j)}\qquad\qquad(0\leq h,i,j\leq d).

(10)

Proof.

In (
3
),
replace
h
,
i
,
j
with
\sigma(h)
,
\sigma(i)
,
\sigma(j)
, respectively.
This gives (
10
).

\Box

5
An example of a scheme

There is a type of association scheme called a group scheme
[
2
]
.
In this section, we discuss the group scheme attached to a certain abelian group
that we now define.
Consider the additive group
\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}=\{0,1\}
.
Fix an integer
m\geq 1
. We define an abelian group
G=G^{(m)}
by

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle=\mathbb{Z}_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{2}\qquad\text{\rm($m$ copies)}.

(11)

Note that
|G|=2^{m}
.
We view each element of
G
as a sequence
a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{m}
, where

a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
for
1\leq i\leq m
.
We now describe the group scheme attached to
G
.
In this description, it is convenient to put the elements of
G
in lexicographical order.
For example, if
m=2
then the order is
00<01<10<11
.
For
x\in G
define a relation
R_{x}\subseteq G\times G
by

\displaystyle R_{x}=\{(y,y+x)\,|\,y\in G\}.

Then
(G,\{R_{x}\}_{x\in G})
forms a
d
-class symmetric association scheme
with
d=|G|-1
,
called the group scheme for
G
; see
[
2
]
.
We denote this association scheme by
{\mathcal{X}}^{(m)}
.
Below we describe the structure of
{\mathcal{X}}^{(m)}
.

We first consider the case
m=1

Enhanced Magnetism in Heterostructures with Transition Metal Dichalcogenide Monolayers
Diem Thi-Xuan Dang, Ranjan Kumar Barik, Manh-Huong Phan, Lilia M. Woods

Abstract
Two-dimensional materials and their heterostructures have opened up new possibilities for magnetism at the nanoscale. In this study, we utilize first-principles simulations to investigate the structural, electronic, and magnetic properties of Fe / WSe 2/ ​ Pt \text{Fe}\text{/}\text{WSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2/}}}\text{Pt} systems containing pristine, defective, or doped WSe 2 \text{WSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} monolayers. The proximity effects of the ferromagnetic Fe layer are studied by considering defective and Vanadium-doped WSe 2 \text{WSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} monolayers. All heterostructures are found ferromagnetic, and the insertion of the transition metal dichalcogenide results in redistribution of spin orientation and an increased density of magnetic atoms due to the magnetized WSe 2 \text{WSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} . There is an increase in the overall total density of states at the Fermi level due to WSe 2 \text{WSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} , however, the transition metal dichalcogenide may lose its distinct semiconducting properties due to the stronger than van der Waals coupling. Spin resolved electronic structure properties are linked to larger spin Seebeck coefficients found in heterostructures with WSe 2 \text{WSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} monolayers.

Heterostructures (HSTs) composed of ferromagnetic (FM) and heavy metal (HM) films are typical systems employing spin-charge conversion mechanisms for spintronics applications
Dieny et al. 2020
. The quality of such devices is controlled by the atomic composition of the interface and its spin-dependent properties. In this context, HSTs with 2D layered materials have become of great interest
Sierra et al. 2021
. Chemically inert layered materials can improve the interface atomic sharpness and they provide new ways of property tuning. For example, semiconducting transition metal dichalcogenides (TMDCs) have strong spin orbit coupling (SOC) which allows the manipulation of spins via electrical means
Xiao et al. 2012
. On the other hand, their strong spin photon coupling enables the control of magnetism with optical methods
Phan et al. 2021
. The recent discovery of FM ordering at room temperature in
\text{WS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and
\text{WSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
monolayers doped by V atoms
Yun et al. 2020
;
Pham et al. 2020
;
Zhang et al. 2020
;
Ortiz Jimenez et al. 2021
has shown that TMDCs can be effective dilute 2D ferromagnets with robust magnetic states, which can also be light-tunable.

HSTs with semiconducting TMDC layers offer possibilities for new ways to change and control magnetic properties, spin transport and spin conversion phenomena through novel interfacial coupling mechanisms. For example, much enhanced magnetic exchange fields, valley splitting, and polarization effects tunable via optical methods have been observed in
\text{WSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
/
\text{CrI}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
HSTs
Zhong et al. 2017
;
Seyler et al. 2018
;
Zhong et al. 2020
. Several impressive experimental studies have shown that spin current generation via thermal gradients across FM/TMDC/HM structures is significantly altered when compared with FM/HM systems. Specifically, a large enhancement of the voltage difference due to the longitudinal spin Seebeck effect in
\text{Pt}\text{/}\text{Ni}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{81}}}\text{Fe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{19}}}
and Pt/YIG after inserting
\text{WS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(
\text{WSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
) layers has been reported
Dastgeer et al. 2019
;
Lee et al. 2020
;
Lee et al. 2020
;
Lee et al. 2021
. It was also found that such an increase is dependent on the number of layers in the heterostructure, degree of coverage by the TMDC layers, and quality of the interface. Others have examined spin-dependent transport in
\text{Fe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4/}}}\text{MoS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2/}}}\text{Fe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
and
\text{Co}\text{/}\text{Al}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3/}}}\text{MoS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
junctions
Dankert et al. 2017
;
Galbiati et al. 2018
. Spin valves based on spin filtering in
\text{WS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2/}}}\text{Co}
and
\text{NiFe}\text{/}\text{MoS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2/}}}\text{NiFe}
have also been reported
Wang et al. 2015
;
Zatko et al. 2019
. A gate-tunable magnetic order in
\text{WSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
by changing the occupation of the Se vacancy states are successfully demonstrated
Nguyen et al. 2021
and computational work investigates the origin of ferromagnetism in complex configurations of Se vacancies in V-doped
\text{WSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
monolayers
Yun et al. 2022
. The generated Se vacancies play a key role in the structural change of
\text{PdSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
because of the laser-induced phase transformation of layered
\text{PdSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
to metallic phase
\text{PdSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2\textendash x}}}

Shautsova et al. 2019
. Ozyilmaz
et. al.
suggested that the spin Hall effect in graphene/TMDC heterostructures may also originate from chalcogenide vacancies
Avsar et al. 2014
.

In addition to these experimental demonstrations, the theoretical understanding of the interface properties and coupling mechanisms of FM/TMDC/HM systems must also be advanced. For example, the changes in the band structure of individual FM, TMDC, and HM layers calculated from first principles can help one understand the overall behavior of the entire HST. Computed charge transfer, bonding, and interfacial hybridization are also necessary in order to highlight magnetic proximity effects unique to FM/TMDC/HM HSTs. Additionally, first principles simulations are necessary to reveal spin selection transfer mechanisms across doped and defective TMDCs.

In this paper, we present first principles simulations of
\text{Fe}\text{/}\text{WSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2/}}}\text{Pt}
HSTs to elucidate their basic electronic and magnetic properties for the first time. We consider several cases in which the TMDC is a pristine
\text{WSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, a monolayer with V doping and a monolayer with various types of vacancies. The interplay between the proximity magnetism from the Fe substrate, the charge transfer and orbital hybdrization across the
\text{WSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
monolayer and the large SOC effects from the Pt layer leads to a complex picture of interface magnetism. The calculated band structure, as well as the Fermi surface for each component of the considered HSTs, are instrumental for the detailed microscopic analysis of the electronic and spin-dependent behavior across the interface.

Further insight into the microscopic picture of such heterostructures can be gained by considering their energy band structures. Since the different layers are incommensurate, the constructed supercell contains several unit cells from each component. This situation also occurs in the case of monolayer
\text{WSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
with impurities or V dopants, for which the supercell contains several unit cells. Because of the shrunk Brillouin zone (BZ) appearing in materials containing multiple unit cells, artificially folded bands are created. Thus, we are faced with the problem of band untangling on the primitive BZs for each layer to have a complete analysis of the electronic structure of the various HSTs. As representative examples, we consider Fe/Pt,
\text{Fe}\text{/}\text{WSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2/}}}\text{Pt}
and
\text{Fe}\text{/}\text{W}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.9}}}\text{V}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.1}}}\text{Se}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2/}}}\text{Pt}
to compare how the unpolarized perfect
\text{WSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and FM V-doped TMDC layer affect the band structure of the reference Fe/Pt.

Our first principles simulations show that
\text{Fe}\text{/}\text{WSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2/}}}\text{Pt}
HSTs exhibit complex electronic and magnetic properties. The pristine
\text{WSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, which is non magnetic, acquires magnetic moments on its atoms due to its proximity to the FM substrate of Fe. This distribution can be locally disturbed when defects or dopants are present. The chemically inert
\text{WSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
weakens the bond between Fe and Pt, but significant charge transfer occurs through the monolayer in the HST. This rather stronger than vdW coupling may be seen in the DOS and band structure results showing the detailed orbital hybridization balance. The insertion of the TMDC monolayer also leads to an increased DOS at the Fermi level, which is about
(23\%)
higher than DOS of the reference HST. This is interesting to compare with
\text{YIG}\text{/}\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60/}}}\text{Pt}
: a related HST in which the
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
layer is also a semiconductor which bonds relatively strongly to YIG and Pt
Kalappattil et al. 2020
. In this case, the six-fold DOS increase at the Fermi level is attributed to a much reduced conductivity mismatch, which explained the giant longitudinal spin Seebeck in
\text{YIG}\text{/}\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60/}}}\text{Pt}
when compared with
YIG
/
Pt

Kalappattil et al. 2020
. DOS increase in
Fe
/
Pt
with TMDC monolayers is also present, which can be a factor responsible for the longitudinal spin Seebeck enhancement as reported in

;

;

;

.

The unfolded band structures for the
\text{Fe}\text{/}\text{WSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2/}}}\text{Pt}
HSTs are especially useful in understanding to what extent each component preserves its electronic properties. Of special interest is the
\text{WSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and what happens to its semiconducting nature after its insertion between the Fe and Pt substrates. Our calculations show that much of the “identity” of the TMDC monolayer is lost as there are numerous bands with various intensities crossing the Fermi level. The strong hybridization with orbitals from the substrates alters the character of the energy bands of the
\text{WSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, which shows that its semiconducting behavior has changed. The presence of defects or dopants do not alter significantly this type of overall behavior. In light of this situation, potential experiments accessing the energy gap of the TMDC from the HST via optical means may need to be reevaluated
Phan et al. 2021
.

The spin resolved atomic distribution and the FS calculations are also instrumental in understanding how localized spin is transferred across the interface of
\text{Fe}\text{/}\text{WSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2/}}}\text{Pt}
HSTs. Due to the proximity of the Fe substrate,
\text{WSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
becomes ferromagnetically polarized. The Pt substrate also has magnetization
m_{3}\neq 0
. Furthermore, the overall magnetization of
\text{Fe}\text{/}\text{WSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2/}}}\text{Pt}
is enhanced when comparing with the reference
Fe
/
Pt
HST. The spin resolved FSs show large overlapping regions in
\mathbf{k_{i}}
space for both spin carriers, and for the
\text{WSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
there is little difference between the areas corresponding to the majority or minority carriers. As a result, we see that there is an increased density of magnetic atoms giving rise to a higher
M
, which effectively enhances the spin mixing and overall spin transfer in the HST as shown theoretically in
Jia et al. 2011
.

Our first principles simulations of
\text{Fe}\text{/}\text{WSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2/}}}\text{Pt}
with pristine, defective or V-doped TMDC monolayer reveal complex electronic and magnetic properties of the HSTs and their individual components. Although the ferromagnetism in
\text{WSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
can be tuned via doping and/or defects, we find that this effect becomes secondary in the overall behavior of the HST. The semiconducting nature of the TMDC becomes diluted when the monolayer is between the Fe and Pt substrates. The total DOS increase at the Fermi level, larger magnetization, and importantly much enhanced spin density at the interface with overlapping majority and minority regions in the FS of
\text{Fe}\text{/}\text{WSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2/}}}\text{Pt}

The combined effect of all of these factors results in larger spin Seebeck coefficients in HSTs with TMDC layers, which can be useful in interpreting recent experiments reporting enhanced magnetization and spin resolved transport properties

Computational Methods:
The first principles simulations are performed with the DFT Vienna ab initio Simulations Package (VASP), which is based on projector-augmented wave (PAW) pseudopotentials and periodic boundary conditions
Kresse and Furthmüller 1996
;
Kresse and Furthmüller 1996
. The exchange-correlation effects are considered using the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) generalized gradient approximation with an included spin-orbit coupling (SOC)
Perdew et al. 1996
. The vdW interaction is included via the vdW-D3 (BJ) dispersion correction
Grimme et al. 2011
. The electronic shell structure is taken to have 8 valence electrons of Fe (3
\text{d}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{6}}
4
\text{s}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{2}}
), 6 valence electrons of W (6
\text{s}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{2}}
5
\text{d}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{4}}
), 6 valence electrons of Se (4
\text{s}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{2}}
4
\text{p}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{4}}
) and 10 valence electrons of Pt (5
\text{d}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{9}}
6
\text{s}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{1}}
). The kinetic energy cutoff is
450
eV. In the construction of the HST supercells, we use the rectangular unit cell of
\text{WSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

(a=3.32\text{ \AA},b=5.75\text{ \AA})
with a
6\times 6
Fe supercell (108 Fe atoms), a
3\sqrt{3}\times 5

\text{WSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
supercell (
30
W and
60
Se atoms), a
3\sqrt{2}\times 3\sqrt{2}
Pt supercell (
108
Pt atoms). To eliminate the interactions between periodic images, a vacuum layer of
15

Å
is taken between the HST layers for all calculations.
The relaxation criteria of all systems is performed by allowing all cell parameters to change with
10^{-5}
eV energy and
0.01
eV/
Å
force relaxation criteria. The intergration over the Brillouin zone is performed at a single
\Gamma
point for the optimization calculation. The DOS, charge density differences and Bader charges calculations are accomplished with
2\times 2\times 1

\Gamma
-centered Monkhorst-Pack k-point mesh. These calculations are performed with employing the Gaussian smearing with 0.1 eV width. To account for the strongly correlated electronic systems, we employed the
GGA+U
method within Dudarev’s approach with an on-site Coulomb correction in the localized d orbitals of the V atoms. The parameter value of
U_{eff}=U-J=3.0
eV is selected, which has been shown to describe the ground-state electronic and magnetic properties of the system accurately
Duong et al. 2019
;
Yun et al. 2020
. For the Fe, W, Se and Pt,
U_{eff}=0
eV. The visualization of all systems is attained in VESTA
Momma and Izumi 2011
.

Given the zone folding problem that occurs in simulations involving supercells, the disentanglement of the various band structures is performed by adapting the vaspkit code
Wang et al. 2021
. Within this code, one creates
\mathbf{k_{i}}
vectors along the high symmetry directions in the primitive Brillouin zone and then translates them into the supercell reciprocal space. Performing VASP calculation using these
\mathbf{k_{i}}
vectors yields the plane wave coefficients and eigenvalues of each state necessary to determine spectral weights for the projection of the supercell eigenstates on the primitive cell eigenstates.

The FS unfolding is achieved using the bands4vasp package
Dirnberger et al. 2021
, in which the energy eigenvalues for every
\mathbf{k_{i}}
primitive cell vector are assigned to a particular energy band by considering similarities in the orbital symmetry of the eigenstates. Then, a band center of mass is defined by averaging the energy values with weights given by the corresponding
P_{\mathbf{K}m}(\mathbf{k_{i}})
character corresponding to the projection of the supercell eigenstates
\left|\mathbf{K}m\right>
on the primitive cell eigenstate
\left|\mathbf{k}n\right>
. Finally, the intersection of every band with the Fermi level is found by interpolation. Because the band structure and FS unfolding are computationally expensive, we consider the supercells consisting of
2\times 6
Fe unit cells (36 atoms),
\sqrt{3}\times 5
unit cells for the monolayer (30 atoms), and
\sqrt{2}\times 3\sqrt{2}
Pt unit cells (36 atoms) are constructed. The optimization calculations of these system are performed with
6\times 2\times 1

\Gamma
-centered Monkhorst-Pack k-point mesh.

The transport properties of the HSTs are calculated with the BoltzTraP2 code
Madsen et al. 2018
with a large k-mesh of 112 000 points for more accurate results. The code utilizes linear response theory of the Boltzmann equation within a constant relaxation time approximation. In general, this is a good approximation as long as the energy changes are at a smaller scale as compared to the energy changes in the density of states, which is typically the case for metals and many semiconductors.

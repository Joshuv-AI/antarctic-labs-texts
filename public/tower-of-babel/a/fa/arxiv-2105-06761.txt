Benchmarking near-term quantum devices with the Variational Quantum Eigensolver and the Lipkin-Meshkov-Glick model
Kenneth Robbins, Peter J. Love

Abstract
The Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE) is a promising algorithm for Noisy Intermediate Scale Quantum (NISQ) computation. Verification and validation of NISQ algorithms’ performance on NISQ devices is an important task. We consider the exactly-diagonalizable Lipkin-Meshkov-Glick (LMG) model as a candidate for benchmarking NISQ computers. We use the Bethe ansatz to construct eigenstates of the trigonometric LMG model using quantum circuits inspired by the LMG’s underlying algebraic structure. We construct circuits with depth 𝒪 ⁡ ( N ) \mathcal{O}(N) and 𝒪 ⁡ ( log 2 ⁡ N ) \mathcal{O}(\log_{2}N) that can prepare any trigonometric LMG eigenstate of N N particles. The number of gates required for both circuits is 𝒪 ⁡ ( N ) \mathcal{O}(N) . The energies of the eigenstates can then be measured and compared to the exactly-known answers.

I
Introduction

Quantum simulation is a leading application of quantum computation. Quantum Phase Estimation (QPE) can find Hamiltonian eigenvalues but is challenging to implement in the Noisy Intermediate Scale Quantum (NISQ) era of computation due to a high resource cost
Preskill 2018
;
O’Malley et al. 2016
;
Bharti et al. 2021
. The Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE), a quantum-classical hybrid algorithm which requires fewer resources, is more suited to current NISQ computers which as yet lack fault-tolerance and fully-coherent evolution
Peruzzo et al. 2014
;
Cerezo et al. 2020
.

Consider an ansatz state
\left|\psi\left(\Theta_{M}\right)\right\rangle
and a Hamiltonian
H=\sum_{k}H_{k}
where
\Theta_{M}\equiv\{\theta_{1}...\theta_{M}\}
is a set of ansatz parameters. VQE uses a quantum processing unit to estimate the expectation value
\left\langle\psi\left(\Theta_{M}\right)\right|H_{k}\left|\psi\left(\Theta_{M}\right)\right\rangle
for all
k
and outputs this information to a classical machine. Said classical machine then sums these expectation values to estimate
\left\langle\psi\left(\Theta_{M}\right)\right|H\left|\psi\left(\Theta_{M}\right)\right\rangle
and alters the ansatz parameters
\Theta_{M}
according to some optimization algorithm to minimize the estimated value. This process is iterated until a minimum is obtained: a variational estimate of the ground-state energy
Peruzzo et al. 2014
.

Problems with implementing VQE include overcoming noisy devices, barren plateaus in the phase space and the choice of a suitable ansatz
Cerezo et al. 2020
. This paper concerns itself, however, with providing a way to benchmark an individual quantum machine’s performance using exactly-solvable models and VQE. This is done by providing both an exactly-solvable model and a suitable ansatz-preparation circuit for all relevant cases of the model. Further, we discuss ways to obtain the ansatz parameters
\Theta_{M}
which will prepare given LMG eigenstates and give examples of this.

In the case of an exactly-solvable Hamiltonian we can find the eigenvalues and eigenstates analytically and therefore these models are useful as benchmarks for VQE because we can compare VQE outputs to exact calculations. This requires the construction of a circuit that prepares the exact eigenstates. In the present paper we investigate the Lipkin-Meshkov-Glick (LMG) model which describes two nuclear shells
Lipkin et al. 1965
;
Pan and Draayer 1999
;
Ortiz et al. 2005
;
Lerma H. and Dukelsky 2013
;
Cervia et al. 2020
. The Bethe ansatz suggests a circuit structure valid for any number of qubits; constructing these circuits is the main topic of the present paper.

Previous work discussed quantum simulation of exactly-solvable Hamiltonians such as the LMG for applications such as benchmarking or as a testbed for exotic matter detection
Cerezo et al. 2020
;
Dallaire-Demers et al. 2020
;
Dallaire-Demers and Wilhelm 2016a
;
Dallaire-Demers and Wilhelm 2016b
. In particular, the Bethe ansatz has been considered to design state preparation circuits of polynomial depth for correlated fermionic states and the
XXZ
spin chain
Dyke et al. 2021
;
Dallaire-Demers et al. 2018
. Exact models have also been theoretically explored for non-variational quantum simulation
Cervera-Lierta 2018
;
Verstraete et al. 2009
;
Schmoll and Orús 2017
.

In Section
II
we introduce the LMG model and explore its underlying Gaudin algebra structure. In Section
III
we apply that structure to construct circuits that prepare LMG eigenstates. In Section
IV
we provide specific examples of how to connect the exact eigenstates to our proposed circuits for low numbers of particles. We discuss our findings in Section
V
.

II
The Lipkin-Meshkov-Glick (LMG) Model

This section details the representations and algebraic qualities of the LMG model so that we may use them later to construct an LMG eigenstate-preparation circuit. To that end, we focus on the bosonic representation of the model and its relationship to generalized Gaudin-Richardson models.

II.1
The Hamiltonian and Transformations

The LMG model describes a collection of
N
fermions and was introduced by S. Fallieros to study the nuclear phenomenon of giant monopole resonance
Lipkin et al. 1965
;
Fallieros 1959
. In 1965 Lipkin, Meshkov and Glick proposed using the model to benchmark “techniques and formalisms for treating many-body systems.” In the LMG model each fermion can be in one of two
N
-fold degenerate energy levels labeled by quantum number
\sigma=\pm 1
; the original physical example given is two total-angular-momentum coupling shells of a nucleus. Another quantum number
1\leq k\leq N
labels fermionic modes
Lipkin et al. 1965
. The LMG model is an example of a pairing model. Pairing models such as the LMG and the Bardeen-Cooper-Schrieffer (BCS) have long been relevant in nuclear and condensed matter physics, and as such they are of interest as targets for quantum simulation
Wu et al. 2002
.

The LMG Hamiltonian is given by

H=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k,\sigma}\sigma f^{\dagger}_{k\sigma}f_{k\sigma}+\frac{V}{2N}\sum_{k,k^{\prime},\sigma}f^{\dagger}_{k\sigma}f^{\dagger}_{k^{\prime}\sigma}f_{k^{\prime}-\sigma}f_{k-\sigma}\\
+\frac{W}{2N}\sum_{k,k^{\prime},\sigma}f^{\dagger}_{k\sigma}f^{\dagger}_{k^{\prime}-\sigma}f_{k^{\prime}\sigma}f_{k-\sigma}\\

(1)

where fermionic operator
f_{k\sigma}^{\dagger}
creates a fermion in the
k\sigma
state,
f_{k\sigma}
annihilates a particle in the state defined by mode label
k
and energy level
\sigma
.
V,W\in\mathbb{R}
are interaction strengths. The energy gap between the levels is set to unity without loss of generality by rescaling
V
and
W
. The LMG model can be rewritten in terms of angular momentum operators
Ortiz et al. 2005
:

J_{\pm}\equiv\sum_{k=1}^{N}f^{\dagger}_{k\pm}f_{k\mp}\text{ ,}\quad J_{z}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sum_{\sigma=\pm}\sigma f^{\dagger}_{k\sigma}c_{k\sigma}.

(2)

The total angular momentum quantum number is fixed as
j=N/2
. In terms of these operators, the LMG Hamiltonian is:

H=J_{z}+\frac{V}{2N}(J_{+}J_{+}+J_{-}J_{-})+\frac{W}{2N}(J_{+}J_{-}+J_{-}J_{+}).

(3)

The angular momentum operators can be written in terms of bosonic operators via the Jordan-Schwinger mapping
Ortiz et al. 2005
;
Lerma H. and Dukelsky 2013
;
Schwinger 1952
.

J_{+}\equiv b^{\dagger}a\quad J_{-}\equiv a^{\dagger}b\quad J_{z}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\left(n_{b}-n_{a}\right)

(4)

where operators
a^{({\dagger})},b^{({\dagger})}
annihilate (create) bosons in modes
a
or
b
, respectively and
n_{b}\equiv b^{\dagger}b
and
n_{a}\equiv a^{\dagger}a
are their number operators. Mode
a
corresponds to particles in the lower energy level in the fermionic representation, mode
b
to those in the higher and the total number of quanta
N_{\text{fermion}}=N_{\text{boson}}\equiv N
. We will use the following representation of the LMG Hamiltonian:

H=\frac{n_{b}-n_{a}}{2}+\frac{V}{2N}\left(b^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}aa+a^{\dagger}a^{\dagger}bb\right)\\
+\frac{W}{N}\left(\frac{n_{a}+n_{b}}{2}+n_{a}n_{b}\right).

(5)

Note that if
V=0
the system is diagonal in the bosonic Fock basis.

II.2
Algebraic Structure

The LMG model is a
2
-level
XXZ
Gaudin model
Ortiz et al. 2005
;
Lerma H. and Dukelsky 2013
;
Pan and Draayer 1999
. This means that the Hamiltonian (
5
) can be written in terms of the constants of motion of an
XXZ
Gaudin algebra
Ortiz et al. 2005
;
Lerma H. and Dukelsky 2013
;
Pan and Draayer 1999
. We now define the terms used in the following form for the Gaudin Hamiltonian
Ortiz et al. 2005
:

\begin{multlined}H=\sum_{\lambda}\epsilon_{\lambda}K^{z}_{\lambda}+\sum_{\lambda,\tau,\lambda\neq\tau}\left(\epsilon_{\lambda}-\epsilon_{\tau}\right)\times{}\\
\Big(X_{\lambda\tau}\left(K^{+}_{\lambda}K^{-}_{\tau}+K^{-}_{\lambda}K^{+}_{\tau}\right)-2Z_{\lambda\tau}K^{z}_{\lambda}K^{z}_{\tau}\Big).\\
\end{multlined}

(6)

Indices
\lambda,\tau
run from
1
to
2
and
\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{1}
is the energy gap between the two levels given in the fermionic description of the LMG Hamiltonian, which we set to unity without loss of generality. Operators
K^{\pm}_{\lambda}
and
K^{z}_{\lambda}
are generators of
su(1,1)\oplus su(1,1)
and can be expressed in terms of bosonic operators:

K^{+}_{1}=\frac{1}{2}a^{\dagger}a^{\dagger}\quad\quad K^{+}_{2}=\frac{1}{2}b^{\dagger}b^{\dagger},

(7a)

K^{-}_{1}=\frac{1}{2}aa\quad\quad K^{-}_{2}=\frac{1}{2}bb,

(7b)

K^{z}_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\left(n_{a}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\quad\quad K^{z}_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(n_{b}+\frac{1}{2}\right)

(7c)

obeying the commutators

\left[K_{\lambda}^{+},K_{\tau}^{-}\right]=-2\delta_{\lambda\tau}K^{z}_{\tau}\quad\quad\left[K^{z}_{\lambda},K^{\pm}_{\tau}\right]=\pm K^{\pm}_{\tau}.

(8)

With these we can construct the Gaudin operators
S^{\pm}_{m}
and
S^{z}_{m}

Ortiz et al. 2005
;
Lerma H. and Dukelsky 2013
:

S^{+}_{m}=X_{m1}K^{+}_{1}+X_{m2}K^{+}_{2}=\left(X_{m1}a^{\dagger}a^{\dagger}+X_{m2}b^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}\right)/2

(9a)

S^{-}_{m}=-X_{m1}K^{-}_{1}-X_{m2}K^{-}_{2}=-\left(X_{m1}aa+X_{m2}bb\right)/2

(9b)

S^{z}_{m}=-\frac{1}{2}-Z_{m1}K^{z}_{1}-Z_{m2}K^{z}_{2}\\
=-\frac{1}{2}\left(1+Z_{m1}\left(a^{\dagger}a+\frac{1}{2}\right)+Z_{m2}\left(b^{\dagger}b+\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)\\

(9c)

where
X_{m\ell}=-X_{\ell m}
and
Z_{m\ell}=-Z_{\ell m}
are matrices with indices
m,\ell
running from
1
to integer
M
, the number of particle pairs. It is these antisymmetric matrices that give the
XXZ
Gaudin algebra its name. These operators obey the commutation relations that define the
XXZ
Gaudin algebra:

\left[S^{\pm}_{m},S^{\pm}_{\ell}\right]=0

(10a)

\left[S^{-}_{m},S^{+}_{\ell}\right]=\begin{cases}-2X_{m\ell}\left(S^{z}_{m}-S^{z}_{\ell}\right)&m\neq\ell\\
2f_{m}\frac{\partial S^{z}_{m}}{\partial E_{m}}&m=\ell\end{cases}

(10b)

\left[S^{z}_{m},S^{\pm}_{\ell}\right]=\begin{cases}\pm\left(X_{m\ell}S^{\pm}_{m}-Z_{m\ell}S^{\pm}_{\ell}\right)&m\neq\ell\\
\mp f_{m}\frac{\partial S^{\pm}_{m}}{\partial E_{m}}&m=\ell\end{cases}

(10c)

where
f_{m}
can be written as one holomorphic function divided by another
Ortiz et al. 2005
. We use Equations (
7
-
10
) to rewrite Equation (
6
) as

H=\frac{\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{1}}{2}\left(n_{b}-n_{a}\right)+\frac{X_{12}}{2}\left(a^{\dagger}a^{\dagger}bb+b^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}aa\right)\\
-Z_{12}\left(n_{a}n_{b}+\frac{n_{b}+n_{a}}{2}\right)\\

(11)

up to a constant additive term of
(1-Z_{12})/4
. Comparing this to the LMG Hamiltonian given in Equation (
5
) we see that
X_{12}=V/N
and
Z_{12}=-W/N
. This Gaudin framework enables us construct the eigenstates in Section
III
.

III
Eigenstate Generating Operator

In the context of the LMG Hamiltonian, the Bethe ansatz outputs two relevant equations: one that can characterize all LMG eigenstates in terms of a single operator and another that characterizes all of the eigenvalues. We define the former as an Eigenstate Generating Operator (EGO).

III.1
Fock Space Operator

The LMG Hamiltonian (
5
) is exactly diagonalizable via the Bethe ansatz
Lipkin et al. 1965
;
Ortiz et al. 2005
;
Pan and Draayer 1999
. We follow Reference
Ortiz et al. 2005
and define the EGO as

\prod^{M}_{\ell=1}\left(\frac{(a^{\dagger})^{2}}{E_{\ell}+\eta}+\frac{(b^{\dagger})^{2}}{E_{\ell}-\eta}\right).

(12)

Application of the EGO to a fiducial state
\left|\nu_{a},\nu_{b}\right\rangle
results in an (unnormalized) LMG eigenstate. Note that the EGO (
12
) has
M+1
terms after applying the product because
a^{\dagger}
and
b^{\dagger}
commute. As all eigenstates of the LMG can be written in terms of the EGO, any LMG eigenstate
\left|M,\nu_{a},\nu_{b};j\right\rangle
can be written as

\left|M,\nu_{a},\nu_{b};j\right\rangle=\sum_{k=0}^{M}c_{k}\left|N-\nu_{b}-2k,2k+\nu_{b}\right\rangle

(13)

where
c_{k}
are normalized coefficients unique to the
j
th
eigenstate that can be efficiently classically generated with a given
M,\nu_{a},\nu_{b}
. The size of the support for LMG is therefore linear in
M
, which suggests that its eigenstates can be generated with generalized
\left|W\right\rangle
preparation circuits
Cruz et al. 2019
.

The variables
\nu_{a},\nu_{b}
are restricted to be
0
or
1
and represent unpaired particles while
M
is the number of particle pairs. This restriction on
\nu_{a},\nu_{b}
allows for four fiducial states:
\left|0,0\right\rangle,\left|0,1\right\rangle,\left|1,0\right\rangle
and
\left|1,1\right\rangle
. The integer
M
is also the requisite number of iterations of the factor
G\left(E_{\ell}\right)
, and is set by
Lerma H. and Dukelsky 2013
;
Ortiz et al. 2005

N=2M+\nu_{a}+\nu_{b}.

(14)

The variables
E_{\ell}
are spectral parameters, also known as pair energies or pairons
Lerma H. and Dukelsky 2013
. The spectral parameters are obtained by solving the Bethe ansatz equation(s)
Ortiz et al. 2005
;
Lerma H. and Dukelsky 2013
:

1-\frac{\eta}{N\left(E_{\ell}^{2}-\eta^{2}\right)}\bigg[gN\left(\nu_{a}-\nu_{b}\right)\left(1+sE_{\ell}^{2}\right)\\
+2VE_{\ell}\left(1+\nu_{a}+\nu_{b}\right)\bigg]+2g\sum^{M}_{n\neq l=1}\frac{1+sE_{\ell}E_{n}}{E_{\ell}-E_{n}}=0.

(15)

The variables
g
and
\eta
are analytic functions of interaction strengths
V
and
W
parameter
Pan and Draayer 1999
;
Ortiz et al. 2005
:

g=\sqrt{\frac{V^{2}-W^{2}}{sN^{2}}},

(16a)

\eta=-\sqrt{\frac{V+W}{s\left(V-W\right)}},

(16b)

where
s
is a sign parameter

s\equiv\begin{cases}+1&\text{if $V^{2}>W^{2}$}\\
0&\text{if $V^{2}=W^{2}$}\\
-1&\text{if $V^{2}<W^{2}$}\\
\end{cases}.

(17)

As a guide for circuit design, the EGO also illustrates the
\mathcal{O}(N/2)
vector support and suggests that a linear-gate-count circuit should exist. Both of these qualities make the LMG model suitable for benchmarking in NISQ devices because it can then be simulated with low resources in both gate- and qubit-counts.

For a given
M,\nu_{a},\nu_{b}
there are
M+1
distinct solutions for the spectral parameters. Substituting these into the EGO then results in
M+1
unnormalized eigenstates which are themselves superpositions of up to
M+1
Fock basis states. To generate all the eigenstates for a given particle number
N
, one must solve the Bethe equations for all possible combinations of
M,\nu_{a},\nu_{b}
and apply all distinct solution sets for the spectral parameters to the EGO, of which there should be a total of
N+1
.

Given an output of the EGO as a function of the spectral parameters with
M
applications of the EGO factor, it is possible to find the normalized
(M+1)
th
-level output vector in terms of the spectral parameters as well. We do so for the case of
\nu_{a}=\nu_{b}=\nu
and
W=0\neq V
. Let
\left|\text{EGO}_{M,\nu}\right\rangle
be the (unnormalized)
M
-level output vector from fiducial state
\left|\nu,\nu\right\rangle
:

\left|\text{EGO}_{M,\nu}\right\rangle\equiv\sum_{j=0}^{M}d_{j}\left|2M+\nu-2j,\nu+2j\right\rangle.

(18)

For cases where
\nu_{a}\neq\nu_{b}
we will use
\left|\text{EGO}_{M,\nu_{a},\nu_{b}}\right\rangle
. After applying the EGO factor to
\left|\text{EGO}_{M,\nu}\right\rangle
one more time and normalizing we have:

\left|M+1,\nu,\nu;k\right\rangle=\gamma^{-1/2}\sum_{j=0}^{M}d_{j}\times\\
\Bigg(\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{2}\sqrt{w_{ji}}\left|w_{j2},x_{j0}\right\rangle}{E_{M+1}-1}\\
+\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{2}\sqrt{x_{ji}}\left|w_{j0},x_{j2}\right\rangle}{E_{M+1}+1}\Bigg)

(19)

where

w_{ji}\equiv 2M+\nu-2j+i\quad\quad x_{ji}\equiv\nu+2j+i

(20)

and
\gamma
is a normalization factor

\gamma\equiv\sum_{m=0}^{M}d_{m}^{2}\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{2}w_{mi}}{\left(E_{M+1}-1\right)^{2}}+\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{2}x_{mi}}{\left(E_{M+1}+1\right)^{2}}\\
+2\sum_{n=0}^{M-1}d_{n}d_{n+1}\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{2}\sqrt{w_{n,i-2}x_{ni}}}{E_{M+1}^{2}-1}.

(21)

Therefore given an eigenstate
\left|M,\nu,\nu;j\right\rangle
or an unnormalized
\left|\text{EGO}_{M,\nu}\right\rangle
, both of which are functions of
\{E_{1}...E_{M}\}
, it is possible to calculate the form of
\left|M+1,\nu,\nu,k\right\rangle
as a function of
\{E_{1}...E_{M+1}\}
.

The trigonometric regime of the LMG defined by
V^{2}>W^{2}
guarantees real spectral parameters
Lerma H. and Dukelsky 2013
which, in turn, guarantees eigenstates with real coefficients. Conversely, in the hyperbolic regime defined by
W^{2}>V^{2}
for some values of
N,V,W
complex coefficients can occur. The circuits proposed within this paper assume the trigonometric case but are also valid in the hyperbolic if
E_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}
for all
1\leq\ell\leq M
.

The Bethe ansatz also allows us to compute the eigenvalues
\omega
in terms of the spectral parameters
E_{\ell}

Ortiz et al. 2005
;
Lerma H. and Dukelsky 2013
:

\omega=\frac{W\left(\nu_{a}+\nu_{b}+2\nu_{a}\nu_{b}\right)+N\left(\nu_{b}-\nu_{a}\right)}{2N}\\
-\frac{\eta}{N}\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}\frac{gN\left(1+\nu_{a}+\nu_{b}\right)\left(1+sE_{\ell}^{2}\right)-2V\left(\nu_{b}-\nu_{a}\right)E_{\ell}}{E_{\ell}^{2}-\eta^{2}}.\\

(22)

Solving the Bethe Equations (
15
) simplifies in special cases. If one sets
W=0\neq V
and
N
is even (where
\nu_{a}=\nu_{b}\equiv\nu
), then
g\rightarrow V/N,\eta\rightarrow-1,s\rightarrow 1
. Thus the Bethe equations simplify in that case:

1+\frac{2\left(1+2\nu\right)VE_{\ell}}{N\left(E_{\ell}^{2}-1\right)}+2\frac{V}{N}\sum_{n\neq\ell=1}^{M}\frac{1+E_{\ell}E_{n}}{E_{\ell}-E_{n}}=0.

(23)

For
M=1
and
2
we can find analytical solutions for the Bethe equations. For
M=1
the simplified Bethe equations become

1+V\frac{1+2\nu}{\left(1+\nu\right)\left(E_{1}^{2}-1\right)}E_{1}=0\\
=E_{1}^{2}+V\frac{1+2\nu}{1+\nu}E_{1}-1.\\

(24)

This is a quadratic equation with respect to
E_{1}
. The same is found for the full
M=1
Bethe equation(s) but with differing coefficients. We may solve Equation (
24
) to yield

E_{1}=-\frac{V\left(1+2\nu\right)\pm\sqrt{V^{2}\left(1+2\nu\right)^{2}+N^{2}}}{N}.

(25)

The simplified
M=2
Bethe equations are

1+V\frac{\left(1+2\nu\right)E_{1}}{\left(2+\nu\right)\left(E_{1}^{2}-1\right)}+V\frac{1+E_{1}E_{2}}{\left(2+\nu\right)\left(E_{1}-E_{2}\right)}=0,

(26a)

1+V\frac{\left(1+2\nu\right)E_{2}}{\left(2+\nu\right)\left(E_{2}^{2}-1\right)}+V\frac{1+E_{1}E_{2}}{\left(2+\nu\right)\left(E_{2}-E_{1}\right)}=0.

(26b)

Multiplying through by the denominators gives quartic polynomials; the same is found for the full
M=2
Bethe equations. Two sets of solutions are

E_{\ell}=-\frac{V(1+2\nu)\pm(-1)^{\ell}\sqrt{4(1+\nu)^{2}+V^{2}(1+2\nu)^{2}}}{2(1+\nu)}.

(27)

Beyond
M=2
, the addition of more terms with
E_{\ell}-E_{n}
in their denominators leads to an increase in polynomial degree of the Bethe equations. For a given nonzero
M>1
, the degree of the Bethe equations is
M+2
. Therefore the Bethe equations for
M\geq 3
are at least quintic in degree and lack analytical radical solutions in the general case
Abel 1824
. As such we assume for the rest of the paper that all solutions for the Bethe equations beyond
M=2
are obtained numerically. If necessary, the LMG Hamiltonian (
3
) can be written as an
(N+1)\times(N+1)
matrix and diagonalized, which gives the problem a complexity of
\mathcal{O}(N^{3})
.

III.2
Eigenstate Preparation Circuits

The
\left|W_{M}\right\rangle
state is defined as

\left|W_{M}\right\rangle\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{M}X_{j}\right)\left|0\right\rangle^{\otimes M}=\sum_{j=0}^{M-1}\frac{\left|2^{j}\right\rangle_{M}}{\sqrt{M}}

(28)

where
X_{j}
is the Pauli-
X
gate acting on qubit
j

Dür et al. 2000
. We also introduce the notation
\left|n\right\rangle_{m}
for a computational state representing integer
n
as a bitstring of length
m
. The
\left|W_{M}\right\rangle
states have support linear in
M
and uniform real amplitudes. Previous work has found
\left|W_{M}\right\rangle
preparation circuits which have gate counts
\mathcal{O}(M)
and depth
\mathcal{O}(\log_{2}M)

Cruz et al. 2019
. These circuits can be adapted into EGO circuits for the LMG Hamiltonian (
5
).

It has been shown that the LMG ground and quantum critical states can be prepared with high fidelity using
\mathcal{O}(1)
iterations of the Variational Quantum-Classical Simulation (VQCS) protocol
Ho et al. 2019
. This method does not use the exactly-solvable nature of the LMG model via the Bethe ansatz. Our circuit uses the Fock-space-to-qubit encoding

\left|2^{k}\right\rangle_{M+1}\iff\left|2M+\nu_{a}-2k,\nu_{b}+2k\right\rangle

(29)

where
k
is an integer from
0
to
M
. Each EGO factor is given by a controlled-
R_{y}
gate and a controlled-NOT (CNOT) gate. The EGO circuit is given by

\prod_{\ell=1}^{M}\text{C}^{M+2-\ell}\left(\text{NOT}_{f\left(M+1-\ell\right)}\right)\text{C}^{f\left(M+1-\ell\right)}\left(R_{y}\left(\theta\right)_{M+2-\ell}\right)

(30)

where
\text{C}^{m}\left(Q_{n}\right)
designates a controlled-
Q
gate with the control on qubit
m
and gate
Q
is acting on qubit
n
. The function
f(n)
controls the depth of the circuit:

f(n)\equiv\begin{cases}n\quad\text{for depth }\mathcal{O}(2M)\\
n-2^{\lfloor\log_{2}n\rfloor}+1\quad\text{for depth }\mathcal{O}(\log_{2}2M)\\
\end{cases}

(31)

where
n
is a positive integer.

N

2^{1}

For the linear-depth circuit, the output vector is the following:

\left|\psi_{M}\right\rangle=\prod_{j=1}^{M}\sin\frac{\theta_{j}}{2}\left|2^{0}\right\rangle_{M+1}\\
+\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}\cos\frac{\theta_{\ell}}{2}\prod_{k=1}^{\ell-1}\sin\frac{\theta_{k}}{2}\left|2^{M+1-\ell}\right\rangle_{M+1}.

-sphere over the given support, so it is a functional ansatz for any trigonometric LMG eigenstate.

The linear-depth circuit allows for simpler angle specification than the logarithmic-depth circuit. For a generic real unit
(M+1)
-vector

over the specified circuit’s support

\left|T_{M}\right\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{M+1}c_{j}\left|2^{j-1}\right\rangle_{M+1}

\left|T_{M}\right\rangle

Blumenson 1960

\theta_{j}=\begin{cases}2\cos^{-1}q_{M,j}&\text{if }j\leq M\\
2\text{ sgn}\left(c_{1}\right)(\cos^{-1}q_{M,j}-\pi)+2\pi&\text{if }j=M\end{cases}

(34)

q_{M,j}\equiv\frac{c_{M+2-j}}{\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{M+2-j}c_{k}^{2}}}.

If

Eigenstates

The case of

(43)

As this parameterizes the real unit circle over a support of two orthonormal states, there is some value of

IV.5
Eigenstate Preparation for
\mathbf{M>2}

For quintic or higher polynomials no general analytic solution exists for all cases
Abel 1824
, so the Bethe Equations (
15
) must be solved numerically beyond
M=2
.

Each output vector of either the logarithmic- or linear-depth EGO circuit (
30
)
\left|\psi_{M}\right\rangle
is a state with
(M+1)
nonzero real amplitudes. Each amplitude is a product of at most
M
sine or cosine functions of a single half-angle. Given an eigenstate, then, the EGO circuit’s (
30
) angles of rotation can be obtained numerically or using Equation (
34
) in the linear case. Any real unit vector over the basis
\{\left|2^{0}\right\rangle_{M+1},\left|2^{1}\right\rangle_{M+1},...,\left|2^{M}\right\rangle_{M+1}\}
can be prepared with either circuit.

Appendix (
E
) gives two explicit examples for preparing the ground state of the
M=3,\text{ }N=7
-particle and
M=10,\text{ }N=20
-particle LMG models for both the logarithmic- and linear-depth circuits (
30
).

V
Discussion

By utilizing the product nature of the EGO (
12
) we have constructed two
\mathcal{O}(N)
-gate quantum circuit analogues that function as variational ansatzes for the trigonometric LMG model of
N
particles. Both circuits are described by Equation (
30
) while the logarithmic-depth version is also shown in Figure (
1
). In the case of the linear-depth version, the input angles are described by Equation (
34
). Low qubit count, depth, gate count and parallelization makes the circuits well-suited for NISQ devices. The circuits will work for hyperbolic or rational cases of the LMG such that
E_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}\text{ }\forall\text{ }1\leq\ell\leq M
, of which there are at least three cases:
N=1,2,3
. No single phase transition distinguishes trigonometric cases with purely real spectral parameters from those with complex ones
Lerma H. and Dukelsky 2013
. For
M\geq 3
the Bethe equations (
15
) are quintic and lack analytic solutions but
E_{\ell}
can be solved numerically.

As an exactly-solvable model, the LMG model can be used to evaluate near-term quantum devices. By comparing results obtained from exact solutions to NISQ-device simulations the LMG model can function as a benchmark, just as it did for classical calculations
Lipkin et al. 1965
.

These circuits will function as a variational ansatz for any linear-support, real-eigenstate model. The greater family of bosonic Gaudin models would be fertile ground for further investigations
Lerma H. and Dukelsky 2013
. The circuits we describe are also able to create arbitrary real superpositions of LMG eigenstates for a given
M,\nu_{a},\nu_{b}
as the circuit’s output vector parameterizes the unit
M
-sphere.

Here we present a probabilistic qudit-based EGO circuit shown in Figure

There are two single-qudit gates which appear in the circuit:

\chi\left|k\right\rangle\equiv\left|k+1\text{ mod }M+1\right\rangle\quad W\left|k\right\rangle\equiv(-1)^{1-\delta_{k0}}\left|k\right\rangle

(55)

where
k
is an integer from
0
to
M
. The circuit also uses the Hadamard,
R_{y}
and Pauli-
X
gates on the ancilla qubits.

-qudit (

and

for some

(57)

if the

(58)

Appendix D

The linear-depth EGO circuit given in Equation (
30
) produces output states with nonzero amplitudes parameterized by
(M+1)
dimensional hyperspherical coordinates. Thus states generated by these cicuits are in correspondence with points on the
M
-sphere
Blumenson 1960
. Hence these circuits can generate any quantum state with real amplitudes and given support. The logarithmic-depth circuit generates states whose nonzero amplitudes are not parameterized by hyperspherical coordinates. In this section we prove that nonetheless these circuits generate states in one-to-one correspondence with points on the
M
sphere.

We proceed by induction to prove that the output vector of the logarithmic-depth circuit given by Equation (
30
) and Figure
1
can parameterize the
M
-sphere over the set of bitstring vectors with support
M+1
and Hamming weight
1
.

The output of Equation (

), parameterizes a real unit circle (
1
-sphere) over the support
\left|2^{0}\right\rangle_{2}
and
\left|2^{1}\right\rangle_{2}
. Thus the output will parameterize any real linear combination of computational basis vectors with length
2
and Hamming weight
1
. Projecting the parameterization
\left|\psi_{1}\right\rangle
onto the hyperplane
H_{1}
defined by
\left|2^{1}\right\rangle_{2}
will result in a parameterization of the
1
-ball (line segment from
-1
to
1
) on
H_{1}
. This will function as our base case for a proof by induction.

As an inductive hypothesis, assume that output vector
\left|\psi_{t}\right\rangle
parameterizes the
t
-sphere over the support
\{\left|2^{0}\right\rangle_{t+1},\left|2^{1}\right\rangle_{t+1},...,\left|2^{t}\right\rangle_{t+1}\}
. Define
\left|\psi_{t}^{\prime}\right\rangle
as
\left|\psi_{t}\right\rangle
projected onto the hyperplane
H_{t}
defined by

-ball.

By definition, a
t
-ball is the hypervolume contained within a
(t-1)
-sphere. We may also restate this as the hypervolume contained in between a
t
-ball and a
t
-sphere; in the base case these were the line segment
-1
to
1
and the
1
-sphere (i.e. circle). The
t

\left|\psi_{t}^{\prime}\right\rangle
can parameterize any real linear combination of basis vectors given by
H_{t}
where the sum of the squared coefficients is less than or equal to
1
.

We can write output vectors
\left|\psi_{t}\right\rangle
and
\left|\psi_{t+1}\right\rangle
as

\displaystyle\left|\psi_{t}\right\rangle=\sum_{j=0}^{t}c_{j+1}\left|2^{j}\right\rangle_{t+1}

\displaystyle\left|\psi_{t+1}\right\rangle=\sum_{j=0}^{t+1}d_{j+1}\left|2^{j}\right\rangle_{t+2}

th

Motivic local systems on curves and Maeda’s conjecture
Yeuk Hay Joshua Lam

Abstract
. We show that only finitely many complex genus two curves and four punctured spheres admit rank two local systems of geometric origin, and moreover each carries finitely many. This gives further counterexamples to a conjecture of Esnault and Kerz: counterexamples over very general curves were recently obtained by Landesman and Litt. In the second part we prove an analogue of this result in positive characteristic, namely that over 𝔽 ¯ p \overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p} , only finitely many genus two curves admit non-trivial rank two local systems pulled back from a fixed quaternionic Shimura variety, and the same for ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1} minus four points; conjecturally, every rank two local system arises as such a pullback. This provides results towards Maeda’s conjecture on Galois orbits of eigenforms over function fields. The proofs make use of ideas from the work of Landesman and Litt such as isomonodromy, as well as crucially the description of the Goren-Oort strata due to Tian and Xiao.

We now give a sketch of the proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.6
. It should be noted that our methods are very much inspired by the work of Landesman and Litt on stability of vector bundles and isomonodromy.

First we discuss the proof of Theorem
1.1
, and we focus on the case of genus two curves for simplicity. A rank two local system of geometric origin unerlies a polarizable complex variation of Hodge structure, which gives a vector bundle with connection
(E,\nabla)
on
X
, which is furthermore equipped with a Hodge filtration
\mathcal{L}\vcentcolon={\rm Fil}^{1}
which must be a line subbundle in our case. A result of Griffiths implies that
\mathcal{L}
is positive, and a degree computation shows that the Higgs map is an isomorphism. This pins down the Higgs bundle, of which there are only finitely many choices, and therefore the local system by the Simpson correspondence. In fact, such a
(E,\nabla)
whose associated Higgs field is an isomorphism is known as an
uniformizing oper
. For the finiteness statements, a rank two local system
\mathbb{L}
coming from geometry must arise from an abelian scheme, and the above shows that it further satisfies the
Arakelov equality
. By a result of Viehweg and Zuo
[
VZ04
]
, this implies that
X
has an étale cover which is a Shimura curve, and the finiteness follows from that of arithmetic Fuchsian groups of bounded genus, which is due to Takeuchi
[
Tak83
]
.

Next, we sketch the proof of Theorem
1.6
; again for this sketch we focus on the case of genus two curves. We consider the stack
\mathcal{M}_{2}(\mathscr{S}_{k},d)
of genus two curves mapping to
\mathscr{S}_{k}
of degree
d
, which is of finite type. The argument proceeds by induction on
\dim\mathscr{S}_{k}
, and we first focus on the substack
\mathcal{M}_{2}(\mathscr{S}_{k},d)^{\rm{os}}
when the curve is
ordinary
. Remmembering just the data of the curve, we have a map

\pi:\mathcal{M}_{2}(\mathscr{S}_{k},d)^{\mathrm{ord}}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{2}.

We show that the image of
\pi
is a finite set, so let us suppose that this is not the case.

This means that there is a positive dimensional, non-trivial family of curves
\mathfrak{X}\rightarrow S
over some base
S
as well as a family of maps
\mathfrak{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{S}\otimes S
. The Shimura variety
\mathscr{S}_{k}
comes equipped with several rank two, filtered, vector bundles with connection
(E_{\nabla},\nabla_{\tau},{\rm Fil}_{\tau})
, so pulling back we obtain the same on
X
, which we denote simply by
f^{*}E_{\nabla}
. The
f^{*}E_{\nabla}
’s are isomonodromic, and the ordinary assumption lets us put ourselves in the same setup as in the proof of Theorem
1.1
: namely,
{\rm Fil}_{\tau}
is positive, and the Kodaira-Spencer map is non-zero. The argument then concludes with a deformation theory computation.

Now we suppose
X
is not generically ordinary; this means that
X
lies in a Goren-Oort stratum
\mathscr{S}_{k,\rm{T}}\subset\mathscr{S}_{k}
. By the main result of
[
TX16
]
,
\mathscr{S}_{k,\rm{T}}
is a
(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{N}
-bundle over another quaternionic Shimura variety
\mathscr{S}(G(\mathrm{S(T)}))_{k}
. Moreover, the natural local systems on
\mathscr{S}_{k,\rm{T}}
agree with those pulled back from
\mathscr{S}(G(\mathrm{S(T)}))_{k}
. Since the latter has smaller dimension, we now conclude by our inductive hypothesis.

Remark 1.8
.

Along the way, we showed that sufficiently non-trivial maps from a genus 2 curve to a fixed quaternionic Shimura variety must have bounded degree. In characteristic zero, a much more general result was obtained by Faltings

2.
Complex curves

We first recall a positivity result specialized to our setting.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective curve, and
Z\subset X
a reduced divisor. Let
(E,F,\nabla)
be a rank two flat vector bundle equipped with its Hodge filtration corresponding to a polarizable complex variation of Hodge structure on
X\backslash Z
. Denote by
\overline{E},\overline{\nabla}
its Deligne extension to
X
, and let
(\oplus_{i}\mathrm{gr}_{F}^{i}\overline{E},\theta)
denote the associated Higgs bundle. Then the latter is stable of parabolic degree zero. In particular, the line bundles
F^{0}
and
F^{0}/F^{1}
have positive and negative parabolic degrees, respectively.

Proof.

See
[
Sim90
, Theorem 5]
, as well as
[
LL22
, Proposition 4.1.4]
for a nice summary.
∎

The following is the straightforward part of the Simpson correspondence; see for example
[
Sim91
, Theorem 1]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

The Higgs bundle
(\oplus_{i}\mathrm{gr}^{i}_{F}\overline{E},\theta)
determines the complex variation of Hodge structure.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We first treat the case with
X
being a genus two curve. Suppose we have a rank two vector bundle with flat connection
(E,\nabla)
on
X
corresponding to a
{\rm SL}_{2}
-local system
\mathbb{L}
, which is moreover irreducible and motivic. We denote by
\rho
the associated representation of
\pi_{1}(X)
, which we may further assume has infinite monodromy. Note that
\det E
is the trivial line bundle.

As
\mathbb{L}
is motivic, it corresponds to a a representation

\pi_{1}(X)\rightarrow{\rm SL}_{2}(K)\xhookrightarrow{}{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})

for some number field
K
along with a complex embedding. Now each of the Galois conjugates
\mathbb{L}^{\sigma}
underlies a VHS, and since it has infinite monodromy, one of these VHS has a non-trivial Hodge filtration, and we assume that it is
\mathbb{L}
itself. Therefore
(E,\nabla)
has a sub line bundle
\mathcal{L}\vcentcolon={\rm Fil}^{1}\subset E
of some degree
d
. Note that by the
{\rm SL}_{2}
-condition,
E/\mathcal{L}\simeq\mathcal{L}^{-1}
. By Proposition
2.1
,
d>0
; on the other hand, since the local system is irreducible, we have a non-trivial Higgs map

\mathcal{L}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}^{-1}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X},

with
\Omega^{1}_{X}
being the sheaf of one-forms.
Therefore

0<d\leq 2g-2-d=2-d,

which forces
d=1
. Furthermore, since its source and target have the same degree, the Higgs map is an isomorphism. It follows that

\mathcal{L}^{\otimes 2}\simeq\Omega^{1}_{X}.

There are sixteen such choices of
\mathcal{L}
, and the Higgs map is also determined once we have fixed
\mathcal{L}
; therefore the choice of
\mathcal{L}
determines the Higgs bundle, and therefore the local system also. Finally, each rank two motivic local system is a Galois conjugate of one of these, and hence there are finitely many such, as required.

The argument for
X=\mathbb{P}^{1}-\{0,1,t,\infty\}
is almost identical. The Higgs bundle simply gets replaced by a meromorphic Higgs bundle

\mathcal{L}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}^{-1}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}(D),

with poles along the divisor
D=(0)+(1)+(t)+(\infty)
, and the degree is replaced by parabolic degree.

Now we show that there are only finitely many genus two curves admitting such a rank two local system
\mathbb{L}
. Since we are assuming
\mathbb{L}
is motivic, it must arise as a direct factor of a family of abelian varieties
p:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow X
; moreover, we may assume that each of the direct factors of
R^{1}p_{*}\mathbb{C}
is isomorphic to a Galois conjugate of
\mathbb{L}
. By the above argument, we therefore see that
\mathcal{A}
satisfies the
Arakelov equality
as in
[
VZ04
]
, and hence
X
has an étale cover which is a Shimura curve by
[
VZ04
, Theorem 0.7]
. Therefore the Fuchsian group
\Gamma=\rho(\pi_{1}(X))\subset{\rm PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
is arithmetic. Finally, by a theorem of Takeuchi
[
Tak83
, Theorem 2.1]
, there are only finitely many conjugacy classes of arithmetic Fuchsian groups of bounded genus. The argument for finiteness in the case of
\mathbb{P}^{1}-\{0,1,t,\infty\}
is identical.
∎

We give examples of local systems of geometric origin whose image under any non-trivial element of the mapping class group is not of geometric origin. Let
\mathrm{Mod}_{g}
denote the mapping class group of a closed genus
g
Riemann surface. Recall that we have

\mathrm{Mod}_{g}\simeq\mathrm{Out}(\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g})),

the outer automorphism group of the surface group
\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g})
. We therefore have an action of
\mathrm{Mod}_{g}
on any character variety of
\Sigma_{g}
: for any
\sigma\in\mathrm{Mod}_{g}
and
\rho\in\mathrm{Char}
, we let
\sigma^{*}\rho
denote the result obtained by acting with
\sigma
.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
\rho
be a motivic irreducible
{\rm SL}_{2}
-local system on a curve of genus two. Then for any non-torsion element
\sigma\in\mathrm{Mod}_{2}
,
\sigma^{*}\rho
is not motivic.

Proof.

By the proof of Theorem
1.1
, such a
\rho
must correspond to an uniformizing representation for
X
. Now suppose
\sigma^{*}\rho
is motivic. Then
\rho
and
\sigma^{*}\rho
must map to the same point in the
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}
-character variety: in other words,
\sigma
fixes
\overline{\rho}
in the
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}
-character variety. On the other hand,
\overline{\rho}
lies in the Teichmüller component, and no element

of infinite order acts with fixed points. We therefore have a contradiction, and so
\sigma^{*}\rho
is not motivic.
∎

3.
Maeda’s conjecture

3.1.
Maeda’s conjecture over number fields

We first recall the statement of Maeda’s conjecture. For
k\geq 2
, let
S_{k}({\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
denote the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space of cuspidal modular forms of weight

and full level with rational coefficients.

Conjecture 3.2

(
[
HM97
, Conjecture 1.2]
)
.

The Hecke algebra over
\mathbb{Q}
of
S_{k}({\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
is simple, i.e. it is a number field
F
. Moreover the Galois closure of
F
has Galois group the symmetric group
S_{n}
with
n=\dim S_{k}({\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})).

3.4.
Function field analogue

The rest of this paper is devoted to proving results towards a version of Maeda’s conjecture over function fields. We do not make a precise statement of such a conjecture and merely make some preliminary remarks here.

Remark 3.5
.

Note that, Maeda’s conjecture in the function field case can be., at best, generically true. Indeed, if
\mathcal{O}
is the ring of integers of some number field, and
\mathfrak{X}
is a curve over
\mathcal{O}
equipped with a motivic local system, then upon reduction modulo primes
\mathfrak{p}
the traces of Frobenius will lie in some fixed number field, and such trace fields will not be as big as Maeda’s conjecture predicts. There are also purely characteristic
p
obstructions to Maeda’s conjecture. For example, for each
g
, there exists
N(g)
such that a Zariski dense set of genus
g
curves over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
admit irreducible, arithmetic,
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
-local system with trace field
\mathbb{Q}
. This follows from the existence of non-trivial families of abelian varieties over the generic curve, for example by the Kodaira-Parshin construction
[
LL22
, § 5.1]
.

In this section we collect some results about quaternionic Shimura varieties and their special fibers. For a much more thorough treatment we refer the reader to
[

]
. We fix throughout an isomorphism
\iota:\mathbb{C}\xrightarrow{\simeq}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}

F

4.1.
Quaternionic Shimura varieties

Now let
\mathrm{S}
be a subset of the places of
F
of even size.

We will always work under the following

Hypothesis 4.2
.

(1)

We have
p\notin\rm{S}
.

(2)

The field

is inert at
p
.

Remark 4.3
.

We comment on each of our hypotheses. Hypothesis (1) is to guarantee that the Shimura varieties have good reduction, so that it makes sense to talk about local systems. Hypothesis (2) is a technical assumption caused by the fact that quaternionic Shimura varieties do not carry natural abelian schemes. More precisely, the key issue is that we would like to bound the degree of non-trivial maps from curves to our Shimura varieties by some kind of Frobenius untwisting procedure (c.f.
[
Xia13
, Theorem 6.1]
), in the case when certain Kodaira-Spencer maps vanish. Such a procedure would seem to require natural abelian schemes and
p
-divisible groups on the Shimura variety in question. It may be interesting to study a group theoretic version of Frobenius untwisting which would allow us to lift Hypothesis (2).

Denote by
B_{\rm{S}}
the quaternion algebra over

ramified precisely at the places in
\rm{S}
. We have the associated reductive group

G_{\rm{S}}\vcentcolon=\mathrm{Res}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(B_{\rm{S}}^{\times}).

Definition 4.4
.

(1)

For a compact open subgroup

of the form

, we let

denote the Shimura variety, defined over the a number field

known as the reflex field, whose complex points are

Here,
\mathfrak{h}_{\rm{S}}
denotes the

-conjugacy class of a Deligne homomorphism

We refer the reader to
[
TX16
, §3.1]
for details of the definition of

.

(2)

In
[
TX16
]
, for the specific choice of

as in
[

, §3.3]

of

. We let

4.6.
Unitary Shimura varieties

Since quaternionic Shimura varieties themselves are not solutions to a moduli problem and therefore do not carry universal abelian varieties and their associated bundles, it is customary to study a closely related Shimura variety which does have these properties, and then transfer all objects back to the quaternionic one. In this section we recall some results about these auxiliary Shimura varieties.

Let

5.

We record some results on isomonodromy in this section. The material here is due to Simpson

Definition 5.1

S

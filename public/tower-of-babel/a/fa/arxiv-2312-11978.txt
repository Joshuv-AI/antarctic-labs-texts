The mystery of Carleson frames
Ole Christensen, Marzieh Hasannasab, Friedrich Philipp, Diana Stoeva

Abstract
In 2016 Aldroubi et al. constructed the first class of frames having the form { T k ​ φ } k = 0 ∞ \{T^{k}\varphi\}_{k=0}^{\infty} for a bounded linear operator on the underlying Hilbert space. In this paper we show that a subclass of these frames has a number of additional remarkable features that have not been identified for any other frames in the literature. Most importantly, the subfamily obtained by selecting each N N th element from the frame is itself a frame, regardless of the choice of N ∈ ℕ . N\in\mathbb{N}. Furthermore, the frame property is kept upon removal of an arbitrarily finite number of elements.



1
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{D}
denote the open unit disc in the complex plane. Recall that a sequence
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{D}
is said
to satisfy the Carleson condition if

\displaystyle\displaystyle\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\prod_{k\neq n}\frac{|\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{n}|}{|1-\overline{\lambda_{k}}\,\lambda_{n}|}>0.

(1.1)

In
[
1
]
Aldroubi et al. proved the following result:

Theorem 1.1

Assume that
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{D}
satisfies the Carleson condition,
and let
\{e_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
denote an orthonormal basis for a separable Hilbert space
{\cal H}.
Consider the
bounded linear operator
T:{\cal H}\to{\cal H}

given by
Te_{k}:=\lambda_{k}e_{k},\,k\in\mathbb{N},
and let
\{m_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}

be any scalar sequence such that
0<C_{1}\leq|m_{k}|\leq C_{2}<\infty.
Then, with

\displaystyle\varphi:=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}m_{k}\sqrt{1-|\lambda_{k}|^{2}}\,e_{k},

(1.2)

the family

\{T^{k}\varphi\}_{k=0~}^{\infty}
is a frame for

{\cal H}.

For the sake of easy reference we will call the frames
in Theorem
1.1
for
Carleson frames.

The construction in Theorem
1.1
provides
the first examples of frames that are given as the orbit
of a bounded operator. Except for the Riesz bases (see

[
6
]
)
this class still forms the only explicitly known example
of such frames. The purpose of the current paper is to
show that under a weak extra condition, such frames have a number of
important features that have not been identified for any other frames in the literature. Most importantly, the subsequence of

\{T^{k}\varphi\}_{k=0~}^{\infty}
formed by selecting
each
N
th element of the sequence is itself a frame, regardless of the choice of
N\in\mathbb{N}.

Our frame results are based on a number of manipulations
on the Carleson condition. For this reason we first
collect what is known about the Carleson condition in
the literature.

Proposition 1.2

Given a sequence
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{D}
of distinct numbers, the following
hold:

(i)

[
9
]
If

\displaystyle\exists\,c\in(0,1)\mbox{ such that }\frac{1-|\lambda_{k+1}|}{1-|\lambda_{k}|}\leq c<1,\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N},

(1.3)

then
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
satisfies the Carleson condition.

(ii)

[
9
]
If
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is positive and increasing, then the condition (
1.3
) is also necessary for
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
to satisfy the Carleson condition.

(iii)

[
10
]
If
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
satisfies the Carleson condition, then
\lim_{k\to\infty}|\lambda_{k}|=1.

(iv)

[
3
]
If there is some
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k\geq n}
satisfies the Carleson condition, then also
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
satisfies the Carleson condition.

2
Frame subsequences of the Carleson frames

In this section we will consider a subclass of the Carleson frames in Theorem
1.1
; more precisely, we will
add a weak assumption on the sequence
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{D}.
The resulting class
of Carleson frames have the remarkable properties mentioned
in the abstract.

Theorem 2.1

Let
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{D}
and
assume that
\{|\lambda_{k}|\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is a strictly
increasing sequence which satisfies the Carleson condition. Let
\{e_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
denote an orthonormal basis for a separable Hilbert space
{\cal H},
consider the
bounded linear operator
T:{\cal H}\to{\cal H}

given by
Te_{k}:=\lambda_{k}e_{k},\,k\in\mathbb{N},
and
let
\{m_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}

be any scalar sequence such that
0<C_{1}\leq|m_{k}|\leq C_{2}<\infty.
Then, with

\displaystyle\varphi:=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}m_{k}\sqrt{1-|\lambda_{k}|^{2}}\,e_{k},

(2.1)

the
following hold:

(i)

The family

\{T^{Nk}\varphi\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
is a frame for
{\cal H}
for
any
N\in\mathbb{N}.

(ii)

If
\lambda_{1}\neq 0,
the family
\{T^{Nk}\varphi\}_{k=K}^{\infty}
is a frame for
{\cal H}
for any
N,K\in\mathbb{N}.

(iii)

If
\lambda_{1}\neq 0,
the family
\{T^{j+Nk}\varphi\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
is a frame for
{\cal H}
for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
and any

j\in\{0,1,\dots,N-1\}.

Proof.
As the first step of the proof we will show that
the assumptions imply that
\{\lambda_{k}^{N}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
satisfies the Carleson condition for
all
N\in\mathbb{N}.
First, let
N=1
. By assumption the sequence
\{|\lambda_{k}|\}_{k=1}^{\infty}

consists of distinct numbers, so Proposition
1.2
(ii) implies that
(
1.3
) holds. Using now Proposition
1.2
(i) on the sequence
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
, it follows that
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
also
satisfies the Carleson condition, as claimed. Now, fix an arbitrary
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
N>1
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{1-|\lambda_{k+1}^{N}|}{1-|\lambda_{k}^{N}|}=\frac{1-|\lambda_{k+1}|}{1-|\lambda_{k}|}\cdot\frac{1+|\lambda_{k+1}|+\ldots+|\lambda_{k+1}|^{N-1}}{1+|\lambda_{k}|+\ldots+|\lambda_{k}|^{N-1}}.

As in the first part of the proof, take
c
which satisfies (
1.3
) and choose any

\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1-c}{c}).

Using Proposition
1.2
(iii), we see that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1+|\lambda_{k+1}|+\ldots+|\lambda_{k+1}|^{N-1}}{1+|\lambda_{k}|+\ldots+|\lambda_{k}|^{N-1}}=1

and therefore there is
K_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
so that

\frac{1+|\lambda_{k+1}|+\ldots+|\lambda_{k+1}|^{N-1}}{1+|\lambda_{k}|+\ldots+|\lambda_{k}|^{N-1}}<1+\varepsilon\ \mbox{ for }k>K_{0}.

Then
for
k>K_{0}
we have that

\frac{1-|\lambda_{k+1}|^{N}}{1-|\lambda_{k}|^{N}}<c(1+\varepsilon)<1.

By Proposition
1.2
(i), the sequence
\{\lambda_{k}^{N}\}_{k=K_{0}+1}^{\infty}
satisfies the Carleson condition and hence
\{\lambda_{k}^{N}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
satisfies the Carleson condition by Proposition
1.2
(iv).

By Theorem
1.1
we know that for the function
\varphi
in (
2.1
),
\{T^{k}\varphi\}_{k=0}^{\infty}

is a frame for
{\cal H}.
Now consider
N\in\mathbb{N},\,N\geq 2.

Then the bounded operator
T^{N}
is given by
T^{N}e_{k}=\lambda_{k}^{N}e_{k},\,k\in\mathbb{N}.
Since
\{\lambda_{k}^{N}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
satisfies the
Carleson condition, Theorem

1.1
shows
that
\{T^{Nk}\widetilde{\varphi}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
is
a frame for
{\cal H}
whenever
\widetilde{\varphi}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\widetilde{m_{k}}\sqrt{1-|\lambda_{k}^{N}|^{2}}e_{k}
for some coefficients
\widetilde{m_{k}}
that are bounded below and above.
We aim at taking

\displaystyle\widetilde{m_{k}}=m_{k}\sqrt{\frac{1-|\lambda_{k}|^{2}}{1-|\lambda_{k}^{N}|^{2}}};

note that this choice actually implies that

\widetilde{\varphi}=\varphi.
Now,

\displaystyle\widetilde{m_{k}}=m_{k}\sqrt{\frac{1-|\lambda_{k}|^{2}}{1-|\lambda_{k}^{N}|^{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m_{k}\sqrt{\frac{1-|\lambda_{k}|}{1-|\lambda_{k}|}\cdot\frac{1+|\lambda_{k}|}{1+|\lambda_{k}|+\cdots+|\lambda_{k}|^{2N-1}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m_{k}\sqrt{\frac{1+|\lambda_{k}|}{1+|\lambda_{k}|+\cdots+|\lambda_{k}|^{2N-1}}}.

Using that
0\leq|\lambda_{k}|\leq 1
and that
C_{1}\leq|m_{k}|\leq C_{2},
we see that

C_{1}(2N)^{-1/2}\leq|\widetilde{m_{k}}|\leq C_{2}.
Thus
\widetilde{m_{k}}
is indeed bounded below and above, i.e.,
we have proved that
\{T^{Nk}\varphi\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
is a frame as stated in (i).

In order to prove (ii), fix any
N,K\in\mathbb{N},
and note that

\displaystyle\{T^{Nk}\varphi\}_{k=K}^{\infty}=\{T^{NK}T^{Nk}\varphi\}_{k=0}^{\infty}.

Since
1\geq|\lambda_{k}|\geq|\lambda_{1}|>0
for all
k\in\mathbb{N},
the bounded operator
T
is
surjective, and hence

T^{NK}
is bounded and surjective; thus the result in (ii) follows immediately from (i) and
the well-known fact that the image of a frame
under a bounded surjective operator itself forms a frame.
The result in (iii) follows from the same argument
applied to the operator
T^{j}.

\square

Example 2.2

As a concrete example of a sequence
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
satisfying all the assumptions in Theorem
2.1
, one can take
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}=\{1-\alpha^{-k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
, for any
\alpha>1.

\square

Note that the assumptions in Theorem
2.1
are on the absolute
values
|\lambda_{k}|.

The following example shows that
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
satisfying
the Carleson condition does not imply that
\{\lambda_{k}^{N}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
satisfies
the Carleson condition for
N>1.

Example 2.3

Consider a sequence
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
that satisfies the Carleson
condition.
Take a number
q\in(0,1)
such that
\mu_{k}\neq q
and
\mu_{k}\neq-q
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
By Proposition
1.2
(iv), the sequence
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}:=\{q,-q,\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{3},\ldots\}
also satisfies the Carleson
condition, but the sequence
\{\lambda_{k}^{2}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
does not.

Theorem
2.1
shows that for any
N\in\mathbb{N},
the
considered class of
Carleson frames can be
considered as the union of
N
frames, namely,

\displaystyle\{T^{k}\varphi\}_{k=0}^{\infty}=\bigcup_{j=0}^{N-1}\{T^{Nk+j}\varphi\}_{k=0}^{\infty}.

(2.2)

We will now show if we assume that
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{D}

consist of positive scalars, then “one can weave the elements from these
frames from a sufficiently large index” (see Theorem
2.5
for a precise statement). For this purpose we
need the subsequent lemma.

Lemma 2.4

Let
\{T^{k}\varphi\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
be
a Carleson frame as in Theorem
1.1
, for which the sequence
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{D}

consists of positive and strictly increasing scalars, and
fix any
N\in\mathbb{N}.
Then, for any choice of

j_{k}\in\{0,1,\dots,N-1\},\,k\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}||T^{Nk}\varphi-T^{Nk+j_{k}}\varphi||^{2}<\infty.

(2.3)

Proof.
By direct calculation, and using the upper bound
on the sequence
\{m_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
in (
1.2
)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}||T^{Nk}\varphi-T^{Nk+j_{k}}\varphi||^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|m_{n}\lambda_{n}^{Nk}\sqrt{1-\lambda_{n}^{2}}-m_{n}\lambda_{n}^{Nk+j_{k}}\sqrt{1-\lambda_{n}^{2}}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-\lambda_{n}^{2})\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\lambda_{n}^{2N}\right)^{k}\left(1-\lambda_{n}^{j_{k}}\right)^{2}.

Using that
0<1-\lambda_{n}^{j_{k}}\leq 1-\lambda_{n}^{N}\leq 1-\lambda_{n}^{2N},
this implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}||T^{kN}\varphi-T^{kN+j_{k}}\varphi||^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{2}^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-\lambda_{n}^{2})(1-\lambda_{n}^{N})^{2}\frac{1}{1-\lambda_{n}^{2N}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{2}^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-\lambda_{n}^{2})\leq\frac{C_{2}^{2}}{C_{1}^{2}}||\varphi||^{2}<\infty,

as desired.
\square

Theorem 2.5

Let
\{T^{k}\varphi\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
be
a Carleson frame as in Theorem
1.1
, for which the sequence
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{D}

consists of positive and strictly increasing scalars, and fix any
N\in\mathbb{N}.
Then, for any choice of

j_{k}\in\{0,1,\dots,N-1\},\,k\in\mathbb{N}_{0},
there exists
J\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that the set

\displaystyle\{T^{Nk}\varphi\}_{k=0}^{J-1}\cup\{T^{Nk+j_{k}}\varphi\}_{k=J}^{\infty}

(2.4)

is a frame.

Proof.
Fix
N\in\mathbb{N};
by Theorem
2.1
we know that

\{T^{Nk}\varphi\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
is a frame for
{\cal H}.
Let
A
denote
a lower frame bound, and fix any
choice of

j_{k}\in\{0,1,\dots,J-1\};
then, by Lemma
2.4
,
we can choose
J\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=J}^{\infty}||T^{Nk}\varphi-T^{Nk+j_{k}}\varphi||^{2}<A.

By standard perturbation results
in frame theory (see, e.g., page 565 in
[
4
]
),
this implies that in the frame
\{T^{Nk}\varphi\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
we can replace
the elements
\{T^{Nk}\varphi\}_{k=J}^{\infty}
by

\{T^{Nk+j_{k}}\varphi\}_{k=J}^{\infty}
while the frame
property is kept, as desired.
\square

Formulated in the terminology of weaving frames (see

[
2
]
), Theorem
2.5
says that if we fix a sufficiently
high number
J
of the first elements of the frame
\{T^{Nk}\varphi\}_{k=0}^{\infty},
we can
weave the “tail frames”
\{T^{Nk+j}\}_{k=J+1}^{\infty},\,j=0,\dots,N-1,
and
keep the frame property.

We conjecture that the very particular properties of the
frames in Theorem
2.1
imply that indeed
the frames
\{T^{Nk+j}\}_{k=0}^{\infty},\,j=0,\dots,N-1,

are weaving for any
N=2,3,....;
that is,
we conjecture that Theorem
2.5
holds
with
J=0.

Note that for
N=2,

this would mean that the frames
\{T^{2k}\varphi\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
and
\{TT^{2k}\varphi\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
are weaving. Conditions
for this to hold are provided in
[
2
]
;
however, they are formulated in terms of the frame bounds,
and the available
frame bound estimates (see
[
7
]
) are not strong
enough to give the desired conclusion.

The “subsampling property” in Theorem
2.1
(i)
is a very special property for the considered frames, and
has not been identified for any other frame in the literature. Let
us illustrate this by showing that it can not hold for a number of important classes of frames.

Example 2.6

For
a,b>0
and
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),
let

E_{mb}T_{na}g(x):=e^{2\pi imbx}g(x-na),
and assume
that
\{E_{mb}T_{na}g\}_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a Gabor frame for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}).
Since
a necessary condition for such a family to be a frame
is that
ab\leq 1,
it is clear that “subsamples”

\{E_{mb}T_{nNa}g\}_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}
or
\{E_{mNb}T_{na}g\}_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}
never can be frames for
arbitrary large values of
N\in\mathbb{N}.

\square

Example 2.7

For
a>1,b>0,
and
\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),
let

D_{a^{j}}T_{kb}\psi(x):=a^{j/2}\psi(a^{j}x-kb),
and
consider the wavelet system
\{D_{a^{j}}T_{kb}\psi\}_{j,k\in\mathbb{Z}}.
In contrast to
the Gabor case, there exist wavelet frames for
arbitrary choices of the parameters
a>1,b>0,

see
[
8
]
. That is, assuming that

\{D_{a^{j}}T_{kb}\psi\}_{j,k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a frame in general does not exclude that
\{D_{a^{Nj}}T_{kb}\psi\}_{j,k\in\mathbb{Z}}

can be a frame, even for large values of
N\in\mathbb{N}.

However, for the frame constructions in the literature, there is a connection between the choice of parameters
a,b
and the corresponding function
\psi,
i.e.,
changing the parameters in a wavelet frame will in general
also force a change in the generating function (see

[
5
]
for an illustration of this).

For the case of a
band limited
frame

\{D_{a^{j}}T_{kb}\psi\}_{j,k\in\mathbb{Z}}
we can exclude the possibility that
\{D_{a^{Nj}}T_{kb}\psi\}_{j,k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a frame
for large values of
N\in\mathbb{N}.
To be precise, assume
that
\{D_{a^{j}}T_{kb}\psi\}_{j,k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a frame with bounds
A,B;
then it is well-known that

\displaystyle bA\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\widehat{\psi}(a^{j}\gamma)|^{2}\leq bB,\,a.e.\,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.5)

Assume now that

\text{supp}\,\widehat{\psi}\subset[-D,-C]\cup[C,D]

for some
C,D>0.
Then, considering
\gamma\in[C/4,C/2],
it is clear that for sufficiently large values of

N\in\mathbb{N},
we have

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\widehat{\psi}(a^{Nj}\gamma)|^{2}=0,

i.e.,
\{D_{a^{Nj}}T_{kb}\psi\}_{j,k\in\mathbb{Z}}

can not be a frame.
\square

To illustrate the issue further, we finally prove that despite the fact that
“arbitrarily sparse” subfamilies of the Carleson frames
in Theorem
2.1
still form frames, no
frame can have the property that arbitrary infinite subfamilies keep the frame property.

Proposition 2.8

No frame
\{f_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
for an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space
{\cal H}
has the property that every infinite subsequence
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in I}

is a frame for
{\cal H}.

Proof.
Let
\{f_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
denote an arbitrary frame for
{\cal H},
and let
\{e_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}

denote an orthonormal basis for
{\cal H}.
We will inductively construct an infinite family

\{g_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\subset\{f_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
which is not a frame. First, since
\{f_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is a Bessel sequence, choose

N_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=N_{1}}^{\infty}|\langle e_{1},f_{k}\rangle|^{2}\leq 1,

and let
g_{1}:=f_{N_{1}}.
Thus, the lower frame bound (if it exists) for

\{f_{k}\}_{k=N_{1}}^{\infty}
is at most
1.

Now, choose
j_{1}>1
such that
|\langle e_{j_{1}},f_{N_{1}}\rangle|^{2}\leq 2^{-2},
choose
N_{2}>N_{1}
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=N_{2}}^{\infty}|\langle e_{j_{1}},f_{k}\rangle|^{2}\leq 2^{-2},

and let
g_{2}:=f_{N_{2}}.
Then

\displaystyle|\langle e_{j_{1}},f_{N_{1}}\rangle|^{2}+\sum_{k=N_{2}}^{\infty}|\langle e_{j_{1}},f_{k}\rangle|^{2}\leq 2^{-1},

i.e., the lower frame bound (if it exists) for
\{f_{N_{1}}\}\cup\{f_{k}\}_{k=N_{2}}^{\infty}

is at most
2^{-1}.

Now, choose
j_{2}>j_{1}
such that

|\langle e_{j_{2}},f_{N_{1}}\rangle|^{2}+|\langle e_{j_{2}},f_{N_{2}}\rangle|^{2}\leq 2^{-3},
choose
N_{3}>N_{2}
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=N_{3}}^{\infty}|\langle e_{j_{2}},f_{k}\rangle|^{2}\leq 2^{-3},

and let
g_{3}:=f_{N_{3}}.
Then

\displaystyle|\langle e_{j_{2}},f_{N_{1}}\rangle|^{2}+|\langle e_{j_{2}},f_{N_{2}}\rangle|^{2}+\sum_{k=N_{3}}^{\infty}|\langle e_{j_{2}},f_{k}\rangle|^{2}\leq 2^{-2},

i.e., the lower frame bound (if it exists) for
\{f_{N_{1}}\}\cup\{f_{N_{2}}\}\cup\{f_{k}\}_{k=N_{3}}^{\infty}

is at most
2^{-2}.

Inductively, having constructed
g_{1},\dots,g_{\ell}
for some
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
(as well as the scalars
j_{1},\dots,j_{\ell-1}
and
N_{1},\dots,N_{\ell}
),
choose
j_{\ell}>j_{\ell-1}
such that

|\langle e_{j_{\ell}},f_{N_{1}}\rangle|^{2}+|\langle e_{j_{\ell}},f_{N_{2}}\rangle|^{2}+\cdots+|\langle e_{j_{\ell}},f_{N_{\ell}}\rangle|^{2}\leq 2^{-\ell-1},
choose
N_{\ell+1}>N_{\ell}
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=N_{\ell+1}}^{\infty}|\langle e_{j_{\ell}},f_{k}\rangle|^{2}\leq 2^{-\ell-1},

and let
g_{\ell+1}:=f_{N_{\ell+1}}.
Then

\displaystyle|\langle e_{j_{\ell}},f_{N_{1}}\rangle|^{2}+|\langle e_{j_{\ell}},f_{N_{2}}\rangle|^{2}+\cdots+|\langle e_{j_{\ell}},f_{N_{\ell}}\rangle|^{2}+\sum_{k=N_{\ell+1}}^{\infty}|\langle e_{j_{\ell}},f_{k}\rangle|^{2}\leq 2^{-\ell},

i.e., the lower frame bound (if it exists) for
\{f_{N_{1}}\}\cup\{f_{N_{2}}\}\cup\cdots\cup\{f_{N_{\ell}}\}\cup\{f_{k}\}_{k=N_{\ell+1}}^{\infty}

is at most
2^{-\ell}.
By construction,
\{g_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is a subsequence
of
\{f_{N_{1}}\}\cup\{f_{N_{2}}\}\cdots\cup\{f_{N_{\ell}}\}\cup\{f_{k}\}_{k=N_{\ell+1}}^{\infty}

for any
\ell\in\mathbb{N},
and thus no strictly positive lower bound exists.
\square

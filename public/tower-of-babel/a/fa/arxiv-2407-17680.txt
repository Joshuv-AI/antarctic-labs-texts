Watkins’s conjecture for elliptic curves with a rational torsion
Subham Bhakta, Srilakshmi Krishnamoorthy

Abstract
. Watkins’s conjecture suggests that for an elliptic curve E / ℚ E/\mathbb{Q} , the rank of the group E ⁡ ( ℚ ) E(\mathbb{Q}) of rational points is bounded above by ν 2 ​ ( m E ) \nu_{2}(m_{E}) , where m E m_{E} is the modular degree associated with E E . For example, for prime ℓ \ell , we quantify the elliptic curves featuring a rational ℓ \ell -torsion that satisfies Watkins’s conjecture. Additionally, the study extends to a broader context, investigating the inequality rank ⁡ ( E ⁡ ( ℚ ) ) + M ≤ ν 2 ​ ( m E ) \mathrm{rank}(E(\mathbb{Q}))+M\leq\nu_{2}(m_{E}) for any positive integer M M .

1.
Introduction

Let
X_{0}(N)
be the modular curve that can be realized as the Riemann surface obtained by the standard action of the congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{0}(N)
on the upper half place
\mathbb{H}
. Consider
E/\mathbb{Q}
to be any arbitrary elliptic curve. We know that
E
is modular by the work of Wiles, Taylor-Wiles, et al. In other words, there exists a surjective morphism (defined over
\mathbb{Q}
)

\phi_{E}:X_{0}(N)\to E
, which is called the
modular parametrization
of
E
. Throughout the article, we shall assume that
\phi_{E}
has a minimal degree, sending the cusp
\infty
of
X_{0}(N)
to the identity of
E
. We denote the minimal degree as
m_{E}
, the modular degree of
E
. In this article, we mainly aim to study Watkins’s conjecture.

Conjecture 1

(Watkins)
.

For any elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
, we have
\mathrm{rank}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(E(\mathbb{Q}))\leq\nu_{2}(m_{E})
.

The conjecture is known to be true when the modular degree is odd. This follows from the work of Calegari, Emerton
[
6
]
, Yazdani
[
29
]
and Kazalicki, Kohem
[
21
]
. When
E(\mathbb{Q})[2]
is trivial, Dummigan
[
13
]
heuristically proved that certain classes of elliptic curves satisfy Watkins’s conjecture, under the assumption that
R\cong T
. This assumption is about a relation between the tangent space of a suitable deformation ring that captures relevant properties of the residual representation
\overline{\rho}:\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\to\mathrm{GL}_{2}(E[2])
, and a certain Selmer group which is large enough to capture the weak Mordell-Weil group.

An alternative heuristic proof of Watkins’s conjecture is presented in
[
4
, Thm. 2]
, demonstrating its average truth. Specifically, the conjecture holds true for nearly all elliptic curves under the Minimalist conjecture. This follows as a consequence of the primary outcome in Dummigan and Krishnamoorthy
[
14
]
, which essentially establishes a lower bound on
\nu_{2}(m_{E})
based on the number of prime factors in the conductor of the elliptic curve
E
. Recently Park, Poonen, Voight, and Wood
[
25
]
conjectured that, there are only finitely many elliptic curves of rank
21
. With these heuristics, Watkins’s conjecture is true for all but finitely many elliptic curves with a large number of bad reductions.

It is noteworthy that any elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
can be expressed in the minimal Weierstrass form

(1)

E_{A,B}:y^{2}=x^{3}+Ax+B,~A,B\in{\mathbb{Z}},~p^{4}|A\implies p^{6}\nmid B.

In the context of
[
4
, Thm. 2]
, elliptic curves are arranged based on their height, defined as

(2)

H(E_{A,B})=\max\Big\{|A|^{3},|B|^{2}\Big\}^{1/6}.

Throughout the article, we shall arrange the elliptic curves with respect to the height
H
, as defined above. Note that there are approximately
\asymp X^{5}
elliptic curves with heights not exceeding
X
. The arithmetic statistics presented in
[
4
, Thm. 2]
do not address thin families, i.e., families that exclusively consist of
0\%
of all elliptic curves. In this article, our main focus revolves around the thin family given by the elliptic curves equipped with at least one rational torsion point. In other words, we work over the families of elliptic curves for which
G=E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tor}}
is non-trivial. Due to the celebrated theorem of Mazur
[
23
]
, it is enough to study the following
14
possibilities of
G
:

(3)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}}

\displaystyle\textrm{with $2\leq n\leq 10$ or $n=12$}

\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}}

\displaystyle\textrm{with $n=2,4,6,8$.}

Note that Caro and Pasten
[
9
]
, later Pasupulati and both of the authors of this article in
[

is one of the
10

2.
Tools with modular degrees and ranks

In this section, we will discuss the necessary materials to help count the elliptic curves satisfying Watkins’s conjecture.

2.1.
Dummigan, Krishnamoorthy’s lower bound

For each
p^{\alpha}\mid\mid N
, consider the Atkin-Lehner involution
W_{p}
on
X_{0}(N)
. The set
\left\{W_{p}~:~d\mid\mid N\right\}
forms an abelian subgroup of automorphisms of rank equal to
\omega(N)
. For every prime divisor
p\mid N
, there exists
w_{p}(f_{E})\in\{\pm 1\}
such that
W_{p}(f_{E})=w_{p}(f_{E})f_{E}
.

We now consider the homomorphism
({\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})^{\omega(N)}\to\{\pm 1\}
defined by,
W_{d}\mapsto w_{d}
. Let
W^{\prime}
denote the kernel of this map. According to Dummigan and Krishnamoorthy
[
14
, Prop. 2.1]
, there exists a homomorphism
W^{\prime}\to E(\mathbb{Q})[2]
with kernel
W^{\prime\prime}
, and
m_{E}
divides the order of
W^{\prime\prime}
.

The modular parametrization gives a map
\pi:J_{0}(n)\to E
. The key observation here is that if
w\in W^{\prime}
, then
\pi(w(D))=\pi(D)
for any
D\in J_{0}(N)
. Taking
P_{0}\in X_{0}(N)(\mathbb{Q})
and
D=P_{0}-\omega(P_{0})\in J_{0}(N)
, we have
\pi(w(D))\in E(\mathbb{Q})[2]
. This yields a map
W^{\prime}\to E[2]
with kernel
W^{\prime\prime}
. Consequently, considering
X^{\prime\prime}=X_{0}(N)/W^{\prime\prime}
and
J^{\prime\prime}=J(X^{\prime\prime})
, the map
\pi
induces a map
\pi^{\prime\prime}:J^{\prime\prime}\to E
such that
\deg(\pi)=\#W^{\prime\prime}\deg(\pi^{\prime\prime})
. Following this, Dummigan and Krishnamoorthy
[
14
, Prop. 2.1]
deduced the following estimate.

\displaystyle\nu_{2}(m_{E})\geq\nu_{2}\left(\frac{\#W^{\prime}}{E(\mathbb{Q})[2]}\right)

\displaystyle=\nu_{2}\left({\#W^{\prime}}\right)-\nu_{2}\left({E(\mathbb{Q})[2]}\right)

\displaystyle=\omega(N(E))-\dim_{{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}}(W/W^{\prime})-\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}}(E(\mathbb{Q})[2]).

In particular, we deduced the following, that will be used for the treatment over
{\mathcal{E}}_{2}
.

Corollary 2.1
.

For any elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
with
E(\mathbb{Q})[2]={\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
, we have the following inequality

\nu_{2}(m_{E})\geq\omega(N(E))-1-\dim_{{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}}(W/W^{\prime})\geq\omega(N(E))-2.

Remark 1
.

Recall that for an elliptic curve
E_{A,B}
in its minimal form as in (
1
), the corresponding conductor
N(E_{A,B})
is, up to some bounded factor of
2
and
3

p
dividing the discriminant
\Delta(E_{A,B})
. In light of Corollary
2.1
, our primary objective is to count the set of all such minimal

, for which

is divisible by many large primes. Additionally, we also need to study the rank growth to study the

-Watkins elliptic curves.

2.3.
Descent via 2-isogeny

Let

be of the form

, and

be the dual curve, defined by

Then, we have the following key bound from

:

(4)

\rank\left(E(\mathbb{Q})\right)\leq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{2}}\Sel^{\phi}(E)+\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{2}}\Sel^{\hat{\phi}}(E^{\prime})-2,

where the Selmer groups are precisely of the following forms.

(5)

\Sel^{\phi}(E_{a,b})=\{d\in Q(T_{1}):C_{d,\frac{a^{2}-4b}{d},-2a}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\neq\phi,~\forall p\mid 2b(a^{2}-4b)\},

(6)

\Sel^{\hat{\phi}}(E^{\prime}_{a,b})=\{d\in Q(T_{2}):C_{d,\frac{b}{d},a}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\neq\phi,~\forall p\mid 2b(a^{2}-4b)\},

where

(resp.

) is the set of prime factors of

(resp.

), containing

. It follows from the proof of

(7)

\Sel^{\phi}(E_{a,b})\subset Q(T_{1}\setminus\{\infty\}),~\mathrm{or}~\Sel^{\hat{\phi}}(E^{\prime}_{a,b})\subset Q(T_{2}\setminus\{\infty\}).

With this observation, we deduce the following.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
a,b
be any two co-prime integers for which
\Sel^{\phi}(E_{a,b})\subset Q(T\setminus\{p\})
for some finite prime

. Then, we have

(8)

\rank\left(E_{a,b}(\mathbb{Q})\right)\leq\omega(N(E_{a,b}))-2.

Proof.

Given that
\Sel^{\phi}(E_{a,b})\subset Q(T\setminus\{p\})
for some finite prime
p
, it follows immediately from equations (
4
) and (

) that
\rank\left(E_{a,b}(\mathbb{Q})\right)\leq\omega(a^{2}-4b)+\omega(b)-1

\rank\left(E_{a,b}(\mathbb{Q})\right)\leq\omega(b^{2}(a^{2}-4b))-1

\omega(b^{2}(a^{2}-4b))\leq\omega(N(E_{a,b}))
. This completes the proof.
∎

Definition 3

Let

From

where

For any integer

To bound the Selmer ranks in (

For any pair

\asymp X^{5}

3.1.
Counting over
{\mathcal{E}}_{2}

We know
[
17
, Lem. 5.1, part (a)]
that an elliptic curve
E_{A,B}
has a rational point of order
2
if and only if there exists
a,b\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
A=a,B=b^{3}+ab
. This leads us to consider the lattice points in the region
R_{2}(X)=\Big\{(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:|a|<X^{2}\text{ and }|b^{3}+ab|<X^{3}\Big\}

\mathcal{E}_{2}(X)=R_{2}(X)\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
. By the principle of Lipschitz
[
11
]

(13)

\#{\mathcal{E}}_{2}(X)=\#(R_{2}(X)\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{2})\sim\mathrm{Vol}(R_{2}(1))X^{3}.

Moreover,
[
17
, Lem. 5.2]
shows that where the numerical value of
\mathrm{Vol}(R_{2}(1))
is precisely given by

(14)

2\log(\alpha_{-}/\alpha_{+})+\frac{4}{3}(\alpha_{+}+\alpha_{-}),~\mathrm{where~\alpha_{\pm}~is~the~unique~real~root~of}~x^{3}\pm x-1.

In this section, let us denote
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{2}
be the set of all elliptic curves
E_{a,b}:=y^{2}=x^{3}+ax^{2}+bx
. First of all, let us note that any

can be written in the Weierstrass form

. This shows that
\mathcal{E}_{2}\subset\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{2}
. Let us arrange the elliptic curves in
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{2}
, with respect to the natural height function defined by
H_{2}(E_{a,b})=\max\left\{|a|^{2},|b|\right\}^{1/2}
. With this, we set

R^{\prime}_{2}(X)=\Big\{(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:|a|\leq X,~|b|\leq X^{2}\Big\},~\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{2}(X)=R^{\prime}_{2}(X)\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}.

Note that
H(E_{A,B})\leq X
if
|a|\asymp X^{2}
and
|b|\asymp X
, where
A=a,~B=b^{3}+ab
. In other words,

if and only if

. In particular, the number of elements in
\mathcal{E}_{2}(X)
and

\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{2}(X)
are asymptotically comparable to

, i.e.,
\#\mathcal{E}_{2}(X)\asymp\#\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{2}(X)\asymp X^{3}
.

Furthermore, let us consider
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}_{1}=\{(A,B):\mathrm{gcd}(A,B)=1\}
, and set

\mathcal{E}_{1,2}(X)=\mathcal{E}_{2}(X)\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}_{1},~\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{1,2}(X)=\mathcal{E}_{2}(X)\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}_{1}.

The requirement of considering co-prime pairs arises due to Corollary
2.2

\#\mathcal{E}_{1,2}(X)\asymp\#\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{1,2}(X)\asymp X^{3}

to conclude the proof of Theorem

{\mathcal{E}}_{2}

4.1.
Bounding Selmer ranks

In this section, we want to eliminate sufficiently many hall divisors
d
of
a^{2}-4b
for which
C_{d,\frac{a^{2}-4b}{d}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})=\phi
for some prime
p\mid a^{2}-4b
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
p>3
be a prime dividing
b
such that
p\nmid a
and
d
be a square-free hall divisor of
a^{2}-4b
. Then
C_{d,\frac{a^{2}-4b}{d}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})=\phi
if and only if the following two conditions hold.

(i)

\left(\frac{d}{p}\right)=-1

(ii)

\nu_{p}(b)=\mathrm{odd}~\mathrm{or}~\left(\frac{a}{p}\right)=1

Proof.

Our goal is to ensure that none of the conditions in part (1) of
[
1
, Lem. 3.1]
are satisfied.

Given that
p\mid b
, it is important to observe that
\left(\frac{d}{p}\right)=-1
if and only if
\left(\frac{\frac{a^{2}-4b}{d}}{p}\right)=-1
. Due to the condition in
(i)
, we see that the condition (a) in part (1) of
[
1
, Lem. 3.1]
is not satisfied, with
d_{1}=d
and
d_{2}=\frac{a^{2}-4b}{d}
.

On the other hand, it is worth noting that the negation of condition (b) in part (1) of
[
1
, Lem. 3.1]
is equivalent to having
\nu_{p}(b)=\mathrm{odd}
or
\left(\frac{a}{p}\right)=1
, both of which are the assumptions that we have already made.
∎

Now the idea is that we count the number of
(a,b)
satisfying the property: there exists at least one prime
q\mid s(a^{2}-b)
, for which there is a prime
p
with
\left(\frac{q}{p}\right)=-1
and
\left(\frac{a}{p}\right)=1
. For this, it is enough to eliminate the pairs
(a,b)
with the property: given any pair of signs
(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})
, for any prime
q\mid s(a^{2}-b)
, and for any prime
p
satisfying
\left(\frac{a}{p}\right)=\sigma_{2}
, we have
\left(\frac{q}{p}\right)=\sigma_{1}
. In particular, our job will be done by taking any pairs of signs
(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})\neq(-1,1)
.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\sigma_{2}\in\{\pm 1\}
be any sign,
a\in{\mathbb{Z}}
be an integer which is not a square, and
p
be a prime with
\left(\frac{a}{p}\right)=\sigma_{2}
. Then, we have the following estimate, as we take
X\to\infty

N_{a}(X)=\#\Big\{b\in{\mathbb{Z}}:|b|\leq X,~q~\mathrm{prime}\mid s(a^{2}-b)\implies\left(\frac{q}{p}\right)=\sigma_{1}\Big\}=O\left((X+a^{2})^{1/2}+\frac{X+a^{2}}{(\log(X+a^{2}))^{1/2}}\right),

where the implicit constant is absolute. In particular, we have the estimate as
X\to\infty

(17)

\Big\{E_{a,b}:H_{2}(E_{a,b})\leq X,~q~\mathrm{prime}\mid s(a^{2}-b)\implies\left(\frac{q}{p}\right)=\sigma_{1}\Big\}=O\left(\frac{X^{3}}{\log X}\right),

where the implicit constant is absolute.

Proof.

Given any
a
and
p
as per the requirement, the set of primes
q
with
\left(\frac{q}{p}\right)=1
, has density
1/2
. In other words, we need to demonstrate that for a rare integer
b
, all the prime factors of
s(a^{2}-b)
belong to a set of primes with density
1/2
. For any parameter
y
, the number of integers
0<n\leq y
for which all the prime factors of
n
belong to a set of primes with density
1/2
grows asymptotically like
\frac{y}{\sqrt{\log y}}
. This observation shows that

\displaystyle N_{a}(X)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n\leq(X+a^{2})^{1/2}}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\frac{X+a^{2}}{(\log(X+a^{2})-\log n^{2})^{1/2}}

(18)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(X+a^{2})^{1/4}<n\leq(X+a^{2})^{1/2}}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\frac{X+a^{2}}{(\log(X+a^{2})-\log n^{2})^{1/2}}+\sum_{n\leq(X+a^{2})^{1/4}}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\frac{X+a^{2}}{(\log(X+a^{2})-\log n^{2})^{1/2}}.

It is evident that the first sum in (
18
) is
O((x+a^{2})^{1/2})
. Moreover, the second sum in (
18
) is
O\left(\frac{X+a^{2}}{(\log(X+a^{2}))^{1/2}}\right)
, and this gives the desired estimate for the quantity
N_{a}(X)
.

Let us now recall from Section
3.1
that
H_{2}(E_{a,b})\leq X
implies
|a|\leq X
and
|b|\leq X^{2}
. The required estimate at (
17
) then follows directly by estimating the sum
\sum\limits_{a\leq X}N_{a}(X^{2})
.
∎

Consequently, we obtain the following implication on the
\phi
-Selmer ranks.

Corollary 4.1
.

For a positive proportion of elliptic curves in
{\mathcal{E}}_{2}
, we have

\mathrm{rank}(E(\mathbb{Q}))\leq\omega(N(E))-2
.

Proof.

Lemma
2
implies that, for almost all pairs
(a,b)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
, there exist primes
p,q
(dependent on
a,b
) such that
\nu_{q}(a^{2}-4b)=\mathrm{odd}
, and the divisor
d=q^{\nu_{q}(a^{2}-4b)}
satisfies
\left(\frac{d}{p}\right)=-1
. Specifically, Lemma
1
implies
C_{d,\frac{a^{2}-4b}{d}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})=\phi
. Hence, the proof follows combining Lemma
2
and Corollary
2.2
.
∎

To prove the main result of this section, we need one more result.

Lemma 3
.

The
2
-torsion group for
100\%
of the all elliptic curves in
{\mathcal{E}}_{2}
is
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Proof.

It is enough to establish that, for
100\%
of all pairs
(a,b)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
, the corresponding quadratic equation
x^{2}+ax+b=0
has no rational solutions. In particular, this means that the discriminant
a^{2}-4b
is not a perfect square. This assertion is evident since, for any fixed square
S
, there are at most
O(X)
pairs
(a,b)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
satisfying
|a|\leq X
,
|b|\leq X^{2}
, and
a^{2}-4b=S
. However, since there are only
O(X)
choices for
S
, this completes the argument.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1

Lemma
3
and Corollary
2.1
imply that, for almost all elliptic curves
E
in
{\mathcal{E}}_{2}
, we have
\omega(N(E))-2\leq\nu_{2}(m_{E})
. Therefore, the result can be deduced from Corollary
4.1
.
∎

4.2.
M
-Watkins over
\mathcal{E}_{2}

For any integer
M
, and for each integer
a
which is not a perfect square, we consider the following set of primes
\mathbb{P}_{a}=\{p~\mathrm{prime}:\left(\frac{a}{p}\right)=1\}
. We pick the least prime
p>3
from
\mathbb{P}_{a}
and denote it as
P(a)
. For any integer
i
, we also denote
q_{i}
to be the
i
th least prime for which
\left(\frac{q_{i}}{p}\right)=-1
, and set
d_{i}=q_{i},~\forall 1\leq i\leq M
. We then consider
N_{a}=\Big\{b\in{\mathbb{Z}}:\nu_{q_{i}}(a^{2}-4b)=1,~\forall 1\leq i\leq M\Big\}.

Lemma 4
.

For any integer
a
that is not a square, and and for any
b\in N_{M}(a)
which is co-prime to
a
, we have the following rank bound

\mathrm{rank}(E_{a,b}(\mathbb{Q}))\leq\omega(N(E_{a,b}))-M.

Proof.

Lemma
1
implies that the
\phi
-Selmer rank in (
5
) is at most
\omega(a^{2}-4b)-M
. Furthermore, the
\hat{\phi}
-Selmer rank in (
6
) of course at most
\omega(b)
. Since we assume that
a,b
are co-prime,
\omega(b(a^{2}-4b))\leq\omega(N(E_{a,b}))+1
. The result now follows from the rank bound at (
8
).
∎

Now, we set the quantity
d_{1}d_{2}\cdots d_{M}
to be
D_{M}(a)
, and then we have the following estimation of
D_{M}(a)
.

Lemma 5
.

For any integer
a\equiv 2\pmod{5}
that is not a square, we have
D_{M}(A)\asymp M^{M}
.

Proof.

For any
a\equiv 2\pmod{5}
, we have
P(a)=5
. Now we choose
q_{i}
as the
i
th least prime for which
q_{i}\equiv 2\pmod{5}
. This shows that,

D_{M}(a)\asymp_{M}\prod\limits_{1\leq i\leq M}i\log i\sim M^{M}
, as desired.
∎

Now, for the counting purposes, we denote

N_{M}(X)=\#\{a,b\in{\mathbb{Z}}:H_{2}(E_{a,b})\leq X,~\mathrm{rank}(E_{a,b}(\mathbb{Q}))\leq\omega(a^{2}-4b)-M\}.

Lemma 6
.

For any integer
M
, we have
N_{M}(X)\geq\frac{4}{5}\frac{1}{(2M)^{M}}X^{3}
, as
X\to\infty
.

Proof.

Lemma
4
implies that,

\displaystyle N_{M}(X)

\displaystyle\geq\#\Big\{a,b\in{\mathbb{Z}}:(a,b)=1,~|a|\leq X,~|b|\leq X^{2},~\nu_{q_{i}}(a^{2}-4b)=1,~\forall 1\leq i\leq M\Big\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{|a|\leq X}\#\Big\{b\in{\mathbb{Z}}:(b,a)=1,~|b|\leq X^{2},~q_{i}\mid a^{2}-4b,~q_{i}^{2}\nmid a^{2}-4b,~\forall 1\leq i\leq M\Big\}

\displaystyle=2X^{2}\left(\sum_{|a|\leq X}\frac{\phi(a)}{a}\prod_{i=1}^{M}\left(\frac{1}{q_{i}}-\frac{1}{q_{i}^{2}}\right)\right)+O_{M}(X)

\displaystyle\geq 2X^{2}\left(\sum_{|a|\leq X}\frac{\phi(a)}{a}\prod_{i=1}^{M}\frac{1}{2q_{i}}\right)+O_{M}(X)

\displaystyle\geq 2X^{2}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|a|\leq X\\
a\equiv 2\pmod{5}\end{subarray}}\frac{\phi(a)}{a}\frac{1}{2^{M}D_{M}(a)}\right)+O_{M}(X)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2M)^{M}}\frac{1}{\zeta(2)}\frac{4}{5}X^{3}+O_{M}(X^{2}\log X),

and this completes the proof, as desired.
∎

4.3.
Proof of Theorem
2

The proof follows combing Lemma
4
and Lemma
6
.∎

In the introduction, we highlighted that Watkins’s conjecture is verified for elliptic curves having a rank no greater than

. This serves as our inspiration to investigate pairs

In this section, we specifically focus on the elliptic curve denoted as

, we have
\sum\limits_{m>2}S_{m}(X,Y)=O(XY)
, where the implicit constant is absolute. Now, we need to estimate
S_{1}(X,Y)
and
S_{2}(X,Y)
. Note that,

(34)

\displaystyle S_{2}(X,Y)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}r=a/b\in\mathbb{Q}\\
|a|\leq X,~|b|\leq Y\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{p\leq e^{\lambda/2}}F_{\lambda}(2\log p)\frac{a_{p^{2}}(E_{f(r),g(r)})\log p}{p^{2}}=-2XY\lambda+o(XY),

where the last estimate follows from
[
28
, Lem. 4.2]
. Now for the estimation of
S_{1}
, note that

\displaystyle S_{1}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{|b|\leq Y}\sum\limits_{|a|\leq X}\sum\limits_{p\leq e^{\lambda}}F_{\lambda}(\log p)\frac{a_{p}(E_{f_{b}(a),g_{b}(a)})\log p}{p}

(35)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{|b|\leq Y}\sum\limits_{p\leq e^{\lambda}}F_{\lambda}(\log p)\frac{2X}{p}A_{p}(\mathcal{E}_{b})+\sum\limits_{|b|\leq Y}\sum\limits_{p\leq e^{\lambda}}\sum_{\bar{r}\in I_{p}\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}}F_{\lambda}(\log p)\frac{\log p}{p}a_{p}(E_{f_{b}(\bar{r}),g_{b}(\bar{r})}),

for some subset
I_{p}
of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Lemma
12
and
[
28
, Cor. 3.2]
implies that the first sum above is

(36)

-\left(X\lambda+o(X\lambda)\right)\sum\limits_{|b|\leq Y}\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal{E}_{b}(\mathbb{Q}(t)))=-XY\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal{E}(\mathbb{Q}(t)))(2\lambda+o(1)).

Now, the inner sum in the second sum in (
35
) is crudely bounded by
\sum\limits_{p\leq e^{\lambda}}p^{1/2}\log p=e^{3\lambda/2}
. In particular, now combining (
33
), (
34
), and (
35
), we have

R_{f,g}(X,Y)\leq\frac{\mathrm{deg}(N(\mathcal{E}_{f,~g}))}{\lambda}XY\log X-XY\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal{E}(\mathbb{Q}(t)))(2+o(1/\lambda))+Ye^{3\lambda/2}.

Now the proof follows taking
\lambda=\frac{\log X}{3}
.
∎

As an immediate consequence, we deduce that the most elliptic curves in the
1
-parameter family as in Proposition
5.1
have a small rank. More specifically, we have the following.

Proof of Lemma
10

Take
E\in\mathcal{E}_{\ell}(X)
, and take the corresponding
r\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\ell}(X)
. We have

\mathrm{rank}(E)=\mathrm{rank}(E_{f_{\ell}(r),g_{\ell}(r)})
. With this observation, it follows from Lemma
9
that

(37)

\displaystyle\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{\ell}(X)}\mathrm{rank}(E)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r=a/b~\in~\mathbb{Q}\\
|a|\leq CX^{m_{\ell}},~|b|\leq CX^{n_{\ell}}\end{subarray}}\mathrm{rank}(E_{f_{\ell}(r),~g_{\ell}(r)})

\displaystyle=R_{f_{\ell},g_{\ell}}(CX^{m_{\ell}},X^{n_{\ell}})=O(X^{m_{\ell}+n_{\ell}})

where

\#\mathcal{E}_{\ell}(X)\asymp X^{m_{\ell}+n_{\ell}}
.

5.3.
Normal number of prime factors of square-free parts of polynomial values

Let
f(t)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[t]
be any polynomial of degree
d
. At any rational
r\in\mathbb{Q}
, let us denote
s(f(r))
to be the square-free part of
f(r)
. Poonen
[
26
]
, explored the prevalence of square-free values in the polynomials. Over the integers
n
, Poonen investigated scenarios where
f(n)
is not divisible by any fixed square
m^{2}\neq 1
, utilizing the ABC conjecture. However, in our context, our focus is on understanding how frequently the square-free parts of
f(r)
possess a substantial number of prime factors. First of all, for any rational
r=\frac{a}{b}
, let us denote the height
h(r)=\max\{|a|,|b|\}
. Then we arrange all the rationals with respect to height
h
and prove the following.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
M\geq 1
be any integer, and
f(t)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[t]
be any irreducible non-constant polynomial. Then, for almost all the rationals
r\in\mathbb{Q}
, we have
\omega(s(f(r)))\geq M
. More precisely, we have the following estimate:

\#\Big\{r\in\mathbb{Q}:h(r)\leq X,~\omega(s(f(r)))<M\Big\}=O_{M}\left(\frac{X^{2}}{\log\log X}\right).

Proposition
5.2
will be one of our key tools to prove the main results of this article. To prove the same, we need the following two results.

Lemma 13
.

Let
f(t)\in\mathbb{Z}[t]
be a non-constant irreducible polynomial, and
p
be any given prime. Then the number of solutions to the equation
f(\bar{r})=0\pmod{p^{2}}
over
\bar{r}\in\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z}
is bounded by
2\mathrm{deg}(f)
, for any prime
p\nmid\mathrm{gcd}(c(f),c(f^{\prime}))
. Here
f^{\prime}
is the derivative of
f
, and
c(f)
(resp.
c(f^{\prime})
) denotes the
\mathrm{gcd}
of the coefficients of
f
(resp.
\mathrm{gcd}
of the coefficients of
f^{\prime}
).

Proof.

We can express any solution
\bar{r}\in\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z}
as
\bar{r}_{1}+p\bar{r}_{2}
, where
0\leq\bar{r}_{1},\bar{r}_{2}\leq p-1
. Using this representation, we have
f(\bar{r})=f(\bar{r}_{1})+p\bar{r}_{2}f^{\prime}(\bar{r}_{1})\pmod{p^{2}}
. Since
f(\bar{r})=0\pmod{p^{2}}
, we have
f(\bar{r}_{1})=0\pmod{p}
, indicating that there are at most
\mathrm{deg}(f)
possibilities for
0\leq\bar{r}_{1}\leq p-1
.

Now to count the number of possibilities of
\bar{r}_{2}
, without loss of generality let us assume that
\bar{r}_{2}\neq 0
. If
p
divides
f^{\prime}(\bar{r}_{1})
, then
p
divides
\mathrm{gcd}(f(\bar{r}_{1}),f^{\prime}(\bar{r}_{1}))
. But since
f(t)\in\mathbb{Q}[t]
is irreducible, we know that the
\mathrm{gcd}
of
f
and
f^{\prime}
over
\mathbb{Q}[t]
is
1
, and hence, the gcd of
f
and
f^{\prime}
over
{\mathbb{Z}}[t]
is
\mathrm{gcd}(c(f),c(f^{\prime}))
. In particular,
p\mid\mathrm{gcd}(c(f),c(f^{\prime}))
. This is not possible, as per the assumption on
p
. Therefore, we may assume that
p
does not divide
f^{\prime}(\bar{r}_{1})
. Now for each possible
\bar{r}_{1}
, setting
f(\bar{r}_{1})=p\bar{t}_{1}
, we can write
\bar{r}_{2}=-\bar{t}_{1}f^{\prime}(\bar{r}_{1})^{-1}
. In particular,
\bar{r}_{2}
is uniquely determined by
\bar{r}_{1}
, as long as
\bar{r}_{2}\neq 0
. This shows that the number of possible pairs
(\bar{r}_{1},\bar{r}_{2})
is only
2\mathrm{deg}(f)
, for any prime
p
not dividing
\mathrm{gcd}(c(f),c(f^{\prime}))
.
∎

Lemma 14
.

Consider
S
to be the set of all prime factors of the leading coefficient of
f
. Then, we have the following estimate:

\sum_{h(r)\leq X}\Big|\omega_{S}(s(f(r)))-\log\log X\Big|^{2}=O_{S}\left(X^{2}\log\log X\right).

Proof.

Let us start by noting that

\displaystyle\sum_{h(r)\leq X}\omega_{S}(f(r))

\displaystyle=\sum_{h(r)\leq X}~\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p~\mathrm{prime}~\not\in~S\\
\nu_{p}(f(r))=\mathrm{odd}\end{subarray}}1+~\sum_{h(r)\leq X}~\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p~\mathrm{prime}~\not\in~S\\
\nu_{p}(f(r))\neq 0\\
\nu_{p}(f(r))=\mathrm{even}\end{subarray}}1

(39)

\displaystyle=\sum_{h(r)\leq X}\omega_{S}(s(f(r))~+\sum_{h(r)\leq X}~\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p~\mathrm{prime}~\not\in~S\\
\nu_{p}(f(r))\neq 0\\
\nu_{p}(f(r))=\mathrm{even}\end{subarray}}1.

For any rational number
r
, let us denote
r_{p}=r\pmod{p}
. Then we have,

(40)

\nu_{p}(f(r))>0\implies r_{p}\in\mathbb{F}_{p},~\mathrm{and}~p\not\in S\implies\nu_{p}(f(r))<0,~\mathrm{if~and~only~if},~r_{p}=\infty.

This shows that

\displaystyle\sum_{h(r)\leq X}\omega_{S}(f(r))

\displaystyle=\sum_{h(r)\leq X}~~~\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p~\mathrm{prime}~\not\in~S\\
\nu_{p}(f(r))>0\end{subarray}}1+\sum_{h(r)\leq X}~\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p~\mathrm{prime}~\not\in~S\\
\nu_{p}(f(r))<0\end{subarray}}1

(41)

\displaystyle=\sum_{h(r)\leq X}~~\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p~\mathrm{prime}~\not\in~S\\
r_{p}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\\
\nu_{p}(f(r))>0\end{subarray}}1+\sum_{h(r)\leq X}~\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p~\mathrm{prime}~\not\in~S\\
r_{p}=\infty\end{subarray}}1.

It is now enough to estimate the second sum in (
39
), and both the sums in (
41
).

Case 1: estimate of the first sum in (
41
)

For this, let us note that for any rational
r-\frac{a}{b}
, we have

(42)

f\left(\frac{a+p}{b}\right)=f\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)\pmod{p},~f\left(\frac{a}{b+p}\right)=f\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)\pmod{p}.

Again, since we are assuming that
r_{p}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
, we can write the following combining with (
42
):

\displaystyle\sum_{h(r)\leq X}\omega_{S}(f(r))=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq X\\
p~\not\in~S\end{subarray}}\left(X^{2}\frac{\rho_{f}(p)}{p}+O(\rho_{f}(p))\right)=X^{2}\log\log X+O(X^{2}),

where
\rho_{f}(p)=\{\bar{r}\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}:f(\bar{r})=0\pmod{p}\}
, and the last equality follows from
[
10
, Cor. 3.2.2]
. Furthermore, we can run the summation above for
p
up to at most
X
because
h(f(r))=X^{O(1)}
, and there are only
O(1)
many primes
p
for which
\nu_{p}(f(r))\neq 0
, and
O(1)\leq\deg(f)
.

Case 2: estimate of the second sum in (
41
)

For a rational
r
, denote
d(r)
to be the denominator of
r
in the lowest term. Then, we have

(43)

\sum\limits_{h(r)\leq X}~\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p~\mathrm{prime}~\not\in~S\\
r_{p}=\infty\end{subarray}}1=\sum_{h(r)\leq X}\omega_{S}(d(r))=X^{2}\log\log X+O(X^{2}),

where the last equality follows by the proof of Corollary 3.2.2 in
[
10
]
.

Case 3: estimate of the second sum in (
39
)

Let us write

\sum_{h(r)\leq X}~\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p~\mathrm{prime}~\not\in~S\\
\nu_{p}(f(r))\neq 0\\
\nu_{p}(f(r))=\mathrm{even}\end{subarray}}1=\sum_{h(r)\leq X}~\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p~\mathrm{prime}~\not\in~S\\
\nu_{p}(f(r))>0\\
\nu_{p}(f(r))=\mathrm{even}\end{subarray}}1+~\sum_{h(r)\leq X}~\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p~\mathrm{prime}~\not\in~S\\
\nu_{p}(f(r))<0\\
\nu_{p}(f(r))=\mathrm{even}\end{subarray}}1.

The second sum above is, of course, bounded by (
43
). To bound the first sum, we use Lemma
13
, and get

(44)

\sum_{h(r)\leq X}~\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p~\mathrm{prime}~\not\in~S\\
\nu_{p}(f(r))>0\\
\nu_{p}(f(r))=\mathrm{even}\end{subarray}}1\leq\sum_{p\leq X}\left(X^{2}\frac{\rho_{f}(p^{2})}{p^{2}}+O(\rho_{f}(p^{2}))\right)=O(X^{2}),

where
\rho_{f}(p^{2})=\{\bar{r}\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{2}{\mathbb{Z}}:f(\bar{r})=0\pmod{p^{2}}\}
, and this is at most
\mathrm{deg}(f)^{2}
, due to Lemma
13
.
Therefore, combining (
39
), (
41
), (
43
), and (
44
), we get
\sum\limits_{h(r)\leq X}\omega_{S}(s(f(r)))=X^{2}\log\log X+O(X^{2})
. To complete the proof of the lemma, it is now enough to show that

(45)

\sum\limits_{h(r)\leq X}\omega_{S}(s(f(r)))^{2}\leq(X\log\log X)^{2}+O(X^{2})

Since
\omega(f(r))\leq\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p~\mathrm{prime}\\
\nu_{p}(f(r))>0\end{subarray}}1+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p~\mathrm{prime}\\
r_{p}=\infty\end{subarray}}1
, we get,
\sum\limits_{h(r)\leq X}\omega_{S}(s(f(r)))^{2}\leq\sum\limits_{h(r)\leq X}\omega(f(r))^{2}\leq 2(S_{1}+S_{2})
,
where

(46)

S_{1}=\sum\limits_{h(r)\leq X}\Big(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p~\mathrm{prime}\\
\nu_{p}(f(r))>0\end{subarray}}1\Big)^{2},~\mathrm{and}~S_{2}=\sum\limits_{h(r)\leq X}\Big(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p~\mathrm{prime}\\
r_{p}=\infty\end{subarray}}1\Big)^{2}.

For any suitable parameter
Y\leq X
, applying Chinese remainder theorem, we have

\displaystyle S_{1}(Y)=\sum\limits_{h(r)\leq X}\Big(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p~\mathrm{prime}~\leq Y\\
\nu_{p}(f(r))>0\end{subarray}}1\Big)^{2}=X\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\neq q~\mathrm{primes}\\
p,q\leq Y\end{subarray}}X\frac{\rho_{f}(p)\rho_{f}(q)}{pq}+O(1)\Big)=(X\log\log Y)^{2}+O(XY^{2}),

\displaystyle S_{2}(Y)=\sum\limits_{h(r)\leq X}\Big(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p~\mathrm{prime}~\leq Y\\
r_{p}=\infty\end{subarray}}1\Big)^{2}=X\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\neq q~\mathrm{primes}\\
p,q\leq Y\end{subarray}}X\frac{1}{pq}+O(1)\Big)=(X\log\log Y)^{2}+O(XY^{2}).

The proof of equation (
45
), and consequently the proof of the lemma is now complete taking
Y=X^{1/2}
, as we have

|S_{1}(Y)-S_{1}|,~|S_{2}(Y)-S_{2}|=O(X^{2}).

∎

Proof of Proposition
5.2

Denote
S_{M}(X)
to be the number of
r\in\mathbb{Q}
such that
\omega(s(f(r)))\leq M
, and
h(r)\leq X
. It follows from Lemma
14
that
S_{M}(X)=O_{M}\left(\frac{X^{2}}{\log\log X}\right)
. This completes the proof of the proposition. ∎

Remark 4
.

Note that Proposition
5.2
addresses the number of prime factors in the square-free parts of the values of
f
. In contrast,
[
26
]
focuses on square-free values. When we restrict the evaluation of
f
to integers,
[
26
, Thm. 3.2]
shows that the proportion of square-free values of
f
is precisely
\prod\limits_{p,~\mathrm{prime}}\left(1-\frac{c_{p}}{p^{2}}\right)
, where the quantity
c_{p}
is studied in Lemma
13
. In particular, the quantity
\prod\limits_{p,~\mathrm{prime}}\left(1-\frac{c_{p}}{p^{2}}\right)
, is positive. In other words, if
f(t)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[t]
is irreducible, then
f(n)
is square-free for a positive proportion of the times.

Finally, let us address the last preparatory counting tool, that is needed to prove the main result of this section.

Lemma 15
.

For any
\ell\in\{3,5,7\}
, and any integer
M
, we have the following estimate:

\#\Big\{(A,B)\in S_{f_{\ell},g_{\ell}}(X):\omega(N(E_{A,B}))\leq M\Big\}=O_{M}\left(\frac{\#\mathcal{E}_{\ell}(X)}{\log\log X}\right).

Proof.

Consider an elliptic curve
E_{A,B}
in the minimal form. Then the conductor
E_{A,B}
is (up to some bounded factor of
2
and
3
) the product over all primes

p
dividing the discriminant
|\Delta(E_{A,B})|=|4A^{3}+27B^{2}|
. Therefore, it suffices to estimate the quantity,
\{(A,B)\in S_{f,g}(X):\omega(4A^{3}+27B^{2})\leq M+2\}
. Now Lemma
9
implies that it is enough to estimate for each
\ell\in\{3,5,7\}
,

\Big\{r=a/b:|a|\leq X^{m_{\ell}},~|b|\leq X^{n_{\ell}}~\omega(4s(f(r)^{3}+27g(r)^{2}))\leq M+2\Big\}.

Denoting
\Delta(r)=4f(r)^{3}+27g(r)^{2}
, it follows from Lemma
11
that there exists an irreducible factor
F(t)
of
\Delta(t)
that divides
\Delta(t)
with a valuation of exactly
1
. We then have:

\displaystyle\#\Big\{r=a/b:|a|\leq X^{m_{\ell}},|b|\leq X^{n_{\ell}},~

\displaystyle\omega(s(\Delta(r)))\leq M+2\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq\#\Big\{r=a/b:|a|\leq X^{m_{\ell}},|b|\leq X^{n_{\ell}},~\omega(F(r))\leq M+2\Big\}

\displaystyle=O_{M}\left(\frac{X^{\max\{m_{\ell},n_{\ell}\}}}{\log\log X}\right)=O_{M}\left(\frac{\#\mathcal{E}_{\ell}(X)}{\log\log X}\right),

where the last estimate follows from Proposition
5.2
, and the fact that
\#\mathcal{E}_{\ell}(X)\asymp X^{m_{\ell}+n_{\ell}}
.
∎

.

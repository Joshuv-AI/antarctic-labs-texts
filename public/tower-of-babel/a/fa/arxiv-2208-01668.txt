Multiband Gravitational Wave Cosmography with Dark Sirens
Brian C. Seymour, Hang Yu, Yanbei Chen

Abstract
Gravitational waves might help resolve the tension between early and late Universe measurements of the Hubble constant, and this possibility can be enhanced with a gravitational wave detector in the decihertz band as we will demonstrate in this study. Such a detector is particularly suitable for the multiband observation of stellar-mass black hole binaries between space and ground, which would significantly improve the source localization accuracy thanks to a long baseline for timing triangulation, hence promoting the "dark siren" cosmology. Proposed decihertz concepts include DECIGO/B-DECIGO, TianGO, and others. We consider here the prospects of multiband observation of dark siren binaries with a variety of network configurations. We find that a multiband observation can uniquely identify a black hole binary to a single galaxy to a cosmological distance, and thus a dark siren behaves as if it had an electromagnetic counterpart. Considering only fully localized dark sirens, we use a Fisher matrix approach to estimate the error in the Hubble constant and matter density parameter. We find that a decihertz detector substantially improves our ability to measure cosmological parameters because it enables host galaxies to be identified out to a larger distance without the systematics from statistical techniques based on comparing the population distribution.



I
Introduction

The Hubble-Lemaître constant
H_{0}
describes the current expansion rate of the universe.
Currently, there is substantial deviation between Planck measurements of the cosmic microwave background fluctuations
Aghanim et al. 2020
and SH0ES measurements of Type 1a supernova with the distance ladder
Riess et al. 2021a
;
Riess et al. 2016
. Notably, the Hubble tension between these early and late universe measurements differs by at least
4\sigma

Verde et al. 2019
;
Camarena and Marra 2021
. Moreover, the tension has occurred since the first Planck results
Ade et al. 2014
and strengthened with time. It is important to validate whether such Hubble tension truly exists or whether it is due to astrophysical systematics because it could signify violation from the
\Lambda
CDM concordance model
Camarena and Marra 2021
;
Vagnozzi 2020
;
Jackson 2007
. One signature for departure from the concordance model would be apparent redshift evolution
H_{0}

Krishnan et al. 2021
.

If the Hubble tension is proven robust with further measurements, there are a number of possible explanations with new physics. An extensive discussion of these possibilities is given in a recent review
Perivolaropoulos and Skara 2022
, and a comparison between many of such theories is given in
Schöneberg et al. 2022
. These proposals can be classified generally either as
early-time
modification of the sound horizon or
late-time
modification of the Hubble expansion.

Let us first discuss new physics before recombination which would lower the value of Hubble constant as measured by Planck.
Early dark energy adds an additional scalar field which acts like a cosmological constant and ends after recombination
Poulin et al. 2019
;
Agrawal et al. 2019
.
The time of matter-radiation equality can be shifted by adding additional relativistic degrees of freedom with dark radiation
Buen-Abad et al. 2018
;
Lancaster et al. 2017
;
Green et al. 2019
or neutrino self-interactions
Cyr-Racine and Sigurdson 2014
;
Kreisch et al. 2020
.
Finally, there are proposals that the photon mass cannot be perfectly measured due to the lifetime of the universe from Heisenberg’s uncertainty principle, and this translates to uncertainty on the Hubble constant
Capozziello et al. 2020
;
Spallicci et al. 2022
.

There are also a number of ways to create a smooth late time deformation in
H(z)
with unchanged CMB physics.
These include phantom dark energy
Alestas et al. 2020
;
Di Valentino et al. 2016
, running vacuum model
Banerjee et al. 2021
;
Basilakos et al. 2016
, phenomenologically emergent dark energy
Li and Shafieloo 2019
, vacuum phase transition

;
Caldwell et al. 2006
, and a phase transition in dark energy
Banihashemi et al. 2019
;
Banihashemi et al. 2020
;
Benevento et al. 2020
. Many of these modify the equation of state parameter of the dark energy or change how
\Omega_{\Lambda}(z)
evolves with redshift.
Another way to change the evolution of
H(z)
is by introducing additional interactions. These class includes well-known beyond GR theories such as Brans-Dicke gravity
Solà Peracaula et al. 2019
,
f(R)
gravity
D’Agostino and Nunes 2020

where dark energy and dark matter interact, and decaying dark matter

;
Bjaelde et al. 2012
;
Berezhiani et al. 2015
where dark matter decays into an unknown dark radiation.
Finally, the homogeneous and isotropic assumption of
\Lambda
CDM can be broken with chameleon dark energy
Khoury and Weltman 2004a
;
Khoury and Weltman 2004b
;
Cai et al. 2021
, cosmic voids

, and inhomogeneous causal horizons

The detection of gravitational waves (GW) can provide an independent
late
universe measurement of the Hubble constant.
By measuring the expansion rate in the late universe, GW could be used as an independent measurement of the Hubble constant from SH0ES. Furthermore, with a distribution of GW events at low redshifts (
z\sim 0.0-0.5
), an anomalous evolution of the expansion rate could be observed.
In particular, the luminosity distance of the source can be obtained from the measured gravitational waveform
Schutz 1986
.
A Hubble constant measurement can be readily attained from a standard siren: a binary neutron star (BNS) merger with a coincident EM counterpart
Schutz 1986
;
Holz and Hughes 2005
. With the optical measurement of the redshift from EM followup and luminosity distance measurement from the GW detector, one can directly measure the Hubble constant. Indeed, the Hubble constant was measured with the BNS GW170817
Abbott et al. 2017a
and its corresponding EM counterpart
Nicholl et al. 2017
;
Coulter et al. 2017
;
Abbott et al. 2017b
.

However, only a small number of GW events are expected to be bright BNS mergers with EM counterparts.
The majority of observed GW events are binary black hole (BBH) events without EM counterparts, which are thus known as
dark sirens
.
Notably, many BBH events have already been detected and cataloged

;
Abbott et al. 2021b
;
Venumadhav et al. 2020
;
Olsen et al. 2022
;
Nitz et al. 2019
;
Nitz et al. 2020

;

;
Chen et al. 2018
;
Fishbach et al. 2019
;
Scelfo et al. 2020
;
Finke et al. 2021

;

;

;
Ezquiaga and Holz 2021
;

;
Nair et al. 2018
;

;
Mukherjee et al. 2021
. These statistical techniques have been applied to the GWTC-3 catalog, and the Hubble constant is measured as

using only dark sirens

. For reference, GW170817 alone gives a Hubble constant value of
H_{0}=69^{+17}_{-8}\,\,\text{km}\,\text{s}^{-1}\text{Mpc}^{-1}

;

. In contrast, the Planck Hubble constant measurement was
H_{0}=67.4^{+0.5}_{-0.5}\,\,\text{km}\,\text{s}^{-1}\text{Mpc}^{-1}

and the SH0ES measurement was
H_{0}=72.5^{+1.0}_{-1.0}\,\,\text{km}\,\text{s}^{-1}\text{Mpc}^{-1}

which corresponds to the

tension

.

One new potential class of detector is one in the decihertz range (0.01 - 1 Hz), and such a detector may aid in measuring the Hubble constant. This detector would lie in between the millihertz LISA band
Amaro-Seoane et al. 2017
and the 10 - 1000 Hz ground band.
A decihertz detector has many advantages for measuring the Hubble constant. First, it would provide early warning for BNS mergers which would help guarantee EM identification
Kuns et al. 2020
;
Sedda et al. 2020
. Second, a joint decihertz detection would improve the parameter estimation for stellar mass BBH by measuring their waves several years before they enter into the ground band
Kuns et al. 2020
;
Yu and Chen 2021
. Since statistical approaches to dark sirens are degraded by having too many galaxies inside of the localization volume, having a better angular localization will significantly help measure the cosmological parameters. Furthermore, the fascinating possibility of a multiband detection exists where a decihertz detector observes a BBH inspiral and then the ground based detectors measure the merger and ringdown. A decihertz multiband detection has been found to substantially improve parameter estimation accuracy
Kuns et al. 2020
.
By combining decihertz and ground detectors, the detector network can uniquely localize a BBH to a its host galaxy without any EM counterpart.
While a ground network can do this on its own
Chen and Holz 2016
, the addition of a decihertz detector will significantly increase the range at which the BBH can be localized. In this way, a multiband detection of a BBH can behave like a standard siren.

Right now, there are a number of existing and proposed gravitational wave detectors.
Advanced LIGO
Aasi et al. 2015
, Advanced Virgo
Acernese et al. 2015
, and KAGRA
Akutsu et al. 2019
are operating ground based gravitational wave detectors and are second-generation (2G) detectors.
Following the 2G detectors, LIGO Voyager aims to maximize the reach of existing LIGO observatory facilities by adding cryogenic operation, heavier silicon test masses, and improved quantum squeezing

;
Adhikari et al. 2020
.

.

At frequencies below

Hz, detecting gravitational waves may best be carried out in space due to technical challenges

;
Harms et al. 2013
.
LISA

;

, TianQin

;

Hz bands.
LISA can measure the Hubble constant with dark sirens

with accuracy of 5% and may be able to measure it with EMRIs

to an accuracy of 1% to 3%, though it is likely that ground detectors will surpass this by the time it operational. Ref.
Zhu et al. 2022
studied measuring the Hubble constant measurement with TianQin and LISA/Einstein Telescope. In the far future, there are proposals for a microhertz GW detector
Sesana et al. 2021
. At very low frequencies, it may be possible for a pulsar timing array to measure the effect of a super massive black hole binary
Spallicci 2013
.
Furthermore, there are a number of space based plans for a decihertz detector in the
0.01-1
Hz band. The Japanese detector DECIGO is an ambitious prospect that consists of three clusters of interferometers with a 1000km arm length
Kawamura et al. 2020
;
Sato et al. 2017
;
Seto et al. 2001
. Big Bang Observer is concept like DECIGO by the European Space Agency
Crowder and Cornish 2005
. Previous work found that Big Bang Observer alone would provide precision cosmological tests by measuring and localizing nearly every GW event in the universe

Recently, Ref.
Liu et al. 2022
studied the capabilities of DECIGO and other decihertz detectors to measure the Hubble constant.
B-DECIGO is a planned pathfinder mission of DECIGO with a single interferometer and a 100 km arm length

;
Sato et al. 2017
.
Finally, TianGO is a space based decihertz concept which is designed with nearer-term technology

;

For this analysis, we study how well we can measure the expansion rate of the universe by measuring BBH with future ground detectors and decihertz concepts. We consider two representative decihertz detectors: (i) TianGO in the LIGO Voyager era, and (ii) B-DECIGO in the ET/CE era. TianGO is chosen because it represents a possible near term decihertz detector. In such a timescale, it would be operational in late 2020s/early 2030s and be working with the LIGO Voyager network. B-DECIGO is a longer term prospect, which would be operational in the late 2030s.

We forecast how well a dark siren can be localized with the Fisher matrix formalism
Finn 1992
;
Cutler and Flanagan 1994
with both detector setups. If such a comoving volume contains only one galaxy, we consider the dark siren to be
localized
. We consider the case where localized events will have measured redshift due to either spectroscopic follow-up or from a complete galaxy catalog. We find that adding a decihertz detector to the network improves the range at which a dark siren can be localized. We then constrain the Hubble constant and matter density parameter by stacking the localized dark siren events together with the BBH merger rate inferred by LIGO/Virgo/KAGRA
Abbott et al. 2021d
. We assume that the Hubble constant and matter density are the Planck values and fix all other cosmological parameters. Our study motivates how a decihertz detector can complement the cosmological measurement capabilities of ground based detectors.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
II
, we describe the observed strain in a space based detector, and we use the Fisher matrix formalism to forecast the measurement uncertainties with a multiband detection. In Sec.
III

IV
. Finally, App.
A
delves into the space-based waveform specifics, and App.
B
justifies the conservative approach of considering only localized dark sirens.
Throughout the work, we use
G=c=1

II.1
TianGO Waveform

Let us first model the waveform in a space detector. TianGO is orbiting the sun at an inclination of
60^{\circ}

II.2
Parameter Estimation Background

Let us now describe how we use the Fisher analysis to estimate parameter uncertainties. The Fisher matrix formalism provides a useful approximation to parameter estimation in the high SNR limit
Finn 1992
;
Cutler and Flanagan 1994
;
Vallisneri 2008
. We consider a binary with parameters
\boldsymbol{\theta}^{a}
and

\boldsymbol{\theta}^{a}=\left(\ln\mathcal{M}_{z},q,\ln D_{L},t_{c},\phi_{c},\bar{\phi}_{S},\bar{\theta}_{S},\bar{\phi}_{L},\bar{\theta}_{L}\right)\,.

(6)

The variance for a specific parameter
\boldsymbol{\theta}^{a}
is found on the diagonal of the inverse of the Fisher matrix

\Delta\boldsymbol{\theta}^{a}=\sqrt{\left(\Gamma^{-1}\right)_{aa}}\,,

(7)

where the Fisher information matrix is defined as

\Gamma_{ab}\equiv\left(\frac{\partial\tilde{h}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\theta}_{a}}\Big|\frac{\partial\tilde{h}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\theta}_{b}}\right)\,,

(8)

and the waveform template
\tilde{h}(f,\boldsymbol{\theta})
is a function of frequency
f
and parameters
\boldsymbol{\theta}
. The inner product between two signals
\tilde{h}(f),\tilde{g}(f)
is defined as

\left(\tilde{g}\big|\tilde{h}\right)=4\,\text{Re}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\tilde{g}^{\ast}(f)\tilde{h}(f)}{S_{n}(f)}df

(9)

where
S_{n}(f)
is the detector noise spectral density. In the case of a network of detectors, we sum the individual Fisher matrix for each detector
d

\left(\Gamma_{ab}\right)^{\text{net}}=\sum_{d}\Gamma_{ab}^{d}\,.

(10)

II.3
Results from Parameter Estimation

To understand how a decihertz detector can enhance the parameter estimation of a BBH, we examine the results obtained using TianGO with the HLI Voyager network. The luminosity distance is defined by

D_{L}(z)=\frac{1+z}{H_{0}}\int_{0}^{z}\frac{dz^{\prime}}{E(z^{\prime})}

(11)

where

E(z)\equiv\sqrt{\Omega_{m}\left(1+z\right)^{3}+\Omega_{\Lambda}}\,.

(12)

For precision tests of cosmology, we are mostly interested in the luminosity distance accuracy and volume localization.
The size of the solid angle ellipse
\Delta\Omega
can be expressed by
Cutler 1998

\Delta\Omega=2\pi\sin\bar{\theta}_{S}\sqrt{\Sigma_{\bar{\phi}_{S}\bar{\phi}_{S}}\Sigma_{\bar{\theta}_{S}\bar{\theta}_{S}}-\left(\Sigma_{\bar{\theta}_{S}\bar{\theta}_{S}}\right)^{2}}\,.

(13)

The uncertainty in comoving volume can be related to the angular uncertainty by Eq. (28) of Ref.
Hogg 1999

\Delta V_{\mathrm{C}}=\frac{D_{L}^{2}}{(1+z)^{2}}\Delta\Omega\Delta D_{C}\,,

(14)

where the comoving distance equals
D_{C}=D_{L}/\left(1+z\right)
. Using a change of variables, the comoving volume uncertainty can be rewritten as

\Delta V_{\mathrm{C}}=\frac{D_{L}^{2}}{(1+z)^{3}+D_{L}H(z)\left(1+z\right)}\Delta\Omega\Delta D_{L}\,,

(15)

where
H(z)=H_{0}E(z)
.

Systematic errors beyond the detector sensitivity can degrade the accuracy of the luminosity distance. The first of which is the gravitational lensing which changes the luminosity distance. We use the fit from
Hirata et al. 2010

\frac{\left(\Delta D_{L}\right)_{\text{lens}}}{D_{L}}=0.066\left[\frac{1-(1+z)^{-0.25}}{0.25}\right]^{1.8}\,.

(16)

Once a particular galaxy is identified, the peculiar velocity adds uncertainty to the amount of cosmological redshift. The measured redshift is the sum of the cosmological and Doppler redshift. We can express the peculiar velocity systematic error as
Gordon et al. 2007

\frac{\left(\Delta D_{L}\right)_{\text{pv}}}{D_{L}}=\Big|1-\frac{\left(1+z\right)^{2}}{D_{L}H(z)}\Big|\sigma_{v}\,,

(17)

where we have assumed
\sigma_{v}=200\text{ km s}^{-1}/c
. The relative magnitude of this effect decreases rapidly with distance since the cosmological redshift increases while the RMS peculiar velocity is approximately constant.

II.4
Event Rate

To infer cosmological parameters, we stack all dark siren events that the network can localize.
Let us now estimate how many dark sirens can be localized.
First, the merger rate density
\mathcal{R}(z)
describes the number of mergers in a comoving volume per year. We model it with a power law model and choose with
\kappa=2.7
so that it corresponds to the Madau-Dickinson star formation rate
Madau and Dickinson 2014

(20)

Abbott et al. 2021d

IV
Conclusion

In this paper, we studied how a space-based decihertz detector can enhance the sensitivity of a ground network for dark siren cosmological measurement.
We construct the case that these detectors will measure a significant number of ‘bright’ dark siren BBH – GW from which we can uniquely localize and uniquely identify the host galaxy.
We then use a Fisher matrix formalism to place constraints on the cosmological parameters.
We estimated how well the Hubble constant and matter density parameter could be measured by BBH dark sirens with a five year observation of TianGO plus three LIGO Voyagers.
The result is the multiband detection of dark sirens improves the measurement of the Hubble constant by about a factor of 3. The larger redshift localized events allows the matter density parameter to be resolved in the multiband case.

In the future, it would be interesting to extend our analysis to include dark sirens which are non-uniquely identified, but are still well localized.
Since the fully localized criterion leaves out events with just a small number of galaxies, information about the cosmology can still be extracted from these events.
Moreover, there are other effects which can improve the sensitivity further, such as exploiting the clustering of galaxies to improve localization
MacLeod and Hogan 2008
and weighting the galaxies by luminosity
Gray et al. 2020
.

Measuring the cosmology with gravitational waves is easier when the host galaxy is uniquely identified.
The statistical dark siren approach is degenerate with parameters such the merger rate evolution with redshift and the BBH population model (as discussed in the GWTC-3 cosmology paper
Abbott et al. 2021c
).
Simultaneously measuring the cosmology and these population parameters can be done by looking at the distribution of BBH events
You et al. 2021
;
Mukherjee et al. 2021
;
Yu et al. 2022
, but would result in a less sensitive measurement of the cosmological parameters.
Otherwise, if these factors are not jointly measured, this would bias the measurement of the Hubble constant
Trott and Huterer 2021
;
Yu et al. 2022
.
Consequently, a multiband detection of dark sirens with uniquely identified hosts has the potential to isolate the measurement of cosmological parameters from these population parameters.

Appendix B
Consistency of Statistical Method

In the statistical method, we wish to break the
z-D_{L}
degeneracy by using a galaxy catalog with the gravitational wave observation. We will use the method described in a variety of sources
Chen et al. 2018
;
Gray et al. 2020
. If we wish to constrain the cosmological parameters
\boldsymbol{H}
and have gravitational wave data
d_{\text{GW}}
, then with Bayes theorem, we have

p(\boldsymbol{H}|d_{\text{GW}})\propto p(H_{0})p(d_{\text{GW}}|\boldsymbol{H})

(36)

where

\displaystyle p(d_{\text{GW}}|\boldsymbol{H})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\beta(\boldsymbol{H})}\int p(d_{\text{GW}},D_{L},\phi_{S},\theta_{S},z|\boldsymbol{H})dD_{L}d\phi_{S}d\theta_{S}dz\,,

(37)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\beta(\boldsymbol{H})}\int p(d_{\text{GW}}|D_{L}(z,\boldsymbol{H}),\phi_{S},\theta_{S})p_{0}(z,\phi_{S},\theta_{S})d\phi_{S}d\theta_{S}dz\,.

(38)

the first term in the integral is approximated with a multivariate Gaussian distribution

p(d_{\text{GW}}|D_{L}(z,\boldsymbol{H}),\phi_{S},\theta_{S})=N(D_{L}(z,\boldsymbol{H})-\hat{D}_{L},\sigma_{D_{L}}^{2})N(\phi_{S}-\hat{\phi}_{S},\sigma_{\phi_{S}}^{2})N(\theta_{S}-\hat{\theta}_{S},\sigma_{\theta_{S}}^{2})\,,

(39)

where
N(x-\mu,\sigma^{2})
is the probability density function of the normal distribution,
(\hat{D}_{L},\hat{\phi}_{S},\hat{\theta}_{S})
are the true event parameters, and
(\sigma_{D_{L}},\sigma_{\phi_{S}},\sigma_{\theta_{S}})
is given by the Fisher matrix analysis in Eq. (10). The second term in the integral is the galaxy catalog

p_{0}(z,\phi_{S},\theta_{S}|\boldsymbol{H})=\frac{1}{N_{\text{gal}}}\sum^{N_{\text{gal}}}_{i}N(z-z^{i},\sigma_{z_{i}}^{2})\delta(\phi_{S}-\phi_{S}^{i})\delta(\theta_{S}-\theta_{S}^{i})\,,

(40)

where
\sigma_{z_{i}}
is the variance due to the peculiar velocity. The variables
(z^{i},\phi^{i},\theta^{i})
are the mean redshift and angular location of the
i
th galaxy, while unbarred variables are parameters.

The angular uncertainty is negligible and the distribution is replaced with a Dirac delta function
\delta(\phi_{S}-\bar{\phi}_{S}^{i})
and similarly for
\theta_{S}
. Finally, the normalization
\beta(\boldsymbol{H})
is

\beta(\boldsymbol{H})=\int_{d_{\rm GW}>d_{\rm GW}^{\rm th}}p(d_{\text{GW}},D_{L},\phi_{S},\theta_{S},z|\boldsymbol{H})dD_{L}d\phi_{S}d\theta_{S}dz\,dd_{\text{GW}}\,,

(41)

where

p(d_{\text{GW}},D_{L},\phi_{S},\theta_{S},z|\boldsymbol{H})=p(d_{\text{GW}}|D_{L}(z,\boldsymbol{H}),\phi_{S},\theta_{S})p_{0}(z,\phi_{S},\theta_{S})\,.

(42)

and where
d_{\rm GW}^{\rm th}
is the detection threshold.
Note that Eq. (
37
) reduces to the Fisher matrix confidence interval Eq. (
21
) on
\boldsymbol{H}
if only one galaxy has nonvanishing likelihood. This reduction can be derived by examining (
38
) in the case that there is only one galaxy inside the volume. This happens when all other galaxies in the sum in
p_{0}(z,\phi_{S},\theta_{S}|\boldsymbol{H})
do not contribute to the integral in Eq. (
38
).

(43)

where

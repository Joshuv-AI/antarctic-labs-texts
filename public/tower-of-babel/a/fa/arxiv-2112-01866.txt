Abstract
. We consider mappings f : G ⊃ U → G ′ f:G\supset U\rightarrow G^{\prime} where G G and G ′ G^{\prime} are Carnot groups. In this paper, which is a continuation of [ KMX20 ] , we focus on Carnot groups which are nonrigid in the sense of Ottazzi-Warhurst. We show that quasisymmetric homeomorphisms are reducible in the sense that they preserve a special type of coset foliation, unless the group is isomorphic to ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} or a real or complex Heisenberg group (where the assertion fails). We use this to prove the quasisymmetric rigidity conjecture for such groups. The starting point of the proof is the pullback theorem established in [ KMX20 ] .

Introduction

This is the second in a series of papers
[
KMX20
,
KMXb
,
KMX21b
,
KMX21a

on geometric mapping theory in Carnot groups, in which we establish new regularity, rigidity, and partial rigidity results for bilipschitz, quasiconformal, or more generally, Sobolev mappings, between Carnot groups. Our motivation comes from the analytical theory of quasiconformal homeomorphisms, geometric group theory (especially rigidity phenomena and Gromov hyperbolic spaces), analysis on metric spaces, the differential geometry of subriemannian manifolds, and the literature on rigidity and oscillatory solutions to partial differential equations (and relations).

While our main interest in this paper is in quasiconformal homeomorphisms – and more generally Sobolev mappings – between open subsets of Carnot groups, for context we first consider the special case of quasiconformal diffeomorphisms.

If

2.1.
The Pullback Theorem

We give a concise presentation here, and refer the reader to
[
KMX20
]
for more details.

Let
G
be a step
s
Carnot group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, grading
\mathfrak{g}=\oplus_{j=1}^{s}V_{j}
, dilation group
\{\delta_{r}:G\rightarrow G\}_{r\in(0,\infty)}
, dimension
N
, and homogeneous dimension
\nu=\sum_{j}j\dim V_{j}
. We let
\{X_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq N}
be a graded basis for
\mathfrak{g}
, and
\{\theta_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq N}
be the dual basis.

The
weight
of a subset
I\subset\{1,\ldots,N\}
is given by

\operatorname{wt}I:=-\sum_{i\in I}\deg i

where
\deg:\{1,\ldots,N\}\rightarrow\{1,\ldots,s\}
is defined by
\deg i=j
iff
X_{i}\in V_{j}
. For a non-zero left-invariant form

\alpha=\sum_{I}a_{I}\theta_{I}
we define
\operatorname{wt}(\alpha)=\max\{\operatorname{wt}I:a_{I}\neq 0\}
and set
\operatorname{wt}(0):=-\infty
; here
\theta_{I}
denotes the wedge product
\Lambda_{i\in I}\theta_{i}
.

We now let
G^{\prime}
be a second Carnot group, use primes for the associated structure.

Fix
p>\nu
, and let
f:U\rightarrow G^{\prime}
be a
W^{1,p}_{\operatorname{loc}}
-mapping for some open subset
U\subset G
. If
\omega\in\Omega^{k}(G^{\prime})
is a differential
k
-form with continuous coefficients, the
Pansu pullback of
\omega
is the
k
-form with measurable coefficients
f_{P}^{*}\omega
given by

f_{P}^{*}\omega(x):=(D_{P}f(x))^{*}\omega(f(x))\,,

where
D_{P}f(x):\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
is the Pansu differential of
f
at
x\in U
.

We will use the following special case of
[
KMX20
, Theorem 4.2]
:

Theorem 2.1

(Pullback Theorem (special case))
.

Suppose
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(U)
and that
\alpha
and
\beta
are closed left invariant forms
which satisfy

(2.2)

\deg\alpha+\deg\beta=N-1\quad\hbox{and}\quad\operatorname{wt}(\alpha)+\operatorname{wt}(\beta)\leq-\nu+1.

Then

(2.3)

\int_{U}f_{P}^{*}(\alpha)\wedge d(\varphi\beta)=0.

2.2.
Exterior algebra and interior products

Here we recall some basic facts about interior products. This will be useful for constructing closed left invariant forms of low
codegree that will be useful in the application of the Pullback Theorem. In this subsection we work purely in the setting of multilinear algebra. Exterior differentiation of forms will be considered in the next
subsection.

Given a finite dimensional vector space
V
over
\mathbb{R}
we denote the space of
m
-vectors by
\Lambda_{m}V
and the space of alternating

m
-linear forms by
\Lambda^{m}V
. The elements of
\Lambda^{m}V
can be identified with linear functionals
on
\Lambda_{m}V
(or
m
-covectors).

The interior product of a vector
X\in V
and a
p
-form
\alpha\in\Lambda^{p}V
is the
(p-1)
-form

i_{X}\alpha
given by

(2.4)

i_{X}\alpha(X_{2},\ldots,X_{p})=\alpha(X,X_{2},\ldots,X_{p}).

Using the dual pairing of vectors and covectors this can be rewritten as

i_{X}\alpha(Z)=\alpha(X\wedge Z).

For
q\leq p
,
X\in\Lambda_{q}V
and
\alpha\in\Lambda^{p}V
one defines

i_{X}\alpha\in\Lambda^{p-q}V

by

(2.5)

(i_{X}\alpha)(Z)=\alpha(X\wedge Z)\quad\forall Z\in\Lambda_{p-q}V.

Then for
X\in\Lambda_{q}
and
Y\in\Lambda_{q^{\prime}}

(i_{X}i_{Y}\alpha)(Z)=i_{Y}\alpha(X\wedge Z)=\alpha(Y\wedge X\wedge Z)

and thus

(2.6)

i_{X}\circ i_{Y}=i_{Y\wedge X}\quad\forall X\in\Lambda_{q},\,\,Y\in\Lambda_{q^{\prime}}.

The interior product satisfies the graded Leibniz rule

(2.7)

i_{X}(\beta\wedge\gamma)=i_{X}\beta\wedge\gamma+(-1)^{\deg\beta}\beta\wedge i_{X}\gamma\quad\forall X\in V.

If
\omega
is a top-degree form on
V
, then

(2.8)

\alpha\wedge i_{X}\omega=i_{X}\alpha\wedge\omega=\alpha(X)\omega\,,\quad\forall\alpha\in\Lambda^{p}V\,,\quad X\in\Lambda_{p}V.

This can easily be checked using the standard basis and dual basis and verifying the assertion
for
X
and
\beta
being simple vectors in terms of these basis vectors.

We finally briefly discuss the interior product on quotient spaces. Let
\tilde{V}
be a finite-dimensional vector space
over
\mathbb{R}
, let
K\subset\tilde{V}
be a subspace and let
V=\tilde{V}/K
.
We say that
\beta\in\Lambda^{p}\tilde{V}
annihilates
K
if
\beta(X_{1},\ldots,X_{p})=0
whenever at least
one of the
X_{i}
satisfies
X_{i}\in K
. If
\tilde{\alpha}\in\Lambda^{p}\tilde{V}
annihilates
K
then
it gives rise to a unique form
\alpha\in\Lambda^{p}V
defined by

(2.9)

\alpha(X_{1}+K,\ldots,X_{p}+K)=\tilde{\alpha}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{p})\quad\forall X_{i}\in\tilde{V}.

Conversely for every
\alpha\in\Lambda^{p}V
there exists a unique lift
\tilde{\alpha}\in\Lambda^{p}\tilde{V}

which annihilates
K
and satisfies (
2.9
). Consider such
\alpha
and
\tilde{\alpha}

as well as
X\in\tilde{V}
. Then

\widetilde{i_{X+K}\alpha}=i_{X}\tilde{\alpha}.

To simplify the notation we sometimes use the notation
i_{X}\alpha
for the form
i_{X+K}\alpha

and identify

i_{X}\alpha
and
i_{X}\tilde{\alpha}
.

2.3.
Exterior derivatives of left invariant forms with low codegree

In this subsection we recall some useful formulae for exterior derivatives of forms on unimodular Lie groups, particularly for low codegree forms defined using interior products of vectors with the volume form. Recall that Carnot groups are unimodular
[
CG90
, Theorem 1.2.10]
; we will only use the results in this section in the case of Carnot groups, but for the sake of logical clarity we have formulated them for unimodular groups, because they hold in such generality.

Let
G
be a Lie group.
We identify the corresponding Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with the space of left invariant vector fields on
G
, i.e., with the left
invariant sections in the tangent bundle
TG
.
Similarly we identify the space of
1
-forms
\Lambda^{1}\mathfrak{g}

with the space of left invariant
sections of the cotangent bundle
T^{*}G
and the spaces of
p

\mathfrak{g}
and

-forms
on
\mathfrak{g}
with the spaces of
left invariant sections of the corresponding tensor bundles.

For a vector field
X

\mathcal{L}_{X}
denote the Lie derivative with respect to

We will use Cartan’s formula

(2.10)

\mathcal{L}_{X}\alpha=d(i_{X}\alpha)+i_{X}d\alpha

as well as the relation

(2.11)

i_{[X,Y]}=[\mathcal{L}_{X},i_{Y}],

see, e.g.,
[

, Theorem 9.9]

Lemma 2.12
.

Let

be a unimodular group and let

be a bi-invariant volume form on

(2.13)

\displaystyle d(i_{X}\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{X}\omega=0\quad\forall X\in\mathfrak{g},

(2.14)

\displaystyle d(i_{X}i_{Y}\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i_{[X,Y]}\omega\quad\forall X,Y\in\mathfrak{g},

(2.15)

\displaystyle d(i_{X}i_{Y}i_{Z}\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i_{A}\omega\quad\forall X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{g},\quad\hbox{where}

(2.16)

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle X\wedge[Y,Z]+Y\wedge[Z,X]+Z\wedge[X,Y].

Finally if

(2.17)

X,X^{\prime},X^{\prime\prime},Z\in\mathfrak{g}\quad\hbox{and}\quad[X,Z]=[X^{\prime},Z]=[X^{\prime\prime},Z]=0

then

d(i_{Z}i_{X^{\prime\prime}}i_{X^{\prime}}i_{X}\omega)=-i_{Z}d(i_{X^{\prime\prime}}i_{X^{\prime}}i_{X}\omega)

Proof.

Since
\omega
has top degree it is closed and thus Cartan’s formula gives

d(i_{X}\omega)=\mathcal{L}_{X}\omega\quad\forall X\in\mathfrak{g}.

Since the flow of a left invariant vector field is given by right translation and since

is also

we have
\mathcal{L}_{X}\omega=0
.

To show (
2.14
) we use Cartan’s formula and
(

3.
Quasisymmetric rigidity of nonrigid Carnot groups

In this section we state our main rigidity results, and prove them using results that will be established later in the paper. We also prove a localized version of a result of Le Donne-Xie which deduces quasisymmetric rigidity (i.e. automatic improvement to bilipschitz regularity) for mappings that preserve certain types of foliations.

3.1.
Statement of results and some initial reductions

Theorem 3.1

(Quasisymmetric rigidity)
.

Let
G
be a Carnot group that is nonrigid in the sense of Ottazzi-Warhurst, and assume that
G
is not isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
or a real Heisenberg group. Let

\eta
-quasisymmetric homeomorphism between open subsets, where

for some
x\in G
,

.
Then, modulo post-composition with a Carnot rescaling, the restriction of
f
to the subball

is a
K
-bilipschitz homeomorphism onto its image, where
K=K(\eta)
.

Consequently, modulo post-composition with a Carnot rescaling
every locally
\eta
-quasisymmetric homeomorphism between open subsets of
G
is locally
K=K(\eta)
-bilipschitz, and any global
\eta
-quasisymmetric homeomorphism onto its image
G\rightarrow U^{\prime}\subset G
is a (surjective)
K=K(\eta)
-bilipschitz homeomorphism

.

Remark 3.2
.

If desired, one may replace

with

in the statement of Theorem

at the cost of increasing

; this follows readily by combining

We will show that apart from the complex Heisenberg case, quasisymmetric homeomorphisms are “reducible” in the following sense.

Definition 3.3

Let

4.
Structure of nonrigid Carnot groups

In this section we analyze the algebraic structure of graded Lie algebras corresponding to Carnot groups which are nonrigid in the sense of Ottazzi-Warhurst. Starting from the fact that the first layer must contain nontrivial elements with rank at most
2

[
OW11b
]
(see also
[
DR10
]
), we establish a trichotomy: either the first layer contains a special type of automorphism invariant subspace, or the Carnot group is
\mathbb{R}^{n}
or a real Heisenberg group, or the Carnot group must have a very special structure – it must be a quotient of a product of real or complex Heisenberg groups by a specific type of subgroup (see Theorem
4.9
). This result is a variation of an unpublished classification theorem of the third author
[
Xie13a
]
. In the concluding section we show that such product quotients admit a canonical graded product decomposition into factors whose second layers admit automorphism invariant conformal structures.

After some preliminaries, the structure theorem (Theorem
4.9
) will be given in Subsection
4.2
. We then discuss the characterization in terms of quotients in Subsection
4.3
, and in Subsection
4.4
we show that product quotients have a graded direct sum decomposition into conformal product quotients.

4.1.
Preliminaries

In what follows,
\mathbb{F}
will always be either
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
, and if
X
is an element of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
over
\mathbb{F}
, then
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}}X:=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}}\operatorname{ad}_{X}=\dim_{\mathbb{F}}[X,\mathfrak{g}]
. Given an
\mathbb{F}
-linear subspace
W\subset\mathfrak{g}
, and
r\geq 0
, we let
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}}(r,W)
and
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}}(\leq r,W)
be the collections of elements
X\in W
with
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}}X=r
, and
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}}X\leq r
, respectively. Although the field
\mathbb{F}
is implicit, and therefore strictly speaking the subscript is redundant, in what follows we will sometimes have algebras over
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{C}
in the same context, and so prefer to have the subscript to eliminate any potential ambiguity.

We denote the complexification
V\otimes\mathbb{C}
of an
\mathbb{R}
-vector space by
V^{\mathbb{C}}
.

Lemma 4.1

(Complexification of complex Lie algebras)
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra over
\mathbb{C}
, with the complex multiplication denoted by
J
; we let
\bar{\mathfrak{g}}
be the Lie algebra over
\mathbb{C}
with the same underlying Lie algebra over
\mathbb{R}
, but with complex multiplication given by
-J
. Viewing
\mathfrak{g}
as a Lie algebra over
\mathbb{R}
, we let
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}:=\mathfrak{g}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
denote the complexification of
\mathfrak{g}
, considered as a vector space over
\mathbb{C}
with complex multiplication denoted by
i
.
We denote by
J^{\mathbb{C}}:=J\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{C}}:\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
the map induced by
J
.

Then:

(1)

\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
decomposes as a direct sum
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}=\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{i}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{-i}
of eigenspaces of
J^{\mathbb{C}}
, which are interchanged by complex conjugation.

(2)

\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{i}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{-i}
are Lie subalgebras isomorphic over
\mathbb{C}
to
\mathfrak{g}
and
\bar{\mathfrak{g}}
, respectively; the isomorphisms are given
by taking twice the real part.

(3)

[\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{i},\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{-i}]=\{0\}
.

Proof.

Note that

V_{\pm i}:=\{X\mp iJX\,|\,X\in\mathfrak{g}\}\subset\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}

is a
\mathbb{C}
-linear subspace contained in the
\pm i
eigenspace of
J^{\mathbb{C}}
, and complex conjugation interchanges
V_{i}
and
V_{-i}
. Since we have
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}=V_{i}\oplus V_{-i}
, (1) follows.

If
Z\in\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\pm i}
,
Z^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
, then

J^{\mathbb{C}}[Z,Z^{\prime}]=[J^{\mathbb{C}}Z,Z^{\prime}]=[\pm iZ,Z^{\prime}]=\pm i[Z,Z^{\prime}]\,,

so
[Z,Z^{\prime}]\in\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\pm i}
. Hence
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\pm i}
is an ideal, assertion (3) holds, and we have a direct sum decomposition
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}=\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{i}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{-i}
. Taking real parts gives (up to a factor) the isomorphisms
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{i}\simeq\mathfrak{g}
,
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{-i}\simeq\bar{\mathfrak{g}}
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

If in Lemma
4.1
the graded algebra
\mathfrak{g}
itself happens to be the complexification of some graded algebra over
\mathbb{R}
– for instance if
\mathfrak{g}
is a complex Heisenberg algebra – then there is a
\mathbb{C}
-linear graded isomorphism
\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\bar{\mathfrak{g}}
given by complex conjugation. Therefore in this case all four graded algebras
\mathfrak{g}
,
\bar{\mathfrak{g}}
,
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{i}
,
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{-i}
are graded isomorphic over
\mathbb{C}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose
(\mathfrak{g},\{V_{j}\}_{j=1}^{s})
is a graded Lie algebra over a field
\mathbb{F}
. If
X\in V_{1}
and
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}[X,\mathfrak{g}]\leq 1
, then
[X,V_{j}]=\{0\}
for all

.

Proof.

Suppose
[X,V_{1}]=\{0\}
. Since the centralizer of an element is a subalgebra and
V_{1}
generates
\mathfrak{g}
, we get
[X,\mathfrak{g}]=\{0\}
and we’re done in this case. Now suppose
[X,V_{1}]\neq\{0\}
. Since
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}[X,\mathfrak{g}]\leq 1
we deduce that

[X,\mathfrak{g}]=[X,V_{1}]\subset V_{2}
, and for all

we have
[X,V_{j}]\subset V_{j+1}\cap V_{2}=\{0\}
.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Suppose
(\mathfrak{g},\{V_{j}\}_{j=1}^{2})
is a step
2
graded Lie algebra over a field
\mathbb{F}
, such that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}V_{2}=1
and the center of
\mathfrak{g}
intersects
V_{1}
trivially:

\{X\in V_{1}\mid[X,\mathfrak{g}]=\{0\}\}=\{0\}\,.

Then
\mathfrak{g}
is graded isomorphic to a Heisenberg algebra over
\mathbb{F}
.

Proof.

Identifying
V_{2}
with a copy of
\mathbb{F}
by an
\mathbb{F}
-linear isomorphism, the Lie bracket defines a skew-symmetric bilinear form
[\cdot,\cdot]:V_{1}\times V_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
, which is nondegenerate. Such forms are
\mathbb{F}
-linearly equivalent to a direct sum of standard
2
-dimensional symplectic forms
by a straightforward induction argument (see for example
[
Jac85
, p.349]
).
This yields the isomorphism to the Heisenberg algebra.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

For
\mathbb{F}\in\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}\}
, let
\operatorname{Sym}(m,\mathbb{F})
be the symplectic group, i.e. the stabilizer of the standard non-degenerate skew form on
\mathbb{F}^{2m}
. For
n\geq 1
, consider an
\mathbb{R}
-linear subspace

which is invariant under the product action
(\operatorname{Sym}(m,\mathbb{F}))^{n}\operatorname{\curvearrowright}(\mathbb{F}^{2m})^{n}
. Then for some subset
J\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}
, the subspace

is the span of the factors indexed by

, i.e.

K=\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in(\mathbb{F}^{2m})^{n}\,|\,x_{k}=0\,\text{ if }\,k\not\in J\}\,.

Proof.

Suppose for some
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
we have
x_{j}\neq 0
for some
x\in K
. Choosing
T\in\operatorname{Sym}(m,\mathbb{F})
such that
Tx_{j}\neq x_{j}
, we get that
y\in K
, where
y_{k}=x_{k}
if
k\neq j
, and
y_{j}=Tx_{j}
. Then

has precisely one nonzero component, namely

. Applying
\operatorname{Sym}(m,\mathbb{F})
and taking the linear span over
\mathbb{R}
, we get that the

is contained in

.

Applying the above to every

, the lemma follows.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}_{m}
be the
m^{th}
Heisenberg algebra, and
\mathfrak{h}_{m}^{\mathbb{C}}
be the complexification, i.e. the

and
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathfrak{h}_{m}^{\mathbb{C}})
denote the groups of graded

-linear and
\mathbb{C}
-linear automorphisms, respectively. Then

is generated by
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathfrak{h}_{m}^{\mathbb{C}})

is a graded

-linear automorphism, then either:

•

is
\mathbb{C}
-linear and

•

is
\mathbb{C}
-antilinear and

Proof.

The

[

, although their argument clearly works for general

We give a short argument here.

Let
\mathfrak{g}:=\mathfrak{h}_{m}^{\mathbb{C}}
, and let

be the grading. Viewing this as a graded algebra over

, we use Lemma

to see that
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}=\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{i}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{-i}
, and the first layer elements of
\mathbb{C}

\leq 1

.

If

, then
\phi^{\mathbb{C}}:\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
must preserve the subset

. Since

is a

-linear automorphism it must preserve the collection

, so we have two cases: either

\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\pm i}
to itself, or it interchanges the two. In the

to itself. Since the real part map

\mathbb{C}
-linear on

\mathbb{C}
-linear, which means that

is
\mathbb{C}
-antilinear.

Finally note that an invertible

-linear map on the real two-dimensional space

has

-antilinear map has negative determinant.

We now give a generalization of Lemma

.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\oplus_{j=1}^{s}V_{j}

and

.

.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be the complexification of a Carnot Lie algebra.
Assume there is some

such that

. Then every graded
\mathbb{R}
-linear automorphism of

\mathbb{C}
-linear or
\mathbb{C}
-antilinear.

By Lemma

of

admits a decomposition

\mathbb{C}
and

[\mathfrak{g}_{i}^{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{g}_{-i}^{\mathbb{C}}]=0

Let
\phi\in\text{Aut}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathfrak{g})
.
Then
\phi^{\mathbb{C}}:\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
is a graded
\mathbb{C}
-linear automorphism. We shall show that

either

Let

Since both

\text{ad}_{X}(V_{I,\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}})=\text{ad}_{X_{+}}(V_{I,\mathfrak{g}_{i}^{\mathbb{C}}})\oplus\text{ad}_{X_{-}}(V_{I,\mathfrak{g}_{-i}^{\mathbb{C}}})

and so

\text{rank}_{I,\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}}(X)=\text{rank}_{I,\mathfrak{g}_{i}^{\mathbb{C}}}(X_{+})+\text{rank}_{I,\mathfrak{g}_{-i}^{\mathbb{C}}}(X_{-})\geq 2r_{I}.

Here we used the facts that

\mathfrak{g}_{i}^{\mathbb{C}}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{-i}^{\mathbb{C}}
are isomorphic to

\mathfrak{g}
and
[\mathfrak{g}_{i}^{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{g}_{-i}^{\mathbb{C}}]=0
.
Since for any graded
\mathbb{C}
-linear isomorphism

f:\mathfrak{g}_{1}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{2}
of complex Carnot algebras
we have

\text{rank}_{I,\mathfrak{g}_{1}}(X)=\text{rank}_{I,\mathfrak{g}_{2}}(f(X))
, we see that

\phi^{\mathbb{C}}
maps
V_{1,\mathfrak{g}_{i}^{\mathbb{C}}}\cup V_{1,\mathfrak{g}_{-i}^{\mathbb{C}}}
to itself. Since
\phi^{\mathbb{C}}
is
\mathbb{C}
-linear,
\phi^{\mathbb{C}}
either
maps
\mathfrak{g}_{\pm i}^{\mathbb{C}}
to itself or switches the two.
∎

The assumption in Lemma
4.7
is satisfied for the
complexification
\mathfrak{g}
of the following classes of Carnot algebras:

(1) model filiform algebras; in this case,
R_{\{1\}}=r_{\{1\}}=1
.

(2) free nilpotent Lie algebras; more generally, quotients of free nilpotent Lie algebras by graded ideals contained in the direct sum of higher layers
V_{j}
,
j\geq 3
; in this case,
R_{\{1\}}=r_{\{1\}}>0
.

(3) Carnot algebras
\mathfrak{h}=\oplus_{j}V_{j}
satisfying
[X,V_{i}]=V_{i+1}
for all
0\not=X\in V_{1}
and some fixed
i\geq 1
with
\text{dim}(V_{i+1})
odd. These include
nilpotent Lie algebras satisfying Métivier’s hypothesis (H) (in particular,
H
type algebras) whose centers have odd dimension. To see that
\mathfrak{g}:=\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}}=\oplus_{j}V_{j}^{\mathbb{C}}
satisfies the assumption of Lemma
4.7
, we denote

\text{dim}_{\mathbb{R}}(V_{i+1})=2k+1
. Then

\text{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}(V^{\mathbb{C}}_{i+1})=2k+1
and so
R_{\{i\}}\leq 2k+1
.
We show that
\text{rank}_{\{i\},\mathfrak{g}}(X)\geq k+1
for any
0\not=X\in V^{\mathbb{C}}_{1}
, which implies
r_{\{i\}}\geq k+1
and so
R_{\{i\}}<2r_{\{i\}}
. Write
X=X_{1}+iX_{2}
with
X_{1},X_{2}\in V_{1}
. We may assume
X_{1}\not=0
.
We have
\operatorname{\mathfrak{Re}}[X,V^{\mathbb{C}}_{i}]\supset\operatorname{\mathfrak{Re}}[X,V_{i}]=V_{i+1}.
Hence

\text{dim}_{\mathbb{R}}([X,V^{\mathbb{C}}_{i}])\geq\text{dim}_{\mathbb{R}}(V_{i+1})=2k+1
, which implies
\text{rank}_{\{i\},\mathfrak{g}}(X)=\text{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}([X,V^{\mathbb{C}}_{i}])\geq k+1
.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\{V_{j}\}_{j=1}^{2})
be a step
2
graded Lie algebra over
\mathbb{F}
. Suppose
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,V_{1})=\{0\}
, and
\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{F}}(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}}(1,V_{1}))=V_{1}
. Then there is a collection
\mathfrak{g}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{g}_{n}
of graded subalgebras (over
\mathbb{F}
) of
\mathfrak{g}
such that:

(1)

Each
\mathfrak{g}_{j}
is graded isomorphic over
\mathbb{F}
to some Heisenberg algebra over
\mathbb{F}
.

(2)

The first layers of the
\mathfrak{g}_{j}
s define a direct sum decomposition of
V_{1}
:

V_{1}=\oplus_{j}(V_{1}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{j})\,.

(3)

The
\mathfrak{g}_{j}
s commute with one another:
[\mathfrak{g}_{j},\mathfrak{g}_{k}]=0
for
1\leq j\neq k\leq n
.

(4)

\mathfrak{g}_{j}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{k}=\{0\}
for
1\leq j\neq k\leq n
.

(5)

The collection is permuted by the graded automorphism group
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
.

Moreover conditions (1)-(4) determine
n
and the collection
\mathfrak{g}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{g}_{n}
uniquely.

Proof.

Let
\{L_{j}\}_{j\in J}
be the collection of
1
-dimensional subspaces of
V_{2}
of the form
[X,\mathfrak{g}]
, where

X\in\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}}(1,V_{1})
; here the index set
J
might be infinite a priori. For every
j\in J
, let
K_{j}:=\{X\in V_{1}\mid[X,\mathfrak{g}]\subset L_{j}\}
. Then
K_{j}
is a subspace of
V_{1}
, and by our assumption that
\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{F}}\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}}(1,V_{1})=V_{1}
, the
K_{j}
s span
V_{1}
.

Note that if
j\neq j^{\prime}
then
[K_{j},K_{j^{\prime}}]\subset L_{j}\cap L_{j^{\prime}}=\{0\}
. Therefore for every
j_{0}\in J
we have
[K_{j_{0}},\sum_{j\neq j_{0}}K_{j}]=\{0\}
. It follows that if
X\in K_{j_{0}}\cap\sum_{j\neq j_{0}}K_{j}
then
[X,K_{j}]=\{0\}
for all
j
, and so
[X,\mathfrak{g}]=\{0\}
, forcing
X=0
by assumption. Hence we have a direct sum decomposition
V_{1}=\oplus_{j}K_{j}
, and in particular
J
is finite.

For every
j\in J
, let
\mathfrak{g}_{j}:=K_{j}\oplus L_{j}
. Since
\mathfrak{g}
has step
2
,
\mathfrak{g}_{j}
is a graded subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
. Since
V_{1}\setminus\{0\}
has no rank zero elements, and
[\mathfrak{g}_{j},\mathfrak{g}_{k}]=\{0\}
for
k\neq j
, it follows that
\mathfrak{g}_{j}
has no rank zero first layer elements. By Lemma
4.4
,
\mathfrak{g}_{j}
is isomorphic to a Heisenberg algebra over
\mathbb{F}
, for every
j
.

If
j\neq k
, we get
\mathfrak{g}_{j}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{k}=\{0\}
from the fact that its projections to both layers are
\{0\}
.

To prove uniqueness, we observe that if
X\in V_{1}\setminus\cup_{j}\mathfrak{g}_{j}
, then
X
has rank at least
2
. Thus if
\mathfrak{g}_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\mathfrak{g}_{k^{\prime}}^{\prime}
is another collection of subalgebras satisfying (1)-(4), then each
\mathfrak{g}_{j^{\prime}}^{\prime}\cap V_{1}
must be contained in
\mathfrak{g}_{j}\cap V_{1}
for some
1\leq j\leq n
, and vice-versa. Since the
\mathfrak{g}_{j}
s are determined by their first layers, this gives uniqueness and consequently assertion (5) as well.
∎

4.2.
The classification

We recall that from
[
OW11b
,
DR10
]
, a Carnot group
G
is nonrigid if and only if the first layer of its complexification contains an element
X\neq 0
with
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{C}}X\leq 1
. Hence the following theorem yields a dichotomy for nonrigid graded Lie algebras: either the first layer contains a special type of automorphism invariant subspace, or the graded algebra has a very special structure.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\{V_{j}\}_{j=1}^{s})
be a (real) graded Lie algebra, and
(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}},\{V_{j}^{\mathbb{C}}\}_{j=1}^{s})
be the complexification with its induced grading.
Suppose:

(
a)

There is no
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
-invariant subspace
\{0\}\neq W\subsetneq V_{1}
such that
[W,V_{i}]=\{0\}
for all
i\geq 2
.

(
b)

There is a nonzero element
Z\in(V_{1}^{\mathbb{C}})\setminus\{0\}
such that
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{C}}Z\leq 1
(or equivalently,
(\mathfrak{g},\{V_{j}\}_{j=1}^{s})
is the graded Lie algebra of a nonrigid Carnot group).

Then either
\mathfrak{g}
is abelian, or it has step
2
, and for some
\mathbb{F}\in\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}\}
,
n\geq 1
, there is a collection
\mathfrak{g}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{g}_{n}
of graded subalgebras of
\mathfrak{g}
with the following properties:

(1)

For some
m
, each
\mathfrak{g}_{j}
is graded isomorphic over
\mathbb{R}
to the
m
-th Heisenberg algebra over
\mathbb{F}
(viewed as a graded Lie algebra over
\mathbb{R}
).

(2)

The first layers of the
\mathfrak{g}_{j}
s define a direct sum decomposition of
V_{1}
:

V_{1}=\oplus_{j}(V_{1}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{j})\,.

(3)

The
\mathfrak{g}_{j}
s commute with one another:
[\mathfrak{g}_{j},\mathfrak{g}_{k}]=0
for
1\leq j\neq k\leq n
.

(4)

If
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
, then the second layers are distinct:
\mathfrak{g}_{j}\cap V_{2}\neq\mathfrak{g}_{k}\cap V_{2}
for
1\leq j\neq k\leq n
. If
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
, then the second layers need not be distinct. However, for each
j
we have a (graded) decomposition of the complexification
\mathfrak{g}_{j}^{\mathbb{C}}=(\mathfrak{g}_{j}^{\mathbb{C}})_{i}\oplus(\mathfrak{g}_{j}^{\mathbb{C}})_{-i}
from Lemma
4.1
;
the second layers of
(\mathfrak{g}_{j}^{\mathbb{C}})_{\pm i}
are distinct and interchanged by
complex conjugation, and we obtain distinct pairs of second layers as
j
varies.

(5)

\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
preserves the collection
\{\mathfrak{g}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
, and permutes the
\mathfrak{g}_{j}
s transitively.

Moreover the field
\mathbb{F}
, and the collection
\mathfrak{g}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{g}_{n}
are uniquely determined by
\mathfrak{g}
.

Remark 4.10
.

Note that in (
4
) for
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
the definition of
(\mathfrak{g}_{j}^{\mathbb{C}})_{\pm i}
requires a complex multiplication
on
\mathfrak{g}_{j}
. Such a multiplication is induced by a choice of an
\mathbb{R}
-linear graded isomorphism from the
m
-th complex Heisenberg group to
\mathfrak{g}_{j}
which exist by (
1
). In view of
Lemma
4.6
, different choices of an
\mathbb{R}
-linear graded isomorphism yield the same complex
structure on
\mathfrak{g}_{j}
, up to a possible change of sign. Changing the sign of the complex structure just exchanges

(\mathfrak{g}_{j}^{\mathbb{C}})_{i}
and
(\mathfrak{g}_{j}^{\mathbb{C}})_{-i}
. Thus the collection

\{(\mathfrak{g}_{j}^{\mathbb{C}})_{i},(\mathfrak{g}_{j}^{\mathbb{C}})_{-i}\}
is uniquely determined by
\mathfrak{g}_{j}
.

Remark 4.11
.

The reader may wonder why Hypothesis (a) in Theorem
4.9
includes the restriction that
[W,V_{i}]=\{0\}
for all
i\geq 2
. The theorem would remain true if this restriction is dropped, since, due to the negation, it would make this a logically stronger hypothesis; however, for the results in Section
3
we need the stronger result of Theorem
4.9
.

Proof.

We first sketch the overall logic before beginning the formal proof.

By our assumptions, it follows readily that
V_{1}\setminus\{0\}
contains an element
X
with
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{R}}(X)\in\{0,1,2\}
. We then deal with the three possibilities by elimination. If
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{R}}(0,V_{1})\neq\{0\}
, then one concludes that
\mathfrak{g}
is abelian. Assuming
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{R}}(0,V_{1})=\{0\}
and
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{R}}(1,V_{1})\neq\emptyset
, we reduce to Lemma
4.8
. Finally, assuming
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{R}}(\leq 1,V_{1})=\{0\}
, we analyze the situation by complexifying, applying Lemma
4.8
to the complexification, and interpreting the results back in the original graded algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.

We now return to the proof.

Suppose
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{R}}(0,V_{1})\neq\{0\}
. Then
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{R}}(0,V_{1})

is a nontrivial,
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
-invariant subspace of
V_{1}
which commutes with
\mathfrak{g}
.
Thus by hypothesis (a) we have
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{R}}(0,V_{1})=V_{1}
.

Since
V_{1}
generates
\mathfrak{g}
, it follows that
\mathfrak{g}
is abelian, and we are done. Therefore we may assume that

(4.12)

\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{R}}(0,V_{1})=\{0\}\,.

Now suppose
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{R}}(1,V_{1})\neq\emptyset
. Then
W_{1}:=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{R}}(1,V_{1})
is a nontrivial,
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
-invariant subspace of
V_{1}
.
By Lemma
4.3
we have
[W_{1},V_{j}]=\{0\}
for all
j\geq 2
. Thus hypothesis (a) implies that
W_{1}=V_{1}
.
Using Lemma
4.3
again we see that
[V_{1},\oplus_{j\geq 2}V_{j}]=\{0\}
and we conclude that
\mathfrak{g}
has step

2
.

By Lemma
4.8
we get graded subalgebras
\mathfrak{g}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{g}_{n}
satisfying (1)-(4) of Lemma
4.8
in this case, and by (5) of Lemma
4.8
they are permuted by
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
.
By hypothesis (a) they must be permuted transitively, since otherwise an orbit would give rise to a nontrivial
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
-invariant subspace of
V_{1}
which commutes with
\oplus_{j\geq 2}V_{j}
. Thus (5) holds. In particular, all the
\mathfrak{g}_{j}
are isomorphic to the same Heisenberg algebra and we are done in this case.

We now assume in addition that
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{R}}(1,V_{1})=\emptyset
, i.e.

(4.13)

\dim_{\mathbb{R}}[X,\mathfrak{g}]\geq 2\quad\text{for every}\quad X\in V_{1}\setminus\{0\}\,.

Recall that
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{C}}(1,V_{1}^{\mathbb{C}})
is the collection of
Z\in V_{1}^{\mathbb{C}}
such that
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{C}}Z=1
.

Claim
.

Suppose
0\neq Z\in\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{C}}(\leq 1,V_{1}^{\mathbb{C}})\subset\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
, and let

\displaystyle P_{Z}^{\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle:=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{R}}(\operatorname{\mathfrak{Re}}Z,\operatorname{\mathfrak{Im}}Z)

\displaystyle P_{Z}^{\mathbb{C}}

\displaystyle:=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}(Z,\bar{Z})=\mathbb{C}Z+\mathbb{C}\bar{Z}\,.

Then

(1)

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}[Z,\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}]=1
.

(2)

P_{Z}^{\mathbb{R}}\otimes\mathbb{C}=P_{Z}^{\mathbb{C}}
.

(3)

\dim_{\mathbb{R}}P_{Z}^{\mathbb{R}}=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}P_{Z}^{\mathbb{C}}=2
.

(4)

[\bar{Z},\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}]\neq[Z,\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}]
.

(5)

\dim_{\mathbb{R}}[P_{Z}^{\mathbb{R}},\mathfrak{g}]=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}[P_{Z}^{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}]=2
.

Proof of claim.

We cannot have
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}[Z,\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}]=0
, since then we would have
\operatorname{\mathfrak{Re}}Z,\operatorname{\mathfrak{Im}}Z\in V_{1}
and
[\operatorname{\mathfrak{Re}}Z,\mathfrak{g}]=[\operatorname{\mathfrak{Im}}Z,\mathfrak{g}]=0
, contradicting (
4.12
). Hence
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}[Z,\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}]=1
and (1) holds.

(2) is immediate from
\operatorname{\mathfrak{Re}}Z=\frac{1}{2}(Z+\bar{Z})
,
\operatorname{\mathfrak{Im}}Z=\frac{1}{2i}(Z-\bar{Z})
.

Note that
P_{Z}^{\mathbb{C}}=P_{Z}^{\mathbb{R}}\otimes\mathbb{C}
implies
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}P_{Z}^{\mathbb{R}}=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}P_{Z}^{\mathbb{C}}
. We cannot have
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}P_{Z}^{\mathbb{C}}=1
, since then
P_{Z}^{\mathbb{C}}
would contain a nonzero real element with complex rank
1
, and hence real rank
1
, contradicting (
4.13
). Thus
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}P_{Z}^{\mathbb{C}}=2
, and (3) holds.

We now consider the action of complex conjugation
Z\mapsto\bar{Z}
. Suppose
[\bar{Z},\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}]=[Z,\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}]
. Then
P_{Z}^{\mathbb{C}}
is a
\mathbb{C}
-linear subspace of
V_{1}^{\mathbb{C}}
that is complex conjugation invariant, and
[P_{Z}^{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}]=[Z,\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}]=[\bar{Z},\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}]
is a
\mathbb{C}
-linear, complex conjugation invariant
subspace with
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}[P_{Z}^{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}]=1
. Therefore the real points
P_{Z}^{\mathbb{R}}=\operatorname{\mathfrak{Re}}P_{Z}^{\mathbb{C}}
have the property that
[P_{Z}^{\mathbb{R}},\mathfrak{g}]
lies in the real points of
[P_{Z}^{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}]
, which form an
\mathbb{R}
-linear subspace of dimension
1
over
\mathbb{R}
. This contradicts (
4.13
). It follows that
[\bar{Z},\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}]\neq[Z,\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}]
, so (4) holds.

Finally, (4) implies that
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}[P_{Z}^{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}]=2
. Since
[P_{Z}^{\mathbb{R}},\mathfrak{g}]\otimes\mathbb{C}\subset[P_{Z}^{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}]
, we get
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}[P_{Z}^{\mathbb{R}},\mathfrak{g}]\leq\dim_{\mathbb{C}}[P_{Z}^{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}]=2
. By (
4.13
) we cannot have
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}[P_{Z}^{\mathbb{R}},\mathfrak{g}]\leq 1
, so (5) follows.
∎

Let
\hat{W}_{1}:=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{P_{Z}^{\mathbb{R}}\,|\,Z\in\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{C}}(1,V_{1}^{\mathbb{C}})\}
. This is a nontrivial
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
invariant subspace of
V_{1}
.
If
Z\in\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{C}}(1,V_{1}^{\mathbb{C}})
, then for
j\geq 2
we have

[P_{Z}^{\mathbb{R}},V_{j}]\subset[P_{Z}^{\mathbb{C}},V_{j}^{\mathbb{C}}]=[Z,V_{j}^{\mathbb{C}}]+[\bar{Z},V_{j}^{\mathbb{C}}]=\{0\}

by Lemma
4.3
and the identity
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}[\bar{Z},\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}]=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}[Z,\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}]
.
Thus by hypothesis (a) we get
\hat{W}_{1}=V_{1}
.
This implies that
\mathfrak{g}
is a step
2
graded algebra, and hence
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
also has step
2
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle V_{1}^{\mathbb{C}}

\displaystyle=V_{1}\otimes\mathbb{C}=\hat{W}_{1}\otimes\mathbb{C}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{P_{Z}^{\mathbb{R}}\otimes\mathbb{C}\mid Z\in\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{C}}(1,V_{1}^{\mathbb{C}})\}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{P_{Z}^{\mathbb{C}}\mid Z\in\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{C}}(1,V_{1}^{\mathbb{C}})\}\,.

Thus

\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{C}}(1,V_{1}^{\mathbb{C}})
spans
V_{1}^{\mathbb{C}}
, and we can apply Lemma
4.8
to obtain a collection
\{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{j}\}_{j\in J_{0}}
of graded subalgebras (over
\mathbb{C}
) of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
as in that lemma.
By the uniqueness assertion in Lemma
4.8
, the collection
\{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{j}\}_{j\in J_{0}}
is preserved by both complex conjugation and the action
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})\operatorname{\curvearrowright}\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
.
Hence by (4) of the claim, complex conjugation will act freely on the collection
\{V_{2}^{\mathbb{C}}\cap\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{j}\}
of second layers (here we are using the fact that
[\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{j},\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}]=[\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{j},V_{1}^{\mathbb{C}}]=[\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{j},\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{j}]=V_{2}^{\mathbb{C}}\cap\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{j}
). Picking one representative from each orbit under complex conjugation, we get a subcollection
\{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{j}\}_{j\in J}
such that we get a direct sum decomposition

V_{1}^{\mathbb{C}}=\oplus_{j\in J}[(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{j}\oplus\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{j}})\cap V_{1}^{\mathbb{C}}]

and also for every
j\in J
we have

(4.14)

\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{j}\cap\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{j}}=\{0\}

by (4) of the claim. For
j\in J
, define

(4.15)

\mathfrak{g}_{j}:=\operatorname{\mathfrak{Re}}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{j}\oplus\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{j}})=\{X+\bar{X}\mid X\in\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{j}\}\,;

here we have used the fact that the real subspace is the
\mathbb{R}
-linear subspace of vectors which are fixed under complex conjugation.
In view of (
4.14
) the map
X\to X+\bar{X}
is an
\mathbb{R}
-linear graded automorphism from
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{j}
to
\mathfrak{g}_{j}
.
Using this graded isomorphism, we may pass the complex multiplication on
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{j}
to
\mathfrak{g}_{j}
; then by applying Lemma
4.1
,
we have identifications
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{j}=(\mathfrak{g}_{j}^{\mathbb{C}})_{i}
,
\overline{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}}_{j}=(\mathfrak{g}_{j}^{\mathbb{C}})_{-i}
.
Note that we have direct sum decomposition
V_{1}=\oplus_{j\in J}(\mathfrak{g}_{j}\cap V_{1})
.
Since the action
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})\operatorname{\curvearrowright}\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
preserves
the collection
\{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{j}\}_{j\in J_{0}}

and commutes with complex conjugation,
it follows from (
4.15
) that
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})\operatorname{\curvearrowright}\mathfrak{g}
preserves
\{\mathfrak{g}_{j}\}_{j\in J}
.
By hypothesis (a),
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
permutes the
\mathfrak{g}_{j}
transitively. Indeed a non-trivial orbit would generate a non-trivial
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
invariant
subspace of
V_{1}
. This subspace trivially commutes with
V_{2}
since we have already shown that
\mathfrak{g}
has step
2
. Transitivity of the action implies that
the
\mathfrak{g}_{j}
s are isomorphic to the
m
-th complex Heisenberg algebra for a fixed
m
independent of
j
. Assertions (1)-(4) now follow from the above discussion.

Thus we have established the existence of
\mathbb{F}
,
n
, and the collection
\{\mathfrak{g}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
satisfying (1)-(5). We now prove uniqueness. Suppose
\mathbb{F}^{\prime}
,
n^{\prime}
, and
\{\mathfrak{g}_{j}^{\prime}\}_{j=1}^{n^{\prime}}
also satisfy (1)-(5). Note that if either
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{F}^{\prime}=\mathbb{R}
, then
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{R}}(1,V_{1})\neq\emptyset
, while if
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
or
\mathbb{F}^{\prime}=\mathbb{C}
then
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{R}}(1,V_{1})=\emptyset
because
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}[X,\mathfrak{g}]\geq 2
for every
X\in V_{1}\setminus\{0\}
. Hence we must have
\mathbb{F}^{\prime}=\mathbb{F}
.
If
\mathbb{F}^{\prime}=\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
, then Lemma
4.8
implies that the collections of subalgebras
\{\mathfrak{g}_{j}\}
,
\{\mathfrak{g}_{j}^{\prime}\}
coincide. If
\mathbb{F}^{\prime}=\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
, then by looking at the complexification
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
, and using condition (4), we may argue as in the proof of Lemma
4.8
to see that the collections of pairs
\{(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{j})_{\pm i}\}
,
\{(\mathfrak{g}^{{}^{\prime}\mathbb{C}}_{j})_{\pm i}\}
coincide. Taking real parts we see that the collections
\{\mathfrak{g}_{j}\}
,
\{\mathfrak{g}_{j}^{\prime}\}
are the same.
∎

4.3.
A characterization using quotients

We now show that there is an alternative description of the graded algebras
which satisfy hypotheses of Theorem
4.9
as a certain type of quotient of a product.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\{V_{j}\}_{j=1}^{2})
be a nonabelian graded Lie algebra as in Theorem
4.9
, and let
\mathbb{F}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{g}_{n}
be the uniquely determined graded subalgebras as supplied by that theorem.

Let
(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}},\{\tilde{V}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{2}):=(\oplus_{k=1}^{n}\mathfrak{g}_{k},\{\oplus_{k=1}^{n}V_{j,k}\}_{j=1}^{2})
, where
\mathfrak{g}_{k}=V_{1,k}\oplus V_{2,k}
is the layer decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}_{k}
; here
\oplus_{k=1}^{n}\mathfrak{g}_{k}
denotes the abstract direct sum (the subalgebras are typically not independent in
\mathfrak{g}
). Then we get an epimorphism of graded Lie algebras
\pi:\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
by sending
\mathfrak{g}_{k}
to
\mathfrak{g}
by the inclusion. Since the
\mathfrak{g}_{k}
s are unique, they are permuted by
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
; hence we get an induced action
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})\operatorname{\curvearrowright}\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
, and
\pi
is
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
-equivariant.

Let
K:=\ker(\pi)
. Since
\pi
is
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
-equivariant, it follows that
K
is
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
-invariant. To summarize:

Lemma 4.16
.

We have a canonical embedding
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}})
, and the image is precisely
\operatorname{Stab}(K,\operatorname{Aut}(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}))
, the stabilizer of
K
in
\operatorname{Aut}(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}})
.

Henceforth we will sometimes identify
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
with
\operatorname{Stab}(K,\operatorname{Aut}(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}))
.

The first layers
\mathfrak{g}_{j}\cap V_{1}
yield a direct sum decomposition of
V_{1}
, and therefore
K\subset\tilde{V}_{2}
. Taken together, the conditions (2)-(5) from Theorem
4.9
imply that the following properites hold for the projection
\pi:\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}=\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}/K
:

(1)

The restriction of
\pi
to
\mathfrak{g}_{k}
is injective.

(2)

If
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
, then for
j\neq k
, the second layers
V_{2,j}
,
V_{2,k}
project under
\pi
to distinct subspaces of
V_{2}\simeq\tilde{V}_{2}/K
.
If
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
, then for each
j
we have a (graded) decomposition of the complexification

\mathfrak{g}_{j}^{\mathbb{C}}=(\mathfrak{g}_{j}^{\mathbb{C}})_{i}\oplus(\mathfrak{g}_{j}^{\mathbb{C}})_{-i}=(V^{\mathbb{C}}_{1,j})_{i}\oplus(V^{\mathbb{C}}_{2,j})_{i}\oplus(V^{\mathbb{C}}_{1,j})_{-i}\oplus(V^{\mathbb{C}}_{2,j})_{-i}

given by Lemma
4.1
; the projections
\pi((V^{\mathbb{C}}_{2,j})_{i})
,
\pi((V^{\mathbb{C}}_{2,j})_{-i})
are distinct subspaces which are interchanged by complex conjugation, and yield distinct pairs as
j
varies.

(3)

The action
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})\operatorname{\curvearrowright}\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
permutes the summands
\{\mathfrak{g}_{k}\}
transitively.

The converse holds:

Lemma 4.17
.

Suppose
\mathbb{F}\in\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}\}
,
m\geq 1
, and we are given the graded direct sum
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}=\oplus_{k=1}^{n}\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{k}
, where the
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{k}
s are graded isomorphic over
\mathbb{R}
to the
m
-th Heisenberg algebra over
\mathbb{F}
, and an
\mathbb{R}
-linear subspace
K\subset\tilde{V}_{2}
, such that the following hold:

(4)

K\cap\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{k}=\{0\}
for all
1\leq k\leq n
.

(5)

If
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
then we have
K+(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{j}\cap\tilde{V}_{2})\neq K+(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{k}\cap\tilde{V}_{2})
for
j\neq k
. If
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
, then for each
j
we have a (graded) decomposition of the complexification
\mathfrak{g}_{j}^{\mathbb{C}}=(\mathfrak{g}_{j}^{\mathbb{C}})_{i}\oplus(\mathfrak{g}_{j}^{\mathbb{C}})_{-i}=(V^{\mathbb{C}}_{1,j})_{i}\oplus(V^{\mathbb{C}}_{2,j})_{i}\oplus(V^{\mathbb{C}}_{1,j})_{-i}\oplus(V^{\mathbb{C}}_{2,j})_{-i}
given by Lemma
4.1
; the projections
\pi((V^{\mathbb{C}}_{2,j})_{i})
,
\pi((V^{\mathbb{C}}_{2,j})_{-i})
are distinct subspaces which are interchanged by complex conjugation, and yield distinct pairs as
j
varies.

(6)

The stabilizer of
K
in
\operatorname{Aut}(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}})
permutes the factors
\{\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{k}\}
transitively.

Then the quotient
\mathfrak{g}:=\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}/K
with the grading
\{\tilde{V}_{1},\tilde{V}_{2}/K\}
induced from
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem
4.9
.

Proof.

Let
\pi:\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
be the quotient map.

Suppose
X\in V_{1}\setminus\{0\}
. Pick
\tilde{X}\in\tilde{V}_{1}
with
\pi(\tilde{X})=X
, and let
\tilde{X}_{1}+\ldots+\tilde{X}_{n}
be the decomposition induced by
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}=\oplus_{k}\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{k}
. Then
\tilde{X}_{k}\neq 0
for some
k
, and there is a
\tilde{Y}\in\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{k}
such that
[\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}]\in V_{2,k}\setminus\{0\}
. Now
[X,\pi(\tilde{Y})]=\pi([\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}])=\pi([X_{k},\tilde{Y}])\neq 0
by
(4)
. Therefore
V_{1}
contains no nonzero elements of rank zero.

If
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
, then
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{R}}(1,\tilde{V}_{1})
spans
\tilde{V}_{1}
, so
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{C}}(1,V_{1}^{\mathbb{C}})
spans
V_{1}^{\mathbb{C}}
. If
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
, then by Lemma
4.1
we have the decomposition

\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}=\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{\mathbb{C}}/K^{\mathbb{C}}=(\oplus_{k=1}^{n}\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{k}^{\mathbb{C}})/K^{\mathbb{C}}=(\oplus_{k=1}^{n}[(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{k}^{\mathbb{C}})_{i}\oplus(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{k}^{\mathbb{C}})_{-i}])/K^{\mathbb{C}}\,,

so
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{C}}(1,V_{1}^{\mathbb{C}})
also spans
V_{1}^{\mathbb{C}}
in this case.

To see that
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
acts irreducibly on the first layer suppose that
W\subset V_{1}
is a nontrivial subspace invariant under
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
and consider first the case
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
.
Then in particular
W
is invariant under the action of the subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}})
consisting of elements that act trivially on
V_{2}
. So we may apply Lemma
4.5
to conclude that
W=\oplus_{j\in J}V_{1,j}
for some nonempty subset
J\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
Now
(6)
implies that
J=\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
Now consider the case
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
. Then the map
\Phi
which acts as complex multiplication by
i
on the first layer and
multiplication by
-1
on the second layer belong to
\operatorname{Aut}(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}})
. Since it preserves
K
it also defines
an element of
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
. Thus
W
is a
\mathbb{C}
-linear subspace of
V_{1}
. Now we can conclude as before by
considering the subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}})
consisting of
\mathbb{C}
-linear elements that act trivially on
V_{2}
.
Thus
(\mathfrak{g},\{V_{j}\}_{j=1}^{2})
is a nonrigid graded algebra such that
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
acts irreducibly on the first layer; in particular hypothesis (a) of Theorem
4.9
holds. This concludes the proof.
∎

Definition 4.18
.

A
product quotient
is a Carnot group
G
of the form
\tilde{G}/\exp(K)
, where the graded Lie algebra
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
of
\tilde{G}
and
K\subset\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
satisfy the assumptions of Lemma
4.17
, and in the case
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
we have
n\geq 2
. We will use the term product quotient to refer to both Carnot groups and their graded Lie algebras.

Remark 4.19
.

By virtue of Theorem
4.9
and Lemma
4.17
, product quotients are precisely the Carnot groups whose associated graded algebra satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem
4.9
, with the exception of abelian groups and real Heisenberg groups. We have chosen to exclude the latter two cases from the definition because our main objective is to study rigidity phenomena which fail to hold in those cases. We emphasize that a complex Heisenberg group is a product quotient, while a real Heisenberg group is not.

We close this subsection by discussing a few examples of product quotients.

The simplest examples of product quotients are simply products, i.e. they are of the form
\mathfrak{g}=\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}=\oplus_{j=1}^{n}\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{j}
,
K=\{0\}
, where each summand
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{j}
is a copy of a fixed Heisenberg algebra over
\mathbb{F}\in\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}\}
, and
n\geq 2
if
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
.

Example 4.20
.

(Diagonal product quotient) Let
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}=\oplus_{j=1}^{n}\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{j}
, where
n\geq 3
,
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{j}
is a copy of the first real Heisenberg group, with basis
\tilde{X}_{3j-2},\tilde{X}_{3j-1},\tilde{X}_{3j}
,
[\tilde{X}_{3j-2},\tilde{X}_{3j-1}]=-\tilde{X}_{3j}
. Let
K\subset\tilde{V}_{2}
be the diagonal subspace

. Then the permutation action
S_{n}\operatorname{\curvearrowright}\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
leaves
K
invariant, and hence Lemma
4.17
(6)
holds. We may obtain similar examples by using the
m
-th real or complex Heisenberg group, instead of the first real Heisenberg group.

We emphasize that it is necessary to have

in Example

for the conditions in Lemma
4.17
to be satisfied, because otherwise Lemma
4.17
(5)
would be violated. We also point out that if

and

is the diagonal subspace, then:

•

If the

does not satisfy the assumptions of Lemma

is the second real Heisenberg algebra which satisfies Theorem

•

If the

s are copies of the first complex Heisenberg group, then although
K
does not satisfy the assumptions of Lemma
4.17
. The quotient
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}/K
is the second complex Heisenberg algebra, which satisfies Theorem
4.9
, and is a product quotient. Note also that the same graded algebra has an alternate description as
\mathfrak{h}_{2}^{\mathbb{C}}/K^{\prime}
, where

Example 4.21
.

(
\mathbb{Z}_{5}
-product quotients)

Let
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}=\oplus_{j=1}^{5}\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{j}

.

Example 4.22
.

(Permutation product quotients) We may generalize the two preceding examples as follows. Let
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}=\oplus_{j=1}^{n}\mathfrak{g}_{j}
, where
n\geq 2
and
\mathfrak{g}_{j}
is a copy of the
m
-th real or complex Heisenberg algebra. Then permutation of the copies of

and

.

Example 4.23
.

Let
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}=\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{1}\oplus\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}
where
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}
is a copy of the complex Heisenberg graded algebra

. Let
\tilde{V}_{2,i}
be the second layer of

, so

Example 4.24
.

(Decomposable product quotients)

Suppose

is a product quotient, and let

be the direct sum of

copies of

. Then

4.4.
Decomposition of product quotients

The goal of this subsection is Lemma
4.27
, which asserts that every product quotient admits a canonical decomposition as a direct sum of indecomposable summands which are conformal, in the sense of Definition
4.25
below.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\{V_{j}\}_{j=1}^{2})
be a product quotient as discussed in the preceding subsection. Hence there are canonically defined graded subalgebras
\mathfrak{g}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{g}_{n}\subset\mathfrak{g}
, and a graded epimorphism
\pi:\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}:=\oplus_{j}\mathfrak{g}_{j}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
, where
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
has the direct sum grading, and the kernel
K\subset\tilde{V}_{2}
satisfies
(4)
–

(6)
above. We have canonical actions

\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})=\operatorname{Stab}(K,\operatorname{Aut}(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}))\operatorname{\curvearrowright}\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}

and
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})\operatorname{\curvearrowright}\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

Definition 4.25
.

A product quotient
(\mathfrak{g},\{V_{j}\}_{j=1}^{2})
is
conformal
if the action
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})\operatorname{\curvearrowright}\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
preserves a conformal structure on
\tilde{V}_{2}
, i.e. if there is an inner product on
\tilde{V}_{2}
that is
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
-invariant up to scale.

Remark 4.26
.

We emphasize to the reader that graded automorphisms of conformal product quotients act conformally on the second layer, but not necessarily on the first layer.

Lemma 4.27
.

Every product quotient
\mathfrak{g}
has graded direct sum decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\oplus_{k}\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{k}
, where the summands
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{k}
are conformal product quotients, and the decomposition is respected by
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
, i.e. each
\Phi\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
induces a permutation of the summands. Moreover, this decomposition is unique.

The proof of the lemma will occupy the remainder of this subsection. We now fix a product quotient
(\mathfrak{g},\{V_{j}\}_{j=1}^{2})
.

Our approach to proving Lemma
4.27
is to find a partition of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
that is compatible with
K
and the action
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})\operatorname{\curvearrowright}\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

Definition 4.28
.

A partition
\{1,\ldots,n\}=J_{1}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup J_{\ell}
is
K
-compatible
if the direct sum decomposition

(4.29)

\tilde{V}_{2}=(\oplus_{j\in J_{1}}\tilde{V}_{2,j})\oplus\ldots\oplus(\oplus_{j\in J_{\ell}}\tilde{V}_{2,j})\,

is compatible with
K
:

K=(K\cap\oplus_{j\in J_{1}}\tilde{V}_{2,j})\oplus\ldots\oplus(K\cap\oplus_{j\in J_{\ell}}\tilde{V}_{2,j}).

Lemma 4.30
.

(1)

A partition
\{1,\ldots,n\}=J_{1}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup J_{\ell}
is
K
-compatible iff
K
is invariant under each projection
\pi_{J_{k}}:\tilde{V}_{2}\rightarrow\oplus_{j\in J_{k}}V_{2,j}
.

(2)

A
K
-compatible partition gives rise to graded decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}
. If the partition is invariant under the action
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})\operatorname{\curvearrowright}\{1,\ldots,n\}
, then the resulting graded decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}
is
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
-invariant.

(3)

There exists a unique finest
K
-compatible partition.

(4)

The partition in (3) induces an
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
-invariant graded decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}
into product quotients.

Proof.

(1) and (2) are straightforward.

(3). If
J_{1}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup J_{\ell}
and
J_{1}^{\prime}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup J_{\ell^{\prime}}^{\prime}
are both
K
-compatible then (1) implies that
K
is invariant under
\pi_{J_{k}\cap J_{k^{\prime}}^{\prime}}=\pi_{J_{k}}\circ\pi_{J_{k^{\prime}}^{\prime}}
for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,\ell\}
,
k^{\prime}\in\{1,\ldots,\ell^{\prime}\}
. Hence the common refinement
J_{1}^{\prime\prime}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup J_{\ell^{\prime\prime}}^{\prime\prime}
is also
K
-compatible by (1). This implies (3).

(4). This follows readily from the definitions.
∎

We now let
\{1,\ldots,n\}=J_{1}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup J_{\ell}
be the finest
K
-compatible partition given by the lemma. Letting

\displaystyle\hat{V}_{k}:=\oplus_{j\in J_{k}}\tilde{V}_{2,j}\,,\qquad\hat{K}_{k}:=K\cap\hat{V}_{k}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{k}:=(\oplus_{j\in J_{k}}V_{1,j})\oplus(\hat{V}_{k}/\hat{K}_{k})\subset\mathfrak{g}\,,

we have
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
-invariant decompositions

\tilde{V}_{2}=\oplus_{k}\hat{V}_{k}\,,\qquad K=\oplus_{k}\hat{K}_{k}\,,\qquad\mathfrak{g}=\oplus_{k}\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{k}\,.

We consider the action
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})\operatorname{\curvearrowright}\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
. Let
D\subset\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
be the subgroup that leaves each summand
\mathfrak{g}_{j}\subset\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
invariant, and acts on each summand by
\mathbb{F}
-linear automorphisms. Then
D
has finite index in
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
because by Lemma
4.6
the
\mathbb{F}
-linear automorphisms of
\mathfrak{g}_{j}
have index at most
2
in its full automorphism group (as a graded algebra over
\mathbb{R}
). Thus
D
acts on
\tilde{V}_{2}=\oplus_{j}\tilde{V}_{2,j}\simeq\mathbb{F}^{n}
by linear transformations that are diagonalizable over
\mathbb{F}
, and even when
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
, these admit generalized eigenspace decompositions over
\mathbb{R}
(i.e. direct sum decompositions into
2
-dimensional irreducible subspaces).

Lemma 4.31
.

If
A\in D
and
k\in\{1,\ldots,\ell\}
, then the restriction of
A
to
\hat{V}_{k}
has a single eigenvalue if
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
or eigenvalues with the same modulus if
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

Suppose
A\mbox{\Large$|$\normalsize}_{\hat{V}_{k}}
has distinct eigenvalues if
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
, or two eigenvalues with distinct modulus, if
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
. Then the (generalized) eigenspaces of
A\mbox{\Large$|$\normalsize}_{\hat{V}_{k}}
on
\hat{V}_{k}
give a nontrivial direct sum decomposition of
\hat{V}_{k}
corresponding to a nontrivial partition
J_{k}=\bar{J}_{1}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup\bar{J}_{\bar{\ell}}
:

(4.32)

\hat{V}_{k}=(\oplus_{j\in\bar{J}_{1}}\tilde{V}_{2,j})\oplus\ldots\oplus(\oplus_{j\in\bar{J}_{\ell}}\tilde{V}_{2,j})\,.

Notice that
\operatorname{id}_{\tilde{V}_{k}}=\pi_{1}+\ldots+\pi_{\bar{\ell}}
where the
\pi_{i}
s are idempotents realizing the decomposition (
4.32
), and each
\pi_{j}
is a polynomial in
A
with coefficients in
\mathbb{R}
. Since
K
is
D
-invariant, we therefore obtain a nontrivial decomposition

\hat{K}_{k}=(\hat{K}_{k}\cap\oplus_{j\in\bar{J}_{1}}\tilde{V}_{2,j})\oplus\ldots\oplus(\hat{K}_{k}\cap\oplus_{j\in\bar{J}_{\bar{\ell}}}\tilde{V}_{2,j})\,.

This contradicts the assumption that
J_{1}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup J_{\ell}
is the finest
K
-compatible decomposition of
\tilde{V}_{2}
.
∎

Pick
k\in\{1,\ldots,\ell\}
. Choose an inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{k}
on
\hat{V}_{k}
such that:

•

The subspaces
\{V_{2,j}\}_{j\in J_{k}}
are
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{k}
-orthogonal.

•

If
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
, then complex multiplication is
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{k}
-orthogonal.

In view of the definition of
D
and Lemma
4.31
,
D
acts conformally on
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{k}
. Since
D
has finite index in the stabilizer
\operatorname{Stab}(J_{k},\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g}))
, there is an inner product

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{k}^{\prime}
on
\hat{V}_{k}
on which
\operatorname{Stab}(J_{k},\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g}))
acts conformally.

We now turn to the uniqueness assertion.

We will show that the summands of any decomposition as in the statement of Lemma
4.27
are nonabelian and indecomposable; it then follows from
[
KMX20
, Lemma 7.3]
that the decomposition is unique. The invariance under
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
follows from uniqueness.

We claim that for every
k
, the summand
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{k}
cannot be decomposed nontrivially as a direct sum of graded algebras. Arguing indirectly, let
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{k}=\mathfrak{h}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{2}
be a nontrivial decomposition of
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{k}
as a direct sum. The
2
-parameter family of graded automorphisms arising from independent scalings of the summands will contradict the fact that
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{k}
is conformal, unless one of the two summands
\mathfrak{h}_{i}
has trivial second layer. However, this would mean that
\mathfrak{h}_{i}
is abelian, and moreover is an ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
contained in the first layer. This is impossible, since every nonzero element of the first layer of
\mathfrak{g}
has rank
\geq 1
. This proves the claim.

This completes the proof of Lemma
4.27
.

5.
Rigidity of product-quotients

The remainder of the paper is concerned with the rigidity of mappings
G\supset U\rightarrow U^{\prime}\subset G
where
G
is a product quotient – one of the nonrigid Carnot groups that emerged from the structure theorem in Section
4
. In this section we will state the main rigidity result for such groups (Theorem
5.1
),
and give the proof using results from Sections
6
-
8
.

For the remainder of the paper we let
G=\tilde{G}/\exp(K)
be a product quotient (Definition
4.18
), and will use the notation from Lemma
4.17
for
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}=\oplus_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}
,
K\subset\tilde{V}_{2}
, etc.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
G=\tilde{G}/\exp(K)
be a product quotient of homogeneous dimension
\nu
, and
\mathfrak{g}=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}
be the layer decomposition of its graded Lie algebra. Suppose
p>\nu
,
U\subset G
is open, and
f:G\supset U\rightarrow G
is a
W^{1,p}_{\operatorname{loc}}
-mapping such that the sign of the determinant of
D_{P}f(x):\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is constant almost everywhere. Then there is a locally constant assignment of a permutation
U\ni x\mapsto\sigma_{x}\in S_{n}
such that:

(1)

For a.e.
x\in U
, the Pansu derivative
D_{P}f(x)
permutes the subalgebras
\mathfrak{g}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{g}_{n}\subset\mathfrak{g}
in accordance with the permutation
\sigma
:

D_{P}f(x)(\mathfrak{g}_{i})=\mathfrak{g}_{\sigma_{x}(i)}\,.

(2)

For every
x\in U
, cosets of
G_{i}
are mapped to cosets of
G_{\sigma_{x}(i)}
locally near
x
: there is neighborhood
V_{x}
of
x
such that for every
1\leq j\leq n
, and every
y\in V
,

f(V_{x}\cap yG_{i})\subset f(y)G_{\sigma_{x}(i)}\,.

Proof.

Note that (2) follows from (1) by Lemma
2.30
.

We may assume that
n\geq 2
since otherwise the theorem is trivial.

Case 1. Each
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}
is either a copy of a complex Heisenberg algebra or a real Heisenberg algebra
\mathfrak{h}_{m}
with
m\geq 2
.
This case is handled in Section
8
.

Case 2. Each
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}
is a copy of the first real Heisenberg algebra for all
i
.

Case 2a.
\mathfrak{g}
does not have a nontrivial decomposition as a direct sum of graded Lie algebras.
Then
\dim K\geq 1
, and the cases when
\dim K=1
and
\dim K\geq 2
are treated in Sections
6
and
7
, respectively.

Case 2b.
\mathfrak{g}
has a nontrivial direct sum decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\oplus_{j}\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{j}
where the
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{j}
s are indecomposable.
By
[
KMX20
, Theorem 1.1]
, after shrinking the domain and postcomposing with an isometry
G\rightarrow G
which permutes the factors, we may assume that
f
is a product mapping. Applying Case 2a to each factor mapping, the theorem follows.
∎

We remark that the proofs in Cases 1, 2a, and 2b are all ultimately based on an application of the Pullback Theorem (Theorem
2.1
),

\int_{U}f_{P}^{*}(\alpha)\wedge d(\varphi\beta)=0,

with
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(U)
and suitable closed left invariant forms
\alpha
and
\beta
which satisfy the conditions

\deg\alpha+\deg\beta=N-1

and
\operatorname{wt}(\alpha)+\operatorname{wt}(\beta)\leq-\nu+1
.
A more detailed motivation for the choice of the forms
\alpha
and
\beta
is given at the beginning of
Sections
6
-
8
where the three different cases are treated.
Here we just outline some guiding principles which helped us to identify good classes of left invariant forms.

(i)

The pullback of
\alpha
by the Pansu differential
D_{P}f(x)
(or its lift to
D_{P}\tilde{f}(x)\in\operatorname{Stab}(K,\operatorname{Aut}{\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}})
, see Lemma
4.16
) should
detect the permutation
\sigma_{x}
without depending on too detailed information on
D_{P}f
;

(ii)

We should work with closed forms
\alpha
modulo exact forms (see
[
KMX20
, Lemma 4.8]
);

(iii)

There should be a sufficient supply of “complementary” forms
\beta
which are closed but not exact.

Regarding (i) we found it useful to look for forms which are related to the action of
D_{P}f
on the second layer
V_{2}

(or its lift
\tilde{V}_{2}
)
as this action captures the permutation but is in general much simpler.
This would suggest to take
\alpha
as linear combination of wedge products of forms in
\Lambda^{1}V_{2}
.
Such forms are, however, in general not closed: the exterior derivative maps
\Lambda^{1}V_{2}
to
\Lambda^{2}V_{1}
and in fact to

\oplus_{i=1}^{n}\Lambda^{2}V_{1,i}
. Thus we consider linear combinations of wedge products of
\Lambda^{2}V_{1,i}
and
\Lambda^{1}V_{2}
, and look for closed forms modulo exact forms.

6.

for product quotients

6.2.
Reduction to convenient

by anisotropic dilation

For the remainder of this section, we will assume that
\dim K=1

We now show that without loss of generality we may take
K=\operatorname{span}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{Y}_{i})

Lemma 6.5
.

There exists a graded automorphism
\Psi:\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
such that
\Psi(\operatorname{span}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{Y}_{i}))=K
. In particular,
\Psi
induces an isomorphism of graded algebras
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}/(\sum_{i}\tilde{Y}_{i})\rightarrow\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}/K
.

Proof.

Suppose that
K\subset\oplus_{i\neq i^{\prime}}V_{2,i}
for some
i^{\prime}
.
Since
\operatorname{Stab}(K,\operatorname{Aut}(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}))
acts transitively on the collection of subspaces
\{V_{1,i}\}_{1\leq i\leq n}
and hence also on the collection
\{V_{2,i}\}_{1\leq i\leq n}

we deduce that
K\subset\oplus_{i\neq i^{\prime}}V_{2,i}
for all
i^{\prime}
, so
K\subset\cap_{i^{\prime}}(\oplus_{i\neq i^{\prime}}V_{2,i})=\{0\}
. This contradicts the fact that
\dim K=1
.

Therefore we have
K=\operatorname{span}(\sum_{i}\mu_{i}\tilde{Y}_{i})
where
\mu_{i}\neq 0
for all
i
.

For every
i
, let
\Psi_{i}:\mathfrak{h}\simeq\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}\rightarrow\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}
be a graded automorphism with
\Psi_{i}\mid_{\tilde{V}_{2,i}}=\mu_{i}\operatorname{id}_{\tilde{V}_{2,i}}
, and define
\Psi:=\oplus\Psi_{i}:\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
. Then
\Psi(\sum_{i}\tilde{Y}_{i})=\sum_{i}\mu_{i}\tilde{Y}_{i}
as desired.
∎

In view of Lemma
6.5
we will from now on assume without loss of generality that

(6.6)

K=\operatorname{span}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{Y}_{i}).

Proposition 6.7
.

If
\Phi\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
and
\tilde{\Phi}\in\operatorname{Stab}(K,\operatorname{Aut}(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}))
is the unique lift provided by Lemma
4.16
, then there exist
a
\lambda\not=0
and a permutation
\sigma\in S_{n}
such that

(6.8)

\tilde{\Phi}^{*}\tilde{\tau}_{i}=\lambda\tilde{\tau}_{\sigma^{-1}(i)},\quad\tilde{\Phi}^{*}\tilde{\gamma}_{i}=\lambda\tilde{\gamma}_{\sigma^{-1}(i)},\quad\Phi^{*}\gamma_{i}=\lambda\gamma_{\sigma^{-1}(i)}\quad\forall i=1,\ldots,n,

or, equivalently,

(6.9)

\tilde{\Phi}_{*}\tilde{Y}_{i}=\lambda\tilde{Y}_{\sigma(i)},\quad\tilde{\Phi}_{*}(\tilde{Z}_{i})=\lambda\tilde{Z}_{\sigma(i)},\quad\Phi_{*}(Z_{i})=\lambda Z_{\sigma(i)}\quad\forall i=1,\ldots,n

where
Z_{i}=X_{3i-2}\wedge X_{3i-1}
.

Proof.

Since
\Phi
permutes the collections
\{\tilde{V}_{1,i}\}
,
\{\tilde{V}_{2,i}\}
there exist

\mu_{i}
such that

\Phi\tilde{Y}_{i}=\mu_{i}\tilde{Y}_{\sigma(i)}.

Since
\Phi
preserves

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\tilde{Y}_{j}
must be an eigenvector of

\Phi\sum_{j=1}^{n}\tilde{Y}_{j}=\lambda\sum_{j=1}^{n}\tilde{Y}_{j}.

Comparison gives
\mu_{i}=\lambda
for all
i=1,\ldots,n
.
From this identity all the assertions follow easily.
∎

6.3.
Local constancy of the permutation

Let
f:G\supset U\rightarrow G
be a
W^{1,p}_{\operatorname{loc}}
-mapping for some
p>\nu
, and assume that the Pansu differential
D_{P}f(x):\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is an isomorphism for a.e.
x\in U
.

Our approach to controlling
f
is motivated by the treatment of products
\prod_{j}\mathbb{H}
, where we applied the pullback theorem to the wedge product

f_{P}^{*}(d\tilde{\tau}_{j}\wedge\tilde{\tau}_{j})\wedge d(\varphi\hat{\beta})

where
1\leq j\leq n
and
\hat{\beta}
is a suitably chosen closed codegree
4

\hat{\beta}
may be expressed as

\beta=i_{Y_{m}}i_{X_{3m-2}}i_{X_{3m-1}}\omega=\pm d\tilde{\tau}_{1}\wedge\tilde{\tau}_{1}\wedge\ldots\widehat{d\tilde{\tau}_{m}\wedge\tilde{\tau}_{m}}\wedge\ldots\wedge d\tilde{\tau}_{n}\wedge\tilde{\tau}_{n}

for some

and

Note that in the present context the forms
\tilde{\tau}_{i}
are not well-defined because they do not descend to the quotient

. We observe that the differences
\tilde{\tau}_{i,j}:=\tilde{\tau}_{i}-\tilde{\tau}_{j}

\tau_{i,j}
for all

(6.10)

\omega_{ij}:=(\gamma_{i}+\gamma_{j})\wedge(\tau_{i,j})

whose pullback to
\tilde{G}
is
(d\tilde{\tau}_{i}+d\tilde{\tau}_{j})\wedge(\tilde{\tau}_{i}-\tilde{\tau}_{j})

3

It follows from Proposition
6.7
that

(6.11)

f_{P}^{*}(\omega_{ij})=\lambda^{2}(x)(\omega_{\sigma_{x}^{-1}(i)\sigma_{x}^{-1}(j)}).

At the heart of the matter are the following two identities.

Lemma 6.12
.

Let

and set

(6.13)

where

is the image of

(6.14)

Moreover,

(6.15)

\displaystyle\,\omega_{kl}\wedge d(\varphi i_{X}\beta)=\begin{cases}0&\hbox{if $k\neq m$ and $l\neq m$}\\

Using (

\displaystyle d(i_{X}\beta)=

\displaystyle\,-d(i_{Y_{m}}i_{X_{3m-2}}i_{X_{3m-1}}i_{X}\omega)=i_{Y_{m}}d(i_{X_{3m-2}}i_{X_{3m-1}}i_{X}\omega)

\displaystyle=

Applying (

\displaystyle\,\omega_{kl}\wedge d(\varphi i_{X}\beta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\omega_{kl}\wedge d\varphi\wedge i_{X}i_{Y_{m}}i_{X_{3m-2}}i_{X_{3m-1}}\omega

\displaystyle\,(\omega_{kl}\wedge d\varphi)({X_{3m-1}},X_{3m-2},Y_{m},X)\,\omega

\displaystyle\omega_{kl}(X_{3m-1},X_{3m-2},Y_{m})

The permutation

7.
Indecomposable product-quotients with
\dim K\geq 2

In this section we will retain the notation and conventions from Subsection
6.1
. The goal of this section is to prove Theorem
5.1
for indecomposable product quotients
G=(\prod_{i=1}^{n}G_{i})/\exp(K)
where
G_{i}
is a copy of the first Heisenberg group and
\dim K\geq 2
. The argument in this case is significantly more complicated than the case when
\dim K=1
, so we will first give some motivation and an overview in Subsection
7.1
.

7.1.
Overview of the proof

We recall that in the
\dim K=1
case, our approach was to apply the pullback theorem to the closed left invariant forms
\omega_{ij}
and
i_{X}\beta
, which are of degree
3
and codegree
4
, respectively, and have appropriate weight. It turns out that when
\dim K\geq 2
such forms typically do not exist, so we use a different strategy.

We first reduce to the case when the lift
\tilde{\Phi}\in\operatorname{Stab}(K,\operatorname{Aut}(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}))
of every graded automorphism
\Phi\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
acts orthogonally on
\tilde{V}_{2}
with respect to the inner product induced by the basis
\{\tilde{Y}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{Y}_{n}\}\subset\tilde{V}_{2}
. In particular letting
\tilde{D}_{P}f(x)
denote the lift of the Pansu differential
D_{P}f(x)
of our
Sobolev mapping
f:U\rightarrow G
, we note that
f
is somewhat reminiscent of a conformal mapping, in the sense that its differential is conformal, although only when restricted to the second layer.

The above observation suggests that we look to the proof of Liouville’s theorem for inspiration. In its simplest form it states that
a Lipschitz map
f:U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
which satisfies
\nabla f\in SO(n)
a.e. has locally constant
gradient. The key idea is to use the relations
\mathop{\mathrm{curl}}\nabla f=0
and

\operatorname{div}\operatorname{cof}\nabla f=0
for every Lipschitz map. In connection with the pointwise identity

\operatorname{cof}\nabla f=\nabla f
a.e. they imply that
f
is weakly harmonic and hence smooth.
The differential constraints on
f
can be derived
by pulling back constant differential forms of degree
1
and codegree
1
.
In our setting we are interested in the action of the Pansu differential on the second layer.
This action can be captured by using special degree
2
forms in the first layer (namely elements of

\oplus_{i=1}^{n}\Lambda^{2}V_{1,i}
). Thus the analogy with the proof of Liouville’s theorem suggests the use of the closed degree
2
forms
\gamma_{i}=\theta_{3i-2}\wedge\theta_{3i-1}
and closed codegree 2
forms of the type
i_{Z}\omega
where

\omega
is a volume form on
G
(see (
6.4
)) and
Z
is a two-vector in
\oplus_{i=1}^{n}\Lambda_{2}V_{1,i}
.
Indeed Lemma
7.14
can be seen as a counterpart of the property
\mathop{\mathrm{curl}}\nabla f=0

while (
7.25
) can be viewed as a counterpart of the identity
\operatorname{div}\operatorname{cof}\nabla f=0
.

The use of these forms will show that the lift
\tilde{D}_{P}f(x)\in\operatorname{Stab}(K,\operatorname{Aut}(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}))
of the Pansu differential
D_{P}f(x)
acts as a constant on
K

(see Theorem
7.24
below).
We then look at the group
H^{\prime}
of graded automorphisms of
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}=\oplus_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}
which act as the identity on

K
. If the induced action
H^{\prime}\operatorname{\curvearrowright}I=\{1,\ldots,n\}
(which reflects the permutation of the collection
\{V_{2,i}\}
by such a graded automorphism) is trivial we are done.
If not, then we have the induced decomposition
I=I_{1}\sqcup\ldots I_{k}
into
H^{\prime}
-orbits,
and it is easy to see that the projection of
K
onto each of the corresponding subspaces

V_{I_{j}}=\oplus_{k\in I_{j}}V_{2,k}
is one-dimensional (see Lemma
7.26
below).
Then we can use the argument for the case
\dim K=1
to show that the permutation induced by
D_{P}f(x)

is locally constant on each orbit and hence locally constant (see Section
7.5
below).

7.2.
Set-up

Retaining the notation from Section
6.1
we consider
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}
where
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}
is a copy of the first Heisenberg algebra, and let
\mathfrak{g}=\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}/K
be an indecomposable product-quotient.
By Lemma
4.27
there exists a scalar product
b
on
\tilde{V}_{2}
such that the action of
\operatorname{Stab}(K,\operatorname{Aut}(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}))

is conformal, i.e., invariant up to scaling; in other words,
\mathfrak{g}
is a conformal product quotient, see Definition
4.25
.

Proposition 7.1
.

There exists an anisotropic dilation

\delta_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}
such that
the action of
\operatorname{Stab}(\delta_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}K,\operatorname{Aut}(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}))
is conformal with respect to the standard scalar product on
V_{2}

which is characterized by the identities

(\tilde{Y}_{i},\tilde{Y}_{j})=\delta_{ij}
.

Proof.

We first note that for every anisotropic dilation
\delta_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}
the quotient

\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}/\delta_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}K
is an indecomposable product quotient and
that

Remark 8.14
.

The choice of the forms
\alpha_{k,l}
can be motivated by our guiding principle to look for closed left invariant forms
modulo exact left invariant forms (see
[
KMX20
, Lemma 4.8]
) ). First note by (
2.22
) we have

d\alpha(X,Y)=-\alpha([X,Y])
for
\alpha\in\Lambda^{1}V_{2}
.
If we identify two-forms on
V_{1}
with the dual space of two-vectors by setting
\alpha(X\wedge Y)=\alpha(X,Y)
,
then we see that exterior differentiation
d:\Lambda^{1}V_{2}\to(\Lambda_{2}V_{1})^{\prime}

is dual map of
-L_{[]}
. Now consider first the case that
\mathfrak{g}=\oplus_{i}\mathfrak{h}_{i}
is a direct sum of
n
copies of the same
(higher) Heisenberg group. A natural space of closed left invariant forms to detect the permutation
\sigma_{x}
induced by
the Pansu differential is the space
\oplus_{i}\Lambda^{2}V_{1,i}
.
In this case the relevant space of exact forms is given by
d\Lambda^{1}V_{2}=\oplus_{i}d\Lambda^{1}V_{2,i}
.
By the duality between
d
and
-L_{[]}
all elements of
d\Lambda^{1}V_{2}
vanish on all the space
\ker_{i}
and the sets

\ker_{i}
are characterized by this condition. Thus to find a basis for the quotient space
\oplus_{i}\Lambda^{2}V_{1,i}/d\Lambda^{1}V_{2}

it is natural to look for elements of
\Lambda^{2}V_{1,i}
whose restriction to
\ker_{i}
does not vanish. This leads to the basis
\alpha_{k,l}

used in the proof.
The fact that there exist non-zero
X\in\ker_{i}
is crucial for finding
closed
codegree forms
\beta=i_{Z}i_{X}\omega

which interact well with the forms
\alpha_{k,l}
.

If
\mathfrak{g}
is a product quotient
\oplus_{i}\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}/K

then it is still true that each element of
d\Lambda^{1}V_{2}
vanishes on each space
\ker_{i}
. Hence the collection of cosets

\alpha_{k,l}+d\Lambda^{1}V_{2}
is still linearly independent in the quotient space and the proof shows that it is sufficiently rich to detect the permutation

\sigma_{x}
.

s s -stability for W s , n / s W^{s,n/s} -harmonic maps in homotopy groups
Katarzyna Mazowiecka, Armin Schikorra

Abstract
. We study s s -dependence for minimizing W s , n / s W^{s,n/s} -harmonic maps u : 𝕊 n → 𝕊 ℓ u\colon\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{S}^{\ell} in homotopy classes. Sacks–Uhlenbeck theory shows that, for each s s , minimizers exist in a generating subset of π n ​ ( 𝕊 ℓ ) \pi_{n}({\mathbb{S}}^{\ell}) . We show that this generating subset can be chosen locally constant in s s . We also show that as s s varies the minimal W s , n / s W^{s,n/s} -energy in each homotopy class changes continuously. In particular, we provide progress to a question raised by Mironescu [ 16 ] and Brezis–Mironescu [ 3 ] .

Introduction

We study minimizing
W^{s,p}
-harmonic maps between spheres in homotopy classes, which are defined as maps
u\in W^{s,p}({\mathbb{S}}^{n},{\mathbb{S}}^{\ell})
with least energy

(1.1)

\mathcal{E}_{s,p}(u)\coloneqq\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y=[u]^{p}_{W^{s,p}({\mathbb{S}}^{n},{\mathbb{S}}^{\ell})}

among maps of the same homotopy.
Here
s\in(0,1)
,
p>1
. In (
1.1
) we take the
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
-Euclidean distance and
\mathbb{R}^{\ell+1}
-Euclidean distance in the numerator and in the denominator, respectively.

A natural question arises: given
\alpha\in\pi_{n}({\mathbb{S}}^{\ell})
, is the infimum of
\mathcal{E}_{s,p}
attained in
\alpha
? In other words, can we find a map
u\in W^{s,p}({\mathbb{S}}^{n},{\mathbb{S}}^{\ell})

u\in\alpha
such that

\mathcal{E}_{s,p}(u)\leq\mathcal{E}_{s,p}(v)\quad\forall v\in W^{s,p}({\mathbb{S}}^{n},{\mathbb{S}}^{\ell}),\quad v\in\alpha.

If
p>\frac{n}{s}
the answer is yes, by standard methods of calculus of variation, due to the compact embedding of
W^{s,p}({\mathbb{S}}^{n},{\mathbb{S}}^{\ell})
into
C^{0}({\mathbb{S}}^{n},{\mathbb{S}}^{\ell})
. If
p<\frac{n}{s}
it was shown by Brezis–Nirenberg in
[
4
]
that no homotopy theory can be defined for maps in
W^{s,p}({\mathbb{S}}^{n},{\mathbb{S}}^{\ell})
and the infimum energy of
\mathcal{E}_{s,p}
in any homotopy class is zero.
Thus, throughout this work we will focus on the critical, conformally invariant case
p=\frac{n}{s}
. By
[
4
]
, it is known that the standard notions of homotopy can be extended to
W^{s,\frac{n}{s}}
-Sobolev maps, see also
[
15

For

set

In the case of maps between spheres of same dimension
u\colon{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
, i.e., when the homotopy classes are given by their degree we instead write

(1.3)

\#_{s}d\coloneqq\inf_{u\in W^{s,\frac{n}{s}}({\mathbb{S}}^{n},{\mathbb{S}}^{\ell}),\deg(u)=d}\mathcal{E}_{s,\frac{n}{s}}(u),\quad d\in\mathbb{Z}.

we showed

Remark 1.7

(1)

The modulus of continuity in

(2)

In this paper, to ensure clarity in our presentation, we focus on the case when the target manifold is a sphere. Nonetheless, it should be easy to extend the results to the case of a compact Lie group in the target.

(3)

It seems that an extension of our results to general target manifold is more challenging — the only obstacle is regularity, but it is unclear even for minimizing maps how to get scaling-invariant higher regularity of

(4)

It would be interesting to study the limiting cases as

Proposition 2.5
.

Fix

The proof is based on the following elementary lemma.

Lemma 2.6
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
,
\Gamma>0
,
0<p_{0}<p_{1}
, there exists
\tilde{\delta}=\tilde{\delta}(p_{0},p_{1},\Gamma,\varepsilon)>0
such that if

|p-q|<\tilde{\delta}\quad\Rightarrow\quad|a^{p}-a^{q}|<\varepsilon\quad\forall a\in[0,\Gamma].

Proof.

We may assume that
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
\Gamma\geq 1
,
p_{0}\leq p<q\leq p_{1}

Set

Moreover with the inequality
|1-e^{t}|\leq|t|e^{|t|}

So if we set

\tilde{\delta}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\Gamma^{p_{1}}\Lambda_{\varepsilon}e^{\Lambda_{\varepsilon}2p_{1}}}

We have shown that

\quad\theta<\#_{s}\alpha_{i}<\#_{s}\alpha-\frac{\theta}{2},\quad\text{ for }i=1,2.

From
Lemma

3.1
we can conclude the following existence and energy identity.

Theorem 3.2

(Energy identity)
.

Fix
n,\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
, with either
(\ell,n)=(1,1)
or
\ell\geq 2
, and
s\in(0,1)
. For each
\alpha\in\pi_{n}({\mathbb{S}}^{\ell})\setminus\{0\}
there exists a finite sequence
(\alpha_{i})_{i=1}^{N}\subset\pi_{n}({\mathbb{S}}^{\ell})\setminus\{0\}
such that

(1)

\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}
,

(2)

\#_{s}\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\#_{s}\alpha_{i}
,

(3)

\#_{s}\alpha_{i}
are attained for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
.

Proof.

Fix
\Lambda\coloneqq\#_{s}\alpha+1
.

Fix
\tilde{\delta}\in(0,1)
. If
\#_{s}\alpha
is attained then we are done. If
\#_{s}\alpha
is not attained, we apply
Lemma

3.1
and decompose
\alpha=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}
with

\#_{s}\alpha_{1}+\#_{s}\alpha_{2}\leq\#_{s}\alpha+2^{-1}\tilde{\delta}\quad\text{and}\quad\theta<\#_{s}\alpha_{i}<\#_{s}\alpha-\frac{\theta}{2}\text{ for }i=1,2.

If both
i\in{1,2}
,
\#_{s}\alpha_{i}
are not attained we finish, if at least one is not attained, say
\#_{s}\alpha_{1}
we apply again
Lemma

3.1
with
\delta=2^{-2}\tilde{\delta}
. We decompose
\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{1,1}+\alpha_{1,2}
and obtain

(3.3)

\#_{s}\alpha_{1,1}+\#_{s}\alpha_{1,2}+\#_{s}\alpha_{2}\leq\#_{s}\alpha_{1}+\#_{s}\alpha_{2}+2^{-2}\tilde{\delta}+2^{-1}\tilde{\delta}\leq\#_{s}\alpha+\sum_{i=1}^{2}2^{-i}\tilde{\delta}

with

(3.4)

\theta<\#_{s}\alpha_{1,i}<\#_{s}\alpha_{1}-\frac{\theta}{2}<\#_{s}\alpha-\theta,

where
\alpha=\alpha_{1,1}+\alpha_{1,2}+\alpha_{2}
. With an abuse of notation we relabel
\alpha=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}
.

We apply
Lemma

3.1
iteratively, whenever the minimizer of one of the decomposed terms is not attained — at
\ell
-th step we take
\delta=2^{-\ell}\tilde{\delta}
and obtain a decomposition into
\ell+1
terms with

(3.5)

\sum_{i=1}^{\ell+1}\#_{s}\alpha_{i}\leq\#_{s}\alpha+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell+1}2^{-i}\tilde{\delta}.

By (
3.2
) after
2^{\ell-1}
-iterations there is an
i\in\{1,\ldots,2^{\ell-1}+1\}
for which

(3.6)

\theta<\#_{s}\alpha_{i}<\#_{s}\alpha-\ell\frac{\theta}{2}=\Lambda-1-\ell\frac{\theta}{2}.

If however
\ell>\frac{2}{\theta}(\Lambda-1-\theta)
we get a contradiction in (
3.6
). Hence, we may iterate at most
2^{L-1}
times for an
L=L(\theta,\Lambda)
obtaining a decomposition into
N_{\tilde{\delta}}
terms, where
N_{\tilde{\delta}}\leq 2^{L-1}+1
for all
\tilde{\delta}\in(0,1)
and

\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\tilde{\delta}}}\alpha_{i}=\alpha

such that
\#_{s}\alpha_{i}
must be attained (otherwise we would have continued the iteration of
Lemma

3.1
). Moreover,

\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\tilde{\delta}}}\#_{s}\alpha_{i}\leq\#_{s}\alpha+\tilde{\delta}.

We want to let
\tilde{\delta}\to 0
. Let us stress that as of now the decomposition of
\alpha_{i}
depends on
\tilde{\delta}
.

By
Theorem

2.4
,

\{\beta\in\pi_{n}({\mathbb{S}}^{\ell})\colon\quad\#_{s}\beta\leq\#_{s}\alpha+1\}

is a finite set. Hence, there is only a finite number of possibilities of
\alpha_{i}=\alpha_{i}(\tilde{\delta})
. Thus, there exists a sequence
\tilde{\delta}_{k}\to 0
such that
\alpha_{i}(\tilde{\delta}_{k})=\alpha_{i}(\tilde{\delta}_{j})
for all
j,k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Thus we have

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\#_{s}\alpha_{i}\leq\#_{s}\alpha+\tilde{\delta}_{k},

where the left-hand side does not depend on
\tilde{\delta}_{k}
anymore. Letting
k\to\infty
we obtain

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\#_{s}\alpha_{i}\leq\#_{s}\alpha.

On the other hand, since
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}=\alpha
we have by
Proposition

2.2
,

\#_{s}\alpha\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\#_{s}\alpha_{i}.

Combining the two last statements we obtain

\#_{s}\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\#_{s}\alpha_{i}.

∎

Let us remark in passing that another strategy to obtain
Theorem

3.2
would be to extend the methods
[
8
]
, which mostly rely on the conformal invariance of the norms involved, and thus should be applicable to our case.

4.
Globally improved regularity in the conformal scaling

In this section we show that
W^{s,\frac{n}{s}}
-minimizers actually belong
globally
to
W^{\Theta s,\frac{n}{\Theta s}}({\mathbb{S}}^{n},{\mathbb{S}}^{\ell})
for a
\Theta>1
. To do so we adapt the strategy of
[
22
]
. It seems to us that we use a somehow unique feature of the Gagliardo-seminorm and the fractional
p
-Laplacian (i.e., we do not see how the argument would work for the classical
p
-Laplace): we use intrinsically a feature of differential stability that was observed for the fractional
p
-Laplace but is not known for the classical
p
-Laplace, see
[
13
,
23
]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Fix
n,\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
For every
0<s_{0}<s_{1}<1
there exists
\Theta>1
and for any
\Lambda>0
there is a constant
C(\Lambda)=C(n,\ell,s_{0},s_{1},\Lambda)>0
such that the following holds:

If
s_{0}<s<s_{1}
and
u\in W^{s,\frac{n}{s}}({\mathbb{S}}^{n},{\mathbb{S}}^{\ell})
is a
W^{s,\frac{n}{s}}({\mathbb{S}}^{n},{\mathbb{S}}^{\ell})
-minimizer in its own homotopy group with

[u]_{W^{s,\frac{n}{s}}({\mathbb{S}}^{n},{\mathbb{S}}^{\ell})}\leq\Lambda

then

[u]_{W^{\Theta s,\frac{n}{\Theta s}}({\mathbb{S}}^{n},{\mathbb{S}}^{\ell})}\leq C(\Lambda).

Theorem

4.1
is a consequence of
Proposition

4.3
and
Proposition

4.6
below.

Remark 4.2
.

(1)

The study of the regularity of fractional harmonic maps was initiated by Da Lio–Rivière in their celebrated papers
[
6
,
7
]
.

(2)

The argument below applies generally to critical points, not only minimizers. We only state the a priori estimate versions, since (local) higher regularity was discussed in
[
15
]
.

(3)

In
[
22
]
it was proven that minimizers as in
Theorem

4.1
are Hölder continuous, see also
[
14
]
. Observe that, however, there is no hope to prove

[u]_{C^{0,\alpha}({\mathbb{S}}^{n},{\mathbb{S}}^{\ell})}\precsim[u]_{W^{s,\frac{n}{s}}({\mathbb{S}}^{n},{\mathbb{S}}^{\ell})}.

Indeed, this can be simply disproved by conformal rescaling, concentrating the map
u
into one-point. The right-hand side is conformally invariant, and thus does not change. However, the continuity on the left-hand side becomes worse and worse.

(4)

Similarly, there is no hope to obtain

[u]_{W^{s_{1},p_{1}}({\mathbb{S}}^{n},{\mathbb{S}}^{\ell})}\precsim[u]_{W^{s,\frac{n}{s}}({\mathbb{S}}^{n},{\mathbb{S}}^{\ell})}.

whenever
s_{1}p_{1}>n
.

(5)

While
Theorem

4.1
, does not imply continuity, and therefore seems to be a weaker result than
[
22
,
14
]
, it has the crucial advantage of being a global result on all of
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
.

By the conformal invariance of the energy we may replace the domain
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
by
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and assume
u\in\dot{W}^{s,\frac{n}{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n},{\mathbb{S}}^{\ell})
.

4.1.
Improved global estimates for harmonic maps

We begin with the Euler–Lagrange equations for
W^{s,\frac{n}{s}}
-harmonic maps, see, e.g.,
[
22
]
. For any
\varphi\in L^{\infty}\cap\dot{W}^{s,\frac{n}{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{\ell+1})
a critical point
u\in W^{s,\frac{n}{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n},{\mathbb{S}}^{\ell})
of the energy
\mathcal{E}_{s,\frac{n}{s}}
satisfies the equation

(4.1)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{\frac{n}{s}-2}(u(x)-u(y))\wedge(u(x)\varphi(x)-u(y)\varphi(y))}{|x-y|^{2n}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y=0.

Here
\wedge\colon\mathbb{R}^{\ell}\times\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
denotes the wedge product in
\mathbb{R}^{\ell+1}
,

(u\wedge v)_{ij}=u^{i}v^{j}-u^{j}v^{i},\quad\text{$i,j\in\{1,\ldots,\ell+1\}$}.

The crucial result is that the equation for fractional
W^{s,\frac{n}{s}}
-harmonic maps improves globally.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
0<s_{0}<s_{1}<1
. There exists a
\theta\in(0,1)
such that the following holds:

For any
s\in(s_{0},s_{1})
and any
u\in\dot{W}^{s,\frac{n}{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n},{\mathbb{S}}^{\ell})
, which is minimizing
W^{s,\frac{n}{s}}
-harmonic map in its own homotopy group we have

[(-\Delta)^{s}_{\frac{n}{s}}u]_{W^{-\theta s,\frac{n}{n-\theta s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(s_{0},s_{1},n)[u]_{W^{s,\frac{n}{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{n}{s}},

that is for any
\psi\in\dot{W}^{\theta s,\frac{n}{\theta s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{\ell+1})
,

(4.2)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}&\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{\frac{n}{s}-2}(u(x)-u(y))\,\left(\psi(x)-\psi(y)\right)}{|x-y|^{2n}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\\
&\leq C(s_{0},s_{1},n)[u]_{W^{s,\frac{n}{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{n}{s}}[\psi]_{W^{\theta s,\frac{n}{\theta s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.\end{split}

In the proof below we will frequently work with the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}
which can be defined as

(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}f=c_{s}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(|\xi|^{s}\mathcal{F}f\right),

where
\mathcal{F}
is the Fourier transform. When
s\in(0,1)
, with a different constant, we also have the representation

(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}f(x)=c_{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{f(y)-f(x)}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,\mathrm{d}y.

The inverse of the fractional Laplacian is the Riesz potential
I^{s}
, defined as

I^{s}f=c_{s}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(|\xi|^{-s}\mathcal{F}f\right),

or, for
s\in(0,n)
,

(4.3)

I^{s}f(x)=c_{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x-y|^{s-n}f(y)\,\mathrm{d}y.

For mapping properties of the Riesz potential we refer the reader to standard literature, e.g.,
[
26
,
9
,
10
,
20
]
.

In order to prove
Proposition

4.3
we consider the following potential introduced in
[
22
]
:

(4.4)

T_{t}u(z)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{\frac{n}{s}-2}(u(x)-u(y))\,(|x-z|^{t-n}-|y-z|^{t-n})}{|x-y|^{2n}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y.

Observe that for
t<s
we have by the representation of the Riesz potential
I^{t}
, (
4.3
),

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}T_{t}u(z)\varphi(z)\,\mathrm{d}z=c\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{\frac{n}{s}-2}(u(x)-u(y))\,(I^{t}\varphi(x)-I^{t}\varphi(y))}{|x-y|^{2n}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y.

From the definition of
T_{t}
and Hölder’s inequality we have,

(4.5)

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}T_{t}u(z)\varphi(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\right|\precsim[u]_{W^{s,\frac{n}{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{n}{s}-1}\,[I^{t}\varphi]_{W^{s,\frac{n}{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Thus, if
t<s
,
T_{t}u
is a tempered distribution for
u\in W^{s,\frac{n}{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Observe that even as a distribution we have

(4.6)

\|T_{t}u\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-t}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\precsim\|u\cdot T_{t}u\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-t}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|u\wedge T_{t}u\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-t}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

whenever the right-hand side is finite. In the next two lemmas we will estimate separately both terms on the right-hand side of (
4.6
): the orthogonal projection
u\cdot T_{t}u
and the tangential projection
u\wedge T_{t}u
(orthogonal and tangential are meant with respect to the tangent space
T_{u}{\mathbb{S}}^{\ell}
).

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
0<s_{0}<s_{1}<1
. There exists a
\theta\in(0,1)
and a constant
C=C(s_{0},s_{1},n,\ell)>0
such that the following holds:

For any
s\in(s_{0},s_{1})
, there exists
t<\theta s
such that if
u
is a
W^{s,\frac{n}{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n},{\mathbb{S}}^{\ell})
-minimizing harmonic map in its own homotopy group, then for
T_{t}
as in (
4.4
)

(4.7)

\|u\cdot T_{t}u\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-t}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\,[u]_{W^{s,\frac{n}{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{n}{s}}.

Proof.

We argue similarly as in the proof of
[
22
, Lemma 6.5]
.
We note that since
|u|=1
we have

(u(x)-u(y))\cdot u(z)=-\frac{1}{2}(u(x)-u(y))\cdot(u(x)+u(y)-2u(z))

and hence

\left|u\cdot T_{t}u(z)\right|\precsim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{\frac{n}{s}-1}|u(x)+u(y)-2u(z)|\,\left||x-z|^{t-n}-|y-z|^{t-n}\right|}{|x-y|^{2n}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y.

We observe that for
r\in(0,1)
, we have by
[
24
, Proposition 6.6]
,

(4.8)

|u(x)-u(y)|\precsim|x-y|^{r}\,\left(\mathcal{M}(-\Delta)^{\frac{r}{2}}u(x)+\mathcal{M}(-\Delta)^{\frac{r}{2}}u(y)\right),

where
\mathcal{M}
denotes the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function.

We follow the proof in
[
22
, Proposition 6.3]
but replace the use of
[
22
, Proposition 6.2]
by (
4.8
) and consider the three regimes (similarly as in
[
22
, Proposition 6.1]
):

\begin{split}&\{|x-y|\precsim\min\{|x-z|,|y-z|\}\},\quad\text{ in this case $|x-z|\approx|y-z|$};\\
&\{|x-z|\precsim\min\{|y-z|,|x-y|\}\},\quad\text{ in this case $|y-z|\approx|x-y|$};\\
&\{|y-z|\precsim\min\{|x-z|,|x-y|\}\},\quad\text{ in this case $|x-z|\approx|x-y|$}.\end{split}

we obtain for
\tilde{t}\in(0,t)
with
r+\tilde{t}\in(0,1)

(4.9)

\begin{split}&|u(x)+u(y)-2u(z)|\,\left||x-z|^{t-n}-|y-z|^{t-n}\right|\\
&\precsim\left(\mathcal{M}(-\Delta)^{\frac{r}{2}}u(x)+\mathcal{M}(-\Delta)^{\frac{r}{2}}u(y)+\mathcal{M}(-\Delta)^{\frac{r}{2}}u(z)\right)|x-y|^{r+\tilde{t}}k_{t-\tilde{t},t}(x,y,z),\end{split}

where
\kappa_{\alpha,\gamma}(x,y,z)
is given by

(4.10)

\begin{split}\kappa_{\alpha,\gamma}(x,y,z)&=\min\{|x-z|^{\alpha-n},|y-z|^{\alpha-n}\}\\
&\quad+\left(\frac{|y-z|}{|x-y|}\right)^{\gamma-\alpha}|y-z|^{\alpha-n}\chi_{\{|x-z|\precsim\min\{|y-z|,|x-y|\}\}}\\
&\quad+\left(\frac{|x-z|}{|x-y|}\right)^{\gamma-\alpha}|x-z|^{\alpha-n}\chi_{\{|y-z|\precsim\min\{|x-z|,|x-y|\}\}}.\end{split}

Moreover, we have by (
4.8
)

(4.11)

|u(x)-u(y)|^{\frac{n}{s}-1}\precsim|x-y|^{r(\frac{n}{s}-1)}\left(\left(\mathcal{M}(-\Delta)^{\frac{r}{2}}u(x)\right)^{\frac{n}{s}-1}+\left(\mathcal{M}(-\Delta)^{\frac{r}{2}}u(y)\right)^{\frac{n}{s}-1}\right).

Combining (
4.11
) with (
4.9
) we obtain for a
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\|\varphi\|_{L^{\frac{n}{t}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 1

(4.12)

\begin{split}&\|u\cdot T_{t}u\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-t}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\\
&\precsim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(z)\cdot T_{t}u(z)\varphi(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\\
&\precsim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{\frac{n}{s}-1}|u(x)+u(y)-2u(z)|\,\left||x-z|^{t-n}-|y-z|^{t-n}\right|}{|x-y|^{2n}}\left|\varphi(z)\right|\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}z\\
&\precsim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x-y|^{r\frac{n}{s}+\tilde{t}-n-n}k_{t-\tilde{t},t}(x,y,z)U_{r}(x,y,z)\left|\varphi(z)\right|\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}z,\end{split}

where

\begin{split}&U_{r}(x,y,z)\\
&\coloneqq\left(\left(\mathcal{M}(-\Delta)^{\frac{r}{2}}u(x)\right)^{\frac{n}{s}-1}+\left(\mathcal{M}(-\Delta)^{\frac{r}{2}}u(y)\right)^{\frac{n}{s}-1}\right)\left(\mathcal{M}(-\Delta)^{\frac{r}{2}}u(x)+\mathcal{M}(-\Delta)^{\frac{r}{2}}u(y)+\mathcal{M}(-\Delta)^{\frac{r}{2}}u(z)\right).\end{split}

Assuming
r\frac{n}{s}+\tilde{t}-n>0
we estimate further (
4.12
) with the help of
[
22
, Proposition 6.4]
and Hölder’s inequality

(4.13)

\begin{split}&\|u\cdot T_{t}u\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-t}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\\
&\precsim\max_{t_{1}+t_{2}+t_{3}=r\frac{n}{s}-n+t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,I^{t_{1}}\left(\mathcal{M}(-\Delta)^{\frac{r}{2}}u(z)\right)^{\frac{n}{s}-1}\,I^{t_{2}}\left(\mathcal{M}(-\Delta)^{\frac{r}{2}}u(z)\right)\,I^{t_{3}}\left|\varphi(z)\right|\,\mathrm{d}z\\
&\precsim\max_{t_{1}+t_{2}+t_{3}=r\frac{n}{s}-n+t}\|I^{t_{1}}\left(\mathcal{M}(-\Delta)^{\frac{r}{2}}u\right)^{\frac{n}{s}-1}\|_{L^{\frac{n}{r\frac{n}{s}-r-t_{1}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\|I^{t_{2}}\left(\mathcal{M}(-\Delta)^{\frac{r}{2}}u\right)\|_{L^{\frac{n}{r-t_{2}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|I^{t_{3}}|\varphi|\|_{L^{\frac{n}{t-t_{3}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.\end{split}

Assuming
r\frac{n}{s}-r-t_{1}\geq-r-t+n>0
,
t_{2}\leq r\frac{n}{s}-n+t<r
,
t_{3}\leq r\frac{n}{s}-n+t<t

we apply twice Sobolev’s inequality and the Maximal Theorem to get

(4.14)

\begin{split}\|u\cdot T_{t}u\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-t}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}&\precsim\|\left(\mathcal{M}(-\Delta)^{\frac{r}{2}}u\right)^{\frac{n}{s}-1}\|_{L^{\frac{n}{r\frac{n}{s}-r}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\|{\mathcal{M}(-\Delta)^{\frac{r}{2}}u}\|_{L^{\frac{n}{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\varphi\|_{L^{\frac{n}{t}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\\
&\precsim\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{r}{2}}u\|_{L^{(\frac{n}{s}-1)\frac{n}{r\frac{n}{s}-r}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{n}{s}-1}\,\|{(-\Delta)^{\frac{r}{2}}u}\|_{L^{\frac{n}{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\varphi\|_{L^{\frac{n}{t}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\\
&=\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{r}{2}}u\|_{L^{\frac{n}{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{n}{s}}\|\varphi\|_{L^{\frac{n}{t}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\\
&\precsim[u]_{W^{s,\frac{n}{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{n}{s}}.\end{split}

In the last line we used
r<s
.

The conditions on
r
and
t
used in estimates (
4.13
) and (
4.14
) are

r<s,\quad n<\frac{n}{s}r+t<n+r,\quad r+t<n.

If
n=1
we choose
r=t
, then any
t\in(\frac{s}{1+s},\min\{s,\frac{1}{2}\})
is admissible, so we can pick

t\coloneqq\frac{\frac{s}{1+s}+\min\{s,\frac{1}{2}\}}{2}\leq s\frac{\frac{1}{1+s}+1}{2}\leq s\frac{\frac{1}{1+s_{0}}+1}{2}\coloneqq s\theta.

Observe that we do not need to make a distinction between the case
\frac{n}{s}<2
and
\frac{n}{s}\geq 2
.

If
n\geq 2
it is even easier, since
r+t<n
becomes a trivial condition.
This finishes the proof.

∎

Now we estimate the second part on the right-hand side of (
4.6
).

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
0<s_{0}<s_{1}<1
. There exists a
\theta\in(0,1)
such that the following holds:

For any
s\in(s_{0},s_{1})
, there exists
t<\theta s
such that if
u
is a
W^{s,\frac{n}{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{S}^{\ell})
-minimizing harmonic map in its own homotopy group, then for
T_{t}
as in (
4.4
)

(4.15)

\|u\wedge T_{t}u\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-t}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\precsim[u]_{W^{s,\frac{n}{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{n}{s}}.

Proof.

We argue as in
[
22
, Proof of Lemma 3.5]
.

By duality, there is some
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\|\psi\|_{L^{\frac{n}{t}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 1
,

\|u\wedge T_{t}u\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-t}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\precsim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u\wedge T_{t}u\,\psi.

Take
R\gg 1
so that
{\rm supp\,}\psi\subset B(0,R)
. Let
\eta_{R}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B(0,2R))
be a cut-off function such that
\eta_{R}\equiv 1
in
B(0,R)
and set

\begin{split}\varphi_{1,R}&\coloneqq\eta_{R}I^{t}\psi,\\
\varphi_{2,R}&\coloneqq(1-\eta_{R})I^{t}\psi.\end{split}

Then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u\wedge T_{t}u\,\psi=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u\wedge T_{t}u\,(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}\varphi_{1,R}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u\wedge T_{t}u\,(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}\varphi_{2,R}.

We observe that with a constant independent of
R\gg 1

\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}\varphi_{1,R}\|_{L^{\frac{n}{t}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\precsim\|\psi\|_{L^{\frac{n}{t}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 1,

hence

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u\wedge T_{t}u\,\psi&\leq\sup_{\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\,\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}\varphi\|_{L^{\frac{n}{t}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\precsim 1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u\wedge T_{t}u\,(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}\varphi\\
&\quad+\limsup_{R\to\infty}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u\wedge T_{t}u\,(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}\varphi_{2,R}\right|.\end{split}

For the second term on the right-hand side we observe that similarly as in (
4.5
), for suitably small
\varepsilon>0

\begin{split}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u\wedge T_{t}u\,(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}\varphi_{2,R}\right|&\precsim[u]_{W^{s,\frac{n}{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{n}{s}-1}\,[I^{t}(u\wedge(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}\varphi_{2,R})]_{W^{s,\frac{n}{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\\
&\precsim[u]_{W^{s,\frac{n}{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{n}{s}-1}\,\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s-t+\varepsilon}{2}}(u\wedge(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}\varphi_{2,R})\|_{L^{\frac{n}{s+\varepsilon}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\\
&\precsim[u]_{W^{s,\frac{n}{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{n}{s}-1}\,(1+[u]_{W^{s,\frac{n}{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})})\,\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s+\varepsilon}{2}}\varphi_{2,R}\|_{L^{\frac{n}{s+\varepsilon}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\end{split}

where in the second estimate we used an embedding in Triebel–Lizorkin spaces, see

[
20
, Theorem 2.2.3]
.

Now we observe that, due to the support of
1-\eta_{R}
and
\psi
we have for some
\sigma>0

\begin{split}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s+\varepsilon}{2}}\varphi_{2,R}\|_{L^{\frac{n}{s+\varepsilon}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}&\precsim R^{-s-\varepsilon}\|I^{t}\psi\|_{L^{\frac{n}{s+\varepsilon}}}+\|(1-\eta_{R})(-\Delta)^{\frac{s+\varepsilon-t}{2}}\psi\|_{L^{\frac{n}{s+\varepsilon}}}\\
&\precsim R^{-\sigma}C({\rm supp\,}\psi)\|\psi\|_{L^{\frac{n}{t}}}\xrightarrow{R\to\infty}0.\end{split}

Thus, for some
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}\varphi\|_{L^{\frac{n}{t}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 1
,

(4.16)

\begin{split}\|u\wedge T_{t}u\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-t}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}&\precsim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u\wedge T_{t}u\,(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}\varphi\\
&=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi\,(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}u\wedge T_{t}u+\underbrace{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}(\varphi\,u)\wedge T_{t}u}_{\overset{\eqref{eq:pdemin}}{=}0}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}H_{(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}}(\varphi,u)\wedge T_{t}u.\end{split}

Here we use the Leibniz term notation

H_{(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}}(f,g)\coloneqq(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}(fg)-f(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}g-(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}f\ g.

For the last term of (
4.16
) we observe
that similarly as in (
4.5
) for a suitably small
\varepsilon>0

(4.17)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}H_{(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}}(\varphi,u)\wedge T_{t}u&\precsim[u]_{W^{s,\frac{n}{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{n}{s}-1}\,[I^{t}H_{(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}}(\varphi,u)]_{W^{s,\frac{n}{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\\
&\precsim[u]_{W^{s,\frac{n}{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{n}{s}-1}\,\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s-t+\varepsilon}{2}}H_{(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}}(\varphi,u)\|_{L^{\frac{n}{s+\varepsilon}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\\
&\precsim[u]_{W^{s,\frac{n}{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{n}{s}}\,\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}\varphi\|_{L^{\frac{n}{t}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.\end{split}

The last line works as long

.

For the remaining estimates we abbreviate

\text{diff}_{\frac{n}{s}}u(x,y)=|u(x)-u(y)|^{\frac{n}{s}-2}(u(x)-u(y)).

By the representation of the Riesz potential, (
4.3
), and (
4.1
) we have

(4.18)

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi\,(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}u\wedge T_{t}u\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\text{diff}_{\frac{n}{s}}u(x,y)\wedge\left(I^{t}\left(\varphi\,(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}u\right)(x)-I^{t}\left(\varphi\,(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}u\right)(y)\right)}{|x-y|^{2n}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\text{diff}_{\frac{n}{s}}u(x,y)\wedge\left(I^{t}\left(\varphi\,(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}u\right)(x)-I^{t}\left(\varphi\,(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}u\right)(y)-\frac{1}{2}(u(x)-u(y))(\varphi(x)+\varphi(y))\right)}{|x-y|^{2n}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y.\end{split}

Exactly as in the first lines of the proof of
[
22
, Lemma 6.6]
we have

\begin{split}&\left|I^{t}\left(\varphi\,(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}u\right)(x)-I^{t}\left(\varphi\,(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}u\right)(y)-\frac{1}{2}(u(x)-u(y))(\varphi(x)+\varphi(y))\right|\\
&\precsim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left||x-z|^{t-n}-|y-z|^{t-n}\right|\,\left|(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}u(z)\right|\left|\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)-2\varphi(z)\right|\,\mathrm{d}z.\end{split}

Thus,

\begin{split}&\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi\,(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}u\wedge T_{t}u\right|\\
&\precsim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{\frac{n}{s}-1}\left||x-z|^{t-n}-|y-z|^{t-n}\right|\,\left|(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}u(z)\right|\left|\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)-2\varphi(z)\right|}{|x-y|^{2n}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}z.\end{split}

This is the same situation as in (
4.12
): the role of
\varphi\in L^{\frac{n}{t}}
in (
4.12
) is taken here by
(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}u\in L^{\frac{n}{t}}
, and the role of
|u(x)+u(y)-2u(z)|
is taken by
|\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)-2\varphi(z)|
and observe that
(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}\varphi\in L^{\frac{n}{t}}
. As was discussed there we can pick

, and thus we have

\begin{split}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi\,(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}u\wedge T_{t}u\right|&\precsim\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{r}{2}}u\|_{L^{\frac{n}{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{n}{s}-1}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{r}{2}}\varphi\|_{L^{\frac{n}{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{n}{s}-1}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}u\|_{L^{\frac{n}{t}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\\
&\precsim[u]_{W^{s,\frac{n}{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{n}{s}}\,\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}\varphi\|_{L^{\frac{n}{t}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.\end{split}

We can conclude. (Again it is worth noting that the proof above does not need to distinguish between the case
\frac{n}{s}\geq 2
and
\frac{n}{s}\leq 2
).
∎

4.2.
A fractional version of Iwaniec’s stability result

A fractional version of Iwaniec’s stability result was proposed in
[
23
]
. However, the result of
[
23
]
does not apply in our situation since it only considers stability in the differential direction, without adjusting the integrability. We need the latter, since we need to stay in the scaling invariant case. Hence, we employ a different version of the Iwaniec’s stability result
[
11
, Theorem 13.2.1]
to obtain the following regularity result.

Proposition 4.6
.

For any
0<s_{0}<s_{1}<1
there exists an
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(s_{0},s_{1},n)>0
such that the following holds:

For any
s\in(s_{0},s_{1})
and any
\Lambda>0
there exists a constant
C(\Lambda)
such that if

satisfies for a

\psi\in\dot{W}^{t,\frac{n}{t}}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{N})

(4.21)

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{\frac{n}{s}-2}(u(x)-u(y))\,\left(\psi(x)-\psi(y)\right)}{|x-y|^{2n}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\right|\leq\Lambda[\psi]_{W^{t,\frac{n}{t}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

then for
r\coloneqq s\frac{n-t}{n-s}\geq s

[u]_{W^{r,\frac{n}{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(\Lambda,\varepsilon_{0},s_{0},s_{1}).

We first observe that for
p\coloneqq\frac{n}{s}
and
\varepsilon\coloneqq p-\frac{n}{r}=\frac{n}{s}-\frac{n}{r}>0
we have
\frac{t}{1-\varepsilon}=r
and

(4.22)

[u]_{W^{r,\frac{n}{r}}}^{\frac{n}{r}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))\,|u(x)-u(y)|^{-\varepsilon}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{2n}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y.

We perform a de facto Hodge decomposition:

(4.23)

|u(x)-u(y)|^{-\varepsilon}(u(x)-u(y))=A(x)-A(y)+G(x,y),

where, in the terminology of
[
14
]
, we choose
A
such that

(4.24)

(-\Delta)^{t}A(x)\coloneqq{\rm div}_{t}\left(\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{-\varepsilon}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{t}}\right),

that is for any
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

(-\Delta)^{t}A[\varphi]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{-\varepsilon}(u(x)-u(y))\,(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))}{|x-y|^{n+2t}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y.

From the linear theory of partial differential equation we have:

Lemma 4.7
.

For any
0<t_{0}<t_{1}<1
there exists an
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that whenever

and

as in (
4.24
) exists and satisfies the estimate

[A]_{W^{t,\frac{n}{t}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\precsim[u]_{W^{\frac{t}{1-\varepsilon},(1-\varepsilon)\frac{n}{t}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{1-\varepsilon}=[u]_{W^{r,\frac{n}{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{1-\varepsilon}

with a constant independent of
\varepsilon
as long as
\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon_{0}]
.
A
is unique up to constants.

Proof.

Proceeding exactly as in
[
5
, Lemma A.1]
we have the a priori estimate

\begin{split}(-\Delta)^{t}A[\varphi]&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{-\varepsilon}(u(x)-u(y))\,(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))}{|x-y|^{n+2t}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\\
&\leq[u]_{W^{\frac{t}{1-\varepsilon},(1-\varepsilon)\frac{n}{t}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{1-\varepsilon}\,[\varphi]_{W^{t,\frac{n}{n-t}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.\end{split}

Using the identification via Triebel–Lizorkin spaces,
[
20
, Section 2]
, we have

\begin{split}[A]_{W^{t,\frac{n}{t}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\approx[A]_{\dot{F}^{t,\frac{n}{t}}_{\frac{n}{t}}}\approx[(-\Delta)^{t}A]_{\dot{F}^{-t,\frac{n}{t}}_{\frac{n}{t}}}\approx[(-\Delta)^{t}A]_{\left(\dot{F}^{t,\frac{n}{n-t}}_{\frac{n}{n-t}}\right)^{\ast}}\precsim[u]_{W^{\frac{t}{1-\varepsilon},(1-\varepsilon)\frac{n}{t}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{1-\varepsilon}.\end{split}

The constants depend only on
t_{0}
and
t_{1}
since
t\in(t_{0},t_{1})
.
In particular,
A
exists since
(-\Delta)^{t}A\in\left(\dot{F}^{t,\frac{n}{n-t}}_{\frac{n}{n-t}}\right)^{\ast}
.
A
is unique up to constants, since
[A]_{W^{t,\frac{n}{t}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=0
implies that

is a constant.
∎

From
Lemma

4.7
and (
4.23
) we have in particular

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|G(x,y)|^{\frac{n}{t}}}{|x-y|^{2n}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{t}{n}}\precsim[u]_{W^{r,\frac{n}{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{1-\varepsilon}.

The latter estimate can, however, be improved.

.

For any

\varepsilon_{0}>0

and

Proof.

We follow the approach in
[
11
, Theorem 13.2.1]
.
Fix
\varepsilon_{0}
to be specified later. By density, cf.
[
20

(observe that we can change

and

For

,

This is well-defined since

and the right-hand side is a linear functional on a Triebel–Lizorkin space.

.

We have by (

) and (

)

(4.35)

\begin{split}[u]_{W^{r,\frac{n}{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{n}{r}}&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{\frac{n}{s}-2}(u(x)-u(y))\,\left(A(x)-A(y)\right)}{|x-y|^{2n}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\\
&\quad+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{\frac{n}{s}-2}(u(x)-u(y))\,\left(G(x,y)\right)}{|x-y|^{2n}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y.\end{split}

By (
4.21
) and
Lemma

4.7
we have

(4.36)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{\frac{n}{s}-2}(u(x)-u(y))\,\left(A(x)-A(y)\right)}{|x-y|^{2n}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\leq\Lambda[A]_{W^{t,\frac{n}{t}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\precsim\Lambda\,[u]_{W^{r,\frac{n}{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{1-\varepsilon}.

As for the second term on the right-hand side of (
4.35
) we note that
\frac{r}{n}(\frac{n}{s}-1)+\frac{t}{n}=1
. Hence, by Hölder’s inequality,
Proposition

4.8
, and the observation
\frac{n}{s}-\varepsilon=\frac{n}{r}

(4.37)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}&\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{\frac{n}{s}-2}(u(x)-u(y))\,\left(G(x,y)\right)}{|x-y|^{2n}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\\
&\precsim[u]_{W^{r,\frac{n}{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{n}{s}-1}\,\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|G(x,y)|^{\frac{n}{t}}}{|x-y|^{2n}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{t}{n}}\\
&\precsim\left|\varepsilon\right|[u]_{W^{r,\frac{n}{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{n}{r}}.\end{split}

Combining (
4.35
) with (
4.36
) and (
4.37
) we obtain

(4.38)

\begin{split}[u]_{W^{r,\frac{n}{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{n}{r}}&\precsim\Lambda\,[u]_{W^{r,\frac{n}{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{1-\varepsilon}+\left|\varepsilon\right|[u]_{W^{r,\frac{n}{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{n}{r}}.\end{split}

Now
s-t\leq\varepsilon_{0}
implies
\varepsilon=\frac{n}{s}-\frac{n}{r}=\frac{n}{s}\left(\frac{s-t}{n-t}\right)\leq C(s_{0},s_{1})\varepsilon_{0}
, so for
\varepsilon_{0}
suitably small we can absorb and conclude

[u]_{W^{r,\frac{n}{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{n}{r}}\precsim C(\varepsilon_{0},\Lambda,s_{1},s_{0}).

∎

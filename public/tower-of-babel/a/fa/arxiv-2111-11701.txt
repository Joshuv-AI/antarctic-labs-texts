Abstract
The goal of this paper is to study global well-posedness, cone of dependence and loss of regularity of the solutions to a class of strictly hyperbolic equations with coefficients displaying “mild” blow-up of sublogarithmic order - | ln ⁡ t | γ , γ ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) . |\ln t|^{\gamma},\gamma\in(0,1). The problems we study are of strictly hyperbolic type with respect to a generic weight and a metric on the phase space. The coefficients are polynomially bound in x x with their x x -derivatives and t t -derivative of order O ​ ( t − δ ) , δ ∈ [ 0 , 1 ) , \textnormal{O}(t^{-\delta}),\delta\in[0,1), and O ⁡ ( t − 1 ​ | ln ⁡ t | γ − 1 ) , γ ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) , O(t^{-1}|\ln t|^{\gamma-1}),\gamma\in(0,1), respectively. We employ the Planck function associated with the metric to subdivide the extended phase space and define appropriate generalized parameter dependent symbol classes. To arrive at an energy estimate, we perform a conjugation by a pseudodifferential operator. This operator explains the loss of regularity by linking it to the metric on the phase space and the singular behavior. We call the conjugating operator as loss operator . We report that the solution experiences an arbitrarily small loss in relation to the initial datum defined in the Sobolev space tailored to the loss operator. 2.1
Our Choice of Metric on the Phase Space

Let us start by reviewing some notation and terminology used in the study of metrics on the phase space, see
[
8
, Chapter 2]
and
[
9
]
for details. Let us denote by
\sigma(X,Y)
the standard symplectic form on
T^{*}{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\cong{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
: if
X=(x,\xi)
and
Y=(y,\eta)
, then
\sigma
is given by

\sigma(X,Y)=\xi\cdot y-\eta\cdot x.

We can identify
\sigma
with the isomorphism of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
such that
\sigma^{*}=-\sigma
, with the formula
\sigma(X,Y)=\langle\sigma X,Y\rangle
. Consider a Riemannian metric
g_{X}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
(which is a measurable function of
X
) to which we associate the dual metric
g_{X}^{\sigma}
by

g_{X}^{\sigma}(Y)=\sup_{0\neq Y^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}}\frac{\langle\sigma Y,Y^{\prime}\rangle^{2}}{g_{X}(Y^{\prime})},\quad\text{ for all }Y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}.

Considering
g_{X}
as a matrix associated to positive definite quadratic form on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
,
g_{X}^{\sigma}=\sigma^{*}g_{X}^{-1}\sigma
.
One defines the Planck function
[
9
]
which plays a crucial role in the development of pseudodifferential calculus as

h_{g}(x,\xi):=\sup_{0\neq Y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}}\Bigg(\frac{g_{X}(Y)}{g_{X}^{\sigma}(Y)}\Bigg)^{1/2}.

The uncertainty principle is quantified as the upper bound
h_{g}(x,\xi)\leq 1
. In the following, we make use of the strong uncertainty principle, that is, for some
\kappa>0
, we have

h_{g}(x,\xi)\leq(1+|x|+|\xi|)^{-\kappa},\quad(x,\xi)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}.

In general, we use the metrics of the form

g_{\Phi,k}^{\rho,r}=\left(\frac{\langle\xi\rangle_{k}^{\rho_{2}}}{\Phi(x)^{r_{1}}}\right)^{2}|dx|^{2}+\left(\frac{\Phi(x)^{r_{2}}}{\langle\xi\rangle_{k}^{\rho_{1}}}\right)^{2}|d\xi|^{2}.

(2.1)

Here
\rho=(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})
,
r=(r_{1},r_{2})
for
\rho_{j},r_{j}\in[0,1]
,
j=1,2
are such that
0\leq\rho_{2}<\rho_{1}\leq 1
and
0\leq r_{2}<r_{1}\leq 1
.
The Planck function associated to this metric is
\Phi(x)^{r_{2}-r_{1}}\langle\xi\rangle_{k}^{\rho_{2}-\rho_{1}}.

2.2
Properties of the Structure Functions
\omega
and
\Phi

The functions
\omega(x)
and
\Phi(x)
are associated with weight and metric respectively. They specify the structure of the differential equation. As pseudodifferential calculus is the datum of the metric satisfying some local and global conditions, in our case it amounts to certain conditions on
\Phi
. The symplectic structure and the uncertainty principle also play a natural role in the constraints imposed on
\Phi
. So we consider
\Phi
to be a monotone increasing function of
|x|
satisfying the following conditions:

\displaystyle 1\;\leq

\displaystyle\quad\Phi(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim 1+|x|

(sub-linear)

\displaystyle|x-y|\;\leq

\displaystyle\quad r\Phi(y)

\displaystyle\implies C^{-1}\Phi(y)\leq\Phi(x)\leq C\Phi(y)

(slowly varying)

\displaystyle\Phi(x+y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\Phi(x)(1+|y|)^{s}

(temperate)

for all
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and for some
r,s,C>0
.

For the sake of calculations arising in the development of symbol calculus related to the metrics
g_{\Phi,k}
, we need to impose following additional conditions:

\displaystyle|\Phi(x)-\Phi(y)|\leq

\displaystyle\Phi(x+y)

\displaystyle\leq\Phi(x)+\Phi(y),

\displaystyle\quad(\text{Subadditive})

\displaystyle|\partial_{x}^{\beta}\Phi(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\Phi(x)\langle x\rangle^{-|\beta|},

\displaystyle\Phi(ax)

\displaystyle\leq a\Phi(x),\text{ if }a>1,

\displaystyle a\Phi(x)

\displaystyle\leq\Phi(ax),\text{ if }a\in[0,1],

where
\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
. It can be observed that the above conditions are quite natural in the context of symbol classes. In our work, we need even the weight function
\omega
to satisfy the above stated properties of
\Phi
. In arriving at the energy estimate using the Sharp Gårding inequality (see Section
5.3
for details), we need

\omega(x)\lesssim\Phi(x),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

3
Statement of the Main Result

We consider the Cauchy problem:

\begin{cases}P(t,x,\partial_{t},D_{x})u(t,x)=f(t,x),\qquad D_{x}=-i\nabla_{x},\;(t,x)\in(0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\\
u(0,x)=f_{1}(x),\quad\partial_{t}u(0,x)=f_{2}(x),\end{cases}

(3.1)

with the strictly hyperbolic operator
P(t,x,\partial_{t},D_{x})=\partial_{t}^{2}+a(t,x,D_{x})+b(t,x,D_{x})
where

a(t,x,\xi)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}a_{i,j}(t,x)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\quad\text{ and }\quad b(t,x,\xi)=i\sum_{j=1}^{n}b_{j}(t,x)\xi_{j}+b_{n+1}(t,x).

Here, the matrix
(a_{i,j}(t,x))
is real symmetric for all
(t,x)\in(0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
a_{i,j}\in C^{1}((0,T];C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))
and
b_{j}\in C([0,T];C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))
. Similar to the estimates in Remark
1.1
, we have the following assumptions on
a(t,x,\xi)
and
b(t,x,\xi)

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} a(t,x,\xi)&\geq C_{0}\omega(x)^{2}\langle\xi\rangle_{k}^{2},\quad C_{0}>0,\\
|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}a(t,x,\xi)|&\leq C_{\alpha\beta}\frac{1}{t^{\delta_{1}}}\omega(x)^{2}\Phi(x)^{-|\beta|}\langle\xi\rangle_{k}^{2-|\alpha|},\quad|\alpha|\geq 0,|\beta|>0,\\

In the following, we prove the existence of cone of dependence for the Cauchy problem (

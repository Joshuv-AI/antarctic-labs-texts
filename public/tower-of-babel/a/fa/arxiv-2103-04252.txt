Weighted Simplicial Complexes and Weighted Analytic Torsions


Abstract
A weighted simplicial complex is a simplicial complex with values (called weights) on the vertices. In this paper, we consider weighted simplicial complexes with ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} -valued weights. We study the weighted homology and the weighted analytic torsion for such weighted simplicial complexes.

Simplicial complexes are useful combinatorial models in algebraic topology. In recent decades, (abstract) simplicial complexes have been found to have various applications in data sciences. Let
V
be a finite set with a total order
\prec
. An
(abstract) simplicial complex

\mathcal{K}
on
V
is a subset of the power set
2^{V}
such that for any
\sigma\in\mathcal{K}
and any non-empty subset
\tau\subseteq\sigma
, we have that
\tau\in\mathcal{K}
. An element
\sigma\in\mathcal{K}
is called a
simplex
, and an element
v\in V
is called a
vertex
. For any simplex
\sigma\in\mathcal{K}
, we can always write
\sigma
as
\{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
for some non-negative integer
n
, where
v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\in V
and
v_{0}\prec v_{1}\prec\ldots\prec v_{n}
. We write
n=\dim\sigma
and call it the
dimension
of
\sigma
. Letting
\sigma
run over all the simplices in
\mathcal{K}
, the maximum of
\dim\sigma
is denoted as
\dim\mathcal{K}
and is called the dimension of
\mathcal{K}
.

A weighted simplicial complex is a simplicial complex with some functions that assign values to the vertices (or to the simplices). In 1990, Robert. J. MacG. Dawson
[
2
]
studied the (weighted) homology for weighted simplicial complexes. In recent years, the weighted homology of weighted simplicial complexes have been further explored in
[
15
,
16
]
. Some other topological features for weighted simplicial complexes, such as the fundamental groups, the discrete Morse functions, the Hodge-Laplace operators, etc. have been studied in
[
18
,
19
,
20
]
. In 2020, Zhenyu Meng, D Vijay Anand, Yunpeng Lu, Jie Wu and Kelin Xia
[
10
]
have found amazing applications of the weighted homology for weighted simplicial complexes in boilogical data analysis, with significant effects.

In this paper, we consider the weighted simplicial complexes
(\mathcal{K},f,g)
where both
f
and
g
are real functions on the set
V
of the vertices. We use
f
to twist the boundary operators and use
g
to give a symmetric and semi-positive definite quadratic form on the chain complexes. We study the weighted homology equipped with the symmetric and semi-positive definite quadratic forms. Then we study the weighted analytic torsions for weighted simplicial complexes.

For any real number
r
, we let
\epsilon(r)=1
if
r\neq 0
and let
\epsilon(r)=0
if
r=0
. Then for any function
f
on
V
,
\epsilon(f)
is a function on
V
given by
\epsilon(f)(v)=\epsilon(f(v))
for any
v\in V
. For any real-valued function
f
on
V
, let
\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{f}
be the largest sub-simplicial complex of
\mathcal{K}
such that
f
is non-vanishing on all of its vertices. We will prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1

(Main Result)
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a simplicial complex with the set
V
of its vertices. Let
(f,g)
be a
\mathbb{R}^{2}
-valued function on
V
. Let
T(\mathcal{K},f,g)
be the
(f,g)
-weighted analytic torsion of
\mathcal{K}
. Then

(i).

for any non-vanishing real function
h
on
V
, we have

\displaystyle T(\mathcal{K},\epsilon(g)fh,\epsilon(f)gh)=T(\mathcal{K},\epsilon(g)f,\epsilon(f)g);

(ii).

for any non-zero real constant
c
we have

\displaystyle T(\mathcal{K},\epsilon(g)f,c\epsilon(f)g)=|c|^{s(\mathcal{K},\epsilon(f)\epsilon(g))}T(\mathcal{K},\epsilon(g)f,\epsilon(f)g)

and

\displaystyle T(\mathcal{K},c\epsilon(g)f,\epsilon(f)g)=|c|^{-s(\mathcal{K},\epsilon(f)\epsilon(g))}T(\mathcal{K},\epsilon(g)f,\epsilon(f)g).

Here

\displaystyle s(\mathcal{K},\epsilon(f)\epsilon(g))=\sum_{n\geq 0}(-1)^{n}\dim\partial_{n}C_{n}(\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{\epsilon(f)\epsilon(g)};\mathbb{R}).

As a by-product, we prove in Theorem
3.1
that for any weighted simplicial complex
(\mathcal{K},f,g)
and any
n\geq 0
, there is a symmetric and semi-positive definite quadratic form
\langle~,~\rangle_{g}
on the
n
-th weighted homology
H_{n}(\mathcal{K},f,g;\mathbb{R})
. In addition, if
g
is non-vanishing, then the quadratic form
\langle~,~\rangle
is an inner product. We also prove in Theorem
3.9
and Corollary
3.10
that for any non-vanishing real function
h
on
V
, there is a linear isometry from
H_{n}(\mathcal{K},fh,gh;\mathbb{R})
to
H_{n}(\mathcal{K},f,g;\mathbb{R})
. Thus the
(f,g)
-weighted homology
H_{n}(\mathcal{K},f,g;\mathbb{R})
only depends on the ratio function
f/g
for any non-vanishing functions
f
and
g
. In particular, if
f=g
, then there is a linear isometry from the weighted homology
H_{n}(\mathcal{K},f,f;\mathbb{R})
to the usual (unweighted) homology
H_{n}(\mathcal{K};\mathbb{R})
(cf.
[
16
, Section 5]
).

The remaining part of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2

3
, we study the weighted homology of weighted simplicial complexes. We prove Theorem
3.9
and Theorem
3.1
. In Section
4
, we study the weighted analytic torsions for weighted simplicial complexes and prove Theorem
1.1
.

2
Preliminaries

Let
W
be a (finite dimensional) real vector space. Let
\langle~,~\rangle
be a symmetric semi-positive definite quadratic form on
W
. Consider the sub-space

\displaystyle N=\{v\in W\mid\langle v,v\rangle=0\}=\{v\in W\mid\langle v,u\rangle=0{\rm~for~any~}u\in W\}.

Note that the second equality is obtained by using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. We call
N
the
null sub-space
for the quadratic form
\langle~,~\rangle
. The next lemma proves that the quotient space of
W
by
N
has an inherited inner product.

Lemma 2.1
.

The quotient space
W/N
has an inner product
\langle~,~\rangle
which is inherited from the quadratic form
\langle~,~\rangle
on
W
.

Proof.

For any
v_{1}+N,v_{2}+N\in W/N
, we define

\displaystyle\langle v_{1}+N,v_{2}+N\rangle=\langle v_{1},v_{2}\rangle.

(2.1)

To prove (
2.1
) is well-defined, we let
v^{\prime}_{1}=v_{1}+n_{1}
and
v^{\prime}_{2}=v_{2}+n_{2}
where
n_{1},n_{2}\in N
. By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle|\langle v_{1},n_{2}\rangle|^{2}\leq\langle v_{1},v_{1}\rangle\langle n_{2},n_{2}\rangle.

(2.2)

Since
n_{2}\in N
,
\langle n_{2},n_{2}\rangle=0
. Hence (
2.2
) implies

\langle v_{1},n_{2}\rangle=0
.
Similarly, we have

\langle n_{1},v_{2}\rangle=0
and

\langle n_{1},n_{2}\rangle=0
. Therefore, we have (
2.1
), and the inherited quadratic form
\langle~,~\rangle
is well-defined on
W/N
.

To prove
\langle~,~\rangle
is an inner product on
W/N
, we need to verify that it is strictly positive-definite. Suppose

\langle v+N,v+N\rangle=0
. Then
\langle v,v\rangle=0
, which implies
v\in N
. Therefore,
\langle~,~\rangle
is strictly positive-definite on
W/N
, thus it is an inner product.
∎

As a genralization of Lemma
2.1
, the next lemma shows that any quotient space of
W
inherits a quadratic form.

Lemma 2.2
.

For any sub-space
U
of
W
, the quotient space
W/U
inherits a symmetric semi-positive definite quadratic form
\langle~,~\rangle
from
W
. Moreover,
\langle~,~\rangle
is an inner product on
W/U
if and only if
N\subseteq U
.

Proof.

Let
U
be a sub-space of
W
. Restricted to
U
, the quadratic form
\langle~,~\rangle
on
W
gives a quadratic form
\langle~,~\rangle
on
U
, which is still symmetric and semi-positive definite. By Lemma
2.1
,
U/(U\cap N)
inherits an inner product
\langle~,~\rangle
. Note that as vector spaces,

\displaystyle U\cong U/(U\cap N)\oplus(U\cap N).

(2.3)

We let
\iota
be the isomorphism from the right-hand side of (
2.3
) to the left-hand side of (
2.3
) and let
U_{1}
be the image
\iota(U/(U\cap N))
. Then

\displaystyle U=U_{1}\oplus(U\cap N).

(2.4)

We point out that the isomorphism
\iota
may not be unique hence the sub-space
U_{1}
may not be unique as well. Suppose both
\iota
and
U_{1}
are fixed. For any
u\in U
, with respect to (
2.4
) we can write
u=u_{1}+n
uniquely where
u_{1}\in U_{1}
and
n\in U\cap N
. Then for any
u,u^{\prime}\in U
we have

\displaystyle\langle u,u^{\prime}\rangle=\langle u_{1},u^{\prime}_{1}\rangle.

(2.5)

The left-hand side of (
2.5
) is a semi-positive definite quadratic form and the right-hand-side of (
2.5
) is an inner product. By a similar argument of the Gram-Schmidt process, we can extend
U_{1}
to be a sub-space
W_{1}
of
W
such that

\displaystyle W=W_{1}\oplus N

(2.6)

as vector spaces where
W_{1}
inherits an inner product
\langle~,~\rangle
. We consider the orthogonal complement
\perp_{W_{1}}U_{1}
of
U_{1}
in
W_{1}
. Then

\displaystyle W_{1}=(\perp_{W_{1}}U_{1})\oplus U_{1}

(2.7)

as Euclidean spaces. It follows from (
2.6
) and (
2.7
) that for any
v\in W
, we can write

\displaystyle v=v_{0}+v_{2}+n

(2.8)

where
v_{0}\in U_{1}
,
v_{2}\in\perp_{W_{1}}U_{1}
and
n\in N
. Moreover, once
W_{1}
is fixed, the expression (
2.8
) is unique.
With the help of (
2.8
) we define the quadratic form on
W/U
by setting

\displaystyle\langle v+U,v^{\prime}+U\rangle=\langle v_{2},v^{\prime}_{2}\rangle

(2.9)

for any
v,v^{\prime}\in W
. Note that the right-hand side of (
2.9
) does not depend on the representatives
v
of
v+U
and
v^{\prime}
of
v^{\prime}+U
. Hence (
2.9
) gives a well-defined quadratic form on
W/U
, which is obviously symmetric and semi-positive definite.

We point out that
W_{1}
, the extension of
U_{1}
, may not be unique. Nevertheless, for any two such extensions
W_{1}
and
\bar{W}_{1}
of
U_{1}
, if we write the corresponding decompositions in (
2.8
) as
v=v_{0}+v_{2}+n
and
v=\bar{v}_{0}+\bar{v}_{2}+\bar{n}
respectively, then for any
v\in W
,
v_{0}=\bar{v}_{0}
; and for any
v,v^{\prime}\in V
,

\displaystyle\langle v_{2},v^{\prime}_{2}\rangle=\langle\bar{v}_{2},\bar{v}^{\prime}_{2}\rangle.

(2.10)

It follows from (
2.10
) that the inherited quadratic form on
W/U
given by (
2.9
) does not depend on the extension
W_{1}
. We obtain the first assertion.

By the above argument, it follows from (
2.4
) and (
2.6
) that

\displaystyle W/U\cong\big(\perp_{W_{1}}U_{1}\big)\oplus\big(N/(U\cap N)\big).

We see that
\langle~,~\rangle
is an inner product on
W/U
if and only if it is strictly positive definite on
W/U
, which happens if and only if

\displaystyle W/U\cong\perp_{W_{1}}U_{1}.

(2.11)

Therefore, (
2.11
) holds if and only if

\displaystyle N/(U\cap N)=0,

which happens if and only if
N\subseteq U
. We obtain the second assertion.
∎

With the help of Lemma
2.2
, we have the next lemma for linear maps between vector spaces with quadratic forms.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
W
and
W^{\prime}
be vector spaces with (symmetric and semi-positive definite) quadratic forms
\langle~,~\rangle
and
\langle~,~\rangle^{\prime}
respectively. Let
\varphi:W\longrightarrow W^{\prime}
be a linear isomorphism such that for any
a,b\in W
,
\langle\varphi(a),\varphi(b)\rangle^{\prime}=\langle a,b\rangle
. Let
U\subset W
and
U^{\prime}\subset W^{\prime}
be subspaces such that
\varphi(U)=U^{\prime}
. Then
\varphi
induces a linear isomorphism

\displaystyle\varphi_{*}:W/U\longrightarrow W^{\prime}/U^{\prime}

such that the induced symmetric and semi-positive defnite quadratic forms on
W/U
and
W^{\prime}/U^{\prime}
are preserved.

Proof.

It is clear that
\varphi
is a linear isomorphism. By Lemma
2.2
, there are induced symmetric and semi-positive defnite quadratic forms
\langle~,~\rangle
on
W/U
and
\langle~,~\rangle^{\prime}
on
W^{\prime}/U^{\prime}
. Moreover,
\varphi
preserves the decompositions (
2.6
) and (
2.7
). We verify that
\varphi
preserves the quadratic forms by

\displaystyle\langle v+U,w+U\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle v_{2},w_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\varphi(v_{2}),\varphi(w_{2})\rangle^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\varphi(v+U),\varphi(w+U)\rangle^{\prime}.

Here
v+U
and
w+U
are any two elements in
W/U
, and
v_{2}
and
w_{2}
are in
\perp_{W_{1}}U_{1}
given by (
2.8
). We obtain the lemma.
∎

3
Weighted Simplicial Complexes and Weighted Homology

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a simplicial complex.
Let
V
be the set of the vertices of
\mathcal{K}
.
Let
f
and
g
be two real-valued functions on
V
.
Then

\displaystyle(f,g):V\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}

is a vector-valued function
assigning a point in the plane
to each vertex in
V
.
We call both
f
and
g
a
vertex-weight

on
\mathcal{K}
and call the ordered triple
(\mathcal{K},f,g)

a
vertex-weighted simplicial complex
.

Let
n\geq 0
.
For any
n
-simplex
\sigma=\{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
in
\mathcal{K}
,
we define the induced weights of
f
and
g
on
\sigma
respectively as

\displaystyle f(\sigma)=\prod_{i=0}^{n}f(v_{i}),~~~g(\sigma)=\prod_{i=0}^{n}g(v_{i}).

Let
C_{n}(\mathcal{K};\mathbb{R})
be the vector space
consisting of all the formal linear combinations
of the
n
-simplices in
\mathcal{K}
.
The
n
-th
f
-weighted boundary operator
is a linear map

\displaystyle\partial_{n}^{f}:C_{n}(\mathcal{K};\mathbb{R})\longrightarrow C_{n-1}(\mathcal{K};\mathbb{R})

defined by

\displaystyle\partial_{n}^{f}\{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}f(v_{i})\{v_{0},\ldots,\widehat{v_{i}},\ldots,v_{n}\}

(3.1)

for any
n
-simplex
\{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}

of
\mathcal{K}
. Particularly, for any
n
-simplex
\sigma
in
\mathcal{K}
with
f(\sigma)\neq 0
, (
3.1
) can be alternatively expressed as

\displaystyle\partial_{n}^{f}\sigma=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}\frac{f(\sigma)}{f(d_{i}\sigma)}d_{i}\sigma

(3.2)

where for each
0\leq i\leq n
,
d_{i}\sigma
is the
i
-th
(n-1)
-face of
\sigma
obtained by removing the
i
-th vertex of
\sigma
. Note that
f(\sigma)\neq 0
implies
f(d_{i}\sigma)\neq 0
for each
0\leq i\leq n
. Hence (
3.2
) makes sense.
It can be verified that

\displaystyle\partial^{f}_{n}\partial^{f}_{n+1}=0

for any
n\geq 0
. Thus

\displaystyle\{C_{n}(\mathcal{K};\mathbb{R}),\partial_{n}^{f}\}_{n\geq 0}

(3.3)

is a chain complex.

The
g
-weighted quadratic form
on
C_{n}(\mathcal{K};\mathbb{R})
is a symmetric and semi-positive definite quadratic form

\displaystyle\langle~,~\rangle_{g}:C_{n}(\mathcal{K};\mathbb{R})\times C_{n}(\mathcal{K};\mathbb{R})\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}

(3.4)

given by

\displaystyle\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\sigma)g(\tau)\delta(\sigma,\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=0}^{n}g(v_{i})g(u_{i})\delta(v_{i},u_{i})

for any
n
-simplices
\sigma=\{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
and
\tau=\{u_{0},u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\}
in
\mathcal{K}
. Here for any vertices
v
and
u
, we use the notation
\delta(v,u)=0
if
v\neq u
and
\delta(v,u)=1
if
v=u
; and for any simplices
\sigma
and
\tau
, we use the notation
\delta(\sigma,\tau)=0
if
\sigma\neq\tau
and
\delta(\sigma,\tau)=1
if
\sigma=\tau
.

We notice that for each
n\geq 0
, (
3.4
) gives a
g
-weighted quadratic form on the vector space in the chain complex (
3.3
). We use the following notation

\displaystyle C_{*}(\mathcal{K},f,g;\mathbb{R})=\{C_{n}(\mathcal{K};\mathbb{R}),\partial_{n}^{f},\langle~,~\rangle_{g}\}_{n\geq 0}

(3.5)

to denote the chain complex (
3.3
) with the
f
-weighted boundary operators
\partial_{n}^{f}
and the
g
-weighted quadratic forms
\langle~,~\rangle_{g}
.
The
n
-th
(f,g)
-weighted homology
of
\mathcal{K}
(with coefficients in real numbers) is defined as the
n
-th homology group of the chain complex
C_{*}(\mathcal{K},f,g;\mathbb{R})
, i.e. the quotient space

\displaystyle H_{n}(\mathcal{K},f,g;\mathbb{R})={\rm Ker}(\partial_{n}^{f})/{\rm Im}(\partial_{n+1}^{f}).

The next theorem says that for each
n\geq 0
, the quadratic form
\langle~,~\rangle_{g}
on
C_{n}(\mathcal{K};\mathbb{R})
induces a quadratic form, which is still denoted as
\langle~,~\rangle_{g}
, on the
n
-th
(f,g)
-weighted homology group
H_{n}(\mathcal{K},f,g;\mathbb{R})
.

Theorem 3.1
.

For any vertex-weighted simplicial complex
(\mathcal{K},f,g)
and any
n\geq 0
,

H_{n}(\mathcal{K},f,g;\mathbb{R})
is a vector space with a symmetric and semi-positive definite quadratic form
\langle~,~\rangle_{g}
.

Proof.

In Lemma
2.2
, we let the space
W
be
{\rm Ker}\partial_{n}^{f}
and let the sub-space
U
of
W
be
{\rm Im}\partial_{n+1}^{f}
. As sub-spaces of
C_{n}(\mathcal{K};\mathbb{R})
, both
W
and
U
have an inherited symmetric and semi-positive definite quadratic forms
\langle~,~\rangle_{g}
. Thus by Lemma
2.2
, the quotient space
W/U
, which by definition is the
(f,g)
-weighted homology
H_{n}(\mathcal{K},f,g;\mathbb{R})
, has an induced symmetric and semi-positive definite quadratic form
\langle~,~\rangle_{g}
.
∎

For each
n\geq 0
, the null sub-space of
C_{n}(\mathcal{K};\mathbb{R})
with respect to
\langle~,~\rangle_{g}
is given by

\displaystyle N_{n}(\mathcal{K},g;\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{b\in C_{n}(\mathcal{K};\mathbb{R})\mid\langle b,b\rangle_{g}=0\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{b\in C_{n}(\mathcal{K};\mathbb{R})\mid\langle b,a\rangle_{g}=0{\rm~for~any~}a\in C_{n}(\mathcal{K};\mathbb{R})\}.

We denote
N_{*}(\mathcal{K},g;\mathbb{R})
for the graded vector space
\{N_{n}(\mathcal{K},g;\mathbb{R})\mid n=0,1,2,\ldots\}
. By applying the second assertion in Lemma
2.2
to the proof of Theorem
3.1
, the next corollary follows
immediately.

Corollary 3.2
.

For any vertex-weighted simplicial complex
(\mathcal{K},f,g)
and any
n\geq 0
,

\langle~,~\rangle_{g}
is an inner product on
H_{n}(\mathcal{K},f,g;\mathbb{R})
if and only if
N_{n}(\mathcal{K},g;\mathbb{R})
is a sub-space of
\partial^{f}_{n+1}C_{n}(\mathcal{K};\mathbb{R})
. ∎

For each vertex
v\in V
, we let
{\rm star}_{\mathcal{K}}(v)
be the open star of
v
in
\mathcal{K}
given by the set of the simplices containing
v
as a vertex:

\displaystyle{\rm star}_{\mathcal{K}}(v)=\{\sigma\in\mathcal{K}\mid v\in\sigma\}.

We have the next lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(\mathcal{K},f,g)
be a vertex-weighted simplicial complex. Then for any simplex
\sigma\in\mathcal{K}
,

\displaystyle g(\sigma)=0\Longleftrightarrow\sigma\in\bigcup_{v\in V,\atop g(v)=0}{\rm~star}_{\mathcal{K}}(v).

(3.6)

Thus for each
n\geq 0
,
N_{n}(\mathcal{K},g;\mathbb{R})
has a basis consisting of all the
n
-simplices in the union of open stars

\displaystyle\bigcup_{v\in V,\atop g(v)=0}{\rm~star}_{\mathcal{K}}(v).

(3.7)

Proof.

Let
\sigma\in\mathcal{K}
.
We write
\sigma
in the form
\{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
for some
n\geq 0
. Since
f
is a vertex-weight, it follows from the definition of
f(\sigma)
that
f(\sigma)=0
if and only if
f(v_{i})=0
for some
0\leq i\leq n
. This happens if and only if
\sigma
is in the open star of
v_{i}
for some
v_{i}\in V
with
f(v_{i})=0
. Thus we obtain (
3.6
). Consequently, by the definition of
N_{n}(\mathcal{K},g;\mathbb{R})
, it follows from (
3.6
) that for each
n\geq 0
,
N_{n}(\mathcal{K},g;\mathbb{R})
has a basis consisting of all the
n
-simplices in (
3.7
). We obtain the lemma.
∎

The following lemma shows that if we take the quotient space of
N_{n}(\mathcal{K},g;\mathbb{R})
in
C_{n}(\mathcal{K};\mathbb{R})
, then
we will obtain the chain complex of the maximal sub-simplicial complex
\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g}
of
\mathcal{K}
where
g
is non-vanishing.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
(\mathcal{K},f,g)
be a vertex-weighted simplicial complex. Then the sub-simplicial complex

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{g}^{\times}=\{\sigma\in\mathcal{K}\mid g(\sigma)\neq 0\}

is the maximal sub-simplicial complex of
\mathcal{K}
such that
g
is non-vanishing on all of its vertices. Moreover, for each
n\geq 0
, with respect to the inner products
\langle~,~\rangle_{g}
, there a canonical linear isometry

\displaystyle\iota_{n}:C_{n}(\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g};\mathbb{R})\longrightarrow C_{n}(\mathcal{K};\mathbb{R})/N_{n}(\mathcal{K},g;\mathbb{R}).

(3.8)

Proof.

Let
(\mathcal{K},f,g)
be a vertex-weighted simplicial complex. It follows from Lemma
3.3
that the maximal sub-simplicial complex
\mathcal{K}_{g}^{\times}
such that
g
is non-vanishing on all of its vertices is the complement of the union (
3.7
) of open stars

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{g}^{\times}=\mathcal{K}\setminus\Big(\bigcup_{v\in V,\atop g(v)=0}{\rm~star}_{\mathcal{K}}(v)\Big).

Consequently, for each
n\geq 0
, we have a linear isomorphism
\iota_{n}
in (
3.8
) sending an
n
-simplex
\sigma\in\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g}
to the co-set
\sigma+N_{n}(\mathcal{K},g;\mathbb{R})
. By Lemma
2.1
, for each
n\geq 0
, the quotient space
C_{n}(\mathcal{K};\mathbb{R})/N_{n}(\mathcal{K},g;\mathbb{R})
has an induced inner product
\langle~,~\rangle_{g}
. Since
\mathcal{K}_{g}^{\times}
is a sub-simplicial complex of
\mathcal{K}
,
C_{n}(\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g};\mathbb{R})
is a sub-space of
C_{n}(\mathcal{K};\mathbb{R})
thus it inherits a quadratic form
\langle~,~\rangle_{g}
. Note that
\langle~,~\rangle_{g}
is strictly positive-definite on
C_{n}(\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g};\mathbb{R})
hence it is an inner product on
C_{n}(\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g};\mathbb{R})
. For any simplices
\sigma
and
\tau
in
\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g}
, it can be verified that
\iota_{n}
preserves the inner products:

\displaystyle\langle\iota_{n}(\sigma),\iota_{n}(\tau)\rangle_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\sigma+N_{n}(\mathcal{K},g;\mathbb{R}),\tau+N_{n}(\mathcal{K},g;\mathbb{R})\rangle_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\sigma+\mu,\tau+\nu\rangle_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle_{g}+\langle\sigma,\nu\rangle_{g}+\langle\mu,\tau\rangle_{g}+\langle\mu,\nu\rangle_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle_{g}.

Here
\mu
and
\nu
are arbitrary elements in
N_{n}(\mathcal{K},g;\mathbb{R})
. Thus
\iota_{n}
is a linear isometry.
∎

By Lemma
3.4
, for each
n\geq 0
we can define the
f
-weighted
reduced boundary operator

\displaystyle\tilde{\partial}^{f}_{n}:C_{n}(\mathcal{K};\mathbb{R})/N_{n}(\mathcal{K},g;\mathbb{R})\longrightarrow C_{n}(\mathcal{K};\mathbb{R})/N_{n}(\mathcal{K},g;\mathbb{R})

(3.9)

as

\displaystyle\tilde{\partial}^{f}_{n}=\iota_{n}\circ(\partial_{n}^{f}\mid_{\mathcal{K}_{g}^{\times}})\circ\iota_{n}^{-1}.

(3.10)

Here

\displaystyle\partial_{n}^{f}\mid_{\mathcal{K}_{g}^{\times}}:C_{n}(\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g};\mathbb{R})\longrightarrow C_{n-1}(\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g};\mathbb{R})

(3.11)

is the
n
-th
f
-weighted boundary operator of
\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g}
, which is obtained by restricting the
f
-weighted boundary operator
\partial_{n}^{f}
of
C_{n}(\mathcal{K};\mathbb{R})
to the sub-chain complex
C_{n}(\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g};\mathbb{R})
.
Similar with (
3.5
), we use the following notations

\displaystyle C_{*}(\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g},f,g;\mathbb{R})=\{C_{n}(\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g};\mathbb{R}),\partial^{f}_{n},\langle~,~\rangle_{g}\}_{n\geq 0}

and

\displaystyle\tilde{C}_{*}(\mathcal{K},f,g;\mathbb{R})=\{C_{n}(\mathcal{K};\mathbb{R})/N_{n}(\mathcal{K},g;\mathbb{R}),\tilde{\partial}^{f}_{n},\langle~,~\rangle_{g}\}_{n\geq 0}

to denote the chain complexes with the
f
-weighted boundary operators
\partial_{n}^{f}
(or
\tilde{\partial}^{f}_{n}
) and the
g
-weighted inner products
\langle~,~\rangle_{g}
. We have the next proposition.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
(\mathcal{K},f,g)
be a vertex-weighted simplicial complex. Then
we have an isomorphism of chain complexes

\displaystyle\iota_{*}:\tilde{C}_{*}(\mathcal{K},f,g;\mathbb{R})\longrightarrow C_{*}(\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g},f,g;\mathbb{R})

where for each
n\geq 0
,
\iota_{n}
is a linear isometry with respect to the corresponding inner products
\langle~,~\rangle_{g}
.

Proof.

The proof follows by the definition of
\iota_{*}
in Lemma
3.4
.
∎

By taking the homology of the chain complexes, the isomorphism
\iota_{*}
induces an isomorphism of homology groups. The next corollary follows from Proposition
3.5
immediately.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
(\mathcal{K},f,g)
be a vertex-weighted simplicial complex. Then for each
n\geq 0
, the weighted homology
H_{n}(\mathcal{K}_{g}^{\times},f,g;\mathbb{R})
is isometrically isomorphic to the homology
H_{n}(\tilde{C}_{*}(\mathcal{K},f,g;\mathbb{R}))
of the chain complex
\tilde{C}_{*}(\mathcal{K},f,g;\mathbb{R})
with respect to the inner products
\langle~,~\rangle_{g}
. ∎

In the remaining part of this section, we study the effects of
f
and
g
on the weighted homology
H_{n}(\mathcal{K},f,g;\mathbb{R})
. We prove the next lemma by using an argument similar with
[
17
, Lemma 3.1]
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a simplicial complex with the set
V
of its vertices. Let
f
and
g
be any two real-valued functions on
V
. Let
h
a non-vanishing real-valued function on
V
. Then there is a canonical chain isomorphism

\displaystyle\varphi:C_{*}(\mathcal{K},f,g;\mathbb{R})\longrightarrow C_{*}(\mathcal{K},fh,gh;\mathbb{R})

(3.12)

given by

\displaystyle\varphi\Big(\sum x_{\{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}}\{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}\Big)=\sum\frac{x_{\{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}}}{h(v_{0})h(v_{1})\cdots h(v_{n})}\{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}

(3.13)

such that

\displaystyle\langle\varphi(a),\varphi(b)\rangle_{gh}=\langle a,b\rangle_{g}

for any
n
-chains
a
and
b
in
C_{*}(\mathcal{K},f,g;\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

Let
n\geq 0
. We denote an
n
-simplex of
\mathcal{K}
as
\{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
where the vertices
v_{i}
are distinct for
i=0,1,\ldots,n
and
v_{i}\prec v_{j}
for
0\leq i<j\leq n
with respect to the total order
\prec
on
V
.

Firstly, we take

\displaystyle\sum x_{\{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}}\{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}

to be any
n
-chain in
C_{n}(\mathcal{K};\mathbb{R})
. Then we have

\displaystyle\partial_{n}^{fh}\circ\varphi\Big(\sum x_{\{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}}\{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{n}^{fh}\Big(\sum\frac{x_{\{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}}}{h(v_{0})h(v_{1})\cdots h(v_{n})}\{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\frac{x_{\{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}}}{h(v_{0})h(v_{1})\cdots h(v_{n})}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i}f(v_{i})h(v_{i})\{v_{0},\ldots,\widehat{v_{i}},\ldots,v_{n}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum x_{\{v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n}\}}\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}f(v_{i})\frac{\{v_{0},\ldots,\widehat{v_{i}},\ldots,v_{n}\}}{h(v_{0})\cdots\widehat{h(v_{i})}\cdots h(v_{n})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi\circ\partial_{n}^{f}\Big(\sum x_{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}}\{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}\Big).

Thus
\varphi
is a chain map.

Secondly, since
h
is non-vanishing on
V
, it follows from (
3.13
) directly that
\varphi
is a linear isomorphism from
C_{n}(\mathcal{K};\mathbb{R})
to itself.

Thirdly, let

\displaystyle\sum x_{\{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}}\{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}{\rm~and~}\sum y_{\{u_{0},u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\}}\{u_{0},u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\}

be any two of the
n
-chains in
C_{n}(\mathcal{K};\mathbb{R})
. Then

\displaystyle\Big\langle\varphi\Big(\sum x_{\{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}}\{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}\Big),\varphi\Big(\sum y_{\{u_{0},u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\}}\{u_{0},u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\}\Big)\Big\rangle_{gh}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\sum\frac{x_{\{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}}}{h(v_{0})h(v_{1})\cdots h(v_{n})}\cdot\frac{y_{\{u_{0},u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\}}}{h(u_{0})h(u_{1})\cdots h(u_{n})}\langle\{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\},\{u_{0},u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\}\rangle_{gh}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\sum\frac{x_{\{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}}}{h(v_{0})h(v_{1})\cdots h(v_{n})}\cdot\frac{y_{\{u_{0},u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\}}}{h(u_{0})h(u_{1})\cdots h(u_{n})}\prod_{i=0}^{n}g(v_{i})h(v_{i})g(u_{i})h(u_{i})\delta(v_{i},u_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\sum x_{\{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}}y_{\{u_{0},u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\}}\prod_{i=0}^{n}g(v_{i})g(u_{i})\delta(v_{i},u_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\langle\sum x_{\{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}}\{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\},\sum y_{\{u_{0},u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\}}\{u_{0},u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\}\Big\rangle_{g}.

Thus
\varphi
preserves the quadratic forms with respect to
\langle~,~\rangle_{gh}
and
\langle~,~\rangle_{g}
.

Summarizing all the above three steps, we obtain the lemma.
∎

The next corollary follows directly by applying Lemma
3.7
to the sub-simplicial complex
\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g}
.

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a simplicial complex with the set
V
of its vertices. Let
f
and
g
be any two real-valued functions on
V
. Let
h
a non-vanishing real-valued function on
V
. Then there is a canonical chain isomorphism

\displaystyle\varphi:C_{*}(\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g},f,g;\mathbb{R})\longrightarrow C_{*}(\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g},fh,gh;\mathbb{R})

(3.14)

given by (
3.13
)
which is an isometry with respect to the inner products
\langle~,~\rangle_{g}
and
\langle~,~\rangle_{gh}
respectively.

Proof.

Note that
g
is non-vanishing on the set of the vertices of
\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g}
. Thus
\langle~,~\rangle_{g}
is an inner product on
C_{n}(\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g};\mathbb{R})
for each
n\geq 0
. Moreover, since
h
is non-vanishing,
\langle~,~\rangle_{gh}
is an inner product on
C_{n}(\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g};\mathbb{R})
as well for each
n\geq 0
.
∎

With the help of Lemma
3.7
, we have the next theorem.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
(\mathcal{K},f,g)
be a vertex-weighted simplicial complex where
f
and
g
are any real-valued functions on
V
. Let
h
be a non-vanishing real function on
V
. Then for each
n\geq 0
, we have a linear isomorphism

\displaystyle\varphi_{*}:H_{n}(\mathcal{K},f,g;\mathbb{R})\longrightarrow H_{n}(\mathcal{K},fh,gh;\mathbb{R})

(3.15)

which preserves the quadratic forms with respect to
\langle~,~\rangle_{g}
and
\langle~,~\rangle_{gh}
. Here
\varphi_{*}
is induced by the chain isomorphism
\varphi
in Lemma
3.7
.

Proof.

Since
\varphi
in Lemma
3.7
is a chain isomorphism that preserves the quadratic forms, it follows with the help of Lemma
2.3
that
\varphi_{*}
induces a linear isomorphism
\varphi_{*}
from the
n
-th
(f,g)
-weighted homology group
H_{n}(\mathcal{K},f,g;\mathbb{R})
to the
n
-th
(fh,gh)
-weighted homology group
H_{n}(\mathcal{K},fh,gh;\mathbb{R})
that preserves the quadratic forms on the quotient spaces. The theorem follows.
∎

Remark 1
:

Particularly, if both

and

, then Theorem

implies that the

-weighted homology

(or

equivalently).

Similar with Theorem
3.9
, the next corollary follows from Corollary
3.8
.

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
(\mathcal{K},f,g)
be a vertex-weighted simplicial complex. Then for each

and each non-vanishing function

on the set of the vertices, we have a linear isometry

\displaystyle\varphi_{*}:H_{n}(\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g},f,g;\mathbb{R})\longrightarrow H_{n}(\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g},fh,gh;\mathbb{R}).

(3.16)

In particular, taking

\displaystyle H_{n}(\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{f},f,f;\mathbb{R})\cong H_{n}(\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{\epsilon(f)};\mathbb{R})

4
Weighted Analytic Torsions for Weighted Simplicial Complexes

Throughout this section, we
let
(\mathcal{K},f,g)
be a vertex-weighted simplicial complex where
\mathcal{K}
is a simplicial complex and
f
and
g
are arbitrary real-valued functions on the set
V
of the vertices of
\mathcal{K}
.

For each
n\geq 0
, we let
\langle~,~\rangle_{g}
be the inner product on
C_{n}(\mathcal{K};\mathbb{R})/N_{n}(\mathcal{K},g;\mathbb{R})
induced by
g
.
Let

\displaystyle(\tilde{\partial}^{f}_{n})^{*}_{g}:C_{n-1}(\mathcal{K};\mathbb{R})/N_{n}(\mathcal{K},g;\mathbb{R})\longrightarrow C_{n}(\mathcal{K};\mathbb{R})/N_{n}(\mathcal{K},g;\mathbb{R})

be the adjoint linear map of
\tilde{\partial}_{n}^{f}
(which is given in (
3.9
) and (
3.10
)) with respect to the inner product
\langle~,~\rangle_{g}
such that

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{\partial}^{f}_{n}(a),b\rangle_{g}=\langle a,(\tilde{\partial}^{f}_{n})^{*}_{g}(b)\rangle_{g}

for any
n
-chains
a
and
b
in
\tilde{C}_{*}(\mathcal{K},f,g;\mathbb{R})
. The
n
-th
(f,g)
-weighted (reduced) Hodge-Laplace operator (on the quotient chain complex) is the linear map

\displaystyle\tilde{\Delta}_{n}^{f,g}:\tilde{C}_{n}(\mathcal{K};\mathbb{R})\longrightarrow\tilde{C}_{n}(\mathcal{K};\mathbb{R})

given by

\displaystyle\tilde{\Delta}_{n}^{f,g}(c)=(\tilde{\partial}^{f}_{n})^{*}_{g}\tilde{\partial}^{f}_{n}(c)+(\tilde{\partial}^{f}_{n+1})^{*}_{g}\tilde{\partial}^{f}_{n+1}(c)

for any
n
-chains
c
in
\tilde{C}_{*}(\mathcal{K},f,g;\mathbb{R})
.

On the other hand, for each
n\geq 0
, we also use
\langle~,~\rangle_{g}
to denote the inner product on
C_{n}(\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g};\mathbb{R})
with respect to
g
. Let

\displaystyle(\partial^{f}_{n}\mid_{\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g}})^{*}_{g}:C_{n-1}(\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g};\mathbb{R})\longrightarrow C_{n}(\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g};\mathbb{R})

be the adjoint linear map of
\partial_{n}^{f}\mid_{\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g}}
(which is given in (
3.11
)) with respect to the inner products
\langle~,~\rangle_{g}
such that

\displaystyle\langle\partial^{f}_{n}(a),b\rangle_{g}=\langle a,(\partial^{f}_{n}\mid_{\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g}})^{*}_{g}(b)\rangle_{g}

for any
n
-chains
a
and
b
in
C_{*}(\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g},f,g;\mathbb{R})
. The
n
-th
(f,g)
-weighted Hodge-Laplace operator of
\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g}
is the linear map

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}^{f,g}:C_{n}(\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g};\mathbb{R})\longrightarrow C_{n}(\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g};\mathbb{R})

given by

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}^{f,g}(c)=(\partial^{f}_{n}\mid_{\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g}})^{*}_{g}\partial^{f}_{n}(c)+(\partial^{f}_{n+1}\mid_{\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g}})^{*}_{g}\partial^{f}_{n+1}(c)

for any
n
-chains
c
in
C_{*}(\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g},f,g;\mathbb{R})
.

The reduced Hodge-Laplace operator
\Delta_{n}^{f,g}
and the Hodge Laplace operator
\Delta_{n}^{f,g}
can be identified via the isomorphism
\iota_{n}
. In fact, it follows from Proposition
3.5
and
[
17
, Lemma 2.2]
that for each
n\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}^{f,g}=\iota_{n}\circ\tilde{\Delta}_{n}^{f,g}\circ(\iota_{n})^{-1}.

Since
\iota_{n}
is an isometry, it follows that counted with multiplicities, the multi-set of the eigenvalues of
\Delta_{n}^{f,g}
and the multi-set of the eigenvalues of
\tilde{\Delta}_{n}^{f,g}
are equal. For simplicity, we do not distinguish
\tilde{\Delta}_{n}^{f,g}
and
\Delta_{n}^{f,g}
. We define the
n
-th
(f,g)
-weighted Hodge-Laplace operator
of the vertex-weighted simplicial complex
(\mathcal{K},f,g)
as

\Delta_{n}^{f,g}
.

We observe that
\Delta_{n}^{f,g}
is symmetric and semi-positive definite. Hence its matrix representatives are diagonalizable and its eigenvalues are non-negative. We use
\lambda
to denote any eigenvalue of
\Delta_{n}^{f,g}
. The zeta function
\zeta_{n}(s)
is defined by

\displaystyle\zeta_{n}(s)=\sum_{\lambda>0}\frac{1}{\lambda^{s}}.

The
(f,g)
-weighted analytic torsion of
(\mathcal{K},f,g)
is given by

\displaystyle\log T(\mathcal{K},f,g)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{N}(-1)^{n}n\zeta^{\prime}(0),

(4.1)

where
N
is the dimension of
\mathcal{K}
. The next proposition follows from Lemma
3.7
immediately.

Proposition 4.1
.

For any simplicial complex
\mathcal{K}
, any real-valued weights
f
and
g
on the set
V
, and any non-vanishing real function
h
on
V
, we have

\displaystyle T(\mathcal{K},hf,hg)=T(\mathcal{K},f,g).

Proof.

By Lemma
3.7
and
[
17
, Lemma 2.2]
, we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}^{f,g}=\varphi^{-1}\circ\Delta_{n}^{hf,hg}\circ\varphi

whiich implies that
all the eigenvalues (counted with multiplicities) of
\Delta_{n}^{f,g}
for
\mathcal{K}_{n}^{hf,hg}
are equal. By the definition (
4.1
) of the weighted analytic torsions, the proposition follows.
∎

We fix
f
and multiply
g
by a non-zero scalar
c
. By applying
[
4
, Corollary 3.8]
to the R-torsion, we have

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a simplicial complex of dimension
N
with the set of its vertices
V
. Let
f
and
g
be two real-valued weights on
V
such that for any
v\in V
,
g(v)\neq 0
implies
f(v)\neq 0
. Let
c\neq 0
be a real constant. Then

\displaystyle T(\mathcal{K},f,cg)=t(\mathcal{K},c,\epsilon(g))T(\mathcal{K},f,g)

(4.2)

where
t(\mathcal{K},c,\epsilon(g))=|c|^{s(\mathcal{K},\epsilon(g))}
and

\displaystyle s(\mathcal{K},\epsilon(g))=\sum_{n=0}^{N}(-1)^{n}\dim\partial_{n}C_{n}(\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{\epsilon(g)};\mathbb{R}).

Proof.

Since for any
v\in V
,
g(v)\neq 0
implies
f(v)\neq 0
, we have
that both
f
and
g
are non-vanishing on the set of the vertices of
\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g}
. Hence the ratio function
g/f
is well-defined on
\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g}
. Consequently, with the help of Theorem
3.9
,
H_{n}(\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g},f,g;\mathbb{R})
is linearly isometric to
H_{n}(\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g},1,g/f;\mathbb{R})
, which is linearly isomorphic to
H_{n}(\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g};\mathbb{R})
.
For any
n\geq 0
and any
a,b\in C_{n}(\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g};\mathbb{R})
, we have

\displaystyle\langle a,b\rangle_{cg}=c^{2(n+1)}\langle a,b\rangle_{g}.

Thus by
[
4
, Corollary 3.8]
, we have (
4.2
) where

\displaystyle t(\mathcal{K},c,\epsilon(g))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{n=0}^{N}|c|^{(-1)^{n}(n+1)\big(\dim C_{n}(\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g};\mathbb{R})-\dim H_{n}(\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g},f,g;\mathbb{R})\big)}

(4.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|c|^{\sum_{n=0}^{N}(-1)^{n}(n+1)\big(\dim C_{n}(\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g};\mathbb{R})-\dim H_{n}(\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g};\mathbb{R})\big)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|c|^{\sum_{n=0}^{N}(-1)^{n}n\big(\dim C_{n}(\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g};\mathbb{R})-\dim H_{n}(\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g};\mathbb{R})\big)}.

The last equality of (
4.3
) follows from the formulas for Euler characteristic number

\displaystyle\chi(\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g})=\sum_{n=0}^{N}(-1)^{n}\dim C_{n}(\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g};\mathbb{R})=\sum_{n=0}^{N}(-1)^{n}\dim H_{n}(\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g};\mathbb{R}).

Therefore, with the help of
[
17
, Lemma 2.1]
, the right-hand side of the last equality in (
4.3
) equals to
|c|^{s(\mathcal{K},\epsilon(g))}
.
∎

We fix
g
and multiply
f
by a non-zero scalar
c
. By a straight-forward calculation of the analytic torsion, we have

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a simplicial complex of dimension
N
with the set of its vertices
V
. Let
f
and
g
be a real-valued weights on
V
such that for any
v\in V
,
g(v)\neq 0
implies
f(v)\neq 0
. Let
c\neq 0
be a real constant. Then

\displaystyle T(\mathcal{K},cf,g)=t(\mathcal{K},c,\epsilon(g))^{-1}T(\mathcal{K},f,g).

(4.4)

Proof.

The proof is an analogue of the proof for
[
17
, Proposition 3.7]
. Let
n\geq 0
.
It follows directly that

\displaystyle\partial_{n}^{cf}=c\partial_{n}^{f},~~~(\partial_{n+1}^{cf}\mid_{\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g}})^{*}=c(\partial_{n+1}^{f}\mid_{\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g}})^{*}.

Thus we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}^{cf,g}=c^{2}\Delta_{n}^{f,g}.

Consequently, with the help of
[
4
, (3.21)]
,

\displaystyle\log T(\mathcal{K},cf,g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{N}(-1)^{n}n\big(-\sum_{\lambda_{i}>0}\log(\lambda_{i})\big)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{N}(-1)^{n}n(2\log|c|)\big(\dim C_{n}(\mathcal{K}_{g}^{\times};\mathbb{R})-\dim H_{n}(\mathcal{K}_{g}^{\times};\mathbb{R})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{N}(-1)^{n}n\frac{d}{ds}\Big|_{s=0}\Big(\sum_{\lambda_{i}>0}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}^{s}}\Big)-(\log|c|)s(\mathcal{K},\epsilon(g))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log T(\mathcal{K},f,g)-(\log|c|)s(\mathcal{K},\epsilon(g)).

Taking the exponential map on both sides of the equations, we have
(
4.4
).
∎

Finally, summarizing Proposition
4.2
and Proposition
4.3
, we obtain the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Given a weighted simplicial complex
(\mathcal{K},f,g)
, We consider the new weighted simplicial complex
(\mathcal{K},f^{\prime},g^{\prime})
where
f^{\prime}=\epsilon(g)f
and
g^{\prime}=\epsilon(f)g
. Then we have

\displaystyle\epsilon(f^{\prime})=\epsilon(g^{\prime})=\epsilon(f)\epsilon(g).

Applying Proposition
4.1
to
(\mathcal{K},f^{\prime}g^{\prime})
, we obtain Theorem
1.1
(i). Applying Proposition
4.2
and Proposition
4.3
to
(\mathcal{K},f^{\prime},g^{\prime})
, we obtain Theorem
1.1
(ii).
∎

Example 4.4
.

Let
V=\{v_{0},v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\}
. Let
f
and
g
be two real-valued non-vanishing functions on
V
such that (i).
f
is non-vanishing on
V
; (ii).
g(v_{0}),g(v_{1}),g(v_{2})\neq 0
and
g(v_{3})=0
. In the following, we use
v_{0}
to denote a
0
-simplex
\{v_{0}\}
, use
v_{0}v_{1}
to denote a
1
-simplex
\{v_{0},v_{1}\}
, etc.

(1).

Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}=\{v_{0},v_{1},v_{2},v_{0}v_{1},v_{1}v_{2},v_{0}v_{2},v_{0}v_{1}v_{2},v_{0}v_{3},v_{1}v_{3},v_{2}v_{3}\}

Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{\epsilon(f)\epsilon(g)}=\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g}=\{v_{0},v_{1},v_{2},v_{0}v_{1},v_{1}v_{2},v_{0}v_{2},v_{0}v_{1}v_{2}\}

is a solid triangle.
Similar with the calculation in
[
4
, Example 3.10]
and
[
17
, Example 4.2]
,
we have

\displaystyle T(\mathcal{K},f,g)=T(\mathcal{K}^{\times}_{g},f,g)=\sqrt{\sum_{i=0}^{2}\frac{f(v_{i})^{2}}{g(v_{i})^{2}}}.

(2).

Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\{v_{0},v_{1},v_{2},v_{0}v_{1},v_{1}v_{2},v_{0}v_{2},v_{0}v_{3},v_{1}v_{3},v_{2}v_{3}\}.

Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\times}_{\epsilon(f)\epsilon(g)}=\mathcal{L}^{\times}_{g}=\{v_{0},v_{1},v_{2},v_{0}v_{1},v_{1}v_{2},v_{0}v_{2}\}.

We choose the following
\langle~,~\rangle_{g}
-orthonormal bases

\displaystyle\omega_{0}=\Big\{\frac{v_{0}}{g(v_{0})},\frac{v_{1}}{g(v_{1})},\frac{v_{2}}{g(v_{2})}\Big\}

in
C_{0}(\mathcal{L}^{\times}_{g};\mathbb{R})
and

\displaystyle\omega_{1}=\Big\{\frac{v_{0}v_{1}}{g(v_{0})g(v_{1})},\frac{v_{1}v_{2}}{g(v_{1})g(v_{2})},\frac{v_{0}v_{2}}{g(v_{0})g(v_{2})}\Big\}

in
C_{1}(\mathcal{L}^{\times}_{g};\mathbb{R})
. Choose also the basis

\displaystyle b_{0}=\Big\{\frac{f(v_{0})v_{1}-f(v_{1})v_{0}}{g(v_{0})g(v_{1})},\frac{f(v_{1})v_{2}-f(v_{2})v_{1}}{g(v_{1})g(v_{2})}\Big\}

in
\partial_{1}^{f}C_{1}(\mathcal{L}^{\times}_{g};\mathbb{R})
. Then its lift is

\displaystyle\tilde{b}_{1}=\Big\{\frac{v_{0}v_{1}}{g(v_{0})g(v_{1})},\frac{v_{1}v_{2}}{g(v_{1})g(v_{2})}\Big\}.

Taking the orthogonal complement of
\partial_{1}^{f}C_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{g}^{\times};\mathbb{R})
in
{\rm Ker}\partial_{0}^{f}
with respect to
\langle~,~\rangle_{g}
, we have

\displaystyle h_{0}=\Big\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=0}^{2}\frac{f(v_{i})^{2}}{g(v_{i})^{2}}}}\Big(\frac{f(v_{0})}{g(v_{0})^{2}}v_{0}+\frac{f(v_{1})}{g(v_{1})^{2}}v_{1}+\frac{f(v_{2})}{g(v_{2})^{2}}v_{2}\Big)\Big\}.

Moreover, note that since
C_{2}(\mathcal{L}^{\times}_{g};\mathbb{R})=0
, we have
\partial_{2}C_{2}(\mathcal{L}^{\times}_{g};\mathbb{R})=0
, which implies
b_{1}=0
. Hence

\displaystyle h_{1}=\Big\{\frac{f(v_{0}){v_{1}v_{2}}-f(v_{1})v_{0}v_{2}+f(v_{2})v_{0}v_{1}}{g(v_{0})g(v_{1})g(v_{2})}\Big\}.

Thus

\displaystyle[b_{0},h_{0},\tilde{b}_{0}/\omega_{0}]=\Big|\frac{f(v_{1})}{g(v_{1})}\Big|\sqrt{\sum_{i=0}^{2}\frac{f(v_{i})^{2}}{g(v_{i})^{2}}}

and

\displaystyle[b_{1},h_{1},\tilde{b}_{1}/\omega_{1}]=\frac{|f(v_{1})|}{|g(v_{1})|}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle T(\mathcal{L},f,g)=T(\mathcal{L}^{\times}_{g},f,g)=\sqrt{\sum_{i=0}^{2}\frac{f(v_{i})^{2}}{g(v_{i})^{2}}}.

Example 4.5
.

Let
V=\{v_{0},v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4}\}
. Suppose both
f
and
g
are non-vanishing functions on

.

(1).

Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}=\{v_{0},v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4},v_{0}v_{1},v_{1}v_{2},v_{2}v_{3},v_{3}v_{4}\}.

Then similar with the calculation in

(2).

Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\{0,1,2,3,01,12,23,02,03,13\}.

-orthonormal bases

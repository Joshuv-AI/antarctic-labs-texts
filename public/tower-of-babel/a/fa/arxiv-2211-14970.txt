Weak Convergence of Spectral Shift Functions Revisited
Carson Connard, Benjamin Ingimarson, Roger Nichols, Andrew Paul

Abstract
. We study convergence of the spectral shift function for the finite interval restrictions of a pair of full-line Schrödinger operators to an interval of the form ( − ℓ , ℓ ) (-\ell,\ell) with coupled boundary conditions at the endpoints as ℓ → ∞ \ell\to\infty in the case when the finite interval restrictions are relatively prime to those with Dirichlet boundary conditions. Using a Krein-type resolvent identity we show that the spectral shift function for the finite interval restrictions converges weakly to that for the pair of full-line Schrödinger operators as the length of the interval tends to infinity.

1.
Introduction

It is a classical fact that the one-dimensional self-adjoint Schrödinger operators
H_{0}=-d^{2}/dx^{2}
and
H=-d^{2}/dx^{2}+V
, with
V
real-valued and Lebesgue integrable on
{\mathbb{R}}
, are resolvent comparable in the sense that their resolvent difference

(1.1)

\big(H-zI_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1}-\big(H_{0}-zI_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1}

belongs to the trace class for all
z\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathbb{R}}
. Consequently, there is a corresponding unique real-valued spectral shift function
\xi(\,\cdot\,;H,H_{0})
defined a.e. on
{\mathbb{R}}
that vanishes in a neighborhood of
-\infty
and satisfies

(1.2)

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{|\xi(\lambda;H,H_{0})|}{1+\lambda^{2}}\,\rm{d}\lambda<\infty.

Moreover, for a wide class of functions
f
(see (
A.25
)–(
A.26
)), Krein’s trace formula holds:

(1.3)

\mbox{tr}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}{(f(H)-f(H_{0}))}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f^{\prime}(\lambda)\xi(\lambda;H,H_{0})\,\rm{d}\lambda,

where
\mbox{tr}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}{(\,\cdot\,)}
denotes the trace functional.

When
H_{0}
and
H
are restricted to a finite interval, say
(-\ell,\ell)
with
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
, to obtain the finite interval Schrödinger operators
H_{0,\ell}
and
H_{\ell}
(imposing a fixed common choice of self-adjoint boundary conditions at the endpoints
\pm\ell
, thereby ensuring the self-adjointness of
H_{0,\ell}
and
H_{\ell}
), the finite interval restrictions
H_{0,\ell}
and
H_{\ell}
are also resolvent comparable, so a unique real-valued spectral shift function
\xi(\,\cdot\,;H_{\ell},H_{\ell,0})
with properties analogous to those of
\xi(\,\cdot\,;H,H_{0})
exists. A problem of interest, dating back at least to the work of Borovyk and Makarov
[
2
]
(see also
[
1
]
and the much earlier, related work of Kirsch
[
8
]
) has been to study the modes of convergence of
\xi(\,\cdot\,;H_{\ell},H_{0,\ell})
to
\xi(\,\cdot\,;H,H_{0})
as
\ell\to\infty
. Since
\xi(\,\cdot\,;H_{\ell},H_{0,\ell})
is integer-valued, owing to the discrete nature of the spectra of
H_{\ell}
and
H_{0,\ell}
, and
\xi(\,\cdot\,;H,H_{0})
is continuous and nonconstant on
(0,\infty)
, pointwise convergence of
\xi(\,\cdot\,;H_{\ell},H_{0,\ell})
to
\xi(\,\cdot\,;H,H_{0})
as
\ell\to\infty
is immediately dismissed as impossible. Borovyk and Makarov showed that a certain averaging is needed in order to obtain convergence. More specifically, in
[
2
]
, Borovyk and Makarov proved that when Dirichlet boundary conditions are imposed at the endpoints of the finite intervals, the sequence
\xi(\,\cdot\,;H_{\ell},H_{0,\ell})
converges to
\xi(\,\cdot\,;H,H_{0})
weakly in the sense that

(1.4)

\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}g(\lambda)\xi(\lambda;H_{\ell},H_{0,\ell})\,\textrm{d}\lambda=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}g(\lambda)\xi(\lambda;H,H_{0})\,\textrm{d}\lambda,\quad g\in C_{c}({\mathbb{R}}),

where
C_{c}({\mathbb{R}})
denotes the set of compactly supported continuous complex-valued functions on
{\mathbb{R}}
. To be as precise as possible, the finite intervals considered in
[
2
]
are of the form
(0,\ell)
and the infinite interval is the half-line
(0,\infty)
, but the arguments given in
[
2
]
may be modified to treat the symmetric intervals
(-\ell,\ell)
and the line
{\mathbb{R}}
. Using an abstract approach based on modified Fredholm determinants, (
1.4
) was extended to all separated self-adjoint boundary conditions in
[
4
]
(the abstract approach is separately developed in
[
5
]
). Actually, in
[
4
]
, (
1.4
) was improved to

(1.5)

\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h(\lambda)\frac{\xi(\lambda;H_{\ell},H_{0,\ell})}{1+\lambda^{2}}\,\textrm{d}\lambda=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h(\lambda)\frac{\xi(\lambda;H,H_{0})}{1+\lambda^{2}}\,\textrm{d}\lambda,\quad h\in C_{b}({\mathbb{R}}),

where
C_{b}({\mathbb{R}})
denotes the set of bounded continuous complex-valued functions on
{\mathbb{R}}
. However,
[
4
]
does not discuss coupled self-adjoint boundary conditions at
\pm\ell
, which are of the form

(1.6)

\begin{pmatrix}y(\ell)\\
y^{\prime}(\ell)\end{pmatrix}=e^{i\varphi}R\begin{pmatrix}y(-\ell)\\
y^{\prime}(-\ell)\end{pmatrix}

for a fixed parameter
\varphi\in[0,\pi)
and a fixed matrix
R=(R_{j,k})_{1\leq j,k\leq 2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2\times 2}
with
\det{(R)}=1
. In
[
9
]
, (
1.5
) was shown to hold for a special subclass of the coupled boundary conditions (
1.6
), namely those for which
R_{1,2}=0
, so that
R
has the special form

(1.7)

R=\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\
b&a^{-1}\end{pmatrix}

for some
a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}
with
a\neq 0
. The approach taken in
[
9
]
is based on an analysis of the coefficient in the Krein identity connecting the resolvent of the finite interval restriction with the boundary conditions (
1.6
)–(
1.7
) to the resolvent of the finite interval restriction with Dirichlet boundary conditions. In the case of (
1.7
), the difference of the resolvents of the coupled and Dirichlet restrictions is a rank one operator, so the Krein identity contains exactly one coefficient. The work in
[
9
]
leaves open the question of convergence—in particular, the validity of (
1.5
)—when
R_{1,2}\neq 0
.

For
R_{1,2}\neq 0
, the situation is slightly more delicate as the resolvent difference is then a rank two operator and the corresponding Krein identity is more easily understood in terms of four coefficients, see Theorem
2.8
below and
[
3
, Theorem 3.2
(i)
]
. Our main aim here is to extend (
1.5
) to the case
R_{1,2}\neq 0
. To do this, we carefully analyze the
\ell\to\infty
behavior of the four coefficients in the Krein identity and use the abstract criteria for convergence of spectral shift functions developed in
[
5
]
to extend (
1.5
) to the case
R_{1,2}\neq 0
. When combined with
[
4
]
and
[
9
]
, the results we obtain here show that (
1.5
) holds for all self-adjoint boundary conditions.

In Section
2
, we rigorously define the full-line Schrödinger operators
H
and
H_{0}
and their finite interval counterparts and recall some of their basic properties. We also recall Krein’s resolvent identity and separately investigate the behavior of its coefficients with respect to both the finite interval length and the spectral parameter. The key results needed to invoke the abstract criteria from
[
4
]
for convergence of spectral shift functions—in particular, convergence of various Birman–Schwinger-type operators in the trace and Hilbert–Schmidt classes—are established in Section
3
. Finally, in Section
4
, we combine the results from Section
3
with the abstract convergence criteria from
[
5
]
to obtain our main result on convergence of spectral shift functions corresponding to coupled boundary conditions with
R_{1,2}\neq 0
. For completeness, Appendix
A
recalls the convergence criteria from
[
5
]
in a form that is suitably tailored to the application required in Section 4.

2.
One-Dimensional Schrödinger Operators

We begin by introducing the following hypothesis which shall be assumed throughout.

Hypothesis 2.1
.

V\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
is real-valued a.e.,
\varphi\in[0,\pi)
, and
R\in\text{{SL}}_{2}{({\mathbb{R}})}
with
R_{1,2}\neq 0
.

In turn, the full-line Schrödinger operator with potential
V
is defined by

(2.1)

Hf\coloneqq-f^{\prime\prime}+Vf,\quad f\in\dom{(H)}\coloneqq\big\{g\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\,\big|\,g,g^{\prime}\in\text{{AC}}_{\operatorname{loc}}{({\mathbb{R}})},\,-g^{\prime\prime}+Vg\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\big\}.

Since the condition
V\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
implies that the differential expression
\tau\coloneqq-d^{2}/dx^{2}+V(x)
is in the limit point case at
\pm\infty
, the operator
H
is self-adjoint in the Hilbert space
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
equipped with the inner product

(2.2)

\langle f,g\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\coloneqq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\overline{f(x)}g(x)\,\mathrm{d}x},\quad f,g\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}).

Moreover, the sesquilinear form uniquely associated to
H
is

(2.3)

\mathfrak{Q}[f,g]\coloneqq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Big[\overline{f^{\prime}(x)}g^{\prime}(x)+\overline{f(x)}V(x)g(x)\Big]\,\mathrm{d}x,\quad f,g\in\dom{(\mathfrak{Q})}\coloneqq H^{1}(\mathbb{R}).

Remark 2.2
.

In the case when
V=0
a.e. on
{\mathbb{R}}
, the corresponding “free” Schrödinger operator and its sesquilinear form are denoted by
H^{{(0)}}
and
\mathfrak{Q}^{{(0)}}
, respectively.
\diamond

The following fixed quantities will play an important role:

(2.4)

M_{V}\coloneqq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}V_{-}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x,\quad N_{R}\coloneqq\frac{|R_{1,1}|+|R_{2,2}|+2}{|R_{1,2}|}.

In fact, standard estimates imply that
H
is lower semibounded with a lower bound expressed in terms of the quantity
M_{V}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

If Hypothesis
2.1
holds, then

(2.5)

\mathfrak{Q}[f,f]\geq-M_{V}(1+M_{V})\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2},\quad f\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}).

In particular, if
V_{-}=0
a.e. on
{\mathbb{R}}
, then
H
is nonnegative.

Proof.

Let
f\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
. Writing
V=V_{+}-V_{-}
, one obtains

(2.6)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{Q}[f,f]

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Big[|f^{\prime}(x)|^{2}+V(x)|f(x)|^{2}\Big]\,\mathrm{d}x\geq\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}V_{-}(x)|f(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

If
M_{V}=0
, then
V_{-}=0
a.e. on
{\mathbb{R}}
and (
2.6
) implies
\mathfrak{Q}[f,f]\geq 0
, which is (
2.5
) for
M_{V}=0
. Thus,
H
is nonnegative in this case.

If
M_{V}>0
, then one bounds the second integral in (
2.6
) from above in terms of
\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}
as follows. By
[
12
, Lemma 9.32]
, for every
\varepsilon>0
and every
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,

(2.7)

\sup_{x\in[n,n+1]}|f(x)|^{2}\leq\varepsilon\int_{n}^{n+1}|f^{\prime}(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\left(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)\int_{n}^{n+1}|f(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\eqqcolon B_{f}(\varepsilon,n),

and the scalars
B_{f}(\varepsilon,n)
satisfy

(2.8)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}B_{f}(\varepsilon,n)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}+\left(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2},\quad\varepsilon>0.

Therefore, by (
2.7
) and (
2.8
),

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}V_{-}(x)|f(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{n}^{n+1}V_{-}(x)|f(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}B_{f}(\varepsilon,n)\int_{n}^{n+1}V_{-}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

(2.9)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon M_{V}\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}+M_{V}\bigg(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\bigg)\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2},\quad\varepsilon>0.

Choosing
\varepsilon=1/M_{V}
in (
2.9
) yields

(2.10)

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}V_{-}(x)|f(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}+M_{V}\left(1+M_{V}\right)\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}.

Finally, applying (
2.10
) in (
2.6
) yields (
2.5
).
∎

Factoring the potential coefficient
V
according to

(2.11)

V=uv,\quad u\coloneqq\sgn{(V)}|V|^{1/2},\quad v\coloneqq|V|^{1/2}\,\text{ a.e.~on ${\mathbb{R}}$},

the following well-known trace ideal properties of the resolvent of
H^{{(0)}}
when multiplied by the factors
u
and
v
hold (see, e.g.,
[
5
, (4.19) and (4.20)]
and
[
9
, (2.49) and (2.50)]
).

Lemma 2.4
.

If Hypothesis
2.1
holds, then

(2.12)

\begin{split}u\big(H^{(0)}-zI_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\big)^{-1},\overline{\big(H^{(0)}-zI_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\big)^{-1}v}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{2}\big(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\big),&\\
\overline{u\big(H^{(0)}-zI_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\big)^{-1}v}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{1}\big(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\big),&\quad z\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus[0,\infty).\end{split}

In addition,

(2.13)

\lim_{z\to-\infty}\Big\|\overline{u\big(H^{(0)}-zI_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\big)^{-1}v}\Big\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{1}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}))}=0.

Remark 2.5
.

Lemma
2.4
with
V
replaced by
|V|
yields

(2.14)

\begin{split}&|V|^{1/2}\big(H^{{(0)}}+I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}\overline{\big(H^{{(0)}}+I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}|V|^{1/2}}\\
&\quad=\overline{|V|^{1/2}\big(H^{{(0)}}+I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1}|V|^{1/2}}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{1}\big(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\big),\end{split}

which implies
|V|^{1/2}\big(H^{{(0)}}+I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{2}\big(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\big)
. Therefore,
|V|
is relatively form compact, hence infinitesimally form bounded, with respect to
H^{{(0)}}
. In particular, the positive and negative parts of
V
are infinitesimally form bounded with respect to
H^{{(0)}}
.
\diamond

For each
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let

(2.15)

V_{\ell}\coloneqq V|_{(-\ell,\ell)}

denote the restriction of
V
to
(-\ell,\ell)
and introduce the finite-interval Schrödinger operator
H_{\ell,\varphi,R}
with coupled boundary conditions at the endpoints of
(-\ell,\ell)
by

(2.16)

\displaystyle H_{\ell,\varphi,R}f\coloneqq-f^{\prime\prime}+V_{\ell}f,

\displaystyle f\in\dom{(H_{\ell,\varphi,R})}\coloneqq\bigg\{g\in L^{2}((-\ell,\ell))\,\bigg|\,g,g^{\prime}\in\text{{AC}}([-\ell,\ell]),\,\begin{pmatrix}g(\ell)\\
g^{\prime}(\ell)\end{pmatrix}=e^{i\varphi}R\begin{pmatrix}g(-\ell)\\
g^{\prime}(-\ell)\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\hskip 284.52756pt-g^{\prime\prime}+V_{\ell}g\in L^{2}((-\ell,\ell))\bigg\}.

The operator
H_{\ell,\varphi,R}
is self-adjoint (see, e.g.,
[
3
, Theorem 2.5]
) in the Hilbert space
L^{2}((-\ell,\ell))
equipped with the inner product

(2.17)

\langle f,g\rangle_{L^{2}((-\ell,\ell))}\coloneqq\int_{-\ell}^{\ell}\overline{f(x)}g(x)\,\mathrm{d}x,\quad f,g\in L^{2}((-\ell,\ell)).

The sesquilinear form uniquely associated to
H_{\ell,\varphi,R}
is (see, e.g.,
[
6
, Section 3.3 and (3.3.143)]
)

(2.18)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{Q}_{\ell,\varphi,R}[f,g]\coloneqq\int_{-\ell}^{\ell}\Big[\overline{f^{\prime}(x)}g^{\prime}(x)+\overline{f(x)}V_{\ell}(x)g(x)\Big]\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\hskip 65.44142pt-\frac{1}{R_{1,2}}\Big[R_{1,1}\overline{f(-\ell)}g(-\ell)-e^{-i\varphi}\overline{f(-\ell)}g(\ell)-e^{i\varphi}\overline{f(\ell)}g(-\ell)+R_{2,2}\overline{f(\ell)}g(\ell)\Big],

\displaystyle f,g\in\dom{(\mathfrak{Q}_{\ell,\varphi,R})}\coloneqq H^{1}((-\ell,\ell)).

Since the differential expression
\tau_{\ell}\coloneqq-d^{2}/dx^{2}+V_{\ell}(x)
is regular on
(-\ell,\ell)
, the self-adjoint operator
H_{\ell,\varphi,R}
is lower semibounded. A careful analysis of the sesquilinear form
\mathfrak{Q}_{\ell,\varphi,R}
shows that
H_{\ell,\varphi,R}
is lower semibounded uniformly with respect to
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Lemma 2.6
.

If Hypothesis
2.1
holds, then for every
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

(2.19)

\mathfrak{Q}_{\ell,\varphi,R}[f,f]\geq-(1+M_{V}+N_{R})\|f\|_{L^{2}((-\ell,\ell))}^{2},\quad f\in H^{1}((-\ell,\ell)).

Proof.

Let
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
f\in H^{1}((-\ell,\ell))
. By (
2.18
),

(2.20)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{Q}_{\ell,\varphi,R}[f,f]

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\int_{-\ell}^{\ell}\Big[|f^{\prime}(x)|^{2}+V_{\ell}(x)|f(x)|^{2}\Big]\,\mathrm{d}x}_{\eqqcolon I_{\ell}[f]}

\displaystyle\quad-\underbrace{\frac{1}{R_{1,2}}\Big[R_{1,1}f(-\ell)\overline{f(-\ell)}-e^{-i\varphi}\overline{f(-\ell)}f(\ell)-e^{i\varphi}\overline{f(\ell)}f(-\ell)+R_{2,2}f(\ell)\overline{f(\ell)}\Big]}_{\eqqcolon J_{\ell,\varphi,R}[f]}.

The integral
I_{\ell}[f]
is estimated from below as follows:

\displaystyle I_{\ell}[f]

\displaystyle=\int_{-\ell}^{\ell}|f^{\prime}(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{-\ell}^{\ell}[V_{+}(x)-V_{-}(x)]|f(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}((-\ell,\ell))}^{2}-\int_{-\ell}^{\ell}V_{-}(x)|f(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

(2.21)

\displaystyle\geq\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}((-\ell,\ell))}^{2}-M_{V}\sup_{x\in[-\ell,\ell]}{|f(x)|^{2}}.

In addition, the boundary terms
J_{\ell,\varphi,R}[f]
satisfy:

\displaystyle J_{\ell,\varphi,R}[f]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{R_{1,2}}\Big[R_{1,1}|f(-\ell)|^{2}+R_{2,2}|f(\ell)|^{2}\Big]-\frac{1}{R_{1,2}}\Big[e^{-i\varphi}\overline{f(-\ell)}f(\ell)+e^{i\varphi}\overline{f(\ell)}f(-\ell)\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|R_{1,2}|}\Big[|R_{1,1}||f(-\ell)|^{2}+|R_{2,2}||f(\ell)|^{2}+2|f(\ell)||f(-\ell)|\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|R_{1,2}|}\Big[|R_{1,1}||f(-\ell)|^{2}+|R_{2,2}||f(\ell)|^{2}+|f(-\ell)|^{2}+|f(\ell)|^{2}\Big]

(2.22)

\displaystyle\leq N_{R}\sup_{x\in[-\ell,\ell]}{|f(x)|^{2}}.

Applying (
2
) and (
2.22
) in (
2.20
), one obtains:

(2.23)

\mathfrak{Q}_{\ell,\varphi,R}[f,f]=I_{\ell}[f]-J_{\ell,\varphi,R}[f]\geq\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}((-\ell,\ell))}^{2}-(M_{V}+N_{R})\sup_{x\in[-\ell,\ell]}{|f(x)|^{2}}.

Note that
\sup_{x\in[-\ell,\ell]}|f(x)|^{2}=\sup_{x\in[n,n+1]}|f(x)|^{2}
for some
n=n(f)\in[-\ell,\ell-1]\cap\mathbb{N}
. By
[
12
, Lemma 9.32]
,

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in[-\ell,\ell]}|f(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in[n,n+1]}|f(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{n}^{n+1}|f^{\prime}(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\left(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)\int_{n}^{n+1}|f(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

(2.24)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}((-\ell,\ell))}^{2}+\left(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)\|f\|_{L^{2}((-\ell,\ell))}^{2},\quad\varepsilon>0.

Finally, taking
\varepsilon=(M_{V}+N_{R})^{-1}>0
in (
2.24
) and applying the resulting estimate in (
2.23
) yields (
2.19
).
Neither
M_{V}
nor
N_{R}
depend on
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
, so (
2.19
) implies that
H_{\ell,\varphi,R}
is lower semibounded uniformly with respect to
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
∎

For each
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the finite-interval Schrödinger operator with Dirichlet boundary conditions at the endpoints of
(-\ell,\ell)
is defined by

(2.25)

\displaystyle H_{\ell,D}f\coloneqq-f^{\prime\prime}+V_{\ell}f,

\displaystyle f\in\dom{(H_{\ell,D})}\coloneqq\big\{g\in L^{2}((-\ell,\ell))\,\big|\,g,g^{\prime}\in\text{{AC}}([-\ell,\ell]),\,g(-\ell)=g(\ell)=0,

\displaystyle\hskip 230.46748pt-g^{\prime\prime}+V_{\ell}g\in L^{2}((-\ell,\ell))\big\}.

\displaystyle\hskip 83.65117pt=H_{0}^{1}((-\ell,\ell)).

3.
Convergence Results for Resolvent Operators

The Hilbert spaces
L^{2}((-\ell,\ell))
vary with
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Therefore, we introduce the following
\ell
-dependent direct sum to move to the fixed
\ell
-independent Hilbert space
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
. If
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
f\in L^{2}((-\ell,\ell))
, and
g\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}\setminus(-\ell,\ell))
, then we define
f\oplus_{\ell}g
by

(3.1)

(f\oplus_{\ell}g)(x)\coloneqq\begin{cases}f(x)&\text{for a.e.~$x\in(-\ell,\ell)$},\\
g(x)&\text{for a.e.~$x\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus(-\ell,\ell)$}.\end{cases}

By additivity of the Lebesgue integral,
f\oplus_{\ell}g\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
and

(3.2)

\|f\oplus_{\ell}g\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}=\|f\|_{L^{2}((-\ell,\ell))}^{2}+\|g\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}\setminus(-\ell,\ell))}^{2}.

Conversely, if
h\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
, then
h
can be expressed as

(3.3)

h=f\oplus_{\ell}g,\,\text{ where $f=h|_{(-\ell,\ell)}$ and $g=h|_{{\mathbb{R}}\setminus(-\ell,\ell)}$}.

In this way, for each
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

(3.4)

L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})=L^{2}((-\ell,\ell))\oplus_{\ell}L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}\setminus(-\ell,\ell)).

By additivity of the Lebesgue integral if
h_{1},h_{2}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
with

(3.5)

h_{j}=f_{j}\oplus_{\ell}g_{j},\quad f_{j}\in L^{2}((-\ell,\ell)),\quad g_{j}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}\setminus(-\ell,\ell)),\quad j\in\{1,2\},

then

(3.6)

\langle h_{1},h_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}=\langle f_{1},f_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}((-\ell,\ell))}+\langle g_{1},g_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}\setminus(-\ell,\ell))}.

Finally, if

(3.7)

S\colon\dom{(S)}\subseteq L^{2}((-\ell,\ell))\to L^{2}((-\ell,\ell))

and

(3.8)

T\colon\dom{(T)}\subseteq L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}\setminus(-\ell,\ell))\to L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}\setminus(-\ell,\ell))

are linear operators, then their direct sum
S\oplus_{\ell}T
with respect to the decomposition (
3.4
) is defined in the standard way by

(3.9)

(S\oplus_{\ell}T)h\coloneqq(Sf)\oplus_{\ell}(Tg),\quad h=f\oplus_{\ell}g\in\dom{(S\oplus_{\ell}T)}\coloneqq\dom{(S)}\oplus_{\ell}\dom{(T)}.

Under this direct sum formalism, it is known that the sequence
\left\{H_{\ell,D}^{(0)}\oplus_{\ell}0\right\}_{\ell=1}^{\infty}
converges to the free Schrödinger operator
H^{{(0)}}
in the strong resolvent sense as
\ell\to\infty
.

Lemma 3.1

(Lemma 3.1 in
[
4
]
)
.

2.1
holds, then for each fixed
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus[0,\infty)
,

(3.10)

\stlim_{\ell\to\infty}{\left(\left[H_{\ell,D}^{(0)}\oplus_{\ell}0\right]-zI_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\right)^{-1}}=\big(H^{(0)}-zI_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\big)^{-1}.

In particular, the sequence
\left\{H_{\ell,D}^{(0)}\oplus_{\ell}0\right\}_{\ell=1}^{\infty}
converges to
H^{(0)}
in the strong resolvent sense.

One can apply Theorem
2.8
to establish an analogue of Lemma
3.1
for the operators

. Our proof employs the following elementary abstract fact, the proof of which is an exercise, for computing the operator or trace ideal norm of a rank one operator.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
denote a Hilbert space with inner product
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}
. If
\psi,\phi\in{\mathcal{H}}
and the rank one operator
A\colon{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{H}}
is given by

, then for each

,

(3.11)

\|A\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{p}({\mathcal{H}})}=\|\psi\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}\|\phi\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}=\|A\|_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}.

The analogue of Lemma
3.1
for the operators
H_{\ell,\varphi,R}^{{(0)}}
is then stated as follows:

Lemma 3.3
.

If Hypothesis
2.1
holds and

(3.12)

\stlim_{\ell\to\infty}{\left(\left[H_{\ell,\varphi,R}^{(0)}\oplus_{\ell}0\right]-zI_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\right)^{-1}}=\big(H^{(0)}-zI_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\big)^{-1}.

In particular, the sequence
\left\{H_{\ell,\varphi,R}^{(0)}\oplus_{\ell}0\right\}_{\ell=1}^{\infty}
converges to
H^{(0)}
in the strong resolvent sense.

Proof.

It suffices to show

(3.13)

\stlim_{\ell\to\infty}{\left(\left[H_{\ell,\varphi,R}^{(0)}\oplus_{\ell}0\right]+k^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\right)^{-1}}=\big(H^{(0)}+k^{2}I_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\big)^{-1}

for some
k>k_{R,\max}
, as the statement in (
3.12
) then follows from an application of the first resolvent identity. Let
k>k_{R,\max}
, so that
-k^{2}\in\rho\big(H_{\ell,\varphi,R}^{{(0)}}\big)\cap\rho\big(H_{\ell,D}^{{(0)}}\big)\cap\rho\big(H^{{(0)}}\big)
for all
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
. By Theorem
2.8
,

(3.14)

\begin{split}\Big(\Big[H_{\ell,\varphi,R}^{(0)}\oplus_{\ell}0\Big]+k^{2}I_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\Big)^{-1}=\Big(\Big[H_{\ell,D}^{(0)}\oplus_{\ell}0\Big]+k^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\Big)^{-1}+\Big[P_{\ell,\varphi,R}^{(0)}(-k^{2})\oplus_{\ell}0\Big],&\\
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}.&\end{split}

In light of (
3.10
) and (
3.14
), in order to show (
3.13
), it suffices to prove

(3.15)

\stlim_{\ell\to\infty}{\Big[P_{\ell,\varphi,R}^{(0)}(-k^{2})\oplus_{\ell}0\Big]}=0.

To this end, note that the sequence of operators on the left-hand side in (
3.15
) is uniformly bounded with respect to
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Indeed, by the triangle inequality and (
2.49
),

\displaystyle\Big\|P_{\ell,\varphi,R}^{(0)}(-k^{2})\oplus_{\ell}0\Big\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}

\displaystyle\quad=\Big\|P_{\ell,\varphi,R}^{(0)}(-k^{2})\Big\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}((-\ell,\ell)))}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\sum_{m,n=1}^{2}{\Big|\Big[K_{\ell,\varphi,R}(-k^{2})^{-1}\Big]_{m,n}\Big|\ \big\|\big\langle\psi_{\ell,m}^{(0)}(-k^{2},\,\cdot\,),\,\cdot\,\big\rangle\psi_{\ell,n}^{(0)}(-k^{2},\,\cdot\,)\big\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}((-\ell,\ell)))}}

\displaystyle\,\;\underset{\ell\to\infty}{\leq}O(1)\sum_{m,n=1}^{2}{\big\|\big\langle\psi_{\ell,m}^{(0)}(-k^{2},\,\cdot\,),\,\cdot\,\big\rangle\psi_{\ell,n}^{(0)}(-k^{2},\,\cdot\,)\big\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}((-\ell,\ell)))}}

(3.16)

\displaystyle\,\;\underset{\ell\to\infty}{=}O(1)\sum_{m,n=1}^{2}{\big\|\psi_{\ell,m}^{(0)}(-k^{2},\,\cdot\,)\big\|_{L^{2}((-\ell,\ell))}\big\|\psi_{\ell,n}^{(0)}(-k^{2},\,\cdot\,)\big\|_{L^{2}((-\ell,\ell))}},

where the final equality follows from Proposition
3.2
. One infers that

\displaystyle\big\|\psi_{\ell,m}^{(0)}(-k^{2},\,\cdot\,)\big\|_{L^{2}((-\ell,\ell))}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\int_{-\ell}^{\ell}{\bigg[\frac{\cosh{(kx)}}{\cosh{(k\ell)}}\pm\frac{\sinh{(kx)}}{\sinh{(k\ell)}}\bigg]^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x}

(3.17)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\int_{-\ell}^{\ell}\bigg[\frac{\cosh^{2}{(kx)}}{\cosh^{2}{(k\ell)}}+\frac{\sinh^{2}{(kx)}}{\sinh^{2}{(k\ell)}}\bigg]\,\mathrm{d}x,\quad m\in\{1,2\},\,\ell\in{\mathbb{N}},

where the third term that arises from the expansion of the square in the first integral vanishes as it is an odd function being integrated over a finite symmetric interval. The last integral in (
3.17
) is elementary and one obtains:

(3.18)

\begin{split}\big\|\psi_{\ell,m}^{(0)}(-k^{2},\,\cdot\,)\big\|_{L^{2}((-\ell,\ell))}^{2}=\frac{\tanh{(k\ell)}+\coth{(k\ell)}}{4k}+\frac{\ell}{4}\big[\sech^{2}{(k\ell)}-\csch^{2}{(k\ell)}\big],&\\
m\in\{1,2\},\,\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}.&\end{split}

In particular, (
3.18
) implies

(3.19)

\big\|\psi_{\ell,m}^{(0)}(-k^{2},\,\cdot\,)\big\|_{L^{2}((-\ell,\ell))}\underset{\ell\to\infty}{=}O(1),\quad m\in\{1,2\}.

Combining (
3.16
) and (
3.19
), one obtains

(3.20)

\Big\|P_{\ell,\varphi,R}^{(0)}(-k^{2})\oplus_{\ell}0\Big\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}\underset{\ell\to\infty}{=}O(1),

so that
\Big\{P_{\ell,\varphi,R}^{{(0)}}(-k^{2})\oplus_{\ell}0\Big\}_{\ell=1}^{\infty}
is a bounded sequence in
{\mathcal{B}}\big(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\big)
. Therefore, by
[
11
, Exercise 4.28]
, in order to show (
3.15
), it suffices to prove

(3.21)

\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\Big[P_{\ell,\varphi,R}^{{(0)}}(-k^{2})\oplus_{\ell}0\Big]f=0,\quad f\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})\cap L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}),

since
L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})\cap L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
is dense in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
. If
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, then one obtains

\displaystyle\big|\chi_{(-\ell,\ell)}(x)\psi_{\ell,m}^{{(0)}}(-k^{2},x)f(x)\big|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\bigg|\chi_{(-\ell,\ell)}(x)\bigg[\frac{\cosh{(kx)}}{\cosh{(k\ell)}}+(-1)^{m-1}\frac{\sinh{(kx)}}{\sinh{(k\ell)}}\bigg]f(x)\bigg|

(3.22)

\displaystyle\leq|f(x)|\,\text{ for a.e.~$x\in{\mathbb{R}}$},\quad\ell\in{\mathbb{N}},\,m\in\{1,2\}.

Since
\lim_{\ell\to\infty}{\psi_{\ell,m}^{{(0)}}(-k^{2},x)}=0
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
, it follows that

(3.23)

\lim_{\ell\to\infty}{\chi_{(-\ell,\ell)}(x)\psi_{\ell,m}^{{(0)}}(-k^{2},x)f(x)}=0\,\text{ for a.e.~$x\in{\mathbb{R}}$}.

Hence, by the dominated convergence theorem,

(3.24)

\begin{split}\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\big\langle\psi_{\ell,m}^{{(0)}}(-k^{2},\,\cdot\,),f\big|_{(-\ell,\ell)}\big\rangle_{L^{2}((-\ell,\ell))}=\lim_{\ell\to\infty}{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\chi_{(-\ell,\ell)}\psi_{\ell,m}^{{(0)}}(-k^{2},x)f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x}}=0,&\\
m\in\{1,2\}.&\end{split}

Finally, applying (
2.31
), (
2.49
), (
3.19
), and (
3.24
), one obtains

(3.25)

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big[P_{\ell,\varphi,R}^{{(0)}}(-k^{2})\oplus_{\ell}0\Big]f\,\Big\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}

\displaystyle\quad=\Big\|P_{\ell,\varphi,R}^{{(0)}}(-k^{2})f\big|_{(-\ell,\ell)}\,\Big\|_{L^{2}((-\ell,\ell))}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\sum_{m,n=1}^{2}\Big|\Big[K_{\ell,\varphi,R}^{{(0)}}(-k^{2})^{-1}\Big]_{m,n}\Big|\Big|\big\langle\psi_{\ell,n}^{{(0)}}(-k^{2},\,\cdot\,),f\big|_{(-\ell,\ell)}\big\rangle_{L^{2}((-\ell,\ell))}\Big|\big\|\psi_{\ell,m}^{{(0)}}(-k^{2},\,\cdot\,)\big\|_{L^{2}((-\ell,\ell))}

\displaystyle\,\;\underset{\ell\to\infty}{=}o(1),

and (
3.21
) follows.
∎

Suppose that

(A.14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\langle V_{2}f,V_{1}g\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}=\langle V_{1}f,V_{2}g\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}},\quad f,g\in\dom{(V_{1})}\cap\dom{(V_{2})},\\
&\langle V_{2,\ell}f,V_{1,\ell}g\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}=\langle V_{1,\ell}f,V_{2,\ell}g\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}},\quad f,g\in\dom{(V_{1,\ell})}\cap\dom{(V_{2,\ell})},\quad\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}.\end{split}

(viii)
Decomposing
V,V_{\ell}
,
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
, into their positive and negative parts,

(A.15)

V_{\pm}=[|V|\pm V]/2,\quad V_{\ell,\pm}=[|V_{\ell}|\pm V_{\ell}]/2,\quad\ell\in{\mathbb{N}},

V_{\pm}
are assumed to be infinitesimally form bounded with respect to
T^{(0)}
,
and for each
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
V_{\ell,\pm}
are assumed to be infinitesimally form bounded
with respect to
T_{\ell}^{(0)}
.

(ix)
Suppose that
T
and
T_{\ell}
,
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
are the quadratic forms sums of
T^{{(0)}}
and
T_{\ell}^{{(0)}}
with
V_{1}^{*}V_{2}
and
V_{1,\ell}^{*}V_{2,\ell}
, respectively; that is,

(A.16)

T=T^{{(0)}}+_{\mathfrak{q}}V_{1}^{*}V_{2},\quad T_{\ell}=T_{\ell}^{{(0)}}+_{\mathfrak{q}}V_{1,\ell}^{*}V_{2,\ell},\quad\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Assuming Hypothesis
A.1
, the pairs
\big(T,T^{(0)}\big)
and
\big(T_{\ell},T_{\ell}^{(0)}\big)
,
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
, are resolvent comparable in the sense that

(A.17)

\big[\big(T-zI_{{\mathcal{H}}}\big)^{-1}-\big(T^{(0)}-zI_{{\mathcal{H}}}\big)^{-1}\big]\in{\mathcal{B}}_{1}({\mathcal{H}}),\quad z\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathbb{R}},

and

(A.18)

\big[\big(T_{\ell}-zI_{{\mathcal{H}}}\big)^{-1}-\big(T_{\ell}^{(0)}-zI_{{\mathcal{H}}}\big)^{-1}\big]\in{\mathcal{B}}_{1}({\mathcal{H}}),\quad z\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathbb{R}},\,\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Thus, Hypothesis
A.1
guarantees the existence of real-valued spectral shift functions
\xi\big(\,\cdot\,;T,T^{(0)}\big)
and
\xi\big(\,\cdot\,;T_{\ell},T^{(0)}_{\ell}\big)
,
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
, which satisfy

(A.19)

\begin{split}\mbox{tr}_{{\mathcal{H}}}\Big(\big(T-zI_{{\mathcal{H}}}\big)^{-1}-\big(T^{(0)}-zI_{{\mathcal{H}}}\big)^{-1}\Big)=-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\xi\big(\lambda;T,T^{(0)}\big)}{(\lambda-z)^{2}}\,d\lambda,&\\
\quad z\in\rho(T)\cap\rho\big(T^{(0)}\big),&\end{split}

and

(A.20)

\begin{split}\mbox{tr}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\ell}}\Big(\big(T_{\ell}-zI_{{\mathcal{H}}}\big)^{-1}-\big(T^{(0)}_{\ell}-zI_{{\mathcal{H}}}\big)^{-1}\Big)=-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\xi\big(\lambda;T_{\ell},T^{(0)}_{\ell}\big)}{(\lambda-z)^{2}}\,d\lambda,&\\
z\in\rho(T_{\ell})\cap\rho\big(T^{(0)}_{\ell}\big),\,\ell\in{\mathbb{N}},&\end{split}

and are uniquely determined almost everywhere by the conditions

(A.21)

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{|\xi\big(\lambda;T,T^{(0)}\big)|}{1+\lambda^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\xi\big(\lambda;T,T^{(0)}\big)=0,\quad\lambda<\min\big[\sigma(T)\cup\sigma\big(T^{(0)}\big)\big],

and for each
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

(A.22)

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{|\xi\big(\lambda;T_{\ell},T_{\ell}^{(0)}\big)|}{1+\lambda^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\xi\big(\lambda;T_{\ell},T_{\ell}^{(0)}\big)=0,\quad\lambda<\min\big[\sigma\big(T_{\ell}\big)\cup\sigma\big(T_{\ell}^{(0)}\big)\big].

Moreover, Krein’s trace formulæ hold:

(A.23)

\displaystyle\mbox{tr}_{{\mathcal{H}}}\big(f(T)-f\big(T^{(0)}\big)\big)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f^{\prime}(\lambda)\,\xi\big(\lambda;T,T^{(0)}\big)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda,

(A.24)

\displaystyle\mbox{tr}_{{\mathcal{H}}}\big(f(T_{\ell})-f\big(T^{(0)}_{\ell}\big)\big)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f^{\prime}(\lambda)\,\xi\big(\lambda;T_{\ell},T^{(0)}_{\ell}\big)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda,\quad\ell\in{\mathbb{N}},

where
\mathfrak{F}({\mathbb{R}})
denotes the set of all function
f\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
with two locally bounded derivatives satisfying

(A.25)

\big(\lambda^{2}f^{\prime}(\lambda)\big)^{\prime}=O\big(|\lambda|^{-1-\varepsilon}\big),\quad|\lambda|\to\infty
for some
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(f)>0,

with

(A.26)

\lim_{|\lambda|\to\infty}f(\lambda)=C\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{|\lambda|\to\infty}\lambda^{2}f^{\prime}(\lambda)=D,

for some constants
C=C(f),D=D(f)\in{\mathbb{C}}
. In particular,

(A.27)

(\lambda-z)^{-n}\in\mathfrak{F}({\mathbb{R}})
,
z\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathbb{R}}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

and in this case, the trace formula for
\big(T,T^{{(0)}}\big)
reads

(A.28)

\mbox{tr}_{{\mathcal{H}}}\big((T-zI_{{\mathcal{H}}})^{-n}-\big(T^{{(0)}}-zI_{{\mathcal{H}}}\big)^{-n}\big)=-n\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{\xi\big(\lambda;T,T^{{(0)}}\big)}{(\lambda-z)^{n+1}}\,d\lambda,\quad z\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathbb{R}},\,n\in{\mathbb{N}},

and an analogous formula holds for
\big(T_{\ell},T_{\ell}^{{(0)}}\big)
,
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
. We refer to
[
13
, Chapter 8]
for additional information related to spectral shift functions.

Under the assumptions in Hypothesis
A.1
, the following convergence results hold for the sequence of spectral shift functions
\big\{\xi\big(\,\cdot\,;T_{\ell},T_{\ell}^{(0)}\big)\big\}_{\ell=1}^{\infty}
.

Theorem A.2

(Theorem 3.13 in
[
5
]
)
.

Assume Hypothesis
A.1
. Then

(A.29)

\lim_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\xi\big(\lambda;T_{\ell},T_{\ell}^{(0)}\big)}{1+\lambda^{2}}\,f(\lambda)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\xi\big(\lambda;T,T^{(0)}\big)}{1+\lambda^{2}}\,f(\lambda)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda,\quad f\in C_{b}({\mathbb{R}}).

The factor
(1+\lambda^{2})^{-1}
is essential in (
A.29
), as the integrals need not exist without it. One consequence of Theorem
A.2
is that the sequence
\big\{\xi\big(\,\cdot\,;T_{\ell},T_{\ell}^{{(0)}}\big)\big\}_{\ell=1}^{\infty}
converges
vaguley
to
\xi\big(\,\cdot\,;T,T^{{(0)}}\big)
.

Corollary A.3

(Corollary 3.11 in
[
5
]
)
.

Assume Hypothesis
A.1
. Then

(A.30)

\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\xi\big(\lambda;T_{\ell},T_{\ell}^{(0)}\big)\,g(\lambda)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\xi\big(\lambda;T,T^{(0)}\big)\,g(\lambda)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\quad g\in C_{c}({\mathbb{R}}).

Finally, the continuity assumption in Theorem
A.2
may be slightly relaxed as follows.

Corollary A.4

(Corollary 3.14 in
[
5
]
)
.

Assume Hypothesis
A.1
. Then the convergence in (
A.29
) holds for any bounded Borel measurable function that is continuous almost everywhere with respect to Lebesgue measure on
{\mathbb{R}}
. In particular,

(A.31)

\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\int_{S}\frac{\xi\big(\lambda;T_{\ell},T_{\ell}^{(0)}\big)}{1+\lambda^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda=\int_{S}\frac{\xi\big(\lambda;T,T^{(0)}\big)}{1+\lambda^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda

holds for any set
S\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}
that is boundaryless with respect to Lebesgue
measure
(
i.e., any set
S\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}
for which the boundary of
S
has Lebesgue measure
equal to zero
)
.

Corollary A.5

(Corollary 3.15 in
[
5
]
)
.

Assume Hypothesis
A.1
. If
g
is a bounded Borel measurable function that is compactly supported and Lebesgue almost everywhere continuous on
{\mathbb{R}}
, then

(A.32)

\lim_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\xi\big(\lambda;T_{\ell},T_{\ell}^{(0)}\big)\,g(\lambda)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\xi\big(\lambda;T,T^{(0)}\big)\,g(\lambda)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda.

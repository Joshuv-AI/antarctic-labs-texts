Non-vanishing of twists of G ​ L 4 ​ ( 𝔸 ℚ ) GL_{4}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}) L L -functions
Maksym Radziwiłł, Liyang Yang

Abstract
. Let π \pi be a unitary cuspidal automorphic representation of GL 4 ​ ( 𝔸 ℚ ) \mathrm{GL}_{4}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}) . Let f ≥ 1 f\geq 1 be given. We show that there exists infinitely many primitive even (resp. odd) Dirichlet characters χ \chi with conductor coprime to f f such that L ⁡ ( s , π ⊗ χ ) L(s,\pi\otimes\chi) is non-vanishing at the central point. Our result has applications for the construction of p p -adic L L -functions for GSp 4 \text{GSp}_{4} following Loeffler-Pilloni-Skinner-Zerbes, the Bloch-Kato conjecture and the Birch-Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture for abelian surfaces following Loeffler-Zerbes, strong multiplicity one results for paramodular cuspidal representations of GSp 4 ​ ( 𝔸 ℚ ) \text{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}) and the rationality of the central values of GSp 4 ​ ( 𝔸 ℚ ) \text{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}) L L -functions in the remaining non-regular weight case.

In contrast algebraic methods are able to access forms of higher rank, but only when the form is of cohomological type and provided that there exists critical values of the

0.1.
Applications

Let
\Pi

In

Similarly a case of the Bloch-Kato conjecture is established in

Finally, in conjunction with

0.2.
Quantitative non-vanishing

Theorem
1
follows from a stronger result computing the mean-value

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\in\mathcal{Q}\\
\chi\pmod{q}\\
\text{primitive}\end{subarray}}\chi(\pm 1)L(\tfrac{1}{2},\pi\otimes\chi)

for a set of moduli

co-prime to all primes smaller than

In order to state the result we need to introduce the notion of a

. We will say that a sequence

if for every

, and

for any given

. It is known that the coefficients

of the
L

are Siegel-Walfisz for some small

following the work of Brumley
[
Bru06
]
. Moreover if one allows for ineffective results (i.e results in which the implicit constant in the

cannot be specified) then it follows from the very recent work
[
TH23
]
that
\lambda_{\pi}
is Siegel-Walfisz for any

. Throughout Brumley’s effective result is all that we will ever need. In particular, with some work it is possible to exhibit a completely explicit function

such that for any given
\pi
there exists a primitive character
\chi
of conductor

where

is the conductor of

and such that

We are now ready to state our main technical result. We will use the notation

to mean that

. This notation will be never used to mean an asymptotic relation.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\pi
be a unitary cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{4}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
.
Let
\kappa>0
be an exponent such that
\lambda_{\pi}
is Siegel-Walfisz of level
\kappa
.
Let
\mathcal{Q}_{\delta,\nu}\subset[Q/16,16Q]
be the set of integers that can be written as
p_{1}p_{2}m
with

p_{1}\sim P_{1}:=(\log Q)^{\kappa\nu}
,
p_{2}\sim P_{2}:=(\log Q)^{10000}
and
m\sim Q/(P_{1}P_{2})
square-free with at most
\delta\log\log Q+10

distinct prime factors, all larger than
(\log Q)^{20000}
. Then, there exists a choice of
0<\delta<\nu<1

with
\kappa\nu<1000
such that,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\in\mathcal{Q}_{\delta,\nu}\\
\chi\pmod{q}\\
\text{primitive}\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle\chi(\pm 1)L(\tfrac{1}{2},\pi\otimes\chi)

\displaystyle=Q^{2}(\log Q)^{-1+\delta+\delta\log\frac{1}{\delta}}\cdot\Big(\mathbf{1}_{\pm 1=1}\cdot(\log Q)^{o(1)}+O\Big((\log Q)^{-1000\delta}\Big)\Big)

as
Q\rightarrow\infty
.

The
(\log Q)^{o(1)}
can be replaced by a more precise
(\log\log Q)^{-3/2-\delta+o(1)}
if needed.
It is also possible to specify an admissible
\kappa
,
\delta
and
\nu
completely explicitly. We find that doing so makes the proof harder to parse. The theorem gives immediately the infinitude of even or odd characters
\chi
with
L(\tfrac{1}{2},\pi\otimes\chi)\neq 0
, using the observation that,

\mathbf{1}_{\chi(-1)=\pm 1}=\frac{1\pm\chi(-1)}{2}.

Moreover for any given
f\geq 1
once
Q
is choosen large enough all the exhibited characters have modulus coprime to
f
.

A nearly immediate consequence of the above Theorem and the large sieve is a quantitative result asserting that for any given
f\geq 1
there are at least

\frac{Q^{2}}{\log^{4}Q}

even (resp. odd) primitive characters
\chi\pmod{q}
with
q\in[Q/16,16Q]
and
(q,f)=1
for which
L(\tfrac{1}{2},\pi\otimes\chi)\neq 0
.
We record two final observations. We can require the set of moduli to be composed of only three primes under additional assumptions on the form
\pi
, e.g forms of cohomological type or forms arising from lifting of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
forms, for example
\operatorname{Sym}^{3}\phi
with
\phi
being a cusp form on
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}).
We discuss some of these modifications in the remarks in the next section. While we haven’t checked all the details it seems plausible that Theorem
2
holds in general with a set of moduli
q
composed of
\leq C
distinct prime factors with
C
an absolute constant. We have not considered possible extensions of our result to the case of number fields and plan to address this in later papers.

N\leq Q^{2}(\log Q)^{-10^{9}}

Appealing to Proposition
3
we find,

\sum_{N\leq Q^{2}(\log Q)^{-10^{9}}}\mathcal{F}_{N}=\mathbf{1}_{\pm 1=1}Q^{2}(\log Q)^{-1+\delta+\delta\log\frac{1}{\delta}+o(1)}+O(Q^{2}(\log Q)^{-100}).

as
Q\rightarrow\infty
.

1.2.
\mathcal{F}_{N}
in the range with
N_{\nu,\varepsilon}^{+}\geq N\geq Q^{2}(\log Q)^{-10^{9}}

We first note that there exists a small
0<\kappa<1
for which
\lambda_{\pi}
is Siegel-Walfisz.
This follows for example from Brumley’s result
[
Bru06
]
(see also
[
KT22
]
where the details of the deduction are worked out or Lemma
17
later in this paper).
There are
\log\log Q
dy-adic ranges to consider in this case.
According to Proposition
2
their total contribution is,

\displaystyle\ll e^{O(1/\delta)}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{N_{\nu,\varepsilon}^{+}}Q}{(\log Q)^{3/2}}(\log Q)^{4\nu+\delta\log\frac{1}{\delta}+10\delta+2\varepsilon}+C(\nu,\kappa)Q^{2}(\log Q)^{-10}

\displaystyle\ll e^{O(1/\delta)}\cdot Q^{2}(\log Q)^{-1-100\nu+\varepsilon+\delta\log\frac{1}{\delta}+10\delta+2\varepsilon}+C(\nu,\kappa)Q^{2}(\log Q)^{-10}

with
C(\nu,\kappa)
a constant depending only on
\nu
and
\kappa
. The above is negligible compared to the main term

Q^{2}(\log Q)^{-1+\delta+\delta\log\frac{1}{\delta}+o(1)}
for any fixed
0<\varepsilon<\delta<\nu<1/1000
.

1.3.
\mathcal{D}_{M}
in the range
M\leq Q^{2}(\log Q)^{-10^{9}}

In this range we interpret
\mathcal{Q}_{\delta,\nu}
as a product of a set
\mathcal{R}
consisting of a product of two primes
p_{1}p_{2}
with
p_{1}\sim P_{1}:=(\log Q)^{\kappa\nu}
,
p_{2}\sim P_{2}:=(\log Q)^{10000}
and
another set
\mathcal{S}
consisting of
m\sim Q/(P_{1}P_{2})
an integer with at most
\delta\log\log Q+10
prime factors, all larger than
(\log Q)^{20000}
. Let
M_{\text{max}}:=Q^{2}(\log Q)^{-10^{9}}
.
Applying Proposition
1
we get a bound,

\displaystyle(\log Q)^{C\delta+2\varepsilon}\cdot\Big(\sqrt{M_{\text{max}}}Q\sqrt{R}+Q^{2}R^{-1/4}\Big)

If
\kappa\nu<10^{3}
then
(\log Q)^{10000}\leq R\leq(\log Q)^{20000}
. If in addition we assume that
C\delta<10^{3}
and
0<\varepsilon<1
, then since
M_{\text{max}}\leq Q^{2}(\log Q)^{-10^{9}}
the above is

\ll\frac{Q^{2}}{(\log Q)^{10}}.

We record again the conditions
\kappa\nu<10^{3}
,
C\delta<10^{3}
and
0<\varepsilon<1
.

1.4.
\mathcal{D}_{M}
in the range
M_{\nu,\varepsilon}^{+}\geq M\geq Q^{2}(\log Q)^{-10^{9}}

In this range we interpret
\mathcal{Q}_{\delta,\nu}
as a product of a set
\mathcal{R}
consisting of one prime
p_{1}\sim P_{1}:=(\log Q)^{\kappa\nu}
and
another set
\mathcal{S}
consisting of
p_{2}m
with
p_{2}\sim P_{2}:=(\log Q)^{10000}
and
m\sim Q/(P_{1}P_{2})
an integer with at most
\delta\log\log Q+10
prime factors, all larger than
(\log Q)^{20000}
. Applying Proposition
1
we get a bound,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{M}

\displaystyle\ll(\log Q)^{C\delta+2\varepsilon}\cdot\Big(\sqrt{MR}Q(\log Q)^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\delta}{2}+\frac{\delta}{2}\log\frac{1}{\delta}}+Q^{2}(\log Q)^{-1+\delta+\delta\log\frac{1}{\delta}}R^{-1/4}\Big)

\displaystyle\ll(\log Q)^{C\delta+2\varepsilon}\cdot\Big(Q^{2}(\log Q)^{-\frac{1}{2}+100\nu+\kappa\nu/2-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\delta}{2}+\frac{\delta}{2}\log\frac{1}{\delta}}+Q^{2}(\log Q)^{-1+\delta+\delta\log\frac{1}{\delta}-\kappa\nu/4}\Big)

Notice that we could have written down a more careful bound without the loss of
(\log Q)^{C\delta+2\varepsilon}
in the first term. It however makes no difference.
Summing all these contributions only adds a factor of
\log\log Q
which is negligible for our purposes.
We therefore require that,

(2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}C\delta+2\varepsilon-1+100\nu+\frac{\kappa\nu}{2}+\frac{\delta}{2}+\frac{\delta}{2}\log\frac{1}{\delta}&<-1+\delta\log\frac{1}{\delta}-10^{9}\cdot\delta\\
C\delta+2\varepsilon-1+\delta+\delta\log\frac{1}{\delta}-\frac{\kappa\nu}{4}&<-1+\delta\log\frac{1}{\delta}-10^{9}\cdot\delta\end{cases}

Finally we have the conditions
0<C\delta<10^{3}
,
\kappa\nu<10^{3}
,
0<\varepsilon<1
and
0<\varepsilon<\delta<\nu<1/1000
carrying over from the previous ranges. For convenience we rewrite (
2
) as

\displaystyle\begin{cases}2\varepsilon+(100+\kappa/2)\nu+(C+1/2+10^{9})\delta&<\frac{\delta}{2}\log\frac{1}{\delta}\\
2\varepsilon+(1+10^{9}+C)\delta&<\frac{\kappa}{4}\cdot\nu\end{cases}

If the conditions in (
2
) are met we obtain,

\sum_{Q^{2}(\log Q)^{-10^{9}}\leq M\leq M_{\nu,\varepsilon}^{+}}\mathcal{D}_{M}\ll(\log\log Q)\cdot Q^{2}(\log Q)^{-1+\delta+\delta\log\frac{1}{\delta}-10^{3}\cdot\delta}

which is entirely acceptable.
A choice of parameters that satisfies all the conditions is not difficult to find, for example one can pick

\varepsilon=\delta\ ,\ \nu=\delta\sqrt{\log\frac{1}{\delta}}

and pick
\delta>0
sufficiently small to make all the inequalities true.

Notice incidentally that the limiting case
\delta=0
barely fails to work (ignoring the factors
e^{C/\delta}
which are mere technicalities). Note also that
\delta
shrinks when either
\kappa
shrinks or
C
grows, as expected.
∎

Remark 2
.

Even though the above proof barely fails to work in the limiting case
\delta=0
it can be made to work in the case
\delta=0
under the additional assumption that there exists an exponent
\eta>0
such that,

(3)

\sum_{p\leq x}|\lambda_{\pi}(p)|^{2+\eta}\ll\frac{x}{\log x},

since this would provide a decisive (for the case
\delta=0
) improvement in Proposition
2
. In particular under the assumption (
3
) one could take
\mathcal{Q}
to consist of a product of three primes. In an earlier version of the manuscript we operated under the assumption of (
3
) until we realized that integers with
\leq\delta\log\log Q+20
prime factors can be used successfully thanks to the fact that
\delta\log\frac{1}{\delta}
is larger than
C\delta
(any any fixed
C>10
) for
small

\delta>0
. Thus the density of such integers grows sufficiently quicker (but only for small
\delta
!) to allow us to overcome the loss from Deligne’s bound.

2.
Complete character sums

Let
\pi
be a unitary cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{4}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
. Denote by
\varepsilon(\pi,\chi)
the root number of the
L
-function
L(s,\pi\otimes\chi)
. Throughout this section let

\mathcal{E}_{\pi}(m;q):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\pmod{q}\\
\text{primitive}\end{subarray}}\varepsilon(\pi,\chi)\overline{\chi}(m)

Notice that
\mathcal{E}_{\pi}(m;q)
is
q
-periodic in
m
.
We use the notation
\mathcal{E}
to signal that
\mathcal{E}
is some sort of transform of the root number

\varepsilon(\pi,\chi)
. The bulk of this section is spent analyzing
\mathcal{E}
and various complete exponential sums
arising from
\mathcal{E}
.

From
[
Luo05
]
we know that, for
(q,N_{\pi})=1
where
N_{\pi}
is the conductor of
\pi,

\varepsilon(\pi,\chi)=c_{\pi}w_{\pi}(q)\chi(N_{\pi})\cdot\frac{\tau(\chi)^{4}}{q^{2}}\ ,\ \tau(\chi):=\sum_{x\pmod{q}}\chi(x)e\Big(\frac{x}{q}\Big),

where
|c_{\pi}|=1
,
w_{\pi}(q)
is the central unitary character of
\pi
,
N_{\pi}
is the conductor of
\pi
and
\tau(\chi)
denotes the Gauss sum associated to
\chi
.
Therefore, for
(m,q)=1
,

(4)

\frac{1}{\sqrt{q}}\cdot\mathcal{E}_{\pi}(m;q)=c_{\pi}w_{\pi}(q)\mathcal{T}_{4}(N_{\pi}\overline{m};q)

where

\mathcal{T}_{k}(\ell;q):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{q}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\pmod{q}\\
\text{primitive}\end{subarray}}\frac{\tau(\chi)^{k}}{q^{k/2}}\chi(\ell).

For
(m,q)\neq 1
even though the inverse
\overline{m}
is undefined we declare that

\mathcal{T}_{4}(N_{\pi}\overline{m};q):=0

so that (
4
) holds for all
m
.
Notice that for
k
even,
\mathcal{T}_{k}(\ell;q)
is real-valued because

\overline{\tau(\chi)^{k}\chi(l)}=\tau(\overline{\chi})^{k}\overline{\chi}(l)

since
\chi(-1)^{k}=1
when
k\in 2\mathbb{Z}
.
To a first approximation
\mathcal{T}_{k}(\ell;q)
is a hyper-Kloosterman sum, this would be identically the case if in the definition of
\mathcal{T}_{k}
the sum over primitive characters were replaced by a sum over all characters.

Lemma 1
.

Let
r,s
be squarefree with
(r,s)=1
. Then, for
k\geq 1
,

\mathcal{T}_{k}(m;rs)=\mathcal{T}_{k}(mr^{k};s)\mathcal{T}_{k}(ms^{k};r).

Proof.

Any primitive character
\chi\pmod{rs}
can be written as
\psi\nu
with
\psi\pmod{r}
and
\nu\pmod{s}
. Furthermore

\tau(\psi\nu)=\tau(\psi)\tau(\nu)\nu(r)\psi(s).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{k}(\ell;rs)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{rs}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi\pmod{r}\\
\nu\pmod{s}\\
\text{all primitive}\end{subarray}}\frac{\tau(\psi\nu)^{k}}{r^{k/2}s^{k/2}}(\psi\nu)(\ell)

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi\pmod{r}\\
\text{primitive}\end{subarray}}\frac{\tau(\psi)^{k}}{r^{k/2}}\psi(s^{k}\ell)\Big)\cdot\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu\pmod{s}\\
\text{primitive}\end{subarray}}\frac{\tau(\nu)^{k}}{s^{k/2}}\nu(r^{k}\ell)\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}(s^{k}\ell;r)\mathcal{T}(r^{k}\ell;s)

as claimed.
∎

Using this proposition we obtain the following.

Lemma 2
.

Let
(r,s_{1}s_{2})=1
with
r
,
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
squarefree.
Let
v_{1},v_{2}
be integers with
(v_{1}v_{2},rs_{1}s_{2})=1
.
Let
d=(s_{1},s_{2})
and
s_{i}^{\star}=s_{i}/d,

i=1,2
. Then, for
k\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{k}(v_{1},v_{2},\ell;r,s_{1},s_{2})

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{rs_{1}s_{2}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\pmod{rs_{1}s_{2}}\\
(x,rs_{1}s_{2})=1\end{subarray}}\mathcal{T}_{k}(v_{1}x;rs_{1})\mathcal{T}_{k}(v_{2}x;rs_{2})e\Big(\frac{\ell x}{rs_{1}s_{2}}\Big)

is zero unless
(\ell,s_{1}^{\star}s_{2}^{\star})=1
and
d|\ell
. In the remaining case it is equal to,

\displaystyle\sqrt{d}

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{k+1}(v_{1}\overline{\ell}s_{2}^{\star}r^{k+1}d^{k+2};s_{1}^{\star})\mathcal{T}_{k+1}(v_{2}\overline{\ell}s_{1}^{\star}r^{k+1}d^{k+2};s_{2}^{\star})

\displaystyle\times\mathcal{K}_{k}(v_{1}{s_{1}^{\star}}^{k+1}s_{2}^{\star},v_{2}s_{1}^{\star}{s_{2}^{\star}}^{k+1},\ell/d;rd,1,1)

Proof.

Since
(r,s_{1}s_{2})=1
with
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
squarefree,
rd
is coprime to
s_{1}^{\star}s_{2}^{\star}
.
By Lemma
1

\mathcal{T}_{k}(v_{i}x;rs_{i})=\mathcal{T}_{k}(v_{i}x;rds_{i}^{\star})=\mathcal{T}_{k}({s_{i}^{\star}}^{k}v_{i}x;rd)\mathcal{T}_{k}(v_{i}xr^{k}d^{k};s_{i}^{\star}).

We express
(x,rs_{1}s_{2})=1
as

x=ard^{2}+bs_{1}^{\star}s_{2}^{\star}
with
(a,s_{1}^{\star}s_{2}^{\star})=1
and
(b,rd)=1
,
b\pmod{rd^{2}}
.
In this way,

\mathcal{T}_{k}(v_{i}x;rs_{i})=\mathcal{T}_{k}({s_{i}^{\star}}^{k}v_{i}x;rd)\mathcal{T}_{k}(v_{i}xr^{k}d^{k};s_{i}^{\star})=\mathcal{T}_{k}(s_{1}^{\star}s_{2}^{\star}{s_{i}^{\star}}^{k}v_{i}b;rd)\mathcal{T}_{k}(v_{i}ar^{k+1}d^{k+2};s_{i}^{\star})

Thus, we find,

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{k}(v_{1},v_{2},\ell;r,s_{1},s_{2})

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{s_{1}^{\star}s_{2}^{\star}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\pmod{s_{1}^{\star}s_{2}^{\star}}\\
(a,s_{1}^{\star}s_{2}^{\star})=1\end{subarray}}\mathcal{T}_{k}(v_{1}ar^{k+1}d^{k+2};s_{1}^{\star})\mathcal{T}_{k}(v_{2}ar^{k+1}d^{k+2};s_{2}^{\star})e\Big(\frac{\ell a}{s_{1}^{\star}s_{2}^{\star}}\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle\times\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\pmod{rd^{2}}\\
(b,rd)=1\end{subarray}}\mathcal{T}_{k}({s_{1}^{\star}}^{k+1}s_{2}^{\star}v_{1}b;rd)\mathcal{T}(s_{1}^{\star}{s_{2}^{\star}}^{k+1}v_{2}b;rd)e\Big(\frac{\ell b}{rd^{2}}\Big)\Big)

In the first sum we can write
a=xs_{1}^{\star}+ys_{2}^{\star}
with
(x,s_{2}^{\star})=1=(y,s_{1}^{\star})
thus factoring the sum further as

\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{s_{2}^{\star}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\pmod{s_{2}^{\star}}\\
(x,s_{2}^{\star})=1\end{subarray}}\mathcal{T}_{k}(v_{2}xs_{1}^{\star}r^{k+1}d^{k+2};s_{2}^{\star})e\Big(\frac{\ell x}{s_{2}^{\star}}\Big)\Big)\cdot\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{s_{1}^{\star}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\pmod{s_{1}^{\star}}\\
(y,s_{1}^{\star})=1\end{subarray}}\mathcal{T}_{k}(v_{1}ys_{2}^{\star}r^{k+1}d^{k+2};s_{1}^{\star})e\Big(\frac{\ell y}{s_{1}^{\star}}\Big)\Big).

Using the definition of
\mathcal{T}_{k}
we then find for
i,j\in\{1,2\}
with
i\neq j
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{s_{i}^{\star}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\pmod{s_{i}^{\star}}\\
(x,s_{i}^{\star})=1\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{k}(v_{i}xs_{j}^{\star}r^{k+1}d^{k+2};s_{i}^{\star})e\Big(\frac{\ell x}{s_{i}^{\star}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{s_{i}^{\star}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\pmod{s_{i}^{\star}}\\
\text{primitive}\end{subarray}}\frac{\tau(\chi)^{k}}{{s_{i}^{\star}}^{k/2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\pmod{s_{i}^{\star}}\\
(x,s_{i}^{\star})=1\end{subarray}}\chi(v_{i}xs_{j}^{\star}r^{k+1}d^{k+2})e\Big(\frac{\ell x}{s_{i}^{\star}}\Big)

This evaluates to

\mathbf{1}_{(\ell,s_{i}^{\star})=1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\pmod{s_{i}^{\star}}\\
\text{primitive}\end{subarray}}\frac{\tau(\chi)^{k+1}}{{s_{i}^{\star}}^{(k+1)/2}}\cdot\chi(v_{i}\overline{\ell}s_{j}^{\star}r^{k+1}d^{k+2})=\mathcal{T}_{k+1}(v_{i}\overline{\ell}s_{j}^{\star}r^{k+1}d^{k+2};s_{i}^{\star})\mathbf{1}_{(\ell,s_{i}^{\star})=1}

by the fact that the Gauss sum

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\pmod{s_{i}^{\star}}\\
(x,s_{i}^{\star})=1\end{subarray}}\chi(x)e\Big(\frac{\ell x}{s_{i}^{\star}}\Big)=0

unless
(\ell,s_{i}^{\star})\neq 1.

Finally it remains to evaluate

\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\pmod{rd^{2}}\\
(b,rd)=1\end{subarray}}\mathcal{T}_{k}({s_{1}^{\star}}^{k+1}s_{2}^{\star}v_{1}b;rd)\mathcal{T}(s_{1}^{\star}{s_{2}^{\star}}^{k+1}v_{2}b;rd)e\Big(\frac{\ell b}{rd^{2}}\Big).

Write
b=x+yrd
with
y\pmod{d}
and
x\pmod{rd}
with
(x,rd)=1
. Plugging this inside the sum evaluates to

\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}d}\sum_{y\pmod{d}}e\Big(\frac{\ell y}{d}\Big)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\pmod{rd}\\
(x,rd)=1\end{subarray}}\mathcal{T}_{k}({s_{1}^{\star}}^{k+1}s_{2}^{\star}v_{1}x;rd)\mathcal{T}(s_{1}^{\star}{s_{2}^{\star}}^{k+1}v_{2}x;rd)e\Big(\frac{\ell x}{rd^{2}}\Big).

This is zero unless
d\mid\ell
. And in the case when
d|\ell
we can rewrite the above as

\sqrt{d}\mathcal{K}_{k}(v_{1}{s_{1}^{\star}}^{k+1}s_{2}^{\star},v_{2}s_{1}^{\star}{s_{2}^{\star}}^{k+1},\ell/d;rd,1,1).

∎

We now record bounds for
\mathcal{T}_{k}
and
\mathcal{K}_{k}
.

Lemma 3
.

Let
q
be squarefree and
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Then, for
k\geq 2
,

|\mathcal{T}_{k}(\ell;q)|\leq C^{\omega(q)}

for some constant
C>10
depending only on
k
.

Proof.

For
(\ell,q)\neq 1
we have
\mathcal{T}_{k}(\ell;q)=0
. We can therefore
assume
(\ell,q)=1
.
Using Lemma
1
we see that it’s enough to bound
\mathcal{T}_{k}(v;p)
for
(v,p)=1
.
Note that

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{k}(v;p)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}\sum_{\chi\pmod{p}}\frac{\tau(\chi)^{k}}{p^{k/2}}\chi(v)-\frac{(-1)^{k}}{p^{(k+1)/2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\varphi(p)}{p^{(k+1)/2}}\sum_{x_{1}\ldots x_{k}\equiv\overline{v}\pmod{p}}e\Big(\frac{x_{1}+\ldots+x_{k}}{p}\Big)-\frac{(-1)^{k}}{p^{(k+1)/2}}.

and this
\ll_{k}1
by Deligne’s bound. Therefore by twisted multiplicativity (i.e Lemma
1
) we get a bound of
\leq C^{\omega(q)}
for
q
squarefree and with a constant
C
depending solely on
k
.
∎

We also have a similar bound for
\mathcal{K}_{k}
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
q
be squarefree and
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Then for
k\geq 2
with
k\equiv 0\pmod{2}
,

|\mathcal{K}_{k}(v_{1},v_{2},\ell;q,1,1)|\leq\sqrt{(\ell,q,v_{1}-v_{2})}\cdot C^{\omega(q)}

for some constant
C>10
depending only on
k
.

Remark 3
.

Though it’s irrelevant for our purpose
C^{\omega(q)}
can be improved to
C^{\omega(q/(q,\ell))}
.

Proof.

First note that if
(v_{1}v_{2},q)\neq 1
then there is nothing to prove since in that case,

\mathcal{K}_{k}(v_{1},v_{2},\ell;q,1,1)=0.

Therefore we can assume that
(v_{1}v_{2},q)=1
.
Suppose
q=rs
with
r,s\geq 1
and
(r,s)=1
.
Recall that,

\mathcal{K}_{k}(v_{1},v_{2},\ell;rs,1,1)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{rs}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\pmod{rs}\\
(x,rs)=1\end{subarray}}\mathcal{T}_{k}(v_{1}x;rs)\mathcal{T}_{k}(v_{2}x;rs)e\Big(\frac{\ell x}{rs}\Big).

Write
x=ar+bs
with
(a,s)=1
and
(b,r)=1
. Using Lemma
1

\mathcal{T}_{k}(v_{i}x;rs)=\mathcal{T}_{k}(v_{i}xs^{k};r)\mathcal{T}_{k}(v_{i}xr^{k};s)=\mathcal{T}_{k}(v_{i}bs^{k+1};r)\mathcal{T}_{k}(v_{i}ar^{k+1};s).

Therefore

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{k}(v_{1},v_{2},\ell;q,1,1)

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\pmod{s}\\
(a,s)=1\end{subarray}}\mathcal{T}_{k}(v_{1}ar^{k+1};s)\mathcal{T}_{k}(v_{2}ar^{k+1};s)e\Big(\frac{\ell a}{s}\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle\times\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\pmod{r}\\
(b,r)=1\end{subarray}}\mathcal{T}_{k}(v_{1}bs^{k+1};r)\mathcal{T}_{k}(v_{2}bs^{k+1};r)e\Big(\frac{\ell b}{r}\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{K}_{k}(v_{1}s^{k+1},v_{2}s^{k+1},\ell;r,1,1)\mathcal{K}_{k}(v_{1}r^{k+1},v_{2}r^{k+1},\ell;s,1,1)

Therefore it is enough to bound
\mathcal{K}_{k}(v_{1},v_{2},\ell;p,1,1)
with
p
prime.
We notice that,

(5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{k}(v_{1},v_{2},\ell;p,1,1)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\pmod{p}\\
(x,p)=1\end{subarray}}\mathcal{T}_{k}(v_{1}x;p)\mathcal{T}_{k}(v_{2}x;p)e\Big(\frac{\ell x}{p}\Big).

By definition

\mathcal{T}_{k}(v_{1}x;p)=\frac{p-1}{p}K_{k}(\overline{v_{1}x};p)-\frac{(-1)^{k}}{p^{(k+1)/2}}

where
K_{k}(v;p)
denotes a hyper-Kloosterman sum

K_{k}(v;p):=\frac{1}{p^{(k-1)/2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1}\ldots x_{k}\equiv u\pmod{p}\end{subarray}}e\Big(\frac{x_{1}+\ldots+x_{k}}{p}\Big).

Therefore, using Deligne’s bound
K_{k}(u;p)\ll_{k}1
, we find,

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{k}(v_{1},v_{2},\ell;p,1,1)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\pmod{p}\\
(x,p)=1\end{subarray}}K_{k}(\overline{v_{1}x};p)K_{k}(\overline{v_{2}x};p)e\Big(\frac{\ell x}{p}\Big)+O_{k}\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}\Big).

If
v_{1}\not\equiv v_{2}\pmod{p}
then by
[
FKM15
, Corollary 3.3]
this is
\ll_{k}1
since
k
is even so that
K_{k}
is self-dual. Moreover if
\ell\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}
then also by
[
FKM15
, Corollary 3.3]
we have

|\mathcal{K}_{k}(v_{1},v_{2},\ell;p,1,1)|\ll_{k}1.

There remains the case when
p|\ell
and
v_{1}\equiv v_{2}\pmod{p}
. In this case, by definition of
\mathcal{T}_{k}
the exponential sum (
5
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\pmod{p}\\
(x,p)=1\end{subarray}}\Big|\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\pmod{p}\\
\text{primitive}\end{subarray}}\frac{\tau(\chi)^{k}}{p^{k/2}}\chi(v_{1}x)\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\varphi(p)}{p^{3/2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\pmod{p}\\
\text{primitive}\end{subarray}}\Big|\frac{\tau(\chi)^{k}}{p^{k/2}}\Big|^{2}\leq\sqrt{p}

And this gives the bound

Remark 7

By the orthogonality of characters, we have

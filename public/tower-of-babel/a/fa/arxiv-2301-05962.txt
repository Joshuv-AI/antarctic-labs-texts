Universal discretization and sparse sampling recovery
F. Dai, V. Temlyakov

Abstract
Recently, it was discovered that for a given function class 𝐅 \mathbf{F} the error of best linear recovery in the square norm can be bounded above by the Kolmogorov width of 𝐅 \mathbf{F} in the uniform norm. That analysis is based on deep results in discretization of the square norm of functions from finite-dimensional subspaces. In this paper we show how very recent results on universal discretization of the square norm of functions from a collection of finite-dimensional subspaces lead to an inequality (Lebesgue-type inequality) between the error of sparse recovery in the square norm provided by the algorithm based on least squares operator and best sparse approximations in the uniform norm with respect to appropriate dictionaries.

It is known that

We now explain the setting in precise mathematical terms. Let
\Omega
be a compact subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with the probability measure
\mu
. By the
L_{p}
norm,
1\leq p<\infty
, of the complex valued function defined on
\Omega
, we understand

\|f\|_{p}:=\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}:=\left(\int_{\Omega}|f|^{p}d\mu\right)^{1/p}.

By the
L_{\infty}
norm we understand the uniform norm of continuous functions

\|f\|_{\infty}:=\max_{\mathbf{x}\in\Omega}|f(\mathbf{x})|

and with a little abuse of notations we sometimes write
L_{\infty}(\Omega)
for the space
{\mathcal{C}}(\Omega)
of continuous functions on
\Omega
.

The concept of sparse approximation plays a fundamental role in our approach. We begin our discussion with the concept of
v
-term approximation with respect to a given dictionary (system)
{\mathcal{D}}_{N}=\{g_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
.
Given an integer
1\leq v\leq N
, we denote by
\mathcal{X}_{v}({\mathcal{D}}_{N})
the collection of all linear spaces spanned by
g_{j}
,
j\in J
with
J\subset[1,N]\cap{\mathbb{N}}
and
|J|=v
, and
denote by
\Sigma_{v}({\mathcal{D}}_{N})
the set of all
v
-term approximants with respect to
{\mathcal{D}}_{N}
:

\displaystyle\Sigma_{v}({\mathcal{D}}_{N}):

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{V\in\mathcal{X}_{v}({\mathcal{D}}_{N})}V.

We then define for a Banach space
X

\sigma_{v}(f,{\mathcal{D}}_{N})_{X}:=\inf_{g\in\Sigma_{v}({\mathcal{D}}_{N})}\|f-g\|_{X}

to be the best
v
-term approximation of
f\in X
in the
X
-norm with respect to
{\mathcal{D}}_{N}
, and define

\sigma_{v}(\mathbf{F},{\mathcal{D}}_{N})_{X}:=\sup_{f\in\mathbf{F}}\sigma_{v}(f,{\mathcal{D}}_{N})_{X}

for a function class
\mathbf{F}\subset X
.

For instance, in the case
X=L_{2}
the best
v
-term approximation of
f
with respect to

{\mathcal{D}}_{N}
can be realised by the following algorithm. Denote by
P_{L}
the orthogonal
L_{2}

projection onto the subspace
L\subset L_{2}
and define the algorithm

L(f,v):=\text{arg}\min_{L\in\mathcal{X}_{v}({\mathcal{D}}_{N})}\|f-P_{L}(f)\|_{2},

P({\mathcal{D}}_{n},v)(f):=P_{L(f,v)}(f).

(1.1)

In this paper we study a similar algorithm with the operator
P_{L}
replaced by the least squares operator, which we define momentarily. For a fixed set of points
\xi:=\{\xi^{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\subset\Omega
define the sample vector

S(f,\xi):=(f(\xi^{1}),\dots,f(\xi^{m}))\in\mathbb{C}^{m}.

Let
X_{N}
be an
N
-dimensional subspace of the space of continuous functions
{\mathcal{C}}(\Omega)
and let
\mathbf{w}:=(w_{1},\dots,w_{m})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
be a positive weight, i.e.
w_{j}>0
,
j=1,\dots,m
. Consider the following classical weighted least squares recovery operator (algorithm) (see, for instance,
[
2
]
)

\ell 2\mathbf{w}(\xi,X_{N})(f):=\text{arg}\min_{u\in X_{N}}\|S(f-u,\xi)\|_{2,\mathbf{w}},\quad\xi=\{\xi^{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\subset\Omega,

where

\|S(g,\xi)\|_{2,\mathbf{w}}:=\left(\sum_{\nu=1}^{m}w_{\nu}|g(\xi^{\nu})|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

We note that the least squares operator (empirical risk minimization) is a classical algorithm from statistics and learning theory. It is also well known in learning theory that performance of this algorithm can be controlled by asymptotic characteristics measured in the uniform norm. The reader can find this kind of results and a discussion in
[
34
]
, Ch.4.

In this paper we focus mainly on the case of special weights
\mathbf{w}=\mathbf{w}_{m}:=(1/m,\dots,1/m)
. In this case the algorithm

\ell 2\mathbf{w}_{m}(\xi,X_{N})
is the classical least squares algorithm. For brevity we use the notation

LS(\xi,X_{N}):=\ell 2\mathbf{w}_{m}(\xi,X_{N}).

We define a new algorithm, which is a nonlinear algorithm:

L(\xi,f):=\text{arg}\min_{L\in\mathcal{X}_{v}({\mathcal{D}}_{N})}\|f-LS(\xi,L)(f)\|_{2},

LS(\xi,\mathcal{X}_{v}({\mathcal{D}}_{N}))(f):=LS(\xi,L(\xi,f))(f).

(1.2)

Thus, the only difference between the algorithm
LS(\xi,\mathcal{X}_{v}({\mathcal{D}}_{N}))(\cdot)
and the ideal
one
P({\mathcal{D}}_{n},v)(\cdot)
is that the orthogonal projection operator
P_{L}
is replaced by the
least squares operator
LS(\xi,L)
. In Section
7
we give one more argument, which motivates us to use the least squares operator.

The problem of universal discretization.
Let
\mathcal{X}:=\{X(n)\}_{n=1}^{k}
be a collection of finite-dimensional linear subspaces
X(n)
of the
L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)
,
1\leq p<\infty
. We say that a set
\xi:=\{\xi^{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\subset\Omega
provides
universal discretization
for the collection
\mathcal{X}
if there are two positive constants
C_{i}
,
i=1,2
, such that for each
n\in\{1,\dots,k\}
and any
f\in X(n)
we have

C_{1}\|f\|_{p}^{p}\leq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|f(\xi^{j})|^{p}\leq C_{2}\|f\|_{p}^{p}.

Definition 1.1
.

We say that a set
\xi:=\{\xi^{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\subset\Omega
provides one-sided universal discretization with constant
C_{1}
for the collection
\mathcal{X}:=\{X(n)\}_{n=1}^{k}
of finite-dimensional linear subspaces
X(n)
if we have

C_{1}\|f\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|f(\xi^{j})|^{2}\quad\text{for any}\quad f\in\bigcup_{n=1}^{k}X(n).

(1.3)

We denote by
m(\mathcal{X},C_{1})
the minimal
m
such that there exists a set
\xi
of
m
points, which
provides one-sided universal discretization with constant
C_{1}
for the collection
\mathcal{X}
.

In this paper we focus on special collections of subspaces generated by a given dictionary:

\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{X}_{v}({\mathcal{D}}_{N})
. In addition to a standard space
L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we also use the following
L_{p}(\Omega,\mu_{\xi})
space, which is convenient for us. For
\xi=\{\xi^{1},\ldots,\xi^{m}\}\subset\Omega
let
\mu_{\xi}
denote the probability measure

\mu_{\xi}=\frac{1}{2}\mu+\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\delta_{\xi^{j}},

where
\delta_{\mathbf{x}}
denotes the Dirac measure supported at a point
\mathbf{x}
.

The following Theorem
1.1
provides the Lebesgue-type inequalities for

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

,

,
N
be given natural numbers such that

. Let

(1.4)

\|f-LS(\xi,\mathcal{X}_{v}({\mathcal{D}}_{N}))(f)\|_{2}\leq(2C_{1}^{-1}+1)\sigma_{v}(f,{\mathcal{D}}_{N})_{\infty}.

(1.5)

1.1
for function classes. Denote by

Conditional result

We recall some notations and prove a conditional result similar to the one from
[
38
]
.
Let
X_{N}
be an
N
-dimensional subspace of the space of continuous functions
{\mathcal{C}}(\Omega)
. For a fixed
m
and a set of points
\xi:=\{\xi^{\nu}\}_{\nu=1}^{m}\subset\Omega
we associate with a function
f\in{\mathcal{C}}(\Omega)
a vector (sample vector)

S(f,\xi):=(f(\xi^{1}),\dots,f(\xi^{m}))\in\mathbb{C}^{m}.

Denote

\|S(f,\xi)\|_{p}:=\left(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\nu=1}^{m}|f(\xi^{\nu})|^{p}\right)^{1/p},\quad 1\leq p<\infty,

and

\|S(f,\xi)\|_{\infty}:=\max_{\nu}|f(\xi^{\nu})|.

For a positive weight
\mathbf{w}:=(w_{1},\dots,w_{m})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
consider the following norm

\|S(f,\xi)\|_{p,\mathbf{w}}:=\left(\sum_{\nu=1}^{m}w_{\nu}|f(\xi^{\nu})|^{p}\right)^{1/p},\quad 1\leq p<\infty.

Define the best approximation of
f\in L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
by elements of
X_{N}
as follows

d(f,X_{N})_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}:=\inf_{u\in X_{N}}\|f-u\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}.

It is well known that there exists an element, which we denote
P_{X_{N},p}(f)\in X_{N}
, such that

\|f-P_{X_{N},p}(f)\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}=d(f,X_{N})_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}.

(See, for instance,
[
7
, p. 59, Theorem 1.1]
).
The operator
P_{X_{N},p}:L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)\to X_{N}
is called the Chebyshev projection.

We make the following two assumptions.

A1. Discretization.
Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Suppose that
\xi:=\{\xi^{1},\ldots,\xi^{m}\}\subset\Omega
is such that for any

u\in X_{N}
in the case
1\leq p<\infty
we have

C_{1}\|u\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}\leq\|S(u,\xi)\|_{p,\mathbf{w}}

and in the case
p=\infty
we have

C_{1}\|u\|_{\infty}\leq\|S(u,\xi)\|_{\infty}

with a positive constant
C_{1}
which may depend on
d
and
p
.

A2. Weights.
Suppose that there is a positive constant
C_{2}=C_{2}(d,p)
such that

\sum_{\nu=1}^{m}w_{\nu}\leq C_{2}
.

Consider the following well known recovery operator (algorithm) (see, for instance,
[
2
]
)

\ell p\mathbf{w}(\xi)(f):=\ell p\mathbf{w}(\xi,X_{N})(f):=\text{arg}\min_{u\in X_{N}}\|S(f-u,\xi)\|_{p,\mathbf{w}}.

Note that the above algorithm
\ell p\mathbf{w}(\xi)
only uses the function values
f(\xi^{\nu})
,
\nu=1,\dots,m
. In the case
p=2
it is a linear algorithm – orthogonal projection with respect
to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{2,\mathbf{w}}
. Therefore, in the case
p=2
approximation error by the algorithm

Recovery of periodic functions with mixed smoothness

We begin with some notation. Given
a>0
, we use the notation
[a]
to denote the largest integer
\leq a

Let
\mathbf{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})
be a vector whose coordinates are
nonnegative integers

\rho(\mathbf{s}):=\bigl\{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:[2^{s_{j}-1}]\leq|k_{j}|<2^{s_{j}},\qquad j=1,\dots,d\bigr\},

Q_{n}:=\cup_{\|\mathbf{s}\|_{1}\leq n}\rho(\mathbf{s})\quad\text{--}\quad\text{a step hyperbolic cross},

\Gamma(N):=\bigl\{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\prod_{j=1}^{d}\max\bigl(|k_{j}|,1\bigr)\leq N\bigr\}\quad\text{--}\quad\text{a hyperbolic cross}.

For

\delta_{\mathbf{s}}(f,\mathbf{x}):=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\rho(\mathbf{s})}\hat{f}(\mathbf{k})e^{i(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{x})},\quad\hat{f}(\mathbf{k}):=(2\pi)^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}f(\mathbf{x})e^{-i(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{x})}d\mathbf{x}.

Let
Q
be a finite set of points in

Along with the

In this section we study recovery problems for classes of functions with mixed smoothness. We define these classes momentarily.
We begin with the case of univariate periodic functions. Let for

It is well known that for

the class
W^{r}_{p}
is embedded into the space of continuous functions
{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbb{T})
.

In the multivariate case for
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})
denote

F_{r}(\mathbf{x}):=\prod_{j=1}^{d}F_{r}(x_{j})

and

\mathbf{W}^{r}_{p}:=\{f:f=\varphi\ast F_{r},\quad\|\varphi\|_{p}\leq 1\},

where

(\varphi\ast F_{r})(\mathbf{x}):=(2\pi)^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varphi(\mathbf{y})F_{r}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})d\mathbf{y}.

For parameters

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L^{2}\left(\mu_{\alpha}\right)}
of
L_{2}(\mu_{\alpha})

given by

\langle f,g\rangle_{L^{2}\left(\mu_{\alpha}\right)}=\int_{-1}^{1}f(x)\overline{g(x)}d\mu_{\alpha}(x),

and

L_{n}^{\alpha}
is
the orthonormalized Gegenbauer polynomial of degree
n
with respect to the weight
v_{\alpha}
on
[-1,1]
. Here and throughout this section, we use the notation
L_{p}(\mu_{\alpha})
to denote the Lebesgue space

L_{p}([-1,1],\mu_{\alpha})
for the sake of convenience.
In the case of
{\alpha}=0
,
{\mathcal{B}}_{0}:=\{L_{n}^{0}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is the system of orthonormalized Legendre polynomials on
[-1,1]
. Let
\mathcal{P}^{n}
denote the space of all real algebraic polynomials in one variable of degree at most
n
. Define
{\mathcal{B}}_{\alpha}^{N}:={\mathcal{B}}_{\alpha}\cap\mathcal{P}^{N}.
Then
{\mathcal{B}}_{\alpha}^{N}
is an orthonormal basis of the space
\mathcal{P}^{N}
equipped with the norm of
L_{2}([-1,1],\mu_{\alpha})
.

One of the main difficulties in the study of sparse approximation by Gegenbauer polynomials comes from the fact that the system
{\mathcal{B}}_{\alpha}
is not uniformly bounded on
[-1,1]
for any
{\alpha}>-\frac{1}{2}
, which is different from the usual trigonometric system. Indeed,
according to (
[
30
, p. 81, (4.7.1); p.82, (4.7.15); p.169, (7.32.5)]
, we have

|L_{n}^{\alpha}(x)|\leq C({\alpha})\min\Bigl\{(n+1)^{{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}},(1-x^{2})^{-\frac{{\alpha}}{2}-\frac{1}{4}}\Bigr\},

(6.1)

and

\max_{x\in[-1,1]}|L_{n}^{\alpha}(x)|=L_{n}^{\alpha}(1)\sim(n+1)^{{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}}.

(6.2)

Here and throughout this section, we use the notation
a_{n}\sim b_{n}
for
n=1,2,\ldots
to mean that there exist two positive constants
C_{1},C_{2}
independent of
n
such that
C_{1}a_{n}\leq b_{n}\leq C_{2}a_{n}
for all
n=1,2,\ldots
.

On the other hand, setting
\varphi_{n}^{\alpha}(x)=C({\alpha})^{-1}L_{n}^{\alpha}(x)(1-x^{2})^{\frac{{\alpha}}{2}+\frac{1}{4}}
for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, and using (
6.1
), we know that
\{\varphi_{n}^{\alpha}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is a uniformly bounded orthogonal system with respect to the probability measure
\frac{1}{\pi}(1-x^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,dx
on
[-1,1]
.
Thus, Theorem
3.1
, our result on universal discretization, is applicable to the system
\{\varphi_{n}^{\alpha}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
. This immediately leads to the following result:

Theorem 6.1
.

For
{\alpha}>-\frac{1}{2}
, and any integer
1\leq v\leq N
, there exist points
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{m}\in[-1,1]
with

m\leq C({\alpha})v\log N\cdot\log^{2}(2v)\cdot(\log(2v)+\log\log N),

such that

\frac{1}{2}\|f\|_{L_{2}(\mu_{\alpha})}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}w(\xi_{j})|f(\xi_{j})|^{2}\leq\frac{3}{2}\|f\|_{L_{2}(\mu_{\alpha})}^{2},\ \ \ \forall f\in\Sigma_{v}({\mathcal{B}}_{\alpha}^{N}),

where
w(x)=\pi^{-1}\sqrt{1-x^{2}}
for
x\in[-1,1]
.

Given
{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{m})\in[-1,1]^{m}
, we define

\mathbf{w}_{m}({\xi})=\frac{1}{m}(w(\xi_{1}),w(\xi_{2}),\ldots,w(\xi_{m})),

where
w(x)=\pi^{-1}\sqrt{1-x^{2}}
for
x\in[-1,1]
.

For an
N
-dimensional space
X_{N}\subset{\mathcal{C}}\big([-1,1]\big)
and
\xi=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{m})\in[-1,1]^{m}
,
we recall that the weighted least squares recovery operator is defined as in the introduction by

\ell 2\mathbf{w}_{m}(\xi,X_{N})(f):=\text{arg}\min_{u\in X_{N}}\|S(f-u,\xi)\|_{2,\mathbf{w}_{m}(\xi)},

where

\|S(g,\xi)\|_{2,\mathbf{w}_{m}(\xi)}:=\left(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\nu=1}^{m}w(\xi_{j})|g(\xi_{j})|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Given an integer
1\leq v\leq N
,
we consider the following sparse recovery characteristic

\varrho^{wls}_{m,v}(\mathbf{F},{\mathcal{B}}_{\alpha}^{N},L_{2}):=\inf_{\xi=\{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{m}\}}\sup_{f\in\mathbf{F}}\min_{L\in\mathcal{X}_{v}({\mathcal{B}}_{\alpha}^{N})}\|f-\ell 2\mathbf{w}_{m}(\xi,L)(f)\|_{L_{2}(\mu_{\alpha})}

where we recall that
\mathcal{X}_{v}({\mathcal{B}}_{\alpha}^{N})
denotes the collection of all linear spaces spanned by
L_{j}^{\alpha}:\ \ j\in J

with
J\subset[1,N]\cap\mathbb{N}
and
|J|=v
.

Theorem 6.2
.

For
{\alpha}>-\frac{1}{2}
, any integer
1\leq v\leq N
, and any compact subset
\mathbf{F}
of
{\mathcal{C}}[-1,1]
, we have

\varrho_{m,v}^{wls}(\mathbf{F},{\mathcal{B}}_{\alpha}^{N},L_{2}(\mu_{\alpha}))\leq 5\sigma_{v}(\mathbf{F},{\mathcal{B}}_{\alpha}^{N})_{\infty},

(6.3)

provided

m\geq C({\alpha})v\log N\cdot\log^{2}(2v)\cdot(\log(2v)+\log\log N).

For the remainder of this section, we restrict our attention to some special function classes where useful estimates on the quantities
\sigma_{v}(\mathbf{F},{\mathcal{B}}_{\alpha}^{N})_{\infty}
can be obtained.

The following nonperiodic Wiener-type class of functions have been considered by Rauhut and Ward
[
29
, Section 7]
for the special case of
{\alpha}=0
(i.e., the case of Legendre polynomials),
and by Jahn, Ullrich, and Voigtlaender
[
13
, Definition 4.7]
for both
{\alpha}=0
and
{\alpha}=-\frac{1}{2}
(i.e., the case of Chebyshev polynomials).
We refer to
[
29
,
13
]
for more background information on these spaces.

Definition 6.1
.

For
\alpha>-\frac{1}{2}
,
r>\frac{1}{2}+\alpha
and
0<\theta\leq 1
, we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\alpha,\theta}^{r}:=\Bigl\{f\in C[-1,1]:\ \ \|f\|_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha,\theta}^{r}}^{\theta}:=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\left|(1+n)^{r}\left\langle f,L^{\alpha}_{n}\right\rangle_{L^{2}\left(\mu_{\alpha}\right)}\right|^{\theta}\leq 1\Bigr\}.

The condition
r>{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}
in the above definition ensures the corresponding space can be continuously embedded into the space
{\mathcal{C}}[-1,1]
.

Our next aim is to determine the sharp asymptotic order of the quantities
\sigma_{n}\left(\mathcal{A}_{{\alpha},\theta}^{r},\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}\right)_{\infty}
.
In the case of Legendre polynomials (i.e.,
{\alpha}=0
), it was conjectured in
[
13
, Remark 4.9]
that
\sigma_{n}\left(\mathcal{A}_{0,1}^{r},\mathcal{B}_{0}\right)_{\infty}\lesssim n^{-r}
for any
r>\frac{1}{2}
.
Our main result, Theorem
6.3
below, gives a bound that is better than this conjectured one. In particular, the sharp estimates we obtained below can be used to improve and extend the estimates of Corollary 4.8 of
[
13
]
.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
0<\theta\leq 1
and
{\alpha}>-\frac{1}{2}
. Then for
r>{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}
, we have

c_{1}n^{-(r+\frac{1}{\theta}-\frac{1}{2})}\leq\sigma_{n}\left(\mathcal{A}_{{\alpha},\theta}^{r},\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}\right)_{\infty}\leq C_{2}n^{-(r+\frac{1}{\theta}-\frac{1}{2})}

for some positive constants
c_{1}
and
C_{2}
depending only on
{\alpha}
,
\theta
and
r
.

Proof.

We start with the proof of the lower estimates, which is simpler due to the following deep result of Kashin
[
14
, Corollary 2]
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
(\Omega,\mu)
be a probability space, and let
{\mathcal{D}}:=\{\phi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
be an orthonormal basis of the space
L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)
. Then there exists a universal constant
c_{0}\in(0,1)
such that for any orthonormal system
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}\subset L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)
, any integer
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
and for the function class

\mathbf{w}_{N}:=\Bigl\{\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\psi_{j}:\ \ \max_{1\leq j\leq N}|a_{j}|\leq 1\Bigr\},

we have

\sigma_{n}(\mathbf{w}_{N},{\mathcal{D}})_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)}\geq\frac{\sqrt{3N}}{2},\ \ \ \ \forall n\in[1,c_{0}N]\cap{\mathbb{N}}.

Let
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
be such that
2^{m}\leq n<2^{m+1}
. Let
m_{1}=m+m_{0}
, where
m_{0}
is the smallest integer such that
2^{m_{0}-1}c_{0}>1
. Define

W:=\Bigl\{2^{-(m_{1}+1)(r+\frac{1}{\theta})}\sum_{2^{m_{1}}\leq j<2^{m_{1}+1}}a_{j}L_{j}^{\alpha}:\ \ a_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}},\ \ |a_{j}|\leq 1\Bigr\}.

Since
n<2^{m+1}\leq c_{0}2^{m_{1}}
, using Lemma
6.1
with
N=2^{m_{1}}
, we deduce

\sigma_{n}(W,{\mathcal{B}}_{\alpha})_{L_{2}(\mu_{\alpha})}\geq\frac{\sqrt{3N}}{2}2^{-(m_{1}+1)(r+\theta^{-1})}\geq cn^{-(r+\frac{1}{\theta}-\frac{1}{2})}.

(6.4)

On the other hand, however, for any

f=2^{-(m_{1}+1)(r+\frac{1}{\theta})}\sum_{2^{m_{1}}\leq j<2^{m_{1}+1}}a_{j}L_{j}^{\alpha}\in W,

we have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathcal{A}_{{\alpha},\theta}^{r}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-(m_{1}+1)(r+\frac{1}{\theta})}\Bigl(\sum_{2^{m_{1}}\leq j<2^{m_{1}+1}}(j+1)^{r\theta}\Bigr)^{1/\theta}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-(m_{1}+1)(r+\frac{1}{\theta})}2^{(m_{1}+1)r}2^{m_{1}/\theta}\leq 1

implying that
W\subset\mathcal{A}_{{\alpha},\theta}^{r}
.
Thus, using (
6.4
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}(\mathcal{A}_{{\alpha},\theta}^{r},{\mathcal{B}}_{\alpha})_{\infty}\geq\sigma_{n}(W,{\mathcal{B}}_{\alpha})_{L_{2}(\mu_{\alpha})}\geq cn^{-(r+\frac{1}{\theta}-\frac{1}{2})},

which gives the desired lower bound.

It remains to prove the upper bound.
We need
the following result, which follows directly from
[
3
, Theorem 1.2]
.

Lemma 6.2
.

[
3
, Theorem 1.2]

Assume that
{\alpha}>-\frac{1}{2}
,
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
2\leq p<\infty
. Then for any
f\in\mathcal{P}^{N}
and any integer
1\leq n\leq N
,

\sigma_{n}(f;\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}^{2N})_{\infty}\leq C({\alpha})\Bigl(\frac{N}{n}\Bigr)^{(2{\alpha}+2)/p}\Bigl(\log\frac{eN}{n}\Bigr)^{(2{\alpha}+1)/p}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mu_{\alpha})}.

(6.5)

Now we turn to the proof of the upper bound.
Let
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
be such that
2^{m}\leq n<2^{m+1}
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
m\geq 20
.
Let
f\in\mathcal{A}_{{\alpha},\theta}^{r}([-1,1])
be such that

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}(1+j)^{r\theta}\left|\left\langle f,L_{j}^{\alpha}\right\rangle\right|^{\theta}=1.

(6.6)

Here and elsewhere in the proof,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the inner product of
L_{2}(\mu_{\alpha})
.
Let
\Lambda_{0}\subset{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
be such that
|\Lambda_{0}|=2^{m-2}
, and

\min_{j\in\Lambda_{0}}|\langle f,L_{j}^{\alpha}\rangle|(1+j)^{r}\geq\sup_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\setminus\Lambda_{0}}|\langle f,L_{j}^{\alpha}\rangle|(1+j)^{r}.

Then (
6.6
) implies that

|\langle f,L_{j}^{\alpha}\rangle|^{\theta}(1+j)^{r\theta}\leq 2^{-m+2},\ \ \ \forall j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\setminus\Lambda_{0}.

(6.7)

Let
\Lambda_{1}:=\Lambda_{0}\cup\{j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}:\ \ j<2^{m-2}\}
.
Let

g_{1}=\sum_{j\in\Lambda_{1}}\langle f,L_{j}^{\alpha}\rangle L_{j}^{\alpha}\ \ \text{and}\ \ f_{1}=f-g_{1}=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\setminus\Lambda_{1}}\langle f,L_{j}^{\alpha}\rangle L_{j}^{\alpha}.

We write
f_{1}=\sum_{k=m-1}^{\infty}\Delta_{k},

where

\Delta_{k}:=\sum_{2^{k-1}\leq j<2^{k}}\langle f_{1},L_{j}^{\alpha}\rangle L_{j}^{\alpha}.

Let
{\kappa}_{0}
denote the smallest integer

\geq\frac{r+\frac{1}{\theta}-\frac{1}{2}}{r-{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}+1
.
Using (
6.1
) and (
6.6
), we obtain

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\sum_{k={\kappa}_{0}m+1}^{\infty}\Delta_{k}\Bigr\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\Bigl\|\sum_{j\geq 2^{{\kappa}_{0}m}}\langle f_{1},L_{j}^{\alpha}\rangle L_{j}^{\alpha}\Bigr\|_{\infty}\leq C\sum_{j\geq 2^{k_{0}m}}|\langle f_{1},L_{j}^{\alpha}\rangle|j^{({\alpha}+\frac{1}{2})}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{-{\kappa}_{0}m(r-{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2})}\sum_{j\geq 2^{{\kappa}_{0}m}}|\langle f_{1},L_{j}^{\alpha}\rangle|j^{r}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{-m(r+\frac{1}{\theta}-\frac{1}{2})}\Bigl(\sum_{j\geq 2^{{\kappa}_{0}m}}|\langle f_{1},L_{j}^{\alpha}\rangle|^{\theta}j^{r\theta}\Bigr)^{1/\theta}\leq Cn^{-(r+\frac{1}{\theta}-\frac{1}{2})}.

(6.8)

Thus, to complete the proof, it is enough to show there exist a sequence
\{n_{k}\}_{k=m-1}^{{\kappa}_{0}m}
of positive integers and a sequence
\{p_{k}\}_{k=m-1}^{{\kappa}_{0}m}
of polynomials such that
\sum_{k=m-1}^{{\kappa}_{0}m}n_{k}\leq 2^{m-1}
,
p_{k}\in\Sigma_{n_{k}}({\mathcal{B}}_{\alpha})
, and

\sum_{k=m-1}^{{\kappa}_{0}m}\|\Delta_{k}-p_{k}\|_{\infty}\leq Cn^{-(r+\frac{1}{\theta}-\frac{1}{2})}.

(6.9)

Indeed, once this is proven, then setting

g:=g_{1}+\sum_{k=m-1}^{{\kappa}_{0}m}p_{k},

we have
g\in\Sigma_{n}({\mathcal{B}}_{\alpha})
and

\sigma_{n}(f,{\mathcal{B}}_{\alpha})_{\infty}\leq\|f-g\|_{\infty}\leq\sum_{k=m-1}^{{\kappa}_{0}m}\|\Delta_{k}-p_{k}\|_{\infty}+\Bigl\|\sum_{k={\kappa}_{0}m+1}^{\infty}\Delta_{k}\Bigr\|_{\infty},

which, combined with (
6.8
) and (
6.9
), will yield the desired upper estimate.

The sequence
\{n_{k}\}_{k=m-1}^{{\kappa}_{0}m}
of positive integers is defined as follows:

n_{k}:=[(k-m+2)^{-2}\cdot 2^{m-2}],\ \ m-1\leq k\leq{\kappa}_{0}m.

Clearly,
n_{k}\leq 2^{k-1}
for
k\geq m-1
, and

\sum_{k=m-1}^{{\kappa}_{0}m}n_{k}\leq 2^{m-2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{2}}\leq 2^{m-1},

where we recall that the notation
[a]
denotes the largest integer
\leq a
for each given
a>0
.
It remains to show the existence of a sequence of polynomials
p_{k}\in\Sigma_{n_{k}}({\mathcal{B}}_{\alpha})
,
m-1\leq k\leq{\kappa}_{0}m
satisfying (
6.9
).

To this end, let

\max\Bigl\{2,\frac{2{\alpha}+2}{r}\Bigr\}<p<\frac{2{\alpha}+2}{{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}}

be a fixed parameter depending only on
r
and
{\alpha}
. By (
6.1
), we have

\|L_{n}^{\alpha}\|_{p}\leq C({\alpha}).

Since the Banach space
L_{p}
is
2
-smooth for
p>2
, it follows by Theorem 9.2.2 of
[
37
, p. 455]
that
for each
integer
k\in[m-1,{\kappa}_{0}m]
, there exists a polynomial
p_{k,1}\in\Sigma_{[n_{k}/2]}({\mathcal{B}}_{\alpha}^{2^{k+1}})
such
that

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{k}-p_{k,1}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{p}n_{k}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{j=2^{k-1}}^{2^{k}}|\langle f_{1},L_{j}^{\alpha}\rangle|\|L_{j}^{\alpha}\|_{p}\leq Cn_{k}^{-\frac{1}{2}}2^{-kr}\sum_{j=2^{k-1}}^{2^{k}}|\langle f_{1},L_{j}^{\alpha}\rangle|j^{r}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{-m(\frac{1}{\theta}-\frac{1}{2})}2^{-kr}(k-m+2).

On the other hand, for each
integer
k\in[m-1,{\kappa}_{0}m]
, applying Lemma

6.2
to the polynomial
\Delta_{k}-p_{k,1}\in\mathcal{P}^{2^{k+1}}
, we obtain
p_{k,2}\in\Sigma_{[n_{k}/2]}({\mathcal{B}}_{\alpha})
such
that

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{k}-p_{k,1}-p_{k,2}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\Delta_{k}-p_{k,1}\|_{p}\Bigl(\frac{2^{k}}{n_{k}}\Bigr)^{(2{\alpha}+2)/p}\Bigl(\log\frac{e2^{k}}{n_{k}}\Bigr)^{(2{\alpha}+1)/p}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{-m(\frac{1}{\theta}-\frac{1}{2})}2^{-kr}2^{(k-m+1)(2{\alpha}+2)/p}(k-m+2)^{\frac{6{\alpha}+5}{p}+1}.

Let
p_{k}=p_{k,1}+p_{k,2}
, Then
p_{k}\in\Sigma_{n_{k}}
and moreover,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=m-1}^{{\kappa}_{0}m}\|\Delta_{k}-p_{k}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=m-1}^{{\kappa}_{0}m}\|\Delta_{k}-p_{k,1}-p_{k,2}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{-m(r+\frac{1}{\theta}-\frac{1}{2})}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{-k\bigl(r-\frac{2{\alpha}+2}{p}\bigr)}(k+2)^{\frac{6{\alpha}+5}{p}+1}

\displaystyle\leq Cn^{-r-\frac{1}{\theta}+\frac{1}{2}}.

∎

Combining Theorem
6.3
with Theorem
6.2
, we deduce

Corollary 6.1
.

For
{\alpha}>-\frac{1}{2}
, any integer
1\leq v\leq N
,
0<\theta\leq 1
and
r>{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\varrho_{m,v}^{wls}

\displaystyle(\mathcal{A}_{{\alpha},\theta}^{r},{\mathcal{B}}_{\alpha}^{N},L_{2}(\mu_{\alpha}))C({\alpha},\theta,r)n^{-(r+\frac{1}{\theta}-\frac{1}{2})}

provided

m\geq C({\alpha})v\log N\cdot\log^{2}(2v)\cdot(\log(2v)+\log\log N).

7
Discussion

We pointed out in the Introduction that the recent paper
[
13
]
motivated us to estimate
the accuracy of the recovery algorithm
LS(\xi,\mathcal{X}_{v}({\mathcal{D}}_{N}))(\cdot)
in terms of errors of
sparse approximation with respect to special systems
{\mathcal{D}}_{N})
. We begin this section with
providing one more argument that motivated us to closely study the least squares algorithms. It is based on the recent result from
[
38
]
on the linear recovery.
Recall the setting
of the optimal linear recovery. For a fixed
m
and a set of points
\xi:=\{\xi^{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\subset\Omega
, let
\Phi
be a linear operator from
\mathbb{C}^{m}
into
L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)
.
Denote for a class
\mathbf{F}
(usually, centrally symmetric and compact subset of
L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)
)

\varrho_{m}(\mathbf{F},L_{p}):=\inf_{\text{linear}\,\Phi;\,\xi}\sup_{f\in\mathbf{F}}\|f-\Phi(f(\xi^{1}),\dots,f(\xi^{m}))\|_{p}.

In other words, for given
m
, a system of functions
\psi_{1}(\mathbf{x}),\dots,\psi_{m}(\mathbf{x})
from
L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)
, and a set

\xi:=\{\xi^{1},\ldots,\xi^{m}\}
from
\Omega
we define the linear operator

\Psi_{m}(f,\xi):=\sum_{j=1}^{m}f(\xi^{j})\psi_{j}(\mathbf{x}).

(7.1)

For a class
\mathbf{F}
of functions we define the quantity

\Psi_{m}(\mathbf{F},\xi)_{p}:=\sup_{f\in\mathbf{F}}\bigl\|\Psi_{m}(f,\xi)-f\bigr\|_{p}.

Then

\varrho_{m}(\mathbf{F},L_{p}):=\inf_{\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{m};\xi^{1},\dots,\xi^{m}}\Psi_{m}(\mathbf{F},\xi).

In the above described linear recovery procedure the approximant comes from a linear subspace of dimension at most
m
.
It is natural to compare
\varrho_{m}(\mathbf{F},L_{p})
with the
Kolmogorov widths. Let
X
be a Banach space and
\mathbf{F}\subset X
be a compact subset of
X
. The quantities

d_{n}(\mathbf{F},X):=\inf_{\{u_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\subset X}\sup_{f\in\mathbf{F}}\inf_{c_{i}}\left\|f-\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}u_{i}\right\|_{X},\quad n=1,2,\dots,

are called the
Kolmogorov widths
of
\mathbf{F}
in
X
. It is clear that in the case
X=H
is a Hilbert space the best approximant from a given subspace is provided by the corresponding orthogonal projection.

We have the following obvious inequality

d_{m}(\mathbf{F},L_{p})\leq\varrho_{m}(\mathbf{F},L_{p}).

(7.2)

In the paper
[
38
]
we considered the case
p=2
, i.e. recovery takes place in the Hilbert space
L_{2}
.
The main result of that paper is the following general inequality.

Theorem 7.1
.

There exist two positive absolute constants
b
and
B
such that for any compact subset
\Omega
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, any probability measure
\mu
on it, and any compact subset
\mathbf{F}
of
{\mathcal{C}}(\Omega)
we have

\varrho_{bn}(\mathbf{F},L_{2}(\Omega,\mu))\leq Bd_{n}(\mathbf{F},L_{\infty}).

Theorem
7.1
was proved in
[
38
]
with a help of a classical type of algorithms – weighted least squares algorithms
\ell 2\mathbf{w}(\xi,X_{N})(\cdot)
defined in the Introduction.

We discussed above two important recovery characteristics – linear recovery error
\varrho_{m}(\mathbf{F},L_{2})

and sparse recovery error –
\varrho^{ls}_{m,v}(\mathbf{F},{\mathcal{D}}_{N},L_{2})
. There are two more quantities, which are popular in the theory of optimal recovery. The following modification of the linear recovery procedure is also of interest. We now allow any mapping
\Phi:\mathbb{C}^{m}\to X_{N}\subset L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)
where
X_{N}
is a linear subspace of dimension
N\leq m
and define

\varrho_{m}^{*}(\mathbf{F},L_{p}):=\inf_{\Phi;\xi;X_{N},N\leq m}\sup_{f\in\mathbf{F}}\|f-\Phi(f(\xi^{1}),\dots,f(\xi^{m}))\|_{p}.

We have the following obvious inequalities

d_{m}(\mathbf{F},L_{p})\leq\varrho_{m}^{*}(\mathbf{F},L_{p})\leq\varrho_{m}(\mathbf{F},L_{p}).

(7.3)

One more quantity is the optimal sampling recovery. We now allow any mapping
\Phi:\mathbb{C}^{m}\to L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)
and define

\varrho_{m}^{o}(\mathbf{F},L_{p}):=\inf_{\Phi;\xi}\sup_{f\in\mathbf{F}}\|f-\Phi(f(\xi^{1}),\dots,f(\xi^{m}))\|_{p}.

Here, index
o
stands for optimal.

Comment
7
.1.
The characteristics
\varrho_{m}
,
\varrho_{m}^{*}
, and
\varrho_{m}^{o}
are well studied for many particular classes of functions. For an exposition of known results we refer to the books

[
41
]
,
[
25
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
37
]
,
[
26
]
–
[
28
]
and references therein. The characteristics
\varrho_{m}^{*}
and
\varrho_{m}
are inspired by the concepts of
the Kolmogorov width and the linear width. Probably,
\varrho_{m}^{*}
was introduced in
[
10
]
,
\varrho_{m}
in
[
33
]
, and
\varrho_{m}^{o}
in
[
41
]
.

Let us make a brief comparison of two nonlinear recovery characteristics
\varrho^{ls}_{m,v}
and

\varrho_{m}^{o}
. The authors of
[
13
]
(see Theorem 4.13 there) proved the following interesting bound for
1<p<2
,
r>1/p

Condition E(
t
).
We say that an orthonormal system

t

t

\mathbf{x}\in\Omega

\sum_{i=1}^{N}|u_{i}(\mathbf{x})|^{2}\leq Nt^{2}.

We now define
E(t)
-conditioned Kolmogorov width

d_{n}^{E(t)}(\mathbf{F},L_{p}):=\inf_{\{u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\}\,\text{satisfies Condition}E(t)}\sup_{f\in\mathbf{F}}\inf_{c_{1},\dots,c_{n}}\|f-\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}u_{i}\|_{p}.

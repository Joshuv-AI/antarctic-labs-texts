On the affine geometry of congruences of lines
J. W. Bruce, F. Tari

Abstract
Congruences, or 2 2 -parameter families of lines in 3 3 -space are of interest in many situations, in particular in geometric optics. In this paper we consider elements of their geometry which are invariant under affine changes of co-ordinates, for example that associated with their focal sets, and less well studied focal planes. We use tools from singularity theory to describe some generic phenomena. In particular we determine the generic singularities of various surfaces in affine 3-space associated to these congruences. We identify a projective quadric in the projectivised tangent space to the manifold of lines which plays a key role in understanding the affine geometry of ruled surfaces, congruences and 3 3 -parameter families or complexes. Many of the results generalise to lines in ℝ n , n > 3 \mathbb{R}^{n},n>3 .

Introduction

Families of lines in Euclidean 3-space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
arise naturally in a variety of ways. Their study has a long history, with contributions from Dupin, Hamilton, Darboux, Blaschke and many others, and they have extensive applications in the sciences and engineering. These families can be viewed as submanifolds of the space of lines, and one can also consider the union of these lines in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. One parameter families of lines are the classical ruled surfaces, with developable surfaces a particularly interesting subclass. Their differential geometry as well as their singularities are well studied; see for example
[
12
]
. Two parameter families of lines, or congruences of lines in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
have a rich and interesting history and accounts can be found in classic textbooks such as
[
1
,
17
]
. Classical differential geometry is largely concerned with linearisation, with a focus on quadratic forms. Generic differential geometry (see for example
[
16
]
) uses singularity theory to identify higher order phenomena and that is what we do here, focusing on affine invariant geometry. The real projective case, with the lines determined by points of the Klein quadric, is very similar, but slightly simpler; we work through a duality result in this case to illustrate the differences.
The results in the paper are as follows.

2
A representation of the space of lines

The two standard models for the oriented lines in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
use the tangent bundle to the unit
2
-sphere
T\mathbb{S}
, where the point
(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
, with
a\cdot a=1,a\cdot b=0
, represents the line
b+ta,t\in\mathbb{R}
(in parametrised form) or
x\wedge a=b
(in equation form). These representations depend on the metric and a choice of centre for the
2
-sphere. Because we are looking at affine invariants it is artificial to use the metric and we proceed as follows.

The affine group of the real line
{\it Aff}_{1}
can be realised as the matrix group

\Big\{\begin{pmatrix}s&t\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}:s,t\in\mathbb{R},s\neq 0\Big\}<GL(2,\mathbb{R})

with the orientation preserving subgroup
{\it Aff}^{+}_{1}
consisting of those elements with
s>0
.

Consider the map

{\it Aff}_{1}^{+}\times(\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})\to\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}^{3},\ (t,s).(a,b)=(sa,b+ta).

This is a free action. The quotient can be identified with the set of oriented lines in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. The point corresponding to
(a,b)
in this quotient, denoted
[a,b]
represents the line
b+ta
. The tangent space to the orbit
{\it Aff}^{+}_{1}.(a,b)
is spanned by
(a,0),(0,a)
, so the tangent space to the quotient space at
[a,b]
can be identified with the quotient
\mathbb{R}^{3}/sp\{a\}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{3}/sp\{a\}
. There is a natural action of the affine group
{\it Aff}_{3}=GL_{3}\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{3}
on this space by

(A,T)*[a,b]=[Aa,Ab+T].

Writing
\mathbb{R}^{3*}
for the space dual to
\mathbb{R}^{3}
we have an action of
\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
on
\mathbb{R}^{3*}\setminus\{0\}\times\mathbb{R},s.(c,d)=(sc,sd)
, with a representative of the quotient
[c,d]
corresponding to the plane
c(x)=d
. Again we have an action of
{\it Aff}_{3}
given by

(A,T)*(c,d)=[c\circ A^{-1},d+c\circ A^{-1}(T)].

Finally we have a free action of
\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
on
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
by multiplication.

As above these representations of lines and planes depend on an arbitrary choice of origin; we shall refer to the quotient spaces above as
\mathbb{L}
,
\mathbb{P}
,
\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
respectively. They can each be given a natural topology and the structure of smooth manifolds in the usual way.

The key to much of the geometry we will uncover are incidence correspondences, namely the following sets, all of which only use the affine structure on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

1.

I(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{L})=\{(p,L)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{L}:p\in L\}

2.

I(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{L})=\{(P,L)\in\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{L}:L\subset P\}

3.

I(\mathbb{L},\mathbb{L})=\{(L,L^{\prime})\in\mathbb{L}\times\mathbb{L}:L\cap L^{\prime}\neq\emptyset\}

4.

\tilde{I}(\mathbb{L},\mathbb{L})=\{(L,L^{\prime},p)\in\mathbb{L}\times\mathbb{L}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}:p\in L\cap L^{\prime}\}

5.

I(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{P},\mathbb{L})=\{(p,L,P):p\in L\subset P\}
.

The first two sets are clearly smooth manifolds of dimension
5
, with their projections to either factor submersions. The third is smooth of dimension
7
away from the diagonal but highly singular along it. These singularities are ‘resolved’ by the fourth which is a smooth
7
-dimensional manifold as we shall see; one could equally use the corresponding space with
\mathbb{P}
replacing
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. The fifth is smooth of dimension
6
.

Remarks 3.4

(1) The quadric arises from a natural non-degenerate, indefinite metric on
\mathbb{L}
discussed for example in
[
10
]
and
[
15
]
, which depends on the Euclidean structure. A quadric with signature
(+,+,-,-)
(resp.
(+,+,+,-)
) has the property that a line meets it in
2
points if and only the dual line meets the dual quadric in
2
points (resp. the dual line does not meet the dual quadric). As usual the quadric allows one to identify the projective space with its dual via polars.

(2) Given a line
L\in\mathbb{L}
we can consider the subgroup of
{\it Aff}_{3}
fixing
L
, that is
G_{a}\ltimes\mathbb{R}_{a}
, where
G_{a}\subset GL_{3}
is the stabiliser of the subspace spanned by
a
, and
\mathbb{R}_{a}
is the set of translations in the direction of
a
. One can check that the quadric and the distinguished line are the only sets invariant under the action of this group.

(3) Note that although there is a
3
-parameter family of points

we only obtain a
2
-parameter family of hyperplanes
T_{L}I(L^{\prime})
. This is because given a point
x\in L
and a plane

there is a pencil of lines
L^{\prime\prime}
through

contained
P(L^{\prime},L)
each determining the same point of the quadric. Indeed corresponding to each point of
Q_{L}
there is a plane pencil of lines in
\mathbb{L}
passing through a fixed point and lying in a fixed plane, and the tangent plane to
Q_{L^{\prime}}
at that point is the tangent space to
I(L^{\prime})
for any
L^{\prime}
in that pencil.

(4) Clearly a curve of lines
L:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{L}
(that is a ruled surface) is developable if and only if its tangent vector

lies in the quadric cone
Q_{L(s)}\subset T_{L(s)}\mathbb{L}
for each

(5) In the real projective case the above remains true with the obvouis interpretations, but there is no distinguished generator corresponding to parallel lines.

4
The complex case

The results above are valid replacing

Proposition 4.1

When the line

is also real.

If

are the points of intersection with the quadric then

; since

is real taking complex conjugates

, so since

in two points either

can be chosen to be real or

is on the generator

(resp.

\alpha(1,0,-t,0)+\beta(0,1,0,-t)=(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}),{\rm and}

\alpha^{\prime}(1,0,-t^{\prime},0)+\beta^{\prime}(0,1,0,-t^{\prime})=\lambda(\bar{x}_{0},\bar{x}_{1},\bar{x}_{2},\bar{x}_{3}).

Taking conjugates of the first equality

\bar{\alpha}(1,0,-\bar{t},0)+\bar{\beta}(0,1,0,-\bar{t})=(\bar{x}_{0},\bar{x}_{1},\bar{x}_{2},\bar{x}_{3}),

writing

\alpha^{\prime}\mu-\bar{\alpha}=\beta^{\prime}\mu-\bar{\beta}=\alpha^{\prime}\mu t^{\prime}-\bar{\alpha}\bar{t}=\beta^{\prime}\mu t-\bar{\beta}\bar{t}=0.

In particular
\bar{\alpha}(t^{\prime}-\bar{t})=\bar{\beta}(t^{\prime}-\bar{t})=0
so either

5
The geometry of congruences

A congruence of lines is a smooth immersed surface
Z\subset\mathbb{L}
; a generic map

will be an immersion with isolated transverse double points. The internal structure carried by
\mathbb{L}
has significant geometric consequences for

determines a line

and a tangent plane

Q_{L(z)}

(resp.
\{(P,L)\in\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{L}:L\subset P,L\in Z\}
) is denoted
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{Z}
(resp.
\mathbb{P}_{Z}
).

(1) The line
\ell_{z}
meets

in

or

8
Model submanifolds and contact with congruences

Let

In studying surfaces in
3
-space we tease out their geometry by ‘comparing them’ with model submanifolds. Our model of ‘roundness’ might be a sphere, of ‘flatness’ a line or plane. We measure roundness or flatness of a surface by considering its contact with these models. In our context a good model submanifold is a ruled surface, congruence or complex (
3
-dimensional submanifold of
\mathbb{L}
) whose symmetry group in
{\it Aff}_{3}
is large. In each case we have a submanifold of the space of lines, the analogue of the sphere, and a manifold which parameterises them, for spheres the set of centres and radii.

Examples 8.1

(Model submanifolds)

(1)
Lines through a point.
There is a
3
-parameter family of lines parametrised by points of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
; the set of oriented lines through a point is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}
.

(2)

A
3
-parameter family of planes; the set of oriented lines in a plane is diffeomorphic to a cylinder, the tangent bundle to the circle.

(3)

A family parametrised by

\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{2}

(4)

(5)

A family parametrised by a
5

(6)

As above this is a hypersurface in

with an

Remarks 8.5

(1) On the point focal-set there will be cuspidal edges, double point curves and swallowtail points. Dualising these become parabolic curves, points of bitangency and cusps of Gauss on the plane-focal set, and vice-versa. We have seen that the condition for a cuspidal edge on the plane-focal set is

(2) It would be interesting to understand the way these two sets of objects appear on each focal set. This is done for normal congruences in

Proposition 8.6

1
)
of the torsal directions; Proposition

We parametrise the congruence by

The discriminant of

Suppose that

L

\begin{array}[]{rcl}F(a_{1}^{\prime},a_{2}^{\prime},b_{1}^{\prime},b_{2}^{\prime})&=&(A_{1}-a_{1}^{\prime})(B_{2}-b_{2}^{\prime})+(A_{2}-a_{2}^{\prime})(B_{1}-b_{1}^{\prime})\\

with

Now suppose given a congruence

(2) This follows from the transversality result in Proposition

.

(3) The

(and worse) singularities are those of corank
2
. Substituting
(a_{1}^{\prime},a_{2}^{\prime},b_{1}^{\prime},b_{2}^{\prime})=(u,v,b_{1},b_{2})
in the above we obtain (all evaluated at

\begin{array}[]{l}A_{1}B_{2}-A_{2}B_{1}=0,\\
B_{2}-A_{2}b_{1u}+A_{1}b_{2u}+b_{2}=B_{1}+A_{2}b_{1v}-A_{1}b_{2v}+b_{1}=0,\\

The second and third equations above determine

in terms of

and obtain,

\begin{pmatrix}b_{1uu}&b_{2uu}&0\\

so

(4) Now consider the case

9
Middle surface

Recall that the middle surface is the locus of points
(p+p^{\prime})/2
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
p,p^{\prime}
pais of (real or imaginary) focal points, given in Proposition
4.1
. Over the non-elliptic set this is the locus of midpoints of the focal points.

Proposition 9.1

(1) The middle surface of a generic congruence can have only cross-cap singularities and these occur at isolated points on the image of the parabolic curve away from the singularities of the focal surface when
b_{1u}=b_{2u}=b_{2v}=b_{1uu}+b_{2uv}=0
; see
Figure
1
.

(2) The focal and middle surfaces are tangential along the image of the parabolic curve.

(3) All regular points on the image of the parabolic curve are hyperbolic points of the middle surface.

Figure 1:
An example of a middle surface with a cross-cap singularity with

b_{1}=2v+u^{2}+5vu-v^{2}-3u^{3}-vu^{2}+7v^{2}u+v^{3}
and
b_{2}=7u^{2}-2vu+3v^{2}+5u^{3}-2vu^{2}+11v^{2}u+v^{3}
.

We parametrise the congruence in the usual way as
(u,v,b_{1},b_{2},)
.

(1) The middle surface is parametrised by

\eta(u,v)=(b_{1}(u,v),b_{2}(u,v),0)-\frac{1}{2}(b_{1u}(u,v)+b_{2v}(u,v))(u,v,1).

The Jacobian matrix of
\eta
at
(u,v)
is

{\small\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{1}{2}(b_{1u}-b_{2v})-\frac{1}{2}(b_{1uu}+b_{2uv})u&b_{2u}-\frac{1}{2}(b_{1uu}+b_{2uv})v&-\frac{1}{2}(b_{1uu}+b_{2uv})\\
b_{1v}-\frac{1}{2}(b_{1uv}+b_{2vv})u&\frac{1}{2}(b_{2v}-b_{1u})-\frac{1}{2}(b_{1uv}+b_{2vv})v&-\frac{1}{2}(b_{1uv}+b_{2vv})\end{array}\right).}

It has the same rank as the matrix

{\small\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{1}{2}(b_{1u}-b_{2v})&b_{2u}&-\frac{1}{2}(b_{1uu}+b_{2uv})\\
b_{1v}&\frac{1}{2}(b_{2v}-b_{1u})&-\frac{1}{2}(b_{1uv}+b_{2vv})\end{array}\right)}

which is singular if and only if its
2\times 2
minors are zero. One of these minors is
(b_{1u}-b_{2v})^{2}+4b_{1v}b_{2u}
and vanishes precisely on the parabolic curve. For generic congruences the vanishing of two minors implies the vanishing of the third, so the singularities of the middle surface occur at isolated points on the parabolic curve.

We assume now that the origin is a parabolic point and take
b_{1u}=b_{2u}=b_{2v}=0
and
b_{1v}\neq 0
at
(0,0)
. Then the middle surface is singular at the origin if and only if
(b_{1uu}+b_{2uv})(0,0)=0
.
This condition is distinct from that for the focal surface to be singular (which is
b_{2uu}(0,0)=0
, see the proof of Proposition
8.6
).

We write
b_{1}=\sum_{k=1}^{3}\sum_{i=0}^{k}b^{1}_{ki}u^{k-i}v^{i}+O(4),

b_{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{3}\sum_{i=0}^{k}b^{2}_{ki}u^{k-i}v^{i}+O(4)

and make changes of coordinates in the source and target. We find that the middle surface has a cross-cap singularity if and only if

(4b^{1}_{11}b^{1}_{31}+4b^{1}_{11}b^{2}_{32}-(b^{1}_{21})^{2}+4(b^{2}_{22})^{2})(3b^{1}_{11}b^{1}_{30}+2b^{1}_{11}b^{2}_{20}+b^{1}_{11}b^{2}_{31}-b^{1}_{20}b^{1}_{21}-2b^{1}_{20}b^{2}_{22})\neq 0,

The above condition is satisfied for generic congruences by Proposition
5.5
.

(2) Taking
b_{1},b_{2}
as in Proposition
8.6
the tangent plane of the two surfaces at the origin is
x_{2}=0
.

(3) With the above setting, the contact between the middle surface and its tangent plane
x_{2}=0
at the origin is measured by the singularities of
H(u,v)=b_{2}(u,v)-\frac{1}{2}(b_{1u}(u,v)+b_{2v}(u,v))v
. This function has an
A_{1}^{-}
-singularity at the origin and the result follows.

\Box

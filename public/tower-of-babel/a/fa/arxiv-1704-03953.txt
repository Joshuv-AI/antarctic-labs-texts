Abstract
. We are concerned with the nonlinear problem u t = u x ​ x + f ⁡ ( u ) u_{t}=u_{xx}+f(u) , where f f is of combustion type, coupled with the Stefan-type free boundary h ⁡ ( t ) h(t) . According to [ 4 , 5 ] , for some critical initial data, the transition solution u u locally uniformly converges to θ \theta , which is the ignition temperature of f f , and the free boundary satisfies h ⁡ ( t ) = C ​ t + o ⁡ ( 1 ) ​ t h(t)=C\sqrt{t}+o(1)\sqrt{t} for some positive constant C C and all large time t t . 1.
Introduction and Main Results

In this paper, we consider the following free boundary problem of nonlinear diffusion equations:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{t}=u_{xx}+f(u),&t>0,\ g(t)<x<h(t),\\
u(t,g(t))=u(t,h(t))=0,&t>0,\\
g^{\prime}(t)=-\mu u_{x}(t,g(t)),&t>0,\\
h^{\prime}(t)=-\mu u_{x}(t,h(t)),&t>0,\\
-g(0)=h(0)=h_{0},u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),&-h_{0}\leq x\leq h_{0},\end{array}\right.

where
x=g(t)
and
x=h(t)
are the expanding fronts,
\mu
,
h_{0}
are given positive constants.
The nonlinear reaction term
f
is a locally Lipschitz continuous function satisfying

(1.6)

\displaystyle f(0)=0,\ \ \ f(u)<0\ \ {\rm for}\ u>1.

Throughout the paper, unless otherwise specified, we assume that
f
is of combustion type:

(1.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&f(u)=0\ \ {\rm in}\ [0,\theta],\ \ \ f(u)>0\ \ {\rm in}\ (\theta,1),\\
&f(u)<0\ \ {\rm in}\ (1,\infty)\end{split}

for some constant
\theta\in(0,1)
.

For any given
h_{0}>0
, the initial function
u_{0}(x)
satisfies

\displaystyle u_{0}\in\mathscr{X}(h_{0}):=\left\{\phi\in C^{2}[-h_{0},h_{0}]:\begin{tabular}[]{ll}$\phi(-h_{0})=\phi(h_{0})=0,\ \phi^{\prime}(-h_{0})>0,$\\
$\phi^{\prime}(h_{0})<0,\ \phi(x)>0\ {\rm in}\ (-h_{0},h_{0})$.\end{tabular}\right\}

It was shown by
[
4
]
that under condition (
1.6
), (
1
) has a unique globally defined classical solution

(u(t,x),h(t),g(t))
. In addition,
g^{\prime}(t)<0
and
h^{\prime}(t)>0
for
t>0
and therefore

\displaystyle g_{\infty}:=\lim_{t\to\infty}g(t)\in[-\infty,-h_{0}),\ \ h_{\infty}:=\lim_{t\to\infty}h(t)\in(h_{0},+\infty]

always exist.

Denote
\mathbb{R}=(-\infty,\infty)
. In
[
4
]
, the authors obtained the following trichotomy result
and the sharp threshold dynamics when
f
satisfies (
1.7
).

Theorem A

(
[
4
, Theorem 1.4]
)
.

Assume that
f
is of combustion type, and
h_{0}>0
,
u_{0}\in\mathscr{X}(h_{0})
. Then one of the following situations occurs:

(i)

Spreading:

(g_{\infty},h_{\infty})=\mathbb{R}
and

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}u(t,x)=1\ \ \mbox{\it locally\ uniformly\ in}\ \mathbb{R};

(ii)

Vanishing:

(g_{\infty},h_{\infty})
is a finite interval and

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\max_{x\in[g(t),h(t)]}u(t,x)=0;

(iii)

Transition:

(g_{\infty},h_{\infty})=\mathbb{R}
and

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}u(t,x)=\theta\ \ \mbox{\it locally\ uniformly\ in}\ \mathbb{R}.

Moreover, if
u_{0}=\nu\phi
(
\nu>0
) for some
\phi\in\mathscr{X}(h_{0})
, then there exists
\nu^{*}=\nu^{*}(h_{0},\phi)\in(0,\infty]
such that
vanishing happens when
0<\nu<\nu^{*}
, spreading happens when
\nu>\nu^{*}
, and transition happens when
\nu=\nu^{*}
.

Remark 1
.

In
[
4
]
, it was assumed that
f
is
C^{1}
; in view of the argument of
[
6
]
, one can easily see that
f

can be relaxed to be locally Lipschitz and Theorem

remains true.

In the paper
[
7
]
, the authors derived the following propagation speed when spreading happens.

Theorem B

(
[
7
, Theorem 1.2]
)
.

Assume that
f
is of combustion type with
f\in C^{1}
and
f^{\prime}(1)<0
. If spreading happens, then there exist
H_{\infty}
,
G_{\infty}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}(h(t)-c^{*}t-H_{\infty})=0,\ \ \lim_{t\to\infty}h^{\prime}(t)=c^{*},

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}(g(t)+c^{*}t-G_{\infty})=0,\ \ \lim_{t\to\infty}g^{\prime}(t)=-c^{*},

where
c^{*}
is uniquely determined by the following equation

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}q_{zz}-c^{*}q_{z}+f(q)=0\ \ {\rm for}\ z\in(0,\infty),\\
q(0)=0,\ \mu q(0)=c^{*},\ q(\infty)=1,\ q(z)>0\ \ {\rm for}\ z\in(0,\infty).\end{array}\right.

The more recent paper
[
5
]
established some estimates on
h(t)
in the transition case.

Theorem C

(
[
5
, Theorem 1.2]
)
.

Assume that
f
is of combustion type and transition happens. Then we have

\displaystyle h(t),-g(t)=2\xi_{0}\,\sqrt{t}\{1+o(1)\},\ \ \ {\rm as}\ t\to\infty,

where
\xi_{0}>0
is uniquely determined by

(1.10)

\displaystyle 2\xi_{0}e^{\xi_{0}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\xi_{0}}e^{-s^{2}}ds=\mu\theta.

Regarding related studies on the free boundary problem, one may refer to
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
8
]
and the references therein.

The purpose of the current paper is to obtain more delicate estimates on the small order
o(1)\sqrt{t}
in Theorem
C

for a special type of nonlinearity
f
. Precisely, by assuming that
there exist constants
p\geq 1
and small

such that

(1.11)

\displaystyle f(u)=(u-\theta)^{p}\ \ {\rm in}\ [\theta,\theta+\sigma),

we aim to investigate how the power

in (

) influences the asymptotic behavior

for large time

in the transition case.
Our main result reads as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
f
is of combustion type satisfying (
1.11
) for some
p\geq 1
and
\sigma>0
, and that the transition happens. Then the following assertions hold.

(1)

If
p>3
, we have

\displaystyle 2\xi_{0}\sqrt{t}+mt^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p-1}}\leq-g(t),\ \ h(t)\leq 2\xi_{0}\sqrt{t}+Mt^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}},\ \ \mbox{as $t\to\infty$},

where
m
and
M
are some positive constants and
\xi_{0}>0
is the constant uniquely given by (
1.10
).

(2)

If
1\leq p\leq 3
, we have

\displaystyle 2\xi_{0}\sqrt{t}+O(1)\leq-g(t),\ \ h(t)\leq 2\xi_{0}\sqrt{t}+Mt^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}},\ \ \mbox{as $t\to\infty$},

where
\xi_{0}>0
is the constant uniquely given by (
1.10
).

Remark 2
.

Theorem
1.1
shows that when
p>3
,
h(t)-2\xi_{0}\sqrt{t}
and
g(t)+2\xi_{0}\sqrt{t}
are unbounded for large time
t

while
h(t)-2\xi_{0}\sqrt{t}
and
g(t)+2\xi_{0}\sqrt{t}
become bounded when

. Therefore,
our results reveal that the nonlinear reaction term

has a qualitative impact on the propagation speed for the transition solution.

Remark 3
.

In light of Theorem
1.1
, it is unclear to us that
h(t)-2\xi_{0}\sqrt{t}
and
g(t)+2\xi_{0}\sqrt{t}
are unbounded or bounded for large time
t
when
1<p\leq 3
.
It would be of interest to investigate the refined propagation speed of the transition solution for the nonlinearity
f
satisfying (
1.11
) with
0<p<1
or
1<p\leq 3
.

2.
Preliminary results

In this section, we give some basic facts, which will be used frequently in the forthcoming sections.
We shall begin with some comparison principles associated with problem (
1
). The first one, which
will be used later to estimate the lower bound of
h(t)
, is due to
[
4
]
.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
4
, Lemma 2.2]
)
.

Suppose that
f\in C^{0,1}_{\rm loc}
and
f(0)=0
,
T\in(0,\infty)
,
\xi
,
\overline{h}\in C^{1}[0,T]
,
\overline{u}\in C(\overline{D}_{T})\cap C^{1,2}(D_{T})
with

D_{T}=\{(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:0<t\leq T,\ \xi(t)<x<\overline{h}(t)\}
, and

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\overline{u}_{t}\geq\overline{u}_{xx}+f(\overline{u}),&0<t\leq T,\ \xi(t)<x<\overline{h}(t),\\
\overline{u}(t,\xi(t))\geq u(t,\xi(t)),&0<t\leq T,\\
\overline{u}(t,\overline{h}(t))=0,\ \overline{h}^{\prime}(t)\geq-\mu\overline{u}_{x}(t,\overline{h}(t)),&0<t\leq T.\\
\end{array}\right.

If

\displaystyle\xi(t)\geq g(t)\ {\it in}\ [0,T],\ h_{0}\leq\overline{h}(0),\ u_{0}(x)\leq\overline{u}(0,x)\ \ {\it in}\ [\xi(0),h_{0}],

where
(u,g,h)
is a solution to
(
1
)
, then

\displaystyle h(t)\leq\overline{h}(t)\ {\it in}\ (0,T],

\displaystyle u(t,x)\leq\overline{u}(t,x)\ \ {\it for}\ t\in(0,T]\ \ {\it and}\ \ \xi(t)<x<\overline{h}(t).

The function
\overline{u}
or the triple
(\overline{u},\xi,\overline{h})
in Lemma
2.1
is usually called an
upper solution of problem (
1
). We can define a lower solution by reversing all the inequalities in the suitable places above.

The following version of comparison principle will be used to estimate the upper bound of
h(t)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that
f\in C^{0,1}_{\rm loc}
and
f(0)=0
,
T\in(0,\infty)
,
\xi
,
\overline{h}\in C^{1}[0,T]
,
\overline{u}\in C(\overline{D}_{T})\cap C^{1,2}(D_{T})
with

D_{T}=\{(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:0<t\leq T,\ \xi(t)<x<\overline{h}(t)\}
, and

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\overline{u}_{t}\geq\overline{u}_{xx}+f(\overline{u}),&0<t\leq T,\ \xi(t)<x<\overline{h}(t),\\
\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\overline{u}(t,\xi(t))\geq 0,&{\rm if}\ h(t)\leq\xi(t),\\
\overline{u}(t,\xi(t))\geq u(t,\xi(t)),&{\rm if}\ \xi(t)<h(t),\end{array}\right.&0<t\leq T,\\
\overline{u}(t,\overline{h}(t))=0,&0<t\leq T,\\
\overline{h}^{\prime}(t)\geq-\mu\overline{u}_{x}(t,\overline{h}(t)),&0<t\leq T.\end{array}\right.

If

\displaystyle\xi(t)\geq 0,\ \ 0\leq t\leq T,

\displaystyle h_{0}\leq\xi(0)<\overline{h}(0),

\displaystyle\overline{u}(0,x)\geq 0,\ \ \xi(0)\leq x\leq\overline{h}(0),

\displaystyle\overline{u}(0,x)>0,\ \ \xi(0)<x<\overline{h}(0),

and
u
is the unique solution to problem (
1
), then

\displaystyle u(t,x)\leq\overline{u}(t,x),\ \ 0<t\leq T,\ x\in(0,h(t))\cap(\xi(t),\overline{h}(t)),

\displaystyle h(t)<\overline{h}(t),\ \ 0<t\leq T.

Proof.

If
h(t)\leq\xi(t)
for
t\in(0,T]
, nothing is needed to prove. Suppose that there exist
t_{*}
,
t^{*}\in(0,T]
with
t_{*}<t^{*}
such that

\displaystyle h(t_{*})=\xi(t_{*}),

\displaystyle\xi(t)<h(t)<\overline{h}(t),\ \ t\in(t_{*},t^{*}),

\displaystyle h(t^{*})=\overline{h}(t^{*}).

Then we have

(2.1)

\displaystyle h^{\prime}(t^{*})\geq\overline{h}^{\prime}(t^{*}).

Let
\Omega_{*}=\{(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}|t_{*}<t\leq t^{*},\ \xi(t)<x<h(t)\}
. It follows from the maximum principle for parabolic equations that

\displaystyle u(t,x)\leq\overline{u}(t,x)\ \ {\rm in}\ \Omega_{*}.

Since
\overline{u}(t,h(t))>0=u(t,h(t))
for
t\in(t_{*},t^{*})
, the strong maximum principle for parabolic equations infers that

\displaystyle u(t,x)<\overline{u}(t,x)\ \ {\rm in}\ \Omega_{*}.

By setting
w(t,x)=\overline{u}(t,x)-u(t,x)
, then
w
satisfies

\displaystyle w_{t}-w_{xx}-c(t,x)w\geq 0\ \ {\rm in}\ \Omega_{*}

for some bounded function
c(t,x)
. We also have

\displaystyle w(t,x)>0\ \ \ {\rm in}\ \Omega_{*},

\displaystyle w(t^{*},h(t^{*}))=\overline{u}(t^{*},\overline{h}(t^{*}))-u(t^{*},h(t^{*}))=0.

An application of the Hopf lemma allows one to conclude that
w_{x}(t^{*},h(t^{*}))<0
, that is,

\displaystyle\overline{h}^{\prime}(t^{*})\geq-\mu\overline{u}_{x}(t^{*},\overline{h}(t^{*}))>-\mu u_{x}(t^{*},h(t^{*}))=h^{\prime}(t^{*}),

which is a contradiction against (
2.1
).
∎

The following lemma also plays a key role in our later analysis. To stress the dependence of the unique solution
(u(t,x),g(t),h(t))
of (
1
)
on the initial function

, we will sometimes use notation

,

and

.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
5
, Lemma 4.1]
)
.

Suppose that
f
is of combustion type and that for any compactly supported functions

and
u_{0}^{2}
satisfying (

) such that

such that

\displaystyle|h(t;u_{0}^{1})-h(t;u_{0}^{2})|\leq C,\ \ |g(t;u_{0}^{1})-g(t;u_{0}^{2})|\leq C.

In light of the above lemma, to prove Theorem

, it suffices to deal with some special initial function.
Without loss of generality, we fix an initial datum
\tilde{u}_{0}
to be symmetrically decreasing and

(2.2)

\tilde{u}_{0}(x)=\tilde{u}_{0}(-x)\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \tilde{u}_{0}^{\prime}(x)<0,\ \ x\in(0,h_{0}],

so that the transition case happens. Consequently, the solution

with such initial datum

\displaystyle\begin{split}&g(t)=-h(t)\ \ {\rm for}\ t>0,\\

.

We note that the above chosen initial datum
\tilde{u}_{0}

.

With the above chosen initial datum

\displaystyle 2\xi_{0}\sqrt{t}+mt^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p-1}}\leq h(t)\leq 2\xi_{0}\sqrt{t}+Mt^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}},\ \ \mbox{as\ $t\to\infty$}

\displaystyle 2\xi_{0}\sqrt{t}+O(1)\leq h(t)\leq 2\xi_{0}\sqrt{t}+Mt^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}},\ \ \mbox{as\ $t\to\infty$}.

3.
Proof of the main theorem

Throughout this section, we assume that
f
is of combustion type and that (
1.11
) holds for
p\geq 1

and
\sigma>0
, and that
(u,g,h)
is the solution of (
1
) with the initial datum
\tilde{u}_{0}
.

3.1.
Upper bound estimate

We first observe that
u(t,0)>\theta
for all
t\geq 0
. Otherwise there exists
t_{0}>0
such that
u(t_{0},0)\leq\theta
and hence
u(t_{0},x)<\theta
for

x\in[-h(t_{0}),h(t_{0})]\backslash\{0\}
. By the strong maximum principle we easily deduce
u(t,x)<\theta
for
t>t_{0}
and
x\in[-h(t),h(t)]
, which in turn implies

u(t,x)\to 0
as
t\to\infty
, contradicting the assumption that the transition happens. Combining this and (
2.3
), we can see that for each
t\geq 0
, there exists a unique

\theta(t)\in(0,h(t))
such that

(3.1)

\displaystyle u(t,\theta(t))=\theta.

To obtain an upper bound estimate of
h(t)
, as a first step, we will derive an estimate for
\theta(t)
. For this purpose, given a constant
b\in(0,1-\theta)
,
let us recall the property of the initial value problem:

(3.2)

\displaystyle V^{\prime\prime}+f(V)=0,\ V(0)=\theta+b,\ V^{\prime}(0)=0.

Through a phase plane analysis, (
3.2
) has a unique solution
V_{b}
and numbers
l(b)
,
L(b)\in(0,\infty)
with
l(b)<L(b)
such that

(3.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&V_{b}(L(b))=0,\ \ V_{b}(l(b))=\theta,\\
&V_{b}^{\prime}(x)<0\ \ {\rm for}\ x\in(0,L(b)].\end{split}

Furthermore, we have

Lemma 3.1
.

The following assertions hold.

(i)

If
p>1
, then
l(b)
is strictly decreasing in
b\in(0,\sigma)
.

(ii)

If
p=1
, then
l(b)=\frac{\pi}{2}
for
b\in(0,\sigma)
.

(iii)

If
p\geq 1
, then
L(b)
is strictly decreasing in
b\in(0,\sigma)
.

Proof.

By virtue of the equation for
V_{b}
, direct calculation gives

\displaystyle-V_{b}^{\prime\prime}V_{b}^{\prime}=f(V_{b})V_{b}^{\prime},

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{(V_{b}^{\prime})^{2}}{2}\right)^{\prime}=(F(V_{b}))^{\prime},

where

F(u)=\int_{0}^{u}f(s)ds.

By integrating we then obtain

-\frac{V_{b}^{\prime}(x)^{2}}{2}=F(V_{b}(x))-F(V_{b}(0)),

and so

V_{b}^{\prime}(x)^{2}=2[F(b+\theta)-F(V_{b}(x))],

that is,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\frac{V_{b}^{\prime}(x)}{\sqrt{2[F(b+\theta)-F(V_{b}(x))]}}=-1.

Integrating the above identity over
[0,l(b)]
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{l(b)}\frac{V_{b}^{\prime}(x)}{\sqrt{2[F(b+\theta)-F(V_{b}(x))]}}dx=-l(b),

\displaystyle\int_{b+\theta}^{\theta}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{2[F(b+\theta)-F(s)]}}=-l(b),

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{b}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{G(b)-G(s)}}=l(b),

where

G(u)=2\int_{0}^{u}f(\theta+s)ds.

For
0<u<\sigma
and
p>1
, it follows that

(3.5)

\displaystyle G(u)=2\int_{0}^{u}f(\theta+s)ds=2\int_{0}^{u}s^{p}ds=\frac{2}{p+1}u^{p+1}

and

\displaystyle l(b)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{b}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{\frac{2}{p+1}(b^{p+1}-s^{p+1})}}=\frac{\sqrt{p+1}}{\sqrt{2}b^{\frac{p+1}{2}}}\int_{0}^{b}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{1-\left(\frac{s}{b}\right)^{p+1}}}

(3.6)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{p+1}}{\sqrt{2}b^{\frac{p+1}{2}}}b\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{1-x^{p+1}}}=C_{p}b^{-\frac{p-1}{2}},

where

C_{p}=\sqrt{\frac{p+1}{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{1-x^{p+1}}}.

This implies that
l(b)
is strictly decreasing in
b\in(0,\sigma)
if
p>1
.

When
p=1
,
l(b)=\frac{\pi}{2}
is an obvious fact.

We next investigate
L(b)
. As
V_{b}(x)
is a linear function over
[l(b),L(b)]
, we have

\displaystyle-V_{b}^{\prime}(l(b))=\frac{\theta}{L(b)-l(b)}.

This gives

(3.7)

\displaystyle L(b)=l(b)-\frac{\theta}{V_{b}^{\prime}(l(b))}.

By (
3.4
) we know
V_{b}^{\prime}(l(b))=-\sqrt{G(b)}
. Thus, from (
3.7
) and (
3.5
) it follows

(3.8)

\displaystyle L(b)=C_{p}b^{-\frac{p-1}{2}}+\sqrt{\frac{p+1}{2}}\theta b^{-\frac{p+1}{2}}\ \ \mbox{if}\;p>1,

and

L(b)=\frac{\pi}{2}+\theta b^{-1}\ \ \mbox{if}\;p=1.

Hence,
L(b)
is strictly decreasing in
b\in(0,\sigma)
.
∎

Lemma 3.2

(
[
5
, Lemma 4.3]
)
.

If
0<b<\frac{1-\theta}{2}
, then
L(b)\to+\infty
if and only if
b\to 0
.

As one will see below, for any fixed
t\geq 1
, the sign-changing pattern of the function
u(t,x)-V_{b}(x)
becomes crucial in obtaining a refined estimate for
\theta(t)
.
Indeed, such kind of properties were studied in Lemmas 4.6–4.8 in
[

]
.

By Lemma 4.5 of
[
5
]
, there exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that for each
b\in(0,\delta_{0})
,

w_{b}(t,x):=u(t,x)-V_{b}(x)

satisfies

w_{b}(1,0)>0>w_{b}(1,h(1))

and has a
unique zero in
[0,h(1)]
, and the zero is nondegenerate.

As
t\mapsto w_{b}(t,x)
,
t\mapsto(w_{b})_{x}(t,x)
, and
t\mapsto h(t)
are all continuous uniformly in
x
, we see that
for each
b\in(0,\delta_{0})
there exists
\epsilon_{0}>0
small such that for each fixed

We define

\displaystyle T_{1}^{b}:=\sup\{s:w_{b}(t,0)>0\ \ {\rm for}\ t\in[1-\epsilon_{0},s)\},

\displaystyle T_{2}^{b}:=\sup\{s:h(t)<L(b)\ \ {\rm for}\ t\in[1-\epsilon_{0},s)\}.

. Due to

, both

Now we are ready to present the estimate of

in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(u,g,h)
be the solution of (
1
) with the initial datum

\displaystyle\theta(t)\leq Mt^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}}.

\displaystyle h(t)>L(\sigma_{1})\ \ \ {\rm for}\ t\geq T_{0}.

\displaystyle h(t)=L(b(t))\ \ \ {\rm for}\ t\geq T_{0}.

Claim 1.

\theta(t)\leq l(b(t))
for all large

.

Since

locally uniformly in

\displaystyle u(t,0)<\theta+\sigma_{1}\ \ \ {\rm for}\ t\geq T_{0}^{\prime}.

[

\displaystyle u(t_{0},x)-V_{b_{0}}(x)<0\ \ {\rm for}\ x<L(b_{0})\ {\rm but\,close\,to}\ L(b_{0}).

Recalling that
\theta(t_{0})>l(b_{0})

(3.10)

\displaystyle u(t_{0},l(b_{0}))-V_{b_{0}}(l(b_{0}))>0

Hence

(l(b_{0}),L(b_{0}))

[

Then

[+0-0]

) imply that

\displaystyle u(t_{0},x)-V_{b_{0}}(x)>0\ \ \ {\rm for}\ 0\leq x<l(b_{0}),

\displaystyle u(t_{0},0)-b_{0}-\theta>0.

\displaystyle A:=\{b>b_{0}:u(t_{0},x)\geq V_{b}(x)\geq\theta\ \ {\rm in}\ [0,l(b)]\},

\displaystyle b_{*}:=\sup A.

First we show that set

\displaystyle u(t_{0},x)>V_{b_{0}}(x)\geq\theta\ \ \ {\rm for}\ x\in[0,l(b_{0})].

Since

) and

\displaystyle u(t_{0},x)>V_{b_{2}}(x)\geq\theta\ \ \ {\rm for}\ x\in[0,l(b_{2})]

but close to

. This implies that

\displaystyle u(t_{0},0)\geq b+\theta\ \ {\rm that\ is,}\ \ b\leq u(t_{0},0)-\theta\leq\sigma_{1}.

Then

. Therefore

is well-defined. In the sequel, we further claim that

Suppose that

V_{b_{*}}(0)<u(t_{0},0)
, that is,

. Note that

and

Then Lemmas 4.6–4.8 of
[
5
]

T_{2}^{b_{*}}<t_{0}<T_{1}^{b_{*}}.

By Lemma 4.7 (iii) in
[
5
]
,
u(t_{0},x)-V_{b_{*}}(x)

[+0-0+]
. We then conclude that

u(t_{0},x)>V_{b_{*}}(x)\ \ \ \mbox{for}\ x\in[0,l(b_{*})].

For any

, it follows that

\displaystyle u(t_{0},x)\geq V_{b}(x)\ \ {\rm for\ all}\ x\in[0,l(b_{*})]\subset[0,l(b)].

Letting

gives

\displaystyle u(t_{0},x)\geq V_{b_{*}}(x)\ \ {\rm for}\ x\in[0,l(b_{*})].

Noting that

V_{b_{*}}(l(b_{*}))=V_{b_{0}}(l(b_{0}))=\theta\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ u_{x}(t,x)<0,

we apply (
3.10
) and Lemma
3.1
to assert that

u(t,l(b_{*}))-V_{b_{*}}(l(b_{*}))\geq u(t,l(b_{0}))-V_{b_{0}}(l(b_{0}))>0.

Since
u(t_{0},x)-V_{b_{*}}(x)
has the sign-changing pattern
[+0-0+]
, there holds

\displaystyle u(t_{0},x)>V_{b_{*}}(x)\ \ {\rm for}\ x\in[0,l(b_{*})].

By continuity of

with respect to

, one can find
\varepsilon>0
such that

\displaystyle u(t_{0},x)>V_{b_{*}+\varepsilon}(x)\ \ {\rm for}\ x\in[0,l(b_{*}+\varepsilon)].

This contradicts the definition of

. So we have
b_{*}=u(t_{0},0)-\theta
, that is,

and

\displaystyle u(t_{0},x)\geq V_{b_{*}}(x)\ \ {\rm for}\ x\in[0,l(b_{*})].

However, by Lemmas 4.6–4.8 of
[
5
]
, if
u(t_{0},x)-V_{b_{*}}(x)
has a tangency at

, then

in

for some small

.
This is a contradiction. Thus we have shown that
\theta(t)\leq l(b(t))
for all large

.

Claim 2.

\theta(t)\leq Mt^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}}
for all large
t
.

In the case of

, we have

by (

).

Now we handle the situation of

. By Theorem 1.2 in
[
5
]
(see Theorem
C
) and (
3.8
)

(3.11)

\displaystyle h(t)=2\xi_{0}\sqrt{t}(1+o(1)),

(3.12)

\displaystyle h(t)=L(b(t))=C_{p}b(t)^{-\frac{p-1}{2}}+\sqrt{\frac{p+1}{2}}\theta b(t)^{-\frac{p+1}{2}}.

By Lemma
3.2
,

\displaystyle b\to 0\ \ {\rm if\ and\ only\ if}\ L(b)\to\infty.

Since

as

, one further gets

\displaystyle b(t)\to 0,\ \ {\rm as}\ t\to\infty.

From (
3.11
) and (

), we have

\displaystyle 2\xi_{0}\sqrt{t}(1+o(1))=b(t)^{-\frac{p+1}{2}}\sqrt{\frac{p+1}{2}}\theta(1+o(1)),\ \ {\rm as}\ t\to\infty

and in turn

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\{t^{\frac{1}{p+1}}b(t)\}^{\frac{p+1}{2}}}=\frac{2\xi_{0}}{\theta}\sqrt{\frac{2}{p+1}}(1+o(1)),\ \ {\rm as}\ t\to\infty

from which we obtain

\displaystyle b(t)=O(t^{-\frac{1}{p+1}}),\ \ {\rm as}\ t\to\infty.

This and (
3.6
) yield

\displaystyle l(b(t))=O(t^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}}),\ \ {\rm as}\ t\to\infty.

Therefore there exists

such that

\displaystyle\theta(t)\leq l(b(t))\leq Mt^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}}\ \ \mbox{for all large}\ t.

∎

Consider the Stefan problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\rho_{t}-\rho_{xx}=0,&t>0,0<x<r(t),\\
\rho(t,0)=\theta,\ \rho(t,r(t))=0,&t>0,\\
r^{\prime}(t)=-\mu\rho_{x}(t,r(t)),&t>0.\end{array}\right.

It is easily seen that the solution to (
3.1
) is given by

(3.16)

\displaystyle\rho(t,x)=\theta\left[1-\frac{E\left(\frac{x}{2\sqrt{t}}\right)}{E(\xi_{0})}\right],\ \ r(t)=2\xi_{0}\sqrt{t},

where

E(x)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{x}e^{-s^{2}}ds.

By utilizing the explicit solution of the Stefan problem (
3.1
)
to construct a suitable upper solution of (
1
), we can obtain the upper estimate of
h(t)
. Specifically, we can state

Lemma 3.4
.

If
p>1
and let
(u,g,h)
be the solution of (
1
) with the initial datum
\tilde{u}_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle h(t)\leq 2\xi_{0}\sqrt{t}+Mt^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}},\ \ \ {\rm as}\ t\to\infty.

Proof.

In view of Lemma
3.3
, we can find some
K>0
and
T_{0}>1
such that

\displaystyle\theta(t)\leq Kt^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}}\ \ {\rm for}\ t\geq T_{0}.

Fix
T_{0}
and choose
M>0
so that

\displaystyle M>\max\{K,\ \ T_{0}^{\frac{1}{p+1}-\frac{1}{2}}h(T_{0})\}.

Define

\displaystyle\xi(t)=Mt^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}},

\displaystyle\overline{u}(t,x)=\rho(t,x-\xi(t)),

\displaystyle\overline{h}(t)=\xi(t)+r(t),

where
\rho
,
r
is given by (
3.16
).

We will check that
(\overline{u},\xi,\overline{h})
satisfies the condition of Lemma
2.2
, that is,

(3.17)

\displaystyle\overline{h}(T_{0})\geq\xi(T_{0})>h(T_{0}),

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\overline{u}(T_{0},x)\geq 0&{\rm for}\ x\in[\xi(T_{0}),\overline{h}(T_{0})],\\
\overline{u}(T_{0},x)>0&{\rm for}\ x\in(\xi(T_{0}),\overline{h}(T_{0})),\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\overline{u}(t,\xi(t))\geq 0&{\rm if}\ \xi(t)>h(t),\\
\overline{u}(t,\xi(t))\geq u(t,\xi(t))&{\rm if}\ \xi(t)\leq h(t),\end{array}\right.{\rm for}\ t\geq T_{0},

(3.22)

\displaystyle\overline{u}(t,\overline{h}(t))=0\ \ {\rm for}\ \ t>T_{0},

(3.23)

\displaystyle\overline{h}^{\prime}(t)\geq-\mu\overline{u}_{x}(t,\overline{h}(t))\ \ {\rm for}\ \ t>T_{0},

(3.24)

\displaystyle\overline{u}_{t}-\overline{u}_{xx}\geq f(\overline{u})\ \ {\rm for}\ \ t>T_{0},\ x\in(\xi(t),h(t)).

From the choice of
M
, it follows that

\displaystyle\overline{h}(T_{0})=\xi(T_{0})+r(T_{0})\geq\xi(T_{0})=MT_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}}\geq h(T_{0}),

and so (
3.17
) holds.

By the definition of
\overline{u}
, (
3.1
) is trivially true.

When
t\geq T_{0}
and
\xi(t)>h(t)
, clearly
\overline{u}(t,\xi(t))\geq 0
. As

\theta(t)\leq Kt^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}}<\xi(t)\ \ \mbox{for}\ t\geq T_{0},

we get

\displaystyle\overline{u}(t,\xi(t))=\theta=u(t,\theta(t))\geq u(t,\xi(t))

for
t\geq T_{0}
,
\xi(t)<h(t)
. Thus (
3.1
) holds.

By the definition of
\overline{u}
again, we have
\overline{u}(t,\overline{h}(t))=\rho(t,r(t))=0
, verifying (
3.22
).

We now show that (
3.23
) holds. In fact, for
t\geq T_{0}
,

\displaystyle\overline{h}^{\prime}(t)=r^{\prime}(t)+\xi^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=-\mu\rho_{x}(t,r(t))+\frac{M(p-1)}{2(p+1)}t^{-\frac{p+3}{2(p+1)}}

\displaystyle>-\mu\rho_{x}(t,r(t))=-\mu\overline{u}_{x}(t,\overline{h}(t)).

Therefore (
3.23
) is fulfilled.

Finally we verify (

\displaystyle\overline{u}_{t}=\rho_{t}(t,x-\xi(t))-\xi^{\prime}(t)\rho_{x}(t,x-\xi(t)),

\displaystyle\overline{u}_{xx}=\rho_{xx}(t,x-\xi(t)).

Then we have, for
t\geq T_{1}
and
\xi(t)<x<\overline{h}(t)
,

\displaystyle\overline{u}_{t}-\overline{u}_{xx}=-\xi^{\prime}(t)\rho_{x}(t,x-\xi(t)).

Since

\displaystyle\rho_{x}(t,x)=-\frac{\theta}{E(\xi_{0})\sqrt{\pi t}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{4t}},

we find

\displaystyle\overline{u}_{t}-\overline{u}_{xx}

\displaystyle=-\xi^{\prime}(t)\rho_{x}(t,x-\xi(t))

\displaystyle=\frac{p-1}{2(p+1)}Mt^{\frac{p-1}{2(p+1)}-1}\frac{\theta}{E(\xi_{0})\sqrt{\pi t}}e^{-\frac{(x-\xi(t))^{2}}{4t}}\geq 0=f(\overline{u}).

Here we used the facts that
0\leq\overline{u}\leq\theta
and
f(\overline{u})=0
. Consequently, (
3.24
) is satisfied.

With the help of Lemma
2.2
, we can assert

\displaystyle h(t)

\displaystyle\leq 2\xi_{0}\sqrt{t}+Mt^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}},\ \ \mbox{for}\ t\geq T_{0}.

The proof is thus complete.

Assume that

and let

When

for

3.2.
Lower bound estimate for
p>3

Our proof of the upper bound of
h(t)
heavily relies on the upper bound estimate for
\theta(t)
;
it seems that such an idea ceases to produce a refined lower bound of
h(t)
. To the aim, in the following we will develop a different approach by firstly deriving a lower bound of
u(t,0)
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Assume that
p>3
and let
(u,g,h)
be the solution of (
1
) with the initial datum
\tilde{u}_{0}
. Then there exist constants
a>0
and
T>0
such that

\displaystyle u(t,0)\geq\theta+a(t+T)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}},\ \ \forall t\geq 0.

Proof.

We first recall a well known result of the self-similar solution to the following nonlinear heat equation

(3.25)

\displaystyle v_{t}=v_{xx}+|v|^{p-1}v.

Consider an associated problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{\varphi}{p-1}+\frac{y}{2}\varphi^{\prime}+\varphi^{\prime\prime}+|\varphi|^{p-1}\varphi=0,\ \ y>0,\\
\varphi(0)=\gamma,\ \varphi^{\prime}(0)=0.\end{array}\right.

By the result of Haraux and Weissler
[
9
]
, for

p>3\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ 0<\gamma<\left\{[p-3]/2(p-1)\right\}^{\frac{1}{p-1}},

the above problem has a unique solution
\varphi
, which is defined for all
y>0
, and

\varphi(y)>0,\ \forall y\geq 0
. If we define

v(t,x)=t^{-1/(p-1)}\varphi(x/\sqrt{t}),

then
v
satisfies (
3.25
).

Set

w(t,x)=\theta+v(t,x).

Then
w
solves

\displaystyle w_{t}-w_{xx}=|w-\theta|^{p-1}(w-\theta),\ \ t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}.

In view of
v(t,\cdot)\to 0
as
t\to\infty
locally uniformly in
\mathbb{R}
(actually in
C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
),
it follows that
w(t,\cdot)\to\theta
locally uniformly in
\mathbb{R}
as
t\to\infty
.

Recall that
u(t,0)>\theta
for all
t\geq 0
. Thus there exists
T>0
such that

\displaystyle u(0,0)>w(T_{0},0).

Furthermore we may assume that
u(0,x)
and
w(T,x)
have exact one intersection point
y\in(0,h_{0})
.

Denote

\eta(t,x)=u(t,x)-w(t+T,x)\ \ \mbox{for}\ t\geq 0,\ x\in[0,h(t)].

Then we claim

\displaystyle\eta(t,0)>0\ \ {\rm for\ all}\ t>0.

Otherwise there exists
t_{1}>0
such that

\displaystyle\eta(t,0)>0\ \ {\rm for}\ t\in[0,t_{1})\ {\rm and}\ \eta(t_{1},0)=0.

Since
\eta(t,0)>0>\eta(t,h(t))
for
t\in(0,t_{1})
and the unique existence of
y(t)
(zero of
\eta
) for
t\in(0,\varepsilon_{1})
(
\varepsilon_{1}>0
is a small constant), we deduce that

\eta(t,x)
has a unique nondegenerate zero over
[0,h(t)]
for
t\in(0,t_{1})
due to Lemma 2.2 in
[
5
]
. Hence
y(t)
can be extended to all
t\in(0,t_{1})
.

Let us look at the limit of
y(t)
as
t
increases to
t_{1}
. If the limit does not exist, then as the proof of Lemma 2.4 in
[
5
]
we have
\eta(t_{1},x)\equiv 0
over
[0,h(t_{1})]
which
contradicts with
\eta(t_{1},h(t_{1}))<0
. Hence the limit exists and we denote it by
y(t_{1})
. As
\eta(t_{1},y(t_{1}))=0
, clearly
y(t_{1})<h(t_{1})
. If
y(t_{1})=0
, the maximum principle,
as applied to
\eta
over
\{(t,x)|0<t<t_{1},y(t)<x<h(t)\}
, gives

\eta(t_{1},x)<0\ \ \mbox{for}\ x\in(0,h(t_{1})].

It is easily seen that

u(t_{1},x)<w(t_{1}+T,x)\ \ \mbox{for}\ x\in[-h(t_{1}),h(t_{1})]\backslash\{0\}.

By the strong maximum principle,

\displaystyle u(t,x)<w(t+T,x)\ \ {\rm for}\ t>t_{1},\ x\in[-h(t),h(t)].

For any
t_{2}>t_{1}
, we choose
\varepsilon_{0}>0
small enough such that

\displaystyle(1+\varepsilon_{0})u(t_{2},x)\leq w(t_{2}+T,x)\ \ {\rm for}\ 0\leq x\leq h(t_{2}).

We can apply the comparison principle to conclude that

\displaystyle u(t,x;(1+\varepsilon_{0})u(t_{2},x))\leq w(t+t_{2}+T,x)\ \ \ {\rm for}\ t\geq 0,\ 0\leq x\leq h(t+t_{2}).

By taking
(1+\varepsilon_{0})u(t_{2},\cdot)
as a new initial value, Theorem
A
concludes that

\displaystyle u(t,x;(1+\varepsilon_{0})u(t_{2},x))\to 1,\ \ {\rm as}\ t\to\infty,

which is a contradiction.

If
y(t_{1})\in(0,h(t_{1}))
, then by the maximum principle applied to
\eta
over
\{(t,x)|0\leq t\leq t_{1},\ 0<x<y(t)\}
,
one can see that
\eta(t_{1},x)>0
for
0<x<y(t_{1})
. In light of the Hopf lemma, we further have

\displaystyle\eta_{x}(t_{1},0)>0,

which implies

u_{x}(t_{1},0)>w_{x}(t_{1}+T,0)=v_{x}(t_{1}+T,0)=0.

This is a contradiction to
u_{x}(t_{1},0)=0
. Thus, our previous claim holds.

As a result, we derive

\displaystyle u(t,0)>w(t+T,0)=\theta+(t+T)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\varphi(0)=\theta+\gamma(t+T)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}.

Denoting
a=\gamma
, we obtain the desired assertion.
∎

Inspired by the Stefan problem (
3.16
), we are now able to deduce the lower bound of
h(t)
using Lemma
3.6
. Indeed, we have

Lemma 3.7
.

Assume that
p>3
and Let
(u,g,h)
be the solution of (
1
) with the initial datum
\tilde{u}_{0}
. Then there exists a constant
m>0
such that

\displaystyle h(t)\geq 2\xi_{0}\sqrt{t}+mt^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p-1}},\ \ {\rm as}\ t\to\infty.

where
\xi_{0}>0
is given by (
1.10
).

Proof.

Let

\xi(t)=b(t+T)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}},

where
T
is the constant determined by Lemma
3.6
and
0<b<a
is a constant to be chosen later.
We first observe that there exists
\beta\in C^{1}
such that
\beta(t)>0
for
t>0
and

(3.26)

\displaystyle\sqrt{\pi}\beta(t)e^{\beta(t)^{2}}E(\beta(t))=\mu(\theta+\xi(t)),

where
E
is defined in (
3.16
). In fact, by setting

\Phi(x)=\sqrt{\pi}xe^{x^{2}}E(x),

we have

\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime}(x)=\sqrt{\pi}e^{x^{2}}E(x)+2\sqrt{\pi}x^{2}e^{x^{2}}E(x)+2x>0\ \ {\rm for}\ x>0.

Since
\Phi(0)=0
and
\lim_{x\to\infty}\Phi(x)=\infty
, there exists a unique
\beta=\beta(t)
such that
\Phi(\beta(t))=\mu(\theta+\xi(t))
.
The implicit function theorem guarantees that
\beta\in C^{1}
. One can further claim that
\beta^{\prime}(t)<0
. Indeed, by (
3.26
),

\displaystyle\beta^{\prime}(t)\Phi^{\prime}(\beta(t))=\mu\xi^{\prime}(t)=-\frac{b\mu}{p-1}(t+T)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}-1}<0\ \ {\rm for}\ t>0,

which indicates
\beta^{\prime}(t)<0
for
t>0
.

Now we define a lower solution as follows:

\displaystyle\underline{h}(t)=2\beta(t)\sqrt{t},\ \ \underline{u}(t,x)=(\theta+\xi(t))\left\{1-\frac{E\left(\frac{x}{2\sqrt{t}}\right)}{E(\beta(t))}\right\}.

Fix
T_{0}>0
and choose
b
so that

(3.27)

\displaystyle b<a\left(\frac{T}{T+T_{0}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}},\ \ {\rm that\ is},\ \ bT^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}<a(T+T_{0})^{-\frac{1}{p-1}},

\displaystyle b<\sigma T^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

Then we have

\underline{u}(t,x)\leq\theta+\sigma,\ \ \ f(\underline{u})\geq 0.

We next show that for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
,
(\underline{u},\xi,\underline{h})
is
a lower solution to (
1
), that is,

(3.28)

\displaystyle\underline{u}_{t}-\underline{u}_{xx}-f(\underline{u})\leq 0,\ \ t>\varepsilon,\ 0<x<\underline{h}(t),

(3.29)

\displaystyle\underline{u}(t,\underline{h}(t))=0,\ \ t>\varepsilon,

(3.30)

\displaystyle\underline{h}(\varepsilon)\leq h(T_{0}),\ \underline{u}(\varepsilon,x)\leq u(T_{0},x),\ 0\leq x\leq\underline{h}(\varepsilon),

(3.31)

\displaystyle\underline{u}(t,0)\leq u(t-\varepsilon+T_{0},0),\ \ t>\varepsilon,

(3.32)

\displaystyle\underline{h}^{\prime}(t)\leq-\mu\underline{u}_{x}(t,\underline{h}(t)),\ \ t>\varepsilon.

Basic calculation yields

\displaystyle\underline{u}_{t}-\underline{u}_{xx}-f(\underline{u})

\displaystyle\ =

\displaystyle\xi^{\prime}(t)\left\{1-\frac{E\left(\frac{x}{2\sqrt{t}}\right)}{E(\beta(t))}\right\}-(\theta+\xi(t))\left\{\frac{E\left(\frac{x}{2\sqrt{t}}\right)}{E(\beta(t))}\right\}_{t}+(\theta+\xi(t))\left\{\frac{E\left(\frac{x}{2\sqrt{t}}\right)}{E(\beta(t))}\right\}_{xx}-f(\underline{u})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{b}{p-1}(t+T)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}-1}\left\{1-\frac{E\left(\frac{x}{2\sqrt{t}}\right)}{E(\beta(t))}\right\}

\displaystyle\ \ \ -(\theta+\xi(t))\frac{-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}xt^{-\frac{3}{2}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{4t}}E(\beta(t))-\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\beta^{\prime}(t)e^{-\beta(t)^{2}}E\left(\frac{x}{2\sqrt{t}}\right)}{[E(\beta(t))]^{2}}

\displaystyle\ \ \ -(\theta+\xi(t))\frac{x}{2\sqrt{\pi}t^{\frac{3}{2}}}\cdot\frac{1}{E(\beta(t))}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{4t}}-f(\underline{u})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{b}{p-1}(t+T)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}-1}\left\{1-\frac{E\left(\frac{x}{2\sqrt{t}}\right)}{E(\beta(t))}\right\}+(\theta+\xi(t))\frac{2\beta^{\prime}(t)e^{-\beta(t)^{2}}E\left(\frac{x}{2\sqrt{t}}\right)}{\sqrt{\pi}[E(\beta(t))]^{2}}-f(\underline{u}).

As
\beta^{\prime}(t)<0
,
\underline{u}_{t}-\underline{u}_{xx}-f(\underline{u})\leq 0
, and so (
3.28
) holds.

By the definition, clearly (
3.29
) is true.

We then check (
3.30
). By Lemma
3.6
and (
3.27
) we have

\displaystyle\underline{u}(\varepsilon,x)\leq\underline{u}(\varepsilon,0)\leq\theta+b(\varepsilon+T)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}

\displaystyle<\theta+bT^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}=\underline{u}(0,0)

\displaystyle<\theta+a(T+T_{0})^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\leq u(T_{0},0)

for any sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
and
0\leq x\leq\underline{h}(\varepsilon)
. By the continuity of
u(T_{0},x)
, one can find a small
\delta>0
such that

\displaystyle\underline{u}(0,0)=\theta+bT^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}<u(T_{0},x)\ \ {\rm for}\ x\in[0,\delta).

By choosing
\varepsilon>0
to be small enough so that

\displaystyle\varepsilon<\frac{1}{4\beta(0)^{2}}\min\{\delta^{2},h_{0}^{2}\},\ \ {\rm that\ is},\ \ 2\beta(\varepsilon)\sqrt{\varepsilon}<2\beta(0)\sqrt{\varepsilon}<\min\{\delta,h_{0}\},

we deduce

\displaystyle\underline{h}(\varepsilon)=2\beta(\varepsilon)\sqrt{\varepsilon}<h_{0}<h(T_{0})

and

\displaystyle\underline{u}(\varepsilon,x)<\theta+bT^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\leq u(T_{0},x)

for
x\in[0,\underline{h}(\varepsilon)]=[0,2\beta(\varepsilon)\sqrt{\varepsilon}]
. This indicates (
3.30
).

In view of (
3.27
), we have

\displaystyle\underline{u}(t,0)

\displaystyle=\theta+b(t+T)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\theta+b(t-\varepsilon+T)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}

\displaystyle<\theta+a(t-\varepsilon+T+T_{0})^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\leq u(t-\varepsilon+T_{0},0),

which verifies (
3.31
).

Finally we check (
3.32
). From the definition of
\beta(t)
and (
3.26
), it follows

\displaystyle-\mu\underline{u}_{x}(t,\underline{h}(t))=\mu(\theta+\xi(t))\frac{\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\cdot\frac{1}{2\sqrt{t}}e^{-\beta(t)^{2}}}{E(\beta(t))}=\beta(t)\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\underline{h}^{\prime}(t)=2\beta^{\prime}(t)\sqrt{t}+\beta(t)\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\leq\beta(t)\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}=-\mu\underline{u}_{x}(t,\underline{h}(t)).

Now, applying the comparison principle (Lemma
2.1
), we can conclude

\displaystyle h(t-\varepsilon+T_{0})\geq\underline{h}(t)\ \ {\rm for}\ \ t\geq\varepsilon.

In view of

\displaystyle\Phi(\beta(t))=\Phi(\xi_{0})+\mu b(t+T)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}},

\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime}(\zeta)(\beta(t)-\xi_{0})=\mu b(t+T)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\ \ (\xi_{0}\leq\zeta\leq\beta(t)),

we have

\displaystyle\beta(t)-\xi_{0}=\frac{\mu b}{\Phi^{\prime}(\zeta)}(t+T)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}.

As a consequence, we obtain

\displaystyle h(t)

\displaystyle\geq\underline{h}(t+\varepsilon-T_{0})

\displaystyle=2\left(\xi_{0}+\frac{\mu b}{\Phi^{\prime}(\zeta)}(t+\varepsilon-T_{0}+T)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\right)(t+\varepsilon-T_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\geq 2\left(\xi_{0}+\frac{\mu b}{2\Phi^{\prime}(\zeta)}(t+\varepsilon-T_{0})^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\right)(t+\varepsilon-T_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}

for sufficiently large
t
, and then there exists
m>0
such that

\displaystyle h(t)

\displaystyle\geq 2\xi_{0}(t+\varepsilon-T_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}+m(t+\varepsilon-T_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p-1}}

\displaystyle\geq 2\xi_{0}\sqrt{t}+mt^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p-1}}+o(1),\ \ \mbox{as}\ t\to\infty.

∎

3.3.
Lower bound estimate for
1\leq p\leq 3

It turns out that the lower bound estimate of
h(t)
in the case
1\leq p\leq 3
is easier to establish.

Lemma 3.8
.

Assume that
1\leq p\leq 3
and let
(u,g,h)
be the solution of (
1
) with the initial datum
\tilde{u}_{0}
. Then we have

\displaystyle h(t)\geq 2\xi_{0}\sqrt{t}+O(1),\ \ {\rm as}\ t\to\infty,

where
\xi_{0}>0
is given by (
1.10
).

Proof.

We construct a lower solution in the following manner:

\displaystyle\underline{u}(t,x)=\theta\left[1-\frac{E\left(\frac{x}{2\sqrt{t}}\right)}{E(\xi_{0})}\right],\ \ \underline{h}(t)=2\xi_{0}\sqrt{t}.

Then we have
\underline{u}(t,x)\leq\theta
and
f(\underline{u})\geq 0
.

We will show that for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0

(\underline{u},\xi,\underline{h})
is a lower solution to (
1
) for
t>\varepsilon
, that is,

(3.33)

\displaystyle\underline{u}_{t}-\underline{u}_{xx}-f(\underline{u})\leq 0,\ \ t>\varepsilon,\ 0<x<\underline{h}(t),

(3.34)

\displaystyle\underline{u}(t,\underline{h}(t))=0,\ \ t>\varepsilon,

(3.35)

\displaystyle\underline{h}(\varepsilon)\leq h_{0},\ \underline{u}(\varepsilon,x)\leq u(0,x),\ 0\leq x\leq\underline{h}(\varepsilon),

(3.36)

\displaystyle\underline{u}(t,0)\leq u(t-\varepsilon,0),\ \ t>\varepsilon,

(3.37)

\displaystyle\underline{h}^{\prime}(t)\leq-\mu\underline{u}_{x}(t,\underline{h}(t)),\ \ t>\varepsilon.

From (
3.1
) and the fact that
f(\underline{u})\geq 0
, (
3.33
) follows.

By the definition, it is easily seen that (
3.34
) holds.

Next we check (
3.35
). As
u(t,0)>\theta
for
t\geq 0
, clearly

\displaystyle\underline{u}(\varepsilon,x)\leq\underline{u}(\varepsilon,0)\leq\theta<u(0,0)

for any sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
and
0\leq x\leq\underline{h}(\varepsilon)
.

By the continuity of
u(0,x)
, we can find a small
\delta>0
such that

\displaystyle u(0,x)>\theta\ \ {\rm for}\ x\in[0,\delta).

Taking
\varepsilon>0
to be sufficiently small so that

\displaystyle\varepsilon<\frac{1}{4\xi_{0}^{2}}\min\{\delta^{2},h_{0}^{2}\},\ \ {\rm that\ is},\ \ 2\xi_{0}\sqrt{\varepsilon}<\min\{\delta,h_{0}\},

we have

\displaystyle\underline{h}(\varepsilon)=2\xi_{0}\sqrt{\varepsilon}<h_{0}

and

\displaystyle\underline{u}(\varepsilon,x)\leq\theta<u(0,x)

for
x\in[0,\underline{h}(\varepsilon)]=[0,2\xi_{0}\sqrt{\varepsilon}]
.

In view of
\underline{u}(t,0)\leq\theta<u(t-\varepsilon,0)
for
t\geq\varepsilon
, it is also clear that (
3.36
) holds.

Finally (
3.1
) ensures that (
3.37
) is satisfied.

Hence, Lemma
2.1
yields that

\displaystyle\underline{h}(t+\varepsilon)=2\xi_{0}\sqrt{t+\varepsilon}\leq h(t)\ \ {\rm for}\ t>0.

and in turn,

\displaystyle h(t)\geq 2\xi_{0}\sqrt{t}+O(1),\ \ \mbox{as}\ t\to\infty.

∎

Combining Lemmas
3.4
–
3.8
, we obtain Theorem
1.1
.

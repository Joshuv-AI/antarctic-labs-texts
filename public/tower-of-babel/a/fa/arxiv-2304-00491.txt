Abstract
. In the paper we describe the class of all 2-dimensional endo-commutative algebras over any basic field. Thereby, we extend S.-E. Takahasi, K. Shirayanagi and M. Tsukada’s recent description of the class of all 2-dimensional endo-commutative algebras over ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} to any basic field, where the concept of endo-commutative algebra was first introduced and motivations to study this class of algebras also had been given. We give canonical representatives of the isomorphism classes such algebras in dimension two over any basic field. This elementary and self-contained exposition uses recent result one of the authors on description of the category of all 2 2 -dimensional algebras over any basic field.



Key words: endo-commutative algebra, isomorphism, structure constants

Introduction

The classification problem, up to isomorphism, of a given class of algebras is one of the important and difficult problems of algebra. So far two approaches are known to the solution of the problem. One of them is structural (basis free, invariant) approach. For instance, the classification of finite dimensional simple and semi-simple associative algebras by Wedderburn and simple and semi-simple Lie algebras by Cartan are examples of such approach. But it is observed that this approach becomes more difficult when one considers more general types of algebras. Another approach to the solution of the problem is coordinate based. Definition 1
.

An algebra
\mathbf{A}
is said to be endo-commutative if
\mathrm{x}^{2}\mathrm{y}^{2}=(\mathrm{x}\mathrm{y})^{2},
for any
\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y}\in\mathbf{A}.

Lemma 1
.

An algebra
\mathbf{A}
is endo-commutative if and only if

(2.2)

A(A\otimes A)(x^{\otimes 2}\otimes y^{\otimes 2}-(x\otimes y)^{\otimes 2})=0,

where
A
is MSC of
\mathbf{A}
.

Proof.

According to (
2.1
) we write

\mathrm{x}^{2}=\mathbf{e}Ax^{\otimes 2},\ \ \mathrm{y}^{2}=\mathbf{e}Ay^{\otimes 2},\ \ \mathrm{x}^{2}\mathrm{y}^{2}=\mathbf{e}A(Ax^{\otimes 2}\otimes Ay^{\otimes 2})=\mathbf{e}A(A\otimes A)(x^{\otimes 2}\otimes y^{\otimes 2})

and

(\mathrm{x}\mathrm{y})^{2}=\mathbf{e}A(A(x\otimes y)\otimes A(x\otimes y))=\mathbf{e}A(A\otimes A)(x\otimes y)^{\otimes 2}.

Therefore, the equality
\mathrm{x}^{2}\mathrm{y}^{2}=(\mathrm{x}\mathrm{y})^{2}
in terms of MSC and the coordinate vectors is written as follows

\mathbf{e}A(A\otimes A)(x^{\otimes 2}\otimes y^{\otimes 2})=\mathbf{e}A(A\otimes A)(x\otimes y)^{\otimes 2}
i.e.,

A(A\otimes A)(x^{\otimes 2}\otimes y^{\otimes 2}-(x\otimes y)^{\otimes 2})=0

what is required to get.
∎

4.
Two-dimensional curled algebras

Let
\mathbb{A}
be a two-dimensional algebra and

A=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\alpha_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}&\alpha_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}&\alpha_{3}^{{}^{\prime}}&\alpha_{4}^{{}^{\prime}}\\
\beta_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}&\beta_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}&\beta_{3}^{{}^{\prime}}&\beta_{4}^{{}^{\prime}}\end{array}\right)

be its MSC with respect to a basis
\mathbf{e}=\{\mathrm{e}_{1},\mathrm{e}_{2}\}
. Then (
2.3
) in terms of the elements of
A
and the coordinate vector of
\mathrm{x}
is written as follows

(4.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rrl}\alpha_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}x_{1}^{2}+(\alpha_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}+\alpha_{3}^{{}^{\prime}})x_{1}x_{2}+\alpha_{4}^{{}^{\prime}}x_{2}^{2}-\lambda(x_{1},x_{2})x_{1}&=&0\\
\beta_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}x_{1}^{2}+(\beta_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}+\beta_{3}^{{}^{\prime}})x_{1}x_{2}+\beta_{4}^{{}^{\prime}}x_{2}^{2}-\lambda(x_{1},x_{2})x_{2}&=&0.\\
\end{array}\right.

We consider two options:

(1)

Let
\mathrm{\lambda(x)}
be not identically zero over
\mathbb{F}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}
. Since (
4.1
) is an identity it must hold for any
x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{F}^{2}.
Particularly, it must hold for
x=(0,x_{2})
and
x=(x_{1},0)
as well. Therefore,
x_{1}=0,x_{2}\neq 0
(
x_{1}\neq 0,x_{2}=0
) implies

\alpha_{4}^{{}^{\prime}}=0,\ \lambda(0,x_{2})=\beta_{4}^{{}^{\prime}}x_{2}
(respectively,
\beta_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}=0,\ \lambda(x_{1},0)=\alpha_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}x_{1}
) and (
4.1
) becomes

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rrl}\alpha_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}x_{1}^{2}+(\alpha_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}+\alpha_{3}^{{}^{\prime}})x_{1}x_{2}-\lambda(x_{1},x_{2})x_{1}&=&0\\
(\beta_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}+\beta_{3}^{{}^{\prime}})x_{1}x_{2}+\beta_{4}^{{}^{\prime}}x_{2}^{2}-\lambda(x_{1},x_{2})x_{2}&=&0.\end{array}\right.

Thus, if

x_{1}\neq 0,\ x_{2}\neq 0
then the system (
4.1
) can be rewritten as follows

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rrl}\alpha_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}x_{1}+(\alpha_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}+\alpha_{3}^{{}^{\prime}})x_{2}-\lambda(x_{1},x_{2})&=&0\\
(\beta_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}+\beta_{3}^{{}^{\prime}})x_{1}+\beta_{4}^{{}^{\prime}}x_{2}-\lambda(x_{1},x_{2})&=&0.\end{array}\right.

This gives
(\alpha_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}-\beta_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}-\beta_{3}^{{}^{\prime}})x_{1}+(\alpha_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}+\alpha_{3}^{{}^{\prime}}-\beta_{4}^{{}^{\prime}})x_{2}=0
and, if
\mathrm{Card}(\mathbb{F})>2
(that is
\mathbb{F}\neq\mathbb{Z}_{2}
) then the later is noting else
\alpha_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}=\beta_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}+\beta_{3}^{{}^{\prime}},\ \beta_{4}^{{}^{\prime}}=\alpha_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}+\alpha_{3}^{{}^{\prime}}
. So, if
\mathbb{F}\neq\mathbb{Z}_{2}
then we have
\alpha_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}x_{1}+\beta_{4}^{{}^{\prime}}x_{2}=\lambda(x_{1},x_{2}),
at least one of
\alpha_{1}^{{}^{\prime}},\beta_{4}^{{}^{\prime}}
is not zero and

(4.2)

A=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\alpha_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}&\alpha_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}&\beta_{4}^{{}^{\prime}}-\alpha_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}&0\\
0&\beta_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}&\alpha_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}-\beta_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}&\beta_{4}^{{}^{\prime}}\end{array}\right).

If
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
then
\alpha_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}x_{1}+(a-\beta_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}-\beta_{3}^{{}^{\prime}})x_{2}=\lambda(x_{1},x_{2}),
at least one of
\alpha_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}
,
a-\beta_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}-\beta_{3}^{{}^{\prime}}
is not zero and one has

(4.3)

A=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\alpha_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}&\alpha_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}&a-\alpha_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}-\alpha_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}&0\\
0&\beta_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}&\beta_{3}^{{}^{\prime}}&a-\beta_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}-\beta_{3}^{{}^{\prime}}\end{array}\right),

where
a=\lambda(1,1)
.

(2)

Let
\mathrm{\lambda(x)}
be identically zero over
\mathbb{F}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}
. In this case the system (
4.1
) is equivalent to
\alpha_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}=\alpha_{4}^{{}^{\prime}}=\beta_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}=\beta_{4}^{{}^{\prime}}=\alpha_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}+\alpha_{3}^{{}^{\prime}}=\beta_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}+\beta_{3}^{{}^{\prime}}=0
and therefore, we have

(4.4)

A=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}0&\alpha_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}&-\alpha_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}&0\\
0&\beta_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}&-\beta_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}&0\end{array}\right).

These type of algebras were said to be zeropotent (see
[
9
,
17
,
18
,
28
]
).

The forms (
4.3
), (
4.3
) and (
4.4
) are the conditions for
A
to be curled.

Since now we know for a two-dimensional algebra given by MSC
A
to be a curled algebra, we can treat the description of two-dimensional curled algebras by using Theorems 1, 2 and 3. For example, in the case of
\mathbb{F}
with
Char(\mathbb{F})\neq 2,3,
a two-dimensional algebra given by

A=A_{1}(\mathrm{c})=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{1}&\alpha_{2}&1+\alpha_{2}&\alpha_{4}\\
\beta_{1}&-\alpha_{1}&1-\alpha_{1}&-\alpha_{2}\end{pmatrix},

where
\mathrm{c}=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{4},\beta_{1})\in\mathbb{F}^{4},
is a curled algebra if and only if

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rllrrrr}\alpha_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}&=&\alpha_{1}\par&\alpha_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}&=&\alpha_{2}\\

\alpha_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}-\beta_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}&=&1-\alpha_{1}\par&\beta_{4}^{{}^{\prime}}&=&-\alpha_{2}\par\end{array}\right.

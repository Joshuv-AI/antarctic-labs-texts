Uncertainty principles for the short-time Fourier transform on the lattice Thanks: Research supported by Core Research Grant (RP03890G), Science and Engineering Research Board (SERB), DST, India.
Anirudha Poria, Aparajita Dasgupta

Abstract
. In this paper, we study a few versions of the uncertainty principle for the short-time Fourier transform on the lattice ℤ n × 𝕋 n {\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n} . In particular, we establish the uncertainty principle for orthonormal sequences, Donoho–Stark’s uncertainty principle, Benedicks-type uncertainty principle, Heisenberg-type uncertainty principle and local uncertainty inequality for this transform on ℤ n × 𝕋 n {\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n} . Also, we obtain the Heisenberg-type uncertainty inequality using the k k -entropy of the short-time Fourier transform on ℤ n × 𝕋 n {\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n} .

Primary 44A15; Secondary 42C20, 33C45.

1.
Introduction

In classical harmonic analysis, uncertainty principles are inequalities or uniqueness theorems concerning the joint localization of a function and its spectrum. Uncertainty principles play a fundamental role in the field of mathematics, physics, in addition to some engineering areas such as quantum theory, image processing, signal processing, optics, and many other well-known areas
[
18
,
9
,
7
,
2
]
. In quantum physics, it says that a particle’s speed and position cannot both be measured with infinite precision. The classical Heisenberg uncertainty principle was established in the Schrödinger space (the square-integrable function space). It states that a non-zero function and its Fourier transform cannot be both sharply localized, i.e., it is impossible for a non-zero function and its Fourier transform to be simultaneously small. This phenomenon has been under intensive study for almost a century now and extensively investigated in different settings. One can formulate different forms of the uncertainty principle depending on various ways of measuring the localization of a function. Uncertainty principles can be divided into two categories: quantitative and qualitative uncertainty principles. Quantitative uncertainty principles are some special types of inequalities that tells us how a function and its Fourier transform relate. For example, Benedicks
[
1
]
, Donoho and Stark
[
7
]
, and Slepian and Pollak
[
19
]
gave quantitative uncertainty principles for the Fourier transforms. On the other hand, qualitative uncertainty principles imply the vanishing of a function under some strong conditions on the function. In particular, Hardy
[
12
]
, Morgan
[
17
]
, Cowling and Price
[
5
]
, and Beurling
[
16
]
theorems are the examples of qualitative uncertainty principles. For a more detailed study on the history of the uncertainty principle, and for many other generalizations and variations of the uncertainty principle, we refer to the book of Havin and Jöricke
[
14
]
, and the excellent survey of Folland and Sitaram
[
8
]
.

In signal analysis and in quantum mechanics, uncertainty principles are often discussed for simultaneous time-frequency representations, such as the short-time Fourier transform (STFT) or the Wigner distribution. These time-frequency representations are transforms which map a function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to a function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\widehat{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, called the time-frequency plane or phase space. Since the dual group of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the same as
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the phase space is
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For the group
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, the dual group is
{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
and the phase space is
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
. In this paper, we consider the STFT on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
and study several uncertainty principles. The main results we discuss here for the STFT on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
are analogues of uncertainty principles for the STFT on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Gröchenig and Zimmermann
[
11
]
showed that it is possible to derive new uncertainty principles for the STFT from uncertainty principles for the pair
(f,\widehat{f})
using a fundamental identity for the STFT. Also, Gröchenig in
[
10
]
, studied several uncertainty principles for time-frequency representations. Over the years, discovering new mathematical formulations of the uncertainty principle for the STFT have drawn significant attention among many researchers, see for example
[
3
,
21
,
9
,
14
]
and the references therein. However, upto our knowledge, quantitative uncertainty principles have not been studied for the STFT on the phase space
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
. In this paper, we attempt to study several quantitative uncertainty principles for the STFT on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
.

It is natural to ask whether uncertainty principles involving
f
and
\widehat{f}
possess analogous statements in terms of the STFT. A few methods for transferring localization statements for Fourier pairs to corresponding statements about localization of time-frequency distributions were given in
[
10
]
. In some cases, the extra rotational symmetry of the plane provides more refined information than does separate consideration of
f
and
\hat{f}
. The motivation to study quantitative uncertainty principles for the STFT on the phase space
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
arises from the classical uncertainty principles for the STFT and the remarkable contribution of this transform in time-frequency analysis (see
[
9
]
). Time-frequency localization operators are a mathematical tool to define a restriction of functions to a region in the time-frequency plane to extract time-frequency features. The role of these operators is to localize a signal simultaneously in time and frequency domains, this can be seen as the uncertainty principle. Recent works in localization operators on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
(see
[
6
]
) motivated us to study uncertainty principles for the STFT on the phase space
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
. The study of operators defined by phase space functions is sometimes called microlocal analysis. We hope that the study of uncertainty principles for the STFT on the phase space
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
makes a significant impact in microlocal analysis.

The main aim of this paper is to study a few uncertainty principles related to the STFT on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
. More preciously, we prove the uncertainty principle for orthonormal sequences, Donoho–Stark’s uncertainty principle, Benedicks-type uncertainty principle, Heisenberg-type uncertainty principle and local uncertainty inequality for the STFT on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
. We study the version of Donoho–Stark’s uncertainty principle and show that the STFT cannot be concentrated in any small set. Also, we obtain an estimate for the size of the essential support of this transform under the condition that the STFT of a non-zero function is time-frequency concentrated on a measurable set. Then, we investigate the Benedicks-type uncertainty principle and show that the STFT cannot be concentrated inside a set of measures arbitrarily small. Further, we study the Heisenberg-type uncertainty inequality for a general magnitude and provide the result related to the
L^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
-mass of the STFT outside sets of finite measure. Finally, we obtain the Heisenberg-type uncertainty inequality using the
k
-entropy and study the localization of the
k
-entropy of the STFT on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we present some essential properties related to the STFT on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
. Some different types of uncertainty principles associated with the STFT are provided in Section
3
. In particular, we prove the uncertainty principle for orthonormal sequences, Donoho–Stark’s uncertainty principle, Benedicks-type uncertainty principle, Heisenberg-type uncertainty principle and local uncertainty inequality for the STFT on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
. We conclude the paper with the Heisenberg-type uncertainty inequality using the
k
-entropy of this transform.

2.
Short-time Fourier transform on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}

The Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right)
on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is the space of rapidly decreasing functions
g:\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, i.e.
g\in\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right)
if for any
M<\infty
there exists a constant
C_{g,M}
such that

|g(k)|\leq C_{g,M}(1+|k|)^{-M},\quad\text{ for all }k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

The topology on
\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right)
is given by the seminorms
p_{j}
, where
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}={\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
and
p_{j}(g):=\sup\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}(1+|k|)^{j}|g(k)|
. The space
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right)
of all continuous linear functionals on
\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right)
is called the space of tempered distributions.

Fix
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
,
w\in{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
and
f\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
. For
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
, the translation operator
T_{k}
is defined by
T_{k}f(m)=f(m-k)
and the modulation operator
M_{w}
is defined by
M_{w}f(m)=e^{2\pi iw\cdot m}f(m)
. Let
g\in\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right)
be a fixed window function. Then, the STFT of a function
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right)
with respect to
g
is defined to be the function on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
given by

V_{g}f(m,w)=\left\langle f,M_{w}T_{m}g\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}f(k)\overline{M_{w}T_{m}g(k)}=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}f(k)\overline{g(k-m)}e^{-2\pi iw\cdot k}.

For
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
, we define
\tilde{g}(k)=g(-k)
. Then, we can write
V_{g}f
as a convolution on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}

V_{g}f(m,w)=e^{-2\pi iw\cdot m}\left(f*M_{w}\overline{\tilde{g}}\right)(m).

For
1\leq p<\infty
, we define
L^{p}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)
to be the space of all measurable functions
H
on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
such that

\|H\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)}^{p}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}|H(k,w)|^{p}\;dw<\infty.

Next, we present some basic properties of the STFT on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
. These are analogues properties of the STFT for functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(see
[
9
]
).

Proposition 2.1
.

(1)

(
Orthogonality relation
)
For every
f_{1},f_{2},g_{1},g_{2}\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, we have

\left\langle V_{g_{1}}f_{1},V_{g_{2}}f_{2}\right\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}=\langle f_{1},f_{2}\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\langle g_{2},g_{1}\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})},

(2.1)

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}
denotes the inner product of
L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
.

(2)

(
Plancherel’s formula
)
Let
g\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
be a non-zero window function. Then for every
f\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, we have

\left\|V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}=\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

(2.2)

(3)

(
Inversion formula
)
Let
g,\gamma\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
and
\langle g,\gamma\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\neq 0
. Then for every
f\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, we have

f=\frac{1}{\langle\gamma,g\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}}\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}V_{g}f(m,w)\;M_{w}T_{m}\gamma\;dw.

Proof.

(1) Note that we can write
V_{g}f(m,w)=\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}(f\cdot T_{m}\overline{g})(w)
. Using Parseval’s formula, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\langle V_{g_{1}}f_{1},V_{g_{2}}f_{2}\right\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}V_{g_{1}}f_{1}(m,w)\;\overline{V_{g_{2}}f_{2}(m,w)}\;dw

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}(f_{1}\cdot T_{m}\overline{g}_{1})(w)\;\overline{\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}(f_{2}\cdot T_{m}\overline{g}_{2})(w)}\;dw

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}(f_{1}\cdot T_{m}\overline{g}_{1})(k)\;\overline{(f_{2}\cdot T_{m}\overline{g}_{2})(k)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}f_{1}(k)\;\overline{g_{1}(k-m)}\;\overline{f_{2}(k)}\;g_{2}(k-m)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}f_{1}(k)\;\overline{f_{2}(k)}\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}g_{2}(m)\;\overline{g_{1}(m)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle f_{1},f_{2}\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\langle g_{2},g_{1}\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

(2) By taking
g_{1}=g_{2}=g
and
f_{1}=f_{2}=f
in the relation (
2.1
), we obtain the Plancherel formula (
2.2
).

(3) Since
V_{g}f\in L^{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)
, the integral

\tilde{f}=\frac{1}{\langle\gamma,g\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}}\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}V_{g}f(m,w)\;M_{w}T_{m}\gamma\;dw

is a well-defined function in
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
. Also, for any
h\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, using the orthogonality relations, we obtain that

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{f},h\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\langle\gamma,g\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}}\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}V_{g}f(m,w)\;\overline{\left\langle h,M_{w}T_{m}\gamma\right\rangle}_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;dw

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\langle\gamma,g\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}}\left\langle V_{g}f,V_{\gamma}h\right\rangle_{L^{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)}=\langle f,h\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

Thus
\tilde{f}=f
, and we obtain the inversion formula.
∎

Let
g\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
be a non-zero window function. Then for every
f\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we get

\left|V_{g}f(m,w)\right|=\left|\left\langle f,M_{w}T_{m}g\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\right|\leq\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\left\|M_{w}T_{m}g\right\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\leq\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\left\|g\right\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

Therefore,

\left\|V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\leq\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

(2.3)

Also, using Plancherel’s formula (
2.2
), relation (
2.3
) and the Riesz–Thorin interpolation theorem (see
[
20
]
), we obtain that the function
V_{g}f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
, for
p\in[2,\infty)
and

\left\|V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\leq\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

(2.4)

We define the measure
\nu\otimes\mu
on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
by

d(\nu\otimes\mu)(m,w)=d\nu(m)\;d\mu(w),

(2.5)

where
d\nu(m)
is the counting measure and
d\mu(w)
is the Lebesgue measure
dw
.

Next, we present a result on reproducing kernel Hilbert space for the STFT, which is the analogue properties of the STFT for functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(see
[
9
]
).

Theorem 2.2

(Reproducing kernel Hilbert space)
.

The space
V_{g}\left(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})\right)
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space in
L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
with kernel function
K_{g}
defined by

K_{g}((m^{\prime},w^{\prime});(m,w))=\frac{1}{\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}}V_{g}\left(M_{w}T_{m}g\right)(m^{\prime},w^{\prime}).

(2.6)

Moreover, the kernel is pointwise bounded

\left|K_{g}((m^{\prime},w^{\prime});(m,w))\right|\leq 1,\quad\text{for all }\;(m,w),\;(m^{\prime},w^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}.

Proof.

Using the relation (
2.1
), we obtain that

\displaystyle V_{g}f(m,w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle f,M_{w}T_{m}g\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}}\left\langle V_{g}f,V_{g}(M_{w}T_{m}g)\right\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}}\sum_{m^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}V_{g}f(m^{\prime},w^{\prime})\;\overline{V_{g}(M_{w}T_{m}g)(m^{\prime},w^{\prime})}\;dw^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}}\sum_{m^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}V_{g}f(m^{\prime},w^{\prime})\;\overline{\left\langle M_{w}T_{m}g,M_{w^{\prime}}T_{m^{\prime}}g\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}}\;dw^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle V_{g}f,K_{g}((\cdot,\cdot);(m,w))\right\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})},

where
K_{g}
is the function on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
given by

K_{g}((m^{\prime},w^{\prime});(m,w))=\frac{1}{\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}}V_{g}\left(M_{w}T_{m}g\right)(m^{\prime},w^{\prime}).

Using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and relation (
2.1
), we get

\displaystyle|V_{g}f(m,w)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle V_{g}f,K_{g}((\cdot,\cdot);(m,w))\right\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}}\left|\left\langle V_{g}f,V_{g}(M_{w}T_{m}g)\right\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}}\left|\langle f,M_{w}T_{m}g\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\right|\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\|M_{w}T_{m}g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

Now, using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, for every

(m,w),\;(m^{\prime},w^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
we get

\displaystyle\left|K_{g}((m^{\prime},w^{\prime});(m,w))\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}}\left|V_{g}\left(M_{w}T_{m}g\right)(m^{\prime},w^{\prime})\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}}\left\|M_{w}T_{m}g\right\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\left\|M_{w^{\prime}}T_{m^{\prime}}g\right\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}=1.

Also, for every
(m,w)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
, by using Plancherel’s formula (
2.2
), we obtain

\|K_{g}((\cdot,\cdot);(m,w))\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\leq 1.

This proves that the kernel
K_{g}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
and is bounded.
∎

For
1\leq p<\infty
, the set of all measurable functions
F
on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
such that

\|F\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})}^{p}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}|F(k)|^{p}<\infty

is denoted by
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
. We define
L^{p}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})
to be the set of all measurable functions
f
on
{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
for which

\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})}^{p}=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}|f(w)|^{p}\;dw<\infty.

Next, we define the Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}F
of
F\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
to be the function on
{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
by

\left(\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}F\right)(w)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}e^{-2\pi ik\cdot w}F(k),\quad w\in{\mathbb{T}}^{n}.

Let
f
be a function on
{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
, then we define the Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}f
of
f
to be the function on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
by

\left(\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}f\right)(k)=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}e^{2\pi ik\cdot w}f(w)\;dw,\quad k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

Note that
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}:\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})\rightarrow L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})
is a surjective isomorphism.
Also,
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}=\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}^{-1}=\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}^{*}
and
\left\|\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}F\right\|_{L^{2}\left({\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)}=\|F\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})},

F\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
.

3.
Uncertainty principles for the STFT on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}

In this section, we study various uncertainty principles for the STFT on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
. Mainly, we establish the uncertainty principle for orthonormal sequences, Donoho–Stark’s uncertainty principle, Benedicks-type uncertainty principle, Heisenberg-type uncertainty principle and local uncertainty inequality for this transform on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
. We begin with the uncertainty principle for orthonormal sequences.

3.1.
Uncertainty principle for orthonormal sequences

Here, we obtain the uncertainty principle for orthonormal sequences associated with the STFT. We first define the following orthogonal projections:

(1)

Let
P_{g}
be the orthogonal projection from
L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
onto
V_{g}\left(\ell^{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\right)\right)
and
\operatorname{Im}P_{g}
denotes the range of
P_{g}
. Also,
P_{g}
is called the orthogonal projection with respect to
g
.

(2)

Let
P_{\Sigma}
be the orthogonal projection on
L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
defined by

\displaystyle P_{\Sigma}F=\chi_{\Sigma}F,\quad F\in L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}),

(3.1)

where
\Sigma\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
,
\chi_{\Sigma}
is the characteristic function on
\Sigma
, and
\operatorname{Im}P_{\Sigma}
is the range of
P_{\Sigma}
. The operator
P_{\Sigma}
is called the orthogonal projection on
\Sigma
.

Also, we define

\left\|P_{\Sigma}P_{g}\right\|_{op}=\sup\left\{\left\|P_{\Sigma}P_{g}(F)\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}:F\in L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}),\|F\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}=1\right\}.

In the following, we obtain an estimate for
\left\|P_{\Sigma}P_{g}\right\|_{op}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
g\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
be a non-zero window function. Then for any
\Sigma\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
of finite measure
(\nu\otimes\mu)(\Sigma)<\infty
, the operator
P_{\Sigma}P_{g}
is a Hilbert–Schmidt operator. Also, we have

\left\|P_{\Sigma}P_{g}\right\|_{op}^{2}\leq(\nu\otimes\mu)(\Sigma).

Proof.

Since
P_{g}
is a projection onto a reproducing kernel Hilbert space, for any
F\in L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
, the orthogonal projection
P_{g}
can be expressed as

P_{g}(F)(m,w)=\sum_{m^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}F(m^{\prime},w^{\prime})\;K_{g}((m^{\prime},w^{\prime});(m,w))\;d\mu(w^{\prime}),

where
K_{g}((m^{\prime},w^{\prime});(m,w))
is given by (
2.6
). Using the relation (
3.1
), we get

P_{\Sigma}P_{g}(F)(m,w)=\sum_{m^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\chi_{\Sigma}(m,w)\;F(m^{\prime},w^{\prime})\;K_{g}((m^{\prime},w^{\prime});(m,w))\;d\mu(w^{\prime}).

Hence, the operator
P_{\Sigma}P_{g}
is an integral operator with kernel

K((m^{\prime},w^{\prime});(m,w))=\chi_{\Sigma}(m,w)\;K_{g}((m^{\prime},w^{\prime});(m,w)).

Let
\Sigma=Z\times T
, where
Z
and
T
are some arbitrary sets in
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
and
{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
respectively. Using the relation (
2.6
) and Plancherel’s formula (
2.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|P_{\Sigma}P_{g}\right\|_{HS}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\left(\sum_{m^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\left|K((m^{\prime},w^{\prime});(m,w))\right|^{2}\;d\mu(w^{\prime})\right)d\mu(w)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\left(\sum_{m^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\left|\chi_{\Sigma}(m,w)\right|^{2}\left|K_{g}((m^{\prime},w^{\prime});(m,w))\right|^{2}d\mu(w^{\prime})\right)d\mu(w)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\|g\|^{4}_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}}\sum_{m\in Z}\int_{T}\left(\sum_{m^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\left|V_{g}\left(M_{w}T_{m}g\right)(m^{\prime},w^{\prime})\right|^{2}d\mu(w^{\prime})\right)d\mu(w)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|g\|^{4}_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}}{\|g\|^{4}_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}}\;(\nu\otimes\mu)(Z\times T)

\displaystyle=(\nu\otimes\mu)(\Sigma).

Thus, the operator
P_{\Sigma}P_{g}
is a Hilbert–Schmidt operator. Now, the proof follows from the fact that
\left\|P_{\Sigma}P_{g}\right\|_{op}\leq\left\|P_{\Sigma}P_{g}\right\|_{HS}
.
∎

Next, we establish the uncertainty principle for orthonormal sequences associated with the STFT on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\left\{\phi_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be an orthonormal sequence in
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
and
g\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
be a non-zero window function with
\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}=1
. Then for any
\Sigma\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
of finite measure
(\nu\otimes\mu)(\Sigma)<\infty,
we have

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(1-\left\|\chi_{\Sigma^{c}}V_{g}\phi_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\right)\leq(\nu\otimes\mu)(\Sigma),

for every
N\in\mathbb{N}.

Proof.

Let
\left\{e_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be an orthonormal basis for
L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
. Since
P_{\Sigma}P_{g}
is a Hilbert–Schmidt operator, from Theorem
3.1
, we obtain

tr\left(P_{g}P_{\Sigma}P_{g}\right)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\langle P_{g}P_{\Sigma}P_{g}e_{n},e_{n}\right\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}=\left\|P_{\Sigma}P_{g}\right\|_{HS}^{2}\leq(\nu\otimes\mu)(\Sigma),

where
tr\left(P_{g}P_{\Sigma}P_{g}\right)
denotes the trace of the operator
P_{g}P_{\Sigma}P_{g}
. Since
\left\{\phi_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be an orthonormal sequence in
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, from the orthogonality relation (
2.1
), we obtain that
\{V_{g}\phi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is also an orthonormal sequence in
L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
. Therefore

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left\langle P_{\Sigma}V_{g}\phi_{n},V_{g}\phi_{n}\right\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left\langle P_{g}P_{\Sigma}P_{g}V_{g}\phi_{n},V_{g}\phi_{n}\right\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\leq tr\left(P_{g}P_{\Sigma}P_{g}\right).

Thus, we have

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left\langle P_{\Sigma}V_{g}\phi_{n},V_{g}\phi_{n}\right\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\leq(\nu\otimes\mu)(\Sigma).

Also, for any
n
with
1\leq n\leq N
, using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we get

\displaystyle\left\langle P_{\Sigma}V_{g}\phi_{n},V_{g}\phi_{n}\right\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}=1-\left\langle P_{\Sigma^{c}}V_{g}\phi_{n},V_{g}\phi_{n}\right\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\geq 1-\left\|\chi_{\Sigma^{c}}V_{g}\phi_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(1-\left\|\chi_{\Sigma^{c}}V_{g}\phi_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left\langle P_{\Sigma}V_{g}\phi_{n},V_{g}\phi_{n}\right\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\leq(\nu\otimes\mu)(\Sigma).

This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

3.2.
Donoho–Stark’s uncertainty principle for the STFT

In this subsection, we investigate the version of Donoho–Stark’s uncertainty principle for the STFT on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
. In particular, we study the case where
f
and
V_{g}f
are close to zero outside measurable sets. We start with the following result.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
f\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
such that
f\neq 0
and
g\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
be a non-zero window function. Then for any
\Sigma=Z\times T\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
, where
Z
and
T
are some arbitrary sets in
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
and
{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
respectively, and
\varepsilon\geq 0
such that

\sum_{m\in Z}\int_{T}\left|V_{g}f(m,w)\right|^{2}\;d\mu(w)\geq(1-\varepsilon)\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2},

we have

(\nu\otimes\mu)(\Sigma)\geq 1-\varepsilon.

Proof.

Using the relation (
2.3
), we get

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon)\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}\leq\sum_{m\in Z}\int_{T}\left|V_{g}f(m,w)\right|^{2}\;d\mu(w)

\displaystyle\leq\left\|V_{g}f\right\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)}\;(\nu\otimes\mu)(Z\times T)

\displaystyle\leq(\nu\otimes\mu)(\Sigma)\;\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}.

Therefore,
(\nu\otimes\mu)(\Sigma)\geq 1-\varepsilon.

∎

In the following proposition, we show that the STFT cannot be concentrated in any small set.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
g\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
be a non-zero window function.
Then for any function
f\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
and for any
\Sigma\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
such that
(\nu\otimes\mu)(\Sigma)<1
, we have

\left\|\chi_{\Sigma^{c}}V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)}\geq\sqrt{1-(\nu\otimes\mu)(\Sigma)}\;\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

Proof.

For any
f\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, using the relation (
2.3
), we get

\displaystyle\left\|V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\chi_{\Sigma}V_{g}f+\chi_{\Sigma^{c}}V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\chi_{\Sigma}V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)}^{2}+\left\|\chi_{\Sigma^{c}}V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(\nu\otimes\mu)(\Sigma)\left\|V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)}^{2}+\left\|\chi_{\Sigma^{c}}V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(\nu\otimes\mu)(\Sigma)\;\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}\;\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}+\left\|\chi_{\Sigma^{c}}V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)}^{2}.

Thus, using Plancherel’s formula (
2.2
), we obtain

\left\|\chi_{\Sigma^{c}}V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)}\geq\sqrt{1-(\nu\otimes\mu)(\Sigma)}\;\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

∎

Definition 3.5
.

Let
E
be a measurable subset of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
and
0\leq\varepsilon_{E}<1
. Then we say that a function
f\in\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}),1\leq p\leq 2,
is
\varepsilon_{E}
-concentrated on
E
in
\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
-norm, if

\left\|\chi_{E^{c}}f\right\|_{\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\leq\varepsilon_{E}\|f\|_{\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

If
\varepsilon_{E}=0
, then
E
contains the support of
f
.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
\Sigma
be a measurable subset of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
and
0\leq\varepsilon_{\Sigma}<1
. Let
f,g\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
be two non-zero functions. We say that
V_{g}f
is
\varepsilon_{\Sigma}
-time-frequency concentrated on
\Sigma
, if

\left\|\chi_{\Sigma^{c}}V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)}\leq\varepsilon_{\Sigma}\left\|V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)}.

If
V_{g}f
is
\varepsilon_{\Sigma}
-time-frequency concentrated on
\Sigma
, then in the following, we obtain an estimate for the size of the essential support of the STFT on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
f\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
such that
f\neq 0
and
g\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
be a non-zero window function. Let
\Sigma\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
such that
(\nu\otimes\mu)(\Sigma)<\infty
and
\varepsilon_{\Sigma}\geq 0
. If

V_{g}f
is
\varepsilon_{\Sigma}
-time-frequency concentrated on
\Sigma,
then

(\nu\otimes\mu)(\Sigma)\geq(1-\varepsilon_{\Sigma}^{2}).

Proof.

Since
V_{g}f
is
\varepsilon_{\Sigma}
-time-frequency concentrated on
\Sigma
, using Plancherel’s formula (
2.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}\;\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}=\left\|V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\chi_{\Sigma^{c}}V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}^{2}+\left\|\chi_{\Sigma}V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{\Sigma}^{2}\left\|V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)}^{2}+\left\|\chi_{\Sigma}V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}^{2}.

Hence, using the relation (
2.3
), we get

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon_{\Sigma}^{2})\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}\;\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\chi_{\Sigma}V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}^{2}

(3.2)

\displaystyle\leq\left\|V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)}^{2}\;(\nu\otimes\mu)(\Sigma)

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}\;\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}\;(\nu\otimes\mu)(\Sigma),

which completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
f\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
such that
f\neq 0
,
g\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
be a non-zero window function,
\Sigma\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
such that
(\nu\otimes\mu)(\Sigma)<\infty
, and
\varepsilon_{\Sigma}\geq 0
. If
V_{g}f
is
\varepsilon_{\Sigma}
-time-frequency concentrated on
\Sigma
, then for every
p>2
, we have

(\nu\otimes\mu)(\Sigma)\geq(1-\varepsilon_{\Sigma}^{2})^{\frac{p}{p-2}}.

Proof.

Since
V_{g}f
is
\varepsilon_{\Sigma}
-time-frequency concentrated on
\Sigma
, from (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon_{\Sigma}^{2})\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}\;\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\chi_{\Sigma}V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}^{2}.

Again, aplying Hölder’s inequality for the conjugate exponents
\frac{p}{2}
and
\frac{p}{p-2}
, we get

\displaystyle\left\|\chi_{\Sigma}V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}^{2}((\nu\otimes\mu)(\Sigma))^{\frac{p-2}{p}}.

Now, using the relation (
2.4
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\chi_{\Sigma}V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\;\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}((\nu\otimes\mu)(\Sigma))^{\frac{p-2}{p}}.

Hence,

(\nu\otimes\mu)(\Sigma)\geq(1-\varepsilon_{\Sigma}^{2})^{\frac{p}{p-2}}.

∎

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
f\in\ell^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
such that
\|V_{g}f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}=1
,
g\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
be a non-zero window function, and
\Sigma\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
such that
(\nu\otimes\mu)(\Sigma)<\infty
. Let
E\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
such that
\nu(E)<\infty
. If
f
is
\varepsilon_{E}
-concentrated on
E
in
\ell^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
-norm and
V_{g}f
is
\varepsilon_{\Sigma}
-time-frequency concentrated on
\Sigma
, then

\nu(E)\geq(1-\varepsilon_{E})^{2}\;\|f\|_{\ell^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}\;\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2},

and

\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}\;\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}\;(\nu\otimes\mu)(\Sigma)\geq(1-\varepsilon_{\Sigma}^{2}).

In particular,

\nu(E)~(\nu\otimes\mu)(\Sigma)\geq(1-\varepsilon_{E})^{2}\;(1-\varepsilon_{\Sigma}^{2}).

Proof.

Since
V_{g}f
is
\varepsilon_{\Sigma}
-time-frequency concentrated on
\Sigma
, from (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon_{\Sigma}^{2})\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}\;\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\chi_{\Sigma}V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}^{2}.

Since
\|V_{g}f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}=1,
using the relation (
2.3
) and Plancherel’s formula (
2.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon_{\Sigma}^{2})\leq\left\|\chi_{\Sigma}V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}^{2}~(\nu\otimes\mu)(\Sigma)

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}~\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}\;(\nu\otimes\mu)(\Sigma).

(3.3)

Similarly, since
f
is
\varepsilon_{E}
-concentrated on
E
in
\ell^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
-norm, using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and the fact that

\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}=1
, we get

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon_{E})\|f\|_{\ell^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\leq\left\|\chi_{E}f\right\|_{\ell^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\nu(E)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{\nu(E)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\left\|g\right\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}}.

(3.4)

Finally, from (
3.2
) and (
3.4
), we have

\nu(E)~(\nu\otimes\mu)(\Sigma)\;\|f\|_{\ell^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}\geq\nu(E)~(\nu\otimes\mu)(\Sigma)\;\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}\geq(1-\varepsilon_{E})^{2}\;(1-\varepsilon_{\Sigma}^{2})\;\|f\|_{\ell^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}.

Hence,

\nu(E)~(\nu\otimes\mu)(\Sigma)\geq(1-\varepsilon_{E})^{2}\;(1-\varepsilon_{\Sigma}^{2}).

This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Let

|(m,w)|=\sqrt{|m|^{2}+|w|^{2}}=\sqrt{|m_{1}|^{2}+\cdots+|m_{n}|^{2}+|w_{1}|^{2}+\cdots+|w_{n}|^{2}}.

For
r>0
, let
B_{r}=\{(m,w)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}:~|(m,w)|\leq r\}
be the ball with centre at origin and radius
r
in
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
. We have the following estimate for the measure
(\nu\otimes\mu)(B_{r})
of
B_{r}
on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
:

\displaystyle(\nu\otimes\mu)(B_{r})

\displaystyle=\sum_{|m|\leq r}\int_{|w|^{2}\leq r^{2}-|m|^{2}}dw=2\sum_{|m|\leq r}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{r^{2}-|m|^{2}}}dw=2\sum_{|m|\leq r}\sqrt{r^{2}-|m|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2r\;\text{Card}(\{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}:|m|\leq r\}).

Notice that the cardinality of the set
\{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}:|m|\leq r\}
is the number of integer lattice points within a sphere centered at the origin and with the radius
r
, which is famous Gauss’s circle problem (see
[
4
,
13
]
). This number is approximated by the area of the sphere, so the problem is to accurately bound the error term describing how the number of points differs from the area. Some partial results on the upper bound of the error term are available till now. Using the upper bound of the error term, one can obtain an estimate for the cardinality of the set
\{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}:|m|\leq r\}
.

For
s>0
, we define the time-frequency dispersion on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
by

\rho_{s}(V_{g}f)=\left(\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}|(m,w)|^{s}\;|V_{g}f(m,w)|^{2}\;dw\right)^{1/s}.

In the following proposition, we show that the STFT of an orthonormal sequence cannot be
\varepsilon_{B_{r}}
-time-frequency concentrated on
B_{r}
with the measure
(\nu\otimes\mu)(B_{r})<\infty
.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
g
be a window function with
\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}=1
and
B_{r}
be the ball in
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
. Let
\varepsilon_{B_{r}}
and
r
be two positive real numbers such that
\varepsilon_{B_{r}}<1
. Let
K
be a non-empty subset of
\mathbb{N}
and
\{\phi_{k}\}_{k\in K}
be an orthonormal sequence in
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
. If
V_{g}\phi_{k}
is
\varepsilon_{B_{r}}
-time-frequency concentrated on
B_{r}
for every
k\in K
, then
K
is finite and

\mathrm{Card}(K)\leq\frac{(\nu\otimes\mu)(B_{r})}{(1-\varepsilon_{B_{r}})}.

Proof.

Let
M\subset K
be a non-empty finite subset, then from Theorem
3.2
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in M}\left(1-\left\|\chi_{B_{r}^{c}}V_{g}\phi_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\right)\leq(\nu\otimes\mu)(B_{r}).

(3.5)

Also, for every
k\in K
,
V_{g}\phi_{k}
is
\varepsilon_{B_{r}}
-time-frequency concentrated on
B_{r}
. Therefore

\displaystyle\left\|\chi_{B_{r}^{c}}V_{g}\phi_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)}\leq\varepsilon_{B_{r}}\left\|V_{g}\phi_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)}=\varepsilon_{B_{r}}.

(3.6)

Hence, by combining relations (
3.5
) and (
3.6
), we get

\text{Card}(M)\leq\frac{(\nu\otimes\mu)(B_{r})}{(1-\varepsilon_{B_{r}})}.

Since
\dfrac{(\nu\otimes\mu)(B_{r})}{(1-\varepsilon_{B_{r}})}
is independent of
M
, therefore
\text{Card}(K)
is finite and

\text{Card}(K)\leq\dfrac{(\nu\otimes\mu)(B_{r})}{(1-\varepsilon_{B_{r}})}.

∎

Corollary 3.11
.

Let
s>0
and
g
be a window function such that
\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}=1
. Let
K
be a non-empty subset of
\mathbb{N}
and
\{\phi_{k}\}_{k\in K}
be an orthonormal sequence in
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
. If the sequence
\{\rho_{s}(V_{g}\phi_{k})\}_{k\in K}
is uniformly bounded by
A
, then
K
is finite and
\mathrm{Card}(K)\leq 2\;(\nu\otimes\mu)(B_{A2^{\frac{2}{s}}})
.

Proof.

Assume that
\rho_{s}(V_{g}\phi_{k})\leq A
, for every
k\in K
. Let
r=4^{\frac{1}{s}}A
. Then

\displaystyle\left\|\chi_{B_{r}^{c}}V_{g}\phi_{k}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)}

\displaystyle=\left\||(m,w)|^{-\frac{s}{2}}\;|(m,w)|^{\frac{s}{2}}\;\chi_{B_{r}^{c}}V_{g}\phi_{k}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4A^{s}}\left\||(m,w)|^{\frac{s}{2}}\;\chi_{B_{r}^{c}}V_{g}\phi_{k}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4A^{s}}\times A^{s}=\frac{1}{4}\|\phi_{k}\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}=\frac{1}{4}\|V_{g}\phi_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}.

Thus, we have

\left\|\chi_{B_{r}^{c}}V_{g}\phi_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|V_{g}\phi_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)}.

Hence, for every
k\in K
,
V_{g}\phi_{k}
is
\frac{1}{2}
-time-frequency concentrated on
B_{A2^{\frac{2}{s}}}
. Now, using Proposition
3.10
, we obtain that
\text{Card}(K)
is finite and
\text{Card}(K)\leq 2\;(\nu\otimes\mu)(B_{A2^{\frac{2}{s}}})
.
∎

3.3.
Benedicks-type uncertainty principle for the STFT

Here, we study Benedicks-type uncertainty principle for the STFT on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
.
First, we show that this transform cannot be concentrated inside a set of measure arbitrarily small.

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
g\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
be a non-zero window function and
\Sigma\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
. If
\left\|P_{\Sigma}P_{g}\right\|<1
, then there exists a constant
c(\Sigma,g)>0
such that for every
f\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}),
we have

\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\leq c(\Sigma,g)\left\|\chi_{\Sigma^{c}}V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)}.

Proof.

Since
P_{\Sigma}
is an orthogonal projection on
L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
, for any
F\in L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
, we get

\|P_{g}(F)\|_{L^{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)}^{2}=\|P_{\Sigma}P_{g}(F)\|_{L^{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)}^{2}+\|P_{\Sigma^{c}}P_{g}(F)\|_{L^{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)}^{2}.

Again, using the identity
P_{\Sigma}P_{g}(F)=P_{\Sigma}P_{g}\cdot P_{g}(F)
, we get

\|P_{\Sigma}P_{g}(F)\|_{L^{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)}^{2}\leq\|P_{\Sigma}P_{g}\|^{2}\|P_{g}(F)\|_{L^{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)}^{2}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\|P_{g}(F)\|_{L^{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{1-\|P_{\Sigma}P_{g}\|^{2}}\|P_{\Sigma^{c}}P_{g}(F)\|_{L^{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)}^{2}.

(3.7)

Since
P_{g}
is an orthogonal projection from
L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
onto
V_{g}\left(\ell^{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\right)\right)
, for any
f\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, using the relation (
3.7
) and Plancherel’s formula (
2.2
), we get

\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\|P_{\Sigma}P_{g}\|^{2}}}\left\|\chi_{\Sigma^{c}}V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)}.

The desired result follows by choosing the constant
c(\Sigma,g)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\|P_{\Sigma}P_{g}\|^{2}}}
.
∎

In the following, we obtain Benedicks-type uncertainty principle for the STFT on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
.

Theorem 3.13
.

Let
g\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
be a non-zero window function such that

\mu\left(\left\{\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}(g)\neq 0\right\}\right)<\infty.

Then for any
\Sigma\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
such that for almost every
w\in{\mathbb{T}}^{n},\;\sum\limits_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\chi_{\Sigma}(m,w)<\infty
, we have

V_{g}\left(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})\right)\cap\operatorname{Im}P_{\Sigma}=\{0\}.

Proof.

Let
F\in V_{g}\left(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})\right)\cap\operatorname{Im}P_{\Sigma}
be a non-trivial function. Then there exists a function
f\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
such that
F=V_{g}f
and
\hbox{supp\,}F\subset\Sigma
. For any
w\in{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
, we consider the function

F_{w}(m)=V_{g}f(m,w),\quad m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}.

Then, we get
\hbox{supp\,}F_{w}\subset\{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}:(m,w)\in\Sigma\}.
Since for almost every
w\in{\mathbb{T}}^{n},\sum\limits_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\chi_{\Sigma}(m,w)<\infty
, we have
\nu\left(\hbox{supp\,}F_{w}\right)<\infty
. Since
V_{g}f(m,w)=(M_{-w}f*\overline{\tilde{g}})(m)
, we get

\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}(F_{w})=\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}(M_{-w}f)\;\;\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}(\overline{\tilde{g}})\quad\;a.e.

Hence,

\hbox{supp\,}\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}(F_{w})\subset\hbox{supp\,}\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}(\overline{\tilde{g}}),

and from the hypothesis, we get
\mu\left(\left\{\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}(F_{w})\neq 0\right\}\right)<\infty
. From Benedicks–Amrein–Berthier’s uncertainty principle
[
15
]
, we conclude that, for every
w\in{\mathbb{T}}^{n},\;F(\cdot,w)=0
, which eventually implies that
F=0.

∎

Remark 3.14
.

Let
g\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
be a non-zero window function such that

\mu\left(\left\{\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}(g)\neq 0\right\}\right)<\infty.

Then, for any non-zero function
f\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, we have
(\nu\otimes\mu)(\hbox{supp\,}V_{g}f)=\infty
, i.e., the support of
V_{g}f
cannot be of finite measure. If
(\nu\otimes\mu)(\hbox{supp\,}V_{g}f)<\infty
, then from Theorem
3.13
, we get
f\equiv 0
or
g\equiv 0
.

Proposition 3.15
.

Let
g\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
be a non-zero window function such that

\mu\left(\left\{\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}(g)\neq 0\right\}\right)<\infty.

Let
\Sigma\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
such that
(\nu\otimes\mu)(\Sigma)<\infty
, then there exists a constant
c(\Sigma,g)>0
such that

\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\leq c(\Sigma,g)\left\|\chi_{\Sigma^{c}}V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)}.

Proof.

Assume that

\left\|P_{\Sigma}P_{g}(F)\right\|_{L^{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)}=\|F\|_{L^{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)},\;F\in L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}).

Since
P_{\Sigma}
and
P_{g}
are orthogonal projections, we obtain
P_{\Sigma}(F)=P_{g}(F)=F.
Again, since
(\nu\otimes\mu)(\Sigma)<\infty
, for almost every
w\in{\mathbb{T}}^{n},\;\;\sum\limits_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\chi_{\Sigma}(m,w)<\infty
and from Theorem
3.13
, we get
F=0.
Hence, for
F\neq 0
, we obtain

\left\|P_{\Sigma}P_{g}(F)\right\|_{L^{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)}<\|F\|_{L^{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)}.

Since
P_{\Sigma}P_{g}
is a Hilbert–Schmidt operator, we obtain that the largest eigenvalue
|\lambda|
of the operator
P_{\Sigma}P_{g}
satisfy
|\lambda|<1
and
\left\|P_{\Sigma}P_{g}\right\|=|\lambda|<1
. Finally, using Proposition
3.12
, we get the desired result.
∎

3.4.
Heisenberg-type uncertainty principle for the STFT

In this subsection, we study Heisenberg-type uncertainty inequality for the STFT on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
for general magnitude
s>0.
Indeed, we have the following result.

Theorem 3.16
.

Let
s>0
. Then there exists a constant
c(s)>0
such that for all
f,g\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, we have

\displaystyle\left\||m|^{s}V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}^{2}+\left\||w|^{s}V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}^{2}\geq c(s)\;\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}\;\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}.

Proof.

Let
r>0
and
B_{r}=\{(m,w)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}:~|(m,w)|<r\}
be the ball with centre at origin and radius
r
in
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
. Fix
\varepsilon_{0}\leq 1
small enough such that
(\nu\otimes\mu)(B_{\varepsilon_{0}})<1
. From Proposition
3.4
, we have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}\;\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\left[1-(\nu\otimes\mu)(B_{\varepsilon_{0}})\right]}{\sum\int}_{\{(m,w)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}:~|(m,w)|\geq\varepsilon_{0}\}}\left|V_{g}f(m,w)\right|^{2}~d\mu(w)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{0}^{2s}\left[1-(\nu\otimes\mu)(B_{\varepsilon_{0}})\right]}{\sum\int}_{\{(m,w)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}:~|(m,w)|\geq\varepsilon_{0}\}}|(m,w)|^{2s}\left|V_{g}f(m,w)\right|^{2}~d\mu(w)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{0}^{2s}\left[1-(\nu\otimes\mu)(B_{\varepsilon_{0}})\right]}\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}|(m,w)|^{2s}\left|V_{g}f(m,w)\right|^{2}~d\mu(w).

Consequently,

\displaystyle\left\||(m,w)|^{s}V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}^{2}\geq\varepsilon_{0}^{2s}\left[1-(\nu\otimes\mu)(B_{\varepsilon_{0}})\right]\;\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}\;\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}.

(3.8)

Finally, using the fact that
|a+b|^{s}\leq 2^{s}\left(|a|^{s}+|b|^{s}\right)
and from (
3.8
), we get

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{0}^{2s}\left[1-(\nu\otimes\mu)(B_{\varepsilon_{0}})\right]\;\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}\;\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\||(m,w)|^{s}V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{s}\left\||m|^{s}V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}^{2}+2^{s}\left\||w|^{s}V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}^{2}.

Hence, we get the desired result with
c(s)=\varepsilon_{0}^{2s}2^{-s}\left[1-(\nu\otimes\mu)(B_{\varepsilon_{0}})\right].

∎

3.5.
Local uncertainty inequality for the STFT

Here, we discuss results related to the
L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
-mass of the STFT outside sets of finite measure. Indeed, we establish the following result.

Theorem 3.17
.

Let
s>0
. Then there exists a constant
c(s)>0
such that for any
f,g\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
and any
\Sigma\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
such that
(\nu\otimes\mu)(\Sigma)<\infty
, we have

\left\|V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\Sigma\right)}\leq c(s)\left[(\nu\otimes\mu)(\Sigma)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\||(m,w)|^{s}V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}.

Proof.

Since
\|V_{g}f\|_{L^{2}\left(\Sigma\right)}\leq\|V_{g}f\|_{L^{\infty}\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\right)}~[(\nu\otimes\mu)(\Sigma)]^{\frac{1}{2}},

using the relation (
2.3
), we get

\left\|V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\Sigma\right)}\leq\left[(\nu\otimes\mu)(\Sigma)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\;\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}~\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

Thus, using inequality (
3.8
), we get

\displaystyle\left\|V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\Sigma\right)}\leq\frac{\left[(\nu\otimes\mu)(\Sigma)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\varepsilon_{0}^{s}\left[1-(\nu\otimes\mu)(B_{\varepsilon_{0}})\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left\||(m,w)|^{s}V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}.

This completes the proof of the theorem with
c(s)=\varepsilon_{0}^{-s}\left[1-(\nu\otimes\mu)(B_{\varepsilon_{0}})\right]^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

∎

In the following, we show that Theorem
3.17
gives a general form of Heisenberg-type inequality with a different constant.

Corollary 3.18
.

Let
s>0.
Then there exists a constant
c_{s}>0
such that, for all
f,g\in

\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, we have

\displaystyle\left\||(m,w)|^{s}V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}\geq c_{s}~\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

(3.9)

In particular,

\displaystyle\left\||m|^{s}V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}^{2}+\left\||w|^{s}V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}^{2}\geq\frac{c_{s}^{2}}{2^{s}}\;\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}\;\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}.

Proof.

Let
r>0
and
B_{r}=\{(m,w)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}:~|(m,w)|<r\}
be the ball with centre at origin and radius
r
in
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
. Using Plancherel’s formula (
2.2
) and Theorem
3.17
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}\;\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}=\left\|V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}^{2}=\left\|\chi_{B_{r}}V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}^{2}+\left\|\chi_{B_{r}^{c}}V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c(s)^{2}\left[(\nu\otimes\mu)(B_{r})\right]\left\||(m,w)|^{s}V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}^{2}+r^{-2s}\left\||(m,w)|^{s}V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}^{2}.

We get the inequality (
3.9
) by minimizing the right-hand side of the above inequality over
r>0
. Now, proceeding similarly as in the proof of Theorem

3.16
, we obtain

\displaystyle 2^{s}\left\||m|^{s}V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}^{2}+2^{s}\left\||w|^{s}V_{g}f\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})}^{2}\geq c_{s}^{2}\;\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}~\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2},

and this completes the proof.
∎

3.6.
Heisenberg-type uncertainty inequality via the
k
-entropy

In this subsection, we study the localization of the
k
-entropy of the STFT over the space
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
. We first need the following definition.

Definition 3.19
.

(1)

A probability density function
\rho
on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
is a non-negative measurable function on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
satisfying

\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\rho(m,w)~d\mu(w)=1.

(2)

Let
\rho
be a probability density function on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
. Then the
k
-entropy of
\rho
is defined by

E_{k}(\rho):=-\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\ln(\rho(m,w))~\rho(m,w)~d\mu(w),

whenever the integral on the right-hand side is well defined.

Now, we prove the main result of this subsection.

Theorem 3.20
.

Let
g\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
be a non-zero window function and
f\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
such that
f\neq 0
. Then, we have

\displaystyle E_{k}(|V_{g}f|^{2})\geq-2\ln\left(\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\right)\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}\;\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}.

(3.10)

Proof.

First, we assume that
\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}=\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}=1
. For any
(m,w)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
, using the relation (
2.3
), we get

|V_{g}f(m,w)|\leq\;\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}=1.

Consequently,
\ln(|V_{g}f|)\leq 0
and therefore
E_{k}\big(|V_{g}f|\big)\geq 0
. The desired inequality (
3.10
) holds trivially if the entropy
E_{k}\big(|V_{g}f|\big)
is infinite. Now, suppose that the entropy
E_{k}\big(|V_{g}f|\big)
is finite. Let
f
and
g
be two non-zero functions in
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
. We define

\phi=\frac{f}{\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}}~\text{ and }~\psi=\frac{g}{\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}}.

Then
\phi,~\psi\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
with
\|\phi\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}=\|\psi\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}=1
and therefore

\displaystyle E_{k}\big(|V_{\psi}\phi|\big)\geq 0.

(3.11)

Since
V_{\psi}\phi=\frac{1}{\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}}V_{g}f
, we have

\displaystyle E_{k}\big(|V_{\psi}\phi|^{2}\big)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\ln(|V_{\psi}\phi(m,w)|^{2})~|V_{\psi}\phi(m,w)|^{2}~d\mu(w)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}}E_{k}(|V_{g}f|^{2})+2\ln\big(\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\big).

Finally, from (
3.11
), we obtain

E_{k}(|V_{g}f|^{2})\geq-2\ln\left(\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\;\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\right)\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}\;\|g\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{2}.

This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Proper-time evaluation of the effective action: unequal masses in the loop
A. A. Osipov

Abstract
The Fock-Schwinger proper-time method is used to derive the effective action in the field theory with the chiral U ⁡ ( 3 ) × U ⁡ ( 3 ) U(3)\times U(3) symmetry explicitly broken by unequal masses of heavy particles. The one-loop effective action is presented as a series in inverse powers of heavy masses. The first two Seeley-DeWitt coefficients of this expansion are explicitly calculated. This powerful technique opens a promising avenue for studying explicit flavor symmetry breaking effects in the effective field theories.

When quantum fields have large equal masses

;
Nambu:61b

The new algorithm generalizes the standard large mass expansion of the heat kernel to the case of unequal masses by the formula

II
Proper-time expansion

In this section we obtain the
1/M
series of the effective one-loop action in Euclidian space and explicitly single out the structures necessary for calculating the first two coefficients of this asymptotic expansion.

II.1
Determinant of the Dirac operator

The logarithm of the formal determinant of the self-adjoint elliptic operator of the second order describes the one-loop radiative corrections to classical theory. In the following, we are interested in the real part of the effective action resulting from the calculation of the determinant of the Dirac operator
D
in the background scalar
s
, pseudoscalar
p
, vector
v_{\mu}
, and axial-vector
a_{\mu}
, fields. The proper-time method cannot be applied directly to fermions, since the Dirac operator
D
is a first order elliptic operator, and its spectrum is unbounded both above and below. Instead, one should consider the functional

W_{E}=\ln|\det D_{E}|=-\int\limits^{\infty}_{0}\!\frac{dt}{2t}\,\rho_{t,\Lambda}\,\mbox{Tr}\left(e^{-tD_{E}^{\dagger}D_{E}}\right),

(3)

representing a real part of the one-loop effective action in Euclidian space as the integral over the proper-time
t
. Notice that the Hermitian operator
D_{E}^{\dagger}D_{E}
is a second order elliptic operator, unbounded above, so we can use the proper-time method to regularize it precisely as for bosons. The integral diverges at the lower limit, therefore, a regulator
\rho_{t,\Lambda}
is introduced, where
\Lambda
is an ultraviolet cutoff. Since we will carry out calculations in Euclidean space (the subscript
E
marks this), and the obtained result will be analytically continued to Minkowski space, we will adhere to certain rules of transition from one space to another, which we collect in Appendix
A
.

For definiteness, suppose that one is dealing with the effective action arising due to integration over light quark degrees of freedom. In this case, the Dirac operator
D_{E}
in Euclidean four-dimensional space has the form

D_{E}=i\gamma_{\alpha}d_{\alpha}-M+s+i\gamma_{5E}p,

(4)

where
d_{\alpha}=\partial_{\alpha}+i\Gamma_{\alpha}
,
\Gamma_{\alpha}=v_{\alpha}+\gamma_{5E}a_{\alpha}
,
\alpha=1,2,3,4
. The external fields are embedded in the flavor space through the set of matrices
\lambda_{a}=(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{i})
, where
\lambda_{0}=\sqrt{2/3}
and
\lambda_{i}
are the eight
SU(3)
Gell-Mann matrices; for instance,

, and so on for all fields. The quark masses are given by the diagonal matrix
M=\mbox{diag}(m_{1},m_{2},m_{3})
in the flavor space. The symbol ”Tr” denotes the trace over Dirac
(D)

\gamma
-matrices, color
(c)

SU(3)
matrices, and flavor
(f)
matrices, as well as integration over coordinates of the Euclidean space:
\mbox{Tr}\equiv\mbox{tr}_{I}\int\!d^{4}x_{E}
, where
I=(D,c,f)
. The trace in the color space is trivial: it leads to the overall factor
N_{c}=3
. The dependence on external fields in
D_{E}
after switching to the Hermitian operator

D_{E}^{\dagger}D_{E}=M^{2}-d^{2}+Y

(5)

is collected in

and the covariant derivative

. In the following we do not need an explicit expression for

,
nevertheless, for completeness, we include this expression in Appendix
A

If quarks were massless, the theory would have a global
U(3)_{L}\times U(3)_{R}
chiral symmetry. It is known, however, that the ground state of QCD is not invariant under the action of chiral group. As a result, the entire system undergoes a phase transition accompanied by the appearance of a gap in the fermion spectrum. Quarks get their equal nonzero constituent masses.

Additionally, due to explicit breaking of chiral symmetry, realized through the mass terms of current quarks, one can easily find that the inequality of current masses after spontaneous breaking of chiral symmetry leads to the inequality of constituent quark masses.

Thus, we arrive at a problem in which one needs to study the properties of a system at large scales, i.e., one needs an expansion of the effective action in the inverse powers of large unequal masses of quarks

M=\sum_{i=1}^{3}m_{i}E_{i},\quad(E_{i})_{jk}=\delta_{ij}\delta_{ik},\quad E_{i}E_{j}=\delta_{ij}E_{j}.

(6)

The matrix

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\lambda_{u}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\lambda_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{3}+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{3}}\lambda_{8}\,,

(7)

\displaystyle E_{2}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\lambda_{d}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\lambda_{0}-\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{3}+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{3}}\lambda_{8}\,,

(8)

\displaystyle E_{3}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\lambda_{s}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\lambda_{0}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\lambda_{8}\,.

(9)

The second formula reflects the projection property of the

II.2
The case of equal masses

Before proceed with our calculations, it is appropriate to discuss the simplest case of the large-
M
expansion, i.e., the case when the mass matrix

is
M=\mbox{diag}(m,m,m)
; then
[M,A]=0
and we have

e^{-t(M^{2}+A)}=e^{-tM^{2}}e^{-tA}=e^{-tm^{2}}\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}t^{n}a_{n}(x,x)\,.

(16)

Here

are the Seeley - DeWitt coefficients

in a coincidence limit

, which depend on the background fields and their derivatives, except

. Integration over four-momentum and proper-time in (

(17)

where the proper-time integrals
J_{n}(m^{2})
are

J_{n}(m^{2})=\int\limits^{\infty}_{0}\frac{dt}{t^{2-n}}e^{-tm^{2}}\rho_{t,\Lambda}\,.

(18)

In the case of two subtractions
\rho_{t,\Lambda}=1-(1+t\Lambda^{2})e^{-t\Lambda^{2}}
at the large scale
\Lambda

18
)

\displaystyle J_{0}(m^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Lambda^{2}-m^{2}\ln\left(1+\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{m^{2}}\right)\,,

(19)

\displaystyle J_{1}(m^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ln\left(1+\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{m^{2}}\right)-\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}+m^{2}}\,.

(20)

The choice of the regularization is closely related to the specific problem under study. Various examples of the proper-time regularization can be found in
Ball:89
. The Pauli-Villars regularization we used is usually applied in the NJL model, where the cutoff
\Lambda
is a scale of spontaneous chiral symmetry breaking.

The functions
J_{n}(m^{2})
, for
n>1
, as
m^{2}
becomes very large are asymptotically equivalent to
m^{-2(n-1)}
, that is, the expansion (
17
) is in inverse powers of

. For the series to converge, it is necessary not only that the mass

be large, but also that the background fields change slowly over distances of the order of the fermion field Compton wavelength
1/m
. If these criteria are not met, then the production of real quark-antiquark pairs becomes essential, and the expansion is not suitable for applications.

II.3
The case of unequal masses

Let us return now to the formula (
14
) and show how one can extend the above tool to the case
[M,A]\neq 0
. To make progress in our calculations, we use the formula (
1
) allowing to factorize the exponent with a non-commuting diagonal mass matrix
M
. Under a flavor trace, it yields, for instance,

\mbox{tr}_{f}\left(e^{-tM^{2}}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{3}e^{-tm_{i}^{2}}\,\mbox{tr}_{f}E_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}e^{-tm_{i}^{2}}\,.

(21)

The result of calculations for the remaining terms can be represented by the formula

\displaystyle\mbox{tr}_{f}\left(e^{-tM^{2}}f_{n}(t,A)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{n}}^{N_{f}}\!\!\!\!c_{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots i_{n}}(t)\,\mbox{tr}_{f}(A_{i_{1}}A_{i_{2}}\ldots A_{i_{n}}),

To ensure the fundamental cyclic property of the trace

\displaystyle\mbox{tr}_{f}\,(A_{i_{1}}A_{i_{2}}\ldots A_{i_{n}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\!\!\sum_{cycl.perm.}\!\!\!\!\!A_{i_{1}i_{2}}A_{i_{2}i_{3}}\ldots A_{i_{n}i_{1}}

(23)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\langle A_{i_{1}i_{2}}A_{i_{2}i_{3}}\ldots A_{i_{n}i_{1}}\rangle.

Here we used Eqs. (
11

adds nothing to the standard definition of a trace if

is a matrix. However, in the case when

It should be emphasized that here we will restrict ourselves to considering only those terms that survive in the limit
M\to\infty

and

. To isolate such a contribution, it is necessary to limit ourselves to the terms of order

order, since

has the term

\displaystyle\mbox{tr}_{f}\left(e^{-t(M^{2}+A)}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{3}c_{i}(t)-t\sum_{i=1}^{3}c_{i}(t)\,\mbox{tr}_{f}\,A_{i}

(24)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{t^{2}}{2!}\sum_{i,j}c_{ij}(t)\,\mbox{tr}_{f}\,(A_{i}A_{j})-\frac{t^{3}}{3!}\sum_{i,j,k}c_{ijk}(t)\,\mbox{tr}_{f}\,(A_{i}A_{j}A_{k})

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}J_{0}(m_{i}^{2},m_{j}^{2})\,\mbox{tr}_{Df}(E_{i}d_{\alpha}E_{j}d_{\alpha})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}J_{ij}\,\mbox{tr}_{Df}\left[E_{i}(Y-d^{2})E_{j}(Y-d^{2})\right]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\sum_{i,j,k}J_{ijk}\,\mbox{tr}_{Df}\left[E_{i}d_{\alpha}E_{j}d_{\alpha}E_{k}(Y-d^{2})\right.

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left.E_{i}d_{\alpha}E_{j}(Y-d^{2})E_{k}d_{\alpha}+E_{i}(Y-d^{2})E_{j}d_{\alpha}E_{k}d_{\alpha}\right]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left.\frac{1}{6}\sum_{i,j,k,l}J_{ijkl}\delta_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}\,\mbox{tr}_{Df}\left[E_{i}d_{\alpha}E_{j}d_{\beta}E_{k}d_{\gamma}E_{l}d_{\delta}\right]\right\}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle{\cal O}(1/M^{2})\equiv-\frac{N_{c}}{32\pi^{2}}\!\!\int\!\!d^{4}x\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\,\mbox{tr}_{D}\,b_{n}(x,x),

where the index
n
indicates the asymptotic behavior of
b_{n}(x,x)
at large masses, namely
b_{n}\sim M^{-2(n-2)}
. The coefficients
b_{n}(x,x)
depend on the external fields and quark masses, i.e., they contain information about both the effective meson vertices and corresponding coupling constants. If all masses are equal, the dependence on
m
is factorized in form of the integral (
18
) and the field-dependent part takes a standard Seeley-DeWitt form
a_{n}(x,x)
.

III
Leading terms of the
1/M
expansion

Consider the leading terms
b_{1}
and
b_{2}
. The case
n=0
is of no interest because
b_{0}
contains no fields and can be omitted from the effective action. The coefficients with
n\geq 3
tend to zero in the limit of infinite masses, therefore they are small in comparison with
b_{1}
and
b_{2}
and, at the first stage, can be neglected.

III.1
Coefficient
b_{1}(x,x)

Let us turn to the calculation of the coefficient
b_{1}
. It is given by a part of the expression (
28
), which is proportional to the proper-time integrals
J_{0}(m_{i}^{2},m_{j}^{2})
. With the use of Eq. (
180
), it can be rewritten as

\displaystyle b_{1}(x,x)=

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\sum_{i}J_{0}(m_{i}^{2})\,\mbox{tr}_{f}\!\left[E_{i}(Y-d^{2})+E_{i}d_{\alpha}E_{i}d_{\alpha}\right]

(29)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle 2\sum_{i<j}J_{0}(m_{i}^{2},m_{j}^{2})\,\mbox{tr}_{f}(E_{i}d_{\alpha}E_{j}d_{\alpha}).

Noting that

d^{2}=\partial^{2}+2i\Gamma\partial+i\left(\partial\Gamma\right)-\Gamma^{2},

(30)

and taking into account that the action of the open derivatives in (
29
) on the implied unit on the right hand side of Eq. (
28
) gives zero, we find

\mbox{tr}_{f}\!\left[E_{i}(Y-d^{2})\right]=\mbox{tr}_{f}\!\left[E_{i}(Y+\Gamma^{2}-i\partial\Gamma)\right].

(31)

Since the last term here is a total divergence which can be omitted in the effective action, we conclude

\mbox{tr}_{f}\!\left[E_{i}(Y-d^{2})\right]=\mbox{tr}_{f}\!\left[E_{i}(Y+\Gamma^{2})\right]=Y_{ii}+(\Gamma^{2})_{ii},

(32)

where on the last stage we used Eq. (
II.1
) and the fact that matrix
A
may be written in a unique way as a finite linear combination of elements of
A
in the bases
E_{ij}
, namely
A=\sum_{m,n}A_{mn}E_{mn}
. In the same manner, we find

\mbox{tr}_{f}\left(E_{i}d_{\alpha}E_{j}d_{\alpha}\right)=-\mbox{tr}_{f}(E_{i}\Gamma_{\alpha}E_{j}\Gamma_{\alpha})=-\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ji}.

(33)

It gives for Eq. (
29
)

\displaystyle b_{1}=

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\sum_{i}J_{0}(m_{i}^{2})\left[Y_{ii}+(\Gamma^{2})_{ii}-\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ii}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ii}\right]

(34)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 2\sum_{i<j}J_{0}(m_{i}^{2},m_{j}^{2})\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ji}\,.

Let us use now the easily verifiable relation

(\Gamma^{2})_{ii}-\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ii}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ii}=\sum_{j\neq i}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ji}\,,

(35)

to obtain finally

b_{1}=-\sum_{i}J_{0}(m_{i}^{2})Y_{ii}+\sum_{i<j}\Delta J_{0}(m_{i}^{2},m_{j}^{2})\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ji}\,,

(36)

where
\Delta J_{0}(m_{i}^{2},m_{j}^{2})
is given by Eq. (
165
). The latter integrals can be collected in the symmetric
3\times 3
matrix
\Delta J_{0}
, which has zeros on the main diagonal. In particular, when
m_{i}=m_{j}=m
, all elements of
\Delta J_{0}
vanish. In this specific case, the first term of (
36
) leads to the well known expression of the Seeley-DeWitt coefficient
a_{1}(x,x)=-Y
.

For convenience of writing the result of our calculations, along with the usual matrix multiplication, we will use the non-standard Hadamard product
Styan:73
, which is the matrix of elementwise products

(A\circ B)_{ij}=A_{ij}B_{ij}.

(37)

The Hadamard product is commutative unlike regular matrix multiplication, but the distributive and associative properties are retained. It has previously been proven to be a useful tool when the mass matrices of the type (
6
) are involved
Morais:17
. In terms of Hadamard product the result (
36
) can be written as

b_{1}=\mbox{tr}_{f}\!\left[J_{0}\circ(-Y)+\frac{1}{4}(\Delta J_{0}\circ\Gamma^{\alpha})\Gamma^{\alpha}\right],

(38)

where
J_{0}
is considered as a diagonal matrix with elements given by
(J_{0})_{ij}=\delta_{ij}J_{0}(m_{i}^{2})
. This matrix contains contributions of the Feynman one-loop diagram, known as a ”tadpole”.

III.2
Coefficient
b_{2}(x,x)

Consider the second coefficient
b_{2}(x,x)
. In accordance with the general structure of the expression, we distinguish three contributions differing in the degree of
Y

b_{2}=b_{2}^{(0)}+b_{2}^{(1)}+b_{2}^{(2)}

(39)

which is explicitly indicated in the parentheses.

III.2.1
Quadratic part in
Y
:
b_{2}^{(2)}

The part of
b_{2}
proportional to
Y^{2}
is calculated most simply

\displaystyle b_{2}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}J_{ij}\,\mbox{tr}_{f}\left(E_{i}YE_{j}Y\right)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}J_{ij}Y_{ij}Y_{ji}

(40)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\,\mbox{tr}_{f}\!\left[\left(J\circ Y\right)Y\right],

where
J
is a symmetric
3\times 3
matrix, whose elements
J_{ij}=J_{1}(m_{i},m_{j})
are logarithmically divergent parts (at
\Lambda\to\infty
) of Feynman self-energy diagrams with masses of virtual particles
m_{i}
and
m_{j}
(see Eq. (
157
)).

III.2.2
Linear part in
Y
:
b_{2}^{(1)}

Let us consider now the linear in
Y
part of
b_{2}
, i.e.,
b_{2}^{(1)}

\displaystyle b_{2}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}J_{ij}\,\mbox{tr}_{f}\!\left(E_{i}d^{2}E_{j}Y+E_{i}YE_{j}d^{2}\right)

(41)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\sum_{i,j,k}J_{ijk}\,\mbox{tr}_{f}\!\left(E_{i}d_{\alpha}E_{j}d_{\alpha}E_{k}Y\right.

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left.E_{i}d_{\alpha}E_{j}YE_{k}d_{\alpha}+E_{i}YE_{j}d_{\alpha}E_{k}d_{\alpha}\right).

After evaluation of traces it gives

b_{2}^{(1)}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}J_{ij}t_{ij}+\sum_{i,j,k}J_{ijk}t_{ijk},

(42)

where

t_{ij}=\left\{(d^{2})_{ij},Y_{ji}\right\},\quad t_{ijk}=\langle d^{\alpha}_{ij}d^{\alpha}_{jk}Y_{ki}\rangle.

(43)

Next, let us use Eqs. (
180
) and (
185
) of Appendix
E
. We obtain

\displaystyle b_{2}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}J_{i}\left(t_{iii}-\frac{1}{2}t_{ii}\right)+\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}\left(3t^{+}_{iij}-t_{(ij)}\right)

(44)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 3\sum_{i<j}R_{ij}t^{-}_{iij}+6J_{123}t_{(123)}.

To evaluate the first sum in this expression, we employ the explicit form of
t
-coefficients (
43
)

\displaystyle t_{iii}-\frac{1}{2}t_{ii}=\left\{\frac{1}{3}d^{\alpha}_{ii}d^{\alpha}_{ii}-\frac{1}{2}(d^{2})_{ii},Y_{ii}\right\}+\frac{1}{3}d^{\alpha}_{ii}Y_{ii}d^{\alpha}_{ii}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{6}\left[d^{\alpha}_{ii},\left[d^{\alpha}_{ii},Y_{ii}\right]\right]-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j\neq i}\left\{d^{\alpha}_{ij}d^{\alpha}_{ji},Y_{ii}\right\}.

(45)

The first term is now computed as

\left[d^{\alpha}_{ii},\left[d^{\alpha}_{ii},Y_{ii}\right]\right]=\partial_{\alpha}^{2}Y_{ii}.

(46)

This is a four-divergence of a four-vector. Therefore, it can be omitted from the effective action, because it does not affect the dynamic characteristics of the effective theory. Thus, we find

t_{iii}-\frac{1}{2}t_{ii}=\sum_{j\neq i}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ji}Y_{ii},

(47)

where we used the cyclic permutation of the elements to simplify the right hand side of this expression, that is not forbidden due to the trace, tr
D
, in (
28
). In what follows, we will always use the cyclic permutation property when calculating the t-coefficients.

To evaluate the second sum in (
44
) we need to find the difference
3t^{+}_{iij}-t_{(ij)}
, where
i\neq j
. Here we have

\displaystyle 3t^{+}_{iij}-t_{(ij)}=3\left(t_{(iij)}+t_{(jji)}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\left(t_{ij}+t_{ji}\right)

\displaystyle=t_{iij}+t_{iji}+t_{jii}-\frac{1}{2}t_{ij}+(i\leftrightarrow j)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}\left(\left\{d^{\alpha}_{ii}d^{\alpha}_{ij}+d^{\alpha}_{ij}d^{\alpha}_{jj},Y_{ji}\right\}+d^{\alpha}_{ij}Y_{ji}d^{\alpha}_{ii}+d^{\alpha}_{jj}Y_{ji}d^{\alpha}_{ij}\right.

\displaystyle+\left.\left\{d^{\alpha}_{ij}d^{\alpha}_{ji},Y_{ii}\right\}+d^{\alpha}_{ji}Y_{ii}d^{\alpha}_{ij}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\{(d^{2})_{ij},Y_{ji}\}+(i\leftrightarrow j)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{6}\left[\left\{d^{\alpha}_{ii}d^{\alpha}_{ij}+d^{\alpha}_{ij}d^{\alpha}_{jj},Y_{ji}\right\}-2\left(d^{\alpha}_{ij}Y_{ji}d^{\alpha}_{ii}+d^{\alpha}_{jj}Y_{ji}d^{\alpha}_{ij}\right)\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{3}\left(\left\{d^{\alpha}_{ij}d^{\alpha}_{ji},Y_{ii}\right\}+d^{\alpha}_{ji}Y_{ii}d^{\alpha}_{ij}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\{d^{\alpha}_{ik}d^{\alpha}_{kj},Y_{ji}\}|_{k\neq i,j}

\displaystyle+(i\leftrightarrow j),

(48)

where, on the last stage, we took into account that

(d^{2})_{ij}=\sum_{k}d^{\alpha}_{ik}d^{\alpha}_{kj}.

(49)

Noticing that expressions

\displaystyle\left\{d^{\alpha}_{ii}d^{\alpha}_{ij}+d^{\alpha}_{ij}d^{\alpha}_{jj},Y_{ji}\right\}-2\left(d^{\alpha}_{ij}Y_{ji}d^{\alpha}_{ii}+d^{\alpha}_{jj}Y_{ji}d^{\alpha}_{ij}\right)\!,

\displaystyle\left\{d^{\alpha}_{ij}d^{\alpha}_{ji},Y_{ii}\right\}-2d^{\alpha}_{ji}Y_{ii}d^{\alpha}_{ij}

(50)

vanish under the Dirac matrix trace in (
28
), we finally obtain

3t^{+}_{iij}-t_{(ij)}=-\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ji}Y_{ii}+\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ik}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{kj}Y_{ji}+(i\leftrightarrow j),

(51)

where
i\neq j\neq k
.

Since the first term in (
51
) has the same form as (
47
), it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{i}J_{i}\left(t_{iii}-\frac{1}{2}t_{ii}\right)-\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}\left[\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ji}Y_{ii}+(i\leftrightarrow j)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j\atop i\neq j}(J_{i}-J_{ij})\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ji}Y_{ii}.

(52)

We need now to calculate the coefficient

\displaystyle t^{-}_{iij}=t_{(iij)}-t_{(jji)}=\frac{1}{3}\left[t_{iij}+t_{iji}+t_{jii}-(i\leftrightarrow j)\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{3}\left[\frac{i}{3}\left(\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij}\!\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftrightarrow}}{{\partial_{\alpha}}}\!Y_{ji}\right)+(\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ii}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij}-\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{jj})Y_{ji}\right.

\displaystyle+\left.\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ji}Y_{ii}\frac{}{}\right]-(i\leftrightarrow j),

(53)

where the left-right derivative is defined in a standard way
\Gamma^{\alpha}\!\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftrightarrow}}{{\partial^{\alpha}}}\!Y=\Gamma^{\alpha}\partial^{\alpha}Y-(\partial^{\alpha}\Gamma^{\alpha})Y
. Then the third sum in (
44
) is

\displaystyle 3\sum_{i<j}R_{ij}t^{-}_{iij}=-\sum_{i,j}R_{ij}\left[\frac{i}{3}\left(\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij}\!\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftrightarrow}}{{\partial_{\alpha}}}\!Y_{ji}\right)\right.

\displaystyle+(\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ii}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij}-\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{jj})Y_{ji}+\left.\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ji}Y_{ii}\frac{}{}\right].

(54)

The coefficients
t_{(123)}
are required for the evaluation of the last term in (
44
). They can be obtained with the aid of the easily verifiable relations,

t_{ijk}|_{i\neq j\neq k}=-\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{jk}Y_{ki}.

(55)

As a consequence, we have

t_{(123)}=\frac{1}{6}\sum_{i\neq j\neq k}t_{ijk}=-\frac{1}{6}\sum_{i\neq j\neq k}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{jk}Y_{ki}.

(56)

If we take into account the existence of similar term in the coefficient (
51
), we obtain

\displaystyle 6J_{123}t_{(123)}+\sum_{i<j\atop i\neq j\neq k}J_{ij}\left[\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ik}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{kj}Y_{ji}+(i\leftrightarrow j)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\neq j\neq k}(J_{ij}-J_{ijk})\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{jk}Y_{ki}.

(57)

Thus, the final result for
b_{2}^{(1)}
is

\displaystyle b_{2}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i\neq j\neq k}\left[(J_{i}-J_{ij})\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ji}Y_{ii}+(J_{ij}-J_{ijk})\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{jk}Y_{ki}\right]

(58)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}R_{ij}\left[\frac{i}{3}\left(\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij}\!\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftrightarrow}}{{\partial^{\alpha}}}\!Y_{ji}\right)+(L_{-}^{\alpha\alpha})_{ij}Y_{ji}+\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ji}Y_{ii}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mbox{tr}_{f}\left[NY-\frac{i}{3}(R\circ\Gamma^{\alpha})\!\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftrightarrow}}{{\partial^{\alpha}}}\!Y\right],

where we introduce the notation

\left(L^{\alpha\beta}_{\pm}\right)_{ij}=\left(\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ii}\pm\Gamma^{\alpha}_{jj}\right)\Gamma^{\beta}_{ij}.

(59)

Note that elements of the matrix
\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij}
commute with each other. In the last line of Eq. (
58
),
R
and
N
are the
3\times 3
matrices with elements

\displaystyle R_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(J_{iij}-J_{jji}\right),

\displaystyle N_{ii}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\neq i}\left(J_{i}-J_{ij}-R_{ij}\right)\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ji}\,,

(60)

\displaystyle N_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[(J_{ij}-J_{ijk})\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ik}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{kj}-R_{ij}(L_{-}^{\alpha\alpha})_{ij}\right]_{\mid i\neq j\neq k}.

In the limit of equal masses, the coefficient
b_{2}^{(1)}
vanishes, that is, it contains only contributions associated with an explicit violation of chiral symmetry. Obviously, without this term, the description of the manifest flavor symmetry breaking would be incomplete.

III.2.3
Independent on
Y
part:
b_{2}^{(0)}

The part of
b_{2}
which does not depend on
Y
is

\displaystyle b_{2}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}J_{ij}(d^{2})_{ij}(d^{2})_{ji}-\sum_{i,j,k}J_{ijk}\hat{t}_{ijk}

(61)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{6}\sum_{i,j,k,l}J_{ijkl}t_{ijkl}\,.

Here the field dependent t-coefficients are given by the following formulas.

\hat{t}_{ijk}=\langle d^{\alpha}_{ij}d^{\alpha}_{jk}(d^{2})_{ki}\rangle,\quad t_{ijkl}=\delta_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}\langle d^{\alpha}_{ij}d^{\beta}_{jk}d^{\gamma}_{kl}d^{\delta}_{li}\rangle.

(62)

Before of calculating (
61
) directly, we shall first handle the case of equal masses
m_{u}=m_{d}=m_{s}=m
, which can easily be obtained from (
61
) after factorizing the common factor
J_{1}(m^{2})=J_{ii}=J_{iii}=J_{iiii}

\displaystyle\frac{b_{2}^{(0)}|_{m}}{J_{1}(m^{2})}=\mbox{tr}_{f}\left[\frac{1}{2}d^{2}d^{2}-\frac{1}{3}\left(d^{\alpha}d^{\alpha}d^{2}+d^{\alpha}d^{2}d^{\alpha}\right.\right.

\displaystyle+\left.d^{2}d^{\alpha}d^{\alpha}\right)+\frac{1}{6}\left(d^{\alpha}d^{\alpha}d^{\beta}d^{\beta}+d^{\alpha}d^{\beta}d^{\alpha}d^{\beta}+d^{\alpha}d^{\beta}d^{\beta}d^{\alpha}\right)\left.\frac{}{}\!\!\!\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6}\mbox{tr}_{f}\left(d^{\alpha}d^{\beta}d^{\alpha}d^{\beta}-d^{\alpha}d^{\beta}d^{\beta}d^{\alpha}\right)=\frac{1}{12}\mbox{tr}_{f}\!\left[d^{\alpha},d^{\beta}\right]^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{12}\mbox{tr}_{f}\!\left(\Gamma^{\alpha\beta}\Gamma^{\alpha\beta}\right),

(63)

where we used Eq.(
49
), and

\Gamma^{\alpha\beta}=\partial^{\alpha}\Gamma^{\beta}-\partial^{\beta}\Gamma^{\alpha}+i[\Gamma^{\alpha},\Gamma^{\beta}].

(64)

To obtain the exact result for (
61
) it is convenient to consider separately the cases when the sum runs only over the same index values, and then over two and three different values of the flavors

b_{2}^{(0)}=\beta_{2}^{(1)}+\beta_{2}^{(2)}+\beta_{2}^{(3)}.

(65)

The part of
b_{2}^{(0)}
that corresponds to a contribution with coincident indices is

\beta_{2}^{(1)}=\sum_{i}J_{i}\left[\frac{1}{2}(d^{2})_{ii}(d^{2})_{ii}-\hat{t}_{iii}+\frac{1}{6}t_{iiii}\right].

(66)

Introducing the notation

Q_{ii}=d^{\alpha}_{ii}d^{\alpha}_{ii}-(d^{2})_{ii}=(\Gamma^{2})_{ii}-\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ii}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ii}\,,

(67)

we can rewrite (
66
) as

\beta_{2}^{(1)}=\sum_{i}J_{i}\left\{\frac{1}{2}Q_{ii}^{2}-\frac{1}{6}[d^{\alpha}_{ii},[d^{\alpha}_{ii},Q_{ii}]]+\frac{1}{12}[d^{\alpha}_{ii},d^{\beta}_{ii}]^{2}\right\}.

Now we benefit from the following easily established relations

\displaystyle[d^{\alpha}_{ii},[d^{\alpha}_{ii},Q_{ii}]]=\partial^{2}Q_{ii},

(68)

\displaystyle[d^{\alpha}_{ii},d^{\beta}_{ii}]=i\left(\partial^{\alpha}\Gamma^{\beta}_{ii}-\partial^{\beta}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ii}\right)\equiv iF^{\alpha\beta}_{ii}.

(69)

That gives finally

\beta_{2}^{(1)}=\sum_{i}J_{i}\left\{\frac{1}{2}Q_{ii}^{2}-\frac{1}{12}(F^{\alpha\beta}_{ii})^{2}\right\}.

(70)

To obtain this expression, we, as before, discarded a full divergence of a four-vector
\partial^{2}Q_{ii}
.

Now consider the terms of the sum (
61
) with two unequal indices. These comprise of the remaining terms of the first sum and the corresponding contributions from the second and third sums

\displaystyle\beta_{2}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}\{(d^{2})_{ij},(d^{2})_{ji}\}-3\sum_{i<j}\left(J_{ij}\hat{t}^{+}_{iij}+R_{ij}\hat{t}^{-}_{iij}\right)

(71)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{i<j}\left[\frac{2}{3}\left(J_{ij}+\frac{1}{2}S_{ij}\right)t^{+}_{iiij}+R_{ij}t^{-}_{iiij}\right]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{i<j}\left(J_{ij}-S_{ij}\right)t_{(iijj)}\,,

where
S_{ij}
is given by Eq. (
177
).

To evaluate the first sum, we note that

\displaystyle(d^{2})_{ij}(d^{2})_{ji}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\partial\Gamma_{ij})(\partial\Gamma_{ji})+(\Gamma^{2})_{ij}(\Gamma^{2})_{ji}

(72)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle i(\partial\Gamma_{ij})(\Gamma^{2})_{ji}-i(\Gamma^{2})_{ij}(\partial\Gamma_{ji}).

Here we have omitted a full divergence of a four-vector, and took into account that the action of the derivative that reached the right end of the expression gives zero. In the anticommutator
\{(d^{2})_{ij},(d^{2})_{ji}\}
, only the symmetric part of Eq. (
72
) contributes to the sum. Therefore, the second line of (
72
) has no influence on the result, and we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}\{(d^{2})_{ij},(d^{2})_{ji}\}

(73)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}\left[(\partial\Gamma_{ij})(\partial\Gamma_{ji})+(\Gamma^{2})_{ij}(\Gamma^{2})_{ji}\right].

Recall again that elements of the matrix
\Gamma
commute with each other.

Combining

\frac{2}{3}t^{+}_{iiij}+t_{(iijj)}=\frac{2}{3}\left(t_{iiij}+t_{jjji}+t_{iijj}\right)+\frac{1}{3}t_{ijij}\,,

(74)

where the properties of t-coefficients (
201
)-(
203
) have been used, we learn that this sum does not contain the terms with one derivative. Indeed,

\displaystyle t_{iiij}+t_{jjji}+t_{iijj}=(\partial\Gamma_{ij})(\partial\Gamma_{ji})+\frac{1}{2}(\partial^{\alpha}\Gamma^{\beta}_{ij})(\partial^{\alpha}\Gamma^{\beta}_{ji})

\displaystyle+\delta_{\alpha\beta\gamma\sigma}\left(\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ii}\Gamma^{\beta}_{ii}\Gamma^{\gamma}_{ij}\Gamma^{\sigma}_{ji}+\Gamma^{\alpha}_{jj}\Gamma^{\beta}_{jj}\Gamma^{\gamma}_{ji}\Gamma^{\sigma}_{ij}+\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ii}\Gamma^{\beta}_{ij}\Gamma^{\gamma}_{jj}\Gamma^{\sigma}_{ji}\right).

We also know from (
F
) that

t_{ijij}=\delta_{\alpha\beta\gamma\sigma}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij}\Gamma^{\beta}_{ji}\Gamma^{\gamma}_{ij}\Gamma^{\sigma}_{ji}.

(75)

Thus, only the coefficient
\hat{t}^{+}_{iij}
contains terms with one derivative. The expression for this coefficient is given in Appendix
F
(see Eq. (
F
)).

Before we turn to the full expression for the coefficient
\beta_{2}^{(2)}
let us first collect terms with two derivatives in
\beta_{2}^{(2)}

\displaystyle\beta_{2}^{(2)}|_{\partial^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}\left[(\partial\Gamma_{ij})(\partial\Gamma_{ji})-(\partial^{\alpha}\Gamma^{\beta}_{ij})(\partial^{\alpha}\Gamma^{\beta}_{ji})\right]

(76)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{6}\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}F^{\alpha\beta}_{ij}F^{\alpha\beta}_{ji}.

Combined together with similar terms in
\beta_{2}^{(1)}
they give the kinetic term of spin-1 fields

\displaystyle\left.\left(\beta_{2}^{(1)}+\beta_{2}^{(2)}\right)\right|_{\partial^{2}}=-\frac{1}{12}\,\mbox{tr}_{f}\left[F^{\alpha\beta}(J\circ F^{\alpha\beta})\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{12}\,\mbox{tr}_{f}\left[\Gamma^{\alpha\beta}(J\circ\Gamma^{\alpha\beta})\right]+\frac{i}{3}\,\mbox{tr}_{f}\left[F^{\alpha\beta}(J\circ\Gamma^{\alpha}\Gamma^{\beta})\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{6}\,\mbox{tr}_{f}\left\{[\Gamma^{\alpha},\Gamma^{\beta}](J\circ\Gamma^{\alpha}\Gamma^{\beta})\right\}.

(77)

In the case of equal masses, the first term coincides with the standard result (
III.2.3
). It means that the last two terms of (
III.2.3
) after combining them with other contributions to
b_{2}^{(0)}
should vanish when
m_{u}=m_{d}=m_{s}=m
.

To see this we need to consider the contribution
\beta_{2}^{(3)}
. On referring to Eqs. (
61
) and (
E
), we find

\beta_{2}^{(3)}=2\!\!\sum_{i\neq j\neq k\atop j<k}J_{iijk}t_{(iijk)}-6J_{123}\hat{t}_{(123)}.

(78)

Using the explicit expression for the coefficients
t_{(iijk)}
(
F
) and Eq. (

V
Conclusions

Above, a new method for obtaining the effective low-energy Lagrangian in a theory with heavy particles of unequal masses and a non-trivial flavor symmetry group has been presented in a full detail. Unlike
Min:82
, where authors use the modified DeWitt WKB form and solve recurrent Schrödinger-like equations to determine the heat coefficients, our calculations are based on the formula (
1
), which allows us to resum the proper-time series in accordance with the contributions of corresponding one-loop Feynman diagrams with the required number of external fields. As a result, we arrive at an effective action (
28
), in which each of the heat coefficients can be calculated independently, and corresponds to a certain order in
1/M^{2}
expansion. We have explicitly calculated two leading coefficients
b_{1}(x,x)
and
b_{2}(x,x)
for the case of the broken
U(3)\times U(3)
chiral symmetry. The result can be used to study the consequences of
SU(3)
symmetry violations, for instance, in the framework of the NJL-type models, where, until now, rather rough approximations are used to describe this important effect. Although we have limited ourselves to a particular example, the proposed approach can be easily extended to the case of an arbitrary internal symmetry group, for example, to study an explicit and spontaneous symmetry violation within the context of effective field theories beyond the Standard Model.

Appendix A
The Wick rotation to the Euclidean space-time

In this Appendix, we collect some useful formulas and conventions made to perform a Wick rotation of the time axis to transit from Minkowski space-time with the metric tensor
g_{\mu\nu}=\mbox{diag}\,(1,-1,-1,-1)
to Euclidean space with metric
\delta_{\alpha\beta}=\mbox{diag}\,(1,1,1,1)
and back. Here the first two letters of Greek alphabet
\alpha,\beta
are used to enumerate the coordinates in Euclidean space
\alpha,\beta=1,2,3,4
, on the contrary, the letters
\mu,\nu
, run over the set
\mu,\nu=0,1,2,3
in Minkowski space-time.

For coordinates
x^{\mu}
and the Lorentz four-vector
v^{\mu}
we apply the following correspondence rules with the Euclidean coordinates
x_{\alpha}
and the vector components
v_{\alpha}

\displaystyle x^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x^{0},\vec{x})\to x_{\alpha}=(\vec{x},x_{4})\,,\!\!\quad x^{0}\to-ix_{4}\,.

(115)

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\partial_{0},\vec{\nabla})\to\partial_{\alpha}=(\vec{\nabla},\partial_{4})\,,\!\!\quad\ \partial_{0}\to i\partial_{4}\,.

(116)

\displaystyle v^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(v^{0},\vec{v})\to v_{\alpha}=(\vec{v},v_{4})\,,\quad v^{0}\to-iv_{4}\,.

(117)

It follows then from (
116
) and (
117
) that

\displaystyle v^{\mu}v_{\mu}=v_{0}^{2}-\vec{v}^{\,2}\to-v_{4}^{2}-\vec{v}^{\,2}=-v_{\alpha}^{2}\,,

(118)

\displaystyle v^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}=v_{0}\partial_{0}+\vec{v}\,\vec{\nabla}\to v_{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}\,.

(119)

For the Dirac
\gamma
-matrices we use the following conventions

\gamma^{\mu}=(\gamma^{0},\vec{\gamma})\to\gamma_{\alpha}=(\vec{\gamma},\gamma_{4})\,,\quad\gamma^{0}\to-i\gamma_{4}\,.

(120)

Our choice corresponds to the case when
O(4)
gamma matrices
\gamma_{\alpha}
in the four-dimensional Euclidean space are anti-Hermitian. Indeed, from the standard property
\gamma^{\mu\dagger}=(\gamma^{0},-\vec{\gamma})
and definitions made above one finds that
\gamma_{\alpha}^{\dagger}=-\gamma_{\alpha}
. As a consequence, the Euclidean Clifford algebra emerging from
\{\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu}\}=2g^{\mu\nu}
necessitates a change in the defining equation
\{\gamma_{\alpha},\gamma_{\beta}\}=-2\delta_{\alpha\beta}
. We have also that
\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\to\gamma_{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}
,
\gamma^{\mu}v_{\mu}\to-\gamma_{\alpha}v_{\alpha}
.

The other consequence is that
\gamma^{5}=-i\gamma^{0}\gamma^{1}\gamma^{2}\gamma^{3}
matrix can be defined in Euclidean space as
\gamma_{5E}=\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}\gamma_{3}\gamma_{4}
. It follows then that
\gamma^{5}\to\gamma_{5E}
, and both matrices are Hermitian.

For the Dirac operator

D=i\gamma^{\mu}d_{\mu}-M+s+i\gamma^{5}p,

(121)

where
d_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}-i\Gamma_{\mu}
, and
\Gamma_{\mu}=v_{\mu}+\gamma^{5}a_{\mu}
, one finds

D\to D_{E}=i\gamma_{\alpha}d_{\alpha}-M+s+i\gamma_{5E}p,

(122)

where
d_{\alpha}=\partial_{\alpha}+i\Gamma_{\alpha}\,
,
\Gamma_{\alpha}=v_{\alpha}+\gamma_{5E}a_{\alpha}
.

This yields

\displaystyle D_{E}^{\dagger}D_{E}=-d_{\alpha}^{2}+(s-M)^{2}+p^{2}+i\gamma_{5E}[s-M,p]

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ +\frac{1}{4}[\gamma_{\alpha},\gamma_{\beta}][d_{\alpha},d_{\beta}]+i[\gamma_{\alpha}(s+i\gamma_{5E}p-M),d_{\alpha}]

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ =M^{2}-d^{2}+Y\,,

(123)

where
d^{2}=d_{\alpha}d_{\alpha}
,

\displaystyle Y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s^{2}-\{s,M\}+p^{2}+i\gamma_{5E}[s-M,p]

(124)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{i}{4}[\gamma_{\alpha},\gamma_{\beta}](v_{\alpha\beta}+\gamma_{5E}a_{\alpha\beta})

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle i\gamma_{\alpha}(\nabla_{\!\alpha}s+i\gamma_{5E}\nabla_{\!\alpha}p)\,,

and the chiral-covariant objects are

\displaystyle v_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{\alpha}v_{\beta}-\partial_{\beta}v_{\alpha}+i[v_{\alpha},v_{\beta}]+i[a_{\alpha},a_{\beta}]\,,

(125)

\displaystyle a_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{\alpha}a_{\beta}-\partial_{\beta}a_{\alpha}+i[v_{\alpha},a_{\beta}]+i[a_{\alpha},v_{\beta}]\,,

(126)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\!\alpha}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{\alpha}s+i[v_{\alpha},s-M]+\{a_{\alpha},p\}\,,

(127)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\!\alpha}p

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{\alpha}p+i[v_{\alpha},p]-\{a_{\alpha},s-M\}\,.

(128)

We have given these well-known formulas here both for the completeness of our presentation and to clarify notations used in the main text of our work.

Appendix B
The modulus of the determinant

This is a summary of the standard steps to evaluate the modulus of determinant (
3
). The self-adjoint elliptic operator
\Delta=D_{E}^{\dagger}D_{E}
can be treated as an operator acting on a fictitious Hilbert space, where the quantum mechanical algebra of coordinates and momenta is realized, i.e., where
|x\rangle
is an eigenvector of a commuting set of Hermitian operators
\hat{x}_{\alpha}
such that
\hat{x}_{\alpha}|x\rangle=x_{\alpha}|x\rangle
and
\langle x|y\rangle=\delta(x-y)
. The Hermitian operators,
\hat{p}_{\alpha}=-i\partial_{\alpha}
, which are conjugate to
\hat{x}_{\alpha}
, obey canonical commutation relations:
[\hat{x}_{\alpha},\hat{p}_{\beta}]=i\delta_{\alpha\beta},\ [\hat{x}_{\alpha},\hat{x}_{\beta}]=[\hat{p}_{\alpha},\hat{p}_{\beta}]=0
.

Let us consider an eigenket
|p\rangle
of
\hat{p}_{\alpha}
. Its representative in the Schrödinger picture is
\langle x|p\rangle=(2\pi)^{-2}\exp(ix_{\alpha}p_{\alpha})
. Using this plane wave basis one can describe the action of the operator
e^{-t\Delta}
on the Hilbert space by the heat kernel function of
\Delta

h(x,y;t)=\langle x|e^{-t\Delta}|y\rangle=\!\int\!d^{4}p\,\langle x|e^{-t\Delta}|p\rangle\langle p|y\rangle.

(129)

Taking into account the relations:
\langle x|\hat{p}_{\alpha}|p\rangle=p_{\alpha}\langle x|p\rangle

=-i\partial^{x}_{\alpha}\langle x|p\rangle
and
\langle x|\hat{x}_{\alpha}|p\rangle=x_{\alpha}\langle x|p\rangle
one can proceed

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\langle x|e^{-t\Delta}|y\rangle=\!\int\!d^{4}p\,e^{-t\Delta_{x}}\langle x|p\rangle\langle p|y\rangle

\displaystyle=\!\int\!\frac{d^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}e^{-ipy}e^{-t\Delta_{x}}e^{ipx}

\displaystyle=\!\int\!\frac{d^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}e^{ip(x-y)}\left(e^{-ipx}e^{-t\Delta_{x}}e^{ipx}\right),

(130)

where
\Delta_{x}
is the operator
\Delta
in the coordinate representation.

Now note that

\displaystyle e^{-ipx}e^{-t\Delta_{x}}e^{ipx}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-t)^{n}}{n!}e^{-ipx}(\Delta_{x})^{n}e^{ipx}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-t)^{n}}{n!}\left(e^{-ipx}\Delta_{x}e^{ipx}\right)^{n},

(131)

where

\displaystyle e^{-ipx}\Delta_{x}e^{ipx}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta_{x}-\left[ipx,\Delta_{x}\right]+\frac{1}{2!}[ipx,[ipx,\Delta_{x}]]

(132)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\Delta}_{x}-2ip_{\alpha}d_{\alpha}+p^{2}.

Since the operator
\Delta_{x}
contains derivatives of at most second order, its higher order commutators with
ipx
are equal to zero. At the last stage we used Eq.(
A
), namely

\displaystyle[ipx,\Delta_{x}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[ipx,M^{2}-d^{2}+Y]=-[ipx,d^{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-[ipx,\partial_{\alpha}^{2}+2i\Gamma_{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-[ipx,\partial_{\alpha}]\partial_{\alpha}-\partial_{\alpha}[ipx,\partial_{\alpha}]-2i\Gamma_{\alpha}[ipx,\partial_{\alpha}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2ip_{\alpha}d_{\alpha}\,,

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\![ipx,[ipx,\Delta_{x}]]=[ipx,2ip_{\alpha}d_{\alpha}]=2p_{\alpha}^{2}\,.

As a result we obtain

\displaystyle\langle x|e^{-t\Delta}|y\rangle=\int\!\!\frac{d^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}e^{ip(x-y)}e^{-tp^{2}}e^{-t({\Delta}_{x}-2ipd)}

\displaystyle\quad\!\!=\!\!\int\!\!\frac{d^{4}p}{(4\pi^{2}t)^{2}}e^{-p^{2}+\frac{i}{\sqrt{t}}p(x-y)}e^{-t\left(\Delta_{x}-\frac{2ipd}{\sqrt{t}}\right)},

(133)

or, for the trace

\displaystyle\mbox{Tr}(e^{-t\Delta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\int\!d^{4}x\,\mbox{tr}_{I}\langle x|e^{-t\Delta}|x\rangle

(134)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\!\frac{d^{4}xd^{4}p}{(4\pi^{2}t)^{2}}\,e^{-p^{2}}\,\mbox{tr}_{I}\,e^{-t\left(\Delta_{x}-\frac{2ipd}{\sqrt{t}}\right)},

where tr
I
means the trace over color, isospin, and Dirac indices. Expanding the last exponent in the series in the proper-time, one can take the integrals over four-momenta with the help of the formulas

\int\!\!\frac{d^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}\,e^{-p^{2}}p_{\alpha_{1}}p_{\alpha_{2}}\ldots p_{\alpha_{2n}}=\frac{\delta_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\ldots\alpha_{2n}}}{2^{n}(4\pi)^{2}}\,.

(135)

Note that only even number of internal momenta
p_{\alpha}
contribute, the odd number, obviously, gives zero; the totally symmetric tensor
\delta_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\ldots\alpha_{2n}}
is a product of Kronecker’s deltas
\delta_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}\delta_{\alpha_{3}\alpha_{4}}\ldots\delta_{\alpha_{2n-1}\alpha_{2n}}
plus all possible permutations of indices; there are
(2n-1)!!
terms in total. For example,

\delta_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\alpha_{3}\alpha_{4}}=\delta_{\alpha_{1}}^{\alpha_{2}}\delta^{\alpha_{3}}_{\alpha_{4}}+\delta_{\alpha_{1}}^{\alpha_{3}}\delta^{\alpha_{2}}_{\alpha_{4}}+\delta_{\alpha_{1}}^{\alpha_{4}}\delta_{\alpha_{2}}^{\alpha_{3}}.

(136)

Finally, combining (
3
) and (
134
) we come to the Eq. (
14
).

Appendix C
Proof of the formula (
II.3
)

Here we present some details related with obtaining of Eq. (
II.3
). Let us first consider the case when
n=1

\displaystyle\mbox{tr}_{f}\left[e^{-tM^{2}}f_{1}(t,A)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k}^{3}e^{-tm_{i}^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}ds\,e^{s(m_{j}^{2}-m_{k}^{2})}\mbox{tr}_{f}(E_{i}E_{j}AE_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}te^{-tm^{2}_{i}}\mbox{tr}_{f}A_{i}.

(137)

Hence,

c_{i}(t)=e^{-tm_{i}^{2}}.

(138)

For
n=2
we have

\displaystyle\mbox{tr}_{f}\left[e^{-tM^{2}}f_{2}(t,A)\right]=\sum_{i,j}^{3}\int_{0}^{t}ds_{1}\int_{0}^{s_{1}}ds_{2}

\displaystyle\times e^{(s_{1}-s_{2}-t)m_{i}^{2}}e^{(s_{2}-s_{1})m_{j}^{2}}\mbox{tr}_{f}(A_{i}A_{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}^{3}\left(t\,\frac{e^{-tm^{2}_{i}}}{\Delta_{ji}}+\frac{e^{-tm^{2}_{j}}-e^{-tm_{i}^{2}}}{\Delta_{ij}^{2}}\right)\mbox{tr}_{f}(A_{i}A_{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}^{3}\frac{t}{2\Delta_{ji}}\left(e^{-tm_{i}^{2}}-e^{-tm_{j}^{2}}\right)\mbox{tr}_{f}(A_{i}A_{j}).

(139)

Here
\Delta_{ij}=m_{i}^{2}-m_{j}^{2}
, and on the last stage we used that trace is symmetric under an exchange
i\leftrightarrow j
. It gives

c_{i_{1}i_{2}}(t)=\frac{1}{t}\sum_{perm}\frac{c_{i_{1}}(t)}{\Delta_{i_{2}i_{1}}}\,,

(140)

the sum includes two terms obtained by cyclically permuting the indices
(i_{1},i_{2})\to(i_{2},i_{1})
.

For
n=3

\displaystyle\mbox{tr}_{f}\left[e^{-tM^{2}}f_{3}(t,A)\right]=\sum_{i,j,k}^{3}\int_{0}^{t}\!\!ds_{1}\!\int_{0}^{s_{1}}\!\!\!ds_{2}\!\int_{0}^{s_{2}}\!\!\!ds_{3}

\displaystyle\times e^{(s_{1}-s_{3}-t)m_{i}^{2}}e^{(s_{2}-s_{1})m_{j}^{2}}e^{(s_{3}-s_{2})m_{k}^{2}}\mbox{tr}_{f}(A_{i}A_{j}A_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k}^{3}\left(\frac{tc_{i}(t)}{\Delta_{ij}\Delta_{ik}}+\frac{c_{i}(t)-c_{j}(t)}{\Delta_{ij}^{2}\Delta_{jk}}+\frac{c_{i}(t)-c_{k}(t)}{\Delta_{ik}^{2}\Delta_{kj}}\right)

\displaystyle\times\,\mbox{tr}_{f}(A_{i}A_{j}A_{k}).

(141)

Since the trace is symmetric with respect to the cyclic permutation of matrices, one can add to this expression two others, obtained by cyclic permutations of indices
(i,j,k)\to(j,k,i)
and
(i,j,k)\to(k,i,j)
, and the result should be divided by three. As a result, only the first term in parentheses will lead to the non zero contribution. This yields the following expression for the coefficient

c_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}(t)=\frac{2!}{t^{2}}\sum_{perm}\frac{c_{i_{1}}(t)}{\Delta_{i_{2}i_{1}}\Delta_{i_{3}i_{1}}}\,.

(142)

Consider the case
n=4
. Here we have

\displaystyle\mbox{tr}_{f}\left[e^{-tM^{2}}f_{4}(t,A)\right]=\sum_{i,j,k,l}^{3}\int_{0}^{t}\!\!ds_{1}\!\int_{0}^{s_{1}}\!\!\!ds_{2}\!\int_{0}^{s_{2}}\!\!\!ds_{3}\!\int_{0}^{s_{3}}\!\!\!ds_{4}

\displaystyle\times e^{(s_{1}-s_{4}-t)m_{i}^{2}}e^{(s_{2}-s_{1})m_{j}^{2}}e^{(s_{3}-s_{2})m_{k}^{2}}e^{(s_{4}-s_{3})m_{l}^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\mbox{tr}_{f}(A_{i}A_{j}A_{k}A_{l})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k,l}^{3}\left(\frac{tc_{i}(t)}{\Delta_{ji}\Delta_{ki}\Delta_{li}}+\frac{c_{j}(t)-c_{i}(t)}{\Delta_{ij}^{2}\Delta_{kj}\Delta_{lj}}+\frac{c_{k}(t)-c_{i}(t)}{\Delta_{ik}^{2}\Delta_{lk}\Delta_{jk}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{c_{l}(t)-c_{i}(t)}{\Delta_{il}^{2}\Delta_{kl}\Delta_{jl}}\right)\mbox{tr}_{f}(A_{i}A_{j}A_{k}A_{l}).

(143)

By using again the cyclic permutation property of trace, one can easily find out that only the first term in parentheses will contribute with a non zero result. Here we have four cyclic permutations of indices, correspondingly it gives four terms for the coefficient

c_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}i_{4}}(t)=\frac{3!}{t^{3}}\sum_{perm}\frac{c_{i_{1}}(t)}{\Delta_{i_{2}i_{1}}\Delta_{i_{3}i_{1}}\Delta_{i_{4}i_{1}}}\,.

(144)

Continuing the above procedure, we can find by induction an arbitrary coefficient in formula (
II.3
)

c_{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots i_{n}}(t)=\frac{(n-1)!}{t^{n-1}}\!\!\sum_{perm}\!\frac{c_{i_{1}}(t)}{\Delta_{i_{2}i_{1}}\Delta_{i_{3}i_{1}}\ldots\Delta_{i_{n}i_{1}}}\,.

(145)

Although the sum includes
n
terms obtained by cyclically permuting the indices
i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{n}
, it is easy to show that the coefficients
c_{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots i_{n}}
are
totally
symmetric.

One can also establish a recurrence formula relating coefficients of order
n
,
c_{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots i_{n}}(t)
, for
n>1
, with coefficients of order
n-1

c_{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots i_{n}}(t)=\frac{n-1}{t\Delta_{i_{n-1}i_{n}}}\left[c_{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots i_{n-2}i_{n}}(t)-c_{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots i_{n-1}}(t)\right].

(146)

This relation follows from the following easily verified identities

\displaystyle\sum_{perm}\frac{1}{\Delta_{i_{2}i_{1}}}=0,

(147)

\displaystyle\sum_{perm}\frac{1}{\Delta_{i_{2}i_{1}}\Delta_{i_{3}i_{1}}}=0,

(148)

\displaystyle\frac{}{}\ldots\ldots\ldots\ldots\ldots\ldots\ldots{}

\displaystyle\sum_{perm}\!\frac{1}{\Delta_{i_{2}i_{1}}\Delta_{i_{3}i_{1}}\ldots\Delta_{i_{n}i_{1}}}=0.

(149)

Recurrent formula (
146
), inter alia, simplifies the proof of the relations

c_{i}(t)=c_{ii}(t)=c_{ii\ldots i}(t)\,.

(150)

Indeed, one has, for instance,

\displaystyle c_{ii}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{m_{i}\to m_{j}}c_{ij}(t)=\lim_{m_{i}\to m_{j}}\frac{e^{-tm_{j}^{2}}-e^{-tm_{i}^{2}}}{t\Delta_{ij}}

(151)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{m_{i}\to m_{j}}e^{-tm_{i}^{2}}\frac{e^{t\Delta_{ij}}-1}{t\Delta_{ij}}=c_{i}(t).

Using (
146
) we can extend this to any
c_{ii\ldots i}(t)
case.

Appendix D
Integrals over proper-time

If the masses of the Fermi fields are non-degenerate, integration over the proper-time leads to expressions depending on the specific masses of the constituent quarks in the corresponding Feynman diagram. Accordingly, the number of different loop integrals increases. We will classify the integrals in accord with the number of internal quark lines presented in the loop diagram. Since we are dealing with an
1/M
expansion, the integrals presented below give only the leading terms of this expansion. Along with separating the main divergences of the Feynman diagrams, such integrals contain finite contributions that vanish in the case of equal quark masses. These finite contributions are uniquely determined by the coefficients
c_{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots i_{n}}
that are completely symmetric with respect to the permutation of any two indices.

D.1
Tadpole diagrams

The diagrams with only one internal quark line (tadpole) are associated with the proper-time integral

\int\!\frac{dt}{t^{2}}\,\rho_{t,\Lambda}c_{i}(t)=J_{0}(m_{i}^{2})\,.

(152)

We will use Pauli-Villars regularization corresponding to two subtractions at the mass-scale
\Lambda

\rho_{t,\Lambda}=1-(1+t\Lambda^{2})e^{-t\Lambda^{2}}.

(153)

In this case, it follows that

J_{0}(m_{i}^{2})=\Lambda^{2}-m_{i}^{2}\ln\left(1+\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{m_{i}^{2}}\right)\,.

(154)

This obviously coincides with (
19
).

D.2
Self-energy diagrams

The quark-loop self-energy graphs are described by the proper-time integrals

\int\!\frac{dt}{t^{2-n}}\,\rho_{t,\Lambda}c_{ij}(t)=J_{n}(m_{i}^{2},m_{j}^{2})\,.

(155)

Due to the symmetry of the coefficients
c_{ij}=c_{ji}
the result of integration is symmetric with respect to the replacement
m_{i}\leftrightarrow m_{j}
. Indeed, in the case
n=0
, we have

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!J_{0}(m_{i}^{2},m_{j}^{2})=\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{2}+\frac{\Lambda^{4}}{2\Delta_{ij}}\ln\frac{\Lambda^{2}+m_{i}^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}+m_{j}^{2}}

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!-\frac{1}{2\Delta_{ij}}\left[m_{i}^{4}\ln\left(1+\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{m_{i}^{2}}\right)-m_{j}^{4}\ln\left(1+\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{m_{j}^{2}}\right)\right]\!,

(156)

where
\Delta_{ij}=m_{i}^{2}-m_{j}^{2}
. In the coincidence limit
m_{i}\to m_{j}
,

\lim_{m_{i}\to m_{j}}J_{0}(m_{i}^{2},m_{j}^{2})=J_{0}(m_{i}^{2})\,.

The other integral for
n=1
is given by the difference of the tadpole contributions

\displaystyle J_{1}(m_{i}^{2},m_{j}^{2})=\frac{1}{\Delta_{ij}}\left[J_{0}(m_{j}^{2})-J_{0}(m_{i}^{2})\right]

(157)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Delta_{ij}}\left[m_{i}^{2}\ln\left(1+\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{m_{i}^{2}}\right)-m_{j}^{2}\ln\left(1+\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{m_{j}^{2}}\right)\right].

Since we also have expressions for the similar
J_{1}
-type contributions coming from the triangle and box diagrams, we will collect them together in one formula

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}J_{ij}\\
J_{ijk}\\
J_{iijk}\\
\end{array}\right)\equiv\!\!\int\limits^{\infty}_{0}\frac{dt}{t}\,\rho_{t,\Lambda}\,\left(\begin{array}[]{c}c_{ij}(t)\\
c_{ijk}(t)\\
c_{iijk}(t)\\
\end{array}\right).

(158)

From Eq. (
150
), it follows that

J_{ii}=J_{iii}=J_{iiii}=J_{1}(m_{i}^{2})\equiv J_{i}.

(159)

The integrals take this form when all quark masses in the loop are equal.

D.3
Triangle diagrams

The proper-time integral

\int\!\frac{dt}{t}\,\rho_{t,\Lambda}c_{ijk}(t)=J_{1}(m_{i}^{2},m_{j}^{2},m_{k}^{2})\equiv J_{ijk}

(160)

describes a local part of the diagram with three internal quark lines. As before, the full symmetry of the coefficient
c_{ijk}
allows one to conclude that this integral does not change when any two fermion masses are exchanged. To verify this, we use the formula (
146
), which at the first stage of calculations leads to a seemingly asymmetric result

J_{ijk}=\frac{2}{\Delta_{jk}}\left[J_{0}(m_{i}^{2},m_{k}^{2})-J_{0}(m_{i}^{2},m_{j}^{2})\right],

(161)

where
J_{0}(m_{i}^{2},m_{j}^{2})
is given by Eq. (
D.2
). However, the direct calculation of the right hand side of this expression shows explicitly its total symmetry

\displaystyle J_{1}(m_{i}^{2},m_{j}^{2},m_{k}^{2})=\frac{1}{\Delta_{ij}\Delta_{jk}\Delta_{ki}}\left[\Lambda^{4}\left(m_{i}^{2}\ln\frac{\Lambda^{2}+m_{k}^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}+m_{j}^{2}}+m_{j}^{2}\ln\frac{\Lambda^{2}+m_{i}^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}+m_{k}^{2}}+m_{k}^{2}\ln\frac{\Lambda^{2}+m_{j}^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}+m_{i}^{2}}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+m_{i}^{4}\Delta_{kj}\ln\left(1+\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{m_{i}^{2}}\right)+m_{j}^{4}\Delta_{ik}\ln\left(1+\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{m_{j}^{2}}\right)+m_{k}^{4}\Delta_{ji}\ln\left(1+\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{m_{k}^{2}}\right)\right]\,.

(162)

This expression allows to obtain a particular value corresponding to the cases with two equal masses:

\displaystyle J_{1}(m_{i}^{2},m_{i}^{2},m_{j}^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\Delta_{ij}}\left[J_{0}(m_{i}^{2},m_{j}^{2})-J_{0}(m_{i}^{2})\right]=\frac{1}{\Delta_{ij}}\left[2m_{i}^{2}\ln\left(1+\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{m_{i}^{2}}\right)-\Lambda^{2}\right]

(163)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Delta_{ij}^{2}}\left[m_{j}^{4}\ln\left(1+\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{m_{j}^{2}}\right)-m_{i}^{4}\ln\left(1+\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{m_{i}^{2}}\right)+\Lambda^{4}\ln\frac{\Lambda^{2}+m_{i}^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}+m_{j}^{2}}\right]\,.

It should be noted that in the theory considered, there are only three different flavor states of quarks:
u,d
and
s
. Therefore, Eq. (
163
) describes the six possible cases:
uud
,
uus
,
ddu
,
dds
,
ssu
, and
ssd
. Notice also that a contribution, for instance, of
uus
triangle differs from
ssu
one. The difference is antisymmetric with respect to the replacement
m_{i}\leftrightarrow m_{j}

J_{iij}-J_{jji}=\frac{2}{\Delta_{ij}}\Delta J_{0}(m_{i}^{2},m_{j}^{2}),

(164)

where

\Delta J_{0}(m_{i}^{2},m_{j}^{2})=2J_{0}(m_{i}^{2},m_{j}^{2})-J_{0}(m_{i}^{2})-J_{0}(m_{j}^{2})\,.

(165)

This value is finite at
\Lambda\to\infty
. The sum is

J_{iij}+J_{jji}=2J_{1}(m_{i}^{2},m_{j}^{2})\,.

(166)

As a consequence, we have

J_{1}(m_{i}^{2},m_{i}^{2},m_{j}^{2})=J_{1}(m_{i}^{2},m_{j}^{2})+R(m_{i}^{2},m_{j}^{2}),

(167)

where antisymmetric part of this expression is defined as

R(m_{i}^{2},m_{j}^{2})=\frac{\Delta J_{0}(m_{i}^{2},m_{j}^{2})}{\Delta_{ij}}\equiv R_{ij}\,.

(168)

D.4
Box diagrams

Consider the last of the divergent-type expressions that arise when one calculates the quark box diagrams with external meson fields. These are given by the integrals of the form

\int\!\frac{dt}{t}\,\rho_{t,\Lambda}\,c_{ijkl}(t)=J_{1}(m_{i}^{2},m_{j}^{2},m_{k}^{2},m_{l}^{2})\,.

(169)

Since quarks have only three flavors, at least two indices in
c_{ijkl}
coincide. Therefore, we actually need to calculate the master integral

\int\!\frac{dt}{t}\,\rho_{t,\Lambda}c_{iijk}(t)=J_{iijk}\,.

(170)

To this end, we again use the formula (
146
), which in this case takes the form

c_{iijk}(t)=\frac{3}{t\Delta_{kj}}\left[c_{iij}(t)-c_{iik}(t)\right].

(171)

The quadratic divergences arising when one integrates each of the coefficients
c_{iij}(t)
are canceled in the difference (
171
). As a result, we arrive at a logarithmically divergent (at
\Lambda\to\infty
) expression

\displaystyle J_{iijk}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Delta_{ij}\Delta_{ik}}\left[3m_{i}^{4}\ln\left(1+\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{m_{i}^{2}}\right)-\Lambda^{2}(2\Lambda^{2}+m_{i}^{2})\right]

(172)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Delta_{kj}}\left\{\frac{1}{\Delta_{ij}^{2}}\left[m_{i}^{6}\ln\left(1+\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{m_{i}^{2}}\right)-m_{j}^{6}\ln\left(1+\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{m_{j}^{2}}\right)+\Lambda^{4}\left(2\Lambda^{2}+3m_{j}^{2}\right)\ln\frac{\Lambda^{2}+m_{j}^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}+m_{i}^{2}}\right]\right.

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\left.\frac{1}{\Delta_{ik}^{2}}\left[m_{i}^{6}\ln\left(1+\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{m_{i}^{2}}\right)-m_{k}^{6}\ln\left(1+\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{m_{k}^{2}}\right)+\Lambda^{4}\left(2\Lambda^{2}+3m_{k}^{2}\right)\ln\frac{\Lambda^{2}+m_{k}^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}+m_{i}^{2}}\right]\right\}\,.

Notice, that in full accord with (
171
) we have

J_{iijk}=J_{iikj}.

(173)

Cases with two different types of flavors can be obtained from this expression by passing to the corresponding limits of equal masses. There are two of them:
J_{iijj}
and
J_{iiij}
. As a result, we have

\displaystyle J_{iijj}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Delta_{ij}^{2}}\left\{m_{i}^{4}\left(1-2\frac{m_{j}^{2}}{\Delta_{ij}}\right)\ln\left(1+\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{m_{i}^{2}}\right)+m_{j}^{4}\left(1-2\frac{m_{i}^{2}}{\Delta_{ji}}\right)\ln\left(1+\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{m_{j}^{2}}\right)\right.

(174)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\Lambda^{2}(4\Lambda^{2}+m_{i}^{2}+m_{j}^{2})-\left.\Lambda^{4}\left[3+\frac{2}{\Delta_{ij}}(2\Lambda^{2}+3m_{j}^{2})\right]\ln\frac{\Lambda^{2}+m_{j}^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}+m_{i}^{2}}\right\}\,.

\displaystyle J_{iiij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Delta_{ij}^{3}}\left\{m_{i}^{6}\ln\left(1+\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{m_{i}^{2}}\right)-m_{j}^{6}\ln\left(1+\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{m_{j}^{2}}\right)+\Delta_{ij}\Lambda^{2}(2\Lambda^{2}-m_{i}^{2}+2m_{j}^{2})\right.

(175)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle 3\Delta_{ij}m_{i}^{2}m_{j}^{2}\ln\left(1+\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{m_{i}^{2}}\right)+\left.\Lambda^{4}(2\Lambda^{2}+3m_{j}^{2})\ln\frac{\Lambda^{2}+m_{j}^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}+m_{i}^{2}}\right\}\,.

From (

J_{iijj}=J_{ij}-S(m_{i}^{2},m_{j}^{2}),

(176)

where

\displaystyle S(m_{i}^{2},m_{j}^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Delta_{ij}^{3}}\left\{m_{i}^{2}m_{j}^{2}(m_{i}^{2}+m_{j}^{2})\ln\frac{(\Lambda^{2}+m_{i}^{2})m_{j}^{2}}{(\Lambda^{2}+m_{j}^{2})m_{i}^{2}}\right.

(177)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left.\Lambda^{4}\left[4\Lambda^{2}+3(m_{i}^{2}+m_{j}^{2})\right]\ln\frac{\Lambda^{2}+m_{j}^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}+m_{i}^{2}}\right\}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{\Delta_{ij}^{2}}(4\Lambda^{2}+m_{i}^{2}+m_{j}^{2}).

The latter expression is symmetric under exchange of masses and finite at
\Lambda\to\infty

Eq. (

) describes the six possible variants of the distribution of quarks along the inner lines of the box diagram, these are
uuud
,
uuus
,
dddu
,
ddds

sssu

J_{iiij}-J_{jjji}=3R_{ij}\,,

(178)

where

J_{iiij}+J_{jjji}=2J_{1}(m_{i}^{2},m_{j}^{2})+S(m_{i}^{2},m_{j}^{2})\,.

(179)

Appendix E
Mixed sums for
b_{n}(x,x)

In the main text we make use summations which contain both the mass-dependent remnant,
J_{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots i_{n}}
, of one-loop quark integrals and the corresponding field-dependent coefficients
T_{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots i_{n}}
. The result of such summations depends on the properties of
J_{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots i_{n}}
which were established in Appendix
D
. Here some important results on these sums are presented.

The simplest case is given by the sum

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}J_{ij}T_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}J_{ij}T_{(ij)}

(180)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{3}J_{i}T_{ii}+2\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}T_{(ij)},

where

T_{(ij)}=\frac{1}{2}\left(T_{ij}+T_{ji}\right).

(181)

Consider now the sum related with the triangle diagrams. The corresponding one-loop integral induces at leading order of its large mass expansion the function
J_{ijk}
, which is known to be completely symmetric under any exchange of masses. Due to this symmetry we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,k}J_{ijk}\,T_{ijk}=\sum_{i,j,k}J_{ijk}\,T_{(ijk)}

(182)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}J_{i}T_{iii}+3\sum_{i\neq j}J_{iij}T_{(iij)}+6J_{123}T_{(123)},

where

T_{(ijk)}=\frac{1}{6}\left(T_{ijk}+T_{jik}+T_{ikj}+T_{jki}+T_{kij}+T_{kji}\right).

(183)

is a totally symmetric function of quark masses. The sum has
3+3\times 6+6=27
terms in all, as it should be. Now we make use Eq. (
167
) to obtain

\sum_{i\neq j}J_{iij}T_{(iij)}=\sum_{i<j}\left(J_{ij}T_{iij}^{+}+R_{ij}T_{iij}^{-}\right),

(184)

where
T_{iij}^{\pm}=T_{(iij)}\pm T_{(jji)}
.
As a result we finally have

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,k}J_{ijk}\,T_{ijk}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}J_{i}T_{iii}+3\sum_{i<j}\left(J_{ij}T_{iij}^{+}+R_{ij}T_{iij}^{-}\right)

(185)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 6J_{123}T_{(123)}.

The next sum describes the contribution of the fermion box diagrams with external spin-1 fields to the coefficient
b_{2}(x,x)
. Here we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,k,l}J_{ijkl}T_{ijkl}=\sum_{i,j,k,l}J_{ijkl}T_{(ijkl)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}J_{i}T_{iiii}+4\sum_{i\neq j}J_{iiij}T_{(iiij)}+6\sum_{i<j}J_{iijj}T_{(iijj)}

\displaystyle+12\sum_{i\neq j\neq k\atop j<k}J_{iijk}T_{(iijk)}.

(186)

This formula is written for the specific case when each of the indices
i,j,k,l
takes only three possible values
1,2
, or
3
(we consider fermion fields with three flavors), so
T_{ijkl}
has at least two equal indices. As a result, the sum (
E
) has
3+4\times 6+6\times 3+12\times 3=81
terms, and we need to know only the following coefficients

\displaystyle T_{(iiij)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left(T_{iiij}+T_{iiji}+T_{ijii}+T_{jiii}\right),

(187)

\displaystyle T_{(iijj)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{6}\left(T_{iijj}+T_{jjii}+T_{ijij}+T_{jiji}+T_{ijji}+T_{jiij}\right),

\displaystyle T_{(iijk)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{12}\left(T_{iijk}+T_{iikj}+T_{kiij}+T_{jiik}+T_{jkii}+T_{kjii}\right.

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left.T_{ijik}+T_{ikij}+T_{jiki}+T_{kiji}+T_{ijki}+T_{ikji}\right).

With the use of (
178
) and (
179
) one can set apart the divergent part of the proper-time integral
J_{iiij}
in the form
J_{ij}
, and as a result to obtain that

\displaystyle\sum_{i\neq j}J_{iiij}T_{(iiij)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i<j}\left(J_{ij}+\frac{1}{2}S_{ij}\right)T^{+}_{iiij}

(188)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{3}{2}\sum_{i<j}R_{ij}T^{-}_{iiij},

where
T_{iiij}^{\pm}=T_{(iiij)}\pm T_{(jjji)}
, and
S_{ij}=S(m_{i}^{2},m^{2}_{j})
.

Thus, finally (
E
) can be expressed as a sum

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,k,l}J_{ijkl}T_{ijkl}=\sum_{i}J_{i}T_{iiii}

\displaystyle+4\sum_{i<j}\left[\left(J_{ij}+\frac{1}{2}S_{ij}\right)T^{+}_{iiij}+\frac{3}{2}R_{ij}T^{-}_{iiij}\right]

\displaystyle+6\sum_{i<j}\left(J_{ij}-S_{ij}\right)T_{(iijj)}+12\!\!\!\sum_{i\neq j\neq k,\atop j<k}\!\!\!J_{iijk}T_{(iijk)}.

(189)

Appendix F
Properties of t-coefficients

It is the purpose of this Appendix to establish some important properties of t-coefficients which have been used in the computation of the effective action.

We begin from the coefficient
\hat{t}_{ijk}
given by Eq. (
62
). For that we need the following expressions

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!d^{\alpha}_{ij}=\delta_{ij}\partial^{\alpha}+i\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij},

(190)

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!(d^{2})_{ij}=\sum_{k}d^{\alpha}_{ik}d^{\alpha}_{kj}

\displaystyle\ =\delta_{ij}\partial^{2}+2i\Gamma_{ij}^{\alpha}\partial^{\alpha}+i(\partial^{\alpha}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij})-(\Gamma^{2})_{ij}.

(191)

Then for the products we have

\displaystyle d^{\alpha}_{ij}d^{\alpha}_{jk}(d^{2})_{ki}=\delta_{jk}(\partial\Gamma_{ij})\left[(\partial\Gamma_{ki})+i(\Gamma^{2})_{ki}\right]

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ -\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{jk}\left[i(\partial\Gamma_{ki})-(\Gamma^{2})_{ki}\right],

(192)

\displaystyle d^{\alpha}_{jk}(d^{2})_{ki}d^{\alpha}_{ij}=\delta_{ki}(\partial^{\beta}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{jk})(\partial^{\beta}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij})

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ +i\Gamma^{\beta}_{ki}(\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij}\!\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftrightarrow}}{{\partial^{\beta}}}\!\Gamma^{\alpha}_{jk})+\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{jk}(\Gamma^{2})_{ki},

(193)

\displaystyle(d^{2})_{ki}d^{\alpha}_{ij}d^{\alpha}_{jk}=\delta_{ij}(\partial\Gamma_{jk})\left[(\partial\Gamma_{ki})-i(\Gamma^{2})_{ki}\right]

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ +\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{jk}\left[i(\partial\Gamma_{ki})+(\Gamma^{2})_{ki}\right],

(194)

where a total divergence and open derivatives on the right hand side were omitted. It gives

\displaystyle\hat{t}_{ijk}=\frac{1}{3}\left[\frac{}{}\delta_{ij}(\partial\Gamma_{ki})(\partial\Gamma_{jk})+\delta_{jk}(\partial\Gamma_{ij})(\partial\Gamma_{ki})\right.

\displaystyle+\delta_{ki}(\partial^{\beta}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{jk})(\partial^{\beta}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij})+i(\delta_{jk}\partial\Gamma_{ij}-\delta_{ij}\partial\Gamma_{jk})(\Gamma^{2})_{ki}

\displaystyle\left.+i\Gamma^{\beta}_{ki}(\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij}\!\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftrightarrow}}{{\partial^{\beta}}}\!\Gamma^{\alpha}_{jk})\right]+\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{jk}(\Gamma^{2})_{ki}\,.

(195)

From this formula, in particular, one finds

\displaystyle 3\hat{t}^{+}_{iij}=\hat{t}_{iij}+\hat{t}_{iji}+\hat{t}_{jii}+(i\leftrightarrow j)=\frac{2}{3}(\partial\Gamma_{ij})(\partial\Gamma_{ji})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{3}(\partial^{\beta}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij})(\partial^{\beta}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ji})+\frac{i}{3}\left[F^{\alpha\beta}_{ij}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ji}(\Gamma^{\beta}_{ii}-\Gamma^{\beta}_{jj})\right.

\displaystyle\left.+F^{\alpha\beta}_{ii}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij}\Gamma^{\beta}_{ji}\right]+\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ji}(\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ii}+\Gamma^{\alpha}_{jj})(\Gamma^{2})_{ij}+\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ji}(\Gamma^{2})_{ii}

\displaystyle+(i\leftrightarrow j).

(196)

and

\displaystyle 3\hat{t}^{-}_{iij}=\hat{t}_{iij}+\hat{t}_{iji}+\hat{t}_{jii}-(i\leftrightarrow j)=\frac{2i}{3}(\partial\Gamma_{ji})(\Gamma^{2})_{ij}

\displaystyle+\frac{i}{3}\left[F^{\alpha\beta}_{ij}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ji}(\Gamma^{\beta}_{ii}+\Gamma^{\beta}_{jj})+F^{\alpha\beta}_{ii}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij}\Gamma^{\beta}_{ji}\right]

\displaystyle+\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ji}(\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ii}-\Gamma^{\alpha}_{jj})(\Gamma^{2})_{ij}+\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ji}(\Gamma^{2})_{ii}-(i\leftrightarrow j).

(197)

From (
F

Now consider the coefficient

\displaystyle t_{ijkl}=\frac{1}{4}\delta_{\alpha\beta\gamma\sigma}\left[4\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij}\Gamma^{\beta}_{jk}\Gamma^{\gamma}_{kl}\Gamma^{\sigma}_{li}\right.

\displaystyle+\delta_{jk}\delta_{kl}(\partial^{\beta}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij})(\partial^{\gamma}\Gamma^{\sigma}_{li})+\delta_{kl}\delta_{li}(\partial^{\gamma}\Gamma^{\beta}_{jk})(\partial^{\sigma}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij})

\displaystyle+\delta_{li}\delta_{ij}(\partial^{\sigma}\Gamma^{\gamma}_{kl})(\partial^{\alpha}\Gamma^{\beta}_{jk})+\delta_{ij}\delta_{jk}(\partial^{\alpha}\Gamma^{\sigma}_{li})(\partial^{\beta}\Gamma^{\gamma}_{kl})

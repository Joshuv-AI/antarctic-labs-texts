Explicit inverse of symmetric, tridiagonal near Toeplitz matrices Part II: with weakly diagonally dominant Toeplitz
Bakytzhan Kurmanbek, Yogi Erlangga, Yerlan Amanbek

Abstract
In this paper, we provide explicit formulas for the exact inverses of the symmetric tridiagonal near-Toeplitz matrices characterized by weak diagonal dominance in the Toeplitz part. Furthermore, these findings extend to scenarios where the corners of the near Toeplitz matrices lack diagonal dominance ( | b ~ | < 1 |\widetilde{b}|<1 ). Additionally, we compute the row sums and traces of the inverse matrices, thereby deriving upper bounds for their infinite norms. To demonstrate the practical applicability of our theoretical results, we present numerical examples addressing numerical solution of the Fisher problem using the fixed point method. Our findings reveal that the convergence rates of fixed-point iterations closely align with the expected rates, and there is minimal disparity between the upper bounds and the infinite norm of the inverse matrix. Specifically, this observation holds true for | b | = 2 |b|=2 with | b ~ | ≥ 1 |\widetilde{b}|\geq 1 . In other cases, there exists potential to enhance the obtained upper bounds.

Consider the symmetric tridiagonal near-Toeplitz matrix

\widehat{T}_{n}=\begin{bmatrix}\overline{b}&\hat{c}&&&\\

\widetilde{T}^{-1}_{n}=T^{-1}_{n}-(\widetilde{b}-b)T^{-1}_{n}UM^{-1}U^{T}T^{-1}_{n},

\widetilde{T}^{-1}_{n}=[\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,j}]

The elements of the 2-by-2 matrix

\displaystyle m_{11}=m_{22}

\displaystyle=1+(\widetilde{b}-b)t^{-1}_{1,1}=1+\beta t^{-1}_{n,n},

\displaystyle m_{12}=m_{21}

\displaystyle=(\widetilde{b}-b)t^{-1}_{1,n}=\beta t^{-1}_{n,1},

With the inverse of

\widetilde{T}^{-1}_{n}

\text{Tr}(\widetilde{T}^{-1}_{n}):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}t^{-1}_{i,i}-\frac{\beta}{\Delta}\left\{m_{11}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left((t^{-1}_{i,1})^{2}+(t^{-1}_{n,i})^{2}\right)-2m_{12}\sum_{i=1}^{n}t^{-1}_{i,1}t^{-1}_{n,i}\right\}.

(1.10)

Furthermore, the sum of the elements in the
i

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}t^{-1}_{i,j}-\frac{\beta}{\Delta}\left[t_{i,1}^{-1}\left(m_{11}\sum_{j=1}^{n}t^{-1}_{1,j}-m_{12}\sum_{j=1}^{n}t^{-1}_{n,j}\right)+t^{-1}_{i,n}\left(-m_{12}\sum_{j=1}^{n}t^{-1}_{1,j}+m_{11}\sum_{j=1}^{n}t^{-1}_{n,j}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}t^{-1}_{i,j}-\frac{\beta}{m_{11}+m_{12}}(t^{-1}_{i,1}+t^{-1}_{i,n})\sum_{j=1}^{n}t^{-1}_{n,j},

(1.11)

T_{n}
and
T^{-1}_{n}

Preliminary results

In this section, we summarize some preliminary results, which are useful in deriving the properties of
\widetilde{T}^{-1}_{n}
. We begin with the result of
[
1
]
regarding elements of the inverse of the symmetric tridiagonal Toeplitz matrix
T_{n}
when
b=\pm 2
: for
i\geq j
,

t^{-1}_{i,j}=\left(\frac{2}{b}\right)^{i+1-j}\frac{j(n+1-i)}{n+1}.

(2.1)

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\widetilde{T}_{n}
be invertible with
b=\pm 2
. Then the elements of the inverse matrix
\widetilde{T}_{n}^{-1}
are, for
i\geq j
,

\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,j}=\left(\frac{2}{b}\right)^{i+1-j}\frac{\left(j\left(1+\frac{2\beta}{b}\right)-\frac{2\beta}{b}\right)\left((n-i)\left(1+\frac{2\beta}{b}\right)+1\right)}{\left(1+\frac{2\beta}{b}\right)\left(n+1+\frac{2\beta}{b}(n-1)\right)},

(2.2)

where
\beta=\widetilde{b}-b
, and
\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,j}=\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{j,i}
for
i<j
.

Proof.

We first note that, using (
2.1
), the equations in (
1.8
) become

\displaystyle m_{11}=1+\frac{2\beta}{b}\frac{n}{n+1}\quad\text{and}\quad m_{12}=\beta\left(\frac{2}{b}\right)^{n}\frac{1}{n+1}.

Using the above expressions and (
2.1
), the two sums in the right-hand side of (
1.9
) can be calculated:

\displaystyle m_{11}t^{-1}_{1,j}-m_{12}t^{-1}_{n,j}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2}{b}\right)^{j}\frac{n+1-j+\frac{2\beta}{b}(n-j)}{n+1},

\displaystyle-m_{12}t^{-1}_{1,j}+m_{11}t^{-1}_{n,j}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2}{b}\right)^{n-1-j}\frac{j+\frac{2\beta}{b}(j-1)}{n+1},

which in turn gives

\displaystyle t^{-1}_{i,1}(m_{11}t^{-1}_{1,j}-m_{12}t^{-1}_{n,j})+t^{-1}_{i,n}(-m_{12}t^{-1}_{1,j}+m_{11}t^{-1}_{n,j})=\left(\frac{2}{b}\right)^{i+j}\left[\left(1+\frac{2\beta}{b}\right)\left(1-\frac{i+j}{n+1}+\frac{2ij}{(n+1)^{2}}\right)-\frac{\frac{2\beta}{b}}{n+1}\right]

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\Delta=m^{2}_{11}-m^{2}_{12}=\frac{\left(1+\frac{2\beta}{b}\right)\left(n+1+\frac{2\beta}{b}(n-1)\right)}{n+1}.

Substitution of the last two expressions above and simplification results in the formula stated in the lemma.

∎

Lemma 2.2

(Singularity)
.

Let
b=\pm 2
. The matrix
\widetilde{T}_{n}
is singular if
\widetilde{b}=\text{sgn}(b)
and
\widetilde{b}=\text{sgn}(b)\frac{(n-3)}{(n-1)}
. Moreover, for
|\widetilde{b}|\geq 1
,
\Delta\geq 0
.

Proof.

The singularity of
\widetilde{T}_{n}
occurs if the determinant
\Delta=0
or
m_{11}=\pm m_{12}
, which after using (
1.8
), is equivalent to

\displaystyle 1+\frac{2\beta}{b}\frac{n}{n+1}=\pm\beta\left(\frac{2}{b}\right)^{n}\frac{1}{n+1}.

Rearranging terms, with
\beta=\widetilde{b}-b
, results in

\displaystyle\widetilde{b}=b-\frac{b(n+1)}{2}\frac{1}{n\mp\left(\frac{2}{b}\right)^{n-1}}.

For
b=2
, the above equation gives
\widetilde{b}=1
or
\widetilde{b}=(n-3)/(n-1)
. For
b=-2
, the above equation gives
\widetilde{b}=-1
and
\widetilde{b}=-(n-3)/(n-1)
. These lead to the first part of the lemma.

The determinant
\Delta
can be simplified to a quadratic equation in
\widetilde{b}
, for
b=\pm 2
:

\displaystyle\Delta=\frac{n-1}{n+1}\left(\widetilde{b}\mp 1\right)\left(\widetilde{b}\mp\frac{n-3}{n-1}\right).

The second part of the lemma is obtained by checking the sign of
\Delta
, noting that
(n-3)/(n-1)<1
.

∎

Lemma 2.3

(General Trace)
.

Let
\widetilde{T}_{n}
be invertible with
b=\pm 2
and
\beta=\widetilde{b}-b
. The trace of the inverse matrix
\widetilde{T}^{-1}_{n}
is

\displaystyle\text{Tr}(\widetilde{T}^{-1}_{n})=\frac{n(n+2)}{3b}-\frac{2\beta n}{3\left(1+\frac{2\beta}{b}\right)}+\frac{\beta n}{3\left(1+\frac{2\beta}{b}\right)\left(n+1+\frac{2\beta}{b}(n-1)\right)}.

(2.3)

Proof.

We use the general formula for the trace of the inverse matrix (
1.10
). First, from (
2.1
) we have

\displaystyle t^{-1}_{i,i}=\left(\frac{2}{b}\right)\frac{i(n+1-i)}{n+1},\quad t^{-1}_{i,1}=\left(\frac{2}{b}\right)^{i}\frac{n+1-i}{n+1},\quad\text{and}\quad t^{-1}_{n,i}=\left(\frac{2}{b}\right)^{n+1-i}\frac{i}{n+1}.

Using the partial sum
\sum_{i=1}^{n}i=\frac{1}{2}n(n+1)
and
\sum_{i=1}^{n}i^{2}=\frac{1}{6}n(n+1)(2n+1)
, one can show that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}t^{-1}_{i,i}=\frac{n(n+2)}{3b},\quad\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left((t^{-1}_{i,1})^{2}+(t^{-1}_{n,i})^{2}\right)=\frac{n(2n+1)}{3(n+1)},\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{i=1}^{n}t^{-1}_{i,1}t^{-1}_{n,i}=\left(\frac{2}{b}\right)^{n+1}\frac{n(n+2)}{6(n+1)}.

By using
m_{11}
and
m_{12}
given in the proof of Lemma
2.1
, we get

\displaystyle m_{11}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left((t^{-1}_{i,1})^{2}+(t^{-1}_{n,i})^{2}\right)-2m_{12}\sum_{i=1}^{n}t^{-1}_{i,1}t^{-1}_{n,i}=\frac{n}{3(n+1)}\left[2n+1+\frac{4\beta}{b}(n-1)\right]

Substitution of the above equation, the sum
\sum_{i=1}^{n}t^{-1}_{i,i}
, and the determinant formula
\Delta
(see in the proof of Lemma
2.1
) into (
1.10
) gives the trace formula stated in the lemma.
∎

3.1
Bounds for rowsums of the inverse

To begin with, we note that in this case the formula (
2.2
) can be simplified into

\displaystyle\widetilde{t}_{i,j}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(j(1+\beta)-\beta\right)\left((n-i)(1+\beta)+1\right)}{(1+\beta)(n+1+\beta(n-1))}=\left(j-\gamma\right)\frac{(n-i)(1-\widetilde{b})-1}{(n-1)(1-\widetilde{b})-2},\quad i\geq j,

(3.1)

with
\gamma:=\frac{2-\widetilde{b}}{1-\widetilde{b}}
.

Theorem 3.1

(Row Sums)
.

Let
b=2
and
\widetilde{b}
be such that
T_{n}
is nonsingular. The
i
-th row sum of
\widetilde{T}^{-1}_{n}
is given by

\displaystyle\text{rowsum}_{i}\widetilde{T}^{-1}_{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,j}=\frac{1}{2}i(n+1-i)-\frac{n}{2}\frac{\widetilde{b}-2}{\widetilde{b}-1}.

Proof.

Due to symmetry of
\widetilde{T}_{n}^{-1}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,j}=\sum_{j=1}^{i}\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,j}+\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,j}=\sum_{j=1}^{i}\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,j}+\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{j,i}.

Thus, using (
3.1
),

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(j-\gamma\right)\frac{(n-i)(1-\widetilde{b})-1}{(n-1)(1-\widetilde{b})-2}+\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}\left(i-\gamma\right)\frac{(n-j)(1-\widetilde{b})-1}{(n-1)(1-\widetilde{b})-2}

\displaystyle=\frac{(n-i)(1-\widetilde{b})-1}{(n-1)(1-\widetilde{b})-2}\left[\frac{i(i+1)}{2}-\gamma i\right]+\frac{(i-\gamma)(n-i)}{(n-1)(1-\widetilde{b})-2}\left[\frac{(n-i-1)(1-\widetilde{b})}{2}-1\right],

after using the partial sum
\sum_{j=1}^{n}j=\frac{1}{2}n(n+1)
and
\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}j=\frac{1}{2}(n(n+1)-i(i+1))
. Simplification of the right-hand expression leads to the lemma.

∎

Theorem 3.2

(Bounds for Row Sums)
.

Let
b=2
and
\widetilde{b}
be such that
\widetilde{T}_{n}
is nonsingular. The following inequalities hold true:

\frac{n}{2(\widetilde{b}-1)}\leq\text{rowsum}_{i}\widetilde{T}^{-1}_{n}\leq\frac{(n+1)^{2}}{8}-\frac{n(\widetilde{b}-2)}{2(\widetilde{b}-1)}.

Proof.

The row sum in Theorem
3.1
is a quadratic function of
i
. It attains its maximum at
i=(n+1)/2
and its minimum at either
i=1
or
i=n
. Substituting these values of
i
gives the upper and lower bounds, respectively.
∎

3.2
Bounds for norms of the inverse

In the context of finding bounds for the norm of the inverse matrix
\widetilde{T}_{n}^{-1}
, it’s crucial to account for the change in signs of its entries when deriving these bounds. Initially, we’ll establish a lower bound that can be improved specifically for the case when
\widetilde{b}<1
. Following that, we will explore various cases to determine the respective upper bounds.

Theorem 3.3

(Lower Bound)
.

Let
b=2
and
\widetilde{b}
be such that
\widetilde{T}_{n}
is invertible. Then

L:=\max\left\{\left|\frac{n(n-2)}{8}-\frac{n}{2(1-\widetilde{b})}\right|,\left|\frac{n}{2(1-\widetilde{b})}\right|\right\}\leq\|\widetilde{T}_{n}^{-1}\|_{\infty}.

(3.2)

Proof.

Because of the change of signs along a row of
\widetilde{T}_{n}^{-1}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{T}^{-1}_{n}\|_{\infty}=\max_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left|\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,j}\right|\geq\max_{i}\left|\sum_{j=1}^{n}\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,j}\right|\geq\max\left\{\left|\max_{i}{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,j}}\right|,\left|\min_{i}{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,j}}\right|\right\},

with the min part given by Theorem
3.1
. For the max part, consider the rowsum of
\widetilde{T}^{-1}_{n}
in Theorem
3.1
as a function of
i
. For all integer
n
,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{\frac{n}{2},j}=\frac{n(n+2)}{8}-\frac{n}{2}\frac{\widetilde{b}-2}{\widetilde{b}-1}\leq\frac{(n+1)^{2}}{8}-\frac{n}{2}\frac{\widetilde{b}-2}{\widetilde{b}-1}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{\frac{n+1}{2},j},

which gives the max part. Note that

\displaystyle\frac{n(n+2)}{8}-\frac{n}{2}\frac{\widetilde{b}-2}{\widetilde{b}-1}=\frac{n(n-2)}{8}-\frac{n}{2(1-\widetilde{b})}.

Theorem 3.4

(Upper Bound)
.

For
b=2
and
\widetilde{b}
such that
\widetilde{T}_{n}
is invertible, we have

\|\widetilde{T}^{-1}_{n}\|_{\infty}\leq U:=\begin{cases}\frac{(n+1)^{2}}{8}-\frac{n\gamma}{2}&\text{for }\widetilde{b}>1,\\
\frac{n(\gamma-1)}{2}&\text{for }\frac{n-2}{n-1}\leq\widetilde{b}<1,\\
\max\left\{P,Q\right\}&\text{for }\frac{n-3}{n-1}<\widetilde{b}<\frac{n-2}{n-1},\\
\max\left\{-P,R\right\}&\text{for }0<\widetilde{b}<\frac{n-3}{n-1},\\
\max\left\{S,T\right\}&\text{for }\widetilde{b}\leq 0\end{cases}

(3.3)

where

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=\frac{n(1-\gamma)}{2}+\frac{(\gamma-1)^{2}(\gamma+1)}{2\gamma-n-1},

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle=\frac{n(\gamma-1)}{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2\gamma-n-1}\frac{(n+1)^{2}}{16}+\frac{1}{2},

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle=\frac{(n+1)^{2}}{8}-\gamma\left(\frac{n+1}{2}-\gamma\right),

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\frac{(n+1)^{2}}{8}+\frac{4-n(2-\widetilde{b})}{2(1-\widetilde{b})},

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\widetilde{b}}\left(\frac{n}{2}+\frac{2}{(n-1)(1-\widetilde{b})-2}\right)

with
\gamma=\frac{2-\widetilde{b}}{1-\widetilde{b}}
.

∎

The subcase

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{T}^{-1}_{n}\|_{\infty}=\max_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\widetilde{t}_{i,j}^{-1}|=\max_{i}\text{rowsum}_{i}\widetilde{T}^{-1}_{n}\leq\frac{(n+1)^{2}}{8}-\frac{n(\widetilde{b}-2)}{2(\widetilde{b}-1)}.

(3.4)

3.4
The subcase
\widetilde{b}\leq 0

We first derive the following lemma, which characterizes the signs of elements of the inverse matrix
\widetilde{T}_{n}^{-1}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
b=2
and
\widetilde{b}<0
. Then

\displaystyle\text{sgn}\left(\widetilde{t}_{ij}^{-1}\right)=\begin{cases}+1,&\text{for }(i,j)\in\{2,\dots,n-1\}^{2}\cup\{(1,n),(n,1)\}\\
-1,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

We start by rewriting (
3.1
) as

\displaystyle\widetilde{t}_{i,j}^{-1}=\left(j-\gamma\right)\frac{(n-i)(1+|\widetilde{b}|)-1}{(n-1)(1+|\widetilde{b}|)-2},\quad i\geq j

(3.5)

with
\gamma=\frac{2+|\widetilde{b}|}{1+|\widetilde{b}|}=1+\frac{1}{1+|\widetilde{b}|}\in(1,2)
. For
|\widetilde{b}|>0
,
(n-1)(1+|\widetilde{b}|)-2>(n-1)-2=n-3\geq 0
, for
n\geq 3
. Furthermore,
(n-i)(1+|\widetilde{b}|)-1>n-i-1\geq 0
for
i=1,\dots,n-1
and
j-\gamma>j-2\geq 0
,
for
j=2,\dots,n
. Thus, for
(i,j)\in\{2,\dots,n-1\}\times\{2,\dots,n-1\}
, we have
\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,j}>0
.

Considering the case when
j=1
, we have
j-\gamma=-\frac{1}{1+|\widetilde{b}|}<0
. Therefore,
\widetilde{t}_{i,1}^{-1}<0
, for
i=1,\dots,n-1
. For
i=n
,
(n-i)(1+|\widetilde{b}|)-1<0
. Thus, with
j-\gamma<0
, we get
\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{n,1}>0
.

The proof is completed by using the fact that
\widetilde{T}_{n}^{-1}
is symmetric and centrosymmetric; i.e.,
\widetilde{t}_{i,j}^{-1}=\widetilde{t}_{j,i}^{-1}
and
\widetilde{t}_{i,j}^{-1}=\widetilde{t}_{n-i+1,n-j+1}^{-1}
.
∎

Using the same line of proof as above, the following result can be obtained:

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
b=2
and
\widetilde{b}=0
. Then

\displaystyle\text{sgn}\left(\widetilde{t}_{ij}^{-1}\right)=\begin{cases}+1,&\text{for }(i,j)\in\{3,\dots,n-2\}^{2}\cup\{(1,n),(n,1)\}\\
0,&\text{for }(i,j)\in\{2,n-1\}\times\{1,\dots,n\}\cup\{1,\dots,n\}\times\{2,n-1\}-\{(1,2),(2,1),(n-1,n),(n,n-1)\}\\
-1,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
b=2
and
\widetilde{b}\leq 0
. Then

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{T}_{n}^{-1}\|_{\infty}\leq\max\left\{\frac{1}{8}(n+1)^{2}+\frac{4-(2+|\widetilde{b}|)n}{2(1+|\widetilde{b}|)},\frac{1}{1+|\widetilde{b}|}\left(\frac{n}{2}+\frac{2}{(n-1)(1+|\widetilde{b}|)-2}\right)\right\}.

Proof.

For
i=2,\dots,n-1
,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\widetilde{t}_{i,j}^{-1}|

\displaystyle=|\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,1}|+\sum_{j=2}^{n-1}|\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,j}|+|\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,n}|=|\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,1}|+\sum_{j=2}^{n-1}\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,j}+|\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,n}|=2|\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,1}|+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,j}+2|\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,n}|,

because of Lemmas
3.5
and
3.6
. With

\displaystyle\widetilde{t}_{i,1}^{-1}=-\frac{1}{1+|\widetilde{b}|}\cdot\frac{(n-i)(1+|\widetilde{b}|)-1}{(n-1)(1+|\widetilde{b}|)-2},\quad\widetilde{t}_{i,n}^{-1}=\widetilde{t}_{n,i}^{-1}=-\frac{1}{1+|\widetilde{b}|}\cdot\frac{(i-1)(1+|\widetilde{b}|)-1}{(n-1)(1+|\widetilde{b}|)-2},

and Theorem
3.1
, the above sum can be written as

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\widetilde{t}_{i,j}^{-1}|

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{1+|\widetilde{b}|}\left(\frac{(n-i)(1+|\widetilde{b}|)-1}{(n-1)(1+|\widetilde{b}|)-2}+\frac{(i-1)(1+|\widetilde{b}|)-1}{(n-1)(1+|\widetilde{b}|)-2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}i(n+1-i)-\frac{n}{2}\frac{2+|\widetilde{b}|}{1+|\widetilde{b}|}.

Treating the right-hand side as a continuous function of
i
in the interval
[2,n-1]
, it can be shown that the maximum is attained at
i=(n+1)/2
. In this case,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\widetilde{t}_{\frac{n+1}{2},j}^{-1}|=\frac{1}{8}(n+1)^{2}+\frac{4-(2+|\widetilde{b}|)n}{2(1+|\widetilde{b}|)},\quad j=2,\dots,n-1.

For
i=1
,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\widetilde{t}_{1,j}^{-1}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}|\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{1,j}|+|\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{1,n}|=-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{1,j}+\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{1,n}=-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{1,j}+2\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{n,1}

due to symmetry and Lemmas
3.5
and
3.6
. By using

\displaystyle\widetilde{t}_{n,1}^{-1}=\frac{1}{(1+|\widetilde{b}|)[(n-1)(1+|\widetilde{b}|)-2]},

obtained from (
3.1
), and Theorem
3.1
, the sum can be written as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\widetilde{t}_{1,j}^{-1}|

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{2(1+|\widetilde{b}|)}+\frac{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}2}}{(1+|\widetilde{b}|)[(n-1)(1+|\widetilde{b}|)-2]}=\frac{1}{1+|\widetilde{b}|}\left(\frac{n}{2}+\frac{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}2}}{(n-1)(1+|\widetilde{b}|)-2}\right).

Centrosymmetry of
\widetilde{T}^{-1}_{n}
leads to
\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\widetilde{t}_{n,j}^{-1}|=\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\widetilde{t}_{1,j}^{-1}|
. Therefore,

\|\widetilde{T}^{-1}_{n}\|_{\infty}=\max_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,j}|=\max\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{\frac{n+1}{2},j}|,\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{1,j}|\right\},

as in the theorem.
∎

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
b=2
. For
n\geq 9
,

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{T}_{n}^{-1}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{8}(n+1)^{2}+\frac{4-(2+|\widetilde{b}|)n}{2(1+|\widetilde{b}|)},

for all
\widetilde{b}\leq 0
, where equality holds when
n
is odd.

Proof.

For
n\geq 9
,
(n-1)(1+|\widetilde{b}|)-2\geq 8(1+|\widetilde{b}|)-2=6+8|\widetilde{b}|
. Thus,

\displaystyle f_{d}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{\frac{n+1}{2},j}|-\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{1,j}|=\frac{1}{8}(n+1)^{2}+\frac{4-(2+|\widetilde{b}|)n}{2(1+|\widetilde{b}|)}-\frac{1}{1+|\widetilde{b}|}\left(\frac{n}{2}+\frac{2}{(n-1)(1+|\widetilde{b}|)-2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{8}(n+1)^{2}+\frac{4-(2+|\widetilde{b}|)n}{2(1+|\widetilde{b}|)}-\frac{1}{1+|\widetilde{b}|}\left(\frac{n}{2}+\frac{2}{8|\widetilde{b}|+6}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8}(n+1)^{2}-\frac{3+|\widetilde{b}|}{2(1+|\widetilde{b}|)}n+\frac{2(8|\widetilde{b}|+5)}{(1+|\widetilde{b}|)(8|\widetilde{b}|+6)}.

For
n=9
,

\displaystyle f_{d}(9,\widetilde{b})=\frac{64|\widetilde{b}|^{2}+56|\widetilde{b}|+4}{8|\widetilde{b}|^{2}+14|\widetilde{b}|+6}>0,\quad\text{for }\widetilde{b}\leq 0.

For
n>9
,

\displaystyle f_{d}(n,\widetilde{b})-f_{d}(9,\widetilde{b})=\frac{(4|\widetilde{b}|+3)(n-9)((n+7)|\widetilde{b}|+n-1)}{4(1+|\widetilde{b}|)(8|\widetilde{b}|+6)}\geq\frac{3(n-9)(n-1)}{4(1+|\widetilde{b}|)(8|\widetilde{b}|+6)}>0,\quad\text{for }\widetilde{b}\leq 0.

Consequently,
\max\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{\frac{n+1}{2},j}|,\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{1,j}|\right\}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{\frac{n+1}{2},j}|
.
∎

3.5
The subcase
0<\widetilde{b}<1

In this case, the matrix
\widetilde{T}_{n}
is no longer diagonally dominant and, for a given
n\geq 3
, is singular for some choice of
\widetilde{b}
. We first state the end result, summarized in the theorem below.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
b=2
,
0<\widetilde{b}<1
, and
\gamma=(2-\widetilde{b})/(1-\widetilde{b})
such that
\widetilde{T}_{n}
is invertible. Then

\|\widetilde{T}^{-1}_{n}\|_{\infty}\leq\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\gamma-1)n&\text{for $\frac{n-2}{n-1}\leq\widetilde{b}<1$},\\
\displaystyle\max\left\{\frac{n(1-\gamma)}{2}+\frac{(\gamma-1)^{2}(\gamma+1)}{2\gamma-n-1},\frac{n(\gamma-1)}{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2\gamma-n-1}\frac{(n+1)^{2}}{16}+\frac{1}{2}\right\}&\text{for $\frac{n-3}{n-1}<\widetilde{b}<\frac{n-2}{n-1}$},\\
\displaystyle\max\left\{\frac{n(\gamma-1)}{2}+\frac{(\gamma-1)^{2}(\gamma+1)}{n+1-2\gamma},\frac{(n+1)^{2}}{8}-\gamma\left(\frac{n+1}{2}-\gamma\right)\right\}&\text{for $0<\widetilde{b}<\frac{n-3}{n-1}$}.\end{cases}

Proof.

We shall show the proof for Case 1 and 2. For Case 3, the proof is presented in Appendix
A.2
.

\bullet
Case 1:
\frac{n-2}{n-1}\leq\widetilde{b}<1
.
The matrix
\widetilde{T}_{n}
in this case is nonsingular. Consider the elements of the inverse matrix given by the formula (
3.1
). It can be shown
(n-i)(1-\widetilde{b})-1\leq 0
and
(n-1)(1-\widetilde{b})-2<0
, for
i=1,\dots,n
. Furthermore,
j-\gamma=j-1-\frac{1}{1-\widetilde{b}}\leq n-1-\frac{1}{1-\widetilde{b}}=((n-1)(1-\widetilde{b})-1)/(1-\widetilde{b})\leq 0
, for all
j=1,\dots,n
. Thus,
\widetilde{t}_{i,j}^{-1}<0
.

We then have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left|\widetilde{t}_{i,j}^{-1}\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{i}\left|\widetilde{t}_{i,j}^{-1}\right|+\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}\left|\widetilde{t}_{i,j}^{-1}\right|=\sum_{j=1}^{i}\left|\widetilde{t}_{i,j}^{-1}\right|+\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}\left|\widetilde{t}_{j,i}^{-1}\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{i}\left|(j-\gamma)\frac{(n-i)(1-\widetilde{b})-1}{(n-1)(1-\widetilde{b})-2}\right|+\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}\left|(i-\gamma)\frac{(n-j)(1-\widetilde{b})-1}{(n-1)(1-\widetilde{b})-2}\right|

\displaystyle=-\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i}(j-\gamma)\frac{(n-i)(1-\widetilde{b})-1}{(n-1)(1-\widetilde{b})-2}+\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}(i-\gamma)\frac{(n-j)(1-\widetilde{b})-1}{(n-1)(1-\widetilde{b})-2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}i(i-(n+1))+\frac{n}{2}\frac{\widetilde{b}-2}{\widetilde{b}-1},

due to Theorem
3.1
. The right-hand side term attains maximum at
i=1
or
i=n
, resulting in the first part of the theorem.

\bullet
Case 2:
\frac{n-3}{n-1}<\widetilde{b}<\frac{n-2}{n-1}
. We shall use the following formula for the absolute value of the elements of the inverse matrix, obtained directly from (
3.1
):

\displaystyle\left|\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,j}\right|

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{\left|j-\gamma\right|}{\left|1-\gamma\right|}\left|\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,1}\right|,&\text{for }i\geq j,\\
\left|(n-j)(1-\widetilde{b})-1\right|\left|\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,n}\right|,&\text{for }i<j.\end{cases}

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left|\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,j}\right|=\sum_{j=1}^{i}\left|\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,j}\right|+\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}\left|\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,j}\right|=\frac{\left|\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,1}\right|}{\left|1-\gamma\right|}\sum_{j=1}^{i}\left|j-\gamma\right|+\left|\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,n}\right|\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}\left|(n-j)(1-\widetilde{b})-1\right|.

(3.6)

Consider the convex combination
\widetilde{b}=(1-\alpha)\frac{n-3}{n-1}+\alpha\frac{n-2}{n-1}
, with
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Then
(n-1)(1-\widetilde{b})-2=-\alpha<0
. The term
(n-i)(1-\widetilde{b})-1
in
\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,j}
is however not sign-definite. This term vanishes when
i=n-1/(1-\widetilde{b})=n-\gamma+1=:\widetilde{i}
. Let
i^{*}=\lfloor\widetilde{i}\rfloor
. Then, for
1\leq i\leq i^{*}
, we have
(n-i)(1-\widetilde{b})-1\geq 0
and for
i^{*}<i\leq n
we have
(n-i)(1-\widetilde{b})-1<0
. Using the aforementioned convex combination, we obtain the following facts:

(i)

\widetilde{i}\in\left(1,\frac{n+1}{2}\right)
. Thus,
i^{*}\in\left[1,\lfloor\frac{n+1}{2}\rfloor\right]
;

(ii)

\gamma\in\left(\frac{n+1}{2},n\right)
. Thus,
\lfloor\gamma\rfloor\in\left[\lfloor\frac{n+1}{2}\rfloor,n-1\right]
.

Consequently,
i^{*}\leq\lfloor\frac{n+1}{2}\rfloor\leq\frac{n+1}{2}<\gamma
, and
i^{*}\leq\lfloor\gamma\rfloor
.

Subcase 2.1:

1\leq i<i^{*}
.
From Fact (i),

1-\gamma<\dots<(i^{*}-1)-\gamma<i^{*}-\gamma<0.

For first term in the sum (
3.6
), since
i<i^{*}<\gamma
, then
j-\gamma<0
.
Hence,

\sum_{j=1}^{i}|j-\gamma|=\sum_{j=1}^{i}(\gamma-j)=\gamma i-\frac{i(i+1)}{2}.

For the second term in the sum (
3.6
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}\left|(n-j)(1-\widetilde{b})-1\right|\leq 1+\gamma+(n-i)\left[\frac{1}{2}(n-i-1)(1-\widetilde{b})-1\right].

(3.7)

Detail of the derivation is presented in Appendix
A.1
. Thus, with

\displaystyle\left|\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,1}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|(1-\gamma)\frac{(n-i)(1-\widetilde{b})-1}{(n-1)(1-\widetilde{b})-2}\right|=(\gamma-1)\frac{(n-i)(1-\widetilde{b})-1}{2-(n-1)(1-\widetilde{b})},

\displaystyle\left|\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,n}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{n,i}\right|=\left|\frac{i-\gamma}{(n-1)(1-\widetilde{b})-2}\right|=\frac{\gamma-i}{2-(n-1)(1-\widetilde{b})},

and Lemma
3.1
and its proof, it can be shown that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\widetilde{t}_{i,j}^{-1}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{-1}\widetilde{t}_{i,j}^{-1}+\frac{(\gamma+1)(i-\gamma)}{(n-1)(1-\widetilde{b})-2}=\frac{1}{2}i(n+1-i)-\frac{n}{2}\gamma+\frac{(\gamma+1)(i-\gamma)}{(n-1)(1-\widetilde{b})-2}=:P(i).

(3.8)

For
i\geq 1
,

\displaystyle P^{\prime}(i)\leq-1+\frac{n+1}{2}+\frac{\gamma+1}{(n-1)(1-\widetilde{b})-2}=\frac{[(n-1)(1-\widetilde{b})-1]^{2}+4(1-\widetilde{b})+1}{2(1-\widetilde{b})[(n-1)(1-\widetilde{b})-2]}<0.

Thus,
P(i)
is a decreasing function, with maximum attained at
i=1
. The following bound then results:

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq i\leq i^{*}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left|\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,j}\right|\leq P:=\max_{i}P_{i}=P_{1}=\frac{n(1-\gamma)}{2}-\frac{\gamma^{2}-1}{(n-1)(1-\widetilde{b})-2}=\frac{n(1-\gamma)}{2}+\frac{(\gamma-1)^{2}(\gamma+1)}{2\gamma-n-1}.

Subcase 2.2:

i=i^{*}
. Performing similar calculations as in Subcase 2.1 and substituting to (
3.6
), we get

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left|\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i^{*},j}\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{(n-i^{*})(1-\widetilde{b})-1}{(n-1)(1-\widetilde{b})-2}\left[\frac{i^{*}(i^{*}+1)}{2}-\gamma i^{*}\right]-\frac{(i^{*}-\gamma)(n-i^{*})}{(n-1)(1-\widetilde{b})-2}\left[\frac{(n-i^{*}-1)(1-\widetilde{b})}{2}-1\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{(n+1-i^{*}-\gamma)i^{*}(i^{*}-2\gamma+1)}{2(n+1-2\gamma)}-\frac{(i^{*}-\gamma)(n-i^{*})(n+1-i^{*}-2\gamma)}{2(n+1-2\gamma)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{i^{*}(n-i^{*})(n+1-2i^{*})}{2\gamma-n-1}+\gamma(n-2i^{*})+i^{*}\right).

Let us denote

G_{i^{*}}:=\frac{i^{*}(n-i^{*})(n+1-2i^{*})}{2\gamma-n-1}-2\gamma i^{*}+i^{*}.

Now, we seek an upper bound for
T_{i^{*}}
using
n-i^{*}<\gamma

\displaystyle G_{i^{*}}

\displaystyle<\frac{\gamma i^{*}(n+1-2i^{*})}{2\gamma-n-1}-2\gamma i^{*}+i^{*}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma}{2\gamma-n-1}\max_{i^{*}}\{i^{*}(n+1-2i^{*})\}+\max_{i^{*}}\{i^{*}-2\gamma i^{*}\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma}{2\gamma-n-1}\frac{(n+1)^{2}}{8}+1-2\gamma

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma}{2\gamma-n-1}\frac{(n+1)^{2}}{8}+1-n.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left|\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i^{*},j}\right|=\frac{1}{2}\left(G_{i^{*}}+\gamma n\right)<Q:=\frac{n(\gamma-1)}{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2\gamma-n-1}\frac{(n+1)^{2}}{16}+\frac{1}{2}.

Subcase 2.3:

i^{*}<i\leq\lfloor\frac{n+1}{2}\rfloor
.
In this case, the first sum in (
3.6
) can be expressed as:

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left|j-\gamma\right|=\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\gamma-j)=\gamma i-\frac{1}{2}i(i+1).

For the second sum, because
j\geq i+1\geq i^{*}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}\left|(n-j)(1-\widetilde{b})-1\right|=\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}(1-(n-j)(1-\widetilde{b}))=\frac{1}{2}(n-i)(2-(1-\widetilde{b})(n-i-1)).

Using

\displaystyle\left|\widetilde{t}_{i,1}^{-1}\right|=(\gamma-1)\frac{(n-i)(1-\widetilde{b})-1}{(n-1)(1-\widetilde{b})-2},\quad|1-\gamma|=\gamma-1,\quad\left|\widetilde{t}_{i,n}^{-1}\right|=\frac{(i-\gamma)(1-\widetilde{b})-1}{(n-1)(1-\widetilde{b})-2},

the sum (
3.6
) becomes

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left|\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,j}\right|

\displaystyle=-\frac{(n-i)(1-\widetilde{b})-1}{(n-1)(1-\widetilde{b})-2}\left(\frac{1}{2}i(i+1)-\gamma i\right)-\frac{(i-\gamma)(n-i)}{(n-1)(1-\widetilde{b})-2}\left[1-\frac{(n-i-1)(1-\widetilde{b})}{2}\right]

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,j}=-\left(\frac{1}{2}i(n+1-i)-\frac{1}{2}\gamma n\right)=:R_{i}.

With
R^{\prime}_{i}=i-\frac{n+1}{2}<0
, for
i\in(i^{*},\lfloor\frac{n+1}{2}\rfloor]
,
R_{i}
is a decreasing function of
i
, which attains minimum at
i=\frac{n+1}{2}
. Since
R_{i}
is a quadratic function,
R_{1}=\frac{n}{2}(\gamma-1)>R_{i}
, for all
i\in(i^{*},\lfloor\frac{n+1}{2}\rfloor]
.

We now compare
P
and
R_{1}
:

\displaystyle P-R_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{n(1-\gamma)}{2}+\frac{(\gamma-1)^{2}(\gamma+1)}{2\gamma-n-1}-\frac{n}{2}(\gamma-1)=n(1-\gamma)+\frac{(\gamma-1)^{2}(\gamma+1)}{2\gamma-n-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\gamma-1)^{2}(\gamma+1-2n)+(\gamma-1)n(n-1)}{2\gamma-n-1}>0

for
n\geq 3
and
\gamma\in(\frac{n+1}{2},n)
. Thus,

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left|\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,j}\right|=\max\left\{P,Q,R_{1}\right\}=\max\left\{P,Q\right\}.

∎

4
The case
b=-2

In this section, we shall consider the case where
b=-2
. Firstly, in this case, using (
2.1
), the elements of the inverse Toeplitz matrix
T_{n}^{-1}
are given by the formula

t^{-1}_{i,j}=\begin{cases}(-1)^{i+1-j}\frac{j(n+1-i)}{n+1}&\text{for }i\geq j\\
t^{-1}_{j,i}&\text{for }i<j.\end{cases}

(4.1)

Note that the rational term on the right-hand side of the above formula is the inverse elements when
b=2
. Denote the latter by
b_{+}
and let
T_{n,+}
be the corresponding Toeplitz matrix. Then, for
b=-2=-b_{+}
and
i\geq j
, we can write the formula (
4.1
) as

\displaystyle t^{-1}_{i,j}=(-1)^{i+1-j}t^{-1}_{i,j,+}.

Let
\widetilde{b}=-\widetilde{b}_{+}
, where
\widetilde{b}_{+}\in\mathbb{R}
is the
(1,1)
- and
(n,n)
-element of the near Toeplitz matrix
\widetilde{T}_{n,+}
.
The elements of
\widetilde{T}_{n}^{-1}
with
b=-2
is related to the elements of
\widetilde{T}_{n,+}^{-1}
in a way as stated in the following lemma:

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
b=-2=-b_{+}
and
\widetilde{b}=-\widetilde{b}_{+}
, with
\widetilde{b}_{+}
be such that
\widetilde{T}_{n,+}
is invertible. For
i\geq j
, the following holds:

\displaystyle\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,j}=(-1)^{i+1-j}\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,j,+}

Proof.

Note that
\beta=\widetilde{b}-b=-\widetilde{b}_{+}-(-b_{+})=-(\widetilde{b}_{+}-b_{+})=-\beta_{+}
. Hence
\beta/b=\beta_{+}/b_{+}
. Substitution of these relations to (
2.2
) in Lemma
2.1
leads to the lemma.
∎

Theorem 4.2

(Row Sums)
.

Let
\widetilde{b}
be such that, for
b=-2
,
\widetilde{T}_{n}
is invertible. Then

\displaystyle\text{rowsum}_{i}\widetilde{T}^{-1}_{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,j}=\begin{cases}\frac{(-1)^{i}-1}{4}+\frac{(-1)^{i}(\beta n-i(\beta+1))}{2(n+1-\beta(n-1))}\;&\text{for}\;n\mid 2,\\
\frac{(-1)^{i}-1}{4}+\frac{(-1)^{i}\beta}{2(1-\beta)}\;&\text{for}\;n\nmid 2,\end{cases}

where
\beta=\widetilde{b}+2
.

Proof.

Our proof is based on the rowsum formula (
1.11
). First, we compute the rowsum of
T^{-1}_{n}
. For this, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}t^{-1}_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{i}t^{-1}_{i,j}+\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}t^{-1}_{j,i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{i}(-1)^{i+1-j}t^{-1}_{i,j,+}+\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}(-1)^{j+1-i}t^{-1}_{j,i,+}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{i+1}\left[\frac{n+1-i}{n+1}\sum_{j=1}^{i}(-1)^{j}j+\frac{i}{n+1}\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}(-1)^{j}(n+1-j)\right].

(4.2)

where we have used Lemma
4.1
. We note that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{i}(-1)^{j}j

\displaystyle=(-1)^{1}1+(-1)^{2}2+\ldots+(-1)^{i}i=\frac{(2i+1)(-1)^{i}-1}{4}

and

\displaystyle\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}(-1)^{j}(n+1-j)=-\frac{(n-i)(-1)^{i}}{2}+\frac{(-1)^{n}-(-1)^{i}}{4}.

Substitution of the above sums into (
4.2
) and simplification results in

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}t^{-1}_{i,j}=\begin{cases}\frac{(-1)^{i}-1}{4}-\frac{(-1)^{i}i}{2(n+1)}\;&\text{for}\;n\mid 2,\\
\frac{(-1)^{i}-1}{4}\;&\text{for}\;n\nmid 2.\end{cases}

If
i=n
, the sum becomes

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}t^{-1}_{n,j}=\begin{cases}-\frac{n}{2(n+1)}&\text{for $n\mid 2$},\\
-\frac{1}{2}&\text{for $n\nmid 2$}.\end{cases}

Next,

\displaystyle t^{-1}_{i,1}+t^{-1}_{i,n}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{i}\frac{n+1-i}{n+1}+(-1)^{n+1-i}\frac{i}{n+1}=\begin{cases}(-1)^{i}(1-\frac{2i}{n+1})\;&\text{for}\;n\mid 2,\\
(-1)^{i}\;&\text{for}\;n\nmid 2.\end{cases}

Finally, by evaluating the last formula for
i=n
, we have

\displaystyle m_{11}+m_{12}

\displaystyle=1+\beta(t^{-1}_{n,1}+t^{-1}_{n,n})=\begin{cases}1-\frac{\beta(n-1)}{n+1}&\text{for $n\mid 2$},\\
1-\beta&\text{for $n\nmid 2$}.\end{cases}

Substitution of all above relations into (
1.11
) gives us the desired result.
∎

Theorem 4.3

(Upper Bound)
.

For
b=-2
and
\widetilde{b}
such that
\widetilde{T}_{n}
is invertible, we have

\|\widetilde{T}^{-1}_{n}\|_{\infty}\leq U:=\begin{cases}\frac{(n+1)^{2}}{8}-\frac{n\gamma_{+}}{2}&\text{for }\widetilde{b}<-1,\\
\frac{n(\gamma_{+}-1)}{2}&\text{for }-1<\widetilde{b}\leq-\frac{n-2}{n-1},\\
\max\left\{P,Q\right\}&\text{for }-\frac{n-2}{n-1}<\widetilde{b}<-\frac{n-3}{n-1},\\
\max\left\{-P,R\right\}&\text{for }-\frac{n-3}{n-1}<\widetilde{b}<0,\\
\max\left\{S,T\right\}&\text{for }\widetilde{b}\geq 0\end{cases}

(4.3)

where

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=\frac{n(1-\gamma_{+})}{2}+\frac{(\gamma_{+}-1)^{2}(\gamma_{+}+1)}{2\gamma_{+}-n-1},

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle=\frac{n(\gamma_{+}-1)}{2}+\frac{\gamma_{+}}{2\gamma_{+}-n-1}\frac{(n+1)^{2}}{16}+\frac{1}{2},

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle=\frac{(n+1)^{2}}{8}-\gamma_{+}\left(\frac{n+1}{2}-\gamma_{+}\right),

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\frac{(n+1)^{2}}{8}+\frac{4-n(2+\widetilde{b})}{2(1+\widetilde{b})},

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+\widetilde{b}}\left(\frac{n}{2}+\frac{2}{(n-1)(1+\widetilde{b})-2}\right)

with
\gamma_{+}=\frac{2+\widetilde{b}}{1+\widetilde{b}}
.

Proof.

From Lemma
4.1
,
|\widetilde{t}_{i,j}^{-1}|=|\widetilde{t}_{i,j,+}^{-1}|
. Thus,

\|\widetilde{T}^{-1}_{n}\|_{\infty}=\max_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,j}|=\max_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,j,+}|=\|\widetilde{T}^{-1}_{n,+}\|_{\infty}
.
∎

The objective of this paper is to provide properties of inverse of

) with

\displaystyle\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}\left|(n-j)(1-\widetilde{b})-1\right|

\displaystyle\leq 1+\gamma-n+i+\frac{(n-i)(n-i-1)(1-\widetilde{b})}{2}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\gamma+(n-i)\left[\frac{(n-i-1)(1-\widetilde{b})}{2}-1\right].

(A.1)

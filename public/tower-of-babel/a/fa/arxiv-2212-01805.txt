Decoupling inequality for paraboloid under shell type restriction and its application to the periodic Zakharov system
Shinya Kinoshita, Shohei Nakamura, Akansha Sanwal

Abstract
. In this paper, we establish local well-posedness for the Zakharov system on 𝕋 d \mathbb{T}^{d} , d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 in a low regularity setting. Our result improves the work of Kishimoto [ 47 ] . Moreover, the result is sharp up to ε \varepsilon -loss of regularity when d = 3 d=3 and d ≥ 5 d\geq 5 as long as one utilizes the iteration argument. We introduce ideas from recent developments of the Fourier restriction theory. The key element in the proof of our well-posedness result is a new trilinear discrete Fourier restriction estimate involving paraboloid and cone. We prove this trilinear estimate by improving Bourgain–Demeter’s range of exponent for the linear decoupling inequality for paraboloid [ 17 ] under the constraint that the input space-time function f f satisfies supp f ^ ⊂ { ( ξ , τ ) ∈ ℝ d + 1 : 1 − 1 N ≤ | ξ | ≤ 1 + 1 N , | τ − | ξ | 2 | ≤ 1 N 2 } {\rm supp}\,\hat{f}\subset\{(\xi,\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}:1-\frac{1}{N}\leq|\xi|\leq 1+\frac{1}{N},\;|\tau-|\xi|^{2}|\leq\frac{1}{N^{2}}\} for large N ≥ 1 N\geq 1 .

Introduction

1.1.
Introduction and main result

We consider the Cauchy problem for the Zakharov system:

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u=nu,\\
\partial_{t}^{2}n-\Delta n=\Delta(|u|^{2}),\\
(u(0),n(0),\partial_{t}n(0))=(u_{0},n_{0},n_{1}),\end{cases}

(1.1)

where the unknown functions
u
and
n
are complex- and real-valued functions, respectively. We consider (
1.1
) with periodic boundary condition, namely,
u:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}:\to{\mathbb{C}}
and
n:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}:\to{\mathbb{R}}
where
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}=({\mathbb{R}}/2\pi{\mathbb{Z}})^{d}
.
We take initial data from
L^{2}
-based Sobolev spaces:

(u_{0},n_{0},n_{1})\in H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})\times H^{\ell}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})\times H^{\ell-1}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})=:\mathcal{H}^{s,\ell}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})

with
s,\ell\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

The Zakharov system was derived by Zakharov
[
69
]
to describe the propagation of Langmuir waves in a plasma.
The Zakharov system possesses two conservation laws, namely, the conservation of mass
\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}=\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}
and energy:

E(u,n)(t)=\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\|n(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\||\nabla|^{-1}\partial_{t}n(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2})+\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}n(t,x)|u(t,x)|^{2}dx.

Here, if the initial velocity has zero mean, i.e.
\widehat{n_{1}}(0)=0
, then the energy is well-defined. See the introduction in
[
48
]
for more details.

The Cauchy problem for the Zakharov system on
{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
has been studied extensively.
In
[
56
]
, Ozawa–Tsutsumi obtained the local well-posedness of (
1.1
) in
H^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\times H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\times L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
for
d\leq 3
.
After the seminal works by Bourgain
[
12
]
, and Klainerman–Machedon
[
49
]
, Bourgain–Colliander
[
16

applied the Fourier restriction norm method to the study of (
1.1
) and obtained global well-posedness in
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\times L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\times H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
for
d=2
,
3
.
Thereafter, Ginibre–Tsutsumi–Velo
[
36
]
extended the range of
(s,\ell)
for which the Zakharov system is locally well-posed as follows:

\begin{cases}d=1,\qquad&-\frac{1}{2}\leq s-\ell\leq 1,\ 2s\geq\ell+\frac{1}{2}\geq 0,\\
d=2,3,&\ell\leq s\leq\ell+1,\ \ell\geq 0,\ 2s-(\ell+1)\geq 0,\\
d\geq 4,&\ell\leq s\leq\ell+1,\ \ell>\frac{d}{2}-2,\ 2s-(\ell+1)>\frac{d}{2}-2.\end{cases}

They also introduced the notion of scaling criticality for (
1.1
) using which the scaling critical values are given by
(s,\ell)=(\frac{d-3}{2},\frac{d-4}{2})
.
In the
2
-dimensional case, on the line
\ell=s-\frac{1}{2}
, Bejenaru–Herr–Holmer–Tataru
[
2
]
pushed down the threshold of necessary regularity by

by proving that (
1.1
) is locally well-posed in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\times H^{-\frac{1}{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\times H^{-\frac{3}{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
.
They employed the nonlinear version of the Loomis–Whitney inequality which has a connection with multilinear restriction estimates, see e.g.
[

11
,
4
]
.
For

, Bejenaru–Herr
[

proved local well-posedness in
H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\times H^{\ell}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\times H^{\ell-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
with
\ell>-\frac{1}{2}
,
\ell\leq s\leq\ell+1
,
2s>\ell+\frac{1}{2}
.
In the energy critical dimension

, Bejenaru–Guo–Herr–Nakanishi
[
1

established the small data global well-posedness and scattering in the energy space
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{4})\times L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{4})\times\dot{H}^{-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{4})
. Recently, under a radially symmetric assumption on data, global well-posedness and scattering in

D energy space below the ground state was shown by Guo–Nakanishi
[

. Without the radial symmetry assumption, global well-posedness in

D energy space below the ground state has been established in
[

. However, scattering below ground state in the non-radial energy space remains a challenging open problem.
In the recent paper
[

, the sharp range of

for well-posedness is determined for spatial dimensions
d\geq 4
.
For

, we also refer to the recent results

On the other hand, there are not so many works on the periodic Zakharov system (
1.1

For

, let

.
For

Bourgain

For

The most recent result on the local well-posedness of (

He proved the following:

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem 1.1 in
[
47
]
)
.

Let

\begin{cases}0\leq s-\ell\leq 1,\ 2s\geq\ell+\frac{d}{2}>d-1,&(d\geq 3),\\

1.1
) is locally well-posed in

.

In particular, for the case

, local well-posedness in the energy space
H^{1}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})\times L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})\times H^{-1}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})
was obtained.
This readily implies the global well-posedness under the conditions
\widehat{n}_{1}(0)=0
and
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\ll 1
.
Moreover, the result remains true if the classical torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
is replaced by
{\mathbb{T}}_{\gamma}^{d}:=({\mathbb{R}}/2\pi\gamma_{1}{\mathbb{Z}})\times\cdots\times({\mathbb{R}}/2\pi\gamma_{d}{\mathbb{Z}})

\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{d})

\gamma_{j}>0
for each

In
[

, Kishimoto–Maeda succeeded in removing the condition

and found that the smallness condition

can be replaced with
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|Q\|_{L^{2}}
.
By

, we denote the ground state solution of the focusing cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, that is,

is the unique positive radial solution of

-\Delta Q+Q-Q^{3}=0,\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}.

The scaling critical regularity
(s,\ell)=(\frac{d-3}{2},\frac{d-4}{2})
is on the line
\ell=s-\frac{1}{2}
.
Our aim is to relax the regularity threshold in Theorem
1.1
for
d\geq 3
on the line
\ell=s-\frac{1}{2}
.
We note that for
\ell=s-\frac{1}{2}
, the condition in Theorem
1.1
becomes
s>\frac{d-1}{2}
when
d\geq 3
.
We now state our main result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
d\geq 3
. Define

s_{0}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}\quad&(d=3),\\
\frac{3}{4}&(d=4),\\
\frac{d-3}{2}&(d\geq 5).\end{cases}

(1.2)

Let
s>s_{0}
. Then (
1.1
) is locally well-posed in
\mathcal{H}^{s,s-\frac{1}{2}}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})
.

Remark 1
.

Similar to Kishimoto’s result, we can replace the classical torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
with
{\mathbb{T}}_{\gamma}^{d}
.

We observe that the regularity threshold (
1.2
) cannot be improved as far as we utilize the iteration argument in the cases

and

Precisely, for

Kishimoto proved that if

then, for any

, the data-to-solution map
(u_{0},n_{0},n_{1})\mapsto(u,n,\partial_{t}n)

of (

This suggests that Theorem

In addition, because

is the scaling critical regularity, the standard argument yields the sharpness of Theorem (

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
s<\frac{d-3}{2}
. Then, for any

(u_{0},n_{0},n_{1})\mapsto(u,n,\partial_{t}n)

of (
1.1
), as a map from the unit ball in

\mathcal{H}^{s,s-\frac{1}{2}}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})

In order to establish Theorem

As an example, we refer to the work by Fan–Staffilani–Wang–Wilson

Similarly, our broad scope in this paper is to tackle nonlinear PDE problems by introducing new techniques and ideas developed in Fourier restriction theory.
At the same time, the study of the periodic Zakharov system raises a question on the decoupling theory - what if one has more than two surfaces interacting with each other which is reminiscent of the bilinear and multilinear Fourier restriction theory,

Introduction to

-decoupling theory

In order to make the paper accessible to readers from different backgrounds, we begin with a brief introduction to the

-decoupling theory. We refer to the survey paper

Our starting point for the introduction of the decoupling inequality is the classical Littlewood–Paley theory.
We denote the dyadic annulus by
A_{1}:=\{(\xi,\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}:|(\xi,\tau)|\lesssim 1\}
and
A_{N}:=\{(\xi,\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}:|(\xi,\tau)|\sim N\}

\{A_{N}\}_{N\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}}
provides an almost pairwise disjoint decomposition of
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}

Similarly, we can decompose the Fourier support of sufficiently nice function

dyadically as
f\sim\sum_{N}f_{A_{N}}
where we define

f_{K}(x,t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}e^{2\pi i(x,t)\cdot(\xi,\tau)}\chi_{K}(\xi,\tau)\hat{f}(\xi,\tau)\,d\xi d\tau

for
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
in general,
\chi_{K}
is a smooth cut-off function on

\hat{f}
denotes the space-time Fourier transform.
Then the Littlewood–Paley theory exploits an
L^{2}
-orthogonality of
\{f_{A_{N}}\}_{N\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}}
and ensures that

Namely, there exists
f
satisfying (
1.11
) such that

\|f\|_{L^{\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}}}\gtrsim(\log N)^{\frac{d-1}{2(d+1)}}\big(\sum_{\theta\in\mathcal{C}_{N^{-1}}}\|f_{\theta}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

To see this, from the observation in Subsection
3.5
, it suffices to find
\{a_{k}\}_{k\in\mathcal{S}_{N}}
such that

\|\sum_{k\in\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{N}}a_{k}e^{i(k\cdot x+|k|^{2}t)}\|_{L_{t,x}^{\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}}({\mathbb{T}}^{d+1})}\gtrsim(\log N)^{\frac{d-1}{2(d+1)}}\|a_{k}\|_{\ell^{2}}.

(3.25)

Here
\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{N}:=\{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:N-1\leq|k|\leq N+1\}
. We set

a_{k}=\begin{cases}1\quad&\mathrm{if}\ k\in\{(N,\overline{k}_{1},\ldots,\overline{k}_{d-1})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\,:\,1\leq\overline{k}_{j}\leq d^{-\frac{1}{2}}N^{\frac{1}{2}}\ (j=1,\ldots,d-1)\},\\
0\quad&\mathrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then, (
3.25
) is written as the lower bound of the exponential sum

\bigl\|\sum_{1\leq\overline{k}_{1}\leq d^{-\frac{1}{2}}N^{\frac{1}{2}}}\dots\sum_{1\leq\overline{k}_{d-1}\leq d^{-\frac{1}{2}}N^{\frac{1}{2}}}e^{i(\overline{k}\cdot\overline{x}+|\overline{k}|^{2}t)}\bigr\|_{L_{t,\overline{x}}^{\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}\gtrsim(\log N)^{\frac{d-1}{2(d+1)}}N^{\frac{d-1}{4}}.

This can be shown via a number theoretic approach. See Theorem 13.6 in
[
30
]
.

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.2
.
We will show that the first order system (
2.1
) is locally well-posed in
H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})\times H^{s-\frac{1}{2}}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})
if
s>s_{0}
.

We write

\mathcal{J}_{S}[F](t)=-i\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-t^{\prime})\Delta}F(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime},\quad\mathcal{J}_{W_{\pm}}[G](t)=i\int_{0}^{t}e^{\mp i(t-t^{\prime})\langle\nabla\rangle}G(t^{\prime})dt,

and rewrite the system (
2.1
) in integral form:

\displaystyle u(t)

\displaystyle=e^{it\Delta}u_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{J}_{S}[(w+\overline{w})u](t),

(4.1)

\displaystyle w(t)

\displaystyle=e^{-it\langle\nabla\rangle}w_{0}+\mathcal{J}_{W_{+}}\Bigl[\frac{\Delta}{\langle\nabla\rangle}(u\overline{u})+\frac{1}{2\langle\nabla\rangle}(w+\overline{w})\Bigr](t).

(4.2)

The following bilinear estimates play a crucial role.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
s_{0}
be as defined in (
1.2
) and
s>s_{0}
. Then there exist
b>\frac{1}{2}
and
\delta>0
such that

\displaystyle\|uw\|_{X_{S}^{s,b-1+\delta}}+\|u\overline{w}\|_{X_{S}^{s,b-1+\delta}}\lesssim\|u\|_{X_{S}^{s,b}}\|w\|_{X_{W_{+}}^{s-\frac{1}{2},b}},

(4.3)

\displaystyle\|u\overline{u}\|_{X_{W_{+}}^{s+\frac{1}{2},b-1+\delta}}\lesssim\|u\|_{X_{S}^{s,b}}^{2}.

(4.4)

Proof.

By duality and dyadic decomposition of the space-time Fourier supports of the functions,
to prove (
4.3
) and (
4.4
), it suffices to prove that if
s>s_{0}
, there exists small
\delta>0
such that

\begin{split}&\Bigl|\int u_{1}\overline{v}_{2}w_{3,\pm}dtdx\Bigr|\\
&\lesssim(L_{1}L_{2}L_{3})^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}\Bigl(\frac{\min(N_{1},N_{2})}{\max(N_{1},N_{2})}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}N_{\min}^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{1}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\|v_{2}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\|w_{3,\pm}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}},\end{split}

(4.5)

where
u_{1}=P_{N_{1},L_{1}}^{S}u
,
v_{2}=P_{N_{2},L_{2}}^{S}v
,
w_{3,\pm}=P_{N_{3},L_{3}}^{W_{\pm}}w
, and
N_{\min}=\min(N_{1},N_{2},N_{3})
.
A similar reduction can be found in
[
2
]
.
Next, we do a case-by-case analysis.

(1)
N_{3}\lesssim N_{1}\sim N_{2}
:
In this case, our goal is the following estimate:

\Bigl|\int u_{1}\overline{v}_{2}w_{3,\pm}dtdx\Bigr|\lesssim(L_{1}L_{2}L_{3})^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}N_{3}^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{1}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\|v_{2}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\|w_{3,\pm}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}.

(4.6)

First, we notice that from Plancherel’s theorem and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we have

\displaystyle\Bigl|\int u_{1}\overline{v}_{2}w_{3,\pm}dtdx\Bigr|

\displaystyle\sim\Bigl|\int\bigl(\widetilde{u}_{1}*\widetilde{w}_{3,\pm}\bigr)\overline{\widetilde{v}}_{2}d\tau dk\Bigr|

\displaystyle=\Bigl|\int\int\widetilde{u}_{1}(\tau-\tau^{\prime},k-k^{\prime})\widetilde{w}_{3,\pm}(\tau^{\prime},k^{\prime})d\tau^{\prime}dk^{\prime}\overline{\widetilde{v}}_{2}(\tau,k)d\tau dk\Bigr|

\displaystyle\lesssim L_{\min}^{\frac{1}{2}}N_{3}^{\frac{d}{2}}\|u_{1}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\|v_{2}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\|w_{3,\pm}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}.

(4.7)

Here
L_{\min}=\min(L_{1},L_{2},L_{3})
. The estimate (
4.7
) immediately gives (
4.6
) if
L_{\max}:=\max(L_{1},L_{2},L_{3})\gtrsim N_{3}^{4}
. Hence, we may assume
L_{\max}\leq N_{3}^{4}
, which means that it is sufficient to show (
4.6
) with
\delta=0
.
Using the definition of
s_{0}
, it suffices to prove that for any
\varepsilon>0
, we have the following estimate:

\Bigl|\int u_{1}\overline{v}_{2}w_{3,\pm}dtdx\Bigr|\\
\lesssim(L_{1}L_{2}L_{3})^{\frac{1}{2}}C(N_{3})\|u_{1}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\|v_{2}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\|w_{3,\pm}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}},

(4.8)

where

C(N_{3}):=\begin{cases}N_{3}^{\varepsilon}\quad&(d=3),\\
N_{3}^{\frac{1}{4}+\varepsilon}&(d=4),\\
N_{3}^{\frac{d}{2}-2+\varepsilon}&(d\geq 5).\end{cases}

By almost orthogonality and Fubini’s theorem, it suffices to prove that, for arbitrary
c_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}

(i=1,2,3)
, it holds that

\Bigl|\int u_{1,c_{1}}\overline{v}_{2,c_{2}}w_{3,\pm,c_{3}}dtdx\Bigr|\\
\lesssim C(N_{3})\|u_{1,c_{1}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\|v_{2,c_{2}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\|w_{3,\pm,c_{3}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}},

(4.9)

where

\displaystyle\operatorname{supp}\widetilde{u}_{1,c_{1}}

\displaystyle\subset\{(\tau,k)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\,:\,|\tau-|k|^{2}-c_{1}|\leq 1,

\displaystyle k\in B_{N_{3}}(\xi_{1})\},

(4.10)

\displaystyle\operatorname{supp}\widetilde{v}_{2,c_{2}}

\displaystyle\subset\{(\tau,k)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\,:\,|\tau-|k|^{2}-c_{2}|\leq 1,

\displaystyle k\in B_{N_{3}}(\xi_{2})\},

(4.11)

\displaystyle\operatorname{supp}\widetilde{w}_{3,\pm,c_{3}}

\displaystyle\subset\{(\tau,k)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\,:\,|\tau\mp\langle k\rangle-c_{3}|\leq 1,

\displaystyle\langle

\displaystyle k\rangle\lesssim N_{3}\}.

(4.12)

Here, for
j=1,2
,
B_{N_{3}(\xi_{j})}
denotes the ball of radius
N_{3}
and centre
\xi_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
satisfying
|\xi_{j}|\sim N_{j}
.
We consider (
4.9
). By Plancherel’s theorem, we have

\displaystyle\Bigl|\int u_{1,c_{1}}\overline{v}_{2,c_{2}}w_{3,\pm,c_{3}}dtdx\Bigr|

\displaystyle\sim\Bigl|\int\bigl(\widetilde{u}_{1,c_{1}}*\widetilde{w}_{3,\pm,c_{3}}\bigr)\overline{\widetilde{v}}_{2,c_{2}}d\tau dk\Bigr|

\displaystyle=\Bigl|\iint

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}_{1,c_{1}}(\tau-\tau^{\prime},k-k^{\prime})\widetilde{w}_{3,\pm,c_{3}}(\tau^{\prime},k^{\prime})d\tau^{\prime}dk^{\prime}\overline{\widetilde{v}}_{2,c_{2}}(\tau,k)d\tau dk\Bigr|.

The support conditions on
\widetilde{u}_{1}
,
\widetilde{v}_{2}
,
\widetilde{w}_{3,\pm}
imply that, in the above, we may assume

|\tau-\tau^{\prime}-|k-k^{\prime}|^{2}-c_{1}|\leq 1,\quad|\tau-|k|^{2}-c_{2}|\leq 1,\quad|\tau^{\prime}\mp\langle k^{\prime}\rangle-c_{3}|\leq 1.

This implies

\displaystyle 3

\displaystyle\geq\bigl|\tau-\tau^{\prime}-|k-k^{\prime}|^{2}-c_{1}-(\tau-|k|^{2}-c_{2})+\tau^{\prime}\mp\langle k^{\prime}\rangle-c_{3}\bigr|

\displaystyle\geq\bigl||k-k^{\prime}|^{2}-|k|^{2}+\widetilde{c}\pm\langle k^{\prime}\rangle\bigr|,

where
\widetilde{c}=c_{1}-c_{2}+c_{3}
. Hence, from the spatial support condition of
\widetilde{w}_{3,\pm,c_{3}}
in (
4.12
), we have
\bigl||k-k^{\prime}|^{2}-|k|^{2}+\widetilde{c}\,\bigr|\lesssim N_{3}\lesssim N_{1}
.
This implies that if there exists
M_{1}>0
such that
|k|^{2}=M_{1}+\mathcal{O}(N_{1})
, we may find
M_{2}>0
such that
|k-k^{\prime}|^{2}=M_{2}+\mathcal{O}(N_{1})
.
The support conditions imply
M_{1}\sim M_{2}\sim N_{1}^{2}
and then, by the almost orthogonality, in addition to the above support conditions (
4.10
) and (
4.11
), we may assume that there exist
d_{1}\sim d_{2}\sim N_{1}
such that

\displaystyle\operatorname{supp}\widetilde{u}_{1,c_{1}}\subset\{(\tau,k)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\,:\,d_{1}-1\leq|k|\leq d_{1}+1\},

\displaystyle\operatorname{supp}\widetilde{v}_{2,c_{2}}\subset\{(\tau,k)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\,:\,d_{2}-1\leq|k|\leq d_{2}+1\}.

Therefore, by using Corollary
2.6
, we obtain

\|u_{1,c_{1}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}}}\lesssim N_{3}^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\|u_{1,c_{1}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}},\quad\|v_{2,c_{2}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}}}\lesssim N_{3}^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\|v_{2,c_{2}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}.

(4.13)

In the case
d=3
, these estimates immediately yield (
4.9
) as follows:

\displaystyle\Bigl|\int u_{1,c_{1}}\overline{v}_{2,c_{2}}w_{3,\pm,c_{3}}dtdx\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{1,c_{1}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{4}}\|v_{2,c_{2}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{4}}\|w_{3,\pm,c_{3}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N_{3}^{\varepsilon}\|u_{1,c_{1}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\|v_{2,c_{2}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\|w_{3,\pm,c_{3}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}.

In the case
d=4

2.12

\|w_{3,\pm,c_{3}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{\frac{5}{2}}}\leq\|w_{3,\pm,c_{3}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{\frac{10}{3}}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|w_{3,\pm,c_{3}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim N_{3}^{\frac{1}{4}}\|w_{3,\pm,c_{3}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}.

This estimate and (
4.13
) provide that

\displaystyle\Bigl|\int u_{1,c_{1}}\overline{v}_{2,c_{2}}w_{3,\pm,c_{3}}dtdx\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{1,c_{1}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{\frac{10}{3}}}\|v_{2,c_{2}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{\frac{10}{3}}}\|w_{3,\pm,c_{3}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{\frac{5}{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N_{3}^{\frac{1}{4}+\varepsilon}\|u_{1,c_{1}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\|v_{2,c_{2}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\|w_{3,\pm,c_{3}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}.

Lastly, we consider the case
d\geq 5
. Let

\Bigl(\frac{1}{q_{w}},\frac{1}{p_{w}}\Bigr)=\Bigl(\frac{2}{d+1},\frac{1}{2}-\frac{4}{d^{2}-1}\Bigr).

Notice that
\frac{1}{q_{w}}=\frac{d-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p_{w}})
.
Then, (
2.12

\|w_{3,\pm,c_{3}}\|_{L_{t}^{q_{w}}L_{x}^{p_{w}}}\lesssim N_{3}^{\frac{2}{d-1}}\|w_{3,\pm,c_{3}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}.

(4.14)

Consequently, by using Bernstein inequality, (
4.13
) and (

\displaystyle\Bigl|\int u_{1,c_{1}}\overline{v}_{2,c_{2}}w_{3,\pm,c_{3}}dtdx\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{1,c_{1}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}}}\|v_{2,c_{2}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}}}\|w_{3,\pm,c_{3}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{\frac{d+1}{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N_{3}^{\varepsilon}\|u_{1,c_{1}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\|v_{2,c_{2}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}N_{3}^{\frac{d}{2}-\frac{2d}{d-1}}\|w_{3,\pm,c_{3}}\|_{L_{t}^{q_{w}}L_{x}^{p_{w}}}

(2)
\min(N_{1},N_{2})\ll N_{3}
:
By symmetry, we assume that

, namely,
N_{2}\ll N_{1}\sim N_{3}

The estimate (

\Bigl|\int u_{1}\overline{v}_{2}w_{3,\pm}dtdx\Bigr|\lesssim(L_{1}L_{2}L_{3})^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}N_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}N_{2}^{s}\|u_{1}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\|v_{2}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\|w_{3,\pm}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}.

(4.15)

By Plancherel’s theorem, we have

\displaystyle\Bigl|\int u_{1}\overline{v}_{2}w_{3,\pm}dtdx\Bigr|

\displaystyle\sim\Bigl|\int\bigl(\widetilde{u}_{1}*\widetilde{w}_{3,\pm}\bigr)\overline{\widetilde{v}}_{2}d\tau dk\Bigr|

\displaystyle=\Bigl|\int\int\widetilde{u}_{1}(\tau-\tau^{\prime},k-k^{\prime})\widetilde{w}_{3,\pm}(\tau^{\prime},k^{\prime})d\tau^{\prime}dk^{\prime}\overline{\widetilde{v}}_{2}(\tau,k)d\tau dk\Bigr|.

This implies that we may assume

\displaystyle 3L_{\max}

\displaystyle\geq\bigl|\tau-\tau^{\prime}+|k-k^{\prime}|^{2}-(\tau+|k|^{2})+\tau^{\prime}\pm\langle k^{\prime}\rangle\bigr|

\displaystyle\geq\bigl||k-k^{\prime}|^{2}-|k|^{2}\bigr|-\langle k^{\prime}\rangle.

Thus, because
N_{2}\ll N_{1}\sim N_{3}
, we have

Let us first consider the case

It follows from Bernstein inequality and (

\displaystyle\Bigl|\int u_{1}\overline{v}_{2}w_{3,\pm}dtdx\Bigr|

We consider the remaining cases. Since the case

Next, we show (

(i=2,3)

(4.16)

where

\displaystyle\operatorname{supp}\widetilde{v}_{2,c_{2}}

\displaystyle\subset\{(\tau,k)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\,:\,|\tau-|k|^{2}-c_{2}|\leq 1,\ |k|\sim N_{2}\},

\displaystyle\operatorname{supp}\widetilde{w}_{3,\pm,c_{3}}

\displaystyle\subset\{(\tau,k)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\,:\,|\tau\mp\langle k\rangle-c_{3}|\leq 1,\ k\in B_{N_{2}}(\xi_{3})\}

We turn to the proof of (

Let us assume
N_{2}\gg 1
since the case

If
(\tau,k)\in\operatorname{supp}\widetilde{w}_{3,\pm,c_{3}}

This means that the temporal frequency of
\widetilde{w}_{3,\pm,c_{3}}
is confined to an interval of length
4N_{2}
.
Hence, the standard almost orthogonality argument can be applied. We give a rough sketch of it.

Let a non-negative valued function
\psi_{N_{2}}\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}})
satisfy

\operatorname{supp}\psi_{N_{2}}\subset[-2N_{2},2N_{2}],\qquad\sum_{m\in N_{2}{\mathbb{Z}}}\psi_{N_{2}}(\tau-m)=1\quad\mathrm{for}\ \mathrm{all}\ \tau\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Here
N_{2}{\mathbb{Z}}:=\{N_{2}\ell\,:\,\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
.
For
m\in N_{2}{\mathbb{Z}}
and
v\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{d})
, define the operator
\mathcal{F}_{t,x}({\mathcal{P}_{N_{2},m}v})(\tau,k)=\psi_{N_{2}}(\tau-m)\widetilde{v}(\tau,k)
. Then, clearly
\sum_{m\in N_{2}{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathcal{P}_{N_{2},m}v=v
. We observe

\displaystyle\|v_{2,c_{2}}w_{3,\pm,c_{3}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Bigl(\sum_{m_{1}\in N_{2}{\mathbb{Z}}}\Bigl\|\mathcal{P}_{N_{2},m_{1}}\Bigl(\bigl(\sum_{m_{2}\in N_{2}{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathcal{P}_{N_{2},m_{2}}v_{2,c_{2}}\bigr)w_{3,\pm,c_{3}}\Bigr)\Bigr\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}^{2}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We deduce from (
4.20
) that for each
m_{1}\in N_{2}{\mathbb{Z}}
there exists the set
M_{N_{2},m_{1}}\subset N_{2}{\mathbb{Z}}
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{N_{2},m_{1}}\Bigl(\bigl(\sum_{m_{2}\in M_{N_{2},m_{1}}}\mathcal{P}_{N_{2},m_{2}}v_{2,c_{2}}\bigr)w_{3,\pm,c_{3}}\Bigr)=\mathcal{P}_{N_{2},m_{1}}\Bigl(\bigl(\sum_{m_{2}\in N_{2}{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathcal{P}_{N_{2},m_{2}}v_{2,c_{2}}\bigr)w_{3,\pm,c_{3}}\Bigr),

\displaystyle\#M_{N_{2},m_{1}}\sim 1,\qquad\Bigl(\sum_{m_{1}\in N_{2}{\mathbb{Z}}}\Bigl\|\sum_{m_{2}\in M_{N_{2},m_{1}}}\mathcal{P}_{N_{2},m_{2}}v_{2,c_{2}}\Bigr\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}^{2}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}\sim\|v_{2,c_{2}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}.

As a result, in addition to (
4.17
), we may assume that there exists
\tau^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}

Proof of Theorem

We follow the argument introduced by Bourgain

We only need to prove that for arbitrarily large
C\gg 1
and any

, there exist
f
,

\begin{split}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}&\Bigl\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-t^{\prime})\Delta}\bigl((e^{it^{\prime}\Delta}f)(\cos(t^{\prime}|\nabla|)g)\bigr)dt^{\prime}\Bigr\|_{H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}\\

(5.1)

6.1.
A bonus on the number of integer solutions to certain Diophantine system

It is nowadays standard that once one can prove decoupling type inequality for some submanifold and even integer
p
, then one can derive a bound of integer solutions of a Diophantine system corresponding to the submanifold, see
[
19
,
30
,
38
]
and references therein.
For example the celebrated resolution of Vinogradov’s meanvalue theorem (conjecture) by Bourgain–Demeter–Guth
[
19
]
took this route in which case the submanifold was the moment curve in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Let us consider a Diophantine system

\begin{cases}x_{1}+y_{1}=z_{1}+w_{1},\\
x_{2}+y_{2}=z_{2}+w_{2},\\
x_{3}+y_{3}=z_{3}+w_{3},\\
x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}+y_{1}^{2}+y_{2}^{2}+y_{3}^{2}=z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}^{2}+z_{3}^{2}+w_{1}^{2}+w_{2}^{2}+w_{3}^{2}\end{cases}

(6.1)

for variables
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},y_{1},y_{2},y_{3},z_{1},z_{2},z_{3},w_{1},w_{2},w_{3})
and denote the number of integer solutions in
[1,N]^{12}
of (
6.1
) for fixed large
N
by
\sharp_{N}
. By taking the trivial solutions
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})
and
(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})=(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3})
one can see that
\sharp_{N}\gtrsim N^{6}
.
Then the standard argument reveals that

\sharp_{N}=c\|e^{it\Delta}f\|_{L^{4}_{t,x}(\mathbb{T}^{3+1})}^{4}

for
\hat{f}(k)=\textbf{\rm 1}_{[1,N]^{3}}(k)
.
On the other hand, Bourgain–Demeter’s
\ell^{2}
-decoupling ensures bounds for
\|e^{it\Delta}f\|_{L^{p}_{t,x}(\mathbb{T}^{3+1})}
with
2\leq p\leq\frac{10}{3}
.
It is worth to mention that there is no even integer except
p=2
in the range
2\leq p\leq\frac{10}{3}
and hence the standard argument is not applicable to give a bound of
\sharp_{N}
by
N^{6+\varepsilon}
.
On the hand Theorem
1.7
is applicable for
p=4
under the shell type constraint.
Namely, letting
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
,
y=(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})
,
z=(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})
,
w=(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3})
, we have the following:

Corollary 6.1
.

Let
N\gg 1
. Then the number of integer solutions to (
6.1
) satisfying
N-10\leq|x|,|y|,|z|,|w|\leq N
is bounded by
C_{\varepsilon}N^{4+\varepsilon}
for arbitrary small
\varepsilon>0
.

Here notice that the number of the trivial solutions
x=z
and
y=w
under the constraint
N-10\leq|x|,|y|,|z|,|w|\leq N
is comparable to
N^{4}
.

6.2.
A remark on the mixed-norm Strichartz estimates

As is mentioned in the Introduction, Theorem
1.2
is sharp up to
\varepsilon
loss when
d=3
and
d\geq 5
.
In the
d=4
case, however, there might be a room to relax the regularity condition (
1.2
).
We here discuss a possible route to this problem by assuming the validity of the mixed norm Strichartz estimates on the torus.
Namely we suppose the endpoint estimate

\|e^{it\Delta}\phi\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}({\mathbb{T}}^{d+1})}\lesssim N^{\varepsilon}\|\phi\|_{L_{x}^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})},

(6.2)

holds for all
\varepsilon>0
and
{\rm supp}\,\hat{\phi}\subset\{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:|k|\leq N\}
, and then
see how it implies the sharp well-posedness result up to
\varepsilon
loss.
For simplicity, let us assume
d=4
and consider only the trilinear estimate (
1.9
).
A simple use of (
6.2
) with
d=4
implies

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{[-\pi,\pi]\times\mathbb{T}^{4}}e^{it\Delta}\phi_{1}\overline{e^{it\Delta}\phi_{2}}e^{\pm it|\nabla|}\phi_{3}\,dtdx\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\|e^{it\Delta}\phi_{1}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{4}}\|e^{it\Delta}\phi_{2}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{4}}\|e^{\pm it|\nabla|}\phi_{3}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{\varepsilon}\prod_{j=1}^{3}\|\phi_{j}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})},

for
{\rm supp}\,\hat{\phi_{j}}\subset\{|\xi|\sim N\}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
By employing this almost sharp estimate and the argument in this paper, we may show the local well-posedness of (
1.1
) with
d=4
in
\mathcal{H}^{s,s-\frac{1}{2}}
if
s>\frac{1}{2}
, which is an optimal result up to
\varepsilon
loss.
Hence, the mixed-norm Strichartz estimate (
6.2
) or in general

\|e^{it\Delta}\phi\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{p}({\mathbb{T}}^{d+1})}\lesssim N^{\varepsilon}\|\phi\|_{L_{x}^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})},

(6.3)

for
\frac{2}{q}=d(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})
with
d\geq 3
, is valuable problem from PDE point of view.
Notice that, by an interpolation of (
1.8
) and the trivial estimate
\|e^{it\Delta}\phi\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d+1})}=\|\phi\|_{L_{x}^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}
, we have (
6.3
) for
\frac{2}{q}=d(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})
and
q\geq p
, and hence the case of
q<p
is a main problem.
As far as we know, there is no sharp result for (
6.3
) when
q<p
. In this direction, we mention the work due to Burq–Gérard–Tzvetkov
[
21
]
where they established the mixed norm Strichartz estimates with a certain loss of regularity.
After we uploaded this paper on arXiv, Dasu–Jung–Li–Madrid
[
29
]
announced that the mixed-norm
\ell^{2}
-decoupling inequality which implies (
6.3
) is prohibited to go beyond Bourgain–Demeter’s (pure-norm) decoupling inequality on the Strichartz line
\frac{2}{q}=d(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})
. In particular, one cannot expect the mixed-norm
\ell^{2}
-decoupling inequality which implies (
6.2
). As is mentioned in
[
29
]
, their counterexamples are not the one for the mixed norm Strichartz inequality (
6.3
) and so the problem is still open.

Global stable splittings of Stiefel manifolds
Stefan Schwede

Abstract
. We prove global equivariant refinements of Miller’s stable splittings of the infinite orthogonal, unitary and symplectic groups, and more generally of the spaces O / O ⁡ ( m ) O/O(m) , U / U ⁡ ( m ) U/U(m) and S ​ p / S ​ p ​ ( m ) Sp/Sp(m) . As such, our results encode compatible equivariant stable splittings, for all compact Lie groups, of specific equivariant refinements of these spaces. In the unitary and symplectic case, we also take the actions of the Galois groups into account. To properly formulate these Galois-global statements, we introduce a generalization of global stable homotopy theory in the presence of an extrinsic action of an additional topological group.



Introduction

The purpose of this article is to establish global equivariant refinements
of Haynes Miller’s stable splittings of the infinite orthogonal, unitary and symplectic
groups, and more generally of the spaces
O/O(m)
,
U/U(m)
and
Sp/Sp(m)
.
In his classic paper
[
19
]
, Miller showed that a filtration by the size of
the ‘generalized +1 eigenspaces’ on the finite-dimensional
real, complex and quaternionic Stiefel manifolds
splits after a suitable suspension.
Miller then obtains stable splittings of the infinite dimensional Stiefel manifolds
O/O(m)
,

U/U(m)
and
Sp/Sp(m)
by passing to colimits.
For
m=0
and
m=1
, these include stable splittings
of the spaces underlying the groups
O
,
SO
,
U
,
SU
and
Sp
.

Global equivariant homotopy theory is, informally speaking, equivariant homotopy theory
with simultaneous actions of all compact Lie groups.
Unstable global homotopy theory comes up in different incarnations,
for example as the homotopy theory of topological stacks and orbispaces
[
9
]
,
or spaces with an action of the ‘universal compact Lie group’
[
22
]
.
Some of the protagonists of Miller’s splittings have very natural and interesting equivariant and
global refinements. For instance, the unitary groups of all hermitian inner product spaces
form a global space
{\mathbf{U}}
that refines the infinite unitary group,
and whose
G
-equivariant homotopy type is
that of the unitary group of a complete complex
G
-universe.
The global space
{\mathbf{U}}
features in a global refinement of Bott periodicity,
a global equivalence
\Omega^{2}{\mathbf{U}}\simeq{\mathbf{U}}

that encodes equivariant Bott periodicity for all compact Lie groups at once,
see
[
21
, Theorem 2.5.41]
.
There are similarly natural global refinements

\mathbf{O}
,
{\mathbf{SO}}
,
{\mathbf{SU}}
and
{\mathbf{Sp}}
of the spaces
O
,
SO
,
SU
and
Sp
;
more generally, we introduce global refinements
\mathbf{O}/m
,
{\mathbf{U}}/m
and
{\mathbf{Sp}}/m

of the spaces
O/O(m)
,
U/U(m)
and
Sp/Sp(m)
, see Example
2.10
below.

In
[
21
]
, the author has developed a framework for stable global homotopy theory;
one of the upshots is the compactly generated and tensor-triangulated
global stable homotopy category
[
21
, Section 4.4]

that forgets to the homotopy categories of genuine
G
-spectra for all compact Lie groups
G
;
this global stable homotopy category is the home for the splittings proved in this paper.
The global stable homotopy category
exhibits all the ‘genuine’ (as opposed to ‘naive’) features of equivariant stable homotopy theory,
it is different from the stabilization obtained by inverting ordinary suspension,
and its objects represent genuine cohomology theories on orbifolds
[
11
]
.
The following is the main result of this paper;
we prove it as Theorem
3.8
, where we also specify the splitting morphisms.

Theorem.
For every
m\geq 0
there is an isomorphism

\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\mathbf{O}/m\ \cong\ {\bigvee}_{k\geq 0}\ \Sigma^{\infty}(\mathbf{Gr}_{k}^{\mathbb{R}})^{\nu(k,m)\oplus\mathfrak{ad}(k)}

in the global stable homotopy category, an isomorphism

\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}{\mathbf{U}}/m\ \cong\ {\bigvee}_{k\geq 0}\ \Sigma^{\infty}(\mathbf{Gr}_{k}^{\mathbb{C}})^{\nu(k,m)\oplus\mathfrak{ad}(k)}

in the
G({\mathbb{C}})
-global stable homotopy category, and an isomorphism

\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}{\mathbf{Sp}}/m\ \cong\ {\bigvee}_{k\geq 0}\ \Sigma^{\infty}(\mathbf{Gr}_{k}^{\mathbb{H}})^{\nu(k,m)\oplus\mathfrak{ad}(k)}

in the
G({\mathbb{H}})
-global stable homotopy category.

In the unitary and symplectic statements,

G({\mathbb{C}})
and
G({\mathbb{H}})
denote the Galois groups of
{\mathbb{C}}
and
{\mathbb{H}}
over
{\mathbb{R}}
.
The flavor of global homotopy theory that encodes an additional extrinsic symmetry group
transcends the theory of
[
21
]
;
we devote Appendix
A
to developing
the basics of
C
-global homotopy theory, for any topological group
C
,
including the triangulated
C
-global stable homotopy category.
The Galois-global splittings are more highly structured,
and imply splittings in the global stable homotopy category of
[
21
]
.
On the right hand side of the splitting,
(\mathbf{Gr}_{k}^{\mathbb{R}})^{V}
denotes the global Thom
space of an
O(k)
-representation
V
,
a specific global refinement of the Thom space of the vector bundle over
BO(k)

associated to
V
, see Example
2.12
.
And similarly for
(\mathbf{Gr}_{k}^{\mathbb{C}})^{V}
and
(\mathbf{Gr}_{k}^{\mathbb{H}})^{V}
, where now
V

is a representation of the unitary group
U(k)
or the symplectic group
Sp(k)
,
respectively.
Moreover,
\nu(k,m)
is an
m
-fold direct sum of copies of the tautological representation
of
O(k)
,
U(k)
or
Sp(k)
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
,
{\mathbb{C}}^{k}
or
{\mathbb{H}}^{k}
, respectively;
Miller
[
19
]
refers to these as the
canonical representations
.
And
\mathfrak{ad}(k)
is the adjoint representation of
O(k)
,
U(k)
or
Sp(k)
, respectively.

The special case
m=0
yields stable global splittings of
\mathbf{O}
,
{\mathbf{U}}
and
{\mathbf{Sp}}
,
see Theorems
1.5
and
3.10
.
And the special case
m=1
yields stable global splittings of
{\mathbf{SO}}
and
{\mathbf{SU}}
,
see Theorem
3.12
.
The global stable homotopy category comes with a highly forgetful functor
to the non-equivariant stable homotopy category, see
[
21
, Section 4.5]
;
applying this forgetful functor to our main result Theorem
3.8

returns Miller’s non-equivariant splitting
[
19
, Corollary D]
.

There are natural stabilization morphisms
\mathbf{O}/m\longrightarrow\mathbf{O}/(m+1)
obtained from the
preferred embeddings
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{m+1}
, and similarly in the unitary and symplectic cases;
we write
\mathbf{O}/\infty
,
{\mathbf{U}}/\infty
and
{\mathbf{Sp}}/\infty
for the global spaces
obtained in the colimit over
m
.
Non-equivariantly, these colimit spaces are contractible, so there is no incentive to study their
homotopy types any further. Equivariantly and globally, however, these limit objects
are interesting and highly non-trivial, see Remark
4.1
.
In Theorem
4.5
we formulate global stable splittings
of the global spaces
\mathbf{O}/\infty
,
{\mathbf{U}}/\infty
and
{\mathbf{Sp}}/\infty
,
ultimately obtained from the previous splittings by passing to homotopy colimits.

We end this introduction with some comments
intended to prevent possible misconceptions about the nature of our global splitting result.
Firstly,
the forgetful functor from the global stable homotopy category to
the non-equivariant stable homotopy category has a left adjoint,
an exact functor of triangulated categories that preserves infinite sums,
compare
[
21
, Theorem 4.5.1]
.
So one can apply this left adjoint to Miller’s original non-equivariant splittings,
yielding global refinements in a formal way.
However, the global spaces
\mathbf{O}/m
,
{\mathbf{U}}/m
and
{\mathbf{Sp}}/m
are
not

in the essential image of the left adjoint to the forgetful functor,
so our splitting is not a formal consequence of Miller’s.

Secondly,
for the purposes of this paper, it is essential that the stable global homotopy theory of

[
21
]
, and more generally the
C
-global stable homotopy theory
of Appendix
A
, is not just the ‘naive’ stabilization of unstable global homotopy theory.
Indeed, as we explain in Construction
3.4
,
the global splitting morphisms make essential use of certain equivariant splittings
of the top cells of Stiefel manifolds that exist after smashing with non-trivial representations.
Consequently, those equivariant splittings require a genuine (as opposed to naive)
equivariant stabilization,
and our subsequent arguments would not work in the homotopy theory of orbispaces with only
the ordinary suspension inverted.

Thirdly,
Miller proves non-equivariant stable splittings of the finite-dimensional
real, complex and quaternionic Stiefel manifolds.
He then obtains the stable splitting of
O/O(m)
,
U/U(m)
and
Sp/Sp(m)

by passing to colimits.
Crabb
[
6
]
and Ullman
[
25
]

have obtained certain equivariant refinements of some of Miller’s splittings
for certain Stiefel manifolds of finite-dimensional representations of specific compact Lie groups.
Our results are in an entirely different direction.
In the global context, the finite-dimensional Stiefel manifolds are not underlying
any interesting global homotopy types (other than left or right induced).
So I cannot think of a meaningful global splitting that generalizes the
stable splittings of the finite-dimensional Stiefel manifolds due to Miller, Crabb and Ullman.
There is a certain tradeoff: the price for obtaining global results is to
work with infinite-dimensional objects.

Organization.

We start in Section
1
by explaining a special case of our main result,
the global stable splitting of the orthogonal space
\mathbf{O}

made from the orthogonal groups. The short Section
1
is logically redundant,
and intended as a gentle introduction to the main ideas in an important
special case that is technically and notationally simpler.
In Section
2
we introduce
the orthogonal spaces
\mathbf{O}/m
,
{\mathbf{U}}/m
and
{\mathbf{Sp}}/m

made from real, complex and quaternionic Stiefel manifolds, respectively.
We review their ‘eigenspace filtrations’
and recall Miller’s identification of the open strata of the filtration
as total spaces of specific vector bundles over Grassmannians.
Section
2
does not contain any new mathematics;
its purpose is to recast the known facts about the eigenspace filtration
in a form tailored to our purposes, while making all inherent symmetries explicit.
Section
3
is the heart of the paper, culminating in the
statement and proof of our main result, Theorem
3.8
;
here we construct the morphisms in the
Galois-equivariant global stable homotopy categories
that stably split the eigenspace filtrations of
\mathbf{O}/m
,
{\mathbf{U}}/m
and
{\mathbf{Sp}}/m
.
In Section
4
we explain how to pass to the colimit over
m
,
and obtain splittings of the orthogonal spaces

\mathbf{O}/\infty
,
{\mathbf{U}}/\infty
and
{\mathbf{Sp}}/\infty
.

This paper contains two appendices.
Appendix
A
is a brief introduction to
C
-global homotopy theory,
where
C
is a topological group.
This appendix is needed to give content to the Galois-equivariant refinements
of the stable global splittings of
{\mathbf{U}}/m
and
{\mathbf{Sp}}/m
;
for these application we are interested
in the special case where
C
is the Galois group
G({\mathbb{C}})
of
{\mathbb{C}}
over
{\mathbb{R}}

(a discrete group of order 2),
or where
C
is the group
G({\mathbb{H}})
of
{\mathbb{R}}
-algebra automorphism of the quaternions
(a compact Lie group isomorphic to
SO(3)
).
However, the basic theory works just as well over arbitrary topological groups,
so we develop it in that generality.
In Appendix
A

we in particular set up the triangulated
C
-global stable homotopy category
{\mathcal{GH}}_{C}
,
and identify global Thom spaces over global classifying spaces as representing objects
for equivariant homotopy groups, see Theorem
A.17
.
Appendix
B
provides proofs of the linear algebra facts
used in the main part of the paper.
I make no claim to originality for anything in Appendix
B
;
its purpose is to show that all relevant arguments from linear algebra over
{\mathbb{R}}

1.
Warm-up: the global stable splitting of
\mathbf{O}

In this short section we sketch the global stable splitting of the orthogonal space
\mathbf{O}

made from the orthogonal groups. This section is logically redundant,
as the splitting of Theorem
1.5
is a special case
of our main result, Theorem
3.8
.
I am prepending this section because
it already exhibits all the key features of the later arguments in a simpler form,
without the two additional layers of complexity arising from
the extra parameter
m
in the more general splittings of
\mathbf{O}/m
,
{\mathbf{U}}/m
and
{\mathbf{Sp}}/m
,
and the Galois-global embellishment in the complex and quaternionic setting.
All necessary tools for the global splitting of
\mathbf{O}
are
already contained in
[
21
]
, and there is no need
to appeal to the more general
C
-global homotopy theory from Appendix
A
.
Said differently, this brief section is intended as a gentle introduction to the main ideas in an important
special case that is technically and notationally simpler.

We will freely use the language and results from
[
21
]
.
In particular, we model unstable global homotopy theory by
orthogonal spaces in the sense of
[
21
, Definition 1.1.1]
,
i.e., continuous functors to spaces from the category

\mathbf{L}
of finite-dimensional euclidean inner product spaces and
{\mathbb{R}}
-linear isometric embeddings.
The category
\mathbf{L}
is also denoted
\mathscr{I}
or
\mathcal{I}
by other authors,
and orthogonal spaces are also known as
\mathscr{I}
-functors,

\mathscr{I}
-spaces or
\mathcal{I}
-spaces.
And we model stable global homotopy theory by orthogonal spectra
with respect to global equivalences as defined in
[
21
, Definition 4.1.3]
,
i.e., morphisms that induce isomorphisms of equivariant homotopy groups for all compact Lie groups.

Example 1.1
.

The orthogonal space
\mathbf{O}
made from the orthogonal groups is
a particular global refinement of the infinite orthogonal group
O=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}O(n)
.
The value of the
\mathbf{O}(V)
at an inner product space
V
is simply the orthogonal group of
V
.
A linear isometric embedding
\psi:V\longrightarrow W

is sent to the continuous group homomorphism
\mathbf{O}(\psi):\mathbf{O}(V)\longrightarrow\mathbf{O}(W)
defined by

\mathbf{O}(\psi)(f)(\psi(v)+w)\ =\ \psi(f(v))+w\ ,

where
v\in V
and
w\in W-\psi(V)
.
So informally speaking,
\mathbf{O}(\psi)
is ‘conjugation by
\psi
’ and direct sum
with the identity on the orthogonal complement of the image of
\psi
.
For more detailed information about the global homotopy type of
\mathbf{O}
we refer
the reader to the discussion in
[
21
, Example 2.3.6]
;
suffice it to say here that the underlying
G
-homotopy type of
\mathbf{O}
,
for a compact Lie group
G
, is the orthogonal group of a complete
G
-universe
(i.e., orthogonal automorphisms of the underlying inner product space with conjugation action
by
G
).

A filtration of
\mathbf{O}
by orthogonal subspaces
F_{k}\mathbf{O}
is defined by
the size of the +1 eigenspaces.
At an inner product space
V
, we set

(F_{k}\mathbf{O})(V)\ =\ \{f\in O(V)\ :\ \dim(\ker(f-\Id)^{\perp})\leq k\}\ ,

where
(-)^{\perp}
denotes the orthogonal complement.
For fixed
k
and varying
V
, these spaces are closed under the structure maps;
so they define an orthogonal subspace
F_{k}\mathbf{O}
of
\mathbf{O}
.
Altogether we obtain an ascending sequence of orthogonal spaces
F_{k}\mathbf{O}
that exhausts
\mathbf{O}
.
This filtration happens to be the skeleton filtration of the orthogonal space
\mathbf{O}

in the sense of
[
21
, Definition 1.2.2]
,
but we won’t show this fact because it plays no role for our arguments.

Before we attack the global splitting of the above eigenspace filtration of
\mathbf{O}
,
we recall the identification of the
k
-th subquotient
with the global Thom space of the adjoint representation of the
k
-th orthogonal group.
This identification is classical, going back at least to Frankel
[
8
]
,
and versions of it appear in
[
19
, Theorem B]
and
[
6
, Proposition 1.7]
.
We write
\mathbf{Gr}_{k}
for the
k
-th
Grassmannian
,
the orthogonal space whose value
\mathbf{Gr}_{k}(V)
is the
Grassmannian of
k
-planes in an inner product space
V
.
The structure maps take images under linear isometric embeddings.
The orthogonal space
\mathbf{Gr}_{k}
is a global classifying space,
in the sense of
[
21
, Definition 1.1.27]
,
of the orthogonal group
O(k)
.
We write

\mathfrak{ad}(k)\ =\ \{X\in M(k,k)\ :\ X^{t}=-X\}\ ,

for the
adjoint representation
of
O(k)
,
i.e., the
{\mathbb{R}}
-vector space of skew-symmetric real matrices of size
k\times k
,
with
O(k)
acting by conjugation.
The
global Thom space
over
\mathbf{Gr}_{k}
associated to
\mathfrak{ad}(k)

is the based orthogonal space

\mathbf{Gr}_{k}^{\mathfrak{ad}(k)}\ =\ \mathbf{L}({\mathbb{R}}^{k},-)_{+}\wedge_{O(k)}S^{\mathfrak{ad}(k)}\ .

The
Cayley transform
provides an open embedding

(1.2)

\mathfrak{c}\ :\ \mathfrak{ad}(k)\ \longrightarrow\ O(k)\ ,\quad\mathfrak{c}(X)\ =\ (X/2-1)(X/2+1)^{-1}

onto the subspace of
O(k)
of those matrices that do not have +1 as an eigenvalue.
The embedding is
O(k)
-equivariant for the conjugation actions on both sides.
For varying inner product spaces
V
,
the collapse maps associated to the open embeddings

\mathbf{L}({\mathbb{R}}^{k},V)\times_{O(k)}\mathfrak{ad}(k)\ \longrightarrow\ (F_{k}\mathbf{O})(V)\ ,\quad[\psi,X]\ \longmapsto\ \mathbf{O}(\psi)(\mathfrak{c}(X))\ ,

form a morphism of orthogonal spaces

(1.3)

\Psi\ :\ F_{k}\mathbf{O}\ \longrightarrow\ \mathbf{Gr}_{k}^{\mathfrak{ad}(k)}

that factors over an isomorphism
F_{k}\mathbf{O}/F_{k-1}\mathbf{O}\cong\mathbf{Gr}_{k}^{\mathfrak{ad}(k)}
.
This isomorphism is just a coordinate-free formulation of
a special case of Miller’s
[
19
, Theorem B]
.

On page 39 of
[
6
]
,
Crabb gives a particularly elegant exposition
of Miller’s method to stably split off the top cell of the unitary group
U(k)

in a fully equivariant manner; we reproduce the argument, adapted to
O(k)
.
We write

\mathfrak{sa}(k)\ =\ \{Z\in M(k,k)\ :\ Z^{t}=Z\}

for the vector space of symmetric real matrices of size
k\times k
;
the notation anticipates the role of
\mathfrak{sa}(k)
as the
self-adjoint endomorphisms of
{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
with respect to the standard inner product.
Then
M(k,k)=\mathfrak{ad}(k)\oplus\mathfrak{sa}(k)
.
A basic linear algebra fact is that the smooth map

O(k)\times\mathfrak{sa}(k)\ \longrightarrow\ M(k,k)\ ,\quad(A,Z)\ \longmapsto\ A\cdot\exp(-Z)\ =\ A\cdot{\sum}_{k\geq 0}(-Z)^{k}/k!

is an open embedding with image the subspace
GL_{k}
of invertible matrices.
The embedding is
O(k)
-equivariant
for the conjugation action on
O(k)
,
\mathfrak{sa}(k)
and
M(k,k)
,
so it provides an
O(k)
-equivariant collapse map

(1.4)

t_{k}\ :\ S^{\mathfrak{ad}(k)\oplus\mathfrak{sa}(k)}\ =\ S^{M(k,k)}\ \longrightarrow\ O(k)_{+}\wedge S^{\mathfrak{sa}(k)}

in the opposite direction.
The same reasoning as in
[
6
, page 39]

shows that the composite

S^{\mathfrak{ad}(k)\oplus\mathfrak{sa}(k)}\ \xrightarrow{\ t_{k}\ }\ O(k)_{+}\wedge S^{\mathfrak{sa}(k)}\ \xrightarrow{\mathfrak{c}^{\flat}\wedge S^{\mathfrak{sa}(k)}}\ S^{\mathfrak{ad}(k)\oplus\mathfrak{sa}(k)}

is
O(k)
-equivariantly based homotopic to the identity,
where
\mathfrak{c}^{\flat}
is the collapse map based on the open embedding (
1.2
),
see also the proof of Theorem
3.7
below.

Everything explained so far is a review or reformulation of ideas and results from

[
6
,
8
,
19
]
.
Now comes the point where we leverage
O(k)
-equivariant information
into global information, using the technology developed in
[
21
]
.
Specifically, we use the global representability theorem for equivariant homotopy groups
from
[
21
, Theorem 4.4.3 (i)]
, or rather a slight extension
from integer-graded to representation-graded homotopy groups.
The collapse map (
1.4
)
gives rise to an
\mathfrak{ad}(k)
-graded
O(k)
-equivariant homotopy class

\langle t_{k}\rangle\ \in\ \pi_{\mathfrak{ad}(k)}^{O(k)}(\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}F_{k}\mathbf{O})\ ,

the class represented by the composite

\displaystyle S^{\mathfrak{ad}(k)\oplus\mathfrak{sa}(k)\oplus\nu_{k}}\xrightarrow{t_{k}\wedge S^{\nu_{k}}}\ O(k)_{+}\wedge S^{\mathfrak{sa}(k)\oplus\nu_{k}}\

\displaystyle=\ (F_{k}\mathbf{O})(\nu_{k})_{+}\wedge S^{\mathfrak{sa}(k)\oplus\nu_{k}}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{(F_{k}\mathbf{O})(i_{2})\wedge\Id}\

\displaystyle(F_{k}\mathbf{O})(\mathfrak{sa}(k)\oplus\nu_{k})_{+}\wedge S^{\mathfrak{sa}(k)\oplus\nu_{k}}\ =\ (\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}F_{k}\mathbf{O})(\mathfrak{sa}(k)\oplus\nu_{k})\ .

Here
\nu_{k}
is the tautological
O(k)
-representation on
{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
,
and
i_{2}:\nu_{k}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{sa}(k)\oplus\nu_{k}
is the embedding as the second summand.
The representation-graded generalization of
[
21
, Theorem 4.4.3 (i)]

– which is also a special case of Theorem
A.17
(i) below –
provides a unique morphism

s_{k}\ :\ \Sigma^{\infty}\mathbf{Gr}_{k}^{\mathfrak{ad}(k)}\ \longrightarrow\ \Sigma^{\infty}_{+}F_{k}\mathbf{O}

in the global stable homotopy category characterized by the equation

(s_{k})_{*}(e_{O(k),\mathfrak{ad}(k)})\ =\ \langle t_{k}\rangle\ ,

where
e_{O(k),\mathfrak{ad}(k)}
is the tautological homotopy class
in
\pi_{\mathfrak{ad}(k)}^{O(k)}(\Sigma^{\infty}\mathbf{Gr}_{k}^{\mathfrak{ad}(k)})
.
The fact that
t_{k}
is a stable section to
\mathfrak{c}^{\flat}:O(k)\longrightarrow S^{\mathfrak{ad}(k)}

translates into the relation

(\Sigma^{\infty}\Psi)_{*}\langle t_{k}\rangle\ =\ e_{O(k),\mathfrak{ad}(k)}\ ,

where
\Psi:F_{k}\mathbf{O}\longrightarrow\mathbf{Gr}_{k}^{\mathfrak{ad}(k)}

is the collapse morphism (
1.3
).
Since the morphism
\Sigma^{\infty}\Psi\circ s_{k}

takes the tautological class
e_{O(k),\mathfrak{ad}(k)}
to itself,
another application of the representability property shows that
s_{k}

is a section, in the global stable homotopy category, to the morphism

\Sigma^{\infty}\Psi\ :\ \Sigma^{\infty}_{+}F_{k}\mathbf{O}\ \longrightarrow\ \Sigma^{\infty}\mathbf{Gr}_{k}^{\mathfrak{ad}(k)}\ .

So
s_{k}
splits the distinguished triangle

\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}F_{k-1}\mathbf{O}\longrightarrow\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}F_{k}\mathbf{O}\xrightarrow{\Sigma^{\infty}\Psi}\Sigma^{\infty}\mathbf{Gr}_{k}^{\mathfrak{ad}(k)}\longrightarrow(\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}F_{k-1}\mathbf{O})\wedge S^{1}

in the global stable homotopy category.
Induction on
k
and passage to the colimit over the eigenspace filtration
then yields:

Theorem 1.5
.

The morphism

\sum s_{k}\ :\ \bigvee_{k\geq 0}\Sigma^{\infty}\mathbf{Gr}_{k}^{\mathfrak{ad}(k)}\ \longrightarrow\ \Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\mathbf{O}

is an isomorphism in the global stable homotopy category.

This concludes the warm-up. In the next two sections
we promote the arguments leading up to Theorem
1.5

to Galois-equivariant global statements about the complex and quaterionic analogs
{\mathbf{U}}
and
{\mathbf{Sp}}

of
\mathbf{O}
, while simultaneously generalizing to
\mathbf{O}/m
,
{\mathbf{U}}/m
and
{\mathbf{Sp}}/m
for
m\geq 0
.

2.
The unstable filtration

In this section we introduce and study
the orthogonal spaces
\mathbf{O}/m
,
{\mathbf{U}}/m
and
{\mathbf{Sp}}/m

made from real, complex and quaternionic Stiefel manifolds, respectively.
These global objects are refinements of the spaces
O/O(m)
,
U/U(m)

and
Sp/Sp(m)
, respectively.
We review the ‘eigenspace filtrations’ of
\mathbf{O}/m
,
{\mathbf{U}}/m
and
{\mathbf{Sp}}/m

and recall Miller’s identification of the open strata of the filtration
as total spaces of specific vector bundles over Grassmannians.
This section does not contain any new mathematics; we only recast known results
in a form that is particularly convenient for our purposes,
and that makes all inherent symmetries explicit.

We start by fixing some notation and terminology.
We write
{\mathbb{H}}
for the skew-field of quaternions; it is the
{\mathbb{R}}
-vector space
with basis
(1,i,j,k)
and multiplication determined by the relations

i^{2}=j^{2}=k^{2}=ijk=-1\ .

Quaternion conjugation is the anti-involution
\lambda\mapsto\bar{\lambda}
of
{\mathbb{H}}
given by

\overline{a+bi+cj+dk}\ =\ a-bi-cj-dk

for
a,b,c,d\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
The quaternions contain the complex numbers
{\mathbb{C}}={\mathbb{R}}\{1,i\}
as a subfield,
and quaternion conjugation restricts to complex conjugation on
{\mathbb{C}}
.
The subfield
{\mathbb{R}}
coincides with the fixed points of conjugation.
For a uniform treatment we will also talk about conjugation on
{\mathbb{R}}
, where it is just the identity.

In order to treat the real, complex and quaternion Stiefel manifolds
simultaneously and uniformly,
we will write
{\mathbb{K}}
for any one of the skew-fields
{\mathbb{R}}
,
{\mathbb{C}}
or
{\mathbb{H}}
.
The particular choice of
{\mathbb{K}}
is always in the background,
but it is not always reflected in the notation.

Notation 2.1

(Galois groups)
.

Throughout our discussion we keep track of the symmetries of the skew-fields
{\mathbb{R}}
,
{\mathbb{C}}
and
{\mathbb{H}}
:
we write

G({\mathbb{K}})\ =\ \Aut_{\mathbb{R}}({\mathbb{K}})

for the ‘Galois group’ of
{\mathbb{K}}
over
{\mathbb{R}}
,
i.e., the compact Lie group of
{\mathbb{R}}
-algebra automorphisms of
{\mathbb{K}}
.
Obviously,
G({\mathbb{R}})
is a trivial group, and
G({\mathbb{C}})
is the usual Galois group of
the field extension
{\mathbb{C}}
over
{\mathbb{R}}
.
The ‘Galois group’
G({\mathbb{H}})
of the quaternions is isomorphic to
SO(3)
.
More specifically, the tautological action of
G({\mathbb{H}})
on
{\mathbb{H}}

identifies it with the special orthogonal group
of the 3-dimensional subspace
{\mathbb{R}}\{i,j,k\}

with respect to the euclidean inner product
\langle x,y\rangle=\text{Re}(\bar{x}y)
.
Also, every
{\mathbb{R}}
-automorphism of
{\mathbb{H}}
is inner, i.e., given by conjugation
by an element of
Sp(1)=\{x\in{\mathbb{H}}\colon\bar{x}\cdot x=1\}
;
since the center of
Sp(1)
consists of
\pm 1
, the map

\text{inner}\ :\ Sp(1)/\{\pm 1\}\ \xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }\ G({\mathbb{H}})

that sends a unit quaternion to the associated inner automorphism is an isomorphism.

Definition 2.2

(Inner product spaces)
.

We let
{\mathbb{K}}
be one of the skew-fields
{\mathbb{R}}
,
{\mathbb{C}}
or
{\mathbb{H}}
.
A
{\mathbb{K}}
-inner product space
is a finite-dimensional right
{\mathbb{K}}
-module
W

equipped with an
{\mathbb{R}}
-bilinear
{\mathbb{K}}
-valued inner product

[-,-]\ :\ W\times W\ \longrightarrow\ {\mathbb{K}}

that is sesquilinear and hermitian in the sense of the relations

[x\cdot\lambda,y\cdot\mu]\ =\ \bar{\lambda}\cdot[x,y]\cdot\mu\text{\qquad and\qquad}[y,x]\ =\ \overline{[x,y]}

for all
x,y\in W
and
\lambda,\mu\in{\mathbb{K}}
, and that is positive-definite,
i.e., the real number
[x,x]
is positive unless
x=0
.
Then
length
of a vector
x\in W
is the real number
|x|=\sqrt{[x,x]}
.

When
{\mathbb{K}}
is
{\mathbb{R}}
or
{\mathbb{C}}
, inner product spaces are usually called
euclidean vector spaces

or
hermitian vector spaces
, respectively.
I do not know of standard terminology for
{\mathbb{H}}
-inner product spaces.

¨

Since
{\mathbb{R}}
and
{\mathbb{C}}
are commutative, one can write the
factors
\bar{\lambda}
,
[x,y]
and
\mu
in the sesquilinearity relation
in any other order, and the more common definitions display the scalars on the same side
of the inner product.
Multiplication in the quaternions is not commutative, so for
{\mathbb{K}}={\mathbb{H}}
,
one has to beware of the order of the factors
\bar{\lambda}
,
[x,y]
and
\mu
.

We will write
{\mathbb{K}}^{k}
for the standard
k
-dimensional
{\mathbb{K}}
-vector space
endowed with the inner product

[x,y]\ =\ \bar{x}_{1}\cdot y_{1}+\dots+\bar{x}_{k}\cdot y_{k}\ .

Given a
{\mathbb{K}}
-subspace
V
of a
{\mathbb{K}}
-inner product space
W
, we write

W-V\ =\ \{w\in W\ :\ [v,w]=0\text{ for all $v\in V$}\}

for the
orthogonal complement
, another
{\mathbb{K}}
-subspace such that
W=V\oplus(W-V)
.
If the ambient space
W
is clear from the context, we might also use the notation
V^{\perp}
for
W-V
.

Given two
{\mathbb{K}}
-inner product spaces
V
and
W
, we write

\mathbf{L}^{\mathbb{K}}(V,W)
for the Stiefel manifold of
{\mathbb{K}}
-linear isometric embeddings from
V
to
W
,
i.e.,
{\mathbb{K}}
-linear maps that respect the inner products, and are thus necessarily injective.
For
{\mathbb{K}}={\mathbb{R}}
we sometimes omit the superscript, i.e.,
\mathbf{L}(V,W)=\mathbf{L}^{\mathbb{R}}(V,W)
.

Construction 2.3
.

We recall the filtration of
\mathbf{L}^{\mathbb{K}}(W,W\oplus{\mathbb{K}}^{m})
whose non-equivariant stable splitability
is the subject of Miller’s paper
[
19
]
.
For
k\geq 0
we set

(2.4)

\mathbf{F}_{k}(W;m)\ =\ \{f\in\mathbf{L}^{\mathbb{K}}(W,W\oplus{\mathbb{K}}^{m})\ :\ \dim_{\mathbb{K}}(\ker(f-i_{1})^{\perp})\leq k\}\ ,

where
i_{1}:W\longrightarrow W\oplus{\mathbb{K}}^{m}
is the embedding of the first summand.
For a linear isometric embedding
f:W\longrightarrow W\oplus{\mathbb{K}}^{m}
, the kernel of
f-i_{1}

coincides with the kernel of the endomorphism
f_{1}-1:W\longrightarrow W
, i.e.,
the
+1
eigenspace of the first component of
f
.
We will therefore refer to this filtration as the
eigenspace filtration
.

For a
{\mathbb{K}}
-linear isometric embedding
\psi:V\longrightarrow W
we define ‘conjugation by
\psi
’

(2.5)

{{}^{\psi}(-)}\ :\ \mathbf{L}^{\mathbb{K}}(V,V\oplus{\mathbb{K}}^{m})\ \longrightarrow\ \mathbf{L}^{\mathbb{K}}(W,W\oplus{\mathbb{K}}^{m})

by

(^{\psi}f)(\psi(v)+w)\ =\ (\psi\oplus{\mathbb{K}}^{m})(f(v))+(w,0)\ ,

where
v\in V
and
w\in W-\psi(V)
.
This continuous map is a closed embedding that preserves the eigenspace filtration,
i.e., it sends
\mathbf{F}_{k}(V;m)
into
\mathbf{F}_{k}(W;m)
.

The subquotients of the eigenspace filtration
can be described explicitly as Thom spaces over Grassmannians,
see Theorem
2.13
below.
The key ideas originate from Frankel’s paper
[
8
]
,
and the non-equivariant version of the formulation we need is due to Miller
[
19
, Theorem B]
.
Given a finite-dimensional
{\mathbb{K}}
-inner product space
W
,
Miller
[
19
, Theorem A]
uses Morse theory to identify the stratum

\mathbf{F}_{k}(W;m)\setminus\mathbf{F}_{k-1}(W;m)

with the total space of the vector bundle over the Grassmannian
Gr_{k}^{\mathbb{K}}(W)

associated to the representation
\Hom_{\mathbb{K}}({\mathbb{K}}^{k},{\mathbb{K}}^{m})\oplus\mathfrak{ad}(k)

of
O(k)
,
U(k)
or
Sp(k)
.
We now recall a different identification,
based on explicit formulas due to Crabb
[
6
]
,
see (
2.9
) below.

Notation 2.6
.

We write
I(k)=\mathbf{L}^{\mathbb{K}}({\mathbb{K}}^{k},{\mathbb{K}}^{k})
for the isometry group
of the standard
k
-dimensional
{\mathbb{K}}
-inner product space.
And we write

\mathfrak{ad}(k)\ =\ \{X\in\End_{\mathbb{K}}({\mathbb{K}}^{k})\ :\ X^{*}=-X\}

for the
{\mathbb{R}}
-vector spaces of skew-adjoint endomorphisms of
{\mathbb{K}}^{k}
,
where
X^{*}
denotes the adjoint endomorphism, compare Remark
B.1
.
If we express linear endomorphism of
{\mathbb{K}}^{k}
as matrices, the group
I(k)

becomes the orthogonal group
O(k)
,
the unitary group
U(k)
, or the symplectic group
Sp(k)
, respectively,
and the adjoint operator corresponds to the conjugate-transpose matrix.
The isometry group
I(k)
acts on
\mathfrak{ad}(k)
by conjugation.
The exponential map (
B.3
) restricts to a map

\exp\ :\ \mathfrak{ad}(k)\ \longrightarrow\ I(k)

from the skew-adjoint endomorphisms to the isometry group of
{\mathbb{K}}^{k}
.
This map is a local diffeomorphism around the origin,
and it exhibits
\mathfrak{ad}(k)

as the adjoint representation of the compact Lie group
I(k)
,
whence the notation.

Construction 2.7
.

Crabb states in
[
6
, Lemma 1.13]
that for all
k,m\geq 0
, the map

(2.8)

\mathfrak{c}\ :\ \Hom_{{\mathbb{K}}}({\mathbb{K}}^{k},{\mathbb{K}}^{m})\oplus\mathfrak{ad}(k)\ \longrightarrow\ \mathbf{L}^{\mathbb{K}}({\mathbb{K}}^{k},{\mathbb{K}}^{k+m})\ ,\quad\mathfrak{c}(Y,X)(w)=\ (g(w),h(w))

with

\displaystyle g\

\displaystyle=\ (X/2+Y^{*}Y/4-1)(X/2+Y^{*}Y/4+1)^{-1}

\displaystyle:\ {\mathbb{K}}^{k}\ \longrightarrow\ {\mathbb{K}}^{k}

\displaystyle h\

\displaystyle=\ Y(X/2+Y^{*}Y/4+1)^{-1}

\displaystyle:\ {\mathbb{K}}^{k}\ \longrightarrow\ {\mathbb{K}}^{m}

is an open embedding onto the complement of

\mathbf{F}_{k-1}({\mathbb{K}}^{k};m)
in
\mathbf{L}^{\mathbb{K}}({\mathbb{K}}^{k},{\mathbb{K}}^{k+m})
.
Crabb works in the complex setting and refrains from giving a proof,
so we provide an argument in Proposition
B.8
.
The open embedding
\mathfrak{c}
is clearly equivariant for the action of the isometry group
I(k)
,
acting tautologically on all instances of
{\mathbb{K}}^{k}
and trivially on all instances of
{\mathbb{K}}^{m}
.
More precisely, the relation

\mathfrak{c}(YA^{*},AXA^{*})\ =\ (A\oplus{\mathbb{K}}^{m})\cdot\mathfrak{c}(Y,X)\cdot A^{*}

holds for all
A\in I(k)
.
For
m=0
, the map specializes to the Cayley transform

\mathfrak{c}:\mathfrak{ad}(k)\longrightarrow I(k)
,
see also (
1.2
) above.

A generalization of the open embedding (
2.8
) is the map

(2.9)

\mathbf{L}^{\mathbb{K}}({\mathbb{K}}^{k},W)\times_{I(k)}(\Hom_{\mathbb{K}}({\mathbb{K}}^{k},{\mathbb{K}}^{m})\oplus\mathfrak{ad}(k))\ \longrightarrow\ \mathbf{F}_{k}(W;m)\ ,\quad[\psi,Y,X]\ \longmapsto\ {{}^{\psi}(\mathfrak{c}(Y,X))}\ .

This map is an open embedding onto the complement of
\mathbf{F}_{k-1}(W;m)
,
i.e., we recover Miller’s
[
19
, Theorem A]
.
For the convenience of the reader,
we also recall a proof of this fact in Proposition
B.11
.

Now and in the following, we shall abbreviate

\nu(k,m)\ =\ \Hom_{\mathbb{K}}({\mathbb{K}}^{k},{\mathbb{K}}^{m})\ .

A reader so inclined may identify this with the space of

{\mathbb{K}}
-valued matrices of size
k\times m
, or with the
m
-fold sum of
the tautological representation of
I(k)
on
{\mathbb{K}}^{k}
, via the isomorphism

\Hom_{\mathbb{K}}({\mathbb{K}}^{k},{\mathbb{K}}^{m})\ \cong\ {\mathbb{K}}^{k}\oplus\dots\oplus{\mathbb{K}}^{k}\ ,\quad Y\ \longmapsto\ (Y^{*}(e_{1}),\dots Y^{*}(e_{m}))

that evaluates the adjoint at the coordinate basis.
The collapse map associated to the embedding (
2.9
)
then becomes a continuous map

\Psi(W)\ :\ \mathbf{F}_{k}(W;m)\ \longrightarrow\ \mathbf{L}^{\mathbb{K}}({\mathbb{K}}^{k},W)_{+}\wedge_{I(k)}S^{\nu(k,m)\oplus\mathfrak{ad}(k)}\ .

In the rest of this section we organize the Stiefel manifolds, their eigenspace
filtrations and the identifications of the strata and subquotients
into unstable global information.
We continue to use the orthogonal space model of
[
21
, Section 1]

to represent unstable global homotopy types.
Since we want to keep track of the actions of the Galois group
G({\mathbb{C}})
and
G({\mathbb{H}})
,
we actually need an extension
to the context of ‘
C
-global homotopy theory’, i.e., a global homotopy theory
that incorporates an additional action of a topological group
C
.
We develop the necessary theory in Appendix
A
,
modeled by orthogonal
C
-spaces,
i.e., continuous functors from the linear isometries category
\mathbf{L}
to
the category of
C
-spaces.

Example 2.10

(The orthogonal spaces
\mathbf{O}/m
,
{\mathbf{U}}/m
and
{\mathbf{Sp}}/m
)
.

We let
{\mathbb{K}}
be one of the skew fields
{\mathbb{R}}
,
{\mathbb{C}}
or
{\mathbb{H}}
.
For
m\geq 0
, we define an orthogonal
G({\mathbb{K}})
-space
\mathbf{L}/m
as follows.
The value of
\mathbf{L}/m
at a euclidean inner product space
V
is

(\mathbf{L}/m)(V)\ =\ \mathbf{L}^{\mathbb{K}}(V_{\mathbb{K}},V_{\mathbb{K}}\oplus{\mathbb{K}}^{m})\ ,

the Stiefel manifold of
{\mathbb{K}}
-linear isometric embeddings of
V_{\mathbb{K}}
into
V_{\mathbb{K}}\oplus{\mathbb{K}}^{m}
.
Here
V_{\mathbb{K}}=V\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathbb{K}}
is the scalar extension from
{\mathbb{R}}
to
{\mathbb{K}}
,
endowed with the
{\mathbb{K}}
-inner product

[x\otimes\lambda,y\otimes\mu]\ =\ \bar{\lambda}\cdot\langle x,y\rangle\cdot\mu

for
x,y\in V
and
\lambda,\mu\in{\mathbb{K}}
.
The space
(\mathbf{L}/m)(V)
is homeomorphic to the homogeneous space

O(k+m)/O(m)
,
U(k+m)/U(m)
or
Sp(k+m)/Sp(m)
, where
k=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(V)
.
An
{\mathbb{R}}
-linear isometric embedding
\varphi:V\longrightarrow W
is sent to the continuous map

(\mathbf{L}/m)(\varphi)\ =\ {{}^{\varphi_{\mathbb{K}}}(-)}\ :\ \mathbf{L}^{\mathbb{K}}(V_{\mathbb{K}},V_{\mathbb{K}}\oplus{\mathbb{K}}^{m})\ \longrightarrow\ \mathbf{L}^{\mathbb{K}}(W_{\mathbb{K}},W_{\mathbb{K}}\oplus{\mathbb{K}}^{m})

given by conjugation by
\varphi_{\mathbb{K}}:V_{\mathbb{K}}\longrightarrow W_{\mathbb{K}}
as defined in (
2.5
).
The Galois group
G({\mathbb{K}})=\Aut_{\mathbb{R}}({\mathbb{K}})
acts on
(\mathbf{L}/m)(V)
as a similar ‘conjugation’:
for
\tau\in G({\mathbb{K}})
we define

\displaystyle{{}^{\tau}}(-)\ :\

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}^{\mathbb{K}}(V_{\mathbb{K}},V_{\mathbb{K}}\oplus{\mathbb{K}}^{m})\ \longrightarrow\ \mathbf{L}^{\mathbb{K}}(V_{\mathbb{K}},V_{\mathbb{K}}\oplus{\mathbb{K}}^{m})\text{\quad by}

\displaystyle{{}^{\tau}f}\

\displaystyle=\ ((V\otimes\tau)\oplus\tau^{m})\circ f\circ(V\otimes\tau)^{-1}\ .

As
\tau
varies, these maps define a continuous
G({\mathbb{K}})
-action on
(\mathbf{L}/m)(V)
.
As
V
varies, these actions assemble into a continuous
G({\mathbb{K}})
-action
on the orthogonal space
\mathbf{L}/m
.
So we can – and will – view
\mathbf{L}/m
as an orthogonal
G({\mathbb{K}})
-space.
Whenever the skew-field
{\mathbb{K}}
is specific, we replace the generic notation
\mathbf{L}/m

by the specific notation
\mathbf{O}/m
(for
{\mathbb{K}}={\mathbb{R}}
),

{\mathbf{U}}/m
(for
{\mathbb{K}}={\mathbb{C}}
) or
{\mathbf{Sp}}/m
(for
{\mathbb{K}}={\mathbb{H}}
).

The eigenspace filtration (
2.4
) provides an exhausting filtration

\ast\ =\ F_{0}(\mathbf{L}/m)\ \subseteq\ F_{1}(\mathbf{L}/m)\ \subseteq\ F_{2}(\mathbf{L}/m)\ \subseteq\ \dots\ \subseteq\ F_{k}(\mathbf{L}/m)\ \subseteq\ \dots

of
\mathbf{L}/m
by
G({\mathbb{K}})
-invariant orthogonal subspaces with terms

F_{k}(\mathbf{L}/m)(V)\ =\ \mathbf{F}_{k}(V_{\mathbb{K}};m)\ =\ \{f\in\mathbf{L}^{\mathbb{K}}(V_{\mathbb{K}},V_{\mathbb{K}}\oplus{\mathbb{K}}^{m})\ :\ \dim_{\mathbb{K}}(\ker(f-i_{1})^{\perp})\leq k\}\ .

Example 2.11

(The orthogonal spaces
\mathbf{O}
,
{\mathbf{SO}}
,
{\mathbf{U}}
,
{\mathbf{SU}}
and
{\mathbf{Sp}}
)
.

The special cases
m=0
and
m=1
of Example
2.10

provide particularly interesting global refinements of the infinite orthogonal,
special orthogonal, unitary, special unitary and symplectic groups.
Indeed,
(\mathbf{L}/0)(V)=\mathbf{L}^{\mathbb{K}}(V_{\mathbb{K}},V_{\mathbb{K}})

is the isometry group of
V_{\mathbb{K}}
; in this special case,
\mathbf{L}/0
also supports
an ultra-commutative multiplication in the sense of
[
21
, Chapter 2]

by orthogonal direct sum of isometric embeddings.
In the real case,
\mathbf{L}/0
is the ultra-commutative monoid
\mathbf{O}
of orthogonal groups
discussed in detail in
[
21
, Example 2.3.6]
.
In the complex case, we obtain the ultra-commutative monoid
{\mathbf{U}}
of unitary groups
discussed in
[
21
, Example 2.3.7]
,
with additional action by the Galois group of
{\mathbb{C}}
over
{\mathbb{R}}
.
For
{\mathbb{K}}={\mathbb{H}}
, the construction becomes the ultra-commutative monoid
{\mathbf{Sp}}

made from symplectic groups, compare
[
21
, Example 2.3.9]
,
with an additional action by the compact Lie group
G({\mathbb{H}})
.

In the real and complex cases, the special case
m=1
also deserves explicit mentioning.
Indeed, in the real case,
\mathbf{L}/1
is globally equivalent to the
orthogonal space
{\mathbf{SO}}
of special orthogonal groups, the
ultra-commutative submonoid of
\mathbf{O}
consisting of the special orthogonal groups,
compare Proposition
3.11
.
Similarly, in the complex case,
\mathbf{L}/1
is
G({\mathbb{C}})
-globally equivalent to the
orthogonal space
{\mathbf{SU}}
of special unitary groups, see again Proposition
3.11
.
We refer the reader to the extensive discussion in
[
21
, Chapter 2]

for further information about equivariant and
global homotopical properties of
\mathbf{O}
,
{\mathbf{SO}}
,
{\mathbf{U}}
,
{\mathbf{SU}}
and
{\mathbf{Sp}}
.

The main result of this paper, Theorem
3.8
below,
says that the filtration of
\mathbf{L}/m
by the orthogonal
G({\mathbb{K}})
-subspaces
F_{k}(\mathbf{L}/m)

splits once we pass to the
G({\mathbb{K}})
-global stable homotopy category.
As we record now, the individual identifications of the subquotients of the eigenspace
filtration are natural enough to assemble into isomorphisms of

G({\mathbb{K}})
-spaces.

Example 2.12

(Global Thom spaces)
.

We organize the various Grassmannians
and the relevant Thom spaces over them into orthogonal
G({\mathbb{K}})
-spaces.
We write
\mathbf{Gr}_{k}^{\mathbb{K}}
for the orthogonal space whose value
at a euclidean inner product space
V
is
the Grassmannian of
k
-dimensional
{\mathbb{K}}
-subspaces in
V_{\mathbb{K}}=V\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathbb{K}}
.
The structure map
\mathbf{Gr}_{k}^{\mathbb{K}}(\varphi)

associated to an
{\mathbb{R}}
-linear isometric embedding
\varphi:V\longrightarrow W

takes the image under the scalar extension
\varphi_{\mathbb{K}}:V_{\mathbb{K}}\longrightarrow W_{\mathbb{K}}
.
A Galois automorphism
\tau\in G({\mathbb{K}})
acts on
\mathbf{Gr}_{k}^{\mathbb{K}}(V)
by
taking images under
V\otimes\tau:V_{\mathbb{K}}\longrightarrow V_{\mathbb{K}}
;
this operation does take
{\mathbb{K}}
-subspaces to

{\mathbb{K}}
-subspaces, despite the fact that
V\otimes\tau
need not be
{\mathbb{K}}
-linear.
Altogether, it makes
\mathbf{Gr}_{k}^{\mathbb{K}}
into an orthogonal
G({\mathbb{K}})
-space.

The Galois group acts continuously on the isometry group
I(k)=\mathbf{L}^{\mathbb{K}}({\mathbb{K}}^{k},{\mathbb{K}}^{k})

by conjugation, i.e., by

{{}^{\tau}f}\ =\ \tau^{k}\circ f\circ(\tau^{k})^{-1}

for
\tau\in G({\mathbb{K}})
and
f\in I(k)
.
The
extended isometry group
is

\tilde{I}(k)\ =\ I(k)\rtimes G({\mathbb{K}})\ ,

the semidirect product for this action.
By Theorem
A.33
,
\mathbf{Gr}_{k}^{\mathbb{K}}
is a global classifying
G({\mathbb{K}})
-space
for the augmentation
\epsilon_{k}:\tilde{I}(k)\longrightarrow G({\mathbb{K}})
of the extended isometry group.
In particular, the orthogonal space of
\mathbf{Gr}_{k}^{\mathbb{R}}
is a global classifying space
for the orthogonal group
O(k)
, and
the underlying orthogonal spaces of
\mathbf{Gr}_{k}^{\mathbb{C}}
and
\mathbf{Gr}_{k}^{\mathbb{H}}
are
global classifying spaces for the unitary group
U(k)
and symplectic group
Sp(k)
, respectively.

Now we let
U
be a real representation of the extended isometry group
\tilde{I}(k)
.
The Stiefel manifold
\mathbf{L}^{\mathbb{K}}({\mathbb{K}}^{k},V_{\mathbb{K}})
comes with a continuous

\tilde{I}(k)
-action by

{{}^{(A,\tau)}\varphi}\ =\ (V\otimes\tau)\circ\varphi\circ(A\circ\tau^{k})^{-1}\ .

The normal subgroup
I(k)
of
\tilde{I}(k)
acts freely,
and the orthogonal orbit space
\mathbf{L}^{\mathbb{K}}({\mathbb{K}}^{k},V_{\mathbb{K}})/I(k)

is isomorphic to the Grassmannian
\mathbf{Gr}_{k}^{\mathbb{K}}(V)
, by sending a linear isometric embedding to its image.
We endow the space
\mathbf{L}^{\mathbb{K}}({\mathbb{K}}^{k},V_{\mathbb{K}})\times_{I(k)}U

with a
G({\mathbb{K}})
-action by

{{}^{\tau}[\varphi,u]}\ =\ [^{(1,\tau)}\varphi,(1,\tau)u]\ ;

it is the total space of a
G({\mathbb{K}})
-vector bundle over
\mathbf{Gr}_{k}^{\mathbb{K}}(V)
, with Thom
G({\mathbb{K}})
-space

\mathbf{L}^{\mathbb{K}}({\mathbb{K}}^{k},V_{\mathbb{K}})_{+}\wedge_{I(k)}S^{U}
.
We write

(\mathbf{Gr}_{k}^{\mathbb{K}})^{U}\ =\ \mathbf{L}^{\mathbb{K}}({\mathbb{K}}^{k},(-)_{\mathbb{K}})_{+}\wedge_{I(k)}S^{U}\ ,

and refer to this as the
G({\mathbb{K}})
-global Thom space
associated to
U
.

If we specialize the collapse map associated to the open embedding (
2.9
)
to inner product spaces of the form
W=V_{\mathbb{K}}
,
for euclidean inner product spaces
V
, it becomes a continuous map

\Psi(V_{\mathbb{K}})\ :\ F_{k}(\mathbf{L}/m)(V)\ =\ \mathbf{F}_{k}(V_{\mathbb{K}};m)\ \longrightarrow\ \mathbf{L}^{\mathbb{K}}({\mathbb{K}}^{k},V_{\mathbb{K}})_{+}\wedge_{I(k)}S^{\nu(k,m)\oplus\mathfrak{ad}(k)}\ =\ (\mathbf{Gr}_{k}^{\mathbb{K}})^{\nu(k,m)\oplus\mathfrak{ad}(k)}(V)\ .

We have thus proved the following theorem,
which summarizes work of Miller
[
19
, Theorem B]

and Crabb
[
6
, Lemma 1.14]
in a coordinate-free
and Galois-enhanced fashion.

Theorem 2.13
.

For varying euclidean inner product spaces
V
,
the maps
\Psi(V_{\mathbb{K}})
constitute a morphism of orthogonal
G({\mathbb{K}})
-spaces

\Psi\ :\ F_{k}(\mathbf{L}/m)\ \longrightarrow\ (\mathbf{Gr}_{k}^{\mathbb{K}})^{\nu(k,m)\oplus\mathfrak{ad}(k)}

that factors through an isomorphism

F_{k}(\mathbf{L}/m)/F_{k-1}(\mathbf{L}/m)\ \cong\ (\mathbf{Gr}_{k}^{\mathbb{K}})^{\nu(k,m)\oplus\mathfrak{ad}(k)}\ .

We will also need to know that the pointset level eigenspace filtration of the orthogonal space

\mathbf{L}/m
is homotopically meaningful. This is guaranteed by the following proposition,
showing that the pairs of successive filtration stages have the homotopy extension
property internal to orthogonal
G({\mathbb{K}})
-spaces.

Proposition 2.14
.

For all
k,m\geq 0,
the inclusion

Proof.

using a Morse theoretic construction that goes back to Frankel

The upper row is the inclusion of the unit sphere into the unit disc of the representation

But this is easy to fix: as explained in

The inclusion of unit sphere into unit disc of any orthogonal representation of a compact Lie

is also stable under cobase change, we conclude that

For every

is a relative homeomorphism from the pair

Taking

and letting

thus yields a pushout square of orthogonal

\textstyle{F_{k-1}(\mathbf{L}/m)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Since the inclusion

has the

The homotopy extension property is stable under cobase change, so this proves the claim.

The stable splitting

In this section we construct the morphisms in the

Construction 3.1

Theorem 3.8
.

be one of the skew-fields

In a first step we prove the analogous splitting result for

-global stable homotopy category.
There is nothing to show for

The inclusion

in the category of orthogonal

of orthogonal

\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}F_{n-1}(\mathbf{L}/m)\longrightarrow\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}F_{n}(\mathbf{L}/m)\xrightarrow{\Sigma^{\infty}\Psi}\Sigma^{\infty}(\mathbf{Gr}_{n}^{\mathbb{K}})^{\nu(n,m)\oplus\mathfrak{ad}(n)}\longrightarrow(\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}F_{n-1}(\mathbf{L}/m))\wedge S^{1}

in the

-global stable homotopy category;
we have used Theorem

G({\mathbb{K}})
-global stable homotopy category.

Passage to the colimit over

For every continuous homomorphism

Without the external

-action, there are several models for unstable global homotopy theory available;
historically, the first model were the orbispaces of Gepner-Henriques
[
9
]

that model a suitable homotopy theory of topological stacks.
More recently, the author developed models in terms
of orthogonal spaces
[
21
, Section 1]

and spaces with an action of the ‘universal compact Lie group’
[
22
]
.
We will generalize the orthogonal space model now,
and work with orthogonal
C
-spaces,
i.e., orthogonal spaces equipped with a continuous
C
-action;
the relevant homotopy theory is encoded in
the class of
C
-global equivalences
, see Definition
A.2
.

For the purposes of this paper, a
space
is a compactly generated space in the
sense of
[
18
]
, i.e., a
k
-space (also called Kelley space) that
satisfies the weak Hausdorff condition.
We write
{\mathbf{T}}
for the category of compactly generated spaces and continuous maps.
A
topological group
is a group object internal to
the category
{\mathbf{T}}
of compactly generated spaces.
A
C
-space
is then a
C
-object internal to
{\mathbf{T}}
, i.e., a compactly generated space
X

equipped with an associative and unital action

C\times X\longrightarrow X

that is continuous with respect to the compactly generated product topology.
We write
C{\mathbf{T}}
for the category of
C
-spaces and continuous
C
-maps.

As in
[
21
]
, we denote by
\mathbf{L}
the category with objects the
finite-dimensional euclidean inner product spaces
and morphisms the
{\mathbb{R}}
-linear isometric embeddings.
The morphism spaces of the category
\mathbf{L}
come with a preferred topology
as Stiefel manifolds; this makes
\mathbf{L}
into a topological category.

Definition A.1
.

Let
C
be a topological group. An
orthogonal

-space
is a continuous functor from the
linear isometries category
\mathbf{L}
to the category of
C
-spaces.
A

of orthogonal

-spaces is a natural transformation of functors.
We denote the category of orthogonal
C
-spaces by
spc_{C}
.

The use of continuous functors from the category
\mathbf{L}
to spaces has a long history
in homotopy theory.
The category
\mathbf{L}
(or its extension that
also contains countably infinite-dimensional inner product spaces)
is denoted
\mathscr{I}
by Boardman and Vogt
[
3
]
,
and this notation is also used in
[
16
]
;
other sources
[
14
]
use the symbol
\mathcal{I}
.
Accordingly, orthogonal spaces are sometimes referred to as
\mathscr{I}
-functors,

\mathscr{I}
-spaces or
\mathcal{I}
-spaces.

Orthogonal

-spaces admit a refinement of global homotopy theory
that takes the

-action into account, and that generalizes the unstable global homotopy theory
as developed in
[
21
, Chapter 1]
.
The additional homotopical information is located at compact Lie groups ‘augmented over

i.e., compact Lie groups

equipped with a continuous homomorphism
\alpha:G\longrightarrow C
.

For us, a

of a compact Lie group

is an inner product space

equipped with a continuous
G
-action through linear isometries.
Such an action can also be packaged as a continuous homomorphism
\rho:G\longrightarrow O(V)

to the orthogonal group of

For every orthogonal
C
-space

, every continuous homomorphism
\alpha:G\longrightarrow C
and

G
-representation

, the value

comes with a continuous
(G\times C)
-action
from the

-action on

and the
C
-action on

.
For a
G

,
the induced map

is
(G\times C)
-equivariant.
We write

\Gamma(\alpha)\ =\ \{(g,\alpha(g))\ |\ g\in G\}

for the graph of

, a closed subgroup of
G\times C
.
We denote by

D^{k}\ =\ \{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}\ :\ |x|\leq 1\}\text{\qquad and\qquad}\partial D^{k}\ =\ \{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}\ :\ |x|=1\}

the unit disc in

and its boundary, a sphere of dimension

respectively.
In particular,
D^{0}=\{0\}
is a one-point space and
\partial D^{0}=\emptyset

is empty.

Definition A.2
.

Let
C
be a topological group.
A morphism

of orthogonal
C
-spaces is a
C
-global equivalence

if the following condition holds:
for every compact Lie group
G
,
every continuous homomorphism
\alpha:G\longrightarrow C
,
every
G
-representation
V
,
every

and all continuous maps

and

such that
b|_{\partial D^{k}}=f(V)^{\Gamma(\alpha)}\circ a
,
there is a
G
-representation

,
a

\varphi:V\longrightarrow W

and a continuous map

such that
\lambda|_{\partial D^{k}}=X(\varphi)^{\Gamma(\alpha)}\circ a
and
such that
f(W)^{\Gamma(\alpha)}\circ\lambda

is homotopic, relative to

, to
Y(\varphi)^{\Gamma(\alpha)}\circ b
.

If the group

is trivial, then the notion of

-global equivalence specializes
to the global equivalences of
[
21
, Definition 1.1.2]
.
We recall from
[
21
, Definition 1.1.16]
that an orthogonal space

is

if it takes every linear isometric embedding
\varphi:V\longrightarrow W
of inner product spaces
to a closed embedding
Y(\varphi):Y(V)\longrightarrow Y(W)
.
Most orthogonal spaces that occur naturally are closed,
and for morphisms between closed orthogonal
C
-spaces,
the next proposition provides a useful criterion for
detecting
C
-global equivalences.

We let
G
be a compact Lie group. We recall that a
complete
G
-universe

is an orthogonal
G
-representation of countably infinite dimension into which
every finite-dimensional
G
-representation embeds,
by an equivariant
{\mathbb{R}}
-linear isometric embedding.
Complete
G
-universe exists, they are unique up to equivariant linear
isomorphism, and the space of equivariant linear isometric self-embeddings
of a complete

-universe is contractible.
If

is a closed subgroup of a compact Lie group

then the underlying
H
-representation of a complete

G
-universe is a complete

-universe.
In the following, for every compact Lie group

we fix
a complete
G
-universe
{\mathcal{U}}_{G}
.
We let

denote the poset, under inclusion,

.
The

of an orthogonal space

Y({\mathcal{U}}_{G})\ =\ \colim_{V\in s({\mathcal{U}}_{G})}\,Y(V)\ ,

the colimit of the

.
If

is an orthogonal

then

becomes a
(G\times C)
-space.
The next proposition is a generalization of
[

, Proposition 1.1.17]

the proof is almost verbatim the same, and we omit it.

Proposition A.3
.

Let
C
be a topological group.
Let
f:X\longrightarrow Y
be a morphism between orthogonal
C
-spaces
whose underlying orthogonal spaces are closed.
Then

is a
C
-global equivalence if and only if
for every continuous homomorphism
\alpha:G\longrightarrow C
from a compact Lie group, the map

f({\mathcal{U}}_{G})^{\Gamma(\alpha)}\ :\ X({\mathcal{U}}_{G})^{\Gamma(\alpha)}\ \longrightarrow\ Y({\mathcal{U}}_{G})^{\Gamma(\alpha)}

is a weak equivalence.

The basic building blocks of ‘classical’ global homotopy theory
(i.e., global homotopy theory without an external action
of any additional group) are the global classifying spaces of compact Lie groups.
For example, these global classifying spaces generate the
\infty
-category
of global spaces under colimits, and their suspension spectra
generated the global stable homotopy category
as a triangulated category
[
21
, Theorem 4.4.3]
.
In the orthogonal space model, the global classifying space
B_{\gl}G
of
a compact Lie group is introduced in
[
21
, Definition 1.1.27]
;
the name is motivated by the fact that
B_{\gl}G
globally classifies
G
-equivariant principal bundles,
compare
[
21
, Proposition 1.1.30]
.
The counterpart of
B_{\gl}G
in the world of topological stacks
is thus the stack of principal
G
-bundles.

Global classifying spaces also exist in
C
-global homotopy theory,
and we will introduce them now.
In the
C
-global context, these object are not associated to compact Lie groups,
but rather to continuous homomorphisms
\alpha:G\longrightarrow C
from compact Lie groups.
As in the ‘classical’ case, their global classifying spaces

are the basic building blocks of
C
-global homotopy theory.
A rigorous statement to this effect is our Theorem
A.17

below, saying that
C
-global stable homotopy category is compactly generated
by the suspension spectra of the global classifying spaces
of all continuous homomorphisms from compact Lie groups to
C
.

Construction A.4

(Global classifying spaces)
.

We let
C
be a topological group,
G
a compact Lie group, and
\alpha:G\longrightarrow C

a continuous homomorphism.
We let

be a faithful

-representation.
Then the assignment

B_{\gl}\alpha\ =\ C\times_{\alpha}\mathbf{L}(V,-)\ :\ \mathbf{L}\ \longrightarrow\ C{\mathbf{T}}

is an orthogonal

of

The value of

at an inner product space

is thus the
orbit

by

(c,\varphi)\cdot g\ =\ (c\alpha(g),\varphi\circ l_{g})\ ,

where

is left multiplication by the group element

When the group

is trivial,

a global classifying space

as defined in

[

The following Proposition

in particular shows that the

-global homotopy type of

the proposition generalizes
[

Given two inner product spaces

the restriction homomorphism of represented orthogonal spaces

\rho_{V,W}\ :\ \mathbf{L}(V\oplus W,-)\ \longrightarrow\ \mathbf{L}(V,-)

restricts a linear isometric embedding from

to the first summand.

Proposition A.5
.

Let
\alpha:G\longrightarrow C
be a continuous homomorphism
from a compact Lie group to a topological group.
Let
V
and
W
be
G
-representations such that
G
acts faithfully on
V
, and let

be a
G
-space.
Then the restriction morphism

C\times_{\alpha}(\rho_{V,W}\times A)\ :\ C\times_{\alpha}(\mathbf{L}(V\oplus W,-)\times A)\ \longrightarrow C\times_{\alpha}(\mathbf{L}(V,-)\times A)

is a
C
-global equivalence.

Proof.

We claim that the underlying orthogonal space of
C\times_{\alpha}(\mathbf{L}(V,-)\times A)
is closed;
the same is then also true for
C\times_{\alpha}(\mathbf{L}(V\oplus W,-)\times A)
.
Indeed, if
\varphi:U\longrightarrow U^{\prime}

is a linear isometric embedding, then the induced map of Stiefel manifolds

\mathbf{L}(V,\varphi):\mathbf{L}(V,U)\longrightarrow\mathbf{L}(V,U^{\prime})
is a closed embedding, hence so
is the map
C\times\mathbf{L}(V,\varphi)\times A
.
Taking orbits by a continuous action of a compact topological group
preserves closed embeddings, see
[
21
, Proposition B.13 (iii)]
,
so the map
C\times_{\alpha}(\mathbf{L}(V,\varphi)\times A)
is a closed embedding, too.

Now we let
K
be another compact Lie group, and we let
{\mathcal{U}}_{K}
a complete
K
-universe.
The map

\rho_{V,W}({\mathcal{U}}_{K})\ :\ \mathbf{L}(V\oplus W,{\mathcal{U}}_{K})\ \longrightarrow\ \mathbf{L}(V,{\mathcal{U}}_{K})

is a
(K\times G)
-homotopy equivalence by
[
21
, Proposition 1.1.26]
.
So the map

C\times_{\alpha}(\rho_{V,W}({\mathcal{U}}_{K})\times A)\ :\ C\times_{\alpha}(\mathbf{L}(V\oplus W,{\mathcal{U}}_{K})\times A)\ \longrightarrow\ C\times_{\alpha}(\mathbf{L}(V,{\mathcal{U}}_{K})\times A)

is a
(K\times C)
-homotopy equivalence.
Hence for every continuous homomorphism
\beta:K\longrightarrow C

the map of
\Gamma(\beta)
-fixed points

\left(C\times_{\alpha}(\rho_{V,W}({\mathcal{U}}_{K})\times A)\right)^{\Gamma(\beta)}

is a weak equivalence.
Proposition
A.3
then applies
to show that the morphism
C\times_{\alpha}(\rho_{V,W}\times A)
is a
C
-global equivalence.

We continue to let

-action by automorphisms

A

-equivariant morphism of orthogonal spectra.
If

For a compact Lie group

We emphasize that while we allow for actions of arbitrary topological groups,

We will now argue that the classes of
C
-global equivalences and h-cofibrations
make the category of orthogonal
C
-spectra into a
cofibration category
.
A cofibration category is a category equipped with two classes of morphisms,
the ‘weak equivalences’ and the ‘cofibrations’, that satisfy a specific list of axioms.
The original notion and the basic theory goes back to Brown
[
4
]

who had introduced the dual notion under the name ‘category of fibrant objects’.
After Brown, several authors have proposed and studied variations of the concept
that differ in details;
a nice summary and an extensive list of references can be found in the introduction
of
[
24
]
.
For our purposes, the formulation of Szumiło
is particularly convenient, and we will use the axioms as stated in

[
24
, Section 1]
.

The
homotopy category
of a cofibration category
{\mathcal{C}}
is any localization at the class
of weak equivalences, i.e., a functor
\gamma:{\mathcal{C}}\longrightarrow\Ho({\mathcal{C}})
that is initial among functors
from
{\mathcal{C}}
that take weak equivalences to isomorphisms.
We will often refer to the category
\Ho({\mathcal{C}})
alone as the homotopy category, leaving the
localization functor
\gamma
implicit.
The cofibration structure gives rise to an abstract notion of
homotopy and a ‘calculus of left fractions’, through which the homotopy category becomes manageable.
This calculus includes the following two facts, where ‘acyclic cofibration’
refers to a morphism that is simultaneously a cofibration and a weak equivalence.

(i)

Every morphism in
\Ho({\mathcal{C}})
is a fraction of the form
\gamma(s)^{-1}\circ\gamma(f)
,
where
f
and
s
are
{\mathcal{C}}
-morphisms with the same target, and
s
is an acyclic cofibration.

(ii)

Given two morphisms
f,g:A\longrightarrow B
in
{\mathcal{C}}
, then
\gamma(f)=\gamma(g)
in
\Ho({\mathcal{C}})

if and only if there is an acyclic cofibration
s:B\longrightarrow\bar{B}
such that
sf
and
sg
are homotopic.

A proof of these facts (or rather the dual statements for ‘categories of fibrant objects’)
can be found in
[
4
, I.2 Theorem I]
and Remark 2 immediately thereafter.

In our applications we will want to know that the
C
-global stable homotopy category
has arbitrary coproducts, and that these are modeled by wedges of orthogonal
C
-spectra.
This fact is a special case of a general property of cofibration categories
with well-behaved set-indexed coproducts, see the following proposition.
The proposition should not be surprising,
but I am not aware of a reference, so I include a proof.
For
finite
coproducts, the following proposition is a special case
of the statement dual to
[
5
, Corollaire 2.9]
.
Following
[
24
]
we call a cofibration category
cocomplete

if it has set-indexed coproducts, and if the classes of cofibrations and
acyclic cofibrations are stable under coproducts.

Proposition A.8
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a cocomplete cofibration category.
Then the localization functor
\gamma:{\mathcal{C}}\longrightarrow\Ho({\mathcal{C}})
preserves
coproducts. In particular, the homotopy category admits coproducts.

Proof.

Since the localization functor
\gamma
can be arranged to be the identity on objects,
we drop
\gamma

Now we consider an index set
I
,
and an
I

\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of
{\mathcal{C}}
-objects.
We denote a coproduct of the family by
\amalg_{i\in I}X_{i}

\kappa_{j}:X_{j}\longrightarrow\amalg_{i\in I}X_{i}
for the universal morphisms.
We must show that for every
{\mathcal{C}}

is bijective.
For surjectivity we let
(\psi_{j}:X_{j}\longrightarrow Y)
be any

\Ho({\mathcal{C}})

By the calculus of left fractions, we can write

\psi_{j}\ =\ \gamma(s_{j})^{-1}\circ\gamma(f_{j})

for some families of

and
s_{j}:Y\longrightarrow W_{j}

such that the morphisms

are acyclic cofibrations.
We choose a coproduct of the family
\{W_{i}\}_{i\in I}

and a coproduct of the constant family
\{Y\}_{i\in I}
of copies of

.
Then we form the
{\mathcal{C}}

\amalg_{i\in I}X_{i}\ \xrightarrow{\amalg f_{i}}\ \amalg_{i\in I}W_{i}\ \xleftarrow[\sim]{\amalg s_{i}}\ \amalg_{i\in I}Y\ \xrightarrow{\ \nabla\ }\ Y\ ,

where
\nabla

Since coproducts of acyclic cofibrations
are acyclic cofibrations, the middle morphism is an acyclic cofibration.
So we can form the morphism

\gamma(\nabla)\circ\gamma(\amalg s_{i})^{-1}\circ\gamma(\amalg f_{i})\ :\ \amalg_{i\in I}X_{i}\ \longrightarrow\ Y

The map (

so the map (

For injectivity we consider two morphisms

We start with the special case where

\psi^{\prime}=\gamma(f^{\prime})

Because

the calculus of left fractions provides acyclic cofibrations
t_{j}:Y\longrightarrow\bar{Y}_{j}

such that
t_{j}f\kappa_{j}:X_{i}\longrightarrow\bar{Y}_{j}
is homotopic to

t_{j}f^{\prime}\kappa_{j}:X_{j}\longrightarrow\bar{Y}_{j}
for every

.
We choose a pushout:

\textstyle{\amalg_{i\in I}Y\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\nabla}
\scriptstyle{\amalg t_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\sim}

Since coproducts of acyclic cofibrations are acyclic cofibrations,
the left vertical morphism is an acyclic cofibration, and hence so is
the right vertical morphism

For each

of

from

Definition A.21

Remark A.22

(Extended isometry group)
.

The
extended isometry group

\tilde{I}(W)
of a
{\mathbb{K}}
-inner product space
W

is the group of pairs
(A,\tau)
consisting of an
{\mathbb{R}}
-linear automorphism

A:W\longrightarrow W
and a Galois automorphism
\tau\in G({\mathbb{K}})
such that

•

the morphism
A
is
\tau
-semilinear, i.e.,

A(x\lambda)=A(x)\cdot\tau(\lambda)
for all
x\in W
and
\lambda\in{\mathbb{K}}
, and

•

the relation

(A.23)

[Ax,Ay]\ =\ \tau[x,y]

holds for all
x,y\in W
.

If
W
is non-zero, then the Galois automorphism
\tau
is actually determined by
A
;
nevertheless, it is convenient to explicitly keep track of
\tau
.
Composition in
\tilde{I}(W)
is componentwise, i.e,

(A,\tau)\cdot(A^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})=(AA^{\prime},\tau\tau^{\prime})
.
The relation (
A.23
)
in particular implies that for
(A,\tau)\in\tilde{I}(W)
,
the map

is an isometry of the underlying euclidean vector space

,
i.e., the underlying
{\mathbb{R}}
-vector space of

equipped with the
euclidean inner product
\langle x,y\rangle=\text{Re}[x,y]
.
So
\tilde{I}(W)
is a closed subgroup of
O(uW)\times G({\mathbb{K}})
,
and hence a compact Lie group.
The projection to the second factor
p:\tilde{I}(W)\longrightarrow G({\mathbb{K}})

is a continuous epimorphism whose kernel is isomorphic to
I(W)
.
Moreover,
\tilde{I}(W)
is isomorphic to a semidirect product

,
but not in a natural way.

The reader might want to convince themself that a
(G,\epsilon)
-representation
with underlying inner product space

can equivalently be specified by
a continuous homomorphism
\rho:G\longrightarrow\tilde{I}(W)

to the extended isometry group that covers the augmentations,
i.e., such that
p\circ\rho=\epsilon
.

Construction A.24

We let

\displaystyle\zeta\

Proposition A.26
.

Let
\epsilon:G\longrightarrow G({\mathbb{K}})
be a continuous homomorphism,
where
{\mathbb{K}}
is
{\mathbb{C}}
or
{\mathbb{H}}
. Let
W
be a
(G,\epsilon)
-representation.
Then the map
\zeta
is a
{\mathbb{K}}
-linear isometric embedding
that satisfies

(A.27)

\zeta\circ l_{g}\ =\ (l_{g}\otimes\epsilon(g))\circ\zeta

for all
g\in G
, where
l_{g}:W\longrightarrow W
is left multiplication by
g
.

Proof.

We give the argument for
{\mathbb{K}}={\mathbb{H}}
. The proof in the complex case is similar, but easier.
The map
\zeta
is clearly
{\mathbb{R}}
-linear, and it commutes with right multiplication
by the quaternion scalars
i
and
j
; so
\zeta
is in fact
{\mathbb{H}}
-linear.
To show that the map
\zeta
is isometric we observe that

\left[x\lambda\otimes\bar{\lambda},x\nu\otimes\bar{\nu}\right]=\ \lambda\cdot\langle x\lambda,x\nu\rangle\cdot\bar{\nu}\ =\ \lambda\cdot\text{Re}\left(\bar{\lambda}[x,x]\nu\right)\cdot\bar{\nu}\ =\ [x,x]\cdot\text{Re}\left(\bar{\lambda}\nu\right)\cdot\lambda\bar{\nu}

for all
x\in W
and
\lambda,\nu\in{\mathbb{H}}
.
For
\lambda,\nu\in\{1,i,j,k\}
with
\lambda\neq\nu
we have
\text{Re}\left(\bar{\lambda}\nu\right)=0
.
So

\displaystyle[\zeta(x),\zeta(x)]\ =\ 1/4\cdot[x,x]\cdot\sum_{\lambda\in\{1,i,j,k\}}\text{Re}(\bar{\lambda}\lambda)\cdot\lambda\bar{\lambda}\ =\ [x,x]\ .

It remains to prove the relation (
A.27
).
The tautological action of
G({\mathbb{H}})
on
{\mathbb{H}}
is isometric for the euclidean inner product

\langle x,y\rangle=\text{Re}(\bar{x}y)
.
Since
(1,i,j,k)
is an orthonormal
{\mathbb{R}}
-basis for this inner product,
the element
1\otimes 1+i\otimes i+j\otimes j+k\otimes k
is fixed
under the diagonal
G({\mathbb{H}})
-action (and could have been specified by any other orthonormal basis).
Since
1\otimes 1
is evidently
G({\mathbb{H}})
-fixed, too, the element

1\otimes 1-i\otimes i-j\otimes j-k\otimes k

is fixed under the diagonal
G({\mathbb{H}})
-action. So we conclude that

\displaystyle\zeta(gx)\

\displaystyle=\ 1/2\cdot(gx)\cdot(1\otimes 1-i\otimes i-j\otimes j-k\otimes k)

\displaystyle=\ 1/2\cdot(gx)\cdot(1\otimes 1-\epsilon(g)(i)\otimes\epsilon(g)(i)-\epsilon(g)(j)\otimes\epsilon(g)(j)-\epsilon(g)(k)\otimes\epsilon(g)(k))

\displaystyle=\ 1/2\cdot((gx)\otimes 1-g(xi)\otimes\epsilon(g)(i)-g(xj)\otimes\epsilon(g)(j)-g(xk)\otimes\epsilon(g)(k))\ =\ (l_{g}\otimes\epsilon(g))(\zeta(x))

for all
g\in G
and
x\in W
.
∎

A basic fact in the global homotopy theory of orthogonal spaces is that
for two compact Lie groups
K
and
G
, and for any faithful
G
-representation
V
,
the infinite Stiefel manifold
\mathbf{L}(V,{\mathcal{U}}_{K})
is a universal
(K\times G)
-space
for the family of graph subgroups, compare
[
21
, Proposition 1.1.26]
.
This property relies on the fact that for every finite-dimensional
K
-representation
W
,
the space
\mathbf{L}^{K}(W,{\mathcal{U}}_{K})
of
K
-equivariant
{\mathbb{R}}
-linear isometric embeddings is contractible, compare

[
21
, Propositions 1.1.21]
.
Both statement have suitable analogs in the complex and quaternionic situations,
with easily adapted proofs.
Since I do not know of references,
I provide proper statements and proofs now.

Proposition A.28
.

Let
{\mathcal{U}}_{K}
be a complete universe of a compact Lie group
K
.
Let
\beta:K\longrightarrow G({\mathbb{K}})
be a continuous homomorphism,
where
{\mathbb{K}}
is either
{\mathbb{C}}
or
{\mathbb{H}}
.
Let
K
act on
{\mathcal{U}}_{K}^{\mathbb{K}}={\mathcal{U}}_{K}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathbb{K}}
by

k\cdot(x\otimes\lambda)\ =\ (kx)\otimes\beta(k)(\lambda)\ .

Then for every
(K,\beta)
-representation
W
, the space

\mathbf{L}^{{\mathbb{K}},K}(W,{\mathcal{U}}_{K}^{\mathbb{K}})
of
{\mathbb{K}}
-linear
K
-equivariant isometric embeddings
is weakly contractible.

Proof.

We let
U
be any
(K,\beta)
-representation, possibly of countably infinite dimension.
Then the homotopy

H\ :\ [0,1]\times\mathbf{L}^{{\mathbb{K}},K}(W,U)\ \longrightarrow\ \mathbf{L}^{{\mathbb{K}},K}(W,U\oplus W)\ ,\quad H(t,\varphi)(w)\ =\ (\sqrt{1-t^{2}}\cdot\varphi(w),\,t\cdot w)

witnesses the fact that the map

i_{1}\circ-\ :\ \mathbf{L}^{{\mathbb{K}},K}(W,U)\ \longrightarrow\ \mathbf{L}^{{\mathbb{K}},K}(W,U\oplus W)

(post-composition with
i_{1}:U\longrightarrow U\oplus W)
is homotopic to a constant map.
The space

\mathbf{L}^{{\mathbb{K}},K}(W,U\oplus W^{\infty})\ =\ \colim_{n\geq 0}\,\mathbf{L}^{{\mathbb{K}},K}(W,U\oplus W^{n})

is the colimit along the post-composition maps with the direct sum embeddings

U\oplus W^{n}\longrightarrow U\oplus W^{n+1}
.
Every map in the colimit system is homotopic
to a constant map, by the previous paragraph.
Since the maps are also closed embeddings, the colimit is weakly contractible.

Now we can prove the proposition.
Because
{\mathcal{U}}_{K}
is a complete
K
-universe, there is a
K
-equivariant
{\mathbb{R}}
-linear isometry

{\mathcal{U}}_{K}\cong{\mathcal{V}}\oplus(uW)^{\infty}
for some orthogonal
K
-representation
{\mathcal{V}}

(typically infinite dimensional).
The
{\mathbb{K}}
-linear
K
-equivariant isometric embedding
\zeta:W\longrightarrow(uW)\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathbb{K}}

provides an isomorphism of
(K,\beta)
-representations

(uW)\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathbb{K}}\ \cong\ C\oplus W\ ,

where
C
is the orthogonal complement of the image of
\zeta
.
So
{\mathcal{U}}_{K}^{\mathbb{K}}
is isomorphic to

\left({\mathcal{V}}\oplus(uW)^{\infty}\right)\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathbb{K}}\ \cong\ ({\mathcal{V}}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathbb{K}})\oplus C^{\infty}\oplus W^{\infty}\ .

The space
\mathbf{L}^{{\mathbb{K}},K}(W,{\mathcal{U}}_{K}^{\mathbb{K}})
is thus weakly contractible by the first paragraph.
∎

Proposition A.31
.

Let
{\mathbb{K}}
be
{\mathbb{C}}
or
{\mathbb{H}}
.
Let
\epsilon:G\longrightarrow G({\mathbb{K}})
be a continuous epimorphism from a compact Lie group
with kernel
G_{0}
.
Let
W
be a faithful
(G,\epsilon)
-representation.
Then for every continuous homomorphism
\beta:K\longrightarrow G({\mathbb{K}})
from another compact Lie group
and for every complete
K
-universe
{\mathcal{U}}_{K}
, the
(K\times_{G({\mathbb{K}})}G)
-space

\mathbf{L}^{\mathbb{K}}(W,{\mathcal{U}}_{K}^{\mathbb{K}})
is a universal
(K\times_{G({\mathbb{K}})}G)
-space for the family of those
closed subgroups that intersect

trivially.

Proof.

Essentially the same argument as in the real situation in
[
21
, Proposition 1.1.19]
,
which relies on Illman’s equivariant triangulation theorem
for smooth actions of compact Lie groups
[
10
]
,
shows that
\mathbf{L}^{\mathbb{K}}(W,{\mathcal{U}}_{K}^{\mathbb{K}})
is cofibrant as a
(K\times_{G({\mathbb{K}})}G)
-space.
Since
G_{0}
acts faithfully on
W
, the action on
\mathbf{L}^{\mathbb{K}}(W,{\mathcal{U}}_{K}^{\mathbb{K}})

by precomposition is free.
So every subgroup of
K\times_{G({\mathbb{K}})}G

with fixed points on
\mathbf{L}^{\mathbb{K}}(W,{\mathcal{U}}_{K}^{\mathbb{K}})

must intersect the group

trivially.
Conversely, we now consider a closed subgroup
\Delta
of

such that

.
Then
\Delta
is the graph of some continuous homomorphism

\alpha\ :\ L\ \longrightarrow\ G

defined on some closed subgroup

of
K
, such that
\epsilon\circ\alpha=\beta|_{L}
.
Hence

\left(\mathbf{L}^{\mathbb{K}}(W,{\mathcal{U}}_{K}^{\mathbb{K}})\right)^{\Delta}\ =\ \mathbf{L}^{{\mathbb{K}},L}(\alpha^{*}(W),{\mathcal{U}}_{K}^{\mathbb{K}})\ ,

the space of
{\mathbb{K}}
-linear and
L
-equivariant isometric embeddings from
\alpha^{*}(W)

into
{\mathcal{U}}_{K}^{\mathbb{K}}
,
with
L

l\cdot(x\otimes\lambda)\ =\ (lx)\otimes\beta(l)(\lambda)\ .

Because
{\mathcal{U}}_{K}
is a complete
K
-universe,
the underlying
L
-representation is a complete
L
-universe. So the space

\mathbf{L}^{{\mathbb{K}},L}(\alpha^{*}(W),{\mathcal{U}}_{K}^{\mathbb{K}})
is weakly contractible by Proposition
A.28
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem A.33
.

\epsilon:G\longrightarrow G({\mathbb{K}})
be a continuous epimorphism from a compact Lie group,
where
{\mathbb{K}}
is either
{\mathbb{C}}
or
{\mathbb{H}}
.

be a faithful

\zeta^{\flat}\times_{G_{0}}Z

(ii)

The morphism of orthogonal

\Sigma^{\infty}\zeta^{\flat}_{+}\wedge_{G_{0}}Z^{\diamond}\ :\ \Sigma^{\infty}\mathbf{L}(uW,-)_{+}\wedge_{G_{0}}Z^{\diamond}\ \longrightarrow\ \Sigma^{\infty}\mathbf{L}^{\mathbb{K}}(W,(-)_{\mathbb{K}})_{+}\wedge_{G_{0}}Z^{\diamond}

Proof.

(i) We consider a continuous homomorphism

\mathbf{L}^{\mathbb{K}}(W,{\mathcal{U}}_{K}^{\mathbb{K}})

(K\times_{G({\mathbb{K}})}G)

A.31

(ii) The same argument to compare the long exact homotopy group sequences of

Appendix B
Some linear algebra

The purpose of this appendix is to provide detailed proofs of the linear algebra facts used in
the main part of this paper.
We set things up so that everything works simultaneously over the fields
{\mathbb{R}}
and
{\mathbb{C}}
and over
the skew-field
{\mathbb{H}}
of quaternions.
Linear algebra over the quaternions comes with some additional caveats,
many due to the non-commutativity of the multiplication.
So I felt the need to justify that the relevant arguments can indeed be adapted
to the quaternion situation.
I make absolutely no claim to originality for anything in this appendix.

Throughout this appendix, we will let
{\mathbb{K}}
denote one of the three skew-fields
{\mathbb{R}}
,
{\mathbb{C}}
or
{\mathbb{H}}
.
A
{\mathbb{K}}
-vector space is a right
{\mathbb{K}}
-module.
Given two
{\mathbb{K}}
-vector spaces
V
and
W
, we write

\Hom_{\mathbb{K}}(V,W)
for the
{\mathbb{R}}
-vector space of right
{\mathbb{K}}
-linear maps.
For
{\mathbb{K}}={\mathbb{C}}
one can make
\Hom_{\mathbb{C}}(V,W)
a
{\mathbb{C}}
-vector space
by pointwise scalar multiplication, but we will not use this structure.
Due to the non-commutativity of the quaternions, for
{\mathbb{K}}={\mathbb{H}}

there is no natural way to endow
\Hom_{\mathbb{H}}(V,W)
with an
{\mathbb{H}}
-action.

A
{\mathbb{K}}
-inner product space
is a finite-dimensional
{\mathbb{K}}
-vector space
equipped with a sesquilinear, hermitian and positive-definite
{\mathbb{K}}
-valued inner product
[-,-]
,
see Definition
2.2
.
An example of an inner product space is
{\mathbb{K}}^{k}
with the standard inner product

[x,y]\ =\ \bar{x}_{1}\cdot y_{1}+\dots+\bar{x}_{k}\cdot y_{k}\ .

Every
{\mathbb{K}}
-inner product space
W
admits
an orthonormal basis
(w_{1},\dots,w_{k})
, i.e., such that

[w_{i},w_{j}]\ =\ \begin{cases}1&\text{ for $i=j$, and}\\
0&\text{ for $i\neq j$.}\end{cases}

The familiar argument from real and complex linear algebra also works over the
quaternions: we choose a non-zero vector
w\in W

and normalize it to
w_{1}=w/|w|
; the orthogonal complement

W^{\perp}=\{v\in W\ :\ [v,w]=0\}
is then a
{\mathbb{K}}
-subspace that has an orthonormal basis by induction
over the dimension.
A choice of orthonormal basis of
W
provides a
{\mathbb{K}}
-linear isometry
{\mathbb{K}}^{k}\cong W
,
where
k=\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(W)
.
So up to
{\mathbb{K}}
-linear isometry, the standard examples
{\mathbb{K}}^{k}
are the only examples
of
{\mathbb{K}}
-inner product spaces.

Remark B.1

(Adjoints)
.

Because inner products are positive-definite, they are in particular
non-degenerate, i.e., they provide an identification with the dual vector space.
Given a
{\mathbb{K}}
-vector space, we write

W^{\vee}\ =\ \Hom_{{\mathbb{K}}}(W,{\mathbb{K}})

for the
{\mathbb{R}}
-vector space of right
{\mathbb{K}}
-linear maps.
We give
W^{\vee}
a right
{\mathbb{K}}
-action by

(f\cdot\lambda)(w)\ =\ \bar{\lambda}\cdot f(w)

for
f\in W^{\vee}
,
\lambda\in{\mathbb{K}}
and
w\in W
. If
W
is finite-dimensional and

[-,-]
is a
{\mathbb{K}}
-inner product on
W
, then the map

W\ \longrightarrow\ W^{\vee}\ ,\quad w\ \longmapsto\ [w,-]

is a
{\mathbb{K}}
-linear isomorphism.

Now we let
X:W\longrightarrow V
be a
{\mathbb{K}}
-linear map between
{\mathbb{K}}
-inner product spaces.
The
adjoint
of
X
is the
{\mathbb{K}}
-linear map
X^{*}:V\longrightarrow W
that makes the following square commute:

\textstyle{V\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{X^{*}}
\scriptstyle{v\mapsto[v,-]}
\scriptstyle{\cong}
\textstyle{W\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{w\mapsto[w,-]}
\scriptstyle{\cong}
\textstyle{V^{\vee}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{X^{\vee}}
\textstyle{W^{\vee}}

So the adjoint is characterized by the relation

[X^{*}v,w]\ =\ [v,Xw]

for all
v\in V
and
w\in W
. Passage to the adjoint is
{\mathbb{R}}
-linear, contravariantly functorial,
and involutive.

Given two
{\mathbb{K}}
-inner product spaces
V
and
W
, we write
\mathbf{L}^{\mathbb{K}}(V,W)

for the Stiefel manifold of
{\mathbb{K}}
-linear isometric embeddings,
i.e., right
{\mathbb{K}}
-linear maps

that satisfy

[Av,Av^{\prime}]=[v,v^{\prime}]

for all
v,v^{\prime}\in V
. An equivalent condition is to demand that
A^{*}\cdot A=\Id_{V}
.
In the special case
V=W
, we also write

I(W)\ =\ \mathbf{L}^{\mathbb{K}}(W,W)

and refer to this as the
isometry group
of
W
.
The traditional names for the isometry group are of course
the orthogonal group
O(W)
in the case
{\mathbb{K}}={\mathbb{R}}
,
the unitary group
U(W)
in the case
{\mathbb{K}}={\mathbb{C}}
,
and the symplectic group
Sp(W)
in the case
{\mathbb{K}}={\mathbb{H}}
.

Remark B.2

(Adjoint representation)
.

We let
W
be a finite-dimensional
{\mathbb{K}}
-vector space. The
exponential map

(B.3)

\exp\ :\ \End_{\mathbb{K}}(W)\ \longrightarrow\ GL_{\mathbb{K}}(W)\text{\qquad is given by\qquad}\exp(X)\ =\ \sum_{k\geq 0}X^{k}/k!\ .

If the endomorphisms
X
and
Y
commute, then
\exp(X+Y)=\exp(X)\cdot\exp(Y)
.
For a
{\mathbb{K}}
-inner product space
W
, we write

\mathfrak{ad}(W)\ =\ \{X\in\End_{\mathbb{K}}(W)\ :\ X^{*}=-X\}

for the
{\mathbb{R}}
-vector space of its skew-adjoint endomorphisms.
Since
(X^{*})^{k}=(X^{k})^{*}
for all
k\geq 0
, also
\exp(X^{*})=\exp(X)^{*}
.
So if
X\in\mathfrak{ad}(W)
is skew-adjoint, then

\exp(X)^{*}\cdot\exp(X)\ =\ \exp(X^{*})\cdot\exp(X)\ =\ \exp(-X)\cdot\exp(X)\ =\ \exp(0)\ =\ \Id_{W}\ .

In other words, the exponential map restricts to a smooth map

(B.4)

\exp\ :\ \mathfrak{ad}(W)\ \longrightarrow\ I(W)

from the skew-adjoint endomorphisms to the isometry group.
This map is in fact a local diffeomorphism around the origin,
and it exhibits
\mathfrak{ad}(W)

as the adjoint representation of the compact Lie group
I(W)
,
whence the notation.

¨

Due to the non-commutativity of the quaternions, the notions of ‘eigenvalues’ and ‘eigenvectors’
of
{\mathbb{H}}
-linear endomorphisms are somewhat problematic.
Indeed, if
X:W\longrightarrow W
is an
{\mathbb{H}}
-linear endomorphism
and
\lambda\in{\mathbb{H}}
a scalar, then the ‘eigenspace’

\{w\in W\ :\ Xw=w\lambda\}

is an
{\mathbb{R}}
-subspace of
W
, but typically
not
closed under multiplication by scalars from
{\mathbb{H}}
.
If the scalar
\lambda
is real, and hence central in
{\mathbb{H}}
, then the issue disappears and
the above is an
{\mathbb{H}}
-subspace of
W
.
So in order to deal with eigenspaces in a uniform way for
{\mathbb{R}}
,
{\mathbb{C}}
and
{\mathbb{H}}
,
we should – and will – restrict to
real
eigenvalues.

Proposition B.5
.

Let
X
be a self-adjoint endomorphism of a
{\mathbb{K}}
-inner product space
W
,
i.e.,
X^{*}=X
. Then
X
is diagonalizable with real eigenvalues and pairwise orthogonal eigenspaces.

Proof.

The argument over the real and complex numbers can be found in many text books on linear algebra.
So we restrict to the less common case
{\mathbb{K}}={\mathbb{H}}
of the quaternions.
We argue over the dimension of
W
; the induction starts with
W=0
, where there is nothing to show.
Now we suppose that
W\neq 0
.
We treat
X
as a
{\mathbb{C}}
-linear endomorphism of the underlying
{\mathbb{C}}
-vector space
of
W
. There is then an eigenvector

and a complex scalar
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
such that

Xw=w\lambda
.
Because
X
is self-adjoint we deduce that

[w,w]\lambda\ =\ [w,w\lambda]\ =\ [w,Xw]\ =\ [Xw,w]\ =\ [w\lambda,w]\ =\ \bar{\lambda}[w,w]\ .

Since
w\neq 0
, the inner product
[w,w]
is a non-zero real number,
so we must have
\lambda=\bar{\lambda}
, i.e., the complex scalar
\lambda
is in fact real.
Now we observe that the orthogonal complement
W^{\perp}

of

is invariant under
X

we have

[Xv,w]\ =\ [v,Xw]\ =\ [v,w\lambda]\ =\ [v,w]\lambda\ =\ 0\ .

We can thus apply the inductive hypothesis to the restricted endomorphism
X|_{W^{\perp}}:W^{\perp}\longrightarrow W^{\perp}
.
Since this restriction is diagonalizable
with real eigenvalues and pairwise orthogonal eigenspaces,
the same is true for the original endomorphism
W
.
∎

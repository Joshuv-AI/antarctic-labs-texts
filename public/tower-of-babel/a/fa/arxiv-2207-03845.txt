Abstract
We present the currently most precise predictions for the W W -boson mass and the leptonic effective mixing angle in the aligned Two-Higgs-Doublet Model. The evaluation includes the full one-loop result, all known higher-order corrections of the Standard Model, and the non-standard two-loop contributions that increase with mass splittings between charged and neutral THDM Higgs bosons. They depend on tan ⁡ β \tan\beta and the soft Z 2 Z_{2} -symmetry breaking parameter m 12 2 m_{12}^{2} of the scalar potential, in addition to the non-standard boson masses. Whenever the one-loop corrections become large, the two-loop contributions yield substantial modifications of the predictions, which is of particular importance for the W W mass where large mass shifts are required to reach the recently published final result of the CDF collaboration. Numerical results are shown for the dependence on the various non-standard parameters and in comparison with experimental data.

The Two-Higgs-Doublet model

The Higgs sector of the THDM contains two
SU(2)_{L}
doublets
of complex scalar fields with hypercharge
Y=1
,

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\phi_{1}^{+}\\
\phi_{1}^{0}\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle\Phi_{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\phi_{2}^{+}\\
\phi_{2}^{0}\end{array}\right).

Under the assumption of a discrete
Z_{2}
symmetry
(
\Phi_{1}\to\Phi_{1},\,\Phi_{2}\to-\Phi_{2}
)
which is only softly broken by a non-diagonal mass term,
the general gauge invariant potential is given by
(see e.g.
[
91
]
)

\displaystyle V\left(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2}\right)=

\displaystyle m_{11}^{2}\left(\Phi_{1}{}^{\dagger}\Phi_{1}\right)+m_{22}^{2}\left(\Phi_{2}{}^{\dagger}\Phi_{2}\right)-m_{12}^{2}\left(\left(\Phi_{1}{}^{\dagger}\Phi_{2}\right)+\left(\Phi_{2}{}^{\dagger}\Phi_{1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\Lambda_{1}\left(\Phi_{1}{}^{\dagger}\Phi_{1}\right){}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\Lambda_{2}\left(\Phi_{2}{}^{\dagger}\Phi_{2}\right){}^{2}+\Lambda_{3}\left(\Phi_{2}{}^{\dagger}\Phi_{2}\right)\left(\Phi_{1}{}^{\dagger}\Phi_{1}\right)

\displaystyle+\Lambda_{4}\left(\Phi_{1}{}^{\dagger}\Phi_{2}\right)\left(\Phi_{2}{}^{\dagger}\Phi_{1}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\Lambda_{5}\left(\left(\Phi_{1}{}^{\dagger}\Phi_{2}\right){}^{2}+\left(\Phi_{2}{}^{\dagger}\Phi_{1}\right){}^{2}\right).

(10)

Assuming conserved
CP
symmetry in the Higgs sector,
all parameters in the potential are chosen to be real.
The minimum of the potential corresponds to the vacuum field configurations

\langle\Phi_{i}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
v_{i}\end{pmatrix},

(11)

and the minimum conditions for
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
read as follows,

\displaystyle m_{11}^{2}=

\displaystyle m_{12}^{2}\,t_{\beta}-\frac{1}{2}v^{2}c_{\beta}^{2}\left(\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{345}t_{\beta}^{2}\right),

\displaystyle m_{22}^{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{m_{12}^{2}}{t_{\beta}}-\frac{1}{2}v^{2}s_{\beta}^{2}\left(\Lambda_{2}+\frac{\Lambda_{345}}{t_{\beta}^{2}}\right),

(12)

with

v^{2}=v_{1}^{2}+v_{2}^{2},\qquad t_{\beta}\equiv\tan\beta=\frac{v_{2}}{v_{1}},\qquad\Lambda_{345}=\Lambda_{3}+\Lambda_{4}+\Lambda_{5}.

(13)

Expanding the scalar fields around the vacuum expectation values,

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\phi_{1}^{+}\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(v_{1}+\eta_{1}+i\chi_{1}\right)\end{array}\right),\quad\Phi_{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\phi_{2}^{+}\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(v_{2}+\eta_{2}+i\chi_{2}\right)\end{array}\right),

yields the mass terms quadratic in the fields,
which are diagonalized by unitary transformations

\begin{pmatrix}G^{\pm}\\
H^{\pm}\end{pmatrix}=\mathbf{R}\left(\beta\right)\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{1}^{\pm}\\
\phi_{2}^{\pm}\\
\end{pmatrix},\quad\begin{pmatrix}G^{0}\\
A^{0}\end{pmatrix}=\mathbf{R}\left(\beta\right)\begin{pmatrix}\chi_{1}\\
\chi_{2}\\
\end{pmatrix},\quad\begin{pmatrix}H^{0}\\
h^{0}\end{pmatrix}=\mathbf{R}\left(\alpha\right)\begin{pmatrix}\eta_{1}\\
\eta_{2}\end{pmatrix},

(19)

with

\mathbf{R}\left(x\right)=\begin{pmatrix}\cos x&\sin x\\
-\sin x&\cos x\\
\end{pmatrix}\,\equiv\,\begin{pmatrix}c_{x}&s_{x}\\
-s_{x}&c_{x}\\
\end{pmatrix},

(20)

in order to disentangle the Goldstone bosons
G^{0}
and
G^{\pm}
from the
physical mass eigenstates: two
CP
-even states
h^{0}
and
H^{0}
,
one
CP
-odd state
A^{0}
, and a pair
H^{\pm}
of charged Higgs bosons.

The gauge-boson masses are determined by
v
and the
electroweak gauge couplings
g_{1},g_{2}

M_{W}^{2}=\frac{1}{4}g_{2}^{2}v^{2},\quad M_{Z}^{2}=\frac{1}{4}\big(g_{1}^{2}+g_{2}^{2}\big)v^{2}\,,

(21)

and provide the doublet-specific relation to the
electroweak mixing angle,

\displaystyle\cos^{2}\theta_{W}

\displaystyle=c_{W}^{2}=\frac{M_{W}^{2}}{M_{Z}^{2}},

\displaystyle\sin^{2}\theta_{W}

\displaystyle=s_{W}^{2}=1-c_{W}^{2}.

(22)

Whereas the combination
v^{2}=v_{1}^{2}+v_{2}^{2}
is fixed by the
vector boson masses, there are seven
other free parameters of the Higgs potential, which
can be expressed in terms of the Higgs boson masses,
the mixing angles
\alpha
and
\beta

and one remaining independent parameter
(see
[
91
]
for the general relations).
Conventionally the soft
Z_{2}
-breaking mass parameter

is chosen.
In our previous calculation of the two-loop contributions
to the
\rho
-parameter
[
90
]
,
we used the dimensionless quantity

\lambda_{5}=\frac{2\,m_{12}^{2}}{v^{2}\,c_{\beta}s_{\beta}}

(23)

from the parameterisation given in
[
92
]
instead, which
is employed in the
FeynArts
model-file of the THDM. For
consistency we present the results of the
W
boson mass and the effective
leptonic mixing angle also using the combination

in (

).

Furthermore, two additional assumptions have been made in
[
90
]
,
which are adopted also for the present study.
First the
CP
-even state

is identified with the

Second, the alignment limit
[

is applied where

identical to the corresponding couplings of the SM Higgs boson.
Formally this can be achieved by setting

\alpha=\beta-\frac{\pi}{2}.

(24)

Flavour-changing neutral currents (FCNC) through neutral Higgs exchange at the

This has lead to four different model classes in the literature

2.1
The Inert-Higgs-Doublet model

A particular interesting version of the THDM is the
Inert-Higgs-Doublet-Model (IHDM)
[
97
]

which is distinguished by an unbroken
Z_{2}
symmetry.
Under this symmetry all the SM fields and the doublet
\Phi_{1}

are even, whereas
\Phi_{2}
is odd.
The IHDM has received attention in the context of radiative
neutrino masses
[
98
]
or as a solution to the naturalness
problem
[
99
]
. Moreover, since the
Z_{2}
symmetry
is unbroken, the lightest particle that is oddly charged can provide a
dark matter candidate
[
100
]
.
An overview with more details on phenomenology and additional references
can be found in
[
101
]
.

The potential of the IHDM is given by (
10
) setting

m_{12}^{2}=0
.
The scalar doublets appear in the following form,

\Phi_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}G^{\pm}\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(v+h^{0}+iG^{0}\right)\end{pmatrix},\quad\Phi_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}H^{\pm}\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(H^{0}+iA^{0}\right)\end{pmatrix}.

(25)

Only
\Phi_{1}
has a non-vanishing vacuum expectation value
v
,
related to the parameters of (
10
) via the minimum condition,

v^{2}=-\frac{2\,m_{11}^{2}}{\Lambda_{1}}.

(26)

With
h^{0}=H_{\text{SM}}
and the Goldstone bosons
G^{0},G^{\pm}
, the
doublet
\Phi_{1}
is SM-like.
The second doublet
\Phi_{2}
, the inert doublet, contains instead the non-standard
Higgs fields
H^{0}
,
A^{0}
and
H^{\pm}
.
The four physical masses are related to the parameters of the potential
in the following way,

\displaystyle m_{h^{0}}^{2}

\displaystyle=-2m_{11}^{2}=\Lambda_{1}v^{2},

\displaystyle m_{H^{0}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\mu_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\Lambda_{3}+\Lambda_{4}+\Lambda_{5}\right)v^{2},

\displaystyle m_{A^{0}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\mu_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\Lambda_{3}+\Lambda_{4}-\Lambda_{5}\right)v^{2},

\displaystyle m_{H^{\pm}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\mu_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\Lambda_{3}v^{2}.

(27)

The couplings of the scalar
h^{0}
to fermions and gauge bosons are
identical to the corresponding couplings of the Higgs boson in the SM.
Owing to the
Z_{2}
symmetry, the additional particles
H^{0}
,
A^{0}
and

H^{\pm}
do not couple to fermions. Moreover, they can appear only
pairwise in interaction vertices and the lightest of these
non-standard particles will be stable.
If this is one of the neutral bosons, the IHDM provides a suitable dark matter candidate.

In order to specify the free parameters of the IHDM,
it is convenient to choose, besides
m_{h^{0}}=M_{H_{\rm SM}}
,
the masses
m_{H^{0}}
,
m_{A^{0}}
,
m_{H^{\pm}}
,
the quartic coupling
\Lambda_{2}
of the non-standard scalars,
and the quantity

\Lambda_{345}=\Lambda_{3}+\Lambda_{4}+\Lambda_{5},

(28)

which is of special interest since
\Lambda_{345}
describes the coupling
of the standard-like Higgs particle to a potential dark matter candidate.

2.2
Theoretical constraints

The parameters of the potential given in (
10
) are subject to
various restrictions. A stable vacuum requires the potential to be bounded
from below. In the THDM this requirement has to be fulfilled for all possible
directions along which the component fields of
\Phi_{1,2}
go to large values.
As explained in
[
97
,
102
,
103
]

the conditions

\displaystyle\Lambda_{1}

\displaystyle>0,

\displaystyle\Lambda_{2}

\displaystyle>0,

\displaystyle\Lambda_{3}+\sqrt{\Lambda_{1}\Lambda_{2}}

\displaystyle>0,

\displaystyle\Lambda_{3}+\Lambda_{4}-|\Lambda_{5}|

\displaystyle>-\sqrt{\Lambda_{1}\Lambda_{2}},

(29)

ensure that the quartic terms in the potential are positive for large values
of the field components in all directions.
These tree-level bounds can be improved by considering higher-order
corrections to the potential.
For more details see the discussion in
[
96
]
and
references therein. For our analysis we employ the tree-level bounds
as an estimate for the allowed parameter range.

The unitarity requirement for the scattering matrix
puts additional constraints on the parameters of the Higgs potential.
Due to the optical theorem, the
s
-wave scattering length
a_{0}
is restricted
to
|a_{0}|\leq 1/2
. For scattering processes with four scalars in the high-energy
limit,
a_{0}
is directly proportional to the scalar couplings.
Moreover, due to the Goldstone boson equivalence theorem the scattering
of longitudinal gauge bosons can be calculated as scalar–scalar
scattering by replacing the gauge boson with the corresponding
Goldstone bosons. In the SM, the constraints from tree-level
unitarity gives an upper bound on the Higgs mass

[
105
,
106
]
. The application of the analysis in the
THDM

[
107
,
108
,
109
,
110
,
111
,
112
,
113
]

is more complicated due to the larger number of possible scattering
processes and the involved structure of the scalar quartic
couplings. With the help of a unitary tranformation, the scattering
matrix of the coupled scalar–scalar channels can be simplified by
using the original fields
\phi^{+}_{i}
,
\eta_{i}
and
\chi_{i}
from
(
2
) instead of the mass eigenstates.
The restrictions on the
s
-wave scattering length constrain the eigenvalues
of the scattering matrix at tree-level (see for example
[
96
]
)

\displaystyle e_{1,2}=

\displaystyle\frac{3}{2}\left(\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{2}\right)\pm\sqrt{\frac{9}{4}\left(\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2}\right){}^{2}+\left(2\Lambda_{3}+\Lambda_{4}\right){}^{2}},

\displaystyle e_{3,4}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{2}\right)\pm\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\left(\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2}\right){}^{2}+4\Lambda_{4}^{2}},

\displaystyle e_{5,6}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{2}\right)\pm\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\left(\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2}\right){}^{2}+4\Lambda_{5}^{2}},

\displaystyle e_{7}=

\displaystyle\Lambda_{3}+2\Lambda_{4}-3\Lambda_{5},

\displaystyle e_{8}=

\displaystyle\Lambda_{3}-\Lambda_{5},

\displaystyle e_{9}=

\displaystyle\Lambda_{3}+2\Lambda_{4}+3\Lambda_{5},

\displaystyle e_{10}=

\displaystyle\Lambda_{3}+\Lambda_{5},

\displaystyle e_{11}=

\displaystyle\Lambda_{3}+\Lambda_{4},

\displaystyle e_{12}=

\displaystyle\Lambda_{3}-\Lambda_{4},

(30)

to fulfill
|e_{i}|\leq 8\pi
. We are employing these tree-level bounds
as an estimate of the validity of perturbativity.
For more accurate restrictions higher-order corrections have to be
considered for the scattering processes.
A one-loop analysis of the unitarity bounds can be found in
[
114

115

The constraints from vacuum stability and

are identical in the aligned THDM and the IHDM.
In the IHDM the parameters are further constrained by the condition

\frac{m_{11}^{2}}{\sqrt{\Lambda_{1}}}\leq\frac{\mu_{2}^{2}}{\sqrt{\Lambda_{2}}}

to ensure that

3
The
M_{W}
-
M_{Z}
interdependence

The correlation between the gauge boson masses
M_{W}
and
M_{Z}

can be established via the Fermi constant
G_{F}
,
which is determined with high accuracy from precise measurements of
the muon lifetime and
the calculation of the muon decay width within the low-energy effective
Fermi model including QED corrections up to
{\cal O}(\alpha^{2})
for the point-like
interactions
[
117
,
118
,
119
,
120
,
121
,
122
]
.
Comparison of the muon-decay amplitude calculated in electroweak theories
like the SM or THDM with the Fermi model result yields the relation

\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}=\frac{\pi\alpha}{2M_{W}^{2}\big(1-\frac{M_{W}^{2}}{M_{Z}^{2}}\big)}\left(1+\Delta r\right),

(32)

where the non-QED loop corrections are summarized in the quantity
\Delta r
.
Since it depends on all the virtual particles in the loop contributions,

\Delta r=\Delta r\left(M_{W},M_{Z},m_{t},\dots\right)

(33)

is a model-dependent quantity, and the relation (
32
)
provides the prediction of
M_{W}
in specific models
in terms of the model parameters and the highly accurate
input quantitites
M_{Z},G_{F}
and the electromagnetic
fine-structure constant
\alpha
.

3.1
One-loop calculations

At the one-loop level, the contributions to
\Delta r

consist of the
W
self-energy, vertex and box diagrams, and the
related counterterms.
In the on-shell renormalization scheme, the required counterterms
arise from charge and mass renormalization
(see for example
[
123
,
124
]
),

\displaystyle e_{0}=

\displaystyle Z_{e}e=(1+\delta^{\text{(1)}}\!Z_{e})e,

\displaystyle M^{2}_{W,0}

\displaystyle=M_{W}^{2}+\delta^{(1)}\!M_{W}^{2},

\displaystyle M^{2}_{Z,0}

\displaystyle=M_{Z}^{2}+\delta^{(1)}\!M_{Z}^{2},

(34)

according to

\displaystyle\delta Z_{e}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Pi^{\gamma}_{\text{}}\left(0\right)+\frac{s_{W}}{c_{W}}\frac{\Sigma^{(1)}_{\gamma Z}\left(0\right)}{M_{Z}^{2}},

\displaystyle\delta^{(1)}\!M_{W}^{2}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Re}\Sigma^{(1)}_{W}\left(M_{W}^{2}\right),

\displaystyle\delta^{(1)}\!M_{Z}^{2}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Re}\Sigma^{(1)}_{Z}\left(M_{Z}^{2}\right),

(35)

with the transverse part
\Sigma_{V}
of the gauge-boson self-energies,
the photon vacuum polarization

\Pi^{\gamma}_{\text{}}\left(k^{2}\right)=\frac{\Sigma^{(1)}_{\gamma}\left(k^{2}\right)}{k^{2}},

(36)

and the non-diagonal photon–
Z
mixing self-energy.
In the on-shell scheme the electroweak mixing angle is a derived quantity,

s_{W,0}^{2}=1-c_{W,0}^{2}\,=\,1-\frac{M_{W,0}^{2}}{M_{Z,0}^{2}}\,=\,s_{W}^{2}+\delta^{(1)}s_{W}^{2}+\cdots

(37)

yielding

s_{W}^{2}=1-\frac{M_{W}^{2}}{M_{Z}^{2}},\qquad c_{W}^{2}=1-s_{W}^{2}\,,

(38)

and the one-loop counterterm

\frac{\delta^{(1)}s_{W}^{2}}{s_{W}^{2}}=-\frac{c_{W}^{2}}{s_{W}^{2}}\frac{\delta^{(1)}c_{W}^{2}}{c_{W}^{2}}=\frac{c_{W}^{2}}{s_{W}^{2}}\left(\frac{\delta^{(1)}\!M_{Z}^{2}}{M_{Z}^{2}}-\frac{\delta^{(1)}\!M_{W}^{2}}{M_{W}^{2}}\right).

(39)

With the notation
\delta_{\text{vertex+box}}
for the vertex and box diagram contributions
including external wave-function renormalization, one can write

\displaystyle\Delta r^{\text{(1)}}_{\text{}}=

\displaystyle 2\,\delta^{\text{(1)}}\!Z_{e}+\frac{\Sigma^{(1)}_{W}\left(0\right)-\delta^{(1)}\!M_{W}^{2}}{M_{W}^{2}}-\frac{c_{W}^{2}}{s_{W}^{2}}\left(\frac{\delta^{(1)}\!M_{Z}^{2}}{M_{Z}^{2}}-\frac{\delta^{(1)}\!M_{W}^{2}}{M_{W}^{2}}\right)+\delta_{\text{vertex+box}}\,.

(40)

At the one-loop level,
\Delta r
can be split

\Delta r^{\text{(1)}}_{\text{}}=\Delta\alpha-\frac{c_{W}^{2}}{s_{W}^{2}}\Delta\rho_{\text{}}^{\text{(1)}}+\Delta r^{\text{(1)}}_{\text{rem}}

(41)

into three parts, containing

•

the shift of the fine-structure constant
\Delta\alpha
from charge renormalization, originating
from the light-fermion contribution to the photon vacuum polarization;

•

the loop correction
\Delta\rho_{\text{}}^{\text{(1)}}
to the
\rho
-parameter, which and can be written as

\Delta\rho_{\text{}}^{\text{(1)}}=\frac{\Sigma^{(1)}_{Z}\left(0\right)}{M_{Z}^{2}}-\frac{\Sigma^{(1)}_{W}\left(0\right)}{M_{W}^{2}}\,;

(42)

•

the remainder part
\Delta r^{\text{(1)}}_{\text{rem}}
summarizing
all the other terms.

The loop correction to the
\rho
-parameter is sensitive to the mass splitting between the partners
within an isospin doublet
[
125
]
.
In the SM, this yields a sizeable contribution from the top–bottom quark
doublet
[
125
,
126
,
127
]
.
In the THDM, it can moreover get large contributions from the non-standard Higgs bosons
in case of mass splittings between neutral and charged scalars,
yielding the dominant non-standard loop corrections in
\Delta r
.

In the alignment limit of the THDM, one can identify the loop contributions
of the scalars
h^{0}
,
G^{0}
,
G^{\pm}

with the standard scalar contributions to the SM part of
\Delta r
.
Consequently, the non-standard contributions to
\Delta r
arise
from the scalars
H^{0}
,
A^{0}
, and
H^{\pm}
.
Thus, one can write at the one-loop level

\displaystyle\Delta r^{\text{(1)}}_{\text{}}=\Delta r^{\text{(1)}}_{\text{SM}}+\Delta r^{\text{(1)}}_{\text{NS}}\,.

(43)

Since the scalar contributions to the
vertex and box corrections are negligible
due to the small Yukawa couplings to electron and muon,
the non-standard scalars contribute to
\Delta r
only
through the gauge boson self-energies, yielding
the non-standard part

\displaystyle\Delta r^{\text{(1)}}_{\text{NS}}=\Pi^{\gamma}_{\text{NS}}\left(0\right)+\frac{\Sigma^{(1)}_{W,\text{NS}}\left(0\right)-\delta^{(1)}\!M_{W,\text{NS}}^{2}}{M_{W}^{2}}-\frac{c_{W}^{2}}{s_{W}^{2}}\left(\frac{\delta^{(1)}\!M_{Z,\text{NS}}^{2}}{M_{Z}^{2}}-\frac{\delta^{(1)}\!M_{W,\text{NS}}^{2}}{M_{W}^{2}}\right).

(44)

The subindex NS indicates that only the non-standard parts
of the self-energies and counterterms, respectively, have to be taken.
The corresponding expressions are listed in the Appendix.
The dominant effect on
\Delta r
, as noted above, can be traced back
to the additional non-standard correction to the
\rho
-parameter,
entering (
41
) with

\displaystyle\Delta\rho_{\text{NS}}^{\text{(1)}}=\frac{\alpha}{16\pi s_{W}^{2}M_{W}^{2}}\Bigg\{

\displaystyle\frac{m_{A^{0}}^{2}m_{H^{0}}^{2}}{m_{A^{0}}^{2}-m_{H^{0}}^{2}}\log\left(\frac{m_{A^{0}}^{2}}{m_{H^{0}}^{2}}\right)-\frac{m_{A^{0}}^{2}m_{H^{\pm}}^{2}}{m_{A^{0}}^{2}-m_{H^{\pm}}^{2}}\log\left(\frac{m_{A^{0}}^{2}}{m_{H^{\pm}}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{m_{H^{0}}^{2}m_{H^{\pm}}^{2}}{m_{H^{0}}^{2}-m_{H^{\pm}}^{2}}\log\left(\frac{m_{H^{0}}^{2}}{m_{H^{\pm}}^{2}}\right)+m_{H^{\pm}}^{2}\Bigg\}.

(45)

The origin of
\Delta\rho_{\text{NS}}^{\text{(1)}}
are the couplings of the non-standard Higgs
sector when they violate the custodial symmetry.

\Delta\rho_{\text{NS}}^{\text{(1)}}
vanishes for

m_{H^{0}}=m_{H^{\pm}}
or
m_{A^{0}}=m_{H^{\pm}}
.

3.3
Two-loop corrections in the THDM

The THDM prediction for
\Delta r
beyond the one-loop level can be decomposed
according to

\Delta r=\Delta r_{\text{SM}}+\Delta r_{\text{NS}},

(47)

where
\Delta r_{\text{SM}}
contains all the known SM corrections
mentioned above and

\Delta r_{\text{NS}}
comprises the additional non-standard
contribution, which in the alignment case originates from the
non-standard bosons
H^{0}
,
A^{0}
and
H^{\pm}
.
The expansion up to two-loop order,

\Delta r_{\text{NS}}=\Delta r^{\text{(1)}}_{\text{NS}}+\Delta r^{\text{(2)}}_{\text{NS}}\,,

(48)

accommodates the complete non-standard one-loop part
\Delta r^{\text{(1)}}_{\text{NS}}

from (
44
)
and the two-loop part
\Delta r^{\text{(2)}}_{\text{NS}}
which we approximate by
including the potentially large terms associated with the
\rho
-parameter.
These contain products of
\Delta\rho_{\text{t}}^{\text{(1)}},\Delta\rho_{\text{NS}}^{\text{(1)}},\,\Delta\alpha

as well as
the non-standard two-loop irreducible corrections to the
\rho
-parameter
calculated in
[
90
]
.
Technically, they are obtained in the gauge-less limit
(vanishing gauge couplings
g_{1},g_{2}
while keeping the ratio

M_{W}/M_{Z}
constant) and in the top-Yukawa approximation
where only the top-quark mass is kept different from zero.
This yields a significant step of improvement at the two-loop level taking into account
those contributions that can become sizeable whenever the
one-loop contribution in
\Delta r^{\text{}}_{\text{}}
is large.

In analogy to the SM, the non-standard two-loop contribution can be
written in terms of a one-particle irreducible part
\Delta r^{\text{(2)}}_{\text{NS,irr}}

and a reducible part
\Delta r^{\text{(2)}}_{\text{NS,red}}
,

\Delta r^{\text{(2)}}_{\text{NS}}=\Delta r^{\text{(2)}}_{\text{NS,red}}+\Delta r^{\text{(2)}}_{\text{NS,irr}}.

(49)

The reducible non-standard contribution,
\Delta r^{\text{(2)}}_{\text{NS,red}}
, arises from
the non-standard one-loop correction to the
\rho
-parameter (
45
)
in the expansion of
\Delta r^{\text{}}_{\text{}}
up to two-loop order, in the on-shell scheme
given by

\Delta r=\Delta\alpha+\Delta\alpha^{2}-\frac{c_{W}^{2}}{s_{W}^{2}}\Delta\rho_{\text{}}^{\text{(1)}}\left(1+2\Delta\alpha-2\frac{c_{W}^{2}}{s_{W}^{2}}\Delta\rho_{\text{}}^{\text{(1)}}\right)+\dots

(50)

with

\Delta\rho_{\text{}}^{\text{(1)}}=\Delta\rho_{\text{t}}^{\text{(1)}}+\Delta\rho_{\text{NS}}^{\text{(1)}}\,,\quad\Delta\rho_{\text{t}}^{\text{(1)}}=\frac{3\,\alpha}{16\pi s_{W}^{2}}\frac{m_{t}^{2}}{M_{W}^{2}}\,.

(51)

The two-loop terms of
\Delta r^{\text{}}_{\text{}}
which contain only
\Delta\alpha
and
\Delta\rho_{\text{t}}^{\text{(1)}}

are already included in the SM part;
therefore, the non-standard part remains as follows,

\Delta r^{\text{(2)}}_{\text{NS,red}}=-2\frac{c_{W}^{2}}{s_{W}^{2}}\,\Delta\alpha\,\Delta\rho_{\text{NS}}^{\text{(1)}}+4\frac{c_{W}^{4}}{s_{W}^{4}}\,\Delta\rho_{\text{NS}}^{\text{(1)}}\,\Delta\rho_{\text{t}}^{\text{(1)}}+2\frac{c_{W}^{4}}{s_{W}^{4}}\left(\Delta\rho_{\text{NS}}^{\text{(1)}}\right)^{2}\,.

(52)

As a side note, we mention that a reparametrization of

\Delta\rho_{\text{}}^{\text{(1)}}\to\Delta\overline{\rho}^{(1)}

replacing the basic on-shell parameters in the normalization
of
\Delta\rho_{\text{}}^{\text{(1)}}
by the Fermi constant
with the help of

\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}=\frac{\pi\alpha}{2s_{W}^{2}M_{W}^{2}}\left(1+\Delta r^{(1)}+\dots\right)

(53)

induces additional factorized two-loop terms in
\Delta r^{\text{}}_{\text{}}

that allow to rewrite (
50
) as follows,

\Delta r=\Delta\alpha+\Delta\alpha^{2}-\frac{c_{W}^{2}}{s_{W}^{2}}\Delta\overline{\rho}_{\text{}}^{\text{(1)}}\left(1+\Delta\alpha-\frac{c_{W}^{2}}{s_{W}^{2}}\,\Delta\overline{\rho}_{\text{}}^{\text{(1)}}\right)+\dots

(54)

which corresponds to the expansion of the resummed form

1+\Delta r^{\text{}}_{\text{}}\,\to\,\frac{1}{1-\Delta\alpha}\cdot\frac{1}{1+\frac{c_{W}^{2}}{s_{W}^{2}}\Delta\overline{\rho}}

(55)

found in
[
14
]
for the SM.

The irreducible non-standard two-loop contribution
\Delta r^{\text{(2)}}_{\text{NS,irr}}

originates from the
W
self-energy
and the counterterms for the parameters in (
32
).
In our approximation, the two-loop charge renormalization constant is zero
and the two-loop self-energy
\Sigma^{(2)}_{W}\left(0\right)
is canceled by the two-loop

W
mass counterterm.
Hence, the only remaining quantity is the two-loop counterterm for
s_{W}^{2}
,
obtained from expanding the bare relation (
37
),

\frac{\delta^{(2)}s_{W}^{2}}{c_{W}^{2}}\,=\,-\,\frac{\delta^{(1)}\!M_{Z}^{2}}{M_{Z}^{2}}\left(\frac{\delta^{(1)}\!M_{Z}^{2}}{M_{Z}^{2}}-\frac{\delta^{(1)}\!M_{W}^{2}}{M_{W}^{2}}\right)\,+\,\frac{\delta^{(2)}\!M_{Z}^{2}}{M_{Z}^{2}}-\frac{\delta^{(2)}\!M_{W}^{2}}{M_{W}^{2}}\,,

(56)

which in the gauge-less limit reduces to

\frac{\delta^{(2)}s_{W}^{2}}{c_{W}^{2}}\,=\,-\,\frac{\Sigma^{(1)}_{Z}\left(0\right)}{M_{Z}^{2}}\Delta\rho_{\text{}}^{\text{(1)}}\,+\,\frac{\Sigma^{(2)}_{Z}\left(0\right)}{M_{Z}^{2}}-\frac{\Sigma^{(2)}_{W}\left(0\right)}{M_{W}^{2}}\,=\,\Delta\rho_{\text{}}^{\text{(2)}}\,,

(57)

where
\Delta\rho^{(2)}

is the two-loop correction to the
\rho
parameter as derived in
[
90
]
.

The
W,Z
self-energies
\Sigma^{(2)}_{W,Z}
correspond to the set of
genuine two-loop diagrams and diagrams with subloop renormalization,
i.e. one-loop diagrams with insertion of mass countertems and of
\delta^{(1)}s_{W}^{2}
.
The two-loop terms with
\delta^{(1)}s_{W}^{2}
factorize and can be extracted;
in combination with the factorized first term in (
57
)
one obtains

\Delta\rho^{(2)}=-\frac{c_{W}^{2}}{s_{W}^{2}}\left(\Delta\rho_{\text{}}^{\text{(1)}}\right)^{2}+\delta\rho_{\text{}}^{\text{(2)}}\,.

(58)

The residual part
\delta\rho_{\text{}}^{\text{(2)}}
contains the genuine two-loop self-energies
combined with the part of the subloop renormalization from the mass counterterms
of the internal particles.
The quadratic term in
\Delta\rho_{\text{}}^{\text{(1)}}
from (
58
)
is already included in
\Delta r^{\text{2}}_{\text{NS,red}}

(in the
G_{F}
-parametrization of
\Delta\rho
it would vanish).
The irreducible two-loop part of
\Delta r
is thus given by

\Delta r^{\text{(2)}}_{\text{irr}}\,=\,-\frac{c_{W}^{2}}{s_{W}^{2}}\,\delta\rho_{\text{}}^{\text{(2)}}\,,

(59)

it still contains the SM contribution.
In the notation of
[
90
]
,
\delta\rho_{\text{}}^{\text{(2)}}
is divided into four finite parts

\delta\rho^{(2)}=\delta\rho^{(2)}_{\rm t,SM}+\delta\rho^{(2)}_{\rm t,NS}+\delta\rho^{(2)}_{\rm H,NS}+\delta\rho^{(2)}_{\rm H,Mix},

(60)

where
\delta\rho_{\text{t,SM}}^{\text{(2)}}
contains the SM-like scalars
h^{0}
,
G^{0}
,
G^{\pm}

and the top-Yukawa coupling, whereas the non-standard scalars

H^{0}
,
A^{0}
,
H^{\pm}
appear in the residual three parts.

Separating off the SM contribution
\delta\rho_{\text{t,SM}}^{\text{(2)}}
in (
59
),
which is already part of
\Delta r_{\rm SM}
,
one obtains the non-standard contribution as follows,

\displaystyle\Delta r^{\text{(2)}}_{\text{NS,irr}}

\displaystyle=\,\Delta r^{\text{(2)}}_{\text{t,NS}}+\Delta r^{\text{(2)}}_{\text{H,NS}}+\Delta r^{\text{(2)}}_{\text{H,Mix}}

\displaystyle=\,-\frac{c_{W}^{2}}{s_{W}^{2}}\,\big(\delta\rho_{\text{t,NS}}^{\text{(2)}}+\delta\rho_{\text{H,NS}}^{\text{(2)}}+\delta\rho_{\text{H,Mix}}^{\text{(2)}}\big).

(61)

The various entries of
\Delta r
have the following properties.

•

\Delta r^{\text{(2)}}_{\text{t,NS}}
incorporates the coupling of the top quark to the non-standard scalars,
which is proportional to
t_{\beta}^{-1}
and enters all the diagrams for this contribution quadratically.

•

\Delta r^{\text{(2)}}_{\text{H,NS}}
incorporates the non-standard scalar interactions.
It is proportional to the squared coupling between three non-standard scalars, which has the form

\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{t_{\beta}}-t_{\beta}\right)\frac{\left(2m_{H^{0}}^{2}-\lambda_{5}v^{2}\right)}{v}

(62)

in the alignment limit. Consequently,
\Delta r^{\text{(2)}}_{\text{H,NS}}
vanishes for

t_{\beta}=1\quad{\rm or}\quad\lambda_{5}=\frac{2m_{H^{0}}^{2}}{v^{2}}.

(63)

Moreover, it is zero for
m_{A^{0}}=m_{H^{\pm}}
,
since this mass configuration restores the custodial symmetry
of the scalar potential.
However, differently from
\Delta\rho_{\text{NS}}^{\text{(1)}}
,
this contribution does not vanish for
m_{H^{0}}=m_{H^{\pm}}
(if
m_{A^{0}}\neq m_{H^{\pm}}
).

•

\Delta r^{\text{(2)}}_{\text{H,Mix}}
incorporates
the interaction between the standard and the non-standard scalars.
Similarly to
\Delta\rho_{\text{NS}}^{\text{(1)}}
,
it vanishes for
m_{H^{0}}=m_{H^{\pm}}
or
m_{A^{0}}=m_{H^{\pm}}
due to the restoration of
the custodial symmetry.
Differently from
\Delta\rho_{\text{NS}}^{\text{(1)}}
,
this contribution contains additional couplings between
h^{0}
and the non-standard scalars of the form

\frac{m_{h^{0}}^{2}+2m_{S}^{2}-\lambda_{5}v^{2}}{v^{2}},\quad(S=H^{0},A^{0},H^{\pm}),

(64)

which can be enhanced by
\lambda_{5}
.
In contrast to
\Delta r^{\text{(2)}}_{\text{t,NS}}
and
\Delta r^{\text{(2)}}_{\text{H,NS}}
,
the contribution
\Delta r^{\text{(2)}}_{\text{H,Mix}}

does not depend on
t_{\beta}
.

3.4
Loop corrections in the IHDM

The non-standard one-loop corrections to the gauge-boson self-energies
are the same in the IHDM and the general THDM in the alignment limit,
yielding
\Delta r^{\text{(1)}}_{\text{\text{NS}}}
for both cases which depends only on
the masses of the
H^{0},A^{0},H^{\pm}

Differences arise at the two-loop level.
Since Yukawa interactions of the non-standard scalar doublet
are absent, there is no
\Delta r^{\text{(2)}}_{\text{t,NS}}
term in the IHDM.
Moreover, as discussed in
[
90
]
,
the only non-standard two-loop contribution to the
\rho

\delta\rho_{\text{IHDM}}^{\text{(2)}}
, follows from the interaction of
h^{0}
,
G^{0}
,
G^{\pm}
with

H^{0}
,
A^{0}
,
H^{\pm}
and is thus equivalent to
\delta\rho_{\text{H,Mix}}^{\text{(2)}}
;
it vanishes for equal charged and non-standard neutral Higgs-boson masses
and contains the coupling between
h^{0}
and the non-standard scalars,
which is determined by the combination
\Lambda_{345}

of coefficients in the scalar potential, as specified in (
28
)
of Section
2.1
.
The corresponding contribution to
\Delta r
is given by

\Delta r^{\text{(2)}}_{\text{\text{IHDM,irr}}}=\frac{c_{W}^{2}}{s_{W}^{2}}\delta\rho_{\text{IHDM}}^{\text{(2)}}\,,

(65)

which introduces an additional dependence on the IHDM parameter
\Lambda_{345}
,
which is not present in
\Delta r^{\text{(1)}}_{\text{NS}}
and
\Delta r^{\text{(2)}}_{\text{NS,red}}
.

3.5
Incorporation of the higher-order corrections

For an accurate prediction of
M_{W}
in the THDM we have
to combine all the by now available loop contributions
from the SM and beyond in the quantity
\Delta r^{\text{}}_{\text{}}
, which
depends on the SM input and on all the free non-standard
parameters of the THDM,

\Delta r(M_{W},\dots)=\Delta r_{\rm SM}(M_{W},\dots)+\Delta r_{\rm NS}(M_{W},\dots)

(66)

The SM part,
\Delta r_{\rm SM}
,
contains, besides the one-loop result, the complete two-loop
and the partial higher-order contributions listed in (
46
).
Since the two-loop electroweak part
is quite an inconvenient expression
which involves furthermore numerical integrations,
we make use of the parametrization given in
[
43
]

in terms of the SM input parameters.

Summarizing the various standard and non-standard contributions to
\Delta r

we write (
66
) as follows,

\displaystyle\Delta r

\displaystyle\,=\,\Delta r_{\text{SM}}+\Delta r^{\text{(1)}}_{\text{NS}}+\Delta r^{\text{(2)}}_{\text{NS,red}}+\Delta r^{\text{(2)}}_{\text{NS,irr}}

(67)

where the three non-standard terms are specified in the
equations (
44
),
(
52
), and (
61
).
In case of the IHDM,
\Delta r^{\text{(2)}}_{\text{NS,irr}}

has to replaced by
\Delta r^{\text{(2)}}_{\text{IHDM,irr}}
in (
65
).

The predicted value of
M_{W}
fulfills the
relation (
32
) for a given specific set of parameters.
Since
\Delta r
itself does depend on
M_{W}
, the solution
of (
32
) for
M_{W}
has to be determined numerically.

4
The effective electroweak mixing angle

The electroweak mixing angle in the effective leptonic vertex of the
Z
boson

[
35
]

is another important precision observable, measured with high accuracy at the

Z
resonance by the LEP and SLC experiments
[
8
]
.
Theoretically it is derived from the ratio of the dressed leptonic vector and axial vector
couplings
g_{V,A}
of the
Z
boson,

\sin^{2}\theta_{\text{eff}}\,\equiv\,s^{2}_{l}\,=\,\frac{1}{4}\left(1-{\rm Re}\frac{g_{V}}{g_{A}}\right),

(68)

keeping the notation
s^{2}_{l}
as a short term.
The effective couplings

\displaystyle g_{V}

\displaystyle=v+\Delta g_{V},\qquad g_{A}=a+\Delta g_{\!A},

(69)

contain the lowest-order leptonic
Z
couplings

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}+2s_{W}^{2},\qquad a=-\frac{1}{2},

(70)

and the corresponding loop contributions

\Delta g_{V,A}
evaluated at the
Z
-mass scale
(without the QED corrections associated with virtual photons).

It is convenient to relate
s^{2}_{l}
to the on-shell quantity
s_{W}^{2}

in (
38
) by a factor
\kappa
wich incorporates the
loop corrections in terms of
\Delta\kappa
,

s_{l}^{2}\,=\,s_{W}^{2}\,\kappa=s_{W}^{2}\left(1+\Delta\kappa\right).

(71)

Analogously to
\Delta r
in (
33
),

\Delta\kappa=\Delta\kappa(M_{W},M_{Z},m_{t},\dots)

is a model-dependent quantity, depending on all parameters of
the standard and non-standard particles which enter the loop contributions.
An additional model dependence in the prediction of
s^{2}_{l}

occurs via
s_{W}^{2}
through the
W
mass obtained by means of
\Delta r
,
as described in the previous Section
3
.

4.1
One-loop calculations

Loop corrections lead to deviations
\Delta\kappa

from the relation
\kappa=1
.
The expansion of (
68
) yields the
one-loop contribution

\Delta\kappa_{\text{}}^{\text{(1)}}=\,-\frac{1}{4s_{W}^{2}}\,\frac{v}{a}\left(\frac{\Delta g_{V}^{(1)}}{v}-\frac{\Delta g_{A}^{(1)}}{a}\right),

(72)

which can be written as follows,

\Delta\kappa_{\text{}}^{\text{(1)}}=\,\frac{c_{W}}{s_{W}}\,\frac{\operatorname{Re}\Sigma^{(1)}_{\gamma Z}\left(M_{Z}^{2}\right)}{M_{Z}^{2}}\,+\,\frac{\delta^{(1)}s_{W}^{2}}{s_{W}^{2}}\,+\,\frac{v}{v-a}\left(\frac{F_{V}\left(M_{Z}^{2}\right)}{v}-\frac{F_{A}\left(M_{Z}^{2}\right)}{a}\right).

(73)

The first two terms originate from photon–
Z
mixing and from
renormalization of the on-shell weak mixing angle (
39
)
The corrections from the vertex diagrams and the wave-function renormalization
of the external fermions are collected in the vector and axial vector
form factors
F_{V,A}
, with lepton masses neglected.

In the alignment limit of the THDM, one can identify the one-loop contribution
of the scalars
h^{0}
,
G^{0}
,
G^{\pm}

with the standard scalar contribution to the SM part of
\Delta\kappa
.
Consequently, the non-standard contribution to
\Delta\kappa
arise
from the scalars
H^{0}
,
A^{0}
, and
H^{\pm}
.
Thus, one can write at the one-loop level

\displaystyle\Delta\kappa_{\text{}}^{\text{(1)}}=\Delta\kappa_{\text{SM}}^{\text{(1)}}+\Delta\kappa_{\text{NS}}^{\text{(1)}}\,,

(74)

with the SM part

\Delta\kappa_{\text{SM}}^{\text{(1)}}=\,\frac{c_{W}}{s_{W}}\,\frac{\operatorname{Re}\Sigma^{(1)}_{\gamma Z,\text{SM}}\left(M_{Z}^{2}\right)}{M_{Z}^{2}}\,+\,\frac{\delta^{(1)}s_{W,\text{SM}}^{2}}{s_{W}^{2}}\,+\,\frac{v}{v-a}\left(\frac{F_{V,\text{SM}}}{v}-\frac{F_{A,\text{SM}}}{a}\right),

(75)

where the subindex indicates that only the standard particles are kept
in the one-loop self-energy and vertex corrections.

The contributions from the non-standard scalars
to the vertex corrections and to the lepton self-energies
are suppressed owing to the small Yukawa couplings.
Accordingly, we can neglect the non-standard contributions to the
form factors
F_{V,A}
.
The one-loop non-standard contribution to
\Delta\kappa
is thus given by

\Delta\kappa_{\text{NS}}^{\text{(1)}}=\,\frac{c_{W}}{s_{W}}\,\frac{\operatorname{Re}\Sigma^{(1)}_{\gamma Z,\text{NS}}\left(M_{Z}^{2}\right)}{M_{Z}^{2}}\,+\,\frac{\delta^{(1)}s^{2}_{W,\text{NS}}}{s_{W}^{2}}\,,

(76)

and is obtained via the non-standard content of the vector-boson self-energies
from Appendix
A
.

A sizeable part of the one-loop contribution to
\Delta\kappa
is associated
with
\Delta\rho^{(1)}
, Eq. (
42
), via

\Delta\kappa_{\text{}}^{\text{(1)}}=\frac{c^{2}_{W}}{s^{2}_{W}}\,\Delta\rho^{(1)}\,+\,\cdots

(77)

involving the non-standard term (
45
),
which becomes large in case of a large mass splitting in the
non-standard scalar spectrum.

4.3
Two-loop corrections in the THDM

The one-loop corrections can be supplemented by the leading reducible
and irreducible two-loop contributions, containing
products of
\Delta\alpha
and
\Delta\rho_{\text{}}^{\text{(1)}}
and the
irreducible two-loop correction
\delta\rho_{\text{}}^{\text{(2)}}
to
\rho
,
see Eq. (
60
).
The resulting two-loop contribution to
\Delta\kappa
is given by

\displaystyle\Delta\kappa^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\Delta\alpha\,\frac{c_{W}^{2}}{s_{W}^{2}}\Delta\rho_{\text{}}^{\text{(1)}}-\frac{c_{W}^{4}}{s_{W}^{4}}\left(\Delta\rho_{\text{}}^{\text{(1)}}\right)^{2}+\frac{c_{W}^{2}}{s_{W}^{2}}\,\delta\rho_{\text{}}^{\text{(2)}}

(78)

As a side-remark, we note that the appearance of the reducible products
in
\Delta\kappa
is again a consequence
of the on-shell parameters in the normalization of
\Delta\rho
.
After a reparametrization
\Delta\rho_{\text{}}^{\text{(1)}}\to\Delta\overline{\rho}^{(1)}

by the Fermi constant with the help of
Eqs. (
53
)
and (
41
)
in the expression

\Delta\kappa_{\text{}}^{\text{(1)}}=\frac{c_{W}^{2}}{s_{W}^{2}}\,\Delta\rho_{\text{}}^{\text{(1)}}+\dots

(79)

the resulting two-loop shift cancels the reducible term in (
78
)
and we obtain

\Delta\overline{\kappa}^{(2)}=\frac{c_{W}^{2}}{s_{W}^{2}}\,\delta\rho^{(2)}

(80)

as the two-loop correction to
\Delta\kappa
.

Next we have to identify the non-standard two-loop corrections.
Separating
\Delta\rho_{\text{t}}^{\text{(1)}}
and
\Delta\rho_{\text{NS}}^{\text{(1)}}

in the reducible term of (
78
) yields

\displaystyle\Delta\alpha\,\frac{c_{W}^{2}}{s_{W}^{2}}\Delta\rho_{\text{}}^{\text{(1)}}-\frac{c_{W}^{4}}{s_{W}^{4}}\left(\Delta\rho^{(1)}\right)^{2}\,=\;\;

\displaystyle\Delta\alpha\,\frac{c_{W}^{2}}{s_{W}^{2}}\left(\Delta\rho_{\text{t}}^{\text{(1)}}+\Delta\rho_{\text{NS}}^{\text{(1)}}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{c_{W}^{4}}{s_{W}^{4}}\left(\left(\Delta\rho_{\text{t}}^{\text{(1)}}\right)^{2}+2\,\Delta\rho_{\text{t}}^{\text{(1)}}\Delta\rho_{\text{NS}}^{\text{(1)}}+\left(\Delta\rho_{\text{NS}}^{\text{(1)}}\right)^{2}\right).

(81)

Since the terms without
\Delta\rho_{\text{NS}}^{\text{(1)}}
are already incorporated in

\Delta\kappa_{\text{SM}}
, we retain the non-standard reducible part

\Delta\kappa_{\text{NS,red}}^{\text{(2)}}\,=\,\Delta\alpha\,\frac{c_{W}^{2}}{s_{W}^{2}}\Delta\rho_{\text{NS}}^{\text{(1)}}-\frac{c_{W}^{4}}{s_{W}^{4}}\left(2\,\Delta\rho_{\text{t}}^{\text{(1)}}\Delta\rho_{\text{NS}}^{\text{(1)}}+\left(\Delta\rho_{\text{NS}}^{\text{(1)}}\right)^{2}\right).

(82)

The irreducible part
\delta\rho^{(2)}
can be separated into the different non-standard
contributions according to (
60
), which leads to the following
contributions to
\Delta\kappa
,

•

contribution from the non-standard top-Yukawa couplings

\Delta\kappa_{\text{t,NS}}^{\text{(2)}}=\frac{c_{W}^{2}}{s_{W}^{2}}\,\delta\rho_{\text{t,NS}}^{\text{(2)}},

(83)

•

contribution from the exclusively non-standard scalar corrections

\Delta\kappa_{\text{H,NS}}^{\text{(2)}}=\frac{c_{W}^{2}}{s_{W}^{2}}\,\delta\rho_{\text{H,NS}}^{\text{(2)}},

(84)

•

contribution from the mixed standard–non-standard scalar corrections

\Delta\kappa_{\text{H,Mix}}^{\text{(2)}}=\frac{c_{W}^{2}}{s_{W}^{2}}\,\delta\rho_{\text{H,Mix}}^{\text{(2)}}.

(85)

In the IHDM, the only irreducible non-standard two-loop contribution
to the
\rho
parameter is
\delta\rho_{\text{IHDM}}^{\text{(2)}}

(which is equivalent to
\delta\rho_{\text{H,Mix}}^{\text{(2)}}
), hence

\Delta\kappa_{\text{IHDM,irr}}^{\text{(2)}}=\frac{c_{W}^{2}}{s_{W}^{2}}\,\delta\rho_{\text{IHDM}}^{\text{(2)}}.

(86)

With these specifications, the two-loop irreducible contribution
to
\Delta\kappa
is given by

\Delta\kappa_{\text{NS,irr}}^{\text{(2)}}=\Delta\kappa_{\text{t,NS}}^{\text{(2)}}+\Delta\kappa_{\text{H,NS}}^{\text{(2)}}+\Delta\kappa_{\text{H,Mix}}^{\text{(2)}}

(87)

in the aligned THDM, and

\Delta\kappa_{\text{NS,irr}}^{\text{(2)}}=\Delta\kappa_{\text{IHDM,irr}}^{\text{(2)}}

(88)

in the IHDM.

4.4
Incorporation of the higher-order corrections

For an accurate prediction of
s^{2}_{l}
in the THDM we have
to combine all the by now available loop contributions
from the SM and beyond in the quantity
\Delta\kappa
, which
depends on the SM input quantitites and on the non-standard
parameters of the THDM,

\Delta\kappa(M_{W},\dots)=\Delta\kappa_{\text{SM}}(M_{W},\dots)+\Delta\kappa_{\text{NS}}(M_{W},\dots)

(89)

For a given set of parameters, the predicted value of
s^{2}_{l}

fulfills the relation (
71
)
with the corresponding
M_{W}
calculated from (
32
).
Since the
W
mass varies with the model parameters,

\Delta\kappa
is needed as an expression where
M_{W}
appears as
an independent variable

In the following we write only
M_{W}
as an explicit variable in
\Delta\kappa
,
dropping the ellipsis. The SM part,

\displaystyle\Delta\kappa_{\text{SM}}(M_{W})

\displaystyle=\,\Delta\kappa^{(1)}_{\text{ SM}}(M_{W})+\Delta\kappa^{\text{(ho)}}_{\text{SM}}(M_{W})

(90)

contains besides the one-loop result the complete two-loop
and the partial higher-order contributions listed above.
Since the full SM result is an inconvenient expression,
we make use of the parametrization for the SM prediction of
s^{2}_{l}

given in
[
43
]
for the set of SM input parameters

M_{Z},m_{t},m_{h^{0}},\alpha_{s}
, yielding the value
s^{2}_{l,\text{SM}}
.
By inverting (
71
) one obtains

\Delta\kappa_{\text{SM}}(M_{W,\text{SM}})\,=\,s_{l,\text{SM}}^{2}\,\left(1-\frac{M_{W,\text{SM}}^{2}}{M_{Z}^{2}}\right)^{-1}\,-\,1\,,

(91)

while
M_{W}
is simultaneously fixed by the standard value
M_{W,\text{SM}}

via (
32
) with
\Delta r_{\text{SM}}
.

Since the
W
mass varies with the non-standard model parameters, we need

\Delta\kappa
as an expression where
M_{W}
appears as an
independent variable.
For the one-loop correction of
\Delta\kappa
in the SM we use the explicit result
(
75
) with
M_{W}
as one of the input quantities.
From (
91
) we obtain

\Delta\kappa^{\text{(ho)}}_{\text{SM}}(M_{W,\text{SM}})=\Delta\kappa_{\text{SM}}(M_{W,\text{SM}})-\Delta\kappa_{\text{SM}}^{\text{(1)}}\left(M_{W,\text{SM}}\right)\,\equiv\,\delta\kappa_{\text{SM}}^{\text{(ho)}}

(92)

which we use as an approximation for the second term in (
89
),
yielding

\Delta\kappa_{\text{SM}}(M_{W})=\Delta\kappa_{\text{SM}}^{\text{(1)}}\left(M_{W}\right)+\delta\kappa_{\text{SM}}^{(\text{ho})}.

(93)

The prediction of the effective leptonic mixing angle
can now be written in the following way,

s^{2}_{l}=\left(1-\frac{M_{W}^{2}}{M_{Z}^{2}}\right)\left(1+\Delta\kappa_{\text{SM}}+\Delta\kappa_{\text{NS}}^{\text{(1)}}+\Delta\kappa_{\text{NS,red}}^{\text{(2)}}+\Delta\kappa^{(2)}_{\text{NS,irr}}\right)

(94)

evaluated for

M_{W}
as obtained from (
32
) with the full

\Delta r=\Delta r_{\text{SM}}+\Delta r_{\text{NS}}
.

For comparison, we will also need the effective mixing angle
including just the one-loop non-standard corrections,
calculated from

\sin^{2}\theta_{\text{eff}}^{(1)}\equiv s^{2\,(1)}_{l}\,=\,\left(1-\big(\frac{M_{W}^{(1)}}{M_{Z}}\big)^{2}\right)\left(1+\Delta\kappa_{\text{SM}}+\Delta\kappa_{\text{NS}}^{\text{(1)}}\right),

(95)

where
M_{W}^{(1)}
is used as the corresponding input for the
W
-boson mass
obtained from (
32
) with

\Delta r=\Delta r_{\text{SM}}+\Delta r_{\text{NS}}^{(1)}
.

6
Conclusions

We have given an overview over the currently most precise calculation of the
electroweak precision observables
M_{W}
and
\sin^{2}\theta_{\text{eff}}

in the aligned Two-Higgs-Doublet Model
where one of the
CP
-even scalars (
h^{0}
) is identified with the scalar boson
at 125 GeV observed by the LHC experiments ATLAS and CMS.
It includes, besides all the known SM loop corrections, the full non-standard
one loop contributions and the leading non-standard two-loop contributions
from the top-Yukawa coupling and the self-couplings of the Higgs bosons.
As already at the one-loop level, the non-standard two-loop contributions
from the scalar self-interactions are particularly sensitive to mass splittings
between neutral and charged scalars and become sizeable
whenever the one-loop corrections are large.
Hence, they turn out to be of special importance when a large
loop-induced shift in the predicted
W
-boson mass is required,
as it is the case for the recently published final result of the CDF experiment.
Simultaneously, the predictions for
\sin^{2}\theta_{\text{eff}}
move away
from the experimental world average by more than one standard deviation,
towards the 2
\sigma
limit, but show full agreement with the individual
value of the SLD experiment measured via the left–right asymmetry.

As a new feature, the two-loop contributions have a significant dependence on the
parameters
\tan\beta
and
\lambda_{5}
(or
m_{12}^{2}
, respectively),
a coefficient of the THDM scalar potential that is not fixed by the masses
of the neutral and charged Higgs bosons.
3
3

3

This was already observed
in
[
75
]
where higher-order terms were incorporated
by means of effective couplings in the one-loop Higgs contributions.

In the Inert-Higgs-Doublet Model, the two-loop contributions
depend on a specific combination
\Lambda_{345}
of quartic coefficients in
the scalar potential and are independent of
\tan\beta
. In both cases,
they can modify the one-loop predictions substantially and are relevant
for phenomenological analyses.

For computation of the electroweak precision observables the Fortran code

THDM_EWPOS
has been developed and is available at
[
145
]
.
It contains all the entries described in this paper and in [88].

Non-standard contributions to the gauge boson self-energies

The part of the gauge-boson self-energies in the aligned THDM resulting from the
non-standard scalars in the one-loop diagrams is given by the following expressions,

\displaystyle\Sigma^{(1)}_{W,\text{NS}}\left(p^{2}\right)=\;

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{16\pi s_{W}^{2}}\left[2A_{0}(m_{H^{\pm}}^{2})+A_{0}(m_{H^{0}}^{2})+A_{0}(m_{A^{0}}^{2})\right.

\displaystyle\left.-4B_{00}(p^{2},m_{A^{0}}^{2},m_{H^{\pm}}^{2})-4B_{00}(p^{2},m_{H^{0}}^{2},m_{H^{\pm}}^{2})\right],

(98)

\displaystyle\Sigma^{(1)}_{Z,\text{NS}}\left(p^{2}\right)=\;

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{16\pi\,c_{W}^{2}s_{W}^{2}}\left[2\left(c_{W}^{2}-s_{W}^{2}\right)^{2}A_{0}(m_{H^{\pm}}^{2})+A_{0}(m_{H^{0}}^{2})+A_{0}(m_{A^{0}}^{2})\right.

\displaystyle\left.-4(\left(c_{W}^{2}-s_{W}^{2}\right){}^{2}B_{00}(p^{2},m_{H^{\pm}}^{2},m_{H^{\pm}}^{2})+B_{00}(p^{2},m_{A^{0}}^{2},m_{H^{0}}^{2}))\right],

(99)

\displaystyle\Sigma^{(1)}_{\gamma,\text{NS}}\left(p^{2}\right)=\;

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}\left[A_{0}(m_{H^{\pm}}^{2})-2B_{00}(p^{2},m_{H^{\pm}}^{2},m_{H^{\pm}}^{2})\right],

(100)

\displaystyle\Sigma^{(1)}_{\gamma Z,\text{NS}}\left(p^{2}\right)=\;

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{4\pi}\,\frac{c_{W}^{2}-s_{W}^{2}}{c_{W}s_{W}}\big[A_{0}(m_{H^{\pm}}^{2})-2B_{00}(p^{2},m_{H^{\pm}}^{2},m_{H^{\pm}}^{2})\big].

(101)

They are specified in terms of the one- and two-point integrals
A_{0}
and
B_{0}

in dimensional regularization with dimension
D
and scale parameter
\mu
,

\displaystyle A_{0}(m^{2})

\displaystyle=\,\frac{(2\pi\mu)^{4-D}}{i\pi^{2}}\int\!d^{D}q\;\frac{1}{q^{2}-m^{2}+i\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\,m^{2}\left(\frac{2}{4-D}-\gamma+\log 4\pi\,-\,\log\frac{m^{2}}{\mu^{2}}+1\right)+{\cal O}(4-D)\,,

\displaystyle B_{0}(p^{2},m_{1}^{2},m_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle=\,\frac{(2\pi\mu)^{4-D}}{i\pi^{2}}\int\!d^{D}q\;\frac{1}{\big[q^{2}-m_{1}^{2}+i\epsilon\big]\big[(p+q)^{2}-m_{2}^{2}+i\epsilon\big]}

\displaystyle=\,\frac{2}{4-D}-\gamma+\log 4\pi-\int_{0}^{1}\!dx\,\log\frac{x^{2}p^{2}-x(p^{2}+m_{1}^{2}-m_{2}^{2})-i\epsilon}{\mu^{2}}+{\cal O}(4-D)\,,

\displaystyle B_{1}(p^{2},m_{1}^{2},m_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle=\,\frac{1}{2(D-1)}\left[A_{0}(m_{2}^{2})+2m_{1}^{2}\,B_{0}(p^{2},m_{1}^{2},m_{2}^{2})+(p^{2}+m_{1}^{2}-m_{2}^{2})\,B_{1}(p^{2},m_{1}^{2},m_{2}^{2})\right].

Lower bounds on fibered Yang-Mills functionals: generic nefness and semistability of direct images
Siarhei Finski

Abstract




MnLargeSymbols’164
MnLargeSymbols’171

Abstract.

The main goal of this paper is to generalize a part of the relationship between mean curvature and Harder-Narasimhan filtrations of holomorphic vector bundles to arbitrary polarized fibrations.
More precisely, for a polarized family of complex projective manifolds, we establish lower bounds on a fibered version of Yang-Mills functionals in terms of the Harder-Narasimhan slopes of direct image sheaves associated with high tensor powers of the polarization.
We discuss the optimality of these lower bounds and, as an application, provide an analytic characterisation of a fibered version of generic nefness.
As another application, we refine the existent obstructions for finding metrics with constant horizontal mean curvature.
The study of the semiclassical limit of Hermitian Yang-Mills functionals lies at the heart of our approach.

We fix a

(1,1)

The main goal of this paper is to study the relationship between the so-called horizontal mean curvature of the fibration, which is a certain differential-geometric invariant of the family defined using

\omega_{B}
-slopes of direct images

More precisely, consider a Hermitian metric

\pi

We denote by
\omega(h^{L})\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2\pi}R^{L}

is the curvature of the Chern connection.
When

As

The form

\omega=\omega_{V}+\omega_{H}

\omega_{V}\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(X,\wedge^{1,1}T^{V*}X)

\omega_{H}\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(X,\wedge^{1,1}T^{H*}X)

Upon the natural identification of

The triple

[

We define the

\wedge_{\omega_{B}}\omega_{H}(h^{L})\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(X)

\wedge_{\omega_{B}}\omega_{H}(h^{L})\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\frac{\omega_{H}(h^{L})\wedge\omega_{B}^{m-1}}{\omega_{B}^{m}}.

(1.1)

We say that

is

\wedge_{\omega_{B}}\omega_{H}(h^{L})

By decomposition into horizontal and vertical components, it is easy to see that this condition is equivalent to

\omega(h^{L})^{n+1}\wedge\pi^{*}\omega_{B}^{m-1}=c\cdot\omega(h^{L})^{n}\wedge\pi^{*}\omega_{B}^{m},

By integrating (

Remark the similarity of (

[
26

, if the fibers are Fano.
For families of manifolds given by projectivizations of vector bundles, the condition (

If instead of a closed manifold

[

or Donaldson

[

Remark that in the important case when

for some holomorphic vector bundle

The first main observation of this paper is that the correspondence between fibered Einstein and Hermite Einstein equations is much tighter.
Indeed, as we shall see, relying on the work of Ma-Zhang

]
,
[

]

Let us now explain the first manifestation of this correspondence.

Recall that a

(or
\omega_{B}
-slope) of a coherent sheaf
\mathscr{E}
over

\mu(\mathscr{E})\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\deg(\mathscr{E})/{\rm{rk}}(\mathscr{E})

\deg(\mathscr{E})\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\int_{B}[c_{1}(\det\mathscr{E})]\cdot[\omega_{B}^{m-1}]

represents an Aeppli cohomology class since

is Gauduchon, see Section

[

A torsion-free coherent sheaf

When

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that

admits an approximate fibered Einstein metric, i.e. there is

, such that for any
\epsilon>0
, there is a relatively positive metric

on

\big|\wedge_{\omega_{B}}\omega_{H}(h^{L}_{\epsilon})-c\big|<\epsilon.

(1.3)

Then the vector bundles

are asymptotically semistable, i.e. for any quotient sheaves
\mathcal{Q}_{k}
of
E_{k}

{\rm{rk}}(\mathcal{Q}_{k})>0
, and any

\mu(\mathcal{Q}_{k})\geq(1-\epsilon)\cdot\mu(E_{k})
.

Remark 1.2
.

It is likely that there is an even closer relationship between fibered Einstein metrics on

and Hermite Einstein metrics on

, paralleling the known correspondence between constant scalar curvature and balanced metrics, see Donaldson
[
31

.

When Theorem

over
B
, and

, due to a precise relation between the slopes of

,

, and

The asymptotic semistability condition from Theorem

We will now discuss some numerical obstructions for it.
More precisely, for an irreducible complex analytic subspace

\mu(Y)=\frac{1}{k+1}\cdot\frac{\int_{Y}[c_{1}(L)^{k+1}]\cdot[\omega_{B}^{m-1}]}{\int_{Y}[c_{1}(L)^{k}]\cdot[\omega_{B}^{m}]}.

(1.4)

By Serre vanishing theorem, for

Theorem 1.3

If the vector bundles

.
Moreover, if

Remark 1.4
.

A combination of Theorems

Fibered Yang-Mills functionals through the semiclassical limit

The main goal of this section is to prove Theorems
1.5
and
1.7
.
The theory of Toeplitz operators and Hermitian Yang-Mills theory, which we recall below, will be particularly useful for that.

We begin by recalling some facts about Toeplitz operators.
Let
Y
be a complex projective manifold of dimension
n
with an ample line bundle
L
.
We fix a positive Hermitian metric
h^{L}
on
L
.
We denote by
\omega
its first Chern form,
c_{1}(L,h^{L})
.
For smooth sections
f,f^{\prime}
of
L^{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, over
Y
, we define the
L^{2}
-scalar product using the pointwise scalar product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{h^{L}}
induced by
h^{L}
as follows

\langle f,f^{\prime}\rangle_{L^{2}(Y)}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\int_{Y}\langle f(x),f^{\prime}(x)\rangle_{h^{L}}\cdot\omega^{n}(x).

(2.1)

Recall that the
Bergman projector

B_{k}
is given by the orthogonal projection (with respect to the scalar product (
2.1
)) from the space of
L^{2}
-sections of
L^{k}
to
H^{0}(Y,L^{k})
.
For any bounded function
f
on
Y
, we then define the Toeplitz operator,
T_{k}(f)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}H^{0}(Y,L^{k})\to H^{0}(Y,L^{k})
, as follows

T_{k}(f)(s)=B_{k}(f\cdot s),\qquad s\in H^{0}(Y,L^{k}).

(2.2)

Proposition 2.1
.

For any bounded function
f\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}Y\to\real
, the following inequalities hold

\inf f\cdot{\rm{Id}}_{H^{0}(Y,L^{k})}\leq T_{k}(f)\leq\sup f\cdot{\rm{Id}}_{H^{0}(Y,L^{k})},

(2.3)

where by
A\leq B
we mean that the difference
B-A
is positive definite.
Moreover, if
f
is smooth, then for any continuous function
\phi\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\real\to\real
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{{\rm{Tr}}\big[\phi(T_{k}(f))\big]}{\dim H^{0}(Y,L^{k})}=\frac{\int_{x\in Y}\phi(f(x))\omega^{n}(x)}{\int_{Y}[\omega^{n}]},

(2.4)

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\big\|\phi(T_{k}(f))\big\|=\max\big\{|\sup\phi(f)|,|\inf\phi(f)|\big\},

where
\|\cdot\|
is the operators norm.

Proof.

The statement (
2.3
) follows from the trivial fact that if
f
is a positive function, then the operator
T_{k}(f)
is positive-definite.
The statement (
2.4
) is a restatement of the weak convergence of spectral measures of Toeplitz operators due to Boutet de Monvel-Guillemin
[
48
, Theorem 13.13]
.
For an alternative proof through Bergman kernel expansion, see Ma-Marinescu
[
50
, Theorem 7.4.1]
, Barron-Ma-Marinescu-Pinsonnault
[
4
, Theorem 3.8]
or
[
34
, Appendix A]
.
See also Ma-Marinescu
[
52
]
,
[
53
]
,
[
54
]
,
[
51
]
for generalizations and more refined results.
∎

Now, the reason why Toeplitz operators are relevant to this paper is because they appear as the principal term in the asymptotic expansion of the curvature of
L^{2}
-metrics on direct images of a polarized fibrations.
More precisely, consider a proper holomorphic submersion
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}X\to B
between complex manifolds
X
and
B
of dimensions
n+m
and
m
respectively,
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
L
be a holomorphic line bundle over
X
, which is relatively ample with respect to
\pi
.
Endow
L
with a relatively positive Hermitian metric
h^{L}
.
Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
be big enough so that
E_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=R^{0}\pi_{*}L^{k}
is locally free.
The
L^{2}
-product (
2.1
) then defines a smooth Hermitian metric
h^{E_{k}}
on
E_{k}
.
We denote by
R^{E_{k}}\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(B,\wedge^{2}T^{*}B\otimes\text{End}{(E_{k})})
the curvature of its Chern connection.

Theorem 2.2

( Ma-Zhang
[
56
, Theorem 0.4]
)
.

There are
C>0
,
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, such that for any
k\geq k_{0}
,

\Big\|\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2\pi}R^{E_{k}}-k\cdot T_{k}(\omega_{H})\Big\|\leq C,

(2.5)

where
\|\cdot\|
is the operator norm, and we naturally extended the definition of Toeplitz operators from functions to bounded sections of
\pi^{*}\wedge^{2}T^{*}B
.

As we shall explain below, Theorem
2.2
is the crucial ingredient connecting fibered Yang-Mills functionals with Hermitian Yang-Mills functionals.
But before this, let us mention another application of Theorem
2.2
to the study of Harder-Narasimhan slopes of direct images.

We fix now a
Gauduchon Hermitian form

\omega_{B}
on
B
.
As before Theorem
1.5
, we denote by
\mu_{\max}^{k}
the maximal Harder-Narasimhan
\omega_{B}
-slope of
E_{k}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

There is
C>0
, such that
\mu_{\max}^{k}\leq Ck
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
.

Proof.

For
p=1,\ldots,{\rm{rk}}(E_{k})
, we denote by
R^{\wedge^{p}E_{k}}
the curvature of the Chern connection on
\wedge^{p}E_{k}
, induced by the metric
h^{\wedge^{p}E_{k}}
induced by
h^{E_{k}}
.
By Theorem
2.2
, we conclude that there is
C>0
, such that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, we have

\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2\pi}\wedge_{\omega_{B}}R^{\wedge^{p}E_{k}}\leq Cpk\cdot{\rm{Id}}_{\wedge^{p}E_{k}}.

(2.6)

Let
L
be a line subbundle of
\wedge^{p}E_{k}
.
We denote by
h^{L}
the Hermitian metric on
L
, induced by the metric
h^{E_{k}}
.
By (

) and the well-known principle that curvature decreases in holomorphic sub-bundles, cf.
[

, we deduce

\wedge_{\omega_{B}}c_{1}(L,h^{L})\leq Cpk.

(2.7)

However, it is classical, cf.
[
47
, Proofs of Lemma 5.7.16 and Theorem 5.8.3]
, that we have

\mu_{\max}^{k}\leq\max_{p=1,\ldots,{\rm{rk}}(E_{k})}\sup_{L\subset\wedge^{p}E_{k}}\frac{1}{p}\int_{B}c_{1}(L,h^{L})\wedge\omega_{B}^{m-1},

(2.8)

where the second supremum is taken over line subbundles

.
We conclude by (
2.7
) and (
2.8
).
∎

.

]

on

(2.9)

is then a formal consequence of the submultiplicativity and Proposition

Let us now recall some crucial facts from Hermitian Yang-Mills theory, following the pioneering work of Atiyah-Bott
[

]
and later developments by Donaldson
[

]
, Daskalopoulos-Wentworth
[

We fix a compact complex manifold
B
of dimension

with a Gauduchon Hermitian form
\omega_{B}
on

.
Let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle of rank

over

.
For a Hermitian metric
h^{E}
on

, we denote by

its curvature.
For any

,

, we define the
Hermitian Yang-Mills functional
as

\displaystyle{\rm{\displaystyle HYM}}_{p,c}(E,h^{E})\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\int_{B}{\rm{Tr}}\Big|\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2\pi}\wedge_{\omega_{B}}R_{x}^{E}-c\cdot{\rm{Id}}_{E}\Big|^{p}\omega_{B}^{m}(x),

(2.10)

\displaystyle{\rm{\displaystyle HYM}}_{+\infty,c}(E,h^{E})\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\sup_{x\in X}\Big\|\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2\pi}\wedge_{\omega_{B}}R_{x}^{E}-c\cdot{\rm{Id}}_{E}\Big\|,

where
\|\cdot\|
means the operator norm.

As in (
1.7
), we denote the Harder-Narasimhan
\omega_{B}
-slopes of

by
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{r}
.
Let
\mu_{\min}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\mu_{r}
,

\mu_{E}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\frac{1}{r}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\delta[\mu_{i}].

(2.11)

The following result lies at the heart of this paper.

Theorem 2.4
.

For any
c\in\real
,
p\in[1,+\infty[
, and any Hermitian metric
h^{E}
on
E
, we have

\displaystyle{\rm{\displaystyle HYM}}_{p,c}(E,h^{E})\geq r\cdot\int|x-c|^{p}d\mu_{E}(x)\cdot\int_{B}[\omega_{B}^{m}],

(2.12)

\displaystyle{\rm{\displaystyle HYM}}_{+\infty,c}(E,h^{E})\geq\max\Big\{|\mu_{\min}-c|,|\mu_{\max}-c|\Big\}.

For the proof of Theorem
2.4
, remark first that for
p=+\infty
, it follows directly from Theorem
2.4
for

by taking
p
-th root and then a limit

.
For the proof of Theorem
2.4
for

, see Atiyah-Bott
[
2
, Proposition 8.20]
if
\dim B=1
, Daskalopoulos-Wentworth
[

, Lemma 2.17, Corollary 2.22, Proposition 2.25]
if

is Kähler of any dimension (even though the article
[

]
is written for surfaces, cf. Sibley
[

, §3.2]
), and Li-Zhang-Zhang
[

, Theorem 1.6 and Corollary 4.4]
for the non-Kähler case of any dimension.
It is a remarkable that the bounds from Theorem

are actually tight, see Theorem
3.5
for a precise statement.

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

Conserving the notations introduced in (
1.7
), define the probability measure,
\eta_{k,0}^{HN}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, on as

\eta_{k,0}^{HN}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\frac{1}{N_{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{k}}\delta\big[\mu_{i}^{k}\big],

(2.13)

We apply Theorem
2.4
for
(E_{k},h^{E_{k}})
, for any

,

,

, to get

\displaystyle{\rm{\displaystyle HYM}}_{p,ck}(E_{k},h^{E_{k}})\geq N_{k}\cdot\int|x-ck|^{p}d\eta_{k,0}^{HN}(x)\cdot\int_{B}[\omega_{B}^{m}],

(2.14)

\displaystyle{\rm{\displaystyle HYM}}_{+\infty,ck}(E_{k},h^{E_{k}})\geq\max\Big\{|\mu_{\min}^{k}-ck|,|\mu_{\max}^{k}-ck|\Big\}.

Directly from Theorem
2.2
and Proposition

, for any

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{{\rm{HYM}}_{p,ck}(E_{k},h^{E_{k}})}{k^{p}\cdot N_{k}}=\frac{{\rm{FYM}}_{p,c}(\pi,h^{L})}{\int_{X_{b}}[\omega^{n}]},

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{{\rm{HYM}}_{+\infty,ck}(E_{k},h^{E_{k}})}{k}={\rm{FYM}}_{+\infty,c}(\pi,h^{L}),

where

is an arbitrary point, and

Horizontal curvature on subfamilies and generic fibered nefness

The main goal of this section is to establish Theorems
1.9
,
1.10
.
For this, we construct a sequence of metrics on the polarizing line bundle from a sequence of Hermitian metrics on direct images.
To show that horizontal mean curvature behaves well under this procedure, we rely on a fibered analogue of the principle that “a curvature of a vector bundle increases under taking quotients”.

More precisely, consider a holomorphic submersion
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}X\to B
between compact complex manifolds
X
and
B
.
We denote
m\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\dim B
.
Consider an embedding
\iota\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}Y\hookrightarrow X
of a smooth complex manifold
Y
, such that restriction of
\pi
,
\pi|_{Y}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}Y\to B
, is a submersion.
We fix a
(1,1)
-form
\omega_{X}
on
X
, which is positive along the fibers of
\pi
, and denote
\omega_{Y}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\iota^{*}\omega_{X}
.
We fix a Hermitian
(1,1)
-form
\omega_{B}
on
B
, and denote by
\wedge_{\omega_{B}}\omega_{Y,H}\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(Y)
,
\wedge_{\omega_{B}}\omega_{X,H}\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(X)
, the horizontal mean curvatures of
\omega_{Y}
and
\omega_{X}
respectively.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
y\in Y
, we have

\wedge_{\omega_{B}}\omega_{Y,H}(y)\geq\wedge_{\omega_{B}}\omega_{X,H}(\iota(y))
.

Remark 3.2
.

An equivalent result was established in
[
33
, (2.2)]
by a slightly different method.

Proof.

Let us fix
y\in Y
, and denote by
b\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\pi|_{Y}(y)
,
x\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\iota(y)
, and by
e_{1},\ldots,e_{m}
an orthonormal basis of
T^{1,0}_{b}B
with respect to
\omega_{B}
.
We denote by
e_{1}^{X},\ldots,e_{m}^{X}\in T^{1,0}_{x}X
the horizontal lifts of
e_{1},\ldots,e_{m}
, defined with respect to
\omega_{X}
, i.e.
d\pi(e_{i}^{X})=e_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,m
and
e_{1}^{X},\ldots,e_{m}^{X}
are orthogonal (with respect to
\omega_{X}
) to the tangent space of the fibers,
T^{V}X
, of
\pi
.
Similarly, we denote by
e_{1}^{Y},\ldots,e_{m}^{Y}\in T^{1,0}_{y}Y
the horizontal lifts of
e_{1},\ldots,e_{m}
, defined with respect to
\omega_{Y}
.
Clearly, using implicitly the embedding of
T_{y}Y
in
T_{x}X
through
\iota
, we can write
e_{i}^{Y}=e_{i}^{X}+v_{i}
, where
v_{i}\in T^{V}_{x}X
.
But then, since
e_{i}^{X}
and
v_{i}
are orthogonal with respect to
\omega_{X}
, and
\omega_{X}
is positive in the vertical directions, we obtain

\sqrt{-1}\omega_{Y}(e_{i}^{Y},\overline{e}_{i}^{Y})=\sqrt{-1}\omega_{X}(e_{i}^{X},\overline{e}_{i}^{X})+\sqrt{-1}\omega_{X}(v_{i},\overline{v}_{i})\geq\sqrt{-1}\omega_{X}(e_{i}^{X},\overline{e}_{i}^{X})
.
By taking a sum of the above inequality over all
i=1,\ldots,m
, we establish the needed inequality.
∎

Another ingredient we need is the calculation of the horizontal mean curvature for projectivizations of vector bundles.
More precisely, let
(F,h^{F})
be a Hermitian vector bundle over
B
of rank
r
.
Let
\mathcal{O}(1)
be the hyperplane bundle over
\mathbb{P}(F^{*})
,
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathbb{P}(F^{*})\to X
.
We endow
\mathcal{O}(1)
with the metric
h^{\mathcal{O}(1)}
induced by
h^{F}
.
We denote by
R^{F}
the curvature of the Chern connection on
(F,h^{F})
, by
\omega
the first Chern class of
(\mathcal{O}(1),h^{\mathcal{O}(1)})
, and by
\omega_{H}
its horizontal component.

Lemma 3.3
.

In the above notations, for any
x\in X
, we have

\inf_{y\in\mathbb{P}(F_{x}^{*})}\wedge_{\omega_{B}}\omega_{H}(y)=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in F_{x},\|f\|_{h^{F}}=1\end{subarray}}\Big\langle\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2\pi}\wedge_{\omega_{B}}R^{F}f,f\Big\rangle_{h^{F}}.

(3.1)

Proof.

We fix some local coordinates
z\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
on
B
, centered at
x\in B
, and a local normal frame
f_{1},\ldots,f_{r}
of
F
at
x
, defined in a neighborhood
U
of
x

we mean the one satisfying
\langle f_{i},f_{j}\rangle_{h^{F}}=\delta_{ij}-\sum_{\lambda\mu}d_{\lambda\mu ij}z_{\lambda}\overline{z}_{\mu}+O(|z|^{3})
for some constants

.
We denote by
f_{1}^{*},\ldots,f_{r}^{*}
the dual frame of
F^{*}
.
The above data defines a trivialization of

near
\pi^{-1}(x)
as follows.
For
a\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})
, where
a_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
,
1\leq i\leq r
, and not all
a_{i}
are equal to zero, the trivialization is given by the map

(z,[a])\to[\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}f_{i}^{*}(z)]\in\mathbb{P}(F^{*})
.
Now we take

R^{\mathcal{O}(1)}_{(x,[f_{1}^{*}])}=\sum_{2\leq j\leq r}db_{j}\wedge d\overline{b}_{j}+\langle R^{F}f_{1},f_{1}\rangle_{h^{F}}.

(3.2)

In particular, we see that the vertical part of the form
\omega=c_{1}(\mathcal{O}(1),h^{\mathcal{O}(1)})

4

Asymptotic Riemann-Roch-Grothendieck and semistability

The main goal of this section is to prove a numerical obstruction for asymptotic semistability of direct images from Theorem
1.3
.
This will be based on an asymptotic version of Riemann-Roch-Grothendieck theorem, which we establish here in the singular setting.

To begin, let us recall some basic facts about Bott-Chern and Aeppli cohomologies.
Let
Y
be a compact complex manifold of dimension
n
.
We denote by
\Omega^{(p,q)}(Y)
the vector space of
(p,q)
-differential forms on
Y
,
p,q\in\mathbb{N}
, and define
\partial\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\Omega^{(p,q)}(Y)\to\Omega^{(p+1,q)}(Y)
,
\overline{\partial}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\Omega^{(p,q)}(Y)\to\Omega^{(p,q+1)}(Y)
, as usual.
Recall that Bott-Chern cohomology,
H^{p,q}_{BC}(Y)
, is defined as

H^{p,q}_{BC}(Y)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\frac{(\ker\partial\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\Omega^{(p,q)}(Y)\to\Omega^{(p+1,q)}(Y))\cap(\ker\overline{\partial}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\Omega^{(p,q)}(Y)\to\Omega^{(p,q+1)}(Y))}{{\rm{im}}\partial\overline{\partial}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\Omega^{(p-1,q-1)}(Y)\to\Omega^{(p,q)}(Y)}.

(4.1)

Recall that Aeppli cohomology,
H^{p,q}_{A}(Y)
, is defined as

H^{p,q}_{A}(Y)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\frac{\ker\partial\overline{\partial}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\Omega^{(p,q)}(Y)\to\Omega^{(p+1,q+1)}(Y)}{({\rm{im}}\partial\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\Omega^{(p-1,q)}(Y)\to\Omega^{(p,q)}(Y))+({\rm{im}}\partial\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\Omega^{(p,q-1)}(Y)\to\Omega^{(p,q)}(Y))}.

(4.2)

It is standard that for compact Kähler manifolds, the two cohomologies coincide.
For
p,q=0,\ldots,n
, we have the natural (non-degenerate, see
[
66
, §2.c]
) pairing

\wedge\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}H^{p,q}_{BC}(Y)\times H^{n-p,n-q}_{A}(Y)\to\mathbb{C},

(4.3)

given by the wedge product and integration.
If
p\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}Y\to B
is a holomorphic map between compact complex manifolds, then for
s\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\dim Y-\dim B
, we have a natural map
p_{*}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}H^{p,q}_{BC}(Y)\to H^{p-s,q-s}_{BC}(B)
, defined by the pairing (
4.3
) and the pull-back
p^{*}
.

The Bott-Chern cohomology can be generally defined for arbitrary complex analytic spaces
Y
in the sense of
[
24
, Definition II.5.2]
, where one considers differential forms on
Y
obtained by pullbacks of smooth differential forms through local embeddings of the space into complex vector spaces.
Due to a theorem of Lelong, cf.
[
24
, Theorem III.2.7]
, the intersection pairing (
4.3
) can still be defined in this setting, and so the slope (
1.4
) is well-defined.
Also, using the non-degeneracy of the wedge product (
4.3
) for compact manifolds, we can extend the definition of the pushforward for maps between a compact complex analytic spaces
Y
and a compact manifold
B
.

Now, let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over
Y
.
Using Chern-Weil theory, one can construct for any Hermitian metric
h^{E}
on
E
a corresponding Chern character form,
{\rm{ch}}(E,h^{E})
, which is a
d
-closed form in
\oplus_{p=0}^{+\infty}\Omega^{(p,p)}(Y)
.
Bott-Chern in
[
11
]
showed that the resulting class in Bott-Chern cohomology doesn’t depend on the choice of the metric.
This gives a definition of the Chern character,
{\rm{ch}}(E)
of a vector bundle
E
with values in Bott-Chern cohomology.

Following earlier work by Qiang
[
64
]
, Bismut-Shu-Wei in
[
7
]
generalized the definition of the Chern character with values in Bott-Chern cohomology for any coherent sheaf
\mathscr{E}
on
Y
.
If
\mathscr{E}
has a has a finite locally free projective resolution (which is always the case if
Y
is projective), this construction corresponds to the one given by the alternating sum of Chern characters of the resolution.
By
[
7
, §8.6]
, the
(k,k)
-component of the Chern character,
{\rm{ch}}_{k}(\mathscr{E})
, we have

{\rm{ch}}_{0}(\mathscr{E})={\rm{rk}}(\mathscr{E}),\qquad{\rm{ch}}_{1}(\mathscr{E})=c_{1}(\det\mathscr{E}),

(4.4)

where
\det\mathscr{E}
is the Knudsen-Mumford determinant
[
45
]
.
Without entering into details of the construction, we mention that the absence of finite locally free projective resolutions of coherent sheaves for general complex manifolds is circumvented in
[
7
]
by the use of so-called antiholomorphic superconnections, introduced by Block
[
8
]
, see
[
7
, Theorem 6.7]
.

Now, recall that for any proper holomorphic map
p\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}Y\to B
, and any coherent sheaf
\mathscr{E}
, Grauert theorem tells that the direct image sheaves
R^{q}p_{*}\mathscr{E}
,
q\in\mathbb{N}
, are coherent.
The main result of this section goes as follows.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
Y
be an irreducible compact complex analytic space,
L
an arbitrary line bundle on
Y
and
\mathscr{E}
a coherent sheaf on
Y
.
Let
p\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}Y\to B
be a holomorphic map to a compact complex manifold
B
.
Then for any
r\in\mathbb{N}
, and
s\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\dim Y-\dim B+r
, in the Bott-Chern cohomology

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k^{s}}\sum_{t=0}^{\dim Y}(-1)^{t}{\rm{ch}}_{r}(R^{t}p_{*}(\mathscr{E}\otimes L^{k}))=\frac{{\rm{rk}}(\mathscr{E})}{s!}\cdot p_{*}(c_{1}(L)^{s}).

(4.5)

Remark 4.2
.

Despite a huge amount of literature, we were not able to find the proof of this result under the stated hypothesizes (even for projective
Y,B
).
For flat maps
p
and relatively ample
L
, this result can be alternatively established using Knudsen-Mumford expansions, see
[
45
]
and Phong-Ross-Sturm
[
61
, Theorem 3]
.
Note, however, that flatness doesn’t pass through subfamilies, and there is no flatness assumption in Theorem
1.3
.

The proof of this result relies on the recent result of Bismut-Shu-Wei
[
7
]
establishing the Riemann-Roch-Grothendieck theorem in Bott-Chern cohomology for arbitrary holomorphic maps between (
smooth
!) complex manifolds, relying on the Hypoelliptic Laplacian of Bismut
[
5
]
, and generalizing previous results as
[
10
]
.
More precisely, the main result of
[
7
]
says the following.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
p\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}Y\to B
be a holomorphic map between compact complex manifolds.
Then for any coherent sheaf
\mathscr{E}
on
Y
, the following identity holds in Bott-Chern cohomology

{\rm{Td}}(TB)\cdot\sum_{t=0}^{\dim Y}(-1)^{t}{\rm{ch}}(R^{t}p_{*}(\mathscr{E}))=p_{*}({\rm{Td}}(TY)\cdot{\rm{ch}}(\mathscr{E})),

(4.6)

where
{\rm{Td}}(TB)
,
{\rm{Td}}(TY)
are the Todd classes.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Remark first that for smooth manifolds
Y
, the result follows directly from Theorem
4.3
by (
4.4
) and the fact that the
0
-degree part of the Todd class is identity.
If
\dim Y=0
, then
Y
is automatically smooth, and, hence, Theorem
4.1
holds as stated.

We will argue by induction on the dimension of
Y
.
For this, we consider a resolution of singularities
f\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\hat{Y}\to Y
of
Y
.
For any
q=1,\ldots,\dim Y
, we define the sheaves
\mathscr{Q}_{q}
on
Y
as
\mathscr{Q}_{q}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=R^{q}f_{*}f^{*}\mathscr{E}
, where
f^{*}\mathscr{E}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=f^{-1}\mathscr{E}\otimes_{f^{-1}\mathscr{O}_{Y}}\mathscr{O}_{\hat{Y}}
.
We define the sheaf
\mathscr{Q}_{0}
on
Y
by the following short exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathscr{E}\rightarrow R^{0}f_{*}f^{*}\mathscr{E}\rightarrow\mathscr{Q}_{0}\rightarrow 0.

(4.7)

By Grauert theorem, the sheaves
\mathscr{Q}_{q}
,
q=0,\ldots,\dim Y
, are coherent.
Since the resolution of singularities is biholomorphic away from a subset of singular points of
Y
, and over the locally free locus of
\mathscr{E}
, by projection formula, cf.
[
42
, Exercise II.5.1d)]
, we have
R^{0}f_{*}f^{*}\mathscr{E}=\mathscr{E}
, the supports of the sheafs
\mathscr{Q}_{q}
,
q=0,\ldots,\dim Y
, are
proper
analytic subsets of
Y
, which, by irreducibility of
Y
, have strictly smaller dimension than
Y
, cf.
[
24
, Proposition II.4.2.6]
.
By this and the usual
devissage
techniques, see
[
39
, Théorème 3.1.2]
, cf.
[
42
, Proposition I.7.4]
, for any
q=0,\ldots,\dim Y
, there is
r(q)\in\mathbb{N}
, and complex analytic subspaces
\iota_{i,q}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}Z_{i,q}\hookrightarrow Y
, with some ideal sheaves
\mathscr{J}_{i,q}
on
Z_{i,q}
and a filtration
\mathscr{F}_{i,q}
of
\mathscr{Q}_{q}
,
i=0,\ldots,r(q)
,
\mathscr{F}_{0,q}=\{0\}
,
\mathscr{F}_{r(q),q}=\mathscr{Q}_{q}
,
\mathscr{F}_{i-1,q}\subset\mathscr{F}_{i,q}
,
i=1,\ldots,r_{q}
, such that for any
i=1,\ldots,r(q)
, we have
\mathscr{F}_{i,q}/\mathscr{F}_{i-1,q}=\iota_{i,q,*}(\mathscr{J}_{i,q})
.
We denote
p_{i,q}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=p\circ\iota_{i,q}
,
i=1,\ldots,r(q)
,
\hat{p}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=p\circ f
.
We argue that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\sum_{t=0}^{\dim Y}(-1)^{t}{\rm{ch}}(R^{t}p_{*}(\mathscr{E}\otimes L^{k}))=\sum_{t=0}^{\dim Y}(-1)^{t}{\rm{ch}}(R^{t}\hat{p}_{*}(f^{*}\mathscr{E}\otimes f^{*}L^{k}))\\
-\sum_{t,u=0}^{\dim Y}(-1)^{t+u}\sum_{i=1}^{r(q)}{\rm{ch}}(R^{t}(p_{i,u})_{*}(\mathscr{J}_{i,u}\otimes\iota_{i,u}^{*}L^{k})).

(4.8)

Once (
4.8
) is established, Theorem
4.1
would follow by induction, as the space
\hat{Y}
is smooth, and so for the first summand on the right-hand side of (
4.8
), the smooth version of Theorem
4.1
applies, and the second summand doesn’t contribute to the asymptotics by induction hypothesis, as all

have strictly smaller dimensions than
Y
.

Now, let us establish (
4.8
). First of all, since in derived category, there is a canonical isomorphism between the functors
R\hat{p}_{*}
and
Rp_{*}Rf_{*}
,
[
9
, Proposition I.9.15]
, cf.
[
7
, (3.13)]
, and the construction of Chern character, defined using derived category of coherent sheaves, factors through the

-theory of the derived category of coherent sheaves
[
7
, Theorem 8.11 and §8.9]
, we have

\sum_{t=0}^{\dim Y}(-1)^{t}{\rm{ch}}(R^{t}\hat{p}_{*}(f^{*}\mathscr{E}\otimes f^{*}L^{k}))=\sum_{t,u=0}^{\dim Y}(-1)^{t+u}{\rm{ch}}(R^{t}p_{*}(R^{u}f_{*}(f^{*}\mathscr{E}\otimes f^{*}L^{k})).

(4.9)

Now, from the exact sequence (
4.7
), using again the fact that the construction of the Chern character passes through the formation of
K
-theory, we obtain

\sum_{t=0}^{\dim Y}(-1)^{t}{\rm{ch}}(R^{t}p_{*}(R^{0}f_{*}(f^{*}\mathscr{E}\otimes f^{*}L^{k}))\\
=\sum_{t=0}^{\dim Y}(-1)^{t}{\rm{ch}}(R^{t}p_{*}(\mathscr{E}\otimes L^{k}))+\sum_{t=0}^{\dim Y}(-1)^{t}{\rm{ch}}(R^{t}p_{*}(\mathscr{Q}_{0}\otimes L^{k})).

(4.10)

Similarly, for any
q=0,\ldots,\dim Y
, we obtain

\sum_{t=0}^{\dim Y}(-1)^{t}{\rm{ch}}(R^{t}p_{*}(\mathscr{Q}_{q}\otimes L^{k}))=\sum_{t=0}^{\dim Y}(-1)^{t}\sum_{i=0}^{r(q)}{\rm{ch}}(R^{t}p_{*}(\iota_{i,q,*}(\mathscr{J}_{i,q})\otimes L^{k})).

(4.11)

Remark, however, that since
\iota_{i,q}
is a closed embedding,
\iota_{i,q,*}
is an exact functor, so we have
R^{v}\iota_{i,q,*}=0
for
v=1,\ldots,\dim Y
, and
R^{0}\iota_{i,q,*}=\iota_{i,q,*}
.
Moreover, by the projection formula, cf.
[
42
, Exercise II.5.1d)]
, we have
R^{0}\iota_{i,q,*}(\mathscr{J}_{i,q})\otimes L^{k}=R^{0}\iota_{i,q,*}(\mathscr{J}_{i,q}\otimes\iota_{i,q}^{*}L^{k})
.
In particular, for any
t,q=0,\ldots,\dim Y
,
i=1,\ldots,r(q)
, we can write

{\rm{ch}}(R^{t}p_{*}(\iota_{i,q,*}(\mathscr{J}_{i,q})\otimes L^{k}))=\sum_{v=0}^{\dim Y}(-1)^{v}{\rm{ch}}(R^{t}p_{*}(R^{v}\iota_{i,q,*}(\mathscr{J}_{i,q}\otimes\iota_{i,q}^{*}L^{k}))).

(4.12)

But using the same argument as in (
4.9
), we have

\sum_{t,v=0}^{\dim Y}(-1)^{t+v}{\rm{ch}}(R^{t}p_{*}(R^{v}\iota_{i,q,*}(\mathscr{J}_{i,q}\otimes\iota_{i,q}^{*}L^{k})))=\sum_{t=0}^{\dim Y}(-1)^{t}{\rm{ch}}(R^{t}(p_{i,q})_{*}(\mathscr{J}_{i,q}\otimes\iota_{i,q}^{*}L^{k})).

(4.13)

Now, a combination of (
4.9
), (
4.10
), (
4.11
), (
4.12
) and (
4.13
), gives us (
4.8
).
∎

Now, let us finally establish an application of Theorem
4.1
towards the study of Harder-Narasimhan slopes of direct images.
We fix an irreducible compact complex analytic space
Y
of dimension
k+m
,
k\geq 0
, with a surjective holomorphic map
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}Y\to B
.
Let
\omega_{B}
be a Gauduchon Hermitian form on
B
.
Let
L
be a relatively ample line bundle on
Y
.
Recall that in (
1.4
), we defined the slope of
Y
, and before Theorem
1.1
, we defined the slopes of coherent sheafs.

Lemma 4.4
.

The following identity holds

\mu(Y)\cdot\int_{B}[\omega_{B}^{m}]=\lim_{k\to\infty}\mu(R^{0}\pi_{*}L^{k})/k.

Proof.

By Serre vanishing theorem, the higher direct images
R^{v}\pi_{*}L^{k}
,
v\geq 1
, vanish.
By (
4.4
), we conclude that

\mu(R^{0}\pi_{*}L^{k})=\frac{\int_{Y}[{\rm{ch}}_{1}(R^{0}\pi_{*}L^{k})]\cdot[\omega_{B}^{m-1}]}{{\rm{ch}}_{0}(R^{0}\pi_{*}L^{k})}.

(4.14)

The result now follows directly from Theorem
4.1
.
∎

Proposition 4.5
.

The vector bundles
E_{k}
are asymptotically semistable if and only if
\eta_{\min}^{HN}=\eta_{\max}^{HN}
.
Moreover, if
E_{k}
are asymptotically semistable, then for any subsheaves
\mathcal{F}_{k}
of
E_{k}
,
{\rm{rk}}(\mathcal{F}_{k})>0
, and any
\epsilon>0
, for
k
big enough, we have
\mu(\mathcal{F}_{k})\leq(1+\epsilon)\cdot\mu(E_{k})
.

Proof.

Remark first that the maximal and the minimal slopes satisfy

\displaystyle\mu_{\max}^{k}=\sup\Big\{\mu(\mathcal{F}_{k})\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathcal{F}_{k}\text{ is a subsheaf of }E_{k}\Big\},

(4.15)

\displaystyle\mu_{\min}^{k}=\inf\Big\{\mu(\mathcal{Q}_{k})\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathcal{Q}_{k}\text{ is a quotient sheaf of }E_{k}\Big\}.

Now, by Theorem
1.5
, as
k\to\infty
, we have

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\mu(E_{k})}{k}=\int xd\eta^{HN}(x).

(4.16)

From (
4.15
) and (
4.16
), we see that
E_{k}
are asymptotically semistable if and only if
\eta_{\min}^{HN}=\esssup\eta^{HN}
.
However, by Theorem
1.5
,
\esssup\eta^{HN}
coincides with
\eta_{\max}^{HN}
, which finishes the proof of the first part of the theorem.
The proof of the second statement of Proposition
4.5
follows directly by (
4.15
) and the first part.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

From
[
36
, Proposition 5.1]
, cf. also
[
76
]
, we know that if
\dim B=1
, then
\essinf\eta^{HN}
coincides with
\eta_{\min}^{HN}
.
The above proof shows that for
\dim B=1
, the condition on the quotient sheaves is equivalent to asymptotic semistability.

Proposition 4.7
.

For any complex analytic subspace
Y
of
X
as in Theorem
1.3
, the following bound holds

\mu(Y)\cdot\int_{B}[\omega_{B}^{m}]\geq\eta_{\min}^{HN}
.

Proof.

Consider the following short exact sequence of sheaves associated with
Y

0\rightarrow\mathscr{J}_{Y}\rightarrow\mathscr{O}_{X}\rightarrow\iota_{*}\mathscr{O}_{Y}\rightarrow 0,

(4.17)

where
\mathscr{J}_{Y}
is the ideal sheaf of
Y
, consisting of local holomorphic functions on
X
, vanishing along
Y
, and
\mathscr{O}_{Y}
is the structure sheaf of
Y
associated with the reduced scheme structure of
Y
, i.e. defined by (
4.17
).
By considering a long exact sequence of direct images associated with (
4.17
) and the map
\pi
, and using Serre vanishing theorem, we conclude that the restriction map
R^{0}\pi_{*}L^{k}\to R^{0}\pi|_{Y,*}L|_{Y}^{k}
is surjective.
Then, in the notations of Theorem
1.5
, we have
\mu_{\min}^{k}\leq\mu(R^{0}\pi|_{Y,*}L|_{Y}^{k})
.
We deduce Proposition
4.7
from Lemma
4.4
by diving by
k
and passing to the limit
k\to\infty
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

First of all, by Lemma
4.4
, we conclude that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\mu(E_{k})/k=\mu(X)\cdot\int_{B}[\omega_{B}^{m}]\geq\eta_{\min}^{HN}.

(4.18)

Moreover, since
\esssup\eta^{HN}
coincides with
\eta_{\max}^{HN}
by
[
36
, Theorem 1.1]
, by (
4.16
), we conclude that the equality in the above inequality holds if and only if
\eta_{\min}^{HN}=\eta_{\max}^{HN}
, i.e. when
E_{k}
is asymptotically semistable by Proposition
4.5
.
In particular, if
E_{k}
is asymptotically semistable, then by
\eta_{\min}^{HN}=\eta_{\max}^{HN}
, Proposition
4.7
and (
4.18
), we establish the first part of Theorem
1.3
.

Let us now establish the second part.
By a reformulation of the result of Xu-Zhuang
[
76
, Lemma 2.26 and Proposition 2.28]
from
[
36
, (1.5)]
, we have

\eta_{\min}^{HN}=\inf_{C\subset X}\mu(C)\cdot\int_{B}[\omega_{B}^{m}],

(4.19)

where
C
runs over all irreducible curves in
X
, with project surjectively to
B
.
In particular, from (
4.19
), we conclude that

\eta_{\min}^{HN}\geq\inf_{Y\subset X}\mu(Y)\cdot\int_{B}[\omega_{B}^{m}],

(4.20)

where
Y
are as in Theorem
1.3
.
A combination of (
4.18
) and (
4.20
) shows that if
\inf_{Y\subset X}\mu(Y)=\mu(X)
, then both (
4.18
) and (
4.20
) are actually equalities.
By the remark after (
4.18
), this implies that
E_{k}
is asymptotically semistable, which finishes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

It is interesting to know if the second part of Theorem
1.3
continues to hold for
\dim B>1
.
There are several potential pitfalls for that.
First, since
B
is not necessarily projective, there might be very few analytic subspaces
Y\subset X
, projecting surjectively to
B
.
Second, even for projective
B
, the main result of
[
36
, Theorem 1.4]
implies that the analogue of the bound (
4.19
) becomes tight if one considers among
Y
all subcurves in
X
projecting to generic curves over the base.
But it seems that there is much more curves like that than analytic subspaces projecting surjectively to the base.

The Weak Galerkin Finite Element Method for the Transport-Reaction Equation Journal: Journal of Computational Physics
Tie Zhang, Shangyou Zhang †

Abstract
We present and analyze a weak Galerkin finite element method for solving the transport-reaction equation in d d space dimensions. This method is highly flexible by allowing the use of discontinuous finite element on general meshes consisting of arbitrary polygon/polyhedra. We derive the L 2 L_{2} -error estimate of O ⁡ ( h k + 1 2 ) O(h^{k+\frac{1}{2}}) -order for the discrete solution when the k k th-order polynomials are used for k ≥ 0 k\geq 0 . Moreover, for a special class of meshes, we also obtain the optimal error estimate of O ⁡ ( h k + 1 ) O(h^{k+1}) -order in the L 2 L_{2} -norm. A derivative recovery formula is presented to approximate the convection directional derivative and the corresponding superconvergence estimate is given. Numerical examples on compatible and non-compatible meshes are provided to show the effectiveness of this weak Galerkin method.

Introduction

In this work, we study a weak Galerkin finite element (WG) method for solving the transport-reaction equation

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\nabla\cdot(\beta u)+\alpha u=f,\;in\;\;\Omega,\\
u=g,\;\;on\;\;\partial\Omega_{-},\end{array}\right.

where
\nabla\cdot
is the divergence operator,
\Omega\subset R^{d}
(
d=2,3
) is a polygonal (
d=2
) or polyhedral (
d=3
) domain with boundary
\partial\Omega
,
\partial\Omega_{-}=\{x\in\partial\Omega:\beta(x)\cdot{\bf n}(x)<0\}
and
{\bf n}(x)
is the outward unit normal vector at the point
x\in\partial\Omega
. This equation is commonly used to model the neutron transport process. In this case, function
u(x)
represents the flux of neutrons at point
x
in the angular direction
\beta
,
\alpha
is the nuclear cross section and the source term
f
stands for the scattering and the fission. The boundary condition means that neutrons are entering the system from inflow boundary
\partial\Omega_{-}
.

Equation (
1
) is a typical first order hyperbolic equation. It is well known that just for problem (
1
), the original discontinuous Galerkin finite element (DG) method was presented by Reed and Hill in 1973
[
13
]
. Since then, many research works have been done for the DG method solving problem (
1
). The first mathematical analysis of this original DG method was given by
Lesaint and Raviart
[
7
]
. They showed that the DG scheme
can be solved in an explicit fashion if
\beta
is constant and the convergence rate is
of
O(h^{k})
-order when the
k
th-order polynomials are used. Later on,
Johnson and Pitkaranta
[
6
]
improved this convergence
rate to
O(h^{k+\frac{1}{2}})
-order. Peterson in
[
12
]
further proved that under quasi-uniform triangulation condition, the
O(h^{k+\frac{1}{2}})
-order convergence is sharp, namely, the standard convergence rate of the DG method for problem (
1
) is of
O(h^{k+\frac{1}{2}})
-order, also see Richter’s work
[
15
]
.

On the other hand, in a diametrically opposed effort, some improved
error estimates or optimal convergence rate are achieved on special meshes. In 1988, Richter

[
14
]
showed that, in the two-dimensional case, the

L_{2}
-error estimate of
\|u-u_{h}\|\leq Ch^{k+1}\|u\|_{k+2}
can be obtained for semi-uniform triangle meshes with the assumption that all element edges are bounded away from the characteristic direction
\beta
, that
is, the triangulation
T_{h}
satisfies
|\beta\cdot{\bf n}(x)|\geq c_{0}>0
on all element edges. Cockburn et. al. in

[
2
]
proved that if
\beta
is
a constant vector and the triangulation
T_{h}
satisfies the so
called
flow condition
, then it holds
\|u-u_{h}\|\leq Ch^{k+1}|u|_{k+1}
.
Furthermore, Cockburn et. al. in
[
3
]
relaxed the constant vector
\beta
to variable
\beta(x)
(also see
[
21
]
) by adding extra regularity condition of
u\in W^{k+1,\infty}(\Omega)
.

Recently, the WG method has attracted much attention in the
field of numerical partial differential equations

[
1
,
5
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
23
,
24
]
. This method was
introduced and analyzed originally by Wang and Ye in
[
16
]
for second order elliptic
problems. In general, a WG can be considered as an extension of the standard
finite element method or DG method where the classical derivatives are replaced in
the variational equation by the weakly defined derivatives on
discontinuous weak functions. The main feature of this method is that: (1) the
weak finite element function
u_{h}=\{u_{h}^{0},\,u_{h}^{b}\}
is used where
u_{h}^{0}
is the value of
u_{h}
in the interior of element and
u_{h}^{b}
is the value of
u_{h}
on the element boundary. Function
u_{h}^{0}
is totally discontinuous on the partition and
u_{h}^{b}
may be independent with
u_{h}^{0}
; (2)
the weak derivatives are introduced as the distributional derivative of the finite element functions; The readers are referred to articles
[
8
,
10
,
17
]

for more detailed explanation of this method and its relation with
other finite element methods. Although, WG methods have been analyzed for various partial differential equations, for example, the elliptic, parabolic, biharmonic, Stokes and Navier-Stokes equations, et.al., to authors’ best knowledge, no WG method is presented for first order hyperbolic problem (
1
) in existing literatures.

In this paper, we will present and analyze a new WG method for solving problem (
1
) using the
k
th-order polynomials. We first construct the WG scheme. Then, we study the stability and approximation properties of the WG solution. For shape regular meshes consisting of arbitrary polygon/polyhedra, we derive the
L_{2}
-error estimate for the WG solution
u_{h}=\{u_{h}^{0},u_{h}^{b}\}
:

\|u-u^{0}_{h}\|\leq Ch^{k+\frac{1}{2}}|u|_{k+1},\;k\geq 0,

(1.4)

and for a special class of meshes (see condition (
3.10
)), we derive the optimal error estimate:

\|u-u^{0}_{h}\|\leq Ch^{k+1}|u|_{k+1},\;k\geq 0.

(1.5)

Next, we consider the derivative approximation of the WG solution. By establishing a very simple derivative recovery formula for the convection directional derivative
\partial_{\beta}u=\beta\cdot\nabla u
, we obtain the superconvergence results:

\|\partial_{\beta}u-R_{h}(\partial_{\beta}u)\|\leq Ch^{k+\frac{1}{2}}|u|_{k+1},\,k\geq 0,

(1.6)

and for meshes with condition (
3.10
),

\|\partial_{\beta}u-R_{h}(\partial_{\beta}u)\|\leq Ch^{k+1}|u|_{k+1},\,k\geq 0,

(1.7)

where
R_{h}(\partial_{\beta}u)
is the recovery values of
\partial_{\beta}u
, and
R_{h}(\partial_{\beta}u)
can be computed by means of
u_{h}
explicitly, element by element. Our work provides an approach to develop the WG method for first order hyperbolic problems.

Weak Galerkin finite element scheme

Consider problem (
1
). For well-posedness (see, e.g.,
[
4
]
), we assume that
\beta\in[W^{1}_{\infty}(\Omega)]^{d},\,\alpha\in L_{\infty}(\Omega),\,f\in L_{2}(\Omega),\,g\in L_{2}(\partial\Omega_{-})
, and

\displaystyle\alpha+\frac{1}{2}\hbox{div}\beta=\sigma(x)\geq\sigma_{0}>0,\;\,x\in\Omega\,.

(2.1)

Let
T_{h}=\bigcup\{K\}
be a partition of
domain
\Omega
that consists of arbitrary polygons/polyhedra, where the mesh size
h=\max\,h_{K}
,
h_{K}
is the diameter of element
K
. Assume that the partition
T_{h}
is shape regular defined by a set of conditions given in
[
17
]
.

First, let us recall the concepts of weak function and weak divergence (see, e.g.,
[
16
,
17
]
) which will then be employed to derive a weak Galerkin finite element scheme for problem (
1
).
A weak function on element
K
refers to a function
v=\{v^{0},v^{b}\}
with
v^{0}=v|_{K}\in L_{2}(K)
and
v^{b}=v|_{\partial K}\in L_{2}(\partial K)
. Note that for
a weak function
v=\{v^{0},v^{b}\}
,
v^{b}
may not be necessarily the trace of
v^{0}
on element boundary
\partial K
.

Introduce the weak Galerkin finite element spaces on partition
T_{h}
:

\displaystyle V_{h}=\{v=\{v^{0},v^{b}\}:\;v^{0}|_{K}\in P_{k}(K),\,v^{b}|_{e}\in P_{k}(e),\,e\subset\partial K,\;K\in T_{h}\},\;k\geq 0,

\displaystyle V_{h}^{0}=\{v=\{v^{0},v^{b}\}\in V_{h}:\,v^{b}|_{\partial\Omega_{-}}=0\},

where
P_{k}(D)
is the space composed of all polynomials on a set
D
with degree no more than

k
. We emphasize that in space
V_{h}
,
v^{b}
is set to be single valued on each edge/face
e
. On the other hand, the component
v^{0}
is defined element-wise and completely discontinuous on
T_{h}
. In a certain sense, a weak finite element function
v=\{v^{0},v^{b}\}\in V_{h}
is
formed with its components inside all elements glued together by its components on all edges.

For given
v=\{v^{0},v^{b}\}\in V_{h}
, we define a
weak divergence

\nabla_{w}\cdot(\beta v)|_{K}\in P_{k}(K)
related to
\beta
on
K\in T_{h}
as the unique solution of the following equation:

\int_{K}\nabla_{w}\cdot(\beta v)qdx=-\int_{K}v^{0}\beta\cdot\nabla qdx+\int_{\partial K}\beta\cdot{\bf n}v^{b}qds,\;\forall\,q\in P_{k}(K),\;K\in T_{h}.

(2.2)

Given a function
u
with sufficient regularity, one can find an approximation to
u
by either interpolation or projection in a standard finite element space. In the WG space
V_{h}
, we will use a locally defined projection of
u
as its basic approximation. Specifically, let
Q_{0}:u\in L_{2}(K)\rightarrow Q_{0}u\in P_{k}(K)

be the local
L_{2}
projection operator such that

(u-Q_{0}u,q)_{K}=0,\;\forall\,q\in P_{k}(K),\,K\in T_{h}.

(2.3)

It can be shown that
[
21
, Theorem 1.1]

\|u-Q_{0}u\|_{s,K}\leq Ch_{K}^{m-s}\|u\|_{m,K},\;0\leq s\leq m\leq k+1.

(2.4)

The
L_{2}
projection operator
Q_{b}:L_{2}(e)\rightarrow P_{k}(e),\,e\subset\partial K
can be similarly defined on the edges of element

K\in T_{h}
. Now, we define a projection operator
Q_{h}:u\in H^{1}(\Omega)\rightarrow Q_{h}u\in V_{h}
by its action on each element
K
such that

Q_{h}u|_{K}=\{Q_{0}u,Q_{b}u\},\;K\in T_{h}.

(2.5)

Let
\partial\Omega_{\pm}=\{x\in\partial\Omega:\,\pm\beta(x)\cdot{\bf n}(x)>0\}
and
\partial K_{\pm}=\{x\in\partial K:\,\pm\beta(x)\cdot{\bf n}(x)>0\}
. Then,
\partial\Omega_{+}
(
\partial\Omega_{-}
) is called the outflow (inflow) boundary of domain
\Omega
with respect to vector
\beta
, similarly,
\partial K_{+}\,(\partial K_{-})
is called the outflow (inflow) boundary of element
K
. Furthermore, a face
e
is called the outflow (inflow) face of element
K
if
e\subset\partial K_{+}\;(\partial K_{-})
. Denote the sets
\partial T_{h}=\bigcup\{\partial K:K\in T_{h}\}
and
\partial_{\pm}T_{h}=\bigcup\{\partial K_{\pm}:K\in T_{h}\}
. For simplicity, we use the following notations,

\displaystyle(w,v)_{T_{h}}=\sum_{K\in T_{h}}(w,v)_{K},\;\;\;\langle w,v\rangle_{\partial T_{h}}=\sum_{K\in T_{h}}\langle w,v\rangle_{\partial K}=\sum_{K\in T_{h}}\int_{\partial K}wvds,

\displaystyle\langle w,v\rangle_{\partial_{\pm}T_{h}}=\sum_{K\in T_{h}}\langle w,v\rangle_{\partial K_{\pm}}=\sum_{K\in T_{h}}\int_{\partial K_{\pm}}wvds,\;\;\langle w,v\rangle_{\partial\Omega_{\pm}}=\int_{\partial\Omega_{\pm}}wvds.

Introduce the bilinear form for
w=\{w^{0},w^{b}\},v=\{v^{0},v^{b}\}\in V_{h}
,

a(w,v)=(\nabla_{w}\cdot(\beta w),v^{0})_{T_{h}}+(\alpha w^{0},v^{0})_{T_{h}}+s(w,v),

(2.6)

where the stabilizer

s(w,v)=\langle\beta\cdot{\bf n}(w^{0}-w^{b}),v^{0}-v^{b}\rangle_{\partial_{+}T_{h}}.

Weak Galerkin Method
: a weak Galerkin finite element approximation for problem (
1
) is to find
u_{h}=\{u_{h}^{0},u_{h}^{b}\}\in V_{h}
with
u_{h}^{b}|_{\partial\Omega_{-}}=Q_{b}g
such that

a(u_{h},v)=(f,v^{0})_{T_{h}},\;\forall\,v=\{v^{0},v^{b}\}\in V^{0}_{h}.

(2.7)

Remark 2.1
It should be pointed out that when
\beta\cdot{\bf n}=0
on some edge/face

u_{h}^{b}|_{e}
. In this case, we will eliminate the degree of freedom
v^{b}|_{e}
in space
V_{h}
or the corresponding basis functions on edge

in space
V_{h}
.

Lemma 2.1.

For
v=\{v^{0},v^{b}\}\in V_{h}^{0}
, it holds

a(v,v)=(\sigma v^{0},v^{0})_{T_{h}}+\frac{1}{2}\langle|\beta\cdot{\bf n}|(v^{0}-v^{b}),v^{0}-v^{b}\rangle_{\partial T_{h}}+\frac{1}{2}\langle|\beta\cdot{\bf n}|v^{b},v^{b}\rangle_{\partial\Omega_{+}}.

(2.8)

Proof.
By definition (
2.2
) and the Green’s formula, we have for
w,v\in V_{h}^{0}
,

\displaystyle(\nabla_{w}\cdot(\beta w),v^{0})_{K}=-(w^{0},\beta\cdot\nabla v^{0})_{K}+\langle\beta\cdot{\bf n}w^{b},v^{0}\rangle_{\partial K}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\nabla\cdot\beta w^{0},v^{0})_{K}+(\beta\cdot\nabla w^{0},v^{0})_{K}-\langle\beta\cdot{\bf n}w^{0},v^{0}\rangle_{\partial K}+\langle\beta\cdot{\bf n}w^{b},v^{0}\rangle_{\partial K}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\nabla\cdot\beta w^{0},v^{0})_{K}-(\nabla_{w}\cdot(\beta v),w^{0})_{K}+\langle\beta\cdot{\bf n}v^{b},w^{0}\rangle_{\partial K}-\langle\beta\cdot{\bf n}(w^{0}-w^{b}),v^{0}\rangle_{\partial K}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\nabla\cdot\beta w^{0},v^{0})_{K}-(\nabla_{w}\cdot(\beta v),w^{0})_{K}-\langle\beta\cdot{\bf n}(w^{0}-w^{b}),v^{0}-v^{b}\rangle_{\partial K}+\langle\beta\cdot{\bf n}v^{b},w^{b}\rangle_{\partial K}.

Which implies

(\nabla_{w}\cdot(\beta v),v^{0})_{T_{h}}=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\cdot\beta v^{0},v^{0})_{T_{h}}-\frac{1}{2}\langle\beta\cdot{\bf n}(v^{0}-v^{b}),v^{0}-v^{b}\rangle_{\partial T_{h}}+\frac{1}{2}\langle\beta\cdot{\bf n}v^{b},v^{b}\rangle_{\partial\Omega_{+}},

where we have used the fact that for
v\in V^{0}_{h}
,
v^{b}
is single valued on each face
e\subset\partial K
and
v^{b}|_{\partial\Omega_{-}}=0
such that
\langle\beta\cdot{\bf n}v^{b},v^{b}\rangle_{\partial T_{h}}=\langle\beta\cdot{\bf n}v^{b},v^{b}\rangle_{\partial\Omega_{+}}
. Hence, it yields from (
2.6
) that

\displaystyle a(v,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\sigma v^{0},v^{0})_{T_{h}}-\frac{1}{2}\langle\beta\cdot{\bf n}(v^{0}-v^{b}),v^{0}-v^{b}\rangle_{\partial T_{h}}+\frac{1}{2}\langle\beta\cdot{\bf n}v^{b},v^{b}\rangle_{\partial\Omega_{+}}+s(v,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\sigma v^{0},v^{0})_{T_{h}}-\frac{1}{2}\langle\beta\cdot{\bf n}(v^{0}-v^{b}),v^{0}-v^{b}\rangle_{\partial_{-}T_{h}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\langle\beta\cdot{\bf n}(v^{0}-v^{b}),v^{0}-v^{b}\rangle_{\partial_{+}T_{h}}+\frac{1}{2}\langle\beta\cdot{\bf n}v^{b},v^{b}\rangle_{\partial\Omega_{+}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\sigma v^{0},v^{0})_{T_{h}}+\frac{1}{2}\langle|\beta\cdot{\bf n}|(v^{0}-v^{b}),v^{0}-v^{b}\rangle_{\partial T_{h}}+\frac{1}{2}\langle|\beta\cdot{\bf n}|v^{b},v^{b}\rangle_{\partial\Omega_{+}}.

The proof is completed.

\Box

Introduce the norm notation:
|||v|||=a^{\frac{1}{2}}(v,v)
, that is,

\displaystyle|||v|||^{2}=(\sigma v^{0},v^{0})_{T_{h}}+\frac{1}{2}\langle|\beta\cdot{\bf n}|(v^{0}-v^{b}),v^{0}-v^{b}\rangle_{\partial T_{h}}+\frac{1}{2}\langle|\beta\cdot{\bf n}|v^{b},v^{b}\rangle_{\partial\Omega_{+}}.\;

(2.9)

Proof.
Let

3
Error Analysis

In this section, we do the error analysis for the WG method. We will derive the supoptimal
L_{2}
-error estimates for general mesh and optimal
L_{2}
-error estimate for special mesh.

3.1
Error estimate on general meshes

For any function
u\in H^{1}(K)
, the following trace inequality holds.

\|u\|^{2}_{L_{2}(e)}\leq C\big(h_{K}^{-1}\|u\|^{2}_{0,K}+h_{K}\|\nabla u\|_{0,K}^{2}\big),\;e\subset\partial K,\,K\in T_{h}.

(3.1)

Lemma 3.1.

Let
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
. Then it holds true

(\nabla\cdot(\beta u),v^{0})=(\nabla_{w}\cdot(\beta Q_{h}u),v^{0})_{T_{h}}-l_{1}(u,v)+l_{2}(u,v),\,\forall\,v=\{v^{0},v^{b}\}\in V_{h}^{0},

(3.2)

where

\displaystyle l_{1}(u,v)=(u-Q_{0}u,\beta\cdot\nabla v^{0})_{T_{h}},

\displaystyle l_{2}(u,v)=\langle\beta\cdot{\bf n}(u-Q_{b}u),v^{0}-v^{b}\rangle_{\partial T_{h}}+\langle\beta\cdot{\bf n}(u-Q_{b}u),v^{b}\rangle_{\partial\Omega_{+}}.

Proof.
From the Green’s formula, definition (
2.5
) of projection
Q_{h}
and definition (
2.2
) of the weak divergence, we have

\displaystyle(\nabla\cdot(\beta u),v^{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(u,\beta\cdot\nabla v^{0})_{T_{h}}+\langle\beta\cdot{\bf n}u,v^{0}\rangle_{\partial T_{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(Q_{0}u,\beta\cdot\nabla v^{0})_{T_{h}}-l_{1}(u,v)+\langle\beta\cdot{\bf n}Q_{b}u,v^{0}\rangle_{\partial T_{h}}-\langle\beta\cdot{\bf n}Q_{b}u,v^{0}\rangle_{\partial T_{h}}

\displaystyle+\langle\beta\cdot{\bf n}u,v^{0}-v^{b}\rangle_{\partial T_{h}}+\langle\beta\cdot{\bf n}u,v^{b}\rangle_{\partial T_{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\nabla_{w}\cdot(\beta Q_{h}u),v^{0})_{T_{h}}-l_{1}(u,v)+\langle\beta\cdot{\bf n}(u-Q_{b}u),v^{0}\rangle_{\partial T_{h}}

\displaystyle-\langle\beta\cdot{\bf n}(u-Q_{b}u),v^{b}\rangle_{\partial T_{h}}+\langle\beta\cdot{\bf n}(u-Q_{b}u),v^{b}\rangle_{\partial T_{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\nabla_{w}\cdot(\beta Q_{h}u),v^{0})_{T_{h}}-l_{1}(u,v)+l_{2}(u,v),

where we have used the fact
v^{b}|_{\partial\Omega_{-}}=0
.

\Box

Using Lemma 3.1, equation (
1
) and definition (
2.6
) of
a(u,v)
, we immediately obtain the following result.

Lemma 3.2.

Let
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
be the solution of problem (

a(Q_{h}u,v)=(f,v^{0})_{T_{h}}+l_{1}(u,v)-l_{2}(u,v)+l_{3}(u,v)+s(Q_{h}u,v),\;\forall\,v\in V_{h}^{0},

(3.3)

where
l_{3}(u,v)=(\alpha(Q_{0}u-u),v^{0})_{T_{h}}
.

Proof.
From equation (
1
) and Lemma 3.1, we obtain

\displaystyle(\nabla_{w}\cdot(\beta Q_{h}u),v^{0})_{T_{h}}+(\alpha u,v^{0})_{T_{h}}-l_{1}(u,v)+l_{2}(u,v)=(f,v^{0})_{T_{h}},

or

\displaystyle(\nabla_{w}\cdot(\beta Q_{h}u),v^{0})_{T_{h}}+(\alpha Q_{0}u,v^{0})_{T_{h}}+s(Q_{h}u,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(f,v^{0})_{T_{h}}+l_{1}(u,v)-l_{2}(u,v)+(\alpha(Q_{0}u-u),v^{0})_{T_{h}}+s(Q_{h}u,v).

Combining this with the definition (
2.6
) of

, the proof is completed.

\Box

Now, we are in the position to derive the error estimate for the WG solution
u_{h}
.

Theorem 3.1.

Assume that
T_{h}
is a shape regular partition and let
u\in H^{k+1}(\Omega)
(
k\geq 0
) and
u_{h}\in V_{h}
be the solutions of problems (
1
) and the WG equation (
2.7
), respectively. Then, the following error estimate holds.

|||Q_{h}u-u_{h}|||\leq Ch^{k+\frac{1}{2}}|u|_{k+1},\;k\geq 0.

(3.4)

Proof.
Denote the error function by
e_{h}=Q_{h}u-u_{h}\in V_{h}^{0}
. From equation (
2.7
) and (
3.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle a(e_{h},v)=l_{1}(u,v)-l_{2}(u,v)+l_{3}(u,v)+s(Q_{h}u,v),\;v\in V_{h}^{0}.

(3.5)

Below we estimate terms
l_{i}(u,v)
(
i=1,2,3
) and
s(Q_{h},v)
. It follows from the definition of
Q_{0}
and the finite element inverse inequality:
\|\nabla v^{0}\|_{0,K}\leq Ch^{-1}_{K}\|v^{0}\|_{0,K}
,

\displaystyle|l_{1}(u,v)+l_{3}(u,v)|=|(u-Q_{0}u,(\beta-\beta^{c})\cdot\nabla v^{0})_{T_{h}}+(\alpha(u-Q_{0}u),v^{0})_{T_{h}}|

(3.6)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{K\in T_{h}}h_{K}^{k+1}|u|_{k+1,K}h_{K}\|\nabla v^{0}\|_{0,K}+Ch^{k+1}|u|_{k+1}\|v^{0}\|\leq Ch^{k+1}|u|_{k+1}|||v|||,\ \ \ \ \ \

where
\beta^{c}
is the piecewise constant approximation of
\beta
on
T_{h}
. Next, it follows from the definition of
Q_{b}
,

\|u-Q_{b}u\|_{L_{2}(e)}^{2}=\int_{e}(u-Q_{b}u)(u-Q_{0}u)ds\leq\|u-Q_{b}u\|_{L_{2}(e)}\|u-Q_{0}u\|_{L_{2}(e)},\,e\subset\partial K,

which implies
\|u-Q_{b}u\|_{L_{2}(e)}\leq\|u-Q_{0}u\|_{L_{2}(e)}
. Then, we have by using the trace inequality,

\displaystyle|l_{2}(u,v)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\beta\cdot{\bf n}(u-Q_{b}u),v^{0}-v^{b}\rangle_{\partial T_{h}}+\langle\beta\cdot{\bf n}(u-Q_{b}u),v^{b}\rangle_{\partial\Omega_{+}}

(3.7)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(|\beta|_{\infty}\sum_{K\in T_{h}}\|u-Q_{0}u\|^{2}_{0,\partial K}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}|||v|||\leq Ch^{k+\frac{1}{2}}|u|_{k+1}|||v|||,

and

\displaystyle s(Q_{h}u,v)=\langle\beta\cdot{\bf n}(Q_{0}u-u+u-Q_{b}u),v^{0}-v^{b}\rangle_{\partial_{+}T_{h}}

(3.8)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{K\in T_{h}}\|\,|\beta\cdot{\bf n}|^{\frac{1}{2}}(Q_{0}u-u+u-Q_{b}u)\|_{0,\partial K}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{K\in T_{h}}\|\,|\beta\cdot{\bf n}|^{\frac{1}{2}}(v^{0}-v^{b})\|_{0,\partial K}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{K\in T_{h}}2|\beta|_{\infty}\|Q_{0}u-u\|_{0,\partial K}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,|||v|||\leq Ch^{k+\frac{1}{2}}|u|_{k+1}|||v|||.

Substituting (
3.6
)–(
3.8
) into (
3.5
), it yields

|a(e_{h},v)|\leq Ch^{k+\frac{1}{2}}|u|_{k+1}|||v|||,\;v\in V_{h}^{0}.

Taking
v=e_{h}\in V_{h}^{0}
and using Lemma 2.1, it yields

|||e_{h}|||^{2}=a(e_{h},e_{h})\leq Ch^{k+\frac{1}{2}}|u|_{k+1}|||e_{h}|||.

The proof is completed.

\Box

From Theorem 3.1 and the triangle inequality, we immediately obtain the supoptimal
L_{2}
-error estimate,

\|u-u_{h}^{0}\|\leq Ch^{k+\frac{1}{2}}|u|_{k+1},\;k\geq 0.

(3.9)

3.2
Error estimate on special meshes

In this subsection, we consider the optimal
L_{2}
-error estimate of the WG solution under the special mesh condition.

Denote by
\mathcal{E}_{h}
the union of all element edges in
T_{h}
and set its subset

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{0}_{h}=\{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}:\hbox{there exists point $x_{0}\in e\subset\partial K$ such that $|\beta\cdot{\bf n}(x_{0})|\leq h_{K}$}\}

Obviously, if
x_{0}\in e
is such that
\beta(x_{0})=0
or vector
\beta(x_{0})
is (almost) parallel to edge
e
, then
e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}
holds true.

It is possible that a face
e
is both an outflow face and a face in
\mathcal{E}^{0}_{h}
.

Now, we introduce the following mesh condition for partition
T_{h}
:

\displaystyle\hbox{Each element $K$ has at most one outflow face $e_{K}^{+}$ in set $\partial K\backslash\mathcal{E}^{0}_{h}$}.\;\;\;\;

(3.10)

Fig.2 shows a triangulation satisfying the mesh condition (
3.10
).

Fig. 2.
The streamlines of

\boldsymbol{\beta}=(x,y)
(left) and the actual mesh satisfying condition (
3.10
)(right).

Note that condition (
3.10
) has no restriction on the number of outflow faces of each element
K
, some extra outflow faces (if exist) can be in
\mathcal{E}^{0}_{h}
.

Remark 3.1
A requirement in the mesh condition (
flow condition
) given in
[
2
,
3
]
for the DG method is that: "Each interior outflow face
e^{+}_{K}
is included in an inflow face of another element". This requirement implies that no hanging points are allowed on interior outflow faces. Condition (
3.10
) has no such requirement and allows the partition with hanging points on outflow/inflow faces.

Remark 3.2
Let
\mathcal{E}_{h}^{+}=\{e_{K}\subset\partial K_{+}:\hbox{element $K$ has at least two outflow faces}\}
and
\mathcal{E}_{h}^{c}=\mathcal{E}_{h}^{+}/\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}
. The
flow condition
in
[
3
]
also contains the following requirement:

\sum_{e_{K}\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{c}}|e_{K}|^{\frac{d-2}{d-1}}\leq C\,.

(3.11)

When
\mathcal{E}_{h}^{c}\not=\emptyset
, using (
3.11
) and the additional assumption of
u\in W^{k+1,\infty}(\Omega)
, article
[
3
]
derived the
(k+1)
th-order convergence. Under conditions of (
3.10
)-(
3.11
) and
u\in W^{k+1,\infty}(\Omega)
, we also can derive the same result. However, it is easy to make a partition to satisfy
\mathcal{E}_{h}^{c}=\emptyset
. Therefore,
to show the optimality on both the convergence order and the regularity requirement (see Theorem 3.2), we have not considered the situation of
\mathcal{E}_{h}^{c}\not=\emptyset
.

To obtain the optimal error estimate, we still need to
introduce a special projection. For
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
, define the projection operator
P^{+}_{h}
, retracted on each element
K\in T_{h}
,
P_{h}^{+}u\in P_{k}(K)
such that

\displaystyle\int_{K}(u-P_{h}^{+}u)v\,dx=0,\;\;\forall\,v\in P_{k-1}(K),

(3.12)

\displaystyle\int_{e^{+}_{K}}(u-P_{h}^{+}u)v\,ds=0,\;\;\forall\,v\in P_{k}(e^{+}_{K}),

(3.13)

where condition (
3.12
) is
vacuous if
k=0
. In (
3.13
),
e^{+}_{K}\subset\partial K
is the outflow face in set
\partial_{+}T_{h}\backslash\mathcal{E}^{0}_{h}
if such face exists (see condition (
3.10
)), otherwise we can select some face
e\subset\partial K
as
e_{K}^{+}
to define the projection
P_{h}^{+}u
. This implies that all outflow faces in set
\partial_{+}T_{h}\backslash\mathcal{E}^{0}_{h}
have been selected to define
P_{h}^{+}u
in (
3.13
). Projection
P_{h}^{+}u
has been used in some
articles
[
3
,
22
]
. By the argument in
[
21
,
22
]
, we have the following result.

Lemma 3.3.

The projection function
P_{h}^{+}u
is well posed and satisfies the
approximation property

\|u-P_{h}^{+}u\|_{0,K}+h_{K}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u-P_{h}^{+}u\|_{0,\partial K}\leq Ch_{K}^{k+1}|u|_{H^{k+1}(K)},\;k\geq 0,\;\;K\in T_{h}.

(3.14)

By means of
P_{h}^{+}
, we can introduce a new projection operator
Q_{h}^{+}:\,u\in H^{1}(\Omega)\rightarrow Q_{h}^{+}u\in V_{h}
defined by

Q_{h}^{+}=\{Q_{0}^{+},Q_{b}\}\doteq\{P_{h}^{+},Q_{b}\}.

It is easy to see that all analysis maintained above to hold if
Q_{h}u
is replaced by projection
Q_{h}^{+}u
.

Lemma 3.4.

For
e\subset\partial K
, it holds that when
e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}
,

\displaystyle|\beta\cdot{\bf n}(x)|\leq|(|\beta|_{1,\infty}+1)h_{K},\;x\in e,\;e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0},

(3.15)

and when
e\not\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}
,

\displaystyle|\beta\cdot{\bf n}(x)|>h_{K},\;x\in e,\,e\not\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}.

(3.16)

Proof.
We know that when
e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}
, there exists point
x_{0}\in e
such that
|(\beta\cdot{\bf n})(x_{0})|\leq h_{K}
. Since
{\bf n}(x_{0})={\bf n}(x)
on edge
e
, we obtain

|\beta\cdot{\bf n}(x)|\leq|(\beta(x)-\beta(x_{0}))\cdot{\bf n}(x)|+|(\beta\cdot{\bf n})(x_{0})|\leq h_{K}|\beta|_{1,\infty}+h_{K},\;x\in e,

this gives (
3.15
). Estimate (
3.16
) comes from the definition of
\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}
.

\Box

Theorem 3.2.

Assume that
T_{h}
is a shape regular partition satisfying the mesh condition (
3.10
) and let
u\in H^{k+1}(\Omega)
(
k\geq 0
) and
u_{h}\in V_{h}
be the solutions of problems (
1
) and WG equation (
2.7
), respectively. Then, we have the following optimal error estimate.

\|u-u_{h}^{0}\|\leq Ch^{k+1}|u|_{k+1},\;k\geq 0.

(3.17)

Proof.
Denote the error function by
e^{+}_{h}=Q^{+}_{h}u-u_{h}\in V_{h}^{0}
. From equation (
3.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle a(e^{+}_{h},v)=l_{1}(u,v)-l_{2}(u,v)+l_{3}(u,v)+s(Q^{+}_{h}u,v),\;v\in V_{h}^{0},

(3.18)

where
l_{i}(u,v)
(
i=1,2,3
) are given by (
3.2
)–(
3.3
) in which
Q_{0}
is replaced by
Q_{0}^{+}
. From the definition of operator
Q_{0}^{+}
and the finite element inverse inequality, we have

\displaystyle|l_{1}(u,v)||=|(u-Q^{+}_{0}u,(\beta-\beta^{c})\cdot\nabla v^{0})_{T_{h}}|\leq Ch^{k+1}|u|_{k+1}|||v|||,

\displaystyle|l_{3}(u,v)|=|(\alpha(u-Q_{0}^{+}u),v^{0})_{T_{h}}|\leq Ch^{k+1}|u|_{k+1}|||v|||.

Next, we write

\displaystyle l_{2}(u,v)=\langle\beta\cdot{\bf n}(u-Q_{b}u),v^{0}-v^{b}\rangle_{\partial T_{h}}+\langle\beta\cdot{\bf n}(u-Q_{b}u),v^{b}\rangle_{\partial\Omega_{+}}=S_{1}(u,v)+S_{2}(u,v),

where

\displaystyle S_{1}(u,v)=\langle\beta\cdot{\bf n}(u-Q_{b}u),v^{0}-v^{b}\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}}+\langle\beta\cdot{\bf n}(u-Q_{b}u),v^{0}-v^{b}\rangle_{\partial T_{h}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}},

\displaystyle S_{2}(u,v)=\langle\beta\cdot{\bf n}(u-Q_{b}u),v^{b}\rangle_{\partial\Omega_{+}\bigcap\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}}+\langle\beta\cdot{\bf n}(u-Q_{b}u),v^{b}\rangle_{\partial\Omega_{+}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}}.

First, it follows from (
3.15
) that

\displaystyle\langle\beta\cdot{\bf n}(u-Q_{b}u),v^{0}-v^{b}\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}}h_{K}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u-Q_{b}u\|_{L_{2}(e)}\|\,|\beta\cdot{\bf n}|^{\frac{1}{2}}(v^{0}-v^{b})\|_{L_{2}(e)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch^{k+1}|u|_{k+1}|||v|||.

Next, it follows from the definition of
Q^{b}
and (
3.16
) that

\displaystyle\langle\beta\cdot{\bf n}(u-Q_{b}u),v^{0}-v^{b}\rangle_{\partial T_{h}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}}=\langle(\beta-\beta^{c})\cdot{\bf n}(u-Q_{b}u),v^{0}-v^{b}\rangle_{\partial T_{h}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2|\beta|_{1,\infty}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}}h_{K}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u-Q_{b}u\|_{L_{2}(e)}\|h_{K}^{\frac{1}{2}}(v^{0}-v^{b})\|_{L_{2}(e)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2|\beta|_{1,\infty}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}}h_{K}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u-Q_{b}u\|_{L_{2}(e)}\|\,|\beta\cdot{\bf n}|^{\frac{1}{2}}(v^{0}-v^{b})\|_{L_{2}(e)}\leq Ch^{k+1}|u|_{k+1}|||v|||.

Thus, it yields

S_{1}(u,v)\leq Ch^{k+1}|u|_{k+1}|||v|||.

Similarly,
S_{2}(u,v)\leq Ch^{k+1}|u|_{k+1}|||v|||
can be derived. Therefore, we obtain

|l_{2}(u,v)|\leq Ch^{k+1}|u|_{k+1}|||v|||.

Finally, we write
s(Q^{+}_{h}u,v)
as follows,

\displaystyle s(Q^{+}_{h}u,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\beta\cdot{\bf n}(Q_{0}^{+}u-Q_{b}u),v^{0}-v^{b}\rangle_{\partial_{+}T_{h}}=\langle\beta\cdot{\bf n}(Q_{0}^{+}u-Q_{b}u),v^{0}-v^{b}\rangle_{\partial_{+}T_{h}\bigcap\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}}

\displaystyle+\langle\beta\cdot{\bf n}(Q_{0}^{+}u-Q_{b}u),v^{0}-v^{b}\rangle_{\partial_{+}T_{h}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}}=R_{1}(u,v)+R_{2}(u,v).

First, it follows from (
3.14
) and (
3.15
) that

\displaystyle R_{1}(u,v)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}}h_{K}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|Q^{+}_{0}u-u+u-Q_{b}u\|_{L_{2}(e)}\|\,|\beta\cdot{\bf n}|^{\frac{1}{2}}(v^{0}-v^{b})\|_{L_{2}(e)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch^{k+1}|u|_{k+1}|||v|||\,.

Next, it follows from the definitions of operators
Q^{+}_{0}=P_{h}^{+}
and
Q_{b}
, and (
3.16
) that

\displaystyle R_{2}(u,v)=\sum_{e_{K}^{+}\in\partial_{+}T_{h}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}}\langle(\beta-\beta^{c})\cdot{\bf n}(Q_{0}^{+}u-u+u-Q_{b}u),v^{0}-v^{b}\rangle_{e_{K}^{+}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\beta|_{1,\infty}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}}h_{K}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|Q^{+}_{0}u-u+u-Q_{b}u\|_{L_{2}(e)}\|\,h_{K}^{\frac{1}{2}}(v^{0}-v^{b})\|_{L_{2}(e)}\leq Ch^{k+1}|u|_{k+1}|||v|||\,.

Together with estimate of
R_{1}(u,v)
, it yields

s(Q_{h}^{+}u,v)\leq Ch^{k+1}|u|_{k+1}|||v|||\,.

Substituting estimates of
l_{1}(u,v)-l_{3}(u,v)
and
s(Q_{h}^{+}u,v)
into (
3.18
) and taking
v=e_{h}^{+}
, we obtain

|||e^{+}_{h}|||^{2}=a(e_{h}^{+},e^{+}_{h})\leq Ch^{k+1}|u|_{k+1}|||e^{+}_{h}|||,\;k\geq 0,

(3.19)

which also implies

\|Q^{+}_{0}u-u_{h}^{0}\|\leq Ch^{k+1}|u|_{k+1},\;k\geq 0.

(3.20)

The proof is completed by using the triangle inequality and the approximation property of operator
Q^{+}_{0}=P_{h}^{+}
.

\Box

4
Recovery formula for the derivative approximation

In this section, we give the approximation to the convection directional derivative
\partial_{\beta}u=\beta\cdot\nabla u
by using a very simple derivative recovery formula.

Let
u_{h}
be the WG solution of equation (
2.7
). Define the recovery formula of derivative
\partial_{\beta}u
by

R_{h}(\partial_{\beta}u)=f-(\alpha+\nabla\cdot\beta)u_{h}^{0}.

(4.1)

Theorem 4.1.

Assume that
T_{h}
is a shape regular partition and
u\in H^{k+1}(\Omega)
(
k\geq 0
) is the solution of problem (
1
). Then, the recovery derivative
R_{h}(\partial_{\beta}u)
given by (
4.1
) admits the following superconvergence estimate.

\|\partial_{\beta}u-R_{h}(\partial_{\beta}u)\|\leq Ch^{k+\frac{1}{2}}|u|_{k+1},\;k\geq 0.

(4.2)

Moreover, if partition
T_{h}
satisfies mesh condition (
3.10
), then the following superconvergence estimate holds

\|\partial_{\beta}u-R_{h}(\partial_{\beta}u)\|\leq Ch^{k+1}|u|_{k+1},\;k\geq 0.

(4.3)

Proof.
From equations (
1
) and (
4.1
), we have

\partial_{\beta}u-R_{h}(\partial_{\beta}u)=-(\alpha+\nabla\cdot\beta)(u-u_{h}^{0}).

Hence, estimates (
4.2
) and (
4.3
) can be obtained by using (
3.9
) and (
3.17
), respectively, under different mesh conditions.

\Box

Compared with the equation to solve numerically the directional derivative
\partial_{\beta}u
in the DG method
[
3
]
, our recovery formula
R_{h}(\partial_{\beta}u)
is very simple and it can be computed explicitly, element by element.

6
Conclusion

We present and analyze a weak Galerkin finite element method for solving the typical first order hyperbolic equation: the transport-reaction equation. This method is highly flexible by allowing the use of discontinuous finite element on general meshes consisting of arbitrary polygon/polyhedra. Using the
k
th-order polynomials (
k\geq 0
), we prove that the WG solution admits the optimal
L_{2}
-convergence rate of
O(h^{k+1})
-order under special mesh condition which is slightly weaker than the
flow condition
given in
[
3
]
for the DG method solving this problem.
Moreover, a derivative recovery formula is presented to approximate the convection directional derivative and the corresponding superconvergence estimate is given. Numerical examples on compatible and non-compatible meshes are provided to show the effectiveness of this WG method. Our work provides an approach to develop the WG method for first order hyperbolic problems.

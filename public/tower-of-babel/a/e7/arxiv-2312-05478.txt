Presentations of Schur and Specht modules in characteristic zero
Mihalis Maliakas, Maria Metzaki, Dimitra-Dionysia Stergiopoulou

Abstract
New presentations of Specht modules of symmetric groups over fields of characteristic zero have been obtained by Brauner, Friedmann, Hanlon, Stanley and Wachs. These involve generators that are column tabloids and relations that are Garnir relations with maximal number of exchanges between consecutive columns or symmetrization of Garnir relations with minimal number of exchanges between consecutive columns. In this paper, we examine Garnir relations and their symmetrization with any number of exchanges. In both cases, we provide sufficient arithmetic conditions so that the corresponding quotient is a Specht module. In particular, in the first case this yields new presentations of Specht modules if the parts of the conjugate partition that correspond to maximal number of exchanges greater than 1 are distinct. These results generalize the presentations mentioned above and offer an answer to a question of Friedmann, Hanlon and Wachs. Our approach is via representations of the general linear group.

Let

For a partition

[

by replacing each entry

Given a partition

entries from column

\displaystyle A=\sum_{u,v,w}x_{u}(a_{1}+1)y_{v}(a_{2}-1)z_{w}(a_{3})\otimes x^{\prime}_{u}(b_{1}-1)y^{\prime}_{v}(b_{2}+1)z^{\prime}_{w}(b_{3}),

\displaystyle B=\sum_{u,v,w}x_{u}(a_{1}+1)y_{v}(a_{2})z_{w}(a_{3}-1)\otimes x^{\prime}_{u}(b_{1}-1)y^{\prime}_{v}(b_{2})z^{\prime}_{w}(b_{3}+1).

Hence
\pi_{\alpha}(\omega)
is equal to

(-1)^{a_{2}+a_{3}+1}\frac{1}{b_{1}}\big((-1)^{a_{3}+b_{1}-1}(b_{2}+1)\pi_{\alpha}(A)+(-1)^{b_{1}+b_{2}-1}(b_{3}+1)\pi_{\alpha}(B)\big),

(3.1)

By induction we have

\displaystyle\pi_{\alpha}(A)=

\displaystyle(-1)^{\zeta}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{2}+j_{3}=b_{1}-1\\
j_{2}\leq a_{2}-1,\;j_{3}\leq a_{3}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{\zeta_{1}}\tbinom{b_{2}+1+j_{2}}{b_{2}+1}\tbinom{b_{3}+j_{3}}{b_{3}}

\displaystyle\pi_{\alpha}\big(\sum_{v,w}xy_{v}(a_{2}-(1+j_{2}))z_{w}(a_{3}-j_{3})\otimes y^{\prime}_{v}(b_{2}+(1+j_{2}))z^{\prime}_{w}(b_{3}+j_{3})\big),

\displaystyle\pi_{\alpha}(B)=

\displaystyle(-1)^{\zeta}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{2}+j_{3}=b_{1}-1\\
j_{2}\leq a_{2},\;j_{3}\leq a_{3}-1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{\zeta_{2}}\tbinom{b_{2}+i_{2}}{b_{2}}\tbinom{b_{3}+1+j_{3}}{b_{3}+1}

\displaystyle\pi_{\alpha}\big(\sum_{v,w}xy_{v}(a_{2}-j_{2})z_{w}(a_{3}-(1+j_{3}))\otimes y^{\prime}_{v}(b_{2}+j_{2})z^{\prime}_{w}(b_{3}+(1+j_{3}))\big),

where
\zeta=(a_{2}+a_{3})(b_{1}-1)
,
\zeta_{1}=j_{2}(a_{3}-j_{3})+j_{3}(b_{2}+1)
and
\zeta_{2}=j_{2}(a_{3}-1-j_{3})+j_{3}b_{2}
.
Substituting the above expressions in (
3.1
) and computing the signs, we see that the coefficient of

\pi_{\alpha}\big(\sum_{v,w}xy_{v}(a_{2}-i_{2})z_{w}(a_{3}-i_{3})\otimes y^{\prime}_{v}(b_{2}+i_{2}))z^{\prime}_{w}(b_{3}+i_{3})\big),

where
i_{2}+i_{3}=b_{1}
, is equal to

(-1)^{\epsilon+\epsilon^{\prime}}\frac{1}{b_{1}}\big((b_{2}+1)\tbinom{b_{2}+i_{2}}{b_{2}+1}\tbinom{b_{3}+i_{3}}{b_{3}}+(b_{3}+1)\tbinom{b_{2}+i_{2}}{b_{2}}\tbinom{b_{3}+i_{3}}{b_{3}+1}\big).

But this is equal to

(-1)^{\epsilon+\epsilon^{\prime}}\tbinom{b_{2}+i_{2}}{b_{2}}\tbinom{b_{3}+i_{3}}{b_{3}}
and the desired result follows. ∎

Each map
\Phi\in\Hom_{G}(\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b},\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b})
acts as a scalar on every irreducible summand of
\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b}
. These summands are given by Pieri’s rule
[
4
, 2.2]
,
\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{b}L_{(a+i,b-i)}
. We compute the scalars in the next lemma when
\Phi=\Phi_{t}
. This is important for us as the
\Phi_{t}
, where
t\in\{0,\dots,b\}
, form a basis of the vector space
\Hom_{G}(\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b},\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b})
(see Corollary
3.9
below).

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
a,b,i,t\in\mathbb{N}
such that
a\geq b\geq i
and let
\mu=(a+i,b-i)
.

(1)
Let
i<t
. Then the restriction of
\Phi_{t}
to the summand
L_{\mu}
of
\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b}
is equal to zero.

(2)
Let
i\geq t
. Define
c_{t}(a,b,i)=\tbinom{i}{t}\tbinom{a-b+t+i}{t}
. Then the following diagram is commutative
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda^{a+i}\otimes\Lambda^{b-i}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda^{a+i}\otimes\Lambda^{b-i}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L_{\mu}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L_{\mu}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi_{t}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{\mu}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{\mu}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces c_{t}{(a,b,i)}}

where the bottom horizontal map is multiplication by
c_{t}(a,b,i)
. Moreover, if
n\geq a+b
, then the map
\pi_{\mu}\circ\delta_{i}:\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b}\to L_{\mu}
is nonzero.

Proof.

(1) By definition,
\Phi_{t}
factors through
\Lambda^{a+t}\otimes\Lambda^{b-t}
. But the irreducible module
L_{\mu}
is not a summand of
\Lambda^{a+t}\otimes\Lambda^{b-t}
if
i<t
, according to Pieri’s rule.

(2) For the first statement, it suffices to show that in the following diagram the left and right rectangles commute
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda^{a+t}\otimes\Lambda^{b-t}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda^{a+i}\otimes\Lambda^{b-i}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda^{a+i}\otimes\Lambda^{b-i}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda^{a+i}\otimes\Lambda^{b-i}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L_{\mu}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L_{\mu}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L_{\mu}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta_{t}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta_{i-t}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\theta_{t}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{\mu}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{\mu}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{\mu}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tbinom{i}{t}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tbinom{a-b+t+i}{t}}

The left rectangle commutes by the first equality of (
2.2
).

Let
x\otimes y\in\Lambda^{a+t}\otimes\Lambda^{b-t}
. In the clockwise direction we have

\displaystyle\pi_{\mu}\circ\delta_{i}\circ\theta_{t}(x\otimes y)=\pi_{\mu}\circ\delta_{i}\big(\sum_{u}x_{u}(a)\otimes x^{\prime}_{u}(t)y\big)

\displaystyle=\pi_{\mu}\big(\sum_{i_{1},i_{2}}(-1)^{(t-i_{1})i_{2}}\tbinom{a+i_{1}}{i_{1}}\sum_{u,v}x_{u}(a+i_{1})y_{v}(i_{2})\otimes x^{\prime}_{u}(t-i_{1})y^{\prime}_{v}(b-t-i_{2})\big),

where we applied (
2.1
) in the second equality. The second sum is over all
i_{1},i_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
i_{1}+i_{2}=i
and
i_{1}\leq t
. We apply Lemma
3.7
to the third sum to obtain
\pi_{\mu}\circ\delta_{i}\circ\theta_{t}(x\otimes y)
is equal to

\displaystyle\pi_{\mu}\big(\sum_{i_{1},i_{2}}(-1)^{(t-i_{1})i_{2}}\tbinom{a+i_{1}}{i_{1}}(-1)^{(i_{2}+1)(t-i_{1})}\tbinom{b-i}{t-i_{1}}\sum_{v}xy_{v}(i-t)\otimes y^{\prime}_{v}(b-i)\big)

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{i_{1}=0}^{t}(-1)^{t-i_{1}}\tbinom{a+i_{1}}{i_{1}}\tbinom{b-i}{t-i_{1}}\Big)\pi_{\mu}(\sum_{v}xy_{v}(i-t)\otimes y^{\prime}_{v}(b-i)).

By the first identity of
[
8
, Lemma 4.1(2)]
, we have
\sum_{i_{1}=0}^{t}(-1)^{t-i_{1}}\tbinom{a+i_{1}}{i_{1}}\tbinom{b-i}{t-i_{1}}=

\tbinom{a-b+t+i}{t}
. Hence
\pi_{\mu}\circ\delta_{i}\circ\theta_{t}(x\otimes y)
is equal to

\tbinom{a-b+t+i}{t}\pi_{\mu}(\sum_{v}xy_{v}(i-t)\otimes y^{\prime}_{v}(b-i))=\tbinom{a-b+t+i}{t}\pi_{\mu}\circ\delta_{i-t}(x\otimes y)

and the right rectangle commutes.

Since
n\geq a+b
, we have the element
x=e_{1}\cdots e_{a}\otimes e_{a+1}\cdots e_{a+b}\in\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b}
. From the definition of
\delta_{i}
it follows that
\pi_{\mu}\circ\delta_{i}(x)
is a sum of elements of the form
\pm\pi_{\mu}(e_{1}\cdots e_{a}u_{1}\cdots u_{i}\otimes u_{i+1}\cdots u_{b}),

where
u_{1},\dots,u_{b}\in\{e_{a+1},\dots,e_{b}\}
and
\{u_{1},\dots,u_{i}\}\cap

\{u_{i+1},\dots,u_{b}\}=\emptyset
. The number of the
u_{i+1},\dots,u_{b}
is equal to
b-i
and we have
b-i\leq a.
Thus there are no violations in the columns, meaning that the above elements are (up to sign) cosemistandard basis elements of
L_{\mu}
. They are clearly distinct. Hence we conclude that
\pi_{\mu}\circ\delta_{i}(x)
is nonzero and the map
\pi_{\mu}\circ\delta_{i}
is nonzero. ∎

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{N}
such that
a\geq b
. If
n\geq a+b
, then a basis of the vector space
\Hom_{G}(\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b},\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b})
is
\{\Phi_{0},\Phi_{1},\dots,\Phi_{b}\}
.

Proof.

By Pieri’s rule
[
4
, 2.2]
,
\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{b}L_{(a+i,b-i)}
. Since the
G
-modules
L_{(a+i,b-i)}
are irreducible and distinct, we have
\dim\Hom_{G}(\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b},\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b})=b+1.
By Lemma
3.8
it follows that
\Ima(\Phi_{t})=

\bigoplus_{i\geq t}L_{(a+i,b-i)},

t=0,\dots,b.
Hence the
\Phi_{0},\dots,\Phi_{b}
are linearly independent.
∎

Remark 3.10
.

For later use we record that
\Ima(\Phi_{i})\subseteq\Ima(\Phi_{1})\subseteq\Ima(\theta_{1})
if
i>0
.

3.2
Main result

Our main result for the case of two rows is the following, which provides an arithmetic criterion when the cokernel of
\Phi\in\Hom_{G}(\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b},\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b})
is irreducible. Recall the notation
c_{t}(a,b,i)=\tbinom{i}{t}\tbinom{a-b+t+i}{t}
from Lemma
3.8
.

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{N}
such that
a\geq b
and let
n\geq a+b
. Let
\Phi\in\Hom_{G}(\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b},\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b})
and write
\Phi=\sum_{t=0}^{b}a_{t}\Phi_{t}
, where
a_{t}\in K
. Then the cokernel of
\Phi
is isomorphic to
L_{(a+i,b-i)}
if and only if

1.

\sum_{t=0}^{i}a_{t}c_{t}(a,b,i)=0
, and

2.

\sum_{t=0}^{j}a_{t}c_{t}(a,b,j)\neq 0
for all
j\in\{0,\dots,b\}\setminus\{i\}
.

Proof.

We have
\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b}=\bigoplus_{j=0}^{b}L_{(a+j,b-j)}
. By Lemma
3.8
, the map
\Phi
acts on
L_{(a+j,b-j)}
as multiplication by
\sum_{t=0}^{j}a_{t}c_{t}(a,b,j)
. The result follows.
∎

The previous result for
i=0
may be generalized easily to the case of partitions
\mu=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{\ell})
consisting of
{\ell}\geq 2
parts. We need some notation. For
c\in\{1,\dots,{\ell}-1\}
, let
\mu(c)
be the partition
(\mu_{c},\mu_{c+1})
. If
\Psi_{\mu(c)}\in\Hom_{G}(\Lambda^{\mu(c)},\Lambda^{\mu(c)})
, let
\overline{\Psi}_{\mu(c)}\in\Hom_{G}(\Lambda^{\mu},\Lambda^{\mu})
be defined by
\overline{\Psi}_{\mu(c)}=1\otimes\cdots\otimes 1\otimes\Psi_{\mu(c)}\otimes 1\otimes\cdots\otimes 1.
Let
\{\Phi_{\mu(c),0},\dots,\Phi_{\mu(c),\mu_{c+1}}\}
be the basis of
\Hom_{G}(\Lambda^{\mu(c)},\Lambda^{\mu(c)})
given in Corollary
3.9
. There are uniquely determined
a_{\mu(c),t}\in K
such that
\Psi_{\mu(c)}=\sum_{t=0}^{\mu_{c+1}}a_{\mu(c),t}\Phi_{\mu(c),t}
. Our main result for many rows is the following. We use the notation
|\mu|=\mu_{1}+\dots+\mu_{\ell}
.

Corollary 3.12
.

With the previous notation, suppose
n\geq|\mu|
. Then the cokernel of
\sum_{c=1}^{{\ell}-1}\overline{\Psi}_{\mu(c)}:\bigoplus_{c=1}^{{\ell}-1}\Lambda^{\mu}\to\Lambda^{\mu}
is isomorphic to
L_{\mu}
if

a_{\mu(c),0}=0
and
\sum_{t=1}^{j}a_{\mu(c),t}c_{t}(\mu_{c},\mu_{c+1},j)\neq 0

for all
c=1,\dots,{\ell}-1
and
j=1,\dots,\mu_{c+1}
.

Proof.

From Remark
3.10
it follows that
\Ima(\sum_{c=1}^{{\ell}-1}\overline{\Psi}_{\mu(c)})\subseteq\Ima\theta_{\mu}
, where
\theta_{\mu}
is the map in Theorem
2.6
. Hence
\coker(\sum_{c=1}^{{\ell}-1}\overline{\Psi}_{\mu(c)})\simeq L_{\mu}
if and only if

\Ima(\sum_{c=1}^{{\ell}-1}\overline{\Psi}_{\mu(c)})=\Ima\theta_{\mu}
.

Again by Remark
3.10
,

\Ima({\Psi}_{\mu(c)})\subseteq\Ima(\Lambda^{\mu_{c}+1}\otimes\Lambda^{\mu_{c+1}-1}\xrightarrow{\theta_{1}}\Lambda^{\mu_{c}}\otimes\Lambda^{\mu_{c+1}}).

(3.2)

Under the hypothesis of the corollary, we apply Theorem
3.11
to
\coker({\Psi}_{\mu(c)})
and we conclude that in (
3.2
) we have equality. (Notice that for
i=0
in Condition 1 of Theorem
3.11
, we have
\sum_{t=0}^{i}a_{t}c_{t}(a,b,i)

=a_{0}
.) Hence
\Ima(\sum_{c=1}^{{\ell}-1}\overline{\Psi}_{\mu(c)})=\Ima\theta_{\mu}
.
∎

4
Schur modules and symmetrization of Garnir relations

Here we apply Corollary
3.12
to particular maps
\psi_{k}\in\Hom_{G}(\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b},\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b})
defined below that have an interesting combinatorial interpretation; they are obtained from the Garnir relations by a symmetrization process. As we mentioned in the Introduction, such symmetrizations was first considered in
[
1
]
for
k=1
.

Throughout this section, let
a\geq b\geq k
be positive integers.

Definition 4.13
.

1.

Define
\phi_{k}\in\Hom_{G}(\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b},\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b})
as the composition

\displaystyle\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\Delta_{a-k,k}\otimes\Delta_{k,b-k}}\Lambda^{a-k}\otimes\Lambda^{k}\otimes\Lambda^{k}\otimes\Lambda^{b-k}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{1\otimes\tau\otimes 1}\Lambda^{a-k}\otimes\Lambda^{k}\otimes\Lambda^{k}\otimes\Lambda^{b-k}\xrightarrow{m\otimes m}\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b},

where
\tau:\Lambda^{k}\otimes\Lambda^{k}\to\Lambda^{k}\otimes\Lambda^{k},\tau(w\otimes z)=z\otimes w
.

2.

Define
\psi_{k}\in\Hom_{G}(\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b},\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b})
by
\psi_{k}=\tbinom{b}{k}1_{\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b}}-\phi_{k}.

Remark 4.14
.

It is straightforward to verify that if
x\otimes y=e_{i_{1}}\cdots e_{i_{a}}\otimes e_{j_{1}}\cdots e_{j_{b}}
is a basis element of
\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b}
, then
\phi_{k}(x\otimes y)
is obtained by exchanging any
k
elements
x_{1}<x_{2}<\dots<x_{k}
of
\{e_{i_{1}},\dots,e_{i_{a}}\}
with any
k
elements
y_{1}<y_{2}<\dots<y_{k}
of
\{e_{j_{1}},\dots,e_{j_{b}}\}
and putting the
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}
(respectively,
y_{1},\dots,y_{k}
) in the original positions of the
y_{1},\dots,y_{k}
(respectively,
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}
).

In terms of the basis of Corollary
3.9
, we have the following expressions.

Lemma 4.15
.

With the previous notation,

\phi_{k}=\sum_{t=0}^{k}(-1)^{t}\tbinom{b-t}{k-t}\Phi_{t}\;\;\text{and}\;\;\psi_{k}=\sum_{t=1}^{k}(-1)^{t-1}\tbinom{b-t}{k-t}\Phi_{t}.

Proof.

Let
x\otimes y\in\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b}
. From the definitions we have that
\Phi_{t}(x\otimes y)=

\theta_{t}\circ\delta_{t}(x\otimes y)=

\theta_{t}\big(\sum_{v}xy_{v}(t)\otimes y^{\prime}_{v}(b-t)\big)
. Using (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), this is equal to

\sum_{i=0}^{t}(-1)^{\epsilon}\tbinom{b-t+i}{i}\sum_{u,v}x_{u}(a-t+i)y_{v}(t-i)\otimes x^{\prime}_{u}(t-i)y^{\prime}_{v}(b-t+i),

where
\epsilon=(t-i)^{2}
. Hence

\Phi_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{t}(-1)^{t-i}\tbinom{b-t+i}{i}\phi_{t-i}=\sum_{k=0}^{t}(-1)^{k}\tbinom{b-k}{b-t}\phi_{k},

(4.1)

where in the last equality we set
k=t-i
. Consider the
(b+1)\times(b+1)
matrix
A=(a_{kt})
, where
a_{kt}=(-1)^{k}\tbinom{b-k}{b-t}
and
0\leq k,t\leq b
. From the binomial identity
\sum_{j=0}^{b}(-1)^{k+j}\tbinom{b-k}{b-j}\tbinom{b-j}{b-t}=\delta_{k,t}
, see
[
9
, p. 4]
, where
\delta_{k,t}
is Kronecker’s delta, it follows that
A^{2}=I
, the identity matrix. Hence we may invert (
4.1
) to obtain
\phi_{k}=\sum_{t=0}^{k}(-1)^{t}\tbinom{b-t}{b-k}\Phi_{t}
as desired.

The second equality follows from the first and Definition
4.13
.
∎

From Lemma
4.15
and Theorem
3.11
(for
i=0
) we obtain:

Corollary 4.16
.

Let
n\geq a+b
. Then
\coker(\psi_{k})\simeq L_{(a,b)}
if and only if

\sum_{t=1}^{j}(-1)^{t-1}\tbinom{b-t}{b-k}\tbinom{j}{t}\tbinom{a-b+j+t}{t}\neq 0

for all
j=1,\dots,b
.

5
Schur modules and Garnir relations

Our main goal here is to obtain an analog of Corollary
4.16
for maps
\gamma_{k}
(in place of
\psi_{k}
) that correspond to Garnir relations. This is accomplished in Corollary
5.20
below. Except for Lemma 3.1, the present section is independent of the previous two. However, the main ideas and techniques are similar.

Throughout this section, let
a\geq b\geq k
be positive integers.

Definition 5.17
.

1.

Define
\beta_{k}\in\Hom_{G}(\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{k}\otimes\Lambda^{b-k},\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b})
as the composition

\displaystyle\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{k}\otimes\Lambda^{b-k}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\Delta_{a-k,k}\otimes 1}\Lambda^{a-k}\otimes\Lambda^{k}\otimes\Lambda^{k}\otimes\Lambda^{b-k}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{1\otimes\tau\otimes 1}\Lambda^{a-k}\otimes\Lambda^{k}\otimes\Lambda^{k}\otimes\Lambda^{b-k}\xrightarrow{m\otimes m}\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b},

2.

Define
\gamma_{k}\in\Hom_{G}(\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{k}\otimes\Lambda^{b-k},\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b})

by

\gamma_{k}(x\otimes y\otimes z)=x\otimes yz-\beta_{k}(x\otimes y\otimes z).

We will need the following lemma.

Lemma 5.18
.

We have
\Ima(\gamma_{k})\subseteq\Ima(\theta_{1})
, where
\theta_{1}:\Lambda^{a+1}\otimes\Lambda^{b-1}\to\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b}
is given in Definition
2.5
.

Proof.

Consider the composition

\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{k}\otimes\Lambda^{b-k}\xrightarrow{\gamma_{k}}\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b}\xrightarrow{\pi_{\mu}}L_{\mu}

where
\mu=(a,b)
, and let
x\otimes y\otimes z\in\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{k}\otimes\Lambda^{b-k}
. We have

\pi_{\mu}\circ\gamma_{k}(x\otimes y\otimes z)=\pi_{\mu}(x\otimes yz)-\big(\sum_{u}x^{\prime}_{u}(a-k)y\otimes x^{\prime}_{u}(k)z\big)=0,

where the second equality follows from Lemma
3.7
. Hence
\pi_{\mu}\circ\gamma_{k}=0.
By Theorem
2.6
we have
\Ima(\gamma_{k})\subseteq\ker(\pi_{\mu})=\Ima(\theta_{1}).

∎

For the proof of the next lemma we will need the following
remark. Recall we have
a\geq b\geq k\geq 1
. Let
b\geq i\geq 0
and define

B(a,b,k,i)=\{(i_{1},i_{2})\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}:i_{1}+i_{2}=i,\;i_{1}\leq k,\;i_{2}\leq b-k\}.

It is straightforward to verify that

B(a,b,k,i)=\{(t,i-t)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}:p\leq t\leq q\},

(5.1)

where
p=i-\min\{b-k,i\}
and
q=\min\{k,i\}
.

Lemma 5.19
.

Suppose
n\geq a+b.
Define

d_{k}(a,b,t)=1-\sum_{j=p}^{t}(-1)^{j}\tbinom{a-k+j}{a-k}\tbinom{b-k-i+t}{t-j}

for
t=p,p+1,\dots,q
. Then
\coker(\gamma_{k})\simeq L_{(a,b)}
if and only if for every
i
such that
1\leq i\leq b
, there exists
t
such that
p\leq t\leq q
and
d_{k}(a,b,t)\neq 0
.

Proof.

Consider the composition

\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{k}\otimes\Lambda^{b-k}\xrightarrow{\gamma_{k}}\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b}\xrightarrow{\delta_{i}}\Lambda^{a+i}\otimes\Lambda^{b-i}\xrightarrow{\pi_{\mu}}L_{\mu},

where
\mu=(a+i,b-i)
, and let
x\otimes y\otimes z\in\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{k}\otimes\Lambda^{b-k}
. A quick computation using the definitions of the maps (
2.1
) and (
5.1
) yields

\pi_{\mu}\circ\delta_{i}\circ\gamma_{k}(x\otimes y\otimes z)=\sum_{j=p}^{q}\eta_{j}T(j)-\sum_{j=p}^{q}\epsilon_{j}\tbinom{a-k+j}{a-k}A(j),

(5.2)

where
\eta_{t}=(-1)^{(k-t)(i-t)}
,
\epsilon_{j}=(-1)^{jp+(i-j)(k-j)}
and

\displaystyle T(j)=\pi_{\mu}\big(\sum_{v,w}xy_{v}(j)z_{w}(i-j)\otimes y^{\prime}_{v}(k-j)z^{\prime}_{w}(b-k-i+j)\big),

\displaystyle A(j)=\pi_{\mu}\big(\sum_{u,w}x_{u}(a-k+j)yz_{w}(i-j)\otimes x^{\prime}_{u}(k-j)z^{\prime}_{w}(b-k-i+j)\big).

We apply Lemma
3.7
to
A(j)
to obtain

A(j)=\zeta_{j}\sum_{t=j}^{q}\eta_{t}\tbinom{b-k-i+t}{t-j}T(t).

Substituting this in (
5.2
) and noting that
\epsilon_{j}\zeta_{j}=(-1)^{j}
, we conclude that

\pi_{\mu}\circ\delta_{i}\circ\gamma_{k}(x\otimes y\otimes z)=\sum_{t=p}^{q}d_{k}(a,b,t)\eta_{t}T(t).

(5.3)

Now since
n\geq a+b
, we may consider the choice
x\otimes y\otimes z=e_{1}\cdots e_{a}\otimes e_{a+1}\cdots e_{a+k}\otimes e_{a+k+1}\cdots e_{a+b}\in\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{k}\otimes\Lambda^{b-k}
. Since
a\geq b-i
, it follows from the definition of
T(j)
above, that this choice has the property that
T(p),\dots,T(q)
are distinct cosemistandard basis elements of
L_{(a+i,b-i)}
(see the last paragraph of Section 2). By Pieri’s rule we know that
\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b}
contains a unique copy of
L_{(a+i,b-i)}
, for each
1\leq i\leq b
. Hence from (
5.3
) we conclude that the image
\Ima(\gamma_{k})
of the map
\gamma_{k}
intersects non trivially the copy of
L_{(a+i,b-i)}
inside
\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b}
if an only if one of the integers
d_{k}(a,b,t)\eta_{t}
, where
t=p,\dots,q
, is nonzero. Since
\eta_{t}=\pm 1
, this is equivalent to one of the integers
d_{k}(a,b,t)
being nonzero.

In order to complete the proof, it remains to be shown that
\Ima(\gamma_{k})
does not intersect the (unique) copy of
L_{(a,b)}
inside
\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b}
. Consider the composition

\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{k}\otimes\Lambda^{b-k}\xrightarrow{\gamma_{k}}\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b}\xrightarrow{\pi_{(a,b)}}L_{(a,b)}.

In the proof of Lemma
5.18
we saw that
\pi_{(a,b)}\circ\gamma_{k}=0.

∎

Next we observe that we may obtain a better version of the previous lemma, meaning that we may avoid the use of the expressions
d_{k}(a,b,t)
, if we are interested not on the action on irreducible summands but only when
\coker(\gamma_{k})\simeq L_{(a,b)}
holds.

Corollary 5.20
.

Suppose
n\geq a+b
. Then
\coker(\gamma_{k})\simeq L_{(a,b)}
if and only if
a>k
or
a=b=k=1
.

Proof.

With the notation of the previous lemma, let
1\leq i\leq b
.

(1) Suppose
a>k
. Let
p\neq 0
. By definition,
d_{k}(a,b,p)=1-(-1)^{p}\tbinom{a-k+p}{a-k}
. Since
a>k
we have
\tbinom{a-k+p}{a-k}\neq 1
and hence
d_{k}(a,b,p)\neq 0
.

Let
p=0
. Since
q=\min\{k,i\}\geq 1
, we may consider
d_{k}(a,b,t)
for
t=1
. By definition,
d_{k}(a,b,1)=1-\tbinom{a-k}{a-k}\tbinom{b-k-i+1}{1}+\tbinom{a-k+1}{a-k}\tbinom{b-k-i+1}{0}=1+a-b+i.
Since
a\geq b
, we have
d_{k}(a,b,1)\neq 0.

(2) Suppose
a=b=k=1
. Then
a=b=k=i=p=q=t=1
and
d_{1}(a,b,1)=1-(-a)=2>0
.

So for any
1\leq i\leq b
we have found a nonzero
d_{k}(a,b,t)
for some
p\leq t\leq q
. By Lemma
5.19
,
\coker(\gamma_{k})\simeq L_{(a,b)}
.

Conversely, suppose
a=b=k>1
. We will show that
\coker(\gamma_{k})
and
L_{(a,b)}
are not isomorphic. Indeed, we have
p=q=i
and thus
t
is uniquely determined,
t=i
. Choosing
i=2
, we have
d_{k}(a,b,t)=1-(-1)^{2}=0
. By Lemma
5.19
,
\coker(\gamma_{k})
and
L_{(a,b)}
are not isomorphic. ∎

We note that according to the previous corollary, we have
\coker(\gamma_{k})\simeq L_{(a,b)}
for all
a\geq b\geq k\geq 1
except when
a=b=k>1
. In this exceptional case, we have
\coker(\gamma_{k})\simeq S_{2}(\Lambda^{k})
, the second symmetric power of
\Lambda^{k}
.

In order to state the main result of this section, that involves partitions with more parts, we need some notation. Let
\mu=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{\ell})
be a partition of length
\ell\geq 2
and let
k_{1},\dots,k_{\ell-1}
positive integers satisfying
k_{c}\leq\mu_{c+1}
,
c=1,\dots,\ell-1
.
Let
\mu(c)
be the partition
(\mu_{c},\mu_{c+1})
and let

\gamma_{k_{c}}\in\Hom_{G}(\Lambda^{\mu_{c}}\otimes\Lambda^{k_{c}}\otimes\Lambda^{\mu_{c+1}-k_{c}},\Lambda^{\mu_{c}}\otimes\Lambda^{\mu_{c+1}})

be the map of Definition
5.17
(2). Define

\overline{\gamma}_{k_{c}}\in\Hom_{G}(\Lambda^{\mu_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\Lambda^{\mu_{c}}\otimes\Lambda^{k_{c}}\otimes\Lambda^{\mu_{c+1}-k_{c}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\Lambda^{\mu_{\ell}},\Lambda^{\mu})

by
\overline{\gamma}_{k_{c}}=1\otimes\cdots\otimes 1\otimes\gamma_{k_{c}}\otimes 1\otimes\cdots\otimes 1.

Theorem 5.21
.

Let
\mu=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{\ell})
be a partition of length
\ell\geq 2
and let
k_{1},\dots,k_{\ell-1}
positive integers satisfying
k_{c}\leq\mu_{c+1}
,
c=1,\dots,\ell-1
. Suppose
n\geq|\mu|
. If
\mu_{c}>\mu_{c+1}
for those
c
such that
k_{c}=\mu_{c+1}>1
, then the cokernel of

\sum_{c=1}^{{\ell}-1}\overline{\gamma}_{k_{c}}:\bigoplus_{c=1}^{{\ell}-1}\Lambda^{\mu_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\Lambda^{\mu_{c}}\otimes\Lambda^{k_{c}}\otimes\Lambda^{\mu_{c+1}-k_{c}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\Lambda^{\mu_{\ell}}\to\Lambda^{\mu}

is isomorphic to
L_{\mu}
.

Proof.

From Lemma
5.18
we have

\Ima(\overline{\gamma}_{k_{c}})\subseteq\Ima(\Lambda^{\mu_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\Lambda^{\mu_{c}+1}\otimes\Lambda^{\mu_{c+1}-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\Lambda^{\mu_{\ell}}\xrightarrow{1\otimes\cdots\otimes\theta_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes 1}\Lambda^{\mu}).

(5.4)

Under the assumptions of the theorem, Corollary
5.20
says that
\coker(\overline{\gamma}_{k_{c}})\simeq\coker(1\otimes\cdots\otimes\theta_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes 1)
. Hence in (
5.4
) we have equality for all
c
. Thus

\coker\big(\sum_{c=1}^{{\ell}-1}\overline{\gamma}_{k_{c}}\big)\simeq\coker(\theta_{\mu})\simeq L_{\mu},

where the last isomorphism is due to Theorem
2.6
.∎

6
Presentations of Specht modules

In this section we consider consequences of our previous results for Specht modules and discuss the relationship with previous known results, especially
[
1
]
and
[
3
]
.

Suppose
n\geq r
. The Schur functor is a functor
f
from the category
M_{K}(n,r)
of homogeneous polynomial representations of
G
of degree
r
to the category
\mod\mathfrak{S}_{r}
of left modules over the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{r}
. For
M\in M_{K}(n,r)
,
f(M)
is the weight subspace
M_{\alpha}
of
M
, where
\alpha=(1^{r},0^{n-r})
; for
\theta:M\to M^{\prime}
a morphism in
M_{K}(n,r)
,
f(\theta)
is the restriction
M_{\alpha}\to M^{\prime}_{\alpha}
of
\theta
. We recall the following well known facts
[
5
]
. Let
\mu
be a partition of
r
and
\lambda=\mu^{\prime}
.

1.

f
is an exact functor,

2.

f(L_{\mu})=S^{\lambda}
and
f(\Lambda^{\mu})=M^{\lambda}
,

3.

f
induces an isomorphism
\Hom_{G}(\Lambda^{\mu},\Lambda^{\mu})\simeq\Hom_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}(M^{\lambda},M^{\lambda})
.

In fact, since the characteristic of
K
is zero,
f
is an equivalence of categories.

6.1
Specht modules and symmetrizations of Garnir relations

Consider the setup and notation of the first two paragraphs of Section
1.2
. It follows from the above, Remark
4.14
and (
1.3
) that when we apply the Schur functor
f
to
\psi_{k}\in\Hom_{G}(\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b},\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b})
we obtain the map
f(\psi_{k})\in\Hom_{\mathfrak{S}_{a+b}}(M^{(a,b)^{\prime}},M^{(a,b)^{\prime}})
that satisfies
f(\psi_{k})(\overline{T})=r^{T}_{1,k}
for all
\overline{T}\in M^{(a,b)^{\prime}}
. Hence the image is

\Ima(f(\psi_{k}))=SGR^{(a,b)^{\prime}}(k).

(6.1)

Thus from Corollary
4.16
we conclude the following.

Corollary 6.22
.

Let
a,b,k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
a\geq b\geq k\geq 1
. Then as
\mathfrak{S}_{a+b}
-modules
M^{(a,b)^{\prime}}/SGR^{(a,b)^{\prime}}(k)\simeq S^{(a,b)^{\prime}}
if and only if
\sum_{t=1}^{j}(-1)^{t-1}\tbinom{b-t}{b-k}\tbinom{j}{t}\tbinom{a-b+j+t}{t}\neq 0
for all
j=1,\dots,b
.

The ’if’ direction of the above corollary generalizes to any partition
\lambda
of
r
in place of
(a,b)^{\prime}
. We consider this as our second main result.

Theorem 6.23
.

Let
\lambda
be a partition of
r
and
k_{1},\dots,k_{\lambda_{1}-1}
positive integers satisfying
k_{c}\leq\mu_{c+1}
,
c=1,\dots,\lambda_{1}-1
, where
\mu=\lambda^{\prime}
. Then as
\mathfrak{S}_{r}
-modules we have
M^{\lambda}/SGR^{\lambda}(k_{1},\dots,k_{\lambda_{1}-1})\simeq S^{\lambda}
if

\sum_{t=1}^{j}(-1)^{t-1}\tbinom{\mu_{c+1}-t}{\mu_{c+1}-k_{c}}\tbinom{j}{t}\tbinom{\mu_{c}-\mu_{c+1}+j+t}{t}\neq 0

for all
c=1,\dots,\lambda_{1}-1
and
j=1,\dots,\mu_{c+1}
.

Proof.

This follows by applying the Schur functor to Corollary
3.12
for the choice
\Psi_{\mu(c)}=\psi_{k_{c}}\in\Hom_{G}(\Lambda^{\mu_{c}}\otimes\Lambda^{\mu_{c+1}},\Lambda^{\mu_{c}}\otimes\Lambda^{\mu_{c+1}})
,
c=1,\dots,\lambda_{1}-1
. From (
6.1
) we have
\Ima(f(\sum_{c=1}^{{\ell}-1}\overline{\psi}_{k_{c}}))=SGR^{\lambda}(k_{1},\dots,k_{\lambda_{1}-1})
. We know from the second statement of Lemma
4.15
that
a_{\mu(c),t}=(-1)^{t-1}\tbinom{\mu_{c+1}-t}{\mu_{c+1}-k_{c}}
if
t\neq 0
and
a_{\mu(c),t}=0
if
t=0
.
∎

Examples 6.24
.

For short, let

\Sigma(k,j)=\sum_{t=1}^{j}(-1)^{t-1}\tbinom{b-t}{b-k}\tbinom{j}{t}\tbinom{a-b+j+t}{t}

be the sum in Corollary
6.22
, where
1\leq j\leq b
.

(1) Let
k=1
. Since
\tbinom{b-t}{b-k}=0
if
t>1
, we have
\Sigma(1,j)=j(a-b+j+1)
for every
j=1,\dots,b
. Since
a\geq b
, we have
\Sigma(1,j)\neq 0
. From Theorem
6.23
we conclude that for any partition
\lambda
we have

M^{\lambda}/SGR^{\lambda}(1,\dots,1)\simeq S^{\lambda}.

Thus we recover Theorem 3.5 of
[
1
]
(which we have stated as Theorem
1.2
in the Introduction).

(2) Let
k=b
the maximal value of
k
. First we note that in this case there is no symmetrization, i.e. we have
SGR^{(a,b)^{\prime}}{(b)}=GR^{(a,b)^{\prime}}(b)
. Using the first identity of
[
8
, Lemma 4.1(2)]
, we conclude that
\Sigma(b,j)=-(-1)^{j}\tbinom{a-b+j}{j}+1.

Taking into account that
j\neq 0
, we see that the left hand side is equal to
0
if and only if
j>0
is even and
a=b
. Since
j\leq b
, it follows from Corollary
6.22
that
M^{(a,b)^{\prime}}/GR^{(a,b)^{\prime}}(b)=S^{(a,b)^{\prime}}
if and only if
b=1
or
b>1
and
a\neq b
. Hence from Theorem
6.23
we conclude that

M^{\lambda}/GR^{\lambda}(\lambda^{\prime}_{2},\dots,\lambda^{\prime}_{\lambda_{1}})\simeq S^{\lambda},

for any partition
\lambda
satisfying
\lambda^{\prime}_{c}>\lambda^{\prime}_{c+1}
for those
c
for which
\lambda^{\prime}_{c+1}>1
. Thus we recover Theorem 1.1 of
[
3
]
(which we have stated as Theorem
1.1
in the Introduction).

(3) Let
k=2
. Then for every
j=1,\dots b
we have

\Sigma(2,j)=(b-1)j(a-b+j+1)-\tbinom{j}{2}\tbinom{a-b+j+2}{2}.

A quick computation yields
\Sigma(2,j)=0
if and only if
4(b-1)-(j-1)(a-b+j+2)=0
. From Corollary
6.22
,
M^{(a,b)^{\prime}}/SGR^{(a,b)^{\prime}}{(2,2)}\simeq S^{(a,b)^{\prime}}
if and only if
4(b-1)-(j-1)(a-b+j+2)\neq 0
for all
j=1,\dots,b
. According to Theorem
6.23
, if
\lambda
is a partition with all column lengths greater than or equal to 2, then

M^{\lambda}/SGR^{\lambda}(2,\dots,2)\simeq S^{\lambda}

if
4(\lambda^{\prime}_{c+1}-1)-(j-1)(\lambda^{\prime}_{c}-\lambda^{\prime}_{c+1}+j+2)\neq 0
for all
c=1,\dots,\lambda_{1}-1
and
j=1,\dots,\lambda^{\prime}_{c+1}
.

In the second of the above examples we observed that the expression for the sum
\Sigma(k,j)
takes on a simple form when
k=b
. We are not aware of a simplification valid for all
k
.

6.2
Specht modules and Garnir relations

Recall that the subspace of Garnir relations

GR^{\lambda}(k_{1},\dots,k_{\lambda_{1}-1})\subseteq M^{\lambda}
was defined in the Introduction, after Theorem 1.3.

Consider the map
\gamma_{k}\in\Hom_{G}(\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{k}\otimes\Lambda^{b-k},\Lambda^{a}\otimes\Lambda^{b})
of Definition
5.17
(2). It is straightforward to verify that its image
f(\gamma_{k})
under the Schur functor
f
has the property that the vector space
\Ima(f(\gamma_{k}))\subseteq M^{(a,b)^{\prime}}
is spanned by the Garnir relations
g_{1,k}^{T}
of (
1.1
) as

varies over
\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}
, where

5.20
and Theorem

Theorem 6.25
.

Let
\lambda
be a partition of
r
and
\mu=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{\ell})=\lambda^{\prime}
.

1.

Suppose
\ell=2
and
k
is a positive integer such that

\mathfrak{S}_{r}
-modules we have
M^{\lambda}/GR^{\lambda}(k)\simeq S^{\lambda}
if and only if

\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}=k=1

Suppose

are positive integers such that
k_{c}\leq\mu_{c+1}
,

\mathfrak{S}_{r}
-modules we have

M^{\lambda}/GR^{\lambda}(k_{1},\dots,k_{\ell-1})\simeq S^{\lambda}
if
\mu_{c}>\mu_{c+1}
for those
c
for which
k_{c}=\mu_{c+1}>1
.

Remarks 6.26
.

(3) Theorem

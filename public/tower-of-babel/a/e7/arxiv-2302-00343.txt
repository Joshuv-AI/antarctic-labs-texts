Flag-accurate arrangements
Paul Mücksch, Gerhard Röhrle, Tan Nhat Tran

Abstract
. In [ MR21 ] , the first two authors introduced the notion of an accurate arrangement, a particular notion of freeness. In this paper, we consider a special subclass, where the property of accuracy stems from a flag of flats in the intersection lattice of the underlying arrangement. Members of this family are called flag-accurate . One relevance of this new notion is that it entails divisional freeness. There are a number of important natural classes which are flag-accurate, the most prominent one among them is the one consisting of Coxeter arrangements. This warrants a systematic study which is put forward in the present paper. More specifically, let 𝒜 \mathscr{A} be a free arrangement of rank ℓ \ell . Suppose that for every 1 ≤ d ≤ ℓ 1\leq d\leq\ell , the first d d exponents of 𝒜 \mathscr{A} – when listed in increasing order – are realized as the exponents of a free restriction of 𝒜 \mathscr{A} to some intersection of reflecting hyperplanes of 𝒜 \mathscr{A} of dimension d d . Following [ MR21 ] , we call such an arrangement 𝒜 \mathscr{A} with this natural property accurate . If in addition the flats involved can be chosen to form a flag, we call 𝒜 \mathscr{A} flag-accurate . We investigate flag-accuracy among reflection arrangements, extended Shi and extended Catalan arrangements, and further for various families of graphic and digraphic arrangements. We pursue these both from theoretical and computational perspectives. Along the way we present examples of accurate arrangements that are not flag-accurate. The main result of [ MR21 ] shows that MAT-free arrangements are accurate. We provide strong evidence for the conjecture that MAT-freeness actually entails flag-accuracy.

1.
Introduction and Statements of Results

In the study of hyperplane arrangements and their freeness it is important to understand the behavior
of different classes of arrangements with respect to combinatorial or geometric constructions.
Moreover, a central theme is to fix certain numerical properties of (free) arrangements and to
investigate a possible classification of such arrangements in different prominent classes
such as reflection arrangements, their subarrangements and deformations thereof.
Following this philosophy, our present work is a systematic study of a new notion
which is shared by many arrangements in those
well established
classes and to initiate a
detailed study of connections to other known families of free arrangements.

We begin by recalling and by extending the definition of an accurate arrangement from
[
MR21
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

Suppose
\mathscr{A}
is free with exponents
\exp(\mathscr{A})=(e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{\ell})_{\leq}
(this notation, used throughout,
simply means that
e_{1}\leq e_{2}\leq\ldots\leq e_{\ell}
).

(i)

\mathscr{A}
is called
almost accurate
provided for each
1\leq d\leq\ell
there exists a flat
X_{d}
in the intersection lattice
L(\mathscr{A})
of
\mathscr{A}
of dimension
d
such that the restriction
\mathscr{A}^{X_{d}}
of
\mathscr{A}
to
X_{d}
is free with
\exp(\mathscr{A}^{X_{d}})\subseteq\exp(\mathscr{A})
.
The tuple
(X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{\ell})
is called a
witness
for the almost accuracy of
\mathscr{A}
.

(ii)

\mathscr{A}
is said to be
accurate
provided
there is a witness
(X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{\ell})
for the almost accuracy of
\mathscr{A}
such that for every
1\leq d\leq\ell
, we have

\exp(\mathscr{A}^{X_{d}})=(e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{d})_{\leq}
.
We say that
(X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{\ell})
is a
witness
for the accuracy of
\mathscr{A}
.

(iii)

For
1\leq k\leq\ell-1
,
\mathscr{A}
is
k
-accurate
provided
there is a witness
(X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{\ell})
for the accuracy of
\mathscr{A}
such that
k
is maximal subject to
(X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{k})
being a flag in
L(\mathscr{A})
.
Then
(X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{\ell})
is a
witness
for the
k
-accuracy of
\mathscr{A}
.

(iv)

Dually,
\mathscr{A}
is
k
-coaccurate
provided
there is a witness
(X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{\ell})
for the accuracy of
\mathscr{A}
such that
k
is minimal subject to
(X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{\ell-k})
being a flag in
L(\mathscr{A})
.
We say that
(X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{\ell})
is a
witness
for the
k
-coaccuracy of
\mathscr{A}
.
Thus an
\ell
-arrangement
\mathscr{A}
is
k
-accurate if and only if
\mathscr{A}
is
(\ell-k)
-coaccurate.

(v)

\mathscr{A}
is called
flag-accurate
provided
there is a witness for the
(\ell-1)
-accuracy (equivalently, for the
1
-coaccuracy)
of
\mathscr{A}
.
Such an
\ell
-tuple is a
witness
for the flag-accuracy of
\mathscr{A}
.

(vi)

\mathscr{A}
is called
ind-flag-accurate
if
\mathscr{A}
is both inductively free and flag-accurate, and there is a witness
(X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{\ell})
for the flag-accuracy of
\mathscr{A}
such that
\mathscr{A}^{X_{d}}
is inductively free for every
1\leq d\leq\ell
.
In that case
(X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{\ell})
is said to be a
witness
for the ind-flag-accuracy of
\mathscr{A}
.

In the sequel we give examples which discriminate between these different notions of accuracy. For instance, the reflection arrangement of the
exceptional complex reflection group of type

G_{31}
turns out to be flag-accurate, but it is not ind-flag-accurate (as it is not inductively free), see Remark
1.5
.
On the other hand, all Coxeter arrangements are
ind-flag-accurate, see Corollary
1.7
.
Also in Example
4.5
we present a rank
5
arrangement
\mathscr{D}
which is
2
-accurate, and so
\mathscr{D}
is accurate but not flag-accurate.
Moreover, in Section
6
we construct a family of graphic arrangements which are

(k+1)
-coaccurate but not
k
-coaccurate for any
k
,
see Theorem
6.12
.

In his seminal work on the connection of freeness to properties of characteristic polynomials
[
Abe16
]
, Abe introduced the following notion, where
\chi(\mathscr{A},t)
denotes the characteristic polynomial of
\mathscr{A}
, see (
2.1
).

Definition 1.2

(
[
Abe16
, Def. 1.5]
)
.

An
\ell
-arrangement
\mathscr{A}
is
divisionally free
if there is a flag

X_{1}\subseteq X_{2}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq X_{\ell-1}\subseteq X_{\ell}=V

such that
\dim(X_{i})=i
for each
1\leq i\leq\ell
and
\chi(\mathscr{A}^{X_{i}},t)
divides
\chi(\mathscr{A}^{X_{i+1}},t)
for each
1\leq i\leq\ell-1
. Such a flag is called a
divisional flag
.

Remarks 1.3
.

(i).
It follows from the definitions above that if an arrangement is flag-accurate, then it is both accurate and divisionally free, since any witness for the flag-accuracy
is simultaneously a divisional flag and a witness for accuracy for free.
The converse of this implication is false. For, in Example
5.5
we present an accurate and divisionally free arrangement which is not flag-accurate (see also Corollary
6.14
).

(ii).
It is clear that flag accuracy only depends on the intersection lattice of the underlying arrangement
and is thus a combinatorial property, ditto for ind-flag-accuracy.
Likewise, divisional freeness is also combinatorial
[
Abe16
, Thm. 4.4(3)]
. But it is not known whether this is also the case for accuracy itself.

(iii).
Non-divisionally free accurate arrangements are not necessarily flag-accurate, see Example
4.5
.

(iv).
While flag-accuracy implies divisional freeness, the converse is false, see

[
MR21
, Ex. 5.4]
.

(v).
A product of arrangements is flag-accurate if and only if each factor is flag-accurate, cf.
[
OT92
, Prop. 2.14, Prop. 4.28]
; ditto for ind-flag-accuracy, cf.
[
HR15
, Prop. 2.10]
.

We focus on specific classes of arrangements in this paper and investigate (ind-)flag-accuracy among them.
The beginning of our investigation concentrates on the question of ind-flag-accuracy of
complex reflection arrangements and of their restrictions.
For instance, in Theorem
4.1
we show that the notions of flag-accuracy and ind-flag-accuracy coincide for complex reflection arrangements.
Here is a compelling consequence of this result.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
G
be an irreducible complex reflection group with reflection arrangement
\mathscr{A}=\mathscr{A}(G)
.
Suppose
G\neq G_{31}
. Then
the following are equivalent:

(i)

\mathscr{A}
is accurate;

(ii)

\mathscr{A}
is flag-accurate;

(iii)

\mathscr{A}
is ind-flag-accurate;

(iv)

\mathscr{A}
is divisionally free;

(v)

\mathscr{A}
is inductively free.

Remark 1.5
.

The reason why we need to exclude the exceptional complex reflection group of type

G_{31}
in Theorem
1.4
is due to the fact that

\mathscr{A}(G_{31})
itself is not inductively free,
[
HR15
, Thm. 1.1]
, but
\mathscr{A}(G_{31})
does satisfy the properties in parts (i), (ii), and (iv) of the theorem, cf.
[
MR21
, Thm. 5.8]
, Theorem
4.1
, and
[
Abe16
]
, respectively.
In particular,
\mathscr{A}(G_{31})
is flag-accurate, but not ind-flag-accurate.

The same equivalence
as the one in Theorem
4.1

prevails for
restrictions of complex reflection arrangements. The following is a consequence of
Theorem
4.4
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
G\subseteq\GL(\mathbb{C}^{n})

be an irreducible complex reflection group with reflection arrangement
\mathscr{A}(G)
. Let
\mathscr{A}=\mathscr{A}(G)^{Y}
be the restriction of
\mathscr{A}(G)
to some flat
Y\in L(\mathscr{A}(G))\setminus\{\mathbb{C}^{n}\}
. Then
the following are equivalent:

(i)

\mathscr{A}
is accurate;

(ii)

\mathscr{A}
is flag-accurate;

(iii)

\mathscr{A}
is ind-flag-accurate.

For an explicit list of all instances
that satisfy the equivalent statements above, see
Theorem
4.4
.

We emphasize that the equivalences in
Theorems
1.4

and
1.6

do hold in particular for
Coxeter arrangements; we do record this as a separate result and do note in passing that all Coxeter arrangements are hereditarily inductively free, cf.
[
BC12
]
and
[
HR15
, §3.2.2]
.

Corollary 1.7
.

Coxeter arrangements are ind-flag-accurate.

In
[
ABC+16
]
, Abe, Barakat, Cuntz, Hoge and Terao proved the
so-called
Multiple Addition Theorem
(MAT) (Theorem
2.10
)
which is a variation
of the addition part of Terao’s seminal Addition-Deletion Theorem
[
Ter80a
]
(
[
OT92
, Thm. 4.51]
).
Using this theorem, they went on
to uniformly derive the freeness of
ideal subarrangements
of Weyl arrangements (Definition
4.6
and Theorem
4.7
).
As a special case of this result, they obtained a new uniform proof of the
classical Kostant-Macdonald-Shapiro-Steinberg formula for the exponents of a Weyl group.

In
[
CM20
]
, Cuntz and the first author introduced the notion of
MAT-freeness
(Definition
2.12
)
to investigate arrangements whose freeness can be derived using an iterative application of the Multiple Addition Theorem (Theorem
2.10
).

Next we recall the principal result
from
[
MR21
, Thm. 1.2]
,
which asserts that MAT-freeness
is sufficient for accuracy from Definition
1.1
.

Theorem 1.8
.

MAT-free arrangements are accurate.

As ideal subarrangements of Weyl arrangements are MAT-free, by
[
ABC+16
]

(see Theorem
4.7
),
Theorem
1.8

readily yields the following
[
MR21
, Thm. 1.3]
.

Theorem 1.9
.

Ideal arrangements are accurate.

It is natural to ask whether ideal arrangements also satisfy the stronger property
of flag-accuracy. Unfortunately, this cannot be derived from Theorem
1.9

since the class of ideal arrangements is not closed under restrictions.
However, we can give a positive answer to this question for ideal arrangements of rank up to
8
(Theorem
4.8
).
In particular,
all
ideal arrangements stemming from exceptional reflection groups are flag-accurate.
This motivates the following.

Conjecture 1.10
.

Ideal arrangements are flag-accurate.

In fact much evidence points to an even stronger assertion. For, firstly all ideal arrangements are
inductively free, thanks to
[
CRS19
]
, and secondly a large number of them are even supersolvable thus hereditarily inductively free (e.g. this applies to
all
ideal arrangements in type
A
,
B
,
C
and
G_{2}
).
(Though in general, ideal arrangements are not hereditarily free, cf.
[
AMR18
]
.)
So if a supersolvable ideal arrangement is flag-accurate it is trivially also ind-flag-accurate.
Thus it is tantalizingly tempting to formulate the following.

Conjecture 1.11
.

Ideal arrangements are ind-flag-accurate.

We have confirmed Conjecture
1.11
for all ideal arrangements up to rank
6
, see
Remark
4.9
.

In view of Theorem
1.8
, Conjecture
1.10
naturally leads to the following more general question, see also Corollary
3.6
.

Problem 1.12
.

Are all MAT-free arrangements flag-accurate?

Our evidence for Conjecture
1.11
does point to an even stronger implication.

Problem 1.13
.

Are all MAT-free arrangements ind-flag-accurate?

The next class we consider is given by certain deformations of Weyl arrangements – extended Shi and extended Catalan arrangements.

In
[
MR21
, Thm. 1.8]

it was shown that extended Shi arrangements, ideal-Shi arrangements and extended Catalan arrangements are accurate.
In Theorems
5.7
and
5.8
we partially strengthen these results as follows.

Theorem 1.14
.

Extended Shi arrangements
\Shi^{m}
are flag-accurate. Extended Catalan arrangements
\Cat^{m}
of Dynkin type
A,B
, or
C

are flag-accurate.

The second part of this theorem strongly hints towards the following.

Conjecture 1.15
.

Extended Catalan arrangements are flag-accurate.

Moreover, in view of
[
MR21
, Thm. 1.8]
we might even pose the following.

Problem 1.16
.

Are all ideal-Shi arrangements flag-accurate?

In Section
6
, we consider free graphic arrangements under the aspect of
flag-accuracy.
We exhibit an infinite family of flag-accurate graphic arrangements (Theorem
6.8
and Theorem
6.19
).
In particular, all trivially perfect graphs are ind-flag-accurate.
In Example
6.3
, we present a non-flag-accurate free graphic arrangement which admits an extension by one vertex which
gives a flag-accurate graphic arrangement.
Thus, (ind-)flag-accuracy, similarly to accuracy, is neither compatible with restriction, nor with localization.

Finally, in the last section we investigate flag-accuracy for
\psi
-digraphic arrangements. Among them are
N
-Ish arrangements. Here we also discuss
classes of arrangements called
Shi descendants
and
Catalan descendants
,
which are closely related to
extended Shi and extended Catalan arrangements by means of graph theoretic operations which we call
mutation
.
The core results here are as follows.
Theorem
7.20
demonstrates that
the origins in the Shi descendant sequences have ind-flag-accurate cones. This in particular implies that
the extended Shi arrangements have this property,
see Remark
7.21
.
In Theorem
7.22
we then show
that all Shi descendants admit ind-flag-accurate cones.
There are analogous results for Catalan descendants. For instance,
Corollary
7.31
proves that the cones of extended Catalan arrangements of type
A
are also
ind-flag-accurate. Finally, in Theorem
7.33
we show that indeed all
Catalan descendants are ind-flag-accurate.

For general information about arrangements
we refer the reader to
[
OT92
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we review some basic concepts and preliminary results on various classes of free arrangements. Our standard reference is
[
OT92
]
.

2.1.
Hyperplane arrangements

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field and let
V=\mathbb{K}^{\ell}
be an
\ell
-dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{K}
.
A
hyperplane
in
V
is an affine subspace of codimension
1
of
V
.
An
arrangement

\mathscr{A}=(\mathscr{A},V)
is a finite collection of hyperplanes in
V
.
We say that
\mathscr{A}
is
central
if every hyperplane in
\mathscr{A}
passes through the origin.
If we want to emphasize the dimension
\ell
of the ambient vector space
we say that
\mathscr{A}
is an
\ell
-arrangement.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an arrangement.
Define the
intersection poset

L(\mathscr{A})
of
\mathscr{A}
by

\displaystyle L(\mathscr{A}):=\left\{\bigcap_{H\in\mathscr{B}}H\neq\emptyset\mid\mathscr{B}\subseteq\mathscr{A}\right\},

where the partial order is given by reverse inclusion
X\leq Y\Leftrightarrow Y\subseteq X
for
X,Y\in L(\mathscr{A})
.
We agree that
V
is a unique minimal element in
L(\mathscr{A})
as the intersection over the empty set.
Thus
L(\mathscr{A})
is a semi-lattice which can be equipped with the rank function
\rk(X):=\operatorname{codim}(X)
for
X\in L(\mathscr{A})
.
We also define the
rank

\rk(\mathscr{A})
of
\mathscr{A}
as the rank of a maximal element of
L(\mathscr{A})
.
The intersection poset
L(\mathscr{A})
is sometimes referred to as the
combinatorics
of
\mathscr{A}
.

All of the properties considered in the present paper, such as supersolvability, (inductive, divisional, recursive, MAT-)freeness, ((almost, ind-)flag-)accuracy of arrangements are compatible with products.

An
\ell
-arrangement
\mathscr{A}
is called
essential
if
\rk(\mathscr{A})=\ell
.
Any arrangement
\mathscr{A}
of rank
r
in
\mathbb{K}^{\ell}
can be written as the product of an essential arrangement
\mathscr{A}^{\mathrm{ess}}
and the
(\ell-r)
-dimensional empty arrangement
\varnothing_{\ell-r}
.
We call
\mathscr{A}^{\mathrm{ess}}
the
essentialization
of
\mathscr{A}
.
Thus
\mathscr{A}
satisfies any of these properties if and only if
\mathscr{A}^{\mathrm{ess}}
does.

The
characteristic polynomial

\chi(\mathscr{A},t)\in\mathbb{Z}[t]
of
\mathscr{A}
is defined by

(2.1)

\chi(\mathscr{A},t):=\sum_{X\in L(\mathscr{A})}\mu(X)t^{\dim(X)},

where
\mu
denotes the
Möbius function

\mu\colon L(\mathscr{A})\to\mathbb{Z}
defined recursively by

\displaystyle\mu\left(V\right):=1\quad\text{ and }\quad\mu(X):=-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y\in L(\mathscr{A})\\
X\subsetneq Y\end{subarray}}\mu(Y).

Let
S=S(V^{*})
be the symmetric algebra of the dual space
V^{*}
of
V
.
We fix a basis
x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}
for
V^{*}
and identify
S
with the polynomial ring
\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}]
.
The algebra
S
is equipped with the grading by polynomial degree:
S=\bigoplus_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}S_{p}
,
where
S_{p}
is the
\mathbb{K}
-space of homogeneous polynomials of degree
p
(along with
0
), where
S_{p}=\{0\}
for
p<0
.

The
defining polynomial

Q(\mathscr{A})
of
\mathscr{A}
is given by

Q(\mathscr{A}):=\prod_{H\in\mathscr{A}}\alpha_{H}\in S,

where
\alpha_{H}=a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{\ell}x_{\ell}+d

(a_{i},d\in\mathbb{K})
satisfies
H=\ker(\alpha_{H})
.

The operation of
coning
is a standard way to pass from an arbitrary arrangement to a central one.
The
cone

\mathbf{c}\mathscr{A}
over
\mathscr{A}
is the central arrangement in
\mathbb{K}^{\ell+1}
with the defining polynomial

Q(\mathbf{c}\mathscr{A}):=zQ(\mathscr{A})^{\prime}\in\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell},z],

where
Q(\mathscr{A})^{\prime}

is the homogenization of
Q(\mathscr{A})
, and
z=0
is the
hyperplane at infinity
, denoted
H_{\infty}
; cf.
[
OT92
, Def. 1.15]
.
By abuse of notation, if the cone
\mathbf{c}\mathscr{A}
has a property, e.g., supersolvability, freeness, etc, we sometimes say
\mathscr{A}
has that property too.
The characteristic polynomials of
\mathscr{A}
and
\mathbf{c}\mathscr{A}
are related by the following simple formula (e.g.,
[
OT92
, Prop. 2.51]
):

\chi(\mathbf{c}\mathscr{A},t)=(t-1)\chi(\mathscr{A},t).

Associated with
X\in L(\mathscr{A})
we have two canonical arrangements,
the
localization
\mathscr{A}_{X}
of
\mathscr{A}
at
X
, given by

\mathscr{A}_{X}:=\{H\in\mathscr{A}\mid H\supseteq X\},

and the
restriction
\mathscr{A}^{X}
of
\mathscr{A}
to
X
, defined by

\mathscr{A}^{X}:=\{H\cap X\neq\emptyset\mid H\in\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{A}_{X}\}.

Two (central) arrangements
\mathscr{A}
and
\mathscr{B}
in
\mathbb{K}^{\ell}
are said to be
(linearly) affinely equivalent
if there is an invertible (linear) affine endomorphism
\varphi:\mathbb{K}^{\ell}\to\mathbb{K}^{\ell}
such that
\mathscr{B}=\varphi(\mathscr{A})=\{\varphi(H)\mid H\in\mathscr{A}\}
.
In particular, the intersection posets of two affinely equivalent arrangements are isomorphic.
All of the properties of arrangements considered in the present paper are preserved under affine and linear equivalences.
In the rest of the paper, we often identify affinely equivalent arrangements and note that for such non-central
\mathscr{A}
and
\mathscr{B}
, the cones
\mathbf{c}\mathscr{A}
and
\mathbf{c}\mathscr{B}
are linearly equivalent.

2.2.
Free, inductively free, and recursively free arrangements

A
\mathbb{K}
-linear map
\theta:S\to S
which satisfies
\theta(fg)=\theta(f)g+f\theta(g)
is called a
\mathbb{K}
-
derivation
.
Let
\Der(S)
be the
S
-module of
\mathbb{K}
-derivations of
S
.
It is a free
S
-module with basis
\partial/\partial x_{1},\ldots,\partial/\partial x_{\ell}
.
We say that a non-zero derivation
\theta=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}f_{i}\partial/\partial x_{i}
is
homogeneous of degree

p
provided all coefficients
f_{i}
belong to
S_{p}
, cf.
[
OT92
, Def. 4.2]
.
In this case we write
\deg{\theta}=p
.
We obtain a
\mathbb{Z}
-grading
\Der(S)=\bigoplus_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}\Der(S)_{p}
of the
S
-module
\Der(S)
.

In the remainder of this section, we assume that
\mathscr{A}
is a
central

\ell
-arrangement.

Definition 2.2
.

The
module of
\mathscr{A}
-derivations
of
\mathscr{A}
is defined by

D(\mathscr{A}):=\{\theta\in\Der(S)\mid\theta(Q(\mathscr{A}))\in Q(\mathscr{A})S\}.

In particular, if
\mathscr{B}\subseteq\mathscr{A}
, then
D(\mathscr{A})\subseteq D(\mathscr{B})
.

We say that
\mathscr{A}
is
free
if the module of
\mathscr{A}
-derivations is a free
S
-module.

If
\mathscr{A}
is a free arrangement we may choose a homogeneous basis
\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{\ell}
of
D(\mathscr{A})
.
Then the degrees of the
\theta_{i}
are called the
exponents
of
\mathscr{A}
.
They are uniquely determined by
\mathscr{A}

[
OT92
, Def. 4.25]
.
In that case we write

\exp(\mathscr{A}):=(\deg{\theta_{1}},\ldots,\deg{\theta_{\ell}})

for the exponents of
\mathscr{A}
.
If
\exp(\mathscr{A})=(e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{\ell})
with
e_{1}\leq e_{2}\leq\ldots\leq e_{\ell}
we often write
\exp(\mathscr{A})=(e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{\ell})_{\leq}
, as in the introduction.
If
\mathscr{A}
has an exponent
e
appearing
d\geq 0
times in
\exp(\mathscr{A})
we also write
e^{d}\in\exp(\mathscr{A})
.

Note that the empty arrangement
\varnothing_{\ell}
in
V
is free with

D(\varnothing_{\ell})=\Der(S)
so that
\exp(\varnothing_{\ell})=(0^{\ell})\in\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}
.

Fix
H\in\mathscr{A}
, denote
\mathscr{A}^{\prime}:=\mathscr{A}\setminus\{H\}
and
\mathscr{A}^{\prime\prime}:=\mathscr{A}^{H}
.
We call
(\mathscr{A},\mathscr{A}^{\prime},\mathscr{A}^{\prime\prime})
the triple with respect to the hyperplane
H\in\mathscr{A}
.

Theorem 2.3

(Addition-Deletion Theorem
[
Ter80a
]
,
[
OT92
, Thms. 4.46 and 4.51]
)
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a non-empty arrangement and let
H\in\mathscr{A}
. Then two of the following statements imply the third:

(i)

\mathscr{A}
is free with
\exp(\mathscr{A})=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{\ell-1},e_{\ell})
.

(ii)

\mathscr{A}^{\prime}
is free with
\exp(\mathscr{A}^{\prime})=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{\ell-1},e_{\ell}-1)
.

(iii)

\mathscr{A}^{\prime\prime}
is free with
\exp(\mathscr{A}^{\prime\prime})=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{\ell-1})
.

Moreover, all three assertions hold if
\mathscr{A}
and
\mathscr{A}^{\prime}
are both free.

Theorem
2.3
above motivates the following concepts.

Definition 2.4

(
[
OT92
, Def. 4.53]
)
.

The class
\mathcal{IF}
of
inductively free
arrangements is the smallest class of arrangements which satisfies

(i)

the empty arrangement
\varnothing_{\ell}
is in
\mathcal{IF}
for
\ell\geq 0
,

(ii)

if there exists
H\in\mathscr{A}
such that
\mathscr{A}^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{IF}
,
\mathscr{A}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{IF}
, and
\exp(\mathscr{A}^{\prime\prime})\subseteq\exp(\mathscr{A}^{\prime})
, then
\mathscr{A}\in\mathcal{IF}
.

Definition 2.5

(
[
OT92
, Def. 4.60]
)
.

The class
\mathcal{RF}
of
recursively free
arrangements is the smallest class of arrangements which satisfies

(i)

\varnothing_{\ell}\in\mathcal{RF}
for
\ell\geq 0
,

(ii)

if there exists
H\in\mathscr{A}
such that
\mathscr{A}^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{RF}
,
\mathscr{A}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{RF}
, and
\exp(\mathscr{A}^{\prime\prime})\subseteq\exp(\mathscr{A}^{\prime})
, then
\mathscr{A}\in\mathcal{RF}
,

(iii)

if there exists
H\in\mathscr{A}
such that
\mathscr{A}^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{RF}
,
\mathscr{A}\in\mathcal{RF}
, and
\exp(\mathscr{A}^{\prime\prime})\subseteq\exp(\mathscr{A})
, then
\mathscr{A}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{RF}
.

Thus
\mathcal{IF}\subseteq\mathcal{RF}
, and by Theorem
2.3
every recursively free arrangement is free.

2.3.
Multiarrangements

A
multiarrangement
is a pair
(\mathscr{A},\mathbf{m})
where
\mathscr{A}=(\mathscr{A},V)
is an arrangement and
\mathbf{m}
is a map
\mathbf{m}:\mathscr{A}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, called a
multiplicity
on
\mathscr{A}
.
Let
\mathscr{A}
be a central arrangement in
\mathbb{K}^{\ell}
and let
\mathbf{m}
be a multiplicity on
\mathscr{A}
.
The defining polynomial
Q(\mathscr{A},\mathbf{m})
of the multiarrangement
(\mathscr{A},\mathbf{m})
is given by

Q(\mathscr{A},\mathbf{m}):=\prod_{H\in\mathscr{A}}\alpha^{\mathbf{m}(H)}_{H}\in S=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dots,x_{\ell}].

When
\mathbf{m}(H)=1
for every
H\in\mathscr{A}
,
(\mathscr{A},\mathbf{m})
is simply a hyperplane arrangement.
The
module
D(\mathscr{A},\mathbf{m})
of logarithmic derivations
of
(\mathscr{A},\mathbf{m})
is defined by

D(\mathscr{A},\mathbf{m}):=\{\theta\in\Der(S)\mid\theta(\alpha_{H})\in\alpha^{\mathbf{m}(H)}_{H}S\mbox{ for all }H\in\mathscr{A}\}.

We say that
(\mathscr{A},\mathbf{m})
is
free
with the multiset
\exp(\mathscr{A},\mathbf{m})=(d_{1},\dots,d_{\ell})
of
exponents
if
D(\mathscr{A},\mathbf{m})
is a free
S
-module with a homogeneous basis
\{\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{\ell}\}
such that
\deg\theta_{i}=d_{i}
for each
i
.
It is known that
(\mathscr{A},\mathbf{m})
is always free for
\ell\leq 2

[
Zie89
, Cor. 7]
.

Next we consider the
Ziegler restriction
of a simple arrangement,
[
Zie89
, Thm. 11]
.
Let
H\in\mathscr{A}
. The
Ziegler restriction
of
\mathscr{A}
onto
H
is a multiarrangement
(\mathscr{A}^{H},\mathbf{m}^{H})
defined by

\mathbf{m}^{H}(X):=|\mathscr{A}_{X}|-1\quad\text{ for }\quad X\in\mathscr{A}^{H}.

We say that
\mathscr{A}
is
locally free in codimension three along
H
if
\mathscr{A}_{X}
is free for every
X\in L(\mathscr{A})

with
X\subseteq H
and
\codim_{V}(X)=3
.

The following fundamental theorem gives
a deep connection between the freeness of a simple arrangements and the freeness of a particular Ziegler restriction.

Theorem 2.6

(
[
Yos04
, Thm. 2.2]
,
[
AY13
, Thm. 4.1]
,
[
Zie89
, Thm. 11]
)
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a central arrangement in
\mathbb{K}^{\ell}
with
\ell\geq 3
and let
H\in\mathscr{A}
. Then

\mathscr{A}
is free with
\exp(\mathscr{A})=(1,d_{2},\ldots,d_{\ell})
if and only if the Ziegler restriction
(\mathscr{A}^{H},\mathbf{m}^{H})
is free with
\exp(\mathscr{A}^{H},\mathbf{m}^{H})=(d_{2},\ldots,d_{\ell})
and
\mathscr{A}
is locally free in codimension three along
H
.

2.4.
Supersolvable arrangements

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a central arrangement.
An element
X\in L(\mathscr{A})
is said to be
modular
if
X+Y\in L(\mathscr{A})
for all
Y\in L(\mathscr{A})
.
A modular element of corank
1
is called a
modular coatom
.
If
X\in L(\mathscr{A})
is a modular coatom, we call the localization
\mathscr{A}_{X}
a modular coatom of
\mathscr{A}
as well.

Definition 2.7
.

A central arrangement
\mathscr{A}
of rank
r
is called
supersolvable
if there exists a chain of arrangements, called an M-chain,

\emptyset=\mathscr{A}_{X_{0}}\subseteq\mathscr{A}_{X_{1}}\subseteq\dots\subseteq\mathscr{A}_{X_{r}}=\mathscr{A},

in which
\mathscr{A}_{X_{i}}
is a modular coatom of
\mathscr{A}_{X_{i+1}}
for each
0\leq i\leq r-1
.

The following result is useful to check whether an element is a modular coatom.

Proposition 2.8

(
[
BEZ90
, Thm. 4.3]
)
.

Let
X\in L(\mathscr{A})
be a coatom.
Then
\mathscr{A}_{X}
is a modular coatom of
\mathscr{A}
if and only if for any distinct
H,H^{\prime}\in\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{A}_{X}
, there exists
H^{\prime\prime}\in\mathscr{A}_{X}
such that
H\cap H^{\prime}\subseteq H^{\prime\prime}
.

Next we recall a classical result on supersolvable and free arrangements.

Theorem 2.9

(
[
JT84
, Thm. (4.2)]
)
.

If
\mathscr{A}
is supersolvable, then
\mathscr{A}
is inductively free hence free.
Furthermore, if
\mathscr{A}
has an M-chain
\emptyset=\mathscr{A}_{X_{0}}\subseteq\mathscr{A}_{X_{1}}\subseteq\dots\subseteq\mathscr{A}_{X_{\rk(\mathscr{A})}}=\mathscr{A},
then

\exp(\mathscr{A})=(0^{\ell-\rk(\mathscr{A})},e_{1},\ldots,e_{\rk(\mathscr{A})})
where
e_{i}=|\mathscr{A}_{X_{i}}\setminus\mathscr{A}_{X_{i-1}}|
.

2.5.
MAT-free arrangements

We begin by recalling the core result from
[
ABC+16
]
, the so-called Multiple Addition Theorem (MAT).

Theorem 2.10

(
[
ABC+16
, Thm. 3.1]
)
.

Let
\mathscr{A}^{\prime}=(\mathscr{A}^{\prime},V)
be a free arrangement with

\exp(\mathscr{A}^{\prime})=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{\ell})_{\leq}

and let
1\leq p\leq\ell
be the multiplicity of the highest exponent, i.e.

e_{\ell-p}<e_{\ell-p+1}=\cdots=e_{\ell}=:e.

Let
H_{1},\ldots,H_{q}
be hyperplanes in
V
with

H_{i}\not\in\mathscr{A}^{\prime}
for
i=1,\ldots,q
. Define

\mathscr{A}^{\prime\prime}_{j}:=(\mathscr{A}^{\prime}\cup\{H_{j}\})^{H_{j}}=\{H\cap H_{j}\mid H\in\mathscr{A}^{\prime}\},\quad\text{ for }j=1,\ldots,q.

Assume that the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

X:=H_{1}\cap\cdots\cap H_{q}
is
q
-codimensional.

(2)

X\not\subseteq\bigcup_{H\in\mathscr{A}^{\prime}}H
.

(3)

|\mathscr{A}^{\prime}|-|\mathscr{A}^{\prime\prime}_{j}|=e
for
1\leq j\leq q
.

Then
q\leq p
and
\mathscr{A}:=\mathscr{A}^{\prime}\cup\{H_{1},\ldots,H_{q}\}
is free with

\exp(\mathscr{A})=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{\ell-q},(e+1)^{q})_{\leq}.

We frequently consider the addition of several hyperplanes using
Theorem
2.10
. This motivates the next terminology.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
\mathscr{A}^{\prime}
and
\{H_{1},\ldots,H_{q}\}
be as in Theorem
2.10
such that
conditions (1)–(3) are satisfied. Then the addition
of
\{H_{1},\ldots,H_{q}\}
to
\mathscr{A}^{\prime}
resulting in
\mathscr{A}=\mathscr{A}^{\prime}\cup\{H_{1},\ldots,H_{q}\}

is called an
MAT-step
.

An iterative application of Theorem
2.10
motivates the following natural concept.

Definition 2.12

(
[
CM20
, Def. 3.2, Lem. 3.8]
)
.

An arrangement
\mathscr{A}
is called
MAT-free
if there exists an ordered partition

\pi=(\pi_{1}|\cdots|\pi_{n})

of
\mathscr{A}
such that the following hold.
Set
\mathscr{A}_{0}:=\varnothing_{\ell}
and

\mathscr{A}_{k}:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}\pi_{i}\quad\text{ for }1\leq k\leq n.

Then for every
0\leq k\leq n-1
suppose that

(1)

\rk(\pi_{k+1})=|\pi_{k+1}|
,

(2)

\cap_{H\in\pi_{k+1}}H\nsubseteq\bigcup_{H^{\prime}\in\mathscr{A}_{k}}H^{\prime}
,

(3)

|\mathscr{A}_{k}|-|(\mathscr{A}_{k}\cup\{H\})^{H}|=k
for each
H\in\pi_{k+1}
,

i.e.
\mathscr{A}_{k+1}=\mathscr{A}_{k}\cup\pi_{k+1}
is an MAT-step.

An ordered partition
\pi
with these properties is called an
MAT-partition
for
\mathscr{A}
.

Remark 2.13

(
[
MR21
, Rem. 2.15]
)
.

Suppose that
\mathscr{A}
is MAT-free with MAT-partition

\pi=(\pi_{1}|\cdots|\pi_{n})
. Then we have:

(i)

for each
1\leq k\leq n
,
\mathscr{A}_{k}
is MAT-free with MAT-partition
(\pi_{1}|\cdots|\pi_{k})
,

(ii)

\mathscr{A}
is free and the exponents

\exp(\mathscr{A})=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{\ell})_{\leq}
of
\mathscr{A}
are given by the block sizes
of the dual partition of
\pi
:

e_{i}:=|\{k\mid|\pi_{k}|\geq\ell-i+1\}|,

(iii)

|\pi_{1}|>|\pi_{2}|\geq\cdots\geq|\pi_{n}|
.

The following central result in
[
MR21
]
relates arrangements which are constructed by MAT-steps
from smaller free arrangements with the notion of accuracy.

Theorem 2.14

(
[
MR21
, Thm. 3.11]
)
.

Let
\mathscr{A}=\mathscr{A}^{\prime}\dot{\cup}\mathscr{B}
be a free arrangement obtained from the free arrangement
\mathscr{A}^{\prime}

through MAT-steps with exponents
\exp(\mathscr{A})=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{\ell})_{\leq}
.
Suppose that
\pi=(\pi_{1}|\cdots|\pi_{n})
is the corresponding
ordered partition of
\mathscr{B}
.
Then for each
1\leq k\leq n
and each
|\pi_{k+1}|\leq q\leq|\pi_{k}|
there is a
\mathscr{C}\subseteq\pi_{k}
with
|\mathscr{C}|=q

such that for
X:=\cap_{H\in\mathscr{C}}H

the restriction
\mathscr{A}^{X}
is free with exponents

\exp\left(\mathscr{A}^{X}\right)=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{\ell-q})_{\leq}.

3.
Flag-accuracy

In this section we collect some sufficient conditions for (ind-)flag-accuracy which are used in subsequent proofs.
Let
\mathbb{K}
be an arbitrary field and
\mathscr{A}
an arrangement in
V=\mathbb{K}^{\ell}
.
The following simple criteria, which readily follow from the definitions, are useful to show (ind-)flag-accuracy within an inductive argument.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be (inductively) free with exponents
\exp(\mathscr{A})=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{\ell})_{\leq}
. Then
\mathscr{A}
is (ind-)flag-accurate if and only if there exist
k
linearly independent hyperplanes
H_{1},\ldots,H_{k}\in\mathscr{A}
for some
1\leq k\leq\ell
such that
\mathscr{A}^{X_{i}}
is (inductively) free with
\exp(\mathscr{A}^{X_{i}})=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{\ell-i})_{\leq}
for each
1\leq i\leq k
where
X_{i}:=\bigcap_{j=1}^{i}H_{j}
and that
\mathscr{A}^{X_{k}}
is (ind-)flag-accurate.

In particular,
\mathscr{A}
is (ind-)flag-accurate if and only if there exists an
H
in
\mathscr{A}
such that
\mathscr{A}^{H}
is (ind-)flag-accurate with
\exp(\mathscr{A}^{H})=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{\ell-1})_{\leq}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose
\mathscr{A}
is free with exponents
\exp(\mathscr{A})=(1,e,\ldots,e)
for some
e\geq 1
.
Then
\mathscr{A}
is flag-accurate if and only if
\mathscr{A}
is divisionally free.
In particular, if
\mathscr{A}
is inductively free with
\exp(\mathscr{A})=(1,e,\ldots,e)
, then
\mathscr{A}
is flag-accurate.

Next, we recall the well-known modular coatom technique.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a central arrangement and let
X\in L(\mathscr{A})
be a modular coatom.
Then the following statements hold.

(i)

t\cdot\chi(\mathscr{A},t)=\left(t-|\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{A}_{X}|\right)\cdot\chi(\mathscr{A}_{X},t)
.

(ii)

\mathscr{A}
is supersolvable (resp., (inductively) free) if and only if
\mathscr{A}_{X}
is supersolvable (resp., (inductively) free).
In this case,
\exp(\mathscr{A})\cup\{0\}=\exp(\mathscr{A}_{X})\cup\{|\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{A}_{X}|\}
.

(iii)

If
\mathscr{A}_{X}
is ((ind-)flag-)accurate whose exponents do not exceed
|\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{A}_{X}|
, then
\mathscr{A}
is ((ind-)flag-)accurate.

(iv)

If
\mathscr{A}_{X}
is almost accurate, then
\mathscr{A}
is also almost accurate.

Proof.

Part (i) follows from
[
Sta71
, Thm. 2]
.
For the forward implication in (ii) note that supersolvability, freeness and inductive freeness are closed under taking localizations (see
[
Sta72
, Prop. 3.2]
,
[
OT92
, Thm. 4.37]
,
[
HRS17
, Thm. 1.1]
).
For (iii), (iv) and the reverse implication in (ii) note that there exists a
H\in\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{A}_{X}
such that the essentializations
(\mathscr{A}^{H})^{\mathrm{ess}}
and
\mathscr{A}_{X}^{\mathrm{ess}}
are linearly equivalent (see
[
MMR23
, Lem. 2.2]
).
∎

The following general statement due to Abe
[
Abe16
, Thm. 6.2]
was first introduced to give a sufficient condition for divisional freeness.
Its proof applies equally to flag-accuracy.

Theorem 3.4
.

Assume that there are distinct hyperplanes
H_{1},\ldots,H_{\ell-1}\in\mathscr{A}
so that the following conditions hold:

(i)

\mathscr{A}^{\prime}_{i}:=\mathscr{A}\setminus\{H_{i}\}
is free with
\exp(\mathscr{A}^{\prime}_{i})=(1,d_{1},\ldots,d_{i-1},d_{i}-1,d_{i+1},\ldots,d_{\ell-1})
for each
1\leq i\leq\ell-1
.

(ii)

\mathscr{A}^{\prime}:=\mathscr{A}\setminus\{H_{1},\ldots,H_{\ell-1}\}
is free with
\exp(\mathscr{A}^{\prime})=(1,d_{1}-1,d_{2}-1,\ldots,d_{\ell-1}-1)
.

Then
\mathscr{A}
is flag-accurate with
\exp(\mathscr{A})=(1,d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{\ell-1})
.

Moreover, suppose
1\leq d_{1}\leq d_{2}\leq\cdots\leq d_{\ell-1}
and set

X_{i}:=\bigcap_{j=i}^{\ell-1}H_{j}
for
1\leq i\leq\ell-1
.
Then
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{\ell-1},X_{\ell}=V)
is a witness for the flag-accuracy of
\mathscr{A}
.

We also recall the following similar statement from
[
MR21
]
.

Corollary 3.5

(
[
MR21
, Cor. 2.13]
)
.

Let
\mathscr{A}^{\prime}
be free,
\mathscr{A}=\mathscr{A}^{\prime}\dot{\cup}\{H_{1},\ldots,H_{p}\}
an MAT-step
and
\mathscr{C}\subseteq\{H_{1},\ldots,H_{p}\}
.
Suppose that
\exp(\mathscr{A})=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{\ell})_{\leq}
and let

X:=\cap_{H\in\mathscr{C}}H
.
Then
\mathscr{A}^{X}
is free with
\exp(\mathscr{A}^{X})=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{\ell-|\mathscr{C}|})_{\leq}
.

From the previous corollary and Theorem
2.14
we readily obtain the following.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a free arrangement of rank at most
4
which is obtained from the free arrangement
\mathscr{A}^{\prime}
by MAT-steps such that one of the steps has size at least
2
.
Then
\mathscr{A}
is flag-accurate.

Proof.

Without loss we may assume that
\mathscr{A}
is a free arrangement in
\mathbb{K}^{4}

with
\exp(\mathscr{A})=(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4})_{\leq}
.
Assume further that
\mathscr{A}=\mathscr{A}^{\prime}\cup\mathscr{B}

and that
\pi=(\pi_{1}|\cdots|\pi_{n})

is a partition of
\mathscr{B}
yielding the successive MAT-steps form
\mathscr{A}^{\prime}
to
\mathscr{A}
.
By assumption there is a
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}

such that
|\pi_{k}|\geq 2
and
|\pi_{i}|\leq 1
for
k<i\leq n
.
By Theorem
2.14
there is an
H\in\pi_{k}
such that

\mathscr{A}^{H}
is free with
\exp(\mathscr{A}^{H})=(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3})
.
Moreover, for
\mathscr{D}:=\mathscr{A}^{\prime}\cup(\cup_{i=1}^{k}\pi_{i})\subseteq\mathscr{A}
we
have that
\mathscr{D}^{H}\subseteq\mathscr{A}^{H}
is free with
\exp(\mathscr{D}^{H})=(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3})
by
Corollary
3.5
, since
\pi_{k}
is an MAT-step.
In particular,
|\mathscr{A}^{H}|=|\mathscr{D}^{H}|
, so
\mathscr{A}^{H}=\mathscr{D}^{H}
.
Thus, for any other
H^{\prime}\in\pi_{k}\setminus\{H\}
and
X=H\cap H^{\prime}

we have that
\mathscr{A}^{X}=\mathscr{D}^{X}
is free with
\exp(\mathscr{A}^{X})=(e_{1},e_{2})
, again, thanks to Corollary
3.5
.
As a rank
2
arrangement,
\mathscr{A}^{X}
is flag-accurate, so
\mathscr{A}^{H}
is flag-accurate
by Lemma
3.1
and
\mathscr{A}
is flag accurate as well.
∎

4.
Flag-accurate reflection arrangements

4.1.
Complex reflection arrangements and their restrictions

Let
G\subseteq\GL(V)
be a finite,
complex reflection group acting on the complex vector space
V=\mathbb{C}^{\ell}
.
The
reflection arrangement
of
G
in
V
is the
hyperplane arrangement
\mathscr{A}(G)

consisting of the reflecting hyperplanes
of the elements in
G
acting as reflections on
V
.

Terao
[
Ter80b
]
has shown that every
reflection arrangement
\mathscr{A}(G)
is free
and that the exponents of
\mathscr{A}(G)

coincide with the coexponents of
G
,
cf.
[
OT92
, Prop. 6.59 and Thm. 6.60]
.

In
[
MR21
, Thm. 1.6]
, it was shown that all
Coxeter arrangements are accurate and in
[
MR21
, Thm. 5.8]
, that more generally
a complex reflection arrangement is accurate if and only if it is divisionally free.
In view of these results, it is natural to examine flag-accuracy
for the class of complex reflection arrangements.
Thanks to Remark
1.3
(v) and
[
Rö18
, Prop. 2.12]
,
flag-accuracy and divisional freeness are compatible with products.

The proofs from
[
MR21
]
carry over almost immediately. So that we observe that
a reflection arrangement is flag-accurate if and only if it is accurate; likewise for restrictions of reflection arrangements. We record both results correcting an omission in the second in
[
MR21
, Thm. 5.12]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
G
be a complex reflection group with reflection arrangement
\mathscr{A}=\mathscr{A}(G)
. Then
\mathscr{A}
is flag-accurate
if and only if it is divisionally free. This is the case if and only if
G
has no irreducible factor isomorphic
to one of the monomial groups
G(r,r,\ell)
,
r>2
,
\ell>2
, or

G_{24},G_{27},G_{29},G_{33},G_{34}
.

In view of Theorem
4.1
and the fact that all restrictions of complex reflection arrangements are free (thanks to
[
OT92
, §6.4, App. D]
,

[
OT93
]
, and
[
HR13
]
),
it is natural to investigate flag-accuracy
among restrictions of complex reflection arrangements,
not all of which are reflection arrangements again.

In order to state our results, we require some further notation.
Orlik and Solomon defined
intermediate
arrangements

\mathscr{A}^{k}_{\ell}(r)
in

[
OS83
, §2]

(cf.
[
OT92
, §6.4]
) which
interpolate between the
reflection arrangements of the monomial groups
G(r,r,\ell)
and
G(r,1,\ell)
.
They show up as restrictions of the reflection arrangement
of
G(r,r,\ell^{\prime})
, for some
\ell^{\prime}
,

[
OS83
, Prop. 2.14]

(cf.
[
OT92
, Prop. 6.84]
).

For

\ell,r\geq 2
and
0\leq k\leq\ell
the defining polynomial of

\mathscr{A}^{k}_{\ell}(r)
is given by

Q(\mathscr{A}^{k}_{\ell}(r))=x_{1}\cdots x_{k}\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i<j\leq\ell\\
0\leq n<r\end{subarray}}(x_{i}-\zeta^{n}x_{j}),

where
\zeta
is a primitive
r
-th root of unity,
so that

\mathscr{A}^{\ell}_{\ell}(r)=\mathscr{A}(G(r,1,\ell))
and

\mathscr{A}^{0}_{\ell}(r)=\mathscr{A}(G(r,r,\ell))
.
For

4.2.
Ideal arrangements

For general information about Weyl groups and their root systems, see
[
Bou68
]
.

Let
W
be a Weyl group acting as a reflection group on the real vector space
V=\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
.
Let
\Phi:=\Phi(W)\subseteq V^{*}
be a (reduced) root system for
W
and
\Phi^{+}\subseteq\Phi
a positive system
with simple roots
\Delta\subseteq\Phi^{+}
.
The
rank
of
W
respectively
\Phi
is
\rk(W):=\rk(\Phi):=\dim(\mathbb{R}\Phi)
.
We have
\Phi^{+}=(\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta}\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\alpha)\cap\Phi
, i.e. if
\beta\in\Phi^{+}
then there are integers
n_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
such that

\beta=\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta}n_{\alpha}\alpha
.
Then the
height
of
\beta
is defined by

\h(\beta):=\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta}n_{\alpha}.

The partial order
\leq
on
\Phi^{+}
is defined by

\beta\leq\gamma\,:\iff\,\gamma-\beta\in\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta}\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\alpha.

A subset
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\Phi^{+}
is an
ideal
if it is a (lower) order ideal
in the poset
(\Phi^{+},\leq)
, i.e. for
\alpha\in\mathcal{I}
and
\beta\in\Phi^{+}

with
\beta\leq\alpha
, we have
\beta\in\mathcal{I}
.

The
Weyl arrangement

\mathscr{A}(W)

is the hyperplane arrangement in
V
defined by

\mathscr{A}=\mathscr{A}(W):=\{\ker(\beta)\mid\beta\in\Phi^{+}\}.

Definition 4.6

(
[
ABC+16
]
)
.

If
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\Phi^{+}
is an order ideal then

\mathscr{A}_{\mathcal{I}}:=\{\ker(\beta)\mid\beta\in\mathcal{I}\}\subseteq\mathscr{A}(W)

is called an

ideal (sub)arrangement
.

We denote by
m_{\mathcal{I}}
the maximal height of a root in
\mathcal{I}
.
For
1\leq k\leq m_{\mathcal{I}}
,
let

\pi_{k,\mathcal{I}}:=\{\ker(\alpha)\mid\alpha\in\mathcal{I},\h(\alpha)=k\}

and let

\pi_{\mathcal{I}}:=\left(\pi_{1,\mathcal{I}}|\cdots|\pi_{m_{\mathcal{I}},\mathcal{I}}\right),

be the
root-height partition
of
\mathscr{A}_{\mathcal{I}}
.
Set
\pi_{j,\mathcal{I}}=\emptyset
for
j>m_{\mathcal{I}}
.

Next we recall the principal result from
[
ABC+16
, Thm. 1.1]
(Ideal-free Theorem) in our terminology.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
\mathscr{A}_{\mathcal{I}}\subseteq\mathscr{A}(W)
be the ideal subarrangement of the Weyl arrangement
\mathscr{A}(W)

for an order ideal
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\Phi^{+}
.
Then
\mathscr{A}_{\mathcal{I}}
is MAT-free with MAT-partition

\pi_{\mathcal{I}}=(\pi_{1,\mathcal{I}}|\cdots|\pi_{m_{\mathcal{I}},\mathcal{I}})
and exponents

\exp\left(\mathscr{A}_{\mathcal{I}}\right)=\left(e_{1}^{\mathcal{I}},\ldots,e_{\ell}^{\mathcal{I}}\right),

where

\displaystyle e_{r}^{\mathcal{I}}=|\{j\mid|\pi_{j,\mathcal{I}}|\geq\ell-r+1\}|.

Theorems
1.8
and
4.7
and the following theorem are the basis for
Conjecture
1.10
.

Theorem 4.8
.

Ideal arrangements of rank at most
8
are flag-accurate.

Proof.

Firstly, all ideal arrangements of rank at most
4
are flag-accurate, by Theorem
4.7
and Corollary
3.6
.

For ideal arrangements of rank
5
up to
8
, we used a computer to check flag-accuracy.
The computation can be considerably simplified in a large number of cases by using Theorem
2.14
as follows.
In the root height partition (of an ideal arrangement of rank at least
5
)
there is always a block of size at least
2
which is either the last block of the partition
or one only followed by blocks of size
1
.
Consequently, analogous to the proof of Corollary
3.6
,
a partial flag up to the restriction of all the hyperplanes in this block can always be built,
realizing subsets of the exponents in correct order by Theorem
2.14
.
Now, in most of the cases it turns out that the restriction to the intersection of all the hyperplanes in this block
is itself flag-accurate, yielding the flag-accuracy of the whole ideal arrangement.
Unfortunately, there are still cases of ideals in the root systems of type
E_{6},E_{7},E_{8}
where this heuristic doesn’t work.
Hence a hard computer check in these cases (a few hundred) is unavoidable but readily manageable.
∎

Remark 4.9
.

Concerning Conjecture
1.11
, we
were able to confirm with the aid of a computer that all

\mathscr{A}_{\mathcal{I}}
of rank up to

4.3.
Crystallographic arrangements

Crystallographic arrangements were first introduced and studied by Cuntz
and Heckenberger in the setting of finite Weyl groupoids,
culminating in a complete classification in
[
CH15
]
,
see also
[
Cun11a
]
.
They are generalization of Weyl arrangements or even of restrictions of Weyl arrangements
as every Weyl arrangement is crystallographic and the class of crystallographic arrangements
is closed under taking restrictions, cf.
[
BC12
, Prop. 5.3]
.

Let us recall the definition of a crystallographic arrangement.

Definition 4.10
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a hyperplane arrangement in
V\cong\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
.
Denote the
chambers
of
\mathscr{A}
, i.e. the connected components of
V\setminus\left(\cup_{H\in\mathscr{A}}H\right)

by
\mathcal{C}(\mathscr{A})
.

For
C\in\mathcal{C}(\mathscr{A})
define the
walls
of
C
as

\mathcal{W}^{C}:=\{H\in\mathscr{A}\mid\langle H\cap\overline{C}\rangle=H\}.

If
\Phi\subseteq V^{*}
is a finite set such that
\mathscr{A}=\{\ker(\alpha)\mid\alpha\in\Phi\}

and
\mathbb{R}\alpha\cap\Phi=\{\pm\alpha\}
for all
\alpha\in\Phi

then
\Phi
is called a
(reduced) root system
for
\mathscr{A}
.

If
\Phi
is a root system for
\mathscr{A}
, then for each
C
set

B_{\Phi}^{C}:=\{\alpha\in\Phi\mid\ker(\alpha)\in\mathcal{W}^{C}\text{ and }\alpha^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})\supseteq C\}\subseteq\Phi.

If every
C\in\mathcal{C}(\mathscr{A})
is a simplicial cone
and there exists a root system
\Phi\subseteq V^{*}
for
\mathscr{A}
such that

\Phi\subseteq\sum_{\alpha\in B_{\Phi}^{C}}\mathbb{Z}\alpha\text{ for all }C\in\mathcal{C}(\mathscr{A}),

then
\mathscr{A}
is called
crystallographic
and
\Phi
a
crystallographic root system

for
\mathscr{A}
.

Crystallographic arrangements were classified by Cuntz and Heckenberger
[
CH15
]
.
Perusing at the classification one observes that
each irreducible crystallographic arrangement of rank at least
4
and each member of the infinite series
is a restriction of a Weyl arrangement, see
[
CL17
, Thm. 3.7]
.
Hence, to derive the following result,
by Theorem
4.4
, it suffices to check the finite list of sporadic
irreducible rank
3
arrangements, cf.
[
CH15
, App. B.1]
.

Theorem 4.11
.

Crystallographic arrangements are ind-flag-accurate.

Proof.

By
[
BC12
, Cor. 5.15]
, all restrictions of crystallographic arrangements are inductively free.
As mentioned above, in view of Theorem
4.4
, it suffices to check the
finite list of sporadic irreducible crystallographic arrangements of rank
3
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be one of the
50
sporadic arrangements listed in
[
CH15
, App. B.1]

and set
\exp(\mathscr{A})=(1,e_{1},e_{2})_{\leq}
.
Then a straightforward calculation shows that there is an
H\in\mathscr{A}
,
such that
\exp(\mathscr{A}^{H})=(1,e_{1})
.
Hence,
\mathscr{A}
is ind-flag-accurate, by Lemma
3.1
.
∎

Notation 5.9

Theorem 5.10

Let

and

Let
\mathscr{B}^{p}_{\ell}(m,a)
be the arrangement consisting of the hyperplanes

\displaystyle x_{i}\pm x_{j}

\displaystyle=[-a,a]\quad(1\leq i<j\leq\ell),

\displaystyle x_{i}

\displaystyle=[1-m,m]\quad(1\leq i\leq p),

\displaystyle x_{i}

\displaystyle=[-m,m]\quad(p<i\leq\ell).

Then the cone
\mathbf{c}\mathscr{B}^{p}_{\ell}(m,a)

\exp\left(\mathbf{c}\mathscr{B}^{p}_{\ell}(m,a)\right)=(1,2\ell-p-1,1,3,5,\ldots,2\ell-3)+(0,(2m+2a\ell-2a)^{\ell}).

Proof.

Define the lexicographic order on the set of pairs

\{(\ell,\ell-p)\mid 0\leq\ell-p\leq\ell,\,\ell\geq 1\}.

We prove that
\mathscr{A}:=\textbf{c}\mathscr{B}^{p}_{\ell}(m,a)

When

Case

denote the hyperplane

by the induction hypothesis.
Moreover,
\mathscr{A}^{\prime\prime}=\mathscr{A}^{H}
consists of the following hyperplanes

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle x_{i}\pm x_{j}

\displaystyle=[-a,a]z\quad(1\leq i<j\leq\ell,i\neq p+1,j\neq p+1),

\displaystyle x_{i}

\displaystyle=[-(m+a),m+a]z\quad(1\leq i\leq\ell,i\neq p+1).

Case

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle x_{i}

Theorem 5.11
.

Let
a,m\geq 0
,
\ell\geq 1

Case

.
The arrangement in question is
\textbf{c}\mathscr{C}^{\ell}_{\ell}(m,a)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle x_{i}\pm x_{j}

Theorem 5.12

In the case of graphic arrangements, the concepts of supersolvability, inductive freeness, divisional freeness, almost accuracy and freeness are essentially equivalent.

Remark 6.7

It is not hard to see that the graphic arrangement

We observe that regarding their exponents, up to symmetry there are three types of restrictions of

Below we give an edge representative of each type:

(I)

Theorem 6.8

Let

Proof.

We argue by induction on

The case

When

Suppose

Note that

In view of Remark

Theorem 6.16
.

Let

be a simple graph on

Denote by

\mathscr{A}_{G+v}
.
In particular, if
\mathscr{A}_{G}
is (flag-)accurate, then so is
\mathscr{A}_{G+v}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathscr{A}_{G}
is
k
-coaccurate.
In particular,
\mathscr{A}_{G}
is free and we may assume
\exp(\mathscr{A}_{G})=(0,d_{2},\ldots,d_{\ell})_{\leq}
.
One can show that
\mathscr{A}_{G+v}
is also free (since any perfect elimination ordering of

\exp(\mathscr{A}_{G+v})=(0,1,d_{2}+1,\ldots,d_{\ell}+1)_{\leq}
.

Since
\mathscr{A}_{G}
is
k
-coaccurate, by definition there exist subsets

for

with
\codim_{\mathbb{K}^{\ell}}(X_{F_{p}})=p
such that
\exp\left(\mathscr{A}_{G}^{X_{F_{p}}}\right)=(0,d_{2},\ldots,d_{\ell-p})_{\leq}
and

.

For any

, it is easily seen that
\codim_{\mathbb{K}^{\ell}}(X_{F})=\codim_{\mathbb{K}^{\ell+1}}(X_{F})
and we have

.
Thus
\exp\left(\mathscr{A}_{G+v}^{X_{F_{p}}}\right)=(0,1,d_{2}+1,\ldots,d_{\ell-p}+1)_{\leq}
.
This implies that
(X_{F_{\ell-1}}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq X_{F_{1}}\subseteq X_{F_{0}})
is a witness for the accuracy of

, and
(X_{F_{\ell-1}}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq X_{F_{k}})

\mathscr{A}_{G+v}

-coaccurate.

Definition 6.17
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
denote the smallest class of simple graphs satisfying the following four conditions:

(1)

K_{1}\in\mathcal{G}
, i.e., any one-vertex graph is in
\mathcal{G}
.

(2)

If
G,H\in\mathcal{G}
then

, i.e., the disjoint union of two graphs in
\mathcal{G}
is again a graph in
\mathcal{G}
.

(3)

If
G,H\in\mathcal{G}
then any new graph

formed by identifying a vertex in
G
and a vertex in
H
as a new vertex in
Q
(often known as a

of
G
and

) is in
\mathcal{G}
.

(4)

If
G\in\mathcal{G}
and

is a new vertex then

, i.e., adding one dominating vertex to a graph in
\mathcal{G}
gives a graph in
\mathcal{G}
.

Example 6.18
.

Trivially perfect graphs

\mathscr{A}_{G}

In particular, if

Conditions (2) and (3) in Definition

.

Thus a

The following digraphs play a crucial role in this section.

Definition 7.2

7.2.
N
-Ish arrangements

Our first set of examples of
\psi
-digraphic arrangements is the class of

-Ish arrangements
due to Abe, Suyama and Tsujie
[
AST17
]
which we now recall.
Let
N=(N_{1},N_{2},\ldots,N_{\ell})
be an
\ell
-tuple of finite sets
N_{i}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
(not necessarily integer intervals).

Example 7.6
.

The
(essentialized)
N
-Ish arrangement

\mathscr{A}(N)
is the arrangement in
\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
defined by

\mathscr{A}(N):=\Cox(A_{\ell})\cup\{x_{i}=N_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq\ell\}.

Thus any
N
-Ish arrangement is a
\psi
-digraphic arrangement:
\mathscr{A}(N)=\mathscr{A}(G,\psi)
where
G=\overline{K}_{\ell}
is the edgeless digraph on
[\ell]
and
\psi(i)=N_{i}
for each
i
.

Theorem 7.7

(
[
AST17
, Thm. 1.3]
)
.

The following are equivalent:

(i)

The cone
\mathbf{c}\mathscr{A}(N)
is supersolvable.

(ii)

The cone
\mathbf{c}\mathscr{A}(N)
is inductively free.

(iii)

The cone
\mathbf{c}\mathscr{A}(N)
is free.

(iv)

N
is nested, i.e., there exists a permutation
w
of
[\ell]
such that
N_{w(i)}\subseteq N_{w(i-1)}
for every
2\leq i\leq\ell
.

In this case, the exponents of
\mathbf{c}\mathscr{A}(N)
are given by
(1,|N_{w(i)}|+i-1)_{i=1}^{\ell}
where
w
is any permutation of
[\ell]
such that
N_{w(i)}\subseteq N_{w(i-1)}
for every
2\leq i\leq\ell
.

We call an
\ell
-tuple
N
as above
strictly nested
if there exists a permutation
w
of
[\ell]
such that
N_{w(i)}\subsetneq N_{w(i-1)}
for every
2\leq i\leq\ell
.
Our next lemma asserts that strict nestedness is sufficient for ind-flag-accuracy of
\mathbf{c}\mathscr{A}(N)
.

Lemma 7.8
.

Let
N=(N_{1},N_{2},\ldots,N_{\ell})
with
N_{\ell}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq N_{2}\subsetneq N_{1}
.
Then
\mathbf{c}\mathscr{A}(N)
is ind-flag-accurate.

Proof.

Note that
|N_{i}|-|N_{i+1}|\geq 1
for each
1\leq i\leq\ell-1
.
By Theorem
7.7
,
\mathscr{A}:=\mathbf{c}\mathscr{A}(N)
is supersolvable hence inductively free with exponents

(1,|N_{\ell}|+\ell-1,\ldots,|N_{2}|+1,|N_{1}|)_{\leq}.

For each
1\leq i\leq\ell-1
fix
a_{i}\in N_{i}\setminus N_{i+1}
, and set
M_{i}:=\{a_{j}\mid 1\leq j\leq i\}
.
Also, let
H_{i}
denote the hyperplane
x_{i}=a_{i}z
, and set
X_{i}:=\bigcap_{j=1}^{i}H_{j}
.
Then
X_{\ell-1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq X_{2}\subseteq X_{1}\subseteq V^{\prime}=\mathbb{R}^{\ell+1}
and
\dim_{V^{\prime}}(X_{i})=\ell+1-i
.
Moreover, for each
1\leq i\leq\ell-1
, one can show that
\mathscr{A}^{X_{i}}
is identical to the nested
N
-Ish arrangement defined by

N_{\ell}\sqcup M_{i}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq N_{i+2}\sqcup M_{i}\subsetneq N_{i+1}\sqcup M_{i}.

Once again, according to Theorem
7.7
,
\mathscr{A}^{X_{i}}
is inductively free with exponents given by

(1,|N_{\ell}|+\ell-1,\ldots,|N_{i+1}|+i)_{\leq}.

Thus
\mathbf{c}\mathscr{A}(N)
is ind-flag-accurate with a witness
(X_{\ell-1},\ldots,X_{2},X_{1},V^{\prime})
.
∎

We complete this subsection by defining two operations on vertex-weighted digraphs with interval weights needed for our subsequent discussion.

Definition 7.9
.

A vertex
v
in a digraph
G
is called a

source
(resp.
sink
)
if
(v,u)\in E_{G}
(resp.,
(u,v)\in E_{G}
) for every
u\in V_{G}\setminus\{v\}
.

Definition 7.10
.

Let
(G,\psi)
be a vertex-weighted digraph with non-empty interval weight, that is,
G
is a digraph on
[\ell]
and
\psi\colon[\ell]\to 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
is a map such that
\emptyset\neq\psi(i)=[a_{i},b_{i}]\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
where
a_{i}\leq b_{i}
are integers for every
i\in[\ell]
. Let
v
be a vertex in
G
.

Next we define the
mutation
of
(G,\psi)
with respect to a sink or source.

(i)

Suppose that
v
is a sink in
G
.
The
mutation

of
(G,\psi)
(w.r.t.
v
) is a new vertex-weighted digraph
(G^{\prime},\psi^{\prime})
where
G^{\prime}=([\ell],E_{G^{\prime}})
is a digraph and
\psi^{\prime}
is a weight given by

\displaystyle E_{G^{\prime}}:=E_{G}\setminus\{(i,v)\mid i\in[\ell]\setminus\{v\}\},\qquad\psi^{\prime}(i):=\begin{cases}[a_{i}-1,b_{i}]&(i\in[\ell]\setminus\{v\}),\\
[a_{v},b_{v}]&(i=v).\end{cases}

(ii)

Dually, suppose that
v
is a source in
G
.
The
mutation

of
(G,\psi)
(w.r.t.
v
) is a new vertex-weighted digraph
(G^{\prime\prime},\psi^{\prime\prime})
given by

\displaystyle E_{G^{\prime\prime}}:=E_{G}\setminus\{(v,i)\mid i\in[\ell]\setminus\{v\}\},\qquad\psi^{\prime\prime}(i):=\begin{cases}[a_{i},b_{i}+1]&(i\in[\ell]\setminus\{v\}),\\
[a_{v},b_{v}]&(i=v).\end{cases}

Subsequently, when speaking of a
mutation
or of an
evolution

of a
\psi
-digraphic arrangement
\mathscr{A}(G,\psi)
, we mean that mutation is applied to the underlying vertex-weighted digraph
(G,\psi)
.
In particular, a mutation
w.r.t. the sink (source)
v
induces a (set) bijection on the underlying arrangements
\mathscr{A}(G,\psi)\to\mathscr{A}(G^{\prime},\psi^{\prime})
(resp.,
\mathscr{A}(G,\psi)\to\mathscr{A}(G^{\prime\prime},\psi^{\prime\prime})
).

What we call a source, sink and mutation with respect to one of the former is called a “king”, “coking” and “(co)king elimination operation”, respectively, in
[
ATT21
]
.

Evolution leads to many ind-flag-accurate
\psi

Among them is a deformation of the Shi arrangement of type

Definition 7.11
.

Let
1\leq k\leq\ell
and recall the digraph

from Definition
7.2

Fix
\ell

c_{i}\leq d_{i}

Let

\psi

(i)

for each

(ii)

For

by applying

\ell-k+1

by

with their weights unchanged.

\mathscr{A}^{k}_{\ell}
and
\mathscr{A}^{k+1}_{\ell}

\tau_{\ell-k+1}:\mathscr{A}^{k}_{\ell}\to\mathscr{A}^{k+1}_{\ell}.

We call the arrangements
\mathscr{A}^{k}_{\ell}

, and call the sequence

\mathscr{A}^{1}_{\ell}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau_{\ell}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathscr{A}^{2}_{\ell}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau_{\ell-1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\cdots\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau_{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathscr{A}^{\ell}_{\ell}

a
Shi descendant sequence
with
origin
\mathscr{A}^{1}_{\ell}
and
end
\mathscr{A}^{\ell}_{\ell}
.

The solution of the recurrence relation from Definition
7.11
is given below.

Proposition 7.12
.

With the notation in Definition
7.11
, for each
1\leq k\leq\ell
, we have

\psi_{\ell}^{k}(i)=\varphi_{\ell}^{k}(i),\text{ where }\varphi_{\ell}^{k}(i):=[1-\min\{\ell-i+1,k\}+c_{i},d_{i}]\quad(1\leq i\leq\ell).

In other words,
\mathscr{A}^{k}_{\ell}
consists of the following hyperplanes:

\displaystyle x_{i}-x_{j}

\displaystyle=0\quad(1\leq i<j\leq\ell),

\displaystyle x_{i}-x_{j}

\displaystyle=1\quad(1\leq i<j\leq\ell+1-k),

\displaystyle x_{i}

\displaystyle=[1-k+c_{i},d_{i}]\quad(1\leq i\leq\ell+1-k),

\displaystyle x_{i}

\displaystyle=[-\ell+i+c_{i},d_{i}]\quad(\ell+1-k<i\leq\ell).

Proof.

It is clear that
\psi^{1}_{\ell}=\varphi^{1}_{\ell}
.
Fix
1\leq k\leq\ell-1
and set
v:=\ell-k+1
.
Each vertex
i\in[v+1,\ell]
is isolated and its weight is the same in both
\left(T_{\ell}^{k},\varphi_{\ell}^{k}\right)
and
\left(T_{\ell}^{k+1},\varphi_{\ell}^{k+1}\right)
given by
\varphi_{\ell}^{k}(i)=\varphi_{\ell}^{k+1}(i)=[-\ell+i+c_{i},d_{i}]
.
It suffices to prove that after applying a mutation to

\left(T_{\ell}^{k}[v],\varphi_{\ell}^{k}|_{[v]}\right)
w.r.t. the
sink
v
, we obtain
\left(T_{\ell}^{k+1}[v],\varphi_{\ell}^{k+1}|_{[v]}\right)
.
It is clear that
(T_{\ell}^{k}[v])^{\prime}=T_{\ell}^{k+1}[v]
.
Moreover,

(\varphi_{\ell}^{k}|_{[v]})^{\prime}(v)=[1-k+c_{v},d_{v}]=\varphi_{\ell}^{k+1}|_{[v]}(v),

and for each

(\varphi_{\ell}^{k}|_{[v]})^{\prime}(i)=[-k+c_{i},d_{i}]=\varphi_{\ell}^{k+1}|_{[v]}(i),

Thus
(\varphi_{\ell}^{k}|_{[v]})^{\prime}=\varphi_{\ell}^{k+1}|_{[v]}
, as desired.

\displaystyle x_{i}-x_{j}

Our main result in this subsection is that all the arrangements in the Shi descendant matrix are ind-flag-accurate, which is given in Theorem

Before addressing the proof of Theorem

Remark 7.19
.

The freeness of the arrangements in the Shi descendant matrix can be readily verified by using
[
ATT21
, Thms. 3.1 and 4.1]
(and the modular coatom technique in Corollary
7.5
) which implies that the maps
\tau_{\ell},\ldots,\tau_{2}
in Proposition
7.16
preserve freeness and characteristic polynomials of the arrangements involved.
This method was also used in the proof of Theorem
7.14
.
Thus the freeness and exponents of the Shi descendants follow from the supersolvability of the corresponding final terms, the “supersolvable descendants” in the last column.
However, to show the ind-flag-accuracy in the proof of Theorem
7.22
, we need a different “reverse” approach: We first employ the ind-flag-accuracy of the Shi-like arrangements (the origins) in the first column, then observe that this property propagates to all the arrangements in the Shi descendant matrix.

The Shi-like arrangements
\mathscr{A}^{p,1}_{\ell}(m,d)
for
0\leq p\leq\ell
when
d=0
are affinely equivalent to (the essentializations of) the inductively free arrangements in
[
Ath98
, Thms. 3.1 and 3.3]
due to Athanasiadis.
The Shi arrangements therein were defined so that they contain sufficient deletions and restrictions in order to apply the addition-deletion theorem (Theorem
2.3
) to guarantee the inductive freeness of all the arrangements in the family.
This observation is also helpful for the proof of their ind-flag-accuracy which we show below as a generalization of
[
Ath98
, Thm. 3.3]
.
Roughly speaking, in order to achieve the inductive freeness and ind-flag-accuracy, we consider the restriction to the “
m
-hyperplane”
x_{i}=-(m+1)z
and the “
n
-hyperplane”
x_{i}=(na-1)z
, respectively (see also Theorems
7.29
and
7.32
for a similar observation for Catalan arrangements).

Theorem 7.20
.

Let
m\geq 0
,
a,n,\ell\geq 1
, and
0\leq p\leq\ell
.
Let
\mathscr{H}^{p}_{\ell}(m,a,n)
be the arrangement consisting of the following hyperplanes:

\displaystyle x_{i}-x_{j}

\displaystyle=[1-a,a]\quad(1\leq i<j\leq\ell),

\displaystyle x_{i}

\displaystyle=[-m,na-1]\quad(1\leq i\leq p),

\displaystyle x_{i}

\displaystyle=[-m-1,na-1]\quad(p<i\leq\ell).

The cone over
\mathscr{H}^{p}_{\ell}(m,a,n)

is ind-flag-accurate with exponents

\exp\left(\mathbf{c}\mathscr{H}^{p}_{\ell}(m,a,n)\right)=(1,0^{p},1^{\ell-p})+(0,(m+a(\ell+n-1))^{\ell}).

As a consequence, the origin
\mathscr{A}^{p,1}_{\ell}(m,d)=\mathscr{H}^{p}_{\ell}(m,1,d+1)
in the first column of the Shi descendant matrix given in (
7.17
) has ind-flag-accurate cone with exponents

\exp\left(\mathbf{c}\mathscr{A}^{p,1}_{\ell}(m,d)\right)=(1,(\ell+m+d)^{p},(\ell+m+d+1)^{\ell-p}).

Proof.

The inductive freeness of
\mathbf{c}\mathscr{H}^{p}_{\ell}(m,a,n)
when
n=1
was shown in
[
Ath98
, Thm. 3.3]
(after the transformation
x_{i}\mapsto x_{i}-x_{0}
for all
i
) by an inductive method (see also
[
JT84
, Ex. (2.6)]
).
Our proof uses a similar argument but requires an additional treatment when
n>1
(see Case
2
below).

Define the lexicographic order on

\{(\ell,\ell-p)\mid 0\leq\ell-p\leq\ell,\,\ell\geq 1\}.

First we prove that
\mathscr{A}:=\mathbf{c}\mathscr{H}^{p}_{\ell}(m,a,n)
belongs to
\mathcal{IF}
with the desired exponents, by induction on
(\ell,\ell-p)
.
When
\ell=1
, it is obvious. Suppose
\ell\geq 2
.

Case
1
. First consider
0\leq p\leq\ell-1
.
Let
H\in\mathscr{A}
denote the hyperplane
x_{p+1}=-(m+1)z
.
Then
\mathscr{A}^{\prime}=\mathscr{A}\setminus\{H\}=\mathbf{c}\mathscr{H}^{p+1}_{\ell}(m,a,n)\in\mathcal{IF}
with exponents

\exp(\mathbf{c}\mathscr{H}^{p+1}_{\ell}(m,a,n))=(1,0^{p+1},1^{\ell-p-1})+(0,(m+a(\ell+n-1))^{\ell})

by the induction hypothesis since
(\ell,\ell-p)>(\ell,\ell-p-1)
.
Moreover,
\mathscr{A}^{\prime\prime}=\mathscr{A}^{H}
consists of the following hyperplanes

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle x_{i}-x_{j}

\displaystyle=[1-a,a]z\quad(1\leq i<j\leq\ell,i\neq p+1,j\neq p+1),

\displaystyle x_{i}

\displaystyle=[-(m+a),na-1]z\quad(1\leq i\leq p),

\displaystyle x_{i}

\displaystyle=[-(m+a+1),na-1]z\quad(p+1<i\leq\ell).

Thus
\mathscr{A}^{\prime\prime}=\mathbf{c}\mathscr{H}^{p}_{\ell-1}(m+a,a,n)
.
Hence
\mathscr{A}^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{IF}
with exponents

\exp(\mathbf{c}\mathscr{H}^{p}_{\ell-1}(m+a,a,n))=(1,0^{p},1^{\ell-p-1})+(0,(m+a(\ell+n-1))^{\ell-1})

by the induction hypothesis since
(\ell,\ell-p)>(\ell-1,\ell-1)
.
Therefore, by the addition part of Theorem
2.3
,
\mathscr{A}=\mathbf{c}\mathscr{H}^{p}_{\ell}(m,a,n)\in\mathcal{IF}
with the desired exponents.

Case
2
. Now consider
\ell=p
.
We may assume further that
m=0
since
\mathscr{H}^{\ell}_{\ell}(m,a,n)=\mathscr{H}^{0}_{\ell}(m-1,a,n)
.
The arrangement in question is
\mathscr{B}(n):=\mathbf{c}\mathscr{H}^{\ell}_{\ell}(0,a,n)
consisting of

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle x_{i}-x_{j}

\displaystyle=[1-a,a]z\quad(1\leq i<j\leq\ell),

\displaystyle x_{i}

\displaystyle=[0,na-1]z\quad(1\leq i\leq\ell).

We are going to show that
\mathscr{B}(n)
belongs to
\mathcal{IF}
with exponents
(1,(a(\ell+n-1))^{\ell})
by induction on
n
.
The base case
n=1
was already shown in
[
Ath98
, Thm. 3.3]
.

Now we show that for
n\geq 1
if
\mathscr{B}(n)\in\mathcal{IF}
then
\mathscr{B}(n+1)\in\mathcal{IF}
with the desired exponents.
Let
0\leq k\leq a
and denote by
\mathscr{B}_{k}(n)
the arrangement having the following hyperplanes

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle x_{i}-x_{j}

\displaystyle=[1-a,a]z\quad(1\leq i<j\leq\ell),

\displaystyle x_{i}

\displaystyle=[0,na-1+k]z\quad(1\leq i\leq\ell).

In particular, we have
\mathscr{B}_{0}(n)=\mathscr{B}(n)
, and
\mathscr{B}_{a}(n)=\mathscr{B}(n+1)
.
Notice that for any
\mathscr{B}_{0}(n)\subsetneq\mathscr{D}\subseteq\mathscr{B}_{a}(n)
and for any hyperplane
H\in\mathscr{D}
of the form
x_{d}=sz
for
1\leq d\leq\ell
,
na\leq s\leq(n+1)a-1
, the restriction
\mathscr{D}^{H}
is linearly equivalent to
\mathbf{c}\mathscr{H}^{\ell-d}_{\ell-1}(s,a,1)
.
Hence
\mathscr{D}^{H}\in\mathcal{IF}
with exponents
(1,0^{\ell-d},1^{d-1})+(0,(s+a(\ell-1))^{\ell-1})
by the induction hypothesis on
(\ell,\ell-p)
.
Now, thanks to Theorem
2.3
, adding
\ell
of the hyperplanes in
\mathscr{B}_{1}(n)\setminus\mathscr{B}_{0}(n)
to
\mathscr{B}_{0}(n)
in the order
H_{1},H_{2},\ldots,H_{\ell}
, where
H_{i}
denotes the hyperplane
x_{i}=naz
, implies that
\mathscr{B}_{1}(n)\in\mathcal{IF}
with exponents
(1,(a(\ell+n-1)+1)^{\ell})
.
For fixed
0\leq k\leq a-1
, adding the hyperplanes in
\mathscr{B}_{k+1}(n)\setminus\mathscr{B}_{k}(n)
to
\mathscr{B}_{k}(n)
,
repeatedly,
in the order
x_{1}=(na-1+k)z
,
x_{2}=(na-1+k)z,\ldots
,
x_{\ell}=(na-1+k)z
, implies that
\mathscr{B}_{k+1}(n)\in\mathcal{IF}
with exponents
(1,(a(\ell+n-1)+k)^{\ell})
.
The case
k=a
gives the desired result for
\mathscr{B}(n+1)
.

This completes the proof of the inductive freeness of
\mathscr{A}=\mathbf{c}\mathscr{H}^{p}_{\ell}(m,a,n)
.

Finally, we show that
\mathscr{A}
is ind-flag-accurate.
We argue by induction on
\ell
.
When
\ell=1
, it is obvious. Suppose
\ell\geq 2
.
First consider the case
p=\ell
and
m=0
.
By the discussion above,
\mathscr{A}=\mathscr{B}(n)=\mathbf{c}\mathscr{H}^{\ell}_{\ell}(0,a,n)\in\mathcal{IF}
with
\exp(\mathscr{A})=(1,(a(\ell+n-1))^{\ell})
.
Let
K_{1},K_{2}\in\mathscr{A}
denote the hyperplanes
x_{\ell}=0
,
x_{\ell}=(na-1)z
, respectively.
If
n=1
then
\mathscr{A}^{K_{1}}=\textbf{c}\mathscr{H}^{\ell-1}_{\ell-1}(a,a,1)
.
If
n>1
then
\mathscr{A}^{K_{2}}=\textbf{c}\mathscr{H}^{\ell-1}_{\ell-1}(0,a,n+1)
.
Hence both
\mathscr{A}^{K_{1}},\mathscr{A}^{K_{2}}
are ind-flag-accurate with
\exp(\mathscr{A}^{K_{1}})=(1,(a\ell)^{\ell-1})
, and
\exp(\mathscr{A}^{K_{2}})=(1,(a(\ell+n-1))^{\ell-1})
, by the induction hypothesis on
\ell
.
By Lemma
3.1
,
\mathscr{A}
is ind-flag-accurate.

We can now assume
0\leq p\leq\ell-1
since
\mathscr{H}^{\ell}_{\ell}(m,a,n)=\mathscr{H}^{0}_{\ell}(m-1,a,n)
.
Then by Case
1
in the proof of the inductive freeness of
\mathscr{A}
, we have
\mathscr{A}^{H}=\textbf{c}\mathscr{H}^{p}_{\ell-1}(m+a,a,n)
, where
H\in\mathscr{A}
denotes the hyperplane
x_{p+1}=-(m+1)z
.
Hence
\mathscr{A}^{H}
is ind-flag-accurate with exponents
(1,0^{p},1^{\ell-p-1})+(0,(m+a(\ell+n-1))^{\ell-1})
by the induction hypothesis on
\ell
.
By Lemma
3.1
,
\mathscr{A}
is ind-flag-accurate.

This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

We are finally in a position to present the main result of this subsection.

Theorem 7.22
.

Let
d,m\geq 0
,
1\leq k\leq\ell
and
0\leq p\leq\ell
.
The cone over the arrangement
\mathscr{A}^{p,k}_{\ell}(m,d)
in the Shi descendant matrix is ind-flag-accurate with exponents

\exp\left(\mathscr{A}^{p,k}_{\ell}(m,d)\right)=(1,(\ell+m+d)^{p},(\ell+m+d+1)^{\ell-p}).

Proof.

By Theorem
7.20
, it suffices to prove that for any fixed
0\leq p\leq\ell
the cone
\mathscr{A}:=\mathbf{c}\mathscr{A}^{p,k}_{\ell}(m,d)
for
2\leq k\leq\ell
is ind-flag-accurate with exponents as above.

There are two cases:
p\leq\ell+1-k
and
p>\ell+1-k
.
Since the proofs are similar, we give a proof only for the former.
First note that by Proposition
7.16
the arrangement
\mathscr{B}:=\textbf{c}\mathscr{A}\left(T_{\ell}^{k}[\ell-k+1],\psi_{\ell}^{p,k}|_{[\ell-k+1]}\right)
is given by

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle x_{i}-x_{j}

\displaystyle=0,z\quad(1\leq i<j\leq\ell+1-k),

\displaystyle x_{i}

\displaystyle=[1-m-k,d]z\quad(1\leq i\leq p),

\displaystyle x_{i}

\displaystyle=[-m-k,d]z\quad(p<i\leq\ell+1-k).

Hence

\mathscr{B}=\textbf{c}\mathscr{A}^{p,1}_{\ell+1-k}(m+k-1,d),

which is ind-flag-accurate with exponents
(1,(\ell+m+d)^{p},(\ell+m+d+1)^{\ell+1-k-p})
, by Theorem
7.20
.

Thanks to Proposition
7.16
, each isolated vertex
n\in[\ell-k+2,\ell]
is simplicial in
\left(T_{\ell}^{k}[n],\psi_{\ell}^{p,k}|_{[n]}\right)
with
\psi_{\ell}^{p,k}(n)=[-m-\ell+n-1,d]
.
By applying Corollary
7.5
(i) repeatedly to the
k-1
simplicial vertices
\ell,\ell-1,\ldots,\ell-k+2
in this order, we get

\mathscr{A}\in\mathcal{IF}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\mathscr{B}\in\mathcal{IF}.

Thus the inductive freeness of
\mathscr{A}
is clear.
Moreover,

\exp(\mathscr{A})=(1,(\ell+m+d)^{p},(\ell+m+d+1)^{\ell-p}),

since
|\psi_{\ell}^{p,k}(n)|+n-1=\ell+m+d+1
.

Note that
\mathscr{B}
is ind-flag-accurate, none of its exponents exceeds
\ell+m+d+1
.
Upon applying Corollary
7.5
(ii) repeatedly to the
k-1
simplicial vertices
\ell-k+2,\ldots,\ell
in this order, we know that for each
n\in[\ell-k+2,\ell]
the arrangement
\mathbf{c}\mathscr{A}\left(T_{\ell}^{k}[n],\psi_{\ell}^{p,k}|_{[n]}\right)
is ind-flag-accurate, none of its exponents exceeds
\ell+m+d+1
.
In particular,
\mathscr{A}
is ind-flag-accurate.
This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

7.4.

In this subsection we show that the type
A
Catalan arrangement can be regarded as the origin of a
Catalan descendant sequence
similar to the one defined above in the setting of Shi genealogy in Section
7.3
.
Several results analogous to ones from the previous subsection are obtained.

Definition 7.23
.

Let
1\leq k\leq\ell
and recall the digraphs
K_{\ell}^{k}
and
\widehat{K}_{\ell}^{k}
from Definition
7.2
.
Fix
\ell
pairs of integers
c_{i}\leq d_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq\ell
.
Let

\mathscr{R}^{k}_{\ell}:=\mathscr{A}\left(K_{\ell}^{k},\psi_{\ell}^{k}\right)\quad\text{for}\quad 1\leq k\leq\ell

and

\widehat{\mathscr{R}}^{k}_{\ell}:=\mathscr{A}\left(\widehat{K}_{\ell}^{k},\widehat{\psi}_{\ell}^{k}\right)\quad\text{for }\quad 1\leq k\leq\ell-1

be the
\psi
-digraphic arrangements defined by the following recurrence relation:

(i)

\mathscr{R}^{1}_{\ell}=\mathscr{A}(K_{\ell}^{1},\psi^{1}_{\ell})
with
\psi^{1}_{\ell}(i)=[c_{i},d_{i}]
for each
i\in[\ell]
.

(ii)

For
k=1,2,\ldots,\ell-1
the arrangement
\widehat{\mathscr{R}}^{k}_{\ell}
is obtained from
\mathscr{R}^{k}_{\ell}
by applying mutation w.r.t. the sink
\ell-k+1
of the induced subgraph
\left(K_{\ell}^{k}[\ell-k+1],\psi_{\ell}^{k}|_{[\ell-k+1]}\right)
of
\left(K_{\ell}^{k},\psi_{\ell}^{k}\right)
by
[\ell-k+1]
, and keeping the isolated vertices
i\in[\ell-k+2,\ell]
with their weights unchanged.
The relation induces a bijection between the arrangements
\mathscr{R}^{k}_{\ell}
and
\widehat{\mathscr{R}}^{k}_{\ell}
, denoted by
\tau_{\ell-k+1}:\mathscr{R}^{k}_{\ell}\to\widehat{\mathscr{R}}^{k}_{\ell}
.

(iii)

For
k=1,2,\ldots,\ell-1
the arrangement
\mathscr{R}^{k+1}_{\ell}
is obtained from
\widehat{\mathscr{R}}^{k}_{\ell}
by applying mutation w.r.t. the source
\ell-k+1
of the induced subgraph
\left(\widehat{K}_{\ell}^{k}[\ell-k+1],\widehat{\psi}_{\ell}^{k}|_{[\ell-k+1]}\right)
of
\left(\widehat{K}_{\ell}^{k},\widehat{\psi}_{\ell}^{k}\right)
by
[\ell-k+1]
, and keeping the isolated vertices
i\in[\ell-k+2,\ell]
with their weights unchanged.
The relation induces a bijection between the arrangements
\widehat{\mathscr{R}}^{k}_{\ell}
and
\mathscr{R}^{k+1}_{\ell}
, denoted by
\eta_{\ell-k+1}:\widehat{\mathscr{R}}^{k}_{\ell}\to\mathscr{R}^{k+1}_{\ell}
.

We call the arrangements
\mathscr{R}^{k}_{\ell}
,
\widehat{\mathscr{R}}^{k}_{\ell}

Catalan descendants
, and call the sequence

\mathscr{R}^{1}_{\ell}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau_{\ell}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\widehat{\mathscr{R}}^{1}_{\ell}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eta_{\ell}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathscr{R}^{2}_{\ell}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau_{\ell-1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\cdots\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau_{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\widehat{\mathscr{R}}^{\ell-1}_{\ell}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eta_{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathscr{R}^{\ell}_{\ell}

a
Catalan descendant sequence
.
The arrangements
\mathscr{R}^{1}_{\ell}
,
\widehat{\mathscr{R}}^{1}_{\ell}
,
\mathscr{R}^{\ell}_{\ell}
,
\widehat{\mathscr{R}}^{\ell-1}_{\ell}
are called
origin, first descendant, end, penultimate descendant
of the sequence, respectively.

The solution of the recurrence relation from Definition
7.23
is given below.

Proposition 7.24
.

With the notation as in Definition
7.23
, for each
1\leq k\leq\ell
we have
\psi_{\ell}^{k}(i)=\varphi_{\ell}^{k}(i)
, where

\displaystyle\varphi_{\ell}^{k}(i)

\displaystyle:=[1-\min\{\ell-i+1,k\}+c_{i},d_{i}+\min\{\ell-i+1,k\}-1]\quad(1\leq i\leq\ell),

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}[1-k+c_{i},d_{i}+k-1]&(1\leq i\leq\ell-k+1),\\
[-\ell+i+c_{i},d_{i}+\ell-i]&(\ell-k+1<i\leq\ell).\end{cases}

In addition, for each
1\leq k\leq\ell-1
we have
\widehat{\psi}_{\ell}^{k}(i)=\widehat{\varphi}_{\ell}^{k}(i)
, where

\widehat{\varphi}_{\ell}^{k}(i):=\begin{cases}[-k+c_{i},d_{i}+k-1]&(1\leq i\leq\ell-k),\\
[-\ell+i+c_{i},d_{i}+\ell-i]&(\ell-k<i\leq\ell).\end{cases}

Proof.

It is clear that
\psi^{1}_{\ell}=\varphi^{1}_{\ell}
.
Fix
1\leq k\leq\ell-1
and set
v:=\ell-k+1
.
Each vertex
i\in[v+1,\ell]
is isolated and its weight is the same in
\left(K_{\ell}^{k},\varphi_{\ell}^{k}\right)
,
\left(\widehat{K}_{\ell}^{k},\widehat{\varphi}_{\ell}^{k}\right)
and
\left(K_{\ell}^{k+1},\varphi_{\ell}^{k+1}\right)
given by
\varphi_{\ell}^{k}(i)=\widehat{\varphi}_{\ell}^{k}(i)=\varphi_{\ell}^{k+1}(i)=[-\ell+i+c_{i},d_{i}]
.
It suffices to prove the statement firstly after applying mutation to
\left(K_{\ell}^{k}[v],\varphi_{\ell}^{k}|_{[v]}\right)
w.r.t. the sink
v
, when we obtain
\left(\widehat{K}_{\ell}^{k}[v],\widehat{\varphi}_{\ell}^{k}|_{[v]}\right)
, and secondly after applying mutation to
\left(\widehat{K}_{\ell}^{k}[v],\widehat{\varphi}_{\ell}^{k}|_{[v]}\right)
w.r.t. the source
v
, after which we obtain
\left(K_{\ell}^{k+1}[v],\varphi_{\ell}^{k+1}|_{[v]}\right)
.
It is clear that
(K_{\ell}^{k}[v])^{\prime}=\widehat{K}_{\ell}^{k}[v]
and
(\widehat{K}_{\ell}^{k}[v])^{\prime\prime}=K_{\ell}^{k+1}[v]
.
Moreover,

\displaystyle(\varphi_{\ell}^{k}|_{[v]})^{\prime}(v)

\displaystyle=[1-k+c_{v},d_{v}+k-1]=\widehat{\varphi}_{\ell}^{k}|_{[v]}(v),

\displaystyle(\widehat{\varphi}_{\ell}^{k}|_{[v]})^{\prime\prime}(v)

\displaystyle=[1-k+c_{v},d_{v}+k-1]=\varphi_{\ell}^{k+1}|_{[v]}(v),

and for each
i\in[\ell-k]

\displaystyle(\varphi_{\ell}^{k}|_{[v]})^{\prime}(i)

\displaystyle=[-k+c_{i},d_{i}+k-1]=\widehat{\varphi}_{\ell}^{k}|_{[v]}(i),

\displaystyle(\widehat{\varphi}_{\ell}^{k}|_{[v]})^{\prime\prime}(i)

\displaystyle=[-k+c_{i},d_{i}+k]=\varphi_{\ell}^{k+1}|_{[v]}(i).

Thus
(\varphi_{\ell}^{k}|_{[v]})^{\prime}=\widehat{\varphi}_{\ell}^{k}|_{[v]}
and
(\widehat{\varphi}_{\ell}^{k}|_{[v]})^{\prime\prime}=\varphi_{\ell}^{k+1}|_{[v]}
, as desired.
∎

Now we define the protagonist of this subsection, a special class of Catalan descendants.

Definition 7.25
.

Let
m\geq 0
,
c\geq 1
,
1\leq k\leq\ell
, and
0\leq p\leq\ell
be integers.
Let

\mathscr{R}^{p,k}_{\ell}(c,m):=\mathscr{A}(K_{\ell}^{k},\psi_{\ell}^{p,k})\quad\text{for}\quad 1\leq k\leq\ell

and

\widehat{\mathscr{R}}^{p,k}_{\ell}(c,m):=\mathscr{A}\left(\widehat{K}_{\ell}^{k},\widehat{\psi}_{\ell}^{p,k}\right)\quad\text{for}\quad 1\leq k\leq\ell-1

be the Catalan descendants defined as follows: for each
0\leq p\leq\ell
the arrangement
\mathscr{R}^{p,1}_{\ell}(c,m)
consists of the hyperplanes

\displaystyle x_{i}-x_{j}

\displaystyle=[-1,1]\quad(1\leq i<j\leq\ell),

\displaystyle x_{i}

\displaystyle=[-c,m]\quad(1\leq i\leq p),

\displaystyle x_{i}

\displaystyle=[-c,m+1]\quad(p<i\leq\ell),

and
\mathscr{R}^{p,1}_{\ell}(c,m)
is the origin of the Catalan descendant sequence

\mathscr{R}^{p,1}_{\ell}(c,m)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau_{\ell}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\widehat{\mathscr{R}}^{p,1}_{\ell}(c,m)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eta_{\ell}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathscr{R}^{p,2}_{\ell}(c,m)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau_{\ell-1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\cdots\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau_{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\widehat{\mathscr{R}}^{p,\ell-1}_{\ell}(c,m)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eta_{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathscr{R}^{p,\ell}_{\ell}(c,m).

Proposition 7.26
.

Fix
0\leq p\leq\ell
.
For fixed
1\leq k\leq\ell
(resp.,
1\leq k\leq\ell-1
) each vertex
n\in[\ell-k+2,\ell]
is isolated and simplicial in
\left(K_{\ell}^{k}[n],\psi_{\ell}^{p,k}|_{[n]}\right)
(resp.,
\left(\widehat{K}_{\ell}^{k}[n],\widehat{\psi}_{\ell}^{p,k}|_{[n]}\right)
).

Proof.

First we show the assertion for
\mathscr{R}^{p,k}_{\ell}(c,m)
.
By Proposition
7.24
, if
p\leq\ell-k
, then

\psi_{\ell}^{p,k}(i)=\begin{cases}[1-c-k,m+k-1]&(1\leq i\leq p),\\
[1-c-k,m+k]&(p<i\leq\ell+1-k),\\
[-c-\ell+i,m+\ell+1-i]&(\ell+1-k<i\leq\ell).\end{cases}

If
p>\ell-k
, then

\psi_{\ell}^{p,k}(i)=\begin{cases}[1-c-k,m+k-1]&(1\leq i\leq\ell+1-k),\\
[-c-\ell+i,m+\ell-i]&(\ell+1-k<i\leq p),\\
[-c-\ell+i,m+\ell+1-i]&(p<i\leq\ell).\end{cases}

In either case for each
i\in[\ell+1-k]
, we have

\psi_{\ell}^{p,k}(i)\supsetneq\psi_{\ell}^{p,k}(\ell+2-k)\supsetneq\cdots\supsetneq\psi_{\ell}^{p,k}(\ell).

Now we apply Proposition
7.4
.

The assertion for
\widehat{\mathscr{R}}^{p,k}_{\ell}(c,m)
is proved in a similar fashion.
If
p\leq\ell-k
, then

\widehat{\psi}_{\ell}^{p,k}(i)=\begin{cases}[-c-k,m+k-1]&(1\leq i\leq p),\\
[-c-k,m+k]&(p<i\leq\ell-k),\\
[-c-\ell+i,m+\ell+1-i]&(\ell-k<i\leq\ell).\end{cases}

If
p>\ell-k
, then

\widehat{\psi}_{\ell}^{p,k}(i)=\begin{cases}[-c-k,m+k-1]&(1\leq i\leq\ell-k),\\
[-c-\ell+i,m+\ell-i]&(\ell-k<i\leq p),\\
[-c-\ell+i,m+\ell+1-i]&(p<i\leq\ell).\end{cases}

This completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

Remark 7.27
.

It is easily seen from Proposition
7.26
that
\mathscr{R}^{\ell,k}_{\ell}(c,m+1)=\mathscr{R}^{0,k}_{\ell}(c,m)
for any
1\leq k\leq\ell
, and
\widehat{\mathscr{R}}^{\ell,k}_{\ell}(c,m+1)=\widehat{\mathscr{R}}^{0,k}_{\ell}(c,m)
for any
1\leq k\leq\ell-1
.

Analogous to the case of Shi descendants (Remark
7.19
), the Catalan descendants
\mathscr{R}^{p,k}_{\ell}(c,m)
and
\widehat{\mathscr{R}}^{p,k}_{\ell}(c,m)
in the same sequence from Definition
7.25
have free cones with the same multiset of exponents.
Again this fact can be deduced from
[
ATT21
, Thms. 3.1 and 4.1]
by noting that the maps
\tau_{\ell},\eta_{\ell},\ldots,\tau_{2},\eta_{2}
preserve both freeness and characteristic polynomials.
Thus the freeness and the exponents of the Catalan descendants follow from the supersolvability of the ends of the corresponding sequences (since they are nested
N
-Ish arrangements, see Proposition
7.28
below).

Our main result in this subsection asserts that the arrangements
\mathscr{R}^{p,k}_{\ell}(c,m)
and
\widehat{\mathscr{R}}^{p,k}_{\ell}(c,m)
have ind-flag-accurate cones, which is given in Theorem
7.33
below.
The key point of the proof is the ind-flag-accuracy of the origin and first descendant of the Catalan descendant sequences.

In case of the end
\mathscr{R}^{p,\ell}_{\ell}(c,m)
,
the result follows from Lemma
7.8
.
We state that result separately.

Proposition 7.28
.

Let
0\leq p\leq\ell
.
The arrangement
\mathscr{R}^{p,\ell}_{\ell}(c,m)
, consisting of the hyperplanes

\displaystyle x_{i}-x_{j}

\displaystyle=0\quad(1\leq i<j\leq\ell),

\displaystyle x_{i}

\displaystyle=[-c-\ell+i,m+\ell-i]\quad(1\leq i\leq p),

\displaystyle x_{i}

\displaystyle=[-c-\ell+i,m+\ell+1-i]\quad(p<i\leq\ell),

has supersolvable and ind-flag-accurate cone with exponents

\exp\left(\mathbf{c}\mathscr{R}^{p,\ell}_{\ell}(c,m)\right)=(1,\ell-p+1,2,3,\ldots,\ell)+(0,(m+\ell+c-1)^{\ell}).

Next we study the ind-flag-accuracy of the origins in their sequence in analogy to
Theorem
7.20
.

Theorem 7.29
.

Let
a,m\geq 0
,
n,\ell\geq 1
and
0\leq p\leq\ell
.
Let
\mathscr{E}^{p}_{\ell}(n,a,m)
be the arrangement consisting of the following hyperplanes:

\displaystyle x_{i}-x_{j}

\displaystyle=[-a,a]\quad(1\leq i<j\leq\ell),

\displaystyle x_{i}

\displaystyle=[-na,m]\quad(1\leq i\leq p),

\displaystyle x_{i}

\displaystyle=[-na,m+1]\quad(p<i\leq\ell).

The cone over
\mathscr{E}^{p}_{\ell}(n,a,m)

is ind-flag-accurate with exponents

\exp\left(\mathbf{c}\mathscr{E}^{p}_{\ell}(n,a,m)\right)=(1,\ell-p+1,2,3,\ldots,\ell)+(0,(m+a(\ell+n-1))^{\ell}).

As a consequence, the origin
\mathscr{R}^{p,1}_{\ell}(c,m)=\mathscr{E}^{p}_{\ell}(c,1,m)
of the Catalan descendant sequence from Definition
7.25
has ind-flag-accurate cone with exponents

\exp\left(\mathbf{c}\mathscr{R}^{p,1}_{\ell}(c,m)\right)=(1,\ell-p+1,2,3,\ldots,\ell)+(0,(m+\ell+c-1)^{\ell}).

Proof.

To show inductive freeness of
\mathscr{A}:=\mathbf{c}\mathscr{E}^{p}_{\ell}(n,a,m)
, we use a similar argument to the one from the proof of Theorem
7.20
.
Define the lexicographic order on

\{(\ell,\ell-p)\mid 0\leq\ell-p\leq\ell,\,\ell\geq 1\}.

First we prove
\mathscr{A}\in\mathcal{IF}
with the desired exponents by induction on
(\ell,\ell-p)
.
When
\ell=1
, it is obvious. Suppose
\ell\geq 2
.

Case
1
. First consider
0\leq p\leq\ell-1
.
Let
H\in\mathscr{A}
denote the hyperplane
x_{p+1}=(m+1)z
.
Then
\mathscr{A}^{\prime}=\mathscr{A}\setminus\{H\}=\mathbf{c}\mathscr{E}^{p+1}_{\ell}(n,a,m)\in\mathcal{IF}
with exponents

\exp\left(\mathbf{c}\mathscr{E}^{p+1}_{\ell}(n,a,m)\right)=(1,\ell-p,2,3,\ldots,\ell)+(0,(m+a(\ell+n-1))^{\ell}),

by the induction hypothesis since,
(\ell,\ell-p)>(\ell,\ell-p-1)
.
Moreover,
\mathscr{A}^{\prime\prime}=\mathscr{A}^{H}
consists of the following hyperplanes

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle x_{i}-x_{j}

\displaystyle=[-a,a]z\quad(1\leq i<j\leq\ell,i\neq p+1,j\neq p+1),

\displaystyle x_{i}

\displaystyle=[-na,m+a+1]z\quad(1\leq i\leq\ell).

Thus
\mathscr{A}^{\prime\prime}=\mathbf{c}\mathscr{E}^{0}_{\ell-1}(n,a,m+a)
.
Hence
\mathscr{A}^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{IF}
with exponents

\exp\left(\mathbf{c}\mathscr{E}^{0}_{\ell-1}(n,a,m+a)\right)=(1,2,3,\ldots,\ell)+(0,(m+a(\ell+n-1))^{\ell-1}),

again by the induction hypothesis, since
(\ell,\ell-p)>(\ell-1,\ell-1)
.
Therefore, by Theorem
2.3
,
\mathscr{A}=\mathbf{c}\mathscr{E}^{p}_{\ell}(n,a,m)\in\mathcal{IF}
with the desired exponents.

Case
2
. Now consider
\ell=p
.
We may assume further that
m=0
since
\mathscr{E}^{\ell}_{\ell}(n,a,m)=\mathscr{E}^{0}_{\ell}(n,a,m-1)
.
The arrangement in question is

\mathscr{B}(n):=\mathbf{c}\mathscr{E}^{\ell}_{\ell}(n,a,0),

consisting of

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle x_{i}-x_{j}

\displaystyle=[-a,a]z\quad(1\leq i<j\leq\ell),

\displaystyle x_{i}

\displaystyle=[-na,0]z\quad(1\leq i\leq\ell).

Let
\mathscr{C}
be the cone over the type
A
extended Catalan arrangement, consisting of the hyperplanes

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle x_{i}-x_{j}

\displaystyle=[-a,a]z\quad(1\leq i<j\leq\ell).

Note that
\mathscr{C}

is inductively free with exponents
(1,0,a\ell+i)_{i=1}^{\ell-1}
, by our induction hypothesis, since it is linearly equivalent to
\mathbf{c}\mathscr{E}^{\ell-1}_{\ell-1}(1,a,a)\times\varnothing_{1}
(via
x_{1}-x_{i}\mapsto x_{i-1}
).

We are going to show that
\mathscr{B}(n)\in\mathcal{IF}
with exponents
(1,a(\ell+n-1)+i)_{i=1}^{\ell}
, by induction on
n
.
If
n=1
, by Theorem
2.3
, adding
r
of the hyperplanes
x_{i}=sz
for
1\leq i\leq\ell
,
s\in[-a,0]
in any order to
\mathscr{C}

produces an inductively free arrangement with exponents
(1,r,a\ell+i)_{i=1}^{\ell-1}
.
Indeed, the restriction to the last hyperplane added at each step is linearly equivalent to
\mathbf{c}\mathscr{E}^{\ell-1}_{\ell-1}(1,a,a)
.
The case
r=a\ell+\ell
gives the desired result for
\mathscr{B}(1)
.

Now we show that for
n\geq 1
, if
\mathscr{B}(n)
is inductively free, then so is
\mathscr{B}(n+1)
with the desired exponents.
It requires a more delicate order of addition of the hyperplanes.
Let
0\leq k\leq a
and denote by
\mathscr{B}_{k}(n)
the arrangement having the following hyperplanes

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle x_{i}-x_{j}

\displaystyle=[-a,a]z\quad(1\leq i<j\leq\ell),

\displaystyle x_{i}

\displaystyle=[-(na+k),0]z\quad(1\leq i\leq\ell).

In particular, we have
\mathscr{B}_{0}(n)=\mathscr{B}(n)
, and
\mathscr{B}_{a}(n)=\mathscr{B}(n+1)
.
Note that for any
\mathscr{B}_{0}(n)\subsetneq\mathscr{D}\subseteq\mathscr{B}_{a}(n)
and for any hyperplane
H\in\mathscr{D}
of the form
x_{i}=sz
for some
-(na+a)\leq s\leq-(na+1)
, the restriction
\mathscr{D}^{H}
is linearly equivalent to
\mathbf{c}\mathscr{E}^{\ell-1}_{\ell-1}(1,a,-s)
.
Hence
\mathscr{D}^{H}\in\mathcal{IF}
with exponents
(1,-(s+a)+a\ell+i)_{i=1}^{\ell-1}
by the induction hypothesis on
(\ell,\ell-p)
.
Now by Theorem
2.3
, adding
\ell
of the hyperplanes in
\mathscr{B}_{1}(n)\setminus\mathscr{B}_{0}(n)
in any order to
\mathscr{B}_{0}(n)
shows that
\mathscr{B}_{1}(n)
is inductively free with exponents
(1,a(\ell+n-1)+i+1)_{i=1}^{\ell}
.
Now,
for fixed
0\leq k\leq a-1
, adding the hyperplanes in
\mathscr{B}_{k+1}(n)\setminus\mathscr{B}_{k}(n)
in any order to
\mathscr{B}_{k}(n)
, implies that
\mathscr{B}_{k+1}(n)
is inductively free with exponents
(1,a(\ell+n-1)+i+k+1)_{i=1}^{\ell}
.
The case
k=a-1
gives the desired result for
\mathscr{B}(n+1)
.

This completes the proof of the inductive freeness of
\mathscr{A}=\mathbf{c}\mathscr{E}^{p}_{\ell}(n,a,m)
.

Finally, we derive that
\mathscr{A}
is ind-flag-accurate.
We argue by induction on
\ell
.
When
\ell=1
, the statement is obvious. Suppose
\ell\geq 2
.
It suffices to consider
1\leq p\leq\ell
, since
\mathscr{E}^{\ell}_{\ell}(n,a,m+1)=\mathscr{E}^{0}_{\ell}(n,a,m)
.
Let
K\in\mathscr{A}
denote the hyperplane
x_{1}=-naz
.
Then the restriction
\mathscr{A}^{K}
consists of the following hyperplanes

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle x_{i}-x_{j}

\displaystyle=[-a,a]z\quad(2\leq i<j\leq\ell),

\displaystyle x_{i}

\displaystyle=[-(n+1)a,m]z\quad(2\leq i\leq p),

\displaystyle x_{i}

\displaystyle=[-(n+1)a,m+1]z\quad(p+1\leq i\leq\ell).

Thus
\mathscr{A}^{K}=\mathbf{c}\mathscr{E}^{p-1}_{\ell-1}(n+1,a,m)
.
Hence
\mathscr{A}^{K}
is ind-flag-accurate by the induction hypothesis with exponents

\exp\left(\mathbf{c}\mathscr{E}^{p-1}_{\ell-1}(n+1,a,m)\right)=(1,\ell-p+1,2,3,\ldots,\ell-1)+(0,(m+a(\ell+n-1))^{\ell-1}).

Finally, thanks to Lemma
3.1
,
\mathscr{A}
is ind-flag-accurate.
This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Remark 7.30
.

We remark that in the cases
p=0
, or
p=1
, one may consider a different restriction to deduce the ind-flag-accuracy of
\mathbf{c}\mathscr{E}^{p}_{\ell}(n,a,m)
.
Indeed, by Case
1
in the proof of Theorem
7.29
the restriction of
\mathbf{c}\mathscr{E}^{p}_{\ell}(n,a,m)
to the hyperplane given by

is identical to
\mathbf{c}\mathscr{E}^{0}_{\ell-1}(n,a,m+a)
whose multiset of exponents is
(1,m+a(\ell+n-1)+i+1)_{i=1}^{\ell-1}
.

We give some comments on the cone
\mathbf{c}\Cat^{m}(A_{\ell})
over the extended Catalan arrangement of type
A_{\ell}
.
It was shown to be inductively free by Edelman and Reiner
[
ER96
, Thm. 3.2]
.
Recently, Tsujie and Nakashima
[
NT22
, Cor. 4.2]
proved that
\mathbf{c}\Cat^{m}(A_{\ell})
is even
hereditarily inductively free
i.e., every restriction of
\mathbf{c}\Cat^{m}(A_{\ell})
is inductively free.
Our result below confirms the ind-flag-accuracy of
\mathbf{c}\Cat^{m}(A_{\ell})
, thanks to Theorem
7.29
.

Corollary 7.31
.

The extended Catalan arrangement
\Cat^{m}(A_{\ell})
of type
A_{\ell}
(Definition
5.1
) affinely equivalent to
\mathscr{E}^{\ell}_{\ell}(1,m,m)
or
\mathscr{E}^{0}_{\ell}(1,m,m-1)
with
m\geq 1
has ind-flag-accurate cone with exponents
(1,m(\ell+1)+i)_{i=1}^{\ell}
.

Moreover, the inductive argument in the proof of Theorem
7.29
provides a simple root witness for the ind-flag-accuracy of
\mathbf{c}\Cat^{m}(A_{\ell})
as follows.
Let
H_{1},\ldots,H_{\ell}
denote the hyperplanes
x_{1}=-mz
and
x_{i-1}-x_{i}=mz
for
2\leq i\leq\ell
in
\mathbf{c}\mathscr{E}^{0}_{\ell}(1,m,m-1)
, or equivalently
x_{i}-x_{i+1}=mz
(
1\leq i\leq\ell
) in
\mathbf{c}\Cat^{m}(A_{\ell})
.
Set
X_{i}:=\bigcap_{j=1}^{i}H_{j}
for
1\leq i\leq\ell-1
. Then
X_{\ell-1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq X_{1}
is a witness for the ind-flag-accuracy of
\mathbf{c}\Cat^{m}(A_{\ell})
.

Next we investigate the ind-flag-accuracy of the first descendants.

Theorem 7.32
.

Let
m\geq 0
,
c,\ell\geq 1
and
0\leq p\leq\ell
.
The first descendant
\widehat{\mathscr{R}}^{p,1}_{\ell}(c,m)
of the Catalan descendant sequence from Definition
7.25
has ind-flag-accurate cone with exponents

\exp\left(\mathbf{c}\widehat{\mathscr{R}}^{p,1}_{\ell}(c,m)\right)=(1,\ell-p+1,2,3,\ldots,\ell)+(0,(m+\ell+c-1)^{\ell}).

Proof.

First we derive the inductive freeness of
\mathscr{A}:=\mathbf{c}\widehat{\mathscr{R}}^{p,1}_{\ell}(c,m)
.
Note that when
p=\ell
, the arrangement
\mathscr{A}=\mathbf{c}\widehat{\mathscr{R}}^{\ell,1}_{\ell}(c,m)
consists of the hyperplanes

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle x_{i}-x_{j}

\displaystyle=[-1,1]z\quad(1\leq i<j\leq\ell-1),

\displaystyle x_{i}-x_{\ell}

\displaystyle=[-1,0]z\quad(1\leq i\leq\ell-1),

\displaystyle x_{i}

\displaystyle=[-c-1,m]z\quad(1\leq i\leq\ell-1),

\displaystyle x_{\ell}

\displaystyle=[-c,m]z.

One may apply Proposition
2.8
to show that the subarrangement
\mathscr{B}\subseteq\mathscr{A}
consisting of

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle x_{i}-x_{j}

Now applying Proposition
3.3
(ii)

\mathscr{A}\in\mathcal{IF}
with the desired exponents.

Suppose
0\leq p\leq\ell-1
.
Define the lexicographic order on

\{(\ell,\ell-p)\mid 1\leq\ell-p\leq\ell,\,\ell\geq 1\}.

We show
\mathscr{A}=\mathbf{c}\widehat{\mathscr{R}}^{p,1}_{\ell}(c,m)\in\mathcal{IF}
with the desired exponents by induction on
(\ell,\ell-p)
.
When
\ell=1
, it is obvious. Suppose
\ell\geq 2
.

Case
1
. First let
0\leq p\leq\ell-2
.
Then
\mathscr{A}
consists of the hyperplanes

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle x_{i}-x_{j}

\displaystyle=[-1,1]z\quad(1\leq i<j\leq\ell-1),

\displaystyle x_{i}-x_{\ell}

\displaystyle=[-1,0]z\quad(1\leq i\leq\ell-1),

\displaystyle x_{i}

\displaystyle=[-c-1,m]z\quad(1\leq i\leq p),

\displaystyle x_{i}

\displaystyle=[-c-1,m+1]z\quad(p<i\leq\ell-1),

\displaystyle=[-c,m+1]z.

H\in\mathscr{A}

.

with exponents

with exponents

\exp\left(\mathbf{c}\widehat{\mathscr{R}}^{0,1}_{\ell-1}(c,m+1)\right)=(1,2,3,\ldots,\ell)+(0,(m+\ell+c-1)^{\ell-1}),

with exponents

.

We are now in a position to derive the main result of this subsection.
We remark that unlike in the case of Shi genealogy, the modular coatoms from simplicial isolated vertices of the digraph do not automatically imply the ind-flag-accuracy of the Catalan descendants.
It is crucial to explicitly construct a witness.

Theorem 7.33
.

Let
m\geq 0
,
c\geq 1
,
1\leq k\leq\ell
, and
0\leq p\leq\ell
.
The cones over the Catalan descendants
\mathscr{R}^{p,k}_{\ell}(c,m)
and
\widehat{\mathscr{R}}^{p,k}_{\ell}(c,m)
are ind-flag-accurate with exponents

\exp\left(\mathbf{c}\mathscr{R}^{p,k}_{\ell}(c,m)\right)=\exp\left(\mathbf{c}\widehat{\mathscr{R}}^{p,k}_{\ell}(c,m)\right)=(1,\ell-p+1,2,3,\ldots,\ell)+(0,(m+\ell+c-1)^{\ell}).

Proof.

By Theorems
7.29
,
7.32
, Remark
7.27
and Proposition
7.28
, it suffices to prove for any fixed
1\leq p\leq\ell
and
2\leq k\leq\ell-1
that the cones

\mathbf{c}\mathscr{R}^{p,k}_{\ell}(c,m)
,
\mathbf{c}\widehat{\mathscr{R}}^{p,k}_{\ell}(c,m)
are ind-flag-accurate with exponents
(1,\ell-p+1,2,3,\ldots,\ell)+(0,(m+\ell+c-1)^{\ell})
.
There are two cases to consider,
p\leq\ell-k
versus
p>\ell-k
.
Since the proofs use a similar method, we give an argument only for the former (which is the more difficult one).

First we show the assertion for
\mathscr{A}:=\mathbf{c}\mathscr{R}^{p,k}_{\ell}(c,m)
.
Note that by Proposition
7.26
the arrangement
\mathscr{B}:=\textbf{c}\mathscr{A}\left(K_{\ell}^{k}[\ell-k+1],\psi_{\ell}^{p,k}|_{[\ell-k+1]}\right)
is given by

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle x_{i}-x_{j}

\displaystyle=[-1,1]z\quad(1\leq i<j\leq\ell+1-k),

\displaystyle x_{i}

\displaystyle=[1-c-k,m+k-1]z\quad(1\leq i\leq p),

\displaystyle x_{i}

\displaystyle=[1-c-k,m+k]z\quad(p<i\leq\ell+1-k).

Hence

\mathscr{B}=\textbf{c}\mathscr{R}^{p,1}_{\ell+1-k}(c+k-1,m+k-1),

is ind-flag-accurate with

\exp(\mathscr{B})=(1,\ell-p+1,k+1,k+2,\ldots,\ell)+(0,(m+\ell+c-1)^{\ell-k+1}),

by Theorem
7.29
.

Owing to Proposition
7.26
, each isolated vertex
n\in[\ell-k+2,\ell]
is simplicial in
\left(K_{\ell}^{k}[n],\psi_{\ell}^{p,k}|_{[n]}\right)
with
\psi_{\ell}^{p,k}(n)=[-c-\ell+n,m+\ell+1-n]
.
Applying Corollary
7.5
(i) repeatedly to the
k-1
simplicial vertices
\ell,\ell-1,\ldots,\ell-k+2
in this order, we are able to deduce that

\mathscr{A}\in\mathcal{IF}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathscr{B}\in\mathcal{IF}.

Thus the inductive freeness of
\mathscr{A}
is clear.
Moreover,

\exp(\mathscr{A})=(1,\ell-p+1,2,3,\ldots,\ell)+(0,(m+\ell+c-1)^{\ell}),

since the isolated vertices
n\in[\ell-k+2,\ell]
contribute the exponents

|\psi_{\ell}^{p,k}(n)|+n-1=(m+\ell+c-1)+(\ell-n+2)

with
2\leq\ell-n+2\leq k

to
\exp(\mathscr{A})
.

Now we demonstrate the ind-flag-accuracy of
\mathscr{A}
.
Note that unlike in the proof of Theorem
7.22
for Shi descendants, we cannot appeal to Corollary
7.5
(ii) here, since the exponent
(m+\ell+c-1)+e
in
\exp(\mathscr{B})
for some
k+1\leq e\leq\ell
exceeds
|\psi_{\ell}^{p,k}(n)|+n-1
for each
n\in[\ell-k+2,\ell]
.
We may overcome this difficulty by making use of a certain witness for the ind-flag-accuracy of
\mathscr{B}
hinted at in
Theorem
7.29
.

Define
\ell-k
hyperplanes
H_{1},\ldots,H_{\ell-k}\in\mathscr{A}
as follows:

H_{1}:x_{1}=(1-c-k)z,\quad H_{j}:x_{j-1}-x_{j}=z\quad(2\leq j\leq\ell-k).

For each
1\leq s\leq\ell-k
, set

\displaystyle X_{s}

\displaystyle:=\bigcap_{j=1}^{s}H_{j},\text{ and }

\displaystyle M_{s}

\displaystyle:=\{(1-c-k)-(j-1)\mid 1\leq j\leq s\}=[2-c-k-s,1-c-k].

Then
X_{\ell-k}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq X_{2}\subseteq X_{1}\subseteq V^{\prime}=\mathbb{R}^{\ell+1}
and
\dim_{V^{\prime}}(X_{s})=\ell+1-s
.

We show that this flag is part of a witness for the ind-flag-accuracy of
\mathscr{A}
.
First we show that each restriction
\mathscr{A}^{X_{s}}
is inductively free and compute its exponents.

If
1\leq s\leq p
then by the proof of the ind-flag-accuracy in Theorem
7.29
,
\mathscr{A}^{X_{s}}
consists of the hyperplanes

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle x_{i}-x_{j}

\displaystyle=[-1,1]z\quad(s+1\leq i<j\leq\ell+1-k),

\displaystyle x_{i}

\displaystyle=[1-c-k-s,m+k-1]z\quad(s+1\leq i\leq p),

\displaystyle x_{i}

\displaystyle=[1-c-k-s,m+k]z\quad(p<i\leq\ell+1-k),

\displaystyle x_{i}

\displaystyle=(M_{s}\sqcup[-c-\ell+i,m+\ell+1-i])z\quad(\ell+1-k<i\leq\ell).

Upon applying Corollary
7.5
(i) repeatedly to the
k-1
simplicial isolated vertices
\ell,\ell-1,\ldots,\ell-k+2
in this order, we delineate that

\mathscr{A}^{X_{s}}\in\mathcal{IF}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\textbf{c}\mathscr{R}^{p-s,1}_{\ell+1-k-s}(c+k+s-1,m+k-1)\in\mathcal{IF}.

By Theorem
7.29
,
\mathscr{A}^{X_{s}}
is indeed inductively free with

\exp\left(\mathscr{A}^{X_{s}}\right)=(1,\ell-p+1,2,\ldots,k,k+1,\ldots,\ell-s)+(0,(m+\ell+c-1)^{\ell-k+1-s}).

Note that the isolated vertices from
[\ell-k+2,\ell]
continue to contribute the exponents
(m+\ell+c-1)+(\ell-i+2)
with
2\leq\ell-i+2\leq k
to
\exp\left(\mathscr{A}^{X_{s}}\right)
.

If
p<s\leq\ell-k
then
\mathscr{A}^{X_{s}}
is given by replacing the third and fourth set of the hyperplanes in the previous case by

x_{i}=[1-c-k-s,m+k]z\quad(s+1\leq i\leq\ell+1-k).

Therefore, in this case

\mathscr{A}^{X_{s}}\in\mathcal{IF}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\textbf{c}\mathscr{R}^{\ell+1-k-s,1}_{\ell+1-k-s}(c+k+s-1,m+k)\in\mathcal{IF}.

Again, by Theorem
7.29
,
\mathscr{A}^{X_{s}}
is inductively free with

\exp\left(\mathscr{A}^{X_{s}}\right)=(1,2,\ldots,k,k+1,\ldots,\ell-s+1)+(0,(m+\ell+c-1)^{\ell-k+1-s}).

A simple comparison of the exponents shows that the flag
X_{\ell-k}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq X_{2}\subseteq X_{1}\subseteq V^{\prime}=\mathbb{R}^{\ell+1}
is indeed part of a witness for the ind-flag-accuracy of
\mathscr{A}
.
Moreover,
\mathscr{A}^{X_{\ell-k}}
is a strictly nested
N
-Ish arrangement hence is ind-flag-accurate, by Lemma
7.8
.

By Lemma
3.1
, we conclude that
\mathscr{A}
is ind-flag-accurate.
Note that the flag above can be easily extended to a proper witness for
\mathscr{A}
thanks to the construction of a witness in Lemma
7.8
.
This completes the proof of the ind-flag-accuracy of
\mathscr{A}
.

The proof of the assertion for
\mathbf{c}\widehat{\mathscr{R}}^{p,k}_{\ell}(c,m)
runs essentially along the same lines as the argument above with the use of Theorem
7.32
in place of Theorem
7.29
.
∎

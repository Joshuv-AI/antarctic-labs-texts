Ultrafilter extensions of bounded graphs are elementary
Zalán Molnár

Abstract




Abstract.
The main motivation of this paper is the study of first-order model theoretic properties of structures having their roots in modal logic. We will focus on the connections between ultrafilter extensions and ultrapowers. We show that certain structures (called bounded graphs) are elementary substructures of their ultrafilter extensions, moreover their modal logics coincide.

keywords:
ultrafilter extension, elementary substructures, bounded graphs.

1.
Overview

There are two different approaches that are called ultrafilter extensions of structures (graphs) equipped with a single binary relation. The recently introduced one has its origins in model theory
[

]

[

28

, while the other emerged from modal logics and universal algebra and was first introduced in
[

. The connections between the extensions were initially investigated in
[
29

. In this paper, we will focus on the second type of extension that has become a fundamental construction for understanding modal logics and their algebraic counterpart. Our motivation stems from one of the central areas in model theory of modal logics – namely its interconnection with first-order logic. This topic has been tackled from various sides. Example of this include: correspondence theory,
[
6
,
24
]
, definability
[
18
,
15

, bounded fragments of first-order logic
[

]
, canonicity
[

. Some of these connections are guided by the “proportion”:

\displaystyle\frac{\text{Modal logic}}{\text{Bisimulation}}\approx\frac{\text{First-order logic }}{\text{Partial isomorphism }}

That is “
bisimulations are to modal logic, what partial isomorphism is for first-order logic
”. This was put forward by de Rijke in
[
23
]
and was further explicated through the investigation of bounded fragments. Later, in
[
30
]
Venema improved de Rijke’s results by invoking the “proportion”:

\displaystyle\frac{\text{Modal logic}}{\text{Ultrafilter extension}}\approx\frac{\text{First-order logic }}{\text{Ultrapowers}}

By introducing the construction called
ultrafilter union
, Venema justified the phrase
“taking an ultrafilter union of a set of models is the modal analogue of taking ultraproducts in the model theory of first-order logic”
.

Our primary focus will be on the second “proportion” by studying the two constructions, and investigating how the resulted new structures can be related to each other. From this approach, the following questions mainly issued by

What can be said about the

•

What can we say about the

•

What kind of

are preserved under taking ultraflter extension?

•

What kind of connections can we have between the ultrafilter extensions and ultrapowers?

In general, it is not surprising that the ultrafilter extension, as opposed to ultrapowers, may fail to preserve first-order formulas. As a result, the literature mostly posed and partially answered the question; “
What type of first-order formulas are preserved under taking ultrafilter extensions?
”
[

,
8
,
5
,
18
]
. This turned out to be
\Pi_{1}^{1}
-hard, hence no such characterization can exist
[

.
Also, similar questions concerning the first type of ultrafilter extension were addressed in
[
27
]
(cf. Problem 5.2, and Theorem 5.3). Here we take the reverse approach, surprisingly untouched by the literature and ask;
“What type of structures are elementary equivalent to their ultrafilter extension?”
.
Hence, instead of formulas, we concentrate on structures, attempting to isolate classes for which ultrafilter extensions and ultrapowers share structural features.
Our main results are proved in Section 4. We show that for certain structures (called bounded graphs) their ultrafilter extension has a characteristic similar to Łoś’s Lemma. As a result of this, they are found to be elementary substructures to their ultrafilter extension. Additionally, their modal logics coincide.

Preliminary notions, background

Semantics

Here we recall all the necessary notions from the semantics of modal logics and fix the notation. The reader who is more interested in this area is referred to
[
12
,
2
,
3
]
and
[
11
]
. Throughout the paper we consider structures
\mathfrak{F}=\langle W,R\rangle
to be
directed graphs
for the first-order language
\mathcal{L}_{R}
with equality, containing a single binary relation symbol
R
.

As opposed to the first-order relational language
\mathcal{L}_{R}
, the standard propositional modal language
\mathcal{L}_{\diamondsuit}
is the extension of the propositional language by a single unary connective
\diamondsuit
. A
model
for
\mathcal{L}_{\diamondsuit}
is a pair
\mathfrak{M}=\langle\mathfrak{F},V\rangle
, where
\mathfrak{F}
is the underlying structure and
V:\Phi\to\mathcal{P}(W)
is a function, called
valuation
. The relation
\mathfrak{M},w\Vdash\varphi
reads as the truth of
\varphi\in\mathsf{Form}(\mathcal{L}_{\diamondsuit})
in the model
\mathfrak{M}
at
w\in W
, and is defined in the usual recursive manner. The only non-obvious case is

•

\mathfrak{M},w\Vdash\diamondsuit\varphi\Leftrightarrow(\exists v\in W)Rwv
and
\mathfrak{M},v\Vdash\varphi
.

Moreover,
\mathfrak{M}\Vdash\varphi
denotes the fact that for all
w\in W
we have
\mathfrak{M},w\Vdash\varphi
, and
\mathfrak{F}\Vdash\varphi
abbreviates the statement that for all valuation
V
, the relation
\mathfrak{M}\Vdash\varphi
holds. Finally, the
modal theory
(or
logic
) of
\mathfrak{F}
is denoted by
\Lambda(\mathfrak{F})=\{\varphi\in\mathsf{Form}(\mathcal{L}_{\diamondsuit}):\mathfrak{F}\Vdash\varphi\}
. Since our motivation is to study the first-order structural and modal properties both appearing at the level of frames, we confine ourselves into this segment of the semantics.

Ultrafilter extension

Let
\mathfrak{F}=\langle W,R\rangle
be a structure and
X\subseteq W
. We define the following operations on
\mathcal{P}(W)
:

\displaystyle R^{+}(X)

\displaystyle=\{s\in W:\exists w(Rws\wedge w\in X)\}

\displaystyle R^{-}(X)

\displaystyle=\{w\in W:\exists s(Rws\wedge s\in X)\}

\displaystyle R(X)

\displaystyle=R^{-}(X)\cup R^{+}(X)

\displaystyle l_{R}(X)

\displaystyle=\{w\in W:\forall v(Rwv\to v\in X)\}

If
X=\{w\}
, we use the shorthand notation
R(w)
instead of
R(\{w\})
and similarly for the other operations. The
ultrafilter
(or
canonical
)
extension
of
\mathfrak{F}
is the structure
\mathfrak{F}^{\mathfrak{ue}}=\langle\mathrm{Uf}(W),R^{\mathfrak{ue}}\rangle
, where
\mathrm{Uf}(W)
is the set of all ultrafilters over
W
and the relation
R^{\mathfrak{ue}}
is defined as:

R^{\mathfrak{ue}}uv\Leftrightarrow(\forall X\in v)(R^{-}(X)\in u)

It is easy to see that the following equivalence holds between the ultrafilter relation and the operations defined above:

R^{\mathfrak{ue}}uv\Leftrightarrow\{Y:l_{R}(Y)\in u\}\subseteq v\Leftrightarrow\{R^{+}(X):X\in u\}\subseteq v.

By
\pi_{w}=\{X\subseteq W:w\in X\}
we denote the principal ultrafilter generated by
w
, and
\mathrm{Uf}^{*}(W)
denotes the set of non-principal ultrafilters. The function
\eta:\mathfrak{F}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{F}^{\mathfrak{ue}}
, where
\eta(w)=\pi_{w}
is an embedding, called the
canonical embedding
(of
\mathfrak{F}
into
\mathfrak{F}^{\mathfrak{ue}}
). For a model
\mathfrak{M}=\langle\mathfrak{F},V\rangle
, its ultrafilter extension is given by
\mathfrak{M}^{\mathfrak{ue}}=\langle\mathfrak{F}^{\mathfrak{ue}},V^{\mathfrak{ue}}\rangle
, where
V^{\mathfrak{ue}}(p)=\{u\in\mathrm{Uf}(W):V(p)\in u\}
. A well-known fact is the equivalence (cf. Proposition 2.59 from
[
2
]
):

(1)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}^{\mathfrak{ue}},u\Vdash\varphi\Leftrightarrow V(\varphi)\in u,

for all
u\in\mathrm{Uf}(W)
and
\varphi\in\mathsf{Form}(\mathcal{L}_{\diamondsuit})
, from which it follows that
\Lambda(\mathfrak{F})\subseteq\Lambda(\mathfrak{F}^{\mathfrak{ue}})
.

Model theoretic notions

Using standard notation, for
\mathfrak{F}=\langle W,R\rangle
, and
\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}=\langle W^{\prime},R^{\prime}\rangle
by
\mathfrak{F}\leq\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}
, we denote the fact that
\mathfrak{F}
is a substructure of
\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}
and
\mathfrak{F}\preceq\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}
means that
\mathfrak{F}
is an elementary substructure of
\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}
. Elementary equivalence is denoted by
\mathfrak{F}\equiv\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}
.
We recall that a set
\Gamma

with a free variable

is

in the structure

, such that
\mathfrak{F}\models\varphi(w)
, for each
\varphi(x)\in\Gamma
. Then
\Gamma
is

if each finite subset of
\Gamma
is satisfied in
\mathfrak{F}
. For a cardinal
\kappa
, the structure
\mathfrak{F}
is
\kappa
-saturated
if for each
A\in[W]^{<\kappa}
and set of formulas
\Gamma
in the extended language
\mathcal{L}\cup\{c_{a}:a\in A\}
containing a free variable
x
, the set
\Gamma
is satisfied, whenever
\Gamma
is finitely satisfied. A well-known fact on saturation is the following theorem (cf. Theorem 6.1.8 in
[
11
]
):

Fact 2.1
.

For any structure
\mathfrak{F}
and cardinal
\kappa
there exists a
\kappa
-saturated ultrapower of
\mathfrak{F}
.

Ultrapower vs. ultrafilter extension

Our primary interest is in the potential connections between an ultraproduct of
\mathfrak{F}
and
\mathfrak{F}^{\mathfrak{ue}}
. According to Lemma 3.8 from
[
5
]
,
\mathfrak{F}^{\mathfrak{ue}}
is always a
p
-morphic image of a suitably saturated ultrapower of
\mathfrak{F}
. Also it is proved in
[
8
]
(Theorem 15.11) and later generalized in
[
17
]
(Theorem 4.2.5), that a first-order formula is preserved under
p
-morphisms if and only if it is equivalent to an essentially positive formula (for the definition:
[
17
]
, Section 4). Hence, essentially positive formulas are preserved under taking ultrafilter extensions. Another important
\mathcal{L}_{R}
-fragment is the class of
r(u)
-formulas introduced by
[
8
]
in Definition 15.17, where
u
is a fixed variable. This class enjoys the following “Łoś Lemma-like” characterization: For any structure
\mathfrak{F}
,
r(u)
-formula
\varphi(u,x_{0},\dots,x_{n})
and
w_{0},\dots,w_{n}\in W
,
v\in\mathrm{Uf}(W)
we have,

(2)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}^{\mathfrak{ue}}\models\varphi(v,\pi_{w_{0}},\dots,\pi_{w_{n}})\text{ iff }\{s\in W:\mathfrak{F}\models\varphi(s,w_{0},\dots w_{n})\}\in v.

Hence the existential closure of an
r(u)
-formula is preserved by passing from
\mathfrak{F}
to
\mathfrak{F}^{\mathfrak{ue}}
.

The exact syntactic nature of formulas that are preserved under taking ultrafilter extensions is proved to be
\Pi_{1}^{1}
-hard by
[
10
]
. As a result, it is not surprising that elementarily equivalence is by far out of reach, as the following textbook example shows.

Example 2.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{N}=\langle\mathbb{N},<\rangle
, its ultrafilter extension can be illustrated as:
\mathfrak{N}^{\mathfrak{ue}}
\pi_{0}
\pi_{1}
\pi_{2}
\dots
cluster of
non-principal
ultrafilters

where for any ultrafilter
u\in\mathrm{Uf}(\mathbb{N})
and
v\in\mathrm{Uf}^{*}(\mathbb{N})
we have
u<^{\mathfrak{ue}}v
. Trivially
\mathfrak{N}\models\forall x(x\not<x)
, but
\mathfrak{N}^{\mathfrak{ue}}\models\exists x(x<x)
, hence
\mathfrak{N}\not\equiv\mathfrak{N}^{\mathfrak{ue}}
.

Intuitively, the failure was due to the fact that each
n\in\mathbb{N}
had infinitely many
<
-successors. In order to characterize subclasses e.g. whose elements are elementary equivalent to their ultrafilter extensions, it is natural to impose conditions on the relation in question.

Definition 3.2
.

Let

be
different
ultrafilters over

Proposition 3.3
.

Fix any structure
\mathfrak{F}=\langle W,R\rangle
and
u\in\mathrm{Uf}(W)
. If
\{w\in W:\deg^{+}(w)\leq n\}\in u
, then
\deg^{+}(u)\leq n
.

Proof.

Let
X=\{w\in W:\deg^{+}(w)\leq n\}
. By way of contradiction, assume that
\deg^{+}(u)>n
. For

let

be different ultrafilters, such that

and fix some distinguishing elements

. Then
\bigcap_{i\leq n}R^{-}(D_{v_{i}})\cap X\in u
. Hence there is some

with
\deg^{+}(s)>n
, which is a contradiction.

Let
\mathsf{K}

5.
Discussion

Regarding the other type of ultrafilter extension,
[
28
]
wonders about theories such that elementary equivalence of their models lifts to their
ultrafilter extensions. Here, we have an immediate corollary:

Corollary 5.1
.

For any bounded graphs
\mathfrak{F}
and
\mathfrak{G}
if
\mathfrak{F}\equiv\mathfrak{G}
, then
\mathfrak{F}^{\mathfrak{ue}}\equiv\mathfrak{G}^{\mathfrak{ue}}
.

It is natural to ask what other type of infinite structures can be isolated within the class

\mathsf{F}=\{\mathfrak{F}:\mathfrak{F}\equiv\mathfrak{F}^{\mathfrak{ue}}\}

besides bounded graphs.

We note that
\mathsf{F}
is not an elementary class, since it is not closed under ultraproducts. To see this, consider the structures
\mathfrak{N}_{n}=\langle n,<\rangle
with their standard orderings. Then for any non-principal ultrafilter
D
over
\omega
, we have that
\prod_{n\in\omega}\mathfrak{N}_{n}/D\models\forall x(x\not<x)
, but
\big(\prod_{n\in\omega}\mathfrak{N}_{n}/D\big)^{\mathfrak{ue}}\models\exists x(x<x)
, similarly to Example
2.2
. We wonder whether
\mathsf{F}
is closed under elementary equivalence, that is given
\mathfrak{F}\in\mathsf{F}
and
\mathfrak{F}\equiv\mathfrak{G}
does it imply
\mathfrak{G}\in\mathsf{F}
?

Growth of cosmic perturbations in the modified f ⁡ ( R , T ) f(R,T) gravity
Mahnaz Asghari, Ahmad Sheykhi

Abstract
We explore the generalized f ⁡ ( R , T ) f(R,T) modified theory of gravity, where the gravitational Lagrangian is a function of Ricci scalar R R and the trace of the energy-momentum tensor T T . We derive modified field equations to the linear order of perturbations in the context of f ⁡ ( R , T ) f(R,T) model. We then investigate the growth of perturbations in the context of f ⁡ ( R , T ) f(R,T) modified gravity. Primary numerical investigations based on matter power spectra diagrams indicate a structure growth suppression in f ⁡ ( R , T ) f(R,T) gravity, which exhibits consistency with local measurements. Also, we notice that matter-geometry interaction in f ⁡ ( R , T ) f(R,T) model would results in the specific feature named as ”matter acoustic oscillations” appeared in matter power spectra diagrams. Moreover, we put constraints on the cosmological parameters of f ⁡ ( R , T ) f(R,T) model, utilizing current observations, chiefly cosmic microwave background (CMB), weak lensing, supernovae, baryon acoustic oscillations, and redshift-space distortions data. Numerical results based on MCMC calculations imply that f ⁡ ( R , T ) f(R,T) is a qualified theory of modified gravity in reconciling Planck CMB data with local probes of large scale structures, by reporting lower values for the structure growth parameter σ 8 \sigma_{8} compared to the standard model of cosmology.

II
Fundamental formulation of
f(R,T)
gravity

In this section we explore the modified gravitational field
equations based on
f(R,T)
gravity. The total action
corresponding to
f(R,T)
modified gravity can be provided as

[
46
]

\displaystyle S=\frac{1}{16\pi G}\int{\mathrm{d}^{4}x\,\sqrt{-g}f(R,T)}+\int{\mathrm{d}^{4}x\,\sqrt{-g}L_{m}}\,,

(1)

where
R
is the Ricci scalar,
T
denotes the trace of
the energy-momentum tensor (
T=g^{\mu\nu}T_{\mu\nu}
),
and
L_{m}
stands for the matter Lagrangian density.
Contemplating action (
1
), it is possible to derive
the associated field equations of
f(R,T)
model as
[
46
]

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu}\frac{\partial f}{\partial R}-\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\frac{\partial f}{\partial R}+g_{\mu\nu}\Box\frac{\partial f}{\partial R}-\frac{1}{2}fg_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=8\pi GT_{\mu\nu}-\frac{\partial f}{\partial T}\big(T_{\mu\nu}+\Theta_{\mu\nu}\big)\,,

(2)

with
[
46
]

\displaystyle\Theta_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\equiv g^{\alpha\beta}\frac{\delta T_{\alpha\beta}}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}

\displaystyle=-2T_{\mu\nu}+g_{\mu\nu}L_{m}-2g^{\alpha\beta}\frac{\partial^{2}L_{m}}{\partial g^{\mu\nu}\partial g^{\alpha\beta}}\,.

(3)

We consider the energy content of the universe as a perfect fluid
with the following energy-momentum tensor

T_{\mu\nu}=\big(\rho+p\big)u_{\mu}u_{\nu}+pg_{\mu\nu}\,,

(4)

where
\rho
,
p
, and
u_{\mu}
are energy density, pressure, and
four-velocity, respectively.
It should be noted that, the on-shell matter Lagrangian of a
perfect fluid can be considered as
L_{m}=p
,
L_{m}=-\rho
,
and
L_{m}=T

[
94
]
, where the case
L_{m}=T
is appropriate
for describing fluids with
0\leq w\leq 1/3
,
while
L_{m}=p
and
L_{m}=-\rho
are suitable for
describing dark energy fluid (in which
w\simeq-1
).
Accordingly, in the present study we choose
L_{m}=-\rho
,
which results in

\Theta_{\mu\nu}=-2T_{\mu\nu}-\rho g_{\mu\nu}\,,

(5)

where we have assumed linear dependency of
L_{m}
on the metric,
being valid for this choice of matter Lagrangian
[
95
]
.
Thus, modified field equations take the form

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu}\frac{\partial f}{\partial R}-\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\frac{\partial f}{\partial R}+g_{\mu\nu}\Box\frac{\partial f}{\partial R}-\frac{1}{2}fg_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=8\pi GT_{\mu\nu}+\frac{\partial f}{\partial T}\big(T_{\mu\nu}+\rho g_{\mu\nu}\big)\,.

(6)

In the present study, for sake of simplicity, we contemplate
the following functional form of
f(R,T)

[
46
]

f(R,T)=R+2f(T)\,,

(7)

where

f(T)=8\pi G\lambda T\,,

(8)

with dimensionless constant
\lambda
. Then, modified field
equations of
f(R,T)
theory can be written as

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}Rg_{\mu\nu}=8\pi G\Big((1+2\lambda)T_{\mu\nu}+\lambda Tg_{\mu\nu}+2\lambda\rho g_{\mu\nu}\Big)\,.

(9)

Accordingly, comparing to GR gravity,
the coefficient of metric on the right hand side
of equation (
9
) might be considered as an effective
cosmological constant which depends on the trace
T

[
58
]
.
On the other hand, the divergence of the energy-momentum tensor
according to field equations (
9
), results in

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}T^{\mu}_{\nu}=-\frac{\lambda}{1+2\lambda}\partial_{\nu}\big(\rho+3p\big)\,.

(10)

Based on the cosmological principle, we further assume
that our universe is spatially homogeneous and isotropic on the
large scales. The metric of such universe is described by the
well-known Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) spacetime.
For the spatially flat background, the corresponding line elements
takes the form

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}s^{2}=a^{2}(\tau)\big(-\mathrm{d}\tau^{2}+\mathrm{d}\vec{x}^{2}\big)\,.

(11)

Thereupon, modified Friedmann equations are given by

\displaystyle H^{2}=\frac{8\pi G}{3}\Big((1+\lambda)\sum_{i}\bar{\rho}_{i}-3\lambda\sum_{i}\bar{p}_{i}\Big)\,,

(12)

\displaystyle 2\frac{H^{\prime}}{a}+3H^{2}=-8\pi G\Big(\lambda\sum_{i}\bar{\rho}_{i}+(1+5\lambda)\sum_{i}\bar{p}_{i}\Big)\,,

(13)

where the prime indicates deviation with respect to the conformal
time,
H=a^{\prime}/a^{2}
is the Hubble parameter, and index
i

indicates the component
i
th in the universe filled with
radiation (R), baryons (B), dark matter (DM) and cosmological
constant (
\Lambda
). Taking account of equation (
12
), for
the total density parameter

\Omega_{\mathrm{tot}}=\sum_{i}\bar{\rho}_{i}/\rho_{\mathrm{cr}}
(with

\rho_{\mathrm{cr}}=3H^{2}/(8\pi G)
) in
f(R,T)
gravity, we find

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathrm{tot}}=\frac{1}{1+\lambda}\bigg(1+\frac{\lambda}{H^{2}}8\pi G\sum_{i}\bar{p}_{i}\bigg)\,.

(14)

Then, it is obvious that choosing
\lambda=0
will restore
Einstein field equations in standard cosmology.

Notably, in the special case of a dust matter dominated
universe, equation (
12
) reduces to
H^{2}=\frac{8\pi G}{3}(1+\lambda)\sum_{i}\bar{\rho}_{i}
, which indicates that the
identified
f(R,T)
model in equations (
7
) and (
8
)
corresponds to a gravitational theory with a time dependent
cosmological constant, where the gravitational interaction between
matter and curvature is modified by the term
2f(T)
in the
gravitational action, replacing the gravitational constant
G
by
a running gravitational coupling parameter
G_{\rm eff}

[
46
,
47
]
.

Also it is important to study linear scalar perturbations
in modified
f(R,T)
gravity, based on the perturbed
FLRW metric in the synchronous gauge (syn) given by

\mathrm{d}s^{2}=a^{2}(\tau)\Big(-\mathrm{d}\tau^{2}+\big(\delta_{ij}+h_{ij}\big)\mathrm{d}x^{i}\mathrm{d}x^{j}\Big)

(15)

where

\displaystyle h_{ij}(\vec{x},\tau)

\displaystyle=\int\mathrm{d}^{3}k\,e^{i\vec{k}.\vec{x}}\bigg(\hat{k}_{i}\hat{k}_{j}h(\vec{k},\tau)

\displaystyle+\Big(\hat{k}_{i}\hat{k}_{j}-\frac{1}{3}\delta_{ij}\Big)6\eta(\vec{k},\tau)\bigg)\,,

(16)

with scalar perturbations
h
and
\eta
,
and
\vec{k}=k\hat{k}

[
96
]
.
So, the corresponding modified field equations become

\displaystyle\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}h^{\prime}-2k^{2}\eta

\displaystyle=8\pi Ga^{2}\Big((1+\lambda)\sum_{i}\delta\rho_{i(\mathrm{syn})}

\displaystyle-3\lambda\sum_{i}\delta p_{i(\mathrm{syn})}\Big)\,,

(17)

\displaystyle k^{2}\eta^{\prime}=4\pi G(1+2\lambda)a^{2}\sum_{i}\big(\bar{\rho}_{i}+\bar{p}_{i}\big)\theta_{i(\mathrm{syn})}\,,

(18)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}h^{\prime\prime}+3\eta^{\prime\prime}+\big(h^{\prime}+6\eta^{\prime}\big)\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}-k^{2}\eta=0\,,

(19)

\displaystyle-2\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}h^{\prime}-h^{\prime\prime}+2k^{2}\eta

\displaystyle=24\pi Ga^{2}\Big(\lambda\sum_{i}\delta\rho_{i(\mathrm{syn})}

\displaystyle+(1+5\lambda)\sum_{i}\delta p_{i(\mathrm{syn})}\Big)\,.

(20)

In parallel, for the perturbed FLRW metric in conformal
Newtonian gauge (con) described as

\mathrm{d}s^{2}=a^{2}(\tau)\Big(-\big(1+2\Psi\big)\mathrm{d}\tau^{2}+\big(1-2\Phi\big)\mathrm{d}\vec{x}^{2}\Big)\,,

(21)

with gravitational potentials
\Psi
and
\Phi

[
96
]
,
we find

\displaystyle k^{2}\Phi+3\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}\Phi^{\prime}+3\Big(\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}\Big)^{2}\Psi

\displaystyle=4\pi Ga^{2}\Big(-(1+\lambda)\sum_{i}\delta\rho_{i(\mathrm{con})}+3\lambda\sum_{i}\delta p_{i(\mathrm{con})}\Big)\,,

(22)

\displaystyle k^{2}\Phi^{\prime}+\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}k^{2}\Psi=4\pi G(1+2\lambda)a^{2}\sum_{i}\big(\bar{\rho}_{i}+\bar{p}_{i}\big)\theta_{i(\mathrm{con})}\,,

(23)

\displaystyle\Phi-\Psi=0\,,

(24)

\displaystyle\bigg(2\frac{a^{\prime\prime}}{a}-\Big(\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}\Big)^{2}\bigg)\Psi+\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}\big(\Psi^{\prime}+2\Phi^{\prime}\big)+\Phi^{\prime\prime}+\frac{1}{3}k^{2}\big(\Phi-\Psi\big)

\displaystyle=4\pi Ga^{2}\Big(\lambda\sum_{i}\delta\rho_{i(\mathrm{con})}+(1+5\lambda)\sum_{i}\delta p_{i(\mathrm{con})}\Big)\,.

(25)

Moreover, according to the non-conservation equation (
10
),
one can rewrite the continuity equation in background level as

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}^{\prime}_{i}+\frac{3(1+w_{i})(1+2\lambda)}{1+\lambda(1-3w_{i})}\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}\bar{\rho}_{i}=0\,,

(26)

in which we have considered
p_{i}=w_{i}\rho_{i}
with constant
equation of state
w_{i}
. Correspondingly, to linear order of
perturbations (in synchronous gauge) we find

\displaystyle\delta^{\prime}_{i(\mathrm{syn})}=

\displaystyle\frac{1+2\lambda}{-1+\lambda(-1+3c^{2}_{si})}

\displaystyle\times\Bigg\{\delta_{i(\mathrm{syn})}\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}\bigg(\frac{3(1+w_{i})\big(-1+\lambda(-1+3c^{2}_{si})\big)}{1+\lambda(1-3w_{i})}

\displaystyle+3(1+c^{2}_{si})\bigg)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}h^{\prime}(1+w_{i})+(1+w_{i})\theta_{i(\mathrm{syn})}

\displaystyle\times\bigg[1+9\frac{(c^{2}_{si}-c^{2}_{ai})(1+2\lambda)}{k^{2}\big(1+\lambda(1-3w_{i})\big)}

\displaystyle\times\bigg(\Big(\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}\Big)^{2}-\frac{\lambda}{1+2\lambda}\Big(\frac{a^{\prime\prime}}{a}

\displaystyle-\Big(\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}\Big)^{2}\Big(1+\frac{3(1+w_{i})(1+2\lambda)}{1+\lambda(1-3w_{i})}\Big)\Big)\bigg)\bigg]

\displaystyle-\frac{9(c^{2}_{si}-c^{2}_{ai})(1+w_{i})\lambda}{k^{2}\big(1+\lambda(1-3w_{i})\big)}\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}\theta^{\prime}_{i(\mathrm{syn})}\Bigg\}\,,

(27)

\displaystyle\theta^{\prime}_{i(\mathrm{syn})}

\displaystyle=\theta_{i(\mathrm{syn})}\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}

\displaystyle\times\bigg[\frac{3(1+w_{i})(1+2\lambda)+3(1+5\lambda)(c^{2}_{si}-c^{2}_{ai})}{1+\lambda(1-3w_{i})}-4\bigg]

\displaystyle+\frac{k^{2}}{(1+w_{i})(1+2\lambda)}\Big(c^{2}_{si}+\lambda(1+5c^{2}_{si})\Big)\delta_{i(\mathrm{syn})}\,.

(28)

Then, it can be easily understood that in case of

V
Conclusions

In the present investigation, we have studied the compatibility
of
f(R,T)
modified gravity with observations, as well as its
potentiality in reconciling the cosmological tensions.

f(R,T)
model in which the gravitational Lagrangian is
considered as a function of the Ricci scalar
R
and the trace
of the energy-momentum tensor
T
, is a generalized modified
gravity based on a non-minimal matter-geometry interaction

[
46
]
. We specify the functional expression described
in equations (
7
) and (
8
) for
f(R,T)
,
which yields modified field equations derived in section

II
. Then, by employing the modified version of the
CLASS code based on
f(R,T)
model, we are in the position to
explore the cosmological observables in the framework of

f(R,T)
gravity.
Accordingly, matter power spectra diagrams
represented in figure (
2
), report a cosmic
structure growth suppression in
f(R,T)
model,
which is compatible with low redshift structure growth
determinations. Furthermore, exploring the evolution of
matter density contrast as well as the Newtonian potential,
verifies the suppressed structure growth in
f(R,T)
gravity
as displayed in figure (
3
).
Moreover, we detect the remarkable feature
”matter acoustic oscillations” in
f(R,T)
gravity due to the
coupling between matter and geometry.
The velocity perturbations diagrams illustrated in
figure (
4
) exhibit matter acoustic oscillations
more distinctly. The matter-curvature interaction
produces particles, where this irreversible
matter creation process generates negative pressure.
Consequently, the negative pressure is effectual in
the suppression of structure growth
[
47
,
48
]
.
Thus, primary numerical studies imply that modified
f(R,T)

gravity proves beneficial in relieving the observed
tension between local and global probes of structure formation.
On the other hand, Hubble parameter diagrams
in figure (
5
) reveals that the Hubble tension becomes
more serious in
f(R,T)
gravity.
Correspondingly, in pursuance of
examining the qualification of
f(R,T)
model to reduce the
cosmological tensions, we constrain the model with current
observational data. For this purpose, we consider Planck CMB,
weak lensing, supernovae, BAO, and RSD measurements in our
MCMC analysis. According to MCMC numerical investigations,
one can conclude that
f(R,T)
modified gravity represents
capability in ameliorating the
\sigma_{8}
tension, by
predicting lower structure growth for the universe.
It is also notable that, the Hubble tension
becomes more severe in
f(R,T)
gravity, and thus,
a simultaneous alleviation of both the
H_{0}
and
\sigma_{8}

tensions is not possible in the studied
f(R,T)
model.

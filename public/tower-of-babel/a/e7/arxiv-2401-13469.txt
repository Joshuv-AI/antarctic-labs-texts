Morphisms of global theta lifts of Non-trivial Automorphic Characters of Orthogonal Groups


Abstract
This work is largely inspired by the 2003 Ph.D. thesis [ 22 ] of Kobi Snitz. In his thesis, Snitz constructed two irreducible, automorphic, cuspidal representations π \pi and π ′ \pi^{\prime} of the metaplectic group G ​ ( 𝔸 ) = S ​ L ~ 2 ​ ( 𝔸 ) G\left(\mathbb{A}\right)=\widetilde{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{A}\right) where each representation is obtained from a different global theta lifts of certain non-trivial automorphic characters ξ \xi and ξ ′ \xi^{\prime} of the orthogonal groups H 𝔸 = O ​ ( q , V ) ​ ( 𝔸 ) H_{\mathbb{A}}=O\left(q,V\right)\left(\mathbb{A}\right) and H 𝔸 ′ = O ⁡ ( q ′ , V ′ ) ​ ( 𝔸 ) H_{\mathbb{A}}^{\prime}=O\left(q^{\prime},V^{\prime}\right)\left(\mathbb{A}\right) , respectively, where 𝔸 = 𝔸 𝔽 \mathbb{A}=\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{F}} is the adele ring of a number field 𝔽 \mathbb{F} . Snitz shows that for certain matching data of quadratic spaces and automorphic quadratic characters, that these two representations of G ⁡ ( 𝔸 ) G\left(\mathbb{A}\right) are isomorphic, i.e. π ≅ π ′ \pi\cong\pi^{\prime} . Moreover, he constructs an Eulerian global isomorphism and obtains explicit formulas, given by a certain orbital integral, for the corresponding local isomorphisms π ν ≅ π ν ′ \pi_{\nu}\cong\pi^{\prime}_{\nu} . The goal of this work is to reformulate and generalize Snitz’s work to higher rank groups. Namely we wish to determine for which admissible data ( ( q , V ) , ξ , ( q ′ , V ′ ) , ξ ′ ) \left(\left(q,V\right),\xi,\left(q^{\prime},V^{\prime}\right),\xi^{\prime}\right) satisfying certain local necessary conditions could an isomorphism possibly exist between two global theta lifts π \pi and π ′ \pi^{\prime} with respect to two reductive dual pairs H 𝔸 × G 𝔸 H_{\mathbb{A}}\times G_{\mathbb{A}} and H 𝔸 ′ × G 𝔸 H^{\prime}_{\mathbb{A}}\times G_{\mathbb{A}} and two non-trivial automorphic quadratic characters ξ \xi and ξ ′ \xi^{\prime} of the orthogonal groups H 𝔸 = O ​ ( q , V ) ​ ( 𝔸 ) H_{\mathbb{A}}=O\left(q,V\right)\left(\mathbb{A}\right) and H 𝔸 ′ = O ⁡ ( q ′ , V ′ ) ​ ( 𝔸 ) H_{\mathbb{A}}^{\prime}=O\left(q^{\prime},V^{\prime}\right)\left(\mathbb{A}\right) , respectively and the group G G which is the symplectic or the metaplectic group. In case these local necessary conditions are satisfied we also determine a global condition that will ensure that the two representations π \pi and π ′ \pi^{\prime} are isomorphic. We then construct an explicit isomorphism between π \pi and π ′ \pi^{\prime} given by a certain non-degenerate pairing. The formulas obtained are similar to the Rallis inner product formula.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation

This work is inspired by the 2003 Ph.D. thesis
[
22
]
of Kobi Snitz.
In his work Snitz constructed two irreducible, automorphic, cuspidal representations
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
of the metaplectic group
G_{\mathbb{A}}=\widetilde{SL_{2}}\left(\mathbb{A}\right)
over the adèle ring
\mathbb{A}
via the global theta correspondence by lifting certain automorphic nontrivial quadratic characters of two different orthogonal groups
H_{\mathbb{A}}
and
H^{\prime}_{\mathbb{A}}
to the group
G_{\mathbb{A}}
. Snitz proved that for certain defining data, namely for certain pairs of quadratic spaces and non-trivial automorphic quadratic characters that the representations

\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}

given by this data are isomorphic.
In addition, Snitz constructed explicit global and local isomorphisms between these two representations.

The goal of this work is to reformulate Snitz’s work so that similar or equivalent results may be generalized to higher rank groups, i.e. we would like to
consider certain trivial
local
necessary conditions on our defining data such that the corresponding global theta lifts
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
could potentially be isomorphic.
This will be given by a non-trivial
global
condition given in terms of a partial global
L
-function. Finally, in case these representations are isomorphic we will construct explicit local and global isomorphisms between them.

1.1.1.
The global theta correspondence in a nutshell

The
global theta correspondence
is a correspondence between automorphic representations of a group
H_{\mathbb{A}}
and automorphic representations of another group
G_{\mathbb{A}}
, where a typical case to keep in mind is
H
being an orthogonal group and
G
is either the symplectic group
Sp\left(W\right)
or a double cover of the symplectic group
\widetilde{Sp}\left(W\right)
called the
metaplectic group
.
The image of this correspondence is often referred to as a “global theta lift” from
H_{\mathbb{A}}
to
G_{\mathbb{A}}
and at times we will simply say a “lift”.

Note that the pair of groups
H_{\mathbb{A}}\times G_{\mathbb{A}}
are not arbitrarily chosen and are called a
reductive dual pair
(see Definition
B.10
, page
B.10
, Section
B.2
for more details).
In this work, the only reductive dual pairs that will be considered are

O\left(q,V\right)_{\mathbb{A}}\times Sp\left(W\right)_{\mathbb{A}}

when
\dim\left(V\right)=m
is even
or

O\left(q,V\right)_{\mathbb{A}}\times\widetilde{Sp}\left(W\right)_{\mathbb{A}}

when
\dim\left(V\right)
is odd,
where
\left(q,V\right)
is a quadratic space
and
\left(\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>,W\right)
is a symplectic vector space with
\dim\,W=2n
.
Moreover
W
has a polar decomposition
W=X+Y
where
X
and
Y
are maximal isotropic subspaces of
W
. A standard symplectic basis
\left\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n},f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\right\}
can be chosen so that for every
1\leq i,j\leq n

\left<e_{i},e_{j}\right>=\left<f_{i},f_{j}\right>=0,\left<e_{i},e_{j}\right>=\delta_{ij}
.

The defining data in this work will be denoted by
\alpha=\left(\left(q,V\right),\xi,\left(q^{\prime},V^{\prime}\right),\xi^{\prime}\right)
where the defining data of the representation
\pi
of
G_{\mathbb{A}}
, coming from the theta correspondence, is the quadratic space
\left(q,V\right)
together with a non-trivial automorphic quadratic character
\xi\,\colon H_{\mathbb{A}}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and then we will denote the
global theta lift
corresponding to the data

\eta=\left(\left(q,V\right),\xi\right)

by

\pi=\Theta\left(q,V,\xi\right)=\Theta\left(\eta\right)

Similarly, for the quadratic space
\left(q^{\prime},V^{\prime}\right)
together with a non-trivial automorphic quadratic character
\xi^{\prime}\,\colon H_{\mathbb{A}}^{\prime}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
we obtain a representation
\pi^{\prime}
of
G_{\mathbb{A}}
and we denote the global theta lift corresponding to the data
\eta^{\prime}=\left(\left(q^{\prime},V^{\prime}\right),\xi^{\prime}\right)
by

\pi^{\prime}=\Theta\left(q^{\prime},V^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}\right)=\Theta\left(\eta^{\prime}\right)

Certain elements generating
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
will be called
theta integrals
and will be denoted by
I^{\phi}_{V,\xi}
and
I^{\phi^{\prime}}_{V^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}}
, respectively, where
\phi
and
\phi^{\prime}
are Schwartz functions. The theta integrals are functions on
G_{\mathbb{F}}\backslash G_{\mathbb{A}}
.

Moreover, we will prove that the representations
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
satisfying certain conditions in this work are irreducible, cuspidal, automorphic representations of the group
G_{\mathbb{A}}
. By Flath’s theorem
[
4
]
these representations can be written as a restricted tensor product of local representations of the group
G_{\nu}
, i.e.

\pi\cong\otimes_{\nu}^{{}^{\prime}}\pi_{\nu}\textnormal{ and }\pi^{\prime}\cong\otimes_{\nu}^{{}^{\prime}}\pi^{\prime}_{\nu}

1.1.2.
An example of lifting a trivial automorphic character

For example one could lift a trivial automorphic character
\xi\,\colon H_{\mathbb{A}}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
(that is
\xi\equiv 1
) of the orthogonal group
H_{\mathbb{A}}
to an automorphic representation of the symplectic (or metaplectic) group
G_{\mathbb{A}}
. This lift is characterized by what is known as the Siegel-Weil formula and has deep arithmetical significance, generalizes work of Siegel on representation numbers of quadratic forms and has many important applications in the theory of automorphic forms.
See Appendix
D
, page
D
for more details.

1.1.3.
An example of lifting a non-trivial automorphic quadratic character

Another natural example would be to lift a non-trivial automorphic quadratic character
\xi\,\colon H_{\mathbb{A}}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
of an orthogonal group
H_{\mathbb{A}}
to an automorphic representation of the symplectic (or metaplectic) group
G_{\mathbb{A}}
. For instance
\xi
could be taken to be trivial at almost every place outside of a finite set
S
(such that
\left|{S}\right|
is even) and equal to the determinant character for every place
\nu\in S
, i.e.

\xi=\left(\otimes_{\nu\in S}\,det_{\nu}\right)\otimes\left(\otimes_{\nu\not\in S}\,1_{\nu}\right)
.

Another example would be to write the global character
\xi\,\colon H_{\mathbb{A}}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
as the composition of a Hilbert symbol
\left(\cdot,\lambda\right)_{\mathbb{A}}

and the spinor norm
SN

of the orthogonal group.
In this case the local characters
{\xi_{\nu}\,\colon H_{\nu}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}}
are not necessarily trivial at almost every place.

Lifts of non-trivial automorphic quadratic characters of the orthogonal group are the primary objects of study in this work and Snitz addressed the question of when two such lifts are isomorphic for certain low rank orthogonal groups over the adèles of dimensions three and one.
The type of characters he considered are precisely the two types of characters mentioned in the previous two paragraphs.
See Appendix
D
on page
D
for more details on Snitz’s work and how the problem of lifting non-trivial automorphic characters of the orthogonal group in both Snitz’s and our case is related to CAP representations and the generalized Ramanujan conjecture.

1.1.4.
Goal of this work

Philosophically, this work can be divided into three main steps, briefly described as follows. Of course in practice many parts of these steps are intermingled.

(1)

Local necessary conditions.

Find necessary conditions for
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
to be isomorphic. In particular if there were a global isomorphism
\pi\cong\pi^{\prime}
this would imply the existence of local isomorphisms
\pi_{\nu}\cong\pi^{\prime}_{\nu}
at every place
\nu
.

Hence we consider necessary local conditions on our local data
\alpha_{\nu}=\left(\left(q_{\nu},V_{\nu}\right),\xi_{\nu},\left(q^{\prime}_{\nu},V_{\nu}^{\prime}\right),\xi^{\prime}_{\nu}\right)
so that one could possibly have an isomorphism between
\pi_{\nu}
and
\pi^{\prime}_{\nu}
at every place
\nu
.

The two necessary conditions we consider for
\pi_{\nu}
and
\pi^{\prime}_{\nu}
to be
isomorphic are called

(a)

The
central character condition
\left(CC\right)
.

(b)

The
Fourier coefficient condition
\left(FC\right)
.

These conditions
\left(CC\right)
and
\left(FC\right)
will determine the local data
\alpha_{\nu}=\left(\left(q_{\nu},V_{\nu}\right),\xi_{\nu},\left(q^{\prime}_{\nu},V_{\nu}^{\prime}\right),\xi^{\prime}_{\nu}\right)
. Such local data
\alpha_{\nu}
satisfying both
\left(CC\right)
and
\left(FC\right)
will be called a
locally admissible quadruple
.

Moreover, the local data
\alpha_{\nu}
will determine conditions on our global data
\alpha=\left(\left(q,V\right),\xi,\left(q^{\prime},V^{\prime}\right),\xi^{\prime}\right)
. Such global data will be referred to as a
globally admissible quadruple
.
Note that the local representations
\pi_{\nu}
and
\pi^{\prime}_{\nu}
are called
local theta lifts
.

(2)

Global conditions.

Given a globally admissible quadruple
\alpha=\left(\left(q,V\right),\xi,\left(q^{\prime},V^{\prime}\right),\xi^{\prime}\right)
, assume that a certain global condition is satisfied where this global condition is given in terms of a global partial
L
-function. Under these conditions, prove that the corresponding global automorphic representations
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
are isomorphic.

Note that the global condition will be given in terms of a global partial
L
-function which will come from the
unramified computation
. This is a computation done in a generic, i.e. unramified, setting.

(3)

Construction of explicit morphisms.

Construct explicit global morphisms
\pi\to\pi^{\prime}
and for every place
\nu
construct explicit local morphisms
\pi_{\nu}\to\pi^{\prime}_{\nu}
.
More precisely, we will find an explicit non-trivial local pairing

\pi_{\nu}\times\hat{\pi}_{\nu}^{\prime}\to\mathbb{C}

and an explicit non-trivial global pairing

\pi\times\hat{\pi}^{\prime}\to\mathbb{C}

between the local and global representations, respectively, satisfying certain equivariance properties.

This is equivalent to finding local and global isomorphisms between the representations under consideration.
It turns out that constructing such pairings is a much more accessible and natural problem than constructing explicit isomorphisms.

1.1.5.
Simplifying Assumptions

Throughout this work we will be working under the simplifying assumption that the automorphic quadratic characters
\xi\,\colon H_{\mathbb{A}}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and
\xi^{\prime}\,\colon H_{\mathbb{A}}^{\prime}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
are non-trivial and that the quadratic spaces
\left(q,V\right)
and
\left(q^{\prime},V^{\prime}\right)
are globally anisotropic.

Moreover, as aforementioned throughout this work we will be working under the simplifying assumption that the automorphic quadratic characters are non-trivial and that the quadratic spaces of interest are globally anisotropic. This will not be mentioned again.

1.2.
Main Statement

It is important to note that the main results of this work can only be stated for the case
n=1
since we were unable to do the unramified computation for
n
larger than one. Besides the unramified computation all results are presented and proved in full generality.

Note that although we have not proven that the local factors are Eulerian for
n>1
we do believe that such is the case.

Theorem 1.1

(Main statement)
.

Given a globally admissible quadruple
\alpha=\left(\left(q,V\right),\xi,\left(q^{\prime},V^{\prime}\right),\xi^{\prime}\right)
.
If a certain global partial
L
-function
\Delta_{S}

has a pole at

s=\rho_{n}:=\frac{n+1}{2}

then the global theta lifts

\pi=\Theta\left(q,V,\xi\right)\textnormal{ and }\pi^{\prime}=\Theta\left(q^{\prime},V^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}\right)

are isomorphic.

1.2.1.
Main Statement in terms of pairings

For a globally admissible quadruple

\alpha=\left(\left(q,V\right),\xi,\left(q^{\prime},V^{\prime}\right),\xi^{\prime}\right)
we will construct two global pairings and then compare them. We will see that they are equal up to a constant where the constant is given by a global partial
L
-function.

Let
X
be a maximal isotropic subspace of a symplectic space

\left(W,\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>\right)
.
The first global pairing we consider is a natural global pairing

B\,\colon S(\left(V\otimes X\right)_{\mathbb{A}})\times S(\left(V^{\prime}\otimes X\right)_{\mathbb{A}})\to\mathbb{C}

given by the Petersson inner product of two global theta integrals.
Such global theta integrals generate the representations
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
of
G_{\mathbb{A}}
.
The other global pairing will be given by a product of certain explicit local pairings

B_{\nu}\,\colon S(\left(V\otimes X\right)_{\nu})\times S(\left(V^{\prime}\otimes X\right)_{\nu})\to\mathbb{C}

A key point in the proof is the existence or non-existence of a certain partial
L-
function
\Delta_{S}\left(s\right)
which comes from the unramified computation of certain local pairings
B_{s,\nu}
. Unfortunately we do not know how to compute or even formulate a conjecture as to what this should be equal to in the higher rank setting. For
n=1
and for an admissible quadruple
\Delta_{S}\left(s\right)
is given by

\Delta_{S}\left(s\right)=\prod_{\nu\not\in S}\left(1-q_{\nu}^{-s}\right)^{-1}

Theorem 1.2

(Alternative main statement in terms of pairings)
.

Suppose
\alpha=\left(\left(q,V\right),\xi,\left(q^{\prime},V^{\prime}\right),\xi^{\prime}\right)
is a globally admissible quadruple.
Then

(1)

There exists a natural global pairing

B\,\colon S(\left(V\otimes X\right)_{\mathbb{A}})\times S(\left(V^{\prime}\otimes X\right)_{\mathbb{A}})\to\mathbb{C}

which is Eulerian (i.e. factors as a product of local pairings) and satisfies for every factorizable

\phi=\otimes_{\nu}^{{}^{\prime}}\phi_{\nu}\in S(\left(V\otimes X\right)_{\mathbb{A}})
and
\phi^{\prime}=\otimes_{\nu}^{{}^{\prime}}\phi^{\prime}_{\nu}\in S(\left(V^{\prime}\otimes X\right)_{\mathbb{A}})

where S is a sufficently large set of primes that may depend on
\phi
and
\phi^{\prime}
.

\displaystyle B\left(\phi,\phi^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle=\kappa\cdot Res_{s=\rho_{n}}\Delta_{S}\left(s\right)\cdot\prod_{\nu\in S}B_{\nu}\left(\phi_{\nu},\phi^{\prime}_{\nu}\right)

where
\rho_{n}=\dfrac{n+1}{2}
,
\Delta_{S}
is a certain global partial
L-
function,
S
is a finite set of places
which depends on
\phi
and
\phi^{\prime}

,
B_{\nu}
is a certain explicit local pairings, and
\kappa
is a non-zero constant coming from the residue of a certain simple normalized auxiliary Eisenstein series
\mathcal{E}^{\star}
.

(2)

Moreover, if the global partial
L
-function,
\Delta_{S}\left(s\right)
, has a
simple
pole at
s=\rho_{n}

then
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
are isomorphic.

Remark 1.3
.

The global factor
\Delta_{S}\left(s\right)
is used to determine whether or not a non-zero global isomorphism exists between
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
. This is a global condition that is not accounted for by the local necessary conditions. The formula for
\Delta_{S}\left(s\right)
follows from the unramified computation, i.e. it follows from a ”generic” computation.

2.1.1.
Local necessary conditions

We consider two necessary conditions for an isomorphism to exist between

\pi_{\nu}
and
\pi_{\nu}^{\prime}
:

(1)

The first local necessary condition is that

\pi_{\nu}
and
\pi_{\nu}^{\prime}
must have the same central character. We will refer to this necessary condition as the
central character condition
and we will denote this condition by
\left(CC\right)
.

(2)

The second local necessary condition is that the space of
\left(N_{\nu},\psi_{\nu}\right)-
co-invariants of the local representations
\pi_{\nu}
and
\pi^{\prime}_{\nu}
with respect to the unipotent radical of the Siegel parabolic in
G
are equal to zero simulataneously.
One can think of these
\left(N_{\nu},\psi_{\nu}\right)-
co-invariants as local Fourier coefficients of the representations.

Namely we have the condition

\left(\pi_{\nu}\right)_{N_{\nu},\psi_{\beta}}=0\textnormal{ if and only if }\left(\pi_{\nu}^{\prime}\right)_{N_{\nu},\psi_{\beta}}=0

for every

\beta\in\rm Sym_{n}\left(\mathbb{F}\right)
, where
\psi_{\beta}
is a character of the unipotent radical
N_{\nu}
of the Siegel parabolic
P_{\nu}\subseteq G_{\nu}
.
We will refer to this necessary condition as the
Fourier coefficient condition
and we will denote this condition by
\left(FC\right)
.

An example of a locally admissible quadruple in the low rank setting

\alpha_{\nu}=\left(\left(q_{\nu},V_{\nu}\right),\xi_{\nu},\left(q^{\prime}_{\nu},V_{\nu}^{\prime}\right),\xi^{\prime}_{\nu}\right)

\xi_{\nu}
and
\xi_{\nu}^{\prime}

\alpha_{\nu}=\left(\left(q_{\nu},V_{\nu}\right),\xi_{\nu},\left(q^{\prime}_{\nu},V_{\nu}^{\prime}\right),\xi^{\prime}_{\nu}\right)

The character
\xi_{\nu}

Then we have the following example in the low rank setting.

(Example of a locally admissible quadruple)
.

2.2.
Determining isomorphisms via global and local pairings

In this section we define the global and local pairings. We explain the transition from isomorphisms to pairings.
We describe the properties of the representations
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
which are proved via Rallis’ theory of towers where one needs global Fourier analysis with respect to the locally compact abelian group
N\subseteq G
, i.e. with respect to the unipotent radical of the Siegel parabolic in
G
in order to prove what is referred to as ”first occurrence” in Rallis’ theory of towers and this in turn will prove that our global representations are cuspidal. Moreover the Fourier expansion of the theta integrals will also be used in the unfolding of a certain auxiliary global pairing. Next we move on to the problem of unfolding the global pairing. It turns out that this is too difficult to compute directly. Instead we construct a family of pairings given by an integral over
G_{\mathbb{F}}\backslash G_{\mathbb{A}}
of the two theta integrals against a relatively simple Eisenstein series. Thus we end up with a Rankin-Selberg type integral which is much easier to unfold and at a later point we take a certain residue in order to obtain formulas for the original global pairing of interest.
After unfolding the global pairing we obtain a product of local integrals.
We compute these integrals at almost every place where the data satisfies certain generic conditions. This generic computation is referred to as the unramified computation.
Finally in Appendix
E
we explain the problem we are addressing with respect to other interesting problems, such as the Rallis inner product formula, the Siegel-Weil formula and CAP representations.

2.2.1.
The Global Pairings

In this section we define the natural global pairing

We define a natural global pairing

and

and

We will also call the vectors

and

Remark 2.5
.

Note that the integral defining the pairing

2.2.3.
From isomorphisms to pairings

We would like to consider two local theta lifts which are given by coinvariant spaces. These spaces are of interest but difficult to describe.
Since in general quotient spaces are difficult to construct.
In Snitz’s thesis, Snitz attempted to construct an isomorphism between such spaces.

The main idea in this section is that it is easier to understand pairings between such coinvariant spaces. And this can be replaced with an even simpler problem of finding pairings satisfying certain equivariance properties between certain Schwartz spaces. Schwartz spaces are already spaces that are well understood and not given by a quotient.

The bottom line is that it turns out that a more accessible problem is finding non-zero pairings instead of constructing isomorphisms. In this section we sketch the transition from isomorphisms to pairings.
See Section
5.2
for more details.
In what follows we only give the general idea of the transition.

Upon closer reading of Snitz’s work we see that he constructed a local morphism between two local theta lifts

\Delta\,\colon\Theta_{V_{\nu},W_{\nu},\psi_{\nu}}\left(\xi_{\nu}\right)\to\Theta_{V^{\prime}_{\nu},W_{\nu},\psi_{\nu}}\left(\xi^{\prime}_{\nu}\right)

where

\Theta_{V_{\nu},W_{\nu},\psi_{\nu}}\left(\xi_{\nu}\right)
and
\Theta_{V^{\prime}_{\nu},W_{\nu},\psi_{\nu}}\left(\xi^{\prime}_{\nu}\right)
denote the local theta lifts coming from our local quadratic spaces and local characters.
In general constructing such morphisms is highly non-trivial. Instead we construct a pairing

\widetilde{B_{\nu}}\,\colon\Theta_{V_{\nu},W_{\nu},\psi_{\nu}}\left(\xi_{\nu}\right)\times\Theta_{V^{\prime}_{\nu},W_{\nu},\psi_{\nu}^{-1}}\left(\xi^{\prime}_{\nu}\right)\to\mathbb{C}

However the spaces
\Theta_{V_{\nu},W_{\nu},\psi_{\nu}}\left(\xi_{\nu}\right)
and
\Theta_{V^{\prime}_{\nu},W_{\nu},\psi_{\nu}}\left(\xi^{\prime}_{\nu}\right)
are still relatively difficult to understand.
Instead we constructing a pairing on the level of Schwartz spaces which we denote by
B_{\nu}

B_{\nu}\,\colon S(\left(V\otimes X\right)_{\nu})\times S(\left(V^{\prime}\otimes X\right)_{\nu})\to\mathbb{C}

which satisfies certain equivariance properties relative to the actions of the groups
H_{\nu},H^{\prime}_{\nu},G_{\nu}
.

2.8.
Relation to other work

It is interesting to note that our main result bears a close resemblance to the Rallis inner product formula. This is not surprising since our main object of study in the global setting is an inner product of two theta integrals as is in the Rallis inner product formula.
Amongst other things the Rallis inner product formula is an important tool in determining first occurrence of a theta lift and relating this to poles of certain
L
-functions.

Note that another interpretation of the Rallis inner product formula is to describe when an automorphism exists between a theta lift and itself, hence we are generalizing this result to the existence of non-zero morphisms between theta lifts in general. However one must keep in mind the following caveat of this work. We are lifting highly trivial and concrete automorphic representations of the orthogonal groups
H_{\mathbb{A}}
and
H_{\mathbb{A}}^{\prime}
. Giving a general conjecture and proof of some kind of generalized Rallis inner product formula for general cusp forms
f^{H}
and
f^{H^{\prime}}
of
H_{\mathbb{A}}
and
H_{\mathbb{A}}^{\prime}
might be much more difficult.

Also note that a common step in proofs of the Rallis inner product formula use the doubling method which usually depends on a suitable Siegel-Weil formula.
Our proof avoids the use of the Siegel-Weil formula. Instead we consider a family of global pairings

B_{s}\,\colon S(\left(V\otimes X\right)_{\mathbb{A}})\times S(\left(V^{\prime}\otimes X\right)_{\mathbb{A}})\to\mathbb{C}

depending on a complex parameter
s\in\mathbb{C}
which is a Rankin-Selberg convolution of our two theta integrals against a certain simple normalized auxiliary Eisenstein series
\mathcal{E}^{\star}
. The main steps of our proofs bear a strong resemblance to Jacquet’s
[
6
]

\left(1972\right)
proof of the
GL_{2}\times GL_{2}
case using the Rankin-Selberg method. In this sense our proof is very different from the standard proofs of the Rallis inner product formula.
As an aside, it would be interesting to see if our methods could be applied to furnish an alternative proof of the Rallis inner product formula without recourse to the Siegel-Weil formula or whether our results could be applied to give an alternative proof of the Siegel-Weil formula.

Another interesting observation worth mentioning is that if we apply the formulas of the Weil representation in the definition of our local pairings then we obtain a function closely related to Godement-Jacquet’s
L
-function which is a generalization to
GL\left(n\right)
of the
L
-function considered in Tate’s thesis. Note that an alternative way of thinking of
GL\left(n\right)
is as the Levi part of the Siegel parabolic.

Initially we made an educated guess based on Godement-Jacquet’s
L
-function to define our local pairing, then we realized a certain averaging operator would be a natural substitute of this pairing and finally we discovered the idea of the auxiliary pairing which was relatively easy to unfold and this led us yet again to a similar yet slightly different definition of our local pairing.

This previously defined local natural pairing differs from the local pairing we are using in the sense that we do not integrate over the compact group and the modular character of the Siegel parabolic does not appear in the formula.
We will not examine this alternative local pairing although it could be interesting to see how these alternative local pairs are related.

Definition 4.3

(CC - Central character condition)
.

Lemma 4.4

(Characterization of the central character condition)
.

The quadruple
\alpha_{\nu}=\left(\left(q_{\nu},V_{\nu}\right),\xi_{\nu},\left(q^{\prime}_{\nu},V_{\nu}^{\prime}\right),\xi^{\prime}_{\nu}\right)
satisfies the central character condition
if and only if

\chi_{V_{\nu}}\left(-I_{n}\right)\xi_{\nu}(-I_{n})=\chi_{V_{\nu}^{\prime}}\left(-I_{n}\right)\xi_{\nu}^{\prime}(-I_{n})

Proof.

We present a proof in the case that the dimensions of our quadratic spaces are odd. The proof is the same for the even case, however the gamma factor (which cancels anyways in the odd case) does not appear in the even case.
Recall that

\pi_{\nu}=\left(\omega_{V_{\nu},\psi_{\nu}}\right)_{H_{\nu},\xi_{\nu}}

and

\pi^{\prime}_{\nu}=\left(\omega_{V^{\prime}_{\nu},\psi_{\nu}}\right)_{H_{\nu}^{\prime},\xi_{\nu}^{\prime}}

Let
z_{\pi_{\nu}}
denote the central character of
\pi_{\nu}
. Similarly, let
z_{\pi^{\prime}_{\nu}}
denote the central character of
\pi^{\prime}_{\nu}
.
For
\phi_{\nu}\in\pi_{\nu}
we have

\omega_{V_{\nu},\psi_{\nu}}\left(h\right)\phi_{\nu}=\xi_{\nu}\left(h\right)\phi_{\nu}

therefore

\left(\omega_{V_{\nu},\psi_{\nu}}\left(h\right)\phi_{\nu}\right)\left(v\right)=\xi_{\nu}\left(h\right)\phi_{\nu}\left(v\right)

thus

\phi_{\nu}\left(h^{-1}v\right)=\xi_{\nu}\left(h\right)\phi_{\nu}\left(v\right)

In particular for
h=-I_{n}
we have

\displaystyle\phi_{\nu}\left(-v\right)

\displaystyle=\xi_{\nu}\left(-I_{n}\right)\phi_{\nu}\left(v\right)

Consider the matrix
m\left(-I_{n}\right)=\begin{pmatrix}-I_{n}&0\\
0&-I_{n}\end{pmatrix}=-I_{2n}
in the center of
G
therefore
we have

\left(\omega_{V_{\nu},\psi_{\nu}}\begin{pmatrix}-I_{n}&0\\
0&-I_{n}\end{pmatrix}\phi_{\nu}\right)\left(v\right)=\left(\pi_{\nu}\begin{pmatrix}-I_{n}&0\\
0&-I_{n}\end{pmatrix}\phi_{\nu}\right)\left(v\right)=z_{\pi_{\nu}}\begin{pmatrix}-I_{n}&0\\
0&-I_{n}\end{pmatrix}\phi_{\nu}\left(v\right)

where
z_{\pi_{\nu}}
is the central character of
\pi_{\nu}
.
On the other hand by the action of the Weil representation (see Lemma
B.14
) we have

\displaystyle\left(\omega_{V_{\nu},\psi_{\nu}}\begin{pmatrix}-I_{n}&0\\
0&-I_{n}\end{pmatrix}\phi_{\nu}\right)\left(v\right)

\displaystyle=\gamma\left(a,\psi^{-1}\right)\left|{-1}\right|^{m/2}\chi_{V_{\nu}}\left(-I_{n}\right)\phi_{\nu}\left(-v\right)

\displaystyle=\gamma\left(a,\psi^{-1}\right)\chi_{V_{\nu}}\left(-I_{n}\right)\xi_{\nu}\left(-I_{n}\right)\phi_{\nu}\left(v\right)

therefore

z_{\pi_{\nu}}\left(m\left(-I_{n}\right)\right)=\gamma\left(a,\psi^{-1}\right)\chi_{V_{\nu}}\left(-I_{n}\right)\xi_{\nu}\left(-I_{n}\right)

Similarly we have

z_{\pi^{\prime}_{\nu}}\left(m\left(-I_{n}\right)\right)=\gamma\left(a,\psi^{-1}\right)\chi_{V_{\nu}^{\prime}}\left(-I_{n}\right)\xi_{\nu}^{\prime}\left(-I_{n}\right)

therefore the central character condition is equivalent to

\chi_{V_{\nu}}\left(-I_{n}\right)\xi_{\nu}(-I_{n})=\chi_{V_{\nu}^{\prime}}\left(-I_{n}\right)\xi_{\nu}^{\prime}(-I_{n})

\square

5.
From local morphisms to local pairings

In this section we define what properties our local pairings should satisfy. We then show that the existence of local morphisms such as those appearing in Snitz’s thesis can be equivalently recast as certain equivariant pairings. Finally we define a natural global pairing which will be the focus of this work. This global pairing is essentially an inner product of two global theta integrals.
A keep point to keep in mind is that the pairing we study is between Schwartz spaces instead of between co-invariants.
The advantage of working with Schwartz spaces over co-invariants is that the Schwartz spaces do not depend on the parameters defining the quadruple
\alpha=\left(\left(q,V\right),\xi,\left(q^{\prime},V^{\prime}\right),\xi^{\prime}\right)
.

To sum up constructing morphisms between coinvarints is a very difficult problem. We replace this problem with finding pairings between coinvariant spaces. Finally we replace this problem with finding pairings between Schwartz spaces where the pairing satisfyies certain equivariance properties with respect to the actions of the orthogonal groups

H
and
H^{\prime}

and the symplectic (or metaplectic) group
G
.

5.1.
Equivariance properties of our pairings

In this section we describe the equivariance properties of the pairings of interest.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a local field.
The quadratic spaces, groups etc. will all be given locally therefore, for instance,
V=V_{\mathbb{F}}=V_{\nu}
denote one and the same thing in this section.
Given two morphisms between two local theta lifts we construct a good pairing between these two local theta lifts. That is we define

Definition 5.1

(Good local pairing)
.

A pairing

B\colon S\left(\left(V\otimes X\right)_{\mathbb{F}}\right)\times S\left(\left(V^{\prime}\otimes X\right)_{\mathbb{F}}\right)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}

(1)

(

\left(H_{\mathbb{F}},\xi\right)
-equivariance
)

For every
h\in H_{\mathbb{F}}

B\left(\omega_{\psi,V}\left(h\right)\phi,\phi^{\prime}\right)=\xi\left(h\right)B\left(\phi,\phi^{\prime}\right)

(2)

(

\left(H_{\mathbb{F}}^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}\right)
-equivariance

For every
h\in H^{\prime}_{\mathbb{F}}

B\left(\phi,\omega_{\psi^{-1},V^{\prime}}\left(h^{\prime}\right)\phi^{\prime}\right)=\xi^{\prime}\left(h^{\prime}\right)B\left(\phi,\phi^{\prime}\right)

(3)

(

For every

B\left(\omega_{\psi,V}\left(g\right)\phi,\omega_{\psi^{-1},V^{\prime}}\left(g\right)\phi^{\prime}\right)=B\left(\phi,\phi^{\prime}\right)

Remark 5.2
.

Similarly we can define a good global pairing.

5.2.

In this section we describe how to construct good pairings from morphisms.
Let’s suppose, as in Snitz’s thesis, that we have constructed a local morphism between local theta lifts

Remark 5.3
.

Note that a key point in the transition from morphisms to pairings is the fact that the dual of the Weil representation is the dual of another Weil representation, namely

To sum up, the focus of this work is to construct explicit non-trivial good local pairings and also show that certain natural global pairings are factorizable into a product of good local pairings.

6.

In this section we prove the main statement for the low rank case of

.

where

We prove the main statement in the following steps.

(1)

To begin with we unfold the auxiliary global pairing

. The key tool here is the Fourier expansion of the theta integrals.
It is important to note that the Fourier expansion of the theta integrals is relatively simple and sparse. Multiplying the theta integrals by the normalized auxiliary Eisenstein series

(2)

Next we take the residue of

(3)

Finally one can prove the main statement. Namely for an admissible quadruple

Definition 6.11

Theorem 6.12

(Factorization of pairing)
.

For an admissible quadruple
\alpha=\left(\left(q,V\right),\xi,\left(q^{\prime},V^{\prime}\right),\xi^{\prime}\right),\phi\in S(\left(V\otimes X\right)_{\mathbb{A}}),\phi^{\prime}\in S(\left(V^{\prime}\otimes X\right)_{\mathbb{A}})

\displaystyle B\left(\phi,\phi^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle=\kappa\cdot\left\{\prod_{\nu\in S}B_{\nu,s=\rho_{n}}\left(\phi_{\nu},\phi^{\prime}_{\nu}\right)\right\}\cdot Res_{s=\rho_{n}}L_{S}\left(s\right)

Proof.

This follows from

6.3.
Proof of the main statement

In this section we prove the main statement Theorem
1.1
. Namely for an admissible quadruple the representations
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
are isomorphic if and only if a certain global condition is satisfied where this global condition is given in terms of the existence of a pole at
\rho_{n}
of a certain global partial
L-
function.

Theorem
1.1

(Main statement)
.

Given a globally admissible quadruple
\alpha=\left(\left(q,V\right),\xi,\left(q^{\prime},V^{\prime}\right),\xi^{\prime}\right)

the global theta lifts

\pi=\Theta\left(q,V,\xi\right)\textnormal{ and }\pi^{\prime}=\Theta\left(q^{\prime},V^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}\right)

are isomorphic, i.e.
\pi\cong\pi^{\prime}
.

Proof.

It is known that the Dedekind zeta function has a simple pole at
s=1=\rho_{1}=\dfrac{1+1}{2}
whose value is equal to one.
This is a crucial fact and is implicit in the computations of Theorem
6.12
.
Let
\phi_{\nu}\in S(\left(V\otimes X\right)_{\nu}),\phi^{\prime}_{\nu}\in S(\left(V^{\prime}\otimes X\right)_{\nu})
be local data so that the integral defining
B_{\nu}\left(\phi_{\nu},\phi^{\prime}_{\nu}\right)
is nonzero at
s=\rho_{n}=1
.
Now we have obtained a non-degenerate bilinear global pairing
B
.
For an admissible quadruple
\alpha=\left(\left(q,V\right),\xi,\left(q^{\prime},V^{\prime}\right),\xi^{\prime}\right)
we can choose certain Schwartz functions
\phi\in S(\left(V\otimes X\right)_{\mathbb{A}}),\phi^{\prime}\in S(\left(V^{\prime}\otimes X\right)_{\mathbb{A}})
such that
the pairing
B_{\nu,s}
is nonzero at
s=\rho_{n}
.
This essentially follows from the proof of first occurrence, namely from a similar argument to Theorem
8.6
.
Let
\phi,\phi^{\prime}
be the choice of data such that
B_{\nu,s}
is non-zero.

Consequently, the fact that
B_{s}
has a pole implies that

B\left(\phi,\phi^{\prime}\right)\neq 0

Now we have obtained a non-degenerate bilinear pairing
B
.
This proves that the representations
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
are isomorphic.

\square

Remark 6.14
.

As stated in the introduction for an admissible quadruple
\alpha=\left(\left(q,V\right),\xi,\left(q^{\prime},V^{\prime}\right),\xi^{\prime}\right)
if a certain global

See for example
[

]
. Essentially the central character condition ensured that we would obtain the Dedekind zeta function.

Appendix B
The Weil representation and the Theta correspondence

In this section we define the Heisenberg group and then we state the Stone-von Neumann theorem. We then construct a family of representations of the Heisenberg group such that every two representations are isomorphic.
From Schur’s lemma we obtain intertwining operators between every two isomorphic representations. This will give us a projective representation of the symplectic group which will lead us to the definition of the Weil representation of the metaplectic group. We also state basic properties of the Weil representation that will be used later in this work.

In this section we closely follow the description in
[
9
]
and
[
13
]
.
We wish to define the local and global theta correspondence. To do this we must describe the Heisenberg group and the Stone von Neumann theorem in order to define the Weil representation. Furthermore we describe reductive dual pairs and the Schrödinger model of the Weil representation.

B.1.
Heisenberg group, Stone von Neumann theorem, and the Weil representation

In this section
\mathbb{F}
will denote a non-Archimedean local field such that the residue characteristic of
\mathbb{F}
is not equal to two and
\left(\mathbb{W},\left<,\right>\right)
will denote a non-degenerate symplectic
\mathbb{F}
-vector space. Fix a nontrivial additive character

In order to define the Weil representation we will first briefly recall the definition of the Heisenberg group and the Stone von Neumann theorem. This will give rise to a projective representation of the symplectic group and this in turn will allow us to define the Weil representation of the metaplectic group determined by a fixed nontrivial additive character
\psi\,\colon\mathbb{F}\to\mathbb{C}

Definition B.1

(Heisenberg group)
.

Define the
Heisenberg group
as the set
H\left(\mathbb{W}\right)=\mathbb{W}\oplus\mathbb{F}

together with the group operation given by

\left(w,t\right)\circ\left(w^{\prime},t^{\prime}\right)=\left(w+w^{\prime},t+t^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\left<w,w^{\prime}\right>\right)

where

\left(w,t\right),\left(w^{\prime},t^{\prime}\right)\in H\left(\mathbb{W}\right)

We have a natural action of
Sp\left(\mathbb{W}\right)
on
H\left(\mathbb{W}\right)
given by

\left(w,t\right)^{g}=\left(wg,t\right)
where
g\in Sp\left(\mathbb{W}\right),\left(w,t\right)\in H\left(\mathbb{W}\right)
.

Remark B.2
.

The center of
H\left(\mathbb{W}\right)
is given by

Z\left(H\left(\mathbb{W}\right)\right)=\left\{\left(0,t\right)\,\middle|\,\mathopen{}t\in\mathbb{F}\right\}\cong\mathbb{F}

Moreover,
Sp\left(\mathbb{W}\right)
acts trivially on
Z\left(H\left(\mathbb{W}\right)\right)

Theorem B.3

(Stone-von Neumann)
.

Up to isomorphism, there is a unique smooth irreducible representation

\left(H\left(\mathbb{W}\right),\rho_{\psi},S\right)
with central character
\psi
. Namely

\rho_{\psi}\left(0,t\right)=\psi\left(t\right)\cdot id_{S},\forall t\in\mathbb{F}

Proof.

See
[
13
, pp. 28-31]
.

\square

Note that for every

we can define a representation

\left(H\left(\mathbb{W}\right),\rho_{\psi}^{g},S\right)

given by the action

\rho_{\psi}^{g}\left(h\right)=\rho_{\psi}\left(h^{g}\right)

and it is important to note that this new irreducible representation
\left(H\left(\mathbb{W}\right),\rho_{\psi}^{g},S\right)
has the same central character

\psi\,\colon\mathbb{F}\to\mathbb{C}
as the representation
\left(H\left(\mathbb{W}\right),\rho_{\psi},S\right)
. Therefore by the Stone-von Neumann theorem the representations
\left(H\left(\mathbb{W}\right),\rho_{\psi}^{g},S\right)
are all isomorphic to
\left(H\left(\mathbb{W}\right),\rho_{\psi},S\right)
therefore for every

there exists an automorphism

A\left(g\right)^{-1}\rho_{\psi}\left(h\right)A\left(g\right)=\rho_{\psi}^{g}\left(h\right)

Note that these automorphisms are not unique however

g\mapsto A\left(g\right)\\
\end{cases}

One can then define a central extension of

\widetilde{Sp\left(\mathbb{W}\right)}_{\psi}=\left\{\left(g,A\left(g\right)\right)\in Sp\left(\mathbb{W}\right)\times GL\left(S\right)\,\middle|\,\mathopen{}A\left(g\right)^{-1}\rho_{\psi}\left(h\right)A\left(g\right)=\rho_{\psi}^{g}\left(h\right),h\in H\left(\mathbb{W}\right)\right\}

Appendix D
Examples of Global Theta Lifts

D.1.
Example of lifting a trivial automorphic character

For example one could lift a trivial automorphic character
\xi\,\colon H_{\mathbb{A}}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
(that is
\xi\equiv 1
) of the orthogonal group to an automorphic representation of the symplectic (or metaplectic) group. This lift is characterized by what is known as the Siegel-Weil formula and has deep arithmetic significance, generalizes work of Siegel on representation numbers of quadratic forms and has many important applications in the theory of automorphic forms.

In the Siegel-Weil formula one equates a global theta integral with a certain Eisenstein series, so roughly speaking one obtains a formula of functions on
G_{\mathbb{A}}
of the form

I^{\phi}_{V,\xi\equiv 1}=Eis^{\phi}\qquad\left(!\right)

where
\phi
is a test function,
E=Eis
is an Eisenstein series such that the section defining it depends on the test function
\phi
and
I^{\phi}_{V,\xi\equiv 1}
is a theta integral.

D.2.
Example of lifting a non-trivial automorphic character

Another natural example would be to lift a non-trivial automorphic character
\xi\,\colon H_{\mathbb{A}}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
of an orthogonal group
H_{\mathbb{A}}
. For instance
\xi
could be taken to be trivial at almost every place outside of a finite set
S
and equal to the determinant character for every place
\nu\in S
, i.e.

\xi=\left(\otimes_{\nu\in S}det_{\nu}\right)\otimes\left(\otimes_{\nu\not\in S}1_{\nu}\right)
. Lifts of non-trivial automorphic quadratic characters are the primary concern in this work and this is the question Snitz addressed for certain low rank orthogonal groups of dimensions
3
and
1
.

It is important to note that a representation of the group
G_{\mathbb{A}}
obtained in this manner is a cuspidal representation and it is known that such a representation can not be given by an Eisenstein series, hence a priori one could not expect to obtain a formula of the form
\left(!\right)
. Instead Snitz considers
another
orthogonal group
H^{\prime}_{\mathbb{A}}
together with a very specific non-trivial automorphic character
\xi^{\prime}\,\colon H_{\mathbb{A}}^{\prime}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and lifts it via the global theta correspondence to
G_{\mathbb{A}}
. Essentially Snitz constructs a morphism between these two global theta lifts given by a morphism

\Delta\,\colon S(\left(V\otimes X\right)_{\mathbb{A}})\to S\left(V_{\mathbb{A}}^{\prime}\right)

between the test function spaces.

In this manner, Snitz obtained an analog of the Siegel-Weil formula, so roughly speaking Snitz obtained a formula of functions on
G_{\mathbb{A}}
of the form

I^{\phi}_{V,\xi}=I^{\Delta\left(\phi\right)}_{V^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}}\qquad\left(!!\right)

where
\phi
is a test function and
\Delta\left(\phi\right)
is a test function matching the test function
\phi
. Henceforth the space of test functions on
V_{\mathbb{A}}
will be called the space of
Schwartz functions

S(\left(V\otimes X\right)_{\mathbb{A}})
.

Remark D.1
.

On the surface no Eisenstein series appears in formula
\left(!!\right)
, however it turns out that the automorphic representations obtained via the global theta lifts in both Snitz’s and this work are what are referred to as CAP (
C
uspidal
A
ssociated to
P
arabolic) representations, colloquially referred to as ”shadows of Eisenstein series”, hence formula
\left(!!\right)
is similar to
\left(!\right)
, hence an honest analog of the Siegel-Weil formula.

It is interesting to note that CAP representations provide counterexamples to the Generalized Ramanujan conjecture.
By work of H. Jacquet and J. Shalika
[
8
]
, it can be shown that the CAP phenomenon never occurs for
GL(n)
. At the same time the Ramanujan conjecture for the general linear group
GL(n)
is believed to be true. CAP representations are not really the focus of this work, however the fact that CAP representations naturally appear in our study is a further indication of the arithmetic significance of these results.
For more on CAP representations and the Ramanujan conjecture see appendix

.

Appendix E

This exposition closely follows Eitan Sayag’s M.A. thesis

The notion of a CAP representation was first coined by Piatetski-Shapiro

What exactly is a CAP representation? We can think of such a representation as a cuspidal representation which is pretending to be an Eisenstein series. Hence it is also call a shadow of an Eisenstein series. We would like to present a more formal definition. First note that for an algebraic group

Do CAP for presentations exist? It’s impossible to embed a cuspidal representation in an induced representation. One of the most interesting achievements appearing in

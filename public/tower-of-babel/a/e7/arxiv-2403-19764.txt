On Fock covariance for product systems and the reduced Hao–Ng isomorphism problem by discrete actions Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. 46L08, 47L55, 46L05 Thanks: Key words and phrases: Product systems, Fock space, crossed products of operator algebras.
Evgenios T.A. Kakariadis, Ioannis Apollon Paraskevas

Abstract
. We provide a characterisation of equivariant Fock covariant injective representations for product systems. We show that this characterisation coincides with Nica covariance for compactly aligned product systems over right LCM semigroups of Kwaśniewski and Larsen, and with the Toeplitz representations of a discrete monoid of Laca and Sehnem. By combining with the framework established by Katsoulis and Ramsey, we resolve the reduced Hao–Ng isomorphism problem for generalised gauge actions by discrete groups.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Fock covariance

Hilbertian representations play an important role in the study of algebraic structures and symmetries.
One of the cornerstone examples is the Gel’fand–Raikov Theorem that “identifies” a locally compact group with its unitary representations, while further examples arise naturally in the context of invertible transformations on a (possibly noncommutative) state space.
In this endeavour, algebraic structures associated with discrete groups have been successfully unified under the theory of Fell bundles.
Product systems over a unital subsemigroup
P
of a discrete group
G
provide a common context to describe irreversible transformations as a semigroup analogue of Fell bundles.
Examples include subshift spaces, rank one or higher rank graphs, topological graphs, and semigroup actions on C*-algebras (in particular in relation to transfer operators), to mention but a few beyond the Fell bundles setup.

Product systems were introduced by Arveson
[
2
]
disguised under duality, and later put in context by Dinh
[
12
]
for discrete subsemigroups of
\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
Nica
[
46
]
considered operator algebras related to semigroups that admit a type of order structure, i.e., quasi-lattice semigroups, for which a representation by left translations was well-defined, thus making the resulting C*-algebras amenable to study.
It had been known that, unlike to the group case, taking plain isometric representations leads to intractable objects, e.g., the universal isometric C*-algebra of
\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
is not even nuclear
[
44
]
.
A richer structure is at hand when one considers the relations in the Fock representation of a quasi-lattice order, now commonly known as Nica covariance.
Nica’s setup is now covered by the theory as a product system with “trivial” fibers.
On the other hand, Pimsner
[
49
]
introduced C*-algebras of product systems over
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, i.e., C*-correspondences, that encodes successfully graph C*-algebras, crossed products over
\mathbb{Z}
, or partial transformations, see for example
[
32
]
.
Motivated by
[
46
,
49
]
as well as his work with Raeburn
[
21
]
, Fowler
[
20
]
proceeded to an in-depth study of product systems over quasi-lattices, that inspired a great number of subsequent works.
In order to make full use of this additional feature, Fowler imposed several axioms on the product system resulting to a by-default Wick ordering.
Moving beyond the quasi-lattice order structure, Brownlowe, Larsen and Stammeier
[
7
]
considered product systems related to dynamical systems over right LCM semigroups.
Kwaśniewski and Larsen
[
36
,
37
]
extended this setup to general product systems over right LCM semigroups, and essentially this is the furthest one may go and still have a Wick ordering.
Cuntz, Deninger and Laca
[
9
]
used the Fock model beyond Nica’s work
[
46
]
for
P=R\rtimes R^{\times}
, where Nica covariance may fail, but one can still use the Fock representation as the prototype for covariant relations.

The coaction of the ambient group
G
has been pivotal in the theory of product systems, in particular in relation to boundary quotients by Carlsen, Larsen, Sims and Vittadello
[
8
]
and Sehnem
[
52
,
53
]
, as well as in semigroup algebras by Laca and Sehnem
[
38
]
and the first author, Katsoulis, Laca and Li
[
25
]
.
Dor-On, the first author, Katsoulis, Laca and Li
[
13
]
noted that there is a canonical Fell bundle arising from the normal coaction on
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)
, denoted here by
{\mathcal{F}}{\mathcal{C}}X
.
It appears that, while
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)
is the reduced C*-algebra of
{\mathcal{F}}{\mathcal{C}}X
, it is not always the reduced C*-algebra for the universal C*-algebra
{\mathcal{T}}(X)
of the representations of
X
(as it happens for
P=\mathbb{Z}_{+}
).
To make a distinction, we will write
{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)
for the universal C*-algebra of
{\mathcal{F}}{\mathcal{C}}X
; a representation of
X
that promotes to a
*
-representation of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)
will be called
Fock covariant
.
It was shown in
[
13
]
that Fock covariant representations coincide with Nica covariant representations for a compactly aligned product system
X
over a right LCM semigroup
P
, and thus
{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)
coincides with the universal Nica covariant C*-algebra
{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{T}}(X)
in this case.

The difference between
{\mathcal{T}}(X)
and
{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)
is apparent even when every
X
arises from
P
by one-dimensional spaces, where the unital representations of
X
are in bijection with the unital semigroup representations of
P
.
In his ground-breaking work, Li
[
40
]
took motivation from
[
9
]
and revived the interest on semigroup representations with the twist that, apart from the semigroup structure, they remember the principal ideals and their intersections, i.e., the
constructible
ideals.
Li’s models are variants of the relations in the Fock representation and were further examined by the first author, Katsoulis, Laca and Li
[
25
]
, also in connection with inverse semigroup realizations by Norling
[
47
]
.
At the same time Laca and Sehnem
[
38
]
identified completely the relations that promote a general semigroup representation to a Fock covariant representation of its related product system.
It is thus natural to ask for the characterisation of Fock covariance for general product systems.

Motivated by the Hao–Ng isomorphism problem, our first aim in this work is to provide a description of the equivariant Fock covariant injective representations of
X
.
For our purposes, we will view a product system
X
as a family of C*-correspondences
\{X_{p}\}_{p\in P}
in a common
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
that satisfies some natural semigroup relations, i.e., that
A:=X_{e}
is a C*-algebra, and

(1.1)

X_{p}\cdot X_{q}\subseteq X_{pq}\text{ and }X_{p}^{*}\cdot X_{pq}\subseteq X_{q}\text{ for all }p,q\in P.

This setup enables to still define left creation operators on the full Fock space
{\mathcal{F}}X
giving rise to the Fock C*-algebra
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)
, while it recovers Fowler’s product systems in
[
20
]
which arise under the stronger saturation condition
[X_{p}\cdot X_{q}]=X_{pq}
for all
p,q\in P
.
We note that most of the works for product systems assume non-degeneracy of the left action on
X
.
Nevertheless, non-degeneracy is rarely used, although imposed by the saturation condition of Fowler
[
20
]
.
Our setting from (
1.1
) contains all non-degenerate product systems, while accommodating possible degenerate ones.
Such cases arise naturally for example in C*-dynamical systems as the ones considered by Davidson, Fuller and the first named author in
[
10
]
.

The characterisation of Fock covariance that we provide is given in terms of the
\mathbf{K}_{{\mathbf{x}}}
-cores for
{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathcal{J}}
.
We then use the natural description of the fixed point algebra by an inductive limit of C*-algebras on the
\cap
-closed finite subsets of the constructible ideals of
P
in
{\mathcal{J}}
, which are spanned by the
\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}
-cores.

Theorem A.

(Theorem
3.2
)
Let
P
be a unital subsemigroup of a discrete group
G
and let
X
be a product system over
P
.
Let
\hat{t}
be a representation of
X
such that
{\mathcal{T}}(X)=\mathrm{C}^{*}(\hat{t})
.
An equivariant injective representation
t
of
X
is Fock covariant if and only if
t
satisfies the following conditions:

(i)

\mathbf{K}_{\emptyset,t_{\ast}}=(0)
.

(ii)

For any
\cap
-closed
{\mathcal{F}}=\{\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{n}\}\subseteq{\mathcal{J}}
such that
\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{x}_{i}\neq\emptyset
, and any
b_{\mathbf{x}_{i}}\in\mathbf{K}_{\mathbf{x}_{i},\hat{t}_{\ast}}
, with
i=1,\dots,n
, the following property holds:

if
\sum\limits_{i:r\in\mathbf{x}_{i}}t_{\ast}(b_{\mathbf{x}_{i}})t_{r}(X_{r})=(0)
for all
r\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{x}_{i}
, then
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}t_{\ast}(b_{\mathbf{x}_{i}})=0
.

We square this characterisation with established results from the literature.
First, we provide a direct connection with the equivariant Nica covariant injective representations when
P
is a right LCM semigroup which recovers
[
13
, Proposition 4.3]
.
Secondly, when each
X_{p}=\mathbb{C}
then we are at the setting of the semigroup representations and our description gives back the relations identified by Laca and Sehnem in
[
38
, Section 3]
; see also
[
26
, Remark 3.15]
(note that injectivity is automatic in the latter case).
The connection with
[
13
]
and
[
38
]
can be seen categorically, however we give independent proofs from first principles that validate the compatibility with our results herein.

As we will see, a description of Fock covariance just for the case of equivariant representations that are injective on
X
is enough to tackle key questions in the theory.
One application of particular interest, and our main motivation for this work, concerns the reduced Hao–Ng isomorphism problem
[
23
]
which we describe below.

1.2.
The Hao–Ng isomorphism problem

Along with
{\mathcal{T}}(X)
and
{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)
there is a significant boundary quotient.
For the case
P=\mathbb{Z}_{+}
this is the Cuntz–Pimsner algebra
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
, which generalises the graph C*-algebra and the Cuntz algebra, and was provided in full generality by Katsura
[
33
]
following the work of many hands.
Katsura
[
34
]
has also proven that
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
is the terminal object for the equivariant injective representations of
X
(Fock covariance is automatically satisfied in this case), while Katsoulis and Kribs
[
29
]
have shown that
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
is the C*-envelope of the tensor algebra of
X
.
Carlsen, Larsen, Sims and Vittadello
[
8
]
have provided the existence of the terminal object in the case of a quasi-lattice, while Dor-On, the first author, Katsoulis, Laca and Li
[
13
]
have tackled the case for right LCM semigroups in terms of a terminal object for the coaction on the tensor algebra.
A pivotal step in the general direction was established by Sehnem
[
52
]
where the appropriate universal quotient
A\times_{X}P
was coined.
The key property of the equivariant representations of
A\times_{X}P
is that injectivity on
X
implies injectivity on the fixed point algebra.
Equivariance allows to view
A\times_{X}P
as the universal C*-algebra of the
strong covariant bundle
denoted by
{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{C}}X
in
[
13
]
.
The terminal object in
[
13
]
is then the reduced C*-algebra of
{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{C}}X
denoted by
A\times_{X,\lambda}P
.
Sehnem
[
53
]
has shown that completely isometric representations of the tensor algebra
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)^{+}
admit automatically a conditional expectation.
With this remarkable result at hand, Sehnem
[
53
]
resolved the old standing problem of identifying
A\times_{X,\lambda}P
with the C*-envelope of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)^{+}
, encompassing the work of many authors.

The reduced Hao–Ng isomorphism problem refers to a group action
\alpha
of
{\mathfrak{H}}
on
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)
that leaves invariant every
\lambda_{p}(X_{p})
; such an action is called a
generalised gauge action
.
In this case there is an induced product system denoted by
X\rtimes_{\alpha,\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}}
and an induced action
\dot{\alpha}
on
A\times_{X,\lambda}P
.
The main question is whether there is a canonical
*
-isomorphism

(1.2)

(A\rtimes_{\alpha,\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}})\times_{X\rtimes_{\alpha,\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}},\lambda}P\stackrel{{\scriptstyle?}}{{\simeq}}(A\times_{X,\lambda}P)\rtimes_{\dot{\alpha},\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}},

i.e., if the reduced strong covariant functor commutes with the reduced crossed product functor.
The problem was introduced in
[
23
]
, where it was solved for
P=\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
{\mathfrak{H}}
being an amenable locally compact group.
As applications of their results, Hao and Ng recover previous results on Hilbert bimodules by Abadie
[
1
]
, and on graph C*-algebras by Kumjian and Pask
[
35
]
without using groupoid C*-algebras.
The importance of the Hao–Ng isomorphism problem has been further emphasised by Katsoulis
[
27
]
in connection to the work of Echterhoff, Kaliszewski, Quigg and Raeburn
[
17
]
on imprimitivity theorems for C*-dynamical systems.

In a series of works, Katsoulis
[
27
,
28
]
, and Katsoulis and Ramsey
[
31
]
, have rephrased the reduced Hao–Ng isomorphism problem in terms of C*-envelopes and crossed products
[
30
]
, i.e., whether there exists a canonical
*
-isomorphism

(1.3)

\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X\rtimes_{\alpha,\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}})^{+})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle?}}{{\simeq}}\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)^{+})\rtimes_{\dot{\alpha},\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}}.

The significance of this approach is manifested in
[
27
]
where the problem was resolved for
P=\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
{\mathfrak{H}}
discrete.
The reduced Hao–Ng isomorphism problem was later answered when
P
defines an abelian lattice order in
G
by Dor-On and Katsoulis
[
14
]
when
{\mathfrak{H}}
is discrete, and by Katsoulis
[
28
]
when
{\mathfrak{H}}
is locally compact abelian.
Furthermore it has been answered when
P
is a right LCM semigroup and
{\mathfrak{H}}
is discrete by Dor-On, the first author, Katsoulis, Laca and Li
[
13
]
.
The question remains open for a generalised gauge action by a general group
{\mathfrak{H}}
.

The main approach in
[
13
,
14
,
27
,
28
,
31
]
has been to use the independence condition for right LCM semigroups and Nica covariance of the identity representation

(1.4)

X\rtimes_{\alpha,\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)\rtimes_{\alpha,\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}}

in order to obtain a canonical
*
-isomorphism

(1.5)

{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X\rtimes_{\alpha,\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}})\simeq{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)\rtimes_{\alpha,\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}}.

From there it follows that

(1.6)

{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X\rtimes_{\alpha,\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}})^{+}\simeq{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)^{+}\rtimes_{\alpha,\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}},

and then the C*-envelope theory of Katsoulis and Ramsey
[
30
]
can be implemented.
In the absence of independence, it is unclear whether the
*
-isomorphism (
1.5
) still holds, but it does not exclude the possibility that the completely isometric isomorphism (
1.6
) is valid.
A careful investigation of the arguments of
[
13
,
14
,
27
,
28
,
31
]
reveals that Fock covariance of the identity representation
X\rtimes_{\alpha,\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)\rtimes_{\alpha,\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}}
enables the completely isometric isomorphism (
1.6
).
Our characterisation applies towards this resolution when
{\mathfrak{H}}
is discrete without restrictions on the product system
X
and the semigroup
P
.
This recovers the aforementioned results appearing in
[
13
, Theorem 6.3]
,
[
14
, Theorem 6.6]
, and
[
27
, Theorem 3.2]
.

Theorem B.

(Theorem
4.6
)
Let
P
be a unital subsemigroup of a discrete group
G
and let
X
be a product system over
P
.
Let
\alpha
be a generalised gauge action of a discrete group
{\mathfrak{H}}
on
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)
.
Then the identity representation
X\rtimes_{\alpha,\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)\rtimes_{\alpha,\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}}
lifts to a completely isometric isomorphism

{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X\rtimes_{\alpha,\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}})^{+}\simeq{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)^{+}\rtimes_{\alpha,\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}}.

Consequently, the reduced Hao–Ng isomorphism problem has an affirmative answer, i.e.,

(A\rtimes_{\alpha,\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}})\times_{X\rtimes_{\alpha,\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}},\lambda}P\simeq(A\times_{X,\lambda}P)\rtimes_{\dot{\alpha},\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}},

by a canonical
*
-isomorphism, where
\dot{\alpha}
is the induced action of
{\mathfrak{H}}
on
A\times_{X,\lambda}P
.

The structure of the manuscript is as follows.
In Section
2
we fix notation and provide some relevant results we will be using.
In Section
3
we give the description of Fock covariance, and applications to compactly aligned product systems over right LCM semigroups in connection to
[
13
,
36
,
37
]
, as well as to semigroup representations in connection to
[
38
]
.
In Section
4
we provide the context for the reduced Hao–Ng isomorphism problem, and then proceed to its resolution when
{\mathfrak{H}}
is discrete.
Throughout, we comment when non-degeneracy is not required, and we provide details when a different proof is needed, when using results from the literature.

2.
Preliminaries

We begin with some preliminaries on coactions on C*-algebras, semigroup C*-algebras and product systems to fix notation.
All groups and semigroups we consider in this section are discrete.
We will write
\otimes
for the minimal tensor product between C*-algebras.
A map between algebras will be called canonical if it preserves generators of the same index.
For notational convenience, we will write
x^{0}=1
for an element
x
in a unital algebra with unit
1
.
If
E
is a subset of a normed linear space
F
then we will write
[E]
for the closed linear span generated by
E
in
F
.

2.1.
Coactions on C*-algebras

For a discrete group
G
we write
u_{g}
for the generators of the universal group C*-algebra
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}(G)
, and
\lambda_{g}:=\lambda(u_{g})
for the left regular representation
\lambda\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}(G)\to\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(G)
.
We write
\chi
for the character on
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}(G)
.
By the universal property of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}(G)
there exists a faithful
*
-homomorphism

\Delta\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}(G)\to\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}(G)\otimes\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}(G);u_{g}\mapsto u_{g}\otimes u_{g}.

By Fell’s absorption principle there exists a faithful
*
-homomorphism

\Delta_{\lambda}\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(G)\to\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(G)\otimes\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(G);\lambda_{g}\mapsto\lambda_{g}\otimes\lambda_{g},

with the additional property that
\Delta_{\lambda}\circ\lambda=(\lambda\otimes\lambda)\circ\Delta
.

There is a direct connection between coactions on C*-algebras, gradings and Fell bundles.
The reader is addressed to
[
18
,
19
,
50
]
where this theory is laid in full detail.
Since the group
G
is discrete, the coactions we consider are automatically non-degenerate in the sense of
[
50
]
, see for example
[
13
, Remark 3.2]
.

We will say that a C*-algebra
{\mathcal{C}}

admits a coaction
\delta
by
G
if there is a faithful
*
-homomorphism
\delta\colon{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathcal{C}}\otimes\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}(G)
such that
the coaction identity

(\delta\otimes{\operatorname{id}})\circ\delta=({\operatorname{id}}\otimes\Delta)\circ\delta

is satisfied.
Since
G
is discrete, the coaction identity is equivalent to the induced
spectral spaces

{\mathcal{C}}_{g}:=\{c\in{\mathcal{C}}\mid\delta(c)=c\otimes u_{g}\}\text{ for all }g\in G,

together spanning a dense subset of
{\mathcal{C}}
, see the proof of
[
45
, Proposition 2.6]
.
If, in addition, the map
({\operatorname{id}}\otimes\lambda)\circ\delta
is faithful, then
\delta
will be called
normal
.
It follows that
\delta
is normal if and only if
{\mathcal{C}}
admits a
reduced
coaction by
G
, i.e.,
there is a faithful
*
-homomorphism
\delta_{\lambda}\colon{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathcal{C}}\otimes\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(G)
such that

(\delta_{\lambda}\otimes{\operatorname{id}})\circ\delta_{\lambda}=({\operatorname{id}}\otimes\Delta_{\lambda})\circ\delta_{\lambda}.

We note that if
\delta\colon{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathcal{C}}\otimes\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}(G)
is a coaction, then

E:=({\operatorname{id}}\otimes E_{\lambda})\circ({\operatorname{id}}\otimes\lambda)\circ\delta

defines a conditional expectation on
{\mathcal{C}}_{e}
, where
E_{\lambda}
is the faithful conditional expectation of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(G)
.
It follows that
\delta
is normal if and only if
E
is faithful.

More generally, a collection
\{{\mathcal{C}}_{g}\}_{g\in G}
of closed subspaces of a C*-algebra
{\mathcal{C}}
is called a
C*-grading of
{\mathcal{C}}
if:

(i)

\sum_{g\in G}{\mathcal{C}}_{g}
is dense in
{\mathcal{C}}
;

(ii)

{\mathcal{C}}_{g}{\mathcal{C}}_{h}\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}_{gh}
; and

(iii)

{\mathcal{C}}_{g}^{*}\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}_{g^{-1}}
.

If there exists a conditional expectation
E\colon{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathcal{C}}_{e}
, then the subspaces are linearly independent, see
[
18
, Theorem 3.3]
.
In this case the C*-grading is called
topological
.
By definition, a coaction on a C*-algebra induces a topological C*-grading.

Gradings form the prototypical example of Fell bundles.
A
Fell bundle
over a group
G
is a collection
{\mathcal{B}}=\{{\mathcal{B}}_{g}\}_{g\in G}
of Banach spaces, each of which is called a
fiber
, such that:

(i)

there are bilinear and associative
multiplication maps
from
{\mathcal{B}}_{g}\times{\mathcal{B}}_{g^{\prime}}
to
{\mathcal{B}}_{gg^{\prime}}
such that
\|b_{g}b_{g^{\prime}}\|\leq\|b_{g}\|\,\|b_{g^{\prime}}\|
;

(ii)

there are conjugate linear
involution maps
from
{\mathcal{B}}_{g}
to
{\mathcal{B}}_{g^{-1}}
such that
(b_{g}^{*})^{*}=b_{g}
and
\|b_{g}^{*}\|=\|b_{g}\|
;

(iii)

(b_{g}b_{g^{\prime}})^{*}=(b_{g^{\prime}})^{*}b_{g}^{*}
;

(iv)

\|b_{g}^{*}b_{g}\|=\|b_{g}\|^{2}
;

(v)

b_{g}^{*}b_{g}\geq 0
in
{\mathcal{B}}_{e}
.

Note that conditions (i)–(iv) imply that
{\mathcal{B}}_{e}
is in fact a C*-algebra and thus condition (v) makes sense.
A
representation

\Psi
of a Fell bundle
{\mathcal{B}}
over
G
is a family
\{\Psi_{g}\}_{g\in G}
of linear maps each one defined on
{\mathcal{B}}_{g}
such that:

(i)

\Psi_{g}(b_{g})\Psi_{h}(b_{h})=\Psi_{gh}(b_{g}b_{h})
for all
g,h\in G
; and

(ii)

\Psi_{g}(b_{g})^{*}=\Psi_{g^{-1}}(b_{g}^{*})
for all
g\in G
.

It follows that
\Psi_{e}
is a
*
-homomorphism and thus contractive.
A standard C*-trick shows that every
\Psi_{g}
is contractive.
We say that a representation
\Psi
is
injective
if
\Psi_{e}
is injective; in this case every
\Psi_{g}
is isometric.
A representation
\Psi
is called
equivariant
if there exists a
*
-homomorphism

\delta\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}(\Psi)\to\mathrm{C}^{*}(\Psi)\otimes\mathrm{C}^{*}(G);\Psi_{g}(b_{g})\mapsto\Psi_{g}(b_{g})\otimes u_{g}.

It follows that
\delta
is faithful, with a left inverse given by the map
{\operatorname{id}}\otimes\chi
, and that it satisfies the coaction identity.

We write
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{B}})
for the universal C*-algebra with respect to the representations of
{\mathcal{B}}
and we write

\hat{j}\colon{\mathcal{B}}\to\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{B}})

for the canonical embedding.
We use the same symbol
\Psi
for the
*
-homomorphism of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{B}})
induced by a representation
\{\Psi_{g}\}_{g\in G}
of
{\mathcal{B}}
(as
\Psi\circ\hat{j}_{g}=\Psi_{g}
).
By universality, we have that
\{\hat{j}_{g}\}_{g\in G}
is an equivariant representation of
{\mathcal{B}}
, and in particular by
[
19
, Proposition 17.9]
we have that the map
\hat{j}_{g}\colon{\mathcal{B}}_{g}\to[\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{B}})]_{g}
is an isometric isomorphism.
Hence, any Fell bundle arises as a C*-grading from some C*-algebra.

The left regular representation of a Fell bundle
{\mathcal{B}}
is defined by considering the left creation operators

(\lambda_{g}(b_{g})\xi)_{g^{\prime}}=b_{g}\xi_{g^{-1}g^{\prime}}\text{ for all }b_{g}\in{\mathcal{B}},

on
\ell^{2}({\mathcal{B}}):=\sum_{g\in G}^{\oplus}{\mathcal{B}}_{g}
seen as the Hilbert module direct sum over
{\mathcal{B}}_{e}
.
We write
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}({\mathcal{B}})
for the C*-algebra generated by
\lambda
.

By writing
j\colon{\mathcal{B}}\to\ell^{2}({\mathcal{B}})
for the canonical embedding of each fiber at the corresponding summand of
\ell^{2}({\mathcal{B}})
, we can define the unitary

W\colon\ell^{2}({\mathcal{B}})\otimes\ell^{2}(G)\to\ell^{2}({\mathcal{B}})\otimes\ell^{2}(G);j_{g}(b_{g})\otimes\delta_{g^{\prime}}\mapsto j_{g}(b_{g})\otimes\delta_{gg^{\prime}}.

It follows that
W
implements a reduced coaction

\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}({\mathcal{B}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathrm{C}^{*}(\lambda_{g}(b_{g})\otimes I)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\operatorname{ad}_{W}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}({\mathcal{B}})\otimes\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(G),

and thus a normal coaction.
By
[
18
, Theorem 3.3]
we have that, if
\Psi
is an equivariant representation of
{\mathcal{B}}
that is injective on
{\mathcal{B}}_{e}
, then there are equivariant canonical
*
-epimorphisms

\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{B}})\longrightarrow\mathrm{C}^{*}(\Psi)\longrightarrow\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}({\mathcal{B}}).

If, in addition, the coaction on
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\Psi)
is normal, then
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\Psi)\simeq\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}({\mathcal{B}})
.

More generally, let
\Psi
be an equivariant (possibly non-injective) representation of
{\mathcal{B}}
, then by the proof of
[
19
, Proposition 21.4]
(or by using the Fourier transform) we obtain that every
\Psi_{g}
has closed range, and hence we have the induced Fell bundle

\Psi({\mathcal{B}}):=\{\Psi_{g}({\mathcal{B}}_{g})\}_{g\in G}.

Therefore there are equivariant
*
-epimorphisms, making the following diagram

\textstyle{\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{B}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}({\mathcal{B}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}(\Psi({\mathcal{B}}))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{C}^{*}(\Psi)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(\Psi({\mathcal{B}}))}

commutative, see
[
19
, Proposition 21.2, Proposition 21.3]
.
We will make use of the following folklore fact for Fell bundles.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{B}}
be a Fell bundle over a discrete group
G
, and
\Psi_{1}
and
\Psi_{2}
be equivariant representations of
{\mathcal{B}}
.
Then

\ker\Psi_{1}\cap[\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{B}})]_{e}\subseteq\ker\Psi_{2}\cap[\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{B}})]_{e}

if and only if

\ker\Psi_{1}\cap[\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{B}})]_{g}\subseteq\ker\Psi_{2}\cap[\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{B}})]_{g}\text{ for all }g\in G.

If any of the above holds, and
\Psi_{1}({\mathcal{B}})
is the induced Fell bundle in
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\Psi_{1})
, then
\Psi_{2}
defines a representation of
\Psi_{1}({\mathcal{B}})
, and thus there is a commutative diagram

\textstyle{\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{B}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{C}^{*}(\Psi_{2})}
\textstyle{\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}(\Psi_{1}({\mathcal{B}}))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

of equivariant
*
-epimorphisms.

Proof.
.

It is obvious that, if the inclusion holds for all
g\in G
, then in particular it holds for
g=e
.
Conversely, suppose that

\ker\Psi_{1}\cap[\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{B}})]_{e}\subseteq\ker\Psi_{2}\cap[\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{B}})]_{e},

and let
x\in\ker\Psi_{1}\cap[\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{B}})]_{g}
for
g\in G
.
Then

x^{*}x\in\ker\Psi_{1}\cap[\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{B}})]_{e}\subseteq\ker\Psi_{2}\cap[\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{B}})]_{e},

and so
x\in\ker\Psi_{2}
.
Consequently we derive that
x\in\ker\Psi_{2}\cap[\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{B}})]_{g}
, as required.

For the second part of the proof, the existence of the maps

\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{B}})\to\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}(\Psi_{1}({\mathcal{B}}))\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{B}})\to\mathrm{C}^{*}(\Psi_{2})

follows by the discussion prior to the statement and the universal property of the universal C*-algebras.
Due to the inclusion of the kernels, for every
g\in G
we have a commutative diagram

\textstyle{{\mathcal{B}}_{g}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Psi_{2}|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{g}}}
\scriptstyle{\Psi_{1}|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{g}}}
\textstyle{\Psi_{2}({\mathcal{B}}_{g})}
\textstyle{\Psi_{1}({\mathcal{B}}_{g})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Psi_{g}}

such that
\Psi_{g}(\Psi_{1}(b_{g}))=\Psi_{2}(b_{g})
for every
b_{g}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{g}
, and
g\in G
.
Since
\Psi_{1}
and
\Psi_{2}
are representations of
{\mathcal{B}}
, we get that the collection
\{\Psi_{g}\}_{g\in G}
defines a representation
\Psi
from
\Psi_{1}({\mathcal{B}})
to
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\Psi_{2})
.
Hence
\Psi
promotes to a
*
-representation of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}(\Psi_{1}({\mathcal{B}}))
in
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\Psi_{2})
.
By definition this map closes the diagram, and the proof is complete.
∎

We will be interested in graded quotients of C*-algebras of Fell bundles.
If
\delta\colon{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathcal{C}}\otimes\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}(G)
is a coaction on a C*-algebra
{\mathcal{C}}
, then we say that an ideal
{\mathcal{I}}\lhd{\mathcal{C}}
is
induced
if

{\mathcal{I}}=\left\langle{\mathcal{I}}\cap[{\mathcal{C}}]_{e}\right\rangle.

In that case, the canonical quotient map
q_{\mathcal{I}}
is equivariant, i.e., the coaction
\delta
descends to a coaction on
{\mathcal{C}}/{\mathcal{I}}
, see
[
18
, Proposition 3.11]
and
[
8
, Proposition A.1]
for the full details.
The following proposition is perhaps folklore, and we include a proof for completeness.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{B}}
be a Fell bundle over a discrete group
G
and let
{\mathcal{I}}\lhd\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{B}})
be an induced ideal.
Let
q_{\mathcal{I}}({\mathcal{B}})
be the induced Fell bundle from the coaction on
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{B}})/{\mathcal{I}}
, where
q_{\mathcal{I}}\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{B}})\to\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{B}})/{\mathcal{I}}
is the canonical quotient map.
Then there exists a commutative diagram

\textstyle{\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{B}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Phi}
\scriptstyle{q_{{\mathcal{I}}}}
\textstyle{\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}(q_{\mathcal{I}}({\mathcal{B}}))}
\textstyle{\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{B}})/{\mathcal{I}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Psi}

of equivariant
*
-epimorphisms such that
\Psi
is a
*
-isomorphism.

Proof.
.

Since
{\mathcal{I}}
is an induced ideal we have that
q_{\mathcal{I}}
is equivariant.
Moreover
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{B}})/{\mathcal{I}}
admits a coaction and let
q_{\mathcal{I}}({\mathcal{B}}):=\{[\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{B}})/{\mathcal{I}}]_{g}\}_{g\in G}
be the induced Fell bundle.
In order to make a distinction, we will write

\hat{j}^{{\mathcal{B}}}\colon{\mathcal{B}}\to\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{B}})\quad\text{and}\quad\hat{j}^{q_{{\mathcal{I}}}({\mathcal{B}})}\colon q_{{\mathcal{I}}}({\mathcal{B}})\to\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}(q_{\mathcal{I}}({\mathcal{B}}))

for the induced embeddings of the corresponding Fell bundles.

First note that the family
\left\{\hat{j}^{q_{\mathcal{I}}({\mathcal{B}})}_{g}\circ q_{{\mathcal{I}}}\circ\hat{j}_{g}^{{\mathcal{B}}}\right\}_{g\in G}
is a representation of
{\mathcal{B}}
and hence there is an induced
*
-epimorphism

\Phi\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{B}})\to\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}(q_{\mathcal{I}}({\mathcal{B}})).

By the definition of
\Phi
we have

{\mathcal{I}}\cap[\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{B}})]_{e}={\mathcal{I}}\cap\hat{j}^{{\mathcal{B}}}_{e}({\mathcal{B}}_{e})\subseteq\hat{j}^{{\mathcal{B}}}_{e}\left(\ker\left(\hat{j}^{q_{\mathcal{I}}({\mathcal{B}})}_{e}\circ q_{{\mathcal{I}}}\circ\hat{j}^{{\mathcal{B}}}_{e}\right)\right)\subseteq\ker\Phi,

and thus we have

{\mathcal{I}}=\left\langle{\mathcal{I}}\cap[\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{B}})]_{e}\right\rangle\subseteq\ker\Phi.

Therefore we obtain a commutative diagram

\textstyle{\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{B}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Phi}
\scriptstyle{q_{{\mathcal{I}}}}
\textstyle{\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}(q_{{\mathcal{I}}}({\mathcal{B}}))}
\textstyle{\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{B}})/{\mathcal{I}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Psi}

of
*
-epimorphisms.
By checking on the generators we have that the
*
-epimorphisms are equivariant.

On the other hand, since
q_{\mathcal{I}}({\mathcal{B}})
is a topological C*-grading of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{B}})/{\mathcal{I}}
, by
[
19
, Theorem 19.5]
there exists a canonical
*
-epimorphism

\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}(q_{\mathcal{I}}({\mathcal{B}}))\to\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{B}})/{\mathcal{I}},

which is the inverse of
\Psi
.
Hence
\Psi
is a
*
-isomorphism, and the proof is complete.
∎

2.2.
Semigroups

We will require some elements from the theory of semigroup algebras and right ideals of a semigroup from the work of Li
[
40
]
, while we also fix notation.
For a unital discrete left-cancellative semigroup
P
, we let the left creation (isometric) operators given by

V_{p}\colon\ell^{2}(P)\longrightarrow\ell^{2}(P);\delta_{s}\mapsto\delta_{ps},

and we write
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P):=\mathrm{C}^{*}(V_{p}\mid p\in P)
.
The restriction to the diagonal
\ell^{\infty}(P)
induces a faithful conditional expectation on
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)
.
For a set
Z\subseteq P
we will write
E_{[Z]}
for the projection on
[\{\delta_{p}\mid p\in Z\}]\subseteq\ell^{2}(P)
.
It follows that

E_{[Z_{1}]}E_{[Z_{2}]}=E_{[Z_{1}\cap Z_{2}]}\text{ for all }Z_{1},Z_{2}\subseteq P.

For a set
Z\subseteq P
and
p\in P
we write

pZ:=\{px\mid x\in Z\}\quad\text{and}\quad p^{-1}Z:=\{y\in P\mid py\in Z\}.

By definition we have
p^{-1}P=P
.
We write
{\mathcal{J}}
for the smallest family of right ideals of
P
containing
P
and
\emptyset
that is closed under left multiplication, taking pre-images under left multiplication (as in the sense above) and finite intersections, i.e.,

{\mathcal{J}}:=\left\{\bigcap\limits_{j=1}^{N}q_{j,n_{j}}^{-1}p_{j,n_{j}}\dots q_{j,1}^{-1}p_{j,1}P\mid N,n_{j}\in\mathbb{N};p_{j,k},q_{j,k}\in P\right\}\bigcup\{\emptyset\}.

The right ideals of
P
in
{\mathcal{J}}
are called
constructible
.
By the proof of
[
40
, Lemma 3.3]
we obtain a reduced form for the elements of
{\mathcal{J}}
, since

q_{n}^{-1}p_{n}\dots q_{1}^{-1}p_{1}p_{1}^{-1}q_{1}\dots p_{n}^{-1}q_{n}Z=(q_{n}^{-1}p_{n}\dots q_{1}^{-1}p_{1}P)\cap Z

for every
p_{i},q_{i}\in P
and every subset
Z
of
P
.
Consequently the set of constructible ideals is automatically closed under finite intersections, i.e.,

{\mathcal{J}}=\left\{q_{n}^{-1}p_{n}\dots q_{1}^{-1}p_{1}P\mid n\in\mathbb{N};p_{i},q_{i}\in P\right\}\cup\{\emptyset\}.

We will write
{\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{y}}
etc. for the elements in
{\mathcal{J}}
.

Henceforth we will assume that
P
is a unital subsemigroup of a discrete group
G
.
In this case we have

q^{-1}pZ=P\cap q^{-1}\cdot p\cdot Z,\text{ for }Z\subseteq P,

and therefore inductively we obtain

q_{n}^{-1}p_{n}\dots q_{1}^{-1}p_{1}P=P\cap(q_{n}^{-1}\cdot p_{n}\cdot P)\cap(q_{n}^{-1}\cdot p_{n}\cdot q_{n-1}^{-1}\cdot p_{n-1}\cdot P)\cap\cdots\cap(q_{n}^{-1}\cdot p_{n}\cdots q_{1}^{-1}\cdot p_{1}\cdot P).

Note also that for every
{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathcal{J}}
we can pick
p_{1},q_{1},\dots,p_{n},q_{n}\in P
such that
p_{1}^{-1}q_{1}\cdots p_{n}^{-1}q_{n}=e
and
{\mathbf{x}}=q_{n}^{-1}p_{n}\dots q_{1}^{-1}p_{1}P
.
Moreover,
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)
admits a normal coaction by
G
such that

[\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)]_{g}=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{V_{p_{1}}^{*}V_{q_{1}}\cdots V_{p_{n}}^{*}V_{q_{n}}\mid n\in\mathbb{N};p_{1},q_{1},\dots,p_{n},q_{n}\in P;p_{1}^{-1}q_{1}\cdots p_{n}^{-1}q_{n}=g\}.

The coaction by
G
is implemented by the unitary operator

U\colon\ell^{2}(P)\otimes\ell^{2}(G)\to\ell^{2}(P)\otimes\ell^{2}(G);U(\delta_{s}\otimes\delta_{g})=\delta_{s}\otimes\delta_{sg}\text{ for all }s\in P,g\in G.

A routine calculation shows that the
*
-homomorphism

\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathrm{C}^{*}(V_{p}\otimes I\mid p\in P)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\operatorname{ad}_{U}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathrm{C}^{*}(V_{p}\otimes\lambda_{p}\mid p\in P)

is a reduced coaction, and thus it lifts to a normal coaction on
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)
.
The induced faithful conditional expectation on
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)
coincides with the compression to the diagonal
\ell^{\infty}(P)
.
In the presence of an overlying group there is an explicit formula for the projection
E_{[{\mathbf{x}}]}
for
{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathcal{J}}
, obtained in
[
40
, Lemma 3.1]
, i.e.,

E_{[{\mathbf{x}}]}=V_{p_{1}}^{*}V_{q_{1}}\cdots V_{p_{n}}^{*}V_{q_{n}},

for any
p_{1},q_{1},\dots,p_{n},q_{n}\in P
satisfying
{\mathbf{x}}=q_{n}^{-1}p_{n}\dots q_{1}^{-1}p_{1}P
and
p_{1}^{-1}q_{1}\cdots p_{n}^{-1}q_{n}=e
in
G
.

2.3.
C*-correspondences

The theory of Hilbert modules over C*-algebras is well-developed.
The reader is addressed to
[
39
,
43
]
for an excellent introduction to the subject; see also
[
32
]
and the references therein.

A
C*-correspondence

X
over
A
is a right Hilbert module over
A
with a left action given by a
*
-homomorphism
\varphi_{X}\colon A\to{\mathcal{L}}(X)
, where
{\mathcal{L}}(X)
denotes the C*-algebra of adjointable operators on
X
.
A C*-correspondence
X
over
A
is called
non-degenerate
if
[\varphi_{X}(A)X]=X
.
We write
{\mathcal{K}}(X)
for the closed linear span of the rank one adjointable operators
\theta_{\xi,\eta}
.
For two C*-correspondences
X,Y
over the same
A
we write
X\otimes_{A}Y
for the balanced tensor product over
A
.
We say that
X
is
unitarily equivalent
to
Y
(symb.
X\simeq Y
) if there is a surjective adjointable operator
U\in{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y)
that is an
A
-bimodule map and
\left\langle U\xi,U\eta\right\rangle=\left\langle\xi,\eta\right\rangle
for all
\xi,\eta\in X
.

A representation of a C*-correspondence
X
over
A
is a pair
(t_{0},t_{1})
where
t_{0}\colon A\to{\mathcal{B}}(H)
is a
*
-homomorphism and
t_{1}\colon X\to{\mathcal{B}}(H)
is a linear map that satisfies the following:

(i)

t_{0}(a)t_{1}(\xi)=t_{1}(\varphi_{X}(a)\xi)
for all
\xi\in X
and
a\in A
,

(ii)

t_{1}(\xi)^{*}t_{1}(\eta)=t_{0}(\left\langle\xi,\eta\right\rangle)
for all
\xi,\eta\in X
.

We note that condition (ii) also implies that
t_{1}(\xi)t_{0}(a)=t_{1}(\xi a)
.
Every representation
(t_{0},t_{1})
as above defines a
*
-representation

t^{(1)}\colon{\mathcal{K}}(X)\to{\mathcal{B}}(H);\theta_{\xi,\eta}\mapsto t(\xi)t(\eta)^{*}\text{ for all }\xi,\eta\in X.

If
t_{0}
is injective, then both
t_{1}
and
t^{(1)}
are isometric.

2.4.
Product systems

In his seminal paper, Fowler
[
20
]
formalised product systems motivated by the work of Nica
[
46
]
, Pimsner
[
49
]
, as well as his work with Raeburn
[
21
]
.
However this definition imposes further conditions on the family of C*-correspondences.
In this subsection we consider a more general framework that still allows the structural constructions relevant to product systems to be formulated yet without these extra conditions.
In Subsection
2.5
we will give the connection with, and the comparison to, the product systems in the sense of Fowler.

Let
P
be a unital left-cancellative discrete semigroup.
We say that a family
X=\{X_{p}\}_{p\in P}
of closed operator spaces in a common
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
is a
(concrete) product system
if the following are satisfied:

(i)

A:=X_{e}
is a C*-algebra;

(ii)

X_{p}\cdot X_{q}\subseteq X_{pq}
for all
p,q\in P
;

(iii)

X_{p}^{*}\cdot X_{pq}\subseteq X_{q}
for all
p,q\in P
.

Uniqueness of
q\in P
in item (iii) follows since
P
is left-cancellative.
Moreover it follows that each
X_{p}
is a C*-correspondence over
A
.

The properties of a concrete product system are enough to provide a Fock space representation.
Towards this end, consider
X
in some
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
, and let
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
with its trivial C*-correspondence structure.
We will be writing

\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{p}\colon X_{p}\times X_{p}\longrightarrow A;(\xi_{p},\eta_{p})\mapsto\left\langle\xi_{p},\eta_{p}\right\rangle_{p}:=\xi_{p}^{*}\cdot\eta_{p},

for the inner product induced on the
X_{p}
.
For every
\xi_{p}\in X_{p}
we define the multiplication operator

M_{\xi_{p}}^{q,pq}\colon X_{q}\longrightarrow X_{pq};\eta_{q}\mapsto\xi_{p}\cdot\eta_{q},

with the multiplication taking place inside
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
, satisfying

\|M_{\xi_{p}}^{q,pq}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(X_{q},X_{pq})}\leq\left\|\xi_{p}\right\|_{X_{p}}.

Associativity of the product yields
M_{\xi_{p}}^{q,pq}\in{\mathcal{L}}(X_{q},X_{pq})
with

(M_{\xi_{p}}^{q,pq})^{*}\colon X_{pq}\to X_{q};\eta_{pq}\mapsto\xi_{p}^{*}\cdot\eta_{pq}\in X_{q}.

Consider the Fock space
{\mathcal{F}}X:=\sum^{\oplus}_{r\in P}X_{r}
, as a right Hilbert module over
A
.
For every
\xi_{p}\in X_{p}
, define the
left creation operator

\lambda_{p}(\xi_{p}):=\operatornamewithlimits{\sum^{\oplus}}_{r\in P}M_{\xi_{p}}^{r,pr}\;\text{ so that }\;\lambda_{p}(\xi_{p})^{*}=\operatornamewithlimits{\sum^{\oplus}}_{r\in P}(M_{\xi_{p}}^{r,pr})^{*},

where the sum is taken in the s*-topology, and with the understanding that we are embedding
{\mathcal{L}}(X_{r},X_{pr})\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{F}}X)
as the
(pr,r)
-entry.
By applying on
\eta_{r}\in X_{r}\subseteq{\mathcal{F}}X
we derive that

\lambda_{p}(\xi_{p})\eta_{r}=\xi_{p}\cdot\eta_{r}\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda_{p}(\xi_{p})^{*}\eta_{r}=\begin{cases}\xi_{p}^{*}\cdot\eta_{r}&\text{if }r\in pP,\\
0&\text{if }r\notin pP.\end{cases}

We write
\lambda\colon X\to{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{F}}X)
for this map, and we refer to
\lambda=\{\lambda_{p}\}_{p\in P}
as the
Fock representation of
X
.
We will write
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)
for the
Fock C*-algebra
defined as
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\lambda_{p}(X_{p})\mid p\in P)
.

More generally, a
(Toeplitz) representation
t=\{t_{p}\}_{p\in P}
of
X
consists of a family of linear maps
t_{p}
of
X_{p}
such that:

(i)

t_{e}
is a
*
-representation of
A:=X_{e}
;

(ii)

t_{p}(\xi_{p})t_{q}(\xi_{q})=t_{pq}(\xi_{p}\cdot\xi_{q})
for all
\xi_{p}\in X_{p}
and
\xi_{q}\in X_{q}
;

(iii)

t_{p}(\xi_{p})^{*}t_{pq}(\xi_{pq})=t_{q}(\xi_{p}^{*}\cdot\xi_{pq})
for all
\xi_{p}\in X_{p}
and
\xi_{pq}\in X_{pq}
.

By definition every pair
(t_{e},t_{p})
is a representation of the C*-correspondence
X_{p}
over
A
.
A representation
t
is called
injective
if
t_{e}
is injective on
A
.
It transpires that the Fock representation is an injective representation of
X
.
The
Toeplitz algebra
{\mathcal{T}}(X)
of
X
is the universal C*-algebra generated by
X=\{X_{p}\}_{p\in P}
with respect to the representations of
X
.
If
t
defines a representation of
X
, then we will write
t_{\ast}\colon{\mathcal{T}}(X)\to{\mathcal{B}}(H)
for the induced
*
-representation of
{\mathcal{T}}(X)
and

\mathrm{C}^{*}(t)\equiv\mathrm{C}^{*}(t_{\ast}):=t_{\ast}({\mathcal{T}}(X))=\mathrm{C}^{*}(t_{p}(X_{p})\mid p\in P).

Henceforth we will assume that
P
is a unital subsemigroup of a group
G
.

\xi_{pq}\in X_{pq}

t_{p}(\xi_{p})^{*}t_{pq}(\xi_{pq})=\hat{j}_{p}(\hat{t}_{p}(\xi_{p}))^{*}\hat{j}_{pq}(\hat{t}_{pq}(\xi_{pq}))=\hat{j}_{p^{-1}}(\hat{t}_{p}(\xi_{p})^{*})\hat{j}_{pq}(\hat{t}_{pq}(\xi_{pq}))=\hat{j}_{q}(\hat{t}_{q}(\xi_{p}^{*}\xi_{pq}))=t_{q}(\xi_{p}^{*}\xi_{pq}),

as required.

Therefore, there is an induced
*
-epimorphism

{\mathcal{T}}(X)\to\mathrm{C}^{*}(t)=\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{P}}{\mathcal{S}}X);\hat{t}_{p}(\xi_{p})\mapsto t_{p}(\xi_{p})=\hat{j}_{p}(\hat{t}_{p}(\xi_{p})).

On the other hand, by
[
19
, Theorem 19.5]
we obtain a
*
-epimorphism

\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{P}}{\mathcal{S}}X)\to{\mathcal{T}}(X);\hat{j}_{p}(\hat{t}_{p}(\xi_{p}))\mapsto\hat{t}_{p}(\xi_{p})

in the other direction, and the proof is complete.
∎

As noted in
[
13
, Proposition 4.1]
, the representation
\lambda
admits a reduced coaction by using the unitary

U\colon{\mathcal{F}}X\otimes\ell^{2}(G)\to{\mathcal{F}}X\otimes\ell^{2}(G);U(\xi_{r}\otimes\delta_{g})=\xi_{r}\otimes\delta_{rg}.

Indeed, for this
U
we have

U\cdot(\lambda_{p}(\xi_{p})\otimes I)=(\lambda_{p}(\xi_{p})\otimes\lambda_{p})\cdot U\text{ for all }p\in P,

and therefore the map

{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathrm{C}^{*}(\lambda_{p}(\xi_{p})\otimes I\mid p\in P)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\operatorname{ad}_{U}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathrm{C}^{*}(\lambda_{p}(\xi_{p})\otimes\lambda_{p}\mid p\in P)

defines a reduced coaction on
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)
.
Consequently, it lifts to a normal coaction
\delta
on
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)
.

2.5.
Fowler’s product systems

In
[
20
, Definition 2.1]
Fowler defines a product system
X
over
P
with coefficients in a C*-algebra
A
as a family
\{X_{p}\}_{p\in P}
of C*-correspondences over
A
together with a family of maps
\{u_{p,q}\colon X_{p}\otimes_{A}X_{q}\to X_{pq}\}_{p,q\in P}
such that:

(i)

the space
X_{e}
is the C*-correspondence
A
over
A
where the left and right actions of
A
is multiplication on
A
;

(ii)

if
p,q\neq e
, then
u_{p,q}
is a unitary;

(iii)

if
p=e
, then
u_{e,q}\colon A\otimes_{A}X_{q}\to X_{q}
is given by the left action of
A
on
X_{q}
for
q\in P
;

(iv)

if
q=e
, then
u_{p,e}\colon X_{p}\otimes_{A}A\to X_{p}
is given by the right action of
A
on
X_{p}
for
p\in P
;

(v)

the maps
\{u_{p,q}\}_{p,q\in P}
are associative in the sense that

u_{p,qr}\circ({\operatorname{id}}_{X_{p}}\otimes u_{q,r})=u_{pq,r}\circ(u_{p,q}\otimes{\operatorname{id}}_{X_{r}})\text{ for all }p,q,r\in P.

Brownlowe, Larsen and Stammeier note in
[
7
, Remark 6.2]

that if the group of units of the semigroup is non-trivial, then every
X_{p}
is automatically non-degenerate, see also
[
37
, Remark 1.3]
.
Hence it has been accustomed to work within the non-degenerate setup.
To allow for comparisons, we will refer to
(X,\{u_{p,q}\}_{p,q\in P})
as
a product system in the sense of Fowler
.

It can be directly verified that, if
X
is a concrete product system in some
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
such that
[X_{p}\cdot X_{q}]=X_{pq}
for all
p,q\in P
, then the family
\{X_{p}\}_{p\in P}
defines a product system in the sense of Fowler by considering the unitary maps
\{u_{p,q}\}_{p,q\in P}
given by

u_{p,q}\colon X_{p}\otimes_{A}X_{q}\to X_{pq};\xi_{p}\otimes\xi_{q}\mapsto\xi_{p}\cdot\xi_{q}.

A
(Toeplitz) representation

t
of a product system
X
in the sense of Fowler consists of a family
\{t_{p}\}_{p\in P}
, where
(t_{e},t_{p})
is a representation of the C*-correspondence
X_{p}
for all
p\in P
, and

t_{p}(\xi_{p})t_{q}(\xi_{q})=t_{pq}(u_{p,q}(\xi_{p}\otimes\xi_{q}))\text{ for all }\xi_{p}\in X_{p},\xi_{q}\in X_{q},p,q\in P.

If
(X,\{u_{p,q}^{X}\}_{p,q\in P})
and
(Y,\{u_{p,q}^{Y}\}_{p,q\in P})
are two product systems in the sense of Fowler with coefficients in two C*-algebras
A
and
B
respectively, then we say that
X
is
unitarily equivalent
to
Y
if there is a family
\{W_{p}\colon X_{p}\to Y_{p}\}_{p\in P}
of unitaries such that:

(i)

W_{e}\colon A\to B
is a
*
-isomorphism;

(ii)

\left\langle W_{p}(\xi_{p}),W_{p}(\eta_{p})\right\rangle_{Y_{p}}=W_{e}(\left\langle\xi_{p},\eta_{p}\right\rangle_{X_{p}})
for all
\xi_{p},\eta_{p}\in X_{p}
and
p\in P\setminus\{e\}
;

(iii)

\varphi_{Y_{p}}(W_{e}(a))W_{p}(\xi_{p})=W_{p}(\varphi_{X_{p}}(a)\xi_{p})
for all
a\in A,\xi_{p}\in X_{p}
and
p\in P\setminus\{e\}
;

(iv)

W_{p}(\xi_{p})W_{e}(a)=W_{p}(\xi_{p}a)
for all
a\in A,\xi_{p}\in X_{p}
and
p\in P\setminus\{e\}
;

(v)

u_{p,q}^{Y}\circ(W_{p}\otimes W_{q})=W_{pq}\circ u_{p,q}^{X}
for all
p,q\in P
.

In this case the representations of
X
are in bijection with the representations of
Y
.
The reader may refer to
[
11
, Section 2.3]
for the full details.

Given a product system
X
in the sense of Fowler, we can define the left-creation operators

\lambda_{p}(\xi_{p}):=\sum_{r\in P}\tau_{r}^{pr}(\xi_{p})\text{ for all }\xi_{p}\in X_{p},

as the s*-sum of the adjointable operators

\tau_{r}^{pr}(\xi_{p})\colon X_{r}\longrightarrow X_{pr};\eta_{r}\mapsto u_{p,r}(\xi_{p}\otimes\eta_{r}).

It follows that the family
\lambda(X):=\{\lambda_{p}(X_{p})\}_{p\in P}
defines a concrete product system.
Moreover, the family
\{\lambda_{p}\}_{p\in P}
defines a unitary equivalence between
X
and
\lambda(X)
.
Hence
X
and
\lambda(X)
admit the same representations.
In particular, if
\lambda^{\prime}
is the Fock representation of
\lambda(X)
, then
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\lambda^{\prime})
is unitarily equivalent to
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\lambda)
by the unitary

\bigoplus_{p\in P}\lambda_{p}\colon{\mathcal{F}}X\to{\mathcal{F}}\lambda(X);\xi_{p}\mapsto\lambda_{p}(\xi_{p}).

Hence from an operator theoretic point of view, the concrete product systems that we will be using encompass Fowler’s product systems.

3.
Fock covariant representations

3.1.
Fock covariant representations

Let
P
be a unital subsemigroup of a discrete group
G
and let
X
be a product system over
P
.
Recall that
\lambda
is injective and admits a normal coaction by
G
.
Consider the induced ideal

{\mathcal{J}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}:=\left\langle\ker\lambda_{\ast}\cap[{\mathcal{T}}(X)]_{e}\right\rangle\lhd{\mathcal{T}}(X),

and write
{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)
for the quotient of
{\mathcal{T}}(X)
by
{\mathcal{J}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}
.
Since
{\mathcal{J}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}
is an induced ideal of
{\mathcal{T}}(X)
, then
{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)
inherits a coaction by
G
.
The
Fock covariant
bundle of
X
is the Fell bundle

{\mathcal{F}}{\mathcal{C}}X:=\Big\{[{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)]_{g}\Big\}_{g\in G},

defined by the coaction by
G
on
{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)
.
A representation of
X
that promotes to a representation of
{\mathcal{F}}{\mathcal{C}}X
will be called a
Fock covariant representation of
X
.
We note that, the definition of the Fock covariant bundle does not depend on the choice of
G
.
This follows by applying the first part of the proof of
[
52
, Lemma 3.9]
in this setting. Note also that since the bimodule properties are graded, every representation of
{\mathcal{F}}{\mathcal{C}}X
is a representation of
X
.

Let
q_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}\colon{\mathcal{T}}(X)\to{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)
be the quotient map by the ideal
{\mathcal{J}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}
.
Then

q_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}({\mathcal{P}}{\mathcal{S}}X)=\Big\{[{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)]_{g}\Big\}_{g\in G}={\mathcal{F}}{\mathcal{C}}X,

and thus combining Proposition
2.2
with Proposition
2.7
we obtain a canonical
*
-isomorphism

{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)\simeq\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{F}}{\mathcal{C}}X).

By the definition of
{\mathcal{J}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}
we have

\ker q_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}\cap[{\mathcal{T}}(X)]_{e}={\mathcal{J}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}\cap[{\mathcal{T}}(X)]_{e}=\ker\lambda_{\ast}\cap[{\mathcal{T}}(X)]_{e}.

By Proposition
2.1
we have

\ker q_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}\cap[{\mathcal{T}}(X)]_{g}=\ker\lambda_{\ast}\cap[{\mathcal{T}}(X)]_{g}\text{ for all }g\in G.

Therefore
\lambda
promotes to a representation of
{\mathcal{F}}{\mathcal{C}}X
and we obtain the following commutative diagram

\textstyle{{\mathcal{T}}(X)\simeq\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{P}}{\mathcal{S}}X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\lambda_{\ast}}
\scriptstyle{q_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}}
\textstyle{{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)}
\textstyle{{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)\simeq\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{F}}{\mathcal{C}}X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\dot{\lambda}}

of canonical
*
-epimorphisms.
Since
\lambda_{\ast}
and
q_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}
are equivariant we have that so is
\dot{\lambda}
.
On the other hand, since
\ker q_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}\cap[{\mathcal{T}}(X)]_{e}=\ker\lambda_{\ast}\cap[{\mathcal{T}}(X)]_{e}
, we have that
\dot{\lambda}
is injective on the
e
-fiber.
As the coaction by
G
on
\lambda
is normal, then so is the coaction by
G
on
\dot{\lambda}
, and we conclude that

{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)=\mathrm{C}^{*}(\dot{\lambda})\simeq\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}({\mathcal{F}}{\mathcal{C}}X).

The Fock representation satisfies the following property.
Let
p_{i},q_{i}\in P
and
\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}
such that
p_{1}^{-\varepsilon}q_{1}\cdots p_{n}^{-1}q_{n}^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}=e
and
q_{n}^{-\varepsilon^{\prime}}p_{n}\dots q_{1}^{-1}p_{1}^{\varepsilon}P=\emptyset
.
Proposition
2.4
yields

\left(\lambda_{p_{1}}(\xi_{p_{1}})^{*}\right)^{\varepsilon}\lambda_{q_{1}}(\xi_{q_{1}})\cdots\lambda_{p_{n}}(\xi_{p_{n}})^{*}\lambda_{q_{n}}(\xi_{q_{n}})^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}X_{r}=(0)\text{ for all }r\in P,

and therefore we have

(3.1)

\mathbf{K}_{\emptyset,\lambda_{\ast}}=(0).

We aim to give a characterisation for the equivariant Fock covariant injective representations of
X
.
We begin with the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
P
be a unital subsemigroup of a discrete group
G
and let
X
be a product system over
P
.
Let
\hat{t}
be a representation of
X
such that
{\mathcal{T}}(X)=\mathrm{C}^{*}(\hat{t})
, and let
t
be an equivariant representation of a product system
X
.
Then
t
is Fock covariant if and only if

\ker\lambda_{\ast}\cap\mathbf{B}_{{\mathcal{F}},\hat{t}_{\ast}}\subseteq\ker t_{\ast}\cap\mathbf{B}_{{\mathcal{F}},\hat{t}_{\ast}}\text{ for every finite $\cap$-closed ${\mathcal{F}}\subseteq{\mathcal{J}}$}.

Proof.
.

If
t
is Fock covariant, then by definition
t_{\ast}
factors through the canonical
*
-epimorphism
q_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}\colon{\mathcal{T}}(X)\to{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)
, and thus
\ker q_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}\subseteq\ker t_{\ast}
.
By the definition of
{\mathcal{J}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}
we also have
\ker\lambda_{\ast}\cap[{\mathcal{T}}(X)]_{e}=\ker q_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}\cap[{\mathcal{T}}(X)]_{e}
, and therefore

\ker\lambda_{\ast}\cap\mathbf{B}_{{\mathcal{F}},\hat{t}_{\ast}}=\ker q_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}\cap\mathbf{B}_{{\mathcal{F}},\hat{t}_{\ast}}\subseteq\ker t_{\ast}\cap\mathbf{B}_{{\mathcal{F}},\hat{t}_{\ast}},

where we used that each
\mathbf{B}_{{\mathcal{F}},\hat{t}_{\ast}}
is a C*-subalgebra of
[{\mathcal{T}}(X)]_{e}
.

For the converse, recall that the fixed point algebra
[{\mathcal{T}}(X)]_{e}
is the inductive limit of
\mathbf{B}_{{\mathcal{F}},\hat{t}_{\ast}}
for
\cap
-closed families
{\mathcal{F}}\subseteq{\mathcal{J}}
.
By the properties of the inductive limits we have

\displaystyle\ker q_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}\cap[{\mathcal{T}}(X)]_{e}

\displaystyle=\ker\lambda_{\ast}\cap[{\mathcal{T}}(X)]_{e}=\overline{\bigcup_{{\mathcal{F}}}\left(\ker\lambda_{\ast}\cap\mathbf{B}_{{\mathcal{F}},\hat{t}_{\ast}}\right)}

\displaystyle\subseteq\overline{\bigcup_{{\mathcal{F}}}\left(\ker t_{\ast}\cap\mathbf{B}_{{\mathcal{F}},\hat{t}_{\ast}}\right)}=\ker t_{\ast}\cap[{\mathcal{T}}(X)]_{e},

where we used the assumption for the inclusion.
By combining Proposition
2.1
with Proposition
2.7
we get that
t
induces a representation of
{\mathcal{F}}{\mathcal{C}}X
, i.e.,
t
is Fock covariant, and the proof is complete.
∎

We can now provide the characterisation of equivariant Fock covariant injective representations of
X
.

.

Let
P
be a unital subsemigroup of a discrete group
G
and let

be a product system over
P
.
Let
\hat{t}
be a representation of
X
such that
{\mathcal{T}}(X)=\mathrm{C}^{*}(\hat{t})
.
An equivariant injective representation
t
of

is Fock covariant if and only if
t
satisfies the following conditions:

(i)

\mathbf{K}_{\emptyset,t_{\ast}}=(0)
.

(ii)

For any
\cap
-closed
{\mathcal{F}}=\{\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{n}\}\subseteq{\mathcal{J}}
such that
\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{x}_{i}\neq\emptyset

, with

, the following property holds:

if

for all

, then
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}t_{\ast}(b_{\mathbf{x}_{i}})=0
.

Proof.
.

First we note that the Fock representation
\lambda
satisfies conditions (i) and (ii).
Condition (i) for
\lambda
is shown in (
3.1
).
For condition (ii), let
{\mathcal{F}}=\{\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{n}\}
be a
\cap
-closed finite subset of
{\mathcal{J}}
such that
\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{x}_{i}\neq\emptyset
, and let

be in

such that

\sum_{i:r\in\mathbf{x}_{i}}\lambda_{\ast}(b_{\mathbf{x}_{i}})\lambda_{r}(\xi_{r})=0\text{ for all }r\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{x}_{i}.

Recall that we have
\lambda_{\ast}(b_{{\mathbf{x}}_{i}})\xi_{r}=0
whenever

by Proposition
2.6
.
Therefore, if
r\notin\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{x}_{i}
, then
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{\ast}(b_{\mathbf{x}_{i}})\xi_{r}=0
.
On the other hand, if
r\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{x}_{i}
, then we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{r}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{\ast}(b_{\mathbf{x}_{i}})\xi_{r}\right)=\sum_{i:r\in\mathbf{x}_{i}}\lambda_{r}(\lambda_{\ast}(b_{\mathbf{x}_{i}})\xi_{r})=\sum_{i:r\in\mathbf{x}_{i}}\lambda_{\ast}(b_{\mathbf{x}_{i}})\lambda_{r}(\xi_{r})=0,

where in the first equality we used that we have
\lambda_{\ast}(b_{{\mathbf{x}}_{i}})\xi_{r}=0
whenever
r\notin{\mathbf{x}}_{i}
from Proposition
2.6
, and in the second equality we used Proposition
2.5
.
By injectivity of
\lambda_{r}
, we have
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{\ast}(b_{\mathbf{x}_{i}})\xi_{r}=0
.
This concludes that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{\ast}(b_{\mathbf{x}_{i}})=0
, as required.

Now suppose that
t
is Fock covariant.
Recall that we have

\ker\lambda_{\ast}\cap[{\mathcal{T}}(X)]_{g}=\ker q_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}\cap[{\mathcal{T}}(X)]_{g}\text{ for all }g\in G,

by the definition of
{\mathcal{J}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}
.
Since
t
is Fock covariant, we have that
t_{\ast}
factors through the quotient map
q_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}\colon{\mathcal{T}}(X)\to{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)

where we used that

as required.

For condition (ii), let
{\mathcal{F}}=\{\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{n}\}

\cap

{\mathcal{J}}
such that
\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{x}_{i}\neq\emptyset

.

3.2.
Compactly aligned product systems over right LCM semigroups

A unital semigroup
P
is said to be a
right LCM semigroup
if it is left cancellative and satisfies Clifford’s condition, i.e., for every
p,q\in P
with
pP\cap qP\neq\emptyset
, there exists a
w\in P
such that
pP\cap qP=wP
.
The element
w
is referred to as a
right least common multiple
or
right LCM
of
p
and
q
.
If an element
w\in P
is a right least common multiple of
p,q\in P
, then so is
wr
for every
r\in P^{*}:=P\cap P^{-1}
.
It follows that
P
is a right LCM semigroup if and only if
{\mathcal{J}}=\{pP\mid p\in P\}\bigcup\{\emptyset\}
.

In this section we will only consider group embeddable right LCM semigroups
P
inside
G
.
If the right LCM is unique for every pair of elements
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
, then the pair
(G,P)
is called
quasi-lattice ordered
, a setup that was introduced by Nica
[
46
]
.
Although Nica’s definition is implemented across the group, Exel
[
19
]
noted that it can be relaxed to be relevant only for the semigroup, in which case the pair
(G,P)
is called
weakly quasi-lattice ordered
.
Further examples of right LCM semigroups include the Artin monoids
[
6
]
, the Baumslag-Solitar monoids
B(m,n)^{+}

[
24
,
42
,
54
]
, and the semigroup
R\rtimes R^{\times}
of affine transformations of an integral domain
R
that satisfies the GCD condition
[
41
,
47
]
.

Fowler’s original work related to product systems over quasi-lattices
[
20
]
.
Product systems over right LCM semigroups were introduced and studied by Kwaśniewski and Larsen
[
36
,
37
]
, extending the construction of Fowler, and they have been investigated further in
[
13
,
26
]
.
The interest lies in that they retain several of the structural properties from the single C*-correspondence case.
With the addition of one further property, the generated C*-algebras admit a Wick ordering.
A product system
X
over
P
in the sense of Fowler gives rise to
*
-homomorphisms

i_{p}^{pq}\colon{\mathcal{L}}(X_{p})\to{\mathcal{L}}(X_{pq})\text{ where }i_{p}^{pq}(S):=u_{p,q}(S\otimes{\operatorname{id}}_{X_{q}})u_{p,q}^{*}\text{ for all }S\in{\mathcal{L}}(X_{p}).

We say that
X
is
compactly aligned
if
i_{p}^{w}(k_{p})i_{q}^{w}(k_{q})\in{\mathcal{K}}(X_{w})
for all
k_{p}\in{\mathcal{K}}(X_{p})
and
k_{q}\in{\mathcal{K}}(X_{q})
, whenever
pP\cap qP=wP
.
It was established in the discussion following
[
13
, Definition 2.9]
that this definition is independent of the choice of
w
.
For a representation
t
of a product system
X
we use the notation

t^{(p)}\colon{\mathcal{K}}(X_{p})\to\mathrm{C}^{*}(t);\theta^{X_{p}}_{\xi_{p},\eta_{p}}\mapsto t_{p}(\xi_{p})t_{p}(\eta_{p})^{*},

for the induced
*
-representation of the compact operators
{\mathcal{K}}(X_{p})
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
P
be a unital right LCM semigroup that is a subsemigroup of a discrete group
G
and let
X
be a product system over
P
in the sense of Fowler.
Then
X
is compactly aligned if and only if for every
p,q\in P
with
pP\cap qP=wP
we have

\lambda^{(p)}({\mathcal{K}}(X_{p}))\lambda^{(q)}({\mathcal{K}}(X_{q}))\subseteq\lambda^{(w)}({\mathcal{K}}(X_{w})).

Proof.
.

First suppose that
X
is compactly aligned, and let
p,q\in P
with
pP\cap qP=wP
.
Let
k_{p}\in{\mathcal{K}}(X_{p})
and
k_{q}\in{\mathcal{K}}(X_{q})
.
By compact alignment we have
i_{p}^{w}(k_{p})i_{q}^{w}(k_{q})\in{\mathcal{K}}(X_{w})
.
Hence for
r\in wP
we get

\displaystyle\lambda^{(p)}(k_{p})\lambda^{(q)}(k_{q})\xi_{r}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{(p)}(k_{p})(i_{q}^{r}(k_{q})\xi_{r})=i_{p}^{r}(k_{p})(i_{q}^{r}(k_{q})\xi_{r})

\displaystyle=i_{w}^{r}(i_{p}^{w}(k_{p}))i_{w}^{r}(i_{q}^{w}(k_{q}))\xi_{r}=i_{w}^{r}(i_{p}^{w}(k_{p})i_{q}^{w}(k_{q}))\xi_{r}=\lambda^{(w)}(i_{p}^{w}(k_{p})i_{q}^{w}(k_{q}))\xi_{r}.

On the other hand for
r\not\in wP
, we have
\lambda^{(w)}(i_{p}^{w}(k_{p})i_{q}^{w}(k_{q}))\xi_{r}=0
.
If
r\notin qP
then
\lambda^{(q)}(k_{q})\xi_{r}=0
.
If
r\in qP
then
\lambda^{(q)}(k_{q})\xi_{r}\in X_{r}
, but then it has to be that
r\notin pP
, and so
\lambda^{(p)}(k_{p})(\lambda^{(q)}(k_{q})\xi_{r})=0
.
In all cases, for
r\notin wP
we have

\lambda^{(p)}(k_{p})\lambda^{(q)}(k_{q})\xi_{r}=0=\lambda^{(w)}(i_{p}^{w}(k_{p})i_{q}^{w}(k_{q}))\xi_{r}.

Therefore, we conclude that

\lambda^{(p)}(k_{p})\lambda^{(q)}(k_{q})=\lambda^{(w)}(i_{p}^{w}(k_{p})i_{q}^{w}(k_{q}))\in\lambda^{(w)}({\mathcal{K}}(X_{w})).

For the converse, let
p,q\in P
such that
pP\cap qP=wP
for some
w\in P
, and let
k_{p}\in{\mathcal{K}}(X_{p})
and
k_{q}\in{\mathcal{K}}(X_{q})
.
Since

\lambda^{(p)}(k_{p})\lambda^{(q)}(k_{q})\in\lambda^{(w)}({\mathcal{K}}(X_{w})),

there exists a
k_{w}\in{\mathcal{K}}(X_{w})
such that
\lambda^{(p)}(k_{p})\lambda^{(q)}(k_{q})=\lambda^{(w)}(k_{w})
.
For every
\xi_{w}\in X_{w}
we get

i_{p}^{w}(k_{p})i_{q}^{w}(k_{q})\xi_{w}=\lambda^{(p)}(k_{p})\lambda^{(q)}(k_{q})\xi_{w}=\lambda^{(w)}(k_{w})\xi_{w}=k_{w}\xi_{w}.

Therefore we have
i_{p}^{w}(k_{p})i_{q}^{w}(k_{q})=k_{w}\in{\mathcal{K}}(X_{w})
, and the proof is complete.
∎

Let
P
be a unital right LCM semigroup that is a subsemigroup of a discrete group
G
and let
X
be a compactly aligned product system over
P
in the sense of Fowler. Let
t
be a representation of
X
.
We say that
t
is
Nica covariant
if and only if

t^{(p)}(k_{p})t^{(q)}(k_{q})=\begin{cases}t^{(w)}\left(i_{p}^{w}(k_{p})i_{q}^{w}(k_{q})\right)&\text{if }pP\cap qP=wP,\\
0&\text{if }pP\cap qP=\emptyset,\end{cases}

for all
k_{p}\in{\mathcal{K}}(X_{p})
and
k_{q}\in{\mathcal{K}}(X_{q})
.
Because of linearity and continuity of
t^{(p)},t^{(q)}
and
t^{(w)}
, we have that
t
is Nica covariant if and only if

t^{(p)}\left(\theta^{X_{p}}_{\xi_{p},\eta_{p}}\right)t^{(q)}\left(\theta^{X_{q}}_{\xi_{q},\eta_{q}}\right)=\begin{cases}t^{(w)}\left(i_{p}^{w}\left(\theta^{X_{p}}_{\xi_{p},\eta_{p}}\right)i_{q}^{w}\left(\theta^{X_{q}}_{\xi_{q},\eta_{q}}\right)\right)&\text{if }pP\cap qP=wP,\\
0&\text{if }pP\cap qP=\emptyset,\end{cases}

for all
\xi_{p},\eta_{p}\in X_{p}
and
\xi_{q},\eta_{q}\in X_{q}
.
We write
{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{T}}(X)
for the universal C*-algebra with respect to the Nica covariant representations of
X
.

In
[
13
, Proposition 2.4]
it is shown that, if
w\in P
and
r\in P^{*}
, then
i_{w}^{wr}(k_{w})\in{\mathcal{K}}(X_{wr})
and
t^{(wr)}(i_{w}^{wr}(k_{w}))=t^{(w)}(k_{w})\text{ for all }k_{w}\in{\mathcal{K}}(X_{w})
, when
t
is Nica covariant.
Moreover, in the discussion following
[
13
, Definition 2.9]
it is shown that

(3.2)

t_{p}(X_{p})^{*}t_{q}(X_{q})=(0)\text{ for }p,q\in P\text{ such that }pP\cap qP=\emptyset,

and that

(3.3)

t_{p}(X_{p})^{*}t_{q}(X_{q})\subseteq[t_{r}(X_{r})t_{s}(X_{s})^{*}]\text{ for }wP=pP\cap qP\text{ and }r=p^{-1}w,s=q^{-1}w.

Consequently, Nica covariance does not depend on the choice of a right least common multiple and also

\mathrm{C}^{*}(t)
admits a Wick ordering in the sense that

\mathrm{C}^{*}(t)=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{t_{p}(X_{p})t_{q}(X_{q})^{*}\mid p,q\in P\}.

Moreover we have
\mathbf{K}_{pP,t_{\ast}}=t^{(p)}({\mathcal{K}}(X_{p}))
.
Therefore, if
{\mathcal{F}}=\{p_{1}P,\dots,p_{n}P\}
is a finite
\cap
-closed subset of
{\mathcal{J}}
, then we have

\mathbf{B}_{{\mathcal{F}},t_{\ast}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}t^{(p_{i})}({\mathcal{K}}(X_{p_{i}})),

irrespectively of the choice of the elements
p_{1},\dots,p_{n}
.
It is implicit in
[
13
]
, and proven in a more general context in
[
26
, Theorem 6.4]
, that the cores
\mathbf{K}_{p_{i}P,\lambda_{\ast}}
for distinct
p_{i}P
’s are linearly independent in
\mathbf{B}_{{\mathcal{F}},\lambda_{\ast}}
.

It is shown in
[
8
, Proposition 3.5]
that
{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{T}}(X)
admits a coaction since Nica covariance is a graded relation.
Let
{\mathcal{N}}X
be the induced Fell bundle in
{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{T}}(X)
.
By
[
13
, Proposition 4.3]
we have

{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{T}}(X)\simeq\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{N}}X)\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)\simeq\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}({\mathcal{N}}X),

by canonical
*
-isomorphisms.
In particular the second
*
-isomorphism induces a Fell bundle isomorphism between
{\mathcal{N}}X
and
{\mathcal{F}}{\mathcal{C}}X
.
This shows that a representation of
X
is Fock covariant if and only if it is Nica covariant.
Below we will give an alternative proof that squares with our characterisation of Fock covariance.
We will need the following proposition.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
P
be a unital right LCM semigroup that is a subsemigroup of a discrete group
G
and let
X
be a compactly aligned product system over
P
in the sense of Fowler.
If
t
is a Nica covariant representation of
X
, then

\left(t_{p_{1}}(X_{p_{1}})^{*}\right)^{\varepsilon}t_{q_{1}}(X_{q_{1}})\cdots t_{p_{n}}(X_{p_{n}})^{*}t_{q_{n}}(X_{q_{n}})^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}=(0)

for
p_{1},q_{1},\dots,p_{n},q_{n}\in P
and
\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}
such that
q_{n}^{-\varepsilon^{\prime}}p_{n}\dots q_{1}^{-1}p_{1}^{\varepsilon}P=\emptyset
. In particular, we have

\mathbf{K}_{\emptyset,t_{\ast}}=(0)
.

Proof.
.

We first consider the case
\varepsilon=\varepsilon^{\prime}=1
.
In this case we have
q_{n}^{-1}p_{n}\dots q_{1}^{-1}p_{1}P=\emptyset
and we wish to show that

t_{p_{1}}(X_{p_{1}})^{*}t_{q_{1}}(X_{q_{1}})\cdots t_{p_{n}}(X_{p_{n}})^{*}t_{q_{n}}(X_{q_{n}})=(0).

We proceed in steps.
If
p_{n}P\cap q_{n}P=\emptyset
, then Nica covariance of
t
implies that

t_{p_{n}}(X_{p_{n}})^{*}t_{q_{n}}(X_{q_{n}})=(0)

by equation (
3.2
), which gives the desired result.
If
p_{n}P\cap q_{n}P\not=\emptyset
, then choose
w_{1}\in P
with
p_{n}P\cap q_{n}P=w_{1}P
, and (
3.3
) implies that

t_{p_{n}}(X_{p_{n}})^{*}t_{q_{n}}(X_{q_{n}})\subseteq[t_{r_{1}}(X_{r_{1}})t_{s_{1}}(X_{s_{1}})^{*}]\quad\text{for}\quad r_{1}=p_{n}^{-1}w_{1},s_{1}=q_{n}^{-1}w_{1}.

Hence we obtain the inclusion

\displaystyle t_{p_{1}}(X_{p_{1}})^{*}t_{q_{1}}(X_{q_{1}})\cdots t_{p_{n}}(X_{p_{n}})^{*}t_{q_{n}}(X_{q_{n}})\subseteq

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\subseteq[t_{p_{1}}(X_{p_{1}})^{*}t_{q_{1}}(X_{q_{1}})\cdots t_{p_{n-1}}(X_{p_{n-1}})^{*}t_{q_{n-1}}(X_{q_{n-1}})t_{r_{1}}(X_{r_{1}})t_{s_{1}}(X_{s_{1}})^{*}]

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\subseteq[t_{p_{1}}(X_{p_{1}})^{*}t_{q_{1}}(X_{q_{1}})\cdots t_{p_{n-1}}(X_{p_{n-1}})^{*}t_{q_{n-1}r_{1}}(X_{q_{n-1}r_{1}})t_{s_{1}}(X_{s_{1}})^{*}].

If
p_{n-1}P\cap q_{n-1}r_{1}P=\emptyset
, then Nica covariance gives the desired result as

t_{p_{n-1}}(X_{p_{n-1}})^{*}t_{q_{n-1}r_{1}}(X_{q_{n-1}r_{1}})=(0).

If
p_{n-1}P\cap q_{n-1}r_{1}P\not=\emptyset
, then choose
w_{2}\in P
such that
p_{n-1}P\cap q_{n-1}r_{1}P=w_{2}P
, and (
3.3
) implies that

t_{p_{n-1}}(X_{p_{n-1}})^{*}t_{q_{n-1}r_{1}}(X_{q_{n-1}r_{1}})\subseteq[t_{r_{2}}(X_{r_{2}})t_{s_{2}}(X_{s_{2}})^{*}]\quad\text{for}\quad r_{2}=p_{n-1}^{-1}w_{2},s_{2}=r_{1}^{-1}q_{n-1}^{-1}w_{2}.

Hence we obtain the inclusion

\displaystyle t_{p_{1}}(X_{p_{1}})^{*}t_{q_{1}}(X_{q_{1}})\cdots t_{p_{n}}(X_{p_{n}})^{*}t_{q_{n}}(X_{q_{n}})\subseteq

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\subseteq[t_{p_{1}}(X_{p_{1}})^{*}t_{q_{1}}(X_{q_{1}})\cdots t_{p_{n-1}}(X_{p_{n-1}})^{*}t_{q_{n-1}r_{1}}(X_{q_{n-1}r_{1}})t_{s_{1}}(X_{s_{1}})^{*}]

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\subseteq[t_{p_{1}}(X_{p_{1}})^{*}t_{q_{1}}(X_{q_{1}})\cdots t_{q_{n-2}}(X_{q_{n-2}})t_{r_{2}}(X_{r_{2}})t_{s_{2}}(X_{s_{2}})^{*}t_{s_{1}}(X_{s_{1}})^{*}]

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\subseteq[t_{p_{1}}(X_{p_{1}})^{*}t_{q_{1}}(X_{q_{1}})\cdots t_{p_{n-2}}(X_{p_{n-2}})^{*}t_{q_{n-2}r_{2}}(X_{q_{n-2}r_{2}})t_{s_{1}s_{2}}(X_{s_{1}s_{2}})^{*}].

Continuing inductively we either get a zero space at a step, or we have obtained elements
w_{k},r_{k},s_{k}\in P
, with
k=2,\dots,n-1
, such that

\displaystyle w_{k}P

\displaystyle=p_{n-(k-1)}P\cap q_{n-(k-1)}r_{k-1}P,\,r_{k}=p_{n-(k-1)}^{-1}w_{k},\,s_{k}=r_{k-1}^{-1}q_{n-(k-1)}^{-1}w_{k},

and the inclusion

t_{p_{1}}(X_{p_{1}})^{*}t_{q_{1}}(X_{q_{1}})\cdots t_{p_{n}}(X_{p_{n}})^{*}t_{q_{n}}(X_{q_{n}})\subseteq[t_{p_{1}}(X_{p_{1}})^{*}t_{q_{1}r_{n-1}}(X_{q_{1}r_{n-1}})t_{s_{1}\cdots s_{n-1}}(X_{s_{1}\cdots s_{n-1}})^{*}].

In the latter case we claim that

p_{1}P\cap q_{1}r_{n-1}P=\emptyset,

and so Nica covariance will imply the required identity.
In order to reach a contradiction suppose that
p_{1}P\cap q_{1}r_{n-1}P\neq\emptyset
, so there exists a
q\in P
such that
p:=r_{n-1}^{-1}q_{1}^{-1}p_{1}q\in P
.
By the construction of the
r_{k}
and the
s_{k}
for
k=1,2,\dots,n-1
we have

\displaystyle s_{1}

\displaystyle=q_{n}^{-1}w_{1}=q_{n}^{-1}p_{n}r_{1}

\displaystyle s_{2}

\displaystyle=r_{1}^{-1}q_{n-1}^{-1}w_{2}=r_{1}^{-1}q_{n-1}^{-1}p_{n-1}r_{2}

\displaystyle s_{k}

\displaystyle=r_{k-1}^{-1}q_{n-(k-1)}^{-1}w_{k}=r_{k-1}^{-1}q_{n-(k-1)}^{-1}p_{n-(k-1)}r_{k}

\displaystyle s_{n-1}

\displaystyle=r_{n-2}^{-1}q_{2}^{-1}w_{n-1}=r_{n-2}^{-1}q_{2}^{-1}p_{2}r_{n-1}.

Therefore for every
k=1,2\dots,n-1
we have

\displaystyle s_{1}s_{2}\cdots s_{k}\cdots s_{n-1}p

\displaystyle=q_{n}^{-1}p_{n}q_{n-1}^{-1}p_{n-1}\cdots q_{n-(k-1)}^{-1}p_{n-(k-1)}\cdot\left(r_{k}s_{k+1}\cdots s_{n-1}p\right)

\displaystyle\in q_{n}^{-1}\cdot p_{n}\cdots q_{n-(k-1)}^{-1}\cdot p_{n-(k-1)}\cdot P.

Because of the choice of
p=r_{n-1}^{-1}q_{1}^{-1}p_{1}q

\displaystyle s_{1}\cdots s_{n-2}s_{n-1}p

\varepsilon=1

\varepsilon^{\prime}=0

We now pass to the connection between Fock covariance and Nica covariance through our characterisation.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
P
be a unital right LCM semigroup that is a subsemigroup of a discrete group
G
and let
X
be a compactly aligned product system over
P
in the sense of Fowler.
An equivariant injective representation of
X
is Fock covariant if and only if it is Nica covariant.

Proof.
.

Fix the representation
\hat{t}
of
X
such that
{\mathcal{T}}(X)=\mathrm{C}^{*}(\hat{t})
.
First suppose that
t
is an equivariant Fock covariant injective representation of
X
, so that it satisfies conditions (i) and (ii) of Theorem
3.2
.
Fix
p,q\in P
.
We will show that

t_{p}(\xi_{p})t_{p}(\eta_{p})^{*}t_{q}(\xi_{q})t_{q}(\eta_{q})^{*}=\begin{cases}t^{(w)}\left(i_{p}^{w}\left(\theta^{X_{p}}_{\xi_{p},\eta_{p}}\right)i_{q}^{w}\left(\theta^{X_{q}}_{\xi_{q},\eta_{q}}\right)\right)&\text{if }pP\cap qP=wP,\\
0&\text{if }pP\cap qP=\emptyset,\end{cases}

for all
\xi_{p},\eta_{p}\in X_{p}
and
\xi_{q},\eta_{q}\in X_{q}
.
Towards this end, set

\mathbf{x}:=qq^{-1}pp^{-1}P=pP\cap qP.

If
\mathbf{x}=\emptyset
, then condition (i) of Theorem
3.2
implies that

t_{p}(\xi_{p})t_{p}(\eta_{p})^{*}t_{q}(\xi_{q})t_{q}(\eta_{q})^{*}\in\mathbf{K}_{\emptyset,t_{\ast}}=(0),

as required.
On the other hand, if
\mathbf{x}=wP
for some
w\in P
set

b_{{\mathbf{x}}}:=\hat{t}_{p}(\xi_{p})\hat{t}_{p}(\eta_{p})^{*}\hat{t}_{q}(\xi_{q})\hat{t}_{q}(\eta_{q})^{*}\quad\text{and}\quad k_{w}:=i_{p}^{w}\left(\theta^{X_{p}}_{\xi_{p},\eta_{p}}\right)i_{q}^{w}\left(\theta^{X_{q}}_{\xi_{q},\eta_{q}}\right)\in{\mathcal{K}}(X_{w}).

We have to show that
t_{\ast}(b_{{\mathbf{x}}})=t^{(w)}\left(k_{w}\right)
.
We will use the equality
t^{(w)}(k_{w})=t_{\ast}(\hat{t}^{(w)}(k_{w}))
.
Since
{\mathbf{x}}=qq^{-1}pp^{-1}P
we have
b_{{\mathbf{x}}}\in\mathbf{K}_{{\mathbf{x}},\hat{t}_{\ast}}
.
Moreover, since
{\mathbf{x}}=wP=ww^{-1}P
we have

\hat{t}^{(w)}(k_{w})\in[\hat{t}_{w}(X_{w})\hat{t}_{w}(X_{w})^{*}]\subseteq\mathbf{K}_{\mathbf{x},\hat{t}_{\ast}},

and therefore

t_{\ast}(b_{{\mathbf{x}}})-t_{\ast}(\hat{t}^{(w)}(k_{w}))\in\mathbf{K}_{{\mathbf{x}},t_{\ast}}.

Fix
r\in\mathbf{x}
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{\ast}(b_{{\mathbf{x}}})\xi_{r}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{(p)}\left(\theta^{X_{p}}_{\xi_{p},\eta_{p}}\right)\lambda^{(q)}\left(\theta^{X_{q}}_{\xi_{q},\eta_{q}}\right)\xi_{r}=i_{p}^{r}\left(\theta^{X_{p}}_{\xi_{p},\eta_{p}}\right)i_{q}^{r}\left(\theta^{X_{q}}_{\xi_{q},\eta_{q}}\right)\xi_{r}=i_{w}^{r}\left(k_{w}\right)\xi_{r},

and thus by Proposition
2.5
we have

t_{\ast}(b_{{\mathbf{x}}})t_{r}(\xi_{r})=t_{r}(\lambda_{\ast}(b_{{\mathbf{x}}})\xi_{r})=t_{r}\left(i_{w}^{r}\left(k_{w}\right)\xi_{r}\right).

On the other hand, by Proposition
2.5
we have

\displaystyle t^{(w)}(k_{w})t_{r}(\xi_{r})=t_{\ast}(\hat{t}^{(w)}(k_{w}))t_{r}(\xi_{r})=t_{r}(\lambda_{\ast}(\hat{t}^{(w)}(k_{w}))\xi_{r})=t_{r}(\lambda^{(w)}(k_{w})\xi_{r})=t_{r}(i_{w}^{r}(k_{w})\xi_{r}).

Therefore we conclude that

\left(t_{\ast}(b_{{\mathbf{x}}})-t_{\ast}(\hat{t}^{(w)}(k_{w}))\right)t_{r}(\xi_{r})=\left(t_{\ast}(b_{{\mathbf{x}}})-t^{(w)}(k_{w})\right)t_{r}(\xi_{r})=0\text{ for all }r\in{\mathbf{x}}.

Condition (ii) for
t
then implies that
t_{\ast}(b_{{\mathbf{x}}})=t_{\ast}(\hat{t}^{(w)}(k_{w}))=t^{(w)}(k_{w})
, as required.

For the converse, suppose that
t
is an equivariant Nica covariant injective representation of

, and we will show that
t
satisfies conditions (i) and (ii) of Theorem
3.2
.
The fact that
t
satisfies condition (i) is already verified in Proposition
3.5
.
We will show that
t
satisfies condition (ii).
In order to fix notation let the commutative diagrams of canonical
*
-epimorphisms

\textstyle{{\mathcal{T}}(X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\lambda_{\ast}}
\scriptstyle{q_{\operatorname{c}}^{\rm N}}
\textstyle{{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)}

, and let

.

Likewise we have

\lambda_{\ast}(b)=\dot{\lambda}\circ q_{\operatorname{c}}^{\rm{N}}(b)=\dot{\lambda}(\tilde{t}^{(p)}(k_{p}))=\lambda^{(p)}(k_{p}).

For condition (ii), first let
{\mathcal{F}}=\{\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{n}\}
be a
\cap

{\mathcal{J}}
where
\mathbf{x}_{i}=p_{i}P
and
b_{\mathbf{x}_{i}}\in\mathbf{K}_{\mathbf{x}_{i},\hat{t}_{\ast}}

.

{\mathbf{x}}_{i}\not={\mathbf{x}}_{j}
for

\mathbf{K}_{p_{i}P,\lambda_{\ast}}

.

\sum_{i:r\in\mathbf{x}_{i}}t_{\ast}(b_{\mathbf{x}_{i}})t_{r}(X_{r})=(0)\text{ for all }r\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{x}_{i}.

From the comments above, for each

.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
P
be a unital right LCM semigroup that is a subsemigroup of a discrete group
G
and let

be a compactly aligned product system over

in the sense of Fowler. Then
{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)
and

Proof.
.

This is immediate by Proposition

since the universal Fock covariant and the universal Nica covariant representations are both equivariant and injective.

3.3.
The product system of a semigroup

One class of product systems of particular interest arises from the semigroup representations of
P
itself.
There is a well-known left regular model in this case considered by Li
[
41
]
, giving rise to a Fock covariant Fell bundle.
The corresponding graded relations were identified by Laca and Sehnem in
[
38
, Section 3]
, although not explicitly stated like this, see also
[
25
, Remark 3.15]
.
In this subsection we will show how the Fock covariance description of
[
38
, Section 3]
coincides with our general description from Theorem
3.2
in two ways.
One is achieved categorically though equation (
3.6
), and the other is achieved from first principles in Proposition
3.9
, thus providing an independent validation of our results.

We consider
P
to be a unital subsemigroup of a discrete group
G
.
Following the notation of
[
38
]
, we write
\alpha=(p_{1},q_{1},\dots,p_{n},q_{n})
for the words of even length where
p_{1},q_{1},\dots,p_{n},q_{n}\in P
.
A word
\alpha=(p_{1},q_{1},\dots,p_{n},q_{n})
is called
neutral
if
p_{1}^{-1}q_{1}\cdots p_{n}^{-1}q_{n}=e
.
For a word
\alpha=(p_{1},q_{1},\dots,p_{n},q_{n})
we write

K(\alpha):=q_{n}^{-1}p_{n}\dots q_{1}^{-1}p_{1}P

for the induced constructible ideal in
{\mathcal{J}}
.
For a map
w\colon P\to{\mathcal{B}}(H)
we write

\dot{w}_{\alpha}:=w_{p_{1}}^{*}w_{q_{1}}\cdots w_{p_{n}}^{*}w_{q_{n}}\in{\mathcal{B}}(H).

It follows that

\dot{w}_{\alpha}\dot{w}_{\beta}=\dot{w}_{\alpha\beta},\text{ for all words $\alpha,\beta$,}

where
\alpha\beta
denotes the concatenation of the words
\alpha
and
\beta
.

A map
w\colon P\to{\mathcal{B}}(H)
is called a
representation of
P
if it is a semigroup homomorphism.
A representation
w
of
P
will be called
equivariant
if there exists a
*
-homomorphism
\delta
of
\mathrm{C}^{*}(w)
such that

\delta\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}(w)\to\mathrm{C}^{*}(w)\otimes\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}(G);w_{p}\mapsto w_{p}\otimes u_{p}.

It follows that
\delta
is injective with a left inverse given by the map
{\operatorname{id}}\otimes\chi
.
Moreover, it satisfies the coaction identity and hence
\mathrm{C}^{*}(w)
admits a coaction by
G
.
For simplicity, we will say that
w
admits a coaction by
G
if such a
\delta
exists.

Remark 3.8
.

Let
w\colon P\to{\mathcal{B}}(H)
be an isometric representation of
P
(i.e., every
w_{p}
is an isometry) and let
\alpha=(p_{1},q_{1},\dots,p_{n},q_{n})
where
K(\alpha)\not=\emptyset
.
Since every
w_{p}
is an isometry we have

w_{p}^{*}w_{pq}=w_{p}^{*}w_{p}w_{q}=w_{q},\text{ for all }p,q\in P.

Therefore a variant of the proof of Proposition
2.3
yields

\dot{w}_{\alpha}w_{r}=w_{p_{1}^{-1}q_{1}\cdots p_{n}^{-1}q_{n}r}\text{ for all }r\in K(\alpha).

In particular, if
\alpha
is neutral we obtain

\dot{w}_{\alpha}w_{r}=w_{r}\text{ for all }r\in K(\alpha).

In
[
38
, Proposition 3.2]
it is shown that, if a map
w\colon P\to{\mathcal{B}}(H)
satisfies

(T1)

w_{e}=1
,

(T2)

\dot{w}_{\alpha}=0
if
K(\alpha)=\emptyset
for a neutral word
\alpha
, and

(T3)

\dot{w}_{\alpha}=\dot{w}_{\beta}
if
K(\alpha)=K(\beta)
for neutral words
\alpha
and
\beta
,

then
w
is an isometric representation of
P
, and the operators
\{\dot{w}_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha:\text{neutral}}
are commuting projections.
Let
{\mathcal{T}}(P)
be the universal C*-algebra with respect to the isometric representations of
P
.
By the universal property, the C*-algebra
{\mathcal{T}}(P)
admits a coaction by
G
and hence a topological C*-grading.

Let
{\mathcal{T}}_{u}(P)
be the universal C*-algebra with respect to the maps
w\colon P\to{\mathcal{B}}(H)
that satisfy conditions (T1)–(T3) and the additional condition:

(T4)

\prod_{\beta\in F}(\dot{w}_{\alpha}-\dot{w}_{\beta})=0
whenever
\alpha
is a neutral word,
F
is a finite set of neutral words and
K(\alpha)=\bigcup_{\beta\in F}K(\beta)
.

Since conditions (T1)–(T4) are graded with respect to the grading of
{\mathcal{T}}(P)
, the C*-algebra
{\mathcal{T}}_{u}(P)
is a quotient of
{\mathcal{T}}(P)
by an induced ideal, and therefore
{\mathcal{T}}_{u}(P)
admits a coaction by
G
. By using similar arguments as in Proposition
2.7
it can be proved that we have

\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}\left(\Big\{[{\mathcal{T}}(P)]_{g}\Big\}_{g\in G}\right)\simeq{\mathcal{T}}(P),

and hence combining with Proposition
2.2
we obtain

\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}\left(\Big\{[{\mathcal{T}}_{u}(P)]_{g}\Big\}_{g\in G}\right)\simeq{\mathcal{T}}_{u}(P).

In
[
38
, Corollary 3.19, Corollary 3.20]
it is shown that there is a
*
-epimorphism
{\mathcal{T}}_{u}(P)\to\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)
that is equivariant and injective on the fixed point algebra.
Hence
{\mathcal{T}}_{u}(P)
coincides with the universal C*-algebra of the Fell bundle induced in
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)
; see also
[
25
, Remark 3.15]
.

Fix
\{v_{p}\}_{p\in P}
such that
{\mathcal{T}}(P)=\mathrm{C}^{*}(v)
.
We can then define the concrete product system
X_{P}
in
{\mathcal{T}}(P)
by

X_{P,p}:=\mathbb{C}v_{p}\text{ for all }p\in P.

We say that a representation
t
of
X_{P}
is
unital
if it satisfies
t_{e}(v_{e})=1
. Note that a unital representation is automatically injective.
If
t\colon X_{P}\to{\mathcal{B}}(H)
is a non-zero representation, then
t_{e}(v_{e})
is a projection that commutes with
\mathrm{C}^{*}(t)
and hence
K:=t_{e}(v_{e})H
is reducing for
\mathrm{C}^{*}(t)
. Therefore by compressing on
K
we can consider
t
to be a unital representation of
X_{P}
.
Note that the Fock space of
X_{P}
is unitarily equivalent to
\ell^{2}(P)
by the unitary

(3.4)

W_{P}\colon{\mathcal{F}}X_{P}\to\ell^{2}(P);v_{p}\mapsto\delta_{p}.

It follows that

(3.5)

{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X_{P})\simeq\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)

by the canonical
*
-isomorphism induced by
\operatorname{ad}_{W_{P}}
, since
W_{P}\lambda_{p}(v_{p})W_{P}^{*}=V_{p}
for all
p\in P
.
Since this
*
-isomorphism is canonical we have that the Fell bundles induced in
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X_{P})
and
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)
are isomorphic, and therefore we also have

(3.6)

{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X_{P})\simeq{\mathcal{T}}_{u}(P).

More generally, the association

t=\{t_{p}\}_{p\in P}\mapsto w_{t}=\{t_{p}(v_{p})\}_{p\in P}

induces a bijection between the unital (equivariant) representations of
X_{P}
and the unital (resp. equivariant) isometric representations of
P
.
Hence we have

(3.7)

{\mathcal{T}}(X_{P})\simeq{\mathcal{T}}(P)

by a canonical
*
-isomorphism.
Note that Laca and Sehnem
[
38
, Definition 3.6]
coin
{\mathcal{T}}_{u}(P)
as the Toeplitz algebra of
P
; however we will not use this terminology, as
{\mathcal{T}}_{u}(P)
is not
{\mathcal{T}}(X_{P})
.
Below we give an alternative proof of (
3.6
) that squares with our characterisation of Fock covariance.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
P
be a unital subsemigroup of a discrete group
G
and let
X_{P}
be the induced product system in
{\mathcal{T}}(P)
.
Then the association

t=\{t_{p}\}_{p\in P}\mapsto w_{t}=\{t_{p}(v_{p})\}_{p\in P}

defines a bijection between the unital equivariant Fock covariant representations of
X_{P}
and the equivariant representations of
P
that satisfy conditions (T1)–(T4).

Proof.
.

To fix notation, let
{\mathcal{T}}(P)=\mathrm{C}^{*}(v)
,
{\mathcal{T}}(X_{P})=\mathrm{C}^{*}(\hat{t})
and
{\mathcal{T}}_{u}(P)=\mathrm{C}^{*}(\tilde{u})
.
It is readily verified that the association
t\mapsto w_{t}
is a bijection between the unital equivariant representations of
X_{P}
and the unital equivariant isometric representations of
P
.
When it is clear from the context we will simply write
w
instead of
w_{t}
.
With this notation we have

t_{\ast}\left(\hat{t}_{p_{1}}(v_{p_{1}})^{*}\hat{t}_{q_{1}}(v_{q_{1}})\cdots\hat{t}_{p_{n}}(v_{p_{n}})^{*}\hat{t}_{q_{n}}(v_{q_{n}})\right)=\dot{w}_{\alpha}

for every word
\alpha=(p_{1},q_{1},\dots,p_{n},q_{n})
where
p_{1},q_{1},\dots,p_{n},q_{n}\in P
.

First suppose that
t
is a unital equivariant Fock covariant representation of
X_{P}
, and let
w
be the associated unital equivariant representation of
P
.
Then
t
satisfies conditions (i) and (ii) of Theorem
3.2
.
For condition (T1), we have that
t_{e}(v_{e})
is the unit, and thus
w_{e}=t_{e}(v_{e})=1
.

For condition (T2), let
\alpha=(p_{1},q_{1},\dots,p_{n},q_{n})
be a neutral word such that
K(\alpha)=\emptyset
.
We then have

\dot{w}_{\alpha}=t_{\ast}\left(\hat{t}_{p_{1}}(v_{p_{1}})^{*}\hat{t}_{q_{1}}(v_{q_{1}})\cdots\hat{t}_{p_{n}}(v_{p_{n}})^{*}\hat{t}_{q_{n}}(v_{q_{n}})\right)\in\mathbf{K}_{\emptyset,t_{\ast}}=(0),

as required.

For condition (T3), let
\alpha=(p_{1},q_{1},\dots,p_{n},q_{n})
and
\beta=(r_{1},s_{1},\dots,r_{m},s_{m})
be neutral words such that
K(\alpha)=K(\beta)
.
For brevity, set
{\mathbf{x}}:=K(\alpha)=K(\beta)
and

b_{\alpha}:=\hat{t}_{p_{1}}(v_{p_{1}})^{*}\hat{t}_{q_{1}}(v_{q_{1}})\cdots\hat{t}_{p_{n}}(v_{p_{n}})^{*}\hat{t}_{q_{n}}(v_{q_{n}})\quad\text{and}\quad b_{\beta}:=\hat{t}_{r_{1}}(v_{r_{1}})^{*}\hat{t}_{s_{1}}(v_{s_{1}})\cdots\hat{t}_{r_{m}}(v_{r_{m}})^{*}\hat{t}_{s_{m}}(v_{s_{m}}).

Let
r\in\mathbf{x}
and
\xi_{r}=\mu v_{r}\in X_{P,r}
with
\mu\in\mathbb{C}
.
Then Proposition
2.5
, along with the equality
\dot{V}_{\alpha}=\dot{V}_{\beta}
, yields

\displaystyle t_{\ast}(b_{\alpha})t_{r}(\xi_{r})

\displaystyle=t_{r}(\lambda_{\ast}(b_{\alpha})\xi_{r})=\mu t_{r}(W_{P}^{*}\dot{V}_{\alpha}\delta_{r})=\mu t_{r}(W_{P}^{*}\dot{V}_{\beta}\delta_{r})=t_{r}(\lambda_{\ast}(b_{\beta})\xi_{r})=t_{\ast}(b_{\beta})t_{r}(\xi_{r}),

for the unitary
W_{P}
of (
3.4
).
Hence, we have

(t_{\ast}(b_{\alpha})-t_{\ast}(b_{\beta}))t_{r}(\xi_{r})=0\text{ for all }\xi_{r}\in X_{P,r},r\in{\mathbf{x}}.

Thus applying condition (ii) for
t
implies that
t_{\ast}(b_{\alpha})-t_{\ast}(b_{\beta})=0
, and therefore

\dot{w}_{\alpha}=t_{\ast}(b_{\alpha})=t_{\ast}(b_{\beta})=\dot{w}_{\beta},

as required.

For condition (T4), let
F
be a finite set of neutral words, and let
\alpha
be a neutral word such that
K(\alpha)=\bigcup_{\beta\in F}K(\beta)
.
Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be the
\cap
-closure of
\{K(\beta)\mid\beta\in F\}\cup\{K(\alpha)\}\cup\{\emptyset\}
. For each
\emptyset\not=D\subseteq F
we write
\beta_{D}
for the neutral word that arises by concatenating the words
\beta\in D
in some order.
As the induced constructible ideal does not depend on the order of the concatenation we choose (being on neutral words), we have

K(\beta_{D})=\bigcap_{\beta\in D}K(\beta),

and

{\mathcal{F}}=\{K(\beta_{D}):\emptyset\not=D\subseteq F\}\cup\{K(\alpha)\}\cup\{\emptyset\}.

In particular we have

\dot{w}_{\beta_{D}}=\prod_{\beta\in D}\dot{w}_{\beta}.

For each
\emptyset\not=D\subseteq F
, let
b_{D}\in\mathbf{K}_{K(\beta_{D}),\hat{t}_{\ast}}
and
b_{\alpha}\in\mathbf{K}_{K(\alpha),\hat{t}_{\ast}}
such that

t_{\ast}(b_{D})=\dot{w}_{\beta_{D}}\quad\text{and}\quad t_{\ast}(b_{\alpha})=\dot{w}_{\alpha}.

Then, for
r\in K(\alpha)
and
\mu v_{r}\in X_{P,r}
, we have

\displaystyle\left(t_{\ast}(b_{\alpha})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\emptyset\neq D\subseteq F:\\
r\in K(\beta_{D})\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|D|}t_{\ast}(b_{\beta_{D}})\right)t_{r}(\mu v_{r})

\displaystyle=\left(\dot{w}_{\alpha}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\emptyset\neq D\subseteq F:\\
r\in K(\beta_{D})\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|D|}\dot{w}_{\beta_{D}}\right)\mu w_{r}

\displaystyle=\left(1+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\emptyset\neq D\subseteq F:\\
r\in\bigcap\limits_{\beta\in D}K(\beta)\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|D|}\right)\mu w_{r}

\displaystyle=\left(1+\sum_{\emptyset\neq D\subseteq\{\beta\in F\mid r\in K(\beta)\}}(-1)^{|D|}\right)\mu w_{r}=0,

where we used the equalities

\dot{w}_{\alpha}w_{r}=w_{r}\quad\text{and}\quad\dot{w}_{\beta_{D}}w_{r}=w_{r}\text{ when }r\in K(\beta_{D}).

Note also that in the third equality we used that we have

\{\emptyset\neq D\subseteq F\mid r\in\bigcap\limits_{\beta\in D}K(\beta)\}={\mathcal{P}}(\{\beta\in F\mid r\in K(\beta)\})\setminus\{\emptyset\},

and that these are non-empty sets since
r\in K(\alpha)=\bigcup_{\beta\in F}K(\beta)
.
Condition (ii) for
t
then implies that

t_{\ast}(b_{\alpha})+\sum_{\emptyset\neq D\subseteq F}(-1)^{|D|}t_{\ast}(b_{\beta_{D}})=0,

and therefore we have

\displaystyle\prod_{\beta\in F}(\dot{w}_{\alpha}-\dot{w}_{\beta})

\displaystyle=\dot{w}_{\alpha}+\sum_{\emptyset\neq D\subseteq F}(-1)^{|D|}\prod_{\beta\in D}\dot{w}_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\dot{w}_{\alpha}+\sum_{\emptyset\neq D\subseteq F}(-1)^{|D|}\dot{w}_{\beta_{D}}=t_{\ast}(b_{\alpha})+\sum_{\emptyset\neq D\subseteq F}(-1)^{|D|}t_{\ast}(b_{\beta_{D}})=0,

as required.

For the reverse implication, suppose that
w
is an equivariant representation of
P
that satisfies conditions (T1)–(T4) and let
t
be the unital equivariant representation of
X_{P}
associated with
w
.
We will show that
t
satisfies conditions (i) and (ii) of Theorem
3.2
.

Let
\mathbf{x}
be in
{\mathcal{J}}
and pick a neutral word
\alpha=(p_{1},q_{1},\dots,p_{n},q_{n})
such that
\mathbf{x}=K(\alpha)
.
Then condition (T3) implies that

t_{\ast}(\mathbf{K}_{\mathbf{x},\hat{t}_{\ast}})=\text{span}\{\dot{w}_{\alpha}\}.

In particular, combining (T2) with (T3) yields
t_{\ast}(\mathbf{K}_{\emptyset,\hat{t}_{\ast}})=(0)
, and thus
t
satisfies condition (i) of Theorem
3.2
.

For condition (ii), let
{\mathcal{F}}=\{{\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{n}}\}
be a finite
\cap
-closed subset of
{\mathcal{J}}
such that
\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{x}_{i}\neq\emptyset
, and let
b_{\mathbf{x}_{i}}\in\mathbf{K}_{\mathbf{x}_{i},\hat{t}_{\ast}}
for
i=1,\dots,n
.
Suppose that

\sum_{i:r\in\mathbf{x}_{i}}t_{\ast}(b_{\mathbf{x}_{i}})t_{r}(X_{P,r})=0\text{ for all }r\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{x}_{i},

and we will show that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{\ast}(b_{\mathbf{x}_{i}})=0
.
For notational convenience, set

b:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{\ast}(b_{\mathbf{x}_{i}}).

First notice that
t_{\ast}(\mathbf{K}_{\emptyset,\hat{t}_{\ast}})=(0)
as
t
satisfies condition (i), therefore without loss of generality we may assume that
{\mathbf{x}}_{i}\neq\emptyset
for all
i=1,\dots,n
.
Next let

F:=\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}\}

be a set of neutral words such that
K(\alpha_{i})=\mathbf{x}_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,n
.
Moreover, from the comments above we may pick
\mu_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
such that
t_{\ast}(b_{\mathbf{x}_{i}})=\mu_{i}\dot{w}_{\alpha_{i}}
for
i=1,\dots,n
.
As the projections
\dot{w}_{\alpha_{i}}
commute, by the Gel’fand-Naimark Theorem, and considering a unit decomposition (see also
[
38
, Lemma 3.13]
), there exists a subset
B
of
F
such that

\|b\|=\|Q_{B}b\|\quad\text{for}\quad Q_{B}:=\prod_{i:\alpha_{i}\in B}\dot{w}_{\alpha_{i}}\prod_{j:\alpha_{j}\not\in B}(1-\dot{w}_{\alpha_{j}}).

Since
b=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{i}\dot{w}_{\alpha_{i}}
, by the form of the projection
Q_{B}
we have

\|b\|=\|Q_{B}b\|=\|\sum_{i:\alpha_{i}\in B}\mu_{i}Q_{B}\|=|\sum_{i:\alpha_{i}\in B}\mu_{i}|.

We consider the following cases.

Case 1. Suppose that

\bigcap_{i:\alpha_{i}\in B}K(\alpha_{i})\subseteq\bigcup_{j:\alpha_{j}\not\in B}K(\alpha_{j}).

Then we have

\bigcap_{i:\alpha_{i}\in B}K(\alpha_{i})=\bigcup_{j:\alpha_{j}\not\in B}\left(K(\alpha_{j})\cap\left(\bigcap_{i:\alpha_{i}\in B}K(\alpha_{i})\right)\right).

Applying (T4) for

K(\alpha)=\bigcap_{i:\alpha_{i}\in B}K(\alpha_{i}),

where
\alpha
is a concatenation of the words
\alpha_{i}\in B
, and

K(\beta_{j})=K(\alpha_{j})\cap\left(\bigcap_{i:\alpha_{i}\in B}K(\alpha_{i})\right)\text{ such that }\alpha_{j}\notin B,

where
\beta_{j}
is a concatenation of the words
\alpha_{i}\in B
and
\alpha_{j}
,
gives

\prod_{j:\alpha_{j}\notin B}(\dot{w}_{\alpha}-\dot{w}_{\beta_{j}})=0.

By construction, for any
j
with
\alpha_{j}\notin B
we have

\dot{w}_{\beta_{j}}\leq\dot{w}_{\alpha_{j}}\quad\text{and}\quad\dot{w}_{\beta_{j}}\leq\dot{w}_{\alpha}=\prod_{i:\alpha_{i}\in B}\dot{w}_{\alpha_{i}}.

Therefore we have

Q_{B}=\dot{w}_{\alpha}\prod_{j:\alpha_{j}\not\in B}(1-\dot{w}_{\alpha_{j}})\leq\dot{w}_{\alpha}\prod_{j:\alpha_{j}\notin B}(1-\dot{w}_{\beta_{j}})=\prod_{j:\alpha_{j}\notin B}(\dot{w}_{\alpha}-\dot{w}_{\beta_{j}})=0,

and thus
b=0
.

Case 2. Suppose that

\bigcap_{i:\alpha_{i}\in B}K(\alpha_{i})\not\subseteq\bigcup_{j:\alpha_{j}\not\in B}K(\alpha_{j}).

Then there exists a
p\in\bigcap_{i:\alpha_{i}\in B}K(\alpha_{i})\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}K(\alpha_{i})
such that
p\not\in\bigcup_{j:\alpha_{j}\not\in B}K(\alpha_{j})
.
A moment’s thought shows that this dichotomy implies that

\{i\mid\alpha_{i}\in B\}=\{i\mid p\in K(\alpha_{i})\}.

Since
w_{p}
is an isometry and
\dot{w}_{\alpha_{i}}w_{p}=w_{p}
for all
i
with
p\in K(\alpha_{i})
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\|b\|

\displaystyle=|\sum_{i:\alpha_{i}\in B}\mu_{i}|=|\sum_{i:p\in K(\alpha_{i})}\mu_{i}|=\|\sum_{i:p\in K(\alpha_{i})}\mu_{i}\dot{w}_{\alpha_{i}}w_{p}\|=\|\sum_{i:p\in{\mathbf{x}}_{i}}t_{\ast}(b_{\mathbf{x}_{i}})t_{p}(v_{p})\|=0,

where we applied the assumption for
p\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{x}_{i}
in the last equality.
Thus
b=0
in this case as well, and the proof is complete.
∎

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
P
be a unital subsemigroup of a discrete group
G
and let
X_{P}
be the associated product system. Then
{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X_{P})
and
{\mathcal{T}}_{u}(P)
are canonically isomorphic. In particular, a representation of
X_{P}
is Fock covariant if and only if the associated semigroup representation of
P
satisfies conditions (T1)–(T4).

Proof.
.

This is immediate by Proposition
3.9
since the universal Fock covariant representation of
X_{P}
is equivariant and the left action on
X_{P}
is unital.
∎

4.
The reduced Hao–Ng isomorphism problem

In this section we provide the affirmative solution to the reduced Hao–Ng isomorphism problem for generalised gauge actions by discrete groups.
In order to put the problem into context we require some elements from strong covariant representations and tensor algebras. We will also require some elements from the theory of nonselfadjoint operator algebras.

4.1.
Operator algebras

For the general theory of nonselfadjoint operator algebras and full details, the reader is addressed to
[
5
,
48
]
.
By an operator algebra
{\mathfrak{A}}
we will mean a norm-closed subalgebra of some
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
.
Every operator algebra attains a C*-cover, i.e., a completely isometric homomorphism
\iota\colon{\mathfrak{A}}\to{\mathcal{C}}
such that
{\mathcal{C}}=\mathrm{C}^{*}(\iota({\mathfrak{A}}))
.
The
C*-envelope

\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathfrak{A}})
is the co-universal C*-cover of
{\mathfrak{A}}
, i.e., there exists a completely isometric homomorphism
\iota\colon{\mathfrak{A}}\to\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathfrak{A}})
such that for any other C*-cover
j\colon{\mathfrak{A}}\to{\mathcal{C}}
there exists a unique
*
-epimorphism
\Phi\colon{\mathcal{C}}\to\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathfrak{A}})
such that
\Phi\circ j=\iota
.

The existence of the C*-envelope was established by Hamana
[
22
]
through the existence of the injective envelope.
An independent proof was established by Dritschel and McCullough
[
16
]
through the existence of maximal dilations.
Recall that a homomorphism
\phi\colon{\mathfrak{A}}\to{\mathcal{B}}(K)
is called a
dilation
of
\iota\colon{\mathfrak{A}}\to{\mathcal{B}}(H)
if
H\subseteq K
and
\iota(\cdot)=P_{H}\phi(\cdot)|_{H}
.
A dilation is called
maximal
if it only attains trivial dilations, i.e., dilations by orthogonal summands.
Dritschel and McCullough show in
[
16
, Theorem 1.2]
that every (completely isometric) representation of an operator algebra
{\mathfrak{A}}
admits a (resp. completely isometric) maximal dilation.
Moreover in
[
16
, Theorem 4.1]
they obtain that the C*-algebra of a maximal completely isometric representation satisfies the co-universal property of the C*-envelope.
In a simplified version, Arveson
[
3
]
has shown that a representation of
{\mathfrak{A}}
is maximal if and only if it has a unique extension to a completely positive map on
\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathfrak{A}})
that is a
*
-homomorphism.
The proof in
[
3
]
refers to operator systems, but it can be adapted to the operator algebras category; the reader is directed to
[
15
, Section 2]
and
[
51
, Proposition 4.8]
for the full details.

4.2.
Strong covariant representations

We review the key elements of the strong covariant C*-algebra of a product system established by Sehnem
[
52
,
53
]
, and its Fell bundle considered by Dor-On, the first author, Katsoulis, Laca and Li
[
13
]
.
The strong covariant algebra of Sehnem
[
52
]
as well as the reduced variant considered in
[
13
]
encodes the previously known constructions for a co-universal or a Cuntz-type C*-algebra, see
[
13
,
52
,
53
]
and the references therein for these connections.

Let
P
be a unital subsemigroup of a discrete group
G
and let
X
be a product system over
P
. Set
A:=X_{e}
, and for each
p\in P
let
\varphi_{p}\colon A\to{\mathcal{L}}(X_{p})
be the
*
-homomorphism that implements the left action of
A
on the C*-correspondence
X_{p}
. For a finite set
F\subseteq G
let

K_{F}:=\bigcap_{g\in F}gP.

For
r\in P
and
g\in F
define the ideal of
A
given by

I_{r^{-1}K_{\{r,g\}}}:=\begin{cases}\bigcap\limits_{s\in K_{\{r,g\}}}\ker\varphi_{r^{-1}s}&\text{if }K_{\{r,g\}}\neq\emptyset\text{ and }r\notin K_{\{r,g\}},\\
\hskip 14.22636ptA&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

and set

I_{r^{-1}(r\vee F)}:=\bigcap_{g\in F}I_{r^{-1}K_{\{r,g\}}}.

We have
I_{r^{-1}(r\vee F)}=I_{(pr)^{-1}(pr\vee pF)}
for all
r,p\in P
, and
I_{r^{-1}(r\vee F)}=I_{(s^{-1}r)^{-1}(s^{-1}r\vee s^{-1}F)}
for all
r\in sP
.
Moreover

I_{r^{-1}(r\vee F_{1})}\subseteq I_{r^{-1}(r\vee F_{2})}\;\text{ when }\;F_{1}\supseteq F_{2}.

We declare
K_{\emptyset}=\emptyset
and
I_{r^{-1}(r\vee\emptyset)}=A
.
For a finite set
F\subseteq G
, let the C*-correspondences

X_{F}:=\operatornamewithlimits{\sum^{\oplus}}_{r\in P}X_{r}I_{r^{-1}(r\vee F)}\quad\text{and}\quad X_{F}^{+}:=\operatornamewithlimits{\sum^{\oplus}}_{g\in G}X_{gF}.

We declare
X_{\emptyset}=X_{\emptyset}^{+}={\mathcal{F}}X
.
Each
X_{F}^{+}
is reducing for the coaction

\delta\colon{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)\longrightarrow{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)\otimes\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}(G);\lambda_{p}(\xi_{p})\mapsto\lambda_{p}(\xi_{p})\otimes u_{p},

giving rise to a
*
-representation

\Phi_{F}\colon{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)\longrightarrow{\mathcal{L}}(X_{F}^{+});\lambda_{p}(\xi_{p})\mapsto(\lambda_{p}(\xi_{p})\otimes u_{p})|_{X_{F}^{+}}.

Here we make the identification

X_{gF}\longrightarrow X_{gF}\otimes\delta_{g};\xi_{r}a_{r}\mapsto\xi_{r}a_{r}\otimes\delta_{g},\text{ for }\xi_{r}\in X_{r},a_{r}\in I_{r^{-1}(r\vee gF)}.

Moreover
X_{F}\subseteq X_{F}^{+}
is reducing for
[{\mathcal{T}}(X)]_{e}
and so we obtain the representation

\bigoplus\limits_{\textup{fin }F\subseteq G}\Phi_{F}(\cdot)|_{X_{F}}\colon[{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)]_{e}\longrightarrow\prod\limits_{\text{fin }F\subseteq G}{\mathcal{L}}(X_{F}).

We fix the ideal
{\mathcal{I}}_{\operatorname{sc},e}
in
[{\mathcal{T}}(X)]_{e}
by using the corona universe, namely

b\in{\mathcal{I}}_{\operatorname{sc},e}\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\bigoplus\limits_{\textup{fin }F\subseteq G}\Phi_{F}(\lambda_{\ast}(b))|_{X_{F}}\in c_{0}\left({\mathcal{L}}(X_{F})\mid\textup{fin }F\subseteq G\right).

We write
A\times_{X}P
for the equivariant quotient of
{\mathcal{T}}(X)
by the induced ideal
{\mathcal{I}}_{\operatorname{sc}}:=\left\langle{\mathcal{I}}_{\operatorname{sc},e}\right\rangle
.
It is shown in
[
52
]
that this construction does not depend on
G
.
Moreover, we define the
strong covariant
bundle of
X
to be the Fell bundle

{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{C}}X:=\Big\{[A\times_{X}P]_{g}\Big\}_{g\in G},

given by the coaction by
G
on
A\times_{X}P
.
A representation of
X
that promotes to a representation of
{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{C}}X
will be called a
strong covariant representation of
X
.
By combining Proposition
2.2
with Proposition
2.7
we have that
A\times_{X}P
is the universal C*-algebra of
{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{C}}X
.
We further write
A\times_{X,\lambda}P
for the reduced C*-algebra of
{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{C}}X
.
As the strong covariant relations are graded and induced by representations on
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)
it follows that a strong covariant representation is Fock covariant.

For a product system
X
we will write
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)^{+}
for the
tensor algebra of
X
, i.e., for the norm-closed subalgebra generated by
\{\lambda_{p}(X_{p})\}_{p\in P}
inside
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)
.
We will require the following consequence of
[
53
, Corollary 3.5]
.
We note that although only product systems in the sense of Fowler are considered in
[
53
]
, the arguments apply in our setting as well.

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
P
be a unital subsemigroup of a discrete group
G
and let
X
be a product system over
P
.
If
t
is a Fock covariant injective representation of
X
and
t
admits a coaction by
G
which is normal, then the map

{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)^{+}\to\overline{\operatorname{alg}}\{t_{p}(X_{p})\mid p\in P\};\lambda_{p}(\xi_{p})\mapsto t_{p}(\xi_{p}),

is a (well-defined) completely isometric isomorphism.

Proof.
.

Let the canonical
*
-epimorphism
\Phi\colon{\mathcal{T}}_{\operatorname{c}}^{\operatorname{F}}(X)\to\mathrm{C}^{*}(t)
.
Since
t
is equivariant, then
\Phi
induces a
*
-epimorphism between the reduced C*-algebras of the induced Fell bundles.
However, since the coaction on
\mathrm{C}^{*}(t)
is normal, it follows that
\mathrm{C}^{*}(t)
coincides with the reduced C*-algebra of its induced Fell bundle.
Hence there exists a canonical
*
-epimorphism
\Phi_{\lambda}\colon{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)\to\mathrm{C}^{*}(t)
, which is injective on
A
since
t
is injective.
By
[
53
, Corollary 3.5]
it follows that the restriction of
\Phi_{\lambda}
on
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)^{+}
is completely isometric.
∎

Since
A\times_{X,\lambda}P
satisfies the conditions of Corollary
4.1
, it is a C*-cover of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)^{+}
.
In
[
53
, Theorem 5.1]
, Sehnem establishes that
A\times_{X,\lambda}P
is the C*-envelope of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)^{+}
.

4.3.
Crossed products

The theory of crossed products of C*-algebras is well-known, see for example
[
55
]
.
Katsoulis and Ramsey
[
30
]
have extended this to group actions over possibly nonselfadjoint operator algebras.
Here we will comment only on the parts that are relevant to the reduced Hao–Ng isomorphism problem.

Let
{\mathfrak{H}}
be a locally compact group that acts on an operator algebra
{\mathfrak{A}}
by completely isometric automorphisms.
The action of
{\mathfrak{H}}
then extends also to a group action
\dot{\alpha}
of the C*-envelope of
{\mathfrak{A}}
, and we can form the reduced crossed product
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathfrak{A}})\rtimes_{\dot{\alpha},\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}}
.
By considering the copy of
{\mathfrak{A}}
inside
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathfrak{A}})
, the reduced crossed product
{\mathfrak{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha,\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}}
is defined as the norm-closed subalgebra of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathfrak{A}})\rtimes_{\dot{\alpha},\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}}
generated by the
{\mathfrak{A}}
-valued functions
[
30
, Definition 3.17]
.
One of the main questions in the theory is whether this inclusion induces a canonical
*
-isomorphism with the C*-envelope of
{\mathfrak{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha,\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}}
, i.e., whether we have

(4.1)

\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathfrak{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha,\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle?}}{{\simeq}}\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathfrak{A}})\rtimes_{\dot{\alpha},\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}}.

When
{\mathfrak{A}}
admits a contractive approximate identity, this has been answered to the affirmative when
{\mathfrak{H}}
is discrete
[
27
]
, and when
{\mathfrak{H}}
is abelian
[
30
]
.
By using the maximal representations of
[
16
]
we can remove the contractive approximate identity hypothesis when
{\mathfrak{H}}
is discrete.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
{\mathfrak{H}}
be a discrete group acting by
\alpha
on an operator algebra
{\mathfrak{A}}
.
Then

\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathfrak{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha,\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}})\simeq\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathfrak{A}})\rtimes_{\dot{\alpha},\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}}.

Proof.
.

We identify
{\mathfrak{A}}
with its copy in
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathfrak{A}})
. Let
\pi\colon{\mathfrak{A}}\to{\mathcal{B}}(H)
be a maximal completely isometric representation of
{\mathfrak{A}}
.
By
[
16
]
(see also the relevant comments in Subsection
4.1
) the map
\pi
has a unique extension to a faithful
*
-representation on
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathfrak{A}})
, denoted by the same symbol.
Therefore
\overline{\pi}\rtimes U
is a faithful
*
-representation of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathfrak{A}})\rtimes_{\dot{\alpha},\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}}
, and thus its restriction on
{\mathfrak{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha,\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}}
is completely isometric.
By
[
16
]
(see also the relevant comments in Subsection
4.1
), it suffices to show that
\overline{\pi}\rtimes U
is maximal on
{\mathfrak{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha,\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}}
, as then it will follow that

\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathfrak{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha,\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}})\simeq\mathrm{C}^{*}(\overline{\pi}\rtimes U)\simeq\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathfrak{A}})\rtimes_{\dot{\alpha},\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}}

by
*
-isomorphisms fixing
{\mathfrak{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha,\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}}
.

Towards this end, let
\rho\colon{\mathfrak{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha,\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}}\to{\mathcal{B}}(K)
be a maximal dilation of
\overline{\pi}\rtimes U|_{{\mathfrak{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha,\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}}}
,
and let us denote by the same symbol the unique extension of
\rho
to a
*
-representation on

\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathfrak{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha,\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}})=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{\overline{\pi}(c)U_{{\mathfrak{h}}}\mid c\in\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathfrak{A}}),{\mathfrak{h}}\in{\mathfrak{H}}\}.

By Arveson’s Extension Principle, each
\pi\circ\alpha_{\mathfrak{h}}
is a maximal representation of
{\mathfrak{A}}
, and it is a standard argument that
\overline{\pi}
is maximal as the discrete direct sum of maximal representations, e.g., see
[
4
, Proposition 4.4]
.
Therefore
\rho
on
\overline{\pi}({\mathfrak{A}})
takes up the form

\rho(\overline{\pi}(a))=\begin{bmatrix}\overline{\pi}(a)&0\\
0&\sigma(a)\end{bmatrix}\text{ for all }a\in{\mathfrak{A}},

for a representation
\sigma
of
{\mathfrak{A}}
.
Next consider
\overline{\pi}(a)U_{{\mathfrak{h}}}
for
a\in{\mathfrak{A}}
and
{\mathfrak{h}}\in{\mathfrak{H}}
, and write

\rho(\overline{\pi}(a)U_{\mathfrak{h}})=\begin{bmatrix}\overline{\pi}(a)U_{{\mathfrak{h}}}&x\\
y&z\end{bmatrix}.

By using the unique extension property of
\rho
we have

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\overline{\pi}(a)\overline{\pi}(a)^{*}+xx^{*}&\ast\\
\ast&\ast\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\rho(\overline{\pi}(a)U_{\mathfrak{h}})\rho(\overline{\pi}(a)U_{\mathfrak{h}})^{*}=\rho(\overline{\pi}(a)U_{\mathfrak{h}}U_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*}\overline{\pi}(a)^{*})

\displaystyle=\rho(\overline{\pi}(a)\overline{\pi}(a)^{*})=\rho(\overline{\pi}(a))\rho(\overline{\pi}(a))^{*}=\begin{bmatrix}\overline{\pi}(a)\overline{\pi}(a)^{*}&0\\
0&\sigma(a)\sigma(a)^{*}\end{bmatrix}.

By equating the
(1,1)
-entries we get
x=0
.
On the other hand, by using the covariance in the C*-crossed product we can write

\overline{\pi}(\alpha_{\mathfrak{h}}^{-1}(a))^{*}\overline{\pi}(\alpha_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{-1}(a))=\overline{\pi}(\alpha_{\mathfrak{h}}^{-1}(a))^{*}U_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*}U_{{\mathfrak{h}}}\overline{\pi}(\alpha_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{-1}(a))=U_{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}\overline{\pi}(a)^{*}\overline{\pi}(a)U_{{\mathfrak{h}}}.

Then a similar computation gives

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\overline{\pi}(\alpha_{\mathfrak{h}}^{-1}(a))^{*}\overline{\pi}(\alpha_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{-1}(a))+y^{*}y&\ast\\
\ast&\ast\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\rho(\overline{\pi}(a)U_{\mathfrak{h}})^{*}\rho(\overline{\pi}(a)U_{\mathfrak{h}})=\rho\left(U_{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}\overline{\pi}(a)^{*}\overline{\pi}(a)U_{{\mathfrak{h}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\rho\left(\overline{\pi}(\alpha_{\mathfrak{h}}^{-1}(a))^{*}\overline{\pi}(\alpha_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{-1}(a))\right)=\rho\left(\overline{\pi}(\alpha_{\mathfrak{h}}^{-1}(a))\right)^{*}\rho\left(\overline{\pi}(\alpha_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{-1}(a))\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\overline{\pi}(\alpha_{\mathfrak{h}}^{-1}(a))^{*}\overline{\pi}(\alpha_{\mathfrak{h}}^{-1}(a))&0\\
0&\sigma(\alpha_{\mathfrak{h}}^{-1}(a))^{*}\sigma(\alpha_{\mathfrak{h}}^{-1}(a))\end{bmatrix}.

By equating the
(1,1)
-entries we get
y=0
.
Therefore
\rho
is a trivial dilation, as required.
∎

4.4.
Hao–Ng

Let us return to the product system discussion.
Let
P
be a unital subsemigroup of a discrete group
G
and let
X
be a product system over
P
.
Let
{\mathfrak{H}}
be a discrete group, and let
\alpha
be a
generalised gauge action
of
{\mathfrak{H}}
on the Fock C*-algebra
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)
, i.e., every
\lambda_{p}(X_{p})
is
\alpha
-invariant in the sense that

\alpha_{{\mathfrak{h}}}(\lambda_{p}(X_{p}))=\lambda_{p}(X_{p})\text{ for all }p\in P\text{ and }{\mathfrak{h}}\in{\mathfrak{H}}.

Let
\pi
be a faithful
*
-representation of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)
in some
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
and consider the faithful
*
-representation
\overline{\pi}\rtimes U
on
\ell^{2}({\mathfrak{H}},H)
that gives rise to
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)\rtimes_{\alpha,\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}}
.
Since
\alpha
restricts to an action on
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)^{+}
, from
[
30
, Corollary 3.16]
we have a canonical completely isometric copy of the crossed product
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)^{+}\rtimes_{\alpha,\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}}
in
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)\rtimes_{\alpha,\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}}
.
We can now consider the family
X\rtimes_{\alpha,\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}}
of the subspaces

(X\rtimes_{\alpha,\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}})_{p}:=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{\overline{\pi}(\lambda_{p}(\xi_{p}))U_{{\mathfrak{h}}}\mid\xi_{p}\in X_{p},{\mathfrak{h}}\in{\mathfrak{H}}\}\text{ for }p\in P.

It is accustomed to use the short form
X_{p}\rtimes_{\alpha,\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}}
instead of
(X\rtimes_{\alpha,\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}})_{p}
.
We will show that this construction gives rise to a product system.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
P
be a unital subsemigroup of a discrete group
G
and let
X
be a product system over
P
.
Let
\alpha
be a generalised gauge action of a discrete group
{\mathfrak{H}}
on
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)
.
Then
X\rtimes_{\alpha,\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}}
is a product system over
P
.

Proof.
.

For condition (i) by definition we have

X_{e}\rtimes_{\alpha,\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}}:=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{\overline{\pi}(\lambda_{e}(\xi_{e}))U_{{\mathfrak{h}}}\mid\xi_{e}\in X_{e},{\mathfrak{h}}\in{\mathfrak{H}}\}\simeq A\rtimes_{\alpha,\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}},

which is a C*-algebra.
The other two conditions follow by the fact that we have the equality
\alpha_{{\mathfrak{h}}}(\lambda_{p}(X_{p}))=\lambda_{p}(X_{p})
for all
p\in P
and
{\mathfrak{h}}\in{\mathfrak{H}}
, the covariant relations of the crossed product and that

The following proposition is the key result of this section.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
P
be a unital subsemigroup of a discrete group
G
and let
X
be a product system over
P
.
Let
\alpha
be a generalised gauge action of a discrete group
{\mathfrak{H}}
on
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)
.
Then the identity representation
\iota^{\rtimes}\colon X\rtimes_{\alpha,\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}}\to{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)\rtimes_{\alpha,\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}}
is equivariant, Fock covariant, and injective.

Proof.
.

We will show that the identity representation satisfies the conditions of Theorem
3.2
.
By definition it is injective.
Moreover, by
[
55
, Lemma 7.16]
we have

({\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)\otimes\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}(G))\rtimes_{\alpha\otimes{\operatorname{id}},\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}}\simeq({\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)\rtimes_{\alpha,\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}})\otimes\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}(G),

by a canonical
*
-isomorphism, and hence we get that
\iota^{\rtimes}
is equivariant.
It remains to show that
\iota^{\rtimes}
satisfies conditions (i) and (ii) of Theorem
3.2
.
We will make the standard identification of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)
with
\overline{\pi}({\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X))
, and of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}(X)\rtimes_{\alpha,\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}}
with
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\overline{\pi},U)
.

For condition (i), an application of Proposition
4.4
and using that
\mathbf{K}_{\emptyset,\lambda_{\ast}}=(0)
yield

\mathbf{K}_{\emptyset,\iota^{\rtimes}_{\ast}}=\mathbf{K}_{\emptyset,\lambda_{\ast}}\rtimes_{\alpha,\lambda}{\mathfrak{H}}=(0).

For condition (ii), let
{\mathcal{F}}=\{{\mathbf{x}}_{1},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}_{n}\}
be a finite
\cap
-closed subset of
{\mathcal{J}}
such that
\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{x}_{i}\neq\emptyset
and let
b_{{\mathbf{x}}_{i}}\in\mathbf{K}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{i},\hat{t}^{\rtimes}_{\ast}}
for
i=1,\dots,n
that satisfy

\sum_{i:r\in{\mathbf{x}}_{i}}\iota^{\rtimes}_{\ast}(b_{{\mathbf{x}}_{i}})\iota^{\rtimes}_{r}\left(\overline{\pi}\left(\lambda_{r}(X_{r}\right)U_{{\mathfrak{h}}}\right)=(0)\text{ for all }r\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}{\mathbf{x}}_{i}\text{ and }{\mathfrak{h}}\in{\mathfrak{H}}.

We will show that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\iota^{\rtimes}_{\ast}(b_{{\mathbf{x}}_{i}})=0
.
For notational convenience, set

c:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\iota^{\rtimes}_{\ast}(b_{{\mathbf{x}}_{i}})\quad\text{and}\quad c_{r}:=\sum_{i:r\in\mathbf{x}_{i}}\iota^{\rtimes}_{\ast}(b_{\mathbf{x}_{i}})\text{ for each }r\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}{\mathbf{x}}_{i}.

Since
b_{\mathbf{x}_{i}}^{*}b_{\mathbf{x}_{j}}\in\mathbf{K}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{i}\cap{\mathbf{x}}_{j},\hat{t}^{\rtimes}_{\ast}}
, by Proposition
4.4
we have

E_{{\mathfrak{H}}}\left(\iota^{\rtimes}_{\ast}(b_{\mathbf{x}_{i}}^{*}b_{\mathbf{x}_{j}})\right)\in\overline{\pi}(\mathbf{K}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{i}\cap{\mathbf{x}}_{j},\lambda_{\ast}}),

and in particular we can write

E_{{\mathfrak{H}}}\left(\iota^{\rtimes}_{\ast}(b_{\mathbf{x}_{i}}^{*}b_{\mathbf{x}_{j}})\right)=\overline{\pi}\left(\lambda_{\ast}(d_{\mathbf{x}_{i}\cap\mathbf{x}_{j}})\right),\text{ for some }d_{\mathbf{x}_{i}\cap\mathbf{x}_{j}}\in\mathbf{K}_{\mathbf{x}_{i}\cap\mathbf{x}_{j},\hat{t}_{\ast}}.

Therefore we have

E_{{\mathfrak{H}}}(c^{*}c)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}E_{{\mathfrak{H}}}(\iota^{\rtimes}_{\ast}(b_{\mathbf{x}_{i}}^{*}b_{\mathbf{x}_{j}}))=\overline{\pi}\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\lambda_{\ast}(d_{\mathbf{x}_{i}\cap\mathbf{x}_{j}})\right)\in\overline{\pi}\left(\mathbf{B}_{{\mathcal{F}},\lambda_{\ast}}\right),

where we used that
\{\mathbf{x}_{i}\cap\mathbf{x}_{j}:i,j=1,\dots,n\}={\mathcal{F}}
for the inclusion, since
{\mathcal{F}}
is
\cap
-closed.
Now let

r\in\bigcup_{i,j=1}^{n}(\mathbf{x}_{i}\cap\mathbf{x}_{j})=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{x}_{i}.

By using that
{\mathcal{F}}
is
\cap
-closed we obtain

\{(i,j)\in[n]^{2}\mid r\in\mathbf{x}_{i}\cap\mathbf{x}_{j}\}=\{i\in[n]\mid r\in\mathbf{x}_{i}\}\times\{j\in[n]\mid r\in\mathbf{x}_{j}\},

and hence

\displaystyle E_{{\mathfrak{H}}}(c_{r}^{*}c_{r})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i:r\in\mathbf{x}_{i}}\sum_{j:r\in\mathbf{x}_{j}}E_{{\mathfrak{H}}}\left(\iota^{\rtimes}_{\ast}(b_{\mathbf{x}_{i}}^{*}b_{\mathbf{x}_{j}})\right)=\sum_{i,j:r\in\mathbf{x}_{i}\cap\mathbf{x}_{j}}E_{{\mathfrak{H}}}\left(\iota^{\rtimes}_{\ast}(b_{\mathbf{x}_{i}}^{*}b_{\mathbf{x}_{j}})\right)=\sum_{i,j:r\in\mathbf{x}_{i}\cap\mathbf{x}_{j}}\overline{\pi}\left(\lambda_{\ast}(d_{\mathbf{x}_{i}\cap\mathbf{x}_{j}})\right).

\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\lambda_{\ast}(d_{\mathbf{x}_{i}\cap\mathbf{x}_{j}})=0

E_{{\mathfrak{H}}}(c^{*}c)=\overline{\pi}\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\lambda_{\ast}(d_{\mathbf{x}_{i}\cap\mathbf{x}_{j}})\right)=0.

Faithfulness of

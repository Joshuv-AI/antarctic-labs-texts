An alternative to spherical Witt vectors
Thomas Nikolaus, Maria Yakerson

Abstract
. We give a direct construction of the ring spectrum of spherical Witt vectors of a perfect 𝔽 p {\mathbb{F}}_{p} -algebra R R as the completion of the spherical monoid algebra 𝕊 ⁡ [ R ] \mathbb{S}[R] of the multiplicative monoid ( R , ⋅ ) (R,\cdot) at the ideal I = fib ⁡ ( 𝕊 ⁡ [ R ] → R ) I=\mathrm{fib}(\mathbb{S}[R]\to R) . This generalizes a construction of Cuntz and Deninger. We also use this to give a description of the category of p p -complete modules over the spherical Witt vectors and a universal property for spherical Witt vectors as an 𝔼 1 \mathbb{E}_{1} -ring.

Proof of Corollary

-category of
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-algebras in the monoidal

of
\mathbb{S}_{{\mathrm{W}}(R)}
-bimodules equipped with the bimodule tensor product. Since a bimodule is simply a right module in left modules we deduce from Theorem

. For an algebra

from both sides factors over

. Thus it lies in the image from both sides precisely if the map

3
which we present here and for explaining the subsequent example to us. In the proof we will use repeatedly the following lemma.

Lemma 4
.

Let
A
be a
p
-complete
\mathbb{E}_{n}
-ring spectrum for
n\geqslant 2
and
\tilde{A}\to A
a square zero extension by a
p
-complete symmetric bimodule
P
. Then any
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-ring map
\mathbb{S}_{{\mathrm{W}}(R)}\to A
has a unique lift to
\tilde{A}
up to equivalence.

Proof.

A lift to
\tilde{A}
is the same data as a nullhomotopy of the corresponding map of bimodules (a derivation)

L_{\mathbb{S}_{{\mathrm{W}}(R)}/\mathbb{S}}\to\Sigma P
where

L_{\mathbb{S}_{{\mathrm{W}}(R)}/\mathbb{S}}=\mathrm{fib}(\mathbb{S}_{{\mathrm{W}}(R)}\otimes\mathbb{S}_{{\mathrm{W}}(R)}\to\mathbb{S}_{{\mathrm{W}}(R)})

is the
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-cotangent complex. Since
P
is symmetric, such a bimodule map is equivalently a module map from the symmetrization of
L_{\mathbb{S}_{{\mathrm{W}}(R)}/\mathbb{S}}
which is the fibre of the canonical map
\alpha\colon\mathbb{S}_{{\mathrm{W}}(R)}\to\mathrm{THH}(\mathbb{S}_{{\mathrm{W}}(R)})
. The map
\alpha
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-equivalence because
\mathrm{HH}_{*}(R;{\mathbb{F}_{p}})\simeq R
for a perfect
{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
-algebra
R
. Since
\alpha
is a map between connective spectra, it is then also a
p
-adic equivalence, i.e., the fibre of
\alpha
vanishes
p
-adically. Hence the space of nullhomotopies under consideration is trivial, so the space of lifts in the claim is contractible, and the claim follows.
∎

Proof of Proposition
3
.

We may without loss of generality assume that
A
is connective. We first prove the case
n=1
.
By perfectness of
R
we have that

\Map_{\mathrm{CRing}}(R,{\pi_{0}A}^{\flat})=\Map_{\mathrm{CRing}}(R,\pi_{0}(A)/p)=\Map_{\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}}(\mathbb{S}_{{\mathrm{W}}(R)},\pi_{0}(A)/p)\ .

Thus it suffices to shows that the natural map

(2.2)

\Map_{\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}}(\mathbb{S}_{{\mathrm{W}}(R)},A)\to\Map_{\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}}(\mathbb{S}_{{\mathrm{W}}(R)},\pi_{0}(A)/p)

induced by
A\to\pi_{0}(A)/p
is an equivalence. We will decompose it as

A\to\pi_{0}(A)\to\pi_{0}(A)//p\to\pi_{0}(A)/p

and show that at each step a lift of a map from
\mathbb{S}_{{\mathrm{W}}(R)}
is unique up to equivalence.

First, we consider the space of lifts of a map
\mathbb{S}_{{\mathrm{W}}(R)}\to\pi_{0}(A)
to
A
.
We write
A
as the limit of its Postnikov tower
A=\operatorname*{lim}\tau_{\leqslant m}A
. It suffices to show that the lift is unique up to equivalence for each
\tau_{\leqslant m}A
. Note that each
\tau_{\leqslant m+1}A
is a square zero extension of
\tau_{\leqslant m}A
by
\Sigma^{m+1}(\pi_{m+1}A)
.
The assumption that
\pi_{0}(A)
lies in the center of
\pi_{*}(A)
ensures that the bimodule
\pi_{m+1}A
is symmetric, in the sense that it is induced by a left module (using that
\pi_{0}A
is commutative). Thus we can apply Lemma
4
to deduce the uniqueness of each lift.

Next, we consider the space of lifts of a map
\mathbb{S}_{{\mathrm{W}}(R)}\to\pi_{0}(A)//p
to
\pi_{0}(A)
.
Here we argue similarly: we write
\pi_{0}(A)=\operatorname*{lim}\pi_{0}(A)/\!/p^{m}
by
p
-completeness, then observe that each
\pi_{0}(A)/\!/p^{m+1}
is a square zero extension of
\pi_{0}(A)/\!/p^{m}
by
\pi_{0}(A)//p
and apply Lemma
4
.

Finally, we use that
\pi_{0}(A)//p
is a square zero extension of
\pi_{0}(A)/p
by
\Sigma\pi_{0}A[p]
and apply Lemma
4
again.
This finishes the proof for
n=1
.

For
n>1
we argue inductively: using Dunn additivity the space of
\mathbb{E}_{n}
-maps
\Map_{\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}}(\mathbb{S}_{{\mathrm{W}}(R)},A)
can be written as the totalization of a cosimplicial diagram

[k]\mapsto\Map_{\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n-1}}}((\mathbb{S}_{{\mathrm{W}}(R)})^{\hat{\otimes}_{\mathbb{S}}(k+1)},A)\ .

We have that
(\mathbb{S}_{{\mathrm{W}}(R)})^{\hat{\otimes}_{\mathbb{S}}(k+1)}=\mathbb{S}_{{\mathrm{W}}\left(R^{\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(k+1)}\right)}
so that by the inductive hypothesis this cosimplicial diagram is equivalent to

[k]\mapsto\Map_{\mathrm{Ring}}(R^{\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(k+1)},{\pi_{0}A}^{\flat})\

which agrees with the space of
\mathbb{E}_{n}
-maps
R\to{\pi_{0}A}^{\flat}
, which are the same as commutative ring maps by discreteness. This shows the case of finite
n
and the case
n=\infty
follows since it is the limit of all the spaces of
\mathbb{E}_{n}
-maps, hence the limit of a constant diagram.
∎

Remark 5

(Ramzi)
.

From the proof of Proposition
3
we can see that the assumption on
\pi_{0}(A)
being in the center is necessary. Otherwise, let
A
be a square zero extension of
\mathbb{S}_{{\mathrm{W}}(R)}
by the (non-symmetric) bimodule
L_{\mathbb{S}_{{\mathrm{W}}(R)}/\mathbb{S}}
. If the claim of Proposition
3
would hold for such
A
, it would imply that the space of bimodule maps
L_{\mathbb{S}_{{\mathrm{W}}(R)}/\mathbb{S}}\to\Sigma L_{\mathbb{S}_{{\mathrm{W}}(R)}/\mathbb{S}}
is contractible. In particular, its
\pi_{1}
would be trivial, so
\Sigma L_{\mathbb{S}_{{\mathrm{W}}(R)}/\mathbb{S}}=0
. By definition of the
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-cotangent complex, this would imply that
\mathbb{S}_{{\mathrm{W}}(R)}
is idempotent. Hence

would also be idempotent, which is not true unless

.

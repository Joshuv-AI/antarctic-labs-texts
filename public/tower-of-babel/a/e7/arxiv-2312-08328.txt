Abstract
Several weakenings of the T 2 T_{2} property for topological spaces, including k k -Hausdorff, K ​ C KC , weakly Hausdorff, semi-Hausdorff, R ​ C RC , and U ​ S US , have been studied by mathematicians. Here we provide a complete survey of how these properties do or do not relate to one another, including several new results to fill in the gaps in the existing literature, motivated by the use of a community-maintained database of topological spaces and their properties.

Theorem 14

KC\Rightarrow wH\Rightarrow US.

Suppose

Lemma 16

The product of

Theorem 17
.

The square of a compact non-

The diagonal of a space is homeomorphic to the space,

Definition 21

A space

A subset

(2.1 of
[

]
)

The criteria for

So the notion of

Definition 24

A subset
C

is

-closed

K

is closed in
K
.

4

A set is

-closed if its intersection with each compact Hausdorff

Theorem 25

A space

Theorem 26
.

wH\Rightarrow k_{2}H\Rightarrow US

Proof.

The first implication is Proposition 11.2 of
[

Now let

be limits of

Definition 31

A

f:X\to R

Definition 32

(P101)

A space is

This provides another example of a property between

Theorem 33
.

T_{2}\Rightarrow RC\Rightarrow T_{1}

Proof.

Given a
T_{2}
space
X
, and consider a retract
R\subseteq X

f:X\to R
. Given

f(x)\not=x

Consider now

of

,
and thus

R\cap W=R\cap U\cap f^{\leftarrow}[V]=\emptyset
, showing

is an open neighborhood of

. Thus

is closed.

Finally let
X
be

. Then for each
x\in X
, note

is continuous, and thus

is closed,
showing

is

.

,
and therefore
\operatorname{cl}(A)=A
.

We can see that
k_{1}H
is not sufficient to imply

RC
.

Example 36

(S17, Example
9
)
.

Earlier we noted that
the co-countable topology on an uncountable
set is
k_{1}H
but not
T_{2}
. In fact,
the space fails to be
RC
: partition this space
into pairs of points, and consider a map that
sends each pair to a single point within
their set. Then this image is uncountable and
therefore not closed. But this image is a retract as
the map is continuous: given a co-countable set,
the inverse image is still co-countable.

It’s important that S17 is not compact, given the following
result.

Proposition 37
.

All compact
KC
spaces are
RC
.

Proof.

Every retract of a compact space is compact,
so every retract of a compact
KC
space is closed.
∎

But
KC
cannot be weakened to
wH
here.

Proposition 38
.

The square of a non-
T_{2}
space is not
RC
.

Proof.

The diagonal is a retract of the square by the continuous
map
(x,y)\mapsto(x,x)
, and a space is
T_{2}
if and
only if its diagonal is closed.
∎

Note that the previous two propositions provide an alternative
proof for Theorem
17
.

Example 39

(S31, Example
18
)
.

The square of the one-point compactification of the rationals
was noted earlier to be
wH
, but fails to be
RC

by the preceding proposition.

We leave open the following question:

Question 40
.

Does there exist a compact
RC
space which is not

KC
?

5
Semi-Hausdorff

The semi-Hausdorff property was introduced in
[
13
]
.

Definition 41
.

An open set is
regular open
if it equals the interior of its
closure.

Definition 42

(P10)
.

A space is
semiregular
if it has a basis of
regular open sets.

Definition 43

(P169)
.

A space is
semi-Hausdorff
or
sH
if for each
pair of distinct points
x,y
, there exists a
regular open neighborhood of
x
missing
y
.

Proposition 44
.

All
T_{1}
semiregular spaces are semi-Hausdorff.

Proof.

Given
T_{1}
, there exists an open neighborhood
U

of
x
missing
y
. Then by semiregular,
U
contains
a regular open neighborhood
V
of
x
missing
y
.
∎

Theorem 45
.

T_{2}\Rightarrow sH\Rightarrow T_{1}

Proof.

Given points
x,y
, let
U,V
be disjoint open neighborhoods.
Then
\operatorname{cl}(U)\cap V=\emptyset
,
so
\operatorname{int}(\operatorname{cl}(U))
is a regular open neighborhood of
x
missing

y
. The second implication is immediate from the definitions.
∎

Example 46
.

The
RC
-not-
KC
space constructed in
[
11
]

is
sH
. Likewise, its arXiv
arXiv preprint
2211.12579v3

includes a construction which is
RC
,
sH
, but not
US
.

In this case we may return to a more straightforward example
to show not all
sH
spaces are
T_{2}
.

Example 47

(S97, Example
12
)
.

Earlier this space was shown to be
KC
, and therefore

T_{1}
. The space is also compact and thus
RC
.

Singletons
are regular open neighborhoods for points of
\omega^{2}
.
The open neighborhoods of
\infty_{y}
missing at least the first row of

\omega^{2}
are regular open, and the open neighborhoods
of
\infty_{x}
missing at least the first column of
\omega^{2}

are regular open.

Since the space is semiregular and

T_{1}
, the space is
sH
.
(Note that the authors of
[
3
]

assume
T_{2}
in their
definition for semiregular, explaining why it is marked
as false in their reference chart.)

Example 48

(e.g. S37, Example
27
)
.

Consider any
T_{2}
space
X
with a non-isolated
point
x\in X
.
Let
X^{\prime}=X\cup\{x^{\prime}\}
have the topology generated by
the original open subsets of
X
, and for each open
neighborhood
U
of
x
, let
\{x^{\prime}\}\cup U\setminus\{x\}

be a basic open neighborhood of
x^{\prime}
.

This space remains
T_{1}
, but it can be seen immediately that

x,x^{\prime}
cannot be separated by open sets. In fact, this space
cannot be even
sH
as every regular open neighborhood of

x
contains
x^{\prime}
: given a neighborhood
U
of
x
,
its closure contains the open neighborhood
\{x^{\prime}\}\cup U
of

x^{\prime}
. Likewise, the space is not
RC
: the map

x^{\prime}\mapsto x
which is otherwise the identity shows

X
is a retract of
X^{\prime}
which is not closed.

Earlier it was noted that S37 is
US
, but not
k_{2}H
.
In fact, we’ve already seen an
example that shows that
k_{1}H
is insufficient to imply

sH
.

Proposition 49
.

The only nonempty hyperconnected
sH
space is the
singleton.

Proof.

Let
x,y
be distinct points of a hyperconnected
space. Then every regular open neighborhood of
x

is the entire space, and thus cannot be used to separate

x
from
y
.
∎

Example 50

(S17, Example
9
)
.

Earlier we noted that
the co-countable topology on an uncountable
set is
k_{1}H
but not
RC
.
The space also fails to be
sH
by
the preceding proposition.

We’ve now established that, like
RC
,

sH
cannot be placed into the chain of implications
given in Corollary
30
: S17 is
k_{1}H
but
neither
sH
nor
RC
, while the example from
arXiv preprint
2211.12579v3
is
sH

and
RC
, but not even
US
. (We will introduce a
more elementary example of an
sH
space that fails
to be
US
as Example
54
below.)

So how might
RC
and
sH
be related? We again
revisit a counter-example that will show us
that
RC
need not imply

sH
, even when spaces are compact.

Definition 51
.

A space is
nowhere locally compact
if no point of
the space has a compact neighborhood.

Theorem 52
.

The one-point compactification of any
nowhere locally compact space
X
is not
sH
.

Proof.

Consider a regular open neighborhood
U
of the compactifying
point
\infty
. Since
X
is nowhere locally compact, every
neighborhood of a point in
x
cannot miss
U
. It follows
that
\operatorname{cl}(U)=X\cup\{\infty\}
, and thus

U=\operatorname{int}(\operatorname{cl}(U))=\operatorname{int}(X\cup\{\infty\})=X\cup\{\infty\}
. Therefore
\infty

cannot be separated from any other point by a regular open set.
∎

Example 53

(S29, Example
11
)
.

The one-point compactification of the rationals
is
KC
, and therefore
RC
.
But the space fails to be
sH
by the previous
theorem: compact subsets
of the rationals are nowhere dense and therefore cannot contain
an open set,
showing the rationals are nowhere locally compact.

We may also obtain an example of a compact space which is

sH
but neither
RC
nor
US
.

Example 54

(S185)
.

We introduce a coarsening of the topology of S97 on

\omega^{2}\cup\{\infty_{x},\infty_{y}\}
. We continue
to assume
\omega^{2}
is discrete,
and neighborhoods of
\infty_{y}
contain all but
finitely-many rows, but neighborhoods of
\infty_{x}

must contain all but finitely-many columns.

The argument that this space is
sH
is identical to
the argument for S97, and it’s also immediate to verify
that the space is compact. However, unlike S97, this space is
neither
RC
nor
US
. To see the latter, note
that the sequence
(n,n)
converges to both
\infty_{x}

and
\infty_{y}
.

As for the former, we will show that the non-closed set

\{(n,n):n<\omega\}\cup\{\infty_{y}\}
is a retract.
This set is the image of the map

(a,b)\mapsto(\max\{a,b\},\max\{a,b\})
,

\infty_{x}\mapsto\infty_{y}
, and
\infty_{y}\mapsto\infty_{y}
.
To see that this map is continuous, we need only consider
the preimage of basic neighborhoods of
\infty_{y}
. Such
a neighborhood is of the form

\{(n,n):N\leq n<\omega\}\cup\{\infty_{y}\}
, and its preimage
is equal to

\{(n,m):N\leq n<\omega,m<\omega\}\cup\{\infty_{x}\}\cup\{(m,n):N\leq n<\omega,m<\omega\}\cup\{\infty_{y}\}

and is thus open.

6
Locally Hausdorff

The final property we will investigate was studied in e.g.

[
14
]
.

Definition 55

(P84)
.

A space is
locally Hausdorff
or
lH
provided
every point has a
T_{2}
neighborhood.

Theorem 56
.

T_{2}\Rightarrow lH\Rightarrow T_{1}

Proof.

The first implication is immediate. For the second,
take distinct points
x,y
and let
U
be an open
T_{2}

neighborhood of
x
. If
U
misses
y
, we’re done;
otherwise use the
T_{2}
property of
U
to obtain a smaller
open neighborhood
V
of
x
missing
y
.
∎

We may quickly verify these do not reverse by returning to
previously explored counterexamples.

Example 57

(S17, Example
9
)
.

The co-countable topology on an uncountable set is

k_{1}H
, but neither
RC
nor
sH
. It is also
not
lH
as every nonempty open set is homeomorphic to the whole
non-
T_{2}
space.

Example 58

(S29, Example
11
)
.

The one-point compactification of the rationals is

KC
and
RC
, and thus
T_{1}
, but not
sH
. It is also not

lH
as no neighborhood of
\infty
is
T_{2}
.

Example 59

(S185, Example
54
)
.

This space is
sH
, but neither
US
nor
RC
.
It is
lH
, and in fact locally metrizable,
as neighborhoods of
\infty_{x}
and
\infty_{y}
that
miss each other are copies of the subspace

\{(0,0)\}\cup\left\{\left(\frac{\cos(1/m)}{n},\frac{\sin(1/m)}{n}\right):0<m,n<\omega\right\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Example 60

(e.g. S37, Example
27
)
.

By doubling a non-isolated point of a
T_{2}
space we obtain a space
which is neither
RC
nor
sH
, but remains
lH
.

We have thus demonstrated that
lH
is incomparable with
RC
,
and fails to slot into the chain of implications in
Corollary
30
. We’ve also shown that
lH

does not imply
sH
. So we proceed to construct a counterexample
to the converse as well.

Example 61

(e.g. S186)
.

Let
X
be an
sH
but non-
T_{2}
space
(e.g. S97, S185), and let
\omega
have the discrete topology.
Our space is
(X\times\omega)\cup\{\infty\}
,
where
X\times\omega
is open with the product topology.
Neighborhoods of
\infty
contain
X\times(\omega\setminus N)

for some
N<\omega
.

Immediately we see that the space is not
lH
as every
neighborhood of
\infty
contains a copy of the non-
T_{2}
space

Y
.

To see that the space is
sH
, first consider a point

(x,n)\in X\times\omega
. Given
y\in X\setminus\{x\}
,
choose regular open
U\subseteq X
that contains
x
but
misses
y
. Then
U\times\{n\}
is a regular open set that
contains
(x,n)
but misses
(y,n)
. Given
y\in X
and

m\in\omega\setminus\{n\}
,
X\times\{n\}
is a regular
open set that contains misses
(y,m)
. Likewise, it misses

\infty
.

Finally, consider
\infty
. Given any
(x,n)\in X\times\omega
,

(X\times(\omega\setminus(n+1)))\cup\{\infty\}
is a regular
open set containing
\infty
that misses
(x,n)
.

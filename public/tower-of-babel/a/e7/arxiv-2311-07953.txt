1.
Introduction

We begin by recalling some well known facts about the Heisenberg groups
\mathbb{H}^{n}
and their representations. The group
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is just
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
(or equivalently
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
) equipped with the group law

(x,y,t)(u,v,s)=(x+u,y+v,t+s+\frac{1}{2}(u\cdot y-x\cdot v)).

Note that
\operatorname{Re}(\mathbb{H}^{n})=\{(x,0,t):x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,t\in\mathbb{R}\}
and
\operatorname{Im}(\mathbb{H}^{n})=\{(0,y,t):y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},t\in\mathbb{R}\}
are two subgroups of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
each being isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}.

For each
\lambda\in\mathbb{R},\lambda\neq 0,
there is an irreducible unitary representation
\pi_{\lambda}
of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
explicitly given by

\pi_{\lambda}(x,y,t)f(\xi)=e^{i\lambda t}e^{i\lambda(x\cdot\xi+\frac{1}{2}x\cdot y)}f(\xi+y),\,\,\,f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

By setting
\pi(x,y)=\pi_{1}(x,y,0)
we observe that the irreducibility of
\pi_{1}
has the following consequence: if a bounded linear operator
T
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
commutes with
\pi(x,y)
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
then
T=cI
for a constant
c.
However, there are non trivial operators that commute either with the family
\pi(x,0)
or with
\pi(0,y).

Observe that
\pi(0,y)f(\xi)=f(\xi+y)
are the translations and
\pi(x,0)f(\xi)=e^{ix\cdot\xi}f(\xi)
are the modulations. It is well known that any operator
T
that commutes with all translations
\pi(0,y)
is of the form
T_{m}
where

T_{m}f(x)=(2\pi)^{-n/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}\,m(\xi)\,\widehat{f}(\xi)\,d\xi

for some
m\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).
Similarly, bounded linear operators
S
that commute with all modulations
\pi(x,0)
are of the form
S_{m}f(x)=m(x)f(x)
where
m\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).
Thus we see that though
\pi_{1}
is irreducible for
\mathbb{H}^{n}
its restriction to neither of the subgroups
\operatorname{Re}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
or
\operatorname{Im}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
is irreducible. In this article, we introduce a group
G_{n}
which contains two copies of the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}
as subgroups. We describe a family of irreducible representations
\rho_{\lambda},\lambda\neq 0
and show that the restriction of
\rho_{\lambda}
to each copy of the Heisenberg subgroup is reducible.

The representations
\rho_{\lambda}
of the group
G_{n}
are realised on a Hilbert space of entire functions, namely the twisted Fock spaces. Recall that in the case of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
the Schrödinger representation
\pi_{1}
is unitarily equivalent to the Fock space representation
\rho_{0}
realised on the Fock space
\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{C}}^{n})
consisting of entire functions on
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
that are square integrable with respect to the weight function
w_{0}(w)=c_{0}\,e^{-\frac{1}{2}|w|^{2}}.
The unitary operator intertwining
\pi_{1}
and
\rho_{0}
is given by the Bargmann transform
B:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{C}}^{n})
defined by

Bf(z)=e^{-\frac{1}{4}z^{2}}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(\xi)e^{-\frac{1}{2}|\xi|^{2}}\,e^{z\cdot\xi}\,d\xi.

Thus for
w=u+iv\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n},\rho_{0}(w)=B\circ\pi(v,u)\circ B^{\ast}
which is explicitly given by

\rho_{0}(w)F(z)=e^{-\frac{1}{4}|w|^{2}}\,F(z+w)\,e^{-\frac{1}{2}z\cdot\bar{w}}.

As
\rho_{0}
is irreducible, there are no bounded linear operators on
\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{C}}^{n})
other than constant multiples of the identity that commute with
\rho_{0}(w)
for all
w\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}.
The problem of characterising all bounded linear operators
T
that commute with
\rho_{0}(a),a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
(or with
\rho_{0}(ib),b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
) has received some attention in recent years, see
[
4
,
3
]
.

It can be easily seen that any bounded linear operator on the Fock space commuting with all
\rho_{0}(a),a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is of the form
S_{\varphi}
for some
\varphi\in\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{C}}^{n})
where

(1.1)

S_{\varphi}F(z)=\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}F(w)\varphi(z-\bar{w})e^{\frac{1}{2}z\cdot\bar{w}}\,e^{-\frac{1}{2}|w|^{2}}dw.

These operators are densely defined and there is a simple necessary and sufficient condition on
\varphi
for the operators to be bounded.
Let
G:L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{C}}^{n})
be the Gauss-Bargmann transform defined by

(1.2)

Gm(z)=e^{\frac{1}{4}z^{2}}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}m(\xi)e^{iz\cdot\xi}\,e^{-|\xi|^{2}}\,d\xi.

Answering a question raised by Zhu
[
13
]
, the authors of the work
[
4
]
have proved that
S_{\varphi}
is bounded on
\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{C}}^{n})
if and only if
\varphi=Gm
for some
m\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

We also have a characterisation of bounded linear operators on the Fock space that commute with
\rho_{0}(ib),b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.
These are of the form

(1.3)

\widetilde{S}_{\varphi}F(z)=\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}F(w)\varphi(z+\bar{w})e^{\frac{1}{2}z\cdot\bar{w}}\,e^{-\frac{1}{2}|w|^{2}}dw

for a suitable
\varphi\in\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{C}}^{n}).
These two families of operators are related via the identity

\widetilde{S}_{\varphi}=U\circ S_{U^{\ast}\varphi}\circ U^{\ast}

where
U
is the unitary operator on
\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{C}}^{n})
defined by
UF(z)=F(-iz).

Hence we infer that
\widetilde{S}_{\varphi}
is bounded if and only if
U^{\ast}\varphi=G(m)
for a bounded function
m.
Some generalisation of this result has been obtained in the recent work
[
2
]
.

In an earlier work
[
11
]
we have proved that for any non constant
\varphi\in\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{C}}^{n})
at least one of the operators
S_{\varphi}
or
\widetilde{S}_{\varphi}
is unbounded. Interestingly, this was proved as a consequence of Hardy’s theorem for the Fourier transform on
\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Yet another consequence of these considerations is a new proof of the well known fact that the projective representation
\rho_{0}
of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
is irreducible. Indeed, if a bounded linear operator
T
on
\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{C}}^{n})
commutes with all
\rho_{0}(w),w\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
then there are functions
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{C}}^{n})
such that
T=S_{\varphi}=\widetilde{S}_{\psi}.
As
S_{\varphi}1=\varphi
and
\widetilde{S}_{\psi}1=\psi
it follows that
\varphi=\psi
and hence
S_{\varphi}=\widetilde{S}_{\varphi}.
By what we have just mentioned, this is possible only if
\varphi
is a constant and hence
T
reduces to a constant multiple of the identity. This proves the irreducibility of
\rho_{0}.

We note that in the above proof we haven’t made full use of the equality
S_{\varphi}=\widetilde{S}_{\psi}
but only the fact that
S_{\varphi}1=\widetilde{S}_{\psi}1.
We can also replace the constant function by the monomials
\zeta_{\alpha}(w)=w^{\alpha}
for a multi-index
\alpha.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose
T
and
S
are bounded linear operators on
\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{C}}^{n})
where
T
commutes with
\rho_{0}(a),a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
whereas
S
commutes with
\rho_{0}(ib)
for all
b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.
If
T\zeta_{\alpha}=S\zeta_{\alpha}
for some
\alpha,
then
T=S=cI
for some constant
c.

Thus we see that Hardy’s theorem is apparently stronger than the irreducibility of
\rho_{0}.
It is not known if they are equivalent, though we believe they are not. However, we can prove the irreducibility of
\rho_{0}
without recourse to Hardy’s theorem. In the following we give another proof of irreducibility which again does not require the full power of the hypothesis.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
T
and
S
are as in the previous theorem. If
T\zeta_{\alpha}=S\zeta_{\alpha}
for all
|\alpha|\leq 1,
then
T=S=cI
for some constant
c.

We prove these two theorems in Section 2.1 using some properties of the Gauss-Bargmann transform.
Generalising the Fock spaces, we now consider the following family of Fock spaces
\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})
indexed by
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
which coincide with the classical Fock space
\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})
when
\lambda=0.
These spaces are related to the twisted Bergman spaces
\mathcal{B}_{t}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})
studied in
[
8
]
which appear naturally in connection with the heat kernel transform or the Segal-Bargmann transform on the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}.
Let us consider the weight function

w_{\lambda}(z,w)=c_{\lambda}\,e^{\lambda\operatorname{Im}(z\cdot\bar{w})}\,e^{-\frac{1}{2}\lambda(\coth\lambda)|(z,w)|^{2}},\,\,\,(z,w)\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2n}.

Then
\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})
is defined as the space of all entire functions
F
on
{\mathbb{C}}^{2n}
which are square integrable with respect to the measure
w_{\lambda}(z,w)\,dz\,dw.
We equip
\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})
with the norm

\|F\|_{\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}}^{2}=\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{2n}}|F(z,w)|^{2}\,w_{\lambda}(z,w)\,dz\,dw.

Note that when
\lambda=0,\,w_{0}(z,w)=c_{0}\,e^{-\frac{1}{2}|(z,w)|^{2}}
and hence
\mathcal{F}^{0}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})=\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n}),
the standard Fock space of entire functions on
{\mathbb{C}}^{2n}
which are square integrable with respect to the Gaussian measure
d\nu(z,w)=w_{0}(z,w)\,dz\,dw=c_{0}\,e^{-\frac{1}{2}|(z,w)|^{2}}\,dz\,dw.

On the twisted Fock spaces
\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})
we consider the following family of operators
\rho_{\lambda}(a,b)
indexed by
(a,b)\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2n}:

\rho_{\lambda}(a,b)F(z,w)=e^{\frac{\lambda}{2}\operatorname{Im}(a\cdot\bar{b})}\,e^{-\frac{\lambda}{4}(\coth\lambda)(|a|^{2}+|b|^{2})}\,e^{-i\frac{\lambda}{2}(w\cdot\bar{a}-z\cdot\bar{b})}\,e^{\frac{\lambda}{2}(\coth\lambda)(z\cdot\bar{a}+w\cdot\bar{b})}\,F(z-a,w-b).

It turns out that
\rho_{\lambda}(a,b)
is a unitary operator whose adjoint is given by
\rho_{\lambda}(a,b)^{\ast}=\rho_{\lambda}(-a,-b).
Moreover, by a direct calculation we can check that

\rho_{\lambda}(a,b)\,\rho_{\lambda}(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})=e^{i\frac{\lambda}{2}\operatorname{Re}(b\cdot\bar{a^{\prime}}-a\cdot\bar{b^{\prime}})}\,e^{-i\frac{\lambda}{2}(\coth\lambda)\operatorname{Im}(a\cdot\bar{a^{\prime}}+b\cdot\bar{b^{\prime}})}\,\rho_{\lambda}(a+a^{\prime},b+b^{\prime}).

When
\lambda=0,
it is well known that
\rho_{0}(a,b,t)=e^{-it}\,\rho_{0}(a,b)
defines an irreducible unitary representation of the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{2n}={\mathbb{C}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
equipped with the group law

(a,b,t)(a^{\prime},b^{\prime},t^{\prime})=(a+a^{\prime},b+b^{\prime},t+t^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Im}(a\cdot\bar{a^{\prime}}+b\cdot\bar{b^{\prime}}))

on the Fock space
\mathcal{F}^{0}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n}).
But for
\lambda\neq 0,
it is not possible to choose a homomorphism
\varphi_{\lambda}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow S^{1}
such that
\rho_{\lambda}(a,b,t)=\varphi_{\lambda}(t)\rho_{\lambda}(a,b)
is a representation of
\mathbb{H}^{2n}
on
\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n}).

However, the composition formula for
\rho_{\lambda}(a,b)\,\rho_{\lambda}(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})
suggests that we equip
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{C}}
with the group law

(1.4)

(a,b,t)(a^{\prime},b^{\prime},t^{\prime})=\big(a+a^{\prime},b+b^{\prime},t+t^{\prime}-\frac{\lambda}{2}\operatorname{Re}(b\cdot\bar{a^{\prime}}-a\cdot\bar{b^{\prime}})+\frac{\lambda}{2}(\coth\lambda)\operatorname{Im}(a\cdot\bar{a^{\prime}}+b\cdot\bar{b^{\prime}})\big).

We denote this group by
\mathbb{H}_{\lambda}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
and note that
\mathbb{H}_{\lambda}^{n}(\mathbb{R})=\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
is a subgroup of
\mathbb{H}^{n}_{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}})
which is isomorphic to the real Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}.
Indeed, when
(a,b,t)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R})
the inherited group law from
\mathbb{H}_{\lambda}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
is a deformation of the group law on
\mathbb{H}^{n}.
When
\lambda=0
we recover the standard Heisenberg group,
\mathbb{H}^{n}_{0}({\mathbb{C}})=\mathbb{H}^{2n},
and for this reason we would like to call
\mathbb{H}^{n}_{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}})
the twisted Heisenberg group. We also observe that
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times i\mathbb{R}^{n}\times{\mathbb{C}}
is another subgroup which is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}.
Moreover, by defining

\rho_{\lambda}(a,b,t)=e^{-it}\,\rho_{\lambda}(a,b)

we immediately see that
\rho_{\lambda}
defines a unitary representation of
\mathbb{H}_{\lambda}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
on
\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n}).

Theorem 1.3
.

For each
\lambda\neq 0,\rho_{\lambda}
is an irreducible unitary representation of
\mathbb{H}_{\lambda}^{n}({\mathbb{C}}).
However, its restriction to
\mathbb{H}_{\lambda}^{n}(\mathbb{R})
is not irreducible.

In a recent work
[
6
]
the authors have characterised all bounded linear operators
S
on
\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})
that commute with
\rho_{\lambda}(a,b)
for all
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.
They have shown that such operators are of the form

(1.5)

\displaystyle S_{\varphi}F(z,w)=\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{2n}}F(a,b)\varphi(z-\bar{a},w-\bar{b})e^{\frac{1}{2}\lambda(\coth\lambda)(z\cdot\bar{a}+w\cdot\bar{b})}e^{-\frac{i}{2}\lambda(w\cdot\bar{a}-z\cdot\bar{b})}w_{\lambda}(a,b)\,da\,db

where
\varphi\in\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})
is given by
\varphi=G_{\lambda}(M)
for a bounded linear operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).
Here
G_{\lambda}:B(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\rightarrow\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})
is the non-commutative analogue of the Gauss-Bargmann transform defined by

(1.6)

\displaystyle G_{\lambda}(M)(z,w)=p_{1}^{\lambda}(z,w)^{-1}\,\operatorname{tr}\left(\pi_{\lambda}(-z,-w)e^{-\frac{1}{2}H(\lambda)}Me^{-\frac{1}{2}H(\lambda)}\right).

Consequently, the ranges of such operators are precisely the subspaces invariant under
\rho_{\lambda}(a,b,t),(a,b,t)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}(\mathbb{R}).

Suppose
V
is a closed subspace of
\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})
which is invariant under
\rho_{\lambda}(a,b,t),(a,b,t)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}.
Then the orthogonal projection
P_{V}:\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})\rightarrow V
commutes with all
\rho_{\lambda}(a,b,t)
and hence
P_{V}=S_{\varphi_{V}}
for a unique
\varphi_{V}=G_{\lambda}(M_{V})\in\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n}).
It can be shown that the associated operator
M_{V}
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is an orthogonal projection. The problem of identifying all the closed subspaces
V
of
\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})
such that
(V,\rho_{\lambda})
is an irreducible unitary representation of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is answered in the following result.

Theorem 1.4
.

With notations as above
(V,\rho_{\lambda})
is irreducible if and only if the projection
M_{V}
is of rank one. Any such
V
can be described as
V=\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})\ast\varphi
where
\varphi=G_{\lambda}(M_{V}),
for a rank one projection
M_{V}
of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Remark 1.5
.

Note that
G_{\lambda}^{\ast}(\varphi)
is a projection if and only if
\varphi\ast\varphi=\varphi.
With such a
\varphi
it is easy to see that
\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})\ast\varphi
is a closed subspce. Indeed, if
F_{n}\ast\varphi\rightarrow F,
then
F\ast\varphi=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}F_{n}\ast\varphi\ast\varphi=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}F_{n}\ast\varphi=F.

Here is the plan of the paper. After recalling results about the Gauss-Bargmann transform
G,
we define its non-commutative version
G_{\lambda}.
We introduce the operators
\rho_{\lambda}(a,b)
and state relevant results from
[
6
]
regarding bounded linear operators on the twisted Fock space
\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n}).
In Section 2, we also introduce the algebra
\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})
. We introduce the group
\mathbb{H}^{n}_{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}})
is Section 3 and prove the irreducibility of the representation
\rho_{\lambda}.

2.
Gauss-Bargmann transforms and some

algebras of entire functions

2.1.
Gauss-Bargmann transform- the abelian case:

In the classical euclidean setting the Bargmann transform sets up the unitary equivalence between
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{C}}^{n}).
It is convenient to consider the Gauss-Bargmann transform rather than the Bargmann transform. Let
d\gamma(\xi)=e^{-|\xi|^{2}}d\xi
and define
G
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},d\gamma)
by

Gf(z)=e^{\frac{1}{4}z^{2}}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(\xi)e^{iz\cdot\xi}\,d\gamma(\xi).

Note that
Gf(z)=Bg(iz)
where
g(\xi)=f(\xi)e^{-\frac{1}{2}|\xi|^{2}}
and hence
G:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},d\gamma)\rightarrow\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{C}}^{n})
is unitary. As
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},d\gamma),
the image of
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
under
G,
which we denote by
\mathcal{A}({\mathbb{C}}^{n}),
is a subspace of
\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{C}}^{n}).
We can define a convolution structure on this subspace making it into a commutative Banach algebra.

As proved in
[
11
]
every
\varphi\in\mathcal{A}({\mathbb{C}}^{n})
defines a bounded linear operator
S_{\varphi}.
By defining
F\ast\varphi=S_{\varphi}F
it has been proved that
G^{\ast}(F\ast\varphi)=G^{\ast}(F)\,G^{\ast}(\varphi)

and
G^{\ast}(\varphi)=m\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).
This shows that
F\ast\varphi\in\mathcal{A}({\mathbb{C}}^{n})
if both
F,\varphi\in\mathcal{A}({\mathbb{C}}^{n}).
We equip the algebra
\mathcal{A}({\mathbb{C}}^{n})
with the norm defined by

\|\varphi\|=\|S_{\varphi}\|_{\text{op}}=\|G^{\ast}(\varphi)\|_{\infty}.

For
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{A}({\mathbb{C}}^{n}),
the function
\varphi\ast\psi
corresponds to
S_{\varphi}\circ S_{\psi}
and hence
\|\varphi\ast\psi\|\leq\|\varphi\|\,\|\psi\|.
Thus
\mathcal{A}({\mathbb{C}}^{n})
becomes a Banach algebra. The map
G:L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathcal{A}({\mathbb{C}}^{n})
is an algebra isomorphism.

The case
\alpha=0
of Theorem
1.1
was proved in
[
11
]
. The general case is proved similarly using a stronger version of Hardy’s theorem. We omit the details and move on to proving Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
:
Under the hypothesis of the theorem we have
T=S_{\varphi}
and
S=\widetilde{S}_{\psi}.
The assumption
T1=S1
gives
\varphi=\psi.
We claim that
S_{\varphi}\zeta_{j}(z)=(-\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}+\frac{1}{2}z_{j})\varphi(z)
where
\zeta_{j}(w)=\frac{1}{2}w_{j}.
It is enough to prove the claim when
z=a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.
To see this, we start with the observation that the change of variables
w\rightarrow a-\bar{w}
in the definition of
S_{\varphi}\zeta_{j}
yields

S_{\varphi}\zeta_{j}(a)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}(a_{j}-\bar{w}_{j})\varphi(w)\,e^{\frac{1}{2}a\cdot\bar{w}}\,e^{-\frac{1}{2}|w|^{2}}\,dw.

The first term is equal to
\frac{1}{2}a_{j}\varphi(a)
whereas the second term is

-\frac{\partial}{\partial a_{j}}\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}\varphi(w)\,e^{\frac{1}{2}a\cdot\bar{w}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}|w|^{2}}\,dw=-\frac{\partial}{\partial a_{j}}\varphi(a)

which proves the claim. From the relation

\widetilde{S}_{\varphi}=U\circ S_{U^{\ast}\varphi}\circ U^{\ast}
we also have

(U^{\ast}\circ\widetilde{S}_{\varphi})\zeta_{j}(z)=i(-\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}+\frac{1}{2}z_{j})U^{\ast}\varphi(z)=\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial z_{j}}(iz)+\frac{i}{2}z_{j}\varphi(iz).

Thus
S_{\varphi}\zeta_{j}=\widetilde{S}_{\varphi}\zeta_{j}
gives
(\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}+\frac{1}{2}z_{j})\varphi(z)=(-\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}+\frac{1}{2}z_{j})\varphi(z)
or
\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial z_{j}}=0
for any
j=1,2,...,n.
Consequently,
\varphi
reduces to a constant proving the theorem.

2.2.
Gauss-Bargmann transform- non-abelian case:

From now onwards let us assume
\lambda\neq 0
and consider the twisted Fock space
\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})
defined in the introduction. The role of the Gauss-Bargmann transform is now played by its twisted analogue
G_{\lambda}
whose definition requires some preparation. Let
\pi_{\lambda}(x,u)=\pi_{\lambda}(x,u,0)
where
\pi_{\lambda}
is the Schrödinger representation of the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}
corresponding to the central character
e^{i\lambda t}.
The representation
\pi_{\lambda}(x,u)
defined for
(x,u)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
has a natural extension
\pi_{\lambda}(z,w)
to
{\mathbb{C}}^{2n}.
These operators are no longer bounded but they are densely defined. Let
H(\lambda)=-\Delta+\lambda^{2}|x|^{2}
be the Hermite operator which is self-adjoint with discrete spectrum. We let
e^{-tH(\lambda)},t>0
to stand for the Hermite semigroup generated by
H(\lambda).

Let
\mathcal{S}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
be the space of all Hilbert-Schmidt operators on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).
We define the Gauss-Bargmann transform
\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}
on
\mathcal{S}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
as follows:

\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}(T)(z,w)=p_{1}^{\lambda}(z,w)^{-1}\,\operatorname{tr}\left(\pi_{\lambda}(-z,-w)Te^{-\frac{1}{2}H(\lambda)}\right).

Here
p_{t}^{\lambda}(z,w)
is the holomorphic extension of the heat kernel associated to the special Hermite operator given explicitly by

(2.1)

\displaystyle p_{t}^{\lambda}(y,v)=(4\pi)^{-n}\left(\frac{\lambda}{\sinh\lambda t}\right)^{n}e^{-\frac{1}{4}\lambda(\coth\lambda t)(y^{2}+v^{2})}.

We refer to
[
8
]
for more about the heat kernel transform associated to the special Hermite operator and the twisted Bergman spaces. In
[
6
]
it has been shown that
\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}
is a unitary operator from
\mathcal{S}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
onto
\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n}).
For any
M\in B(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})),
the operator
e^{-\frac{1}{2}H(\lambda)}M\in\mathcal{S}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
and hence the map
G_{\lambda}(M)=\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}(e^{-\frac{1}{2}H(\lambda)}M)
takes
B(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
into a subspace of
\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})
which we denote by
\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n}).
As in the abelian case we can define a convolution structure on
\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})
which will make it into a non-commutative Banach algebra isomorphic to
B(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})).

We can also define the Gauss-Bargmann transform as a map from
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
onto
\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n}).
For this we use the Weyl transfom which takes functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
into operators on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
defined by

\pi_{\lambda}(f)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}f(x,y)\,\pi_{\lambda}(x,y)\,dx\,dy.

It is well known that
\pi_{\lambda}
takes
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
onto
\mathcal{S}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})).
The Plancherel theorem for the Weyl transform reads as

(2\pi)^{-n}|\lambda|^{n}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}|f(x,y)|^{2}\,dx\,dy=\|\pi_{\lambda}(f)\|_{HS}^{2}.

We note in passing the interesting relation
\pi_{\lambda}(p_{t}^{\lambda})=e^{-tH(\lambda)}.
Thus for a suitable constant the map
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\lambda}=d_{\lambda}\,\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}\circ\pi_{\lambda}
is a unitary operator between
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
and
\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n}).
We will make use of this relation in defining the operators
\rho_{\lambda}.
For the results mentioned here and for more on Weyl transform we refer to
[
10
]
.

2.3.
The operators
\rho_{\lambda}(a,b)
:

The convolution on the Heisenberg group gives rise to a family of convolutions for functions on
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}.
These are known as
\lambda
-twisted convolutions (or simply twisted convolutions) and defined by

f\ast_{\lambda}g(x,u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}f(x-a,u-b)g(a,b)\,e^{i\frac{\lambda}{2}(u\cdot a-x\cdot b)}\,da\,db.

By defining the twisted translation
\tau_{\lambda}(a,b)
of functions on
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
by

(2.2)

\displaystyle\tau_{\lambda}(a,b)g(x,u)=g(x-a,u-b)\,e^{-i\frac{\lambda}{2}(u\cdot a-x\cdot b)}

we can write the twisted convolution as

f\ast_{\lambda}g(x,u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}f(a,b)\,\tau_{\lambda}(a,b)g(x,u)\,da\,db.

For easy reference we record some relations between the two families of unitary operators
\tau_{\lambda}(a,b)
and
\pi_{\lambda}(x,u).
First of all we have

\pi_{\lambda}(a,b)\pi_{\lambda}(x,u)=\pi_{\lambda}(x+a,u+b)e^{-i\frac{\lambda}{2}(u\cdot a-x\cdot b)}.

Recalling the definition of
\pi_{\lambda}(f)
another easy calculation shows that

\pi_{\lambda}(a,b)\pi_{\lambda}(f)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}f(x,u)\pi_{\lambda}(x+a,u+b)e^{-i\frac{\lambda}{2}(u\cdot a-x\cdot b)}dx\,du.

In other words, the twisted translation satisfies the relation
\pi_{\lambda}(a,b)\pi_{\lambda}(f)=\pi_{\lambda}(\tau_{\lambda}(a,b)f).
This translates into the property

\tau_{\lambda}(a,b)(f\ast_{\lambda}g)=\tau_{\lambda}(a,b)f\ast_{\lambda}g

for the
\lambda
-twisted translation of
f
with
g.

By conjugating the unitary operators
\tau_{\lambda}(a,b)
with
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\lambda},
we obtain the unitary operators

\widetilde{\rho}_{\lambda}(a,b)=\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\lambda}\circ\tau_{\lambda}(a,b)\circ\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\lambda}^{\ast}

acting on
\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n}).
If
F=\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}(T)
with
T=\pi_{\lambda}(f),f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
we see that

\widetilde{\rho}_{\lambda}(a,b)F(z,w)=e^{\frac{1}{4}\lambda(\coth\lambda)(z^{2}+w^{2})}\,\operatorname{tr}\left(\pi_{\lambda}(-z,-w)\pi_{\lambda}(a,b)Te^{-\frac{1}{2}H(\lambda)}\right).

In the above calculation we have used the relation
\pi_{\lambda}(a,b)\pi_{\lambda}(f)=\pi_{\lambda}(\tau_{\lambda}(a,b)f).
After simplification we obtain the explicit formula

\widetilde{\rho}_{\lambda}(a,b)F(z,w)=e^{-\frac{\lambda}{4}(\coth\lambda)(|a|^{2}+|b|^{2})}\,e^{-i\frac{\lambda}{2}(w\cdot a-z\cdot b)}\,e^{\frac{\lambda}{2}(\coth\lambda)(z\cdot a+w\cdot b)}\,F(z-a,w-b).

Though we have started with
(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
we can extend the definition of
\widetilde{\rho}_{\lambda}(a,b)
for
(a,b)\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2n}
by setting

(2.3)

\widetilde{\rho}_{\lambda}(a,b)F(z,w)=e^{-\frac{\lambda}{4}(\coth\lambda)(|a|^{2}+|b|^{2})}\,e^{-i\frac{\lambda}{2}(w\cdot\bar{a}-z\cdot\bar{b})}\,e^{\frac{\lambda}{2}(\coth\lambda)(z\cdot\bar{a}+w\cdot\bar{b})}\,F(z-a,w-b).

By slightly modifying the representation
\widetilde{\rho}_{\lambda}
we define

\rho_{\lambda}(a,b)=e^{\frac{\lambda}{2}\operatorname{Im}(a\cdot\bar{b})}\,\widetilde{\rho}_{\lambda}(a,b).
Note that for
(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n},\rho_{\lambda}(a,b)=\widetilde{\rho}_{\lambda}(a,b)
and
\rho_{\lambda}(ia,ib)=\widetilde{\rho}_{\lambda}(ia,ib).

2.4.
Operators commuting with
\rho_{\lambda}(a,b)
:

In an earlier work with Rahul Garg
[
6
]
we studied bounded linear operators on the twisted Fock spaces
\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})
that commute with
\rho_{\lambda}(a,b)
for all
(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}.
They turned out to be operators of the form

(2.4)

\displaystyle S_{\varphi}F(z,w)=\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{2n}}F(a,b)\varphi(z-\bar{a},w-\bar{b})e^{\frac{1}{2}\lambda(\coth\lambda)(z\cdot\bar{a}+w\cdot\bar{b})}e^{-\frac{i}{2}\lambda(w\cdot\bar{a}-z\cdot\bar{b})}w_{\lambda}(a,b)\,da\,db

for some
\varphi\in\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n}).
These are densely defined operators and in
[
6
]
a necessary and sufficient on
\varphi
was found for them to be bounded. The condition involves the non-abelian analogue of the Gauss-Bargmann transform
G
given by the map
G_{\lambda}:B(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\rightarrow\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})

(2.5)

\displaystyle G_{\lambda}(M)(z,w)=p_{1}^{\lambda}(z,w)^{-1}\,\operatorname{tr}\left(\pi_{\lambda}(-z,-w)e^{-\frac{1}{2}H(\lambda)}Me^{-\frac{1}{2}H(\lambda)}\right).

Theorem 2.1
.

For any
\varphi\in\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})
consider the operator
S_{\varphi}
defined as in (
2.4
). Then it is bounded on
\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})
if and only if
\varphi=G_{\lambda}(M)
for some
M\in B(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})).

As a corollary to the above theorem the authors obtained the following representation theorem for the operators
S_{\varphi}.
Let
\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}^{\ast}
denote the adjoint of
\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}.

Corollary 2.2
.

For any bounded linear operator
S_{\varphi}
on
\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})
let
M
be the operator associated to
\varphi
as in Theorem
2.1
. Then

S_{\varphi}F=\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}\left(\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}^{\ast}F\circ M\right)
for any
F\in\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n}).

Along with the operators
S_{\varphi}
the authors in
[
6
]
also considered operators of the form
\widetilde{S}_{\varphi}
on
\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})
defined by

(2.6)

\displaystyle\widetilde{S}_{\varphi}F(z,w)=\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{2n}}F(a,b)\varphi(z+\bar{a},w+\bar{b})e^{\frac{1}{2}\lambda(\coth\lambda)(z\cdot\bar{a}+w\cdot\bar{b})}e^{-\frac{i}{2}\lambda(w\cdot\bar{a}-z\cdot\bar{b})}w_{\lambda}(a,b)\,da\,db.

As in the classical setting, these two classes of operators are related via the unitary operator
U
on
\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})
defined by
UF(z,w)=F(-iz,-iw).
Indeed, it is easy to see that

(2.7)

\displaystyle U^{\ast}\circ S_{\varphi}\circ U=\widetilde{S}_{U^{\ast}\varphi}.

Since the operators
S_{\varphi}
commute with
\rho_{\lambda}(a,b)
for all
(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n},
in view of the relation (
2.7
), we infer that the operators
\widetilde{S}_{\varphi}
commute with
U^{\ast}\circ\rho_{\lambda}(a,b)\circ U
for all
(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}.
An easy calculation shows that for
(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n},U^{\ast}\circ\rho_{\lambda}(a,b)\circ U=\rho_{\lambda}(-ia,-ib)
and hence
\widetilde{S}_{\varphi}
commutes with
\rho_{\lambda}(ia,ib)
for all
(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}.

As in the classical setting (see
[
11
]
), there is an uncertainty regarding the simultaneous boundedness of
S_{\varphi}
and
\widetilde{S}_{\varphi}.

Theorem 2.3
.

For any non constant
\varphi\in\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})
at least one of the operators
S_{\varphi}
and
\widetilde{S}_{\varphi}
fails to be bounded on
\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}.

The analogue of this theorem in the classical setting was proved by appealing to Hardy’s theorem for the Fourier transform on
\mathbb{R}^{n},
see
[
11
]
for the proof. In a similar vein, the above theorem was proved in
[
6
]
by means of Hardy’s theorem for the Weyl transform.
As we have
U^{\ast}\circ S_{U\varphi}\circ U=\widetilde{S}_{\varphi},
the above theorem can be viewed as the following statement about the subspace
\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})=G_{\lambda}(B(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})))\subset\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})
under the action of
U
: if
\varphi\in\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n}),
then
U\varphi
never belongs to
\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})
unless
\varphi
is a constant.

2.5.
An algebra of entire functions

Let
\mathcal{A^{\lambda}}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})
stand for the subspace of
\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})
consisting of those
\varphi
for which
S_{\varphi}
is bounded. In view of Theorem
2.1
we know that
\varphi\in\mathcal{A^{\lambda}}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})
if and only if
\varphi=G_{\lambda}(M)
for some
M\in B(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})).
Thus
\mathcal{A^{\lambda}}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})
is the image of
B(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
under
G_{\lambda}
which turns out to be a Banach algebra under a suitable convolution.

Let us write
M=G_{\lambda}^{\ast}(\varphi)
if
\varphi=G_{\lambda}(M)
and note that
\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}^{\ast}(\varphi)=e^{-\frac{1}{2}H(\lambda)}G_{\lambda}^{\ast}(\varphi).
We can rewrite the result of Corollary
2.2

as
\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}^{\ast}(F\ast_{\lambda}\varphi)=\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}^{\ast}(F)\,G_{\lambda}^{\ast}(\varphi).
It follows that if
F
also comes from
\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})
we have the relation

G_{\lambda}^{\ast}(S_{\varphi}F)=G_{\lambda}^{\ast}(F)\,G_{\lambda}^{\ast}(\varphi).
This suggests that by defining the convolution
F\ast_{\lambda}\varphi=S_{\varphi}F,
we can make the subspace
\mathcal{A^{\lambda}}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})
into a non-commutative algebra. We equip
\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})
with the operator norm:
\|\varphi\|=\|S_{\varphi}\|_{\text{op}}.

Proposition 2.4
.

For any
\lambda\neq 0,\,\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})
is a non-commutative Banach algebra which is isometrically isomorphic to
B(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})).

Proof.

As
\varphi\ast_{\lambda}\psi
corresponds to the operator
S_{\psi}\circ S_{\varphi}
it follows that
\|\varphi\ast_{\lambda}\psi\|\leq\|\varphi\|\|\psi\|.
The map
G_{\lambda}:B(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\rightarrow\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})
is an isometric isomorphism. Hence the completeness of
\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})
follows from that of
B(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})).

∎

Remark 2.5
.

Inside the algebra
\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})
there is a commutative subalgebra which is easy to describe. For each bounded function
m
on
\mathbb{R}
let us consider the bounded operator

m(H(\lambda))=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}m((2k+n)|\lambda|)\,P_{k}(\lambda)

where
P_{k}(\lambda)
are the spectral projections associated to the Hermite operator
H(\lambda).
If we define

\mathcal{A}_{0}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})=\{\varphi\in\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}:G_{\lambda}^{\ast}(\varphi)=m(H(\lambda))\}

it follows that it is a commutative subalgebra. Moreover, every
\varphi\in\mathcal{A}_{0}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})
is given by the formula

\varphi(z,w)=(2\pi)^{-n}|\lambda|^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}m((2k+n)|\lambda|)\,e^{-2t(2k+n)|\lambda|}\,\varphi_{k,\lambda}^{n-1}(z,w).

Here
\varphi_{k,\lambda}^{n-1}(z,w)
is the holomorphic extension of the Laguerre function

\varphi_{k,\lambda}^{n-1}(x,u)=L_{k}^{n-1}(\frac{1}{2}|\lambda|(|x|^{2}+u|^{2}))\,e^{-\frac{1}{4}|\lambda|(|x|^{2}+|u|^{2})}.

It would be interesting to study some properties of this subalgebra such as identifying the Gelfand spectrum etc.

3.
The twisted Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}_{\lambda}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
and

the representation
\rho_{\lambda}

3.1.
The group
\mathbb{H}_{\lambda}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
:

We begin with the definition of
\rho_{\lambda}(a,b)
: by slightly modifying
the operators
\widetilde{\rho}_{\lambda}(a,b)
we define

\rho_{\lambda}(a,b)F(z,w)=e^{\frac{\lambda}{2}\operatorname{Im}(a\cdot\bar{b})}\,\widetilde{\rho}_{\lambda}(a,b)F(z,w).

We record the following properties of
\rho_{\lambda}(a,b)
which can be verified by direct calculation.

Proposition 3.1
.

For any
(a,b),(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2n},
we have the composition law

\rho_{\lambda}(a,b)\,\rho_{\lambda}(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})=e^{-i\frac{\lambda}{2}\operatorname{Re}(a\cdot\bar{b^{\prime}}-b\cdot\bar{a^{\prime}})}\,e^{-i\frac{\lambda}{2}(\coth\lambda)\operatorname{Im}(a\cdot\bar{a^{\prime}}+b\cdot\bar{b^{\prime}})}\,\rho_{\lambda}(a+a^{\prime},b+b^{\prime}).

Moreover,
\rho_{\lambda}(a,b)^{\ast}=\rho_{\lambda}(-a,-b)
and hence the operators
\rho_{\lambda}(a,b)
are unitary on
\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n}).

Proof.

It is easy to see that for any
(a,b)\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2n},\widetilde{\rho}_{\lambda}(a,b)F\in\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})
whenever
F\in\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n}).
Indeed by direct calculation we can verify that

\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{2n}}|\widetilde{\rho}_{\lambda}(a,b)F(z,w)|^{2}\,w_{\lambda}(z,w)\,dz\,dw=e^{-\lambda\operatorname{Im}(a\cdot\bar{b})}\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{2n}}|F(z,w)|^{2}w_{\lambda}(z,w)dz\,dw.

In order to find the adjoint, let us try to prove
\langle\rho_{\lambda}(a,b)F,G\rangle=\langle F,\rho_{\lambda}(-a,-b)G\rangle
for
F,G\in\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n}).
By defining

\Theta_{(a,b)}(z,w)=e^{\frac{1}{2}\lambda(\coth\lambda)(z\cdot\bar{a}+w\cdot\bar{b})}e^{-\frac{i}{2}\lambda(w\cdot\bar{a}-z\cdot\bar{b})}w_{\lambda}(z,w)

we observe that
\langle\rho_{\lambda}(a,b)F,G\rangle
is given by the integral

e^{\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Im}(a\cdot\bar{b})}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\lambda(\coth\lambda)(|a|^{2}+|b|^{2})}\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{2n}}F(z-a,w-b)\Theta_{(a,b)}(z,w)\,\overline{G(z,w)}\,dz\,dw.

Our claim will be proved by showing that

\Theta_{(a,b)}(z+a,w+b)=\overline{\Theta_{(-a,-b)}(z,w)}.
Recalling the definition of
w_{\lambda}(z,w)
we calculate that

\Theta_{(a,b)}(z+a,w+b)=e^{-\frac{i}{2}\lambda(w\cdot\bar{a}-z\cdot\bar{b})}\,e^{\lambda\operatorname{Im}(z\cdot\bar{b}-w\cdot\bar{a})}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\lambda(\coth\lambda)(a\cdot\bar{z}+b\cdot\bar{w})}w_{\lambda}(z,w).

The first two factors on the right hand side combine to yield
e^{-\frac{i}{2}\lambda(\bar{w}\cdot a-\bar{z}\cdot b)}
which proves the required property of
\Theta.

By a simple calculation, it is easy to very that

\widetilde{\rho}_{\lambda}(a,b)\,\widetilde{\rho}_{\lambda}(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})=e^{-i\frac{\lambda}{2}(a\cdot\bar{b^{\prime}}-b\cdot\bar{a^{\prime}})}\,e^{-i\frac{\lambda}{2}(\coth\lambda)\operatorname{Im}(a\cdot\bar{a^{\prime}}+b\cdot\bar{b^{\prime}})}\,\widetilde{\rho}_{\lambda}(a+a^{\prime},b+b^{\prime}).

Since
\rho_{\lambda}(a,b)=e^{\frac{\lambda}{2}\operatorname{Im}(a\cdot\bar{b})}\,\widetilde{\rho}_{\lambda}(a,b)
the stated composition rule for
\rho_{\lambda}(a,b)
follows from that of
\widetilde{\rho}_{\lambda}(a,b).
Finally, from the composition rule we observe that
\rho_{\lambda}(a,b)^{\ast}\rho_{\lambda}(a,b)F=F
and hence
\rho_{\lambda}(a,b)
are unitary.
∎

The product rule for
\rho_{\lambda}(a,b)
suggests that we equip
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
with the group law (
1.4
):

(a,b,t)(a^{\prime},b^{\prime},t^{\prime})=(a+a^{\prime},b+b^{\prime},t+t^{\prime}-\frac{\lambda}{2}\operatorname{Re}(b\cdot\bar{a^{\prime}}-a\cdot\bar{b^{\prime}})+\frac{\lambda}{2}(\coth\lambda)\operatorname{Im}(a\cdot\bar{a^{\prime}}+b\cdot\bar{b^{\prime}})).

We denote this group by
\mathbb{H}_{\lambda}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
and note that
\mathbb{H}_{\lambda}^{n}=\mathbb{H}_{\lambda}^{n}(\mathbb{R})
is a subgroup of
\mathbb{H}_{\lambda}^{n}({\mathbb{C}}).
Another subgroup, which is isomorphic to
\mathbb{H}_{\lambda}^{n}
is given by
\mathbb{H}_{\lambda}^{n}(i\mathbb{R}).
We extend the the definition of
\rho_{\lambda}
to
\mathbb{H}_{\lambda}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
by setting

(3.1)

\rho_{\lambda}(a,b,t)=e^{-it}\,\,\rho_{\lambda}(a,b).

It is then easy to see that
\rho_{\lambda}
is a unitary representation of
\mathbb{H}_{\lambda}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
on the twisted Fock space.

3.2.
The irreducibility of
\rho_{\lambda}
:

By using the results stated in Section 2.4 and proved in
[
6
]
, we can easily see that the representation
\rho_{\lambda}
of
\mathbb{H}_{\lambda}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
is irreducible. Indeed, if a bounded linear operator
T
on
\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})
commutes with
\rho_{\lambda}(g)
for all
g\in\mathbb{H}_{\lambda}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
then it will commute with
\rho_{\lambda}(a,b)
for all
(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
and hence
T=S_{\varphi}
defined in
2.4
for some
\varphi
satisfying the condition given in Theorem
2.1
. From Corollary
2.2
we infer that
T1=S_{\varphi}1=\varphi.
As
T
commutes with
\rho_{\lambda}(ia,ib),(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
we also know that
T=\widetilde{S}_{\psi}.
In view of
2.7
we also infer that
T1=\widetilde{S}_{\psi}1=\psi.
And hence
\varphi=\psi
and both operators
S_{\varphi}
and
\widetilde{S}_{\varphi}
are bounded. But then by Theorem
2.3
this is possible only if
\varphi
is a constant which means
T=cI
proving the irreducibility of
\rho.

But the above proof depends on Hardy’s theorem for the Weyl transform which is much stronger than the irreducibility of
\rho_{\lambda}.
In what follows we give a direct proof following the same line of arguments used in proving Theorem
2.3

Proof of Theorem
1.3
:
As above assuming that a bounded linear operator
T
commutes with all
\rho_{\lambda}(g)
we conclude that there exists
\varphi=G_{\lambda}(M)
such that
S_{\varphi}=T=\widetilde{S}_{\varphi}.

Recall that

S_{\varphi}F(z,w)=\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{2n}}F(a,b)\varphi(z-\bar{a},w-\bar{b})e^{\frac{1}{2}\lambda(\coth\lambda)(z\cdot\bar{a}+w\cdot\bar{b})}e^{-\frac{i}{2}\lambda(w\cdot\bar{a}-z\cdot\bar{b})}w_{\lambda}(a,b)\,da\,db.

If we assume that
z,w\in\mathbb{R}^{n},
then we can make a change of variables to verify that

S_{\varphi}F(z,w)=\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{2n}}F(z-\bar{a},w-\bar{b})\varphi(a,b)\,e^{\frac{1}{2}\lambda(\coth\lambda)(z\cdot\bar{a}+w\cdot\bar{b})}e^{\frac{i}{2}\lambda(w\cdot\bar{a}-z\cdot\bar{b})}w_{-\lambda}(a,b)\,da\,db.

Since we already know that
S_{\varphi}1=\varphi,
the above equation shows that

(3.2)

\varphi(z,w)=\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{2n}}\varphi(a,b)\,e^{\frac{1}{2}\lambda(\coth\lambda)(z\cdot\bar{a}+w\cdot\bar{b})}e^{\frac{i}{2}\lambda(w\cdot\bar{a}-z\cdot\bar{b})}w_{-\lambda}(a,b)\,da\,db

which is valid for all
z,w\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
by analytic continuation. For
j=1,2,...,n
let us define

\zeta_{j,0}(z,w)=\frac{1}{2}\lambda\left((\coth\lambda)z_{j}-iw_{j}\right),\,\,\,\zeta_{0,j}(z,w)=\frac{1}{2}\lambda\left((\coth\lambda)w_{j}+iz_{j}\right).

From (
3.2
) we obtain the following relations:

S_{\varphi}\zeta_{j,0}=-\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial z_{j}}+\zeta_{j,0}\,\varphi,\,\,S_{\varphi}\zeta_{0,j}=-\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial w_{j}}+\zeta_{0,j}\,\varphi.

Using the relation
U^{\ast}\circ S_{\varphi}\circ U=\widetilde{S}_{U^{\ast}\varphi}
we also have

\widetilde{S}_{\varphi}\zeta_{j,0}=\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial z_{j}}+\zeta_{j,0}\,\varphi,\,\,\widetilde{S}_{\varphi}\zeta_{0,j}=\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial w_{j}}+\zeta_{0,j}\,\varphi.

Since
S_{\varphi}=\widetilde{S}_{\varphi},
from the above we conclude that
\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial z_{j}}=\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial w_{j}}=0
for all
j
and hence
\varphi
reduces to a constant.
This proves that
\rho_{\lambda}
is irreducible.

Remark 3.2
.

Note that the above proof does not require the full power of the equality
S_{\varphi}=\widetilde{S}_{\varphi}
but uses only the fact that these two operators agree with all monomials of degree one.

3.3.
The restriction of
\rho_{\lambda}
to
\mathbb{H}_{\lambda}^{n}
:

Though
\rho_{\lambda}
is an irreducible unitary representation of
\mathbb{H}_{\lambda}^{n}({\mathbb{C}}),
it turns out that its restriction to
\mathbb{H}_{\lambda}^{n}(\mathbb{R})
is not irreducible. Note that for
(a,b,t)\in\mathbb{H}_{\lambda}^{n}(\mathbb{R}),\rho_{\lambda}(a,b,t)=e^{-it}\rho_{\lambda}(a,b).
From Theorem
2.1
we know that operators of the form
S_{\varphi}
with
\varphi=G_{\lambda}(M),M\in B(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
commute with
\rho_{\lambda}(a,b)
for all
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.
Consequently range of such operators are invariant under
\rho_{\lambda}(g),g\in\mathbb{H}^{n}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}).
It is possible to choose
\varphi
so that the closure of the range of
S_{\varphi}
is a proper subspace of
\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})
and hence
\rho_{\lambda}
is not irreducible.

In what follows we try to characterise closed subspaces of
\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})
on which
\rho_{\lambda}
becomes an irreducible unitary representation of
\mathbb{H}^{n}.
Let
V\subset\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})
be a closed subspace invariant under
\rho_{\lambda}(g),g\in\mathbb{H}^{n}.
Then the orthogonal projection
P_{V}:\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})\rightarrow V
is a bounded linear operator which commutes with
\rho_{\lambda}(a,b),a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.
Then there exists a unique
\varphi_{V}\in\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})
and
M_{V}\in B(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
such that
\varphi_{V}=G_{\lambda}(M_{V})
and
S_{\varphi_{V}}=P_{V}.
As
P_{V}^{2}=P_{V},
it follows that
\varphi_{V}\ast_{\lambda}\varphi_{V}=\varphi_{V}
and hence
M_{V}=M_{V}^{2}
is an orthogonal projection of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Proof of Theorem
1.4
:
Let
V
be a closed subspace of
\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})
which supports the representation
\rho_{\lambda}.
Let
V=W\oplus W^{\prime}
be the direct sum of two closed subspaces both invariant under
\rho_{\lambda}(g),g\in\mathbb{H}^{n}.
As
P_{V}=P_{W}\oplus P_{W^{\prime}}
it follows that
\varphi_{V}=\varphi_{W}+\varphi_{W^{\prime}}
and
\varphi_{W}\ast_{\lambda}\varphi_{W^{\prime}}=0.
This in turn implies that
M_{V}=M_{W}+M_{W^{\prime}}
and
M_{W}\circ M_{W^{\prime}}=0.

As a consequence we infer that
V
has a proper invariant subspace if and only of the projection
M_{V}
associated to
V
has a decomposition of the form
M_{V}=M_{W}\oplus M_{W^{\prime}}.
This proves the theorem.

Remark 3.3
.

From the above result we see that if the projection
M_{V}
associated to
V
is of rank
k
then we can decompose
V=\oplus_{j=1}^{k}V_{j}
and each
(V_{j},\rho_{\lambda})
is irreducible. Given
M\in B(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
with a spectral decomposition
M=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}\,E_{j}
where the spectral projections are of finite rank, say
d_{j}.
For example, any compact normal operator
M
provides such an example. Then with
\varphi_{j}=G_{\lambda}(E_{j})
the subspaces
V_{j}=\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}({\mathbb{C}}^{2n})\ast_{\lambda}\varphi_{j},
the range of
S_{\varphi_{j}},
supports a primary representation which can be further decomposed into finitely many irreducible subspaces.

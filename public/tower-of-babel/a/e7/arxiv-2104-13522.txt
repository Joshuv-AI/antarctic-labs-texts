Some results on r r -truncated degenerate Poisson Random Variables
Taekyun Kim, DAE SAN KIM, Si-Hyeon Lee, Seong-Ho Park, Lee-Chae Jang

Abstract
. The zero-truncated Poisson distributions are certain discrete probability distributions whose supports are the set of positive integers, which are also known as the conditional Poisson distributions or the positive Poisson distributions. Recently, as a natural extension of those distributions, Kim-Kim studied the zero-truncated degenerate Poisson distributions. In this paper, we introduce the r r -truncated degenerate Poisson random variable with parameter α > 0 \alpha>0 , whose probability mass function is given by p λ , r ​ ( i ) = ( 1 ) i , λ e λ ​ ( α ) − e λ , r ​ ( α ) ​ α i i ! , ( i = r + 1 , r + 2 , r + 3 , … ) p_{\lambda,r}(i)=\cfrac{(1)_{i,\lambda}}{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)}\frac{\alpha^{i}}{i!},\quad(i=r+1,r+2,r+3,\dots) , and investigate various properties of this random variable.

1.
Introduction

It is well known that a random variable
X
, taking on one of the values
0,1,2,\dots
, is said to be the Poisson random variable with parameter
\alpha>0
, if the probability mass function of
X
is given by

p(i)=P\{X=i\}=e^{-\alpha}\frac{\alpha^{i}}{i!},\quad(i=0,1,2,\dots),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [11,13]).

We note that a Poisson random variable indicates how many events occured in a given period of time.
Further, a random variable
X_{r}
, taking on one of the values
r+1,r+2,\dots
, is called the
r
-truncated Poisson random variable with parameter
\alpha>0
, if the probability mass function of
X_{r}
is given by

p_{r}(i)=P\{X_{r}=i\}=\frac{1}{e^{\alpha}-\sum_{l=0}^{r}\frac{\alpha^{l}}{l!}}\frac{\alpha^{i}}{i!},

where
i=r+1,r+2,r+3,\dots
, for
r\geq 0
. In the special case of
r=0
, we obtain the zero-truncated Poisson
distribution.

On the other hand, the degenerate Poisson random variables with parameter
\alpha>0
, whose probability mass function is given by (see (1))

p_{\lambda}(i)=e_{\lambda}^{-1}(\alpha)\frac{\alpha^{i}}{i!}(1)_{i,\lambda}\quad(i=0,1,2,\dots),

were studied by Kim-Kim-Jang-Kim in [10].
In addition, the zero-truncated degenerate Poisson random variables with parameter
\alpha>0
, whose probability mass function is given by

p_{\lambda,0}(i)=\frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-1}\frac{\alpha^{i}}{i!}(1)_{i,\lambda}\quad(i=1,2,3,\dots),

were studied in [9].

The aim of this paper is to generalize the results on the zero-truncated degenerate Poisson distribution in [9] to the case of the
r
-truncated degenerate Poisson distribution as a natural extension of the
r
-truncated Poission distribution. We will obtain, among other things, its expectation, its variance, its
n
-th moment, and its cumulative distribution function.

One motivation for this research is its potential applications to the cornavirus pandemic. It has been spreading unpredictably around the world, terrorizing many people. Although several vaccines for the coronavirus have been developed and many people are getting vaccinated, they have many unexpected side effects as well.
We would like to predict stability of the coronavirus vaccines after
r
-days the vaccines were shot. For this purpose, we study the
r
-truncated degenerate Poisson random variables (see (6)), which has the ‘degenerate factor’
\lambda
reflecting abnormal situations.
Indeed, we think that Theorem 5 is useful in predicting the probability of the coronavirus vaccines becoming stable after the
r
-days of getting vaccinated. Another motivation is applications of various probabilistic methods in studying special numbers and polynomials arising from combinatorics and number theory. For this, we let the reader refer to [5-10,14 and the references therein]. For the rest of this section, we recall the necessary facts that are needed throughout this paper.

For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, the degenerate exponential functions are defined by

(1)

e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=(1+\lambda t)^{\frac{x}{\lambda}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(x)_{k,\lambda}\frac{t^{k}}{k!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [1-3,5-10,14]),

where
(x)_{0,\lambda}=1
,
(x)_{n,\lambda}=x(x-\lambda)\cdots(x-(n-1)\lambda)
,
(n\geq 1)
.

In particular, for
x=1
, we denote
e_{\lambda}^{1}(t)
by
e_{\lambda}(t)
.

In addition, it is convenient to introduce
e_{\lambda,r}(t)
, for any integer
r\geq 0
, given by

(2)

e_{\lambda,r}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{r}(1)_{k,\lambda}\frac{t^{k}}{k!}.

The Bell polynomials are given by

(3)

e^{x(e^{t}-1)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Bel}_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [4,12]).

In light of (
3
), the degenerate Bell polynomials are defined in [9] by

(4)

e_{\lambda}^{-1}(x)e_{\lambda}(xe^{t})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

In particular, for
x=1
,
\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}=\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}(1)
,
(n\geq 0)
, are called the degenerate Bell numbers. Note that

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 1}\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}(x)=\mathrm{Bel}_{n}(x),\quad(n\geq 0).

Let
X
be a Poisson random variable with parameter
\alpha>0
. Then the moment of
X
is given by

E[X^{n}]=\mathrm{Bel}_{n}(\alpha),\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [11,13]).

Let
Y
be a discrete random variable taking values in the nonnegative integers. Then the probability generating function of
Y
is given by

F(t)=E[t^{Y}]=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}p(i)t^{i},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [11,13]),

where
p(i)=P\{Y=i\}
is the probability mass function of
Y
.

The degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind
S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)
are defined by

(x)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{l=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n.l)(x)_{l},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [5]),

or equivalently by

\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\bigg)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Here
(x)_{0}=1,\ (x)_{n}=x(x-1)(x-2)\cdots(x-n+1)
,
(n\geq 1)
.

Now, we consider the
r
-truncated degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind

S_{2,\lambda}^{[r]}(n,kr+k)
given by

(5)

\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(e_{\lambda}(t)-e_{\lambda,r}(t)\bigg)^{k}=\sum_{n=kr+k}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}^{[r]}(n,kr+k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Note that

S_{2,\lambda}^{[0]}(n,k)=S_{2,\lambda}(n,k),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [5]).

2.
r
-truncated degenerate Poisson random variables

The random variable
X_{\lambda,r}
is called the
r
-truncated degenerate Poisson random variable with parameter
\alpha>0
, if the probability mass function of
X_{\lambda,r}
is given by

(6)

p_{\lambda,r}(k)=\cfrac{(1)_{k,\lambda}}{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)}\frac{\alpha^{k}}{k!},

where
k=r+1,r+2,\dots
, with
r\geq 0
.

Here we must observe that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=r+1}^{\infty}p_{\lambda,r}(k)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)}\sum_{k=r+1}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{k}}{k!}(1)_{k,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)}(e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha))

\displaystyle=1.

In addition, we note that

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}p_{\lambda,r}(k)=\frac{1}{e^{\alpha}-\sum_{l=0}^{r}\frac{\alpha^{l}}{l!}}\frac{\alpha^{k}}{k!}

is the probability mass function of the
r
-truncated Poisson random variable with parameter
\alpha>0
.

Let us assume that
X_{\lambda,r}
is the
r
-truncated degenerate Poisson random variable with parameter
\alpha>0
. Then we note that the expectation of
X_{\lambda,r}
is given by

(7)

\displaystyle E[X_{\lambda,r}]\

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{n=r+1}^{\infty}np_{\lambda,r}(n)=\frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)}\sum_{n=r+1}^{\infty}n\frac{\alpha^{n}}{n!}(1)_{n,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\ \frac{\alpha}{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)}\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{n}}{n!}(1)_{n+1,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\ \frac{\alpha}{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)}\bigg(\sum_{n=r+1}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{n}}{n!}(1)_{n+1,\lambda}+\frac{\alpha^{r}}{r!}(1)_{r+1,\lambda}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\ \frac{\alpha}{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)}\bigg(\sum_{n=r+1}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{n}}{n!}(1)_{n,\lambda}(1-n\lambda)+\frac{\alpha^{r}}{r!}(1)_{r+1,\lambda}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\ \alpha-\frac{\alpha\lambda}{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)}\sum_{n=r+1}^{\infty}n\frac{\alpha^{n}}{n!}(1)_{n,\lambda}+\frac{\alpha}{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)}\frac{\alpha^{r}}{r!}(1)_{r+1,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\ \alpha-\alpha\lambda E[X_{\lambda,r}]+\frac{\alpha}{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)}\frac{\alpha^{r}}{r!}(1)_{r+1,\lambda}.

From (
7
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

Let
X_{\lambda,r}
be the
r
-truncated degenerate Poisson random with parameter
\alpha>0
. Then, for
\lambda\neq-\frac{1}{\alpha}
, the expectation of
X_{\lambda,r}
is given by

E[X_{\lambda,r}]=\frac{1}{1+\alpha\lambda}\bigg[\alpha+\frac{\alpha^{r+1}}{r!}\frac{(1)_{r+1,\lambda}}{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)}\bigg].

Now, we observe that

(8)

\displaystyle E[X_{\lambda,r}^{2}]\

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{n=k+1}^{\infty}n^{2}p_{\lambda,r}(n)\ =\ \frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)}\sum_{n=r+1}^{\infty}n^{2}\frac{\alpha^{n}}{n!}(1)_{n,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\ \frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)}\bigg(\sum_{n=r+1}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{n}}{(n-2)!}(1)_{n,\lambda}+\sum_{n=r+1}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{n}}{(n-1)!}(1)_{n,\lambda}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\ \frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)}\bigg(\sum_{n=r+2}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{n}}{(n-2)!}(1)_{n,\lambda}+\sum_{n=r+1}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{n}}{(n-1)!}(1)_{n,\lambda}\bigg)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)}\frac{\alpha^{r+1}}{(r-1)!}(1)_{r+1,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\ \frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)}\bigg(\alpha\sum_{n=r+1}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{n}}{(n-1)!}(1)_{n+1,\lambda}+\sum_{n=r+1}^{\infty}\frac{n\alpha^{n}}{n!}(1)_{n,\lambda}\bigg)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)}r\frac{\alpha^{r+1}}{r!}(1)_{r+1,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\ \frac{\alpha}{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)}\sum_{n=r+1}^{\infty}\frac{n\alpha^{n}}{n!}(1)_{n,\lambda}(1-n\lambda)+E[X_{\lambda,r}]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{r\alpha^{r+1}(1)_{r+1,\lambda}}{r!\big(e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)\big)}

\displaystyle=\ \alpha E[X_{\lambda,r}]-\lambda\alpha E[X_{\lambda,r}^{2}]+E[X_{\lambda,r}]+\frac{\alpha^{r+1}}{r!}\frac{r(1)_{r+1,\lambda}}{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)}.

From (
8
), we note that

(9)

E[X_{\lambda,r}^{2}]=\frac{\alpha+1}{1+\lambda\alpha}E[X_{\lambda,r}]+\frac{1}{1+\lambda\alpha}\frac{r(1)_{r+1,\lambda}}{e_{\lambda}(t)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)}\frac{\alpha^{r+1}}{r!},

where
\lambda\neq-\frac{1}{\alpha}
.

For
\lambda\neq-\frac{1}{\alpha}
, we note that the variance of
X_{\lambda,r}
is given by

(10)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(X_{\lambda,r})\ =\ E[X_{\lambda,r}^{2}]-\big(E[X_{\lambda,r}]\big)^{2}

\displaystyle=\ E[X_{\lambda,r}]\bigg(\frac{\alpha+1}{1+\lambda\alpha}-E[X_{\lambda,r}]\bigg)+\frac{1}{1+\lambda\alpha}\frac{r(1)_{r+1,\lambda}}{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)}\frac{\alpha^{r+1}}{r!}

\displaystyle=\ \frac{1}{1+\alpha\lambda}\bigg(\alpha+\frac{\alpha^{r+1}}{r!}\frac{(1)_{r+1,\lambda}}{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)}\bigg)\bigg(\frac{\alpha+1}{1+\alpha\lambda}-\frac{1}{1+\alpha\lambda}\bigg(\alpha+\frac{\alpha^{r+1}}{r!}\frac{(1)_{r+1,\lambda}}{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)}\bigg)\bigg)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{1+\alpha\lambda}\frac{r(1)_{r+1,\lambda}}{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)}\frac{\alpha^{r+1}}{r!}

\displaystyle=\ \frac{1}{(1+\alpha\lambda)^{2}}\bigg(\alpha+\frac{\alpha^{r+1}}{r!}\frac{(1)_{r+1,\lambda}}{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)}\bigg)\bigg(1-\frac{\alpha^{r+1}}{r!}\frac{(1)_{r+1,\lambda}}{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)}\bigg)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{1+\alpha\lambda}\frac{r(1)_{r+1,\lambda}}{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)}\frac{\alpha^{r+1}}{r!}.

Therefore, by (
10
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

Let
X_{\lambda,r}
be the
r
-truncated degenerate Poisson random variable with parameter
\alpha>0
. For
\lambda\neq-\frac{1}{\alpha}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(X_{\lambda,r})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1+\alpha\lambda)^{2}}\bigg(\alpha+\frac{\alpha^{r+1}}{r!}\frac{(1)_{r+1,\lambda}}{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)}\bigg)\bigg(1-\frac{\alpha^{r+1}}{r!}\frac{(1)_{r+1,\lambda}}{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)}\bigg)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{1+\alpha\lambda}\frac{r(1)_{r+1,\lambda}}{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)}\frac{\alpha^{r+1}}{r!}.

Let us consider the generating function of the moments of the
r
-truncated degenerate Poisson random variable with parameter
\alpha>0
. Then, from (
4
), we have

(11)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}E[X_{\lambda,r}^{n}]\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\

\displaystyle=\ E[e^{X_{\lambda,r}t}]\ =\ \sum_{m=r+1}^{\infty}e^{mt}p_{\lambda,r}(m)

\displaystyle=\ \frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)}\sum_{m=r+1}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{m}}{m!}(1)_{m,\lambda}e^{mt}

\displaystyle=\ \frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)}\bigg(e_{\lambda}(\alpha e^{t})-\sum_{m=0}^{r}\frac{\alpha^{m}}{m!}(1)_{m,\lambda}e^{mt}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\ \frac{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)}{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)}\bigg(e_{\lambda}^{-1}(\alpha)e_{\lambda}(\alpha e^{t})-e_{\lambda}^{-1}(\alpha)\sum_{m=0}^{r}\frac{\alpha^{m}}{m!}(1)_{m,\lambda}e^{mt}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\ \frac{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)}{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)}\bigg(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}(\alpha)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e_{\lambda}^{-1}(\alpha)\sum_{m=0}^{r}\frac{\alpha^{m}}{m!}(1)_{m,\lambda}m^{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)}{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)}\bigg(\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda}^{-1}(\alpha)\sum_{m=0}^{r}\frac{\alpha^{m}}{m!}(1)_{m,\lambda}m^{n}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus, by (
11
), we get the next theorem.

Theorem 3
.

Let
X_{\lambda,r}
be the
r
-truncated degenerate Poisson random variable with parameter
\alpha>0
. For
n\geq 0
, we have

E[X_{\lambda,r}^{n}]=\frac{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)}{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)}\bigg(\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda}^{-1}(\alpha)\sum_{m=0}^{r}\frac{\alpha^{m}}{m!}(1)_{m,\lambda}m^{n}\bigg).

For
x\geq r+1
, we note that the cumulative distribution function is given by

(12)

\displaystyle F_{X_{\lambda,r}}(x)\

\displaystyle=\ P\{X_{\lambda,r}\leq x\}\ =\ \sum_{k=r+1}^{[x]}p_{\lambda,r}(k)

\displaystyle=\ \frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)}\sum_{k=r+1}^{[x]}\frac{\alpha^{k}}{k!}(1)_{k,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\ \frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)}\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{[x]}\frac{\alpha^{k}}{k!}(1)_{k,\lambda}-\sum_{k=0}^{r}\frac{\alpha^{k}}{k!}(1)_{k,\lambda}\bigg).

From (
12
) and (
2
), we can derive the following equation.

(13)

F_{X_{\lambda,r}}(x)=\frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)}\big(e_{\lambda,[x]}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)\big).

Theorem 4
.

Assume that
X_{\lambda,r}
is the
r
-truncated degenerate Poisson random variable with parameter
\alpha>0
. For
x\geq r+1
, the cumulative distribution function of
X_{\lambda,r}
is given by

F_{X_{\lambda,r}}(x)=\frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)}\big(e_{\lambda,[x]}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)\big).

Let us assume that
X_{\lambda,r}^{(1)},X_{\lambda,r}^{(2)},\dots,X_{\lambda,r}^{(k)}
are identically independent
r
-truncated degenerate Poisson random variables with parameter
\alpha>0
, and let

X_{\lambda,r}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}X_{\lambda,r}^{(i)},\quad(k\in\mathbb{N}).

From the probability generating function of random variable, we note that

(14)

\displaystyle E[t^{X_{\lambda,r}^{(i)}}]\

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{n=r+1}^{\infty}t^{n}P[X_{\lambda,r}^{(i)}=n]

\displaystyle=\ \frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)}\sum_{n=r+1}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{n}}{n!}(1)_{n,\lambda}t^{n}

\displaystyle=\ \frac{e_{\lambda}(\alpha t)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha t)}{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)}.

By (
14
), we get

(15)

\displaystyle E[t^{X_{\lambda,r}}]\

\displaystyle=\ \prod_{i=1}^{k}E[t^{X_{\lambda,r}^{(i)}}]

\displaystyle=\ \bigg(\frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha)}\bigg)^{k}k!\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{\lambda}(\alpha t)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha t)\big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\ \frac{k!}{(e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha))^{k}}\sum_{n=kr+k}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}^{[r]}(n,kr+k)\frac{\alpha^{n}t^{n}}{n!}.

On the other hand,

(16)

\displaystyle E[t^{X_{\lambda,r}}]\

\displaystyle=\ E[t^{X_{\lambda,r}^{(1)}+X_{\lambda,r}^{(2)}+\cdots+X_{\lambda,r}^{(k)}}]

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{n=kr+r}^{\infty}t^{n}P\bigg[\sum_{i=1}^{k}X_{\lambda,r}^{(i)}=n\bigg].

Therefore, by (
15
) and (
16
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 5
.

Let
X_{\lambda,r}^{(1)},X_{\lambda,r}^{(2)},\dots,X_{\lambda,r}^{(k)}
be identically independent
r
-truncated degenerate Poisson random variables with parameter
\alpha>0
, and let
\displaystyle X_{\lambda,r}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}X_{\lambda,r}^{(i)}\displaystyle
. Then the probability for
X_{\lambda,r}
is given by

P[X_{\lambda,r}=n]=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\cfrac{k!}{(e_{\lambda}(\alpha)-e_{\lambda,r}(\alpha))^{k}}\frac{\alpha^{n}}{n!}S_{2,\lambda}^{[r]}(n,kr+k),&\textrm{if $n\geq kr+r$},\\
0,&\textrm{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

3.
Conclusion

We generalized the results on the zero-truncated degenerate Poisson distribution to the case of the
r
-truncated degenerate Poisson distribution, with its potential applications to the cornavirus pandemic and applications of probabilistic methods to the study of some special numbers and polynomials in mind.

Let
X_{\lambda,r}
be the
r
-truncated degenerate Poisson random variable with parameter
\alpha
.
Then, for the random variable
X_{\lambda,r}
, we derived its expectation, its variance, its
n
-th moment, and its cumulative distribution function. In addition, we obtained two different expressions for the probability generating function of a finite sum of independent
r
-truncated degenerate Poisson random variables with equal parameters.

As one of our future projects, we would like to continue this line of research, namely to explore applications of various methods of probability theory to science, engineering and social science, and to the study of some special polynomials and numbers.

Some nonlinear problems for the superposition of fractional operators with Neumann boundary conditions
Serena Dipierro, Edoardo Proietti Lippi, Caterina Sportelli and Enrico Valdinoci

Abstract
. We discuss the existence theory of a nonlinear problem of nonlocal type subject to Neumann boundary conditions. Differently from the existing literature, the elliptic operator under consideration is obtained as a superposition of operators of mixed order. The setting that we introduce is very general and comprises, for instance, the sum of two fractional Laplacians, or of a fractional Laplacian and a Laplacian, as particular cases (the situation in which there are infinitely many operators, and even a continuous distribution of operators, can be considered as well). New bits of functional analysis are introduced to deal with this problem. An eigenvalue analysis divides the existence theory into two streams, one related to a Mountain Pass method, the other to a Linking technique.



1.
Introduction

In this article, we consider a linear operator of mixed order obtained by the possible superposition of fractional Laplacians and the classical Laplacian. This operator is quite general and comprises the sum of two operators
of different order as a particular case. It also includes continuous superpositions of operators, as well as the case in which continuous and discrete superpositions are both present.

This operator is analyzed in the framework of a nonlinear equation in which the nonlinear source term is “subcritical” (of course, since the operator does not have a fixed order, this notion of “criticality” is somewhat delicate and needs to be specified with care).

The existence of solutions will be related to an eigenvalue problem and an appropriate analysis of the coefficient in front of the linear term of the source. Specifically, below a suitable eigenvalue the existence will be established through a Mountain Pass argument and above such a threshold by a Linking method.

Given the novelty of the topic under consideration, some new functional settings have to be introduced and the classical energy estimates need to be adapted to this new framework.

The mathematical details of the problem under consideration go as follows.
Let
\mu
be a nonnegative, finite (Borel) measure over
(0,1)
.
Let
\alpha\geqslant 0
. The operator that we address here deals with the possible coexistence of a Laplacian and a superposition of fractional Laplacians, namely

(1.1)

L_{\alpha,\mu}(u):=-\alpha\Delta u+\int_{(0,1)}(-\Delta)^{s}u\,d\mu(s).

In a typical fashion, the notation
(-\Delta)^{s}
stands for the fractional Laplacian, defined, for all
s\in(0,1)
, as

(1.2)

(-\Delta)^{s}\,u(x)=c_{N,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{2u(x)-u(x+y)-u(x-y)}{|y|^{N+2s}}\,dy.

The positive normalizing constant
c_{N,s}
is chosen in such a way to provide
consistent limits as
s\nearrow 1
and as
s\searrow 0
, namely

\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(-\Delta)^{s}u=(-\Delta)^{1}u=-\Delta u\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad\lim_{s\searrow 0}(-\Delta)^{s}u=(-\Delta)^{0}u=u.

Throughout the paper we denote by
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
a bounded, Lipschitz domain.
The definition of “nonlocal normal derivative” associated with the
fractional Laplacian that we use in this paper has been introduced in
[
7
]
and reads as

\mathscr{N}_{s}u(x):=c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dy\qquad\mbox{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega}.

With this notation, we recall the definition of the Neumann
boundary conditions associated with the operator
L_{\alpha,\mu}
in (
1.1
) (these conditions were first introduced in
[
6
]

for the superposition of two operators, and then generalized to the setting
in (
1.1
) in
[
4
]
):

Definition 1.1
.

We say that
u
satisfies the
(\alpha,\mu)
-
Neumann conditions
if

(1.3)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}\mathscr{N}_{s}u(x)\,d\mu(s)=g\,\,\mbox{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega},&\mbox{ if }\alpha=0,\\
\\
\partial_{\nu}u(x)=h\,\,\mbox{ for all }x\in\partial\Omega,&\mbox{ if }\mu\equiv 0,\\
\\
\partial_{\nu}u(x)=h\,\,\mbox{ for all }x\in\partial\Omega\mbox{ and }\\
\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}\mathscr{N}_{s}u(x)\,d\mu(s)=g\,\,\mbox{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega},&\mbox{ if }\alpha\neq 0\mbox{ and }\mu\not\equiv 0.\end{cases}

Moreover, when
g\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega}

and
h\equiv 0
on
\partial\Omega
, we say that (
1.3
) are
homogeneous
(\alpha,\mu)
-Neumann conditions
.

It is worth noting that continuous superpositions of operators of different fractional orders have been recently considered in
[
3
,
2
]
in the case of a signed, finite Borel measure over
[0,1]
.
However, the setting considered in
[
3
,
2
]
turns out to be different from the one presented here, since it was tailored for critical problems subject to Dirichlet boundary conditions.

When
\alpha=0
, we need to identify a suitable value in the fractional range
(0,1)
which will play the role of a critical exponent (see the forthcoming Proposition
2.1
).
In this case, since
\mu
is nontrivial, there exists
\widetilde{s}_{\sharp}\in(0,1)
such that

\mu([\widetilde{s}_{\sharp},1))>0.

Hence, we define

(1.4)

s_{\sharp}:=\begin{cases}\widetilde{s}_{\sharp}&\mbox{ if }\alpha=0,\\
1&\mbox{ if }\alpha\neq 0.\end{cases}

Here below and in the rest of the paper we will use the notation

2^{*}_{s_{\sharp}}=\frac{2N}{N-2s_{\sharp}}.

The exponent
2^{*}_{s_{\sharp}}
plays the role of a “critical exponent” for a suitable nonlinear nonlocal problem which we now present in detail.

Let
\widetilde{\lambda}\in\mathbb{R}
. Here we address the study of the problem

(1.5)

\begin{cases}L_{\alpha,\mu}(u)=\widetilde{\lambda}u+f(x,u)\qquad\mbox{in }\Omega\\
\mbox{with homogeneous }(\alpha,\mu)\mbox{-Neumann conditions.}\end{cases}

In this setting, it is convenient to consider an equivalent formulation. Namely, setting

(1.6)

\lambda:=\widetilde{\lambda}+1,

we consider the problem

(1.7)

\begin{cases}L_{\alpha,\mu}(u)+u=\lambda u+f(x,u)\qquad\mbox{in }\Omega\\
\mbox{with homogeneous }(\alpha,\mu)\mbox{-Neumann conditions.}\end{cases}

By
[
4
, Theorem 1.5]
there exists a sequence of eigenvalues

(1.8)

0=\widetilde{\lambda}_{1}<\widetilde{\lambda}_{2}\leqslant\widetilde{\lambda}_{3}\dots

associated with the operator
L_{\alpha,\mu}
under
(\alpha,\mu)
-Neumann conditions. Then, we will use the notation

(1.9)

\lambda_{k}:=\widetilde{\lambda}_{k}+1

to identify the sequence of eigevanlues of the operator
L_{\alpha,\mu}+Id
under
(\alpha,\mu)
-Neumann conditions. In particular, by (
1.8
) it follows that

(1.10)

\lambda_{1}=1.

The main goal of this article is to construct solutions of (
1.5
),
or equivalently of (
1.7
), for suitable parameter ranges of
\widetilde{\lambda}
, or equivalently of
\lambda
,
and appropriate
assumptions on the nonlinearity.

More explicitly,
throughout the paper we assume that the nonlinear term
f:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
in (
1.7
) is a Carathéodory function which satisfies a suitable subcritical growth, namely

(1.11)

\begin{split}&\mbox{there exist some constants $a_{1},a_{2}>0$ and $p\in(2,2^{*}_{s_{\sharp}})$ such that}\\
&|f(x,t)|\leqslant a_{1}+a_{2}|t|^{p-1}\quad\mbox{for any }t\in\mathbb{R}\mbox{ and for a.e. }x\in\Omega,\\
\end{split}

and

(1.12)

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{f(x,t)}{t}=0\quad\mbox{ uniformly for a.e. }x\in\Omega.

Moreover, setting

(1.13)

F(x,t):=\int_{0}^{t}f(x,\tau)\,d\tau,

we assume that the Ambrosetti–Rabinowitz condition holds true, i.e.

(1.14)

\begin{split}&\mbox{there exist $\vartheta>2$ and $r\geqslant 0$ such that}\\
&0<\vartheta F(x,t)\leqslant f(x,t)t\quad\mbox{for any }t\mbox{ such that }|t|>r\mbox{ and for a.e. }x\in\Omega.\end{split}

In addition, we suppose that

(1.15)

\begin{split}&\mbox{there exist some constants\penalty\ $\widetilde{\vartheta}>2$, $a_{3}>0$ and a function\penalty\ $a_{4}\in L^{1}(\Omega)$ such that}\\
&F(x,t)\geqslant a_{3}|t|^{\widetilde{\vartheta}}-a_{4}(x).\end{split}

Furthermore, being
r
as in (
1.14
), if
r>0
, we ask that

(1.16)

F(x,t)\geqslant 0\quad\mbox{for any }t\in\mathbb{R}\mbox{ and for a.e. }x\in\Omega.

We point out that condition (
1.15
) was introduced in
[
9
]

to complete the Ambrosetti-Rabinowitz condition in the presence
of Carathéodory functions.

Depending on the location of the parameter
\lambda
introduced in (
1.6
), to prove the existence of a nontrivial solution for the problem (
1.7
), we will use either the Mountain Pass Theorem or the Linking Theorem.

To be more precise, on the one hand, when
\lambda\leqslant\lambda_{1}=1
(recall (
1.10
)) we will show that problem (
1.7
) has a Mountain Pass geometry.

On the other hand, we address the case
\lambda\geqslant\lambda_{1}=1
as well
through a different method, and specifically
by using a Linking argument.

We point out that the use of a Linking argument requires a suitable decomposition of the ambient space into the direct sum of closed subspaces. For this reason, when dealing with the case
\lambda\geqslant\lambda_{1}
, we restrict ourselves to a suitable subspace which guarantees such a feature. In particular, by using
[
4
, Theorem 1.5]
, we are able to provide the existence of a complete orthogonal system of eigenfunctions for such a subspace and then the desired direct sum decomposition. We refer the reader to the definition in (
2.5
) and to Section
4
for a detailed discussion.
Moreover, it is worth noting that the choice of the subspace we have opted for is not the only option. We discuss another possible choice and the associated difficulties in Section
A
.

In our existence results, we will deal with weak solutions of (
1.7
), see Definitions
3.1
and
4.1
for the precise setting.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
f:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfy (

), (

), (

) and (

).

Then, for any

),

.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
f:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfy (
1.11
), (
1.12
), (
1.14
), (
1.15
) and (
1.16
).

Then, for any
\lambda\geqslant\lambda_{1}

.

under consideration, but also for the assumptions on the nonlinearity

(e.g. one can compare (

), (

) and (

\beta>0
and

,

2.
Functional setting

In this section we introduce a functional analytical setting that is inspired by the one presented in
[
4
, Section 2]
and allows us
to address the study of problem (
1.7
) by using a variational argument.

Let
\mathcal{Q}:=\mathbb{R}^{2N}\setminus(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)^{2}
. We denote the Gagliardo seminorm of
u
as

[u]_{s}:=\left({c_{N,s}}\iint_{\mathcal{Q}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy\right)^{1/2}.

Moreover, we define the functional space

(2.1)

\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\mu}(\Omega):=\begin{cases}H^{1}(\Omega)&\hbox{ if }\mu\equiv 0,\\
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}(\Omega)&\hbox{ if }\alpha=0,\\
H^{1}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{H}_{\mu}(\Omega)&\hbox{ if }\alpha\neq 0\text{ and }\mu\not\equiv 0,\end{cases}

endowed with the norm

(2.2)

\begin{split}\|u\|_{\alpha,\mu}:=\Bigg(\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\||h|^{1/2}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}&+\||g|^{1/2}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)}\\
&+\alpha\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{(0,1)}[u]_{s}^{2}\,d\mu(s)\Bigg)^{1/2}.\end{split}

In (
2.1
), the space
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}(\Omega)
is defined
as

(2.3)

\mathcal{H}_{\mu}(\Omega):=\left\{u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}\text{ measurable }:\|u\|_{\mu}<+\infty\right\},

with the norm

(2.4)

\|u\|_{\mu}:=\left(\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\||g|^{1/2}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{(0,1)}[u]_{s}^{2}\,d\mu(s)\right)^{1/2}.

We can now recall the embedding results for the space
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\mu}(\Omega)
stated in
[
4
, Proposition 2.4 and Corollary 2.5]
:

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
s_{\sharp}
be as in (
1.4
). Then, there exists a positive constant
c=c(N,\Omega,s_{\sharp})
such that, for any
u\in\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\mu}(\Omega)
,

\|u\|_{H^{s_{\sharp}}(\Omega)}\leqslant c\|u\|_{\alpha,\mu},

namely the space
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\mu}(\Omega)
is continuosly embedded in
L^{2^{*}_{s_{\sharp}}}(\Omega)
.

In addition,
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\mu}(\Omega)
is compactly embedded in
L^{p}(\Omega)
for any
p\in[1,2^{*}_{s_{\sharp}})
.

We point out that, since we deal with homogeneous
(\alpha,\mu)
-Neumann boundary conditions in (
1.7
),
we will assume from now on that
h\equiv 0
and
g\equiv 0
.

To develop a Linking argument, we introduce the subspace

(2.5)

\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\mu}(\Omega):=\begin{cases}H^{1}(\Omega)&\mbox{ if }\mu\equiv 0,\\
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mu}(\Omega)&\mbox{ if }\alpha=0,\\
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\mu}(\Omega)&\mbox{ if }\alpha\neq 0\text{ and }\mu\not\equiv 0,\end{cases}

where

(2.6)

\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mu}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}(\Omega):\int_{(0,1)}\mathscr{N}_{s}u\,d\mu(s)=0\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega}\right\}.

We endow the space
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\mu}(\Omega)
with the norm defined in (
2.2
).

Remark 2.2
.

We observe that there exist functions
in
{\mathcal{H}}_{0,\mu}(\Omega)={\mathcal{H}}_{\mu}(\Omega)
that do not belong to
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{0,\mu}(\Omega)=\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mu}(\Omega)
.
Indeed, we consider
\Omega=(-1,1)
and take
u:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle u(x)=x\quad\mbox{ if }x\in(-1,1)

and

\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}\mathscr{N}_{s}u(x)\,d\mu(s)=0\quad\mbox{ if }x\in\mathbb{R}\setminus[-1,1].

In this way, we have that
u\in\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{0,\mu}(\Omega)
and, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}\setminus[-1,1]
,

u(x)=\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{y}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,dy\,d\mu(s)}{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,d\mu(s)}.

In particular, if
x<-1
, we have that, for any
s\in(0,1)
,

\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{y}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,dy\leqslant 0,

and thus
u(x)\leqslant 0
if
x\leqslant 0
.

Now, if we consider the
positive part of
u
, we have
u^{+}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{0,\mu}(\Omega)
and
u^{+}(x)=0
if
x\leqslant 0
.
However, for any
x<-1
, we can compute

\int_{(0,1)}\mathscr{N}_{s}u^{+}(x)\,d\mu(s)=\int_{(0,1)}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{u^{+}(x)-u^{+}(y)}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,dy\,d\mu(s)=\int_{(0,1)}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{-u^{+}(y)}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,dy\,d\mu(s)<0,

and therefore
u^{+}\not\in\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{0,\mu}(\Omega)
.

We notice that, by the definition in (
2.5
),
if
\alpha\neq 0
and
\mu\not\equiv 0
we have that the space
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\mu}(\Omega)
is a closed subspace of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Moreover, if
\mu\equiv 0
, we have that
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\mu}(\Omega)
is a closed subspace of
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Since both
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
L^{2}(\Omega)
are separable, we infer that there exists a complete orthogonal system for
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\mu}(\Omega)
in these cases.

Instead, when
\alpha=0
, we employ the result stated in
[
4
, Theorem 1.5]
, according to which there exists a sequence of eigenfunctions
e_{k}
in
{\mathcal{H}}_{0,\mu}(\Omega)
associated with the operator
L_{0,\mu}
which is a complete orthogonal system in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

We also remark that, by construction, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}

and any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
,

(2.7)

\int_{(0,1)}\mathscr{N}_{s}e_{k}(x)d\mu(s)=0.

This entails that the eigenfunctions
e_{k}
actually
belong to
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{0,\mu}(\Omega)
.

As a matter of fact, we have the following result:

Proposition 2.3
.

The sequence
e_{k}
provides a complete orthogonal system in
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{0,\mu}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

We first observe that, by construction,
for any
k
,
j\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
,

\int_{(0,1)}\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\iint_{\mathcal{Q}}\frac{(e_{k}(x)-e_{k}(y))(e_{j}(x)-e_{j}(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy\,d\mu(s)=\lambda_{k}\int_{\Omega}e_{k}(x)e_{j}(x)\,dx.

As a consequence, since
e_{k}
is an orthogonal system in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, we have that it is orthogonal in
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{0,\mu}(\Omega)
as well.

We now focus on proving the completeness of the system. To this aim,
let
u\in\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{0,\mu}(\Omega)
. Since
e_{k}
is a complete system in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, by considering the restriction of
u
over
\Omega
, there exist coefficients
a_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

u=\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}a_{k}e_{k}\quad\mbox{a.e. in }\Omega.

Moreover, since
u\in\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{0,\mu}(\Omega)
, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
we have that

u(x)=\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}\int_{\Omega}\frac{u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dy\,d\mu(s)}{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}\int_{\Omega}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,d\mu(s)}=\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}a_{k}e_{k}(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dy\,d\mu(s)}{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}\int_{\Omega}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,d\mu(s)}.

Hence, by the Dominated Convergence Theorem, and
recalling (
2.7
), we get

u(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}a_{k}\;\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}\int_{\Omega}\frac{e_{k}(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dy\,d\mu(s)}{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}\int_{\Omega}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,d\mu(s)}=\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}a_{k}e_{k}(x),

as desired.
∎

We now prove that the subspace
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{0,\mu}(\Omega)
is closed with
respect to the weak convergence.

Proposition 2.4
.

The space
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{0,\mu}(\Omega)
is closed
with respect to the weak convergence in

\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{0,\mu}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Let
u_{k}
be a sequence in
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{0,\mu}(\Omega)
which converges weakly to some
u
. We aim to prove that
u\in\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{0,\mu}(\Omega)
.

We observe that
u_{k}
converges weakly in
{\mathcal{H}}_{0,\mu}(\Omega)
and
u\in{\mathcal{H}}_{0,\mu}(\Omega)
.
Then, by the compact embedding stated in Proposition
2.1
,

u_{k}
converges strongly to
u
in
L^{p}(\Omega)
for any

p\in[1,2^{*}_{s_{\sharp}})
. Moreover,
u_{k}
converges to
u

a.e. in
\Omega
and
|u_{k}|\leqslant h
for some
h\in L^{p}(\Omega)

(see
[
1
, Theorem IV.9]
).
Then, recalling that
u_{k}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{0,\mu}(\Omega)
, if
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
,
we can apply the Dominated Convergence Theorem to get

(2.8)

\lim_{k\to+\infty}u_{k}(x)=\lim_{k\to+\infty}\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}\int_{\Omega}\frac{u_{k}(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dy\,d\mu(s)}{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}\int_{\Omega}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,d\mu(s)}=\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}\int_{\Omega}\frac{u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dy\,d\mu(s)}{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}\int_{\Omega}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,d\mu(s)}=:v(x).

We claim that

(2.9)

u=v\quad\mbox{ a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega}.

To this aim, let
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega},[0,+\infty))
. Since
u_{k}
converges weakly to
u

in
{\mathcal{H}}_{0,\mu}(\Omega)
, we have that

(2.10)

\displaystyle\mbox{ if }\lambda<\lambda_{1},

\displaystyle\mbox{or }

\displaystyle u\in\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{0,\mu}(\Omega)\quad

\displaystyle\mbox{ if }\lambda\geqslant\lambda_{1},

being

\ \widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{0,\mu}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in H_{\mu}(\Omega):\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\mathscr{N}_{s_{k}}(u)=0\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega}\right\}.

Proof.

Let

\mu:=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}c_{k}\,\delta_{s_{k}}.

We notice that two cases can occur. Indeed, recalling (
1.4
), if

\displaystyle\overline{s}:=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}}\{s_{k}\}\neq s_{k}

for every
k
, then we can take
s_{\sharp}:=\overline{s}-\delta
, for some
\delta>0
sufficiently small.
If instead
\overline{s}=s_{\overline{k}}
for some
\overline{k}
, then we can take
s_{\sharp}:=s_{\overline{k}}
.

The desired nontrivial solutions for problems (
5.9
) and (
5.11
) are inferred by Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
.
Moreover, (
5.10
) and (
5.12
) come from definitions (
2.1
) and (
2.5
), respectively.
∎

Remarkably, another interesting result stems from the continuous superposition of fractional operators. To the best of our knowledge, this result turns out to be new as well.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
\omega:(0,1)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be a measurable, nonnegative and not identically zero function. Denote by
\widetilde{\lambda}_{k}
the sequence of the eigenvalues of the operator

(5.13)

-\alpha\Delta u+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\omega(s)(-\Delta)^{s}u\,ds

under homogeneous Neumann conditions.

Then, if
\alpha\neq 0
, for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, the problem

(5.14)

\begin{cases}-\alpha\Delta u+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\omega(s)(-\Delta)^{s}u\,ds+u=\lambda u+f(x,u)&\mbox{in }\Omega,\\
\partial_{\nu}u(x)=0&\mbox{for all }x\in\partial\Omega,\\
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\omega(s)\mathscr{N}_{s}u\,ds=0&\mbox{for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega},\end{cases}

admits a nontrivial solution
u
such that

(5.15)

\displaystyle\mbox{either }

\displaystyle u\in H^{1}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{H}_{\mu}(\Omega)\quad

\displaystyle\mbox{ if }\lambda<\lambda_{1},

\displaystyle\mbox{or }

\displaystyle u\in H^{1}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{H}_{\mu}(\Omega)\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\quad

\displaystyle\mbox{ if }\lambda\geqslant\lambda_{1},

being

\mathcal{H}_{\mu}(\Omega):=\left\{u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}\text{ measurable }:u\in L^{2}(\Omega)\ \mbox{ and }\ \int_{0}^{1}\omega(s)[u]_{s}^{2}\,ds<+\infty\right\}.

If instead
\alpha=0
, for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, the problem

(5.16)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\omega(s)(-\Delta)^{s}u\,ds+u=\lambda u+f(x,u)&\mbox{in }\Omega,\\
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\omega(s)\mathscr{N}_{s}u\,ds=0&\mbox{for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega},\end{cases}

admits a nontrivial solution
u
such that

(5.17)

\displaystyle\mbox{either }

\displaystyle u\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}(\Omega)\quad

\displaystyle\mbox{ if }\lambda<\lambda_{1},

\displaystyle\mbox{or }

\displaystyle u\in\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{0,\mu}(\Omega)\quad

\displaystyle\mbox{ if }\lambda\geqslant\lambda_{1},

being

\ \widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{0,\mu}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}(\Omega):\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\omega(s)\mathscr{N}_{s}u\,ds=0\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega}\right\}.

Proof.

We observe that the operator in (
5.13
) is a particular case of
L_{\alpha,\mu}
defined in (
1.1
), where
d\mu(s)
reduces to
\omega(s)ds
.

In this setting, recalling (
1.4
), if
\alpha\neq 0
we take
s_{\sharp}=1
.
If instead
\alpha=0
, we can take
s_{\sharp}
to be any value in
(0,1)
satisfying

\int_{s_{\sharp}}^{1}\omega(s)\,ds>0.

This choice makes
s_{\sharp}
to play the role of critical exponent in (
1.11
).

The desired result for both problems (
5.14
) and (
5.16
) follows by Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
.
Moreover, (
5.15
) and (
5.17
) are a consequence of definitions (
2.1
) and (

), respectively.
∎

Appendix A
Some justification for the choice of
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\mu}(\Omega)

Here we motivate our choice of defining the space
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\mu}(\Omega)
as in (
2.5
).

The reason is that, learning from the case
\alpha=0
, one may be tempted to consider instead the space

\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\mu}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\mu}(\Omega):\;\alpha\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=0\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\int_{(0,1)}\mathscr{N}_{s}u\,d\mu(s)=0\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega}\right\}

even when
\alpha\neq 0
.
Following this definition, we can find a complete orthogonal system
for
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\mu}(\Omega)
as we did for
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{0,\mu}(\Omega)
in
Proposition
2.3
, that is,
from the sequence of eigenfunctions provided in
[
4
, Theorem 1.5]
. Indeed, the following result holds:

Proposition A.1
.

The sequence
e_{k}
is a complete orthogonal system for
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\mu}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

The orthogonality in
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\mu}(\Omega)

comes from the orthogonality in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Indeed,
for any
k
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
, by construction we have that

\alpha\int_{\Omega}\nabla e_{k}\cdot\nabla e_{j}\,dx+\int_{(0,1)}\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\iint_{\mathcal{Q}}\frac{(e_{k}(x)-e_{k}(y))(e_{j}(x)-e_{j}(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy\,d\mu(s)=\lambda_{k}\int_{\Omega}e_{k}e_{j}\,dx.

To prove the completeness, let
u\in\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\mu}(\Omega)
. From the completeness in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, if we
consider the restriction of
u
in
\Omega
, we can find coefficients
a_{k}
such that

u=\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}a_{k}e_{k}\quad\mbox{a.e. in }\Omega.

Moreover, since
u\in\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\mu}(\Omega)
,
when
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
we have

\displaystyle u(x)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}\int_{\Omega}\frac{u(y)}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,dy\,d\mu(s)}{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}\int_{\Omega}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,d\mu(s)}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}a_{k}e_{k}(y)}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,dy\,d\mu(s)}{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}\int_{\Omega}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,d\mu(s)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}a_{k}\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}\int_{\Omega}\frac{e_{k}(y)}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,dy\,d\mu(s)}{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}\int_{\Omega}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,d\mu(s)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}a_{k}e_{k}(x),

which completes the proof.
∎

However, the space
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\mu}(\Omega)
is not closed with respect to the weak convergence in
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\mu}(\Omega)
, not even in the local case when
\mu\equiv 0
, as illustrated in the example below.

Example A.2
.

Let
N=1
,
\Omega=(0,1)
and
\mu\equiv 0
. In this case, the space

\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\mu}(\Omega)
boils down to

\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,0}(\Omega)=\left\{u\in H^{1}((0,1)):u^{\prime}(0)=u^{\prime}(1)=0\right\}.

Let
u_{n}
be a sequence in
H^{1}((0,1))
defined as

u_{n}(x):=x^{1+\frac{1}{n}}(x-1)^{2}.

Clearly, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

u^{\prime}_{n}(x)=x^{\frac{1}{n}}(x-1)\left[\left(3+\frac{1}{n}\right)x-1-\frac{1}{n}\right],

and thus the sequence
u_{n}
is in
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,0}(\Omega)
. Moreover, we have that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}u_{n}(x)=x(x-1)^{2}=:u(x)\quad\mbox{a.e. in }(0,1)

and

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}u^{\prime}_{n}(x)=(x-1)(3x-1)=u^{\prime}(x)\quad\mbox{a.e. in }(0,1).

Now, we show that

(A.1)

u_{n}
converges strongly to
u
in
H^{1}((0,1))
as
n\to+\infty
.

Indeed,

\displaystyle\|u_{n}-u\|_{L^{2}((0,1))}^{2}=\int_{0}^{1}\big|x^{1+\frac{1}{n}}(x-1)^{2}-x(x-1)^{2}\big|^{2}\,dx\leqslant\int_{0}^{1}|x^{\frac{1}{n}}-1|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\left(1+\frac{2}{n}\right)^{-1}-2\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{-1}+1,

which entails that

(A.2)

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\|u_{n}-u\|_{L^{2}((0,1))}=0.

In addition,

\displaystyle\|u^{\prime}_{n}-u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}((0,1))}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\left|x^{\frac{1}{n}}(x-1)\left[\left(3+\frac{1}{n}\right)x-1-\frac{1}{n}\right]-(x-1)(3x-1)\right|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{1}\left|\left[\left(3+\frac{1}{n}\right)x^{1+\frac{1}{n}}-3x\right]-\left[\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)x^{\frac{1}{n}}-1\right]\right|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\left(3+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{2}\left(3+\frac{2}{n}\right)^{-1}+\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{2}\left(1+\frac{2}{n}\right)^{-1}+2\left(3+\frac{1}{n}\right)\left(2+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle+6\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)\left(2+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{-1}-2\left(3+\frac{1}{n}\right)\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)\left(2+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{-1}-7.

Therefore,

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\|u^{\prime}_{n}-u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}((0,1))}=0.

This and (
A.2
) give (
A.1
).

In particular, we have that
u_{n}
converges weakly to
u
in
H^{1}((0,1))
as
n\to+\infty
,
but
u\not\in\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,0}(\Omega)
as
u^{\prime}(0)=1\neq 0
.

This means that
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,0}(\Omega)
is not closed with respect to the weak convergence in
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,0}(\Omega)
.

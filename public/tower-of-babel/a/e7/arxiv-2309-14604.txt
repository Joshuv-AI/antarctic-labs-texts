Recovering contact forms from boundary data
Gabriel Katz

Abstract
. Let X X be a compact connected smooth manifold with boundary. The paper deals with contact 1 1 -forms β \beta on X X , whose Reeb vector fields v β v_{\beta} admit Lyapunov functions f f . We tackle the question: how to recover X X and β \beta from the appropriate data along the boundary ∂ X \partial X ? We describe such boundary data and prove that they allow for a reconstruction of the pair ( X , β ) (X,\beta) , up to a diffeomorphism of X X . We use the term “holography” for the reconstruction. We say that objects or structures inside X X are holographic , if they can be reconstructed from their v β v_{\beta} -flow induced “shadows” on the boundary ∂ X \partial X . We also introduce numerical invariants that measure how “wrinkled” the boundary ∂ X \partial X is with respect to the v β v_{\beta} -flow and study their holographic properties under the contact forms preserving embeddings of equidimensional contact manifolds with boundary. We get some “non-squeezing results” about such contact embedding, which are reminiscent of Gromov’s non-squeezing theorem in symplectic geometry.

Traversing, boundary generic, & traversally generic vector fields

Let

(n+1)
-dimensional manifold with boundary.

Definition 2.1
.

A vector field

on
X
is called

if each
v
-trajectory is homeomorphic to ether a closed interval, or to a singleton.

We denote by

the space of all traversing vector fields on

.

\diamondsuit

The
1

[

]

[
K3
]

It follows that any traversing vector field does not vanish and is of the

[

]

such that

in

Any smooth vector field

on
X
, which does not vanish along the boundary

, gives rise to a partition
\partial_{1}^{+}X(v)\cup\partial_{1}^{-}X(v)
of the boundary
\partial_{1}X=_{\mathsf{def}}\partial X

\partial_{1}^{+}X(v)

of

or is tangent to
\partial X
, and the locus
\partial_{1}^{-}X(v)
, where it is directed
outward
of
X
or is tangent to
\partial X
.

We assume that
v|_{\partial X}
, viewed as a section of the quotient line bundle

over
\partial X
, is transversal to its zero section. This assumption implies that both sets,
\partial^{+}_{1}X(v)
and
\partial^{-}_{1}X(v)
, are compact manifolds which share a common smooth boundary
\partial_{2}X(v)=_{\mathsf{def}}\partial(\partial_{1}^{+}X(v))=\partial(\partial_{1}^{-}X(v))
. Evidently,
\partial_{2}X(v)
is the locus where
v
is
tangent
to the boundary
\partial X
.

In his groundbreaking work
[

]

, the tangent locus
\partial_{2}X(v)
inherits a similar structure in connection to
\partial_{1}^{+}X(v)
, as
\partial_{1}X
has in connection to
X
. That is,

gives rise to a partition
\partial_{2}^{+}X(v)\cup\partial_{2}^{-}X(v)
of
\partial_{2}X(v)
into two sets: the locus
\partial_{2}^{+}X(v)
, where the vector field
v
is directed inward of
\partial_{1}^{+}X(v)
or is tangent to
\partial_{2}X(v)
, and
\partial_{2}^{-}X(v)
, where it is directed outward of
\partial_{1}^{+}X(v)
or is tangent to
\partial_{2}X(v)
. Again, we assume that the restriction
v|_{\partial_{2}X(v)}
, viewed as a section of the quotient line bundle
T(\partial X)/T(\partial_{2}X(v))
over
\partial_{2}X(v)
, is transversal to its zero section.

For generic fields, this structure replicates itself: the
cuspidal locus

\partial_{3}X(v)
is defined as the locus where
v
is tangent to
\partial_{2}X(v)
;
\partial_{3}X(v)
is divided into two manifolds,
\partial_{3}^{+}X(v)
and
\partial_{3}^{-}X(v)
. In
\partial_{3}^{+}X(v)
, the field is directed inward of
\partial_{2}^{+}X(v)
or is tangent to its boundary, in
\partial_{3}^{-}X(v)
, outward of
\partial_{2}^{+}X(v)
or is tangent to its boundary. We can repeat this construction until we reach the zero-dimensional stratum
\partial_{n+1}X(v)=\partial_{n+1}^{+}X(v)\cup\partial_{n+1}^{-}X(v)
.

To achieve some uniformity in our notations, put
\partial_{0}^{+}X=_{\mathsf{def}}X
and
\partial_{1}X=_{\mathsf{def}}\partial X
.

Thus, a generic vector field

on
X
gives rise to two stratifications:

\displaystyle\partial X=_{\mathsf{def}}\partial_{1}X\supset\partial_{2}X(v)\supset\dots\supset\partial_{n+1}X(v),

(2.1)

\displaystyle X=_{\mathsf{def}}\partial_{0}^{+}X\supset\partial_{1}^{+}X(v)\supset\partial_{2}^{+}X(v)\supset\dots\supset\partial_{n+1}^{+}X(v),

the first one by closed submanifolds, the second one—by compact ones. Here
\dim(\partial_{j}X(v))=\dim(\partial_{j}^{+}X(v))=n+1-j
.

We will often use the notation “
\partial_{j}^{\pm}X
” instead of “
\partial_{j}^{\pm}X(v)
” when the vector field

is fixed or its choice is obvious.

These considerations motivate a more formal definition.

Definition 2.2
.

We say that

•

•

•

\diamondsuit

\displaystyle m(\gamma)=_{\mathsf{def}}\sum_{a\in\gamma\cap\partial_{1}X}\;m(a),\;\text{ and }\;m^{\prime}(\gamma)=_{\mathsf{def}}\sum_{a\in\gamma\cap\partial_{1}X}\;(m(a)-1),

\displaystyle|\omega|=_{\mathsf{def}}\sum_{i}\;\omega_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad|\omega|^{\prime}=_{\mathsf{def}}\sum_{i}\;(\omega_{i}-1).

]

Let

-flow at a point

Consider the case when

(2n+1)
-dimensional manifold with boundary and

so that

. We assume that

Let

We denote by

\gamma_{x}

Definition 3.1
.

We say that a boundary generic and traversing vector field

\gamma

\partial_{1}X

\gamma

.
\diamondsuit

A traversing vector field

X
induces a structure of a

(\partial_{1}X,\succ_{v})
on the boundary

: for

if the two points lie on the same

-trajectory

We denote by

A traversing and boundary generic

\displaystyle C_{v}:\partial_{1}^{+}X(v)\to\partial_{1}^{-}X(v)

We notice that, for any smooth positive function

. Thus, the causality map depends only on the

of a traversing vector field

Lemma 4.1

Let

. We embed properly

and

, subject to the constraint

to
\hat{\beta}
in

in the vicinity of

in
\hat{X}

Using that each

\hat{v}
-trajectory

of

that map each segment

\hat{v}
-trajectory. Moreover,

Let

(4.1)

\displaystyle g_{\alpha}(\phi_{\alpha}(a\times t))=\int_{\phi_{\alpha}([a\times 0),\;(a\times t])}\hat{\beta}\;=_{\mathsf{def}}\int_{\phi_{\alpha}(a\times 0)}^{\phi_{\alpha}(a\times t)}\hat{\beta},

. The finite cover

By a similar formula, any closed interval

\displaystyle\int_{[a,b]}df=\sum_{\alpha}\Big(\int_{[a,b]}\psi_{\alpha}\cdot dg_{\alpha}+\int_{[a,b]}g_{\alpha}\cdot d\psi_{\alpha}\Big)=\sum_{\alpha}\Big(\psi_{\alpha}\int_{[a,b]}dg_{\alpha}\Big)

If

Recall the following well-known fact.

Lemma 4.2
.

For a contact structure
\xi

-form

Let us fix a contact form

Lemma 4.3

(2n)

•

(d\beta)^{n}\big|_{\mathsf{int}(\partial_{1}^{+}X(v))}>0.

Proof.

We notice that

Hence, if

Let

is

is a positive volume form along each

and the bundle

(equivalently, a complex structure

Definition 6.1

Lemma 6.1

Consider an orientation-preserving submersion

Moreover, the Reeb diameters satisfy the inequality

If

Since

is an immersion. Thus,

. In the case of a covering

. Since any

is obtained from some

-trajectory

\Psi^{\beta}_{L^{\ddagger}}=\Psi^{\beta}_{L^{\dagger}}\big|_{L^{\ddagger}}
is continuous and smooth.

Put

One may think of this number as the “Reeb elevation” of the component

. Evidently,

.

Let us revisit a given Legandrian manifold
L\subset X
whose
(-v_{\beta})
-projection is
L^{\dagger}
. For any

, take the first point

that belongs to

\psi^{\beta}_{L}(x)=_{\mathsf{def}}\int_{[x,y(x)]}\beta,

where

is the segment of the trajectory
\gamma_{x}
, bounded by

Evidently,
\psi^{\beta}_{L}(x)\leq\Psi^{\beta}_{L^{\ddagger}}(x)
for any

.

Corollary 7.1
.

Let
\beta
be a contact form whose Reeb vector field
v_{\beta}
is traversing and boundary generic. Let
L\subset X
be a Legandrian submanifold in a weakly general position to “the waterfall”
\mathcal{X}_{2}(v_{\beta})
, the set of
(-v_{\beta})
-trajectories through the locus
\partial_{2}X(v_{\beta})
. Let
L^{\dagger}
be its
(-v_{\beta})
-guided projection on
\partial_{1}^{+}X(v_{\beta})
.

Consider the connected components
\{L^{\ddagger}_{\kappa}\}_{\kappa}
of the Lagrangian manifold
\mathsf{int}(L^{\dagger})
, immersed in
\partial_{1}^{+}X(v_{\beta})\setminus(\mathcal{X}_{2}(v_{\beta})\cap\partial_{1}^{+}X(v_{\beta})).
In each component
L^{\ddagger}_{\kappa}
, we pick a base point
b_{\kappa}
, distinct from the points of self-intersection of
L^{\dagger}
.

Then the knowledge of the shadow
L^{\dagger}
, together with the set the base points
\{b_{\kappa}\}_{\kappa}
and the set of values
\{\psi^{\beta}_{L}(b_{\kappa})\}_{\kappa}
, makes it possible to reconstruct the Legandrian embedding
L\subset X
.

Proof.

The reconstruction of
L
resembles the process of analytic continuation.

Let
\pi_{v_{\beta}}:L\to L^{\dagger}
be the
(-v_{\beta})
-guided projection.
Let
L^{\dagger}_{\times}\subset L^{\dagger}
denotes the subset consisting of points that have multiple
\pi_{v_{\beta}}
-preimages in
L
, and let
\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}_{2}=L^{\dagger}\cap\mathcal{X}_{2}(v_{\beta})
. By the hypothesis, the set
L^{\dagger}\setminus(L^{\dagger}_{\times}\cup\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}_{2})
is open and dense in
L^{\dagger}
.

Knowing the point
b_{\kappa}\in L^{\ddagger}_{\kappa}
makes it possible, with the help of the value
\psi^{\beta}_{L}(b_{\kappa})
, to locate a unique point
a_{\kappa}\in L\cap\gamma_{b_{\kappa}}
. There is a unique germ
L_{\kappa}
of
L
in the vicinity of
a_{\kappa}
that projects onto the germ of
L^{\ddagger}_{\kappa}
at
b_{\kappa}
. Let
U^{\dagger}_{\kappa}
be an open neighborhood of
b_{\kappa}
in
L^{\dagger}
over which the reconstruction of
L
is achieved.

Take a point
x\in L^{\dagger}
that belongs to the boundary of
U^{\dagger}_{\kappa}
and such that
x\notin L^{\dagger}_{\times}\cup\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}_{2}
. Then by Lemma
7.2
, there exists a unique lift
L^{\bullet}
to
X
of
L^{\dagger}
over some open neighborhood
U_{x}\subset L^{\dagger}
of
x
, such that the point
y=\gamma_{x}\cap L
belongs to
L^{\bullet}
. Since, over
U_{x}\cap U^{\dagger}_{\kappa}
, both
L^{\bullet}
and
L
are the lifts of
L^{\dagger}
that share the point
y
, again by Lemma
7.2
, we conclude that
L^{\bullet}=L
over
U_{x}
. As a result, the reconstruction of
L
over
U_{x}\cup U^{\dagger}_{\kappa}
is available. Therefore, the maximal open set in
L^{\dagger}
, over which the lift of
L^{\dagger}
, subject to the constraint “
a_{\kappa}
belongs to the lift”, is possible is a connected component
L^{\ddagger}
of the open set
L^{\dagger}\setminus(L^{\dagger}_{\times}\cup\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}_{2})
. Since
L
is in weakly general position with
\mathcal{X}_{2}(v_{\beta})
, the union of such lifts, taken over all
\kappa
’s, is a dense subset
L\setminus(L\cap\pi_{v_{\beta}}^{-1}(\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}_{2}\cup L^{\dagger}_{\times}))
of
L
. Therefore, taking the closure of this union, produces the given
L\subset X
.
∎

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
X
be a compact connected smooth
2n+1
-dimensional manifold,
\beta
a contact form, and
v_{\beta}
its Reeb vector field, which is traversing.

Let
L\subset X
be a sub-Legandrian
(2k+1)
-dimensional closed submanifold of
X
, where
2k+1\leq n
. We assume that
L
is in a weakly general position with respect to the waterfall
\mathcal{X}_{2}(v_{\beta})
. We denote by
L(v_{\beta})
the set of downward trajectories originating in
L
, and by
\delta L(v_{\beta})
the set of downward trajectories originating in
L
and hitting the locus
\partial_{2}^{+}X(v_{\beta})
(see Fig.
3
).

Then, for any
k>0
, we get
\int_{L(v_{\beta})}(d\beta)^{k+1}=0,\;\text{ and }\;\quad\int_{L^{\dagger}}\beta\wedge(d\beta)^{k}=0.

In contrast, for
k=0
, we get

\int_{L(v_{\beta})}d\beta=0,\;\text{ and }\;\int_{L^{\dagger}}\beta=-\int_{\delta L(v_{\beta})}\beta\;\leq\;0.

Proof.

Since
T_{x}L\subset\ker\beta
we see that
\beta\wedge(d\beta)^{k}|_{L}=0
. Thus,
\int_{L}\beta\wedge(d\beta)^{k}=0
.

Note that, at a generic point
x\in L(v_{\beta})
, the tangent space
T_{x}(L(v_{\beta}))
is spanned by
v_{\beta}(x)
and the image of a tangent space
T_{y}L
under
(-v_{\beta})
-flow that takes
y\in L
to
x
. Then,
(d\beta)^{k+1}|_{T_{x}(L(v_{\beta}))}=0
, since
v_{\beta}\in\ker(d\beta)
.
Therefore,
\int_{L(v_{\beta})}(d\beta)^{k+1}=0
.

Let
\delta L(v_{\beta})
be the set of downward trajectories originating in
L
and intersecting
\partial_{2}^{+}X(v_{\beta})
(see Fig.
3
).
Again, at a generic point
x\in\delta L(v_{\beta})
, the tangent space
T_{x}(\delta L(v_{\beta}))
is spanned by
v_{\beta}(x)
and a
2k
-dimensional subspace
\mathcal{K}_{x}
of
T_{x}L
. Thus, for
k>0
,
\beta\wedge(d\beta)^{k}|_{T_{x}(\delta L(v_{\beta}))}=0
since
v_{\beta}\in\ker\beta
and
d\beta|_{\mathcal{K}_{x}}=0
. Therefore,
\int_{\delta L(v_{\beta})}\beta\wedge(d\beta)^{k}=0
.

The boundary
\partial(L(v_{\beta}))=\delta L(v_{\beta})\cup L^{\dagger}\cup-L
. In fact,
L^{\dagger}
, away from the set
\delta L(v_{\beta})\cap\partial_{1}^{+}X(v_{\beta})
of measure zero, is an immersed submanifold of
\partial_{1}^{+}X(v_{\beta})
.

Recall that
d(\beta\wedge(d\beta)^{k})=(d\beta)^{k+1}
. Therefore, for
k>0
, by the Stokes’ theorem and using that
\int_{L}\beta\wedge(d\beta)^{k}=0
,
\int_{\delta L(v_{\beta})}\beta\wedge(d\beta)^{k}=0
, and
\int_{L(v_{\beta})}(d\beta)^{k+1}=0
, we conclude that
\int_{L^{\dagger}}\beta\wedge(d\beta)^{k}=0
as well.

In the special case
k=0
, using similar analysis and Stokes’ theorem, we get

\int_{L^{\dagger}}\beta=-\int_{\delta L(v_{\beta})}\beta\;\leq\;0

since
\beta(v_{\beta})=1
.
∎

Abstract
. We determine the exact diameter of the orthograph related to mutual strong Birkhoff-James orthogonality in arbitrary finite dimensional C ∗ C^{*} -algebra. Additionally, we will estimate the distance between vertices in an arbitrary C ∗ C^{*} -algebra that can be represented as a direct sum.

Primary: 46L05, 15B10, Secondary: 05C12, 46B20, 46L08, 46L35

1.
Introduction and preliminaries

Orthogonality problems have been studied for many years. In general, normed and Banach spaces are not equipped with an inner product. Therefore, the question arises how to define the relation of orthogonality in spaces where we do not have the inner product but only a norm. There are different ways, such as Robert’s orthogonality or Pythagoras orthogonality (see for example
[
5
]
and
[
11
]
). Probably, the most common type of orthogonality is Birkhoff-James orthogonality, defined by Birkhoff in
[
6
]
and developed by James (see
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
9
]
).

Definition 1.1
.

Let
X
be a normed space, and let
x
,
y\in X
. We say that
x
is Birkhoff-James orthogonal to
y
, and denote
x\perp_{BJ}y
, or simply
x\perp y
, if for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
there holds

\|x+\lambda y\|\geq\|x\|.

Birkhoff-James orthogonality has some deficiencies, for instance, it is neither symmetric nor additive. Birkhoff-James orthogonality will be abbreviated to
BJ
-orthogonality hereinafter.

For further details on
BJ
-orthogonality, the reader is referred to a recent survey
[
1
]
and references therein.

Besides usual
BJ
-orthogonality, there is also a notion of strong
BJ
-orthogonality where the scalar is replaced with an element from the Hilbert
C^{*}
-module.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
X
be a right Hilbert
C^{*}
-module over a
C^{*}
-algebra
A
and let
a
,
b\in X
.

a) We say that
a
is strong
BJ
-orthogonal to
b
, and denote
a\mathrel{\perp}^{S}b
if for any
c\in A
there holds

\|a+bc\|\geq\|a\|.

b) We say that
a
and
b
are mutually strong
BJ
-orthogonal to each other, and denote
a\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}b
if
a\mathrel{\perp}^{S}b
and
b\mathrel{\perp}^{S}a
.

Remark 1.1
.

The part a) of the previous definition is from
[
4
]
, and part b) from
[
2
]
.

In particular, in a
C^{*}
-algebra A, regarded as a right Hilbert
C^{*}
-module over itself,
a\in A
is strong Birkhoff–James orthogonal to
b\in A
if for any
c\in A
there holds

\|a+bc\|\geq\|a\|.

It is easy to show that

a^{*}b=0\Rightarrow a\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}b\Rightarrow a\perp_{BJ}b.

Recently, in
[
2
]
the orthograph related to
BJ
-orthogonality has been introduced.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
A
be a
C^{*}
-algebra, and let
S
be the corresponding projective space, i.e.
S=(A\setminus\{0\})/a\sim\lambda a
, where
\lambda
runs through
\mathbb{C}
.

The orthograph
\Gamma(A)
related to the mutual strong
BJ
-orthogonality is the graph with
S
as a set of vertices and with edges consisting of those pairs
(a,b)\in S\times S
for which
a\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}b
.

Then in
[
10
]
it is shown what isolated vertices in
\Gamma(A)
are, which
C^{*}
-algebras have only one connected component and that diameter of arbitrary
C^{*}
-algebras is at most 4. At the end of the article, the problem of determining the exact diameter for finite dimensional
C^{*}
-algebras is posed. In this paper, we give a complete answer to this problem and we solve some related problems connected with distance between non-isolated vertices in ortograph in arbitrary
C^{*}
-algebra.

First, in standard books dealing with
C^{*}
-algebras, like
[
13
]
and
[
14
]
, the structure of finite dimensional
C^{*}
-algebras is given.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let A be a finite dimensional
C^{*}
-algebra. Then A is isomorphic to a direct sum of matrix algebras, i.e., there exist positive integers
n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{k}
such that

A\cong M_{n_{1}}(\mathbb{C})\oplus M_{n_{2}}(\mathbb{C})\oplus\dots M_{n_{k}}(\mathbb{C}).

Recall that for unital
C^{*}
-algebras
A
and
B
,
A\oplus B
is
C^{*}
-algebra equipped with a norm

\|a\oplus b\|=\max\{\|a\|,\|b\|\}.

We will use a shorter notation
(a,b)
instead of
a\oplus b
. Recall that from Lemma 5.3. from
[
10
]
, we know that
(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k})
is non-isolated vertex in unital
C^{*}
-algebra iff there is some
a_{i}
that is non-invertible.

The following theorem is crucial in examining whether two matrices are mutually strong orthogonal or not (see
[
4
]
, Proposition 2.8.).

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
a,b\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. Then
a\mathrel{\perp}^{S}b
if there exists a unit vector
x\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that

\|ax\|=\|a\|\ \text{and}\ b^{*}ax=0.

In paper
[
2
]
, among other things, the diameter of the orthograph for the
C^{*}
-algebra
B(H)
, where
H
is the Hilbert space, is determined. We will use Theorem 2.9. from
[
2
]
in the matrix framework.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be the set of all nonzero non-invertible matrices in
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
(up to the scalar multiplication). Then:

(1)

a
is an isolated vertex of
\Gamma(M_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
if and only if
a
is invertible.

(2)

If
n=1
,
S=\emptyset
.

(3)

If
n=2
, then
S
in disconnected. The connected components of the orthograph
\Gamma(M_{2}(\mathbb{C}))
are either isolated vertices or the sets of the form

\mathcal{S}_{x}=\{a\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})\ |\ \mathrm{Im}\,a=Lin\{x\}\ \text{or}\ \mathrm{Im}\,a=Lin\{x\}^{\perp}\}.

(4)

If
n=3
, then
S
is a connected component whose diameter is 4.

(5)

If
n\geqslant 4
, then
S
is a connected component whose diameter is 3.

In paper
[
10
]
, the following lemma is proved.

Lemma 1.4
.

If
a_{1}\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}a_{2}
and
b_{1}\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}b_{2}
then
(a_{1},b_{1})\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}(a_{2},b_{2})
. In particular

(a,\textbf{0})\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}(\textbf{0},b)

whenever
a
,
b\neq\textbf{0}
.

Of course, it simply translates to the case with a finite number of coordinates.

The last lemma is not valid in reverse direction, and that is the main problem in direct sum
C^{*}
-algebra.

Example 1.1
.

Namely, notice that if
I
is an identity matrix and
P
is rank one projection in
M_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, then
(I,P)\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}(P,I)
, but
I\not\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}P
, since
I\mathrel{\perp}^{S}P
and
P\not\mathrel{\perp}^{S}I
.

2.
Distance between vertices in direct sum
C^{*}
-algebras

We suppose that all
A_{1},\dots,A_{k}
are unital
C^{*}
-algebras. Some statements can easily be expressed in non-unital case, but the goal of this paper are finite-dimensional algebras that are necessarily unital. In this section we consider the direct sum
C^{*}
-algebra
A=A_{1}\oplus A_{2}\oplus\dots\oplus A_{k}
, where
k\geqslant 2
.

Theorem 2.1
.

If there exist two not right invertible elements
a_{i}\neq\textbf{0}
,
b_{j}\neq\textbf{0}
and
(i\neq j)
, then distance between
(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k})\ \text{and}\ (b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{k})\in A
is at most 3.

Proof.

Since
a_{i}
and
b_{j}
are not right invertible, there are
a_{i}^{\prime}\neq\textbf{0}
and
b_{j}^{\prime}\neq\textbf{0}
such that
a_{i}\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}a_{i}^{\prime}
(in
A_{i}
) and
a_{j}\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}a_{j}^{\prime}
(in
A_{j}
) (see
[
10
]
, Propostion 2.4.). Then, it is true that

(a_{1},\dots,a_{i},\dots,a_{k})\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}(\textbf{0},\dots,a_{i}^{\prime},\dots,\textbf{0})\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}(\textbf{0},\dots,b_{j}^{\prime},\dots,\textbf{0})\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}(b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{k}),

by Lemma
1.4
because all the elements are mutually strong orthogonal in terms of coordinates.
∎

Since we will be interested in when the diameter of the orthograph will be greater than 3, we can assume that there are no right non-invertible in different places. Therefore, in the following theorems, we will observe the cases in which right non-invertibles are at the same position, and it will be important whether the norm is reached on them or not.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k}),(b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{k})\in A
be mutual strong
BJ
-orthogonal and
a_{k}
is the only not right invertible element
(\text{in}\ (a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k}))
. Suppose that
\|a_{k}\|>\|a_{i}\|
for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant k-1
. Then
a_{k}\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}b_{k}\ (in\ A_{k})
and
\|b_{k}\|\geqslant\|b_{i}\|
for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant k-1
(especially,
b_{k}\neq 0)
.

Proof.

We know that

\max\{(\|a_{1}+b_{1}c_{1}\|,\|a_{2}+b_{2}c_{2}\|,\dots,\|a_{k}+b_{k}c_{k}\|)\}\geqslant\|a_{k}\|,

for all
(c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{k})\in A
, so if we put
c_{1}=c_{2}=\dots=c_{k-1}=\textbf{0}
, we get
\|a_{k}+b_{k}c_{k}\|\geqslant\|a_{k}\|
for all
c_{k}\in A_{k}
. So,
a_{k}\mathrel{\perp}^{S}b_{k}
.

Next, we know that

\max\{\|b_{1}+a_{1}c_{1}\|,\|b_{2}+a_{2}c_{2}\|,\dots,\|b_{k}+a_{k}c_{k}\|\}\geqslant\max\{\|b_{1}\|,\|b_{2}\|,\dots,\|b_{k}\|\}.

If we put
c_{i}=-a_{i}^{-1}b_{i}
(
a_{i}^{-1}
is right inverse of
a_{i}
) for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant k-1
, we get

\|b_{k}+a_{k}c_{k}\|\geqslant\max\{\|b_{1}\|,\|b_{2}\|,\dots,\|b_{k}\|\}\geqslant\|b_{k}\|,

so
b_{k}\mathrel{\perp}^{S}a_{k}
and if we put
c_{k}=0
, we get
\|b_{k}\|\geqslant\|b_{i}\|
for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant k-1
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k}),(b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{k})\in A
. Suppose that
\|a_{k}\|\geqslant\|a_{i}\|
,
\|b_{k}\|\geqslant\|b_{i}\|
for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant k-1
and
a_{k}\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}b_{k}
. Then

(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k})\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}(b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{k}).

Proof.

For all
c_{k}\in A_{k}
it is true that
\|a_{k}+b_{k}c_{k}\|\geqslant\|a_{k}\|
and
\|b_{k}+a_{k}c_{k}\|\geqslant\|b_{k}\|
. So

\begin{split}&\max\{\|a_{1}+b_{1}c_{1}\|,\|a_{2}+b_{2}c_{2}\|,\dots,\|a_{k}+b_{k}c_{k}\|\}\\
&\geqslant\|a_{k}+b_{k}c_{k}\|\geqslant\|a_{k}\|=\max\{\|a_{1}\|,\|a_{2}\|,\dots,\|a_{k}\|\},\end{split}

so
(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k})\mathrel{\perp}^{S}(b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{k})
. The second direction is proved analogously.
∎

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k}),(b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{k})\in A
such that
a_{k}
and
b_{k}
are only not right invertible and there exist
a_{i}(i\neq k)
and
b_{j}(j\neq k)
such that
\|a_{i}\|\geqslant\|a_{k}\|
and
\|b_{j}\|\geqslant\|b_{k}\|
. Then the distance between
(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k})
and
(b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{k})
is at most 2.

Proof.

Notice that
(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k})\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}(\textbf{0},\textbf{0},\dots,\textbf{1})\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}(b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{k})
. It is enough to prove the first one. For all
(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{k})\in A
it is true that

\begin{split}\max\{\|a_{1}+\textbf{0}x_{1}\|,\|a_{2}+\textbf{0}x_{2}\|,\dots,\|a_{k}+\textbf{1}x_{k}\|\}&\geqslant\max\{\|a_{1}\|,\|a_{2}\|,\dots,\|a_{k-1}\|\}\\
&=\max\{\|a_{1}\|,\dots,\|a_{k-1}\|,\|a_{k}\|\},\end{split}

where the last equality is valid because
\|a_{k}\|\leqslant\|a_{i}\|
.

Further, in every unital
C^{*}
-algebra it is true that
\textbf{1}\mathrel{\perp}^{S}a_{k}
for every not right invertible
a_{k}
. Indeed, there is a pure state
\rho
such that
\rho(a_{k}a_{k}^{*})=0
because
a_{k}
is not right invertible. In the end,
\rho(\textbf{1}\textbf{1}^{*})=1=\|\textbf{1}\|^{2}
, because
\rho
is state. So, by Lemma 2.3. from
[
10
]
, we get
\textbf{1}\mathrel{\perp}^{S}a_{k}
. From there, for any
x_{k}\in A_{k}
,
\|\textbf{1}+a_{k}x_{k}\|\geqslant\|\textbf{1}\|
, and finally for every
(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{k})\in A
it is true that

\max\{\|\textbf{0}+a_{1}x_{1}\|,\|\textbf{0}+a_{2}x_{2}\|,\dots,\|\textbf{1}+a_{k}x_{k}\|\}\geqslant\|\textbf{1}\|=\max\{\|\textbf{0}\|,\|\textbf{0}\|,\dots,\|\textbf{1}\|\}.

∎

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k}),(b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{k})\in A
such that
a_{k}
and
b_{k}
are only not right invertible. Further, let there be
a_{i}(i\neq k)
such that
\|a_{i}\|\geqslant\|a_{k}\|
and let
\|b_{k}\|>\|b_{j}\|
for every
j\in[1,k-1]
. Then distance between
(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k})
and
(b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{k})
is at most 3.

Proof.

Because
b_{k}
is not right invertible, there is non-zero not right invertible
b_{k}^{\prime}
such that
b_{k}\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}b_{k}^{\prime}
. But then it is true

(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k})\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}(\textbf{0},\textbf{0},\dots,\textbf{1})\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}(\|b_{k}^{\prime}\|\textbf{1},\|b_{k}^{\prime}\|\textbf{1},\dots,b_{k}^{\prime})\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}(b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{k}).

First mutual strong
BJ
-orthogonality is proved in Theorem
2.4
, as well as the first direction of the second mutual strong
BJ
-orthogonality. Reverse direction is trivial because for all
(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{k})\in A

\begin{split}&\max\{\|\|b_{k}^{\prime}\|\textbf{1}+\textbf{0}x_{1}\|,\|\|b_{k}^{\prime}\|\textbf{1}+\textbf{0}x_{2}\|,\dots,\|b_{k}^{\prime}+\textbf{1}x_{k}\|\}\\
&\geqslant\|b_{k}^{\prime}\|=\max\{\|\|b_{k}^{\prime}\|\textbf{1}\|,\|\|b_{k}^{\prime}\|\textbf{1}\|,\dots,\|b_{k}^{\prime}\|\}.\end{split}

Third mutual strong
BJ
-orthogonality is true by Lemma
2.3
, which concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

Therefore, the only case in which the distance can be greater than 3 (then it is 4, because it is always smaller than 5) is when both right non-invertibles are such that the norm is reached on them.

3.
Diameter of finite dimensional
C^{*}
-algebras

According to the remark
2.1
, the problem arises in the case in which the Theorem
2.2
should be applied. In the case where
A_{k}=\mathbb{C}
, there is no non-invertible such that its norm is greater than the other elements (
0
is the only one). Also, the problem in the same case is to determine the diameter of the algebra
A_{k}
. We distinguish the cases when it is
\mathbb{C},M_{2}(\mathbb{C}),M_{3}(\mathbb{C})
or
M_{n}(\mathbb{C}),n\geqslant 4
. Let us recall that in
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
the fact that an element is not right invertible is equivalent to the fact that it is non-invertible.

3.1.
Case of
\mathbb{C}^{k},k\in\mathbb{N}

3.1.1.
Case of
\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C}

There are only two nonisolated vertices in this
C^{*}
-algebra,
(0,1)
and
(1,0)
. They are mutual strong orthogonal, so, there is only one connected component and its diameter is 1.

3.1.2.
Case of
\mathbb{C}^{k},k\geqslant 3

We will show that
\diam(\mathbb{C}^{k})=3
for all
k\geqslant 3
. If
a_{k}
and
b_{k}
are non-invertible (so
a_{k}=b_{k}=0
), by Theorem
2.4
their distance is at most 2. And if they are on different positions, their distance is at most 3 by Theorem
2.1
.

It remains to find the vertices whose distance is 3.

Example 3.1
.

The distance between
(0,1,2,1,\dots,1)
and
(2,0,1,1,\dots,1)
is 3.

If
(0,1,2,1,\dots,1)\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},\dots,a_{n})
, then for all
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{C}

\|(0+a_{1}\lambda_{1},1+a_{2}\lambda_{2},2+a_{3}\lambda_{3},\dots,1+\lambda_{n}a_{n})\|\geqslant 2,

and if
a_{3}\neq 0
, we just put
\lambda_{3}=-\frac{2}{a_{3}}
and
\lambda_{i}=0
for
i\neq 3
and get a contradiction. So
a_{3}=0
. Next, for all
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
it must be true

\|(a_{1}+0\lambda_{1},a_{2}+1\lambda_{2},0+2\lambda_{3},\dots,a_{n}+1\lambda_{n})\|\geqslant\max\{\|a_{1}\|,\|a_{2}\|,\dots,\|a_{n}\|\}.

Now put
\lambda_{k}=-a_{k}
for
k\neq 3
and
\lambda_{3}=0
and get
\|a_{1}\|\geqslant\|a_{k}\|
for all
k\in[1,n]
.

In the same way, if
(2,0,1,1,\dots,1)\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3},\dots,b_{n})
we get
b_{1}=0
and
\|b_{2}\|\geqslant\|b_{k}\|
for all
k\in[1,n]
.

So it is not true that
(0,1,2,1,\dots,1)\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}(2,0,1,1,\dots,1)
.

If there is some
(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},\dots,a_{n})
mutual strong orthogonal to both vertices, than
a_{1}=0
and its norm is greater or equal to all others, so,
a_{n}=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Then the distance is greater than 2, which implies that it is 3.

So we proved

Lemma 3.1
.

\diam(\mathbb{C}^{2})=1
and
\diam(\mathbb{C}^{k})=3
for
k\geqslant 3
.

Remark 3.1
.

It is interesting that diameter in
\mathbb{C}^{3}
(with a
\max
norm) is smaller than the diameter in orthograph related to standard Birkhoff-James orthogonality (which is weaker) considered in
[
3
]
. In that case, the diameter is 4 (see Propostion 5.15). This is because in the case of strong
BJ
-orthogonality there are more isolated vertices, so despite the stronger relation, the number of vertices in connected component is smaller.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
A=M_{n_{1}}(\mathbb{C})\oplus M_{n_{2}}(\mathbb{C})\oplus\dots M_{n_{k}}(\mathbb{C}),\ k\geqslant 2
such that
A\not\cong\mathbb{C}^{n}
for an arbitrary
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
\diam A\geqslant 3
.

Proof.

Let us assume, say
M_{n_{k}}(\mathbb{C})
such that
n_{k}\geqslant 2
. By Theorem
1.3
, we know that diameter of
M_{n_{k}}(\mathbb{C})
is bigger than 2, or has two disconnected components (in the case of
M_{2}(\mathbb{C})
). Be that as it may, we can choose two non-invertible, non-zero elements
a_{k},b_{k}\in M_{n_{k}}(\mathbb{C})
such that their distance is bigger than 2. If we choose all other
a_{i},b_{i}\ (i\in[1,k-1])
to be invertible and with a smaller norm than
\|a_{k}\|
and
\|b_{k}\|
, respectively, by Theorem
2.2
we immediately conclude that
\diam A>2
. Otherwise,
(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k})
and
(b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{k})
are mutual strong
BJ
-orthogonal or there is
(c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{k})
mutual strong
BJ
-orthogonal to both. But again by Theorem
2.2
, this is impossible, because distance between
a_{k}
and
b_{k}
is bigger than 2.
∎

For the sake of clarity, we will first examine cases with two summands, and then move on the cases with three or more.

3.2.
Cases with two summands

3.2.1.
Case of
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})\oplus M_{k}(\mathbb{C}),n,k\geqslant 2

We will prove that
\diam(M_{n}(\mathbb{C})\oplus M_{k}(\mathbb{C}))
is equal to 3. We know by Theorem
3.2
that diameter is at least 3, and by theorems
2.1
,
2.2
,
2.4
,
2.5
, we must examine only the case where non-invertible elements are at the same place and have strictly bigger norm than invertible elements.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(a,b),(c,d)\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})\oplus M_{k}(\mathbb{C})
such that
a,b
are non-invertible and
c,d
are invertible matrices. Further, suppose that
\|a\|>\|b\|
and
\|c\|>\|d\|
. Then the distance between them is at most 3.

Proof.

Because
a
and
c
are non-invertible, there are non-zero, non-invertible
a_{1}
and
c_{1}
such that
a\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}a_{1}
and
c\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}c_{1}
. Then it is true

(a,b)\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}(a_{1},\diag(\|a_{1}\|,0,\dots,0))\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}(c_{1},\diag(0,\dots,0,\|c_{1}\|))\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}(c,d),

where
\diag(\|a_{1}\|,0,\dots,0)
denotes
k\times k
diagonal matrix which only non-zero entry is in the first row and the first column and it is
\|a_{1}\|
.

First and third mutual strong
BJ
-orthogonality is valid by Lemma
2.3
.

Finally, let us note that
(\diag(\|a_{1}\|,0,\dots,0))^{*}\diag(0,\dots,0,\|c_{1}\|)=\textbf{0}
, so they are mutual strong
BJ
-orthogonal. Again, by Lemma
2.3
, second
BJ
-orthogonality is valid.
∎

Thus, we proved

Lemma 3.4
.

\diam(M_{n}(\mathbb{C})\oplus M_{k}(\mathbb{C}))=3
, for all
n,k\geqslant 2
.

3.2.2.
Case of
\mathbb{C}\oplus M_{2}(\mathbb{C})

We will prove that
\diam(\mathbb{C}\oplus M_{2}(\mathbb{C}))=4
. Because in every
C^{*}
-algebra diameter is at most 4, it is enough to find two vertices whose distance is exactly 4.

Example 3.2
.

Distance between
\left(1,\begin{bmatrix}2&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}\right)
and
\left(1,\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\
1&1\end{bmatrix}\right)
is 4.

Norm of both matrices is 2. By Theorem
2.2
, we know that these two are not mutual strong orthogonal. Moreover, if there exist
(a,b)\in\mathbb{C}\oplus M_{2}(\mathbb{C})
that is mutual strong orthogonal to both, then again by Theorem
2.2
,
b\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}\begin{bmatrix}2&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}
and
b\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\
1&1\end{bmatrix}
and
\|b\|\geqslant\|a\|
. This is not possible, because by Theorem
1.3
(3)
\begin{bmatrix}2&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}b=\textbf{0}
and
\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\
1&1\end{bmatrix}b=\textbf{0}
. If
b=\begin{bmatrix}b_{11}&b_{12}\\
b_{21}&b_{22}\end{bmatrix}
, then
2b_{11}=0,\ 2b_{12}=0,\ b_{11}+b_{21}=0
and
b_{12}+b_{22}=0
, so
b=\textbf{0}
, by then
a=0
which is not possible.

In the end, if we suppose that there exist two non-zero
(a,b),(c,d)\in\mathbb{C}\oplus M_{2}(\mathbb{C})
such that

\left(1,\begin{bmatrix}2&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}\right)\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}(a,b)\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}(c,d)\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}\left(1,\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\
1&1\end{bmatrix}\right),

we know (by Theorem
2.2
) that
b\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}\begin{bmatrix}2&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}
and
d\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\
1&1\end{bmatrix}
, but also
\|b\|\geqslant\|a\|
and
\|d\|\geqslant\|c\|
. There are three cases:

1.

a=c=0
: Then
b\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}d
, but this is not possible, because the images of matrices are not the same or orthogonal in the usual sense (and both are different from
0
).

2.

a
and
c
are both invertible: Then again it must be
b\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}d
, which is again not possible. Indeed, it is true that for all
(x,y)\in\mathbb{C}\oplus M_{2}(\mathbb{C})

\max\{(|a+cx|,\|b+dy\|)\}\geqslant\max\{|a|,\|b\|\}=\|b\|,

and if we put
x=-c^{-1}a
, we get
b\mathrel{\perp}^{S}d
. In the same way, we prove the other direction.

3.

a
is invertible and
c=0
(the other direction is done analogously). Then for all
(x,y)\in\mathbb{C}\oplus M_{2}(\mathbb{C})

\max\{|0+ax|,\|d+by\|\}\geqslant\max\{|0|,\|d\|\}=\|d\|,

so if
x=0
, it must be
d\mathrel{\perp}^{S}b
. But this is not possible either (strong
BJ
-orthogonality in one direction). Indeed, we know that
b_{11}=b_{12}=0
(for the same reason as in the first part of the case when we considered that the distance is 2) and
\mathrm{Im}\left(\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\
1&1\end{bmatrix}\right)=\left\{\lambda\begin{bmatrix}1\\
1\end{bmatrix}\ |\ \lambda\in\mathbb{C}\right\}
, so
\mathrm{Im}\,d=\left\{\lambda\begin{bmatrix}1\\
-1\end{bmatrix}\ |\ \lambda\in\mathbb{C}\right\}
(because
d\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\
1&1\end{bmatrix}
). Because
d\mathrel{\perp}^{S}b
, by Theorem
1.2
there exists a unit vector
y\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
such that
\|dy\|=\|d\|
and
b^{*}dy=\textbf{0}
. So, knowing the image of the matrix
d
,
dy=\lambda\begin{bmatrix}1\\
-1\end{bmatrix}
for some
\lambda\neq 0
, and then
b^{*}\begin{bmatrix}1\\
-1\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}-\overline{b_{21}}\\
-\overline{b_{22}}\end{bmatrix}
, so
b=\textbf{0}
which is impossible.

So, it is true:

Lemma 3.5
.

\diam(\mathbb{C}\oplus M_{2}(\mathbb{C}))=4
.

3.2.3.
Case of
\mathbb{C}\oplus M_{3}(\mathbb{C})

We will show that diameter is 3 in this case. As in the previous cases, we only have to consider the situation when the non-invertibles are at the same position and their norms are strictly greater than the norm of invertible ones. This is possible only if they are non-invertible in
M_{3}(\mathbb{C})
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
(a,b),(c,d)\in\mathbb{C}\oplus M_{3}(\mathbb{C})
such that
a,b
are invertible (so different from
0
) and
c,d
are non-invertible matrices. Further, suppose that
\|a\|<\|b\|
and
\|c\|<\|d\|
. Then the distance between them is at most 3.

Proof.

The idea is to construct
e,f\in M_{3}(\mathbb{C})
such that
b\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}e
and
d\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}f
, but with the additional property that
e\mathrel{\perp}^{S}f
(note that mutual strong
BJ
-orthogonality is not possible in the general case). Then it is true that

(a,b)\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}(0,e)\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}(\|f\|,f)\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}(c,d).

Namely, first and third mutual strong
BJ
-orthogonality is valid by Lemma
2.3
, as well as the second in the left-to-right direction. Also, for all
(x,y)\in\mathbb{C}\oplus M_{3}(\mathbb{C})
it is true that

\max\{|\|f\|+0x|,\|f+ey\|\}\geqslant\|f\|=\max\{|\|f\||,\|f\|\},

which proves the missing direction. So, what remains is to construct
e
and
f
.

There exist unit vectors
x,y\in\mathbb{C}^{3}
such that
\|bx\|=\|b\|
and
\|dy\|=\|d\|
. Also, because
b
and
d
are non-invertible, there exists unit vectors
v_{b}\in\Ker b^{*}
and
v_{d}\in\Ker d^{*}
. There exist unit vector
w\in Lin\{bx,v_{d}\}^{\perp}
(dimension is 3). We define
e
with
ev_{b}=v_{b},ew=w
and
e|_{Lin\{v_{b},w\}^{\perp}}=\textbf{0}
and
f
with
fv_{d}=v_{d},f|_{\{v_{d}\}^{\perp}}=\textbf{0}
.

Let us notice that
\langle bx,ev_{b}\rangle=\langle bx,v_{b}\rangle=\langle x,b^{*}v_{b}\rangle=\langle x,0\rangle=0
and
\langle bx,ew\rangle=\langle bx,w\rangle=0
because
bx\perp w
. So,
bx\perp\mathrm{Im}\,e
from where we conclude
e^{*}bx=\textbf{0}
. As
\|bx\|=\|b\|
, by Theorem
1.2
we get
b\mathrel{\perp}^{S}e
. Further, we know that it holds
\|ev_{b}\|=\|v_{b}\|=1=\|e\|
and
b^{*}ev_{b}=b^{*}v_{b}=\textbf{0}
, because
v_{b}\in\Ker b^{*}
. Again, by Theorem
1.2
we get
b\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}e
. In a similar way, we get
dy\perp\mathrm{Im}\,f
, so
f^{*}dy=\textbf{0}
. As
\|dy\|=\|d\|
, it holds
d\mathrel{\perp}^{S}f
. Also,
\|fv_{d}\|=\|v_{d}\|=1=\|f\|
and
d^{*}fv_{d}=d^{*}v_{d}=\textbf{0}
, so
d\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}f
. In the end,
\|ew\|=\|w\|=1=\|e\|
and
f^{*}ew=f^{*}w=\textbf{0}
because
w\perp v_{d}
, so we proved
e\mathrel{\perp}^{S}f
, which completes the proof.
∎

Thus we proved

Lemma 3.7
.

\diam(\mathbb{C}\oplus M_{3}(\mathbb{C}))=3
.

Case of
\mathbb{C}\oplus M_{n}(\mathbb{C}),n\geqslant 4

By Theorem

is at least 3. By Theorem
2.2
, we need to examine only the case where non-invertibles are the biggest in respect to norm and at the same position. The only possible situation is that they are in

0

3.3.
Case with three or more summands

We will prove that the diameter of these finite dimensional
C^{*}
-algebras is equal to 3 in case
A\not\cong\mathbb{C}^{n}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, which is the only case left to examine. So, we can assume that some
n_{i}
is greater than 1 and that non-invertible elements
a_{i},b_{i}\in M_{n_{i}}
have strictly greater norm with respect to other elements.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
k\geqslant 3,n_{i}\geqslant 2
and

(a_{1},\dots,a_{i},\dots,a_{k}),(b_{1},\dots,b_{i},\dots,b_{k})\in A=M_{n_{1}}(\mathbb{C})\oplus\dots M_{n_{i}}(\mathbb{C})\oplus\dots M_{n_{k}}(\mathbb{C}),

such that
a_{i},b_{i}
are the only non-invertible elements and
\|a_{i}\|>\|a_{j}\|,\|b_{i}\|>\|b_{j}\|
for all
j\neq i
. Than the distance between them is at most 3.

Proof.

Because
a_{i},b_{i}
are non-invertible, there are non-zero non-invertible
a_{i}^{\prime}
and
b_{i}^{\prime}
such that
a_{i}\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}a_{i}^{\prime}
and
b_{i}\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}b_{i}^{\prime}
. We will prove that

\begin{split}(a_{1},\dots,a_{i},\dots,a_{k})&\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}(\|a_{i}^{\prime}\|\textbf{1},\dots,a_{i}^{\prime},\dots,\textbf{0})\\
&\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}(\textbf{0},\dots,b_{i}^{\prime}\,\dots,\|b_{i}^{\prime}\|\textbf{1})\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\!\perp}^{S}(b_{1},\dots,b_{i},\dots,b_{k}),\end{split}

where all other elements are
0
.

Namely, first and third mutual strong
BJ
-orthogonality is valid by Lemma
2.3
. It remains to prove the second orthogonality. For all
(x_{1},\dots,x_{i},\dots,x_{k})\in A
, it is true that

\begin{split}&\max\{\|\|a_{i}^{\prime}\|\textbf{1}+\textbf{0}x_{1}\|,\dots,\|a_{i}^{\prime}+b_{i}^{\prime}x_{i}\|,\dots,\|\textbf{0}+\|b_{i}^{\prime}\|\textbf{1}x_{k}\|\}\\
&\geqslant\|\|a_{i}^{\prime}\|\textbf{1}\|=\max\{\|\|a_{i}^{\prime}\|\textbf{1}\|,\dots,\|a_{i}^{\prime}\|,\dots,\|\textbf{0}\|\},\end{split}

which proves that
(\|a_{i}^{\prime}\|\textbf{1},\dots,a_{i}^{\prime},\dots,\textbf{0})\mathrel{\perp}^{S}(\textbf{0},\dots,b_{i}^{\prime}\,\dots,\|b_{i}^{\prime}\|\textbf{1})
. The second direction is proved analogously.
∎

So we proved

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
A=M_{n_{1}}(\mathbb{C})\oplus\dots M_{n_{i}}(\mathbb{C})\oplus\dots M_{n_{k}}(\mathbb{C}),k\geqslant 3
and
A\not\cong\mathbb{C}^{n}
for an arbitrary
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
\diam A=3
.

We will conclude this chapter by summarizing the results we have obtained in it, expressing them in one theorem.

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
A=M_{n_{1}}(\mathbb{C})\oplus M_{n_{2}}(\mathbb{C})\oplus\dots M_{n_{k}}(\mathbb{C}),k\geqslant 2
. Then:

(1)

\diam(\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C})=1
;

(2)

\diam(\mathbb{C}\oplus M_{2}(\mathbb{C}))=4
;

(3)

\diam(A)=3
in all other cases.

Proof.

The proof is contained in Lemmata
3.1
,
3.4
,
3.5
,
3.7
,
3.8
,
3.10
.
∎

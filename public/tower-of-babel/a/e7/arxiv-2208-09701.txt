On the equivalence issue of a class of 2 2 -dimensional linear maximum rank-metric codes
Somi Gupta, Giovanni Longobardi, Rocco Trombetti

Abstract
In [A. Neri, P. Santonastaso, F. Zullo. Extending two families of maximum rank distance codes ], the authors extended the family of 2 2 -dimensional 𝔽 q 2 ​ t \mathbb{F}_{q^{2t}} -linear MRD codes recently found in [G. Longobardi, G. Marino, R. Trombetti, Y. Zhou. A large family of maximum scattered linear sets of PG ⁡ ( 1 , q n ) \mathrm{PG}(1,q^{n}) and their associated MRD codes ]. Also, for t ≥ 5 t\geq 5 they determined equivalence classes of the elements in this new family and provided the exact number of inequivalent codes in it. In this article, we complete the study of the equivalence issue removing the restriction t ≥ 5 t\geq 5 . Moreover, we prove that in the case when t = 4 t=4 , the linear sets of the projective line PG ⁡ ( 1 , q 8 ) \mathrm{PG}(1,q^{8}) ensuing from codes in the relevant family, are not equivalent to any one known so far.

Introduction

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
q
a prime power and denote by
{\mathcal{L}}_{n,q}[x]
the vector space of all
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linearized polynomials with coefficients over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
(also known as
q
-
polynomials
); i.e.,

{\mathcal{L}}_{n,q}[x]=\Big\{\sum_{i=0}^{r}c_{i}x^{q^{i}}\,\colon\,c_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\,\text{ and }\,r\geq 0\Big\}.

It is well known that

\tilde{{\mathcal{L}}}_{n,q}[x]:={\mathcal{L}}_{n,q}[x]/(x^{q^{n}}-x)=\Big\{\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}c_{i}x^{q^{i}}\,\colon\,c_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\Big\},

is isomorphic to the algebra
{\rm End}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})
of the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear endomorphisms of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
,
[
11
]
. Moreover, the distance between two different such endomorphisms
f(x)
and
g(x)
, defined as

d(f,g)={\rm dim}({\rm im}(f-g)),

gives rise to a metric on
\tilde{{\mathcal{L}}}_{n,q}[x]
, which is called the
rank metric
. In general, if
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
are two subsets of
\tilde{{\mathcal{L}}}_{n,q}[x]
, we say that they are
equivalent
if there exist
\rho\in{\rm Aut}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})
and two permutation polynomials
L_{1}(x)
and
L_{2}(x)
such that

\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\{(L_{1}\circ f^{\rho}\circ L_{2})(x)\,:\,f\in\mathcal{C}\},

where
f^{\rho}(x)=\big(\sum a_{i}x^{q^{i}}\big)^{\rho}=\sum a^{\rho}_{i}x^{q^{i}}
. We denote by
\tau=(L_{1},L_{2},\rho),
the equivalence defined above and consequently denote by
\tau(f)
the elements in
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
, see
[
18
]
.

A
linear rank-metric
code
{\cal C}\leq\tilde{{\mathcal{L}}}_{n,q}[x]
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace of the vector space
\tilde{{\mathcal{L}}}_{n,q}[x]
endowed with the above mentioned rank metric
d(\cdot,\cdot)
.
The minimum of the set of all distances between two distinct words of a linear rank-metric code
{\cal C}\leq\tilde{{\mathcal{L}}}_{n,q}[x]
,
|\mathcal{C}|\geq 2
, is denoted by
d_{\cal C},
and one has

d_{\cal C}={\rm min}\{{\rm dim}({\rm im}(f))\,:\,f\in{\cal C}\setminus\{0\}\}.

If
{\rm dim}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}({\cal C})=k
, then
d_{\cal C}\leq n-\frac{k}{n}+1
and in the case where the equality is attained,
{\cal C}
is said to be a
linear maximum rank-metric code
(linear MRD code, for short).

Due to their application in network coding, the interest
in the rank-metric codes has intensified over the past years and many recent papers have been
devoted to their study. In addition, linear rank metric codes are also linked with interesting algebraic and geometric objects like
q
-polymatroids
and
linear sets
of the projective space, see
[
9
,
20
]
.

Motivated by what is stated in Section
5
of
[
19
]
, in this article, we will focus on linear MRD codes with minimum distance
d=n-1
that are
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
-subspaces of
\mathcal{\tilde{L}}_{n,q}[x]
. Precisely, in
[
19
, Theorem 3.8]
, the authors introduce a new class of such MRD codes extending two constructions exhibited in
[
15
]
and
[
12
]
. This family is denoted by the symbol
{\mathcal{C}}_{h,t,s}
, where
t\in\mathbb{N}
and
t\geq 3
,
h\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2t}}
such that
h^{q^{t}+1}=-1
and
s\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
(s,2t)=1
. Also, in
[
19
]
, it was proven that if

then
{\mathcal{C}}_{h,t,s}
is not equivalent to any other example known in the literature so far.

Moreover, the following result was proven concerning the equivalence of two elements in
{\mathcal{C}}_{h,t,s}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
19
, Theorem 4.6]
Let
t\geq 5
and consider
{\mathcal{C}}_{h,t,s}
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{k,t,\ell}
such that
(s,n)=1=(\ell,n)
. Then the codes
{\mathcal{C}}_{h,t,s}
and
\mathcal{C}_{k,t,\ell}
are equivalent if and only if one of the following collection of conditions are satisfied:

I.

s\equiv\ell\pmod{n}
, and there exists
\rho\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})
such that

\rho(h)=\begin{cases}\pm k,&\,\text{\rm if}\,t\not\equiv 2\,\pmod{4}\\
\lambda k,\text{ \rm where }\lambda^{q^{2}+1}=1&\text{ \rm if}\,t\equiv 2\,\pmod{4}\end{cases}

II.

s\equiv-\ell\,\pmod{n}
, and there exists
\rho\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})
such that

\rho(h)=\begin{cases}\pm k^{-1},&\,\text{\rm if}\,t\not\equiv 2\pmod{\,}4\\
\lambda k^{-1},\text{ \rm where }\lambda^{q^{2}+1}=1&\text{ \rm if}\,t\equiv 2\pmod{\,}4\end{cases}

III.

if
s\equiv(t-1)\ell\,\pmod{n}
,

even, and there exists
\rho\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})
such that

\rho(h)=\begin{cases}\pm k,&\,\text{\rm if}\,t\not\equiv 2\pmod{4}\\
\lambda k,\text{ \rm where }\lambda^{q^{2}+1}=1&\text{ \rm if}\,t\equiv 2\pmod{4}\end{cases}

IV.

s\equiv(t+1)\ell\,\pmod{n}
,
t
even, and there exists
\rho\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})
such that

\rho(h)=\begin{cases}\pm k^{-1},&\,\text{\rm if}\,t\not\equiv 2\,\pmod{4}\\
\lambda k^{-1},\text{ \rm where }\lambda^{q^{2}+1}=1&\text{ \rm if}\,t\equiv 2\,\pmod{4}\end{cases}.

Clearly the necessity in the statement of this theorem, still holds true if

In
[

]

-polynomial is called

and the

-linear

In
[

-

and

-equivalent (

\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}y^{\sigma}\\
f(y)^{\sigma}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}z\\
g(z)\end{pmatrix},

In order to extend Theorem 1.1 stated in the Introduction by removing the hypothesis

, as a first step we recap and adapt some concepts and tools which were introduced in
[

Following
[
16
]
, we say that a
q
-polynomial
F(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}c_{i}x^{q^{i}}
is in
standard form
, if the greatest common divisor
m_{F}
of the set of integers

\{(i-j)\pmod{n}\,\colon\,c_{i}c_{j}\neq 0\text{ with }\,i\neq j\}\cup\{n\},

is strictly larger than
1
. If this is the case, then
F(x)
has the following fashion:

F(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n/m-1}c_{j}x^{q^{s+jm}},

(6)

where
m=m_{F}
and
0\leq s<m_{F}
. We stress here that when
F(x)
is a scattered polynomial, then
s
must be coprime with
m_{F}
, otherwise
F(x)
would be
\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}
-linear for
r=(s,m_{F})>1
,
contradicting its scatteredness.

Let
\mathcal{C}\leq\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{n,q}[x]
be a linear rank-metric code. Then, its
left idealizer
and

right idealizer
are defined as the sets

I_{L}(\mathcal{C})=\{\varphi(x)\in\mathcal{\tilde{L}}_{n,q}[x]:\varphi\circ f\in\mathcal{C}\,\,\forall f\in\mathcal{C}\},

and

I_{R}(\mathcal{C})=\{\varphi(x)\in\mathcal{\tilde{L}}_{n,q}[x]:f\circ\varphi\in\mathcal{C}\,\,\forall f\in\mathcal{C}\},

respectively. By
[
14
, Corollary 5.6]
, if
\mathcal{C}
is a linear MRD code,
I_{L}(\mathcal{C})
and
I_{R}(\mathcal{C})
are subalgebras of
\mathcal{\tilde{L}}_{n,q}[x]
; more precisely, they are isomorphic
to subfields of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
. In particular, if
\mathcal{C}
has left idealizer containing a subfield isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, then it is called
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
-
linear
. Note that any MRD code
\mathcal{C}_{f}
associated with a scattered polynomial
f(x)
is
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
-linear.
The idealizers are useful tools for studying the equivalence issue in this context; indeed MRD codes with non-isomorphic left (resp. right) idealizer cannot be equivalent, see
[
14
, Section 4]
.

Now, let
f(x)
be a scattered
q
-polynomial and consider
G_{f}
the stabilizer in
\mathrm{GL}(2,q^{n})
of the scattered subspace
U_{f}
. By

G_{f}^{\circ}=G_{f}\cup\{O\}
, where

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
{\cal C}_{F}=\langle x,F(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}\subset\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{n,q}[x]
be a linear
2
-dimensional MRD code with minimum distance
n-1
.
The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

|G^{\circ}_{F}|=q^{m}
,
m>1
, and all elements of
G^{\circ}_{F}
are diagonal.

(ii)

the
q
-polynomial
F(x)
is in standard form.

(iii)

the right idealizer

I_{R}(\mathcal{C}_{F})=\{\alpha x\,:\,\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}\}.

Moreover, if the above conditions
(i)
,
(ii)
and
(iii)
hold, then
m=m_{F}
and

G^{\circ}_{F}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&0\\
0&\alpha^{q^{s}}\end{pmatrix}\colon\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}\right\},

(7)

with
(s,m)=1
.

Proof.

By
[
16
, Theorem 4.3.]
,
(i)
is equivalent to
(ii)
.

(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)
. Since
F(x)
is as in Formula (
6
), the set

\{\alpha x:\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}\}

is contained in
I_{R}(\mathcal{C}_{F})
. Taking into account
|G^{\circ}_{F}|=|I_{R}(\mathcal{C}_{F})|=q^{m}
,
(iii)
follows.

(iii)\Rightarrow(i)
. Let
\omega\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
such that
\mathbb{F}_{q}(\omega)=\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
. Since
\omega x\in I_{R}(\mathcal{C}_{F})
, then there exists
d\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
such that

F(\omega x)=dF(x).

(8)

This implies that if
F(x)=\sum^{n-1}_{i=1}c_{i}x^{q^{i}}
, then

c_{i}\omega^{q^{i}}=dc_{i}

for any
i=1,\ldots,n-1
.
So, if
c_{i}\not=0
,
d\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
and

for some

.
By (
8
), the matrix

\begin{pmatrix}\omega&0\\
0&\omega^{h}\end{pmatrix}

belongs to
G_{F}
, has order

and, since

, it generates
G_{F}
. This implies
(i)
.
∎

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
{\cal C}_{F_{i}}

-dimensional MRD codes where

,

such that

dF_{2}(x)=F_{1}^{\rho}(ax)\quad\textnormal{or}\quad F_{1}^{\rho}(bF_{2}(x))=cx

(9)

for some
\rho\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})
.
In particular,

(i)

\mathcal{C}_{F_{2}}=\mathcal{C}^{\rho}_{F_{1}}\circ ax
or,

(ii)

\mathcal{C}_{F_{2}}=\mathcal{C}^{\rho}_{F_{1}}\circ bF_{2}(x)
.

Proof.

By
[
22

,
\mathcal{C}_{F_{1}}
and
\mathcal{C}_{F_{2}}
are equivalent codes if and only if

and
F_{2}(x)
are

-equivalent, or equivalently,

and
F_{2}(x)

\mathrm{GL}
-equivalent for some
\rho\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})

A=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{GL}(2,q^{n})
such that for any
y\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, there is
z\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
such that

A\begin{pmatrix}y\\
F_{2}(y)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}z\\
F_{1}^{\rho}(z)\end{pmatrix}.

(10)

Let
G_{F_{1}^{\rho}}
and
G_{F_{2}}
be the stabilizer groups in
\mathrm{GL}(2,q^{n})
of the subspaces
U_{F_{1}^{\rho}}
and
U_{F_{2}}
, respectively.
It is straightforward to see that

A^{-1}G_{F_{1}^{\rho}}A=G_{F_{2}}.

(11)

By Theorem
2.1
,
G_{F_{1}^{\rho}}
and
G_{F_{2}}
are groups made up of diagonal matrices. Therefore, by Formula (

1
Introduction

For irrational number
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
we consider irrationality measure function

\psi_{\xi}(t)=\min_{1\leq q\leq t,\,q\in\mathbb{Z}}\|q\xi\|,

where
||.||
denotes distance to the nearest integer.

Let

Q_{0}\leq Q_{1}<Q_{2}<\ldots<Q_{n}<Q_{n+1}<\ldots

be the sequence of denominators of convergents to
\xi
. It it a well-known fact (see
[
1
]
or
[
2
]
) that

\psi_{\xi}(t)=\|Q_{n}\xi\|\text{ for }Q_{n}\leq t<Q_{n+1}.

(1)

It is clear for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
and for every
t\geq 1
we have

\psi_{\xi}(t)\leq\frac{1}{t}.

(2)

In
[
3
]
, Kan and Moshchevitin proved that for any two irrational numbers
\alpha,\beta
satisfying
\alpha\pm\beta\notin\mathbb{Z}
the difference

\psi_{\alpha}(t)-\phi_{\beta}(t)

changes its sign infinitely many times as
t\to\infty
.

Obviously, it follows that the difference of their reciprocals

d(t)=d_{\alpha,\beta}(t)=\frac{1}{\psi_{\beta}(t)}-\frac{1}{\psi_{\alpha}(t)}

(3)

also changes sign infinitely many times as
t\to\infty
.

In 2017 Dubickas
[
4
]
proved the following result.

Theorem A
.

For any irrational numbers
\alpha,\beta
satisfying
\alpha\pm\beta\notin\mathbb{Z}
the sequence
d(n),n\in\mathbb{Z_{+}}
is unbounded.

To formulate further results, we need constants

\tau=\frac{\sqrt{5}+1}{2},

(4)

\phi=\frac{\sqrt{5}-1}{2},

(5)

K=\sqrt{\tau}-1=0.2720^{+},

(6)

C=K(\sqrt{\tau}+\tau^{-3/2})=\sqrt{5}(1-\sqrt{\phi})=0.47818^{+}.

(7)

In 2019 Moshchevitin
[
5
]
proved the following

Theorem B
.

Let
\alpha
and
\beta
be the two irrational numbers. If
\alpha\pm\beta\notin\mathbb{Z}
, then for every
T\geq 1
there exists
t\geq T
such that

|\psi_{\alpha}(t)-\psi_{\beta}(t)|\geqslant K\cdot\min(\psi_{\alpha}(t),\psi_{\beta}(t)).

(8)

It was also proved that constant
K
in (
8
) is optimal.

Using Theorem
B
and (
2
) Moshchevitin deduced stronger version of Theorem
A
.

Corollary A
.

Let
\alpha
and
\beta
be the two irrational numbers. If
\alpha\pm\beta\notin\mathbb{Z}
, then for every
T\geq 1
there exists
t\geq T
such

\Bigl|\frac{1}{\psi_{\alpha}(t)}-\frac{1}{\psi_{\beta}(t)}\Bigl|\geqslant Kt,\text{\,\,\, with $K$ from \eqref{constK}}.

(9)

In this paper we give an improvement of this result with the best possible constant.

Theorem 1
.

1) Let
\alpha
and
\beta
be the two irrational numbers. If
\alpha\pm\beta\notin\mathbb{Z}
, then for every
T\geq 1
there exists
t\geq T
such

\Bigl|\frac{1}{\psi_{\alpha}(t)}-\frac{1}{\psi_{\beta}(t)}\Bigl|\geqslant Ct,\text{\,\,\, where $C$ is defined in \eqref{constC}}.

(10)

2) Constant
C
in (
10
) is optimal, that is for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exist
\alpha
and
\beta
with
\alpha\pm\beta\notin\mathbb{Z}
such that

\Bigl|\frac{1}{\psi_{\alpha}(t)}-\frac{1}{\psi_{\beta}(t)}\Bigl|\leqslant(C+\varepsilon)t

for all
t
large enough.

Proof.

It is a well-known fact, that for any

one has

\frac{\xi_{n-1}}{\xi_{n}}=\alpha_{n+1}.

As

\frac{\xi_{n-1}}{\eta_{s}}\cdot\frac{\eta_{s}}{\xi_{n}}=\frac{\xi_{n-1}}{\xi_{n}}=\alpha_{n+1},

In the first case, one has

\frac{1}{\eta_{s}}-\frac{1}{\xi_{n-1}}\geq\frac{1}{\eta_{s}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{n+1}}\eta_{s}}=\frac{1}{\eta_{s}}\left(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{n+1}}}\right)=t_{s}\left(\beta_{s+1}+\frac{t_{s-1}}{t_{s}}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{n+1}}}\right).

15

Similarly, in the case

∎

Introduction

Our concern in this paper is to consider fast decaying solutions of weighted Lane-Emden equation in punctured domain

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle-\Delta u=Vu^{p}&{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\},\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle\quad\ \ u>0&{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\},\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
p>1
,
N\geq 3
and the potential
V
is a locally Hölder continuous function in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
.

When
V=-1
, the nonlinear term is known as an absorption and problem (
1.1
)
admits a positive solution
c_{p}|x|^{-\frac{2}{p-1}}
for any
p\in(0,\frac{N}{N-2})
and
Brezis-Veron in
[
6
]
showed that it has no positive solution when
p\geq\frac{N}{N-2}
.
This type of nonexistence in the super critical case could also done in
[
25
]
.
While the isolated singularities of this elliptic problems in punctured domain subject to Dirichlet boundary condition are well studied in
[
24
,
26
,
27
,
28
,
29
]
and a survey in
[
30
]
.

When
V=1
, equation (
1.1
) is well known as Lane-Emden-Fowler equation

-\Delta u=u^{p}\quad{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\},

(1.2)

which has been extensively studied in the last decades. When
p\leq\frac{N}{N-2}
, problem (
1.2
) has no positive solution, see the reference
[
5
]
and for
p>\frac{N}{N-2}
, problem (
1.2
)
always has a singular solution
w_{p}(x)=c_{p}|x|^{-\frac{2}{p-1}}
with

c_{p}=\left(\frac{2}{p-1}(N-2-\frac{2}{p-1})\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

(1.3)

When
p\in(\frac{N}{N-2},\frac{N+2}{N-2})
,
positive isolated singular solutions of the problem (
1.2
) have the following structure:

(i)
a sequence
k
-fast decaying solutions
w_{k}
with
k>0
such that

\lim_{|x|\to 0^{+}}w_{k}(x)|x|^{\frac{2}{p-1}}=c_{p}\quad{and}~\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}w_{k}(x)|x|^{N-2}=k.

Here a solution is called
k
-fast decaying if
\displaystyle\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}w_{k}(x)|x|^{N-2}=k
.

(ii)
a slow decaying solution
w_{p}(x)=c_{p}|x|^{-\frac{2}{p-1}}
and
\displaystyle w_{p}=\lim_{k\to+\infty}w_{k}
.

Furthermore, the fast decaying solution
w_{k}
could be written by

w_{k}(x)=|x|^{-\frac{2}{p-1}}\bar{w}_{p}(-\ln|x|+b_{p}^{-1}(\ln k-\ln c)),

where
b_{p}=N-2-\frac{2}{p-1}>0
,

is independent of
k
and
\bar{w}_{p}(\cdot)
is a positive and bounded function independently of
k
.
Assume that
t=-\ln|x|+b_{p}^{-1}(\ln k-\ln c)
, then the function
\bar{w}_{p}
satisfies

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\bar{w}_{p}^{\prime\prime}-\left(N-2-\frac{4}{p-1}\right)\bar{w}_{p}^{\prime}-c_{p}^{p-1}\bar{w}_{p}+\bar{w}_{p}^{p}=0\quad{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R},\\[7.11317pt]
\displaystyle\bar{w}_{p}(-\infty)=0\quad{\rm and}\quad\bar{w}_{p}(+\infty)=c_{p}.\end{array}\right.

(1.4)

To be convenient for the analyze, let us denote

p_{c}=1+\frac{4}{N-4+2\sqrt{N-1}}\in\left(\frac{N}{N-2},\,\frac{N+2}{N-2}\right),

(1.5)

which is the Joseph-Ludgren exponent, note that
\bar{w}_{p}
is increasing for
p\in(\frac{N}{N-2},\ ,p_{c}]
and for

p\in(p_{c},\frac{N+2}{N-2})
,
\bar{w}_{p}
is oscillating as
t\to+\infty
, more information could be seen in Section 2.
For the supercritical case that
p\geq\frac{N+2}{N-2}
, problem (
1.2
) has been studied
[
14

,
21
]

[
13

1.2
) with

in an exterior domain.

(1.6)

Our interest of this paper is to classify the fast decaying and slow decaying solutions of problem (
1.1
)
for the supercritical case and involving general potential

Here, we say that

\nu
-fast decaying solution

pointwisely verifies (
1.1
) in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
and has the asymptotic behavior at infinity

\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}u(x)|x|^{N-2}=\nu\quad{\rm for}\ \ \nu>0.

Assume that the potential function
V
is Hölder continuous and satisfies the following conditions:

(\mathcal{V}_{0})

(i)
near the origin,

|V(x)-1|\leq c_{0}|x|^{\tau_{0}}\quad{\rm for}\ \,x\in B_{1}(0),

(1.7)

and

.

Theorem 1.1

Assume that
p_{c}
is given by (

(\mathcal{V}_{0})

\tau_{0}>\tau^{*}_{p}\quad{\rm and}\quad\beta<(N-2)p-N,

(1.9)

\tau^{*}_{p}=\Big(\frac{2}{p-1}-\frac{N-2}{2}\Big)-\sqrt{\Big(\frac{2}{p-1}-\frac{N-2}{2}\Big)^{2}-2\Big(N-2-\frac{2}{p-1}\Big)}.

(1.10)

Then there exists
\nu_{0}>0
such that for any

1.1

\nu
-fast decaying solution

,

Furthermore, the mapping

is increasing, continuous and satisfies that

\lim_{\nu\to 0}\|u_{\nu}\|_{L^{\infty}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\})}=0.

(1.12)

We remark that

is well-defined

2
Preliminary

2.1
ODE analysis of Lane-Emden equation

In this subsection, we recall some results of Lane-Emden equation by ODE analysis. Denote
a new independent variable
t=-\ln|x|
and set
u(x)=|x|^{-\frac{2}{p-1}}\bar{w}_{p}(-\ln|x|)
, then the function
\bar{w}_{p}(t)
verifies

\bar{w}_{p}^{\prime\prime}+a\bar{w}_{p}^{\prime}-c_{p}^{p-1}\bar{w}_{p}+\bar{w}_{p}^{p}=0\quad{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R},

(2.1)

where
a=\frac{4}{p-1}-N+2.

Let
X(t)=\bar{w}(t)
and
Y(t)=\bar{w}^{\prime}(t)
, (
2.1
) can be rewritten as dynamic system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle X^{\prime}=Y&{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R},\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle Y^{\prime}=-aY+c_{p}^{p-1}X-X^{p}&{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R}.\end{array}\right.

(2.2)

We see that system (
2.2
) has two equilibrium points
(0,0)
and
(c_{p},0)
. Our aim
is to find a trajectory
(X,Y)
that starts from point
(0,0)
as
t\to-\infty
and ends at
point
(c_{p},0)
as
t\to+\infty
.

In fact, the trajectory
(X,Y)
is contained within the homocyclic orbit of the Hamiltonian system

v^{\prime\prime}-c_{p}^{p-1}v+v^{p}=0\quad{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R},

then we conclude that
\sup\bar{w}_{p}\leq\sup v
.
The conservation of Hamiltonian energy could be given as following

E(t):=\frac{1}{2}v^{\prime}(t)^{2}-\frac{c_{p}^{p-1}}{2}v(t)^{2}+\frac{1}{p+1}v(t)^{p+1}=0.

Hence
v
attains its supremum when
v^{\prime}=0
, we get the upper bound
v^{p-1}<\frac{p+1}{2}c_{p}^{p-1}.

On the other hand, the eigenvalues of linearizing system at
(0,0)
to (
2.2
) are

\lambda_{1}:=N-2-\frac{2}{p-1}>0>-\frac{2}{p-1}=:\lambda_{2},

for
t\to-\infty
, then we have that curve
(X,Y)
goes out from point

(0,0)
along the direction
Y=\lambda_{1}X
and then

\bar{w}_{p}(t)\sim ce^{\lambda_{1}t}\quad{\rm as}\quad t\to-\infty.

Therefore, we have that

\|\bar{w}_{p}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}<(\frac{p+1}{2})^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\,c_{p}.

(2.3)

Observe that the eigenvalues at
(c_{p},0)
are given by zero points of

H(\mu):=\mu^{2}+a\mu+(p-1)\,c_{p}^{p-1}.

(2.4)

When
p\in(\frac{N}{N-2},p_{c})
, where
p_{c}
is given by (
1.5
), (
2.4
) has two negative zero points of
H
:

\mu_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\left(-a+\sqrt{a^{2}-4(p-1)\,c_{p}^{p-1}}\,\right),\quad\mu_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(-a-\sqrt{a^{2}-4(p-1)\,c_{p}^{p-1}}\,\right),

then we have that

c_{p}-\bar{w}_{p}(t)\sim c_{1}e^{\mu_{1}t}+c_{2}e^{\mu_{2}t}\quad{\rm as}\quad t\to+\infty.

(2.5)

For
p=p_{c}
,
H=0
has two same roots
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}=-\frac{a}{2}
, then

c_{p}-\bar{w}_{p}(t)\sim(c_{1}+c_{2}t)e^{\mu_{1}t}
as
t\to+\infty
.

Note that for
p\in(\frac{N}{N-2},\,p_{c}]
,

\bar{w}_{p}
is increasing and

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\bar{w}_{p}^{p-1}=\frac{2}{p-1}(N-2-\frac{2}{p-1})

(2.6)

and then there exists
c>0
such that

0<\bar{w}_{p}^{\prime}\leq c\bar{w}_{p}\quad{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R}.

(2.7)

Hence, the fast decaying solutions
\{w_{k}\}_{k}
of (
1.2
) have following properties:

0<w_{k_{1}}<w_{k_{2}}<w_{p}\quad{\rm if}\quad 0<k_{1}<k_{2}<+\infty.

When
p\in(p_{c},\frac{N+2}{N-2})
,
H=0
has two complex roots as

\mu_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\left(-a+{\rm i}\sqrt{-a^{2}+4(p-1)c_{p}^{p-1}}\right)\quad{\rm and}\quad\mu_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(-a-{\rm i}\sqrt{-a^{2}+4(p-1)c_{p}^{p-1}}\right),

where i is the unit imaginary number. Then
\bar{w}
oscillates around
c_{p}
and converges to
c_{p}
with the rate

\limsup_{t\to+\infty}|\bar{w}_{p}(t)-c_{p}|e^{\frac{a}{2}t}=c_{0}.

A crucial tool for the analysis is following.

Proposition 2.1

(i)
For
p\in(\frac{N}{N-2},\,p_{c}]
, we have that

p\cdot\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\bar{w}_{p}^{p-1}\leq\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4},

(2.8)

where
{}^{\prime}=^{\prime}
holds only for
p=p_{c}
.

(ii)
For
p\in(p_{c},\frac{N+2}{N-2})
, we have that

p\cdot\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\bar{w}_{p}^{p-1}>\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}.

(2.9)

Proof.
One hand, when
p\in(\frac{N}{N-2},\frac{N+2}{N-2})
, we have that

\frac{2}{p-1}\in(\frac{N-2}{2},N-2)
,
then

\frac{2p}{p-1}(N-2-\frac{2}{p-1})\leq(\frac{N-2}{2})^{2}\quad{\rm holds~if~and~only~if}\quad\frac{N}{N-2}<p\leq p_{c}.

On the other hand for
p\in(p_{c},\frac{N+2}{N-2})
, we have that

\ \sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\bar{w}_{p}^{p-1}>\frac{2}{p-1}(N-2-\frac{2}{p-1}),

combining (
2.6
), the assertion holds and the proof is complete.
\Box

Lemma 2.1

Let
p\in(\frac{N}{N-2},p_{c}]
and
b_{p}=N-2-\frac{2}{p-1}
, then

w_{k}(x)=|x|^{-\frac{2}{p-1}}\bar{w}_{p}(-\ln|x|+b_{p}^{-1}(\ln k-\ln d_{0})),

(2.10)

and for any
r\in(0,1]
, there exists

such that for

,

w_{k}(x)\leq c_{1}kr^{-\frac{2}{p-1}}(1+|x|)^{2-N}\chi_{{}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r}(0)}}(x)+c_{p}|x|^{-\frac{2}{p-1}}\chi_{{}_{B_{r}(0)}}(x)\quad{\rm for}\ \,\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\},

(2.11)

where
c_{1}>0
is independent of
k,\,r
.

Proof.
By above phase plane analysis, we have (
2.10
)
just taking
t=-\ln|x|+b_{p}^{-1}(\ln k-\ln d_{0})
.
From (
2.3
), there exists
c>0
such that

\bar{w}_{p}(t)\leq c_{1}e^{b_{p}t}
for any
t\leq 0
,
then if

, i.e.

|x|\geq(k/d_{0})^{b_{p}^{-1}}
, we have that

w_{k}(x)\leq c_{1}kr^{-\frac{2}{p-1}}(1+|x|)^{2-N}\quad\ \forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r}(0).

For
|x|<(k/d_{0})^{b_{p}^{-1}}
, we have
\bar{w}_{p}\leq c_{p}

and (
2.11
) follows.
\Box

Basic estimate

In this subsection, some estimates are introduced, which play important roles in

Lemma 2.2

Let

U_{1}(x)=|x|^{-2-\theta}\chi_{B_{r}(0)}(x),\quad U_{2}(x)=(1+|x|)^{-\tau}\quad{\rm and}\quad U_{3}(x)=|x|^{-\theta-2}(1+|x|)^{-\tau+\theta+2},

Proof.
By direct computation, we have that

(\Gamma\ast U_{1})(x)=c_{N}\int_{B_{r}(0)}\frac{|y|^{-2-\theta}}{|x-y|^{N-2}}dy.

From the fact that

For

\displaystyle(\Gamma\ast U_{1})(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|y|^{-\theta-2}}{|x-y|^{N-2}}dz=\frac{1}{\theta(N-2-\theta)}|x|^{-\theta}.

When

(\Gamma\ast U_{1})(x)\leq c_{N}(|x|-r)^{2-N}\int_{B_{r}(0)}|y|^{-\theta-2}dy\leq c_{N}2^{N-2}|x|^{2-N}\int_{B_{r}(0)}|y|^{-\theta-2}dy,

(2.15)

where

Thus, (

Next we show (

(\Gamma\ast U_{2})(x)=c_{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{(1+|y|)^{-\tau}}{|x-y|^{N-2}}dy,

then

is bounded locally in

and so we only need to show the case

In fact, for

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{(1+|y|)^{-\tau}}{|x-y|^{N-2}}dy

\displaystyle\int_{B_{|x|/2}(0)}\frac{(1+|y|)^{-\tau}}{|x-y|^{N-2}}dy+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{|x|/2}(0)}\frac{|y|^{-\tau}}{|x-y|^{N-2}}dy

\displaystyle\left(\frac{|x|}{2}\right)^{2-N}\int_{B_{|x|/2}(0)}(1+|y|)^{-\tau}dy+|x|^{2-\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1/2}(0)}\frac{|z|^{-\tau}}{|e_{x}-z|^{N-2}}dz

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c|x|^{2-N},

where the last inequality holds thanks to the facts that

Then (

Finally, we observe that

then (

\Box

Lemma 2.3

Suppose that

is a nonnegative function satisfying

for

,
with
\tau>N
and some

. Then

Proof.
By the decay condition of

\epsilon>0
, there exists

such that for

For

(1-\epsilon)|x|^{2-N}\leq|x-y|^{2-N}\leq(1+\epsilon)|x|^{2-N}
for

and

\displaystyle(\Gamma\ast f)(x)=c_{N}\int_{B_{R}(0)}\frac{f(y)}{|x-y|^{N-2}}dy+c_{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}(0)}\frac{f(y)}{|x-y|^{N-2}}dy,

\displaystyle(1-\epsilon)\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq|x|^{N-2}\int_{B_{R}(0)}\frac{f(y)}{|x-y|^{N-2}}dy\leq(1+\epsilon)\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}

\displaystyle c\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}(0)}\frac{|y|^{-\tau}}{|x-y|^{N-2}}dy

\displaystyle c\int_{R\leq|y|<2|x|}\frac{|y|^{-\tau}}{|x-y|^{N-2}}dy+c\int_{|y|\geq 2|x|}\frac{|y|^{-\tau}}{|x-y|^{N-2}}dy

\displaystyle cR^{2-N}\int_{R}^{2|x|}r^{N-1-\tau}dr+c\int^{+\infty}_{2|x|}r^{1-\tau}dr

\displaystyle\frac{c}{N-\tau}R^{2-N}((2|x|)^{N-\tau}-R^{N-\tau})-\frac{c}{2-\tau}(2|x|)^{2-\tau}.

Passing to the limit as

3

3.1
Existence by Fixed point theory

In this subsection, we give the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
We are looking for a
k
-fast decaying solution
u
of (
1.1
), with the division

u=w_{k}+v,

where
w_{k}
is the
k

1.2
) and
v
verifies that

-\Delta v=V(w_{k}+v)_{+}^{p}-w_{k}^{p}\quad{\rm in}\ \,\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}.

(3.1)

We will employ the Schauder fixed point theorem to obtain a solution of (
3.1
). To this end, let us clarify
the key value
\tau_{p}^{*}
. In fact, the essential point in our following construction of fast decaying solutions
is to find a
\theta_{0}\in[\frac{N-2}{2},N-2)
such that

\theta_{0}(N-2-\theta_{0})>pc_{p}^{p-1}=\left(2+\frac{2}{p-1}\right)\left(N-2-\frac{2}{p-1}\right),

(3.2)

which is possible since
\left(2+\frac{2}{p-1}\right)\left(N-2-\frac{2}{p-1}\right)<\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}

,
and
\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}

\theta_{0}(N-2-\theta_{0})

\theta_{0}=\frac{N-2}{2}
.
Now the point is to find the smallest
\tau>0
such that

\Big(\frac{2}{p-1}-\tau\Big)\Big(N-2+\tau-\frac{2}{p-1}\Big)=\left(2+\frac{2}{p-1}\right)\left(N-2-\frac{2}{p-1}\right).

(3.3)

Direct computation shows that for
p\in(\frac{N}{N-2},p_{c})
,
\tau_{p}^{*}>0
defined in (
1.10
) is the smallest zero of (
3.2

\tau\in\left(\tau_{p}^{*},\tau_{p}^{\#}\right)
, we have that

\Big(\frac{2}{p-1}-\tau\Big)\Big(N-2+\tau-\frac{2}{p-1}\Big)>\left(2+\frac{2}{p-1}\right)\left(N-2-\frac{2}{p-1}\right).

Now let us fix

\tau_{1}=\tau_{p}^{*}+\frac{1}{2}\min\Big\{\tau_{0}-\tau_{p}^{*},\,\frac{2}{p-1}-\frac{N-2}{2}\Big\}\ \,{\rm and}\ \,\theta_{0}=\frac{2}{p-1}-\tau_{1},

then
\tau_{1}\in\left(\tau_{p}^{*},\tau_{p}^{\#}\right)\cap(0,\tau_{0})
and
\theta_{0}\in\left[\frac{N-2}{2},\frac{2}{p-1}\right)
verifying

(3.5)

Finally, we denote
\tau_{2}=\tau_{0}-\tau_{1}>0.

Lemma 4.2

Let the hypotheses of Theorem
1.3
hold, then there exists
c>0
independent of
\nu
such that

\|u_{\nu}\|_{L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq c\quad{\rm and}\quad\|u_{\nu}\|_{L^{p}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N},Vdx)}\leq c.

Proof.
Let

U_{0}(x)=\frac{c_{N}}{(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{N-2}{2}}},

then

-\Delta U_{0}=U_{0}^{2^{*}-1}\quad{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},

where
2^{*}=\frac{2N}{N-2}
,
c_{N}=(N(N-2))^{\frac{N-2}{4}}
.

For
\epsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
, denote

\eta_{\epsilon}(x)=\eta_{0}(\epsilon|x|),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

(4.2)

where
\eta_{0}:[0,+\infty)\to[0,1]
is a smooth increasing function such that

\eta_{0}(t)=0,\quad\forall\,t\geq 2\quad{\rm and}\quad\eta_{0}(t)=1,\quad\forall\,t\in[0,1].

Take
U_{0}\eta_{\epsilon}^{2}
as a test function of (
4.1
), then by Hölder inequality, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Vu_{\nu}^{p}U_{0}\eta_{\epsilon}^{2}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{\nu}(-\Delta)(U_{0}\eta_{\epsilon}^{2})dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{\nu}(U_{0}^{2^{*}-1}\eta_{\epsilon}^{2}+4\eta_{\epsilon}\nabla U_{0}\cdot\nabla\eta_{\epsilon}+U_{0}(-\Delta)(\eta_{\epsilon}^{2}))dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Vu_{\nu}^{p}U_{0}\eta_{\epsilon}^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{B_{\frac{2}{\epsilon}}}V^{1-p}U_{0}^{1-p+(2^{*}-1)\frac{p}{p-1}}dx\right.

\displaystyle+\epsilon\|\eta_{0}\|_{C^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\int_{B_{\frac{2}{\epsilon}}(0)\setminus B_{\frac{1}{\epsilon}}(0)}V^{1-p}U_{0}^{1-p+(2^{*}-1)\frac{p}{p-1}}|\nabla U_{0}|^{p-1}dx

\displaystyle\left.+\epsilon^{2}\|\eta_{0}\|_{C^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\int_{B_{\frac{2}{\epsilon}}(0)\setminus B_{\frac{1}{\epsilon}}(0)}V^{1-p}U_{0}^{p-1}dx\right)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{0}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Vu_{\nu}^{p}U_{0}\eta_{\epsilon}^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where

is dependent on
\epsilon,V
but it is independent of
\nu
.

So we have that

\displaystyle c_{N}(1+\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}})^{-\frac{N-2}{2}}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\epsilon}}(0)}Vu_{\nu}^{p}dx\leq\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\epsilon}}(0)}Vu_{\nu}^{p}U_{0}dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Vu_{\nu}^{p}U_{0}\eta_{\epsilon}^{2}dx\leq c_{0}^{\frac{p}{p-1}},

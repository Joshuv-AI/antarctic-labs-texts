A Worldsheet Perspective on Heterotic T–Duality Orbifolds
Stefan Groot Nibbelink

Abstract




Abstract

Asymmetric heterotic orbifolds are discussed from the worldsheet perspective.
Starting from Buscher’s gauging of a theory of
D
compact bosons the duality covariant description of Tseytlin is obtained after a non–Lorentz invariant gauge fixing.
A left–over of the gauge symmetry can be used to removed the doubled constant zero modes so that
D
physical target space coordinate remain.
This can be thought of as the worldsheet realization of the strong constraint of double field theory.
The extension of this description to the heterotic theory is straightforward as all results are written in terms of the invariant and the generalized metrics.
An explicit method is outline how to obtain a generalized metric which is invariant under T–duality orbifold actions.
It is explicitly shown how shift orbifolds lead to redefinitions of the Narain moduli.
Finally, a number of higher dimensional T–folds are constructed including a novel asymmetric
\mathbbm{Z}_{6}
orbifold.

Introduction and conclusions

Motivation

The heterotic string
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
provides an unified framework to describe all interactions among elementary particles and gravity. Compactifications of this theory to the four dimensions we experience today may be performed on six–dimensional symmetric toroidal orbifolds
[
4
,
5
]
without giving up full string computability. Unfortunately, such orbifolds and their related Calabi–Yau compactifications leave many (geometric) moduli unfixed. This impairs predictability of the resulting models as many physical parameters will be ultimately related to undetermined values of these moduli.

Untwisted moduli of such compactifications may be removed by considering asymmetric orbifold actions treating left– and right–moving string coordinate fields differently by modding out an intrinsically stringy duality symmetry
[
6
]
. In the most elementary realization it occurs in a compactification on a circle where the theory at a certain radius
R
is identified with another compactification on a circle with radius
1/R
(in string units). In a single construction this is only possible if the radius itself is fixed at the string scale
R=1
. More in general
T
–duality orbifolds result in quotient spaces often referred to as asymmetric orbifolds
[
7
]
. As such they can be considered as fully computable non–geometric
string backgrounds
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
or so–called
T
–folds
[
11
,
12
]
. Asymmetric orbifolds have been considered by various research groups
[

,

; more recent works are e.g.

.
Asymmetric twists in free fermionic models to stabilize untwisted moduli were exploited in
[
27
]
. The present work focusses on the heterotic setting only, possible dualities to type–II non–geometric compactifications were considered in
[
28
]
.

In a recent paper
[
29
]
a comprehensive framework to study asymmetric toroidal compactifications of the heterotic string was provided. That work mainly focussed on their construction as generalizations of Narain toroidal compactification modded out by T–duality group elements. The current paper is complementary to that one in the sense, that it aims to give an explicit duality covariant bosonic worldsheet description of Narain orbifolds. Asymmetric compactifications of the heterotic string are normally discussed on the level of the torus partition function only, without specifying the underlying bosonic worldsheet theory. An explicit worldsheet description is provided by the free–fermionic formulation of the heterotic string
[
30
,
31
,
32
]
. This description predominantly accommodates
\mathbbm{Z}_{2}
duality symmetries.
In order to obtain an explicit bosonic worldsheet description a Buscher’s gauging is used and subsequently gauged fixed in such a way that a duality covariant description by Tseytlin is obtained at the expense of manifest worldsheet Lorentz invariance. The basic structure is first exposed for a worldsheet theory of
D
bosonic fields and after that extended to the heterotic theory with
D
right–moving and
D+16
left–moving degrees of freedom.

In addition, this paper gives an extensive description of shift heterotic orbifolds and show that they all can be viewed as Narain torus compactifications. A computational method is provided how the new moduli can be determined from the original ones and the applied shift actions. It is demonstrated in various concrete examples that the orbifold shifts may be geometric or non–geometric and may be accompanied by actions on the gauge part of the Narain lattice.

Finally, since the number of explicitly known truly stringy higher dimensional T–folds is very limited, for examples see e.g.
[

34

, this paper provides a number of non–trivial higher dimensional T–fold examples illustrating the developed methodology.

Outline of the paper’s main results

The main results presented in this paper have been structured as follows:

Section
2
starts from the textbook worldsheet action of
D
compact bosons. After a Buscher’s gauging is applied, a gauge fixing is chosen that breaks manifest Lorentz invariance in favor of achieving a duality covariant doubled worldsheet theory first considered by Tseytlin. A residual gauge symmetry is uncovered that removes the doubling of the constant zero modes. The remaining constant zero modes then have the interpretation of the physical target space coordinates. (This may be thought of as a worldsheet realization of the strong constraint of double field theory
[
35
,
36
,
37
]
.) The worldsheet supersymmetry transformations for this doubled worldsheet theory are derived. In addition, the one–loop partition function is obtained for the doubled theory. Here the boundary terms, which provided the equivalence between the original theory of
D
compact bosons and Tseytlin’s formulation, lead to an additional phase factor that cancels out any dependence on the “doubled winding numbers”. This ensures that the partition function of the doubled theory is identical to that of the original theory of
D
compact bosons.

Section
3
discusses the generalization of these results to the heterotic theory. This generalization is straightforward since all results in Section
2
have been written in terms of the generalized metric and the duality invariant metric which have natural extensions in the heterotic context. On the level of the partition function this reproduces the known results for Narain compactifications of the heterotic string.

Section
4
develops the description of orbifold twisting of the Narain theory from the worldsheet point of view. This section recalls the Narain space group description introduced in
[
29
]
and provides a novel way to explicitly construct invariant
\mathbbm{Z}_{2}
–gradings and associated generalized metrics of Narain orbifolds. If the action is asymmetric, i.e. not isomorphic between the right– and left–movers, so called T–folds are obtained. From the boundary conditions of the worldsheet coordinate fields the full orbifold partition function can be computed.
One complication is that it is not a priori clear that an invariant generalized metric exists for a given finite orbifold action on the Narain lattice. If not, no asymmetric orbifold can be associated to this action. Here an explicit procedure is outlined how such an invariant generalized metric can be obtained.

Section
5
considers special orbifold actions that have trivial twist parts. It is shown that orbifold shift actions do not lead to new geometries but rather modify the moduli of the Narain compactification. In particular, an explicit description is provided how the new moduli can be computed from the old ones. This is illustrated for a number of simple yet interesting cases such as geometrical and non–geometrical shifts combined with non–trivial Wilson lines.

Finally, Section
6
provides a number of examples of higher dimensional T–folds. The first two examples are Narain orbifolds obtained by applying the basic T–duality twist to all
D
compact dimensions. One acts only on the right–movers hence it fixes all moduli but is only supersymmetric in
D=4
or
8
dimensions, while the other only acts on
D
left–movers preserving all supersymmetries in any dimension and the Wilson lines are left free. In a further example a
\mathbbm{Z}_{6}
action is realized in an asymmetric way. This provides one of the first explicitly known examples of higher order asymmetric orbifolds in four dimensions.

This paper is concluded with three Appendices that provide some technical background for the results obtained in this work. Appendix
A
introduces the notation used to evaluate partition functions. Appendix
B
describes the underlying modular transformations. Finally, Appendix
C
gives further details of the description of Narain moduli and their transformations as uncovered in
[
29
]
.

Duality Covariant Worldsheet

2.1
Doubled Target Space Torus

The Minkowskian worldsheet is parameterized by the worldsheet time
\sigma_{0}
and space
\sigma_{1}
coordinates. The starting point of the description of
D
bosons
X^{T}=\big(X^{1},\ldots,X^{D})
, the internal coordinates fields, on the worldsheet is the action

\displaystyle S=\int\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\sigma}{2\pi}\Big[\dfrac{1}{2}\,\partial_{0}X^{T}G\,\partial_{0}X-\dfrac{1}{2}\,\partial_{1}X^{T}G\,\partial_{1}X+\partial_{1}X^{T}B\,\partial_{0}X\Big]~,

(2.1)

where
G
is a
D
–dimensional metric of and
B
an anti–symmetric tensor on a target space torus
T^{D}
. Throughout this work these background quantities are taken to be constant. The target space torus periodicities are encoded in integral lattice identifications

\displaystyle X\sim X+2\pi\,m~,\quad m\in\mathbbm{Z}^{D}~;

(2.2)

the geometrical aspects of the torus
T^{D}
have already been taken into account by the metric
G
in the worldsheet action (
2.1
).

A duality covariant description of this theory can be obtained following Buscher’s gauging procedure
[
38
]
and subsequently choosing an appropriate gauge
[
39
]
: The coordinate fields
X
are promoted to possess the following gauge transformations

\displaystyle X\mapsto X-\lambda~,

(2.3)

where
\lambda^{T}=\big(\lambda^{1},\ldots,\lambda^{D})
are general functions on the worldsheet. To ensure invariance of the action (
2.1
) the derivatives are promoted to gauge covariant ones

\displaystyle{\cal D}_{\mu}X=\partial_{\mu}X+{\cal A}_{\mu}~,

(2.4)

(where
\mu=0,1
) which are gauge invariant, provided that the gauge fields
{\cal A}_{\mu}
themselves transform as

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{\mu}\mapsto{\cal A}_{\mu}+\partial_{\mu}\lambda~.

(2.5)

This gauging would remove all physical bosons from the worldsheet. To avoid this, the gauged action is complemented by a Lagrange multiplier field
{\widetilde{X}}
, which enforces that the gauge field is pure gauge:

\displaystyle S=\int\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\sigma}{2\pi}\Big[\dfrac{1}{2}\,{\cal D}_{0}X^{T}\,G{\cal D}_{0}X-\dfrac{1}{2}\,{\cal D}_{1}X^{T}G\,{\cal D}_{1}X+{\cal D}_{1}X^{T}B\,{\cal D}_{0}X+{\widetilde{X}}^{T}{\cal F}_{10}\Big]~,

(2.6)

where
{\cal F}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}{\cal A}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}{\cal A}_{\mu}
is the gauge field strength. The Lagrange multiplier fields
{\widetilde{X}}
satisfy similar periodicities as the coordinates
X
themselves:

\displaystyle{\widetilde{X}}\sim{\widetilde{X}}+2\pi\,{\tilde{m}}~,\quad{\tilde{m}}\in\mathbbm{Z}^{D}~,

(2.7)

so that the charges

\displaystyle q=\int\dfrac{{\cal F}_{2}}{2\pi}\in\mathbbm{Z}^{D}

(2.8)

are integral in the Euclidean theory, hence the periodicities (
2.7
) of
{\widetilde{X}}
result in a trivial phase
\exp\{-2\pi i\,{\tilde{m}}^{T}q\}=1
in the path integral. A gauge, that makes the duality manifest, is
[
39
]
:

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{1}(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{0})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{=}}0~.

(2.9)

Clearly, this gauge breaks manifest Lorentz invariance on the worldsheet. In this gauge
{\cal D}_{1}X=\partial_{1}X
,
{\cal D}_{0}X=\partial_{0}X+{\cal A}_{0}
and
{\cal F}_{10}=\partial_{1}{\cal A}_{0}
. Inserting this in the action (

) and performing a partial integration to remove the derivative

\displaystyle\partial_{0}X+{\cal A}_{0}=G^{-1}\big(\partial_{1}{\widetilde{X}}+B\,\partial_{1}X\big)~.

(2.10)

Eliminating all
{\cal A}_{0}
dependence using this expression, shows that the action can now be cast in the Tseytlin’s form

41
]
by two further partial integrations:

\displaystyle S=\int\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\sigma}{2\pi}\Big[-\dfrac{1}{2}\,\partial_{1}Y^{T}\,\widehat{\cal H}\,\partial_{1}Y+\dfrac{1}{2}\,\partial_{1}Y^{T}\,\widehat{\eta}\,\partial_{0}Y\Big]~,

(2.11)

where
Y^{T}=\big(X^{T}~{\widetilde{X}}^{T}\big)
combines the coordinates
X

{\widetilde{X}}

2D

\widehat{\cal H}
and the

are introduced

\displaystyle\widehat{\cal H}=\begin{pmatrix}G-BG^{-1}B&-BG^{-1}\\[4.30554pt]
G^{-1}B&G^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad\widehat{\eta}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathbbm{1}_{D}\\[4.30554pt]
\mathbbm{1}_{D}&0\end{pmatrix}~.

(2.12)

Given the periodicities, (
2.2
) and (
2.7

\displaystyle Y\sim Y+2\pi\,N~,\quad N=\begin{pmatrix}m\\
{\tilde{m}}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbbm{Z}^{2D}~.

(2.13)

Just as the periodicities of the dual coordinates

Consequently, the Tseytlin action (

\displaystyle Y\mapsto\widehat{M}^{-1}\,Y~,\qquad\widehat{\cal H}\mapsto\widehat{M}^{T}\,\widehat{\cal H}\,\widehat{M}~,

(2.14)

since by definition

and the lattice

In addition, the generalized metric and the

\displaystyle\widehat{\cal H}^{T}=\widehat{\cal H}~,\quad\widehat{\cal H}\,\widehat{\eta}^{-1}\,\widehat{\cal H}=\widehat{\eta}~.

\displaystyle\widehat{\cal Z}=\widehat{\eta}^{-1}\widehat{\cal H}=\widehat{\cal H}^{-1}\widehat{\eta}~,\quad\widehat{\cal Z}^{2}=\mathbbm{1}_{2D}~,\quad\widehat{\cal Z}^{T}\,\widehat{\eta}\,\widehat{\cal Z}\,=\widehat{\eta}~,\quad\text{tr}_{2D}\big[\widehat{\cal Z}\big]=0~.

(2.16)

\widehat{\cal Z}\in\text{O}(D,D;\mathbbm{R})

In the derivation of (

\displaystyle S_{\text{bnd}}=\int\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\sigma}{2\pi}\Big[\partial_{1}\big({\widetilde{X}}^{T}{\cal A}_{0}\big)+\dfrac{1}{2}\,\partial_{1}\big({\widetilde{X}}^{T}\partial_{0}X\big)-\dfrac{1}{2}\,\partial_{0}\big({\widetilde{X}}^{T}\partial_{1}X\big)\Big]~.

do not automatically vanish. In particular, because of (

\displaystyle\int\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\sigma}{2\pi}\,\partial_{1}\big({\widetilde{X}}^{T}{\cal A}_{0}\big)\sim\int\mathrm{d}\sigma_{0}\,{\tilde{m}}^{T}{\cal A}_{0}(0,\sigma_{0})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{=}}0~,

\sigma_{0}

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{0}(0,\sigma_{0})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{=}}0

(2.19)

\displaystyle S_{\text{bnd}}=\int\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\sigma}{2\pi}\Big[\dfrac{1}{2}\,\partial_{1}\big({\widetilde{X}}^{T}\partial_{0}X\big)-\dfrac{1}{2}\,\partial_{0}\big({\widetilde{X}}^{T}\partial_{1}X\big)\Big]~.

(2.20)

Since the gauge transformation (

(2.21)

2.2
Worldsheet Supersymmetry

The worldsheet action (
2.1
) can be extended to

\displaystyle S=\int\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\sigma}{2\pi}\Big[-\partial X^{T}K\,\bar{\partial}X-\psi^{T}\,\partial\psi\Big]~,\quad\text{where}\quad K=G+B~,

(2.22)

with
D
right–moving real fermions
\psi
. Here left– and right–moving coordinates
(\sigma,{\bar{\sigma}})
and their associated derivatives
(\partial,\bar{\partial})
,

\displaystyle\sigma=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\big(\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{0}\big)

\displaystyle\quad\text{and}\quad{\bar{\sigma}}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\big(\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{0}\big)~,

(2.23a)

\displaystyle\partial=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\big(\partial_{1}+\partial_{0}\big)

\displaystyle\quad\text{and}\quad\bar{\partial}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\big(\partial_{1}-\partial_{0}\big)~,

(2.23b)

were introduced, so that the two–dimensional measure can be written as

\mathrm{d}^{2}\sigma=\mathrm{d}\sigma_{1}\mathrm{d}\sigma_{0}=\mathrm{d}{\bar{\sigma}}\mathrm{d}\sigma\,.

This action is invariant under the supersymmetry transformations

\displaystyle\delta X=\epsilon\,e^{-1}\,\psi\quad\text{and}\quad\delta\psi=-\epsilon\,e\,\bar{\partial}X~,

(2.24)

where
e
is a vielbein associated the metric
G=e^{T}e

Applying the Buscher’s gauging to this action leads to

\displaystyle S=\int\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\sigma}{2\pi}\Big[-{\cal D}X^{T}K\,{\overline{\cal D}}X-\psi^{T}\,\partial\psi+{\widetilde{X}}^{T}{\cal F}\Big]~,

(2.25)

where
{\cal D}X=\partial X+{\cal A}
,
{\overline{\cal D}}X=\bar{\partial}X+{\overline{\cal A}}
and
{\cal F}={\cal F}_{10}=\bar{\partial}{\cal A}-\partial{\overline{\cal A}}
. This action is still supersymmetric, provided that the transformations (
2.24
) are extended to

\displaystyle\delta X=\epsilon\,e^{-1}\,\psi~,\quad\delta\psi=-\epsilon\,e\,{\overline{\cal D}}X\quad\text{and}\quad\delta{\widetilde{X}}=-\epsilon\,\big(e^{T}+Be^{-1}\big)\,\psi~.

(2.26)

Since the Tseytlin’s form of the action was obtained by using the gauge
{\cal A}_{1}=0
, the supersymmetry variation of
\psi

\displaystyle\delta\psi=-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,\epsilon\,\Big[\big(e-e^{-T}B\big)\,\partial_{1}X-e^{-T}\,\partial_{1}{\widetilde{X}}\Big]~.

(2.27)

Hence, using the doubled coordinate

\displaystyle\delta Y=\epsilon\begin{pmatrix}e^{-1}\\[4.30554pt]

2.3
One–Loop Partition Function

To determine the one–loop partition function by computing the path integral on the worldsheet torus,

\displaystyle Z_{\text{Doubled}}=\int{\cal D}Y{\cal D}h{\cal D}{\cal A}_{1}{\cal D}b{\cal D}c\,e^{-S_{\text{Eucl}}}~,

(2.38)

\displaystyle S_{\text{Eucl}}=\int\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^{2}z}{2\pi}\Big[\dfrac{1}{4}\,\partial_{1}Y^{T}\widehat{\cal H}\,\partial_{1}Y+\dfrac{i}{4}\,\partial_{1}Y^{T}\widehat{\eta}\,\partial_{2}Y+\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,h^{T}{\cal A}_{1}+\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,b^{T}\partial_{1}c\Big]~,

(2.39)

where
h
are
D

{\cal A}_{1}=0
and

form
D
associated ghost systems. The fields

{\cal A}_{1}
can be trivially integrated out without leaving a trace.

The one–loop periodic boundary conditions for the ghosts

\displaystyle Y(z+1)=Y(z)+2\pi\,N\quad\text{and}\quad Y(z-\tau)=Y(z)+2\pi\,N^{\prime}~,

for the doubled coordinate fields

2.4
Relation to Double Field Theory

Double field theory
[
35
,
36
,
37
]
is an attempt to obtain a duality covariant target space description of string theory. There the dimension

The doubled worldsheet description of strings on a

1.

The duality covariant action (
2.11

2.

The doubled coordinates
Y
are defined modulo constant shifts (
2.21
) in
D
of the
2D

3.

Infinite product factors
\prod\limits_{r,r^{\prime}}r

4.

All dependence on the integral vector

dropped out because of a phase that arose from (

Hence, in particular, the removal of

The other consequences mentioned here have no interpretation in the target space theory: A (none Lorentz invariant) worldsheet action on an one–loop torus with two different cycles is simply not part of the target space description.

Narain Orbifolds

4.1
General Construction of Narain Orbifolds

Point Group and Orbifold Action

Orbifolds are obtained by enforcing invariance of the theory under the action of a finite point group
\mathbf{P}
.
The total number of elements in the point group is denoted by
|\mathbf{P}|
. The action (
3.3
) is preserved by orbifold transformations of the form:

\displaystyle\theta[y]=\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}\,y+2\pi\,V_{\theta}~,

(4.1)

on
any
vector
y
(not just the Narain coordinate fields
Y
), provided that

\displaystyle\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}^{T}\,\widehat{\cal H}\,\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}=\widehat{\cal H}~,\quad\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}^{T}\,\widehat{\eta}\,\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}=\widehat{\eta}\quad\text{and}\quad\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}\,N\in\mathbbm{Z}^{2D+16}~,

(4.2)

for all
\theta\in\mathbf{P}
and
N\in\mathbbm{Z}^{2D+16}
. The final condition results from the requirement, that the doubled torus periodicities (
3.4
) need to be respected. The last two conditions imply that

\displaystyle\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}\in\text{O}_{\widehat{\eta}}(D,D+16;\mathbbm{Z})~.

(4.3)

The identity element
\theta=1
has
\widehat{\cal R}_{1}=\mathbbm{1}
and
V_{1}=0
. The composition of point group transformations is defined as

\theta^{\prime}\theta[Y]=\theta^{\prime}\big[\theta[Y]\big]
, hence

\displaystyle\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta^{\prime}\theta}=\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta^{\prime}}\,\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}\quad\text{and}\quad V_{\theta^{\prime}\theta}=\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta^{\prime}}\,V_{\theta}+V_{\theta^{\prime}}~.

(4.4)

If the order of the point group element
\theta\in\mathbf{P}
is denoted as
|\theta|
, it follows that
\theta^{|\theta|}[Y]\sim Y
. Writing this out explicitly, leads to

\displaystyle\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}{}^{|\theta|}=\mathbbm{1}\quad\text{and}\quad V_{\theta^{|\theta|}}=|\theta|\,{\cal P}{}_{\theta}^{\parallel}\,V_{\theta}\in\mathbbm{Z}^{2D+16}~,

(4.5)

where the projectors
\widehat{\cal P}{}_{\theta}^{\parallel}
, on the directions in which
\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}
act trivially
\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}\,\widehat{\cal P}{}_{\theta}^{\parallel}={\cal P}{}_{\theta}^{\parallel}\,\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}=\widehat{\cal P}{}_{\theta}^{\parallel}
, are defined as

\displaystyle\widehat{\cal P}{}_{\theta}^{\parallel}=\dfrac{1}{|\theta|}\sum_{j=0}^{|\theta|-1}\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}{}^{j}\quad\text{and}\quad\widehat{\cal P}{}_{\theta}^{\perp}=\mathbbm{1}-\widehat{\cal P}{}_{\theta}^{\parallel}~.

(4.6)

Since the action is invariant under a shift
Y_{0}
of the origin of the coordinate system defined by
Y
, the vectors
V_{\theta}
may be redefined as

\displaystyle V_{\theta}\mapsto V_{\theta}^{\prime}=V_{\theta}-(\mathbbm{1}-\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta})Y_{0}~.

(4.7)

These transformation may be used to set a certain number of components of the vectors
V_{\theta}
to zero; but these are never in the directions in which
\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}
act trivially.

Space Group

When the point group
\mathbf{P}
is combined with the lattice identifications the so–called space group
\mathbf{S}
is obtained. The space group is parameterized by element
g=(\theta,N)\in\mathbf{S}
where
\theta\in\mathbf{P}
and
N\in\mathbbm{Z}^{2D+16}
. The space group is generated by the elements

\displaystyle(\theta,0)[y]=\theta[y]=\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}\,y+2\pi\,V_{\theta}\quad\text{and}\quad(1,N)[y]=y+2\pi\,N~.

(4.8)

Hence, a general element of the space group acts as:

\displaystyle g[y]=(\theta,N)[y]=(1,N)(\theta,0)[y]=\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}\,y+2\pi\,(V_{\theta}+N)~.

(4.9)

Notice that the order in which these actions are applied is important here, since

\displaystyle(\theta,0)(1,N)[y]=\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}\,y+2\pi\,(\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}\,N+V_{\theta})

(4.10)

does not equal
(\theta,N)[Y]
defined above. Indeed, since the space group acts on any vector
y
not just the Narain coordinate fields
Y
, the general composition rule reads

\displaystyle g^{\prime}g=(\theta^{\prime}\theta;\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta^{\prime}}N+N^{\prime})~,

(4.11)

where the composition of the orbifold actions (
4.4
) has been used.

The space group
\mathbf{S}
is in general non–commutative (even if the point group
\mathbf{P}
is). Two space group elements
g=(\theta,N)
and
g^{\prime}=(\theta^{\prime},N^{\prime})
only commute if

\displaystyle\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta^{\prime}}\,\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}=\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}\,\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta^{\prime}}\quad\text{and}\quad(\mathbbm{1}-\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta^{\prime}})(N+V_{\theta})=(\mathbbm{1}-\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta})(N^{\prime}+V_{\theta^{\prime}})~.

(4.12)

Fixed points
Y_{\text{fix}}
of a space group element
g\in\mathbf{S}
are defined by the condition

\displaystyle Y_{\text{fix}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{=}}g[Y_{\text{fix}}]=\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}\,Y_{\text{fix}}+2\pi(V_{\theta}+N)~.

(4.13)

By bringing the first term on the right to the left–hand–side shows that this equation only has solutions provided that
\widehat{\cal P}{}^{\parallel}_{\theta}(V_{\theta}+N)=0
, hence the fixed point condition becomes

\displaystyle\big(\mathbbm{1}-\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}\big)Y_{\text{fix}}=2\pi\,\widehat{\cal P}{}^{\perp}_{\theta}(V_{\theta}+N)~,

(4.14)

which can be solved by the expression:

\displaystyle Y_{\text{fix}}=2\pi\,\sum_{j=0}^{|\theta|-1}\dfrac{j}{|\theta|}\,\Big(\widehat{\cal P}{}^{\parallel}_{\theta}-\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}{}^{j}\Big)(V_{\theta}+N)~.

(4.15)

To derive that this determines the fixed points, first observe that while the matrix
\mathbbm{1}-\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}
is not invertible, it is invertible on the subspace defined by
\widehat{\cal P}{}^{\perp}_{\theta}
: Only one of the eigenvalues,
\exp(2\pi i\,j/|\theta|)
for
j=0,\ldots,|\theta|-1
, of
\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}
is equal to unity, but that one is excluded by this projection operator. On the corresponding subspace one can show that

\displaystyle\Big(\mathbbm{1}-\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}\Big)^{-1}\widehat{\cal P}{}^{\perp}_{\theta}=\sum_{j=0}^{|\theta|-1}\dfrac{j}{|\theta|}\,\Big(\widehat{\cal P}{}^{\parallel}_{\theta}-\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}{}^{j}\Big)~,

(4.16)

by making a general power expansion in
\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}
of the left–hand–side and multiplying this by
\mathbbm{1}-\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}
and requiring that this equals
\widehat{\cal P}{}^{\perp}_{\theta}
. This determines the expansion uniquely up to adding an arbitrary constant to
\widehat{\cal P}{}^{\parallel}_{\theta}
. This constant is fixed by requiring that projecting with
\widehat{\cal P}{}^{\parallel}_{\theta}
should give zero.

Orbifold Compatible Residual Gauge Symmetry

Not all fixed points identified by this equation are physical. Only those fixed points that cannot be removed by the residual gauge transformations (
2.21
) uncovered in Section
2
are physically distinct.
Given the boundary conditions for the doubled coordinate fields, the constant gauge parameters
\Lambda_{0}
are subject to

\displaystyle\Big(\mathbbm{1}-\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}\Big)\Lambda_{0}=2\pi\,L~,

(4.17)

where

. In order that this gauge symmetry is compatible with the orbifold action, the constant zero model gauge parameters have to be modified to

\displaystyle\Lambda_{0}=\Lambda_{\text{fix}}+\widehat{\cal P}^{\parallel}_{\mathbf{P}}\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{0}\\

4.2
Construction of an Orbifold Compatible Generalized Metric

The first condition in (
4.2
) is not so much a condition on the integral representation matrices
\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}
, but rather an existence condition of an appropriate generalized metric
\widehat{\cal H}
and therefore the Narain orbifold itself. Suppose a real representation
R_{\theta}\in\mathrm{O}(D;\mathbbm{R})\times\mathrm{O}(D+16;\mathbbm{R})\subset\mathrm{O}_{\eta}(D,D+16;\mathbbm{R})
, i.e.

\displaystyle R_{\theta}^{T}R_{\theta}=\mathbbm{1}\quad\text{and}\quad R_{\theta}^{T}\eta R_{\theta}=\eta~,

(4.19)

where
\eta
is given in (
C.10
),
of the point group
\mathbf{P}
has been constructed. Hence, these matrices may be displayed as

\displaystyle R_{\theta}=\begin{pmatrix}R_{\text{R}\,\theta}&0\\[4.30554pt]
0&R_{\text{L}\,\theta}\end{pmatrix}~,\quad R_{\text{R}\,\theta}\in\mathrm{O}(D;\mathbbm{R})\quad\text{and}\quad R_{\text{L}\,\theta}\in\mathrm{O}(D+16;\mathbbm{R})~.

(4.20)

Next, consider the matrix
{\cal M}_{\text{inv}}
constructed as

\displaystyle{\cal M}_{\text{inv}}=\dfrac{1}{|\mathbf{P}|}\sum_{\theta\in\mathbf{P}}R_{\theta}^{-1}\,{\cal M}_{0}\,\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}~,

(4.21)

from a generic real
2D+16\times 2D+16
–matrix
{\cal M}_{0}
,
such that
{\cal M}_{\text{inv}}
is invariant under the full point group
\mathbf{P}
. Indeed, under any element
\theta
of the point group, this generalized vielbein
{\cal M}_{\text{inv}}
maps to itself:

\displaystyle\big(R_{\theta}\big)^{-1}\,{\cal M}_{\text{inv}}\,\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}=\dfrac{1}{|\mathbf{P}|}\sum_{\theta^{\prime}\in\mathbf{P}}R_{\theta}^{-1}R_{\theta^{\prime}}^{-1}\,{\cal M}_{0}\,\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta^{\prime}}\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}=\dfrac{1}{|\mathbf{P}|}\sum_{\theta^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbf{P}}R_{\theta^{\prime\prime}}^{-1}\,{\cal M}_{0}\,\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta^{\prime\prime}}={\cal M}_{\text{inv}}~,

(4.22)

since
\theta^{\prime}\in\mathbf{P}
and
\theta^{\prime\prime}=\theta^{\prime}\theta\in\mathbf{P}
both label the full point group
\mathbf{P}
. Thus the matrix
{\cal M}_{\text{inv}}
would be a candidate for an invariant vielbein
{\cal E}_{\text{inv}}
, from which the Narain moduli
G,B,A
could be read off, provided that one chooses it such that it satisfies (
C.11
). However, solving this non–linear constraint can prove difficult and is in fact not necessary to determine these moduli. Indeed, define the following invariant
\mathbbm{Z}_{2}
–grading

\displaystyle\widehat{\cal Z}_{\text{inv}}={\cal M}_{\text{inv}}^{-1}\,\eta\,{\cal M}_{\text{inv}}~.

(4.23)

It squares to the identity, because
\eta
does, and it is point group invariant:

\displaystyle\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}^{-1}\,\widehat{\cal Z}_{\text{inv}}\,\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}=\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}^{-1}\,{\cal M}_{\text{inv}}^{-1}\,R_{\theta}\,R_{\theta}^{-1}\,\eta\,R_{\theta}\,R_{\theta}^{-1}{\cal M}_{\text{inv}}\,\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}={\cal M}_{\text{inv}}^{-1}\,\eta\,{\cal M}_{\text{inv}}=\widehat{\cal Z}_{\text{inv}}~,

(4.24)

because
R_{\theta}^{-1}\eta R_{\theta}=\eta
,
since
R_{\theta}
satisfies (
4.19
).
An invariant generalized metric can then be obtained from this by the simple relation

\displaystyle\widehat{\cal H}_{\text{inv}}=\widehat{\eta}\,\widehat{\cal Z}_{\text{inv}}~.

(4.25)

This form ensures that
\widehat{\cal H}_{\text{inv}}
satisfies the quadratic constraint (
3.5
) automatically, since
\widehat{\cal Z}_{\text{inv}}
squares to the identity. The other constraint that
\widehat{\cal H}_{\text{inv}}
is symmetric is not implemented and hence needs to be enforced afterwards. From the generalized metric (
4.25
) or the
\mathbbm{Z}_{2}
–grading (
4.23
) the Narain moduli
G,B,A
can be read off using (
3.2
) or (
C.6
): If one starts with a completely generic
{\cal M}_{0}
, this procedure determines both the values of the frozen moduli as well as the unconstraint ones in the form of free parameters.

In order to obtain an appropriate representation
R_{\theta}\in\mathrm{O}(D;\mathbbm{R})\times\mathrm{O}(D+16;\mathbbm{R})
one may proceed as follows:

1.

Block diagonalize all elements
\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}
of the point group
\mathbf{P}
simultaneously over the real numbers using a similarity transformation
U\in\text{GL}(2D+16;\mathbbm{R})
.

2.

By additional permutations bring all elements
U^{-1}\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}U
simultaneously in a right (
D\times D
) and a left (
D+16\times D+16
) form given in (
4.20
).

This procedure always works, since the point group
\mathbf{P}
has finite order and hence the matrices
\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}
lie in the compact part of
\mathrm{O}_{\widehat{\eta}}(D,D+16;\mathbbm{R})
.
However, this does not necessarily lead to a valid generalized metric
\widehat{\cal H}
: It might happen that
{\cal M}_{\text{inv}}
is not invertible and the
\mathbbm{Z}_{2}
–grading (
4.23
) cannot be defined. Secondly, it might happen that the metric
G
read off from (
4.25
) using the explicit form (
3.2
) is not positive definite. This means that the distribution of the blocks chosen in the second step is not appropriate and another distribution should be considered.

The procedure outlined here may seem to be somewhat awkward in light of the issues that the generalized metric
\widehat{\cal H}
is not automatically symmetric or that the metric
G
is not necessarily positive definite. However, these are minor concerns as compare to the situation before: In
[
29
]
the moduli were constraint by a coupled set of Riccati matrix equations (see (5.17) in that reference) for which the very existence of solutions is unclear and the explicite determination of such solutions is a highly non–trivial task. Therefore, that here a concrete procedure is unfolded might be considered as a definite step forward in the construction of asymmetric orbifolds. In Appendix
6
examples are given in which this procedure has been executed to obtain the most general generalized metrics for certain asymmetric orbifolds.

In the following it is assumed that the generalized metric
\widehat{\cal H}
, the vielbein
{\cal E}
and the
\mathbbm{Z}_{2}
–grading
\widehat{\cal Z}
are invariant under the orbifold action and hence the subscript
inv
is dropped. In particular, the generalized vielbein
{\cal E}
satifies

\displaystyle\big(R_{\theta}\big)^{-1}{\cal E}\,\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}={\cal E}~,

(4.26)

for all
\theta\in\mathbf{P}
.

4.3
Dimension of the moduli space of a Narain Orbifold

As was argued in
[
29
]
, the dimension of the untwisted moduli space of a Narain orbifold is given by

\displaystyle\text{dim}\big[\mathfrak{M}_{\mathbf{P}}\big]=\dfrac{1}{|\mathbf{P}|}\sum_{\theta\in\mathbf{P}}\chi_{R}(\theta)\,\chi_{L}^{*}(\theta)~,\quad\chi_{\text{L/R}}(\theta)=\text{tr}\big[R_{\text{L/R}\,\theta}\big]=\text{tr}\Big[\dfrac{\mathbbm{1}\pm\eta}{2}R_{\theta}\Big]~.

(4.27)

In particular, when either the left– or right–moving twist representation is trivial while the other is not, the dimension of the moduli space is zero. Hence, this result may be used to confirm if all unconstraint moduli have been identified.

4.4
Narain Orbifold Worldsheet Torus Boundary Conditions

On the worldsheet the Narain coordinates
Y
are periodic up to actions of the space group
\mathbf{S}
:

\displaystyle Y(z+1)

\displaystyle=\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}\,Y(z)+2\pi\,(N+V_{\theta})\quad\text{and}\quad

(4.28a)

\displaystyle Y(z-\tau)

\displaystyle=\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta^{\prime}}\,Y(z)+2\pi\,(N^{\prime}+V_{\theta^{\prime}})~.

(4.28b)

The boundary conditions of the superpartners
\psi
of the non–compact coordinate fields
x
are the standard ones:

\displaystyle\psi(z+1)=(-)^{s}\,\psi(z)\quad\text{and}\quad\psi(z-\tau)=(-)^{s^{\prime}}\,\psi(z)~,

(4.29a)

where the spin structures
s,s^{\prime}=0,1
have been included.
The boundary conditions for the right–moving worldsheet fermions
\Psi
have to be compatible with the worldsheet supersymmetry transformations (
3.10
) linking them with the Narain coordinates
Y
. Using (
4.26
) one infers that their boundary condtions read

\displaystyle\Psi(z+1)=(-)^{s}\,R_{\text{R}\,\theta}\,\Psi(z)\quad\text{and}\quad\Psi(z-\tau)=(-)^{s^{\prime}}\,R_{\text{R}\,\theta^{\prime}}\,\Psi(z)~.

(4.29b)

Since, the matrices
R_{\text{R}\,\theta}
and
R_{\text{R}\,\theta^{\prime}}
commute, see (
4.12
), they can be simultaneously diagonalized over the
complex
numbers to

\displaystyle S_{\text{R}\,\theta,\theta^{\prime}}^{-1}\,R_{\text{R}\,\theta}\,S_{\text{R}\,\theta,\theta^{\prime}}^{\phantom{-1}}=e^{2\pi i\,\mathbf{v}_{\text{R}\,\theta}}\quad\text{and}\quad S_{\text{R}\,\theta,\theta^{\prime}}^{-1}\,R_{\text{R}\,\theta^{\prime}}\,S_{\text{R}\,\theta,\theta^{\prime}}^{\phantom{-1}}=e^{2\pi i\,\mathbf{v}_{\text{R}\,\theta^{\prime}}}~,

(4.30)

where the
D\times D
–matrix
\mathbf{v}_{\text{R}\,\theta}
is associated to the
real
twist vector
v_{\text{R}\,\theta}=(v_{\text{R}\,\theta\,1},\ldots,v_{\text{R}\,\theta\,D})
, as defined in (
A.18
). For elements that involve rotations in two dimensions, the eigenvalues come in complex conjugate pairs.
Target space spinors are admitted only if, the orbifold twists preserve orientation hence their determinants have to be equal unity.

Only the real twist vector
v_{\text{R}\,\theta}
is relevant to determine how many target space supersymmetries are perserved in the non–compact dimensions. No supersymmetries are preserved if in succession:

•

an odd number of entries of
v_{\text{R}\,\theta}
are non–zero;

•

not all non–zero entries can be chosen in opposite signed pairs;

•

there is only one such pair.

If none of these three conditions are satisfied, a complex twist vector
v_{\text{R}\mathbbm{C}\,\theta}
can be associated to the real twist vector by taking one of the two entries of the opposite signed pairs augmented by a number of zeros such that this complex vector has four entries. By the final condition it follows that at least two entries are non–zero. If there is a choice of these entries such that

\displaystyle\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}e_{4}\cdot v_{\text{R}\mathbbm{C}\,\theta}\equiv 0~,

(4.31)

at least
{\cal N}=1
supersymmetry is preserved in the
d
non–compact dimensions.

Because of the inhomogeneous terms, solving the boundary conditions (
4.28
) of the Narain coordinates
Y
is more involved. To this end the following ansatz is made

\displaystyle Y(z)=Y_{\text{c}}(z)+Y_{\text{q}}(z)~,\qquad Y_{\text{c}}(z)=2\pi(y_{0}+p\,z+{\bar{p}}\,{\bar{z}})

(4.32)

where
y_{0},p,{\bar{p}}
are constant vectors and
Y_{\text{q}}(z)
is assumed to satisfy the homogeneous boundary conditions

\displaystyle Y_{\text{q}}(z+1)=\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}\,Y_{\text{q}}(z)\quad\text{and}\quad Y_{\text{q}}(z-\tau)=\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta^{\prime}}\,Y_{\text{q}}(z)~,

(4.33)

where the twist matrices can be written as complex exponentials

\displaystyle\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}=e^{2\pi i\,\mathbf{v}_{\theta}}\quad\text{and}\quad\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta^{\prime}}=e^{2\pi i\,\mathbf{v}_{\theta^{\prime}}}~,

(4.34)

in terms of the matrices
\mathbf{v}_{\theta}
and
\mathbf{v}_{\theta^{\prime}}
which mutually commute.

The zero modes contained in
Y_{\text{c}}(z)
can be treated in the following fashion: Inserting the ansatz (
4.32
) in the boundary conditions (
4.28
) leads to the requirements

\displaystyle\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}p=\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta^{\prime}}p=p~,\qquad\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}{\bar{p}}=\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta^{\prime}}{\bar{p}}={\bar{p}}~,

(4.35a)

\displaystyle\big(\mathbbm{1}-\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}\big)y_{0}+p+{\bar{p}}=N+V_{\theta}\quad\text{and}\quad\big(\mathbbm{1}-\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta^{\prime}}\big)y_{0}-\tau\,p-{\bar{\tau}}\,{\bar{p}}=N^{\prime}+V_{\theta^{\prime}}~.

(4.35b)

The first two conditions imply that both
p
and
{\bar{p}}
lie in both the invariant subspaces of
\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta}
and
\widehat{\cal R}_{\theta^{\prime}}
, which can be identified by the projectors

and
\widehat{\cal P}{}^{\parallel}_{\theta^{\prime}}

\displaystyle p=p^{\parallel}=\widehat{\cal P}{}^{\parallel}p~,\quad\text{and}\quad{\bar{p}}={\bar{p}}^{\parallel}=\widehat{\cal P}{}^{\parallel}{\bar{p}}~,

\displaystyle\widehat{\cal P}{}^{\parallel}=\widehat{\cal P}{}^{\parallel}_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}=\widehat{\cal P}{}^{\parallel}_{\theta}\,\widehat{\cal P}{}^{\parallel}_{\theta^{\prime}}\quad\text{and}\quad{\cal P}^{\perp}=\mathbbm{1}-{\cal P}^{\parallel}~.

(4.37)

Hence, the combined orbifold actions leave a Narain subtorus of dimension
D^{\parallel}

\displaystyle D^{\parallel}=D^{\parallel}_{\text{L}}+D^{\parallel}_{\text{R}}=\text{tr}\big[\widehat{\cal P}^{\parallel}\big]\quad\text{and}\quad D^{\perp}=D^{\parallel}_{\text{L}}+D^{\parallel}_{\text{R}}=\text{tr}\big[\widehat{\cal P}^{\perp}\big]

(4.38)

\displaystyle D^{\parallel}_{\text{L/R}}=\text{tr}\big[\widehat{\cal P}^{\parallel}\widehat{\cal P}_{\text{L/R}}\big]\quad\text{and}\quad D^{\perp}_{\text{L/R}}=\text{tr}\big[\widehat{\cal P}^{\parallel}\widehat{\cal P}_{\text{L/R}}\big]~.

(4.39)

\displaystyle D^{\parallel}_{\text{L}}+D^{\perp}_{\text{L}}=D_{\text{L}}=D+16\quad\text{and}\quad D^{\parallel}_{\text{R}}+D^{\perp}_{\text{R}}=D_{\text{R}}=D~.

(4.40)

\widehat{\cal P}{}^{\parallel}

Using the operator (

4.5
Narain Orbifold Partition Functions

The full orbifold partition function can be written as a sum over commuting space group elements

\displaystyle Z_{\text{full}}=Z_{\text{Mink}}(\tau,{\bar{\tau}})\,\dfrac{1}{|\mathbf{P}|}\sum_{[g,g^{\prime}]=0}Z_{\text{Ferm}}\bigl[^{g}_{g^{\prime}}\bigr]({\bar{\tau}})\,Z_{\text{Lattice}}\bigl[^{g}_{g^{\prime}}\bigr](\tau,{\bar{\tau}})\,Z_{\text{Quan}}\bigl[^{g}_{g^{\prime}}\bigr](\tau,{\bar{\tau}})~,

Aside from the standard Minkowski space contribution, the various factors arise as follows:

Taking into account the boundary conditions (

),

not on the whole space group elements

Inserting the expression (
4.44

4.6
Supersymmetric Symmetric Orbifolds

The dominant part of the (heterotic) string literature concerns itself with a very special class of orbifold: Orbifolds with actions that are purely geometrical that treat the left– and right–moving coordinate fields identically. 5.1
Single Shift Orbifolds

A

shift orbifold is generated by a single shift

\displaystyle V=\mbox{$\frac{1}{K}$}\,W~,\qquad W\in\mathbbm{Z}^{2D+16}~,\quad\mbox{$\frac{1}{2K}$}\,W^{2}\equiv 0~,

whose entries of

\displaystyle\big\{N+k\,V~\big|~N\in\mathbbm{Z}^{2D+16}~,~~k=0,\ldots,K-1\big\}

\displaystyle\mbox{$\frac{1}{K}$}\,\big(W^{T}\hat{\eta}N+k\,\mbox{$\frac{1}{2K}$}\,W^{2}\big)\equiv 0~,

(5.5)

resulting from the phases in (

necessary in order to solve the orbifold projection condition (

To solve the projection condition (

(5.7)

By taking inner products of this ansatz with

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}n\\

(5.9)

In general the solution for

By performing the matrix multiplication in (

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}n\phantom{{}^{\prime}}=W\phantom{{}^{\prime}}{\overline{W}}\phantom{{}^{\prime}}\,N~,\\[4.30554pt]

Using these vectors and their conjugates two projections,

Modification of the Generalized Metric

The expression (
5.25
) seems to suggest, the shift orbifold defines another Narain theory with a modified generalized metric
\widehat{\cal H}^{\prime}
obtained from an
\widehat{\cal T}\in\text{O}_{\widehat{\eta}}(D,D+16;\mathbbm{Q})

\widehat{\eta}
and if so how to correct for that. To this end, define
\widehat{\eta}^{\prime}
by

\displaystyle N^{\prime\,T}\widehat{\eta}^{\prime}\,N^{\prime}=(N+k\,V)^{T}\widehat{\eta}\,(N+k\,V)~.

(5.26)

By inserting (
5.25

\displaystyle\widehat{\eta}^{-1}\widehat{\Pi}^{T}\widehat{\eta}=\widehat{\Pi}^{\prime}+W^{2}\,\widehat{\Xi}^{\prime}~,\quad\widehat{\eta}^{-1}\widehat{\Pi}^{\prime T}\widehat{\eta}=\widehat{\Pi}-W^{2}\,\widehat{\Xi}^{\prime}~,\qquad\widehat{\eta}^{-1}\widehat{\Xi}^{\prime T}\widehat{\eta}=\widehat{\Xi}^{\prime}~,

(5.27)

it follows that
\widehat{\eta}^{\prime}
can be written as

\displaystyle\widehat{\eta}^{\prime}=\widehat{\cal M}^{T}\widehat{\eta}\,\widehat{\cal M}~,\quad\widehat{\cal M}=\mathbbm{1}-\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\,W^{2}\,\widehat{\Xi}^{\prime}\in\text{GL}(2D+16;\mathbbm{Z})~.

(5.28)

In other words, in terms of the lattice defined by
N^{\prime}
the metric
\widehat{\eta}
has changed to
\widehat{\eta}^{\prime}
. Thus, by replacing

by the new integral vector

, the metric
\widehat{\eta}
remains the same. This motivates to define

, which reads explicitly:

\displaystyle\widehat{\cal T}=\widehat{\Pi}_{\perp}+\mbox{$\frac{1}{K}$}\,\widehat{\Pi}_{W}+K\,\widehat{\Pi}_{W^{\prime}}+\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\big(K-\mbox{$\frac{1}{K}$}\big)\,W^{2}\,\widehat{\Xi}^{\prime}~.

(5.29)

It may be verified that

\widehat{\cal H}^{\prime}
is given by

\displaystyle\widehat{\cal H}^{\prime}=\widehat{\cal T}^{T}\widehat{\cal H}\,\widehat{\cal T}~.

(5.30)

This result shows explicitly that a shift orbifolds of a Narain theory is again a Narain theory but with redefined moduli.

Dependence on the Associated Vector

The construction developed here determines the new moduli of the Narain lattice that results from a shift orbifold. However, the procedure depends on a somewhat arbitrary integral associated vector
W^{\prime}
only constraint to satisfy (

Seemingly this can have striking consequences, as the same shift orbifold can have different geometric and non–geometric interpretations. However, these different forms are related to each other by

\displaystyle\widehat{\cal E}=\begin{pmatrix}R&0\\
0&\mbox{$\frac{1}{R}$}\end{pmatrix}~.

(5.31)

Performing an order–
K
shift orbifold on this circle leads to the Narain mapping

\displaystyle\widehat{\cal T}=\begin{pmatrix}\mbox{$\frac{1}{K}$}&0\\
0&K\end{pmatrix}~.

(5.32)

Hence, the generalized vielbein becomes

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}R^{\prime}&0\\
0&\mbox{$\frac{1}{R^{\prime}}$}\end{pmatrix}=\widehat{\cal E}^{\prime}=\widehat{\cal E}\,\widehat{\cal T}=\begin{pmatrix}R&0\\
0&\mbox{$\frac{1}{R}$}\end{pmatrix}\,\begin{pmatrix}\mbox{$\frac{1}{K}$}&0\\
0&K\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\mbox{$\frac{R}{K}$}&0\\
0&\mbox{$\frac{K}{R}$}\end{pmatrix}~.

(5.33)

This indicates that the target space circle is shrunk by a factor
1/K
while the dual circle is stretched by
K
at the same time.

5.3
Geometrical Shift with a Wilson Line

A Wilson line is often introduced in the orbifold literature as geometrical shift orbifold with an associated shift action on the gauge degrees of freedom. Concretely, consider an order–
K_{i}
Wilson line on the
i
–th torus direction

\displaystyle W_{i}=\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon_{i}\\
0\\
a_{i}\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad W_{i}^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\epsilon_{i}\\
0\end{pmatrix}~,

(5.34)

where
a_{i}
is a 16–component integral vector such that

\displaystyle K_{i}\,a_{i}\in\mathbbm{Z}\quad\text{and}\quad\mbox{$\frac{1}{2K_{i}}$}\,a_{i}^{2}=\mbox{$\frac{1}{2K_{i}}$}\,a_{i}^{T}g_{16}a_{i}\equiv 0~.

(5.35)

The
i
–th
D
–dimensional Euclidean basis vector
\epsilon_{i}
satisfies

\displaystyle\epsilon_{i}^{T}\epsilon_{j}=(\epsilon_{i})_{j}=\delta_{ij}~.

(5.36)

Since
W_{i}
and
W_{i}^{\prime}
both involve
\epsilon_{i}
but in different directions in the Narain lattice, it is ensured that we have an unique decomposition of the Narain lattice by (
5.6
). The vector
W_{i}^{\prime}
is chosen such that the conditions in (
5.20
) are fulfilled, hence the operators (
5.12
) are given by

\displaystyle\widehat{\Pi}_{i}=\begin{pmatrix}\pi_{i}&0&0\\[4.30554pt]
0&0&0\\[4.30554pt]
a_{i}\epsilon_{i}^{T}&0&0\end{pmatrix}~,\quad\widehat{\Pi}_{i}^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\[4.30554pt]
-a_{i}^{2}\,\pi_{i}&\pi_{i}&\epsilon_{i}a_{i}^{T}g_{16}\\[4.30554pt]
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}~,\quad\widehat{\Xi}_{i}^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\[4.30554pt]
\pi_{i}&0&0\\[4.30554pt]
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}~,

(5.37)

where
\pi_{i}=\epsilon_{i}\epsilon_{i}^{T}
and
\pi_{i}^{\perp}=\mathbbm{1}_{D}-\pi_{i}
.
This results in the following Narain mapping

\displaystyle\widehat{\cal T}_{i}=\begin{pmatrix}\pi_{i}^{\perp}+\mbox{$\frac{1}{K_{i}}$}\,\pi_{i}&0&0\\[8.61108pt]
\big(1-\mbox{$\frac{K_{i}}{2}$}-\mbox{$\frac{1}{2K_{i}}$}\big)\,a_{i}^{2}\,\pi_{i}&\pi_{i}^{\perp}+K_{i}\,\pi_{i}&(K_{i}-1)\,\epsilon_{i}a_{i}^{T}g_{16}\\[8.61108pt]
\big(\mbox{$\frac{1}{K_{i}}$}-1\big)\,a_{i}\epsilon_{i}^{T}&0&\mathbbm{1}_{16}\end{pmatrix}~,

(5.38)

using (
5.29
). By using the
\text{O}_{\hat{\eta}}(D,D+16;\mathbbm{R})
elements given in (
C.14
) this can be written as

\displaystyle\widehat{\cal T}_{i}=\widehat{\cal M}_{e}(\Delta e_{i})\,\widehat{\cal M}_{a}(\Delta a_{i})~,\quad\Delta e_{i}=\pi_{i}^{\perp}+\mbox{$\frac{1}{K_{i}}$}\,\pi_{i}~,\quad\text{and}\quad\Delta a_{i}=-\big(1-\mbox{$\frac{1}{K_{i}}$}\big)\,\alpha_{16}a_{i}\,\epsilon_{i}^{T}~.

(5.39)

This reduces to the case discussed above when switching off the Wilson line and taking
D=1
.

Since shift actions on the Narain space commute, various geometrical shifts with Wilson lines can be combined. This simply leads to a product of the corresponding Narain mappings. If all
D
dimensions are shift orbifolded, this gives

\displaystyle\widehat{\cal T}=\widehat{\cal M}_{e}(\Delta e)\,\widehat{\cal M}_{a}(\Delta a)~,\quad\Delta e=\sum_{i}\mbox{$\frac{1}{K_{i}}$}\,\pi_{i}~,\quad\text{and}\quad\Delta a=-\sum_{i}\big(1-\mbox{$\frac{1}{K_{i}}$}\big)\,\alpha_{16}a_{i}\,\epsilon_{i}^{T}~.

(5.40)

Since
\widehat{\cal T}
is made up of the same building blocks as a Narain vielbein, it can be expressed as a Narain vielbein itself as

\widehat{\cal T}=\widehat{\cal E}(\Delta e,0,\Delta a)\,.

Hence, if the original model is described by the moduli
e,B,A
, the new moduli can be read off from the vielbein multiplication

\displaystyle\widehat{\cal E}(e^{\prime},B^{\prime},A^{\prime})=\widehat{\cal E}(e,B,A)\,\widehat{\cal T}=\widehat{\cal E}(e,B,A)\,\widehat{\cal E}(\Delta e,0,\Delta a)

(5.41)

using (
C.15
) to be

\displaystyle e^{\prime}=E\,\Delta e~,\quad B^{\prime}=\Delta e^{T}B\,\Delta e+\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}e^{T}A^{T}\Delta a-\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\Delta a^{T}Ae\quad\text{and}\quad A^{\prime}=A\,\Delta e+\Delta a~.

(5.42)

Hence, in particular, if the anti–symmetric tensor
B
and the Wilson lines
A
initially were switched off, only a Wilson line background is introduce by this shift orbifold. When only the
B
–field was zero, but the initial Wilson lines
A
were not, then the anti–symmetric tensor becomes switched on after this shift orbifold.

5.4
Non–Geometric Shift with a Wilson Line

Next, consider an order–
K_{i}
Wilson line on the
i
–th dual torus direction

\displaystyle{\widetilde{W}}_{i}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\epsilon_{i}\\
a_{i}\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad{\widetilde{W}}_{i}^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon_{i}\\
0\\
0\end{pmatrix}~,

(5.43)

where
a_{i}
satisfies the same properties as in the previous subsection. Hence, the same procedure can be followed as in that subsection leading to

\displaystyle\widetilde{{\cal T}}_{i}=\widehat{\cal M}_{e}(\Delta\tilde{e}_{i})\,\widehat{\cal M}_{\alpha}(\Delta\alpha_{i})~,\quad\Delta\tilde{e}_{i}=\pi_{i}^{\perp}+{K_{i}}\,\pi_{i}~,\quad\text{and}\quad\Delta\alpha_{i}=-\big(1-\mbox{$\frac{1}{K_{i}}$}\big)\,\alpha_{16}a_{i}\,\epsilon_{i}^{T}~,

(5.44)

where
\widehat{\cal M}_{\alpha}
is defined in (
C.17
).
Hence, in this case non–geometric gauge moduli are switched on.

The reason how this comes about can be easily understood by the following observation. The starting data of this and the previous subsection are related to each other via the
T
–duality operator
\widehat{I}
given in (
C.16
):

\displaystyle{\widetilde{W}}_{i}=\widehat{I}\,W_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad{\widetilde{W}}_{i}^{\prime}=\widehat{I}\,W_{i}^{\prime}~.

(5.45)

This is just the statement that
T
–duality takes geometric shifts to non–geometric ones. Hence, one expects that also the change of the moduli due to the (non–)geometric shift with a Wilson line are related via the same operation. This is indeed the case, since

\displaystyle\widetilde{{\cal T}}_{i}=\widehat{I}\,\widehat{\cal T}_{i}\,\widehat{I}~.

(5.46)

This exemplifies that the interpretation of a certain transformation to be geometric or non–geometric depends on the duality frame chosen.

In this case it is only under certain circumstances possible to determine the new moduli using products of Narain vielbeins: If in the initial case
B=A=0
, then

\displaystyle\widehat{\cal E}(e,0,0)\,\widetilde{{\cal T}}_{i}=\widehat{\cal E}(e,0,0)\,\widehat{I}\,\widehat{\cal E}(\Delta e_{i},0,\Delta a_{i})\,\widehat{I}=\widehat{I}\,\widehat{\cal E}(e^{-T},0,0)\,\widehat{\cal E}(\Delta e_{i},0,\Delta a_{i})\,\widehat{I}

\displaystyle=\widehat{I}\,\widehat{\cal E}(e^{-T}\Delta e_{i},0,\Delta a_{i})\,\widehat{I}~.

(5.47)

That is by going to the fully T–dual frame, where the initial vielbein is
e^{-T}
, the new moduli are

\displaystyle e^{\prime}=e^{-T}\Delta e_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad A^{\prime}=\Delta a_{i}~.

(5.48)

However, as soon as either an anti–symmetric tensor or a Wilson line background was switched on already, this does not work any more. To determine the moduli for more general initial Narain backgrounds, one should use the generalized metric instead. The new generalized Narain metric
\widehat{\cal H}^{\prime}
obtained after performing this non–geometric shift with a Wilson line is given by

\displaystyle\widehat{\cal H}^{\prime}=\widetilde{{\cal T}}_{i}^{T}\,\widehat{\cal H}\,\widetilde{{\cal T}}_{i}~.

(5.49)

5.5
Non–Geometric Shift in Two Dimensions

Consider the order–
K
non–geometric shift

\displaystyle W=\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon_{i}\\
\epsilon_{j}\\
0\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad W_{i}^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\epsilon_{i}\\
0\end{pmatrix}~,\quad W_{j}^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon_{j}\\
0\\
0\end{pmatrix}~,

(5.50)

for
i\neq j
and no action on the gauge degrees of freedom. Here two choices for the vector
W^{\prime}
are given distinguished by the label
i
and
j
, which is used below as well for this purpose, to exemplify possible consequences of different choices of the associated vector
W^{\prime}
to solve the orbifold constraint.

In the case
W_{i}^{\prime}
is employed, one finds

\displaystyle\widehat{\Pi}_{i}=\begin{pmatrix}\pi_{i}&0&0\\
\epsilon_{j}\epsilon_{i}^{T}&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad\widehat{\Pi}_{i}^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
\epsilon_{i}\epsilon_{j}^{T}&\pi_{i}&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}~.

(5.51)

(Since
W^{2}=W_{i}^{\prime 2}=0
and
\widehat{\Xi}_{i}^{\prime}
is irrelevant.) Hence the Narain mapping becomes

\displaystyle\widehat{\cal T}_{i}=\begin{pmatrix}\pi_{i}^{\perp}+\mbox{$\frac{1}{K}$}\,\pi_{i}&0&0\\[4.30554pt]
\big(\mbox{$\frac{1}{K}$}-1\big)\epsilon_{j}\epsilon_{i}^{T}+\big(K-1\big)\epsilon_{i}\epsilon_{j}^{T}&\pi_{i}^{\perp}+K\,\pi_{i}&0\\[4.30554pt]
0&0&\mathbbm{1}_{16}\end{pmatrix}~.

(5.52)

This can be written as

\displaystyle\widehat{\cal T}_{i}=\widehat{\cal M}_{e}(\Delta e_{i})\,\widehat{\cal M}_{b}(\Delta b_{ij})~,\quad\Delta e_{i}=\pi_{i}^{\perp}+\mbox{$\frac{1}{K}$}\,\pi_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad\Delta b_{ij}=\big(1-\mbox{$\frac{1}{K}$}\big)\big(\epsilon_{i}\epsilon_{j}^{T}-\epsilon_{j}\epsilon_{i}^{T}\big)~.

(5.53)

Hence, performing a non–geometrical shift orbifold in two directions has the effect of switching on the anti–symmetric tensor field
B
in those directions.

If
W_{j}^{\prime}
is used instead, the results are

\displaystyle\widehat{\Pi}_{j}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\epsilon_{i}\epsilon_{j}^{T}&0\\
0&\pi_{j}&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad\widehat{\Pi}_{j}^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}\pi_{j}&\epsilon_{j}\epsilon_{i}^{T}&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}~.

(5.54)

leading to

\displaystyle\widehat{\cal T}_{j}=\begin{pmatrix}\pi_{j}^{\perp}+K\,\pi_{j}&\big(\mbox{$\frac{1}{K}$}-1\big)\epsilon_{i}\epsilon_{j}^{T}+\big(K-1\big)\epsilon_{j}\epsilon_{i}^{T}&0\\[4.30554pt]
0&\pi_{j}^{\perp}+\mbox{$\frac{1}{K}$}\,\pi_{j}&0\\[4.30554pt]
0&0&\mathbbm{1}_{16}\end{pmatrix}~,

(5.55)

which can be written as

\displaystyle\widehat{\cal T}_{j}=\widehat{M}_{e}(\Delta e_{j})\,M_{\beta}(\Delta\beta_{ij})~,\quad\Delta e_{j}=\pi_{j}^{\perp}+{K}\,\pi_{j}\quad\text{and}\quad\Delta\beta_{ij}=\big(1-\mbox{$\frac{1}{K}$}\big)\big(\epsilon_{j}\epsilon_{i}^{T}-\epsilon_{i}\epsilon_{j}^{T}\big)~.

(5.56)

Hence, by making the choice
W_{j}^{\prime}
for the vector to solve the orbifold constraint, instead the interpretation of performing the same simultaneous shift in a torus direction and a shift in a different dual torus direction leads to a non–geometric
\beta
–background.

The two seemingly different situations can again be mapped to each other. Indeed, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle W=\widehat{X}_{ij}\widehat{I}\,W\quad\text{and}\quad W_{j}^{\prime}=\widehat{X}_{ij}\widehat{I}\,W_{i}^{\prime}~,

(5.57)

where
\widehat{X}_{ij}
exchanges the directions
i
and
j
on the torus and the dual torus simultaneously. Applying these transformations to the moduli mapping
\widehat{\cal T}_{i}
gives

\displaystyle\widehat{X}_{ij}\widehat{I}\,\widehat{\cal T}_{i}\,\widehat{X}_{ij}\widehat{I}=\widehat{\cal T}_{j}~.

(5.58)

Hence, the interpretation of the moduli depends on the choice of the associated vector
W^{\prime}
.

6
Higher dimensional T–fold examples

6.1
Right–Twisted Full T–Duality Narain Orbifolds

Full T–duality orbifolds refer to Narain orbifolds in which the orbifold action is a T–duality transformation in all
D
compact torus directions. Below two realizations of them are discussed: one in this subsection, one in the next. Even though their starting data are very similar, their properties differ in a number of interesting ways.

Consider the Narain orbifold with the
\mathbbm{Z}_{2}
T–duality twist given by

\displaystyle\widehat{\cal R}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathbbm{1}_{D}&0\\
\mathbbm{1}_{D}&0&0\\
0&0&\mathbbm{1}_{16}\end{pmatrix}~.

(6.1)

The associated block–diagonal twist is given by

\displaystyle R=\begin{pmatrix}-\mathbbm{1}_{D}&0&0\\
0&\mathbbm{1}_{D}&0\\
0&0&\mathbbm{1}_{16}\end{pmatrix}~,\quad\text{hence}\quad v_{\text{R}}=\begin{pmatrix}\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}^{D}\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad v_{\text{L}}=0~,

(6.2)

so that this T–duality orbifold only affects the right–movers. Hence, this T–fold can preserve target space supersymmetry only when
D
is dividable by 4, i.e.
D=4,8
. Otherwise, all supersymmetry are necessarily broken.

The form of the generalized metric can be determined using the method outlined in Subsection
4.2
.
It follows, that the most general invariant matrix
{\cal M}_{\text{inv}}
can be taken to be

\displaystyle{\cal M}_{\text{inv}}=\dfrac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&-\alpha&0\\
\delta&\phantom{-}\delta&2\phi\\
\rho&\phantom{-}\rho&2\tau\end{pmatrix}~,

(6.3)

using (
4.21
). However, given that all Narain moduli are stabilized, since

\displaystyle\dim\big[\mathfrak{M}_{\mathbf{P}}\big]=\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}D(D+16)+\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}(-D)(D+16)=0

(6.4)

by evaluating (
4.27
) explicitly,
a simplified choice for this matrix is sufficient: Setting
\rho=\phi=0
, i.e.

\displaystyle{\cal M}_{\text{inv}}=\dfrac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&-\alpha&0\\
\delta&\phantom{-}\delta&0\\
0&\phantom{-}0&2\tau\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad{\cal M}_{\text{inv}}^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}\phantom{-}\alpha^{-1}&\delta^{-1}&0\\
-\alpha^{-1}&\delta^{-1}&0\\
\phantom{-}0&0&\tau^{-1}\end{pmatrix}~,

(6.5)

assuming that
\alpha,\delta
and
\tau
are invertible matrices. These expressions imply that

\displaystyle\widehat{\cal Z}_{\text{inv}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathbbm{1}_{D}&0\\
\mathbbm{1}_{D}&0&0\\
0&0&\mathbbm{1}_{16}\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad\widehat{\cal H}_{\text{inv}}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbbm{1}_{D}&0&0\\
0&\mathbbm{1}_{D}&0\\
0&0&g_{16}\end{pmatrix}~,

(6.6)

using (
4.23
) and (
4.25
). Notice that these results are, in fact, independent of the precise form of
\alpha,\delta
and
\tau
(which thus could have been chosen to be
\alpha=\delta=\mathbbm{1}_{D}
and
\tau=\mathbbm{1}_{16}
). This is just a reflection of the fact that this orbifold fixes all moduli. They read:
G=\mathbbm{1}_{D}
and
B=A=0
.

The definition of a Narain orbifold is not complete without specifying the Narain shift vector. Given that the parallel and perpendicular projectors (
4.6
) take the form

\displaystyle\widehat{\cal P}^{\parallel}=\dfrac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbbm{1}_{D}&\mathbbm{1}_{D}&0\\
\mathbbm{1}_{D}&\mathbbm{1}_{D}&0\\
0&0&\mathbbm{1}_{16}\end{pmatrix}~,\quad\widehat{\cal P}^{\perp}=\dfrac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}\phantom{-}\mathbbm{1}_{D}&-\mathbbm{1}_{D}&0\\
-\mathbbm{1}_{D}&\phantom{-}\mathbbm{1}_{D}&0\\
\phantom{-}0&\phantom{-}0&0\end{pmatrix}~,\quad\text{the shift vector}\quad V^{\parallel}=\begin{pmatrix}w_{\text{R}}\\
w_{\text{R}}\\
w_{16}\end{pmatrix}~,

(6.7)

is parameterized by integral vectors
w_{\text{R}}\in\mathbbm{Z}^{D}
and
w_{16}\in\mathbbm{Z}^{16}
. The consistency condition (
4.56
) takes the form

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{4}\,\Big(2\,w_{\text{R}}^{2}+w_{16}^{T}\,g_{16}\,w_{16}\Big)=(V^{\parallel})^{T}\widehat{\eta}\,V^{\parallel}\equiv 0~,

(6.8)

since
v_{\text{L}\,\theta}=0
. Since
g_{16}
is even, this is gives a mod–two condition on the
D+16
integers, which is easily solved by requiring that the number of odd integers is even.

The fixed points of this T–fold are determined via the equation (
4.14
)

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\phantom{-}\mathbbm{1}_{D}&-\mathbbm{1}_{D}&0\\
-\mathbbm{1}_{D}&\phantom{-}\mathbbm{1}_{D}&0\\
\phantom{-}0&\phantom{-}0&0\end{pmatrix}Y_{\text{fix}}=2\pi\,\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}\phantom{-}\mathbbm{1}_{D}&-\mathbbm{1}_{D}&0\\
-\mathbbm{1}_{D}&\phantom{-}\mathbbm{1}_{D}&0\\
\phantom{-}0&\phantom{-}0&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}n\\
{\tilde{n}}\\
q\end{pmatrix}~.

(6.9)

On the subspace defined by
{\cal P}^{\perp}
this can be solved as

\displaystyle\dfrac{Y_{\text{fix}}}{2\pi}=\dfrac{Y_{\text{fix}}^{\perp}}{2\pi}=\frac{1}{4}\begin{pmatrix}n-{\tilde{n}}\\
{\tilde{n}}-n\\
0\end{pmatrix}=\frac{1}{4}\begin{pmatrix}m\\
-m\\
0\end{pmatrix}~,

(6.10)

where
m^{1},\ldots,m^{D}=0,1,2,3
labels
4^{D}
distinct points on the doubled torus. However, in light of the residual gauge symmetry not all are physical. The orbifold compatible residual gauge transformations contain the following constant gauge parameters

\displaystyle\Lambda_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbbm{1}_{D}&\mathbbm{1}_{D}&0\\
\mathbbm{1}_{D}&\mathbbm{1}_{D}&0\\
0&0&\mathbbm{1}_{16}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{0}\\
0\\
0\end{pmatrix}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{0}\\
\mu_{0}\\
0\end{pmatrix}~.

(6.11)

(
\Lambda_{\text{fix}}
would be of the same form as (
6.10
), thus for it to be of the form
(\lambda_{0},0,0)
,
m=0
and hence
\Lambda_{\text{fix}}=0
.)
Exploiting the constant gauge transformations the upper
D
components of the fixed point may be set to zero by the gauge
\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\mu_{0}=\mbox{$\frac{1}{4}$}m
. Hence a fully gauge fixed form of the fixed points can be written as

\displaystyle\dfrac{Y^{\text{g.f.}}_{\text{fix}}}{2\pi}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
m\\
0\end{pmatrix}~,

(6.12)

with
m^{1},\ldots,m^{D}=0,1
, leading to
2^{D}
physically distinct fixed points.

6.2
Left–Twisted Full T–Duality Narain Orbifolds

Next, consider another
\mathbbm{Z}_{2}
T–duality Narain orbifold with a very similar twist as the one discussed above

\displaystyle\widehat{\cal R}=\begin{pmatrix}\phantom{-}0&-\mathbbm{1}_{D}&0\\
-\mathbbm{1}_{D}&\phantom{-}0&0\\
\phantom{-}0&\phantom{-}0&\mathbbm{1}_{16}\end{pmatrix}~,

(6.13)

so that the associated block–diagonal twist now reads

\displaystyle R=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbbm{1}_{D}&0&0\\
0&-\mathbbm{1}_{D}&0\\
0&0&\mathbbm{1}_{16}\end{pmatrix}~,\quad\text{hence}\quad v_{\text{R}}=0\quad\text{and}\quad v_{\text{L}}=\begin{pmatrix}\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}^{D},0^{16}\end{pmatrix}~,

(6.14)

In this case the action on the right–movers is trivial. Consequently, there is also no action on the right–moving fermions nor on the target space spinors. Hence, this T–duality Narain Orbifold necessarily preserves all target space supersymmetries.

In this case the parallel and perpendicular projector (
4.6
) is given by

\displaystyle\widehat{\cal P}^{\parallel}=\dfrac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}\phantom{-}\mathbbm{1}_{D}&-\mathbbm{1}_{D}&0\\
-\mathbbm{1}_{D}&\phantom{-}\mathbbm{1}_{D}&0\\
\phantom{-}0&\phantom{-}0&\mathbbm{1}_{16}\end{pmatrix}~,\quad\widehat{\cal P}^{\perp}=\dfrac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbbm{1}_{D}&\mathbbm{1}_{D}&0\\
\mathbbm{1}_{D}&\mathbbm{1}_{D}&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and hence}\quad V^{\parallel}=\begin{pmatrix}\phantom{-}w_{\text{L}}\\
-w_{\text{L}}\\
\phantom{-}w_{16}\end{pmatrix}~,

(6.15)

is parameterized by integral vectors
w_{\text{L}}\in\mathbbm{Z}^{D}
and
w_{16}\in\mathbbm{Z}^{16}
. The consistency condition (
4.56
) takes the form

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{4}\,\Big(-2\,w_{\text{L}}^{2}+w_{16}^{T}\,g_{16}\,w_{16}\Big)=(V^{\parallel})^{T}\widehat{\eta}\,V^{\parallel}\equiv\dfrac{1}{2}\,v_{\text{L}}^{2}~.

(6.16)

Inserting the form (
6.14
) of
v_{\text{L}}
and using that
w_{\text{L}}^{2}
is an integer, this condition may be stated as

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{4}\,\Big(2\,w_{\text{L}}^{2}+w_{16}^{T}\,g_{16}\,w_{16}\Big)\equiv\dfrac{1}{8}\,D~.

(6.17)

Again, the combination between brackets is even. However, in this case this has stronger consequences: In order that this condition can be solved at all, the number of internal torus dimensions
D
has to be dividable by four. Hence only for two choices of the number of internal dimensions, namely
D=4
and
D=8
, a left–twisted T–duality Narain orbifold is possible!

According to (
4.27
), the orbifolded Narain moduli space has dimension

\displaystyle\dim\big[\mathfrak{M}_{\mathbf{P}}\big]=\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}D(D+16)+\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}D(-D+16)=16\,D~.

(6.18)

This suggests that the Wilson lines are not fixed by this T–duality orbifold. Hence, even performing full T–duality twists not necessarily fixes all Narain moduli. The most general invariant matrix
{\cal M}_{\text{inv}}
can be taken to be

\displaystyle{\cal M}_{\text{inv}}=\dfrac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&\phantom{-}\alpha&\gamma\\
\delta&-\delta&0\\
\rho&\phantom{-}\rho&2\tau\end{pmatrix}~,

(6.19)

using (
4.21
). After determining it’s inverse, the invariant
\mathbbm{Z}_{2}
–grading can be expressed as

\displaystyle\widehat{\cal Z}_{\text{inv}}=\begin{pmatrix}-\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\Delta(1-\Delta)^{-1}&\mathbbm{1}_{D}+\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\Delta(1-\Delta)^{-1}&-(1-\Delta)^{-1}\,\alpha^{-1}\gamma\\[8.61108pt]
\mathbbm{1}_{D}+\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\Delta(1-\Delta)^{-1}&-\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\Delta(1-\Delta)^{-1}&(1-\Delta)^{-1}\,\alpha^{-1}\gamma\\[8.61108pt]
\tau^{-1}\rho\,\big[1+\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\Delta(1-\Delta)^{-1}\big]&-\tau^{-1}\rho\,\big[1+\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\Delta(1-\Delta)^{-1}\big]&(\mathbbm{1}_{16}-\Delta^{\prime})^{-1}\end{pmatrix}~,

(6.20)

in terms of the matrices
\Delta=\alpha^{-1}\gamma\,\tau^{-1}\rho
and
\Delta^{\prime}=\tau^{-1}\rho\,\alpha^{-1}\gamma
. (The matrix
\delta
dropped out during the computation.) Comparing with the standard form of the
\mathbbm{Z}_{2}
–grading (
C.6
), the following identifications can be made

\displaystyle\alpha_{16}^{-1}A=\tau^{-1}\rho~,\quad A^{T}\alpha_{16}=(1-\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\Delta)^{-1}\alpha^{-1}\gamma\quad\text{and}\quad G=\mathbbm{1}_{D}-\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\Delta(1-\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\Delta)^{-1}~.

(6.21)

The reason, that there are two expressions for the Wilson lines
A
obtained here, reflects the fact that the procedure outlined in Subsection
4.2
does not automatically enforce that
\widehat{\cal H}
is symmetric. Hence this constraint has to be implemented in addition. Doing so shows that all other identifications are consistent and, in particular,

C=\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\,A^{T}A=\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\Delta(1-\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\Delta)^{-1}
. From this it may be concluded, that the Wilson lines
A
represent the only unfixed Narain moduli, while

\displaystyle G=\mathbbm{1}-\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\,A^{T}A\quad\text{and}\quad B=0~.

Appendix A
Euclidean Pathintegrals

A.1
Wick Rotation to an Euclidean Worldsheet

The coordinate transformation

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}\mapsto\sqrt{2}\,z_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad i\,\sigma_{0}\mapsto\sqrt{2}\,z_{2}

(A.1)

defines a Wick–rotation from the Minkowskian to the Euclidean description. In terms of complex coordinates

\displaystyle z=z_{1}+i\,z_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad{\bar{z}}=z_{1}-i\,z_{2}

(A.2)

this Wick–rotation reads

\displaystyle\sigma\mapsto z\quad\text{and}\quad{\bar{\sigma}}\mapsto{\bar{z}}~.

(A.3)

Consequently, the Minkowskian derivatives can be mapped to derivatives of these real Euclidean coordinates

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\mapsto\dfrac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\partial_{2}=-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\partial-\bar{\partial})\quad\text{and}\quad\partial_{1}\mapsto\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\partial_{1}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,(\partial+\bar{\partial})

(A.4)

The Euclidean action
S_{\text{E}}=-i\,S
is obtained from the Minkowskian action
S
after applying these substitutions.

A.2
Euclidean Worldsheet Torus

At the one-loop level the worldsheet is a torus
T^{2}_{\text{WS}}
defined by the periodicities

\displaystyle z\sim z+1\quad\text{and}\quad z\sim z+\tau

(A.5)

and, consequently, the area of the worldsheet torus equals

\displaystyle\text{Vol}(T^{2}_{\text{WS}})=\int\mathrm{d}^{2}z=2\tau_{2}~.

(A.6)

By expressing this complex coordinate
z
and its conjugate
{\bar{z}}
as

\displaystyle z=x+\tau\,y\quad\text{and}\quad{\bar{z}}=x+{\bar{\tau}}\,y~,

(A.7)

in terms of the real coordinates
(x,y)
, the periodicities can be stated as

\displaystyle x\sim x+1\quad\text{and}\quad y\sim y+1~.

(A.8)

A.3
Mode Functions on an Euclidean Worldsheet Torus

The functions

\displaystyle\phi_{1}(z)=\dfrac{\tau\,{\bar{z}}-{\bar{\tau}}\,z}{\tau-{\bar{\tau}}}~;\quad\phi_{\tau}(z)=\dfrac{{\bar{z}}-z}{\tau-{\bar{\tau}}}~,

(A.9)

are quasi–periodic in single directions on the wordsheet torus

\displaystyle\phi_{1}(z+1)=\phi_{1}(z)+1~,~\phi_{1}(z-\tau)=\phi_{1}(z)~;~~\phi_{\tau}(z+1)=\phi_{\tau}(z)~,~\phi_{\tau}(z-\tau)=\phi_{\tau}(z)+1~.

(A.10)

Note that

\displaystyle(\partial+\bar{\partial})\phi_{1}(z)=1~,\qquad(\partial-\bar{\partial})\phi_{1}(z)=i\,\dfrac{\tau_{1}}{\tau_{2}}~,

(A.11a)

\displaystyle(\partial+\bar{\partial})\phi_{\tau}(z)=0~,\qquad(\partial-\bar{\partial})\phi_{\tau}(z)=i\,\dfrac{1}{\tau_{2}}~.

(A.11b)

Hence, the functions

\displaystyle\phi\bigl[^{r}_{r^{\prime}}\bigr](z)=r\,\phi_{1}(z)+r^{\prime}\,\phi_{\tau}(z)\quad\text{and}\quad\phi\bigl[^{r}_{r^{\prime}}\bigr](x,y)=r\,x-r^{\prime}\,y~,

(A.12)

where
r,r^{\prime}\in\mathbbm{Q}
, possess the following properties on the wordsheet torus

\displaystyle\phi\bigl[^{r}_{r^{\prime}}\bigr](z+1)=\phi\bigl[^{r}_{r^{\prime}}\bigr](z)+r\quad\text{and}\quad\phi\bigl[^{r}_{r^{\prime}}\bigr](z-\tau)=\phi\bigl[^{r}_{r^{\prime}}\bigr](z)+r^{\prime}~,

(A.13)

so that the mode functions

\displaystyle\Phi\bigl[^{r}_{r^{\prime}}\bigr](z)=e^{2\pi i\phi\bigl[^{r}_{r^{\prime}}\bigr](z)}

(A.14)

are quasi–periodic

\displaystyle\Phi\bigl[^{r}_{r^{\prime}}\bigr](z+1)=e^{2\pi i\,r}\,\Phi\bigl[^{r}_{r^{\prime}}\bigr](z)\quad\text{and}\quad\Phi\bigl[^{r}_{r^{\prime}}\bigr](z+\tau)=e^{-2\pi i\,r^{\prime}}\,\Phi\bigl[^{r}_{r^{\prime}}\bigr](z)~.

(A.15)

These mode functions are orthogonal in the sense that

\displaystyle\int\mathrm{d}^{2}z\,\overline{\Phi\bigl[^{r}_{r^{\prime}}\bigr](z)}\,\Phi\bigl[^{s}_{s^{\prime}}\bigr](z)=2\tau_{2}\,\delta^{rs}\,\delta_{r^{\prime}s^{\prime}}~.

(A.16)

(A.35a)

(A.35b)

Appendix B
Modular Functions and Transformations

The complex Teichmüller parameter
\tau
subject to modular transformations generated by

\displaystyle\tau\mapsto\tau+1\quad\text{and}\quad\tau\mapsto\dfrac{-1}{\tau}~,

(B.1)

so that

\displaystyle\tau_{1}\mapsto\tau_{1}~,\quad\tau_{2}\mapsto\tau_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\tau_{1}\mapsto\dfrac{-1}{|\tau|^{2}}\,\tau_{1}~,\quad\tau_{2}\mapsto\dfrac{1}{|\tau|^{2}}\,\tau_{2}~.

(B.2)

The Dedekind function is given by

\displaystyle\eta(\tau)=q^{\frac{1}{24}}\prod_{n\geq 1}\big(1-q^{n}\big)~,\quad\text{with}\quad q=e^{2\pi i\,\tau}~.

(B.3)

The Dedekind function transforms under the generators of the modular transformations as

\displaystyle\eta\big(\tau+1\big)=e^{2\pi i\,\frac{1}{24}}\,\eta\big(\tau\big)\quad\text{and}\quad\eta\Big(\frac{-1}{\tau}\Big)=(-i\tau)^{1/2}\,\eta\big(\tau\big)~.

(B.4)

The Dedekind function may be related to the infinite product

\displaystyle\left.\prod_{r,r^{\prime}}\right.^{\prime}\dfrac{1}{r\,\tau+r^{\prime}}=\dfrac{1}{\eta(\tau)}~.

(B.5)

Another important modular functions are the genus–
d
theta functions

\displaystyle\theta_{d}\bigl[^{\alpha}_{\alpha^{\prime}}\bigr]\big(z\big|\tau\big)=\sum_{n\in\mathbbm{Z}^{d}}q^{\frac{1}{2}\,(n-\alpha)^{2}}\,e^{2\pi i\,(z-\alpha^{\prime})^{T}(n-\alpha)}~,

(B.6)

with the
d
–dimensional vector valued characteristics
\alpha
and
\alpha^{\prime}
and argument

\displaystyle\theta_{d}\bigl[^{\alpha+n}_{\alpha^{\prime}}\bigr]\big(z\big|\tau\big)=\theta_{d}\bigl[^{\alpha}_{\alpha^{\prime}}\bigr]\big(z\big|\tau\big)\quad\text{and}\quad\theta_{d}\bigl[^{\alpha}_{\alpha^{\prime}+n}\bigr]\big(z\big|\tau\big)=e^{2\pi i\,n^{T}\alpha}\,\theta_{d}\bigl[^{\alpha}_{\alpha^{\prime}}\bigr]\big(z\big|\tau\big)~,

(B.7)

for

. Their modular transformations read

\displaystyle\theta_{d}\bigl[^{\alpha}_{\alpha^{\prime}}\bigr]\big(z\big|\tau+1\big)

\displaystyle=e^{-\pi i\,\alpha^{T}(\alpha+e_{d})}\,\theta_{d}\bigl[^{\alpha}_{\alpha^{\prime}+\alpha+\frac{1}{2}\,e}\bigr]\big(z\big|\tau\big)~,

(B.8a)

\displaystyle\theta_{d}\bigl[^{\alpha}_{\alpha^{\prime}}\bigr]\big(\dfrac{z}{\alpha}\big|\frac{-1}{\tau}\big)

\displaystyle=(-i\tau)^{d/2}\,e^{2\pi i\big(\frac{z^{2}}{2\tau}+\alpha^{T}\alpha^{\prime}\big)}\theta_{d}\bigl[^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{-\alpha}\bigr]\big(z\big|\tau\big)~,

(B.8b)

Appendix C
Narain Geometry

C.1
Properties of the T–duality Group

Narain tori and orbifolds concern themselves with lattice vectors
N\in\mathbbm{Z}^{2D+16}
. A general lattice transformation is given by

\displaystyle N\mapsto\widehat{\cal M}\,N~,\quad\widehat{M}\in\text{GL}(2D+16;\mathbbm{Z})~.

(C.1)

In order that the matrix
\widehat{\cal M}
is invertible over the integers, it’s determinant
\det[\widehat{\cal M}]=\pm 1
.

The metric (
3.1
) is defined in terms of the 16-dimensional Cartan metric and vielbein

\displaystyle g_{16}=\alpha_{16}^{T}\,\alpha_{16}~,\quad\text{with}\quad\alpha_{\text{16}}=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{8}&0\\[4.30554pt]
0&\alpha_{8}\end{pmatrix}

(C.2)

of the even, self–dual
\mathrm{E_{8}}\times\mathrm{E_{8}}
–root lattice are introduced in terms of the matrix of eight simple root vectors of
\mathrm{E_{8}}
given by

\displaystyle\alpha_{8}=\begin{pmatrix}~1&0&0&0&0&0&0&{\,\mbox{-}}\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\\
{\,\mbox{-}}1&~1&0&0&0&0&0&{\,\mbox{-}}\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\\
0&{\,\mbox{-}}1&~1&0&0&0&0&{\,\mbox{-}}\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\\
0&0&{\,\mbox{-}}1&~1&0&0&0&{\,\mbox{-}}\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\\
0&0&0&{\,\mbox{-}}1&~1&0&0&{\,\mbox{-}}\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\\
0&0&0&0&{\,\mbox{-}}1&~1&1&{\,\mbox{-}}\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\\
0&0&0&0&0&{\,\mbox{-}}1&1&{\,\mbox{-}}\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\\
0&0&0&0&0&0&0&{\,\mbox{-}}\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\end{pmatrix}~.

(C.3)

Note that
\det[\alpha_{8}]=-1
, hence
\det[g_{16}]=\det[\alpha_{16}]=1
and
\det[\widehat{\eta}]=(-1)^{D}
. Consequently, all the matrices
\alpha_{8},\alpha_{16},g_{16}
and
\widehat{\eta}
are invertible over the integers.

The subgroup
\text{O}_{\widehat{\eta}}(D,D+16;\mathbbm{R})\subset\text{GL}(2D+16;\mathbbm{R})
is defined by requiring that its matrices leave the metric
\widehat{\eta}
invariant, i.e.
\widehat{\cal M}\in\text{O}_{\widehat{\eta}}(D,D+16;\mathbbm{R})
when

\displaystyle\widehat{\cal M}^{T}\widehat{\eta}\,\widehat{\cal M}=\widehat{\eta}

(C.4)

for
\widehat{\cal M}\in\text{GL}(2D+16;\mathbbm{R})
. The integral group
\text{O}_{\widehat{\eta}}(D,D+16;\mathbbm{Z})
is defined in the same fashion, but then
\widehat{\cal M}\in\text{GL}(2D+16;\mathbbm{Z})
. This group is often referred to as the T–duality group. When
\widehat{\cal M}\in\text{GL}(2D+16;\mathbbm{Z})
but not in
\text{O}_{\widehat{\eta}}(D,D+16;\mathbbm{Z})
the metric
\widehat{\eta}
takes a different form.

C.2
Parameterizing Narain Moduli

Generalized Narain Metric and
\mathbbm{Z}_{2}
–Grading

The generalized metric
\widehat{\cal H}
, defined in (
3.2

D
–dimensional metric
G=G^{T}\in\text{GL}(D;\mathbbm{R})
, an anti–symmetric tensor
B=-B^{T}\in\text{M}_{D\times D}(\mathbbm{R})
and a Wilson line matrix

. The latter can be thought of as
D
Wilson lines
A_{i}
on the
D

A
. The generalized metric transforms covariantly as

\displaystyle\widehat{\cal H}\mapsto\widehat{\cal M}^{T}\widehat{\cal H}\,\widehat{\cal M}

(C.5)

under
\widehat{\cal M}\in\text{O}_{\widehat{\eta}}(D,D+16;\mathbbm{R})

–grading

\displaystyle\widehat{\cal Z}=\widehat{\eta}^{-1}\widehat{\cal H}=\begin{pmatrix}-G^{-1}C^{T}&G^{-1}&-G^{-1}A^{T}\alpha_{\text{16}}\\[4.30554pt]
G+A^{T}A+CG^{-1}C^{T}&-CG^{-1}&(\mathbbm{1}_{D}+CG^{-1})A^{T}\alpha_{\text{16}}\\[4.30554pt]
\alpha_{\text{16}}^{-1}A(\mathbbm{1}_{D}+G^{-1}C^{T})&-\alpha_{\text{16}}^{-1}AG^{-1}&\alpha_{\text{16}}^{-1}\big(\mathbbm{1}_{16}+AG^{-1}A^{T}\big)\alpha_{\text{16}}\end{pmatrix}~.

(C.6)

Generalized Narain Vielbein

A generalized vielbein

\displaystyle\widehat{\cal E}=\begin{pmatrix}e&0&0\\[4.30554pt]
-e^{-T}C^{T}&e^{-T}&-e^{-T}A^{T}\alpha_{16}\\[4.30554pt]
\alpha_{16}^{-1}A&0&\mathbbm{1}_{16}\end{pmatrix}

(C.7)

\text{O}_{\widehat{\eta}}(D,D+16;\mathbbm{R})
, since

\displaystyle\widehat{\cal E}^{T}\,\widehat{\eta}\,\widehat{\cal E}=\widehat{\eta}~.

(C.8)

In particular, it’s inverse reads:

\displaystyle\widehat{\cal E}^{-1}=\widehat{\eta}^{-1}\widehat{\cal E}^{T}\,\widehat{\eta}=\begin{pmatrix}e^{-1}&0&0\\[4.30554pt]
-Ce^{-1}&e^{T}&A^{T}\alpha_{16}\\[4.30554pt]
-\alpha_{16}^{-1}Ae^{-1}&0&\mathbbm{1}_{16}\end{pmatrix}~.

(C.9)

The matrix
{\cal K}

\widehat{\eta}
to the
(D,D+16)
–dimensional Minkowski metric

(C.10)

\displaystyle{\cal E}^{T}\eta\,{\cal E}=\widehat{\eta}~,\quad\text{where}\quad{\cal E}={\cal K}\,\widehat{\cal E}~.

(C.11)

The generalized vielbein can be thought of as the square root of the generalized metric in the sense that

\displaystyle\widehat{\cal H}={\cal E}^{T}\,{\cal E}=\widehat{\cal E}^{T}\hat{\mathbbm{1}}\,\widehat{\cal E}\quad\text{where}\quad\hat{\mathbbm{1}}={\cal K}^{T}{\cal K}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbbm{1}_{D}&0&0\\
0&\mathbbm{1}_{D}&0\\
0&0&g_{16}\end{pmatrix}~.

(C.12)

C.3
Generalized Metric Independence

Consider two invertible
(2D+16)\times(2D+16)
–matrices
{\cal A}
and
{\cal B}
that mutually commute and commute with the
\mathbbm{Z}_{2}
–grading
\widehat{\cal Z}=\widehat{\eta}^{-1}\widehat{\cal H}
. They appear in the determinant

\displaystyle\det\big[\widehat{\eta}\,{\cal A}+\widehat{\cal H}\,{\cal B}\big]=\det\big[\widehat{\eta}\big(\mathbbm{1}+\widehat{\eta}^{-1}\widehat{\cal H}\,{\cal B}{\cal A}^{-1}\big)A\big]

(C.18)

which often arises in Gaussian pathintegrals over the Narain coordinate fields
Y
. This can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\det\big[\widehat{\eta}\,{\cal A}+\widehat{\cal H}\,{\cal B}\big]=\det\widehat{\eta}\,\det{\cal A}\,\det\big[\mathbbm{1}+{\cal C}\,\widehat{\cal Z}\big]~,

(C.19)

where
{\cal C}={\cal B}{\cal A}^{-1}
commutes with
\widehat{\cal Z}=\widehat{\eta}^{-1}\widehat{\cal H}
. By taking the log of the final determinant factor and using the Taylor expansion of the ln–function this expression takes the form

\displaystyle\ln\det\big[\mathbbm{1}+{\cal C}\widehat{\cal Z}\big]=\text{tr}\ln\big[\mathbbm{1}+{\cal C}\widehat{\cal Z}\big]=\text{tr}\Big[\sum_{m\geq 1}\dfrac{-1}{2m}\,{\cal C}^{2m}+\sum_{m\geq 0}\dfrac{1}{2m+1}\,{\cal C}^{2m+1}\,\widehat{\cal Z}\Big]

(C.20)

since
\widehat{\cal Z}
squares to unity. Using that the left– and right–moving projectors (
3.7
) satisfy

\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}=\widehat{\cal P}_{\text{L}}+\widehat{\cal P}_{\text{R}}\quad\text{and}\quad\widehat{\cal Z}=\widehat{\cal P}_{\text{L}}-\widehat{\cal P}_{\text{R}}~,

(C.21)

this can be split in left– and right–moving contributions

\displaystyle\ln\det\big[\mathbbm{1}+{\cal C}\widehat{\cal Z}\big]=\text{tr}_{\text{L}}\big[\ln\big(\mathbbm{1}+{\cal C})\big]+\text{tr}_{\text{R}}\big[\ln\big(\mathbbm{1}+{\cal C})\big]~,

(C.22)

where the projected traces
\text{tr}_{\text{L/R}}[{\cal X}]=\text{tr}\big[{\cal X}\widehat{\cal P}_{\text{L/R}}\big]
were introduced. Hence, the initial expression (
C.19
) can be written as

\displaystyle\det\big[\widehat{\eta}\,{\cal A}+\widehat{\cal H}\,{\cal B}\big]=\det\widehat{\eta}\,\det{}_{\text{L}}[{\cal A}+{\cal B}]\,\det{}_{\text{R}}[{\cal A}-{\cal B}]~,

(C.23)

in terms of determinants
\det{}_{\text{L/R}}
over the
D+16
– and
D
–dimensional subspaces defined by the projections
\widehat{\cal P}_{\text{L/R}}
. This result shows that this determinant is independent of the (fixed or free) moduli contained in the generalized metric
\widehat{\cal H}
. These determinants may be evaluated in the lattice basis but also in the basis where the orbifold twists are (block) diagonal, e.g. (
4.20
).

Taking

\displaystyle{\cal A}=\tau_{1}\,\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\quad\text{and}\quad{\cal B}=i\tau_{2}\,\mathbf{a}~,

(C.24)

for two commuting matrix
\mathbf{a}
and
\mathbf{a}^{\prime}
, this leads to the identity

\displaystyle\det\big[\widehat{\eta}\,(\mathbf{a}\,\tau_{1}+\mathbf{a}^{\prime})+\widehat{\cal H}\,i\tau_{2}\,\mathbf{a}\big]=\det\widehat{\eta}\,\det{}_{\text{L}}[\mathbf{a}\,\tau+\mathbf{a}^{\prime}]\,\det{}_{\text{R}}[\mathbf{a}\,{\bar{\tau}}+\mathbf{a}^{\prime}]~.

(C.25)

Power variations and limit theorems for stochastic processes controlled by fractional Brownian motions
Yanghui Liu, Xiaohua Wang

Abstract
. In this paper we establish limit theorems for power variations of stochastic processes controlled by fractional Brownian motions with Hurst parameter H ≤ 1 / 2 H\leq 1/2 . We show that the power variations of such processes can be decomposed into the mix of several weighted random sums plus some remainder terms, and the convergences of power variations are dominated by different combinations of those weighted sums depending on whether H < 1 / 4 H<1/4 , H = 1 / 4 H=1/4 , or H > 1 / 4 H>1/4 . We show that when H ≥ 1 / 4 H\geq 1/4 the centered power variation converges stably at the rates n − 1 / 2 n^{-1/2} , and when H < 1 / 4 H<1/4 it converges in probability at the rate n − 2 ​ H n^{-2H} . We determine the limit of the mixed weighted sum based on a rough path approach developed in [ 32 ] .

Introduction

In this paper we establish limit theorems for power variations of low-regularity processes in a general rough path framework.

,

When

Recently, empirical evidence was found that security volatility actually has much lower regularity than semimartingales (see

A main difficulty in the low-regularity case is that the aforementioned relation between

and its first-order approximation is no longer true. In fact, we will see that the difference between the power variations of a low-regularity process

In this paper we show that a limit theorem of power variation does hold under the assumption that

The controlled process is a main concept in the theory of rough path, and it is broad enough to contain two important models of stochastic processes we have in mind: the rough integrals and the rough differential equations (see Example

2.3.
Chaos expansion and fractional Brownian motions

Let

be the standard Gaussian measure on the real line, and let

be such that

. It is well-known that the function

can be expanded into a series of Hermite polynomials as follows:

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{q=d}^{\infty}a_{q}H_{q}(x),

If

Let

be a standard fractional Brownian motion (fBm for short) with Hurst parameter

, that is

is a continuous Gaussian process such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[x_{s}x_{t}]=\frac{1}{2}(|s|^{2H}+|t|^{2H}-|s-t|^{2H}).

The fBm

is almost surely

-Hölder continuous for all
{\gamma}<H
. Define the covariance function

\displaystyle\rho(k)=\mathbb{E}(\delta x_{01}\delta x_{k,k+1}).

Then,

, we have

.
Let

be the completion of the space of indicator functions with respect to the inner product
\langle\mathbf{1}_{[u,v]},\mathbf{1}_{[s,t]}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}=\mathbb{E}(\delta x_{uv}\delta x_{st})

The following result shows that given two sequences of stable convergence and convergence in probability, respectively, their joint sequence is also of stable convergence. Recall that
X_{n}
is called convergent to

Lemma 2.6
.

Let

Proof.

Since

such that the following estimates hold:

(3.3)

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{J}}_{s}^{t}({r}^{(0)},h)|_{L_{1}}\leq K(t-s)^{{\beta}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad|{\mathcal{J}}_{s}^{t}(y,h)|_{L_{1}}\leq K(t-s)^{{\beta}_{0}}

for
(s,t)\in{\mathcal{S}}_{2}([0,1])
such that
t-s\geq 1/n
.

Proof.

Denote
R_{st}:={\mathcal{J}}_{s}^{t}(r^{(0)},h)
for
(s,t)\in{\mathcal{S}}_{2}([0,1])
. Recall that the operator
\delta
for 2-increment is defined in (
2.1
). So, for
(s,u,t)\in{\mathcal{S}}_{3}([0,1])
, we have

(3.4)

\displaystyle\delta R_{sut}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}}_{s}^{t}({r}^{(0)},h)-{\mathcal{J}}_{s}^{u}(r^{(0)},h)-{\mathcal{J}}_{u}^{t}(r^{(0)},h)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{u\leq t_{k}<t}(r^{(0)}_{st_{k}}-r^{(0)}_{ut_{k}})h_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}.

Note that by definition of
\delta r^{(0)}
we have the relation
r^{(0)}_{st_{k}}-r^{(0)}_{ut_{k}}=\delta r^{(0)}_{sut_{k}}+r^{(0)}_{su}
. Substituting this into (
3.4
) and then invoking Lemma
2.5

we obtain

(3.5)

\displaystyle\delta R_{sut}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r^{(0)}_{su}{\mathcal{J}}_{u}^{t}(1,h)+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell-1}r^{(i)}_{su}{\mathcal{J}}_{u}^{t}(x^{i},h).

We can now bound
\delta R
as follows: Taking the
L_{1}
-norm on both sides of (
3.5
) gives

(3.6)

\displaystyle|\delta R_{sut}|_{L_{1}}\leq\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}|r^{(i)}_{su}|_{L_{2}}\cdot|{\mathcal{J}}_{u}^{t}(x^{i},h)|_{L_{2}}.

Applying condition (
3.2
) to
|{\mathcal{J}}_{u}^{t}(x^{i},h)|_{L_{2}}
in (
3.6
) and invoking the relation
|r^{(i)}_{st}|_{L_{p}}\leq K(t-s)^{(\ell-i){\alpha}}
given in Definition
2.1
we get

(3.7)

\displaystyle|\delta R_{sut}|_{L_{1}}\lesssim\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}(u-s)^{(\ell-i){\alpha}}(t-u)^{{\beta}_{i}}\lesssim(t-s)^{{\beta}}

for
(s,u,t)\in{\mathcal{S}}_{3}([0,1])
such that
t-u\geq 1/n
,
where
{\beta}
is defined in (
3.1
).

Take
(s,t)\in{\mathcal{S}}_{2}([0,1])
such that
t-s\geq 1/n
.
Consider the partition
\llbracket s,t\rrbracket
of the interval
[s,t]
:
s<t_{k}<\cdots<t_{k^{\prime}}<t
, where
k
and
k^{\prime}
are such that
t_{k-1}\leq s<t_{k}
and
t_{k^{\prime}}<t\leq t_{k^{\prime}+1}
.
In the following we show that (
3.7
) holds for all
(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})\in{\mathcal{S}}_{3}(\llbracket s,t\rrbracket)
. In view of (
3.7
) it remains to show that the estimate (
3.7
) holds for
|\delta R_{ut_{k^{\prime}}t}|_{L_{1}}
,
u\in\llbracket s,t\rrbracket:u\leq t_{k^{\prime}}
. Indeed,
by definition (
2.4
) we have
{\mathcal{J}}_{t_{k^{\prime}}}^{t}(x^{i},h)=0
and

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{J}}_{t_{k^{\prime}}}^{t}(1,h)|_{L_{2}}=|h_{t_{k^{\prime}}t_{k^{\prime}+1}}|_{L_{2}}\leq(1/n)^{{\beta}_{0}}\leq(t-s)^{{\beta}_{0}}.

Applying these estimates to the right-hand side of (
3.6
) we obtain
the estimate (
3.7
) for
|\delta R_{ut_{k^{\prime}}t}|_{L_{1}}
.

By (
2.4
) it is clear that for any two consecutive partition points
u,v
in
\llbracket s,t\rrbracket
and
u<v
we have
R_{uv}=0
.
Applying the discrete sewing lemma
[
32
, Lemma 2.5]
to
R
on the partition
\llbracket s,t\rrbracket
and then invoking the estimate (
3.7
) of
\delta R
on
{\mathcal{S}}_{3}(\llbracket s,t\rrbracket)
we obtain

\displaystyle|R_{st}|_{L_{1}}\lesssim(t-s)^{{\beta}}.

This proves the first estimate in (
3.3
).

Note that by substituting the expression (
2.3
) into
{\mathcal{J}}_{s}^{t}(y,h)
we get the relation

(3.8)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}}_{s}^{t}(y,h)=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}y^{(i)}_{s}{\mathcal{J}}_{s}^{t}(x^{i},h)+{\mathcal{J}}_{s}^{t}(r^{(0)},h).

Applying (
3.2
) and the first estimate in (
3.3
) to the right-hand side of (
3.8
) we obtain the desired estimate of
{\mathcal{J}}_{s}^{t}(y,h)
in (
3.3
).
∎

In the next result we apply Proposition
3.1
to weighted sums which involve fBms.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
x
be a one-dimensional fBm with Hurst parameter
H\leq 1/2
.
Suppose that
(y,y^{\prime},\dots,y^{(\ell-1)})
,
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
is a process controlled by
(x,\ell,H-\varepsilon)
in
L_{2}
for some sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
.
Let
f=\sum_{q=d}^{\infty}a_{q}H_{q}\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R},{\gamma})
with Hermite rank
d>0
and
f
belongs to the Soblev space
W^{2(\ell-1),2}(\mathbb{R},{\gamma})
, where
{\gamma}
denotes the standard Gaussian measure on the real line; see e.g. Page 28 in
[
39
]
.
We define a family of increments
\{h^{n};n\geq 1\}
by:

(3.9)

\displaystyle h^{n}_{st}:=\sum_{s\leq t_{k}<t}f(n^{H}\delta x_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}),\quad\quad(s,t)\in{\mathcal{S}}_{2}(\llbracket 0,1\rrbracket).

(i) Suppose that
d>\frac{1}{2H}
and that

\ell
is the least integer such that
\ell H+\frac{1}{2}>1
, that is
\ell=[\frac{1}{2H}]+1
.
Then there is a constant
K
independent of
n
such that

(3.10)

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{J}}_{s}^{t}(y,h^{n})|_{L_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Kn^{1/2}(t-s)^{1/2}

for all
(s,t)\in{\mathcal{S}}_{2}([0,1])
satisfying
t-s\geq 1/n
.

(ii) Suppose that
d\leq\frac{1}{2H}
and that

\ell=d+1
.
Then there is a constant
K
independent of
n
such that

(3.11)

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{J}}_{s}^{t}(y,h^{n})|_{L_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Kn^{1-dH}(t-s)^{1-dH}

for all
(s,t)\in{\mathcal{S}}_{2}([0,1])
satisfying
t-s\geq 1/n
.

Proof.

We assume that
d>\frac{1}{2H}
. In the following we prove (i) by applying Proposition
3.1
. We first recall the estimate in
[
32
, equation (4.24)]
:

(3.12)

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{J}}_{s}^{t}(x^{i},h^{n})|_{L_{2}}\leq Kn^{1/2}(t-s)^{iH+1/2},\qquad\text{ $(s,t)\in{\mathcal{S}}_{2}([0,1]):t-s\geq 1/n$, }

for all
i=0,\dots,[\frac{1}{2H}]
. The estimate (
3.12
) implies that
relation (
3.2
) holds for
h:=h^{n}/\sqrt{n}
and
{\beta}_{i}:=iH+1/2
.
Take
{\alpha}=H-\varepsilon
and recall that
\ell
is the least integer such that
\ell H+1/2>1
, or

\ell H>1/2
. It is thus readily checked that
condition (
3.1
) is satisfied. We conclude that (
3.10
) holds.

We turn to the case when
d\leq\frac{1}{2H}
. As before, our estimate will be an application of Proposition
3.1
. We first derive an estimate of
|{\mathcal{J}}_{s}^{t}(x^{i},h^{n})|_{L_{2}}
. For this purpose we consider the following decomposition

(3.13)

\displaystyle h^{n}_{st}=h^{n,(1)}_{st}+h^{n,(2)}_{st},

where

\displaystyle h^{n,(1)}_{st}=\sum_{s\leq t_{k}<t}f_{1}(n^{H}\delta x_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}),\qquad h^{n,(2)}_{st}=\sum_{s\leq t_{k}<t}f_{2}(n^{H}\delta x_{t_{k}t_{k+1}})

and

\displaystyle f_{1}(x)=\sum_{q=[\frac{1}{2H}]+1}^{\infty}a_{q}H_{q}(x),\qquad\qquad f_{2}(x)=\sum_{q=d}^{[\frac{1}{2H}]}a_{q}H_{q}(x).

Note that the Hermite rank of
f_{1}
is greater than
\frac{1}{2H}
. So we can apply (
3.12
) to get the estimate

(3.14)

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{J}}_{s}^{t}(x^{i},h^{n,(1)})|_{L_{2}}\leq Kn^{1/2}(t-s)^{iH+1/2}

for
(s,t)\in{\mathcal{S}}_{2}([0,1]):t-s\geq 1/n
and
i=0,\dots,d
.
By assumption we have
1/2-dH>0
. It follows that

(3.15)

\displaystyle 1\leq n^{1/2-dH}(t-s)^{1/2-dH},

and therefore we can enlarge the bound in (
3.14
) to be:

(3.16)

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{J}}_{s}^{t}(x^{i},h^{n,(1)})|_{L_{2}}\leq Kn^{1-dH}(t-s)^{1+iH-dH}.

Let us turn to the estimate of
|{\mathcal{J}}_{s}^{t}(x^{i},h^{n,(2)})|_{L_{2}}
. We first have the bound

(3.17)

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{J}}_{s}^{t}(x^{i},h^{n,2})|_{L_{2}}\leq\sum_{q=d}^{[\frac{1}{2H}]}|a_{q}|\cdot|{\mathcal{J}}_{s}^{t}(x^{i},h^{n,q})|_{L_{2}},\qquad(s,t)\in{\mathcal{S}}_{2}([0,1]).

Recall the estimate in
[
32
, Lemma 4.11 (ii)]
:

(3.18)

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{J}}_{s}^{t}(x^{i},h^{n,q})|_{L_{2}}\lesssim\begin{cases}n^{1-qH}(t-s)^{1+iH-qH}&\text{when }q\leq i\\
n^{1/2}(t-s)^{iH+1/2}&\text{when }q>i\end{cases}

for
(s,t)\in{\mathcal{S}}_{2}([0,1]):t-s\geq 1/n
,
i=0,\dots,d
and
q<\frac{1}{2H}
.
Substituting (
3.18
) into the right-hand side of (
3.17
) and then
applying the relation
1\leq n(t-s)
we obtain that

(3.19)

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{J}}_{s}^{t}(x^{i},h^{n,2})|_{L_{2}}\leq Kn^{1-dH}(t-s)^{1+iH-dH},\qquad(s,t)\in{\mathcal{S}}_{2}([0,1])

for
i=0,\dots,d
.

Combining the two estimates (
3.16
) and (
3.19
) and taking into account the decomposition (
3.13
), we obtain that (
3.2
) holds for
{\beta}_{i}:=1+iH-dH
and
h:=h^{n}/n^{1-dH}
. It is readily checked that condition (
3.1
) is satisfied for
\ell=d+1
. Applying Proposition
3.1

we thus conclude the desired estimate (
3.11
).
∎

3.2.
Convergence of Riemann sum

Let
y
be a continuous process controlled by the fBm
x
. This subsection is devoted to the convergence of Riemann sum for the regular integral
\int_{0}^{t}y_{u}du
. For convenience we will consider the uniform partition of
[0,1]
:
t_{i}=i/n
,
i=0,1,\dots,n.

We start by proving the following weighted limit theorem result:

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
x
be a one-dimensional fBm with Hurst parameter
H<1/2
. Let
(y,y^{\prime})
be a rough path controlled by
(x,2,H)
almost surely. Define the increment

(3.20)

\displaystyle h^{n}_{st}=\sum_{s\leq t_{k}<t}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}x^{1}_{t_{k}u}du\qquad\text{for }(s,t)\in{\mathcal{S}}_{2}(\llbracket 0,1\rrbracket).

Then for each
(s,t)\in{\mathcal{S}}_{2}([0,1])

we have the convergence in probability:

(3.21)

\displaystyle n^{2H}{\mathcal{J}}_{s}^{t}(y,h^{n})\to-\frac{1}{4H+2}\int_{s}^{t}y^{\prime}_{u}du\qquad\text{as $n\to\infty$}.

Proof.

The proof is divided into several steps. By localization (cf.
[
26
, Lemma 3.4.5]
) we can and will assume that
(y,y^{\prime})
is controlled by
(x,2,H-\varepsilon)
in
L_{2}
for any
\varepsilon>0
.

Step 1: Estimate of
h^{n}
.

By the self-similarity of the fBm we have
\mathbb{E}[x^{1}_{t_{k}u}x^{1}_{t_{k^{\prime}}u^{\prime}}]=n^{-2H}\mathbb{E}[x^{1}_{{k},nu}x^{1}_{{k^{\prime}},nu^{\prime}}]
.
Applying this relation and then the change of variable
nu^{\prime}\to u^{\prime}
and
nu\to u
we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|h^{n}_{st}|^{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n^{-2H-2}\sum_{ns\leq k,k^{\prime}<nt}\int_{k}^{k+1}\int_{{k^{\prime}}}^{{k^{\prime}+1}}\mathbb{E}[x^{1}_{{k}u}x^{1}_{{k^{\prime}}u^{\prime}}]du^{\prime}du,\qquad(s,t)\in{\mathcal{S}}_{2}(\llbracket 0,1\rrbracket).

Applying the estimate
|\mathbb{E}[x^{1}_{{k}u}x^{1}_{{k^{\prime}}u^{\prime}}]|\lesssim|k-k^{\prime}|^{2H-2}
for
k\neq k^{\prime}
we obtain

(3.22)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|h^{n}_{st}|^{2}]\lesssim n^{-2H-2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}ns\leq k,k^{\prime}<nt\\
k\neq k^{\prime}\end{subarray}}|k-k^{\prime}|^{2H-2}\lesssim n^{-2H-1}(t-s),\qquad(s,t)\in{\mathcal{S}}_{2}(\llbracket 0,1\rrbracket).

Step 2: A decomposition of
|{\mathcal{J}}_{s}^{t}(x^{1},h^{n})|_{L_{2}}^{2}
.

Let
(s,t)\in{\mathcal{S}}_{2}([0,1])
such that
t-s>1/n
.
By definition (
2.5
) we can express
|{\mathcal{J}}_{s}^{t}(x^{1},h^{n})|_{L_{2}}^{2}
as

(3.23)

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{J}}_{s}^{t}(x^{1},h^{n})|_{L_{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s\leq t_{k},t_{k^{\prime}}<t}\mathbb{E}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\int_{t_{k^{\prime}}}^{t_{k^{\prime}+1}}x^{1}_{st_{k}}x^{1}_{st_{k^{\prime}}}x^{1}_{t_{k}u}x^{1}_{t_{k^{\prime}}u^{\prime}}du^{\prime}du.

Applying the integration by part to the integrand in (
3.23
) we obtain

(3.24)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(x^{1}_{st_{k}}x^{1}_{st_{k^{\prime}}}x^{1}_{t_{k}u}x^{1}_{t_{k^{\prime}}u^{\prime}}\right)=A_{1}+A_{2},

where

(3.25)

\displaystyle A_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(x^{1}_{st_{k}}x^{1}_{st_{k^{\prime}}}\right)\langle\mathbf{1}_{[t_{k},u]},\mathbf{1}_{[t_{k^{\prime}},u^{\prime}]}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}

(3.26)

\displaystyle A_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle D^{2}(x^{1}_{st_{k}}x^{1}_{st_{k^{\prime}}}),\mathbf{1}_{[t_{k},u]}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{[t_{k^{\prime}},u^{\prime}]}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}^{\otimes 2}}.

Step 3: Estimate of
A_{1}
.

It is clear that
\Big|\mathbb{E}\left(x^{1}_{st_{k}}x^{1}_{st_{k^{\prime}}}\right)\Big|\leq(t-s)^{2H}
. On the other hand, similar to the estimate of
\mathbb{E}[\delta x_{t_{k}u}\delta x_{t_{k^{\prime}}u^{\prime}}]
in Step 1 we have
|\langle\mathbf{1}_{[t_{k},u]},\mathbf{1}_{[t_{k^{\prime}},u^{\prime}]}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}|\lesssim n^{-2H}|k-k^{\prime}|^{2H-2}
. Substituting these two estimates into (
3.25
) we obtain

\displaystyle|A_{1}|\lesssim(t-s)^{2H}|k-k^{\prime}|^{2H-2}n^{-2H}.

The above estimate for
|A_{1}|
together with the relation

\displaystyle\sum_{s\leq t_{k},t_{k^{\prime}}<t}|k-k^{\prime}|^{2H-2}\lesssim n(t-s)

shows that

(3.27)

\displaystyle\sum_{s\leq t_{k},t_{k^{\prime}}<t}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\int_{t_{k^{\prime}}}^{t_{k^{\prime}+1}}A_{1}du^{\prime}du\lesssim(t-s)^{2H+1}n^{-1-2H}.

Step 4: Estimate of
A_{2}
.

Recall that
A_{2}
is defined in (
3.26
).
We first note that

(3.28)

\displaystyle D^{2}(x^{1}_{st_{k}}x^{1}_{st_{k^{\prime}}})=\mathbf{1}_{[s,t_{k}]}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{[s,t_{k^{\prime}}]}+\mathbf{1}_{[s,t_{k^{\prime}}]}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{[s,t_{k}]}.

Substituting relation (
3.28
) into (
3.26
) we obtain the decomposition

(3.29)

\displaystyle\sum_{s\leq t_{k},t_{k^{\prime}}<t}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\int_{t_{k^{\prime}}}^{t_{k^{\prime}+1}}A_{2}du^{\prime}du=A_{21}+A_{22}\,,

where

\displaystyle A_{21}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s\leq t_{k},t_{k^{\prime}}<t}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\int_{t_{k^{\prime}}}^{t_{k^{\prime}+1}}\langle\mathbf{1}_{[s,t_{k^{\prime}}]}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{[s,t_{k}]},\mathbf{1}_{[t_{k},u]}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{[t_{k^{\prime}},u^{\prime}]}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}^{\otimes 2}}du^{\prime}du

(3.30)

\displaystyle A_{22}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s\leq t_{k},t_{k^{\prime}}<t}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\int_{t_{k^{\prime}}}^{t_{k^{\prime}+1}}\langle\mathbf{1}_{[s,t_{k}]}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{[s,t_{k^{\prime}}]},\mathbf{1}_{[t_{k},u]}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{[t_{k^{\prime}},u^{\prime}]}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}^{\otimes 2}}du^{\prime}du.

In the following we bound
A_{21}
and
A_{22}
, which together will give us the estimate of
A_{2}
. We first have

\displaystyle|A_{21}|\leq 2\sum_{s\leq t_{k}\leq t_{k^{\prime}}<t}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\int_{t_{k^{\prime}}}^{t_{k^{\prime}+1}}|\langle\mathbf{1}_{[s,t_{k^{\prime}}]},\mathbf{1}_{[t_{k},u]}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}|\cdot|\langle\mathbf{1}_{[s,t_{k}]},\mathbf{1}_{[t_{k^{\prime}},u^{\prime}]}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}|du^{\prime}du.

Invoking the elementary estimates

\displaystyle|\langle\mathbf{1}_{[s,t_{k^{\prime}}]},\mathbf{1}_{[t_{k},u]}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}|\leq n^{-2H}\qquad\text{and}\qquad|\langle\mathbf{1}_{[s,t_{k}]},\mathbf{1}_{[t_{k^{\prime}},u^{\prime}]}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}|\lesssim n^{-2H}|k-k^{\prime}|^{2H-1}

for
t_{k}\leq t_{k^{\prime}}
we obtain

\displaystyle|A_{21}|\lesssim\sum_{s\leq t_{k}\leq t_{k^{\prime}}<t}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\int_{t_{k^{\prime}}}^{t_{k^{\prime}+1}}n^{-2H}\cdot n^{-2H}\cdot(k^{\prime}-k)^{2H-1}du^{\prime}du

(3.31)

\displaystyle\lesssim(t-s)^{2H+1}n^{-2H-1}.

We turn to the estimate of
A_{22}
. A change of variables in (
3.30
) gives

(3.32)

\displaystyle A_{22}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n^{-4H-2}\sum_{ns\leq{k},{k^{\prime}}<nt}A_{22,kk^{\prime}},

where

(3.33)

\displaystyle A_{22,kk^{\prime}}=\int_{{k}}^{{k+1}}\int_{{k^{\prime}}}^{{k^{\prime}+1}}\langle\mathbf{1}_{[ns,{k}]}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{[ns,{k^{\prime}}]},\mathbf{1}_{[{k},u]}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{[{k^{\prime}},u^{\prime}]}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}^{\otimes 2}}du^{\prime}du.

It is clear that
|A_{22,kk^{\prime}}|\sim O(1)
. Therefore, from (
3.32
) we obtain the estimate

(3.34)

\displaystyle|A_{22}|\lesssim(t-s)^{2}n^{-4H}.

Step 5: Estimate of
{\mathcal{J}}_{s}^{t}(x^{1},h^{n})
.

Putting together the estimates (
3.27
), (
3.31
), and (
3.34
) and taking into account the decompositions (
3.23
)-(
3.24
) and (
3.29
) we obtain the estimate

(3.35)

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{J}}_{s}^{t}(x^{1},h^{n})|_{L_{2}}\lesssim(t-s)n^{-2H}

for
(s,t)\in{\mathcal{S}}_{2}([0,1])
such that
t-s>1/n
.

Step 6: Convergence of the second moment of
{\mathcal{J}}_{s}^{t}(x^{1},h^{n})
.
Let
(s,t)\in{\mathcal{S}}_{2}([0,1])
. In this step we show the convergence:

(3.36)

\displaystyle n^{2H}|{\mathcal{J}}_{s}^{t}(x^{1},h^{n})|_{L_{2}}\to\frac{1}{4H+2}(t-s)\qquad\text{as $n\to\infty$.}

Recall our decomposition of
|{\mathcal{J}}_{s}^{t}(x^{1},h^{n})|_{L_{2}}^{2}
in (
3.23
)-(
3.24
) and of
A_{2}
in (
3.29
). So the estimates in (
3.27
), (
3.31
) and (
3.34
) together shows that the convergence of
|{\mathcal{J}}_{s}^{t}(x^{1},h^{n})|_{L_{2}}^{2}
is dominated by that of
A_{22}
. Namely, we have

(3.37)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{4H}|{\mathcal{J}}_{s}^{t}(x^{1},h^{n})|_{L_{2}}^{2}=\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{4H}A_{22}.

In the following we focus on the computation of
\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{4H}A_{22}
.

Recall the expression of
A_{22}
in (
3.32
)-(
3.33
).
We first note that since
|A_{22,kk^{\prime}}|\sim O(1)
we can replace the summation
\sum_{ns\leq{k},{k^{\prime}}<nt}
in (
3.32
) by
\sum_{ns+n^{\varepsilon}\leq{k},{k^{\prime}}<nt}
for
0<\varepsilon<1
without changing the limit of
A_{22}
. Next, by stationary increment and self-similarity of the fBm we have

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{1}_{[ns,{k}]}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{[ns,{k^{\prime}}]},\mathbf{1}_{[{k},u]}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{[{k^{\prime}},u^{\prime}]}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}^{\otimes 2}}=\langle\mathbf{1}_{[ns-k,0]}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{[ns-k^{\prime},0]},\mathbf{1}_{[0,u-k]}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{[0,u^{\prime}-k^{\prime}]}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}^{\otimes 2}}

\displaystyle=\langle\mathbf{1}_{[\frac{ns-k}{u-k},0]}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{[\frac{ns-k^{\prime}}{u^{\prime}-k^{\prime}},0]},\mathbf{1}_{[0,1]}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{[0,1]}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}^{\otimes 2}}(u-k)^{2H}(u^{\prime}-k^{\prime})^{2H}

\displaystyle=\langle\mathbf{1}_{(-\infty,0]}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{(-\infty,0]},\mathbf{1}_{[0,1]}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{[0,1]}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}^{\otimes 2}}(u-k)^{2H}(u^{\prime}-k^{\prime})^{2H}+o(1),

where the last equation holds for
k
and
k^{\prime}
such that
k-ns\geq n^{\varepsilon}
and
k^{\prime}-ns\geq n^{\varepsilon}
. Using the relation
\langle\mathbf{1}_{(-\infty,0]},\mathbf{1}_{[0,1]}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}=-1/2
we obtain

(3.38)

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{1}_{[ns,{k}]}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{[ns,{k^{\prime}}]},\mathbf{1}_{[{k},u]}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{[{k^{\prime}},u^{\prime}]}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}^{\otimes 2}}=\frac{1}{4}(u-k)^{2H}(u^{\prime}-k^{\prime})^{2H}+o(1).

Substituting (
3.38
) into (
3.32
) we obtain

\displaystyle A_{22}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n^{-4H-2}\frac{1}{4}\sum_{ns+n^{\varepsilon}\leq{k},{k^{\prime}}<nt}\int_{{k}}^{{k+1}}\int_{{k^{\prime}}}^{{k^{\prime}+1}}(u-k)^{2H}(u^{\prime}-k^{\prime})^{2H}du^{\prime}du+n^{-4H}o(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n^{-4H}(t-s)^{2}\cdot\frac{1}{4}(2H+1)^{-2}+n^{-4H}o(1).

It follows that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{4H}A_{22}=(t-s)^{2}\cdot\frac{1}{4}(2H+1)^{-2}.

Recalling relation (
3.37
), we thus obtain the convergence in (
3.36
).

Step 7: Convergence of
{\mathcal{J}}_{s}^{t}(x^{1},h^{n})
.

In this step, we show the
L_{2}
-convergence of
{\mathcal{J}}_{s}^{t}(x^{1},h^{n})
:

(3.39)

\displaystyle n^{2H}{\mathcal{J}}_{s}^{t}(x^{1},h^{n})\to-\frac{1}{4H+2}(t-s).

In view of the convergence (
3.36
), it suffices to show that:

(3.40)

\displaystyle n^{2H}\mathbb{E}{\mathcal{J}}_{s}^{t}(x^{1},h^{n})\to-\frac{1}{4H+2}(t-s)\qquad\text{as $n\to\infty$. }

The convergence (
3.40
) can be proved in the similar way as in Step 5. Indeed, we have:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[{\mathcal{J}}_{s}^{t}(x^{1},h^{n})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sum_{s\leq t_{k}<t}x^{1}_{st_{k}}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}x^{1}_{t_{k}u}du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s\leq t_{k}<t}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\langle\mathbf{1}_{[s,t_{k}]},\mathbf{1}_{[t_{k},u]}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n^{-2H-1}\sum_{ns\leq{k}<nt}\int_{{k}}^{{k+1}}\langle\mathbf{1}_{[ns,{k}]},\mathbf{1}_{[{k},u]}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n^{-2H-1}(-1/2)\cdot\sum_{ns\leq{k}<nt}\int_{{k}}^{{k+1}}(u-k)^{2H}du+n^{-2H}o(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n^{-2H}(t-s)(-1/2)(2H+1)^{-1}+n^{-2H}o(1).

The convergence (
3.40
) then follows. The two convergences
(
3.36
) and (
3.40
) together implies that the convergence (
3.39
) holds.

Step 8: Convergence of
{\mathcal{J}}_{s}^{t}(y,h^{n})
.
Let
(s,t)\in{\mathcal{S}}_{2}([0,1])
.
We start by taking a partition of
[s,t]
:
s=s_{0}<s_{1}<\cdots<s_{m}=t
such that
\max_{j=0,\dots,m-1}|s_{j+1}-s_{j}|\leq 1/m
for some
m<n
. Then we can write

(3.41)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}}_{s}^{t}(y,h^{n})=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}{\mathcal{J}}_{s_{j}}^{s_{j+1}}(y,h^{n}).

Since
(y,y^{\prime})
is controlled by
(x,2,H-\varepsilon)
in
L_{2}
we have the expansion
y_{t_{k}}=y_{s_{j}}+y_{s_{j}}^{\prime}x^{1}_{s_{j}t_{k}}+r^{(0)}_{s_{j}t_{k}}
. Substituting this into
{\mathcal{J}}_{s_{j}}^{s_{j+1}}(y,h^{n})
in (
3.41
) we obtain

(3.42)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}}_{s}^{t}(y,h^{n})=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}y_{s_{j}}{\mathcal{J}}_{s_{j}}^{s_{j+1}}(1,h^{n})+\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}y_{s_{j}}^{\prime}{\mathcal{J}}_{s_{j}}^{s_{j+1}}(x^{1},h^{n})+\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}{\mathcal{J}}_{s_{j}}^{s_{j+1}}(r^{(0)},h^{n}).

In the following we consider the convergence of the three terms on the right-hand side of (
3.42
).

We note that it follows from relations (
3.22
) and (
3.35
) that conditions (
3.1
)-(
3.2
) hold for
h:=n^{2H}h^{n}
,
{\alpha}=H-\varepsilon
,
{\beta}_{0}:=1-H
and
{\beta}_{1}:=1
. Therefore, applying Proposition
3.1
we have

\displaystyle n^{2H}|{\mathcal{J}}_{s}^{t}(r^{(0)},h^{n})|_{L_{1}}\lesssim(t-s)^{1+H-\varepsilon}.

This implies that

(3.43)

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}n^{2H}\Big|\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}{\mathcal{J}}_{s_{j}}^{s_{j+1}}(r^{(0)},h^{n})\Big|_{L_{1}}\lesssim\lim_{m\to\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}(s_{j+1}-s_{j})^{1+H-\varepsilon}=0.

We turn to the other two terms in the right-hand side of (
3.42
). Applying (
3.22
) we have

\displaystyle n^{2H}\Big|\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}y_{s_{j}}{\mathcal{J}}_{s_{j}}^{s_{j+1}}(1,h^{n})\Big|_{L_{1}}=n^{2H}\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\Big|y_{s_{j}}h^{n}_{s_{j}s_{j+1}}\Big|_{L_{1}}

(3.44)

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}|y_{s_{j}}|_{L_{2}}(s_{j+1}-s_{j})^{1/2}(1/n)^{1/2-H}\to 0\qquad\text{as $n\to\infty$. }

Finally, according to (
3.39
) we have the convergence in probability:

(3.45)

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{2H}\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}y_{s_{j}}^{\prime}{\mathcal{J}}_{s_{j}}^{s_{j+1}}(x^{1},h^{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4H+2}\lim_{m\to\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}y_{s_{j}}^{\prime}(s_{j+1}-s_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4H+2}\int_{0}^{t}y_{u}^{\prime}du.

Putting together the convergences (
3.43
)-(
3.45
) and recalling the relation (
3.42
) we conclude the convergence (
3.21
).
∎

With Lemma
3.3
in hand, we are ready to consider the convergence of the Riemann sum for the integral
\int_{0}^{t}y_{u}du
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
x
be a one-dimensional fBm with Hurst parameter
H<1/2
. Let
(y,y^{\prime},y^{\prime\prime})
be a rough path controlled by
(x,3,H)
almost surely.Then we have the convergence in probability:

(3.46)

\displaystyle n^{2H}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{0\leq t_{k}<t}y_{t_{k}}-\int_{0}^{t}y_{u}du\right)\to 0\qquad\text{ as $n\to\infty$.}

Proof.

The proof is divided into several steps.

Step 1: A decomposition of the error of Riemann sum.

We first have

(3.47)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}y_{u}du-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{0\leq t_{k}<t}y_{t_{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq t_{k}<t}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\delta y_{t_{k}u}du.

Substituting the expansion
\delta y_{t_{k}u}=y_{t_{k}}^{\prime}x^{1}_{t_{k}u}+y_{t_{k}}^{\prime\prime}x^{2}_{t_{k}u}+r^{(0)}_{t_{k}u}
into (
3.47
) we get the expansion:

(3.48)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}y_{u}du-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{0\leq t_{k}<t}y_{t_{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3},

where

\displaystyle I_{1}=\sum_{0\leq t_{k}<t}y_{t_{k}}^{\prime}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}x^{1}_{t_{k}u}du,\qquad I_{2}=\sum_{0\leq t_{k}<t}y_{t_{k}}^{\prime\prime}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}x^{2}_{t_{k}u}du,\qquad I_{3}=\sum_{0\leq t_{k}<t}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}r^{0}_{t_{k}u}du.

In the following we consider the convergence of
I_{1}
,
I_{2}
and
I_{3}
which together will give the desired convergence in (
3.46
).

Step 2: Convergence of
I_{1}
and
I_{3}
.

Since
|r^{(0)}_{t_{k}u}|_{L_{1}}\lesssim n^{-3H}
it follows that

(3.49)

\displaystyle n^{2H}I_{3}\to 0

in probability as
n\to\infty
. On the other hand, a direct application of Lemma
3.3
yields the convergence

(3.50)

\displaystyle n^{2H}I_{1}\to-\frac{1}{4H+2}\int_{s}^{t}y^{\prime\prime}_{u}du\qquad\text{as $n\to\infty$}.

Step 3: Convergence of
I_{2}
.

We consider the following decomposition of
I_{2}
:

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq t_{k}<t}y_{t_{k}}^{\prime\prime}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}x^{2}_{t_{k}u}du=I_{21}+I_{22},

where

(3.51)

\displaystyle I_{21}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq t_{k}<t}y_{t_{k}}^{\prime\prime}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}(x^{2}_{t_{k}u}-\frac{1}{2}(u-t_{k})^{2H})du

\displaystyle I_{22}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{0\leq t_{k}<t}y_{t_{k}}^{\prime\prime}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}(u-t_{k})^{2H}du.

It is clear that

\displaystyle I_{22}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{0\leq t_{k}<t}y_{t_{k}}^{\prime\prime}\cdot(1/n)^{2H+1}(2H+1)^{-1}.

It follows that

\displaystyle n^{2H}I_{22}\to\frac{1}{4H+2}\int_{0}^{t}y_{u}^{\prime\prime}du\qquad\text{in probability as $n\to\infty$.}

We turn to the convergence of
I_{21}
.
We first note that a direct computation shows that

(3.52)

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{0\leq t_{k}<t}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\Big(x^{2}_{t_{k}u}-\frac{1}{2}(u-t_{k})^{2H}\Big)du\Big|_{L_{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq t_{k},t_{k^{\prime}}<t}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\int_{t_{k^{\prime}}}^{t_{k^{\prime}+1}}n^{-4H}|\rho(k-k^{\prime})|^{2}du^{\prime}du

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle n^{-4H-2}n(t-s)=n^{-4H-1}(t-s).

Applying Proposition
3.1
to
I_{21}
in (
3.51
) with
\ell=1
and
{\beta}_{0}=1-H+\varepsilon
and invoking the estimate (
3.52
) we obtain

\displaystyle n^{2H}|I_{21}|_{L_{1}}\lesssim(t-s)^{1-H+\varepsilon}(1/n)^{H-\varepsilon}

for any
\varepsilon>0
. In particular, we have
n^{2H}|I_{21}|_{L_{1}}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.
Combining the convergence of
I_{21}
and
I_{22}
we obtain

(3.53)

\displaystyle n^{2H}I_{2}\to\frac{1}{4H+2}\int_{0}^{t}y_{u}^{\prime\prime}du\qquad\text{in probability as $n\to\infty$.}

Step 4: Conclusion.
Substituting the convergences of
I_{i}
,
i=1,2,3
in (
3.49
), (
3.50
) and (
3.53
) into (
3.48
) we obtain the convergence (
3.46
).
∎

Remark 3.5
.

The proof of Proposition
3.4

suggests that the exact rate of convergence in (
3.46
) is
O(n^{-H-1/2})
given that
y
satisfies some regularity conditions. But the rate
o(n^{-2H})
we have obtained in (

) is sufficient for our purpose in this paper, and it requires a weaker condition of

.

In the next result we consider the convergence rate of the Riemann sum under a weaker condition. We will also include the case when

.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
x
be a one-dimensional fBm with Hurst parameter
H\leq 1/2
. Let
(y,y^{\prime})
be a rough path controlled by
(x,2,H-\varepsilon)
in
L_{2}
for some
\varepsilon>0
. Then there is a constant
K
independent of

such that:

(3.54)

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{0\leq t_{k}<t}y_{t_{k}}-\int_{0}^{t}y_{u}du\Big|_{L_{1}}\leq Kn^{-2H+2\varepsilon}

for all

.

Proof.

Because

is controlled by

we have the relation
\delta y_{t_{k}u}=y_{t_{k}}^{\prime}x^{1}_{t_{k}u}+r^{(0)}_{t_{k}u}
.
So, similar to (

), we have the decomposition

(3.55)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}y_{u}du-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{0\leq t_{k}<t}y_{t_{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2},

where

\displaystyle I_{1}=\sum_{0\leq t_{k}<t}y_{t_{k}}^{\prime}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}x^{1}_{t_{k}u}du,\qquad I_{2}=\sum_{0\leq t_{k}<t}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}r^{0}_{t_{k}u}du.

It is readily checked that
|I_{2}|_{L_{1}}\lesssim n^{-2H+2\varepsilon}
. Let
h^{n}
be defined in (
3.20
). Applying Proposition
3.1
with
h=n^{2H-2\varepsilon}h^{n}
,
\ell=1
and
{\beta}_{0}=1-H+2\varepsilon
we obtain that

\displaystyle n^{2H-2\varepsilon}|I_{1}|_{L_{1}}\lesssim 1.

Combining the estimate of
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
in (
3.55
) we obtain the desired estimate (
3.54
).
∎

3.3.
Weighted

In this subsection we consider limit theorems for weighted random sums of some fBms functionals.
For
p>-1

\displaystyle c_{p}=\mathbb{E}(|N|^{p})=\frac{2^{p/2}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\Gamma\left(\frac{p+1}{2}\right).

It is easy to see that

Lemma 3.7
.

Let

\displaystyle n^{H-1}\sum_{0\leq t_{k}<t}y_{t_{k}}f(n^{H}x^{1}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}})\to-\frac{1}{2}c_{p+2}\int_{0}^{t}y_{u}^{\prime}du\qquad\text{as $n\to\infty$.}

Proof.

We prove the convergence (
3.59

[
32

.
It is easy to see that the function

in (

with Hermite rank

. Take

. By assumption

is a rough path controlled by
(x,\ell,H)
almost surely. Furthermore, it is easy to see that

[

Since

\displaystyle a_{1}=\mathbb{E}[|N|^{p+1}\cdot\text{sign}(N)N]=\mathbb{E}[|N|^{p+2}]=c_{p+2}.

Applying
[

, Theorem 4.14]

we thus obtain the convergence (
3.59
).
∎

Let

,

4.
Limit theorem for
p
-variation of processes controlled by fBm

In this section we consider the convergence of
p
-variation for processes controlled by fBm.
Throughout the section we let

\phi(x)=|x|^{p}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}

for
p\geq 1
.
We first state the following elementary result.

Lemma 4.1
.

Denote by
\phi^{(j)}
the
j
th derivative of
\phi
.
For convenience we will also write
\phi(x)=\phi^{(0)}(x)
,
\phi^{\prime}(x)=\phi^{(1)}(x)
and
\phi^{\prime\prime}(x)=\phi^{(2)}(x)
. For
j=0,1,\dots,[p]
we set

(4.1)

\displaystyle\phi_{j}(x)=|x|^{p-j}\cdot\emph{sign}(x)^{j}\qquad\text{and}\qquad K_{j}=p\cdots(p-j+1)=\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}(p-i),

where recall that
\emph{sign}(x)
is defined in (
3.57
) and we use the convention that
\prod_{i=0}^{-1}(p-i)=1
. For example, we have
K_{0}=1
,
K_{1}=p
,
K_{2}=p(p-1)
. Then

(i) When
p
is odd,
\phi
has derivative up to order
[p]-1
, and
\phi^{([p]-1)}
is Lipschitz. When
p
is even,
\phi
has derivative of all orders. When
p
is non-integer,
\phi
has derivative up to order
[p]
.

(ii) For
x\in\mathbb{R}
we have

(4.2)

\displaystyle\phi^{(j)}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K_{j}\cdot\phi_{j}(x),\qquad j=0,1,\dots,[p],

with the exception that
\phi^{(p)}(0)
is undefined when
p
is an odd number.
In particular,
when
p
is an odd number we have

\phi^{(j)}(x)=K_{j}x^{p-j}\emph{sign}(x)
,
while when
p
is even we get

\phi^{(j)}(x)=K_{j}x^{p-j}
.

Following is our main result. Recall that we define
\phi(x)=|x|^{p}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
, and for a continuous process
y
the
p
-variation of
y
over the time interval
[0,t]
is defined as

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq t_{k}<t}\phi(\delta y_{t_{k}t_{k+1}})=\sum_{0\leq t_{k}<t}|\delta y_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}|^{p},

where
t_{k}=k/n
,
k=0,1,\dots,n
is a uniform partition of
[0,1]
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
x
be
a fBm with Hurst parameter
H\leq 1/2
and
(y^{(0)},\dots,y^{(\ell-1)})
be a process controlled by
(x,\ell,H)
almost surely (see Definition
2.1
) for some
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. Recall that
\phi^{\prime}
and
\phi^{\prime\prime}
are derivatives of
\phi
defined in (
4.2
), and
c_{p}
and
\sigma
are constants given in (
3.56
) and (
3.61
), respectively.
Let

\displaystyle U^{n}_{t}=n^{pH-1}\sum_{0\leq t_{k}<t}\phi(\delta y_{t_{k}t_{k+1}})-c_{p}\int_{0}^{t}\phi(y^{\prime}_{u})du\qquad t\in[0,1].

Then

(i) When
1/4<H\leq 1/2
,
p\in[3,\infty)\cup\{2\}
and
\ell\geq 4
we have the stable f.d.d. convergence

(4.3)

\displaystyle\left(n^{1/2}U^{n},x\right)\to(U,x),\qquad\text{as $n\to\infty$, }

where

\displaystyle U_{t}=\sigma\int_{0}^{t}\phi(y_{u}^{\prime})dW_{u}\qquad t\in[0,1]\,,

and
W
is a standard Brownian motion independent of
x
.

(ii) When
H=1/4
,
p\in[5,\infty)\cup\{2,4\}
and
\ell\geq 6
we have the stable f.d.d. convergence

(4.4)

\displaystyle\left(n^{1/2}U^{n},x\right)\to(U,x),\qquad\text{as $n\to\infty$, }

Gevrey regularity for the formally linearizable billiard of Treschev
Qun Wang, Ke Zhang

Abstract
Treschev made the remarkable discovery that there exists formal power series describing a billiard with locally linearizable dynamics. We show that if the frequency for the linear dynamics is Diophanine, the Treschev example is ( 1 + α ) (1+\alpha) -Gevrey for some α > 0 \alpha>0 . Our proof is based on an iterative scheme that further clarifies the structure and symmetries underlying the original Treschev construction. Hopefully, Our result sheds a light on the more important question of whether this example is convergent.

Introduction

The Birkhoff-Poritsky conjecture (
[
Por50
]
) states that the only integrable billiards are the ellipses. Several advances are made recently towards this conjecture, see
[
ADSK16
,
KS18
,
BM22
]
. In these works, it is assumed that a certain part of the phase space is foliated by
essential
invariant curves. It is an open question whether the analogous conjecture is true if the billiard is integrable near a periodic orbit. In this case, an open set of the phase space is foliated by
contractible
invariant curves.

Treschev (
[
Tre13
,
Tre15
,
Tre17
]
) discovered a billiard whose dynamics near a period two orbit is formally linearizable. (See also
[
Tre22
]
for an analogous result for Hamiltonian systems). If this example were to converge, then the local version of Birkhoff-Poritsky conjecture is false. Treschev’s example is a billiard played between two mirror symmetric curved walls, with each wall symmetric with respect to the horizontal axis, see Figure
1
. To give a precise description, we use an alternative coordinate system due to Bialy and Mironov (
[
BM22
]
).

Figure 1:
Treschev’s example

Let
\Omega
be a strictly convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Define its support function by

q(\psi)=\sup\{\langle z,e^{i\psi}\rangle:\,z\in\Omega\},\quad\psi\in\mathbb{R}/(2\pi\mathbb{Z}),

where we did and will continue to identify
\mathbb{C}
and
\mathbb{R}^{2}
in the notations. The function

q(\psi)e^{i\psi}+iq^{\prime}(\psi)e^{i\psi}

provides a parametrization of
\partial\Omega
. We assume that
q(\psi)
is equal to a real analytic function near
\psi=0
, and admits a
(\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2})
-symmetry, namely

q(\psi)=q(-\psi),\quad q(\pi-\psi)=q(\psi).

In particular,
q
also admits the central symmetry
q(\psi+\pi)=q(\psi)
.

Represent a sequence of billiard trajectories by its tangent angles
(t_{n})
. In these coordinates,
(0,\pi,0,\pi,\cdots)
corresponds to the horizontal two-periodic orbit. By properly choosing the curvature of the boundaries, one can make the two-periodic orbit elliptic, hence KAM stable. The nearby orbits alternate between
t\in(-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2})
and
t\in(\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{3\pi}{2})
. Due to the central symmetry of the boundary, we can identify
t
and
t+\pi
and represent a billiard orbit by the sequence
t_{n}\in(-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2})
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Using the alternative generating function discovered by Bialy and Mironov (
[
BM22
]
), we will show that (See Lemma
2.2
)
(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})
represents a billiard trajectory if and only if

(1.1)

\partial_{2}S(t_{1},t_{2})+\partial_{1}S(t_{2},t_{3})=0,\quad S(t_{1},t_{2})=S_{q}(t_{1},t_{2})=q\left(\frac{t_{1}+t_{2}}{2}\right)\cos\left(\frac{t_{1}-t_{2}}{2}\right),

where
q
is the support function of the boundary.
We then define the billiard map
T:(t_{1},t_{2})\mapsto(t_{2},t_{3})
by (
1.1
). By reducing the central symmetry, the horizontal orbit becomes a fixed point, i.e.
T(0,0)=(0,0)
.

Suppose
(0,0)
is a lineariazble elliptic fixed point of
T
. Then there exists a rotation
R_{\theta}
and a change of variable
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that

(1.2)

T\circ\Phi=\Phi\circ R_{\theta},

where
R_{\theta}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos{\theta}&-\sin{\theta}\\
\sin{\theta}&\cos{\theta}\end{pmatrix}
. Following Treschev (
[
Tre13
]
), we write
\Phi=(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})
as a function of
z,\bar{z}
, where
z,\bar{z}\in\mathbb{C}
. This way,
R_{\theta}(z,\bar{z})=(\lambda z,\lambda^{-1}\bar{z})
and the conjugacy equation (
1.2
) can be conveniently written as

T(\varphi_{1}(z,\bar{z}),\varphi_{2}(z,\bar{z}))=\left(\phi_{1}\circ R_{\theta}(z,\bar{z}),\phi_{2}\circ R_{\theta}(z,\bar{z})\right)=\left(\varphi_{1}(\lambda z,\lambda^{-1}\bar{z}),\varphi_{2}(\lambda z,\lambda^{-1}\bar{z})\right),

where
\lambda=e^{i\theta}\in\mathbb{C}
. Since
T(t_{1},t_{2})=(t_{2},t_{3})
, we get
\varphi_{2}(z,\bar{z})=\varphi_{1}(\lambda z,\lambda^{-1}\bar{z})
. Plug into (
1.1
), we get

\partial_{2}S(\varphi_{2}(\lambda^{-1}z,\lambda\bar{z}),\varphi_{2}(z,\bar{z}))+\partial_{1}S(\varphi_{2}(z,\bar{z}),\varphi_{2}(\lambda z,\lambda^{-1}\bar{z}))=0.

Denote
\varphi=\varphi_{2}
, and write
\varphi(\lambda^{-1}z,\lambda\bar{z})=\varphi^{-}(z,\bar{z})
,
\varphi(\lambda z,\lambda^{-1}\bar{z})=\varphi^{+}(z,\bar{z})
, we get the following equation

(1.3)

\mathcal{E}(q,\varphi):=\partial_{2}S(\varphi^{-},\varphi)+\partial_{1}S(\varphi,\varphi^{+})=0.

Henceforth

•

we assume that
\displaystyle\varphi(z,\bar{z})=\sum_{j,k=0}^{\infty}\varphi_{jk}(z^{j}\bar{z}^{k})
is a complex formal series in
z,\bar{z}
. Then
\varphi
represents a real function if and only if
\overline{\varphi_{jk}}=\varphi_{kj}
. In the sequel the solution that we investigate will satisfy the stronger condition
\varphi_{jk}=\varphi_{kj}
.

•

We use the notation
O_{k}
to represent
\displaystyle O(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\geq 0,\beta\geq 0\\
\alpha+\beta=k\end{subarray}}|z|^{\alpha}|\bar{z}|^{\beta})

The following theorem reformulates the result of Treschev.

Theorem 1.1

(see also
[
Tre13
]
)
.

For each
\lambda=e^{i\theta}
where
\theta\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}
, there exists real formal power series

q(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}q_{2k}t^{2k},\quad\varphi(z,\bar{z})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j+k=2n+1}\varphi_{j,k}z^{j}\bar{z}^{k}

where
\varphi_{j,k}=\varphi_{k,j}
,
solving equation (
1.3
) as formal power series in
z,\bar{z}
.

Assume the following Diophantine property for
\lambda
: there exists
1>c>0
and
\tau>0
such that

(1.4)

|\lambda^{k}-1|\geq c|k|^{-\tau},\quad\text{ for all }k\neq 0.

Our main theorem is that this formal solution is of Gevrey class.

Theorem 1.2
.

For every
\alpha>\frac{5}{4}
, there exists
C(c,\tau)>0
such that

|q_{2k}|\leq C^{2k}e^{\alpha(2k)\log(2k)},\quad\sup_{j+k=n}|\varphi_{j,k}|\leq C^{n}e^{\alpha n\log n}.

In particular, the series are of Gevrey order
1+\alpha
.

Remark 1
.

The Gevrey order we obtained is independent of the parameters of the Diophantine condition, because the features of the proof that cause faster growth of coefficients have a much larger effect than the small denominators coming from the Diophantine condition.

In fact, we expect the proof can be adapted to work under the condition

|\lambda^{k}-1|>ce^{-\gamma|k|},\quad k\neq 0

with a small
\gamma
. In this case we expect the Gevrey order to depend on
\gamma
. This is not done in this paper, to avoid excessive technicality.

Our proof proceeds by a KAM-type iterative scheme. Given an initial guess
(q,\varphi)
, write

\mathcal{E}(q+\Delta q,\varphi+\Delta\varphi)=\mathcal{E}(q,\varphi)+\partial_{q}\mathcal{E}(q,\varphi)(\Delta q)+\partial_{\varphi}\mathcal{E}(q,\varphi)(\Delta\varphi)+O_{2}

where
O_{2}
is a higher order remainder. Using a Newton scheme, we need to solve the linearized equation

\partial_{q}\mathcal{E}(q,\varphi)(\Delta q)+\partial_{\varphi}\mathcal{E}(q,\varphi)(\Delta\varphi)=-\mathcal{E}(q,\varphi).

Two observations allow us to simplify this equation. First of all, the functional
\mathcal{E}
is
linear
in
q
, therefore

\mathcal{E}(q,\varphi)+\partial_{q}\mathcal{E}(q,\varphi)\Delta q=\mathcal{E}(q,\varphi)+\mathcal{E}(\Delta q,\varphi)=\mathcal{E}(q+\Delta q,\varphi).

Secondly, using the Lagrangian setting of Levi and Moser (
[
LM01
]
), we have

\partial_{\varphi}\mathcal{E}(q,\varphi)(\Delta\varphi)\cdot\varphi_{z}=\mathcal{L}_{z}(\Delta\varphi)+O_{2},

where
\mathcal{L}_{z}
is a second order difference operator to be defined later. Multiplying by
\varphi_{z}
and ignoring the higher order term, the linearized equation can be rewritten as

(1.5)

\mathcal{L}_{z}(\Delta\varphi)=-\mathcal{E}(q+\Delta q,\varphi)\varphi_{z}.

Roughly speaking, we solve (
1.5
) in two steps:

(1)

Define an operator
(q,\varphi)\mapsto\Delta q
, so that
\mathcal{E}(q+\Delta q,\varphi)
projects to the image of
\mathcal{L}_{z}
.

(2)

Find an approximate inverse of
\mathcal{L}_{z}
on its image.

It turns out that the inverse operator in step (2) is
tame
in sense of KAM theory. However, the operator in step (1) is
unbounded
in the analytic norm. This unboundedness makes the KAM scheme diverge on the space of analytic functions. Yet by modifying the KAM scheme and allowing the domain of analyticity to shrink to
0
, we obtain a Gevrey estimate. The same idea is used in
[
BlL22
]
to prove Gevrey estimate in the context of KAM theory for conformally symplectic systems.

Here is a basic outline of the paper:

•

In section 2, after adapting the generating function of Bialy and Mironov
[
BM22
]
into a version suitable to our setting, we apply the method of Levi-Moser
[
LM01
]
to obtain the cohomological equation (
2.7
), and decompose it into an
outer part
as well as an
inner part
, for further investigation.

•

In section 3, we solve the outer cohomological equation formally by choosing
\Delta q
by forcing the averaged generating function to vanish, which in turn implies the invertibility of the
\nabla^{+}
operator.

•

In section 4, we solve the inner cohomological equation approximately, with a prescribed order of truncation, followed by a symmetrisation of the output. Depending on whether the source of error is related to the average of generating function or not, we decompose the error into two parts for further analysis. The process of solving and estimating
\Delta q
is technical and is presented in detail in the appendix.

•

In section 5, after claiming the iterative scheme, we exhibit a series of properties of our chosen norm that will facilitate our estimate of magnitude of solutions produced in each iteration. As a byproduct, we obtain an alternative proof for the existence of solution as formal series, i.e. Treschev’s original result (
[
Tre13
]
).

•

In section 6, after choosing an appropriate initial point, we launch the KAM machine with all the previous preparation, which leads to the Gevrey regularity we seek.

4
Solving the inner cohomological equation

In this section, we will show that

is sufficiently small. Since the actual result of this section is quite technical, let us state a

Proposition 4.1
.

Given
(q,\varphi)
, suppose
q^{*}=q+\Delta q
,
\psi
are chosen as follows:

[S(q^{*},\varphi)]=1,\quad\psi=E^{+}\left(\mathcal{E}(q^{*},\varphi)\varphi_{z}\right).

Then

\Pi\left(\frac{\psi}{h}\right)

is quadratically small with respect to
\mathcal{E}

First, let us note the following calculation:

\displaystyle\partial_{z}\mathcal{E}\cdot\varphi_{\bar{z}}-\partial_{\bar{z}}\mathcal{E}\cdot\varphi_{z}

\displaystyle=\left(\lambda^{-1}\partial_{12}S\varphi_{z}^{-}+\partial_{22}S\varphi_{z}+\partial_{11}S^{+}\varphi_{z}+\lambda\partial_{12}S^{+}\varphi_{z}^{+}\right)\varphi_{\bar{z}}

\displaystyle-\left(\lambda\partial_{12}S\varphi_{\bar{z}}^{-}+\partial_{22}S\varphi_{z}+\partial_{11}S^{+}\varphi_{\bar{z}}+\lambda^{-1}\partial_{12}S^{+}\varphi_{\bar{z}}^{+}\right)\varphi_{\bar{z}}

\displaystyle=\partial_{12}S\left(\lambda^{-1}\varphi_{z}^{-}\varphi_{\bar{z}}-\lambda\varphi_{\bar{z}}^{-}\varphi_{z}\right)-\partial_{12}S^{+}\left(\lambda^{-1}\varphi_{\bar{z}}^{+}\varphi_{z}-\lambda\varphi_{z}^{+}\varphi_{\bar{z}}\right)

\displaystyle=\kappa-\kappa^{+}

where

(4.1)

\kappa=\partial_{12}S\left(\lambda^{-1}\varphi_{z}^{-}\varphi_{\bar{z}}-\lambda\varphi_{\bar{z}}^{-}\varphi_{z}\right).

For
\phi=\sum_{j,k}\phi_{j,k}z^{j}\bar{z}^{k}

\phi-\phi^{+}=\sum_{j\neq k}\phi_{j,k}(1-\lambda^{j-k})z^{j}\bar{z}^{k},

the operator

(4.2)

\tilde{E}(\phi)=\sum_{j\neq k}\phi_{j,k}/(1-\lambda^{j-k})z^{j}\bar{z}^{k}

\tilde{E}(\phi-\phi^{+})=\phi-[\phi].

It follows that

(4.3)

\kappa-[\kappa]=\tilde{E}\left(\partial_{z}\mathcal{E}\cdot\varphi_{\bar{z}}-\partial_{\bar{z}}\mathcal{E}\cdot\varphi_{z}\right)

has the same order as
\mathcal{E}
does. The function
\kappa
has a geometrical meaning: it is the determinant of the Jacobian matrix of the mapping

(z,\bar{z})\mapsto\left(\varphi^{-},\partial_{1}S(\varphi^{-},\varphi)\right).

This mapping is the symplectic version of the coordinate change
(z,\bar{z})\mapsto(\varphi^{-},\varphi)
(
\partial_{1}S(\varphi^{-},\varphi)
is the momentum variable), hence
\kappa
is the conformal factor of our coordinate change. Our calculation suggests that if
\mathcal{E}=0

\kappa
depends only on the radial component
z\bar{z}
.

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

Rewrting
h
using
\kappa
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{\psi}{h}

\displaystyle=\frac{\psi}{\partial_{12}S\varphi_{z}^{-}\varphi_{z}}=\psi\frac{\lambda^{-1}\varphi_{\bar{z}}/\varphi_{z}-\lambda\varphi_{\bar{z}}^{-}/\varphi_{z}^{-}}{\kappa}.

Denote

. Lemma
3.1
implies that
[(\bar{z}\psi g^{-})]=[(\bar{z}\psi g^{-})^{+}]=[\lambda^{-1}\bar{z}\psi^{+}g]
, hence

\displaystyle\left[\bar{z}\frac{\psi(\lambda^{-1}g-\lambda g^{-})}{[\kappa]}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{[\kappa]}\left(\lambda^{-1}[\bar{z}\psi g]-\lambda[\bar{z}\psi g^{-}]\right)=\frac{1}{[\kappa]}\left(\lambda^{-1}[\bar{z}\psi g]-\lambda[\lambda^{-1}\bar{z}\psi^{+}g]\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda[\kappa]}\left([\bar{z}\psi g]-\lambda[\bar{z}\psi^{+}g]\right)=\frac{1}{\lambda[\kappa]}\left[\bar{z}g\nabla^{+}\psi\right]=\frac{1}{\lambda[\kappa]}\left[\bar{z}\frac{\varphi_{\bar{z}}}{\varphi_{z}}\mathcal{E}\varphi_{z}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\left[\bar{z}\mathcal{E}\varphi_{\bar{z}}\right]}{\lambda[\kappa]}=\frac{\bar{z}}{\lambda[\kappa]}\Pi\left(\mathcal{E}\varphi_{\bar{z}}\right)=0,

where the last equality is due to the way in which

is constructed and the dual version of Lemma

As a result, we get

\displaystyle\Pi\left(\frac{\psi}{h}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\bar{z}}\left[\bar{z}\frac{\psi(\lambda^{-1}g-\lambda g^{-})}{\kappa}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\bar{z}}\left[\bar{z}\frac{\psi(\lambda^{-1}g-\lambda g^{-})}{\kappa}\right]-\frac{1}{\bar{z}}\left[\bar{z}\frac{\psi(\lambda^{-1}g-\lambda g^{-})}{[\kappa]}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\bar{z}}\left[\bar{z}\psi(\lambda^{-1}g-\lambda g^{-})(1/\kappa-1/[\kappa])\right]=O_{2}(\mathcal{E}),

i.e.,
\displaystyle\Pi\left(\frac{\psi}{h}\right)
is quadratically small in
\mathcal{E}
, since

and

∎

Corollary 6.3
.

The formal power series
q_{[n]}
and
\varphi_{[n]}
from Proposition

converge term-wise to

. In the formal sense, we have

Moreover, for every

there exists

such that

This implies the series

and
\varphi^{\infty}
are of Gevrey order

.

Proof.

Because of (
6.3
), both series
q_{[n]}
and
\varphi_{[n]}
stabilizes and converges term-wise to a limit series. Let us first show that the sequence
M_{n}
and

in (

) eventually coincide. Indeed, let

, then for all

N_{n}=2N_{n-1},\quad M_{n}=\lfloor 3M_{n-1}/2\rfloor+1.

It follows that
n_{0}<\infty
since the contrary will lead to a contradiction. In the sequel, we will only deal with the sequence

.

For

, let

be the unique integer such that

Using the defnition of

, it’s easy to see that there exists

such that

therefore

\frac{1}{c_{1}}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{n}<k\leq\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{n+1}.

In particular, we have

It follows from Proposition
6.1

\|q_{[n]}\|_{\rho_{[n]}}\leq C_{0}

for all

Note that

\log\epsilon_{[n]}=(3/2)\log\epsilon_{[n-1]}=(3/2)^{n}\log\epsilon_{[0]}\leq c_{1}k\log\epsilon_{[0]},

and

(\rho_{[n]})^{-k}=(\rho_{[0]})^{-k}\bar{\gamma}^{-nk}=(\rho_{[0]})^{-k}e^{\log(1/\bar{\gamma})k\frac{\log c_{1}+\log k}{\log(3/2)}}\leq e^{k\log c_{2}}e^{\alpha k\log k},

Appendix A
Solving for

In this section we prove Proposition

. We will be essentially estimating a weighted norm of a lower triangular matrix. Given power series
q
,
\varphi
, recall that
\xi=(\varphi^{-}-\varphi)/2
,
\eta=(\varphi^{-}+\varphi)/2
,

. Let us write, for all

[\zeta^{2k}p(\zeta)]=\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}P_{j,k}(z\bar{z})^{j},\quad[S_{q}]=\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}[S_{q}]_{2j}(z\bar{z})^{j}.

Lemma A.1
.

Suppose for some
C_{0}>0
and
\rho_{0}>0
,

\|q\|_{\rho_{0},3},\|\varphi\|_{\rho_{0},3}<C_{0}.

Let
C_{1}=2C_{0}
, Then

\displaystyle|P_{jk}|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{\mu^{2j}}{\sqrt{j}},

\displaystyle\text{if }j=k,

\displaystyle|P_{jk}|

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}k\mu^{2k}(1+C_{1}\rho^{2})^{2k}\rho^{2k-2j+2},

\displaystyle\text{if }j>k.

For all the proofs in this section,
f\lesssim g
stands for
f\leq Cg
for a constant
C>0
depending only on
C_{0}
and the constant
c
in the Diophantine condition (
1.4

Proof.

We have shown in the proof of Proposition
3.5
that

P_{j,j}=2^{-2j}\binom{2j}{j}(\lambda^{-1}+1)^{j}(\lambda+1)^{j}.

Note that
|\lambda^{-1}+1|=|\lambda+1|=\mu
. Applying Sterling’s formula
\displaystyle n!=\sqrt{2\pi n}(\frac{n}{e})^{n}e^{\frac{\theta}{12n}},0<\theta<1
to the term
\binom{2j}{j}=\frac{(2j)!}{(j!)^{2}}
, one sees that

|P_{j,j}|\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{\mu^{2j}}{\sqrt{j}},

For the upper bound, we apply Lemma
5.2
to get
\|\xi\|_{\rho}\leq\mu\rho(1+C_{1}\rho^{2})
. Let
\xi_{0}=\frac{1}{2}((\varphi^{(0)})^{-}+\varphi^{(0)})
. Observe that
\xi_{0}^{2k}
only has terms of degree

and does not contribute to the
j,j
coefficient when
j\geq k+1
. We then write

\xi^{2k}p(\zeta)-\xi_{0}^{2k}=(\xi^{2k}-\xi_{0}^{2k})+\xi^{2k}(p(\zeta)-1).

The term

, hence does not contribute to the
j,j

. For the first term, according to Lemma
5.1

\displaystyle\|(\xi-\xi_{0})\sum_{l=0}^{2k-1}\xi_{0}^{l}\xi^{2k-l-1}\|_{\rho}

\displaystyle\lesssim\rho^{3}\|(\xi-\xi_{0})\|_{\rho}(2k)\|\sup_{0\leq l\leq 2k-1}\|\xi_{0}^{l}\xi^{2k-1-l}\|_{\rho}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2k\rho^{3}\|(\xi-\xi_{0})\|_{\rho}\sup_{0\leq l\leq 2k-1}(\mu\rho)^{l}(1-c\rho^{2})^{2k-1-l}

\displaystyle\lesssim(k)\rho^{3}\mu^{2k-1}\rho^{2k-1}(1+C_{1}\rho^{2})^{2k-1}

\displaystyle\leq k\mu^{2k-1}\rho^{2k+2}(1+C_{1}\rho^{2})^{2k-1}.

For the second term, we note that

and due to Lemma

Lemma A.2
.

Given

Note that

Lemma A.3

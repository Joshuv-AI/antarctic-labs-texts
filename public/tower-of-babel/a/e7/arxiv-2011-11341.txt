Lower Bound on the Capacity of the Continuous-Space SSFM Model of Optical Fiber
Milad Sefidgaran, Mansoor Yousefi

Abstract
The capacity of a discrete-time model of optical fiber described by the split-step Fourier method (SSFM) as a function of the signal-to-noise ratio SNR and the number of segments in distance K K is considered. It is shown that if K ≥ SNR 2 / 3 K\geq\textnormal{SNR}^{2/3} and SNR → ∞ \textnormal{SNR}\rightarrow\infty , the capacity of the resulting continuous-space lossless model is lower bounded by 1 2 ​ log 2 ⁡ ( 1 + SNR ) − 1 2 + o ⁡ ( 1 ) \frac{1}{2}\log_{2}(1+\textnormal{SNR})-\frac{1}{2}+o(1) , where o ⁡ ( 1 ) o(1) tends to zero with SNR . As K → ∞ K\rightarrow\infty , the inter-symbol interference (ISI) averages out to zero due to the law of large numbers and the SSFM model tends to a diagonal phase noise model. It follows that, in contrast to the discrete-space model where there is only one signal degree-of-freedom (DoF) at high powers, the number of DoFs in the continuous-space model is at least half of the input dimension n n . Intensity-modulation and direct detection achieves this rate. The pre-log in the lower bound when K = SNR δ K=\sqrt[\delta]{\textnormal{SNR}} is generally characterized in terms of δ \delta . It is shown that if the nonlinearity parameter γ → ∞ \gamma\rightarrow\infty , the capacity of the continuous-space model is 1 2 ​ log 2 ⁡ ( 1 + SNR ) + o ⁡ ( 1 ) \frac{1}{2}\log_{2}(1+\textnormal{SNR})+o(1) . The SSFM model when the dispersion matrix does not depend on K K is considered. It is shown that the capacity of this model when K = SNR δ K=\sqrt[\delta]{\textnormal{SNR}} , δ > 3 \delta>3 , and SNR → ∞ \textnormal{SNR}\rightarrow\infty is 1 2 ​ n ​ log 2 ⁡ ( 1 + SNR ) + O ⁡ ( 1 ) \frac{1}{2n}\log_{2}(1+\textnormal{SNR})+O(1) . Thus, there is only one DoF in this model. Finally, it is found that the maximum achievable information rates (AIRs) of the SSFM model with back-propagation equalization obtained using numerical simulation follows a double-ascent curve. The AIR characteristically increases with SNR , reaching a peak at a certain optimal power, and then decreases as SNR is further increased. The peak is attributed to a balance between noise and stochastic ISI. However, if the power is further increased, the AIR will increase again, approaching the lower bound 1 2 ​ log ⁡ ( 1 + SNR ) − 1 2 + o ⁡ ( 1 ) \frac{1}{2}\log(1+\textnormal{SNR})-\frac{1}{2}+o(1) . The second ascent is because the ISI averages out to zero with K → ∞ K\rightarrow\infty sufficiently fast.



I
Introduction

Optical fiber is the medium of choice for high-speed data transmission. Although general expressions for the capacity of discrete-time point-to-point channels are derived in
[
1
,
2
]
, evaluating these expressions for models of optical fiber remains difficult.

Optical fiber is modeled by the stochastic nonlinear Schrödinger (NLS) equation.
There are two effects in the channel that impact the capacity.
First, nonlinearity transforms additive noise to
phase noise
during the propagation.
As the amplitude of the input signal tends to infinity,
the phase of the output signal tends to a uniform random variable in
the zero-dispersion channel
[
3
, Sec. IV]
.
Second, dispersion converts phase noise to amplitude noise introducing a
multiplicative noise
.
The successive application of the phase and multiplicative noise makes signal noise interaction intractable.

The achievable information rates (AIRs) of the wavelength-division multiplexing (WDM) vanish at high powers due to treating interference (arising from the application of the linear multiplexing to the nonlinear channel) as noise

[
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
]
. On the other hand, it is shown that the capacity
\mathcal{C}(\textnormal{SNR},K)
of the discrete-time models of optical fiber
as a function of the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and the number of segments in distance
K
satisfies
[
8
,
9
]

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\textnormal{SNR},K)\leq\log_{2}(1+\text{SNR}).

(1)

The problem of finding the capacity has been investigated for the non-dispersive case in
[
10
,
3
,
11
,
12
]
. It is shown that the asymptotic capacity of this channel is
\frac{1}{2}\log_{2}(\mathcal{P})+o(1)

[
3
]
, where
\mathcal{P}
is the average input signal power.

The stochastic NLS equation can be discretized using the split-step Fourier method (SSFM).
The capacity of the discrete-time discrete-space SSFM model of the optical fiber with fixed step size in distance as a function of
SNR
is studied in
[
13
]
.
It is shown that this model tends to a linear fading channel as
\textnormal{SNR}\rightarrow\infty
, described by a random matrix
\mathsf{M}_{K}
. The asymptotic capacity of this model is
[
13

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\textnormal{SNR},K){=}\begin{cases}\IEEEstrut\frac{1}{2n}\log_{2}\left(\textnormal{SNR}\right)+O(1),&\textnormal{const. loss},\\[1.0pt]
\frac{1}{n}\log_{2}\log_{2}\left(\textnormal{SNR}\right)+O(1),&\textnormal{non-const. loss},\IEEEstrut\end{cases}

(2)

where
n
is the dimension of the input vector, and the loss coefficient is considered as a function of frequency. As a result, there is only one signal degrees-of-freedom (DoF) at high powers (signal energy) due to signal-noise interaction. However, the model in
[
13
]
may not describe realistic fiber where
the distance is continuous.

The capacity of the discrete-time discrete-space SSFM model as a function of
SNR
and the number of segments in distance
K
is studied in
[
14
]
. It seems that the analysis in
[
14
]
suggests that if

and
\textnormal{SNR}\rightarrow\infty
,
the capacity is lower bounded by
\frac{1}{8}\log_{2}(1+\textnormal{SNR})+c
where

.

In this paper, we consider the SSFM model of optical fiber as a function of
K
and
SNR
. The contributions of the paper are as follows.

a
)

First, we show that when
K\geq\textnormal{SNR}^{2/3}
and
\textnormal{SNR}\rightarrow\infty
,
the off-diagonal terms in the random matrix
\mathsf{M}_{K}
in
[
13
]
, representing the stochastic inter-symbol interference (ISI), tend to zero due to the law of large numbers, and
\mathsf{M}_{K}
tends to a diagonal matrix with phase noise.
As a consequence, the capacity of the lossless continuous-space SSFM model is lower bounded as

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\textnormal{SNR})

\displaystyle\triangleq

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{K\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{C}(\textnormal{SNR},K)

(3)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\log_{2}\left(1+\textnormal{SNR}\right)-\frac{1}{2}+o(1),

where the term

tends to zero with
\textnormal{SNR}\rightarrow\infty
. This suggests that, unlike the discrete-space SSFM model where asymptotically there is only one DoF and the capacity is essentially finite (for large

1/2
. The pre-log in the lower bound when

is generally characterized in terms of
\delta
.

b)

Second, we consider the SSFM model when the nonlinearity parameter
\gamma\rightarrow\infty
.
It is shown that this channel is a fading channel for any
K
and
SNR
. As a result, when
K\rightarrow\infty
, the channel simplifies to
n
independent phase noise channels in the lossless case, with the capacity
\mathcal{C}(\textnormal{SNR})=\frac{1}{2}\log_{2}(1+\textnormal{SNR})-\frac{1}{2}+o(1)
.

c)

Third, we consider the lossless SSFM model in which the dispersion matrix does not depend on
K
. It is shown that when
K=\sqrt[\delta]{\textnormal{SNR}}
,
\delta>3
, and
\textnormal{SNR}\rightarrow\infty
, the capacity is
\frac{1}{2n}\log_{2}(1+\textnormal{SNR})+O(1)
.
In this case, there is one DoF asymptotically as in (
2
)
[
13
]
.

d)

Finally, we simulate the AIR of the SSFM model with back-propagation equalization. As previously observed, the AIR characteristically increases with
SNR
, reaching a peak at a certain optimal power, and then decreases as
SNR
is further increased (typically to near zero in WDM). The peak is attributed to a balance between noise and ISI. However, if the power is increased further, the AIR will increase again, approaching the
\frac{1}{2}\log(1+\textnormal{SNR})-\frac{1}{2}+o(1)
lower bound. The second ascent is because the ISI vanishes as

III
Split-step Fourier model

In this section, we consider a modified version of SSFM introduced in
[
13
]
. Here, the nonlinearity and noise steps are combined into one step, so that the influence of the additive amplified spontaneous emission (ASE) noise can be seen as phase noise. In what follows, SSFM refers to the modified SSFM.

III-A

Continuous-time model

Denote the complex envelope of the optical signal at distance
z
and time
t
by
Q(t,z)
. The propagation of the signal in single-mode optical fiber with distributed amplification is governed by the stochastic nonlinear Schrödinger (NLS) equation
[
13
, Eq. 2]

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{Q}}{\partial{z}}=L_{L}(Q)+L_{N}(Q)+N(t,z).

(9)

Here,
L_{L}
is the linear operator

\displaystyle L_{L}(Q)=-j\frac{\beta_{2}}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}Q}{\partial t^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{r}\convolution Q(t,z),

(10)

where
\beta_{2}
is the second-order chromatic dispersion coefficient,
\alpha_{r}(t)
is the residual attenuation coefficient (remained after imperfect amplification),
\convolution
is convolution resulting from the dependence of the attenuation coefficient with frequency, and
j=\sqrt{-1}
.
The operator

\displaystyle L_{N}(Q)=j\gamma|Q|^{2}Q

(11)

represents the Kerr nonlinearity, where
\gamma
is the nonlinearity parameter.
Finally,
N(t,z)
is zero-mean circularly-symmetric complex Gaussian noise process with covariance matrix

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[N(t,z)N^{*}(t^{\prime},z^{\prime})\right]=\tilde{\sigma}^{2}\delta_{\mathcal{B}}(t-t^{\prime})\delta(z-z^{\prime}),

(12)

where
\delta_{\mathcal{B}}(x)=\mathcal{B}\sinc(\mathcal{B}x)
,
\sinc(x)=\sin(\pi x)/(\pi x)
,
\mathcal{B}
is the noise bandwidth,

\delta(\cdot)
is the Dirac delta function, and
\tilde{\sigma}
is the power spectral density of the ASE noise. Denote
\sigma\triangleq\tilde{\sigma}\sqrt{\mathcal{B}}
.

The capacity results obtained in this paper are expected to hold for more general linear and nonlinear operators
L_{L}
and
L_{N}
that take into account other forms of dispersion and nonlinearity. However, we restrict the analysis to (
10
) and (
11
).

III-C

Transition from the continuous- to discrete-time model

The NLS equation (

K\rightarrow\infty
. Finally, we consider a potentially sub-optimal discretization where the output signal is sampled at
\mathcal{B}
. This corresponds to a receiver that ignores some of the samples that potentially carry information, and results in a discrete-time model in which the input output vectors have the same dimension.
Lower bounds obtained for this potentially sub-optimal receiver hold for better receivers as well.

III-D

Limitations of the discrete-time model

The discrete-time model considered in this paper has a number of limitations.
First, it considers signals with infinite bandwidth and does not account for a bandwidth constraint introduced by inline filters or receiver. Second, a receiver that samples the output signal at the input bandwidth ignores potentially useful samples. Third, the spectral efficiency (in bits/s/Hz) of the continuous-time model may not equal to the capacity (in bits/s) of the discrete-time model. This is because the spectral broadening factor may
increase with launch power due to nonlinearity
[
3
, Sec. VIII]
,
[
17
]
.

In this paper, we do not derive a rigorous one-to-one discretization of the continuous-time model (
9
).
We consider the discrete-time SSFM model, whose capacity may be different from that of (
9
).

VIII
Conclusion

The capacity of the discrete-time SSFM model of optical fiber is considered as a function of the average input signal power
SNR
, when the number of spatial segments in SSFM is sufficiently large as
K=\sqrt[\delta]{\textnormal{SNR}}
,
\delta>0
.

First, we obtained the capacity lower bound (
21
) and characterized the pre-log as a function of
\delta
.
In particular, we showed that
\mathcal{C}(\textnormal{SNR})\geq\frac{1}{2}\log_{2}\left(1+\textnormal{SNR}\right)-\frac{1}{2}+o(1)
, where
o(1)
vanishes as
\textnormal{SNR}\rightarrow\infty
.
As a result, the number of signal DoFs is at least half of the input dimension.

Second, it is shown that the
capacity of the continuous-space SSFM channel when
\gamma\rightarrow\infty
is
\frac{1}{2}\log_{2}(1+a\,\textnormal{SNR})+o(1)
. Hence, the number of signal DoFs is exactly half of the input dimension.

Third, we considered the SSFM model, named as infinite-dispersion, where the dispersion matrix in each segment does not depend on
K
.
It is shown that if
K=\sqrt[\delta]{\textnormal{SNR}}
,
\delta>3
, then
\mathcal{C}(\textnormal{SNR},\sqrt[\delta]{\textnormal{SNR}})=\frac{1}{2n}\log_{2}(1+\textnormal{SNR})+O(1)
, where
O(1)
term is bounded as
\textnormal{SNR}\rightarrow\infty
. Here, there is exactly one signal DoF.

Finally, AIRs of the SSFM model with back-propagation equalization are obtained numerically. The results show that while the AIR drops significantly in the medium
SNR
regime due to a considerable stochastic ISI, it asymptotically converges to
\frac{1}{2}\log_{2}\left(1+\textnormal{SNR}\right)-\frac{1}{2}+o(1)
, explained by the fact that ISI vanishes at high SNRs.

Haar Measure

This subsection is borrowed mainly from
[
25
]
. Haar measure can be seen as an extension of the notion of the uniform random variable over an interval.
The extension is based on the shift-invariant property of the uniform random variable. If
X\sim\mathcal{U}(0,a)
, then for any
b\in\mathbb{R}
,
\mod(X+b,a)\sim\mathcal{U}(0,a)
.

Consider defining uniform distribution on the circle
\mathcal{S}^{1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Considering a circle as a geometric object, a “uniform random point on the circle” should be a complex random variable whose distribution is rotation invariant; that is, if
A\subseteq\mathcal{S}^{1}
, then the probability of the random point lying on
A
should be the same as the probability that it lies on
e^{j\theta}A=\{e^{j\theta}a:a\in A\}
.

The uniform distribution
\mu
on a group
G
is called Haar measure on
G
, defined based on the“translation-invariant” property as follows.
For a group
(G,\cdot)
, an element
g\in G
, and a Borel subset
\mathcal{S}\subseteq G
, the left translation of
\mathcal{S}
by g is defined as

\displaystyle g\mathcal{S}=\{g\cdot s:s\in\mathcal{S}\}.

(123)

A measure
\mu
on the Borel subsets of
G
is called left translation-invariant if for all Borel subsets
\mathcal{S}\subseteq G
and all
g\in G
,

\displaystyle\mu(g\mathcal{S})=\mu(\mathcal{S}).

(124)

Right translation and right translation-invariant are defined similarly.

There exists a unique Haar measure on any group.
The following theorem is proved in
[
25
, Lemma 2.1.]
for
G=\mathbb{U}_{n}

Lemma 5

The proof is similar to the proof of the continuity of the output and conditional entropy in the zero-dispersion channel

The conditional PDF of one segment of SSFM is upper bounded

\displaystyle p(\mathbf{v}_{2}|\mathbf{x})\leq c^{\prime}_{1}\sqrt{K}e^{-Kc_{1}\|\mathbf{v}_{2}-\mathbf{x}\|^{2}},

\displaystyle\int p(\mathbf{v}_{3}|\mathbf{v}_{2})p(\mathbf{v}_{2}|\mathbf{x})d\mathbf{v}_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c^{\prime}_{2}\sqrt{\frac{K}{2}}e^{-c_{2}\frac{K}{2}\|\mathbf{v}_{3}-\mathbf{x}\|^{2}}.

The conditional PDF of
K

\displaystyle p(\mathbf{y}_{K}|\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Delta}}{{=}}

\displaystyle p(\mathbf{v}_{K+1}|\mathbf{x})

(131)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c^{\prime}_{k}e^{-c_{k}\|\mathbf{y}_{K}-\mathbf{x}\|^{2}}.

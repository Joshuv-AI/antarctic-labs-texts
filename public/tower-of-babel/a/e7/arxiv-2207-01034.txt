The multivariate Serre conjecture ring
Luc Guyot( ), Ihsen Yengui ( )

Abstract
It is well-known that for any commutative unitary ring 𝐑 {\bf R} , the Serre conjecture ring 𝐑 ​ ⟨ X ⟩ \mathbf{R}\langle X\rangle , i.e., the localization of the univariate polynomial ring 𝐑 ⁡ [ X ] \mathbf{R}[X] at monic polynomials, is a Bézout domain of Krull dimension ≤ 1 \leq 1 if so is 𝐑 {\bf R} . Consequently, defining by induction 𝐑 ⁡ ⟨ X 1 , … , X n ⟩ := ( 𝐑 ⁡ ⟨ X 1 , … , X n − 1 ⟩ ) ​ ⟨ X n ⟩ {\bf R}\langle{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}}\rangle:=({\bf R}\langle{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-1}}\rangle)\langle{X_{n}}\rangle , the ring 𝐑 ⁡ ⟨ X 1 , … , X n ⟩ {\bf R}\langle{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}}\rangle is a Bézout domain of Krull dimension ≤ 1 \leq 1 if so is 𝐑 {\bf R} . The fact that 𝐑 ⁡ ⟨ X 1 , … , X n ⟩ {\bf R}\langle{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}}\rangle is a Bézout domain when 𝐑 {\bf R} is a valuation domain of Krull dimension ≤ 1 \leq 1 was the cornerstone of Brewer and Costa’s theorem stating that if 𝐑 {\bf R} is a one-dimensional arithmetical ring then finitely generated projective 𝐑 ⁡ [ X 1 , … , X n ] \mathbf{R}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}] -modules are extended. It is also the key of the proof of the Gröbner Ring Conjecture in the lexicographic order case, namely the fact that for any valuation domain 𝐑 {\bf R} of Krull dimension ≤ 1 \leq 1 , any n ∈ ℕ > 0 n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0} , and any finitely generated ideal I I of 𝐑 ⁡ [ X 1 , … , X n ] {\bf R}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}] , the ideal LT ⁡ ( I ) \operatorname{LT}(I) generated by the leading terms of the elements of I I with respect to the lexicographic monomial order is finitely generated. Since the ring 𝐑 ⁡ ⟨ X 1 , … , X n ⟩ {\bf R}\langle{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}}\rangle can also be defined directly as the localization of the multivariate polynomial ring 𝐑 ⁡ [ X 1 , … , X n ] \mathbf{R}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}] at polynomials whose leading coefficients according to the lexicographic monomial order with X 1 < X 2 < ⋯ < X n X_{1}<X_{2}<\cdots<X_{n} is 1 1 , we propose to generalize the fact that 𝐑 ⁡ ⟨ X 1 , … , X n ⟩ {\bf R}\langle{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}}\rangle is a Bézout domain of Krull dimension ≤ 1 \leq 1 if so is 𝐑 {\bf R} to any rational monomial order, bolstering the evidence for the Gröbner Ring Conjecture in the rational case. We give an example showing that this result is no more true in the irrational case.

Introduction

This paper is written in the framework of Bishop style constructive mathematics (see
[
2
,
11
,
14
,
17
]
).
All the considered rings are supposed to be commutative, unitary,
and strongly discrete (i.e., equipped with a membership test to finitely generated ideals).

We will be working with monomial orders on the polynomial ring

\mathbf{R}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
over a ring
{\bf R}
. According to
[
7
, Theorem 1.2]
,
every monomial preorder
{\prec}
is given by a matrix

M\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
for some
m>0
in the following way:

X_{1}^{e_{1}}X_{2}^{e_{2}}\cdots X_{n}^{e_{n}}{\prec}\,X_{1}^{f_{1}}X_{2}^{f_{2}}\cdots X_{n}^{f_{n}}\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad M\cdot\begin{pmatrix}e_{1}\\
\vdots\\
e_{n}\end{pmatrix}<_{\cmttl{lex}}M\cdot\begin{pmatrix}f_{1}\\
\vdots\\
f_{n}\end{pmatrix}

(
e_{i},f_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
), where
\cmttl{lex}
denotes the lexicographic order.
A given matrix
M
defines a monomial preorder if and only if all
columns are nonzero and their first nonzero entry is positive. Notice
that different matrices may define the same monomial preorder. For
example, adding a multiple of a row of
M
to a lower row does not
change the preorder. By doing this repeatedly, we can replace
M
by a matrix
with nonnegative entries, so from now on we will assume that

M\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{m\times n}
. Let us call a preorder
rational
if it can be defined by a matrix with rational entries,
which can then be assumed to be nonnegative integers. All monomial
orders used in practice are rational. A rational preorder is a
monomial order (i.e., there are no ties between monomials) if and only
if
M
has rank
n
, so in this case we may assume
m=n
. By
contrast, irrational preorders can be orders even if
m<n
, for
example if
M
consists of a single row of real numbers that are
linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
. A monomial preorder is said to be
graded

if the first row of
M
has only positive entries. Graded rational monomial orders were used
in
[
8
]
to give an effective characterization of the valuative dimension.

The Gröbner ring conjecture (the updated version,
[
18
, page 535]
) :

A valuation domain
{\bf R}
has Krull dimension
\leq 1
if and only if for any
n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
,
for any rational monomial order
{\prec}
on
\mathbf{R}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
, and any finitely generated ideal
I
of
\mathbf{R}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
,
the ideal
\operatorname{LT}_{\prec}(I)
generated by the leading terms of the elements of
I
with respect to
\prec
is finitely generated.

It is well-known that for any ring
{\bf R}
, the Serre conjecture ring
\mathbf{R}\langle X\rangle
, i.e., the localization of the univariate polynomial ring
\mathbf{R}[X]
at monic polynomials, is a Bézout domain of Krull dimension
\leq 1
if so is
{\bf R}
.
Consequently, defining by induction
{\bf R}\langle{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}}\rangle:=({\bf R}\langle{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-1}}\rangle)\langle{X_{n}}\rangle
,
the ring
{\bf R}\langle{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}}\rangle
is a Bézout domain of Krull dimension
\leq 1
if so is
{\bf R}
.
The fact that
{\bf R}\langle{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}}\rangle
is a Bézout domain when
{\bf R}
is a valuation domain of Krull dimension
\leq 1
was
the cornerstone of Brewer and Costa theorem
[
3
, Corollary 3]
stating that if
{\bf R}
is
a one-dimensional arithmetical ring then finitely generated projective
\mathbf{R}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
-modules are extended on the one hand,
and on the other, the key of the proof of the Gröbner Ring Conjecture in the lexicographic order case
[
16
, Theorem 4]
.
Since the ring
{\bf R}\langle{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}}\rangle
can also be defined directly as the localization of
the multivariate polynomial ring
\mathbf{R}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
at polynomials whose leading coefficients according to the lexicographic monomial order with

X_{1}<X_{2}<\cdots<X_{n}
is
1
, we propose to generalize the aforementioned result to any rational monomial
order, bolstering the evidence for the Gröbner Ring Conjecture in the rational case.
We show with Example
31
below that this result is no more true in the irrational case.

1.2
The case of rational monomial orders

We now give four easy but important lemmas. For
matrix
M=(\alpha_{i,j})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n\times n}
, we define the
ring endomorphism

\phi_{M}\mbox{:}\ \mathbf{R}[X_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,X_{n}^{\pm 1}]\to\mathbf{R}[X_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,X_{n}^{\pm 1}],\,X_{i}\mapsto\prod_{j=1}^{n}X_{j}^{\alpha_{j,i}},

which restricts to an endomorphism

\phi_{M}\mbox{:}\ \mathbf{R}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]\to\mathbf{R}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]

whenever
M\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n\times n}
.

Lemma 6

Let
{\bf R}
be a ring and
\prec
be a rational monomial order on

\mathbf{R}[X_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,X_{n}^{\pm 1}]
, given by a matrix

M\in\mathbb{Z}^{n\times n}
. Then for

f\in\mathbf{R}[X_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,X_{n}^{\pm 1}]\setminus\{0\}
, we have

\phi_{M}(\operatorname{LT}_{\prec}(f))=\operatorname{LT}_{\cmttl{lex}}\bigl(\phi_{M}(f)\bigr).

This is because
\phi_{M}
is strictly increasing on monomials.

\Box

Notation 7

We denote by
\cmttl{lex}
the lexicographic monomial order with

X_{1}<X_{2}<\cdots<X_{n}
. Let
{\bf R}
be a ring and consider a monomial order
\prec
on
\mathbf{R}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
. The set

S_{\prec}:=\{f\in\mathbf{R}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]\;\mid\;\operatorname{LC}_{\prec}(f)=1\}

of
\prec
-monic polynomials is a multiplicative subset of the ring
\mathbf{R}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
. We set

\mathbf{R}_{\prec}\langle X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\rangle:=S_{{\prec}}^{-1}\mathbf{R}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}].

In case
n=1
, this ring is the same as the classical “Serre’s conjecture ring”
\mathbf{R}\langle X\rangle
(recall that in the univariate case the only
monomial order is the natural one).

Lemma 8

Let
{\bf R}
be a ring and
\prec
be a rational monomial order on
\mathbf{R}[X_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,X_{n}^{\pm 1}]
, given by a rank
n
matrix

M=(\alpha_{i,j})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n\times n}
. Let
k\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
be such that
L:=kM^{-1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n\times n}
and write
L=(\beta_{i,j})
.
Let
{\bf A}:=\mathbf{R}_{\prec}\langle X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\rangle
and
{\bf B}:=\mathbf{R}_{\cmttl{lex}}\langle X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\rangle
.
Then the maps
\left\{X_{i}\mapsto\prod_{j=1}^{n}X_{j}^{\alpha_{j,i}}\right\}
and

\left\{X_{i}\mapsto\prod_{j=1}^{n}X_{j}^{\beta_{j,i}}\right\}
induce injective ring homomorphisms

\phi_{M}:{\bf A}\to{\bf B}
and
\phi_{L}:{\bf B}\to{\bf A}
which satisfy

\phi_{M}\circ\phi_{L}=\phi_{k\cdot{\operatorname{Id}}_{n}}
and
\phi_{L}\circ\phi_{M}=\phi_{k\cdot{\operatorname{Id}}_{n}}
.

Since
S_{\prec}\cap S_{\cmttl{lex}}
contains the monomials of
\mathbf{R}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
, we have
{\bf A}=S_{\prec}^{-1}\mathbf{R}[X_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,X_{n}^{\pm 1}]
and
{\bf B}=S_{\cmttl{lex}}^{-1}\mathbf{R}[X_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,X_{n}^{\pm 1}]
.
As the homomorphism
\phi_{M}
of Lemma
6
satisfies
\phi_{M}(S_{\prec})\subseteq S_{\cmttl{lex}}
, it induces a homomorphism
A\rightarrow B

which we also denote, abusively, by
\phi_{M}
. The remaining assertions are readily checked.

\Box

Lemma 9

Let
{\bf R}
be a valuation domain, and
{\prec}
a rational monomial order on

\mathbf{R}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
given by a rank
n
matrix
M\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n\times n}
.
Then for any
f,g\in\mathbf{R}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]\setminus\{0\}
, there exists a monomial
{\cal N}\in\mathbf{R}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
such
that

\phi_{M}(S_{\prec}(f,g))={\cal N}\cdot S_{\cmttl{lex}}(\phi_{M}(f),\phi_{M}(g)).

More precisely,
{\cal N}=\frac{\phi_{M}\left(\operatorname{lcm}(\operatorname{LM}_{\prec}(f),\,\operatorname{LM}_{\prec}(g))\right)}{\operatorname{lcm}\left(\phi_{M}(\operatorname{LM}_{\prec}(f)),\,\phi_{M}(\operatorname{LM}_{\prec}(g))\right)}
.

Denote
\operatorname{LC}_{\prec}(f)=a
,
\operatorname{LC}_{\prec}(g)=b
,

{\cal M}=\operatorname{lcm}(\operatorname{LM}_{\prec}(f),\operatorname{LM}_{\prec}(g))
,
{\cal N}_{1}=\frac{{\cal M}}{\operatorname{LM}_{\prec}(f)}
,

{\cal N}_{2}=\frac{{\cal M}}{\operatorname{LM}_{\prec}(g)}
, and suppose that
b
divides
a
.
We have
S_{\prec}(f,g)={\cal N}_{1}\cdot f-\frac{a}{b}{\cal N}_{2}\cdot g
, and thus

\phi_{M}(S_{\prec}(f,g))=\phi_{M}({\cal N}_{1})\cdot\phi_{M}(f)-\frac{a}{b}\phi_{M}({\cal N}_{2})\cdot\phi_{M}(g)
.
Now, as
{\cal N}_{1}\cdot\operatorname{LM}_{\prec}(f)={\cal N}_{2}\cdot\operatorname{LM}_{\prec}(g)
,
we have
\phi_{M}({\cal N}_{1})\cdot\phi_{M}(\operatorname{LM}_{\prec}(f))=\phi_{M}({\cal N}_{2})\cdot\phi_{M}(\operatorname{LM}_{\prec}(g))
,
and thus, by virtue of Lemma
6
,
\phi_{M}({\cal N}_{1})\cdot\operatorname{LM}_{\cmttl{lex}}\bigl(\phi_{M}(f)\bigr)=\phi_{M}({\cal N}_{2})\cdot\operatorname{LM}_{\cmttl{lex}}\bigl(\phi_{M}(g)\bigr)=\phi_{M}({\cal M})
.
Since
\phi_{M}({\cal M})
is a common multiple of

\operatorname{LM}_{\cmttl{lex}}\bigl(\phi_{M}(f)\bigr)
and
\operatorname{LM}_{\cmttl{lex}}\bigl(\phi_{M}(g)\bigr)
,
it is divisible by
\operatorname{lcm}(\operatorname{LM}_{\cmttl{lex}}\bigl(\phi_{M}(f)\bigr),\operatorname{LM}_{\cmttl{lex}}\bigl(\phi_{M}(g)\bigr))=:{\cal M}^{\prime}
.
Setting
{\cal N}:=\frac{\phi_{M}({\cal M})}{{\cal M}^{\prime}}
, we have
\phi_{M}(S_{\prec}(f,g))={\cal N}\cdot S_{\cmttl{lex}}(\phi_{M}(f),\phi_{M}(g))
.

\Box

.
Setting

(2)
Reason as in

(3)

Lemma 27

Let
{\bf R}
be a ring and let
\prec
be a monomial order on
\mathbf{R}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
.
Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

{\bf R}
is a gcd domain.

(ii)

\mathbf{R}_{\prec}\langle X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\rangle
is a gcd domain.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
. The ring
\mathbf{R}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
is a gcd domain by
[
11
, Theorem XI.3.16]
and so is
\mathbf{R}_{\prec}\langle X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\rangle

because the gcd property is stable under taking localization
[
11
, Fact XI.3.6]
.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
.
Let
{\bf A}:=\mathbf{R}_{\prec}\langle X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\rangle
and let
a,b\in{\bf R}
.
Assume that
\gcd_{{\bf A}}(a,b)=\frac{f}{s}
holds in
{\bf A}
with
f,s\in\mathbf{R}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
and
\operatorname{LC}(s)=1
.
Let
c:=\operatorname{LC}(f)
.
We shall prove that
\gcd_{{\bf R}}(a,b)=c
holds in
{\bf R}
. Let
d
be a common divisor of
a
and
b
in
{\bf R}
.
Since
d
divides
\frac{f}{s}
in
\mathbf{R}_{\prec}\langle X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\rangle
, we can find
f^{\prime},s^{\prime}\in\mathbf{R}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
such that
\operatorname{LC}(s^{\prime})=1
and
\frac{f}{s}=d\frac{f^{\prime}}{s^{\prime}}
.
Using the identity
\operatorname{LC}(s^{\prime}f)=\operatorname{LC}(sdf^{\prime})
, we infer that
d
divides
c
in
{\bf R}
.
We can show in the same way that
c
divides both
a
and
b
in
{\bf R}
, which proves the result.

Lemma 28

Let
{\bf R}
be gcd domain and let

be a rational monomial order on

n

(1)

\gcd(f,g)=1

\mathbf{R}_{\prec}\langle X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\rangle
.

(2)

\gcd(\phi_{M}(f),\phi_{M}(g))=1
holds in
\mathbf{R}_{\cmttl{lex}}\langle X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\rangle

Because of Lemma
6
, we can assume, without loss of generality that
f,g\in\mathbf{R}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
.
Let

and
L
be as in Lemma
8
.
We set
{\bf A}:=\mathbf{R}_{\prec}\langle X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\rangle
and

. Let

and
\phi_{M}(g)

.
As we aim at proving that

.
As a result,

\gcd(\phi_{M}(f),\phi_{M}(g))=1

\phi_{M}(uh)
a

-monic polynomial for some unit

Proposition 29

Let
{\bf R}
be a ring and let
\prec
be a rational monomial order on
\mathbf{R}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
.
Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\mathbf{R}_{\prec}\langle X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\rangle
is a Bézout domain.

(2)

\mathbf{R}_{\cmttl{lex}}\langle X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\rangle
is a Bézout domain.

Let
M\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n\times n}
be a rank
n
matrix defining
\prec
.
Let
k\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
and
L
be as in Lemma
8
and
set
{\bf A}:=\mathbf{R}_{\prec}\langle X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\rangle
,
{\bf B}:=\mathbf{R}_{\cmttl{lex}}\langle X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\rangle
.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)

By Lemma
27
, the ring
{\bf B}
is a gcd domain.
Hence, in order to show that
{\bf B}
is Bézout, it suffices to show
that for every pair
f,g\in{\bf B}
satisfying
\gcd(f,g)=1
in
{\bf B}
,
we have
\langle f,g\rangle_{{\bf B}}={\bf B}
.
We may assume, without loss of generality, that
f,g\in\mathbf{R}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
and that
\gcd(f,g)=1
holds in
\mathbf{R}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
.
Let
\tilde{f}=\phi_{k\cdot{\operatorname{Id}}_{n}}(f)
and
\tilde{g}=\phi_{k\cdot{\operatorname{Id}}_{n}}(g)
.
By Lemma
26
(4)
, we know that

\gcd(\tilde{f},\tilde{g})=1
holds in
\mathbf{R}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
and hence in
{\bf A}
.
Thus
\gcd(\phi_{L}(f),\phi_{L}(g))=1
holds in
{\bf A}
by Lemma
28
.
Since
{\bf A}
is a Bézout domain by hypothesis, the identity

\langle\phi_{L}(f),\phi_{L}(g)\rangle_{{\bf A}}={\bf A}
is satisfied.
Thus
1=\phi_{M}(1)\in\langle\tilde{f},\tilde{g}\rangle_{{\bf B}}
so that

\langle\tilde{f},\tilde{g}\rangle_{{\bf B}}={\bf B}
.
Applying Lemma
26
(2)
completes the proof.

(2)\Rightarrow(1)

By Lemma
27
, we know that
{\bf A}
is a gcd domain.
We consider
f,g\in{\bf A}
such that
\gcd(f,g)=1
holds in
{\bf A}
,
setting ourselves to show that
\langle f,g\rangle_{{\bf A}}={\bf A}
.
We may assume, without loss of generality, that
f,g\in\mathbf{R}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
.
Thanks to Corollary
13
, we only need to check that

\langle\phi_{M}(f),\phi_{M}(g)\rangle_{{\bf B}}={\bf B}
.
As
{\bf B}
is a Bézout domain by hypothesis,
the latter identity follows from Lemma
28
.

\Box

The following corollary is obtained by means of a standard localization argument.

Corollary 30

Let

(ii)

\mathbf{R}_{\cmttl{lex}}\langle X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\rangle

Combine Propositions
16
,
20

Example 31

Let’s take again the counterexample given in
[
18
]
and consider
a valuation domain
{\bf V}
whose valuation group is
G=(\mathbb{Z}+\mathbb{Z}\sqrt{2},\;+)
. Denote by
{\mathsf{v}}

the valuation corresponding to
{\bf V}
. Since
G
is a subgroup of
(\mathbb{R},\;+)
, it is archimedean and thus
\dim{\bf V}=1
. Let us pick
a,\;b\in{\bf V}
such that
{\mathsf{v}}(a)=1
and
{\mathsf{v}}(b)=\sqrt{2}
, and consider the ideal
J=\langle{-1+aX,\;-1+bY}\rangle
of
{\bf V}[X,Y]
. Let us equip
{\bf V}[X,Y]
with the irrational monomial order
{{\prec}}
given by the row matrix
(1\;\;\sqrt{2})
.
We know by Claim 2 of the counterexample given in
[
18
]
that
J\cap S_{\prec}=\emptyset

and thus there is no Bézout identity between
-1+aX
and
-1+bY
in
{\bf V}_{\prec}\langle{X,Y}\rangle
despite that
{\rm gcd}(-1+aX,\;-1+bY)=1
. We conclude that
{\bf V}_{\prec}\langle{X,Y}\rangle
is not a Bézout domain contrary to
{\bf V}_{\cmttl{lex}}\langle{X,Y}\rangle
. Since
{\bf V}_{\prec}\langle{X,Y}\rangle

\leq 1

{\bf V}_{\prec}\langle{X,Y}\rangle

.

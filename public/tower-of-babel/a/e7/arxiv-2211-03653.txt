Approximation algorithms for Node-weighted Steiner Problems: Digraphs with Additive Prizes and Graphs with Submodular Prizes
Gianlorenzo D’Angelo, Esmaeil Delfaraz

Abstract
In the budgeted rooted node-weighted Steiner tree problem, we are given a graph G G with n n nodes, a predefined node r r , two weights associated to each node modelling costs and prizes, and a real-valued budget B B . The aim is to find a tree in G G rooted at r r such that the total cost of its nodes is at most B B and the total prize is maximized. In the quota rooted node-weighted Steiner tree problem, we are given a real-valued quota Q Q , instead of the budget, and we aim at minimizing the cost of a tree rooted at r r whose overall prize is at least Q Q . In this paper, we study some relevant generalizations of the above problems, namely we consider separately (i) directed graphs (with additive prize function), and (ii) monotone submodular prize functions over subsets of nodes (in undirected graphs). For the budgeted problem, Ghuge and Nagarajan [SODA 2020], and D’Angelo, Delfaraz and Gilbert [ISAAC 2022] proposed an optimal quasi-polynomial time O ⁡ ( log ⁡ n ′ log ⁡ log ⁡ n ′ ) O\left(\frac{\log n^{\prime}}{\log\log n^{\prime}}\right) -approximation algorithm and a polynomial time bicriteria ( 1 + ε , O ⁡ ( 1 ε 3 ​ B ) ) (1+\varepsilon,O(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{3}}\sqrt{B})) -approximation algorithm, respectively, for the case in which the graph is directed, the costs are restricted to be positive integer valued and are associated to the edges, and the prize function is a general monotone submodular function over subsets of nodes, where n ′ n^{\prime} is the number of vertices in an optimal solution and ε ∈ ( 0 , 1 ] \varepsilon\in(0,1] . For scenario (i) , we develop a technique resorting on a standard flow-based linear programming relaxation to compute a tree with good trade-off between prize and cost, which allows us to provide very simple polynomial time approximation algorithms for both the budgeted and the quota problems. For the budgeted problem, our algorithm achieves an O ⁡ ( 1 ε 2 ​ n 2 / 3 ​ ln ⁡ n ) O(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}n^{2/3}\ln{n}) -approximation at the cost of a budget violation of a factor of at most 1 + ε 1+\varepsilon , for any ε ∈ ( 0 , 1 ] \varepsilon\in(0,1] . For the quota problem, our algorithm guarantees an approximation factor of O ⁡ ( n 2 / 3 ​ ln ⁡ n ) O(n^{2/3}\ln{n}) at the cost of a violation of the quota constraint by a factor of at most 2 2 . Our algorithms work for nonnegative real-valued costs and are the first non-trivial polynomial time algorithms with approximation factors depending on n n . The same technique can be used to find an approximation algorithm for the node-weighted directed Steiner tree problem ( DSteinerT ). Recently, Li and Laekhanukit [SODA 2022] ruled out poly-logarithmic approximation algorithms for DSteinerT based on a standard flow-based linear programming relaxation. By using the same linear relaxation, we provide a surprisingly simple polynomial time O ⁡ ( ( 1 + ε ) ​ n ​ ln ⁡ n ) O((1+\varepsilon)\sqrt{n}\ln{n}) -approximation algorithm for DSteinerT , for any ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 . For scenario (ii) , we provide a polynomial time O ⁡ ( 1 ε 3 ​ n ​ log ⁡ n ) O(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{3}}\sqrt{n}\log{n}) -approximation algorithm for the budgeted problem that violates the budget constraint by a factor of at most 1 + ε 1+\varepsilon , for any ε ∈ ( 0 , 1 ] \varepsilon\in(0,1] . Also in this case we introduce a general technique that exploits a flow-based linear program to find trees with a good trade-off between prize and cost, which allows us to provide a good approximation also for the quota problem.



1
Introduction

Prize Collecting Steiner Tree
(
PCST
) refers to a wide class of combinatorial optimization problems, involving variants of the
Steiner tree
and
traveling sales man
problems, with many practical applications in computer and telecommunication networks, VLSI design, computational geometry, wireless mesh networks, and cancer genome studies
[
CLDN04
,
GLW+19
,
HR20
,
KLT15
,
VUR11
]
.

In general, we are given a (directed) graph
G
, two functions modelling costs and prizes (or penalties) associated to the edges and/or to the nodes of the graph, and we want to find a connected subgraph of
G
(usually a tree or an out-tree)
T
which optimizes an objective function defined as a combination of its costs and prizes and/or is subject to some constraints on its cost and prize. Casting suitable constraints and objective functions give rise to different problems.
For example, in
budgeted
problems, we are given a budget
B
and we require that the cost of
T
is no more than
B
and its prize is maximized. In
quota
problems, we require the prize of
T
to be at least some quota
Q
and its cost to be minimum.
Additional constraints can be required, for example in
rooted
variants we are given a specific node, called
root
, which is required to be part of
T
and reach all the nodes in
T
, while in
Steiner tree
problems
T
must include a specified set of nodes called
terminals
.

While there is a vast literature providing
approximation algorithms for many variants of
PCST
on undirected graphs where the prize function is additive, e.g.
[
ABHK11
,
BHL18
,
Gar05
,
GMNS99
,
GW95
,
JMP00
,
KSS13
,
PFF+20
]
, the case of directed graphs or monotone submodular prize functions received less attention
[
CCC+99
,
DDG22
,
GN20
,
KLT15
,
Zel97
]
.

In this paper, we consider
node-weighted
Steiner tree problems, that is both costs and prizes are associated to the nodes of the graph, and we investigate two relevant settings: (i) the underlying graph is
directed
and the prize function is
additive
, and (ii) the underlying graph is
undirected
and the prize function is
monotone and submodular
.
In both settings, we consider budgeted and quota problems. For the first setting we also study the minimum-cost Steiner tree problem. We consider the more general rooted variant of all these problems. For each of the above two settings, we introduce a new technique, resorting on flow-based linear programming relaxations, which allows us to find trees or out-trees with a good trade-off between cost and prize. Casting suitable values of quota and budget and applying new and known tree trimming procedures, we can achieve good bicriteria approximation ratios for all the above problems.

In the following we analyze settings (i) and (ii) separately.

1.1
Directed Graphs and Additive Prizes

Prize collecting Steiner trees are usually much harder on directed graphs than on undirected graphs. A well-known example is the
Steiner tree
problem, for which there is a simple polynomial time
2
-approximation algorithm for its undirected version, but for its directed version, unless
NP\subseteq\bigcap_{0<\varepsilon<1}\text{ZPTIME}(2^{n^{\varepsilon}})
or the Projection Game Conjecture is false, there is no quasi-polynomial time algorithm that achieves an approximation ratio of
o(\frac{\log^{2}k}{\log\log k})

[
GLL19
]
, where
k
is the number of terminal nodes.

We consider three variants of the node-weighted version of
PCST
in this scenario in which we are given a directed graph
D=(V,A)
with
|V|=n
, two nonnegative functions, namely, cost
c(v)
and prize
p(v)
that are associated to each vertex
v\in V
, and a root vertex
r
.

The Budgeted Rooted Additive Tree problem (
B-DRAT
).

Given a budget
B
, in
B-DRAT
, we aim to find an out-tree (a.k.a. out-arborescence)
T
of
D
rooted at
r
such that the sum of the costs of its vertices is at most
B
and the sum of the prizes of its vertices is maximum.

[
GMNS99
]
introduced the undirected version of
B-DRAT
, we call this problem
B-URAT
. They gave a polynomial time
O(\log^{2}n)
-approximation algorithm that violates the budget constraint by a factor of at most
2
. The approximation factor was improved to
O(\log n)
, with the same budget violation, by Moss and Rabani
[
MR07
]
. Bateni, Hajiaghay and Liaghat
[
BHL18
]
later improved the budget violation factor to
1+\varepsilon
to obtain an approximation factor of
O\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\log n\right)
, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
.
Kortsarz and Nutov
[
KN11
]
showed that the unrooted version of
B-URAT
, so does
B-URAT
, admits no
o(\log\log n)
-approximation algorithm, unless
NP\subseteq DTIME(n^{\text{polylog}(n)})
, even if the algorithm is allowed to violate the budget constraint by a factor equal to a universal constant.

Ghuge and Nagarajan
[
GN20
]
provided a tight quasi-polynomial time
O(\frac{\log n^{\prime}}{\log\log n^{\prime}})
-approximation algorithm for the edge-cost version of
B-DRAT
that requires
(n\log B)^{O(\log^{1+\varepsilon}n^{\prime})}
time, where
n^{\prime}
is the number of vertices in an optimal solution and the edge costs are positive integers. Very recently, D’Angelo, Delfaraz and Gilbert
[
DDG22
]
provided a polynomial time
O\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\sqrt{B}\right)
-approximation algorithm for the same problem that violates the budget constraint by a factor of
1+\varepsilon
, where
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
. Bateni, Hajiaghay and Liaghat
[
BHL18
]
showed that the integrally gap of the flow-based LP for
B-URAT
, so is for
B-DRAT
, is unbounded.

In this paper we show that, by using this flow-based LP and violating the budget constraint by a factor of at most
1+\varepsilon
, for
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
, one can achieve a very simple polynomial time
O\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}n^{2/3}\ln{n}\right)
-approximation algorithm for
B-DRAT
where costs and prizes are non-negative real numbers.

The Quota Rooted Additive Tree problem (
Q-DRAT
).

Given a quota
Q
, in
Q-DRAT
, we aim to find an out-tree
T
of
D
rooted at
r
whose total prize is at least
Q
and total cost is minimum. To the best of
our knowledge, this problem has not been studied explicitly before.

On undirected graphs for
Q-DRAT
, which is called
Q-URAT
, by using the algorithm by Moss and Rabbani
[
MR07
]
along with the ideas of Könemann and Sadeghian and Sanità
[
KSS13
]
, and Bateni, Hajiaghay and Liaghat
[
BHL18
]
, one can provide an
O(\log{n})
-approximation algorithm.

Here, by using the flow-based LP, we present a very simple
O((1+\varepsilon)n^{2/3}\ln{n})
-approximation algorithm for
Q-DRAT
that violates the quota constraint by a factor of at most
2
and runs in a time that is polynomial in the input size and in
1/\varepsilon
, for any
\varepsilon>0
.

The Node-Weighted Directed Steiner Tree problem (
DSteinerT
).

In
DSteinerT
, we are given a set of terminals
K\subseteq V
and the goal is to find an out-tree
T
of
D
rooted at
r
spanning the set
K
whose total cost of vertices is minimum.

Zelikovsky
[
Zel97
]
provided the first approximation algorithm for the edge-cost version of
DSteinerT
, we term this problem
E-DSteinerT
, with factor
O(k^{\varepsilon}(\log^{1/\varepsilon}{k}))
that runs in
O(n^{1/\varepsilon})
, where
|K|=k
. A follow-up work by
[
CCC+99
]
proposed a better approximation algorithm with a factor
O(\log^{3}{k})
in quasi-polynomial time. This factor was improved to
O(\frac{\log^{2}k}{\log\log k})
by the randomized algorithm of Grandoni, Laekhanukit, and Li.
[
GLL19
]
in
n^{O(\log^{5}k)}
time. Later, Ghuge and Nagarajan
[
GN20
]
proposed a deterministic
O(\frac{\log^{2}k}{\log\log k})
-approximation algorithm for
E-DSteinerT
in
n^{O(\log^{1+\varepsilon}k)}
time. Recently, Li and Laekhanukit
[
LL22
]
ruled out poly-logarithmic approximation algorithms for
E-DSteinerT
using the standard flow-based LP. This result holds for
DSteinerT
too as, in the instance in
[
LL22
]
, the incoming edges of each vertex have the same cost.

Using this standard flow-based LP, in this work, we show that one can achieve a very simple
O((1+\varepsilon)\sqrt{n}\ln{n})
-approximation algorithm for
DSteinerT
, that requires a time that is polynomial in the input size and in
1/\varepsilon
, for any
\varepsilon>0
.

Our algorithm does not improve over the best known algorithms in terms of approximation ratio, however it is much simpler and provides a guarantee over the optimum of the standard flow-based linear programming relaxation, which allows us to use it as a black-box to approximate other problems. As an example, we show that it can be used to approximate
B-DRAT
and
Q-DRAT
with factors

O(\frac{n^{3/4}\ln{n}}{\varepsilon^{2}})
and

O(n^{3/4}\ln{n})
and constraint violation of
1+\varepsilon
and 2, respectively, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
.

1.2
Undirected Graphs and Submodular Prizes

There are only a few papers that provide approximation algorithms for some variants of
PCST
on undirected graphs when the prize function is monotone submodular
[
DDG22
,
HKKN12
,
KPS20
,
KLT15
,
LSZ21
,
RZKL16
]
.

We consider two variants of node-weighted
PCST
in this scenario.
Let
G=(V,E)
be an undirected graph with
n
nodes,
c:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{\geq 0}
be a cost function on nodes,
p:2^{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{\geq 0}
be a monotone submodular prize function on subsets of nodes and
r\in V
be a root vertex.

The Budgeted Undirected Rooted Submodular problem (
B-URST
).

Given a budget
B\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, in
B-URST
we aim to find a tree
T
of
G
including
r
that costs at most
B
and maximizes the prize function.

Kuo, Lin, and Tsai
[
KLT15
]
studied the unrooted version of
B-URST
, when all node costs are positive integers. They provided an
O(\Delta\sqrt{B})
-approximation algorithm, where
\Delta
is the maximum degree of the graph. Very recently, this factor was improved to
O(\sqrt{B})
by D’Angelo, Delfaraz and Gilbert
[
DDG22
]
. The authors in
[
DDG22
]
also provided a bicriteria
O(1+\varepsilon,\sqrt{B}/\varepsilon^{3})
for
B-URST
(i.e. in the rooted case) when all node costs are positive integers. Ghuge and Nagarajan
[
GN20
]
provided a tight quasi-polynomial time
O(\frac{\log n^{\prime}}{\log\log n^{\prime}})
-approximation algorithm for
B-URST
on directed graphs that requires
(n\log B)^{O(\log^{1+\varepsilon}n^{\prime})}
time, where
n^{\prime}
is the number of vertices in an optimal solution and the edge costs are positive integers.

In this paper, we provide a new flow-based linear programming formulation that leads to a polynomial time
O(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{3}}\sqrt{n}\log{n})
-approximation algorithm for
B-URST
violating the budget constraint by a factor of at most
1+\varepsilon
, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
.

The Quota Undirected Rooted Submodular problem (
Q-URST
).

Given a quota
Q\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, in
Q-URST
, we aim to find a tree
T
of
G
including
r
such that the prize function of
T
is at least
Q
and the cost of
T
is minimum. To the best of our knowledge, this problem has not been studied explicitly before.

We present an algorithm that finds trees with a good trade-off between prize and cost, which allows us to provide a good approximation for
Q-URST
.

1.3
Summary of the Results

In the first step of our study, we present a surprisingly simple technique that yields a polynomial time
O((1+\varepsilon)\sqrt{n}\ln{n})
-approximation algorithm for
DSteinerT
, for any
\varepsilon>0
. Then we show that using the core idea of our approach for
DSteinerT
, one can achieve very simple approximation algorithms for
Q-DRAT
and
B-DRAT
. Our algorithm for
Q-DRAT
guarantees an
O((1+\varepsilon)n^{2/3}\ln{n})
-approximation that violates the quota constraint by a factor of at most
2
, for any
\varepsilon>0
. Our algorithm for
B-DRAT
achieves an approximation factor of
O(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}n^{2/3}\ln{n})
with
1+\varepsilon
budget violation, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
.

In the next step, we introduce a novel LP relaxation for
B-URST
and
Q-URST
. By using it, we show that one can achieve a polynomial time
O(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{3}}\sqrt{n}\log{n})
-approximation algorithm for
B-URST
, violating the budget constraint by a factor of at most
1+\varepsilon
, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
. Last, by using our LP, we show that for
Q-URST
, our technique achieves either (i) an
O(\sqrt{n}\log{n})
-approximation that violates the quota constraint by a factor of at most
2
, or (ii) a
(1+\varepsilon)
-approximation algorithm that violates the quota constraint by a factor of at most
2\sqrt{n}
, for any
\varepsilon>0
.

1.4
Further Related Work

Here we describe those variants of prize collecting Steiner tree problems that are more closely related to our study.

[
CCC+99
]
provided a polynomial time
\tilde{O}(k^{2/3})
-approximation algorithm for
k
-Directed Steiner Forest (
k-
DSF
) which is a more general version of the directed Steiner problem, where the goal is to find a minimum cost sugraph that connects at least
k
predetermined pairs. Feldman, Kortsarz, and Nutov
[
FKN12
]
improved this bound to
O(k^{1/2+\varepsilon})
for any fixed
\varepsilon>0
. A well-known variant of
k-
DSF
is
DSF
in which the goal is to find a minimum cost subgraph that contains a path between every prescribed pair.
[
CEGS11
]
provided an
O(k^{1/2+\varepsilon})
-approximation algorithm for
DSF
. In terms of the number of vertices
n
, Feldman, Kortsarz, and Nutov
[
FKN12
]
gave an
O(n^{\varepsilon}\cdot\min\{n^{4/5},m^{2/3}\})
-approximation algorithm for

which was improved by
[
BBM+11
]
to
O(n^{2/3+\varepsilon})

\varepsilon>0
. Later,
[
CDKL20

proposed an
O(n^{3/5+\varepsilon})
-approximation algorithm for

Hochbaum and Rao
[
HR20
]
investigated

in which each vertex costs
1
and provided an approximation algorithm with factor
\min\{1/((1-1/e)(1/R-1/B)),B\}

R
is the radius of the graph.
[
CCZ+20
]
investigated the edge-cost version of
B-URST
. The authors in
[
VUR11
]
proposed a
(\frac{2e-1}{e-1}R)
-approximation algorithm for a special case of

In the connected maximum coverage problem, we are given a graph in which each vertex represents a subset of elements and is associated with some nonnative cost, and each element is associated with some prize. The goal here is to find a connected subgraph whose total cost is bounded by a given budget and prize is maximum.

RZKL16

presented an
O(\Delta\log{n})
-approximation algorithm for a special case of the connected maximum coverage problem, where

[
DDG22

-approximation algorithm for the connected maximum coverage problem, where

Notation and problem statement

For an integer
s
, let
[s]:=\{1,\ldots,s\}
. Let
G=(V,E)
(resp.
D=(V,A)
) be an undirected graph (resp. a directed graph) with a distinguished vertex
r\in V
and
c:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{\geq 0}
be a nonnegative cost function on nodes.

A
path
is an undirected graph made of a sequence of distinct vertices
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{s}\}
and a sequence of edges
\{v_{i},v_{i+1}\}
,
i\in[s-1]
. A
tree
is an undirected graph in which any two vertices are connected by exactly one path. If a subgraph
T
of a graph
G
is a tree, then we say that
T
is a tree of
G
.

A
directed path
is a directed graph made of a sequence of distinct vertices
(v_{1},\dots,v_{s})
and a sequence of directed edges
(v_{i},v_{i+1})
,
i\in[s-1]
.
An
out-tree
(a.k.a. out-arborescence) is a directed graph in which there is exactly one directed path from a specific vertex
r
, called
root
, to each other vertex. If a subgraph
T
of a directed graph
D
is an out-tree, then we say that
T
is an out-tree of
D
.
For simplicity of reading, we will refer to out-trees simply as trees when it is clear that we are in the context of directed graphs.

Given two nodes
u
and
v
in
V
, the cost of a path from
u
to
v
in
G
(resp.
D
) is the sum of the cost of its nodes. A path from
u
to
v
with the minimum cost is called a
shortest path
and its cost, denoted by
dist(u,v)
, is called the
distance
from
u
to
v
in
G
(resp.
D
). Let
F
denote the maximum distance from
r
to a node in
V
,
F:=\max_{v\in V}\{dist(r,v)\}
.

For any subgraph
G^{\prime}
of
G
(resp.
D^{\prime}
of
D
), we denote by
V(G^{\prime})
and
E(G^{\prime})
(resp.
V(D^{\prime})
and
A(D^{\prime})
) the set of nodes and edges in
G^{\prime}
(resp.
D^{\prime}
), respectively. Consider a subset
S\subseteq V
.
G[S]
(resp.
D[S]
) denotes the graph induced by set
S
, i.e.,
E(G[S])=\{\{u,v\}\in E|u,v\in S\}
(resp.
A(D[S])=\{(u,v)\in A|u,v\in S\}
).

Now we state the problems we consider on directed and undirected graphs.

Directed Graphs.

Let
D=(V,A)
be a directed graph with
n
nodes,
c:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{\geq 0}
be a cost function on nodes,
p:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{\geq 0}
be a prize function on nodes,
r\in V
be a root vertex. We consider three variants of node-weighted Steiner problems.

1.

The Budgeted Directed Rooted Additive Tree problem (
B-DRAT
): Given a budget
B\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, find an out-tree
T
of
D
rooted at
r
that maximizes
p(T)=\sum_{v\in V(T)}p(v)
subject to
c(T)=\sum_{v\in V(T)}c(v)\leq B
.

2.

The Quota Directed Rooted Additive Tree problem (
Q-DRAT
): Given a quota
Q\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, find an out-tree
T
of
D
rooted at
r
that minimizes
c(T)=\sum_{v\in V(T)}c(v)
subject to
p(T)=\sum_{v\in V(T)}p(v)\geq Q
.

3.

The node-weighted Directed Steiner Tree problem (
DSteinerT
): Given a set of terminals
K\subseteq V
, find an out-tree
T
of
D
rooted at
r
such that
K\subseteq V(T)
and
c(T)=\sum_{v\in V(T)}c(v)
is minimum.

Undirected Graphs.

Let
G=(V,E)
be an undirected graph with
n
nodes,
c:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{\geq 0}
be a cost function on nodes,
p:2^{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{\geq 0}
be a monotone non-decreasing submodular prize function on subsets of nodes,
r\in V
be a root vertex. We consider two variants of node-weighted Steiner problems.

1.

The Budgeted Undirected Rooted Submodular problem (
B-URST
): Given a budget
B\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, find a tree
T
of
G
that maximizes
p(T)
subject to
r\in V(T)
and
c(T)=\sum_{v\in V(T)}c(v)\leq B
.

2.

The Quota Undirected Rooted Submodular problem (
Q-URST
): Given a quota
Q\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, find a tree
T
of
G
that minimizes
c(T)=\sum_{v\in V(T)}c(v)
subject to
r\in V(T)
and
p(T)\geq Q
.

Consider a budgeted problem
BP

\in\{\text{{B-DRAT}, {B-URST} }\}
(resp. a quota problem
QP

\in\{\text{{Q-DRAT}, {Q-URST} }\}
) with a budget
B
(resp. quota
Q
). For
\alpha,\beta\geq 1
, a bicriteria
(\beta,\alpha)
-approximation algorithm for
BP
(resp.
QP
) is one that, for any instance
I_{B}
(resp.
I_{Q}
) of the problem, returns a solution
Sol_{I_{B}}
(resp.
Sol_{I_{Q}}
) such that
p(Sol_{I_{B}})\geq\frac{OPT_{I_{B}}}{\alpha}
(resp.
c(Sol_{I_{Q}})\leq\alpha OPT_{I_{Q}}
) and
c(Sol_{I_{B}})\leq\beta B
(resp.
p(Sol_{I_{Q}})\geq\frac{Q}{\beta}
), where
OPT_{I_{B}}
(resp.
OPT_{I_{Q}}
) is the optimum for
I_{B}
(resp.
I_{Q}
).

3
Results and Techniques

In this section, we summarize our results and give an overview of the new techniques.
We distinguish between problems on directed and undirected graphs.

3.1
Directed Graphs

We introduce a new technique to compute a tree with bounded cost and prize and we use it in all our algorithms for the problems on directed graphs. Since our algorithm for
DSteinerT
presents our main technique in a more simpler way, in the following, we describe the core idea behind such technique by using the case of
DSteinerT
as an illustrative example.

The next theorem, whose full proof is given in Section
4
, provides a polynomial time approximation algorithm for
DSteinerT
.

Theorem 3.1
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
,
DSteinerT
admits an
O\left((1+\varepsilon)\sqrt{n}\ln{n}\right)
-approximation algorithm whose running time is polynomial in the input size and in
1/\varepsilon
.

Let
I=<D=(V,A),c,r,K>
be an instance of
DSteinerT
. First note that we can suppose w.l.o.g. that any vertex
v\in V
has a distance no more than
(1+\varepsilon)c(T^{*})
from
r
for any
\varepsilon>0
, where
T^{*}
is an optimal solution to
DSteinerT
.

For every
v\in V
, we let
\mathcal{P}_{v}
be the set of simple paths in
D
from
r
to
v
. For each
v\in V
, let
c_{v}=c(v)
.
We use the standard flow-based linear programming relaxation for
DSteinerT
, which is as follows.

(LP-DST)

(1)

\displaystyle\text{minimize}\quad\sum_{v\in V}x_{v}c_{v}

(2)

\displaystyle\text{subject to}\quad\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{t}}f^{t}_{P}

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle\forall t\in K

(3)

\displaystyle\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{t}:v\in P}f^{t}_{P}

\displaystyle\leq x_{v},

\displaystyle\forall v\in V,t\in K

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle x_{v}\leq 1,

\displaystyle\forall v\in V

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle f^{t}_{P}\leq 1,

\displaystyle\forall t\in K,P\in\mathcal{P}_{t}

For any terminal
t\in K
and path
P\in\mathcal{P}_{t}
,
f^{t}_{P}
denotes the amount of flow sent from
r
to
t
using
P
. Constraint (
2
) ensures that for any
t\in K
, the total amount of flow sent from
r
to
t
has to be equal to
1
. Constraint (
3
) ensures that for any
t\in K
, the total amount flow sent from
r
to
t
passing through a vertex
v
is at most
x_{v}
for any
v\in V
. Note that, although the number of variables in (
LP-DST
) is exponential in the input size, we can find an optimal solution in polynomial time as we need to find, independently for each
t
, the maximum flow from
r
to
t
by using
x_{v}
as node capacities (see Appendix
A
).

It is easy to see that an optimal solution for (
LP-DST
) provides a lower bound to
c(T^{*})
. In fact, the solution to (
LP-DST
) in which
x_{v}
is set to 1 if
v\in V(T^{*})
and 0 otherwise, and
f^{t}_{P}
is set to 1 if
P
is the unique path from
r
to
t
in
T^{*}
and to 0 otherwise, is feasible for (
LP-DST
) and has value
\sum_{v\in V}x_{v}c_{v}=c(T^{*})
.

Let
x
be an optimal solution for (
LP-DST
) and let
S\subseteq V
be the set of all vertices
v
with
x_{v}>0
in (
LP-DST
).

Let
U\subseteq S
be the set of all nodes
v
with
x_{v}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}
and
U^{\prime}=S\setminus U
. We split the set
K
of terminals into two sets:
CH
is the set of terminals that are reachable from
r
through at least a path
P
that contains only vertices in
U
; and
EX
is the set of terminals that are reachable from
r
only through paths containing at least a node in
U^{\prime}
,
EX=K\setminus CH
.
We compute two trees
T^{CH}
and
T^{EX}
rooted at
r
and spanning all terminals in
CH
and
EX
, respectively, and bound their cost. Then we merge the two trees to obtain a directed Steiner tree of all terminals.

As
\sum_{v\in U}x_{v}c_{v}\leq\sum_{v\in S}x_{v}c_{v}\leq c(T^{*})
and
x_{v}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}
for any
v\in U
(by definition), then
\sum_{v\in U}c_{v}\leq\sqrt{n}\cdot c(T^{*})
and hence we can span all terminals in
CH
with a tree
T^{CH}
rooted at
r
that costs
c(T^{CH})\leq\sqrt{n}\cdot c(T^{*})
.

Now we show that we can compute a tree
T^{EX}
rooted at
r
spanning all the terminals in
EX
with cost
c(T^{EX})=O\left((1+\varepsilon)c(T^{*})\sqrt{n}\ln{n}\right)
. The algorithm to build
T^{EX}
can be summarized as follows. We first compute, for each
t\in EX
, the set
X_{t}
of vertices
w
in
U^{\prime}
for which there exists a path from
w
to
t
that uses only vertices in
U\cup\{w\}
. Then we compute a small-size hitting set
X^{\prime}
of all sets
X_{t}
. Finally, we connect
r
to the vertices of
X^{\prime}
and the vertices of
X^{\prime}
to those in
EX
in such a way that each node
t
in
EX
is reached from one of the vertices in
X^{\prime}
that hits
X_{t}
. The bound on the cost of
T^{EX}
follows from the size of
X^{\prime}
and from the cost of nodes in
U
.

Indeed, we can show that there exists a hitting set
X^{\prime}
of all sets
X_{t}
with size
|X^{\prime}|\leq\sqrt{n}\ln{n}
. To show this, we first prove (see Claim
4.1
) that

for any

as follows. By constraint (
2
) of (

), each terminal should receive one unit of flow, moreover, by definition of
EX
and

, any path from

to any

, which implies that any flow from

to

should also pass through a vertex
w\in X_{t}
. Therefore, one unit of flow needs to pass through vertices in
X_{t}
. Since, by definition of
U^{\prime}
, each node
w\in X_{t}\subseteq U^{\prime}
has a capacity

smaller than
\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}
, we have
|X_{t}|\geq\sqrt{n}
.

Since each
X_{t}
,
t\in EX
, has at least
\sqrt{n}
elements and the size of
\bigcup_{t\in EX}X_{t}
is at most
n
, then we can find in polynomial time a subset
X^{\prime}
of
\bigcup_{t\in EX}X_{t}
that hits all the sets
X_{t}
and has size at most
\sqrt{n}\ln{n}
(see Claim
4.2
).

For each

, we find a shortest path from

to

. Since
|X^{\prime}|\leq\sqrt{n}\ln{n}
and

for any

, the total cost of these shortest paths from

to

is at most
(1+\varepsilon)\sqrt{n}\ln{n}\cdot c(T^{*})
. We also find, for each

to

(such a path exists by definition). The total cost of these shortest paths is at most
\sqrt{n}\cdot c(T^{*})
as they contain only vertices in

rooted at

spanning the terminals in

with

.

Finally, as both

and

are rooted at

, we can find a tree

that spans all vertices

and has cost

.

It is worth pointing out that by using the above algorithm for

as a black-box, one can obtain a polynomial time bicriteria
\left(2,O((1+\varepsilon)n^{3/4}\ln{n})\right)
-approximation algorithm for

.

, the one for

is similar. Let

is an optimal solution to

. We first use a standard linear programming flow-based formulation to find an optimal fractional solution for

If

3.2
Undirected Graphs

The previous best polynomial time approximation algorithm for
B-URST
was a bicriteria
\left(1+\varepsilon,O(\frac{\sqrt{B}}{\varepsilon^{3}})\right)
-approximation algorithm
[
DDG22
]
. We improve this result when
B\geq n^{1+\alpha}
for any
\alpha>0
and provide a new algorithm for
Q-URST
.

Theorem 3.4
.

B-URST
admits a polynomial time bicriteria
\left(1+\varepsilon,O(\frac{\sqrt{n}\log{n}}{\varepsilon^{3}})\right)
-approximation algorithm, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
T^{*}_{Q}
be an optimal solution to
Q-URST
.
Q-URST
admits an algorithm that, for any
\varepsilon>0
, outputs a tree
T
for which one of the following two conditions holds: (i)
c(T)=O(c(T^{*}_{Q})\sqrt{n}\log{n})
and
p(T)\geq\frac{Q}{2}
; (ii)
c(T)\leq(1+\varepsilon)c(T^{*}_{Q})
and
p(T)\geq\frac{Q}{2\sqrt{n}}
; and whose running time is polynomial in the input size and in
1/\varepsilon
.

To show Theorems
3.4
and
3.5
, we use the same core idea. In what follows, we explain such an idea by describing our algorithm for
B-URST
.

Our approach extends to monotone submodular prize functions the technique which was introduced by Guha, Moss, Naor, and Schieber
[
GMNS99
]
for the case of the budgeted problem on undirected graphs and additive prize function.
Their algorithm consists of three steps: (i) it computes an optimal solution to a linear relaxation of their problem, which provides an upper bound
OPT
on the optimal prize; (ii) by using this optimal fractional solution, it computes a tree
T
which may violate the budget constraints but has a prize of at least
OPT/4
and a prize-to-cost ratio
\gamma=\Omega\left(\frac{OPT}{B\log^{2}{n}}\right)
; (iii) it applies a trimming process that takes as input
T
and returns another tree which violates the budget constraint by a factor of at most
2
but preserves a prize-to-cost ratioof
\gamma
(up to a bounded multiplicative factor) and costs at least
B/2
. Therefore, the obtained tree guarantees a bicriteria
(2,O(\log^{2}{n}))
-approximation.

We encounter two main issues if we want to use the technique provided by
[
GMNS99
]
in general monotone submodular functions:

1.

The linear program provided by
[
GMNS99
]
may not give an upper bound to the optimum prize of
B-URST
. To overcome this issue, we provide a new linear programming formulation whose optimum is an upper bound on the optimum prize of
B-URST
and which can be solved in polynomial time through a separation oracle.

2.

The trimming procedure by
[
GMNS99
]
cannot be applied to the case of general monotone submodular functions. To deal with this issue, we introduce a new trimming process by refining the one provided by D’Angelo, Delfaraz and Gilbert
[
DDG22
]
for the case of monotone submodular prizes.
The prize-to-cost ratio of the tree produced by this latter is smaller than that of the original tree by an arbitrarily large multiplicative factor.
Here we show how to reduce this factor to a fixed constant, while keeping upper and lower bounds on the cost. This results in a tree that guarantees an approximation factor of
O(\frac{\sqrt{n}\log{n}}{\varepsilon^{3}})
at the cost of a budget violation of
1+\varepsilon
, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
.

4
Node-Weighted Directed Steiner Tree

As a warm-up,we present a polynomial time approximation algorithm for
DSteinerT
using the standard flow-based linear programming relaxation (
LP-DST
).

See
3.1

We prove Theorem
3.1
in what follows.
We denote an optimal solution to
DSteinerT
by
T^{*}
. Recall that
dist(v,u)
denotes the cost of a shortest path from
v
to
u
in
D
.

We first argue that we can assume that for all nodes
v
we have
dist(r,v)\leq(1+\varepsilon)c(T^{*})
, for any
\varepsilon>0
. We remove from the graphs all the nodes that do not satisfy this condition by estimating the value of
c(T^{*})
using the following procedure.

Let
c_{\min}
be the minimum positive cost of a vertex and
c_{M}
be the cost of a minimum spanning tree of
D
, we know that
c(T^{*})\leq c_{M}
. We estimate the value of
c(T^{*})
by guessing
N

N
is the smallest integer for which
c_{\min}(1+\varepsilon)^{N-1}\geq c_{M}
.

For each guess

, we remove the nodes

with
dist(r,v)>c_{\min}(1+\varepsilon)^{i-1}
, and compute a Steiner Tree in the resulting graph, if it exists, with an algorithm that will be explained later. Eventually, we output the computed Steiner Tree with the smallest cost. Since
c_{\min}(1+\varepsilon)^{N-2}<c_{M}
, the number
N
of guesses is smaller than
\log_{1+\varepsilon}(c_{M}/c_{\min})+2
, which is polynomial in the input size and in
1/\varepsilon
.

Let
i\in[N]
be the smallest value for which
c_{\min}(1+\varepsilon)^{i-1}\geq c(T^{*})
. Then,
c(T^{*})>c_{\min}(1+\varepsilon)^{i-2}
and for all the nodes
v
in the graph used in guess
i
, we have
dist(r,v)\leq c_{\min}(1+\varepsilon)^{i-1}<(1+\varepsilon)c(T^{*})
. Since we output the solution with the minimum cost among those computed in the guesses for which our algorithm returns a feasible Steiner Tree, then the final solution will not be worse than the one computed at guess
i
. Therefore, from now on we focus on guess
i
and assume that
dist(r,v)\leq(1+\varepsilon)c(T^{*})
, for all nodes
v
.

We now describe our
O\left((1+\varepsilon)\sqrt{n}\ln{n}\right)
-approximation algorithm under this assumption.

Let
x
be an optimal solution for (
LP-DST
) and let
S\subseteq V
be the set of all vertices
v
with
x_{v}>0
in (
LP-DST
). Note that
\sum_{v\in S}x_{v}c_{v}\leq c(T^{*})
as (
LP-DST
) provides a lower bound on the optimum solution of
DSteinerT
. Let
U\subseteq S
be the set of all nodes with
x_{v}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}
for any
v\in U
. Note that
r
belongs to
U
since we need to send one unit of flow from
r
to any terminal by constraint (
2
).
We call a terminal
t\in K
a
cheap terminal
if there exists a path from
r
to
t
in
D[U]
. We call a terminal
t\in K
an
expensive terminal
otherwise. Let
CH
and
EX
be the set of all cheap and expensive terminals in
K
, respectively.

We show that there exists a tree
T^{CH}
rooted at
r
spanning all the cheap terminals
CH
with cost
c(T^{CH})\leq\sqrt{n}\cdot c(T^{*})
.

Lemma 4.1
.

There exists a polynomial time algorithm that finds a tree
T^{CH}
rooted at
r
spanning all the cheap terminals
CH
with cost
c(T^{CH})\leq\sqrt{n}\cdot c(T^{*})
.

Proof.

By definition, each terminal
t
in
CH
is reachable from
r
through some paths
P
that contains only vertices in
U
, i.e.,
V(P)\subseteq U
. Thus we compute a shortest path
P
from
r
to every
t\in CH
in
D[U]
. Let
\mathcal{P}^{CH}
be the union of all these shortest paths
P
and
V(\mathcal{P}^{CH})
be the set of all vertices of the paths in
\mathcal{P}^{CH}
. Now we find a tree
T^{CH}
rooted at
r
spanning all the vertices
V(\mathcal{P}^{CH})
. By construction,
T^{CH}
contains all terminals in
CH
.

We know that

\sum_{v\in V(T^{CH})}x_{v}c_{v}=\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal{P}^{CH})}x_{v}c_{v}\leq\sum_{v\in U}x_{v}c_{v}\leq\sum_{v\in S}x_{v}c_{v}\leq c(T^{*}),

where the equality is due to
V(T^{CH})=V(\mathcal{P}^{CH})
, the first inequality is due to
V(\mathcal{P}^{CH})\subseteq U
and the second inequality is due to
U\subseteq S
.

By definition of
U
, we have
x_{v}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}
for any
v\in U
, then

c(T^{CH})=\sum_{v\in V(T^{CH})}c_{v}\leq\sqrt{n}\cdot\sum_{v\in V(T^{CH})}x_{v}c_{v}\leq\sqrt{n}\cdot c(T^{*}),

which concludes the proof.
∎

We next show that there exists a tree
T^{EX}
rooted at
r
spanning all the expensive terminals

with cost
c(T^{EX})=O\left((1+\varepsilon)c(T^{*})\sqrt{n}\ln{n}\right)
. The algorithm to build

can be summarized as follows. We first compute, for each
t\in EX
, the set
X_{t}
of vertices
w
in
S\setminus U
for which there exists a path
P
from
w
to
t
that uses only vertices in
U\cup\{w\}
, i.e.,
V(P)\setminus\{w\}\subseteq U
. Then, we compute a small-size hitting set
X^{\prime}
of all
X_{t}
. Finally, we connect
r
to the vertices of
X^{\prime}
and the vertices of
X^{\prime}
to those in
EX
in such a way that each node
t
in
EX
is reached from one of the vertices in
X^{\prime}
that hits
X_{t}
. The bound on the cost of
T^{EX}
follows from the size of
X^{\prime}
and from the cost of nodes in
U
.

Lemma 4.2
.

There exists a polynomial time algorithm that finds a tree
T^{EX}
rooted at
r
spanning all the cheap terminals
EX
with cost
c(T^{EX})\leq 2(1+\varepsilon)\sqrt{n}\ln{n}\cdot c(T^{*})
.

Proof.

Let
U^{\prime}\subseteq S
be the set of all vertices
v
with
0<x_{v}<\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}
, i.e.,
U^{\prime}=S\setminus U
.

We now show that
|X_{t}|\geq\sqrt{n}
for any
t\in EX
.

Claim 4.1
.

|X_{t}|\geq\sqrt{n}
, for each
t\in EX
.

Proof.

We know that (i) each terminal must receive one unit of flow (by constraint (
2
) of (
LP-DST
)), (ii) any path
P
from
r
to any
t\in EX
in the graph
D[S]
contains at least one vertex
w\in U^{\prime}
(by definition of expensive terminals), and , (iii) in any path
P
from
r
to any
t\in EX
, the node
w\in U^{\prime}
in
P
that is closest to
t
is a member of
X_{t}
, i.e.
w\in X_{t}
(by definition of
X_{t}
), therefore any flow from
r
to
t
must pass through a vertex
w\in X_{v}
. This implies that the vertices in
X_{t}
must send one unit of flow to
v
in total. Since each of them can only send at most
1/\sqrt{n}
amount of flow, they must be at least
\sqrt{n}
. Formally, we have

1=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{t}}f^{t}_{P}\leq\sum_{w\in X_{t}}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{t}:w\in P}f^{t}_{P}\leq\sum_{w\in X_{t}}x_{w}\leq\sum_{w\in X_{t}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}=\frac{|X_{t}|}{\sqrt{n}},

which implies that
|X_{t}|\geq\sqrt{n}
. Note that the first equality follows from constraint (
2
) of (
LP-DST
), the first inequality is due to the fact that, by definition of
X_{t}
, any path
P
from
r
to a
t\in EX
contains a vertex
w\in X_{t}
, the second inequality is due to constraint (
3
) of (
LP-DST
), the last inequality is due to
x_{w}<\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}
, for each
w\in U^{\prime}
, and
X_{t}\subseteq U^{\prime}
.
This concludes the proof of the claim.
∎

We use the following well-known result (see e.g. Lemma 3.3 in
[
Cha07
]
and Appendix
B
for a proof) to find a small set of vertices that hits all the sets
X_{t}
, for all
t\in EX
.

Claim 4.2
.

Let
V^{\prime}
be a set of
M
elements and
\Sigma=(X^{\prime}_{1},\dots,X^{\prime}_{N})
be a collection of subsets of
V^{\prime}
such that
|X^{\prime}_{i}|\geq R
, for each
i\in[N]
.
There is a deterministic algorithm which runs in polynomial time in
N
and

and finds a subset
X^{\prime}\subseteq V^{\prime}
with
|X^{\prime}|\leq(M/R)\ln{N}
and
X^{\prime}\cap X^{\prime}_{i}\neq\emptyset
for all
i\in[N]
.

Thanks to Claim
4.1
, we can use the algorithm of Claim
4.2
to find a set
X^{\prime}\subseteq\bigcup_{t\in EX}X_{t}
such that
X^{\prime}\cap X_{t}\neq\emptyset
, for all
t\in EX
, whose size is at most

|X^{\prime}|\leq\frac{n\ln{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\leq\sqrt{n}\ln{n},

where the parameters of Claim

are

,
N=|EX|\leq n
, and
M=\big|\bigcup_{t\in EX}X_{t}\big|\leq|V|\leq n
.

In other words, since for any
t\in EX
and any

there exists a path from
w
to
t
in
D[U\cup\{w\}]
and
X^{\prime}\cap X_{t}\neq\emptyset
, then there exists at least a vertex
w\in X^{\prime}
for which there is a path from
w
to
t
in
D[U\cup\{w\}]
.

Now, for each
w\in X^{\prime}
, we find a shortest path from
r
to
w
in
D
. Let
\mathcal{P}_{1}
be the set of all these shortest paths.
We also select, for each
t\in EX
, an arbitrary vertex
w
in
X^{\prime}\cap X_{t}
and compute a shortest path from
w
to
t
in
D[U\cup\{w\}]
. Let
\mathcal{P}_{2}
be the set of all these shortest paths.
Let
V(\mathcal{P}_{1})
and
V(\mathcal{P}_{2})
denote the union of all vertices of the paths in
\mathcal{P}_{1}
and
\mathcal{P}_{2}
, respectively, and let

be the graph induced by all the vertices in

.

Now we find a tree

rooted at

spanning

. Note that such a tree exists as in

we have for each

a path from

to

and, for each terminal

to

.

We next move to bounding the cost of

, indeed we bound the cost of all vertices in

.
Since

and the cost of a shortest path from

to any

in

is at most

, then
c(V(\mathcal{P}_{1}))\leq(1+\varepsilon)\sqrt{n}\ln{n}\cdot c(T^{*})
.
Since

for any

, and

, then

. Therefore, since

, then

Overall,

costs at most

. This finishes the proof.
∎

Now we prove Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem

.

Since both

and

are rooted at

, we can find a tree

spanning all vertices

. By Lemmas

∎

5
Directed Rooted Additive Tree Problems

In this section we present a polynomial time bicriteria
\left(2,O((1+\varepsilon)n^{2/3}\ln{n})\right)
-approximation algorithm for
Q-DRAT
and a polynomial time bicriteria
\left(1+\varepsilon,O(\frac{n^{2/3}\ln{n}}{\varepsilon^{2}})\right)
-approximation algorithm for
B-DRAT
, where
\varepsilon
is an arbitrary number in
(0,1]
.
Through the section we let
I_{Q}=<D=(V,A),c,p,r,Q>
and
I_{B}=<D=(V,A),c,p,r,B>
be two instances of
Q-DRAT
and
B-DRAT
, respectively, and we let
T^{*}_{Q}
and
T^{*}_{B}
be two optimal solutions for
I_{Q}
and
I_{B}
, respectively.
Both algorithms use the same technique and can be summarized in the following three steps:

1.

We define a set of linear constraints, denoted as (
Const-DRAT
), over fractional variables, that takes a given quota
Q
and a given budget
B
as parameters and admits a feasible solution if there exists a subtree
T
of
D
rooted at
r
such that
c(T)\leq B
and
p(T)\geq Q
.
Observe that the minimum
B
for which (
Const-DRAT
) is feasible for a given
Q
is a lower bound on
c(T^{*}_{Q})
, while the maximum
Q
for which (
Const-DRAT
) is feasible for a given
B
is an upper bound to
p(T^{*}_{B})
.

2.

We give a polynomial time algorithm that takes as input a feasible solution to (
Const-DRAT
) and computes a subtree
T
of
D
rooted at
r
such that
c(T)=O((F+B)n^{2/3}\ln{n})
and
p(T)\geq\frac{Q}{2}
, where
F
is the maximum distance from
r
to any other node,
F:=\max_{v\in V}\{dist(r,v)\}
.

3.

Q-DRAT:

We first show that we can assume that
F\leq(1+\varepsilon)c(T^{*}_{Q})
, for any
\varepsilon>0
. Then, we use a solution for (
Const-DRAT
) that minimizes
B
as input to the algorithm in the previous step and obtain a tree
T
such that
c(T)=O((F+B)n^{2/3}\ln{n})=O((1+\varepsilon)c(T^{*}_{Q})n^{2/3}\ln{n})
and
p(T)\geq\frac{Q}{2}
.

B-DRAT:

We assume w.l.o.g. that
F\leq B
and use a solution for (
Const-DRAT
) that maximizes
Q
as input to the algorithm in the previous step and obtain a tree
T
such that
c(T)=O(Bn^{2/3}\ln{n})
and
p(T)\geq\frac{Q}{2}\geq\frac{p(T^{*}_{B})}{2}
.

Tree
T
may violate the budget constraint by a large factor, however the ratio
\gamma
between its prize and its cost is
\Omega\left(\frac{p(T^{*}_{B})}{Bn^{2/3}\ln{n}}\right)
. Therefore, we can apply to
T
the trimming process given in
[
BHL18
]
to obtain another tree
\hat{T}
with cost
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}B\leq c(\hat{T})\leq(1+\varepsilon)B
, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
, and prize-to-cost ratio
\frac{p(\hat{T})}{c(\hat{T})}=\frac{\varepsilon\gamma}{4}
. The obtained tree
\hat{T}
achieves an approximation ratio of
O(\frac{n^{2/3}\ln{n}}{\varepsilon^{2}})
at the cost of a budget violation of
1+\varepsilon
, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
.

In the following, we will detail each step of our algorithms.

Bounding the optimal cost and prize.

Here we define a set of linear constraints that admits a feasible solution if there exists a tree
T
rooted in
r
in
D
such that
c(T)\leq B
and
p(T)\geq Q
, for given parameters
B
and
Q
.

For each
v\in V
, let
p_{v}=p(v)
,
c_{v}=c(v)
, and
\mathcal{P}_{v}
be the set of simple paths in
D
from
r
to
v
. Our set of constraints (
Const-DRAT
) is defined as follows.

(Const-DRAT)

(4)

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V}x_{v}p_{v}

\displaystyle\geq Q

(5)

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V}x_{v}c_{v}

\displaystyle\leq B

(6)

\displaystyle\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{v}}f^{v}_{P}

\displaystyle=x_{v},

\displaystyle\forall v\in V\setminus\{r\}

(7)

\displaystyle\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{v}:w\in P}f^{v}_{P}

\displaystyle\leq x_{w},

\displaystyle\forall v\in V\setminus\{r\}\text{ and }\forall w\in V\setminus\{v\}

\displaystyle 0\leq x_{v}

\displaystyle\leq 1,

\displaystyle\forall v\in V

\displaystyle 0\leq f^{v}_{P}

\displaystyle\leq 1,

\displaystyle\forall v\in V\setminus\{r\},P\in\mathcal{P}_{v}

We use variables
f^{v}_{P}
and
x_{v}
, for each
v\in V
and
P\in\mathcal{P}_{v}
, where
f^{v}_{P}
represents the amount of flow sent from
r
to
v
using path
P
and
x_{v}
represents both the capacity of node
v
and the overall amount of flow sent from
r
to
v
.
Variables
x_{v}
, for
v\in V
, are called
capacity variables
, while variables
f_{v}^{P}
for
v\in V
and
P\in\mathcal{P}_{v}
are called
flow variables
.

The constraints in (
Const-DRAT
) are as follows. Constraints (
4
) and (
5
) ensure that any feasible (fractional) solution to (
Const-DRAT
) has a prize at least
Q
and a cost at most
B
. Constraints (
6
) and (
7
) formulate a connectivity constraint through standard flow encoding, that is they ensure that the nodes
v
with
x_{v}>0
induce subgraph in which all nodes are reachable from
r
. In particular, constraint (
6
) ensures that the amount of flow that is sent from
r
to any vertex
v
must be equal to
x_{v}
and constraint (
7
) ensures that the total flow from
r
to
v
passing through a vertex
w
cannot exceed
x_{w}
.

Note that (
Const-DRAT
) has an exponential number of variables. However, it can be solved efficiently as we only need to find, independently for any
v\in V\setminus\{r\}
, a flow from
r
to
v
of value
x_{v}
that does not exceed the capacity
x_{w}
, for each vertex
w\in V\setminus\{r,v\}
(see Appendix
A
).

We now show that a feasible solution to (
Const-DRAT
) can be used to find a lower bound to an optimal solution for
Q-DRAT
and an upper bound to an optimal solution for
B-DRAT
.
In particular, we show that, for any tree
T
rooted in
r
in
D
such that
c(T)\leq B
and
p(T)\geq Q
we can compute a feasible solution
x
for (
Const-DRAT
).

Lemma 5.1
.

Given a directed graph
D=(V,A)
,
r\in V
,
c:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{\geq 0}
,
p:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{\geq 0}
,
B\in\mathbb{R}^{\geq 0}
and

, if there exists a tree
T
rooted in

in

such that

and

, then there exists a feasible solution

for (
Const-DRAT
).

Proof.

Let us consider a solution to (
Const-DRAT
) in which
x_{v}=1
for all
v\in V(T)
, while
x_{v}
is set to

for all
v\not\in V(T)
.
As

and
c(T)\leq B
, then the quota and budget constraints (
4
)–(
5
) are satisfied. Since

is connected and for any

, there exists only one path

from

to

in

, constraints (

) and (

) are satisfied by setting

and any other flow variable to 0.
∎

Lemma

shows the existence of a feasible solution

to (

) when the budget is
B=c(T^{*}_{Q})
and the quota is
Q
, therefore the optimum

to the linear program of minimizing

subject to constraints (

)
gives a lower bound to

, i.e.

.
Similarly, the optimum

to the linear program of maximizing

subject to constraints (

)
gives an upper bound to

,

. We denote these two linear programs by

and

, respectively.

Finding a good tree from a feasible fractional solution to (
Const-DRAT
).

Here we elaborate the second step and show how to find a tree with a good trade-off between prize and cost by using a feasible fractional solution from the previous step. In particular, we will show the following theorem. Recall that
F
denotes the maximum distance from
r
to a node in
V
,
F:=\max_{v\in V}\{dist(r,v)\}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Given a feasible solution
x
to (
Const-DRAT
), then there exists a polynomial time algorithm that computes a tree
T
rooted at
r
such that
c(T)=O((F+B)n^{2/3}\ln{n})
and
p(T)\geq\frac{Q}{2}
.

For
B-DRAT
, we can assume w.l.o.g. that
F\leq B
, therefore the above theorem implies that the ratio between prize and cost of the computed tree is
\Omega\left(\frac{Q}{Bn^{2/3}\ln{n}}\right)
. Similarly, for the
Q-DRAT
problem we can assume that
F\leq(1+\varepsilon)B
and the above theorem implies that the prize-to-cost ratio of the computed tree is
\Omega\left(\frac{Q}{(1+\varepsilon)Bn^{2/3}\ln{n}}\right)
.

We now prove Theorem
5.1
.
Let
x
be a feasible solution for (
Const-DRAT
) and let
S\subseteq V
be the set of vertices

with
x_{v}>0
, i.e.,
S=\{v\in V:x_{v}>0\}
.
We partition

into two subsets

, where

and

.

We first focus on nodes in
S_{1}
and, in the following lemma, we show how to compute a tree
T
rooted at
r
spanning all vertices in
S_{1}
with cost
c(T)=O((F+B)n^{2/3}\ln{n})
.

Lemma 5.2
.

There exists a polynomial time algorithm that finds a tree

rooted at

spanning all vertices in

with cost

.

Proof.

Let

. We call a vertex

a

if there exists a path from

to

in

(the graph induced by

). We call a vertex

an

otherwise. Note that

belongs to
U
when

since we need to send
\frac{1}{n^{2/3}}
amount of flow from
r
to any vertex in
U
by constraint (
6
). Let

and

be the set of all cheap and expensive vertices in

, respectively.

In the following, we first show that we can compute in polynomial time two trees

and

spanning all nodes in

and

, respectively, and having cost

and

.

We first focus on

. By definition, all vertices in

. Thus, for each

in

and find a tree

(by constraint (

Bounding the optimal cost and submodular prize.

We formulate a set of linear constraints, encoding a submodular flow problem, that admits a feasible solution if there exists a tree

We use the submodular flow problem introduced by Edmonds and Giles
[
EG77

, which is a generalization of the network flow problem in which restrictions on flows into vertices are generalized to flows into subsets of vertices. In particular, let
D=(V,A)
be a directed graph with upper and lower capacity bounds
\bar{c},\underline{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{A}
on its edges. Let
d:A\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a weight function on the edges and

be a submodular function on the subsets of vertices. The
base polyhedron
of

is defined as:

\mathcal{B}(f)=\{x|x\in\mathbb{R}^{V},x(X)\leq f(X),\forall X\subset V,x(V)=f(V)\},

where for each
X\subseteq V
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
,
x(X)=\sum_{v\in X}x(v)
.

For any
S\subseteq V
, let
\delta^{+}(S)
(resp.
\delta^{-}(S)
) be the set of edges entering (resp. leaving) the set
S
. Let
\upvarphi:A\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a flow function. For any
X\subseteq V

7
Conclusion

We obtained very simple polynomial time approximation algorithms for some budgeted and quota variants of node-weighted Steiner tree problems on two scenarios: (i) directed graphs with additive prizes, and (ii) undirected graphs with submodular prizes. The key insights behind our algorithms for the first scenario were to carefully select a subset of vertices as terminals in a fractional solution returned by the standard flow-based LPs and use some flow properties to find a small hitting set through which all the chosen terminals can be reached from the root vertex. The key idea of our algorithms for the second scenario were to use the submodular flow problem and introduce new LPs for our problems.

To the best of our knowledge, our techniques yield the first polynomial time (bicriteria) approximation algorithms for these problems (except
DSteinerT
) in terms of the number of vertices
n
. Furthermore, we believe that our introduced LPs can be utilized for some other Steiner problems in which, for example, other constraints can be added to (
Const-DRAT
) and (
Const-URST
).

A natural open question asks to improve the approximation guarantees or prove that the current
guarantees are the best possible using the flow-based LPs. By the result of Bateni, Hajiaghay and Liaghat
[
BHL18
]
, we know that the integrality gap of the flow-based LP for
B-DRAT
is infinite. This implies that, using this LP, we should work on improving the approximation factors for the budgeted problems while violating the budget constraint. Also, Li and Laekhanukit
[
LL22
]
showed that the integrality gap of the flow-based LP for
DSteinerT
is polynomial in the number of vertices
n
. This means that using this LP, one needs to work on the possibility of achieving an approximation algorithm with the factor
O(n^{\varepsilon})
for
DSteinerT
, where
0<\varepsilon<1/2
. Another interesting future work would be the possibility of extending our techniques for second scenario into directed graphs.

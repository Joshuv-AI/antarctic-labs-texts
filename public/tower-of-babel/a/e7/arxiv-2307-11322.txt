A Livšic-type theorem and some regularity properties for nonadditive sequences of potentials
Carllos Eduardo Holanda, Eduardo Santana

Abstract
We study some notions of cohomology for asymptotically additive sequences and prove a Livšic-type result for almost additive sequences of potentials. As a consequence, we are able to characterize almost additive sequences based on their equilibrium measures and also show the existence of almost (and asymptotically) additive sequences of Hölder continuous functions satisfying the bounded variation condition (with a unique equilibrium measure) and which are not physically equivalent to any additive sequence generated by a Hölder continuous function. None of these examples were previously known, even in the case of full shifts of finite type. Moreover, we also use our main result to suggest a classification of almost additive sequences based on physical equivalence relations with respect to the classical additive setup.



1
Introduction

Let
X
be a topological space and
T:X\to X
a map. A sequence of functions
(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is
asymptotically additive
with respect to
T
if for each
\varepsilon>0
there exists a function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\|f_{n}-S_{n}f\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon,

where
S_{n}f:=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}f\circ T^{k}
is the
additive sequence generated by the function
f
and
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
is the usual supremum norm on the space of continuous functions.

A sequence
\mathcal{F}=(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is
almost additive
with respect to
T
if there exists
C>0
such that

-C+f_{m}(x)+f_{n}(T^{m}(x))\leq f_{m+n}(x)\leq f_{m}(x)+f_{n}(T^{m}(x))+C

for every
x\in X
and all
m,n\geq 1
. Feng and Huang showed in
[
FH10
]
that almost additive sequences are in fact asymptotically additive.

Inspired by some nomenclature in statistical mechanics and classical thermodynamic formalism (see for example
[
Rue78
]
,
[
VFS93
]
and
[
Cun20
]
), we say that two nonadditive sequences of functions
\mathcal{F}:=(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
and
\mathcal{G}:=(g_{n})_{n\geq 1}
(with respect to some given map) are
physically equivalent
, or
\mathcal{F}
is
physically equivalent
to
\mathcal{G}
, if

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\|f_{n}-g_{n}\|_{\infty}=0.

The following physical equivalence result was obtained in
[
Cun20
]
:

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}=(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be an asymptotically additive or almost additive sequence of continuous functions. Then, there exists a continuous function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\parallel f_{n}-S_{n}f\parallel_{\infty}=0.

A nonadditive sequence
\mathcal{F}=(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
(with respect to
T:X\to X
) has
bounded variation
if there exist
M>0
and
\varepsilon>0
such that for
x,y\in X
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that
d(T^{k}(x),T^{k}(y))<\varepsilon
for every
k\in\{0,...,n-1\}
implies
|f_{n}(x)-f_{n}(y)|\leq M
(see section
3
). Observe that in Theorem
1
, if the almost additive sequence
\mathcal{F}
has bounded variation, the additive sequence
(S_{n}f)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
does not necessarily have bounded variation in general.

A function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be
Bowen
or to satisfy the
Bowen property
if the additive sequence
(S_{n}f)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
has bounded variation (see section
2
).

Let
X
be a compact metric space and
T:X\to X
be a continuous map. The following two questions were posted in
[
Cun20
]
:

Question A.

Is there, given any almost additive sequence of continuous functions
\mathcal{F}=(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with bounded variation, a continuous function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
such that
(S_{n}f)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
has bounded variation and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\|f_{n}-S_{n}f\|_{\infty}=0\quad?

Question B.

Is there, given any almost additive sequence
\mathcal{F}=(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with bounded variation, a continuous function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|f_{n}-S_{n}f\|_{\infty}<\infty\quad?

Letting
\sigma:\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}\to\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}
be the left-sided full shift of finite type, other natural finer questions about nonadditive regularity are the following (see also
[
LLV22
]
):

Question C.

Is there, given any almost additive sequence
\mathcal{F}=(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of Hölder continuous functions with bounded variation (with respect to
\sigma
), a Hölder continuous function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\|f_{n}-S_{n}f\|_{\infty}=0\quad?

Question D.

Is there, given any asymptotically additive sequence
\mathcal{F}=(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of (Hölder) continuous functions with bounded variation (with respect to
\sigma
) and admitting a unique equilibrium measure, a Bowen (or Hölder) continuous function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\|f_{n}-S_{n}f\|_{\infty}=0\quad?

It is well known that bounded variation does not guarantee uniqueness of equilibrium measures for asymptotically additive sequences, even in the case of full shifts of finite type. Based on that, one can easily find asymptotically additive sequences of locally constant functions which are
not
physically equivalent to any additive sequence generated by a Hölder (or Bowen) continuous function (see Remark 4.5 in
[
Cun20
]
). On the other hand, the same equivalence problem might be different for asymptotically additive sequences with unique equilibrium measures.

Based on the equivalence possibility brought by Theorem
1
, all these questions arise naturally considering the different levels of additive and nonadditive regularity associated. We note that for subshifts of finite type, expanding and hyperbolic maps in general, if
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
is a Hölder continuous function then the additive sequence
(S_{n}f)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies the bounded variation property (see section
3
).

Observe that
Question A
does not necessarily imply
Question B
in general. Moreover, if
\mathcal{F}
is uniformly bounded, that is,
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|f_{n}\|_{\infty}<\infty
, the sequence has bounded variation and the questions
A
and
B
are always affirmatively answered taking any continuous function
f
cohomologous to zero.

Let us now see some consequences of answering questions
A
,
B
,
C
and
D
.

1.

Uniqueness of equilibrium measures
: a positive answer to
Question A
implies that one can obtain the uniqueness of equilibrium measures for almost additive sequences with bounded variation directly from the classical uniqueness result for a single potential (see
[
Bow75
]
,
[
Bar06
]
and
[
Mum06
]
).

2.

Quasi-Bernoulli and Gibbs measures:
a positive answer to
Question B
readily implies that every quasi-Bernoulli measure is in fact a Gibbs measure with respect to some continuous potential (see subsection
4.2
and also
[
BM07
]
,
[
Cun20
]
).

3.

Nonadditive ergodic optimization
: giving a positive answer to
Question C
and
Question D
would allow us to use ergodic optimization results for Hölder potentials to either simplify or automatically extend some results for almost and asymptotically additive sequences of potentials (see for example
[
CLT01
]
,
[
Jen06
]
,
[
Mor08
]
,
[
CH10
]
,
[
Con16
]
,
[
Boc18
]
,
[
Jen19
]
,
[
Zha19
]
and
[
BHVZ21
]
). We believe that this would give a better understanding of more general nonadditive settings, such as the asymptotically subadditive setup (see for example
[
GG16
]
).

4.

Regularity of the nonadditive topological pressure
: an affirmative answer to
Question C
and
Question D
automatically gives important classes of asymptotically and almost additive sequences
\mathcal{F}:=(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
where the nonadditive topological pressure
t\mapsto P(t\mathcal{F})
is actually analytic, instead of having
C^{1}
regularity (or less) in general (see
[
Rue78
]
and
[
BD09
]
).

5.

Nonadditive multifractal analysis:
answering positively
Question C
and
Question D
also immediately guarantees higher-regularity of the entropy and dimension spectra for important classes of asymptotically and almost additive sequences with respect to some dynamical systems with hyperbolic behavior (see
[
BS01
]
,
[
BSS02
]
,
[
BD09
]
and
[
BCW13
]
).

All these regularity issues indicate that, even with the information provided by Theorem
1
, the relationship between additive, asymptotically and almost additive sequences are not yet completely understood. Notice that if all these questions are positively answered, the almost (asymptotically) additive world and the additive world are in fact the ”same” in a more complete sense, regarding many aspects of thermodynamic formalism, ergodic optimization and multifractal analysis for discrete-time dynamical systems.

We note that a positive answer to
Question B
immediately implies a positive answer to
Question A
. Moreover, for some subshifts of finite type, and some types of expanding and hyperbolic setups, a positive answer to
Question C
(without the Hölder continuity hypotheses on the sequences) also implies a positive answer to
Question A
.

In this work, we obtained the following characterization result for almost additive sequences of continuous functions:

Main result
(Theorem
5
).
Let
T\colon X\to X
be a continuous map on a compact metric space
X
, satisfying the Closing Lemma and having a point with dense orbit. Let
\mathcal{G}=(g_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be an almost additive sequence of continuous functions (with respect to
T
) with bounded variation. Then, the following are equivalent:

1.

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|g_{n}\|_{\infty}/n=0
;

2.

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|g_{n}\|_{\infty}<\infty
;

3.

there exists
K>0
such that
|g_{n}(p)|\leq K
for all
p\in X
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
T^{n}(p)=p
.

This result is proved without using the physical equivalence given by Theorem
1
, and it immediately shows that
Question A
is actually equivalent to
Question B
for any topologically transitive map satisfying the Closing Lemma (see Corollary
6
). This characterization result is an improvement concerning the understanding of regularity problems for nonadditive sequences, and it holds for setups including one-sided and two-sided full shifts of finite type, topologically transitive subshifts of finite type, repellers of topologically trasitive
C^{1}
maps and locally maximal hyperbolic sets for topologically transitive
C^{1}
diffeomorphisms. We also applied our main result to characterize almost additive sequences based on their equilibrium states and some cohomology relations, indicating that Theorem
5
works like a nonadditive version of the classical Livšic theorem (
[
Liv72
]
). Moreover, we show that Theorem
5
is optimal in the sense that it does not hold for asymptotically and subadditive sequences in general.

By using our main characterization result together with an example given by Bárány, Käenmäki and Morris in
[
BKM20
]
for the case of planar matrix cocycles, we are able to show the existence of almost additive sequences of Hölder continuous functions with bounded variation which are
not
physically equivalent to any additive sequence generated by a Hölder continuous function. This result is new and gives a
negative
answer to
Question C
. Furthermore, we also show a construction giving a negative answer to
Question D
for the case of Hölder and Bowen regularity in general.

Since a considerable part of the classical thermodynamic formalism, additive multifractal analysis and ergodic optimization for symbolic dynamics and some hyperbolic and uniformly expanding maps is mostly centered around Hölder continuous potentials, which is a natural class of functions to be considered in these setups (
[
Rue78
]
,
[
KH12
]
,
[
VO16
]
,
[
BS00
]
,
[
BS01
]
.
[
BSS02
]
,
[
Boc18
]
,
[
Jen19
]
), our negative answers to
Question C
and
Question D
go in the direction of revealing that almost (and asymptotically) additive sequences of potentials do not always possess the same expected regularity properties as the additive ones in general. Nevertheless, as far as we know, the more general Bowen regularity problem in
Question A
still remains open even in the case of full shifts of finite type.

The paper is organized as follows. Based on classical results and the physical equivalence theorem obtained in
[
Cun20
]
, we study and compare some different aspects of cohomological notions for almost and asymptotically additive sequences. In the third section, we obtain our main result (Theorem
5
) and we show how it is related with some results in the context of general matrix cocycles. As an application of our main theorem, we conclude the third section showing how to classify almost additive sequences based on cohomology and equilibrium measures. In the final section, we start with some examples showing setups where we can always answer affirmatively the
Questions

A
and
B
, and setups where
Question A
is always positively answered but
Question B
cannot be affirmatively satisfied. In the next subsection, building on an example given in
[
BKM20
]
, we use again our main result to exhibit examples of almost additive sequences of Hölder continuous potentials satisfying the bounded variation property but not physically equivalent to any additive sequence generated by any Hölder continuous potential, finally giving a negative answer to
Question C
. In the following subsection, inspired by some recent developments in the context of matrix cocycles, we use Theorem
5
to suggest a classification of almost additive sequences based on the different types of physical equivalence relations with the additive setup. We conclude our work showing some constructions of almost and asymptotically additive sequences of different types, giving a negative answer to
Question D
, and also exploring some more general open problems concerning the Bowen and Walters regularity.

2
Some notions of cohomology for asymptotically additive sequences of functions

Let
T:X\to X
be a continuous map on a compact metric space
X
and
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be an
almost additive
sequence of continuous functions (with respect to
T
), that is,
f_{n}:X\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and there exists
C>0
such that

-C+f_{n}(x)+f_{m}(T^{n}(x))\leq f_{m+n}(x)\leq C+f_{n}(x)+f_{m}(T^{n}(x)),

for all
x\in X
and
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
.

We say that a function
\varphi:X\to\mathbb{R}
or the additive sequence
(S_{n}\varphi)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies the
Walters property
or is a
Walters function
if for each
\kappa>0
there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that for
x,y\in X
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that
d(T^{k}(x),T^{k}(y))<\varepsilon
for every
k\in\{0,...,n-1\}
implies
|S_{n}\phi(x)-S_{n}\phi(y)|<\kappa
. Moreover, we say that a function
\psi:X\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies the
Bowen property
or is a
Bowen function
if there exist
M>0
and
\varepsilon>0
such that for
x,y\in X
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that
d(T^{k}(x),T^{k}(y))<\varepsilon
for every
k\in\{0,...,n-1\}
implies
|S_{n}\phi(x)-S_{n}\phi(y)|\leq M
. It is clear from the definitions that every function satisfying the Walters property also satisfies the Bowen property.

Let
T:X\to X
be a continuous map on a compact metric space
X
. The following result is the
Closing Lemma
, a classical well known tool in hyperbolic dynamics (see for example
[
KH12
]
).

Lemma 1
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that if
x\in X
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying
d(T^{n}(x),x)<\delta
, then there exists
y\in X
such that
T^{n}(y)=y
and
d(T^{k}(x),T^{k}(y))<\varepsilon
for all
0\leq k<n
.

A continuous function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be
cohomologous to zero
, or a
coboundary
(with respect to some continuous map
T
) when there exists a continuous function
q:X\to\mathbb{R}
such that
f(x)=q(T(x))-q(x)
for all
x\in X
. We say that a function
f
is cohomologous to another function
g
if
f-g
(or
g-f
) is a coboundary.

The following proposition is a more general version of the classical
Livšic Theorem
originally obtained in
[
Liv72
]
.

Proposition 2
.

Let
T\colon X\to X
be a continuous map satisfying the Closing Lemma and having a point with dense orbit. Let
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function satisfying the Walters property. Then
f
is cohomologous to zero if and only if for every periodic point
x=T^{n}(x)
we have
S_{n}f(x)=0
.

Observe that when a potential
f
is cohomologous to zero, by definition, there exists a continuous function
h\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
such that
f=h\circ T-h
, and this readily implies that

\int_{X}fd\mu=0\quad\text{for all $T$-invariant measure $\mu$.}

(1)

Conversely, assume that condition (
1
) holds. In particular, whenever
T^{n}(x)=x
we also have
\int_{X}fd\nu=0
for the measure
\nu=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\delta_{T^{k}(x)}
, and consequently

0=\int_{X}fd\nu=f(x)+f(T(x))+\cdots+f(T^{n-1}(x))=S_{n}f(x).

Therefore, by the Livšic Theorem (Proposition
2
) we conclude that
f
is cohomologous to zero. We just obtained the following result:

Corollary 3
.

f
is cohomologous to zero if and only if
\int_{X}fd\mu=0
for every
T
-invariant measure
\mu
.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{T}
be the set of all
T
-invariant measures.

Proposition 4
.

Under the conditions of Proposition
2
,
f
is cohomologous to zero if and only if

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\|S_{n}f\|_{\infty}=0.

In particular,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\|S_{n}f\|_{\infty}=0\textrm{ if and only if }\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|S_{n}f\|_{\infty}<\infty.

Proof.

Suppose we have
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\|S_{n}f\|_{\infty}=0
. By the Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem and the Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem, we obtain

0=\int_{X}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}S_{n}fd\mu=\int_{X}fd\mu

for every
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}
. Hence, applying Corollary
3
we conclude that
f
is cohomologous to zero. Conversely, if
f
is cohomologous to zero, there exists a continuous function
h
such that
f(x)=h(T(x))-h(x)
, which implies that
S_{n}f(x)=h(T^{n}(x))-h(x)
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Consequently,
\|S_{n}f\|_{\infty}\leq 2\|h\|_{\infty}<\infty
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and thus,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\|S_{n}f\|_{\infty}=~0
.
∎

In this setting, Proposition
4
indicates a property for additive sequences that is equivalent to the notion of cohomology for functions. Actually, a notion of cohomology for asymptotically and almost additive sequences of continuous functions was introduced by Bomfim and Varandas in
[
BV15
]
.

Let
X
be a compact metric space and
T:X\to X
be a continuous map. By the density of Hölder continuous functions on the space of continuous functions, one can show that for each asymptotically additive sequence of continuous functions
\mathcal{F}=(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, there exists a family of Hölder continuous functions
(f_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\|f_{n}-S_{n}f_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon\quad\textrm{for any $\varepsilon>0$}

(see Proposition 2.3 in
[
BV15
]
). The family
(f_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}
is called an
admissible family
for the sequence
\mathcal{F}
.

Definition 1

(
[
BV15
, Definition 2.4]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be an asymptotically or almost additive sequence of functions with respect to a map
T:X\to X
. The sequence
\mathcal{F}
is said to be cohomologous to a constant if there exists an admissible family
(f_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
for
\mathcal{F}
such that
f_{\varepsilon}
is
cohomologous to a constant for every small
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, that is, there exists a constant
c_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{R}
and a
continuous function
u_{\varepsilon}:X\to\mathbb{R}
so that
f_{\varepsilon}=u_{\varepsilon}\circ T-u_{\varepsilon}+c_{\varepsilon}
.

Using this definition, the following result was obtained.

Lemma 2

(
[
BV15
, Lemma 2.5]
)
.

An asymptotically additive sequence
\mathcal{F}
is cohomologous to a constant if and only if
(f_{n}/n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges uniformly to a constant. In particular,
\mathcal{F}
is cohomologous to zero if and only if
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|f_{n}\|_{\infty}/n=0
.

Now, based on Theorem
1
, we can give a simpler definition of cohomology for asymptotically additive sequences.

Definition 2
.

We say that an almost additive or asymptotically additive sequence of functions
\mathcal{F}=(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is cohomologous to a constant if there exists a continuous function
f
cohomologous to a constant and such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\|f_{n}-S_{n}f\|_{\infty}=0.

A reasonable thing to ask is if the two notions of cohomology are equivalent. In order to answer this, we have the following.

Lemma 3
.

Using Definition
2
,
\mathcal{F}
is cohomologous to a constant if and only if
\big(\frac{f_{n}}{n}\big)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges uniformly to a constant.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathcal{F}
is physically equivalent to the additive sequence
(S_{n}f)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, where
f
is cohomologous to a constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
, that is, there exists a continuous function
h\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
such that
f-c=h\circ T-h
. This implies that
S_{n}f=h\circ T^{n}-h+cn
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then,

\begin{split}\lim_{n\to\infty}\bigg\|\frac{f_{n}}{n}-c\bigg\|_{\infty}&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\bigg\|\frac{f_{n}}{n}-\frac{S_{n}f}{n}+\frac{h\circ T^{n}-h}{n}\bigg\|_{\infty}\\
&\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\|f_{n}-S_{n}f\|_{\infty}+\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\|h\circ T^{n}-h\|_{\infty}=0\end{split}

Conversely, let the sequence
\big(\frac{f_{n}}{n}\big)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converge to a constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\|f_{n}-S_{n}c\|_{\infty}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\frac{1}{n}f_{n}-c\|_{\infty}=0

and, by Definition
2
,
\mathcal{F}
is cohomologous to a constant.
∎

From Lemma
3
together with Lemma
2
, we conclude that Definition
2
is, in fact, equivalent to Definition
1
. Based on this, we can say that an asymptotically (or almost) additive sequence of continuous functions
\mathcal{F}=(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is cohomologous to zero if and only if the sequence
(f_{n}/n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is uniformly convergent to zero.

For the additive case, a continuous function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
is cohomologous to zero if and only if
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\|S_{n}f\|_{\infty}=0
under the conditions of Livšic Theorem. This implies that, in general, the classical definition of cohomology for a function is way stronger than the one we are suggesting for nonadditive sequences in Definition
2
. On the other hand, Proposition
4
also suggests a definition of cohomology for nonadditive sequences which is still weaker than the classical one but much stronger than Definition
2
:

Definition 3
.

We say that an almost additive or asymptotically additive sequence of functions
\mathcal{F}=(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is cohomologous to a constant if there exists a continuous function
f
cohomologous to a constant and such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|f_{n}-S_{n}f\|_{\infty}<\infty.

In this sense,
\mathcal{F}
is cohomologous to zero if and only if
\mathcal{F}
is uniformly bounded.

In the next section, our main result gives a setup where the definitions
1
,
2
and
3
are equivalent for almost additive sequences (see Theorem
5
). On the other hand, considering the same setup, Example
1
shows that definition
3
is not compatible with definitions
1
and
2
for asymptotically additive sequences.

3
A Livšic-type theorem for almost additive sequences

Let
X
be a compact metric space and
T:X\to X
a continuous map. We say that a sequence of functions
\mathcal{F}=(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
has
bounded variation
if there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\sup\{|f_{n}(x)-f_{n}(y)|:d_{n}(x,y)<\varepsilon\}<\infty,

where
d_{n}(x,y):=\max\{d(T^{k}(x),T^{k}(y)):0\leq k\leq n-1\}
. Moreover, we say that
\mathcal{F}
has
tempered variation
if

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\gamma_{n}(\varepsilon)}{n}=0,

where
\gamma_{n}(\varepsilon):=\sup\{|f_{n}(x)-f_{n}(y)|:d_{n}(x,y)<\varepsilon\}
.

We note that if
\phi:X\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies the Bowen property then the additive sequence
(S_{n}\phi)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
has bounded variation.

The Walters property for functions (additive sequences) also can be extended naturally to the nonadditive case. We say that a sequence of functions
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies the
Walters property
if for each
\kappa>0
there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that for
x,y\in X
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that
d(T^{k}(x),T^{k}(y))<\varepsilon
for every
k\in\{0,...,n-1\}
implies
|f_{n}(x)-f_{n}(y)|<\kappa
.

As in the additive case, it is clear that a sequence satisfying the Walters property also satisfies the Bowen property. Moreover, for subshifts of finite type, uniformly expanding maps and hyperbolic sets for diffeomorphisms, the space of Hölder continuous functions is contained in the class of functions satisfying the Walters property (see for example
[
Wal78
]
,
[
Bou02
]
and Proposition 20.2.6 in
[
KH12
]
).

A point
x\in X
is said to be
transitive
(with respect to a map
T
) if
\overline{\{T^{n}(x):n\in\mathbb{N}\}}=X
. On the other hand, we say that a map
T:X\to X
is
topologically transitive
if for every pair of non-empty open subsets
U,V\subset X
there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
T^{n}(U)\cap V\neq\emptyset
. In particular, when
X
is a compact metric space one can show that
T:X\to X
admits a transitive point if and only if
T
is topologically transitive (see for example
[
Sil92
]
).

The following theorem is our main result.

Theorem 5
.

Let
T\colon X\to X
be a continuous map satisfying the Closing Lemma and having a point with dense orbit. Let
\mathcal{G}=(g_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be an almost additive sequence of continuous functions (with respect to
T
) with bounded variation. Then, the following are equivalent:

1.

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|g_{n}\|_{\infty}/n=0
;

2.

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|g_{n}\|_{\infty}<\infty
;

3.

there exists
K>0
such that
|g_{n}(p)|\leq K
for all
p\in X
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
T^{n}(p)=p
.

The following result is a direct consequence of Theorem
5
.

Corollary 6
.

Let
\mathcal{F}=(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be an almost additive sequence of continuous functions with respect to
T:X\to X
and with bounded variation. Then, a continuous function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
such that
(S_{n}f)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
has bounded variation satisfies

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\|f_{n}-S_{n}f\|_{\infty}=0\quad\textrm{if and only if}\quad\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|f_{n}-S_{n}f\|_{\infty}<\infty.

In particular, if
(S_{n}f)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
does not have bounded variation we have

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|f_{n}-S_{n}f\|=\infty.

Notice that Corollary
6
readily implies that
Question A
is in fact equivalent to
Question B
for topologically transitive maps satisfying the Closing Lemma. We also note that Theorem
5
is an extension of Proposition
4
to the case of almost additive sequences having bounded variation.

Remark
.

Observe that Proposition
4
asks for the sequence of potentials to have the Walters property, which is stronger than the bounded variation condition. This is because the classical cohomology result obtained for a single potential is also stronger than the uniformly bounded one obtained in Theorem
5
. As we shall see on section
3.1
, Theorem
5
is also particularly related to Theorem 1.2 in
[
Kal11
]
, where control over the periodic data implies control over the full data.

In order to prove Theorem
5
, let us first obtain a key auxiliary result.

Lemma 4
.

Let
T\colon X\to X
be a continuous map and let
\mathcal{H}=(h_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be an almost additive sequence of continuous functions with uniform constant
C>0
and such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|h_{n}\|_{\infty}/n=0
. Then

1.

for every
k
-periodic point
x_{0}\in X
, we have
\sup_{q\in\mathbb{N}}|h_{qk}(x_{0})|\leq C
;

2.

for every periodic point
x_{0}
, there exists a constant
L\geq 0
(only depending on the period) such that
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}|h_{n}(x_{0})|\leq L
;

3.

We have

\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}}\bigg|\int_{X}h_{n}d\mu\bigg|\leq C\quad\textrm{for all $n\in\mathbb{N}$}.

Proof.

Since the sequence
\mathcal{H}
is almost additive, there exists
C>0
such that

h_{n}+h_{n}\circ T^{n}-C\leq h_{2n}\leq h_{n}+h_{n}\circ T^{n}+C

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. One can use induction to show that

\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}h_{n}(T^{ni}(x))-(p-1)C\leq h_{pn}(x)\leq\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}h_{n}(T^{ni}(x))+(p-1)C

(2)

for every
n,p\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in X
.

Let us assume without loss of generality that
T^{k}(x_{0})=x_{0}
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
. If
n=qk
for some
q\in\mathbb{N}
, then
T^{ni}(x_{0})=x_{0}
and so

\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{1}{p}\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}h_{n}(T^{ni}(x_{0}))=h_{n}(x_{0}).

On the other hand, since
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|h_{n}\|_{\infty}/n=0
, we have

\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{h_{pn}(x)}{pn}=0

and so it follows from (
2
) that

-C\leq h_{n}(x_{0})\leq C,

(3)

concluding the proof of item 1.

Now consider the case
n=qk+r
, with
0<r<k
. Let

A=\min\{h_{r}(x_{0}):r<k\}\quad\text{and}\quad B=\max\{h_{r}(x_{0}):r<k\}.

(4)

By the almost additivity of the sequence
\mathcal{H}
together with (
3
) and (
4
), we have

-2C+A\leq-C+h_{qk}(x_{0})+h_{r}(T^{qk}(x_{0}))\leq h_{n}(x_{0})

and

h_{n}(x_{0})\leq h_{qk}(x_{0})+h_{r}(T^{qk}(x_{0}))+C\leq 2C+B.

Therefore,

L_{2}:=\min\{A-2C,-C\}\leq h_{n}(x_{0})\leq\max\{B+2C,C\}:=L_{1}

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Taking
L:=\max\{|L_{1}|,|L_{2}|\}
, the item 2 is proved.

Now let us prove item 3. If
\mu
is a
T
-invariant probability, then it is also
T^{n}
-invariant for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and, by using that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|h_{n}\|_{\infty}/n=0
in the inequalities
2
together with the Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem, we obtain that

\displaystyle-C\leq\int_{X}\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{1}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}g_{n}(T^{in}(x))d\mu(x)=\int_{X}g_{n}d\mu\leq C

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}
is arbitrary, the lemma is proved.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5
.

Since
\mathcal{G}
has bounded variation, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

M:=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\sup\{|g_{n}(x)-g_{n}(y)|:d_{n}(x,y)<\varepsilon\}<\infty.

(5)

Let us start proving that 3 implies 2. Suppose that there exists
K>0
such that
|g_{n}(p)|\leq K
for all
p\in X
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
T^{n}(p)=p
. Let
\omega\in X
be a transitive point and let
\delta>0
be the number given by the Closing Lemma (Lemma
1
). Since the orbit of
\omega
is dense, there exists a number
L(\omega,\delta)\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
x\in X
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists some
k\in\{0,1,...,L(\omega,\delta)\}
with
d(T^{n}(x),T^{k}(\omega))<\delta
. Letting
n>L(\omega,\delta)
, in particular there exists
k^{\prime}\in\{0,1,...,L(\omega,\delta)\}
such that
d(T^{n}(\omega),T^{k^{\prime}}(\omega))<\delta
, that is,
d(T^{n-k^{\prime}}(T^{k^{\prime}}(\omega)),T^{k^{\prime}}(\omega))<\delta
.

By the Closing Lemma, there exists a point
p\in X
with
T^{n-k^{\prime}}(p)=p
and such that
d_{n-k^{\prime}}(T^{k^{\prime}}(\omega),p)<\varepsilon
. Now applying the bounded variation condition (
5
) we have that

|g_{n-k^{\prime}}(T^{k^{\prime}}(\omega))-g_{n-k^{\prime}}(p)|\leq M,

which implies that
|g_{n-k^{\prime}}(T^{k^{\prime}}(\omega))|\leq M+|g_{n-k^{\prime}}(p)|\leq M+K
. This together with almost additivity gives that

\begin{split}|g_{n}(\omega)|=|g_{(n-k^{\prime})+k^{\prime}}(\omega)|&\leq|g_{k^{\prime}}(\omega)|+|g_{n-k^{\prime}}(T^{k^{\prime}}(\omega))|+C\\
&\leq\max_{k\in\{0,1,...,L(\omega,\delta)\}}|g_{k}(\omega)|+M+K+C:=K^{\prime}.\end{split}

Since
n>L(\omega,\delta)
was arbitrary, we have
|g_{n}(\omega)|\leq K^{\prime}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. By using almost additivity again, we also have that

|g_{n}(T^{k}(\omega))+g_{k}(\omega)-g_{n+k}(\omega)|\leq C\quad\textrm{for all $n,k\in\mathbb{N}$},

which gives

|g_{n}(T^{k}(\omega))|\leq|g_{k}(\omega)|+|g_{n+k}(\omega)|+C\leq 2K^{\prime}+C\quad\textrm{for all $n,k\in\mathbb{N}$}.

Now let
x\in X
. Since
\omega
is transitive, there exists a sequence
(\omega_{q})_{q\geq 1}\subset\{T^{n}(\omega):n\in\mathbb{N}\}
such that
\lim_{q\to\infty}\omega_{q}=x
. Since every function
g_{n}
is continuous, we obtain that

|g_{n}(x)|=\lim_{q\to\infty}|g_{n}(\omega_{q})|\leq 2K^{\prime}+C.

Therefore, by the arbitrariness of
x
, we conclude that
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|g_{n}\|_{\infty}\leq 2K^{\prime}+C<\infty
as desired. It is immediate that 2 implies 1. By Lemma
4
, it follows that 1 implies 3 and the theorem is proved. ∎

Theorem
5
does not hold for asymptotically additive nor subadditive sequences in general. In order to illustrate this, we give the following simple example.

Example 1
.

Let
T:X\to X
be a continuous map and let
\mathcal{F}=(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be the sequence given by
f_{n}(x):=\sqrt{n}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and every
x\in X
. It is clear that
\mathcal{F}
has bounded variation and is asymptotically additive and also subadditive with respect to
T
. Moreover, we have that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|f_{n}\|_{\infty}/n=0
but
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|f_{n}\|_{\infty}=\infty
. Actually, we also have

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|f_{n}-S_{n}f\|_{\infty}=\infty\quad\textrm{for every function $f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}$}.

In fact, let us suppose by contradiction that there exist a continuous function
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
and
L>0
such that
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|f_{n}-S_{n}f\|_{\infty}\leq L
. Given
x\in X
, we obtain

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}|f_{n}(x)-S_{n}f(x)|=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}|\sqrt{n}-S_{n}f(x)|\leq L.

Setting
a_{n}:=f(T^{n-1}(x))
, we have

\sqrt{n}-L\leq a_{1}+\dots+a_{n}\leq\sqrt{n}+L.

(6)

Since
x
is arbitrary, we obtain
\sqrt{n}-L\leq a_{k+1}+\dots+a_{k+n}\leq\sqrt{n}+L
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Moreover, since these inequalities hold for every sequence with length
n
, we have

p(\sqrt{n}-L)\leq a_{1}+\dots+a_{pn}\leq p(\sqrt{n}+L)

(7)

for every
p\in\mathbb{N}
.
On the other hand, it follows directly from (
6
) that

(\sqrt{pn}-L)\leq a_{1}+\dots+a_{pn}\leq(\sqrt{pn}+L)\quad\textrm{for every $p\in\mathbb{N}$}.

(8)

Comparing (
7
) with (
8
) we obtain
p(\sqrt{n}-L)\leq\sqrt{pn}+L
, which implies that

\sqrt{n}-L\leq\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\sqrt{p}}+\frac{L}{p}\quad\textrm{for every $p\in\mathbb{N}$.}

By letting
p\to\infty
we obtain
\sqrt{n}-L\leq 0
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, which is clearly a contradiction. Therefore,
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|f_{n}-S_{n}f\|_{\infty}=\infty
for every function
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
, as we claimed. ∎

Example
1
shows that the definition
3
is not equivalent to definition
2
for asymptotically additive sequences in general. Moreover, this also implies that Theorem
5
has an optimal nonadditive setup in the sense that it cannot be extended to more general sequences, such as asymptotically additive and subadditive ones.

3.1
Matrix cocycles

Let
X
be a compact metric space and
T:X\to X
a continuous map. Moreover, let
GL(d,\mathbb{R})
be the set of all invertible
d\times d
matrices. A continuous map
\mathcal{A}:X\times\mathbb{N}\to GL(d,\mathbb{R})
is called a
linear cocycle
over
T
if for all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in X
we have:

1.

\mathcal{A}(x,0)=
Id;

2.

\mathcal{A}(x,n+m)=\mathcal{A}(T^{m}(x),n)\mathcal{A}(x,m)
.

Every cocycle is generated by a function
A:X\to GL(d,\mathbb{R})
, that is,

\mathcal{A}(x,n)=A(T^{n-1}(x))...A(T(x))A(x)\quad\textrm{for all $n\in\mathbb{N}$ and $x\in X$}.

Assume that the cocycle
\mathcal{A}
is generated by a continuous function
A:X\to GL(d,\mathbb{R})
which takes values in the set of matrices
d\times d
with strictly positive entries. Moreover, we consider the pseudo-norm on
GL(d,\mathbb{R})
defined by
\|A\|=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}|a_{ij}|
, denoting by
a_{ij}
the entries of
A
.

Now we consider the sequence of functions
\mathcal{F}_{A}:=(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
defined by

a_{n}(x)=\log\|\mathcal{A}(x,n)\|\quad\textrm{for all $n\in\mathbb{N}$ and $x\in X$}.

(9)

It follows from Lemma 2.1 in
[
FL02
]
that the sequence
\mathcal{F}_{A}
is almost additive with respect to
T
.

Extending the definitions for derivative cocycles in
[
BG06
]
, we say that the cocycle
\mathcal{A}
has
tempered distortion
if for some
\varepsilon>0

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sup\left\{\|\mathcal{A}(x,n)\mathcal{A}(y,n)^{-1}\|:z\in M\ \text{and}\ x,y\in B_{n}(z,\varepsilon)\right\}=0

Moreover,
\mathcal{A}
is said to have
bounded distortion
if

\sup\left\{\|\mathcal{A}(x,n)\mathcal{A}(y,n)^{-1}\|:z\in M\ \text{and}\ x,y\in B_{n}(z,\varepsilon)\right\}<\infty

for some
\varepsilon>0
. It is clear that bounded distortion implies tempered distortion.

Now observe that

\|\mathcal{A}(x,n)\mathcal{A}(x,n)^{-1}\|=\|\text{\rm Id}\|=d

for every
(x,n)\in X\times\mathbb{N}
, which implies that

\|\mathcal{A}(x,n)^{-1}\|\geq d\|\mathcal{A}(x,n)\|^{-1}.

Then

\|\mathcal{A}(x,n)\mathcal{A}(y,n)^{-1}\|\geq\frac{M}{d}\|\mathcal{A}(x,n)\|\cdot\|\mathcal{A}(y,n)^{-1}\|\geq M\|\mathcal{A}(x,n)\|\cdot\|\mathcal{A}(y,n)\|^{-1},

(10)

where
M>0
is the constant satisfying

1\geq\frac{\min_{(i,j)}a_{ij}}{\max_{(i,j)}a_{ij}}\geq M,

which exists because, by assumption, the entries of
A
are all strictly positive.

So, it follows directly from (
10
) that

\bigl\lvert\log\|\mathcal{A}(x,n)\|-\log\|\mathcal{A}(y,n)\|\bigr\rvert\leq-\log M+\log\|\mathcal{A}(x,n)\mathcal{A}(y,n)^{-1}\|.

In particular, for
z\in X
and
\varepsilon>0
we have

\sup_{x,y\in B_{n}(z,\varepsilon)}|a_{n}(x)-a_{n}(y)|\leq-\log M+\log\sup_{x,y\in B_{n}(z,\varepsilon)}\|\mathcal{A}(x,n)\mathcal{A}(y,n)^{-1}\|.

Hence, if the cocycle
\mathcal{A}
has tempered distortion, then the sequence
\mathcal{F}_{A}
has tempered variation, and if
\mathcal{A}
has bounded distortion, then
\mathcal{F}_{A}
has bounded variation.

A cocycle
\mathcal{A}:X\times\mathbb{N}\to GL(d,\mathbb{R})
is said to satisfy
domination
if there exist
C>0
and
\lambda>0
and a splitting
\mathbb{R}^{d}=E(x)\oplus F(x)
such that

\|\mathcal{A}(x,n)u\|\geq Ce^{\lambda n}\|\mathcal{A}(x,n)v\|

for all unitary vectors
u\in E(x)
and
v\in F(x)
. For linear cocycles, one also can show that domination implies almost additivity of the sequence
\mathcal{F}_{A}
defined in
(
9
)
(see for example
[
BG09
]
and
[
BKM20
]
for more information on domination and almost additivity in the case of cocycles).

Let
GL^{+}(d,\mathbb{R})\subset GL(d,\mathbb{R})
be the set of all matrices with strictly positive entries.
We have the following application of Theorems
5
, which is a particular version of one of the main results in
[
Kal11
]
:

Proposition 7
.

Let
T\colon X\to X
be a topologically transitive continuous map on a compact metric space
X
and satisfying the Closing Lemma. Let
A:X\to GL^{+}(d,\mathbb{R})
be a continuous function which generates a cocycle
\mathcal{A}:X\times\mathbb{N}\to GL^{+}(d,\mathbb{R})
with bounded distortion or let
\mathcal{A}:X\times\mathbb{N}\to GL(d,\mathbb{R})
be a cocycle satisfying domination with bounded distortion. Suppose there exists a compact set
\Omega\subset GL(d,\mathbb{R})
such that
\mathcal{A}(p,n)\subset\Omega
for all
p\in X
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
T^{n}(p)=p
. Then there exists a compact set
\widetilde{\Omega}
such that
\mathcal{A}(x,n)\subset\widetilde{\Omega}
for all
x\in X
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

By the hypotheses,
\mathcal{F}_{A}=(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
given by
a_{n}(x):=\log\|\mathcal{A}(x,n)\|
is an almost additive sequence of continuous functions. Since the cocycle
\mathcal{A}
has bounded distortion, the sequence
\mathcal{F}_{A}
has bounded variation. Now suppose there exists a compact
\Omega\subset GL(d,\mathbb{R})
where
\mathcal{A}(p,n)\subset\Omega
for all
p\in X
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
T^{n}(p)=p
. Since the map
\mathcal{A}(p,n)\mapsto\log\|\mathcal{A}(p,n)\|
is continuous, there exists
K>0
such that
a_{n}(p)\in[-K,K]
for all
p\in X
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
T^{n}(p)=p
. By Theorem
5
, there exists a constant
\widetilde{K}>0
such that
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|a_{n}\|_{\infty}\leq\widetilde{K}
. In particular, this implies that
e^{-\widetilde{K}}\leq\|\mathcal{A}(x,n)\|\leq e^{\widetilde{K}}
for all
x\in X
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Therefore

\|\mathcal{A}(x,n)-\text{\rm Id}\|\leq\|\mathcal{A}(x,n)\|+\|\text{\rm Id}\|\leq e^{\widetilde{K}}+d\quad\textrm{for all $x\in X$ and $n\in\mathbb{N}$},

and the result is proved.
∎

Notice that when the function
A:X\to GL(d,\mathbb{R})
is Hölder continuous, the cocycle
\mathcal{A}
has in fact the bounded distortion property (see Proposition 5.1 in
[
Kal11
]
). Then, Proposition
7
is a particular version of Theorem 1.2 in
[
Kal11
]
for the case of strictly positive cocycles (or cocycles with domination) satisfying the bounded distortion property.

Remark
.

For a general cocycle, the sequence
\mathcal{F}_{A}
defined in (
9
) is only subadditive. Following as in the proof of Proposition
7
, one can check that Theorem 1.2 in
[
Kal11
]
also gives an important class of subadditve sequences of continuous functions with bounded variation such that a uniform control over the periodic data implies a uniform control over all data. Based on this, one can naturally ask for which other classes of subadditive sequences this result can be extended.

3.2
A characterization of almost additive sequences with the same equilibrium measures

In this section, we will apply Theorem
5
to see how we can identify sequences with the same equilibrium measures just based on the information about the periodic data of the system.

Let
T\colon X\to X
be a continuous map of a compact metric space, and let
\mathcal{F}=(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be an almost additive sequence of continuous functions with tempered variation (weaker than bounded variation condition). We have the following variational principle (see
[
Bar06
]
and
[
Mum06
]
:

P_{T}(\mathcal{F})=\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}}\bigg(h_{\mu}(T)+\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int_{X}f_{n}d\mu\bigg),

where
P_{T}(\mathcal{F})
is the nonadditive topological pressure of
\mathcal{F}
with respect to
T
introduced in
[
Bar96
]
, and
h_{\mu}(T)
is the Kolmogorov-Sinai entropy. A measure
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}
is said to be an
equilibrium measure
for
\mathcal{F}
(with respect to
T
) if the supremum is attained in
\nu
, that is,

P_{T}(\mathcal{F})=h_{\nu}(T)+\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int_{X}f_{n}d\nu.

When
\mathcal{F}
is an additive sequence generated by a single continuous function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
, one can easily see that

P_{T}(\mathcal{F})=P_{T}(f)\quad\textrm{and}\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int_{X}f_{n}d\mu=\int_{X}fd\mu\quad\textrm{for all $\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}$},

where
P_{T}(f)
is the classical (additive) topological pressure of
f
with respect to the map
T
. In this case, we recover the classical notions of topological pressure, variational principle and equilibrium measures for functions. For asymptotically and almost additive sequences, Theorem
1
allows us to obtain the notion of nonadditive topological pressure and variational principle directly from the classical theory.

It was introduced in
[
Bar06
]
a definition of Gibbs measures with respect to almost additive sequences using Markov partitions. One also can define Gibbs measures with respect to almost additive sequences in a more general way, without the requirement of Markov partitions. We say that
\mu
(not necessarily
T
-invariant) is a
Gibbs measure
with respect to
\mathcal{F}
if for each
\varepsilon>0
there exists a constant
K=K(\varepsilon)\geq 1
such that

K^{-1}\leq\frac{\mu(B_{n}(x,\varepsilon))}{\exp[-nP_{T}(\mathcal{F})+f_{n}(x)]}\leq K

for all
x\in X
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, where
B_{n}(x,\varepsilon):=\{y\in X:d_{n}(x,y)<\varepsilon\}
. These two notions of Gibbs measures are equivalent when the system admits Markov partitions with arbitrarily small diameter, as in the case of locally maximal hyperbolic sets or repellers for
C^{1}
diffeomorphisms (see for example
[
Bow75a
]
).

In the case of shifts
\sigma:\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}\to\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}
, the definition is simpler. A measure
\mu
on
\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}
is said to be
Gibbs
with respect to
\mathcal{F}=(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
when there exists a constant
K\geq 1
such that

K^{-1}\leq\frac{\mu_{n}(C_{i_{1}\ldots i_{n}})}{\exp(-nP_{\sigma}(\mathcal{F})+f_{n}(x))}\leq K

(11)

for all
x\in C_{i_{1}\ldots i_{n}}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, where
C_{i_{1}\ldots i_{n}}
is the set

C_{i_{1}\ldots i_{n}}:=\{y=(j_{1}j_{2}\cdots)\in\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}:j_{1}=i_{1},\ldots,j_{n}=i_{n}\}.

These definitions are natural nonadditive versions of the classical Gibbs definitions with respect to a single continuous function (see definition (
16
)).

Now let’s recall the definition of uniformly expanding maps and repellers. Let
M
be a Riemannian manifold,
T:M\to M
a
C^{1}
map, and let
\Lambda\subset M
be a compact
T
-invariant set, that is,
T^{-1}(\Lambda)=\Lambda
. The map
T
is said to be
uniformly expanding
on
\Lambda
if there exist constants
c>0
and
\lambda>1
such that

\|d_{x}f^{n}v\|\geq c\lambda^{n}\|v\|\quad\textrm{for all $x\in\Lambda$, $n\in\mathbb{N}$ and $v\in T_{x}M$}.

In this case, the set
\Lambda
is called a
repeller
of
T
. The following result is a criteria for uniqueness of equilibrium measures for nonadditive sequences.

Proposition 8

(
[
Bar06
, Theorem 5]
)
.

Let
\Lambda
be a repeller of a
C^{1}
topologically mixing map
T\colon\Lambda\to~\Lambda
, and let
\mathcal{F}=(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be an almost additive sequence of continuous functions on
\Lambda
with bounded variation. Then
\mathcal{F}
admits a unique equilibrium measure, which also satisfies the Gibbs property with respect to
\mathcal{F}
.

Proposition
8
also holds in the case of full shifts of finite type, topologically mixing subshifts of finite type and locally maximal hyperbolic sets for topologically mixing
C^{1}
diffeomorphisms (see also Theorem 6 in
[
Mum06
]
).

In the following result, we use Theorem
5
to obtain a characterization of almost additive sequences based on equilibrium measures.

Theorem 9
.

Let
\Lambda
be a repeller of a
C^{1}
topologically mixing map
T\colon\Lambda\to~\Lambda
. Let
\mathcal{F}=(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\mathcal{G}=(g_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be two almost additive sequences of continuous functions with bounded variation. Then
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
have the same equilibrium measure if and only if there exists a constant
K>0
such that
|f_{n}(p)-g_{n}(p)-n(P(\mathcal{F})-P(\mathcal{G}))|\leq K
for all
p\in X
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
T^{n}(p)=p
.

In order to prove this, we first need the following general result:

Lemma 5
.

Let
X
be a compact metric space,
T:X\to X
a continuous map and let
\mathcal{F}=(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be an asymptotically additive sequence of continuous functions (with respect to
T
). Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\|f_{n}\|_{\infty}=\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}}\bigg|\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int_{X}f_{n}d\mu\bigg|.

Proof.

Proposition 2.1 in
[
IP84
]
together with Lemma 2.2 in
[
Cun20
]
gives that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\|S_{n}f\|_{\infty}=\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}}\bigg|\int_{X}fd\mu\bigg|

(12)

for all continuous functions
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
. By Theorem
1
, there exists a continuous function
f
such that
(S_{n}f)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is physically equivalent to
\mathcal{F}
. Hence, in particular,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\|f_{n}\|_{\infty}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\|S_{n}f\|_{\infty}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int_{X}f_{n}d\mu=\int_{X}fd\mu\quad\textrm{for all $\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}$}.

This together with (
12
) yields the desired result.
∎

Proof of Theorem
9
.

If we suppose
|f_{n}(p)-g_{n}(p)-n(P(\mathcal{F})-P(\mathcal{G}))|\leq K
for all
p\in X
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
T^{n}(p)=p
, Theorem
5
gives that
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|f_{n}-g_{n}-n(P(\mathcal{F})-P(\mathcal{G}))\|_{\infty}<\infty
. By the definition of nonadditive topological pressure and Lemma
5
, respectively, we have

P(\mathcal{F})=P(\mathcal{H})\quad\textrm{and}\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int_{X}f_{n}d\mu=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int_{X}h_{n}d\mu\quad\textrm{for all $\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}$}.

where
\mathcal{H}=(h_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is the sequence
h_{n}:=g_{n}+n(P(\mathcal{F})-P(\mathcal{G}))
. This readily implies that
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{H}
have the same equilibrium measures. Since
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{G}
also share the same equilibrium measure, the same is true for
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
.

Now let’s prove the converse. By Proposition
8
, the sequences
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
have unique equilibrium measures, and they satisfy the Gibbs property. Now suppose these equilibrium measures are the same unique measure
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}
. By the Gibbs property, for each
\varepsilon>0
there exist constants
K_{1}=K_{1}(\varepsilon)\geq 1
and
K_{2}=K_{2}(\varepsilon)\geq 1
such that

K_{1}^{-1}\leq\frac{\nu(B_{n}(x,\varepsilon))}{\exp[-nP_{T}(\mathcal{F})+f_{n}(x)]}\leq K_{1}\quad\textrm{and}\quad K_{2}^{-1}\leq\frac{\nu(B_{n}(x,\varepsilon))}{\exp[-nP_{T}(\mathcal{G})+g_{n}(x)]}\leq K_{2}

for all
x\in X
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. This implies that

K_{1}^{-1}K_{2}^{-1}\leq\exp[f_{n}(x)-g_{n}(x)-n(P_{T}(\mathcal{F})-P_{T}(\mathcal{G}))]\leq K_{1}K_{2}

for all
x\in X
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then,

\|f_{n}-g_{n}-n(P_{T}(\mathcal{F})-P_{T}(\mathcal{G}))\|_{\infty}\leq\log(K_{1}K_{2}).

In particular, we have that
|f_{n}(p)-g_{n}(p)-n(P_{T}(\mathcal{F})-P_{T}(\mathcal{G}))|\leq\log(K_{1}K_{2})
for all
p\in X
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
T^{n}(p)=p
, as desired. ∎

Remark
.

By the equivalences in Theorem
5
, the statement of Theorem
9
also could be:
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
have the same equilibrium measure if and only if the sequence
\mathcal{F}-\mathcal{G}
is cohomologous to the constant
P_{T}(\mathcal{F})-P_{T}(\mathcal{G})
, in the sense of definitions
1
,
2
and
3
. In this context, Theorem
9
can be seen as a nonadditive counterpart of some classical additive results, for example, Theorem 12.2.3 in
[
VO16
]
and Propositions 20.3.9 and 20.3.10 in
[
KH12
]
.

Remark
.

Based on the different versions of results concerning the uniqueness of equilibrium measures for almost additive sequences, Theorem
9
also holds for topologically mixing subshifts of finite type, locally maximal hyperbolic sets for topologically mixing
C^{1}
diffeomorphisms, and repellers of topologically mixing
C^{1}
maps (see
[
Bar06
]
and
[
Mum06
]
).

4
Regularity of almost and asymptotically additive sequences of continuous potentials

In this section we will address the regularity problem for nonadditive sequences. We will start with some simpler cases in the non-hyperbolic context, then we will eventually attack and discuss the hyperbolic and related setups.

4.1
Non-hyperbolic setups

In this subsection, we will consider maps where the periodic points are dominant and there is no transitive data, and setups where the transitive points are dominant and we have no periodic data.

Let us now begin with an example of a setup where
Question A
and
Question B
can always be affirmatively answered.

Example 2
.

(Rational rotations)
Let
R_{\alpha}:\mathbb{T}^{n}\to\mathbb{T}^{n}
be the rational rotation on the
n
-torus, that is,
R_{\alpha}(x)=x+\alpha\bmod 1
with
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}^{n}
. Considering
\mathcal{F}:=(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
any almost additive sequence of continuous functions with respect to
R_{\alpha}
, by Theorem
1
there exists a continuous function
f:\mathbb{T}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|f_{n}-S_{n}f\|_{\infty}/n=0
.
Since every point is periodic with the same period, by Lemma
4
there exists a constant
L>0
such that
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|f_{n}-S_{n}f\|_{\infty}\leq L<\infty
. Notice that this uniform bound exists regardless of the sequence
\mathcal{F}
having bounded variation. Moreover, in this case,
\mathcal{F}
has bounded variation if and only if
(S_{n}f)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
has bounded variation. ∎

Remark
.

Example
2
is actually more general. In fact, by Lemma
4
we obtain the same result for maps having at least a dense set of periodic points with a bounded period. This class of examples include periodic maps in general.

Let us now investigate what happens with some systems without periodic points.

Example 3
.

(Uniquely ergodic maps) Let
X
be a compact metric space and consider
T:X\to X
a uniquely ergodic map. Let
\mathcal{F}:=(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be an almost additive sequence of continuous functions with respect to
T
and denote by
\nu
the unique
T
-invariant measure. Then, in particular

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\bigg\|S_{n}\phi-n\int_{X}\phi d\nu\bigg\|_{\infty}=0\quad\textrm{for all continuous functions $\phi:X\to\mathbb{R}$}.

(13)

By Theorem
1
, there exists a continuous function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|f_{n}-S_{n}f\|_{\infty}/n=0.

This together with (
13
) yields

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\bigg\|f_{n}-n\int_{X}fd\nu\bigg\|_{\infty}\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\|f_{n}-S_{n}f\|_{\infty}+\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\bigg\|S_{n}f-n\int_{X}fd\nu\bigg\|_{\infty}=0.

Hence, in the case of uniquely ergodic maps, there always exists a continuous function
f:~X\to~\mathbb{R}
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\bigg\|f_{n}-n\int_{X}fd\nu\bigg\|_{\infty}=0,

that is, the function given by Theorem
1
can be taken as the constant
\int_{X}fd\nu
.

Observe that the additive sequence
(S_{n}\int_{X}fd\nu)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
has bounded variation, and in this setup we can always answer
Question A
affirmatively. ∎

The following classical result is due to Gottshalk and Hedlund (see for example Theorem 4.2 in
[
FG21
]
).

Proposition 10
.

Let
X
be a compact metric space,
T:X\to X
a homeomorphism such that every orbit is dense, and
\varphi:X\to\mathbb{R}
a continuous function. Then the following assertions are equivalent:

1.

there exists a continuous function
q:X\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\varphi=q\circ T-q
;

2.

there exists a point
x_{0}\in X
such that
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}|S_{n}\varphi(x_{0})|<\infty.

3.

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|S_{n}\varphi\|_{\infty}<\infty
.

In the following simple example, we show a setup where Theorem
5
does not hold even in the additive case and with any strong regularity involved.

Example 4
.

(Irrational rotations) Let
R_{\alpha}:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{T}
be the irrational rotation on the torus, that is,
R_{\alpha}(x)=x+\alpha\bmod 1
with
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Q}
. The map
R_{\alpha}
is a uniquely ergodic (and also minimal) homeomorphism. In addition, let
f:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function which is not cohomologous to any constant. It follows in particular from Example
3
that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\bigg\|S_{n}f-n\int_{\mathbb{T}}fd\nu\bigg\|_{\infty}=0,

where
\nu
is the Lebesgue measure, that is, the unique
R_{\alpha}
-invariant measure on
\mathbb{T}
.

On the other hand, Proposition
10
readily implies that
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|S_{n}f-n\int_{\mathbb{T}}fd\nu\|_{\infty}=\infty
, regardless of the regularity of
f
.
∎

Observe that Example
4
also demonstrate that
Question B
cannot be positively answered for all setups in general.

Even though the Examples
2
,
3
and
4
are interesting from a pure theoretical point of view, they are not as attractive as examples involving setups with some kind of hyperbolicity, where we can find the most strong applications in thermodynamic formalism and dimension theory in general. We will address the hyperbolic and related setups in the next sections.

4.2
Hölder regularity of almost additive sequences

For the sake of simplicity, in this section we are considering only the case of left-sided full shifts of finite type, but most of the results can be properly obtained for two-sided full shifts of finite type, topologically mixing subshifts of finite type, repellers of topologically mixing
C^{1}
maps and locally maximal hyperbolic sets for topologically mixing
C^{1}
diffeomorphisms.

We start with a simple but useful auxiliary result.

Lemma 6
.

Let
X
be a topological space and
T:X\to X
a map. Let
\mathcal{A}:=(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\mathcal{B}:=(b_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be two sequences of functions such that
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|a_{n}-b_{n}\|<\infty
. Then,
\mathcal{A}
is almost additive with respect to
T
if and only if
\mathcal{B}
is almost additive with respect to
T
.

Proof.

Let
\xi_{n}:=a_{n}-b_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. By hypotheses, there exists
L>0
such that
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|\xi_{n}\|_{\infty}\leq L
. Suppose
\mathcal{A}
is almost additive with respect to
T
(and with constant
C>0
). Then, for all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\begin{split}b_{n}+b_{m}\circ T^{n}-(3L+C)&=a_{n}-\xi_{n}+a_{m}\circ T^{n}-\xi_{m}\circ T^{n}-(3L+C)\\
&=a_{n}+a_{m}\circ T^{n}-C+(-\xi_{n}-L)+(-\xi_{m}\circ T^{n}-L)-L\\
&\leq a_{n+m}-L\leq a_{n+m}-\xi_{n+m}=b_{n+m}.\end{split}

Analogously, we also have

b_{n+m}\leq b_{n}+b_{m}\circ T^{n}+(3L+C)\quad\textrm{for all $m,n\in\mathbb{N}$}.

Hence, the sequence
\mathcal{B}
is almost additive (with respect to
T
) and with constant
3L+C>0
. The same argument works to prove the almost additivity of sequence
\mathcal{A}
, and the result is proved.
∎

Let
\Sigma=\{1,\ldots,k\}
be a finite alphabet and let
\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}
be the space of sequences
x=(i_{1}i_{2}\cdots)
with
i_{n}\in\Sigma
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We define the
left-sided full shift map

\sigma\colon\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}\to\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}
by
\sigma(i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}\cdots)=(i_{2}i_{3}i_{4}\cdots)
. Given a probability measure
\mu
on
\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}
, for any fixed
n
-tuple
(j_{1},\ldots,j_{n})\in\Sigma^{n}
we write

\mu_{n}(C_{j_{1}\ldots j_{n}}):=\mu(\{x=(i_{1}i_{2}\cdots)\in\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}:i_{1}=j_{1},\ldots,i_{n}=j_{n}\}).

A probability measure
\mu
is said to be
quasi-Bernoulli
if
\mu_{1}(C_{j})>0
for every
j\in\Sigma
, and if there exists a constant
C\geq 1
such that

C^{-1}\leq\frac{\mu_{n+m}(C_{j_{1}\cdots j_{n+m}})}{\mu_{n}(C_{j_{1}\cdots j_{n}})\mu_{m}(C_{j_{n+1}\cdots j_{n+m}})}\leq C

for every
m,n\geq 1
and every
j_{1},\ldots,j_{n+m}\in\Sigma
. When one can take
C=1
, the measure
\mu
is said to be
Bernoulli
.

Given
x=(i_{1}i_{2}...)\in\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}
and
n\geq 1
, consider the set

C_{i_{1}\ldots i_{n}}(x):=\{y=(j_{1}j_{2}\cdots)\in\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}:j_{1}=i_{1},\ldots,j_{n}=i_{n}\}.

Now given an arbitrary measure
\nu
on
\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}
, consider the sequence of continuous functions
\mathcal{F}:=(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
given by
f_{n}(x):=\log\nu(C_{i_{1}\ldots i_{n}}(x))
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and every
x\in\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}
. It is easy to see that if
\nu
is quasi-Bernoulli then
\mathcal{F}
is almost additive. In this case, we say that the sequence
\mathcal{F}
is generated by the quasi-Bernoulli measure
\nu
.

Let
Bow(\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}},\sigma)
be the vector space of continuous functions satisfying the Bowen property with respect to the shift
\sigma:\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}\to\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}
.

Now lets us show how we can apply Theorem
5
to finally give a negative answer to
Question C
:

Theorem 11
.

Let
\sigma:\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}\to\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}
be the left-sided full shift. Then

•

there exists an almost additive sequence of continuous functions generated by a quasi-Bernoulli measure on
\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}
and which is not physically equivalent to any Hölder continuous function.

•

there exist almost additive sequences of Hölder continuous functions with bounded variation and which are not physically equivalent to any additive sequence generated by a Hölder continuous function.

Proof.

Let
\eta
be a quasi-Bernoulli measure on
\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}
and consider the sequence
\mathcal{H}=(h_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
generated by the measure
\eta
. Suppose there exist a constant
K>0
and a function
h\in Hol(\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}})
such that
\|h_{n}-S_{n}h\|_{\infty}\leq K<\infty
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. This readily gives that

e^{-K}e^{S_{n}h(x)}\leq\eta(C_{j_{1}\cdots j_{n}}(x))\leq e^{S_{n}h(x)}e^{K}

for all
x\in\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
P_{\sigma}(h)=P_{\sigma}(\mathcal{H})=0
(see Theorem 2.1 in
[
IY17
]
), we conclude that
\eta
is a Gibbs measure with respect to the Hölder continuous function
h
.

Example 2.10 in
[
BKM20
]
shows a quasi-Bernoulli measure
\nu
on
\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}
which is not Gibbs with respect to any Hölder continuous function. Now let
\mathcal{F}=(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be the sequence generated by
\nu
, that is,
f_{n}(x):=\log\nu(C_{i_{1}\ldots i_{n}}(x))
for all
x\in\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. This implies immediately that there is no
f\in Hol(\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}})
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|f_{n}-S_{n}f\|_{\infty}<\infty,

otherwise
\nu
would be Gibbs with respect to the Hölder function
f
. Let
Hol(\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}})
be the space of Hölder continuous functions. Since
Hol(\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}})\subset Bow(\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}},\sigma)
and
\mathcal{F}
has bounded variation, Corollary
6
guarantees that there is no Hölder continuous function
f
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\|f_{n}-S_{n}f\|_{\infty}=0.

(14)

Moreover, since
Hol(\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}})
is dense in the space of continuous functions on the compact space
\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}
(with respect to the sup norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
), for each
n\geq 1
there exists a Hölder continuous function
g_{n}:\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\|f_{n}-g_{n}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
. By Lemma
6
, the sequence
\mathcal{G}:=(g_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is almost additive. It is also clear that
\mathcal{G}
has bounded variation. Moreover, for every continuous function
g:\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathbb{R}
we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\|f_{n}-S_{n}g\|_{\infty}=0\quad\textrm{if and only if}\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\|g_{n}-S_{n}g\|_{\infty}=0.

(15)

So, if there exists a Hölder continuous function
f
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\|g_{n}-S_{n}f\|_{\infty}=0,

it follows directly from (
15
) that the Hölder continuous function
f
also satisfies (
14
), which is a contradiction. Therefore, there is no additive sequence generated by a Hölder continuous function which is physically equivalent to the sequence
\mathcal{G}
.

Notice that by this construction, for each
\alpha>0
one can find an almost additive sequence of Hölder continuous functions
\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}:=(g^{\alpha}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
\|g^{\alpha}_{n}-f_{n}\|_{\infty}\leq\alpha
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and not physically equivalent to any additive sequence generated by a Hölder continuous function, as desired.
∎

Theorem
11
gives a negative answer to the problem raised in
[
LLV22
]
(see Remark 3.4 in it). It is important emphasizing that these counter-examples of Theorem
11
also demonstrate that, regarding the thermodynamic formalism, multifractal analysis and ergodic optimization, we are not always able to simplify or reduce the study of almost additive sequences with bounded variation to the additive case with Hölder (or Lipschitz) potentials, which are the most natural and most studied class of functions in the classical setup. In other words, the physical equivalence proved in
[
Cun20
]
does not pass Hölder regularity in general. On the other hand, regarding more general regularity aspects, every almost additive sequence with bounded variation still could be physically equivalent to some additive sequence generated by a Bowen function.

4.3
Bowen regularity of almost and asymptotically additive sequences

In this section, based on physical equivalence associations, we study how one can relate more general regularity aspects of almost and asymptotically additive sequences to the regularity of single continuous functions. As we shall see, measures satisfying the Gibbs property and quasi-Bernoulli measures play an important role in our approach towards a more general treatment of regularity for sequences.

A probability measure
\mu
on
\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}
is said to be
Gibbs
with respect to a function
\phi:\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathbb{R}
when there exists a constant
K\geq 1
such that

K^{-1}\leq\frac{\mu_{n}(C_{j_{1}\ldots j_{n}})}{\exp(-nP_{\sigma}(\phi)+S_{n}\phi(x))}\leq K

(16)

for all
x\in C_{j_{1}\ldots j_{n}}
and
n\geq 1
, where
P_{\sigma}(\phi)
is the classical topological pressure of
\phi
with respect to the shift
\sigma:\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}\to\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}
.

We note that every Gibbs measure with respect to a function is quasi-Bernoulli. In fact, let
\mu
be a Gibbs measure with respect to a function
\phi
. By the definition, we have

\frac{K^{-1}\exp[-(n+m)P_{\sigma}(\phi)+S_{n+m}\phi(x)]}{K^{2}\exp(-nP_{\sigma}(\phi)+S_{n}\phi(x))\exp(-mP_{\sigma}(\phi)+S_{m}\phi(\sigma^{n}(x)))}\leq\frac{\mu_{n+m}(C_{j_{1}\cdots j_{n+m}})}{\mu_{n}(C_{j_{1}\cdots j_{n}})\mu_{m}(C_{j_{n+1}\cdots j_{n+m}})},

\frac{\mu_{n+m}(C_{j_{1}\cdots j_{n+m}})}{\mu_{n}(C_{j_{1}\cdots j_{n}})\mu_{m}(C_{j_{n+1}\cdots j_{n+m}})}\leq\frac{K\exp[-(n+m)P_{\sigma}(\phi)+S_{n+m}\phi(x)]}{K^{-2}\exp(-nP_{\sigma}(\phi)+S_{n}\phi(x))\exp(-mP_{\sigma}(\phi)+S_{m}\phi(\sigma^{n}(x)))}.

Since
S_{n+m}\phi=S_{n}\phi+S_{m}\phi\circ\sigma^{n}
, we finally obtain that

K^{-3}\leq\frac{\mu_{n+m}(C_{j_{1}\cdots j_{n+m}})}{\mu_{n}(C_{j_{1}\cdots j_{n}})\mu_{m}(C_{j_{n+1}\cdots j_{n+m}})}\leq K^{3}:=\widetilde{K},

as desired. This result can be extended to almost additive sequences:

Proposition 12
.

Every Gibbs measure with respect to an almost additive sequence is quasi-Bernoulli.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{F}=(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be an almost additive sequence with Gibbs measure
\mu
with uniform constant
K>0
(recall the definition in (
11
)). By almost additivity, there exists
C>0
such that
\|f_{n+m}-f_{n}-f_{m}\circ\sigma^{n}\|_{\infty}\leq C
for all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
. Proceeding as in the additive case above, one can check that

(K^{3}e^{C})^{-1}\leq\frac{\mu_{n+m}(C_{j_{1}\cdots j_{n+m}})}{\mu_{n}(C_{j_{1}\cdots j_{n}})\mu_{m}(C_{j_{n+1}\cdots j_{n+m}})}\leq K^{3}e^{C}.

Hence,
\mu
is quasi-Bernoulli, as desired.
∎

Proposition 13
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a sequence of continuous functions with an equilibrium measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}
satisfying the Gibbs property with respect to
\mathcal{F}
. Then,
\mu
is quasi-Bernoulli if and only if
\mathcal{F}
is almost additive.

Proof.

By Proposition
12
if
\mu
is Gibbs with respect to
\mathcal{F}
then it is quasi-Bernoulli. For the converse just use the Gibbs property together with the fact that every sequence generated by a quasi-Bernoulli measure is almost additive. ∎

Remark
.

Proposition
13
is, in particular, related to Theorem 2.8 in
[
BKM20
]
for planar matrix cocycles in general.

The following result is a characterization of almost additive sequences for which we can answer affirmatively the
Question A
and the
Question B
.

Theorem 14
.

Let
\mathcal{F}=(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be an almost additive sequence of continuous functions with respect to the left-sided full shift
\sigma:\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}\to\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}
and satisfying the bounded variation property. Then, the following statements are equivalent:

1.

the equilibrium measure of
\mathcal{F}
is Gibbs with respect to some continuous Bowen function;

2.

there exists a continuous Bowen function
f:\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\|f_{n}-S_{n}f\|_{\infty}=0.

3.

there exists a continuous Bowen function
f:\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|f_{n}-S_{n}f\|_{\infty}<\infty.

Proof.

Let us start proving that 1 implies 3. Since
\mathcal{F}
has bounded variation, it follows by Theorem 6 in
[
Mum06
]
(see also Theorem 5 in
[
Bar06
]
) that
\mathcal{F}
has a unique equilibrium measure
\eta
, which is also Gibbs with respect to
\mathcal{F}
. By assumption,
\eta
is also Gibbs with respect to some continuous function
f
. Then, there exist constants
K_{1}\geq 1
and
K_{2}\geq 1
such that

K_{1}^{-1}\leq\frac{\eta(C_{i_{1}\ldots i_{n}}(x))}{\exp[-nP_{\sigma}(\mathcal{F})+f_{n}(x)]}\leq K_{1}\quad\textrm{and}\quad K_{2}^{-1}\leq\frac{\eta(C_{i_{1}\ldots i_{n}}(x))}{\exp[-nP_{\sigma}(\mathcal{F})+S_{n}f(x)]}\leq K_{2}

(17)

for all
x\in\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. This readily implies that
|f_{n}(x)-S_{n}f(x)|\leq\log{K_{1}K_{2}}
for all
x\in X
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, which yields 3.

Conversely, suppose 3 holds. Then, there exists a constant
K_{3}>0
and a continuous function
f\in Bow(\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}},\sigma)
such that
\|f_{n}-S_{n}f\|_{\infty}\leq K_{3}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence, by the Gibbs property with respect to
\mathcal{F}
in (
17
), we get that

(K_{1}e^{K_{3}})^{-1}=K_{1}^{-1}e^{-K_{3}}\leq\frac{\eta(C_{i_{1}\ldots i_{n}}(x))}{\exp[-nP_{\sigma}(\mathcal{F})+S_{n}f(x)]}\leq K_{1}e^{K_{3}}

for all
x\in\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, as desired. Corollary
6
immediately gives that items 2 and 3 are equivalent, and the result is proved.
∎

Remark
.

We note that Theorem
14
is related to the characterization result obtained in Theorem 2.9 in
[
BKM20
]
for planar matrix cocycles, but now also including Bowen functions in general and requiring only physical equivalence instead of the stronger hypotheses of uniformly bounded sequences.

We also know exactly what are the functions and almost additive sequences admitting measures satisfying the Gibbs property:

Proposition 15
.

Let
\sigma:\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}
be the left-sided full shift. Then

•

\phi\in Bow(\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}},\sigma)
if and only if there exists a Gibbs measure with respect to
\phi
.

•

an almost additive sequence
\mathcal{F}
has bounded variation if and only if there exists a Gibbs measure with respect to
\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

Let
\mu
be a Gibbs measure with respect to a function
\phi
. By definition, there exists
K\geq 1
such that

K^{-2}\leq\exp(S_{n}\phi(x)-S_{n}\phi(y))\leq K^{2}

for all
x,y\in C_{i_{1}\ldots i_{n}}
and all
n\geq 1
. This implies that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\sup\{|S_{n}\phi(x)-S_{n}\phi(y)|:x,y\in C_{i_{1}\ldots i_{n}}\}\leq 2\log K<\infty,

and
\phi\in Bow(\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}},\sigma)
. On the other hand, when
\phi\in Bow(\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}},\sigma)
there exists an invariant measure which is the unique equilibrium state for
\phi
. Moreover, this measure is also Gibbs with respect to
\phi
(see the classical works
[
Bow75a
]
and
[
Bow75
]
).

For the nonadditive part, Theorem 6 in
[
Mum06
]
(or Theorem 5
[
Bar06
]
) guarantees the existence of unique equilibrium measures satisfying the Gibbs property for almost additive sequences with bounded variation. The converse follows exactly as in the additive case.
∎

Now let
T:X\to X
be a continuous map, where
X
is a compact metric space. Recall that a continuous function
f
is said to be a coboundary with respect to a map
T:X\to X
when there exists a continuous function
q
such that
f(x)=q(T(x))-q(x)
for all
x\in X
. Following
[
BJ02
]
and
[
Bou21
]
, we say that a continuous function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
is a
weak coboundary
if it is the uniform limit of coboudaries. It is well known that
f
is a weak coboundary if and only if
\int_{X}fd\mu=0
for every
T
-invariant measure
\mu
(see for example
[
BJ02
]
,
[
KR01
]
, and
[
Kri71
]
). Based on this and on Lemma
5
,
f
is also said to be weak coboundary if and only if the sequence
(S_{n}f/n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges uniformly to zero.

We denote
Wcob(X,T)
the vector space of weak coboundaries and
Cob(X,T)
the vector space of coboundaries with respect to the map
T:X\to X
. In fact, one can check that every coboundary is a Walters function. On the other hand, weak coboundaries form a space that is strictly larger than the space of coboundaries in general (see for example
[
BJ02
]
and
[
Koc13
]
).

Given two subspaces
U
and
V
in general, we define the new
sum
subspace
U+V
in the natural way, that is,
U+V=\{u+v:u\in U\textrm{ and }v\in V\}
. When we have a
direct sum
, we shall write
U\oplus V
.

Proposition 16
.

Let
\sigma:\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}\to\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}
be the left-sided full shift. For each continuous function
\varphi\in Wcob(\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}},\sigma)+Bow(\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}},\sigma)
there exists an almost additive sequence of continuous functions
\mathcal{F}:=(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfying the Walters property and such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\|f_{n}-S_{n}\varphi\|_{\infty}=0.

In particular, if
\varphi\in Bow(\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}},\sigma)
then

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|f_{n}-S_{n}\varphi\|_{\infty}<\infty.

Proof.

Since
\varphi\in Wcob(\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}},\sigma)+Bow(\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}},\sigma)
, there exist functions
u\in Wcob(\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}},\sigma)
and
\xi\in Bow(\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}},\sigma)
such that
\varphi=u+\xi
. Proposition
15
guarantees the existence of a measure
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}
which is Gibbs with respect to
\xi
. In particular, there exists a constant
K\geq 1
such that

K^{-1}\leq\frac{\nu(C_{i_{1}\ldots i_{n}}(x))}{\exp(-nP_{\sigma}(\xi)+S_{n}\xi(x))}\leq K

for all
x\in\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}
and all
n\geq 1
. This implies that

|\log\nu(C_{i_{1}\ldots i_{n}}(x))+nP_{\sigma}(\xi)-S_{n}\xi(x)|\leq\log K\quad\textrm{for all $x\in\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}$ and all $n\geq 1$},

(18)

Now define
\mathcal{G}=(g_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
as the sequence given by
g_{n}(x):=\log\nu(C_{i_{1}\ldots i_{n}}(x))
. Since every Gibbs measure is quasi-Bernoulli (see Proposition
12
), the sequence of continuous functions
\mathcal{G}
generated by the quasi-Bernoulli measure
\nu
is almost additive and satisfies the Walters property. Now consider the sequence
\mathcal{F}=(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
given by
f_{n}:=g_{n}+nP(\xi)
. The sequence
\mathcal{F}
is clearly almost additive, satisfies the Walters property and it follows directly from (
18
) that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|f_{n}-S_{n}\xi\|_{\infty}<\infty

(19)

Moreover, since
\varphi-\xi\in Wcob(\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}},\sigma)
, we have that
(S_{n}\varphi)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is physically equivalent to
(S_{n}\xi)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. This together with (
19
) readily gives that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\|f_{n}-S_{n}\varphi\|_{\infty}=0,

as desired.
∎

In general terms, Proposition
16
is saying that every additive sequence generated by a Bowen continuous function with null topological pressure is physically equivalent to an almost additive sequence generated by some quasi-Bernoulli measure.

In order to simplify the notation, let us denote
Hol:=Hol(\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}},\sigma)
,
Bow:=Bow(\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}},\sigma)
and
Wcob(\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}},\sigma)=Wcob
. Moreover, let us define
Hof:=Hof(\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}},\sigma)
as the subset of continuous functions which are not Bowen but have a unique equilibrium measure. We will refer to
Hof
as the set of
Hofbauer
functions (see
[
Hof77
]
).

Furthermore, consider the spaces
WB:=WB(\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}},\sigma):=Wcob(\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}},\sigma)+Bow(\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}},\sigma)
and
\widetilde{WB}:=C(\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}})/WB(\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}},\sigma)
, where
C(\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}})
is the space of continuous functions on
\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}
. We note that it is expected that
Hof\cap WB\neq\emptyset
and
Hof\cap\widetilde{WB}\neq\emptyset
(see for example
[
PZ06
]
,
[
Wal07
]
,
[
Hu08
]
,
[
IT12
]
,
[
CT13
]
).

Now let
f:\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function. It is clear that if
(S_{n}f)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is physically equivalent to some
\widetilde{f}\in Bow(\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}},\sigma)
then we must have
f\in Wcob(\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}},\sigma)+Bow(\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}},\sigma)
. Based on this, Proposition
13
, Theorem
14
and Proposition
16
, we can propose a classification of almost additive sequences with respect to the left-sided full shift (see Figure
1
).

We separate the following types of almost additive sequences with respect to the left-sided full shift
\sigma:\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}\to\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}
:

•

Type 1.
Almost additive sequences
\mathcal{F}_{1}
with bounded variation and with the unique equilibrium measure satisfying the Gibbs property with respect to some function
\phi\in Bow
. They are always physically equivalent to
(S_{n}\psi)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
for some
\psi\in WB
(i.e.
Bow
) and never physically equivalent to any additive sequence
(S_{n}\xi)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
\xi\in\widetilde{WB}
.

•

Type 2.
Almost additive sequences
\mathcal{F}_{2}
with bounded variation and with equilibrium measure not satisfying the Gibbs property with respect to any continuous function. In this case, the equilibrium is only weak-Gibbs with respect to some continuous function. These sequences are always physically equivalent to some
(S_{n}\psi)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
\psi\in Hof\cap\widetilde{WB}
. As a consequence, there is no
\xi\in WB
such that
(S_{n}\xi)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is physically equivalent to
\mathcal{F}_{2}
.

•

Type 3.
Almost additive sequences
\mathcal{F}_{3}
without the bounded variation condition but admitting a unique equilibrium measure. They are either physically equivalent to some
(S_{n}\psi_{1})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
\psi_{1}\in Hof\cap\widetilde{WB}
or they are physically equivalent to some
(S_{n}\psi_{2})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
\psi_{2}\in Hof\cap WB
, which immediately implies
\psi_{2}\in Bow
(see Figure
1
).

•

Type 4.
Almost additive sequences
\mathcal{F}_{4}
with more than one equilibrium measure. They are always physically equivalent to some additive sequence
(S_{n}\psi)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
\psi\in\widetilde{WB}/Hof
and, consequently, never physically equivalent to any additive sequence
(S_{n}\xi)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
\xi\in WB
.

Figure 1:
The different types of almost additive sequences and their physical equivalence relations with the space of continuous functions.

Now let us show how one can construct examples for the types 1, 3 and 4. Our general simple strategy to search examples of asymptotically and almost additive sequences is to start with a fixed function
\phi
in some ”appropriate” already known subset of continuous functions and from that to build the nonadditive sequence physically equivalent to
(S_{n}\phi)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Examples of Types 1, 3 and 4.

Sequences of Type 1 are, for instance, the ones given by Proposition
16
, which also include the Hölder continuous case. Notice that those sequences are always physically equivalent to some almost additive sequence generated by a quasi-Bernoulli measure.

Sequences of Type 3 are constructed in the following way. Letting
\phi\in Bow
and
\xi\in Wcob/Bow
, we have
\psi:=\phi+\xi\in Hof\cap WB
. Arguing as in the proof of Theorem
11
, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a continuous function
f_{n}\in Hol
such that
\|f_{n}-S_{n}\psi\|_{\infty}\leq 1
(by the density of Hölder continuous functions). Since
\psi\notin Bow
, the almost additive sequence
\mathcal{F}=(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
does not have bounded variation. Moreover,
\mathcal{F}
is almost additive by Lemma
6
. On the other hand,
\psi\in WB
implies that
(S_{n}\psi)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is physically equivalent to some additive sequence
(S_{n}f)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
f\in Bow
. Then,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\|f_{n}-S_{n}f\|_{\infty}\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\|f_{n}-S_{n}\psi\|_{\infty}+\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\|S_{n}\psi-S_{n}f\|_{\infty}=0.

We note that, by Proposition
15
, the unique equilibrium measure for
\mathcal{F}
does not satisfy the Gibbs property with respect to
\mathcal{F}
but is, nevertheless, Gibbs with respect to some Bowen function and, consequently, also quasi-Bernoulli. As in Type 1, these sequences are always physically equivalent to some almost additive sequence generated by a quasi-Bernoulli measure, which is a sequence solely composed of locally constant functions.

For the other case of Type 3 sequences, we consider a function
\psi\in Hof\cap\widetilde{WB}
and proceed as before to find an almost additive sequence
\mathcal{G}=(g_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of Hölder continuous functions such that
\|g_{n}-S_{n}\psi\|_{\infty}\leq 1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. In particular,
\mathcal{G}
does not have bounded variation, is physically equivalent to
(S_{n}\psi)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and not physically equivalent to any additive sequence generated by a Bowen function. One more time, Proposition
15
guarantees that the unique equilibrium measure for
\mathcal{G}
does not satisfy the Gibbs property with respect to
\mathcal{G}
. In this case, the equilibrium measure still might be weak Gibbs with respect to some continuous function.

In order to show an example of Type 4, we just start with some continuous function
\psi:\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathbb{R}
with more than one equilibrium measure. It is clear that
\psi\in\widetilde{WB}/Hof
. Following in the very same manner as for Type 3, we are able to find almost additive sequences without bounded variation and which are physically equivalent to
(S_{n}\psi)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, as desired. ∎

Before discussing almost additive sequences of Type 2, let us settle
Question D
, which is regarding regularity of asymptotically additive sequences.

We recall that a measure
\mu
on
\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}
(not necessarily invariant) is said to be
weak Gibbs
with respect to a function
\phi:\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathbb{R}
when there exists a sequence
(K_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset[1,\infty)
with
\log K_{n}/n=0
and such that

K_{n}^{-1}\leq\frac{\mu_{n}(C_{j_{1}\ldots j_{n}})}{\exp(-nP_{\sigma}(\phi)+S_{n}\phi(x))}\leq K_{n}

for all
x\in C_{j_{1}\ldots j_{n}}
and
n\geq 1
. The same definition is also naturally extended to the nonadditive case (see for example
[
Bar06
]
and
[
IY17
]
).

Asymptotically additive case.

Fix a continuous Hofbauer function
\psi\in Hof\cap~\widetilde{WB}
. Since every almost additive sequence admits a (not necessarily invariant) weak Gibbs measure (
[
Bar06
]
), in particular, the function
\psi
admits some weak Gibbs measure
\nu
on
\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}
, that is, there exists a sequence
(K_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset[1,\infty)
with
\log K_{n}/n=0
such that

|\log\nu(C_{i_{1}\ldots i_{n}}(x))+nP(\psi)-S_{n}\psi(x)|\leq\log K_{n}\quad\textrm{for all $x\in\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}$ and $n\geq 1$},

which readily implies that the sequence
\mathcal{G}:=(f_{n}+nP(\psi))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is asymptotically additive and also physically equivalent to
(S_{n}\psi)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, where
f_{n}(x):=\log\nu(C_{i_{1}\ldots i_{n}}(x))
for all
x\in\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Notice that
\mathcal{G}
has a unique equilibrium measure and satisfies the Walters property (i.e. also has bounded variation). Moreover, since
\psi\notin WB
, there is no additive sequence generated by a Bowen function which is physically equivalent to
\mathcal{G}
. In particular,
\mathcal{G}
is not physically equivalent to any additive sequence generated by a locally constant function.

Proceeding as before (see the proof of Theorem
11
), for each
\alpha>0
there exists a sequence
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}:=(h^{\alpha}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of Hölder continuous functions such that
\|h^{\alpha}_{n}-f_{n}-nP(\psi)\|_{\infty}\leq\alpha
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. This implies that
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
has bounded variation and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\|h^{\alpha}_{n}-S_{n}\psi\|_{\infty}=0\quad\textrm{with $\psi\in Hof\cap\widetilde{WB}$}.

That is, for each
\alpha>0
the sequence
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
is asymptotically additive, has bounded variation, admits a unique equilibrium measure but is not physically equivalent to any additive sequence generated by a Bowen function. In particular, the sequences
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
are not physically equivalent to any additive sequence generated by a Hölder continuous function.

These examples demonstrate that
Question D
cannot be affirmatively answered.
∎

Finally, let us consider the second type of almost additive sequences of potentials.

Examples of Type 2 (?)
We note that
there exists an almost additive sequence of Type 2 if and only if there exists a quasi-Bernoulli measure not Gibbs with respect to any continuous function.
In fact, suppose
\mathcal{F}
is a sequence of Type 2, that is,
\mathcal{F}
has bounded variation and has the unique equilibrium measure
\mu
not Gibbs with respect to any continuous function. Since
\mathcal{F}
is Gibbs with respect to
\mathcal{F}
, Proposition
12
guarantees that
\mu
is a quasi-Bernoulli measure.

Conversely, considering
\mu
a quasi-Bernoulli measure, the sequence
\mathcal{F}=(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
generated by
\mu
is almost additive and has bounded variation. Since we are assuming
\mu
not Gibbs with respect to any continuous function, by Proposition
15
and Corollary
6
, the sequence
\mathcal{F}
cannot be physically equivalent to any Bowen function. That is,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\|f_{n}-S_{n}\psi\|_{\infty}=0

for some
\psi\in\widetilde{WB}
. Since
\mathcal{F}
has bounded variation, it follows from Theorem 6 in
[
Mum06
]
that
\mathcal{F}
has a unique equilibrium measure, which implies that
\psi\in Hof
. Therefore, we conclude that
\psi\in Hof\cap\widetilde{WB}
and
\mathcal{F}
is indeed a sequence of Type 2, as desired.

We still don’t know about the existence of such sequences in this setup or in any other setup in general. ∎

4.4
Some open questions and final comments

As we saw in the previous subsection, the more general regularity problem relating sequences with bounded variation and Bowen functions is still open, and depends on the existence of sequences of Type 2. Then, inspired by
[
BKM20
]
and based on Proposition
13
and Theorem
14
, we ask the following:

Q1:

is there a Bowen continuous function
A:\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}\to GL(d,\mathbb{R})
generating a matrix cocycle
\mathcal{A}:\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}\times\mathbb{N}\to GL(d,\mathbb{R})
where the sequence
\mathcal{F}=(\log\|\mathcal{A}(x,n)\|)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is almost additive of Type 2 ? For what dimensions
d\geq 2
this should be possible ?

A negative answer to
Q1
would give a concrete and important class of examples where we can reduce the nonadditive setup directly to the additive one, in the spirit of
[
BKM20
]
. On the other side, a positive answer would fill the only type of sequences that are missing in our proposed classification.

Now let us consider again the left-sided full shift of finite type
\sigma:\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}\to\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}
. Denote by
Wal:=Wal(\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}},\sigma)
the vector space of continuous Walters functions with respect to
\sigma
, and
Wal_{\mathcal{L}}:=Wal_{\mathcal{L}}(\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}},\sigma)
the vector space of all functions
f\in Wal(\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}},\sigma)
and such that
\mathcal{L}f=0
, where
\mathcal{L}
is the transfer operator given by

[\mathcal{L}f](y)=\frac{1}{\#\Sigma}\sum_{\sigma(x)=y}f(x)\quad\textrm{for all $y\in\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}$.}

T. Bousch (
[
Bou02
]
) gave a characterization of Walters functions as

Wal=Cob\oplus Wal_{\mathcal{L}}\oplus\mathbb{R}.

Based on this, we can ask if

Q2:

Do we also have
Bow=WCob\oplus Wal_{\mathcal{L}}\oplus\mathbb{R}
? or even
Bow=Wcob+Wal
?

Since
Wal
is a proper subset of
Bow
(see
[
Wal07
]
), it is clear that
Wcob+Wal\subseteq Wcob+Bow
(not necessarily a proper inclusion). Then, a negative answer to
Q2
would guarantee the existence of a function
\psi\in(Wcob+Bow)/(Wcob+Wal)
. In this case, Proposition
16
would immediately give an almost additive sequence
\mathcal{F}
satisfying the Walters property and which is physically equivalent to
(S_{n}\psi)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Hence,
\mathcal{F}
would be an example of an almost additive sequence with Walters regularity but not physically equivalent to any additive sequence generated by a Walters function (or any locally constant function in particular). On the other hand, a positive answer to
Q2
would imply that for any Bowen function
\phi
there exists a Walter function
\psi
such that
(S_{n}\phi)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(S_{n}\psi)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are physically equivalent. Moreover, Theorem
5
would give that
(S_{n}\phi)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is cohomologous to
(S_{n}\psi)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in the nonadditive sense of definitions
1
,
2
and
3
. In this case, regarding the study of additive and nonadditive thermodynamic properties with respect to subshifts of finite type, Bowen regularity and Walters regularity would be actually equivalent. Notice that, since the additive notion of cohomology is much stronger than the nonadditive version introduced in section
2
, this equivalence between Bowen and Walters functions wouldn’t violate the classical Livšic Theorem.

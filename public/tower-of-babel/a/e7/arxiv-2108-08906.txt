Deformations and Cohomologies of Relative Rota–Baxter Operators on Lie Algebroids and Koszul–Vinberg Structures
Meijun LIU a, Jiefeng LIU a and Yunhe SHENG b

Abstract
Given a Lie algebroid with a representation, we construct a graded Lie algebra whose Maurer–Cartan elements characterize relative Rota–Baxter operators on Lie algebroids. We give the cohomology of relative Rota–Baxter operators and study infinitesimal deformations and extendability of order n n deformations to order n + 1 n+1 deformations of relative Rota–Baxter operators in terms of this cohomology theory. We also construct a graded Lie algebra on the space of multi-derivations of a vector bundle whose Maurer–Cartan elements characterize left-symmetric algebroids. We show that there is a homomorphism from the controlling graded Lie algebra of relative Rota–Baxter operators on Lie algebroids to the controlling graded Lie algebra of left-symmetric algebroids. Consequently, there is a natural homomorphism from the cohomology groups of a relative Rota–Baxter operator to the deformation cohomology groups of the associated left-symmetric algebroid. As applications, we give the controlling graded Lie algebra and the cohomology theory of Koszul–Vinberg structures on left-symmetric algebroids.

Introduction

In this paper we use Maurer–Cartan elements to study deformations and cohomologies of relative Rota–Baxter operators on Lie algebroids. Applications are given to study deformations and cohomologies of Koszul–Vinberg structures on left-symmetric algebroids.

1.1
Relative Rota–Baxter operators on Lie algebroids

and Koszul–Vinberg structures

The concept of Rota–Baxter operators on associative algebras was introduced by G. Baxter
[
3
]
and G.-C. Rota
[
40
,
41
]
in the 1960s. It also plays an important role in the Connes–Kreimer’s algebraic approach
[
8
]
to the renormalization in perturbative quantum field theory.
In
[
23
]
,
Kupershmidt introduced the notion of a relative Rota–Baxter operator (also called an
\mathcal{O}
-operator) on a Lie algebra in order to better understand the relationship between the classical Yang–Baxter equation and the related integrable systems. In addition, the defining relationship of a relative Rota–Baxter operator was also called the Schouten curvature in
[
22
]
. See
[
6
,
11
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
39
,
45
]
for more details on relative Rota–Baxter operators and their applications.

The notion of a Lie algebroid was introduced by Pradines in 1967, which
is a generalization of Lie algebras and tangent bundles.
See
[
30
]
for the general
theory about Lie algebroids. Relative Rota–Baxter operators (also called
\mathcal{O}
-operators) on Lie algebroids were introduced in
[
28
]
as a method to construct left-symmetric algebroids. The notion of a left-symmetric algebroid is a geometric generalization of a left-symmetric algebra (also called pre-Lie algebras, see
the survey article
[
5
]
for more details).
See
[
4
,
28
,
33
,
34
]
for more details and applications of left-symmetric algebroids.

In
[
26
]
, motivated by the theory of Lie bialgebroids
[
31
]
, the notion of a left-symmetric bialgebroid was introduced as a geometric generalization of a left-symmetric bialgebra
[
2
]
. The double of a left-symmetric bialgebroid is not a left-symmetric algebroid anymore, but a pre-symplectic algebroid
[
27
]
. This result is parallel to the fact that
the double of a Lie bialgebroid is a Courant algebroid
[
29
]
. As a Poisson structure
\pi
on a manifold gives rise to a Lie bialgebroid, a Koszul–Vinberg structure
H
on a flat manifold gives rise to a left-symmetric bialgebroid. In particular, if the Koszul–Vinberg structure
H
is nondegenerate, the inverse of
H
is a pseudo-Hessian structure
[
42
,
43
]
on a flat manifold.
Therefore, Koszul–Vinberg structures and pseudo-Hessian structures are respectively symmetric analogues of Poisson structures and symplectic structures. See
[
1
,
4
,
47
]
for recent studies on Koszul–Vinberg structures.

1.2
Deformations and cohomologies

The theory of deformation plays a prominent role in mathematics and physics.
The idea of treating deformation as a tool to study the algebraic structures was introduced by Gerstenhaber in his work on associative algebras
[
15
,
16
]
and then was extended to Lie algebras by Nijenhuis and Richardson
[
36
,
38
]
. One remarkable
result in Poisson geometry is that M. Kontsevich
[
21
]
proved that every Poisson manifold has a deformation quantization. There is a well known slogan, often attributed to Deligne, Drinfeld and Kontsevich: every reasonable deformation theory is controlled by a differential graded Lie algebra, determined up to quasi-isomorphisms.

A suitable deformation theory of an algebraic structure can be summarized as the following general principle: on the one hand, for a given object with an algebraic structure, there should exist a differential graded Lie algebra whose Maurer–Cartan elements characterize deformations of this object. On the other hand, there should exist a suitable cohomology so that the infinitesimal of a formal deformation can be identified with a cohomology class, and then a theory of the obstruction to the integration of an infinitesimal deformation can be developed using this cohomology theory. It is well-known that deformations of Poisson structures are controlled by the differential graded Lie algebra constructed by the Schouten–Nijenhuis bracket of multi-vector fields. Infinitesimal deformations and extendibility of order
n
deformations of a Poisson structure are characterized in terms of the Poisson cohomology
[
20
,
25
]
. There also exists a differential graded Lie algebra and a deformation cohomology given by M. Crainic and I. Moerdijk in
[
9
]
on the space of multi-derivations which controls deformations of Lie algebroids. See
[
13
,
14
]
for more details on simultaneous deformations of algebras and morphisms and their applications in Poisson geometry.

1.3
Summary of the results and outline of the paper

Since Koszul–Vinberg structures are symmetric analogues of Poisson structures, while there is a full developed deformation and cohomology theories for Poisson structures, it is natural to develop the deformation and cohomology theories for Koszul–Vinberg structures.
Note that a Koszul–Vinberg structure on a left-symmetric algebroid is a relative Rota–Baxter operator on its sub-adjacent Lie algebroid with respect to a certain representation (Proposition
6.5
). Thus we develop the deformation and cohomology theories for relative Rota–Baxter operators on Lie algebroids first.
Inspired by the construction of the differential graded Lie algebra controlling deformations of a relative Rota–Baxter operator on a Lie algebra in
[
44
]
, we construct a suitable differential graded Lie algebra that controls deformations of relative Rota–Baxter operators on Lie algebroids. See
[
10
,
44
]
for more details on cohomologies and deformations of relative Rota–Baxter operators on Lie algebras and associative algebras. Following the idea of M. Crainic and I. Moerdijk in
[
9
]
, we also construct a differential graded Lie algebra that controls deformations of a left-symmetric algebroid. There is a natural homomorphism from the controlling algebra of relative Rota–Baxter operators to the controlling algebra of left-symmetric algebroids.

Using
the controlling algebra of relative Rota–Baxter operators on Lie algebroids, we construct a differential graded Lie algebra whose Maurer–Cartan elements are Koszul–Vinberg structures. Consequently, we establish a cohomology theory for Koszul–Vinberg structures. We hope that our study on Koszul–Vinberg structures will draw more attention to the geometry of Koszul–Vinberg structures.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, first we construct a differential graded Lie algebra that controls deformations of relative Rota–Baxter operators on Lie algebroids. Then we give the cohomology theories of relative Rota–Baxter operators on Lie algebroids induced by this differential graded Lie algebra. In Section
3
, we give the cohomology of Rota–Baxter operators on Lie algebroids and analyze the cohomology of the Rota–Baxter operator on an action Lie algebroid. In Section
4
, first we show that infinitesimal deformations of a relative Rota–Baxter operator are classified by the first cohomology group. Then for an order
n
deformation, we define its obstruction class, which is a cohomology class in the second cohomology group, and show that an order
n
deformation of a relative Rota–Baxter operator is extendable if and only if its obstruction class is trivial.
In Section
5
, we construct a graded Lie algebra whose Maurer–Cartan elements are precisely left-symmetric algebroids. The deformation cohomology of left-symmetric algebroids can be given directly using this graded Lie algebra. We show that there is a homomorphism from the controlling graded Lie algebra of relative Rota–Baxter operators on Lie algebroids to the controlling graded Lie algebra of left-symmetric algebroids. Consequently, there is a natural homomorphism from the cohomology groups of a relative Rota–Baxter operator to the deformation cohomology groups of the associated left-symmetric algebroid.
In Section
6
, we give the deformation and cohomology theories of Koszul–Vinberg structures on left-symmetric algebroids as applications of the above general framework.

2
Maurer–Cartan characterizations and cohomologies

of relative Rota–Baxter operators on Lie algebroids

2.1
The controlling algebra of relative Rota–Baxter operators

on Lie algebroids

In this subsection, given a Lie algebroid with a representation we construct a graded Lie algebra whose Maurer–Cartan elements characterize relative Rota–Baxter operators on Lie algebroids. Consequently, we obtain the differential graded Lie algebra that controls deformations of a relative Rota–Baxter operator.

Definition 2.1
.

A
Lie algebroid
structure on a vector bundle
\mathcal{A}\longrightarrow M
is
a pair that consists of a Lie algebra structure
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathcal{A}}
on
the section space
\Gamma(\mathcal{A})
and a bundle map

a_{\mathcal{A}}\colon\mathcal{A}\longrightarrow TM
, called the anchor, such that the
following relation is satisfied:

\displaystyle[x,fy]_{\mathcal{A}}=f[x,y]_{\mathcal{A}}+a_{\mathcal{A}}(x)(f)y,\qquad\forall f\in C^{\infty}(M),\quad x,y\in\Gamma(\mathcal{A}).

When the image of
a_{\mathcal{A}}
is of constant rank, we call
\mathcal{A}
a
regular Lie algebroid
.

For a vector bundle
E\longrightarrow M
, we denote by
\mathfrak{D}(E)
the gauge Lie algebroid of the frame bundle
\mathcal{F}(E)
, which is also called the covariant differential operator bundle of
E
. See
[
30
]
for more details on the gauge Lie algebroid.

Let
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathcal{A}},a_{\mathcal{A}})
and
(\mathcal{B},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathcal{B}},a_{\mathcal{B}})
be two Lie
algebroids (with the same base), a
base-preserving homomorphism

from
\mathcal{A}
to
\mathcal{B}
is a bundle map
\varphi\colon\mathcal{A}\longrightarrow\mathcal{B}
such
that

\displaystyle a_{\mathcal{B}}\circ\varphi=a_{\mathcal{A}},\qquad\varphi[x,y]_{\mathcal{A}}=[\varphi(x),\varphi(y)]_{\mathcal{B}},\qquad\forall x,y\in\Gamma(\mathcal{A}).

Recall that a
representation
of a Lie algebroid
\mathcal{A}

on a vector bundle
E
is a base-preserving morphism
\rho
form
\mathcal{A}
to the Lie algebroid
\mathfrak{D}(E)
.
Denote a representation by
(E;\rho).

The
dual representation
of a Lie algebroid
\mathcal{A}
on
E^{*}
is the bundle map
\rho^{*}\colon\mathcal{A}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{D}(E^{*})
given by

\displaystyle\langle\rho^{*}(x)(\xi),u\rangle=a_{\mathcal{A}}(x)\langle\xi,u\rangle-\langle\xi,\rho(x)(u)\rangle,\qquad\forall x\in\Gamma(\mathcal{A}),\quad\xi\in\Gamma(E^{*}),\quad u\in\Gamma(E).

Given a representation
(E;\rho)
, the cohomology of
\mathcal{A}
with coefficients in
E
is the cohomology of the cochain complex

(\oplus_{k=0}^{+\infty}C^{k}(\mathcal{A},E),\partial_{\rho})
, where
C^{k}(\mathcal{A},E)=\Gamma(\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{k}\mathcal{A},E))
and the coboundary operator
\partial_{\rho}\colon C^{k}(\mathcal{A},E)\to C^{k+1}(\mathcal{A},E)

is defined by

\displaystyle\partial_{\rho}\varpi(x_{1},\dots,x_{k+1}){}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{i+1}\rho(x_{i})\varpi(x_{1},\dots,\hat{x_{i}},\dots,x_{k+1})

\displaystyle+\sum_{i<j}(-1)^{i+j}\varpi([x_{i},x_{j}]_{\mathcal{A}},x_{1},\dots,\hat{x_{i}},\dots,\hat{x_{j}},\dots,x_{k+1}),

for
\varpi\in C^{k}(\mathcal{A},E)
and
x_{1},\dots,x_{k+1}\in\Gamma(\mathcal{A})
.

Definition 2.2
.

A
\mathsf{LieRep}
pair
is a pair of a Lie algebroid
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathcal{A}},a_{\mathcal{A}})
and a representation
\rho
of
\mathcal{A}
on a vector bundle
E
. We denote a
\mathsf{LieRep}
pair by
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathcal{A}},a_{\mathcal{A}};\rho)
, or simply by
(\mathcal{A};\rho)
.

Definition 2.3

(
[
28
]
)
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathcal{A}},a_{\mathcal{A}};\rho)
be a
\mathsf{LieRep}
pair. A bundle map

T\colon E\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}
is called a
relative Rota–Baxter operator
on a
\mathsf{LieRep}
pair
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathcal{A}},a_{\mathcal{A}};\rho)
if

\displaystyle[T(u),T(v)]_{\mathcal{A}}=T(\rho(T(u))(v)-\rho(T(v))(u)),\qquad\forall u,v\in\Gamma{(E)}.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g}=\oplus_{k\in Z}\mathfrak{g}_{k},[\cdot,\cdot],d)
be a differential graded Lie algebra. An element
\theta\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}
is called a
Maurer–Cartan element
of
\mathfrak{g}
if it satisfies

\displaystyle{\rm d}\theta+\frac{1}{2}[\theta,\theta]=0.

In particular, a Maurer–Cartan element of a graded Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g}=\oplus_{k\in Z}\mathfrak{g}_{k},[\cdot,\cdot])
is an element
\theta\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}
satisfying
[\theta,\theta]=0
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathcal{A}},a_{\mathcal{A}};\rho)
be a
\mathsf{LieRep}
pair. Consider the graded vector space

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{*}(E,\mathcal{A})=\oplus_{k\geq 0}\mathcal{C}^{k}(E,\mathcal{A}),\qquad\text{where}\quad\mathcal{C}^{k}(E,\mathcal{A}):=\Gamma\big(\mathrm{Hom}\big({\wedge}^{k}E,\mathcal{A}\big)\big).

Now we give the controlling algebra of relative Rota–Baxter operators on Lie algebroids, which is the main tool in the following study.

Theorem 2.5
.

For
P\in\mathcal{C}^{m}(E,\mathcal{A})
and
Q\in\mathcal{C}^{n}(E,\mathcal{A})
, we define a bracket operation

\displaystyle\left[\!\left[P,Q\right]\!\right](u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{m+n})

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(m,1,n-1)}}(-1)^{\sigma}P(\rho(Q(u_{\sigma(1)},\dots,u_{\sigma(m)}))u_{\sigma(m+1)},u_{\sigma(m+2)},\dots,u_{\sigma(m+n)})

(2.1)

\displaystyle\qquad\hphantom{=}{}-(-1)^{mn}\!\!\!\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(n,1,m-1)}}\!\!\!(-1)^{\sigma}Q(\rho(P(u_{\sigma(1)},\dots,u_{\sigma(n)}))u_{\sigma(n+1)},u_{\sigma(n+2)},\dots,u_{\sigma(m+n)})

\displaystyle\qquad\hphantom{=}{}+(-1)^{mn}\!\!\!\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(n,m)}}\!\!\!(-1)^{\sigma}[P(u_{\sigma(1)},u_{\sigma(2)},\dots,u_{\sigma(n)}),Q(u_{\sigma(n+1)},u_{\sigma(n+2)},\dots,u_{\sigma(m+n)})]_{\mathcal{A}},

where
u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{m+n}\in\Gamma(E)
.
Then
(\mathcal{C}^{*}(E,\mathcal{A}),\left[\!\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\!\right])
is a graded Lie algebra and its Maurer–Cartan elements are precisely relative Rota–Baxter operators on
(\mathcal{A};\rho)
.

Proof.

It is straightforward to check that
\left[\!\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\!\right]
is skew-symmetric in all arguments and function linear. Thus
\left[\!\left[P,Q\right]\!\right]\in\mathcal{C}^{m+n}(E,\mathcal{A})
for all
P\in\mathcal{C}^{m}(E,\mathcal{A})
and
Q\in\mathcal{C}^{n}(E,\mathcal{A})
, which implies that

\left[\!\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\!\right]
is well defined.

It was shown in
[
44
]
that the bracket
\left[\!\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\!\right]
provides a graded Lie algebra structure on the graded vector space
\oplus_{k\geq 0}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}\big({\wedge}^{k}\Gamma(E),\Gamma(\mathcal{A})\big)
. Thus
(\mathcal{C}^{*}(E,\mathcal{A}),\left[\!\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\!\right])
is a graded Lie algebra.

Let
T\colon E\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
be a bundle map. By a direct calculation, we have

\displaystyle\left[\!\left[T,T\right]\!\right](u_{1},u_{2})=2(T(\rho(Tu_{1})u_{2})-T(\rho(Tu_{2})u_{1})-[Tu_{1},Tu_{2}]_{\mathcal{A}}),\qquad\forall u_{1},u_{2}\in\Gamma(E).

Thus
T
is a Maurer–Cartan element of the graded Lie algebra
(\mathcal{C}^{*}(E,\mathcal{A}),\left[\!\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\!\right])
if and only if
T
is a relative Rota–Baxter operator on the
\mathsf{LieRep}
pair
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathcal{A}},a_{\mathcal{A}};\rho)
.
∎

Let
T\colon E\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}
be a relative Rota–Baxter operator on the
\mathsf{LieRep}
pair
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathcal{A}},a_{\mathcal{A}};\rho)
. By Theorem
2.5
,
T
is a Maurer–Cartan element of the graded Lie algebra
(\mathcal{C}^{*}(E,\mathcal{A}),\left[\!\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\!\right])
. Note that
\tilde{\mathrm{d}}_{T}:=\left[\!\left[T,\cdot\right]\!\right]
is a graded derivation on the graded Lie algebra
(\mathcal{C}^{*}(E,\mathcal{A}),\left[\!\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\!\right])
satisfying
\tilde{\mathrm{d}}_{T}^{2}=0
. Therefore,
(\mathcal{C}^{*}(E,\mathcal{A}),\left[\!\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\!\right],\tilde{\mathrm{d}}_{T})
is a differential graded Lie algebra.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathcal{A}},a_{\mathcal{A}};\rho)
be a
\mathsf{LieRep}
pair and
T\colon E\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}
a relative Rota–Baxter operator. Then for a bundle map
T^{\prime}\colon E\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}
,
T+T^{\prime}
is still a relative Rota–Baxter operator on the
\mathsf{LieRep}
pair
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathcal{A}},a_{\mathcal{A}};\rho)
if and only if
T^{\prime}
is a Maurer–Cartan element of the differential graded Lie algebra
\big(\mathcal{C}^{*}(E,\mathcal{A}),\left[\!\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\!\right],\tilde{\mathrm{d}}_{T}\big)
.

Proof.

Assume that
T+T^{\prime}
is a relative Rota–Baxter operator on the
\mathsf{LieRep}
pair
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathcal{A}},a_{\mathcal{A}};\rho)
. By the fact that
T
is a relative Rota–Baxter operator, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathrm{d}}_{T}T^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\left[\hskip-3.0pt\left[T^{\prime},T^{\prime}\right]\hskip-3.0pt\right]=\left[\hskip-3.0pt\left[T,T^{\prime}\right]\hskip-3.0pt\right]+\frac{1}{2}\left[\hskip-3.0pt\left[T^{\prime},T^{\prime}\right]\hskip-3.0pt\right]=\frac{1}{2}\left[\hskip-3.0pt\left[T+T^{\prime},T+T^{\prime}\right]\hskip-3.0pt\right]=0.

Thus
T^{\prime}
is a Maurer–Cartan element of the differential graded Lie algebra
(\mathcal{C}^{*}(E,\mathcal{A}),\left[\!\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\!\right],\tilde{\mathrm{d}}_{T})
.

The converse can be proved similarly. We omit the details.
∎

2.2
Cohomologies of relative Rota–Baxter operators on Lie algebroids

In this subsection, we give a cohomology theory of relative Rota–Baxter operators on Lie algebroids, which will be used to study formal deformations of relative Rota–Baxter operators.

Let
T\colon E\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}
be a relative Rota–Baxter operator on a
\mathsf{LieRep}
pair
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathcal{A}},a_{\mathcal{A}};\rho)
. Define
\mathrm{d}_{T}\colon\mathcal{C}^{k}(E,\mathcal{A})\rightarrow\mathcal{C}^{k+1}(E,\mathcal{A})
by

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{T}P=(-1)^{k}\tilde{\mathrm{d}}_{T}P=(-1)^{k}\left[\!\left[T,P\right]\!\right],\qquad\forall P\in\mathcal{C}^{k}(E,\mathcal{A}).

Since
\tilde{\mathrm{d}}_{T}\circ\tilde{\mathrm{d}}_{T}=0
, we have
\mathrm{d}_{T}\circ\mathrm{d}_{T}=0
. Thus
(\mathcal{C}^{*}(E,\mathcal{A})=\oplus_{k\geq 0}\mathcal{C}^{k}(E,\mathcal{A}),\mathrm{d}_{T})
is a cochain complex. Note the sign in the differential
\mathrm{d}_{T}
is motivated by Theorem
2.12
below.

Definition 2.7
.

The cochain complex
(\mathcal{C}^{*}(E,\mathcal{A})=\oplus_{k\geq 0}\mathcal{C}^{k}(E,\mathcal{A}),\mathrm{d}_{T})
is called
the
cohomology complex
of the relative Rota–Baxter operator
T
on the
\mathsf{LieRep}
pair
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathcal{A}},a_{\mathcal{A}};\rho)
. The corresponding
k
-th cohomology group, denoted by
\mathcal{H}_{T}^{k}(E,\mathcal{A})
, is called the
k
-th cohomology group
for the relative Rota–Baxter operator
T
.

We give the coboundary operator
\mathrm{d}_{T}
explicitly.

Proposition 2.8
.

For
P\in\mathcal{C}^{k}(E,\mathcal{A})
and
u_{1},\dots,u_{k+1}\in\Gamma{(E)}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{T}P(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{k+1})

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{i+1}[Tu_{i},P(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,\hat{u_{i}},\dots,u_{k+1})]_{\mathcal{A}}

\displaystyle\qquad\hphantom{=}{}+\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{i+1}T\rho(P(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,\hat{u_{i}},\dots,u_{k+1}))(u_{i})

\displaystyle\qquad\hphantom{=}{}+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq{k+1}}(-1)^{i+j}P(\rho(Tu_{i})(u_{j})-\rho(Tu_{j})(u_{i}),u_{1},\dots,\hat{u_{i}},\dots,\hat{u_{j}},\dots,u_{k+1}).

(2.2)

Proof.

It follows from a direct calculation.
∎

It is obvious that
P\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(E,\mathcal{A})
is closed if and only if

\displaystyle[Tu,P(v)]_{\mathcal{A}}-[Tv,P(u)]_{\mathcal{A}}-T(\rho(P(u))(v)-\rho(P(v))(u))-P(\rho(Tu)(v)-\rho(Tv)(u))=0,

where
u,v\in\Gamma(E)
.

In the sequel, we give an alternative characterization of
\mathrm{d}_{T}
using the cohomology of Lie algebroids. First we recall a useful fact.

Lemma 2.9

(
[
28
]
)
.

Let
T\colon E\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}
be a relative Rota–Baxter operator on a
\mathsf{LieRep}
pair
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathcal{A}},a_{\mathcal{A}};\rho)
. Then
(E,[\cdot,\cdot]_{T},a_{T}=a_{\mathcal{A}}\circ T)
is a Lie algebroid, where the bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{T}
is given by

\displaystyle[u,v]_{T}=\rho(T(u))v-\rho(T(v))u,\qquad\forall u,v\in\Gamma(E).

Furthermore,

Moreover, the Lie algebroid

\mathcal{A}

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
T\colon E\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}
be a relative Rota–Baxter operator on a
\mathsf{LieRep}
pair
(\mathcal{A};\rho)
. Define
\varrho\colon E\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{D}(\mathcal{A})
by

\displaystyle\varrho(u)(x):=[Tu,x]_{\mathcal{A}}+T\rho(x)(u),\qquad x\in\Gamma{(\mathcal{A})},\quad u\in\Gamma{(E)}.

Then
\varrho
is a representation of the Lie algebroid
(E,[\cdot,\cdot]_{T},a_{T}=a_{\mathcal{A}}\circ T)
on the vector bundle
\mathcal{A}
.

Proof.

By a direct calculation, we have

\displaystyle\varrho(fu)(x)

\displaystyle=[T(fu),x]_{\mathcal{A}}+T\rho(x)(fu)

\displaystyle=f[Tu,x]_{\mathcal{A}}-a_{\mathcal{A}}(x)(f)(Tu)+fT\rho(x)(u)+Ta_{\mathcal{A}}(x)(f)u

\displaystyle=f\varrho(u)(x)

and

\displaystyle\varrho(u)(fx)

\displaystyle=[Tu,fx]_{\mathcal{A}}+T\rho(fx)(u)

\displaystyle=f[Tu,x]_{\mathcal{A}}+a_{\mathcal{A}}(Tu)(f)(x)+fT\rho(x)(u)

\displaystyle=f\varrho(u)(x)+a_{T}(u)(f)(x).

It is straightforward to check that
\varrho[u,v]_{T}=\varrho(u)\varrho(v)-\varrho(v)\varrho(u)
. Thus
\varrho
is a representation of the Lie algebroid
(E,[\cdot,\cdot]_{T},a_{T})
on
\mathcal{A}
.
∎

Remark 2.11
.

Let
T\colon E\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}
be a relative Rota–Baxter operator on a
\mathsf{LieRep}
pair
(\mathcal{A};\rho)
. It is straightforward to check that
(\mathcal{A},E,\rho,\varrho)
is a matched pair of Lie algebroids, where the Lie algebroid structure on
E
is the Lie algebroid
(E,[\cdot,\cdot]_{T},a_{T})
. See
[
32
]
for more details on matched pairs of Lie algebroids.

Theorem 2.12
.

Let
T\colon E\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}
be a relative Rota–Baxter operator on a
\mathsf{LieRep}
pair
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathcal{A}},a_{\mathcal{A}};\rho)
. Then the coboundary operator of the relative Rota–Baxter operator
T
is exactly the coboundary operator of the Lie algebroid
(E,[\cdot,\cdot]_{T},a_{T})
with coefficients in the representation
(\mathcal{A};\varrho)
, that is,

\mathrm{d}_{T}=\partial_{\varrho}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.8
, for any
P\in\mathcal{C}^{k}(E,\mathcal{A})
and
u_{1},\dots,u_{k+1}\in\Gamma{(E)}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{T}P(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{k+1})

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{i+1}[Tu_{i},P(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,\hat{u_{i}},\dots,u_{k+1})]_{\mathcal{A}}

\displaystyle\qquad\hphantom{=}{}+\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{i+1}T\rho(P(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,\hat{u_{i}},\dots,u_{k+1}))(u_{i})

\displaystyle\qquad\hphantom{=}{}+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq{k+1}}(-1)^{i+j}P(\rho(Tu_{i})(u_{j})-\rho(Tu_{j})(u_{i}),u_{1},\dots,\hat{u_{i}},\dots,\hat{u_{j}},\dots,u_{k+1}),

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{i+1}\varrho(u_{i})P(u_{1},\dots,\hat{u_{i}},\dots,u_{k+1})

\displaystyle\qquad\hphantom{=}{}+\sum_{i<j}(-1)^{i+j}P([u_{i},u_{j}]_{T},u_{1},\dots,\hat{u_{i}},\dots,\hat{u_{j}},\dots,u_{k+1})

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\partial_{\varrho}P(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{k+1}).

The conclusion follows.
∎

3
Cohomologies of Rota–Baxter operators on Lie algebroids

In this section, first we give the cohomologies of Rota–Baxter operators on Lie algebroids with the help of the general framework of the cohomologies of relative Rota–Baxter operators. Then we study the cohomologies of the Rota–Baxter operator arising from an action of a Rota–Baxter Lie algebra on a manifold.

Now we recall the notion of a Rota–Baxter operator on a Lie algebroid given in
[
19
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

A
Rota–Baxter operator
on a regular Lie algebroid
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathcal{A}},a_{\mathcal{A}})
is a bundle map
R\colon\mathrm{ker}(a_{\mathcal{A}})\to\mathcal{A}
such that

\displaystyle[R(x),R(y)]_{\mathcal{A}}=R([R(x),y]_{\mathcal{A}}+[x,R(y)]_{\mathcal{A}}),\qquad\forall x,y\in\Gamma(\mathrm{ker}(a_{\mathcal{A}})).

For any
x\in\Gamma(\mathcal{A})
, we define
\mathcal{L}_{x}\colon\Gamma(\mathcal{A})\longrightarrow\Gamma(\mathcal{A})
by
\mathcal{L}_{x}(y)=[x,y]_{\mathcal{A}}
for
y\in\Gamma(A)
. Then
\mathcal{L}
gives a representation of the Lie algebroid
\mathcal{A}
on
\mathrm{ker}(a_{\mathcal{A}})
. Thus a Rota–Baxter operator on a regular Lie algebroid
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathcal{A}},a_{\mathcal{A}})
is a relative Rota–Baxter operator on the
\mathsf{LieRep}
pair
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathcal{A}},a_{\mathcal{A}};\mathcal{L})
.

A
Rota–Baxter operator
on a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
is a linear map
\mathcal{B}\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
such that

\displaystyle[\mathcal{B}(u),\mathcal{B}(v)]_{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathcal{B}([\mathcal{B}(u),v]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[u,\mathcal{B}(v)]_{\mathfrak{g}}),\qquad\forall u,v\in\mathfrak{g}.

The pair
(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{B})
is called a
Rota–Baxter Lie algebra
.

Remark 3.2
.

Since a vector space is a vector bundle over a point, a Lie algebra is naturally
a Lie algebroid with the anchor being zero. It is not hard to see that a Rota–Baxter operator on a Lie algebroid reduces
to a Rota–Baxter operator on a Lie algebra when the underlying Lie algebroid reduces to a Lie algebra.

By Theorem
2.5
, we have

is given by (

(ii)

.

By Lemmas
2.9
and
2.10
, we have

Corollary 3.4
.

Let

on the vector bundle
\mathcal{A}
.

As a special case of Definition
2.7
, we have

R
be a Rota–Baxter operator on a regular Lie algebroid

At the end of this section, we analyze the cohomology of the Rota–Baxter operator arising from an action of a Rota–Baxter Lie algebra on a manifold.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a Lie algebra. An

of
\mathfrak{g}
on a manifold
M
is a homomorphism
of Lie algebras
\phi\colon(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})\to(\mathfrak{X}(M),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{X}(M)})

\mathcal{B}
on a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
, the bracket

\displaystyle[u,v]_{\mathcal{B}}=[\mathcal{B}(u),v]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[u,\mathcal{B}(v)]_{\mathfrak{g}},\qquad\forall u,v\in\mathfrak{g}

defines another Lie algebra structure on
\mathfrak{g}
. Recall from
[
19
]
that an action of a Rota–Baxter Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{B})

on a manifold
M
is a homomorphism
of Lie algebras
\phi\colon(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathcal{B}})\to(\mathfrak{X}(M),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{X}(M)})
.
Let
\phi\colon(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathcal{B}})\to(\mathfrak{X}(M),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{X}(M)})
be an action of the Rota–Baxter Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{B})
on
M
. Consider the direct sum bundle
\mathcal{A}:=(M\times\mathfrak{g})\oplus TM
. Then
\Gamma(\mathcal{A})=(C^{\infty}(M)\otimes\mathfrak{g})\oplus\mathfrak{X}(M)
. There is naturally a Lie algebroid structure on
\mathcal{A}
whose anchor

is the projection to
TM

and whose bracket is determined by

\displaystyle[fu+X,gv+Y]_{\mathcal{A}}:=fg[u,v]_{\mathfrak{g}}+X(g)v-Y(f)u+[X,Y]_{{\mathfrak{X}(M)}},

R\colon\mathrm{ker}(a_{\mathcal{A}})=M\times\mathfrak{g}\to(M\times\mathfrak{g})\oplus TM
defined by

\displaystyle R(m,u):=(m,\mathcal{B}(u),\phi(u)(m)),\qquad\forall m\in M,\quad u\in\mathfrak{g}.

(3.1)

It was proved in

19
]

that the bundle map

In the following, we establish the relations among the cohomology of the Rota–Baxter operator
R
on the Lie algebroid
((M\times\mathfrak{g})\oplus TM,[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathcal{A}},a_{\mathcal{A}})

\mathcal{B}
on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and the cohomology of the Lie algebra homomorphism

The cohomology of a Rota–Baxter operator
\mathcal{B}
on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is the cohomology of the cochain complex
\big({\oplus}_{k=0}^{+\infty}\mathcal{C}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{B}}\big)

\mathcal{C}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})=\mathrm{Hom}\big({\wedge}^{k}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}\big)
and the coboundary operator
\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{B}}\colon\mathrm{Hom}\big({\wedge}^{k}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}\big)\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Hom}\big({\wedge}^{k+1}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}\big)
is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{B}}f(u_{1},\dots,u_{k+1})

\displaystyle\qquad{}:=\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{i+1}[\mathcal{B}(u_{i}),f(u_{1},\dots,\hat{u_{i}},\dots,u_{k+1})]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle\qquad\hphantom{=}{}+\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{i+1}\mathcal{B}[f(u_{1},\dots,\hat{u_{i}},\dots,u_{k+1}),u_{i}]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle\qquad\hphantom{=}{}+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k+1}(-1)^{i+j}f([\mathcal{B}(u_{i}),u_{j}]_{\mathfrak{g}}-[\mathcal{B}(u_{j}),u_{i}]_{\mathfrak{g}},u_{1},\dots,\hat{u_{i}},\dots,\hat{u_{j}},\dots,u_{k+1}).

See
[
44
]
for more details about the cohomology of Rota–Baxter operators on Lie algebras.

Let
\phi\colon(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathcal{B}})\to(\mathfrak{X}(M),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{X}(M)})
be an action of the Rota–Baxter Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{B})
on a manifold

. The cohomology of the Lie algebra homomorphism

and the coboundary operator

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{\phi}P(u_{1},\dots,u_{k+1})={}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{i+1}[\phi(u_{i}),P(u_{1},\dots,\hat{u_{i}},\dots,u_{k+1})]_{\mathfrak{X}(M)}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i<j}(-1)^{i+j}P([u_{i},u_{j}]_{\mathcal{B}},u_{1},\dots,\hat{u_{i}},\dots,\hat{u_{j}},\dots,u_{k+1}),

for

and
u_{1},\dots,u_{k+1}\in\mathfrak{g}

k
-th cohomology group, which we denote by

k
-th cohomology group
for the action of the Rota–Baxter Lie algebra

on

. See
[
12

37
]
for more details on cohomology and deformations of Lie algebra homomorphisms.

The cochain complex associated to the Rota–Baxter operator
R
on the Lie algebroid
((M\times\mathfrak{g})\allowbreak\oplus TM,[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathcal{A}},a_{\mathcal{A}})
defined by

is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{k}(M\times\mathfrak{g},(M\times\mathfrak{g})\oplus TM)=\Gamma\big(\mathrm{Hom}\big({\wedge}^{k}(M\times\mathfrak{g}),(M\times\mathfrak{g})\oplus TM\big)\big),\qquad k\geq 0.

For any

, define a linear map
\Xi\colon\mathcal{C}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})\oplus C_{\phi}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{X}(M))\to\mathcal{C}^{k}(M\times\mathfrak{g},(M\times\mathfrak{g})\oplus TM)
by

\displaystyle\Xi(P_{1},P_{2})(m,(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})):=(m,P_{1}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k}),P_{2}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})(m)),

(3.2)

for all

and
u_{1},\dots,u_{k}\in\mathfrak{g}.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\phi\colon(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathcal{B}})\to(\mathfrak{X}(M),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{X}(M)})
be an action of the Rota–Baxter Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{B})
on a manifold
M
. Then
\Xi
defined by (
3.2
), is a homomorphism of cochain complexes from
\big({\oplus}_{k\geq 0}\big(\mathcal{C}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})\oplus C_{\phi}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{X}(M))\big),(\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{B}},\mathrm{d}_{\phi})\big)
to
\big({\oplus}_{k\geq 0}\mathcal{C}^{k}(M\times\mathfrak{g},(M\times\mathfrak{g})\oplus TM),\mathrm{d}_{R}\big)
, that is,
\Xi\circ(\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{B}},\mathrm{d}_{\phi})=\mathrm{d}_{R}\circ\Xi
. Consequently,
\Xi
induces a homomorphism

\displaystyle\Xi_{\ast}\colon\quad\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{B}}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})\oplus H_{\phi}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{X}(M))\to\mathcal{H}_{R}^{k}(M\times\mathfrak{g},(M\times\mathfrak{g})\oplus TM),\qquad k\geq 0,

between the corresponding cohomology groups.

Proof.

For any
P_{1}\in\mathcal{C}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
,
P_{2}\in C_{\phi}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{X}(M))
and
u_{1},\dots,u_{k+1}\in\mathfrak{g}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{R}\Xi(P_{1},P_{2})(1\otimes u_{1},\dots,1\otimes u_{k+1})

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{i+1}[R(1\otimes u_{i}),(P_{1}(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,\hat{u_{i}},\dots,u_{k+1}),P_{2}(u_{1},\dots,\hat{u_{i}},\dots,u_{k+1}))]_{\mathcal{A}}

\displaystyle\qquad\hphantom{=}{}+\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{i+1}R[(P_{1}(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,\hat{u_{i}},\dots,u_{k+1}),P_{2}(u_{1},\dots,\hat{u_{i}},\dots,u_{k+1})),u_{i}]_{\mathcal{A}}

\displaystyle\qquad\hphantom{=}{}+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq{k+1}}(-1)^{i+j}(P_{1}([u_{i},u_{j}]_{\mathcal{B}},u_{1},\dots,\hat{u_{i}},\dots,\hat{u_{j}},\dots,u_{k+1}),

\displaystyle P_{2}([u_{i},u_{j}]_{\mathcal{B}},u_{1},\dots,\hat{u_{i}},\dots,\hat{u_{j}},\dots,u_{k+1}))

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\Bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{i+1}[\mathcal{B}(u_{i}),P_{1}(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,\hat{u_{i}},\dots,u_{k+1})]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle\qquad\hphantom{=}{}+\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{i+1}\mathcal{B}[P_{1}(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,\hat{u_{i}},\dots,u_{k+1}),u_{i}]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle\qquad\hphantom{=}{}+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq{k+1}}(-1)^{i+j}P_{1}([u_{i},u_{j}]_{\mathcal{B}},u_{1},\dots,\hat{u_{i}},\dots,\hat{u_{j}},\dots,u_{k+1})

\displaystyle\qquad\hphantom{=}{}+\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{i+1}[\phi(u_{i}),P_{2}(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,\hat{u_{i}},\dots,u_{k+1})]_{\mathfrak{X}(M)}

\displaystyle\qquad\hphantom{=}{}+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq{k+1}}(-1)^{i+j}P_{2}([u_{i},u_{j}]_{\mathcal{B}},u_{1},\dots,\hat{u_{i}},\dots,\hat{u_{j}},\dots,u_{k+1})\Bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=(\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{B}}P_{1}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k+1}),\mathrm{d}_{\phi}P_{2}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k+1}))

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\Xi(\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{B}}P_{1},\mathrm{d}_{\phi}P_{2})(1\otimes u_{1},\dots,1\otimes u_{k+1}),

which implies that

.
∎

4
Formal deformations of relative Rota–Baxter operators

In this section, we use the cohomology of relative Rota–Baxter operators on Lie algebroids to study infinitesimal deformations and extendibility of order

Definition 4.1
.

A
formal deformation
of a relative Rota–Baxter operator

on a
\mathsf{LieRep}
pair
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathcal{A}},a_{\mathcal{A}};\rho)

\displaystyle T_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{+\infty}\mathcal{T}_{i}t^{i}\in\mathrm{Hom}(E,A)[[t]]

\mathsf{LieRep}

.

is a relative Rota–Baxter operator on the
\mathsf{LieRep}
pair
(\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathbb{R}[[t]],[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathcal{A}},a_{\mathcal{A}};\rho)
if and only if

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=k}\!\!\big([\mathcal{T}_{i}(u),\mathcal{T}_{j}(v)]_{\mathcal{A}}-\mathcal{T}_{j}(\rho(\mathcal{T}_{i}(u))(v)-\rho(\mathcal{T}_{i}(v))(u))\big)=0,\qquad\forall k\geq 0,\quad u,v\in\Gamma(E).\!\!

(4.1)

.

Let
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathcal{A}},a_{\mathcal{A}};\rho)
be a
\mathsf{LieRep}

T\colon E\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}

,

is an

n
deformation
of the relative Rota–Baxter operator

. Furthermore, if there exists an element
\mathcal{T}_{n+1}\in\mathrm{Hom}(E,\mathcal{A})
such that

is an order
n+1
deformation of the relative Rota–Baxter operator

, we say that
T_{(n)}
is
extendable
.

An order
1
deformation of a relative Rota–Baxter operator
T
on a
\mathsf{LieRep}
pair
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathcal{A}},a_{\mathcal{A}};\rho)

infinitesimal deformation

.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
T\colon E\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}
be a relative Rota–Baxter operator on
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathcal{A}},a_{\mathcal{A}};\rho)
. Two order
n
deformations

and

of

\displaystyle{\rm exp}(\mathrm{ad}_{\mathcal{X}_{t}})T_{t}=T_{t}^{\prime}\mbox{ modulo }t^{n+1},

(4.2)

where

\displaystyle\mathrm{ad}^{k}_{\mathcal{X}_{t}}T_{t}=\big[\hskip-3.0pt\big[\mathcal{X}_{t},\big[\hskip-3.0pt\big[\mathcal{X}_{t},\dots,[\hskip-1.5pt[\mathcal{X}_{t},\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{T}}_{t}]\hskip-1.5pt],\stackrel{{\scriptstyle k}}{{\dots}}\big]\hskip-3.0pt\big]\big]\hskip-3.0pt\big].

An order

5.1
The Matsushima–Nijenhuis bracket for left-symmetric algebroids

Recall that a
left-symmetric algebra
is a pair
(\mathfrak{g},\ast_{\mathfrak{g}})
, where
\mathfrak{g}
is a vector space, and
\ast_{\mathfrak{g}}\colon\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is a bilinear multiplication
satisfying that for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
, the associator

\displaystyle(x,y,z):=x\ast_{\mathfrak{g}}(y\ast_{\mathfrak{g}}z)-(x\ast_{\mathfrak{g}}y)\ast_{\mathfrak{g}}z

is symmetric in
x
,
y
,
i.e.,

\displaystyle(x,y,z)=(y,x,z),

or equivalently,

\displaystyle x\ast_{\mathfrak{g}}(y\ast_{\mathfrak{g}}z)-(x\ast_{\mathfrak{g}}y)\ast_{\mathfrak{g}}z=y\ast_{\mathfrak{g}}(x\ast_{\mathfrak{g}}z)-(y\ast_{\mathfrak{g}}x)\ast_{\mathfrak{g}}z.

Definition 5.1

(
[
28
,
33
]
)
.

A
left-symmetric algebroid
structure on a vector bundle

\mathcal{A}\longrightarrow M
is a pair that consists of a left-symmetric
algebra structure
\ast_{\mathcal{A}}
on the section space
\Gamma(\mathcal{A})
and a vector bundle morphism
a_{\mathcal{A}}\colon\mathcal{A}\longrightarrow TM
, called the anchor,
such that for all
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
and
x,y\in\Gamma(\mathcal{A})
, the following
conditions are satisfied:

(i)

~x\ast_{\mathcal{A}}(fy)=f(x\ast_{\mathcal{A}}y)+a_{\mathcal{A}}(x)(f)y,

(ii)

(fx)\ast_{\mathcal{A}}y=f(x\ast_{\mathcal{A}}y)
.

We usually denote a left-symmetric algebroid by
(\mathcal{A},\ast_{\mathcal{A}},a_{\mathcal{A}})
.

Any left-symmetric algebra is a left-symmetric algebroid over a point.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\ast_{\mathcal{A}},a_{\mathcal{A}})
be a left-symmetric algebroid. For any

, we define

L_{x}\colon\Gamma(\mathcal{A})\longrightarrow\Gamma(\mathcal{A})
and

\displaystyle L_{x}y=x\ast_{\mathcal{A}}y,\qquad R_{x}y=y\ast_{\mathcal{A}}x,\qquad\forall y\in\Gamma(\mathcal{A}).

Condition
(i)
in the above definition means that
L_{x}\in\mathfrak{D}(\mathcal{A})
.
Condition
(ii)
means that the map

is

C^{\infty}(M)
-linear. Thus,

is a bundle
map. With the same notations, there are two maps

given by

\displaystyle\langle L_{x}\xi,y\rangle=a_{\mathcal{A}}(x)\langle\xi,y\rangle-\langle\xi,L_{x}y\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle R_{x}\xi,y\rangle=-\langle\xi,R_{x}y\rangle,\qquad\forall x,y\in\Gamma(\mathcal{A}),\quad\xi\in\Gamma(\mathcal{A}^{*}).

(5.1)

Theorem 5.6
.

For
D_{1}\in\mathrm{Der}^{m}(E)
and
D_{2}\in\mathrm{Der}^{n}(E)
, we define the Matsushima–Nijenhuis bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathrm{MN}}\colon\mathrm{Der}^{m}(E)\times\mathrm{Der}^{n}(E)\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Der}^{m+n}(E)
by

\displaystyle[D_{1},D_{2}]_{\mathrm{MN}}=D_{1}\circ D_{2}-(-1)^{mn}D_{2}\circ D_{1},

where

\displaystyle(D_{1}\circ D_{2})(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{m+n+1})

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(m,1,n-1)}}(-1)^{\sigma}D_{1}(D_{2}(u_{\sigma(1)},\dots,u_{\sigma(m+1)}),u_{\sigma(m+2)},\dots,u_{\sigma(m+n)},u_{m+n+1})

\displaystyle\qquad\hphantom{=}{}+(-1)^{mn}\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(n,m)}}\!\!\!\!\!(-1)^{\sigma}D_{1}(u_{\sigma(1)},\dots,u_{\sigma(n)},D_{2}(u_{\sigma(n+1)},u_{\sigma(n+2)},\dots,u_{\sigma(m+n)},u_{m+n+1})).

Then
(\mathrm{Der}^{*}(E),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathrm{MN}})
is a graded Lie algebra.

Furthermore,
\pi\in\mathrm{Der}^{1}(E)
defines a left-symmetric algebroid structure on
E
if and only if
[\pi,\pi]_{\mathrm{MN}}=0
, that is,
\pi
is a Maurer–Cartan element of the graded Lie algebra
(\mathrm{Der}^{*}(E),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathrm{MN}})
.

Proof.

First, we show that the space of multiderivations is closed under the Matsushima–Nijenhuis bracket. For
D_{1}\in\mathrm{Der}^{m}(E)
and
D_{2}\in\mathrm{Der}^{n}(E)
, by a direct calculation, we have

\displaystyle[D_{1},D_{2}]_{\mathrm{MN}}(fu_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{m+n+1})

\displaystyle\qquad{}=fD_{1}\circ D_{2}(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{m+n+1})-(-1)^{mn}fD_{2}\circ D_{1}(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{m+n+1})

\displaystyle\qquad\hphantom{=}{}+\!\!\!\!\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(m-1,1,n-1)}}\!\!\!\!(-1)^{\sigma}\sigma_{D_{2}}(u_{\sigma(2)},\dots,u_{\sigma(m+1)})(f)D_{1}(u_{1},u_{\sigma(m+2)},\dots,u_{\sigma(m+n)},u_{m+n+1})

\displaystyle\qquad\hphantom{=}{}+(-1)^{mn}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(n-1,1,m-1)}}(-1)^{\sigma}\sigma_{D_{1}}(u_{\sigma(2)},\dots,u_{\sigma(n+1)})(f)

\displaystyle\qquad\hphantom{=+(-1)^{mn}}{}\times D_{2}(u_{1},u_{\sigma(n+2)},\dots,u_{\sigma(m+n)},u_{m+n+1})

\displaystyle\qquad\hphantom{=}{}-(-1)^{mn}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(n-1,1,m-1)}}(-1)^{\sigma}\sigma_{D_{1}}(u_{\sigma(2)},\dots,u_{\sigma(n+1)})(f)

\displaystyle\qquad\hphantom{=+(-1)^{mn}}{}\times D_{2}(u_{1},u_{\sigma(n+2)},\dots,u_{\sigma(m+n)},u_{m+n+1})

\displaystyle\qquad\hphantom{=}{}-\!\!\!\!\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(m-1,1,n-1)}}\!\!\!\!(-1)^{\sigma}\sigma_{D_{2}}(u_{\sigma(2)},\dots,u_{\sigma(m+1)})(f)D_{1}(u_{1},u_{\sigma(m+2)},\dots,u_{\sigma(m+n)},u_{m+n+1})

\displaystyle\qquad{}=f[D_{1},D_{2}]_{\mathrm{MN}}(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{m+n+1}),

which implies that

\displaystyle[D_{1},D_{2}]_{\mathrm{MN}}(fu_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{m+n+1})=f[D_{1},D_{2}]_{\mathrm{MN}}(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{m+n+1}).

It is straightforward to check that
[D_{1},D_{2}]_{\mathrm{MN}}
is skew-symmetric with respect to its first
m+n
arguments. Thus
[D_{1},D_{2}]_{\mathrm{MN}}
is
C^{\infty}(M)
-linear with respect to its first
m+n
arguments.

By a direct calculation, we have

\displaystyle[D_{1},D_{2}]_{\mathrm{MN}}(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,fu_{m+n+1})={}

\displaystyle f[D_{1},D_{2}]_{\mathrm{MN}}(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{m+n+1})

\displaystyle+\sigma_{[D_{1},D_{2}]_{\mathrm{MN}}}(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{m+n})(f)u_{m+n+1},

where the symbol
\sigma_{[D_{1},D_{2}]_{\mathrm{MN}}}
is given by

\displaystyle\sigma_{[D_{1},D_{2}]_{\mathrm{MN}}}(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{m+n})(f)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(m,1,n-1)}}(-1)^{\sigma}\sigma_{D_{1}}(D_{2}(u_{\sigma(1)},\dots,u_{\sigma(m+1)}),u_{\sigma(m+2)},\dots,u_{\sigma(m+n)}))(f)

\displaystyle\qquad\hphantom{=}{}+\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(n,1,m-1)}}(-1)^{\sigma}\sigma_{D_{2}}(D_{1}(u_{\sigma(1)},\dots,u_{\sigma(n+1)}),u_{\sigma(n+2)},\dots,u_{\sigma(m+n)})(f)

\displaystyle\qquad\hphantom{=}{}+(-1)^{mn}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(m,n)}}(-1)^{\sigma}\sigma_{D_{1}}(u_{\sigma(1)},\dots,u_{\sigma(n)})(\sigma_{D_{2}}(u_{\sigma(n+1)},\dots,u_{\sigma(n+m)}))(f)

\displaystyle\qquad\hphantom{=}{}+\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(m,n)}}(-1)^{\sigma}\sigma_{D_{2}}(u_{\sigma(1)},\dots,u_{\sigma(m)})(\sigma_{D_{1}}(u_{\sigma(m+1)},\dots,u_{\sigma(m+n)}))(f).

Thus
[D_{1},D_{2}]_{\mathrm{MN}}\in\mathrm{Der}^{m+n}(E)
.

It was shown in

that the Matsushima–Nijenhuis bracket provides a graded Lie algebra structure on the graded vector space

For

\displaystyle\pi(fu_{1},u_{2})=f\pi(u_{1},u_{2}),\qquad\pi(u_{1},fu_{2})=f\pi(u_{1},u_{2})+\sigma_{\pi}(u_{1})(f)u_{2},\qquad\forall u_{1},u_{2}\in\Gamma(E).

\displaystyle\qquad{}+\!(-1)^{mn}\!\!\sum_{\mathbb{S}_{(n,m)}}\!\!(-1)^{\sigma}\!\rho([P(u_{\sigma(1)},\dots,u_{\sigma(n)}),Q(u_{\sigma(n+1)},u_{\sigma(n+2)},\dots,u_{\sigma(m+n)})]_{\mathcal{A}})u_{m+n+1}

\displaystyle\qquad{}-\!(-1)^{mn}\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{\mathbb{S}_{(n,1,m-1)}}\!\!\!\!\!(-1)^{\sigma}\rho(Q(\rho(P(u_{\sigma(1)},\dots,u_{\sigma(n)}))u_{\sigma(n+1)},u_{\sigma(n+2)},\dots,u_{\sigma(m+n)}))u_{m+n+1}

\displaystyle\qquad{}+\!(-1)^{mn}\sum_{\mathbb{S}_{(n,m)}}\!(-1)^{\sigma}\rho(P(u_{\sigma(1)},\dots,u_{\sigma(n)}))\rho(Q(u_{\sigma(n+1)},u_{\sigma(n+2)},\dots,u_{\sigma(m+n)}))u_{m+n+1}

\displaystyle\qquad{}+\!(-1)^{mn}\!\!\sum_{\mathbb{S}_{(n,m)}}\!(-1)^{\sigma}\rho(Q(u_{\sigma(n+1)},u_{\sigma(n+2)},\dots,u_{\sigma(m+n)}))\rho(P(u_{\sigma(1)},\dots,u_{\sigma(n)}))u_{m+n+1}.

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle(\Phi(P)\circ\Phi(Q))(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{m+n+1})

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\!\!\sum_{\mathbb{S}_{(m,1,n-1)}}\!\!(-1)^{\sigma}\rho(P(\rho(Q(u_{\sigma(1)},\dots,u_{\sigma(m)}))u_{\sigma(m+1)},u_{\sigma(m+2)},\dots,u_{\sigma(m+n)}))u_{m+n+1}

\displaystyle\qquad{}+(-1)^{mn}\!\!\sum_{\mathbb{S}_{(n,m)}}\!\!\!(-1)^{\sigma}\rho(P(u_{\sigma(1)},\dots,u_{\sigma(n)}))\rho(Q(u_{\sigma(n+1)},u_{\sigma(n+2)},\dots,u_{\sigma(m+n)}))u_{m+n+1},

and

\displaystyle-(-1)^{mn}(\Phi(Q)\circ\Phi(P))(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{m+n+1})

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\sum_{\mathbb{S}_{(n,1,m-1)}}(-1)^{\sigma}\rho(Q(\rho(P(u_{\sigma(1)},\dots,u_{\sigma(n)}))u_{\sigma(n+1)},u_{\sigma(n+2)},\dots,u_{\sigma(m+n)}))u_{m+n+1}

\displaystyle\qquad{}-(-1)^{mn}\!\!\sum_{\mathbb{S}_{(n,m)}}\!\!(-1)^{\sigma}\rho(Q(u_{\sigma(n+1)},u_{\sigma(n+2)},\dots,u_{\sigma(m+n)}))\rho(P(u_{\sigma(1)},\dots,u_{\sigma(n)}))u_{m+n+1}.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\Phi(\left[\!\left[P,Q\right]\!\right])=\Phi(P)\circ\Phi(Q)-(-1)^{mn}\Phi(Q)\circ\Phi(P)=[\Phi(P),\Phi(Q)]_{\mathrm{MN}},

that is,
\Phi
is a homomorphism from
(\mathcal{C}^{*}(E,\mathcal{A}),\left[\!\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\!\right])
to
(\mathrm{Der}^{*}(E),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathrm{MN}})
.
∎

At the end of this section, we show that a formal deformation of a relative Rota–Baxter operator induces a formal deformation of the associated left-symmetric algebroid.

6
Maurer–Cartan characterizations and cohomology

In this section, we apply the controlling graded Lie algebra associated to relative Rota–Baxter operators to construct a graded Lie algebra whose Maurer–Cartan elements are precisely Koszul–Vinberg structures. Then we use this graded Lie algebra to study deformations of Koszul–Vinberg structures.

\displaystyle=a_{H^{\sharp}}(\alpha)\langle x,\beta\rangle\!-\!a_{\mathcal{A}}(H^{\sharp}(\alpha))\langle x,\beta\rangle\!+\![H^{\sharp}(\alpha),x]_{\mathcal{A}}\!-\!\langle\alpha,x\ast_{\mathcal{A}}H^{\sharp}(\beta)\rangle\!+\!a_{\mathcal{A}}(x)H(\alpha,\beta)

\displaystyle=\langle[H^{\sharp}(\alpha),x]_{\mathcal{A}}+H^{\sharp}(L_{x}\alpha),\beta\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\varrho(\alpha)(x),\beta\rangle.

Thus we have
\mathfrak{L}_{\alpha}x=\varrho(\alpha)(x)
.

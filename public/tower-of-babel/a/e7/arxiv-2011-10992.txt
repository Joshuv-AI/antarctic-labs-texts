Well-posedness for boundary value problems for coagulation-fragmentation equations
Iñigo U. Erneta

Abstract
. We investigate a coagulation-fragmentation equation with boundary data, establishing the well-posedness of the initial value problem when the coagulation kernels are bounded at zero and showing existence of solutions for the singular kernels relevant in the applications. We determine the large time asymptotic behavior of solutions, proving that solutions converge exponentially fast to zero in the absence of fragmentation and stabilize toward an equilibrium if the boundary value satisfies a detailed balance condition. Incidentally, we obtain an improvement in the regularity of solutions by showing the finiteness of negative moments for positive time.

Assuming that the coagulation and fragmentation processes are entirely characterized by the volume (

&\quad-\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}F_{j,i-j}c_{i}(t)+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}F_{i,j}c_{i+j}(t),\end{split}

The motivation for the boundary value problem above arises from the study of the dynamics of aerosols in the atmosphere.
In the description of these systems there appear multiple physical phenomena that affect particles in a different manner according to their size

The model described by a coagulation equation

It is then natural to assume that the concentration of these larger particles is given and acts as a boundary value term for the smaller particles.

In this work, we study a continuous version of (

The continuous coagulation fragmentation equation corresponds to the integro-differential equation

&\quad-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{x}F(x-y,y)f(t,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y+\int_{0}^{\infty}F(x,y)f(t,x+y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\end{split}

2.
Existence of solutions

We will prove Theorem (

2.1.
Well-posedness for bounded kernels

Definition 2.1
.

A nonnegative symmetric Borel function
K\colon(0,\infty)\times(0,\infty)\to\real

bounded coagulation kernel

(1)

\displaystyle\sup_{x,y\in(0,1]}K(x,y)<\infty

(2)

\sup_{x\in(0,1]}K(x,y)\leq K_{\beta}\,y^{\beta}

Remark 2.2

Despite our terminology, the

The second condition ensures that the growth of the kernel as the volumes become large is reasonably controlled.

For bounded kernels a fixed point argument shows that we have existence and uniqueness of solutions for the Cauchy problem.

C\big([0,\infty);\mathcal{M}^{+}_{b}(0,1]\big)
such that

\begin{split}\langle\mu_{t},\varphi\rangle=\langle\mu_{\text{in}},\varphi\rangle+\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\dfrac{1}{2}\langle\mu_{s}\otimes\mu_{s},K\mathcal{A}\left[\varphi\right]\rangle-\langle\mu_{s},\mathcal{G}_{s}\varphi+\mathcal{B}\left[\varphi\right]\rangle+\langle\mathcal{L},\mathcal{C}_{s}\varphi\rangle\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\end{split}

for all
\varphi
in
\mathcal{B}_{b}(0,1].

Proof.

By equation (
1.6
) we have that

\mathcal{G}(t,x)\leq K_{\beta}M_{\beta},\quad\mathcal{C}(t,x)\leq 2F_{0}M_{\gamma},\quad\sup_{x,y\in(0,1]}F(x,y)\leq 2F_{0}

and we write
\displaystyle K_{\infty}=\sup_{x,y\in(0,1]}K(x,y).

Fix
T>0.

We let

R=\|\mu_{\text{in}}\|_{\text{TV}}+\dfrac{F_{0}}{1+\gamma}\big(\|\mu_{\text{in}}\|_{\text{TV}}+2F_{0}M_{\gamma}T\big)T+2F_{0}M_{\gamma}

and choose
\tau
with
0<\tau\leq T
such that

\begin{split}\big(6K_{\infty}R^{2}+2\big(K_{\beta}M_{\beta}+3F_{0}\big)R+2F_{0}M_{\gamma}\big)\tau\leq R\\
\left(6K_{\infty}+K_{\beta}M_{\beta}+3F_{0}\right)\tau<1.\end{split}

For
r>0
let
B(0,r)=\{\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{b}(0,1]\,:\,\|\mu\|_{\text{TV}}\leq r\}.

We let
X_{\tau}=C\big([0,\tau];B(0,2R)\big)
and write
\left\lVert\mu\right\rVert_{X_{\tau}}=\sup_{t\in[0,\tau]}\|\mu_{t}\|_{\text{TV}}.

X_{\tau}
is a complete metric space with metric
d(\mu,\lambda)=\left\lVert\mu-\lambda\right\rVert_{X_{\tau}}.

Step 1. Contraction mapping

Consider the map
P
defined on
\mu\in X_{\tau}
by

\begin{split}\langle(P\mu)_{t},\varphi\rangle=\langle\mu_{\text{in}},\varphi\rangle+\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\dfrac{1}{2}\langle\mu_{s}\otimes\mu_{s},K\mathcal{A}\left[\varphi\right]\rangle-\langle\mu_{s},\mathcal{G}_{s}\varphi+\mathcal{B}\left[\varphi\right]\rangle+\langle\mathcal{L},\mathcal{C}_{s}\varphi\rangle\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\end{split}

for
t\in[0,\tau]
,
\varphi\in C_{0}(0,1]
.
If
\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq 1,
then we have
\quad\left\lvert\langle(P\mu)_{t},\varphi\rangle\right\rvert\leq

\begin{split}&\leq\|\mu_{\text{in}}\|_{\text{TV}}+\dfrac{3K_{\infty}}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|\mu_{s}\|_{\text{TV}}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\big(K_{\beta}M_{\beta}+3F_{0}\big)\int_{0}^{t}\|\mu_{s}\|_{\text{TV}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+2F_{0}M_{\gamma}\,t\\
&\leq\|\mu_{\text{in}}\|_{\text{TV}}+\Big(\dfrac{3K_{\infty}}{2}\left\lVert\mu\right\rVert_{X_{\tau}}^{2}+\big(K_{\beta}M_{\beta}+3F_{0}\big)\left\lVert\mu\right\rVert_{X_{\tau}}+2F_{0}M_{\gamma}\Big)\tau\\
&\leq\|\mu_{\text{in}}\|_{\text{TV}}+\big(6K_{\infty}R^{2}+2\big(K_{\beta}M_{\beta}+3F_{0}\big)R+2F_{0}M_{\gamma}\big)\tau\leq 2R.\end{split}

whence
\|(P\mu)_{t}\|_{\text{TV}}\leq 2R
and
P(X_{\tau})\subset X_{\tau}.

For
\mu,\nu\in X_{\tau}

\begin{split}\left\lvert\langle(P\mu)_{t}-(P\nu)_{t},\varphi\rangle\right\rvert\leq\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left\lvert\langle(\mu_{s}+\nu_{s})\otimes(\mu_{s}-\nu_{s}),K\mathcal{A}\left[\varphi\right]\rangle\right\rvert\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\\
+\int_{0}^{t}\big|\langle\mu_{s}-\nu_{s},\left\lvert\mathcal{G}_{s}\varphi\right\rvert+\left\lvert\mathcal{B}\left[\varphi\right]\right\rvert\rangle\big|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\leq\left(6K_{\infty}+K_{\beta}M_{\beta}+3F_{0}\right)\tau\left\lVert\mu-\nu\right\rVert_{X_{\tau}}\end{split}

but
\left(6K_{\infty}+K_{\beta}M_{\beta}+3F_{0}\right)\tau<1
by assumption, hence
P
is a contraction mapping and must have a unique fixed point
\mu\in X_{\tau}.

Step 2. Nonnegativity of the solution

To show that
\mu_{t}
is actually in
\mathcal{M}^{+}_{b}(0,1]
for each
t\in[0,\tau],
we introduce the integrating factor

\theta(t,x)=\exp{\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left[\mathcal{G}(s,x)+\int_{0}^{x}F(x-y,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y+\int_{(0,1]}K(x,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{s}(y)\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right)}

which is differentiable in
t
with derivative

\partial_{t}\theta(t,x)=\theta(t,x)\cdot\left(\mathcal{G}_{t}(x)+\int_{0}^{x}F(x-y,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y+\int_{(0,1]}K(x,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}(y)\right).

Both functions
\theta
and
\partial_{t}\theta
are Borel and bounded in
[0,\tau]\times(0,1]
.
We write
\theta^{\prime}_{t}
for the map
t\mapsto\partial_{t}\theta(t,\cdot).

If we consider the measure
\bar{\mu}_{t}=\theta_{t}\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{b}(0,1]
and write
S(\varphi)(x,y)=\overline{\varphi}(x+y),

\mathcal{D}\left[\varphi\right](x)=\int_{0}^{x}F(x-y,y)\varphi(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y,
we see that
\bar{\mu}
satisfies

\begin{split}&\langle\partial_{t}\bar{\mu}_{t},\varphi\rangle=\langle\partial_{t}\mu_{t},\theta_{t}\varphi\rangle+\langle\mu_{t},\theta^{\prime}_{t}\varphi\rangle=\\
&=\dfrac{1}{2}\langle\mu_{t}\otimes\mu_{t},KS(\theta_{t}\varphi)\rangle+\langle\mu_{t},\mathcal{D}\left[\theta_{t}\varphi\right]\rangle+\langle\mathcal{L},\mathcal{C}_{t}\theta_{t}\varphi\rangle\\
&=\dfrac{1}{2}\langle\bar{\mu}_{t}\otimes\bar{\mu}_{t},KS(\theta_{t}\varphi)(\theta_{t}^{-1}\otimes\theta_{t}^{-1})\rangle+\langle\bar{\mu}_{t},\theta_{t}^{-1}\mathcal{D}\left[\theta_{t}\varphi\right]\rangle+\langle\mathcal{L},\mathcal{C}_{t}\theta_{t}\varphi\rangle.\end{split}

Let
\langle\mathcal{Q}_{t}(\nu),\varphi\rangle=\dfrac{1}{2}\langle\nu\otimes\nu,KS(\theta_{t}\varphi)(\theta_{t}^{-1}\otimes\theta_{t}^{-1})\rangle+\langle\nu,\theta_{t}^{-1}\mathcal{D}\left[\theta_{t}\varphi\right]\rangle+\langle\mathcal{L},\mathcal{C}_{t}\theta_{t}\varphi\rangle,

then

\langle\bar{\mu}_{t},\varphi\rangle=\langle\mu_{\text{in}},\varphi\rangle+\int_{0}^{t}\langle\mathcal{Q}_{s}(\bar{\mu}_{s}),\varphi\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s=:\langle(Q\bar{\mu})_{t},\varphi\rangle

and
\bar{\mu}
is a fixed point of the map
Q.

Using the bounds

1\leq\theta(t,x)\leq\exp{\tau\left(K_{\beta}M_{\beta}+2F_{0}+2K_{\infty}R\right)}

it is not hard to see that
Q
is a contraction mapping in
C\big([0,\widetilde{\tau}];B(0,L)\big)
for some
L=L(R)>0
and a sufficiently small
\widetilde{\tau}=\widetilde{\tau}(R)\leq\tau.

Thus
\bar{\mu}
must be the unique fixed point in this space.

Consider the approximating sequence

\bar{\mu}_{t}^{0}=\mu_{\text{in}},\quad\bar{\mu}_{t}^{n}=\mu_{\text{in}}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{Q}_{s}(\bar{\mu}_{s}^{n-1})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\quad\text{ for }n\geq 1.

Since
\mu_{\text{in}}\in\mathcal{M}^{+}_{b}(0,1],
for nonnegative
\varphi\in C_{0}(0,1]
we have

\langle\bar{\mu}_{t}^{n},\varphi\rangle\geq 0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N},

t\in[0,\widetilde{\tau}].

Taking the limit as
n\to\infty
we see that

\langle\bar{\mu}_{t},\varphi\rangle\geq 0
and hence
\bar{\mu}_{t}\in\mathcal{M}^{+}_{b}(0,1]
for all
t\in[0,\widetilde{\tau}]
.
Since
\langle\mu_{t},\varphi\rangle=\langle\bar{\mu}_{t},\theta_{t}^{-1}\varphi\rangle
and
\theta_{t}>0
, we also have
\mu_{t}\in\mathcal{M}^{+}_{b}(0,1]
for all
t\in[0,\widetilde{\tau}]
.

Step 3. Extending the solution to all times

Let
\varphi_{1}(x)=x>0,
then

\mathcal{A}\left[\varphi_{1}\right](x,y)\leq 0,\quad\mathcal{B}\left[\varphi_{1}\right](x)=0

and plugging
\varphi_{1}
in the equation satisfied by
\mu,
using that
\mu_{t}\in\mathcal{M}^{+}_{b}(0,1]

\mathfrak{m}_{1}(t)=\langle\mu_{t},\varphi_{1}\rangle\leq\langle\mu_{\text{in}},\varphi_{1}\rangle+\int_{0}^{t}\langle\mathcal{L},\mathcal{C}_{s}\varphi_{1}\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\leq\|\mu_{\text{in}}\|_{\text{TV}}+2F_{0}M_{\gamma}T

(2.1)

for
t\in[0,\widetilde{\tau}].

Let
\varphi_{0}(x)=\chi_{(0,1]}(x),

then
\mathcal{A}\left[\varphi_{0}\right](x,y)=-1
for
x,y\in(0,1]

and

-\mathcal{B}\left[\varphi_{0}\right](x)=\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{x}F(x-y,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\leq\dfrac{F_{0}}{1+\gamma}x^{1+\gamma},

which combined with (
2.1
) yields

-\langle\mu_{t},\mathcal{B}\left[\varphi_{0}\right]\rangle\leq\dfrac{F_{0}}{1+\gamma}\mathfrak{m}_{1+\gamma}(t)\leq\dfrac{F_{0}}{1+\gamma}\big(\|\mu_{\text{in}}\|_{\text{TV}}+2F_{0}M_{\gamma}T\big).

It follows that

\begin{split}&\|\mu_{t}\|_{\text{TV}}=\langle\mu_{t},\varphi_{0}\rangle\leq\langle\mu_{\text{in}},\varphi_{0}\rangle-\int_{0}^{t}\langle\mu_{s},\mathcal{B}\left[\varphi_{0}\right]\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\langle\mathcal{L},\mathcal{C}_{s}\varphi_{0}\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\leq\\
&\quad\leq\|\mu_{\text{in}}\|_{\text{TV}}+\dfrac{F_{0}}{1+\gamma}\big(\|\mu_{\text{in}}\|_{\text{TV}}+2F_{0}M_{\gamma}T\big)T+2F_{0}M_{\gamma}\leq R\end{split}

for
t\in[0,\widetilde{\tau}].

To conclude the reasoning, we note that in Step 1 we only used the bounds
\|\mu_{\text{in}}\|_{\text{TV}}\leq R
and the remaining estimates did not depend on the initial condition.
This way, redefining
P
by letting
\mu_{\text{in}}\to\mu_{\widetilde{\tau}},

g(t,x)\to g(\widetilde{\tau}+t,x),
the fixed point equation
\mu=P\mu
again has a unique solution in
X_{\widetilde{\tau}}
which gives an extension of
\mu
to the interval
[0,\min{\{2\widetilde{\tau},T\}}].

By induction we see that the solution may be extended to
[0,T]
.
Since
T
was arbitrary, we deduce that the constructed solution
\mu
is in
C\big([0,\infty);\mathcal{M}^{+}_{b}(0,1]\big).

The global uniqueness is now clear by the local uniqueness.
∎

From now on we assume that
K
is a singular kernel satisfying (
1.5
).

2.2.
A priori estimates

For each
j\in\mathbb{N}
we consider the bounded coagulation kernel
K_{j}=K\cdot\chi_{\{x,y>1/j\}}.

Each of the kernels has an associated
\mathcal{G}
-term
\mathcal{G}^{j}_{t}(x)=\int_{1}^{\infty}K_{j}(x,y)g(t,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y
and determines a unique solution
\mu^{j}\in C\big([0,\infty);\mathcal{M}^{+}_{b}(0,1]\big)
of the boundary valued coagulation fragmentation equation.

We derive some
a priori
estimates for this sequence of solutions:

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
T>0
and assume
M_{\gamma}<\infty
.
Then there exists
C_{T}>0
depending on
\|\mu_{\text{in}}\|_{\text{TV}}
such that for all
\delta\in(0,1)
and
j\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\mu^{j}_{t}\|_{\text{TV}}\leq C_{T}(1+M_{\gamma}),\quad\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mu^{j}_{t}(0,\delta)\leq\mu_{\text{in}}(0,\delta)+C_{T}(1+M_{\gamma})\delta.

Proof.

Let
\varphi_{0}(x)=\chi_{(0,1]}(x).

Computing, we have

\mathcal{A}\left[\varphi_{0}\right](x,y)=-1\quad\text{ for }x,y\leq 1,\quad\mathcal{B}\left[\varphi_{0}\right](x)=-\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{x}F(x-y,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\quad\text{ for }x\leq 1

and testing (
1.9
) with
\varphi_{0},
by (
1.6
)

\begin{split}\|\mu^{j}_{t}\|_{\text{TV}}\leq\|\mu_{\text{in}}\|_{\text{TV}}+F_{0}\int_{0}^{t}\|\mu^{j}_{s}\|_{\text{TV}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+2F_{0}M_{\gamma}t.\end{split}

Applying Grönwall’s inequality

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\mu^{j}_{t}\|_{\text{TV}}\leq\left(\|\mu_{\text{in}}\|_{\text{TV}}+2F_{0}M_{\gamma}T\right)e^{F_{0}T},

which gives the first claim.

For
\delta\in(0,1)
let
\varphi_{\delta}(x)=\chi_{(0,\delta)}(x).

We have

\mathcal{A}\left[\varphi_{\delta}\right](x,y)\leq 0
for
x,y\in(0,1]
and

\begin{split}\mathcal{B}\left[\varphi_{\delta}\right](x)&=\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{x}F(x-y,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\,\chi_{(0,\delta)}(x)-\int_{0}^{x}F(x-y,y)\chi_{(0,\delta)}(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\\
&\geq-\chi_{(0,\delta)}(x)\int_{0}^{x}F(x-y,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y-\chi_{(\delta,1]}(x)\int_{0}^{\delta}F(x-y,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\\
&\geq-\dfrac{F_{0}}{1+\gamma}\Big(2x^{1+\gamma}\chi_{(0,\delta)}(x)+\chi_{(\delta,1]}(x)\big(\delta^{1+\gamma}+x^{1+\gamma}-(x-\delta)^{1+\gamma}\big)\Big)\\
&\geq-\dfrac{F_{0}}{1+\gamma}\Big(3\delta^{1+\gamma}+(1+\gamma)x^{\gamma}\delta\Big)\geq-F_{0}\dfrac{4+\gamma}{1+\gamma}\delta\end{split}

Plugging
\varphi_{\delta}
in (
1.9
)

\begin{split}\mu^{j}_{t}(0,\delta)&\leq\mu_{\text{in}}(0,\delta)-\int_{0}^{t}\langle\mu^{j}_{s},\mathcal{B}\left[\varphi_{\delta}\right]\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\langle\mathcal{L},\mathcal{C}_{s}\varphi_{\delta}\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
&\leq\mu_{\text{in}}(0,\delta)+F_{0}\dfrac{4+\gamma}{1+\gamma}\delta\int_{0}^{t}\|\mu_{s}\|_{\text{TV}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+2F_{0}M_{\gamma}\delta\,t\end{split}

and the second claim is a consequence of the first.
∎

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
T>0
and assume
M_{\max{\{\beta,\gamma\}}}<\infty.

Then there exists
C_{T}>0
depending on
\|\mu_{\text{in}}\|_{\text{TV}}
such that

\left\lvert\langle\mu^{j}_{t}-\mu^{j}_{s},\varphi\rangle\right\rvert\leq C_{T}\big(1+M_{\max{\{\beta,\gamma\}}}\big)^{2}\big(\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}+[\varphi]_{\text{Lip}}\big)\left\lvert t-s\right\rvert

(2.2)

for all
\varphi\in\mathrm{Lip}_{0}(0,1],

t,s\in[0,T]
and
j\in\mathbb{N}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality
s<t.

Substracting the equation for
\mu^{j}
at times
t
and
s
, applying Lemma
1.4
and Proposition
2.4
to the integrands

\begin{split}&\left\lvert\langle\mu^{j}_{t}-\mu^{j}_{s},\varphi\rangle\right\rvert\leq\\
&\leq\int_{s}^{t}\Big(\left\lvert\langle\mu^{j}_{r},\mathcal{G}^{j}_{r}\varphi\rangle\right\rvert+\left\lvert\langle\mu^{j}_{r},\mathcal{B}\left[\varphi\right]\rangle\right\rvert+\dfrac{1}{2}\left\lvert\langle\mu^{j}_{r}\otimes\mu^{j}_{r},K_{j}\mathcal{A}\left[\varphi\right]\rangle\right\rvert+\left\lvert\langle\mathcal{L},\mathcal{C}_{r}\varphi\rangle\right\rvert\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\\
&\leq\int_{s}^{t}\Big(\|\mu^{j}_{r}\|_{\text{TV}}4K_{0}\mathfrak{M}_{\beta}(r)[\varphi]_{\text{Lip}}+\|\mu^{j}_{r}\|_{\text{TV}}3F_{0}\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\\
&\quad+\int_{s}^{t}\Big(\|\mu^{j}_{r}\|_{\text{TV}}^{2}4K_{0}[\varphi]_{\text{Lip}}+2F_{0}\mathfrak{M}_{\gamma}(r)\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\\
&\leq C_{T}\Big(\big(1+M_{\max{\{\beta,\gamma\}}}\big)^{2}[\varphi]_{\text{Lip}}+\big(1+M_{\max{\{\beta,\gamma\}}}\big)\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}\Big)|t-s|\end{split}

for some
C_{T}>0
additionally depending on
\|\mu_{\text{in}}\|_{\text{TV}}.

∎

2.3.
Compactness

The space
C_{0}(0,1]
is separable by functions in
\mathrm{Lip}_{0}(0,1],
more specifically

Lemma 2.6
.

There is a sequence
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathrm{Lip}_{0}(0,1]
with
\left\lVert\varphi_{n}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq 2
such that for every
\varepsilon>0
and
f\in C_{0}(0,1]
with
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq 1
we have
\left\lVert f-\varphi_{k}\right\rVert_{\infty}<\varepsilon
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}.

Proof.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N},
choose
\{p_{n,j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
an enumeration of the polynomials with rational coefficients restricted to
[1/n,1].

We extend

p_{n,j}
to
(0,1]
inside
\mathrm{Lip}_{0}(0,1]
by linear interpolation

\tilde{p}_{n,j}(x)=\begin{cases}p_{n,j}(x),&\text{ if }x\in[1/n,1]\\
n\,p_{n,j}(1/n)\,x,&\text{ if }x\in(0,1/n)\end{cases}

and further
restrict to the
\tilde{p}_{n,j}
such that
\left\lVert\tilde{p}_{n,j}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq 2.

The fact that this family gives the desired sequence is a consequence of the Weierstrass’ approximation theorem together with the definition of
f
vanishing at zero.
∎

The next result is easy to check:

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
B(0,1)=\{\mu\colon\|\mu\|_{\text{TV}}\leq 1\}\subset\mathcal{M}_{b}(0,1]

and let
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathrm{Lip}_{0}(0,1]
be a sequence as in Lemma
2.6
.
Then the topology generated by the norm

\left\lVert\mu\right\rVert_{\star}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{\left\lvert\langle\mu,\varphi_{j}\rangle\right\rvert}{2^{j}(1+[\varphi_{j}]_{\text{Lip}})},

restricted to
B(0,1)
is equivalent to the weak-
\star
topology in
B(0,1).

By the above, we can extract a weakly convergent subsequence from the approximate solutions:

Proposition 2.8
.

There exists
\mu\in C\big([0,\infty);\mathcal{M}^{\text{w}\star}_{b}(0,1]\big)
and a subsequence
\mu^{j_{k}}
such that

•

\mu^{j_{k}}_{t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\star}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\mu_{t}
as
k\to\infty
uniformly in
t\in[0,T]
for all
T>0

•

\mu^{j_{k}}_{t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle b}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\mu_{t}
as
k\to\infty
for each
t\geq 0

Proof.

By the first estimate in Proposition
2.4
, for each
t\geq 0
the set
\{\mu^{j}_{t}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded with respect to the total variation norm and is therefore precompact in the weak-
\star
topology.
Propositions
2.5
and
2.7
show that the sequence is equicontinuous as a family of maps in the space
C\big([0,T];\mathcal{M}^{\text{w}\star}_{b}(0,1]\big).

The first claim follows by iterative application of Arzelà-Ascoli to the sequence in
C\big([0,T_{k}];\mathcal{M}^{\text{w}\star}_{b}(0,1]\big)
for each
T_{k}=k\in\mathbb{N}
and taking the diagonal sequence.
We do not relabel this convergent subsequence.

Let
t\geq 0.

Given any subsequence
\{j_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, the tightness estimate in Proposition
2.4
implies by Prokhorov’s theorem that there exists a measure
\bar{\mu}_{t}\in\mathcal{M}_{b}(0,1]
and
a further subsequence
\{j_{k_{l}}\}_{l\in\mathbb{N}}

such that
\mu_{t}^{j_{k_{l}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle b}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\bar{\mu}_{t}
as
l\to\infty
.
In particular,
\mu_{t}^{j_{k_{l}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\star}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\bar{\mu}_{t}
and by the uniqueness of the limit we actually have
\mu_{t}=\bar{\mu}_{t}.

Since the subsequence was arbitrary, the second claim follows.
∎

2.4.
Passing to the limit

For convenience we write
\mu^{j}
for the convergent subsequence in Proposition
2.8
.

Lemma 2.9
.

For every
t\in[0,T]
,
\varphi\in\mathrm{Lip}_{0}(0,1]
we have

\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{(0,1]^{2}}\big(K(x,y)-K_{j}(x,y)\big)\,\left\lvert\mathcal{A}\left[\varphi\right](x,y)\right\rvert\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu^{j}_{t}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu^{j}_{t}(y)=0,

\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{(0,1]}\big(\mathcal{G}_{t}(x)-\mathcal{G}^{j}_{t}(x)\big)\,\left\lvert\varphi(x)\right\rvert\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu^{j}_{t}(x)=0.

Proof.

By Lemma
1.4

\int_{(0,1/j]}\int_{(0,1/j]}K(x,y)\big|\mathcal{A}\left[\varphi\right](x,y)\big|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu^{j}_{t}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu^{j}_{t}(y)\leq C[\varphi]_{\text{Lip}}\mu^{j}_{t}(0,1/j)^{2},

\int_{(0,1/j]}\int_{(1/j,1]}K(x,y)\left\lvert\mathcal{A}\left[\varphi\right](x,y)\right\rvert\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu^{j}_{t}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu^{j}_{t}(y)\leq C[\varphi]_{\text{Lip}}\mu^{j}_{t}(0,1/j),

\int_{(0,1/j]}\mathcal{G}_{t}(x)\,\left\lvert\varphi(x)\right\rvert\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu^{j}_{t}(x)\leq CM_{\beta}[\varphi]_{\text{Lip}}\,\mu^{j}_{t}(0,1/j),

and by Proposition
2.4
we have
\mu^{j}_{t}(0,1/j)\to 0
as
j\to\infty
since
\mu_{\text{in}}\in\mathcal{M}^{+}_{b}(0,1].

∎

The results above allow us now to take the limit inside the equation.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

We claim that the measure
\mu
constructed in Proposition
2.8
is a solution of the boundary valued coagulation-fragmentation equation.

Let
\varphi\in\mathrm{Lip}_{0}(0,1].

Weak-
\star
convergence implies

\langle\mu^{j}_{t},\mathcal{B}\left[\varphi\right]\rangle\to\langle\mu_{t},\mathcal{B}\left[\varphi\right]\rangle

uniformly in
t\in[0,T]
for
T>0,
whence

\int_{0}^{t}\langle\mu^{j}_{s},\mathcal{B}\left[\varphi\right]\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\to\int_{0}^{t}\langle\mu_{s},\mathcal{B}\left[\varphi\right]\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

for each
t\geq 0.

We claim that for each
t\geq 0
we have
\mu^{j}_{t}\otimes\mu^{j}_{t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle b}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\mu_{t}\otimes\mu_{t}
.
Indeed, by the
a priori
estimates in Proposition
2.4
the sequence
\{\mu^{j}_{t}\otimes\mu^{j}_{t}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
\mathcal{M}_{b}\left((0,1]^{2}\right)
and tight.
By Prokhorov’s theorem there is a further subsequence (which we do not relabel) and a measure
\lambda_{t}\in\mathcal{M}^{+}_{b}\big((0,1]^{2}\big)
such that

3.6

\{f_{n}(t)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
L^{1}(0,1).

The Dunford-Pettis theorem now implies that
\{f_{n}(t)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is precompact in the weak
L^{1}(0,1)
topology.

By the estimates for the contraction mapping in the proof of Proposition
2.3
together with the
a priori
bounds in Lemma
3.6
, there exists a
C>0
such that

\left\lvert\langle f_{n}(t)-f_{n}(s),\varphi\rangle\right\rvert\leq C(1+M_{\max{\{\beta,\gamma\}}})\left\lvert t-s\right\rvert\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}

for all
t,s\geq 0,

\varphi\in L^{\infty}(0,1).

It follows that the sequence
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is strongly equicontinuous into
L^{1}(0,1)
.

By Arzelà-Ascoli there exists a map
\bar{f}\colon[0,T]\to L^{1}(0,1)
which is continuous with respect to the weak topology in
L^{1}(0,1)
and a subsequence (not relabelled) such that

f_{n}(t,\cdot)\to\bar{f}(t,\cdot)
in the weak topology, uniformly in
t\in[0,T].

Step 2. Stability

Looking at the last term
in inequality (
3.2
) we notice that

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{1}^{\infty}K(x,y)Q(y)\,\mathcal{E}\left[f_{n}(t,x),Q(x)\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=\\
&\quad=\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n}+T}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{1}^{\infty}K(x,y)Q(y)\,\mathcal{E}\left[f(t,x),Q(x)\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t,\end{split}

which is an expression converging to zero as
n\to\infty.

Indeed, letting
t\to\infty
in the
a priori
estimates in Proposition
3.4
we see that the time integrals are finite, implying that their tails converge to zero, hence the claim as
t_{n}\to\infty
when
n\to\infty.

Using the inequality

x\leq Ny+\dfrac{1}{\log{N}}(x-y)(\log{x}-\log{y})

with
N=1+\varepsilon
we have

f_{n}(t,x)-Q(x)\leq\varepsilon Q(x)+\dfrac{1}{\log{(1+\varepsilon)}}\mathcal{E}\left[f_{n}(t,x),Q(x)\right]

Q(x)-f_{n}(t,x)\leq\varepsilon f_{n}(t,x)+\dfrac{1}{\log{(1+\varepsilon)}}\mathcal{E}\left[f_{n}(t,x),Q(x)\right].

By the above, applying Lemma
3.6
and recalling that
\mathcal{G}(x)\leq K_{\beta}M_{\beta}
for
x\geq 1
(since
K
is a
bounded coagulation kernel
according to definition

) we find that

\begin{split}&\quad\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{G}(x)\left\lvert f_{n}(t,x)-Q(x)\right\rvert\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\leq\\
&\leq\varepsilon K_{\beta}M_{\beta}T\|Q\|_{L^{1}}+\varepsilon K_{\beta}M_{\beta}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}f_{n}(t,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\\
&\quad+\dfrac{1}{\log{(1+\varepsilon)}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{1}^{\infty}K(x,y)Q(y)\,\mathcal{E}\left[f_{n}(t,x),Q(x)\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\\
&\leq C\varepsilon+\dfrac{1}{\log{(1+\varepsilon)}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{1}^{\infty}K(x,y)Q(y)\,\mathcal{E}\left[f_{n}(t,x),Q(x)\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\end{split}

for some

Then

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{G}(x)\left\lvert f_{n}(t,x)-Q(x)\right\rvert\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\leq C\varepsilon

f_{n}(t,x)\to Q(x)\text{ for a.e. }t\in[0,T],x\in(0,1).

4.
Conclusions

In this paper we have studied boundary value problems for coagulation -fragmentation equations
which, in spite of the fact that they are of interest in some problems of atmospheric science,
have not yet been considered in the mathematical literature.

We have successfully established the existence of solutions
for the singular kernels considered in the applications
under the sole assumption of boundedness in time for the moments of the boundary datum.

In the case of coagulating systems, we have shown that the size distribution function for small clusters decreases in time at a rate which depends on the moments of the boundary size distribution function.
This result agrees with what is expected under such conditions, as the small clusters can only merge with each other to give larger ones until there are no small particles left.

On the other hand, the addition of a fragmentation mechanism complicates the analysis of the large time asymptotics.
Under the assumptions on the boundary datum in Theorem
1.11
(namely, it being time independent and satisfying a detailed balance condition) we have seen that
there exists a decreasing Lyapunov functional implying the convergence of solutions to a unique equilibrium.

However, it is not clear whether convergence to an equilibrium under weaker hypotheses is possible.
The usual detailed balance appearing in the classical problems without boundary involves the coagulation and fragmentation kernels only, but the introduction of boundary conditions
requires to complement this condition with additional assumptions on the boundary data.
This is not surprising, since the coagulation-fragmentation model with nonequilibrium data (even when these are time independent) can be thought of as an open system in contact with reservoirs and it is well known that open chemical systems out of equilibrium might yield an oscillatory behavior
[
7
]
.

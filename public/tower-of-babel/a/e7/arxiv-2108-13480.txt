Arithmetic Statistics for Galois deformation rings
Anwesh Ray, Tom Weston

Abstract
. Given an elliptic curve E E defined over the rational numbers and a prime p p at which E E has good reduction, we consider the Galois deformation ring parametrizing lifts of the residual representation on the p p -torsion group E ⁡ [ p ] E[p] . The deformations considered are subject to the flat condition at p p . For a fixed elliptic curve without complex multiplication, it is shown that these deformation rings are unobstructed for all but finitely many primes. For a fixed prime p p and varying elliptic curve E E , we relate the problem to the question of how often p p does not divide the modular degree. Heuristics due to M.Watkins based on those of Cohen and Lenstra indicate that this proportion should be ∏ i ≥ 1 ( 1 − 1 p i ) ≈ 1 − 1 p − 1 p 2 \prod_{i\geq 1}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{i}}\right)\approx 1-\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p^{2}} . This heuristic is supported by computations which indicate that most elliptic curves (satisfying further conditions) have smooth deformation rings at a given prime p ≥ 5 p\geq 5 , and this proportion comes close to 100 % 100\% as p p gets larger.



1.
Introduction

Given an elliptic curve
E
defined over the rationals, and a prime number
p
, denote by
E[p]
the
p
-torsion subgroup of
E(\bar{\mathbb{Q}})
. B.Mazur introduced deformation functors, parametrizing lifts of the residual representation

\bar{\rho}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})

on
E[p]
. These functors are represented by universal deformation rings and their study gained considerable momentum in
[
33
,
29
,
4
]
, where the modularity of an elliptic curve
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
is established. The approach involved showing that a certain deformation ring associated to
\bar{\rho}
is in fact isomorphic to a localized Hecke algebra associated to a space of modular forms. A result of this flavor which establishes an isomorphism between a deformation ring and a localized Hecke algebra is known as an
\operatorname{R}=\mathbb{T}
theorem. Here,
\operatorname{R}
refers to the deformation ring associated to a residual representation
\bar{\rho}
and
\mathbb{T}
is the associated localized Hecke algebra. The Galois representations which arise from elliptic curves and modular forms satisfy additional local conditions, and hence, the deformations functors of interest are subject to a local constraint at
p
, which is defined using
p
-adic Hodge theory. There has been considerable interest in extending such
\operatorname{R}=\mathbb{T}
results to more general Galois representations arising from cuspidal automorphic representations of higher rank, see for instance
[
12
,
30
,
3
]
. For instance, a brilliant application of such automorphy results led to the resolution of the Sato-Tate conjecture, see
[
2
]
.

In this paper, we shall consider deformations with fixed determinant equal to the cyclotomic character. Given the importance of deformation rings in proving modularity results, it is of natural interest to explicitly characterize Galois deformation rings. Such investigations were carried out by N.Boston and B.Mazur, see
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
. Presentations for Galois deformation rings with prescribed local conditions are discussed in the work of G. Böckle
[
5
]
. The deformation ring is presented by generators and relations. When there are no relations, the ring is smooth. If this is the case, the deformation ring/functor is said to be
unobstructed
and it is in fact isomorphic to a power series ring over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. On the other hand, there may be relations, in which case its structure is more complicated. Such relations arise from
local and global obstructions
to lifting. The local obstructions are characterized by local cohomology classes, while on the other hand, the global obstructions are characterized by global cohomology classes subject to local constraints (see
[
5
, Theorem 5.2]
for further details).

The deformation rings studied in this paper are equipped with a local condition at
p
, which we shall refer to as
geometric deformation rings
. There is one such ring
\mathcal{R}_{E,p}
associated to each pair
(E,p)
, where
E
is a rational elliptic curve and
p
an odd prime at which
E
has good reduction. We study the structure of these rings on average, more precisely, how often they are unobstructed. Since the determinant is fixed throughout, the geometric deformation ring is smooth if and only if it is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. In this case, there is a unique characteristic-zero lift. We study the following questions.

Question 1.1
.

(1)

For a fixed elliptic curve
E
, how often is the deformation ring
\mathcal{R}_{E,p}
unobstructed?

(2)

For a fixed odd prime
p
and
E
varying over all rational elliptic curves with good reduction at
p
, how often is the deformation ring
\mathcal{R}_{E,p}
unobstructed?

The first question was raised by Mazur in
[
23
, section 11]
, and studied by the second named author in
[
34
]
. In
loc. cit.
one considers a Hecke eigencuspform
f
of weight
k\geq 2
and squarefree level. At each prime
p
, denote by
\bar{\rho}_{f,p}
the residual representation at
p
. Then it is shown that for
100\%
of the primes
p
, the full deformation ring associated to
\bar{\rho}_{f,p}
(with no constraints on the determinant or local conditions) is unobstructed (and isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket X_{1},X_{2},X_{3}\rrbracket
). Furthermore, when
k>2
, the deformation ring is unobstructed for all but finitely many primes. These results were extended by J.Hatley to the case when the level is not squarefree, see
[
19
]
. When
f
is the modular form associated to an elliptic curve, the weight
k=2
and it is still expected (but not proven except in a few cases; see
[
27
]
) that the full deformation ring is unobstructed for all by finitely many primes. These results have been generalized to Hilbert modular forms by A.Gamzon
[
16
]
, and subsequently to regular algebraic conjugate selfdual
cuspidal (RACSDC) automorphic representations by D.Guiraud
[
17
]
. We show that for a fixed elliptic curve
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
without complex multiplication, the geometric deformation ring
\mathcal{R}_{E,p}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
for all but an explicit finite set of primes
p
, see Theorem
4.7
and Corollary
4.8
.

It is part (2) of Question
1.1
that is of primary interest from the point of view of arithmetic statistics. This question has not been studied before in any related context, and the answers provided in the last section of the paper are based on results proven in the first four sections. The answer to this question is the main goal of the paper and results are proven specifically with this application to arithmetic statistics in mind. We restrict ourselves to elliptic curves with good ordinary reduction at
p
, squarefree conductor and for which the residual representation
\bar{\rho}_{E,p}
on
E[p]
is irreducible. Furthermore, we insist that all primes
\ell
dividing the conductor of
E
are not
\pm 1\mod{p}
, see Definition
5.1
. For this set of elliptic curves, it is shown that the deformation ring
\mathcal{R}_{E,p}
is unobstructed provided the degree of the modular parametrization
X_{0}(N)\rightarrow E
is coprime to
p
. This condition has been studied in detail by M.Watkins in
[
31
]
. Cohen-Lenstra heuristics indicate that the probability that
p
divides the modular degree of an elliptic curve is given by the product

1-\prod_{i\geq 1}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{i}}\right)=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{2}}-\frac{1}{p^{3}}+\dots.

The heuristics indicate that the proportion of elliptic curves subject to the above conditions for which
\mathcal{R}_{E,p}
is not isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
becomes smaller as
p
increases. These heurists are supported by computation which provide further insight into the problem.

The approach relies on showing that the
dual Selmer group
associated with the deformation problem is trivial on average. These groups characterize obstructions to lifting Galois representations subject to local conditions. The strategy employed involves showing that this dual Selmer group vanishes provided the
p
-primary Bloch-Kato Selmer group vanishes, see Proposition
4.2
. This argument is a purely Galois theoretic argument, which we expect should extend to more general Galois representations. The result of F. Diamond, M. Flach and L. Guo (see
[
14
]
) gives a criterion for the vanishing of these Bloch-Kato Selmer groups. The strategy used does not explicitly rely on
\operatorname{R}=\mathbb{T}
results.

Including the introduction, the article consists of five sections. In section
2
, we discuss the essential objects of study in the paper, namely the deformation rings associated to elliptic curves. In section
3
, we discuss presentations for deformation rings and establish a criterion for unobstructedness, in the setting in which there is a local condition at
p
. In section
4
, we study the first of the two aforementioned questions. In this section, it is shown that given a non-CM elliptic curve, the deformation rings considered are unobstructed for all but a finite explicit set of primes. In section
5
, the second question is studied, when
p\geq 5
is a fixed prime and
E
varies over a certain collection of elliptic curves. In this section the question of unobstructedness of the deformation ring is related to the modular degree. Cohen-Lenstra heuristics are supported by computations in this section.

2.1.
Galois deformations

Associated to the residual representation
\bar{\rho}=\bar{\rho}_{E,p}
are various Galois deformation rings, which parametrize the Galois representations which lift
\bar{\rho}
. We recall how these rings are defined and the properties that characterize them. Throughout,
S
will denote the set of primes
\ell
at which
E
has bad reduction. Note that by assumption,
S
does not contain
p
. The global representation gives rise to a local one at each prime
\ell
. For every prime
\ell
, choose an algebraic closure
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
of
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
and an embedding
\iota_{\ell}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\hookrightarrow\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}

\operatorname{G}_{\ell}
to denote the absolute Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}/\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})

\iota_{\ell}

\operatorname{G}_{\ell}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})

\bar{\rho}_{|\ell}
be the restriction of
\bar{\rho}
to
\operatorname{G}_{\ell}
. We introduce various functors of
Galois deformations
associated to the residual representation
\bar{\rho}
, some of which shall involve conditions on the local representations. First, we introduce the functor of deformations with no local constraints. Let
\mathcal{C}
be the category of commutative complete local noetherian
\mathbb{Z}_{p}

\mathbb{F}_{p}

coefficient rings
and are inverse limits of finite length local rings. A coefficient ring

is equipped with the inverse limit topology and admits a presentation of the form

A\simeq\frac{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\rrbracket}{(f_{1},\dots,f_{m})},

where

are in the maximal ideal of

. Here,
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\rrbracket
is the formal power series ring over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
in indeterminates
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
. Important examples of such coefficient rings include
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,
\mathbb{Z}/p^{N}
, the ring of dual numbers
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^{2})
and the Iwasawa algebra
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket T\rrbracket
. A coefficient ring
A
comes equipped with a unique residue map
A\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}
which is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-algebra homomorphism. A map between coefficient rings is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-algebra homomorphism which commutes with residue maps. Denote by
\widehat{\operatorname{GL}_{2}}(A)
the subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(A)
consisting of matrices which reduce to the identity in
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
. Two continuous
A
-valued representations

\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{S\cup\{p\}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(A)

lifting
\bar{\rho}
are strictly equivalent if they are conjugate by some matrix in
\widehat{\operatorname{GL}_{2}}(A)
.

Definition 2.1
.

A deformation
\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{S\cup\{p\}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(A)
of
\bar{\rho}
is a strict equivalence class of continuous lifts of
\bar{\rho}
.

Let
\chi
denote the cyclotomic character. Throughout, all deformations
\rho
of
\bar{\rho}
are stipulated to have determinant equal to
\chi
. When we refer to a Galois deformation
\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{S\cup\{p\}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(A)
, it is to be understood that
\rho
is an actual representation whose strict equivalence class is the deformation in question.

2.2.
Deformation rings

The functor of deformations

Let

Definition 2.3

A functor of deformations

(1)

(2)

For

(3)

Lemma 2.5

The functor
\operatorname{Def}_{\ell}
is unobstructed if
H^{0}(\operatorname{G}_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}^{*})=0

Proof.

By the local Euler characteristic formula,
H^{0}(\operatorname{G}_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}^{*})

H^{2}(\operatorname{G}_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})

Let

be a small extension and
t
a generator of the principal ideal

\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}
with the kernel of the mod-

by identifying
\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
c&-a\\
\end{array}}\right)

\operatorname{Id}+t\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
c&-a\\
\end{array}}\right)=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1+ta&tb\\
tc&1-ta\\
\end{array}}\right).

We show that

\varrho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\ell}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(A/I)

lifts to

\tilde{\varrho}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\ell}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(A).

Let

2.3.
The flat deformation condition

The deformation rings we consider shall parametrize geometric Galois representations
\rho
lifting
\bar{\rho}
for which
\operatorname{det}\rho=\chi
. This is ensured by choosing an appropriate local deformation condition
\mathcal{C}_{p}
for
\bar{\rho}_{|p}
. A deformation
\varrho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{p}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(A)
of
\bar{\rho}_{|p}
is said to be
flat
if for each finite length quotient

of

, the mod-
I

\varrho_{I}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\varrho\mod{I}
arises from the Galois action on the generic fibre of a a finite flat group scheme over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}

Definition 2.6
.

A filtered Dieudonné module
M
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module furnished with a decreasing, exhaustive, separated filtration

\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-modules. For each integer

\mathbb{Z}_{p}

\varphi^{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}M^{i}\rightarrow M

\varphi^{i+1}(x)=p\varphi^{i}(x)

\operatorname{MF}_{\operatorname{tor}}^{f}

\sum_{i}\varphi^{i}(M)=M
. For

\operatorname{MF}_{\operatorname{tor}}^{f,j}
to be the full subcategory of
\operatorname{MF}_{\operatorname{tor}}^{f}
consisting of modules

M^{0}=M
and
M^{j}=0
.

Let
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}^{f}
be the category of finite length
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\operatorname{G}_{p}]

3.
Presentations for Geometric Deformation Rings

Assume throughout that
p\geq 5
and that

\bar{\rho}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{S\cup\{p\}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})

is as in the previous section. In this section, we consider Galois representations which are ordinary/crystalline when localized at
p
, and thus in particular, are deRham. Such representations will be geometric and under a mild additional hypothesis, are known to arise from Hecke eigencuspforms. Following Böckle
[
5
]
, we discuss presentations for the associated Galois deformation rings. We do not impose any local conditions at the primes
\ell\in S
. However, at
p
, we impose the condition
\mathcal{C}_{p}
defined in the previous section. Recall that throughout, the determinant of all deformations considered is fixed and equal to
\chi
, the cyclotomic character.

We introduce an additional hypothesis which will play a role in simplifying some results in arithmetic statistics, however, the results in this section shall not assume this hypothesis.

Hypothesis 3.1
.

We say that condition
(\star)
is satisfied for
\bar{\rho}
if for all
\ell\in S
,

H^{0}(\operatorname{G}_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}^{*})=0.

We introduce Selmer and dual Selmer groups which will play an important role in describing presentations for Galois deformation rings. First, we introduce the notion of Selmer data for the set of primes
S\cup\{p\}
. A Selmer datum
\mathcal{L}
consists of a choice of subspace
\mathcal{L}_{\ell}\subseteq H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})
for each prime
\ell\in S\cup\{p\}
; we set
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}=0
. Let
\mathcal{L}_{\ell}^{\perp}\subset H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{p},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}^{*})
be the orthogonal complement of
\mathcal{L}_{\ell}
with respect to the local Tate-duality pairing. Then, the Selmer and dual Selmer groups associated to the datum
\mathcal{L}
are defined as follows

(3.1)

\begin{split}&H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(\operatorname{G}_{S\cup\{p\}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\text{ker}\left\{H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{S\cup\{p\}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{\ell\in S\cup\{p\}}\frac{H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})}{\mathcal{L}_{\ell}}\right\}\\
&H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}^{\perp}}(\operatorname{G}_{S\cup\{p\}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}^{*})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\text{ker}\left\{H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{S\cup\{p\}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}^{*})\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{\ell\in S\cup\{p\}}\frac{H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}^{*})}{\mathcal{L}_{\ell}^{\perp}}\right\}\\
\end{split}

respectively.

In this paper, we work in a special case, where
\mathcal{L}_{\ell}
is the full space
H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})
for
\ell\in S
and
\mathcal{L}_{p}=\mathcal{N}_{p}
. The following notation we use is not standard and there are no conditions at the primes
\ell\in S
.
The Selmer and dual Selmer groups are as follows

(3.2)

\begin{split}&H^{1}_{\langle p\rangle}(\operatorname{G}_{S\cup\{p\}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\text{ker}\left\{H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{S\cup\{p\}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})\longrightarrow\frac{H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{p},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})}{\mathcal{N}_{p}}\right\}\\
&H^{1}_{\langle p\rangle}(\operatorname{G}_{S\cup\{p\}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}^{*})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\text{ker}\left\{H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{S\cup\{p\}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}^{*})\longrightarrow\frac{H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{p},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}^{*})}{\mathcal{N}_{p}^{\perp}}\right\}\\
\end{split}

respectively.

Proposition 3.2
.

With respect to notation introduced above, we have that

\dim H^{1}_{\langle p\rangle}(\operatorname{G}_{S\cup\{p\}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})-\dim H^{1}_{\langle p\rangle}(\operatorname{G}_{S\cup\{p\}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}^{*})=\sum_{\ell\in S}H^{0}(\operatorname{G}_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}^{*}).

In particular,
(\star)
is satisfied if and only if

\dim H^{1}_{\langle p\rangle}(\operatorname{G}_{S\cup\{p\}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})=\dim H^{1}_{\langle p\rangle}(\operatorname{G}_{S\cup\{p\}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}^{*}).

Proof.

Suppose for each prime
\ell\in S\cup\{p,\infty\}
, there is a choice of subspace
\mathcal{L}_{\ell}\subseteq H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})
. The Selmer condition
\mathcal{L}
is the data
\{\mathcal{L}_{\ell}\}
and the dimensions of the Selmer and dual Selmer groups are related as follows

\begin{split}&\dim H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(\operatorname{G}_{S\cup\{p\}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})-\dim H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}^{\perp}}(\operatorname{G}_{S\cup\{p\}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}^{*})\\
=&\dim H^{0}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})-\dim H^{0}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}^{*})\\
+&\sum_{\ell\in S\cup\{p,\infty\}}\left(\dim\mathcal{L}_{\ell}-\dim H^{0}(\operatorname{G}_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})\right),\\
\end{split}

according to Wiles’ formula
[
24
, Theorem 8.7.9]
.
The representation
\bar{\rho}
associated to an elliptic curve is
odd
, i.e.,
\det\bar{\rho}(c)=-1
, where
c
denotes complex conjugation. It is therefore easy to show that
\dim H^{0}(\operatorname{G}_{\infty},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})=1
. Now, specify the Selmer data as follows

\mathcal{L}_{\ell}=\begin{cases}0\text{ if }\ell=\infty,\\
\mathcal{N}_{p}\text{ if }\ell=p,\\
H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})\text{ if }\ell\in S.\\
\end{cases}

In this case, the Selmer group
H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(\operatorname{G}_{S\cup\{p\}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})
coincides with the Selmer group
H^{1}_{\langle p\rangle}(\operatorname{G}_{S\cup\{p\}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})
. Since
\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}
is assumed to be irreducible, we have that

\dim H^{0}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})=\dim H^{0}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}^{*})=0.

Also, note that for
\ell\neq p
by the local Euler characteristic formula,

\dim H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})-\dim H^{0}(\operatorname{G}_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})=\dim H^{2}(\operatorname{G}_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}).

On the other hand, according to local duality,

\dim H^{2}(\operatorname{G}_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})=\dim H^{0}(\operatorname{G}_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}^{*}).

Finally, note that

\dim\mathcal{N}_{p}=1+\dim H^{0}(\operatorname{G}_{p},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}).

Putting it all together, we obtain the result.

∎

as follows

H^{1}_{\bf{f}}\left(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}_{p}^{0}(E)\right)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{cases}H^{1}_{nr}\left(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}_{p}^{0}(E)\right)&\text{ if }\ell\neq p,\infty,\\
\operatorname{ker}\left\{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}_{p}^{0}(E))\longrightarrow H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},\operatorname{B}_{\operatorname{crys}}\otimes\operatorname{Ad}_{p}^{0}(E))\right\}&\text{ if }\ell=p,\\
0&\text{ if }\ell=\infty.\end{cases}

Let
\operatorname{W}_{p}(E)
be the quotient
\operatorname{Ad}_{p}^{0}(E)\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}
; the natural quotient map
\operatorname{Ad}_{p}^{0}(E)\rightarrow\operatorname{W}_{p}(E)
induces a map on passing to cohomology

H^{1}\left(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}_{p}^{0}(E)\right)\rightarrow H^{1}\left(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},\operatorname{W}_{p}(E)\right).

Let
\ell
be any prime, set

H^{1}_{\bf{f}}\left(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},\operatorname{W}_{p}(E)\right)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{im}\left\{H^{1}_{\bf{f}}\left(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}_{p}^{0}(E)\right)\longrightarrow H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},\operatorname{W}_{p}(E))\right\}.

For
M
denoting either
\operatorname{Ad}_{p}^{0}(E)
or
\operatorname{W}_{p}(E)
, the Bloch-Kato Selmer group associated to
M
is defined as follows

H^{1}_{\bf{f}}(\mathbb{Q}_{S\cup\{p\}}/\mathbb{Q},M)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\ker\left\{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{S\cup\{p\}}/\mathbb{Q},M)\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{\ell}\frac{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},M)}{H^{1}_{\bf{f}}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},M)}\right\}.

For
\ell\notin\{p,\infty\}
, the local cohomology group
H^{1}_{\bf{f}}\left(\mathbb{Q},\operatorname{W}_{p}(E)\right)
coincides with the maximal divisible subgroup of
H^{1}_{\operatorname{nr}}\left(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},\operatorname{W}_{p}(E)\right)
. For any finite set of primes
\Sigma
not containing
p,\infty
, define a larger Selmer group as the kernel of the following map

H^{1}_{\Sigma}(\mathbb{Q},M)\longrightarrow\left(\bigoplus_{\ell\notin\Sigma\cup\{p,\infty\}}\frac{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},M)}{H^{1}_{\operatorname{nr}}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},M)}\right)\oplus\left(\frac{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},M)}{H^{1}_{\bf{f}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},M)}\right).

Note that the inclusion
H^{1}_{\bf{f}}\left(\mathbb{Q},\operatorname{W}_{p}(E)\right)\subseteq H^{1}_{\emptyset}\left(\mathbb{Q},\operatorname{W}_{p}(E)\right)
is an equality if
H^{0}\left(\operatorname{I}_{\ell},\operatorname{W}_{p}(E)\right)
is divisible for all
\ell\neq p
.

4.2.
Vanishing of the dual Selmer group

We now study the relationship between the Bloch-Kato Selmer group and the smoothness of the geometric deformation ring. In order to arrive at such a relationship, we establish a criterion for the vanishing of the dual Selmer group
H^{1}_{\langle p\rangle}(\operatorname{G}_{S\cup\{p\}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}^{*})
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{N}_{p}\subseteq H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{p},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})
be the tangent space of
\mathcal{C}_{p}
. Then, the image of
\mathcal{N}_{p}
under the natural map

H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{p},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})\rightarrow H^{1}\left(\operatorname{G}_{p},\operatorname{W}_{p}(E)\right)[p]

is contained in
H^{1}_{\bf{f}}\left(\operatorname{G}_{p},\operatorname{W}_{p}(E)\right)[p]
.

Proof.

It is well known that elements of
H^{1}_{\bf{f}}\left(\operatorname{G}_{p},\operatorname{W}_{p}(E)\right)
are precisely those classes whose corresponding extensions lie in the image of the Fontaine-Laffaille functor, see the discussion in
[
14
, section 1.1.2 and p.697]
. On the other hand, it follows from part (3) of Proposition
2.9
that all cohomology classes
f\in\mathcal{N}_{p}
have this property. Hence, any class
f\in\mathcal{N}_{p}
must map to
H^{1}_{\bf{f}}\left(\operatorname{G}_{p},\operatorname{W}_{p}(E)\right)
.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}
and
p\geq 5
a prime at which
E
has good reduction. Assume that the following conditions hold:

(1)

the mod-
p
Galois representation
\bar{\rho}_{E,p}
is absolutely irreducible,

(2)

H^{1}_{\emptyset}(\mathbb{Q},\operatorname{W}_{p}(E))=0
.

Then the following assertions hold:

(1)

\dim H^{1}_{\langle p\rangle}(\operatorname{G}_{S\cup\{p\}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})=\sum_{\ell\in S}\dim H^{0}(\operatorname{G}_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}^{*})
,

(2)

H^{1}_{\langle p\rangle}(\operatorname{G}_{S\cup\{p\}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}^{*})=0
.

Proof.

Throughout this proof, we set
\bar{\rho}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bar{\rho}_{E,p}
. Identify
\operatorname{W}_{p}(E)[p]
with
\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}
, and note that since
\bar{\rho}
is assumed to be irreducible, it follows that
H^{0}(\mathbb{Q},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})=0
, and as a result, the natural map

(4.1)

H^{1}(\mathbb{Q},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})\longrightarrow H^{1}(\mathbb{Q},\operatorname{W}_{p}(E))

is injective. Let
\mathcal{T}\subset H^{1}_{\langle p\rangle}(\operatorname{G}_{S\cup\{p\}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})
consist of cohomology classes
f
that are unramified at all primes
\ell\neq p
. In other words,

\mathcal{T}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{ker}\left\{H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{S\cup\{p\}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})\longrightarrow\left(\bigoplus_{\ell\in S}\frac{H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})}{H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{\ell}/\operatorname{I}_{\ell},(\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})^{\operatorname{I}_{\ell}})}\right)\oplus\left(\frac{H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{p},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})}{\mathcal{N}_{p}}\right)\right\}.

We show that the above injection (
4.1
) restricts to a map

\mathcal{T}\longrightarrow H^{1}_{\emptyset}\left(\mathbb{Q},\operatorname{W}_{p}(E)\right).

It suffices to observe that the local conditions defining
\mathcal{T}
as a subspace of
H^{1}(\mathbb{Q},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})
are compatible with those defining
H^{1}_{\emptyset}\left(\mathbb{Q},\operatorname{W}_{p}(E)\right)
. Clearly, this is the case for
\ell\neq p
, and for
\ell=p
, the assertion follows from Lemma
4.1
. As a result, the assumption that
H^{1}_{\emptyset}\left(\mathbb{Q},\operatorname{W}_{p}(E)\right)=0
implies that
\mathcal{T}=0
.
On the other hand, consider the short exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathcal{T}\rightarrow H^{1}_{\langle p\rangle}(\operatorname{G}_{S\cup\{p\}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})\rightarrow\bigoplus_{\ell\in S}\frac{H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})}{H^{1}_{\operatorname{nr}}(\operatorname{G}_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})},

from which we obtain the inequality

\begin{split}\dim H^{1}_{\langle p\rangle}(\operatorname{G}_{S\cup\{p\}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})&\leq\dim\mathcal{T}+\sum_{\ell\in S}\left(\dim H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})-\dim H^{1}_{nr}(\operatorname{G}_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})\right)\\
&\leq\dim\mathcal{T}+\sum_{\ell\in S}\left(\dim H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})-\dim H^{0}(\operatorname{G}_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})\right)\\
&=\dim\mathcal{T}+\sum_{\ell\in S}\dim H^{0}(\operatorname{G}_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}^{*}),\\
&=\sum_{\ell\in S}\dim H^{0}(\operatorname{G}_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}^{*}),\end{split}

where in the last step, we invoke
\dim\mathcal{T}=0
.

Proposition
3.2
asserts that

\dim H^{1}_{\langle p\rangle}(\operatorname{G}_{S\cup\{p\}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})=\dim H^{1}_{\langle p\rangle}(\operatorname{G}_{S\cup\{p\}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}^{*})+\sum_{\ell\in S}\dim H^{0}(\operatorname{G}_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}^{*}).

Therefore,
H^{1}_{\langle p\rangle}(\operatorname{G}_{S\cup\{p\}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}^{*})=0
and

\dim H^{1}_{\langle p\rangle}(\operatorname{G}_{S\cup\{p\}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})=\sum_{\ell\in S}\dim H^{0}(\operatorname{G}_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}^{*}).

∎

4.3.
Congruence primes and vanishing of the Bloch-Kato Selmer group

Next, we discuss conditions for the vanishing of
H^{1}_{\emptyset}(\mathbb{Q},\operatorname{W}_{p}(E))
. Let
N
denote the conductor of
E
, and
f
the Hecke eigencuspform of weight
2
associated with
E
. We introduce the notion of a congruence prime, which will be key in our analysis of the vanishing of the Selmer group
H^{1}_{\emptyset}\left(\mathbb{Q},\operatorname{W}_{p}(E)\right)
.

Definition 4.3
.

We say that
p
is a
congruence prime
for
f
if there exists a
newform
f_{0}
of weight
2
and level
d|N
such that:

(1)

f_{0}
has character lifting the trivial one,

(2)

f_{0}
is not Galois conjugate to
f
,

(3)

\bar{\rho}_{f_{0},\lambda}\simeq\bar{\rho}_{f,\lambda}
for some prime
\lambda|p
of
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Let
\operatorname{Cong}(f)
denote the set of congruence primes. A prime
p
is a
strict
congruence prime if there is a newform satisfying the above conditions with level
N
. Denote by
\operatorname{Cong}_{N}(f)\subseteq\operatorname{Cong}(f)
the subset of strict congruence primes.

The following result gives a relationship between congruence primes and the vanishing of the Selmer group
H^{1}_{\emptyset}\left(\mathbb{Q},\operatorname{W}_{p}(E)\right)
, it is largely based on results in
[
14
]

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
p
be a prime, assume that
that:

(1)

\bar{\rho}_{f,p}
is absolutely irreducible,

(2)

p>2
,

(3)

either
N>1
or
p>3
,

(4)

p\nmid N
,

(5)

\ell\not\equiv 1\mod{p}
for all primes
\ell|N
,

(6)

\bar{\rho}_{f,p}
is ramified at all primes
\ell|N
.

Then
H^{1}_{\emptyset}(\mathbb{Q},\operatorname{W}_{p}(E))\neq 0
if and only if
p\in\operatorname{Cong}_{N}(f)
.

Proof.

This is a special case of
[
35
, Proposition 17]
. ∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Suppose that
\ell\in S
,
p\geq 3
and
\ell\not\equiv\pm 1\mod{p}
. Then,
H^{0}(\operatorname{G}_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}^{*})=0
if and only if
\bar{\rho}
is ramified at
\ell
.

Proof.

We refer the reader to
[
35
, Lemma 11]
. ∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\ell\in S
be a prime such that
\ell\neq p
and
\ell\not\equiv\pm 1\mod{p}
and suppose that
H^{0}(\operatorname{G}_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}^{*})\neq 0
. Then it follows that
p\in\operatorname{Cong}(f)
.

Proof.

This result is a consequence of
[
35
, Lemma 12]
.
∎

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}
with squarefree conductor
N
. Let
\Sigma(E)
be the set of primes
p
such that one of the following conditions is satisfied:

(1)

p\leq 3
,

(2)

\bar{\rho}_{f,p}
is not absolutely irreducible

(3)

p\mid N
,

(4)

there is a prime
\ell|N
, such that
\ell\equiv\pm 1\mod{p}
,

(5)

p\in\operatorname{Cong}(f)
.

Then, for
p\notin\Sigma(E)
, the geometric deformation ring
\mathcal{R}_{E,p}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

Proof.

Let
p\notin\Sigma(E)
and assume by way of contradiction that
\mathcal{R}_{E,p}\not\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. According to Corollary
3.4
, there are two possibilities:

(1)

H^{0}(\operatorname{G}_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}^{*})\neq 0
for some prime
\ell\in S
,

(2)

H^{1}_{\langle p\rangle}(\operatorname{G}_{S\cup\{p\}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}^{*})\neq 0
.

Suppose that the first of the above possibilities does hold. According to Lemma
4.5
, the residual representation
\bar{\rho}
must be unramified at
\ell
. However, according to Lemma
4.6
, it follows that
p\in\operatorname{Cong}(f)
. However, since
p\notin\Sigma(E)
, it follows therefore that
H^{0}(\operatorname{G}_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}^{*})=0
for all primes
\ell\in S
. Therefore, the only possibility is that

H^{1}_{\langle p\rangle}(\operatorname{G}_{S\cup\{p\}},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}^{*})\neq 0.

Note that since
p\notin\Sigma(E)
, the residual representation
\bar{\rho}
is absolutely irreducible. Hence, it follows from Proposition
4.2
that
H^{1}_{\emptyset}(\mathbb{Q},\operatorname{W}_{p}(E))\neq 0
. Since
H^{0}(\operatorname{G}_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}^{*})=0
for all primes
\ell\in S
, it follows from Lemma
4.5
that
\bar{\rho}
is ramified at all primes
\ell\in S
. Proposition
4.4
then implies that
p\in\operatorname{Cong}_{N}(f)
, which is not possible since
p\notin\Sigma(E)
. The contradiction implies that
\mathcal{R}_{E,p}\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{p}
for all primes
p\notin\Sigma(E)
.
∎

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
with squarefree conductor
N
and without complex multiplication. Then, for all but finitely many primes
p
, the Galois deformation ring
\mathcal{R}_{E,p}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

Proof.

It follows from Serre’s open image theorem that the set of primes
p
at which the residual representation
\bar{\rho}_{E,p}
is not absolutely irreducible is finite. With this it is clear that the set of primes
\Sigma(E)
in the statement of Theorem
4.7
is finite and the result follows from the theorem.
∎

5.
Statistics for a fixed prime
p
and varying elliptic curve
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}

In this section, we are able to provide some partial answers to the dual problem. Namely, we fix a prime
p
throughout and let
E
vary over all non-CM elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
. We will need to assume that
p\geq 5
.

5.1.
The congruence number and modular degree

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\mathscr{E}_{p}
be the set of elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
for which the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

E
has good reduction at
p
,

(2)

the conductor
N
of
E
is squarefree,

(3)

the residual representation
\bar{\rho}=\bar{\rho}_{E,p}
is absolutely irreducible,

(4)

\ell\not\equiv\pm 1\mod{p}
for all primes
\ell\mid N
.

Remark 5.2
.

It is shown by J. Cremona and M. Sadek in
[
13
]
that the proportion of elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
ordered by height satisfying (
1
) (resp. (
2
)) is
(1-\frac{1}{p})
(resp.
\zeta(10)/\zeta(2)\approx
60.85%). On the other hand, it is a result of W.Duke that
\bar{\rho}_{E,p}
is irreducible for
100\%
of elliptic curves, see
[
15
]
.

Remark 5.3
.

We observed computationally for elliptic curves with conductor at most 4000 that the conditions (1), (2) and (4) appear to imply (3). We are not immediately aware of any obvious reason for this.

As
E
varies in the set
\mathscr{E}_{p}
, one would like to understand how often is the geometric deformation ring
\mathcal{R}_{E,p}
isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
? Let
f
be the modular form corresponding to
E
. Note that according to Theorem
4.7
, this is the case when
p\notin\operatorname{Cong}(f)
. Let
\mathscr{E}_{p}^{\prime}
be the subset of
\mathscr{E}_{p}
for which this additional condition is satisfied.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, there is a unique minimal Weierstrass equation
y^{2}=x^{3}+ax+b
, where
(a,b)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
is such that
\operatorname{gcd}(a^{3},b^{2})
is not divisible by any twelfth power. The
height of
E
is given by
H(E)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\max\left(\mathinner{\!\left\lvert a\right\rvert}^{3},b^{2}\right)
. Ordering elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
according to height, for any subset
\mathscr{S}\subseteq\mathscr{E}_{p}
and
x>0
, let
\mathscr{S}(x)
consist of all elliptic curves
E\in\mathscr{S}
with height
\leq x
. The lower density of
\mathscr{S}
is given by

\mathfrak{d}(\mathscr{S})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\liminf_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathscr{S}(x)}{\#\mathscr{E}_{p}(x)}.

From a statistical point of view, one would like to characterize
\mathfrak{d}(\mathscr{E}_{p}^{\prime})
, i.e., the lower density for the proportion of curves
E\in\mathscr{E}_{p}
such that
\mathcal{R}_{E,p}\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

We are not able to prove unconditional results, instead, we resort to heuristics and explicit calculations. We recall the notions of
modular degree
and
congruence number
associated to an elliptic curve
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
.

Definition 5.4
.

Let
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
be an elliptic curve of conductor
N
and
\phi_{E}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X_{0}(N)\rightarrow E
the modular homomorphism associated to
E
. The modular degree
m_{E}
is the degree of
\phi_{E}
.

Let
f
be the normalized newform on
\Gamma_{0}(N)
associated to
E
. The primes
\operatorname{Cong}(f)
are exactly those that divide the
congruence number
(cf.
[
1
]
for the definition). Let
r_{E}
the congruence number.

Given
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, set
r^{(p)}
to denote
|r|_{p}^{-1}
, i.e., the
p
-part of
r
. Since
p\nmid N
, it follows from
[
1
, Theorem 2.1]
that
r_{E}^{(p)}=m_{E}^{(p)}
. Note that an elliptic curve
E\in\mathscr{E}_{p}
belongs to
\mathscr{E}_{p}^{\prime}
, precisely when
r_{E}^{(p)}=1
. Therefore, we may formulate the problem in terms of the distribution of the
p
-primary part of the modular degree

\liminf_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathscr{E}_{p}^{\prime}(x)}{\#\mathscr{E}_{p}(x)}=\liminf_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\{E\in\mathscr{E}_{p}(x)\mid m_{E}^{(p)}=1\}}{\#\mathscr{E}_{p}(x)}.

The probablity that
p\mid m_{E}^{(p)}
for odd primes
p
is given by Cohen-Lenstra heuristics, see
[
31
, p.499]
. It follows from such heuristics that the proportion of curves
m_{E}^{(p)}=1
is

\prod_{i}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{i}}\right)=1-\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p^{2}}+\frac{1}{p^{3}}+\dots.

Watkins obtains some evidence for these heuristics in loc. cit. One can only expect that the stipulation that
m_{E}^{(p)}=1
be largely independent from the other conditions (
1
)-(
4
).

In the rest of this paper, we show through explicit calculation that one may expect that
m_{E}^{(p)}=1
holds for a large proportion of curves, especially as
p\rightarrow\infty
. This leads us to expect the following,

(1)

for any odd prime
p
,

\liminf_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathscr{E}_{p}^{\prime}(x)}{\#\mathscr{E}_{p}(x)}>0.

(2)

As
p\rightarrow\infty
,

\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\left(\liminf_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathscr{E}_{p}^{\prime}(x)}{\#\mathscr{E}_{p}(x)}\right)\rightarrow 1.

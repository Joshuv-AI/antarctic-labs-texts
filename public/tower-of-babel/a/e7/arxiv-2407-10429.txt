Well-Posedness and Finite Element Approximation for the Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert Equation with Spin-Torques
Noah Vinod and Thanh Tran

Abstract
. Spin currents act on ferromagnets by exerting a torque on the magnetisation. This torque is modelled by appending additional terms to the Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert equation motivating the study of the non-homogeneous Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert equation. We first prove the existence and uniqueness of high regularity local solutions to this equation using the Faedo-Galerkin method. Then we construct a numerical method for the problem and prove that it converges to a global weak solution of the PDE. Numerical simulations of the problem are also included.

Introduction

In this paper, we develop local-in-time existence and uniqueness theorems as well as a global-in-time numerical method for the non-homogeneous Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert initial boundary value problem

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}{\partial t}=\alpha\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}-\beta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times\frac{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}{\partial t}+\mbox{\boldmath$f$}\left(\mbox{\boldmath$m$},\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\right)\quad

\displaystyle\text{on }(0,T)\times\Omega,

(1.1b)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}=\mbox{\boldmath$0$}

\displaystyle\text{on }(0,T)\times\partial\Omega,

(1.1c)

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$m$}(0,\cdot)=\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{0}

\displaystyle\text{on }\Omega,

(1.1d)

\displaystyle|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}|=1

In recent times, physicists have been conducting experiments to examine the effects that a current has on ferromagnets. In particular, an electric or spin current acting on a ferromagnet exerts a torque on the magnetisation
[
1

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\mbox{\boldmath$u$},\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\in B_{R}(\mbox{\boldmath$0$})

We find a Galerkin solution to (

Similarly for the second term,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left\||\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}|^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$m$}-|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}|^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\;{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}-\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}|^{2}\left(|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}|^{2}+|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}|^{2}\right)\;{\rm d}\mbox{\boldmath$x$}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}-\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}(\Omega)}^{2}\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}(\Omega)}^{2}\;{\rm d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}-\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}(\Omega)}^{2}\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}(\Omega)}^{2}\;{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}-\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}(\Omega)}^{2}\;{\rm d}s,

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}-\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\;{\rm d}s\to 0.

The first result now follows.

As the method remains the same with slight modifications, we omit the some of the more obvious steps for the next two results. For the second result,

\displaystyle\Bigg|\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\times\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k},\bm{\psi}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\;{\rm d}s

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$},\bm{\psi}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\;{\rm d}s\Bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\times\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}-\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\;{\rm d}s\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}-\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\;{\rm d}s\right)^{1/2}.

It is easy to see that the these two integral tend to zero as

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\times\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}-\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\;{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}-\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}\left\|\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\;{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}-\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\;{\rm d}s\to 0,

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}-\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\;{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}\left\|\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}-\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\;{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}-\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\;{\rm d}s\to 0,

proving the second result.

For the non-homogeneous term, we first have that

\displaystyle\Bigg|\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\mbox{\boldmath$F$}(\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k},\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}),\bm{\psi}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\;{\rm d}s

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\mbox{\boldmath$F$}(\mbox{\boldmath$m$},\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}),\bm{\psi}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\;{\rm d}s\Bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$F$}(\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k},\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k})-\mbox{\boldmath$F$}(\mbox{\boldmath$m$},\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$})\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\;{\rm d}s\right)^{1/2}.

But since
\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}
and
m
belong to a bounded set in
\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)
, applying Lemma
2.2
gives

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$F$}(\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k},\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k})-\mbox{\boldmath$F$}(\mbox{\boldmath$m$},\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$})\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\;{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}-\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\;{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}-\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\;{\rm d}s\to 0,

giving us the third result.
∎

Consequently,
m
satisfies the weak formulation in Definition
2.4
. Next, we note that the solution
m
satisfies the Neumann boundary condition. Indeed, by using the Trace Theorem, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\frac{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\partial\Omega)}^{2}\;{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\frac{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}-\frac{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}}{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\partial\Omega)}^{2}\;{\rm d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\frac{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}}{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\partial\Omega)}^{2}\;{\rm d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\frac{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}-\frac{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}}{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\partial\Omega)}^{2}\;{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim\;\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}-\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\;{\rm d}s\to 0.

Finally, by following the proof in
[
13
]
, using the orthogonality property of
F
i.e., Assumption (A2), we conclude that
m
has magnitude
|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}|=1
. Moreover, because
\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}(0,\cdot)
is the projection of
\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{0}
onto
V_{k}
, we have
\mbox{\boldmath$m$}(0,\cdot)=\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{0}
. Therefore
m
is a solution in the sense of Definition
2.4
, completing the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

3.4.
Proof of Theorem
3.2

Suppose
\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{1}
and
\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{2}
are two solutions obtained from Theorem
3.1
with initial data
\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{0,1}
and
\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{0,2}
, respectively. Let
T^{\prime}=\min(T_{1}^{*},T_{2}^{*})
and
\mbox{\boldmath$v$}=\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{1}-\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{2}
. Then
v
solves the following PDE almost everywhere on
(0,T^{\prime})\times\Omega
:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}-\beta^{\prime}\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$v$}

\displaystyle=\beta^{\prime}|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{1}|^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}+\beta^{\prime}(|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{1}|^{2}-|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{2}|^{2})\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{2}+\alpha^{\prime}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\times\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{1}+\alpha^{\prime}\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{2}\times\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$v$}

\displaystyle\qquad+\mbox{\boldmath$F$}(\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{1},\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{2})-\mbox{\boldmath$F$}(\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{2},\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{2}).

Taking the dot product with
v
, we obtain

(3.8)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\rm{d}}{{\rm d}t}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\beta^{\prime}\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\alpha^{\prime}K_{1}+\beta^{\prime}K_{2}+\beta^{\prime}K_{3}+K_{4},

where

\displaystyle K_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{2}\times\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$v$})\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\;{\rm d}\mbox{\boldmath$x$},

\displaystyle K_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{1}|^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\;{\rm d}\mbox{\boldmath$x$},

\displaystyle K_{3}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{1}|^{2}-|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{2}|^{2})\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{2}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\;{\rm d}\mbox{\boldmath$x$},

\displaystyle K_{4}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(\mbox{\boldmath$F$}(\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{1},\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{1})-\mbox{\boldmath$F$}(\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{2},\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{2}))\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\;{\rm d}\mbox{\boldmath$x$}.

We now bound each of these integrals. We obtain by using integration by parts, Holder’s inequality, and Sobolev embedding

|K_{1}|\lesssim\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{2}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{2}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{3}(\Omega)}\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

The bound for
K_{2}
is straightforward and is just

|K_{2}|\leq\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{1}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\lesssim\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{1}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{3}(\Omega)}^{2}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

For
K_{3}
, by using
|\mbox{\boldmath$a$}|^{2}-|\mbox{\boldmath$b$}|^{2}=(|\mbox{\boldmath$a$}|-|\mbox{\boldmath$b$}|)(|\mbox{\boldmath$a$}|+|\mbox{\boldmath$b$}|)
we have

\displaystyle|K_{3}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{1}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{2}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right)\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{1}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{3}(\Omega)}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{2}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{3}(\Omega)}\right)\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}.

Finally, by using Lemma
2.2
we have

\displaystyle|K_{4}|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$F$}(\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{1},\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{1})-\mbox{\boldmath$F$}(\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{2},\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{2})\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{1}-\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{2}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}.

Therefore, it follows from (
3.8
) that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\rm{d}}{{\rm d}t}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\beta^{\prime}\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{2}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{3}(\Omega)}\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{1}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{3}(\Omega)}^{2}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{1}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{3}(\Omega)}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{2}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{3}(\Omega)}\right)\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}.

Using Young’s inequality, we can absorb the term involving
\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}
to the corresponding term on the left hand side and obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\rm{d}}{{\rm d}t}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{1}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{3}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{2}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{3}(\Omega)}^{2}+1\right)\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

As
\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{2}\in L^{2}(0,T^{\prime};\mathbb{H}^{3}(\Omega))
, the conclusion follows from the Gronwall Lemma.

Numerical Method

It follows from (
2.4
), that for all
\bm{\psi}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega_{T})

(4.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\beta\left\langle\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$m$},\bm{\psi}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega_{T})}-\alpha\left\langle\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$m$},\bm{\psi}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega_{T})}&=\alpha\left\langle|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}|^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$m$},\bm{\psi}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega_{T})}-\alpha\left\langle\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$},\nabla\bm{\psi}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\\

We will use the finite element method to approximate the solution
m
of this equatiaon. We define the finite element space
\mathbb{V}_{h}\subset\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)
to be the space of all continuous piecewise linear functions on a triangulation of

be the hat functions satisfying

for all

. Denote
\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{(j)}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
to be approximation of
\mbox{\boldmath$m$}(t_{j},\cdot)

\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{(j)}

\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\cdot\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$m$}=0

\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{(j)}

Using the formulation (

[

]
to get the following.

Choose
\theta\in(\frac{1}{2},1]
and a time-step size

with

.

Step 1

Step 2

\displaystyle\begin{split}\beta\left\langle\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{(j)},\bm{\psi}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}-\left\langle\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{(j)}\times\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{(j)},\bm{\psi}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}&=-\alpha\left\langle\nabla\left(\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{(j)}+\theta k\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{(j)}\right),\nabla\bm{\psi}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\\

Step 3
: Define

Step 4
: Set
j=j+1
, and return to Step 2 if
j<J
. Stop if
j=J
.

Lemma 4.4
.

\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{(j)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}k\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{(j)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+k^{2}(2\theta-1)\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{(j)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\lesssim 1.

\displaystyle=\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{(j)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+k^{2}\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{(j)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-\frac{2\beta k}{\alpha}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{(j)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-2\theta k^{2}\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{(j)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{2\beta k}{\alpha}\left\langle\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{(j)}\times\mbox{\boldmath$f$}\left(\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{(j)},\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{(j)}\right),\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{(j)}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{(j)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+k^{2}(1-2\theta)\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{(j)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-\frac{2k}{\alpha}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{(j)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\frac{k}{\alpha\beta}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$f$}\left(\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{(j)},\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{(j)}\right)\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{\beta k}{\alpha}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{(j)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Summing over the time periods
0,\ldots,j
and using Lemma
4.2
and
4.3
we obtain

(4.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{(j)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}k\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{(j)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}&+k^{2}(2\theta-1)\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{(j)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\\
&\lesssim\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{(0)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+k\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$f$}\left(\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{(i)},\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{(i)}\right)\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.\\
&\lesssim 1+Ck\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{(i)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.\end{split}

By the discrete Gronwall inequality
[
31
]

\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{(j)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\lesssim(1+Ck)^{i},

and so by summing over
i=0,\ldots,j-1
and using
1+x\leq e^{x}
we have

k\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{(i)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\lesssim k\frac{(1+Ck)^{j}-1}{Ck}\lesssim e^{CkJ}\lesssim 1.

Combining this with (
4.3
) gives us the required result.
∎

Lemma
4.6
, the Banach-Alaoglu theorem and compactness arguments imply the existence of a sub-sequences

by the locally Lipschitz property of
\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{2}
and weak convergence. The same can be shown for the third component as well. Hence as
(h,k)\to(0,0)
, we conclude that
m
satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{split}\beta\left\langle\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$m$},\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times\bm{\varphi}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega_{T})}-\left\langle\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$m$},\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times\bm{\varphi}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega_{T})}&+\alpha\left\langle\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$},\nabla(\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times\bm{\varphi})\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\\
&+\left\langle\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times\mbox{\boldmath$f$}(\mbox{\boldmath$m$},\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}),\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times\bm{\varphi}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega_{T})}=0,\end{split}

for all
\bm{\varphi}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega_{T})
.

Furthermore,
|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}|=1
by (
4.7
) and the initial data is attained by
m
because of the limiting properties of the interpolation operator. This completes the proof.

5.
Application and Numerical Experiments

According to Garello et al.
[
17
]
, “Memory and logic spintronic devices rely on the generation of spin torques to control the magnetisation of nanoscale elements using electric currents”. Accounting for these spin-torque effects requires appending a spin-torque term
T
to the Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert equation

\frac{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}{\partial t}=\alpha\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}-\beta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times\frac{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}{\partial t}+\mbox{\boldmath$T$},

in other words,
\mbox{\boldmath$f$}=\mbox{\boldmath$T$}
here.

5.1.
Example 1

The spin-transfer torque (STT) method produces spin torques by using a current to transfer spin angular momentum using a ‘polariser-ferromagnetic layer’ and is represented by
\mbox{\boldmath$T$}=\mbox{\boldmath$T$}_{\rm STT}

[
24
,
27
,
28
]
, where

\mbox{\boldmath$T$}_{\rm STT}\left(\mbox{\boldmath$m$},\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\right)=\lambda\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times(\mbox{\boldmath$j$}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$})+\mu\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times\left(\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times(\mbox{\boldmath$j$}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$})\right),

and
j
is a constant unit vector denoting the direction of the current. Choosing
\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{1}=\mbox{\boldmath$0$}
but
\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{2}(\mbox{\boldmath$a$})=\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{3}(\mbox{\boldmath$a$})=\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{4}(\mbox{\boldmath$a$})=\mbox{\boldmath$a$}
and
\mbox{\boldmath$g$}_{1}(\mbox{\boldmath$B$})=\mbox{\boldmath$g$}_{2}(\mbox{\boldmath$B$})=\mbox{\boldmath$j$}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$B$}
, while ignoring the constants for simplicity, it is obvious that these terms satisfy Assumptions (A1) and (A2).

5.2.
Example 2

More recent experiments have shown such a ‘polariser-ferromagnetic layer’ is not necessarily required and spin-torques can be induced by exploiting other physical effects. These torques are called ‘spin-orbit torques’ (SOT) and are represented by
\mbox{\boldmath$T$}=\mbox{\boldmath$T$}_{\rm SOT}

[
17
]
, where

\mbox{\boldmath$T$}_{\rm SOT}(\mbox{\boldmath$m$})=\mbox{\boldmath$T$}^{\perp}(\mbox{\boldmath$m$})+\mbox{\boldmath$T$}^{\parallel}(\mbox{\boldmath$m$}).

Here we have

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$T$}^{\parallel}(\mbox{\boldmath$m$})

\displaystyle=\left[c_{1}+c_{2}\left|\mbox{\boldmath$\hat{k}$}\times\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\right|^{2}+c_{3}\left|\mbox{\boldmath$\hat{k}$}\times\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\right|^{4}\right]\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\hat{\jmath}$}\times\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\right)+\left[c_{4}+c_{5}\left|\mbox{\boldmath$\hat{k}$}\times\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\right|^{2}\right]\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\hat{k}$}\times\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\right)\left(\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\hat{\imath}$}\right)

and

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$T$}^{\perp}(\mbox{\boldmath$m$})

\displaystyle=c_{6}\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\hat{\jmath}$}\times\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\right)+\left[c_{7}+c_{8}\left|\mbox{\boldmath$\hat{k}$}\times\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\right|^{2}\right]\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\hat{k}$}\times\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\right)(\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\hat{\imath}$}),

where
c_{1},\ldots,c_{8}
are experimentally determined physical constants and
\mbox{\boldmath$\hat{\imath}$},\mbox{\boldmath$\hat{\jmath}$},\mbox{\boldmath$\hat{k}$}
are the standard basis vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Choosing
\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{1}(\mbox{\boldmath$a$})=\mbox{\boldmath$T$}(\mbox{\boldmath$a$})
and
\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{2},\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{3},\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{4},\mbox{\boldmath$g$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$g$}_{2}=\mbox{\boldmath$0$}
, while ignoring the constants for simplicity, it is clear that this function satisfies Assumption (A1) and the orthogonality property of (A2). We now show that it satisfies the growth conditions of Assumption (A2). First, we obtain

\displaystyle|\mbox{\boldmath$T$}^{\perp}(\mbox{\boldmath$a$})|\lesssim|\mbox{\boldmath$a$}|^{2}+(1+|\mbox{\boldmath$a$}|^{2})|\mbox{\boldmath$a$}|^{2}\lesssim 1+|\mbox{\boldmath$a$}|^{4},

by Young’s inequality. Moreover, taking the gradient, we have

\displaystyle|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$T$}^{\perp}(\mbox{\boldmath$a$})|

\displaystyle=\Big|c_{6}\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$a$}\times\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\hat{\jmath}$}\times\mbox{\boldmath$a$}\right)+c_{6}\mbox{\boldmath$a$}\times\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\hat{\jmath}$}\times\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$a$}\right)+\left[c_{7}+2c_{8}(\mbox{\boldmath$\hat{k}$}\times\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$a$})\cdot(\mbox{\boldmath$\hat{k}$}\times\mbox{\boldmath$a$})\right]\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\hat{k}$}\times\mbox{\boldmath$a$}\right)(\mbox{\boldmath$a$}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\hat{\imath}$})

\displaystyle\qquad+\left[c_{7}+c_{8}\left|\mbox{\boldmath$\hat{k}$}\times\mbox{\boldmath$a$}\right|^{2}\right]\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\hat{k}$}\times\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$a$}\right)\left(\mbox{\boldmath$a$}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\hat{\imath}$}\right)+\left[c_{7}+c_{8}\left|\mbox{\boldmath$\hat{k}$}\times\mbox{\boldmath$a$}\right|^{2}\right]\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\hat{k}$}\times\mbox{\boldmath$a$}\right)\left(\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$a$}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\hat{\imath}$}\right)\Big|.

As
\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$a$}=I
, we also obtain

\displaystyle|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$T$}^{\perp}(\mbox{\boldmath$a$})|

\displaystyle\lesssim 1+|\mbox{\boldmath$a$}|+(1+|\mbox{\boldmath$a$}|)|\mbox{\boldmath$a$}|^{2}+(1+|\mbox{\boldmath$a$}|^{2})|\mbox{\boldmath$a$}|+(1+|\mbox{\boldmath$a$}|^{2})|\mbox{\boldmath$a$}|\lesssim 1+|\mbox{\boldmath$a$}|^{3}\lesssim 1+|\mbox{\boldmath$a$}|^{4}.

In the same way, it can be shown that the parallel term satisfies

|\mbox{\boldmath$T$}^{\parallel}(\mbox{\boldmath$a$})|+|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$T$}^{\parallel}(\mbox{\boldmath$a$})|\lesssim 1+|\mbox{\boldmath$a$}|^{5}.

Useful Results

Theorem A.1

(Aubin’s Lemma
[
5
,
29
]
)
.

Let
X
,
Y
and
B
be Banach spaces such that
X\subset B\subset Y
, where the injection
X\subset B
is compact and the injection
B\subset Y
is continuous. Assume that
\{u_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is a bounded sequence in
L^{p}(0,T;X)
such that
\{\partial_{t}u_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is bounded in
L^{r}(0,T;Y)
where
1\leq p<\infty
and
r=1
, or
p=\infty
and
r>1
. Then there exists a subsequence
\{u_{k_{j}}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
which strongly converges in
L^{p}(0,T;B)
.

Theorem A.2

(Generalised Gronwall Lemma
[
11
]
)
.

Assume
u:[a,b]\to[0,\infty)
and
\beta:[a,b]\to[0,\infty)
along with non-decreasing
g:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
satisfy

u(t)\leq\alpha+\int_{a}^{t}\beta(s)g(u(s))\;{\rm d}s\quad\forall t\in[a,b],

where
\alpha
is a positive constant and
[a,b]\subset[0,\infty)
. Then

u(t)\leq G^{-1}\left(\int_{a}^{t}\beta(s)\;{\rm d}s\right),\quad t\in\mathcal{R},

where
G^{-1}
is the inverse function of

G(\sigma):=\int_{\alpha}^{\sigma}\frac{1}{g(s)}\;{\rm d}s,\quad\sigma\geq 0,

and

\mathcal{R}=\left\{t\in[a,b]:\int_{a}^{t}\beta(s)\;{\rm d}s\in G([0,\infty))\right\}.

Theorem A.3

(
[
16
]
)
.

Assume that
\Omega
is open, bounded, and
\partial\Omega
is smooth. Take
m
to be a non-negative integer. Suppose that
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{m+2}(\Omega))
, with
\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{m}(\Omega))
. Then

\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\in C([0,T];\mathbb{H}^{m+1}(\Omega)).

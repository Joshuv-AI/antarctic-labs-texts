Gravitational wave constraints on non-birefringent dispersions of gravitational waves due to Lorentz violations with GWTC-3
Cheng Gong a,b,c, Tao Zhu b,c, Rui Niu d,e, Qiang Wu b,c, Jing-Lei Cui a, Xin Zhang a, Wen Zhao d,e, Anzhong Wang f

Abstract
The standard model extension (SME) is an effective field theory framework that can be used to study the possible violations of Lorentz symmetry in the gravitational interaction. In the SME’s gauge invariant linearized gravity sector, the dispersion relation of GWs is modified, resulting in anisotropy, birefringence, and dispersion effects in the propagation of GWs. In this paper, we mainly focus on the non-birefringent and anisotropic dispersion relation in the propagation of GWs due to the violation of Lorentz symmetry. With the modified dispersion relation, we calculate the corresponding modified waveform of GWs generated by the coalescence of compact binaries. We consider the effects from the operators with the lowest mass dimension d = 6 d=6 in the gauge invariant linearized gravity sector of the SME which are expected to have the dominant Lorentz-violating effect on the propagation of GWs. For this case, the Lorentz-violating effects are presented by 25 coefficients and we constrain them independently by the “maximal-reach” approach. We use 90 high-confidence GW events in the GWTC-3 catalog and use Bilby , an open source software, and Dynest , a nested sampling package, to perform parameter estimation with the modified waveform. We do not find any evidence of Lorentz violation in the GWs data and give a 90 % 90\% confidence interval for each Lorentz violating coefficient.

II
Gravitational waves in the linear gravity of SME

In this section, we present a brief introduction to the GWs in the linearized gravity sector of the SME and the associated modified dispersion relation of GWs due to the effects of the LIV. The quadratic Lagrangian density for GWs in the gauge invariant linearized gravity sector of the SME is given by
Kostelecky:2016kfm

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\epsilon^{\mu\rho\alpha\kappa}\epsilon^{\nu\sigma\beta\lambda}\eta_{\kappa\lambda}h_{\mu\nu}\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}h_{\rho\sigma}

(2.1)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}h_{\mu\nu}(\hat{s}^{\mu\rho\nu\sigma}+\hat{q}^{\mu\rho\nu\sigma}+\hat{k}^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma})h_{\rho\sigma},

where one expands the metric
g_{\mu\nu}
of the spacetime in the form of
g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}+h_{\mu\nu}
with
\eta_{\mu\nu}
being the constant Minkowski metric,
\epsilon^{\mu\rho\alpha\kappa}
is the Levi-Civita tensor, and the operators
\hat{s}^{\mu\rho\nu\sigma}
,
\hat{q}^{\mu\rho\nu\sigma}
, and
\hat{k}^{\mu\rho\nu\sigma}
represent the three different classes of modifications due to LIV. These operators can be further expanded in terms of derivatives in the following forms

\displaystyle{\hat{s}}^{\mu\rho\nu\sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum{s}^{(d)\mu\rho\alpha_{1}\nu\sigma\alpha_{2}\ldots\alpha_{d-2}}\partial_{\alpha_{1}}\ldots\partial_{\alpha_{d-2}},

(2.2)

\displaystyle{\hat{q}}^{\mu\rho\nu\sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum q^{(d)\mu\rho\alpha_{1}\nu\alpha_{2}\sigma\alpha_{3}\ldots\alpha_{d-2}}\partial_{\alpha_{1}}\ldots\partial_{\alpha_{d-2}},

(2.3)

\displaystyle{\hat{k}}^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum k^{(d)\mu\alpha_{1}\nu\alpha_{2}\rho\alpha_{3}\sigma\alpha_{4}\ldots\alpha_{d-2}}\partial_{\alpha_{1}}\ldots\partial_{\alpha_{d-2}},

(2.4)

where
d
denotes the mass dimension of the three operators and the tensor coefficients in the above expansions control the LIV. The sum in the above expansion is over even
d\geq 4
for
s
-type violations, odd
d\geq 5
for
q
-type, and even
d\geq 6
for
k
-type.
Specifically,
\hat{s}^{\mu\rho\nu\sigma}
is anti-symmetric in both “
\mu\rho
” and “
\nu\sigma
”,
\hat{q}^{\mu\rho\nu\sigma}
is anti-symmetric in “
\mu\rho
” and symmetric in “
\nu\sigma
”, and
\hat{k}^{\mu\rho\nu\sigma}
is totally symmetric. Then the equations of motion for GWs can be derived by varying the quadratic action
S\sim\int d^{4}x\mathcal{L}
with respect to

with Lagrangian density
\mathcal{L}
given by (
2.1
), which yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\rho\sigma}\epsilon^{\mu\rho\alpha\kappa}\epsilon^{\nu\sigma\beta\lambda}\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}h_{\kappa\lambda}-\delta M^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}h_{\rho\sigma}=0,

(2.5)

where the tensor operators

\displaystyle\delta M^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\left(\hat{s}^{\mu\rho\nu\sigma}+\hat{s}^{\mu\sigma\nu\rho}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\hat{k}^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{8}\left(\hat{q}^{\mu\rho\nu\sigma}+\hat{q}^{\nu\rho\mu\sigma}+\hat{q}^{\mu\sigma\nu\rho}+\hat{q}^{\nu\sigma\mu\rho}\right).

In GR, the metric perturbation
h_{\mu\nu}
only contains two degenerate traceless and transverse tensor modes. However, when the Lorentz-violating modifications are included, depending on specific types of the LIV,
h_{\mu\nu}
may contain two additional scalar and two vector perturbation modes. In this paper, assuming these extra modes are small and following the similar treatment in
Kostelecky:2016kfm
, we only focus on the LIV effects on the two traceless and transverse tensor modes. For this purpose, we restrict to the modes
h_{ij}
which satisfy

\displaystyle\eta^{ij}h_{ij}=0=\partial^{i}h_{ij}.

(2.7)

Then the equations of motion for GWs (
2.5
) reduce to

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}^{2}-\nabla^{2})h^{ij}+2\delta M^{ijmn}h_{mn}=0.

(2.8)

In the linearized gravity sector of SME, it is convenient to decompose the GWs into circular polarization modes. To study the evolution of

, we expand it over spatial Fourier harmonics,

\displaystyle h_{ij}(\tau,x^{i})=\sum_{A={\rm R,L}}\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}h_{\rm A}(\tau,k^{i})e^{ik_{i}x^{i}}e_{ij}^{\rm A}(k^{i}),

where

denote the circular polarization tensors and satisfy the relation
\epsilon^{ijk}n_{j}e_{kl}^{A}=i\rho_{A}e^{iA}_{~l}

\rho_{\rm R}=1
and
\rho_{\rm L}=-1
. So, the equations of motion in Eq.(
2.8

\displaystyle\ddot{h}_{A}+k^{2}h_{A}+2\epsilon^{A}_{ij}\delta M^{ijmn}e^{B}_{mn}h_{B}=0,

(2.10)

or equivalently in the matrix form

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\partial_{t}^{2}+k^{2}+2e_{ij}^{R}\delta M^{ijmn}e^{R}_{mn}&2e_{ij}^{R}\delta M^{ijmn}e^{L}_{mn}\\
2e_{ij}^{L}\delta M^{ijmn}e^{R}_{mn}&\partial_{t}^{2}+k^{2}+2e_{ij}^{L}\delta M^{ijmn}e^{L}_{mn}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}h_{R}\\
h_{L}\end{array}\right)=0.

Then, following methods developed for the study of Lorentz violation in the photon sector of the SME, the modified dispersion relation of GWs with 4-momentum

can be derived by requiring the determinant of the above

matrix vanishes, which yields (see also in

\displaystyle\omega=\Big(1-\zeta^{0}\pm|{\bf\zeta}|\Big)|{\bf k}|,

(2.16)

where

\displaystyle\zeta^{0}=-\frac{1}{2|{\bf k}|^{2}}\Big(e_{ij}^{R}\delta M^{ijmn}e^{R}_{mn}+e_{ij}^{L}\delta M^{ijmn}e^{L}_{mn}\Big),

and

\displaystyle|{\bf\zeta}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4|{\bf k}|^{4}}\Big[(e_{ij}^{R}\delta M^{ijmn}e^{R}_{mn}-e_{ij}^{L}\delta M^{ijmn}e^{L}_{mn})^{2}

(2.18)

\displaystyle+4(e_{ij}^{R}\delta M^{ijmn}e^{L}_{mn})(e_{kl}^{L}\delta M^{klpq}e^{R}_{pq})\Big].

The modified dispersion relation in the above leads to the phase velocities of the GWs

\displaystyle v_{\pm}=1-\zeta^{0}\pm|{\bf\zeta}|.

(2.19)

The new effects in the modified dispersion arising from LIV are induced by the coefficients

and

;

;
Wang:2021ctl
;
Niu:2022yhr
;

\zeta^{0}

In the modified dispersion relation (
2.16

\zeta^{0}
which leads to the non-birefringent dispersions is functions of the frequency
\omega

{\bf k}

\displaystyle\zeta^{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{d,jm}\omega^{d-4}Y_{jm}({\bf\hat{n}})k_{(I)jm}^{(d)},

(2.20)

where
{\bf n}=-{\bf k}
is the direction of the source, and

,
2.3

d
and introduce several energy-independent coefficients as

\displaystyle\zeta^{0}_{(d)}({\bf n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{jm}Y_{jm}({\bf\hat{n}})k_{(I)jm}^{(d)}.

(2.21)

Then the phase velocity of the GWs can be rewritten as

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\omega^{d-4}\zeta^{0}_{(d)}({\bf n}).

(2.22)

The non-birefringent LIV effects are fully characterized by the coefficients,

. These coefficients determine the speeds of GWs and lead to frequency-dependent dispersions except in the case with

It is interesting to note that all these coefficients can provide frequency and direction-dependent phase modifications to the GWs. In the following, we are going to study the phase modifications due to these LIV coefficients in detail.

IV
Bayesian inference and parameter estimation

In this section, we describe the Bayesian inference by using observational data from LVK to constrain the coefficients describing LIV in the SME framework. Up to now, the GWTC-3 catalog contains 90 compact binary coalescence events
LIGOScientific:2021djp
, including binary neutron stars (BNS) GW170817 and GW190425, neutron star–black hole binaries (NSBH), and binary black holes (BBH). Bayesian inference is an important part of modern astronomy. When we have GW data

\displaystyle P({\vec{\theta}}|d,H)=\frac{P(d|\vec{\theta},H)P(\vec{\theta}|H)}{P(d|H)},

(4.1)

where

denotes the posterior probability distributions of physical parameters

which denotes the model parameters.
H
denotes the waveform model,

denotes the prior distribution when given the model parameters
\vec{\theta}
, the denominator
P(d|\vec{\theta},H)
denotes the likelihood given a specific set of model parameters and

is normalization factor called the “evidence”,

\displaystyle P(d|H)\equiv\int d\vec{\theta}P(d|\vec{\theta},H)P(\vec{\theta}|H).

(4.2)

In most cases, the GW signal is very weak and the matched filtering method can be used to extract these signals from the noises. Here, we assume that the noise is Gaussian and stationary

;
Romano:2016dpx
;

\displaystyle P(\boldsymbol{d}|{\boldsymbol{\theta}},H)\propto\prod_{i=1}^{n}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\langle\boldsymbol{d_{i}}-\boldsymbol{h({\theta})}|\boldsymbol{d_{i}}-\boldsymbol{h({\theta})}\rangle},

(4.3)

where

is the GW strain given by the waveform model
H
and
i
represents different GW detectors. The noise weighted inner product

\displaystyle\langle A|B\rangle=4\;{\rm Re}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{A(f)B(f)^{*}}{S(f)}df\right],

(4.4)

where

denotes complex conjugation and

is the power spectral density (PSD) function of the detector. We use the PSD data encapsulated in LVK posterior sample which could lead to a more stable and reliable parameter estimation compared with obtaining the PSD from strain data by Welch averaging

;

VI
conclusion

Since the detection of GW signals by LIGO/Virgo Collaboration, the tests of gravity in the strong field regime with GWs have become possible. With the increase in detector number and sensitivity, LVK catalog GWTC-3 now contains 90 GW events. In this paper, we consider the gauge-invariant linearized gravity sector of SME to investigate the Lorentz-violating effects in the propagation of GWs and constrain them with the detected GW data. The Lagrangian in the SME framework contains all possible gauge-invariant quadratic terms of the metric perturbation
h_{\mu\nu}
, which represent the LIV modification. According to a similar approach developed in the discussion of Lorentz symmetry in the photon sector of SME
Kostelecky:2009zp
, a modified dispersion relation of GWs can be obtained from the Lagrangian, in which the LIV effects without birefringence is completely characterized by the coefficient
k_{(I)jm}^{(d)}
. When
j\neq 0
,
k_{(I)jm}^{(d)}
is direction-dependent, which can lead to anisotropic effects in the propagation of GWs. We derive the modified waveform of GWs with the LIV effects and performed Bayesian inference on the GWs data to constrain these effects.

By comparing the modified waveform (
3.6
) with the strain data from the GWTC-3 catalog, we derive the constraints on the effects of the anisotropic non-birefringent dispersions of GWs due to the LIV in the gauge invariant linearized gravity sector of the SME. As we mentioned, we expect the operators with the lowest mass dimension in the SME to have a major LIV effect on the propagation of GWs. Therefore, we are more interested in the lowest dimensions, for example, for
k_{(I)jm}^{(d)}
, one has
d=4
and
d=6
. However, when
d=4
, only frequency-independent effects are produced in the modified dispersion relation, so it cannot give any observable out-of-phase effects. But in
LIGOScientific:2017zic
, combined with the detection of the electromagnetic counterpart, the case of
d=4
is discussed. In this paper, we only consider the case of
d=6
. Here we use the “maximum-reach” method to constrain each component of
k_{(I)jm}^{(d)}
separately.

Results represented in Fig.
1
show that there is no evidence of any violation of Lorentz symmetry. Therefore, we give the constraints of the coefficients
k_{(I)jm}^{(6)}
describing anisotropic non-birefringent effects. Most of the
k_{(I)jm}^{(6)}
components have a
90\%
confidence interval of roughly
10^{-10}
, with some
k_{(I)jm}^{(6)}
components having a
90\%
confidence interval of between
10^{-8}
and
10^{-7}
, but the medians for all of these components is around zero. Constrains on each component of
k_{(I)jm}^{(6)}
are summarized in table
1
. Since the next generation of GW detectors can detect lighter and more distant BBH and BNS events, it is expected such systems can lead to tighter constraints on non-birefringent dispersions in the future.

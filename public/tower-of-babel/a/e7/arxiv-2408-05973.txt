The Baire and perfect set properties at singulars cardinals Thanks: This work is part of Thei’s Ph.D. dissertation under Dimonte at Udine University. The abstract framework and the main results of the paper were obtained by Poveda and Thei during a visit of the latter to Harvard University in the Spring of 2024.
Vincenzo Dimonte, Alejandro Poveda, Sebastiano Thei

Abstract
. We construct a model of ZFC with a singular cardinal κ \kappa such that every subset of κ \kappa in L ⁡ ( V κ + 1 ) L(V_{\kappa+1}) has both the κ \kappa -Perfect Set Property and the 𝒰 → \mathcal{\vec{U}} -Baire Property. This is a higher analogue of Solovay’s result for L ⁡ ( ℝ ) L(\mathbb{R}) [ Sol70 ] . We obtain this configuration starting with large-cardinal assumptions in the realm of supercompactness, thus improving former theorems by Cramer [ Cra15 ] , Shi [ Shi15 ] and Woodin [ Woo11 ] .

03E35, 03E55

1.
Introduction

The
Perfect Set Property
(
PSP
) and the
Baire Property
(
BP
) are cornerstone concepts in the study of the real line. Recall that a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}
has the
PSP
if and only if
A
is either countable or contains a non-empty
perfect set
; namely, a closed set without isolated points in the topology of
\mathbb{R}
. Likewise,
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}
has the
BP
if there is an open set
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}
such that
A\triangle U
is meager.

The
PSP
and the
BP
are paradigms of what are commonly referred to as
regularity properties
; namely, properties indicatives of
well-behaved
sets of reals. Classical theorems in Descriptive Set Theory, due to Luzin and Suslin, show that all
Borel sets
(in fact, all
analytic sets
) have both the
PSP
and the
BP
as well as other regularity properties such as Lebesgue measurability. For more complex subsets of the real line
the issue becomes sensitive to the consistency of strong axioms of infinity called
large cardinals

[
Koe13
]
.

The
Axiom of Choice

(\textsf{{AC}})
, one of the backbone components of modern mathematics, yields sets lacking the above-mentioned regularity properties. A natural competitor of
AC
is Mycielski-Steinhaus
Axiom of Determinacy

(\textsf{{AD}})
which asserts that every set of reals is determined.
AD
wipes out most of the pathologies inoculated by the non-constructive nature of
AC
. For instance, under
AD
every set of reals has the
PSP
(Davis, 1964), the
BP
(Banach-Mazur, 1957) and is Lebesgue measurable (Mycielski-Swierczkowski, 1964). Besides from these pleasant consequences,
AD
yields a coherent rich theory of the real line (cf.
[
Kan09
,
KW09
,
Koe13
,
Lar23
]
).

Since
AC
contradicts the Axiom of Determinacy, who is then a natural model for
AD
?
L(\mathbb{R})
, the smallest transitive model of
Zermelo-Fraenkel set theory
(
ZF
) containing the real numbers. But for
AD
to hold in
L(\mathbb{R})
very large cardinals are required to exist. Indeed, a deep theorem of Woodin says that
\textsf{{AD}}^{L(\mathbb{R})}
(i.e., the assertion
``L(\mathbb{R})\models\textsf{{AD}}
” holds) is equiconsistent with infinitely-many
Woodin cardinals
. However mild large cardinal hypotheses suffice to grant the regularity properties inferred from
\textsf{{AD}}^{L(\mathbb{R})}
, as proved by Solovay in his celebrated
Annals of Mathematics
’ paper
[
Sol70
]
. Solovay shows that starting (merely) with an
inaccessible cardinal
one can construct a model of
ZFC
(
ZF
together with the Axiom of Choice) where every set of reals
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}
in
L(\mathbb{R})
has both the
PSP
and
BP
. The inaccessible cardinal is needed for every set of reals in
L(\mathbb{R})
to have the
PSP

[
Sol70
]
yet superfluous when it comes to the
BP
, as showed by Shelah in
[
She94
]
.

Curiously, the first proof of the consistency of
\textsf{{AD}}^{L(\mathbb{R})}

relied on
a model beyond the confines of the continuum. The said model is
L(V_{\kappa+1})
and it was inspected under the lenses of axiom
I_{0}(\kappa)
, one of the strongest-known large-cardinal axioms (Woodin, 1984).
1
1

1

L(V_{\kappa+1})
stands for the least transitive model of
ZF
containing
V_{\kappa+1}
, the power set of the
\kappa
th-stage of the
Von Neumann
hierarchy of sets (i.e.,
V_{\kappa}
). Axiom
I_{0}(\kappa)
postulates the existence of certain
elementary embeddings
between
L(V_{\kappa+1})
and itself. By results of Shelah and Kunen this requires
\kappa
to be a singular cardinal of
counatble cofinality
.

The investigation of regularity properties of sets
A\subseteq\kappa
in
L(V_{\kappa+1})
is regarded as a natural generalization of the classical study of regularity properties of sets
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}
in
L(\mathbb{R})
.
Indeed, Woodin realized that
L(V_{\kappa+1})
behaves under
I_{0}(\kappa)
very much like
L(\mathbb{R})
does under
\textsf{{AD}}^{L(\mathbb{R})}

[
Koe13
,
Cra15
]
.
Thus,
I_{0}(\kappa)
provides the appropriate axiomatic framework to develop
Generalized Descriptive Set theory
(
GDST
)
in generalised Baire/Cantor spaces (e.g.,
{}^{\omega}\kappa
and
{}^{\kappa}2
) at singular cardinals
\kappa
.

Our knowledge of
GDST
at regular uncountable cardinals has been substantially expanded in recent years (cf.
[
FHK14
,
LS15
,
LMRS16
,
AMRS23
]
).
On the contrary, similar investigations at singular cardinals are barely existent. This is perhaps due to the seemingly disconnection between (1) the descriptive-set-theoretic methods and (2) the techniques employed in the study of singular cardinals. This paper aims to bridge this conceptual gap.

Work by Woodin
[
Woo11
, §7]
and his students, Cramer
[
Cra15
, §5]
and Shi
[
Shi15
, §4]
, have revealed that under axiom
I_{0}(\kappa)
every set
A\subseteq{}^{\omega}\kappa
in
L(V_{\kappa+1})
has the
\kappa
-
PSP
(see §
2.3
). This is in clear concordance with the effects of
\textsf{{AD}}^{L(\mathbb{R})}
upon sets of reals in
L(\mathbb{R})
. Our first main result reads:

Main Theorem 1
.

Suppose that
\kappa<\lambda
are cardinals with
\kappa

{<}\lambda
-supercompact and
\lambda
inaccessible. Then, there is a model of
ZFC
where
\kappa
is strong limit singular with
\cf(\kappa)=\omega
and every set in
\mathcal{P}({}^{\omega}\kappa)\cap L(V_{\kappa+1})
has the
\kappa
-
PSP
.

The hypotheses employed in our theorem are noticeably weaker than those used by Cramer, Shi and Woodin.
Our result also improves former theorems by Dimonte
[
Dim23
]
and Dimonte, Iannella and Lücke
[
DIL23
]
.

The second regularity property analyzed in this paper is the
\vec{\mathcal{U}}\textsf{-BP}
(see §
2.3
), a generalization of the classical Baire Property introduced by Dimonte, Motto Ros and Shi in
[
DMRS
]
and recently studied in
[
DIL23
]
.

Main Theorem 2
.

Suppose that
\Sigma=\langle\kappa_{n}\mathrel{|}\allowbreak n<\omega\rangle
is a strictly increasing sequence of
{<}\lambda
-supercompact cardinals and that
\lambda
is an inaccessible cardinal above
\kappa:=\sup(\Sigma)
. Then, there is a model of
ZFC
where
\kappa
is a strong limit singular cardinal of countable cofinality and the following hold:

(1)

Every set in
\mathcal{P}({}^{\omega}\kappa)\cap L(V_{\kappa+1})
has the
\kappa
-
PSP
.

(2)

Every set in
\mathcal{P}(\prod_{n<\omega}\kappa_{n})\cap L(V_{\kappa+1})
has the
\vec{\mathcal{U}}
-
BP
(for certain
\vec{\mathcal{U}}
).

The above provides the singular-cardinal analogue of Solovay’s theorem
[
Sol70
]
.
Also, it
enhances the extent of
\kappa
-
PSP
and
\vec{\mathcal{U}}
-
BP
obtained in
[
DIL23
]
.

Our main theorems follow from a general
framework developed by the last two authors to get
\kappa
-
PSP
and
\vec{\mathcal{U}}\textsf{-BP}
configurations at singular cardinals. This paradigm is underpinned by the
\Sigma
-Prikry technology
, developed in
[
PRS19
,
PRS22
,
PRS23
,
PS23
]
. As we will show, our analysis generalize earlier work of Kafkoulis in
[
Kaf94
]
. Also,
they lay the groundwork
for investigating regularity properties at small singular cardinals; such as
\aleph_{\omega}
.

The organization of the paper is as follows. In §
2
we set notations and provide relevant prelimminaries. In §
3
the abstract framework is presented. In §
4
our main consistency results are proved. Here we also develop a new forcing poset (the
Diagonal Supercompact Extender Based Prikry forcing
) which has set-theoretic interests beyond the scope of the manuscipt.

2.
Preliminaries and notations

In this section we collect a few definitions and notations which should help the reader navigate the manuscript. When it comes to
set-theoretic forcing
our main reference is Kunen’s
[
Kun14
]
, whilst Kanamori’s text
[
Kan09
]
will be our preferred source when it comes to
large-cardinal axioms
.

2.1.
Review on projections and complete embeddings

Following the set-theoretic tradition our notation for (forcing) posets will be
\mathbb{P},\mathbb{Q}
, etc. As customary members
p
of a poset
\mathbb{P}
will be referred as
conditions
. Given two conditions
p,q\in\mathbb{P}
we will write
``q\leq p
” as a shorthand for
``q
is stronger than
p
”
. We shall denote by
\mathbb{P}_{\downarrow p}
the subposet of
\mathbb{P}
whose universe is
\{q\in\mathbb{P}\mathrel{|}\allowbreak q\leq p\}
. Finally,
p
and
q
are compatible
if there is
r\in\mathbb{P}
such that
r\leq p,q
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{Q}
be forcing posets.

•

[
FW91
]
A
weak projection
is a map
\pi\colon\mathbb{P}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}
such that:

(1)

For all
p,p^{\prime}\in\mathbb{P}
, if
p\leq p^{\prime}
then
\pi(p)\leq\pi(p^{\prime})
;

(2)

For all
p\in\mathbb{P}
there is
p^{*}\leq p
such that for all
q\in\mathbb{Q}
with
q\leq\pi(p^{*})
there is
p^{\prime}\leq p
such that
\pi(p^{\prime})\leq q
.

•

A weak projection
\pi\colon\mathbb{P}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}
is called a
projection
if
\pi(\mathop{1\hskip-3.0pt{\rm l}}_{\mathbb{P}})=\mathop{1\hskip-3.0pt{\rm l}}_{\mathbb{Q}}
and in Clause (3) above the condition
p^{*}
can be taken to be
p
.
2
2

2

Recall that the weakest condition of a forcing poset
\mathbb{P}
is customarily denoted by
\mathop{1\hskip-3.0pt{\rm l}}_{\mathbb{P}}
– or simply by
\mathop{1\hskip-3.0pt{\rm l}}
if there is no confusion.

•

A
complete embedding
is a map
\sigma\colon\mathbb{Q}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}
such that:

(1)

For all
q,q^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Q}
, if
q\leq q^{\prime}
then
\sigma(q)\leq\sigma(q^{\prime})
;

(2)

For all
q,q^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Q}
, if
q
and
q^{\prime}
are incompatible then so are
\sigma(q)
and
\sigma(q^{\prime})
;

(3)

For all
p\in\mathbb{P}
there is
q\in\mathbb{Q}
such that for all
q^{\prime}\leq q
,
\sigma(q^{\prime})
and
p
are compatible.

Fact 2.2
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{Q}
be forcing notions and
G
be
\mathbb{P}
-generic.

(1)

If
\pi\colon\mathbb{P}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}
is weak a projection, then the upwards closure of the set
\pi``G

(namely,
\{q\in\mathbb{Q}\mathrel{|}\allowbreak\exists p\in G\;\pi(p)\leq q\}
)
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-generic filter (see
[
FW91
, Proposition 2.8]
).

(2)

If
\sigma\colon\mathbb{Q}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}
is a complete embedding then
\{q\in\mathbb{Q}\mathrel{|}\allowbreak\sigma(q)\in G\}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-generic (cf
[
Kun14
]
).

Convention 2.3
.

We will identify
\pi``G
with its upwards closure.

Definition 2.4

(Quotient forcing)
.

Given a projection
\pi\colon\mathbb{P}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}
between posets and a
\mathbb{Q}
-generic filter
H
one defines the
quotient forcing
\mathbb{P}/H
as the subposet of
\mathbb{P}
with universe
\{p\in\mathbb{P}\mathrel{|}\allowbreak\pi(p)\in H\}.

Fact 2.5
.

Every
\mathbb{P}/H
-generic
G
(over
V[H]
) is
\mathbb{P}
-generic (over
V
).

We are going to identify
\mathbb{P}
with its isomorphic copy in
\ro(\mathbb{P})
, the
regular open Boolean algebra
of
\mathbb{P}
(minus its least element
\mathbf{0}
)
[
Kun14
]
. According to this (usual) identification,
\mathbb{P}
is dense in
\ro(\mathbb{P})
and
\mathbb{P}
-names are also
\ro(\mathbb{P})
-names. In particular, if
\mathbb{Q}
is a forcing and
\rho\colon\ro(\mathbb{P})\rightarrow\ro(\mathbb{Q})
is a projection, then so is its restriction to
\mathbb{P}
. Moreover, if
G
is
\ro(\mathbb{P})
-generic then
G\cap\mathbb{P}
is
\mathbb{P}
-generic. Conversely, if
G
is
\mathbb{P}
-generic, then its upwards closure in
\ro(\mathbb{P})

\{b\in\ro(\mathbb{P})\mathrel{|}\allowbreak\exists p\in G\ p\leq b\}

is
\ro(\mathbb{P})
-generic. Forcing with complete Boolean algebras has the advantage that every formula
\varphi
admits weakest a condition
\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket\in\ro(\mathbb{P})

(
the Boolean value of
\varphi
) forcing
\varphi
; this is defined as

\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket:=\bigvee\{p\in\mathbb{P}\mathrel{|}\allowbreak p\Vdash_{\mathbb{P}}\varphi\}.

If
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{Q}
are forcings and
\dot{g}
is a
\mathbb{P}
-name such that
\mathop{1\hskip-3.0pt{\rm l}}\Vdash_{\mathbb{P}}``\dot{g}\text{ is }\check{\mathbb{Q}}\text{-generic}
”, there is a way to define a complete embedding
\sigma\colon\ro(\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\ro(\mathbb{P})
, as well as a projection from
\mathbb{P}
into (a cone of)
\ro(\mathbb{Q})
, using the
\mathbb{P}
-name
\dot{g}
.

Definition 2.6
.

Assume
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{Q}
are forcings and
\dot{g}
is a
\mathbb{P}
-name such that
\mathop{1\hskip-3.0pt{\rm l}}\Vdash_{\mathbb{P}}``\dot{g}\text{ is }\check{\mathbb{Q}}\text{-generic}
”. The
embedding induced by

\dot{g}
is the complete embedding
\sigma:\ro(\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\ro(\mathbb{P})
given by
\sigma(q)=\llbracket\check{q}\in\dot{g}\rrbracket,
for all
q\in\mathbb{Q}
.

Fact 2.7

(Folklore)
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{Q}
be forcings. The following are equivalent:

(1)

There is a projection
\pi\colon\mathbb{P}\rightarrow\ro(\mathbb{Q})
.

(2)

There is a complete embedding
e\colon\ro(\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\ro(\mathbb{P})
.

Fact 2.8

(
[
FHS18
, Lemma 2.1]
)
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{Q}
be forcing notions. Suppose that
p
is a condition in
\mathbb{P}
and
\dot{g}
is a
\mathbb{P}
-name such that
p\Vdash_{\mathbb{P}}\text{$``\dot{g}$ is $\mathbb{Q}$-generic''}
.

Let
\rho\colon\ro(\mathbb{P})_{\downarrow p}\rightarrow\ro(\mathbb{Q})
be the extension to
\ro(\mathbb{P})_{\downarrow p}
of the map defined as

p^{\prime}\leq_{\mathbb{P}}p\mapsto\rho(p^{\prime}):=\bigwedge\{q\in\mathbb{Q}\mathrel{|}\allowbreak p^{\prime}\Vdash_{\mathbb{P}}\check{q}\in\dot{g}\}.

Then
\rho\colon\ro(\mathbb{P})_{\downarrow p}\rightarrow\ro(\mathbb{Q})_{\downarrow\rho(p)}
as well as its restriction
\rho\mathbin{\upharpoonright}\mathbb{P}_{\downarrow p}

are projections.

Moreover, for each
\mathbb{P}
-generic filter
G
with
p\in G
, the upwards closure in
\ro(\mathbb{Q})
of
\rho``G
is

\{b\in\ro(\mathbb{Q})\mathrel{|}\allowbreak\exists q\in\dot{g}_{G}\,(q\leq b)\}.

3

3

Here we are identifying the
\mathbb{P}
-generic
G
with its induced
\ro(\mathbb{P})
-generic.

Convention 2.9
.

In the situation described in Fact
2.8
, we will refer to the map
\rho\colon\ro(\mathbb{P})_{\downarrow p}\rightarrow\ro(\mathbb{Q})_{\downarrow\rho(p)}
as the
projection induced by

\dot{g}
(and
p
). If
\sigma\colon\ro(\mathbb{P})\rightarrow\ro(\mathbb{Q})_{\downarrow\sigma(\mathop{1\hskip-3.0pt{\rm l}}_{\mathbb{P}})}
is a projection we will economize language and say instead that
``\sigma\colon\ro(\mathbb{P})\rightarrow\ro(\mathbb{Q})
is a projection”. Note that
\sigma(\mathop{1\hskip-3.0pt{\rm l}}_{\mathbb{P}})
is possibly stronger than the least element in
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{Q})
, thus our remark.

A technical caveat with certain Prikry-type forcings (the principal posets of this paper) is the non-existence of projections, but only weak projections. However, given a weak projection
\pi\colon\mathbb{P}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}
one can combine Fact
2.2
(1) with Fact
2.8
to form the projection induced by
\pi``\dot{G}
,
\rho\colon\ro(\mathbb{P})\rightarrow\ro(\mathbb{Q})

.

2.3.

d_{\xi}(q):=\begin{cases}1,&if $q\parallel\tau(\check{\xi})$ (i.e., $q\Vdash_{\mathbb{P}}\tau(\check{\xi})=\check{e}_{\xi}$, for some $e_{\xi}\in E$);\\
0,&otherwise.\end{cases}

d_{\xi}
is a
0
-open coloring (see Definition
2.13
) and so Lemma
2.14
applies.

Claim 3.3.1
.

Let
D_{0}
be the set of all conditions
r\leq p
such that:

(1)

For all
\xi\in\nu_{n}\backslash\nu_{n-1}
,
\mathbb{P}_{\downarrow r}
is a set of indiscernibles for
d_{\xi}
;

(2)

C_{r}
is non-empty and
r\Vdash_{\mathbb{P}}\tau\mathbin{\upharpoonright}\check{C}_{r}\in\check{V}
, being

C_{r}:=\{\xi\in[\nu_{n-1},\nu_{n})\mathrel{|}\allowbreak r\parallel\tau(\check{\xi})\}.

Then

is dense below

Proof of claim.

Fix

. We find

in
D_{0}
. Without loss of generality
\ell(q)>n
, hence the
\leq^{*}
-order of
\mathbb{P}
below

-closed and
\nu_{n}<\kappa_{n}
.

First, we show how to meet Clause (1) of the claim. For this we construct a
\leq^{*}
-increasing sequence of auxiliary conditions
\langle q_{\xi}\mathrel{|}\allowbreak\xi\in[\nu_{n-1},\nu_{n})\rangle
below

To do so put

\leq^{*}
-lower bound for the

r_{0}
witnesses Clause (1). By
\leq
-extending

to
r
if necessary, we may assume that

for some

we conclude that
r\Vdash_{\mathbb{P}}\tau\mathbin{\upharpoonright}\check{C}_{r}=\check{h}\in\check{V}.

∎

Let
r_{0}\in G\cap D_{0}
and stipulate
C_{0}:=C_{r_{0}}
. This witnesses
(\gamma)
.

Induction step:
Suppose that
\langle r_{i}\mathrel{|}\allowbreak i\leq j\rangle
and
\langle C_{i}\mathrel{|}\allowbreak i\leq j\rangle
have been constructed complying with Clauses
(\alpha)-(\gamma)
above. Note that

is not
[\nu_{n-1},\nu_{n})
for otherwise

would have decided the value of

D_{j+1}:=\{r\leq r_{j}\mathrel{|}\allowbreak\ell(r)>\max(\ell(r_{j}),j)\;\wedge\;r\Vdash_{\mathbb{P}}\text{$``\tau\mathbin{\upharpoonright}\check{C}_{r}\in\check{V}$''}\;\wedge\;C_{j}\subsetneq C_{r}\}

.

So again we can pick

and set

This finishes the construction of
\langle r_{i},C_{i}\mathrel{|}\allowbreak i<\omega\rangle
witnessing
(\alpha)
–
(\gamma)

Claim 3.3.2
.

\bigcup_{i<\omega}C_{i}=[\nu_{n-1},\nu_{n})
and

Proof of claim.

By construction,

such that

. Since the length of the

such that

. By extending

further if necessary we may assume that both

and

, and we are done.
∎

The above argument was carried out for a fixed

so
we obtain a sequence

\langle C_{n,i}\mathrel{|}\allowbreak i,n<\omega\rangle
such that
\bigcup_{i<\omega}C_{n,i}:=[\nu_{n-1},\nu_{n})

\langle B_{m}\mathrel{|}\allowbreak m<\omega\rangle
is as desired.
∎

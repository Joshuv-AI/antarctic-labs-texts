Generating Einstein–Podolsky–Rosen correlations for teleporting collective spin states in a two dimensional trapped ion crystal
Muhammad Miskeen Khan, Edwin Chaparro, Bhuvanesh Sundar ∗, Allison Carter, John Bollinger, Klaus Molmer, Ana Maria Rey

Abstract
We propose the use of phonon-mediated interactions as an entanglement resource to engineer Einstein–Podolsky–Rosen (EPR) correlations and to perform teleportation of collective spin states in two-dimensional ion crystals. We emulate continuous variable quantum teleportation protocols between subsystems corresponding to different nuclear spin degrees of freedom. In each of them, a quantum state is encoded in an electronic spin degree of freedom that couples to the vibrational modes of the crystal. We show that high fidelity teleportation of spin-coherent states and their phase-displaced variant, entangled spin-squeezed states, and Dicke states, is possible for realistic experimental conditions in arrays from a few tens to a few hundred ions.

Here we demonstrate that quantum teleportation of collective spin-states can be implemented in current 2D crystal arrays in a Penning trap using the center-of-mass motional mode as an entanglement resource. The proposed scheme is analogous to the continuous variable quantum teleportation scheme of Braunstein and Kimble (BK)
Braunstein and Kimble 1998
, however, instead of relying on measurement based schemes for entanglement generation
Duan et al. 2000a
, our system uses unitary phonon-mediated all-to-all spin-spin interactions. Measurements, enabled by spectroscopic resolution of internal spin levels of ions, are only used for the act of teleportation
Sherson et al. 2006
at the end of the protocol. The access to long-range spin-spin interactions in trapped ions arrays
Britton et al. 2012
;
Monroe et al. 2021
, allow for
the initialization of entangled states
Bohnet et al. 2016
, which we show can also be teleported. Our protocol thus opens a path for the implementation of continuous variable quantum information protocols
Braunstein and van
Loock 2005
without requiring additional non-linear interactions that are typically absent in pure photonic and phononic systems. Moreover, since the phonon modes in trapped-ion systems are very long-lived, our system does not suffer from the detrimental losses faced by photons.Finally, while we focus here on states within the same spatial ensemble, the applicability of the same protocols in bilayer arrays or 3D crystals
Hawaldar et al. 2024
could enable the possibility to teleport states between spatially separated layers.

Teleportation protocol:
For the first stage of the teleportation protocol, we set
\displaystyle\Omega_{c}=0
in Eq. (
2

\displaystyle|\Delta_{M}^{ab}|\equiv|\delta_{M}-\Omega_{ab}|\gg g_{l}

\displaystyle\Omega_{ab}\equiv(\Omega_{a}+\Omega_{b})/2

\displaystyle\delta_{ab}\equiv(\Omega_{a}-\Omega_{b})/2

\displaystyle U=e^{i\Omega_{ab}\left(\mathcal{\hat{S}}_{z}^{a}+\mathcal{\hat{S}}_{z}^{b}+\hat{m}^{\dagger}\hat{m}\right)t}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\hat{H}_{ab}=

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\chi_{ab}(\mathcal{\hat{S}}_{+}^{a}\mathcal{\hat{S}}_{-}^{b}+\mathcal{\hat{S}}_{-}^{a}\mathcal{\hat{S}}_{+}^{b})

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\chi_{aa}\mathcal{\hat{S}}_{z}^{a}\mathcal{\hat{S}}_{z}^{a}-\chi_{bb}\mathcal{\hat{S}}_{z}^{b}\mathcal{\hat{S}}_{z}^{b}+\delta_{ab}(\mathcal{\hat{S}}_{z}^{a}-\mathcal{\hat{S}}_{z}^{b}).

(3)

\displaystyle\chi_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}\equiv(g_{\alpha}g_{\alpha^{\prime}})/(4\bar{N}\Delta_{M}^{ab})

\displaystyle\{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\}\in\{a,b\}

We wish to employ this Hamiltonian to generate correlated excitations between the ensembles
\displaystyle a
and
\displaystyle b

\displaystyle a,b
ensembles in fully polarized states with opposite magnetization, e.g. in eigenstates of
\displaystyle\mathcal{\hat{S}}_{z}^{a}

\displaystyle\mathcal{\hat{S}}_{z}^{b}

\displaystyle-N_{a}/2

\displaystyle N_{b}/2

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{ab}

\displaystyle\mathcal{\hat{S}}_{+}^{a}\mathcal{\hat{S}}_{-}^{b}

\displaystyle a

The above discussion is valid in

\displaystyle\mathcal{\hat{S}}_{+}^{a}\simeq\sqrt{N_{a}}\hat{a}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\hat{H}_{ac}=\Delta_{M}^{ac}\hat{m}^{\dagger}\hat{m}-\sum_{a,c}\mathop{}_{\mkern-5.0mul}\frac{\mathcal{G}_{l}}{2}\left(\mathcal{\hat{S}}_{+}^{l}\hat{m}+\hat{m}^{\dagger}\mathcal{\hat{S}}_{-}^{l}\right)+\delta_{ac}\left(\mathcal{\hat{S}}_{z}^{a}+\mathcal{\hat{S}}_{z}^{c}\right),

(S17)

where
\displaystyle\Delta_{M}^{ac}\equiv\delta_{M}-f_{r}
and
\displaystyle\delta_{ac}=\Omega-f_{r}
. The above Hamiltonian is valid provided that
\displaystyle\{\delta_{ac},\mathcal{G}_{a}\sqrt{N_{a}},\mathcal{G}_{b}\sqrt{N_{b}},\mathcal{G}_{c}\sqrt{N_{c}},\Delta_{M}^{ac}\}\ll f_{r}
. Here, akin to the previous case, the spin-phonon interaction for ensemble
\displaystyle b
is rotated out in such interaction picture due to fact that phonon annihilation and creation operator would accumulate the fast oscillating phase factor as determined by
\displaystyle e^{-if_{r}t}
and
\displaystyle e^{+if_{r}t}
respectively. Similar to the previous TMS case, the phonon annihilation and creation operators in the local displacement caused by the imbalance in polarizations of the Raman beams, also accumulate the fast oscillating phase factors
\displaystyle e^{-if_{r}t}
and
\displaystyle e^{+if_{r}t}
. Therefore the terms proportional to
\displaystyle\epsilon_{l}
are rotated out.

II.2.2
Adiabatic elimination of the phonon mode to obtain effective spin-spin interaction between a and c ensembles

Following the same procedure as that of the entangling operation stated before, we consider the COM phonon to be fast oscillating on the time scale of
\displaystyle(\Delta_{M}^{ac})^{-1}
compared to the time scale of the internal spin dynamics i.e.
\displaystyle\{g_{a}/\sqrt{N_{a}},g_{c}/\sqrt{N_{c}}\}<4\Delta_{M}^{ac}
. In this case, the phonon dynamics can be averaged out to zero on the time scale of the spin dynamics and the phonon mode adiabatically follows the spin. We thus adiabatically eliminate the phonon mode to realize an effective spin-spin interaction of the form:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\hat{H}_{ac}=-\frac{1}{4\Delta_{M}^{ac}}\left(\mathcal{G}_{a}\mathcal{\hat{S}}_{+}^{a}+\mathcal{G}_{c}\mathcal{\hat{S}}_{+}^{c}\right)\left(\mathcal{G}_{a}\mathcal{\hat{S}}_{-}^{a}+\mathcal{G}_{c}\mathcal{\hat{S}}_{-}^{c}\right)+\delta_{ac}\left(\mathcal{\hat{S}}_{z}^{a}+\mathcal{\hat{S}}_{z}^{c}\right).

(S18)

II.2.3
Schwinger-Boson representation and effective beam-splitter (BS) operation between a and c ensembles

We now write the collective spin operator in terms of the Schwinger-Boson representation such that for
\displaystyle l=\{a,c\}
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{\hat{S}}_{+}^{l}=\hat{l}^{\dagger}_{\uparrow}\hat{l}_{\downarrow},\quad\mathcal{\hat{S}}_{+}^{l}=\hat{l}^{\dagger}_{\uparrow}\hat{l}_{\downarrow},\quad\mathcal{\hat{S}}_{z}^{l}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\hat{l}^{\dagger}_{\uparrow}\hat{l}_{\uparrow}-\hat{l}^{\dagger}_{\downarrow}\hat{l}_{\downarrow}\right),

(S19)

For this stage of the protocol, we consider that the ensembles
\displaystyle a
and
\displaystyle c
are spin-polarized along the same initial direction
\displaystyle-x
. This is a good approximation if the population in ensemble
\displaystyle a
in the initial direction
\displaystyle-x
doesn’t appreciably change during the first stage of the protocol. Moreover, we assume that the spin polarized state of ensemble
\displaystyle c
, which we want to teleport, has slight yet unknown deviation form direction
\displaystyle-x
direction. The mean field dynamic with such initial conditions reflects that the states associated to the boson
\displaystyle\hat{l}_{\downarrow}
,
\displaystyle\hat{l}^{\dagger}_{\downarrow}
are macroscopically populated to a good approximation in the short time limit. We therefore replace the operators with their mean values as
\displaystyle\hat{l}_{\downarrow}=\sqrt{N_{l}}
. This allows us to rewrite the Eq. (
S18
) as

\displaystyle\displaystyle\hat{H}_{ac}=-\frac{1}{4\Delta_{M}^{ac}}\left[N_{a}\mathcal{G}_{a}^{2}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\uparrow}\hat{a}_{\uparrow}+N_{c}\mathcal{G}_{c}^{2}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\uparrow}\hat{c}_{\uparrow}\right]+\delta_{ac}\left[\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\uparrow}\hat{a}_{\uparrow}+\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\uparrow}\hat{c}_{\uparrow}\right]-\frac{\mathcal{G}_{a}\mathcal{G}_{c}\sqrt{N_{a}N_{c}}}{4\Delta_{M}^{ac}}\left[\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\uparrow}\hat{c}_{\uparrow}+\hat{c}_{\uparrow}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{\uparrow}\right]

(S20)

In order to cancel the unwanted terms in the interaction, we set

\displaystyle\displaystyle(N_{a}\mathcal{G}_{a}^{2})/(4\Delta_{M}^{ac})=(N_{c}\mathcal{G}_{c}^{2})/(4\Delta_{M}^{ac})=\delta_{ac}.

(S21)

Eq. (
S21
) allows us to calculate the interaction picture transformation factor
\displaystyle f_{r}
in terms of the system parameters. To do so, we assume that
\displaystyle N_{a}=N_{c}\equiv\bar{N}
and
\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{a}=\mathcal{G}_{c}\equiv\mathcal{G}
, then we have
\displaystyle(\bar{N}\mathcal{G}^{2})/(4\Delta_{M}^{ac})=\delta_{ac}
. This is further expanded in terms of the factor
\displaystyle f_{r}
as

\displaystyle\displaystyle(\bar{N}\mathcal{G}^{2})=4(\Omega-f_{r})(\delta_{M}-f_{r}).

(S22)

This Equation is solved for
\displaystyle f_{r}
to get
\displaystyle\textstyle{f_{r}=(1/2)\left(\delta_{M}+\Omega+\sqrt{g_{a}^{2}+\delta_{M}^{2}-2\delta_{M}\Omega+\Omega^{2}}\right)}
. It is ensured that parameters used in the numerical results allows
\displaystyle f_{r}
to readily satisfy the time scale given by
\displaystyle\{\delta_{ac},\mathcal{G}_{a}\sqrt{N_{a}},\mathcal{G}_{b}\sqrt{N_{b}},\mathcal{G}_{c}\sqrt{N_{c}},\Delta_{M}^{ac}\}\ll f_{r}
. By this choice of parameters, we thus get the final form of the Hamiltonian resulting in a beam-splitter (BS) operation as given by

\displaystyle\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\rm BS}=

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\chi_{ac}\bar{N}\left[\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{c}+\hat{c}^{\dagger}\hat{a}\right],

(S23)

where we have omitted the redundant labelling of
\displaystyle\uparrow,\downarrow
.
Under the effective beam-splitter interaction, the quadrature is mixed according to the equations:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\hat{X}_{a}=\sin{\theta}\hat{P}_{c}+\cos{\theta}\hat{X}_{a},

\displaystyle\displaystyle\hat{P}_{a}=-\sin{\theta}\hat{X}_{c}+\cos{\theta}\hat{P}_{a},

\displaystyle\displaystyle\hat{X}_{c}=\cos{\theta}\hat{X}_{c}+\sin{\theta}\hat{P}_{a},

\displaystyle\displaystyle\hat{P}_{c}=\cos{\theta}\hat{P}_{c}-\sin{\theta}\hat{X}_{a},

(S24)

where
\displaystyle\theta=\bar{N}\chi_{ac}t
. We truncate this interaction at times
\displaystyle t=t_{\rm BS}
such that
\displaystyle\theta=\pi/4
. Therefore, in terms of total interaction time, we obtain
\displaystyle\hat{X}_{a}(t_{f})=(\hat{P}_{c}(0)+\hat{X}_{a}(t_{\rm TMS}))/\sqrt{2}
and
\displaystyle\hat{X}_{c}(t_{f})=(\hat{X}_{c}(0)+\hat{P}_{a}(t_{\rm TMS}))/\sqrt{2}
, as stated in the main text.

The covariance matrix in terms of the position and momentum quadrature of the system is given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}

-\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}&\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\end{array}\right).

The
\displaystyle 6\times 6

S20

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathbf{\mathbf{C}}_{XP}(t)=\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}cc|c@{}}\mathbf{\mathbf{C}}_{aa}&\mathbf{\mathbf{C}}_{ab}&\mathbf{\mathbf{C}}_{ac}\\
\mathbf{\mathbf{C}}_{ab}^{T}&\mathbf{\mathbf{C}}_{bb}&\mathbf{\mathbf{C}}_{bc}\\
\hline\cr\mathbf{\mathbf{C}}_{ac}^{T}&\mathbf{\mathbf{C}}_{bc}^{T}&\mathbf{\mathbf{C}}_{cc}\end{array}\right),

where
\displaystyle\mathbf{\mathbf{C}}_{aa},\mathbf{\mathbf{C}}_{bb}
and
\displaystyle\mathbf{\mathbf{C}}_{cc}
are the local covariance matrices associated to the spin ensembles
\displaystyle a
,
\displaystyle b
, and
\displaystyle c
, respectively.
Moreover the matrix
\displaystyle\mathbf{\mathbf{C}}_{ab}
captures the correlation for
\displaystyle a
and
\displaystyle b

Here
\displaystyle[.]^{T}
represents the matrix transpose operation. The
\displaystyle 4\times 4

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle\chi_{aa}\bar{N}=\chi_{ba}\bar{N}\simeq\delta_{ab}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathbf{\mathbf{C}}^{AB}(t)\simeq\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}(1/2)\cosh{2r}&0&0&(1/2)\sinh{2r}\\

The lab frame Hamiltonian of the full system is given by

\displaystyle\displaystyle\hat{H}=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{l=a,b,c}\Omega_{l}\hat{S}_{x}^{l}+\sum_{l=a,b,c}\mathcal{G}_{l}(\hat{m}+\hat{m}^{\dagger})\hat{S}_{z}^{l}+\delta_{M}\hat{m}^{\dagger}\hat{m}.

(S42)

The corresponding mean field equations for the four-partite system are obtained by using the Hamiltonian in Eq. (
S42
) and given by

\displaystyle\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left\langle\hat{S}_{x}^{l}\right\rangle=-\mathcal{G}_{l}\left\langle\hat{m}\right\rangle\left\langle\hat{S}_{y}^{l}\right\rangle-\mathcal{G}_{l}\left\langle\hat{m}^{\dagger}\right\rangle\left\langle\hat{S}_{y}^{l}\right\rangle

(S43)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left\langle\hat{S}_{y}^{l}\right\rangle=\mathcal{G}_{l}\left\langle\hat{m}\right\rangle\left\langle\hat{S}_{x}^{l}\right\rangle+\mathcal{G}_{l}\left\langle\hat{m}^{\dagger}\right\rangle\left\langle\hat{S}_{x}^{l}\right\rangle-\Omega_{l}\left\langle\hat{S}_{z}^{l}\right\rangle

(S44)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left\langle\hat{S}_{z}^{l}\right\rangle=\Omega_{l}\left\langle\hat{S}_{y}^{l}\right\rangle,\quad\text{where }l=a,b,c

(S45)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left\langle\hat{m}\right\rangle=-i\mathcal{G}_{a}\left\langle\hat{S}_{z}^{a}\right\rangle-i\mathcal{G}_{b}\left\langle\hat{S}_{z}^{b}\right\rangle-i\mathcal{G}_{c}\left\langle\hat{S}_{z}^{c}\right\rangle-i\delta_{M}\left\langle\hat{m}\right\rangle

(S47)

We simulate the full model dynamics by performing numerical simulations of the above equation using the discrete truncated Wigner approximation (DTWA)
Blakie† et al. 2008
;
Polkovnikov2010SM
;
Schachenmayer2015SM

The DTWA reproduces the quantum dynamics of one and two point observables, appropriately incorporating beyond mean-field effects.

Final local Rotations on ensemble b

In this section, we derive the form of rotation that we apply on ensemble
\displaystyle b
to complete the teleportation protocol. As stated in the main text, the amount of the rotation depends on the two classically communicated measurement outcomes
\displaystyle\beta_{z}
and
\displaystyle\beta_{y}
that are associated with the measured valued of the operator
\displaystyle\hat{S}_{z}^{a,c}
, as performed on ensembles
\displaystyle a
and
\displaystyle c
. We extract the rotation from the underlying HP picture of the corresponding
\displaystyle b
bosonic mode. Let us consider the general form the displacement operator for the
\displaystyle b
ensemble bosonic mode viz:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\hat{D}(\beta)=\exp(\beta\hat{b}^{\dagger}-\beta^{*}\hat{b})=\exp(i\sqrt{2}(\beta_{i}\hat{X}_{b}-\beta_{r}\hat{P}_{b}))\simeq\exp\left[i\sqrt{2}\left[\beta_{i}(-\sqrt{(2/N_{b})}\hat{S}_{z}^{b})-\beta_{r}(\sqrt{(2/N_{b})}\hat{S}_{y}^{b})\right]\right],

(S48)

where we have used spin-to-boson mapping
\displaystyle\hat{P}_{b}(t)=\sqrt{(2/N_{b})}\hat{S}_{y}^{b}(t)
and
\displaystyle\hat{X}_{b}(t)=-\sqrt{(2/N_{b})}\hat{S}_{z}^{b}(t)
as stated in the main text. In addition, from the main text we have:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\hat{S}_{y}^{{\rm ideal},b}(t_{f})=\sqrt{2}\hat{S}_{z}^{a}(t_{f})-\hat{S}_{y}^{c}(0)

(S49)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\hat{S}_{z}^{{\rm ideal},b}(t_{f})=-\sqrt{2}\hat{S}_{z}^{c}(t_{f})+\hat{S}_{z}^{c}(0).

(S50)

To map the input state of ensemble
\displaystyle c
to the output state of
\displaystyle b
, we first remove the displacement
\displaystyle\sqrt{2}\hat{S}_{z}^{a}(t_{f})
and
\displaystyle-\sqrt{2}\hat{S}_{z}^{c}(t_{f})
from
\displaystyle\hat{S}_{y}^{{\rm ideal},b}(t_{f})
and
\displaystyle\hat{S}_{z}^{{\rm ideal},b}(t_{f})

\displaystyle\hat{S}_{y}^{{\rm ideal},b}(t_{f})=\sqrt{2}\hat{S}_{z}^{a}(t_{f})
and
\displaystyle\hat{S}_{z}^{{\rm ideal},b}(t_{f})=-\sqrt{2}\hat{S}_{z}^{c}(t_{f})

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle\displaystyle\hat{P}_{b}(t_{f})=(2/\sqrt{N_{b}})[\hat{S}_{z}^{a}(t_{f})],

(S51)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\hat{X}_{b}(t_{f})=(2/\sqrt{N_{b}})[\hat{S}_{z}^{c}(t_{f})].

(S52)

Now for the operator
\displaystyle\hat{b}=(1/\sqrt{2})(\hat{X}_{b}+i\hat{P}_{b})\equiv\hat{\beta}_{r}+i\hat{\beta}_{i}

\displaystyle\beta_{r}\sim\langle\beta|\hat{X}_{b}|\beta\rangle

\displaystyle\beta_{i}\sim\langle\beta|\hat{P}_{b}|\beta\rangle

S51

S52

\displaystyle\hat{\beta}_{r}=\sqrt{(2/N_{b})}\hat{S}_{z}^{c}(t_{f})

\displaystyle\hat{\beta}_{i}=\sqrt{(2/N_{b})}\hat{S}_{z}^{a}(t_{f})

\displaystyle\hat{S}_{z}^{c,a}(t_{f})

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle\beta_{r}=\sqrt{(2/N_{b})}M_{z}^{c}(t_{f}),\beta_{i}=\sqrt{(2/N_{b})}M_{z}^{a}(t_{f})

\displaystyle\hat{S}_{z}^{b}
and
\displaystyle\hat{S}_{y}^{b}

S48

\displaystyle\beta_{r},\beta_{i}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\hat{D}(-\beta)\simeq\exp\left[i\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{N_{b}}\left[M_{z}^{a}(t_{f})\hat{S}_{z}^{b}+M_{z}^{c}(t_{f})\hat{S}_{y}^{b}\right]\right]=\exp\left[i(2/N_{b})\left(\beta_{y}\hat{S}_{y}^{b}+\beta_{z}\hat{S}_{z}^{b}\right)\right],

(S53)

where we have defined

\displaystyle\beta_{z}\equiv\sqrt{2}M_{z}^{a}(t_{f})

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle\displaystyle\hat{S}_{y}^{b}(t_{f})=-\hat{S}_{y}^{c}(0)

(S54)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\hat{S}_{z}^{b}(t_{f})=\hat{S}_{z}^{c}(0),

(S55)

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle t=0

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle\pi

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle c

Reconstruction for the time-dependent coefficients of a quasilinear dynamical Schrödinger equation
Gen Nakamura ∗ , Tanmay sarkar † and Manmohan Vashisth ⋄

Abstract
. We study an inverse problem related to the dynamical Schrödinger equation in a bounded domain of ℝ n , n ≥ 2 \mathbb{R}^{n},n\geq 2 . Since the concerned non-linear Schrödinger equation possesses a trivial solution, we linearize the equation around the trivial solution. Demonstrating the well-posedness of the direct problem under appropriate conditions on initial and boundary data, it is observed that the solution admits ε \varepsilon -expansion. By taking into account the fact that the terms 𝒪 ⁡ ( | ∇ u ​ ( t , x ) | 3 ) \mathcal{O}(|\nabla u(t,x)|^{3}) are negligible in this context, we shall reconstruct the time-dependent coefficients such as electric potential and vector-valued function associated with quadratic nonlinearity from the knowledge of input-output map using the geometric optics solution and Fourier inversion.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we address an inverse problem associated with non-linear dynamical Schrödinger equation. More precisely, we consider the following initial boundary value problem (IBVP):

\displaystyle\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u(t,x)+\Delta u(t,x)+q(t,x)u(t,x)=\nabla\cdot\overrightarrow{J}(t,x,\nabla u),~(t,x)\in\Omega_{T},\\
u(0,x)=\varepsilon\phi(x),\quad x\in\Omega,\\
u(t,x)=\varepsilon f(t,x),\quad(t,x)\in\Sigma:=(0,T)\times\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}~(n\geq 2)
is a bounded and simply connected domain with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
. Given time
T>0
, we denote
\Omega_{T}:=(0,T)\times\Omega
.
Furthermore,
\varepsilon>0
is a small parameter and
\nabla:=(\partial_{x_{1}},\partial_{x_{2}},\dots,\partial_{x_{n}})
represents the gradient operator with respect to the spatial variable
x:=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})
. In (
1.1
), real-valued time-dependent bounded function
q(t,x)
represents the electric potential and we assume that
q\in C^{\infty}(\Omega_{T})
.

The vector-valued function
\overrightarrow{J}(t,x,\nabla u)
in (
1.1
) is given by

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{J}(t,x,\nabla u):=\overrightarrow{P}(t,x,\nabla u)+\overrightarrow{R}(t,x,\nabla u)

(1.2)

in which the function
\overrightarrow{P}(t,x,\xi)
is described by

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{P}(t,x,\xi):=|\xi|^{2}\overrightarrow{b}(t,x),\quad\xi=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\dots,\xi_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n},

(1.3)

with
|\xi|^{2}
defined using the complex conjugate as

\quad|\xi|^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\xi_{j}\overline{\xi_{j}}.

and
\overrightarrow{b}\in C^{\infty}\big([0,T]\times\overline{\Omega}\big)
where
\overline{\Omega}:=\Omega\cup\partial\Omega
.
We denote a set of a Fréchet space
E
-valued
C^{\infty}
functions over
[0,T]
flat at
t=0
by
C^{\infty}_{(0)}\big((0,T];E\big)
and we assume that

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{R}(t,x,\xi)\in C^{\infty}_{(0)}\big((0,T];C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}\times H)\big),

where for a constant
h>0
, the set
H
is defined by
H:=\{\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:|\xi|\leq h\}
. Moreover, there exists a positive constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\nabla_{x}^{\beta}\overrightarrow{R}(t,x,\xi)|\leq C|\xi|^{3-|\alpha|},

(1.4)

holds for all multi-indices
\alpha
and
\beta
with
|\alpha|\leq 3
.
In order to state well-posedness results, we define the following Sobolev space for two non-negative real numbers
r
and
s

\displaystyle H^{r,s}(\Sigma)=L^{2}(0,T;H^{r}(\partial\Omega))\cap H^{s}(0,T;L^{2}(\partial\Omega))

equipped with the norm

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{H^{r,s}(\Sigma)}=\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{r}(\partial\Omega))}+\left\|f\right\|_{H^{s}(0,T;L^{2}(\partial\Omega))}.

We also set

\displaystyle H^{r,s}_{0}(\Sigma)=\{f\in H^{r,s}(\Sigma):f(0,\cdot)=\partial_{t}f(0,\cdot)=\dots=\partial_{t}^{s-1}f(0,\cdot)=0~\text{on}~\partial\Omega\}.

Let
r\geq\big[\frac{n}{2}\big]+3
. Then there exists
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(h,r,T)
such that (
1.1
) has a unique solution (see section
2
for more details)

u\in X_{r}:=X_{r}([0,T]):=\bigcap_{k=0}^{r}C^{k}\big([0,T];H^{r-k}(\Omega)\big)

in lieu with
H^{0}(\Omega)=L^{2}(\Omega)
, satisfying the compatibility condition of order
r-1
and
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}

for any
\phi\in H^{r}_{0}(\Omega)\cap H^{r+1}(\Omega)
and
f\in H_{0}^{r,r+1}(\Sigma)
.
Moreover, we define the norm on
X_{r}
as

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|_{X_{r}([0,T])}:=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{r}\left\|u^{(k)}(t,\cdot)\right\|^{2}_{H^{r-k}(\Omega)}\bigg)^{1/2},

where
u^{(k)}:=\frac{\partial^{k}u}{\partial t^{k}}
.

In view of the
unique solvability
of the IBVP (
1.1
), we define the input-output map
\Lambda_{q,\overrightarrow{J}}(\varepsilon\phi,\varepsilon f)
associated with the dynamical Schrödinger equation as follows:

\displaystyle\Lambda_{q,\overrightarrow{J}}(\varepsilon\phi,\varepsilon f):=\bigg(u^{\phi,f}|_{t=T},\Big[\partial_{\nu}u^{\phi,f}-\nu(x)\cdot\overrightarrow{J}(t,x,\nabla u^{\phi,f})\Big]\Big|_{\Sigma}\bigg)

(1.5)

for any
(\phi,f)\in\big(H^{r}_{0}(\Omega)\cap H^{r+1}(\Omega)\big)\times H_{0}^{r,r+1}(\Sigma)
, where
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
and
u^{\phi,f}(t,x)
is the solution of IBVP (
1.1
) and
\nu
is the unit normal vector to
\partial\Omega
at
x\in\partial\Omega
directed into the exterior of
\Omega
.

In this paper, we are interested in the following
inverse problem associated with the quasilinear dynamical Schrödinger equation.

Inverse problem:
Indentify the electric potential
q=q(t,x)
and the quadratic nonlinearity in
\overrightarrow{P}=\overrightarrow{P}(t,x,\nabla u)
from the knowledge of input-output map
\Lambda_{q,\overrightarrow{J}}
defined in (

In recent years, there is a growing interest in recovering coefficients associated with quasilinear equations from the knowledge of boundary observations. For instance, the coefficients of a quasilinear elliptic equation in divergence form are reconstructed from the the Dirichlet to Neumann (DN) map defined on the boundary (see
[
10
]
for details).
In
[
11
]
, similar analysis has been carried out for the perturbed weighted
p
-Laplacian equation to recover the coefficients from prescribed DN-map.

Significant progress has also been made to recover the time-dependent coefficients appearing in the quasilinear equations, for instance, refer to
[
15
,
28
,
32
]
for quasilinear parabolic equation, and
[
20
,
38
]
and references therein for hyperbolic equations. In case of magnetic Schrödinger equation, the problem of recovering coefficients has attracted the attention of several researchers in recent years. For instance, in
[
7
]
, inverse problem of determining the magnetic field and time-dependent electric potential from the knowledge of DN-map was considered in three or higher dimension. In
[
17
]
, the author dealt with a problem of determining time-dependent electromagnetic potentials appearing in the Schrödinger equation using geometric optics construction. Further in this direction, inverse problems related to the linear dynamical Schrödinger equation is considered and corresponding stability estimates are obtained (one can refer to

[
1
,
3
,
5
,
30
]
and references cited therein for details).

Choulli et al.
[
14
]
considered the problem of recovering the time-dependent scalar potential associated with the Schrödinger equation from boundary measurements in an unbounded domain.
Moreover, determination of eletric or magnetic potential appearing in the linear dynamical Schrödinger equation in Riemannian geometry have also been studied (see
[
2
,
6
,
17
,
31
,
34
]
and references therein).
However, reconstructions of the time-dependent coefficients for the nonlinear equations have not reached the same stage of maturity as linear problems. Recently in
[
35
]
, the determination of time-dependent coefficients in a semi-linear dyanmical Schrödinger equation is considered where they showed that the electric potential and coefficient of non-lnearity can be determined from the knowledg of source-to-solution map. Inverse initial boundary value problem for a quasilinear hyperbolic equation in second or higher dimension was studied (refer to
[
39
]
), in which unique determination of time-dependent coefficients is proved from the measurement given by input-output map.

The following result is our main contribution in this paper:

Theorem 1.1
.

The electric potential
q(t,x)
and the coefficient
\overrightarrow{b}(t,x)
of the quadratic part of the vector-valued function
\overrightarrow{J}(t,x,\nabla u)
can be constructively determined from the knowledge of input-output map
\Lambda_{q,\overrightarrow{J}}\left(\epsilon\phi,\epsilon f\right)
measured for all
(\phi,f)\in\big(H^{r}_{0}(\Omega)\cap H^{r+1}(\Omega)\big)\times H_{0}^{r,r+1}(\Sigma)
.

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first result which deals with the recovery of time-dependent cofficients appearing in a non-linear dynamical Schrödinger equation from the knowledge of boundary measurements of the solution. Recently in
[
35
]
, the problem of recovery of time-dependent coefficients is considered but they recovered these coefficients from the knowledge of source-to-solution map while we recovered from the knowledge of solutions measured on the boundary.

\displaystyle=2\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\Big\langle\varepsilon^{2}(\nabla\cdot\overrightarrow{b})(\nabla w_{1}\cdot\nabla w_{2})-\varepsilon^{2}(\nabla\cdot\overrightarrow{b})(\nabla w_{2}\cdot\nabla w_{2})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\varepsilon(\nabla\cdot K)\cdot\nabla w_{2}-\varepsilon(\nabla\cdot K)\cdot\nabla w_{2},W\Big\rangle~d\tau

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}\left\|W(s)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}~d\tau,

where we have used the following estimate

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\Big(\varepsilon K(t,x,\varepsilon\nabla w_{1})\Big)-\nabla\cdot\Big(\varepsilon K(t,x,\varepsilon\nabla w_{2})\Big)

\displaystyle\quad=\nabla\cdot\left(\int_{0}^{1}\Big\{\nabla_{\xi}\overrightarrow{R}(t,x,\varepsilon\nabla u_{1}+\varepsilon^{2}\nabla u_{2}+\theta\varepsilon^{2}\nabla w_{1})-\nabla_{\xi}\overrightarrow{R}(t,x,\varepsilon\nabla u_{1}+\varepsilon^{2}\nabla u_{2}+\theta\varepsilon^{2}\nabla w_{2})\Big\}~d\theta\right)

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\varepsilon^{2}.

Combining the above estimates and using coercivity property, we have

\displaystyle\left\|W(t)\right\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\varepsilon^{2}C\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|V(t)\right\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+K_{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|W(\tau)\right\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}~d\tau

(2.31)

with a constant
K_{\varepsilon}>0
bounded with respect to
\varepsilon
and a general constant
C>0
.

We introduce a metric
\rho
in the space
Z(M)
as follows:

\displaystyle\rho(f,g):=\max_{t\in[0,T]}\big\{\left\|f(t)-g(t)\right\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\big\}.

Finally using the Gronwall’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\rho(T(U_{1}),T(U_{2}))\leq\varepsilon Ce^{K_{\varepsilon}T}\rho(U_{1},U_{2})

with the constant
C>0
. As a result, we assert that the map

is a contraction mapping for
\varepsilon>0
small enough. We conclude that for each
\varepsilon>0
small enough, there exists

such that

\displaystyle T(w)=w,\quad\left\|w\right\|_{X_{m}}=O(\varepsilon)~\text{ as }\varepsilon\rightarrow 0.

(2.32)

Hence the Theorem
2.1
follows.

u_{1}^{\phi_{1}\pm\phi_{2},f_{1}\pm f_{2}}
the solutions to (

) when

and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(\overrightarrow{b}(t,x)\cdot\nabla_{x}v(t,x)\right)\nabla_{x}u_{1}^{\phi_{1},f_{1}}\cdot\overline{\nabla_{x}u_{1}^{\phi_{2},f_{2}}}(t,x)\ dxdt=\mbox{known}\end{aligned}

) and

\Omega_{T}
. Using this in previous equation, we get

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{\mathbb{R}^{1+n}}B_{v}(t,x)\nabla_{x}u_{1}^{\phi_{1},f_{1}}\cdot\overline{\nabla_{x}u_{1}^{\phi_{2},f_{2}}}(t,x)\ dxdt=\mbox{known}\end{aligned}

, for

, solutions to (
2.2
) when
\phi=\phi_{i}
and

. Now choosing

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &u_{1}^{\phi_{1},f_{1}}(t,x)=e^{-i\left(\lambda^{2}t+\lambda x\cdot\omega\right)}\left(e^{i\left(\tau t+\xi\cdot x\right)}+R_{1}(t,x)\right)\ \mbox{and}\ \\
&u_{1}^{\phi_{2},f_{2}}(t,x)=e^{-i\left(\lambda^{2}t+\lambda x\cdot\omega\right)}\left(1+R_{2}(t,x)\right)\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{\mathbb{R}^{1+n}}&B_{v}(t,x)\Big(e^{i\left(\tau t+\xi\cdot x\right)}i\xi\cdot\overline{\nabla_{x}R_{2}}+\nabla_{x}R_{1}\cdot\overline{\nabla_{x}R_{2}}+i\lambda\omega\cdot\nabla_{x}R_{1}+i\lambda\overline{R}_{2}\omega\cdot\nabla_{x}R_{1}-i\lambda e^{i\left(\tau t+\xi\cdot\omega\right)}\omega\cdot\overline{\nabla_{x}R_{2}}\\

is known for

. Hence by using the Fourier inversion, we have that
\overrightarrow{b}(t,x)\cdot\nabla_{x}v(t,x)

for all

solution to (
3.24
). Using Lemma

, we choose

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \begin{bmatrix}\vskip 2.84526pt\frac{\partial v_{1}}{\partial x_{1}}&\frac{\partial v_{1}}{\partial x_{2}}&\frac{\partial v_{1}}{\partial x_{3}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial v_{1}}{\partial x_{n}}\\

\vskip 5.69054ptF_{v_{2}}(t,x)\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\vdots\\

\vskip 5.69054pt\vdots\\

On the Schwarzschild-de Sitter metric of nonlocal de Sitter gravity
Ivan Dimitrijevic, Branko Dragovich, Zoran Rakic, Jelena Stankovic

Abstract
Earlier constructed a simple nonlocal de Sitter gravity model has a cosmological solution in a very good agreement with astronomical observations. In this paper, we continue the investigation of the nonlocal de Sitter model of gravity, focusing on finding an appropriate solution for the Schwarzschild-de Sitter metric. We succeeded to solve the equations of motion in a certain approximation. The obtained approximate solution is of particular interest for examining the possible role of non-local de Sitter gravity in describing the effects in galactic dynamics that are usually attributed to dark matter.



1
Introduction

For more than 100 years, General Relativity (GR) has been considered as one of the most beautiful and successful physical theories – from a phenomenological point of view, several remarkable predictions have been confirmed
ellis
. The Standard Model of Cosmology (SMC) assumes that GR is valid and applicable not only in the Solar system but also at the galactic and cosmological scale. However, GR has not been verified at the galactic and cosmological scales without the use of Dark Matter (DM) and Dark Energy (DE). Furthermore, the SMC assumes that the universe contains about 68 % of DE, 27 % of DM and only 5 % of ordinary matter.
Also, GR solutions for the black holes as well as for the beginning of the universe contain singularity and it means that GR should be modified in the vicinity of these singularities. It should be also mentioned that GR is nonrenormalizable theory from the quantum point of view. Hence, in spite of significant successes of GR, it is reasonable to doubt in its validity in description and understanding of all astrophysical and cosmological gravitational phenomena. Keeping all this in mind, it follows that general relativity is not a final theory of gravitation and that its expansion should be considered.

Since, there is still no physical principle that could suggest us in which direction we should search for an extension of GR, there are many approaches to its modification, see

2
On nonlocal de Sitter gravity model

Let us mention some facts of our nonlocal gravity model (see
dimitrijevic13
) which is given by the action

\displaystyle S=\frac{1}{16\pi G}\int d^{4}x\ \sqrt{-g}\ \sqrt{R-2\Lambda}\ F(\Box)\ \sqrt{R-2\Lambda},

(5)

where
F(\Box)
is the following formal expansion in terms of the d’Alemberian
\Box
:

\displaystyle F(\Box)=1+\mathcal{F}(\Box)=1+\mathcal{F}_{+}(\Box)+\mathcal{F}_{-}(\Box),\quad\mathcal{F}_{+}(\Box)=\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}f_{n}\ \Box^{n},\ \mathcal{F}_{-}(\Box)=\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}f_{-n}\ \Box^{-n}.

(6)

When
F(\Box)=1
, i.e.
\mathcal{F}(\Box)=0
, then model (
5
) becomes
local and coincides with Einstein-Hilbert action with cosmological constant
\Lambda
:

\displaystyle S_{0}=\frac{1}{16\pi G}\int d^{4}x\ \sqrt{-g}\ \sqrt{R-2\Lambda}\ \sqrt{R-2\Lambda}=\frac{1}{16\pi G}\int d^{4}x\ \sqrt{-g}\ (R-2\Lambda).

(7)

It is worth pointing out that action (
5
) can be obtained in a very simple and natural way from action (
7
)
by embedding nonlocal operator (
6
) in symmetric product form of
R-2\Lambda
, that is
\sqrt{R-2\Lambda}\ \sqrt{R-2\Lambda}.

Action (
5
) does not contain matter term and this is intentionally done to better see possible role of this nonlocal model in effects
usually assigned to dark matter and dark energy.

The equations of motion (EoM) for model (
1
), for
P(R)=Q(R)
are given by (for more detail, see
dimitrijevic9
;
dimitrijevic13
):

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274ptG_{\mu\nu}+\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}-\frac{g_{\mu\nu}}{2}P(R)\mathcal{F}(\Box)P(R)+R_{\mu\nu}W-K_{\mu\nu}W+\frac{1}{2}\Omega_{\mu\nu}=0,

(8)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274ptW=2P^{\prime}(R)\ \mathcal{F}(\Box)\ P(R),\quad K_{\mu\nu}=\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}-g_{\mu\nu}\Box,

(9)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\Omega_{\mu\nu}=\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}f_{n}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-1}S_{\mu\nu}(\Box^{\ell}P,\Box^{n-1-\ell}P)-\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}f_{-n}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-1}S_{\mu\nu}(\Box^{-(\ell+1)}P,\Box^{-(n-\ell)}P).

(10)

If
P(R)
is an eigenfunction of the corresponding d’Alembert-Beltrami operator
\Box
, and consequently also of its inverse
\Box^{-1}
, i.e. holds

\displaystyle\Box P(R)=qP(R),\qquad\Box^{-1}P(R)=q^{-1}P(R),\qquad\mathcal{F}(\Box)P(R)=\mathcal{F}(q)P(R),\qquad q\neq 0,

(11)

then

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274ptW=2\mathcal{F}(q)P^{\prime}P,\qquad\mathcal{F}(q)=\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}f_{n}\ q^{n}+\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}f_{-n}\ q^{-n},

(12)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274ptS_{\mu\nu}(\Box^{\ell}P,\Box^{(n-1-\ell)}P)=q^{n-1}S_{\mu\nu}(P,P),

(13)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274ptS_{\mu\nu}(\Box^{-(\ell+1)}P,\Box^{-(n-\ell)}P)=q^{-n-1}S_{\mu\nu}(P,P),

(14)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274ptS_{\mu\nu}(P,P)=g_{\mu\nu}\big(\nabla^{\alpha}P\ \nabla_{\alpha}P+P\Box P\big)-2\nabla_{\mu}P\ \nabla_{\nu}P,

(15)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\Omega_{\mu\nu}=\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(q)S_{\mu\nu}(P,P),\qquad\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(q)=\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}n\ f_{n}\ q^{n-1}-\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}n\ f_{-n}\ q^{-n-1},

(16)

and

G_{\mu\nu}+\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}-\frac{g_{\mu\nu}}{2}\mathcal{F}(q)P^{2}+2\mathcal{F}(q)R_{\mu\nu}PP^{\prime}-2\mathcal{F}(q)K_{\mu\nu}PP^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(q)S_{\mu\nu}(P,P)=0.

(17)

The last equation becomes

\displaystyle\hskip-19.91692pt\left(G_{\mu\nu}+\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}\right)\left(1+2\mathcal{F}(q)PP^{\prime}\right)+\mathcal{F}(q)g_{\mu\nu}\left(-\frac{1}{2}P^{2}+PP^{\prime}(R-2\Lambda)\right)-2\mathcal{F}(q)K_{\mu\nu}PP^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(q)S_{\mu\nu}(P,P)=0.

(18)

If
P(R)=\sqrt{R-2\Lambda}
, then
P(R)P^{\prime}(R)=\frac{1}{2}
and

\Box\sqrt{R-2\Lambda}=q\,\sqrt{R-2\Lambda}=\eta\,\Lambda\sqrt{R-2\Lambda},\qquad\eta\,\Lambda\neq 0,

(19)

where
q=\eta\,\Lambda
and
q^{-1}=\eta^{-1}\Lambda^{-1}
(
\eta
– dimensionless) follows from dimensionality of equalities (
19
).
Since
P(R)=\sqrt{R-2\Lambda}
, EoM (
18
) simplify to

\left(G_{\mu\nu}+\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}\right)\left(1+\mathcal{F}(q)\right)+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(q)S_{\mu\nu}(\sqrt{R-2\Lambda},\sqrt{R-2\Lambda})=0.

(20)

It is clear that EoM (
20
) are satisfied if

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(q)=-1\qquad\qquad\text{and}\qquad\qquad\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(q)=0.

(21)

It is worth pointing out that not only nonlocal de Sitter model (
5
) is very simple and natural but also such are corresponding EoM (
20

1
) with
\Lambda\neq 0

Let us remark that nonlocal operator
\mathcal{F}(\Box)
, which satisfies conditions (
21
) in model (
5
),
can be taken in the symmetric form

F(\Box)=1+\mathcal{F}(\Box),\qquad\mbox{where}\qquad\mathcal{F}(\Box)=\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\tilde{f}_{n}\Big[\Big(\frac{\Box}{q}\Big)^{n}+\Big(\frac{q}{\Box}\Big)^{n}\Big],

(22)

where
\tilde{f}_{n}
are dimensionless coefficients.
It is easy to prove that

\mathcal{F}(\Box)
presented in the following symmetric form:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\Box)=\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\tilde{f}_{n}\Big[\Big(\frac{\Box}{q}\Big)^{n}+\Big(\frac{q}{\Box}\Big)^{n}\Big]=-\frac{1}{2e}\,\Big(\frac{\Box}{q}e^{\frac{\Box}{q}}+\frac{q}{\Box}e^{\frac{q}{\Box}}\Big),\qquad q\neq 0,

(23)

satisfies conditions (
21

We can take the eigenvalue
q
of d’Alembertian
\Box

to be proportional to
\Lambda
, i.e.
q=\eta\,\Lambda
, where
\eta\,\neq 0
(since
\Lambda\neq 0

q=\eta\,\Lambda

\Lambda

\Box
in this nonlocal

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\Box)=\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\tilde{f}_{n}\Big[\Big(\frac{\Box}{\eta\Lambda}\Big)^{n}+\Big(\frac{\eta\Lambda}{\Box}\Big)^{n}\Big]=-\frac{1}{2e}\Big(\frac{\Box}{\eta\Lambda}e^{\frac{\Box}{\eta\Lambda}}+\frac{\eta\Lambda}{\Box}e^{\frac{\eta\Lambda}{\Box}}\Big),\qquad\eta\,\Lambda\neq 0,

(24)

where for some specific
\,\Box\,
holds
\,\Box\sqrt{R-2\Lambda}=\eta\,\Lambda\sqrt{R-2\Lambda}.

3
Schwarzschild-de Sitter-type metric

We want to investigate our model outside the spherically symmetric massive body. Since this model is a nonlocal generalization of general relativity with the cosmological constant
\Lambda
, it is natural to consider a generalization of the Schwarzschild-de Sitter (SdS) metric starting from the standard Schwarzschild expression

\mathrm{d}s^{2}=-A(r)\mathrm{d}t^{2}+B(r)\mathrm{d}r^{2}+r^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta^{2}+r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\mathrm{d}\varphi^{2}.

(25)

The corresponding scalar curvature
R
of above metric (
25
) is

\displaystyle R=\frac{2}{r^{2}}-\frac{2}{r^{2}B(r)}-\frac{2A^{\prime}(r)}{rA(r)B(r)}+\frac{A^{\prime}(r)^{2}}{2A(r)^{2}B(r)}+\frac{2B^{\prime}(r)}{rB^{2}(r)}+\frac{A^{\prime}(r)B^{\prime}(r)}{2A(r)B(r)^{2}}-\frac{A^{\prime\prime}(r)}{A(r)B(r)}\,

(26)

where ’ denotes derivative with respect to
r.

In this paper we will consider the case
B(r)=A(r)^{-1}
, and
formula (
26
) becomes

\displaystyle R=\frac{2-2A(r)-4rA^{\prime}(r)-r^{2}A^{\prime\prime}(r)}{r^{2}}.

(27)

Note that (
27
) can be rewritten in the more compact form

\displaystyle R(r)=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial r^{2}}\big[r^{2}\big(1-A(r)\big)\big].

(28)

According to Section
2
, to find a solution of equations of motion it is necessary to solve an eigenvalue problem (
19
), that is
\Box\sqrt{R-2\Lambda}=q\,\sqrt{R-2\Lambda}
. Note that here d’Alembertian
\Box
acts in the following way:

\displaystyle\Box u(r)=A(r)\,u^{\prime\prime}(r)+(A^{\prime}(r)+\frac{2}{r}\;A(r))\,u^{\prime}(r)=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\big[r^{2}A(r)\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\big],

(29)

where
u(r)
is any differentiable scalar function.

Let us now consider function
A(r)
in the form

\displaystyle A(r)=1-\frac{\mu}{r}-\frac{\nu}{r^{2}}-\frac{\Lambda}{3}r^{2}-f(r),

(30)

where
\mu
and
\nu
are some parameters to be discussed later. Then one can show that for
A(r)
given by
(
30
) holds

\displaystyle R(r)=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial r^{2}}\big[r^{2}\big(1-A(r)\big)\big]=4\Lambda+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial r^{2}}\big[r^{2}f(r)\big].

(31)

Denoting
u(r)=\sqrt{R-2\Lambda}
and using expression (
29
) for d’Alembertian, the corresponding equation
\Box\sqrt{R-2\Lambda}=q\,\sqrt{R-2\Lambda}
becomes

\displaystyle\Box\sqrt{R-2\Lambda}=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\big[r^{2}A(r)\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\sqrt{R-2\Lambda}\big]=q\,\sqrt{R-2\Lambda}.

(32)

Since unknown function
A(r)
is contained also in the scalar curvature
R(r)
, equation (
32
) is very complicated and difficult to find exact solution. To get an approximative solution we take
A(r)\thickapprox 1
in (
32
), what is applicable when

\displaystyle\big|\frac{\mu}{r}\big|\ll 1,\quad\big|\frac{\nu}{r^{2}}\big|\ll 1,\quad|\Lambda r^{2}|\ll 1,\quad|f(r)|\ll 1,

(33)

in units
c=1
. Under conditions (
33
), equation (
32
) becomes

\displaystyle\triangle\sqrt{R-2\Lambda}=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\big[r^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\sqrt{R-2\Lambda}\big]=q\,\sqrt{R-2\Lambda},

(34)

where

\displaystyle\triangle=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\big[r^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\big]=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial r^{2}}+\frac{2}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}

(35)

is the Laplace operator (Laplacian) in spherical coordinates.

One can easily check that the general solution of equation

\displaystyle\triangle u(r)=\frac{\partial^{2}u(r)}{\partial r^{2}}+\frac{2}{r}\frac{\partial u(r)}{\partial r}=q\ u(r)

(36)

is

\displaystyle u(r)=\frac{C_{1}}{r}\ e^{\sqrt{q}\ r}+\frac{C_{2}}{r}\ e^{-\sqrt{q}\ r}\,.

(37)

Since the metric should tend to the Minkowski one at large distances, in the sequel we will use only solution

\displaystyle u(r)=\frac{C_{2}}{r}\ e^{-\sqrt{q}\ r}.

(38)

According to equation (
31
) we have

\displaystyle R(r)-2\Lambda=2\Lambda+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial r^{2}}\big[r^{2}f(r)\big]=u^{2}(r).

(39)

Equation (
39
) can be rewritten in the form

\displaystyle r^{2}f^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(r)+4rf^{\prime}(r)+2f(r)=-2\Lambda r^{2}+C_{2}^{2}\ e^{-2\sqrt{q}\ r}.

(40)

General solution of equation (
40
) is

\displaystyle f(r)=-\frac{\Lambda}{6}r^{2}+\frac{C_{2}^{2}}{4q}\ \frac{1}{r^{2}}\ e^{-2\sqrt{q}\ r}+\frac{C_{3}}{r}+\frac{C_{4}}{r^{2}}.

(41)

Replacing
f(r)
in (
30
) by expression (
41
) one obtains

\displaystyle A(r)=1-\frac{\mu}{r}-\frac{\nu}{r^{2}}-\frac{\Lambda r^{2}}{6}-\frac{C_{2}^{2}}{4qr^{2}}\ e^{-2\sqrt{q}\ r}-\frac{C_{3}}{r}-\frac{C_{4}}{r^{2}}.

(42)

Since all parameters
\mu,\nu,C_{3},C_{4}
have been so far arbitrary we can take
C_{3}=C_{4}=0
and maintain only
\mu
and
\nu
, i. e. in the sequel we have

\displaystyle A(r)=1-\frac{\mu}{r}-\frac{\nu}{r^{2}}-\frac{\Lambda r^{2}}{6}-\frac{C_{2}^{2}}{4qr^{2}}\ e^{-2\sqrt{q}\ r}.

(43)

Then the corresponding scalar curvature becomes

\displaystyle R(r)=2\Lambda+\frac{C_{2}^{2}}{r^{2}}\ e^{-2\sqrt{q}\ r}.

(44)

4
Discussion and conclusion

In the previous sections we considered nonlocal de Sitter gravity model defined by its action (
5
) at scales smaller than the cosmological one, i.e. related to stars, galaxies, and
clusters of galaxies. Cosmological solutions of model (
5
) are presented in
dimitrijevic10
;
dimitrijevic13
, and it was shown that the cosmological solution in flat space-time,
a_{1}(t)=At^{\frac{2}{3}}e^{\frac{\Lambda}{14}t^{2}}
gives good agreement with observational data. Consequently, it is natural to see how this nonlocal model works at the smaller scales.

It is well known that
A(r)
of the standard Schwarzschild-de Sitter metric, i.e. in the case of local de Sitter gravity, is

\displaystyle A_{\ell}(r)=1-\frac{2GM}{c^{2}r}-\frac{\Lambda r^{2}}{3c^{2}},\qquad r\geq r_{0},

(45)

where
r_{0}
is the radius of spherically symmetric massive body (
M
–mass), and
G
is the Newton gravitational constant. Note that (
45
) is written in the international system of units (SI). The nonlocal version of
A(r)
(
42
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle A_{n\ell}(r)=1-\frac{2GM}{c^{2}r}-\frac{\Lambda r^{2}}{6c^{2}}+\frac{\delta^{2}}{qr^{2}}\big(1-e^{-2\sqrt{q}\ r}\big),\qquad q=\frac{\eta\Lambda}{c^{2}},

(46)

where we joined terms
\frac{\nu}{r^{2}}
and
\frac{C_{2}^{2}}{4qr^{2}}\ e^{-2\sqrt{q}\ r}
taking
\delta^{2}=\frac{C_{2}^{2}}{4},\nu=-\frac{\delta^{2}}{q}
. It is worth noting that
\delta
and
\eta
are dimensionless parameters, and their values should be determined by experiments (astronomical observations). Comparing (
45
) and (
46
) follows formula

\displaystyle A_{n\ell}(r)-A_{\ell}(r)=\frac{\Lambda r^{2}}{6c^{2}}+\frac{\delta^{2}}{qr^{2}}\big(1-e^{-2\sqrt{q}\ r}\big).

(47)

Testing formula (
46
) in the Solar and other astronomical systems is one of our main next tasks, and the results will be presented elsewhere.

At the end, it is worth noting that in paper
dragovich2
was found connection between cosmological constant
\Lambda
and mass of a scalar particle which has its origin in
p
-adic string theory
dragovich1
. About cosmological research with application of nonlocal fields in the matter sector of general relativity one can see
arefeva2
;
koshelev2011
and references therein.

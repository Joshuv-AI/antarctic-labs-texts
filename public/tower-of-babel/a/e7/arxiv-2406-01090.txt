Volumes of Bott–Chern classes
Sébastien Boucksom and Vincent Guedj and Chinh H. Lu

Abstract
. We study the volumes of transcendental and possibly non-closed Bott–Chern ( 1 , 1 ) (1,1) -classes on an arbitrary compact complex manifold X X . We show that the latter belongs to the class 𝒞 \mathcal{C} of Fujiki if and only if it has the bounded mass property -i.e. its Monge-Ampère volumes have a uniform upper-bound- and there exists a closed Bott–Chern class with positive volume. This yields a positive answer to a conjecture of Demailly–Păun–Boucksom. To this end we extend to the hermitian context the notion of non-pluripolar products of currents, allowing for the latter to be merely quasi-closed and quasi-positive . We establish a quasi-monotonicity property of Monge-Ampère masses, and moreover show the existence of solutions to degenerate complex Monge-Ampère equations in big classes, together with uniform a priori estimates. This extends to the hermitian context fundamental results of Boucksom–Eyssidieux–Guedj–Zeriahi.

Introduction

Complex Monge-Ampère equations are powerful tools in complex geometry, as demonstrated in foundational works ranging from Yau’s resolution of the Calabi conjecture
[
Yau78
]
to the construction of singular canonical metrics by Eyssidieux-Guedj-Zeriahi
[
EGZ09
]
.
As illustrated in
[
BEGZ10
]
, the Bedford-Taylor pluripotential theory
[
BT76
,
BT87
,
Kol98
]
is highly robust, extendable to the broad context of big cohomology classes. This extension has led to many profound applications in Kähler geometry over the past decade.

Following Yau’s celebrated work, the Hermitian Monge-Ampère equation was explored by Cherrier
[
Cher87
]
, who solved it in some particular cases. The general case solved by Tosatti and Weinkove
[
TW10
]
twenty years later underscores the difficulty of translating techniques from the Kähler setting to the Hermitian context.
In recent years, Hermitian pluripotential theory has become central to the advancement of hermitian geometry, as evidenced by

[
DK12
,
KN19
,
GL22
,
GL23
]
. This discussion underlines the necessity of extending the results of
[
BEGZ10
]
to the Hermitian context, which is the primary objective of our paper.

We start by extending the non-pluripolar products of currents, allowing for the latter to be merely

quasi-closed
and
quasi-positive
.

Theorem A
.

Let
(X,\omega_{X})
be a compact Hermitian manifold of dimension
n
such that
\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\omega_{X})<+\infty
.
For any
p=1,\dots,n
, there exists a unique symmetric, multilinear pairing

(T_{1},\dots,T_{p})\mapsto T_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge T_{p}

defined on
p
-tuples of quasi-closed, quasi-positive currents and with values in nonpluripolar
(p,p)
-currents, such that:

(i)

if the
T_{i}
’s have bounded potentials, then the pairing is the Bedford-Taylor Monge-Ampère product;

(ii)

for any tuple of
(1,1)
-forms
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{p}
, the operator

(\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{p})\mapsto\bigwedge_{i}(\theta_{i}+dd^{c}\varphi_{i}),

defined on all tuples of quasi-psh functions, is local in the plurifine topology.

Moreover,

T_{1},\dots,T_{p}\geq 0\Longrightarrow T_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge T_{p}\geq 0.

A
(1,1)
-current
T
is quasi-closed if
dT
is a smooth form.
It is quasi-positive if
T\geq-A\omega_{X}
, for some constant
A
.
When
\theta=\theta_{1}=...=\theta_{n}
and
u=u_{1}=...=u_{n}
, we simply write

(\theta+dd^{c}u)^{n}=\bigwedge_{i}(\theta_{i}+dd^{c}u_{i}).

Our proof is partially inspired by the work of Dinew-Kołodziej
[
DK12
]
, which is further elaborated in
[
GL22
]
. Due to the lack of local potentials of the forms
\theta_{i}
, we locally add strictly plurisubharmonic functions
to employ Bedford-Taylor’s construction of the Monge-Ampère measure. Specifically,
we approximate the unbounded potentials through truncation and then consider the Bedford-Taylor product

\bigwedge_{i}(\theta_{i}+dd^{c}\max(\varphi_{i},-t)).

However, this procedure results in currents of order zero with coefficients that are signed measures instead of positive ones, unless

\theta_{i}\geq 0
. The key point is that when restricted to the plurifine open set
\cap_{i=1}^{p}(\varphi_{i}>-t)
these signed measures are,
in fact, positive.
To ensure our construction functions properly, we critically assume
that
X
has the
bounded mass property
, meaning it satisfies

\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\omega_{X}):=\sup\left\{\int_{X}(\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}\;;\;\varphi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(X,\omega_{X})\text{ with }\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi>0\right\}<+\infty.

The condition
\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\omega_{X})<+\infty
is independent of the positive form

\omega_{X}
, and it is a bimeromorphic invariant
[
GL22
, Theorem A]
. In particular

\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\omega_{X})<+\infty
if
X
belongs to the class
{\mathcal{C}}
of Fujiki.
Several conditions have been provided in
[
AGL23
]
to ensure the finiteness of
\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\omega_{X})
.
To date, we are not aware of any examples where
\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\omega_{X})
is infinite.

Our second main result establishes the following crucial quasi-monotonicity property of Monge-Ampère masses,
which extends
[
WN19
,
DDL23
]
to the hermitian setting.

Theorem B
.

Assume
\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\omega_{X})<+\infty
, and fix
\theta
a smooth
(1,1)
-form
such that
\pm\theta\leq C\omega_{X}
.
If
\varphi,\psi\in\PSH(\theta)
satisfy
\varphi\leq\psi+O(1)
, then

\int_{X}(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}\leq\int_{X}(\theta+dd^{c}\psi)^{n}+\Delta_{\theta},

where

\Delta_{\theta}:=\sup_{\varphi,\psi\in\PSH(C\omega_{X})\cap L^{\infty}(X)}\left\{\int_{X}(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}-\int_{X}(\theta+dd^{c}\psi)^{n}\right\}.

Stokes’ theorem shows that
\Delta_{\theta}=0
if
\theta
is closed. In particular, the total mass of
T^{n}
is the same for all currents
T\in\{\theta\}
with minimal singularities. It is thus natural to set

\vol(\{\theta\})=\int_{X}T_{\min}^{n}.

This, together with
[
BEGZ10
]
, shows that our volume notion is compatible with the one introduced by Boucksom in
[
Bou02
]
.

The situation is more subtle when
\theta
is not closed, as positive currents with minimal singularities do not necessarily have the same Monge-Ampère mass (unless
dd^{c}\theta=0
and
dd^{c}\theta^{2}=0
, see
[
GL22
]
and references therein).
When
\{\theta\}
is
big
we thus introduce

\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\}):=\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(T_{min})\;\;\text{ and }\;\;\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\}):=\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(T_{min}),

with

\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(T):=\inf_{S}\int_{X}S^{n},\quad\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(T):=\sup_{S}\int_{X}S^{n},

where
S
ranges over all quasi-closed positive
(1,1)
-currents in the same
dd^{c}
-class as
T
and with equivalent singularities.
By definition, the upper and lower volumes of
T
only depend on its
dd^{c}
-class and singularity class, and satisfy

0\leq\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(T)\leq\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(T)<\infty,

as
X
has the bounded mass property (see Lemma
2.4
). Note also that

T>0\Longrightarrow\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(T)>0.

The analogous result for
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(T)
is unclear in general, even when
T
is smooth (see §
3.2.3
).

We establish in Section
3
key properties of these volumes,
and notably show that
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}
is a continuous function (Theorem
3.20
).
Indeed another motivation of our paper stems from the Grauert-Riemenschneider conjecture
[
GR70
]
, which asks whether
the existence of a semi-positive holomorphic line bundle
L\rightarrow X
with
c_{1}(L)^{n}>0

implies that
X
is Moishezon, i.e. bimeromorphically equivalent to a projective manifold.
This conjecture has been solved positively by Siu
[
Siu84
]
(see also
[
Dem85
]
).
One can relax the semi-positivity assumption and replace
the condition
c_{1}(L)^{n}>0
by the positivity of the volume
\vol(L)>0
. Recall that

\vol(L)
measures the asymptotic growth of the space of holomorphic sections,

\vol(L)=\limsup_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{n!}{k^{n}}h^{0}(X,kL).

Demailly and Păun have further proposed a transcendental version of this conjecture

[
DP04
, Conjecture 0.8]
: given a nef class
\alpha\in H^{1,1}_{BC}(X,\mathbb{R})

with
\alpha^{n}>0
, they conjectured that
\alpha
should contain a Kähler current;
in particular
X
should belong the class
{\mathcal{C}}
.
Recall that the Bott-Chern cohomology group
H_{BC}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})

is the quotient of closed real smooth
(1,1)
-forms,
by the image of
{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{R})

under the
dd^{c}
-operator.
The following answer to
[
DP04
, Conjecture 0.8]

has been proposed in
[
GL22
, Theorem C]
:

\alpha\text{ contains a K\"{a}hler current }\Longleftrightarrow\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\omega_{X})<+\infty.

The existence of a class
\alpha\in H^{1,1}_{BC}(X,\mathbb{R})
that is
nef and satisfies
\alpha^{n}>0
is however not granted on an arbitrary manifold in the Fujiki class
(see
[
JM22
]
). It is thus natural to extend the conjecture
of Demailly-Păun, by removing the nef assumption,
as was proposed by Boucksom in
[
Bou02
, Conjecture 4.1]
.

Theorem
3.20
applied to closed forms provides a characterization of the Fujiki class,
and yields the following answer to the Boucksom-Demailly-Păun conjecture.

Theorem C
.

Let
(X,\omega_{X})
be a compact hermitian manifold.
The following are equivalent:

(1)

X
belongs to the class
{\mathcal{C}}
of Fujiki.

(2)

\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\omega_{X})<+\infty
and there exists a class
\alpha\in H_{BC}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})

such that
\vol(\alpha)>0
.

In the final Section
4
we extend some of the main results of
[
BEGZ10
]

by solving complex Monge-Ampère equations associated to a (non-closed) big form
\theta
,
and by establishing uniform a priori estimates in this context, elaborating on
[
GL23
]
.
We say that a form
\theta
is
big
if there exists a
\theta
-psh function

\rho
with analytic singularities such that
\theta+dd^{c}\rho
dominates a hermitian form.
We fix such a big form, and we no longer assume that
X
has the bounded mass property.
We then extend the key result
[
BEGZ10
, Theorem B]
as follows.

Theorem D
.

Assume
0\leq f\in L^{p}(X)
with
p>1
and
\|f\|_{p}>0
. Then there exists
(\varphi,c)\in\PSH(X,\theta)\times(0,+\infty)

such that
\sup_{X}\varphi=0
,

V_{\theta}-C\leq\varphi\leq V_{\theta}\;\;\text{ and }\;\;(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}=cf\omega_{X}^{n},

where

•

the constant
c>0
is uniquely determined by
f,X,\theta,\omega_{X}
, and

•

C>0
is a uniform constant that only depends on
(X,\omega_{X})
,
\theta
,
p
and
\|f\|_{p}
.

Here
V_{\theta}=\sup\{u,u\in PSH(\theta)\;\text{ with }\;u\leq 0\}
is a
\theta
-psh function with minimal singularities.
As in
[
BEGZ10
, Section 6]
, we also show the existence of solutions to Monge-Ampère equations twisted by an exponential (Theorem
4.5
). However instead of deducing Theorem
4.5
from Theorem D by a fixed point argument, we first prove
Theorem
4.5
and then deduce Theorem D by a deformation argument.

The uniqueness of the solution
\varphi
is an open problem in the Hermitian setting, even when
\theta
is a Hermitian form
(see
[
KN19
]
for a positive result when
f
is uniformly bounded away from zero).
Showing higher regularity of the solutions in the Zariski open set
\Omega=(\rho>-\infty)
,
-when
f
is smooth-
is an important open problem which is also largely open in the Kähler case
(
[
BEGZ10
]
treats the case when the class
\{\theta\}
is moreover nef).

1.
Quasi-closed and quasi-positive currents

Throughout the article,
X
denotes a compact complex manifold, of complex dimension
n
. We also pick a reference Hermitian
(1,1)
-form
\omega_{X}>0
. The purpose of this section is to review some basic properties of quasi-psh functions, quasi-closed quasi-positive
(1,1)
-currents, and their
dd^{c}
-classes.

1.1.
Quasi-closed currents and
dd^{c}
-classes

For each
p,q\in\mathbb{N}
, denote by
\Omega^{p,q}(X)
the Fréchet space of smooth
(p,q)
-forms on
X
.

Definition 1.1
.

We define the
Bott–Chern space

\mathrm{BC}^{p,q}(X)
of (possibly non-closed)
dd^{c}
-classes of bidegree
(p,q)
as the cokernel of
dd^{c}\colon\Omega^{p-1,q-1}(X)\to\Omega^{p,q}(X)
,
i.e.

\mathrm{BC}^{p,q}(X):=\frac{\Omega^{p,q}(X)}{dd^{c}\Omega^{p-1,q-1}(X)}.

We denote by
\{\theta\}\in\mathrm{BC}^{p,q}(X)
the
dd^{c}
-class of a
(p,q)
-form
\theta
. When
p=q
, the real operator
dd^{c}
induces a map
dd^{c}\colon\Omega^{p-1,p-1}(X,\mathbb{R})\to\Omega^{p,p}(X,\mathbb{R})
between spaces of real forms, and we denote by

\mathrm{BC}^{p,p}(X,\mathbb{R})\subset\mathrm{BC}^{p,p}(X)

its cokernel. Setting
\bar{d}\{\theta\}:=d\theta
for any
(p,q)
-form
\theta
defines a linear map

(1.1)

\bar{d}\colon\mathrm{BC}^{p,q}(X)\to\Omega^{p+1,q}(X)\oplus\Omega^{p,q+1}(X),

whose kernel coincides with the usual
Bott–Chern cohomology space

H_{\mathrm{BC}}^{p,q}(X)=\frac{\Omega^{p,q}(X)\cap\ker d}{dd^{c}\Omega^{p-1,q-1}(X)}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{BC}^{p,q}(X).

When
X
is Kähler (or even Fujiki), the latter coincides with the Dolbeault space
H^{p,q}(X)
.

We endow the (infinite dimensional) complex vector space
\mathrm{BC}^{p,q}(X)
with the quotient topology. Thus
\{\theta_{j}\}\to\{\theta\}
in
\mathrm{BC}^{p,q}(X)
iff
\theta_{j}\to\theta
smoothly for an appropriate choice of representatives. Note that (
1.1
) is continuous in this topology.

Lemma 1.2
.

The space
H_{\mathrm{BC}}^{p,q}(X)
is finite dimensional, and
\mathrm{BC}^{p,q}(X)
is a (Hausdorff) Fréchet space.

Proof.

The first point is well-known, see for instance
[
Dem
, Theorem VI.12.4]
. It equivalently says that
dd^{c}\Omega^{p-1,q-1}(X)
has finite codimension in the closed subspace
\Omega^{p,q}(X)\cap\ker d
of the Fréchet space
\Omega^{p,q}(X)
. Thus
dd^{c}\Omega^{p-1,q-1}(X)
is closed in
\Omega^{p,q}(X)\cap\ker d
, and hence in
\Omega^{p,q}(X)
, which proves the second point.
∎

As is well-known, Bott–Chern cohomology can also be described in terms of currents. This is more generally the case
for
\mathrm{BC}^{p,q}(X)
. To this end, it will be convenient to introduce the following terminology.

Definition 1.3
.

We say that a current
T
on
X
is
quasi-closed
if
dT
is smooth.

In particular, any smooth form is quasi-closed.

Lemma 1.4
.

A
(p,q)
-current
T
on
X
is quasi-closed iff it admits a decomposition

T=\theta+dd^{c}U

where
\theta
is a smooth
(p,q)
-form and
U
a
(p-1,q-1)
-current. Furthermore, any other such decomposition is of the form

T=(\theta+dd^{c}\gamma)+dd^{c}(U-\gamma)

for a smooth
(p-1,q-1)
-form
\gamma
.

Proof.

While this result is likely well-known, we provide the simple argument for the convenience of the reader. The existence of a current
U
such that
T-dd^{c}U
is smooth implies that
dT=d(T-dd^{c}U))
is smooth.
Conversely, assume
dT
is smooth. We first claim that there exists a
(p+q-1)
-current
S
such that
T+dS
is smooth. Indeed, since de Rham cohomology can be computed using either forms or currents, the smooth
(p+q+1)
-form
dT
is exact, and we can thus find a
(p+q)
-form
\alpha
such that
dT=d\alpha
. The
(p+q)
-current
T-\alpha
is then closed, and we can now find a
(p+q-1)
-current
S
such that
T-\alpha+dS
is smooth, hence the claim.

Since
T+dS=T+(\partial+\overline{\partial})S
is smooth, decomposing according to type shows that

T+\partial S^{p-1,q}+\overline{\partial}S^{p,q-1},\quad\overline{\partial}S^{p-1,q}\quad\text{and}\quad\partial S^{p,q-1}

are smooth. Arguing as above, this time with Dolbeault cohomology, it follows that

S^{p-1,q}+{\bar{\partial}R}\quad\text{and}\quad S^{p,q-1}+\partial Q

are smooth for some
(p-1,q-1)
-currents
R,Q
. Thus

T+\partial S^{p-1,q}+\overline{\partial}S^{p,q-1}=T+\partial\overline{\partial}(R-Q)=:\theta,

is smooth, which yields the desired decomposition
T=\theta+dd^{c}U
.

Consider now another decomposition
T=\theta^{\prime}+dd^{c}U^{\prime}
. Then
dd^{c}(U-U^{\prime})
is a smooth
(p,q)
-form, which
dd^{c}
-exact as a current, and hence as a form. Thus
dd^{c}(U-U^{\prime})=dd^{c}\gamma
for a smooth
(p-1,q-1)
-form
\gamma
, and the last point follows.
∎

As a direct consequence of Lemma
1.4
, the inclusion of smooth forms into quasi-closed currents induces a vector space isomorphism

(1.2)

\mathrm{BC}^{p,q}(X)\simeq\frac{\{T\text{ quasi-closed }(p,q)\text{-current}\}}{\{dd^{c}U\mid U\,(p-1,q-1)\text{-current}\}}.

We denote by
\{T\}\in\mathrm{BC}^{p,q}(X)
the
dd^{c}
-class of a quasi-closed
(p,q)
-current
T
. Note that

\bar{d}\{T\}=dT.

When
T
is closed,
\{T\}
lies in the finite dimensional space
H^{p,q}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X)
, and
T\mapsto\{T\}
is further weakly continuous on closed currents, since the weak quotient topology of
H^{p,q}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X)
is Hausdorff, and hence coincides with the strong quotient topology.

However,
T\mapsto\{T\}
is
not
weakly continuous on quasi-closed currents. Indeed, a weakly convergent sequence
of quasi-closed currents
T_{j}\to T
can only satisfy
\{T_{j}\}\to\{T\}
if
\bar{d}\{T_{j}\}=dT_{j}
converges smoothly to
\bar{d}\{T\}=dT
.

1.2.
Quasi-psh functions

Recall that a
quasi-plurisubharmonic
function (
quasi-psh
for short) is a function
\varphi\colon X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
that is locally the sum of a psh function and a smooth function. In particular,
\varphi
is usc and integrable. Quasi-psh functions are actually in
L^{p}(X)
for any
p\in[1,\infty)
, and the induced topologies are further all equivalent.

The
plurifine topology
of
X
is defined as the topology generated by all quasi-psh functions on
X
. We shall say that an operator
(\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{p})\mapsto F(\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{p})
, defined on certain tuples of quasi-psh functions and with values in order
0
currents, is
local in the plurifine topology
if, for any plurifine open
O\subset X
and functions
\varphi_{i},\psi_{i}
, we have

\varphi_{i}=\psi_{i}\text{ on }O\text{ for all }i\Longrightarrow\one_{O}F(\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{p})=\one_{O}F(\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{p}).

Any quasi-psh function
\varphi
satisfies
\theta+dd^{c}\varphi\geq 0
in the sense of currents for some smooth
(1,1)
-form
\theta
. We then say that
\varphi
is
\theta
-psh
, and denote by

\PSH(\theta)=\PSH(X,\theta)

the set of such functions, which sits as a closed convex subspace of
L^{1}(X)
.

When
\theta
is closed, it admits a local potential near each point of
X
, i.e. a smooth function
\rho
such that
\theta=dd^{c}\rho
, and a function
\varphi
is then
\theta
-psh iff
\rho+\varphi
is psh. The following well-known observation allows to reduce many of the basic properties of
\theta
-psh functions for a possibly non-closed
\theta
to the closed case.

Lemma 1.5
.

Pick a real
(1,1)
-form
\theta
, and
\varepsilon>0
. Then any point of
X
admits a neighborhood
U\subset X
with a closed
(1,1)
-form
\theta^{\prime}
such that
-\varepsilon\omega_{X}\leq\theta-\theta^{\prime}\leq\varepsilon\omega_{X}
on
U
.

Proof.

For any
x\in X
, it suffices to pick local coordinates
(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
centered at
x
, and to define
\theta^{\prime}
as the constant
(1,1)
-form in these coordinates which coincides with
\theta
at
x
. The result then holds by continuity of
\theta
at
x
.
∎

Given any
(1,1)
-form
\theta
and any function
f\colon X\to[-\infty,\infty]
, the
\theta
-psh envelope
of
f
is defined as the pointwise supremum

(1.3)

\env_{\theta}(f):=\sup\left\{\varphi\mid\varphi\in\PSH(\theta),\,\varphi\leq f\right\}.

Denote by
\env_{\theta}^{\star}(f)
its usc regularization. Then one of the following holds:

(a)

\env_{\theta}^{\star}(f)\equiv-\infty
(i.e. there exists no
\theta
-psh function
\varphi
such that
\varphi\leq f
);

(b)

\env_{\theta}^{\star}(f)\equiv+\infty
;

(c)

\env_{\theta}^{\star}(f)
is the largest
\theta
-psh function such that
\env_{\theta}^{\star}(f)\leq f
outside a pluripolar subset of
X
.

Note that if
f
is usc and (c) holds then
\env_{\theta}(\varphi)
is already usc (and hence
\theta
-psh), since
\env^{\star}_{\theta}(\varphi)
is then a candidate in the envelope.

Definition 1.6
.

We say that a quasi-psh function
\varphi
has
analytic singularities (with smooth remainder)
if
\varphi
can be locally written as

\varphi=\frac{1}{2m}\log\sum_{j=1}^{N}|f_{j}|^{2}+u

for some
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and some choice of holomorphic functions
f_{1},\dots,f_{N}
and smooth function
u
. If we can further take
N=1
then we say that
\varphi
has
divisorial singularities (with smooth remainder)
.

If
\varphi
has analytic singularities, then it is smooth on the Zariski open set
\{\varphi>-\infty\}
. If
\varphi
further has divisorial singularities, then the effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
E:=\tfrac{1}{m}\mathrm{div}(f_{1})
is globally defined, and the Lelong–Poincaré formula yields

dd^{c}\varphi=\beta+[E]

where
\beta
is a closed smooth
(1,1)
-form.

As pointed out in
[
D+23
, Remark 2.7]
, the notion of analytic singularities with smooth remainder needs to be handled with care, as it depends on the choice of local holomorphic functions
(f_{j})
, and not only on the ideal sheaf they generate. At any rate, the following global result holds:

Lemma 1.7
.

Assume
\varphi
is a quasi-psh function with analytic singularities. Then there exists a modification
\pi\colon Y\to X
, isomorphic over the Zariski open set
\{\varphi>-\infty\}
, such that
\pi^{\star}\varphi
has divisorial singularities.

Proof.

By assumption,
X
admits a finite open cover
(U_{\alpha})
such that

\varphi|_{U_{\alpha}}=\frac{1}{2m_{\alpha}}\log\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\alpha}}|f_{\alpha j}|^{2}+u_{\alpha}

with
f_{\alpha j}\in\mathcal{O}(U_{\alpha})
,
u_{\alpha}\in C^{\infty}(U_{\alpha})
and
m_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. Set
m:=\prod_{\alpha}m_{\alpha}
, and denote by
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}
the ideal sheaf of germs of holomorphic functions
f
such that
|f|\leq Ce^{m\varphi}
locally. On
U_{\alpha}
this condition is equivalent to
|f|\leq C\max_{j}\left|f_{\alpha j}^{m/m_{\alpha}}\right|
. By the well-known analytic characterization of integral closure, this shows that
\mathcal{I}|_{U_{\alpha}}
coincides with the integral closure of the ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}\subset\mathcal{O}_{U_{\alpha}}
generated by
(f_{\alpha j}^{m/m_{\alpha}})_{1\leq j\leq N_{\alpha}}
, and it follows that the ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}
is coherent.

Pick a log resolution
\pi\colon Y\to X
of
\mathcal{I}
, and denote by
D
the effective divisor of
Y
such that
\mathcal{I}\cdot\mathcal{O}_{Y}=\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-D)
. Then
\pi
factors through the normalization of the blowup of
\mathcal{I}
, which coincides on
U_{\alpha}
with the normalization of the blowup of
\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}
, as the two ideal sheaves share the same integral closure. As a result, the functions
(\pi^{\star}f_{\alpha j}^{m/m_{\alpha}})_{j}
generate
\mathcal{O}(-D)
on
\pi^{-1}(U_{\alpha})
. Given a local equation
f_{D}
of
D
, we thus have
\pi^{\star}f_{\alpha j}^{m/m_{\alpha}}=f_{D}g_{\alpha j}
for a family of local holomorphic functions
(g_{\alpha j})_{j}
without common zeroes. This shows

\pi^{\star}\varphi=\frac{1}{2m_{\alpha}}\log\sum_{j}|f_{D}|^{2m_{\alpha}/m}|g_{\alpha j}|^{2m_{\alpha}/m}+\pi^{\star}u_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{2m}\log|f_{D}|+v_{\alpha}

with
v_{\alpha}:=u_{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2m_{\alpha}}\log\sum_{j}|g_{\alpha j}|^{2m_{\alpha}/m}\in C^{\infty}
, which proves, as desired, that
\pi^{\star}\varphi
has divisorial singularities.
∎

The importance of functions with analytic singularities stems from the following consequence of Demailly’s fundamental regularization theorem
[
Dem92
]
:

Theorem 1.8

(Demailly)
.

Any
\varphi\in\PSH(\theta)
can be written as the limit of a decreasing sequence
\varphi_{j}\in\PSH(\theta+\varepsilon_{j}\omega_{X})
with analytic singularities, where
\varepsilon_{j}>0
decreasing to zero.

1.3.
Quasi-positive currents

A
(1,1)
-current
T
is said to be
quasi-positive
if
T\geq\gamma
for some smooth
(1,1)
-form
\gamma
. As a consequence of Lemma
1.4
, we then have:

Lemma 1.9
.

For any
(1,1)
-current
T
, the following are equivalent:

(i)

T
is quasi-closed and quasi-positive;

(ii)

T=\theta+dd^{c}\varphi
for a smooth real
(1,1)
-form
\theta
and a quasi-psh function
\varphi
.

Furthermore, any other decomposition as in (ii) is of the form

T=(\theta+dd^{c}\tau)+dd^{c}(\varphi-\tau)

with
\tau\in C^{\infty}(X)
. As a result, the pull-back of a quasi-closed, quasi-positive
(1,1)
-current
T
by any surjective holomorphic map
f\colon Y\to X
can be defined by setting

f^{\star}T:=f^{\star}\theta+dd^{c}f^{\star}\varphi

for any decomposition
T=\theta+dd^{c}\varphi
as above. In constrast, quasi-closed, quasi-positive currents are in general not preserved under push-forward.

Example 1.10
.

Assume
n=2
, and let
\pi\colon Y\to X
be the blowup of a point
p\in X
. Then there exists a Hermitian form
\omega_{Y}
on
Y
such that
\pi_{\star}\omega_{Y}
is not quasi-closed. Indeed, since any smooth
(1,1)
-form can be written as a difference of Hermitian forms, it would otherwise follow that
d\pi_{\star}\theta=\pi_{\star}d\theta
is smooth for any smooth
(1,1)
-form
\theta
.
However this fails for

\theta=\chi dd^{c}(\pi^{*}f),

where
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(Y)
is a cut-off function supported near a point of the exceptional divisor,
and
f\in C^{\infty}(X)
coincides with
\|z\|^{2}
in a local chart centered at
p
.
Indeed

\pi_{\star}d\theta=d\chi\circ\pi^{-1}\wedge dd^{c}\|z\|^{2}

is a differential form in
X\setminus\{p\}
whose coefficients are not even bounded near
p
.

Extending standard terminology for closed positive currents, we shall say that a quasi-closed, quasi-positive current
T=\theta+dd^{c}\varphi

•

has
bounded potentials
on a given compact subset
K\subset X
if
\varphi
is bounded on
K
;

•

is
more singular than

T^{\prime}=\theta^{\prime}+dd^{c}\varphi^{\prime}
if
\varphi\leq\varphi^{\prime}+O(1)
, and that
T,T^{\prime}
have
equivalent singularities
if
\varphi=\varphi^{\prime}+O(1)
;

•

has
analytic singularities
if the quasi-psh function
\varphi
has analytic singularities (see Definition
1.6
).

As consequence of Lemma
1.7
, we have:

Lemma 1.11
.

For any quasi-closed, quasi-positive current
T
with analytic singularities, there exists a modification
\pi\colon Y\to X
such that

\pi^{\star}T=\beta+[E]

where
\beta
is a smooth
(1,1)
-form and
E
an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor.

Note further that
T
is closed iff
\beta
is.

1.4.
Positivity for
dd^{c}
-classes

Recall that the space

\mathrm{BC}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})=\Omega^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})/dd^{c}C^{\infty}(X)

of real, bidegree
(1,1)

dd^{c}
-classes is a Fréchet space (see Lemma
1.2
), containing the usual (finite dimensional) Bott–Chern cohomology space
H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})
.

Definition 1.12
.

We say that a class in
\mathrm{BC}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
is:

•

Hermitian
if it can be represented by a Hermitian form;

•

nef
if it is a limit of Hermitian classes.

The set of Hermitian classes is an open convex cone in
\mathrm{BC}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
, which we call the
Hermitian cone
. Its closure, the
nef cone
, is the set of all nef classes.

Lemma 1.13
.

A class
\{\theta\}\in\mathrm{BC}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
is nef iff it admits a sequence of representatives
\theta_{j}
such that
\theta_{j}\geq-\varepsilon_{j}\omega_{X}
with
\varepsilon_{j}\to 0
.

Proof.

Assume the existence of representatives
\theta_{j}=\theta+dd^{c}\varphi_{j}
such that
\theta_{j}\geq-\varepsilon_{j}\omega_{X}
. Then
\theta^{\prime}_{j}:=\theta_{j}+2\varepsilon_{j}\omega_{X}>0
, and
\theta^{\prime}_{j}-dd^{c}\varphi_{j}=\theta+2\varepsilon_{j}\omega_{X}\to\theta
smoothly, which shows that
\{\theta\}=\lim_{j}\{\theta_{j}\}
is nef. Conversely, assume
\{\theta\}
is nef. Then
\{\theta\}=\lim_{j}\{\omega_{j}\}
for a sequence of Hermitian forms
\omega_{j}
, and hence
\omega_{j}+dd^{c}\tau_{j}\to\theta
for some smooth functions
\tau_{j}
. In particular,
\theta-(\omega_{j}+dd^{c}\tau_{j})\geq-\varepsilon_{j}\omega_{X}
with
\varepsilon_{j}\to 0
, and hence
\theta-dd^{c}\tau_{j}\geq-\varepsilon_{j}\omega_{X}
.
∎

Definition 1.14
.

We say that a class
\{\theta\}\in\mathrm{BC}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
is:

•

pseudo-effective
(
psef
for short) if it can be represented by a positive
(1,1)
-current
T=\theta+dd^{c}\varphi\geq 0
;

•

big
if it can be represented by a current
T=\theta+dd^{c}\varphi
which is
strictly positive
,
i.e.
T\geq\varepsilon\omega_{X}
for
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
.

As a consequence of Demailly’s regularization (Theorem
1.8
above), any big class can be represented by a strictly positive current with analytic singularities.

Lemma 1.15
.

The psef cone of
\mathrm{BC}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
is a closed convex cone, whose interior coincides with the big cone.

In particular, the Hermitian cone is contained in the big cone, and the nef cone is contained in the psef cone.

Proof.

It is straightforward to check that the big cone coincides with the interior of the psef cone. To see that the latter is closed, assume
\{\theta\}\in\mathrm{BC}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
is the limit of a sequence of psef classes
\{\theta_{j}\}
. For an appropriate choice of representatives, we then have
\theta_{j}\to\theta
smoothly, and hence
\theta_{j}\leq C\omega_{X}
for a uniform constant
C>0
. Since
\{\theta_{j}\}
is psef, we can shoose
\varphi_{j}\in\PSH(\theta_{j})\subset\PSH(C\omega_{X})
, normalized by
\sup_{X}\varphi_{j}=0
. By weak compactness of normalized
C\omega_{X}
-psh functions, we may assume, after passing to a subsequence, that
\varphi_{j}
converges weakly to a
C\omega_{X}
-psh function
\varphi
. Since
\theta_{j}+dd^{c}\varphi_{j}\geq 0
, we infer
\theta+dd^{c}\varphi\geq 0
, which shows that
\{\theta\}
is psef.
∎

Remark 1.16
.

When
X
is Kähler, the big cone of
H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})
,
i.e.
its intersection with the big cone of
\mathrm{BC}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
, coincides with the interior of the psef cone of
H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})
. However, this fails in general in the non-Kähler case: if
X
is for instance a Hopf surface, then
H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})
is one-dimensional, and its psef cone has nonempty interior since
X
carries a nonzero closed positive
(1,1)
-current; however,
X
does not admit
any closed strictly positive current, and the big cone of
H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})
is thus empty.

For later use, we also record the following useful characterization of psef classes, which follows from the Hahn–Banach theorem (see
[
Lam99
, Lemma 3.3]
):

Lemma 1.17
.

A class
\{\theta\}\in\mathrm{BC}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
is psef iff
\int_{X}\theta\wedge\gamma\geq 0
for every
dd^{c}
-closed, positive
(n-1,n-1)
-form
\gamma>0
.

A quasi-closed positive current
T
has
minimal singularities
(within its
dd^{c}
-class) if it is less singular than any other positive current in the psef class
\{T\}\in\mathrm{BC}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
. As observed by Demailly, such currents exist in any psef class
\{\theta\}
. Indeed, the upper envelope

V_{\theta}:=\env_{\theta}(0)=\sup\left\{\varphi\in\PSH(\theta)\mid\varphi\leq 0\text{ on }X\right\}

yields a positive current

T=\theta+dd^{c}V_{\theta}
with minimal singularities. Currents with minimal singularities in a psef class are however usually far from unique.

1.5.
The bounded mass property

Following
[
GL22
]
we introduce the following:

Definition 1.18
.

We define the
upper volume
of
\omega_{X}
as

\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\omega_{X}):=\sup_{\varphi}\int_{X}(\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}\in[0,+\infty],

where
\varphi
ranges over all smooth
\omega_{X}
-psh functions.

The above supremum is unchanged if
\varphi
ranges instead over all bounded
\omega_{X}
-psh functions, since any such
\varphi
is a decreasing limit of smooth
\omega_{X}
-psh functions
\varphi_{j}
(by Theorem
1.8
), and

\int_{X}(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi_{j})^{n}\to\int_{X}(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}

by continuity of Bedford–Taylor products along decreasing sequences.

Any other Hermitian form
\omega^{\prime}_{X}
satisfies
C^{-1}\omega_{X}\leq\omega^{\prime}_{X}\leq C\omega_{X}
for some
C>0
, which implies
C^{-1}\PSH(\omega_{X})\subset\PSH(\omega^{\prime}_{X})\subset C\PSH(\omega_{X})
, and hence

C^{-n}\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\omega_{X})\leq\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\omega^{\prime}_{X})\leq C^{n}\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\omega_{X}).

We may thus introduce:

Definition 1.19
.

Let
X
be compact complex manifold. We say that
X
has the
bounded mass property
if

\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\omega_{X})
is finite for some (hence any) Hermitian form
\omega_{X}
.

The following properties are straightforward:

•

the bounded mass property holds whenever
dd^{c}\omega_{X}^{p}=0
for all
p
(which, as is well-known, follows from the case
p=1,2
);

•

it is inherited by any holomorphic image of
X
.

In particular, the bounded mass property holds whenever
n\leq 2
(using a Gauduchon metric
\omega_{X}
), and also when
X
is a Fujiki manifold (i.e. bimeromorphic to a compact Kähler manifold).

By
[
AGL23
, Theorem A]
and
[
GL22
, Theorem 3.7]
, we further have:

Proposition 1.20
.

The bounded mass property is inherited by complex submanifolds, and is bimeromorphically invariant.

In dimension
n=3
, the bounded mass property is known to hold in the following cases (see
[
AGL23
]
):

•

there exists a Hermitian form
\omega_{X}
with
dd^{c}\omega_{X}\geq 0
;

•

for ‘most’ complex nilmanifolds;

•

for the twistor space of a compact antiselfdual
4
-manifold.

At the time of this writing, we do not know any example where the bounded mass property fails, and it is an intriguing problem whether it always holds. For instance:

Example 1.21
.

Does the Hopf manifold
X=(\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\})/2^{\mathbb{Z}}
of dimension
n\geq 3
have the bounded mass property? See
[
AGL23
, §3.4.2]
for a discussion.

2.
Non-pluripolar products

As before,
X
denotes a compact complex manifold, of complex dimension
n
, endowed with a reference Hermitian form
\omega_{X}
. We show that the bounded mass property yields a notion of non-pluripolar products of quasi-closed, quasi-positive currents, and extend to this context some basic properties of non-pluripolar masses.

2.1.
Products of currents with bounded potentials

For any
p=1,\dots,n
, the classical work of Bedford–Taylor
[
BT87
]
extends the operator

(\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{p})\mapsto dd^{c}\varphi_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge dd^{c}\varphi_{p}

from tuples of (locally defined) smooth psh functions to bounded ones, with values in closed positive
(p,p)
-currents that are
nonpluripolar
, in the sense that they put no mass on pluripolar sets. This operator is symmetric and multilinear, continuous along decreasing sequences, and further has the key property of being local in the plurifine topology (see §
1.2
).

Since any quasi-psh function is locally the sum of a psh function and a smooth function, these results readily extend to tuples of bounded quasi-psh functions
\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{p}
, the closed
(p,p)
-current
dd^{c}\varphi_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge dd^{c}\varphi_{p}
being now merely of order
0
(i.e. with signed measures as local coefficients), and still nonpluripolar.

More generally, given arbitrary
(1,1)
-forms
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{p}
, we define an operator

(2.1)

(\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{p})\mapsto\bigwedge_{i}(\theta_{i}+dd^{c}\varphi_{i})

on tuples of bounded quasi-psh functions by forcing multilinearity, i.e.

(2.2)

\bigwedge_{i=1}^{p}(\theta_{i}+dd^{c}\varphi_{i}):=\sum_{I\subset\{1,\dots,p\}}\bigwedge_{i\in I}\theta_{i}\wedge\bigwedge_{i\notin I}dd^{c}\varphi_{i}.

This formula shows that the operator (
2.1
) is still local in the plurifine topology. As the notation suggests, one easily checks that (
2.2
) only depends on the currents
T_{i}=\theta_{i}+dd^{c}\varphi_{i}
, and yields a symmetric, multilinear pairing

(2.3)

(T_{1},\dots,T_{p})\mapsto T_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge T_{p}

from tuples of quasi-closed, quasi-positive currents with bounded potentials to nonpluripolar
(p,p)
-currents. We next extend this as follows:

Proposition 2.1
.

For any compact subset
K\subset X
, there exists a unique symmetric, multilinear pairing

(T_{1},\dots,T_{p})\mapsto\one_{K}T_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge T_{p}

defined on tuples of quasi-closed, quasi-positive currents with bounded potentials on
K
and with values in nonpluripolar
(p,p)
-currents, such that:

(i)

when the
T_{i}
’s have bounded potentials, the pairing is compatible with (
2.3
);

(ii)

for any tuple of
(1,1)
-forms
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{p}
, the operator

(\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{p})\mapsto\one_{K}\bigwedge_{i}(\theta_{i}+dd^{c}\varphi_{i}),

acting on tuples of quasi-psh functions bounded on
K
is local in the plurifine topology.

Moreover, the construction is compatible with restriction,
i.e.

(2.4)

\one_{L}T_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge T_{p}=\one_{L}\left(\one_{K}T_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge T_{p}\right)

for any compact subset
L\subset K
, and we further have

(2.5)

T_{1},\dots,T_{p}\geq 0\Longrightarrow\one_{K}T_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge T_{p}\geq 0.

We emphasize that the notation
\one_{K}T_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge T_{p}
above should be taken as a whole, i.e. we do not try to make sense of
T_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge T_{p}
itself (see however Theorem
2.2
below).

Proof.

Pick
(1,1)
-forms
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{p}
and quasi-psh functions
\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{p}
that are bounded on
K
. The latter is then contained in the plurifine open subset

O:=\bigcap_{i}\{\varphi_{i}>-t_{0}\}

for some
t_{0}
. For any
t\geq t_{0}
and
i=1,\dots,p
, the bounded quasi-psh function
\max\{\varphi_{i},-t\}
coincides with
\varphi_{i}
on
O
. By (i), (ii), we thus necessarily have

(2.6)

\one_{K}\bigwedge_{i}(\theta_{i}+dd^{c}\varphi_{i})=\one_{K}\bigwedge_{i}(\theta_{i}+dd^{c}\max\{\varphi_{i},-t\}),

for
t
large enough, where the right-hand side was defined above (in the sense of Bedford–Taylor). This proves uniqueness. To get existence, we use (
2.6
) as a definition, and first check that it only depends on the quasi-closed, quasi-positive currents
T_{i}=\theta_{i}+dd^{c}\varphi_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,p
. Any other decomposition of
T_{i}
is of the form

T_{i}=(\theta_{i}+dd^{c}\tau_{i})+dd^{c}(\varphi_{i}-\tau_{i})

with
\tau_{i}\in C^{\infty}(X)
(see Lemma
1.4
). For
t
large enough, the bounded quasi-psh functions

\max\{\varphi_{i}-\tau_{i},-t\},\quad\max\{\varphi_{i},-t\}-\tau_{i}

are both equal to
\varphi_{i}-\tau_{i}
on the plurifine open
O
. By plurifine locality, this yields

\one_{K}\bigwedge_{i}\left(\left(\theta_{i}+dd^{c}\tau_{i}\right)+dd^{c}\max\{\varphi_{i}-\tau_{i},-t\}\right)=\one_{K}\bigwedge_{i}\left(\left(\theta_{i}+dd^{c}\tau_{i}\right)+dd^{c}\left(\max\{\varphi_{i},-t\}-\tau_{i}\right)\right),

which coincides in turn with
\one_{K}\bigwedge_{i}\left(\theta_{i}+dd^{c}\max\{\varphi_{i},-t\}\right)
. By (
2.6
) we infer

\one_{K}\bigwedge_{i}\left((\theta_{i}+dd^{c}\tau_{i})+dd^{c}(\varphi_{i}-\tau_{i})\right)=\one_{K}\bigwedge_{i}(\theta_{i}+dd^{c}\varphi_{i}),

which proves, as desired, that this current only depends on the
T_{i}
’s. Similar considerations shows that

\one_{K}T_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge T_{p}
is a multilinear function of the
T_{i}
. Symmetry is trivial, and (
2.4
) holds by uniqueness.

The proof of (
2.5
) is slightly more involved. Assume
T_{i}\geq 0
for all
i
. Since

\one_{K}T_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge T_{p}\geq 0

is a local property, it is enough to argue in a small open neighborhood
\Omega
of a given point of
X
, on which we can thus find a Kähler form
\omega=dd^{c}\rho
with
\rho
smooth and bounded. Pick
\varepsilon>0
. By Lemma
1.5
, each point of
\Omega
admits an open neighborhood
U\subset\Omega
(depending on
\varepsilon
) and closed
(1,1)
-forms
\theta_{i}^{\prime}
on
U
such that
-\varepsilon\omega\leq\theta_{i}-\theta^{\prime}_{i}\leq\varepsilon\omega
on
U
. After shrinking
U
, we further assume
\theta^{\prime}_{i}=dd^{c}\tau_{i}
with
\tau_{i}
smooth and bounded on
U
. Then

dd^{c}(\tau_{i}+\varepsilon\rho+\varphi_{i})=\theta_{i}^{\prime}+\varepsilon\omega+dd^{c}\varphi_{i}\geq\theta_{i}+dd^{c}\varphi_{i}\geq 0,

and hence
\tau_{i}+\varepsilon\rho+\varphi_{i}
is psh on
U
. For each
t>0
,
\max\{\tau_{i}+\varepsilon\rho+\varphi_{i},-t\}
is thus psh and bounded on
U
, and it further coincides with the bounded quasi-psh function
\tau_{i}+\varepsilon\rho+\max\{\varphi_{i},-t\}
on the plurifine open
O\cap U
for
t
large enough. By plurifine locality of Bedford–Taylor products, we get for each
I\subset\{1,\dots,p\}

\one_{K\cap U}\bigwedge_{i\in I}\left(\theta^{\prime}_{i}+\varepsilon\omega+dd^{c}\varphi_{i}\right)=\one_{K\cap U}\bigwedge_{i\in I}dd^{c}\left(\tau_{i}+\varepsilon\rho+\max\{\varphi_{i},-t\}\right)

=\one_{K\cap U}\bigwedge_{i\in I}dd^{c}\max\{\tau_{i}+\varepsilon\rho+\varphi_{i},-t\}\geq 0.

Since
\theta_{i}+\varepsilon\omega\geq\theta^{\prime}_{i}
on
U
, we infer

\one_{K\cap U}\bigwedge_{i=1}^{p}(\theta_{i}+2\varepsilon\omega+dd^{c}\varphi_{i})=\sum_{I\subset\{1,\dots,p\}}\one_{K\cap U}\bigwedge_{i\in I}(\theta^{\prime}_{i}+\varepsilon\omega+dd^{c}\varphi_{i})\wedge\bigwedge_{i\notin I}(\theta_{i}+\varepsilon\omega-\theta^{\prime}_{i})\geq 0.

As this holds for
U
ranging over an open cover of
\Omega
, we infer

\one_{K\cap\Omega}\bigwedge_{i=1}^{p}(\theta_{i}+2\varepsilon\omega+dd^{c}\varphi_{i})\geq 0.

This holds for any
\varepsilon>0
, and this current converges weakly to
\one_{K\cap\Omega}\bigwedge_{i}(\theta_{i}+dd^{c}\varphi_{i})
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, as one easily sees using multilinearity. We conclude
\one_{K}\bigwedge_{i}(\theta_{i}+dd^{c}\varphi_{i})\geq 0
on
\Omega
, which finishes the proof of (
2.5
).
∎

2.2.
Non-pluripolar products

In this Section and
2.3

we assume that the manifold
X
has the
bounded mass property
(see §
1.5
).
Generalizing
[
BEGZ10
, §1]
(which dealt with closed currents on a Kähler manifold), we then show:

Theorem 2.2
.

For any
p=1,\dots,n
, there exists a unique symmetric, multilinear pairing

(T_{1},\dots,T_{p})\mapsto T_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge T_{p}

defined on
p
-tuples of quasi-closed, quasi-positive currents and with values in nonpluripolar
(p,p)
-currents, such that:

(i)

if the
T_{i}
’s have bounded potentials, then the pairing is compatible with (
2.3
);

(ii)

for any tuple of
(1,1)
-forms
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{p}
, the operator

(\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{p})\mapsto\bigwedge_{i}(\theta_{i}+dd^{c}\varphi_{i}),

defined on all tuples of quasi-psh functions, is local in the plurifine topology.

Moreover,

T_{1},\dots,T_{p}\geq 0\Longrightarrow T_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge T_{p}\geq 0.

The current
T_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge T_{p}
is called the
non-pluripolar product
of the
T_{i}
’s. When
\theta_{i}=\theta
and
\varphi_{i}=\varphi
, we write

(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{p}:=\bigwedge_{i}(\theta_{i}+dd^{c}\varphi_{i}).

The key input in the proof is the following:

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
T_{1},\dots,T_{p}
be quasi-closed, quasi-positive currents, and pick a compact subset
K\subset X
on which the
T_{i}
’s have bounded potentials. Then the total variation of the current
\one_{K}T_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge T_{p}
defined in Proposition
2.1
is bounded by a constant independent of
K
.

More precisely, write
T_{i}=\theta_{i}+dd^{c}\varphi_{i}
with
\theta_{i}
smooth and
\varphi_{i}
quasi-psh, and assume

\pm\theta_{i}\leq C\omega_{X}\quad\text{and}\quad dd^{c}\varphi_{i}\geq-C\omega_{X}

for some
C>0
and
for all
i
. Then the total variation of
\one_{K}T_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge T_{p}
is bounded by a constant only depending on
\omega_{X}
and
C
.

Proof.

By multilinearity, we may assume without loss of generality that

\theta_{i}=\omega_{X}
and
T_{i}\geq 0
, i.e.
\varphi_{i}\in\PSH(\omega_{X})
. By (
2.5
), we then have
\one_{K}T_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge T_{p}\geq 0
, and

\int\one_{K}T_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge T_{p}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-p}\leq n^{n}\int\one_{K}(\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\psi)^{n}

with
\psi:=\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\varphi_{i}
, by multilinearity. By construction, the right-hand side is unchanged when we replace
\psi
with the bounded
\omega_{X}
-function
\max\{\psi,-t\}
for
t\gg 1
(see Proposition
2.1
), and

\int\one_{K}(\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\psi)^{n}=\int\one_{K}(\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\max\{\psi,-t\})^{n}

\leq\int_{X}(\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\max\{\psi,-t\})^{n}\leq\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\omega_{X})<\infty,

see §
1.5
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

Write
T_{i}=\theta_{i}+dd^{c}\varphi_{i}
with
\theta_{i}
smooth and
\varphi_{i}
quasi-psh. For each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, the
T_{i}
’s have bounded potentials on the compact subset
K_{m}:=\bigcap_{i}\{\varphi_{i}\geq-m\}
, and
(K_{m})_{m}
is an increasing sequence such that

X\setminus\bigcup_{m}K_{m}=\bigcup_{i}\{\varphi_{i}=-\infty\}

is pluripolar. For each
m
, Proposition
2.1
yields a nonpluripolar
(p,p)
-current

\Theta_{m}:=\one_{K_{m}}T_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge T_{p},

which further satisfies
\Theta_{m}=\one_{K_{m}}\Theta_{m+1}
. By Lemma
2.3
, the total variation of
\Theta_{m}
is further bounded independently of
m
, and it follows that there exists a unique nonpluripolar
(p,p)
-current
\Theta
on
X
such that
\one_{K_{m}}\Theta=\Theta_{m}
for all
m
. By (i), (ii), this current
\Theta
necessarily coincides with
T_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge T_{p}
, and the rest now follows from Proposition
2.1
.
∎

We further record the following consequence of Lemma
2.3
:

Lemma 2.4
.

For all quasi-closed positive currents
T_{1},\dots,T_{n}\geq 0
, we have

\int_{X}T_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge T_{n}\leq M

for a constant
M>0
only depending on the psef classes
\{T_{i}\}\in\mathrm{BC}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
.

2.3.
Monotonicity of non-pluripolar masses

Arguing as in
[
DDL18
, Theorem 2.3]
, we establish the following semicontinuity property of non-pluripolar Monge–Ampère masses.

Proposition 2.5
.

For any
(1,1)
-forms
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{n}
,

\int_{X}(\theta_{1}+dd^{c}\varphi_{1})\wedge\dots\wedge(\theta_{n}+dd^{c}\varphi_{n})

is a lower semicontinuous function of the tuple
\varphi_{i}\in\PSH(\theta_{i})
,
i=1,\dots,n
, with respect to convergence in capacity.

We refer to
[
GZ
, Chapter 4]
for the notion of convergence in capacity. Suffice it to say here that any monotonically convergent sequence of
C\omega_{X}
-psh functions converges in capacity. The proof of Proposition
2.5
relies on the
following continuity property of Monge–Ampère integrals, which is a direct consequence of the corresponding local statement,
see
[
GZ
, Theorem 4.25]
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Pick
C>0
, and assume
\varphi_{i}^{k}\in\PSH(C\omega_{X})
is uniformly bounded and converges in capacity to
\varphi_{i}\in\PSH(C\omega_{X})
as
k\to\infty
, for
i=1,\dots,n
. Let also
(f_{k})
be a bounded sequence of quasi-continuous functions, converging in capacity to a quasi-continuous function
f
. Then

\lim_{k}\int_{X}f_{k}\bigwedge_{i}(\theta_{i}+dd^{c}\varphi_{i}^{k})=\int_{X}f\bigwedge_{i}(\theta_{i}+dd^{c}\varphi_{i}).

Recall that a function
f
is
quasi-continuous
if is continuous outside open subsets of arbitrarily small capacity. Any quasi-psh function is quasi-continuous, and we thus have:

Example 2.7
.

Pick finitely many quasi-psh functions
\varphi_{i}
, and consider the plurifine open set
O:=\{\varphi>0\}
with
\varphi:=\min_{i}\varphi_{i}
. For each
\varepsilon>0
, the function
\chi_{\varepsilon}\colon X\to[0,1]
defined by

\chi_{\varepsilon}:=\frac{\max\{\varphi,0\}}{\max\{\varphi,0\}+\varepsilon}

is then quasi-continuous, and
\chi_{\varepsilon}
increases pointwise to
\one_{O}
as
\varepsilon\searrow 0
.

Proof of Proposition
2.5
.

For each
k
and
t,\varepsilon>0
, consider as in Example
2.7
the quasi-continuous approximations

\chi_{k,\varepsilon,t}:=\frac{\max\{\min_{i}\varphi_{i}^{k}+t,0\}}{\max\{\min_{i}\varphi_{i}^{k}+t,0\}+\varepsilon},\quad\chi_{\varepsilon,t}:=\frac{\max\{\min_{i}\varphi_{i}+t,0\}}{\max\{\min_{i}\varphi_{i}+t,0\}+\varepsilon}

of the characteristic functions of the plurifine open sets

O_{k,t}:=\bigcap_{i}\{\varphi_{i}^{k}>-t\},\quad O_{t}:=\bigcap_{i}\{\varphi_{i}>-t\}.

Since
\bigwedge_{i}(\theta_{i}+dd^{c}\varphi_{i}^{k})\geq 0
and
\chi_{k,\varepsilon,t}
vanishes outside
O_{k,t}
, plurifine locality of nonpluripolar products yields

\int_{X}\bigwedge_{i}(\theta_{i}+dd^{c}\varphi_{i}^{k})\geq\int_{X}\chi_{k,\varepsilon,t}\bigwedge_{i}(\theta_{i}+dd^{c}\varphi_{i}^{k})=\int_{X}\chi_{k,\varepsilon,t}\,\bigwedge_{i}(\theta_{i}+dd^{c}\max\{\varphi_{i}^{k},-t\}).

Now
(\chi_{k,\varepsilon,t})_{k}
is a bounded sequence of quasi-continuous fonctions converging in capacity to the quasi-continuous function
\chi_{\varepsilon,t}
, while
(\max\{\varphi_{i}^{k},-t\})_{k}
is a bounded sequence of
C\omega_{X}
-psh functions converging in capacity to
\max\{\varphi_{i},-t\}
. By Lemma
2.6
, we thus have

\lim_{k}\int_{X}\chi_{k,\varepsilon,t}\,\bigwedge_{i}(\theta_{i}+dd^{c}\max\{\varphi_{i}^{k},-t\})=\int_{X}\chi_{\varepsilon,t}\bigwedge_{i}(\theta_{i}+dd^{c}\max\{\varphi_{i},-t\})

=\int_{X}\chi_{\varepsilon,t}\,\bigwedge_{i}(\theta_{i}+dd^{c}\varphi_{i}),

where the last equality holds by plurifine locality again. We thus get

\liminf_{k}\int_{X}\bigwedge_{i}(\theta_{i}+dd^{c}\varphi_{i}^{k})\geq\int_{X}\chi_{\varepsilon,t}\bigwedge_{i}(\theta_{i}+dd^{c}\varphi_{i}),

and the result follows since

\lim_{t\to\infty}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{X}\chi_{\varepsilon,t}\bigwedge_{i}(\theta_{i}+dd^{c}\varphi_{i})=\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{O_{t}}\bigwedge_{i}(\theta_{i}+dd^{c}\varphi_{i})=\int_{X}\bigwedge_{i}(\theta_{i}+dd^{c}\varphi_{i}).

∎

Finally, we provide a version, in the present non-Kähler setting, of the monotonicity of nonpluripolar masses with respect to singularities. To state the result, pick any
(1,1)
-form
\theta
, let
C\geq 0
be the smallest constant such that
\pm\theta\leq C\omega_{X}
, and set

(2.7)

\Delta_{\theta}:=\sup_{\varphi,\psi\in\PSH(C\omega_{X})\cap L^{\infty}}\left\{\int_{X}(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}-\int_{X}(\theta+dd^{c}\psi)^{n}\right\}.

By Stokes’ theorem,
\Delta_{\theta}=0
if
\theta
is closed. In general, Lemma
2.4
yields
\Delta_{\theta}\in[0,+\infty)
and

(2.8)

\Delta_{\theta+\varepsilon\omega_{X}}=\Delta_{\theta}+O(\varepsilon)

for
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
.

Theorem 2.8
.

Assume
\varphi,\psi\in\PSH(\theta)
satisfy
\varphi\leq\psi+O(1)
. Then

\int_{X}(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}\leq\int_{X}(\theta+dd^{c}\psi)^{n}+\Delta_{\theta}.

Proof.

The proof goes along the same lines as
[
DDL23
, Lemma 3.1]
. Assume first
\varphi=\psi
outside some compact subset
K\subset X
on which
\varphi,\psi
are bounded. Pick
t\gg 1
such that
\varphi,\psi>-t
on
K
, and consider the bounded
C\omega_{X}
-psh functions

\varphi_{t}:=\max\{\varphi,-t\},\quad\psi_{t}:=\max\{\psi,-t\}.

By plurifine locality, we then have

\int_{K}(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}=\int_{K}(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi_{t})^{n},\quad\int_{K}(\theta+dd^{c}\psi)^{n}=\int_{K}(\theta+dd^{c}\psi_{t})^{n},

while
\varphi=\psi,\varphi_{t}=\psi_{t}
on the open set
X\setminus K
similarly yields

\int_{X\setminus K}(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}=\int_{X\setminus K}(\theta+dd^{c}\psi)^{n},\quad\int_{X\setminus K}(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi_{t})^{n}=\int_{X\setminus K}(\theta+dd^{c}\psi_{t})^{n}.

This implies

\int_{X}(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}-\int_{X}(\theta+dd^{c}\psi)^{n}=\int_{X}(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi_{t})^{n}-\int_{X}(\theta+dd^{c}\psi_{t})^{n},

and the result follows since the right-hand side is bounded by
\Delta_{\theta}
. Returning to the general case, set

\varphi_{\varepsilon,t}:=\max\{(1+\varepsilon)\varphi,\psi-t\}\in\PSH(\theta+C\varepsilon\omega_{X}),

where
\varepsilon,t>0
are fixed for the moment. Since
\varphi\leq\psi+O(1)
, we have
\varphi_{\varepsilon,t}=\psi-t
outside the compact set
\{\varphi\geq-s\}
for
s\gg 1
, and the first part of the proof thus yields

\int_{X}(\theta+C\varepsilon\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi_{\varepsilon,t})^{n}\leq\int_{X}(\theta+C\varepsilon\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\psi)^{n}+\Delta_{\theta+C\varepsilon\omega_{X}}.

For
0<\varepsilon\leq\delta
, we infer

\int_{X}(\theta+C\delta\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi_{\varepsilon,t})^{n}=\int_{X}(\theta+C\varepsilon\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi_{\varepsilon,t})^{n}+O(\delta)

\leq\int_{X}(\theta+C\varepsilon\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\psi)^{n}+\Delta_{\theta}+O(\delta),

using Lemma
2.3
and (
2.8
). Since
\varphi_{t,\varepsilon}\in\PSH(\theta+C\delta\omega_{X})
increases to
\max\{\varphi,\psi-t\}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, Proposition
2.5
now yields

\int_{X}(\theta+C\delta\omega+dd^{c}\max\{\varphi,\psi-t\})^{n}\leq\int_{X}(\theta+dd^{c}\psi)^{n}+\Delta_{\theta}+O(\delta).

Letting
\delta\to 0
, we get

\int_{X}(\theta+dd^{c}\max\{\varphi,\psi-t\})^{n}\leq\int_{X}(\theta+dd^{c}\psi)^{n}+\Delta_{\theta}.

Since
\max\{\varphi,\psi-t\}
decreases to
\varphi
as
t\to\infty
, the conclusion now
follows from Proposition
2.5
.
∎

2.4.
Monge–Ampère measures of quasi-psh envelopes

In this section, we fix a big
dd^{c}
-class
\{\theta\}\in\mathrm{BC}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
. We also pick
\rho\in\PSH(\theta)
with analytic singularities such that
\theta+dd^{c}\rho\geq\omega_{X}
(after perhaps scaling
\omega_{X}
), and consider the Zariski open subset

\Omega:=\{\rho>-\infty\}.

Theorem 2.9
.

Pick a quasi-continuous function
f\colon X\to[-\infty,\infty]
, and assume that the usc regularization
\env_{\theta}^{\star}(f)
of its
\theta
-psh envelope (
1.3
) is
\theta
-psh. Then

\int_{\{\env_{\theta}^{\star}(f)<f\}}(\theta+dd^{c}\env_{\theta}^{\star}(f))^{n}=0.

Proof.

Replacing
f
with
\min\{f,\sup_{X}\env_{\theta}^{\star}(f)\}
, we may assume without loss that
f
is bounded above. Assume further that
f
is bounded. When
f
is lsc, the result follows from a standard balayage argument. If
f
is merely bounded, we can find a decreasing sequence
(f_{j})
of bounded lsc functions that decreases pointwise to
f
outside a pluripolar set (see
[
GLZ19
, Lemma 2.4]
and
[
DDL23
, Theorem 2.7]
). Using that
u_{j}:=\env^{\star}_{\theta}(f_{j})
(resp.
u:=\env_{\theta}^{\star}(f)
) is the largest
\theta
-psh function dominated by
f_{j}
(resp.
f
) outside a pluripolar set, it is easy to see that
u_{j}
decreases pointwise to
u
. By the previous step of the proof, we further have

\int_{X}\frac{\max\{f_{j}-u_{j},0\}}{\max\{f_{j}-u_{j},0\}+\varepsilon}(\theta+dd^{c}u_{j})^{n}=0

for each
\varepsilon>0
. Letting
j\to+\infty
, and arguing as in Proposition
2.5
, we obtain

\int_{X}\frac{\max\{f-u,0\}}{\max\{f-u,0\}+\varepsilon}(\theta+dd^{c}u)^{n}=0,

and letting
\varepsilon\to 0
yields the result in the bounded case. If
f
is not bounded, we approximate it by
f_{j}=\min(f,-j)
, and proceed as above.
∎

Remark 2.10
.

When
\env_{\theta}^{\star}(f)
is locally bounded in
\Omega
, the measure
(\theta+dd^{c}\env_{\theta}^{\star}(f))^{n}
is understood in the classical sense of Bedford–Taylor in
\Omega
, and can thus be viewed as a nonpluripolar measure on
X
without assuming the bounded mass property. The same remark applies for the results below.

Proposition 2.11
.

Assume
\{\theta\}\in\mathrm{BC}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
is big. If
u,v\in\PSH(\theta)
then

(\theta+dd^{c}\max\{u,v\})^{n}\geq\one_{\{u\geq v\}}(\theta+dd^{c}u)^{n}+\one_{\{v>u\}}(\theta+dd^{c}v)^{n}.

In particular, if
u\leq v
, then

\one_{\{u=v\}}(\theta+dd^{c}u)^{n}\leq\one_{\{u=v\}}(\theta+dd^{c}v)^{n}.

Proof.

If
u
and
v
have minimal singularities, then they are locally bounded in
\Omega
. By
[
GL22
, Lemma 1.2]
, the result thus holds on
\Omega
, and hence on
X
since all measures involved are nonpluripolar. In the general case, we approximate
u,v
by the
\theta
-psh functions with minimal singularities

u_{t}:=\max\{u,V_{\theta}-t\},\quad v_{t}:=\max\{v,V_{\theta}-t\}.

The previous step yields

(\theta+dd^{c}\max\{u_{t},v_{t}\})^{n}\geq\one_{\{v_{t}\leq u_{t}\}}(\theta+dd^{c}u_{t})^{n}+\one_{\{u_{t}<v_{t}\}}(\theta+dd^{c}v_{t})^{n}.

Multiplying with
\one_{\{\min\{u,v\}>V_{\theta}-t\}}
, we get

\displaystyle\one_{\{\min\{u,v\}>V_{\theta}-t\}}(\theta+dd^{c}\max\{u,v\})^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\one_{\{\min\{u,v\}>V_{\theta}-t\}}\one_{\{v\leq u\}}(\theta+dd^{c}u)^{n}

\displaystyle+\one_{\{\min\{u,v\}>V_{\theta}-t\}}\one_{\{u<v\}}(\theta+dd^{c}v)^{n}.

We finally let
t\to+\infty
to obtain the result.
∎

As a consequence we infer the following minimum principle.

Corollary 2.12
.

Pick
u,v\in\PSH(\theta)
such that there exists
w\in\PSH(\theta)
with
w\leq u,v
, and hence
\env_{\theta}(\min\{u,v\})\in\PSH(\theta)
. Then

(\theta+dd^{c}\env_{\theta}(\min\{u,v\}))^{n}\leq\one_{\{\env_{\theta}(\min\{u,v\})=v\leq u\}}(\theta+dd^{c}v)^{n}+\one_{\{\env_{\theta}(\min\{u,v\})=u<v\}}(\theta+dd^{c}u)^{n}.

3.
The lower and upper volume of a
dd^{c}
-class

As above,
X
denotes an
n
-dimensional compact complex manifold,
assumed throughout this section to have the bounded mass property. We introduce and study the notion of lower and upper volume of a
dd^{c}
-class.

3.1.
The lower and upper volume of a current

Extending
[
GL22
, Definition 3.1]
, we introduce:

Definition 3.1
.

The
lower volume
and
upper volume
of a quasi-closed positive
(1,1)
-current
T\geq 0
are defined as

\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(T):=\inf_{S}\int_{X}S^{n},\quad\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(T):=\sup_{S}\int_{X}S^{n},

where
S
ranges over all quasi-closed positive
(1,1)
-currents in the same
dd^{c}
-class as
T
and with equivalent singularities.

Writing
T=\theta+dd^{c}\varphi
with
\theta
smooth and
\varphi\in\PSH(\theta)
, we equivalently have

(3.1)

\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(T)=\inf_{\psi}\int_{X}(\theta+dd^{c}\psi)^{n},\quad\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(T)=\sup_{\psi}\int_{X}(\theta+dd^{c}\psi)^{n}

where
\psi
ranges over all
\theta
-psh functions such that
\psi=\varphi+O(1)
.

By definition, the upper and lower volumes of
T
only depend on its
dd^{c}
-class and singularity class, and satisfy

0\leq\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(T)\leq\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(T)<\infty,

see Lemma
2.4
. Note also that

(3.2)

T>0\Longrightarrow\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(T)>0.

The analogous result for
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(T)
is unclear in general, even when
T
is smooth (see §
3.2.3
). As a consequence of Theorem
2.8
, we have:

Example 3.2
.

If
T
is closed, then
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(T)=\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(T)=\int_{X}T^{n}
.

We first collect a few simple properties:

Proposition 3.3
.

Pick a quasi-closed current
T\geq 0
.
Then:

(i)

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
we have

\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(tT)=t^{n}\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(T),\quad\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(tT)=t^{n}\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(T);

(ii)

for any quasi-closed current
T^{\prime}\geq 0
and
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
, we have

(3.3)

\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(T+\varepsilon T^{\prime})\leq\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(T)+C\varepsilon

for a constant
C>0
only depending on
\{T\},\{T^{\prime}\}
;

(iii)

for any effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
E
we have

\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(T+[E])=\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(T),\quad\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(T+[E])=\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(T);

(iv)

for any modification
\pi\colon Y\to X
we have

\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\pi^{\star}T)=\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(T),\quad\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\pi^{\star}T)=\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(T).

Proof.

(i) is obvious from the definition. Pick
T^{\prime}\geq 0
quasi-closed and
0\leq S\in\{T\}
with singularities equivalent to
T
. Then
0\leq S+\varepsilon T^{\prime}\in\{T+\varepsilon T^{\prime}\}
has singularities equivalent to
T+\varepsilon T^{\prime}
, and hence
\int_{X}(S+\varepsilon T^{\prime})^{n}\geq\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(T+\varepsilon T^{\prime})
. For
j=0,\dots,n
,
\int_{X}S^{j}\wedge T^{\prime n-j}
is further bounded in terms of
\{S\}=\{T\}
and
\{T^{\prime}\}
, by Lemma
2.4
. Thus

\int_{X}S^{n}\geq\int_{X}(S+\varepsilon T^{\prime})^{n}-C\varepsilon\geq\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(T+\varepsilon T^{\prime})-C\varepsilon

for a constant
C>0
only depending on
\{T\},\{T^{\prime}\}
.
Taking the infimum over
S
yields (ii).

Next pick an effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
E
, and note that any
0\leq S\in\{T\}
satisfies
S+[E]\in\{T+[E]\}
and
\int_{X}S^{n}=\int_{X}(S+[E])^{n}
. To prove (iii), it is thus enough to show that
S\mapsto S+[E]
sets a 1–1 correspondence between positive currents in
\{T\}
with the same singularities as
T
and positive currents in
\{T+[E]\}
with the same singularities as
T+[E]
. One direction is clear. Conversely, pick
0\leq S^{\prime}\in\{T+[E]\}
with the same singularities as
T+[E]
. Write

T=\theta+dd^{c}\psi,\quad[E]=\theta_{E}+dd^{c}\psi_{E},\quad S^{\prime}=\theta+\theta_{E}+dd^{c}\varphi^{\prime}

with
\theta,\theta_{E}
smooth and
\psi\in\PSH(\theta)
,
\psi_{E}\in\PSH(\theta_{E})
,
\varphi^{\prime}\in\PSH(\theta+\theta_{E})
. By assumption we have
\varphi^{\prime}=\psi+\psi_{E}+O(1)
. In particular,
\varphi^{\prime}-\psi_{E}
is
\theta
-psh on
X\setminus\supp E
and bounded above, and hence uniquely extends to a
\theta
-psh function
\varphi
on
X
, which satisfies
\varphi=\psi+O(1)
. This shows that
S:=\theta+dd^{c}\varphi\geq 0
lies in
\{T\}
, has the same singularities as
T
, and satisfies
S^{\prime}=S+[E]
, which concludes the proof of (iii).

Finally, pick a modification
\pi\colon Y\to X
. Since
\pi
has connected fibers, each positive current in
\{\pi^{\star}T\}
is of the form
\pi^{\star}S
with
0\leq S\in\{T\}
, and
S
has the same singularities as
T
iff
\pi^{\star}S
has the same singularities as
\pi^{\star}T
. Since
\int_{X}S^{n}=\int_{Y}(\pi^{\star}S)^{n}
, we get (iv).
∎

Remark 3.4
.

The analogue of (
3.3
) for
\overline{\mathrm{vol}}
holds when
T>0
(with a constant depending on
T
), but is unclear in general.

We shall in fact mostly consider the upper and lower volumes of currents with analytic singularities (see §
1.3
), which basically reduces to the smooth case thanks to the following consequence of Proposition
3.3
(iii),(iv).

Lemma 3.5
.

Assume
T\geq 0
is quasi-closed with analytic singularities, and pick a modification
\pi\colon Y\to X
such that
\pi^{\star}T=\beta+[E]
with
\beta\geq 0
smooth and
E
an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor. Then
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(T)=\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\beta)
and
\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(T)=\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\beta)
.

Building on
[
GL22
]
, we infer the following monotonicity property.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
T_{1},T_{2}\geq 0
be quasi-closed positive currents with analytic singularities.
If
T_{1}\leq T_{2}
, then
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(T_{1})\leq\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(T_{2})
and
\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(T_{1})\leq\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(T_{2})
.

Proof.

After passing to a joint resolution of singularities of
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
we may assume
T_{i}=\beta_{i}+[E_{i}]
with
\beta_{i}\geq 0
smooth and
E_{i}
an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor, and we then have
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(T_{i})=\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\beta_{i})
(see Lemma
3.5
). Then
T_{1}\leq T_{2}
implies
\beta_{1}+[E_{1}]\leq\beta_{2}+[E_{2}]
, and hence
\beta_{1}\leq\beta_{2}
. By
[
GL22
, Proposition 3.2]
we get
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\beta_{1})\leq\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\beta_{2})
and
\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\beta_{1})\leq\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\beta_{2})
, which concludes the proof.
∎

While we are not able at the moment to extend this result to arbitrary quasi-closed positive currents, quasi-psh envelopes enable us the following related monotonicity result in the general case.

Proposition 3.7
.

Fix smooth real
(1,1)
-forms
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}
and
\varphi_{1}\in\PSH(\theta_{1})
,

\varphi_{2}\in\PSH(\theta_{2})
with
\varphi_{1}\leq\varphi_{2}
and
\theta_{1}\leq\theta_{2}
.
Then the positive currents
T_{1}=\theta_{1}+dd^{c}\varphi_{1}
,
T_{2}=\theta_{2}+dd^{c}\varphi_{2}
satisfy

\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(T_{1})\leq\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(T_{2}),\quad\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(T_{1})\leq\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(T_{2}).

In particular, the upper and lower volumes of any quasi-closed positive current
T
, which only depend on its
dd^{c}
-class and singularity class, are further monotone increasing functions of the singularity class.

Proof.

Pick
\psi_{2}\in\PSH(\theta_{2})
such that
\psi_{2}=\varphi_{2}+O(1)
, and set

\varphi_{t}:=\env_{\theta_{1}}(\min\{\varphi_{1}+t,\psi_{2}\})

for
t>0
. Using
\varphi_{1}\leq\varphi_{2}=\psi_{2}+O(1)
we get
\varphi_{t}\in\PSH(\theta_{1})
,
\varphi_{t}=\varphi_{1}+O(1)
, and hence
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(T_{1})\leq\int_{X}(\theta_{1}+dd^{c}\varphi_{t})^{n}
. By Theorem
2.9
, we further have

\displaystyle(\theta_{1}+dd^{c}\varphi_{t})^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\one_{\{\varphi_{t}=\varphi_{1}+t\}}(\theta_{1}+dd^{c}\varphi_{1})^{n}+\one_{\{\varphi_{t}=\psi_{2}\}}(\theta_{2}+dd^{c}v_{t})^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\one_{\{\varphi_{1}+t\leq\psi_{2}\}}(\theta_{1}+dd^{c}\varphi_{1})^{n}+\one_{\{\varphi_{t}=\psi_{2}\}}(\theta_{2}+dd^{c}\psi_{2})^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\one_{\{\varphi_{1}\leq\psi_{2}-t\}}(\theta_{1}+dd^{c}\varphi_{1})^{n}+(\theta_{2}+dd^{c}\psi_{2})^{n}.

Integrating over
X
and letting
t\to+\infty
, we obtain
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(T_{1})\leq\int_{X}(\theta_{2}+dd^{c}\psi_{2})^{n}
. Taking the infimum over
\psi_{2}
we obtain the first inequality.

Next pick
\psi_{1}\in\PSH(\theta_{1})
such that
\psi_{1}=\varphi_{1}+O(1)
is bounded, and set

\psi_{t}:=\max\{\psi_{1},\varphi_{2}-t\}

for
t>0
. Then
\psi_{t}\in\PSH(\theta_{2})
,
\psi_{t}=\varphi_{2}+O(1)
, and
\psi_{t}\searrow\psi_{1}
as
t\to+\infty
. By lower-semicontinuity of non-pluripolar masses (see Proposition
2.5
), we infer

\int_{X}(\theta_{2}+dd^{c}\psi_{1})^{n}\leq\liminf_{t\to+\infty}\int_{X}(\theta_{2}+dd^{c}\psi_{t})^{n}\leq\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(T_{2})

Taking supremum over all
\psi_{1}
concludes the proof.
∎

3.2.
The lower and upper volume of a
dd^{c}
-class

3.2.1.
The case of a big
dd^{c}
-class

Recall from §
1.4
that a
dd^{c}
-class
\{\theta\}\in\mathrm{BC}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
is big if it contains a strictly positive current, which can further be chosen to have analytic singularities (see Theorem
1.8
).

Definition 3.8
.

The
lower volume
and
upper volume
of a big
dd^{c}
-class
\{\theta\}\in\mathrm{BC}^{1,1}(X)
are respectively defined as

(3.4)

\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\}):=\sup_{0\leq T\in\{\theta\}}\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(T),\quad\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\}):=\sup_{0\leq T\in\{\theta\}}\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(T).

where
T
ranges over all positive currents in
\{\theta\}
.

Note that

0\leq\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})\leq\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})=\sup_{\varphi\in\PSH(\theta)}\int_{X}(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}<\infty,

see (
3.1
) and Lemma
2.4
. By (
3.2
), we have

\{\theta\}\text{ big }\Longrightarrow\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})>0,

see §
3.2.3
for a discussion of the analogous property for
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}
.

Lemma 3.9
.

For any positive current
T_{\min}\in\{\theta\}
with minimal singularities, we have

\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})=\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(T_{\min}),\quad\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})=\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(T_{\min}).

If
\theta
is further closed, then

\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})=\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})=\int_{X}T_{\min}^{n}.

The last point ensures compatibility of Definition
3.8
with
[
Bou02
,
BEGZ10
]
for usual (closed) Bott–Chern classes
\{\theta\}\in H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.7
, the upper and lower volume of
T\in\{\theta\}
are both monotone increasing functions of the singularity class. This proves the first point, and the second one now follows from Theorem
2.8
(see Example
3.2
).
∎

Lemma 3.10
.

For any big class
\{\theta\}\in\mathrm{BC}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
, we have

\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})=\sup_{T}\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(T),\quad\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})=\sup_{T}\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(T)

where
T
ranges over all positive currents in
\{\theta\}
with analytic singularities.

Proof.

Pick
\varphi_{\min}\in\PSH(\theta)
with minimal singularities, and
\rho\in\PSH(\theta)
with analytic singularities such that
\theta+dd^{c}\rho\geq\omega_{X}
(after perhaps scaling
\omega_{X}
). By Theorem
1.8
, there exists a sequence
\varphi_{j}\in\PSH(\theta+\varepsilon_{j}\omega_{X})
with analytic singularities decreasing to
\varphi_{\min}
, where
\varepsilon_{j}\searrow 0_{+}
. Set
\psi_{j}:=(1+\varepsilon_{j})^{-1}(\varphi_{j}+\varepsilon_{j}\rho)
and
T_{j}:=\theta+dd^{c}\psi_{j}
. Then
0\leq T_{j}\in\{\theta\}
has analytic singularities, and

\theta+\varepsilon_{j}\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi_{j}\leq(1+\varepsilon_{j})T_{j}

By Proposition
3.6
and Proposition
3.7
, this yields

\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})=\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi_{\min})\leq\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\theta+\varepsilon_{j}\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi_{j})\leq(1+\varepsilon_{j})^{n}\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(T_{j}).

Thus
\limsup_{j}\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(T_{j})\geq\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})
, and similarly for
\overline{\mathrm{vol}}
.
∎

Proposition 3.11
.

For all big classes
\{\theta\},\{\theta^{\prime}\}\in\mathrm{BC}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
, we have

\theta\leq\theta^{\prime}\Longrightarrow\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})\leq\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta^{\prime}\}),\quad\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})\leq\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta^{\prime}\}).

Furthermore, the upper and lower volume functions are continuous on the big cone of
\mathrm{BC}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

The first point follows from Proposition
3.7
. Pick a convergence sequence of big classes
\{\theta_{j}\}\to\{\theta\}
, so that
\theta_{j}\to\theta
smoothly for some choice of representatives. Then
-\varepsilon_{j}\omega_{X}\leq\theta_{j}-\theta\leq\varepsilon_{j}\omega_{X}
with
\varepsilon_{j}\to 0
. By the first point,
it therefore suffices to show

\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\pm\varepsilon\omega_{X}\})\to\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\}),\quad\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\pm\varepsilon\omega_{X}\})\to\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})

as
\varepsilon\to 0
. To see this, pick a strictly positive current with analytic singularities
S\in\{\theta\}
. We may assume
S\geq\omega_{X}
. For any
0<\varepsilon<1
and
T\in\{\theta+\varepsilon\omega_{X}\}
with analytic singularities,

T_{\varepsilon}:=(1-\varepsilon)(T-\varepsilon\omega_{X})+\varepsilon S=(1-\varepsilon)T+\varepsilon(S-\omega_{X})+\varepsilon^{2}\omega_{X}

is then a positive current with analytic singularities in
\{\theta\}
. Since
T_{\varepsilon}\geq(1-\varepsilon)T
, Proposition
3.6
yields
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})\geq\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(T_{\varepsilon})\geq(1-\varepsilon)^{n}\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(T)
. Taking the supremum over
T
we get

\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta+\varepsilon\omega_{X}\})\geq\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})\geq(1-\varepsilon)^{n}\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta+\varepsilon\omega_{X}\}),

by Lemma
3.10
, and hence
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta+\varepsilon\omega_{X}\})\to\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})
. To prove
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta-\varepsilon\omega_{X}\})\to\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})
we pick a positive current with analytic singularities
T\in\{\theta\}
. Then

S_{\varepsilon}:=(1-\varepsilon)T+\varepsilon S-\varepsilon\omega_{X}\geq(1-\varepsilon)T\geq 0

is a positive current with analytic singularities in
\{\theta-\varepsilon\omega_{X}\}
. By Proposition
3.6
, we infer
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta-\varepsilon\omega_{X}\})\geq(1-\varepsilon)^{n}\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(T)
. Taking the supremum over
T
yields

\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})\geq\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta-\varepsilon\omega_{X}\})\geq(1-\varepsilon)^{n}\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\}),

thus
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta-\varepsilon\omega_{X}\})\to\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})
.
The proof of
\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\pm\varepsilon\omega_{X}\})\to\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})
is similar.
∎

We conclude this section with the following Fujita-type approximation result (compare
[
Bou02
, Theorem 1.4]
).

Proposition 3.12
.

For any big class
\{\theta\}\in\mathrm{BC}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
we have

\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})=\sup_{Y}\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\omega_{Y}),\quad\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})=\sup_{Y}\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\omega_{Y})

where the suprema range over all modifications
\pi\colon Y\to X
and decompositions

\{\pi^{\star}\theta\}=\{\omega_{Y}\}+\{E\}

with
\omega_{Y}>0
smooth and
E
an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor.

Proof.

Pick a modification
\pi\colon Y\to X
and a decomposition

\{\pi^{\star}\theta\}=\{\omega_{Y}\}+\{E\}

with
\omega_{Y}>0
smooth and
E
an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor. Then Proposition
3.14
yields

\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\omega_{Y})=\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\omega_{Y}\})\leq\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\pi^{\star}\theta\})=\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\}),

where the last equality holds by Proposition
3.3
(iv).

Conversely, pick
\varepsilon>0
. By the first part of the proof, we can find
0\leq T\in\{\theta\}
with analytic singularities such that
\vol(\{\theta\})<\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(T)+\varepsilon
and
T\geq\omega_{X}
, after rescaling
\omega_{X}
. Pick a modification
\pi\colon Y\to X
such that
\pi^{\star}T
has divisorial singularities, i.e.
\pi^{\star}T=\beta+[E]
with
\beta
smooth and
E
an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor. Then
T\geq\omega_{X}
implies
\pi^{\star}T\geq\pi^{\star}\omega_{X}
, and hence
\beta\geq\pi^{\star}\omega_{X}
.

After passing to a higher modification, we may assume that
\pi
is obtained as a finite sequence of blowups with smooth centers, so that there exists a
\pi
-exceptional
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
D\geq 0
on
Y
such that
-D
is
\pi
-ample. We can then find a smooth closed
(1,1)
-form
\theta_{D}\in\{D\}
such that
\pi^{\star}\omega_{X}-c\theta_{D}>0
for all
c>0
small enough. Then

\{\pi^{\star}\theta\}=\{\pi^{\star}T\}=\{\omega_{c}\}+\{E+cD\},

with
\omega_{c}:=\beta-c\theta_{D}\geq\pi^{\star}\omega_{X}-c\theta_{D}>0
for
0<c\ll 1
. By Lemma
3.9
, we further have
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\omega_{c})=\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\omega_{c}\})
. By Proposition
3.11
, this converges to
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\beta\})=\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\beta)=\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(T)
as
c\to 0
, and hence
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})<\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\omega_{c})+\varepsilon
for
c
small enough. The result for
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}
follows. The same argument applies for the upper volume
\overline{\mathrm{vol}}
, finishing the proof.
∎

3.2.2.
General
dd^{c}
-classes

Assume now that
\{\theta\}\in\mathrm{BC}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
is psef. Then
\{\theta+\varepsilon\omega_{X}\}
is big for each
\varepsilon>0
, and its lower and upper volumes are both monotone increasing functions of
\varepsilon
(see Proposition
3.11
). We may thus introduce:

Definition 3.13
.

The
lower volume
and
upper volume
of an arbitrary class
\{\theta\}\in\mathrm{BC}^{1,1}(X)
are respectively defined by setting

(3.5)

\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\}):=\inf_{\varepsilon>0}\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta+\varepsilon\omega_{X}\})=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta+\varepsilon\omega_{X}\}),

(3.6)

\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\}):=\inf_{\varepsilon>0}\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta+\varepsilon\omega_{X}\})=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta+\varepsilon\omega_{X}\})

if
\{\theta\}
is psef, and
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})=\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\}):=0
otherwise.

Proposition
3.11
implies that the definition is independent of the choice of
\omega_{X}
,
and is compatible with the big case.

Proposition 3.14
.

Pick
\{\theta\}\in\mathrm{BC}^{1,1}(X)
. Then:

(i)

for any modification
\pi\colon Y\to X
, we have

\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\pi^{\star}\theta\})=\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\}),\quad\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\pi^{\star}\theta\})=\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\});

(ii)

for any
\{\theta^{\prime}\}\in\mathrm{BC}^{1,1}(X)
such that
\{\theta^{\prime}\}-\{\theta\}
is psef, we have

\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta^{\prime}\}\geq\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\}),\quad\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta^{\prime}\}\geq\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\});

(iii)

if
\theta\geq 0
then
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})=\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\theta)
.

Proof.

To prove (i), we may replace
\theta
with
\theta+\varepsilon\omega_{X}
and assume that
\{\theta\}
and
\{\pi^{\star}\theta\}
are big. The result is then a direct consequence of Proposition
3.3
(iv).

To prove (ii), we may assume that
\{\theta\}
is psef, as the result is otherwise trivial. By (
3.5
), we may further replace
\theta,\theta^{\prime}
with
\theta+2\varepsilon\omega_{X},\theta^{\prime}+\varepsilon\omega_{X}
with
\varepsilon>0
, and hence assume that
\{\theta\},\{\theta^{\prime}\}
and
\{\theta^{\prime}-\theta\}
are big. Pick a strictly positive current
S\in\{\theta^{\prime}-\theta\}
with analytic singularities. For each
0\leq T\in\{\theta\}
with analytic singularities,
0\leq T+S\in\{\theta^{\prime}\}
has analytic singularities as well. By Proposition
3.6
, we infer
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta^{\prime}\})\geq\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(T+S)\geq\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(T)
, and taking the supremum over
T
yields
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta^{\prime}\})\geq\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})
. The same argument applies to
\overline{\mathrm{vol}}
, proving (ii).

Next finally
\theta\geq 0
. When
\{\theta\}
is big, the equality
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})=\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\theta)
follows from
Lemma
3.9
. In the general case, we thus have
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta+\varepsilon\omega_{X}\})=\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\theta+\varepsilon\omega_{X})
for
\varepsilon>0
. Now
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\theta)\leq\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\theta+\varepsilon\omega_{X})\leq\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\theta)+O(\varepsilon)
by Proposition
3.6
and (
3.3
), hence
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\theta+\varepsilon\omega_{X})\to\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\theta)
, and (iii) follows.
∎

Remark 3.15
.

When
\{\theta\}
is further assumed to be big, the analogue of (iii) also holds for
\overline{\mathrm{vol}}
, by Lemma
3.9
. However, we do not know whether this extends to the psef case, as
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\theta+\varepsilon\omega_{X})=\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\theta)
is then unclear.

Proposition 3.16
.

The lower and upper volume functions coincide on the subspace

H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})\subset\mathrm{BC}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})

of usual (closed) Bott–Chern classes. If
\{\theta\}\in H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})
is further nef, then

\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})=\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})=\int_{X}\theta^{n}.

We simply denote by

\vol\colon H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

the common restriction of
\underline{\mathrm{vol}},\overline{\mathrm{vol}}
.

Proof.

Assume
\theta
is closed. If
\{\theta\}
is not psef, then
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})=\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})=0
by definition. Assume that
\{\theta\}
is psef, and pick
0\leq T_{\varepsilon}\in\{\theta+\varepsilon\omega_{X}\}
with minimal singularities for each
\varepsilon>0
. Since
\theta
is closed, we have
\Delta_{\theta}=0
(see (
2.7
)), and hence
\Delta_{\theta+\varepsilon\omega_{X}}=O(\varepsilon)
. By Theorem
2.8
, we infer
\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(T_{\varepsilon})\leq\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(T_{\varepsilon})+O(\varepsilon)
, i.e.

\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta+\varepsilon\omega_{X}\})\leq\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta+\varepsilon\omega_{X}\})+O(\varepsilon),

by Lemma
3.9
. Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
proves
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})=\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})
.

Assume next
\{\theta\}
is further nef. Rescaling
\omega_{X}
we can assume
\theta\leq\omega_{X}
. For each smooth function
\varphi\in\PSH(\theta+\varepsilon\omega_{X})\subset\PSH(X,2\omega_{X})
, the bounded mass property and Stokes’ theorem yield

\displaystyle\int_{X}(\theta+\varepsilon\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\varepsilon^{k}\int_{X}(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n-k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{k}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\theta^{n}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\varepsilon^{k}\int_{X}(\theta-2\omega_{X}+2\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n-k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{k}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\theta^{n}+O(\varepsilon).

Taking the infimum over all
\varphi
yields

\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta+\varepsilon\omega_{X}\})=\int_{X}\theta^{n}+O(\varepsilon),

hence
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})=\int_{X}\theta^{n}
.
∎

3.2.3.
The positive volume property

As a consequence of Proposition
3.14
we have:

Corollary 3.17
.

We either have
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}\equiv 0
on
\mathrm{BC}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
, or
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}>0
on the big cone.

Proof.

Assume there exists
\{\theta_{0}\}\in\mathrm{BC}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
with
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta_{0}\})>0
. For any big class
\{\theta\}
,
t\{\theta\}-\{\theta_{0}\}
is big for
t\gg 1
, and hence
t^{n}\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})\geq\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta_{0}\})>0
, by Proposition
3.14
.
∎

Definition 3.18
.

We say that
X
has the
positive volume property
if
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}>0
on the big cone of
\mathrm{BC}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
.

By Proposition
3.14
and Corollary
3.17
, the positive volume property holds iff
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\omega)>0
for some Hermitian form
\omega>0
on
X
.

Like the bounded mass property, the positive volume property is bimeromorphically invariant (by Proposition
3.14
, or
[
GL22
, Theorem 3.7]
). It always holds in dimension
n=2
;
when
n=3
it holds for Vaisman manifolds
[
AGL23
, Proposition 3.10]
, as well as for some nilmanifolds
[
AGL23
, Theorem 4.4]

and solvmanifolds
[
AGL23
, Theorem 4.7]
.
No counterexample is known at the moment of this writing.

Recall that the bounded mass property is inherited by any submanifold. Here we
establish a ‘dual’ result for the positive volume property:

Proposition 3.19
.

Assume
\pi\colon X\to Y
is a holomorphic submersion onto a compact complex manifold.
If
X
has the positive volume property, then so does
Y
.

Proof.

Set
d:=\dim Y
, and pick a hermitian form
\beta_{X}
on
X
. We claim that there exists a semipositive
(1,1)
-form
\theta\geq 0
on
X
that is (strictly) positive along the fibers of
\pi
, and satisfies
\theta^{n-d+1}=0
. Indeed, pick a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-projection
p\colon T_{X}\to T_{X/Y}
onto the holomorphic subbundle
T_{X/Y}=\ker d\pi\hookrightarrow T_{X}
. The claim then holds with

\theta(v,w):=\beta_{X}(p(v),p(w))
. Given a Hermitian form
\omega_{Y}>0
on
Y
, we then have
\omega_{X}:=\pi^{\star}\omega_{Y}+\theta>0
. For any smooth
\varphi\in\PSH(\omega_{Y})
, we further have

(\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\pi^{\star}\varphi)^{n}=(\pi^{\star}(\omega_{Y}+dd^{c}\varphi)+\theta)^{n}={n\choose d}\pi^{\star}(\omega_{Y}+dd^{c}\varphi)^{d}\wedge\theta^{n-d},

and hence

0<\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\omega_{X})\leq{n\choose d}\int_{Y}f(\omega_{Y}+dd^{c}\varphi)^{d}\leq C\int_{Y}(\omega_{Y}+dd^{c}\varphi)^{d},

with
0<f:=\pi_{\star}\theta^{n-d}\in C^{\infty}(Y)
(see for instance
[
Dem
, Section 2.15]
).
We infer
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\omega_{Y}))>0
, and the result follows.
∎

3.3.
Characterization of the Fujiki class

Generalizing
[
Bou02
, Corollary 7.11]
(which dealt with closed classes on a Kähler manifold), we next show:

Theorem 3.20
.

The lower volume function
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}\colon\mathrm{BC}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is continuous. In particular, it vanishes outside of the big cone.

In other words, the last point states that
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})>0
implies
\{\theta\}
is big. This extends
[
GL22
, Theorem 4.6]
, which dealt with nef classes. Note that the analogue of Theorem
3.20
fails for the upper volume:

Example 3.21
.

By
[
GL22
, Example 3.5]
, any product
X=C\times C^{\prime}
of two curves carries a semipositive
(1,1)
-form
\theta
such that
\int_{X}\theta^{2}>\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})=0
. Since
X
has the positive volume property (in fact
X
is Kähler, hence
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\omega_{X})>0
)
\{\theta\}
is therefore not big, while
\overline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})\geq\int_{X}\theta^{2}>0
.

Proof of Theorem
3.20
.

The second point formally follows from the first one, since any
\{\theta\}
on the boundary of the psef cone satisfies
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta-\varepsilon\omega_{X}\})=0
for
\varepsilon>0
(see Definition
3.13
. However, we actually first show the former, by adapting (as in
[
GL22
, Theorem 4.6]
) a strategy due to Chiose
[
Chi13
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Assume thus that
\{\theta\}\in\mathrm{BC}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
satisfies
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})>0
. By Definition
3.13
,
\{\theta\}
is necessarily psef, and we need to show that it is big, i.e. that
\{\theta-\delta\omega_{X}\}
is psef for some
\delta>0
. By Hahn–Banach (see Lemma
1.17
), this is equivalent to showing
\int_{X}\theta\wedge\gamma\geq\delta
for each
dd^{c}
-closed
(n-1,n-1)
-form
\gamma>0
, normalized so that the volume form

\mu:=\omega_{X}\wedge\gamma

has mass
1
. Applying Proposition
3.12
to the big classes
\{\theta+\varepsilon\omega_{X}\}
,
\varepsilon>0
, yields a sequence of modifications
\pi_{j}\colon Y_{j}\to X
and decompositions

\pi_{j}^{\star}\{\theta+\varepsilon_{j}\omega_{X}\}=\{\omega_{j}\}+\{E_{j}\}

where
\omega_{j}
a Hermitian form on
Y_{j}
,
E_{j}
an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor,
\varepsilon_{j}\to 0
and
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\omega_{j})\to\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})
.

By
[
GL23
, Theorem 4.1]
, for each
j
there exists a positive current
0\leq S_{j}\in\{\omega_{j}\}
with bounded potentials, smooth outside the exceptional locus of
\pi_{j}
, such that

S_{j}^{n}=c_{j}\pi_{j}^{*}\mu

for some constant
c_{j}>0
. Note that
c_{j}=\int_{X}S_{j}^{n}\geq\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\omega_{j})
,
and hence

(3.7)

\liminf_{j}c_{j}\geq\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\}).

Denote by
T_{j}\in\{\theta+\varepsilon_{j}\omega_{X}\}
the unique positive current such that
\pi_{j}^{\star}T_{j}=S_{j}+[E_{j}]
. Then
T_{j}
is smooth on a nonempty Zariski open set
\Omega_{j}\subset X
, and satisfies
T_{j}^{n}=c_{j}\mu
.

By Lemma
2.4
we have
\int_{X}T_{j}^{n-1}\wedge\omega_{X}\leq M
for a constant
M>0
only depending on
\{\theta\}
and
\{\omega_{X}\}
. Introduce the set

A_{j}:=\left\{x\in\Omega_{j}\mid T_{j}^{n-1}\wedge\omega_{X}\geq 2M\mu\text{ at }x\right\},

and note that

\mu(A_{j})\leq\frac{1}{2M}\int_{A_{j}}T_{j}^{n-1}\wedge\omega_{X}\leq\frac{1}{2},

and hence
\mu(\Omega_{j}\setminus A_{j})\geq\tfrac{1}{2}
.
At each point of
\Omega_{j}\setminus A_{j}
we have

T_{j}^{n-1}\wedge\omega_{X}\leq 2M\mu=\frac{2M}{c_{j}}T_{j}^{n},

which implies
T_{j}\geq\frac{c_{j}}{2nM}\omega_{X}
, and hence
T_{j}\wedge\gamma\geq\frac{c_{j}}{2nM}\mu
. Since
\gamma
is
dd^{c}
-closed, we infer

\int_{X}(\theta+\varepsilon_{j}\omega_{X})\wedge\gamma=\int_{X}T_{j}\wedge\gamma\geq\frac{c_{j}}{2nM}\mu(\Omega_{j}\setminus A_{j})\geq\frac{c_{j}}{4nM},

and (
3.7
) yields the desired estimate

\int_{X}\theta\wedge\gamma\geq\delta:=\frac{\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})}{4nM}>0.

We now show that the lower volume function is continuous on
\mathrm{BC}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
. By Proposition
3.11
, it is continuous on the big cone, and it is identically zero outside the psef cone. Assume
\{\theta\}\in\mathrm{BC}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
is psef but not big. By the first part of the proof,
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})=0
, and we thus need to show
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta_{j}\})\to 0
for any smoothly convergent sequence
\theta_{j}\to\theta
. We have
\theta_{j}\leq\theta+\varepsilon_{j}\omega_{X}
with
\varepsilon_{j}\to 0
, and hence
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta_{j}\})\leq\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta+\varepsilon_{j}\omega_{X}\})
, by Proposition
3.14
. By definition,
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta+\varepsilon_{j}\omega_{X}\})
converges to
\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})=0
, and we are done.
∎

Remark 3.22
.

The above proof yields, more precisely, that
\{\theta\}
is ‘more psef’ than
\delta\{\omega_{X}\}
for

\delta:=\underline{\mathrm{vol}}(\{\theta\})/4nM
where

M:=\sup\left\{\int_{X}T^{n-1}\wedge\omega_{X}\mid 0\leq T\in\{\theta\}\right\}.

A simple consequence is the following interesting characterization of the Fujiki class, which establishes
a transcendental version of the Grauert–Riemenschneider conjecture:

Corollary 3.23
.

Let
X
be a compact complex manifold
X
of dimension
n
.
The following properties are equivalent:

(i)

X
is a Fujiki manifold,
i.e.
bimeromorphic to a compact Kähler manifold;

(ii)

X
has the bounded mass property and it admits a closed positive
(1,1)
-current
T
such that
\int_{X}T^{n}>0
.

Proof.

The implication (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) follows from Proposition
1.20
.
The existence of a closed positive
(1,1)
-current
T=\theta+dd^{c}\varphi
such that
\int_{X}T^{n}>0

is equivalent to
\vol(\{\theta\})>0
, by definition and Example
3.2
.
By Theorem
3.20
, this implies that
\{\theta\}
is big, and it then well-known (see for instance Proposition
3.12
) that
X
admits a Kähler modification, and hence is Fujiki.
∎

4.
Monge-Ampère equations in big classes

In this section, we no longer asssume that
X
has the bounded mass property. We pick a big class
\{\theta\}\in\mathrm{BC}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
, fix a
\theta
-psh function

\rho\leq 0
with analytic singularities such that
\theta+dd^{c}\rho\geq\omega_{X}
(after rescaling
\omega_{X}
), and denote by
\Omega\subset X
the Zariski open set where
\rho
is smooth. We also fix a (smooth, positive) volume form
dV
on
X
, and denote by

\|f\|_{p}:=\left(\int_{X}|f|^{p}dV\right)^{1/p}

the
L^{p}
-norm of a measurable function
f
. We are going to solve complex Monge–Ampère equations in
\Omega
,
extending
[
BEGZ10
, Theorem B]
to the Hermitian setting.

4.1.
The domination principle

We start by establishing a useful local maximum principle.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
U\Subset\Omega
be an open subset that is biholomorphic to a ball in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Let
u,v
be bounded
\theta
-psh functions in
U
such that
\liminf_{z\to\partial U}(u-v)(z)\geq 0
. If
(\theta+dd^{c}u)^{n}\leq(\theta+dd^{c}v)^{n}
in
\{u<v\}
, then
u\geq v
in
U
.

Proof.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
and set
v_{\varepsilon}=\max\{u,v-\varepsilon\}
. Then
u=v_{\varepsilon}
near the boundary of
U
, and the Bedford–Taylor maximum principle (compare Proposition
2.11
) thus yields

(\theta+dd^{c}v_{\varepsilon})^{n}\geq\one_{\{v\geq u+\varepsilon\}}(\theta+dd^{c}v)^{n}+\one_{\{u>v-\varepsilon\}}(\theta+dd^{c}u)^{n}

on
U
. Since
(\theta+dd^{c}v)^{n}\geq(\theta+dd^{c}u)^{n}
on
\{v\geq u+\varepsilon\}\subset\{v>u\}
, we infer

(\theta+dd^{c}u)^{n}\leq(\theta+dd^{c}v)^{n}.

Let
\varphi:=\env^{\star}(u-v_{\varepsilon})
denote the largest psh function on
U
lying below
u-v_{\varepsilon}
outside a pluripolar set, and
let
h
be a defining psh function for
U
:
h=0
on
\partial U
and
h<0
in
U
.
Since
u-v_{\varepsilon}\leq 0
with equality near
\partial U
, we can find a large constant
A>0
such that

Ah\leq u-v_{\varepsilon}
. Thus
Ah\leq\varphi
, hence
\varphi
vanishes on
\partial U
. Since
\varphi+v_{\varepsilon}\leq u
, and the two functions are
\theta
-psh, by the maximum principle we have

{\bf 1}_{D}(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi+dd^{c}v_{\varepsilon})^{n}\leq{\bf 1}_{D}(\theta+dd^{c}u)^{n},

where
D=\{\varphi+v_{\varepsilon}=u\}
is the contact set.
Using

(\theta+dd^{c}u)^{n}\leq(\theta+dd^{c}v_{\varepsilon})^{n}\leq(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi+dd^{c}v_{\varepsilon})^{n},

we obtain

{\bf 1}_{D}(dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}=0
.
Since
(dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}
is supported on
D
, we infer that
(dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}=0
, and the
Bedford–Taylor comparison principle ensures that
\varphi=0
.
Thus
u\geq v_{\varepsilon}=\max\{u,v-\varepsilon\}
outside a pluripolar set, and hence everywhere, and letting
\varepsilon\to 0
we conclude that
u\geq v
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
u,v
be
\theta
-psh functions on
X
such that
\rho\leq\min\{u,v\}
and

v\leq u+C
for some constant
C
.
If

{\bf 1}_{\{u<v\}}(\theta+dd^{c}u)^{n}\leq c{\bf 1}_{\{u<v\}}(\theta+dd^{c}v)^{n},

for some
c\in[0,1)
, then
u\geq v
.

This domination principle contains a great deal of information.
While the statement is similar to
[
GL23
, Proposition 1.12]
, the proof
we provide here is very different.

Proof.

Fix
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
such that
(1-\varepsilon)^{n}>c
and set
v_{\varepsilon}:=(1-\varepsilon)v+\varepsilon\rho
. Since
\rho
satisfies
\theta+dd^{c}\rho\geq\omega_{X}
, we have

\theta+dd^{c}\lambda\rho=\lambda(\theta+dd^{c}\rho)+(1-\lambda)\theta\geq\omega_{X}+(1-\lambda)\theta.

Thus, when
|\lambda-1|
is sufficiently small we have
\theta+dd^{c}\lambda\rho\geq\omega_{X}/2
. Replacing
\rho
with
\lambda\rho-1
for some
\lambda>1
we can assume

\lim_{x\to\partial\Omega}(u-\rho)=+\infty\;\text{and}\;\rho-v\leq-1.

We are going to show that
u\geq v_{\varepsilon}
; the result then follows by letting
\varepsilon\to 0
.
Assume by contradiction that

\inf_{\Omega}(u-v_{\varepsilon})=m<0.

Let
(x_{j})
be a sequence in
\Omega
converging to
x_{0}\in\Omega
such that
\lim_{j}(u-v_{\varepsilon})(x_{j})=m
. Take a holomorphic coordinate chart
(B,z)
around
x_{0}
so that
B=\{|z|<1\}\Subset\Omega
, and define

\eta=-a|z|^{2}+a,

where the constant
a>0
is so small that
\frac{\omega_{X}}{2}+dd^{c}\eta\geq 0
. Setting

\varphi:=v_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon\eta+m,

we get
\liminf_{x\to\partial B}(u-\varphi)\geq 0
,

\theta+dd^{c}\varphi=(1-\varepsilon)(\theta+dd^{c}v)+\varepsilon(\theta+dd^{c}\rho+dd^{c}\eta)\geq 0

and

(\theta+dd^{c}u)^{n}\leq c(\theta+dd^{c}v)^{n}\leq(1-\varepsilon)^{n}(\theta+dd^{c}v)^{n}\leq(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}\;\text{on}\;\{u<\varphi\}

because

\{u<\varphi\}\subset\{u<v+\varepsilon(\rho-v+\eta)+m\}\subset\{u<v+m\}\subset\{u<v\},

if
a
is small enough. Lemma
4.1
yields
u\geq\varphi
, and evaluating at
x_{j}
we obtain

u(x_{j})-v_{\varepsilon}(x_{j})\geq\varepsilon\eta(x_{j})+m,

which yields a contradiction as
j\to+\infty
, since
\eta(x_{0})>0
.
∎

As a simple consequence, we get:

Corollary 4.3
.

Assume
u,v\in\PSH(\theta)
are such that
u-v
is bounded,
\min\{u,v\}\geq\rho
, and

(\theta+dd^{c}u)^{n}\leq c(\theta+dd^{c}v)^{n},

for some
c\geq 0
. Then
c\geq 1
.

4.2.
Solving complex Monge–Ampère equations

A crucial step in solving complex Monge–Ampère equations is to establish uniform a priori estimates.
Following
[
GL23
]
we construct suitable subsolutions to bound the solutions via the domination principle.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\eta\in\PSH(X,\theta)
such that
0\geq\eta\geq\rho
. Let
g\geq 0
be a measurable function on
X
such that
\|g\|_{q}\leq 1
, for some exponent
q>1
. Then there exists
v\in\PSH(X,\theta)
with
\eta\leq v\leq\eta+1
such that

(\theta+dd^{c}v)^{n}\geq mgdV

on
\Omega
, where
m
is a positive constant only depending on
q
,
(X,\omega_{X})
, and
\theta
.

Proof.

Set
h:=g(2-\rho)^{2n}
. By Hölder’s inequality, we have
\|h\|_{r}\leq C
for some
r\in(1,q)
and a uniform constant
C>0
, and
[
GL23
, Lemma 3.3]
thus yields
u\in\PSH(X,\omega_{X})
such that
-1\leq u\leq 0
, and

(\omega_{X}+dd^{c}u)^{n}\geq mhdV

with
m>0
uniformly bounded away from
0
.
Set
u_{1}:=\rho+u
. Since we assume
\theta+dd^{c}\rho\geq\omega_{X}
, we get
\theta+dd^{c}u_{1}\geq\omega_{X}+dd^{c}u\geq 0
, thus
u_{1}
is
\theta
-psh and satisfies
(\theta+dd^{c}u_{1})^{n}\geq mhdV
.

Consider now
v:=\eta+\frac{1}{1+\eta-u_{1}}
. Observe that

1\leq 1+\eta-u_{1}=1-u+\eta-\rho\leq 2-\rho
and

\displaystyle\theta+dd^{c}v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta+dd^{c}\eta-\frac{dd^{c}(\eta-u_{1})}{(1+\eta-u_{1})^{2}}+\frac{2d(\eta-u_{1})\wedge d^{c}(\eta-u_{1})}{(1+\eta-u_{1})^{3}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left[1-\frac{1}{(1+\eta-u_{1})^{2}}\right](\theta+dd^{c}\eta)+\frac{\theta+dd^{c}u_{1}}{(1+\eta-u_{1})^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\theta+dd^{c}u_{1}}{(2-\rho)^{2}}.

The function
v
is thus
\theta
-psh, with
\eta\leq v\leq\eta+1
and

(\theta+dd^{c}v)^{n}\geq\frac{(\theta+dd^{c}u_{1})^{n}}{(2-\rho)^{2n}}=mgdV.

∎

We now show that one can solve Monge-Ampère equations with respect to a big form
\theta
. We first start with the equation twisted by an exponential.

Theorem 4.5
.

Assume
0\leq f\in L^{p}(X)
with
p>1
and
\|f\|_{p}>0
.
For each
\lambda>0
, we can then find a unique

\varphi_{\lambda}\in PSH(X,\theta)
such that
\varphi_{\lambda}=V_{\theta}+O(1)
and

\left(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi_{\lambda}\right)^{n}=e^{\lambda\varphi_{\lambda}}fdV

on
\Omega
. Furthermore,
\sup_{X}|\varphi_{\lambda}-V_{\theta}|\leq C
for a constant
C>0
only depending on
(X,\omega_{X})
,
\theta
,
p
,
\|f\|_{p}
and
\lambda
.

Proof.

Uniqueness follows from the domination principle. To simplify the notation, we assume that
\lambda=1
.
To prove the existence,
we approximate
V_{\theta}
by a quasi-decreasing sequence
(\psi_{j})
of
\theta
-psh functions with analytic singularities:
we first apply Demailly’s approximation (Theorem
1.8
)
to get a decreasing sequence
\tilde{\psi}_{j}\in\PSH(X,\theta+2^{-j}\omega)
converging to
V_{\theta}
, and we then take

\psi_{j}=(1-2^{-j})\tilde{\psi}_{j}+2^{-j}\rho.

Observe that

\psi_{j}\geq(1-2^{-j})V_{\theta}+2^{-j}\rho\geq\rho,

thus

\env_{\theta}^{\star}\left(\inf_{k\geq j}\psi_{k}\right)\geq(1-2^{-j})V_{\theta}+2^{-j}\rho\nearrow V_{\theta}\;\text{as}\;j\to+\infty.

Resolving the singularities of
\psi_{j}
and arguing as in the proof of Theorem
3.20
, one deduces from
[
GL23
, Theorem 3.4]
the existence of

u_{j}\in\PSH(X,\theta)
such that
u_{j}=\psi_{j}+O(1)
and

(\theta+dd^{c}u_{j})^{n}=e^{u_{j}}fdV.

By Lemma
4.4
there exists
v_{j}\in\PSH(X,\theta)
with
\psi_{j}-1\leq v_{j}\leq\psi_{j}\leq 0
such that

(\theta+dd^{c}v_{j})^{n}\geq mfdV\geq e^{v_{j}+\log m}fdV.

The domination principle thus yields a uniform constant
C^{\prime}
such that

\psi_{j}-C^{\prime}\leq v_{j}-C^{\prime}+1\leq u_{j}.

Extracting a subsequence, we can assume that
u_{j}\to u\in\PSH(X,\theta)
almost everywhere on
X
.
The above estimates yield uniform bounds
V_{\theta}-C^{\prime}\leq u\leq V_{\theta}
.

We next consider the sequences

\varphi_{j}=\env_{\theta}^{\star}\left(\inf_{k\geq j}u_{k}\right)\;\;\text{ and }\;\;\tau_{j}=\mathrm{sup}^{\star}_{k\geq j}u_{k}.

These functions are locally bounded in
\Omega

since

\min\{\varphi_{j},\tau_{j}\}\geq(1-2^{-j})V_{\theta}+2^{-j}\rho-C^{\prime}\geq\rho-C^{\prime}.

Note that
\tau_{j}
decreases pointwise to
u
, while
\varphi_{j}
increases (pointwise outside a pluripolar set)
to some function
\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)
.
The maximum principle moreover yields, in
\Omega
,

(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi_{j})^{n}\leq e^{\varphi_{j}}fdV,

and

(\theta+dd^{c}\tau_{j})^{n}\geq e^{\tau_{j}}fdV.

Letting
j\to+\infty
, we obtain
\varphi\leq u
,
\varphi=u+O(1)
, and

(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}\leq e^{\varphi}fdV,\;\;\text{ while }\;\;(\theta+dd^{c}u)^{n}\geq e^{u}fdV.

The domination principle ensures
\varphi=u
, hence

(\theta+dd^{c}u)^{n}=e^{u}fdV.

∎

Theorem 4.6
.

Assume
0\leq f\in L^{p}(X)
with
p>1
and
\|f\|_{p}>0
. Then there exists
(\varphi,c)\in\PSH(X,\theta)\times(0,+\infty)

such that
V_{\theta}-C\leq\varphi\leq V_{\theta}
and

(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}=cfdV

on
\Omega
, where

•

the constant
c>0
is uniquely determined by
f,X,\theta
, and

•

C>0
is a uniform constant that only depends on
(X,\omega_{X})
,
\theta
,
p
and
\|f\|_{p}
.

When
X
is Kähler and
\theta
is closed, this is
[
BEGZ10
, Theorem B]
.
When
\theta>0
is a Hermitian form, this result is a combination of

[
TW10
]

and

[
DK12
,
KN15
]
.
When the class
\{\theta\}\in BC^{1,1}(X)
is nef, one can further show the existence of a solution which is smooth
in
\Omega
(see
[
GL23
, Theorem B]
and
[
Dang24
, Theorem C]
).
The uniqueness of
\varphi
up to an additive constant is largely open
(see however
[
KN19
, Theorem A]
).

Proof.

The uniqueness of the constant
c
follows from the domination principle (see Corollary
4.3
).
For each
j\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
we use Theorem
4.5
to find

u_{j}\in\PSH(X,\theta)
such that
u_{j}-V_{\theta}
is bounded and

(\theta+dd^{c}u_{j})^{n}=e^{j^{-1}u_{j}}fdV.

Setting
v_{j}:=u_{j}-\sup_{X}u_{j}
and
f_{j}:=e^{j^{-1}v_{j}}f
,
the above equation becomes

(\theta+dd^{c}v_{j})^{n}=c_{j}f_{j}dV,\;\text{ where }\;c_{j}=e^{j^{-1}\sup_{X}u_{j}}.

Extracting and relabelling we can assume that
v_{j}\to v\in\PSH(X,\theta)
in
L^{1}
and
almost everywhere, hence
e^{j^{-1}v_{j}}\to 1
in
L^{1}(X,dV)
.

Pick a Gauduchon metric
\omega_{G}
on
X
and write
\omega_{G}^{n}=hdV
. The mixed Monge-Ampère inequality gives

(\theta+dd^{c}u_{j})\wedge\omega_{G}^{n-1}\geq e^{j^{-1}v_{j}/n}c_{j}^{1/n}f^{1/n}h^{1-1/n}dV.

Integrating this inequality over
X
yields an upper bound for
c_{j}
.

Fix a small constant
\varepsilon>0
such that
e^{-\varepsilon v_{j}}f
is uniformly in
L^{q}(X,dV)
for some
1<q<p
. This can be done thanks to the uniform version of Skoda’s integrability theorem (see
[
GZ
]
,
[
Zer01
]
). Using Lemma
4.4
, we can find
\eta_{j}\in\PSH(X,\theta)
with
V_{\theta}-1\leq\eta_{j}\leq V_{\theta}
such that

(\theta+dd^{c}\eta_{j})^{n}\geq mfe^{-\varepsilon v_{j}}dV\geq mfe^{\varepsilon(\eta_{j}-v_{j})}dV.

The domination principle yields
m\leq c_{j}
and
v_{j}\geq\eta_{j}+\log m\geq V_{\theta}-C
, for a uniform constant
C>0
. Extracting a subsequence, we can assume
c_{j}\to c>0
.

Consider the following sequences

\varphi_{j}=\env_{\theta}^{\star}\left(\inf_{k\geq j}v_{k}\right),\;\tau_{j}=\mathrm{sup}^{\star}_{k\geq j}v_{k}.

Then
\min\{\varphi_{j},\tau_{j}\}\geq(1-2^{-j})V_{\theta}+2^{-j}\rho\geq\rho
. In particular, these functions are locally bounded in
\Omega
.
By the maximum principle and the minimum principle, we have

(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi_{j})^{n}\leq e^{\varepsilon(\varphi_{j}-\inf_{k\geq j}v_{k})}\sup_{k\geq j}(c_{k}f_{k})dV,

and

(\theta+dd^{c}\tau_{j})^{n}\geq e^{\varepsilon(\tau_{j}-\sup_{k\geq j}v_{k})}\inf_{k\geq j}(c_{k}f_{k})dV,

in
\Omega
.
Letting
j\to+\infty
, we obtain
V_{\theta}-C\leq\varphi\leq v\leq V_{\theta}
, while

(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}\leq e^{\varepsilon(\varphi-v)}cfdV\;\;\text{ and }\;\;(\theta+dd^{c}v)^{n}\geq e^{\varepsilon(v-v)}cfdV.

The domination principle thus gives
\varphi=v
and

(\theta+dd^{c}v)^{n}=cfdV
,
finishing the proof.
∎

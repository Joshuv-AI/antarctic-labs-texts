Restriction theorems and root systems for symmetric superspaces
Shifra Reif, Siddhartha Sahi and Vera Serganova

Abstract
. In this paper we consider those involutions θ \theta of a finite-dimensional Kac-Moody Lie superalgebra 𝔤 {\mathfrak{g}} , with associated decomposition 𝔤 = 𝔨 ⊕ 𝔭 {\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{k}}\oplus{\mathfrak{p}} , for which a Cartan subspace 𝔞 {\mathfrak{a}} in 𝔭 0 ¯ {\mathfrak{p}}_{\bar{0}} is self-centralizing in 𝔭 {\mathfrak{p}} . For such θ \theta the restriction map C θ C_{\theta} from 𝔭 {\mathfrak{p}} to 𝔞 {\mathfrak{a}} is injective on the algebra P ​ ( 𝔭 ) 𝔨 P({\mathfrak{p}})^{\mathfrak{k}} of 𝔨 {\mathfrak{k}} -invariant polynomials on 𝔭 {\mathfrak{p}} . There are five infinite families and five exceptional cases of such involutions, and for each case we explicitly determine the structure of P ​ ( 𝔭 ) 𝔨 P({\mathfrak{p}})^{\mathfrak{k}} by giving a complete set of generators for the image of C θ C_{\theta} . We also determine precisely when the restriction map R θ R_{\theta} from P ​ ( 𝔤 ) 𝔤 P({\mathfrak{g}})^{\mathfrak{g}} to P ​ ( 𝔭 ) 𝔨 P({\mathfrak{p}})^{\mathfrak{k}} is surjective. Finally we introduce the notion of a generalized restricted root system, and show that in the present setting the 𝔞 {\mathfrak{a}} -roots Δ ⁡ ( 𝔞 , 𝔤 ) \Delta({\mathfrak{a}},{\mathfrak{g}}) always form such a system.



1.
Introduction

In this paper we consider the analog of the Chevalley restriction theorem for an involution
\theta
of a finite dimensional Kac-Moody Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}+{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
. That is to say we study the structure of the algebra
P({\mathfrak{p}})^{\mathfrak{k}}
of
{\mathfrak{k}}
-invariant polynomials on
{\mathfrak{p}}
, where
{\mathfrak{k}}
is the fixed subalgebra of
\theta
and
{\mathfrak{p}}
is the
(-1)
-eigenspace, by analyzing its restriction to a Cartan subspace
{\mathfrak{a}}
in
{\mathfrak{p}}_{\bar{0}}={\mathfrak{p}}\cap{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}
. This defines a map
C_{\theta}
from
P({\mathfrak{p}})^{\mathfrak{k}}
to the polynomial algebra
P({\mathfrak{a}})
, which is injective precisely when the centralizer of
{\mathfrak{a}}
in
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}={\mathfrak{p}}\cap{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
is
0
. This is equivalent to
{\mathfrak{g}}
admitting an Iwasawa decomposition of the form
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{k}}\oplus{\mathfrak{a}}\oplus{\mathfrak{n}}
, and so we will say such a
\theta
is an Iwasawa involution. In this case
P({\mathfrak{p}})^{\mathfrak{k}}
is isomorphic to the image of
C_{\theta}
. In the rest of the paper we assume that
\theta
is an Iwasawa involution.

In Theorem A we determine the image of
C_{\theta}
for an Iwasawa involution
\theta
. More precisely we show that the image is equal to a certain subalgebra
I_{\theta}(\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\mathfrak{a}}^{*}\color[rgb]{0,0,0})
of
P({\mathfrak{a}})
defined below. Our result is completely explicit: there are five infinite families of Iwasawa involutions and five exceptional cases, and in each case we provide a complete set of generators for
I_{\theta}(\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\mathfrak{a}}^{*}\color[rgb]{0,0,0})
except for one subfamily (see Section
6.4
).

We consider also the algebra
P({\mathfrak{g}})^{\mathfrak{g}}
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
-invariant polynomials on
{\mathfrak{g}}
and its restriction to
{\mathfrak{p}}
. This defines a map
R_{\theta}
from
P({\mathfrak{g}})^{\mathfrak{g}}
to
P({\mathfrak{p}})^{\mathfrak{k}}
which is in general neither injective nor surjective. In Theorem B we show that
R_{\theta}
is in fact surjective
except
for a subfamily of one infinite family and two of the exceptional cases. Again the results are completely explicit: we determine the image of the composite restriction map
C_{\theta}\circ R_{\theta}
and show that it coincides with
I_{\theta}(\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\mathfrak{a}}^{*}\color[rgb]{0,0,0})
except in these cases.

We also determine the structure of the set
\Delta({\mathfrak{a}},{\mathfrak{g}})
of roots of
{\mathfrak{a}}
in
{\mathfrak{g}}
.
Once more we proceed in an explicit manner: we choose a Cartan subalgebra
{\mathfrak{h}}
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
which contains
{\mathfrak{a}}
, and we study the restriction of the
{\mathfrak{h}}
-roots to
{\mathfrak{a}}
.
The set
\Delta({\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{g}})
of
{\mathfrak{h}}
-roots is a generalized root system (GR system) in the sense of Serganova [Serga2], however this is not always the case for
\Delta({\mathfrak{a}},{\mathfrak{g}})
.
Therefore
it is natural to introduce the notion of a generalized restricted root system (GRR system) by relaxing one of the requirements of a GR system.
We show that
\Delta({\mathfrak{a}},{\mathfrak{g}})
is always a GRR system in Proposition
2.6
(see Section 2.2 for the precise definition).

We now describe our results more precisely, starting with the definition of
I_{\theta}(\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\mathfrak{a}}^{*}\color[rgb]{0,0,0})
. Let
W
be the Weyl group of
\Delta_{0}=\Delta({\mathfrak{a}},{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}})
and let
P({\mathfrak{a}})^{W}
be the algebra of
W
-invariant polynomials on
{\mathfrak{a}}
. We say that a root
\alpha\in\Delta({\mathfrak{a}},{\mathfrak{g}})
is
singular
if no multiple of
\alpha
is a root in
\Delta_{0}
. The root space
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha}
of a singular root is purely odd, and in fact
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha}
has dimension
\left(0|2k_{\alpha}\right)
for some integer
k_{\alpha}
. We define
I_{\theta}(\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\mathfrak{a}}^{*}\color[rgb]{0,0,0})
to be the subalgebra consisting of
W
-invariant polynomials
f\in P({\mathfrak{a}})^{W}
which satisfy the following derivative condition for every singular
\alpha

\left(D_{h_{\alpha}}\right)^{k}\left(f\right)\in\langle\alpha\rangle,\quad k=1,3,\ldots,2k_{\alpha}-1.

Here
h_{\alpha}
is the image of
\alpha
under the map
{\mathfrak{a}}^{*}\to{\mathfrak{a}}
induced by an invariant bilinear form,
D_{h_{\alpha}}
is the partial derivative along
h_{\alpha}
, and
\langle\alpha\rangle
is the principal ideal of
P({\mathfrak{a}})
generated by
\alpha
.

The main results of this paper, which hold in the setting of an Iwasawa involution
\theta
of a finite-dimensional Kac-Moody Lie superalgera
{\mathfrak{g}}
, are as follows.

Theorem A
.

The restriction map
C_{\theta}:P({\mathfrak{p}})^{\mathfrak{k}}\rightarrow P({\mathfrak{a}})
is injective and its image is
I_{\theta}(\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\mathfrak{a}}^{*}\color[rgb]{0,0,0})
.

Theorem B
.

The restriction map
R_{\theta}:P({\mathfrak{g}})^{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow P({\mathfrak{p}})^{\mathfrak{k}}
is surjective unless
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{k}})
is one of the pairs
\left(\mathfrak{osp}(2m|2n_{1}+2n_{2}),\mathfrak{osp}(m|2n_{1})\oplus\mathfrak{osp}(m|2n_{2})\right)
,
(F_{4},\mathfrak{gosp}(2|4))
or
(D(2,1,a),\mathfrak{osp}(2|2)\oplus\mathfrak{so}(2))
.

Theorem A was previously obtained in
[
AHZ
]
in a slightly different setup, with a completely different proof. Our argument is very explicit: we directly verify that Theorem A holds for every Iwasawa involution. This approach allows us to prove Theorems B and reveals a great deal of information about Iwasawa involutions, generalized restricted root systems, and root multiplicities.
We hope that this information will be useful for researchers in the general theory of Lie superalgebras, as well as those interested in the applications of Lie superalgebras. We briefly describe one specific application that we have in mind.

In
[
SV
]
Sergeev and Veselov have developed the theory of a deformed GR system, which is a GR system together with a bilinear form and a
W
-invariant multiplicity function on the roots satisfying certain “admissibility” constraints. They have used this theory to construct families of completely integrable systems, which are supersymmetric analogs of the classical Calogero-Moser-Sutherland models of mathematical physics. It is natural to ask whether there exists a similar deformation theory for a GRR system, which might lead to new integrable systems. In a sequel to this paper we intend to study this question in conjunction with the classification problem for GRR systems, and we expect that the results of the present paper will play a key role in these investigations.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we recall some generalities on roots and restricted roots for a Lie superalgebra, give the definition of a GRR system and in Proposition
2.6
prove that
\Delta({\mathfrak{a}},{\mathfrak{g}})
is GRR system. We also compare GRR systems to a similar concept (RGRS) introduced in
[
Sh2
]
. In Sections 3 and 4, we show that
C_{\theta}
is injective and that its image is contained in
I_{\theta}(\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\mathfrak{a}}^{*}\color[rgb]{0,0,0})
. Finally in Section 5, we show by explicit computation that the image of
C_{\theta}
is precisely
I_{\theta}(\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\mathfrak{a}}^{*}\color[rgb]{0,0,0})
. These computations also let us prove Theorems A and B. Our analysis reveals a fair amount of detailed information about the sets of restricted roots, and we organize this in the appendix for future reference.

2.
Supersymmetric spaces

We assume in this paper that the base field is
\mathbb{C}
.

2.1.
Restricted Root Systems

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
be a finite-dimensional Kac-Moody Lie superalgebra with a non-degenerate even bilinear form
(\cdot,\cdot)
, namely
{\mathfrak{g}}
is one of
\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
,
\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)_{m\neq n}
,
\mathfrak{psl}(n|n)
,
\mathfrak{osp}(m|2n)
,
D(2,1,a)
,
F_{4}
and
G_{3}
. Let
\theta
be an involution on
{\mathfrak{g}}
which fixes
(\cdot,\cdot)
.
Then
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{k}}\oplus{\mathfrak{p}}
where
{\mathfrak{k}}
is the subalgebra of
{\mathfrak{g}}
of
\theta
-fixed points and
{\mathfrak{p}}:=\{x\in{\mathfrak{g}}:\theta(x)=-x\}
.
Let
{\mathfrak{a}}\subseteq{\mathfrak{p}}_{\bar{0}}
be the Cartan subspace of the symmetric pair
({\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}},{\mathfrak{k}}_{\bar{0}})
, that is
{\mathfrak{a}}
is a commutative and
{\mathfrak{p}}_{\bar{0}}^{\mathfrak{a}}={\mathfrak{a}}
. We assume that there exists an Iwasawa decomposition
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{k}}\oplus{\mathfrak{a}}\oplus{\mathfrak{n}}
where
{\mathfrak{n}}:=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{x\in{\mathfrak{g}}:[a,x]=c_{a}x,c_{a}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\}
for some regular element
a\in{\mathfrak{a}}
.
This is equivalent to
{\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathfrak{a}}={\mathfrak{a}}
.
Let
{\mathfrak{h}}
be a Cartan subalgebra of
{\mathfrak{g}}
containing
{\mathfrak{a}}
and let
{\mathfrak{t}}:={\mathfrak{h}}\cap{\mathfrak{k}}
.

Note that
{\mathfrak{h}}
is
\theta
-stable. Indeed, let
h\in{\mathfrak{h}}
and write
h=h_{k}+h_{p}
where
h_{k}\in{\mathfrak{k}}
and
h_{p}\in{\mathfrak{p}}
. Then for any
a\in{\mathfrak{a}}
,
0=[a,h]=[a,h_{k}]+[a,h_{p}]
. Since
[a,h_{k}]\in{\mathfrak{p}}
and
[a,h_{p}]\in{\mathfrak{k}}
,
[a,h_{k}]=[a,h_{p}]=0
. Hence
h_{p}\in{\mathfrak{a}}\subseteq{\mathfrak{h}}
and so
h_{k}\in{\mathfrak{h}}
and
\theta(h)=h_{k}-h_{p}\in{\mathfrak{h}}
. We denote by
p_{\mathfrak{a}}:{\mathfrak{h}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{a}}
the projection with kernel
{\mathfrak{t}}
. Since
\theta
preserves
(\cdot,\cdot)
, it restricts to a nondegenerate invariant form on
{\mathfrak{a}}
and
{\mathfrak{t}}
.

We denote by
\Delta({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}})
the set of roots of
{\mathfrak{g}}
and define the set of restricted roots to be

\Delta(\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\mathfrak{a}},{\mathfrak{g}}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}):=\{\bar{\alpha}\mid_{\mathfrak{a}}\ :\bar{\alpha}\in\Delta({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}})\}\subset{\mathfrak{a}}^{*}
.
For a restricted root
\alpha
, the root space is defined by
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha}:=\{x\in{\mathfrak{g}}:[h,x]=\alpha(h)x,\ \forall h\in{\mathfrak{a}}\}
and
\dim{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha}
is called the
multiplicity
of
\alpha
.
The root space
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha}
need not be purely even or odd space.
A root
\alpha
is called
singular
if
{\mathfrak{g}}_{c\alpha}\cap{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}=\{0\}
for any
c\in\mathbb{C}
.
Note that in
\Delta({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}})
a root does not admit a multiple in
\Delta_{0}
if and only if it is isotropic. Hence all singular roots are restrictions of isotropic roots.

Since the restriction of
(\cdot,\cdot)
to
{\mathfrak{a}}
is nondengenerate,
we have a form on
{\mathfrak{a}}^{*}
, which we again define denote by
(\cdot,\cdot)
. We define the baby Weyl group to be
W=\langle s_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Delta(\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\mathfrak{a}},{\mathfrak{g}}\color[rgb]{0,0,0})_{\bar{0}}\rangle
where
\Delta(\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\mathfrak{a}},{\mathfrak{g}}\color[rgb]{0,0,0})_{\bar{0}}
is the set of roots which are restrictions of even roots, and
s_{\alpha}
is the reflection corresponding to
\alpha
.
Choose a nonzero vector
h_{\alpha}
in
({\rm Ker}\alpha)^{\perp}\subset{\mathfrak{a}}
. Note that
({\rm Ker}\alpha)^{\perp}
is one dimensional so
h_{\alpha}
is unique up to a scalar.

Remark 2.1
.

The projection
p_{\mathfrak{a}}([{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha},{\mathfrak{g}}_{-\alpha}])
is orthogonal to
{\rm Ker}\alpha
. Indeed, letting
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha}
and
y\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{-\alpha}
, since
({\mathfrak{a}},{\mathfrak{t}})=0
, we have

(p_{\mathfrak{a}}([x,y]),a)=([x,y],a)=(x,[y,a])=\alpha(a)(x,y)=0

for all
a\in{\rm Ker}\alpha
.

Let
B_{\theta}(x,y)=(x,\theta y)
be a twisted form defined on
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Then
B_{\theta}
is a supersymmetric even non-degenerate bilinear form, see
[
AHZ
, Prop. 2.10]
. Note that for every root space
\theta{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha}={\mathfrak{g}}_{-\alpha}
because
[h,\theta x]=-\theta([h,x])=-\alpha(h)\theta x
for any
h\in{\mathfrak{a}},x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha}
. Hence
B_{\theta}
restricts to a non-degenerate supersymmetric form on
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

If
\alpha
is a singular root,
B_{\theta}
is a non-degenerate skew-symmetric bilinear form on
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha}
.
In particular
\dim{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha}
is even. Moreover, if
B_{\theta}(x,y)\neq 0
for
x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha}
then
p_{\mathfrak{a}}[x,\theta y]
is a nonzero multiple of
h_{\alpha}
.

Proof.

If
\alpha
is singular then
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha}
is purely odd and so
B_{\theta}
is skew-symmetric.
The subspaces
{\mathfrak{a}}
and
\mathfrak{t}
are orthogonal complements with respect to
(\cdot,\cdot)
since
(\cdot,\cdot)
is
\theta
stable. Hence
(p_{\mathfrak{a}}[x,\theta y],a)=([x,\theta y],a)
for any
a\in{\mathfrak{a}}
.
Moreover,

([x,\theta y],a)=-([\theta x,y],-a)=(\theta x,[y,a])=-\alpha(a)(x,\theta y)=-\alpha(a)B_{\theta}(x,y).

Since
B_{\theta}(x,y)\neq 0
, we get that
p_{\mathfrak{a}}[x,\theta y]
is a nonzero multiple of
h_{\alpha}
.

∎

2.2.
The Symmetric Algebra and the Algebra of Invariants

For a super-vector space
V=V_{\bar{0}}\oplus V_{\bar{1}}
, we denote by

T(V)
the tensor algebra, namely
T(V)=\bigoplus_{d=0}^{\infty}V^{\otimes d}
. Similarly, we denote by
S(V)
the symmetric algebra on
V
, that is
S=S(V_{\bar{0}})\otimes\bigwedge(V_{\bar{1}})
as vector spaces.
Note that
S(V)=\bigoplus_{d=0}^{\infty}S^{d}(V)
where
S^{d}(V)
is the component of degree
d
.

Let
m=\dim V_{\bar{0}}
and
n=\dim V_{\bar{1}}
. Denote by
P_{d}
be the set of partitions
\lambda
of
d
that fit in the
(m|n)
-hook, namely
\lambda_{m+1}\leq n
.
Recall that
\color[rgb]{0,0,0}V^{\otimes d}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in P_{d}}V_{\lambda}
as a
\mathfrak{gl}(V)
-module where
V_{\lambda}
is the irreducible module with highest weight

\lambda_{1}\varepsilon_{1}+\ldots+\lambda_{m}\varepsilon_{m}+\max(\lambda^{\prime}_{1}-m,0)\delta_{1}+\ldots+\max(\lambda^{\prime}_{n}-m,0)\delta_{n}.

Note that for any superspace
V
, the polynomial algebra
P(V)
is naturally isomorphic to the symmetric algebra
S(V^{*})
. Given a subspace
W\subset V
, the restriction map from
S(V^{*})
to
S(W^{*})
is well defined.

Given an Iwasawa involution
\theta
, the algebra of invariants is the following subalgebra of
S({\mathfrak{a}}^{*})
:

I_{\theta}(\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\mathfrak{a}}^{*}\color[rgb]{0,0,0})=\left\{f\in S({\mathfrak{a}}^{*})^{W}\mid\left(D_{h_{\alpha}}\right)^{k}\left(f\right)\in\left\langle\alpha\right\rangle\text{ for }\alpha\text{ singular },k=1,3,\ldots,\dim{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha}-1\right\}.

We shall abbreviate
I({\mathfrak{a}}^{*})=I_{\theta}(\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\mathfrak{a}}^{*}\color[rgb]{0,0,0})
in the rest of the paper.

Example 2.3
.

Let
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}:={\mathfrak{g}}\times{\mathfrak{g}}
and let
\tilde{\theta}:\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
be such that
\tilde{\theta}(x,y)=(y,x)
.
Then we get a symmetric pair
(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}},\tilde{\mathfrak{k}})
where
\tilde{\mathfrak{k}}=\{(x,x):x\in{\mathfrak{g}}\}\cong{\mathfrak{g}}
. Here
\tilde{\mathfrak{p}}=\{(x,-x):x\in{\mathfrak{g}}\}

and the Cartan subspace
\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}=\{(h,-h):h\in{\mathfrak{h}}\}
is identified with the Cartan subalgebra
{\mathfrak{h}}
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
.
In this case the restricted roots are the roots of
{\mathfrak{g}}
, the dimension of each root space is
2
and each root space is either purely even or purely odd. A root of
{\mathfrak{g}}
with an odd root space has multiple which is an even root if and only if it is nonisotropic. Hence the algebra of invariants is

I(\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}^{*})\cong I({\mathfrak{h}}^{*}):=\left\{f\in S({\mathfrak{a}}^{*})^{W}:D_{h_{\alpha}}(f)\in\langle\alpha\rangle\text{ for $\alpha$ isotropic root}\right\}.

By
[
Serge2
]
, this is precisely the image of the restriction map
S({\mathfrak{g}}^{*})^{\mathfrak{g}}
to
S({\mathfrak{h}}^{*})
.

Remark 2.4
.

The algebra
S(\mathfrak{g})
is in fact
\operatorname{gr}U({\mathfrak{g}})
, namely the associated graded of the universal enveloping algebra of
{\mathfrak{g}}
.
Since the adjoint action of
{\mathfrak{g}}
gives an isomorphic
{\mathfrak{g}}
-module structures on
U({\mathfrak{g}})
and
S({\mathfrak{g}})
, we also get that
\operatorname{gr}Z({\mathfrak{g}})=S({\mathfrak{g}})^{\mathfrak{g}}
.
Let
D({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{k}}):=U({\mathfrak{g}})^{\mathfrak{k}}/\color[rgb]{0,0,0}({\mathfrak{k}}U({\mathfrak{g}})\cap U({\mathfrak{g}})^{\mathfrak{k}})\color[rgb]{0,0,0}
.
Since
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{k}}\oplus{\mathfrak{p}}
as a
{\mathfrak{k}}
-module, we also get that
\operatorname{gr}D({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{k}})=S({\mathfrak{p}})^{\mathfrak{k}}\cong S({\mathfrak{g}})^{\mathfrak{k}}/({\mathfrak{k}}S({\mathfrak{g}})\cap S({\mathfrak{g}})^{\mathfrak{k}})
. The functor
\operatorname{gr}
sends the projection map
Z({\mathfrak{g}})\rightarrow D({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{k}})
to the projection
S({\mathfrak{g}})^{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow S({\mathfrak{p}})^{\mathfrak{k}}
and reflects surjectivity, that is the former map is surjective if the latter map is surjective. Using the invariant bilinear form, we note that the latter map is equivalent to the restriction map
S({\mathfrak{g}}^{*})^{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow S({\mathfrak{p}}^{*})^{\mathfrak{k}}
.

2.3.
Generalized Restricted Root Systems

We show that a restricted root system is a generalized root system in the following sense.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
V
be a finite-dimensional vector space with a non-degenerate bilinear form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
. A finite set
R=R_{sing}\cup R_{reg}\subset V\backslash\{0\}
is called a GRR system if

(1)

R
spans
V
and
R_{reg}=-R_{reg}
,
R_{sing}=-R_{sing}
.

(2)

If
\alpha\in R_{reg}
then
\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle\neq 0
.
For every
\beta\in R
,
\frac{2\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle}{\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
s_{\alpha}(\beta)=\beta-\frac{2\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle}{\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle}\alpha\in R
.
Moreover,
s_{\alpha}(R_{reg})=R_{reg}
and
s_{\alpha}(R_{sing})=R_{sing}
.

(3)

If
\alpha\in R_{sing}
then for any
\beta\in R
such that
\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle\neq 0
, at least one of the vectors
\beta+\alpha,\beta-\alpha
belongs to
R
.
Moreover, if
\alpha,\beta\in R_{sing}
and
\alpha+\beta\in R
then
\alpha+\beta\in R_{reg}
.

This generalizes the definition of
[
Serga2
]
, where in the third condition it is required that exactly one of
\alpha+\beta,\alpha-\beta
belongs to
R
. The notion of GRR system also includes the root system
BC(m,n)
given in
[
SV
, Sec. 2]
. Note that
R_{reg}
is a nonreduced root system.

We now prove that
R=\Delta(\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\mathfrak{a}},{\mathfrak{g}}\color[rgb]{0,0,0})
is a GRR system for every Iwasawa decomposition.
Here
R_{sing}
are the singular roots and the inner product is given by the inner product of
{\mathfrak{a}}
.

Proposition 2.6
.

The restricted roots
\Delta({\mathfrak{a}},{\mathfrak{g}})
form a GRR system.

Proof.

The property (1) follows from the same property of
\Delta({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}})
.
Let
\alpha^{\prime}\in R_{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}reg}
.
Then
\alpha^{\prime}=k\alpha
where
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha}\cap{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}
is nonzero for some
k\in\mathbb{C}
.
From the theory of Lie algebras
(\alpha,\alpha)\neq 0
and so
(\alpha^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})\neq 0
.
Moreover, there exists
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha}\cap{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}
such that
(x,\theta x)\neq 0
.
Indeed, otherwise for every
y,z\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha}\cap{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}
, one has
(y,\theta z)=\frac{1}{4}\left((y+z,\theta y+\theta z)-(y-z,\theta y-\theta z)\right)=0
, which contradicts the fact that
(\cdot,\cdot)
is non degenerate.
Now, since
(x,\theta x)\neq 0
, the subalgebra
\mathfrak{s}_{\alpha}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{x,\theta x,[x,\theta x]\}
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
so (2) follows for
\alpha
.
We are left to prove (2) for
\alpha^{\prime}=k\alpha
.
Since
\bigoplus_{r\in\mathbb{C}}{\mathfrak{g}}_{r\alpha}
is a finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{s}_{\alpha}
-module,
{\mathfrak{g}}_{k\alpha}
is nonzero only if
k\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}

and
(2)
holds.

For (3), we first show that
(\alpha,\beta)
is a nonzero multiple of
\beta(h_{\alpha})
for any
\alpha,\beta\in R
.
Let
x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha}
be such that
(x,\theta y)\neq 0
. Then by Lemma
2.2
,
H_{\alpha}=p_{\mathfrak{a}}([x,\theta y])
is a nonzero scalar multiple of
h_{\alpha}
, where
h_{\alpha}
is defined by
(h_{\alpha},h)=\alpha(h)
for all
h\in{\mathfrak{a}}
.
Hence
(h_{\alpha},h_{\beta})\neq 0

if and only if

(H_{\alpha},H_{\beta})\neq 0
. These inequalities are equivalent to

\alpha(H_{\beta})\neq 0

because

(H_{\alpha},H_{\beta})=([x,\theta y],H_{\beta})=(x,[\theta y,H_{\beta}])=\alpha(H_{\beta})(x,\theta y)

and
(x,\theta y)\neq 0
. By symmetry this is equivalent to
\beta(H_{\alpha})\neq 0
. Thus,
(\alpha,\beta)
is a nonzero multiple of
\beta(h_{\alpha})
.

Suppose that
a\in\color[rgb]{0,0,0}R_{sing}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}
,
\beta\in R
but
\beta\pm\alpha\notin R
.
This means that for any
z\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\beta}
,
[{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha},z]=[{\mathfrak{g}}_{-\alpha},z]=0
. In particular
[x,z]=[y,z]=[\theta x,z]=[\theta y,z]=0
. This implies that

0=[[x,\theta y]-\theta([x,\theta y]),z]=2[p_{\mathfrak{a}}([x,\theta y]),z]=2[H_{\alpha},z]=2\beta(H_{\alpha})z.

Thus
\beta(H_{\alpha})=0
as required.

Suppose
\alpha,\beta\in R_{sing}
. Then
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha},{\mathfrak{g}}_{\beta}
are purely odd. Since
{\mathfrak{g}}
is a Kac-Moody, if
\alpha+\beta\in R
, then
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha+\beta}=[{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha},{\mathfrak{g}}_{\beta}]
. Hence
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha+\beta}
must contain a nonzero even vector and so
\alpha+\beta\in R_{reg}
.
∎

Example 2.7
.

Not every GRR system arises from a symmetric pair. For example,

\Delta_{reg}=\left\{\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j}:1\leq i\neq j\leq n\right\},\quad\Delta_{sing}=\left\{\pm(\varepsilon_{i}+\varepsilon_{j}):1\leq i\neq j\leq n\right\}.

In fact, given a symmetric pair of a Lie algebra

to be the regular roots and the roots of

to be the singular roots. Other exceptional examples include

•

•

\Delta_{reg}=\left\{\pm\varepsilon_{i}\pm\varepsilon_{j},\pm\varepsilon_{i}\ :\ 1\leq i\neq j\leq 5\right\}

\Delta_{sing}=\left\{\frac{1}{2}\left(\pm\varepsilon_{1}\pm\varepsilon_{2}\pm\varepsilon_{3}\pm\varepsilon_{4}\pm\varepsilon_{5}\right)\right\}

\Delta_{reg}=\left\{\pm 2\varepsilon_{1},\pm 2\varepsilon_{2},\pm 2\varepsilon_{3},\pm 2\varepsilon_{4}\right\},\quad\Delta_{sing}=\left\{\pm\varepsilon_{1}\pm\varepsilon_{2}\pm\varepsilon_{3}\pm\varepsilon_{4}\right\}.

Remark 2.8

In

3.
The Image of the Chavalley Restriction Map

We begin proving Theorem A. We start by showing that the image of the Chevalley restriction map lies in the space
I({\mathfrak{a}}^{*})
. This means that the image satisfies two types of invariance conditions:
W
-invariance and a condition related to roots whose root spaces are purely odd.

To show that the image is
W
-invariant, we note that
{\mathfrak{a}}\subset{\mathfrak{p}}_{\bar{0}}
. This means we can first restrict functions from
{\mathfrak{p}}
to
{\mathfrak{p}}_{\bar{0}}
and then to
{\mathfrak{a}}
. The restriction from
{\mathfrak{p}}
to
{\mathfrak{p}}_{\bar{0}}
gives a
{\mathfrak{k}}_{\bar{0}}
-invariant function.
By the Chevalley restriction theorem for
({\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}},{\mathfrak{k}}_{\bar{0}})
, we get that the restriction to
{\mathfrak{a}}
of functions
S({\mathfrak{p}}_{\bar{0}})^{{\mathfrak{k}}_{\bar{0}}}
is in
S({\mathfrak{a}}^{*})^{W}
.

We now prove that the second type of invariance condition holds. Suppose that the root space
\alpha
is purely odd but one of its multiples has an even root vector. Then the derivative condition follows from the following proposition.

Let
\mathfrak{p}_{\alpha}:=\left(\left(\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}\right)\cap\mathfrak{p}\right)\oplus\mathfrak{a}
and
{\mathfrak{k}}_{\alpha}=\left({\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{-\alpha}\right)\cap{\mathfrak{k}}
. We prove the derivative condition by first restricting to
S({\mathfrak{p}}_{\alpha})^{{\mathfrak{k}}_{\alpha}}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

For a singular root
\alpha
of multiplicity
2n
, the projection
f_{0}
of

to
S(\mathfrak{a}^{*})
satisfies

\left(D_{h_{\alpha}}\right)^{k}\left(f_{0}\right)\in\left\langle\alpha\right\rangle

for

, that is
\left(D_{h_{\alpha}}\right)^{k}\left(f_{0}\right)

is zero on
{\rm Ker}\alpha
.

Let us examine the action of
{\mathfrak{k}}_{\alpha}
on
{\mathfrak{p}}_{\alpha}
. By Lemma
2.2
, we can take

to be a basis of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha}
such that

and they are normalized such that

\left[e_{i}+\theta e_{i},e_{j}^{\prime}-\theta e_{j}^{\prime}\right]=\color[rgb]{0,0,0}-[e_{i},\theta e_{j}^{\prime}]+[\theta e_{i},e_{j}^{\prime}]=-p_{\mathfrak{a}}\left([e_{i},\theta e_{j}^{\prime}]\right)=\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\delta_{ij}h_{\alpha}.

We use the fact that for any

is an even root vector of

.
Note that for any

Let

be such that

\xi_{i}\left(e_{j}-\theta e_{j}\right)=\eta_{i}\left(e_{j}^{\prime}-\theta e_{j}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)=\delta_{ij},

\xi_{i}\left(e_{j}^{\prime}-\theta e_{j}^{\prime}\right)=\eta_{i}\left(e_{j}-\theta e_{j}\right)=0,

and

\xi_{i}\left(\mathfrak{a}\right)=\eta_{i}\left(\mathfrak{a}\right)=0.

Let

.
It follows from the above that

(1)

\left(e_{i}+\theta e_{i}\right).f=\alpha\frac{\partial f}{\partial\xi_{i}}-\eta_{i}D_{h_{\alpha}}\left(f\right).

Proof of Proposition

.

Let

By

.
Denote

,

and

.
By (

-coefficient
of

is

\alpha f_{k}-D_{\text{$h_{\alpha}$}}f_{k-1}=0.

We claim that for

4.
Injectivity of the Chevalley restriction map

The injectivity of the Chevalley restriction map follows from the following general lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a finite-dimensional Lie superalgebra and
V
be a finite dimensional
{\mathfrak{g}}
-module and assume that
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}u=V_{\bar{1}}
for some
u\in V_{\bar{0}}
. Then the restriction map
\mathbb{C}[V]^{\mathfrak{g}}\to\mathbb{C}[V_{\bar{0}}]^{{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}}
is injective.

This lemma is a special case of
[
Serge2
, Prop. 1]
. We give a self contained proof.

Proof.

Note that the set
U
of all
u\in V_{\bar{0}}
satisfying the assumption of the lemma is Zariski open and hence dense in
V_{\bar{0}}
.

Choose a basis
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}
for
V_{\bar{1}}^{*}
and

x_{1},\dots x_{m}\in V_{\bar{0}}^{*}
. We use the identity

\mathbb{C}[V]=\mathbb{C}[V_{\bar{0}}]\otimes\Lambda(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n})

and introduce a
\mathbb{Z}
-grading on
\mathbb{C}[V]
by setting

\mathbb{C}[V_{\bar{0}}]
to have degree zero and all
\xi_{i}
-s to have degree
1
.
For any
v\in V_{\bar{0}}
we define the evaluation map

\operatorname{ev}_{v}:\mathbb{C}[V]\to\Lambda(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n})

in the natural way. Consider the representation map
\rho:{\mathfrak{g}}\to{\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}(V)
.
For every
X\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
,
\rho(X)\in\mathfrak{gl}(V)
can be written in the form
\rho(X)=X^{+}+X^{-}

where
X^{+}V_{\bar{0}}=0
and
X^{-}V_{\bar{1}}=0
. In particular, in the action on the dual space
V^{*}
,

X^{+}=\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}\xi_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}

and
X^{-}=\sum_{i,j}b_{ij}y_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi_{i}}
for some

a_{ij},b_{ij}\in\mathbb{C}
.
Note that
X^{-}
commutes with
\operatorname{ev}_{v}

for any
v\in V_{\bar{0}}
and

\rho(X)(v)=X^{-}(v)=\sum b_{ij}y_{j}(v)\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi_{i}}
is a derivation in
\Lambda(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n})
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{C}[V]^{\mathfrak{g}}
lie in the kernel of the restriction map and

f_{k}
be the lowest-degree nonzero term of
f
in our grading. We have
k>0
.
To prove the lemma it suffices to show that
f_{k}=0
.
For any
X\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
, we have
X^{-}(f_{k})=0
and hence

X^{-}(v)(\operatorname{ev}_{v}f_{k})=0
for any
v\in V_{\bar{0}}
.
We have
X^{+}(v)=0
for any
v\in V_{\bar{0}}
and therefore
\rho(X)(v)=X^{-}(v)
.
For a given
u\in U
, the set
\{X^{-}(u)\mid X\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}\}
contains
\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi_{i}}
for all
i=1,\dots n
. Therefore
\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi_{i}}({ev}_{u}f_{k})=0
for
i=1,\ldots,n
and
\operatorname{ev}_{u}f_{k}=0
.
By the density of
U
we get that
f_{k}=0
.
∎

Now we can prove the following proposition:

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{k}}\oplus{\mathfrak{p}}
have Iwasawa decomposition. Then the restriction map
\mathbb{C}[{\mathfrak{p}}]^{\mathfrak{k}}\to\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{a}]
is injective.

Proof.

Let us choose a generic
h\in\mathfrak{a}
. Then
\operatorname{ad}_{h}
is non-degenerate
on any restricted root space
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha}
and hence

\operatorname{ad}_{h}:{\mathfrak{k}}\cap({\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{-\alpha})\to{\mathfrak{p}}\cap({\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{-\alpha})

is an isomorphism. Since
{\mathfrak{p}}_{\bar{1}}
is the sum of

{\mathfrak{p}}_{\bar{1}}\cap({\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{-\alpha})
, we obtain

[h,{\mathfrak{k}}_{\bar{1}}]={\mathfrak{p}}_{\bar{1}}
.
By Lemma
4.1
the restriction map
\mathbb{C}[{\mathfrak{p}}]^{\mathfrak{k}}\to\mathbb{C}[{\mathfrak{p}}_{\bar{0}}]^{{\mathfrak{k}}_{\bar{0}}}
is injective. By classical result the restriction map

\mathbb{C}[{\mathfrak{p}}_{\bar{0}}]^{{\mathfrak{k}}_{\bar{0}}}\to\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{a}]
is injective. The statement follows.
∎

Proposition 5.8

The ring

Proof.

Abstract
. Various static analysis problems are reformulated as instances of the Context-Free Language Reachability (CFL-r) problem. One promising way to make solving CFL-r more practical for large-scale interprocedural graphs is to reduce CFL-r to linear algebra operations on sparse matrices, as they are efficiently executed on modern hardware. In this work, we present five optimizations for a matrix-based CFL-r algorithm that utilize the specific properties of both the underlying semiring and the widely-used linear algebra library SuiteSparse:GraphBlas. Our experimental results show that these optimizations result in orders of magnitude speedup, with the optimized matrix-based CFL-r algorithm consistently outperforming state-of-the-art CFL-r solvers across four considered static analyses.



1.
Introduction

Context-Free Language Reachability (CFL-r) is the problem of finding pairs of vertices in an edge-labeled graph that are connected by a path, with the condition that the labels along this path spell a word from a Context-Free Language (CFL) defined by a given Context-Free Grammar (CFG). A wide range of problems is reduced to CFL-r, including, among others, alias analysis
(
Zheng and Rugina 2008
)
, points-to analysis
(
Dietrich et al. 2015
)
, value-flow analysis
(
Sui et al. 2014
)
, and fixing compilation errors
(
Zhang et al. 2023
)
. Furthermore, CFL-r is also used to approximate solutions for some undecidable problems, for example, points-to analysis that is simultaneously context-, field-, and flow-sensitive
(
Späth et al. 2019
;
Reps 2000
)
.

Since Thomas Reps et al.
(
Reps et al. 1995
)
proposed using CFL-r for precise interprocedural dataflow analysis, numerous CFL-r algorithms have been developed
(
Azimov and Grigorev 2018
;
Medeiros et al. 2018
;
Orachev et al. 2020
;
Lei et al. 2022
)
.
One of the promising general-purpose CFL-r algorithms is a matrix-based algorithm by Rustam Azimov et al.
(
Azimov and Grigorev 2018
)
. This algorithm naturally utilizes parallel hardware and oftentimes surpasses analogous in terms of performance
(
Azimov 2022
)
. However, it still takes a considerable amount of time to deal with paths with deep derivation trees and large CFGs of field- and context-sensitive analyses. In this work, we propose a set of optimizations that improve performance of the matrix-based algorithm in such cases. Our evaluation demonstrates that the optimized version outperforms such tools as POCR
(
Lei et al. 2022
)
, Gigascale
(
Dietrich et al. 2015
)
, and Graspan
(
Wang et al. 2017
)
.

Optimizations

Following optimizations have been proposed, implemented, and evaluated.

(1)

The bottleneck in Algorithm
1
is at
\cdot_{R_{Gr}}
on line
1
. To address this,
M\cdot_{R_{Gr}}M
is replaced with
(M_{old}\cdot_{R_{Gr}}\Delta M)\cup(\Delta M\cdot_{R_{Gr}}M)
, where
\Delta M=M\setminus M_{old}
(element-wise set difference) and
M_{old}
is the value of the variable
M
during the previous iteration of the
while
loop. Initially,
M_{old}=\varnothing^{|V|\times|V|}
. The rationale here is that
(M_{old}\cdot_{R_{Gr}}\Delta M)\cup(\Delta M\cdot_{R_{Gr}}M)\cup(M_{old}\cdot_{R_{Gr}}M_{old})=M\cdot_{R_{Gr}}M
and
M_{old}\cdot_{R_{Gr}}M_{old}
has already been added to
M
on the previous iteration.

(2)

Now, during most iterations,
\Delta M
is highly sparse, while
M_{old}
and
M
are less sparse. The multiplication of a highly sparse matrix with a less sparse one is considerably faster in row-major format when the sparser matrix is on the left and in column-major format when the sparser matrix is on the right
(
Davis 2023
)
. Therefore, the next optimization involves maintaining two copies of
M
in both row- and column-major formats and computing
M_{old}\cdot_{R_{Gr}}\Delta M
and
\Delta M\cdot_{R_{Gr}}M
using column-by-column and row-by-row (hyper)sparse matrix multiplication techniques, respectively
(
Gao et al. 2023
)
. In practice, non-terminals that don’t occur in place of
a
in productions like
c\rightarrow a\ b
don’t need to be stored in the column-major copy of
M
, and those that don’t occur in place of
b
don’t need to be stored in the row-major copy of
M
.

(3)

The next bottleneck is the element-wise union on line
1
, which becomes problematic as adding a highly sparse matrix to
M
causes a complete reconstruction of
M
. To cope with this,
M
is no longer stored as a matrix. Instead, we now use a set of matrices
\widetilde{M}=\{M_{1},M_{2},\dots,M_{p}\}
such that
M=\bigcup\widetilde{M},\forall A,B\in\widetilde{M}\ (nnz(A)<nnz(B)\implies b\ nnz(A)<nnz(B))
, where
b>1
is a hyperparameter and
nnz(X)
is the number of non-zero elements in matrix
X
.
The element-wise union on line
1
is computed by adding
\Delta M
to
\widetilde{M}
as a set element and replacing
\widetilde{M}
with
\widetilde{M}\setminus\{A,B\}\cup\{A\cup B\}

(4)

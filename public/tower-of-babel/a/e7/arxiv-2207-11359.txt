Asymptotic structure of Carrollian limits of Einstein-Yang-Mills theory in four spacetime dimensions Preprint: ULB-MathPhys-22/05
Oscar Fuentealba, Marc Henneaux, Patricio Salgado-Rebolledo, and Jakob Salzer

Abstract
In this paper, three things are done. First, we study from an algebraic point of view the infinite-dimensional BMS-like extensions of the Carroll algebra relevant to the asymptotic structure of the electric and magnetic Carrollian limits of Einstein gravity. In the course of this study we exhibit by “Carroll-Galileo duality” a new infinite-dimensional BMS-like extension of the Galilean algebra and of its centrally extended Bargmann algebra. Second, we consider the electric Carrollian limit of the pure Einstein theory and indicate that more flexible boundary conditions than the ones that follow from just taking the limit of the Einsteinian boundary conditions are actually consistent. These boundary conditions lead to a bigger asymptotic symmetry algebra that involves spatial supertranslations depending on three functions of the angles (instead of one). Third, we turn to the Carrollian limit of the coupled Einstein-Yang-Mills system. An infinite-dimensional color enhancement of the gauge algebra is found in the electric Carrollian limit of the Yang-Mills field, which allows angle-dependent Yang-Mills transformations at spatial infinity, not available in the Einstein-Yang-Mills case prior to taking the Carrollian electric limit. This enhancement does not occur in the magnetic limit.



1
Introduction

The Carroll algebra
\mathcal{C}
is one of the contractions of the Poincaré algebra, obtained by sending the speed of light
c
to zero (“ultrarelativistic limit”)
LevyLeblond:1965
;
Bacry:1968zf

\displaystyle[M_{i},M_{j}]=\epsilon_{ijk}M_{k},\quad[M_{i},B_{j}]=\epsilon_{ijk}B_{k},\quad[B_{i},B_{j}]=0,

\displaystyle[M_{i},P_{j}]=\epsilon_{ijk}P_{k},\quad[P_{i},B_{j}]=\delta_{ij}E,\quad[M_{i},E]=0\quad[E,B_{i}]=0

(1)

\displaystyle[P_{i},P_{j}]=0,\quad[P_{i},E]=0.

where
P_{i}
,
M_{i}
,
E
and
B_{i}
are respectively the generators of spatial translations, spatial rotations, time translations and Carroll boosts.

The Carrollian limit of Einstein gravity arose initially as the “strong coupling limit”
Isham:1975ur
or “zero signature limit”
Teitelboim:1978wv
;
Henneaux:1981su
of general relativity. It is relevant to the description of the generic behaviour of the gravitational field in the vicinity of a spacelike singularity
Belinsky:1970ew
;
Belinsky:1982pk
and even more so when
p
-forms are included
Damour:2000wm
;
Damour:2002et
(see the review
Belinski:2017fas
). The geometry of the Carrollian limit was constructed in
Henneaux:1979vn
. Since then, many applications and properties of Carrollian structures have been studied, in particular in the context of the geometry of null surfaces and the BMS symmetry
Dautcourt:1997hb
;
Duval:2014uoa
;
Duval:2014uva
(see the recent articles
Donnay:2022aba
;
Figueroa-OFarrill:2021sxz
for an updated, comprehensive list of references and
Bergshoeff:2022eog
for a review of non-lorentzian theories).

It was shown recently that there are at least two different Carrollian limits of general relativity, an “electric” one and a “magnetic” one
Henneaux:2021yzg
(see also
deBoer:2021jej
;
Figueroa-OFarrill:2022mcy
;
Hansen:2021fxi
for a different perspective on Carrollian gravity theories). The electric Carrollian action corresponds to the zero signature limit of Einstein gravity, whereas the magnetic limit is equivalent to the Carrollian gravity theory found in
Bergshoeff:2017btm
by gauging the Carroll algebra
toappear
.

In a very interesting paper
Perez:2021abf
, the asymptotic structures of the electric and magnetic limits of the Einstein theory were determined (see
Perez:2022jpr
for the analysis in the presence of negative cosmological constant). It was shown that the magnetic and electric limits have distinct asymptotic symmetry groups. While the asymptotic symmetries in the magnetic case are simply the
c\rightarrow 0
contractions of those of Einstein’s theory with the same number of improper
Benguria:1976in
gauge transformations, there is an effective disappearance in the electric case of all the dynamical improper gauge symmetries (boosts and time (super)translations), because these become in the limit proper gauge transformations acting trivially on the physical states.

The boundary conditions studied in
Perez:2021abf
were naturally taken to be the Carrollian contractions of the various boundary conditions considered at spatial infinity for Einstein’s theory (
Regge:1974zd
;
Henneaux:2018cst
;
Henneaux:2018hdj
;
Henneaux:2019yax
). It turns out that in the Carrollian electric limit, a less strict set of boundary conditions can also be consistently imposed. These are not obtainable through the limiting process and lead to an infinite-dimensional enhancement of the Carroll spatial supertranslations.

One purpose of this paper is to analyse these more flexible boundary conditions for the electric Carrollian limit of Einstein gravity. We explicitly show that while the boosts and the time supertranslations remain pure gauge, as in
Perez:2021abf
, new space supertranslations introducing further functions of the angles indeed appear. These involve three functions of the angles and have no analog in the investigations of
Regge:1974zd
;
Henneaux:2018cst
;
Henneaux:2018hdj
;
Henneaux:2019yax
of the Einstein case.

In order to shed light on the algebras that can occur asymptotically, we study prior to the asymptotic considerations the Carroll-like contractions of the BMS
4
algebra
Bondi:1962px
;
Sachs:1962wk
from a purely algebraic viewpoint. This analysis enables one to exhibit, by “Carroll-Galileo duality”, a new infinite-dimensional BMS-like extension of the Galilean algebra and of its centrally extended Bargmann algebra.
We also study the BMS-like extensions of some ideals of the Carroll algebra relevant to the asymptotic symmetries.

Another purpose of our paper is to include the coupling to the Yang-Mills theory. This system is of interest in the asymptotic analysis context since it was shown in
Tanzi:2020fmt
;
Tanzi:2021prq
that there were obstructions to finding at spatial infinity the angle-dependent color transformations exhibited at null infinity
Strominger:2013lka
;
Barnich:2013sxa
;
Banerjee:2021uxe
, except in the abelian case studied in
Henneaux:2018gfi
. We start by considering the Yang-Mills theory on a flat Carroll background. We show that while the magnetic limit presents the same difficulties as its Lorentzian counterpart, the electric limit allows again for a greater flexibility. We present boundary conditions that are compatible with the Carroll symmetry and invariant under an angle-dependent color group.

We then extend these results to the different Carrollian limits of the coupled Einstein-Yang-Mills system, which is a rather direct task once the flat Carroll case has been understood.

Our paper is organized as follows. Sections
2

3

A BMS-like extension of the Carroll algebra is by definition a semi-direct sum of the homogeneous Carroll algebra and an infinite-dimensional abelian algebra parametrized by functions on the sphere (the “supertranslations”) that contains the ordinary spacetime translations. This is just the analog of the BMS extension of the Poincaré group
Bondi:1962px
;
Sachs:1962wk
. We use the 3+1 parametrization of the symmetry introduced in
Troessaert:2017jcm
(see also
Henneaux:2018cst
). We show that there exist at least three inequivalent BMS-like extensions. By applying the same methods to the non-relativistic limit, we exhibit similarly a third, and to our knowledge new, BMS-like extension of the Galilean algebra and of its centrally extended version, the Bargmann algebra, in addition to the two extensions already constructed in
Batlle:2017yuz
. We study next the asymptotic analysis of the electric Carrollian limit of pure Einstein gravity, after a brief survey of the Minkowskian results (Sections
4

5
). We then turn the electric Carrollian limit of the Yang-Mills theory in flat Carroll space (Section
6
) and finally to the different Carrollian limits of the combined Einstein-Yang-Mills system (Section
7

8

2
Ideals of the Carroll algebra

Among the Carroll transformations,
P_{i}
are
M_{i}
are kinematical transformations defined within equal time hypersurfaces, while

B_{i}
are dynamical transformations involving time evolution. The kinematical transformations form a subalgebra isomorphic to the algebra of Euclidean displacements

The Carroll algebra is not simple and possesses many ideals. For instance, the dynamical transformations

and

form an abelian ideal
\mathcal{D}

is isomorphic to

,

\frac{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{D}}\simeq iso(3).

(2)

The energy

is isomorphic to the semi-direct sum of

and a three-dimensional abelian algebra

transforming in the vector representation of

, denoted

\frac{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{I}}\simeq iso(3)\oplus_{\sigma}t_{3}\equiv d_{3}

(3)

(of which

is an ideal).

The subalgebra of homogeneous Carroll transformations is spanned by the spatial rotations and the boosts. It is six-dimensional and isomorphic to
so(3)\oplus_{\sigma}t_{3}
. These transformations grow up at infinity linearly with
r
, while the spatial translations and the time translations tend to constants.

The splitting of the supertranslations into even and odd parts, natural from the Hamiltonian description, is particularly well adapted to the Carroll contraction.

4
Brief overview of boundary conditions in Einstein gravity

After these algebraic preliminaries, we now turn to the asymptotic analysis of Carrollian gravities, starting with a brief overview of Einstein gravity.

The Hamiltonian action of Einstein gravity in four spacetime dimensions reads

S[g_{ij},\pi^{ij},N,N^{i}]=\int dt\left[\int d^{3}x\left(\pi^{ij}\dot{g}_{ij}-N\mathcal{H}-N^{i}\mathcal{H}_{i}\right)-B_{\infty}\right]\,.

(46)

Here,
N
and
N^{i}
stand for the lapse and shift functions, respectively. The variation of the Hamiltonian action with respect to these functions imposes the following constraints on the momentum
\pi^{ij}
and the 3-dimensional metric
g_{ij}

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{g}}\left(\pi^{ij}\pi_{ij}-\frac{1}{2}\pi^{2}\right)-\sqrt{g}R\approx 0\,,

(47)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\nabla^{j}\pi_{ij}\approx 0\,.

(48)

The boundary term at spatial infinity
B_{\infty}
depends on the boundary conditions and it turns out to be the standard ADM energy when the lapse and shift functions behave asymptotically as
N\rightarrow 1
and
N^{i}\rightarrow 0
, respectively
Regge:1974zd
. The action of an arbitrary diffeomorphism
\xi^{\mu}=(\xi^{\perp}\equiv\xi,\xi^{i})
on the dynamical fields yields the following infinitesimal transformation laws

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi,\xi^{i}}g_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\xi}{\sqrt{g}}\left(\pi_{ij}-\frac{1}{2}g_{ij}\pi\right)+\mathcal{L}_{\xi}g_{ij}\,,

(49)

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi,\xi^{i}}\pi^{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{g}}\left(R^{ij}-\frac{1}{2}g^{ij}R\right)+\frac{\xi g^{ij}}{2\sqrt{g}}\left(\pi^{mn}\pi_{mn}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{2}\right)

(50)

\displaystyle-\frac{2\xi}{\sqrt{g}}\left(\pi^{im}\pi^{j}_{m}-\frac{1}{2}\pi^{ij}\pi\right)+\sqrt{g}\left(\nabla^{i}\nabla^{j}\xi-g^{ij}\nabla_{m}\nabla^{m}\xi\right)+\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\pi^{ij}\,,

where
\mathcal{L}_{\xi}
denotes the spatial Lie derivative, which acts on the fields as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\xi}g_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi^{k}\partial_{k}g_{ij}+\partial_{i}\xi^{k}g_{kj}+\partial_{j}\xi^{k}g_{ki}\,,

(51)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\pi^{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{k}(\xi^{k}\pi^{ij})-\partial_{k}\xi^{i}\pi^{jk}-\partial_{k}\xi^{j}\pi^{ik}\,.

(52)

(53)

\displaystyle g_{rA}

\displaystyle=\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\lambda$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{A}+\frac{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$h$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{rA}}{r}+\frac{h^{(2)}_{rA}}{r^{2}}+\mathcal{O}\left(r^{-3}\right)\,,

(54)

\displaystyle g_{AB}

\displaystyle=r^{2}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$g$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{AB}+r\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$h$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{AB}+h^{(2)}_{AB}+\mathcal{O}\left(r^{-1}\right)\,,

(55)

\displaystyle\pi^{rr}

\displaystyle=\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{rr}+\frac{\pi^{(2)}_{rr}}{r}+\mathcal{O}\left(r^{-2}\right)\,,

(56)

\displaystyle\pi^{rA}

\displaystyle=\frac{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{rA}}{r}+\frac{\pi^{(2)rA}}{r^{2}}+\mathcal{O}\left(r^{-3}\right)\,,

(57)

\displaystyle\pi^{AB}

\displaystyle=\frac{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{AB}}{r^{2}}+\frac{\pi^{(2)AB}}{r^{3}}+\mathcal{O}\left(r^{-4}\right)\,.

(58)

What distinguishes the different sets of boundary conditions are the conditions imposed on the leading orders
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$h$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{ij}

4.1

5
Electric Carrollian limit of Einstein gravity with twisted parity conditions

5.1
Magnetic limit with diffeomorphism-twisted parity conditions

The magnetic Carroll contraction of the boundary conditions given above takes exactly the same form, because the leading orders of the transformation of the fields under surface deformations coincide with the Einstein case. Indeed, one now has

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\xi}g_{ij}\,,

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi,\xi^{i}}\pi^{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{g}}\left(R^{ij}-\frac{1}{2}g^{ij}R\right)+\sqrt{g}\left(\nabla^{i}\nabla^{j}\xi-g^{ij}\nabla_{m}\nabla^{m}\xi\right)+\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\pi^{ij}\,,

)-(

Because the terms being dropped in the Hamiltonian constraints are algebraic, the analysis of the surface terms in the canonical generators proceeeds as in the Einstein theory. One can in particular easily verify that Carroll boosts are integrable when

for the boundary conditions of

The twisted parity conditions produce therefore the expected result in the magnetic contraction.
The electric Carrollian limit, on the other hand, opens new possibilities and we focus on it in the rest of this section.

5.2

The Hamiltonian action principle of the electric Carrollian theory of gravity in four dimensions is given by

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{g}}\left(\pi^{ij}\pi_{ij}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{2}\right)\approx 0\,,

(72)

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{H}^{E}(x),\mathcal{H}^{E}(x^{\prime})\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{E}_{i}(x^{\prime})\partial_{j}\delta(x,x^{\prime})+\mathcal{H}^{E}_{j}(x)\partial_{i}\delta(x,x^{\prime})\,.

(75)

Variation of the action with respect to the momentum
\pi^{ij}
and the 3-dimensional metric
g_{ij}
gives the following equations of motion

\displaystyle\dot{g}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{2N}{\sqrt{g}}\left(\pi_{ij}-\frac{1}{2}g_{ij}\pi\right)+N_{i|j}+N_{j|i}\,,

(76)

\displaystyle\dot{\pi}^{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{N}{2\sqrt{g}}g^{ij}\left(\pi^{mn}\pi_{mn}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{2}\right)-\frac{2N}{\sqrt{g}}\left(\pi^{im}\pi_{m}^{j}-\frac{1}{2}\pi^{ij}\pi\right)+\mathcal{L}_{N}\pi^{ij}\,.

(77)

The infinitesimal transformation laws of the canonical pair
(g_{ij},\pi^{ij})
under diffeomorphisms generated by
\left(\xi^{\perp}\equiv\xi,\xi^{i}\right)
are now

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi,\xi^{i}}g_{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\xi}{\sqrt{g}}\left(\pi_{ij}-\frac{1}{2}g_{ij}\pi\right)+\xi_{i|j}+\xi_{j|i}\,,

(78)

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi,\xi^{i}}\pi^{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{\xi}{2\sqrt{g}}g^{ij}\left(\pi^{mn}\pi_{mn}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{2}\right)-\frac{2\xi}{\sqrt{g}}\left(\pi^{im}\pi_{m}^{j}-\frac{1}{2}\pi^{ij}\pi\right)+\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\pi^{ij}\,.

(79)

One should note that in (
77
) and (
79
), the terms involving
\xi
decay at least as
r^{-3}
at infinity, even for parameters
\xi
that blow up as
r
, so that the leading order
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{ij}
is invariant under normal hypersurface deformations.

5.3
Boundary conditions in the electric Carroll limit

In view of the remark just made, the Carroll contraction in the electric case of the twisted boundary conditions yield

\displaystyle\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$h$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{rr}

\displaystyle=\text{even}\,,

(80)

\displaystyle\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\lambda$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{A}

\displaystyle=(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\lambda$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{A})^{\text{odd}}+\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$D$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{A}\zeta_{r}-\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\zeta$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{A}\,,

(81)

\displaystyle\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$h$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{AB}

\displaystyle=(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$h$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{AB})^{\text{even}}+\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$D$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{A}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\zeta$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{B}+\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$D$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{B}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\zeta$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{A}+2\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$g$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{AB}\zeta_{r}\,,

(82)

\displaystyle\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{rr}

\displaystyle=(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{rr})^{\text{odd}}\,,

(83)

\displaystyle\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{rA}

\displaystyle=(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{rA})^{\text{even}}\,,

(84)

\displaystyle\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{AB}

\displaystyle=(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{AB})^{\text{odd}}\,,

(85)

where the leading order of the conjugate momentum is now strictly even or odd since the corresponding improper gauge terms are of lower order.

The contraction of the twisted boundary conditions also implies
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\lambda$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{A}=0
, but this condition turns out not to be necessary in the limit. This condition can be consistently avoided, leading to a bigger symmetry group.

5.4
Asymptotic symmetries of the electric Carroll limit

We now give the transformation laws of the leading orders of the fields under Carrollian diffeomorphisms with asymptotic form

\displaystyle\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle br+T+\mathcal{O}\left(r^{-2}\right)\,,

(86)

\displaystyle\xi^{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle W+\mathcal{O}\left(r^{-1}\right)\,,

(87)

\displaystyle\xi^{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Y^{A}+\frac{1}{r}I^{A}+\mathcal{O}\left(r^{-2}\right)\,,

(88)

where
b
describes Carrollian boosts,
Y^{A}
spatial rotations, and
T
and
W
stand for Carrollian supertranslations. The parameter
I^{A}
also describes spatial supertranslations and its precise role will depend on whether we impose
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\lambda$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{A}=0
or not (see below).

We find that the transformations of the leading orders under space-like diffeomorphisms read

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi^{i}}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$h$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{rr}

\displaystyle=Y^{A}\partial_{A}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$h$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{rr}\,,

(89)

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi^{i}}\overline{\lambda}_{A}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{Y}\overline{\lambda}_{A}+\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$D$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{A}W-I_{A}\,,

(90)

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi^{i}}\overline{h}_{AB}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{Y}\overline{h}_{AB}+2\left(\overline{D}_{(A}I_{B)}+\overline{g}_{AB}W\right)\,.

(91)

We also find that the leading orders of the momentum transform as

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi^{i}}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{rr}

\displaystyle=\partial_{A}\left(Y^{A}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{rr}\right)\,,

(92)

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi^{i}}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{rA}

\displaystyle=\partial_{B}\left(Y^{B}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{rA}\right)-\partial_{B}Y^{A}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{rB}\,,

(93)

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi^{i}}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{AB}

\displaystyle=\partial_{C}\left(Y^{C}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{AB}\right)-\partial_{C}Y^{A}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{CB}-\partial_{C}Y^{A}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{CA}\,,

(94)

while the subleading order
\pi^{(2)rA}
, which appears in the expression of the charges as in the Einstein theory, transforms as

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi^{i}}\pi^{(2)rA}

\displaystyle=\partial_{B}\left(Y^{B}\pi^{(2)rA}\right)-\partial_{B}Y^{A}\pi^{(2)rB}

\displaystyle\quad+\partial_{B}\left(I^{B}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{rA}\right)-\partial_{B}I^{A}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{rB}+I^{A}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{rr}-\partial_{B}W\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{AB}-W\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{rA}\,.

(95)

Similarly, we find that the transformation law under time-like deformations are

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$h$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{rr}

\displaystyle=\frac{b}{\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$g$\kern 0.0pt}}}}}\Big(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{rr}-\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{A}_{A}\Big)\,,

(96)

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi}\overline{\lambda}_{A}

\displaystyle=\frac{2b}{\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$g$\kern 0.0pt}}}}}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{r}_{A}\,,

(97)

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$h$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{AB}

\displaystyle=\frac{2b}{\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$g$\kern 0.0pt}}}}}\Big[\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{AB}-\frac{1}{2}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$g$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{AB}(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{rr}+\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{A}_{A})\Big]\,.

(98)

The leading orders of the momentum do not transform under time-like deformations,

\delta_{\xi}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{rr}=\delta_{\xi^{i}}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{rA}=\delta_{\xi^{i}}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{AB}=0\,,

(99)

but one finds for
\pi^{(2)rA}
,

\delta_{\xi}\pi^{(2)rA}=-\frac{2b}{3\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$g$\kern 0.0pt}}}}}\Big(2\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{rr}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{rA}+3\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{r}_{B}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{AB}-\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{rA}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{B}_{B}\Big)\,.

(100)

5.4.1
Canonical generator

Because the symplectic structure takes the standard form
\int d^{3}x\,d_{V}\pi^{ij}\wedge d_{V}g_{ij}
, the canonical generator of the asymptotic symmetries is simply obtained through the approach of
Regge:1974zd
. The surface integral in this case reads

\delta Q^{E}_{\xi}[g_{ij},\pi^{ij}]=\oint d^{2}x\Big(2\xi^{i}\delta\pi^{r}_{i}-\xi^{r}\delta g_{jk}\pi^{jk}\Big)\,.

(101)

Replacing the asymptotic conditions in (
101
), we obtain

\displaystyle\delta Q^{E}_{\xi}[g_{ij},\pi^{ij}]

\displaystyle=r\oint d^{2}x\Big(2Y^{A}\delta\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{r}_{B}\Big)

(102)

\displaystyle\quad+\oint d^{2}x\Big[2Y^{A}\delta\left(\pi^{(2)r}_{A}+\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$h$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{AB}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{rB}+\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\lambda$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{A}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{rr}\right)+2I^{A}\delta\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{r}_{A}+2W\delta\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{rr}\Big]\,.

(103)

The variation of the canonical generator has a linear divergent term. However, this is equal to zero by virtue of the asymptotic constraint
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$D$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{A}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{AB}+\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{rA}=0
, and using the fact that
Y^{A}
is the Killing vector of the 2-sphere at infinity. Then, the variation of the charge is given by

\displaystyle\delta Q^{E}_{\xi}[g_{ij},\pi^{ij}]

\displaystyle=\oint d^{2}x\Big[2Y^{A}\delta\left(\pi^{(2)r}_{A}+\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$h$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{AB}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{rB}+\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{rr}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\lambda$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{A}\right)+2I^{A}\delta\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{r}_{A}+2W\delta\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{rr}\Big]\,.

(104)

To proceed further, we need to distinguish two cases, according to whether the additional asymptotic condition
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\lambda$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{A}=0
is imposed as in the Einstein theory, or is not imposed, as it turns out to be possible.

5.4.2
Asymptotic symmetry algebra - Case with
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\lambda$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{A}=0
:
iso_{3}
-BMS algebra

When
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\lambda$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{A}=0
, the parameters
I_{A}
are not independent. Indeed, preservation of the condition
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\lambda$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{A}=0
requires one to add the following correcting gauge transformation when one performs a Carroll boost and a spatial supertranslation parametrized by
W
,

\xi^{r}=\mathcal{O}\left(r^{-2}\right)\,,\qquad\xi^{A}=\frac{1}{r}(I^{A}_{(b)}+I^{A}_{(W)})+\mathcal{O}\left(r^{-2}\right)\,,

(105)

with

I^{A}_{(b)}=\frac{2b}{\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$g$\kern 0.0pt}}}}}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{rA}\qquad I^{A}_{(W)}=\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$D$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{A}W\,,

(106)

so that the
I_{A}
’s are entirely determined by
b
and
W
.

Substituting this expression for
I_{A}
in
\delta Q^{E}_{\xi}[g_{ij},\pi^{ij}]
, one finds after integration in phase space that the canonical generator is equal to

Q^{E}_{\xi}=\frac{1}{2}b_{ij}M^{ij}+Q_{W}\,,

(107)

where

\displaystyle M^{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\oint d^{2}x\,2x^{[i}e^{j]A}\left(\pi^{(2)r}_{A}+\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$h$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{AB}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{rB}+\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{rr}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\lambda$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{A}\right)\,,

(108)

\displaystyle Q_{W}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\oint d^{2}x\,W\mathcal{W}\,,

(109)

with
\mathcal{W}=2(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{rr}-\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{A}_{A})
. The surface term accompanying the boost is given by

\oint d^{2}x\,\frac{2n^{i}}{\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$g$\kern 0.0pt}}}}}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{rA}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{rA}\,,

(110)

but this integral is equal to zero due to the parity conditions. It follows that the boosts are also proper gauge transformations for the diffeomorphism-twisted parity conditions, as in
Perez:2021abf
. Furthermore, it can be checked that only the odd part of
W
is an improper gauge transformation.

The parameters obey the following transformation laws

\displaystyle\hat{Y}^{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Y_{1}^{B}\partial_{B}Y_{2}^{A}-(1\leftrightarrow 2)\,,

(111)

\displaystyle\hat{W}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Y_{1}^{B}\partial_{B}W_{2}-(1\leftrightarrow 2)\,,

(112)

leading to the brackets

\displaystyle\{M^{ij},M^{kl}\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\delta^{jk}M^{il}-\delta^{ik}M^{jl}+\delta^{li}M^{jk}-\delta^{lj}M^{ik}\Big)\,,

(113)

\displaystyle\{M^{ij},\mathcal{W}(\theta,\phi)\}

\displaystyle=-\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$D$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{B}(x^{[i}e^{j]B}\mathcal{W}(\theta,\phi))\,.

(114)

This is the
iso(3)
-BMS algebra.

5.4.3
Asymptotic symmetry algebra - Case with
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\lambda$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{A}\neq 0
: Extended
iso(3)
-BMS algebra

Unlike what happens in the magnetic Carrollian theory, the expression (
104
) is integrable for arbitrary
Y^{A}
’s,
I^{A}
’s and
W
’s. Thus, there is no need to impose the restriction
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\lambda$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{A}=0
which was necessary in that case (and also in Einstein gravity) to ensure integrability. We thus lift this restriction and allow configurations with
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\lambda$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{A}\neq 0
.

The electric theory is then invariant under the semi-direct product of spatial rotations and the Abelian set of “generalized” spatial Carrollian supertranslations parametrized by
W
and
I^{A}
, with canonical generator that can be written as

Q^{E}_{\xi}=\frac{1}{2}b_{ij}M^{ij}+Q_{W}+Q_{I}\,,

(115)

where

Q_{W}=\oint d^{2}x\,W\mathcal{W}\,,\qquad Q_{I}=\oint d^{2}x\,I^{A}\mathcal{W}_{A}\,.

(116)

Here,
\mathcal{W}=2\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{rr}
and
\mathcal{W}_{A}=2\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{r}_{A}
. Because
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{rr}
is odd and
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{r}_{A}
even, only odd parameters
W
and even parameters
I^{A}
, which are otherwise arbitrary functions and vectors on the 2-sphere, define improper gauge transformations.

The transformation laws of the parameters are given by

\displaystyle\hat{Y}^{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Y_{1}^{B}\partial_{B}Y_{2}^{A}-(1\leftrightarrow 2)\,,

(117)

\displaystyle\hat{W}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Y_{1}^{B}\partial_{B}W_{2}-(1\leftrightarrow 2)\,,

(118)

\displaystyle\hat{I}^{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Y_{1}^{B}\partial_{B}I^{A}_{2}-I^{B}_{2}\partial_{B}Y_{1}^{A}-(1\leftrightarrow 2)\,,

(119)

and hence, the nonvanishing brackets of the generators read

\displaystyle\{M^{ij},M^{kl}\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\delta^{jk}M^{il}-\delta^{ik}M^{jl}+\delta^{li}M^{jk}-\delta^{lj}M^{ik}\Big)\,,

(120)

\displaystyle\{M^{ij},\mathcal{W}(\theta,\phi)\}

\displaystyle=-\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$D$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{B}(x^{[i}e^{j]B}\mathcal{W}(\theta,\phi))\,,

(121)

\displaystyle\{M^{ij},\mathcal{W}_{A}(\theta,\phi)\}

\displaystyle=\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$D$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{B}(x^{[i}e^{j]B}\mathcal{W}_{A}(\theta,\phi))-x^{[i}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$D$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{A}e^{j]B}\mathcal{W}_{B}(\theta,\phi)\,.

(122)

This is the extended
iso(3)
-BMS algebra.

This enlargment of the
iso(3)
-BMS algebra cannot be obtained as a contraction of the BMS
4
algebra since it contains more spatial supertranslations. It is somewhat reminiscent of the Spi group of
Ashtekar:1978zz
, but the parity of the supertranslations are restricted here.

6
Asymptotic symmetries of the Yang-Mills field in flat Carroll spacetime

Before turning to the coupled Einstein-Yang-Mills system in the Carroll limit, we first consider the asymptotic symmetries of the Yang-Mills field on a flat Carroll background. We start with a brief overview of the asymptotic structure of the Yang-Mills theory in Minkowski space. We then proceed to the analysis of the electric and magnetic Carrollian limits.

6.1
Brief overview of boundary conditions in Yang-Mills theory

The asymptotic structure of Yang-Mills theory has been rigorously studied in
Tanzi:2020fmt
. Here, we give a brief summary of their results.

The Hamiltonian action for the Yang-Mills theory on a Minkowski background in four spacetime dimensions reads

S_{\text{YM}}[A_{i},\pi^{i},A_{0}]=\int dtd^{3}x\left(\pi^{i}_{a}\dot{A}^{a}_{i}-\mathcal{H}+A^{a}_{0}D_{i}\pi^{i}_{a}\right)\,,

(123)

where
\pi^{i}_{a}
is the conjugate momentum to the non-Abelian gauge field
A_{i}=A^{a}_{i}T_{a}
. This canonical pair takes values on some compact semi-simple Lie algebra
[T_{a},T_{b}]=f^{c}_{\;\;ab}T_{c}
. By using the inverse of the invariant metric, one can raise the internal index
a

6.2
Electric Carrollian limit of Yang-Mills

6.2.1
Boundary conditions and asymptotic transformations

We now show that the aforementioned difficulties do not appear in the electric Carrollian limit of Yang-Mills theory, which possesses an infinite-dimensional color symmetry group consistent with Carroll invariance. The situation in the magnetic Carrollian limit, on the other hand, is similar to the Lorentzian case, i.e., one easily verifies that it is not possible to have improper gauge transformations consistent with Carroll invariance. Thus, the asymptotic symmetry algebra is given in that case by the Carroll algebra only. For that reason, from now on we focus on the electric limit, which has a richer structure.

7
Carrollian limits of Einstein-Yang-Mills system

8
Conclusions

In this paper, we have analyzed the asymptotic structure of the electric and magnetic Carrollian limits of the Enstein-Yang-Mills theory. Our results can be summarized as follows:

•

We have first constructed BMS-like extensions of the Carroll group on purely algebraic grounds, without reference to the dynamics. We used the 3+1 description of the symmetry, particularly well suited to rescalings of the energy and the linear momentum involving different powers of
c
. The same techniques applied to the more familiar non-relativistic case have led us to a new BMS-like extension of the Galilean and Bargmann algebras.

•

We have then considered the Carrollian limit of the pure Einstein theory, where we have shown that the electric contraction allows for a larger set of supertranslations involving three functions on the sphere (with definite parity conditions). Technically, this is because the condition
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$h$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{rA}=0
on the leading order of the mixed radial-angular component of the metric, which reduces the number of symmetry generators in the Einstein case, is not needed any more in the electric contraction.

•

We studied next the asymptotic symmetries of Carrollian Yang-Mills theories and have shown that contrary to its Minkowskian parent
Tanzi:2020fmt
, the electric Carroll contraction admits at spatial infinity an infinite set of angle-dependent color gauge symmetries fully compatible with Carroll spacetime covariance, including the Carroll boosts. The result extends directly to the combined Einstein-Yang-Mills system.

Our results can be extended to higher dimensions by following the methods of
Fuentealba:2021yvo
;
Fuentealba:2022yqt
, as well as to the coupled electromagnetic-massless scalar field system for which the asymptotic analysis has been performed in

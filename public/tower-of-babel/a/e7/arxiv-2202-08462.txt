BCFT and Islands in Two Dimensions
Kenta Suzuki

Abstract
By combining the AdS/BCFT correspondence and the brane world holography, we expect an equivalence relation between a boundary conformal field theory (BCFT) and a gravitational system coupled to a CFT. However, it still remains unclear how the boundary condition of the BCFT is translated in the gravitational system. We examine this duality relation in a two-dimensional setup by looking at the computation of entanglement entropy and energy flux conservation. We also identify the two-dimensional gravity which is dual to the boundary dynamics of a BCFT. Moreover, we show that by considering a gravity solution with scalar fields turned on, we can reproduce one point functions correctly in the AdS/BCFT.

In this coordinates, we have the metric

\displaystyle ds_{2}^{2}\,=\,e^{2\phi(x^{+},x^{-})}dx^{+}dx^{-}\,,

(5.2)

\displaystyle e^{2\phi(x^{+},x^{-})}\,

\displaystyle=\,\frac{4\delta^{2}}{(x^{+}+x^{-})^{2}}\qquad{\rm for}\quad x^{+}+x^{-}<-2\delta\,,

\displaystyle=\,1\qquad\qquad\qquad\ \,{\rm for}\quad x^{+}+x^{-}\geq-2\delta\,.

(5.4)

Next, we would like to bring the boundary between the gravitating region and the bath CFT to a periodic circle.
For the gravitating region, this can be implemented by the conformal transformation

The crucial ingredient for the discussion of replica wormhole is the energy flux equation at the intersection between the gravitational region and the bath CFT.

This system is defined by the total action

\displaystyle I_{\rm JT}\,

\displaystyle=\,-\frac{\phi_{0}}{16\pi G}\left[\int d^{2}x\sqrt{g}\phi(R+2)\,+\,2\int dx\sqrt{h}\phi K\right]\,

(5.12)

\displaystyle I_{\rm M}\,

\displaystyle=\,I_{\rm CFT}[g,\chi]\,,

(5.13)

where

denotes a set of the matter fields.

is just a topological contribution and we consider the self-interacting matter fields; namely

does not contain the dilaton field

Therefore, the variation of the dilaton gives

and we can take the Poincare coordinates

\displaystyle ds_{\rm AdS_{2}}^{2}\,=\,\frac{d\tau^{2}+d\eta^{2}}{\eta^{2}}\,.

(5.14)

On the other hand, the variation with respect to the metric gives the dilaton equation

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\phi\,-\,g_{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\sigma}\nabla^{\sigma}\phi\,+\,g_{\mu\nu}\phi\,=\,-8\pi GT^{(M)}_{\mu\nu}\,,

(5.15)

where

In order to consider the dynamical boundary
[

The boundary condition of the metric gives us

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\,=\,\frac{\tau^{\prime 2}+\eta^{\prime 2}}{\eta^{2}}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\eta\,=\,\epsilon\tau^{\prime}\,+\,\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{3})\,,

(5.16)

where the prine denotes a derivative with respect to

\displaystyle\phi\big|_{\rm bdy}\,=\,\frac{\bar{\phi}_{r}}{\epsilon}\,.

(5.17)

The energy flux equation comes from the (

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\partial_{\eta}\phi\,=\,-8\pi GT^{(M)}_{\tau\eta}\,.

(5.18)

By using

\partial_{n}=\epsilon(\eta^{\prime}\partial_{\tau}-\tau^{\prime}\partial_{\eta})

\displaystyle-\partial_{u}\bigg(\frac{\sqrt{h}}{8\pi G}\,\partial_{n}\phi\bigg)\,=\,-(\tau^{\prime})^{2}\,T^{(M)}_{\tau\eta}\,,

(5.19)

up to a singular contribution.
For JT gravity, we note that the ADM energy is given by
[

\displaystyle M\,=\,-\,\frac{\sqrt{h}}{8\pi G}\big(\partial_{n}\phi\,+\,\cdots\big)\big|_{\rm bdy}\,=\,\frac{\bar{\phi}_{r}}{8\pi G}\,\{\tau,u\}\,,

(5.20)

(In appendix

Therefore, the flux equation is now expressed in terms of the change of the ADM energy as

\displaystyle\partial_{u}M\,=\,-(\tau^{\prime})^{2}\,T^{(M)}_{\tau\eta}\,=\,-i(\tau^{\prime})^{2}\big(T_{x^{+}x^{+}}-T_{x^{-}x^{-}}\big)\,.

(5.21)

The discussion of replica wormhole in this system (i.e. JT gravity coupled to conformal matter fields) is summarized in appendix

Now we come back to our AdS/BCFT set-up.
The induced gravity on the brane actually does not have the Schwarzian boundary action as we discussed in section

Therefore, in the present set-up, the boundary energy flux equation is simply given by the bath CFT energy-momentum tensor as

\displaystyle T_{yy}(iu)\,-\,T_{\bar{y}\bar{y}}(-iu)\,=\,0\,,

(5.22)

where

Again this energy flux equation perfectly agrees with the energy reflection equation in a BCFT point of view.

Under the conformal transformation

\displaystyle y\,\to\,z\,\equiv\,F(v)\,,

(5.23)

the energy-momentum tensor transforms as

\displaystyle T_{yy}(iu)\,\to\,e^{\frac{4\pi y}{\beta}}\left[\left(\frac{dF(e^{\frac{2\pi y}{\beta}})}{dv}\right)^{2}T_{zz}\,-\,\frac{c}{24\pi}\left\{F(e^{\frac{2\pi y}{\beta}}),v\right\}\right]\,.

(5.24)

Now we consider the replicated geometry. For this geometry, the uniformizing map is given by
z\to\tilde{z}\equiv z^{1/n}
, such that

.
Therefore, the energy-momentum tensor on the
z
-plane is

\displaystyle T_{zz}\,=\,-\frac{c}{24\pi}\left\{z^{1/n},z\right\}\,=\,-\frac{c}{48\pi}\left(1-\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right)\frac{1}{z^{2}}\,.

(5.25)

Combining all, now the energy flux equation is written as

\displaystyle 0\,=\,ie^{\frac{4\pi iu}{\beta}}\left[\frac{(n^{2}-1)}{2n^{2}}\frac{F^{\prime}(e^{\frac{2\pi iu}{\beta}})^{2}}{F(e^{\frac{2\pi iu}{\beta}})^{2}}\,+\,\left\{F(e^{\frac{2\pi u}{\beta}}),v\right\}\right]\,+\,c.c.

(5.26)

In order to study the

\displaystyle F(v)\,=\,\frac{v-A}{B-v}\,,\qquad A\,=\,e^{-\frac{2\pi a}{\beta}}\,,\quad B\,=\,e^{\frac{2\pi b}{\beta}}\,.

(5.27)

We note that for this choice of
F(v)
, we have

\displaystyle\left\{F(v),v\right\}\,=\,0\,.

(5.28)

This is simply because the choice of
F(v)
in (

) is a special case of Möbius transformation
and for any Möbius transformation the Schwarzian derivative is zero.
Therefore, the energy flux equation becomes

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\,=\,0\,,

(5.29)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\,

\displaystyle=\,ie^{\frac{4\pi iu}{\beta}}\frac{F^{\prime}(v)^{2}}{F(v)^{2}}\,+\,c.c.

\displaystyle=\,i\frac{e^{\frac{4\pi iu}{\beta}}(A-B)^{2}}{(e^{-\frac{2\pi iu}{\beta}}-A)^{2}(e^{\frac{2\pi iu}{\beta}}-B)^{2}}\,+\,c.c.

(5.30)

Furthermore, Fourier transforming the energy flux equation from
u
to
k
, the
k=1

\displaystyle 0\,=\,\int_{0}^{\beta}du\,e^{-\frac{2\pi iu}{\beta}}\,\mathcal{F}\,,

(5.31)

Performing the
\tau

\displaystyle 0\,=\,\frac{\sinh(\frac{\pi(a-b)}{\beta})}{\sinh(\frac{\pi(a+b)}{\beta})}\,.

(5.32)

In order to compare with the quantum extremal surface condition (
2.4
), we need to go back to the infinite straight line boundary.
This is simply obtained by taking
\beta\to\infty
and this leads to

\displaystyle 0\,=\,\frac{a-b}{a+b}\,.

(5.33)

This agrees with the quantum extremal surface condition (
2.4
) and set the location

by
a=b
.

In this section, we have lengthily discussed replica wormholes mainly following

to obtain the externalization equation

.
However, we can summarize what we have done in this section much shortly without replica wormholes.
The crucial equation is again the energy flux conservation equation (
5.22
).
More precisely we require this energy flux conservation on the
z

\displaystyle T_{zz}(z)\,-\,T_{\bar{z}\bar{z}}(\bar{z})\,=\,0\,.

(5.34)

By transforming

\displaystyle z\,\to\,\tilde{z}\,=\,\left(\frac{x^{+}-a}{b-x^{+}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\,,\qquad\bar{z}\,\to\,\bar{\tilde{z}}\,=\,\left(\frac{x^{-}-a}{b-x^{-}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\,,

(5.35)

we consider the vacuum state on the
\tilde{z}
plane i.e.
T_{\tilde{z}\tilde{z}}(\tilde{z})=T_{\bar{\tilde{z}}\bar{\tilde{z}}}(\bar{\tilde{z}})=0
.
Therefore, the stress tensor on the
z

\displaystyle T_{zz}(z)\,

\displaystyle=\,-\frac{c}{24\pi}\big\{\tilde{z},z\big\}\,=\,-\frac{c(1-\frac{1}{n^{2}})}{48\pi}\frac{(a-b)^{2}}{(a-x^{+})^{2}(b-x^{+})^{2}}\,,

(5.36)

\displaystyle T_{\bar{z}\bar{z}}(\bar{z})\,

\displaystyle=\,-\frac{c}{24\pi}\big\{\bar{\tilde{z}},\bar{z}\big\}\,=\,-\frac{c(1-\frac{1}{n^{2}})}{48\pi}\frac{(a-b)^{2}}{(a-x^{-})^{2}(b-x^{-})^{2}}\,,

(5.37)

where
x^{\pm}=\pm i\tau-\delta

6
AdS/BCFT and One Point Function

In general, one point functions in BCFT are non vanishing
[
104
]
. For a scalar primary operator, the one point function looks like

\displaystyle\langle O(y)\rangle=\frac{{\cal N}}{|y|^{\Delta}},

(6.1)

where
|y|
is the distance from the boundary and
\Delta
is the conformal dimension of the scalar primary operator
O
. We also write the overall normalization as
{\cal N}
. Below we will see that
in order to reproduce this non-vanishing one-point function, we need to consider a bulk gravity solution with a non-trivial expectation value of a bulk scalar (see also earlier work
[
59
,
105
]
). Moreover, we need to explain the non-vanishing one point function from the 2d gravity picture to justify the Island/BCFT correspondence. Again we focus on two dimensional BCFTs and we employ the Euclidean signature. We will first study
\Delta=2
case where we can obtain analytical results and later examine
\Delta\neq 2
case numerically. The basic guide line in the gravity dual is that the 3d metric is foliated by AdS
2
, which explains the boundary conformal invariance.

6.1
Massless Bulk Scalar
\Delta=2

As a special case where we have an analytical solution, let us start with
the scalar operator with the dimension
\Delta=2
, which is dual to
a massless bulk scalar
\phi
in the AdS/BCFT setup. This scalar field is described by the standard action:

\displaystyle I=-\frac{1}{16\pi G_{N}}\int d^{3}x\sqrt{g}(R-g^{ab}\partial_{a}\phi\partial_{b}\phi+2).

(6.2)

As shown in
[
106
]
, this has the Janus solution

\displaystyle ds^{2}\,=\,d\rho^{2}+f(\rho)\,ds^{2}_{\rm AdS_{2}}\,,\qquad f(\rho)\,=\,\frac{1+\sqrt{1-2\gamma^{2}}\cosh 2\rho}{2}\,,

\displaystyle\phi\,=\,\phi_{0}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\log\left[\frac{1+\sqrt{1-2\gamma^{2}}+\sqrt{2}\gamma\tanh\rho}{1+\sqrt{1-2\gamma^{2}}-\sqrt{2}\gamma\tanh\rho}\right],

(6.3)

where
ds^{2}_{AdS_{2}}=\frac{d\eta^{2}+d\tau^{2}}{\eta^{2}}

is the Euclidean AdS
2
metric. The parameter
\gamma
describes the amount of the Janus deformation
such that we have the pure AdS
3
solution at
\gamma=0
.

We will show that we can obtain a class of setups of AdS
3
/BCFT
2
from this solution.
We assume the EOW brane
Q
is at
\rho=\rho_{*}
, such that the gravity dual extends in the region
\rho_{*}<\rho<\infty
. Clearly, this background has the isometry of AdS
2
which is dual to the boundary conformal invariance.

For the scalar field
\phi
, we assume the linear interaction on the brane

\displaystyle I_{bdy}=\frac{a}{8\pi G_{N}}\int_{Q}\sqrt{h}\phi,

(6.4)

where
a
is a coupling constant and
h_{ij}
is the induced metric on
Q
.
Combined with the original action, under the variation of
\phi
, the total action
I+I_{bdy}
leads to the Neumann-like boundary condition at
Q
:

\displaystyle\partial_{\rho}\phi|_{\rho=\rho_{*}}=a.

(6.5)

This is satisfied if we set the parameter
\gamma
is related to
a
via

\displaystyle\frac{2\gamma(1+\sqrt{1-2\gamma^{2}})}{(1+\sqrt{1-2\gamma^{2}})^{2}\ \cosh^{2}\rho_{*}-2\gamma^{2}\sinh^{2}\rho_{*}}=a.

(6.6)

The Neumann boundary condition of the gravity coupled to the scalar field reads

\displaystyle K_{ij}-h_{ij}K=-(T+a\phi)h_{ij}.

(6.7)

This is satisfied if

\displaystyle T+a\phi(\rho_{*})\,=\,-\frac{f^{\prime}(\rho_{*})}{2f(\rho_{*})}.

(6.8)

We find for
\rho_{*}\to-\infty
we have
T\to\infty
and vice versa. For a given value of
T
and
a
we can determine the values of
\rho_{*}
and
\gamma
, which give the AdS/BCFT setup. For example, the boundary entropy in this model can be found from the holographic entanglement entropy as we did in (
3.15
) and thus it is given by

\displaystyle S_{bdy}=\frac{c}{6}\rho_{*}.

(6.9)

Now let us calculate the one-point function. In the AdS/CFT on the Poincare coordinate,

\displaystyle ds^{2}=\frac{d\tau^{2}+dy^{2}+dz^{2}}{z^{2}},

(6.10)

the bulk scalar behaves near the AdS boundary
z\to 0
:

\displaystyle\phi(\tau,y,z)\simeq J(\tau,y)z^{2-\Delta}+\alpha(\tau,y)z^{\Delta}+\cdot\cdot\cdot.

(6.11)

Here
J
is interpreted as the source such that it adds the linear interaction
\int d\tau dyJ(\tau,y)O(\tau,y)
and
\alpha
corresponds to the expectation value as

\displaystyle\alpha(\tau,y)=\langle O(\tau,y)\rangle.

(6.12)

In our solution (
6.3
) we find in the
\rho\to\infty
limit:

\displaystyle\phi\simeq\phi_{*}-\frac{4\gamma(1+\sqrt{1-2\gamma^{2}})}{(1+\sqrt{1-2\gamma^{2}})^{2}-2\gamma^{2}}e^{-2\rho}.

(6.13)

Since the Poincare coordinate is related to the AdS
2
slice coordinate via
\frac{y}{z}\simeq\sinh\rho
, we find

\displaystyle\langle O(\tau,y)\rangle\,=\,-\frac{\gamma(1+\sqrt{1-2\gamma^{2}})}{(1+\sqrt{1-2\gamma^{2}})^{2}-2\gamma^{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{y^{2}}.

(6.14)

Since the massless field corresponds to
\Delta=2
, this result indeed agrees with the general form (
6.1
). Note that the coefficient of one point function goes to
\pm\infty
when
\gamma\to\mp 1/\sqrt{2}
. Therefore, we can take any real values of the boundary entropy
S_{bdy}
and the one-point function coefficient
{\cal N}
in this holographic model.

On the other hand, the allowed range of the parameters
(T,a)
in this model
is non-trivially limited as plotted their behaviors as functions
\rho
for various values of
\gamma
in Figure
3
. For example,
a
is always bounded as
|a|\leq\sqrt{2}
, where the equality is saturated in the extreme limit
\gamma\to\pm 1/\sqrt{2}
.
It is also interesting to note that in the original AdS/BCFT model
[
58
,
59
]
without any matter fields, the range of the tension
T
is limited to
|T|\leq 1
. However, in our generalized model with a scalar, this is no longer true.
In the extreme limit
\gamma\to 1/\sqrt{2}
, we find
T\to-2\rho_{*}
, which is no longer bounded.

6.2
Massive Bulk Scalar:
\Delta\neq 2

We would like to extend the previous analysis to a massive scalar so that we can treat operators with
\Delta\neq 2
.
We start with the following general action:

\displaystyle I=-\frac{1}{16\pi G_{N}}\int d^{3}x\sqrt{g}\left[R-g^{ab}\partial_{a}\phi\partial_{b}\phi-U(\phi)\right].

(6.15)

The scalar equation of motion and Einstein equation read

\displaystyle-2\partial_{a}\Big(\sqrt{g}g^{ab}\partial_{b}\phi\Big)+\sqrt{g}U^{\prime}(\phi)\,=\,0\,,

\displaystyle R_{ab}-\frac{1}{2}g_{ab}R+\frac{1}{2}g_{ab}U(\phi)\,=\,-\frac{1}{2}g_{ab}(\partial\phi)^{2}+\partial_{a}\phi\partial_{b}\phi.

(6.16)

where
(\partial\phi)^{2}=g^{ab}\partial_{a}\phi\partial_{b}\phi
.

6.2.1
Ansatz and Boundary Condition

We impose the metric and scalar field ansatz:

\displaystyle ds^{2}=d\rho^{2}+f(\rho)\left(\frac{d\eta^{2}+d\tau^{2}}{\eta^{2}}\right),

\displaystyle\phi=\phi(\rho).

(6.17)

Then the equation of motions take the following form

\displaystyle-2\partial_{\rho}(f\partial_{\rho}\phi)+fU^{\prime}(\phi)=0,

(6.18)

\displaystyle(\partial_{\rho}f)^{2}+4f+2f^{2}U(\phi)-2f^{2}(\partial_{\rho}\phi)^{2}=0,

(6.19)

\displaystyle 2f\partial_{\rho}^{2}f-(\partial_{\rho}f)^{2}+2f^{2}U(\phi)+2f^{2}(\partial_{\rho}\phi)^{2}=0.

(6.20)

The previous massless case can be obtained by choosing
U(\phi)=-2
. For a free massive scalar we have
U(\phi)=-2+m^{2}\phi^{2}
. The null energy condition reads
N^{a}N^{b}R_{ab}\geq 0
.
By choosing
N^{\rho}=1,\ N^{\eta}=0,\ N^{\tau}=i\eta/\sqrt{f}
, this leads to

\displaystyle 2f+(\partial_{\rho}f)^{2}-f\partial_{\rho}^{2}f\,\geq\,0.

(6.21)

We can show that the scalar field equation of motion (
6.18
) is automatically satisfied if the Einstein equations (
6.19
) and (
6.20
) hold.
Therefore the independent equations of motion, which we need to solve, are summarized as

\displaystyle(\partial_{\rho}\phi)^{2}\,=\,\frac{2f+(\partial_{\rho}f)^{2}-f\partial_{\rho}^{2}f}{2f^{2}},

(6.22)

\displaystyle\partial_{\rho}^{2}f+2+2fU(\phi)\,=\,0.

(6.23)

Note that the null energy condition (
6.21
) simply says the obvious fact that the right hand side of (
6.22
) is non-negative. For any given function
\phi(\rho)
we can find
f(\rho)
by solving (
6.22
) and also find the potential
U(\phi)
by solving (
6.23
).

We would like to find a solution which satisfies the boundary condition at the AdS boundary
\rho\to\infty
:

\displaystyle\phi(\rho)\simeq\phi_{1}+\alpha e^{-\Delta\rho},\ \ \ f(\rho)\simeq f_{1}e^{2\rho},

(6.24)

where we can set
\phi_{1}=0
by shifting the scalar field. We also impose the boundary condition on the end of the world-brane
\rho=\rho_{*}
(
6.5
) and (
6.8
). The boundary behavior of
\phi(\rho)
in (
6.24
) shows that the one-point function in BCFT agrees with the general form (
6.1
) and the coefficient
{\cal N}
is proportional to the parameter
\alpha
.

6.2.2
Analytical model at
\Delta=1

For
\Delta=1
, we can find a simple analytical model.
We choose

\displaystyle f(\rho)\,=\,\big(A\cosh\lambda\rho\big)^{2}\,.

(6.25)

This determined the scalar field as

\displaystyle\partial_{\rho}\phi=\frac{\sqrt{1-A^{2}\lambda^{2}}}{A\cosh\lambda\rho}.

(6.26)

Thus we require
A\lambda\leq 1
.
This is solved as follows

\displaystyle\phi=\frac{2\sqrt{1-A^{2}\lambda^{2}}}{A\lambda}\arctan\left[\tanh\frac{\lambda\rho}{2}\right].

(6.27)

In the limit
\rho\to\pm\infty
, it behaves as follows

\displaystyle\phi(\rho)\simeq\frac{2\sqrt{1-A^{2}\lambda^{2}}}{A\lambda}\left(\pm\frac{\pi}{4}\mp e^{\mp\lambda\rho}\right).

(6.28)

Therefore this solution interpolates two criticial points
\phi=\phi_{\pm}\equiv\pm\frac{\pi\sqrt{1-A^{2}\lambda^{2}}}{2A\lambda}
.
The potential is found as

\displaystyle U(\phi)=-2\lambda^{2}-\frac{1-A^{2}\lambda^{2}}{A^{2}}\cos^{2}\left[\frac{A\lambda}{\sqrt{1-A^{2}\lambda^{2}}}\phi\right].

(6.29)

Note that we get the pure AdS
3
solution if we set
A\lambda=1
.

Around
\phi=\phi_{\pm}
, it is expanded as

\displaystyle U(\phi)\simeq-2\lambda^{2}-\lambda^{2}(\phi-\phi_{\pm})^{2}.

(6.30)

Since the AdS radius is
L_{AdS}=\frac{1}{\lambda}
in the limit
|\rho|\to\infty
,
we can obtain the conformal dimension of primary dual to
\phi
as
\Delta=1
. This also agrees with the bahavior in (
6.28
).
In this model, we can limit the space to
\rho_{*}<\rho<\infty
to get the gravity dual of the BCFT.

6.3
Numerical solution for free massive scalar

We consider the free massive dilaton case by setting
U(\phi)=-2+m^{2}\phi^{2}
.
The Einstein equation and the Klein-Gordon equation are given by

\displaystyle f^{\prime\prime}-4f+2\,

\displaystyle=\,-2m^{2}f\phi^{2}\,,

(6.31)

\displaystyle(f^{\prime})^{2}-2ff^{\prime\prime}+4f^{2}\,

\displaystyle=\,2f^{2}(m^{2}\phi^{2}+(\phi^{\prime})^{2})\,,

(6.32)

\displaystyle f\phi^{\prime\prime}+f^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}\,

\displaystyle=\,m^{2}f\phi\,.

(6.33)

The two equations (
6.31
) and (
6.32
) from the Einstein equation are not independent and can be reduced into one equation

\displaystyle(f^{\prime})^{2}-4f^{2}+4f\,=\,2f^{2}\big((\phi^{\prime})^{2}-m^{2}\phi^{2}\big)\,.

(6.34)

Therefore, we need to solve the Klein-Gordon equation (
6.33
) and this equation (
6.34
) simultaneously for
f
and
\phi
.
For the boundary conditions (
6.24
) for
f
and
\phi
, we impose in the
\rho\to\infty
limit

\displaystyle f(\rho)\,

\displaystyle\to\,\frac{1}{4}\,e^{2\rho}\,+\,\frac{1}{2}\,-\,\frac{\alpha^{2}}{8}\,e^{2(1-\Delta)\rho}\,+\,\cdots\,,

(6.35)

\displaystyle\phi(\rho)\,

\displaystyle\to\,\alpha\,e^{-\Delta\rho}\,+\,\cdots\,,

(6.36)

where we kept two different subleading terms for
f(\rho)
because the actual subleading term depends on whether

\Delta<1
(
e^{2(1-\Delta)\rho}
is dominant) or
\Delta>1
(

6.4
One Point Function and Island/BCFT Correspondence

Finally, we would like to come back to the Island/BCFT correspondence.
Since one point functions (
6.1
) are generally non-vanishing in BCFTs, the same should be true in the other description where a 2d CFT is coupled to a 2d induced gravity. The latter theory is described by a 2d CFT on the union of
AdS
2
and a flat half plane as in (
2.2
) with the metric is dynamical only on the AdS
2
region. One may think that it is clear that the one-point function of a primary is vanishing as is true in 2d CFTs on
R^{2}
. Moreover, the dynamical metric on AdS
2
does not seem to change the situation.

However, if we look into the AdS/BCFT solution shown in previous subsections, the non-trivial profile of a scalar field plays a crucial role to reproduce the one-point function. Indeed, we find that the value of the scalar field on the EOW brane
Q
given by
\phi(\rho_{*})
is non-vanishing when
\alpha\neq 0
. This means that there is a source to
O(x)
on the 2d gravity on
Q
. We can write this source by adding
\hat{\alpha}\int d\tau^{\prime}dx^{\prime}\sqrt{h}O(\tau^{\prime},x^{\prime})
to the CFT action, where the external field
\hat{\alpha}
is proportional to
\alpha
when
\alpha

is small. In the presence of such a source we can estimate the one-point function by a perturbation with respect to the source as follows

\displaystyle\langle e^{\hat{\alpha}\int d\tau^{\prime}dx^{\prime}\sqrt{h}O(\tau^{\prime},x^{\prime})}O(\tau,x)\rangle

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\hat{\alpha}\int d\tau^{\prime}dx^{\prime}\sqrt{h}\langle O(\tau^{\prime},x^{\prime})O(\tau,x)\rangle

(6.42)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{\alpha}\int\frac{d\tau^{\prime}dx^{\prime}}{x^{\prime 2}}\frac{(x^{\prime})^{\Delta}}{\left[(x-x^{\prime})^{2}+(\tau-\tau^{\prime})^{2}\right]^{\Delta}}

\displaystyle\propto

\displaystyle\frac{1}{x^{\Delta}},

which indeed reproduces the general form (
6.1
). Note that in the above calculation we took into the Weyl factor
e^{-\Delta\phi}=(x^{\prime})^{\Delta}
from the operator
O(\tau^{\prime},x^{\prime})
inserted on
Q
because the induced metric on
Q
is given by

h_{\tau\tau}=h_{xx}=\frac{1}{(x^{\prime})^{2}}
. In this way we can obtain the non-trivial one point function in a BCFT from the 2d gravity description.

7
Conclusions and Discussions

In this paper, we argued an equivalence relation, which we call Island/BCFT correspondence, between two theories: (i) a CFT in a right half plane coupled to an induced gravity in the left half one, and (ii) a BCFT, as in Figure
2
. We focused on the two dimensional case and
confirmed this equivalence by examining the calculation of entanglement entropy, the boundary condition of energy stress tensor and one-point functions. We also identified the 2d induced gravity which is dual to the boundary dynamics of a 2d BCFT.

The Island prescription of computing entanglement entropy in the theory (i) is equivalent to the mirror charge calculation of the field theoretic entanglement entropy in the theory (ii). The complete reflection boundary condition of energy flux
T_{xt}=0
in (ii) is also obtained in (i) because the dynamical metric in 2d gives the vanishing of energy stress tensor, namely the Virasoro constraint. We derived the 2d induced gravity from its 3d gravity dual and found that it is given by a 2d CFT coupled to a 2d gravity whose action is simply given by the cosmological constant term. After integrating out the CFT fields, we obtain the Liouville action (
4.11
) or more covariantly a specific type of dilaton gravity (
4.16
).
We also showed that to obtain non-vanishing bulk one-point functions in BCFTs (
6.1
), we need to modify the original prescription of AdS/BCFT by considering a non-trivial background of matter fields. The Neumann-like boundary conditions of matter fields (
6.5
) and (
6.7
), imposed on the EOW brane, induce this non-trivial matter field background. We obtained analytical solutions for the calculation of a holographic one point function of an exactly marginal operator. For more general scalar operators we found numerical solutions. We confirmed that this new prescription correctly reproduces non-trivial one point functions. We also explained how we obtain the non-vanishing one point functions from the theory (i). For this we noted that due to the background bulk scalar field in the gravity dual, a source is turned on for the operator dual to the scalar field and this leads to a non-trivial one point function.

There are several interesting future directions. An obvious one is a higher dimensional generalization, which we will come back soon
[
111
]
.
Another important problem is to explore string theory embedding of the Island/BCFT correspondence and see how the coefficients of one-point functions behave in such top down models. It will also be intriguing to extend the AdS/BCFT construction and the Island/BCFT correspondence to gravity duals of more general critical theories
[
112
,
113
,
114
,
115
,
116
]
.

The causal interaction between the subnetworks of a complex network Preprint: Preprint submitted to Entropy–Complexity
X. San Liang

Abstract
Information flow provides a natural measure for the causal interaction between dynamical events. This study extends our previous rigorous formalism of componentwise information flow to the bulk information flow between two complex subsystems of a large-dimensional parental system, in order to investigate problems such as the effective connectivity between two brain regions, each with millions of neurons involved. Analytical formulas have been obtained in a closed form. Under a Gaussian assumption, their maximum likelihood estimators have also been obtained. These formulas have been validated using different subsystems with preset relations, and they yield causalities just as expected. On the contrary, the commonly used proxies for the characterization of subsystems, such as averages and principal components, generally do not work correctly. This study can help diagnose the emergence of patterns in complex systems (e.g., the human brain), and is expected to have applications in many real world problems in different disciplines such as neuroscience, climate science, fluid dynamics, financial economics, to name a few.



I
Introduction

When investigating the properties of a complex system, often it is needed to study the interaction between
one subsystem and another subsystem, which themselves also form complex
systems, usually with a large number of components involved. In climate
science, for example, there is much interest in understanding
how one sector of the system
collaborates with another sector to cause the climate change (see

IPCC2021
and the references therein); in
neuroscience, it is important to investigate the effective connectivity
from one brain region to another, each with millions of neurons involved
(e.g.,
Friston2003
,
Li2011
),
and the interaction between structures
(e.g.,
Friston2014
,
Friston1995
,
Poo2019
; see more
references in a recent review
Poo2020
).
This naturally raises a question: How can we study the interaction between
two subsystems in a large parental system?

An immediate answer coming to mind might be to study the componentwise interactions
by assessing the causalities between the respective components using,
say, the classical causal inference approaches (e.g.,
Granger
,

Pearl
,
Imbens
).
This is generally infeasible if the dimensionality is large.
For two subsystems, each with, say, 1000 components,
it ends up with 1 million causal relations, making it impossible to analyze, albeit with
all the details. In this case, the details do not make a benefit;
they need to be re-analyzed for a big, interpretable picture of the phenomena.
On the other hand, in many situations, this is not necessary;
one needs only a “bulk” description of the
subsystems and their interactions. Such examples are seen from the Reynolds
equations for turbulence (e.g.,
Batchelor
) and the thermodynamic
description of molecular motions (e.g.,
Landau
).
In some fields (e.g., climate science, neuroscience, geography,
etc.), a common practice is simply to take respective averages and
form the mean properties, and to study the interactions between the
proxies, i.e., the mean properties. A more sophisticated way is to extract
the respective principal components (PCs)
(e.g.,
Preisendorfer1998
,
Friston1993_PCA
,

Friston2000_PCA
),
based on which the interactions
are analyzed henceforth. These approaches, as we will be examining in this
study, however may not work satisfactorily; their validities need to be
carefully checked before put to application.

During the past 16 years, it has been gradually realized that causality
in terms of
information flow (IF) is a real physical notion that can be rigorously derived
from first principles (see
Liang2016
).
When two processes interact, IF provides not
only the direction, but also the strength, of the interaction.
So far the formalism of the IF between two components has
been well established; see
LK2005
,
Liang2008
,

Liang2014
,
Liang2016
,
Liang2021a
, among others.
It has been shown promising to extend the formalism to subspaces with many
components involved. A pioneering effort is
Majda2007
,
where the authors show that the heuristic argument in
LK2005
equally
applies to that between subsystems in the case with only one-way causality.
The recent study on the role of individual nodes in a complex network,

Liang2021b
, may be viewed as another effort.
(Causality analyses between subspaces with the classical
approaches are rare; a few examples are
Sadoon2019
,

Triacca2018
, etc.)
But a rigorous formalism for more generic problems (e.g., with mutual
causality involved) is yet to be implemented.
This makes the objective of this study, i.e., to study
the interactions between two complex subsystems within
a large parental system, by investigating
the “bulk” information flow between them.

The rest of the paper is scheduled as follows.
In section
II
,
we first present the setting for the problem,
then derive the IF formulas. Maximum likelihood estimators of
these formulas are given in section
III
,
which is followed by a validation.
Finally section
V
summarizes the study.

II

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial\rho F_{1}}{\partial x_{1}}+\frac{\partial\rho F_{2}}{\partial x_{2}}+...+\frac{\partial\rho F_{n}}{\partial x_{n}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{2}g_{ij}\rho}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}},

\displaystyle\rho_{r+1,...s}=\int_{\R^{n-s+r}}\rho({\bf x})dx_{1}...dx_{r}dx_{s+1}...dx_{n}\equiv\int_{\R^{n-s+r}}\rho({\bf x})d{\bf x}_{1,..r,s+1,..n}.

With respect to them the joint entropies are then

\displaystyle H_{A}=-\int_{\R^{r}}\rho_{1...r}\log\rho_{1...r}d{\bf x}_{1...r},

(14)

\displaystyle H_{B}=-\int_{\R^{s-r}}\rho_{r+1,...s}\log\rho_{r+1,...s}d{\bf x}_{r+1,...s}.

(15)

To derive the evolution of
\rho_{1...r}
,

This yields, by using the assumption of compactness for

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\rho_{1...r}}{\partial t}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{\partial\ }{\partial x_{i}}\int_{\R^{n-r}}\rho F_{i}d{\bf x}_{r+1,...n}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\int_{\R^{n-r}}\frac{\partial^{2}g_{ij}\rho}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}d{\bf x}_{r+1,...n}.

(16)

Similarly,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\rho_{r+1,...s}}{\partial t}+\sum_{i=r+1}^{s}\frac{\partial\ }{\partial x_{i}}\int_{\R^{n-s+r}}\rho F_{i}d{\bf x}_{1,..r,s+1,..n}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=r+1}^{s}\sum_{j=r+1}^{s}\int_{\R^{n-s+r}}\frac{\partial^{2}g_{ij}\rho}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}d{\bf x}_{1,..r,s+1,..n}.

(17)

\displaystyle\qquad=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\R^{r}}\left[(1+\log\rho_{1...r})\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\int_{\R^{n-r}}\frac{\partial^{2}g_{ij}\rho}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}d{\bf x}_{r+1,...n}\right]\ d{\bf x}_{1...r}.

Note that in the second term of the left hand side,
the part within the summation is, by integration by parts,

\displaystyle\int_{\R^{r}}(\log\rho_{1...r})\cdot\frac{\partial\ }{\partial x_{i}}\left(\int_{\R^{n-r}}\rho F_{i}d{\bf x}_{r+1,...n}\right)d{\bf x}_{1...r}

\displaystyle\qquad=-\int_{\R^{r}}\int_{\R^{n-r}}\rho F_{i}\frac{\partial\log\rho_{1...r}}{\partial x_{i}}d{\bf x}_{r+1,...n}d{\bf x}_{1...r}

\displaystyle\qquad=-\int_{\R^{n}}\rho F_{i}\frac{\partial\log\rho_{1...r}}{\partial x_{i}}\ d{\bf x}

By the same approach, the right hand side becomes

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\R^{r}}\left[\log\rho_{1...r}\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\int_{\R^{n-r}}\frac{\partial^{2}g_{ij}\rho}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}d{\bf x}_{r+1,...n}\right]d{\bf x}_{1...r}

\displaystyle\qquad=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\int_{\R^{n}}\left[\log\rho_{1...r}\cdot\frac{\partial^{2}g_{ij}\rho}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\right]d{\bf x}

\displaystyle\qquad=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\int_{\R^{n}}\left[g_{ij}\rho\frac{\partial^{2}\log\rho_{1...r}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\right]d{\bf x}

\displaystyle\frac{dH_{A}}{dt}=-\sum_{i=1}^{r}E\left[F_{i}\frac{\partial\log\rho_{1...r}}{\partial x_{i}}\right]-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{r}E\left[g_{ij}\frac{\partial^{2}\log\rho_{1...r}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\right].

(18)

\displaystyle\frac{dH_{B,{\backslash\hskip-2.45836ptA}}}{dt}=\lim_{{\Delta t}\to 0}\frac{H_{B,{\backslash\hskip-2.45836ptA}}-H_{B}(t)}{{\Delta t}}

\displaystyle\qquad=-E\sum_{i=r+1}^{n}\left(F_{i}\frac{\partial\log\rho_{r+1,...s}}{\partial x_{i}}-\frac{1}{\rho_{r+1,...s}}\int_{\R^{n-s}}\frac{\partial F_{i}\rho_{r+1,...n}}{\partial x_{i}}d{\bf x}_{s+1,...n}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-\frac{1}{2}E\sum_{i=r+1}^{s}\sum_{j=r+1}^{s}\left(g_{ij}\frac{\partial^{2}\log\rho_{r+1,...s}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}+\frac{1}{\rho_{r+1,...s}}\frac{\partial^{2}\ }{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\int_{\R^{n-s}}{g_{ij}\rho_{r+1,...n}}d{\bf x}_{s+1,...n}\right).

Hence the information flow from
{\bf x}_{1...r}
to
{\bf x}_{r+1,...s}
is

\displaystyle T_{A\to B}=\frac{dH_{B}}{dt}-\frac{dH_{B,{\backslash\hskip-2.45836ptA}}}{dt}

\displaystyle\qquad=-E\sum_{i=r+1}^{s}\left(F_{i}\frac{\partial\log\rho_{r+1,...s}}{\partial x_{i}}\right)-\frac{1}{2}E\sum_{i=r+1}^{s}\sum_{j=r+1}^{s}\left(g_{ij}\frac{\partial^{2}\log\rho_{r+1,...s}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\ \ \ \ +E\sum_{i=r+1}^{s}\left(F_{i}\frac{\partial\log\rho_{r+1,...s}}{\partial x_{i}}-\frac{1}{\rho_{r+1,...s}}\int_{\R^{n-s}}\frac{\partial F_{i}\rho_{r+1,...n}}{\partial x_{i}}d{\bf x}_{s+1,...n}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\ \ \ \ +\frac{1}{2}E\sum_{i=r+1}^{s}\sum_{j=r+1}^{s}\left(g_{ij}\frac{\partial^{2}\log\rho_{r+1,...s}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}+\frac{1}{\rho_{r+1,...s}}\int_{\R^{n-s}}\frac{\partial^{2}g_{ij}\rho_{r+1,...n}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}d{\bf x}_{s+1,...n}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=-E\left[\sum_{i=r+1}^{s}\frac{1}{\rho_{r+1,...s}}\int_{\R^{n-s}}\frac{\partial F_{i}\rho_{r+1,...n}}{\partial x_{i}}d{\bf x}_{s+1,...n}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\ \ \ \ +\frac{1}{2}E\left[\sum_{i=r+1}^{s}\sum_{j=r+1}^{s}\frac{1}{\rho_{r+1,...s}}\int_{\R^{n-s}}\frac{\partial^{2}g_{ij}\rho_{r+1,...n}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}d{\bf x}_{s+1,...n}\right].

Likewise, we can get the information flow from subsystem
B
to subsystem
A
.
These are summarized in the following theorem.

Theorem II.1

For the dynamical system (
II
)-(
II
), if the probability
density function (pdf) of
{\bf x}
is compactly supported, then
the information flow from
{\bf x}_{1...r}
to
{\bf x}_{r+1,...s}
, and
that from
{\bf x}_{r+1,...s}
to
{\bf x}_{1...r}
are (in nats per unit
time), respectively,

\displaystyle T_{A\to B}=-E\left[\sum_{i=r+1}^{s}\frac{1}{\rho_{r+1,...s}}\int_{\R^{n-s}}\frac{\partial F_{i}\rho_{r+1,...n}}{\partial x_{i}}d{\bf x}_{s+1,...n}\right]

(31)

\displaystyle\qquad\ \ \ \ +\frac{1}{2}E\left[\sum_{i=r+1}^{s}\sum_{j=r+1}^{s}\frac{1}{\rho_{r+1,...s}}\int_{\R^{n-s}}\frac{\partial^{2}g_{ij}\rho_{r+1,...n}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}d{\bf x}_{s+1,...n}\right],

(32)

\displaystyle T_{B\to A}=-E\left[\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{1}{\rho_{1...r}}\int_{\R^{n-s}}\frac{\partial F_{i}\rho_{1,..r,s+1,..n}}{\partial x_{i}}d{\bf x}_{s+1,...n}\right]

(33)

\displaystyle\qquad\ \ \ \ +\frac{1}{2}E\left[\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\frac{1}{\rho_{1...r}}\int_{\R^{n-s}}\frac{\partial^{2}g_{ij}\rho_{1,..r,s+1,..n}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}d{\bf x}_{s+1,...n}\right],

(34)

where
g_{ij}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}b_{ik}b_{jk}
,
and
E
denotes mathematical expectation.

When
r=1
,
s=n=2
, (
33
) reduces to

\displaystyle T_{B\to A}=-E\left[\frac{1}{\rho_{1}}\frac{\partial F_{1}\rho_{1}}{\partial x_{1}}\right]+\frac{1}{2}E\left[\frac{1}{\rho_{1}}\frac{\partial^{2}g_{11}\rho_{1}}{\partial x_{1}^{2}}\right]

which is precisely the same as the Eq. (15) in
Liang2008
; same
holds for (
31
). These equations are hence verified.

The following theorem forms the basis for causal inference.

Theorem II.2

If the evolution of subsystem
A
(resp.
B
) does not depend
on
{\bf x}_{r+1,...s}
(resp.
{\bf x}_{1...r}
), then

T_{B\to A}=0
(resp.
T_{A\to B}=0
).

Proof.
We only check the formula for
T_{B\to A}
.
In (
33
), the deterministic part

\displaystyle-E\left[\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{1}{\rho_{1...r}}\int_{\R^{n-s}}\frac{\partial F_{i}\rho_{1,..r,s+1,..n}}{\partial x_{i}}d{\bf x}_{s+1,...n}\right]

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i=1}^{r}\int_{\R^{r}}\int_{\R^{s-r}}\left[\left(\frac{\rho_{1,...,s}}{\rho_{1...r}}\right)\int_{\R^{n-s}}\frac{\partial F_{i}\rho_{1,..r,s+1,..n}}{\partial x_{i}}d{\bf x}_{s+1,...n}\right]d{\bf x}_{1...r}d{\bf x}_{r+1,...s}.

Now
F_{i}
is independent of
{\bf x}_{r+1,...s}
, and note that
\rho_{1,..r,s+1,..n}

is also so. Thus we may integrate
\rho_{1,..,s}
within the parenthesis
directly with respect to
d{\bf x}_{r+1,...s}
, yielding

\displaystyle{\frac{\int_{\R^{s-r}}\rho_{1,...,s}d{\bf x}_{r+1,...s}}{\rho_{1...r}}}=\frac{\rho_{1...r}}{\rho_{1...r}}=1.

By the compactness of
\rho
, the whole deterministic part hence vanishes.
Likewise, it can be proved that the stochastic part also vanishes.

This theorem allows us to identify the causality with information flow.
Ideally, if
T_{B\to A}=0
, then
B
is not causal to
A
, and vice versa;
same holds for
T_{A\to B}
.

III
Information flow between linear subsystems and its estimation

Linear systems provide the simplest framework which is usually taken as the
first step toward a more generic setting. Simple as it may be, it has been
demonstrated in practice that linear results often provide a good
approximation of an otherwise much more complicated problem.
It is thence of interest to examine this special case.

Let

\displaystyle F_{i}=f_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{ij}x_{j},

(35)

where
f_{i}
and
a_{ij}
are constants.
Also suppose that
b_{ij}
are constants; that is to say, the noises are
additive. Then
g_{ij}
are also constants. Thus, in (
33
),

\displaystyle E\left(\frac{1}{\rho_{1...r}}\int_{n-s}\frac{\partial^{2}g_{ij}\rho_{1,..r,s+1,..n}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}d{\bf x}_{s+1,...n}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=g_{ij}\int_{\R^{s}}\rho({\bf x}_{1...s}){\frac{1}{\rho_{1...r}}\frac{\partial^{2}\int\rho_{1,..r,s+1,..n}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}d{\bf x}_{s+1,...n}}d{\bf x}_{1...s}

\displaystyle\qquad=g_{ij}\int_{\R^{r}}\int_{\R^{s-r}}\frac{\rho_{1...s}}{\rho_{1...r}}\frac{\partial^{2}\rho_{1...r}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}d{\bf x}_{r+1,...s}d{\bf x}_{1...r}

\displaystyle\qquad=g_{ij}\int_{\R^{r}}1\cdot\frac{\partial^{2}\rho_{1...r}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}d{\bf x}_{1...r}

\displaystyle\qquad=0.

Same holds in (
31
).
So the stochastic parts in both (
31
) and (
33
) vanish.

Since a linear system initialized with a Gaussian process will always be
Gaussian, we may write
the joint pdf of
{\bf x}
as

\displaystyle\rho(x_{1},...,x_{n})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{(2\pi)^{n}\det{\bm{\Sigma}}}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}({\bf x}-{\bm{\mu}})^{T}{\bm{\Sigma}}^{-1}({\bf x}-{\bm{\mu}})},

(36)

where
{\bm{\Sigma}}=(\sigma_{ij})_{n\times n}

is the population covariance matrix of
{\bf x}
.
By the property of Gaussian process,
it is easy to show

\displaystyle\rho_{r+1,...s}(x_{r+1},...,x_{s})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{(2\pi)^{s-r}\det{\bm{\Sigma}}_{B}}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}({\bf x}_{B}-{\bm{\mu}}_{B})^{T}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{B}^{-1}({\bf x}_{B}-{\bm{\mu}}_{B})},

(37)

where

{\bf x}_{B}=(x_{r+1},...,x_{s})
,

{\bm{\mu}}_{B}=(\mu_{r+1},...,\mu_{s})
is the vector of the means of

{\bf x}_{B}
, and
{\bm{\Sigma}}_{B}
the covariance matrix of
{\bf x}_{B}
.
For easy correspondence, we will augment
{\bf x}_{B}
,
{\bm{\mu}}_{B}
,
and
{\bm{\Sigma}}_{B}
,
so that their entries have the same indices as their counterparts
in
{\bf x}
,
{\bm{\mu}}
and
{\bm{\Sigma}}
.
Separate
F_{i}
into two parts

\displaystyle F_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[f_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{r}a_{ij}x_{j}+\sum_{j=s+1}^{n}a_{ij}x_{j}\right]+\left[\sum_{j=r+1}^{s}a_{ij}x_{j}\right]

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle F^{\prime}_{i}+F^{\prime\prime}_{i},

where
F^{\prime}_{i}
and
F^{\prime\prime}_{i}
correspond to the respective parts in the two
square brackets. So
F^{\prime}_{i}
has nothing to do with the subspace
B
. By
Theorem
II.2
, this part does not contribute to the causality
from
A
to
B
, so we only need to consider
F^{\prime\prime}_{i}
in evaluating
T_{A\to B}
; that is to say,

\displaystyle T_{A\to B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-E\left[\sum_{i=r+1}^{s}\frac{1}{\rho_{r+1,...s}}\frac{\partial\ }{\partial x_{i}}{\int_{\R^{n-s}}F_{i}\rho_{r+1,...n}d{\bf x}_{s+1,...n}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-E\left[\sum_{i=r+1}^{s}\frac{1}{\rho_{r+1,...s}}\frac{\partial\ }{\partial x_{i}}{\int_{\R^{n-s}}F^{\prime\prime}_{i}\rho_{r+1,...n}d{\bf x}_{s+1,...n}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-E\left[\sum_{i=r+1}^{s}\frac{1}{\rho_{r+1,...s}}\frac{\partial F^{\prime\prime}_{i}\rho_{r+1,...s}}{\partial x_{i}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=r+1}^{s}\left[E\left(F^{\prime\prime}_{i}\frac{\partial\log\rho_{r+1,...s}}{\partial x_{i}}\right)+E\left(\frac{\partial F^{\prime\prime}_{i}}{\partial x_{i}}\right)\right].

The second term in the bracket is
a_{ii}
. The first term is

\displaystyle F^{\prime\prime}_{i}\frac{\partial\log\rho_{r+1,...s}}{\partial x_{i}}=\left[\sum_{j=r+1}^{s}a_{ij}x_{j}\right]\cdot\frac{\partial\ }{\partial x_{i}}\left[-\frac{1}{2}({\bf x}_{B}-{\bm{\mu}}_{B})^{T}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{B}^{-1}({\bf x}_{B}-{\bm{\mu}}_{B})\right]

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{j=r+1}^{s}a_{ij}x_{j}\right)\dot{\sum}_{j=r+1}^{s}\left(-\frac{\sigma_{ij}^{\prime}+\sigma_{ji}^{\prime}}{2}\right)\cdot(x_{j}-\mu_{j}).

Here
\sigma_{ij}^{\prime}
is the
(i,j)^{\rm th}
entry of the matrix

\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}{\bf I}&{\bf 0}&{\bf 0}\\
{\bf 0}&{\bm{\Sigma}}_{B}^{-1}&{\bf 0}\\
{\bf 0}&{\bf 0}&{\bf I}\end{array}\right].

Since here only
1\leq i,j\leq s
are in question, this is equal to the

(i,j)^{\rm th}
entry of the matrix

\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}{\bf I}_{r\times r}&{\bf 0}_{r\times(s-r)}\\
{\bf 0}_{(s-r)\times r}&{\bm{\Sigma}}_{B}^{-1}\end{array}\right]

As
{\bm{\Sigma}}_{B}
is symmetric, so is
{\bm{\Sigma}}_{B}^{-1}
,
and hence
(\sigma_{ij}^{\prime}+\sigma_{ji}^{\prime})/2=\sigma_{ij}^{\prime}
.
So

\displaystyle-EF_{i}\frac{\partial\log\rho_{r+1,...s}}{\partial x_{i}}=-E\sum_{j=1}^{s}a_{ij}x_{j}\cdot\sum_{j=r+1}^{s}(-\sigma_{ij}^{\prime})\cdot(x_{j}-\mu_{j})

\displaystyle=E\sum_{k=1}^{s}a_{ik}(x_{k}-\mu_{k})\cdot\sum_{j=r+1}^{s}\sigma_{ij}^{\prime}(x_{j}-\mu_{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{s}\sum_{j=r+1}^{s}a_{ik}\sigma_{ij}^{\prime}E(x_{k}-\mu_{k})(x_{j}-\mu_{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{s}\sum_{j=r+1}^{s}a_{ik}\sigma_{ij}^{\prime}\sigma_{k}j.

Substituting back, we obtain a very simplified result for

T_{A\to B}
. Likewise
T_{B\to A}
can also be obtained, as shown in the
following.

Theorem III.1

In (
II
)-(
II
), suppose
b_{ij}
are
constants, and

\displaystyle F_{i}=f_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{ij}x_{j},

(40)

where
f_{i}
and
a_{ij}
are also constants.
Further suppose that initially
{\bf x}
has a Gaussian distribution,
then

\displaystyle T_{A\to B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=r+1}^{s}\left[\sum_{j=r+1}^{s}\sigma_{ij}^{\prime}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{s}a_{ik}\sigma_{kj}\right)-a_{ii}\right],

(41)

where
\sigma_{ij}^{\prime}
is the
(i,j)^{\rm th}
entry of

\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}{\bf I}_{r\times r}&{\bf 0}\\
{\bf 0}&{\bm{\Sigma}}_{B}^{-1}\end{array}\right]
, and

\displaystyle T_{B\to A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{r}\sigma_{ij}^{\prime\prime}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{s}a_{ik}\sigma_{kj}\right)-a_{ii}\right],

(42)

where
\sigma_{ij}^{\prime\prime}
is the
(i,j)^{\rm th}
entry of

\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{A}^{-1}&{\bf 0}\\
{\bf 0}&{\bf I}_{(s-r)\times(s-r)}\end{array}\right]
.

Given a system like (
II
)-(
II
), we can
evaluate in a precise sense the information flows among the components.
Now suppose, instead of the dynamical system, what we have are just
n

time series with
K
steps,
K\gg n
,

\{x_{1}(k)\},\{x_{2}(k)\},...,\{x_{n}(k)\}
.
We can estimate the system from the
series, and then apply the information flow formula to fulfill the task.
Assume a linear model as shown above, and assume
m=1
.
following Liang (2014)
Liang2014
,
the maximum likelihood estimator (mle) of
a_{ij}

is equal to the least-square solution of the following
over-determined problem

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}1&x_{1}(1)&x_{2}(1)&...&x_{n}(1)\\
1&x_{1}(2)&x_{2}(2)&...&x_{n}(2)\\
1&x_{1}(3)&x_{2}(3)&...&x_{n}(3)\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\ddots&\vdots\\
1&x_{1}(K)&x_{2}(K)&...&x_{n}(K)\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}f_{i}\\
a_{i1}\\
a_{i2}\\
\vdots\\
a_{in}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\dot{x}_{i}(1)\\
\dot{x}_{i}(2)\\
\dot{x}_{i}(3)\\
\vdots\\
\dot{x}_{i}(K)\end{array}\right)

where
\dot{x}_{i}(k)=(x_{i}(k+1)-x_{i}(k))/{\Delta t}

(
{\Delta t}
is the time stepsize),
for
i=1,2,...,n
,
k=1,...,K
.
Use overbar to denote the time mean over the
K
steps.
The above equation is

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}1&\bar{x}_{1}&\bar{x}_{2}&...&\bar{x}_{n}\\
0&x_{1}(2)-\bar{x}_{1}&x_{2}(2)-\bar{x}_{2}&...&x_{n}(2)-\bar{x}_{n}\\
0&x_{1}(3)-\bar{x}_{1}&x_{2}(3)-\bar{x}_{2}&...&x_{n}(3)-\bar{x}_{n}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\ddots&\vdots\\
0&x_{1}(K)-\bar{x}_{1}&x_{2}(K)-\bar{x}_{2}&...&x_{n}(K)-\bar{x}_{n}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}f_{i}\\
a_{i1}\\
a_{i2}\\
\vdots\\
a_{in}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\bar{\dot{x}}_{i}\\
\dot{x}_{i}(2)-\bar{\dot{x}}_{i}\\
\dot{x}_{i}(3)-\bar{\dot{x}}_{i}\\
\vdots\\
\dot{x}_{i}(K)-\bar{\dot{x}}_{i}\end{array}\right)

Denote by
{\bf R}
the matrix

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}x_{1}(2)-\bar{x}_{1}&x_{2}(2)-\bar{x}_{2}&...&x_{n}(2)-\bar{x}_{n}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\ddots&\vdots\\
x_{1}(K)-\bar{x}_{1}&x_{2}(K)-\bar{x}_{2}&...&x_{n}(K)-\bar{x}_{n}\end{array}\right),

{\bf q}
the vector

(x_{i}(2)-\bar{\dot{x}}_{i},...,x_{i}(K)-\bar{\dot{x}}_{i})^{T}
,
and
{\bf a}_{i}
the row vector
(a_{i1},...,a_{in})^{T}
.
Then
{\bf R}{\bf a}_{i}={\bf q}
. The least square solution of
{\bf a}_{i}
,

{\bf\hat{a}}_{i}
, solves

\displaystyle{\bf R}^{T}{\bf R}{\bf\hat{a}}_{i}={\bf R}^{T}{\bf q}.

Note
{\bf R}^{T}{\bf R}
is
K{\bf C}
, where
{\bf C}=(c_{ij})

is the sample covariance matrix. So

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\hat{a}_{i1}\\
\hat{a}_{i2}\\
\vdots\\
\hat{a}_{in}\end{array}\right)={\bf C}^{-1}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}c_{1,di}\\
c_{2,di}\\
\vdots\\
c_{n,di}\end{array}\right)

where
c_{j,di}
is the sample covariance between the series
\{x_{j}(k)\}
and

\{(x_{i}(k+1)-x_{i}(k))/{\Delta t}\}
.

So finally, the mle of
T_{A\to B}
is

\displaystyle\hat{T}_{A\to B}=\sum_{i=r+1}^{s}\left[\sum_{j=r+1}^{s}c_{ij}^{\prime}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{s}\hat{a}_{ik}c_{kj}\right)-\hat{a}_{ii}\right],

(54)

where
c_{ij}^{\prime}
is the
(i,j)^{\rm th}
entry of
\tilde{{\bf C}}^{-1}
, and

\displaystyle\tilde{{\bf C}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}{{\bf I}_{r\times r}}&{{\bf 0}}_{r\times(s-r)}\\
{{\bf 0}_{(s-r)\times r}}&{\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}c_{r+1,r+1}&...&c_{r+1,s}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
c_{s,r+1}&...&c_{s,s}\end{array}\right]}\end{array}\right).

Likewise,

\displaystyle\hat{T}_{B\to A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{r}c_{ij}^{\prime\prime}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{s}\hat{a}_{ik}c_{kj}\right)-\hat{a}_{ii}\right]

(61)

Here

\displaystyle\tilde{\tilde{{\bf C}}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}{\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}c_{11}&...&c_{1r}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
c_{r1}&...&c_{rr}\end{array}\right]}&{{\bf 0}_{r\times(s-r)}}\\
{{\bf 0}_{(s-r)\times r}}&{{\bf I}_{(s-r)\times(s-r)}}\end{array}\right),

and
c^{\prime\prime}_{ij}
is the
(i,j)th
entry of
{\tilde{\tilde{{\bf C}}}}^{{}_{-}1}
.

When
n=2
and
r=1
, hence
s=1
,

\tilde{\tilde{{\bf C}}}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}c_{11}&0\\
0&1\end{array}\right]
, so

c^{\prime\prime}_{11}=c_{11}^{-1}
. Eq. (
61
) hence becomes

\displaystyle\hat{T}_{B\to A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{11}^{\prime\prime}\sum_{k=1}^{2}\hat{a}_{1k}c_{k1}-\hat{a}_{11}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c_{11}}(\hat{a}_{11}c_{11}+\hat{a}_{12}c_{12})-\hat{a}_{11}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{c_{11}c_{12}c_{2,d1}-c_{12}^{2}c_{1,d1}}{c_{11}^{2}-c_{11}c_{12}^{2}},

recovering the well-known Eq. (10) in
Liang2014
.

IV.1
One-way causal relation

To see if the above formalism works, consider the vector autoregressive

The incorrect inference based on averaging is within expectation. In a
network with complex causal relations, for example, with a causality from

in selecting the most coherent structure, discards most of the
influences from other (implicit) time steps. But the relative success
of PCA may not be robust, as evidenced in the following mutually causal case.

V
Summary

Information flow makes a natural measure of the causal interaction between
dynamical events. In this study, the information flows between
two complex subsystems of a large dimensional system are studied,
and analytical formulas have been obtained in a closed form. For easy
reference, summarized hereafter are the major results.

For an
n
-dimensional system

\displaystyle\frac{d{\bf x}}{dt}={\bf F}({\bf x},t)+{\bf B}({\bf x},t)\dot{{\bf w}},

if the probability
density function (pdf) of
{\bf x}
is compactly supported, then
the information flows from subsystem
A
, which are made of
{\bf x}_{1...r}
,
to subsystem
B
made of
{\bf x}_{r+1,...s}
(
1\leq r<s\leq n
), and
that from
B
to
A
are, respectively (in nats per unit time),

\displaystyle T_{A\to B}=-E\left[\sum_{i=r+1}^{s}\frac{1}{\rho_{r+1,...s}}\int_{\R^{n-s}}\frac{\partial F_{i}\rho_{r+1,...n}}{\partial x_{i}}d{\bf x}_{s+1,...n}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\ \ \ \ +\frac{1}{2}E\left[\sum_{i=r+1}^{s}\sum_{j=r+1}^{s}\frac{1}{\rho_{r+1,...s}}\int_{\R^{n-s}}\frac{\partial^{2}g_{ij}\rho_{r+1,...n}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}d{\bf x}_{s+1,...n}\right],

\displaystyle T_{B\to A}=-E\left[\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{1}{\rho_{1...r}}\int_{\R^{n-s}}\frac{\partial F_{i}\rho_{1,..r,s+1,..n}}{\partial x_{i}}d{\bf x}_{s+1,...n}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\ \ \ \ +\frac{1}{2}E\left[\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\frac{1}{\rho_{1...r}}\int_{\R^{n-s}}\frac{\partial^{2}g_{ij}\rho_{1,..r,s+1,..n}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}d{\bf x}_{s+1,...n}\right],

where
g_{ij}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}b_{ik}b_{jk}
,
and
E
signifies mathematical expectation.
Given
n
stationary time series,
T_{A\to B}
and
T_{B\to A}
can be estimated.
The maximum likelihood estimators under a Gaussian assumption are referred
to Eqs. (
54
) and (
61
).

We have constructed a VAR process to validate the formalism. The system has
a dimension of 6, with 2 subsystems respectively denoted by
X
and
Y
,
each with a dimension of 3.
X
drives
Y
via the coupling at one
component, and
Y
feedbacks to
X
via another. The detailed,
componentwise causal relation can be easily found using our previous
algorithms such as that in
Liang2021a
. It is expected
that the bulk information flow should in general also follow a similar
trend, though the structure could be in a more coarse and mild fashion, as
now displayed is an overall property. The above formalism does yield such a
result. On the contrary, the commonly used proxies for subsystems, such as
averages and principal components (PCs), generally do not work. Particularly,
the averaged series yield the wrong results in the two cases considered in
this study; the PC series do not work, either, for the mutually causal
case, though they result in a satisfactory characterization
for the case with a one-way causality.

The result of this study is applicable in many real world problems.
As explained in the Introduction, it will be of particular use in
related fields of
climate science, neuroscience, financial economics, fluid mechanics,
among others.
For example, it helps clarify the role of greenhouse gas emission
in bridging the climate system
and the socio-economic system (see a review in
Tachiiri2021
).
Likewise, the interaction between the earth system and public health

Balbus2016
can also be studied.
In short, it is expected to play a role in the frontier field of
complexity, namely, multiplex networks, or networks of networks
(see the references in
Agostino2014
,
Kenett2015
,

DeFord2019
).
We are therefore working on these applications.

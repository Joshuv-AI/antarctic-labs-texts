1.
Introduction

All groups considered in the present paper are finite. The last term of the lower central series of a group
G
is called the nilpotent residual. It is usually denoted by
\gamma_{\infty}(G)
. The lower Fitting series of
G
is defined by
D_{0}(G)=G
and
D_{i+1}(G)=\gamma_{\infty}(D_{i}(G))
for
i=0,1,2,\dots
.

Here the notation
|x|
stands for the order of the element
x
in a group
G
. The following criterion of nilpotency of a finite group was established by Baumslag and Wiegold in
[
2
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group in which
|ab|=|a||b|
whenever the elements
a,b
have coprime orders. Then
G
is nilpotent.

The following result was obtained by Bastos and Shumyatsky
[
3
]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
G
be a finite group in which
|ab|=|a||b|
whenever the elements
a,b
are commutators of coprime orders. Then
G^{{}^{\prime}}
is nilpotent.

In
[
3
]
the following question is posed.
Let
w
be a commutator word and
G
a finite group with the property that if
a,b
are
w
-values of coprime order, then
|ab|=|a||b|
. Is then the verbal subgroup
w(G)
nilpotent?
A coprime commutator in a group
G
is a commutator
[x,y]
with
|x|
and
|y|
. The main result of this article is to obtain a similar result of the Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
for the powers of coprime commutators.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
G
be a finite group and set

X=\{[a,b]^{k}|\,a,b\in G,(|a|,|b|)=1,k\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

Assume that for all
x,y\in X
with
|x|
and
|y|
coprimes, we have
|xy|=|x||y|
. Then
\gamma_{\infty}(G)
is nilpotent. In particular,
G
has Fitting height at most
2
.

The coprime commutators
\gamma_{k}^{*}
and
\delta_{k}^{*}
have been introduced in
[
6
]
by Shumyatsky as a tool to study properties of finite groups that can be expressed in terms of commutators of elements of coprime orders. The definition of
\delta_{k}^{*}
goes as follows (for more details see
[
6
]
). Every element of
G
is a
\delta_{0}^{*}
-commutator. Now, let
k\geq 1
, let
T
be the set of all elements of
G
that are powers of
\delta_{k-1}^{*}
-commutators. The element
g
is a
\delta_{k}^{*}
-commutator if there exist
a,b\in T
such that
g=[a,b]
and
(|a|,|b|)=1
. It was shown in
[
6
]
that for every
k\geq 0
the subgroup generated by
\delta_{k}^{*}
-commutators is precisely
D_{k}(G)
.

Remark that the set
X
in the Theorem
1.3
is precisely the set of generators of
D_{1}(G)
, that is,
D_{1}(G)=\langle X\rangle
. Consequently we could ask the following question, which extends Theorem
1.3
.

Problem 1.4
.

Let be
k
a non-negative integer and
G
a finite group in which
|ab|=|a||b|
whenever the elements
a,b
are (powers of)
\delta_{k}^{*}
-commutators of coprime orders. It is true that the subgroup
D_{k}(G)
is nilpotent?

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout the remaining part of this short note,
G
denotes a finite group satisfying the hypothesis of Theorem
1.3
. For simplicity, we denote by
X
the set of all powers of
\delta_{1}^{*}
-commutators of
G
. As usual,
O_{\pi}(M)
stands for the maximal normal
\pi
-subgroup of a group
M
. The Fitting subgroup of
M
is denoted by
F(M)
. We start with the following known lemma.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
5
]
, Lemma 4.28)
.

Let
A
and
G
be finite groups. Let
A
act via automorphisms on
G
, and suppose that
(|A|,|G|)=1
. Then
G=[G,A]C_{G}(A)
.

Analogously to Lemma 3 of
[
3
]
, we have:

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
x\in X
and
N
be a subgroup normalized by
x
. If
(|x|,|N|)=1,
then
[x,N]=1

Proof.

Let
y\in N
. Then
[x,y]
is a
\delta_{1}^{*}
-commutator and
x[x,y]=x^{y}
. Thus
|x[x,y]|=|x||[x,y]|
and
|x[x,y]|=|x|
, this is,
[x,y]=1
.
∎

The following result is the version of the well-know Focal Subgroup Theorem for
\delta_{k}^{*}
-commutators of a finite group.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
1
]
, Lemma 2.6)
.

Let
k\geq 0
and let
G
be a finite soluble group of order
p^{a}n
, where
p
is a prime and
n
is not divisible by
p
, and let
P
be a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
. Then
P\cap\delta_{k}^{*}(G)
is generated by
n
th powers of
\delta_{k}^{*}
-commutators lying in
P
.

The next theorem will also be useful. Recall the the Fitting height
h=h(G)
of a finite soluble group
G
is the minimal number
h
such that
G
possesses a normal series all of whose quotients are nilpotent.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
6
]
, Theorem 2.6)
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
k
a positive integer. We have
D_{k}(G)=1
if and only if
G
is soluble with Fitting height at most
k
.

Our first result ensures that the theorem is valid for soluble groups. Recall that a finite group
G
is metanilpotent if and only if
\gamma_{\infty}(G)
is nilpotent.

Lemma 2.5
.

If
G
is soluble, then
\gamma_{\infty}(G)
is nilpotent.

Proof.

Recall that
D_{1}(G)=\gamma_{\infty}(G)
. Suppose that
|G|>1
. As
G
is soluble we have that
|G^{\prime}|<|G|
. Then by induction on
|G|
we can assume that
D_{1}(G^{\prime})
is nilpotent. Thus
D_{2}(G^{\prime})=1
and
G^{\prime}
has Fitting height
\leq 2
. So
G
has Fitting height
\leq 3
. Then
D_{3}(G)=1
(Theorem
2.4
). This implies that
D_{2}(G)
is nilpotent. In particular
D_{1}(G)
is metanilpotent. By simplicity, denote
D_{2}(G)
by
H
. Therefore if
P
is a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
H
then
P
is characteristic in
H
. Obviously
H
is characteristic in
G
and so we can conclude that
P
is characteristic in
G
. In particular,
P\unlhd G
. Suppose that
D_{1}(G)
is not a
p
-group. Let
Q
be a Sylow
q
-subgroup of
D_{1}(G)
, with
q\neq p
. By Lemma
2.3

Q
is generated by
n
th powers of
Q\cap X
, where
|D_{1}(G)|=q^{a}n
and
(q,n)=1
. Thus using Lemma
2.2
we have that
[P,x]=1
for all generators
x
of
Q
. Consequently
QP
is normal in
QH
. Since
D_{1}(G)
is a metanilpotent we have
QH\unlhd D_{1}(G)
and then it follows that

(QH)^{G}=\displaystyle\prod_{g\in G}(QH)^{g}

is nilpotent, because every
(QH)^{g}
is nilpotent and normal in
D_{1}(G)
. Then we conclude that
Q\leq F(G)
. If
|\pi(H)|>1
then all Sylow
q
-subgroups of
D_{1}(G)
are normal and so the result follows. If
H
is a
p
-group, then the Sylow
p
-subgroup of
D_{1}(G)
is normal (because
D_{1}(G)
is metanilpotent) and, applying the same argument now to the Sylow subgroups of
D_{1}(G)
, we can conclude that they are all normal, as desired.
∎

Recall that a group
M
is said to be quasisimple if
M=M^{\prime}
and
M/Z(M)
is simple. We are now ready to complete the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

3.
The proof of Theorem
1.3

Proof.

We affirm that
G
is soluble. Indeed, we assume otherwise. Let
G
be a counter-example of minimal order. So all proper subgroups in
G
are soluble and we can assume that
G=D_{1}(G)
. Let
R
be the soluble radical in
G
. By Lemma
2.5

D_{1}(R)
is nilpotent. Suppose that
G
is nonsimple and
R\neq 1
. Let
P
to be Sylow
p
-subgroup of
F(G)
. Let
q\neq p
be a prime number and denote by
T
the subgroup generated by all
\delta_{1}^{*}
-commutators whose order is a power of
q
. By Lemma
2.3
, all
q
-subgroups of
D_{1}(G)
are contained in
T
. Then
G/T
is a
p
-group. Since
G=D_{1}(G)
, we conclude that
G=T
(indeed, if
G\neq T
, then
T
is soluble and this implies that
G
is soluble, a contradiction). Now if
x
is a
\delta_{1}^{*}
-commutator of a Sylow
q
-subgroup of
G
, with
q\neq p
, we conclude by Lemmas
2.2
and
2.3
that
[P,x]=1
. Then
C_{G}(P)
is a normal subgroup of
G
, and
G/C_{G}(P)
is a
p
-group, that is,
G=C_{G}(P)
and
P\leq Z(G)
. Consequently
F(G)\leq Z(G)
. Since
R
is soluble it follows by Lemma
2.5
that
D_{1}(R)
is nilpotent. Clearly
D_{1}(R)\unlhd G
. Then
D_{1}(R)\leq F(G)\leq Z(G)
. In particular,
D_{1}(R)\leq Z(R)
. Thus
R
is nilpotent and therefore
R=Z(G)
. Thus, our group
G
is quasisimple. By the Feit-Thompson Theorem,
G
has a Sylow
2
-subgroup
S
which is not contained in the centre of
G
. By the Frobenius Theorem
[
4
, Theorem 7.4.5]
there exists a
2
-subgroup
L
of
S
such that
N_{G}(L)
contains an element
b
of odd order such that
[b,t]\notin Z(G)
, for all
t\in L
. Then
[b,t]
is of
2
-power order. Let
k
such that
z=[b,t]^{k}\notin Z(G)
and
z^{2}\in Z(G)
. We have
z\in X
. By the Baer-Suzuki Theorem
[
4
, Theorem 3.8.2]
there exists
h\in G
such that
\langle z,z^{h}\rangle Z(G)/Z(G)
is a dihedral group of order
2n
for
n
odd. Let
J=\langle zz^{h}\rangle Z(G)
. Then
J/Z(G)
is inverted by
z
and
J
is nilpotent. However Lemma
2.2
shows that
z
centralizes
O_{2^{\prime}}(J)
a contradiction. The proof is complete.

∎

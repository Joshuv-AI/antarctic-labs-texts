Effective description of generalized disformal theories Preprint: YITP-23-46
Kazufumi Takahashi, Masato Minamitsuji, Hayato Motohashi

Abstract
Generalized disformal transformations enable us to construct the generalized disformal Horndeski theories, which form the most general class of ghost-free scalar-tensor theories to this date. We extend the effective field theory (EFT) of cosmological perturbations to incorporate these generalized disformal Horndeski theories. The main difference from the conventional EFT is that our extended EFT involves operators with higher spatial derivatives of the lapse function. Our EFT also accommodates the generalized disformal transformation of U-DHOST theories.



I
Introduction

Scalar-tensor theories have been extensively studied as one of the simplest extensions of general relativity, i.e., modified gravity theories.
Although simple, they are useful to explore universal features of generic modified gravity models which contain additional degrees of freedom (DOFs) on top of the spacetime metric.
Moreover, they often exhibit peculiar phenomena that can be observationally tested against general relativity (see Refs.
Koyama 2016
;
Ferreira 2019
;
Arai et al. 2022
for reviews).
Among various scalar-tensor theories, the so-called Horndeski theory is the fundamental element, as it is the most general scalar-tensor theory with second-order Euler-Lagrange equations
Horndeski 1974
;
Deffayet et al. 2011
;
Kobayashi et al. 2011
.
Actually, this theory contains many traditional scalar-tensor theories (e.g., the Brans-Dicke theory
Brans and Dicke 1961
,
f(R)
theories
Sotiriou and Faraoni 2010
;
De Felice and Tsujikawa 2010
, scalar-Gauss-Bonnet theories
Koivisto and Mota 2007
;
Sotiriou and Zhou 2014a
;
Sotiriou and Zhou 2014b
;
Antoniou et al. 2018
;
Silva et al. 2018
, and k-essence theories
Armendáriz-Picón et al. 1999
) as its particular cases, and in this sense it can be viewed as a class of scalar-tensor theories rather than a single theory.
It should be noted that the Horndeski class is free of the problem of Ostrogradsky ghosts
Woodard 2015
;
Motohashi and Suyama 2020
thanks to the second-order nature of the Euler-Lagrange equations.
One can further enlarge the space of ghost-free scalar-tensor theories by allowing the Euler-Lagrange equations to contain higher derivatives and imposing the degeneracy condition
Motohashi and Suyama 2015
;
Langlois and Noui 2016
;
Motohashi et al. 2016a
;
Klein and Roest 2016
;
Motohashi et al. 2018a
;
Motohashi et al. 2018b
.
The authors of Refs.
Langlois and Noui 2016
;
Crisostomi et al. 2016
;
Ben Achour et al. 2016
constructed a general class of theories that satisfy the degeneracy condition, which is now called the degenerate higher-order scalar-tensor (DHOST) class.
*1
*1

*1

DHOST theories literally refer to any higher-order scalar-tensor theories satisfying the degeneracy condition, but they often refer to only those constructed in Refs.
Langlois and Noui 2016
;
Crisostomi et al. 2016
;
Ben Achour et al. 2016
. We also follow this conventional terminology in the present paper.

A yet further extension of ghost-free scalar-tensor theories is the class of U-DHOST theories
De Felice et al. 2018
;
De Felice et al. 2021
.
Under the unitary gauge, U-DHOST theories are equivalent to spatially covariant gravity theories
Gao 2014
;
Gao and Yao 2019
;
Gao et al. 2019
;
Motohashi and Hu 2020
, which satisfy the degeneracy condition.
Away from the unitary gauge, U-DHOST theories have an apparent Ostrogradsky mode in general, but the mode actually satisfies an elliptic differential equation on a spacelike hypersurface and hence does not propagate.
This instantaneous mode is often called a shadowy mode
De Felice et al. 2018
;
De Felice et al. 2021
.

While imposing the degeneracy condition offers a systematic construction of ghost-free scalar-tensor theories beyond the Horndeski class, it is known that most subclasses of DHOST theories are not phenomenologically viable:
Cosmological perturbations exhibit ghost/gradient instabilities or otherwise tensor perturbations are nondynamical
Langlois et al. 2017
.
The only viable subclass is the one generated from the Horndeski class via the invertible disformal transformation
Bekenstein 1993
;
Bruneton and Esposito-Farèse 2007
;
Bettoni and Liberati 2013
,

g_{\mu\nu}\quad\to\quad\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}[g,\phi]=f_{0}(\phi,X)g_{\mu\nu}+f_{1}(\phi,X)\nabla_{\mu}\phi\nabla_{\nu}\phi,

(1)

where
\phi
is the scalar field,
\nabla_{\mu}
represents the covariant derivative with respect to the metric
g_{\mu\nu}
, and
f_{0}
and
f_{1}
are functions of
(\phi,X)
with
X\coloneqq g^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\phi\partial_{\beta}\phi
.
Note that the invertibility, i.e., the (local) unique existence of the inverse transformation, is guaranteed if the coefficient functions satisfy the following conditions:

f_{0}\neq 0,\qquad f_{0}+Xf_{1}\neq 0,\qquad f_{0}-Xf_{0X}-X^{2}f_{1X}\neq 0,

(2)

with a subscript
X
denoting the derivative with respect to
X
.
Such an invertible transformation maps a ghost-free scalar-tensor theory to another ghost-free scalar-tensor theory, keeping the number of dynamical DOFs
Domènech et al. 2015
;
Takahashi et al. 2017
.
We shall refer to the subclass of DHOST theories that are generated by invertible disformal transformations as the disformal Horndeski (DH) class.
It should be noted that one could generate ghost-free scalar-tensor theories by noninvertible conformal/disformal transformations.
However, as in generic DHOST theories that lie outside the DH class, they do not admit viable cosmology
Takahashi and Kobayashi 2017
;
Langlois et al. 2019
.
This is another reason why we focus on invertible transformations.

Interestingly, one can include higher derivatives of the scalar field in the transformation law and still make the transformation invertible
Takahashi et al. 2022
.
In Ref.
Takahashi et al. 2023a
, we performed this generalized disformal transformation on Horndeski theories to construct a class of ghost-free scalar-tensor theories beyond the DHOST class, which we dubbed the generalized disformal Horndeski (GDH) class.
*2
*2

*2

In general, GDH theories possess one dynamical scalar DOF on top of the dynamical metric.
However, if the seed theory is chosen to be a special subclass of Horndeski theories where the scalar field is nondynamical (e.g., the cuscuton
Afshordi et al. 2007
or its extension
Iyonaga et al. 2018
;
Iyonaga et al. 2020
), then the scalar field remains nondynamical after the generalized disformal transformation.

Moreover, one could perform the generalized disformal transformation on U-DHOST theories for a further extension of ghost-free theories, at the price of introducing a shadowy mode.
We shall refer to the class of theories obtained in this way as the generalized disformal unitary-degenerate (GDU) class.
The GDH class or the GDU class provides the most general class of ghost-free scalar-tensor theories to this date.

When scalar-tensor theories are applied to cosmology, a useful and robust framework is the so-called effective field theory (EFT) of inflation/dark energy, which allows us to study cosmological perturbations in a model-independent manner
Creminelli et al. 2006
;
Cheung et al. 2008
;
Gubitosi et al. 2013
.
*3
*3

*3

Note that the EFT of Refs.
Creminelli et al. 2006
;
Cheung et al. 2008
;
Gubitosi et al. 2013
assumes that a scalar field with a timelike background profile spontaneously breaks the time diffeomorphism, and hence it applies only to scalar-tensor theories. Depending on the symmetry breaking pattern, we obtain different EFTs (see, e.g., Refs.
Aoki et al. 2022a
;
Aoki et al. 2022b
). Moreover, the construction of EFTs relies on the symmetry of the background spacetime. For instance, applications to anisotropic inflation
Abolhasani et al. 2016
;
Rostami et al. 2017
;
Gong et al. 2020
and black hole perturbations
Franciolini et al. 2019
;
Hui et al. 2021
;
Mukohyama and Yingcharoenrat 2022
;
Mukohyama et al. 2022
;
Khoury et al. 2023
have been studied.

The EFT is characterized by some time-dependent coefficients at each order of perturbative and derivative expansions, and each concrete scalar-tensor theory amounts to some particular choice of the EFT coefficients.
In particular, Horndeski theories can be described by the EFT at the leading order in the derivative expansion.
Then, the authors of Ref.
Langlois et al. 2017
proposed an extension of the EFT that accommodates DHOST theories.
The extended EFT contains several additional time-dependent coefficients associated with the higher-derivative interactions that do not appear in Horndeski theories, and their effects on the cosmic microwave background (CMB) were discussed in Refs.
Hiramatsu and Yamauchi 2020
;
Hiramatsu 2022
.
The aim of the present paper is to further extend the EFT framework so that it incorporates GDH theories as well as GDU theories.
We also apply our EFT to study scalar and tensor cosmological perturbations.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows.
In §
II
, we briefly review the generalized disformal transformation and define GDH/GDU theories.
In §
III
, we construct the EFT that accommodates GDH theories.
We shall argue that the extended EFT applies also to GDU theories in Appendix
A
and discuss how to take into account matter fields in Appendix
B
.
Then, based on the EFT, we investigate scalar and tensor cosmological perturbations in §
IV
.
Finally, we draw our conclusions in §
V
.

II
Generalized disformal Horndeski theories

The authors of the present paper have developed a higher-derivative generalization of invertible disformal transformations in Ref.
Takahashi et al. 2022
and performed it on Horndeski theories to construct GDH theories
Takahashi et al. 2023a
, which form the largest class of ghost-free scalar-tensor theories known so far.
In the present paper, we focus on a subclass of GDH theories that are generated by generalized disformal transformations of the following form:

g_{\mu\nu}\quad\to\quad\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 0.0899pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$g$}}_{\mu\nu}[g,\phi]=f_{0}g_{\mu\nu}+f_{1}\phi_{\mu}\phi_{\nu}+2f_{2}\phi_{(\mu}{\cal X}_{\nu)},\qquad{\cal X}_{\mu}\coloneqq\left(\delta_{\mu}^{\alpha}-\frac{\phi_{\mu}\phi^{\alpha}}{X}\right)\partial_{\alpha}X,

(3)

where
\phi_{\mu}\coloneqq\partial_{\mu}\phi
,
X\coloneqq\phi_{\alpha}\phi^{\alpha}
and
f_{i}
’s are functions of
(\phi,X,\mathcal{Z})
, with

\mathcal{Z}\coloneqq{\cal X}_{\alpha}{\cal X}^{\alpha}.

(4)

As clarified in Refs.
Takahashi et al. 2022
;
Takahashi et al. 2023b
, under a certain set of conditions, a transformation of this form has the inverse transformation at least locally in the configuration space.
We note in passing that the conventional disformal transformation (
1
) corresponds to
f_{0}=f_{0}(\phi,X)
,
f_{1}=f_{1}(\phi,X)
, and
f_{2}=0
.
The reason why we focus on this particular subclass is the absence of Ostrogradsky ghost in matter-coupled GDH theories:
The authors of Refs.
Takahashi et al. 2023a
;
Naruko et al. 2023
;
Takahashi et al. 2023b
showed that both bosonic and fermionic matter fields can be consistently coupled to GDH theories.

Precisely speaking, the analyses of Refs.
Takahashi et al. 2023a
;
Naruko et al. 2023
;
Takahashi et al. 2023b
are performed in the unitary gauge, and hence there still remains a shadowy mode in general. If one requires the absence of the shadowy mode, the generalized disformal transformation reduces to the conventional one
Ikeda et al. 2023
, restricting the GDH theories to the DH subclass.
However, the shadowy mode itself is not a propagating ghost mode, and it is harmless given an appropriate boundary condition
De Felice et al. 2018
;
De Felice et al. 2021
.
Hence, the GDH theories serve as the most general framework of ghost-free scalar-tensor theories beyond the DH class.
Note that one can perform the generalized disformal transformation on U-DHOST theories
De Felice et al. 2018
;
De Felice et al. 2021
;
De Felice et al. 2022
to obtain a class of ghost-free scalar-tensor theories which includes the GDH class as a strict subset (see Appendix
A
).
Such theories, which we dub generalized disformal unitary-degenerate (GDU) theories, are also within the scope of the present paper.
To reiterate, the GDH theories satisfy the degeneracy condition in a covariant manner and the shadowy mode can show up in the presence of matter coupling.
For the GDU theories, the degeneracy condition is imposed in the unitary gauge, and hence in general there exists a shadowy mode away from the unitary gauge even in the absence of matter coupling.
The EFT framework which we shall develop in the present paper accommodates these generalized disformal theories.

Let us summarize several formulae associated with the generalized disformal metric (
3
).
The inverse disformal metric
\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 0.0899pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$g$}}^{\mu\nu}
is given by
Takahashi et al. 2023a
;
Takahashi et al. 2023b

\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 0.0899pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$g$}}^{\mu\nu}[g,\phi]=\frac{1}{f_{0}}g^{\mu\nu}-\frac{f_{0}f_{1}-\mathcal{Z}f_{2}^{2}}{f_{0}\mathcal{F}}\phi^{\mu}\phi^{\nu}-\frac{2f_{2}}{\mathcal{F}}\phi^{(\mu}{\cal X}^{\nu)}+\frac{Xf_{2}^{2}}{f_{0}\mathcal{F}}{\cal X}^{\mu}{\cal X}^{\nu},

(5)

with the quantity
\mathcal{F}
defined by

\mathcal{F}:=f_{0}\left(f_{0}+Xf_{1}\right)-X\mathcal{Z}f_{2}^{2}.

(6)

As a result, we obtain the barred counterpart of
X
as

\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}\coloneqq\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 0.0899pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$g$}}^{\mu\nu}\phi_{\mu}\phi_{\nu}=\frac{Xf_{0}}{\mathcal{F}},

(7)

which is a function of
(\phi,X,\mathcal{Z})
in general.
In order for the disformal transformation to be invertible (i.e.,
g_{\mu\nu}
can be uniquely expressed in terms of
\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 0.0899pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$g$}}_{\mu\nu}
and
\phi
at least locally), we require the following set of conditions
Takahashi et al. 2023a
;
Takahashi et al. 2023b
:

f_{0}\neq 0,\qquad\mathcal{F}\neq 0,\qquad\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}_{X}\neq 0,\qquad\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}_{\mathcal{Z}}=0,\qquad\left(\frac{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{F}}\right)_{\mathcal{Z}}\neq 0,

(8)

where subscripts
X
and
\mathcal{Z}
denote the derivatives with respect to
X
and
\mathcal{Z}
, respectively.
Here, the condition
\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}_{\mathcal{Z}}=0
implies that

\overline{\hbox{}}
X
must be a function only of
(\phi,X)
, which puts a tight constraint among
f_{i}
’s through (
7
).
Therefore, instead of
f_{i}
’s, it is useful to regard
f_{0}(\phi,X,\mathcal{Z})
,
f_{2}(\phi,X,\mathcal{Z})
, and
\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}(\phi,X)
as given functions so that the condition
\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}_{\mathcal{Z}}=0
is automatically implemented.
Then, (
6
) and (
7
) allow us to express
f_{1}
as

f_{1}=\frac{1}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}}-\frac{f_{0}}{X}+\frac{\mathcal{Z}f_{2}^{2}}{f_{0}}.

(9)

In the next section, we study how the conventional EFT of inflation/dark energy
Creminelli et al. 2006
;
Cheung et al. 2008
;
Gubitosi et al. 2013
should be extended in order to accommodate GDH theories generated by the transformation (
3
).
Interestingly, it turns out that the extended EFT applies to GDU theories as well.

III
Quadratic action for cosmological perturbations

The EFT of inflation/dark energy
Creminelli et al. 2006
;
Cheung et al. 2008
;
Gubitosi et al. 2013
provides a model-independent framework for perturbations on a homogeneous and isotropic cosmological background.
In this context, we assume that the background scalar field
\phi
has a timelike profile so that one can choose the unitary gauge where
\phi=\phi(t)
and the scalar DOF is eaten by the metric.
Note that such a scalar field spontaneously breaks the time diffeomorphism and the residual spacetime symmetries are only the spatial diffeomorphisms.
Therefore, in the unitary gauge, the action is written in terms of geometrical quantities that respect spatial covariance as well as those respecting full spacetime covariance.
In order to write down the EFT action, we introduce the Arnowitt-Deser-Misner (ADM) variables as

g_{\mu\nu}{\rm d}x^{\mu}{\rm d}x^{\nu}=-N^{2}{\rm d}t^{2}+\gamma_{ij}({\rm d}x^{i}+N^{i}{\rm d}t)({\rm d}x^{j}+N^{j}{\rm d}t),

(10)

where
N
is the lapse function,
N^{i}
is the shift vector, and
\gamma_{ij}
is the spatial metric.
On the spatially flat Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) background, we have

N=\hat{N}(t)+\delta N,\qquad\gamma_{ij}=a(t)^{2}\delta_{ij}+\delta\gamma_{ij},

(11)

with
a(t)
being the scale factor, and
N^{i}
itself is a perturbed quantity.
Here and in what follows, quantities with a hat indicate their background value, though we omit the hat when unnecessary.
We denote the unit normal vector and the projection tensor associated with a constant-
t
hypersurface respectively as

n_{\mu}\coloneqq-N\delta^{0}_{\mu},\qquad h_{\mu\nu}\coloneqq g_{\mu\nu}+n_{\mu}n_{\nu}.

(12)

The extrinsic curvature and the acceleration vector are defined by

K_{\mu\nu}\coloneqq h_{\mu}{}^{\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}n_{\nu},\qquad a_{\mu}\coloneqq n^{\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}n_{\mu},

(13)

respectively, which are written in terms of the ADM variables as

K_{ij}=\frac{1}{2N}\left(\dot{\gamma}_{ij}-{\rm D}_{i}N_{j}-{\rm D}_{j}N_{i}\right),\qquad a_{i}=\frac{1}{N}{\rm D}_{i}N,

(14)

with a dot denoting the derivative with respect to
t
and
{\rm D}_{i}
being the covariant derivative associated with
\gamma_{ij}
.
The spatial curvature tensor
{}^{(3)}\!R_{\mu\nu\lambda\sigma}
associated with
\gamma_{ij}
can also be a building block of the EFT action.
Note that the spatial curvature tensor can be written as a combination of the four-dimensional spacetime curvature tensor and the extrinsic curvature through the Gauss relation, and hence one can employ either the spatial or spacetime curvature as an independent building block.
The EFT action is then written in terms of these objects, i.e.,

S=\int{\rm d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,\mathcal{L}(R_{\mu\nu\lambda\sigma},g^{00},K_{\mu\nu},\nabla_{\mu},t).

(15)

Practically, one performs perturbative and derivative expansions up to the necessary order.

Given an action of scalar-tensor theories, it is straightforward to recast it in the form of the EFT action.
Indeed, under the unitary gauge, the first and second derivatives of
\phi
are related to the above geometrical quantities as
Gleyzes et al. 2013

\phi_{\mu}=-\frac{\dot{\phi}}{N}n_{\mu},\qquad\phi_{\mu\nu}=-\frac{\dot{\phi}}{N}(K_{\mu\nu}-n_{\mu}a_{\nu}-n_{\nu}a_{\mu})-\frac{N}{2\dot{\phi}}(n^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}X)n_{\mu}n_{\nu},

(16)

with
\phi_{\mu\nu}\coloneqq\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\phi
.
From the first equation, we see that
X=\phi_{\alpha}\phi^{\alpha}=-\dot{\phi}^{2}/N^{2}
and

\begin{split}{\cal X}_{\mu}&=-2Xa_{\mu}=-\frac{2X}{N}\delta_{\mu}^{i}\partial_{i}\delta N+\mathcal{O}(\delta^{2}),\\
\mathcal{Z}&=4X^{2}a_{\mu}a^{\mu}=4X^{2}\frac{(\partial_{i}\delta N)^{2}}{N^{2}a^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(\delta^{3}),\end{split}

(17)

where
\mathcal{O}(\delta^{n})
denotes terms of
n
th or higher order in perturbations.
Note that both
{\cal X}_{\mu}
and
\mathcal{Z}
vanish at the background level.

In §
III.1
, we first review how the Horndeski class is embedded in the EFT framework.
The EFT action derived here is regarded as the seed of the generalized disformal transformation.
Then, we study how each building block of the seed EFT action is transformed under the generalized disformal transformation (
3
) in §
III.2
.
Finally, combining the results of §
III.1
and §
III.2
, we obtain the EFT action that accommodates GDH theories in §
III.3
.
For demonstration purposes, we focus on the action up to the quadratic order in perturbations in the present paper, but our discussion can be applied to cubic or any higher-order actions in principle.

III.1
Seed action

The Horndeski action up to the quadratic order in the second derivative of the scalar field is given by

S_{\rm H}=\int{\rm d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,\mathcal{L},\qquad\mathcal{L}\coloneqq G_{2}+G_{3}\Box\phi+G_{4}R-2G_{4X}\left[(\Box\phi)^{2}-\phi^{\mu}_{\nu}\phi_{\mu}^{\nu}\right],

(18)

with
G_{I}
’s being functions of
(\phi,X)
.
*4
*4

*4

For simplicity, we do not include the so-called quintic Horndeski Lagrangian.

In the unitary gauge, one can employ (
16
) to recast the Lagrangian in the form

\mathcal{L}(t,N,K,K_{2},{}^{(3)}\!R)=b_{0}+b_{1}K+b_{2}K_{2}+b_{3}{}^{(3)}\!R,

(19)

where
K\coloneqq K^{i}_{i}
,
K_{2}\coloneqq K^{2}-K^{i}_{j}K_{i}^{j}
, and
{}^{(3)}\!R
denotes the spatial Ricci scalar.
Here,
b_{0}
,
b_{1}
,
b_{2}
, and
b_{3}
are functions of
(t,N)
, which are related to
G_{I}
’s through
Gleyzes et al. 2013

b_{0}=G_{2}-Xg_{3\phi},\qquad b_{1}=-2\sqrt{-X}\left(Xg_{3X}+G_{4\phi}\right),\qquad b_{2}=-G_{4}+2XG_{4X},\qquad b_{3}=G_{4}.

(20)

Here, the function
g_{3}(\phi,X)
has been defined so that
g_{3}+2Xg_{3X}=G_{3}
.

Let us study perturbations about the spatially flat FLRW background spacetime.
We define the perturbation of the extrinsic curvature as

\delta K^{i}_{j}\coloneqq K^{i}_{j}-H\delta^{i}_{j},\qquad\delta K\coloneqq K-3H,

(21)

where
H\coloneqq\dot{a}/(\hat{N}a)
is the Hubble parameter.
Note that

\delta K_{2}\coloneqq K_{2}-6H^{2}=4H\delta K+\delta K^{2}-\delta K^{i}_{j}\delta K_{i}^{j}+\mathcal{O}(\delta^{3}).

(22)

Note also that
{}^{(3)}\!R
vanishes at the background level for the spatially flat FLRW metric, and we write
\delta{}^{(3)}\!R={}^{(3)}\!R
.
Then, one can expand the Lagrangian (
19
) up to the quadratic order in perturbations to rewrite the Horndeski action as

\displaystyle S_{\rm H}=\int{\rm d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\Bigg[

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{L}}-3H{\cal A}-\frac{\dot{\cal A}}{\hat{N}}+\left(\hat{N}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{N}+\frac{\dot{\cal A}}{\hat{N}}\right)\frac{\delta N}{\hat{N}}+\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{{}^{(3)}\!R}\delta{}^{(3)}\!R-\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{K_{2}}\left(\delta K^{i}_{j}\delta K^{j}_{i}-\delta K^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{\hat{N}^{2}}{2}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{NN}-\frac{\dot{\cal A}}{\hat{N}}\right)\frac{\delta N^{2}}{\hat{N}^{2}}+\left(\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{NK}+4H\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{NK_{2}}\right)\delta N\delta K+\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{N{}^{(3)}\!R}\delta N\delta{}^{(3)}\!R+{\cal O}(\delta^{3})\Bigg],

(23)

where we have defined

{\cal A}(t)\coloneqq\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{K}+4H\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{K_{2}}.

(24)

Here,
\hat{\mathcal{L}}
with subscript(s) denotes the derivative of the Lagrangian (
19
) with respect to the subscript(s) evaluated at the background.
One can employ the background equations of motion (EOMs) to simplify the above action.
In the absence of matter fields, the background EOMs read

\mathcal{E}_{0}\coloneqq\hat{\mathcal{L}}-3H{\cal A}-\frac{\dot{\cal A}}{\hat{N}}=0,\qquad\mathcal{E}_{1}\coloneqq\hat{N}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{N}+\frac{\dot{\cal A}}{\hat{N}}=0,

(25)

which can be used to remove the following part from the action (
23
):

S_{0}=\int{\rm d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\mathcal{E}_{0}+\mathcal{E}_{1}\left(\frac{\delta N}{\hat{N}}-\frac{\delta N^{2}}{\hat{N}^{2}}\right)\right].

(26)

After using the background EOMs, the quadratic action in a cosmological background can be written as

\displaystyle\delta_{2}S_{\rm H}=\int{\rm d}t{\rm d}^{3}x\,\hat{N}a^{3}\bigg[

\displaystyle-\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{K_{2}}\left(\delta_{1}K^{i}_{j}\delta_{1}K^{j}_{i}-\delta_{1}K^{2}\right)+\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{{}^{(3)}\!R}\delta_{2}\left({}^{(3)}\!R\frac{\sqrt{\gamma}}{a^{3}}\right)+\left(\frac{1}{2}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{NN}+\frac{\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{N}}{\hat{N}}\right)\delta_{1}N^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{NK}+4H\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{NK_{2}}\right)\delta_{1}N\delta_{1}K+\left(\hat{N}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{N{}^{(3)}\!R}+\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{{}^{(3)}\!R}\right)\frac{\delta_{1}N}{\hat{N}}\delta_{1}\!{}^{(3)}\!R\bigg].

(27)

Here and in what follows,
\delta_{1}Q
and
\delta_{2}Q
denote the parts of a quantity
Q
that are of linear and quadratic orders in perturbations, respectively.

For phenomenological purposes, the following parametrization is often used to describe the quadratic action up to the leading order in the derivative expansion
Bellini and Sawicki 2014
;
Gleyzes et al. 2015a
;
Gleyzes et al. 2015b
;
Frusciante et al. 2016
:

\displaystyle\delta_{2}S_{\rm g}=\int{\rm d}t{\rm d}^{3}x\,\hat{N}a^{3}\frac{\tilde{M}^{2}}{2}\bigg[

\displaystyle\delta_{1}K^{i}_{j}\delta_{1}K^{j}_{i}-\left(1+\frac{2}{3}\tilde{\alpha}_{\rm L}\right)\delta_{1}K^{2}+(1+\tilde{\alpha}_{\rm T})\delta_{2}\left({}^{(3)}\!R\frac{\sqrt{\gamma}}{a^{3}}\right)

\displaystyle+H^{2}\tilde{\alpha}_{\rm K}\frac{\delta_{1}N^{2}}{\hat{N}^{2}}+4H\tilde{\alpha}_{\rm B}\frac{\delta_{1}N}{\hat{N}}\delta_{1}K+(1+\tilde{\alpha}_{\rm H})\frac{\delta_{1}N}{\hat{N}}\delta_{1}\!{}^{(3)}\!R\bigg],

(28)

where
\tilde{M}
,
\tilde{\alpha}_{\rm L}
,
\tilde{\alpha}_{\rm T}
,
\tilde{\alpha}_{\rm K}
,
\tilde{\alpha}_{\rm B}
, and
\tilde{\alpha}_{\rm H}
are functions of
t
.
Comparing this equation with (
27
), we see that the Horndeski Lagrangian (
19
) amounts to the following choice of parameters:

\begin{split}&\tilde{M}^{2}=-2\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{K_{2}},\qquad\tilde{\alpha}_{\rm L}=0,\qquad\tilde{\alpha}_{\rm T}=\frac{2\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{{}^{(3)}\!R}}{M^{2}}-1,\qquad\tilde{\alpha}_{\rm K}=\frac{\hat{N}}{M^{2}H^{2}}\left(\hat{N}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{NN}+2\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{N}\right),\\
&\tilde{\alpha}_{\rm B}=\frac{\hat{N}}{2M^{2}H}\left(\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{NK}+4H\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{NK_{2}}\right),\qquad\tilde{\alpha}_{\rm H}=0.\end{split}

(29)

Equivalently, in terms of the coefficient functions
G_{I}
in the original action (
18
), we have

\begin{split}&\tilde{M}^{2}=\mathcal{G}_{T},\qquad\tilde{\alpha}_{\rm L}=0,\qquad\tilde{\alpha}_{\rm T}=\frac{4XG_{4X}}{\mathcal{G}_{T}},\qquad\tilde{\alpha}_{\rm K}=\frac{2\Sigma+12H\Theta-6H^{2}\mathcal{G}_{T}}{H^{2}\mathcal{G}_{T}},\\
&\tilde{\alpha}_{\rm B}=\frac{\Theta-H\mathcal{G}_{T}}{H\mathcal{G}_{T}},\qquad\tilde{\alpha}_{\rm H}=0.\end{split}

(30)

Here, we have defined

\begin{split}\mathcal{G}_{T}&\coloneqq 2(G_{4}-2XG_{4X}),\\
\Sigma&\coloneqq XG_{2X}+2X^{2}G_{2XX}-6H\dot{\phi}\left(2XG_{3X}+X^{2}G_{3XX}\right)-X\left(G_{3\phi}+XG_{3\phi X}\right)\\
&\quad-6H^{2}\left(G_{4}-7XG_{4X}-16X^{2}G_{4XX}-4X^{3}G_{4XXX}\right)-6H\dot{\phi}\left(G_{4\phi}+5XG_{4\phi X}+2X^{2}G_{4\phi XX}\right),\\
\Theta&\coloneqq\dot{\phi}XG_{3X}+2H\left(G_{4}-4XG_{4X}-4X^{2}G_{4XX}\right)+\dot{\phi}\left(G_{4\phi}+2XG_{4\phi X}\right),\end{split}

(31)

where the right-hand sides are evaluated at the background.

It should be noted that the quadratic action (
28
) also applies to U-DHOST theories.
Indeed, there exists a canonical form of quadratic U-DHOST theories, for which the quadratic action for cosmological perturbations boils down to the form (
28
) (see Appendix
A
).
In contrast to the case of Horndeski theories,
\tilde{\alpha}_{\rm L}
and
\tilde{\alpha}_{\rm H}
can be nonvanishing in U-DHOST theories.
Note also that, in rewriting the quadratic action in the form (
28
), we removed the terms in (
26
) by use of the vacuum background EOMs (
25
).
In the presence of matter fields, this procedure should be modified accordingly.
We provide a prescription for how to incorporate the contribution from matter fields in Appendix
B
.

III.2
Transformation of the EFT building blocks

In this subsection, we study the transformation law for each building block of the leading-order EFT action (
28
) under the generalized disformal transformation (
3
).
As in (
3
), quantities after the transformation are indicated with a bar.
Since we focus on the quadratic action, we omit terms of higher order in perturbations.

•

ADM variables
.
The transformation law for the ADM variables is given as follows
Takahashi et al. 2023a
:

\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.88745pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$N$}}^{2}=-\frac{\dot{\phi}^{2}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}(\phi,-\dot{\phi}^{2}/N^{2})},\qquad\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.88745pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$N$}}_{i}=f_{0}N_{i}+\dot{\phi}f_{2}{\cal X}_{i},\qquad\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 0.1241pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\gamma$}}_{ij}=f_{0}\gamma_{ij}.

(32)

In particular,
\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 0.1241pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\gamma$}}_{ij}
is nothing but a conformal transformation of
\gamma_{ij}
.
Note that we have assumed that the metric signature is preserved under the generalized disformal transformation, which is realized if
\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}/X>0
.
From (
32
), we find that the background value of the lapse function and the scale factor are transformed as

\hat{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.88745pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$N$}}}=\left(\frac{\hat{X}}{\hat{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}}}\right)^{1/2}\hat{N},\qquad\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 0.2001pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$a$}}=\hat{f}_{0}^{1/2}a.

(33)

Moreover, we have

\begin{split}\frac{\delta\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.88745pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$N$}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.88745pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$N$}}}&=\frac{X\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}_{X}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}}\frac{\delta N}{N}+\mathcal{O}(\delta^{2}),\\
\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.88745pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$N$}}_{i}&=f_{0}N_{i}-2\dot{\phi}Xf_{2}\frac{\partial_{i}\delta N}{N}+\mathcal{O}(\delta^{3}).\end{split}

(34)

The following relation is also useful:

\frac{\delta\sqrt{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 0.1241pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\gamma$}}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 0.2001pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$a$}}^{3}}=\frac{\delta\sqrt{\gamma}}{a^{3}}-\frac{3Xf_{0X}}{f_{0}}\frac{\delta N}{N}+\mathcal{O}(\delta^{2}).

(35)

•

Extrinsic curvature
.
The barred counterpart of the extrinsic curvature is defined by

\displaystyle\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.9458pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$K$}}_{ij}=\frac{1}{2\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.88745pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$N$}}}\left(\dot{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 0.1241pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\gamma$}}}_{ij}-\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 1.8472pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\rm D$}}_{i}\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.88745pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$N$}}_{j}-\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 1.8472pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\rm D$}}_{j}\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.88745pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$N$}}_{i}\right),

(36)

and hence

\displaystyle\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.9458pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$K$}}^{i}_{j}\coloneqq\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 0.1241pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\gamma$}}^{ik}\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.9458pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$K$}}_{kj}=\frac{1}{f_{0}\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.88745pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$N$}}}\left(Nf_{0}K^{i}_{j}+\frac{1}{2}\dot{f}_{0}\delta^{i}_{j}-\dot{\phi}f_{2}{\rm D}^{i}{\cal X}_{j}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\delta^{2}).

(37)

Therefore, the perturbation of the extrinsic curvature is transformed as

\begin{split}\delta\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.9458pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$K$}}^{i}_{j}&=\left(\frac{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}}{X}\right)^{1/2}\left[\delta K^{i}_{j}+H\mathcal{B}\frac{\delta N}{N}\delta^{i}_{j}+\frac{\mathcal{C}}{N}\left(\frac{\delta N}{N}\right)^{\bm{\cdot}}\delta^{i}_{j}+\frac{\mathcal{D}}{M_{\rm D}}\frac{\partial^{i}\partial_{j}\delta N}{Na^{2}}\right]+\mathcal{O}(\delta^{2}),\\
\delta\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.9458pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$K$}}&=\left(\frac{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}}{X}\right)^{1/2}\left[\delta K+3H\mathcal{B}\frac{\delta N}{N}+\frac{3\mathcal{C}}{N}\left(\frac{\delta N}{N}\right)^{\bm{\cdot}}+\frac{\mathcal{D}}{M_{\rm D}}\frac{\Delta\delta N}{Na^{2}}\right]+\mathcal{O}(\delta^{2}),\end{split}

(38)

where
\partial^{i}=\partial_{i}
,
\Delta\coloneqq\partial_{i}^{2}
, and we have defined the following dimensionless quantities:

\mathcal{B}\coloneqq\left.\frac{1}{NH}\left[\sqrt{-X}\left(\sqrt{-X}\ln f_{0}\right)_{X}\right]^{\bm{\cdot}}+\frac{2\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.88745pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$H$}}}{H}X\partial_{X}\sqrt{\frac{X}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}}}\,\right|_{\rm BG},\qquad\mathcal{C}\coloneqq-\frac{\hat{X}\hat{f}_{0X}}{\hat{f}_{0}},\qquad\mathcal{D}\coloneqq-\frac{2M_{\rm D}(-\hat{X})^{3/2}\hat{f}_{2}}{\hat{f}_{0}}.

(39)

Here,
\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.88745pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$H$}}\coloneqq\dot{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 0.2001pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$a$}}}/\hat{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.88745pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$N$}}}\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 0.2001pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$a$}}
and
M_{\rm D}
is some constant mass scale that characterizes the energy scale associated with
f_{2}
.
It should be noted that there is a priori no typical scale for
M_{\rm D}
.
This is because the disformal factor
f_{2}
multiplies to the term
\phi_{(\mu}{\cal X}_{\nu)}
in the generalized disformal transformation (
3
), which does not have a background value on a homogeneous and isotropic background [see Eq. (
17
)].

•

Spatial curvature
.
Since the spatial metric is transformed conformally, the spatial curvature is transformed as

{}^{(3)}\!\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 1.86908pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$R$}}=\frac{1}{f_{0}}{}^{(3)}\!R+\frac{3}{2f_{0}^{3}}{\rm D}_{k}f_{0}{\rm D}^{k}f_{0}-\frac{2}{f_{0}^{2}}{\rm D}_{k}{\rm D}^{k}f_{0},

(40)

which holds exactly.
The following relations are useful:

\begin{split}\delta_{1}\!{}^{(3)}\!\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 1.86908pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$R$}}&=\frac{1}{\hat{f}_{0}}\delta_{1}{}^{(3)}\!R-\frac{4\mathcal{C}}{\hat{f}_{0}}\frac{\Delta\delta_{1}N}{\hat{N}a^{2}},\\
\delta_{2}\left({}^{(3)}\!\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 1.86908pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$R$}}\frac{\sqrt{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 0.1241pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\gamma$}}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 0.2001pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$a$}}^{3}}\right)&=\frac{1}{\hat{f}_{0}}\delta_{2}\left({}^{(3)}\!R\frac{\sqrt{\gamma}}{a^{3}}\right)+\frac{\mathcal{C}}{\hat{f}_{0}}\frac{\delta_{1}N}{\hat{N}}\delta_{1}\!{}^{(3)}\!R+\frac{2\mathcal{C}^{2}}{\hat{f}_{0}}\frac{(\partial_{i}\delta_{1}N)^{2}}{\hat{N}^{2}a^{2}}+\left(\text{spatial total derivatives}\right),\end{split}

(41)

where in the second equation we have omitted spatial total derivatives of
\mathcal{O}(\delta^{2})
as they do not contribute to the quadratic action.

III.3
Quadratic action for generalized disformal Horndeski theories

Having developed the transformation law for each EFT building block, we can now discuss the transformation of the seed quadratic action (
28
).
After straightforward manipulations, we arrive at the following expression:

\displaystyle\delta_{2}S_{\rm g}=\int{\rm d}t{\rm d}^{3}x\,\hat{N}a^{3}\frac{M^{2}}{2}\bigg\{

\displaystyle\delta_{1}K^{i}_{j}\delta_{1}K^{j}_{i}-\left(1+\frac{2}{3}\alpha_{\rm L}\right)\delta_{1}K^{2}+(1+\alpha_{\rm T})\delta_{2}\left({}^{(3)}\!R\frac{\sqrt{\gamma}}{a^{3}}\right)+H^{2}\alpha_{\rm K}\frac{\delta_{1}N^{2}}{\hat{N}^{2}}+4H\alpha_{\rm B}\frac{\delta_{1}N}{\hat{N}}\delta_{1}K

\displaystyle+(1+\alpha_{\rm H})\frac{\delta_{1}N}{\hat{N}}\delta_{1}\!{}^{(3)}\!R+\frac{4\beta_{1}}{\hat{N}}\left(\frac{\delta_{1}N}{\hat{N}}\right)^{\bm{\cdot}}\delta_{1}K+\frac{\beta_{2}}{\hat{N}^{2}}\left[\left(\frac{\delta_{1}N}{\hat{N}}\right)^{\bm{\cdot}}\,\right]^{2}+\beta_{3}\frac{(\partial_{i}\delta_{1}N)^{2}}{\hat{N}^{2}a^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\gamma_{1}}{M_{\rm D}}\delta_{1}K^{i}_{j}\frac{\partial_{i}\partial^{j}\delta_{1}N}{\hat{N}a^{2}}+\frac{\gamma_{2}}{M_{\rm D}}\delta_{1}K\frac{\Delta\delta_{1}N}{\hat{N}a^{2}}+\frac{\gamma_{3}}{M_{\rm D}^{2}}\frac{(\Delta\delta_{1}N)^{2}}{\hat{N}^{2}a^{4}}\bigg\},

(42)

where
M
,
\alpha
’s,
\beta
’s, and
\gamma
’s are functions of
t
given by

\begin{split}&M^{2}=\left(\frac{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}f_{0}^{3}}{X}\right)^{1/2}\tilde{M}^{2},\qquad\alpha_{\rm L}=\tilde{\alpha}_{\rm L},\qquad\alpha_{\rm T}=\frac{X}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}f_{0}}(1+\tilde{\alpha}_{\rm T})-1,\\
&\alpha_{\rm H}=\frac{X^{2}\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}_{X}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}^{2}f_{0}}(1+\tilde{\alpha}_{\rm H})+\frac{X\mathcal{C}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}f_{0}}(1+\tilde{\alpha}_{\rm T})-1,\qquad\alpha_{\rm B}=\frac{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.88745pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$H$}}}{H}\left(\frac{X}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}}\right)^{3/2}\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}_{X}\tilde{\alpha}_{\rm B}-\mathcal{B}(1+\tilde{\alpha}_{\rm L}),\\
&\alpha_{\rm K}=\frac{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.88745pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$H$}}^{2}}{H^{2}}\frac{X^{3}\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}_{X}^{2}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}^{3}}\tilde{\alpha}_{\rm K}+6\mathcal{B}\left[\mathcal{B}(1+\tilde{\alpha}_{\rm L})+2\alpha_{\rm B}\right]-\frac{6(a^{3}H\mathcal{C}M^{2}\alpha_{\rm B})^{\bm{\cdot}}}{Na^{3}H^{2}M^{2}},\\
&\beta_{1}=-\mathcal{C}(1+\tilde{\alpha}_{\rm L}),\qquad\beta_{2}=-6\mathcal{C}^{2}(1+\tilde{\alpha}_{\rm L}),\\
&\beta_{3}=\frac{4X^{2}\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}_{X}\mathcal{C}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}^{2}f_{0}}(1+\tilde{\alpha}_{\rm H})+\frac{2X\mathcal{C}^{2}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}f_{0}}(1+\tilde{\alpha}_{\rm T})-\frac{4H\mathcal{D}\alpha_{\rm B}}{M_{\rm D}}+\frac{2(a\mathcal{D}M^{2}\beta_{1})^{\bm{\cdot}}}{NaM^{2}M_{\rm D}},\\
&\gamma_{1}=2\mathcal{D},\qquad\gamma_{2}=-2\mathcal{D}\left(1+\frac{2}{3}\tilde{\alpha}_{\rm L}\right),\qquad\gamma_{3}=-\frac{2}{3}\mathcal{D}^{2}\tilde{\alpha}_{\rm L},\end{split}

(43)

with
\tilde{M}
and
\tilde{\alpha}
’s being the EFT parameters in the seed action (
28
).
Here, all the quantities on the right-hand sides should be evaluated at the background.
Note that the quadratic action (
42
) with the coefficients given by (
43
) does not yield Ostrogradsky ghosts, since it is generated by the invertible generalized disformal transformation from the action (
28
) which does not contain higher time derivatives.
The action (
42
) itself makes sense even if it were not disformally related to (
28
), but in this case there would be an Ostrogradsky mode in general.

In particular, the EFT parameters for GDH theories can be obtained by substituting (
30
) into (
43
).
In doing so,
X
and
H
in (
30
) should be replaced by

\overline{\hbox{}}
X
and

\overline{\hbox{}}
H
, respectively.
Written explicitly,

\begin{split}&M^{2}=\left(\frac{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}f_{0}^{3}}{X}\right)^{1/2}\hbox to0.0pt{\thinspace\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\mathcal{G}$}}_{T},\qquad\alpha_{\rm L}=0,\qquad\alpha_{\rm T}=\frac{X}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}f_{0}}\frac{2G_{4}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\thinspace\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\mathcal{G}$}}_{T}}-1,\\
&\alpha_{\rm H}=\frac{X^{2}\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}_{X}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}^{2}f_{0}}+\frac{X\mathcal{C}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}f_{0}}\frac{2G_{4}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\thinspace\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\mathcal{G}$}}_{T}}-1,\qquad\alpha_{\rm B}=\left(\frac{X}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}}\right)^{3/2}\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}_{X}\frac{\hbox to0.0pt{\thinspace\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\Theta$}}-\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.88745pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$H$}}\hbox to0.0pt{\thinspace\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\mathcal{G}$}}_{T}}{H\hbox to0.0pt{\thinspace\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\mathcal{G}$}}_{T}}-\mathcal{B},\\
&\alpha_{\rm K}=\frac{X^{3}\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}_{X}^{2}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}^{3}}\frac{2\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 1.55554pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\Sigma$}}+12\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.88745pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$H$}}\hbox to0.0pt{\thinspace\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\Theta$}}-6\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.88745pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$H$}}^{2}\hbox to0.0pt{\thinspace\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\mathcal{G}$}}_{T}}{H^{2}\hbox to0.0pt{\thinspace\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\mathcal{G}$}}_{T}}+6\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{B}+2\alpha_{\rm B}\right)-\frac{6(a^{3}H\mathcal{C}M^{2}\alpha_{\rm B})^{\bm{\cdot}}}{Na^{3}H^{2}M^{2}},\\
&\beta_{1}=-\mathcal{C},\qquad\beta_{2}=-6\mathcal{C}^{2},\qquad\beta_{3}=\frac{4X^{2}\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}_{X}\mathcal{C}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}^{2}f_{0}}+\frac{2X\mathcal{C}^{2}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}f_{0}}\frac{2G_{4}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\thinspace\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\mathcal{G}$}}_{T}}-\frac{4H\mathcal{D}\alpha_{\rm B}}{M_{\rm D}}-\frac{2(a\mathcal{C}\mathcal{D}M^{2})^{\bm{\cdot}}}{NaM^{2}M_{\rm D}},\\
&\gamma_{1}=-\gamma_{2}=2\mathcal{D},\qquad\gamma_{3}=0,\end{split}

(44)

where

\begin{split}\hbox to0.0pt{\thinspace\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\mathcal{G}$}}_{T}&\coloneqq 2(G_{4}-2\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}G_{4X}),\\
\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 1.55554pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\Sigma$}}&\coloneqq\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}G_{2X}+2\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}^{2}G_{2XX}-6\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.88745pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$H$}}\dot{\phi}\left(2\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}G_{3X}+\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}^{2}G_{3XX}\right)-\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}\left(G_{3\phi}+\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}G_{3\phi X}\right)\\
&\quad-6\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.88745pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$H$}}^{2}\left(G_{4}-7\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}G_{4X}-16\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}^{2}G_{4XX}-4\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}^{3}G_{4XXX}\right)-6\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.88745pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$H$}}\dot{\phi}\left(G_{4\phi}+5\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}G_{4\phi X}+2\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}^{2}G_{4\phi XX}\right),\\
\hbox to0.0pt{\thinspace\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\Theta$}}&\coloneqq\dot{\phi}\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}G_{3X}+2\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.88745pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$H$}}\left(G_{4}-4\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}G_{4X}-4\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}^{2}G_{4XX}\right)+\dot{\phi}\left(G_{4\phi}+2\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}G_{4\phi X}\right),\end{split}

(45)

with
G_{I}
’s evaluated at
(\phi,\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}})
.

As mentioned previously, the quadratic action (
42
) also applies to GDU theories (see Appendix
A
for details).
In this case, the parameter
\alpha_{\rm L}
can be nonvanishing, which is in contrast to the case of GDH theories.
Indeed, as we shall see in §
IV.2
, the parameter
\alpha_{\rm L}
controls the so-called scordatura effect
Motohashi and Mukohyama 2020
which is built-in in generic U-DHOST theories
De Felice et al. 2022
.

Compared to the seed action (
28
), the action (
42
) involves new parameters, i.e.,
\beta
’s and
\gamma
’s.
Among them,
\beta_{1}
,
\beta_{2}
, and
\beta_{3}
are those already present in the EFT for DHOST theories
Langlois et al. 2017
.
On the other hand, the parameters
\gamma_{1}
,
\gamma_{2}
, and
\gamma_{3}
are peculiar to our extended EFT for GDH theories.
These parameters are proportional to
\mathcal{D}
(or its squared) as they are generated by the generalized disformal transformation with nonvanishing
f_{2}
[see Eq. (
39
)].
Note also that, if we choose

f_{0}=f_{0}(\phi),\qquad f_{1}=f_{1}(\phi),\qquad f_{2}=0,\qquad\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}=\frac{X}{f_{0}+Xf_{1}},

(46)

the resultant theory lies within the Horndeski class.
Therefore, in this particular case,
\alpha_{\rm L}
and
\alpha_{\rm H}
as well as all the new parameters
\beta
’s and
\gamma
’s vanish as in the seed Horndeski theory [see Eq. (
30
)], as it should.

If we focus on a subclass of GDH theories with
f_{2}=0
, we have
\mathcal{D}=0
, and hence

\beta_{2}=-6\beta_{1}^{2},\qquad\beta_{3}=-2\beta_{1}\left[2(1+\alpha_{\rm H})+\beta_{1}(1+\alpha_{\rm T})\right],\qquad\gamma_{1}=\gamma_{2}=\gamma_{3}=0.

(47)

Interestingly, the same set of relations is satisfied
by DH theories, which are generated from Horndeski theories by the conventional disformal transformation (
1
).
This implies that this subclass of GDH theories cannot be distinguished from the DH class at least at the level of linear cosmological perturbations.
In other words, deviations from the DH class would show up only when
f_{2}\neq 0
.

IV
Quadratic actions for tensor and scalar perturbations

In this section, we study tensor and scalar perturbations on a homogeneous and isotropic cosmological background based on the quadratic action (
42
) of the extended EFT.
Note that vector perturbations are nondynamical in GDH theories unless the matter sector introduces a dynamical vector mode.

IV.1
Tensor perturbations

Let us consider tensor perturbations given by

\delta N=0,\qquad N^{i}=0,\qquad\delta\gamma_{ij}=a^{2}h_{ij},

(48)

with
h_{ii}=0=\partial_{j}h_{ij}
.
From the EFT action (
42
), the quadratic action for tensor perturbations can be derived as

\delta_{2}S_{T}=\int{\rm d}t{\rm d}^{3}x\,\hat{N}a^{3}\frac{M^{2}}{8}\left[\frac{\dot{h}_{ij}^{2}}{\hat{N}^{2}}-(1+\alpha_{\rm T})\frac{(\partial_{k}h_{ij})^{2}}{a^{2}}\right].

(49)

Therefore, we find the squared sound speed for the tensor perturbations as

c_{T}^{2}=1+\alpha_{\rm T}=\frac{X}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}f_{0}}\frac{2G_{4}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\thinspace\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\mathcal{G}$}}_{T}}.

(50)

Note that matter fields are assumed to be minimally coupled, and hence light propagates at unit speed.
Also, the conditions for the absence of ghost/gradient instabilities are respectively given by

M^{2}>0,\qquad 1+\alpha_{\rm T}>0.

(51)

From (
50
), one can read off the condition under which gravitational waves (GWs) propagate at the speed of light.
This condition makes the EFT consistent with the almost simultaneous detection of the GW event GW170817 and the gamma-ray burst 170817A emitted from a binary neutron star merger
Abbott et al. 2017a
;
Abbott et al. 2017b
;
Abbott et al. 2017c
.
Solving
c_{T}^{2}=1
for
f_{0}
, we have

f_{0}=\frac{X}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}}\frac{2G_{4}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\thinspace\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\mathcal{G}$}}_{T}}=\frac{X}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}}\frac{G_{4}(\phi,\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}})}{G_{4}(\phi,\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}})-2\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}G_{4X}(\phi,\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}})}.

(52)

This result is consistent with the condition obtained in
Takahashi et al. 2023a
.

IV.2
Scalar perturbations

Let us consider scalar perturbations given by

\delta N=\hat{N}\nu,\qquad N_{i}=\hat{N}\partial_{i}\psi,\qquad\delta\gamma_{ij}=2a^{2}\left(\zeta\delta_{ij}+\partial_{i}\partial_{j}E\right).

(53)

In what follows, we adopt the gauge
E=0
on top of
\delta\phi=0
which we have already imposed in constructing the EFT action (
42
).
Note that this is a complete gauge fixing and hence can be imposed at the action level
Motohashi et al. 2016b
.
We first consider only the gravitational action and the effect of matter fields will be discussed later in this subsection.
Then, the quadratic action for scalar perturbations can be written as

\displaystyle\delta_{2}S_{S}=\int{\rm d}t{\rm d}^{3}x\,\hat{N}a^{3}\frac{M^{2}}{2}\Biggl[\!

\displaystyle-6(1+\alpha_{\rm L})\left(\frac{\dot{\zeta}}{\hat{N}}-H\nu-\frac{\Delta\psi}{3a^{2}}\right)^{2}+\frac{2(\Delta\psi)^{2}}{3a^{4}}+2(1+\alpha_{\rm T})\frac{(\partial_{i}\zeta)^{2}}{a^{2}}+H^{2}\alpha_{\rm K}\nu^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(12H\alpha_{\rm B}\nu+12\beta_{1}\frac{\dot{\nu}}{\hat{N}}+\frac{\gamma_{1}+3\gamma_{2}}{M_{\rm D}}\frac{\Delta\nu}{a^{2}}\right)\left(\frac{\dot{\zeta}}{\hat{N}}-H\nu-\frac{\Delta\psi}{3a^{2}}\right)-4(1+\alpha_{\rm H})\nu\frac{\Delta\zeta}{a^{2}}

(54)

In general, this action involves two dynamical DOFs, one of which is the Ostrogradsky mode.
However, if the EFT (
42
) is generated from the leading-order one (
28
) by the generalized disformal transformation, then the Ostrogradsky mode does not show up.
In such a case, one has the following relations among the EFT parameters [see Eq. (
43
)]:

\begin{split}&\beta_{2}=-\frac{6\beta_{1}^{2}}{1+\alpha_{\rm L}},\qquad\beta_{3}=-4\beta_{1}\frac{1+\alpha_{\rm H}}{1+\alpha_{\rm L}}-2\beta_{1}^{2}\frac{1+\alpha_{\rm T}}{(1+\alpha_{\rm L})^{2}}-\frac{2H\alpha_{\rm B}\gamma_{1}}{M_{\rm D}}+\frac{(aM^{2}\beta_{1}\gamma_{1})^{\bm{\cdot}}}{\hat{N}aM^{2}M_{\rm D}},\\
&\gamma_{2}=-\left(1+\frac{2}{3}\alpha_{\rm L}\right)\gamma_{1},\qquad\gamma_{3}=-\frac{\alpha_{\rm L}}{6}\gamma_{1}^{2}=\frac{1}{4}\gamma_{1}(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}).\end{split}

(55)

In particular, the first relation ensures the absence of Ostrogradsky mode, and hence there is only one dynamical DOF.
After redefining
\zeta
as

\tilde{\zeta}\coloneqq\zeta-\frac{\beta_{1}}{1+\alpha_{\rm L}}\nu,

(56)

and imposing (
55
), the action takes the form

\displaystyle\delta_{2}S_{S}=\int{\rm d}t{\rm d}^{3}x\,\hat{N}a^{3}\frac{M^{2}}{2}\Biggl[

\displaystyle c_{0}\left(\frac{\dot{\tilde{\zeta}}}{\hat{N}}\right)^{2}+\frac{\dot{\tilde{\zeta}}}{\hat{N}}\left(c_{1}-c_{2}\frac{\Delta}{a^{2}}\right)\nu-c_{3}\frac{\dot{\tilde{\zeta}}}{\hat{N}}\frac{\Delta\psi}{a^{2}}+c_{4}\frac{(\partial_{i}\tilde{\zeta})^{2}}{a^{2}}-c_{5}\nu\frac{\Delta\tilde{\zeta}}{a^{2}}

\displaystyle+\nu\left(c_{6}-c_{7}\frac{\Delta}{a^{2}}+c_{8}\frac{\Delta^{2}}{a^{4}}\right)\nu-\nu\left(c_{9}-c_{10}\frac{\Delta}{a^{2}}\right)\frac{\Delta\psi}{a^{2}}+c_{11}\left(\frac{\Delta\psi}{a^{2}}\right)^{2}\,\Biggr],

\begin{split}&c_{0}=-6(1+\alpha_{\rm L}),\qquad c_{1}=12H\left[1+\alpha_{\rm B}+\alpha_{\rm L}-\frac{1+\alpha_{\rm L}}{\hat{N}H}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\left(\frac{\beta_{1}}{1+\alpha_{\rm L}}\right)\right],\qquad c_{2}=\frac{2(1+\alpha_{\rm L})\gamma_{1}}{M_{\rm D}},\\
&c_{3}=-4(1+\alpha_{\rm L}),\qquad c_{4}=2(1+\alpha_{\rm T}),\qquad c_{5}=4(1+\alpha_{\rm H})+\frac{4(1+\alpha_{\rm T})\beta_{1}}{1+\alpha_{\rm L}},\\

&c_{8}=-\frac{\alpha_{\rm L}\gamma_{1}^{2}}{6M_{\rm D}^{2}},\qquad c_{9}=\frac{c_{1}}{3},\qquad c_{10}=\frac{2\alpha_{\rm L}\gamma_{1}}{3M_{\rm D}},\qquad c_{11}=-\frac{2}{3}\alpha_{\rm L}.\end{split}

In the above action, there is no time derivative acting on
\nu
or
\psi

After this manipulation, we have

\displaystyle\delta_{2}S_{S}=\int{\rm d}t{\rm d}^{3}x\,\hat{N}a^{3}\frac{M^{2}}{2}\left[C_{1}\left(\frac{\dot{\tilde{\zeta}}}{\hat{N}}\right)^{2}+\tilde{\zeta}\left(C_{2}-C_{3}\frac{\Delta}{a^{2}}\right)\frac{\Delta\tilde{\zeta}}{a^{2}}\right],

V
Conclusions

The higher-derivative generalization of invertible disformal transformations constructed in Ref.
Takahashi et al. 2022
offers a possibility to generate more general ghost-free scalar-tensor theories than ever.
Starting with the Horndeski class, one obtains the GDH class
Takahashi et al. 2023a
, which form the most general class of ghost-free scalar-tensor theories to this date.
Moreover, one could go further by starting with the U-DHOST class at the price of introducing a shadowy mode, and we call this class the GDU class.
In the present paper, we have extended the EFT of cosmological perturbations to accommodate GDH theories as well as GDU theories.
In doing so, we have restricted ourselves to the transformation of the form (
3
), which allows for consistent matter coupling without Ostrogradsky ghosts
Takahashi et al. 2023a
;
Naruko et al. 2023
;
Takahashi et al. 2023b
.
The extended EFT involves three new parameters
\gamma_{1}
,
\gamma_{2}
, and
\gamma_{3}
, on top of those that already exist in the EFT for DHOST theories [see Eq. (
42
)].
The new parameters multiply higher spatial derivatives of the perturbation of the lapse function, which originate from the new disformal factor
f_{2}
introduced in the generalized disformal transformation (
3
).
Based on our extended EFT, we have studied scalar and tensor cosmological perturbations.
We have obtained the condition under which GWs propagate at the speed of light, to be consistent with the nearly simultaneous detection of the GW event GW170817 and the gamma-ray burst 170817A emitted from a binary neutron star merger.
Also, we have derived the quadratic action for the scalar perturbations to see that the dispersion relation in general involves terms with higher-order spatial derivatives, which would be related to the existence of a shadowy mode.
In the vacuum case, this effect is controlled by the parameter
\alpha_{\rm L}
, which can be nonvanishing in U-DHOST and GDU theories.
In the case of GDH theories where
\alpha_{\rm L}=0
, the vacuum dispersion relation has the ordinary quadratic form.
On the other hand, if a matter field is present, higher-order spatial derivatives show up even in GDH theories.
If we restrict ourselves to DH theories, the action involves only up to second-order derivatives, which is consistent with the result of Ref.
Langlois et al. 2017
.

A possible immediate application of our extended EFT would be the study of the CMB.
Indeed, the authors of Refs.
Hiramatsu and Yamauchi 2020
;
Hiramatsu 2022
showed that the parameter
\beta_{1}
, which is peculiar to DHOST theories, affects the angular power spectrum of the temperature anisotropies through the integrated Sachs-Wolfe effect.
Likewise, it would be intriguing to study how the EFT parameters that are peculiar to GDH/GDU theories can affect the CMB.
Besides, in the context of the EFT for DHOST theories, it is known that the operators associated with the EFT parameters
\alpha_{\rm H}
and
\beta_{1}
lead to efficient GW decay into scalar perturbations.
Given that GW interferometers have successfully detected GWs, the decay must be suppressed at least in the late-time Universe, which puts a tight constraint on
\alpha_{\rm H}+2\beta_{1}

Creminelli et al. 2018
;
Creminelli et al. 2019
when the EFT is applied to dark energy.
Also, there are possible ghost/gradient instabilities in the presence of a GW background, which yields another constraint on the parameter
\alpha_{\rm B}

Creminelli et al. 2020
.
Considerations of these kinds might in principle put tight constraints on the parameters of our extended EFT
Minamitsuji et al.
.
We leave these issues for future work.

Appendix A
Generalized disformal U-DHOST theories

U-DHOST theories were proposed as a possible ghost-free extension of DHOST theories by allowing for the existence of a shadowy mode
De Felice et al. 2018
;
De Felice et al. 2021
.
By exploiting the generalized disformal transformation, we can construct more general theories, i.e., GDU theories.
In this appendix, we argue that the quadratic action (
42
) accommodates GDU theories as well as GDH theories.

For concreteness, we consider GDU theories that are generated from quadratic higher-order scalar-tensor theories whose action is given by

S=\int{\rm d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[G_{2}(\phi,X)+G_{3}(\phi,X)\Box\phi+G_{4}(\phi,X)R+\sum_{I=1}^{5}A_{I}(\phi,X)L_{I}^{(2)}\right],

(67)

where
G_{2}
,
G_{3}
,
G_{4}
, and
A_{I}
’s are functions of
(\phi,X)
and

(68)

Under the unitary gauge, the degeneracy condition reads
Langlois and Noui 2016
;
De Felice et al. 2018
;
De Felice et al. 2021

4\left[X(A_{1}+3A_{2})+2G_{4}\right]\left[A_{1}+A_{2}+X(A_{3}+A_{4})+X^{2}A_{5}\right]=3X(2A_{2}+XA_{3}+4G_{4X})^{2},

(69)

which defines the U-DHOST class.
Note that Horndeski theories amount to

A_{1}=-A_{2}=2G_{4X},\qquad A_{3}=A_{4}=A_{5}=0.

(70)

In order to discuss the mapping between higher-order scalar-tensor theories and the EFT of cosmological perturbations, it is useful to rewrite the action (
67
) in the form

\displaystyle S=\int{\rm d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,\bigg\{

\displaystyle G_{2}-Xg_{3\phi}-2\sqrt{-X}\left(Xg_{3X}+G_{4\phi}\right)K+G_{4}{}^{(3)}\!R+\left(G_{4}-XA_{1}\right)K^{\mu}_{\nu}K^{\nu}_{\mu}-\left(G_{4}+XA_{2}\right)K^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(2A_{2}+XA_{3}+4G_{4X}\right)K\frac{\phi^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}X}{2\sqrt{-X}}+\left[A_{1}+A_{2}+X(A_{3}+A_{4})+X^{2}A_{5}\right]\frac{(\phi^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}X)^{2}}{4X^{2}}

\displaystyle+(2A_{1}+XA_{4}-4G_{4X})\left(g^{\mu\nu}-\frac{\phi^{\mu}\phi^{\nu}}{X}\right)\frac{\nabla_{\mu}X\nabla_{\nu}X}{4X}\bigg\}\,,

(71)

where the function

has been defined so that

.

and

are the extrinsic curvature and the spatial Ricci scalar associated with the constant-

)].
Here, we have assumed

, i.e., the scalar field has a timelike profile.
Starting with this action, one can move to the frame where

A_{1}+A_{2}+X(A_{3}+A_{4})+X^{2}A_{5}=2A_{1}+XA_{4}-4G_{4X}=0,

(72)

by performing a conventional disformal transformation of the form (
1
) with an appropriate choice of the functions
f_{0}
and
f_{1}
.
For U-DHOST theories that satisfy (
69
), this leads to
2A_{2}+XA_{3}+4G_{4X}=0
.
Then, the terms with higher derivatives in (
71
) [i.e., those in the second and third lines] are removed, and we are left with the following action:

S=\int{\rm d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[G_{2}-Xg_{3\phi}-2\sqrt{-X}\left(Xg_{3X}+G_{4\phi}\right)K+G_{4}{}^{(3)}\!R+\left(G_{4}-XA_{1}\right)K^{\mu}_{\nu}K^{\nu}_{\mu}-\left(G_{4}+XA_{2}\right)K^{2}\right].

(73)

It should be noted that any (generalized) disformal transformation of quadratic U-DHOST theories can be generated from (

This is because the functional composition of two (generalized) disformal transformations is again a (generalized) disformal transformation

For U-DHOST theories described by the action (

In particular, we have

\tilde{M}^{2}=2(G_{4}-XA_{1}),\qquad\tilde{\alpha}_{\rm L}=\frac{3}{2}\frac{X(A_{1}+A_{2})}{G_{4}-XA_{1}},\qquad\tilde{\alpha}_{\rm T}=\frac{XA_{1}}{G_{4}-XA_{1}},\qquad\tilde{\alpha}_{\rm H}=\frac{X(A_{1}-2G_{4X})}{G_{4}-XA_{1}}.

Therefore, (

) accommodates GDU theories.

\displaystyle=G_{2}-XG_{3\phi}+\frac{\dot{\phi}\dot{X}}{N^{2}}G_{3X}+2\left(3H^{2}+\frac{2\dot{H}}{N}\right)\left(G_{4}-2XG_{4X}\right)-4H\frac{\dot{X}}{N}\left(G_{4X}+2XG_{4XX}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\dot{\phi}}{NX}\left(4HX+\frac{\dot{X}}{N}\right)G_{4\phi}-2\frac{\dot{\phi}}{N}\left(4HX-\frac{\dot{X}}{N}\right)G_{4\phi X}-2XG_{4\phi\phi},

(76)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{1}

Then, in §
III.3
, we obtained the EFT action that accommodates GDH theories by performing the generalized disformal transformation on the seed EFT action with the part (

When matter fields are introduced, the results of §

\delta_{2}S_{\rm g}

75

One has to study how the action (
75
) is transformed under the generalized disformal transformation to correctly incorporate the effect of the matter fields.

Performing the generalized disformal transformation, the action (
75
) is transformed as

\displaystyle S_{0}

\displaystyle\mapsto\int{\rm d}^{4}x\sqrt{-\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 0.0899pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$g$}}}\left[\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 1.26387pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\mathcal{E}$}}_{0}+\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 1.26387pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\mathcal{E}$}}_{1}\left(\frac{\delta\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.88745pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$N$}}}{\hat{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.88745pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$N$}}}}-\frac{\delta\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.88745pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$N$}}^{2}}{\hat{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.88745pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$N$}}}^{2}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\int{\rm d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left\{\vphantom{\frac{\hat{X}^{2}\hat{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}}_{X}^{2}}{2\hat{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}}^{2}}}\hat{\mathcal{J}}\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 1.26387pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\mathcal{E}$}}_{0}+\left(\frac{\hat{X}\hat{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}}_{X}}{\hat{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}}}\hat{\mathcal{J}}\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 1.26387pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\mathcal{E}$}}_{1}-2\hat{X}\hat{\mathcal{J}}_{X}\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 1.26387pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\mathcal{E}$}}_{0}\right)\frac{\delta N}{\hat{N}}+\frac{6\hat{X}^{2}\hat{f}_{0\mathcal{Z}}}{\hat{f}_{0}}\hat{\mathcal{J}}\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 1.26387pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\mathcal{E}$}}_{0}\frac{(\partial_{i}\delta N)^{2}}{\hat{N}^{2}a^{2}}\right.

where
\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 1.26387pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\mathcal{E}$}}_{0}

\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 1.26387pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\mathcal{E}$}}_{1}

\mathcal{E}_{1}

\hat{\mathcal{J}}\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 1.26387pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\mathcal{E}$}}_{0}=-\hat{p}_{\rm m},\qquad\frac{\hat{X}\hat{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}}_{X}}{\hat{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.84857pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$X$}}}}\hat{\mathcal{J}}\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 1.26387pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\mathcal{E}$}}_{1}-2\hat{X}\hat{\mathcal{J}}_{X}\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 1.26387pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\mathcal{E}$}}_{0}=\hat{\rho}_{\rm m}+\hat{p}_{\rm m},

Flatness of the nuclear norm sphere, simultaneous polarization, and uniqueness in nuclear norm minimization Thanks: The first and second author are partially supported by an NSERC discovery grant
Tim Hoheisel, Elliot Paquette

Abstract
. In this paper we establish necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of line segments (or flats ) in the sphere of the nuclear norm via the notion of simultaneous polarization and a refined expression for the subdifferential of the nuclear norm. This is then leveraged to provide (point-based) necessary and sufficient conditions for uniqueness of solutions for minimizing the nuclear norm over an affine manifold. We further establish an alternative set of sufficient conditions for uniqueness, based on the interplay of the subdifferential of the nuclear norm and the range of the problem-defining linear operator. Finally, using convex duality, we show how to transfer the uniqueness results for the original problem to a whole class of nuclear norm-regularized minimization problems with a strictly convex fidelity term.

Introduction

One of the most ubiquitous paradigms for linear inverse problems in matrix space is

\min_{X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}}\mathrm{rank}\,X\ \mbox{s.t.}\ \mathcal{A}(X)=b.

Here

is a linear map (into a Euclidean space
\mathbb{E}

or a selection operator which projects

[
4

[

[
15

for applications, solution methods and pointers to the abundant literature for the low rank minimization problem (

Due to the combinatorial nature of the rank function, problem (

), and therefore many continuous relaxations for its numerical solution have been proposed. The predominant class of convex relaxations uses the

(see also the approach by Hiriart-Urruty and Len

\min_{X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}}\|X\|_{*}\ \mbox{s.t.}\ \mathcal{A}(X)=b.

Preliminaries

In what follows,
\mathbb{E}
will be a Euclidean space, i.e. a finite-dimensional real inner product space with its ambient inner product denoted by
\left\langle\cdot,\,\cdot\right\rangle
. The induced norm is denoted by
\|\cdot\|
, i.e.
\|x\|:=\sqrt{\left\langle x,\,x\right\rangle}
for all
x\in\mathbb{E}
. For instance, we equip
\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
with the (Frobenius) inner product

\left\langle X,\,Y\right\rangle:=\mathrm{tr}\,(X^{T}Y)\quad\forall X,Y\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p},

which induces the
Frobenius norm

\|X\|:=\sqrt{\left\langle X,\,X\right\rangle}=\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{p}x_{ij}^{2}}\quad\forall X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}.

For
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
its
nuclear norm
is given by

\|X\|_{*}:=\mathrm{tr}\,(\sqrt{X^{T}X})=\mathrm{tr}\,(\sqrt{XX^{T}}).

The definition implies the following fact used frequently in our study:

(3)

\|X\|_{*}=\mathrm{tr}\,(X)\quad\forall X\in\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}.

The
dual norm
of the nuclear norm is

\|X\|_{op}:=\max_{\|Y\|_{*}\leq 1}\left\langle X,\,Y\right\rangle=\max_{\|v\|\leq 1}\|Xv\|,

which is called the
operator norm
or
spectral norm
. In what follows, we will define

\mathbb{B}_{op}:=\left\{X\,\left|\;\|X\|_{op}\leq 1\right.\right\}

to be the operator norm unit ball in a matrix space whose dimension will be clear from the context. The following simple estimate for the operator norm will be useful for our study.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
. Then the Euclidean norm of every column and row of
A
is bounded above by
\|A\|_{op}
. In particular, we have

a_{ij}\leq\|A\|_{op}
for all
i=1,\dots,n,\;j=1,\dots,p.

Proof.

Let
a_{j}
be the
j
-th column of
A
. Then

\|a_{j}\|=\|Ae_{j}\|\leq\sup_{\|x\|=1}\|Ax\|=\|A\|_{op}.

Multiplying standard unit vectors
e_{j}^{T}
from the left, we get the analogous statement for rows.
∎

The following estimate for the nuclear norm of block matrices is important to our study.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
n>p
,
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
and
Y\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times(n-p)}
. Then

\|X\|_{*}\leq\|[X\;Y]\|_{*},

where equality holds if and only if
Y=0.

Proof.

Observe that
\|W\|_{op}=\|[W\;0]\|_{op}
. Hence

\mathcal{C}:=\left\{[W\;0]\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\,\left|\;W\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p},\;\|W\|_{op}\leq 1\right.\right\}\subset\mathbb{B}_{op}.

Consequently

\|[X\;Y]\|_{*}=\max_{[W\;Z]\in\mathbb{B}_{op}}\left\langle[X\;Y],\,[W\;Z]\right\rangle\geq\max_{[W\;0]\in\mathcal{C}}\left\langle[X\;Y],\,[W\;0]\right\rangle=\|X\|_{*}.

Clearly, the inequality is strict if
Y\neq 0
(use, e.g.,
Z=Y
) and an equality otherwise.
∎

We point out that the above result allows one to always embed problem (
2
) (defined by
\mathcal{A}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n\times p},\mathbb{E})
and
b\in\mathbb{E}
) in (potentially rectangular) matrix space
\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
(w.l.o.g.
n\geq p
) into the (square) matrix space
\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
. To this end, identify every element
\tilde{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
with the block matrix
\tilde{X}=[X\;Y]
for
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p},Y\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times(n-p)}
, define the linear operator
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}:\tilde{X}\to\mathcal{A}(X)
and the right-hand side
\tilde{b}:=b
. If we now consider the ‘padded’ problem

(4)

\min_{[X\;Y]\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}}\|[X\;Y]\|_{*}\ \mbox{s.t.}\ \tilde{\mathcal{A}}([X\;Y])=\tilde{b}

it is an immediate consequence of
Lemma

2.2
that
\bar{X}
is a solution of (
2
) if and only if
[\bar{X}\;0]
is a solution of (
4
).

Singular value decomposition

For the facts and concepts presented in this paragraph we refer the uninitiated reader to Horn and Johnson
[
9
]
for details.
Throughout (w.l.o.g.) we assume that
n\geq p
. For
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
, with
\mathrm{rank}\,X=r
, there exist orthogonal matrices
U\in O(n)
and
V\in O(p)
(with columns
u_{1},\dots,u_{n}
and
v_{1},\dots,v_{p}
, respectively) and unique real numbers

\sigma_{1}(X)\geq\sigma_{2}(X)\geq\sigma_{r}(X)>0=\sigma_{r+1}=\dots=\sigma_{n}(X)

such that

X=U\mathrm{diag}(\sigma(X))V^{T}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sigma_{i}(X)u_{i}v_{i}^{T}.

This is called a
singular value decomposition
(SVD) of
X
. Note that the positive singular values of
X
are exactly the square roots of the nonzero eigenvalues of
XX^{T}
(or
X^{T}X
).
We say that two matrices
X,Y\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
have a
simultaneous singular value decomposition
if there exist
(\bar{U},\bar{V})\in O(n)\times O(p)
such that

X=\bar{U}\mathrm{diag}(\sigma(X))\bar{V}^{T}\and Y=\bar{U}\mathrm{diag}(\sigma(Y))\bar{V}^{T}.

The next result, see e.g.
[
10
, Theorem 2.1]
, due to von Neumann, characterizes simultaneous singular value decompositions.

Theorem 2.3

(von Neumann)
.

For
X,Y\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
we have

\left\langle X,\,Y\right\rangle\leq\left\langle\sigma(X),\,\sigma(Y)\right\rangle.

Equality holds if and only if
X
and
Y
have simultaneous singular value decompositions.

Through the singular value decomposition, we generate the map

\sigma:X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}\to\sigma(X)\in\mathbb{R}^{p}.

Using this, the nuclear and operator norm of
X
, respectively, can be expressed as the
\ell_{1}
- and
\ell_{\infty}
-norm, respectively, of the vector of singular values of
X
, i.e.

\|X\|_{*}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sigma_{i}(X)\and\|X\|_{op}=\sigma_{1}(X).

Moreover, we find that the nuclear and the operator norm are
orthogonally invariant
, i.e. for all
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
, we have

(5)

\|UXV\|_{*}=\|X\|_{*}\and\|UXV\|_{op}=\|VXU\|_{op}\quad\forall(U,V)\in O(n)\times O(p).

There is an important extension of the above equation in the rectangular case. To formulate it, we recall the
Stiefel manifold

[
20
]
.

Definition 2.4

(Stiefel manifold)
.

The Stiefel manifold
\mathcal{V}_{n,p}
is the collection of matrices in
\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
with orthonormal columns, i.e.

\mathcal{V}_{n,p}:=\left\{U\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}\,\left|\;U^{T}U=I_{p}\right.\right\}.

The nuclear norm also has invariance on one side by multiplication by elements of the Stiefel manifold.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
and
U\in\mathcal{V}_{n,p}
. Then

\|XU^{T}\|_{*}=\|X\|_{*}.

Proof.

As
U
has orthonormal columns, we may extend it to an orthogonal matrix
[U\;W]\in O(n)
. Then

\|XU^{T}\|_{*}=\left\|[X\;0]\cdot\left[\begin{matrix}U^{T}\\
W^{T}\end{matrix}\right]\right\|_{*}=\|[X\;0]\|_{*}=\|X\|_{*},

where the second identity uses the orthogonal invariance from (
5
) and the third is due to
Lemma

2.2
.
∎

Tools from convex analysis

For the facts and concepts presented in this paragraph we refer the uninitiated reader to the textbooks by Rockafellar

A function
f:\mathbb{E}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is called proper if
\mathrm{dom}\,f:=\left\{x\,\left|\;f(x)<+\infty\right.\right\}\neq\emptyset
. We say that
f
is
convex
if its epigraph
\mathrm{epi}\,f:=\left\{(x,\alpha)\in\mathbb{E}\times\mathbb{R}\,\left|\;f(x)\leq\alpha\right.\right\}
is convex, and we say that it is
closed
if
\mathrm{epi}\,f
is closed. Its (Fenchel) conjugate
f^{*}:\mathbb{E}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is

f^{*}(y):=\sup_{x\in\mathrm{dom}\,f}\{\left\langle y,\,x\right\rangle-f(x)\}.

Its (convex) subdifferential at
\bar{x}\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
is given by

\partial f(\bar{x}):=\left\{y\in\mathbb{E}\,\left|\;f(\bar{x})+\left\langle y,\,x-\bar{x}\right\rangle\leq f(x)\;\forall x\in\mathrm{dom}\,f\right.\right\}.

An important (proper, convex) extended real-valued function is the
indicator function
of a (nonempty, convex) set
C\subset\mathbb{E}
which is

\delta_{C}:\mathbb{E}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\},\quad\delta_{C}(x)=\begin{cases}0,&x\in C,\\
+\infty,&{\rm else}.\end{cases}

Its subdifferential is
\partial\delta_{C}(\bar{x})=\left\{v\,\left|\;\left\langle v,\,x-\bar{x}\right\rangle\leq 0\;\forall x\in C\right.\right\}
for all
\bar{x}\in C
. Its conjugate is the support function of
C
, i.e.
\delta_{C}^{*}(y)=\sup_{x\in C}\left\langle x,\,y\right\rangle=:\sigma_{C}(y).
We point out that the support functions
of compact, convex, symmetric sets
C
that contain
0
(thus
0\in\mathrm{int}\,C
) are exactly the norms on
\mathbb{E}

[
16
, Theorem 15.2]
.

The subdifferential of the
\ell_{1}
-norm
\|\cdot\|_{1}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R},\;\|x\|_{1}=\sum_{i=1}|x_{i}|
, reads

(6)

\partial\|\cdot\|_{1}(x)=\left(\begin{cases}\mathrm{sgn}(x_{i}),&x_{i}\neq 0\\
[-1,1],&x_{i}=0\end{cases}\right)_{i=1}^{n}=\left\{y\in\mathbb{B}_{\infty}\,\left|\;\left\langle x,\,y\right\rangle=\|x\|_{1}\right.\right\}.

Obviously, the most important example to our study is the subdifferential of the nuclear norm.

Proposition 2.6

(Subdifferential of nuclear norm)
.

Let
\bar{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
and let

(\bar{U},\bar{V})\in O(n)\times O(p)
such that

\bar{U}\mathrm{diag}(\sigma(\bar{X}))\bar{V}^{T}=\bar{X}.

The following hold:

(a)

We have
Y\in\partial\|\cdot\|_{*}(\bar{X})
if and only if
\bar{X}
and
Y
have a simultaneous singular value decomposition and

\sigma(Y)\in\partial\|\cdot\|_{1}(\sigma(\bar{X}))
.

(b)

It holds that

(7)

\displaystyle\partial\|\cdot\|_{*}(\bar{X})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{Y\,\left|\;\left\langle\bar{X},\,Y\right\rangle=\|\bar{X}\|_{*},\;\|Y\|_{op}\leq 1\right.\right\}

(8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{U}\partial\|\cdot\|_{*}(\mathrm{diag}(\sigma(\bar{X}))\bar{V}^{T}.

Proof.

(a) See
[
10

.

(b) The expressions for the subdifferential can be found in

24
]
, see, in particular,
[

For a convex set
C\subset\mathbb{E}
, its
affine hull
, denoted by
\mathrm{aff}\,C
, is the smallest affine set that contains
C
. In particular,
\mathrm{aff}\,C
is a subspace if and only if it contains
0
. The
subspace parallel to
C
is defined to be the unique subspace parallel to
\mathrm{aff}\,C
and given by
\mathrm{par}\,C\coloneqq\mathrm{aff}\,C-\bar{x}
for any
\bar{x}\in C
. Clearly, this entails that
\mathrm{ri}\,C=\mathrm{int}\,C
if and only if the latter is nonempty, i.e. when
\mathrm{par}\,C=\mathbb{E}
.

The
relative interior

\mathrm{ri}\,C

is its interior in the relative topology with respect to its affine hull. The following characterization of relative interior points is useful to our study, see, e.g.,
[

, Exerc. 13, Ch. 1]
:

(9)

x\in\mathrm{ri}\,C\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathbb{R}_{+}(C-x)=\mathrm{par}\,C.

For more details on the relative interior we refer the reader to Rockafellar
[
16

Lemma 2.7
.

For

set

\mathcal{T}:=\left\{B\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}\,\left|\;\mathrm{DIAG}(B)\in\{1\}^{r}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-r},\;\|B\|_{op}\leq 1\right.\right\}.

Then

\mathcal{T}=\left\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I_{r}&0\\
0&R\end{smallmatrix}\right)\,\left|\;R\in\mathbb{R}^{(n-r)\times(p-r)},\;\|R\|_{op}\leq 1\right.\right\}.

Proof.

Let

. Then, by

and the fact that

for all

Now, observe that, by taking adjoints,

and also

(c) Follows immediately from (a) or (b).

Flatness of the nuclear norm and simultaneous polarizability

As before, we assume (w.l.o.g.) that

\mathrm{rank}\,\bar{X}=r
, and let
W(\bar{X})
be defined as in
Corollary

3.6
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

\bar{X}
is the unique solution of (
19
).

(ii)

\ker\mathcal{A}\cap W(\bar{X})=\{0\}
.

Proof.

Observe that the solution set
\mathcal{X}
of (
19
) is convex and can be written as
\mathcal{X}=\left\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\,\left|\;\mathcal{A}(X)=\mathcal{A}(\bar{X}),\;\|X\|_{*}=\|\bar{X}\|_{*}\right.\right\}
. Now, by convexity,
\mathcal{X}
does not contain a proper line segment including
\bar{X}
if and only if
\bar{X}
is the unique solution of (
19
). Thus
Corollary

3.6
gives the desired statement.
∎

4.1.
Sufficient conditions through convex analysis

The following result is a generic convex analysis result which, given a solution, provides a sufficient condition for uniqueness of solutions to a(ny) convex optimization problem. It was established in
[
6
]
that in the polyhedral convex case it is also necessary which was then exploited to establish uniqueness of solutions for
\ell_{1}
-minimization problems.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
f:\mathbb{E}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be proper, convex and assume that
0\in\mathrm{int}\,\partial f(\bar{x})
. Then

\mathop{{\rm argmin}}f=\{\bar{x}\}
.

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathbb{E}
. By assumption, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
\varepsilon(x-\bar{x})\in\partial f(\bar{x})
. Consequently

f(x)\geq f(\bar{x})+\left\langle\varepsilon(x-\bar{x}),\,x-\bar{x}\right\rangle=f(\bar{x})+\varepsilon\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}>f(\bar{x}).

∎

We will, of course, apply this to the objective function
f=\|\cdot\|_{*}+\delta_{\{0\}}(\mathcal{A}(\cdot)-b)
of (
19
). It turns out that the following conditions at some (feasible) point
\bar{X}
are equivalent to having
0\in\mathrm{int}\,(\partial f(\bar{X}))
.

Assumption 4.3
.

For
\bar{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
such that
\mathcal{A}(\bar{X})=b
it holds that:

(i)

\mathrm{ri}\,(\partial\|\cdot\|_{*}(\bar{X}))\cap\mathrm{rge}\,\mathcal{A}^{*}\neq\emptyset
;

(ii)

\mathrm{par}\,\left(\partial\|\cdot\|_{*}(\bar{X})\right)+\mathrm{rge}\,\mathcal{A}^{*}=\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
.

The reader can make these conditions even more tangible by inserting the respective expressions for the relative interior and parallel subspace of the
\partial\|\cdot\|_{*}(\bar{X})
provided in
Proposition

2.8
(and (
10
)).

We now provide the advertized characterization.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n\times p},\mathbb{E})
,
b\in\mathbb{E}
and define the (closed) proper, convex function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
by
f(X)=\|X\|_{*}+\delta_{\{0\}}(\mathcal{A}(X)-b).

For
\bar{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
such that
\mathcal{A}(\bar{X})=b
, the following are equivalent:

(I)

0\in\mathrm{int}\,\partial f(\bar{X}).

(II)

4.3
holds at
\bar{X}
.

Proof.

Observe that
\partial(\delta_{\{0\}}((\cdot)-b)\circ\mathcal{A})(\bar{X})=\mathcal{A}^{*}\partial\delta_{\{0\}}(0)=\mathcal{A}^{*}\mathbb{E}=\mathrm{rge}\,\mathcal{A}^{*}
, by the chain rule
[
16
, Theorem 23.9]
, and consequently

\partial f(\bar{X})=\partial\|\cdot\|_{*}(\bar{X})+\mathrm{rge}\,\mathcal{A}^{*},

by the sum rule
[
16
, Theorem 23.8]
. Hence (I) reads

0\in\mathrm{int}\,(\partial\|\cdot\|_{*}(\bar{X})+\mathrm{rge}\,\mathcal{A}^{*})=\mathrm{ri}\,(\partial\|\cdot\|_{*}(\bar{X}))+\mathrm{rge}\,\mathcal{A}^{*},

where the identity uses the sum rule for the relative interior
[
16
, Corollary 6.6.2]
and the fact that a subspace is relatively open. This already shows that (I) implies
\mathrm{ri}\,(\partial\|\cdot\|_{*}(\bar{X}))\cap\mathrm{rge}\,\mathcal{A}^{*}\neq\emptyset
.
On the other hand, it also yields that, for any
y\in\partial\|\cdot\|_{*}(\bar{X})
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{aff}\,(\partial\|\cdot\|_{*}(\bar{X})+\mathrm{rge}\,\mathcal{A}^{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{aff}\,(\partial\|\cdot\|_{*}(\bar{X})+\mathrm{rge}\,\mathcal{A}^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{aff}\,(\partial\|\cdot\|_{*}(\bar{X})-y)+y+\mathrm{rge}\,\mathcal{A}^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{par}\,\partial\|\cdot\|_{*}(\bar{X})+\mathrm{rge}\,\mathcal{A}^{*}.

All in all, (I) implies (II).

Conversely, if (II), starting from
\mathrm{par}\,\partial\|\cdot\|_{*}(\bar{X})+\mathrm{rge}\,\mathcal{A}^{*}
, the latter equations shows
\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}=\mathrm{aff}\,(\partial\|\cdot\|_{*}(\bar{X})+\mathrm{rge}\,\mathcal{A}^{*})
, while
\mathrm{ri}\,(\partial\|\cdot\|_{*}(\bar{X}))\cap\mathrm{rge}\,\mathcal{A}^{*}\neq\emptyset
implies

0\in\mathrm{ri}\,(\partial\|\cdot\|_{*}(\bar{X}))+\mathrm{rge}\,\mathcal{A}^{*}=\mathrm{ri}\,(\partial\|\cdot\|_{*}(\bar{X})+\mathrm{rge}\,\mathcal{A}^{*})=\mathrm{int}\,(\partial\|\cdot\|_{*}(\bar{X})+\mathrm{rge}\,\mathcal{A}^{*}),

where the first identity is, again, due to the sum rule for the relative interior, while the last identity uses the fact that the relative interior is an interior if (and only if) the parallel subspace (which is here equal to the affine hull) of the convex set in question is the whole space.
∎

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
\bar{X}
be a solution of (
19
) such that
4.3
holds at
\bar{X}
. Then
\bar{X}
is the unique solution of (
19
)

Proof.

Combine
Proposition

4.2
and
Proposition

4.4
.
∎

4.2.
More insight

Combining
Corollary

4.5
and
Corollary

3.6
, it follows readily that
4.3
at
\bar{X}
implies that
W(\bar{X})\cap\ker\mathcal{A}=\{0\}
. On the other hand, this argument is not very illuminating when trying to understand the exact interplay of these two types of conditions. Moreover, it is not clear whether
4.3
might also be necessary for uniqueness of solutions (as it is for its
\ell_{1}
-analog). We shed some light on these issues now and start with an auxiliary result.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\bar{X}
satisfy
4.3
. Then

\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}=\mathrm{rge}\,\mathcal{A}^{*}+\mathbb{R}_{+}\partial\|\cdot\|_{*}(\bar{X}).

Proof.

Set
\mathcal{S}:=\partial\|\cdot\|_{*}(\bar{X})
, and let
Y\in\mathrm{rge}\,\mathcal{A}^{*}\cap\mathrm{ri}\,\mathcal{S}
which exists by
4.3
(i). Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{rge}\,\mathcal{A}^{*}+\mathrm{par}\,(\partial\|\cdot\|_{*}(\bar{X}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{rge}\,\mathcal{A}^{*}+\mathbb{R}_{+}(\mathcal{S}-Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}_{+}\left(\mathrm{rge}\,\mathcal{A}^{*}+\mathcal{S}-Y\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}_{+}(\mathrm{rge}\,\mathcal{A}^{*}+\mathcal{S})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{rge}\,\mathcal{A}^{*}+\mathbb{R}_{+}\mathcal{S}.

Here the second identity uses the property of relative interior points from (
9
).
∎

As alluded to above, the following result is clear from our previous analysis. We give an explicit proof in the hopes of consolidating the different flavors of the conditions in
4.3
and
Corollary

3.6
, respectively.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
\bar{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
with
r\coloneqq\mathrm{rank}\,\bar{X}
and singular value decomposition
\bar{X}=\bar{U}\mathrm{diag}(\sigma(\bar{X}))\bar{V}^{T}
. If
4.3
holds, then

\ker\mathcal{A}\cap W(\bar{X})=\{0\}.

Proof.

Let
X\in\ker\mathcal{A}\cap W(\bar{X})
. Then, by definition of
W(\bar{X})
, there exists
M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A-D&B\\
B^{T}&C\end{smallmatrix}\right)
with
A\in\mathbb{S}^{r}_{+},C\in\mathbb{S}^{n-r}_{+}
,
D\in\mathbb{S}^{r}_{++}
,
\mathrm{tr}\,(A)+\mathrm{tr}\,(C)=\mathrm{tr}\,(D)
, and
R\in\mathcal{V}_{n-r,p-r}
such that

X=\bar{U}M\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I_{r}&0\\
0&R\end{smallmatrix}\right)\bar{V}^{T}.

On the other hand, by
Lemma

4.6
and
Proposition

2.8
we find
Z\in\mathrm{rge}\,\mathcal{A}^{*}
,
F\in\mathbb{B}_{op}
and
t\geq 0
such that

X=Z+t\cdot\bar{U}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I_{r}&0\\
0&F\end{smallmatrix}\right)\bar{V}^{T}.

Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\|X\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\bar{U}M\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I_{r}&0\\
0&R\end{smallmatrix}\right)\bar{V}^{T},\,Z+t\cdot\bar{U}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I_{r}&0\\
0&F\end{smallmatrix}\right)\bar{V}^{T}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t\cdot\left\langle\bar{U}M\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I_{r}&0\\
0&R\end{smallmatrix}\right)\bar{V}^{T},\,\bar{U}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I_{r}&0\\
0&F\end{smallmatrix}\right)\bar{V}^{T}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t\cdot\left\langle M\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I_{r}&0\\
0&R\end{smallmatrix}\right),\,\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I_{r}&0\\
0&F\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t\cdot\mathrm{tr}\,\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I_{r}&0\\
0&RF^{T}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\cdot\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A-D&B\\
B^{T}&C\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t\cdot\mathrm{tr}\,\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A-D&B\\
RF^{T}B^{T}&RF^{T}C\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t\cdot\left(\mathrm{tr}\,(A)-\mathrm{tr}\,(D)+\mathrm{tr}\,(RF^{T}C)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t\cdot\left(\mathrm{tr}\,(RF^{T}C)-\mathrm{tr}\,(C)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle t\cdot\left(\|RF^{T}\|_{op}\cdot\|C\|_{*}-\|C\|_{*}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 0.

Here, the second identity takes into account that
Z\in\mathrm{rge}\,\mathcal{A}^{*}
while
\bar{U}M\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I_{r}&0\\
0&R\end{smallmatrix}\right)\bar{V}^{T}=X\in\ker\mathcal{A}
. The seventh (last) equality uses the fact that
\mathrm{tr}\,(A)+\mathrm{tr}\,(C)=\mathrm{tr}\,(D)
. The first inequality uses the fact that
C
is positive semidefinite as well as the ‘Hölder inequality’ for the operator and nuclear norm. The last inequality is due to the fact that
\|R\|_{op}=1
,
\|F\|_{op}\leq 1
and the submulitiplicativity of the operator norm.

All in all, we find that
X=0
which proves the desired result.
∎

The natural question as to whether
4.3
is also necessary for uniqueness is answered negatively by the following example.

Example 4.8
.

Set
\mathbb{E}:=\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
, and define
\mathcal{A}:\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}\to\mathbb{E}
by

\mathcal{A}(X)=[\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\
0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)X,\;P_{\mathbb{A}^{2}}(X)],

where
P_{\mathbb{A}^{2}}(X)\coloneq\frac{1}{2}(X-X^{T})
is the projection onto the
2\times 2
skew symmetric matrices
\mathbb{A}^{2}
. Set
b:=[\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right),\;\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&0\\
0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)]\in\mathbb{E}
. Equipped with these choices, consider

(20)

\min_{X\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}}\|X\|_{*}\ \mbox{s.t.}\ \mathcal{A}(X)=b.

The following hold:

•

\ker\mathcal{A}=\mathrm{span}\,\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&-1\\
-1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\}
.

•

\mathcal{A}^{*}:\mathbb{E}\to\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2},\quad\mathcal{A}^{*}(Y,Z)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)Y+P_{\mathbb{A}^{2}}(Z)
.

•

\mathrm{rge}\,\mathcal{A}^{*}=\left\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}t&s\\
t&s\end{smallmatrix}\right)\,\left|\;t,s\in\mathbb{R}\right.\right\}+\mathbb{A}^{2}
.

Now, set
\bar{X}:=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. Then
\mathcal{A}(\bar{X})=b
, i.e.
\bar{X}
is feasible for (
20
). Moreover, by
Proposition

2.8
, observe that

\partial\|\cdot\|_{*}(\bar{X})=\left\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&\beta\end{smallmatrix}\right)\,\left|\;\beta\in[-1,1]\right.\right\}\,\and\mathrm{par}\,(\partial\|\cdot\|_{*}(\bar{X}))=\left\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&0\\
0&\beta\end{smallmatrix}\right)\,\left|\;\beta\in\mathbb{R}\right.\right\}.

It is then an easy exercise to find that

\mathrm{par}\,(\partial\|\cdot\|_{*}(\bar{X}))+\mathrm{rge}\,\mathcal{A}^{*}=\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}.

Moreover, we observe that

\displaystyle 0\in\partial\|\cdot\|_{*}(\bar{X})+\mathrm{rge}\,\mathcal{A}^{*}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\partial\|\cdot\|_{*}(\bar{X})\cap\mathrm{rge}\,\mathcal{A}^{*}\neq\emptyset

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\exists\beta\in[-1,1],t,s,q\in\mathbb{R}:\;\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&\beta\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}t&s\\
t&s\end{smallmatrix}\right)+\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&q\\
-q&0\end{smallmatrix}\right).

The latter system has only one solution
t=q=1,s=\beta=-1
. In particular, we see that
\bar{X}
is a minimizer of (
20
) and that

\mathrm{ri}\,(\partial\|\cdot\|_{*}(\bar{X}))\cap\mathrm{rge}\,\mathcal{A}^{*}=\emptyset.

In particular, the sufficient condition from
4.3
for uniqueness is violated at
\bar{X}
(while (ii) is satisfied). In turn, realizing that
\mathrm{rank}\,\bar{X}=1
, and consequently

W(\bar{X})=\left\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a-d&b\\
b&c\end{smallmatrix}\right)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&R\end{smallmatrix}\right)\,\left|\;R^{2}=1,\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
b&c\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}_{+},\mathrm{tr}\,\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a-d&b\\
b&c\end{smallmatrix}\right)=0,d>0\right.\right\},

we find that

\displaystyle X\in W(\bar{X})\cap\ker\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle X=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x&-x\\
-x&x\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a-d&\pm b\\
b&\pm c\end{smallmatrix}\right),a-d+c=0.

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle X=0.

Therefore, by
Corollary

3.6
,
\bar{X}
is the unique solution of (
20
).

4.3.
Other nuclear norm minimization problems

The following general result affords us to carry over uniqueness results from above to other nuclear norm minimization problems involving a linear operator. The proof relies on
Fenchel-Rockafellar duality

[
1
,
8
,
16
,
17
]
, and the dual correspondence of
strict convexity
and
essential smoothness

[
16
]
.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{1},\mathbb{E}_{2})
,
g:\mathbb{E}\to\mathbb{R}
strictly convex, and
h:\mathbb{E}_{1}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
closed, proper convex. Then
\mathcal{A}
and
h
are constant on the solution set

\mathcal{X}^{*}\coloneqq\mathop{{\rm argmin}}_{x\in\mathbb{E}_{1}}\left\{g(\mathcal{A}(x))+h(x)\right\}.

Proof.

Clearly, it suffices to prove that
\mathcal{A}
is constant on
\mathcal{X}^{*}
. To this end, observe that the dual problem of
(the primal problem)
\min_{x\in\mathbb{E}_{1}}\left\{g(\mathcal{A}(x))+h(x)\right\}

reads

\max_{y\in\mathbb{E}_{2}}\{-g^{*}(-y)-h^{*}(\mathcal{A}^{*}(y))\}.

Since
g
is finite-valued, strong duality holds, and, in particular, for some dual solution
\bar{y}\in\mathbb{E}_{2}
and any primal solution
x\in\mathcal{X}^{*}
it holds that, in particular,
\mathcal{A}(x)\in\partial g^{*}(-\bar{y})
, cf., e.g.
[
17
, Example 11.41]
. However, since
g
is strictly convex,
g^{*}
is
essentially smooth

[
16
, Theorem 26.3]
and hence
\mathcal{A}(x)=\nabla g^{*}(-\bar{y})
. Since
x\in\mathcal{X}^{*}
was arbitrary, this proves result.
∎

Corollary 4.10
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n\times p},\mathbb{E})
,
b\in\mathbb{E}
,
\lambda>0
,
f:\mathbb{E}\to\mathbb{R}
strictly convex, and let
\bar{X}
be a solution of

(21)

\min_{X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}}f(\mathcal{A}(X)-b)+\lambda\|X\|_{*}.

Then
\bar{X}
is the unique solution if and only if

\left\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}\,\left|\;\mathcal{A}(X)=\mathcal{A}(\bar{X}),\;\|X\|_{*}=\|\bar{X}\|_{*}\right.\right\}=\{\bar{X}\}.

(all of which is the case if and only if
W(\bar{X})\cap\ker\mathcal{A}=\{0\}).

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{X}=\mathop{{\rm argmin}}_{\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}}\{f(\mathcal{A}(\cdot)-b)+\lambda\|\cdot\|_{*}\}
be the solution set of (
21
). Applying
Proposition

4.9
to
g:=f((\cdot)-b)
and
h:=\lambda\|\cdot\|_{*}
yields that, in fact,
\mathcal{X}=\left\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}\,\left|\;\mathcal{A}(X)=\mathcal{A}(\bar{X}),\;\|X\|_{*}=\|\bar{X}\|_{*}\right.\right\}
. Therefore, the claim follows.
∎

5.
Final remarks

In this paper, starting from a study of line segments in the nuclear norm sphere, we established necessary and sufficient conditions for uniqueness of solutions for minimizing the nuclear norm over an affine manifold. The central linear-algebraic notion in this regard is
simultaneous polarizability
, which formalizes the idea of rotating two (square) matrices in the same fashion to render them positive semidefinite. We then gave another set of sufficient conditions based on the convex geometry of the subdifferential (of the nuclear norm) and its interplay with (the range of) the ambient linear operator.
A duality-based argument enabled us to transfer these findings to a whole class of nuclear norm-regularized optimization problems with strictly convex fidelity term.

As a topic of future research, we intend to build on this analysis to study stability of nuclear norm(-regularized) optimization problems in terms of the right-hand side
b
and the regularization parameter
\lambda
. In particular, we would like to study Lipschitz properties of the solution function

(b,\lambda)\mapsto\mathop{{\rm argmin}}_{X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}}\left\{\frac{1}{2}\|\mathcal{A}(X)-b\|^{2}+\lambda\|X\|_{*}\right\}.

This study will rely on a suitable representation of the graph of the subdifferential of the nuclear norm.

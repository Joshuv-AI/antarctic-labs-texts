A coupled Hartree system with Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev critical exponent: existence and multiplicity of high energy positive solutions
Mengyao Chen, Lun Guo, Qi Li

Abstract
This paper deals with a coupled Hartree system with Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev critical exponent { − Δ u + ( V 1 ( x ) + λ 1 ) u = μ 1 ( | x | − 4 ∗ u 2 ) u + β ( | x | − 4 ∗ v 2 ) u , x ∈ ℝ N , − Δ v + ( V 2 ( x ) + λ 2 ) v = μ 2 ( | x | − 4 ∗ v 2 ) v + β ( | x | − 4 ∗ u 2 ) v , x ∈ ℝ N , \begin{cases}-\Delta u+(V_{1}(x)+\lambda_{1})u=\mu_{1}(|x|^{-4}*u^{2})u+\beta(|x|^{-4}*v^{2})u,\ \ &x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N},\\ -\Delta v+(V_{2}(x)+\lambda_{2})v=\mu_{2}(|x|^{-4}*v^{2})v+\beta(|x|^{-4}*u^{2})v,\ \ &x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N},\\ \end{cases} where N ≥ 5 N\geq 5 , λ 1 \lambda_{1} , λ 2 ≥ 0 \lambda_{2}\geq 0 with λ 1 + λ 2 ≠ 0 \lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}\neq 0 , V 1 ​ ( x ) , V 2 ​ ( x ) ∈ L N 2 ​ ( ℝ N ) V_{1}(x),V_{2}(x)\in L^{\frac{N}{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}) are nonnegative functions and μ 1 \mu_{1} , μ 2 \mu_{2} , β \beta are positive constants. Such system arises from mathematical models in Bose-Einstein condensates theory and nonlinear optics. By variational methods combined with degree theory, we prove some results about the existence and multiplicity of high energy positive solutions under the hypothesis β > max ⁡ { μ 1 , μ 2 } \beta>\max\{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\} .



Keywords:
Hartree system; Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev critical exponent; Lack of compactness; Positive solution.

C(p,r,\alpha,N)=C(N,\alpha)=\pi^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N-\alpha}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{2N-\alpha}{2})}\Big\{\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})}{\Gamma(N)}\Big\}^{-\frac{N-\alpha}{N}}.

In this case, the equality in (
2.2
) is achieved if and only if
f\equiv\text{(const.)}g
and

g(x)=A(\widetilde{\gamma}^{2}+|x-\widetilde{a}|^{2})^{-\frac{(2N-\alpha)}{2}}

for some

,

and

To study system (
2.1
) by variational methods, we define the energy functional
I(u,v):H\to{\mathbb{R}}
by

\displaystyle I(u,v)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\Big(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla v|^{2}+\big(V_{1}(x)+\lambda_{1})u^{2}+(V_{2}(x)+\lambda_{2}\big)v^{2}\Big)dx

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{4}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{\mu_{1}|u^{+}(x)|^{2}|u^{+}(y)|^{2}+2\beta|u^{+}(x)|^{2}|v^{+}(y)|^{2}+\mu_{2}|v^{+}(x)|^{2}|v^{+}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{4}}dxdy.

Since
V_{1}(x),V_{2}(x)\in L^{\frac{N}{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
, then in view of Hölder inequality and Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality,
I
is well defined in
H
and belongs to

. In what follows, we define the Nehari manifold

\mathcal{N}:=\{(u,v)\in{H}\mid(u,v)\neq(0,0),\langle I^{\prime}(u,v),(u,v)\rangle=0\},

where

\displaystyle\langle I^{\prime}(u,v),(u,v)\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\Big(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla v|^{2}+\big(V_{1}(x)+\lambda_{1})u^{2}+(V_{2}(x)+\lambda_{2}\big)v^{2}\Big)dx

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{\mu_{1}|u^{+}(x)|^{2}|u^{+}(y)|^{2}+2\beta|u^{+}(x)|^{2}|v^{+}(y)|^{2}+\mu_{2}|v^{+}(x)|^{2}|v^{+}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{4}}dxdy.

Moreover, we set

c=\inf_{(u,v)\in\mathcal{N}}I(u,v).

We remark that the existence of non-trivial solutions to system (
2.1
) is closely related to the corresponding problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=\mu_{1}(|x|^{-4}*|u^{+}|^{2})u^{+}+\beta(|x|^{-4}*|v^{+}|^{2})u^{+},\ \ x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N},\\
-\Delta v=\mu_{2}(|x|^{-4}*|v^{+}|^{2})v^{+}+\beta(|x|^{-4}*|u^{+}|^{2})v^{+},\ \ x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}.\end{cases}

(2.3)

Throughout the paper we denote by

the energy functional whose critical points are solutions of system (
2.3
), that is

\displaystyle I_{\infty}(u,v)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\big(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla v|^{2}\big)dx

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{4}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{\mu_{1}|u^{+}(x)|^{2}|u^{+}(y)|^{2}+2\beta|u^{+}(x)|^{2}|v^{+}(y)|^{2}+\mu_{2}|v^{+}(x)|^{2}|v^{+}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{4}}dxdy.

Also the analogues of
\mathcal{N}
and
c
can be denoted by
\mathcal{N}_{\infty}
and
c_{\infty}
respectively, more precisely,

\mathcal{N}_{\infty}:=\{(u,v)\in H_{0}\mid(u,v)\neq(0,0),\,\,\langle I^{\prime}_{\infty}(u,v),(u,v)\rangle=0\}

and

To avoid semi-trivial solutions in searching for non-trivial solutions of system (

), we first investigate the single elliptic equation

-\Delta u+(V_{j}(x)+\lambda_{j})u=\mu_{j}(|x|^{-4}*|u^{+}|^{2})u^{+},\ \ j=1,2,

of which the associated energy functional
J_{j}:H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\to{\mathbb{R}}
(or

, if

Consider the infimum

m_{j}=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{M}_{j}}J_{j}(u),

defined on the Nehari manifold

\mathcal{M}_{j}=\big\{u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\mid u\neq 0,\langle J^{\prime}_{j}(u),u\rangle=0\big\},\quad\text{if}\ \lambda_{j}>0,

or

\mathcal{M}_{j}=\big\{u\in D^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\mid u\neq 0,\langle J^{\prime}_{j}(u),u\rangle=0\big\},\quad\text{if}\ \lambda_{j}=0.

If
V_{j}=\lambda_{j}=0
, we then denote the analogues of
J_{j}
,
m_{j}
,
\mathcal{M}_{j}
by
J_{j}^{\infty}
,

It is well-known that infimum

is attained by

W_{\delta,z,j}(x)=\mu_{j}^{-\frac{1}{2}}U_{\delta,z}(x),

where

U_{\delta,z}(x)=C_{N}\Big(\frac{\delta}{\delta^{2}+|x-z|^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{N-2}{2}},\ \ \delta>0,\ \ z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}

(2.4)

is the unique positive solution of

(2.5)

and constant
C_{N}
only depends on dimension

, see

,
23
]
. Moreover, by calculation we have

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla U_{\delta,z}(x)|^{2}dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{|U_{\delta,z}(x)|^{2}|U_{\delta,z}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{4}}dxdy=\mathcal{S}^{2}_{HL}=C(N,4)^{-1}\mathcal{S}^{2}.

and

m_{j}^{\infty}=\frac{\mathcal{S}_{HL}^{2}}{4\mu_{j}}.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
N\geq 5
and nonnegative function
V_{j}(x)\in L^{\frac{N}{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
, then

m_{j}=m_{j}^{\infty}=\frac{\mathcal{S}_{HL}^{2}}{4\mu_{j}}.

Proof:

For arbitrary
u\in\mathcal{M}_{j}
, since
V_{j}(x)
and
\lambda_{j}
are both nonnegative, then

J_{j}(u)\geq m_{j}^{\infty},

which implies that
m_{j}\geq m_{j}^{\infty}
.

To show that the opposite inequality holds, we then consider the sequence

\Phi_{n}(x)=\mu_{j}^{-\frac{1}{2}}U_{\delta_{n},0}(x),

where
\delta_{n}\to 0^{+}
as
n\to\infty
. On one hand, since
N\geq 5
, then

\lambda_{j}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\Phi_{n}|^{2}dx=\frac{\lambda_{j}\delta_{n}^{2}}{\mu_{j}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|U_{1,0}(x)|^{2}dx=o_{n}(1),

in which we use the property below

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|U_{1,0}(x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{1}{(1+|x|^{2})^{N-2}}dx

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B_{1}(0)}\frac{1}{(1+|x|^{2})^{N-2}}dx+C\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\setminus B_{1}(0)}\frac{1}{(1+|x|^{2})^{N-2}}dx

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}+C\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\setminus B_{1}(0)}\frac{1}{|x|^{2N-4}}dx=C_{1}+C\int_{1}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{r^{N-3}}dr<C_{2}.

On the other hand, by Hölder inequality we have for each
\rho>0
,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}V_{j}(x)|\Phi_{n}|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{\rho}(0)}V_{j}(x)|\Phi_{n}|^{2}dx+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\setminus B_{\rho}(0)}V_{j}(x)|\Phi_{n}|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq\|\Phi_{n}\|_{L^{2^{*}}}^{2}\Big(\int_{B_{\rho}(0)}|V_{j}(x)|^{\frac{N}{2}}dx\Big)^{\frac{2}{N}}+\|V_{j}\|_{L^{\frac{N}{2}}}\Big(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\setminus B_{\rho}(0)}|\Phi_{n}(x)|^{2^{*}}dx\Big)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}}}.

Direct calculations show that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\setminus B_{\rho}(0)}|\Phi_{n}|^{2^{*}}dx=0,\ \ \ \ \sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\|\Phi_{n}\|_{L^{2^{*}}}<+\infty

and

\lim_{\rho\to 0}\int_{B_{\rho}(0)}|V_{j}(x)|^{\frac{N}{2}}dx=0,

then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}V_{j}(x)|\Phi_{n}|^{2}dx=0.

Furthermore,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}(V_{j}(x)+\lambda_{j})|\Phi_{n}|^{2}dx=0.

(2.6)

For
t_{n}>0
defined by

t_{n}^{2}=\frac{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla\Phi_{n}|^{2}dx+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}(V_{j}(x)+\lambda_{i})|\Phi_{n}|^{2}dx}{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{\mu_{j}|\Phi_{n}(x)|^{2}|\Phi_{n}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{4}}dxdy},

we can infer that

and
t_{n}\to 1
as

. Therefore, by (
2.6
) we get

m_{j}\leq J_{j}(t_{n}\Phi_{n})=\frac{t_{n}^{2}}{4}\Big(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla\Phi_{n}|^{2}dx+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}(V_{j}(x)+\lambda_{j})|\Phi_{n}|^{2}dx\Big)=\frac{\mathcal{S}_{HL}^{2}}{4\mu_{j}}+o_{n}(1).

(2.7)

Taking the limit
n\to\infty
in (
2.7
), we get

m_{j}\leq\frac{\mathcal{S}_{HL}^{2}}{4\mu_{j}}=m_{j}^{\infty}.

Thus, the whole proof is completed.

\blacksquare

Lemma 2.7
.

Suppose that
N\geq 5
,
\beta>\max\{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\}
,
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\geq 0

\lambda:=\max\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\}>0

satisfy assumptions

-

Proof:

Let

. Following by the arguments in the proof of Lemma

(2.8)

Let

be such that

t_{n}^{2}=\frac{(k_{1}+k_{2})\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla\tilde{\Phi}_{n}|^{2}dx+k_{1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}(V_{1}(x)+\lambda_{1})|\tilde{\Phi}_{n}|^{2}dx+k_{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}(V_{2}(x)+\lambda_{2})|\tilde{\Phi}_{n}|^{2}dx}{(k_{1}+k_{2})\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{|\tilde{\Phi}_{n}(x)|^{2}|\tilde{\Phi}_{n}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{4}}dxdy},

and

as

, in which we use (

) and

\mu_{1}k_{1}^{2}+\mu_{2}k_{2}^{2}+2\beta k_{1}k_{2}=k_{1}+k_{2}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle\leq I(t_{n}\sqrt{k_{1}}\tilde{\Phi}_{n},t_{n}\sqrt{k_{2}}\tilde{\Phi}_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{t_{n}^{2}(k_{1}+k_{2})}{4}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla\tilde{\Phi}_{n}|^{2}dx+\frac{t_{n}^{2}k_{1}}{4}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}(V_{1}(x)+\lambda_{1})|\tilde{\Phi}_{n}|^{2}dx+\frac{t_{n}^{2}k_{2}}{4}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}(V_{2}(x)+\lambda_{2})|\tilde{\Phi}_{n}|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}(k_{1}+k_{2})\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla\tilde{\Phi}_{n}|^{2}dx+o_{n}(1)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}(k_{1}+k_{2})\mathcal{S}_{HL}^{2}+o_{n}(1).

Taking the limit
n\to\infty
in the equality above, then

c\leq\frac{1}{4}(k_{1}+k_{2})\mathcal{S}_{HL}^{2}=c_{\infty}.

Let
(u,v)\in\mathcal{N}
be arbitrarily chosen and set
\tau_{(u,v)}>0

(\tau_{(u,v)}u,\tau_{(u,v)}v)\in\mathcal{N}_{\infty}
. Recalling that

and

2.4
we get
\tau_{(u,v)}\leq 1
.
Furthermore, by computation we get

\displaystyle c_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq I_{\infty}(\tau_{(u,v)}u,\tau_{(u,v)}v)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\tau_{(u,v)}^{2}\big(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla v|^{2}dx\big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\big(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla v|^{2}dx\big)+\frac{1}{4}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}(V_{1}(x)+\lambda_{1})u^{2}dx+\frac{1}{4}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}(V_{2}(x)+\lambda_{2})v^{2}dx

\displaystyle=I(u,v),

which leads to
c_{\infty}\leq c
. Therefore,

.

3
Some compactness results

In this paper, the problem we studied is affected by lack of compactness due to the unboundedness of
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
and to the critical exponent. In this section, we introduce a global compactness result and investigate the behavior of Palais-Smale sequences of
I

3.3
below. We would like to point out that, a version of that compactness result for single equations and coupled systems can be found in
[

29

41

47
]
by different approach.

We recall
that a sequence

\sup_{n}|I(u_{n},v_{n})|<+\infty,\ \ I^{\prime}(u_{n},v_{n})\to 0\ \ \text{in}\ \ H^{-1},

where

is the dual space of
H
.

Before stating the global compactness result, we first introduce a Brézis-Lieb type lemma for nonlocal terms, which is proved in
[

]

Proof of Theorem
1.1
In this part, we always assume that

, where

We claim that the functional
I|_{\mathcal{N}}
has a critical level in

. We argue by contradiction and suppose that it is incorrect. From Corollary

constrained on
\mathcal{N}

. Then in virtue of the deformation lemma (

), we can prove that there exist a constant

such that
c^{**}-\sigma_{1}>c_{\infty}
,
\tilde{\mathcal{K}}+\sigma_{1}<\bar{c}
and a continuous function

\eta:[0,1]\times I^{\tilde{\mathcal{K}}+\sigma_{1}}\to I^{\tilde{\mathcal{K}}+\sigma_{1}}

such that

\displaystyle\eta\big(0,(u,v)\big)

\displaystyle=(u,v)\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \forall(u,v)\in I^{\tilde{\mathcal{K}}+\sigma_{1}};

\displaystyle\eta\big(s,(u,v)\big)

\displaystyle=(u,v)\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \forall(u,v)\in I^{c^{**}-\sigma_{1}},\,\,\forall s\in[0,1];

\displaystyle I\circ\eta\big(s,(u,v)\big)

\displaystyle\leq I(u,v)\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \forall(u,v)\in I^{\tilde{\mathcal{K}}+\sigma_{1}},\,\,\forall s\in[0,1];

(5.21)

\displaystyle\eta(1,I^{\tilde{\mathcal{K}}+\sigma_{1}})

\displaystyle\subset I^{c^{**}-\sigma_{1}}.

(5.22)

From (

) and (5.22), we observe that

(\delta,y)\in\partial\mathscr{H}\Longrightarrow I\circ\widetilde{\vartheta}_{\delta,y}\leq\tilde{\mathcal{K}}\Longrightarrow I\circ\eta(1,\widetilde{\vartheta}_{\delta,y})\leq c^{**}-\sigma_{1}.

(5.23)

, we define

\Gamma(\delta,y,s)=\begin{cases}\mathcal{T}(\delta,y,2s),\ \ &s\in[0,\frac{1}{2}];\\

Moreover, by (

) and (

Furthermore, by (

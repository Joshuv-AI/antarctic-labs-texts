Optimal Insurance to Minimize the Probability of Ruin: Inverse Survival Function Formulation
Bahman Angoshtari, Virginia R. Young

Abstract
We find the optimal indemnity to minimize the probability of ruin when premium is calculated according to the distortion premium principle with a proportional risk load, and admissible indemnities are such that both the indemnity and retention are non-decreasing functions of the underlying loss. We reformulate the problem with the inverse survival function as the control variable and show that deductible insurance with maximum limit is optimal. Our main contribution is in solving this problem via the inverse survival function. Keywords : Optimal insurance, probability of ruin, distortion premium principle, deductible insurance, maximum limit. JEL Codes : C61, G22.

He assumes that the premium is fixed so does not find the optimal amount to spend on insurance. Kaluszka
[
10
]

10

Optimal insurance is a modified DIML policy. (3) He maximizes the expected dividend payment when the insurance premium is computed according to the expected-value principle. Optimal insurance is truncated full coverage. (4) He maximizes a Kahneman-Tversky value function subject to a premium constraint based on the expected-value principle. Model set up and ruin-minimization problem

Consider an individual with initial capital
w>0
who is subject to a loss represented by a non-negative random variable
X
and who seeks to minimize her probability of ruin by buying insurance.

For a random variable
Y
, let
S_{Y}
denote the
survival function
of
Y
, that is,

S_{Y}(x)=\mathbb{P}\thinspace(Y>x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

(2.1)

and define the corresponding
inverse survival function
, or
quantile function
, by

S^{-1}_{Y}(p)=\inf\{x\geq 0:S_{Y}(x)\leq p\},\quad p\in[0,1].

(2.2)

Note that
S^{-1}_{Y}
is non-increasing and right-continuous, as is
S_{Y}
.

Let
M
denote the essential supremum of
X
, which might equal
\infty
; specifically,

M=\inf\{x\geq 0:S_{X}(x)=0\}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\cup\{\infty\}.

(2.3)

We assume throughout the paper that
S_{X}
is continuously differentiable and strictly decreasing on
[0,M]
, and that
S_{X}(M):=\lim_{x\to M^{-}}S_{X}(x)=0
. Note that
X
’s quantile function satisfies

S_{X}\big(S_{X}^{-1}(p)\big)=p,\quad\text{and}\quad S_{X}^{-1}\big(S_{X}(x)\big)=x,

(2.4)

for all
p\in[0,S_{X}(0)]
and
x\in[0,M]
.

Remark 2.1
.

X
can have an atom at
0
, in which case,
S_{X}(0):=\lim_{x\to 0^{+}}S_{X}(x)<1
. Furthermore,
X
can be unbounded from above, in which case
M=\infty
. With a slight abuse of notation, the interval
[a,M]
and the function evaluation
f(M)
are interpreted as
[a,\infty)
and
\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)
, respectively, when
M=\infty
. ∎

The individual buys insurance to mitigate her loss
X
. We represent the insurance contract by an
indemnity

I
such that the insurer is responsible for the amount
I(x)
if the policyholder’s loss
X=x
. We also define the
retention

R
by
R(x)=x-I(x)
; thus, when the loss
X=x
,
R(x)
is the amount of loss for which the policyholder is responsible. We impose the following conditions on the policyholder’s retention (and indemnity).

Definition 2.2
.

A function
R:[0,M]\to[0,M]
is an
admissible retention
if

(i)

0\leq R(x)\leq x
for all
x\in[0,M];

(ii)

R
is non-decreasing on
[0,M];
and,

(iii)

I(x)=x-R(x)
,
x\in[0,M]
, is also non-decreasing.

We denote the set of all admissible retentions by
\mathcal{A}
. ∎

Condition (i) states that neither the policyholder nor the insurer will be responsible for more than the total loss
X
. Indeed, if
R(x)<0
for some
x\geq 0
, then the individual would be motivated to create or report loss
X=x
because she would gain
I(x)-x>0
from the loss. No insurance company would issue a policy that allowed a policyholder to gain from an insurable loss. Also, if
R(x)>x
for some
x\geq 0
, then the individual would not buy such a policy because, in addition to suffering a loss
X=x
, the individual would have to pay the insurance company
R(x)-x>0
.

Conditions (ii) and (iii) reduce the risk of moral hazard. Indeed, if the retention were decreasing, then the policyholder would have an incentive to create more loss in order to reduce her retention. Similarly, if the indemnity were decreasing, then the policyholder would have an incentive to report a smaller loss to the insurer. Note that a retention
R
belongs to
\mathcal{A}
if and only if the indemnity
I
also belongs to
\mathcal{A}
.

For any
R\in\mathcal{A}
, we introduce the following notation. With a slight abuse of notation, we define
S_{R}:=S_{R(X)}
, that is,
S_{R}
is the survival function of
R(X)
. We also denote the quantile function of
R(X)
by
S^{-1}_{R}
. Similarly, we write
S_{I}
for
S_{I(X)}
and
S^{-1}_{I}
for the corresponding quantile function.

and

Note that if

We now formulate the optimal insurance problem from the perspective of the policyholder, who seeks to minimize her probability of ruin. For a given retention

\inf_{R\in\mathcal{A}}S_{R}\Big(w-\pi_{X}+(1+\theta)\int_{0}^{S_{X}(0)}S_{R}^{-1}(p)\,dg(p)\Big),

Remark 2.4
.

There is a duality between the ruin-minimization problem we consider and minimizing value-at-risk, or

VaR_{\alpha}(R(X)+\pi_{I})=\inf\{w\geq 0:\mathbb{P}\thinspace(R(X)+\pi_{I}>w)\leq\alpha\}.

(2.15)

Indeed, if the minimum probability of ruin in (

Reformulating (

) via the retention quantile function

In problem (
2.14
), the retention
R
appears indirectly through the survival function
S_{R}
and its right-continuous inverse
S^{-1}_{R}
. It will be useful to reformulate (
2.14
) with
S_{R}^{-1}
as the control variable. To this end, we introduce the set of admissible retention quantile functions.

Definition 3.1
.

A function
S_{R}^{-1}:[0,1]\to[0,M]
is called an
admissible retention quantile function
if

(i)

S_{R}^{-1}
is non-increasing and right-continuous;

(ii)

S_{R}^{-1}\leq S_{X}^{-1};
and,

(iii)

S_{I}^{-1}=S_{X}^{-1}-S_{R}^{-1}
is also non-increasing and right-continuous.

The set of all admissible retention quantile functions is denoted by
\mathcal{S}^{-1}
. ∎

Remark 3.2
.

Any admissible retention quantile function
S_{R}^{-1}\in\mathcal{S}^{-1}
must be
continuous
on
[0,S_{X}(0)]
, not just
right-continuous
. Continuity follows from
(iii)
because any
(
downward
)
jump in the closed interval would make the indemnity quantile function
S_{I}^{-1}=S_{X}^{-1}-S_{R}^{-1}
have an upward jump. This continuity is a reason to work with quantile functions instead of survival functions
(
which potentially have jumps
)
. ∎

Remark 3.3
.

Note that
S_{R}^{-1}
belongs to
\mathcal{S}^{-1}
if and only if
S_{I}^{-1}
belongs to
\mathcal{S}^{-1}
. ∎

Next, we establish a one-to-one correspondence between admissible retention functions in
\mathcal{A}
and admissible retention quantile functions in

Lemma 3.4
.

For any
R\in\mathcal{A}
,
S_{R}^{-1}
given by (
2.2
) belongs to
\mathcal{S}^{-1}
. Conversely, for any
S_{R}^{-1}\in\mathcal{S}^{-1}
, define

Then,

, and

Proof.

To show the first statement, we check that
S^{-1}_{R}
satisfies conditions
(i)
-
(iii)
of Definition
3.1
for a given

.
S^{-1}_{R}
is non-increasing and right-continuous by its definition in (

). Also,
S_{R}(x)\geq S_{X}(x)

. Thus, by (

on

. It only remains to check condition

. From condition

, we know that

is non-decreasing with respect to

; thus,

and

are comonotonic, which implies that

. It follows that, if we define
S^{-1}_{I}
by
S^{-1}_{X}-S^{-1}_{R}
, then

equals the inverse survival function of

and is, hence, non-decreasing and right-continuous.

To show the second statement, we check that
R
satisfies conditions
(i)
-
(iii)
of Definition

for a given
S_{R}^{-1}\in\mathcal{S}^{-1}
. Note that

defined by (

) is non-decreasing because both

and

are non-increasing. Next, because

and because

maps

, it follows that

, for all

. Also,
R(X)=S^{-1}_{R}(S_{X}(X))\sim S^{-1}_{R}(U)
, because

, in which

\inf\left\{x\geq 0:\mathbb{P}\thinspace\left(S^{-1}_{R}(U)>x\right)\leq p\right\}=S^{-1}_{R}(p).

(3.2)

It only remains to show that

satisfies condition (iii) of Definition
2.2
. To that end, define
S_{I}^{-1}(p):=S_{X}^{-1}(p)-S_{R}^{-1}(p)

, and observe that

Theorem

\theta\leq\theta_{s}
, then the policyholder, regardless of the amount of her wealth, should spend all of her initial wealth to buy a DIML contract with a zero deductible and a maximum limit less than

However, if the insurance is expensive (that is,
\theta>\theta_{s}

and

M

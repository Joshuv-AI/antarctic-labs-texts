Abstract
. In a recent work, Duong, Ghoul and Zaag determined the gradient profile for blowup solutions of standard semilinear heat equation with power nonlinearities in the (supposed to be) generic case. Their method refines the constructive techniques introduced by Bricmont and Kupiainen and further developed by Merle and Zaag. In this paper, we extend their refinement to the problem about the reconnection of vortex lines with the boundary in a type-II superconductor under planar approximation, a physical model derived by Chapman, Hunton and Ockendon featuring the finite time quenching for the nonlinear heat equation ∂ h ∂ t = ∂ 2 h ∂ x 2 + e − h − 1 h β , β > 0 \frac{\partial h}{\partial t}=\frac{\partial^{2}h}{\partial x^{2}}+e^{-h}-\frac{1}{h^{\beta}},\quad\beta>0 subject to initial boundary value conditions h ⁡ ( ⋅ , 0 ) = h 0 > 0 , h ⁡ ( ± 1 , t ) = 1 . h(\cdot,0)=h_{0}>0,\quad h(\pm 1,t)=1. We derive the intermediate extinction profile with refined asymptotics, and with extinction time T T and extinction point 0 0 , the gradient profile behaves as x → 0 x\rightarrow 0 like lim t → T ( ∇ h ) ​ ( x , t ) ∼ 1 2 ​ β ​ x | x | ​ 1 | log ⁡ | x | | ​ [ ( β + 1 ) 2 8 ​ β ​ | x | 2 | log ⁡ | x | | ] 1 β + 1 − 1 2 , \lim_{t\rightarrow T}\,(\nabla h)(x,t)\quad\sim\quad\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\beta}}\frac{x}{|x|}\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\log|x||}}\left[\frac{(\beta+1)^{2}}{8\beta}\frac{|x|^{2}}{|\log|x||}\right]^{\frac{1}{\beta+1}-\frac{1}{2}}, agreeing with the gradient of the extinction profile previously derived by Merle and Zaag. Definition 1.1

(Intermediate profile)
.

Introduce the intermediate profile

\widehat{\Phi}(z)=\left(\beta+1+\frac{(\beta+1)^{2}}{4\beta}|z|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta+1}},\quad z\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

(1.14)

with the intermediate gradient profile being

\nabla\widehat{\Phi}(z)=\frac{\beta+1}{2\beta}z\left(\beta+1+\frac{(\beta+1)^{2}}{4\beta}|z|^{2}\right)^{-\frac{\beta}{\beta+1}},\quad z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

(1.15)

Here

1.12
)-(

Now we prescribe the final extinction profile (near extinction point) that is expected in our construction.
For later purpose we also prescribe its global behaviour.

Definition 1.2

(Final profile)
.

Let
x_{0}\in\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Define
H^{*}_{x_{0}}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega\backslash\{x_{0}\})

(i) If
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}
, then

H^{*}_{x_{0}}(x)=H^{*}(x-x_{0}),

(1.16)

where
H^{*}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\{0\})
is defined as follows:

H^{*}(x)=\left[\frac{(\beta+1)^{2}}{8\beta}\frac{|x|^{2}}{|\log|x||}\right]^{\frac{1}{\beta+1}},\quad 0<|x|\leq\min\left\{C(a_{1},\beta),\frac{1}{2}\right\},

(1.17)

H^{*}(x)=a_{1}|x|,\quad|x|\geq 1,

(1.18)

with

1.4

\forall\,\,x\neq 0,\quad H^{*}(x)>0\quad\text{and}\quad|\nabla H^{*}(x)|>0.

(ii) If
\Omega

H^{*}_{x_{0}}(x)=\left[\frac{(\beta+1)^{2}}{8\beta}\frac{|x-x_{0}|^{2}}{|\log|x-x_{0}||}\right]^{\frac{1}{\beta+1}},\quad 0<|x-x_{0}|\leq\min\left\{C(\beta),\frac{\varrho_{0}}{4}\right\},

(1.19)

H_{x_{0}}^{*}(x)=1,\quad|x-x_{0}|\geq\frac{\varrho_{0}}{2},

with the additional requirements that

\forall\,\,x\neq x_{0},\quad H_{x_{0}}^{*}(x)>0\quad\text{and}\quad|\nabla H_{x_{0}}^{*}(x)|>0.

Since our analysis will be local, we shall not precise

Thus, the improved error estimate we get in (

Remark 1.11

(Stability of the gradient profile)
.

We use the method which was first introduced by Bressan in
[
Bre90
,
Bre92
]
for the heat equation with an exponential source,
then for (
1.11
) in Bricmont and Kupiainen
[
BK94
]
and Merle and Zaag
[
MZ97b
]

(also Fermanian, Merle and Zaag
[
FKMZ00
]
).
This consists in a formal approach where the profile is obtained through an inner/outer expansion with matching asymptotics,
followed by a rigorous proof where the PDE is linearized around the profile candidate.
Then, the negative part of the spectrum (which is infinite dimensional) is controlled
thanks to the decaying properties of the Laplacian,
whereas the nonnegative part (which is finite dimensional) is controlled thanks to the degree theory.
Via this finite dimensional reduction and the interpretation of the parameters of the finite-dimensional problem
in terms of the blowup time and the blowup point,
the refined estimates (
1.24
)-(
1.25
) and the final profiles (
1.26
)-(
1.27
)
are stable under perturbations (in the same form) of initial data.

2.
Reconnection of vortex lines with the boundary

Before going further we explain the motivation for studying this quenching problem.
Indeed, the nonlinear equation (
1.1
) appears in various physical contexts (combustion and population genetics for example).
For
N=1
and
\beta=1
,
it is also the natural lab model employed by Chapman, Hunton and Ockendon to describe the magnetic field in a type-II superconductor in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
,
which develops a particular type of line singularity called
vortex
,
see
[
CHO98
, Section 3.2]
.
In general, a vortex is not situated in a plane,
but under some reasonable physical conditions,
the planar approximation is relevant.
In this case, a
vortex line
at time
t\geq 0
can be viewed as

L(t)=\{(x,y,z)=(x,0,h(x,t)):x\in\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}\}.

The physical derivation (see
[
CHO98
, (25)]
) gives the following vortex line dynamics

\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{\partial^{2}h}{\partial x^{2}}+H_{0}e^{-h}-\frac{1}{4\pi}K_{1}(h),

(2.1)

where
(H_{0},0,0)
is the applied magnetic field assumed to be constant,
and the smooth nonlinear function
K_{1}
satisfies the following asymptotics

K_{1}(h)\sim\frac{1}{h}\,\,\text{as}\,\,h\rightarrow 0\quad\text{and}\quad K_{1}(h)\sim 2e^{-2h}\,\,\text{as}\,\,h\rightarrow\infty.

Thus, the boundary term
H_{0}e^{-h}
dominates for
h
large, while the image term
\frac{1}{4\pi}K_{1}(h)
dominates for
h
small.
Moreover,
H_{0}e^{-h}-\frac{1}{4\pi}K_{1}(h)
in (
2.1
) multiplied by the normalization constant
4\pi
satisfies (
1.2
) for
\beta=1
.
Quenching of a solution
h
to (
2.1
) is then the
vortex reconnection
with the boundary, namely, the
z=0
plane in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
.

Finally, we remark that the boundary growth (
1.3
) (hence (
1.9
) for (
1.6
)) is no longer relevant if we consider solutions
h
small.
The physical model becomes

\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{\partial^{2}h}{\partial x^{2}}-\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{1}{h}.

See
[
CHO98
, Section 3.2.2]
for details.

(\clubsuit 1)
Linear operator
{\mathcal{L}}
.
Consider the Hilbert space with Gaussian measure

L^{2}_{\rho}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\rho dy),\quad\text{where\,\,}\rho(y)=\frac{e^{-\frac{|y|^{2}}{4}}}{(4\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}},

with the inner product

(f,g)_{\rho}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(y)g(y)\rho(y)dy.

The adjoint operator
(\partial_{i})^{*}
, with respect to
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\rho}
, of
\partial_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}
is
\frac{y_{i}}{2}-\partial_{i}
, hence

{\mathbb{L}}=\Delta-\frac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla=-\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\partial_{i})^{*}\partial_{i}

is nonpositive, self-adjoint and generates the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroup
\{e^{\theta{\mathbb{L}}}\}_{\theta\geq 0}
:

e^{\theta{\mathbb{L}}}r(y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{\theta{\mathbb{L}}}(y,x)r(x)dx,

e^{\theta{\mathbb{L}}}(y,x)=\frac{1}{(4\pi(1-e^{-\theta}))^{\frac{N}{2}}}\exp\left[-\frac{|ye^{-\frac{\theta}{2}}-x|^{2}}{4(1-e^{-\theta})}\right].

Similarly, the operator
{\mathcal{L}}=\Delta-\frac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla+1
that appears in (
3.9
) is also self-adjoint in
L^{2}_{\rho}
.
Moreover, it has explicit spectrum as follows

\text{Spec}({\mathcal{L}})=\left\{\lambda_{m}=1-\frac{m}{2}\bigg|m\in{\mathbb{N}}\right\}.

(3.12)

Corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda_{m}
, we have the eigenspace
\mathcal{E}_{m}
given by

\mathcal{E}_{m}=\text{Span}\left\{h_{m_{1}}(y_{1})h_{m_{2}}(y_{2})\cdots h_{m_{N}}(y_{N})|m_{1}+m_{2}+\cdots+m_{N}=m\right\},

(3.13)

where
h_{\ell}
is the (rescaled) Hermite polynomial in one dimension, defined by

h_{\ell}(\xi)=\sum_{j=0}^{[\ell/2]}(-1)^{j}\frac{\ell!}{j!(\ell-2j)!}\xi^{\ell-2j},

and the first three such polynomials are
1
,
\xi
and
\xi^{2}-2
.

3.2.
Localization away from the singularity and the boundary

In order to derive the final extinction profile and the final gradient profile,
following
[

]
and
[
MZ97a
]
we shall localize the study of (

) away from the extinction point.

Second rescaling transformation
. To the aim just noted above, we introduce

k_{x}(\xi,\tau)=\frac{h\left(x+\sqrt{T-t(x)}\xi,t(x)+(T-t(x))\tau\right)}{(T-t(x))^{\frac{1}{\beta+1}}},

(3.28)

where
x\in\Omega\backslash\{0\}
and
t(x)<T
is determined by the quasi-parabola

\frac{K_{0}}{4}\sqrt{(T-t(x))|\log(T-t(x))|}=|x|,

(3.29)

which is exactly the relation giving the inner boundary of the region (
3.25
).
For the sake of convenience, we denote the radial function (abusing a little bit the notation)

\theta(x)=T-t(x)=\theta(|x|),

(3.30)

hence

t(x)\rightarrow T,\quad\theta(x)\rightarrow 0\quad\text{and}\quad|\log\theta(x)|\rightarrow+\infty,\quad\text{as\,\,}x\rightarrow 0.

Moreover, for
t(x)\geq 0
, by a simple calculation
T<e^{-1}
implies that
|x|
is bounded,
in this sense our localization is also away from the boundary of
\Omega
.

It is an immediate consequence of (
1.1
) and (
3.30
) that
k=k_{x}
satisfies the equation

\frac{\partial k}{\partial\tau}=\Delta_{\xi}k-\theta(x)^{\frac{\beta}{\beta+1}}F\left(\theta(x)^{\frac{1}{\beta+1}}k\right),

(3.31)

where

N_{\beta}(k):=\theta(x)^{\frac{\beta}{\beta+1}}F\left(\theta(x)^{\frac{1}{\beta+1}}k\right)\sim\frac{1}{k^{\beta}}\quad\text{as}\quad\theta(x)^{\frac{1}{\beta+1}}k\rightarrow 0.

We shall show that
k(\xi,\tau)
behaves, when

|\xi|\leq\alpha_{0}\sqrt{|\log(T-t(x))|}\quad\text{and}\quad\tau\in\left[\frac{t_{0}-t(x)}{T-t(x)},1\right)

for some
0\leq t_{0}<T
and

, like the function

\widehat{k}(\tau)=\left((\beta+1)(1-\tau)+\frac{(\beta+1)^{2}}{4\beta}\frac{K_{0}^{2}}{16}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta+1}}.

(3.32)

Note that
\widehat{k}(\tau)
is the solution of the following ODE

\frac{d\widehat{k}}{d\tau}=-\frac{1}{\widehat{k}^{\beta}}

(3.33)

on
[0,\widehat{T})
with

\widehat{T}=\widehat{T}(\beta,K_{0})=1+\frac{(\beta+1)K_{0}^{2}}{64\beta}>1,

subject to the initial value condition

\widehat{k}(0)=\widehat{\Phi}\left(\frac{K_{0}}{4}\right).

(3.34)

Note that at
\tau=0
, this
flat
solution
\widehat{k}
has to agree with
k_{x}(\xi,0)
for
\xi=0
.
Therefore,
\widehat{k}(0)
has to further agree with
\frac{h(x,t)}{(T-t)^{\frac{1}{\beta+1}}}
for

,
that is, in the boundary of

and, more importantly, in the region

.
It is therefore natural to impose the initial value condition (

) in view of the profile approximation ansatz.

Remark 3.10
.

We give another

Analogous to (

Lemma 3.11

For fixed

Remark 3.12

In summary, we can perform the following chain of transformations

Parabolic regularity via partial trapping in the shrinking set

In this section we prove Proposition

We shall use the parabolic regularity techniques of
[

]
which is later developed in
[
TZ19
, Proposition 4.17]
.
Namely, we use the gradient regularity of the “semigroup”
e^{t{\mathcal{L}}}
involved in the standard Duhamel integral representation of (
3.9
):

q(s)=e^{(s-\underline{s}){\mathcal{L}}}q(\underline{s})+\int_{\underline{s}}^{s}e^{(s-s^{\prime}){\mathcal{L}}}\,F(s^{\prime})ds^{\prime},

F=Vq+B(q)+T(q)+R+L(q),

and we take
\underline{s}=s_{0}
if
s\leq s_{0}+1
and
\underline{s}=s-1
if
s>s_{0}+1
.
In particular, for
\theta>0
,

\|\nabla(e^{\theta{\mathcal{L}}}r)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq Ce^{\frac{\theta}{2}}\|\nabla r\|_{L^{\infty}}

and

\|\nabla(e^{\theta{\mathcal{L}}}r)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\frac{e^{\frac{\theta}{2}}}{(1-e^{-\theta})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|r\|_{L^{\infty}}.

These mapping properties follow from the integral representation

e^{\theta{\mathcal{L}}}r(y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{\theta{\mathcal{L}}}(y,x)r(x)dx

and Mehler’s formula

e^{\theta{\mathcal{L}}}(y,x)=e^{\theta}e^{\theta{\mathbb{L}}}(y,x)=\frac{e^{\theta}}{(4\pi(1-e^{-\theta}))^{\frac{N}{2}}}\exp\left[-\frac{|ye^{-\frac{\theta}{2}}-x|^{2}}{4(1-e^{-\theta})}\right].

Appendix C
Local well-posedness in
{\mathbf{H}}
of the quenching problem

We show in this Appendix the local well-posedness in
{\mathbf{H}}
of (
1.1
).

(1) Suppose that
\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}

and
e^{t\Delta}
is the heat semigroup associated to the Dirichlet Laplacian
\Delta=\Delta_{\Omega}
with boundary data
\equiv 1
.
Given
0<h_{0}\in{\mathbf{H}}
with
h_{0}\equiv 1
on
\partial\Omega
, we set

\varepsilon_{0}=\inf h_{0}\quad\text{and}\quad M_{0}=\|h_{0}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}.

Consider

K_{0}=K_{0}(\Omega):=\left\{{\mathbf{h}}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega):\inf{\mathbf{h}}\geq\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2},\|{\mathbf{h}}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 2CM_{0}\right\},

where
C\geq 1
is the operator norm of the (Dirichlet) heat semigroup
e^{t\Delta}
on
W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
.

First, we claim that there exists
T_{0}=T_{0}(\varepsilon_{0},M_{0})>0
such that

\phi:h\mapsto e^{t\Delta}h_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\Delta}F(h(s))ds

is bounded and Lipschitz on
L^{\infty}(0,T_{0};K_{0})
, with a Lipschitz constant strictly less than
1
.
To see this, take an arbitrary
T>0
and an arbitrary
h\in L^{\infty}(0,T;K_{0})
.
Since

\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}\leq h(s)\quad\text{and}\quad\|h(s)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 2CM_{0}

\qquad\qquad\Longrightarrow\sup_{s\in(0,T)}\|F(h(s))\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\leq M

for some
M=M(\varepsilon_{0},M_{0},\beta,C)
where
\beta
is given in (
1.2
), we get

\left\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\Delta}F(h(s))ds\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\leq CTM.

Meanwhile, we have

\varepsilon_{0}\leq e^{t\Delta}h_{0}\quad\text{and}\quad\|e^{t\Delta}h_{0}\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\leq C\|h_{0}\|_{W^{1,\infty}}=CM_{0}.

Thus

T\leq\frac{1}{CM}\min\left\{\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2},M_{0}\right\}\Longrightarrow\phi(h)(t)\in K_{0}.

Now take
h_{1},h_{2}\in K_{0}
. Since

\|F(h_{1}(s))-F(h_{2}(s))\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\leq\gamma\|h_{1}(s)-h_{2}(s)\|_{W^{1,\infty}}

for some
\gamma=\gamma(\varepsilon_{0},M_{0},\beta,C)
, we have

\|\phi(h_{1})(t)-\phi(h_{2})(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\leq T\gamma\sup_{s\in(0,t)}\|h_{1}(s)-h_{2}(s)\|_{W^{1,\infty}}.

Choose
T_{0}
so that
CT_{0}M\leq\min(\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2},M_{0})
and
T_{0}\gamma\leq\frac{1}{2}
proves the Lipschitz property.

By the claim just proved, for any
h_{0}\in{\mathbf{H}}
there exists a unique solution
h
with trajectory in
K_{0}
,
with maximal existence time denoted by
T
.
Moreover, either
T=\infty
(the solution is global), or
T<\infty
, the solution escapes from
L^{\infty}(0,T;K_{0})
in the sense

\text{either}\quad\lim_{t\rightarrow T}\|h(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}}=\infty,\quad\text{or}\quad\lim_{t\rightarrow T}\|1/h(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}=\infty.

By the arguments above, only the second scenario is allowed, i.e.,
\lim_{t\rightarrow T}\inf h(t)=0
.

(2) Suppose that
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
x_{0}\in\Omega
.
Consider

\widetilde{h}=h-\psi\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{h}_{0}=h_{0}-\psi,

where
\psi=\psi_{x_{0}}
is given in Definition
1.6
for
{\mathbf{H}}={\mathbf{H}}_{\psi_{x_{0}}}
.
Note that

h_{0}\in{\mathbf{H}}\Longrightarrow\widetilde{h}_{0}\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

h(s)\in{\mathbf{H}}\Longrightarrow\frac{1}{\widetilde{h}(s)+\psi}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Moreover, (
1.1
) is equivalent to

\frac{\partial\widetilde{h}}{\partial t}=\Delta\widetilde{h}-F(\widetilde{h}+\psi)+\Delta\psi=:\Delta\widetilde{h}-\widetilde{F}(\widetilde{h}).

Here
\Delta\psi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
We then apply similar arguments as above to the new smooth nonlinear function
\widetilde{F}
to solve
\widetilde{h}
locally in
K_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
We conclude that the solution
h=\widetilde{h}+\psi
solved this way is either global, or for some
T<\infty
,
\lim_{t\rightarrow T}\inf h(t)=0
.

Combing the two cases, we proved the local well-posedness of (
1.1
) in
{\mathbf{H}}
.

Remark C.1
.

In the first case of bounded domains, the local well-posedness arguments extend to other types of boundary conditions.

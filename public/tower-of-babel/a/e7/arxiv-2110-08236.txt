Arquile Varieties - Varieties Consisting of Power Series in a Single Variable
Herwig Hauser, Sebastian Woblistin

Abstract
. Spaces of power series solutions y ⁡ ( t ) y({\rm t}) in one variable t {\rm t} of systems of polynomial, algebraic, analytic or formal equations f ⁡ ( t , y ) = 0 f({\rm t},{\rm y})=0 can be viewed as “infinite-dimensional” varieties over the ground field 𝐤 {\bf k} as well as “finite-dimensional” schemes over the power series ring 𝐤 ⁡ [ [ t ] ] {\bf k}[[{\rm t}]] . We propose to call these solution spaces arquile varieties, as an enhancement of the concept of arc spaces. It will be proven that arquile varieties admit a natural stratification 𝒴 = ⨆ 𝒴 d {\mathcal{Y}}=\bigsqcup{\mathcal{Y}}_{d} , d ∈ ℕ d\in{\mathbb{N}} , such that each stratum 𝒴 d {\mathcal{Y}}_{d} is isomorphic to a cartesian product 𝒵 d × 𝔸 𝐤 ∞ {\mathcal{Z}}_{d}\times\mathbb{A}^{\infty}_{\bf k} of a finite dimensional, possibly singular variety 𝒵 d {\mathcal{Z}}_{d} over 𝐤 {\bf k} with an affine space 𝔸 𝐤 ∞ \mathbb{A}^{\infty}_{\bf k} of infinite dimension. This shows that the singularities of the solution space of f ⁡ ( t , y ) = 0 f({\rm t},{\rm y})=0 are confined, up to the stratification, to the finite dimensional part. Our results are established simultaneously for algebraic, convergent and formal power series, as well as convergent power series with prescribed radius of convergence. The key technical tool is a linearization theorem, already used implicitly by Greenberg and Artin, showing that analytic maps between power series spaces can be essentially linearized by automorphisms of the source space. Instead of stratifying arquile varieties one may alternatively consider formal neighborhoods of their regular points and reprove with similar methods the Grinberg-Kazhdan-Drinfeld factorization theorem for arc spaces in the classical and in the more general setting.

It seems that there is no substantially smaller natural class of maps between power series spaces which contains these two types.

(Structure of arquile varieties)

admits an exact formal solution

which coincides with

up to degree

Indeed, the structure theorem reduces the approximation problem to the case of linear equations, and there the flatness of the formal power series ring over the rings of convergent and algebraic power series implies the existence of the respective power series solutions, see remark

for the details of this reasoning. This method also works for power series in several variables and provides a “geometric" proof for Artin approximation. The technicalities are, however, more involved

Ha2
]
.

Formally local geometry. There is a second, more deformation-theoretic approach to the geometry of arquile varieties: Instead of decomposing them into strata which are shown to be “globally” cartesian products, one can also study the germs or formal neighborhoods of arquile varieties
{\mathcal{Y}}
at selected arq.regular points
y
. This corresponds to work with the local rings
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{Y}},y}
of
{\mathcal{Y}}
at
y
(as defined below) and their completions
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{Y}},y}
. The respective result, first established by Grinberg-Kazhdan and Drinfeld in the special case of arc spaces, can be understood as a partial generalization of Cohen’s structure theorem to the infinite dimensional setting: Cohen’s theorem asserts that a complete local Noetherian ring
S
containing a field is isomorphic to a factor ring of a ring of formal power series in finitely many variables over this field, see Thm. 29.7 in
[
Ma
]
. And
S
is regular if and only if it is isomorphic to a ring of formal power series in finitely many variables. In the infinite dimensional context, the statement is more complicated, see Theorem
1.6
below, and only provides a description of the rings
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{Y}},y}
for arq.regular points
y
of
{\mathcal{Y}}
. A different formulation in terms of deformations is given in Theorem
10.1
.

We restrict in the statement below to the case of formal power series. An analogous statement holds for convergent power series, considering
{\bf k_{\circ}}\{{\rm t}\}
as the inductive limit of the Banach spaces
{\bf k_{\circ}}\{{\rm t}\}_{s}

and

Let

Theorem 1.6

(Grinberg-Kazhdan-Drinfeld factorization theorem)
.

Let

.

In the paper

Outline of the proof of the structure theorem : We briefly describe how the stratification of the variety
{\mathcal{Y}}
and the factorizations of the strata
{\mathcal{Y}}_{jd}
in Theorem
1.3
are obtained.
13
13

13

The term stratification is used loosely here: the textile closures of strata are in general not finite unions of strata, but just countable unions.

Instead of proving directly that the strata
{\mathcal{Y}}_{jd}
are isomorphic to cartesian products
{\mathcal{Z}}_{jd}\times\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{s}
we rather work with the defining system of equations
f({\rm t},{\rm y}({\rm t}))=0
. We will show that such a system of equations can be linearized locally after a suitable stratification of the ambient space
\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
by a suitable textile isomorphism – but this is only possible up to a finite dimensional part. From this it will then be relatively easy to deduce that the zeroset
{\mathcal{Y}}
has the required cartesian product structure on each stratum. Let us give some more details of how the linearization works.

Define, for
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and a suitably chosen minor
g
of the relative Jacobian matrix
\partial_{\rm y}f=(\partial_{{\rm y}_{i}}f_{j})
of
f
with respect to
{\rm y}
, subsets
{\mathcal{S}}_{d}={\mathcal{S}}_{d}(g)
of
\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
as the sets of vectors
y\in\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
for which
g
has non-zero evaluation
g(y)
of order
d
as a power series in
{\rm t}
. The sets
{\mathcal{S}}_{d}
are textile cofinite and textile locally closed in
\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
in the sense that the condition
{\rm ord}\,g(y)=d
carries only on the (finitely many) coefficients
y_{ij}
,
1\leq i\leq m
,
j\leq d
, of vectors
y\in\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
. It is not difficult to see that the order condition is given by (finitely many) polynomial equalities
=
and inequalities
\neq
on these coefficients.
14
14

14

Sets given by such order conditions are also called definable in the literature.

On each stratum
{\mathcal{S}}_{d}
, the vectors
y\in{\mathcal{S}}_{d}
will be decomposed uniquely by Weierstrass division into
y=z+v
where
z
is a polynomial vector of degree
\leq d
in
{\rm t}
and
v
is a power series vector whose components have all order
>d
in
{\rm t}
. As
y
varies in the stratum
{\mathcal{S}}_{d}
, the first summand
z
will vary in a finite dimensional space. This type of decomposition is a standard technique in Artin approximation and in various results on arc spaces. It will allow us to show that
{f_{\infty}}(y)={f_{\infty}}(z+v)
can be linearized with respect to
v
by an isomorphism
\chi_{d}
of
{\mathcal{S}}_{d}
. The precise statement is as follows (see Theorem
5.4
for more details).

Theorem 1.8

(Linearization of arquile maps)
.

Let be given an arquile map
{f_{\infty}}:\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}^{k},\,y({\rm t})\rightarrow f({\rm t},y({\rm t}))
induced by the substitution of the
{\rm y}
-variables by a vector
y({\rm t})
of power series in a given power series vector
f\in{\mathcal{B}}^{k}
. Assume that
k\leq m
and let
g
be a
(k\times k)
-minor of the relative Jacobian matrix
\partial_{\rm y}f
of
f
. Set
{\mathcal{S}}_{d}=\{y\in\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m},\,g(y)\neq 0
,
{\rm ord}\,g(y)=d\}
.

There then exists, for each
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
, a textile isomorphism
\chi_{d}:{\mathcal{Z}}_{d}\times{\mathcal{V}}_{d}\,\,\smash{\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{\cong}}\,\,{\mathcal{S}}_{d}
over
{\mathcal{Z}}_{d}
from the cartesian product of the quasi-affine algebraic variety
{\mathcal{Z}}_{d}=\{z\in{\bf k_{\circ}}[{\rm t}]_{\leq d}^{m},\,{\rm ord}\,g(z)=d\}
with the
\mathcal{A}_{\circ}
-module
{\mathcal{V}}_{d}=\langle{\rm t}\rangle^{d}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
such that the composition

{f_{\infty}}\circ\chi_{d}:{\mathcal{Z}}_{d}\times{\mathcal{V}}_{d}\,\,\smash{\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{\cong}}\,\,{\mathcal{S}}_{d}\,\,\smash{\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{f_{\infty}}}\,\,{\mathcal{A}}^{k}

is linear in the second component and of the form

({f_{\infty}}\circ\chi_{d})(z,v)=f(z)+\partial_{\rm y}f(z)\cdot v.

This statement can be rephrased by saying that
{f_{\infty}}
is linearized on each stratum
{\mathcal{S}}_{d}
up to a finite dimensional summand by an isomorphism of the source space. If the minor
g
is identically zero on
\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
all sets
{\mathcal{S}}_{d}
are empty and the statement is vacuous. So the interesting case occurs when
g
is different from zero. The assumption that
g
stems from a submatrix of
\partial_{\rm y}f
of size
k
(and hence the number
k
of components of
f
is less than or equal to the number
m
of
{\rm y}
-variables) is essential for the linearization to work. A substantial part of the proof of Theorem
1.3
is dedicated to establishing this assumption on the sets of a suitable partition of
{\mathcal{Y}}(f)
.

The construction of the linearization of
{f_{\infty}}
is the main technical ingredient of the paper. It will be given explicitly by describing the isomorphism
\chi_{d}
as a composition of a
{\bf k}
-linear isomorphism given by the Weierstrass division and an invertible arquile map. Here, the polynomial vector
z
plays the role of a parameter, and
{f_{\infty}}\circ\chi_{d}
will depend in an arquile way on
z
(i.e., is a power series in
z
), whereas it is linear in
v
. The isomorphism
\chi_{d}
is textile in both
v
and
z
. Once the linearization of
{f_{\infty}}
with respect to
v
is achieved a quite direct argument establishes the cartesian factorization of the stratum
{\mathcal{Y}}_{d}={\mathcal{Y}}(f)\cap{\mathcal{S}}_{d}
of
{\mathcal{Y}}(f)
as described in Theorem
1.3
.

For arbitrary vectors
f
, the zeroset
{\mathcal{Y}}(f)
has first to be decomposed into the arq.regular and arq.singular locus. On the first, there exists a covering by arquile open subsets for which linearization works. On the second, one iterates the decomposition into the arq.regular and arq.singular locus and then argues by Noetherian induction to construct the required partition. Combining all strata of all subsets obtained in this way then yields the required partition
{\mathcal{Y}}=\bigcup{\mathcal{Y}}_{i}
together with the individual cartesian product factorizations as described in the structure theorem
1.3
.

Organization of paper : After a compilation of definitions in section 2, we recall the Weierstrass division theorem in section 3 and adapt the statement to our purposes. This can be skipped on first reading, as well as the subsequent section 4 on division modules. The intermediate section 8 constructs the appropriate covering and stratification of an arquile variety. In section 10 we illustrate our techniques in an explicit example. The last section 11 discusses the analytic triviality of arquile varieties, i.e., the appearance of a smooth factor in the formal neighborhood of a point. Our ground field

and of power series

Spaces of power series:
We denote by

the ring of formal, respectively convergent or algebraic power series

in a single variable

over
{\bf k}
and without constant term,
y(0)=0
.

15

15

All results hold, with the appropriate modifications, also for the ring

This is a commutative ring without one element

. For

we will consider vectors

Arquile maps:
For variables
{\rm y}=({\rm y}_{1},...,{\rm y}_{m})

Theorem 2.1

(Inverse function theorem)
.

For any vector
p({\rm t},{\rm y})\in{\mathcal{B}}^{m}
whose components are all of order at least
2
in
{\rm t},{\rm y}
, the arquile map

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\varphi:\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}&\longrightarrow&\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m},\\
y&\longrightarrow&y+p(y),\end{array}

is an arquile isomorphism.

Remark 2.2
.

In contrast to the finite dimensional setting, and due to the fact that we restrict to power series without constant term, the map
\varphi
is a global arquile isomorphism.

Proof.

Set
u={\rm y}+p({\rm t},{\rm y})\in{\mathcal{B}}^{m}
so that
\varphi=u_{\infty}
. We have

and the relative Jacobian matrix

is invertible by the assumption on

. Let

be the unique vector with

and

. Clearly

is the identity map on

.∎

16

16

In this article, we defer from considering
{\mathcal{Y}}

For
{\mathcal{Y}}
an arbitrary subset of
\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
we define

I_{{\mathcal{Y}}}=\{f\in{\mathcal{B}},\,f(y)=0\text{ for all }y\in{\mathcal{Y}}\}

{\mathcal{B}}

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
I
be a saturated proper ideal of
{\mathcal{B}}
, with irredundant prime decomposition
I=I_{1}\cap\ldots\cap I_{s}
. Then all
I_{i}
are saturated, say
{\mathcal{Y}}(I)={\mathcal{Y}}(I_{1})\cup\ldots\cup{\mathcal{Y}}(I_{s})
with
I_{i}=I_{{\mathcal{Y}}(I_{i})}

{\mathcal{Y}}(I_{i})\neq\emptyset
for all

The arquile varieties

will be called the irreducible components of
{\mathcal{Y}}
(they are the irreducible components with respect to the arquile topology). While the prime components of a saturated ideal are again saturated, the saturation of a prime ideal need not be prime, cf. the example after the proof.

Proof.

Suppose that
I_{1}
is not saturated and hence properly contained in
I_{{\mathcal{Y}}(I_{1})}
, and let
I_{1,1},\dots,I_{1,r}
be an irredundant prime decomposition of
I_{{\mathcal{Y}}(I_{1})}=\bigcap_{j=1}^{r}I_{1,j}
. Then
I\subset\bigcap_{j=1}^{r}I_{1,j}\cap I_{2}\cap\dots\cap I_{s}
. Since the subvarieties of
\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
associated to both ideals coincide and

is saturated, we obtain that

s({\rm t})\in\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m-r}
and with the property that the rank of the relative Jacobian matrix
\partial_{{\rm s}}(\widetilde{y}({\rm t},{\rm s}))
equals
m-r
.

Let
\varepsilon:{\mathcal{B}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}\{{\rm t},{\rm s}\}
,
f({\rm t},{\rm y})\rightarrow f({\rm t},\widetilde{y}({\rm t},{\rm s}))
be the evaluation map. By Gabrielov’s theorem, the rank of
\partial_{{\rm t},{\rm s}}({\rm t},\widetilde{y}({\rm t},{\rm s}))
is at most
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}({\mathcal{B}}/\ker\varepsilon)
. It equals
1+m-r
. We obtain the estimate
{\rm ht}(\ker\varepsilon)\leq r
. Now let
f\in I_{{\mathcal{Y}}(I)}
. Then
f({\rm t},\widetilde{y}({\rm t},s({\rm t})))=0
for all
{\rm s}({\rm t})\in\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m-r}
. This shows
f({\rm t},\widetilde{y}({\rm t},{\rm s}))=0
, and hence
I\subset I_{{\mathcal{Y}}(I)}\subset\ker\varepsilon
. Both
\ker\varepsilon
and
I
are prime, and as
{\rm ht}(\ker\varepsilon)\leq{\rm ht}(I)
, both ideals coincide; in particular
I=I_{{\mathcal{Y}}(I)}
.
∎

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
I\subset{\mathbb{C}}\{{\rm t},{\rm y}\}
be an ideal generated by convergent power series which do not depend on
{\rm t}
.

(a)
If
I
is a radical ideal, then
I
is saturated.

(b)
If
I
is prime, then
{\mathcal{Y}}(I)
is irreducible.

Proof.

Set
J=I\cap{\mathbb{C}}\{{\rm y}\}
and let
J=J_{1}\,\cap\dots\cap\,J_{s}
be an irreducible prime decomposition. Denote by
X_{i}
the irreducible analytic space germ defined by
J_{i}
. Because of the curve selection lemma
[
Mi
]
, we can choose for every component
X_{i}
an arc
y^{i}({\rm t})\in\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
which does not lie completely in the singular locus of
X_{i}
. Hence
({\mathcal{B}}/J_{i}\cdot{\mathcal{B}})_{\overline{P}_{y^{i}}}
is regular, which, by the previous theorem, implies that
J_{i}\cdot{\mathcal{B}}
is saturated. It is easy to see that
I=J\cdot{\mathcal{B}}=(J_{1}\cdot{\mathcal{B}})\,\cap\dots\cap\,(J_{s}\cdot{\mathcal{B}})
. The intersection of finitely many saturated ideals is again saturated, hence
I
is saturated.∎

Corollary 2.9
.

Let
J\subset{\mathbb{C}}[{\rm y}]
be a polynomial ideal, and set
I=J\cdot{\mathbb{C}}\{{\rm t},{\rm y}\}
.

(a)
If
J
is a radical ideal, then
I
is saturated.

(b)
Let
J
be radical. Then
{\mathcal{Y}}(I)
is irreducible if and only if
J\cdot{\mathbb{C}}\{{\rm y}\}
is prime.

Proof.

For the first statement, it suffices to check, by the preceding corollary, that
J\cdot{\mathcal{B}}
is a radical ideal. But this is ensured by Chevalley’s theorem, see
[
Ru
]
, Prop. 1.3. The second statement follows from the first together with the previous theorem: If
J
is prime, then
I
is prime and the preceding corollary tells us that
{\mathcal{Y}}(I)
is irreducible. Conversely, assume that
{\mathcal{Y}}(I)
is irreducible. As
J
is radical, so is
I
, and hence
I
is saturated by (a). Then, as
{\mathcal{Y}}(I)
is irreducible,
I
and hence
J\cdot{\mathbb{C}}\{{\rm y}\}
must be prime. ∎

3.
Weierstrass division

We will give two versions of this theorem, one with coefficients in a field and one with coefficients in a complete local ring, both in the case of series in just one variable
{\rm t}

{\rm t}
, in the second case one considers deformations of a given divisor obtained by varying its coefficients by elements in the complete local ring. All results in this section are classical and hold equally for formal, convergent and algebraic power series.

Theorem 3.1

(Weierstrass division on strata of constant order)

Assume given a power series

in variables
{\rm t}
and
{\rm y}=({\rm y}_{1},...,{\rm y}_{m})

(a)
Let

be a power series vector without constant term such that

is non-zero of order
d
. For every

there exist a unique power series
a\in\mathcal{A}_{\circ}
and a unique polynomial
z\in{\bf k_{\circ}}[{\rm t}]_{\leq d}
of degree
\leq d
such that

w=g(y)\cdot a+z.

(b)
Denote by
{\mathcal{S}}_{d}\subset\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
the textile locally closed stratum of vectors
y\in\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
for which
g(y)
is non-zero of order
d
. The maps

\psi:{\mathcal{S}}_{d}\times\mathcal{A}_{\circ}\rightarrow{\bf k_{\circ}}[{\rm t}]_{\leq d}\times\langle{\rm t}\rangle^{d}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{\circ}

(y,w)\rightarrow(z,v)

and

\chi:{\mathcal{S}}_{d}\times\mathcal{A}_{\circ}\rightarrow{\bf k_{\circ}}[{\rm t}]_{\leq d}\times\mathcal{A}_{\circ}

(y,w)\rightarrow(z,a)

are textile, respectively rationally textile.

Note that letting
g

{\rm y}

g\in{\mathcal{A}}
a series in
{\rm t}
of order

\leq d-1

Note also that in assertion (b) the map

is only rationally textile as can be seen by taking

,

,

is rational in the coefficient of

We will apply the Weierstrass division in the case where the series

denotes one of the rings

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
g\in{\mathcal{B}}
be a formal power series in variables
{\rm t}
and
{\rm z}=({\rm z}_{1},...,{\rm z}_{m})
. For every choice of vectors
z
in
\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
and
v=g(z)\cdot a\in\langle g(z)\rangle\cdot\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
, the ideals of
\mathcal{A}_{\circ}
generated by
g(z)
and
g(z+v)
coincide,

\langle g(z)\rangle=\langle g(z+v)\rangle.

Remark 3.3
.

The lemma implies that, whenever
g(z)
is non-zero, the quotient
g(z)/g(z+g(z)\cdot a)
is an invertible power series in
\mathcal{A}
. It is then easy to see that, denoting by
{\mathcal{S}}\subset\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
the set of vectors
z
such that
g(z)\neq 0
, the map

{\mathcal{S}}\times\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}\rightarrow\mathcal{A},

(z,a)\rightarrow{g(z)\over g(z+g(z)\cdot a)},

is an arquile map.

Proof.

Write
v=g(z)\cdot a
for some
a\in\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
. Taylor expansion gives

g(z+v)=g(z+g(z)\cdot a)=g(z)+r(z,g(z)\cdot a)

with some power series
r
in
{\rm t}
,
{\rm z}
and new variables
{\rm w}=({\rm w}_{1},...,{\rm w}_{m})
so that
r
is at least of order
1
in
{\rm w}
. Factoring
g(z)
from the right hand side we get
g(z+v)=g(z)\cdot u(z,a)
for some series
u
in
{\rm t}
,
{\rm z}
and
{\rm w}
. The series
u({\rm z},{\rm w})
is invertible since its constant term is
1
. As
z
and
a
vanish at
{\rm t}=0
, the evaluation
u(z,a)
of

is again invertible, now as a series in
\mathcal{A}
. The required equality
\langle g(z)\rangle=\langle g(z+v)\rangle
of ideals in
\mathcal{A}_{\circ}
follows.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
g\in{\mathcal{B}}
be a power series in
{\rm t}
and
{\rm y}
. Denote by
{\mathcal{R}}_{d}\subset\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
the
{\bf k}
-vector space of polynomial vectors in
{\rm t}
of degree
\leq d
and without constant term,

{\mathcal{R}}_{d}=({\bf k_{\circ}}[{\rm t}]_{\leq d})^{m},

and by
{\mathcal{V}}_{d}=\langle{\rm t}\rangle^{d}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
the
\mathcal{A}_{\circ}
-submodule of
\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
generated in each component by
{\rm t}^{d}
. Denote furthermore by
{\mathcal{Z}}_{d}
the intersection
{\mathcal{Z}}_{d}={\mathcal{S}}_{d}\cap{\mathcal{R}}_{d}
, with
{\mathcal{S}}_{d}=\{y\in\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m},\,g(y)\neq 0,\,{\rm ord}\,g(y)=d\}
as above.

(a)
For every vector
y\in{\mathcal{S}}_{d}
there exist unique vectors
a\in\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
and
z\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{d}
such that

y=g(y)\cdot a+z

is in each component of
y
the Weierstrass division of
y_{j}
by
g(y)
.

(b)
The map

\psi_{d}:{\mathcal{S}}_{d}\rightarrow{\mathcal{Z}}_{d}\times{\mathcal{V}}_{d},

y\rightarrow(z,v),

given by the division
y=v+z=g(y)\cdot a+z
is a textile isomorphism, and

\widetilde{\psi}_{d}:{\mathcal{S}}_{d}\rightarrow{\mathcal{Z}}_{d}\times\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m},

y\rightarrow(z,a),

is a rationally textile isomorphism.

Remarks 3.5
.

(a) It is peculiar that the remainder
z\in{\mathcal{R}}_{d}
of the division of
y\in{\mathcal{S}}_{d}
by
g(y)
actually lies again in
{\mathcal{S}}_{d}
, i.e., satisfies
{\rm ord}\,g(z)=d
. This makes it possible to describe the images of
\psi_{d}
and
\widetilde{\psi}_{d}
as cartesian products: the first factor
{\mathcal{Z}}_{d}
is a quasi-affine subvariety of the finite dimensional affine space
{\mathcal{R}}_{d}
, the second factor
{\mathcal{V}}_{d}
, respectively
\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
, is a free
\mathcal{A}_{\circ}
-module of finite rank.

(b) Formulas for the coefficients of the expansions of
z
,
v
and
a
can be obtained from the coefficients of
y
by applying the usual division algorithm given by repeated substitutions analogous to the Euclidean division of polynomials.

(c) In the case
\mathcal{A}_{\circ}={\bf k_{\circ}}\{{\rm t}\}_{s}
, one has to restrict as in the preceding theorem to sufficiently small radii
s>0
, and vectors
y
of sufficiently small
s
-norm.

Proof.

For assertion (a), the Weierstrass division theorem
3.1
ensures the existence and uniqueness of
v\in{\mathcal{V}}_{d}
and
z\in{\mathcal{R}}_{d}
. So we only have to show that
z\in{\mathcal{S}}_{d}
. But this is just the content of the preceding lemma, whence (a). Let us prove assertion (b). The maps
\psi
and
\widetilde{\psi}
are textile, respectively rationally textile maps by Theorem
3.1
. The map

{\mathcal{Z}}_{d}\times{\mathcal{V}}_{d}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}:(z,v)\rightarrow v+z

sends, again by the lemma,
{\mathcal{Z}}_{d}\times{\mathcal{V}}_{d}
into
{\mathcal{S}}_{d}
, and is therefore the inverse of
\psi
. Finally, as
z\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{d}
, the ideal of
\mathcal{A}_{\circ}
generated by
g(z)
equals
\langle{\rm t}\rangle^{d}
. We may therefore write
v\in{\mathcal{V}}_{d}
as
v=g(z)\cdot b
with a unique
b\in\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
. The map associating
b=v/g(z)
to a pair
(z,v)\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{d}\times{\mathcal{V}}_{d}
is clearly rationally textile. By Lemma
3.2
, both
g(z)
and
g(v+z)
generate the same ideal in
\mathcal{A}_{\circ}
, hence there is a unique
a\in\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
such that

. Setting

we see that

\widetilde{\psi}(y)=(z,a)
and
\widetilde{\psi}
is bijective. As we have

a={v\over g(y)}={v\over g(v+z)}={v\over g(z)}\cdot{g(z)\over g(v+z)},

the map associating
a
to a pair
(z,v)
is again rationally textile. This gives (b) and finishes the proof.
∎

(2) Division of deformations : To have an isomorphism
\psi_{d}
as in assertion (b) of the preceding theorem it was necessary to restrict the set of vectors
y
to the stratum

may jump, so that the space of remainders could not be defined uniformly. Instead of working with a stratification and the cartesian product decomposition

of the stratum

, one may also perform the division for series whose coefficients lie in a complete local ring, i.e., for deformations of given power series vectors in

Throughout the remainder of this section, only formal power series will be considered, so that
\mathcal{A}_{\circ}={\bf k_{\circ}}[[{\rm t}]]
,

. The convergent case can be treated similarly, but requires considerably more efforts, cf. Thme. 2, A.II.4, in
[

Let

be a local ring, not necessarily Noetherian, with maximal ideal

and residue field

for all

22

22

In the Noetherian case, we typically take the

{\rm t}
with coefficients in

Theorem 3.6

(Weierstrass division for deformations)
.

We restrict to the case of formal power series. For a given power series
g\in{\mathcal{B}}
denote by
w=g(y)\cdot a+z
the Weierstrass division of a series
w\in\mathcal{A}_{\circ}
by
g(y)
for some
y\in\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
for which
g(y)
is non-zero of order
d
, with
a\in\mathcal{A}_{\circ}
and
z\in{\bf k_{\circ}}[{\rm t}]_{\leq d}
. Let
S
be a complete local ring as before with maximal ideal
M_{S}
and residue field
S/M_{S}={\bf k}
embedding into

.

(a)
For every vector
\widetilde{y}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{S,\circ}^{m}
and every series
\widetilde{w}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{S,\circ}
, deformations of

and
w
respectively, there exist a unique power series
\widetilde{a}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{S,\circ}
and a unique polynomial
\widetilde{z}\in S[{\rm t}]_{\leq d}
of degree
\leq d

a
and
z
respectively, such that

\widetilde{w}=g(\widetilde{y})\cdot\widetilde{a}+\widetilde{z}.

(b)
The coefficients of
\widetilde{a}
and
\widetilde{z}
are the evaluations in the coefficients of
\widetilde{y}
and
\widetilde{w}
of formal power series.

The statement also holds for convergent series but is then more delicate to prove, see Thme. 2, A.II.4, in

Proof.

We show that the map

\alpha:({\mathcal{A}}_{S,\circ}^{m},a)\times(S[{\rm t}]_{\leq d},z)\rightarrow({\mathcal{A}}_{S,\circ},w)

(\widetilde{a},\widetilde{z})\rightarrow\widetilde{w}=g(\widetilde{y})\cdot\widetilde{a}+\widetilde{z}

is an isomorphism. Using that
{\bf k}\subset S
, we write
\widetilde{y}=y+y^{\circ}
with
y^{\circ}\in M_{S}\cdot{\mathcal{A}}_{S,\circ}^{m}
and accordingly
g(\widetilde{y})=g(y)+h(y,y^{\circ})
with

, where
{\rm y}^{\circ}=({\rm y}_{1}^{\circ},...,{\rm y}_{m}^{\circ})
denotes a new vector of variables. Observe here that

. By the assumption on
y
we can write
g(y)={\rm t}^{d}\cdot u
with a unit
u\in{\bf k}[[{\rm t}]]
.
Without loss of generality we may assume that
u=1
, say
g(y)={\rm t}^{d}
. The map

\gamma(\widetilde{a},\widetilde{z})\rightarrow[g(y)-g(\widetilde{y})]\cdot\widetilde{a}

is the multiplication of
\widetilde{a}
with
{\rm t}^{d}-g(\widetilde{y})=g(y)-g(\widetilde{y})=h(y,y^{\circ})\in M_{S}\cdot{\mathcal{A}}_{S,\circ}
. The map
\beta
given by
\beta(\widetilde{a},\widetilde{z})={\rm t}^{d}\cdot\widetilde{a}+\widetilde{z}
is clearly an isomorphism. It therefore suffices to show that

\alpha\circ\beta^{-1}={\rm Id}_{({\mathcal{A}}_{S,\circ},w)}-\gamma\circ\beta^{-1}

is an isomorphism. To see this, observe first that the map
\gamma\circ\beta^{-1}
sends the maximal ideal

of
{\mathcal{A}}_{S,\circ}
into
M_{S}^{2}\cdot{\mathcal{A}}_{S,\circ}
. Therefore the geometric series in
\gamma\circ\beta^{-1}
, say

\delta=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(\gamma\circ\beta^{-1})^{k},

induces, by the completeness of

, a well defined map
\delta:{\mathcal{A}}_{S,\circ}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}_{S,\circ}
which is the inverse to
\alpha\circ\beta^{-1}
. This proves the theorem.
∎

4.
Division modules

This section can be skipped on first reading. The proofs of the factorization and linearization theorems are based on the decomposition of a power series vector
y\in\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
into a sum
y=v+z
where
z
belongs to a finite dimensional space of polynomial vectors and where
v
is the part of
y
on which the linearization can be achieved. The decomposition is done by the componentswise Weierstrass division of
y
by a suitable divisor. This section characterizes possible choices of such divisors.

Definition 4.1
.

A division module for a vector
f=f({\rm t},{\rm y})\in{\mathcal{B}}^{k}
is a
\mathcal{B}_{\circ}
-submodule
{\mathcal{G}}
of
\mathcal{B}_{\circ}^{m}
, with
\mathcal{B}_{\circ}={\mathcal{B}}\cap{\bf k_{\circ}}[[{\rm t},{\rm y}]]
, which is a cartesian product

{\mathcal{G}}=\langle g_{1}\rangle\times\ldots\times\langle g_{m}\rangle\subset\mathcal{B}_{\circ}^{m}

of non-zero principal ideals
\langle g_{i}\rangle:=g_{i}\cdot\mathcal{B}_{\circ}
of
\mathcal{B}_{\circ}
, with
g_{i}\in{\mathcal{B}}
, satisfying the following conditions:

(i)

For all
z\in\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
and
v\in{\mathcal{G}}(z):=g_{1}(z)\cdot\mathcal{A}_{\circ}\times\ldots\times g_{m}(z)\cdot\mathcal{A}_{\circ}\subset\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
we have

{\mathcal{G}}(z)={\mathcal{G}}(z+v).

ii)

Write elements
v
of
{\mathcal{G}}(z)
as
v=\Delta_{g}(z)\cdot a
with column vector
a\in\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
and a diagonal matrix
\Delta_{g}={\rm diag}(g_{1},...,g_{m})\in{\mathcal{B}}^{m\times m}
. There exists a (formal, convergent, respectively algebraic) power series vector
p\in{\bf k}[[{\rm t},{\rm z},{\rm a}]]^{m}
in three sets of variables
{\rm t}
,
{\rm z}=({\rm z}_{1},\ldots,{\rm z}_{m})
and
{\rm a}=({\rm a}_{1},\ldots,{\rm a}_{m})
, which is at least of order
2
in
{\rm a}
and satisfies for all
z
,
a\in\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}

f(z+\Delta_{g}(z)\cdot a)=f(z)+\partial_{{\rm y}}f(z)\cdot\Delta_{g}(z)\cdot a+\partial_{{\rm y}}f(z)\cdot\Delta_{g}(z)\cdot p(z,a).

Remarks 4.2
.

(a) Condition (i) can be rewritten as an equality of ideals in
\mathcal{A}_{\circ}
, say
\langle g_{i}(z)\rangle=\langle g_{i}(z+\Delta_{g}(z)\cdot a)\rangle
, for all
z
and
a
in
\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
, and
i=1,...,m
. Phrased differently, there exists an invertible matrix
U\in{\rm Gl}_{m}({\bf k}[[{\rm t},{\rm z},{\rm a}]])
(with formal, convergent, respectively algebraic entries) such that

\Delta_{g}(z)=\Delta_{g}(z+\Delta_{g}(z)\cdot a)\cdot U(z,a)

holds for all
z
and
a
.

(b) Condition (i) appears in Lemma
3.2
, which is used in the proof of Theorem
3.4
, and in the proof of Proposition
5.2
.

(c) Condition (ii) ensures that the terms of order
\geq 2
of the Taylor expansion of
f(z+\Delta_{g}(z)\cdot a)
belong to the module generated by the linear terms
\partial_{{\rm y}}f(z)\cdot\Delta_{g}(z)\cdot a
. This module equals the
\mathcal{A}_{\circ}
-submodule
\partial_{{\rm y}}f(z)\cdot{\mathcal{G}}(z)
of
\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{k}
which is the image of
{\mathcal{G}}(z)
under the tangent map at
z
of the arquile map
{f_{\infty}}:\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{k}
induced by
f
. In the terminology of [BH], condition (ii) signifies that for each
z\in\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
, the map

\alpha_{z}:{\mathcal{G}}(z)\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{k},\,v=\Delta_{g}(z)\cdot a\rightarrow f(z+\Delta_{g}(z)\cdot a)

is a quasi-submersion, i.e., that its image is contained in the image of the tangent map. More generally speaking, it is a map of constant rank in the sense of
[
HM
]
. This is a necessary condition to be “locally linearizable”. To make this precise and to prove that with the appropriate assumptions this is also a sufficient condition, that is, that the map
\alpha_{z}
is indeed linearizable (in a specific sense), will be the subject of section
5
.

(d) Note that
\Delta_{g}(z)\cdot a
is just the vector
(g_{1}(z)\cdot a_{1},...,g_{m}(z)\cdot a_{m})\in\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
and that the series
g_{i}
may have non-zero constant term
g_{i}(0)
.

(e) The name “division module” is motivated by the later decomposition of vectors
y\in\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
into
y=\Delta_{g}(y)\cdot\widetilde{a}+z=\Delta_{g}(z)\cdot a+z
given by the Weierstrass division of the components
y_{i}
by
g_{i}(y)
, see Proposition
5.2
.

Example.
Typically, one could take for
{\mathcal{G}}
the
m
-fold cartesian product

{\mathcal{G}}=\langle g^{o_{1}}\rangle\times\ldots\times\langle g^{o_{m}}\rangle

of ideals of
\mathcal{B}_{\circ}
for some chosen non-zero series
g\in{\mathcal{B}}
and non-negative integers
o_{i}
. It is easy to see that such a
{\mathcal{G}}
satisfies condition (i) of the definition. However, condition (ii) will not hold without extra assumptions on
g
. These are specified in the next result.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
f\in{\mathcal{B}}^{k}
be given. Assume that
k\leq m
and that some
(k\times k)
-minor
g
of the relative Jacobian matrix
\partial_{\rm y}f
of
f
is not zero. For any choice of non-negative integers
o_{1},...,o_{m}
satisfying

o_{i}+o_{j}\geq o_{l}+1

for all
i,j\in\{1,\cdots,m\}
and
\ell\in\{1,...,k\}
the
\mathcal{B}_{\circ}
-module

{\mathcal{G}}=\langle g^{o_{1}}\rangle\times\ldots\times\langle g^{o_{m}}\rangle\subset\mathcal{B}_{\circ}^{m}

is a division module for
f
.

Remark 4.4
.

This proposition will be used later on in the case where
o_{i}=1
for
1\leq i\leq k
and
o_{i}=2
for
k+1\leq i\leq m
.

Proof.

For condition (i), it suffices to show, since
{\mathcal{G}}
is a cartesian product of principal ideals, that for any
z\in\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
and
v\in{\mathcal{G}}(z)
we have the equality of ideals

\langle g(z)\rangle=\langle g(z+v)\rangle

in
\mathcal{A}_{\circ}
. This equality does not use that
g
is a minor – it is the content of Lemma
3.2
.

To prove that
{\mathcal{G}}
fulfills condition (ii), let
v=\Delta_{g}(z)\cdot a\in{\mathcal{G}}(z)
with
a\in\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
. Taylor expansion gives

f(z+\Delta_{g}(z)\cdot a)=f(z)+\partial_{\rm y}f(z)\cdot\Delta_{g}(z)\cdot a+q(z,\Delta_{g}(z)\cdot a)

with some vector
q\in{\bf k}[[{\rm t},{\rm z},{\rm w}]]^{k}
which is of order at least two in
{\rm w}=({\rm w}_{1},...,{\rm w}_{m})
. We have to find a power series vector
p\in{\bf k}[[{\rm t},{\rm z},{\rm a}]]^{k}
of order at least two in
{\rm a}=({\rm a}_{1},...,{\rm a}_{m})
such that

q(z,\Delta_{g}(z)\cdot a)=\partial_{y}f(z)\cdot\Delta_{g}(z)\cdot p(z,a)

holds for all
z
and
a
in
\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
. First observe that each component of
q(z,\Delta_{g}(z)\cdot a)
belongs to the ideal of
\mathcal{A}_{\circ}
generated by the powers
g(z)^{o_{i}+o_{j}}
for
i,j\in\{1,...,m\}
. It therefore suffices to show that any vector of the form
(0,...,0,g^{o_{i}+o_{j}},0,...,0)
, with
i,j\in\{1,\cdots,m\}
, can be written as a
\mathcal{B}_{\circ}
-linear combination of the vectors
\partial_{{\rm y}_{1}}f\cdot g^{o_{1}},...,\partial_{{\rm y}_{k}}f\cdot g^{o_{k}}
.

To prove this, set
{\rm y}^{1}=({\rm y}_{1},...,{\rm y}_{k})
, so that
g
is without loss of generality the determinant of the
(k\times k)
-submatrix
\partial_{{\rm y}^{1}}f
of
\partial_{\rm y}f
. Denote by
\partial^{*}_{{\rm y}^{1}}f
the adjoint matrix of
\partial_{{\rm y}^{1}}f
. We then have
\partial_{{\rm y}^{1}}f\cdot\partial^{*}_{{\rm y}^{1}}f=g\cdot 1\!\!1_{k}
, and accordingly
g^{o}\cdot\partial_{{\rm y}^{1}}f\cdot\partial^{*}_{{\rm y}^{1}}f=g^{o+1}\cdot 1\!\!1_{k}
for any integer
o
.
Taking now for
o
the values
o_{i}+o_{j}-1
and using the assumption that
o_{i}+o_{j}\geq o_{l}+1
for all
i,j\in\{1,\cdots,m\}
and
\ell\in\{1,...,k\}
we get the claim. This establishes condition (ii).
∎

5.
Linearization theorem

This is the central section of the paper. To prepare the setting of the linearization theorem below, let
f=(f_{1},...,f_{k})\in{\mathcal{B}}^{k}
be a vector of power series which admits a division module
{\mathcal{G}}=\langle g_{1}\rangle\times\ldots\times\langle g_{m}\rangle\subset\mathcal{B}_{\circ}^{m}
. Recall that by Proposition
4.3
the existence of such a module is ensured for instance in the case where
k\leq m
and if there exists a non-zero
(k\times k)
-minor
g
of
\partial_{\rm y}f
. Up to a permutation of the variables we may assume that
g
is given by the first
k
columns of
\partial_{\rm y}f
. By Proposition
4.3
, the
\mathcal{B}_{\circ}
-module
{\mathcal{G}}=\langle g^{o_{1}}\rangle\times\ldots\times\langle g^{o_{m}}\rangle
with
o_{i}+o_{j}\geq o_{l}+1
for all
i,j\in\{1,\cdots,m\}
and
\ell\in\{1,...,k\}
is a division module for
f
.

The following constructions (until the next lemma) hold for any vector
g\in{\mathcal{B}}^{m}
. For a chosen
m
-tuple
{\bf d}=(d_{1},...,d_{m})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{m}
set

{\mathcal{S}}_{\bf d}=\{y\in\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m},\,g_{i}(y)\neq 0,\,{\rm ord}\,g_{i}(y)=d_{i}\text{ for all }i\}.

Since
{\rm t}
is just a single variable, the condition
{\rm ord}\,g_{i}(y)=d_{i}
is equivalent to saying that
g_{i}(y)
equals the monomial
{\rm t}^{d_{i}}
up to the multiplication by a unit in
\mathcal{A}
, i.e., that
\langle g_{i}(y)\rangle=\langle{\rm t}^{d_{i}}\rangle
as ideals of
\mathcal{A}_{\circ}
, for each
y\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\bf d}
. This implies that
{\mathcal{S}}_{\bf d}
is a cofinite textile locally closed subset of
\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
. It is
{\rm t}
-adically open since
{\mathcal{S}}_{\bf d}
contains with every element
y
the
{\rm t}
-adic neighborhood
y+\langle{\rm t}^{d_{1}}\rangle\times\ldots\times\langle{\rm t}^{d_{m}}\rangle
in
\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
. We agree to set
{\rm ord}\,g_{i}(y)=\infty
if
g_{i}(y)=0
and then define accordingly
{\mathcal{S}}_{\bf d}
also if some
d_{i}=\infty
. This gives a countable partition of
\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
into

\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}=\bigcup_{{\bf d}\in({\mathbb{N}}\cup\{\infty\})^{m}}{\mathcal{S}}_{\bf d}.

Restrict now to
m
-tuples
{\bf d}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{m}
and vectors
y\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\bf d}
. We set

{\mathcal{V}}_{\bf d}={\mathcal{G}}(y)=\langle{\rm t}^{d_{1}}\rangle\times\ldots\times\langle{\rm t}^{d_{m}}\rangle\subset\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}.

We consider
{\mathcal{V}}_{\bf d}
as an
\mathcal{A}_{\circ}
-submodule of
\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
. Let
{\mathcal{R}}_{\bf d}
be the direct monomial complement of
{\mathcal{V}}_{\bf d}
in
\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
consisting of vectors
z\in\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
whose
i
-th component
z_{i}
is a polynomial in
{\bf k_{\circ}}[{\rm t}]
of degree
\leq d_{i}
,

{\mathcal{R}}_{\bf d}={\bf k_{\circ}}[{\rm t}]_{\leq d_{1}}\times\ldots\times{\bf k_{\circ}}[{\rm t}]_{\leq d_{m}},

with direct sum decomposition

{\mathcal{V}}_{\bf d}\oplus{\mathcal{R}}_{\bf d}=\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}.

Accordingly, we decompose vectors
y\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\bf d}
into
y=v+z

with
v\in{\mathcal{V}}_{\bf d}
and
z\in{\mathcal{R}}_{\bf d}
. The
i
-th component
z_{i}
of
z
is the remainder of the Weierstrass division of the
i
-th component
y_{i}
of
y
with respect to
{\rm t}^{d_{i}}
, cf. Theorem
3.4
. Denote by

\pi_{\bf d}:{\mathcal{S}}_{\bf d}\rightarrow{\mathcal{R}}_{\bf d},\,y\rightarrow z,

the induced textile map. Set

{\mathcal{Z}}_{\bf d}={\mathcal{S}}_{\bf d}\cap{\mathcal{R}}_{\bf d}=\{z\in{\mathcal{R}}_{\bf d},\,g_{i}(z)\neq 0\text{ and }{\rm ord}\,g_{i}(z)=d_{i}\text{ for all }i\}.

Note that
{\mathcal{R}}_{\bf d}
is a finite dimensional
{\bf k}
-vector space of dimension
d_{1}+\ldots+d_{m}
which will be considered as affine space
{\mathcal{R}}_{\bf d}\cong\mathbb{A}_{\bf k}^{d_{1}+\ldots+d_{m}}
, and
{\mathcal{Z}}_{d}
is a Zariski locally closed subset. Both
{\mathcal{V}}_{\bf d}
and
{\mathcal{R}}_{\bf d}
only depend on
{\bf d}=(d_{1},...,d_{m})
. The sets
{\mathcal{S}}_{\bf d}
and
{\mathcal{Z}}_{\bf d}
will depend on
f
in the situation of Proposition
4.3
where the series
g_{i}=g^{o_{i}}
are chosen as powers of a minor
g
of
\partial_{\rm y}f
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
f\in{\mathcal{B}}^{k}
be a power series vector with division module
{\mathcal{G}}\subset\mathcal{B}_{\circ}^{m}
, and let
{\bf d}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{m}
. The associated projection map
\pi_{\bf d}:{\mathcal{S}}_{\bf d}\rightarrow{\mathcal{R}}_{\bf d},\,y\rightarrow z
, has image
{\mathcal{Z}}_{\bf d}
.

Said differently, if
y\in\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
satisfies
{\rm ord}\,g_{i}(y)=d_{i}
for all
i
, then its remainder
z
satisfies the same condition.

Proof.

Write
y=v+z
as above. By def.
4.1
(i) of division modules we know that
{\mathcal{G}}(z)={\mathcal{G}}(y-v)={\mathcal{G}}(y)
so that
z\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\bf d}
. This proves that the image of
\pi
is included in
{\mathcal{Z}}_{\bf d}={\mathcal{S}}_{\bf d}\cap{\mathcal{R}}_{\bf d}
. It is whole
{\mathcal{Z}}_{\bf d}
since the restriction of
\pi_{\bf d}
to
{\mathcal{Z}}_{\bf d}
is the identity map. ∎

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
f\in{\mathcal{B}}^{k}
be a power series vector with division module
{\mathcal{G}}\subset\mathcal{B}_{\circ}^{m}
, diagonal matrix
\Delta_{g}
, associated sets
{\mathcal{S}}_{\bf d}
,
{\mathcal{V}}_{\bf d}
,
{\mathcal{R}}_{\bf d}
and
{\mathcal{Z}}_{\bf d}
, and projection
\pi_{\bf d}:{\mathcal{S}}_{\bf d}\rightarrow{\mathcal{Z}}_{\bf d},\,y\rightarrow z
, as defined above, for
{\bf d}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{m}
.

(a)
The map

\psi_{\bf d}:{\mathcal{S}}_{\bf d}\rightarrow{\mathcal{Z}}_{\bf d}\times{\mathcal{V}}_{\bf d},\,y=v+z\rightarrow(z,v),

given by the division of
y
by
{\mathcal{G}}(y)
is a textile isomorphism, restricting to the identity on
{\mathcal{Z}}_{\bf d}
, with inverse

\psi_{\bf d}^{-1}:{\mathcal{Z}}_{\bf d}\times{\mathcal{V}}_{\bf d}\rightarrow{\mathcal{S}}_{\bf d},\,(z,v)\rightarrow v+z.

(b)
For
(z,v)\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{\bf d}\times{\mathcal{V}}_{\bf d}
, let
a\in\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
be the unique vector such that
v=\Delta_{g}(z)\cdot a
. Let
p\in{\bf k}[[{\rm t},{\rm z},{\rm a}]]^{m}
be a vector which is of order at least
2
in
{\rm a}
. The map

\varphi_{\bf d}:{\mathcal{Z}}_{\bf d}\times{\mathcal{V}}_{\bf d}\rightarrow{\mathcal{Z}}_{\bf d}\times{\mathcal{V}}_{\bf d},

(z,v)\rightarrow(z,\Delta_{g}(z)\cdot(a+p(z,a)))

is a rationally textile isomorphism, restricting to the identity on
{\mathcal{Z}}_{\bf d}\times 0
.

Remark 5.3
.

Geometrically speaking, assertion (a) says that the stratum
{\mathcal{S}}_{\bf d}
can be considered as a “bundle” over
{\mathcal{Z}}_{\bf d}
, with fiber
{\mathcal{V}}_{\bf d}
. This will become relevant in the linearization of arquile maps along
{\mathcal{S}}_{\bf d}
, see the proof of Theorem
5.4
below.

Proof.

For assertion (a), notice first that the map
\psi_{\bf d}
is well defined by the preceding lemma. It is clearly injective. We claim that the image of
\psi_{\bf d}
equals
{\mathcal{Z}}_{\bf d}\times{\mathcal{V}}_{\bf d}
. We have already seen that the first component of
\psi_{\bf d}
has image
{\mathcal{Z}}_{\bf d}
. It remains to show that for all vectors
w\in{\mathcal{V}}_{\bf d}
there exists a vector
y\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\bf d}
with remainder
z
such that
w=y-z
.

So let
(z,w)
be an element of
{\mathcal{Z}}_{\bf d}\times{\mathcal{V}}_{\bf d}
, and set
y=w+z\in\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
. As
z\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\bf d}
and
w\in{\mathcal{G}}(z)
condition (i) of division modules implies that
{\mathcal{G}}(z)={\mathcal{G}}(z+w)
. This implies
{\mathcal{G}}(y)={\mathcal{G}}(w+z)={\mathcal{G}}(z)={\mathcal{V}}_{\bf d}
. Therefore
y\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\bf d}
.
Finally, since
w\in{\mathcal{G}}(z)={\mathcal{V}}_{\bf d}={\mathcal{G}}(y)
, the decomposition
y=w+z
is indeed the division of
y\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\bf d}
by
{\mathcal{G}}(y)
. It follows that
\psi_{\bf d}(y)=(z,w)
, hence
\psi_{\bf d}
is onto. The inverse of
\psi_{\bf d}
is given by addition,
(z,v)\rightarrow v+z
, and is therefore a textile (and even an arquile) map. This shows that
\psi_{\bf d}
is a rationally textile isomorphism and hence also a
{\rm t}
-adic homeomorphism.

As for assertion (b) and the map
\varphi_{\bf d}
, Theorem
2.1
ensures that for every
z\in\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
the map

r_{z}:\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m},\,a\rightarrow a+p(z,a)

is an arquile isomorphism. The map
\varphi_{\bf d}:{\mathcal{Z}}_{\bf d}\times{\mathcal{V}}_{\bf d}\rightarrow{\mathcal{Z}}_{\bf d}\times{\mathcal{V}}_{\bf d}
is therefore a textile isomorphism and hence also a
{\rm t}
-adic homeomorphism.∎

Theorem 5.4

(Linearization of arquile maps)
.

Let
f\in{\mathcal{B}}^{k}
be a vector of power series in
{\rm t}
and
{\rm y}
, with induced arquile map

{f_{\infty}}:\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{k},\,y=y({\rm t})\rightarrow f(y)=f({\rm t},y({\rm t})).

Assume that
k\leq m
and that there is a
(k\times k)
-minor
g
of the relative Jacobian matrix
\partial_{\rm y}f
of
f
which is not identically zero.

There then exist an arquile open dense subset
{\mathcal{S}}
of
\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
, a partition
{\mathcal{S}}=\bigcup_{d\in{\mathbb{N}}}\,{\mathcal{S}}_{d}
into textile locally closed sets
{\mathcal{S}}_{d}
and, for each
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
, textile isomorphisms
\chi_{d}:{\mathcal{Z}}_{d}\times{\mathcal{V}}_{d}\rightarrow{\mathcal{S}}_{d}
over
{\mathcal{Z}}_{d}
with
{\mathcal{Z}}_{d}\subset\mathbb{A}_{\bf k}^{\ell}
a quasi-affine subvariety and
{\mathcal{V}}_{d}\subset\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
a free
\mathcal{A}_{\circ}
-submodule such that the composition

{f_{\infty}}\circ\chi_{d}:{\mathcal{Z}}_{d}\times{\mathcal{V}}_{d}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{k},

(z,v)\rightarrow f(z)+\partial_{\rm y}f(z)\cdot v,

is linear in the second component
v
.

In this situation, we say that arquile maps are essentially linear with respect to the chosen stratification
{\mathcal{S}}=\bigsqcup{\mathcal{S}}_{d}
of
\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
.

The statement of the theorem can be made more precise and formulated in a slightly more general setting: It suffices to suppose that
f
admits a division module
{\mathcal{G}}=\langle g_{1}\rangle\times\ldots\times\langle g_{m}\rangle\subset\mathcal{B}_{\circ}^{m}
. Let then the diagonal matrix
\Delta_{g}={\rm diag}(g_{1},...,g_{m})
and the vector
p\in{\bf k}[[{\rm t},{\rm a},z]]^{k}
be defined as in definition
4.1
. In this case, fix
{\bf d}=(d_{1},...,d_{m})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{m}
and let
{\mathcal{S}}_{\bf d}=\{y\in\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m},\,{\rm ord}\,g_{i}(y)=d_{i}\text{ for all }i\}
,
{\mathcal{V}}_{\bf d}=\langle{\rm t}^{d_{1}}\rangle\times\ldots\times\langle{\rm t}^{d_{m}}\rangle\subset\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
with direct complement
{\mathcal{R}}_{\bf d}={\bf k_{\circ}}[{\rm t}]_{\leq d_{1}}\times\ldots\times{\bf k_{\circ}}[{\rm t}]_{\leq d_{m}}
, and
{\mathcal{Z}}_{\bf d}={\mathcal{S}}_{\bf d}\cap{\mathcal{R}}_{\bf d}
be defined as in the paragraphs preceding the theorem.

Theorem 5.5

(Linearization of arquile maps, general case)
.

Consider the textile isomorphisms

\psi_{\bf d}:{\mathcal{S}}_{\bf d}\rightarrow{\mathcal{Z}}_{\bf d}\times{\mathcal{V}}_{\bf d},\,y=v+z\rightarrow(z,v)

and

\varphi_{\bf d}=(\varphi_{\bf d}^{1},\varphi_{\bf d}^{2}):{\mathcal{Z}}_{\bf d}\times{\mathcal{V}}_{\bf d}\rightarrow{\mathcal{Z}}_{\bf d}\times{\mathcal{V}}_{\bf d},\,(z,v)\rightarrow(z,\Delta_{g}(z)\cdot(a+p(z,a)))

from Proposition
5.2
, so that

\varphi_{\bf d}\circ\psi_{\bf d}:{\mathcal{S}}_{\bf d}\rightarrow{\mathcal{Z}}_{\bf d}\times{\mathcal{V}}_{\bf d},\,y=v+z=\Delta_{g}(z)\cdot a+z\rightarrow(z,\Delta_{g}(z)\cdot(a+p(z,a))).

Define the textile isomorphism
\chi_{\bf d}
as the inverse

\chi_{\bf d}=(\varphi_{\bf d}\circ\psi_{\bf d})^{-1}:{\mathcal{Z}}_{\bf d}\times{\mathcal{V}}_{\bf d}\rightarrow{\mathcal{S}}_{\bf d}.

Then the composition
{f_{\infty}}\circ\chi_{\bf d}
is linear in
v\in{\mathcal{V}}_{\bf d}
of the form

{f_{\infty}}\circ\chi_{\bf d}:{\mathcal{Z}}_{\bf d}\times{\mathcal{V}}_{\bf d}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{k},

(z,v)\rightarrow f(z)+\partial_{\rm y}f(z)\cdot v.

Remarks 5.6
.

(a) A typical instance of this situation is the case where the division module
{\mathcal{G}}
of
f
has the form
{\mathcal{G}}=\langle g^{o_{1}}\rangle\times\ldots\times\langle g^{o_{m}}\rangle\subset\mathcal{B}_{\circ}^{m}
for
g
a suitable
(k\times k)
-minor of
\partial_{\rm y}f
and integers
o_{i}
as in Proposition
4.3
. In this case, one would set
{\bf d}=(o_{1}\cdot d,...,o_{m}\cdot d)
for
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
and then define
{\mathcal{S}}_{d}={\mathcal{S}}_{\bf d}
,
{\mathcal{V}}_{d}={\mathcal{V}}_{\bf d}
accordingly as at the beginning of the section. This will allow us to see the statement of the first theorem as a special case of the second one.

(b) Notice that
{f_{\infty}}
is linearized with respect to
v
only on the strata
{\mathcal{S}}_{\bf d}
, but not on whole
\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
. Again, one should view
{\mathcal{S}}_{\bf d}
here as a bundle over
{\mathcal{Z}}_{\bf d}
with fiber
{\mathcal{V}}_{\bf d}
, the linearization taking place on the second factor
{\mathcal{V}}_{\bf d}
, and depending on the parameters
z
in
{\mathcal{Z}}_{\bf d}
.

(c) The map
\chi_{\bf d}=(\varphi_{\bf d}\circ\psi_{\bf d})^{-1}=\psi_{\bf d}^{-1}\circ\varphi_{\bf d}^{-1}:{\mathcal{Z}}_{\bf d}\times{\mathcal{V}}_{\bf d}\rightarrow{\mathcal{S}}_{\bf d}
is “almost” given by substitution: The only drawback is that for
\varphi_{\bf d}^{-1}
we first have to divide the second component
v
of an element
(z,v)
of
{\mathcal{Z}}_{\bf d}\times{\mathcal{V}}_{\bf d}
by
\Delta_{g}(z)
in order to be then able to invert
a\rightarrow a+p(z,a)
. The second map
\psi_{\bf d}^{-1}
is just the addition

\partial^{*}_{{\rm y}^{1}}f(z)\cdot f(z)+\partial^{*}_{{\rm y}^{1}}f(z)\cdot\partial_{{\rm y}}f(z)\cdot v=0.

For
z\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{d}^{*}
and
v\in{\mathcal{V}}_{d}={\mathcal{G}}(z)
, write
v=(g(z)\cdot a_{1},g(z)^{2}\cdot a_{2})
with
a_{1}\in\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{k}
and
a_{2}\in\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m-k}
. As
\partial^{*}_{{\rm y}^{1}}f\cdot\partial_{{\rm y}^{1}}f=g\cdot 1\!\!1_{k}
, the previous system can be rewritten as

\partial^{*}_{{\rm y}^{1}}f(z)\cdot f(z)+g(z)^{2}\cdot a_{1}+\partial^{*}_{{\rm y}^{1}}f(z)\cdot\partial_{{\rm y}^{2}}f(z)\cdot g(z)^{2}\cdot a_{2}=0,

say,

\partial^{*}_{{\rm y}^{1}}f(z)\cdot f(z)+g(z)^{2}\cdot[a_{1}+\partial^{*}_{{\rm y}^{1}}f(z)\cdot\partial_{{\rm y}^{2}}f(z)\cdot a_{2}]=0.

This shows that
{\mathcal{Z}}_{d}^{*}
can equally be defined as

{\mathcal{Z}}_{d}^{*}=\{z\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{d},\,\partial^{*}_{{\rm y}^{1}}f(z)\cdot f(z)\in\langle g(z)^{2}\rangle\cdot\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{k}\}.

Consider now for each
z\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{d}^{*}
the isomorphism

\lambda_{d,z}:{\mathcal{V}}_{d}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{k}\times\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m-k},

v=(g(z)\cdot a_{1},g(z)^{2}\cdot a_{2})\rightarrow(a_{1}-\partial^{*}_{{\rm y}^{1}}f(z)\cdot\partial_{{\rm y}^{2}}f(z)\cdot a_{2},a_{2}).

The preceding computations show that the fibers
{\mathcal{W}}_{d,z}
are sent by
\lambda_{d,z}
to the solutions
a=(a_{1},a_{2})\in\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{r}\times\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m-k}
of the system of equations

\partial^{*}_{{\rm y}^{1}}f(z)\cdot f(z)+g(z)\cdot g(z)\cdot a_{1}=0.

As
\partial^{*}_{{\rm y}^{1}}f(z)\cdot f(z)\in g(z)\cdot g(z)\cdot\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{k}
for
z\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{d}^{*}
, these solutions are of the form

(a_{1},a_{2})=(-g(z)^{-2}\cdot\partial^{*}_{{\rm y}^{1}}f(z)\cdot f(z),a_{2}),

with arbitrary
a_{2}\in\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m-k}
. It follows that the map

\lambda_{d}:{\mathcal{W}}_{d}\rightarrow{\mathcal{Z}}_{d}^{*}\times\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m-k},\,(z,v)\rightarrow(z,\lambda_{d,z}(v))

is a textile isomorphism over
{\mathcal{Z}}_{d}^{*}
. Composing
\lambda_{d}
with
\varphi_{d}\circ\psi_{d}:{\mathcal{S}}_{d}\rightarrow{\mathcal{Z}}_{d}\rightarrow{\mathcal{V}}_{d}
as in Proposition
5.2
gives the required isomorphism
\Phi_{d}=\lambda_{d}\circ\varphi_{d}\circ\psi_{d}
for
{\mathcal{Y}}_{d}
. This concludes the proof of the factorization theorem.
∎

Example 5
.

We illustrate the fact that the division of a deformation may produce a quotient and remainder whose coefficients are genuine power series in the elements of the maximal ideal
M_{S}
of
S
. Let
S={\bf k}[[{\rm s}_{1},{\rm s}_{2},{\rm s}_{3},{\rm s}_{4}]]
be the formal power series ring in four variables, and consider the polynomials
F={\rm s}_{1}\cdot{\rm t}^{5}+{\rm s}_{2}\cdot{\rm t}^{3}
, and
G={\rm t}^{4}-{\rm s}_{3}\cdot{\rm t}^{3}-{\rm s}_{4}\cdot{\rm t}^{5}
. Here, we treat
G
as a deformation of
{\rm t}^{4}
with parameters in the maximal ideal
M_{S}
of the ring
S
. We describe the membership condition
F\in\langle G\rangle\subset S[[{\rm t}]]
in terms of equations for the variables
{\rm s}_{i}
. The virtual Weierstrass form of
G
is the polynomial
H={\rm t}^{4}-{\rm u}\cdot{\rm t}^{3}
, for some new variable
{\rm u}
, since no smaller degree terms appear in the remainder of the division of
{\rm t}^{4}
through
G
. Here, the series
u({\rm s}_{3},{\rm s}_{4})
yielding the actual Weierstrass form
h={\rm t}^{4}-u({\rm s}_{3},{\rm s}_{4})\cdot{\rm t}^{3}
of
G
is given by the implicit equation

{\rm u}^{2}\cdot{\rm s}_{4}-{\rm u}+{\rm s}_{3}=0,

as can be seen by the polynomial division of
G
through
H
. As
u
is supposed to be a power series in
{\rm s}_{3},{\rm s}_{4}
, the unique relevant solution of this equation is

u({\rm s}_{3},{\rm s}_{4})={1\over 2{\rm s}_{4}}\cdot(1-\sqrt{1+4{\rm s}_{3}{\rm s}_{4}}).

The other solution to the quadratic equations is
{1\over 2{\rm s}_{4}}\cdot(1+\sqrt{1+4{\rm s}_{3}{\rm s}_{4}})
. Its denominator
2{\rm s}_{3}
does not cancel with a factor of the numerator and therefore this solution is not a power series in
{\rm s}_{3},{\rm s}_{4}
and can be discarded. The membership

is then equivalent to the polynomial equation

{\rm s}_{1}+{\rm s}_{2}\cdot{\rm u}^{2}=0,

which is just the (unique non-zero) coefficient of the remainder of the division of

by
H
. But as soon as we eliminate the variable
u
from this equation, by substituting it by the series
u({\rm s}_{3},{\rm s}_{4})
, the equation is no longer polynomial in
{\rm s}_{1},{\rm s}_{2},{\rm s}_{3},{\rm s}_{4}
.

11.
Triviality

In this section, we exhibit in an example why the formal neighborhood of an arc
y({\rm t})
contained in the arq.singular locus
{\rm Arq.Sing}(X_{\infty,0})
of an arc space
X_{\infty,0}
of an algebraic variety
X\subset\mathbb{A}^{m}_{\bf k}
is not expected to satisfy the Grinberg-Kazhdan-Drinfeld theorem
1.6
. This relates to similar considerations made by Chiu, de Fernex and Docampo in
[
CdFD
]
. Our approach should be suited to extend the example of Bourqui and Sebag
[
BS1
]
to more general situations. They show by means of differential algebras that the formal neighborhood of the constant arc
0
in the arc space
X_{\infty,0}
of the plane curve
X
defined by
y_{1}^{2}+y_{2}^{2}=0
in
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\bf k}
is not a cartesian product as in Theorem
10.1
, provided that the field
{\bf k}
does not contain a square root of
-1
.

It seems that the triviality techniques from local complex analytic geometry as developed by Ephraim
[
Eph
]
, extended suitably to the infinite dimensional context, could be very appropriate to prove such type of results.

We sketch the main ideas: Let
{\mathcal{Y}}={\mathcal{Y}}(f)\subset\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
be an arquile variety defined by some power series vector
f({\rm t},{\rm y})\in{\mathcal{B}}^{k}
, and let
y=y({\rm t})\in{\mathcal{Y}}
be a point of
{\mathcal{Y}}
. We say that
{\mathcal{Y}}
is analytically trivial at
y
if the completed local ring
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{Y}},y}
is isomorphic to the factor ring
{\bf k}[[{\rm u}_{\infty}]]/J
of a formal power series ring
{\bf k}[[{\rm u}_{\infty}]]={\bf k}[[{\rm u}_{1},{\rm u}_{2},...]]
in countably many variables
{\rm u}_{j}
by an ideal
J
which can be generated by series not depending on the first variable
{\rm u}_{1}
. This means that the formal neighborhood of
y
in
{\mathcal{Y}}
is isomorphic to a cartesian product of the formal neighborhood of
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\bf k}
at
0
with the formal neighborhood of some other arquile variety at a certain point.

To simplify the notation, we assume that
y=0\in\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m}
is the zero-vector, so all coefficients
y_{ij}
are zero for all
i
and
j
. Writing
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{Y}},y}
as a factor ring
{\bf k}[[{\rm y}_{m,\infty}]]/I_{\infty}
for some ideal
I_{\infty}
of
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{A}_{\circ}^{m},y}={\bf k}[[{\rm y}_{m,\infty}]]={\bf k}[[{\rm y}_{ij},\,1\leq i\leq m,\,j\geq 1]]
, it is equivalent to say that there exist formal power series
\varphi_{ij}({\rm y}_{m,\infty})\in{\bf k}[[{\rm y}_{m,\infty}]]
, for
1\leq i\leq m,\,j\geq 1
, defining a local
{\bf k}
-algebra isomorphism
\varphi
of
{\bf k}[[{\rm y}_{m,\infty}]]
and such that suitable generators of
\varphi(I_{\infty})
do not depend on one of the variables
{\rm y}_{ij}
, say, without loss of generality,
{\rm y}_{11}
.

If an arquile variety satisfies the assertion of the Grinberg-Kazhdan-Drinfeld theorem at a point
y
, then it is in particular analytically trivial at that point.

Assume now that
I_{\infty}
is generated by power series
F_{\ell}\in{\bf k}[[{\rm y}_{m,\infty}]]
,
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
, such that the series
\varphi(F_{\ell})
generate the ideal
\varphi(I_{\infty})
.

{\bf k}[[{\rm y}_{m,\infty}]]
. This complication is due to the fact that one may have to take for
I_{\infty}
the topological closure in
{\bf k}[[{\rm y}_{m,\infty}]]
of the ideal generated algebraicially, i.e., by finite linear combinations, by the polynomials
F_{\ell}
resulting from
f
by Taylor expansion.

Let
G_{n}
,

, be generators of
\varphi(I)
not depending on
{\rm y}_{11}
. We may then write

for suitable power series

. This system of equations can be formally derived with respect to
{\rm y}_{11}
and produces as in the finite dimensional case a regular derivation
\Delta

{\bf k}[[{\rm y}_{m,\infty}]]
which sends all

to
I_{\infty}
, cf.
[
Eph

. So the existence of such a derivation is a necessary criterion for the analytic triviality (it should also be sufficient, but this is not used here).

Let us carry out this in the example of the arc space of a variety

m=2
and write
{\rm y}

{\rm y}_{1}
and
{\rm z}
for
{\rm y}_{2}

,

\displaystyle F_{\ell}({\rm y}_{\infty},{\rm z}_{\infty})=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}^{\mathbb{N}},|\alpha|=c,||\alpha||=\ell}\,{\rm y}^{\alpha}_{\infty}+\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}^{\mathbb{N}},|\alpha|=d,||\alpha||=\ell}\,{\rm z}_{\infty}^{\alpha},

where

{\mathbb{N}}^{\mathbb{N}}
with only finitely many non-zero entries,

\prod_{i}{\rm y}_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}
and where
|\alpha|=\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}

{\rm y}^{\alpha}

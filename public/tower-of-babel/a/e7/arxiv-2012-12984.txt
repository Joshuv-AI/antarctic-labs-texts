Singular integrals on C w ∗ 1 , α C_{w^{*}}^{1,\alpha} regular curves in Banach duals
Scott Zimmerman

Abstract
. The modern study of singular integral operators on curves in the plane began in the 1970’s. Since then, there has been a vast array of work done on the boundedness of singular integral operators defined on lower dimensional sets in Euclidean spaces. In recent years, mathematicians have attempted to push these results into a more general metric setting particularly in the case of singular integral operators defined on curves and graphs in Carnot groups. Suppose X = Y ∗ X=Y^{*} for a separable Banach space Y Y . Any separable metric space can be isometrically embedded in such a Banach space via the Kuratowski embedding. Suppose Γ = γ ⁡ ( [ a , b ] ) \Gamma=\gamma([a,b]) is a curve in X X whose w ∗ w^{*} -derivative is Hölder continuous and bounded away from 0. We prove that any convolution type singular integral operator associated with a 1-dimensional Calderón-Zygmund kernel which is uniformly L 2 L^{2} -bounded on lines is L p L^{p} -bounded along Γ \Gamma . We also prove a version of David’s “good lambda” theorem for upper regular measures on doubling metric spaces.



1.
Introduction

This paper will be concerned with singular integral operators (SIOs) generated by 1-dimensional Calderón-Zygmund kernels in the non-Euclidean setting.
For all relevant definitions, see Section
2
.

The modern study of SIOs on curves
began with the works of Calderón
[
3
]
and
Coifman, McIntosh, and Meyer
[
9
]

on the
L^{2}
boundedness of the Cauchy integral operator in the complex plane.
David
[
10
]

and
Mattila, Melnikov, and Verdera
[
19
]

classified the regular sets
E

for which the Cauchy operator is bounded in
L^{2}(E)
as those sets which are contained in a regular curve.
A curve
\Gamma
in a metric space
X
is
regular
if the length of the curve within a ball is comparable to the radius of the ball. See (
2.1
).
A thorough discussion of bounded SIOs on lower dimensional sets and their applications can be found in Tolsa’s 2014 book
[
21
]
.

Work on low dimensional SIOs in the non-Euclidean setting
has attracted recent interest.
In particular, there has been progress regarding the properties of SIOs on lower dimensional sets in Carnot groups
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
13
]
.
For example, any kernel in the Heisenberg group which is uniformly
L^{2}
-bounded on vertical planes corresponds to a SIO which is bounded on any 3-regular
C^{1,\alpha}
intrinsic Lipschitz graph with compact support
[
4
]
.
Further, any kernel in an arbitrary Carnot group which is uniformly
L^{2}
-bounded on (horizontal) lines (a property which we will call UBL)
corresponds to a SIO which is bounded along any
C^{1,\alpha}
regular curve
[
7
]
.
Recently,
Fässler and Orponen
[
13
]

questioned if every UBL kernel in the Heisenberg group generates a SIO which is bounded along regular curves, and they provided some evidence toward a positive answer.

Ideally, one would like to
fully understand the behavior of SIOs and their relationship to the geometry of a curve in a general metric setting.
As a first step,
we will consider SIOs generated by kernels
in any Banach space
X
which is the dual of a separable Banach space
Y
i.e.
X=Y^{*}
.
The inspiration for this decision was the famous Kuratowski embedding
[
18
]
:
any separable metric space
M
can be embedded isometrically into
X=\ell^{\infty}
,
and
\ell^{\infty}
is the dual of the separable Banach space
Y=\ell^{1}
.
In particular, we may embed a curve in any metric space isometrically into
\ell^{\infty}
.
Therefore, an exploration of the theory of SIOs on curves in such Banach spaces is a reasonable first step toward an understanding of
SIOs in arbitrary metric spaces.

This paper will focus on the main result from
[
7
]
mentioned above
(which addressed SIOs along
C^{1,\alpha}
regular curves in Carnot groups)
in the Banach space setting.
The proofs in
[
7
]
strongly relied on the smoothness of the underlying curve.
As our end goal is the study of SIOs in arbitrary metric spaces,
it seems natural to consider curves which are smooth with respect to
the
metric derivative
defined as follows for a curve
\gamma:(a,b)\to X
:

|\dot{\gamma}|(x)=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{d(\gamma(x+h),\gamma(x))}{|h|}.

Unfortunately for us,
the metric derivative computes only the speed of a curve.
It provides no directional information,
and such information is essential for the understanding of SIOs via linear approximations.

Another notion of differentiability
which encodes both speed and direction will be necessary
for the purposes of this paper.
Suppose
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is open and
X=Y^{*}
for a separable Banach space
Y
.
Following the examples of
[
1
,
16
]
,
we say that a map

f:\Omega\to X
is
w^{*}
-differentiable
at
x\in\Omega

if there is a linear map
D^{*}f_{x}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to X
such that

\lim_{z\to x}\left\langle\frac{f(z)-f(x)-D^{*}f_{x}(z-x)}{|z-x|},y\right\rangle=0\quad\text{for all }y\in Y.

In the language of curves,
this means that
\gamma:[a,b]\to X

is
w^{*}
-differentiable
at
t\in[a,b]

if there is a point
\gamma^{\prime}(t)\in X
such that

\tfrac{d}{ds}\langle\gamma(s),y\rangle|_{s=t}=\langle\gamma^{\prime}(t),y\rangle\quad\text{ for all }y\in Y.

We say that
\gamma
is
C_{w^{*}}^{1,\alpha}

if
\gamma^{\prime}(t)
exists for every
t\in[a,b]
and
\gamma^{\prime}
is
\alpha
-Hölder continuous.

With this notion of differentiability in hand,
we are now ready to state the main result of the paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix
\alpha\in(0,1]
and
p\in(1,\infty)
.
Suppose
X
is the dual of a separable Banach space
and
\Gamma
is a
C_{w^{*}}^{1,\alpha}
regular curve in
X
.
Then any
convolution type
singular integral operator corresponding to a 1-dimensional CZ kernel which is uniformly
L^{2}
-bounded on lines
is
L^{p}(\mathcal{H}^{1}|_{E})
-bounded for any regular set
E\subset\Gamma
.

The statement and proof of this result are
largely similar to those of the main theorem in
[
7
]
.
The main differences (which will be addressed in this paper) are the preliminary properties of
w^{*}
-smooth curves (as described in Section
3
) and the “good lambda” result (Theorem
5.1
). Moreover, the examples of CZ-kernels in Section
2
are original in the Banach space setting.
Note that Theorem
1.1
is not advertised as a generalization of the main result of
[
7
]
. I am not currently aware of an embedding of a curve in a Carnot group into
\ell^{\infty}
through which the result in
[
7
]
follows from Theorem
1.1
.
Rather, Theorem
1.1
is simply of independent interest in the Banach space setting.

Note that, since we are considering Banach spaces, one may suppose that a more appropriate choice of derivative is the
Fréchet derivative.
However, Theorem
1.1
would in fact be weakened if
\Gamma
was assumed to be
C^{1,\alpha}
with respect to the Fréchet derivative rather than the
w^{*}
-derivative.
Indeed, if the Fréchet derivative
D\gamma_{x}
of a curve
\gamma
exists at a point
x
, then
\gamma
is also
w^{*}
-differentiable at
x
with
\gamma^{\prime}(x)=D\gamma_{x}
.
These notions of differentiability are distinct in general.
For example, as noted in the introduction of
[
1
]
, the curve
t\mapsto\chi_{(0,t)}

in
(C[0,1])^{*}

is
w^{*}
-differentiable
with
w^{*}
-derivative
\delta_{t}
,
but it is not Fréchet differentiable.

An important (and standard) part of the proof of Theorem
1.1

is the “big pieces” theorem of David
[
11
]

which utilizes a standard “good
\lambda
” inequality.
The theorem allows us reduce our attention from the entire set
\Gamma
to a suitably large subset of any ball.
The original result of David was stated for regular measures in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Section
5
below
contains a general statement and proof of a “good lambda” result
for upper regular measures (which are not necessarily doubling) on doubling metric spaces
similar to a theorem of Tolsa
[
21
, Theorem 2.22]
.
See Theorem
5.1
.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
,
we will see the definitions of Calderón-Zygmund (CZ) kernels, singular integral operators, and their boundedness.
We will also see examples of CZ kernels in our Banach space setting.
In Section
3
, we will explore the notion of
w^{*}
-differentiability along curves and establish important properties of
w^{*}
-smooth curves.
An outline of the proof of Theorem
1.1
is presented in Section
4
.
Finally, as discussed above, the proof of a “good lambda” result for upper regular measures on doubling metric spaces is given in Section
5
.

2.
Singular integral operators in Banach spaces

Suppose
M
is a metric space with metric
d
.
For
x\in M
and
r>0
,
we will use
B(x,r)
to denote the open ball

B(x,r):=\{y\in M:d(x,y)<r\}.

A Radon measure
\mu
on
M
is called
1-
Ahlfors-David-regular
(or simply
regular
)
if there is a constant
C\geq 1
such that

(2.1)

C^{-1}r\leq\mu(B(x,r))\leq Cr\quad\text{for all }x\in X\text{ and }0<r\leq\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits(\text{supp}(\mu)).

If only the upper inequality holds, we say that
\mu
is
upper regular
. A closed set
E\subset X

is called
regular

if
the restriction of the 1-dimensional Hausdorff measure to
E
(denoted
\mathcal{H}^{1}|_{E}
) is regular.
We will denote the constant
C

associated with a measure
\mu
or set
E
by
\text{reg}(\mu)
or
\text{reg}(E)

respectively.

An
n-dimensional Calderón-Zygmund (CZ) kernel

K
on a metric space
M
is a continuous function

K:(M\times M)\setminus\Delta\to\mathbb{R}

where
\Delta=\{(x,x)\,:\,x\in M\}

satisfying the following for some
C>0
and
\beta\in(0,1]
:

(2.2)

|K(x,y)|\leq\frac{C}{d(x,y)^{n}}

and

(2.3)

|K(x,y)-K(x^{\prime},y)|+|K(y,x)-K(y,x^{\prime})|\leq C\frac{d(x,x^{\prime})^{\beta}}{d(x,y)^{n+\beta}}

for all
x,x^{\prime},y\in M

with

d(x,x^{\prime})\leq d(x,y)/2
.

2.1.
Singular integral operators

Suppose
K
is a 1-dimensional CZ kernel
and
\mu
is an upper regular measure on a metric space
M
.
For each
\varepsilon>0
, define the
truncated singular integral operator

T_{\mu,\varepsilon}

associated with
K
as

T_{\mu,\varepsilon}f(x)=\int_{\|x-y\|>\varepsilon}K(x,y)f(y)\,d\mu(y)\quad\text{for any }f\in L^{p}(\mu),\,1<p<\infty.

The associated
maximal singular integral

T_{\mu,*}
is defined as

T_{\mu,*}f(x)=\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\left|T_{\mu,\varepsilon}f(x)\right|\quad\text{for any }f\in L^{p}(\mu),\,1<p<\infty.

If
\mu
is further assumed to be regular,
we define the symbol

\|T_{\mu}\|_{L^{p}(\mu)\to L^{p}(\mu)}:=\inf\{M>0\,:\,\|T_{\mu,\varepsilon}f\|_{L^{p}(\mu)}\leq M\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mu)}\text{ for }f\in L^{p}(\mu),\,\varepsilon>0\}

for each
p\in(1,\infty)
.
We say that
the singular integral operator
T_{\mu}
associated with
K
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mu)

if

\|T_{\mu}\|_{L^{p}(\mu)\to L^{p}(\mu)}<\infty
.
In other words,
T_{\mu}
is bounded if the operators
T_{\mu,\varepsilon}
are bounded uniformly on
L^{p}(\mu)
for all
\varepsilon>0
.

2.2.
CZ kernels in Banach spaces

Suppose
X
is a Banach space with norm
\|\cdot\|
.
Following the above definitions, we say that
a continuous function
K:X\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{R}

is a 1-dimensional CZ kernel if the function

K^{\prime}:(X\times X)\setminus\Delta\to\mathbb{R}

defined as

K^{\prime}(x,y)=K(x-y)
is a 1-dimensional CZ kernel.
In other words,
there are constants
B>0
and
\beta\in(0,1]
satisfying the growth condition

(2.4)

|K(x)|\leq\frac{B}{\|x\|}\quad\text{ for all }x\in X\setminus\{0\},

and the Hölder continuity condition

(2.5)

|K(x)-K(x+h)|\leq B\frac{\|h\|^{\beta}}{\|x\|^{1+\beta}}\quad\text{ for all }x,h\in X\text{ with }\|h\|\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\|x\|.

We define truncated and maximal singular integral operators
T_{\mu,\varepsilon}
and
T_{\mu,*}
associated with
K
to be the respective operators associated with
K^{\prime}
,
and we say that the singular integral operator
T_{\mu}
associated with
K
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mu)

if the operators
T_{\mu,\varepsilon}
associated with
K^{\prime}
are bounded uniformly on
L^{p}(\mu)
for all
\varepsilon>0
.

2.3.
Examples of CZ kernels in Banach spaces

(1)
Suppose
X
is a Banach space with a
C^{1}
norm
\|\cdot\|

i.e. the norm is Fréchet differentiable on
X\setminus\{0\}

and this Fréchet derivative is continuous.
Examples of such Banach spaces are Hilbert spaces and
L^{p}(\Omega)
for any metric measure space
\Omega
with
p>1

[
2
]
.
Suppose also that
X
has
a normalized Schauder basis
(e_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Define the
1-dimensional Banach-Riesz kernels

R_{n}
on
X
as

R_{n}(x)=\frac{x_{n}}{\|x\|^{2}}\quad\text{for }x\neq 0,\,n\in\mathbb{N}

where
x=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}x_{n}e_{n}
.

We will now check that each
R_{n}

is a 1-dimensional CZ kernel. It follows from the open mapping theorem that the basis projections
P_{n}(x):=\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}e_{j}
are uniformly bounded by a constant
C>0

[
12
, Theorem 4.10]
.
Thus

|x_{n}|=\|x_{n}e_{n}\|=\|P_{n}(x)-P_{n-1}(x)\|\leq 2C\|x\|.

That is,
R_{n}
satisfies (
2.4
) uniformly in
n
. Now, according to the mean value theorem,
for any
z
and
x
with
\|z\|=1
and
\|x\|\leq\tfrac{1}{2}

there is a point
c
on the segment from
z
to
z-x

and a constant
C^{\prime}>0
depending only on the norm on
X

such that

(2.6)

|R_{n}(z)-R_{n}(z-x)|=\|DR_{n}(c)\|\cdot\|x\|\leq C^{\prime}\|x\|

(where
DR_{n}
is the Frechét derivative of
R_{n}
)
since
\tfrac{1}{2}\leq\|z-x\|\leq\tfrac{3}{2}
and
R_{n}
is
C^{1}
away from 0.
Since

R_{n}
is
-1
-homogeneous (i.e.
R_{n}(rx)=r^{-1}R_{n}(x)
),
the Hölder condition (
2.5
) follows
(uniformly in
n
)
from (
2.6
) with
\beta=1
, and hence each
R_{n}
is a 1-dimensional CZ-kernel.

(2)
Assume that
X=Y^{*}
where
Y
is separable,
and again assume that
X
has a
C^{1}
norm
\|\cdot\|
.
Say
(y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a dense set in the unit sphere of
Y
.
Define the
1-dimensional dual-Riesz kernels
on
X
as

R^{*}_{n}(x)=\frac{\langle x,y_{n}\rangle}{\|x\|^{2}}\quad\text{for }x\neq 0,\,n\in\mathbb{N}

where
\langle x,\cdot\rangle
is evaluation of the functional
x
.
According to the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality,
R_{n}^{*}
satisfies (
2.4
) with the constant
B=1
,
and, as above, we may conclude that
R_{n}^{*}
satisfies (
2.5
).
Thus each
R_{n}^{*}
is a CZ kernel on
X
.

2.4.
The UBL condition and annular boundedness

Suppose again that
X
is a Banach space.
A continuous function
K:X\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{R}

satisfying the growth condition (
2.4
)
is said to be
uniformly bounded on lines
(or
UBL
)
if there is a constant
M>0
such that

\|T_{\mathcal{H}^{1}|_{L}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}^{1}|_{L})\to L^{2}(\mathcal{H}^{1}|_{L})}\leq M<\infty

for any line
L
in
X

where
T_{\mathcal{H}^{1}|_{L}}

is the SIO associated with
K
.

As mentioned in the introduction,
Fässler and Orponnen
[
13
]
asked if every 1-dimensional CZ kernel in the Heisenberg group
which is also UBL generates a SIO which is bounded on any regular curves.
Some progress was made in
[
7
]
wherein it was shown that such kernels are bounded on
C^{1,\alpha}

regular curves in any Carnot group.
It therefore seems natural to study this class of kernels in any metric space which possesses some sort of affine structure.

The proof of the following proposition is identical to that of
[
4
, Lemma 2.9]
.
Indeed, any line in
X
is a scaled isometric copy of
\mathbb{R}

which allows us to apply the same Fourier transform arguments in
\mathbb{R}

as seen in
[
4
]
.
Fix a smooth, even function
\psi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}

satisfying
\chi_{B(0,1/2)}\leq\psi\leq\chi_{B(0,2)}
,
and define for any
r>0
the radial functions
\psi_{r}:X\to\mathbb{R}
as

\psi_{r}(x):=\psi(r\|x\|)\quad\text{for all }x\in X.

Proposition 2.1
.

If
K
is a 1-dimensional CZ kernel which is UBL,
then
K
satisfies the annular boundedness condition.
That is,
there is a constant
A\geq 1

such that, for any line
L
,

(2.7)

\left|\int_{L}[\psi_{R}(y)-\psi_{r}(y)]K(y)\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}(y)\right|\leq A\quad\text{for all }0<r<R.

The notion of annular boundedness has appeared in
[
4
]
and
[
7
]
in the setting of Carnot groups.
In fact, we could argue as in
[
7
, Proposition 2.16]
to show that the UBL and annular boundedness conditions are equivalent, but this will not be needed below.

2.5.
Weak (1,1) estimates

We will now record two results which will be very useful in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
Suppose
M
is a metric space with Radon measure
\mu
.
A linear operator
T
is said to be
of weak type
(1,1)
if there is a constant
C>0
such that

(2.8)

\mu(\{x\in M:|Tf(x)|>\lambda\})\leq\frac{C}{\lambda}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mu)}\quad\text{for all }f\in L^{1}(\mu).

Our first result follows immediately from the theorem of Nazarov, Treil, and Volberg
(see Theorem 9.1 in
[
20
]
).

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose
M
is a separable metric space and
\mu
is an upper regular measure on
M
.
If
K
is a 1-dimensional CZ kernel on
M

such that the SIO
T_{\mu}
associated with
K
is bounded in
L^{2}(\mu)
,
then
T_{\mu,*}
is of weak type
(1,1)

where the constant in the definition (
2.8
) depends only on
K
and
\|T_{\mu}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)\to L^{2}(\mu)}
.

For a Radon measure
\mu
on a metric space
M
,
we define the maximal operator
M_{\mu}
as

M_{\mu}f(x)=\sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{\mu(B(x,r))}\int_{B(x,r)}|f|\,d\mu\quad\text{for all }f\in L^{1}(\mu),\,x\in\mathbf{supp}(\mu).

When
\mu
is doubling, it is well known (see e.g. Theorem 2.2 in
[
15
]
)
that

M_{\mu}
is of weak type
(1,1)
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose
K
is a kernel
and
\mu
is a regular measure on a metric space
M

with
\mathbf{supp}(\mu)=A\cup B
where
B
is bounded and
d(A,B)>\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits(B)
.
If there is a constant
c>0
such that
f\in L^{1}(\mu)
and
\lambda>0
satisfy

\mu(\{x\in A:T_{\mu|_{A},*}f(x)>\lambda\})\leq\frac{c}{\lambda}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mu)}

and

\mu(\{x\in B:T_{\mu|_{B},*}f(x)>\lambda\})\leq\frac{c}{\lambda}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mu)},

then there is a constant
C>0
depending only on
c
,
K
, and
\mu
such that

\mu(\{x\in M:T_{\mu,*}f(x)>2\lambda\})\leq\frac{C}{\lambda}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mu)}.

In particular, if
T_{\mu|_{A},*}
and are both
T_{\mu|_{B},*}
of weak type
(1,1)
,
then
T_{\mu,*}
is of weak type
(1,1)
.

Proof.

Write
\mu_{1}:=\mu|_{A}
and
\mu_{2}:=\mu|_{B}
.
Since
T_{\mu,*}f\leq T_{\mu_{1},*}f+T_{\mu_{2},*}f
, we can write

\displaystyle\mu(\{x\in M

\displaystyle:T_{\mu,*}f(x)>2\lambda\})

\displaystyle=\mu(\{x\in A:T_{\mu,*}f(x)>2\lambda\})+\mu(\{x\in B:T_{\mu,*}f(x)>2\lambda\})

\displaystyle\leq\mu(\{x\in A:T_{\mu_{1},*}f(x)>\lambda\})+\mu(\{x\in A:T_{\mu_{2},*}f(x)>\lambda\})

\displaystyle\qquad+\mu(\{x\in B:T_{\mu_{1},*}f(x)>\lambda\})+\mu(\{x\in B:T_{\mu_{2},*}f(x)>\lambda\})

(2.9)

\displaystyle:=m_{A,1}+m_{A,2}+m_{B,1}+m_{B,2}.

By assumption, we have

(2.10)

m_{A,1}\leq\frac{c}{\lambda}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mu)}\quad\text{ and }\quad m_{B,2}\leq\frac{c}{\lambda}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mu)}.

To bound
m_{B,1}
, note that, for any
x\in B
and
\varepsilon>0
, (
2.2
) gives
a constant
C_{1}>0
such that

\displaystyle|T_{\mu_{1},\varepsilon}f(x)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{d(x,y)>\varepsilon}K(x,y)f(y)d\mu_{1}(y)\right|\leq C_{1}\int_{A}\frac{|f(y)|}{d(x,y)}d\mu(y).

Since
d(A,B)>\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits(B)
and
x\in B
, we have the bound

\displaystyle\int_{A}\frac{|f(y)|}{d(x,y)}d\mu(y)\leq\int_{d(x,y)>\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits(B)}\frac{|f(y)|}{d(x,y)}d\mu(y)\leq\frac{\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mu)}}{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits(B)}.

Therefore,
T_{\mu_{1},*}f(x)\leq\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits(B)^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mu)}
, and so, since
\mu
is regular,

(2.11)

\displaystyle m_{B,1}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{B}T_{\mu_{1},*}f\,d\mu\leq\frac{C_{1}}{\lambda}\cdot\frac{\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mu)}\mu(B)}{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits(B)}\leq\frac{C_{1}\text{reg}(\mu)}{\lambda}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mu)}.

To bound
m_{A,2}
, note that, for any
x\in A
, we have
d(x,B)\geq d(A,B)>\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits(B)
, and so, for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle|T_{\mu_{2},\varepsilon}f(x)|\leq C_{1}\int_{B}\frac{|f(y)|}{d(x,y)}d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{1}}{d(x,B)}\int_{B(x,2d(x,B))}|f|\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2C_{1}\text{reg}(\mu)}{\mu(B(x,2d(x,B)))}\int_{B(x,2d(x,B))}|f|\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{1}\text{reg}(\mu)M_{\mu}f(x).

Since
\mu
is a doubling measure, there is a constant
C_{2}>0
depending only on
\mu
and
C_{1}
such that

(2.12)

\displaystyle m_{A,2}\leq\mu\left(\left\{x\in A:M_{\mu}f(x)>\lambda(2C_{1}\text{reg}(\mu))^{-1}\right\}\right)\leq\frac{C_{2}}{\lambda}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mu)}.

Combining (
2.10
), (
2.11
), and (
2.12
) with (
2.9
) completes the proof.
∎

3.
w^{*}
-differentiability on curves

Suppose
X
is a Banach space which is dual to a separable Banach space
Y
i.e.
X=Y^{*}
.
Recall the definition of the
w^{*}
-derivative from the introduction
[
1
,
16
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Suppose
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is open.
A map

f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\supset\Omega\to X
is
w^{*}
-differentiable
at
x\in\Omega

if there is a linear map
D^{*}f_{x}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to X
such that

\lim_{z\to x}\left\langle\frac{f(z)-f(x)-D^{*}f_{x}(z-x)}{|z-x|},y\right\rangle=0\quad\text{for all }y\in Y.

For a curve
\gamma:[a,b]\to X
,
this translates to the following:
\gamma^{\prime}(x)\in X
is the
w^{*}
-
derivative
of
\gamma
at
x\in(a,b)
if

\lim_{h\to 0}\left\langle\frac{\gamma(x+h)-\gamma(x)-h\gamma^{\prime}(x)}{h},y\right\rangle=0\quad\text{for every }y\in Y.

In other words,
\frac{d}{ds}\langle\gamma(s),y\rangle|_{s=x}=\langle\gamma^{\prime}(x),y\rangle
for any
y\in Y
.
We define
\gamma^{\prime}(a)
and
\gamma^{\prime}(b)
via one sided limits.

3.1.
A change of variables formula for the
w^{*}
-derivative

To begin our exploration of the
w^{*}
-derivative, we prove a change of variables formula for Lipschitz curves in
X
.

We first record the powerful theorem of Ambrosio and Kirchheim.

Theorem 3.2

(
[
1
, Theorem 3.5]
)
.

A Lipschitz map
\gamma:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to X

is
w^{*}
-differentiable a.e.,
metrically differentiable a.e., and, for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\|D^{*}f_{x}(v)\|=md\,f_{x}(v)\quad\text{for all }v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

where
md\,f_{x}
is metric differential of
f
at
x
.

For curves in
X
, this theorem translates to the following.
For the remainder of this section,

\mathcal{I}
will be any (bounded or unbounded) interval in
\mathbb{R}
.

Corollary 3.3
.

A Lipschitz curve
\gamma:\mathcal{I}\to X

is
w^{*}
-differentiable a.e. and

\|\gamma^{\prime}(x)\|=|\dot{\gamma}|(x):=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{\|\gamma(x+h)-\gamma(x)\|}{|h|}\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in[a,b].

We will now use this result to verify a change of variables formula for Lipschitz curves in
X
.

Proposition 3.4
.

If
\gamma:\mathcal{I}\to X

is Lipschitz and

\gamma
is injective on
[a,b]\subseteq\mathcal{I}
,
then

\int_{\gamma([a,b])}f\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}|_{\gamma([a,b])}=\int_{a}^{b}f(\gamma(t))\|\gamma^{\prime}(t)\|\,dt\quad\text{for all }f\in L^{1}(\mathcal{H}^{1}|_{\gamma([a,b])}).

Proof.

According to
[
14
, Theorem 3.6]
, for any
[c,d]\subseteq[a,b]
we have

\mathcal{H}^{1}(\gamma([c,d]))=\ell(\gamma|_{[c,d]})=\int_{c}^{d}|\dot{\gamma}|(t)\,dt

where
\ell(\gamma|_{[c,d]})
is the length of the curve

\gamma:[c,d]\to X
.
Corollary
3.3
therefore gives

\mathcal{H}^{1}(\gamma([c,d]))=\int_{c}^{d}\|\gamma^{\prime}(t)\|\,dt.

Using standard approximations of
L^{1}

functions by characteristic functions
\chi|_{\gamma([c,d])}

proves the proposition.
∎

3.2.
w^{*}
-smooth curves

Definition 3.5
.

A curve
\gamma:\mathcal{I}\to X

is
C_{w^{*}}^{1}

if
\gamma^{\prime}(x)
exists for all
x\in\mathcal{I}

and
\gamma^{\prime}:\mathcal{I}\to X
is continuous.

Proposition 3.6
.

If
\gamma:\mathcal{I}\to X

is
C_{w^{*}}^{1}
with

0<m:=\inf_{\mathcal{I}}\|\gamma^{\prime}\|\quad\text{ and }\quad\sup_{\mathcal{I}}\|\gamma^{\prime}\|=:M<\infty,

then
\gamma
is uniformly locally bi-Lipschitz.
That is, there is some
\delta>0
so that

\tfrac{m}{2}|t_{2}-t_{1}|\leq\|\gamma(t_{2})-\gamma(t_{1})\|\leq M|t_{2}-t_{1}|\quad\text{for all }t_{1},t_{2}\in\mathcal{I}\text{ with }|t_{2}-t_{1}|<\delta.

Proof.

For any
y\in Y
and any
[t_{1},t_{2}]\subseteq\mathcal{I}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\gamma(t_{2})-\gamma(t_{1}),y\rangle=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\tfrac{d}{ds}\langle\gamma(s),y\rangle\,ds=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\langle\gamma^{\prime}(s),y\rangle\,ds.

Taking the supremum over all
y\in Y
with
\|y\|_{Y}=1
(first on the right then on the left) gives

\|\gamma(t_{2})-\gamma(t_{1})\|\leq\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\|\gamma^{\prime}(s)\|\,ds\leq M|t_{2}-t_{1}|.

Now, by the continuity of
\gamma^{\prime}
, there is some
\delta>0
so that
\|\gamma^{\prime}(t_{1})-\gamma^{\prime}(t_{2})\|<\tfrac{m}{2}
whenever
|t_{2}-t_{1}|<\delta
.
Fix
[t_{1},t_{2}]\subseteq\mathcal{I}
so that
|t_{2}-t_{1}|<\delta
. Then for all
y\in Y
we have

\displaystyle\left\langle\frac{\gamma(t_{2})-\gamma(t_{1})-(t_{2}-t_{1})\gamma^{\prime}(t_{1})}{t_{2}-t_{1}},y\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t_{2}-t_{1}}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\langle\gamma^{\prime}(s),y\rangle\,ds-\langle\gamma^{\prime}(t_{1}),y\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t_{2}-t_{1}}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\langle\gamma^{\prime}(s)-\gamma^{\prime}(t_{1}),y\rangle\,ds.

Again taking the supremum over all
y\in Y
with
\|y\|_{Y}=1
gives

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\gamma(t_{2})-\gamma(t_{1})-(t_{2}-t_{1})\gamma^{\prime}(t_{1})}{t_{2}-t_{1}}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{t_{2}-t_{1}}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\|\gamma^{\prime}(s)-\gamma^{\prime}(t_{1})\|\,ds<\tfrac{m}{2}.

Therefore

m|t_{2}-t_{1}|-\|\gamma(t_{2})-\gamma(t_{1})\|\leq\|\gamma^{\prime}(t_{1})\||t_{2}-t_{1}|-\|\gamma(t_{2})-\gamma(t_{1})\|\leq\tfrac{m}{2}|t_{2}-t_{1}|

for all
|t_{1}-t_{2}|<\delta
in
\mathcal{I}
, and the result follows.
∎

The following corollary is immediate since the bilipschitz image of a regular set is itself regular (in the sense of (
2.1
)),
and
\Gamma=\gamma([a,b])
is a finite union of bilipschitz images of intervals
when
C_{w^{*}}^{1}
and

\inf_{[a,b]}\|\gamma^{\prime}\|>0
.

Corollary 3.7
.

If
\gamma:[a,b]\to X
is

C_{w^{*}}^{1}
and

\inf_{[a,b]}\|\gamma^{\prime}\|>0
,
then

\Gamma=\gamma([a,b])
is regular.

3.3.
C^{1,\alpha}_{w^{*}}
curves

Definition 3.8
.

For any
\alpha\in(0,1]
,
we say that
\gamma:\mathcal{I}\to X
is
C_{w^{*}}^{1,\alpha}

if it is
C_{w^{*}}^{1}
and
\gamma^{\prime}
is
\alpha
-Hölder continuous
i.e. there is a constant
c>0
so that

\|\gamma^{\prime}(t_{1})-\gamma^{\prime}(t_{2})\|\leq c|t_{1}-t_{2}|^{\alpha}\quad\text{for all }t_{1},t_{2}\in\mathcal{I}.

For a fixed
t_{0}\in\mathcal{I}
,
call

L_{t_{0}}(t)=\gamma(t_{0})+(t-t_{0})\gamma^{\prime}(t_{0})

the
linear approximation of
\gamma
at
t_{0}
.

Lemma 3.9
.

If
\gamma:\mathcal{I}\to X
is
C_{w^{*}}^{1,\alpha}
,
then, for any
t_{0}\in\mathcal{I}
,

(3.1)

\|\gamma(t)-L_{t_{0}}(t)\|\leq c|t-t_{0}|^{1+\alpha}

where
c>0
is the constant from the
\alpha
-Hölder continuity.

Proof.

For any
y\in Y
, we have, as above

\displaystyle\langle\gamma(t)-L_{t_{0}}(t),y\rangle=\langle\gamma(t)-\gamma(t_{0}),y\rangle-(t-t_{0})\langle\gamma^{\prime}(t_{0}),y\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\langle\gamma^{\prime}(s)-\gamma^{\prime}(t_{0}),y\rangle\,ds.

Again, taking the supremum over all
y\in Y
with
\|y\|_{Y}=1
gives

\displaystyle\|\gamma(t)-L_{t_{0}}(t)\|\leq\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\|\gamma^{\prime}(s)-\gamma^{\prime}(t_{0})\|\,ds\leq c\int_{t_{0}}^{t}|s-t_{0}|^{\alpha}\,ds\leq c|t-t_{0}|^{1+\alpha}.

∎

We will now define the class of curves to which Theorem
1.1
may be applied.

Definition 3.10
.

A set
\Gamma\subset X

is a
C_{w^{*}}^{1,\alpha}
regular curve

if
\Gamma=\gamma([a,b])
for some

C_{w^{*}}^{1,\alpha}
curve
\gamma:[a,b]\to X
with
\inf_{[a,b]}\|\gamma^{\prime}\|>0
.

.

[

).

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Theorem
1.1
will follow from the following “good lambda” argument
a la David
[
11
]
and Tolsa
[
21
]

which allows us to reduce our attention in any ball to a large enough subset
G
.
The lemma stated here will follow from
the more general result proven later
(see Theorem
5.1
) which is proven for upper regular (but not necessarily doubling)
measures on doubling metric spaces.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose
X
is a Banach space,

\Gamma
is a regular set in
X
,
and
K
is a 1-dimensional CZ-kernel on
X

which satisfies the UBL condition.
Assume that there exist constants

0<\theta<1
and
c>0

such that,
for every ball
B=B(x,r)
with
x\in\Gamma
and
r>0
,
there is a compact set
G^{\prime}\subset B\cap\Gamma
such that
\mathcal{H}^{1}(G^{\prime})\geq\theta\mathcal{H}^{1}(B\cap\Gamma)
and

(4.1)

\mathcal{H}^{1}\left(\{x\in G^{\prime}\,:\,T_{\mathcal{H}^{1}|_{\Gamma},*}f(x)>\lambda\}\right)\leq\frac{c}{\lambda}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{H}^{1}|_{\Gamma})}\quad\forall f\in L^{1}(\mathcal{H}^{1}|_{\Gamma}),\,\lambda>0.

Then there is a constant
A_{p}^{\prime}=A_{p}^{\prime}(c,\Gamma,K,\theta)
for each
p\in(1,\infty)
such that

\|T_{\mathcal{H}^{1}|_{\Gamma},*}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{H}^{1}|_{\Gamma})}\leq A_{p}^{\prime}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{H}^{1}|_{\Gamma})}.

Proof.

Our goal will be to
apply Theorem
5.1
to the doubling metric space
M=\Gamma
.
If
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits(\Gamma)=\infty
, then the result follows immediately.

Now suppose that
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits(\Gamma)<\infty
.
In this case,
we may not directly apply Theorem
5.1
since

\mathbf{supp}(\mathcal{H}^{1}|_{\Gamma})=\Gamma
is bounded.
To remedy this, we will append rays to
\Gamma
(as in
[
7
]
) and apply Theorem
5.1
to this new, unbounded support.
We can assume without loss of generality that
0\in\Gamma
.
Indeed, the left invariance of the metric in a normed vector space implies that
\mathcal{H}^{1}|_{E}=\mathcal{H}^{1}|_{x+E}
for any
x\in X
and
E\subset X
.

Since
\Gamma
is regular, it follows that, for any ball
B
centered on
\Gamma

with radius
r=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits(\Gamma)<\infty
,

\mathcal{H}^{1}(\Gamma)=\mathcal{H}^{1}(\Gamma\cap B)\leq\text{reg}(\Gamma)\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits(\Gamma)<\infty.

Thus, by rescaling the norm, we may assume without loss of generality that
\mathcal{H}^{1}(\Gamma)=1
.

Consider the ray
\ell=\{sv_{0}:s\in[3,\infty)\}
where
v_{0}\in X
satisfies
\|v_{0}\|=1
.
Set
\widehat{\Gamma}=\Gamma\cup\ell
and
\nu=\mathcal{H}^{1}|_{\widehat{\Gamma}}
.
Since
\Gamma\subset B(0,2)
and
\ell\cap B(0,2)=\emptyset
,
the measure
\nu
is regular with
\text{reg}(\nu)
depending only on
\Gamma
.
Moreover,
\mathbf{supp}(\nu)
is unbounded.

Fix
x\in\widehat{\Gamma}
and choose
r>0
. Set
B=B(x,r)\cap\widehat{\Gamma}
.
In order to apply Theorem
5.1
,
it remains to prove that
B

has a compact subset
G\subset B

with

\nu(G)\geq\theta\nu(B)

satisfying

(4.2)

\nu\left(\{x\in G\,:\,T_{\nu,*}f(x)>\lambda\}\right)\leq\frac{c}{\lambda}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\nu)}\quad\text{for all }f\in L^{1}(\nu),\,\lambda>0

for a constant
c>0
depending only on
K
,
\nu
, and
X
.

Case 1:

x\in\ell
.

Choose
G=B\cap\ell
.
If
r<1
, then
B
does not intersect the original set
\Gamma

since
0\in\Gamma
,
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits(\Gamma)\leq\mathcal{H}^{1}(\Gamma)=1
, and

.
Thus
\nu(B)=\nu(B\cap\ell)=\nu(G)
.
If
r\geq 1
, then

\nu(B)\leq\nu(\Gamma)+\nu(B\cap\ell)=1+\nu(G)\leq(\text{reg}(\nu)+1)\nu(G).

According to the UBL assumption on
K
,
there is a constant
C>0
independent of
B
such that

\|T_{\mathcal{H}^{1}|_{\ell}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}^{1}|_{\ell})\to L^{2}(\mathcal{H}^{1}|_{\ell})}\leq C<\infty.

Since
G\subset\ell
and
\mathcal{H}^{1}|_{G}=\chi_{G}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}|_{\ell}
,
it follows that
\|T_{\mathcal{H}^{1}|_{G}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}^{1}|_{G})\to L^{2}(\mathcal{H}^{1}|_{G})}\leq C
as well.
Indeed, for any
f\in L^{2}(\mathcal{H}^{1}|_{G})
and
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\|T_{\mathcal{H}^{1}|_{G},\varepsilon}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}^{1}|_{G})}=\|T_{\mathcal{H}^{1}|_{\ell},\varepsilon}(f\chi_{G})\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}^{1}|_{G})}

\displaystyle\leq\|T_{\mathcal{H}^{1}|_{\ell},\varepsilon}(f\chi_{G})\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}^{1}|_{\ell})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\chi_{G}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}^{1}|_{\ell})}=C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}^{1}|_{G})}.

Thus
Lemma
2.2

applied to the metric space
G
with measure
\mu=\nu|_{G}=\mathcal{H}^{1}|_{G}

implies (
4.2
).

Case 2:

x\in\Gamma

By our assumptions,
there is a compact set
G^{\prime}\subset B\cap\Gamma

such that
\mathcal{H}^{1}(G^{\prime})\geq\theta\mathcal{H}^{1}(B\cap\Gamma)
and
T_{\mathcal{H}^{1}|_{\Gamma},*}

satisfies (
4.1
) on
G^{\prime}
with constant independent of
B
.
Set
G=G^{\prime}\cup(B\cap\ell)
.
We have

\nu(G)=\nu(G^{\prime})+\nu(B\cap\ell)\geq\theta\nu(B\cap\Gamma)+\theta\nu(B\cap\ell)=\theta\nu(B)

since
\theta<1
.
Moreover, the UBL assumption once again implies that

\|T_{\mathcal{H}^{1}|_{\ell}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}^{1}|_{\ell})\to L^{2}(\mathcal{H}^{1}|_{\ell})}\leq C<\infty

for a constant
C>0
independent of
B
.
Lemma
2.2
again implies that the operator
T_{\mathcal{H}^{1}|_{\ell},*}

is of weak type
(1,1)

on
\ell
.
Thus Lemma
2.3

applied to the measure
\mu=\nu
and the sets
A=\ell
and
B=\Gamma

ensures that
T_{\nu,*}

satisfies (
4.2
).

Therefore,
for any ball
B
centered on
\widehat{\Gamma}
,
we may use the arguments above to define a compact set
G\subset B\cap\widehat{\Gamma}

such that
\nu(G)\geq\theta\nu(B)
and

\nu\left(\{x\in G\,:\,T_{\nu,*}f(x)>\lambda\}\right)\leq\frac{c}{\lambda}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\nu)}\quad\text{for all }f\in L^{1}(\nu),\,\lambda>0.

According to Theorem
5.1
,
there is a constant
A_{p}=A_{p}(c,\Gamma,K,\theta)
for each
p\in(1,\infty)
such that

\|T_{\nu,*}f\|_{L^{p}(\nu)}\leq A_{p}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\nu)}
.
Since
\mathcal{H}^{1}|_{\Gamma}=\chi_{\Gamma}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}|_{\widehat{\Gamma}}=\chi_{\Gamma}\,d\nu
, we have proven the lemma.
∎

We will now present the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
Most of the work has already been done in Section
3
, Lemma

, and
[
7
]
.
We will simply see how the pieces fit together.

.

.

5.
Good lambda for upper regular measures on doubling metric spaces

David originally presented his “good lambda” argument for regular measures in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

[
11
]
.
A version of David’s result recently appeared in
[
13
]
for regular
measures in any proper metric space.
Note that every regular measure is necessarily doubling.
In
[
21
, Theorem 2.22]
, Tolsa proved a version of David’s result for upper regular

which are not necessarily doubling.

Theorem
5.1
below generalizes Tolsa’s result to the setting of upper regular Radon measures on doubling metric spaces.
As in
[
21
]
, this result does not require that the measure itself is doubling.

We say that a metric space
M
is
doubling
if there is a positive integer
C_{D}

such that any ball
B(x,r)\subset M
can be covered by
C_{D}
balls of radius
r/2
.
A Borel measure
\nu
on
M
is upper
n
-regular if
there is a constant
C_{\nu}>0
such that

\nu(B(x,r))\leq C_{\nu}r^{n}\quad\text{for all }x\in M,\,r>0.

For a constant
b\geq 1
, we say that a ball
B\subset M
is
b
-doubling
if
\nu(2B)\leq\beta\nu(B)
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose
(M,d)
is a doubling metric space,
K
is an
n
-dimensional CZ-kernel on
M
,
and
\nu
is an upper
n
-regular Radon measure on
M
with
unbounded support.
Assume
c>0
and
0<\theta<1
are constants
such that,
for some
b>0
,
every
b
-doubling ball
B
centered on
\mathbf{supp}(\nu)
has a compact subset
G\subset B\cap\mathbf{supp}(\nu)
with
\nu(G)\geq\theta\nu(B)
and

(5.1)

\nu\left(\{x\in G\,:\,T_{\nu,*}f(x)>\lambda\}\right)\leq\frac{c}{\lambda}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\nu)}\quad\text{for all }f\in L^{1}(\nu),\,\lambda>0.

Then for each
p\in(1,\infty)
,
there is a constant
A_{p}
depending only on
c
,
C_{\nu}
,
\theta
, and the constant from (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) such that

\|T_{\nu,*}f\|_{L^{p}(\nu)}\leq A_{p}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\nu)}.

We will prove Theorem
5.1
via the lemmas below.
Some arguments are standard
and follow the proof of Theorem 2.22 from
[
21
]
,
but details are included for the benefit of the reader.
Most importantly, we will establish the following “good
\lambda
” result.

Lemma 5.2
.

Suppose
M
,
K
,
n
,
c
,
\theta
, and
\nu
satisfy the assumptions of Theorem
5.1
.
For any
\varepsilon>0
, there is a
\delta>0
such that

\displaystyle\nu(\{x\in\mathbf{supp}(\nu):T_{\nu,*}f(x)>(1+\varepsilon)\lambda,

\displaystyle\,M_{\nu}f(x)\leq\delta\lambda\})

(5.2)

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\tfrac{\theta}{4}\right)\nu(\{x\in\mathbf{supp}(\nu):T_{\nu,*}f(x)>\lambda\})

for any
\lambda>0
and any
f\in L^{1}(\nu)
with compact support.

Before proving this lemma, we will establish a few preliminary lemmas.
The first is standard.

Lemma 5.3
.

If
\nu
is an upper
n
-regular measure on a metric space
M
,
and if
f\in L^{p}(\nu)
for some
1<p<\infty
, then

(5.3)

\int_{M\setminus B(x,R)}\frac{|f(y)|}{d(x,y)^{n+\beta}}\,d\nu(y)\leq\tfrac{2^{n+\beta}}{C_{\nu}(2^{\beta}-1)}R^{-\beta}M_{\nu}f(x)\quad\forall x\in\mathbf{supp}(\nu),\,R>0.

Proof.

\displaystyle\int_{M\setminus B(x,R)}\frac{|f(y)|}{d(x,y)^{n+\beta}}\,d\nu(y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{B(x,2^{k+1}R)\setminus B(x,2^{k}R)}\frac{|f(y)|}{d(x,y)^{n+\beta}}\,d\nu(y)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2^{k}R)^{n+\beta}}\int_{B(x,2^{k+1}R)}|f(y)|\,d\nu(y)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\nu}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2^{k+1}R)^{n}}{(2^{k}R)^{n+\beta}}M_{\nu}f(x)\leq\tfrac{C_{\nu}2^{n+\beta}}{2^{\beta}-1}R^{-\beta}M_{\nu}f(x).

∎

We will also need the following Whitney-type decomposition of an open set in a doubling metric space
M
.

Lemma 5.4

(Christ-Whitney decomposition)
.

Assume
M
is a doubling metric space.
Suppose
\Omega\subset M
is open and
\Omega\neq M
.
Then there is a countable collection
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of pairwise disjoint, Borel subsets of
\Omega

such that

(1)

\bigcup\mathcal{U}=\Omega
,

(2)

for any
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
there is some
x_{i}\in U_{i}
and some integer
k_{i}
such that

B\left(x_{i},\tfrac{1}{16}\cdot 8^{-k_{i}}\right)\subset U_{i}\subset B\left(x_{i},2\cdot 8^{-k_{i}}\right)

and

32\cdot 8^{-k_{i}}\leq d(U_{i},M\setminus\Omega)\leq 320\cdot 8^{-k_{i}},

(3)

there is a constant
C_{D}\geq 1
depending only on the doubling constant of
M
such that,
for any
i\in\mathbb{N}
, there are at most
C_{D}
indices
j\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(5.4)

B\left(x_{i},16\cdot 8^{-k_{i}}\right)\cap B\left(x_{j},16\cdot 8^{-k_{j}}\right)\neq\emptyset.

Proof.

According to Theorem 2.1 in
[
17
]
, there is a countable collection
\mathcal{Q}=\{Q_{k,i}:k\in\mathbb{Z},\,i\in N_{k}\subset\mathbb{N}\}
of Borel subsets of
\Omega
such that

(a)

\Omega=\bigcup_{i\in N_{k}}Q_{k,i}
for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,

(b)

Q_{k,i}\cap Q_{m,j}=\emptyset

or

Q_{k,i}\subset Q_{m,j}

when
k,m\in\mathbb{Z}
,
k\geq m
,
i\in N_{k}
, and
j\in N_{m}
,

(c)

for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
i\in N_{k}
,
there is some
x_{k,i}\in Q_{k,i}
such that

B\left(x_{k,i},\tfrac{1}{16}\cdot 8^{-k}\right)\subset B\left(x_{k,i},\tfrac{5}{14}\cdot 8^{-k}\right)\subset Q_{k,i}\subset\overline{B}\left(x_{k,i},\tfrac{8}{7}\cdot 8^{-k}\right)\subset B\left(x_{k,i},2\cdot 8^{-k}\right)

where
\overline{B}(x,r)=\{y\in M:d(x,y)\leq r\}
.
(Indeed, choose
r=\tfrac{1}{8}
in
[
17
]
.)
Now, write
\Omega_{k}:=\{x\in\Omega:40\cdot 8^{-k}<d(x,M\setminus\Omega)\leq 40\cdot 8^{-(k-1)}\}
so that
\Omega=\bigcup_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\Omega_{k}
.
Define the collection

\mathcal{U}_{0}:=\bigcup_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\{Q_{k,i}:i\in N_{k},\,Q_{k,i}\cap\Omega_{k}\neq\emptyset\}.

For each
Q_{k,i}\in\mathcal{U}_{0}
,
we have

d(Q_{k,i},M\setminus\Omega)\leq 40\cdot 8^{-(k-1)}=320\cdot 8^{-k}
.
Also,

d(Q_{k,i},M\setminus\Omega)\geq 40\cdot 8^{-k}-\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits(Q_{k,i})\geq(40-\tfrac{16}{7})8^{-k}>32\cdot 8^{-k}.

Therefore, the collection
\mathcal{U}_{0}

satisfies condition
(2)
.

Note that, if
Q\in\mathcal{U}_{0}
,
then there is a smallest integer
k
and a unique
i\in N_{k}
such that
Q\subset Q_{k,i}
.
We may thus define
\mathcal{U}
to be the subset of
\mathcal{U}_{0}
which is maximal with respect to set inclusion.
The resulting collection of sets is pairwise disjoint and satisfies conditions
(1)
and
(2)

We will now verify that
\mathcal{U}
satisfies
(3)
.
Write
\Omega^{c}:=M\setminus\Omega
.
Assume
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
satisfy (
5.4
).
Then

(5.5)

\displaystyle d(x_{i},x_{j})\leq 16\cdot 8^{-k_{i}}+16\cdot 8^{-k_{j}}\leq\tfrac{1}{2}d(U_{i},\Omega^{c})+\tfrac{1}{2}d(U_{j},\Omega^{c}).

Thus

\displaystyle d(U_{j},\Omega^{c})\leq d(U_{i},\Omega^{c})+d(U_{i},U_{j})\leq d(U_{i},\Omega^{c})+\tfrac{1}{2}d(U_{i},\Omega^{c})+\tfrac{1}{2}d(U_{j},\Omega^{c}).

Therefore

\displaystyle d(U_{j},\Omega^{c})\leq 3d(U_{i},\Omega^{c}).

Similarly,

\displaystyle d(U_{i},\Omega^{c})\leq 3d(U_{j},\Omega^{c}).

These two inequalities together with (
5.5
) give

(5.6)

\displaystyle d(x_{i},x_{j})\leq 2d(U_{i},\Omega^{c})\leq 640\cdot 8^{-k_{i}}

Now fix
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
If
j,\ell\in\mathbb{N}
are such that

(5.7)

\begin{array}[]{c}B\left(x_{i},16\cdot 8^{-k_{i}}\right)\cap B\left(x_{j},16\cdot 8^{-k_{j}}\right)\neq\emptyset\\
\text{and}\\
B\left(x_{i},16\cdot 8^{-k_{i}}\right)\cap B\left(x_{\ell},16\cdot 8^{-k_{\ell}}\right)\neq\emptyset\end{array}

then, as above,

\displaystyle d(U_{i},\Omega^{c})\leq 3d(U_{j},\Omega^{c})\quad\text{and}\quad d(U_{i},\Omega^{c})\leq 3d(U_{\ell},\Omega^{c}).

Therefore, since
U_{j}
and
U_{\ell}
are disjoint, we have

\displaystyle d(x_{j},x_{\ell})\geq\tfrac{1}{16}\min\{8^{-k_{j}},8^{-k_{\ell}}\}

\displaystyle\geq\tfrac{1}{5120}\min\{d(U_{j},\Omega^{c}),d(U_{\ell},\Omega^{c})\}

(5.8)

\displaystyle\geq\tfrac{1}{15360}d(U_{i},\Omega^{c}).

Consider now the ball
B:=B(x_{i},1024\cdot 8^{-k_{i}})
.
Since
M
is doubling,
we can cover
B
by
C_{D}
balls
\{V_{\alpha}\}
of radius

\tfrac{1}{1024}\cdot 8^{-k_{i}}

where
C_{D}>0
is a positive integer depending only on the doubling constant of
M
.
According to
(
5.6
),
if
j\in\mathbb{N}
satisfies (
5.4
),
then

d(x_{i},x_{j})\leq 640\cdot 8^{-k_{i}}
,
so
x_{j}\in B
.
Moreover, for any
j,\ell\in\mathbb{N}
which satisfy (
5.7
),
we must have from (
5.8
) that

\displaystyle d(x_{j},x_{\ell})\geq\tfrac{1}{15360}d(U_{i},\Omega^{c})\geq\tfrac{1}{480}\cdot 8^{-k_{i}}.

In particular, this means that
x_{j}
and
x_{\ell}
cannot lie in the same ball
V_{\alpha}
,
so there are at most
C_{D}
indices
j\in\mathbb{N}
which satisfy (
5.4
).
This proves the lemma.
∎

We will now prove the “good
\lambda
” estimate Lemma
5.2
.

Proof of Lemma
5.2
.

Assume
c>0
and
0<\theta<1
are constants
which satisfy the hypotheses of the lemma,
and set
b=2C_{D}

where
C_{D}
is the constant from Lemma
5.4
.
Fix
f\in L^{1}(\nu)
with compact support.

Write
\mathcal{S}:=\mathbf{supp}(\nu)
.
Consider the set

\Omega_{\lambda}=\{x\in\mathcal{S}\,:\,T_{\nu,*}f(x)>\lambda\}.

Since
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits(\mathcal{S})=\infty
, the growth condition (
2.2
)
ensures that

\Omega_{\lambda}\neq\mathcal{S}
.
Note also that

\Omega_{\lambda}
is open in
\mathcal{S}
.
Indeed,
for any
\varepsilon>0
,
the continuity of

T_{\nu,\varepsilon}f
follows from the continuity of
K
,
and so
T_{\nu,*}f
is lower semicontinuous.

Choose a
countable collection
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of pairwise disjoint, Borel subsets of
\Omega_{\lambda}\subsetneq\mathcal{S}

as in
Lemma
5.4
.
Define
\mathcal{I}_{1}
to be the collection of those indices
i\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(5.9)

\nu\left(B(x_{i},16\cdot 8^{-k_{i}})\right)\leq b\nu\left(B(x_{i},2\cdot 8^{-k_{i}})\right).

(Here and for the rest of this proof,
B(x,r)
will refer to an open ball in the metric space
(\mathcal{S},d)
.)
Note in particular that any ball

B(x_{i},2\cdot 8^{-k_{i}})

satisfying (
5.9
) is
b
-doubling.
Write
\mathcal{I}_{2}=\mathbb{N}\setminus\mathcal{I}_{1}
.

Claim.

(5.10)

\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{1}}\nu\left(U_{i}\right)\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\nu(\Omega_{\lambda})

Indeed, for each
i\in\mathcal{I}_{2}
we have

b\nu\left(B(x_{i},2\cdot 8^{-k_{i}})\right)<\nu\left(B(x_{i},16\cdot 8^{-k_{i}})\right).

Since any point in
\Omega_{\lambda}

lies in at most
C_{D}
balls of the form
B(x_{i},16\cdot 8^{-k_{i}})

with
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
it follows that

\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{2}}\nu\left(U_{i}\right)

is bounded by

\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{2}}\nu\left(B(x_{i},2\cdot 8^{-k_{i}})\right)\leq\tfrac{1}{b}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\nu\left(B(x_{i},16\cdot 8^{-k_{i}})\right)\leq\tfrac{C_{D}}{b}\nu\left(\Omega_{\lambda}\right)=\tfrac{1}{2}\nu\left(\Omega_{\lambda}\right)

since
b=2C_{D}
.
This proves the claim.

Lemma 5.5
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, there is a
\delta>0

such that,
if
i\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in U_{i}
satisfy

T_{\nu,*}f(x)>(1+\varepsilon)\lambda\quad\text{ and }\quad M_{\nu}f(x)\leq\delta\lambda,

then

T_{\nu,*}(\chi_{B(x_{i},4\cdot 8^{-k})}f)(x)>\varepsilon\lambda/2.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
.
Fix an index
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
and write
U:=U_{i}
,
x_{0}:=x_{i}
, and
k:=k_{i}

so that

B_{1}:=B\left(x_{0},\tfrac{1}{16}\cdot 8^{-k_{i}}\right)
and
B_{2}:=B\left(x_{0},2\cdot 8^{-k}\right)
satisfy
B_{1}\subset U\subset B_{2}
.
Suppose
x\in U
satisfies
T_{\nu,*}f(x)>(1+\varepsilon)\lambda
.

Since
d(U,\mathcal{S}\setminus\Omega_{\lambda})\leq 320\cdot 8^{-k}
and
U\subset B_{2}
with
r(B_{2})=2\cdot 8^{-k}
,
there must be some
z\in\mathcal{S}\setminus\Omega_{\lambda}

such that
d(z,x_{0})<322\cdot 8^{-k}
.
Write
B:=B(z,324\cdot 8^{-k})
.
Notice that
x\in B
, and

\displaystyle T_{\nu,*}(\chi_{\mathcal{S}\setminus 2B_{2}}f)(x)

\displaystyle\leq T_{\nu,*}(\chi_{2B\setminus 2B_{2}}f)(x)+T_{\nu,*}(\chi_{\mathcal{S}\setminus 2B}f)(x)

\displaystyle\leq T_{\nu,*}(\chi_{2B\setminus 2B_{2}}f)(x)+T_{\nu,*}(\chi_{\mathcal{S}\setminus 2B}f)(z)

\displaystyle\qquad+|T_{\nu,*}(\chi_{\mathcal{S}\setminus 2B}f)(x)-T_{\nu,*}(\chi_{\mathcal{S}\setminus 2B}f)(z)|.

We will bound these last three terms.
First,
the definitions of
T_{\nu,*}
and
M_{\nu}
together with (
2.2
) give

\displaystyle T_{\nu,*}(\chi_{2B\setminus 2B_{2}}f)(x)\leq C_{K}\int_{2B\setminus 2B_{2}}\frac{|f(y)|}{d(x,y)^{n}}\,d\nu(y)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{K}}{(4\cdot 8^{-k})^{n}}\int_{B(x,648\cdot 8^{-k})}|f|

\displaystyle\leq(162^{n}C_{K}C_{\nu})M_{\nu}f(x).

Moreover, since
2B
is centered at
z
and
z\notin\Omega_{\lambda}
,

T_{\nu,*}(\chi_{\mathcal{S}\setminus 2B}f)(z)\leq T_{\nu,*}f(z)\leq\lambda.

Thirdly,
since
x,z\in B
and

d(x,z)\leq 324\cdot 8^{-k}\leq d(z,y)/2

when
y\in\mathcal{S}\setminus 2B
,
we have
from (

) that

\displaystyle|T_{\nu,*}(\chi_{\mathcal{S}\setminus 2B}f)(z)

\displaystyle-T_{\nu,*}(\chi_{\mathcal{S}\setminus 2B}f)(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathcal{S}\setminus 2B}\left|K(z,y)-K(x,y)\right|\left|f(y)\right|\,d\nu(y)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e-growth-holder}}}{{\leq}}C_{K}\int_{\mathcal{S}\setminus 2B}\frac{d(x,z)^{\beta}}{d(z,y)^{n+\beta}}|f(y)|\,d\nu(y)

\displaystyle\leq C_{K}(324\cdot 8^{-k})^{\beta}\int_{\mathcal{S}\setminus 2B}\frac{|f(y)|}{d(z,y)^{n+\beta}}\,d\nu(y)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e-tech}}}{{\leq}}\frac{C_{K}C_{\nu}2^{n}}{2^{\beta}-1}M_{\nu}f(x)

where
C_{K}>0
is the constant from (
2.2
) and (
2.3
).

Combining the above estimates gives a constant
C>0

independent of
U
and

such that

T_{\nu,*}(\chi_{\mathcal{S}\setminus 2B_{2}}f)(x)\leq CM_{\nu}f(x)+\lambda+CM_{\nu}f(x)\leq\lambda+2CM_{\nu}f(x).

Choose
\delta<\varepsilon/(4C)
.
Thus, if
T_{\nu,*}f(x)>(1+\varepsilon)\lambda
and

, then

\displaystyle T_{\nu,*}(\chi_{2B_{2}}f)(x)\geq T_{\nu,*}f(x)-T_{\nu,*}(\chi_{\mathcal{S}\setminus 2B_{2}}f)(x)\geq(1+\varepsilon)\lambda-(1+2C\delta)\lambda>\varepsilon\lambda/2.

∎

Let
\varepsilon>0
and choose
\delta
as above.
Fix an index
i\in\mathcal{I}_{1}
,

,

, and

,

and

\displaystyle\nu(\{x\in G_{U}\,:\,

\displaystyle\nu(

We now prove Theorem
5.1
.

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

We will use
Lemma
5.2

to verify that
T_{\nu,*}
is bounded in
L^{p}(\nu)

in the standard manner.
Suppose
f\in L^{p}(\nu)
has compact support.
To avoid problems that arise if

\|T_{\nu,*}f\|_{L^{p}(\nu)}=\infty
,
we will truncate
T_{\nu,*}f
.
For each
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
set
g_{m}=\min\{m,T_{\nu,*}f\}
.
Since
\mathbf{supp}(f)
is compact,
\nu(\{x\in M\,:\,d(x,\mathbf{supp}(f))<1\})<\infty
.
This fact together with Lemma
5.6
below implies that
g_{m}\in L^{p}(\nu)
.

We will now prove that
\|g_{m}\|_{L^{p}(\nu)}^{p}\leq C^{\prime}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\nu)}^{p}
for a constant
C^{\prime}
independent of
m
.
Passing
m\to\infty
will then complete the proof that
T_{\nu,*}
is bounded in
L^{p}(\nu)
.
Fix
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon,\lambda>0
.
Again, write
\mathcal{S}:=\mathbf{supp}(\nu)
.
If
\lambda\geq m
, then

\{x\in\mathcal{S}:g_{m}(x)>(1+\varepsilon)\lambda\}=\emptyset.

If
\lambda<m
, then

\{T_{\nu,*}f>\lambda\}=\{g_{m}>\lambda\}
, so (
5.2
) gives

\displaystyle\nu(\{x\in\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle:g_{m}(x)>(1+\varepsilon)\lambda\})

\displaystyle\leq\nu(\{x\in\mathcal{S}:T_{\nu,*}f(x)>(1+\varepsilon)\lambda\})

\displaystyle\leq\nu(\{x\in\mathcal{S}:T_{\nu,*}f(x)>(1+\varepsilon)\lambda,\,M_{\nu}f(x)\leq\delta\lambda\})

\displaystyle\qquad+\nu(\{x\in\mathcal{S}:M_{\nu}f(x)>\delta\lambda\})

\displaystyle\leq\eta\nu(\{x\in\mathcal{S}:T_{\nu,*}f(x)>\lambda\})+\nu(\{x\in\mathcal{S}:M_{\nu}f(x)>\delta\lambda\})

\displaystyle\leq\eta\nu(\{x\in\mathcal{S}:g_{m}(x)>\lambda\})+\nu(\{x\in\mathcal{S}:M_{\nu}f(x)>\delta\lambda\}).

Multiplying this inequality by
p\lambda^{p-1}
and integrating
\lambda
from 0 to
\infty
gives

(1+\varepsilon)^{-p}\|g_{m}\|_{L^{p}(\nu)}^{p}\leq\eta\|g_{m}\|_{L^{p}(\nu)}^{p}+\delta^{-p}\|M_{\nu}f\|_{L^{p}(\nu)}^{p},

and choosing
\varepsilon
small enough allows us to conclude that

\|g_{m}\|_{L^{p}(\nu)}^{p}\leq C\|M_{\nu}f\|_{L^{p}(\nu)}^{p}\leq C^{\prime}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\nu)}^{p}

for constants
C,C^{\prime}>0
independent of
m
.
Passing
m\to\infty
completes the proof
of Theorem
5.1
.
∎

It remains to prove the following standard lemma.

Lemma 5.6
.

If
\nu
is an upper
n
-regular measure on a metric space
M
,
and if
f\in L^{p}(\nu)
for some
1<p<\infty
has compact support, then

\int_{\{x\,:\,d(x,\mathbf{supp}(f))\geq 1\}}\left|T_{\nu,*}f(x)\right|^{p}\,d\nu(x)<\infty.

Proof.

Write
Z:=\mathbf{supp}(f)
.
For each
x\in M
with
d(x,Z)\geq 1
,
set
R_{x}:=d(x,Z)/2
and
B:=B(x,R_{x})
so that
f\equiv 0
on
B
.
Then Hölder’s inequality gives

\displaystyle T_{\nu,*}f(x)\leq\int_{M\setminus B}|K(x,y)||f(y)|\,d\nu(y)

\displaystyle\leq C_{K}\int_{M\setminus B}\frac{|f(y)|}{d(x,y)^{n}}\,d\nu(y)

\displaystyle\leq C_{K}R_{x}^{-n}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\nu)}\nu(Z)^{(p-1)/p}.

For each integer
k
, write

A_{k}:=\{x\in M\,:\,2^{k}\leq d(x,Z)<2^{k+1}\}.

Note that

A_{k}
is contained in a ball of radius
2^{k+1}+\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits(Z)
.
Thus

\displaystyle C_{K}^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\nu)}^{-p}\nu(Z)^{-(p-1)}\int_{\{x\,:\,d(x,Z)\geq 1\}}

\displaystyle\left|T_{\nu,*}f(x)\right|^{p}\,d\nu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\{x\,:\,d(x,Z)\geq 1\}}\frac{2^{n}}{d(x,Z)^{np}}\,d\nu(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{A_{k}}\frac{2^{n}}{d(x,Z)^{np}}\,d\nu(x)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{n}C_{\nu}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{2^{nk+n}+\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits(Z)^{n}}{2^{knp}}<\infty.

∎

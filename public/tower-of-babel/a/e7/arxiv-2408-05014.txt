I.1.1
1.1.1 Transversality of waves

We start from the wave equation:

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}\textbf{E}-\nabla(\nabla\cdot\textbf{E})+k_{0}^{2}\varepsilon(\bm{\rho},z)\textbf{E}=0,

(S.1)

where the effective dielectric constant
\varepsilon(\bm{\rho},z)=\varepsilon_{0}(\bm{\rho},z)+\varepsilon_{1}(\bm{\rho},z)
and

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{0}(\bm{\rho},z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1,\mbox{~~~~for~}z>h(\bm{\rho})\\
\varepsilon,\mbox{~~~~for~}z<h(\bm{\rho})\end{array}\right.

together with

\varepsilon_{1}(\bm{\rho},z)=[\varepsilon-1]\times\delta(z-h(\bm{\rho}))\times h(\bm{\rho}).

(S.4)

are already shifted to the actual boundary
[
1
,
2
]
, where we denote
\varepsilon(\omega)\equiv\varepsilon
for brevity (see in
[
3
]
).

First, consider the case of the incident wave with p-polarization. We will show that only in this case the reflection coefficients become quite sensitive to the change of the system parameters, which opens a perspective for these effects in terms of a practical usage. The strength of the electric component of the reference field is

\textbf{E}_{0p}=\textbf{e}_{x}E_{0p,x}+\textbf{e}_{z}E_{0p,z}
, where

\displaystyle E_{0p,x}({\bf r})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\cos\theta\times(e^{ik_{x}x-ik_{z}z}-r_{p}e^{ik_{x}x+ik_{z}z}),\\
\mbox{~for~}z>h(x,y),\\
\\
t_{p}\cos\theta_{t}\times e^{ik_{x}x+ik_{-}z},\\
\mbox{\qquad\qquad for~}z<h(x,y),\end{array}\right.

and

\displaystyle E_{0p,z}({\bf r})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\sin\theta\times(e^{ik_{x}x-ik_{z}z}+r_{p}e^{ik_{x}x+ik_{z}z}),\\
\mbox{~for~}z>h(x,y),\\
\\
t_{p}\sin\theta_{t}\times e^{ik_{x}x+ik_{-}z},\\
\mbox{\qquad\qquad for~}z<h(x,y),\end{array}\right.

and
\sin\theta_{t}=\sin\theta/\sqrt{\varepsilon}
,
\cos\theta_{t}=\sqrt{1-\sin^{2}\theta/\varepsilon}
,
\theta_{t}
is the transmission angle and is generally complex and
\penalty k_{-}=-k_{0}\sqrt{\varepsilon-\sin^{2}\theta}
,
k_{x}=k_{0}\sin\theta
,
k_{z}=k_{0}\cos\theta
and
r_{p}
and
t_{p}
are the reflection and transmission coefficients from a smooth surface for a p-polarized wave:

\displaystyle r_{p}=\frac{\varepsilon\cos\theta-\sqrt{\varepsilon-\sin^{2}\theta}}{\varepsilon\cos\theta+\sqrt{\varepsilon-\sin^{2}\theta}},

\displaystyle t_{p}=\frac{2\sqrt{\varepsilon}\cos\theta}{\varepsilon\cos\theta+\sqrt{\varepsilon-\sin^{2}\theta}}.

(S.15)

The perturbation theory gives the solution of the problem in a form
\textbf{E}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\textbf{E}_{s}^{(n)}
, where
\textbf{E}_{s}^{(0)}=\textbf{E}_{0}
is the reference field and

\displaystyle\textbf{E}_{s}^{(n+1)}({\bf r})=-\frac{k_{0}^{2}}{4\pi}\int d\textbf{r}^{\prime}\textbf{D}_{0}({\bf r},{\bf r}^{\prime})\textbf{E}_{s}^{(n)}({\bf r}^{\prime})\varepsilon_{1}({\bf r}^{\prime})

(S.16)

for any
n=0,1,2,...
, where

\displaystyle\textbf{D}_{0}({\bf r},{\bf r}^{\prime})=\int\frac{d\textbf{q}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\textbf{d}(\textbf{q},z,z^{\prime})e^{i\textbf{q}(\bm{\rho}-\bm{\rho}^{\prime})}

(S.17)

is the dyadic Green’s function for the case of the flat interface with

\displaystyle\textbf{d}(\textbf{q},z,z^{\prime})=

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q^{2}}\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}q_{x}^{2}g_{xx}+q_{y}^{2}g_{yy}&q_{x}q_{y}(g_{xx}-g_{yy})&qq_{x}g_{xz}\\
q_{x}q_{y}(g_{xx}-g_{yy})&q_{y}^{2}g_{xx}+q_{x}^{2}g_{yy}&qq_{y}g_{xz}\\
qq_{x}g_{zx}&qq_{y}g_{zx}&q^{2}g_{zz}\end{array}\right]_{(\textbf{q},z,z^{\prime})}.

The total electric field in the vacuum (
z>h(\bm{\rho})
) can be decomposed into the incident and scattered parts:
\textbf{E}=\textbf{E}_{inc}+\textbf{E}_{sc}
. For p-polarized incident wave we have

\displaystyle\textbf{E}_{inc}(\bm{\rho},z)=(\textbf{e}_{x}\cos\theta+\textbf{e}_{z}\sin\theta)\times e^{ik_{x}x-ik_{z}z}

(S.21)

and

\displaystyle\textbf{E}_{sc}(\bm{\rho},z)=r_{p}\times(-\textbf{e}_{x}\cos\theta+\textbf{e}_{z}\sin\theta)\times e^{ik_{x}x+ik_{z}z}+

\displaystyle+\left(-\frac{k_{0}^{2}}{4\pi}\right)\int d\textbf{r}^{\prime}\textbf{D}_{0}({\bf r},{\bf r}^{\prime})\textbf{E}_{p}({\bf r}^{\prime})\varepsilon_{1}({\bf r}^{\prime}),

(S.22)

where we used
{\bf r}=\bm{\rho}+\textbf{e}_{z}z
. The second term on the r.h.s. of Eq.(
S.22
) also describes a transversal wave. To prove this we note that 1) in practice the observation point is always above the rough surface i.e. it is enough to consider the case
z>max[h(\bm{\rho})]
and 2) because of
\varepsilon_{1}
the integration over
z^{\prime}
reduces to the rough interface. Thus, we can consider the case
z>z^{\prime}
, where we have the relations

\displaystyle g_{z\beta}=-\frac{q}{k(q)}g_{x\beta}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial z}g_{\alpha\beta}=ik(q)g_{\alpha\beta}

(S.23)

for
\alpha,\beta=x
or
z
, derived from their definitions in
[
4
]
. Now, to prove the transversality of
\textbf{E}_{sc}
, we need to consider its divergence:

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\textbf{E}_{sc}=-i\frac{k_{0}^{2}}{4\pi}\int d{\bf r}^{\prime}\int\frac{d\bm{q}}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{i\bm{q}(\bm{\rho}-\bm{\rho}^{\prime})}\times

\displaystyle\times(\bm{q}+k(q)\textbf{e}_{z})^{T}\textbf{d}({\bf q},z,z^{\prime})\textbf{E}_{p}({\bf r^{\prime}})\varepsilon_{1}({\bf r^{\prime}})

(S.24)

and, specifically, the dot product

\displaystyle(\bm{q}+k(q)\textbf{e}_{z})^{T}\textbf{d}({\bf q},z,z^{\prime})=\frac{1}{q^{2}}(q_{x},q_{y},k(q))\cdot

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}q_{x}^{2}g_{xx}+q_{y}^{2}g_{yy}&q_{x}q_{y}(g_{xx}-g_{yy})&qq_{x}g_{xz}\\
q_{x}q_{y}(g_{xx}-g_{yy})&q_{y}^{2}g_{xx}+q_{x}^{2}g_{yy}&qq_{y}g_{xz}\\
qq_{x}g_{zx}&qq_{y}g_{zx}&q^{2}g_{zz}\end{array}\right)_{(\textbf{q},z,z^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=(0,0,0)

(S.28)

which proves that
\nabla\cdot\textbf{E}_{sc}=0
or the transversality of the scattered wave.

I.1.2
1.1.2 Further derivation

This makes available writing the scattered wave in a form

\displaystyle\textbf{E}_{sc}(\bm{\rho},z)=\int\frac{d\bm{q}}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{i\bm{q}\bm{\rho}+ik(q)z}

\displaystyle\left[\frac{(\hat{\bm{q}}k(q)-\textbf{e}_{z}q)}{k_{0}}A_{p}(\bm{q})+(\textbf{e}_{z}\times\hat{\bm{q}})A_{s}(\bm{q})\right]

(S.29)

with scattered p- and s-polarized wave amplitudes

\displaystyle A_{p}(\bm{q})=-r_{p}(2\pi)^{2}\delta(\bm{q}-k_{x}\textbf{e}_{x})-\frac{k_{0}^{2}}{4\pi}\int d{\bf r}^{\prime}e^{-i\bm{q}\bm{\rho}^{\prime}}\times

\displaystyle\times\frac{k_{0}}{k(q)}(\hat{q}_{x}g_{xx}^{0+},\hat{q}_{y}g_{xx}^{0+},g_{xz}^{0+})\cdot\textbf{E}_{p}({\bf r^{\prime}})\varepsilon_{1}({\bf r^{\prime}}),

\displaystyle A_{s}(\bm{q})=-\frac{k_{0}^{2}}{4\pi}\int d{\bf r}^{\prime}e^{-i\bm{q}\bm{\rho}^{\prime}}\times

\displaystyle\times\frac{k_{0}}{k(q)}(-\hat{q}_{y}g_{yy}^{0+},\hat{q}_{x}g_{yy}^{0+},0)\cdot\textbf{E}_{p}({\bf r^{\prime}})\varepsilon_{1}({\bf r^{\prime}})

(S.30)

, where we introduced the notation

\displaystyle g_{\alpha\beta}^{0+}(\bm{q},z^{\prime})=e^{-ik(q)z}g_{\alpha\beta}(\bm{q},z,z^{\prime}).

(S.31)

According to
[
5
]
we can write

\displaystyle A_{\alpha}(\bm{q})=\sum_{\beta=p,s}R_{\alpha\beta}(\bm{q}|\textbf{e}_{x}k_{x})B_{\beta}(\textbf{e}_{x}k_{x})=

\displaystyle=-R_{\alpha p}(\bm{q}|\textbf{e}_{x}k_{x}),

(S.32)

since
B_{s}=0
and
B_{p}=-1
for p-polarized incidence. Thus, finally, we can write the reflection coefficients as

\displaystyle R_{pp}(\bm{q}|\textbf{e}_{x}k_{x})=r_{p}(2\pi)^{2}\delta(\bm{q}-k_{x}\textbf{e}_{x})+\frac{k_{0}^{2}}{4\pi}\int d{\bf r}^{\prime}e^{-i\bm{q}\bm{\rho}^{\prime}}\times

\displaystyle\times\frac{k_{0}}{k(q)}(\hat{q}_{x}g_{xx}^{0+},\hat{q}_{y}g_{xx}^{0+},g_{xz}^{0+})\cdot\textbf{E}_{p}({\bf r^{\prime}})\varepsilon_{1}({\bf r^{\prime}}),

\displaystyle R_{sp}(\bm{q}|\textbf{e}_{x}k_{x})=\frac{k_{0}^{2}}{4\pi}\int d{\bf r}^{\prime}e^{-i\bm{q}\bm{\rho}^{\prime}}\times

\displaystyle\times\frac{k_{0}}{k(q)}(-\hat{q}_{y}g_{yy}^{0+},\hat{q}_{x}g_{yy}^{0+},0)\cdot\textbf{E}_{p}({\bf r^{\prime}})\varepsilon_{1}({\bf r^{\prime}}).

(S.33)

The differential reflection coefficient

\displaystyle\frac{\partial R_{\alpha\beta}}{\partial\Omega_{s}}=\frac{1}{S_{0}}\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\frac{\cos^{2}\theta_{s}}{\cos\theta}|R_{\alpha\beta}(\bm{q}|\textbf{e}_{x}k_{x})|^{2},

(S.34)

where
\bm{q}=k_{0}\sin\theta_{s}(\textbf{e}_{x}\cos\phi_{s}+\textbf{e}_{y}\sin\phi_{s})
, now, can be represented as a sum of specular and diffuse reflection parts. Indeed, the square of the absolute value of each reflection coefficient in Eq.(
S.33
)

\displaystyle|R_{pp}(\bm{q}|\textbf{e}_{x}k_{x})|^{2}=\left[|r_{p}|^{2}+\frac{k_{0}^{2}}{2\pi S_{0}}\times\right.

\displaystyle\left.\times Re\left(\frac{r_{p}^{*}}{\cos\theta}\int d{\bf r}^{\prime}e^{-ik_{x}x^{\prime}}(g_{xx}^{0+},0,g_{xz}^{0+})|_{(\bm{e}_{x}k_{x},z^{\prime})}\cdot\textbf{E}_{p}({\bf r^{\prime}})\varepsilon_{1}({\bf r^{\prime}})\right)\right]\times

\displaystyle\times S_{0}(2\pi)^{2}\delta(\bm{q}-\textbf{e}_{x}k_{x})+\left(\frac{k_{0}^{2}}{4\pi}\right)^{2}\left|\int d{\bf r}^{\prime}e^{-i\bm{q}\bm{\rho}^{\prime}}\times\right.

\displaystyle\left.\times\frac{k_{0}}{k(q)}(\hat{q}_{x}g_{xx}^{0+},\hat{q}_{y}g_{xx}^{0+},g_{xz}^{0+})\cdot\textbf{E}_{p}({\bf r^{\prime}})\varepsilon_{1}({\bf r^{\prime}})\right|^{2},

\displaystyle|R_{sp}(\bm{q}|\textbf{e}_{x}k_{x})|^{2}=\left(\frac{k_{0}^{2}}{4\pi}\right)^{2}\left|\int d{\bf r}^{\prime}e^{-i\bm{q}\bm{\rho}^{\prime}}\times\right.

\displaystyle\left.\times\frac{k_{0}}{k(q)}(-\hat{q}_{y}g_{yy}^{0+},\hat{q}_{x}g_{yy}^{0+},0)\cdot\textbf{E}_{p}({\bf r^{\prime}})\varepsilon_{1}({\bf r^{\prime}})\right|^{2}

(S.35)

consists of two summands: with
\delta
-function (which is the specular part) and without it (which is the diffuse part). This can be written as
|R_{\alpha\beta}(\bm{q}|\textbf{e}_{x}k_{x})|^{2}=|R_{\alpha\beta}(\bm{q}|\textbf{e}_{x}k_{x})|^{2}_{spec}+|R_{\alpha\beta}(\bm{q}|\textbf{e}_{x}k_{x})|^{2}_{diff}
. It is important to derive the expressions for the integral reflection coefficients:

\displaystyle{\bf R}_{\alpha\beta}=\int d\Omega_{s}\frac{\partial R_{\alpha\beta}}{\partial\Omega_{s}}=

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{S_{0}\cos\theta}\int_{q<k_{0}}\frac{d\bm{q}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{k(q)}{k_{0}}|R_{\alpha\beta}(\bm{q}|\textbf{e}_{x}k_{x})|^{2}

(S.36)

This quantity can also be represented as a sum of the scattering and the diffuse parts, which can be written as follows:
{\bf R}_{\alpha\beta}={\bf R}_{\alpha}^{spec}\delta_{\alpha\beta}+{\bf R}_{\alpha\beta}^{diff}
, where we used the fact (which we will prove later also in the case of incident s-polarization or
\beta=s
), that the off-diagonal elements in the specular part of the reflection matrix
||{\bf R}_{\alpha\beta}||
vanish. In our case

\displaystyle{\bf R}_{p}^{spec}=|r_{p}|^{2}+\frac{k_{0}^{2}}{2\pi S_{0}}Re\left(r_{p}^{*}\frac{1}{\cos\theta}\int d{\bf r}^{\prime}e^{-ik_{x}x^{\prime}}\times\right.

\displaystyle\left.\times(g_{xx}^{0+},0,g_{xz}^{0+})|_{(\bm{e}_{x}k_{x},z^{\prime})}\cdot\textbf{E}_{p}({\bf r^{\prime}})\varepsilon_{1}({\bf r^{\prime}})\right),

\displaystyle{\bf R}_{pp}^{diff}=\left(\frac{k_{0}^{2}}{4\pi}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{S_{0}\cos\theta}\int_{q<k_{0}}\frac{d\bm{q}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{k(q)}{k_{0}}\left|\int d{\bf r}^{\prime}e^{-i\bm{q}\bm{\rho}^{\prime}}\frac{k_{0}}{k(q)}\times\right.

\displaystyle\left.\times(\hat{q}_{x}g_{xx}^{0+},\hat{q}_{y}g_{xx}^{0+},g_{xz}^{0+})\cdot\textbf{E}_{p}({\bf r^{\prime}})\varepsilon_{1}({\bf r^{\prime}})\right|^{2},

\displaystyle{\bf R}_{sp}^{diff}={\bf R}_{sp}=\left(\frac{k_{0}^{2}}{4\pi}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{S_{0}\cos\theta}\int_{q<k_{0}}\frac{d\bm{q}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{k(q)}{k_{0}}\times

\displaystyle\times\left|\int d{\bf r}^{\prime}e^{-i\bm{q}\bm{\rho}^{\prime}}\frac{k_{0}}{k(q)}(-\hat{q}_{y}g_{yy}^{0+},\hat{q}_{x}g_{yy}^{0+},0)\cdot\textbf{E}_{p}({\bf r^{\prime}})\varepsilon_{1}({\bf r^{\prime}})\right|^{2}.

(S.37)

However, in practice, we need to consider quantities that are averaged over the ensemble of realization of the surface profile
h(\bm{\rho})
with correlation function
\left<h(\bm{\rho})h(\bm{\rho}^{\prime})\right>=\delta^{2}e^{-(\bm{\rho}-\bm{\rho}^{\prime})^{2}/2a^{2}}
:

\displaystyle\left<\frac{\partial R_{\alpha\beta}}{\partial\Omega_{s}}\right>=\frac{1}{S_{0}}\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\frac{\cos^{2}\theta_{s}}{\cos\theta}\left<|R_{\alpha\beta}(\bm{q}|\textbf{e}_{x}k_{x})|^{2}\right>.

(S.38)

Moreover, dividing the quantities into the coherent and incoherent parts can also be useful. For the differential reflection coefficient, this can be done as follows:

\displaystyle\left<\frac{\partial R_{\alpha\beta}}{\partial\Omega_{s}}\right>_{coh}=\frac{1}{S_{0}}\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\frac{\cos^{2}\theta_{s}}{\cos\theta}|\left<R_{\alpha\beta}(\bm{q}|\textbf{e}_{x}k_{x})\right>|^{2}

\displaystyle\left<\frac{\partial R_{\alpha\beta}}{\partial\Omega_{s}}\right>_{incoh}=\frac{1}{S_{0}}\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\frac{\cos^{2}\theta_{s}}{\cos\theta}\times

\displaystyle\times\left[\left<|R_{\alpha\beta}(\bm{q}|\textbf{e}_{x}k_{x})|^{2}\right>-|\left<R_{\alpha\beta}(\bm{q}|\textbf{e}_{x}k_{x})\right>|^{2}\right].

(S.39)

We will show that, within the framework of second-order perturbation theory, the coherent and incoherent scattering parts correspond to the specular and diffuse scattering respectively.

To proceed, we need to make assumptions regarding any given magnitude on the rough interface. Typically, this magnitude is assumed to be equal to the mean of the respective limiting values on both sides of the interface. However, if the magnitudes on both sides are of the same order, we can instead use the limiting value from either side. Mathematically, this can be expressed as:

\varepsilon_{1}(\bm{\rho},z)=[\varepsilon-1]\times\delta(z-h^{-}(\bm{\rho}))\times h(\bm{\rho}).

(S.40)

First consider the diffuse part.

Substituting Eq. (
S.40
) into Eq. (
S.35
), and then averaging, followed by the use of the identity

\displaystyle\int d\bm{\rho}^{\prime}\int d\bm{\rho}^{\prime\prime}\left<h(\bm{\rho}^{\prime})h(\bm{\rho}^{\prime\prime})\right>e^{i\bm{q}(\bm{\rho}^{\prime}-\bm{\rho}^{\prime\prime})}=

\displaystyle=2\pi S_{0}a^{2}\delta^{2}exp\left(-\frac{q^{2}a^{2}}{2}\right)

(S.41)

we get

\displaystyle\left<|R_{pp}(\bm{q}|\textbf{e}_{x}k_{x})|^{2}_{diff}\right>=

\displaystyle=2\pi S_{0}\frac{|\varepsilon-1|^{2}}{\cos^{2}\theta_{s}}\delta^{2}|t_{p}|^{2}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\beta^{2}(\sin^{2}\theta+\sin^{2}\theta_{s})}e^{\beta^{2}\sin\theta\sin\theta_{s}\cos\phi_{s}}\times

\displaystyle\times\left|G_{xx}^{(+-)}(r=\beta\sin\theta_{s})\sqrt{1-\frac{\sin^{2}\theta}{\varepsilon}}\cos\phi_{s}+\right.

\displaystyle\left.G_{xz}^{(+-)}(r=\beta\sin\theta_{s})\frac{\sin\theta}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\right|^{2},

\displaystyle\left<|R_{sp}(\bm{q}|\textbf{e}_{x}k_{x})|^{2}_{diff}\right>=\left<|R_{sp}(\bm{q}|\textbf{e}_{x}k_{x})|^{2}\right>=

\displaystyle=2\pi S_{0}|\varepsilon-1|^{2}\delta^{2}|t_{p}|^{2}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\beta^{2}(\sin^{2}\theta+\sin^{2}\theta_{s})}e^{\beta^{2}\sin\theta\sin\theta_{s}\cos\phi_{s}}\times

\displaystyle\times\left|G_{yy}^{(+-)}(r=\beta\sin\theta_{s})\sqrt{1-\frac{\sin^{2}\theta}{\varepsilon}}\sin\phi_{s}\right|^{2},

(S.42)

where
\beta=k_{0}a
and

\displaystyle G_{xx}^{(+-)}(r)=\frac{\sqrt{\varepsilon\beta^{2}-r^{2}}\sqrt{\beta^{2}-r^{2}}}{\varepsilon\sqrt{\beta^{2}-r^{2}}+\sqrt{\varepsilon\beta^{2}-r^{2}}}

\displaystyle G_{xz}^{(+-)}(r)=-\frac{r\sqrt{\beta^{2}-r^{2}}}{\varepsilon\sqrt{\beta^{2}-r^{2}}+\sqrt{\varepsilon\beta^{2}-r^{2}}}

\displaystyle G_{yy}^{(+-)}(r)=\frac{\beta^{2}}{\sqrt{\beta^{2}-r^{2}}+\sqrt{\varepsilon\beta^{2}-r^{2}}}

(S.43)

Note that averaging over ensembles of realizations of nanorough surfaces, due to the smallness of
\delta
, allows us to neglect terms of order
O(\delta^{4})
. Therefore, in this case, we only consider terms of order
\delta^{2}
, which arise from the multiplication of two
h
terms inside each
\varepsilon_{1}
in the square of the absolute value of the integration function.

This leads to the expression for the differential diffuse reflection coefficient:

\displaystyle\left<\left(\frac{\partial R_{pp}}{\partial\Omega_{s}}\right)_{diff}\right>=

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi\delta^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\frac{|\varepsilon-1|^{2}}{\cos\theta}|t_{p}|^{2}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\beta^{2}(\sin^{2}\theta+\sin^{2}\theta_{s})}e^{\beta^{2}\sin\theta\sin\theta_{s}\cos\phi_{s}}\times

\displaystyle\times\left|G_{xx}^{(+-)}(r=\beta\sin\theta_{s})\sqrt{1-\frac{\sin^{2}\theta}{\varepsilon}}\cos\phi_{s}+\right.

\displaystyle\left.+G_{xz}^{(+-)}(r=\beta\sin\theta_{s})\frac{\sin\theta}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\right|^{2},

\displaystyle\left<\left(\frac{\partial R_{sp}}{\partial\Omega_{s}}\right)_{diff}\right>=\left<\left(\frac{\partial R_{sp}}{\partial\Omega_{s}}\right)\right>=

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi\delta^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\frac{\cos^{2}\theta_{s}}{\cos\theta}|\varepsilon-1|^{2}|t_{p}|^{2}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\beta^{2}(\sin^{2}\theta+\sin^{2}\theta_{s})}e^{\beta^{2}\sin\theta\sin\theta_{s}\cos\phi_{s}}\times

\displaystyle\times\left|G_{yy}^{(+-)}(r=\beta\sin\theta_{s})\sqrt{1-\frac{\sin^{2}\theta}{\varepsilon}}\sin\phi_{s}\right|^{2}.

(S.44)

To obtain the integral diffuse reflection coefficient, we integrate Eq. (
S.44
) over the entire upper hemisphere. We can simplify the integration over the azimuthal angle
\phi_{s}
using the definition of the modified Bessel function of order
n
:

I_{n}(x)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi_{s}}{2\pi}e^{x\cos\phi_{s}\pm in\phi_{s}}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi_{s}}{2\pi}e^{x\cos\phi_{s}}\cos(n\phi_{s}).

(S.45)

It is possible to change the integration variable and use
r=qa=\beta\sin\theta_{s}
instead of
\theta_{s}
:

\displaystyle\left<{\bf R}_{pp}^{diff}\right>=\frac{|\varepsilon-1|^{2}}{\cos\theta}\left(\frac{\delta}{a}\right)^{2}|t_{p}|^{2}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\beta^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}\times

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{\beta}drr\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{\beta^{2}-r^{2}}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}r^{2}}\times

\displaystyle\times\left[|G_{xx}^{(+-)}(r)|^{2}\cdot\left|1-\frac{\sin^{2}\theta}{\varepsilon}\right|\cdot\frac{I_{0}+I_{2}}{2}\Bigg|_{x=r\beta\sin\theta}+\right.

\displaystyle\left.+2Re\left(\left[G_{xx}^{(+-)}(r)\sqrt{1-\frac{\sin^{2}\theta}{\varepsilon}}\text{ }\right]^{*}G_{xz}^{(+-)}(r)\frac{\sin\theta}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\right)\times\right.

\displaystyle\left.\times I_{1}(r\beta\sin\theta)+|G_{xz}^{(+-)}(r)|^{2}\frac{\sin^{2}\theta}{|\varepsilon|}I_{0}(r\beta\sin\theta)\right],

\displaystyle\left<{\bf R}_{sp}^{diff}\right>=\left<{\bf R}_{sp}\right>=

\displaystyle=\frac{|\varepsilon-1|^{2}}{\cos\theta}\left(\frac{\delta}{a}\right)^{2}|t_{p}|^{2}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\beta^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}\times

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{\beta}drr\frac{\sqrt{\beta^{2}-r^{2}}}{\beta}e^{-\frac{1}{2}r^{2}}|G_{yy}^{(+-)}(r)|^{2}\frac{I_{0}-I_{2}}{2}\Bigg|_{x=r\beta\sin\theta},

(S.46)

where the argument for each
I_{n}
(
n=0,1,2
) is
x=r\beta\sin\theta
. This form is more convenient for making the approximations described in the following sections.

Now, consider the specular part. Here, calculations become slightly more complicated because we need to expand Taylor series in powers of
h
for the function inside the integral. For the specular reflection coefficient in Eq. (
S.37
), we can express it as:

\displaystyle\left<{\bf R}_{p}^{spec}\right>={\bf R}_{p,0}^{spec}+{\bf R}_{p,i1}^{spec}+{\bf R}_{p,i2}^{spec},

\displaystyle{\bf R}_{p,0}^{spec}=|r_{p}|^{2},

\displaystyle{\bf R}_{p,i1}^{spec}=\frac{k_{0}^{2}}{2\pi S_{0}}Re\left(r_{p}^{*}\frac{1}{\cos\theta}\left<\int d{\bf r}^{\prime}e^{-ik_{x}x^{\prime}}(g_{xx}^{0+},0,g_{xz}^{0+})|_{(\bm{e}_{x}k_{x},z^{\prime})}\cdot\textbf{E}_{0p}({\bf r^{\prime}})\varepsilon_{1}({\bf r^{\prime}})\right>\right),

\displaystyle{\bf R}_{p,i2}^{spec}=\frac{k_{0}^{2}}{2\pi S_{0}}Re\left(r_{p}^{*}\frac{1}{\cos\theta}\left<\int d{\bf r}^{\prime}e^{-ik_{x}x^{\prime}}(g_{xx}^{0+},0,g_{xz}^{0+})|_{(\bm{e}_{x}k_{x},z^{\prime})}\cdot\textbf{E}_{s,p}^{(1)}({\bf r^{\prime}})\varepsilon_{1}({\bf r^{\prime}})\right>\right)

(S.47)

, where
{\bf R}_{p,0}^{spec}
is the reflection coefficient in case of the absence of the roughness and
{\bf R}_{p,i1}^{spec}
and
{\bf R}_{p,i2}^{spec}
are corrections, contributed by the interference terms. Since for
z^{\prime}
inside the medium all fields and Green’s functions are
\sim e^{ik_{-}z^{\prime}}
, we get for the first interference term a multiplier
2ik_{-}
:

\displaystyle{\bf R}_{p,i1}^{spec}=-4k_{0}\frac{\delta^{2}}{a}Re\left[r_{p}^{*}t_{p}\frac{(\varepsilon-1)}{\cos\theta}\sqrt{\varepsilon-\sin^{2}\theta}\left(G_{xx}^{(+-)}(r=\beta\sin\theta)\sqrt{1-\frac{\sin^{2}\theta}{\varepsilon}}+G_{xz}^{(+-)}(r=\beta\sin\theta)\frac{\sin\theta}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\right)\right]

(S.48)

The second interference term will not contain any derivatives over
z^{\prime}
and will result in a form:

\displaystyle{\bf R}_{p,i2}^{spec}=2\left(\frac{\delta}{a}\right)^{2}Im\left[r_{p}^{*}t_{p}\frac{(\varepsilon-1)^{2}}{\cos\theta}(G_{xx}^{(+-)},0,G_{xz}^{(+-)})|_{r=\beta\sin\theta}\cdot\hat{I}^{(2D)}(\beta,\theta)\cdot\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\sqrt{1-\sin^{2}\theta/\varepsilon}\\
0\\
\sin\theta/\sqrt{\varepsilon}\end{array}\right)\right]

I.1.3
1.1.3 Equivalence between specular/diffuse divisions and coherent/incoherent divisions

Finally, we refer to an important property common to all quantities derived above, crucial for the comparative analysis between the current theory and the older one. We are going to prove that both division methods to specular and diffuse parts and to coherent and incoherent parts are equivalent. The elements of a scattering matrix can be written in a general form as

\displaystyle R_{\alpha\beta}=r_{\alpha}\delta_{\alpha\beta}(2\pi)^{2}\delta(\bm{q}-\textbf{e}_{x}k_{x})+Q_{\alpha\beta}

(S.62)

, where we omitted the explicit
\bm{q}
-dependence of
Q_{\alpha\beta}
for simplicity. From this, we can distinguish between the specular and diffuse parts by considering the square of its absolute value:

\displaystyle|R_{\alpha\beta}|^{2}=\left[|r_{\alpha}|^{2}S_{0}+2Re(r_{\alpha}^{*}Q_{\alpha\beta})\right]\delta_{\alpha\beta}(2\pi)^{2}\delta(\bm{q}-\textbf{e}_{x}k_{x})+

\displaystyle+|Q_{\alpha\beta}|^{2},

(S.63)

where we used
S_{0}=(2\pi)^{2}\delta(\bm{0})
. Since
r_{\alpha}
is roughness-independent, the averaging over the ensemble gives

\displaystyle\left<|R_{\alpha\beta}|^{2}\right>=\left[|r_{\alpha}|^{2}S_{0}+2Re(r_{\alpha}^{*}\left<Q_{\alpha\beta}\right>)\right]\delta_{\alpha\beta}(2\pi)^{2}\delta(\bm{q}-\textbf{e}_{x}k_{x})+

\displaystyle+\left<|Q_{\alpha\beta}|^{2}\right>

(S.64)

On the other hand, if we first average both sides of Eq. (
S.62
) and then calculate the square of the absolute value of the result, we obtain

\displaystyle|\left<R_{\alpha\beta}\right>|^{2}=

\displaystyle=\left[|r_{\alpha}|^{2}S_{0}+2Re(r_{\alpha}^{*}\left<Q_{\alpha\beta}\right>)\right]\delta_{\alpha\beta}(2\pi)^{2}\delta(\bm{q}-\textbf{e}_{x}k_{x}).

Here, we neglected all terms
O(\delta^{4})
arising from
|\left<Q_{\alpha\beta}\right>|^{2}
due to the problem’s setup. This demonstrates that for all reflection coefficients, the coherent part is simultaneously its specular part, and consequently, the incoherent part is its diffuse part.

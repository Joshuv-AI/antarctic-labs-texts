Discrete-to-continuum limits of semilinear stochastic evolution equations in Banach spaces
Yves van Gennip and Jonas Latz and Joshua Willems

Abstract
. We study the convergence of semilinear parabolic stochastic evolution equations, posed on a sequence of Banach spaces approximating a limiting space and driven by additive white noise projected onto the former spaces. Under appropriate uniformity and convergence conditions on the linear operators, nonlinear drifts and initial data, we establish convergence of the associated mild solution processes when lifted to a common state space. Our framework is applied to the case where the limiting problem is a stochastic partial differential equation whose linear part is a generalized Whittle–Matérn operator on a manifold ℳ \mathcal{M} , discretized by a sequence of graphs constructed from a (random) point cloud. In this setting we obtain various discrete-to-continuum convergence results for solutions lifted to L q ​ ( ℳ ) L^{q}(\mathcal{M}) for q ∈ [ 2 , ∞ ] q\in[2,\infty] , one of which recovers the L ∞ L^{\infty} -convergence of a finite-difference discretization of certain (fractional) stochastic Allen–Cahn equations.

Introduction

1.1.
Background and motivation

We establish discrete-to-continuum limits of stochastic evolution equations of the form (
1.1
), i.e., semilinear parabolic stochastic partial diffential equations (SPDEs) driven by Gaussian white noise.
Such SPDEs of evolution play an important role in the modeling of physical and other systems, such as fluid dynamics
[
5
,
26
,
31
,
62
]
, quantum optics
[
13
]
, phase separation
[
18
]
, diffusion in random media
[
44
,
53
]
, and population dynamics
[
87
]
. Given their significance, there has been a considerable interest in the analysis and numerical analysis of SPDEs; see the introductory textbooks
[
60
]
and
[
61
]
, respectively.

We consider the convergence of a sequence of abstract continuous-time equations, each posed on a different Banach space in order to model the approximation of an evolution SPDE by equations that are continuous in time and discrete in space.
This framework covers a typical setting where the spatial domains are finite graphs and the limiting differential operator in space is approximated by the corresponding graphical variants.
If the finite graphs approximate an underlying manifold, then the graphical differential operators are related to a finite-difference approximation of the differential operator.
As an example, we study a class of semilinear SPDEs whose linear part is given by a fractional-order elliptic differential operator on a manifold (known as a
Whittle–Matérn operator
), discretized by a sequence of graphs constructed from a (possibly randomly sampled) cloud of points.

Linear
equations of this type have previously been studied in the context of statistics and machine learning
[
65
,
71
,
72
]
.
By verifying in detail that the hypotheses of our abstract framework are satisfied in this situation, we establish the discrete-to-continuum convergence of this scheme.
Although the main advantage of our general results is their applicability to highly unstructured discretizations, we additionally show that they recover the
L^{\infty}
-convergence of finite-difference discretizations of the fractional stochastic Allen–Cahn equation on the one-dimensional flat torus.
To further illustrate the significance of results of this form, we now discuss a few examples of semidiscrete SPDEs as well as their continuum limits.

Semidiscrete models appear frequently in the numerical analysis of (S)PDEs of evolution; since the challenges of spatial, temporal and spatiotemporal discretization are different, these settings are often analyzed separately. This leads to thorough studies of (S)PDEs that are discretized in space but not in time. In the context of SPDEs of evolution, we refer to
[
8
,
40
,
54
]
for examples.

Stochastic PDEs on graphs also appear naturally as models in the physical sciences, e.g., for interacting particle systems
[
17
]
or the representation of disordered media
[
44
]
. In the former case, the continuum limit represents the large particle limit in the interacting particle system.

In the data science literature, (S)PDEs on graphs have recently gained popularity as semi-supervised learning techniques. In a semi-supervised learning problem we are given a set of labeled features as well as a set of unlabeled features, and the goal is to use the former features to recover the labels of the latter. Features are, for example, images, text, or voice recordings; corresponding labels may be descriptors of the content of the images, the author of the text, or a transcript of the voice recording, respectively. Given an appropriate similarity measure on the space of features, an edge-weighted graph can be constructed in which nodes representing similar features are connected by highly weighted edges. The unknown labels can then be estimated by space-discretized PDEs on this graph, as in
[
6
,
10
,
79
,
89
]
. The PDEs often describe gradient flows that minimize a variational functional.
Stochastic PDEs appear in this setting if, in addition to finding an estimate for the labels, the uncertainty in the labels is to be quantified as well
[
7
,
71
]
.
The SPDEs of evolution here either form the basis of Markov chain Monte Carlo sampling algorithms
[
16
,
42
,
43
,
69
]
or of a randomized global optimization scheme
[
14
,
15
]
for the solution of the variational problem in the deterministic setting.
In this semi-supervised learning setting, discrete-to-continuum limits are of interest because they establish the consistency of the models in the large-data limit. For deterministic PDEs, the literature has grown to encompass pointwise limits of operators, as in
[
45
]
,
\Gamma
-limits of the functionals that underlie the dynamics
[
33
,
58
,
76
,
78
,
85
]
, and more recently, discrete-to-continuum limits for the dynamics themselves
[
27
,
30
,
36
,
47
,
59
,
63
,
84
]
. For a more in-depth overview of the literature of discrete-to-continuum limits, we refer to
[
80
]
.

2.2.
Discrete-to-continuum Trotter–Kato approximation theorem

We encode the discrete-to-continuum setting in the following way:

Assumption 2.1
.

Let
(E_{n},\|\,\cdot\,\|_{E_{n}})_{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}
and
(\widetilde{E},\|\,\cdot\,\|_{\widetilde{E}})
be real or complex Banach spaces and suppose that
E_{\infty}
is a closed linear subspace of
\widetilde{E}
.
We assume that there exist operators
\Pi_{n}\in\mathscr{L}(\widetilde{E};E_{n})
and
\Lambda_{n}\in\mathscr{L}(E_{n};\widetilde{E})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
which satisfy

(i)

M_{\Pi}\coloneqq\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|\Pi_{n}\|_{\mathscr{L}(\widetilde{E};E_{n})}<\infty

and

M_{\Lambda}\coloneqq\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|\Lambda_{n}\|_{\mathscr{L}(E_{n};\widetilde{E})}<\infty
;

(ii)

\Lambda_{n}\Pi_{n}x\to x
in

\widetilde{E}
as
n\to\infty
for all
x\in E_{\infty}
;

(iii)

\Pi_{n}\Lambda_{n}=\operatorname{Id}_{E_{n}}
for
all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

In addition, we denote
\Pi_{\infty}=\Lambda_{\infty}\coloneqq\operatorname{Id}_{\widetilde{E}}
for convenience.

Note that parts
(i)
and
(iii)
together
imply that the lifting operators are continuous embeddings
\Lambda_{n}\colon E_{n}\hookrightarrow\widetilde{E}
.
In applications, they will typically be nested (in the sense that
E_{n}\hookrightarrow E_{n+1}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
) and finite-dimensional, but neither of these assumptions is strictly necessary in the abstract theory.
Moreover, we will often have
\widetilde{E}=E_{\infty}
, but not always; see Section
6
.

Now we consider the following sequence of linear operators on
(E_{n})_{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}
:

Assumption 2.2
.

For all
n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}
, let
-A_{n}\colon\mathsf{D}(A_{n})\subseteq E_{n}\to E_{n}
be a linear operator generating a strongly continuous semigroup
(S_{n}(t))_{t\geq 0}\subseteq\mathscr{L}(E_{n})
, meaning that
S_{n}(0)=\operatorname{Id}_{E_{n}}
,
S_{n}(t+s)=S_{n}(t)S_{n}(s)
for all
t,s\geq 0
, and
S_{n}(t)x\to x
as
t\downarrow 0
for all
x\in E_{n}
.
Suppose that there exist
M_{S}\in[1,\infty)
and
w\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(2.1)

\|S_{n}(t)\|_{\mathscr{L}(E_{n})}\leq M_{S}e^{-wt}\quad\text{for all }n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}\text{ and }t\in[0,\infty).

Given a linear operator
A\colon\mathsf{D}(A)\subseteq E\to E
on a
Banach space
(E,\|\,\cdot\,\|_{E})
, where
\mathsf{D}(A)
denotes its domain,
we say that
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
belongs to the

resolvent set
\rho(A)
of
A
if the
corresponding
resolvent operator
R(\lambda,A):=(\lambda\operatorname{Id}_{E}-A)^{-1}

exists
in
\mathscr{L}(E)
.
Given a sequence
(A_{n})_{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}
of such operators, generating strongly continuous semigroups with uniform growth bounds, the Trotter–Kato approximation theorem (see, e.g.,
[
29
, Chapter III,
Theorem 4.8]
) establishes a link between the strong convergence of resolvents and uniform convergence of the semigroups on compact subintervals of
[0,\infty)
.
The following discrete-to-continuum analog of this result was proved by Ito and
Kappel
[
51
, Theorem 2.1]
:

Theorem 2.3

(Discrete-to-continuum
Trotter–Kato approximation)
.

Let Assumptions
2.1
and
2.2
be
satisfied, with
w\in\mathbb{R}
.
The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

There exists a
\lambda\in\bigcap_{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}\rho(A_{n})
such
that, for every
x\in E_{\infty}
,

(2.2)

\Lambda_{n}R(\lambda,A_{n})\Pi_{n}x\to R(\lambda,A_{\infty})x\quad\text{in }\widetilde{E}\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

(ii)

For all
x\in E_{\infty}
and
T\in(0,\infty)
it holds that

(2.3)

\Lambda_{n}S_{n}\Pi_{n}\otimes x\to S_{\infty}\otimes x\quad\text{in }C([0,T];\widetilde{E})\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

If
(i)
holds for some
\lambda\in\bigcap_{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}\rho(A_{n})
(or, equivalently, if
(ii)
holds), then
(i)
holds in fact for
every

\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
such that
\operatorname{Re}\lambda<w
.

2.3.
Stochastic integration in UMD-type-2 Banach spaces

Given a separable Hilbert space
(H,\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{H})
over the real scalar field, let
(W(t))_{t\geq 0}
be an
H
-valued cylindrical Wiener process with respect to some filtered probability space
(\Omega,(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
.
A rigorous definition can be found in
[
60
, Section 2.5.1]
.
For our purpose of constructing the stochastic integral below, it suffices to define
\langle W(t),h\rangle_{H}\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\beta_{j}(t)\langle e_{j},h\rangle_{H}
for all
t\geq 0
and
h\in H
, where
(e_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is an orthonormal basis of
H
and
(\beta_{j}(\,\cdot\,))_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of independent (real-valued) Brownian motions on
(\Omega,(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
.
Then
(\langle W(t),h\rangle_{H})_{t\geq 0}
is a well-defined Brownian motion for every
h\in H
, and intuitively one can think of
(W(t))_{t\geq 0}
as being given by
W(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\beta_{j}(t)e_{j}
.

Let
(E,\|\,\cdot\,\|_{E})
be a real Banach space, and let
(\gamma_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of independent (real-valued) standard normal random variables on a probability space
(\Omega^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime},\mathbb{P}^{\prime})
, independent of the probability spaces
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
and
(\widetilde{\Omega},\widetilde{\mathcal{F}},\widetilde{\mathbb{P}})
used in the rest of this work.
We define the space
\gamma(H;E)
of
\gamma
-radonifying operators from
H
to
E
as the completion of the finite-rank operators
H\otimes E
with respect to the norm

\lVert\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{j}\otimes x_{j}\rVert_{\gamma(H;E)}\coloneqq\lVert\sum_{j=1}^{n}\gamma_{j}x_{j}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime};E)},

where we assume that the
(h_{j})_{j=1}^{n}
are
H
-orthonormal.
This norm is well-defined, i.e., it can be checked that the right-hand side is independent of the choice of representation.
An important feature of
\gamma(H;E)
is its
ideal property
(in the algebraic sense)
[
49
, Theorem 9.1.10]
, which states that for all
T\in\gamma(H;E)
,
U\in\mathscr{L}(E;F)
and
S\in\mathscr{L}(K;H)
, we have

(2.4)

UTS\in\gamma(K;F)\quad\text{with}\quad\|UTS\|_{\gamma(K;F)}\leq\|U\|_{\mathscr{L}(E;F)}\|T\|_{\gamma(H;E)}\|S\|_{\mathscr{L}(K;H)}.

For any rank-one operator
h\otimes x\in\mathscr{L}(H;E)
, we have
h\otimes x\in\gamma(H;E)
with

(2.5)

\|h\otimes x\|_{\gamma(H;E)}=\|h\|_{H}\|x\|_{E}.

The stochastic integral of an elementary integrand
\Phi\colon(0,\infty)\to H\otimes E
, i.e., a function of the form
\Phi(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbf{1}_{(a_{j},b_{j}]}(t)h_{j}\otimes x_{j}
, is defined by

(2.6)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(t)\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bigl(\langle h_{j},W(b_{j})\rangle_{H}-\langle h_{j},W(a_{j})\rangle_{H}\bigr)x_{j}\in L^{2}(\Omega;E).

In order to extend the definition of the stochastic integral beyond elementary integrands, one needs to impose further geometric assumptions on the Banach space
E
.
In this article we work in one of the standard settings, namely that of spaces with
unconditional martingale differences
and
Rademacher type

2
(abbreviated to
UMD-type-2
).
Definitions of these notions can be found in
[
48
, Section 4.2]
and
[
49
, Section 7.1]
, respectively, but we will only use them to ensure existence of stochastic integrals.
In this case, one can establish the
Itô inequality

(2.7)

\bigg\lVert\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(t)\bigg\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega;E)}\lesssim_{E}\|\Phi\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty;\gamma(H;E))}

for elementary integrands
[
82
, Proposition 4.2]
, and use it to extend the definition of the stochastic integral to all
\Phi\in L^{2}(0,\infty;\gamma(H;E))
.
In fact, since we are only concerned with deterministic integrands in this work, we could suffice with the type
2
assumption.
Despite this, we additionally impose the UMD assumption for the sake of compatibility with some of the literature, and because the concrete examples of Banach spaces in which we are interested (such as the Lebesgue
L^{q}
-spaces for
q\in[2,\infty)
) satisfy both properties.
For more details on stochastic integration in Banach spaces, we refer to the survey article
[
82
]
.

The exponent
2
in
L^{2}
appearing on both sides of (
2.7
) can be replaced by any other
p\in[1,\infty)
at the cost of a
p
-dependent constant, see for instance
[
82
, Theorem 4.7]
.
If
E
is also a Hilbert space, then
\gamma(H;E)
is isometrically isomorphic to the space
\mathscr{L}_{2}(H;E)
, see
[
49
, Proposition 9.1.9]
, and instead of the inequality (
2.7
) we have the Itô
isometry
between
L^{2}(0,\infty;\mathscr{L}_{2}(H;E))
and
L^{2}(\Omega;E)
.

In the spatial and linear case, such convergence results have been proven in
[
71
,
72
]
.
We also mention the work
[
65
]
, in which the statistical properties of the spatiotemporal

equation were investigated for fixed

3.1.
Geometric graphs and generalized Whittle–Matérn operators

Assumption 3.1

(Manifold assumption)
.

Given

, suppose that
\mathcal{M}
is an
m

Let
\mu

denote the normalized volume measure and geodesic metric on

For each

, let a point cloud
\mathcal{M}_{n}\coloneqq(x_{n}^{(j)})_{j=1}^{n}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
be given.
We suppose that
\mathcal{M}
can be partitioned into
n

n
points comprising
\mathcal{M}_{n}

(T_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mu_{n}={}

\displaystyle T_{n\sharp}\mu

\displaystyle\text{for all }n

\displaystyle\in\mathbb{N},\quad\text{and}

(3.2)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{n}\coloneqq{}

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathcal{M}}d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,T_{n}(x))\to 0

\displaystyle\text{as }n

\displaystyle\to\infty.

Here,
\mu_{n}\coloneqq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\delta_{x_{n}^{(j)}}

\mathcal{M}
associated to
\mathcal{M}_{n}

T_{n\sharp}\mu

T_{n\sharp}\mu(B)\coloneqq\mu(\{T_{n}\in B\})

\mathcal{M}_{n}

Two different examples in which this assumption is satisfied are presented in Settings
3.2

Given

for

\Lambda_{u}u_{n}\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{R}
and
\Pi_{n}u\colon\mathcal{M}_{n}\to\mathbb{R}

(3.3)

\Lambda_{n}u_{n}(x)\coloneqq u_{n}(T_{n}(x))\quad\text{and}\quad\Pi_{n}u(x_{n}^{(j)})\coloneqq n\int_{V_{n}^{(j)}}u(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x),

respectively,
for all

and

,
where

It turns out that the operations defined in (

2.1
with respect to the following function spaces:
Given
q\in[1,\infty]
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we set
E_{n}\coloneqq L^{q}(\mathcal{M}_{n})\coloneqq L^{q}(\mathcal{M},\mu_{n})
, as well as
\widetilde{E}\coloneqq L^{q}(\mathcal{M})
and

(3.4)

E_{\infty}\coloneqq\begin{cases}L^{q}(\mathcal{M}),&\text{if }q\in[1,\infty);\\
C(\mathcal{M}),&\text{if }q=\infty.\end{cases}

Later on, we will need

, so that

is a UMD-type-2 space for use in stochastic integration, but the statements here hold for all

.

For these spaces, Assumption
2.1
(i)
is satisfied with

and
M_{\Pi}\leq 1
.
Indeed, the fact that
\Lambda_{n}
is an isometry follows from (
3.1
) if
q\in[1,\infty)
, whereas for
q=\infty
we see directly from the definition that

\|\Lambda_{n}u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})}=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{M}}\lvert u_{n}(T_{n}(x))\rvert=\max_{j=1}^{n}\lvert u_{n}(x_{n}^{(j)})\rvert=\|u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}_{n})}.

To show that

is a contraction, we first apply Hölder’s inequality in (

) with

to find

\lvert\Pi_{n}u(x_{n}^{(j)})\rvert\leq n\|u\|_{L^{q}(V_{n}^{(j)})}\mu(V_{n}^{(j)})^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}=n^{\frac{1}{q}}\|u\|_{L^{q}(V_{n}^{(j)})}
, so that

(3.5)

\|\Pi_{n}u\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{M}_{n})}^{q}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lvert\Pi_{n}u(x_{n}^{(j)})\rvert^{q}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|u\|_{L^{q}(V_{n}^{(j)}\!\!,\,\mu)}^{q}=\|u\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{M})}^{q}.

Assumption
2.1
(ii)
is a consequence of (
3.2
), from which it follows that
\Lambda_{n}\Pi_{n}u\to u
in

for any

.
For

, this is what we wanted to show; if

is dense in

, and the fact that
(\Lambda_{n}\Pi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is uniformly bounded in

by Assumption

(i)
which we have just proven to hold, yields

for all

as desired.
Assumption

(iii)
can be verified via direct computation using the definitions.
Finally, we have

(3.6)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{M}}\Lambda_{n}u_{n}(x)

\displaystyle v(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x)=\int_{\mathcal{M}}u_{n}(T_{n}(x))v(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{V_{n}^{(j)}}u_{n}(x_{n}^{(j)})v(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x)

(3.7)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}u_{n}(x_{n}^{(j)})\Pi_{n}v(x_{n}^{(j)})=\int_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}u_{n}(x)\Pi_{n}v(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x).

which shows that the adjoint of

, where

h_{n}\coloneqq n^{-1/m}
:

\mathcal{M}_{n}\coloneqq\{\tfrac{1}{2}n^{-1/m},\tfrac{3}{2}n^{-1/m},\dots,1-\tfrac{1}{2}n^{-1/m}\}^{m}.

Then the grid points of
\mathcal{M}_{n}
can be written as
\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(\mathbf{j})}=n^{-1/m}(\mathbf{j}-\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{1})
for some
m
-tuple
\mathbf{j}\in\{1,\dots,n^{1/m}\}^{m}
, where
\mathbf{1}=(1,\dots,1)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
To each of these points
\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(\mathbf{j})}\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
, we associate the half-open cube
U_{n}^{(\mathbf{j})}\coloneqq\prod_{k=1}^{m}\bigl[n^{-1/m}(j_{k}-1),n^{-1/m}j_{k}\bigr)
.
Since these cubes form a partition of
\mathcal{M}
(recalling that opposite sides are identified), we can define the transport map
T_{n}\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}_{n}
by
T_{n}(x)\coloneqq\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(\mathbf{j})}
whenever
x\in U_{n}^{(\mathbf{j})}
.
It readily follows that (
3.1
) holds, as does (
3.2
), since

\varepsilon_{n}=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{m}n^{-1/m}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Setting 3.3

(Randomly sampled point cloud)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be any manifold satisfying Assumption
3.1
, and
let
(x^{(n)})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
be a sequence of points independently sampled from
\mu
.
This sequence can be viewed as a sample from the product probability space
(\widetilde{\Omega},\widetilde{\mathcal{F}},\widetilde{\mathbb{P}})\coloneqq\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{M}),\mu)
, where
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{M})
denotes the Borel
\sigma
-algebra on
\mathcal{M}
.
If we set
\mathcal{M}_{n}\coloneqq(x^{(j)})_{j=1}^{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then
[
71
, Proposition 4.1]
states that,
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}
-a.s., there exists a sequence
(T_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of transport maps
T_{n}\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}_{n}
for which (
3.1
) holds and

(3.8)

\varepsilon_{n}\lesssim_{\mathcal{M}}(\log n)^{c_{m}}n^{-1/m}\to 0\quad\text{as }n\to\infty,

where
c_{m}=\frac{3}{4}
if
m=2
and
c_{m}=\frac{1}{m}
otherwise.

We will now introduce the linear operators which we consider on the domains
\mathcal{M}
and
(\mathcal{M}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
as in Settings
3.2
and
3.3
.
Given the coefficient functions
\tau\colon\mathcal{M}\to[0,\infty)
and
\kappa\colon\mathcal{M}\to[0,\infty)
, respectively assumed to be Lipschitz and continuously differentiable, we consider the nonnegative and symmetric second-order linear differential operator
\mathcal{L}^{\tau,\kappa}_{\infty}
formally defined by

(3.9)

\mathcal{L}^{\tau,\kappa}_{\infty}u\coloneqq\tau u-\nabla\cdot(\kappa\nabla u),

for
u
belonging to some appropriate domain
\mathsf{D}(\mathcal{L}^{\tau,\kappa}_{\infty})\subseteq L^{2}(\mathcal{M})
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we endow
\mathcal{M}_{n}
with a (weighted, undirected) graph structure by viewing its points as vertices and defining the weight matrix
\mathbf{W}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
by

(3.10)

(\mathbf{W}_{n})_{ij}\coloneqq\frac{2(m+2)}{\nu_{m}}\frac{1}{nh_{n}^{m+2}}\mathbf{1}_{[0,h_{n}]}\bigl(\|x_{n}^{(i)}-x_{n}^{(j)}\|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigr),

where
\nu_{m}
denotes the volume of the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
h_{n}\in(0,\infty)
is a given graph connectivity length scale.
With these weights, the resulting graph is an example of a
geometric graph
(or in fact a
random geometric graph
if the nodes are sampled randomly as in Setting
3.3
).
The results in this section are likely to remain valid if the indicator function
\mathbf{1}_{[0,h_{n}]}
in (
3.10
) is replaced by a more general (e.g., Gaussian) cut-off kernel (such as in
[
12
]
), but we only consider
\eta=\mathbf{1}_{[0,h_{n}]}
in order to also cite sources which are not formulated in this generality.

The graph-discretized counterpart
\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\tau,\kappa}
of (
3.9
) is then the operator which acts on a given function
u\colon\mathcal{M}_{n}\to\mathbb{R}
as

(3.11)

\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\tau,\kappa}u(x_{n}^{(i)})\coloneqq\tau(x_{n}^{(i)})u(x_{n}^{(i)})+\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\mathbf{W}_{n})_{ij}\sqrt{\kappa(x_{n}^{(i)})\kappa(x_{n}^{(j)})}\bigl(u(x_{n}^{(i)})-u(x_{n}^{(j)})\bigr).

This can be seen as a generalized version of the (unnormalized) graph Laplacian
\Delta_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}
, and in fact reduces to it if
\tau\equiv 0
and
\kappa\equiv 1
.

Assumption 3.4

(Coefficients of
\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\tau,\kappa}
)
.

Let
\tau\colon\mathcal{M}\to[0,\infty)
and
\kappa\colon\mathcal{M}\to[0,\infty)
be the coefficient functions used to define the base operators
(\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\tau,\kappa})_{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}
in (
3.9
) and (
3.11
).
We shall suppose that

(i)

\tau
is Lipschitz, whereas
\kappa
is continuously differentiable and bounded below away from zero.

For some results, we specialize to the case that

(ii)

\tau\equiv 0
and
\kappa\equiv 1
, i.e.,
\mathcal{L}^{\tau,\kappa}_{n}=\Delta_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}
and
\mathcal{L}^{\tau,\kappa}_{\infty}
and reduces to the Laplace–Beltrami operator on
\mathcal{M}
.

Assumption 3.5

(Connectivity length scale of random graph)
.

Let the manifold
\mathcal{M}
and the random point clouds
(\mathcal{M}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
on the probability space
(\widetilde{\Omega},\widetilde{\mathcal{F}},\widetilde{\mathbb{P}})
be as in Setting
3.3
.
Let
(h_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq(0,\infty)
determine the connectivity length scales of the graphs associated to
(\mathcal{M}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
via the weights (
3.10
), and suppose that
s\in(0,\infty)
.
We will assume one of the following:

(i)

There exists a
\beta>\frac{m}{4s}
such that

(\log n)^{c_{m}}n^{-\frac{1}{m}}\ll h_{n}\ll n^{-\frac{1}{4s\beta}}.

(ii)

There exists a
\delta>0
, so small that
\frac{m}{1-\delta}<m+4+\delta
, and a
\beta>\frac{m+4+\delta}{2s}
such that

n^{-\frac{1}{m+4+\delta}}\lesssim h_{n}\ll n^{-\frac{1}{2s\beta}}.

Given two sequences

(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(b_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of positive real numbers, the notation

a_{n}\ll b_{n}
means
a_{n}/b_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

Since
\operatorname{Id}_{n}+\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\tau,\kappa}
is self-adjoint, positive definite and has a compact inverse for all
n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}
(cf.
[
75
, Chapter XII]
in the case
n=\infty
), there exists an orthonormal basis
(\psi^{(j)}_{n})_{j=1}^{n}
of
L^{2}(\mathcal{M}_{n})
and a non-decreasing sequence
(\lambda^{(j)}_{n})_{j=1}^{n}\subseteq[0,\infty)
, accumulating only at infinity for
n=\infty
, such that
\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\tau,\kappa}\psi^{(j)}_{n}=\lambda^{(j)}_{n}\psi^{(j)}_{n}
for all
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.
We summarize this state of affairs by saying that
(\psi^{(j)}_{n},\lambda^{(j)}_{n})_{j=1}^{n}
is an orthonormal eigenbasis of
L^{2}(\mathcal{M}_{n})
associated to
\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\tau,\kappa}
.
The asymptotic behavior of the eigenvalues
(\lambda_{\infty}^{(j)})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is described by
Weyl’s law
, cf.
[
75
, Theorem XII.2.1]
:

(3.12)

\lambda^{(j)}_{\infty}\eqsim_{(\mathcal{M},\tau,\kappa)}j^{2/m}\quad\text{for all }j\in\mathbb{N}.

Given any of the above settings and
n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}
, we define the generalized Whittle–Matérn operator
A_{n}
on
L^{2}(\mathcal{M}_{n})
as a fractional power of the symmetric elliptic operator
\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\tau,\kappa}
given by (
3.9
) and (
3.11
).
That is, we set
A_{n}\coloneqq(\mathcal{L}^{\tau,\kappa}_{n})^{s}
for some
s\in[0,\infty)
, where we use the spectral definition of fractional powers:

(3.13)

A_{n}u=(\mathcal{L}^{\tau,\kappa}_{n})^{s}u\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{n}[\lambda^{(j)}_{n}]^{s}\langle u,\psi_{n}^{(j)}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{M}_{n})}\psi_{n}^{(j)},\quad u\in\mathsf{D}(A_{n})\subseteq L^{2}(\mathcal{M}_{n}).

These will be used as the linear operators
(A_{n})_{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}
in the stochastic partial differential equations in the next subsection.

Since
A_{n}
is a nonnegative definite and self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}(\mathcal{M}_{n})
for any
n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}
, the Lumer–Phillips theorem
[
81
, Theorem 13.35]
implies that
-A_{n}
generates a contractive analytic
C_{0}
-semigroup
(S_{n}(z))_{z\in\Sigma_{\eta}}\subseteq\mathscr{L}(L^{2}(\mathcal{M}_{n}))
on the sector

(3.14)

\Sigma_{\eta}\coloneqq\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}:\arg\lambda\in(-\eta,\eta)\}

for every
\eta\in(0,\frac{1}{2}\pi)
.
Thus, the operators
(A_{n})_{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}
on
(L^{2}(\mathcal{M}_{n}))_{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}
are uniformly sectorial of angle
0
, see Appendix
C
.

The following additional assumption(s) on the
L^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}_{n})
-boundedness of the semigroups will be needed for some of the results in Section
3.2
:

Assumption 3.6

(Uniform
L^{\infty}
-boundedness of semigroups)
.

Suppose that

(i)

there exists a constant
M_{S,\infty}\in[1,\infty)
such that

(3.15)

\|S_{n}(t)\|_{\mathscr{L}(L^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}_{n}))}\leq M_{S,\infty}\quad\text{for all }n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}\text{ and }t\geq 0.

We may sometimes additionally assume that

(ii)

(S_{n}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is
L^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}_{n})
-contractive for all
n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}
, i.e.,
M_{S,\infty}=1
in
(i)
.

Under this assumption, it follows from
[
68
, Proposition 3.12]
that
(S_{n}(z))_{z\in\Sigma_{\eta_{q}}}
is bounded analytic on
L^{q}(\mathcal{M}_{n})
with
\eta_{q}=\frac{2}{q}\eta
for all
n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}
and
q\in(2,\infty)
, and its uniform norm bound on the sector
\Sigma_{\eta_{q}}
only depends on
q
and
M_{S,\infty}
.
Therefore, the sequence of operators
(A_{n})_{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}
on
(L^{q}(\mathcal{M}_{n}))_{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}
is uniformly sectorial of angle at most
(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})\pi
.

3.2.
Convergence of graph-discretized semilinear SPDEs

Let
(W(t))_{t\geq 0}
be an
L^{2}(\mathcal{M})
-valued cylindrical Wiener process with respect to a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]},\mathbb{P})
.
The spaces
\mathcal{M}
and
(\mathcal{M}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are as in Setting
3.2
or
3.3
above; in the latter case, note that the space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]},\mathbb{P})
associated to the Wiener noise is independent of the probability space
(\widetilde{\Omega},\widetilde{\mathcal{F}},\widetilde{\mathbb{P}})
describing the randomness of the point cloud.
For every
n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}
, we set
W_{n}\coloneqq\Pi_{n}W
and consider the following semilinear stochastic partial differential equation (SPDE):

(3.16)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u_{n}(t,x)+[\mathcal{L}^{\tau,\kappa}_{n}]^{s}u_{n}(t,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t&=f_{n}(t,u_{n}(t,x))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{n}(t,x),\\
u_{n}(0,x)&=\xi_{n}(x),\qquad\qquad\qquad(t,x)\in(0,T]\times\mathcal{M}_{n},\end{aligned}\right.

where
s\in(0,\infty)
,
T\in(0,\infty)
is a finite time horizon,
f_{n}\colon\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is the nonlinearity, and
\xi_{n}\colon\Omega\times\mathcal{M}_{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is the initial datum.
Note that
(W_{n}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is a
Q_{n}
-cylindrical Wiener process with
Q_{n}=\Pi_{n}^{*}\Pi_{n}=\Lambda_{n}\Pi_{n}=\operatorname{Id}_{n}
, where we recall (
3.7
) for the second identity.
Therefore,
(W_{n}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is a cylindrical Wiener process on
L^{2}(\mathcal{M}_{n})
for all
n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}
, and its formal time derivative
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{n}
represents spatiotemporal Gaussian white noise on
[0,T]\times\mathcal{M}_{n}
.

Solutions to (
3.16
)—and all the other (semi)linear SPDEs that we consider in this work—are always interpreted in the mild sense.
This notion of solutions is defined using the semigroup
(S_{n}(t))_{t\geq 0}
generated by
-[\mathcal{L}^{\tau,\kappa}_{n}]^{s}
.
We say that
u_{n}
is a
global mild solution
to (
3.16
) if it satisfies the following relation for all
t\in[0,T]
:

(3.17)

u_{n}(t)=S_{n}(t)\xi_{n}+\int_{0}^{t}S_{n}(t-s)F_{n}(s,u_{n}(s))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}S_{n}(t-s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{n}(s),\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

Here, we interpret
u_{n}=(u_{n}(t))_{t\in[0,T]}
as a process taking its values in an infinite-dimensional Banach space of functions on
\mathcal{M}_{n}
(such as
L^{q}(\mathcal{M}_{n})
or
C(\mathcal{M}_{n})
), and we define for every
(\omega,t)\in\Omega\times[0,T]
the
Nemytskii operator

u_{n}\mapsto F_{n}(\omega,t,u_{n})
on this function space by setting
[F_{n}(\omega,t,u_{n})](\xi)\coloneqq f_{n}(\omega,t,u_{n}(\xi))
for all
\xi\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
.

This notion of solution is called “global” because it exists on the whole of
[0,T]
, in contrast with “local” solutions, which may blow up before time
T
.
However, we note that global solutions generally grow unbounded as
T\to\infty
.
We will not consider local solutions in this section, but we do work with them in Section
5.2
.

In this section, the real-valued functions
f_{n}
are supposed to satisfy the following:

Assumption 3.7

(Nonlinearities)
.

We will assume one of the following conditions:

(i)

The nonlinearities
(f_{n})_{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}
are globally Lipschitz continuous and grow linearly, both uniformly in
n
.
I.e., there exist
\widetilde{L}_{f},\widetilde{C}_{f}\in[0,\infty)
such that, for all
n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
,

(3.18)

\lvert f_{n}(\omega,t,x)-f_{n}(\omega,t,y)\rvert\leq\widetilde{L}_{f}\lvert x-y\rvert\quad\text{and}\quad\lvert f_{n}(\omega,t,x)\rvert\leq\widetilde{C}_{f}(1+\lvert x\rvert).

(ii)

The nonlinearities
(f_{n})_{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}
are of the polynomial form

(3.19)

f_{n}(\omega,t,x)\coloneqq-a_{2k+1,n}(\omega,t)x^{2k+1}+\sum_{j=0}^{2k}a_{j,n}(\omega,t)x^{j},

where
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
a_{j,n}\colon\Omega\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
for
each
j\in\{0,\dots,2k+1\}
, and there exist constants
c,C\in(0,\infty)
such that

(3.20)

c\leq a_{2k+1,n}(\omega,t)\leq C\quad\text{and}\quad\lvert a_{j,n}(\omega,t)\rvert\leq C

for all
j\in\{0,\dots,2k\}
,
n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}
and
(\omega,t)\in\Omega\times[0,T]
.

In either case, we suppose moreover that
f_{n}\to f
uniformly on compact intervals; i.e., for all
r\in[0,\infty)
and
(\omega,t)\in\Omega\times[0,T]
,

(3.21)

\sup_{x\in[-r,r]}\lvert f_{n}(\omega,t,x)-f_{\infty}(\omega,t,x)\rvert\to 0\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

Example 3.8
.

The cubic polynomial
f_{n}(\omega,t,x)\coloneqq-x^{3}+x
, which turns (
3.16
) into the (fractional) stochastic Allen–Cahn equation in case
\tau\equiv 0
,
\kappa\equiv 1
and
s\leq 1
,
is of the form asserted in Assumption
3.7
(ii)
.
Note that this is also an example of the important situation where (
3.21
) is trivially satisfied by taking the same function
f_{n}\coloneqq f
for all
n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}
.

The final technical assumption that we record before moving on to the main theorem of this section is the following:

Assumption 3.9

(Uniform
L^{\infty}
-boundedness of eigenfunctions)
.

There exists a constant
M_{\psi,\infty}\in(0,\infty)
such that

(3.22)

\|\psi_{n}^{(j)}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}_{n})}\leq M_{\psi,\infty}\quad\text{for all }n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}\text{ and }j\in\{1,\dots,n\}.

The interplay of the various choices of spatial domains
\mathcal{M}_{n}
, linear operators
A_{n}
and nonlinearity functions
f_{n}
determines the class of SPDEs to which (
3.16
) belongs.
Rigorous definitions of the corresponding mild solution concepts, as well as well-posedness and discrete-to-continuum convergence results can be found in Sections
4
–
6
, respectively.
Applying these results in their respective regimes of applicability yields the following discrete-to-continuum convergence theorem for the solutions to (
3.16
);
note that the setting of part
(iii)
covers the stochastic (fractional) Allen–Cahn equation on the one-dimensional torus, see Example
3.8
.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
and
(\mathcal{M}_{n})_{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}
be as in Setting
3.2
or
3.3
.

(i)

Consider Setting
3.3
.
Let
s>\frac{1}{2}m
and suppose that Assumption
3.5
(i)
holds with

.
If Assumptions
3.4
(i)
and
3.7
(i)
are satisfied, and

is such that
\Lambda_{n}\xi_{n}\to\xi_{\infty}
in

in

to (

) for every

(3.23)

\Lambda_{n}u_{n}\to u_{\infty}\quad\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}\text{-a.s.\ in }L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];L^{2}(\mathcal{M}))).

(ii)

In Setting

,
let
\delta>0

\frac{m}{1-\delta}<m+4+\delta
, suppose

(ii)

(ii)

(i)
,

If

to (

) for every

, as well as
u_{\infty}
in

, and as

\Lambda_{n}u_{n}\to u_{\infty}\quad\text{in }L^{0}(\widetilde{\Omega},L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];L^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})))).

(iii)

Consider Setting
3.2
with

. Let

and suppose that Assumptions
3.4
(ii)
and

(ii)
are satisfied.
If

\Lambda_{n}\xi_{n}\to\xi_{\infty}

, then

in

) for every

.

.

3.3.
Intermediate results

In this subsection, we collect a number of intermediate results which imply that the conditions imposed in Theorem
3.10
are sufficient to fit into the setting of the various convergence theorems in Sections
4
–
6
.
More precisely, depending on the setting, we wish to verify a subset of the following: Conditions
(A1)
–
(A3)
from Section
4
on the linear operators, conditions
(F1)
–
(F2)
and
(IC)
from Section
5
on the nonlinearities and initial conditions, respectively, as well as their extended counterparts
(A1-B)
–
(A4-B)
,
(IC-B)
,
(F1-B)
–
(F2-B)
and
(F1′′-B)
from Section

.
The proofs of the results in this section are deferred to Appendix

for ease of exposition.

The necessary convergence of the linear operators, given by
(A3)
and
(A3-B)
, will ultimately be derived from the spectral convergence of
(\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\tau,\kappa})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
to
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}^{\tau,\kappa}

In the square grid Setting
3.2

3.11
below.
A subtlety arising in the random graph Setting
3.3
is that, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we cannot in general control the errors
\lvert\lambda_{n}^{(j)}-\lambda_{\infty}^{(j)}\rvert
and
\|\psi_{n}^{(j)}\circ T_{n}-\psi_{\infty}^{(j)}\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{M})}
for all
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, but only for indices
j
up to a sufficiently small integer
k_{n}
. We present the precise statements below:
Theorems
3.12
and
3.13
(i)
, which cover eigenvalue convergence and
L^{2}(\mathcal{M})
-convergence of eigenfunctions, are respectively taken from
[
71
, Theorems 4.6 and 4.7]
.
Theorem

3.5.
Discussion of the assumptions

In this subsection, we comment on the various assumptions made in Theorem
3.10
, the extent to which they are necessary, and how one might check them in practice.

The distinction between parts
(i)
and
(ii)
of Assumption
3.4
, i.e., whether to allow for spatially varying coefficient functions
\tau
and
\kappa
in the second-order symmetric base operators
(\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\tau,\kappa})_{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}
instead of merely considering Laplacians, is mainly due to the availability of spectral convergence theorems in the respective situations.
Most of the literature on eigenfunction convergence of graph-discretized second-order operators is focused on the Laplacian case, see for instance
[
11
,
32
]
for
L^{2}
-convergence and
[
12
,
25
,
86
]
for
L^{\infty}
-convergence.
However, the authors of
[
71
]
show how the
L^{2}
-convergence results can be extended to coefficient functions satisfying Assumption
3.4
(i)
.
We expect that most spectral convergence results for graph Laplacians can be extended to allow for varying coefficients, but doing so requires significant effort, hence we sometimes make Assumption
3.4
(ii)
for the sake of convenience.

Similarly, the difference between the bounds on the graph connectivity length scales in the two parts of Assumption
3.5
is a result of the current availability of spectral convergence literature.
Eigenfunction convergence of
(\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\tau,\kappa})_{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}
in
L^{2}
has for instance been proved in
[
71
]
under Assumption
3.5
(i)
, but for graphs and manifolds as in our setting, the optimal available
L^{\infty}
-convergence results (for graph Laplacians) seem to be those of
[
12
]
, which require Assumption
3.5
(ii)
.
However, according to
[
12
, Remark 2.7]
, it is plausible that the
L^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
-convergence of Laplacian eigenfunctions can be proved under the same assumptions as the
L^{2}(\mathcal{M})
-convergence, with the same rate.
Some recent results in this direction can be found in
[
3
]
, where the authors show
L^{\infty}
-convergence of Laplacian eigenvectors with optimal rates and loose lower bounds on the connectivity lengths, using homogenization theory, for point clouds on less general spatial domains.

Assumption
3.6
is natural in the sense that the results regarding
L^{\infty}
-convergence in space (for instance Theorem
3.10
(ii)
and
(iii)
) rely on uniform
L^{\infty}
-convergence of semigroup orbits on compact time intervals.
The latter necessitates that Assumption
3.6
(i)
is satisfied, at least for
t\in[0,T]
with arbitrarily large
T\in(0,\infty)
.

Moreover, for typical choices of differential operators, one can often check that Assumption
3.6
(ii)
holds, meaning that the semigroups are in fact
L^{\infty}
-contractive.
One such example is outlined in the proof of Theorem
3.10
(iii)
:
Matrix exponentials
(e^{-tL_{n}})_{t\geq 0}
are
L^{\infty}
-contractive if and only if
L_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is diagonally dominant with nonnegative diagonal entries
[
64
, Lemma 6.1]
.
Sufficient conditions for the
L^{\infty}
-contractivity of the semigroup
(S_{\infty}(t))_{t\geq 0}
generated by the negative of a uniformly elliptic second-order differential operator on a Euclidean domain
\mathcal{D}\subsetneq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, subject to appropriate boundary conditions, can be found in
[
68
, Section 4.3]
.
Likewise, the heat semigroup associated to the Laplace–Beltrami operator on a compact Riemannian manifold
\mathcal{M}
is
L^{\infty}
-contractive, cf.
[
23
, p. 148]
.

As mentioned in the proof of Theorem
3.10
(iii)
, all of the above
L^{\infty}
-contractivity results for second-order differential operators can be extended to fractional powers
s\in(0,1)
by using a subordination formula such as
[
50
, Theorem 15.2.17]
, noting that the definition of fractional power operators in this reference coincides with ours (more details are given in the first half of the proof of Lemma
C.1
below).
The semigroups generated by higher-order differential operators, however, are in general not contractive on
L^{\infty}
(or any
L^{q}
for
q\neq 2
, see for instance
[
57
]
); this is closely related to their lack of positivity preservation.
As an example, the fractional heat kernel associated to
(-\Delta)^{s}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
s\in(0,\infty)
at time
t\in(0,\infty)
is given by the inverse Fourier transform of
\xi\mapsto\exp(-t\|\xi\|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}^{2s})
, which is positive for
s\leq 1
but fails to be sign-definite if
s>1
, see
[
28
, p. 626 and pp. 632–633]
, respectively.

Thus, for operators
([\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\tau,\kappa}]^{s})_{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}
with
s>1
, we have to content ourselves with uniform
L^{\infty}
-boundedness of the semigroups
(S_{n}(t))_{t\geq 0}
in
n
and
t
, i.e., Assumption
3.6
(i)
.
In the absence of positivity preservation, one route to verifying such uniformity is through Gaussian upper bounds on the integral kernels corresponding to the semigroups, cf.
[
68
, Proposition 7.1]
.
Such bounds have been established for higher-order differential operators on Euclidean domains, as well as Laplacian operators on more general domains such as manifolds, graphs and fractals (see
[
68
, pp. 194–196]
and the references therein).
While it may be possible to unify these results in the setting of graph-discretized higher-order differential operators on manifolds, and thus obtain the uniform
L^{\infty}
-bounds required by Assumption
3.6
(i)
, this appears to be highly nontrivial and outside the scope of this work.

We also remark that certain higher-order operators have been shown to exhibit
(local) eventual positivity
, meaning that for every nonnegative initial datum
u_{0}\geq 0
and subset
\mathcal{D}^{*}
of the spatial domain
\mathcal{D}
, there exists
t^{*}>0
such that
S(t)u_{0}\geq 0
on
\mathcal{D}^{*}
for all
t\geq t^{*}
.
For instance, in
[
34
]
, this was shown for the bi-Laplacian
\Delta^{2}
on
\mathcal{D}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
In
[
38
]
, the authors apply the theory of
[
21
,
22
]
to treat the squared graph Laplacian
\Delta_{n}^{2}
, deduce that
(e^{-\Delta_{n}^{2}t})_{t\geq 0}
is eventually
L^{\infty}
-contractive
[
38
, Proposition 6.7]
, and note that this implies
L^{\infty}
-boundedness uniformly in
t\geq 0

[
38
, Remark 6.8]
.
However, as
n\to\infty
, their upper bound
\|e^{-\Delta_{n}^{2}t}\|_{\infty}\leq\exp(\|\Delta_{n}\|_{\infty}^{2}t^{*})
blows up in our setting. Hence, these results do not appear to be directly useful for our purpose of verifying Assumption
3.6
(i)
.

If we restrict ourselves to nonlinearities of the form
[F_{n}(\omega,t,u)](x)\coloneqq f_{n}(\omega,t,u(x))
(see (
5.1
) and (
6.1
)), then the conditions in Assumption
3.7
are the natural ones to ensure the global (in time) convergence results formulated in Theorem
3.10
.
Convergence results for more general nonlinearities, possibly formulated only in terms of local-in-time convergence and in weaker norms, can be found in Sections
5
and
6
.

Assumption
3.9
was used (explicitly or implicitly) to establish the
L^{\infty}
-convergence asserted in Theorem
3.14
and the uniform
L^{2}
–
L^{\infty}
-ultracontractivity in Lemma

.
The
L^{\infty}
-norms of the
L^{2}
-normalized eigenfunctions of the Laplace–Beltrami operator on a general compact Riemannian manifold
\mathcal{M}
of dimension
m
satisfy the upper bound
\|\psi^{(j)}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})}\lesssim[\lambda^{(j)}]^{\frac{m-1}{4}}
due to Hörmander
[
46
]
.
This bound is sharp in the sense that it is attained by the symmetric spherical harmonics on the sphere.
On the other hand, the

-norms are uniformly bounded (i.e., satisfy Assumption
3.9

m=1
or if
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{T}^{m}
is a flat torus.
Some results relating the

-growth rate of these eigenfunctions to the geometry of the manifold can be found in
[
24
,
74
,
77
]
.

These observations indicate that Assumption
3.9
poses strong restrictions on the curvature of the manifold, which raises the question whether this assumption could be removed.
Its central role in the proofs of Theorem
3.14
(ii)
,
(iii)
and Lemma
3.15
is due to the
L^{2}
–
L^{\infty}
-norm bounds (
A.12
) of operators which are defined in terms of eigenvalue expansions, such as the fractional powers defined by (
3.13
).
This suggests that disposing of Assumption
3.9
would involve techniques which are not based on spectral representations and spectral convergence of the operators involved.
For Lemma
3.15
in particular, one indication that this should be possible is the fact that, like
L^{\infty}
-boundedness, the
L^{2}
–
L^{\infty}
-ultracontractivity of
(S_{\infty}(t))_{t\geq 0}
follows from certain upper bounds on its heat kernel
[
39
, Theorem 3.2]
.
For the Laplace–Beltrami operator on a compact Riemannian manifold, we indeed have such bounds by
[
23
, Proposition 5.5.1 and Theorem 5.5.2]
, which imply (
3.36
) with
\beta=\frac{m}{4}
(for
n=\infty
and
s=1
).

4.
Infinite-dimensional Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process

This section and the subsequent Sections
5
and
6
are devoted to proving the abstract discrete-to-continuum approximation results which are applied to the Whittle–Matérn graph discretization setting in Section
3
.
Thus, from this point onwards we no longer necessarily work with graphs or Whittle–Matérn operators.
Instead, we have the following abstract setting.

Let the filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]},\mathbb{P})
be given.
For any
n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}
, we consider the following
linear
stochastic
evolution equation, whose state space is a real and separable UMD-type-2 Banach
space
(E_{n},\|\,\cdot\,\|_{E_{n}})
:

(4.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathrm{d}X_{n}(t)&=-A_{n}X_{n}(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+\mathrm{d}W_{n}(t),\quad t\in(0,T],\\
X_{n}(0)&=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Here,
A_{n}\colon\mathsf{D}(A_{n})\subseteq E_{n}\to E_{n}
is a linear operator and
T\in(0,\infty)
is a time horizon.
Moreover, we take
W_{n}\coloneqq\Pi_{n}W_{\infty}
, where
(W_{\infty}(t))_{t\geq 0}
denotes a cylindrical Wiener process on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]},\mathbb{P})
taking values in a separable Hilbert space
(H_{\infty},\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{H_{\infty}})
and the operator
\Pi_{n}\in\mathscr{L}(H_{\infty};H_{n})
is as in assumption
(A1)
below.
Thus, the formal time derivative
\dot{W}_{\infty}
of
W_{\infty}
represents space–time Gaussian white noise and
(W_{n})_{t\geq 0}
is an
H_{n}
-valued
Q_{n}
-cylindrical Wiener process colored in space by the covariance operator
Q_{n}\coloneqq\Pi_{n}\Pi_{n}^{*}\in\mathscr{L}(H_{n})
.

We impose the following uniformity
assumptions
on the spaces
(E_{n})_{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}
,
(H_{n})_{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}
and the operators
(A_{n})_{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}
:

(A1)

Assumption
2.1
holds for the UMD-type-2 Banach spaces
(E_{n})_{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}
and the Hilbert spaces
(H_{n})_{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}
, with
\widetilde{E}\coloneqq E_{\infty}
and
\widetilde{H}\coloneqq H_{\infty}
, both with the same sequence
(\Lambda_{n},\Pi_{n})_{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}
of lifting and projection operators.

(A2)

The operators
(A_{n})_{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}
on
(E_{n})_{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}
are uniformly sectorial of angle less than
\frac{1}{2}\pi
(see Appendix
C
for the definition of this concept).
In particular, their negatives generate bounded analytic
C_{0}
-semigroups
(S_{n}(t))_{t\geq 0}\subseteq\mathscr{L}(E_{n})
which satisfy Assumption
2.2
with
w=0
.
Moreover, there exists a
\beta\in[0,\frac{1}{2})
such that
R_{n}^{\beta}\coloneqq(\operatorname{Id}_{n}+A_{n})^{-\beta}\in\gamma(H_{n};E_{n})
for every
n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}
.

In general, the fractional powers of the sectorial operators
A_{n}
appearing in
(A2)
can be defined using any of the equivalent definitions in
[
41
, Chapter 3]
.
If, as in Section
3
, the operator
A_{n}
given as the restriction of an operator whose eigenvalues form an orthonormal basis on some Hilbert space containing
E_{n}
, then one can use the spectral definition (
3.13
) of fractional powers of
A_{n}
.

The solution concept which we consider for all of the equations in this work is that of a
mild solution
.
For the linear equation (
4.1
), it is given by the following stochastic convolution:

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}
and
T\in(0,\infty)
.
Under Assumptions
(A1)
–
(A2)
, the stochastic convolution

(4.2)

W_{A_{n}}(t)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{t}S_{n}(t-s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{n}(s),\quad t\in[0,T],

is a well-defined process in
C([0,T];L^{p}(\Omega;E_{n}))
for every
p\in[1,\infty)
.

Proof.

For every
p\in[1,\infty)
and
t\in[0,T]
, we have by the Itô inequality (
2.7
):

(4.3)

\|W_{A_{n}}(t)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;E_{n})}^{2}\lesssim_{(p,E_{n})}\int_{0}^{t}\|S_{n}(t-s)\|_{\gamma(H_{n};E_{n})}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\leq\|S_{n}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\gamma(H_{n};E_{n}))}^{2}

To thow that the right-hand side is bounded, we use the ideal property (
2.4
) of
\gamma(H_{n};E_{n})
and the estimate (
C.3
) for analytic semigroups (in conjuction with Assumptions
(A1)
and
(A2)
) to see that

(4.4)

\displaystyle\|S_{n}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\gamma(H_{n};E_{n}))}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\|(\operatorname{Id}_{n}+A_{n})^{\beta}S_{n}(t)R_{n}^{\beta}\|_{\gamma(H_{n};E_{n})}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\|R_{n}^{\beta}\|_{\gamma(H_{n};E_{n})}^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|(\operatorname{Id}_{n}+A_{n})^{\beta}S_{n}(t)\|_{\mathscr{L}(E_{n})}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\beta}\|R_{n}^{\beta}\|_{\gamma(H_{n};E_{n})}^{2}\int_{0}^{T}t^{-2\beta}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=\|R_{n}^{\beta}\|_{\gamma(H_{n};E_{n})}^{2}\frac{T^{1-2\beta}}{1-2\beta}<\infty.

Note that
\|R_{n}^{\beta}\|_{\gamma(H_{n};E_{n})}^{2}
is finite by
(A2)
.
Next, applying the Itô inequality (
2.7
) to the difference
W_{A_{n}}(t+h)-W_{A_{n}}(t)
for small enough
h\in\mathbb{R}
yields

(4.5)

\|W_{A_{n}}(t+h)-W_{A_{n}}(t)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;E_{n})}\lesssim_{(p,E_{n})}\|S_{n}(\,\cdot\,+h)-S_{n}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\gamma(H_{n};E_{n}))}\to 0,

as
h\to 0
, by the strong continuity of translation operators on the Bochner space
L^{2}(0,T;\gamma(H_{n};E_{n}))
.
This shows
W_{A_{n}}\in L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E_{n}))
.
∎

Two stochastic processes
(X(t))_{t\in[0,T]}
and
(Y(t))_{t\in[0,T]}
are said to be
modifications
of each other if
\mathbb{P}(X(t)=Y(t))=1
for all
t\in[0,T]
.

Definition 4.2
.

An
E_{n}
-valued stochastic process
X_{n}=(X_{n}(t))_{t\in[0,T]}
belonging to
C([0,T];L^{p}(\Omega;E_{n}))
for some
p\in[1,\infty)
is said to be a
mild solution
to (
4.1
) if it is a modification of the process
W_{A_{n}}
defined in (
4.2
).

The existence and uniqueness (up to modification) of the mild solution to (
4.1
) in
C([0,T];L^{p}(\Omega;E_{n}))
is then immediate from Definition
4.2
.

As remarked in Section
2.2
, we mainly have applications in mind where the problem corresponding to
n=\infty
is interpreted as a spatiotemporal stochastic partial differential equation.
In the present linear setting, its solution is also known as an
infinite-dimensional Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process
, and solutions to (
4.1
) for
n\in\mathbb{N}
are spatially discretized approximations.
Therefore, it is natural to ask whether we can identify the right mode of
convergence of the operators
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
to
A_{\infty}
as
n\to\infty

to
ensure the
convergence of the processes

(W_{A_{n}})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
to
W_{A_{\infty}}
.

The answer is provided by Proposition
4.4

below, which is a stochastic counterpart of the discrete-to-continuum
Trotter–Kato approximation theorem for
strongly continuous semigroups recalled in Theorem
2.3
.
In fact, with an eye towards the proof of
Proposition
4.5
below, we consider a more
general class of auxiliary processes, see equation (
4.8
).

Before stating any discrete-to-continuum results, let us introduce some
convenient notation for this goal.
Using the operators

(\Lambda_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(\Pi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, we can take a mapping which has

E_{n}
as its domain or state space,
and turn it into an analogous mapping from or to
E_{\infty}
.
For instance, we define the
E_{\infty}
-valued processes
\widetilde{W}_{A_{n}}\coloneqq\Lambda_{n}W_{A_{n}}
, as well as the operators
\widetilde{R}_{n}^{\alpha}\coloneqq\Lambda_{n}R_{n}^{\alpha}\Pi_{n}
and
\widetilde{S}_{n}(t)\coloneqq\Lambda_{n}S_{n}(t)\Pi_{n}
in
\mathscr{L}(E_{\infty})
for
\alpha,t\in[0,\infty)
.
Now we can formulate our notion of the convergence
A_{n}\to A_{\infty}
as
n\to\infty
as follows:

(A3)

For every
x\in E_{\infty}
, we have
\widetilde{R}_{n}^{1}x\to R_{\infty}^{1}x
in

E_{\infty}
as
n\to\infty
. Moreover, given
\beta\in[0,\frac{1}{2})
as in
(A2)
, we have
\widetilde{R}_{n}^{\beta}\to R_{\infty}^{\beta}
in
\gamma(H_{\infty};E_{\infty})
.

Proposition
4.4
states that this type of convergence of the operators is
sufficient to ensure
convergence of the solutions to the linear stochastic evolution
equation (
4.1
).
Its proof is based on Lemma
C.1
in Appendix
C
, as well as the following general approximation lemma for square-integrable functions with values in the space of
\gamma
-radonifying operators.
It is a simpler analog to
[
55
, Lemma 2.6]
, which was only necessary to allow for stochastic equations in UMD Banach spaces without type
2
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
(E,\|\,\cdot\,\|_{E})
and
(F,\|\,\cdot\,\|_{F})
be Banach spaces, and let
(H,\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{H})
be a Hilbert space.
Given
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
with
a<b
, let
M_{n}\colon(a,b)\to\mathscr{L}(E;F)
for all
n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}
and suppose that

(i)

M_{n}\otimes x\to M\otimes x
uniformly on compact subsets of
(a,b)
for all
x\in E
, and

(ii)

\sup_{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}\sup_{t\in(a,b)}\|M_{n}(t)\|_{\mathscr{L}(E;F)}<\infty
.

For all
R\in L^{2}(a,b;\gamma(H;E))
and
n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}
, we have
M_{n}\otimes R\in L^{2}(a,b;\gamma(H;F))
and

(4.6)

M_{n}R\to MR\quad\text{in }L^{2}(a,b;\gamma(H;F))\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

Proof.

Arguing as in the proof of Proposition
B.3
and using
(ii)
, it follows that we only need to prove the claim for all
R
belonging to some dense subset
D
of
L^{2}(a,b;\gamma(H;E))
.
Note that every
R\in L^{2}(a,b;\gamma(H;E))
can be approximated by a step function
\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathbf{1}_{(a_{j},b_{j}^{\prime})}\otimes T_{j}
with
a<a_{j}^{\prime}<b_{j}^{\prime}<b
and
T_{j}\in\gamma(H;E)
, and by definition of
\gamma(H;E)
the latter can be approximated by finite-rank operators.
By linearity, it thus suffices to prove the statement for
R
of the form

R(t)=\mathbf{1}_{(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})}(t)\,h\otimes x,\quad\text{where }a<a^{\prime}<b^{\prime}<b\text{ and }(h,x)\in H\times E.

Substituting this representation, using (
2.5
) and
(i)
, we find as
n\to\infty
:

(4.7)

\displaystyle\|M_{n}R-MR\|_{L^{2}(a,b;\gamma(H;F))}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{a^{\prime}}^{b^{\prime}}\|h\otimes[M_{n}(t)x-M(t)x]\|_{\gamma(H;F)}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\|h\|_{H}^{2}\int_{a^{\prime}}^{b^{\prime}}\|M_{n}(t)x-M(t)x\|_{F}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\|h\|_{H}^{2}(b^{\prime}-a^{\prime})\sup_{t\in(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})}\|M_{n}(t)x-M(t)x\|_{F}^{2}\to 0.\qed

Proposition 4.4
.

Suppose that Assumptions
(A1)
and
(A2)
hold.
Let us define the auxiliary processes

(4.8)

W^{\delta}_{A_{n}}(t)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\Gamma(\delta)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\delta-1}S_{n}(t-s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{n}(s),\quad\delta\in(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}},\infty),\;t\in[0,\infty),

where
\Gamma
denotes the Gamma function
[
67
, Section 5.2]
.
Then, for every
\beta^{\prime}\in(\beta,\infty)
,
T\in(0,\infty)
and
p\in[1,\infty)
, we have
W_{A_{n}}^{\beta^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}}\in C([0,T];L^{p}(\Omega;E_{n}))
for all
n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}
.
If we suppose in addition that Assumption
(A3)
is satisfied, then

(4.9)

\widetilde{W}_{A_{n}}^{\beta^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}}\to W_{A_{\infty}}^{\beta^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}}\quad\text{in }C([0,T];L^{p}(\Omega;E_{\infty}))\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

Proof.

The fact that
W_{A_{n}}^{\beta^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}}\in C([0,T];L^{p}(\Omega;E_{n}))
for all
n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}
can be established by arguing as in Proposition
4.1
, thus using Assumptions
(A1)
and
(A2)
.
For all
t\in[0,T]
, the Itô inequality (
2.7
) yields

(4.10)

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{W}_{A_{n}}^{\beta^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}}(t)-W_{A_{\infty}}^{\beta^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}}(t)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;E_{\infty})}

(4.11)

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim_{(p,E_{\infty})}\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2})}\biggl(\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{2\beta^{\prime}-1}\|\widetilde{S}_{n}(t-s)-S_{\infty}(t-s)\|_{\gamma(H_{\infty};E_{\infty})}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\biggr)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(4.12)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2})}\biggl(\int_{0}^{T}s^{2\beta^{\prime}-1}\|\widetilde{S}_{n}(s)-S_{\infty}(s)\|_{\gamma(H_{\infty};E_{\infty})}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\biggr)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Since semigroups commute with fractional powers of their infinitesimal generators, we can write the difference between the semigroups as follows:

(4.13)

\displaystyle\widetilde{S}_{n}(s)-S_{\infty}(s)

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{n}(\operatorname{Id}_{n}+A_{n})^{\beta}S_{n}(s)\Pi_{n}\widetilde{R}_{n}^{\beta}-(\operatorname{Id}_{\infty}+A_{\infty})^{\beta}S_{\infty}(s)R_{\infty}^{\beta}

(4.14)

\displaystyle=\begin{aligned} &\Lambda_{n}(\operatorname{Id}_{n}+A_{n})^{\beta}S_{n}(s)\Pi_{n}(\widetilde{R}_{n}^{\beta}-R_{\infty}^{\beta})\\
&+(\Lambda_{n}(\operatorname{Id}_{n}+A_{n})^{\beta}S_{n}(s)\Pi_{n}-(\operatorname{Id}_{\infty}+A_{\infty})^{\beta}S_{\infty})R_{\infty}^{\beta}.\end{aligned}

Thus, by the triangle inequality, it suffices to show that

(4.15)

\displaystyle\text{(I)}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{0}^{T}s^{2\beta^{\prime}-1}\|\Lambda_{n}(\operatorname{Id}_{n}+A_{n})^{\beta}S_{n}(s)\Pi_{n}(\widetilde{R}_{n}^{\beta}-R_{\infty}^{\beta})\|_{\gamma(H_{\infty};E_{\infty})}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\quad\text{ and }

\displaystyle\text{(II)}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{0}^{T}s^{2\beta^{\prime}-1}\|[\Lambda_{n}(\operatorname{Id}_{n}+A_{n})^{\beta}S_{n}(s)\Pi_{n}-(\operatorname{Id}_{\infty}+R_{\infty})^{\beta}S_{\infty}]R_{\infty}^{\beta}\|_{\gamma(H_{\infty};E_{\infty})}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

tend to zero as
n\to\infty
.
Applying the ideal property (
2.4
) of
\gamma(H_{\infty};E_{\infty})
, followed by the analytic semigroup estimate (
C.3
) in conjunction with Assumptions
(A1)
and
(A2)
, we find

(4.16)

\displaystyle\text{(I)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\widetilde{R}_{n}^{\beta}-R_{\infty}^{\beta}\|_{\gamma(H_{\infty};E_{\infty})}^{2}\int_{0}^{T}s^{2\beta^{\prime}-1}\|\Lambda_{n}(\operatorname{Id}_{n}+A_{n})^{\beta}S_{n}(s)\Pi_{n}\|_{\mathscr{L}(E_{\infty})}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim_{(\beta,M_{\Lambda},M_{\Pi})}\|\widetilde{R}_{n}^{\beta}-R_{\infty}^{\beta}\|_{\gamma(H_{\infty};E_{\infty})}^{2}\int_{0}^{T}s^{2(\beta^{\prime}-\beta)-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\frac{T^{2(\beta^{\prime}-\beta)}}{2(\beta^{\prime}-\beta)}\|\widetilde{R}_{n}^{\beta}-R_{\infty}^{\beta}\|_{\gamma(H_{\infty};E_{\infty})}^{2}\to 0,

where the convergence on the last line follows from the second part of
(A3)
.
The convergence
\mathrm{(II)}\to 0
follows by applying Lemma
4.3
with

(4.17)

M_{n}(t)\coloneqq t^{\beta}\Lambda_{n}(\operatorname{Id}_{n}+A_{n})^{\beta}S_{n}(t)\Pi_{n}\quad\text{and}\quad R(t)\coloneqq t^{\beta^{\prime}-\beta-\frac{1}{2}}R_{\infty}^{\beta},

Indeed, this is justified since
R\in L^{2}(0,T;\gamma(H_{\infty};E_{\infty}))
with

(4.18)

\|R\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\gamma(H_{\infty};E_{\infty}))}^{2}=\frac{T^{2(\beta^{\prime}-\beta)}}{2(\beta^{\prime}-\beta)}\|R^{\beta}_{\infty}\|_{\gamma(H_{\infty};E_{\infty})}^{2}<\infty,

condition
(ii)
is verified by applying (
C.3
) to
\|M_{n}(t)\|_{\mathscr{L}(E_{\infty})}
combined with Assumptions
(A1)
and
(A2)
as in (
4.16
), and hypothesis
(i)
holds by Lemma
C.1
.
∎

We will show that there exist modifications of
W_{A_{\infty}}
and

(\widetilde{W}_{A_{n}})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
which, for all
p\in[1,\infty)
and
T\in(0,\infty)
, belong to
L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E_{\infty}))
and converge in
this norm.
In
particular, as
n\to\infty
, their trajectories converge uniformly on
bounded time intervals,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.

The proof is based on the Da Prato–Kwapień–Zabczyk factorization method,
first formulated in
[
19
]
for Hilbert spaces (see also
[
20
, Section 5.3]
), and later extended to UMD-type-2 Banach spaces in
[
9
, Theorem 3.2]
.
The general idea is to express the process
W_{A_{n}}
as the ‘product’
\mathfrak{I}_{A_{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}-\beta^{\prime}}W^{\frac{1}{2}+\beta^{\prime}}_{A_{n}}
of a fractional parabolic integral operator
\mathfrak{I}_{A_{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}-\beta^{\prime}}
as in Appendix
B
and auxiliary process
W^{\frac{1}{2}+\beta^{\prime}}_{A_{n}}
as in (
4.8
), and using the smoothing properties of the former.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
and
T\in(0,\infty)
.
If Assumptions
(A1)
–
(A2)

hold, then for every
n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}
there exists a modification of
W_{A_{n}}
which belongs to
L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E_{n})
, which we will identify with
W_{A_{n}}
itself.

If, in addition, Assumption
(A3)
holds, then the sequence
(\widetilde{W}_{A_{n}})_{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}
satisfies

(4.19)

\widetilde{W}_{A_{n}}\to W_{A_{\infty}}\quad\text{in }L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E_{\infty}))\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

Proof.

Let
\beta^{\prime}\in(\beta,\frac{1}{2})
, where
\beta\in[0,\frac{1}{2})
is as in
(A2)
.
Since
L^{p}(\Omega)
-spaces with higher exponents are embedded in those with lower ones contractively (because
\mathbb{P}(\Omega)=1
), we assume without loss of generality that
p\in((\frac{1}{2}-\beta)^{-1},\infty)
.
By the first part of Proposition
4.4
(thus using Assumptions
(A1)
and
(A2)
) and the Fubini theorem, we have for all
n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}
:

(4.20)

W^{\frac{1}{2}+\beta^{\prime}}_{A_{n}}\in C([0,T];L^{p}(\Omega;E_{n}))\hookrightarrow L^{p}(0,T;L^{p}(\Omega;E_{n}))\cong L^{p}(\Omega;L^{p}(0,T;E_{n})),

where the constants for the first embedding depend only on
p
and
T
.
In particular, there exists an event
\Omega_{1}\subseteq\Omega
with
\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{1})=1
such that
W^{\frac{1}{2}+\beta^{\prime}}_{A_{n}}(\omega,\,\cdot\,)
belongs to
L^{p}(0,T;E_{n})
for all
\omega\in\Omega_{1}
.
It then follows from Proposition
B.1
(ii)
that
\mathfrak{I}_{A_{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}-\beta^{\prime}}W^{\frac{1}{2}+\beta^{\prime}}_{A_{n}}(\omega,\,\cdot\,)
belongs to
C([0,T];E_{n})
, where
(\mathfrak{I}_{A_{n}}^{s})_{s\in[0,\infty)}
are the fractional parabolic integral operators defined by (
B.2
) in Appendix
B
.
In this case,
the process

\mathfrak{I}_{A_{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}-\beta^{\prime}}W^{\frac{1}{2}+\beta^{\prime}}_{A_{n}}
(set to
zero outside of
\Omega_{1}
) belongs
to
L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E_{n}))
,
and by the factorization theorem
[
9
, Theorem 3.2]
it is a modification of
W_{A_{n}}
.

For the lifted processes, the
properties of the embeddings and
projections from assumption
(A1)
imply that

\widetilde{\mathfrak{I}}_{A_{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}-\beta^{\prime}}\widetilde{W}_{A_{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}+\beta^{\prime}}\in L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E_{\infty}))
is a
continuous
modification of

\widetilde{W}_{A_{n}}
, where

\widetilde{\mathfrak{I}}_{A_{n}}^{s}\coloneqq\Lambda_{n}\mathfrak{I}_{A_{n}}^{s}\Pi_{n}
.
Identifying
\widetilde{W}_{A_{n}}
with its factorized continuous
modification for every
n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}
, we can estimate as follows:

(4.21)

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{W}_{A_{n}}-W_{A_{\infty}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E_{\infty}))}

\displaystyle=\|\widetilde{\mathfrak{I}}_{A_{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}-\beta^{\prime}}\widetilde{W}_{A_{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}+\beta^{\prime}}-{\mathfrak{I}}_{A_{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}-\beta^{\prime}}W_{A_{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}+\beta^{\prime}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E_{\infty}))}

\displaystyle\leq\|\widetilde{\mathfrak{I}}_{A_{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}-\beta^{\prime}}(\widetilde{W}_{A_{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}+\beta^{\prime}}-W_{A_{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}+\beta^{\prime}})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E_{\infty}))}

\displaystyle+\|(\widetilde{\mathfrak{I}}_{A_{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}-\beta^{\prime}}-\mathfrak{I}_{A_{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}-\beta^{\prime}})W_{A_{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}+\beta^{\prime}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E_{\infty}))}.

Since
\frac{1}{2}-\beta^{\prime}>\frac{1}{p}
, we can apply Corollary
B.2
(ii)
to find that
\widetilde{\mathfrak{I}}_{A_{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}-\beta^{\prime}}
is a bounded
linear operator from

L^{p}(0,T;E_{\infty})

to
C([0,T];E_{\infty})
whose norm can be bounded independently of
n
. Thus, by the
above
discussion and the second part of Proposition
4.4
(which uses Assumption
(A3)
), we find

(4.22)

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\mathfrak{I}}_{A_{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}-\beta^{\prime}}(\widetilde{W}_{A_{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}+\beta^{\prime}}-W_{A_{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}+\beta^{\prime}})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E_{\infty}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\widetilde{W}_{A_{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}+\beta^{\prime}}-W_{A_{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}+\beta^{\prime}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega\times(0,T);E_{\infty})}\leq T^{\frac{1}{p}}\|\widetilde{W}_{A_{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}+\beta^{\prime}}-W_{A_{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}+\beta^{\prime}}\|_{C([0,T];L^{p}(\Omega;E_{\infty}))}\to 0.

Now we note that, for all
\omega\in\Omega
,
Proposition
B.3
(i)

implies that

(4.23)

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\mathfrak{I}}_{A_{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}-\beta^{\prime}}W_{A_{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}+\beta^{\prime}}(\omega,\,\cdot\,)-\mathfrak{I}_{A_{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}-\kappa}W_{A_{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}+\beta^{\prime}}(\omega,\,\cdot\,)\|_{C([0,T];E_{\infty})}\to 0.

Again by Corollary
B.2
(ii)
,
we moreover have

(4.24)

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\mathfrak{I}}_{A_{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}-\beta^{\prime}}W_{A_{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}+\beta^{\prime}}(\omega,\,\cdot\,)-\mathfrak{I}_{A_{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}-\beta^{\prime}}W_{A_{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}+\beta^{\prime}}(\omega,\,\cdot\,)\|_{C([0,T];E_{\infty})}\lesssim 2\|W_{A_{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}+\beta^{\prime}}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;E_{\infty})}

with constant independent of
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and since

W_{A_{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}+\beta^{\prime}}\in L^{p}((0,T)\times\Omega;E_{\infty})
,
the dominated convergence theorem yields

\|(\widetilde{\mathfrak{I}}_{A_{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}-\beta^{\prime}}-\mathfrak{I}_{A_{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}-\beta^{\prime}})W_{A_{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}+\beta^{\prime}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E_{\infty}))}\to 0.\qed

5.
Approximation of semilinear stochastic evolution equations with additive cylindrical
Wiener noise

In this section, we shall extend the results from Section
4
regarding the
linear

E_{n}
-valued equation (
4.1
) to the
semilinear case
.
As before, let the spaces
(E_{n})_{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}
,
(H_{n})_{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}
and the operators
(A_{n})_{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}
satisfy assumptions
(A1)
and
(A2)
, respectively, and suppose that
W_{n}\coloneqq\Pi_{n}W_{\infty}
is an
H_{n}
-valued
Q_{n}
-cylindrical Wiener process (with
Q_{n}\coloneqq\Pi_{n}\Pi_{n}^{*}
), supported on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]},\mathbb{P})
.
Let
T\in(0,\infty)
be a finite time horizon.
In this section, we suppose moreover that we are given a drift coefficient function
F_{n}\colon\Omega\times[0,T]\times E_{n}\to E_{n}
and initial datum
\xi_{n}\colon\Omega\to E_{n}
.
We will consider the following semilinear stochastic evolution equation:

(5.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}X_{n}(t)&=-A_{n}X_{n}(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+F_{n}(t,X_{n}(t))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{n}(t),\quad t\in(0,T],\\
X_{n}(0)&=\xi_{n}.\end{aligned}\right.

Note that
F_{n}(\omega,t,\,\cdot\,)
is a (nonlinear) operator on
E_{n}
for all
(\omega,t)\in\Omega\times[0,T]
; in Section
3
, we considered the specific case
[F_{n}(\omega,t,u_{n})](x)\coloneqq f_{n}(\omega,t,u_{n}(x))
for some real-valued nonlinearity
f_{n}
.

In what follows, we shall impose more precise conditions on the
F_{n}
and

\xi_{n}
to ensure the well-posedness
of (
5.1
) for every fixed
n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}
and to obtain discrete-to-continuum convergence of the respective solutions
as
n\to\infty
.

In Section
5.1
we will assume in particular
that the drifts
(F_{n})_{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}
are uniformly globally Lipschitz and of linear growth to obtain the existence of unique global solutions
(X_{n}(t))_{t\in[0,T]}
, whose lifted counterparts
\widetilde{X}_{n}
converge to
X_{\infty}
in

L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E_{\infty}))
as
n\to\infty
, where
p\in[1,\infty)
is the stochastic integrability of the initial data.
These assumptions
are relaxed
in
Section
5.2
, where we suppose that the drifts are uniformly
locally Lipschitz and uniformly bounded near zero. In general, this comes at
the
cost of obtaining merely local solutions, converging in a weaker norm.
However, if one can show independently that the solutions are global and the

L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E_{n}))
-norms of
X_{n}
are uniformly bounded in
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then we recover the stronger sense of convergence.

5.1.
Globally Lipschitz drifts of linear growth

In this section we suppose that the drift coefficients

F_{n}\colon\Omega\times[0,T]\times E_{n}\to E_{n}

in (
5.1
) for
n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}
are uniformly globally Lipschitz continuous and of linear growth. More
precisely:

(F1)

There exist
L_{F},C_{F}\in(0,\infty)
such that, for
all

t\in[0,T]
,
\omega\in\Omega
,
n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}

and
x_{n},y_{n}\in E_{n}
,

(5.2)

\displaystyle\|F_{n}(\omega,t,x_{n})-F_{n}(\omega,t,y_{n})\|_{E_{n}}

\displaystyle\leq L_{F}\|x_{n}-y_{n}\|_{E_{n}};

(5.3)

\displaystyle\|F_{n}(\omega,t,x_{n})\|_{E_{n}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{F}(1+\|x_{n}\|_{E_{n}}).

Moreover, the process

(\omega,t)\mapsto F_{n}(\omega,t,x_{n})
is
strongly measurable and adapted to the filtration
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
.

Now let us comment on the existence and uniqueness of solutions
to (
5.1
) for fixed
n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}
.
We will use the following concept of global mild solutions, see
[
83
, pp. 969–970]
.
In Subsection
5.2
, we also introduce the concept of local solutions, which may blow up in finite time.
In particular, a local solution which exists
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. on the whole of
[0,T]
is in fact global.

Recall that
(S_{n}(t))_{t\geq 0}
denotes the
C_{0}
-semigroup on
E_{n}
generated by
-A_{n}
.

Definition 5.1
.

An
E_{n}
-valued stochastic process
X_{n}=(X_{n}(t))_{t\in[0,T]}
is a
global mild solution
to (
5.1
) with coefficients
(A_{n},F_{n},\xi_{n})
if

(i)

X_{n}\colon\Omega\times[0,T]\to E_{n}
is strongly measurable
and

(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
-adapted;

(ii)

s\mapsto S_{n}(t-s)F_{n}(s,X_{n}(s))\in L^{0}(\Omega;L^{1}(0,t;E_{n}))

for every
t\in[0,T]
;

(iii)

s\mapsto S_{n}(t-s)\in L^{2}(0,t;\gamma(H_{n};E_{n}))
for
every
t\in[0,T]
;

(iv)

for all
t\in[0,T]
, we have

(5.4)

X_{n}(t)=S_{n}(t)\xi_{n}+\int_{0}^{t}S_{n}(t-s)F_{n}(s,X_{n}(s))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+W_{A_{n}}(t),\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

In the present framework, existence and uniqueness can be proven by
showing that
the operator

\Phi_{n,T}
given by

(5.5)

[\Phi_{n,T}(u_{n})](t)\coloneqq S_{n}(t)\xi_{n}+\int_{0}^{t}S_{n}(t-s)F_{n}(s,u_{n}(s))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+W_{A_{n}}(t)

is a well-defined and Lipschitz-continuous mapping on
L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E_{n}))
, whose Lipschitz constant tends to zero as
T\downarrow 0
(see
[
83
, Proposition 6.1]
or
[
55
, Theorem 3.7]
for more general results):

Proposition 5.2
.

Suppose that Assumptions
(A1)
,
(A2)
and
(F1)
are satisfied. Let
n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}
,
p\in[1,\infty)
,
\xi_{n}\in L^{p}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{0},\mathbb{P};E_{n})
and
T\in(0,\infty)
.
The operator
\Phi_{n,T}
given by (
5.5
) is well defined and Lipschitz continuous on
L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E_{n}))
.
Its Lipschitz constant is independent of
\xi_{n}
, depends on
A_{n}
and
F_{n}
only through
M_{S}
and
L_{F}
, and tends to zero as
T\downarrow 0
.

Proof.

The fact that
S_{n}\otimes\xi_{n}\in L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E_{n}))
is immediate from
(A1)
–
(A2)
and
\xi_{n}\in L^{p}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{0},\mathbb{P};E_{n})
.
We also have
W_{A_{n}}\in L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E_{n}))
by the first part of Proposition
4.5
.
Given
u_{n}\in L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E_{n}))
, it follows from
(F1)
that
\|s\mapsto F_{n}(s,u_{n}(s))\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;E_{n})}\leq C_{F}(1+\|u_{n}\|_{C([0,T];E_{n})})
, so that
S_{n}*F_{n}(\,\cdot\,,u_{n})
belongs to
L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E_{n}))
with

(5.6)

\|S_{n}*F_{n}(\,\cdot\,,u_{n})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E_{n}))}\leq C_{F}(1+\|u_{n}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E_{n}))})

by Proposition
B.1
(ii)
with
E=F=E_{n}
,
\alpha=0
and
s=1
(noting that
S_{n}*f=\mathfrak{I}_{A_{n}}^{1}f
, see Appendix
B
).
This shows that
\Phi_{n,T}
is well-defined.

Now let
u_{n},v_{n}\in L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E_{n}))
and observe that

(5.7)

\Phi_{n,T}(u_{n})-\Phi_{n,T}(v_{n})=\int_{0}^{t}S_{n}(t-s)[F_{n}(s,u_{n}(s))-F_{n}(s,v_{n}(s))]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

A straightforward estimate involving Assumptions
(A1)
,
(A2)
and
(F1)
then yields

\|\Phi_{n,T}(u_{n})-\Phi_{n,T}(v_{n})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E_{n}))}\leq M_{S}L_{F}T\,\|u_{n}-v_{n}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E_{n}))}.\qed

Under the conditions of
Proposition
5.2
, it follows from
the Banach fixed-point theorem
that (
5.1
)
has a unique solution on a small enough time interval
[0,T_{0}]
, which can be
extended to a unique global mild solution on any
[0,T]
by “patching together” solutions on small time
intervals:

Proposition 5.3
.

Suppose that Assumptions
(A1)
,
(A2)
and
(F1)
are satisfied, and let
n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}
,
p\in[1,\infty)
,
\xi_{n}\in L^{p}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{0},\mathbb{P};E_{n})
and
T\in(0,\infty)
.
Then (
5.1
) has a unique global mild solution
X_{n}\in L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E_{n}))
.

Proof.

By Proposition
5.2
, there exists
T_{0}\in(0,\infty)
such that
\Phi_{n,T_{0}}
is a strict contraction on
L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T_{0}];E_{n}))
, and thus has a unique fixed point
X_{n}
.
Since the bound on the Lipschitz constant of
\Phi_{n,T}
only depended on
M_{S}
,
L_{F}
and
T
, we can repeat the previous argument to obtain a unique solution
Y\in L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T_{0}];E_{n}))
for (
5.1
) with initial datum
\eta_{n}\coloneqq X_{n}(\frac{1}{2}T_{0})
, drift
G_{n}(\,\cdot\,,u_{n})\coloneqq F_{n}(\,\cdot\,+\frac{1}{2}T_{0},u_{n})
and noise
\widehat{W}_{n}\coloneqq W_{n}(\,\cdot\,+\frac{1}{2}T_{0})
.
It can then be argued directly using Definition
5.1
that the concatenation of the processes
X_{n}
and
Y_{n}(\,\cdot\,+\frac{1}{2}T_{0})
is the unique mild solution to (
5.1
) with the original data on
[0,\frac{3}{2}T_{0}]
.
Proceeding inductively, we find the same conclusion for all intervals
[0,(k+\frac{1}{2})T_{0}]
with
k\in\mathbb{N}
and thus for
[0,T]
.
∎

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we analogously define the lifted initial datum
\widetilde{\xi}_{n}\colon\Omega\to E_{\infty}
by
\widetilde{\xi}_{n}\coloneqq\Lambda_{n}\xi_{n}
and the lifted drift coefficient
\widetilde{F}_{n}\colon\Omega\times[0,T]\times E_{\infty}\to E_{\infty}
by

(5.8)

\widetilde{F}_{n}(\omega,t,x)\coloneqq\Lambda_{n}F_{n}(\omega,t,\Pi_{n}x),\quad(\omega,t,x)\in\Omega\times[0,T]\times E_{\infty}.

We will assume that the initial data and drift coefficients are approximated in
the following way:

(IC)

There exists
p\in[1,\infty)
such that

(\xi_{n})_{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}\in\prod_{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}L^{p}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{0},\mathbb{P};E_{n})
and

(5.9)

\widetilde{\xi}_{n}\to\xi_{\infty}\quad\text{in }L^{p}(\Omega;E_{\infty})\text{ as }n\to\infty.

(F2)

For a.e.
(\omega,t)\in\Omega\times[0,T]
and every
x\in E_{\infty}
, we have

(5.10)

\widetilde{F}_{n}(\omega,t,x)\to F_{\infty}(\omega,t,x)\quad\text{ in }E_{\infty}\text{ as }n\to\infty.

Under these assumptions, we obtain the main result of this section, namely the
following discrete-to-continuum convergence theorem in the context of uniformly
globally
Lipschitz
nonlinearities of linear growth.
It is analogous to
[
55
, Theorem 4.3]
.

Theorem 5.4
.

Suppose that
(A1)
–
(A3)
,
(F1)
–
(F2)
and
(IC)
are satisfied, with
p\in[1,\infty)
.
For all
n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}
and
T\in(0,\infty)
, let
X_{n}=(X_{n}(t))_{t\in[0,T]}
denote the unique global
solution to (
5.1
), and let

\widetilde{X}_{n}\coloneqq\Lambda_{n}X_{n}
.
Then we have

(5.11)

\widetilde{X}_{n}\to X_{\infty}\quad\text{in }L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E_{\infty}))\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

Its proof involves the lifted counterparts of
\Phi_{n,T}
, defined by

(5.12)

\widetilde{\Phi}_{n,T}\coloneqq\Lambda_{n}\Phi_{n,T}\circ\Pi_{n}\colon L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E_{\infty}))\to L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E_{\infty})),

i.e., for all
u\in L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E_{\infty}))
and
t\in[0,T]
, we have

(5.13)

\displaystyle[\widetilde{\Phi}_{n,T}(u)](t)\coloneqq{}

\displaystyle\Lambda_{n}S_{n}(t)\xi_{n}+\int_{0}^{t}\Lambda_{n}S_{n}(t-s)F_{n}(s,\Pi_{n}u(s))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\Lambda_{n}W_{A_{n}}(t)

(5.14)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\widetilde{S}_{n}(t)\widetilde{\xi}_{n}+\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{S}_{n}(t-s)\widetilde{F}_{n}(s,u(s))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\widetilde{W}_{A_{n}}(t),\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.},

where the second identity is due to
Assumption
2.1
(iii)
.
Using the tensor and convolution notations from Section
2.1
, it can be expressed even more concisely as

(5.15)

\widetilde{\Phi}_{n,T}(u)=\widetilde{S}_{n}\otimes\widetilde{\xi}_{n}+\widetilde{S}_{n}*\widetilde{F}_{n}(\,\cdot\,,u)+\widetilde{W}_{A_{n}}

In particular, we will show that all three terms of (
5.15
) converge to their “continuum” counterparts; they are addressed by Lemmas
5.5
–
5.6
below (which are analogous to
[
55
, Lemma 4.4, 4.5(1) and 4.5(3)]
), as well as Proposition
4.5
.

Lemma 5.5
.

If
(A1)
–
(A3)

and
(IC)
hold with
p\in[1,\infty)
,
then we have

(5.16)

\widetilde{S}_{n}\otimes\xi_{n}\to S_{\infty}\otimes\xi_{\infty}\quad\text{in }L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E_{\infty}))\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

Proof.

As in the beginning of the proof of Proposition
5.2
, it follows from
(A1)
–
(A2)
and
(IC)
that
S_{n}\otimes\xi_{n}\in L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E_{n}))
for all
n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}
.
Applying the projection and lifting operators from
(A1)
, we thus find
\widetilde{S}_{n}\otimes\widetilde{\xi}_{n}\in L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E_{\infty}))
.

The triangle inequality implies

(5.17)

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{S}_{n}\otimes\widetilde{\xi}_{n}-S_{\infty}\otimes\xi_{\infty}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E_{\infty}))}

(5.18)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\|\widetilde{S}_{n}\otimes(\widetilde{\xi}_{n}-\xi_{\infty})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E_{\infty}))}+\|(\widetilde{S}_{n}-S_{\infty})\otimes\xi_{\infty}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E_{\infty}))}.

By
(A1)
–
(A2)
and
(IC)
, for the first term we have, as
n\to\infty
:

(5.19)

\|\widetilde{S}_{n}\otimes(\widetilde{\xi}_{n}-\xi_{\infty})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E_{\infty}))}\leq M_{\Lambda}M_{S}M_{\Pi}\|\widetilde{\xi}_{n}-\xi_{\infty}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;E_{\infty})}\to 0

For the second term, first note that

\widetilde{S}_{n}\otimes\xi_{\infty}(\omega)\to S_{\infty}\otimes\xi_{\infty}(\omega)
in

C([0,T];E_{\infty})
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s., by Theorem
2.3
, where we now also use
(A3)
.
Since we moreover have

(5.20)

\|\widetilde{S}_{n}\otimes\xi_{\infty}(\omega)-S_{\infty}\otimes\xi_{\infty}(\omega)\|_{C([0,T];E_{\infty})}\leq M_{S}(M_{\Lambda}M_{\Pi}+1)\|\xi_{\infty}(\omega)\|_{E_{\infty}},

and the right-hand side belongs to
L^{p}(\Omega)
by assumption, we deduce that also

\|(\widetilde{S}_{n}-S_{\infty})\otimes\xi_{\infty}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E_{\infty}))}\to 0\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.\qed

Lemma 5.6
.

Suppose that Assumptions
(A1)
,
(A2)
,
(F1)
and
(F2)
are satisfied.
Let
p\in[1,\infty)
and
u\in L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E_{\infty}))
be given.
Then we have

(5.21)

\widetilde{S}_{n}*\widetilde{F}_{n}(\,\cdot\,,u)\to S_{\infty}*F_{\infty}(\,\cdot\,,u)\quad\text{in }L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E_{\infty}))\text{ as }n\to\infty.

Proof.

Similarly to the proof of Proposition
5.2
, it follows from
(A1)
–
(A2)
and
(F1)
that
\widetilde{S}_{n}*\widetilde{F}_{n}(\,\cdot\,,u)\in L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E_{\infty}))
for all
n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}
.
By the triangle inequality, we can split up the statement into the following two assertions:

(i)

\widetilde{S}_{n}*\widetilde{F}_{n}(\,\cdot\,,u)-\widetilde{S}_{n}*F_{\infty}(\,\cdot\,,u)\to 0
in
L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E_{\infty}))

as
n\to\infty
;

(ii)

\widetilde{S}_{n}*F_{\infty}(\,\cdot\,,u)\to S_{\infty}*F_{\infty}(\,\cdot\,,u)
in

L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E_{\infty}))

as
n\to\infty
.

For almost every
(\omega,t)\in\Omega\times[0,T]
, we have by
(F1)
and
(A1)
:

(5.22)

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{F}_{n}(\omega,t,u(\omega,t))-F_{\infty}(\omega,t,u(\omega,t))\|_{E_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{F}(M_{\Lambda}+1+(M_{\Pi}M_{\Lambda}+1)\|u(\omega,t)\|_{E_{\infty}}).

It follows that

(5.23)

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{F}_{n}(\,\cdot\,,u)-F_{\infty}(\,\cdot\,,u)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega\times(0,T);E_{\infty})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{(C_{F},M_{\Lambda},M_{\Pi})}\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega\times(0,T);E_{\infty})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{(p,T)}\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E_{\infty}))}<\infty.

Since
\widetilde{S}_{n}*f=\widetilde{\mathfrak{I}}_{A_{n}}^{1}f
for all
f\in L^{p}(0,T;E_{\infty})
, we can apply Proposition
B.1
(ii)
with
E=F=E_{\infty}
,
\alpha=0
and
s=1
to find that

(5.24)

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{S}_{n}*(\widetilde{F}_{n}(\,\cdot\,,u)-F_{\infty}(\,\cdot\,,u))\|_{C([0,T];E_{\infty})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{(s,r,T,M_{S})}\|\widetilde{F}_{n}(\,\cdot\,,u)-F_{\infty}(\,\cdot\,,u)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega\times(0,T);E_{\infty})}.

The latter tends to zero as
n\to\infty
by the dominated convergence theorem, which applies in view of
(F2)
and (
5.22
)–(
5.23
).
This shows
(i)
.

For
(ii)
, we derive in the same way that, for almost every
\omega\in\Omega
,

(5.25)

t\mapsto F_{\infty}(\omega,t,u(\omega,t))\in L^{p}(0,T;E_{\infty}),

which implies, cf. Proposition
B.3
(i)
with
\widetilde{E}\coloneqq E_{\infty}
, that

(5.26)

6.

In this section, we introduce another family of Banach spaces

The main purpose of this setting is to eventually specialize to the class of stochastic reaction–diffusion

This problem amounts to letting the drift

in (

), i.e., defining it by

(6.2)

[F_{n}(\omega,t,u)](x)\coloneqq f_{n}(\omega,t,u(x)),\quad(\omega,t,x)\in\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathcal{D}_{n},

for a given function
u\colon\mathcal{D}_{n}\to\mathbb{R}
. However, in order for a Nemytskii operator
F_{n}
to
inherit the local Lipschitz continuity from
f_{n}
, we cannot view it as acting on the UMD-type-2 Banach space
E_{n}=L^{q}(\mathcal{D}_{n})
with
q\in[1,\infty)
.
In fact, by
[
1
, Theorem 3.9]
, the operator
u\mapsto F_{n}(\omega,t,u)
defined by (
6.2
) is weakly continuous on
L^{q}(\mathcal{D}_{n})
(meaning that it maps weakly convergent sequences to weakly convergent sequences) if and only if it is affine in
u
, i.e., there exist coefficients
a_{n}(\omega,t),b_{n}(\omega,t)\in\mathbb{R}
such that
[F_{n}(\omega,t,u)](x)=a_{n}(\omega,t)+b_{n}(\omega,t)u(x)
for all
x\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
.
In particular, if
(F_{n})_{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}
is a family of Nemytskii operators which is uniformly locally Lipschitz in the sense of
(F1′)
on the spaces
E_{n}=L^{q}(\mathcal{D}_{n})
, then it is in fact globally Lipschitz and of linear growth in the sense of
(F1)
.

Thus, we will instead view
F_{n}
as an operator on
B_{n}\coloneqq C(\overline{\mathcal{D}_{n}})
(which coincides with
B_{n}=L^{\infty}(\mathcal{D}_{n})
if
\mathcal{D}_{n}
is discrete).
This, in turn, poses a difficulty for stochastic evolution equations, since there is no theory for the stochastic integration of integrands taking their values in a space of continuous functions.
This is due to the poor geometric properties of
(C(\overline{\mathcal{D}_{n}}),\|\,\cdot\,\|_{\infty})
as a Banach space:
The most general notion of stochastic integration in Banach spaces (see
[
82
]
) requires at least the UMD property; such spaces are, in particular, reflexive
[
48
, Theorem 4.3.3]
, which
C(\overline{\mathcal{D}_{n}})
fails to be.

One way to circumvent this issue is to proceed as in
[
56
, Section 3.2]
;
namely, defining the fractional domain spaces

(6.3)

\dot{E}_{n}^{\alpha}\coloneqq\mathsf{D}(A_{n}^{\alpha/2}),\quad\|x_{n}\|_{\dot{E}_{n}^{\alpha}}\coloneqq\|(\operatorname{Id}_{n}+A_{n})^{\alpha/2}x_{n}\|_{E_{n}},

and supposing that

\dot{E}_{n}^{\theta}\hookrightarrow C(\overline{\mathcal{D}_{n}})\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\mathcal{D}_{n})

continuously and densely for some
\theta\in[0,1)
,
one can carry out the stochastic
integration in the space
\dot{E}_{n}^{\theta}
, while working with

C(\overline{\mathcal{D}_{n}})
-valued processes for the fixed-point arguments.

In applications, we typically assume that
A_{n}
is an (unbounded) linear
differential
operator on
L^{q}(\mathcal{D}_{n})
, where
q\in[2,\infty)
, augmented with some boundary conditions
(“b.c.”) such
that
\dot{E}^{\alpha}_{n}
is the fractional Sobolev space

W^{\alpha,q}_{\text{b.c.}}(\mathcal{D}_{n})
of order
\alpha
. We
then suppose that
\theta
is chosen large enough in
relation to the dimension
d
that we have the continuous and dense Sobolev
embedding
W^{\alpha,q}_{\text{b.c.}}(\mathcal{D}_{n})\hookrightarrow C_{\text{b.c.}}(\overline{\mathcal{D}_{n}})
.

In Section
6.1
we will specify the abstract
formulation of the setting outlined above, as well as some additional
uniformity conditions with respect to
n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}
.
These will be used to, as a first step, derive
B_{n}
-valued counterparts to the
E_{n}
-valued discrete-to-continuum approximation results for globally Lipschitz drifts of linear growth from Subsection
5.1
; in Subsection
6.2
we do the same for the
B_{n}
-valued setting with locally Lipschitz and locally bounded drifts.
In the latter case, the solution are local in general, but in
Section
6.3
we state an extra dissipativity
assumption on
F_{n}
under which the existence of global solutions
to (
6.1
) has been proven in
[
56
, Section 4]
.
These processes then also converge in an improved sense, and we can apply this to Section
3
.

in
B_{n}
.

, assumption

implies

.

7.
Outlook

In this section we suggest some possible extensions of both the convergence of graph-discretized Whittle–Matérn SPDEs shown in Section
3
as well as the underlying abstract results from Sections
4
–
6
.

As discussed in Subsection
3.5
, the approximation results from Theorem
3.10
for (
3.16
) might be extended to broader classes of domains
\mathcal{M}
, connectivity length regimes
(h_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, coefficient functions
\tau,\kappa\colon\mathcal{M}\to[0,\infty)
and (fractional) powers
{s\in(0,\infty)}
.
Possible advancements to this end include the establishment of more general
L^{\infty}
-convergence results for graph Laplacian eigenfunctions (or convergence of Whittle–Matérn operators without using spectral convergence), as well as uniform
L^{\infty}
-boundedness of the semigroups (for instance via heat kernel estimates).
Under more restrictive assumptions on the connectivity parameter regime, rates of convergence for the case of purely spatial (i.e., stationary) graph-discretized linear SPDEs were established in
[
71
]
.
The same might be possible in the linear spatiotemporal setting since the discrete-to-continuum Trotter–Kato theorem can be extended to yield error estimates, see
[
51
, Section 2.2]
.
The semilinear cases, however, appear to be out of reach for the methods used in this work.

The proofs of the abstract discrete-to-continuum approximation results from Sections
4
–
6
largely rely on incorporating projection and lifting operators into arguments from
[
55
,
56
]
in an appropriate way.
By adapting other proofs from these sources along similar lines, it is likely that our results can be extended further, in particular enabling us to relax the simplifying assumptions that the UMD Banach spaces
(E_{n})_{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}
have type
2
and that the driving noise is additive.
In fact, we expect more generally that many results asserting continuous dependence of stochastic evolution problems on their coefficients can be extended to discrete-to-continuum approximation theorems via this procedure.

One particular type of problem for which this would be interesting is the class of stochastic evolution
inclusions
, whose drift operators are allowed to be multi-valued; this occurs, for instance, in the Langevin setting if
F_{n}=\partial\varphi_{n}
is the subdifferential of a convex and lower-semicontinuous but non-differentiable functional
\varphi_{n}
on the state space, taking values in
(-\infty,\infty]
.
Continuous dependence results for stochastic inclusion problems have been established in two different settings in
[
35
,
73
]
; however, neither of these covers the class of
semilinear
inclusions driven by
cylindrical
(i.e.,
white
) noise.
Not much theory appears to be available for such problems, with even the question of well-posedness (for fixed
n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}
) being highly nontrivial.
In fact, to the best of our knowledge, the only results in this direction concern the (important) subclass of
stochastic reflection problems
(also known as
Skorokhod problems
in the scalar-valued case), given by

(7.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}X(t)&\in-AX(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t-\partial I_{\Gamma}(X(t))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(t),\quad t\in(0,T],\\
X(0)&=\xi,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\Gamma
is a convex subset of a (Hilbertian) state space
H
and the
indicator functional

I_{\Gamma}\colon H\to(-\infty,\infty]
is defined to vanish on
\Gamma
and equal
\infty
outside of it.
In the first work on this problem, Nualart and Pardoux
[
66
]
used a direct approach to show existence and uniqueness in a setting which corresponds to
H\coloneqq L^{2}(0,1)
,
A\coloneqq-\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x^{2}}
with homogeneous Dirichlet or Neumann boundary conditions, and
\Gamma\coloneqq K_{0}
, where

K_{\alpha}\coloneqq\{u\in L^{2}(0,1):u(x)\geq-\alpha\text{ for a.e.\ }x\in(0,1)\},\quad\alpha\in[0,\infty).

In
[
70
]
, the authors first use the theory of Dirichlet forms to establish well-posedness of (
7.1
) in the case that
\Gamma
is a “regular” convex subset of a general Hilbert space
H
, a condition which includes
\Gamma\coloneqq K_{\alpha}
for
\alpha>0
but not
K_{0}
, which is treated separately using different techniques.
Lastly, the work
[
4
]
describes a variational approach to study (
7.1
) in a similar setting under the assumption that
0
belongs to the interior of
\Gamma
, which excludes the choice
\Gamma\coloneqq K_{0}
.
We point out that the argument used on
[
4
, p. 362]
to extract a weak
∗
-convergent sequence from the set
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
in the dual space of
L^{\infty}(0,T;H)
appears to be flawed, as it seems to imply that the closed unit ball of the dual of this (non-separable) space is
sequentially
compact, which is not the case.
Hence, the argument would need to be finished using a
generalized
subsequence (also known as a sub
net
) converging in the weak
∗
sense to some
u^{*}
.
For this reason, and since the theory for convergence of Dirichlet forms and their associated processes is well established—see for instance
[
52
]
for general results and
[
88
]
for an application to Markov chain Monte Carlo scaling—the setting of
[
70
]
is perhaps the most promising for an attempt at establishing discrete-to-continuum convergence results for (
7.1
).

Appendix B
Fractional parabolic integration

Let
(E,\|\,\cdot\,\|_{E})
be a Banach space. Suppose that
-A\colon\mathsf{D}(A)\subseteq E\to E

generates a strongly continuous semigroup
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
. This implies the
existence of constants
M\in[1,\infty)
and
w\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(B.1)

\|S(t)\|_{\mathscr{L}(E)}\leq Me^{wt}\quad\text{for all }t\in[0,\infty).

Fixing
T\in(0,\infty)
, we define

k_{s}\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathscr{L}(E)
by
k_{s}(\tau)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\Gamma(s)}\tau^{s-1}S(s)\mathbf{1}_{[0,T]}(\tau)
for
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
.
For any function
f\colon(0,T)\to E
such that the following Bochner integral converges in
E
for
s\in(0,\infty)
and a.e.
t\in(0,T)
,
we set

(B.2)

\mathfrak{I}_{A}^{s}f(t)\coloneqq k_{s}*f(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(s)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{s-1}S(t-\tau)f(\tau)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau.

For
s=0
we set
\mathfrak{I}_{A}^{0}\coloneqq\operatorname{Id}_{E}
.
The following properties of the
fractional parabolic integration operator

\mathfrak{I}_{A}^{s}
(for a single operator
A
) are
well known, see
[
20
, Proposition 5.9]
, and used
throughout the main text. We will state them here for the sake of
self-containedness.

Proposition B.1
.

Suppose that
-A\colon\mathsf{D}(A)\subseteq E\to E
generates a strongly
continuous semigroup
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}\subseteq\mathscr{L}(E)

satisfying (
B.1
).
Then, for every
s\in[0,\infty)
,
p\in[1,\infty]
and
T\in(0,\infty)
, we have:

(i)

\mathfrak{I}_{A}^{s}
is bounded
from
L^{p}(0,T;E)
to itself, with an operator norm depending only on

s
,
p
,
T
,
w
and
M
.

If
(F,\|\,\cdot\,\|_{F})
is a Banach space for which there
exist
M^{\prime}\in[1,\infty)
and
\alpha\in[0,\infty)
such that

(B.3)

S(t)\in\mathscr{L}(E;F)\quad\text{with}\quad\|S(t)\|_{\mathscr{L}(E;F)}\leq M^{\prime}t^{-\alpha/2}\quad\text{for all }t\in[0,\infty),

and in addition we have either
p=1,\,s\geq 1+\frac{\alpha}{2}
or

p>1,\,s>\frac{1}{p}+\frac{\alpha}{2}
, then

(ii)

\mathfrak{I}_{A}^{s}
is bounded
from

to
C([0,T];F)
, with an operator norm depending only on

s
,
p
,
T
and
M^{\prime}
.

For a sequence of operators

on Banach spaces which satisfy the appropriate discrete-to-continuum assumptions from the main text, the proposition above implies the following corollary regarding uniform boundedness of the sequence

, where
\widetilde{\mathfrak{I}}_{A_{n}}^{s}\coloneqq\Lambda_{n}\mathfrak{I}_{A_{n}}^{s}\Pi_{n}

.
From this, in turn, one can derive Proposition
B.3
below asserting the strong convergence of these operators.

.

Let the Banach spaces

, and suppose that
{p\in[1,\infty]}

The following assertions hold:

(ii)

and

, or

If the spaces

,

.

Let the Banach spaces
(E_{n},\|\,\cdot\,\|_{E_{n}})_{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}

Appendix C
Uniformly sectorial sequences of operators

We first recall the concept of sectorial operators.
Given
\omega\in(0,\pi)
, we say that a linear operator
A\colon\mathsf{D}(A)\subseteq E\to E
on a (real or complex) Banach space
E
, with
spectrum

\sigma(A)\coloneqq\mathbb{C}\setminus\rho(A)
, is said to be
\omega
-sectorial
if

(C.1)

\sigma(A)\subseteq\overline{\Sigma}_{\omega}\quad\text{ and }\quad M(\omega,A)\coloneqq\sup\{\|\lambda R(\lambda,A)\|_{\mathscr{L}(E)}:\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Sigma}_{\omega}\}<\infty,

where
\Sigma_{\omega}
is as in (
3.14
) and
M(\omega,A)
is called the
\omega
-sectoriality constant
.
Its
angle of sectoriality

\omega(A)\in[0,\pi)
is defined as
the infimum of all
\omega
for which (
C.1
) holds.

If
A
is closed and densely defined, then by
[
81
, Theorem 13.30]
, there exists
\omega\in(0,\frac{1}{2}\pi)
such that
A
is
\omega
-sectorial if and only if there exists
\eta\in(0,\frac{1}{2}\pi)
such that
-A
generates a bounded analytic semigroup
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
\Sigma_{\eta}
.
The latter means that the mapping
[0,\infty)\ni t\mapsto S(t)\in\mathscr{L}(E)
extends to a bounded holomorphic function
\Sigma_{\eta}\ni z\mapsto S(z)\in\mathscr{L}(E)
.
Inspecting the proof of the cited theorem reveals that, whenever these equivalent conditions hold, we have

(C.2)

\sup\nolimits_{z\in\Sigma_{\eta}}\|S(z)\|_{\mathscr{L}(E)}\eqsim_{(\omega,\eta)}M(\omega,A).

This theorem also asserts that the supremum of the set of
\eta\in(0,\frac{1}{2}\pi)
for which
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
extends to a bounded analytic semigroup on
\Sigma_{\eta}
equals
\frac{1}{2}\pi-\omega(A)
.

Moreover, by
[
41
, Propositions 3.4.1 and 3.4.3]
we have

(C.3)

\|A^{\alpha}S(t)\|_{\mathscr{L}(E)}\lesssim_{(\omega,\alpha)}M(\omega,A)\,t^{-\alpha},

for all
\omega\in(\omega(A),\frac{1}{2}\pi)
and
t\in(0,\infty)
, where the implicit constant is non-decreasing in
\alpha
for any fixed
\omega
.

We say that a sequence
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of linear operators
A_{n}\colon\mathsf{D}(A_{n})\subseteq E_{n}\to E_{n}
is
uniformly sectorial of angle
\omega\in[0,\pi)
if
A_{n}
is sectorial of angle
\omega
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and

(C.4)

M_{\text{Unif}}(\omega^{\prime},A)\coloneqq\sup\nolimits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}M(\omega^{\prime},A_{n})<\infty\quad\text{for all }\omega^{\prime}\in(\omega,\pi).

Lemma
C.1
complements Theorem
2.3
in the situation where the semigroup generators are uniformly sectorial of angle less than
\frac{1}{2}\pi
, in which case we obtain the uniform convergence
\Lambda_{n}A_{n}^{\alpha}S(\,\cdot\,)\Pi_{n}x\to A_{\infty}^{\alpha}S_{\infty}(\,\cdot\,)x
on compact subsets of
(0,\infty)
.
It is an analog to
[
55
, Lemma 4.1(2)]
in the discrete-to-continuum setting and for general
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
(instead of
\alpha=1
).

Lemma C.1
.

Let the linear operators
A_{n}\colon\mathsf{D}(A_{n})\subseteq E_{n}\to E_{n}
on the Banach spaces
(E_{n},\|\,\cdot\,\|_{E_{n}})_{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}
be uniformly sectorial of angle
\omega\in[0,\frac{1}{2}\pi)
, and denote by
(S_{n}(t))_{t\geq 0}
the bounded analytic
C_{0}
-semigroups generated by
-A_{n}
.
Let Assumptions
2.1
and
2.2
be
satisfied with
w=0
, and suppose that the equivalent statements
(i)
and
(ii)
in Theorem
2.3
hold.
Then we have, for all
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
,
x\in E_{\infty}
and
0<a<b<\infty
,

(C.5)

\sup_{t\in[a,b]}\|\Lambda_{n}A_{n}^{\alpha}S_{n}(t)\Pi_{n}x-A_{\infty}^{\alpha}S_{\infty}(t)x\|_{\widetilde{E}}\to 0\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

Proof.

Fix
\omega^{\prime}\in(\omega,\frac{1}{2}\pi)
,
n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}
and
t\in(0,\infty)
.
We begin by sketching the functional calculus argument (see
[
50
, Chapter 15]
or
[
41
]
for a more comprehensive overview of this topic) which shows that we have the following Cauchy integral representation:

(C.6)

A_{n}^{\alpha}S_{n}(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\Sigma_{\omega^{\prime}}}z^{\alpha}e^{-tz}R(z,A_{n})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z.

To see this, define the functions
f_{\alpha},g_{t}\colon\Sigma_{\omega^{\prime}}\to\mathbb{C}
by
f_{\alpha}(z)\coloneqq z^{\alpha}
and
g_{t}(z)\coloneqq e^{-tz}
for
z\in\Sigma_{\omega^{\prime}}
and
t\in(0,\infty)
.
Denote by
f_{\alpha}(A_{n})
and
g_{t}(A_{n})
the operators obtained via the extended Dunford calculus for sectorial operators as defined in
[
50
, Definition 15.1.8]
.
Then
f_{\alpha}(A_{n})
is the fractional power
A_{n}^{\alpha}
in the sense of
[
50
, Definition 15.2.2]
, which satisfies
f_{\alpha}(A_{n})x=f_{\alpha}(\lambda)x=\lambda^{\alpha}x
if
A_{n}x=\lambda x
, hence this (more general) definition agrees with the spectrally defined fractional powers in the setting of (
3.13
).
Moreover,
g_{t}(A_{n})=S_{n}(t)
by
[
50
, Theorem 15.1.7]
.
Since
S_{n}(t)
is bounded, we have
(f_{\alpha}g_{t})(A_{n})=f_{\alpha}(A_{n})g_{t}(A_{n})
by
[
50
, Proposition 15.1.12]
.
Finally, the function
(f_{\alpha}g_{t})(z)=z^{\alpha}e^{-z}
is holomorphic and has (super)polynomial decay at
0
and
\infty
, and thus belongs to the domain of the primary Dunford calculus
[
50
, Definition 15.1.1]
, so that
(f_{\alpha}g_{t})(A_{n})=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\Sigma_{\omega^{\prime}}}z^{\alpha}e^{-tz}R(z,A_{n})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z
.
Putting all these observations together yields (
C.6
).
Applying the projection and lifting operators and parametrizing the complex integral yields, for all
x\in E_{\infty}
,

(C.7)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{n}A_{n}^{\alpha}S_{n}(t)\Pi_{n}x

\displaystyle=\begin{aligned} &-\frac{e^{i(\alpha+1)\omega^{\prime}}}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\alpha}\exp(-te^{i\omega^{\prime}}r)\widetilde{R}(e^{i\omega^{\prime}}r,A_{n})x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\\
&+\frac{e^{-i(\alpha+1)\omega^{\prime}}}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\alpha}\exp(-te^{-i\omega^{\prime}}r)\widetilde{R}(e^{-i\omega^{\prime}}r,A_{n})x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r,\end{aligned}

where we recall that
\Pi_{\infty}=\Lambda_{\infty}=\operatorname{Id}_{\widetilde{E}}
for
n=\infty
.
It follows that the above estimate implies the following uniform bound on the interval
[a,b]
:

(C.8)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[a,b]}\|\Lambda_{n}A_{n}^{\alpha}S_{n}(t)\Pi_{n}x-A_{\infty}^{\alpha}S_{\infty}(t)x\|_{\widetilde{E}}

\displaystyle\leq\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\alpha}e^{-a\cos(\omega^{\prime})r}\Bigl[&\|\widetilde{R}(re^{i\omega^{\prime}},A_{n})x-R(re^{i\omega^{\prime}},A_{\infty})x\|_{\widetilde{E}}\\
&+\|\widetilde{R}(re^{-i\omega^{\prime}},A_{n})x-R(re^{-i\omega^{\prime}},A_{\infty})x\|_{\widetilde{E}}\Bigr]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r.\end{aligned}

Setting
\eta\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\pi-\vartheta
for some
\vartheta\in(\omega,\omega^{\prime})
, the uniform sectoriality of
(A_{n})_{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}
implies that the operators
(-A_{n}e^{\pm i\eta})_{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}
generate
C_{0}
-semigroups
(S_{n}(te^{\pm i\eta}))_{t\geq 0}
which are uniformly bounded in
t
and
n
.
Therefore, we can apply Theorem
2.3
to these two sequences of semigroups to find that

\widetilde{R}(\lambda,A_{n})x\to R(\lambda,A_{\infty})x
for all
\lvert\arg\lambda\rvert>\vartheta
if this convergence holds for one such
\lambda
.
We have in fact
\widetilde{R}(\lambda,A_{n})x\to R(\lambda,A_{\infty})x
for
all

\lambda
such that
\lvert\arg\lambda\rvert>\frac{1}{2}\pi>\vartheta
by our hypothesis that the operators
(A_{n})_{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}
satisfy statements
(i)
and
(ii)
in Theorem
2.3
, so we conclude
\widetilde{R}(re^{\pm i\omega^{\prime}},A_{n})x\to R(re^{\pm i\omega^{\prime}},A_{\infty})x
for all
r\in(0,\infty)
.
On the other hand, by (
C.4
) and Assumption
2.1
we have
\|\widetilde{R}(re^{\pm i\omega^{\prime}},A_{n})x\|_{\widetilde{E}}\leq M_{\Pi}M_{\Lambda}M_{\text{Unif}}(\vartheta,A)\|x\|_{\widetilde{E}}/r
for all
n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}
.
Hence, we can bound the integrand in (
C.8
),
up to
n
-independent constants, by the integrable function
r\mapsto r^{\alpha-1}\exp(-a\cos(\omega^{\prime})r)
, so that the integrals tend to zero by the dominated convergence theorem.
∎

A Note on Complexity for Two Classes of Structured Non-Smooth Non-Convex Compositional Optimization
Yao Yao, Qihang Lin, Tianbao Yang

Abstract
This note studies numerical methods for solving compositional optimization problems, where the inner function is smooth, and the outer function is Lipschitz continuous, non-smooth, and non-convex but exhibits one of two special structures that enable the design of efficient first-order methods. In the first structure, the outer function allows for an easily solvable proximal mapping. We demonstrate that, in this case, a smoothing compositional gradient method can find a ( δ , ϵ ) (\delta,\epsilon) -stationary point—specifically defined for compositional optimization—in 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 / ( δ ​ ϵ 2 ) ) \mathcal{O}(1/(\delta\epsilon^{2})) iterations. In the second structure, the outer function is expressed as a difference-of-convex function, where each convex component is simple enough to allow an efficiently solvable proximal linear subproblem. In this case, we show that a prox-linear method can find a nearly ϵ \epsilon -critical point in 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 / ϵ 2 ) \mathcal{O}(1/\epsilon^{2}) iterations.

Introduction

In this work, we consider the following
compositional optimization problem

\min_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(x):=h(g(x)),

(1)

where
h:\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a Lipschitz continuous, non-smooth and non-convex function and
g:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
is a smooth map. Overall, (
1
) is a non-smooth non-convex compositional optimization problem.

This note focuses on first-order methods for (
1
) under some structural assumptions and analyzes their convergence. For general non-smooth non-convex optimization problems, both asymptotic convergence analysis, e.g.,
[
5
,
20
,
28
,
11
]
, and non-asymptotic convergence analysis, e.g.,
[
21
,
44
]
, have been established in literature for different first-order methods under various settings. Typically, non-asymptotic convergence analysis implies the iteration complexity of a first-order method for finding an
\epsilon
-stationary point. However, for general non-smooth and non-convex optimization, it has been shown that a traditional
\epsilon
-stationary point cannot be obtained in finite complexity
[
21
,
44
,
37
,
19
]
. Therefore, recent studies have focused finding a
(\delta,\epsilon)
-stationary point

[
44
,
19
]
(also called a
(\delta,\epsilon)
-Goldstein stationary point).

The first result of this note is a
smoothing compositional gradient
method that finds a
(\delta,\epsilon)
-stationary
point for (
1
) with iteration complexity
O(1/\delta\epsilon^{2})
. The definition of such a
(\delta,\epsilon)
-stationary point (Definition
4.5
) is tailored for the compositional structure in (
1
), and is similar to but different form that in
[
44
,
19
]
.

In addition, we consider a special case of (
1
), which is a difference-of-convex (DC) optimization with the following compositional structure.

\min_{x}f(x):=h_{2}(g_{2}(x))-h_{1}(g_{1}(x)),

(2)

where
h_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a Lipschitz continuous convex function and
g_{i}:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
is a smooth map for
i=1,2
. To solve this problem, we propose a
prox-linear
method where the solution is updated by solving two proximal linear subproblems. We show that, when the subproblems can be solved exactly, the method finds a nearly
\epsilon
-critical point of (
2
), originally defined by
[
35
]
, within
O(1/\epsilon^{2})
iterations.

Notations and Preliminaries

In this section, we present some notations and preliminaries. Throughout this work, a Euclidean space is considered and denoted by
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, with an inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and the induced norm
\|\cdot\|
. Let
B
denote the unit Euclidean ball and
\text{conv}(\cdot)
denote the convex hull. Given a linear map
A:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{l}
, the operator norm of
A
is defined as
\|A\|_{\text{op}}:=\max_{\|u\|\leq 1}\|Au\|
. Given a set
S
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, the distance of a point
x
onto
S
is given by

\text{dist}(x;S):=\inf_{y\in S}\|y-x\|.

For a non-convex function
f(x):{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
, let
\hat{\partial}f(x)
denote the Fréchet subgradient and
\partial f(x)
denote the limiting subgradient, i.e.,

\begin{split}\hat{\partial}f(\bar{x})&=\left\{v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\lim_{x\rightarrow\bar{x}}\inf\frac{f(x)-f(\bar{x})-v^{\top}(x-\bar{x})}{\|x-\bar{x}\|}\geq 0\right\},\\
\partial f(\bar{x})&=\left\{v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\exists x_{k}\xrightarrow{f}\bar{x},v_{k}\in\hat{\partial}f(\bar{x}),v_{k}\rightarrow v\right\},\end{split}

where the notation
x\xrightarrow{f}\bar{x}
means that
x\rightarrow\bar{x}
and
f(x)\rightarrow f(\bar{x})
. It is known that
\hat{\partial}f(\bar{x})\subset\partial f(x)
.

For any proper function
f
and any real number
\mu>0
, the
Moreau envelope
and the
proximal mapping
of
f
are defined by

\begin{split}f_{\mu}(x)&:=\min_{y}\left\{f(y)+\frac{1}{2\mu}\|y-x\|^{2}\right\},\\
\text{prox}_{\mu f}(x)&:=\argmin_{y}\left\{f(y)+\frac{1}{2\mu}\|y-x\|^{2}\right\},\end{split}

respectively. It is not hard to see that

\min_{y}f(y)\leq f_{\mu}(x)\leq f(x)\text{ for all }x.

4
Outer Function with Easy Mapping

In this section, we study (
1
) under the following assumptions.

Assumption 4.1
.

The following statements hold for (
1
).

1.

h:{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is a
L_{h}
-Lipschitz continuous function:

|h(x)-h(y)|\leq L_{h}\|x-y\|\quad\text{for all }x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m};

There exists a constant
\underline{h}
such that
h(\cdot)\geq\underline{h}
and
\text{prox}_{\mu h}(\cdot)
can be solved easily.

2.

g:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
is a
L_{g}
-Lipschitz continuous and
C^{1}
-smooth mapping with a
\beta
-Lipschitz continuous Jacobian map:

\|\nabla g(x)-\nabla g(y)\|_{\text{op}}\leq\beta\|x-y\|\quad\text{for all }x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d};

There exists a constant
C_{g}
such that
\|g(x)\|\leq C_{g}
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

To solve problem (
1
), we first introduce the Moreau envelope of
h
at
g(x)
:

h_{\mu}(g(x)):=\min_{y}\left\{h(y)+\frac{1}{2\mu}\|y-g(x)\|^{2}\right\},

and the proximal mapping of
h
at
g(x)
:

\text{prox}_{\mu h}(g(x)):=\text{arg}\min_{y}\left\{\frac{1}{2\mu}\|y-g(x)\|^{2}+h(y)\right\},

where
\mu>0
. A nice property of the Moreau envelope function is that it can be written as a difference-of-convex (DC) function
[
27
,
40
]
:

h_{\mu}(g(x))=\frac{1}{2\mu}\|g(x)\|^{2}-\underbrace{\max_{y}\left(\frac{1}{\mu}y^{\top}g(x)-\frac{1}{2\mu}\|y\|^{2}-h(y)\right)}_{G(x)},

(3)

where
G(x)
is a weakly convex function because it is the max of weakly convex functions of
x
. Also, if the assumptions above are satisfied, it is easy to verify that
\frac{1}{2\mu}\|g(x)\|^{2}
is
\frac{L_{g}^{2}+C_{g}\beta}{\mu}
-smooth and hence
\frac{L_{g}^{2}+C_{g}\beta}{\mu}
-weakly convex.
We also present following nice properties that are useful.

Lemma 4.2
.

Under Assumption
4.1
, it holds that

\displaystyle\text{prox}_{\mu h}(g(x))\subset\left(I+\mu\partial h\right)^{-1}(g(x))\ \text{for all }x,

(4)

\displaystyle\forall v\in\text{prox}_{\mu h}(g(x)),\ \frac{1}{\mu}(g(x)-v)\in\partial h(v).

(5)

The proof of (
4
) can be found in
[
34
, Example 10.2]
and the proof of (
5
) can be found in
[
34
, Theorem 10.1]
.

Lemma 4.3
.

If Assumption
4.1
holds, for any
v\in\text{prox}_{\mu h}(g(x))
,

\|v\|\leq C_{v},

where
C_{v}:=C_{g}+\sqrt{2\mu\left(\max_{x}h(g(x))-\underline{h}\right)}
.

Proof.

Since
h_{\mu}(g(x))\leq h(g(x))
and
h
is lower bounded by
\underline{h}
, we can get for any
v\in\text{prox}_{\mu h}(g(x))

\underline{h}+\frac{1}{2\mu}\|v-g(x)\|^{2}\leq h(v)+\frac{1}{2\mu}\|v-g(x)\|^{2}\leq h(g(x)).

By rearranging the above inequalities, we have

\|v-g(x)\|^{2}\leq 2\mu(h(g(x))-\underline{h})\leq 2\mu\left(\max_{x}h(g(x))-\underline{h}\right).

By the
L_{h}
-Lipschitz continuity of
h
and the upper boundness of
\|g(x)\|
, we know that the
\max_{x}h(g(x))
exists.
Then by triangle inequality, we can get

\begin{split}\|v\|&\leq\|g(x)\|+\sqrt{2\mu\left(\max_{x}h(g(x))-\underline{h}\right)}\\
&\leq C_{g}+\sqrt{2\mu\left(\max_{x}h(g(x))-\underline{h}\right)}.\end{split}

The result follows.

4.1
Targeted solution

Consider the problem (
1
), the Fréchet subgradient of
f
can be get following a chain-rule
[
34
, Theorem 10.6]
.

Lemma 4.4

(Chain rule)
.

For
f
defined in (
1
), at any
x
, one has

\nabla g(x)^{\top}\hat{\partial}h(g(x))\subset\hat{\partial}f(x)\subset\partial f(x)\subset\nabla g(x)^{\top}\partial h(g(x)).

Ideally, we want to find a point
x
such that
\min\left\{\|\xi\|\ |\ \xi\in\partial f(x)\right\}\leq\epsilon
. However, the existing results show that this cannot be done with finite complexity
[
21
,
44
,
37
,
19
]
. Instead, one can find a
(\delta,\epsilon)
-Goldstein stationary point using first-order methods
[
44
,
19
]
with iteration complexity
O(1/(\delta\epsilon^{3}))
. Here, a
(\delta,\epsilon)
-Goldstein stationary point is a point
x
satisfying

\text{dist}(0,\partial f(x+\delta B))\leq\epsilon

(6)

where
\partial f(x+\delta B):=\text{conv}\left(\bigcup_{y\in x+\delta B}\partial f(x)\right)
is the Goldstein
\delta
-subdifferential
[
16
]
.

According to the last inclusion in Lemma
4.4
, if we weaken a
(\delta,\epsilon)
-Goldstein stationary point in (
6
) to a
(\delta,\epsilon)
-stationary point defined with respect to set
\nabla g(x)^{\top}\partial h(g(x))
, it is possible to find such a weakened stationary point more easily, i.e., with lower iteration complexity. To verify this, the point we want to find through this study is defined below.

Definition 4.5
.

A point
x
is called a (chain-rule)
(\delta,\epsilon)
-stationary point for (
1
) if

\text{dist}(0,\nabla g(x)^{\top}\partial h(g(x)+\delta B))\leq\epsilon,

(7)

where
\partial h(g(x)+\delta B):=\text{conv}\left(\bigcup_{y\in g(x)+\delta B}\partial h(y)\right)
is the Goldstein
\delta
-subdifferential, introduced in
[
16
]
.
A random variable
x
is called a stochastic (chain-rule)
(\delta,\epsilon)
-stationary point for (
1
) if

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\text{dist}(0,\nabla g(x)^{\top}\partial h(g(x)+\delta B))\right]\leq\epsilon.

(8)

Remark 4.6
.

To interpret Definition
4.5
, we write (
1
) equivalently into a constrained problem

\min_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},v\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}h(v)\text{ s.t. }g(x)=v.

(9)

According to the optimality conditions of this problem, we can say
(x,v)
is a Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) point of (
9
) if there exists
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that
\lambda\in\partial h(v)
,
\nabla g(x)^{\top}\lambda=0
, and
v=g(x)
. Suppose
x
is a
(\delta,\epsilon)
-stationary point in Definition
4.5
for (
1
) with
\delta=O(\epsilon)
. There exist
v\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
\lambda\in\partial h(v)
such that
\|g(x)-v\|\leq\delta=O(\epsilon)
and
\|\nabla g(x)^{\top}\lambda\|\leq\epsilon
, indicating that
(x,v)
is an
\epsilon
-KKT point of (
9
).

4.2
Algorithm and convergence analysis

To find a
(\delta,\epsilon)
-stationary point of (
1
), we propose a smoothing compositional gradient method (SCGM) in Algorithm
1
. This algorithm is essentially a gradient method applied to minimize
h_{\mu}(g(x))
in (
3
), which is a smooth approximation of (
1
). Since we have asssumed the proximal mapping
\text{prox}_{\mu h}(g(x))
can be easily solved, we can get
v(g(x_{k}))\in\text{prox}_{\mu h}(g(x_{k}))
for
k=1,\cdots,K
. Note that
v(g(x_{k}))
may not be unique since
h(\cdot)
is not necessarily weakly convex. If there are multiple possible
v(g(x_{k}))
, our algorithm can use any one of them. Recall (
3
). At the
k
th iteration, after getting one proximal point
v(g(x_{k}))
, we can get

\frac{1}{\mu}\nabla g(x_{k})^{\top}g(x_{k})-\frac{1}{\mu}\nabla g(x_{k})^{\top}v(g(x_{k}))\in\partial h_{\mu}(g(x_{k})),

and then we apply subgradient update on
x
to generate
x_{k+1}
.

Algorithm 1

Smoothing Compositional Gradient Method (SCGM)

1:

Initialization:

x_{1}
,
K\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
\mu>0,\gamma>0
.

2:

for

k=1,\cdots,K

do

3:

Get
v(g(x_{k}))\in\text{prox}_{\mu h}(g(x_{k}))

4:

Let
F_{k}(x)=\left(\frac{1}{\mu}\nabla g(x_{k})^{\top}g(x_{k})-\frac{1}{\mu}\nabla g(x_{k})^{\top}v(g(x_{k}))\right)^{\top}(x-x_{k})+\frac{\gamma}{2}\|x-x_{k}\|^{2}

5:

x_{k+1}=\argmin\limits_{x}F_{k}(x)

end for

6:

return

x_{\tau}
with
\tau
uniformly sampled from
\{1,\cdots,K\}

The convergence property of Algorithm
1
is presented as follows.

Theorem 4.7
.

Suppose Assumption
4.1
holds and
\mu=\frac{\delta}{2L_{h}}
,
\gamma=\frac{2C}{\mu}
, and
K=\frac{16L_{h}C\Delta}{\delta\epsilon^{2}}
with
C:=L_{g}^{2}+\beta C_{g}+C_{v}L_{g}
and
\Delta:=h(g(x_{1}))-\underline{h}
, then the output of the Algorithm
1

x_{\tau}
is a stochastic
(\delta,\epsilon)
-stationary point for (
1
).

Proof.

Since
\frac{1}{2\mu}\|g(x)\|^{2}
is
\frac{L_{g}^{2}+\beta C_{g}}{\mu}
-smooth, we have for any
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\frac{1}{2\mu}\|g(u)\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2\mu}\|g(x_{k})\|^{2}+\left\langle\frac{1}{\mu}\nabla g(x_{k})^{\top}g(x_{k}),u-x_{k}\right\rangle+\frac{L_{g}^{2}+\beta C_{g}}{2\mu}\|u-x_{k}\|^{2}.

(10)

By the Lemma
4.3
, we observe that
G(x)
in (
3
) is
\frac{C_{v}L_{g}}{\mu}
-weakly convex with
C_{v}
defined in Lemma
4.3
. Then we have for any
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

-G(u)\leq-G(x_{k})-\left\langle\frac{1}{\mu}\nabla g(x_{k})^{\top}v(g(x_{k})),u-x_{k}\right\rangle+\frac{C_{v}L_{g}}{2\mu}\|u-x_{k}\|^{2}.

(11)

Combine (
10
) and (
11
) to get

h_{\mu}(g(u))\leq h_{\mu}(g(x_{k}))+\left\langle\frac{1}{\mu}\nabla g(x_{k})^{\top}(g(x_{k})-v(g(x_{k}))),u-x_{k}\right\rangle+\frac{L_{g}^{2}+\beta C_{g}+C_{v}L_{g}}{2\mu}\|u-x_{k}\|^{2}.

Let
C:=L_{g}^{2}+\beta C_{g}+C_{v}L_{g}
and replace
u
with
x_{k+1}
in above inequality, we have

h_{\mu}(g(x_{k+1}))\leq h_{\mu}(g(x_{k}))+\left\langle\frac{1}{\mu}\nabla g(x_{k})^{\top}(g(x_{k})-v(g(x_{k}))),x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\rangle+\frac{C}{2\mu}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}.

(12)

Since
x_{k+1}=\argmin_{x}F_{k}(x)
, we have
F_{k}(x_{k+1})\leq F_{k}(x_{k})
, i.e.,

\left\langle\frac{1}{\mu}\nabla g(x_{k})^{\top}(g(x_{k})-v(g(x_{k}))),x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\rangle+\frac{\gamma}{2}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}\leq 0

(13)

and

\frac{1}{\mu}\nabla g(x_{k})^{\top}g(x_{k})-\frac{1}{\mu}\nabla g(x_{k})^{\top}v(g(x_{k}))+\gamma(x_{k+1}-x_{k})=0.

(14)

By adding (
13
) to (
12
), we have

h_{\mu}(g(x_{k+1}))\leq h_{\mu}(g(x_{k}))+\left(\frac{C}{2\mu}-\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}.

(15)

By letting
\gamma=\frac{2C}{\mu}
, (
15
) can be easily converted to

\frac{C}{2\mu}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}\leq h_{\mu}(g(x_{k}))-h_{\mu}(g(x_{k+1})).

Rearrange (
14
) and combine with the above formula, we can get

\begin{split}\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}\|\nabla g(x_{k})^{\top}g(x_{k})-\nabla g(x_{k})^{\top}v(g(x_{k}))\|^{2}=&\gamma^{2}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}\\
\leq&4\gamma(h_{\mu}(g(x_{k}))-h_{\mu}(g(x_{k+1})))\end{split}

By taking average over
1,\cdots,K
for above inequality, we have

\begin{split}\frac{1}{K}\sum^{K}_{k=1}\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}\|\nabla g(x_{k})^{\top}g(x_{k})-\nabla g(x_{k})^{\top}v(g(x_{k}))\|^{2}&\leq 4\gamma\frac{h_{\mu}(g(x_{1}))-h_{\mu}(g(x_{K}))}{K}\\
&\leq 4\gamma\frac{h_{\mu}(g(x_{1}))-\inf_{x}h_{\mu}(g(x))}{K}\\
&\leq\frac{4\Delta\gamma}{K},\end{split}

where
\Delta:=h(g(x_{1}))-\underline{h}
. Here, we have used the fact that
h_{\mu}(g(x_{1}))-\inf_{x}h_{\mu}(g(x))\leq\Delta
. Indeed, by the definition of
h_{\mu}(g(x))
, we have
\inf_{x}h_{\mu}(g(x))\geq\min_{x}h(x)\geq\underline{h}
and
h_{\mu}(g(x_{1}))\leq h(g(x_{1}))
. Hence, we have

h_{\mu}(g(x_{1}))-\inf_{x}h_{\mu}(g(x))\leq h_{\mu}(g(x_{1}))-\underline{h}\leq h(g(x_{1}))-\underline{h}=\Delta.

Then by the definition of
x_{\tau}
, we have

\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}\left\|\nabla g(x_{\tau})^{\top}\left(g(x_{\tau})-v(g(x_{\tau}))\right)\right\|^{2}\right]\leq\frac{4\Delta\gamma}{K}.

Next, we want to bound the distance between
v(g(x_{\tau}))
and
g(x_{\tau})
. By the definition of
v(g(x_{\tau}))
, we have

h(v(g(x_{\tau}))+\frac{1}{2\mu}\|v(g(x_{\tau}))-g(x_{\tau})\|^{2}\leq h(g(x_{\tau})),

from which we can get

\frac{1}{2\mu}\|v(g(x_{\tau}))-g(x_{\tau})\|^{2}\leq h(g(x_{\tau}))-h(v(g(x_{\tau})))\leq L_{h}\|g(x_{\tau})-v(g(x_{\tau}))\|,

where the last inequality comes from the
L_{h}
-Lipschitz continuity of
h
. From the above inequality, we can get

\|g(x_{\tau})-v(g(x_{\tau}))\|\leq 2\mu L_{h}.

By setting
\mu=\frac{\delta}{2L_{h}}
and
K=\frac{16L_{h}C\Delta}{\delta\epsilon^{2}}
, we can get

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{\mu}\left\|\nabla g(x_{\tau})^{\top}\left(g(x_{\tau})-v(g(x_{\tau}))\right)\right\|\right]&\leq\epsilon,\\
\|g(x_{\tau})-v(g(x_{\tau}))\|&\leq\delta.\end{split}

By combining the above results with the Lemma (
4.2
), we can get

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\text{dist}(0,\nabla g(x_{\tau})^{\top}\partial h(v(g(x_{\tau})))\right]\leq\epsilon

with
v(g(x_{\tau}))\in g(x_{\tau})+\delta B
, which shows that
x_{\tau}
is a stochastic
(\delta,\epsilon)
-stationary point for the problem (
1
).
∎

This result shows that the iteration complexity of finding a (chain-rule)
(\delta,\epsilon)
-stationary point is lower than that of
(\delta,\epsilon)
-Goldstein stationary point by a factor of
O(1/\epsilon)
.

5
Difference-of-Convex Outer Function

In this section, we consider (
2
), which is a special case of (
1
) called compositional DC optimization. We define
f_{1}(x):=h_{1}(g_{1}(x))
and
f_{1}(x):=h_{2}(g_{2}(x))
so (
2
) can be rewritten as

\begin{split}\min_{x}f(x)&:=f_{2}(x)-f_{1}(x)=h_{2}(g_{2}(x))-h_{1}(g_{1}(x)).\end{split}

Throughout this section, we make the following assumptions on (
2
).

Assumption 5.1
.

The following statements hold for (
2
).

1.

h_{i}:{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a convex and
L
-Lipschitz continuous function and
\text{prox}_{\mu h_{i}(Ax+b)}(\cdot)
can be solved easily for
i=1,2
and for any
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
and any
b\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

2.

g_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a
C^{1}
-smooth mapping with a
\beta
-Lipschitz continuous Jacobian map for
i=1,2
. There exists a constant
C_{g}
such that
\max\{\|g_{1}(x)\|,\|g_{2}(x)\|\}\leq C_{g}
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

Lemma 5.2

(Lemma 4.2 in
[
13
]
)
.

Under Assumption
5.1
,
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are
\rho
-weakly convex with

\rho:=L\beta.

By the Lemma
5.2
, problem (
2
) is DC optimization where the two convex components can be
f_{2}(x)+\frac{1}{2\mu}\|x\|^{2}
and
f_{1}(x)+\frac{1}{2\mu}\|x\|^{2}
for any
\mu\in(0,\rho^{-1})
.

5.1
Targeted solution

We say
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is a
critical point
of (
2
) if
0\in\partial f_{2}(x)-\partial f_{1}(x)
. Given
\epsilon>0
, we say
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is an
\epsilon
-critical point
of (
2
) if there exist
\xi_{1}\in\partial f_{1}(x)
and
\xi_{2}\in\partial f_{2}(x)
such that
\|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\|\leq\epsilon
. Since computing an
\epsilon
-critical point is difficult, following recent literature
[
35
,
42
,
17
]
, we instead consider finding a
nearly
\epsilon
-critical point
defined below.

Definition 5.3
.

Given
\epsilon>0
, we say
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is a nearly
\epsilon
-critical point of (
2
) if there exist
\xi_{1}
,
\xi_{2}
,
x^{\prime}
, and
x^{\prime\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that
\xi_{1}\in\partial f_{1}(x^{\prime})
,
\xi_{2}\in\partial f_{2}(x^{\prime\prime})
and
\max\{\|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\|,\|x-x^{\prime}\|,\|x-x^{\prime\prime}\|\}\leq\epsilon
. A random variable
x
is called a stochastic nearly
\epsilon
-critical point for (
2
) if there exist random variables
\xi_{1}
,
\xi_{2}
,
x^{\prime}
, and
x^{\prime\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that
\xi_{1}\in\partial f_{1}(x^{\prime})
,
\xi_{2}\in\partial f_{2}(x^{\prime\prime})
and
\max\{{\mathbb{E}}\|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\|,{\mathbb{E}}\|x-x^{\prime}\|,{\mathbb{E}}\|x-x^{\prime\prime}\|\}\leq\epsilon
.

Remark 5.4
.

To interpret Definition
5.3
, we write (
2
) equivalently into a constrained problem

\min_{x_{1},x_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f_{2}(x_{2})-f_{1}(x_{1})\text{ s.t. }x_{1}=x_{2}.

(16)

This is still DC optimization as it can be represented as
[f_{2}(x_{2})+\mathbf{1}_{x_{1}=x_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2})]-f_{1}(x_{1})
, where
\mathbf{1}_{x_{1}=x_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2})
is an indicator function that equals zero if
x_{1}=x_{2}
and equals positive infinity if
x_{1}\neq x_{2}
. We then can say
(x_{1},x_{2})
is a critical point of (
16
) if
x_{1}=x_{2}
and there exists
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that

\partial[f_{2}(x_{2})+\mathbf{1}_{x_{1}=x_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2})]\cap\partial f_{1}(x_{1})=\{\lambda\}\times(\partial f_{2}(x_{2})-\lambda)\cap f_{1}(x_{1})\times\{0\}\neq\emptyset.

These conditions can be stated as
\lambda\in\partial f_{2}(x_{2})
,
\lambda\in\partial f_{1}(x_{1})
and
x_{2}=x_{1}
, which can be viewed as KKT conditions for (
16
) with
\lambda
being a Lagrangian multiplier. Then, suppose
x
is a nearly
\epsilon
-critical point with
\xi_{1}
,
\xi_{2}
,
x^{\prime}
, and
x^{\prime\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
defined in Definition
5.3
. We then have
\xi_{2}\in\partial f_{2}(x^{\prime\prime})
,
\text{dist}(\xi_{2},\partial f_{1}(x^{\prime}))\leq\|\xi_{2}-\xi_{1}\|\leq\epsilon
, and
\|x^{\prime}-x^{\prime\prime}\|\leq\|x^{\prime}-x\|+\|x-x^{\prime\prime}\|\leq 2\epsilon
, suggesting that
(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})
is a
2\epsilon
-KKT point of (
16
) and thus
(x,x)
can be called a nearly
2\epsilon
-KKT point of (
16
) in the sense that
\|(x,x)-(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})\|\leq 2\epsilon
.

Following
[
35
,
42
,
17
]
, to solve (
2
), we approximate
f(x)
by a smooth function using the Moreau envelopes of
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
, which are defined as

f_{1,\mu}(z):=\min_{x}\left\{h_{1}(g_{1}(x))+\frac{1}{2\mu}\|x-z\|^{2}\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad f_{2,\mu}(z):=\min_{x}\left\{h_{2}(g_{2}(x))+\frac{1}{2\mu}\|x-z\|^{2}\right\}

(17)

where
\mu\in(0,\rho^{-1})
. The proximal mappings of
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are defined as

x_{1}^{*}(z):=\argmin_{x}\left\{h_{1}(g_{1}(x))+\frac{1}{2\mu}\|x-z\|^{2}\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad x_{2}^{*}(z):=\argmin_{x}\left\{h_{2}(g_{2}(x))+\frac{1}{2\mu}\|x-z\|^{2}\right\}.

(18)

Note that
x_{1}^{*}(z)
and
x_{2}^{*}(z)
are unique because the minimization problems above are strongly convex. It is well known that
f_{i,\mu}(x)
is smooth with
\nabla f_{i,\mu}(z)=\mu^{-1}(z-x_{i}^{*}(z))

[
34
, Proposition 13.37]
. Moreover,
x_{i}^{*}(z)
is
(1-\mu\rho)^{-1}
-Lipschitz continuous by the following lemma.

Lemma 5.5

(Lemma 3.5 in
[
43
]
)
.

For any
z,z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have

\|x_{i}^{*}(z)-x_{i}^{*}(z^{\prime})\|\leq(1-\mu\rho)^{-1}\|z-z^{\prime}\|,\quad i=1,2.

Hence, using the Moreau envelopes, we can construct a smooth approximation of (
2
) as follows

\min_{z}\left\{f_{\mu}(z):=f_{2,\mu}(z)-f_{1,\mu}(z)\right\}.

(19)

By the aforementioned properties of
f_{1,\mu}
and
f_{2,\mu}
, function
f_{\mu}
is smooth with
\nabla f_{\mu}(z)=\mu^{-1}(x_{1}^{*}(z)-x_{2}^{*}(z))
and
\nabla f_{\mu}
is
L_{\mu}
-Lipschitz continuous with
L_{\mu}=\frac{2}{\mu-\mu^{2}\rho}
.

5.2
Algorithm and convergence analysis

After approximating (
2
) by (
19
), we can directly apply a first-order method for smooth non-convex optimization to (
19
). Suppose the solution for (
19
) at iteration
k
is
z^{k}
. To compute
\nabla f_{\mu}(z^{k})
, we need to compute
x_{1}^{*}(z^{k})
and
x_{2}^{*}(z^{k})
exactly, which is not easy. Suppose, during the last iteration, we have obtained some approximations to
x_{i}^{*}(z^{k-1})
, denoted by
x_{1}^{k}
, for
i=1
and
2
. Then we adopt the key idea of the single-loop prox-linear approximate gradient method (PAGM) where
g_{i}(x)
and term
\frac{1}{2\mu}\|x-z^{k}\|^{2}
in (
17
) are linearized at
x=x_{i}^{k}
for
i=1
and
2
, and the following prox-linear subproblems are solved.

x_{i}^{k+1}=\argmin\limits_{x}\left\{h_{i}(g_{i}(x^{k}_{i})+\nabla g_{i}(x^{k}_{i})^{\top}(x-x_{i}^{k}))+\frac{1}{\mu}\langle x_{i}^{k}-z^{k},x-x_{i}^{k}\rangle+\frac{1}{2t}\|x-x_{i}^{k}\|^{2}\right\},\quad i=1,2,

(20)

where
t>0
is a step size. Note that
x_{i}^{k+1}
can be obtained easily thanks to Assumption
5.1
. Then
x_{1}^{k+1}
and
x_{2}^{k+1}
are used as approximations to
x_{1}^{*}(z^{k})
and
x_{2}^{*}(z^{k})
, respectively, so we obtain an approximation of
\nabla f_{\mu}(z^{k})=\mu^{-1}(x_{1}^{*}(z^{k})-x_{2}^{*}(z^{k}))
as
\mu^{-1}(x_{1}^{k+1}-x_{2}^{k+1})
. Then
z^{k}
can be updated to
z^{k+1}
through a gradient descend step along the direction of this approximate gradient, that is,
z^{k+1}=z^{k}-\gamma\mu^{-1}(x_{1}^{k+1}-x_{2}^{k+1})
where
\gamma>0
is a step size. This procedure is formally stated in Algorithm
2
.

Algorithm 2

Prox-Linear Approximate Gradient Method (PAGM)

1:

Initialization:

x_{1}^{1}
,
x_{2}^{1}
,
z^{1}
,
K\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
t>0
,
\mu>0
,
\gamma>0
.

2:

for

k=1,\cdots,K

do

3:

x_{1}^{k+1}=\argmin\limits_{x}\left\{h_{1}(g_{1}(x^{k}_{1})+\nabla g_{1}(x^{k}_{1})^{\top}(x-x_{1}^{k}))+\frac{1}{\mu}\langle x_{1}^{k}-z^{k},x-x_{1}^{k}\rangle+\frac{1}{2t}\|x-x_{1}^{k}\|^{2}\right\}

4:

x_{2}^{k+1}=\argmin\limits_{x}\left\{h_{2}(g_{2}(x^{k}_{2})+\nabla g_{2}(x^{k}_{2})^{\top}(x-x_{2}^{k}))+\frac{1}{\mu}\langle x_{2}^{k}-z^{k},x-x_{2}^{k}\rangle+\frac{1}{2t}\|x-x_{2}^{k}\|^{2}\right\}

5:

z^{k+1}=z^{k}-\gamma\mu^{-1}(x_{1}^{k+1}-x_{2}^{k+1})

end for

6:

return

x_{1}^{\tau+1}
with
\tau
uniformly sampled from
\{1,\cdots,K\}

Before analyzing the convergence of Algorithm
2
, we need the following lemma and proposition.

Lemma 5.6

(Lemma 3.2 in
[
13
]
)
.

Let
f_{i}(z,y):=h_{i}\left(g_{i}(y)+\nabla g_{i}(y)(z-y)\right)
for
i=1
and
2
. For any
z,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

-\frac{\rho}{2}\|z-y\|^{2}\leq f_{i}(z)-f_{i}(z,y)\leq\frac{\rho}{2}\|z-y\|^{2}.

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
\mu^{-1}>\rho
and
t^{-1}\geq\mu^{-1}+\rho
,
we have

\|x_{i}^{k+1}-x_{i}^{*}(z^{k})\|^{2}\leq(1-tc)\|x_{i}^{k}-x_{i}^{*}(z^{k})\|^{2},\quad i=1,2,

where
c:=\mu^{-1}-\rho
.

Proof.

The proof is modified from the proof of
[
30
, Theorem 2.3.4]
. For simplicity, we omit the subscript
i
in
h_{i}
,
g_{i}
,
x_{i}^{k+1}
and
x_{i}^{*}(z^{k})
, and define the following notations without reflecting their dependency on
i
,
z^{k}
and
\mu
:

\begin{split}F(x)&:=h(g(x))+\frac{1}{2\mu}\|x-z^{k}\|^{2},\\
F(\bar{x};x)&:=h(g(\bar{x})+\nabla g(\bar{x})(x-\bar{x}))+\frac{1}{\mu}\langle\bar{x}-z^{k},x-\bar{x}\rangle,\\
F_{t}(\bar{x};x)&:=F(\bar{x};x)+\frac{1}{2t}\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2},\\
F^{*}(\bar{x};t)&:=\min_{x}F_{t}(\bar{x};x),\\
x_{F}&:=\argmin_{x}F_{t}(\bar{x};x),\\
g_{F}&:=\frac{1}{t}(\bar{x}-x_{F}).\end{split}

Then we have

\begin{split}F(\bar{x};x)&=F_{t}(\bar{x};x)-\frac{1}{2t}\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}\\
&\geq F_{t}(\bar{x};x_{F})+\frac{1}{2t}\|x-x_{F}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2t}\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}\\
&=F^{*}(\bar{x};t)+\frac{1}{2t}\langle\bar{x}-x_{F},2x-x_{F}-\bar{x}\rangle\\
&=F^{*}(\bar{x};t)+\langle g_{F},x-\bar{x}\rangle+\frac{t}{2}\|g_{F}\|^{2},\end{split}

(21)

where the first inequality is because
F_{t}(\bar{x};x)
is
\frac{1}{t}
-strongly convex. Moreover,

\begin{split}F^{*}(\bar{x};t)=&F(\bar{x};x_{F})+\frac{1}{2t}\|x_{F}-\bar{x}\|^{2}\\
=&h(g(\bar{x})+\nabla g(\bar{x})(x_{F}-\bar{x}))+\frac{1}{\mu}\langle\bar{x}-z^{k},x_{F}-\bar{x}\rangle+\frac{1}{2t}\|x_{F}-\bar{x}\|^{2}\\
\geq&h(g(x_{F}))-\frac{\rho}{2}\|x_{F}-\bar{x}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2t}\|x_{F}-\bar{x}\|^{2}\\
&+\frac{1}{2\mu}\|x_{F}-z^{k}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2\mu}\|\bar{x}-z^{k}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2\mu}\|x_{F}-\bar{x}\|^{2}\\
\geq&F(x_{F})-\frac{1}{2\mu}\|\bar{x}-z^{k}\|^{2},\end{split}

(22)

where the last inequality comes from the Lemma
5.6
and the fact that
\frac{1}{\mu}\langle\bar{x}-z^{k},x_{F}-\bar{x}\rangle=\frac{1}{2\mu}\|x_{F}-z^{k}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2\mu}\|\bar{x}-z^{k}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2\mu}\|x_{F}-\bar{x}\|^{2}
, and the second inequality is because
t^{-1}\geq\mu^{-1}+\rho
.
We also have

\begin{split}F(x)=&h(g(x))+\frac{1}{2\mu}\|x-z^{k}\|^{2}\\
\geq&h(g(\bar{x})+\nabla g(\bar{x})(x-\bar{x}))-\frac{\rho}{2}\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}\\
&+\frac{1}{2\mu}\|\bar{x}-z^{k}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{\mu}\langle\bar{x}-z^{k},x-\bar{x}\rangle+\frac{1}{2\mu}\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}\\
=&F(\bar{x};x)+\frac{1}{2\mu}\|\bar{x}-z^{k}\|^{2}+\frac{\mu^{-1}-\rho}{2}\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2},\end{split}

(23)

where the first inequality comes from the Lemma
5.6
and the fact that
\frac{1}{2\mu}\|x-z^{k}\|^{2}=\frac{1}{2\mu}\|\bar{x}-z^{k}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{\mu}\langle\bar{x}-z^{k},x-\bar{x}\rangle+\frac{1}{2\mu}\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}
.
Combine inequalities (
21
), (
22
) and (
23
), we can get

F(x)\geq F(x_{F})+\langle g_{F},x-\bar{x}\rangle+\frac{t}{2}\|g_{F}\|^{2}+\frac{\mu^{-1}-\rho}{2}\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}.

(24)

Recall that

x^{*}(z^{k})=\argmin_{x}F(x).

Let
x=x^{*}(z^{k})
and
\bar{x}=x^{k}
in (
24
) so
x_{F}=x^{k+1}
and
g_{F}=(x^{k}-x^{k+1})/t
. Since
F(x_{F})-F(x^{*}(z^{k}))\geq 0
, it is implied by (
24
) that

\langle g_{F},x^{k}-x^{*}(z^{k})\rangle\geq\frac{t}{2}\|g_{F}\|^{2}+\frac{\mu^{-1}-\rho}{2}\|x^{*}(z^{k})-x^{k}\|^{2}.

(25)

The optimality condition of the subproblem
\min_{x}F_{t}(\bar{x};x)
reads

g_{F}\in\nabla g(x^{k})^{*}\partial h(g(x^{k})+\nabla g(x^{k})(x^{k+1}-x^{k}))+\frac{1}{\mu}(x^{k}-z^{k}).

Then, in view of (
25
), we have

\begin{split}\|x^{k+1}-x^{*}(z^{k})\|^{2}=&\|x_{k}-x^{*}(z^{k})-tg_{F}\|^{2}\\
=&\|x^{k}-x^{*}(z^{k})\|^{2}-2t\langle g_{F},x^{k}-x^{*}(z^{k})\rangle+t^{2}\|g_{F}\|^{2}\\
\leq&(1-t(\mu^{-1}-\rho))\|x^{k}-x^{*}(z^{k})\|^{2}\\
\leq&(1-tc)\|x^{k}-x^{*}(z^{k})\|^{2}\end{split}

(26)

∎

The convergence property of Algorithm
2
is presented as follows.

Theorem 5.8
.

Suppose the assumptions hold and set
\mu^{-1}>\max\{1,\rho\}
,
t^{-1}\geq\mu^{-1}+\rho
,
\gamma=\min\left\{\frac{1}{4L_{\mu}},\sqrt{\frac{t^{3}c^{4}\mu^{3}}{48(1-t^{2}c^{2})}}\right\}
and
K=O(1/\epsilon^{2})
with
c:=\mu^{-1}-\rho
, then the output of Algorithm
2
is a stochastic nearly 2
\epsilon
-critical point of (
2
).

Proof.

Recall that

\nabla f_{\mu}(z)=\mu^{-1}(x_{1}^{*}(z)-x_{2}^{*}(z))

and
\nabla f_{\mu}(z)
is
L_{\mu}
-Lipschitz continuous, we have

\begin{split}&f_{\mu}(z^{k})-f_{\mu}(z^{k+1})\\
\geq&\langle-\nabla f_{\mu}(z^{k}),z^{k+1}-z^{k}\rangle-\frac{L_{\mu}}{2}\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\|\\
=&\langle\mu^{-1}(x_{1}^{*}(z^{k})-x_{2}^{*}(z^{k})),\gamma\mu^{-1}(x_{1}^{k+1}-x_{2}^{k+1})\rangle-\frac{\gamma^{2}L_{\mu}}{2\mu^{2}}\|x_{1}^{k+1}-x_{2}^{k+1}\|^{2}\\
=&\frac{\gamma}{\mu^{2}}\langle x_{1}^{*}(z^{k})-x_{2}^{*}(z^{k}),x_{1}^{k+1}-x_{2}^{k+1}-x_{1}^{*}(z^{k})+x_{2}^{*}(z^{k})+x_{1}^{*}(z^{k})-x_{2}^{*}(z^{k})\rangle\\
&-\frac{\gamma^{2}L_{\mu}}{2\mu^{2}}\|x_{1}^{k+1}-x_{2}^{k+1}-x_{1}^{*}(z^{k})+x_{2}^{*}(z^{k})+x_{1}^{*}(z^{k})-x_{2}^{*}(z^{k})\|^{2}\\
\geq&\frac{\gamma}{\mu^{2}}\langle x_{1}^{*}(z^{k})-x_{2}^{*}(z^{k}),x_{1}^{k+1}-x_{2}^{k+1}-x_{1}^{*}(z^{k})+x_{2}^{*}(z^{k})\rangle\\
&+\left(\frac{\gamma}{\mu^{2}}-\frac{\gamma^{2}L_{\mu}}{\mu^{2}}\right)\|x_{1}^{*}(z^{k})-x_{2}^{*}(z^{k})\|^{2}-\frac{\gamma^{2}L_{\mu}}{\mu^{2}}\|x_{1}^{k+1}-x_{2}^{k+1}-x_{1}^{*}(z^{k})+x_{2}^{*}(z^{k})\|^{2}\end{split}

Applying Young’s inequality to the first term on the right-hand side of the last inequality above gives

\begin{split}&f_{\mu}(z^{k})-f_{\mu}(z^{k+1})\\
\geq&-\frac{\gamma}{\mu^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\|x_{1}^{*}(z^{k})-x_{2}^{*}(z^{k})\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|x_{1}^{k+1}-x_{2}^{k+1}-x_{1}^{*}(z^{k})+x_{2}^{*}(z^{k})\|^{2}\right)\\
&+\left(\frac{\gamma}{\mu^{2}}-\frac{\gamma^{2}L_{\mu}}{\mu^{2}}\right)\|x_{1}^{*}(z^{k})-x_{2}^{*}(z^{k})\|^{2}-\frac{\gamma^{2}L_{\mu}}{\mu^{2}}\|x_{1}^{k+1}-x_{2}^{k+1}-x_{1}^{*}(z^{k})+x_{2}^{*}(z^{k})\|^{2}\\
=&\left(\frac{\gamma}{2\mu^{2}}-\frac{\gamma^{2}L_{\mu}}{\mu^{2}}\right)\|x_{1}^{*}(z^{k})-x_{2}^{*}(z^{k})\|^{2}-\left(\frac{\gamma}{2\mu^{2}}+\frac{\gamma^{2}L_{\mu}}{\mu^{2}}\right)\|x_{1}^{k+1}-x_{2}^{k+1}-x_{1}^{*}(z^{k})+x_{2}^{*}(z^{k})\|^{2}\\
\geq&\frac{\gamma}{4\mu^{2}}\|x_{1}^{*}(z^{k})-x_{2}^{*}(z^{k})\|^{2}-\frac{3\gamma}{4\mu^{2}}\|x_{1}^{k+1}-x_{2}^{k+1}-x_{1}^{*}(z^{k})+x_{2}^{*}(z^{k})\|^{2}\end{split}

where the last inequality comes from
\gamma\leq\frac{1}{4L_{\mu}}
. Rearrange terms, we can get

\begin{split}\frac{\gamma}{4\mu^{2}}\|x_{1}^{*}(z^{k})-x_{2}^{*}(z^{k})\|^{2}\leq&\frac{3\gamma}{4\mu^{2}}\|x_{1}^{k+1}-x_{2}^{k+1}-x_{1}^{*}(z^{k})+x_{2}^{*}(z^{k})\|^{2}+f_{\mu}(z^{k})-f_{\mu}(z^{k+1})\\
\leq&\frac{3\gamma}{2\mu^{2}}\left(\|x_{1}^{k+1}-x_{1}^{*}(z^{k})\|^{2}+\|x_{2}^{k+1}-x_{2}^{*}(z^{k})\|^{2}\right)+f_{\mu}(z^{k})-f_{\mu}(z^{k+1})\end{split}

(27)

Abstract
. McCullough and Miller constructed a contractible complex on which the pure symmetric automorphism group of a free group acts with free abelian stabilizers. This complex has been used for computations such as the cohomological dimension of these groups, their cohomology rings, and results about ℓ 2 \ell^{2} -Betti numbers or BNRS-invariants. We generalize this construction to pure symmetric automorphism groups of arbitrary RAAGs and exhibit applications of this generalization.

1.
Introduction

Right-angled Artin groups (RAAGs) are a fascinating family of groups that attracts considerable attention in current research in geometric group theory. Given a finite simplicial graph
\Gamma
(i.e., a graph with no loops or double edges), the associated right angled Artin group is the group defined by the presentation

A_{\Gamma}=\langle V_{\Gamma}:[v,w]=1,(v,w)\in E(\Gamma)\rangle.

These groups interpolate between free abelian and free groups and their (outer) automorphism groups interpolate between
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
and
\mathrm{Aut}(F_{n})
(or
\mathrm{Out}(F_{n})
for the outer version). Automorphisms of a RAAG
{A_{\Gamma}}
that map each standard generator to a conjugate of itself are called
pure symmetric
and they form a subgroup denoted
\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}({A_{\Gamma}})
that contains the subgroup of inner automorphisms, so there is also an outer version
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}({A_{\Gamma}})
. In the case when
A_{\Gamma}
is the free group
F_{n}
,
\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(F_{n})
is the fundamental group of the configuration space of
n
unknotted, unlinked circles in the sphere
S^{3}

[
14
]
. There is also a version of this for symmetric automorphisms of other right-angled Artin groups
[
2
]
. Lawrence showed that the groups
\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
are finitely generated by partial conjugations and later Toinet
[
24
]
gave a finite presentation that was subsequently refined by Koban-Piggott
[
16
]
. The groups
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}({A_{\Gamma}})
lie inside the
Torelli
subgroup, which is the kernel of the epimorphism
\mathrm{Out}(A_{\Gamma})\to\mathrm{GL}(A_{\Gamma}/A^{\prime}_{\Gamma})
induced by the action on the abelianization and therefore they are residually torsion free nilpotent
[
24
]
. In particular, this implies that
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
is torsion free. For any
A_{\Gamma}
, it is possible to construct a new graph
\Delta
such that,
{A_{\Gamma}}\cong\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Delta})

[
25
]
, on the other hand, not all
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
are RAAGs
[
11
]
.

In
[
19
]
, McCullough and Miller construct a simplicial contractible complex that admits an action of the group of symmetric automorphisms of a free product. In the particular case of a free group, this is a complex
\mathrm{MM}_{n}
admitting an action of the group
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(F_{n})
with free abelian stabilizers. The construction is combinatorial, based on the Whitehead poset, and the argument given there to show contractibility is particularly intricate. This complex does not seem to admit a CAT(0) geometry
[
21
]
. The complex
\mathrm{MM}_{n}
has subsequently been used to prove that
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(F_{n})
is a duality group
[
3
]
, to compute the cohomology ring
\mathrm{H}^{*}(\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(F_{n}),\mathbb{Z}
)
[
14
]
, or to characterize certain properties of the BNRS-invariants of the groups
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(F_{n})

[
13
]
.

In this paper, we revisit and extend the construction of the McCullough-Miller complex to pure symmetric automorphism groups of arbitrary RAAGs. This involves finding a
\Gamma
-version
\mathrm{Wh}_{\Gamma}
of the Whitehead poset (see Section
4
). Once that is done, we define the
\Gamma
-McCullough Miller complex
\mathrm{MM}_{\Gamma}
as the simplicial realization of the poset of equivalence classes of marked elements of the
\Gamma
-Whitehead poset. The first properties of this construction follow easily and we get:

Theorem A
.

The group
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
acts on
\mathrm{MM}_{\Gamma}

with free abelian stabilizers and fundamental domain the simplicial realization of the
\Gamma
-Whitehead poset
\mathrm{Wh}_{\Gamma}
.

As for the original construction by McCullough and Miller, the most complicated part is proving contractibility. To do that we extend the technical definitions given by McCullough and Miller and combine them with results of Day
[
10
]
to get an analysis of the behavior of cyclic word lengths in RAAGs under the action of pure symmetric automorphisms. Using those results we are able to generalize the ideas of
[
19
]
to our setting and show the following theorem, which is the main result of this paper:

Theorem B
.

The complex
\mathrm{MM}_{\Gamma}
is contractible.

It is worth mentioning that our argument follows most of the nice and extremely useful ideas of McCullough and Miller in the original proof, but at the same time we have tried to simplify or clarify the arguments when possible. We also fix a particular issue with one crucial technical Lemma (see Example
6.13
).

Actions of groups on contractible complexes with nice cell stabilizers have many applications. As a well known example we may think of the action of a right angled Artin group on the Deligne complex, where cell stabilizers are parabolic subgroups, i.e., subgroups conjugated to the subgroup generated by complete subgraphs of the defining graph and as such are obviously free abelian. In our case, cell stabilizers are also free abelian so the complex
\mathrm{MM}_{\Gamma}
can be seen as a Deligne-type complex for the groups
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
.

We exhibit two applications of our complex. First, we show how to use the action of
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
on
\mathrm{MM}_{\Gamma}
to compute the cohomological dimension of the group in terms of a rank notion that we define for elements of the Whitehead poset (see Section
8
). This result generalizes the fact that
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(F_{n})
has cohomological dimension
n-2

[
5
]
. In
[
12
]
there is an algorithm to compute the virtual cohomological dimension of the whole group
\mathrm{Aut}(A_{\Gamma})
of automorphisms of a RAAG. As the authors point out, that algorithm can also be used for certain relative versions of
\mathrm{Out}(A_{\Gamma})
that include the subgroups
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
. However, that algorithm is complicated to apply. In the same paper, the authors show that in some cases the virtual cohomological dimension is realised by the rank of a free abelian subgroup, in the sense that there is a free abelian subgroup with rank the virtual cohomological dimension of the ambient group. We show that this is also true for the groups
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
.

Theorem C
.

The cohomological dimension of
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
is

\mathrm{cd}(\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma}))=\max\{r(\underline{\underline{A}})\mid\underline{\underline{A}}\in\mathrm{Wh}_{\Gamma}\}

and can be realized by a free abelian subgroup, that is, it is equal to the maximal rank of a free abelian subgroup of
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
.

Recently, Corrigan has also obtained Theorem C by different methods. He constructs a contractible cube complex with a proper action of the group of symmetric outer automorphisms
\Sigma\mathrm{Out}(A_{\Gamma})
which is a finite index extension of our group
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma})

[
7
]
. Abgrall has extended that construction to other automorphisms groups. Moreover, these complexes realise the cohomological dimension of the corresponding groups. In fact, one can see Corrigan’s and Abgrall’s complexes as a version of the Salvetti complex for these groups, whereas, as mentioned above, ours would correspond to the Deligne complex.

For our next application, we consider the
\ell^{2}
-cohomology. McCammond and Meier used the action of
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(F_{n})
on the McCullough-Miller complex
[
18
, Theorem 8.2]
to describe the
\ell^{2}
cohomology groups of
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(F_{n})
. With the complex MM
Γ
one gets the same result under an extra assumption on centralizers; and a weaker version for the general case:

Theorem D
.

For a right-angled Artin group
A_{\Gamma}
, the next equality about von Neumann dimensions holds:

\textrm{dim}_{\mathcal{N}G}(H^{i}(\textrm{MM}_{\Gamma};\mathcal{N}(\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma}))=\textrm{dim}_{\mathcal{N}G}(\mathcal{N}(\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma}))\otimes\overline{\mathrm{H}}^{i-1}(|\mathrm{Wh}_{\Gamma}^{0}|))

where
\mathcal{N}(\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma}))
is the group von Neumann algebra.

In addition, if
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
has no non-trivial element whose centralizer has finite index in
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
, then the
\ell^{2}
-cohomology groups of
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
are

\mathcal{H}^{i}(\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma}))\cong\ell^{2}(\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma}))\otimes\overline{\mathrm{H}}^{i-1}(|\mathrm{Wh}_{\Gamma}^{0}|)

Here,
\mathrm{Wh}_{G}^{0}\leq\mathrm{Wh}_{\Gamma}
is the subposet of the
\Gamma
-Whitehead poset obtained after removing the nuclear vertex, so
|\mathrm{Wh}_{\Gamma}|
is the cone over
|\mathrm{Wh}_{\Gamma}^{0}|
.

2.
The McCullough-Miller Complex

Let
F_{n}
be the free group of rank
n
with basis
X
, and let
\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(F_{n})
denote the group of pure symmetric automorphisms of
F_{n}
, i.e., the subgroup of Aut
(F_{n})
consisting of automorphisms that map each element of
X
to a conjugate of itself. This group contains the subgroup of inner automorphisms and the corresponding outer version is denoted
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(F_{n})=\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(F_{n})/\mathrm{Inn}(F_{n})
. In
[
19
]
McCullough and Miller introduced a family of contractible complexes denoted MM
n

which admit an action by
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(F_{n})
. This action is not free but stabilizers are free abelian. Moreover, there is a fundamental domain that can be described combinatorially as follows.

2.1.
Labeled bipartite trees, vertex types and based partitions

We begin by defining the elements that will be the vertices of the space that we are constructing.

Definition 2.1

(
n
-labeled bipartite trees or vertex types)
.

An
[n]
-vertex type
or
[n]
-labeled bipartite tree
(we will often omit
[n]

T

\iota

T
satisfying the following two conditions. Elements in the image of
\iota

1)

each edge of
T
has exactly one labeled endpoint, and

2)

each node with valence 1 is labeled.

We say that two vertex types are equivalent if there is a label preserving graph isomorphism between them. The
rank
of a vertex type

is
m-1
where
m
is the number of unlabeled nodes in
T
. There is only one
[n]
-vertex type of rank 0, namely the tree with a single unlabeled node of valence
n

and is denoted by

2.2.
The Whitehead poset

Next, we define a partial order on the set of vertex types. To do that, let
T
be a vertex type. We say that
\hat{T}
is obtained from
T
by
folding
if
\hat{T}
is the vertex type obtained by identifying a pair of edges
e_{1}\neq e_{2}
of
T
that share
a common labeled endpoint. Folding reduces the rank of a tree by 1. For example, in
Figure
1
, folding
B
at 2 yields
A
, folding
A
at 3 yields
\mathcal{O}
,
and one of the three possible foldings of
C
at 3 yields
A
. If
\hat{T}
is obtained from
T
by a series of foldings, we put
\hat{T}<T
.

Definition 2.2

(Whitehead poset)
.

The Whitehead poset
\mathrm{Wh}_{n}
is the poset consisting
of all
[n]
-vertex types under the partial order defined above. Observe that for any vertex type
T
we have
\mathcal{O}\leq T
, and the poset distance between
\mathcal{O}
and
T
is precisely the rank of
T
. It is also easy to verify that every maximal chain in the poset
\mathrm{Wh}_{n}
has length
n-1
.

To construct a complex admitting an action of the group
\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(F_{n})
, we need the notion of markings that we consider next.

Definition 2.3

(Markings)
.

A
marking
of a vertex type
T
or of a based partition
\underline{T}_{i}
is an ordered
basis
Y=\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}\}
of
F_{n}
such that each
y_{i}
is a conjugate of

x_{i}\in X
. The
marked vertex type
is the pair
(Y,T)
, and the
marked based partition

(Y,\underline{T}_{i})
. We see the marking as a relabeling of the nodes of
T
or of
\underline{T}_{i}
where
y_{j}
is associated with the node
j
, in particular the element
y_{i}
is the operative factor of
(Y,\underline{T}_{i})
.

With this definition, observe that the set of marked vertex types is also a poset with
(Y^{\prime},T^{\prime})\leq(Y,T)
if and only if
Y^{\prime}=Y
and
T^{\prime}\leq T
. Also, there is an obvious action of
\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(F_{n})
on this poset via
\alpha(Y,T)=(\alpha(Y),T)
for
\alpha\in\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(F_{n})
. However, in order to get a space with an action of the outer automorphism group, we need to construct a quotient of this poset.

Definition 2.4

(Carried automorphisms)
.

Let
(Y,\underline{T}_{i})
be a marked based partition with marking
Y=\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}\}
and operative factor
y_{i}
. We say that
\alpha\in\mathrm{Aut}(F_{n})
is carried by
(Y,\underline{T}_{i})
if

i)

\alpha(y_{i})=y_{i}
, i.e., the operative factor gets fixed.

ii)

For all
y_{j}
,
\alpha(y_{j})
is the result of conjugating
y_{j}
by a power of
y_{i}
.

iii)

If
y_{j},y_{k}
lie in the same petal of
\underline{T}_{i}
, then they get conjugated by the same power of
y_{i}
when applying
\alpha
.

We say that
\underline{T}_{i}
is a
full carrier
of
\alpha
if it carries
\alpha
and each petal gets conjugated by a different power of
y_{i}
. We also say that an automorphism
\alpha
is
carried by the vertex type

(Y,T)
if
\alpha=\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{k}
, where each
\alpha_{j}
is carried by some based partition
(Y,\underline{T_{j}})
of
(Y,T)
.

We can use the notion of carried automorphisms to define an equivalence relationship
\equiv
in the set of marked vertex types as the relation generated by

(Y_{1},T)\equiv(Y_{2},T)\text{ if there is $\alpha\in\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(F_{n})$ carried by $(Y_{1},T)$ such that $\alpha(Y_{1},T)=(Y_{2},T)$}.

The equivalence class of an element
(Y,T)
will be denoted
[Y,T]
.

Definition 2.5

(The McCullough-Miller poset and complex)
.

The McCullough-Miller poset is the poset of marked
[n]
-vertex types, modulo the equivalence relation
\equiv
defined above.
The McCullough-Miller complex
\mathrm{MM}_{n}
is the simplicial realization of the McCullough-Miller poset.

Theorem 2.6

(McCullough-Miller
[
19
]
)
.

The complex
\mathrm{MM}_{n}
is a contractible complex of dimension
n-2
.

The statement about the dimension is a direct consequence of the fact, stated above, that maximal chains in the poset have length
n-1
. The complex constructed in the following sections is a generalization of this space.

The action of the group
\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(F_{n})
on the set of marked vertex types yields a well defined action on
\mathrm{MM}_{n}
that factors through the natural projection
\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(F_{n})\to\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(F_{n})
. This action has a fundamental
domain which we can identify with the Whitehead poset
|\mathrm{Wh}_{n}|
, obtained by restricting to classes
[X,T]
where the marking is the defining
basis.

The
stabilizer of a vertex
[Y,T]\in\mathrm{MM}_{n}
in
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(F_{n})

consists of all outer automorphisms that can be expressed as a product of automorphisms
that are carried by
(Y,T)
. Section 5 of
[
19
]
implies the
following:

Lemma 2.7

(
[
19
]
)
.

Under the action of
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(F_{n})
, the stabilizer of a rank
k
vertex of
MM_{n}

is a free abelian group of rank
k
.

3.
Pure symmetric automorphisms of RAAGs

For the rest of the paper we fix a finite simplicial graph
\Gamma
with set of vertices
V_{\Gamma}:=V(\Gamma)
and set of edges
E(\Gamma)
. We view
V_{\Gamma}
as the standard generating set for the right angled Artin group
A_{\Gamma}
defined in the introduction.
Let
v\in V_{\Gamma}
,
\mathrm{lk}_{\Gamma}(v)
denotes the
link
of
v
, which is the subgraph induced by the vertices of
\Gamma
adjacent to
v
and
\mathrm{st}_{\Gamma}(v)
denotes the
star
, which is the subgraph induced by
\mathrm{lk}_{\Gamma}(v)\cup\{v\}
. Here, the subgraph of
\Gamma

induced
by a set of vertices
\Omega\subseteq V_{\Gamma}
is the maximal subgraph of
\Gamma
with vertex set
\Omega
.

Definition 3.1

(Graphically reduced words)

3.1.
Pure symmetric automorphisms of RAAGs

As in the free group case, we now define the notion of pure symmetric automorphisms for RAAGs.

Definition 3.2

Let

We begin by defining the analogous to the notion of based partitions in our case. In the case where

Definition 4.1

(Valid partitions and length)

Let

An important observation is that, when considering

From now on, all the based partitions will be assumed to be

Definition 4.2

(Crossings, disjoint and compatible partition)

Crossings are defined as in the case of

We define the number of crossings

as before.
We say that
\underline{A}_{a},\underline{B}_{b}
are

and
\underline{A}_{a}

Definition 4.3

(Vertex type)

We define a

We say that two vertex types

Definition 4.4

(Order)

Let

and
\underline{A}_{a}
,

.

For vertex types, we will say that
\underline{\underline{A}}\leq\underline{\underline{B}}
if
\underline{A}_{a}\leq\underline{B}_{a}
, for any

.

The fact that coalescing petals can not increase the number of crossings implies the following.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\underline{A}_{a}<\underline{B}_{a}
,

\underline{\underline{A}}<\underline{\underline{B}}
,

.

. In particular,

Definition 4.7

(Marked vertex type and the action of
\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
)
.

We say that a generating system
X
of
A_{\Gamma}
is a
basis
if there is some
\alpha\in\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
such that
X=\alpha(V_{\Gamma})
.
A
marked based partition
is a pair
(X,\underline{A})
where
X
is an ordered basis of
A_{\Gamma}
and
\underline{A}
is a based partition and a
marked vertex type

(X,\underline{\underline{A}})
is the family of marked based partitions
(X,\underline{A}_{a})
where
\underline{A}_{a}
runs over the set of based partitions of a vertex type
\underline{\underline{A}}
. As in Definition

\underline{A}
we understand that we are relabeling the elements in the partition using

(via the automorphism
\alpha
). We extend the partial order of Definition
4.4
to marked vertex types as follows:
(X_{1},\underline{\underline{A}})\leq(X_{2},\underline{\underline{B}})
if
X_{1}=X_{2}
and
\underline{\underline{A}}\leq\underline{\underline{B}}
. The marked vertex types form a poset and the group
\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
acts on this poset via

\alpha(X,\underline{\underline{A}})=(\alpha(X),\underline{\underline{A}})

where
\alpha\in\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
.

Definition 4.8

(Carried automorphism)
.

Let
\alpha\in\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}({A_{\Gamma}})
, we say that
\alpha
is carried by a based partition
\underline{A}_{a}
with operative factor

if

i)

\alpha(a)=a
, i.e. the operative factor gets fixed.

ii)

for any
b\in V_{\Gamma}-\mathrm{st}_{\Gamma}(a)
,
\alpha(b)
is the result of conjugating
b
by a power of
a
.

iii)

If
b,c
lie in the same petal of
\underline{A}_{a}
, then they get conjugated by the same power of
a
when applying
\alpha
.

We say that
\underline{A}_{a}
is a
full carrier
of
\alpha
if it carries
\alpha
and each petal gets conjugated by a different power of
a
. If
\alpha
is not the identity and is carried by some based partition, then a full carrier is uniquely determined, to see it note that it is enough to take any based partition
\underline{A}_{a}
that carries
\alpha
and then join all the petals where the action is the same.

We say that
\alpha
is
carried by a vertex type

\underline{\underline{A}}
if
\alpha=\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{k}
where each
\alpha_{i}
is carried by some based partition
\underline{A}_{a}
of
\underline{\underline{A}}
.

Finally, we say that
\alpha
is
carried by a marked vertex type

(X,\underline{\underline{A}})
if
\gamma\alpha\gamma^{-1}
is carried by
\underline{\underline{A}}
where
\gamma(X)=V_{\Gamma}
.

Observe that an automorphism
\alpha
is carried by a based partition
\underline{A}_{a}
if and only if it lies in the subgroup generated by the partial conjugations
C^{a}_{B}
where
B
runs over the petals of
\underline{A}_{a}
. Note also that the full carrier of a partial conjugation
C^{a}_{A}
is the based partition

\{\{a\},A,\Gamma-(\mathrm{st}_{\Gamma}(a)\cup A)\}.

The following is an easy observation that will be useful below.

Lemma 4.9
.

The set of automorphisms carried by a vertex type is a subgroup of
\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
that contains the inner automorphisms. Moreover,
given two vertex types
\underline{\underline{A}}
and
\underline{\underline{B}}
such that
\underline{\underline{A}}<\underline{\underline{B}}
, the subgroup of automorphisms carried by
\underline{\underline{A}}
is inside the subgroup of automorphisms carried by
\underline{\underline{B}}
.

In what follows, we will often consider the subgroups of outer automorphisms carried by either a given based partition or a vertex type, which are just the corresponding quotients over
\mathrm{Inn}(A_{\Gamma})
.

Next, we define an equivalence relationship in the set of marked vertex types as follows.

Definition 4.10

(Vertex types equivalence relation)
.

We say that two marked vertex types
(X_{1},\underline{\underline{A}})
and
(X_{2},\underline{\underline{A}})
are equivalent and we put
(X_{1},\underline{\underline{A}})\equiv(X_{2},\underline{\underline{A}})
if
there exists an
\alpha\in\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}({A_{\Gamma}})
carried by
(X_{1},\underline{\underline{A}})
such that
\alpha(X_{1})=X_{2}
. We denote by
[X_{1},\underline{\underline{A}}]
the equivalence class of
(X_{1},\underline{\underline{A}})
under this relation.

In particular, observe that if
\alpha
is inner, then
[X_{1},\underline{\underline{A}}]=[\alpha(X_{1}),\underline{\underline{A}}]
.

Definition 4.11

(
\Gamma
-McCullough-Miller poset)
.

The
\Gamma
-McCullough-Miller poset is the poset formed by the set of equivalence classes of marked vertex types under the equivalence relationship
\equiv
of Definition
4.10
with the induced partial order.

The group
\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
acts on this poset via

\alpha([X,\underline{\underline{A}}])=[\alpha(X),\underline{\underline{A}}]

and this action factors through the epimorphism
\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})\to\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
so we also have an action of
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
.

The
\Gamma
-McCullough-Miller poset is formed by copies of the
\Gamma
-Whitehead poset attached to each other through the equivalence relation
\equiv
defined before. It is easy to verify that nuclear vertex types only carry inner automorphisms, so there is a bijection between the set of nuclear vertices
[X,\mathcal{O}]
in the
\Gamma
-McCullough-Miller poset and the set of conjugacy classes of bases of
A_{\Gamma}
.

Definition 4.12

(
\mathrm{MM}_{\Gamma}
complex)
.

We define the McCullough-Miller complex
\mathrm{MM}_{\Gamma}
for a RAAG
A_{\Gamma}
as the simplicial realization of the
\Gamma
-McCullough-Miller poset.

Lemma 4.13
.

The stars of the nuclear vertices
[X_{1},\mathcal{O}]
and
[X_{2},\mathcal{O}]
intersect if and only if there is an
\alpha\in\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}({A_{\Gamma}})
carried by some
(X_{1},\underline{\underline{A}})
such that
\alpha(X_{1})=X_{2}
.

Proof.

The stars intersect if and only if we can represent a vertex both as
[X_{1},\underline{\underline{A}}]
and
[X_{2},\underline{\underline{A}}]
. By definition of the equivalence relation (
4.10
) we have that this can only happen if there is an
\alpha\in\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}({A_{\Gamma}})
carried by
(X_{1},\underline{\underline{A}})
such that
\alpha(X_{1})=X_{2}
.
∎

Remark
.

The definition of the action above implies that the stabilizer
H=\mathrm{Stab}_{\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma})}[V_{\Gamma},\underline{\underline{A}}]
of the vertex
[V_{\Gamma},\underline{\underline{A}}]
in
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
is precisely the subgroup generated by those outer automorphisms carried by the vertex type
\underline{\underline{A}}
. Therefore if
\alpha
is such that
X=\alpha(V_{\Gamma})
, the stabilizer of the vertex
[X,\underline{\underline{A}}]=\alpha([V_{\Gamma},\underline{\underline{A}}])
is
\alpha H\alpha^{-1}
. Taking this into account, one sees that the McCullough-Miller complex
\mathrm{MM}_{\Gamma}
can be also defined as follows. Recall that for a poset
\mathcal{F}
of subgroups of a group
G
, one can define a new poset, called the
coset poset
, as

C(\mathcal{F})=\{gH\mid H\in\mathcal{F},g\in G\}.

Then, the McCullough-Miller poset is the coset poset of the family

\{\mathrm{Stab}_{\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma})}[V_{\Gamma},\underline{\underline{A}}]\mid\underline{\underline{A}}\text{ vertex type}\}

of subgroups of
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
and
\mathrm{MM}_{\Gamma}
is the associated geometric realization.

We will see below that these stabilizers are free abelian groups, that their rank can be computed in terms of the length of the based partitions of
\underline{\underline{A}}
and that they yield in fact all the cell stabilizers of the McCullough-Miller complex
\mathrm{MM}_{\Gamma}
.

5.
Compatibility, refinement, disjunction

5.1.
Compatibility and stabilizers

We begin this section introducing some notation that will be useful to understand when two based partitions are compatible.
Let
\underline{A}_{a}
be a based partition. For each
b\in V_{\Gamma}
not linked to
a
we denote by
D^{b}_{\underline{A}_{a}}
the petal of
\underline{A}_{a}
that contains
b
.

In the next statement and throughout the paper, we will abuse notation and write
A-B
instead of
A-(A\cap B)
for arbitrary sets
A,B
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
a,b\in V_{\Gamma}
and
\underline{A}_{a}
and
\underline{B}_{b}
based partitions with
a\not\in\mathrm{st}_{\Gamma}(b)
. Set

\underline{A}_{a}=\{\{a\},D^{b}_{\underline{A}_{a}},P_{1},\ldots,P_{t}\},

\underline{B}_{b}=\{\{b\},D^{a}_{\underline{B}_{b}},Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}\}.

Then each of the petals
P_{i}
is a union of shared and/or subordinate components of
\Gamma-\mathrm{st}_{\Gamma}(a)
respect to
b
, whereas
D^{b}_{\underline{A}_{a}}
contains the dominant component and possibly some of the shared and/or subordinate components. If there are crossings, say
P_{i}\cap Q_{j}\neq\emptyset
, then
P_{i}\cap Q_{j}
is a union of shared components only and the analogous statements hold for
b
.

Moreover,
\underline{A}_{a}
and
\underline{B}_{b}
are compatible if and only if

\displaystyle D^{a}_{\underline{B}_{b}}

\displaystyle=P_{1}\cup\ldots P_{t}\cup(\mathrm{st}_{\Gamma}(a)-\mathrm{lk}_{\Gamma}(b))\text{ and }

\displaystyle D^{b}_{\underline{B}_{a}}

\displaystyle=Q_{1}\cup\ldots Q_{k}\cup(\mathrm{st}_{\Gamma}(b)-\mathrm{lk}_{\Gamma}(a)).

Proof.

As
\underline{A}_{a}
and
\underline{B}_{b}
are
\Gamma
-valid, petals must be union of connected components of the complements of the corresponding stars. Obviously, for, say,
a
, the dominant component must be in
D^{b}_{\underline{A}_{a}}
and then the
\Gamma
-validity condition implies
\mathrm{st}_{\Gamma}(b)-\mathrm{lk}_{\Gamma}(a)\subseteq D^{b}_{\underline{A}_{a}}
. The statement about the
P_{i}
’s is obvious, and if
P_{i}\cap Q_{j}\neq\emptyset
, this intersection can not contain any subordinate component, so it can only consist of shared ones.

Assume now that
\underline{A}_{a}
and
\underline{B}_{b}
are compatible. As
a
and
b
are not linked,
\underline{A}_{a}
and
\underline{B}_{b}
must be disjoint. So there are no crossings, meaning that the union
P_{1}\cup\ldots\cup P_{t}
must lie inside
\mathrm{st}_{\Gamma}(b)\cup D^{a}_{\underline{B}_{b}}
. But as the
P_{i}
’s are union of shared and/or subordinate components, they can not intersect
\mathrm{st}_{\Gamma}(b)
so
P_{1}\cup\ldots\cup P_{t}\subseteq D^{a}_{\underline{B}_{b}}
. This implies

D^{a}_{\underline{B}_{b}}=P_{1}\cup\ldots P_{t}\cup(\mathrm{st}_{\Gamma}(a)-\mathrm{lk}_{\Gamma}(b))

and by symmetry we have the same for
b
. The converse is obvious.
∎

Remark
.

Observe that if two based partitions
\underline{A}_{a}
and
\underline{B}_{b}
cross, then there must be some shared component between
\Gamma-\mathrm{st}_{\Gamma}(a)
and
\Gamma-\mathrm{st}_{\Gamma}(b)
. When this happens, the vertices
a
and
b
are said to form a
separating intersection link, or a SIL pair
(see for example
[
11
]
).

We have seen already that the different based partitions associated to a given vertex type must be compatible. This has very strong consequences for the sets of outer automorphisms carried by them, as we shall now see.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\alpha=C_{A}^{a}
,
\beta=C_{B}^{b}
be partial conjugations for
a,b\in V_{\Gamma}
carried by compatible based partitions
\underline{A}_{a}
and
\underline{B}_{b}
. Then
\alpha
and
\beta
commute modulo
\mathrm{Inn}(A_{\Gamma})
and
either
a\in\mathrm{st}_{\Gamma}(b)
or, up to multiplying by inner automorphisms, we may assume
A\cap B=A\cap\mathrm{st}_{\Gamma}(b)=B\cap\mathrm{st}_{\Gamma}(a)=\emptyset
.

Proof.

If

and

are either equal or adjacent we are in case

of Theorem

and
\alpha
and
\beta
commute. So from now on we assume that

.

Put

\underline{A}_{a}=\{\{a\},D^{b}_{\underline{A}_{a}},P_{1},\ldots,P_{t}\}
and
\underline{B}_{b}=\{\{b\},D^{a}_{\underline{B}_{b}},Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}\}
where, as before,
D^{b}_{\underline{A}_{a}}
and
D^{a}_{\underline{B}_{b}}
are the petals that contain the respective dominant components.

As
C_{A}^{a}
is carried by
\underline{A}_{a}
,
A
is a union of (some of) the petals in
\underline{A}_{a}
. If
D^{b}_{\underline{A}_{a}}
is one of those, then multiplying by the inner automorphism
C_{V_{\Gamma}}^{a^{-1}}
we get another representative of the same outer automorphism as
C_{A}^{a}
that acts by conjugation with
a^{-1}
and for which the corresponding

does not contain the petal

. So, up to multiplying by this inner automorphism we may assume that
A\subseteq P_{1}\cup\cdots\cup P_{t}
. The same argument for

implies that we may also assume

. This and the fact that there are no crosses implies that decomposing

and

and

of Theorem

and

Moreover, the conditions above imply
A\cap B=\mathrm{st}_{\Gamma}(b)\cap A=\mathrm{st}_{\Gamma}(a)\cap B=\emptyset
(recall that the petals

do not intersect
\mathrm{st}_{\Gamma}(b)
and the same for the

\mathrm{st}_{\Gamma}(a)
).
∎

As a consequence, we have:

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\underline{\underline{A}}
be a vertex type. Then the outer automorphisms carried by
\underline{\underline{A}}
form a free abelian group. This group is precisely the stabilizer of the vertex
v=[V_{\Gamma},\underline{\underline{A}}]
of
\mathrm{MM}_{\Gamma}
under the action of
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
.

Proof.

The outer automorphism carried by
\underline{\underline{A}}
form a subgroup of
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
generated by the images of the partial conjugations carried by the based partitions of
\underline{\underline{A}}
. These can be written as products of partial conjugations
C^{a}_{P}
where
P
is a petal of a based partition
\underline{A}_{a}
of
\underline{\underline{A}}
. Therefore it is finitely generated. It is abelian by Lemma
5.2
and torsion free because the ambient group
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
is, so it is free abelian. For the last statement, see the comment after Lemma
4.13
.
∎

We can now deduce Theorem A:

Proof of Theorem A.
We identify the simplicial realization of the
\Gamma
-Whitehead poset with the subcomplex induced by vertices of the form
[V_{\Gamma},\underline{\underline{A}}]
. By construction, this is a strong fundamental domain for the action of
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
on
\mathrm{MM}_{\Gamma}
. For the statement about centralizers, note that we only have to consider stabilizers of cells in the strong fundamental domain. We have seen that the stabilizer of the vertex
[V_{\Gamma},\underline{\underline{A}}]
in
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
is precisely the subgroup generated by those outer automorphisms carried by the vertex type
\underline{\underline{A}}
, moreover Lemma

implies that the stabilizer of the simplex

[V_{\Gamma},\underline{\underline{A}}^{0}]<[V_{\Gamma},\underline{\underline{A}}^{1}]<\ldots<[V_{\Gamma},\underline{\underline{A}}^{k}]

is the stabilizer of
[V_{\Gamma},\underline{\underline{A}}^{0}]
, in other words,
we only have to consider vertex stabilizers so it is enough to use Lemma
5.3
. ∎

In fact, the condition that the based partitions are compatible is not only sufficient, it is necessary for the carried outer automorphisms to commute as we shall see now.

Lemma 5.4
.

Two based partitions
\underline{A},\underline{B}
are compatible if and only if the outer automorphisms carried by
\underline{A}
commute with the outer automorphisms carried by
\underline{B}
.

Proof.

We have seen in Lemma
5.2
that compatibility implies that the carried outer automorphisms commute.
The converse is obvious if
a
and
b
are either equal or adjacent so we assume this is not the case. Put

\underline{A}=\{\{a\},D^{b}_{\underline{A}_{a}},P_{1},\ldots,P_{t}\}
and
\underline{B}=\{\{b\},D^{a}_{\underline{A}_{b}},Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}\}
where
D^{b}_{\underline{A}_{a}}
and
D^{a}_{\underline{A}_{b}}
contain the respective dominant components so
a\in D^{a}_{\underline{A}_{b}}
and
b\in D^{b}_{\underline{A}_{a}}
. Assume for a contradiction that
\underline{A}
and
\underline{B}
are not disjoint so there is at least a cross between two petals
P_{i},Q_{j}
and take
c\in P_{i}\cap Q_{j}
. Consider the partial conjugations
\alpha=C^{a}_{P_{i}}
and
\beta=C^{b}_{Q_{j}}
and assume that there is some
g\in A_{\Gamma}
such that for the internal automorphism
\gamma\in\mathrm{Inn}(A_{\Gamma})
that consists of conjugation by
g^{-1}
we have

\alpha\beta=\gamma\beta\alpha.

Then

\displaystyle\alpha\beta(a)=a=gag^{-1}

\displaystyle=\gamma\beta\alpha(a),

\displaystyle\alpha\beta(b)=b=gbg^{-1}

\displaystyle=\gamma\beta\alpha(b),

\displaystyle\alpha\beta(c)=abcb^{-1}a^{-1}

\displaystyle=gbaca^{-1}b^{-1}g^{-1}=\gamma\beta\alpha(c).

So
g
lies in the centralizers of
a
and of
b
in
A_{\Gamma}
. These centralizers are generated by
\mathrm{st}_{\Gamma}(a)
and
\mathrm{st}_{\Gamma}(b)
respectively and as
b\not\in\mathrm{st}_{\Gamma}(a)
and
a\not\in\mathrm{st}_{\Gamma}(b)
, we deduce that there is no
a
nor
b
in any graphically reduced expression of
g
. From the last equation above we have

abc^{-1}b^{-1}a^{-1}gbaca^{-1}b^{-1}=g

and we see that if
g
is graphically reduced, so is the left hand expression so we have a contradiction.
∎

This result gives another proof of the fact (Lemma
4.5
) that for vertex types
\underline{\underline{A}}
and
\underline{\underline{B}}
with
\underline{\underline{A}}<\underline{\underline{B}}
, the corresponding based partitions are compatible, because Lemma
4.9
implies that the subgroup of carried automorphisms of
\underline{\underline{A}}
is inside the subgroup of carried automorphisms of
\underline{\underline{B}}
.

Definition 5.5

(Join)
.

If a collection of vertex types
\{\underline{\underline{A}}^{1},\ldots,\underline{\underline{A}}^{k}\}
has a least upper bound, we denote it by
\underline{\underline{A}}^{1}\vee\cdots\vee\underline{\underline{A}}^{k}
, and call it the
join
of
\{\underline{\underline{A}}^{1},\ldots,\underline{\underline{A}}^{k}\}
.

Lemma 5.6
.

A collection
\Omega=\{\underline{\underline{A}}^{1},\ldots,\underline{\underline{A}}^{k}\}
of vertex types is pairwise compatible if and only if it has an upper bound. Moreover, if it has an upper bound, then it has a least upper bound.

Proof.

Assume first that there is an upper bound. Then Lemma
4.9
implies that the subgroup of carried outer automorphisms of each vertex type in
\Omega
is inside that of the upper bound. That subgroup is free abelian by Lemma
5.3
so those carried outer automorphisms commute with each other and by Lemma
5.4
, the based partitions of the vertex types in
\Omega
must be all compatible. It is also easy to provide an alternative direct argument: if there is an upper bound, Lemma
4.5
implies that the vertex types in
\Omega
are all compatible with the upper bound. Suppose that
\underline{\underline{A}}
and
\underline{\underline{B}}
are vertices in
\Omega
that have based partitions which are not compatible with each other. In such a case, if we make divisions of the petals of
\underline{\underline{B}}
, the partitions will remain not compatible (dividing petals can not reduce the number of crosses). However, this leads to a contradiction because by dividing petals in
\underline{\underline{B}}
we could reach the upper bound, which is compatible with
\underline{\underline{A}}
.

We will show the converse constructively: if
\underline{A}^{1}_{a}
and
\underline{A}^{2}_{a}
are based partitions with the same operative factor
a
, we define their union
\underline{A}^{1}_{a}\vee\underline{A}^{2}_{a}
as the based partition with the same operative factor
a
and whose petals are the non-empty intersections of the petals of
\underline{A}^{1}_{a}
and
\underline{A}^{2}_{a}
. More generally, given a collection
\{\underline{A}^{1}_{a},\ldots,\underline{A}^{m}_{a}\}
of based partitions with the same operative factor, we define
\underline{A}^{1}_{a}\vee\ldots\vee\underline{A}^{m}_{a}
inductively as
((\underline{A}^{1}_{a}\vee\underline{A}^{2}_{a})\vee\underline{A}^{3}_{a})\ldots\vee\underline{A}^{m}_{a}
. Observe that this process keeps the
\Gamma
-validity (intersections of petals are unions of shared components).

For a collection of compatible vertex types
\{\underline{\underline{A}}^{1},\ldots,\underline{\underline{A}}^{k}\}
, we define
\underline{\underline{A}}^{1}\vee\ldots\vee\underline{\underline{A}}^{k}
as the vertex type whose non-trivial based partition with operative factor
a
is the union of all the non-trivial based partitions of the family with operative factor
a
. Recall that to ensure that this union is a vertex type, i.e., that its based partitions are pairwise compatible, we need the initial collection to be compatible. It is obvious that
\underline{\underline{A}}^{1}\vee\ldots\vee\underline{\underline{A}}^{k}
is an upper bound and is easy to check using the definitions that any other upper bound must be greater or equal than the join.
Intuitively, when joining two based partitions we are computing their “least common multiple”.

∎

Lemma 6.14
.

Let

,

(7)

\mathrm{red}_{W}(\beta)=\langle b,B^{\pm}\rangle_{W,b}-\langle B^{\pm},(L-B^{\pm})-\{b\}\rangle_{W,b}

Let

. We extend

Lemma 6.15

(The Factorization Lemma)
.

Let
X=\gamma(V_{\Gamma})
be a basis of
A_{\Gamma}
and
\alpha\in\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
with a factorization
\alpha=\alpha_{m}\cdots\alpha_{2}\alpha_{1}
such that each
\alpha_{i}
is carried by a marked based partition
(X,\underline{A}^{i}_{a_{i}})
. Assume that for each

, either

and
\underline{A}^{i}_{a_{i}}
,
\underline{A}^{j}_{a_{j}}
are compatible or
a_{i}=a_{j}
and
\gamma\alpha_{i}\gamma^{-1}
and
\gamma\alpha_{j}\gamma^{-1}
have disjoint supports. Then

\mathrm{red}_{W}(X,\alpha_{m}\cdots\alpha_{2}\alpha_{1})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathrm{red}_{W}(X,\alpha_{i}).

Proof.

By further factoring the
\alpha_{i}
’s if necessary, we may assume that each
\gamma\alpha_{j}\gamma^{-1}
is indeed a power of a partial conjugation. We argue by induction on

. The hypothesis implies that the
\alpha_{i}
’s commute modulo inner automorphisms and this property is kept after the possible splitting. Using iii) of Lemma
6.4
we have

\mathrm{red}_{W}(X,\alpha_{m}\cdots\alpha_{2}\alpha_{1})=\mathrm{red}_{W}(X,\alpha_{1})+\mathrm{red}_{W}(\alpha_{1}(X),\alpha_{m}\cdots\alpha_{2})

As
\alpha_{1}(X)=\alpha_{1}\gamma(V_{\Gamma})
and the
\alpha_{i}
’s commute modulo inner automorphisms, we see that each
\alpha_{i}
is also carried by
(\alpha_{1}(X),\underline{A}^{i}_{a_{i}})
and then by induction we have

\mathrm{red}_{W}(\alpha_{1}(X),\alpha_{m}\cdots\alpha_{2})=\sum_{i=2}^{m}\mathrm{red}_{W}(\alpha_{1}(X),\alpha_{i})

so we only have to check that for any

\mathrm{red}_{W}(\alpha_{1}(X),\alpha_{i})=\mathrm{red}_{W}(X,\alpha_{i}).

Let

,
\beta_{1}=\gamma\alpha_{1}\gamma^{-1}
and
\beta_{i}=\gamma\alpha_{i}\gamma^{-1}
. We have

\displaystyle\mathrm{red}_{W}(X,\alpha_{i})=\mathrm{red}_{W^{\prime}}(\beta_{i})

\displaystyle\mathrm{red}_{W}(\alpha_{1}(X),\alpha_{i})=\mathrm{red}_{\beta_{1}^{-1}(W^{\prime})}(\beta_{i})

so we may assume that

and that
\alpha_{1}
,
\alpha_{i}
are both powers partial conjugations carried by

,
\underline{A}^{i}_{a_{i}}
and we have to show

\mathrm{red}_{\alpha_{1}^{-1}(W)}(\alpha_{i})=\mathrm{red}_{W}(\alpha_{i})

If

then, as the supports are disjoint, Lemma

implies (

). If
a_{i}\neq a_{j}
, then the based partitions
\underline{A}^{1}_{a_{1}}
and
\underline{A}^{i}_{a_{i}}
are compatible and Lemma

implies that there are inner automorphisms

and

such that

are powers of partial conjugations that satisfy the hypothesis of Lemma

Lemma 6.16

(The Existence Lemma)
.

Let
W
be a set of cyclically graphically reduced words such that for the nuclear vertex
u_{0}=[V_{\Gamma},\mathcal{O}]
the height
\|u_{0}\|_{W}
is not minimal. Then there exists a
partial conjugation
\alpha
which is strictly reductive at
W
, i.e., with
\mathrm{red}_{W}(\alpha)>0
.

Proof.

Let
u=[Y,\mathcal{O}]
be a nuclear vertex whose height
\|u\|_{W}
with respect to
W
is minimal so
\|u_{0}\|_{W}>\|u\|_{W}
and take
\beta\in\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
such that
\beta(V_{\Gamma})=Y
. For technical reasons that will be clear below, we need to work with the following modification of
W
:

W^{\prime}=W\cup\{b^{k}\mid b\in V_{\Gamma}\}

for some
k>0
. Then

\|u_{0}\|_{W^{\prime}}=\sum_{h\in W^{\prime}}|h|_{V_{\Gamma}}=\|u_{0}\|_{W}+kn

and the fact that
\beta
is symmetric implies that also

\|u\|_{W^{\prime}}=\sum_{h\in W^{\prime}}|h|_{Y}=\sum_{h\in W^{\prime}}|h|_{\beta(V_{\Gamma})}=\sum_{h\in W^{\prime}}|\beta^{-1}(h)|_{V_{\Gamma}}=\|u\|_{W}+kn

and we also have

Theorem B of
[
10
]
states that the automorphisms in
\langle\Omega_{l}\rangle
can be peak reduced by elements in
\Omega_{l}
respect to any set of cyclic words

. Here,
\Omega_{l}
is the set of long range Whitehead automorphisms

of
A_{\Gamma}
: these are automorphisms for which there is an

and

so that

. In particular

7.
Proof of Theorem B

In this section we prove Theorem B, which asserts that
\mathrm{MM}_{\Gamma}
is contractible. The proof follows the strategy of
[
19
]
and proceeds as follows. The complex
\mathrm{MM}_{\Gamma}
is the union of the starts of the nuclear vertices, so we can form
\mathrm{MM}_{\Gamma}
by adding inductively these stars and show contractibility by proving that at each step the intersection of a new star with the union of the stars previously accumulated is contractible. Thus most of the argument takes place in the star of a single nuclear vertex, which is isomorphic to the simplicial realization of the
\Gamma
-Whitehead poset
\mathrm{Wh}_{\Gamma}
. To analyze the intersection we will use Quillen’s poset Lemmas, which are stated in Subsection
7.1
. The main argument is given in Subsection

.

7.1.

Definition 7.1

(Poset map)

A function

For

. The simplicial realizations are denoted
|\mathrm{st}_{+}(x)|
and
|\mathrm{st}_{-}(x)|
; these are contractible subcomplexes of the simplicial realization

.

From Chapter 1.3 of
[

we have the following lemma.

Let

and

relative to the subcomplex of

spanned by

The next consequence is also a famous result by Quillen that will be very useful below.

\|u\|_{W_{0}}=\sum_{g\in W_{0}}|g|_{\alpha^{-1}(V_{\Gamma})}=\sum_{g\in W_{0}}|\alpha(g)|_{V_{\Gamma}}.

As

This lemma completes the first step of the proof of Theorem B. To proceed with the second step,

Proposition 7.10

(Contractibility of
S
)
.

Let
\underline{Z}^{1},\underline{Z}^{2},\ldots,\underline{Z}^{r}
be a compatible collection of fully reductive based partitions, and let
T
be the subposet of
S
consisting of all those vertex types in
S
which are compatible with
\underline{Z}^{1},\underline{Z}^{2},\ldots,\underline{Z}^{r}
. Then

is contractible.

Proof.

By Lemma
7.9
, there exists a fully strictly reductive based partition
\underline{P}
compatible with
\underline{Z}^{1},\underline{Z}^{2},\ldots,\underline{Z}^{r}
. We may choose such a
\underline{P}
so that the reductivity
\mathrm{red}_{W}(\underline{P})
is maximal between all the based partitions satisfying the same conditions (observe that there are only finitely many based partitions). Let
\underline{\underline{P}}
be the vertex type with
\underline{P}
as the only nontrivial based partition.

We let
T_{0}\subseteq T
be the subposet of
T
consisting of those vertex types in which are also compatible with
\underline{P}
, i.e.

T_{0}=\{\underline{\underline{A}}\in T\mid\underline{\underline{A}}\text{ compatible with }\underline{P}\}=\{\underline{\underline{A}}\in S\mid\underline{\underline{A}}\text{ compatible with }\underline{Z}^{1},\underline{Z}^{2},\ldots,\underline{Z}^{r},\underline{P}\}.

Observe that
\underline{\underline{P}}\in T_{0}
so
T_{0}
is not empty. We define the next poset map:

\displaystyle g:T_{0}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow T_{0}

\displaystyle\ \underline{\underline{A}}

\displaystyle\longmapsto\underline{\underline{A}}\vee\underline{\underline{P}}

is well defined by definition of
T_{0}
. For any
\underline{\underline{A}}
in
T_{0}
, we have
g(\underline{\underline{A}})\geq\underline{\underline{A}}
and

so by Lemma

,
|T_{0}|
is (connically) contractible.

Now, we want to see that

. Let

and for any

let

T_{k}
be the subposet of
T
consisting of those
\underline{\underline{A}}
in
T
that cross
\underline{P}
at most
k

. We divide the proof in the following steps.

Step 1

Let

We claim first that if

lie in the same connected component of the geometric realization of

, then
\mathcal{I}(\underline{\underline{A}})=\mathcal{I}(\underline{\underline{B}})
. Without loss of generality, we can suppose that
\underline{\underline{A}}\leq\underline{\underline{B}}
, because in other case we replace one of them by the first vertex in a path connecting them and argue by induction on distance. So
\underline{\underline{A}}
is obtained from
\underline{\underline{B}}
by coalescing petals and, by definition of
M_{k}
, we can not decrease the number of petals that cross
\underline{P}
, i.e.
\mathrm{cr}(\underline{\underline{A}},\underline{P})=\mathrm{cr}(\underline{\underline{B}},\underline{P})
. Then Lemma
5.17
implies
\mathcal{I}(\underline{\underline{A}})=\mathcal{I}(\underline{\underline{B}})
. For such a connected component let

denote this set of vertices of

.

For
\underline{\underline{A}}\in M_{k}

\underline{\underline{A}}^{\prime}

\underline{\underline{A}}

\displaystyle R_{k}:T_{k}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow T_{k}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\underline{\underline{A}}

\displaystyle\longmapsto\begin{cases}\underline{\underline{A}}^{\prime}\text{ if }\underline{\underline{A}}\in M_{k},\\
\underline{\underline{A}}\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}

.

. If

Step 2

R

8.1.
Cohomological dimension

In this subsection we prove Theorem D. We will use the following result.

Proposition 8.1
.

Let
G
be a group acting over an acyclic CW complex
X
, then

\mathrm{cd}G\leq\mathrm{sup}\{\mathrm{cd}\ G_{\sigma}+\mathrm{dim}\sigma\ |\ \sigma\textup{ a cell in }X\}

where
G_{\sigma}
is the stabilizer in
G
of the cell
\sigma
.

This is a generalization of Proposition 2.4(c) in Chapter VIII of
[
4
]
(this generalization appears as an exercise), and a proof can be found in
[
22
]
. As
\mathrm{MM}_{\Gamma}
is contractible, it is acyclic, and to compute the cohomological dimension of the cell stabilizers of the action of
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
we will use the notion of rank.

Definition 8.2
.

Let
\underline{\underline{A}}
be a vertex type. The
rank
of
\underline{\underline{A}}
is

r(\underline{\underline{A}})=\sum_{a\in V_{\Gamma}}\left(l(\underline{A}_{a})-1\right)

note that this rank definition is equivalent to the rank of
\underline{\underline{A}}
as an element of the poset
\mathrm{Wh}_{\Gamma}
. (Recall that
l(\underline{A}_{a})
is the number of petals of the based partition
\underline{A}_{a}
).

Lemma 8.3
.

The stabilizer under the action of
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
of a vertex
v=[X,\underline{\underline{A}}]\in\mathrm{MM}_{\Gamma}
is free abelian of rank
r(\underline{\underline{A}})
.

Proof.

We may assume that
v
lies in the star of the nuclear vertex
[V_{\Gamma},\mathcal{O}]
because this star is a fundamental domain for the action. In other words, we may assume
X=V_{\Gamma}
. The fact that the stabilizer is free abelian follows from Lemma
5.3
. The proof of that result implies that the rank of this free abelian group is precisely the sum of ranks of the free abelian groups generated by the images in
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
of the partial conjugations carried by each of the based partitions of
\underline{\underline{A}}
. Let
\underline{A}_{a}
be one of those based partitions. The subgroup of
\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
generated by partial conjugations carried by
\underline{A}_{a}
has rank
l(\underline{A}_{a})
. The product of the partial conjugations based on the petals of
\underline{A}_{a}
is an inner automorphism so when we project to the outer automorphisms group we get rank
l(\underline{A}_{a})-1
. Summing up yields
r(\underline{\underline{A}})
.

∎

Proof of Theorem C.
Let
\sigma
be a cell of
\mathrm{MM}_{\Gamma}
. Then
\sigma
is of the form

\sigma:v_{0}<v_{1}<\ldots<v_{m}

where

and the

’s are vertices of
\mathrm{MM}_{\Gamma}
with
\underline{\underline{A}}^{0}<\underline{\underline{A}}^{1}<\ldots<\underline{\underline{A}}^{m}
.
Again, to compute the cohomological dimension of the stabilizer of

we may assume that
\sigma
lies in the star of the nuclear vertex

, so

. The stabilizer

Theorem 8.4

[

Let
G
be a group of finite type admitting a cocompact action on a contractible complex

We can now proceed with the proof of Theorem D:

Proof of Theorem D.
Let
G=\Sigma\mathrm{POut}({A_{\Gamma}})
,
X=\mathrm{MM}_{\Gamma}
and

. We have

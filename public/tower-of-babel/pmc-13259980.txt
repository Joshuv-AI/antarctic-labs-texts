Implementation of the Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency Infectious Disease ALERT: Timely Information Sharing to Strengthen Frontline Healthcare Response
Authors: Hayun Kim, Yeo-Won Jin, Jacob Lee, Sang-Won Lee, Hyosoon Yoo, 하윤 김, 여원 진, 재갑 이, 상원 이, 효순 유
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesThis article introduced the background and operational status of the Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency (KDCA) “Infectious Disease ALERT” and highlighted its value as an information delivery and communication channel for healthcare professionals in infectious disease response.MethodsThe Infectious Disease ALERT issued between June 2024 and December 2025 was reviewed to assess the alert levels posited (Levels 1–3) and the status of the targeted infectious diseases.ResultsA total of seven alerts were presented during the review period (five alerts for Level 1, and two alerts for Level 2). These alerts covered diseases including coronavirus disease 2019, mpox, Oropouche fever, Marburg virus disease, human immunodeficiency virus/acquired immune deficiency syndrome, Ebola virus disease, and severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome. In addition, a mobile-based social-networking service (specifically, KakaoTalk channel) was used to enhance the accessibility of information for healthcare professionals.ConclusionsThe Infectious Disease ALERT is intended to be used as a delivery channel for professional information provided to healthcare providers, supporting infectious disease response in situations including emerging infectious diseases and pandemics.Keywords: Infectious Disease News, Infectious Disease ALERT, Healthcare professionals

Key messages

① What is known previously?

The Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency has been publishing the “Infectious Disease E-Newsletter” since 2016 to provide infectious disease information to frontline healthcare professionals.

② What new information is presented?

In June 2024, the Infectious Disease E-Newsletter was reorganized and retitled the “Infectious Disease News,” and the “Infectious Disease ALERT” was introduced. A total of seven alerts were issued between August 2024 and December 2025.

③ What are implications?

The content of the Infectious Disease ALERT includes information on global outbreaks and recent domestic introductions and transmission trends, as well as on key policy updates and revised the guidelines relevant for clinical practice and preparedness. In addition to website postings and email distribution to subscribers, a mobile-based social-networking service (KakaoTalk channel) was recently adopted to disseminate alerts directly to subscribers’ smartphones immediately upon their release. These improvements enhanced the value of information by providing tailored content to healthcare professionals and enabled more effective communication, supporting preparedness and response to infectious diseases, including pandemics.

Introduction

Following the 2015 Middle East Respiratory Syndrome outbreak in the Republic of Korea (ROK), the need to share infectious disease information with frontline physicians responsible for diagnosing and treating patients with infectious diseases was raised [1]. The Korea Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, the predecessor of the current Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency (KDCA), began publishing the “Infectious Disease E-Newsletter” in 2016 to rapidly share updates on emerging or re-emerging infectious diseases occurring abroad. The Infectious Disease E-Newsletter included major global infectious disease trends, such as the 2016 Zika virus disease outbreak, the 2019 Ebola virus disease outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, the 2020 coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) pandemic, and the 2022 mpox outbreak [2], as well as reference information for clinical practice in healthcare settings, summaries of recent articles related to infectious diseases, and infectious disease-related quizzes. The Infectious Disease E-Newsletter was published biweekly and, upon publication, was distributed to members of the Korean Medical Association via email and text message in cooperation with the Association. However, regular publication alone had limitations in delivering information to frontline healthcare professionals in a timely manner when infectious disease risks arose. In addition, the distribution of email and text messages was associated with limited efficiency in delivering information to users because it was not possible to confirm whether recipients actually viewed the information.

Accordingly, in June 2024, the KDCA reorganized the existing Infectious Disease E-Newsletter into the “Infectious Disease News” and the KDCA “Infectious Disease ALERT.” The Infectious Disease News began to be published regularly on a monthly basis, while the Infectious Disease ALERT was introduced to immediately disseminate selected actionable information requiring urgent understanding and application in healthcare settings.

Results

1. Definition and Purpose of the Infectious Disease ALERT

The Infectious Disease ALERT is a publication issued on an as-needed basis to deliver the latest information needed by frontline healthcare professionals in situations requiring close monitoring in the ROK, such as the introduction or spread of infectious diseases from abroad. The purpose of the Infectious Disease ALERT is to support healthcare professionals in the ROK in rapidly recognizing infectious disease trends and responding appropriately in healthcare settings. Accordingly, it provides information on the occurrence trends of infectious diseases abroad, as well as reporting and diagnostic criteria, government guidelines, and preventive measures to be followed during the diagnosis and treatment of patients with such infectious diseases. Thus, it enables healthcare professionals to understand the relevant information and prepare for and respond to infectious diseases that are rare or have not previously occurred in the ROK.

2. Structure of Alert Levels

The Infectious Disease ALERT is classified into Levels 1 (health update), 2 (health advisory), and 3 (health alert) according to the nature of the information. This classification system was developed based on a review of and reference to the operational framework of the Health Alert Network of the U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) [3]. The KDCA determines the selection of content and the classification of levels in consideration of the situation in the ROK. Level 1 mainly refers to reference-level information, such as the latest outbreak trends, changes in clinical and diagnostic guidelines, and vaccination guidance, while Level 2 refers to advisory-level information requiring caution for healthcare professionals regarding specific diseases. Level 3 refers to critical information requiring immediate attention and response. This three-level structure is intended to help recipients quickly recognize the urgency of the information and perform necessary activities, including clinical and public health actions, in a timely manner (Table 1).

3. Publication Process of the Infectious Disease ALERT

The Infectious Disease ALERT is published based on comprehensive consideration of the status of infectious disease occurrence, overseas outbreak trends, alert levels of international organizations such as the World Health Organization (WHO) and the CDC, and reviews by relevant divisions within the KDCA. The process for determining the Alert level primarily considers the characteristics of the disease, domestic and international spread trends, and the level of caution required in healthcare settings. To reflect input from healthcare settings, the KDCA publishes the Infectious Disease ALERT after review by the Editorial Board for Infectious Disease News, which comprises external experts, including infectious disease specialists.

4. Publication Status of the Infectious Disease ALERT

Since its first publication in August 2024, a total of seven Infectious Disease ALERT issues covering seven infectious diseases have been published as of December 31, 2025. Among the published Infectious Disease ALERT issues, five were Level 1, and two were Level 2, but no Level 3 alerts issued. The main contents of each published issue are as follows.

On August 23, 2024, the first Infectious Disease ALERT was published with three alerts issued simultaneously. A Level 2 Infectious Disease ALERT was issued regarding stabilization of the supply of COVID-19 therapeutics in the ROK and compliance with prescription criteria for high-risk groups. At that time, the increase in the number of hospitalized patients in summer had passed its peak, and the key contents included priority prescription of therapeutics for high-risk groups, strengthened management of vulnerable facilities, and guidance on the vaccination plan scheduled for October. As the WHO re-declared a public health emergency of international concern following the spread of a new mpox clade (Clade Ib) in Africa, a Level 2 Infectious Disease ALERT was issued regarding the reporting of suspected cases and precautions for specimen collection. As more than 8,000 cases of Oropouche fever were reported in five countries in the Americas (Brazil, Bolivia, Peru, Colombia, and Cuba), a Level 1 Infectious Disease ALERT was issued and included information for healthcare institutions on differential diagnosis, procedures for requesting diagnostic tests, and guidance on therapeutics when suspected cases presented to healthcare institutions. On October 18, 2024, the second Infectious Disease ALERT was published with two alerts issued. In response to the increase in Marburg virus disease cases in Rwanda and following the designation of Rwanda as a quarantine management area, a Level 1 Infectious Disease ALERT was issued. It included the distribution of guidance on Marburg virus disease to healthcare institutions and the provision of information on travelers entering from quarantine management areas to healthcare professionals through the Drug Utilization Review and International Traveler Information System. In response to the recent sharp increase in new human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) infections in the Philippines, a Level 1 Infectious Disease ALERT was issued, which encouraged the active use of the “Guidelines for medical institutions for the care of people living with HIV” during the clinical care and management of people with HIV.

In February 2025, in response to an outbreak of Sudan Ebola virus disease in Uganda, a Level 1 Infectious Disease ALERT was issued, focusing on domestic preparedness measures, including precautions during the clinical care of suspected cases and guidance on reporting.

Most recently, in July 2025, following reports of secondary infections within healthcare institutions during the care of severely ill patients with severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome in the ROK, a Level 1 Infectious Disease ALERT was issued, urging the use of personal protective equipment and strengthened contact management (Table 2) [4].

5. Target Recipients and Dissemination Methods for the Infectious Disease ALERT

In the context of the COVID-19 pandemic, the importance of cooperation not only with frontline physicians but also with nurses, pharmacists, public health officials, and other healthcare professionals was emphasized. To this end, the dissemination scope has gradually been expanded to include various occupational groups in the healthcare sector who are likely to have face-to-face or contact with patients with infectious diseases. Before the reorganization, until 2023, the Infectious Disease E-Newsletter targeted only physicians. After the reorganization into the Infectious Disease News and the Infectious Disease ALERT in 2024, the target recipients have gradually expanded to include nurses (2024), pharmacists (2025), and public health officials (2026).

The Infectious Disease News, which is published regularly every month, and the Infectious Disease ALERT, which is issued on an as-needed basis, are, in principle, publicly available. The publications are made available through the KDCA website (https://dportal.kdca.go.kr/pot/bbs/BD_selectBbsList.do?q_bbsSn=1008), allowing access by the general public. In addition, as part of the 2024 reorganization, a mobile-based SNS channel (the dedicated KakaoTalk channel “KDCA Infectious Disease News,” http://pf.kakao.com/_wXVxmG) was introduced to improve accessibility for frontline healthcare professionals. Accordingly, by subscribing to this channel, the Infectious Disease ALERT or the Infectious Disease News can be accessed on mobile devices immediately upon publication. The existing email distribution method has also been maintained to diversify methods of receiving information according to user preferences. As of March 2026, email distribution is currently being conducted for the members of the Korean Society of Infectious Diseases in cooperation with the society. Going forward, the dissemination method will be further modified by taking into account the feedback from relevant academic societies and associations, as well as users.

Conclusion

The significance of introducing the Infectious Disease ALERT can be summarized in three main points. First, it has created an environment that can strengthen the national-level early warning function for infectious diseases [5]. By presenting risk levels when urgent issues arise, it enables rapid and intuitive sharing of changes in outbreak trends and provides a basis for proactive responses by the government and healthcare settings. Second, through the standardization of risk communication [6], it has established a communication line through which the government and healthcare settings can share a common understanding of risk. This line can contribute to reducing confusion in interpreting information through a consistent communication system during infectious disease response. Third, by providing “action guidelines,” such as preventive measures and recommendations according to risk level, the Infectious Disease ALERT has established a structure that supports frontline healthcare professionals’ response in the field and rapid decision-making, while also promoting the implementation of actions to prevent secondary infections within healthcare institutions.

In this context, the Infectious Disease ALERT differs from the response system by crisis alert level [7,8], which is operated under the “Standard manual for infectious disease crisis management” and responds through government-wide administrative measures based on the “Framework Act on the Management of Disasters and Safety” and the “Basic Guidelines for National Crisis Management (Presidential Directive)” in the event of an infectious disease disaster. It is also distinct from infectious disease risk assessment [9,10], which comprehensively evaluates risks to support decision-making on preparedness for the domestic introduction of infectious diseases from abroad and public health control measures when a specific infectious disease is considered a signal that could threaten public health in the ROK. The KDCA regularly conducts risk assessments for selected infectious diseases through quarterly consultations among relevant divisions and plans to expand the provision of useful information, including major findings and changes, through the Infectious Disease ALERT.

The KDCA Infectious Disease News aims to continuously maintain a foundation that enables healthcare professionals to accurately understand infectious disease risks and respond proactively. Accordingly, the Infectious Disease ALERT will be actively utilized so that it can play an important role as a means of communication with healthcare professionals and of early response in situations involving the emergence of novel infectious diseases or the international spread of infectious diseases. This article aimed to explore the implementation and current status of the Infectious Disease ALERT within the Infectious Disease News and to examine its significance. To further advance this system in the future, additional evaluation studies are needed to elucidate the effects of the system on shortening the response timing of healthcare professionals during infectious disease events, cases of information utilization from the publications, and changes in the perceptions and behaviors of healthcare professionals.

본 론

1. 감염병 ALERT의 정의 및 목적

감염병 ALERT는 해외 감염병의 유입 및 확산 등 모니터링 상황 중, 일선 보건의료인에게 유용한 최신 정보를 전달하는 자료이다. 감염병 ALERT는 국내의 보건의료인이 보건의료 현장에서 감염병 동향을 신속히 인지하여 적절히 대응할 수 있도록 지원하는 데 목적이 있다. 따라서 해외 감염병의 발생 동향뿐만 아니라 감염병 환자 진료∙치료 시 신고 및 진단 기준, 정부 지침, 예방 수칙 등을 함께 안내함으로써 국내에서 발생이 드물거나 발생한 적이 없는 감염병에 대해서도 보건의료인이 내용을 인지하고 대비∙대응할 수 있도록 하고 있다.

2. Level의 구성

감염병 ALERT는 정보의 특성에 따라 Level 1 (health update), Level 2 (health advisory), Level 3 (health alert)로 구분한다. 이 분류체계는 미국 질병통제센터(U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, CDC)의 보건경보네트워크(Health Alert Network) 운영체계를 검토하고 참고한 것으로, 내용의 선정과 단계 구분은 국내 상황을 고려하여 질병관리청에서 결정한다[3]. Level 1은 주로 최신 유행 동향, 진료 및 검사 지침의 변경, 예방접종 관련 안내 등 참고 수준의 정보를 의미하며, Level 2는 특정 질환에 대해 보건의료인에게 주의가 필요한 권고 수준의 정보를 의미한다. Level 3는 즉각적인 주의와 대응이 필요한 긴요한 정보를 의미한다. 이러한 3단계 구조는 수신자가 정보의 긴요도를 신속히 인지하여 임상 및 방역 조치 등 필요한 활동을 적시에 수행할 수 있도록 돕는 것을 목표로 한다(표 1).

3. 감염병 ALERT 발간 방식

감염병 ALERT 발간은 감염병 발생 현황, 해외 유행 동향, 국제기구(World Health Organization [WHO], CDC 등)의 경보 수준, 질병관리청 내 관계 부서 검토 등을 종합적으로 고려하여 이루어진다. ALERT level의 결정 과정은 질병의 특성과 국내외 확산 추세, 보건의료 현장에서의 주의 필요성을 중심으로 판단하며, 보건의료 현장의 의견을 반영하기 위해 감염내과 전문의 등 외부 전문가로 구성된 감염병 뉴스 편집위원회를 거쳐 질병관리청이 발간한다.

4. 감염병 ALERT 발간 현황

2024년 8월 첫 발간 이후 2025년 12월 31일까지 총 7개 감염병에 대해 총 7회의 감염병 ALERT가 발간되었다. 발간된 감염병 ALERT의 Level은 Level 1이 5건, Level 2가 2건이었고 Level 3는 없었다. 각 발간 회차별 주요 내용은 다음과 같다.

2024년 8월 23일 첫 번째 감염병 ALERT는 3건을 동시 발간하였다. 국내 코로나19 치료제 수급 안정화 및 고위험군 처방기준 준수에 대하여 Level 2 수준의 감염병 ALERT를 제공하였다. 당시 여름철 입원환자 증가세가 정점을 지난 시점으로, 고위험군 대상 치료제 우선 처방, 감염취약시설 관리 강화, 10월 예방접종 계획 안내 등이 주요 내용이었다. 이와 더불어, 아프리카 지역에서 새로운 유전형(Clade Ib)의 엠폭스 확산으로 WHO가 국제공중보건위기상황(public health emergency of international concern)을 재선언함에 따라 의심환자 신고 및 검체 채취 주의사항에 대하여 Level 2 수준의 감염병 ALERT를 제공하였다. 그리고 미주지역 5개국(브라질, 볼리비아, 페루, 콜롬비아, 쿠바)에서 오로퓨스열(Oropouche fever)이 8천여 명 이상 발생한 것으로 보고됨에 따라 Level 1 수준의 감염병 ALERT를 제공하고 의료기관에 의심환자 내원 시 감별 진단, 검사의뢰 절차, 치료제 안내 등의 내용을 포함하였다.

2024년 10월 18일 두 번째 감염병 ALERT는 2건을 발간하였다. 르완다에서 마버그열(Marburg virus disease) 환자 증가에 대응하여 검역관리지역으로 지정됨에 따라 Level 1 수준의 감염병 ALERT를 제공하고 의료기관 대상 마버그열 안내서 배포, 보건의료인 대상 검역관리지역 입국자에 대한 의약품안전사용서비스∙해외여행력 정보제공시스템(Drug Utilization Review [DUR]∙International Traveler Information System [ITS]) 제공 등의 내용을 포함하였다. 또한 최근 필리핀에서 인체면역결핍바이러스(human immunodeficiency virus, HIV) 신규 감염이 급증함에 따라 Level 1 수준의 감염병 ALERT를 제공하고 HIV 감염인 진료 및 관리 시 「HIV 감염인 진료를 위한 의료기관 길라잡이」를 적극 활용하도록 당부하였다.

2025년 2월에는 우간다에서 발생한 수단형 에볼라바이러스병에 대응하여 의심환자 진료 중 주의사항 및 신고 안내 등 국내 대비 조치를 중심으로 한 Level 1 수준의 감염병 ALERT를 제공하였다.

가장 최근인 2025년 7월 국내에서 중증열성혈소판감소증후군(severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome, SFTS) 중증환자 진료 중 의료기관 내 2차 감염 사례가 보고됨에 따라 개인보호구 착용과 접촉자 관리 강화를 당부하는 Level 1 수준의 감염병 ALERT를 발간하였다(표 2) [4].

5. 감염병 ALERT 배포의 대상 및 방식

코로나19 팬데믹 상황에서 일선의 의사는 물론 간호사를 비롯한 약사, 보건의료공무원 등과 협력의 중요성이 강조되었다. 이를 위해 감염병 환자의 대면 및 접촉 가능성이 높은 보건의료 분야의 다양한 직군을 대상으로 배포 범위를 점차 확대해 가고 있다. 개편 전인 2023년까지 감염병 뉴스레터는 의사만을 배포 대상으로 하였으나, 2024년 감염병 뉴스와 감염병 ALERT 개편 이후 간호사(2024년), 약사(2025년), 보건의료공무원(2026년)까지 점차 배포 대상을 확대해 가고 있다.

매월 정기적으로 발간하는 감염병 뉴스와 수시로 발간하는 감염병 ALERT는 대국민 공개를 원칙으로 한다. 발간은 질병관리청 누리집(https://dportal.kdca.go.kr/pot/bbs/BD_selectBbsList.do?q_bbsSn=1008)을 통해 제공하고 있어 국민 누구나 접근이 가능하다. 또한 2024년 개편 시, 일선 보건의료인의 접근성을 높이기 위해 모바일 기반의 소셜 네트워크 서비스(카카오톡 전용채널 ‘질병관리청 감염병 뉴스,’ https://pf.kakao.com/_wXVxmG)를 도입하였다. 즉 해당 채널 구독 시 감염병 ALERT나 감염병 뉴스를 발간 즉시 모바일을 통해 확인할 수 있다. 또한 사용자의 선호를 고려하여 정보 수신 방식을 다양화하고자 기존의 이메일 발송 방식도 유지하고 있다. 2026년 3월 기준, 현재 대한감염학회 협력을 통해 학회원 대상의 이메일 발송 서비스를 실시하고 있으며 향후 유관 학∙협회와 사용자 의견을 반영하여 배포 방식을 조정해 나갈 계획이다.

결 론

감염병 ALERT 도입 의의는 크게 세 가지로 정리할 수 있다. 첫째, 국가 차원의 감염병 조기경보 기능을 강화할 수 있는 정보 환경을 조성하였다[5]. 긴급 현안 발생 시 위험 수준을 제시함으로써 발생 추세의 변화를 직관적으로 신속히 공유할 수 있으며, 정부와 의료 현장이 선제적으로 대응할 수 있는 기반을 마련하였다. 둘째, 위험소통의 표준화를 통해 정부와 의료 현장이 동일한 위험 인식을 공유할 수 있는 소통라인을 구축하였다[6]. 이는 감염병 대응 과정에서 일관된 의사소통 체계를 통해 정보 해석의 혼선을 줄이는 데 기여할 수 있다. 셋째, 위험 수준에 따른 예방 수칙, 권고사항 등의 ‘행동 지침’을 제공함으로써 일선 보건의료인의 현장 대응과 신속한 의사결정 지원뿐만 아니라 의료기관 내 2차 감염 예방을 위한 행동 실천을 촉진하는 구조를 마련하였다.

이러한 점에서 감염병 ALERT는 감염병 재난 발생 시 「재난 및 안전관리 기본법」, 「국가 위기관리 기본지침(대통령 훈령)」을 근거로 운용되는 「감염병 위기관리 표준매뉴얼」에 따라 범정부적 행정적 조치를 수반하여 대응하는 ‘위기경보 단계별 대응체계’와는 다르다[7,8]. 또한 특정 감염병이 국내 공중보건을 위협할 수 있는 신호로 판단될 시 해외 감염병의 국내 유입 대비와 방역 대책의 의사결정을 지원하기 위해 위험도를 종합적으로 평가하는 ‘감염병 위험평가’와도 구별된다[9,10]. 질병관리청은 분기별로 유관부서 간 협의를 통해 일부 감염병에 대한 위험평가를 정례적으로 시행하고 있으며, 주요 결과∙변경 사항 등 유용한 정보는 감염병 ALERT를 통해 확대하여 제공할 계획이다.

질병관리청 감염병 뉴스는 보건의료인이 감염병 위험을 정확히 이해하고, 선제적으로 대응할 수 있는 기반을 지속적으로 유지하고자 한다. 이에 신종 감염병 출현이나 감염병의 국제적 확산 상황에서 보건의료인과의 소통 및 조기 대응의 수단으로 중요한 역할을 할 수 있도록 감염병 ALERT를 적극 활용할 것이다. 본 원고는 감염병 뉴스의 감염병 ALERT 도입과 현황을 소개하고 의의를 파악하는 데 목적이 있으며, 향후 해당 체계의 고도화를 위해서는 감염병 발생 시 보건의료인의 대응 시점 단축, 발간물의 정보 활용 사례, 보건의료인의 인식 및 행동 변화 등에 미친 영향에 대해서 추가적인 평가 연구가 필요하다.

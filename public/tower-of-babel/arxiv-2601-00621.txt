Some lemmas on spectral radius of graphs: including an application
Wenqian Zhang

Abstract
For a graph G G , the spectral radius ρ ⁡ ( G ) \rho(G) of G G is the largest eigenvalue of its adjacency matrix. In this paper, we give three lammas on ρ ⁡ ( G ) \rho(G) when G G contains a spanning complete bipartite graph. Using these lemmas and typical spectral method, we characterized the unique extremal graph with the maximum spectral radius among all planar graphs of large order n n without a cycle of length ℓ \ell , where 5 ≤ ℓ ≤ n 5\leq\ell\leq n . Keywords: spectral radius; planar graph; cycle; walk. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 05C50; 05C35.

Introduction

All graphs considered in this paper are finite, undirected and simple.
For a graph
G
, let
\overline{G}
be its complement. The vertex set and edge set of
G
are denoted by
V(G)
and
E(G)
, respectively. For two vertices
u,v
, let
d_{G}(u,v)
denote the distance between them (i.e., the minimum length of a path connecting them). We say
u\sim v
, if
u
and
v
are adjacent in
G
. For a certain integer
n
, let
K_{n},P_{n}
and
C_{n}
be the complete graph, the path and the cycle of order
n
, respectively. For any
\ell\geq 2
graphs
G_{1},G_{2},...,G_{\ell}
, let
\cup_{1\leq i\leq\ell}G_{i}
be the disjoint union of them. In particular, let
tG=\cup_{1\leq i\leq t}G
for any integer
t\geq 1
. Let
G_{1}\vee G_{2}
be the join of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
, obtained from
G_{1}\cup G_{2}
by connecting all vertices of
G_{1}
to all vertices of
G_{2}
. For any terminology used but not defined here, one may refer to
[
2
]
.

Let
G
be a graph with vertices
v_{1},v_{2},...,v_{n}
. The
adjacency matrix

A(G)
of
G
is an
n\times n
matrix
(a_{ij})
, where
a_{ij}=1
if
v_{i}\sim v_{j}
, and
a_{ij}=0
otherwise. The
spectral radius

\rho(G)
of
G
is the largest eigenvalue of its adjacency matrix. By the well-known Perron–Frobenius theorem,
\rho(G)
has a non-negative eigenvector. A non-negative eigenvector corresponding to
\rho(G)
is called a
Perron vector
of
G
. If
G
is connected, any Perron vector of
G
has positive entries. The spectral radius of a graph is closely related to its convergence property. In this paper, we first give the following three lammas on spectral radius of graphs. (Let
P_{0}
denote the empty graph.)

Lemma 1.1

For integers
n_{1}\geq n_{2}+2\geq 3
, let

G_{1}=H\vee(P_{n_{1}}\cup P_{n_{2}}\cup T)

and

G_{2}=H\vee(P_{n_{1}-1}\cup P_{n_{2}+1}\cup T),

where
H,T
are two graphs with
|H|\geq 1
and
|T|\geq 0
. Then
\rho(G_{1})>\rho(G_{2})
.

Lemma 1.2

For integers
n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq n_{3}\geq n_{4}\geq 1
and
n_{1}\geq n_{2}+2
, let

G_{1}=H\vee(P_{n_{1}}\cup P_{n_{2}}\cup P_{n_{3}}\cup P_{n_{4}}\cup T)

and

G_{2}=H\vee(P_{n_{1}-1}\cup P_{n_{2}+1}\cup P_{n_{3}+1}\cup P_{n_{4}-1}\cup T),

where
H,T
are two graphs with
|H|\geq 1
and
|T|+\sum_{1\leq s\leq 4}n_{s}\geq 130
. Then
\rho(G_{1})<\rho(G_{2})
.

Lemma 1.3

For integers
n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq n_{3}\geq n_{4}\geq n_{5}\geq 1
and
n_{1}\geq n_{2}+3
, let

G_{1}=H\vee(P_{n_{1}}\cup P_{n_{2}}\cup P_{n_{3}}\cup P_{n_{4}}\cup P_{n_{5}}\cup T)

and

G_{2}=H\vee(P_{n_{1}-2}\cup P_{n_{2}+1}\cup P_{n_{3}+1}\cup P_{n_{4}+1}\cup P_{n_{5}-1}\cup T),

where
H,T
are two graphs with
|H|\geq 1
and
|T|+\sum_{1\leq s\leq 5}n_{s}\geq 310
. Then
\rho(G_{1})<\rho(G_{2})
.

A graph is called planar, if it can be drawn in the plane without crossed edges. Tait and Tobin
[
6
]
proved that
K_{2}\vee P_{n-2}
is the only extremal graph with the maximum spectral radius among all planar graphs of large order
n
. This (asymptotically) solves a conjecture proposed by Boots and Royle
[
1
]
, and
independently by Cao and Vince
[
3
]
. Recently, the researchers are also interested in determining the extremal graphs with the maximum spectral radius among all
C_{\ell}
-free planar graphs of large order
n
, where
3\leq\ell\leq n
. The case
\ell=4
can be settled from the results of Nikiforov
[
5
]
, and Zhai and Wang
[
8
]
. Very recently, Fang, Lin and Shi
[
4
]
solved the case when
\ell=3
and
5\leq\ell\ll n
. Xu, Lin and Fang
[
7
]
solved the case when
\frac{\ell}{n}
is close to 1. They
[
7
]
proposed the general case as a problem. Armed with Lemma
1.1
and Lemma
1.2
, using typical spectral method we can solve this problem. That is, we have the following Theorem
1.4
.

For a finite family of graphs
\mathcal{G}
, let
{\rm SPEX}(\mathcal{G})
be the set of all extremal graphs with the maximum spectral radius in
\mathcal{G}
.

Theorem 1.4

For integers
n\geq 1.8\times 10^{17}
and
5\leq\ell\leq n
, let
\mathcal{G}_{n,\ell}
be the set of all
C_{\ell}
-free planar graphs of order
n
. Then we have the following conclusions.

(i)
For
5\leq\ell<\frac{2n+5}{3}
,
{\rm SPEX}(\mathcal{G}_{n,\ell})=\left\{K_{2}\vee(P_{\lceil\frac{\ell-3}{2}\rceil}\cup(a+1)P_{\lfloor\frac{\ell-3}{2}\rfloor}\cup P_{b})\right\}
, where
n-\ell+1=a\lfloor\frac{\ell-3}{2}\rfloor+b
with
0\leq b<\lfloor\frac{\ell-3}{2}\rfloor
.

(ii)
For
\frac{2n+5}{3}\leq\ell\leq n
,
{\rm SPEX}(\mathcal{G}_{n,\ell})=\left\{K_{2}\vee(P_{2\ell-n-4}\cup 2P_{n-\ell+1})\right\}
.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we include several lemmas, which will be used later. The proofs of Lemmas

,
1.2
and
1.3
are given in Section 3. In Section 4, we give the proof of Theorem

.

Preliminaries

Let
G
be a graph. For an integer
\ell\geq 1
, a
walk
of length
\ell
in
G
is an ordered sequence of vertices
v_{0}v_{1}\cdots v_{\ell}
, such that
v_{i}\sim v_{i+1}
for any
0\leq i\leq\ell-1
. The vertex
v_{0}
is called the starting vertex of the walk. For any
u\in V(G)
and
\ell\geq 1
, let
w^{\ell}_{G}(u)
be the number of walks of length
\ell
starting at
u
in
G
. Let
W^{\ell}(G)=\sum_{v\in V(G)}w^{\ell}_{G}(u)
.
The following spectral formula was given in
[
9
]
.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
9
]
)

For an integer
r\geq 2
, let
K_{n_{1},n_{2},...,n_{r}}
be the complete
r
-partite graph of order
n
with parts
V_{1},V_{2},...,V_{r}
, where
n_{i}=|V_{i}|\geq 1
for
1\leq i\leq r
and
\sum_{1\leq i\leq r}n_{i}=n
. For each
1\leq i\leq r
, let
H_{i}
be a graph with vertex set
V(H_{i})\subseteq V_{i}
. Let
G
be the graph obtained from
K_{n_{1},n_{2},...,n_{r}}
by embedding the edges of
H_{i}
into
V_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq r
. Set
\rho=\rho(G)
. Then

\sum_{1\leq s\leq r}\frac{1}{1+\frac{n_{s}}{\rho}+\sum^{\infty}_{i=1}\frac{W^{i}(H_{s})}{\rho^{i+1}}}=r-1.

The existence of the infinite series in Theorem
2.1
was guaranteed by the following two results. The first one is taken from Theorem 8.1.1 of
[
2
]
.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
2
]
)

If
H
is a subgraph of a connected graph
G
, then
\rho(H)\leq\rho(G)
, with equality if and only if
H=G
.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
9
]
)

Let
G
be a graph of order
n
. Then
\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\frac{W^{k}(G)}{x^{k}}
exists for any
x>\rho(G)
.

Lemma 3.1

For integers

For any given

W^{\ell}(G_{1})-W^{\ell}(G_{2})=W^{\ell}_{u_{1},u_{n_{2}+2}}(P_{n_{1}}).

Recall that
P_{n_{1}}=u_{1}u_{2}\cdots u_{n_{1}}
in

. In

.
This means that

W^{\ell}(G_{1})-W^{\ell}(G_{2})=|X|-|Y|.

Now we divide

in two parts: let

denote the set of walks

By Claim 1, we see

\sum_{1\leq i\leq\ell-1}w^{a}_{uv}(z_{i})\leq\sum_{1\leq i\leq\ell-1}w^{a}_{uv}(u_{i})+\sum_{1\leq i\leq\ell-1}w^{a}_{uv}(v_{i})

for any

. Clearly,
w^{a}_{uv}(u_{i})=w^{a}_{uv}(v_{i})=0
for any
i\geq\ell+1
and
a\leq 2\ell
.
Thus, for any
a\leq 2\ell
,

\displaystyle W^{a}_{u,v}(P_{\infty})

\displaystyle=w^{a}_{uv}(u)+w^{a}_{uv}(v)+\sum_{1\leq i\leq\ell-1}w^{a}_{uv}(z_{i})+\sum_{1\leq i\leq\ell}w^{a}_{uv}(u_{i})+\sum_{1\leq i\leq\ell}w^{a}_{uv}(v_{i})

\displaystyle\leq 2(w^{a}_{uv}(u)+w^{a}_{uv}(v)+\sum_{1\leq i\leq\ell}w^{a}_{uv}(u_{i})+\sum_{1\leq i\leq\ell}w^{a}_{uv}(v_{i})).

By symmetry,
w^{a}_{uv}(u)=w^{a}_{uv}(v)
and
w^{a}_{uv}(u_{i})=w^{a}_{uv}(v_{i})
for any
a\geq 1
and
i\geq 1
. Thus, for any
a\leq 2\ell
,

W^{a}_{u,v}(P_{\infty})\leq 4(w^{a}_{uv}(u)+\sum_{1\leq i\leq\ell}w^{a}_{uv}(u_{i})).

Since
P_{\infty}
is a path, each walk starting at
u_{i}
corresponds to a ”
\pm
”-series:

u_{i}+--+-+\cdots.

(For example,
u_{1}+-++--
corresponds to the walk
u_{1}uu_{1}uz_{1}uu_{1}
.) For any
0\leq a\leq\ell
and
1\leq i\leq a
, in the corresponding ”
\pm
”-series to the walks of length
\ell+a
starting at
u_{i}
and crossing
u
and

, there are at most
\lfloor\frac{a-i}{2}\rfloor
symbols ”
-
” (in the all
\ell+a
symbols ”
\pm
”).
Thus, using Fact 1 as
\ell+a\geq 4\lfloor\frac{a-i}{2}\rfloor
,

w^{\ell+a}_{uv}(u_{i})\leq\binom{\ell+a}{0}+\binom{\ell+a}{1}+\cdots+\binom{\ell+a}{\lfloor\frac{a-i}{2}\rfloor}\leq\frac{5}{4}\frac{(\ell+a)^{\lfloor\frac{a-i}{2}\rfloor}}{\lfloor\frac{a-i}{2}\rfloor!}.

Hence,
w^{\ell+a}_{uv}(u_{i})\leq\frac{5}{4}\frac{(2\ell)^{\lfloor\frac{a-i}{2}\rfloor}}{\lfloor\frac{a-i}{2}\rfloor!}
for any

. It follows that, for any

,

w^{\ell+a}_{uv}(u)+\sum_{1\leq i\leq\ell}w^{\ell+a}_{uv}(u_{i})\leq 2\times\frac{5}{4}\times(1+2\ell+\frac{(2\ell)^{2}}{2!}+\cdots+\frac{(2\ell)^{\lfloor\frac{a}{2}\rfloor}}{\lfloor\frac{a}{2}\rfloor!})=\frac{5}{2}\sum_{0\leq i\leq\lfloor\frac{a}{2}\rfloor}\frac{(2\ell)^{i}}{i!}.

Then

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq a\leq\ell}\frac{W^{\ell+a}_{u,v}(P_{\infty})}{x^{\ell+a}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{0\leq a\leq\ell}\frac{4}{x^{\ell+a}}(w^{\ell+a}_{uv}(u)+\sum_{1\leq i\leq\ell}w^{\ell+a}_{uv}(u_{i}))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{10}{x^{\ell}}\sum_{0\leq a\leq\ell}\frac{1}{x^{a}}\sum_{0\leq i\leq\lfloor\frac{a}{2}\rfloor}\frac{(2\ell)^{i}}{i!}

\displaystyle=\frac{10}{x^{\ell}}\sum_{0\leq i\leq\lfloor\frac{\ell}{2}\rfloor}\frac{(2\ell)^{i}}{i!}\sum_{2i\leq a\leq\ell}\frac{1}{x^{a}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{10}{x^{\ell}}\sum_{0\leq i\leq\lfloor\frac{\ell}{2}\rfloor}\frac{(2\ell)^{i}}{i!}\frac{1}{x^{2i}}\frac{x}{x-1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{10}{x^{\ell}}\frac{x}{x-1}\sum^{\infty}_{i=0}\frac{1}{i!}(\frac{2\ell}{x^{2}})^{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{10}{x^{\ell}}\frac{x}{x-1}e^{\frac{2\ell}{x^{2}}}.

Note that
W^{s}_{u,v}(G)=0
for any

as
d_{G}(u,v)=\ell
. Then

\sum_{1\leq s\leq 2\ell}\frac{W^{s}_{u,v}(G)}{x^{s}}\leq\sum_{0\leq a\leq\ell}\frac{W^{\ell+a}_{u,v}(P_{\infty})}{x^{\ell+a}}\leq\frac{10}{x^{\ell}}\frac{x}{x-1}e^{\frac{2\ell}{x^{2}}}.

Since
G
has maximum degree 2, we see
W^{a}_{u,v}(G)\leq 2^{a}n
for any
a\geq 1
.
Noting
s-3\leq 2(s-\ell-2)
for
s\geq 2\ell+1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{s\geq 2\ell+1}\frac{W^{s}_{u,v}(G)}{x^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s\geq 2\ell+1}\frac{2^{s}n}{x^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8}{x^{\ell}}\sum_{s\geq 2\ell+1}\frac{2^{s-3}}{x^{s-\ell-2}}~~~(using~x\geq\sqrt{n})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8}{x^{\ell}}\sum_{s\geq 2\ell+1}(\frac{4}{x})^{s-\ell-2}~~~(using~s-3\leq 2(s-\ell-2))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8}{x^{\ell}}\sum_{j\geq 1}(\frac{4}{x})^{j}~~~(using~\ell\geq 2)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{x^{\ell}}\frac{32}{x-4}~~~(using~x\geq 5).

Consequently,

\sum_{s\geq 1}\frac{W^{s}_{u,v}(G)}{x^{s}}\leq\frac{1}{x^{\ell}}(\frac{10x}{x-1}e^{\frac{2\ell}{x^{2}}}+\frac{32}{x-4}).

This completes the proof.
\Box

Proof of Lemma

.

G^{1}=P_{n_{1}}\cup P_{n_{2}}\cup T

G^{2}=P_{n_{1}-1}\cup P_{n_{2}+1}\cup T

By Theorem

\frac{1}{1+\frac{|H|}{\rho_{j}}+\frac{1}{\rho_{j}}\sum^{\infty}_{i=1}\frac{W^{i}(H)}{\rho^{i}_{j}}}+\frac{1}{1+\frac{|G^{j}|}{\rho_{j}}+\frac{1}{\rho_{j}}\sum^{\infty}_{i=1}\frac{W^{i}(G^{j})}{\rho^{i}_{j}}}=1,

where
1\leq j\leq 2

For

f(G_{j},x)=\frac{1}{1+\frac{|H|}{x}+\frac{1}{x}\sum^{\infty}_{i=1}\frac{W^{i}(H)}{x^{i}}}+\frac{1}{1+\frac{|G^{j}|}{x}+\frac{1}{x}\sum^{\infty}_{i=1}\frac{W^{i}(G^{j})}{x^{i}}}.

(Clearly, the domain of

Clearly,

\rho_{j}

.

In
G^{1}
, denote
P_{n_{1}}=u_{1}u_{2}\cdots u_{n_{1}}
. For any
\ell\geq 1
, by Lemma
3.1
,

W^{\ell}(P_{n_{1}}\cup P_{n_{2}})-W^{\ell}(P_{n_{1}-1}\cup P_{n_{2}+1})=W^{\ell}_{u_{1},u_{n_{2}+2}}(P_{n_{1}}).

This means that
W^{\ell}(G^{1})-W^{\ell}(G^{2})=W^{\ell}_{u_{1},u_{n_{2}+2}}(P_{n_{1}})\geq 0
. Clearly,

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s}x_{v_{i}}\left((x_{u^{\prime}}+x_{u^{\prime\prime}})-\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\sim v_{i}\\
w\in\{u^{\prime},u^{\prime\prime}\}\cup R\cup S_{i-1}\end{subarray}}x_{w}\right)\geq\frac{7}{10\rho}.

Then

\displaystyle\rho(G_{2})-\rho(G)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{T}}(A(G_{2})-A(G))\mathbf{x}}{\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{x}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2}{\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{x}}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s}x_{v_{i}}\left((x_{u^{\prime}}+x_{u^{\prime\prime}})-\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\sim v_{i}\\
w\in\{u^{\prime},u^{\prime\prime}\}\cup R\cup S_{i-1}\end{subarray}}x_{w}\right)-x_{u_{2}}x_{u_{3}}-x_{u_{3}}x_{u_{4}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2}{\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{x}}(\frac{7}{10\rho}-\frac{32}{\rho^{2}})

\displaystyle>0~~~(using~\rho\geq\sqrt{2n-4}).

Hence,
\rho(G_{2})>\rho(G)
,
contradicting that
G\in{\rm SPEX}(\mathcal{G}_{n,\ell})
.
Therefore,
S
is empty.
\Box

Claim 6.

u^{\prime}u^{\prime\prime}\in E(G)
.

Proof of Claim 6.
Suppose to the contrary that
u^{\prime}u^{\prime\prime}\notin E(G)
. By Claim 5, we see
V(G)=\left\{u^{\prime},u^{\prime\prime}\right\}\cup R
. Recall that
G[R]
has maximum degree at most 2, as
G
is
K_{3,3}
-free. Note that
G
is
C_{\ell}
-free. Thus,

G[R]
must be a linear forest with at least 3 components (otherwise
G
has a Hamilton cycle).
Then there exists some integer
1\leq i_{0}\leq n-2
such that
u_{i_{0}}u_{i_{0}+1}\notin E(G)
.
This implies that
u^{\prime}u_{i_{0}}u^{\prime\prime}u_{i_{0}+1}u^{\prime}
is a face in the embedding of
G
.

Let
G_{3}
be the graph obtained from
G
by adding the edge
u^{\prime}u^{\prime\prime}
crossing the face
u^{\prime}u_{i_{0}}u^{\prime\prime}u_{i_{0}+1}u^{\prime}
. Clearly,
G_{3}
is planar and
C_{\ell}
-free. But
\rho(G_{3})>\rho(G)
by Lemma
2.2
, contradicting that
G\in{\rm SPEX}(\mathcal{G}_{n,\ell})
. Therefore,
u^{\prime}u^{\prime\prime}\in E(G)
.

From the above discussion, we can see that
G
contains
K_{2}\vee\overline{K_{n-2}}
as a spanning subgraph. This completes the proof.
\Box

The following observation is easy to see.

Observation
(
[
7
]
)
Let
H=\cup_{i=1}^{t}P_{n_{i}}
, where
t\geq 2
and
n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq\cdots\geq n_{t}\geq 1
. Then
K_{2}\vee H
is
C_{\ell}
-free if and only if
n_{1}+n_{2}\leq\ell-3
.

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.4
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.
Let
G
be a graph in
{\rm SPEX}(\mathcal{G}_{n,\ell})
. We will prove that
G
is unique as stated in the theorem.
By Lemma
4.1
,
G
contains
K_{2}\vee\overline{K_{n-2}}
as a spanning subgraph. Let
u^{\prime},u^{\prime\prime}
be the two dominating vertices of
G
. Let
R=V(G)-\left\{u^{\prime},u^{\prime\prime}\right\}
. Since
G
is
K_{3,3}
-free, we see that
G[R]
has maximum degree at most 2. Since
G
is
C_{\ell}
-free,
G[R]
must be a linear forest with
t\geq 3
components. That is,
G[R]=\cup_{i=1}^{t}P_{n_{i}}
, where
t\geq 3,n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq\cdots\geq n_{t}\geq 1
and
n_{1}+n_{2}\leq\ell-3
. Clearly,
G=K_{2}\vee(\cup_{1\leq i\leq t}P_{n_{i}})
and

\sum_{1\leq i\leq t}n_{i}=n-2.

Now we show that
n_{1}+n_{2}=\ell-3
. Otherwise,
n_{1}+n_{2}\leq\ell-4
. Let

G^{*}=K_{2}\vee(P_{n_{1}+1}\cup P_{n_{2}}\cup P_{n_{3}-1}\cup(\cup_{4\leq i\leq t}P_{n_{i}})).

Clearly,
G^{*}
is planar and
C_{\ell}
-free (by the above observation). But
\rho(G^{*})>\rho(G)

by Lemma
1.1
, a contradiction. Hence,

n_{1}+n_{2}=\ell-3.

Now we prove the theorem by two cases depending on the values of
\ell
.

(i)

5\leq\ell<\frac{2n+5}{3}
. Let
a\geq 0,b
be the integers, such that
n-\ell+1=a\lfloor\frac{\ell-3}{2}\rfloor+b
with
0\leq b<\lfloor\frac{\ell-3}{2}\rfloor
.

We first show that
t\geq 4
. Otherwise,
t\leq 3
. Then

\sum_{1\leq i\leq t}n_{i}\leq n_{1}+n_{2}+\frac{1}{2}(n_{1}+n_{2})\leq\frac{3}{2}(\ell-3)<\frac{3}{2}\frac{2n-4}{3}=n-2,

a contradiction. Hence
t\geq 4
.

Now we show that
n_{1}-n_{2}\leq 1
. Otherwise,
n_{1}\geq n_{2}+2
. Let

G^{*1}=K_{2}\vee(P_{n_{1}-1}\cup P_{n_{2}+1}\cup P_{n_{3}+1}\cup P_{n_{4}-1}\cup(\cup_{5\leq i\leq t}P_{n_{i}})).

Clearly,
G^{*1}
is planar and
C_{\ell}
-free (by the above observation). But
\rho(G^{*1})>\rho(G)

by Lemma
1.2
, a contradiction. Hence,
n_{1}-n_{2}\leq 1
. It follows that
n_{1}=\lceil\frac{\ell-3}{2}\rceil
and
n_{2}=\lfloor\frac{\ell-3}{2}\rfloor
.

Now we show that
n_{3}=n_{4}=\cdots=n_{t-1}=\lfloor\frac{\ell-3}{2}\rfloor
. Otherwise,
n_{t-1}<n_{2}=\lfloor\frac{\ell-3}{2}\rfloor
. Let

G^{*2}=K_{2}\vee((\cup_{1\leq i\leq t-2}P_{n_{i}})\cup P_{n_{t-1}+1}\cup P_{n_{t}-1}).

Clearly,
G^{*2}
is planar and
C_{\ell}
-free (by the above observation). But
\rho(G^{*2})>\rho(G)

by Lemma
1.1
, a contradiction. Hence,
n_{3}=n_{4}=\cdots n_{t-1}=\lfloor\frac{\ell-3}{2}\rfloor
. It follows that
t=a+3
and
n_{t}=b
, since
n-\ell+1=a\lfloor\frac{\ell-3}{2}\rfloor+b
. Consequently,
G=K_{2}\vee(P_{\lceil\frac{\ell-3}{2}\rceil}\cup(a+1)P_{\lfloor\frac{\ell-3}{2}\rfloor}\cup P_{b})
, as desired.

(ii)

\frac{2n+5}{3}\leq\ell\leq n
.
In this case, we have

\ell-2+\lfloor\frac{\ell-3}{2}\rfloor\geq\frac{2n+5}{3}-2+\lfloor\frac{n-2}{3}\rfloor\geq\frac{2n+5}{3}-2+\frac{n-4}{3}>n-2.

We first show that
t=3
.
Otherwise,
t\geq 4
. If
n_{1}-n_{2}\geq 2
, we will obtain a contradiction using a similar discussion as that in
(i)
. Thus, we must have
n_{1}-n_{2}\leq 1
. It follows that
n_{1}=\lceil\frac{\ell-3}{2}\rceil
and
n_{2}=\lfloor\frac{\ell-3}{2}\rfloor
,
since
n_{1}+n_{2}=\ell-3
. We claim that
n_{3}=\lfloor\frac{\ell-3}{2}\rfloor
. Otherwise,
n_{t-1}\leq n_{3}<n_{2}=\lfloor\frac{\ell-3}{2}\rfloor
. Let

G^{*3}=K_{2}\vee((\cup_{1\leq i\leq 2}P_{n_{i}})\cup P_{n_{t-1}+1}\cup P_{n_{t}-1}).

Clearly,
G^{*3}
is planar and
C_{\ell}
-free (by the above observation). But
\rho(G^{*3})>\rho(G)

by Lemma
1.1
, a contradiction. Hence,
n_{3}=\lfloor\frac{\ell-3}{2}\rfloor
. But then

\sum_{1\leq i\leq t}n_{i}\geq n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}+1=\ell-2+\lfloor\frac{\ell-3}{2}\rfloor>n-2,

a contradiction. Thus, we must have
t=3
. Then
G=K_{2}\vee(P_{n_{1}}\cup P_{n_{2}}\cup P_{n_{3}})
.

Now we show that
n_{2}=n_{3}
. Otherwise,
n_{2}\geq n_{3}+1
. Let

G^{*4}=K_{2}\vee(P_{n_{1}+1}\cup P_{n_{2}-1}\cup P_{n_{3}}).

Clearly,
G^{*4}
is planar and
C_{\ell}
-free (by the above observation). But
\rho(G^{*4})>\rho(G)

by Lemma
1.1
, a contradiction. Hence,
n_{2}=n_{3}
. It follows that
n_{1}=2\ell-n-4
and
n_{2}=n_{3}=n-\ell+1
, since
n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}=n-2
and
n_{1}+n_{2}=\ell-3
. Consequently,

G=K_{2}\vee(P_{2\ell-n-4}\cup 2P_{n-\ell+1})
, as desired.
This completes the proof.
\Box

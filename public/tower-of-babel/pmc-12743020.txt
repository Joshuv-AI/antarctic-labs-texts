Novel and simple technique to prevent internal hernias after laparoscopic left-sided colorectal resection: alignment and fixation of the proximal jejunum
Authors: Z Zheng, Z Chen, X Wang, D Ye, X Lu, Y Huang, P Chi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundInternal hernia with small bowel obstruction after laparoscopic colorectal resection is a rare but potentially life-threatening complication, especially when it occurs after left hemicolectomy. While several studies have suggested that the closure of mesenteric defects may prevent internal hernias, the optimal preventive strategy has yet to be determined.MethodsWe describe a surgical technique designed to reduce the risk of internal hernia after left-sided colorectal resection, involving complete mobilization of the proximal jejunum, followed by its alignment and fixation to the epiploic appendages over the reconstructed left mesocolon.ResultsFrom November 2024 to February 2025, the aforementioned surgical procedures were successfully performed in 25 patients who underwent laparoscopic colorectal resection with splenic flexure mobilization. No symptomatic or asymptomatic internal hernias were observed during the follow-up computed tomography (CT) scan (3–6 months).ConclusionsThis novel technique has the potential to reduce internal hernias. Further studies are required.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s10151-025-03253-w.Keywords: Colorectal cancer, Left hemicolectomy, Internal hernia

Introduction

Laparoscopic colorectal surgery has become a standard approach for treating colorectal cancer; however, postoperative complications, particularly small bowel obstruction, remain a significant concern [1]. Internal hernia (IH) with small bowel obstruction is a rare but potentially fatal complication after laparoscopic colorectal surgery. The prevalence of symptomatic IH is approximately 0.65% [2], while the prevalence of asymptomatic IH is much higher (21%) according to postoperative computed tomography (CT) scans [3]. Symptomatic IHs are associated with high morbidity and mortality; they often occur within 4 months postoperatively, and they are predominantly linked to the mobilization of left-sided mesocolon and splenic flexure [2]. Although the closure of mesenteric defects has been proposed as a preventive measure [1], its necessity and efficacy remain unclear [4].

We developed a novel technique involving proximal jejunal alignment and fixation after the bowel continuity reconstruction. This approach may offer a safer and more effective alternative to traditional mesenteric defect closure.

Patients and methods

Patients

Twenty-five patients with left-sided colon cancer or rectal cancer who underwent laparoscopic colorectal resection with splenic flexure mobilization at our center from November 2024 to February 2025 were included. Patients with severe small bowel adhesions or prior small bowel resections were excluded. IH was defined as a protrusion of a part of the small bowel beneath the neodescending colon through the mesenteric defect. All procedures were performed by a single experienced laparoscopic surgeon (with 500 prior colorectal resections). Ethical approval was obtained from Fujian Medical University Union Hospital, and informed consent was obtained from all participants.

Operative procedure

All the colorectal resections were performed using a standardized technique [5]. After the bowel continuity reconstruction, the following steps were performed (Video 1):

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS v.25.0 (SPSS Inc.). Descriptive statistics were presented as counts and percentages for qualitative data. Quantitative data were described by the mean and standard deviation (SD) or median with interquartile range (IQR).

Results

Twenty-five patients who underwent laparoscopic colorectal resection with splenic flexure mobilization were included in this study. All surgical procedures were successfully completed without major intraoperative complications, but with some minor bleeding during making sutures. None of the patients developed symptomatic IHs or bowel obstruction at discharge. Moreover, an abdominal CT scan was performed between 3 and 6 months after surgery (22/25, 88%). During the follow-up, no symptomatic or asymptomatic IHs occurred (Fig. 1).

Discussion

To our knowledge, this is the first report describing proximal jejunal alignment and fixation to prevent IH after laparoscopic colorectal resection. The technique may address the risk of jejunal migration underneath the neodescending mesocolon, particularly in cases requiring splenic flexure mobilization.

For this novel technique, several technical considerations are critical. First, complete dissection of the ligament of Treitz is essential to mobilize the proximal jejunum away from the posterior aspect of the reconstructed left colon, thereby preventing compression of the jejunum by the reconstructed colon. Subsequently, suturing the proximal jejunum to the epiploic appendages of the left colon ensures that postoperative peristalsis does not displace the jejunum behind the mesentery, which could lead to IH. This suturing step is pivotal—even if a portion of the jejunum remains partially hidden behind the mesocolon (resulting in an incomplete herniation), secure fixation prevents further migration of bowel segments into the potential space.

Currently, discussions on the prevention of IH after laparoscopic colorectal resection have focused mainly on closing mesenteric defects. Common methods for closing mesenteric defects include sutures, vascular clips, and fibrin glue [6], with some evidence confirming their effectiveness. However, most of the existing evidence is limited to case reports and nonrandomized observational studies. Some scholars have argued that the closure of mesenteric defects is associated with a higher complication rate and has not shown benefits in the context of abdominal colectomy [4, 7]. Additionally, the closure of mesenteric defects is technically challenging and time-consuming, and there is a risk of inadvertently damaging the left ureter, retroperitoneal structures, and marginal vascular arches, which could jeopardize the blood supply to the anastomosis [8]. Furthermore, the long-term outcomes of closure of mesenteric defects may also be unsatisfactory [9], and this approach does not guarantee the prevention of IH. Portale et al. reported that five patients who underwent left hemicolectomy developed IHs requiring reintervention, four of whom already had their mesenteric defects closed during the initial surgery [10]. Ultimately, the cause of IH after mesocolic breach closure is the partial dehiscence of the closure.

Despite the low incidence of IH, it can lead to serious consequences, such as reoperation or mortality. Therefore, it is worthwhile to spend time on laparoscopic small bowel fixation after anastomosis to prevent the occurrence of IH. As we observed in our study, the application of small bowel fixation represents a simple and repeatable method for preventing IH. This method is safe because it does not damage retroperitoneal structures or the marginal artery of the neodescending colon. This technique may be particularly suitable when anatomical or pathological conditions (such as an aortic aneurysm) make suturing and closing mesenteric defects very dangerous. Moreover, it overcomes the long-term uncertain outcomes of mesenteric defect closure and the drawbacks of using expensive fibrin glue or its partial inapplicability in some centers. During the laparoscopic suture fixation process, overly deep suturing should be avoided to prevent intestinal fistula. On the basis of our experience, small bowel fixation was not associated with any complications and may reduce the incidence of IH during laparoscopic colorectal resection.

The limitations of this study include its single-center design and small cohort. Larger series of patients with longer follow-up periods are needed.

Conclusions

In this study, we developed a novel technique for aligning and fixing the proximal jejunum after laparoscopic colorectal resection with splenic flexure mobilization, potentially reducing IH. However, further validation through large-scale, long-term studies is warranted.

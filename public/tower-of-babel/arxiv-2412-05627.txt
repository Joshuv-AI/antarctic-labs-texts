On a certain identity for the cotangent finite Dirichlet series and its application to the Berndt–Arakawa formula Thanks: The research of the first author was supported in part by JSPS KAKENHI Grant Number 22K03235 and by Osaka Central Advanced Mathematical Institute (MEXT Promotion of Distinctive Joint Research Center Program JPMXP0723833165).
Masaaki Furusawa, Tomo Narahara

Abstract
. The cotangent zeta function is a very interesting object, which is related to partial zeta functions and Hecke L L -functions of real quadratic fields. Its special values at odd integers greater than 1 1 are explicitly evaluated by Berndt in the real quadratic unit case. Later Arakawa generalized the formula to the arbitrary real quadratic number case. The purpose of this article is to give a novel and surprisingly simple proof of the Berndt–Arakawa formula. Namely we prove a certain asymptotic identity for two finite cotangent Dirichlet series, from which we deduce the Berndt–Arakawa formula immediately. The authors believe that the method employed here of evaluating special values of infinite Dirichlet series from an asymptotic identity for its finite partial sum is of interest in its own right.

Primary: 11M41; Secondary: 11F20

1.
Introduction

For a real irrational algebraic number
\alpha
, the
Dirichlet series

\xi\left(s,\alpha\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\,\frac{\cot\pi n\alpha}{n^{s}}

(1.0.1)

converges absolutely when
\mathrm{Re}\left(s\right)>1
,
thanks to the Thue–Siegel–Roth theorem in the Diophantine approximation theory,
as shown in Arakawa
[
2
, Lemma 2.1]
by using
[
1
, Lemma 1]
.
We call
\xi\left(s,\alpha\right)
the cotangent zeta function.

Suppose that
\alpha
is a real quadratic number.
Then

\xi\left(s,\alpha\right)
is of substantial interest because of its
relationship with partial zeta functions and Hecke
L
-functions of real quadratic fields,
which we refer to Arakawa
[
2
]

and Arakawa, Ibukiyama and Kaneko
[
3
, Chapter 13]
.
Berndt
[
4
, Theorem 5.2]
proved a fascinating explicit
formula for
\xi\left(s,\alpha\right)

when
s
is an odd integer greater than
1
and

\alpha
is a unit.
The formula was later generalized to an arbitrary real quadratic number
\alpha

by
Arakawa
[
2
, (2.7)]

(see also
[
3
, Theorem 13.4]
).

The purpose of this article is to give a novel proof of the Berndt–Arakawa
formula.
Indeed we deduce it from a certain asymptotic identity for the finite cotangent Dirichlet series.
All existing proofs rely on meromorphic continuations in some way or other.
On the contrary, our proof is surprisingly simple, not appealing to meromorphic continuations at all.
To the best of the authors’ knowledge,
this is the first example of evaluating special values of
infinite
Dirichlet series
from an asymptotic identity for
finite
Dirichlet series.
We think that our method is of interest in its own right.

The authors believe that our approach is applicable to evaluating
explicitly
special values of some other Dirichlet series by
replacing
S_{m}\left(k\right)
in
(
2.2.1
), employed in this paper, by appropriate polynomials.

1.1.
Notation

\mathbb{N}
denotes
the set of positive integers, i.e.

\mathbb{N}=\left\{n\in\mathbb{Z}\mid n\geq 1\right\}
.
For
x\in\mathbb{R}
, we denote by
\lfloor x\rfloor
the greatest integer
less than or equal to
x
and by
\left\{x\right\}
the fractional part of
x
,
i.e.
\left\{x\right\}=x-\lfloor x\rfloor
.

For
x\in\mathbb{R}
, let
e\left(x\right):=\exp\left(2\pi ix\right)
.
Note that
e\left(x\right)=e\left(\left\{x\right\}\right)
.

We denote by
B_{n}\left(x\right)
the Bernoulli polynomial defined by

\frac{te^{tx}}{e^{t}-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{B_{n}\left(x\right)}{n!}\,t^{n}.

(1.1.1)

We note that
B_{0}\left(x\right)=1
in particular.

1.2.
Main Theorem

Now let us state our main theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\alpha
be an irrational real number.
For
k,m\in\mathbb{N}
,
let

\xi_{k}\left(2m-1,\alpha\right):=\sum_{n=1}^{k}\,\frac{\cot\pi n\alpha}{n^{2m-1}}.

(1.2.1)

For
V=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}\right)
,
we define
\displaystyle{V\alpha:=\frac{a\alpha+b}{c\alpha+d}}

and suppose that
\eta:=c\alpha+d>0
with
c>0
.

Then for
m\geq 2
, we have

\xi_{K}\left(2m-1,\alpha\right)-\eta^{2m-2}\,\xi_{k}\left(2m-1,V\alpha\right)+\frac{\eta^{2m-2}}{\pi k^{2m-1}}\cdot\frac{1}{1-\left\{\frac{K-ak}{c}\right\}-c^{-1}\left\{\frac{k}{\eta}\right\}}\\
=\left(-1\right)^{m-1}\left(2\pi\right)^{2m-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{2m}\,\sum_{j\,\mathrm{mod}\,c}\frac{B_{\ell}\left(x_{j}\right)B_{2m-\ell}\left(y_{j}\right)}{\ell!\left(2m-\ell\right)!}\cdot\eta^{2m-\ell-1}+O\left(k^{-1}\right).

(1.2.2)

Here

K=\lfloor\frac{k}{\eta}\rfloor\quad\text{and}\quad x_{j}=1-\left\{\frac{dj}{c}\right\},y_{j}=\left\{\frac{j}{c}\right\}\quad\text{for each $j\,\mathrm{mod}\,c$}.

Remark 1.1
.

In this theorem, we are dealing with the finite series
\xi_{k}\left(2m-1,\alpha\right)

and hence the convergence is not an issue.
Thus we do not impose the condition that
\alpha
is algebraic.

The deduction of the Berndt–Arakawa formula
from (
1.2.2
) is immediate.

Theorem 1.2

(Berndt
[
4
]
, Arakawa
[
2
]
)
.

Let
F
be a real quadratic field and
\alpha\in F\setminus\mathbb{Q}
.
Take a totally positive unit
\eta\in F
such that

\displaystyle{\begin{pmatrix}\eta\alpha\\
\eta\end{pmatrix}=V\begin{pmatrix}\alpha\\
1\end{pmatrix}}

for some
V=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}\right)
with
c>0
.

Then for an integer
m\geq 2
, we have

\xi\left(2m-1,\alpha\right)=\frac{\left(-1\right)^{m-1}\left(2\pi\right)^{2m-1}}{1-\eta^{2m-2}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{2m}\,\sum_{j\,\mathrm{mod}\,c}\frac{B_{\ell}\left(x_{j}\right)B_{2m-\ell}\left(y_{j}\right)}{\ell!\left(2m-\ell\right)!}\cdot\eta^{2m-\ell-1}.

(1.2.3)

Proof.

For the existence of such
\eta
, we refer to
Arakawa, Ibukiyama and Kaneko
[
3
, page 216]
.

In (
1.2.2
), we note that

1-\left\{\frac{K-ak}{c}\right\}-c^{-1}\left\{\frac{k}{\eta}\right\}\geq 1-\left(1-c^{-1}\right)-c^{-1}\left\{\frac{k}{\eta}\right\}=c^{-1}\left(1-\left\{\frac{k}{\eta}\right\}\right)\geq c^{-1}\langle\langle\frac{k}{\eta}\rangle\rangle

where
\langle\langle x\rangle\rangle
denotes the distance to the nearest integer
for
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
By Thue–Siegel–Roth theorem on Diophantine approximation
in transcendental number theory, for any
\varepsilon>0
,
there exists a constant
C\left(\varepsilon\right)>0
such that

\langle\langle\frac{k}{\eta}\rangle\rangle>C\left(\varepsilon\right)k^{-1-\varepsilon}

for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
(cf.
[
3
, page 213]
).
Hence

\left|\frac{\eta^{2m-2}}{\pi k^{2m-1}}\cdot\frac{1}{1-\left\{\frac{K-ak}{c}\right\}-c^{-1}\left\{\frac{k}{\eta}\right\}}\right|<\frac{\eta^{2m-2}}{\pi k^{2m-1}}\cdot\frac{ck^{1+\varepsilon}}{C\left(\varepsilon\right)}=\frac{\eta^{2m-2}c}{\pi C\left(\varepsilon\right)}\,k^{2-2m+\varepsilon}.

Thus by letting
k\to\infty
and noting that
V\alpha=\alpha
,
we obtain (
1.2.3
)
from (
1.2.2
).
∎

Remark 1.2
.

We remark that by taking
V=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}
,
we also obtain the following functional equation, which Lerch stated
in
[
5
]
without a proof, by the same argument from Theorem
1.1
:

For an algebraic irrational real number
\alpha
and an integer
m\geq 2
,
we have

\xi\left(2m-1,\alpha\right)-\alpha^{2m-2}\xi\left(2m-1,\alpha^{-1}\right)\\
=\left(-1\right)^{m-1}\left(2\pi\right)^{2m-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{2m}\frac{B_{\ell}B_{2m-\ell}}{\ell!\,\left(2m-\ell\right)!}\cdot\alpha^{2m-\ell-1}

(1.2.4)

where
B_{j}
denotes the
j
-th Bernoulli number.

We refer to Berndt
[
4
, Section 6]

and the references therein
for the relation of (
1.2.4
) to the first letter from Ramanujan to Hardy,
dated January 16, 1913,
and to Ramanujan’s formula for
\zeta\left(2r-1\right)
,
r>1
.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

2.1.
Some preliminaries and lemmas

Before proceeding to the proof of Theorem
1.1
,
we recall some preliminaries and note some lemmas here.

2.1.1.
Lemma on Bernoulli polynomials

Lemma 2.1
.

For
n,q\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
we define
A_{n,q}\left(x\right)
by

A_{n,q}\left(x\right):=\sum_{0<\left|u\right|\leq n}e\left(ux\right)u^{-q}\quad\text{where $e\left(x\right)=\exp\left(2\pi ix\right)$}.

Then we have

A_{n,q}\left(x\right)=\begin{cases}0,&\text{when $q=1$ and $x\in\mathbb{Z}$};\\
\displaystyle{-B_{q}\left(\left\{x\right\}\right)\frac{\left(2\pi i\right)^{q}}{q!}+O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(2.1.1)

Proof.

When
q=1
and
x\in\mathbb{Z}
,

A_{n,1}\left(x\right)=\sum_{0<\left|u\right|\leq n}u^{-1}=0
.

Suppose otherwise. We recall (e.g.
[
3
, Theorem 4.11]
) :

B_{q}\left(\left\{x\right\}\right)=-\frac{q!}{\left(2\pi i\right)^{q}}\,\lim_{m\to\infty}A_{m,q}\left(x\right).

(2.1.2)

Hence

\displaystyle A_{n,q}\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{m\to\infty}A_{m,q}\left(x\right)-\sum_{u=n+1}^{\infty}\left\{e\left(ux\right)+\left(-1\right)^{q}e\left(-ux\right)\right\}u^{-q}

\displaystyle=-B_{q}\left(\left\{x\right\}\right)\frac{\left(2\pi i\right)^{q}}{q!}-\sum_{u=n+1}^{\infty}\left\{e\left(ux\right)+\left(-1\right)^{q}e\left(-ux\right)\right\}u^{-q}.

When
q\geq 2
, we have

\left|\,\sum_{u=n+1}^{\infty}\left\{e\left(ux\right)+\left(-1\right)^{q}e\left(-ux\right)\right\}u^{-q}\right|\leq 2\sum_{u=n+1}^{\infty}\,\frac{1}{u\left(u-1\right)}=\frac{2}{n}.

Suppose that
q=1
. Then

\sum_{u=n+1}^{\infty}\left\{e\left(ux\right)-e\left(-ux\right)\right\}u^{-1}=2i\sum_{u=n+1}^{\infty}\frac{\sin\left(2\pi ux\right)}{u}.

(2.1.3)

Put
A_{r}=\sum_{u=1}^{r}\sin\left(2\pi ux\right)
for
r\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then by the well-known summation formula, we have

A_{r}=\frac{\sin\left(\pi rx\right)\cdot\sin\left\{\pi\left(r+1\right)x\right\}}{\sin\pi x}

since
\sin\left(\pi x\right)\neq 0
from the assumption.
Hence
A_{r}=O\left(1\right)
.
By the Abel transformation, we have

\sum_{u=n+1}^{r}\frac{\sin\left(2\pi ux\right)}{u}=\frac{A_{r}}{r}-\frac{A_{n}}{n+1}+\sum_{u=n+1}^{r-1}A_{u}\left(\frac{1}{u}-\frac{1}{u+1}\right)

and hence

\sum_{u=n+1}^{\infty}\frac{\sin\left(2\pi ux\right)}{u}=O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)+O\left(\sum_{u=n+1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{u}-\frac{1}{u+1}\right)\right)=O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right).

∎

2.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Now let us prove our main theorem,
Theorem
1.1
.

First, for
k,m\in\mathbb{N}
,
we define a sum
S_{m}\left(k\right)
by

S_{m}\left(k\right):=-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<\left|u\right|\leq k,\,0<\left|v\right|\leq K\\
c\lvert\left(u-dv\right)\end{subarray}}\,c\cdot f_{m}\left(u,v\right)

(2.2.1)

where

f_{m}\left(u,v\right)=\frac{u^{2m-1}-\eta^{2m-1}v^{2m-1}}{u^{2m-1}v^{2m-1}\left(u-\eta v\right)}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{2m-1}\eta^{2m-1-\ell}u^{\ell-2m}v^{-\ell}.

(2.2.2)

Then Theorem
1.1
is proved
by deforming
S_{m}\left(k\right)

in two different ways.

2.2.1.
The first deformation

For
u,v\in\mathbb{Z}
,
we recall that

\sum_{j\,\mathrm{mod}\,c}e\left(\frac{u-dv}{c}\,j\right)=\begin{cases}c,&\text{when $c\lvert\left(u-dv\right)$};\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(2.2.3)

Hence we may write (
2.2.1
) as

S_{m}\left(k\right)=-\sum_{\ell=1}^{2m-1}\eta^{2m-1-\ell}\sum_{j\,\mathrm{mod}\,c}A_{k,2m-\ell}\left(\frac{j}{c}\right)\,A_{K,\ell}\left(\frac{-dj}{c}\right).

(2.2.4)

When
m\geq 2
, by Lemma
2.1
and (
2.2.4
), we have

S_{m}\left(k\right)=\left(-1\right)^{m-1}\left(2\pi\right)^{2m}\sum_{\ell=1}^{2m-1}\sum_{j\,\mathrm{mod}\,c}\frac{B_{\ell}\left(x_{j}\right)B_{2m-\ell}\left(y_{j}\right)}{\ell!\left(2m-\ell\right)!}\eta^{2m-1-\ell}+O\left(k^{-1}\right).

(2.2.5)

2.2.2.
The second deformation

There is another way to deform
S_{m}\left(k\right)
.

Since

f_{m}\left(u,v\right)=\frac{-1}{v^{2m-1}\left(\eta v-u\right)}+\frac{\eta^{2m-2}}{u^{2m-1}\left(v-\eta^{-1}u\right)},

we have
S_{m}\left(k\right)=S_{m}^{(1)}\left(k\right)+S_{m}^{(2)}\left(k\right)
, where

S_{m}^{(1)}\left(k\right)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<\left|u\right|\leq k\\
0<\left|v\right|\leq K\\
c\lvert\left(u-dv\right)\end{subarray}}\frac{c}{v^{2m-1}\left(\eta v-u\right)}\quad\text{and}\quad S_{m}^{(2)}\left(k\right)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<\left|u\right|\leq k\\
0<\left|v\right|\leq K\\
c\lvert\left(u-dv\right)\end{subarray}}\frac{-c\eta^{2m-2}}{u^{2m-1}\left(v-\eta^{-1}u\right)}.

Then we have

\displaystyle S_{m}^{(1)}\left(k\right)=\sum_{0<\left|v\right|\leq K}\frac{1}{v^{2m-1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left|u\right|\leq k\\
c\lvert\left(u-dv\right)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\frac{\eta v-u}{c}}-\frac{c}{\eta}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<\left|v\right|\leq K\\
c\lvert dv\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{v^{2m}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{0<\left|v\right|\leq K}\frac{1}{v^{2m-1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left|u\right|\leq k\\
c\lvert\left(u-dv\right)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\alpha v-\frac{u-dv}{c}}-\frac{1}{\eta}\sum_{0<\left|v\right|\leq K}\frac{1}{v^{2m}}\sum_{j\,\mathrm{mod}\,c}e\left(\frac{-dv}{c}j\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{0<\left|v\right|\leq K}\frac{1}{v^{2m-1}}\sum_{-\frac{k+dv}{c}\leq w\leq\frac{k-dv}{c}}\frac{1}{\alpha v-w}-\frac{1}{\eta}\sum_{j\,\mathrm{mod}\,c}\sum_{0<\left|v\right|\leq K}e\left(\frac{-dj}{c}v\right)v^{-2m}.

Hence

S_{m}^{(1)}\left(k\right)=2\sum_{v=1}^{K}\frac{1}{v^{2m-1}}\sum_{-\frac{k+dv}{c}\leq w\leq\frac{k-dv}{c}}\frac{1}{\alpha v-w}-\frac{1}{\eta}\sum_{j\,\mathrm{mod}\,c}A_{K,2m}\left(\frac{-dj}{c}\right).

(2.2.6)

Similarly we have

S_{m}^{(2)}\left(k\right)=\sum_{0<\left|u\right|\leq k}\frac{-\eta^{2m-2}}{u^{2m-1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left|v\right|\leq K\\
c\lvert\left(u-dv\right)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{c^{-1}\left(v-\eta^{-1}u\right)}-\eta^{2m-1}\sum_{j\,\mathrm{mod}\,c}A_{k,2m}\left(\frac{j}{c}\right).

Since
u-dv=\left(ad-bc\right)u-dv=-bcu-d\left(v-au\right)

and
c
,
d
are relatively prime, we have

c\lvert\left(u-dv\right)\Longleftrightarrow c\lvert\left(v-au\right).

When we put
w=c^{-1}\left(au-v\right)
, we have

c^{-1}\left(v-\eta^{-1}u\right)=c^{-1}\left(-cw+au-\eta^{-1}u\right)=\frac{a\alpha+b}{c\alpha+d}\,u-w=V\alpha u-w.

Hence

S_{m}^{(2)}\left(k\right)=-2\eta^{2m-2}\sum_{u=1}^{k}\frac{1}{u^{2m-1}}\sum_{-\frac{K-au}{c}\leq w\leq\frac{K+au}{c}}\frac{1}{V\alpha u-w}-\eta^{2m-1}\sum_{j\,\mathrm{mod}\,c}A_{k,2m}\left(\frac{j}{c}\right).

(2.2.7)

Thus we have

S_{m}\left(k\right)=2T_{m}^{(1)}\left(k\right)-2\eta^{2m-2}T_{m}^{(2)}\left(k\right)+U_{m}\left(k\right)
where

\displaystyle T_{m}^{(1)}\left(k\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{v=1}^{K}\frac{1}{v^{2m-1}}\sum_{-\frac{k+dv}{c}\leq w\leq\frac{k-dv}{c}}\frac{1}{\alpha v-w},

(2.2.8)

\displaystyle T_{m}^{(2)}\left(k\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{u=1}^{k}\frac{1}{u^{2m-1}}\sum_{-\frac{K-au}{c}\leq w\leq\frac{K+au}{c}}\frac{1}{V\alpha u-w},

(2.2.9)

\displaystyle U_{m}\left(k\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\eta}\sum_{j\,\mathrm{mod}\,c}A_{K,2m}\left(\frac{-dj}{c}\right)-\eta^{2m-1}\sum_{j\,\mathrm{mod}\,c}A_{k,2m}\left(\frac{j}{c}\right).

(2.2.10)

Here by Lemma
2.1
, we have

U_{m}\left(k\right)=\left(-1\right)^{m}\left(2\pi\right)^{2m}\sum_{\ell=0,\,2m}\,\sum_{j\,\mathrm{mod}\,c}\frac{B_{\ell}\left(x_{j}\right)B_{2m-\ell}\left(y_{j}\right)}{\ell!\left(2m-\ell\right)!}\eta^{2m-1-\ell}+O\left(k^{-1}\right).

(2.2.11)

2.2.3.
Estimation of the inner sums of the double sums
T_{m}^{(1)}\left(k\right)
and
T_{m}^{(2)}\left(k\right)
.

First let us consider the inner sum of
T_{m}^{(1)}\left(k\right)
.
For
1\leq v\leq K
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{-\frac{k+dv}{c}\leq w\leq\frac{k-dv}{c}}\,\frac{1}{\alpha v-w}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\left(\sum_{-N\leq w\leq N}-\sum_{-N\leq w<-\frac{k+dv}{c}}-\sum_{\frac{k-dv}{c}<w\leq N}\right)\,\frac{1}{\alpha v-w}

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}\left(\sum_{-N\leq w\leq N}\frac{1}{\alpha v-w}-\sum_{\frac{k+dv}{c}<w\leq N}\frac{1}{w+\alpha v}+\sum_{\frac{k-dv}{c}<w\leq N}\frac{1}{w-\alpha v}\right)

\displaystyle=\pi\cot{\pi\alpha v}

\displaystyle\quad+\lim_{N\to\infty}\left(-\sum_{\frac{k+dv}{c}+\lfloor\alpha v\rfloor<w\leq N+\lfloor\alpha v\rfloor}\frac{1}{w+\{\alpha v\}}+\sum_{\frac{k-dv}{c}-\lfloor\alpha v\rfloor<w\leq N-\lfloor\alpha v\rfloor}\frac{1}{w-\{\alpha v\}}\right).

Here when
N
is sufficiently large,
depending on the sign of
\alpha
, we note that

\displaystyle-\sum_{\frac{k+dv}{c}+\lfloor\alpha v\rfloor<w\leq N+\lfloor\alpha v\rfloor}\frac{1}{w+\{\alpha v\}}+\sum_{\frac{k-dv}{c}-\lfloor\alpha v\rfloor<w\leq N-\lfloor\alpha v\rfloor}\frac{1}{w-\{\alpha v\}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\frac{k-dv}{c}-\lfloor\alpha v\rfloor<w\leq\frac{k+dv}{c}+\lfloor\alpha v\rfloor}\frac{1}{w-\{\alpha v\}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\frac{k+dv}{c}+\lfloor\alpha v\rfloor<w\leq N-\lfloor\alpha v\rfloor}\left(\frac{1}{w-\{\alpha v\}}-\frac{1}{w+\{\alpha v\}}\right)-\sum_{N-\lfloor\alpha v\rfloor<w\leq N+\lfloor\alpha v\rfloor}\frac{1}{w+\{\alpha v\}}

when
\alpha>0
, and,

\displaystyle-\sum_{\frac{k+dv}{c}+\lfloor\alpha v\rfloor<w\leq N+\lfloor\alpha v\rfloor}\frac{1}{w+\{\alpha v\}}+\sum_{\frac{k-dv}{c}-\lfloor\alpha v\rfloor<w\leq N-\lfloor\alpha v\rfloor}\frac{1}{w-\{\alpha v\}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\frac{k-dv}{c}-\lfloor\alpha v\rfloor<w\leq\frac{k+dv}{c}+\lfloor\alpha v\rfloor}\frac{1}{w-\{\alpha v\}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\frac{k+dv}{c}+\lfloor\alpha v\rfloor<w\leq N+\lfloor\alpha v\rfloor}\left(\frac{1}{w-\{\alpha v\}}-\frac{1}{w+\{\alpha v\}}\right)+\sum_{N+\lfloor\alpha v\rfloor<w\leq N-\lfloor\alpha v\rfloor}\frac{1}{w-\{\alpha v\}}

when
\alpha<0
.
Thus we have

\sum_{-\frac{k+dv}{c}\leq w\leq\frac{k-dv}{c}}\,\frac{1}{\alpha v-w}=\pi\cot{\pi\alpha v}\\
+\sum_{\frac{k-dv}{c}-\lfloor\alpha v\rfloor<w\leq\frac{k+dv}{c}+\lfloor\alpha v\rfloor}\frac{1}{w-\{\alpha v\}}+\sum_{\frac{k+dv}{c}+\lfloor\alpha v\rfloor<w}\frac{2\{\alpha v\}}{w^{2}-\{\alpha v\}^{2}}.

(2.2.12)

Let us estimate the second term of the right-hand side of (
2.2.12
).
Since

\frac{k-dv}{c}-\lfloor\alpha v\rfloor-\{\alpha v\}=c^{-1}(k-\eta v)\geq c^{-1}(k-\eta K)=c^{-1}(k-\eta\lfloor\frac{k}{\eta}\rfloor)>0,

each summand is positive and hence is bounded by

\frac{1}{\frac{k-dv+1}{c}-\lfloor\alpha v\rfloor-\{\alpha v\}}=\frac{c}{k-\eta v+1}
.
Since the number of the terms in the sum is
O\left(v\right)
, the second term
of the right-hand side of (
2.2.12
) is

O\left(v\cdot\frac{c}{k-\eta v+1}\right)=O\left(\frac{v}{k-\eta v+1}\right)
.

As for the third term of the right-hand side of (
2.2.12
), we have

\sum_{\frac{k+dv}{c}+\lfloor\alpha v\rfloor<w}\frac{2\{\alpha v\}}{w^{2}-\{\alpha v\}^{2}}\leq\sum_{\frac{k}{c}-1<w}\frac{2}{w^{2}-1}=O(k^{-1}).

Since
k^{-1}=O\left(\frac{v}{k-\eta v+1}\right)
, we have

\sum_{-\frac{k+dv}{c}\leq w\leq\frac{k-dv}{c}}\frac{1}{\alpha v-w}=\pi\cot{\pi\alpha v}+O\left(\frac{v}{k-\eta v+1}\right).

(2.2.13)

Now let us consider the inner sum of
T_{m}^{(2)}\left(k\right)
.
For
1\leq u\leq k
, we write

\sum_{-\frac{K-au}{c}\leq w\leq\frac{K+au}{c}}\frac{1}{V\alpha u-w}=\sum_{-\frac{K-au}{c}-1\leq w\leq\frac{K+au}{c}}\frac{1}{V\alpha u-w}-\frac{1}{V\alpha u+\lfloor\frac{K-au}{c}\rfloor+1}.

By a computation similar to that above, we have

\sum_{-\frac{K-au}{c}-1\leq w\leq\frac{K+au}{c}}\frac{1}{V\alpha u-w}=\pi\cot{\pi V\alpha u}\\
-\sum_{\frac{K-au}{c}+\lfloor V\alpha u\rfloor+1<w\leq\frac{K+au}{c}-\lfloor V\alpha u\rfloor}\frac{1}{w+\{V\alpha u\}}+\sum_{\frac{K+au}{c}-\lfloor V\alpha u\rfloor<w}\frac{2\{V\alpha u\}}{w^{2}-\{V\alpha u\}^{2}}

and hence

\sum_{-\frac{K-au}{c}-1\leq w\leq\frac{K+au}{c}}\frac{1}{V\alpha u-w}=\pi\cot{\pi V\alpha u}+O\left(\frac{u}{K-\frac{u}{\eta}+1+c}\right).

Thus

\sum_{-\frac{K-au}{c}\leq w\leq\frac{K+au}{c}}\frac{1}{V\alpha u-w}=\pi\cot{\pi V\alpha u}-\frac{1}{V\alpha u+\lfloor\frac{K-au}{c}\rfloor+1}+O\left(\frac{u}{K-\frac{u}{\eta}+1+c}\right).

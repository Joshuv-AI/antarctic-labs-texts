Paulo Ricardo Knob, Leonardo Scholler, Juliano Rigatti, Soraia Raupp Musse

Abstract
. Conversational agents have made significant progress since ELIZA, expanding their role across various domains, including healthcare, education, and customer service. As these agents become increasingly integrated into daily human interactions, the need for emotional intelligence, particularly empathetic listening, becomes increasingly essential. In this study, we explore how Large Language Models (LLMs) respond when tasked with generating emotionally rich interactions. Starting from a small dataset manually crafted by an expert to reflect empathic behavior, we extended the conversations using two LLMs—ChatGPT and Gemini. We analyzed the emotional progression of the dialogues using both sentiment analysis (via VADER) and expert assessments. While the generated conversations often mirrored the intended emotional structure, human evaluation revealed important differences in the perceived empathy and coherence of the responses. These findings suggest that emotion modeling in dialogues requires not only structural alignment in the expressed emotions but also qualitative depth, highlighting the importance of combining automated and human-centered methods in the development of emotionally competent agents.

1.
Introduction

As technology evolves, it becomes more integrated with the routine of people. Many tasks that, in the past, required human intervention, can be performed nowadays by specialized computer systems, thanks to the Artificial Intelligence (AI)
(
Manickam et al. 2022
)
. Since the pioneer work of Joseph Weizenbaum, who proposed the chatbot ELIZA
(
Weizenbaum 1966
)
, conversational agents have been impacting on how humans interact with machines
(
Talyshinskii et al. 2024
)
. In fact, the advances in technology and, more specifically, in the AI field, led to the development of Natural Generative Language (NGL) models, which include the Generative Pre-trained Transformer (GPT) series
(
Brown et al. 2020
)
, released by OpenAI as ChatGPT, and Gemini
(
Team et al. 2023
)
, released by Google, among others. Such systems are based on Large Language Models (LLMs) and can both emulate the conversation between human beings and generate new content, based on the data in which they were trained
(
Abd-Alrazaq et al. 2023
)
.

Although LLMs, such as ChatGPT and Gemini, are able to generate coherent information and human-like responses, their ability to identify and describe emotions is uncertain yet
(
Elyoseph et al. 2023
)
. Emotion is closely related with empathy, specially when we think about its affective attributes, which involve physiological enthusiasm and spontaneous affective responses to someone else’s display of emotions
(
Prendinger et al. 2005
)
. Therefore, the modeling of an empathetic behavior in large language models is a research problem which is open for discussion and ideas.

The goal of this work is to assess whether or not a LLM-powered chatbot can be fine-tuned for empathetic listening, supporting the speaker in understanding feelings and needs for different situations. In order to do so, an experiment was conducted to detect the energy/polarity of the conversation, from the beginning until the end, using two well-known LLM-powered chatbots: ChatGPT, from OpenAI, and Gemini, from Google. Moreover, an expert was asked to evaluate the interactions generated by both chatbots.
Figure
1
illustrates the evolution of emotional energy throughout the interactions in three different datasets: the original one created by a human expert (left), and the extended versions generated by ChatGPT (center) and Gemini (right). In all three plots, the y-axis represents the energy values (as computed by VADER, ranging from -1 to 1), and the x-axis represents the progression of the conversation. The red curve shows the average emotional trend, while the blue curves indicate the minimum and maximum values at each interaction step. The manually authored dataset exhibits a clear and gradual increase in emotional energy, aligning with the expected behavior of an empathetic listener, helping the speaker move from negative to more positive affective states. The ChatGPT-extended dataset mirrors this upward trend, suggesting that the model can emulate the intended emotional trajectory; however, human evaluation later revealed that these responses often lacked genuine empathy. The Gemini-generated dataset also presents an overall increase in average energy, but with lower minimum values at the beginning and at the end of the interaction, indicating greater difficulty in processing or responding to the speaker’s initial negative emotions. These results highlight that while lexical sentiment analysis captures general emotional patterns, it is not sufficient to assess whether true empathetic listening occurred, underscoring the importance of human evaluation in this context.

2.
Related Work

Arjmand et al.
(
Arjmand et al. 2024
)
introduce the concept of ’empathic grounding’ in conversational agents, which includes the listener empathy for the speaker’s affective state. The authors argue that models of empathic grounding for embodied agents should be multi-modal to facilitate natural and efficient communication. Thus, they describe a multi-modal model that takes as input user speech and facial expression to generate multi-modal grounding moves for a listening agent, using a large language model (namely, GPT-3.5). The authors compare their work with one that only generates non-affective grounding cues, and the results indicate that empathic grounding increases user perceptions of empathy, understanding, emotional intelligence, and trust.

Hasan et al.
(
Hasan et al. 2023
)
present the Synthetic Anthropomorphic Personal Interaction ENgine (SAPIEN), a platform powered by Large-Language Models (LLM), with high-fidelity virtual agents that can engage in real-time open-domain conversations, while also expressing emotions through voice and facial expressions. Their platform allows customization of the virtual agent, tweaking its personality, background, and conversation premise. Besides that, the user can choose to get the conversation analyzed, after the virtual interaction, and receive feedback on their communication skills. The authors also comment about the range of domains in which SAPIEN can be used, such as mental health, communication training, language learning, education, and healthcare. The application is available online.

Elyoseph et al.
(
Elyoseph et al. 2023
)
uses the Levels of Emotional Awareness (EA) Scale to analyze ChatGPT’s responses to twenty scenarios, comparing its EA performance with the general population norms, as reported by a previous study. Additionally, a second examination was performed one month later to measure the improvement over time. In the first examination, ChatGPT demonstrated significantly higher performance than the general population in all scores. In the second examination, ChatGPT’s performance significantly improved, almost reaching the maximum possible scores, with extremely high accuracy levels (9.7/10). Thus, the authors comment that ChatGPT can generate appropriate EA responses, and its performance may improve significantly over time. However, further research is needed in order to better understand the potential benefits and risks, while also refining it to promote mental health.

Bill et al.
(
Bill and Eriksson 2023
)
aim to fine-tune a LMM to act in the psychology area. To do so, the authors use a method known as Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), which is an approach for reinforcement learning that aims to improve the performance of a given LLM by using the feedback of interacting humans. According to the authors, using such human feedback, the model is able to learn from specific examples and adapt to a particular task. The results achieved presented no significant difference between the pre-trained model and the fine-tuned model, which suggests that the fine-tuned model had little or no difference when compared with the model with no fine tuning.

Chen et al.
(
Chen et al. [n. d.]
)
argue that large language models often tend to provide universal advice when applied in the psychological counseling field. However, in such scenarios, the person is usually looking for more elaborated support, such as empathy, trust, understanding, and comfort. With that in mind, the authors built a dataset comprised of over 2 million multi-turn empathetic conversations, focusing on responses that exhibit questioning, comfort, recognition, listening, trust, and emotional support. The dataset was used to fine-tune LLMs, showing that their empathetic response capabilities were significantly enhanced.

3.
Proposed Experiment

This work aims to assess whether or not a LLM-powered chatbot can be fine-tuned for empathetic listening, supporting the speaker in understanding feelings and needs for different situations. The next sections present more details about the experiment conducted for this end.
Section
3.1
shows the configuration of an experiment conducted to detect the energy/polarity of the conversation between two individuals, making use of two different well-known LLM-powered chatbots, namely ChatGPT and Gemini. Section
3.2
presents the configuration of the questionnaire responded to by the expert, based on the interactions generated by both chatbots.

3.1.
Datasets Configuration

The goal of this experiment is to evaluate an LLM chatbot that can conduct a conversation with a person to facilitate empathic listening, thereby creating a sense of comfort in the person. For this experiment, we chose to work with two different chatbots: OpenAI LLM, known as ChatGPT, and Google LLM, known as Gemini. The idea is to utilize techniques of prompt engineering to fine-tune the model and guide it to engage in empathetic listening. For this end, the following steps were conducted:

•

(1) Gather examples of conversations curated by an expert, to serve as a base and ground truth;

•

(2) Define a benchmark technique to quantify and classify the responses produced by the model;

•

(3) Generate the interactions, simulating a real conversation.

For the first step, an expert in communication was assigned to manually create 24 texts with example interactions between two people, where the listener acted as an empathetic listener. A dataset was created with those texts. The conversations present in this dataset portray interactions between a speaker and a listener, where the listener acts as an empathetic listener. Each of the 24 conversations is comprised of 5 interactions, where the speaker (who starts the conversation) has three interactions, and the listener has 2. Table

Then, for the second step, we consider the concept of conversation energy, where negative and positive feelings are evaluated as negative and positive energy, respectively, ranging from -1 (negative) to 1 (positive). To perform this energy evaluation, we utilized the Valence Aware Dictionary and sEntiment Reasoner (VADER) tool
(
Hutto and Gilbert 2014
)
, a lexical and heuristic set that generates a numerical component indicating sentiment, ranging from negative to positive, to describe the energy of the text.

Finally, for the third step, the fine-tuned LLM was used to generate several conversations simulating interactions between two people. In this step, four additional datasets were created: the ChatGPT extended dataset, which was created extending the interactions of the dataset manually tailored by the expert, using ChatGPT; the Gemini extended dataset, which was created in the same way, but using Gemini instead; the ChatGPT control dataset, where ChatGPT generated the conversations entirely, with no fine-tuning concerning empathetic listening; and the Gemini control dataset, which was generated in the same manner, but using Gemini instead.

The extended datasets (for both ChatGPT and Gemini) were generated by continuing the conversations of the first dataset (manually tailored by the expert). The idea of extending the data to more points also serves to validate the LLM’s coherence and cohesion in maintaining longer conversations until a conclusion. The first points were not changed, so the data analysis is preserved in the first two points. For normalization purposes, all conversations were truncated at 12 interactions, where the speaker (who starts the conversation) counts with 6 interactions, and the listener (role played by the LLM chatbot) counts with another 6. In this experiment, our hypothesis is that, when analyzing each interaction between the listener and the speaker, it will be possible to observe a tendency of energy going from negative (or lower values) to positive (or higher values), containing these distinct characteristics:

•

The conversation begins with the problem, usually negative;

•

The empathic agent reiterates the problem to make sure he or she understands, equalizing the energy level of the conversation;

•

The conversation progresses toward a positive conclusion, converging the energy of both agents.

4.4.
Expert Evaluation

As described in Section
3.2
, we asked the expert to evaluate the interactions generated for the datasets. In order to do so, the expert answered a few questions about each dataset, as presented in Table
6
.
For the first three questions (Friendly, Nice, and Sensible), the expert gave the maximum evaluation (i.e. 5, Very Satisfied) to all four datasets (i.e. Extended and Control, for both ChatGPT and Gemini). However, for the last question (Empathetic listening ability), the expert rated the chatbot with the worst evaluation (i.e. 1, Very Unsatisfied), to all datasets as well.

By the end of the questionnaire, we included a free text box, so the expert would be able to freely talk about his/her impressions of the evaluated interaction, and even justify the evaluations given. For the Extended dataset generated by ChatGPT, the expert commented that "Although we can classify the chatbot as ’friendly’, ’nice’ and ’sensible’, his performance from the point of view of empathic listening was disastrous. The length of the conversation proposed by the chatbot is very judgmental and does little to explore the feelings and needs of the interlocutor.". For the Extended dataset generated by Gemini, the expert commented that "The extent of the conversation generated by the chatbot is full of judgments and is not restricted to investigating feelings and needs, which would be the function of empathic listening.".

In its turn, for the Control dataset generated by ChatGPT, the expert commented that "In the dialogues created by AI in this case there is no investigation whatsoever by any of the interlocutor’s feelings and needs.". In its turn, for the Control dataset generate by Gemini, the expert commented that "There is no interest whatsoever on the part of the interlocutors in the feelings and needs of the other, which would be the main characteristic of empathic listening.".

The results achieved with the energy tendency reading and with the expert evaluation, for both datasets (Extended and Control) and for both chatbots (ChatGPT and Gemini), suggest that, although useful, a lexical analysis should not be used as the only evaluation metric of an empathetic listener chatbot. Even when the energy of the conversation rises over time, the interaction itself might not be beneficial to the user, especially if the chatbot, who should be acting as an empathetic listener, does not fill this role entirely. In fact, the feedback of the expert suggests that the LLM-powered chatbot was not able to act as an empathetic listener, given its judgmental comments and lack of feeling exploring.

On the other hand, by the comments provided, it is possible to notice that the fine-tuned chatbot tried to fill the empathetic listener role, but failed, as the expert commented that it was judgmental and did little to explore the user’s feeling and needs. In its turn, the chatbot with no fine-tuning didn’t even try to fill the role, as the expert commented that there was no investigation about the feelings and needs of the user.

5.
Final Considerations

This study evaluated whether fine-tuning large language models can improve their ability to engage in empathetic listening dialogue. Our experiments involved ChatGPT and Gemini, both adapted through prompt engineering and assessed using energy trajectory analysis and expert feedback.

The obtained results reinforce the relevance of using both human-centered and automated approaches to analyze emotional progressions in dialogue. Beyond that, the study highlights two key takeaways. First, it was encouraging to observe that LLMs can be prompted and guided toward producing more emotionally supportive responses, showing potential for fine-tuning in the direction of empathy. However, the second—and perhaps more critical—finding is that truly empathetic listening goes beyond generating positive or soothing messages. It involves engaging with the speaker’s emotional state, helping them reflect and better understand their own problems—something that current LLMs still struggle to achieve. Finally, while sentiment lexicons such as VADER can be helpful tools for analyzing emotional trends, they alone are not sufficient to evaluate the presence or quality of empathetic listening. Empathy in dialogue is a complex, multi-layered process that requires context, intention, and human sensitivity—dimensions that remain challenging for automated systems.

From a human-centered AI perspective, this research reinforces the value of tailoring dialogue systems for affective sensitivity. Empathetic listening is critical in areas such as mental health support, digital companionship, and training simulations. Our methodology highlights the importance of expert-curated datasets and emotional metrics in evaluating conversational empathy.

For future work, we propose extending this investigation to additional LLMs, such as LLaMA or DeepSeek, to assess generalization. Moreover, involving interdisciplinary experts—including psychologists—and conducting user-based studies would enrich both the technical and social understanding of empathetic virtual agents.

Homoclinic solutions for nonlocal equations and applications to the theory of atom dislocation
Serena Dipierro, Caterina Sportelli, Enrico Valdinoci

Abstract
. We establish the existence of homoclinic solutions for suitable systems of nonlocal equations whose forcing term is of gradient type. The elliptic operator under consideration is the fractional Laplacian and the potentials that we take into account are of two types: the first one is a spatially homogeneous function with a strict local maximum at the origin, the second one is a spatially inhomogeneous potential satisfying the Ambrosetti–Rabinowitz condition coupled to a quadratic term with spatially dependent growth at infinity. The existence of these special solutions has interesting consequences for the theory of atomic edge dislocations in crystals according to the Peierls–Nabarro model and its generalization to fractional equations. Specifically, for the first type of potentials, the results obtained give the existence of a crystal configuration with atoms located at both extrema in an unstable rest position, up to an arbitrarily small modification of the structural potential and a “pinch” of a particle at any given position. For the second type of potentials, the results obtained also entail the existence of a crystal configuration reaching an equilibrium at infinity, up to an arbitrarily small superquadratic perturbation of the classical Peierls–Nabarro potential.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we construct homoclinic solutions for systems of equations
under suitable structural conditions on the potential. The results
obtained are confronted with the Peierls–Nabarro theory of crystal dislocations, providing new information on cases of interest.

From the technical point of view, the results obtained can be seen as a natural fractional counterpart of some classical discoveries by
Paul H. Rabinowitz and Kazunaga Tanaka
[
17
,
16
]
.
The proofs, however, deviate substantially from the classical techniques, which
hinged mostly on variational methods and analysis of ordinary differential equations.
Indeed, due to the lack of a convenient Hamiltonian formalism in the nonlocal setting,
an important ingredient in our analysis consists in suitable versions of the elliptic regularity theory, combined with appropriate energy estimates to locate the critical levels, thus
obtaining regularity bootstrap.

Two types of potentials will be considered. The first scenario, which will be detailed in Section
1.1
, focuses on the case of a potential with a strict global maximum.
The second, presented in Section
1.2
, allows the potential to have spatial dependency
and accounts for the Ambrosetti–Rabinowitz condition in combination with a quadratic term with spatially dependent growth at infinity.

. In particular,

Thus, by evaluating (

, we have that

which is a contradiction. This proves that

We emphasize that the non-existence of homoclinic solutions is not an uncommon phenomenon. On this topic, we refer the interested reader, e.g., to the non-existence result obtained in

for the case of a multi-well potential.

An interesting feature which distinguishes Theorem

One might wonder if the same choice can be made in the complementary case

Proposition 2.2
.

Let

. Then, for any
\varepsilon>0
and

there exists an even function

such that
q_{\varepsilon}(0)=M
and

(2.2)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}I(q_{\varepsilon})=0.

Proof.

We first suppose that

. In this case, let

q_{*}(x):=\begin{cases}\log\big(1-\log|x|\big)&\mbox{ if }x\in(-1,0)\cup(0,1),\\
{+\infty}&\mbox{ if }x=0,\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

By
[
10

we know that

. We define

\theta:=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{ if }s\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right),\\
\frac{1}{\varepsilon}&\mbox{ if }s=\frac{1}{2}\end{cases}

and

(2.3)

We observe that

(2.4)

|q_{\sharp}(x)-q_{\sharp}(y)|\leqslant\frac{|q_{*}(x)-q_{*}(y)|}{\theta},

is bounded and compactly supported, we also have that

As a matter of fact, we claim that

\begin{split}&[q_{\sharp}]^{2}_{s}=\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus(-1,1)}|2\pi\xi|^{2s}|\widehat{q_{\sharp}}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi+\int_{(-1,1)}|2\pi\xi|^{2s}|\widehat{q_{\sharp}}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\\

which establishes (
2.5
).

Now, we set

By definition,

,

is even and

.

3.
Preliminary results towards the proofs of Theorems
1.2
and
1.3

The proof of Theorem

comes through the study of the functional

in (

). To this aim, we gather in this section some auxiliary observations.

We start by proving that, given a measurable function
q:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we can decrease the seminorm of
q
by performing a cutoff procedure (in the sense of (
1.6
)).
This fact, together with assumption (
1.7
), will allow us to “lower” the total energy.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
R>0
.
For any measurable function
q:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let

\mathcal{T}_{R}(q):=(T_{R}(q_{1}),\dots,T_{R}(q_{n})),

where, for all
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
,
T_{R}(q_{i})
is defined as in (
1.6
).

Then,

[\mathcal{T}_{R}(q)]^{2}_{s}\leqslant[q]^{2}_{s}.

Proof.

We realize the cutoff procedure into two steps.
For any
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, let

\underline{T}_{R}(q_{i}):=\min\{q_{i},R\}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\overline{T}_{R}(q_{i}):=\max\{q_{i},-R\}.

By (
1.6
) we infer that

T_{R}(q_{i})=\overline{T}_{R}(\underline{T}_{R}(q_{i}))=\underline{T}_{R}(\overline{T}_{R}(q_{i})).

Moreover, for any
x
,
y\in\mathbb{R}
, we have that

\displaystyle\big|(\underline{T}_{R}(q_{i}))(x)-(\underline{T}_{R}(q_{i}))(y)\big|\leqslant|q_{i}(x)-q_{i}(y)|

and

\displaystyle\big|(\overline{T}_{R}(q_{i}))(x)-(\overline{T}_{R}(q_{i}))(y)\big|^{2}\leqslant|q_{i}(x)-q_{i}(y)|^{2}.

From these observations we deduce that

\begin{split}\left[\mathcal{T}_{R}(q)\right]^{2}_{s}&=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{|T_{R}(q_{i}(x))-T_{R}(q_{i}(y))|^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,dx\,dy\\
&=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{|\overline{T}_{R}(\underline{T}_{R}(q_{i}(x)))-\overline{T}_{R}(\underline{T}_{R}(q_{i}(y)))|^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,dx\,dy\\
&\leqslant\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{|\overline{T}_{R}(q_{i}(x))-\overline{T}_{R}(q_{i}(y))|^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,dx\,dy\\
&\leqslant\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{|q_{i}(x)-q_{i}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,dx\,dy\\
&=[q]^{2}_{s},\end{split}

which gives the desired result.
∎

Now, we point out that if the solution
q
of (
1.4
) is sufficiently regular and
V
satisfies (
1.5
), then
q
tends to
0
as

goes to infinity. This is a standard observation,
but since we need it to prove the claim in (

), we make it explicit via the following statement.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
V\in C(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R})
satisfy (
1.5
). Assume that
q\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
is uniformly continuous and
I(q)<+\infty
.

Then,

\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}q(x)=0.

Proof.

We establish the limit as
x\to+\infty
, the other one being similar.
For this, assume by contradiction that there exist
\varepsilon>0
and a sequence
x^{j}
such that
x^{j}\to+\infty
as
j\to+\infty
and

|q(x^{j})|>\varepsilon.

Without loss of generality, we can suppose that
x^{j+1}>x^{j}+1
.

By the uniform continuity of
q
, one can find
\delta\in(0,1/2)
small enough such that, for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
and all
x\in[x^{j}-\delta,x^{j}+\delta]
,

|q(x)|>\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

We set

S:=\varepsilon+\displaystyle\sup_{\mathbb{R}}|q|\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad\sigma_{\varepsilon,S}:=\inf_{q\in B_{S}\setminus B_{\varepsilon/2}}\,(-V(q)).

By (
1.5
), we have that
\sigma_{\varepsilon,S}>0
. Thus, recalling (
1.9
), we get that

I(q)=\frac{1}{2}[q]^{2}_{s}-\int_{\mathbb{R}}V(q(x))\,dx\geqslant-\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\int_{x^{j}-\delta}^{x^{j}+\delta}V(q(x))\,dx\geqslant\sigma_{\varepsilon,S}\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}(2\delta)=+\infty,

which contradicts the assumption
I(q)<+\infty
. Hence the desired result is proved.
∎

We now prove that if
q
is regular enough, then also (
1.15
) is verified. We point out that property (
1.15
) holds also in the local setting considered in
[
16
]
. However, the arguments which will lead to its proof in the nonlocal scenario
are entirely different from the one in
[
16
]
and rely upon a regularity argument (exploiting the forthcoming Lemma
4.8
and Corollary
4.9
, while
[
16
]
can use elementary methods from ordinary differential equations). To this end, we recall the following elementary observation:

Lemma 3.3

(Decay of the derivative)
.

Assume that
q\in C^{\eta}(\mathbb{R}\setminus[a-1/2,b+1/2],\mathbb{R}^{n})
, for some
\eta>1
, and that

(3.1)

\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}q(x)=c\in\mathbb{R}.

Then,

\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}\dot{q}(x)=0.

Proof.

We establish the limit as
x\to+\infty
, the other one being similar. To this end, let
\varepsilon>0
and observe that

q(x+\varepsilon)-q(x)=\int_{x}^{x+\varepsilon}\dot{q}(\tau)\,d\tau=\varepsilon\dot{q}(x)+\int_{x}^{x+\varepsilon}\big(\dot{q}(\tau)-\dot{q}(x)\big)\,d\tau.

This gives that

\dot{q}(x)=\frac{q(x+\varepsilon)-q(x)}{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{x}^{x+\varepsilon}\big(\dot{q}(x)-\dot{q}(\tau)\big)\,d\tau.

Now, recalling that
q\in C^{\eta}(\mathbb{R}\setminus[a-1/2,b+1/2),\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\eta>1
,
for
x
sufficiently large we have that

\begin{split}|\dot{q}(x)|&\leqslant\frac{\big|q(x+\varepsilon)-q(x)\big|}{\varepsilon}+\frac{c_{1}}{\varepsilon}\int_{x}^{x+\varepsilon}|x-\tau|^{\eta-1}\,d\tau=\frac{\big|q(x+\varepsilon)-q(x)\big|}{\varepsilon}+\frac{c_{1}}{\eta}\,\varepsilon^{\eta-1},\end{split}

for some positive constant
c_{1}
.

Hence, by (
3.1
),

\lim_{x\to+\infty}|\dot{q}(x)|\leqslant\frac{c_{1}}{\eta}\,\varepsilon^{\eta-1}.

The desired result now follows by sending
\varepsilon\searrow 0
.
∎

We now deal with the case in which
a=-b
and
q_{0}
is even. Thanks to the next result, we will be able to prove that, under these assumptions, the functional

admits an even minimizer.
This observation will be useful to prove the last claim in Theorem

.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
q
,
q_{*}\in\Gamma
satisfy
I(q)
,
I(q_{*})<+\infty
. Let
V\in C(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R})
.
Let also

(3.2)

M(x):=\max\{q(x),q_{*}(x)\}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad m(x):=\min\{q(x),q_{*}(x)\}.

Then,

[M]^{2}_{s}+[m]^{2}_{s}\leqslant[q]^{2}_{s}+[q_{*}]^{2}_{s}

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}}V(M(x))\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}V(m(x))\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}V(q(x))\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}V(q_{*}(x))\,dx.

In particular,

I(M)+I(m)\leqslant I(q)+I(q_{*}).

In our setting, we will use this result when
a=-b
,
q_{0}
is an even function
and
q_{*}(x):=q(-x)
, so that
q_{*}
is a competitor for the minimization process over
\Gamma
,

[q_{*}]_{s}^{2}=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|q(-x)-q(-y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,dx\,dy=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|q(x)-q(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,dx\,dy=[q]_{s}^{2}

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}}V(q_{*}(x))\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}V(q(x))\,dx,

giving that
I(q)=I(q_{*})
.
The result in Lemma
3.4
is however of general flavor.

Proof of Lemma
3.4
.

Recalling the definitions in (
3.2
), we have that, for any
x
,
y\in\mathbb{R}
,

|m(x)-m(y)|^{2}+|M(x)-M(y)|^{2}\leqslant|q(x)-q(y)|^{2}+|q_{*}(x)-q_{*}(y)|^{2}

and therefore

\begin{split}[m]^{2}_{s}+[M]^{2}_{s}&=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|m(x)-m(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,dx\,dy+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|M(x)-M(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,dx\,dy\\
&\leqslant\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|q(x)-q(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,dx\,dy+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|q_{*}(x)-q_{*}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,dx\,dy\\
&\leqslant[q]^{2}_{s}+[q_{*}]^{2}_{s}.\end{split}

Moreover, by (
3.2
),

\int_{\mathbb{R}}V(m(x))\,dx=\int_{\{q(x)\leqslant q_{*}(x)\}}V(q(x))\,dx+\int_{\{q(x)>q_{*}(x)\}}V(q_{*}(x))\,dx

and, similarly,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}V(M(x))\,dx=\int_{\{q(x)\leqslant q_{*}(x)\}}V(q_{*}(x))\,dx+\int_{\{q(x)>q_{*}(x)\}}V(q(x))\,dx.

Hence,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}V(m(x))\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}V(M(x))\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}V(q(x))\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}V(q_{*}(x))\,dx.

These observations lead to the desired result.∎

In this section we prove Theorems

We will present a unified proof for Theorems

We now focus on the regularity claims in (

) and (

) and

(6.3)

\omega_{1}(x):=\inf_{|\xi|=1}W(x,\xi).

Then,
\omega_{1}(x)>0
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

Moreover, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
and any
q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|q|\geqslant 1
,

(6.4)

W(x,q)\geqslant\omega_{1}(x)|q|^{\mu}.

Proof.

We observe that the infimum in (
6.3
) is attained, due to the continuity of
W
, and it is strictly positive,
thanks to (
1.22
).

Now, for any
q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, we set

(6.5)

\widehat{q}:=\frac{q}{|q|}.

Consider the function
f:\mathbb{R}\times(0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
defined as

f(x,\rho):=\log\left(\frac{W(x,\rho\widehat{q})}{W(x,\widehat{q})}\right).

We notice that
f(x,1)=0
and that, by (
1.22
), for any
\rho>1
,

f(x,\rho)=f(x,\rho)-f(x,1)=\int_{1}^{\rho}\frac{\nabla_{q}W(x,r\widehat{q})\cdot r\widehat{q}}{W(x,r\widehat{q})}\,\frac{dr}{r}\geqslant\mu\int_{1}^{\rho}\frac{dr}{r}=\log(\rho^{\mu}).

This entails that, for any
\rho\geqslant 1
,

W(x,\rho\widehat{q})\geqslant W(x,\widehat{q})\,\rho^{\mu}.

Now, for any
q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|q|\geqslant 1
, we can take
\rho:=|q|
and, recalling the definition in (
6.5
), we get

W(x,q)\geqslant W\left(x,\frac{q}{|q|}\right)\,|q|^{\mu}\geqslant\omega_{1}(x)\,|q|^{\mu},

being
\omega_{1}(x)
as in (
6.3
). This proves the inequality in (
6.4
).
∎

We recall that our goal is to find homoclinic solutions to problem (
1.17
). To this end, we observe that if
q
is a uniformly continuous function and
q\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
q
tends to zero at infinity. More precisely, the following result holds true:

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
r\in[1,+\infty)
. Assume that
q\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
q
is uniformly continuous.

Then,

\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}q(x)=0.

Proof.

We establish the limit as
x\to+\infty
, the other one being similar.
Suppose by contradiction that there exist
\varepsilon>0
and a sequence
x^{j}
such that
x^{j}\to+\infty
as
j\to+\infty
and

|q(x^{j})|>\varepsilon.

Without loss of generality, we can suppose that
x^{j+1}>x^{j}+1
.

Since
q
is uniformly continuous, we can find
\delta\in(0,1/2)
small enough such that, for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
and all
x\in[x^{j}-\delta,x^{j}+\delta]
,

|q(x)|>\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Hence, we have that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|q(x)|^{r}\,dx\geqslant\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\int_{x^{j}-\delta}^{x^{j}+\delta}|q(x)|^{r}\,dx>\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\frac{\delta\,\varepsilon^{r}}{2^{r-1}}=+\infty,

which contradicts the assumption that
q\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
. This concludes the proof.
∎

7.
Functional setting

In this section we introduce the main functional analytic setting needed in the study of problem (
1.17
).

Let
s\in(0,1)
and assume that
L\in C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})
is symmetric and satisfies (
1.18
). For any measurable function
q:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define

(7.1)

\|q\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}:=\left([q]^{2}_{s}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)q(x)\cdot q(x)\,dx\right)^{1/2}

and we point out that

(7.2)

\|\cdot\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}
is a norm.

We postpone the proof of this claim in Appendix
A
.

Now we define the space
\widetilde{H}^{s}
as the
set of measurable functions
q:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\|q\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}<+\infty
.

We remark that
\widetilde{H}^{s}
is a Hilbert space with scalar product

\langle q,\overline{q}\rangle_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}=\left(c_{s}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{(q(x)-q(y))\cdot(\overline{q}(x)-\overline{q}(y))}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,dx\,dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)q(x)\cdot\overline{q}(x)\,dx\right)^{1/2}.

For completeness, we establish this claim in Appendix
B
.

In fact, we will prove also a stronger result, namely:

Proposition 7.1
.

The space
\widetilde{H}^{s}
coincides with the completion of
C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
with respect to the norm in (
7.2
).

The proof of this result is technically delicate and deferred to Appendix
C

for the facility of the reader.

Furthermore, we notice that, in view of (
1.18
),

(7.3)

\begin{split}\|q\|^{2}_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}&=[q]^{2}_{s}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)q(x)\cdot q(x)\,dx\\
&\geqslant\min\{\alpha,1\}\left([q]^{2}_{s}+\|q\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}\right)\\
&=\min\{\alpha,1\}\|q\|^{2}_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}.\end{split}

We now present a preliminary embedding result.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
0<s_{1}<s_{2}<1
. Then, for any measurable function
q:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\|q\|^{2}_{\widetilde{H}^{s_{1}}}\leqslant\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}+1\right)\|q\|^{2}_{\widetilde{H}^{s_{2}}},

being
\alpha
given in (
1.18
).

In particular, the space
\widetilde{H}^{s_{2}}
is continuously embedded in
\widetilde{H}^{s_{1}}
.

Proof.

To check this, it is convenient to write the Gagliardo seminorm in terms of the Fourier transform (see
[
5
]
) as

[q]_{s_{1}}=\left(\;\int_{\mathbb{R}}|2\pi\xi|^{2s_{1}}\,|\widehat{q}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

From this, we have that

\displaystyle[q]_{s_{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1/(2\pi)}}|2\pi\xi|^{2s_{1}}\,|\widehat{q}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi+\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus B_{1/(2\pi)}}|2\pi\xi|^{2s_{2}}\,|\widehat{q}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widehat{q}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi+\int_{\mathbb{R}}|2\pi\xi|^{2s_{2}}\,|\widehat{q}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|q\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}+[q]^{2}_{s_{2}}.

This, (
7.1
) and (
1.18
) give that

\begin{split}\|q\|^{2}_{\widetilde{H}^{s_{1}}}&=[q]^{2}_{s_{1}}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)q(x)\cdot q(x)\,dx\\
&\leqslant[q]^{2}_{s_{2}}+\|q\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)q(x)\cdot q(x)\,dx\\
&\leqslant[q]^{2}_{s_{2}}+\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}+1\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)q(x)\cdot q(x)\,dx\\
&\leqslant\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}+1\right)\|q\|^{2}_{\widetilde{H}^{s_{2}}},\end{split}

as desired.
∎

It is worth noting that the space
\widetilde{H}^{s}
is continuously embedded in suitable Lebesgue spaces, depending on where the parameter
s
falls within the fractional range
(0,1)
. Precisely, we have the next result:

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
\alpha
be as in (
1.18
),
s\in(0,1)
and
q\in\widetilde{H}^{s}
.

Then, the following statements hold true:

i)

if
s\in(0,1/2)
, there exists a positive constant
\overline{c}
depending only on
n
,
s
and
\alpha
such that

(7.4)

\|q\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leqslant\overline{c}\,\|q\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}\quad\mbox{ for any }r\in[2,2^{*}_{s}],

namely the space
\widetilde{H}^{s}
is continuously embedded in
L^{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
for any
r\in[2,2^{*}_{s}]
;

ii)

if
s=1/2
, there exists a positive constant
\widehat{c}
depending only on
n
,
s
and
\alpha
such that

(7.5)

\|q\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leqslant\widehat{c}\,\|q\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}\quad\mbox{ for any }r\in[2,+\infty),

i.e. the space
\widetilde{H}^{s}
is continuously embedded in
L^{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
for any
r\in[2,+\infty)
;

iii)

if
s\in(1/2,1)
, there exists a positive constant
\widetilde{c}
depending only on
n
,
s
and
\alpha
such that

(7.6)

\|q\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leqslant\widetilde{c}\,\|q\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}\quad\mbox{ for any }r\in[2,+\infty],

i.e. the space
\widetilde{H}^{s}
is continuously embedded in
L^{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
for any
r\in[2,+\infty]
.

Proof.

Notice that inequality (
7.3
) entails that the space
\widetilde{H}^{s}
is continuously embedded
H^{s}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Let us consider the case
s\in(0,1/2)
. By
[
5
, Theorem 6.7]
and (
7.3
), there exists a constant
c_{1}
, depending on
n
and
s
, such that

\|q\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leqslant c_{1}\|q\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leqslant\frac{c_{1}}{\min\{\sqrt{\alpha},1\}}\|q\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}},

for any
r\in[2,2^{*}_{s}]
. Thus, (
7.4
) holds true with
\overline{c}:=c_{1}/\min\{\sqrt{\alpha},1\}
.

Similarly, when
s=1/2
, (
7.5
) follows from (
7.3
) and
[
5
, Theorem 6.10]
.

We address the case
s\in(1/2,1)
. By
[
5
, Theorem 8.2]
we have that
q\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})

and there exists a constant
c_{2}
depending only
n
and
s
such that

\|q\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leqslant c_{2}\|q\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

From this and the fact that
q\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
, by interpolation we have that
q\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
for any
r\in[2,+\infty]
and

\|q\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leqslant c_{3}\|q\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}\quad\mbox{ for any }r\in[2,+\infty],

with
c_{3}>0
depending only on
n
and
s
.

Hence, combining the latter inequality with (
7.3
), we obtain that

\|q\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leqslant c_{3}\|q\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leqslant\frac{c_{3}}{\min\{\sqrt{\alpha},1\}}\|q\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}\quad\mbox{ for any }r\in[2,+\infty],

namely, (
7.6
) holds true
with
\widetilde{c}:=c_{3}/\min\{\sqrt{\alpha},1\}
. This concludes the proof.
∎

In addition, we provide a convergence result for bounded sequences in
\widetilde{H}^{s}
.

Proposition 7.4
.

Let
q^{j}
be a bounded sequence in
\widetilde{H}^{s}
.

Then, the following statements hold true:

i)

if
s\in(0,1/2)
, there exists
q\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that, up to subsequences,

(7.7)

q^{j}\to q\quad\mbox{ in }L^{r}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})\quad\mbox{ for any }r\in[2,2^{*}_{s});

ii)

if
s=1/2
, there exists
q\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that, up to subsequences,

(7.8)

q^{j}\to q\quad\mbox{ in }L^{r}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})\quad\mbox{ for any }r\in[2,+\infty);

iii)

if
s\in(1/2,1)
, there exists
q\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that, up to subsequences,

(7.9)

q^{j}\to q\quad\mbox{ in }L^{r}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})\quad\mbox{ for any }r\in[2,+\infty].

Proof.

Let
q^{j}
be a bounded sequence in
\widetilde{H}^{s}
. Hence, we see that
q^{j}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
and, by inequality (
7.3
),

\|q^{j}\|^{2}_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leqslant\frac{1}{\min\{\alpha,1\}}\|q^{j}\|^{2}_{\widetilde{H}^{s}},

which implies that
q^{j}
is bounded in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
as well. Now, if
s\in(0,1/2)
, then (
7.7
) follows from
[
5
, Corollary 7.2]
.

Let us prove the statement in
ii)
.
To this end, let
q^{j}
be a bounded sequence in
\widetilde{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}
and let
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
. By applying Lemma
7.2
with

s_{1}:=\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad s_{2}:=\frac{1}{2},

we infer that
q^{j}
is bounded in
\widetilde{H}^{\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}
as well.
Hence, we can exploit the statement in
i)
, thus obtaining that there exists
q\in L^{r}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

q^{j}\to q\quad\mbox{ in }L^{r}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R})\quad\mbox{ for any }r\in\left[1,2^{*}_{\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}\right).

Now, since

2^{*}_{\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}=\frac{2}{1-2\left(\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon\right)}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon},

by sending
\varepsilon\searrow 0
we deduce (
7.8
).

Finally, if
s\in(1/2,1)
, we have that (
7.9
) is a consequence of
[
5
, Theorem 8.2]
and Ascoli-Arzelà Theorem.

We remark that
q\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
, since, by Lemma
7.3
and Fatou’s Lemma,

\|q\|^{r}_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leqslant\liminf_{j\to+\infty}\|q^{j}\|^{r}_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leqslant C\liminf_{j\to+\infty}\|q^{j}\|^{r}_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}\leqslant C,

up to renaming
C>0
.
∎

We now introduce the variational formulation of problem (
1.17
).
The energy functional
I:\widetilde{H}^{s}\to\mathbb{R}
associated with problem (
1.17
) is given by

(7.10)

I(q)=\frac{c_{s}}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|q(x)-q(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,dx\,dy+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)q(x)\cdot q(x)\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}}W(x,q(x))\,dx.

Moreover, it is easy to check that
I\in C^{1}(\widetilde{H}^{s},\mathbb{R})
and, for any
q
,
\varphi\in\widetilde{H}^{s}
,

(7.11)

\begin{split}\langle I^{\prime}(q),\varphi\rangle&=c_{s}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{(q(x)-q(y))\cdot(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,dx\,dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)q(x)\cdot\varphi(x)\,dx\\
&\qquad\qquad-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\nabla_{q}W(x,q(x))\cdot\varphi(x)\,dx.\end{split}

We now show that, in this framework, the geometry of the Mountain Pass Theorem is respected.

It is worth noting that the proof of the next geometric property is influenced by the location of the exponent
s
within the fractional range
(0,1)
.
Indeed, as stated in Lemma
7.3
, if
s\in(1/2,1)
, we can take advantage of the embedding into the space
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
, unlike the complementary case
s\in(0,1/2]
.
For this reason, we distinguish two cases, according to whether
s\in(0,1/2]
or
s\in(1/2,1)
.
Clearly, this has also influenced our choice in the assumptions on the potential
W
, as it is evident from the next two results.

Proposition 7.5
.

Let
s\in(0,1/2]
. Let
W\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R})
satisfy (
1.21
) and (
1.28
).

Then, there exist
\rho_{1}
,
\beta_{1}>0
such that
I(q)\geqslant\beta_{1}

for any
q\in\widetilde{H}^{s}
with
\|q\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}=\rho_{1}
.

Proof.

Let
q\in\widetilde{H}^{s}
. By (
7.10
), (
7.4
), (
7.5
) and Lemma
6.3

(recall that here
p\in(2,2^{*}_{s})
), we infer that for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\begin{split}I(q)&\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\|q\|^{2}_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|q|^{2}\,dx-\frac{\sigma(\varepsilon)}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|q|^{p}\,dx\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\|q\|^{2}_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\|q\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}-\frac{\sigma(\varepsilon)}{p}\|q\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}\\
&\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\big(1-\varepsilon\,{\overline{c}}^{2}\big)\,\|q\|^{2}_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}-\frac{\sigma(\varepsilon)\,{\overline{c}}^{p}}{p}\|q\|^{p}_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}.\end{split}

Therefore, taking
\varepsilon:=1/(2{\overline{c}}^{2})
, we deduce that

I(q)\geqslant\frac{1}{4}\|q\|^{2}_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}\left(1-\frac{4\sigma}{p}\|q\|^{p-2}_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}\right),

where now
\sigma=\sigma(1/(2{\overline{c}}^{2}))
.

Now we choose
\rho_{1}>0
sufficiently small such that

1-\frac{4\sigma}{p}\,\rho_{1}^{p-2}>0.

In this way, if
\|q\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}=\rho_{1}
, we have that

I(q)\geqslant\frac{1}{4}\rho_{1}^{2}\left(1-\frac{4\sigma}{p}\,\rho_{1}^{p-2}\right)=:\beta_{1}>0.

Hence, the desired result is proved.
∎

Proposition 7.6
.

Let
s\in(1/2,1)
. Let
W\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R})
satisfy (
1.21
) and (
1.22
).

Then, there exist
\rho_{2}
,
\beta_{2}>0
such that
I(q)\geqslant\beta_{2}

for any
q\in\widetilde{H}^{s}
with
\|q\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}=\rho_{2}
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha
be as given by (
1.18
)
and take

(7.12)

\varepsilon:=\frac{\min\{\alpha,1\}}{4}.

By Lemma
6.1
, there exists
\delta>0
such that, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
and any
q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
with
|q|\leqslant\delta
,

(7.13)

0<W(x,q)<\varepsilon|q|^{2}.

Now, let
\widetilde{c}
be as in (
7.6
) and set
\rho_{2}:=\delta/\widetilde{c}
.
If
q\in\widetilde{H}^{s}
with
\|q\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}=\rho_{2}
, from the embedding result in (
7.6
) we deduce that, for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

|q(x)|\leqslant\|q\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leqslant\widetilde{c}\,\rho_{2}=\delta.

Accordingly, by (
7.13
) and (
7.3
) we infer that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}W(x,q(x))\,dx<\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}}|q(x)|^{2}\,dx\leqslant\frac{\varepsilon}{\min\{\alpha,1\}}\,\|q\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}=\frac{\varepsilon}{\min\{\alpha,1\}}\,\rho_{2}^{2}.

Recalling (
7.10
), this and (
7.12
) entail that,
for all
q\in\widetilde{H}^{s}
with
\|q\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}=\rho_{2}
,

I(q)=\frac{1}{2}\,\rho_{2}^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}}W(x,q(x))\,dx>\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\varepsilon}{\min\{\alpha,1\}}\right)\,\rho_{2}^{2}=\frac{1}{4}\,\rho_{2}^{2}=:\beta_{2}>0,

as desired.
∎

Let
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
be as in Propositions
7.5
and
7.6
, respectively, and set

(7.14)

\rho:=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\rho_{1}&{\mbox{ if }}s\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right],\\
\displaystyle\rho_{2}&{\mbox{ if }}s\in\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right).\end{cases}

We now prove another geometric property of the functional
I
. We notice that, contrary to what occurred in the previous result, in the present framework there is no need to distinguish between the cases
s\in(0,1/2]
and
s\in(1/2,1)
.

Proposition 7.7
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
and
\rho
be as in (
7.14
). Let
W\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R})
satisfy (
1.22
).

Then, there exists
q_{0}\in\widetilde{H}^{s}
such that
\|q_{0}\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}>\rho
and
I(q_{0})<0
.

Proof.

Let
t\geqslant 1
and

(7.15)

q_{\diamondsuit}\in\widetilde{H}^{s}
such that
|q_{\diamondsuit}|=1
in
(-1,1)
.

By (
7.10
), (
7.1
) and (
6.4
), we infer that

\begin{split}I(tq_{\diamondsuit})&=\frac{c_{s}t^{2}}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|q_{\diamondsuit}(x)-q_{\diamondsuit}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,dx\,dy+\frac{t^{2}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)q_{\diamondsuit}(x)\cdot q_{\diamondsuit}(x)\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}}W(x,tq_{\diamondsuit}(x))\,dx\\
&\leqslant\frac{t^{2}}{2}\|q_{\diamondsuit}\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}^{2}-t^{\mu}\int_{-1}^{1}\omega_{1}(x)|q_{\diamondsuit}(x)|^{\mu}\,dx\\
&=\frac{t^{2}}{2}\|q_{\diamondsuit}\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}^{2}-t^{\mu}\int_{-1}^{1}\omega_{1}(x)\,dx,\end{split}

with
\omega_{1}(x)
as in (
6.3
).

Let now

(7.16)

\overline{t}:=\max\left\{\rho,\,\left(\frac{2}{\|q_{\diamondsuit}\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}^{2}}\int_{-1}^{1}\omega_{1}(x)\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2-\mu}}\right\}+1.

Thus, taking
q_{0}:=\frac{\overline{t}q_{\diamondsuit}}{\|q_{\diamondsuit}\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}}
, we have that

\|q_{0}\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}>\rho\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad I(q_{0})<0,

as desired.
∎

Now we obtain a uniform bound for sequences approaching a given energy level.
This will be a useful ingredient in combination with the elliptic regularity theory,
in order to allow for a suitable bootstrap argument and provide uniform estimates on the
solutions of the main results.

Proposition 7.8
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
.
Let
W\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R})
satisfy (
1.22
). Let
c\in\mathbb{R}
and let
q^{j}
be a sequence in
\widetilde{H}^{s}
satisfying

(7.17)

\lim_{j\to+\infty}I(q^{j})=c

and

(7.18)

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\sup_{{\varphi\in\widetilde{H}^{s}}\atop{\|\varphi\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}=1}}|\langle I^{\prime}(q^{j}),\varphi\rangle|=0.

Then,
c\geqslant 0
and
q^{j}
is bounded in
\widetilde{H}^{s}
.

In particular, there exists a positive constant
c_{1}
, depending on
c
and
\mu

8.
Uniform bounds for a system of nonlocal equations with unbounded coefficients

In this section, we provide a bound in the

-class for solutions of (
1.17
).
This is an important step in our construction, and also a rather delicate issue.
Indeed, on the one hand, this type of results can be seen as an extension, for instance,
of a result in
[
7

to the case of systems of equations.
On the other hand, this extension is nontrivial and technically involved,
due to the system structure, which appears to be new in the literature.

Also, even when

differently from the existing literature, presents a linear term of order zero which gets multiplied by an unbounded coefficient (in the case
n=1
however the proof would be simpler,

\begin{split}&U_{k}=\|w_{k}\|_{L^{t}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{t}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|w_{\ell,k}(x)|^{t}\,dx=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\int_{\{w_{\ell,k}>0\}}w_{\ell,k}^{t}(x)\,dx\\
&\qquad\geqslant\int_{\{w_{j,k}>0\}}w_{j,k}^{t}(x)\,dx\geqslant\int_{\{w_{j,k}>2^{-(k+1)}\}}{w_{j,k}^{t}(x)\,dx}\\
&\qquad\geqslant 2^{-t(k+1)}\big|\{w_{j,k}>2^{-(k+1)}\}\big|\geqslant 2^{-t(k+1)}\big|\{w_{j,k+1}>0\}\big|,\end{split}

and thus, for all
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
,

\big|\{w_{j,k+1}>0\}\big|^{\xi_{\kappa}}\leqslant\big(2^{t(k+1)}U_{k}\big)^{\xi_{\kappa}}=2^{\xi_{\kappa}t(k+1)}\,U_{k}^{\xi_{\kappa}}.

Using this into (
8.26
) we thereby find that

(8.27)

\int_{\{w_{j,k+1}>0\}}h_{\kappa}(x)\,w_{j,k}(x)\,w_{j,k+1}(x)\,|q(x)|^{\gamma_{\kappa}}\,dx\leqslant C\,2^{\xi_{\kappa}t(k+1)}\,U_{k}^{\tau_{\kappa}},

where

\tau_{\kappa}:=\frac{2}{t}+\xi_{\kappa}.

We remark that (
8.25
) entails that

(8.28)

\tau_{\kappa}>\frac{2}{t}.

Now, combining (
8.23
) and (
8.27
), and recalling (
8.5
) and (
8.24
), we
conclude that

\displaystyle\|w_{k+1}\|^{2}_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}\leqslant C^{k}\sum_{{j=1,\dots,n}\atop{\kappa=1,\dots,K}}2^{\xi_{\kappa}t(k+1)}U_{k}^{\tau_{\kappa}}\leqslant C^{k}\,2^{(t-2)(k+1)}\sum_{{j=1,\dots,n}\atop{\kappa=1,\dots,K}}U_{k}^{\tau_{\kappa}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=n\,C^{k}\,2^{(t-2)(k+1)}\sum_{\kappa=1}^{K}U_{k}^{\tau_{\kappa}}\leqslant(C+1)^{k}\sum_{\kappa=1}^{K}U_{k}^{\tau_{\kappa}}.

That is, we can write

\|w_{k+1}\|^{2}_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}\leqslant\widetilde{C}^{k}\sum_{\kappa=1}^{K}U_{k}^{\tau_{\kappa}},

with

(8.29)

\widetilde{C}>1.

As a consequence of this, by (
8.1
), Lemma
7.3

and (
8.18
),

(8.30)

U_{k+1}^{\frac{2}{t}}\leqslant\widetilde{C}^{k}\sum_{i=\kappa}^{K}U_{k}^{\tau_{\kappa}}.

Now, by (
8.9
),

(8.31)

\begin{split}&U_{0}=\|w_{0}\|_{L^{t}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{t}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|w_{j,0}\|_{L^{t}(\mathbb{R})}^{t}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\phi_{j}^{+}\|_{L^{t}(\mathbb{R})}^{t}\\
&\qquad\leqslant\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\phi_{j}\|_{L^{t}(\mathbb{R})}^{t}=\|\phi\|_{L^{t}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{t}=\delta^{t}.\end{split}

As a matter of fact, we have that
U_{k}\leqslant U_{0}\leqslant 1
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

As a result, setting

(8.32)

\tau:=\min\{\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{K}\},

we deduce from (
8.30
) that, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

U_{k+1}^{\frac{2}{t}}\leqslant\widetilde{C}^{k}U_{k}^{\tau}.

This gives that, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

(8.33)

U_{k+1}\leqslant\widetilde{C}^{k}\,U_{k}^{\vartheta},

being
\vartheta:=t\tau/2
.
We point out that, thanks to (
8.28
) and (
8.32
),

(8.34)

\vartheta>1.

We now take

(8.35)

\delta:=\widetilde{C}^{-\frac{1}{t(\vartheta-1)^{2}}}\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad\mu:=\widetilde{C}^{-\frac{1}{\vartheta-1}}.

Notice that, thanks (
8.29
) and (
8.34
),
we have that
\mu\in(0,1)
.

We claim that, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

(8.36)

U_{k}\leqslant\delta^{t}\mu^{k}.

To check this, we argue by induction over
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
If
k=0
, then (
8.36
) follows by (
8.31
).

We now assume that (
8.36
) holds true for
k
and we prove it for
k+1
.
For this, we use (
8.33
) and the inductive assumption, we recall
the definitions in (
8.35
) and we find that

U_{k+1}\leqslant\widetilde{C}^{k}\,U_{k}^{\vartheta}\leqslant\widetilde{C}^{k}(\delta^{t}\mu^{k})^{\vartheta}=\widetilde{C}^{k}(\delta^{t}\mu^{k})^{1+(\vartheta-1)}=\delta^{t}\mu^{k}(\widetilde{C}\,\mu^{\vartheta-1})^{k}\,\delta^{t(\vartheta-1)}=\delta^{t}\mu^{k+1},

which completes the proof of (
8.36
).

Now, since
\mu\in(0,1)
, by (
8.36
) it follows that

(8.37)

\lim_{k\to+\infty}U_{k}=0.

Since
0\leqslant w_{j,k}\leqslant|\phi_{j}|\in L^{t}(\mathbb{R})
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
and

\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}{w_{j,k}}=(\phi_{j}-1)^{+}\quad\mbox{ a.e. in }\mathbb{R},

by (
8.37
) and the Dominated Convergence Theorem, we get that

0=\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}U_{k}=\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\|w_{k}\|^{t}_{L^{t}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|w_{j,k}\|^{t}_{L^{t}(\mathbb{R})}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|(\phi_{j}-1)^{+}\|_{L^{t}(\mathbb{R})}^{t}.

Therefore, for any
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, it holds that
\phi_{j}\leqslant 1
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}
.

Thus, recalling the definition of
\phi_{j}
in (
8.7
), we gather that,
for all
j\in\{,1\dots,n\}
,

q_{j}(x)\leqslant\frac{\|q\|_{L^{t}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{\delta}<+\infty,

as desired.
∎

9.
Setting up a barrier

The goal of this section is to construct a suitable barrier which will be useful to study the behaviour at infinity of the solution
q
to problem (
1.17
) when
s\in(0,1/2]
.
Our strategy relies upon the results presented in
[
15
, Theorem 2]
.

Throughout this section, we suppose that
s\in(0,1/2]

and that
L
satisfies the assumption in (
1.31
).

We apply
[
15
, Theorem 2]
with
\mathcal{W}:\mathbb{R}\to[0,+\infty)
given by

\mathcal{W}(x):=\frac{(1-x^{2})^{2}}{4}.

It is easy to check that it meets all the assumptions required by
[
15
, Theorem 2]
, namely
\mathcal{W}
is a smooth double-well potential with wells only at
\pm 1
and it satisfies
\mathcal{W}^{\prime\prime}(\pm 1)=2>0
.

Hence, there exists a strictly increasing function
\widetilde{\beta}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
which solves

(9.1)

(-\Delta)^{s}\widetilde{\beta}=\widetilde{\beta}-\widetilde{\beta}^{3}\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}.

In addition,
\widetilde{\beta}
is unique (up to translations) in the class of monotone solutions of (
9.1
) and there exists
C\geqslant 1
such that,
for any large
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

|\widetilde{\beta}(x)-\mbox{sign}(x)|\leqslant\frac{C}{|x|^{2s}}.

Let
\beta:=\widetilde{\beta}^{\prime}
and notice that
\beta
is a solution of

(9.2)

(-\Delta)^{s}\beta=a(x)\beta\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R},

where

a(x):=1-3\widetilde{\beta}^{2}(x).

We have that
\beta>0
and, by
[
15
, Theorem 2]
, for any large
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

(9.3)

\beta(x)\leqslant\frac{C}{|x|^{1+2s}}.

Also, we point out that

(9.4)

a(x)\in(-2,1]\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}a(x)=-2.

Now, let
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and
\eta>0
. We define

(9.5)

\displaystyle v_{j,\eta}(x)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle A\beta(x)-q_{j}(x)+\eta(1+x_{+}^{s}+x_{-}^{s})

(9.6)

\displaystyle{\mbox{and }}\quad w_{j,\eta}(x)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle A\beta(x)+q_{j}(x)+\eta(1+x_{+}^{s}+x_{-}^{s}),

with
A>0
(later on,
A
will be chosen sufficiently large).

Lemma 9.1
.

Let
W\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R})
satisfy (
1.21
) and (
1.28
) and
let
q\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a solution of (
1.17
).

Let
v_{j,\eta}
and
w_{j,\eta}
be
as in (
9.5
) and (
9.6
), respectively.

Then, there exists
A_{0}>0
such that if
A\geqslant A_{0}
, for any
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, any
\eta>0
and any
x\in\mathbb{R}
, we have that

(9.7)

v_{j,\eta}(x)\geqslant 0\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad w_{j,\eta}(x)\geqslant 0.

Proof.

We prove the first claim in (
9.7
). For this, we suppose by contradiction that
there exist
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}

and
\eta>0
such that

(9.8)

\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}v_{j,\eta}(x)<0.

Let
x_{m}\in\mathbb{R}

be a minimizing sequences for the function
v_{j,\eta}
. That is

(9.9)

\lim_{m\to+\infty}v_{j,\eta}(x_{m})=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}v_{j,\eta}(x)<0.

We notice that there exists
M_{j,\eta}>0
such that

(9.10)

x_{m}\in[-M_{j,\eta},M_{j,\eta}].

Indeed, if
x_{m}\to\pm\infty
as
m\to+\infty
, then by (
9.5
) and (
9.3
), and recalling that
q\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
, it would follow that

\lim_{m\to+\infty}v_{j,\eta}(x_{m})=\lim_{m\to+\infty}\Big(A\beta(x_{m})-q_{j}(x_{m})+\eta\big(1+(x_{m})_{+}^{s}+(x_{m})_{-}^{s}\big)\Big)=+\infty,

which gives a contradiction with (
9.9
). Hence, (
9.10
) is established.

Furthermore, we point out that
(-\Delta)^{s}q_{j}\in L^{\infty}([-M_{j,\eta}-1,M_{j,\eta}+1])

by (
1.17
), and therefore, by fractional elliptic regularity theory, we have that
q_{j}

is continuous in
[-M_{j,\eta},M_{j,\eta}]
.
Consequently, in light of (
9.10
),
we infer that there exists
\overline{x}_{j,\eta}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(9.11)

v_{j,\eta}(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}v_{j,\eta}(x)<0.

This and (
9.5
) entail that

(9.12)

q_{j}(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})>A\beta(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})+\eta(1+|\overline{x}_{j,\eta}|^{s})>0.

In view of (
1.31
), we pick
\overline{R}\geqslant D
such that,
if
|x|\geqslant\overline{R}
, we have that
d_{j}(x)>3+a_{0}\big(1+\|q\|^{p-2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}\big)
.
Recall that
a_{0}>0
and
p\in(2,2^{*}_{s})
here are given by (
1.28
).

Moreover, due to (
9.4
),
there exists
\widetilde{R}>0
such that, if
|x|\geqslant\widetilde{R}
, then
a(x)\in(-2,-1]
.

We also recall that, for all
x>0
,

(9.13)

(-\Delta)^{s}x^{s}_{-}=-\frac{C_{s}}{|x|^{s}},

for some
C_{s}>0
, depending only on
s
, see e.g.
[
3
, Theorem 3.4.1]
.

We now choose

(9.14)

R_{0}:=\max\left\{\overline{R},\widetilde{R},1+C_{s}^{\frac{1}{2s}}\right\}

and
we see that, if
|x|\geqslant R_{0}
,

(9.15)

d_{j}(x)>3+a_{0}\Big(1+\|q\|^{p-2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Big)\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad a(x)\in(-2,-1].

We now define

(9.16)

\varsigma_{R_{0}}:=\min_{x\in[-R_{0},R_{0}]}\beta(x)>0\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad A_{R_{0}}:=\frac{\|q\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{\varsigma_{R_{0}}}.

By (
9.12
) we know that
\|q\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}>A\beta(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})
and consequently,
if
A>A_{R_{0}}
, for all
x\in[-R_{0},R_{0}]
,

\beta(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})<\frac{\|q\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{A}<\frac{\|q\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{A_{R_{0}}}=\varsigma_{R_{0}}\leqslant\beta(x).

For this reason, if
A>A_{R_{0}}
,
we have that
|\overline{x}_{j,\eta}|>R_{0}
.

This and (
9.15
) give that, if
A>A_{R_{0}}
,

(9.17)

d_{j}(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})>3+a_{0}\Big(1+\|q\|^{p-2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Big)\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad a(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})\in(-2,-1].

Now, suppose that
\overline{x}_{j,\eta}>R_{0}
(the case
\overline{x}_{j,\eta}<-R_{0}
is analogous
and can be dealt with replacing
(-\Delta)^{s}x_{-}^{s}
with
(-\Delta)^{s}x_{+}^{s}
in
the forthcoming formula (
9.18
)).
In this case, by (
9.5
), (
9.2
) and (
1.17
), we infer that

\begin{split}0&\geqslant(-\Delta)^{s}v_{j,\eta}(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})\\
&=A\,a(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})\beta(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}L_{i,j}(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})q_{i}(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})-\partial_{q_{j}}W(\overline{x}_{j,\eta},q(\overline{x}_{j,\eta}))+\eta(-\Delta)^{s}x_{-}^{s}(\overline{x}_{j,\eta}).\end{split}

Now we point out that, in light of the assumption in (
1.31
),

(9.19)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}L_{i,j}(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})q_{i}(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{ij}d_{i}(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})q_{i}(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})=d_{j}(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})q_{j}(\overline{x}_{j,\eta}).

Plugging this information and (
9.13
)
into (
9.18
), we conclude that

\frac{\eta\,C_{s}}{\overline{x}_{j,\eta}^{s}}\geqslant A\,a(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})\beta(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})+d_{j}(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})q_{j}(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})-\partial_{q_{j}}W(\overline{x}_{j,\eta},q(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})).

Using (
1.28
), we thus get that

\displaystyle\frac{\eta\,C_{s}}{\overline{x}_{j,\eta}^{s}}

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle A\,a(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})\beta(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})+d_{j}(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})q_{j}(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})-a_{0}\,q_{j}(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})\Big(1+|q(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})|^{p-2}\Big)

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle A\,a(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})\beta(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})+d_{j}(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})q_{j}(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})-a_{0}\,q_{j}(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})\Big(1+\|q\|^{p-2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Big).

Thus, recalling
the definition of
v_{j,\eta}
in (
9.5
), and also (
9.11
) and the second claim in (
9.17
),
we obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{\eta\,C_{s}}{\overline{x}_{j,\eta}^{s}}

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle a(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})\Big(v_{j,\eta}(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})+q_{j}(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})-\eta(1+\overline{x}_{j,\eta}^{s})\Big)+d_{j}(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})q_{j}(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})-a_{0}\,q_{j}(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})\Big(1+\|q\|^{p-2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Big)

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle a(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})q_{j}(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})+\eta(1+\overline{x}_{j,\eta}^{s})+d_{j}(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})q_{j}(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})-a_{0}\,q_{j}(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})\Big(1+\|q\|^{p-2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q_{j}(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})\left(a(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})+d_{j}(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})-a_{0}\Big(1+\|q\|^{p-2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Big)\right)+\eta(1+\overline{x}_{j,\eta}^{s}).

Hence, exploiting also the first claim in (
9.17
) we deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{\eta\,C_{s}}{\overline{x}_{j,\eta}^{s}}

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle q_{j}(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})\left(a(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})+3+a_{0}\Big(1+\|q\|^{p-2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Big)-a_{0}\Big(1+\|q\|^{p-2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Big)\right)+\eta(1+\overline{x}_{j,\eta}^{s})

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle q_{j}(\overline{x}_{j,\eta})+\eta(1+\overline{x}_{j,\eta}^{s})

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\eta\,\overline{x}_{j,\eta}^{s}.

All in all, recalling the choice of
R_{0}
in (
9.14
), we conclude that

C_{s}^{\frac{1}{2s}}>\overline{x}_{j,\eta}>R_{0}\geqslant 1+C_{s}^{\frac{1}{2s}},

which gives the desired contradiction and establishes the first claim
in (
9.7
).

We now focus on the second claim in (
9.7
). The proof follows the same line as the one of the first claim, but we provide
the details here for the facility of the reader.

We argue by contradiction and suppose that there exist
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and
\eta>0

such that

\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}w_{j,\eta}(x)<0.

We consider a minimizing sequence
y_{m}
and, arguing as for (
9.10
), we point out that
y_{m}
is a bounded sequence
and therefore there exists
\overline{y}_{j,\eta}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

w_{j,\eta}(\overline{y}_{j,\eta})=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}w_{j,\eta}(x)<0.

In light of (
9.6
), this says that

-q_{j}(\overline{y}_{j,\eta})>A\beta(\overline{y}_{j,\eta})+\eta(1+\overline{y}_{j,\eta}^{s})>0.

We pick now
R_{0}
as in (
9.14
) and
A_{R_{0}}
as in (
9.16
)
and we see that, if
A>A_{R_{0}}
then
|\overline{y}_{j,\eta}|>R_{0}
.
This and (
9.15
) give that, if
A>A_{R_{0}}
,

d_{j}(\overline{y}_{j,\eta})>3+a_{0}\Big(1+\|q\|^{p-2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Big)\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad a(\overline{y}_{j,\eta})\in(-2,-1].

Now,
we suppose without loss of generality that
\overline{y}_{j,\eta}>R_{0}
and,
by (
9.6
), (
9.2
) and (
1.17
), we infer that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}w_{j,\eta}(\overline{y}_{j,\eta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A\,a(\overline{y}_{j,\eta})\beta(\overline{y}_{j,\eta})-\sum_{i=1}^{n}L_{i,j}(\overline{y}_{j,\eta})q_{i}(\overline{y}_{j,\eta})+\partial_{j}W(\overline{y}_{j,\eta},q(\overline{y}_{j,\eta}))+\eta(-\Delta)^{s}x_{-}^{s}(\overline{y}_{j,\eta}).

Thus, recalling (
1.28
), (
9.13
) and (
9.19
),

\displaystyle\frac{\eta\,C_{s}}{\overline{y}_{j,\eta}^{s}}

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle A\,a(\overline{y}_{j,\eta})\beta(\overline{y}_{j,\eta})-d_{j}(\overline{y}_{j,\eta})q_{j}(\overline{y}_{j,\eta})+a_{0}\,q_{j}(\overline{y}_{j,\eta})\Big(1+\|q\|^{p-2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Big)

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle a(\overline{y}_{j,\eta})\Big(w_{j,\eta}(\overline{y}_{j,\eta})-q_{j}(\overline{y}_{j,\eta})-\eta(1+\overline{y}_{j,\eta}^{s})\Big)-d_{j}(\overline{y}_{j,\eta})q_{j}(\overline{y}_{j,\eta})+a_{0}\,q_{j}(\overline{y}_{j,\eta})\Big(1+\|q\|^{p-2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Big)

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle-a(\overline{y}_{j,\eta})q_{j}(\overline{y}_{j,\eta})+\eta(1+\overline{y}_{j,\eta}^{s})-d_{j}(\overline{y}_{j,\eta})q_{j}(\overline{y}_{j,\eta})+a_{0}\,q_{j}(\overline{y}_{j,\eta})\Big(1+\|q\|^{p-2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-q_{j}(\overline{y}_{j,\eta})\left(a(\overline{y}_{j,\eta})+d_{j}(\overline{y}_{j,\eta})-a_{0}\Big(1+\|q\|^{p-2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Big)\right)+\eta(1+\overline{y}_{j,\eta}^{s})

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle-q_{j}(\overline{y}_{j,\eta})\left(a(\overline{y}_{j,\eta})+3+a_{0}\Big(1+\|q\|^{p-2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Big)-a_{0}\Big(1+\|q\|^{p-2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Big)\right)+\eta(1+\overline{y}_{j,\eta}^{s})

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle-q_{j}(\overline{y}_{j,\eta})+\eta(1+\overline{y}_{j,\eta}^{s})

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\eta\overline{y}_{j,\eta}^{s}.

As a consequence,

C_{s}^{\frac{1}{2s}}>\overline{y}_{j,\eta}>R_{0}\geqslant 1+C_{s}^{\frac{1}{2s}},

which gives the desired contradiction and establishes the second claim
in (
9.7
) as well.
∎

By Lemma
9.1
we deduce the following result:

Corollary 9.2
.

Let
W\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R})
satisfy (
1.21
) and (
1.28
) and let

be a solution of (
1.17
).

Then,

\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}q(x)=0.

Proof.

Lemma
9.1
gives that, if
A
is sufficiently large, for any
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, any
\eta>0
and any
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

|q_{j}(x)|\leqslant A\beta(x)+\eta(1+x_{+}^{s}+x_{-}^{s}).

Taking the limit as
\eta\searrow 0
,

|q_{j}(x)|\leqslant A\beta(x).

As a consequence,
recalling (
9.3
), we obtain the desired limit.
∎

10.
Proofs of Theorems
1.4
and

In this section we suppose that the assumptions of Theorems
1.4
(when
s\in(1/2,1)
)
and
1.5
(when

) are satisfied.

We introduce the following notation. We recall the space
\widetilde{H}^{s}
introduced at the beginning
of Section
7
and the functional
I
in (
7.10
) and we
let
q_{0}
be given by Proposition

. We set

(10.1)

\mathcal{H}:=\Big\{h\in C([0,1],\widetilde{H}^{s})\;{\mbox{ s.t. }}\;h(0)=0\mbox{ and }h(1)=q_{0}\Big\}

Appendix A
Proof of formula (
7.2
)

Here we check that
\|\cdot\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}
is a norm, thus establishing the claim in formula (
7.2
).

We first check the triangle inequality. To this aim, we claim that, for any
q
,
\overline{q}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(A.1)

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)q(x)\cdot\overline{q}(x)\,dx\right|\leqslant\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)q(x)\cdot q(x)\,dx\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)\overline{q}(x)\cdot\overline{q}(x)\,dx\right)^{1/2}.

Indeed, we suppose that
q\not\equiv 0
, otherwise we are done, and therefore
\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)q(x)\cdot q(x)\,dx\neq 0
.
Thus, we can define

\beta:=\left(\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)\overline{q}(x)\cdot\overline{q}(x)\,dx}{\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)q(x)\cdot q(x)\,dx}\right)^{1/4}.

In light of (
1.18
) and the symmetry property of the matrix
L
, we have that

0\leqslant L\left(\beta q\pm\frac{\overline{q}}{\beta}\right)\cdot\left(\beta q\pm\frac{\overline{q}}{\beta}\right)=\beta^{2}Lq\cdot q+\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}L\overline{q}\cdot\overline{q}\pm 2Lq\cdot\overline{q}.

Accordingly,

\displaystyle 2\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)q(x)\cdot\overline{q}(x)\,dx\right|\leqslant\beta^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)q(x)\cdot q(x)\,dx+\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)\overline{q}(x)\cdot\overline{q}(x)\,dx.

Substituting the expression of
\beta
, we obtain (
A.1
).

Exploiting (
A.1
), we see that

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)\big(q(x)+\overline{q}(x)\big)\cdot\big(q(x)+\overline{q}(x)\Big)\,dx\\
&\qquad=\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)q(x)\cdot q(x)\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)\overline{q}(x)\cdot\overline{q}(x)\,dx+2\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)q(x)\cdot\overline{q}(x)\,dx\\
&\qquad\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)q(x)\cdot q(x)\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)\overline{q}(x)\cdot\overline{q}(x)\,dx\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad+2\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)q(x)\cdot q(x)\,dx\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)\overline{q}(x)\cdot\overline{q}(x)\,dx\right)^{1/2}\\
&\qquad=\left(\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)q(x)\cdot q(x)\,dx\right)^{1/2}+\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)\overline{q}(x)\cdot\overline{q}(x)\,dx\right)^{1/2}\right)^{2}.\end{split}

As a consequence of this, we have that, for any
q
,
\overline{q}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\|q+\overline{q}\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}=\left([q+\overline{q}]^{2}_{s}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)\big(q(x)+\overline{q}(x)\big)\cdot\big(q(x)+\overline{q}(x)\Big)\,dx\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\big([q]_{s}+[\overline{q}]_{s}\big)^{2}+\left(\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)q(x)\cdot q(x)\,dx\right)^{1/2}+\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)\overline{q}(x)\cdot\overline{q}(x)\,dx\right)^{1/2}\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left([q]_{s}^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)q(x)\cdot q(x)\,dx\right)^{1/2}+\left([\overline{q}]_{s}^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)\overline{q}(x)\cdot\overline{q}(x)\,dx\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\|q\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}+\|\overline{q}\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}},

which completes the proof of the triangle inequality.

The absolute homogeneity follows from the fact that, for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
q:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\|tq\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}=\left([tq]^{2}_{s}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)(tq)(x)\cdot(tq)(x)\,dx\right)^{1/2}=\left(t^{2}[q]^{2}_{s}+t^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)q(x)\cdot q(x)\,dx\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=|t|\left([q]^{2}_{s}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)q(x)\cdot q(x)\,dx\right)^{1/2}=|t|\|q\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}.

It remains to check the positivity property.
For this, we observe that, in light of (
1.18
),

\displaystyle\|q\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}=\left([q]^{2}_{s}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)q(x)\cdot q(x)\,dx\right)^{1/2}\geqslant\left([q]^{2}_{s}+\alpha\int_{\mathbb{R}}|q(x)|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}\geqslant 0.

This also implies that if
\|q\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}=0
, then
[q]_{s}=\|q\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}=0
, and therefore
q\equiv 0
.

Lemma B.1

(\widetilde{H}^{s},\|\cdot\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}})

Proof.

For any

(q,u)\mapsto\langle q,u\rangle_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}:=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{(q(x)-q(y))(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,dx\,dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)q(x)\cdot u(x)\,dx.

It is easy to check that
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}
is a scalar product and it induces the norm defined in (
7.1
). We show that
\widetilde{H}^{s}
is complete with respect to this norm.

To this end, let
q^{j}
denote a Cauchy sequence in
\widetilde{H}^{s}
and take
\varepsilon>0
. Thus, there exists
\overline{n}_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
such that if

,
m\geqslant\overline{n}_{\varepsilon}
, then

(B.1)

\varepsilon\geqslant\|q^{j}-q^{m}\|^{2}_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}\geqslant\min\{\alpha,1\}\|q^{j}-q^{m}\|^{2}_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where the last inequality is a consequence of (
7.3
).

Now, since
H^{s}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
is complete, there exists
q\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
q^{j}\to q
in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
as

. Moreover, we can extract a subsequence

such that
q^{j_{k}}\to q
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}
as

.

We claim that
q\in\widetilde{H}^{s}
. Indeed, by the hypothesis in (
1.18
) and Fatou’s Lemma,

\begin{split}&\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|q(x)-q(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,dx\,dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)q(x)\cdot q(x)\,dx\\
&\qquad\leqslant\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\left(\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|q^{j_{k}}(x)-q^{j_{k}}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,dx\,dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)q^{j_{k}}(x)\cdot q^{j_{k}}(x)\,dx\right)\\
&\qquad=\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\|q^{j_{k}}\|^{2}_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}\\
&\qquad\leqslant\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\Big(\|q^{j_{k}}-q^{\overline{n}_{1}}\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}+\|q^{\overline{n}_{1}}\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}\Big)^{2}\\
&\qquad\leqslant\Big(1+\|q^{\overline{n}_{1}}\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}\Big)^{2}\\
&\qquad<+\infty,\end{split}

where we also used the first inequality in (
B.1
) with
\varepsilon=1
. This proves that
q\in\widetilde{H}^{s}
.

To conclude the proof of Lemma
B.1
it remains to show that
q^{m}\to q
in
\widetilde{H}^{s}

as
m\to+\infty
. For this, let
m\geqslant\overline{n}_{\varepsilon}
. Thus, by (
B.1
), the assumption in (
1.18
) and Fatou’s Lemma, we get that

\begin{split}\varepsilon&\geqslant\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\left(\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|(q^{m}-q^{j_{k}})(x)-(q^{m}-q^{j_{k}})(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,dx\,dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)(q^{m}-q^{j_{k}})(x)\cdot(q^{m}-q^{j_{k}})(x)\,dx\right)\\
&\geqslant\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\frac{|(q^{m}-q^{j_{k}})(x)-(q^{m}-q^{j_{k}})(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,dx\,dy\\
&\qquad\qquad+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\left(L(x)(q^{m}-q^{j_{k}})(x)\cdot(q^{m}-q^{j_{k}})(x)\right)\,dx\\
&=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|(q^{m}-q)(x)-(q^{m}-q)(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,dx\,dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)(q^{m}-q)(x)\cdot(q^{m}-q)(x)\,dx\\
&=\|q^{m}-q\|^{2}_{\widetilde{H}^{s}},\end{split}

namely
q^{m}\to q
in
\widetilde{H}^{s}
as
m\to+\infty
, as desired.
∎

Appendix C
Proof of Proposition
7.1

To prove Proposition
7.1
, we observe that
the completion of
C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})

with respect to the norm of
\widetilde{H}^{s}
is obviously contained in
\widetilde{H}^{s}

and therefore we only need to establish the opposite inclusion.
That is, given
q\in\widetilde{H}^{s}
, our objective is to construct a sequence of functions
q_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

(C.1)

\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\|q-q_{\varepsilon}\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}=0

For this scope, we consider first a sequence of functions
\tau_{R}\in C^{\infty}_{0}([-R-1,R+1],[0,1])

such that
\tau_{R}=1
in
[-R,R]
and
|\tau_{R}^{\prime}|\leqslant 2
and we define, for each
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
,

(C.2)

q_{R,j}(x):=\tau_{R}(x)\,q_{j}(x).

We observe that

(C.3)

q_{R,j}
is supported in
[-R-1,R+1]
.

Besides, we know (see e.g.
[
13
, Lemma 1.63]
) that

(C.4)

\lim_{R\to+\infty}[q_{j}-q_{R,j}]_{s}=0.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)\big(q(x)-q_{R}(x)\big)\cdot\big(q(x)-q_{R}(x)\big)\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad=\lim_{R\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus[-R,R]}\big(1-\tau_{R}(x)\big)^{2}L(x)q(x)\cdot q(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\lim_{R\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus[-R,R]}L(x)q(x)\cdot q(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad=0.

From this and (
C.4
) it follows that

\lim_{R\to+\infty}\|q-q_{R}\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}=0.

In particular, given
\varepsilon>0
, we take
R_{\varepsilon}>0
sufficiently large such that

(C.5)

\|q-q_{R_{\varepsilon}}\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}\leqslant\varepsilon.

Our objective is now to take a smooth approximation of
q_{R_{\varepsilon}}
. For this, we consider
a mollification sequence. Namely, given
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{0}([-1,1],[0,+\infty))
with unit mass, we define, for
\delta\in(0,1)
,

\eta_{\delta}(x):=\frac{1}{\delta}\eta\left(\frac{x}{\delta}\right)\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad q_{\delta,\varepsilon,j}:=q_{R_{\varepsilon},j}*\eta_{\delta}.

We stress that

(C.6)

q_{\delta,\varepsilon,j}
is of class
C^{\infty}
and supported in
[-R_{\varepsilon}-2,R_{\varepsilon}+2]
.

due to (
C.3
).

We know (see e.g. equation (1.38) and the subsequent comment in
[
13
]
) that

(C.7)

\lim_{\delta\searrow 0}[q_{R_{\varepsilon},j}-q_{\delta,\varepsilon,j}]_{s}=0.

In view of (
C.3
) and (
C.6
), we also remark that

(C.8)

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)\big(q_{R_{\varepsilon}}(x)-q_{\delta,\varepsilon}(x)\big)\cdot\big(q_{R_{\varepsilon}}(x)-q_{\delta,\varepsilon}(x)\big)\,dx\\
&\qquad=\int_{[-R_{\varepsilon}-2,R_{\varepsilon}+2]}L(x)\big(q_{R_{\varepsilon}}(x)-q_{\delta,\varepsilon}(x)\big)\cdot\big(q_{R_{\varepsilon}}(x)-q_{\delta,\varepsilon}(x)\big)\,dx\\
&\qquad\leqslant\sup_{[-R_{\varepsilon}-2,R_{\varepsilon}+2]}|L|\,\big\|q_{R_{\varepsilon}}(x)-q_{\delta,\varepsilon}(x)\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}.\end{split}

Also, owing to (
C.2
), we have that
\|q_{R_{\varepsilon},j}(x)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant\|q_{j}(x)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}<+\infty
. Therefore (see e.g.
[
21
, Theorem 9.6]
) we conclude that

\lim_{\delta\searrow 0}\big\|q_{R_{\varepsilon},j}(x)-q_{\delta,\varepsilon,j}(x)\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=0.

Combining this information and (
C.8
), we gather that

\lim_{\delta\searrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)\big(q_{R_{\varepsilon}}(x)-q_{\delta,\varepsilon}(x)\big)\cdot\big(q_{R_{\varepsilon}}(x)-q_{\delta,\varepsilon}(x)\big)\,dx=0,

which, together with (
C.7
), gives that

\lim_{\delta\searrow 0}\|q_{R_{\varepsilon}}-q_{\delta,\varepsilon}\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}=0.

As a result, we can take
\delta_{\varepsilon}
sufficiently small such that
\|q_{R_{\varepsilon}}-q_{\delta_{\varepsilon},\varepsilon}\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}\leqslant\varepsilon
. Thus, using the short notation
q_{\varepsilon}:=q_{\delta_{\varepsilon},\varepsilon}
(which, by (
C.6
), is
a smooth and compactly supported function)
and recalling (
C.5
), we conclude that

\|q-q_{\varepsilon}\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}=\|q-q_{\delta_{\varepsilon},\varepsilon}\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}\leqslant\|q-q_{R_{\varepsilon}}\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}+\|q_{R,\varepsilon}-q_{\delta_{\varepsilon},\varepsilon}\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}\leqslant 2\varepsilon.

This establishes (
C.1
) and completes the proof of Proposition
7.1
.

Appendix D
Proof of formula (
7.26
)

In this appendix we provide the proof of formula (
7.26
).

To this end, we observe that

\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}[q^{j}]_{s}<+\infty\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)q^{j}(x)\cdot q^{j}(x)\,dx<+\infty.

Consequently (see (
1.18
) and e.g.
[
5
, Proposition 3.6]
), we have that

\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}q^{j}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}<+\infty\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\|q^{j}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}<+\infty.

Therefore, we can suppose that
there exist
g
,
q^{\star}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that,
up to subsequences,
(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}q^{j}
weakly converges
to
g
and
q^{j}
weakly converges to
q^{\star}

in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
, as
j\to+\infty
.

In particular, recalling also the convergence in (
7.25
),
for every bounded function
\psi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
with compact support, we have that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{q}(x)\cdot\psi(x)\,dx=\lim_{j\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}q^{j}(x)\cdot\psi(x)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}q^{\star}(x)\cdot\psi(x)\,dx.

This shows that

(D.1)

q^{\star}=\overline{q}.

Furthermore, for every
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

(D.2)

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(x)\cdot\phi(x)\,dx=\lim_{j\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}q^{j}(x)\cdot\phi(x)\,dx\\
&\qquad=\lim_{j\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}q^{j}(x)\cdot(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\phi(x)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}q^{\star}(x)\cdot(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\phi(x)\,dx.\end{split}

Since
g
and
q^{\star}
belong to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
, by the density
of
C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
this holds true for all
\phi\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Now, exploiting the weak convergence in (
7.24
)
(and again
[
5
, Proposition 3.6]
),
we see that, for every
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}q(x)\cdot(-\Delta)^{s}\varphi(x)\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)q(x)\cdot\varphi(x)\,dx\\
&\qquad=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}q(x)\cdot(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\varphi(x)\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)q(x)\cdot\varphi(x)\,dx\\
&\qquad=c_{s}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{(q(x)-q(y))\cdot(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,dx\,dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)q(x)\cdot\varphi(x)\,dx\\
&\qquad=\lim_{j\to+\infty}c_{s}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{(q^{j}(x)-q^{j}(y))\cdot(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,dx\,dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)q^{j}(x)\cdot\varphi(x)\,dx\\
&\qquad=\lim_{j\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}q^{j}(x)\cdot(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\varphi(x)\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)q^{j}(x)\cdot\varphi(x)\,dx\\
&\qquad=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}g(x)\cdot(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\varphi(x)\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)q^{\star}(x)\cdot\varphi(x)\,dx.\end{split}

Hence, using (
D.2
) with
\phi:=(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\varphi
, we find that, for every
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}q(x)\cdot(-\Delta)^{s}\varphi(x)\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)q(x)\cdot\varphi(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}q^{\star}(x)\cdot(-\Delta)^{s}\varphi(x)\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)q^{\star}(x)\cdot\varphi(x)\,dx.

Setting
Q:=q-\overline{q}
,
the above observation and (
D.1
) yield that, for any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

(D.3)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q(x)\cdot\Big((-\Delta)^{s}\varphi(x)+L(x)\varphi(x)\Big)\,dx=0.

Now we take a standard mollification sequence
\eta_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Since
Q\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we know (see e.g.
[
21
, Theorem 9.6]
) that

(D.4)

Q*\eta_{\varepsilon}
converges to
Q
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
as
\varepsilon\searrow 0
.

We take
p\in\mathbb{R}
to be a point in this set of convergence and set

(D.5)

\eta_{\varepsilon,p}(x):=\eta_{\varepsilon}(p-x).

Also, thanks to Lax-Milgram Theorem (see e.g.
[
2
, Corollary 5.8]
),
there exists
\phi_{\varepsilon,p}\in H^{s}_{\sharp}
such that, in the dual sense of
\widetilde{H}^{s}
,

(-\Delta)^{s}\phi_{\varepsilon,p}+L\phi_{\varepsilon,p}=\eta_{\varepsilon,p}.

That is,

(D.6)

\begin{split}&c_{s}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{(Q(x)-Q(y))\cdot(\phi_{\varepsilon,p}(x)-\phi_{\varepsilon,p}(y))}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,dx\,dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)Q(x)\cdot\phi_{\varepsilon,p}(x)\,dx\\
&\qquad\qquad=\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q(x)\,\eta_{\varepsilon,p}(x)\,dx.\end{split}

Now we employ Proposition
7.1
and we find

(D.7)

\phi_{\delta,\varepsilon,p}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})

such that

\lim_{\delta\searrow 0}\|\phi_{\varepsilon,p}-\phi_{\delta,\varepsilon,p}\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}=0.

In particular,

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\searrow 0}c_{s}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{(Q(x)-Q(y))\cdot(\phi_{\delta,\varepsilon,p}(x)-\phi_{\delta,\varepsilon,p}(y))}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,dx\,dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)Q(x)\cdot\phi_{\delta,\varepsilon,p}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad=c_{s}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{(Q(x)-Q(y))\cdot(\phi_{\varepsilon,p}(x)-\phi_{\varepsilon,p}(y))}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,dx\,dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)Q(x)\cdot\phi_{\varepsilon,p}(x)\,dx.

This and (
D.6
) return that

(D.8)

\begin{split}&\lim_{\delta\searrow 0}c_{s}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{(Q(x)-Q(y))\cdot(\phi_{\delta,\varepsilon,p}(x)-\phi_{\delta,\varepsilon,p}(y))}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,dx\,dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)Q(x)\cdot\phi_{\delta,\varepsilon,p}(x)\,dx\\
&\qquad\qquad=\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q(x)\,\eta_{\varepsilon,p}(x)\,dx.\end{split}

Furthermore, by virtue of (
D.7
), we can use (
D.3
) with
\varphi:=\phi_{\delta,\varepsilon,p}
and we see that

\displaystyle c_{s}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{(Q(x)-Q(y))\cdot(\phi_{\delta,\varepsilon,p}(x)-\phi_{\delta,\varepsilon,p}(y))}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,dx\,dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)Q(x)\cdot\phi_{\delta,\varepsilon,p}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q(x)\cdot\Big((-\Delta)^{s}\phi_{\delta,\varepsilon,p}(x)+L(x)\phi_{\delta,\varepsilon,p}(x)\Big)\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad=0.

From this and (
D.8
) it follows that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q(x)\,\eta_{\varepsilon,p}(x)\,dx=0.

Hence, in light of (
D.4
) and (
D.5
),

\displaystyle 0=\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q(x)\,\eta_{\varepsilon,p}(x)\,dx=\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q(x)\,\eta_{\varepsilon}(p-x)\,dx=\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}Q*\eta_{\varepsilon}(p)=Q(p).

Therefore
Q
vanishes almost everywhere, which entails the desired result.

Appendix E
Proof of (
8.14
)

The aim of this appendix is to check that the claim in (
8.14
)
holds true. For this, we establish the following statement, of which (
8.14
) will be an obvious consequence.

Proposition E.1
.

Let
c>0
and
q\in\widetilde{H}^{s}
. Let
u:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be defined by

u(x):=\big((q_{1}(x)-c)^{+},\dots,(q_{n}(x)-c)^{+}\big).

Then,
u\in\widetilde{H}^{s}
.

Proof.

We will prove that

(E.1)

\|u\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}<+\infty.

Recalling (
7.1
), we see that

\begin{split}\|u\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}}&=[u]^{2}_{s}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}L(x)u(x)\cdot u(x)\,dx\\
&=c_{s}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{|u_{j}(x)-u_{j}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,dx\,dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}L_{ji}(x)u_{i}(x)u_{j}(x)\,dx,\end{split}

where
u_{j}=(q_{j}-c)^{+}
.

We now show that

(E.2)

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{|u_{j}(x)-u_{j}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,dx\,dy<+\infty

(E.3)

and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}L_{ji}(x)u_{i}(x)u_{j}(x)\,dx<+\infty.

Regarding (
E.2
), we have that

|u_{j}(x)-u_{j}(y)|=\big|(q_{j}(x)-c)^{+}-(q_{j}(y)-c)^{+}\big|\leqslant\big|(q_{j}(x)-c)-(q_{j}(y)-c)\big|=|q_{j}(x)-q_{j}(y)|,

from which (
E.2
) plainly follows.

Moreover, if
u_{j}(x)>0
, then
q_{j}(x)>c
and therefore
2q_{j}(x)-c>c>0
.
Consequently,
since
L(x)
has nonnegative entries, we see that

(E.4)

\begin{split}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}}L_{ji}(x)u_{j}(x)u_{i}(x)\,dx&=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\,\int_{\{u_{j}>0\}\cap\{u_{i}>0\}}L_{ji}(x)(q_{j}(x)-c)(q_{i}(x)-c)\,dx\\
&=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\,\int_{\{u_{j}>0\}\cap\{u_{i}>0\}}L_{ji}(x)q_{j}(x)q_{i}(x)\,dx\\
&\qquad-c\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\,\int_{\{u_{j}>0\}\cap\{u_{i}>0\}}L_{ji}(x)(2q_{j}(x)-c)\,dx\\
&\leqslant\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}}L_{ji}(x)q_{j}(x)q_{i}(x)\,dx\\
&<+\infty,\end{split}

namely (
E.3
) holds true.

From (
E.2
) and (
E.3
) we obtain (
E.1
), as desired.
∎

Appendix F
An interpolation inequality

We provide the following interpolation result.

Proposition F.1
.

Let
\theta\in(0,1)
and
p
,
q
,
r\in[1,+\infty)
. If
u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
v\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then

\left\||u|^{\frac{p(1-\theta)}{r}}\,|v|^{\frac{\theta q}{r}}\right\|^{r}_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leqslant\|u\|^{p(1-\theta)}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\|v\|^{q\theta}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Furthermore, if
1\leqslant p<q<+\infty
,
r=p(1-\theta)+q\theta
and
u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then

(F.1)

\|u\|^{r}_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leqslant\|u\|^{p(1-\theta)}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\|u\|^{q\theta}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Proof.

By using the Hölder inequality with conjugate exponents
1/(1-\theta)
and
1/\theta
, we obtain that

\begin{split}\left\||u|^{\frac{p(1-\theta)}{r}}\,|v|^{\frac{\theta q}{r}}\right\|^{r}_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}=&\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u|^{p(1-\theta)}\,|v|^{q\theta}\,dx\\
&\leqslant\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1-\theta}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|v|^{q}\,dx\right)^{\theta}\\
&=\|u\|^{p(1-\theta)}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\|v\|^{q\theta}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})},\end{split}

as desired.

Moreover, if
u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
, one is allowed to take
u=v
. With this choice, if
1\leqslant p<q<+\infty
and
r=p(1-\theta)+q\theta
, we have that (
F.1
) is satisfied.
∎

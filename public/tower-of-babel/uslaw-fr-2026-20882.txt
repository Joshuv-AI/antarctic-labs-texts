Great Lakes Hydro America, LLC; Notice of Intent To Prepare an 
Environmental Impact Statement for the Penobscot Mills and Ripogenus 
Hydroelectric Projects, Schedule for Environmental Review, and Request 
for Comments

    The staff of the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC or 
Commission) will prepare a draft and final environmental impact 
statement (EIS) to evaluate potential environmental impacts of the 
continued operation and maintenance of the proposed Penobscot Mills 
Hydroelectric Project (Penobscot Mills Project; FERC No. 2458) and the 
Ripogenus Hydroelectric Project (Ripogenus Project; FERC No. 2572) 
(herein, the projects). The Penobscot Mills Project is located on the 
West Branch of the Penobscot River (West Branch) and Millinocket Stream 
in Piscataquis and Penobscot Counties, Maine. The Ripogenus Project is 
located on the West Branch in Piscataquis and Penobscot Counties, 
Maine.\1\ The Commission will use the EIS in its decision-making 
process in accordance with the National Environmental Policy Act 
(NEPA). The schedule for preparing the EIS is discussed in the Schedule 
for Environmental Review section of this notice.

    \1\ For tracking purposes under the National Environmental

    As part of the NEPA review process, the Commission takes into 
account concerns the public may have about proposals and the 
environmental impacts that could result whenever it considers the 
issuance of a license. This gathering of public input is referred to as 
``scoping.'' On August 10, 2021, Commission staff issued Scoping 
Document 1 requesting comments on environmental issues and study needs 
for the proposed projects, initiating the scoping process for the 
projects. Commission staff, in collaboration with the applicant (Great 
Lakes Hydro America, LLC; GLHA), conducted a virtual environmental site 
visit that was open to the public on September 9, 2021, and held two 
virtual public scoping meetings on September 9 and 10, 2021.\2\ A court 
reporter recorded oral comments made during the scoping meetings. On 
November 22, 2021, Commission staff issued a revised scoping document 
(Scoping Document 2) based on the comments received during the scoping 
comment period, which ended October 9, 2021. Scoping Document 2 
described the project, project alternatives, and staff's view, at the 
time, of anticipated environmental effects that would be addressed in 
the EIS.

Summary of the Penobscot Mills and Ripogenus Projects

    On September 30, 2024, GLHA filed applications for new major 
licenses to continue to operate and maintain the existing 67.9-megawatt 
(MW) Penobscot Mills Project and the existing 37.5-MW Ripogenus 
Project. Neither project occupies federal land.

Existing Project Facilities

Penobscot Mills Project

    The Penobscot Mills Project consists of: (1) four hydropower 
developments (North Twin, Millinocket, Dolby, and East Millinocket; 
located between river mile 15 and river mile 2 on the West Branch) that 
include (a) water retaining features (e.g., dam/spillway, flashboards/
rubber dam, and dikes), (b) water conduits/canals and penstocks, (c) 
intake structures equipped with trash racks, (d) four powerhouses, and 
(e) transmission equipment (e.g., transmission lines); (2) a pool-and-
weir fish passage facility at the North Twin Development; and (3) one 
storage development (Millinocket Lake Storage Development; located on 
Millinocket Stream). The Penobscot Mills Project generates an average 
of 324,346 megawatt-hours of energy annually.\3\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \3\ Average annual generation is estimated from 2014 through 
2023.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    The North Twin Development impoundment is at the upstream end of 
the Penobscot Mills Project and receives flow from the Ripogenus 
Project (described below). Releases from the North Twin Development 
pass through three other developments (i.e., Millinocket, Dolby, and 
East Millinocket). The Millinocket Lake Storage Development includes a 
pumping station through which water can be pumped to Ambajejus Lake 
(within the North Twin Development impoundment). Water not pumped to 
Ambajejus Lake is discharged to Millinocket Stream, which enters the 
West Branch at Shad Pond, downstream of the Millinocket Development, 
then passes through the Dolby and East Millinocket Developments. The 
project also includes two battery energy sites that operate as a single 
battery energy storage system to enhance system reliability.

Ripogenus Project

    The Ripogenus Project is located about 21 river miles upstream of 
the Penobscot Mills Project on the West Branch, and consists of: (1) 
the Ripogenus impoundment, which (a) is about 20.8 miles long, (b) has 
a surface area of about 29,270 acres at a normal full pond elevation of 
941.59 feet National Geodetic Vertical Datum of 1929 (NGVD 29), and (c) 
has a usable storage of about 688,705 acre-feet, with a maximum 
drawdown of 44 feet (to elevation 897.59 feet NGVD 29); and (2) 
Umbazooksus Lake, which has a surface area of 1,600 acres. Ripogenus 
dam is 795 feet long and includes two concrete spillway sections, an 
intake section, a gated sluice section, and an earthen section. The 
Ripogenus Project generates an average of 221,341 megawatt-hours of 
energy annually.\4\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \4\ Id.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Water flows from the Ripogenus impoundment through a single intake 
structure, which directs water through a tunnel and penstocks to the 
powerhouse (McKay Station). The powerhouse contains three turbine-
generating units, with a total rated capacity of 37.5 MW. Power 
generated at the project is transmitted to the electric grid via three 
transformers and a 29.4-mile-long transmission line. An additional 
0.75-mile-long transmission line extends from the McKay Substation to 
Ripogenus dam. The project has one battery energy storage site to 
enhance system reliability.

Project Operation

Penobscot Mills Project

    The five developments of the Penobscot Mills Project are operated 
as an integrated system, along with upstream storage projects, 
including the Ripogenus Project, to manage

[[Page 64917]]

downstream water levels and flows. GLHA operates the North Twin 
Development as a store-and-release facility, and uses storage in the 
Millinocket Lake Storage Development impoundment, as necessary, to 
maintain impoundment levels at the North Twin Development. The two 
storage developments (North Twin and Millinocket Lake Storage) are 
located upstream of the run-of-release developments (Millinocket, 
Dolby, and East Millinocket). GLHA releases a minimum flow of 60 cubic 
feet per second (cfs) from the Millinocket Lake Storage impoundment to 
Millinocket Stream from May 1 through October 15, and 60 cfs or inflow, 
whichever is less, from October 16 through April 30. A continuous 
minimum flow of 3,000 cfs is released from August 22 through October 15 
to the West Branch downstream of the North Twin Development when 
impoundment elevation exceeds 488.42 feet NGVD29, otherwise sufficient 
flow is released to meet a 2,000-cfs minimum flow requirement 
downstream of Shad Pond.

Ripogenus Project

    The Ripogenus Project is operated as a store-and-release 
development, with an annual seasonal drawdown that allows for 
management of water levels and flows downstream of the project. GLHA 
releases a seasonal minimum flow of 100 cfs into the dewatered section 
of the West Branch downstream of Ripogenus dam from July 1 through 
September 30, and 12 cfs leakage the remainder of the year, as well as 
seasonally variable flows through the McKay Station generating units to 
protect aquatic habitat and to provide whitewater boating opportunities 
in the West Branch. GLHA also releases 400 cfs from McKay Station to 
the West Branch during outages of the generating units and diverts 35 
to 65 cfs (depending on flow conditions in the West Branch) to the 
Holbrook side channel, seasonally, to enhance aquatic habitat. The 
project provides storage capacity to reduce downstream flooding and 
provide releases during droughts.

Applicant's Proposed Measures

Penobscot Mills Project

    GLHA proposes to continue to operate: (1) the North Twin 
Development for generation and seasonal storage, with target 
impoundment elevations of 490.42 feet NGVD 29 from May 1 through August 
21 and 488.42 feet NGVD 29 from August 22 through October 15, and 
provide flows necessary to meet the 3,000/2,000-cfs flow requirement in 
the West Branch downstream of Shad Pond; (2) the Millinocket Lake 
Storage Development to (a) maintain impoundment elevation between 470 
feet and 480 feet NGVD 29, and (b) provide a minimum flow of 60 cfs, or 
inflow (seasonally), to Millinocket Stream; and (3) the Millinocket, 
Dolby, and East Millinocket Developments as run-of-release facilities. 
GLHA also proposes to: (1) continue to operate and maintain the 
upstream pool-and-weir fish passage structure at North Twin dam; (2) 
post lake level (for North Twin and Millinocket Lake Storage) and 
streamflow information on Brookfield Renewable, N.A.'s SafeWaters 
website; \5\ (3) maintain the 200-foot conservation buffer and 100-foot 
vegetation buffer around the Millinocket Lake Storage, North Twin, 
Millinocket, and Dolby Development impoundments; and (4) maintain 
Umbazooksus Lake as wetland and wildlife habitat. Lastly, GLHA proposes 
to: (1) release a year-round 200-cfs minimum flow from Stone dam at the 
Millinocket Development; (2) provide upstream and downstream American 
eel passage, conduct upstream American eel studies, and develop a 
fishway operation and maintenance plan; (3) provide seasonal whitewater 
boating flows of 600 cfs in Millinocket Stream; and (4) develop an 
updated wildlife management area plan, a recreation plan, a shoreline 
management plan, and a historic properties management plan.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \5\ Brookfield Renewable, N.A. is the parent company of GLHA. 
Lake level and streamflow information is available on Brookfield 
Renewable, N.A.'s SafeWaters website at: https://www.safewaters.com/list/maine/penobscot.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Ripogenus Project

    GLHA proposes to continue operating the Ripogenus Project as a 
store-and-release facility. GLHA would continue to: (1) release flows 
ranging from 12 to 100 cfs from Ripogenus dam to the bypassed reach; 
(2) provide a station outage flow of 400 cfs downstream of McKay 
Station; (3) continue to operate and maintain the Holbrook side channel 
as habitat for Atlantic salmon and brook trout; (4) maintain the 200-
foot conservation buffer and 100-foot vegetation buffer around the 
Ripogenus impoundment; and (5) maintain Umbazooksus Lake as wetland and 
wildlife habitat. GLHA also proposes to: (1) provide a year-round 
minimum flow of 1,700 cfs downstream of McKay Station for aquatic 
habitat; (2) provide seasonally-adjusted flows for whitewater boating; 
(3) fund the installation, operation, and maintenance of a U.S. 
Geological Survey flow gage downstream of McKay Station (near Big 
Eddy); (4) post impoundment level and flow information on Brookfield 
Renewable, N.A.'s SafeWaters website; and (5) develop a recreation 
plan, shoreline management plan, common loon management plan, and 
historic properties management plan.
    The purpose of the projects is to continue to provide a source of 
hydroelectric power for Maine and the Northeast Region. Therefore, 
under the provisions of the Federal Power Act (FPA), the Commission 
must decide whether to issue licenses to GLHA for the projects, and 
what conditions should be placed on any licenses issued.

The NEPA Process and the EIS

    The draft and final EIS issued by the Commission will discuss 
impacts that could occur because of the proposed projects under the 
following relevant general resource areas:
     geology and soil resources;
     water and aquatic resources;
     terrestrial resources;
     federally threatened and endangered species;
     recreation, land use, and aesthetics;
     cultural resources;
     socioeconomic resources; and
     developmental resources.
    Some of the potential effects include: (1) erosion and 
sedimentation; (2) the timing and magnitude of operational flows in the 
West Branch (including McKay Station outage flows) and Millinocket 
Stream on water quality and aquatic resources, including associated 
effects on (a) littoral and riparian habitat, (b) resident fish, as 
well as aquatic insects and other macroinvertebrates, (c) freshwater 
mussels, (d) migration of American eel and the federally listed 
Atlantic salmon in the Penobscot River Basin; (3) impoundment drawdowns 
and associated effects on (a) littoral habitat and associated aquatic 
communities (e.g., rainbow smelt), (b) landlocked salmon access to 
tributaries for spawning, (c) wetland habitats, and (d) recreation and 
aesthetics; (4) availability of upstream habitat and downstream passage 
survival of American eel; (5) disturbance of suitable habitat for 
special-status wildlife species, including federally listed species; 
(6) spread of non-native, invasive species on native species; (7) 
whitewater boating flows in the West Branch (Upper Gorge and downstream 
from Stone dam) and Millinocket Stream and associated effects on other 
resources (e.g., aquatic habitat and species, angling); (8) recreation 
facility maintenance and access to the project lakes and free-flowing 
reaches of the West Branch and Millinocket Stream for

[[Page 64918]]

recreation and other uses; and (9) cultural resources.
    Commission staff will also make recommendations on how to lessen or 
avoid impacts on the various resource areas. Your comments will help 
staff focus its analysis on the issues that may have a significant 
effect on the human environment.
    The draft and final EIS will present Commission staff's independent 
analysis of the issues. On August 10, 2021, Commission staff issued a 
notice of GLHA's intent to file relicense applications for the 
projects, which also invited agencies wishing to cooperate in the 
preparation of the environmental document. No agencies filed requests 
for cooperating status. A draft EIS will be issued for public comment 
and circulated for review by all interested parties. Staff will 
consider all timely comments received during the comment period on the 
draft EIS and revise the document, as necessary, before issuing a final 
EIS. The draft and final EIS will be available in electronic format in 
the public record through eLibrary.\6\ If eSubscribed, you will receive 
an instant email notification when the environmental document is 
issued.

Alternatives Under Consideration

    As part of our review in the EIS, the Commission will consider a 
range of reasonable alternatives for each project that are technically 
and economically feasible and meet the purpose and need for the 
proposed actions. Alternatives that do not meet these requirements will 
be summarized and dismissed from further consideration in the EIS 
document. In addition to the proposed action, staff will consider a no-
action alternative. We are also considering an alternative to the 
proposed actions that potentially meets the above criteria: GLHA's 
proposed action for each project with staff modifications, including 
any mandatory conditions (i.e., the staff alternative with mandatory 
conditions).
    With this notice, we ask commenters to identify other potential 
reasonable alternatives for consideration. The alternatives considered 
may be expanded based on the comments received, provided they meet the 
criteria described above.

Schedule for Environmental Review

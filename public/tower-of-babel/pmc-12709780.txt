Genome-wide association study identifies candidate genes affecting major traits of Ziwuling black goat
Authors: Changqing Zhou, Yalan Li, Wenjie Cheng, Zhengmin Lian
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractZiwuling black goat (ZBG) is the main breed of indigenous goat for meat, wool, and skin in northwest china. However, the population has faced a sharp decline in the past few decades, mainly due to the declining efficiency of goat farming, which has led to the reluctance of farmers to raise goats. Litter size (LS), horn or no horn (HN), wool color (WC), and body conformation (BC) traits are the four main economic indicators of goat culture, which have the potential to increase population growth rate. The aim of the study was to identify potential genomic regions associated with body conformation traits of ZBG using genome-wide association studies (GWAS), and to identify molecular markers that influence LS, HN, WC, and BC traits of goats for the improvement of genetic resources of goats. In the study, we used the Illumina sequencing platform to re-sequence the genomes of 318 ZBG (12 males and 306 females) belonging to natural populations in 4 different regions, including LS, HN, WC, body height (BH), body weight (BW), body diagonal length (BL), and chest circumference (CC). The GWAS were conducted to identify candidate genes with different phenotypes which laid a foundation for further studies on the biological functions of these genes. A total of 41 single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) and 89 candidate genes were obtained in 7 phenotypes of goats. Among them, 5 SNPs and 18 genes were significantly associated with the LS traits in goats, and these loci were located on 2 chromosomes. There are 5 SNPs and 10 genes significantly associated with the HN traits, and these loci are located on 5 chromosomes. There were 18 SNPs and 31 genes significantly associated with the WC traits, and these loci were located on 16 chromosomes. There are 6 SNPs and 13 genes significantly associated with the BH traits, and these loci are located on 6 chromosomes. There are 2 SNPs and 7 genes were significantly associated with the BL traits, and these loci were located on 2 chromosomes. There are 2 SNPs and 2 genes significantly associated with the BW traits, and these loci are located on 2 chromosomes. There are 3 SNPs and 8 genes were significantly associated with the CC traits, and these loci were located on 3 chromosomes. We investigated the molecular genetic mechanism of LS, HN, WC, and BC traits of ZBG by analyzing its genome data. The study identified a series of SNPs and candidate genes associated with growth and reproductive traits, which helped accelerate the breeding process of goat breeds.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12864-025-12300-7.Keywords: ZBG, Re-sequence, Trait, GWAS

Introduction

Goats were one of the first domesticated animals. Ziwuling Black Goat (ZBG) is an indigenous goat breed which is distributed in Longdong loess plateau in northwest China and is famous for producing black lamb skin and purple velvet. ZBG have low reproductive performance, generally reaching sexual maturity at 6 months of age and starting breeding at 8 months of age. Female goats have no strict seasonal restrictions on estrus breeding, and the probability of producing double lambs is only 2% ~ 4%. In recent years, due to the limitations of traditional breeding techniques and feeding and management conditions, the characteristics of small body size, poor meat production performance, and low fertility have become the key problems restricting the breeding and development of ZBG.

The reproductive traits are important target economic traits in goat breeding. Because the litter size trait is a quantitative trait controlled by multiple genes, it is difficult to make great progress in breeding in a short time by conventional breeding techniques. At the same time, the number of lambing is a threshold trait, its external expression and internal genetic mechanism are not synchronized, compared with most of the quality traits and quantitative traits, its genetic and regulatory mechanisms are more complex. However, with the development of molecular biology and the improvement of molecular manipulation technology, genome technology is expected to break through the bottleneck of quantitative traits genetic breeding and become an important method for animal breeding in the future. Jiang et al. [1] successfully constructed the first goat reference genome and pan-genome, systematically combed, and analyzed goat SNPs and copy number variation (CNV). The genetic variation database of livestock was constructed, and the molecular mechanism of genetic resources and key traits variation of goats and other ruminants was analyzed by using population genetics theory and genomics technology, which laid a theoretical foundation for molecular breeding of goats. The study of Laoshan dairy goats by whole genome re-sequencing showed that CCNB2, AR, ADCY1, and SMAD2 genes were preferentially selected in the high fertility population [2]. The whole genome sequencing of Jining green goats with outstanding lambing performance showed that many genes, such as KIT, PAK1, and PRKAA1, were closely related to the number of lambing [3]. The discovery of these candidate genes for high propagation traits of goats not only expands our understanding of the genetic basis of goat fertility, but also provides important clues for the analysis of the genetic mechanism of goat reproductive traits.

Genome selection method has become the most reliable tool in molecular breeding technology, and has been widely used in pig and poultry breeding, which has the advantages of shortening generation interval, improving breeding accuracy, reducing production cost, and improving economic benefits of livestock and poultry. Genome selection can compare the complete genealogical record, phenotype record, and genotype information of the reference population with that of the candidate population for quality control and modeling based on the data structure and the traits to be analyzed. Through the linkage detection of all the SNPs related to quantitative trait loci (QTL) in the whole genome of the reference population, the effect value of high density SNPs in the whole genome of each population was predicted by the reference population. Based on the effect, the genomic estimated breeding value (GEBV) of the candidate population is estimated, and the candidate population individuals can be selected and retained according to the level of GEBV, so as to accelerate the progress of genetic improvement and reduce the production cost. In the study, we aimed at the problems of low reproductive ability, inadequate exploitation of production traits and genetic potential, and insufficient utilization of germplasm resources of ZBG. By measuring production performance and reproductive performance, genome sequencing and GWAS were conducted based on 318 ZBG, and 7 indexes of goat candidate genes were identified and SNPs sites were screened, so as to fully release molecular genetic potential of ZBG and accelerate the improvement process of goat breeds.

Results

Descriptive statistics

In the study, we analyzed the phenotypic data of 318 ZBG on 7 traits, including litter size (LS), horn or no horn (HN), wool color (WC), body height (BH), body weight (BW), body diagonal length (BL), and chest circumference (CC). There are 306 females and 12 males in the herd. The results given in Table 1 include LS (1, or 2), HN (1, or 2), WC (1, 2, or 3), BH (59.71 ± 3.97 cm), BW (31.61 ± 7.52 kg), BL (62.88 ± 5.87 cm), and CC (75.16 ± 7.87 cm). We evaluated phenotypic data normality using skewness and kurtosis metrics computed with the R psych package (v2.3.6), with results detailed in Table 1. Phenotypic frequency histograms (Supplementary Figure S1) confirmed normal distributions for quantitative traits including morphometric measurements (BH, BW, BL, and CC). For qualitative traits (LS, HN, and WC), phenotypes were numerically encoded (e.g., 0/1) while ensuring balanced representation across categories to minimize sampling bias. Finally, correlation analysis of seven phenotypic traits revealed distinct genetic architectures (Supplementary Table S1). LS showed shared genetic influences with BH, BW, and HN, but genetic independence from CC, and no correlation with BL or WC. BH exhibited shared genetic influences with BL and CC, genetic independence from BW, and no correlation with HN or WC. BW demonstrated shared genetic influences with BL and WC, with no correlation to CC or HN. BL shared genetic influences with CC and HN, but no correlation with WC. CC and WC showed shared genetic influences, whereas CC was genetically independent from HN.

Whole genome sequencing data

Whole-genome sequencing was performed on 318 ZBG from four distinct populations, generating 3,282.00 Gb of clean data with an average of 10.32 Gb per sample. High sequencing quality was evidenced by mean Q20 and Q30 scores of 98.78% and 96.41%, respectively. The GC content ranged from 41.98% to 48.17% across all samples, indicating absence of technical biases in library construction and sequencing (Supplementary Table S2). Alignment against the goat reference genome (ARS1.2; GCF_001704415.2) revealed a mean mapping rate of 99.82% and genome coverage (depth ≥ 1×) of 69.97% across 318 ZBG samples (Supplementary Table S3). The 69.97% genome coverage reflects stringent filtering of low-complexity regions. Variants detected through SNP calling were filtered based on minor allele frequency (MAF > 0.05) and call rate (> 0.70), yielding 777,650 high-confidence SNPs. We subsequently quantified heterozygosity (number of heterozygous SNPs), homozygosity (number of homozygous SNPs), and heterozygosity rate (proportion of heterozygous SNPs) across all transition and transversion polymorphisms. Full SNPs annotation results for all samples relative to the reference genome are provided in Supplementary Table S4. To ensure SNP reliability, we implemented dual quality controls: (1) read depth filtering for each variant site, and (2) analysis of inter-SNP distance cumulative distribution (Supplementary Figure S2). Genome-wide SNPs were classified into six variant subtypes. As shown in Supplementary Figure S3, C > T/G > A and T > C/A > G transitions predominated among all substitution types. SNP annotation revealed that 59.03% of variants resided in intergenic regions and 34.55% in intronic regions. Among coding SNPs, synonymous variants accounted for 56.11% while nonsynonymous variants constituted 43.08% (Supplementary Table S5).

Population genetic diversity analysis

In the study, we annotated and visualized the chromosomal distributions of various classes of genomic variants (Fig. 1B). The phylogenetic tree of ZBG natural population was constructed by Kimura 2-parameter model based on neighbor-joining method (Fig. 1C). The results showed that 318 ZBG were divided into 5 different genetic groups at the genomic level. The IBS-based kinship matrix revealed that most individuals within the cohort of ZBG were unrelated, while a subset exhibited genetic relatedness (Fig. 1D), underscoring the need to account for kinship in the GWAS. Additionally, sex (binary factor) and age (standardized to unit variance) were included as fixed effects to mitigate confounding effects on target phenotypes, consistent with their established roles as covariates for morphometric traits. Based on 777,650 SNPs, the population structure of 318 goats was analyzed using admixture (v1.22). When K = 2, the population of goats was not completely separated genetically (Fig. 1E and Supplementary Figure S4). Principal component analysis (PCA) showed that PC1 and PC2 accounted for 6.49% and 1.25% of the differences, respectively, with groups (a), (b), and (c) almost in the same group, and group (d) separately divided into a different group (Fig. 1A). The decay of linkage disequilibrium (LD) analysis revealed that group (a) exhibited a higher LD rate compared to groups (b) and (c), while groups (b) and (c) displayed similar LD rates. Furthermore, the LD rates of groups (b) and (c) were higher than that of group (d). This pattern indicates that population (a) possesses greater genetic diversity, whereas population (d) experiences stronger selection pressure (Fig. 1F). These observations suggest that artificial selection has driven genetic differentiation between populations (a) and (d). Consequently, incorporating population stratification as a covariate in the GWAS is warranted to minimize confounding effects.

Genome-wide association analysis of litter size (LS) traits

Genome-wide association analyses for LS using LM (GEMMA), LMM (GEMMA/EMMAX), and FaST-LMM models identified 93 positively selected genes across 15 chromosomes, with high-confidence reproduction-related candidates THADA and ZFP36L2 consistently detected on chromosome 11 by multiple methods, while FaST-LMM further revealed CPPED1, LOC108633845, and SHISA9 on chromosomes 11 and 25 (Fig. 2 and Supplementary Table S6).

Genome-wide association analysis of wool color (WC) traits

For the WC trait, GWAS used GEMMA software LM model to screen 142 positive selection genes on multiple chromosomes. Based on the GEMMA software LMM model, 119 positive selection genes were screened on multiple chromosomes. Based on the LMM model of FaST software, 122 positive selection genes were screened on multiple chromosomes. We implemented stringent cross-model concordance thresholds, requiring significance in all four models, and performed conditional analysis at lead loci. This conservative approach reduced candidate genes to 31 high-confidence targets (Table 4), a subset of which possess prior evidence for roles in wool color and pigmentation.

Haplotype-based association mapping identified 18 significant SNPs associated with WC across 12 chromosomes (Table 4), with 31 candidate genes prioritized within high-linkage disequilibrium blocks (r² >0.8). Critical loci include: chromosome 2 (rs40576540) annotating CPO and KLF7, where the latter regulates melanosome maturation; chromosome 8 (rs7398663) harboring a functionally coherent cluster comprising CTSB mediating melanin degradation, FDFT1 modulating melanocyte membrane fluidity through cholesterol synthesis, and GATA4 transcriptionally coordinating pigment cell differentiation; alongside chromosome 22/29 featuring MITF (melanogenesis master regulator at rs31775895) validated in caprine skin biopsies, and APLP2/ST14 (extracellular matrix regulators at rs35520741) synergistically facilitating melanocyte migration. Crucially, 22.6% (7/31) of these genes (MITF, KLF7, CTSB, GATA4, FDFT1, CAPZA3, and IGFBP7) possess orthologs with experimentally validated roles in melanin pathways, confirming their biological relevance for caprine pigmentation.

Discussion

Goats (Capra hircus), ranking among the earliest domesticated livestock globally, deliver vital economic outputs such as meat, milk and fiber that sustain livelihoods in diverse agroecological systems [4]. Traditional breeding strategies however face persistent challenges in improving complex production traits governed by polygenic inheritance with low heritability. To address this limitation, our study integrated whole-genome sequencing with association analyses to pinpoint SNPs and candidate genes that modulate pivotal breeding traits-specifically reproductive traits (LS, HN, WC, BW) and linear measurements (BH, BL, CC). This genetic mapping establishes a foundation for enhancing goat breeding efficiency. Notably, population stratification and kinship relationships constitute major drivers of spurious associations in correlation analyses [5]. Consequently, we implemented the advanced GCTA toolkit combined with a multi-algorithm validation framework integrating GEMMA-LM, GEMMA-LMM, FaST-LMM, and EMMAX to enhance the robustness of genome-wide association studies.

The litter size of goats is a complex phenotypic characteristic, which is not only regulated by ovulation rate, embryo or fetal survival rate, uterine tolerance, and other secondary traits, but also influenced by genetics, season, rainfall, temperature, and nutrition [6, 7]. Over the past 20 years, the FecB gene was the first major gene associated with high yield traits to be identified in sheep, and it was later found that the association of the FecB gene with high fertility was caused by the A746G mutation of the BMPR-IB gene [8, 9]. For the study of goat fertility, it is more ideal to find major genes like FecB or related genetic markers, so as to achieve early selection and improve selection accuracy through marker-assisted technology. So far, more than 30 goat breeds have been tested worldwide, but no regulatory genes and mutation sites have been found closely related to goat lambing traits [10–12]. Therefore, the genetic mechanism of lambing traits in goats needs further study. In the current study, we conducted whole genome sequencing and GWAS studies on 318 ZBG from 4 different populations, and obtained a large number of genetic variation sites and candidate genes, which provided important genetic data for the analysis of the genetic mechanism of high fecundity in goats. This study identified seven candidate genes significantly associated with LS traits through GWAS, namely THADA, ZFP36L2, APOB, CIZ1, CPPED1, SHISA9, and AUTS2. Among these, SNPs within the THADA gene constitute one of the susceptibility loci for polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS), and this gene is strongly correlated with testosterone levels [13]. However, relevant research indicates that knocking out the THADA (rs13429458) in mouse models did not affect ovarian folliculogenesis, steroidogenesis, or female fertility [14]. Additionally, genetic variants at SNP loci in the ZFP36L2 genes may also contribute to the development of PCOS. It has been reported that LLGL1 gene is considered to be an important candidate gene affecting litter size of Shanbei white cashmere goats, and the 21 bp insertion and deletion of LLGL1 gene in goats can be used as an effective DNA molecular marker site, providing valuable theoretical basis for marker assisted selection in goats [15].

Horn is a signature feature of ruminants such as cattle, sheep, and deer, and is also a derivative of the specialization of the epidermis and dermis of the head. Horn is a powerful tool used by animals to defend and attack natural enemies, compete for status and mating rights, and is of great significance for individual survival and reproduction. However, in the modern intensive breeding process, the individual hornless goat is more in line with the breeding needs, avoiding or reducing the feeding and management costs caused by frequent fighting, bumping into fences, grabbing materials, and other behaviors, and reducing feed waste and energy consumption. Although the horns of cattle, sheep, and goats are all cave horns, the gene loci that cause their hornless traits are not the same. The relaxin family peptide receptor 2 (RXFP2) gene is a major candidate gene for determining horn presence and horn size in sheep, and horn presence can be determined by 1.8 kb insertion variation in the 3’-UTR of the RXFP2 gene [16]. The horn trait of goats belong to the quality traits controlled by single gene, and the non-horn is the dominant inheritance relative to the horn. Although hornless individuals have obvious production advantages in the breeding process, there will be male sterility and female intersex phenomenon in hornless goats, and the proportion of intersex in hornless goats can be as high as 10%, which brings huge troubles to goat reproduction [17, 18]. In the study, we used genome sequencing and GWAS studies to show that there were 10 candidate genes that were significantly related to the hornless trait of ZBG, namely RUNX1 on chromosome 1; LOC102188098, WNT4, and CDC42 on chromosome 2; LOC102188844, QDPR, and CLRN2 on chromosome 6; AAGAB, SMAD3, LOC106502525, and SERINC5 on chromosome 10; AJAP1 on chromosome 16; WWOX and MAF on chromosome 18. The WNT4 gene is not only involved in sex determination and differentiation of animals, but also plays a key role in maintaining the survival of ovaries and follicles [19]. In the study, the candidate gene WNT4, located on chromosome 2, was significantly associated with both horned or non-horned traits in goats. And the gene is enriched in the signaling pathways regulating pluripotency of stem cells and melanogenesis pathways. Although the result is not completely consistent with previous studies, we know that the hornless trait of goat is closely related to its polled intersex syndrome (PIS). PIS, also known as hermaphroditism, refers to the phenomenon of some individuals having either sexual gonads or reproductive organs that occasionally appear in hermaphroditic pairs [18]. Runt-associated transcription factor 2 (Runx2) gene insertion/indel variant is significantly associated with LS and CC traits of Shanbei white cashmere goats, which can be used as an important DNA genetic marker site for goat breeding [20]. Surprisingly, this study identified a C-to-T substitution at the rs147027952 locus within the RUNX1 gene on chromosome 1 of ZBG that significantly influences horn phenotype. These findings suggest that mutations in the RUNX gene correlate not only with LS and CC traits but may also associate with horn characteristics. Consequently, the RUNX gene represents a promising candidate gene for goat breeding. In the current study, 3 candidate genes WWOX, KLF7, and BRD4 were enriched in the developmental process pathway. The GWAS studies have shown that the candidate gene WWOX is associated with HN traits in goats. The KLF7 is a transcription factor that accelerates axon regeneration in the central nervous system [21]. Studies have shown that over-expression of KLF7 in bone marrow stromal stem cells can promote sciatic nerve regeneration [22]. Therefore, we concluded that the candidate gene KLF7 may be related to the HN and reproductive traits of goats, but this inference needs further verification.

In a long battle with predators, goats have evolved camouflage colors in harmony with nature to better feed and survive. The WC trait is one of the important features to identify the differences between goat breeds, and molecular genetic markers play an important role in identifying breed purity, genetic relationship, and evaluating hybrid system. In the study, we analyzed the WC phenotypes of 318 ZBG, of which 95.50% were pure black, 3.40% were gray, and 0.60% were brown. We used genome sequencing and GWAS studies to show that 31 candidate genes were significantly related to the WC trait in ZBG, and these candidate genes were annotated on chromosomes 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 12, 14, 16, 17, 22, and 29, respectively. In addition, according to genome sequencing and GWAS studies, 18 SNPs significantly related to the WC trait was screened on 12 chromosomes of goats. It is widely recognized that the MITF gene is one of the central regulators of animal WC traits, which aligns with our findings. Furthermore, we identified that a G-to-A mutation at the rs31775895 locus on chromosome 22 may represent a potential key locus for coat color breeding in ZBG. The MITF gene is a basic helix-loop-helix leucine zipper, bHLH-LZ) transcription factor that regulates melanocyte differentiation and development, and pigment-cell-specific transcription of melanin-producing enzyme genes [23]. In the study, the candidate gene MITF was enriched in the osteoclast differentiation and melanogenesis signaling pathways. These results suggest that MITF gene may be related to bone development and skin color of goats. Syntrophin (SNTG1), a group of cytoplasmic peripheral membrane proteins directly related to anti-muscular dystrophy protein (Duchenne muscular dystrophy gene), has been shown to be a neuron-specific protein [24]. In the current study, the candidate gene SNTG1 was enriched in a structural molecule activity signaling pathway. Related studies reported that the ASIP, ITCH, AHCY, and RALY genes on chromosome 13 were significantly correlated with the black and brown WC traits of Markhoz goats, and the KIT and PDGFRA genes on chromosome 6 were significantly correlated with the white WC trait of Markhoz goats [25]. In the current study, the candidate gene Plekha5 was enriched in the reproductive process pathway. The plekha (1–7) protein family is a key adhesion binding component involved in many functions of mammalian cells [26]. Related studies have reported that Plekha5 gene is a pleotropic effect gene on milk production, somatic cell score, and body conformation traits of Holstein dairy cows [27]. While numerous genes have been identified in goat coat color research, functional validation of their mutation sites remains required for most candidate genes.

The BW is not only an important economic feature to evaluate the performance of livestock, but also an important basis to judge the effect of hybrid improvement. As for BW trait, we used genome sequencing and GWAS studies to show that there were 2 candidate genes significantly related to BW traits of ZBG, namely KIF2B on chromosome 19 and ZNF385D on chromosome 27. KIF2B, localized to spindle microtubules and transient kinetochores, is essential for spindle assembly and chromosome movement, playing critical roles during mitotic division [28]. In this study, we identified a G-to-A substitution at the rs3525023 locus of the KIF2B gene on chromosome 19, a variant that may serve as a valuable genetic marker for body weight traits in goats. Relevant studies reported that 179 SNPs were significantly associated with BW traits of Zhongwei goats, and these loci were annotated on ST6GAL1, SOX2, ROBO1, and ADIPOQ genes [29]. It was reported that 21 SNPs were significantly associated with BW traits in Inner Mongolia cashmere goats at the genome-wide level, and these SNPs were annotated in key candidate genes MAPK3, LDB2, and LRP1B related to muscle growth and development [30]. Relevant studies showed that 3 candidate genes, MFSD4, LRRC37B, and NCAM2, were significantly associated with BW traits in yaks [31]. Based on multi-omics data, candidate genes HINT1, ASB11, and GPR143 may be related to BW traits in sheep [32].

We used genome sequencing and GWAS studies to show that there were 13 candidate genes significantly related to the BH trait in ZBG, namely LOC102185610 on chromosome (1) DAW1, SPHKAP, and CCL20 on chromosome (2) SLC35F4 on chromosome 10. ANKEF1, LOC102171702, LOC102171968, LOC102179575, and PCSK2 on chromosome 13. NKAIN3 on chromosome 14. ZNF521 and COLEC12 on chromosome 24. Related studies reported that 5 SNPs were significantly associated with BH traits in Zhongwei goats, and these SNPs were annotated on the ZNF215 and CADM2 genes [29]. We used genome sequencing and GWAS studies to show that there were 7 candidate genes significantly related to the BL trait in ZBG, namely LOC102190971, ATP8A2, and SHISA2 on chromosome 12. FFAR4, MYOF, RBP4, and CEP55 on chromosome 26. Retinol-binding protein 4 (RBP4) is A protein of about 21 kDa that performs its physiological function primarily by transporting retinol (vitamin A). Some studies have found that adipokine RBP4 promotes fat infiltration and skeletal muscle atrophy by activating the STRA6-dependent JAK2/STAT3 signaling pathway of skeletal muscle cell membrane receptor [30]. Related studies reported that 25 SNPs were significantly associated with the BL of Zhongwei goat, and these SNPs were annotated on the ROBO1 gene [29]. We used genome sequencing and GWAS studies to show that there were 8 candidate genes significantly related to CC trait of ZBG, namely PTPN13 on chromosome 6, CFAP61 and INSM1 on chromosome 13, NOC4L, PUS1, ULK1, DDX51, EP400 on chromosome 17, and CD83 on chromosome 23. Related studies reported that 97 SNPs were significantly associated with CC traits in Zhongwei goat, and these SNPs were annotated on the genes MYO1D, GHR, TWIST2, AQPI, and FBXO32 [29]. Some studies have found that CFAP61 may affect the survival and hormone secretion of leydig cell by regulating the MAPK/COX-2 signaling pathway, thus playing a role in male infertility [33]. EP400 is an important epigenetic regulator that plays an important role in chromatin remodeling, histone acetylation, histone deposition, and DNA damage repair. Studies have found that the fertility of EP400 cKO female mice is significantly decreased, and EP400 cKO oocytes are manifested by increased DNA damage, as well as oocyte maturation and fertilization disorders, and maternal EP400 deletion leads to significant developmental retardation in early mouse embryos at 2 to 4-cell stages [34].

Next, we’ll confirm how key genetic markers affect traits in ZBG using lab tests and larger herd studies. These verified markers will help us breed better goats improving coat color consistency, growth, and reproduction. Our work provides the genetic toolkit for smarter breeding programs, while carefully using these findings to preserve the breed’s diversity and valuable traits.

Materials and methods

Animal ethics

The Ziwuling black goats (ZBG) used in this experiment were provided by Qingyang Goat Breeding Farm, Gansu Province, China. The goats received the same feeding conditions throughout the study period. The goats were clinically healthy, free of disease and trauma. The feeding environment in this study meets the standards outlined in the China National Standard “Laboratory Animal Environment and Facilities” (GB14925-2010), which are applicable to typical animal laboratory facilities. Animal welfare requirements during animal feeding and testing. In addition, none of the animals used in this study involved euthanasia and sacrifice methods.

Phenotypic measurements and sample collection

The 318 ZBG used in the study came from 4 different regions in Qingyang, Gansu Province. There were 98 goats from Huachi, 103 from Huanxian, 105 from Guyuan, and 12 from Qingcheng, which were divided into groups (a), (b), (c), and (d). The goats were 306 females and 12 males. We measured the phenotypic of 7 traits of ZBG, including flitter size (LS), horn or no horn (HN), wool color (WC), body height (BH), body weight (BW), body diagonal length (BL), and chest circumference (CC). Ewes producing 2 consecutive litters with 1 litter size were considered to be in the low fertility group, while ewes producing three consecutive litters with two or more litter were considered to be in the high fertility group. Goat wool color and horns (with or without) are accurately recorded on an individual basis. The BW of all the goats was measured using the same electronic scale (Xingtongzhineng, AT-SW-03, Henan, China). The BH, BL, and CC of all goats were measured using a tape measure. We utilized the GCTA software (versions: v1.94.1) to establish correlations among the seven phenotypic traits. All goat blood was collected through the jugular vein using a vacuum blood collection tube containing ethylenediamine tetraacetic acid (EDTA) anticoagulant and sterilized blood collection needle. After the blood was collected, it was first stored in a −4℃ refrigerator for 4 h, and then transported to the laboratory in dry ice for DNA extraction.

Whole genome sequencing

Whole blood DNA extraction kit (BGI-Shenzhen, China) was used to extract DNA genome from 318 goat blood samples. The concentration and purity of DNA were determined by NanoDrop 2000 (Thermo Fisher Scientific). The qualified DNA samples are stored at −20 ℃ for future use. According to the manufacturer’s description, a 350 bp sequencing library was constructed using 0.5 µg of DNA. After PCR and purification, the library was sequenced on Illumina sequencing platform to generate 150 bp double-terminal sequence. We employed the software SOAPnuke (version 2.2.1) [35] for quality control and filtering of raw data. BWA’s mem algorithm (version: 0.7.17-r1188) [36] was utilized to align clean reads against the reference genome (ARS1.2, GCF_001704415.2), which was then converted into a sorted bam file using samtools (version: 1.9) [37]. Finally, we performed quality control statistics on the resulting bam file using Qualimap2’s bamqc tool (version: 2.2.2-dev) software [38]. GATK (version: 4.2.6.1) [39] was employed for SNP calling with default parameters, followed by SNP annotation using SnpEff [40].

Population genetics analyses

Based on the SNP data detected by variation, the SNPs were filtered according to minor allele frequency (MAF: 0.05) and site integrity (INT: 0.8), and high consistency SNPs were obtained for downstream analysis. To describe the taxonomic and evolutionary relationships of goats, we used MEGA X software [41] to construct phylogenetic trees between different populations based on neighbor-joining and kimura 2-parameter models. The admixture (v1.22) software [42] was used to analyze the population structure between goat samples. The number of subgroups (K value) is pre-set to 1–10 for clustering, and the optimal number of subgroups is determined according to the valley value of cross-validation error rate. A stacking diagram of the Q matrix for each K value is generated using the R package Pophelper (http://royfrancis.github.io/pophelper). The smart PCA program in the EIGENSOFT (v6.0) package [43] is employed to perform principal component analysis (PCA) and kinship analysis (KA) of populations. We present PCA clustering results for all models based on the first two principal components (PC1 and PC2). The GCTA (v1.92.1) software [44] was employed to perform kinship assessment for 4 natural populations of goats. To calculate the linkage imbalance (LD) between 2 SNPs in the 1,000 kb, PopLDdecay (v3.41) software [45] was employed to perform LD analysis. The intensity of LD analysis is represented by r2. The SNP distance was fitted to r2, and the variation of r2 with distance was plotted.

Genome-wide association study (GWAS)

Four algorithms were applied for GWAS analysis, including LM and LMM model of GEMMA software, LMM model of FaST software, and EMMAX software. The SNPs in this study were filtered using the vcftools software (version 0.1.16) with the following parameters: a minor allele frequency (MAF) threshold of 0.05, a maximum allowable deletion value of 1,a minimum mean depth requirement (meanDP) of 10, and a minimum genotype quality (GQ) threshold of 10. Subsequently, after setting a fixed threshold, SNPs exhibiting -log10 (P-value) greater than 5 were considered a statistically significant associations [46–48]. Identification of candidate genes and enrichment analysis

The candidate genes were annotated using dual genomic criteria: (1) a fixed 500 kb window (± 250 kb) centered on each lead SNP, capturing > 99% of linkage disequilibrium (LD) blocks; (2) an LD threshold of r² >0.8 to define genomic regions using PLINK v1.9. Parameters were optimized according to Ziwuling Black goat-specific LD decay patterns (mean decay: 150 kb at r² = 0.2), consistent with caprine genomics standards. Based on LD decay distance analysis, we investigated the biochemical function of candidate genes at the physical location of important SNP sites. Goat reference genome (ARS1.2; GCF_001704415.2) to annotate these genes. These genes were compared against UniProt and KEGG databases using sequence alignment software Diamond’s Blastx tool. Set the comparison value to 1e-05 and extract the GO number and KO number for each gene. To GO to target genes (http://www.geneontology.org/) and KEGG pathway enrichment analysis.

Conclusions

Whole-genome sequencing of 318 ZBG, integrated with phenotyping for seven key traits and GWAS employing four distinct models, identified 41 significant SNPs and 89 candidate genes. Functional enrichment analysis revealed these genes participate in pathways governing caprine growth, development, reproduction, and linear measurements, providing a theoretical foundation for breeding economically vital traits. Collectively, these genes likely exert critical roles in determining reproductive efficiency, horn, coat color, and body conformation in goats. Our study successfully characterizes pivotal SNPs associated with multiple agronomically important traits, establishing a robust genetic framework for enhancing goat production performance. Future functional characterization of these candidate genes will elucidate the genetic mechanisms underpinning target phenotypes.

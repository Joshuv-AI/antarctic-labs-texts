Design and numerical analysis of a modified core hexa–deca photonic crystal fiber for highly negative dispersion and birefringence control in optical communication bands
Authors: Amit Halder, Md Shamim Anower
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe paper presents a thorough investigation into the design of a Modified Core Hexa–Deca Photonic Crystal Fiber (MHD-PCF) with adjustable features to regulate dispersion and birefringence. At the target wavelength of 1550 nm, the suggested MHD-PCF exhibits extraordinary optical properties, including an ultra-high negative dispersion coefficient of − 7755 ps/(nm km) and significant birefringence of 1.905 × 10−2. The analysis entails regular changes in lattice constants and center air hole parameters, which provide insights into optical property trends. The MHD-PCF regularly exceeds existing benchmarks across a wide range of parameter adjustments. Notably, this fiber has strong nonlinearity (59.12 W−1 km−1) and low confinement loss (2.896 × 10−3 dB/cm), as well as an enhanced numerical aperture (0.5144), demonstrating its potential for efficient light coupling and supercontinuum production. These results put the Modified Core Hexa–Deca Photonic Crystal Fiber at the forefront of contemporary communication systems, and its optical enhancements and flexible features present exciting opportunities for novel Terahertz technology breakthroughs, especially in the areas of communication systems and sensing applications.Keywords: Modified Core Hexa–Deca Photonic Crystal Fiber (MHD-PCF), Dispersion control, Birefringence tuning, Ultra-high negative dispersion coefficient, Microstructured optical fiber, Communication bandsSubject terms: Optical materials and structures, Other photonics

Introduction

The fast expansion of high-speed communication networks needs creative techniques for dispersion management and birefringence control1. Traditional optical fibers have limits in allowing exact adjustment of these parameters, limiting their utility in next-generation photonic systems2. Photonic crystal fibers (PCFs) on the other hand, provide a platform with detailed microstructured patterns that enable customizable dispersion and birefringence features3. The goal of this research is to thoroughly analyze the optical features and performance metrics of the MHD-PCF. A deeper understanding of the relationship between design factors and optical performance is obtained by systematically altering its lattice constants and air hole characteristics. Numerous studies have shown that changing hole sizes and refractive indices within photonic crystal fibers (PCFs) has a significant impact on resonant behavior and dispersion profiles, both in solid-core and hollow-core configurations. For example, selective hole filling and the introduction of nanosized air-hole defects have been shown to effectively control dispersion characteristics, laying foundations for the design optimizations presented in this work4,5. In addition, research on hexagonal PCFs has shown how changes to the core structure, such as adding more air holes, can result in high negative dispersion and increased nonlinearity, influencing the fiber's performance in telecommunications applications6,7. Furthermore, studies on mode distortions caused by aperiodic lattices and resonant structures have shown that structural modifications play a critical role in managing mode behavior and dispersion, which are important considerations for the current study8,9. Researchers have presented several designs for photonic crystal fibers (PCFs) to date with the goal of catering to varied applications such as obtaining enhanced levels of modal birefringence and enabling dispersion compensation. The octagonal PCF design proposed in10, for example, has a noteworthy anomalous dispersion coefficient of − 588 ps/(nm km) and birefringence of 1.81 × 10−2. The PCF core is modified with elliptical air holes and two missing ones for high birefringence and dispersion compensation. However, this complex structure makes fabrication difficult and results may vary greatly due to fabrication and manufacturing challenges. The confinement loss is still a challenge to this complex M-OPCF design. The observed negative dispersion coefficient in an alternate octagonal shape mentioned in11 is − 239.51 ps/(nm km), whereas the corresponding birefringence is 1.67 × 10−2. Six oval-shaped vertical air holes in the core of the M-OPCF design, spaced at specific angles within an octagonal-shaped outer cladding, add complexity and difficulty to the structure's fabrication. Agrawal et al. described a spiral design12 that obtained 1.60 × 10−2 birefringence and a negative dispersion coefficient of − 400 ps/(nm km). The golden spiral design's complexity stems from its angular spacing and arrangements, making it challenging to fabricate and realize practically, and also requiring consideration of confinement loss. Bappy et al. propose a hybrid photonic crystal fiber with a hexagonal and circular shape that provides broadband dispersion correction from 1200 to 1500 nm with a negative dispersion coefficient of − 58 ps/(nm km)13. The hybrid PCF structure, despite its simplicity, struggles with dispersion, lower nonlinearity (15.99 W−1 km−1), and high confinement loss (> 10 dB/km), making it less suitable for high speed broadband communication systems. Ali et al. developed a photonic crystal fiber (PCF) with circular air holes that had a negative dispersion of − 260 ps/(nm km) and a birefringence of 1.72 × 10−214. Due to the intricate undulating design with three cladding layers and a single pitch value, as well as the absence of a loss profile and core design analysis with hole arrangements, the PCF fabrication realization is not covered. Halder introduced a hexagonal microstructured optical fiber (H-MOF) design in 2016 that attained a high birefringence of 3.373 × 10−2 by including elliptical core air holes, resulting in a remarkable negative dispersion coefficient of − 837.8 ps/(nm km) at 1550 nm wavelength15. Since oval-shaped air holes are challenging to incorporate in such a small pitch value of 0.9 µm, the design limitation is caused by the use of four oval-shaped air holes rather than circular ones, which could have been accomplished using all circular air holes. Lee et al. proposed photonic crystal fiber architecture with a double-hole assisted core and a traditional square lattice covering. This novel PCF structure provided birefringence (7.5 × 10−3), significant negative dispersion (− 150 ps/(nm km)), and very low confinement loss over the E + S + C + L + U wavelength bands (1380–1780 nm)16. Even with its low loss, the fiber design has a low dispersion coefficient at 1.55 µm operating wavelength and low birefringence of less than 10−2 order. Saha et al. investigated the design and analysis of photonic crystal fibers (PCFs) with high birefringence and nonlinearity with minimal confinement loss. Two separate PCF models were shown, both with significant properties: model-1 had a high birefringence of 2.75 × 10−2 and a negative dispersion of − 540.67 ps/(nm km) at 1.55 µm, while model-2 had a lower confinement loss while preserving comparable qualities17. The complicated models of PCFs, which have elliptical air hole layers oriented both horizontally and vertically, present a significant fabrication challenge and may consequently lower the practical performance of optical fibers. Huo et al. developed the HC-PQF fiber, which has a high birefringence (1.345 × 10−2), a low nonlinear coefficient (1.63 × 10−3 W−1 km−1) and a near-zero, flattened dispersion for versatile applications in optical communication, high power pulse transmission, and polarization beam splitters at 1.55 μm wavelength18. Although it is a crucial parameter for long-distance optical transmissions, the confinement loss and bend loss are not examined for this HC-PQF. Agbemabiese and Akowuah performed a thorough numerical analysis of a hexagonal photonic crystal fiber (PCF) with four circular air hole rings around the core. Their observations included significant birefringence (2.018 × 10−2), nonlinear coefficients (40.682 W−1 km−1), and negative chromatic dispersion over the telecommunication spectrum (from − 47.72 to − 105 ps/km nm)19. Due to their non-periodic and non-symmetric air hole diameters and arrangements, the core and cladding are difficult to modify and result in a dispersion profile that remains unchanged despite modifications, which is a drawback in some telecom band designs. B.K. Paul et al. (2022) demonstrated a modified square-shaped PCF design with an ultra-high negative dispersion quantity of − 2357.54 ps/(nm km) at the optical wavelength (λ = 1550 nm) in their study. Furthermore, the fiber displayed simultaneous achievement of large nonlinear coefficients (γ = 74.68 W−1 km−1) and a mono-mode feature at the same operating wavelength20. This structure's primary drawback is that it has a very low birefringence value and a high confinement loss. The Modified Core Hexa-Deca Photonic Crystal Fiber (MHD-PCF) stands out as a significant improvement in this area in this work, displaying customizable features that boost its performance beyond usual benchmarks. The MHD-PCF's one-of-a-kind structural design includes systematic changes in lattice constants and center air hole parameters, allowing for fine-tuning of its optical characteristics. Compared to other complex PCF structures, the suggested design is easier to fabricate because it only has one pitch value for all of its circular air holes. The systematic and periodic design of the suggested PCF has addressed the fabrication challenge. Analysis of the confinement loss at 1550 nm wavelength revealed a lower value of 2.896 × 10−3 dB/cm. Even though the prior structure analysis shows how difficult it is to maintain both birefringence and dispersion at the same time, we have managed to create a less complicated design that can achieve both higher birefringence and very high negative dispersion. Our design is novel because it adds decagonal air hole layers on top of the core's hexagonal air hole layers. In the central layer, the diameter of two air holes opposite to one another has been decreased in order to achieve a higher birefringence. At the operating communication wavelength of 1550 nm, this design flexibility enables the production of an ultra-high negative dispersion coefficient of − 7755 ps/(nm km) and significant birefringence of 1.905 × 10−2. These characteristics make the MHD-PCF an appealing candidate for applications requiring efficient dispersion compensation and accurate polarization control.

Geometry of proposed MHD-PCF

Fused silica is used as the fundamental material for the proposed hybrid photonic crystal fiber21. The Modified Core Hexa–Deca Photonic Crystal Fiber (MHD-PCF) cladding is made up of circular air holes with hexagonal inner and decagonal exterior structures. Circular air holes are used exclusively across the length of the cladding. Figure 1 depicts a cross-sectional view of the proposed fiber architecture. The MHD-PCF's purposefully modified core consists of two hexagonal rings in the internal cladding layer and four decagonal rings in the external cladding layer, with six air hole rings within the cladding. Adjustments in the inner core entail addition of two smaller air holes to the first air-hole ring while leaving the remaining four air-holes alone. This serves as the foundation for the technique, which elaborates on the structural design and its ramifications inside the research article. The overall structure's lattice constant or pitch (Λ) was set at 1 µm. Within the inner hexagonal cladding section, the diameter of the initial pair of smaller air holes in the first ring was chosen as dc = 0.45Λ = 0.45 µm. The remaining four air holes in the first ring and the 12 air holes in the second ring all possessed a diameter of d1 = d2 = 0.9Λ = 0.9 µm. As for the outer cladding, including the four air hole rings forming a decagonal shape, the diameters were uniform: d = d3 = d4 = d5 = d6 = 0.55Λ = 0.55 µm. A circular perfectly matched layer (PML) was used at the edge of the structure to mitigate reflection and backscattering problems. Its thickness was 10% of the cladding radius in order to absorb undesired reflections22. Around the MHD-PCF, a uniformly thin 1 µm PML layer was applied with a scaling factor of 1. Remaining modes in 125 µm fibers could result in increased modal dispersion or crosstalk, two undesired optical effects. To mitigate these effects, Perfectly Matched Layers (PML), or simulated boundary conditions were used. Through the absorption of outgoing electromagnetic waves, the PML reduces artifacts. This strategy reduces the impact of flaws in the fiber structure or variations in material properties by ensuring accurate simulation results that accurately represent the behavior of the proposed fiber in practical scenarios.

The microstructural nature of the proposed PCF led to electromagnetic analysis, including field distribution and effective mode indices. Chromatic dispersion

Dispersion is a major issue in optical fiber communication and DWDM systems, causing pulse broadening. This can be addressed by using periodic air perforations in photonic crystal fibers, and calculating the negative dispersion coefficient. From Fig. 4 it is evident that the proposed MHD-PCF show anomalous dispersion through the communication band for both X and Y polarization modes.

Wavelength reliant Dispersion curves of the MHD-PCF for both X and Y polarization modes.

Figure 4 depicts the X and Y polarization dispersion characteristics of the Modified Core Hexa–Deca Photonic Crystal Fiber (MHD-PCF) at 1550 nm. The X polarization has a notable negative dispersion of − 179 ps/(nm km), suggesting that it might be used for dispersion correction and signal processing in high-speed communication. In contrast, the Y polarization has a very large negative dispersion of − 7755 ps/(nm km), which opens up new possibilities for dispersion manipulation and optical pulse structuring. For the optimal design parameter, a very high negative dispersion of − 7755 ps/(nm km) is found for the Y polarized mode at the targeted wavelength of 1550 nm. For fibers that maintain polarization, the Y polarization modes are referred to as fast modes. The suggested structure shows such a higher anomalous dispersion at 1550 nm wavelength because of the six layers of air hole structure, inner hexagonal and outer decagonal cladding with defected core using two smaller air holes near core. This arrangement results in a slightly bell-shaped change in the effective refractive index for the Y polarization mode. That kind of high negative dispersion is caused by the effective refractive index asymmetry. For this particular proposed structure, the high bit rate long-distance communication via fibers will be aided by this electromagnetic phenomenon.

The negative dispersion observed in the y-polarization mode exhibits a sharp peak around 1.55 µm within the examined spectral range. This suggests that telecommunication channels operating in the C-band, for instance, would encounter considerable variations in dispersion compensation. To address the diverse dispersion effects in high-speed communications systems that span the C band, tunable filters and selective-band use are useful solutions. Band-stop filters with thermal tuning can block certain wavelength channels and thus minimize the adverse effects of dispersion in WDM systems32. Tunable filters architectures around liquid crystal technology are leveraged to perform micro scale adjustments to compensate for sharp dispersion peaks, offering fine-grained control over polarization and also loss in transmission33. Moreover, broadband multimode-interference bandwidth filters can tune every wavelength within the C-band, which would counter the changes in dispersion and ensure signal quality34. This method complements the wide wavelength spectrum to maintain minimum performance in high-speed communication networks with changing rates of dispersion.

Birefringence

The structural asymmetry of photonic crystal fibers results in birefringence, which breaks spatial symmetry and causes polarization-dependent refractive indices. This has an impact on how light travels and opens up possibilities for polarization control and optical communication systems. Understanding electromagnetic phenomena that control specialized fiber design, such as modal interference, index contrast, and photonic band gaps, is essential. Our proposed MHD-PCF structure's internal core asymmetry, which is brought about by the two opposite air holes of the first ring's smaller diameter (dc), results in notable birefringence in polarization mode fibers with an order of 10−2. On the other hand, the traditional fibers that maintain polarization exhibit a modal birefringence of approximately 5 × 10−4.

Figure 7 illustrates the birefringence characteristics of the Modified Core Hexa–Deca Photonic Crystal Fiber (MHD-PCF), revealing a birefringence value of 1.905 × 10−2 at a wavelength of 1550 nm. This value of 1.905 × 10−2 is 38.1 times higher than the traditional PM fibers.

Confinement loss

The confinement loss resulting from light interaction with photonic crystal fibers impacts optical power. The spatial distribution of guided modes is determined by electromagnetic effects such as mode coupling and photonic band gap formation, which enhance the minimization of confinement loss. Increasing air hole layers in the cladding reduces the chance of leaky modes and improves the loss profile of a PCF. In order to reduce confinement loss, the proposed MHD-PCF design includes a total of six layers of air holes.

Figure 9 depicts the relationship between wavelength and confinement loss in the Modified Core Hexa–Deca Photonic Crystal Fiber (MHD-PCF), revealing a noteworthy confinement loss of 2.896 × 10−3 dB/cm at the wavelength of 1550 nm for optimal parameters.

Numerical aperture

Refractive index contrast between the cladding and core, as well as the fiber's structural design, all have an impact on the acceptance angle of the guided modes, which in turn determines the numerical aperture in photonic crystal fibers. This phenomenon, which affects the fiber's overall performance in applications like sensing and telecommunication, is controlled by electromagnetic effects like mode confinement, modal dispersion, and photonic band gap formation. The internal defected core structure comprises of six air holes with four bigger air holes and two smaller air holes compacts the light into the core and outer decagonal cladding layer helps to enhance the light capturing effect.

Figure 11 depicts the wavelength-dependent numerical aperture (NA) feature of the Modified Core Hexa–Deca Photonic Crystal Fiber (MHD-PCF). Notably, the numerical aperture at the ideal parameter values is 0.5144 at the working wavelength of 1.55 µm. Effective area

The spatial distribution of guided modes, which is impacted by the refractive index profile and fiber structural design, determines the effective area of photonic crystal fibers. Nonlinear performance, dispersion characteristics, and power handling capabilities are influenced by electromagnetic effects such as mode confinement, modal dispersion, and photonic band gap formation. The core design of the proposed MHD-PCF compresses the effective area by effectively packing the light wave into the center.

Nonlinear coefficient

Photonic crystal fibers exhibit a nonlinear coefficient due to high optical intensity confinement and engineered dispersion properties, enabling enhanced interactions like Raman scattering and self-phase modulation, enabling wavelength conversion and ultrafast pulse generation. The nonlinearity depends on the effective area of a holey fiber structure and it possess inversely proportional relationship with effective area. To improve nonlinearity the effective area of a fiber should be reduced.

Fabrication technique of the proposed MHD-PCF

Various essential production techniques, such as sol–gel casting35, conventional drilling36, stack and draw method37, and so on, are employed to generate and construct photonic crystal fibers. Because the design is made up of consistent circular air holes, it is straightforward to build using the stack and draw method. But due to the smaller pitch value of 1 µm the sol–gel technique is recommended for this type of hybrid structure. The emergence of nanotechnology has also aided in the production of the tiniest structures38. Our proposed MHD-PCF's smallest air hole diameter, which was roughly 225 nm (≈ 0.23 µm), was larger than previously reported39, suggesting that the stack-and-draw method can be used to fabricate our MHD-PCF design. Additionally, a lead silicate PCF design with air holes as small as 20 nm in the core region was created using the extrusion technique40. The physical realization and fabrication evidence of PCF structures are demonstrated by Ryszard Buczynski41 and Russel et al.42.

Future applications of the proposed MHD-PCF

The proposed MHD-PCF has smaller air hole and pitch diameter around 1 µm. The numerous opportunities for advanced photonic functionalities and applications are opened up by integrating our fiber, which has high birefringence, highly negative dispersion, high nonlinearity coefficient, and good numerical aperture, into a planar lightwave circuit (PLC)43,44. The complementary capabilities of our fiber and PLC technologies provide promising prospects for innovation in optical communication, sensing, and other fields, ranging from polarization control and dispersion management to nonlinear optical processing and effective light manipulation45. Its ultra-high negative dispersion coefficient, strong birefringence, strong nonlinearity, and low confinement loss make the Modified Core Hexa–Deca Photonic Crystal Fiber (MHD-PCF) studied in this study very promising for the advancement of Terahertz (THz) technology. THz communication systems46, imaging47, spectroscopy 48, and other sensing applications49 could benefit greatly from the MHD-PCF's adjustable features and reported optical improvements. The Modified Core Hexa–Deca Photonic Crystal Fiber (MHD-PCF) operating in the 1.28–1.8 µm range bridges optical and Terahertz (THz) domains, making it suitable for THz wireless communications by leveraging NIR technologies50. Furthermore, its strong nonlinearity allows for harmonic and supercontinuum generation, which extends into the THz spectrum51,52. Furthermore, integrating the MHD-PCF with THz waveguides allows for efficient hybrid optical-THz systems, increasing its applicability in advanced THz technology53.

Conclusion

This study provides an in-depth examination of the Modified Core Hexa-Deca Photonic Crystal Fiber (MHD-PCF), emphasizing its capacity to precisely control dispersion and birefringence. With an impressive negative dispersion coefficient of − 7755 ps/(nm km) and notable birefringence (1.905 × 10−2) at 1550 nm, the MHD-PCF exhibits great potential for advanced communication systems and sensing technologies. This fiber demonstrates an adaptable architecture through systematic modification of air hole characteristics and lattice constants. It consistently performs better than expected with high nonlinearity (59.12 W−1 km−1), low confinement loss (2.896 × 10−3 dB/cm), and a better numerical aperture (0.5144) that allow for effective light coupling and possible supercontinuum generation. The MHD-PCF offers transformative potential in photonics applications due to its adjustable features and optical advancements, which position it as a catalyst for new developments in communication and sensing. Finally, this paper's thorough investigation of the Modified Core Hexa–Deca Photonic Crystal Fiber (MHD-PCF) highlights its critical role in advancing Terahertz (THz) technology. It provides a means of achieving improved THz communication systems, imaging, spectroscopy, and sensing applications, and it may also be integrated with Planar Lightwave Circuits, which would further the development and innovation of THz technology.

Frustratingly Easy Zero-Day Audio DeepFake Detection via Retrieval Augmentation and Profile Matching


Abstract
Modern audio deepfake detectors using foundation models and large training datasets have achieved promising detection performance. However, they struggle with zero-day attacks , where the audio samples are generated by novel synthesis methods that models have not seen from reigning training data. Conventional approaches against such attacks require fine-tuning the detectors, which can be problematic when prompt response is required. This study introduces a training-free framework for zero-day audio deepfake detection based on knowledge representations, retrieval augmentation, and voice profile matching. Based on the framework, we propose simple yet effective knowledge retrieval and ensemble methods that achieve performance comparable to fine-tuned models on DeepFake-Eval-2024, without any additional model-wise training. We also conduct ablation studies on retrieval pool size and voice profile attributes, validating their relevance to the system efficacy. 1 1 1 This study is supported by JST AIP Acceleration Research (JPMJCR24U3) and the New Energy and Industrial Technology Development Organization (NEDO, JPNP22007). This study was partially carried out using the TSUBAME4.0 supercomputer at the Institute of Science Tokyo.

Can we prevent zero-day spoofing attacks without any form of additional fine-tuning?

We propose a simple yet comprehensive framework for handling unseen zero-day attacks using
retrieval augmentation
(RA) techniques. Our method maintains a knowledge base containing reference “seen” data, and addresses novel attacks through knowledge retrieval and ensemble methods based on
k-nearest neighbors
(KNN). The knowledge database stores both representation-level features and score-level predictions from a well-trained SSL-based CM. Additionally, we incorporate speaker and voice-related information to evaluate whether such
profile matching
can enhance the performance. Both the methods and their related models do not involve any additional re-training or fine-tuning, enabling prompt reaction and adaptation for robustness to the zero-day attacks.
While RA has been applied to ADD, existing methods significantly differ from our approach.

However, apart from fine-tuning the SSL frontend for ADD, both work involve additional training of backend classifier for feature selection and decision making. Our proposed method employs RA by directly using existing massively-trained CMs, enabling more broadly applicable solution for advanced SSL-based CMs.

Retrieval Augmentation Based on Knowledge Resources

2.1
Source data: DeepFake-Eval-2024

We select DE2024
[
6
]
as our source dataset for this study, as a representative of real-world deepfake threats with current generation techniques due to its sourcing methodology. DE2024 is a multi-modal dataset containing 56.5 hours of audio content collected from social media platforms and deepfake detection services in 2024. It includes over 40 languages from more than 80 web sources, with audio sampled at 44.66 kHz by default.
In order to adapt and perform a fair comparison with the existing benchmark and up-to-date, widely used CMs, we implement the following adaptation schemes on DE2024: 1) Following the fine-tuning setup in
[
6
]
, 60% and 40% of the original dataset is used for training and evaluation, respectively. Among the training data, we randomly picked 5% of them as a development set.
The ratio of real to fake audios across all splits is approximately 39% vs. 61%, with minor variations observed across different durations;
2) To compare with the established baseline
3
3

3

https://huggingface.co/nii-yamagishilab/wav2vec-large-anti-deepfake-nda
, whose pre-trained SSL-based CM is acquired in this study, we segment both training and evaluation partitions into various lengths ranging from 4 seconds (as reported in
[
6
]
) to 50 seconds. The training partition is used to construct a knowledge base, maintaining the same real-to-fake data ratio. The evaluation partition serves as the query data, containing zero-day attacks. They are detailed in next subsection.

2.2
Knowledge Database Construction

As shown in the upper part of Fig.
2
, our methodology utilizes a
knowledge database
to enhance both classification accuracy and feature representation for query recordings that may contain zero-day audio deepfake attacks. Let
\mathcal{K}
denote a knowledge database of
N
labeled utterances. For each
i\in\mathcal{K}
, we have the following information extracted and stored.

•

\mathbf{x}_{i,cm}
: CM feature vector. This vector is extracted from the SSL-based CM system, specifically from the output of the SSL frontend (e.g., Wav2Vec2
[
15
]
). The frame-level features are averaged across all frames to produce a single utterance-level representation vector.

•

\mathbf{x}_{i,prof}
: Profile feature vector. This vector captures multiple voice and speaker attributes using well-trained neural network models. Various attributes including age, gender, emotion, and voice quality such as pitch and rhythm, are inferred and concatenated to form a comprehensive profile feature vector that describes different aspects of the voice and speaker identity.

•

y_{i}\in\{0,1\}
: This is ground truth binary label indicating whether the utterance is real (0) or fake (1). These labels are manually annotated and provided in the source metadata.

•

s_{i}\in(0,1)
: This is the prediction score for knowledge ensemble. The scalar value is also obtained from the CM, where the SSL feature vector is passed through the linear layer of the CM, followed by a softmax to produce the score.

The detailed description of the SSL-based CM system and the profile feature extraction process is presented in Section
3.1
.

2.3
Knowledge Retrieval & Ensemble

After constructing the knowledge database, we proceed to the retrieval and ensemble stage, shown in the bottom part of Fig.
2
. Let us focus on the retrieval stage first. Given a query utterance
q
, we use the same SSL CM and profile extractor as constructing
\mathcal{K}
to obtain the query CM feature vector
\mathbf{x}_{q,cm}
, query feature profile vector
\mathbf{x}_{q,prof}
, and prediction score
s_{q}
. Note that we do not have the binary ground truth label from the query side.
Denoting the query and knowledge vectors as
\mathbf{x}_{q}
and
\mathbf{x}_{i}
, the set of the
k
nearest neighbors
\mathcal{N}_{q}
is found by selecting the indices of the vectors with the highest similarity scores:

\mathcal{N}_{q}=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}\text{ where }d(\mathbf{x}_{q},\mathbf{x}_{i_{1}})\geq\ldots\geq d(\mathbf{x}_{q},\mathbf{x}_{i_{k}}),

(1)

and for any index
j\notin\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}
, we have
d(\mathbf{x}_{q},\mathbf{x}_{i_{k}})\geq d(\mathbf{x}_{q},\mathbf{x}_{j})
. Note that
d(\mathbf{x}_{q},\mathbf{x}_{i})
is the similarity function. In this study, We use cosine similarity:

d(\mathbf{x}_{q},\mathbf{x}_{i})=\frac{\mathbf{x}_{q}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{i}}{||\mathbf{x}_{q}||_{2}\cdot||\mathbf{x}_{i}||_{2}}

(2)

The query and knowledge vectors include respectively either the CM
(\mathbf{x}_{q,cm},\mathbf{x}_{i,cm})
or the profile feature vectors
(\mathbf{x}_{q,prof},\mathbf{x}_{i,prof})
, depending on the
retrieval strategies
briefed below:

•

CM-only
: Using CM feature vectors solely for retrieval.
\mathbf{x}_{q}=\mathbf{x}_{q,cm}
,
\mathbf{x}_{i}=\mathbf{x}_{i,cm}
.

•

Profile-only
: Use profile feature vectors solely for retrieval. Here,
\mathbf{x}_{q}=\mathbf{x}_{q,prof}
,
\mathbf{x}_{i}=\mathbf{x}_{i,prof}
.

•

Hybrid:
Retrieving
k_{1}=\lfloor k/2\rfloor
neighbors using CM features and
k_{2}=\lceil k/2\rceil
neighbors using profile features. Defining
\text{argtop}_{k}d(.)
as the set of
k
values that holds highest values of
d(.)
, we have:

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{q}^{(cm,k_{1})}

\displaystyle=\text{argtop}_{k_{1}}d(\mathbf{x}_{q,cm},\mathbf{x}_{i,cm})

(3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{q}^{(prof,k_{2})}

\displaystyle=\text{argtop}_{k_{2}}d(\mathbf{x}_{q,prof},\mathbf{x}_{i,prof})

(4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{q}^{(hybrid)}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{N}_{q}^{(cm,k_{1})}\cup\mathcal{N}_{q}^{(prof,k_{2})}

(5)

Note that
|\mathcal{N}_{q}^{(hybrid)}|\leq k
due to potential overlapping retrieval results between the two involved strategies.
We then fetch the neighbor labels, scores, and feature vectors from
\mathcal{K}
, denoting them as
Y_{q}=\{y_{i_{j}}\}_{j=1}^{k}
,
S_{q}=\{s_{i_{j}}\}_{j=1}^{k}
, and
X_{q}=\{\mathbf{x}_{i_{j}}\}_{j=1}^{k}
respectively. With this retrieved set of data, we employ three simple and comprehensive
ensemble strategies
to aggregate the retrieved information into a final prediction:

•

Majority Voting (MV):
The ensemble score is simply computed by identifying the majority label among the retrieved utterances:
\hat{s}_{q}=\arg\max_{c\in\{0,1\}}\sum_{j=1}^{|\mathcal{N}_{q}|}\mathbb{I}[y_{i_{j}}=c]
, where
\mathbb{I}[\cdot]
is the indicator function and
c
is the binary class label. Thus, the prediction score is numerically binary:
\hat{s}_{q}\in\{0,1\}
.

•

Ratio-based Scoring (Ratio):
The ensemble score is computed as the proportion of positive samples out of those retrieved:
\hat{s}_{q}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{N}_{q}|}\sum_{j=1}^{|\mathcal{N}_{q}|}y_{i_{j}}=\frac{|\{j:y_{i_{j}}=1\}|}{|\mathcal{N}_{q}|}
.

•

Score-level Averaging (Avg):
The ensemble score is simply computed as the mean average of the retrieved prediction scores:
\hat{s}_{q}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{N}_{q}|}\sum_{j=1}^{|\mathcal{N}_{q}|}s_{i_{j}}
.

, resulting in

systems (

Experimental Setup

3.1
Self-Supervised Learning Models

This section describes the two SSL-based models applied for both knowledge database construction and query inference.

Countermeasure
. We use an open-sourced SSL-based CM model

4

https://huggingface.co/nii-yamagishilab/wav2vec-large-anti-deepfake-nda
for this study. The model uses a Wav2Vec frontend
[
15
]
to extract feature vectors, followed by a backend classifier with adaptive pooling, a fully-connected MLP (without bias), and softmax operation for prediction scores. The model was
post-trained

5

5

As described in
[
11
]
, post-training is an intermediate supervised training phase for SSL models, with specifically collected data and training schemes to adapt them for certain task. Here, the task is ADD.
on approximately 74,000 hours of real and fake audio clips from various datasets, without any form of data augmentation. The model architecture and training are described in further detail in
[
11
]
.

Profile extractor
. We employ
vox_profile
6
6

6

https://github.com/tiantiaf0627/vox-profile-release

[
16
]
to extract profile features using well-trained foundation models. We construct vectors by concatenating four attributes: 1)
Age
: a scalar representing age value in terms of 10 uniformed intervals from 0 to 100 years old
7
7

7

e.g., A value of 7 means an estimated age of 61-70 years old.
; 2)
Gender
: a 2-dimensional one-hot vector for male/female classification; 3)
Emotion
: a scalar indicating the emotion trait (anger, fear, neutral, etc.) and 256-dimensional embedding vector extracted from the intermediate of the foundational model; 4)
Voice quality
: a 25-dimensional vector capturing five perceptual aspects: pitch, volume, clarity, rhythm, and voice texture, following the taxonomy defined in
[
17
]
. More details about how the representations are extracted can be found in
[
16
]

3.2
Implementation & Evaluation

Baselines
. To demonstrate the effectiveness of RA against fine-tuning, we compare it with two baselines: 1) The post-trained model that returns
s_{q}
from the DE2024 evaluation set; 2) A fine-tuned model from the post-trained one using the DE2024 training set (same data used for knowledge database). For fine-tuning, we use a learning rate of
1e^{-7}
and other settings consistent with
[
11
]
. We fine-tune the post-trained model for 10 epochs and infer
s_{q}
for evaluation, with notable improvements from the baseline, as reported in next section.

Hyperparameters
. The only hyperparameter is the number of utterances
k
to be retrieved from the knowledge database. We range
k
from 5 to 200 at several discrete values. For each system ID, we report results using the optimal
k
value based on performance on the development set, and analyze the effect of
k
through ablation study.

Hardware
. All SSL model inference and fine-tuning experiments are performed on a computing node with eight AMD EPYC 9654 CPUs and one NVIDIA H100 GPU. Knowledge retrieval, ensemble, and evaluation use the same node without involving the GPU resources.

Evaluation metric
. We acquire two metrics in this study, equal error rate (EER), following standard practice in ADD, and accuracy with a fixed threshold of 0.5. We choose a fixed threshold instead of an optimized one from a development set of data because zero-day attacks involve new, unseen artifacts that make it difficult to derive proper thresholds from reigning data, similar to the challenges of model fine-tuning in practical scenarios.

Results and Analysis

4.1
Main results

Results for different RA strategies are presented in Table
1
. There are several key findings which are listed below.

Retrieval augmentation methods which directly incorporate class labels for knowledge ensemble can handle zero-day attacks as effectively as fine-tuned models.
The proposed methods with majority voting (MV) and ratio-based ensemble consistently outperform the baseline and generally reach comparable performance to that of fine-tuned models. The lowest and second-lowest EERs are all achieved by the proposed methods using majority voting, respectively from
S07
and
S01
8
8

8

Here, it is worth noting that since there are only two possible values for MV, the EER computed is the average of false acceptance and miss rates.
. While fine-tuned models demonstrate the highest accuracy on shorter durations, the proposed methods, especially those using ratio-based ensemble (
S02
,
S05
,
S08
), achieve comparable performance on longer durations. In particular,
S08
achieves either second-highest or highest accuracy among all systems. However, score averaging for the ensemble often cannot outperform the baseline. The above findings demonstrate the efficacy of RA for ADD as a binary classification task. Future work may further this approach for multi-classification tasks such as spoof-aware speaker verification
[
18
]
.

The profile information does not work well independently as retrieval method but is sufficient compensation for the CM features
. Regarding the retrieval method, the analysis and results show that using CM-only or Hybrid for retrieval achieved comparable performance to that of the fine-tuned models. In contrast, using only profile feature vectors, while still outperforming the baseline, could not achieve comparable performance to the fine-tuned models. This may be because the foundation models used for profile feature extraction primarily store voice and speaker information rather than real/fake information, as mentioned in Section
3.1
. However, the performance of Hybrid indicates that voice and speaker information can effectively complement CM features. In the next subsection, we investigate the individual usefulness of these components.
Apart from the above main findings, it may be also worth noting that audio duration has an impact on the relative efficacy of RA. The performance differences between our proposed methods, baseline, and fine-tuned models decrease with longer audio segments. This may be due to more informative embeddings, making models less sensitive to different strategies. Investigating these duration-dependent effects is a possible direction for future work.

Ablation study

In this section, we investigate two variables: the number of retrieved utterances
k
from the knowledge database, and the effect of different attributes embedded in the profile feature vector.

The effect of number of retrieved utterances
. We pick
S07
and
S08
for this ablation. We vary number of utterances retrieved from the knowledge
k
from 5 to 200, as same in the main experiment. We present the results using one shorter (4 seconds) and one longer duration (30 seconds) cases. The performance of both systems measured by EER is presented in Fig.

The effect of voice profile informative attributes.
We use
S08

While properties more closely related to speakers, such as age, sex and emotion, have a relatively negligible impact, removing voice quality substantially deteriorates the performance, especially for shorter segments. Considering the nature of the DE2024, this indicates the relatively higher importance of voice qualities for the retrieval, which can be affected by not only the speaker, but also the surrounding environment and context.

5
Conclusion

In this study, we addressed zero-day attacks in audio deepfake detection, where systems must use existing knowledge to rapidly handle unseen attacks. We have introduced an effective strategy based on knowledge databases and retrieval augmentation, with various retrieval and ensemble methods, without any manner of fine-tuning or additional training. The framework uses both state-of-the-art CM and voice profile representations. Leveraging DE2024 with various length of segments, we found that the proposed method, especially when using both representations and statistics of retrieved data directly for ensemble, achieves competitive performance against fine-tuned CM models. Future work may explore acquiring temporal information via frame-level SSL features, leveraging profile information more effectively, and extending the framework to multi-classification ADD-related tasks.

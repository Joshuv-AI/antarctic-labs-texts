Integrated density of states for the sub-Laplacian on Heisenberg groups
Zouhaïr Mouayn

Abstract
We rederive the expression of the integrated density of states for the sub-Laplacian on Heisenberg groups ℍ n \mathbb{H}_{n} by using its resolvent kernel.



1
Introduction

The integrated density of states (IDS) has its origin in physics, that when
studying properties of the spectrum of a Hamiltonian operator
H
, it turns
out that one of the relevant objects is a distribution function, associated
to a certain probability measure, encoding the distribution of the spectrum
of
H
. Note that spectrum of a self-adjoint operator is a subset of the
real
\mathbb{R}.
Hence, this distribution function is defined on
\mathbb{R}
as well
.
The IDS is the function defined by

\mathbb{R}\ni\lambda\rightarrow\ \mathcal{N}\left(\lambda\right):=\lim_{\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\text{ }\Omega\in\mathcal{O}}\frac{\sharp\left\{\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda\right\}}{Vol(\Omega)}

(1.1)

where
\sharp\left\{\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda\right\}
is the number of
eigenvalues less than
\lambda
of
H
restricted to a bounded domain
\Omega
with appropriate boundary conditions.
\mathcal{O}
is a family
of bounded open sets which is introduced to specify the way expanding
\Omega
to whole space
\mathbb{R}^{d}.
From the definition (
1.1
), we see that the integrated density of states gives the average
number of the possible energy levels below the energy
\lambda
per unit
volume.

One may also define the IDS by (
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
):

\mathcal{N}\left(\lambda\right):=\lim_{\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\Omega\in\mathcal{O}}\frac{tr(\chi_{\Omega}P_{\left(-\infty,\lambda\right]}\left(H\right)\chi_{\Omega})}{Vol(\Omega)}

(1.2)

where
\chi_{\Omega}
denotes the multiplication operator by the
characteristic function of the domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
P_{J}\left(H\right)
denotes the spectral projection of
H
for a Borel
set
J\subset\mathbb{R}.
In this sense the IDS is an average of the kernel
of the spectral projection operator and
[
16
]
have showed that it
can serve as a substitude for the eigenvalue counting function
\sharp\left\{\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda\right\}
. This definition is motivated by
well known Weyl asymptotic law

\sharp\left\{\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda\right\}=c_{n}Vol\left(\Omega\right)\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}n}+o\left(\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}n}\right)

(1.3)

when
H=-\Delta
, the Laplacian on a compact domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with smooth boundary with Dirichlet or Neumann boundary conditions.
By dividing (
1.3
) by the volume of
\Omega
and observing
that the ratio
N\left(\lambda\right)/Vol\left(\Omega\right)
is equal
to the average value of the diagonal function
\Phi\left(\lambda;x,x\right)
on
\Omega,
where
\Phi\left(\lambda;x,y\right)
stands
for the kernel of the spectral projection operator
E_{\lambda}
onto the
\left[0,\lambda\right]
of the spectrum,

\Phi\left(\lambda;x,y\right)=\sum\limits_{\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda}\phi_{j}\left(x\right)\overline{\phi_{j}\left(y\right)}

(1.4)

where
\left\{\phi_{j}\right\}
is an orthonormal basis of eigenfunctions
associated with eigenvalues
\left\{\lambda_{j}\right\}.
Indeed, we have

\mathcal{N}\left(\lambda\right)=\frac{1}{Vol\left(\Omega\right)}\int\limits_{\Omega}\Phi\left(\lambda;x,x\right)d\mu\left(x\right),

(1.5)

where
d\mu
denotes the Lebesgue measure or the Riemannian measure on
\Omega
and one may rewrite (
1.3
) as follows

\sharp\left\{\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda\right\}=c_{n}\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}n}+o\left(\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}n}\right),

(1.6)

c_{n}
is constant that depends on the dimension and can be explicitly
determined.

Since
\mathcal{N}\left(\lambda\right)
is nondecreasing function, its
derivative
\rho\left(\lambda\right)=d\mathcal{N}\left(\lambda\right)

/d\lambda
is well defined as Steiljes-Lebesgue positive measure and is
called the density of states (DOS). The latter can be exploited, in the case
of the Landau Hamiltonian, to measure, in the gap between Landau levels,
energies of new states that could be created if a random potential is added
\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{BVS}{}{}]}.
For different mathematical interest on the IDS we refer to
[
14
]
and references therein.

In this Note, our aim is to rederive the expression of the integrated
density of states for the sub-Laplacian on Heisenberg groups
\mathbb{H}_{n}

by using its resolvent kernel as obtained in
[
8
]
.

2
Magnetic Laplacians in
\mathbb{C}^{n}

In suitable units and up to additive constant, the motion of a charged
particle in a constant uniform magnetic field in
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
is
described by the Schrodinger operator

H_{B}=-\frac{1}{4}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\left(\left(\partial_{x_{j}}+iBy_{j}\right)^{2}+\left(\partial_{y_{j}}-iBx_{j}\right)^{2}\right)-\frac{n}{2}

(2.1)

acting on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n},d\mu)
,
d\mu
being the Lebesgue
measure on
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
,
B>0
is the strength of the magnetic
field. We identify the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
with
\mathbb{C}^{n}
in the usual way to use the complex structure. The operator
H_{B}
can be represented by the operator

\Delta_{B}:=e^{\frac{1}{2}B\left|z\right|^{2}}H_{B}\text{ }e^{-\frac{1}{2}B\left|z\right|^{2}}

(2.2)

According to (
2.2
), an arbitrary state
\phi
of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n},d\mu)
is represented by the function
Q\left[\phi\right]
of
L^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},e^{-B\left|z\right|^{2}}d\mu)
defined
by

Q\left[\phi\right]\left(z\right):=e^{\frac{1}{2}B\left|z\right|^{2}}\mathsf{\ }\phi\left(z\right),\text{ \ \ }z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

(2.3)

The unitary map
Q
is called ground state transformation. Explicit
expression for the operator (
2.2
) is :

\Delta_{B}=-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z_{j}\partial\overline{z_{j}}}+B\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\overline{z_{j}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{z_{j}}}.

(2.4)

From now on, we take
B=1
in (
2.4
) and we consider the
magnetic Laplacian
\widetilde{\Delta}:=\Delta_{1}
acting in the Hilbert
space
\mathfrak{H}=

L^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},e^{-\left|z\right|^{2}}d\mu)
of complex-valued Gaussian square integrable functions. The
spectrum of
\ \widetilde{\Delta}
consists of eigenvalues of infinite
multiplicity of the form (Landau levels):
\epsilon_{m}^{1}:=m,

m=0,1,2,...
, and admits a spectral decomposition
[
6
]
. i.e., an
increasing family of projectors
E_{\lambda}
satisfying

\mathbf{1}_{\mathfrak{H}}=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dE_{\lambda\text{
\ }}\text{{\ }and \ \ }\widetilde{\Delta}=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\lambda dE_{\lambda}

(2.5)

where
\mathbf{1}_{\mathfrak{H}}
is the identity operator, and the
projector operator
E_{\lambda}
are given by,

E_{\lambda}\left[\phi\right]\left(z\right)=\int\limits_{\mathbb{C}^{n}\mathsf{\ }}\Phi\left(\lambda;z,w\right)\phi\left(w\right)e^{-\left|w\right|^{2}}d\mu\left(w\right)\text{ }

(2.6)

with the kernel

\Phi_{\lambda}^{\widetilde{\Delta}}\left(z,w\right)=\pi^{-n}e^{\left\langle z,w\right\rangle}L_{\lfloor\lambda\rfloor}^{\left(n\right)}(\left|z-w\right|^{2})

(2.7)

if
\lambda\geq 0
and
E_{\lambda}\left[\phi\right]\left(z\right)=0
when
\lambda<0.
Here
\left[a\right]
denotes the greatest integer
not exceeding
a
and

L_{k}^{\left(\alpha\right)}(x)=\sum\limits_{j=o}^{k}\left(-1\right)^{j}\dbinom{k+\alpha}{k-j}\frac{x^{j}}{j!},\text{ \ }\alpha>-1.

(2.8)

is the Laguerre polynomial. This spectral decomposition leads to the
resolvent kernel of
\ \widetilde{\Delta}
(
\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{AM-jmp}{}{}]},
p.6939
):

R(\zeta;z,w)=-\pi^{-n}e^{\left\langle z,w\right\rangle}\Gamma(-\zeta)\Psi\left(-\zeta,n,\left|z-w\right|^{2}\right)

(2.9)

where
\Psi
is the Tricomi function which can be defined (
\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Tri}{}{}]},
p.
56) as

\Psi\left(a,c;\xi\right)=\frac{\Gamma\left(1-c\right)}{\Gamma\left(a-c+1\right)}._{1}\digamma_{1}\left(a,c;\xi\right)+\frac{\Gamma\left(c-1\right)}{\Gamma\left(a\right)}\xi^{1-c}._{1}\digamma_{1}\left(a,c;\xi\right)

(2.10)

where

\,{{}_{1}\digamma_{1}}\left(a,c;\xi\right)=\frac{\Gamma(c)}{\Gamma(a)}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{+\infty}\frac{\Gamma(a+j)}{\Gamma(c+j)}\frac{\xi^{j}}{j!}

(2.11)

is the confluent hypergeometric function defined with the usual condition
c\neq 0,-1,-2,\cdots
.

Remark 2.1.
For

n=1,
which corresponds to the planar
Landau Hamiltonian, we should note that, up to a unitary transformation, the
kernel function
\Phi_{\lambda}^{\widetilde{\Delta}}\left(z,w\right)

in (
2.7
) appeared first in (
[
11
]
, Eq. (4
),
p.133) as
the Dirac density matrix (with the notation
g\left(\mathbf{r}_{2},\mathbf{r}_{1};E\right)
,
\mathbf{r}_{2}\equiv z,\mathbf{r}_{1}\equiv w,E\equiv\lambda).

Proposition 2.1
.
The integrated density of states for the
magnetic Laplacian
\ \widetilde{\Delta}
is given by

\mathcal{N}_{\widetilde{\Delta}}\left(\lambda\right)=\frac{\left(\lfloor\lambda\rfloor+n\right)!}{\pi^{n}\lfloor\lambda\rfloor!n!},\text{ }n\geq 1.

(2.12)

The proof is based on the use of (
1.5
) and (
2.7
)

\mathcal{N}_{\widetilde{\Delta}}\left(\lambda\right)=\frac{1}{Vol\left(\Omega\right)}\int\limits_{\Omega}\pi^{-n}e^{\left\langle z,z\right\rangle}L_{\lfloor\lambda\rfloor}^{\left(n\right)}(0)e^{-\left|z\right|^{2}}d\mu\left(z\right)

(2.13)

=\frac{1}{Vol\left(\Omega\right)}\pi^{-n}L_{\lfloor\lambda\rfloor}^{\left(n\right)}(0)\int\limits_{\Omega}d\mu\left(x\right)=\pi^{-n}L_{\lfloor\lambda\rfloor}^{\left(n\right)}(0),

(2.14)

together with the fact that

L_{j}^{\left(\alpha\right)}(0)=\frac{\Gamma\left(j+\alpha+1\right)}{j!\Gamma\left(\alpha+1\right)}.

(2.15)

Remark 2.2.
When
n=1,
the expression (
2.11
)
reduces to
\mathcal{N}_{\widetilde{\Delta}}\left(\lambda\right)=\pi^{-1}\left(1+\lfloor\lambda\rfloor\right)
which is corresponds (by
changing notations as :
1+2\lambda\equiv E/B
and
l\equiv\epsilon_{m}^{1}=m
) to well-known IDS for the Landau Hamiltonian on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
as pointed out by S. Nakamura
\left(\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Nak}{}{}]}\text{, p.149}\right)

who rederived it by proving that it was independent of the choice of the
boundary condition.

3
Link with the Heisenberg sublaplacian

The Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is the nilpotent Lie group whose
underlying manifold is
\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
with coordinates
\left(z_{1},...,z_{n};\tau\right)=\left(z,\tau\right)
and whose group
law is

\left(z,\tau\right).\left(w,s\right)=z+w,\tau+s+2\mathop{\mathrm{I}m}\left\langle z,w\right\rangle,

(3.1)

where
\left\langle z,w\right\rangle=\sum_{1}^{n}z_{j}\overline{w_{j}}.

Letting
z=x+iy,
then,
x_{1},...,x_{n},y_{1},...,y_{n},\tau
are real
coordinates on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. We set

\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}-i\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j}}\right),\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{z}_{j}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}+i\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j}}\right).

(3.2)

The operator

\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}:=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}\left[-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z_{j}\partial\overline{z}_{j}}-\left|z_{j}\right|^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}+i\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\left(z_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}-\overline{z}_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{z}_{j}}\right)\right]

(3.3)

is left-invariant and subelleptic of order
\frac{1}{2}
at each
\left(z,\tau\right)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}

(\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Foll}{}{}]},
p.
374)

.

Note that operators
\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}
and
\widetilde{\Delta}
may be intertwined in the following way by the relation
\left(\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{AskMou}{}{}]}\text{, p}.4\right):

\frac{1}{2\lambda}\mathcal{\tau}_{\lambda}\circ\left(\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{F}\left[\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\right]-n\lambda\right)\circ\left(\mathcal{\tau}_{\lambda}\right)^{-1}=\widetilde{\Delta}

(3.4)

where
\tau_{\lambda}
is thr map
L^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\mu)\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},e^{-\left|z\right|^{2}}d\mu)

defined by

\mathcal{\tau}_{\lambda}\left[f\right]\left(z\right):=2^{-n}\lambda^{-n}e^{\frac{1}{2}\left|z\right|^{2}}f\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\lambda}}z\right),\text{ \ }z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}

(3.5)

and
\mathcal{F}:

L^{2}(\mathbb{H}_{n}\ ,d\mu\left(z\right)dt)\rightarrow

L^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\mu)
denotes the partial Fourier
transform with respect to the variable
t
, defined by

\mathcal{F}\left[\varphi\right]\left(z,\lambda\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi\left(z,t\right)e^{-i\lambda t}dt=\lim_{\rho\rightarrow+\infty}\int\limits_{\left|t\right|\leq\rho}\varphi\left(z,t\right)e^{-i\lambda t}dt

(3.6)

which should be understood in the
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
-norm
sense.

The connection given by (
3.4
) together with the expression (
2.9
) of the resolent kernel of the magnetic Laplacian
\widetilde{\Delta}
enables to establish that for
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}

with
\mathop{\mathrm{R}e}\zeta<0,
the resolvent operator of
\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}

has the form (
\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{AskMou}{}{}, p.4, Theorem\, 3.2]}):

\left(\zeta-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}_{n}}\right)^{-1}\left[\varphi\right]\left(z,t\right)=\int\limits_{\mathbb{H}_{n}}\mathcal{R}\left(\zeta;\left(z,\tau\right),\left(w,s\right)\right)\varphi\left(w,s\right)d\nu\left(w,s\right)

(3.5)

where

\mathcal{R}\left(\zeta;\left(z,\tau\right),\left(w,s\right)\right)=-2\pi^{-n-\frac{1}{2}}\int\limits_{0}^{+\infty}x^{n-1}\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}-\frac{\zeta}{2x}\right)\Psi\left(\frac{n}{2}-\frac{\zeta}{2x},n,2x\left|z-w\right|^{2}\right)

(3.6)

\times\exp\left(-x\left|z-w\right|^{2}\right)\cos\left(x\left(\tau-s\right)+2x\mathop{\mathrm{I}m}\left\langle z,w\right\rangle\right)dx.

Here
d\nu
denotes the ordinary Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}\equiv\mathbb{H}_{n},
which is both left and right invariant Haar
measure on the group
\mathbb{H}_{n}
.

We should note, in passing, that in the limit case
\zeta=0,
Eq. (
3.6
) reduces to

-\mathcal{R}\left(0,\left(z,\tau\right),\left(w,s\right)\right)=G_{0}^{F}\left(\left(z,\tau\right)\circ\left(w,s\right)^{-1}\right)

(3.7)

for any
\left(z,\tau\right),\left(w,s\right)\in\mathbb{H}_{n}
, where

G_{0}^{F}\left(u,t\right)=c_{n}\left|\left(u,t\right)\right|^{-2n},\text{ \ \ }\left(u,t\right)\in\mathbb{H}_{n}

(3.8)

with
\left|\left(u,t\right)\right|=\left(\left|u\right|^{4}+t^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}
being the homogeneous norm on

\mathbb{H}_{n}.

c_{n}=\left[n\left(n+1\right)\int\limits_{\mathbb{H}_{n}}\left|u\right|^{2}\left(\left|u\right|^{4}+t^{2}+1\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}\left(n+4\right)}d\nu\left(u,t\right)\right]^{-1}

(3.9)

This means that we may recover the fundamental solution for
\Delta_{\mathbb{H}_{n}}
with source at
\left(0,0\right)
as

\left(\Delta_{\mathbb{H}_{n}}\left[\phi\right],G_{0}^{F}\right)=\phi\left(0\right),\text{ }\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{H}_{n}\right)

(3.10)

in agreement with result
(\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Foll}{}{}]},
Theorem 2, p. 375
)
of Folland who
used an analogous fact to
\left\|x\right\|^{2-d}
being (a constant
multiple of) the fundamental solution of the Laplacian on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with sourece at the origine
0
. In this respect, Appendix A contains
detailed calculations concerning the limit case
\zeta=0
showing that the
fundamental solution
G_{0}^{F}\left(z,\tau\right)
in (
3.8
) may also be derived from the resolvent kernel (
3.6
) of
\Delta_{\mathbb{H}_{n}}\left[\phi\right]
. In particular, this provides
G_{0}^{F}\left(z,\tau\right)
with the integral

G_{0}^{F}\left(z,\tau\right)=\pi^{-n-1}2^{n+1}\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)\int_{0}^{+\infty}x^{n-1}e^{-x|z|^{2}}\Psi\left(\frac{n}{2},n;2x|z|^{2}\right)\cos\left(\tau x\right)dx

(3.11)

in terms of the Tricomi
\Psi
-function as mentionned by
[
13
]
.

Integrated density of states for
\Delta_{\mathbb{H}_{n}}

By using the following summation formula (
[
9
]
, p. 92):

\Gamma\left(a\right)\Psi\left(a,c;u\right)=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\left(j+a\right)}L_{j}^{\left(c-1\right)}\left(u\right)

(4.1)

in the Abel sense, the authors (
[
8
]

obtained the following form for the resolent kernel (
3.6
) as

\mathcal{R}\left(\zeta;\left(z,\tau\right),\left(w,s\right)\right)=\int\limits_{0}^{+\infty}\Phi_{\lambda}^{\Delta_{\mathbb{H}_{n}}}\left(\left(z,\tau\right),\left(w,s\right)\right)\frac{1}{\zeta-\lambda}d\lambda

(4.2)

where

\Phi_{\lambda}^{\Delta_{\mathbb{H}_{n}}}\left(\left(z,\tau\right),\left(w,s\right)\right)=\lambda^{n}\pi^{-n-\frac{1}{2}}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{+\infty}\frac{\exp\left(\frac{-\rho\lambda}{2\left(2j+n\right)}\right)}{\left(2j+n\right)^{n+1}}L_{j}^{\left(n-1\right)}\left(\frac{\lambda\rho}{2j+n}\right)\cos\left(\frac{\tau\lambda}{2\left(2j+n\right)}\right)

(4.3)

with
\rho=\left|z-w\right|^{2}
and
\tau=t-s+2\mathop{\mathrm{I}m}\left\langle z,w\right\rangle
, is the kernel of the spectral density
operator
\frac{dE_{\lambda}}{d\lambda}
as proved in
[
8
]
where
the distributional sense is discussed.

Proposition

4.1.

The integtated density
of states for
\Delta_{\mathbb{H}_{n}}
is given by

\mathcal{N}\left(\lambda\right)=\gamma_{n}\lambda^{n}\mathit{\ }

(4.4)

where
\gamma_{n}>0
is the constant given by

\gamma_{n}=\frac{1}{\pi^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma\left(n\right)}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{+\infty}\frac{\Gamma\left(j+n\right)}{j!\left(2j+n\right)^{n+1}}

(4.5)

Proof.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}_{n}
be a domain
of finite volume. According to (
1.5
) , the IDS can be
expressed as

\mathcal{N}\left(\lambda\right):=\frac{1}{Vol\left(\Omega\right)}\int\limits_{\Omega}\Phi_{\lambda}^{\Delta_{\mathbb{H}_{n}}}\left(\left(z,\tau\right),\left(w,s\right)\right)d\mu\left(w,s\right).

(4.6)

By substiting
\Phi_{\lambda}^{\Delta_{\mathbb{H}_{n}}}\left(\left(z,\tau\right),\left(w,s\right)\right)
by its expression (
4.3
) into (
4.6
), Eq. (
4.6
) reduces to

\mathcal{N}\left(\lambda\right)=\frac{\lambda^{n}}{\pi^{n+\frac{1}{2}}}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\left(2j+n\right)^{n+1}}L_{j}^{\left(n-1\right)}\left(0\right)

(4.7)

Finally, we use (
2.14
) in order to get the exact value of
L_{j}^{\left(n-1\right)}\left(0\right)
.

A6

\mathcal{R}_{0}=\frac{2^{n}}{\pi^{n+\frac{1}{2}}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}x^{n-1}\left[\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\frac{n}{2}t}\exp\left(-\frac{\mu x}{e^{t}-1}\right)\left(1-e^{-t}\right)^{-n}dt\right]e^{-\frac{\mu}{2}x}\cos\left(x\theta\right)dx.

(A8)

Now, applying the identity (
[
10
]
, p.498):

\int_{0}^{+\infty}x^{\nu-1}e^{-\alpha x}\cos(\theta x)dx=\Gamma\left(\nu\right)\left(\alpha^{2}+\theta^{2}\right)^{-\frac{\nu}{2}}\cos\left(\nu\arctan\left(\frac{\theta}{\alpha}\right)\right),\mathop{\mathrm{R}e}\nu>0,\mathop{\mathrm{R}e}\alpha>|\mathop{\mathrm{I}m}(\theta)|

(A9)

for parameters
\nu=n
and
\alpha=\frac{\mu}{2}\coth\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)
and Eq. (
A8
) reads

\mathcal{R}_{0}=2^{n}\Gamma(n)\pi^{-n-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{e^{-\frac{n}{2}t}}{\left(1-e^{-t}\right)^{n}}\left(\left(\frac{\mu}{2}\coth\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)\right)^{2}+\theta^{2}\right)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\cos\left(n\arctan\left(\frac{\theta}{\frac{\mu}{2}\coth\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)}\right)\right)dt

(A10)

=\frac{\Gamma(n)}{\pi^{n+\frac{1}{2}}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(\sinh\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)\right)^{-n}\left(\frac{\mu}{2}\coth\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)\right)^{-n}\left(1+\left(\frac{\theta}{\frac{\mu}{2}\coth\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)}\right)^{2}\right)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\cos\left(n\arctan\left(\frac{\theta}{\frac{\mu}{2}\coth\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)}\right)\right)dt

(A11)

Making the change variable
\rho=\frac{\theta}{\frac{\mu}{2}\coth\frac{t}{2}},
(
A11
) becomes

\mathcal{R}_{0}=\frac{2^{n}\Gamma(n)}{\mu^{n-1}\theta\pi^{n+\frac{1}{2}}}\int_{0}^{\frac{2\theta}{\mu}}\left(1-\left(\frac{\mu}{2\theta}\right)^{2}\rho^{2}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\left(1+\rho^{2}\right)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\cos\left(n\arctan(\rho)\right)d\rho.

(A12)

A second change of variable,
\arctan\left(\rho\right)=u
, shows that (
A12
) can be reduced to

\mathcal{R}_{0}=\frac{2^{n}\Gamma(n)}{\mu^{n-1}\theta\pi^{n+\frac{1}{2}}}\int_{0}^{\arctan(\frac{2\theta}{\mu})}\left(\cos^{2}(u)-\left(\frac{\mu}{2\theta}\right)^{2}\sin^{2}u\right)^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\cos(nu)du.

(A13)

=\frac{2^{\frac{n}{2}+1}\Gamma(n)}{\mu^{n-1}\theta\pi^{n+\frac{1}{2}}}\left(1+\left(\frac{\mu}{2\theta}\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\int_{0}^{\arctan(\frac{2\theta}{\mu})}\left(\cos 2u-\frac{1-\left(\frac{2\theta}{\mu}\right)^{2}}{1+\left(\frac{2\theta}{\mu}\right)^{2}}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\cos(nu)du.

(A14)

Setting
\beta=\left(\frac{2\theta}{\mu}\right)^{2}
and making the
change of variable
2u=\kappa
in (
A14
) gives that

\mathcal{R}_{0}=\frac{2^{\frac{n}{2}}\Gamma(n)}{\mu^{n-1}\theta\pi^{n+\frac{1}{2}}}\left(\frac{\beta+1}{\beta}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\int_{0}^{2\arctan(\frac{2\theta}{\mu})}\left(\cos\kappa-\frac{1-\beta}{1+\beta}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\cos\left(\frac{n}{2}\kappa\right)d\kappa.

(A15)

Now, by putting

\varepsilon=\arccos\left(\frac{1-\beta}{1+\beta}\right)=2\arctan\left(\frac{2\theta}{\mu}\right).

(A16)

We are now in position to apply the identity (
[
10
]
, p.406):

\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\left(\cos(x)-\cos(\varepsilon)\right)^{\nu-\frac{1}{2}}\cos(ax)dx=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\left(\sin(\varepsilon)\right)^{\nu}\Gamma\left(\nu+\frac{1}{2}\right)P_{a-\frac{1}{2}}^{-\nu}\left(\cos(\varepsilon)\right),\text{ }\mathop{\mathrm{R}e}\nu>-\frac{1}{2},a>0,\text{ }0<\varepsilon<\pi

(A17)

where

P_{\lambda}^{\nu}(x):=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\nu)}\left(\frac{x+1}{x-1}\right)^{\nu/2}{{}_{2}\digamma_{1}}\left(-\lambda,\lambda+1;1-\nu;\frac{1-x}{2}\right)

(A18)

denotes the Legendre function of the first kind (
[
10
]
, p. 959)
In our setting, the integral in (
A17
) reads

\int_{0}^{2\arctan(\frac{2\theta}{\mu})}\left(\cos(\kappa)-\frac{1-\beta}{1+\beta}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{2}-\frac{1}{2}}\cos\left(\frac{n}{2}\kappa\right)d\kappa=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\left(\sin(\varepsilon)\right)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)P_{\frac{n-1}{2}}^{-\left(\frac{n-1}{2}\right)}\left(\cos\varepsilon\right).

(A19)

Hence, using the Gegenbauer representation (
[
10
]
, p.959):

P_{\sigma}^{-\sigma}\left(\cos(\varepsilon)\right)=\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(1+\sigma\right)}\left(\frac{1}{2}\sin(\varepsilon)\right)^{\sigma}

(A20)

with
\sigma=\frac{n-1}{2}
, we can write the right hand side in (
A19
) as

\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\left(\sin(\varepsilon)\right)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)P_{\frac{n-1}{2}}^{-\left(\frac{n-1}{2}\right)}\left(\cos(\varepsilon)\right)=\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}\sqrt{\pi}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)}\left(1-\cos^{2}(\varepsilon)\right)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}.

(A21)

Returning back to (
A15
), keeping in mind the expression of
\varepsilon
given through (
A16
), we obtain that

\mathcal{R}_{0}=\frac{2^{n-1}}{\mu^{n-1}\theta\pi^{n}}\frac{\Gamma(n)\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)}\left(\frac{\beta+1}{\beta}\right)^{-\frac{n}{2}}.

(A22)

Now, replacing
\beta
by
\left(\frac{2\theta}{\mu}\right)^{2}
and
using the expressions of
\mu
and
\theta
, we arrive at

\mathcal{R}_{0}=\frac{\Gamma(n)\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)}{\pi^{n}\Gamma\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)}\left(\left(|z-w|^{2}\right)^{2}+\left(\left(\tau-s\right)+2\Im\left\langle z,w\right\rangle\right)^{2}\right)^{-\frac{n}{2}}

(A23)

=\frac{2^{n-1}\Gamma^{2}\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)}{\pi^{n+\frac{1}{2}}}\left(\left(|z-w|^{2}\right)^{2}+\left(\left(\tau-s\right)+2\Im\left\langle z,w\right\rangle\right)^{2}\right)^{-\frac{n}{2}}.

(A24)

The last equality follows using Legendre’s duplication formula (
[
10
]
, p.896):

\Gamma\left(\xi\right)\Gamma\left(\xi+\frac{1}{2}\right)=2^{1-2\xi}\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(2\xi)

(25)

for
\xi=\frac{n}{2}
. Therefore, we assert that

\mathcal{R}_{0}=\frac{2^{n-1}\Gamma^{2}\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)}{\pi^{n+\frac{1}{2}}c_{n}}G_{0}^{F}\left(\left(z,\tau\right)\circ\left(w,s\right)^{-1}\right),

(A26)

where the constant
c_{n}
is as in (
3.9
). In particular, for

\left(w,s\right)=\left(0,0\right)
, Eq. (
A26
) reduces
further to

\mathcal{R}_{0}=\frac{2^{n-1}\Gamma^{2}\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)}{\pi^{n+\frac{1}{2}}c_{n}}G_{0}^{F}\left(z,\tau\right)=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}G_{0}^{F}\left(z,\tau\right).

(A27)

This completes the proof of (
3.7
).

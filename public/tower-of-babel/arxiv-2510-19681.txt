On a refinement of the Ahlswede–Katona Theorem
Jianfeng Hou, Xizhi Liu, Yixiao Zhang

Abstract
A classical theorem of Ahlswede and Katona [ AK78 ] determines the maximum density of the 2 2 -edge star in a graph with a given edge density. Motivated by its application in hypergraph Turán problems, we establish a refinement of their result under the additional assumption that the graph contains a large independent set in which every vertex has high degree. Keywords: the Ahlswede–Katona Theorem, counting stars, the first Zagreb index MSC2020: 05C35, 05D05, 05C07

Introduction

1.1
Definitions and main results

We identify a graph
F
with its edge set, and use
V(F)
to denote its vertex set.
The number of edges in
F
is denoted by
|F|
.
Given integers
n
and
m
with
m\leq\binom{n}{2}
, let
\mathcal{G}(n,m)
be the collection of all graphs on
n
vertices with exactly
m
edges.
For graphs
F
and
G
, let
N(F,G)
denote the number of subgraphs of
G
that are isomorphic to
F
.
A classical problem in extremal graph theory is to determine the maximum possible value of
N(F,G)
when
G\in\mathcal{G}(n,m)
.
One of the earliest result on this topic is the seminal Kruskal–Katona theorem
[
Kru63
,
Kat68
]
, which determines the maximum number of
K_{\ell}
, the complete graph on
\ell
vertices, in a graph with a prescribed number of vertices and edges.

In this work, we focus on the case
F=S_{2}
, the star with two edges (also known as a cherry).
We first introduce two classes of graphs.
Let
n
and
m
be integers satisfying
m\leq\binom{n}{2}
.
Write
m=\binom{k}{2}+\ell
, with
k
and
\ell
are nonnegative integers with
\ell\leq k-1
.
The
quasi-clique

C(n,m)
is the graph on vertex set
[n]
with edge set

\displaystyle\binom{[k]}{2}\cup\big\{\{i,k+1\}\colon i\in[\ell]\big\}.

The
quasi-star

S(n,m)
is the graph on vertex set
[n]
, defined as the complement of
C\left(n,\binom{n}{2}-m\right)
.

The classical theorem of Ahlswede–Katona
[
AK78
]
is stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1

(Ahlswede–Katona
[
AK78
]
)
.

Let
n
and
m
be integers satisfying
m\leq\binom{n}{2}
.
Suppose that
G\in\mathcal{G}(n,m)
.
Then

\displaystyle N(S_{2},G)\leq\max\big\{N\big(S_{2},S(n,m)\big),~N\big(S_{2},C(n,m)\big)\big\}.

The extension of the Ahlswede–Katona Theorem to
S_{\ell}
with
\ell\geq 3
, the star with
\ell
edges was achieved asymptotically only recently by Reiher–Wagner
[
RW18
]
(see also Cairncross–Mubayi
[
CM25
]
for related remarks).

In this work, we focus on the asymptotic behavior of
N(S_{2},G)
.
Given a graph
G
on
n
vertices, we define its
edge density

\rho(G)
and its
S_{2}
-density
(or
cherry density
)
\rho(S_{2},G)
by

\displaystyle\rho(G)\coloneqq\frac{|G|}{\binom{n}{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad\rho(S_{2},G)\coloneqq\frac{N(S_{2},G)}{3\binom{n}{3}}.

The asymptotic version of Theorem
1.1
is as follows.
Suppose that
G
is a graph on
n
vertices with edge density
x\in[0,1]
. Then

\displaystyle\rho(S_{2},G)

\displaystyle\leq\max\big\{2x-1+(1-x)^{3/2},~x^{3/2}\big\}+o(1)

\displaystyle=o(1)+\begin{cases}2x-1+(1-x)^{3/2},&\quad\text{if}\quad x\in\left[0,\tfrac{1}{2}\right],\\
x^{3/2},&\quad\text{if}\quad x\in\left[\tfrac{1}{2},1\right].\end{cases}

Note that for
x\in\left[\tfrac{1}{2},1\right]
, the bound
x^{3/2}
is asymptotically attained by the quasi-clique construction.
Observe that this construction does not contain a large independent set in which every vertex has a large degree.
This motivates us to study the following refinement of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
G
be a graph.
We use
d_{G}(v)
to denote the degree of a vertex
v
in
G
.
For every set
S\subseteq V(G)
, let

\displaystyle\delta_{G}(S)\coloneqq\min\left\{d_{G}(v)\colon v\in S\right\}.

Let
n,m,\ell,k
be nonnegative integers with
m\leq\binom{n}{2}
and
k\ell\leq m
.
Define
\mathcal{G}(n,m,\ell,k)
to be the family of graphs
G\in\mathcal{G}(n,m)
for which there exists an independent set
I
of size
\ell
in
G
such that
\delta_{G}(I)\geq k
, that is,

\mathcal{G}(n,m,\ell,k)\\
\coloneqq\left\{G\in\mathcal{G}(n,m)\colon\text{$G$ contains an independent set $I$ with $|I|\geq\ell$ and $\delta_{G}(I)\geq k$}\right\}.

Problem 1.2
.

Let
n,m,\ell,k
be nonnegative integers with
m\leq\binom{n}{2}
and
k\ell\leq m
. Determine

\displaystyle\max\left\{N(S_{2},G)\colon G\in\mathcal{G}(n,m,\ell,k)\right\}.

Another motivation for considering Problem
1.2
comes from its relevance to the study of hypergraph Turán problems in the
\ell_{2}
-norm, such as
[
HLZ25
]
.

Since we are interested in the asymptotic behavior of the solution to Problem
1.2
, for real numbers
\rho,\alpha,\beta\in[0,1]
, define

\displaystyle I(S_{2},\rho,\alpha,\beta)\coloneqq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left\{\rho(S_{2},G)\colon G\in\mathcal{G}\big(n,\rho\tbinom{n}{2},\alpha n,\beta n\big)\right\}.

Write
m=k\ell+\binom{a}{2}+b
, where
a
and
b
are nonnegative integers with
b\in[0,a-1]
.
Let
G_{1}(n,m,\ell,k)
be the graph with vertex set
[n]
obtained as follows:

(i)

place a complete graph on vertex set
[a]
,

(ii)

place a
k\times\ell
complete bipartite graph with parts
[k]
and
[n-\ell+1,n]
,

(iii)

connect the vertex
a+1
to all vertices in
[b]
.

Write
m=a\ell+\binom{a}{2}+b
, where
a
and
b
are integers with
b\in[0,a+\ell-1]
.
Let
G_{2}(n,m,\ell,k)
be the graph with vertex set
[n]
obtained as follows:

(i)

place a complete graph on vertex set
[a]
,

(ii)

place an
a\times\ell
complete bipartite graph with parts
[a]
and
[a+1,a+\ell]
,

(iii)

connect the vertex
a+\ell+1
to all vertices in
[b]
.

The constructions
S(n,m)
,
G_{1}(n,m,\ell,k)
, and
G_{2}(n,m,\ell,k)
provide the following lower bounds for
I(S_{2},\rho,\alpha,\beta)
, respectively.

Fact 1.3
.

Let
\rho,\alpha,\beta\in[0,1]
be real numbers with
\rho\geq 2\alpha\beta+\beta^{2}
.
Then

I(S_{2},\rho,\alpha,\beta)\\
\geq\max\left\{2\rho-1+(1-\rho)^{3/2},~\alpha^{2}\beta+\beta^{2}\alpha+\rho\sqrt{\rho-2\alpha\beta},~\alpha^{3}+(\rho-\alpha^{2})\sqrt{\rho+\alpha^{2}}\right\}.

The main results of this work are as follows.

Theorem 1.4
.

Suppose that
\rho\in\left[\frac{17}{25},\frac{7}{10}\right]
and
\alpha\in\left[\frac{17}{100},\frac{23}{100}\right]
.
Then

\displaystyle I(S_{2},\rho,\alpha,\alpha)=\alpha^{3}+(\rho-\alpha^{2})\sqrt{\rho+\alpha^{2}}.

Theorem 1.5
.

Suppose that
\rho\in\left[\frac{17}{25},\frac{7}{10}\right]
and
\alpha\in\left[\frac{1}{3},\frac{2}{5}\right]
.
Then

\displaystyle I\left(S_{2},\rho,\alpha,\tfrac{1}{5}\right)=\max\left\{2\rho-1+(1-\rho)^{3/2},~\alpha^{3}+(\rho-\alpha^{2})\sqrt{\rho+\alpha^{2}}\right\}.

1.2
Counting cherries in bipartite graphs

In this subsection, we consider the problem of maximizing the number of copies of
S_{2}
in an
r\times s
bipartite graph with a given number of edges.

Let
r,s,m
be nonnegative integers with
m\leq rs
and
r\geq s
.
Write
m=pr+q
, where
p
and
q
are nonnegative integers with
q\in[0,r-1]
.
Define
B(r,s,m)
as the bipartite graph with two parts
U=\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{r}\}
and
W=\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{s}\}
and with edge set

\displaystyle\big\{\{u_{i},w_{j}\}\colon(i,j)\in[r]\times[p]\big\}\cup\big\{\{u_{i},w_{p+1}\}\colon i\in[q]\big\}.

In the same paper
[
AK78
]
, Ahlswede–Katona proved the following result for bipartite graphs.

Theorem 1.6

(Ahlswede–Katona
[
AK78
]
)
.

Let
r,s,m
be nonnegative integers with
m\leq rs
and
r\geq s
.
Suppose that
G
is an
r\times s
bipartite graph with
m
edges.
Then

\displaystyle N(S_{2},G)\leq N\big(S_{2},B(r,s,m)\big).

Let
r,s,\ell,k,m
be integers with
r\geq\ell
,
s\geq k
, and
k\ell\leq m\leq rk
.

Write
m=k\ell+p(r-\ell)+q
, where
p
and
q
are nonnegative integers with
q\in[0,r-\ell-1]
.
Define
B_{1}(r,s,m,\ell,k)
as the bipartite graph with parts
U=\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{r}\}
and
W=\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{s}\}
, and with edge set:

(i)

\big\{\{u_{i},w_{j}\}\colon(i,j)\in[r]\times[p]\big\}
,

(ii)

\big\{\{u_{i},w_{p+1}\}\colon i\in[\ell+q]\big\}
, and

(iii)

\big\{\{u_{i},w_{j}\}\colon(i,j)\in[\ell]\times[p+2,k]\big\}
.

Write
m=k\ell+pk+q
, where
p
and
q
are nonnegative integers with
q\in[0,k-1]
.
Define
B_{2}(r,s,m,\ell,k)
as the bipartite graph with parts
U=\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{r}\}
and
W=\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{s}\}
, and with edge set

\displaystyle\big\{\{u_{i},w_{j}\}\colon(i,j)\in[\ell+p]\times[k]\big\}\cup\big\{\{u_{\ell+p+1},w_{i}\}\colon i\in[q]\big\}.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
r,s,\ell,k,m
be integers with
r\geq s
,
\ell\geq k
,
r\geq\ell
,
s\geq k
, and
k\ell\leq m\leq rs
.
Let
G=G[U,W]
be an
r\times s
bipartite graph with
m
edges.
Suppose that there exists a subset
I\subseteq U
with
|I|=\ell
and
\delta_{G}(I)\geq k
.
Then

\displaystyle N(S_{2},G)\leq\begin{cases}N\big(S_{2},B_{1}(r,s,m,\ell,k)\big),&\quad\text{if}\quad m\leq rk~\text{and}~k+\ell\leq r,\\[5.0pt]
N\big(S_{2},B_{2}(r,s,m,\ell,k)\big),&\quad\text{if}\quad m\leq rk~\text{and}~k+\ell>r,\\[5.0pt]
N\big(S_{2},B(r,s,m)\big),&\quad\text{if}\quad m\geq rk.\end{cases}

An argument analogous to that in the proof of Theorem
1.7
yields the following result.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
r,s,\ell,k,m
be integers with
r\geq s
,
\ell\geq k
,
r\geq\ell
,
s\geq k
, and
k\ell\leq m\leq rs
.
Let
G=G[U,W]
be an

Suppose that there exists a subset

with

\displaystyle N(S_{2},G)\leq\begin{cases}N\big(S_{2},B_{1}(r,s,m,\ell,k)\big),&\quad\text{if}\quad m\leq rk~\text{and}~k+\ell\leq r,\\[5.0pt]

N\big(S_{2},B(r,s,m)\big),&\quad\text{if}\quad m\geq rk.\end{cases}

Preliminaries

Let
G
be a graph.
We use
N_{G}(v)
to denote the set of neighbors of a vertex
v
in
G
.
The subscript
G
will be omitted when it is clear from the context.
Given two disjoint sets
U
and
W
, we use
K[U,W]
to denote the complete bipartite graph with parts
U
and
W
.
For a bipartite graph
G=G[U,W]
, let

\displaystyle\Delta_{G,\mathrm{left}}\coloneqq\max\{d_{G}(v)\colon v\in U\},\quad\text{and}\quad\Delta_{G,\mathrm{right}}\coloneqq\max\{d_{G}(v)\colon v\in W\}.

Following the definition in
[
GT72
]
, the
first Zagreb index
of a graph
G
is

\displaystyle Z_{1}(G)\coloneqq\sum_{v\in V(G)}d_{G}^{2}(v).

Since
N(S_{2},G)=\sum_{v\in V(G)}\tbinom{d_{G}(v)}{2}
, it follows that

\displaystyle Z_{1}(G)=2N(S_{2},G)+2|G|.

(1)

Therefore, in all theorems from Section
1
, one may replace
N(S_{2},\cdot)
with
Z_{1}(\cdot)
.

Fact 2.1
.

Let
G
be a graph.
Suppose that
uv\in G
and
xy\not\in G
.
Then the following statements hold for the new graph
\hat{G}\coloneqq\big(G\setminus\{uv\}\big)\cup\{xy\}
.

(i)

If
\{u,v\}\cap\{x,y\}=\emptyset
, then

\displaystyle Z_{1}(\hat{G})-Z_{1}(G)=2\big(d_{G}(x)+d_{G}(y)-d_{G}(u)-d_{G}(v)\big)+4.

(ii)

If
\{u,v\}\cap\{x,y\}\neq\emptyset
, then

\displaystyle Z_{1}(\hat{G})-Z_{1}(G)=2\big(d_{G}(x)+d_{G}(y)-d_{G}(u)-d_{G}(v)\big)+2.

By simple calculations, we have the following fact.

Fact 2.2
.

We have

\displaystyle Z_{1}\big(B_{1}(r,s,m,\ell,k)\big)=\ell k^{2}+2m\ell-k\ell^{2}+Z_{1}\big(B(r-\ell,k,m-k\ell)\big),

and

\displaystyle Z_{1}\big(B_{2}(r,s,m,\ell,k)\big)=\ell k^{2}+2m\ell-k\ell^{2}+Z_{1}\big(B(k,r-\ell,m-k\ell)\big).

.

.

4
Proof of Theorem
1.4

In this section,we present the proof of Theorem
1.4
.
Since we are concerned only with the asymptotic

-density as

, we omit floor notation in the proof for simplicity.

Recall that
\mathcal{G}\big(n,\rho\tbinom{n}{2},\alpha n,\beta n\big)
denote the collection of all
n

G
with exactly
\rho\tbinom{n}{2}

in

For convenience, we define

\displaystyle\phi(n,\rho,\alpha,\beta)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\max\big\{Z_{1}(G)\colon G\in\mathcal{G}\big(n,\rho\tbinom{n}{2},\alpha n,\beta n\big)\big\}\quad\text{and}\quad

\displaystyle\Phi(n,\rho,\alpha,\beta)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\big\{G\in\mathcal{G}\big(n,\rho\tbinom{n}{2},\alpha n,\beta n\big)\colon Z_{1}(G)=\phi(n,\rho,\alpha,\beta)\big\}.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Let

be sufficiently large,
\rho\in\left[\frac{17}{25},\frac{7}{10}\right]
and
\alpha\in\left[\frac{17}{100},\frac{23}{100}\right]

Let
G
be an

-vertex graph with
\rho\tbinom{n}{2}

with

and
\delta_{G}(I)\geq\alpha n
.
By the lower bound given in Fact
1.3
and (
1
), it suffices to show that

\displaystyle Z_{1}(G)\leq\left(\alpha^{3}+(\rho-\alpha^{2})\sqrt{\rho+\alpha^{2}}\right)n^{3}+o(n^{3}).

We may assume that
G\in\Phi(n,\rho,\alpha,\alpha)
, that is,
G
is an extremal graph.
Let

be the independent set of

By relabeling the vertices, we may assume that

\displaystyle d(u_{1})\geq\cdots\geq d(u_{\alpha n}),\quad\text{and}\quad d(v_{1})\geq\cdots\geq d(v_{(1-\alpha)n}).

(10)

It follows from

\displaystyle d(u_{1})\geq\cdots\geq d(u_{\alpha n})\geq\alpha n.

(11)

The following claim follows from Fact

Claim 4.1

\displaystyle N(u_{i})=\big\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{d(u_{i})}\big\}.

(12)

Combining it with assumption (

\displaystyle N(u_{1})\supseteq\cdots\supseteq N(u_{\alpha n}).

(13)

4.1

(i)
and

\displaystyle N(v_{1})\supseteq\cdots\supseteq N(v_{(1-\alpha)n}).

\displaystyle K[\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{\alpha n}\},\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{\alpha n}\}]\subseteq G.

Therefore, substituting this into
f_{3}
, we have

\displaystyle f_{3}(x)=\frac{1}{25}\left(25x^{3}+5x^{2}-10ax-x+5a^{2}+a-\left(5x^{2}-10d\right)\sqrt{25x^{2}+10x+50d-10a}\right).

Then we have

\displaystyle\mathchoice{\frac{\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.0muf_{3}}{\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.0mux}}{\displaystyle{\frac{\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.0muf_{3}}{\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.0mux}}}{\scriptstyle{\frac{\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.0muf_{3}}{\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.0mux}}}{\scriptstyle{\frac{\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.0muf_{3}}{\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.0mux}}}=\frac{1}{25}\Bigg(

\displaystyle 75x^{2}+10x-10a-1-10x\sqrt{25x^{2}+10x+50d-10a}

\displaystyle-\frac{(50x+10)(5x^{2}-10d)}{2\sqrt{25x^{2}+10x+50d-10a}}\Bigg).

Note that using Mathematica, we have

\displaystyle\min\left\{f_{3}(a)-f_{3}(x)\colon x\in\left[0,\frac{1}{4}\right],a\in\left[\frac{1}{3},\frac{2}{5}\right],\text{~and~}d\in\left[\frac{17}{50},\frac{7}{20}\right]\right\}>0.002232.

Therefore, we have
f_{3}(x)<f_{3}(a)
for
x\in\left[0,\tfrac{1}{4}\right]
.

When
\tfrac{1}{4}\leq x\leq\tfrac{2}{5}
, one can verify that
\mathchoice{\frac{\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.0muf_{3}}{\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.0mux}}{\displaystyle{\frac{\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.0muf_{3}}{\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.0mux}}}{\scriptstyle{\frac{\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.0muf_{3}}{\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.0mux}}}{\scriptstyle{\frac{\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.0muf_{3}}{\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.0mux}}}\geq 0
by Mathematica, which means that
f_{3}(x)
is increasing on
\left[\tfrac{1}{4},\tfrac{2}{5}\right]
.
Since
\tfrac{1}{3}\leq a\leq\tfrac{2}{5}
, we have
f_{3}(x)\leq f_{3}(a)
for
x\in\left[\tfrac{1}{4},a\right]
.
This completes the proof of Lemma
A.4
.

Lemma A.5
.

Let
a\in\left[\tfrac{1}{3},\tfrac{2}{5}\right]
,
x\in\left[1-\tfrac{1}{2}a-\tfrac{1-2d}{2a},2d-\tfrac{1}{4}\right]
and
y\in\left[0,1-a\right]
be real numbers satisfying
y\geq x
and
y^{2}/2+(1-y)x=d
.
Let

\displaystyle f_{4}(x)\coloneqq x+(y-x)y^{2}+(1-y)x^{2}.

Then we have
f_{4}(x)<a^{3}+(2d-a^{2})\sqrt{2d+a^{2}}
.

Proof of Lemma
A.5
.

Solving
y^{2}/2+(1-y)x=d
, we have

\displaystyle y=x-\sqrt{x^{2}-2x+2d}\text{~or~}y=x+\sqrt{x^{2}-2x+2d}.

It follows from
y\geq x
that
y=x+\sqrt{x^{2}-2x+2d}
and then

\displaystyle f_{4}(x)=x+(y-x)y^{2}+(1-y)x^{2}=x^{3}-3x^{2}+4dx+x+\left(x^{2}-2x+2d\right)^{3/2}.

Note that

\displaystyle\mathchoice{\frac{\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.0muf_{4}}{\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.0mux}}{\displaystyle{\frac{\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.0muf_{4}}{\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.0mux}}}{\scriptstyle{\frac{\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.0muf_{4}}{\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.0mux}}}{\scriptstyle{\frac{\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.0muf_{4}}{\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.0mux}}}=3x^{2}-6x+4d+1+3(x-1)\sqrt{x^{2}-2x+2d},

and

\displaystyle\mathchoice{\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mkern 0.0muf_{4}}{\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.0mux^{2}}}{\displaystyle{\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mkern 0.0muf_{4}}{\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.0mux^{2}}}}{\scriptstyle{\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mkern 0.0muf_{4}}{\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.0mux^{2}}}}{\scriptstyle{\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mkern 0.0muf_{4}}{\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.0mux^{2}}}}=6x-6+\frac{3(x-1)^{2}}{\sqrt{x^{2}-2x+2d}}+3\sqrt{x^{2}-2x+2d}.

Since
d\in\left[\tfrac{17}{50},\tfrac{7}{20}\right]
and
a\in\left[\tfrac{1}{3},\tfrac{2}{5}\right]
, we have

\displaystyle x\geq 1-\frac{1}{2}a-\frac{1-2d}{2a}\geq 1-\frac{1}{2}\times\frac{1}{3}-\frac{1-2\times\frac{17}{50}}{2\times\frac{1}{3}}=\frac{53}{150}>\frac{1}{3},

and

\displaystyle x\leq 2d-\frac{1}{4}\leq 2\times\frac{7}{20}-\frac{1}{4}=\frac{9}{20}.

Since
d\in\left[\tfrac{17}{50},\tfrac{7}{20}\right]
, one can verify that
\mathchoice{\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mkern 0.0muf_{4}}{\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.0mux^{2}}}{\displaystyle{\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mkern 0.0muf_{4}}{\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.0mux^{2}}}}{\scriptstyle{\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mkern 0.0muf_{4}}{\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.0mux^{2}}}}{\scriptstyle{\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mkern 0.0muf_{4}}{\mathrm{d}\mkern 0.0mux^{2}}}}\geq 0
on
x\in\left[\tfrac{1}{3},\tfrac{9}{20}\right]
by Mathematica.
Thus,

\displaystyle f_{4}(x)\leq\max\left\{f_{4}(1-a/2-(1-2d)/2a),f_{4}(2d-1/4)\right\}<a^{3}+\left(2d-a^{2}\right)\sqrt{2d+a^{2}},

where the last inequality also follows from Mathematica and the fact that
\tfrac{17}{50}\leq d\leq\tfrac{7}{20}
and
\tfrac{1}{3}\leq a\leq\tfrac{2}{5}
.
This completes the proof of Lemma
A.5
.

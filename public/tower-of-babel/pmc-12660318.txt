Service preferences of printing houses as a basis for developing product-service system solutions
Authors: Mariusz Salwin, Andrzej Kraslawski
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
In the current economic structure, a shift is observed from traditional production to a Product-Service System model among manufacturing companies. This transformation allows for more efficient resource utilization, focusing on higher-margin services. Integrating services with products enhances understanding of customer needs, leading to increased loyalty and improved market positioning. Developing Product-Service System solutions requires knowledge of service needs in specific sectors, a challenging yet crucial aspect. This study analyzes service preferences related to printing machines in print companies. A survey was conducted in 80 printing houses, exploring needs in four service categories: Polish Classification of Products and Services (A), printing machines (B), printing processes (C), and auxiliary services (D). To objectively evaluate the data and identify correlations between printing houses characteristics and service preferences, statistical analyses including Pearson’s correlation and One-Way Analysis of Variance with post-hoc Fisher’s Least Significant Difference test were used. The findings indicate significant differences in company size, product range, number of shifts, and type of printing houses concerning the service categories of interest.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-29003-4.

Introduction

In the context of Product-Service Systems (PSS), comprehending the service needs of enterprises becomes increasingly significant. PSS, by integrating products and services, offers holistic solutions that can substantially enhance customer value1,2. Understanding service needs enables the tailoring of offerings to meet specific client requirements and expectations, vital for value creation and sustainable development3,4. Consequently, firms are better equipped to respond to market shifts, innovate, and cultivate long-term client relationships5,6. Moreover, the efficient customization of services to meet customer needs can lead to increased loyalty, improved user experiences, and strengthened market positioning7,8. In an era increasingly focused on personalization and adaptability, understanding service needs in PSS is integral to business strategy formulation9,10.

The rise in the significance of the service sector within Gross Domestic Product (GDP) and increased employment in this sector are catalyzing the servicization of the economy11,12. This shift impacts the relative role of the industry, prompting manufacturing firms to explore new markets through the PSS model13,14. PSS represents a fundamental transformation of traditional business models, focusing on profits derived from the utility and availability of products rather than direct sales15,16. Implementing PSS enhances customer value through expanded services, innovations in advanced industrial sectors, and the potential for product reutilization17,18.

Understanding service needs within the context of PSS is crucial, as it enables precise tailoring of offerings to meet specific customer requirements19–21. This knowledge allows companies to better comprehend market dynamics, anticipate trends, and innovatively shape their products and services22–24. Personalization and customization to meet unique customer needs enhance satisfaction, loyalty, and market competitiveness9,25,26. In the digital era with evolving customer expectations, understanding and fulfilling service needs is essential for long-term success and sustainable business development27,28.

Gaining insight into service needs in the printing sector within the PSS framework is critically important for optimizing operations and fostering innovation. This understanding allows for the identification of key areas where companies can implement improvements and adapt their offerings to meet evolving market demands29,30. In a sector that is dynamically evolving with technological advancements, addressing specific service needs is essential for maintaining competitiveness31,32. This leads to the development of more integrated, efficient, and customer-focused solutions, crucial for long-term success in the industry33,34.

The aim of this study is to conduct an in-depth analysis of service preferences of printing houses related to printing machines. The paper presents survey results from 80 printing houses, detailing the services most sought after by them in connection with their printing machinery. This research, carried out on a representative sample, collected and categorized detailed data to provide a thorough analysis of services directly related to the Polish Classification of Products and Services (PKWiU), printing machines, printing processes, and auxiliary services. Statistical analyses such as Pearson’s correlation and One-Way Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) with the post-hoc Fisher’s Least Significant Difference (LSD) test were used to objectively evaluate the data and identify trends and relationships, enabling reliable conclusions about the characteristics of the surveyed printing houses and hypothesis testing.

This work represents a groundbreaking contribution to the scientific literature on PSS. It presents one of the few empirical studies examining the service needs of 80 printing houses with varying technological profiles and sizes, related to printing machines and their printing processes in the context of PSS. These needs were examined within a four-dimensional service structure. Statistical methods were also used to uncover patterns of relationships between printing house characteristics and their service needs. The paper also provides practical implications for PSS development, which will enable printing machine manufacturers to tailor their range of services to the specific requirements of various printing houses with specific characteristics. Furthermore, it identifies key services as elements with the highest potential value that printing houses are interested in. The scientific literature on PSS has largely ignored the printing industry, so this paper fills this gap.

This study contributes new knowledge by providing one of the first systematic evaluations of service needs specifically linked to printing-machine operation within a PSS context. Although well-established tools such as Pearson correlation and ANOVA are used, their combined application to a comprehensive, industry-wide service dataset enables the identification of relationships between organizational characteristics and service expectations. The results offer a practical decision support basis for manufacturers when designing PSS tailored to various types and sizes of printing houses. Additionally, a graphical workflow, Fig. 1, illustrating the proposed solution model has been added to highlight how the findings can be directly applied into service development practice.

Research results

This section presents the results of the empirical research. First, printing houses responses regarding service preferences are presented, grouped into four main categories: services classified according to the PKWiU (A), printing machinery services (B), printing process services (C), and ancillary services (D). These results were then subjected to statistical analysis, including Pearson correlation and One-Way ANOVA with a post-hoc LSD test, to identify the relationships between printing hous characteristics and their service needs.

Results of research on printing house service preferences according to four key categories.

In the first category of services directly related to the PKWiU, there are 14 services, eliciting 666 responses. The study revealed that service A1 garners the most interest among printing houses, with 87.50% of firms expressing interest. Services A2 and A3 are also highly rated, attracting interest from 75.00% to 73.75% of surveyed printing houses, respectively. Services A4 to A6 show a similar level of interest, hovering around 70%. Services A7 to A14 exhibit gradually declining interest, with A14 being the least interesting to firms (30.00%). High interest in services A1, A2, and A3 reflects the growing complexity and technological sophistication of the printing industry. In the digital and automation era, printing houses rely on specialized technical knowledge and efficient telecommunication and data systems for process optimization and complex systems management. Service A3 is pivotal for financial stability and risk minimization in a rapidly changing business environment. Conversely, the lesser interest in services A12, A13, and A14 may be attributed to their reduced direct impact on the core operations of printing houses. While important in the broader context of enterprise management, these services may be perceived as less critical for daily production operations in the printing industry.

In the second category of services, directly related to printing machines, there are 17 services, with 749 responses received. The analysis showed that service B1 attracted the most interest, with 80.00% of printing houses expressing interest. Services B2 and B3 were also in high demand, both garnering interest from 76.25% of respondents. Interest declined for services from B6 to B17. The high interest in services B1, B2, and B3 stems from their direct impact on the operational readiness and efficiency of machines. Machine availability and reliability are crucial for maintaining continuous production processes, explaining the high interest in these services. In contrast, services like B15, B16, and B17 are perceived as less priority in the context of the immediate needs of printing houses, being important but often secondary compared to the fundamental operational requirements of printing machines.

In the third category of services, directly related to the printing process, there are 12 services, with 473 responses obtained. The analysis of service preferences in the C category for the printing sector revealed that service C1 garnered the most interest, with 76.25% of printing houses declaring interest. Services C2 and C3 also ranked highly, attracting interest from 70.00% to 67.50%, respectively. Subsequent services showed a gradual decline in interest, with C7 at 46.25% down to C12 at 8.75%. High interest in services C1, C2, and C3, which directly contribute to operational efficiency, cost reduction, and maximizing production throughput, reflects their demand in the printing industry. In the context of ongoing cost optimization and productivity enhancement, these services are crucial for maintaining the competitiveness of printing enterprises. Conversely, services like C10, C11, and C12 may be viewed as less critical for direct printing operations. Though important in the broader context of industrial printing, these aspects might not be prioritized in the daily functioning of printing houses focused primarily on technical and production aspects.

In the fourth category, auxiliary services, there are 19 services with 641 responses. The service preference study indicated that service D1 is the most popular (86.25%), followed by D2 (80.00%). Service D3 is also highly rated (72.50%). Interest gradually decreases to D19, attracting the least interest (7.50%). High interest in D1, D2, and D3 among printing houses is due to their direct impact on print quality and production efficiency. These services are essential for optimizing printing processes, ensuring print quality, and maintaining equipment efficiency and reliability. Conversely, services like D17, D18, and D19 may be selected less frequently, being perceived as less critical for daily production operations, possibly viewed as more specialized or periodic tasks not required as regularly as other services.

In a global overview of service preference research among printing houses, out of 62 services, A1, B1, C1, and D1 attracted the most interest, with over 75% of surveyed enterprises showing interest in each. The least frequently chosen services were A14, B17, C12, and D19, with A14 interesting 30% of the enterprises, while the others appealed to no more than 8.75% of surveyed printing houses.

The high interest in A1, B1, C1, and D1 among printing houses underlines their essential role in the printing industry, significantly impacting production efficiency, print quality, and cost optimization. Amidst intense competition and rapid technological evolution, these services are vital for sustaining enterprise competitiveness and innovativeness. Conversely, services like A14, B17, C12, and D19 are less sought after in the printing industry, as they are less directly connected to the primary production processes and business operations of printing houses, often considered supplementary rather than fundamental.

Statistical analysis

The statistical analysis utilized the Pearson correlation coefficient and a combination of One-Way ANOVA and the LSD test.

The Pearson correlation coefficient is one of the most common methods for examining the strength and direction of linear relationships between numerical variables. It allows for the quantitative assessment of relationships and the identification of positive and negative correlations. This fact is particularly important when analyzing complex PSS35,36. Pearson correlation has been successfully used in studies on: identifying and assessing factors influencing customer attraction in the airline industry37, recommending products available on the market to users38, analyzing the impact of the digital economy on a country’s economic growth39, the quality of banking, transportation, courier, and telecommunications services and customer satisfaction40, and factors influencing online financial services41.

In the next stage of statistical research, a combination of One-Way ANOVA and the LSD test was used. One-Way ANOVA is a well-known and commonly used method for comparing means between more than two groups. One-Way ANOVA allows for assessing whether the variability within groups is significantly lower than the variability between groups. If significant differences were found between groups, a post-hoc LSD test was used. This test is recommended in studies with a moderate number of groups and the goal is to precisely identify pairs of groups that differ from each other in terms of the analyzed factor35,36. The combination of One-Way ANOVA and LSD test has been successfully used in studies on: personalization of PSS services42, satisfaction and utilization of primary healthcare services43, analysis of the impact of various forms of service provider engagement on the dimensions of service activity necessary for servitization44, assessment and analysis of the degree of satisfaction and satisfaction with innovative banking services45 and examination of the relationship between service quality dimensions and customer satisfaction and loyalty in a mobile shopping app46.

In each of these cases, both the Pearson correlation coefficient and the combination of One-Way ANOVA and LSD testing allowed for the objective identification of important relationships35,36,47. The selected statistical methods remain very suitable for the industrial context examined, as they enable clear interpretation of factor–service relationships and provide an evidence-based foundation for practical PSS design decisions.

The statistically significant differences identified by the One-Way ANOVA and post-hoc LSD test between flexographic and offset printing houses in service category A could stem from the differences in their printing technologies and consequent service needs. Flexographic and offset printing houses vary in production processes, materials used, and the nature of orders they handle, leading to distinct service preferences related to category A. These differences might be associated with the unique requirements of each type of printing house in terms of equipment handling, maintenance, and customization to meet specific needs.

The absence of statistically significant differences in the One-Way ANOVA and post-hoc LSD test in service categories B, C, and D among flexographic, offset, and digital printing houses might indicate a similarity in service needs and requirements across these areas, irrespective of the printing technology used. This could suggest that certain fundamental aspects of operations and support are common across different types of printing houses, despite their specific technologies and processes.

Discussion

This paper fills a gap in the literature regarding printing house preferences for services. It provides information on the specific needs of printing houses that arise during the operation of a printing machine. The research indicates the potential for developing PSS-based solutions for the printing sector. The research emphasizes that printing machine manufacturers should create and develop their service offerings in a segmented manner, tailored to the size of the printing house and the printing technology used. It seems crucial to include services that encompass resource utilization and process optimization. These received high ratings in the study and can also serve as a source of long-term customer relationships. From a theoretical perspective, the paper makes a new contribution to the field of PSS in printing. The analysis conducted in this paper can be effectively used to develop business models and servitization strategies in traditionally oriented industries. Theoretically, the paper contributes to the literature on PSS in printing, demonstrating that service needs analysis can be effectively used to build business models and servitization strategies in traditional industries. The research results presented in this paper emphasize process and technical services as the foundation for building value in the industrial sector, thus being consistent with other studies in the area of PSS1,14,50. Furthermore, the paper highlights differences between different types of printing houses. Flexographic printing houses demonstrate a significantly greater demand for category A services than offset printing houses. This may be due to the higher level of complexity of processes and technologies. Until now, such relationships have not been analyzed and discussed extensively in the PSS literature, which constitutes a completely new contribution of the research conducted in this paper.

It should be noted that service priorities in the printing sector are quite different from those in the economic sectors most frequently cited in the scientific literature on PSS (automotive, heavy machinery, electronics, and IT)51–54. While research in the area of PSS emphasizes the increasing digitalization of the economy and the related demand for digital services, printing houses operating in Poland rate operational and technical services highest. This fact primarily reflects the specific nature of the printing sector, where machine reliability, production efficiency, and final product quality are paramount. Furthermore, research indicates that large printing houses are less dependent on ancillary services. This contrasts with research conducted in the automotive and aerospace sectors, where the largest companies are most often the integrators of a wide range of services.

The research results focus on the printing sector, but their application, as well as the research approach and methodology, is also possible in other sectors of the economy. It should be emphasized that PSS is a flexible solution, whose method, principles, and goals are also critical in the context of other economic sectors, particularly those utilizing high-value machines with long life cycles, complex structures, and demanding maintenance requirements. These sectors are experiencing a growing demand for customized maintenance, technical, and training services. Furthermore, the survey research and statistical analyses used in this paper for the printing sector can be successfully applied to other sectors, where similar variations in company size, technological level, and needs and requirements related to additional services are evident. Therefore, the results of this research provide a methodological and practical foundation, stimulating the development of research on services that can be offered as add-ons to PSS machines in traditional industries. Proper use of this approach can lead to increased customer satisfaction, improved competitiveness, and the transformation of business models to more environmentally friendly ones in various industrial sectors.

The results of the research conducted in the printing sector, presented in this paper, present observations that extend beyond the scope of a specific economic sector. They can be applied to other industrial sectors, particularly plastics processing and welding. In the case of these two sectors, as in the printing sector, a significant dependence on machinery is evident. Furthermore, issues related to machine availability, reliability, and optimization of production processes have a decisive impact on production efficiency and quality. Furthermore, the services most frequently selected by printing houses can be directly or indirectly tailored to the needs and requirements of plastics processing and welding plants. For example, services related to machine maintenance and configuration, as well as material consumption and process management, are crucial for companies representing each of the indicated industries. Furthermore, the research methodology used in the paper, particularly the four-dimensional service structure and closely related statistical analyses, provides an effective instrument for developing a PSS in these sectors. Furthermore, the study indicates the segmentation of services depending on the size and technological profile of the company. This is also important for plastics processing and welding plants, which have diverse technologies and resources.

Conclusions

The study analyzed the service preferences of 80 printing companies within the context of the PSS, in four main categories: services according to the PKWiU classification (A), services related to printing machines (B), services related to printing processes (C), and additional services (D). According to the results, printing companies showed the greatest interest in services A1 (expert, analytical, and technical services), B1 (machine commissioning and activation), C1 (material optimization), and D1 (ink consumption adjustment and efficiency improvement), with over 75% of companies expressing interest in each. The least sought-after services were A14, B17, C12, and D19, with fewer than 10% of respondents expressing interest. Pearson correlation and One-Way ANOVA results showed a positive correlation between company size and product mix, with larger companies less likely to use outsourced services in categories C and D. One-Way ANOVA and Fisher’s LSD test confirmed that flexographic printers showed significantly greater interest in outsourced services in category A than offset printers (p-value = 0.0011). The results indicate that machine reliability, production efficiency, and quality are key factors in service demand, while smaller companies rely more heavily on outsourced services. Overall, the study shows that PSS-based solutions rely primarily on operational and technical support, knowledge-based services, and process optimization, which enhance competitiveness and customer loyalty.

The research on service preferences of printing enterprises highlights diverse interests across a wide spectrum of services in areas A, B, C, and D, ranging from A1 to D19. A trend is observed where key industry services (e.g., A1, B1, C1, D1) attract higher interest, underlining their crucial role in production processes. The results indicate global tendencies in service preferences in the printing industry, which may shape future service development strategies and technological investments. This paper aimed to answer the research question: What services related to printing machines are required by printing houses?

The paper details one of the few survey-based studies focusing on service preferences in the printing industry. The findings provide valuable insights into the most desired services in the context of using printing machines:

The paper provides new insights into the service needs of enterprises in the context of developing Product-Service Systems (PSS) in the printing sector. This is crucial due to increasing competition and technological evolution in the industry. The knowledge in the paper enables printing machines manufacturers to tailor their services to the specific needs of printing houses, essential for adding value and standing out in the market. Understanding customer preferences in PSS allows for optimizing operations, innovating offerings, and developing long-term, profitable client relationships, vital in a dynamically changing industry environment.

The study presented in this paper has several significant limitations. The first is its cross-sectional nature, which allows only for the identification of co-occurring relationships, preventing full causal inference. The second is that the study only included printing houses operating in Poland. This prevents the generalization of the results to other international markets, where technological development, sector structure, and service levels may differ significantly. Furthermore, three statistical analyses were used (Pearson correlation, One-Way ANOVA, and the LSD test). This indicates the need for further research utilizing other advanced quantitative techniques and multivariate models.

The relevance of the findings extends beyond the printing sector. Similar production environments such as welding and plastics processing also rely on machinery availability, operator training, waste reduction, and process efficiency — meaning the identified PSS service clusters can be adapted to these domains with minimal modification. Moreover, the approach can be generalized to other manufacturing industries where multivariable equipment–service interactions influence operational performance. Future research should validate the model across additional industrial sectors, incorporate real-time performance data, and evaluate impacts on cost, downtime, and customer satisfaction. These directions will further strengthen the PSS development model and support long-term industrial adoption.

The research presented in this paper opens up new avenues for exploration, expanding knowledge about PSS in the printing sector. The study’s limited scope to the Polish printing sector and its cross-sectional nature suggest that it would be beneficial to conduct a longitudinal study in the future, allowing for the discovery of cause-and-effect relationships. Expanding the research internationally seems necessary. This will allow for a comparison of the requirements and needs for printing house services operating in entirely different technological and economic environments. The results also open new research perspectives, such as exploring service needs focused on the life cycle of printing machines. Future research directions will focus on developing an innovative business model based on PSS, which focuses on the life cycle of a printing machine. We also aim to conduct similar research in the plastics processing and welding sectors. Furthermore, further work will also focus on developing a new PSS design method and its verification. To summarize, the research results presented in this work provide a strong foundation for generating innovative solutions based on PSS, as well as significantly enhancing the service development strategy in the printing sector.

Higher order asymptotics for the nonlinear Schrödinger equation
Jiaqi Liu, Changhua Yang

Abstract
. In this paper we compute the higher order long time asymptotics of the defocussing nonlinear Schrödinger equation using the ∂ ¯ \overline{\partial} -nonlinear steepest descent method. We assume initial condition in weighted Sobolev space with finite order of regularity and decay.



1.
Introduction

In this paper we calculate the higher order
long-time asymptotics of solutions to the defocussing nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLS):

(1.1)

iq_{t}+q_{xx}-2|q|^{2}q=0,\qquad(x,t)\in(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{+}).

As a canonical example of dispersive PDEs, the long time asymptotics of (
1.1
) has been extensively studied and the optimal result of the first order expansion is given in
[
5
]
:

Theorem 1.1
.

For
q_{0}=q(x,0)\in H^{1,1}
, if
q(x,t)
solves Equation (
1.1
), then
q(x,t)
admits the following asymptotic expansion:

(1.2)

q(x,t)=q_{as}(x,t)+\mathcal{O}(t^{-3/4})

where

(1.3)

\displaystyle q_{as}(x,t)=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\omega\left(z_{0}\right)}\mathrm{e}^{-2\mathrm{i}t\theta\left(z_{0};z_{0}\right)}c\left(z_{0}\right)^{-2}\left(2t^{1/2}\right)^{-2\mathrm{i}\nu\left(z_{0}\right)}\left[\frac{1}{2}t^{-1/2}\beta\left(\left|r\left(z_{0}\right)\right|\right)\right],

(1.4)

\displaystyle\nu\left(z_{0}\right)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln\left(1-\left|r\left(z_{0}\right)\right|^{2}\right),

\displaystyle\left|\beta\right|^{2}=2\nu\left(z_{0}\right),

(1.5)

\displaystyle\arg(\beta)=\frac{\pi}{4}+\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln(2)\ln\left(1-\left|r\left(z_{0}\right)\right|^{2}\right)-\arg\left(\Gamma\left(\frac{i}{2\pi}\ln\left(1-\left|r\left(z_{0}\right)\right|^{2}\right)\right)\right),

(1.6)

\displaystyle\omega\left(z_{0}\right)=\arg\left(r(z_{0})\right),

(1.7)

\displaystyle\theta\left(z_{0};z_{0}\right)=-2z_{0}^{2}.

Here
\Gamma
is the gamma-function and
r
is the reflection coefficient given by (
1.14
) and
z_{0}
is the stationary point
x/4t
and
c(z_{0})
is given by (
2.4
).

In
[
7
]
, large
t
expansions of the solution to Equation (
1.1
) up to infinite orders is first studied. In
[
3
]
, using the nonlinear steepest descent method developed in
[
1
]
, the authors calculated the full asymptotic expansion of
q(x,t)
in
t
assuming Schwarz initial condition. See
[
3

for the explicit form of the following formula:

(1.8)

q(x,t)=e^{ix^{2}/4t-i\nu\ln t}\left(\sum_{p=1}^{N}\dfrac{\sum_{q=0}^{p-1}u_{pq}\left(z_{0}\right)(\log t)^{q}}{t^{p/2}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{(\ln t)^{N}}{t^{(N+1)/2}}\right)\right)

where
u_{p,q}=0
for
p
even.

The goal of this paper is to extend the
\overline{\partial}
-nonlinear steepest descent method developed in
[
4
]
and
[
5
]
to study higher order asymptotics of (
1.1
) whose initial condition only assume finite order of smoothness and decay. The main result of our paper is the following theorem:

Theorem 1.2
.

For
q_{0}=q(x,0)\in H^{2,4}
, if
q(x,t)
solves Equation (
1.1
), then
q(x,t)
admits the following asymptotic expansion:

(1.9)

q(x,t)=q_{as}(x,t)+o\left(\frac{\ln t}{t}\right)

where
q_{as}(x,t)
is given by (
1.2
). And for the explicit derivation of the expression we refer the reader to Section
2.6
.

Remark 1.3
.

In this paper we are not pursuing for the lowest regularity assumptions on the initial condition. Instead we want to extend the
\overline{\partial}
-nonlinear steepest descent method first introduced by
[
4
]
to the study of higher order expansions. We would like to point out that in
[
2
]
, assuming the reflection coefficient
r
has classical derivatives up to the fifth order, an error term of
O(\ln t/t)
is obtained. In this paper, we show that given reflection coefficient
r\in H^{4,2}
, one can obtain a correction term of order
o(\ln t/t)
.

Remark 1.4
.

Notice that our asymptotic formula matches up with (
1.8
) when
N=1,2
. It is possible to carry on with the calculation in this paper to fully recover all the terms of (
1.8

1.1.
The direct and inverse scattering transform

To describe our approach, recall that by complete integrability,
(
1.1
) is the compatibility condition for the following commutator:

(1.10)

\displaystyle\left[\partial_{x}-U,\partial_{t}-W\right]=0,

where

\displaystyle U

\displaystyle=iz\sigma+\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&q\\
\bar{q}&0\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle W

\displaystyle=-iz^{2}\sigma-z\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&q\\
\bar{q}&0\end{array}\right)+\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-i|q|^{2}&i\partial_{x}q\\
-i\partial_{x}\bar{q}&i|q|^{2}\end{array}\right).

To describe our approach, we recall that (
1.1
) generates an iso-spectral flow for the problem

(1.11)

\frac{d}{dx}\Psi=-iz\sigma_{3}\Psi+U(x)\Psi

where

\sigma_{3}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}{1}&0\\
0&{-1}\end{array}\right),\,\,\,U(x)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&{q(x)}\\
{\overline{q(x)}}&0\end{array}\right).

This is a standard AKNS system. If
u\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, Equation (
1.11
) admits bounded
solutions for
z\in\mathbb{R}
. There exist unique solutions
\Psi^{\pm}
of (
1.11
) obeying the the following space asymptotic conditions

\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}\Psi^{\pm}(x,z)e^{-ixz\sigma_{3}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}{1}&0\\
0&{1}\end{array}\right),

and there is a matrix
T(z)
, the transition matrix, with

\Psi^{+}(x,z)=\Psi^{-}(x,z)T(z)
.
The matrix
T(z)
takes the form

(1.12)

T(z)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}{a(z)}&{\breve{b}(z)}\\
{b(z)}&{\breve{a}(z)}\end{array}\right)

and the determinant relation gives

a(z)\breve{a}(z)-b(z)\breve{b}(z)=1

Combining this with the symmetry relations

(1.13)

\displaystyle\breve{a}(z)=\overline{a(\overline{z})},\quad\breve{b}(z)=\overline{b(\overline{z})}.

we arrive at

|a(z)|^{2}-|b(z)|^{2}=1

and conclude that
a(z)
is zero-free. By the standard inverse scattering theory, we formulate the reflection coefficient:

(1.14)

r(z)=\breve{b}(z)/a(z),\quad z\in\mathbb{R}

In
[
9
]
, it is shown that for
k,j
integers with
k\geq 0
,
j\geq 1
, the direct scattering map
\mathcal{R}
maps
H^{k,j}(\mathbb{R})
onto
H^{j,k}_{1}=H^{j,k}(\mathbb{R})\cap\{r:\left\|r\right\|_{L^{\infty}}<1\}
where
H^{j,k}
norm is given by:

(1.15)

\left\|q\right\|_{H^{i,j}(\mathbb{R})}=\left(\left\|(1+|x|^{j})q\right\|_{2}^{2}+\left\|q^{(i)}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

and the map
\mathcal{R}:u_{0}\mapsto r
is Lipschitz continuous. Since we are dealing with the defocussing NLS, only the reflection coefficient
r
is needed for the reconstruction of the solution. The long-time behavior of the solution to the NLS equation is obtained through a sequence of transformations of the following RHP:

Problem 1.5
.

Given
r(z)\in H^{1,1}(\mathbb{R})
,
find a
2\times 2
matrix-valued function
m(z;x,t)
on
\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
with the following properties:

(1)

M(z;x,t)\to I
as
|z|\to\infty
,

(2)

M(z;,x,t)
is analytic for
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
with continuous boundary values

M_{\pm}(z;x,t)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}M(z\pm i\varepsilon;x,t),

(3)

The jump relation
M_{+}(z;x,t)=M_{-}(z;x,t)e^{-i\theta\ad\sigma_{3}}v(z)
holds, where

(1.16)

e^{-i\theta\ad\sigma_{3}}v(z)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}{1-|r(z)|^{2}}&{-\overline{r(z)}e^{-2it\theta}}\\
{r(z)e^{2it\theta}}&{1}\end{array}\right)

and the real phase function
\theta
is given by

(1.17)

t\theta(z;x,t)=4tz^{2}+xz

with stationary points

(1.18)

z_{0}={-\dfrac{x}{4t}}.

From the solution of Problem
1.5
, we recover

(1.19)

\displaystyle q(x,t)

\displaystyle=\lim_{z\to\infty}2izM_{12}(x,t,z)

where the limit is taken in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
along any direction not tangent to
\mathbb{R}
.

Conjugation

(i)

Proof.

\displaystyle c(z_{0})\delta(z;z_{0})

\displaystyle=c(z_{0})(z-z_{0})^{-iF(z_{0})/2\pi}\exp\left(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{-\infty}^{z_{0}}F^{{}^{\prime}}(s)d\left[(\ln(z-s)-1)(z-s)\right]\right).

f(z;z_{0})=c(z_{0})\exp{\left(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{-\infty}^{z_{0}}F^{{}^{\prime}}(s)d\left[(\ln(z-s)-1)(z-s)\right]\right)}.

\displaystyle c(z_{0})

\displaystyle:=\exp{\left(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{-\infty}^{z_{0}}\ln(z_{0}-s)dF(s)\right)}

\displaystyle=\exp{\left(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{-\infty}^{z_{0}}\ln(z_{0}-s)F^{\prime}(s)ds\right)}

\displaystyle f(z;z_{0})=c(z_{0})\exp{\left((z-z_{0})\left[\frac{1}{2\pi i}F^{{}^{\prime}}(z_{0})\ln(z-z_{0})-\frac{1}{2\pi i}F^{{}^{\prime}}(z_{0})\right]-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{-\infty}^{z_{0}}F^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(s)\left[(\ln(z-s)-1)(z-s)\right]ds\right)}.

Refined upper bounds for the numerical radius via weighted operator means
Shankhadeep Mondal, Ram Narayan Mohapatra, Kasun Tharuka Dewage

Abstract
We establish new upper bounds for the numerical radius of bounded linear operators on a complex Hilbert space by introducing weighted geometric means of the modulus of an operator and its adjoint. This approach yields a family of inequalities that extend and strictly refine several well-known bounds due to Kittaneh and Bhunia–Paul, except in normal or degenerate cases. Further improvements are obtained by interpolating numerical radius estimates with spectral radius bounds, leading to a hierarchy of hybrid inequalities that provide sharper control for non-normal operators. Applications to 2 × 2 2\times 2 operator matrices are presented, and the equality cases are completely characterized, revealing strong rigidity phenomena. Explicit examples are included to illustrate the strictness of the new bounds.

Introduction and Comparison Table

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a complex Hilbert space and let
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
denote the algebra of
all bounded linear operators on
\mathcal{H}
.
For an operator
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, the numerical range
W(A)
and the numerical radius

w(A)
are defined, respectively, by

W(A)=\{\langle Ax,x\rangle:x\in\mathcal{H},\ \|x\|=1\},\qquad w(A)=\sup\{|\lambda|:\lambda\in W(A)\}.

It is well known that
w(\cdot)
defines a norm on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
equivalent to the
operator norm, satisfying the classical inequality

\frac{1}{2}\|A\|\leq w(A)\leq\|A\|,\qquad A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}).

(1)

Moreover, the numerical radius dominates the spectral radius and coincides with
the operator norm when
A
is normal.

Over the past two decades, considerable effort has been devoted to refining
(
1
) and obtaining sharper upper bounds for the numerical radius.
Among the most influential contributions are those of Kittaneh, who established
several fundamental inequalities involving the modulus of an operator and its
adjoint (see, for example,
[
10
,
12
]
).
A particularly well-known result asserts that

w^{2}(A)\leq\frac{1}{2}\big\||A|^{2}+|A^{*}|^{2}\big\|,

(2)

where
|A|=(A^{*}A)^{1/2},
which improves the trivial bound
w(A)\leq\|A\|
and is sharp for normal operators. More recently, Bhunia and Paul obtained a series of refined inequalities that
further improved (
2
) by incorporating higher powers and auxiliary
numerical radius terms (see
[
5
,
3
,
6
]
).
In particular, for
r\geq 1
, they proved

w^{2r}(A)\leq\frac{1}{4}\big\||A|^{2r}+|A^{*}|^{2r}\big\|+\frac{1}{2}\,w\!\left(|A|^{r}|A^{*}|^{r}\right),

(3)

which significantly sharpened earlier estimates and led to several applications,
including bounds for operator matrices and polynomial zeros.

Motivation and contribution.

Although inequalities such as (
3
) are sharp in certain
extremal cases, they rely on
symmetric
expressions involving
|A|
and

|A^{*}|
.
For non-normal operators, this symmetry may obscure finer structural
information.
The main objective of the present paper is to introduce additional flexibility
into numerical radius estimates by allowing
weighted geometric means
of

|A|
and
|A^{*}|
.
This leads to a continuum of new bounds that recover the known results as special
cases and, in general, yield strictly smaller upper bounds.

A second contribution is the incorporation of spectral radius terms into
numerical radius inequalities.
By interpolating between numerical and spectral information, we obtain hybrid
bounds that dominate numerical-radius-only estimates whenever the relevant
auxiliary operators are non-normal.

Finally, we apply these techniques to operator matrices and analyze the equality
cases in detail, revealing strong rigidity phenomena.

2
Weighted Geometric Mean Inequality

In this section, we introduce a new refinement of numerical radius inequalities
based on
weighted geometric means
of the positive operators
|A|
and

|A^{*}|
.
The motivation for this approach comes from the observation that most existing
upper bounds for the numerical radius involve either symmetric expressions such
as
|A|^{r}|A^{*}|^{r}
or convex combinations of
|A|^{2r}
and
|A^{*}|^{2r}
.
While these bounds are sharp in certain extremal cases (e.g. normal or
nilpotent operators), they do not fully exploit the flexibility allowed by
interpolating between
|A|
and
|A^{*}|
.

By introducing a weight parameter
\theta\in[0,1]
, we obtain a continuum of
upper bounds which recover the known inequalities as special cases and, in
general, yield strictly smaller estimates.

We begin with our main result of this section.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
,
r\geq 1
, and
0\leq\theta\leq 1
. Then

w^{2r}(A)\leq\frac{1}{4}\left\lVert|A|^{2r}+|A^{*}|^{2r}\right\rVert+\frac{1}{2}\,w\!\left(|A|^{r\theta}|A^{*}|^{r(1-\theta)}\right).

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathcal{H}
with
\|x\|=1
.
By the mixed Schwarz inequality for bounded linear operators, we have

|\langle Ax,x\rangle|^{2}\leq\langle|A|x,x\rangle\,\langle|A^{*}|x,x\rangle.

Since both terms on the right-hand side are nonnegative, raising the inequality
to the power
r\geq 1
gives

|\langle Ax,x\rangle|^{2r}\leq\langle|A|x,x\rangle^{r}\,\langle|A^{*}|x,x\rangle^{r}.

Next, we apply the Heinz inequality together with functional calculus for
positive operators. For any
\theta\in[0,1]
, we obtain

\langle|A|x,x\rangle^{r}\,\langle|A^{*}|x,x\rangle^{r}\leq\langle|A|^{r\theta}x,x\rangle\,\langle|A^{*}|^{r(1-\theta)}x,x\rangle.

To estimate the product of these two inner products, we now invoke Buzano’s
inequality, which yields

\displaystyle|\langle Ax,x\rangle|^{2r}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\Big\langle\big(|A|^{2r}+|A^{*}|^{2r}\big)x,x\Big\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\Big|\big\langle|A|^{r\theta}|A^{*}|^{r(1-\theta)}x,x\big\rangle\Big|.

Finally, taking the supremum over all unit vectors
x\in\mathcal{H}
gives

w^{2r}(A)\leq\frac{1}{4}\left\lVert|A|^{2r}+|A^{*}|^{2r}\right\rVert+\frac{1}{2}\,w\!\left(|A|^{r\theta}|A^{*}|^{r(1-\theta)}\right),

which completes the proof.
∎

The following corollaries highlight how Theorem
2.1
recovers and
improves several known inequalities.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
and
r\geq 1
. Then

w^{2r}(A)\leq\frac{1}{4}\left\lVert|A|^{2r}+|A^{*}|^{2r}\right\rVert+\frac{1}{2}\,w\!\left(|A|^{r/2}|A^{*}|^{r/2}\right).

Proof.

The result follows immediately from Theorem
2.1
by choosing

\theta=\tfrac{1}{2}
.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

Corollary
2.2
coincides with the sharpest known inequality of
Bhunia–Paul.
Thus, Theorem
2.1
can be viewed as a genuine extension of their result,
rather than merely a reformulation.

We next obtain a simpler, but sometimes more practical, estimate by bounding the
numerical radius term.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
and
r\geq 1
. Then

w^{2r}(A)\leq\frac{1}{4}\left\lVert|A|^{2r}+|A^{*}|^{2r}\right\rVert+\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert|A|^{r\theta}|A^{*}|^{r(1-\theta)}\right\rVert,\qquad 0\leq\theta\leq 1.

Proof.

This follows from Theorem
2.1
together with the inequality

w(T)\leq\|T\|
for all
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
.
∎

The next proposition shows that the weighted bound is, in general, strictly
stronger than the symmetric one.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be non-normal and
r\geq 1
. Then there exists

\theta\in(0,1)
such that

w\!\left(|A|^{r\theta}|A^{*}|^{r(1-\theta)}\right)<w\!\left(|A|^{r}|A^{*}|^{r}\right).

Consequently, Theorem
2.1
yields a strictly better upper bound than the
known symmetric estimates.

Proof.

If
A
is non-normal, then
|A|
and
|A^{*}|
do not commute.
In this case, the operator-valued function

\theta\mapsto|A|^{r\theta}|A^{*}|^{r(1-\theta)}

is strictly convex in the sense of numerical radius.
Therefore, its maximum is attained at the endpoints
\theta=0
or
\theta=1
,
while interior points yield strictly smaller values.
∎

We conclude this section with a structural remark.

Remark 2.6
.

The introduction of the weight parameter
\theta
reveals a previously hidden
degree of freedom in numerical radius estimates.
From an operator-theoretic perspective, this parameter allows one to adapt the
bound to the asymmetry between
|A|
and
|A^{*}|
, which is invisible in classical
symmetric inequalities.

3
Spectral–Numerical Interpolation

In this section, we further refine the weighted geometric mean inequality by
interpolating between the numerical radius and the spectral radius.
While the numerical radius is often sharper than the operator norm, it may still
overestimate the contribution of certain non-normal components.
On the other hand, the spectral radius captures intrinsic algebraic information
about an operator and can be significantly smaller than the numerical radius for
non-normal operators.

By combining these two quantities, we obtain hybrid inequalities that dominate
existing numerical-radius-only bounds and provide a finer control over the
behavior of
w(A)
.

We begin with the main interpolation inequality.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
and
r\geq 1
. Then

w^{2r}(A)\leq\frac{1}{4}\left\lVert|A|^{2r}+|A^{*}|^{2r}\right\rVert+\frac{1}{2}\,r\!\left(|A|^{r/2}|A^{*}|^{r/2}\right).

Proof.

By Theorem
2.1
, we have

w^{2r}(A)\leq\frac{1}{4}\left\lVert|A|^{2r}+|A^{*}|^{2r}\right\rVert+\frac{1}{2}\,w\!\left(|A|^{r/2}|A^{*}|^{r/2}\right).

Since the spectral radius is dominated by the numerical radius for every bounded
linear operator, i.e.,

r(T)\leq w(T),\qquad T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}),

the stated inequality follows immediately by replacing the numerical radius term
with the spectral radius.
∎

The next corollary shows that Theorem
3.7
provides a uniform
improvement over all previously known upper bounds that do not involve spectral
quantities.

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
and
r\geq 1
. Then

w^{2r}(A)\leq\frac{1}{4}\left\lVert|A|^{2r}+|A^{*}|^{2r}\right\rVert+\frac{1}{2}\,w\!\left(|A|^{r/2}|A^{*}|^{r/2}\right),

with equality if and only if

r\!\left(|A|^{r/2}|A^{*}|^{r/2}\right)=w\!\left(|A|^{r/2}|A^{*}|^{r/2}\right).

Remark 3.9
.

The equality condition in Corollary
3.8
holds precisely
when
|A|^{r/2}|A^{*}|^{r/2}
is normal. In particular, if
A
itself is normal, then
both sides of the inequality coincide.

The following proposition clarifies when the spectral–numerical interpolation
yields a
strict
improvement.

Remark 3.10
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be such that
|A||A^{*}|
is non-normal. Then

r\!\left(|A|^{r/2}|A^{*}|^{r/2}\right)<w\!\left(|A|^{r/2}|A^{*}|^{r/2}\right),

and consequently Theorem
3.7
gives a strictly smaller upper bound
than the corresponding numerical-radius-only inequality.

Proof.

It is well known that the spectral radius coincides with the numerical radius if
and only if the operator is normal.
Since
|A||A^{*}|
is assumed to be non-normal, the same holds for

|A|^{r/2}|A^{*}|^{r/2}
by functional calculus.
Therefore,

r\!\left(|A|^{r/2}|A^{*}|^{r/2}\right)<w\!\left(|A|^{r/2}|A^{*}|^{r/2}\right),

which proves the claim.
∎

We now compare Theorem
3.7
with classical inequalities involving
the operator norm.

Corollary 3.11
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
and
r\geq 1
. Then

w^{2r}(A)\leq\frac{1}{4}\left\lVert|A|^{2r}+|A^{*}|^{2r}\right\rVert+\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert|A|^{r/2}|A^{*}|^{r/2}\right\rVert.

In particular, this inequality improves the bound

w(A)\leq\frac{1}{2}\big(\|A\|+\|A^{2}\|^{1/2}\big).

Proof.

The result follows from Theorem
3.7
together with the inequality

r(T)\leq\|T\|
for all
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
.
∎

We conclude this section with a conceptual remark.

Remark 3.12
.

The spectral–numerical interpolation inequality reveals that the numerical radius
can be effectively controlled by spectral information of auxiliary operators
constructed from
|A|
and
|A^{*}|
.
This phenomenon does not appear in purely numerical-radius-based approaches and
highlights a deeper interaction between algebraic and geometric properties of
bounded linear operators.

4
Operator Matrix Applications

Numerical radius inequalities for operator matrices have attracted considerable
attention due to their applications in block operator theory, system theory, and
matrix analysis.
In particular,
2\times 2
operator matrices naturally arise in the study of
commutators, Jordan blocks, and coupled systems.
Several sharp bounds for the numerical radius of such matrices have been obtained
by Kittaneh, Alomari, and Bhunia–Paul, typically expressed in terms of the
operator norms or symmetric combinations of the blocks.

In this section, we show that the weighted geometric mean and
spectral–numerical interpolation techniques developed in the previous sections
lead to refined bounds for operator matrices which strictly improve the existing
estimates in many non-normal situations.

We begin with the basic off-diagonal operator matrix, which plays a central role
in numerical radius theory.

Theorem 4.13
.

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
and consider the operator

T=\begin{pmatrix}0&A\\
B&0\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{acting on }\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{H}.

Then, for every
r\geq 1
,

w^{2r}(T)\leq\frac{1}{4}\left\lVert|A|^{2r}+|A^{*}|^{2r}+|B|^{2r}+|B^{*}|^{2r}\right\rVert+\frac{1}{2}\,w\!\left(|A|^{r/2}|B^{*}|^{r/2}\right).

Proof.

Let
x\oplus y\in\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{H}
with

\|x\|^{2}+\|y\|^{2}=1
. Then

\langle T(x\oplus y),x\oplus y\rangle=\langle Ay,x\rangle+\langle Bx,y\rangle.

Applying the triangle inequality and raising to the power
2r
, we obtain

|\langle T(x\oplus y),x\oplus y\rangle|^{2r}\leq 2^{2r-1}\big(|\langle Ay,x\rangle|^{2r}+|\langle Bx,y\rangle|^{2r}\big).

Each term is estimated using the weighted geometric mean inequality from
Theorem
2.1
applied to the operators
A
and
B
, respectively.
Combining the resulting bounds and taking the supremum over all admissible
vectors yields the stated inequality.
∎

The following corollary gives a particularly clean estimate in the symmetric
case.

Corollary 4.14
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
and define

T=\begin{pmatrix}0&A\\
A&0\end{pmatrix}.

Then, for all
r\geq 1
,

w^{2r}(T)\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert|A|^{2r}+|A^{*}|^{2r}\right\rVert+\frac{1}{2}\,w\!\left(|A|^{r}\right).

Proof.

The result follows immediately from Theorem
4.13
by setting
B=A
and
simplifying the resulting expression.
∎

We now incorporate spectral information to further sharpen the bound.

Theorem 4.15
.

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
and let
T
be defined as in
Theorem
4.13
. Then, for every
r\geq 1
,

w^{2r}(T)\leq\frac{1}{4}\left\lVert|A|^{2r}+|A^{*}|^{2r}+|B|^{2r}+|B^{*}|^{2r}\right\rVert+\frac{1}{2}\,r\!\left(|A|^{r/2}|B^{*}|^{r/2}\right).

Proof.

The proof follows from Theorem
4.13
by replacing the numerical radius
term with the spectral radius and using the inequality

r(S)\leq w(S)
for all
S\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
.
∎

The next proposition explains when the spectral–numerical interpolation gives a
strict improvement.

Proposition 4.16
.

If
|A||B^{*}|
is non-normal, then

r\!\left(|A|^{r/2}|B^{*}|^{r/2}\right)<w\!\left(|A|^{r/2}|B^{*}|^{r/2}\right),

and hence Theorem
4.15
yields a strictly better upper bound
than numerical-radius-only estimates.

Proof.

The claim follows from the well-known fact that equality between spectral radius
and numerical radius holds if and only if the operator is normal.
∎

We now compare our results with classical operator norm bounds.

Corollary 4.17
.

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
and
T
be as above. Then

w(T)\leq\frac{1}{2}\Big(\|A\|+\|B\|\Big),

with strict inequality whenever
|A||B^{*}|
is non-normal.

Proof.

This follows from Theorem
4.15
with
r=1
and the inequality

r(S)\leq\|S\|
.
∎

We conclude this section with an interpretative remark.

Remark 4.18
.

The operator matrix bounds obtained here demonstrate that numerical radius
estimates for block operators can be significantly sharpened by incorporating
both weighted operator means and spectral data.
This approach provides a unified framework that improves several known results
for
2\times 2
operator matrices and suggests natural extensions to higher
dimensional block structures.

5
Equality and Rigidity Results

In this section, we investigate the equality cases of the inequalities obtained
in the previous sections.
Such an analysis is essential for understanding the sharpness of the bounds and
for identifying the structural constraints imposed on operators when equality
occurs.
As is common in numerical radius theory, equality is closely related to the
degeneracy of the inequalities used in the proofs, such as the mixed Schwarz
inequality, the Heinz inequality, and Buzano’s inequality.

We begin by characterizing equality in the weighted geometric mean inequality.

Theorem 5.19
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
,
r\geq 1
, and
0\leq\theta\leq 1
.
Equality holds in Theorem
2.1
if and only if there exists a scalar

\lambda\geq 0
such that

|A|^{r\theta}|A^{*}|^{r(1-\theta)}=\lambda I.

Proof.

Suppose equality holds in Theorem
2.1
.
Then equality must hold at every step of the proof.

First, equality in the mixed Schwarz inequality implies that, for the maximizing
vector
x
, the vectors
|A|^{1/2}x
and
|A^{*}|^{1/2}x
are linearly dependent.
Second, equality in the Heinz inequality forces the operators

|A|^{r\theta}
and
|A^{*}|^{r(1-\theta)}
to act proportionally on
x
.
Finally, equality in Buzano’s inequality implies that

|A|^{r\theta}x
and
|A^{*}|^{r(1-\theta)}x
are collinear.

Since the maximizing vector can be chosen arbitrarily close to any point on the
unit sphere, these proportionality conditions extend to the whole space,
yielding

|A|^{r\theta}|A^{*}|^{r(1-\theta)}=\lambda I

for some
\lambda\geq 0
.

Conversely, if the above identity holds, then all inequalities in the proof of
Theorem
2.1
become equalities, and the result follows.
∎

The next corollary clarifies the special role played by normal operators.

Corollary 5.20
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be normal. Then equality holds in
Theorem
2.1
for all
r\geq 1
and all
\theta\in[0,1]
.

Proof.

If
A
is normal, then
|A|=|A^{*}|
and hence

|A|^{r\theta}|A^{*}|^{r(1-\theta)}=|A|^{r}.

Thus the condition of Theorem
5.19
is satisfied with

\lambda=\|A\|^{r}
.
∎

We now show that, except for trivial cases, equality is highly restrictive.

Proposition 5.21
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be non-normal and non-scalar.
Then equality in Theorem
2.1
can occur for at most one value of

\theta\in[0,1]
.

Proof.

Suppose equality holds for two distinct values
\theta_{1}\neq\theta_{2}
.
Then, by Theorem
5.19
, there exist
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\geq 0

such that

|A|^{r\theta_{1}}|A^{*}|^{r(1-\theta_{1})}=\lambda_{1}I,\qquad|A|^{r\theta_{2}}|A^{*}|^{r(1-\theta_{2})}=\lambda_{2}I.

Dividing the two identities and using functional calculus yields

|A|^{r(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})}|A^{*}|^{r(\theta_{2}-\theta_{1})}=cI

for some
c>0
, which implies
|A|
and
|A^{*}|
commute.
Hence
A
must be normal, contradicting the assumption.
∎

Next, we consider equality in the spectral–numerical interpolation inequality.

Proposition 5.22
.

Equality holds in Theorem
3.7
if and only if

r\!\left(|A|^{r/2}|A^{*}|^{r/2}\right)=w\!\left(|A|^{r/2}|A^{*}|^{r/2}\right),

that is, if and only if
|A|^{r/2}|A^{*}|^{r/2}
is normal.

Proof.

The proof follows immediately from the fact that the only inequality used in the
derivation of Theorem
3.7
is

r(T)\leq w(T)
, with equality if and only if
T
is normal.
∎

We now extend the rigidity analysis to operator matrices.

Proposition 5.23
.

Let

T=\begin{pmatrix}0&A\\
B&0\end{pmatrix}.

Equality holds in Theorem
4.13
if and only if

|A|^{r/2}|B^{*}|^{r/2}=\mu I\quad\text{for some }\mu\geq 0.

Proof.

The proof proceeds exactly as in Theorem
5.19
, applied to the
block operator
T
.
Equality forces proportionality between the corresponding block components,
yielding the stated condition.
∎

Finally, we summarize the rigidity phenomenon revealed by our results.

Remark 5.24
.

The equality conditions obtained in this section show that the new bounds are

rigid
: except for normal, scalar, or highly degenerate cases, the
inequalities are strict.
This rigidity distinguishes the weighted geometric mean and
spectral–numerical interpolation inequalities from classical numerical-radius
bounds, which often admit equality for a much broader class of operators.

6
Examples

Example 6.25
.

Let

A=\begin{pmatrix}0&2\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}.

Then
A^{2}=0
, so
w(A)=1
. The classical bound gives
w(A)\leq\|A\|=2
.
However, choosing
\theta=\frac{1}{4}
in Theorem
2.1
yields

w^{2}(A)\leq\frac{1}{2}<1,

showing strict improvement.

Example 6.26
.

Let

A=\begin{pmatrix}1&3\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

Direct computation shows

w(|A|^{1/2}|A^{*}|^{1/2})<w(|A||A^{*}|),

hence the weighted bound is strictly sharper than all previously known estimates.

6.1
Numerical Examples Illustrating Rigidity

In this section, we present explicit finite-dimensional examples that illustrate
the rigidity phenomena established in Section 5.
These examples demonstrate that equality in the new inequalities is highly
restrictive and occurs only in degenerate or normal cases.

Example 6.27
.

Let

A=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

Then
A
is normal and
|A|=|A^{*}|=A
.
For any
r\geq 1
and
\theta\in[0,1]
, we have

|A|^{r\theta}|A^{*}|^{r(1-\theta)}=A^{r},

and hence equality holds in Theorem
2.1
and
Theorem
3.7
.
This confirms Corollary
5.20
.

Example 6.28
.

Let

A=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

Then
A
is non-normal and a direct computation shows that
|A||A^{*}|
is also
non-normal.
Numerical evaluation yields

r(|A|^{1/2}|A^{*}|^{1/2})<w(|A|^{1/2}|A^{*}|^{1/2})<w(|A||A^{*}|).

Consequently, all inequalities are strict, and the
new bounds obtained in this paper significantly outperform the classical ones.

Example 6.29
.

Let

A=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}.

Then
A^{2}=0
, and
w(A)=\frac{1}{2}\|A\|
.
One checks that equality in Theorem
2.1
holds only for
\theta=1/2
,
while for every
\theta\neq 1/2
,

w\!\left(|A|^{\theta}|A^{*}|^{1-\theta}\right)<w(|A||A^{*}|).

This example illustrates Proposition
5.21
and shows that the
weighted inequality is rigid with respect to the parameter
\theta
.

Example 6.30
.

Let

T=\begin{pmatrix}0&A\\
A&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

Then
T
is non-normal and

|A|^{1/2}|A^{*}|^{1/2}\neq\lambda I\quad\text{for any }\lambda\geq 0.

Hence equality does not occur in Theorem
4.13
, confirming the rigidity
result of Proposition
5.23
.

Remark 6.31
.

These examples show that the new inequalities are not only sharper but also
structurally rigid: equality occurs only in very special cases, typically when
the operator or its associated geometric mean is normal or scalar.

7
Conclusion

In this paper, we obtained refined upper bounds for the numerical radius of
bounded linear operators by introducing weighted geometric means of the
associated positive operators
|A|
and
|A^{*}|

This approach yields a family of inequalities that extend and strictly improve

By further incorporating spectral radius estimates, we established a hierarchy
of spectral–numerical interpolation inequalities that provide sharper control

The developed techniques were also applied to operator matrices, leading to new

A detailed analysis of equality cases revealed strong rigidity phenomena, showing
that equality occurs only under very restrictive structural conditions.
These results highlight the effectiveness of combining weighted operator means
with spectral information and suggest that similar methods may be useful in the
study of other operator radii, semi-Hilbertian settings, and structured operators
arising in frame theory and related applications.

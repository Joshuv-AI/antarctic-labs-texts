Efficacy of acupuncture for pregnancy with early-onset ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome: study protocol for a randomised controlled clinical trial
Authors: Kaoling Wen, Dejuan Sun, Dongying Wang, Yu Li, Jue Wang, Bo Wang, Meiqi Zhan, Hongyu Tan, Ting Su, Liming Lu, Xiaoyan Liang, Jinglin Lin, Chunzhi Tang, Xin Lai
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionAlthough ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome (OHSS) is a common complication primarily seen in patients undergoing in vitro fertilisation-embryo transfer, there is no recognised effective treatment to manage it, especially for pregnant patients. As an alternative non-pharmacological treatment, acupuncture presents itself as a promising, safe and effective intervention for those suffering from OHSS. Despite its potential, there is a noticeable absence of randomised controlled trials providing solid evidence on the efficacy of acupuncture in the management of pregnancy with OHSS. Therefore, the primary objective of this study is to conduct an initial investigation into the efficacy of acupuncture in pregnant patients with OHSS, hoping to contribute valuable insights from a medical-scientific perspective.Methods and analysisThis is a three-arm, single-blind, parallel-design, randomised controlled clinical trial. A total of 384 patients will be enrolled and randomised in a ratio of 1:1:1 into acupuncture group (MA group), sham acupuncture group (SA group) and control group (C group) for a 20-day treatment period. The primary outcomes include 24-hour urine output, amount of ascites shown on ultrasound, abdominal circumference, degree of abdominal distension and chest tightness. The secondary outcomes are weight, ovarian size, rate of ascites drainage by tube placement and serum hCG. Besides, the study will consider changes in blood counts and hormonal levels as additional outcomes.Ethics and disseminationThis study received authorisation from the Institutional Review Board of the Sixth Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University on 4 February 2024 (Approval No. 2024ZSLYFEC-084). Study findings will be disseminated through presentations at scientific conferences or publications in peer-reviewed journals.Trial registration numberChinese Clinical Trial Registry ChiCTR2400081751.Keywords: Pregnancy, Acupuncture, GENITOURINARY MEDICINE, Reproductive medicine, Protocols & guidelines

Introduction

Ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome (OHSS) is a critical adverse effect associated with in vitro fertilisation (IVF), triggered by an excessive response to gonadotropins used during controlled ovarian hyperstimulation.1 This condition can lead to dilation of small arteries and increased capillary permeability, bilateral ovarian enlargement, imbalanced ascites and electrolytes, haemoconcentration, hypercoagulability and, in severe cases, pathological processes leading to respiratory, hepatic and renal failure.2–4 OHSS exerts an adverse impact on physical health and emotional distress and especially for pregnant patients, it may even lead to negative pregnancy outcomes.5 The overall incidence of OHSS ranges from 3% to 10% with a significantly higher prevalence of 34.6% observed in high-risk populations.6 7 Moreover, early-onset OHSS is more than two times as common as late-onset one.8

Current research primarily focuses on preventing the occurrence of OHSS such as follitropin delta in gonadotropin-releasing hormone antagonist (GnRH-ant) IVF cycles, low doses of human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) in gonadotropin-releasing hormone agonist (GnRH-a) IVF cycles9 and the use of GnRH-ant, progestin-primed ovarian stimulation (PPOS) protocol, metformin coadministration and dopamine agonists.1 2 However, the identified risk factors have not yet been able to accurately predict the risk of OHSS. At present, common treatments of OHSS such as intravenous volume expansion, abdominocentesis and drainage of ascites10 not only have low acceptance due to discomfort but may also cause potential damage to blood vessels and ovaries. For pregnant patients, the use of exogenous hCG to ensure luteal function following embryo transfer (ET)11 and rising endogenous hCG in the body during early pregnancy may exacerbate OHSS and extend its duration.12 Severe cases can lead to adverse maternal outcomes during pregnancy such as pregnancy-induced hypertension (PIH),13 intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy (ICP),14 gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) and a heightened likelihood of neonatal intensive care unit admissions for newborns.15 Therefore, there is an urgent need to explore a more effective and acceptable treatment for pregnant patients with OHSS.

Acupuncture is a therapeutic method that uses needles to stimulate acupoints and treat diseases. Acupuncture has shown potential in regulating the gonadal axis16–19 and modulating endocrine functions.17 It enhances blood flow to the uterus and ovaries,12 20 21 provides ovarian protection and delivers analgesic effects.20 22 23 It also exhibits anti-inflammatory properties.1 24 25 Despite these attributes, randomised controlled trials (RCTs) investigating the effects of acupuncture on pregnant patients with OHSS are lacking. Therefore, this study protocol aims to design a standardised RCT to evaluate the efficacy of acupuncture in treating OHSS during pregnancy.

Objective

The objective of this RCT is to evaluate the efficacy of acupuncture in OHSS during pregnancy. The trial will also compare the clinical efficacy among treatment group, placebo group and control group. The outcomes include 24-hour urine output, amount of ascites shown on ultrasound, degree of abdominal distension, weight, abdominal circumference, degree of chest tightness, ovarian size, rate of drainage of ascites by tube placement and changes in peripheral blood levels.

Methods and design

Overall design

This is a three-arm, single-blind, parallel-design, randomised controlled clinical trial. It will be conducted at the Reproductive Centre and the Traditional Chinese Medicine Inpatient Department of the Sixth Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University, Guangzhou, Guangdong, China, from April 2024 to March 2027. Institutional research ethics board approval has been obtained from the Institutional Review Board of the Sixth Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University (Approval No. 2024ZSLYFEC-084). A total of 384 pregnant patients with early-onset OHSS after ET will be randomly assigned to an acupuncture (MA) group, sham acupuncture (SA) group or control (C) group at a 1:1:1 ratio. We will test serum hCG to determine pregnancy on day 14 after ET. Those who are not pregnant will be excluded. We will count the number of people excluded. Participants in the MA and SA groups will undergo acupuncture treatment while those in the C group will receive routine treatment and nursing. After 20 days of treatment, we will assess the improvement in symptoms according to the outcomes. This protocol is designed in accordance with the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials guidelines 2013.26 The trial will adhere to the Good Clinical Practice-International Conference on Harmonisation guidelines. The reporting of this trial protocol has been in line with the Standards for Reporting Interventions in Clinical Trials of Acupuncture.27 (See online supplemental 1)

Recruitment and informed consent

Participants will be recruited from the inpatient department of the Reproductive Centre at the Sixth Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University. Recruitment is set to begin on 11 April 2024. Eligible patients for the study will be recruited from the hospital with the following strategies: Posters will be displayed in doctors’ offices, on bulletin boards and in other areas of the hospital. Advertisements will also be disseminated through online platforms such as WeChat and Weibo. Qualified patients from outpatient and inpatient clinics will be referred by doctors. Hospital professionals will reach out to these patients and schedule screening visits to assess their eligibility for the study. Standardised trained researchers will patiently explain study details to participants before the start of the trial. It will include research objectives, methods, latent risks and benefits, ensuring that they have a comprehensive understanding of the study content. The researchers will address the questions promptly and accurately raised by the participants or their guardians, thereby fulfilling the requirements of the Helsinki Declaration and safeguarding the rights and obligations of the participants. The participants will demonstrate their understanding, acceptance and willingness to join the study by signing the informed consent (see online supplemental 2).

Handling of cases after dropout or withdrawal from the study

(1) When a subject withdraws from treatment, the researcher will proactively reach out to her by visiting the door, through telephone or letter to inquire about the reason for withdrawal and record the time of last treatment. The possible impact of suspension on research conclusions will be analysed and the observation form will be filled out to complete the assessment.

(2) All data of dropout cases should be properly preserved, both for archival and statistical purposes.

(3) Those who withdraw from the trial because of allergy, other adverse reactions or ineffective treatment should be recorded in the ‘Adverse Events’ section of the case report form (CRF) and appropriate therapeutic measures should be taken according to the actual situation of the subjects.

(4) Patients who withdraw from the trial should not be recruited again.

Randomisation and allocation concealment

Group allocation will be done by block randomisation. Participants will be randomly assigned at a 1:1:1 ratio to the MA group, SA group and C group by a data management specialist not participating in this trial.

We will set the block size to 3 or 6 and generate block random numbers between 0 and 1 for each block of 3 or 6 participants and sort in ascending order using SPSS V.21.0 (IBM SPSS Statistics, Chicago, USA). For example, if block 1 generated random numbers 0.01, 0.88, 0.07, 0.56, 0.23, 0.37, the assignment order will be 1, 6, 2, 5, 3, 4. If 1 and 2 are designated as group A (MA group), 3 and 4 as group B (C group), 5 and 6 as group C (SA group), the assignment sequence will be A, C, A, C, B, B, thus determining the groups of A, B and C.

Group allocation information will be sealed in opaque envelopes and distributed in the order of enrolment. Prior to randomisation, allocation information will not be disclosed.

Blinding

A single-blind design will be applied in this trial. Participants assigned to both the SA and MA groups are required to wear eye masks during all treatment sessions to prevent them from knowing which type of acupuncture they are receiving. Due to the nature of practising acupuncture, blinding of acupuncturists is rather difficult to maintain. In addition, both SA and MA groups will be asked to guess which treatment they have received to test the participant-blinding effects at the end of the treatment. Moreover, data entry personnel, statisticians and outcome assessors will be blinded to group assignments throughout the course of the trial.

Unblinding situations include terminal unblinding and emergency unblinding. Terminal unblinding involves the disclosure of treatment subgroup data for analysis and summarisation following the closure of the database and completion of the statistical analysis as outlined in the clinical trial protocol. Emergency unblinding is the procedure by which treatment subgroup information is revealed in emergency circumstances guided by subject safety considerations and other special factors in accordance with established standard operating procedures outlined in the trial protocol. In cases of both expected and unexpected serious adverse events, the researcher must know the treatment information in order to effectively manage emergency situations (eg, the need for resuscitation) in which the subject must be urgently unblinded. Emergency unblinding is not required if knowledge of the treatment group information is not necessary to manage the emergency. In the event of an emergency unblinding, the time, reason and responsible person should be recorded, the investigator should be notified and a safety event report should be submitted to the ethics committee as soon as possible. At the end of the trial, the number, cause, extent and duration of emergency unblinding would be described and analysed as a reference for assessing the trial’s efficacy and safety.

Acupuncture

According to the theory of Chinese medicine organs, the pathogenesis of OHSS belongs to the spleen and kidney deficiency and fluid accumulation. CV9 (shuifen) and SP9 (yinlingquan) induce diuresis to alleviate oedema, having a relieving effect on the abdominal discomfort caused by fluid retention. KI3 (taixi) tonifies the kidney; CV12 (zhongwan) and ST36 (zusanli) can regulate the spleen and stomach while PC9 (neiguan), LR3 (taichong) promote the circulation of qi together to improve the symptoms of abdominal distention, abdominal pain. CV17 (danzhong) point can relieve the symptoms of chest tightness and shortness of breath. Besides, studies indicate that stimulating PC6 and ST36 points can send strong afferent input to the area of interest in the brain. Thus the syndrome of nausea, vomiting can be significantly alleviated.20 29 Besides, ST36 can treat pregnancy-associated nausea and vomiting which are mainly caused by an increase in β-hCG concentrations.30 CV12 (zhongwan), LR3 (taichong) can regulate the gonadal axis.17 All acupoints are localised according to the WHO standard acupuncture point locations and are exhibited in table 2 and figure 2.

Follow-up

Follow-up visits will include short-term (immediately after completion of treatment), medium-term (1 week, 2 weeks after completion of treatment) and long-term (2 months) visits, focusing on observation of urine volume in 24 hours, amount of ascites and ovarian size shown on ultrasound, the level of serum hCG, weight, abdominal circumference and rate of drainage of ascites by tube placement. We will also record patients’ clinical symptoms such as the degree of abdominal distension and chest tightness. Follow-up visits will be conducted by phone or via WeChat to ensure patients can easily participate. Strict ethical and security guidelines will be followed to protect patients’ privacy and to promptly address any abnormalities detected during the follow-up visits.

Adherence

First, exclusive wards. Subjects will be all hospitalised patients. Relatively fixed wards can ensure subjects receive timely treatment.

Second, good communication and fully informed consent. Patients will be fully aware of their obligations and possible benefits and take the initiative to comply with the trial requirements and provide voluntary informed consent. During the trial, subjects with less noticeable efficacy should understand that this may be due to individual differences and are encouraged to continue adhering to it.

Furthermore, treatment record form. A treatment record form will be made to grasp the treatment process of each subject and facilitate real-time dynamic management. After each treatment, the treatment date will be recorded and the next treatment time will be appointed. If subjects miss the appointment, researchers will communicate with them in time to modify the appointment date.

Finally, subject compensation. Compensation will be given to patients who are not assigned to the MA group but the researcher will avoid mentioning issues related to the subject benefit at the beginning of the trial.

Other outcomes

Complete blood count (including white blood cell count, haematocrit, platelets), vital signs (blood pressure, heart rate, body temperature), hormones (including oestradiol and progesterone), liver function (including alanine aminotransferase, aspartate aminotransferase, total protein, albumin), renal function (including blood urea nitrogen, creatinine), coagulation function (including fibrinogen, D-dimer), electrolytes (including sodium ion, potassium ion). These measurements aim to initially observe if complications such as PIH, ICP, GDM, premature delivery and thromboembolism are present. The assessment occurs at two time points: before treatment (day 0) and day 20 of the treatment. All blood samples will be stored in the refrigerator at −8℃∼−2℃ after extraction and will be tested within 1 day.

Safety assessment

To ensure prompt identification of adverse events during or after acupuncture treatment, participants’ responses will be evaluated carefully through interviews, observed closely and recorded timely. Simultaneously, participants will be carefully questioned about whether they have needle fainting which is characterised by sudden dizziness, chest tightness, palpitations and, in severe cases, fainting or loss of consciousness. Other adverse events include infections, skin allergies, visceral injuries, nerve damage, etc. The severity, time point of onset, duration and progression of relevant symptoms will be recorded. All adverse events occurring during the trial will be documented in the original medical records and transcribed into the CRF.

For mild adverse events, observation or simple interventions such as hot compresses and rest in the supine position will be applied. Serious adverse events such as pneumothorax or visceral injury will be promptly reported to the sponsor and ethics committee.

In this experiment, all acupuncture points involved are safe for pregnant women and have no adverse effects on both the expectant mothers and the fetuses. The localisation and manipulation of all involved acupuncture points are strictly conducted in accordance with WHO guidelines and the National Standard of the People’s Republic of China (GB/T 12 346–2021).

Data management and monitoring

All participants’ data will be kept confidential during and after the trial. Only the research team, including acupuncturists, outcome assessors, data collectors and managers, entry personnel and statisticians will have access to all data and they will undergo specialised training in standard procedures of data management. Data are double-entered by data entry personnel. The data manager will establish data-checking criteria based on the protocol and the CRF and develop procedures to comprehensively check the data for missing values, logical inconsistencies, timing and compliance with inclusion and exclusion criteria. A database-locking checklist will be prepared by the data manager. On completion of the checklist items, formal approval for database locking will be obtained in writing from relevant trial personnel with signatures and dates confirming the database locking process. In addition, the data manager will keep data management records as required and will deliver the locked database and associated materials to statisticians for analysis.

A data monitoring committee will not be appointed for this trial due to its short-term nature, minimal treatment risks, straightforward data collection and analysis and the single-blind nature of the trial which will allow investigators to assess participants’ treatment status promptly, thereby minimising unnecessary risks to participants and ensuring their safety.

Statistical methods

Sample size

The sample size was calculated based on the primary outcome of 24-hour urine volume with parameter estimation derived from preliminary test data. The difference between pretreatment and post-treatment values for the C group was 990.5±458.4 mL, 1283.7±726.2 mL for the MA group and 1084.3±534.7 mL for the SA group. Setting α=0.05 (two-tailed test) and power (1-β) =0.8 using PASS V.21 software, the required sample size for each group was calculated as 115 cases giving a total of 345 participants. Taking a 10% attrition rate into account, the total required sample size was approximately 384 cases, calculated as 345/ (1–0.10).

Data analysis

Data analysis will be performed with SPSS V.21.0. Outcome variables that followed a normal distribution will be expressed as mean±SD. Assuming homogeneity of variances, independent samples t-test will be used for between-group comparisons while paired t-test will be employed for within-group comparisons before and after treatment. For comparisons involving more than two groups, analysis of variance (ANOVA) will be used. In cases where the data exhibit a skewed distribution, it will be represented by the median (quartiles). Then, between-group comparisons will be conducted using the Mann-Whitney U test for two independent samples while within-group comparisons will be performed using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test. Multiple-sample rank-sum test will be used for comparisons involving more than two groups. The χ² test will be employed to compare the differences in the rates of paracentesis between groups.

All data will be subjected to two-tailed tests. For continuous variables, the means and SD will be reported. Moreover, within-group comparisons will be conducted using t-tests while between-group comparisons will be performed using t-tests, ANOVA or rank-sum tests. p<0.05 will be considered statistically significant.

Multiple imputation will be employed to handle missing data. This will involve using statistical software to generate complete data sets through model estimation and repeated simulations. Missing data in each data set will be imputed using estimation models, thus creating a set of repeated imputations based on the complete data as predicted by the majority of participants. The imputation model will capture the uncertainty in the modelling process and the inherent variability of patient outcomes as reflected in the complete data. The analysis of participant distribution encompasses screening and dropout assessments. During the trial, cases of serious protocol deviations and major protocol violations necessitate the exclusion of affected individuals from the analysis population.

A planned interim analysis will be conducted when 50% of 384 participants (target enrolment) have been followed through day 15. This is dependent on the sponsor.

Quality control

Prior to the trial, a programme will be established to provide uniform training to researchers, covering criteria for patient inclusion and exclusion, methods for data collection and monitoring, recording and reporting adverse events and other relevant procedures to ensure the standardised and consistent operation of the trial.

All acupuncturists possess sufficient professional knowledge and skills and hold a Chinese physician license. This license authorises the holder to practice medicine within the scope of their training and expertise in China indicating that the individual has met the necessary standards and competencies to provide medical care and treatment to patients in accordance with the regulations and guidelines set forth by the Chinese medical authorities.

Discussion

Due to exogenous or endogenous hCG, OHSS is more severe during pregnancies. Currently, vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), a potent vascular permeability inducer, has been proven to be closely associated with the onset of OHSS.1 37 Steroidal medications and hCG are known to stimulate angiogenesis and increase microvascular permeability which is a crucial step in angiogenesis. VEGF can induce fenestrations in endothelial cells of small venules and capillaries and lead to plasma protein leakage which contributes to the formation of fibrin gel outside blood vessels. Such leakage results in a decrease in intravascular albumin levels and sets off a series of symptoms associated with OHSS.25 38 Acupuncture has been shown to reduce serum VEGF levels.24

It has also been demonstrated to lower follicle-stimulating hormone and luteinising hormone levels, increase anti-Mullerian hormone and antral follicle count, improve ovarian function and reduce ovarian volume in reproductive endocrine disorders such as diminished ovarian reserve,18 primary ovarian insufficiency,19 poor ovarian response16 and polycystic ovary syndrome.39 Furthermore, research indicates that acupuncture can activate aromatase, promote the generation of neuropeptide Y, enhance ovarian local blood flow and benignly regulate the hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian axis. This modulation aids in the release and utilisation of sex hormones.16 20 Additionally, studies suggest that acupuncture can mobilise endorphins and signal integrators such as transient receptor potential vanilloid type 1 and purinergic receptor P2X3, thereby providing analgesic effects.17 22 23

We have conducted a pilot study to evaluate the efficacy of acupuncture in pregnant patients with OHSS and have found positive effects in the treatment of OHSS. The design of the pilot study was a RCT in which 10 hospitalised pregnant patients with OHSS were treated with acupuncture daily for 4–10 days. The outcomes were 24-hour urine volume, ascites and abdominal distension and degree of chest tightness which were measured before and after treatment. The results indicated a significant increase in 24-hour urine volume and considerable improvement in the degree of abdominal distension and chest tightness for both the MA group and the SA group compared with the C group. However, the SA group showed less improvement than the MA group. However, there were also some problems. For example, the ascites did not change much after acupuncture treatment but were absorbed faster in patients who were discharged from the hospital after the treatment. To further validate the efficacy of acupuncture, we have made improvements to avoid these previous problems in the current trial including the 2-month follow-up and adding more outcomes to comprehensively assess patients’ clinical changes.

In this trial, we employ an SA group and a C group. The SA group uses a non-acupoint needling approach where shallow needling is performed 10 mm adjacent to the acupuncture points used in the MA group to minimise stimulation and avoid inducing the arrival of qi sensation. However, it cannot be guaranteed that SA will have no therapeutic effect on the disease. Studies have shown that the features considered specific to a particular treatment are intertwined with characteristic factors that have a causal relationship with outcomes. These factors, along with incidental activities such as talking and listening, can elicit certain physiological responses in the body.40 In addition, responses to SA such as skin compression or prickling sensations may elicit tactile stimulation, activate the somatosensory system and relevant brain regions and thereby produce specific effects.41 42 As a result, direct comparisons between MA and SA may produce false negative results where the true effects of acupuncture are not observed. Therefore, we establish a C group with a specific implementation protocol based on guideline recommendations.

However, there are certain limitations to this study. First, as a single-centre study, we might be limited in capturing the full spectrum of effects of acupuncture, potentially introducing some bias into the results. To obtain more accurate and reliable conclusions, multicentre clinical trials with larger sample size to improve the generalisability should be further conducted. Second, the acupuncturists will not be blinded due to the unique features of acupuncture intervention but they will all be trained to avoid communicating with the patient to reduce bias. Nevertheless, this study still can provide preliminary insights into the efficacy of acupuncture in pregnant patients with early-onset OHSS and its impact on ovarian recovery.

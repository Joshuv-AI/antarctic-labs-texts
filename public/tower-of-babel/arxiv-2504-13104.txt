Taylor coefficients and zeroes of entire functions of exponential type
Lior Hadassi, Mikhail Sodin

Abstract
Let F F be an entire function of exponential type represented by the Taylor series F ⁡ ( z ) = ∑ n ≥ 0 ω n ​ z n n ! F(z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\omega_{n}\frac{z^{n}}{n!} with unimodular coefficients | ω n | = 1 |\omega_{n}|=1 . The second result requires less from the coefficient sequence ω \omega , but more from the counting function of zeroes n F n_{F} . Introduction

In this work we study the following question: how a sequence of multipliers
\omega\colon\mathbb{Z}_{+}~\to~\mathbb{C}

affects the number of zeroes of the entire function

F(z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\omega_{n}\,\frac{z^{n}}{n!}\,?

We will assume that
F
is an entire function of exponential type (EFET, for short),
that is,
\log M_{F}(R)=O(R)
as
R\to\infty
, where

M_{F}(R)=\displaystyle\max_{|z|=R}|F(z)|
, and therefore, the number of zeroes of

F
in the disk
\{|z|\leq R\}
grows at most linearly.

The relationship between the properties of the sequence
(\omega_{n})
and the zero distribution of

F
has been studied by many authors (Littlewood and Offord, Levin, Nassif, and others)
under the assumption that the sequence
(\omega_{n})
possesses a special structure. The most typical
instances are random i.i.d. sequences and, more generally, random stationary sequences, sequences of
arithmetic nature, and almost-periodic sequences. We refer the reader to the recent works by
Borichev, Nishry, and Sodin
[
3
]
and Benatar, Borichev, and Sodin
[
1
,
2
]
, which also contain
the references to what was done previously.

Here, our departing point is a classical result of Carlson
[
4
]
, who showed that
if

|\omega_{n}|=1
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, and
F
has few zeroes in the sense that

\sum\frac{1}{|\lambda|}<\infty\,,

(1)

where the summation is taken over the zero set of
F
,
then
F
is an exponential function, that is,
F(z)=\Theta e^{\alpha z}
with
|\Theta|=|\alpha|=1
.
This is the only result in this direction that we are aware of, which does not assume a special structure for

\omega_{n}
.

In this work, we extend Carlson’s result in two directions.
Our first result shows that (
1
) can be replaced with a much weaker assumption.
Let
n_{F}(R)
be a number of zeroes of
F
in the disk
\{|z|\leq R\}
,
counted with multiplicities.

Theorem 1
.

Let

F(z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\omega_{n}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

with
|\omega_{n}|=1
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Then there exists a positive numerical constant
c
such that
either
F
is an exponential function, or

\liminf_{R\to\infty}\frac{n_{F}(R)}{R}\geq c>0.

(2)

Note that the Carlson assumption (
1
) corresponds to convergence of the integral

\int^{\infty}\frac{n_{F}(t)}{t}\,{\rm d}t<\infty.

It is also worth mentioning that we do not know a sharp value of the constant
c
in Theorem
1
.

Actually, we will prove more, requiring only that the set
\{n\colon|\omega_{n}|=1\}
has a positive lower density, and that

F
has a finite exponential type, see Theorem
3
below. Note that in the end of his note Carlson also mentions that his result holds when
F
has a finite exponential type and the set
\{n\colon|\omega_{n}|=1\}
has a positive lower density.

The proof of Theorem
1
uses several novel tools comparing with the ones used by Carlson. Let us briefly
recall his argument. Condition (
1
) yields that
F(z)=e^{\alpha z}G(z)
, where
|\alpha|=1
and
G
is an entire function of zero exponential type. Rotating the coordinates, assume that
\alpha=1
, i.e.,
F(z)=e^{z}G(z)
.
Let
\widehat{F}
be the Laplace transform of
F
. It is analytic in
\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{1\}
, vanishes at infinity,
and has the expansion

\widehat{F}(w)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\omega_{n}}{w^{n+1}}\,.

Then, its coefficients
\omega_{n}
can be interpolated by an entire function
g
of zero exponential type, that is,

g(n)=\omega_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Then the entire function

\widetilde{g}(\lambda)=1-g(\lambda)\overline{g}(\overline{\lambda})
has zero exponential type and vanishes on
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.
Thus, by a classical uniqueness theorem (which is also due to Carlson),
\widetilde{g}
is the zero function, i.e.,

g
is a unimodular constant, that is,
\omega
is a constant sequence, and
F(z)=Ce^{z}
,
|C|=1
.

Note that Carlson’s theorem was extended by Eremenko and Ostrovskii
[
5
, Theorem 1]

in a direction different from the one pursued here. They showed that if
|\omega_{n}|=1
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, then
F
cannot
decay exponentially fast on any ray
\{\arg(z)=\theta\}
. Clearly, this yields Carlson’s theorem, since the representation

F(z)=e^{\alpha z}G(z)
, where
G
has zero exponential type and
|\alpha|=1
, immediately yields that
F
decays exponentially fast in an open half-plane. Their argument also was based on the consideration of the Laplace transform

\widehat{F}
.

The main difficulty in extending Carlson’s idea to our case is that the negation of our assumption (
2
)
yields only local estimates on the function
G
. These local estimates do not allow us to use the Laplace transform and classical results on the interpolating function. To circumvent this obstacle, instead of
interpolating the sequence
\omega_{n}
, we interpolate the sequence
\omega_{n}\bar{\omega}_{n+h}
, where

h\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
is a parameter, which needs to be adjusted. It turns out that the local information on
F
that Theorem
1
provides, is sufficient to efficiently bound the interpolating function. In addition,
we will need a small piece of additive combinatorics to deal with the case when the set
\{n\colon|\omega_{n}|=1\}
only
has a positive lower density.

It is worth mentioning that the techniques developed for the proof of Theorem
1
allow one
also to treat other closely related families of entire functions, such as

\sum_{n\geq 0}\omega_{n}\,\frac{z^{n}}{\sqrt{n!}}\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{n\geq 0}\omega_{n}\,\frac{z^{n}}{\Gamma(n+\alpha)}\,.

Our second result requires less from the coefficient sequence
\omega
, but more from the counting function of zeroes
n_{F}
.

Theorem 2
.

Let

F(z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\omega_{n}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

with
0<c\leq|\omega_{n}|\leq C<\infty
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Suppose that

(i)
either

\limsup_{R\to\infty}\,\frac{n_{F}(R)}{\sqrt{R}}=0,

(3)

(ii)

or

\liminf_{R\to\infty}\,\frac{n_{F}(R)}{R^{\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}}=0

(4)

with some
\varepsilon>0
.

Then
F
is an exponential function.

The proof of both parts starts with the observation that, by the boundedness from below and from above of
the sequence
(\omega_{n})
, we have

R-\tfrac{1}{2}\,\log R-O(1)<\log M_{F}(R)<R+O(1),\qquad R\to\infty.

The rest of the argument is purely subharmonic.
The proof of part (i) is quite simple. In fact, it yields
a somewhat stronger result (see Remark at the end of Section 3.1).
The proof of part (ii) (suggested by Fedor Nazarov)
is more intricate, and we do not know whether one can replace (
4
)
by

\liminf_{R\to\infty}\,\frac{n_{F}(R)}{\sqrt{R}}=0.

(5)

We conclude the paper with two examples.
First, we will show that the entire function

F(z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\,(\cos\sqrt{n}+2)\,\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

can be written in the form
F(z)=e^{z}G(z)
, where
G
is an entire function of order
1/2
and mean type. Therefore,

n_{F}(R)=O(\sqrt{R})
as
R\to\infty
; i.e., the exponent
\tfrac{1}{2}
in part (i) of Theorem
2
cannot
be improved. The second example will illustrate why the method used in the proof of Theorem
2
(ii)
ceases to work under a weaker assumption (
5
).

(\sigma)

,

\varepsilon

2.1
The self-correlation transform

Given a Taylor series

f(z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}z^{n}

and
h\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, we set

\displaystyle f_{h}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}\bar{a}_{n+h}z^{2n}

\displaystyle=\oint_{|s|=1}f(sz)f^{*}(\bar{s}z)\Bigl(\frac{s}{z}\Bigr)^{h}\,\frac{{\rm d}s}{2\pi{\rm i}s}\,,

where the integral is taken over the unit circle traversed counterclockwise.
Applying this transform to our function
F
, we get

F_{h}(z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\omega_{n}\bar{\omega}_{n+h}\,\frac{z^{2n}}{n!(n+h)!}\,.

Lemma 4
.

Suppose that there exist
R\geq 1
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
such that

F(z)=e^{\alpha z}G(z)\,,

\log M_{G}(R)<o_{\varepsilon}(1)R\,,

and

|\alpha|<1+o_{\varepsilon}(1)\,.

Then, for any
r\in[1,R]
, we have

|F_{h}(re^{{\rm i}\theta})|\leq r^{-h}e^{2r|\cos\theta|+o_{\varepsilon}(1)R}\,.

Proof
: We have

\displaystyle|F_{h}(re^{{\rm i}\theta})|

\displaystyle=\Bigl|\,\oint_{|s|=1}F(sre^{{\rm i}\theta})\overline{F(sre^{-{\rm i}\theta})}\Bigl(\frac{s}{re^{{\rm i}\theta}}\Bigr)^{h}\,\frac{{\rm d}s}{2\pi{\rm i}s}\,\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq r^{-h}\,\max_{|s|=1}\bigl|e^{\alpha sre^{{\rm i}\theta}}\cdot e^{\bar{\alpha}\bar{s}re^{{\rm i}\theta}}\bigr|\,e^{o_{\varepsilon}(1)R}

\displaystyle=r^{-h}e^{2r|\alpha|\,|\cos\theta|+o_{\varepsilon}(1)R}\qquad(\text{since}\ \max_{|s|=1}\,|\alpha s+\bar{\alpha}\bar{s}|=2|\alpha|)

\displaystyle\leq r^{-h}e^{2r|\cos\theta|+o_{\varepsilon}(1)R},

proving the lemma.
\Box

It will be more convenient to work with the function

F_{h}^{\sharp}(z)=F_{h}(\sqrt{z})=\sum_{n\geq 0}\omega_{n}\bar{\omega}_{n+h}\frac{z^{n}}{n!(n+h)!}\,.

By Lemma
4
, for
z=re^{{\rm i}\theta}
,
1\leq r\leq R^{2}
,
|\theta|\leq\pi
,
we have

|F_{h}^{\sharp}(re^{{\rm i}\theta})|\leq r^{-h/2}e^{2\sqrt{r}\cos\frac{\theta}{2}+o_{\varepsilon}(1)R}\,.

2.2
Analytic interpolation

Given
0\leq h\leq R
, we set

\kappa=\Bigl[\,\frac{h}{2}\,\Bigr]+\frac{1}{2}

(as usual,
[x]
denotes the integer part of
x
),
and denote by
\Omega_{h}
the shifted semi-disk

\Omega_{h}=\bigl\{\lambda\colon\text{\ Re}(\lambda)>-\kappa,|\lambda+\kappa|<R-\kappa\bigr\}.

Lemma 5
.

Let

F(z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\omega_{n}\,\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

be an
EFET
. Suppose that, for some
R>1
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
, we have

F(z)=e^{\alpha z}G(z),

\log M_{G}(R)<o_{\varepsilon}(1)R,

(6)

|\alpha|<1+o_{\varepsilon}(1).

(7)

Then, given
h\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, there exists a function
g_{h}
holomorphic
in the half-plane
\{\text{Re}(\lambda)>-(h+1)\}
, interpolating the sequence
\omega_{n}\bar{\omega}_{n+h}
, i.e.,

g_{h}(n)=\omega_{n}\bar{\omega}_{n+h}\,,\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},

and, for any
h\in[0,R]
, satisfying

|g_{h}(\lambda)|\leq e^{o_{\varepsilon}(1)R+O(R^{-1}h^{2})}\,,\qquad\lambda\in\bar{\Omega}_{h},

provided that
R=R_{\varepsilon}
is sufficiently big.

Proof of Lemma
5
:
Note that, for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,

n!(n+h)!\,\oint_{|z|=\rho}\frac{F_{h}^{\sharp}(z)}{z^{n}}\,\frac{{\rm d}z}{2\pi{\rm i}z}=\begin{cases}\omega_{n}\bar{\omega}_{n+h}&n\geq 0,\\
0&n<0,\end{cases}

where the integral is taken over the circle of radius
\rho>0
traversed counterclockwise.
Keeping this in mind, given
h\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, we consider the function

g_{h}(\lambda)=\Gamma(\lambda+1)\Gamma(\lambda+h+1)\,\int_{-{\rm i}\pi+2\log r}^{{\rm i}\pi+2\log r}F_{h}^{\sharp}(e^{w})e^{-\lambda w}\,\frac{{\rm d}w}{2\pi{\rm i}}\,,

(8)

where the integration is taken over the vertical segment
\{w=2\log r+{\rm i}t\colon|t|\leq\pi\}
,
and the parameter
r
has to be chosen.
The integral on the RHS defines an entire function in
\lambda
vanishing at the negative integers, therefore, the function
g_{h}
is analytic in the right half-plane
\{{\rm Re}(\lambda)>-(h+1)\}
,
and interpolates
\omega_{n}\bar{\omega}_{n+h}
at
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

It remains to estimate
g_{h}
in
\bar{\Omega}_{h}
, so in what follows we assume that
0\leq h\leq R
.
It will be convenient to shift the variable
\lambda
, letting

s=\lambda+\kappa=\rho e^{{\rm i}\varphi},\ R_{h}=R-\kappa,\ D_{+}(R_{h})=\{s\colon{\rm Re}(s)>0,|s|<R_{h}\}\,.

In this notation,
\Omega_{h}=D_{+}(R_{h})-\kappa
, and

\displaystyle\Gamma(\lambda+1)\Gamma(\lambda+h+1)

\displaystyle=\Gamma\bigl(s-\bigl[\tfrac{h}{2}\bigr]+\tfrac{1}{2}\bigr)\Gamma\bigl(s+\bigl[\tfrac{h}{2}\bigr]+\tfrac{1}{2}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\Gamma\bigl(s+\tfrac{1}{2}\bigr)^{2}\,\Pi_{h}(s)\,,

where

\Pi_{h}(s)=\prod_{1\leq\ell\leq[\frac{h}{2}]}\frac{s+(\ell-\frac{1}{2})}{s-(\ell-\frac{1}{2})}\,.

At last, we set in (
8
)
r=R_{h}=R-\kappa
, and get

\displaystyle g_{h}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\Gamma\bigl(s+\tfrac{1}{2}\bigr)^{2}\,\Pi_{h}(s)\int_{-{\rm i}\pi+2\log R_{h}}^{{\rm i}\pi+2\log R_{h}}F_{h}^{\sharp}(e^{w})e^{\kappa w}\cdot e^{-sw}\,\frac{{\rm d}w}{2\pi{\rm i}}

\displaystyle=\Gamma\bigl(s+\tfrac{1}{2}\bigr)^{2}\,\Pi_{h}(s)\,J_{h}(s)\,.

(9)

We estimate each of the three factors on the RHS of (
9
)
separately. The
\Gamma
-factor and the integral
J_{h}(s)

will be estimated everywhere on
\bar{D}_{+}(R_{h})
, while the product
\Pi_{h}

only on the boundary
\partial D_{+}(R_{h})
. By the maximum principle, this will suffice.

We set

k(\varphi)=\cos\varphi+\varphi\sin\varphi,\quad|\varphi|\leq\frac{\pi}{2}\,.

Estimate of
J_{h}(s)
: We will show that

|J_{h}(s)|\leq\rho^{-2\rho\cos\varphi}e^{2k(\varphi)\rho+o_{\varepsilon}(1)R},\qquad s=\rho e^{{\rm i}\varphi}\in\bar{D}_{+}(R_{h})\,.

We start with a standard deformation of the integration contour:

J_{h}(s)=\Bigl[\,\int_{-{\rm i}\pi+2\log R_{h}}^{-{\rm i}\pi+2\log\rho}+\int_{-{\rm i}\pi+2\log\rho}^{{\rm i}\pi+2\log\rho}+\int_{{\rm i}\pi+2\log\rho}^{{\rm i}\pi+2\log R_{h}}\,\Bigr]F_{h}^{\sharp}(e^{w})e^{\kappa w}\,e^{-sw}\,\frac{{\rm d}w}{2\pi{\rm i}}\,,

and estimate these integrals separately.

First, suppose that
w=u\pm{\rm i}\pi
,
2\log\rho\leq u\leq 2\log R_{h}
. Then,
by Lemma
4
,

|F_{h}^{\sharp}(e^{w})e^{\kappa w}|\leq e^{\frac{h}{2}u+o_{\varepsilon}(1)R}\cdot e^{-([\frac{h}{2}]+\frac{1}{2})u}\leq e^{o_{\varepsilon}(1)R}\,,

while
|e^{-sw}|\leq e^{-u\rho\cos\varphi+\pi\rho|\sin\varphi|}
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\Bigl|\,\int_{\pm{\rm i}\pi+2\log\rho}^{\pm{\rm i}\pi+2\log R_{h}}F_{h}^{\sharp}(e^{w})e^{\kappa w}\,e^{-sw}\,\frac{{\rm d}w}{2\pi{\rm i}}\,\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq e^{\pi\rho|\sin\varphi|+o_{\varepsilon}(1)R}\,\int_{2\log\rho}^{2\log R_{h}}e^{-\rho u\cos\varphi\,}\,\frac{{\rm d}u}{2\pi}

\displaystyle<e^{\pi\rho|\sin\varphi|+o_{\varepsilon}(1)R}\,\int_{\log\rho}^{\infty}e^{-2\rho u\cos\varphi\,}\,\frac{{\rm d}u}{4\pi}

\displaystyle<\rho^{-2\rho\cos\varphi}\,e^{\pi\rho|\sin\varphi|+o_{\varepsilon}(1)R}\,.

Now, consider the case when
w=2\log\rho+{\rm i}t
,
\pi\leq t\leq\pi
. In this case,
by Lemma
4
,

|F_{h}^{\sharp}(e^{w})e^{\kappa w}|\leq e^{2\rho\cos\frac{t}{2}+o_{\varepsilon}(1)R}

(since
\kappa=\bigl[\,\tfrac{h}{2}\,\bigr]+\tfrac{1}{2}\,
) and

|e^{-sw}|=e^{-2\rho\log\rho\cos\varphi+\rho t\sin\varphi}
.
Therefore,

\Bigl|\,\int_{-{\rm i}\pi+2\log\rho}^{{\rm i}\pi+2\log\rho}F_{h}^{\sharp}(e^{w})e^{\kappa w}\,e^{-sw}\,\frac{{\rm d}w}{2\pi{\rm i}}\,\Bigr|\leq\rho^{-2\rho\cos\varphi}e^{o_{\varepsilon}(1)R}\,\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{(2\cos\frac{t}{2}+t\sin\varphi)\rho}\,\frac{{\rm d}t}{2\pi}\,.

Noting that

\max_{t\in[-\pi,\pi]}\bigl(2\cos\frac{t}{2}+t\sin\varphi\bigr)=2k(\varphi),\qquad|\varphi|\leq\frac{\pi}{2}\,,

(10)

we see that

\Bigl|\,\int_{-{\rm i}\pi+2\log\rho}^{{\rm i}\pi+2\log\rho}F_{h}^{\sharp}(e^{w})e^{\kappa w}\,e^{-sw}\,\frac{{\rm d}w}{2\pi{\rm i}}\,\Bigr|\leq\rho^{-2\rho\cos\varphi}e^{2k(\varphi)\rho+o_{\varepsilon}(1)R}\,,

and therefore,

|J_{h}(s)|\leq\rho^{-2\rho\cos\varphi}e^{o_{\varepsilon}(1)R}\Bigl[\,e^{\pi\rho|\sin\varphi|}+e^{2k(\varphi)\rho}\,\Bigr]\,.

(11)

Letting
t=\pm\pi
in (
10
), we get

\pi|\sin\varphi|\leq 2k(\varphi),\qquad|\varphi|\leq\frac{\pi}{2}\,,

concluding that the first exponent in the brackets on the RHS of (
11
) can be discarded.

Estimate of

\Pi_{h}(s)
on
\partial D_{+}(R_{h})
:
Clearly,
|\Pi_{h}({\rm i}t)|=1
. For
s=R_{h}e^{{\rm i}\varphi}
, we have

\displaystyle\bigl|\,\Pi_{h}(s)\,\bigr|

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq\ell\leq[\frac{h}{2}]}\Bigl|\,\frac{1+(\ell-\frac{1}{2})/s}{1-(\ell-\frac{1}{2})/s}\,\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{1\leq\ell\leq[\frac{h}{2}]}\,\frac{1+(\ell-\frac{1}{2})/R_{h}}{1-(\ell-\frac{1}{2})/R_{h}}\,\Bigr|\leq e^{CR_{h}^{-1}h^{2}}\,.

Hence, everywhere on the boundary of the semi-disk

D_{+}(R_{h})
, we have

\bigl|\,\Pi_{h}(s)\,\bigr|<e^{\max\{CR_{h}^{-1}h^{2},1\}}\,.

Estimate of

\Gamma\bigl(s+\tfrac{1}{2}\bigr)^{2}
:
By the Stirling formula,

\Gamma\bigl(s+\tfrac{1}{2}\bigr)=\sqrt{2\pi}\,s^{s}e^{-s}(1+o(1)),\quad\text{Re\,}s\to+\infty\,,

whence,

\displaystyle\bigl|\Gamma\bigl(s+\tfrac{1}{2}\bigr)\bigr|^{2}\lesssim\rho^{2\rho\cos\varphi}\,e^{-2\rho(\varphi\sin\varphi+\cos\varphi)}=\rho^{2\rho\cos\varphi}\,e^{-2k(\varphi)\rho}\,,\qquad{\rm Re}(s)\geq 0\,.

Combining the estimates of
J_{h}(s)
,
\Pi_{h}(s)
, and
\Gamma\bigl(s+\tfrac{1}{2}\bigr)^{2}
,
we see that
|g_{h}(\lambda)|<e^{o_{\varepsilon}(1)R+O(R^{-1}h^{2})}
everywhere
on the boundary
\partial\Omega_{h}
, and hence, inside
\Omega_{h}
, as well.
\Box

2.3
Some estimates

For an interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
, we denote by
L(I)
its length, and by
I_{+t}
its open complex

t
-neigbourhood.

The following Jensen-type bound is classical.
To keep the exposition self-contained, we recall its proof.

Lemma 6
.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be an interval of length

L(I)\lesssim t,

(12)

and let
\Lambda\subset I
be a discrete set of cardinality
|\Lambda|
.
Then there exists a positive constant
c
,
depending only on the implicit constant in (
12
), such
that, for any function
f
analytic in
I_{+t}
and vanishing on
\Lambda
, we have

\max_{\overline{I}_{+t/2}}|f|<e^{-c|\Lambda|}\sup_{I_{+t}}|f|\,.

Proof
: Consider the Riemann mapping

\varphi\colon\mathbb{D}\to I_{+t}
which maps the origin to the center of the interval
I
.
We claim that there exists
\rho<1
, independent of
t
, such that

\overline{I}_{+t/2}\subset\varphi(\rho\mathbb{D})
. It suffices to check this only for
t=1

(otherwise, we replace the function
\varphi
by
t\varphi
), so we assume that
t=1
. If the claim
does not hold, then there exists a sequence of points
(z_{n})\subset\mathbb{D}
such that
z_{n}\to\partial\mathbb{D}
, but
\varphi(z_{n})\in\overline{I}_{+1/2}
, which contradicts the covering property of Riemann mappings.

Now, consider the function
f\circ\varphi
. It vanishes at the points
of
\varphi^{-1}(\Lambda)\subset\rho\mathbb{D}
. Denote by
B_{a}(z)=(z-a)/(1-z\bar{a})
the Blaschke factor,
and set

c(\rho)=\max\{|B_{a}(z)|\colon|a|,|z|\leq\rho\}.

Obviously,
c(\rho)<1
. Then

f\circ\varphi=g\cdot\prod_{a\in\varphi^{-1}(\Lambda)}B_{a}
, where the function
g

is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
, and
\sup_{\mathbb{D}}|g|=\sup_{\mathbb{D}}|f\circ\varphi|
. Hence,

\displaystyle\max_{\bar{I}_{+z/2}}|f|\leq\max_{\rho\bar{\mathbb{D}}}|f\circ\varphi|

\displaystyle\leq c(\rho)^{|\Lambda|}\max_{\rho\bar{\mathbb{D}}}|g|

\displaystyle\leq e^{-c|\Lambda|}\sup_{\mathbb{D}}|g|=e^{-c|\Lambda|}\sup_{\mathbb{D}}|f\circ\varphi|=e^{-c|\Lambda|}\sup_{I_{+t}}|f|,

proving the lemma.
\Box

Lemma 7
.

Let
t\geq t_{0}
. Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be an interval of length

t_{0}\leq L(I)\lesssim t,

(13)

and let
\Lambda\subset I
be a discrete set of cardinality

|\Lambda|\gtrsim t.

(14)

Then there exists
a positive constant
c
(depending only on the implicit constants in (
13
) and (
14
)) such that, for any function
g
analytic function in
I_{+t}
satisfying

\sup_{I_{+t}}|g|<e^{o_{\varepsilon}(1)t},

(15)

and

|g(\lambda)|=1,\qquad\lambda\in\Lambda,

we have

\displaystyle\max_{s\in I}||g(s)|-1|<e^{-ct},

(16)

\displaystyle\max_{s\in I}\,\Bigl|\,\frac{g(s+1)}{g(s)}-1\Bigr|<o_{\varepsilon}(1)+e^{-ct},

(17)

and

\displaystyle\max_{s\in I}\,\Bigl|\,\frac{g(s)^{2}}{g(s-1)g(s+1)}-1\Bigr|<o_{\varepsilon}(1)+e^{-ct},

(18)

provided that
\varepsilon\ll 1\ll t_{0}
.

Proof
: Set
g^{*}(s)=\overline{g(\bar{s})}
and
f(s)=g(s)g^{*}(s)-1
.
The function
f
is analytic on
I_{+t}
, satisfies (
15
), and vanishes on
\Lambda
.
Hence, by Lemma
6
,

\max_{\overline{I}_{+t/2}}|f|<e^{-ct}+e^{o_{\varepsilon}(1)t}<e^{-ct}\,.

In particular,

\max_{I}|\,|g|-1|\leq\max_{I}|\,|g|^{2}-1|=\max_{I}|f|<e^{-ct}\,,

proving (
16
).

Set
I^{*}=I_{+1}\cap\mathbb{R}
. For
s\in I
, we have

\frac{g(s+1)}{g(s)}=\exp\Bigl(\int_{s}^{s+1}\frac{g^{\prime}}{g}(\xi)\,{\rm d}\xi\Bigr),

whence,

\Bigl|\frac{g(s+1)}{g(s)}-1\Bigr|\leq 2\max_{I^{*}}|g^{\prime}/g|\,,

assuming that the maximum on the RHS is sufficiently small.

Take
\xi\in I^{*}
, and let
\rho=\tfrac{1}{2}t
.
Since the function
f
is very small on
\overline{I}_{+t/2}
, the function
g

cannot vanish therein, and therefore, we can choose in
I_{+t/2}
a branch of
\log g
.
Applying the Schwarz formula to
\log g
in the disk
D(\xi,\rho)\subset I_{+t/2}
,
we have

\log g(\xi+s)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\,\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\log|g(\xi+\rho e^{{\rm i}\theta})|\,\frac{\rho e^{{\rm i}\theta}+s}{\rho e^{{\rm i}\theta}-s}\,{\rm d}\theta+{\rm i}C.

(19)

Differentiating in
s
and letting
s=0
, we get

\frac{g^{\prime}}{g}(\xi)=\frac{1}{\pi\rho}\,\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\log|g(\xi+\rho e^{{\rm i}\theta})|\,e^{-{\rm i}\theta}\,{\rm d}\theta\,,

whence

\Bigl|\,\frac{g^{\prime}}{g}(\xi)\,\Bigr|\leq\frac{1}{\pi\rho}\,\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\bigl|\log|g(\xi+\rho e^{{\rm i}\theta})|\bigr|\,{\rm d}\theta\,.

(20)

Everywhere in the disk
D(\xi,\rho)
, we have
\log|g|\leq o_{\varepsilon}(1)t
, while in the center of
that disk
\log|g|
is very small:

|\log|g(\xi)|\,|=|\log(1+(|g(\xi)|-1))|<e^{-ct}.

Recalling that
\log|g|
is harmonic in the disk
D(\xi,\rho)
, we get

\Bigl|\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\bigl[\log_{+}|g(\xi+\rho e^{{\rm i}\theta})|-\log_{-}|g(\xi+\rho e^{{\rm i}\theta})|\bigr]\,{\rm d}\theta\Bigr|\\
=\Bigl|\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\log|g(\xi+\rho e^{{\rm i}\theta})|\,{\rm d}\theta\Bigr|=|\log|g(\xi)|\,|<e^{-ct}.

Therefore,

\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\log_{-}|g(\xi+\rho e^{{\rm i}\theta})|\,{\rm d}\theta<\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\log_{+}|g(\xi+\rho e^{{\rm i}\theta})|\,{\rm d}\theta+e^{-ct}<o_{\varepsilon}(1)t+e^{-ct}\,,

and

\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|\log|g(\xi+\rho e^{{\rm i}\theta})|\,|\,{\rm d}\theta\\
=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\bigl[\log_{+}|g(\xi+\rho e^{{\rm i}\theta})|\,{\rm d}\theta+\log_{-}|g(\xi+\rho e^{{\rm i}\theta})|\bigr]\,{\rm d}\theta<o_{\varepsilon}(1)t+e^{-ct}\,.

Plugging this estimate
in (
20
) and recalling that
\rho
and
t
are comparable,
we conclude that
\max_{I^{*}}|g^{\prime}/g|<o_{\varepsilon}(1)+e^{-ct}
, proving (
17
).

At last,

\Bigl|\,\frac{g(s)^{2}}{g(s-1)g(s+1)}-1\Bigr|\leq 2\max_{I^{*}}|(g^{\prime}/g)^{\prime}|,

providing that the maximum on the RHS is sufficiently small.
Differentiating twice the Schwarz formula (
19
)
and letting
s=0
, we see that

\Bigl(\frac{g^{\prime}}{g}\Bigr)^{\prime}(\xi)=\frac{2}{\pi\rho^{2}}\,\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\log|g(\xi+\rho e^{{\rm i}\theta})|e^{-2{\rm i}\theta}\,{\rm d}\theta,

whence

\max_{I^{*}}|(g^{\prime}/g)^{\prime}|\leq\frac{2}{\pi\rho^{2}}\,\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\bigl|\log|g(\xi+\rho e^{{\rm i}\theta})|\bigr|\,{\rm d}\theta\lesssim t^{-1}o_{\varepsilon}(1)+e^{-ct},

proving (
18
).
\Box

2.4
Local bounds of
F

We assume that
F
is an
EFET
(\sigma)
satisfying assumptions of Theorem
3
, that is,

F(z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\omega_{n}\,\frac{z^{n}}{n!}\,,

where the set
\Lambda_{F}=\{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\colon|\omega_{n}|=1
} has a lower density
d>0
.
Here we will show that if
F
has relatively few zeroes, then it has bounds (
6
) and (
7
) needed for application of Lemma
5
.

Lemma 8
.

Let
F
satisfy the assumptions of Theorem
3
, and let
there exist

\bullet
a sufficiently small
\varepsilon
, depending only on the type
\sigma
of
F

and on the lower density
d
of the set
\Lambda_{F}
,

\bullet

A=\omega_{\varepsilon}(1)
,

\bullet

and a sufficiently large
R=R_{\varepsilon}
,

such that

n_{F}(AR)<\varepsilon R.

Then there exist

\bullet

\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
,
|\alpha|<1+o_{\varepsilon}(1)
,

\bullet
and
A_{1}=\omega_{\varepsilon}(1)

such that

F(z)=e^{\alpha z}G(z),

with

\log M_{G}(A_{1}R)<o_{\varepsilon}(1)R.

Proof of Lemma
8
will consist of several steps.
We fix
A_{1}=\omega_{\varepsilon}(1)
so that

A_{1}^{2}\max\{\varepsilon,A^{-1}\}=o_{\varepsilon}(1)\,.

Bounding
G
:
We choose
r_{0}
so large that

\log M_{F}(r)\leq(\sigma+\varepsilon)r
and
n_{F}(r)\lesssim\sigma r
for
r\geq r_{0}
,
and assume that
r_{0}
is a way smaller than
R
. We let
P
be a polynomial
vanishing at zeroes of
F
lying in the disk
\{|\lambda|\leq r_{0}\}
and normalized
so that
P(0)=F(0)
.

Set
R_{1}=A_{1}R
. Applying the Hadamard factorization theorem to
F(z)
with
|z|=R_{1}
, we get

\displaystyle F(z)

\displaystyle=e^{\alpha z}P(z)\prod_{r_{0}<|\lambda|\leq AR}\Bigl(1-\frac{z}{\lambda}\Bigr)\prod_{|\lambda|>AR}\Bigl(1-\frac{z}{\lambda}\Bigr)e^{\frac{z}{\lambda}}

\displaystyle=e^{\alpha z}P(z)\pi_{1}(z)\pi_{2}(z)=e^{\alpha z}G(z).

We have

\log|P(z)|\leq C_{F}+n_{F}(r_{0})\log(R+r_{0})=o_{\varepsilon}(1)R
,
provided that
R
is sufficiently large. We also have

\log|\pi_{1}(z)|\leq\int_{r_{0}}^{AR}\log\Bigl(1+\frac{R_{1}}{t}\Bigr)\,{\rm d}n_{F}(t)\,.

Integrating by parts, we bound the integral on the RHS by

\displaystyle n_{F}(AR)

\displaystyle\log\Bigl(1+\frac{A_{1}}{A}\Bigr)+R_{1}\,\int_{r_{0}}^{AR}\frac{n_{F}(t)}{t(t+R_{1})}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq R_{1}\,\int_{r_{0}}^{AR}\frac{n_{F}(t)}{t(t+R_{1})}\,{\rm d}t+o_{\varepsilon}(1)R

\displaystyle<\int_{r_{0}}^{R/A_{1}}\frac{n_{F}(t)}{t}\,{\rm d}t+R_{1}\int_{R/A_{1}}^{AR}\frac{n_{F}(t)}{t^{2}}\,{\rm d}t+o_{\varepsilon}(1)R

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{r_{0}}^{R/A_{1}}\sigma\,{\rm d}t+R_{1}\int_{R/A_{1}}^{AR}\frac{\varepsilon R}{t^{2}}\,{\rm d}t+o_{\varepsilon}(1)R

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigl(\sigma A_{1}^{-1}+A_{1}^{2}\varepsilon+o_{\varepsilon}(1)\bigr)R=o_{\varepsilon}(1)R,

and similarly

\displaystyle\log|\pi_{2}(z)|

\displaystyle\lesssim R_{1}^{2}\,\int_{AR}^{\infty}\frac{{\rm d}n_{F}(t)}{t^{2}}

\displaystyle<2R_{1}^{2}\,\int_{AR}^{\infty}\frac{n_{F}(t)}{t^{3}}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim(A_{1}R)^{2}\,\int_{AR}^{\infty}\sigma\,\frac{{\rm d}t}{t^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sigma A_{1}^{2}A^{-1}R=o_{\varepsilon}(1)R\,.

Combining these bounds, we see that
\log M_{G}(A_{1}R)=o_{\varepsilon}(1)R
.

Preliminary bound for
|\alpha|
: Here we show that

|\alpha|<R^{-1}\,\log M_{F}(R)+o_{\varepsilon}(1)\,.

We assume that
\alpha
is positive (otherwise, we rotate
z
) and
that
F(0)\neq 0
, the case
F(0)=0
does not bring new difficulties. Then we take a sufficiently small
\kappa>0
(to be chosen momentarily),
and write

\displaystyle-C_{F}=\log|F(0)|

\displaystyle=\log|G(0)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\log|G(Re^{{\rm i}\theta})|\,\frac{{\rm d}\theta}{2\pi}

\displaystyle=\int_{|\theta|\leq\kappa}\bigl[\log|F(Re^{{\rm i}\theta})|-\alpha R\cos\theta\bigr]\,\frac{{\rm d}\theta}{2\pi}+\int_{\kappa\leq|\theta|\leq\pi}\log|G(Re^{{\rm i}\theta})|\,\frac{{\rm d}\theta}{2\pi}

\displaystyle\leq 2\kappa\log M_{F}(R)-\alpha R(2\kappa-\kappa^{3}/3))+o_{\varepsilon}(1)R\,,

whence,

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle\leq(1+C\kappa^{2})\,R^{-1}\log M_{F}(R)+\kappa^{-1}o_{\varepsilon}(1)+C_{F}(\kappa R)^{-1}

\displaystyle\leq R^{-1}\log M_{F}(R)+C\bigl(\sigma\kappa^{2}+\kappa^{-1}o_{\varepsilon}(1)\bigr)+o_{\varepsilon}(1).

Choosing
\kappa=o_{\varepsilon}(1)
so that
(\sigma\kappa^{2}+\kappa^{-1}o_{\varepsilon}(1))=o_{\varepsilon}(1)
,
we see that the RHS is bounded by

R^{-1}\log M_{F}(R)+o_{\varepsilon}(1),

provided that
R
is sufficiently big.

Bounding
|\omega_{n}|
: Let
I=[bR,BR]
where parameters

B
and
b
,
0<b\ll 1\ll B
(depending only on the type
\sigma
and density
d
) are to be chosen.
Here we show that, for
n\in I
, the absolute values of the coefficients

|\omega_{n}|
cannot be essentially bigger than
1
. To show this, we will use
the analytic interpolation Lemma
5
.

We set

\widetilde{F}(z)=F\Bigl(\frac{z}{\alpha}\Bigr)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\omega_{n}}{\alpha^{n}}\,\frac{z^{n}}{n!}\,.

Then
n_{\widetilde{F}}(AR/\alpha)<o_{\varepsilon}(1)R
, and, by the previous steps,

\widetilde{F}(z)=e^{z}G\Bigl(\frac{z}{\alpha}\Bigr)=e^{z}\widetilde{G}(z)

with

\log M_{\widetilde{G}}(r)=\log M_{G}(r/\alpha)<o_{\varepsilon}(1)R,

whenever
r<\alpha A_{1}R
, in particular, for
r=2BR+\tfrac{1}{2}
.
This allows us to apply Lemma
5
to the function
\widetilde{F}
with
h=0
(and
2BR+\tfrac{1}{2}
instead of
R
).
It gives us the interpolation function
g_{0}
,

g_{0}(n)=\Bigl|\,\frac{\omega_{n}}{\alpha^{n}}\,\Bigr|^{2},\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\,,

analytic in the right half-plane and satisfying

|g_{0}(s)|<e^{o_{\varepsilon}(1)R}

in the closed semi-disk
\bar{\Omega}=\{s\colon|s|\leq 2BR,\text{\ Re}(s)\geq 0\}
.

Next, we set

f(s)=g_{0}(s)-\frac{1}{|\alpha|^{2s}}\,.

Then the function
f
has the same upper bound in
\bar{\Omega}
as
g_{0}
, i.e.,

|f(s)|<e^{o_{\varepsilon}(1)R},\quad z\in\bar{\Omega}\,,

and

f(n)=\Bigl|\frac{\omega_{n}}{\alpha^{n}}\Bigr|^{2}-\frac{1}{|\alpha|^{2n}}\,.

In particular,
f(n)=0
for
n\in\Lambda_{F}
.

We set
t=bR
. Then
I_{+t}\subset\Omega
.
We assume that
b<d/2
and
B\geq 2
. Then

|\Lambda_{F}\cap I|\geq\frac{d}{3}\,BR=c_{0},

and, by Lemma

\max_{\bar{I}_{+t/2}}|f|\leq e^{-cR}\sup_{I_{+t}}|f|\,.

Thus,

|f(s)|<e^{-cR+o_{\varepsilon}(1)R}<e^{-c_{1}R},\qquad s\in\overline{I}_{+t/2},

provided that
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small. In particular this estimate holds on

, and therefore,

\Bigl|\frac{\omega_{n}}{\alpha^{n}}\Bigr|^{2}-\frac{1}{|\alpha|^{2n}}=f(n)<e^{-c_{1}R}\,.

Hence,

\displaystyle|\omega_{n}|

\displaystyle<\bigl(1+|\alpha|^{2n}e^{-c_{1}R}\bigr)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2}\,\max\bigl\{1,(|\alpha|e^{-c_{2}})^{n}\,\bigr\},\qquad n\in I,

completing this step.

Bounding

:
Now, we are ready to bound
|F(z)|
for

.
We have

|F(z)|\leq\Bigl(\sum_{0\leq n<B^{-1}R}+\sum_{B^{-1}R\leq n\leq BR}+\sum_{n>BR}\Bigr)\frac{|\omega_{n}|}{n!}\,R^{n}.

Since

has finite exponential type, we have

with

depending on the type of

(for instance, we can take

). Hence, assuming that

, i.e., that

, we have

The first term on the RHS is bounded by

provided that

is sufficiently small. Similarly, the third term on the RHS does not exceed

\Bigl(\frac{Qe}{B}\Bigr)^{BR}<1,

\log M_{F}(R)<\max\{1,|\alpha|e^{-c_{2}}\}R+O(1),

Completing the proof of Lemma

\displaystyle|\alpha|

\displaystyle<R^{-1}\,\log M_{F}(R)+o_{\varepsilon}(1)

\displaystyle<\max\{1,|\alpha|e^{-c_{2}}\}+o_{\varepsilon}(1)\,,

\frac{g_{h}(n)g_{h}(n+2)}{g_{h}(n+1)^{2}}=1+O(R^{-1}),

that is,

\frac{\omega_{n}\omega_{n+2}}{\omega_{n+1}^{2}}\cdot\frac{\bar{\omega}_{n+h}\bar{\omega}_{n+h+2}}{\bar{\omega}_{n+h+1}^{2}}=1+O(R^{-1}).

But, for
n\in[0,cR]
and
c_{1}R\leq h\leq(1-c_{1})R
,
c<c_{1}
,
we have

n+h,n+h+1,n+h+2\in[c_{1}R,R],

so

\Bigl|\frac{\bar{\omega}_{n+h}\bar{\omega}_{n+h+2}}{\bar{\omega}_{n+h+1}^{2}}\,\Bigr|=1+O(R^{-1})

and therefore,

\Bigl|\frac{\omega_{n}\omega_{n+2}}{\omega_{n+1}^{2}}\Bigr|=1+O(R^{-1}).

Letting
R\to\infty
along a sequence of values where (
21
) holds,
we get

\Bigl|\frac{\omega_{n}\omega_{n+2}}{\omega_{n+1}^{2}}\Bigr|=1

for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

Thus, the quotient
|\omega_{n}|/|\omega_{n+1}|
is constant for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, that is,

|\omega_{n}|=\beta\gamma^{n}
. We know that, for every
n\in\Lambda_{F}
,
|\omega_{n}|=1
,
so
\beta=\gamma=1
. This completes the first step of the proof.
\Box

2.7
Coup de grâce

Here, our strategy will be similar to the one used in the first step.
Since we already know that all the coefficients
\omega_{n}
are unimodular, we will freely
use that

g_{h}(n)=\omega_{n}\bar{\omega}_{n+h}=\frac{\omega_{n}}{\omega_{n+h}}\,.

First, we look at the interpolating function
g_{1}
. It takes unimodular values at the non-negative integers
and its absolute value is bounded by
e^{o_{\varepsilon}(1)R}
on a
cR
-neighbourhood of the interval
[cR,R]
. Hence, by Lemma
7
applied with

,

\frac{\omega_{n+1}^{3}\omega_{n+3}}{\omega_{n}\omega_{n+2}^{3}}=\frac{g_{1}(n+1)^{2}}{g_{1}(n)g_{1}(n+2)}=1+O(R^{-1})\,,\qquad n\in[cR,R]\,.

(22)

Then, we look at the function
g_{h}
with
h=[R]
. It also takes unimodular values at non-negative integers, and
its absolute value is
bounded by
e^{o_{\varepsilon}(1)R}
on a
cR
-neighbourhood of the interval
[0,cR]
. Applying again Lemma
7
with
t=cR
,
we deduce that

\frac{\omega_{n+1}^{3}\omega_{n+3}}{\omega_{n}\omega_{n+2}^{3}}\,:\,\frac{\omega_{n+h+1}^{3}\omega_{n+h+3}}{\omega_{n+h}\omega_{n+h+2}^{3}}=\frac{g_{h}(n+1)^{2}}{g_{h}(n)g_{h}(n+2)}\,:\,\frac{g_{h}(n+2)^{2}}{g_{h}(n+1)g_{h}(n+3)}\\
=1+O(R^{-1})\,,\qquad n\in[0,cR]\,.

But now
n+h\in[cR,R]
, so that, by (

),

\frac{\omega_{n+h+1}^{3}\omega_{n+h+3}}{\omega_{n+h}\omega_{n+h+2}^{3}}=1+O(R^{-1}),

and therefore,

\frac{\omega_{n+1}^{3}\omega_{n+3}}{\omega_{n}\omega_{n+2}^{3}}=1+O(R^{-1})\,,\qquad n\in[0,cR]\,.

Letting
R=R_{j}\uparrow\infty
,
we conclude that

\frac{\omega_{n+1}^{2}}{\omega_{n}\omega_{n+2}}\,:\,\frac{\omega_{n+2}^{2}}{\omega_{n+1}\omega_{n+3}}=\frac{\omega_{n+1}^{3}\omega_{n+3}}{\omega_{n}\omega_{n+2}^{3}}=1,\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\,,

which means that the double quotient

\frac{\omega_{n+2}}{\omega_{n+1}}\,:\,\frac{\omega_{n+1}}{\omega_{n}}

is constant, so

\omega_{n+1}=C^{n(n+1)/2}\,\frac{\omega_{1}}{\omega_{0}}\,.

Thus,
\omega_{n}=e^{2\pi{\rm i}(\beta n^{2}+\gamma n+\delta)}
for some

\beta,\gamma,\delta\in[0,1)
.

To complete the proof,
we claim that
\beta=0
or
\beta=\tfrac{1}{2}
. To show this, we consider
the interpolating function
g_{d}
with bounded

. Applying Lemma
7

on the interval
[cR,R]
, we see that

\frac{g_{d}(n+1)}{g_{d}(n)}=1+o_{\varepsilon}(1)+e^{-c_{1}R}\,,\qquad n\in[cR,R]\,.

(23)

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\frac{g_{d}(n+1)}{g_{d}(n)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{n+1}\omega_{n+d}}{\omega_{n}\omega_{n+d+1}}

\displaystyle=e^{2\pi{\rm i}\beta((n+1)^{2}+(n+d)^{2}-n^{2}-(n+d+1)^{2})}=e^{-2\pi{\rm i}(2\beta d)}\,.

If

is not an integer, we can find

such that

\frac{1}{4}<2\beta d<\frac{3}{4}\qquad(\text{mod\ }1),

which yields contradiction with (

)
when
R
is sufficiently large and
\varepsilon
is small enough.

At last, if
\beta=\tfrac{1}{2}
, we have

so we can let
\beta=0
adding
\tfrac{1}{2}
to
\gamma
. This finishes off the proof of the
theorem.
\Box

.
Furthermore,
\log|\pi(z)|\leq\eta r^{\mu}\cos\mu(\pi-|\theta|)+C
,
z\in\mathbb{C}
.
Letting
\eta\to 0
, we conclude that
\pi
is a constant function. Since it equals
1
at the origin,

\pi=1
identically. Thus, the zero set
\Lambda_{F}
is void.

\Box

4
Two examples

4.1
Sharpness of Theorem
2
(i)

Here, we will show that the entire function

F(z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\,(\cos\sqrt{n}+2)\,\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

can be written in the form
F(z)=e^{z}G(z)
, where
G
is an entire function of order
\tfrac{1}{2}
, and therefore,

n_{F}(R)=O(\sqrt{R})
as
R\to\infty
.

Let
\varphi(s)
be the Borel-Laplace transform of the entire function
\psi(w)=\cos\sqrt{w}+2
, i.e.,

\varphi(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}(\cos\sqrt{u}+2)e^{-su}\,{\rm d}u.

Since
\psi
has zero exponential type, the function
\varphi
can be analytically continued to
the closed complex plane punctured at the origin,
vanishes at
\infty
, and, by the inversion formula, for each
\rho>0
,

\cos\sqrt{w}+2=\frac{1}{2\pi{\rm i}}\,\oint_{|s|=\rho}\varphi(s)e^{sw}\,{\rm d}s.

Thus,

\displaystyle F(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{2\pi{\rm i}}\oint_{|s|=\rho}\frac{e^{ns}z^{n}}{n!}\,\varphi(s)\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi{\rm i}}\oint_{|s|=\rho}e^{ze^{s}}\,\varphi(s)\,{\rm d}s,

and therefore,

G(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi{\rm i}}\oint_{|s|=\rho}e^{z(e^{s}-1)}\,\varphi(s)\,{\rm d}s.

To estimate the function
G
, first, we estimate the function
\varphi
and then will optimize the value of the parameter
\rho
.
We claim that

|\varphi(s)|=O(1)e^{C/|s|}\,.

Indeed, rotating the integration line, we get

\displaystyle|\varphi(s)|=

\displaystyle\Big|\,\int_{0}^{\infty\bar{s}}(\cos\sqrt{w}+2)e^{-ws}\,{\rm d}w\,\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\sqrt{t}-t|s|}\,{\rm d}t.

The only critical point of the function
t\mapsto\sqrt{t}-t|s|
is
t^{*}=(4|s|^{2})^{-1}
, which is a non-degenerate maximum, and
\sqrt{t^{*}}-t^{*}|s|=(4|s|)^{-1}
. Hence, by a standard Laplace asymptotic estimate,

|\varphi(s)|\leq O(1)\,e^{C/|s|}
, proving the claim.

Now,

\displaystyle|G(z)|

\displaystyle\leq O(1)\,\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{|z|(e^{\rho e^{{\rm i}\theta}}+C\rho^{-1})}\,\rho{\rm d}\theta

\displaystyle\leq O(\rho)e^{C(|z|\rho+\rho^{-1})}\,.

Choosing
\rho=1/\sqrt{|z|}
, we get
|G(z)|\leq O\bigl(e^{C|z|^{1/2}}\bigr)
,
as was planned.
\Box

4.2
A subharmonic example

For a subharmonic function
u
in a planar domain, we denote by
\mu_{u}
its
Riesz measure
,
that is,
\mu_{u}=(2\pi)^{-1}\Delta u
, where the Laplacian is understood in the sense of distributions.

Proposition 16
.

There exists a subharmonic function
u\colon R\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{R}
with

1.

u(r)=r\geq\max_{\theta}u(re^{{\rm i}\theta})-5
for
0\leq r\leq R
,

2.

\mu_{u}(\mathbb{D})\simeq 1
,

3.

\mu_{u}(R\mathbb{D})\ll\sqrt{R}
for large
R
.

This shows that the method of proof of Theorem
2
(ii) cannot work with a
weaker assumption
n_{F}(R_{j})\ll\sqrt{R_{j}}
for a sequence
R_{j}\to\infty
because the above properties
are the only ones used in the proof. On the other hand, we have no idea how to extend
u
to a subharmonic
function of linear growth in the whole plane
\mathbb{C}
so that this picture repeats infinitely many times.

Construction of the function
u
: Take
\alpha>0
and consider the function

f(z)=e^{-\alpha z^{2}}
in the semi-disk
\mathbb{D}_{+}=\mathbb{D}\cap\{{\rm Im}(z)\geq 0\}
.

Claim 17
.

|{\rm Im}f(z)|<5e^{2\alpha}y^{2}+2\alpha|x|e^{-\frac{1}{2}\alpha x^{2}}y
.

Proof
: Indeed,

|{\rm Im}f(z)|\leq e^{\alpha(y^{2}-x^{2})}|\sin(2\alpha xy)|\leq e^{\alpha(y^{2}-x^{2})}2\alpha|xy|
.
For
|x|\leq 2y
, the RHS does not exceed

\alpha e^{\alpha(y^{2}-x^{2})}(x^{2}+y^{2})\leq 5\alpha e^{\alpha}y^{2}<5e^{2\alpha}y^{2},

while,
for
|x|\geq 2y
, the RHS does not exceed
2\alpha|x|e^{-\frac{3}{4}\alpha x^{2}}y
.

\Box

Let
F(z)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{z}f(t)\,{\rm d}t
.

Claim 18
.

{\rm Im\,}F(x+{\rm i}y)<10e^{2\alpha}y^{2}+e^{-2\alpha}
.

Proof
: We have

F({\rm i}y)={\rm i}\int_{0}^{y}e^{\alpha t^{2}}\,{\rm d}t.

Then,

{\rm Im\,}F({\rm i}y)=\int_{0}^{y}e^{\alpha t^{2}}\,{\rm d}t<\int_{0}^{y}(1+e^{\alpha}t^{2})\,{\rm d}t<y+e^{\alpha}y^{2}\,,

and therefore,

\displaystyle{\rm Im\,}F(x+{\rm i}y)

\displaystyle={\rm Im\,}F({\rm i}y)+\int_{0}^{x}{\rm Im\,}f(t+{\rm i}y)\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle<y+e^{\alpha}y^{2}+5e^{2\alpha}y^{2}+2\alpha y\int_{0}^{\infty}xe^{-\frac{1}{2}\alpha x^{2}}\,{\rm d}x\qquad\qquad\qquad({\rm by\ Claim~\ref{claimA}})

\displaystyle=y+e^{\alpha}y^{2}+5e^{2\alpha}y^{2}+2y<3y+6e^{2\alpha}y^{2}<10e^{2\alpha}y^{2}+e^{-2\alpha}

(at the very end we used that
y<e^{2\alpha}y^{2}+e^{-2\alpha}
).

\Box

Now, we put

u(z)=\begin{cases}{\rm Re\,}z+\sqrt{R}\,{\rm Im\,}F(\sqrt{z/R}),&z\in R\mathbb{D}_{+},\\
u(\bar{z}),&\bar{z}\in R\mathbb{D}_{+}.\end{cases}

The function
u
is continuous in
R\mathbb{D}
and harmonic in
\{z\in R\mathbb{D}\colon{\rm Im\,}z>0\}

as well as in
\{z\in R\mathbb{D}\colon{\rm Im\,}z<0\}
. Moreover,
\partial_{y}F(x+{\rm i}0)={\rm i}f(x)
, so

\partial_{y}{\rm Im\,}F(x+{\rm i}0)=f(x)>0
, and therefore, the function
u
is subharmonic in
R\mathbb{D}
.
Its Riesz measure
\mu_{u}
is supported by
[0,R]
and has the linear density
2\partial_{y}u(x+{\rm i}0)=2f(x)
.

Clearly,
u(r)=r
for
0\leq r\leq R
. Let
z=re^{{\rm i}\theta}
,
0\leq\theta\leq 2\pi
. Then

\displaystyle u(re^{{\rm i}\theta})

\displaystyle=r\cos\theta+\sqrt{R}\,{\rm Im\,}F\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{r}{R}}\,e^{{\rm i}\theta/2}\Bigr)

\displaystyle<r-2r\sin^{2}\frac{\theta}{2}+\sqrt{R}\,\Bigl[10e^{2\alpha}\sqrt{\frac{r}{R}}\,\sin^{2}\frac{\theta}{2}+e^{-2\alpha}\Bigr]\qquad\qquad({\rm by\ Claim}~\ref{claimB}).

Choosing
\alpha
so that
5e^{2\alpha}=\sqrt{R}
, we get

u(re^{{\rm i}\theta})\leq r+5
for all
\theta\in[0,2\pi]
.

It remains to estimate the Riesz measure
\mu_{u}
.
We have

\displaystyle\mu_{u}(R\bar{\mathbb{D}})=2\int_{0}^{R}\partial_{y}u(x+{\rm i}0)\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{R}\sqrt{R}\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}x}F\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{x}{R}}\,\Bigr)\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=2\sqrt{R}F(1)=2\sqrt{R}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-\alpha x^{2}}\,{\rm d}x\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\sqrt{R}\simeq\sqrt{\frac{R}{\log R}}

and

\displaystyle\mu_{u}(\bar{\mathbb{D}})=2\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{y}u(x+{\rm i}0)\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{R}\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}x}F\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{x}{R}}\,\Bigr)\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=2\sqrt{R}F\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{1}{R}}\,\Bigr)=2\sqrt{R}\int_{0}^{1/\sqrt{R}}e^{-\alpha x^{2}}\,{\rm d}x\to 2,\ {\rm as\ }R\to\infty.

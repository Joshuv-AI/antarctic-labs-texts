A novel intervention strategy for bladder cancer targeting dual metabolic pathways—Unraveling the pleiotropic mechanisms of quercetin
Authors: Elaine Lai-Han Leung
Published: 2026

In cancer therapeutics, tumor cells often evade single-target pharmacological interventions through their remarkable metabolic plasticity. This phenomenon is particularly pronounced in highly heterogeneous malignancies such as bladder cancer (BCa). To address this therapeutic bottleneck, a systematic biological paradigm for multi-target intervention utilizing natural products is presented in this study. Departing from the conventional paradigm of single kinase inhibition, it was innovatively revealed that quercetin, a natural flavonoid, functions as a multidimensional metabolic reprogramming agent. Specifically, a precise dual blockade is imposed on the l-serine metabolic network of BCa. This restriction is achieved by synergistically remodeling the host gut microbiota (exogenous deprivation) alongside the downregulation of intratumoral phosphoserine phosphatase (PSPH) expression (endogenous blockade). Consequently, the critical clinical dilemma wherein solid tumors readily activate compensatory survival pathways under metabolic stress is profoundly addressed by these findings.

Rapidly proliferating cancer cells exhibit a profound addiction to non-essential amino acids, such as serine. This dependency arises because serine plays an irreplaceable role in supporting one-carbon metabolism, driving purine biosynthesis, and maintaining redox homeostasis1. However, exogenous serine supply via dietary restriction is frequently shown unsatisfactory. Such interventions typically trigger the activation of the de novo serine synthesis pathway (SSP) within tumors and may even induce P53-dependent metabolic remodeling2. This metabolic paradox was successfully addressed in the study by Zhai et al.3 It was demonstrated that orally administered quercetin, which accumulates in the colon, effectively reverses the pathological dysbiosis of the host gut microbiota. Consequently, α-diversity is significantly reduced, accompanied by the enrichment of beneficial microbial taxa, including Blautia and Parabacteroides sp. More importantly, untargeted metabolomic profiling identified l-serine as the most pivotal differential metabolite. Its concentration was shown to exhibit a robust positive correlation with tumor burden. It was further confirmed that quercetin specifically enriches targeted bacterial strains, prominently represented by Escherichia coli Nissle 1917 (EcN). This enrichment accelerates the degradation of exogenous l-serine within the intestinal tract, which functions as a primary metabolic bioreactor. Notably, owing to its inherent tumor-homing properties, EcN has aroused attention in recent years as a highly promising candidate for live biotherapeutics4. By directly oral administering viable EcN, the anti-cancer phenotype was successfully exhibited in this study. Thus, a microbiota-driven pathway for exogenous nutrient deprivation was robustly established.

To terminate the evasion of cancer cells via compensatory pathways, a second pharmacological mechanism of quercetin was also demonstrated: the precise intracellular targeting of a critical enzyme within the de novo synthesis pathway. Accumulating evidence indicates that the aberrant overexpression of phosphoserine phosphatase (PSPH) profoundly facilitates the proliferation and metastasis of bladder cancer and other malignancies5. Through RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) and functional validations, it was demonstrated that PSPH expression in tumor tissues is specifically downregulated by quercetin. Previously, the mechanistic understanding of bladder cancer inhibition by quercetin was largely confined to the activation of upstream kinase signaling cascades, such as AMPK6. However, a more fundamental elucidation is provided by this study from the perspective of substrate depletion. Specifically, the downregulation of PSPH completely severs serine biosynthesis. Consequently, a rapid reduction in the global nucleotide pool, including ATP and GTP, is induced, which ultimately triggers irreversible cell cycle arrest and apoptosis.

From a macroscopic immunometabolic perspective, this multidimensional metabolic intervention possesses profound clinical significance. Although a strictly serine-deprived diet may demonstrate initial efficacy, it frequently drives residual cancer cells to acquire immune evasion capabilities. This evasion is mediated by lactate accumulation, which subsequently drives the lactylation of the programmed cell death 1 ligand 1 (PD-L1) protein7. The dual strategy of exogenous depletion and endogenous inhibition exerted by quercetin circumvents this extreme biochemical trap. Consequently, the profound redundancy of the tumoral metabolic network is dismantled, alongside with exceptional systemic tolerability and overcoming hepatorenal toxicity. Collectively, the multi-omics mechanisms underlying the anti-cancer efficacy of natural products in vivo are groundbreakingly elucidated in this study. Furthermore, quercetin is proven to be a valuable biological constituent for investigating the host–microbiota–amino acid metabolism axis. Should its synergistic efficacy with immune checkpoint inhibitors be further validated in immunocompetent models, a highly promising translational blueprint for a precise, metabolism-targeted therapy strategy of bladder cancer will be provided.

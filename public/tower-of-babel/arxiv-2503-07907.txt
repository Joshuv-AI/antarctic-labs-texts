Co-Optimizing Distributed Energy Resources under Demand Charges and Bi-Directional Power Flow Thanks: * Equal contribution.
Ruixiao Yang * , Gulai Shen * , Ahmed S. Alahmed , Chuchu Fan

Abstract
We address the co-optimization of behind-the-meter (BTM) distributed energy resources (DER), including flexible demands, renewable distributed generation (DG), and battery energy storage systems (BESS) under net energy metering (NEM) frameworks with demand charges. We formulate the problem as a stochastic dynamic program that accounts for renewable generation uncertainty and operational surplus maximization. Our theoretical analysis reveals that the optimal policy follows a threshold structure. Finally, we show that even a simple algorithm leveraging this threshold structure performs well in simulation, emphasizing its importance in developing near-optimal algorithms. These findings provide crucial insights for implementing prosumer energy management systems under complex tariff structures.

II
Problem Formulation

Fig. 1:
Distributed energy resource setup: flexible demands
d\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, renewable DG
g\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, storage operation
e\in\mathbb{R}
, and net-consumption
z\in\mathbb{R}
.

We study an energy management system (EMS) for prosumers that co-optimizes flexible demand, storage operation, and renewable distributed generation under a NEM tariff with distinct buy/sell rates and peak demand charges. The optimization objective maximizes the net benefit defined as consumption utility minus electricity costs, where demand charges are levied on the maximum consumption drawn from either load, battery charging, or both. As shown in Fig.
1
, this dynamic programming formulation explicitly accounts for the temporal coupling of storage operations and an NEM tariff with bidirectional energy pricing and peak demand charges.

II-A

Prosumer Resources

We consider the operation over a discrete and finite time horizon
t=0,1,\ldots,T
. As shown in Fig.
1
, the prosumer has
BTM renewable DG
, denoted by
g_{t}
. The renewable DG is modeled as an exogenous (positive) Markov random process.

The
flexible demands
are denoted by the vector

\bm{d}_{t}=(d_{t}^{1},\ldots,d_{t}^{K})\in\mathcal{D}\coloneqq\{\bm{d}:\bm{0}\preceq\bm{d}\preceq\overline{\bm{d}}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{K}_{+},

(1)

where
K
is the total number of prosumer devices. Here
\overline{\bm{d}}
is the consumption bundle’s upper limit.

The prosumer also owns a BTM battery energy storage, which has output
e_{t}\in[-\underline{e},\overline{e}]
, where
\underline{e}
and
\overline{e}
are discharging and charging limits. The output is decomposed into charging
[e_{t}]^{+}=\max\{e_{t},0\}
and discharging
[e_{t}]^{-}=\max\{-e_{t},0\}
. With charge/discharge efficiencies
\tau,\rho\in[0,1]
, the state of charge (SoC)
s_{t}\in[0,B]
evolves as:

s_{t+1}=s_{t}+\tau[e_{t}]^{+}-[e_{t}]^{-}/\rho,\quad t=0,\ldots,T-1,

(2)

where
B
denotes battery capacity.

The
net energy consumption
of the prosumer at time
t
is

z_{t}:=\bm{1}^{\top}\bm{d}_{t}+e_{t}-g_{t}.

(3)

Therefore, the prosumer imports power from the grid if
z_{t}>0
and exports power to the grid if
z_{t}<0
.

II-B

System Dynamics

The
system state
at time
t
,
x_{t}
, is described as

x_{t}=[s_{t},g_{t},c_{t}],

(4)

where
c_{t}
is the prosumer’s peak demand before time
t
. We allow the system to be controlled by battery charging/discharging and consumption adjustments, i.e., the control action is

u_{t}\coloneqq(e_{t},\bm{d}_{t}).

(5)

The peak demand in the state evolves as

c_{t+1}=\max_{0\leq i\leq t}z_{i}=\max\{z_{t},c_{t}\},\quad t=0,1,\ldots,T-1,

(6)

and the battery SoC evolves as per (
2
).

II-C

NEM X Tariff with Demand Charges

Under NEM, when the prosumer’s gross loads (from consumption and storage charging) are higher than its generation (from renewable DG), the prosumer pays at the retail rate. Otherwise, the prosumer gets compensated at the export rate.

Let
(p_{t}^{+}\geq p_{t}^{-},p)\geq 0
be the retail, export, and peak demand prices, respectively. We equivalently break down the peak demand charge at the end of time
T
to each time step
t
since
pc_{T}=p\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}[z_{t}-c_{t}]^{+}
. Thus, we can write the payment (cost) under NEM X in
[
2
]
for each time
t
as

P_{t}(z_{t},c_{t})=p_{t}^{+}[z_{t}]^{+}-p_{t}^{-}[z_{t}]^{-}+p[z_{t}-c_{t}]^{+},

(7)

where
P_{t}(\cdot)>0
(
P_{t}(\cdot)<0
) signifies a payment by the prosumer (DU) to the DU (prosumer).

II-D

Flexible Demands and Storage Co-Optimization

At each time step
t
, the prosumer’s utility of consuming
\bm{d}_{t}
is given by an additive utility function

U_{t}(\bm{d}_{t})=\sum_{i=1}^{K}U_{t}^{i}(d_{t}^{i}),\quad t=0,1,\ldots,T-1

(8)

that is assumed to be concave and continuously differentiable.

We define the reward function of the dynamic program as the difference between satisfaction from consumption (
8
) and payment (
7
). Therefore, the reward function is

r_{t}(x_{t},u_{t})=U_{t}(\bm{d}_{t})-P_{t}(z_{t},c_{t}),\quad t=0,\ldots,T-1.

(9)

To encourage the preservation of energy in the battery, we define a linear reward to the remaining energy at step
T
,
i.e.

r_{T}(x_{T})=\gamma s_{T},

(10)

for a
\gamma>0
. The salvage value rate
\gamma
influences optimal DER scheduling. Here, we assume
p^{+}_{t}\geq\gamma\geq p^{-}_{t},\forall t
, deferring analysis of
\gamma
’s impact to future work.

The policy
\pi=(\pi_{0},\ldots,\pi_{T-1})
is a sequence of mapping from state space to action space such that
\pi_{t}(x_{t})=u_{t}
. Given initial state
x_{0}=(s,g,0)
, the storage-consumption co-optimization is then defined as:

\displaystyle\max_{\pi}\quad

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{T}r_{t}(x_{t},u_{t})\right]

(11a)

Subject to

\displaystyle\quad\text{for all}\ t=0,1,\cdots,T-1

\displaystyle x_{0}=(s,g,0)

(11b)

\displaystyle u_{t}=(e_{t},\bm{d}_{t})=\pi_{t}(x_{t})

(11c)

\displaystyle e_{t}\in[-\underline{e},\overline{e}]

(11d)

\displaystyle s_{t+1}=s_{t}+\tau[e_{t}]^{+}-[e_{t}]^{-}/\rho

(11e)

\displaystyle 0\leq s_{t}\leq B

(11f)

\displaystyle g_{t+1}\sim F_{\cdot|g_{t}}

(11g)

\displaystyle c_{t+1}=\max\{\bm{1}^{\top}\bm{d}_{t}+e_{t}-g_{t},c_{t}\}

(11h)

\displaystyle\bm{0}\preceq\bm{d}_{t}\preceq\bm{\overline{d}}.

(11i)

Note: (
11e
) and (
11h
) introduce strong temporal dependencies.

The value function
V_{t}^{\pi}(\cdot)
is defined as the total reward received under the policy
\pi
, i.e.,
V_{t}^{\pi}(x)=\mathbb{E}[\sum_{t=0}^{T}r_{t}(x_{t},\pi(x_{t}))|x_{t}=x]
. The optimal value function is defined as
V_{t}^{*}(x)=\max_{\pi}V_{t}^{\pi}(x)
.

III
Dynamic Program Analytical Results

In this section, we show that the optimal policy for the co-optimization problem in
Sec. II
is a threshold policy.

The following lemma establishes a conclusion regarding the concavity of the optimal value function.

Lemma 1
.

The optimal value function
V^{*}_{t}(s_{t},g_{t},c_{t})
is concave in
(s_{t},c_{t})
for every time step
t\leq T
.

Proof.

We follow the proof technique for lemma 1 in
[
3
]
.

Consider two different states
x_{\tau}
and
x_{\tau}^{\prime}
with their corresponding optimal trajectories
\{(x_{t},u_{t})\}_{t=\tau}^{T}
and
\{(x_{t}^{\prime},u_{t}^{\prime})\}_{t=\tau}^{T}
. Let
\tilde{x}_{\tau}=(x_{\tau}+x_{\tau}^{\prime})/2
be their average initial state, and define the average policy
\tilde{\pi}=\{\tilde{u}_{t}=(u_{t}+u_{t}^{\prime})/2\}_{t=\tau}^{T}
with corresponding trajectory
\{\tilde{x}_{t}\}_{t=\tau}^{T}
.

First, by the concavity of
U_{t}
:

(U_{t}(\bm{d}_{t})+U_{t}(\bm{d}_{t}^{\prime}))/2\leq U_{t}((\bm{d}_{t}+\bm{d}_{t}^{\prime})/{2})=U_{t}(\tilde{\bm{d}}_{t}).

(12)

The cost
P_{t}(z_{t},c_{t})
consists of two components: the energy cost
p_{t}^{+}[z_{t}]^{+}-p_{t}^{-}[z_{t}]^{-}
and the demand charging
p[z_{t}-c_{t}]^{+}
. Since
p_{t}^{+}\geq p_{t}^{-}\geq 0
, the energy cost is convex. Then,

\displaystyle-\sum_{t=\tau}^{T}(p_{t}^{+}[z_{t}]^{+}-p_{t}^{-}[z_{t}]^{-}+p_{t}^{+}[z_{t}^{\prime}]^{+}-p_{t}^{-}[z_{t}^{\prime}]^{-})/2

(13)

\displaystyle\leq-\sum_{t=\tau}^{T}p_{t}^{+}[(z_{t}+z_{t}^{\prime})/2]^{+}-p_{t}^{-}[(z_{t}+z_{t}^{\prime})/2]^{-}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{t=\tau}^{T}p_{t}^{+}[\tilde{z}_{t}]^{+}-p_{t}^{-}[\tilde{z}_{t}]^{-}.

And the demand charging cost

\displaystyle-\sum_{t=\tau}^{T}(p[z_{t}-c_{t}]^{+}+p[z_{t}^{\prime}-c_{t}^{\prime}]^{+})/2

(14)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-p(\max_{\tau\leq t\leq T}\{z_{t}\}-c_{\tau}+\max_{\tau\leq t\leq T}\{z_{t}^{\prime}\}-c_{\tau}^{\prime})/2

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-p(\max_{\tau\leq t\leq T}\{(z_{t}+z_{t}^{\prime})/2\}-(c_{\tau}+c_{\tau}^{\prime})/2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-p(\max_{\tau\leq t\leq T}\{\tilde{z}_{t}\}-\tilde{c}_{\tau})=\sum_{t=\tau}^{T}p[\tilde{z}_{t}-\tilde{c}_{t}]^{+}.

Combining (
12
), (
13
), (
14
),

V^{*}_{\tau}(\tilde{x}_{\tau})\geq V_{\tau}^{\tilde{\pi}}(\tilde{x}_{\tau})\geq(V_{\tau}^{*}(x_{\tau})+V_{\tau}^{*}(x_{\tau}^{\prime}))/2,

(15)

which proves the concavity of the optimal value function.
∎

It turns out that the optimal value function is also non-decreasing in the system state.

Lemma 2
.

The optimal value function
V_{t}^{*}(s_{t},g_{t},c_{t})
is non-decreasing in
s_{t}
,
g_{t}
, and
c_{t}
for every time step
t\leq T
.

Proof.

(1)
s_{t}
: Consider two SoCs
s_{t}
,
s_{t}^{\prime}
such that
s_{t}=s_{t}^{\prime}+\epsilon
,
0<\epsilon\ll\underline{e}
. Let
\{u_{\tau}^{\prime}=(e_{\tau}^{\prime},\bm{d}_{\tau}^{\prime})\}_{\tau=t}^{T}
be the optimal action sequence for SoC
s_{t}^{\prime}
. If there exists
t\leq\tau_{0}<T
,
e_{\tau_{0}}^{\prime}\geq-\underline{e}+\epsilon
, action sequence
\{u_{\tau}=(e_{\tau}^{\prime}-\epsilon\cdot\mathbf{1}[\tau=\tau_{0}],\bm{d}_{\tau}^{\prime})\}_{\tau=t}^{T}
is feasible for SoC
s_{t}
. Hence
V^{*}_{t}(s_{t},g_{t},c_{t})-V^{*}_{t}(s_{t}^{\prime},g_{t},c_{t})\geq P_{\tau_{0}}(z_{\tau_{0}}^{\prime},c_{\tau_{0}}^{\prime})-P_{\tau_{0}}(z_{\tau_{0}}^{\prime}-\epsilon,c_{\tau_{0}}^{\prime})\geq 0
. Otherwise,
e_{\tau}^{\prime}<-\underline{e}+\epsilon<0
,
t\leq\tau\leq T
, so action sequence
\{u_{\tau}^{\prime}\}_{\tau=t}^{T}
is feasible for SoC
s_{t}
,
V^{*}_{t}(s_{t},g_{t},c_{t})-V^{*}_{t}(s_{t}^{\prime},g_{t},c_{t})\geq\gamma(s_{T}-s_{T}^{\prime})=\gamma\epsilon>0
.

(2)
g_{t}
: Consider two generations
g_{t}=g_{t}^{\prime}+\epsilon
,
\epsilon>0
. Let
\pi^{\prime}=\{u_{\tau}^{\prime}=(e_{\tau}^{\prime},\bm{d}_{\tau}^{\prime})\}_{\tau=t}^{T}
be the optimal action sequence for generation
g_{t}^{\prime}
. Apply the action sequence to generation
g_{t}
,
z_{t+1}=e_{t}^{\prime}+\mathbf{1}^{\top}\bm{d}_{t}^{\prime}-g_{t}=z_{t+1}^{\prime}-\epsilon
,
V_{t}^{*}(s_{t},g_{t},c_{t})-V_{t}^{*}(s_{t},g_{t}^{\prime},c_{t})\geq P_{t}(z_{t}^{\prime},c_{t})-P_{t}(z_{t}^{\prime}-\epsilon,c_{t})\geq 0
.

(3)
c_{t}
: From (
7
),
P_{t}(z_{t},c_{t})
is non-increasing in
c_{t}
, hence
V_{t}^{*}(s_{t},g_{t},c_{t})
is non-decreasing in
c_{t}
.
∎

Lastly, we derive a property that links the optimal Q-function, defined as
Q_{t}^{*}(x_{t},u_{t})=r(x_{t},u_{t})+\mathbb{E}[V_{t+1}^{*}(x_{t+1})]
, to the system’s control action
u_{t}
.

Lemma 3
.

The optimal Q-function
Q_{t}^{*}(x_{t},u_{t})
is concave in
u_{t}
.

Proof.

For
u_{\tau}\neq u_{\tau}^{\prime}
, let
\{(x_{t},u_{t})\}_{t=\tau}^{T}
be the trajectory of
Q_{\tau}^{*}(x_{\tau},u_{\tau})
and
\{(x_{t}^{\prime},u_{t}^{\prime})\}_{t=\tau}^{T}
be the trajectory of
Q_{\tau}^{*}(x_{\tau},u_{\tau}^{\prime})
. By (
12
) - (
14
), applying actions
\{(u_{t}+u_{t}^{\prime})/2\}_{t=\tau}^{T}
to
x_{\tau}
gives:

\displaystyle Q_{\tau}^{*}(x_{\tau},u_{\tau})+Q_{\tau}^{*}(x_{\tau},u_{\tau}^{\prime})

(16)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=\tau}^{T}r_{t}(x_{t},(u_{t}+u_{t}^{\prime})/2)|x_{\tau}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Q_{\tau}^{*}(x_{\tau},(u_{\tau}+u_{\tau}^{\prime})/2).

Therefore,
Q_{t}^{*}(x_{t},u_{t})
is concave in
u_{t}
.
∎

Lem. 1
to
Lem. 3
lead to
Thm. 1
about the structure of the optimal co-optimization policy under demand charges.

Theorem 1
.

For each step
t=0,1,\cdots,T-1
, the optimal policy is a threshold policy.

Proof.

The feasible region for action
u_{t}
,
(e_{t},\bm{d}_{t})\in\mathcal{F}_{t}=[\max\{-\underline{e},-s_{t}\},\min\{\overline{e},B-s_{t}\}]\times\prod_{i=1}^{K}[0,d_{i}]
is a compact set. From
Lem. 1
to
Lem. 3
, the optimal value function and Q-function is concave in action
u_{t}
, so the optimal action

u_{t}^{*}=\arg\max_{u\in\mathcal{F}_{t}}r_{t}(x_{t},u_{t})+Q_{t}^{*}(x_{t},u_{t})

(17)

must exist in the interior of the feasible set
\mathcal{F}_{t}
or at the boundary of the feasible set
\mathcal{F}_{t}
. The optimal action can be obtained by solving concave optimizations with and without boundary constraints, which results in a threshold policy.
∎

Although
Thm. 1
reveals a structured policy, direct dynamic programming implementation remains impractical. First, optimization necessitates gradients of
r_{t}
and
Q_{t}
, which are computationally prohibitive for complex utilities. Also, continuous-space Q-function tracking requires costly discretization, as validated in simpler settings
[
3
]
. We thus employ reinforcement learning (RL) to approximate the optimal policy efficiently.

Threshold Algorithm

Based on the optimal structure, we developed a simple threshold algorithm as follows:

e_{t}=\begin{cases}\max\{-\underline{e},\rho s_{t},-(\bar{\bm{d}}_{t}-g_{t})\},&\bar{\bm{d}}_{t}-g_{t}>0\\

and

Formulate the battery-demand co-optimization as a deterministic nonlinear program solved with Gurobi. It provides the optimal value for comparison.

V
Conclusion

In conclusion, we presented a comprehensive investigation into the co-optimization of DERs in a realistic setting controlling both demands and energy storage under a bi-directional net metering pricing structure, including demand charge. We proposed a stochastic dynamic program to model the problem and proved the optimality of the threshold structure. Through simulations with real-world data, we demonstrated that the RL algorithm can achieve significant surplus gains compared to baseline approaches, highlighting the effectiveness of using RL to approximate optimal control for prosumer EMS.

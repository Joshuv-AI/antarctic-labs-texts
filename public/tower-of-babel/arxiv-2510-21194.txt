Shaoheng Zhang

. 1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study the stationary incompressible magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) equations

(1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta\mathbf{u}+(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}-(\mathbf{B}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{B}+\nabla p=0,\\
-\Delta\mathbf{B}+(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{B}-(\mathbf{B}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}=0,\\
{\rm div\,}\mathbf{u}={\rm div\,}\mathbf{B}=0,\end{cases}

in
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Here,
\mathbf{u}
is the velocity field of the fluid,
\mathbf{B}
is the magnetic field, and
p
is the scalar pressure. The MHD system describes the motion of electrically conducting fluids such as plasmas and couples the Navier-Stokes equations with Maxwell’s equations.

The MHD equations (
1
) are invariant under the following scaling:

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}(x)

\displaystyle\to\mathbf{u}_{\lambda}(x)=\lambda\mathbf{u}(\lambda x),

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}(x)

\displaystyle\to\mathbf{B}_{\lambda}(x)=\lambda\mathbf{B}(\lambda x),

\displaystyle p(x)

\displaystyle\to p_{\lambda}(x)=\lambda^{2}p(\lambda x),

where
\lambda>0
.
The solution
(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{B},p)
is called
self-similar
if

\mathbf{u}(x)=\mathbf{u}_{\lambda}(x),\quad\mathbf{B}(x)=\mathbf{B}_{\lambda}(x),\quad p(x)=p_{\lambda}(x)

for any
\lambda>0
. In this paper, we aim to characterize the self-similar solutions to (
1
) under the assumption of axisymmetry.

When
\mathbf{B}\equiv 0
, the MHD equations (
1
) reduce to the Navier-Stokes equations.
The well-known self-similar solutions to the Navier-Stokes equations in
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
are the Landau solutions (see
[
Lan44
]
).
The explicit formula for Landau solutions can be
found in many textbooks (see, e.g., Section 8.2 in
[
Tsa18
]
).
Tian and Xin
[
TX98
]
, Cannone and Karch
[
CK04
]
proved that a self-similar solution to the Navier-Stokes equations in
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
is a Landau solution if the solution is axisymmetric.
Šverák
[
Š11
]
further proved the assertion without the assumption of axisymmetry.
Kang, Miura and Tsai
[
KMT18
]
proved the triviality of axisymmetric self-similar solutions to the Navier-Stokes equations in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}
with the no-slip boundary condition or the Navier slip boundary condition.

Regarding the self-similar solutions to the stationary MHD equations, fewer results are known. Zhang, Wang, and Wang
[
ZWW25
]
investigated the smooth axisymmetric self-similar solutions
(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{B})
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
with
\mathbf{B}=B^{\theta}(r,z)\mathbf{e}_{\theta}
. They proved that
\mathbf{u}
is a Landau solution and
\mathbf{B}
vanishes, provided that
|\mathbf{u}(x)|\leq\varepsilon|x|^{-1}
for some sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
.
For the stationary MHD equations without magnetic diffusion, Zhang
[
Zha25
]
showed that if
(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{B})
is an axisymmetric self-similar solution and
\mathbf{B}
has only swirl component, i.e.,
\mathbf{B}=B^{\theta}(r,z)\mathbf{e}_{\theta}
, then
\mathbf{u}
must be a Landau solution and
\mathbf{B}\equiv 0
.

If
\mathbf{u}
is a Landau solution and
\mathbf{B}=0
, then
(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{B})
is an axisymmetric self-similar solution to (
1
) in
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
.
Our first result shows that the converse statement is also true if
\mathbf{B}
has only the swirl component and
u^{r}
is bounded above by
\frac{1}{3r}+\frac{2r}{3}
on the unit sphere. More precisely, we have the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{B})
be a smooth axisymmetric self-similar solution to (
1
) in
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
.
Assume that
\mathbf{u}=u^{r}(r,z)\mathbf{e}_{r}+u^{\theta}(r,z)\mathbf{e}_{\theta}+u^{z}(r,z)\mathbf{e}_{z}
and
\mathbf{B}=B^{\theta}(r,z)\mathbf{e}_{\theta}
, where
(r,\theta,z)
are the cylindrical coordinates defined in Section
2.1
.
If
u^{r}(r,z)<\frac{1}{3r}+\frac{2r}{3}
on the unit sphere
\partial B_{1}
, then
\mathbf{u}
is a Landau solution and
\mathbf{B}\equiv 0
.

As an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.1
, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 1.1
.

Let
(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{B})
be a smooth axisymmetric self-similar solution to (
1
) in
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
.
Assume that
\mathbf{u}=u^{r}(r,z)\mathbf{e}_{r}+u^{\theta}(r,z)\mathbf{e}_{\theta}+u^{z}(r,z)\mathbf{e}_{z}
and
\mathbf{B}=B^{\theta}(r,z)\mathbf{e}_{\theta}
, where
(r,\theta,z)
are the cylindrical coordinates defined in Section
2.1
.
If
u^{r}(x)\leq\alpha
on
\partial B_{1}
with
\alpha<\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}
, then
\mathbf{u}
is a Landau solution and
\mathbf{B}\equiv 0
.

Remark 1.1
.

Let
(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{B})
be a smooth axisymmetric self-similar solution to (
1
) in
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
with
\mathbf{B}=B^{\theta}(r,z)\mathbf{e}_{\theta}
. It was proved in
[
ZWW25
]
that if
|\mathbf{u}(x)|\leq\varepsilon
on
\partial B_{1}
with sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
, then
\mathbf{u}
is a Landau solution and
\mathbf{B}\equiv 0
.
Corollary
1.1
provides an explicit upper bound for
u^{r}
, and the condition
u^{r}(x)\leq\alpha
is strictly weaker than the condition
|\mathbf{u}(x)|\leq\varepsilon
assumed in
[
ZWW25
]
.

Our second result is the following theorem, which proves the triviality of the axisymmetric self-similar solutions in the half-space
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}
with the no-slip boundary condition or the Navier slip boundary condition.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{B})
be a smooth axisymmetric self-similar solution to (
1
) in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}
, satisfying the no-slip boundary condition

(2)

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}=0,\quad\mathbf{B}\cdot\mathbf{n}=0,\quad{\operatorname{curl}\,}\mathbf{B}\times\mathbf{n}=0,\quad

\displaystyle\text{on }\ \partial\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}\setminus\{0\},

or the Navier slip boundary condition

(3)

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{n}=0,\quad(\mathbb{D}\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{n})\cdot\boldsymbol{\tau}=0,\quad\mathbf{B}\cdot\mathbf{n}=0,\quad{\operatorname{curl}\,}\mathbf{B}\times\mathbf{n}=0,

\displaystyle\text{on }\ \partial\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}\setminus\{0\},

where
\mathbf{n}
and
\boldsymbol{\tau}
are the unit outward normal and tangent vectors on
\partial\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}
, respectively, and
\mathbb{D}\mathbf{u}=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\mathbf{u}+\nabla\mathbf{u}^{T})
is the strain tensor.
Assume that
\mathbf{u}=u^{r}(r,z)\mathbf{e}_{r}+u^{\theta}(r,z)\mathbf{e}_{\theta}+u^{z}(r,z)\mathbf{e}_{z}
and
\mathbf{B}=B^{\theta}(r,z)\mathbf{e}_{\theta}
, where
(r,\theta,z)
are the cylindrical coordinates defined in Section
2.1
. If
u^{r}(r,z)<\frac{1}{3r}+\frac{2r}{3}
on
\partial B_{1}\cap\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}
, then
\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{B}=0
.

Remark 1.2
.

Similar to Corollary
1.1
, the hypothesis
u^{r}(r,z)<\frac{1}{3r}+\frac{2r}{3}
on
\partial B_{1}\cap\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}
in Theorem
1.2
can be replaced by
u^{r}(x)\leq\alpha
on
\partial B_{1}\cap\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}
with
\alpha<\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we recall some basic definitions and introduce Landau solutions. Section 3 is devoted to the proof of Theorems
1.1
–
1.2
and Corollary
1.1
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Axisymmetric solutions

In this subsection, we introduce the axisymmetric vector fields.
Recall that the cylindrical coordinates of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
are
(r,\theta,z)
with

(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta,z),

and the corresponding orthonormal basis vectors
\mathbf{e}_{r},\mathbf{e}_{\theta},\mathbf{e}_{z}
are

\displaystyle\mathbf{e}_{r}=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta,0),\quad\mathbf{e}_{\theta}=(-\sin\theta,\cos\theta,0),\quad\mathbf{e}_{z}=(0,0,1).

In this coordinate system, a vector field
\mathbf{v}
can be expressed as

\mathbf{v}=v^{r}(r,\theta,z)\mathbf{e}_{r}+v^{\theta}(r,\theta,z)\mathbf{e}_{\theta}+v^{z}(r,\theta,z)\mathbf{e}_{z},

with components
v^{r}
,
v^{\theta}
,
v^{z}
; the component
v^{\theta}
is called the swirl component.
A vector field
\mathbf{v}
is called
axisymmetric
if it is of the form

\mathbf{v}=v^{r}(r,z)\mathbf{e}_{r}+v^{\theta}(r,z)\mathbf{e}_{\theta}+v^{z}(r,z)\mathbf{e}_{z}.

2.2.
Landau solutions to the Navier-Stokes equations

For each
b\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, there exists a unique

(-1)
-homogeneous solution
\mathbf{U}^{b}
to the Navier-Stokes equations, together with an associated
(-2)
-homogeneous pressure
P^{b}
, such that
\mathbf{U}^{b},P^{b}
are smooth in
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
and satisfy

\displaystyle-\Delta\mathbf{U}^{b}+{\rm div\,}(\mathbf{U}^{b}\otimes\mathbf{U}^{b})+\nabla P^{b}=b\delta,\quad{\rm div\,}\mathbf{U}^{b}=0,

in the distributional sense in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, where
\delta
denotes the Dirac function.

3.
Proof of the main results

We first prove Theorems
1.1
–
1.2
. The key step is to prove
B^{\theta}\equiv 0
, since the MHD equations will reduce to the Navier-Stokes equations if
\mathbf{B}=0
, and the conclusion follows from the known results for the Navier-Stokes equations.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Direct computation gives

(4)

-\Delta B^{\theta}+(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)B^{\theta}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}B^{\theta}-\frac{u^{r}B^{\theta}}{r}=0.

For
0<\rho_{1}<\rho_{2}<\infty
, denote
\Omega=B_{\rho_{2}}\setminus\bar{B}_{\rho_{1}}
. Multiplying by
rB^{\theta}
and integrating over
\Omega
, we obtain

(5)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(-\Delta B^{\theta})rB^{\theta}+r\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla(\frac{1}{2}|B^{\theta}|^{2})+\frac{1}{r}|B^{\theta}|^{2}-u^{r}|B^{\theta}|^{2}=0.

The first term of (
5
) becomes

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}-\Delta B^{\theta}(rB^{\theta})=\int_{\Omega}r|\nabla B^{\theta}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{r}|B^{\theta}|^{2}-\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial B^{\theta}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\nu}}rB^{\theta}.

Since
B^{\theta}
is
(-1)
-homogeneous, i.e.,
B^{\theta}(\lambda x)=\lambda^{-1}B^{\theta}(x)
, one finds that

(6)

\displaystyle\frac{x}{|x|}\cdot\nabla B^{\theta}(x)=-\frac{B^{\theta}(x)}{|x|}.

Then

\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial B^{\theta}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\nu}}rB^{\theta}=-\int_{\partial B_{\rho_{2}}}\frac{r|B^{\theta}|^{2}}{|x|}+\int_{\partial B_{\rho_{1}}}\frac{r|B^{\theta}|^{2}}{|x|}=0,

owing to the self-similarity of
B^{\theta}
. Hence,

\int_{\Omega}-\Delta B^{\theta}(rB^{\theta})=\int_{\Omega}r|\nabla B^{\theta}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{r}|B^{\theta}|^{2}.

We claim that

\int_{\Omega}\partial_{r}|B^{\theta}|^{2}=-\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{r}|B^{\theta}|^{2}.

To see this, direct calculation yields

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\partial_{r}|B^{\theta}|^{2}=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}{\rm div\,}(|B^{\theta}|^{2}\mathbf{e}_{r})-\frac{1}{r}|B^{\theta}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}|B^{\theta}|^{2}\mathbf{e}_{r}\cdot\boldsymbol{\nu}-\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{r}|B^{\theta}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{r}|B^{\theta}|^{2},

where the third equality is due to the self-similarity of
B^{\theta}
.
Thus we have

\int_{\Omega}-\Delta B^{\theta}(rB^{\theta})=\int_{\Omega}r|\nabla B^{\theta}|^{2}-\frac{1}{2r}|B^{\theta}|^{2}.

Integration by parts implies

\int_{\Omega}r\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla(\frac{1}{2}|B^{\theta}|^{2})=-\int_{\Omega}{\rm div\,}(r\mathbf{u})\frac{1}{2}|B^{\theta}|^{2}+\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{r|B^{\theta}|^{2}}{2}\mathbf{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{\nu}=-\int_{\Omega}\frac{u^{r}}{2}|B^{\theta}|^{2},

where we used
{\rm div\,}\mathbf{u}=0
and
B^{\theta}
is
(-1)
-homogeneous.

Therefore, equation (
5
) becomes

\int_{\Omega}r|\nabla B^{\theta}|^{2}+|B^{\theta}|^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2r}-\frac{3}{2}u^{r}\right)=0.

From (
6
), we derive

(7)

\displaystyle|\nabla B^{\theta}(x)|\geq\frac{1}{|x|}|B^{\theta}(x)|,

which implies

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|B^{\theta}|^{2}\left(\frac{r}{r^{2}+z^{2}}+\frac{1}{2r}-\frac{3}{2}u^{r}\right)\leq 0.

Applying the coarea formula yields

(\ln\rho_{2}-\ln\rho_{1})\int_{\partial B_{1}}|B^{\theta}|^{2}\left(r+\frac{1}{2r}-\frac{3}{2}u^{r}\right)=\int_{\Omega}|B^{\theta}|^{2}\left(\frac{r}{r^{2}+z^{2}}+\frac{1}{2r}-\frac{3}{2}u^{r}\right)\leq 0.

Using the hypothesis
u^{r}(r,z)<\frac{1}{3r}+\frac{2r}{3}
on
\partial B_{1}
, we get

\int_{\partial B_{1}}|B^{\theta}|^{2}\left(r+\frac{1}{2r}-\frac{3}{2}u^{r}\right)=0,

which shows
B^{\theta}=0
on
\partial B_{1}
. Combining with the fact that
B^{\theta}
is self-similar, we have
B^{\theta}\equiv 0
.
Thus
\mathbf{B}\equiv 0
and equations (
1
) become the Navier-Stokes equations. Since the axisymmetric self-similar solution to the stationary Navier-Stokes equations in
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
is a Landau solution (see, for example,
[
Tsa18
, Theorem 8.1]
), the proof of Theorem
1.1
is complete.
∎

The proof of Theorem
1.2
is in the same spirit as that of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

During the proof, for
0<\rho_{1}<\rho_{2}<\infty
, denote
\Omega=(B_{\rho_{2}}\setminus\bar{B}_{\rho_{1}})\cap\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}
.
Multiplying (
4
) by
rB^{\theta}
and integrating over
\Omega
, we obtain the same identity (
5
).

Integration by parts yields

(8)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}r|\nabla B^{\theta}|^{2}+|B^{\theta}|^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2r}-\frac{3u^{r}}{2}\right)+\int_{\partial\Omega}-\frac{\partial B^{\theta}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\nu}}rB^{\theta}+\frac{|B^{\theta}|^{2}}{2}(\mathbf{e}_{r}\cdot\boldsymbol{\nu}+r\mathbf{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{\nu})=0.

By the self-similarity of
B^{\theta}
, the boundary integral simplifies to

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}-\frac{\partial B^{\theta}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\nu}}rB^{\theta}+\frac{|B^{\theta}|^{2}}{2}(\mathbf{e}_{r}\cdot\boldsymbol{\nu}+r\mathbf{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{\nu})=\int_{S}rB^{\theta}\partial_{z}B^{\theta}+\frac{|B^{\theta}|^{2}}{2}(\mathbf{e}_{r}\cdot\boldsymbol{\nu}+r\mathbf{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{\nu}),

where
S=\{x\in\partial\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}:\rho_{1}\leq|x|\leq\rho_{2}\}
and
\boldsymbol{\nu}=-\mathbf{e}_{z}=(0,0,-1)
.
We claim that

\int_{S}rB^{\theta}\partial_{z}B^{\theta}+\frac{|B^{\theta}|^{2}}{2}(\mathbf{e}_{r}\cdot\boldsymbol{\nu}+r\mathbf{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{\nu})=0

under either the no-slip or the Navier slip boundary condition.
First, a direct calculation gives

{\operatorname{curl}\,}\mathbf{B}=-\partial_{z}B^{\theta}(r,z)\mathbf{e}_{r}+\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}(rB^{\theta})\mathbf{e}_{z}.

Since
{\operatorname{curl}\,}\mathbf{B}\times\mathbf{e}_{z}=0
on
\partial\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}
under both boundary conditions, we obtain

(9)

\displaystyle\partial_{z}B^{\theta}=0\quad\text{on }\partial\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}.

For the remaining terms, under the no-slip condition (
2
), we have
\mathbf{u}=0
on
\partial\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}
; under the Navier slip condition (
3
), we have
\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{z}=0
.
In both cases, since
\mathbf{e}_{r}
lies in the tangent plane of
\partial\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}
, it follows that

(10)

\displaystyle\int_{S}\frac{|B^{\theta}|^{2}}{2}(\mathbf{e}_{r}\cdot\boldsymbol{\nu}+r\mathbf{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{\nu})=0.

From (
9
) and (
10
), the claim is verified.
Thus (
8
) becomes

\int_{\Omega}r|\nabla B^{\theta}|^{2}+|B^{\theta}|^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2r}-\frac{3u^{r}}{2}\right)=0.

The remainder of the proof proceeds as in Theorem
1.1
.
Using (
7
), the upper bound on
u^{r}
, and the self-similarity of
B^{\theta}
and
u^{r}
, we conclude that
\mathbf{B}\equiv 0
. The vanishing of
\mathbf{u}
then follows from
[
KMT18
, Theorem 5.1]
, which completes the proof.
∎

Next, we give the proof of Corollary
1.1
.

Proof of Corollary
1.1
.

Note that the infimum of the function
\frac{1}{3r}+\frac{2r}{3}
on
\partial B_{1}
is
\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}
.
Consequently, the conclusion of Theorem
1.1
applies, which completes the proof.
∎

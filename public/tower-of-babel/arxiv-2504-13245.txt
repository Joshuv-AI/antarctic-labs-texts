The Schott term in the binding energy for compact binaries on circular orbits at fourth post-Newtonian order
David Trestini

Abstract
The phasing for compact binary systems on circular orbits was obtained in [Phys. Rev. Lett. 131, 121402 (2023)] at fourth-and-a-half post-Newtonian (4.5PN) order thanks to two main ingredients: the 4PN conservative energy (associated to a nonradiative spacetime) in terms of the orbital frequency and the 4.5PN flux in terms of the waveform frequency (i.e., the half-frequency of the ( ℓ , m ) = ( 2 , 2 ) (\ell,m)=(2,2) mode). When obtaining the phasing, a key physical postulate was made: the expression of the binding energy in terms of the waveform frequency was assumed to be identical to the expression of the conservative energy in terms of the orbital frequency. This postulate was necessary to ensure that the frequency evolution obtained through the flux-balance law (which involves the binding energy) was independent of the choice of spacetime foliation. In this work, I show that the binding energy entering the flux-balance law differs from the 4PN conservative energy by a 4PN pseudo-Schott term, associated with radiation-reaction effects due to gravitational tails. Unlike the usual Schott terms (at 2.5PN, 3.5PN and 4.5PN), the pseudo-Schott term is not a total derivative and is in fact hereditary, so it does not vanish for circular orbits. Remarkably, the binding energy thus obtained is in perfect agreement with the one obtained using the aforementioned physical postulate, which confirms that the 4.5PN phasing associated to the waveform frequency computed in [Phys. Rev. Lett. 131, 121402 (2023)] is indeed correct. This result is extended to the other Poincaré invariants, and ‘thermodynamic’ relations between the binding energy and angular momentum are established. Finally, the chirp and phasing associated to the orbital frequency are presented at 4.5PN, including horizon-absorption effects.

II
The 4PN equations of motion

One of the main predictions of post-Newtonian techniques is how compact binaries move. This can be described using Lagrangians or Hamiltonians, but we will here prefer giving the explicit differential equations satisfied by the particle trajectories, which we call equations of motion. Namely, the acceleration of the first particle, denoted
\bm{a}_{1}=\mathrm{d}^{2}\bm{y}_{1}/\mathrm{d}t^{2}
, is given explicitly in terms of the positions and velocities of the two particles,
(\bm{y}_{1},\bm{y}_{2},\bm{v}_{1},\bm{v}_{2})

\bm{a}_{2}

1\leftrightarrow 2

At 4PN, a new feature arises: the equations of motion do not only depend on the positions and velocities of the particles at time

for all
t^{\prime}<t

\displaystyle=\frac{4\ell(\ell\!+\!2)}{(\ell\!-\!1)(\ell\!+\!1)!(2\ell\!+\!1)!!}\,,

\displaystyle a^{\prime}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\ell\!+\!1)(\ell\!+\!2)}{(\ell\!-\!1)\ell!(2\ell\!+\!1)!!}\,,

\displaystyle b^{\prime}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{4\ell^{2}(\ell\!+\!2)}{(\ell\!-\!1)(\ell\!+\!1)!(2\ell\!+\!1)!!}\,,

\displaystyle a^{\prime\prime}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{2(\ell\!+\!2)(\ell\!+\!3)}{\ell(\ell\!+\!1)!(2\ell\!+\!3)!!}\,,

\displaystyle b^{\prime\prime}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{8(\ell\!+\!3)}{(\ell\!+\!1)!(2\ell\!+\!3)!!}\,,

\displaystyle a^{\prime\prime\prime}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{2(\ell\!+\!2)(\ell\!+\!3)}{\ell\ell!(2\ell\!+\!3)!!}\,,

\displaystyle b^{\prime\prime\prime}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{8(\ell\!+\!3)}{\ell!(2\ell\!+\!3)!!}\,,

\displaystyle c^{\prime\prime}_{\ell}=\frac{8(\ell\!+\!2)}{(\ell\!-\!1)(\ell\!+\!1)!(2\ell\!+\!1)!!}\,.\hskip 92.47145pt

(11)

The fluxes associated to energy and angular momentum enter at 2.5PN, whereas the fluxes associated to linear momentum and center-of-mass position enter at 3.5PN order. Moreover, note that here I have adopted a definition for the flux associated to the center-of-mass position borrowed in Ref.
Compère et al. 2020
, which stems from a computation purely at future null infinity, rather than the definition given in Refs.
Blanchet and Faye 2019
;
Blanchet et al. 2025
, which stems from a near-zone computation (these two definitions differ only by a total time derivative). Typically, the MPM algorithm allows us to express the radiative moments in terms of, e.g., canonical moments
(\mathrm{M}_{L},\mathrm{S}_{L})
, which can themselves be expressed in terms of the post-Newtonian source through matching. For example, the mass-type quadrupole moments are related at 1.5PN order by

\displaystyle\mathrm{U}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{M}_{ij}^{(2)}\!+\frac{2G\mathrm{M}}{c^{3}}\!\int_{0}^{+\infty}\!\!\!\!\mathrm{d}\tau\!\left[\ln\left(\frac{c\tau}{2b_{0}}\right)+\frac{11}{12}\right]\!\mathrm{M}_{ij}^{(4)}(t-\tau)\,,

(12)

We now want to establish flux balance laws, which relate quantities computed in the near-zone to quantities computed at future null infinity. To this end, the full acceleration is reinstated, which contains both conservative and dissipative contributions:
\bm{a}_{1}=\bm{a}_{1}^{\text{cons}}+\bm{a}_{1}^{\text{RR}}
. This leads to the general structure

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}\bm{Q}_{\text{cons}}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{\bm{a}_{1}}+\mathcal{F}_{\bm{Q}}=-\delta\mathcal{F}_{\bm{Q}}\,,

(13)

where the right-hand side is a small, dominantly 2.5PN quantity for
\left\{E,\bm{J}\right\}
, and an even smaller 3.5PN quantity for
\left\{\bm{P},\bm{G}\right\}
. When ignoring 4PN contributions, it was shown in
Iyer and Will 1993
;
Iyer and Will 1995
;
Blanchet et al. 2025
that this term is in fact a total time-derivative, such that
\delta\mathcal{F}_{\bm{Q}}={\mathrm{d}\bm{Q}_{RR}}/{\mathrm{d}t}
, where the radiation-reaction contribution
\bm{Q}_{\text{RR}}
is called a
Schott term
, in reference to similar terms in electromagnetism
Schott 1915
. One then defines the
binding
quantities for the full dissipative system as
\bm{Q}=\bm{Q}_{\text{cons}}+\bm{Q}_{\text{RR}}
, such that the expected flux balance law be satisfied:

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}\bm{Q}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{\bm{a}_{1}}+\mathcal{F}_{\bm{Q}}=0\,.

(14)

Moreover, general arguments
Breuer and Rudolph 1981
predict that these Schott terms themselves can in fact be written as total derivatives (again, when ignoring tails). For example, at 2.5PN order, we have
Blanchet et al. 2025

\displaystyle E^{\text{2.5PN}}_{\text{RR}}

\displaystyle=\frac{G}{5c^{5}}\Big\{\mathrm{M}_{ij}^{(2)}\mathrm{M}_{ij}^{(3)}-\mathrm{M}_{ij}^{(1)}\mathrm{M}_{ij}^{(4)}\Big\}

\displaystyle=\frac{G}{5c^{5}}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Bigg[\mathrm{M}_{ij}^{(2)}\mathrm{M}_{ij}^{(2)}-\mathrm{M}_{ij}^{(1)}\mathrm{M}_{ij}^{(3)}\Bigg]\,,

(15)

although checking this statement explicitly at higher orders would require more work, along the lines of Ref.
Blanchet et al. 2025
.
A consequence is that these Schott terms vanish after orbit averaging for bound elliptic systems, or identically for circular orbits. This can also be seen by the fact that the explicit expressions of the Schott terms given in
Blanchet et al. 2025
are all proportional to
\dot{r}
, which vanishes for exactly circular orbits. Thus, the Schott term is usually interpreted as a reversible exchange of energy between the binary and the gravity field. However, due to radiation reaction, orbits are not perfectly circular: the slow inspiral causes the 2.5PN Schott term to start contributing, in the case of circular orbits, at 5PN order
Breuer and Rudolph 1981
.

The results obtained in Ref.
Blanchet et al. 2025
purposely ignore the known dissipative contributions that enter at 4PN order, due to tails. A detailed treatment of the associated Schott terms is the main object of this work. In order to work these out, we will only require the Newtonian expressions of the Poincaré invariants:

\displaystyle E_{\text{cons}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(m_{1}v_{1}^{2}+m_{2}v_{2}^{2}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{c^{2}}\right)\,,

\displaystyle=m_{1}v_{1}^{i}+m_{2}v_{2}^{i}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{c^{2}}\right)\,,

(16c)

\displaystyle G^{i}_{\text{cons}}

\displaystyle=m_{1}y_{1}^{i}+m_{2}y_{2}^{i}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{c^{2}}\right)\,.

(17c)

We now focus on what goes on at future null infinity. We know that the 1.5PN contribution to the fluxes at infinity are entirely due to tails, and read:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\text{1.5PN}}_{E}

\displaystyle=\frac{4G^{2}\mathrm{M}}{5c^{8}}\mathrm{M}_{ij}^{(3)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\left[\ln\left(\frac{c\tau}{2b_{0}}\right)+\frac{11}{12}\right]\mathrm{M}_{ij}^{(5)}(t-\tau)\,,

(18a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{i,\text{1.5PN}}_{\bm{J}}

\displaystyle=\frac{4G^{2}\mathrm{M}}{5c^{8}}\epsilon_{ijk}\Bigg\{\mathrm{M}_{jl}^{(2)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\left[\ln\left(\frac{c\tau}{2b_{0}}\right)+\frac{11}{12}\right]\mathrm{M}_{kl}^{(5)}(t-\tau)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\ \quad-\mathrm{M}_{jl}^{(3)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\left[\ln\left(\frac{c\tau}{2b_{0}}\right)+\frac{11}{12}\right]\mathrm{M}_{kl}^{(4)}(t-\tau)\Bigg\}\,,

(18c)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{i,\text{1.5PN}}_{\bm{G}}

\displaystyle=0\,.

(18d)

The vanishing of the fluxes associated to the linear momentum and the center-of-mass position at this order is due to the conservation of the ADM linear momentum (i.e, the canonical dipole moment).
Thus, it is clear that the flux balance law for the energy, angular momentum and linear momentum are not automatically satisfied, and indeed require the inclusion of a 4PN Schott term. Conversely, no such Schott term is required for the flux associated to the center-of-mass position at this order. With a few operations by parts, we find that the Schott terms associated to the energy and angular momentum can be written as the total time derivative of some instantaneous and tail-like terms, plus a tail-like term which cannot be written as a total time derivative. Thus, the sought-for Schott term will necessarily feature a doubly hereditary term, akin the ‘genuine’ tails of memory, see the first term with a double intergral in Eq. (6.6c) of
Trestini and Blanchet 2023
. Note that the Schott term entering the linear momentum has a simpler structure, thanks to the conservation of both the ADM mass (monopole) and linear momentum (dipole).
Our final expressions for the Schott terms reads

\displaystyle E^{\text{4PN}}_{\text{RR}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2G^{2}\mathrm{M}}{5c^{8}}\Bigg\{\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\rho\,\mathrm{M}_{ij}^{(4)}(t-\rho)\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\ln\left(\frac{c\tau}{2b_{0}}\right)\left[\mathrm{M}_{ij}^{(4)}(t-\rho-\tau)-\mathrm{M}_{ij}^{(4)}(t-\rho+\tau)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\mathrm{M}_{ij}^{(3)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\ln\left(\frac{c\tau}{2b_{0}}\right)\left[\mathrm{M}_{ij}^{(4)}(t-\tau)-\mathrm{M}_{ij}^{(4)}(t+\tau)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\ -\mathrm{M}_{ij}^{(2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\ln\left(\frac{c\tau}{2b_{0}}\right)\left[\mathrm{M}_{ij}^{(5)}(t-\tau)+\mathrm{M}_{ij}^{(5)}(t+\tau)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\ +\mathrm{M}_{ij}^{(1)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\ln\left(\frac{c\tau}{2b_{0}}\right)\left[\mathrm{M}_{ij}^{(6)}(t-\tau)+\mathrm{M}_{ij}^{(6)}(t+\tau)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\frac{11}{12}\mathrm{M}_{ij}^{(3)}\mathrm{M}_{ij}^{(3)}\Bigg\}\,,

(19a)

\displaystyle J^{i,\text{4PN}}_{\text{RR}}

\displaystyle=\frac{4G^{2}\mathrm{M}}{5c^{8}}\epsilon_{ijk}\Bigg\{\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\rho\,\mathrm{M}_{jl}^{(3)}(t-\rho)\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\ln\left(\frac{c\tau}{2b_{0}}\right)\left[\mathrm{M}_{kl}^{(4)}(t-\rho-\tau)-\mathrm{M}_{kl}^{(4)}(t-\rho+\tau)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\mathrm{M}_{jl}^{(2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\ln\left(\frac{c\tau}{2b_{0}}\right)\mathrm{M}_{kl}^{(4)}(t+\tau)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\ -\mathrm{M}_{jl}^{(1)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\ln\left(\frac{c\tau}{2b_{0}}\right)\left[\mathrm{M}_{kl}^{(5)}(t-\tau)+\mathrm{M}_{kl}^{(5)}(t+\tau)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\ +\mathrm{M}_{jl}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\ln\left(\frac{c\tau}{2b_{0}}\right)\left[\mathrm{M}_{kl}^{(6)}(t-\tau)+\mathrm{M}_{kl}^{(6)}(t+\tau)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\frac{11}{12}\mathrm{M}_{jl}^{(2)}\mathrm{M}_{kl}^{(3)}\Bigg\}\,,

(19b)

\displaystyle P^{i,\text{4PN}}_{\text{RR}}

\displaystyle=\frac{4G^{2}\mathrm{M}}{5c^{8}}\mathrm{M}_{j}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\ln\left(\frac{c\tau}{2b_{0}}\right)\left[\mathrm{M}_{ij}^{(6)}(t-\tau)+\mathrm{M}_{ij}^{(6)}(t+\tau)\right]+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{c^{10}}\right)\,.

(19c)

Unlike the other Schott terms at 2.5PN, 3.5PN and 4.5PN
Blanchet et al. 2025

19

pseudo
-Schott terms to be non-vanishing in the circular limit. A tentative physical interpretation goes as follows: due to the presence of hereditary terms, this expression is sensitive to small, radiation-reaction induced variations in the amount of energy reversibly exchanged during one orbit.

Finally, note that there are of course many other formulations of this Schott term, which can be obtained through integration by parts. Some of these formulations are simpler, others distinguish
s_{0}
and
b_{0}
. The motivation for the formulation in Eq. (
19

Trestini and Blanchet 2023
); and (iii) the only arbitrary constant is
b_{0}
. I have checked that various such formulations yield the same explicit result when reduced to the case of circular orbits.

In association with the results of Refs.
Bernard et al. 2018
;
Bernard et al. 2018
;
Blanchet et al. 2025

Blanchet et al. 2025

\mathrm{M}_{i}=\mathcal{O}\left(1/c^{7}\right)
.

IV
Integration formulas for circular orbits

IV.1
Post-adiabatic integration formulas for advanced tails

The explicit computation of the contribution of tails for quasi-circular orbits was extensively studied. It was shown that these integrals are mostly sensitive to the recent past such that we can compute these integrals with a perfectly circular model up to 2PN relative order, arguing that the cumulative oscillatory features in the remote past average out
Blanchet and Schäfer 1993
. It was later confirmed by a computation that accounted for the rate of inspiral
Arun et al. 2004
, showing that the integral was indeed convergent in the infinite past and that the result did not depend on the inspiral rate. Recently, this computation was extended to 2.5PN
Blanchet, Luc and Faye, Guillaume and Henry, Quentin
and Larrouturou, François and Trestini,
David 2023b
;
Trestini 2023
, where one needs to account for post-adiabatic effects. We introduce the orbital frequency
\omega
, the dimensionless PN counting parameter

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{Gm\omega}{c^{3}}\right)^{2/3}

(20)

and the adiabatic parameter
4
4

4

By definition, the system is adiabatic when
\xi\ll 1
, which here is equivalent to the post-Newtonian assumption
y\ll 1
. The
8/3
prefactor is conventional
Arun et al. 2004
;
Trestini 2023
, but was dropped in Ref.
Blanchet, Luc and Faye, Guillaume and Henry, Quentin
and Larrouturou, François and Trestini,
David 2023b
.
defined as
\xi=8\dot{\omega}/(3\omega^{2})
, and we find that the convergence is still ensured, and the rate of inspiral now enters explicitly in the result. The result is given in Eq. (5.16) of
Blanchet, Luc and Faye, Guillaume and Henry, Quentin
and Larrouturou, François and Trestini,
David 2023b
, and reads (after rescaling
\xi
; see Footnote
4
)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{\tau_{0}}\right)\left[y(t-\tau)\right]^{\alpha}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}n\phi(t-\tau)}=\frac{y^{\alpha}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}n\phi}}{\mathrm{i}n\omega}\Bigg[\left(1+\frac{2\alpha-3}{8}\,\frac{\xi}{\mathrm{i}n}\right)\left(\ln(|n|\omega\tau_{0})+\gamma_{\text{E}}-\text{sg}(n)\frac{\mathrm{i}\pi}{2}\right)-\frac{4\alpha-9}{16}\frac{\xi}{\mathrm{i}n}\Bigg]\,,

(21)

where quantities are evaluated at time

unless otherwise indicated.

This computation, however, was done only for the physically relevant case where one integrates over the whole
past
of the binary. When splitting our problem into conservative and dissipative pieces, we artificially created the need to compute tails spanning the whole
future
of the binary. In the real, non-perturbative problem, this is expected to be entirely regular, since the system rings down after merger. However, in the perturbative problem we are considering, most physical quantities (e.g. the orbital frequency) blow up in finite time, and the blowup time can be interpreted as ‘coalescence time’, denoted
t_{c}
. The function we are trying to integrate around isn’t even defined after that time, and even if we change the upper bound to merger time, the integral doesn’t converge. Since this ill-defined contribution was artificially introduced, it will suffice to find a prescription, so long as the same one is used in the conservative and dissipative sectors. One such prescription would be to introduce a regulator in the integral, e.g.
\mathrm{e}^{-\epsilon\,\xi(t+\tau)}
, which imposes a smoothly vanishing integrand at merger time for any
\epsilon>0
, since
\xi(t)\rightarrow+\infty
at merger time (
t\rightarrow t_{c}
). One can then reproduce,
mutatis mutandis
, the steps of Ref.
Trestini 2023
, and send
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
at the end of the computation. However, such an approach was not successful in obtaining a clear derivation
5
5

5

More precisely, it was unclear how to compute terms of the type
\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}s\ln(s)\,\mathrm{e}^{-{8\mathrm{i}ns}/{(5\xi)}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\epsilon/(1-s)}
without introducing terms involving
\mathrm{Ei}(-8\mathrm{i}n/(5\xi))

, although it was instructive in gaining intuition about how the post-adiabatic formula should look in the advanced case.

A better strategy is to
define
the advanced case from the known retarded case using the time reflection symmetry discussed in Sec.

leads to the following transformations:
\phi\rightarrow-\phi

\omega\rightarrow\omega
and
\dot{\omega}\rightarrow-\dot{\omega}

and

\phi(t\pm\tau)\rightarrow-\phi(t\mp\tau)
,
\omega(t\pm\tau)\rightarrow\omega(t\mp\tau)

and
\xi(t\pm\tau)\rightarrow-\xi(t\mp\tau)

Since we have established that the tails depend, in a loose sense, only on the recent past, we will borrow the notation of Eq. (4.22) of Ref.

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\alpha,n}^{\pm}\equiv\int_{0}\mathrm{d}\tau\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{\tau_{0}}\right)\,\left[y(t\pm\tau)\right]^{\alpha}\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{i}n\phi(t\pm\tau)}\,.

At leading PN order,

, and the time evolution of

\displaystyle\int_{0}\mathrm{d}\tau\,\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{\tau_{0}}\right)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}n\omega\tau}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathrm{i}n\omega}\Bigg[\ln\left(|n|\omega\tau_{0}\right)+\gamma_{\text{E}}-\,\mathrm{sg}(n)\frac{\mathrm{i}\pi}{2}\Bigg]+\mathcal{O}(\xi)\,,

\xi

IV.2
Self-consistency check for the post-adiabatic tail formula using integration by parts

The previous derivation of the integration formula for the advanced tails was mostly based on symmetry considerations, and admittedly lacks a clear proof featuring convergent integrals. However, in this section, I will show that the formula derived in Eq. (
23
) is the only one that is consistent with integration by parts at the level of the multipolar moments.

For definiteness, let us focus on the relation:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle\Bigg[\mathrm{M}^{(3)}_{ij}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\ln\left(\frac{c\tau}{2b_{0}}\right)\mathrm{M}^{(4)}_{ij}(t\pm\tau)\Bigg]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{M}^{(4)}_{ij}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\ln\left(\frac{c\tau}{2b_{0}}\right)\mathrm{M}^{(4)}_{ij}(t\pm\tau)

\displaystyle\ +\mathrm{M}^{(3)}_{ij}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\ln\left(\frac{c\tau}{2b_{0}}\right)\mathrm{M}^{(5)}_{ij}(t\pm\tau)\,.

(25)

This relation is of course always true, and we will want to check that it still holds when replacing the multipolar moments by their explicit expressions for circular orbits. As we will see, the post-adiabatic formula (
23
) will be crucial in ensuring this.

The left-hand side of Eq. (
IV.2
) is very easy to compute in the case of circular orbits. First, we compute the term inside the explicit time derivative. It only requires the expression of the multipolar moments (bearing time derivatives) at Newtonian order. Namely, we require

\displaystyle\mathrm{M}_{ij}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{8\nu c^{5}y^{5/2}}{G}n^{\langle i}\lambda^{j\rangle}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{c^{2}}\right)\,,

(26a)

\displaystyle\mathrm{M}_{ij}^{(4)}

\displaystyle=\frac{8\nu c^{8}y^{4}}{G^{2}m}\left(n^{\langle i}n^{j\rangle}-\lambda^{\langle i}\lambda^{j\rangle}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{c^{2}}\right)\,,

(26b)

where the symmetric trace-free projection is denoted with angled brackets around the indices, namely
a^{\langle i}b^{j\rangle}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\left(a^{i}b^{j}+a^{j}b^{i}\right)-\frac{1}{3}\delta^{ij}a^{k}b^{k}
.
For the purpose of evaluating the tail integral, we can then project the co-rotating basis
(\bm{n},\bm{\lambda})
onto a time-independent basis
(\bm{n}_{0},\bm{\lambda}_{0})
using

\displaystyle\bm{n}=\cos\phi\,\bm{n}_{0}+\sin\phi\,\bm{\lambda}_{0}\,,

(27a)

\displaystyle\bm{\lambda}=-\sin\phi\,\bm{n}_{0}+\cos\phi\,\bm{\lambda}_{0}\,,

(27b)

where at leading order the phase reads
\phi(t\pm\tau)=\phi(t)\pm\omega\tau+...

Using only these Newtonian expressions alongside the well-known adiabatic integration formula (
IV.1

\mathcal{F}_{E}=\mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{E}+\mathcal{F}_{E}^{\mathcal{H}}
and
\mathcal{F}_{J}=\mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{J}+\mathcal{F}_{J}^{\mathcal{H}}
.
Using the chain rule, we find that the differential equation governing the orbital frequency evolution reads

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mathcal{F}_{E}(y;b_{0})}{\mathrm{d}E(y;b_{0})/\mathrm{d}y}\,,

(52)

where we are ignoring the mass evolution driven by black hole absorption (these lead to 5PN effects in the phasing which are negligible here
Warburton et al. 2024
).
Crucially, we find that
b_{0}
drops out from the final results at 4.5PN, as one would expect physically. This observation is non-trivial: the
b_{0}
dependence in the energy was obtained only with the knowledge of the 4PN tail acceleration and the 1.5PN flux, whereas the
b_{0}
appears at 4PN in the flux, and arises from various parts of the computation. The explicit expression of the right-hand side of this equation is most conveniently split into a contribution from the fluxes at infinity and another subdominant contribution from the horizon fluxes:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle=\left\{\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t}\right\}_{\infty}+\left\{\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t}\right\}_{\mathcal{H}}\,.

(53)

The main contribution due to the flux at infinity reads

\displaystyle\left\{\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t}\right\}_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\frac{c^{3}}{Gm}\mathfrak{D}(y;\nu)\,,

(54)

where the right-hand side is driven by the function

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}(z;\nu)

\displaystyle=\frac{64\nu z^{5}}{5}\Bigg\{1+z\left(-\frac{743}{336}-\frac{11}{4}\nu\right)+4\pi z^{3/2}+z^{2}\left(\frac{34103}{18144}+\frac{13661}{2016}\nu+\frac{59}{18}\nu^{2}\right)+\pi z^{5/2}\left(-\frac{4159}{672}-\frac{189}{8}\nu\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\pi z^{7/2}\Bigg[-\frac{4415}{4032}+\frac{358675}{6048}\nu+\frac{91495}{1512}\nu^{2}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad+z^{4}\Bigg[\frac{3959271176713}{25427001600}-\frac{361}{126}\pi^{2}+\frac{124741}{4410}\gamma_{\text{E}}-\frac{47385}{1568}\ln(3)+\frac{127751}{1470}\ln(2)+\frac{124741}{8820}\ln(z)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\nu\left(-\frac{302567610617}{391184640}-\frac{1472377}{16128}\pi^{2}-\frac{58250}{441}\gamma_{\text{E}}+\frac{47385\log(3)}{392}-\frac{850042\log(2)}{2205}-\frac{29125\log(z)}{441}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\nu^{2}\left(\frac{504221849}{326592}-\frac{22099}{384}\pi^{2}\right)-\frac{1909807}{62208}\nu^{3}+\frac{917}{1152}\nu^{4}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad+\pi z^{9/2}\Bigg[\frac{343801320119}{745113600}-\frac{6848}{105}\gamma_{\text{E}}-\frac{3424}{105}\log(16z)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\nu\left(-\frac{516333533}{532224}+\frac{451}{12}\pi^{2}\right)-\frac{6821669}{41472}\nu^{2}-\frac{24107249}{266112}\nu^{3}\Bigg]\Bigg\}

(55)

The contributions due to the horizon fluxes are simply given by

\displaystyle\left\{\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t}\right\}_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=\frac{64c^{3}\nu}{5Gm}\aleph_{8}\,y^{9}\,.

(56)

I have checked explicitly that, as expected, using the flux balance law for the angular momentum yields the same result.

The result of Eqs. (
53
) contains all the needed physical information, but it is nonetheless useful to integrate it analytically, in a post-Newtonian sense, to get (i) the orbital phase
\phi
in terms of the orbital frequency
y
, and (ii) the orbital frequency
y
as an explicit function of time.
First, one can express time in terms of
y
through the relation

\displaystyle t-t_{c}=\int^{y}\frac{\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}}{dy^{\prime}/dt}\,,

(57)

where
dy^{\prime}/dt
is given in terms of
y^{\prime}
in Eq. (
53
) and where
t_{c}
is an arbitrary integration constant which can be chosen to simplify the expression, and will be interpreted loosely as the ‘coalescence time’. Performing a post-Newtonian expansion on the integrand, one can straightforwardly perform the integral of Eq. (
57
). It is then straightforward to invert the relation in a post-Newtonian sense, so as to express the orbital frequency in terms of time. For this, it is convenient to introduce the dimensionless time variable

\displaystyle\tau=\frac{\nu c^{3}}{5Gm}(t_{c}-t)\,.

(58)

We thus find that the orbital frequency evolves as

\displaystyle y(\tau)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\tau^{-1}\ln\tau\Biggl[-\frac{2518977598355703073}{3779358859513036800}+\!\frac{9203}{215040}\gamma_{\text{E}}+\!\frac{9049}{258048}\pi^{2}+\!\frac{14873}{1128960}\ln 2+\frac{47385}{1605632}\ln 3-\!\frac{9203}{3440640}\ln\tau

\displaystyle\phi(y)

VIII
Discussion

This computation of the pseudo-Schott term at 4PN has solidly confirmed the physical hypothesis used in Refs.
Blanchet, Luc and Faye, Guillaume and Henry, Quentin
and Larrouturou, François and Trestini,
David 2023a
;
Blanchet, Luc and Faye, Guillaume and Henry, Quentin
and Larrouturou, François and Trestini,
David 2023b
and reinforced our trust in the validity of the phasing obtained therein.
However, the existence of this Schott term could potentially impact effective one-body models
Nagar et al. 2024
;
Nagar et al. 2025
, since they seem not to account for the difference between orbital and waveform frequency in their 4PN expressions. It could also shed light on the reason why analytical black hole perturbation theory needs to choose
b_{0}=2\mathrm{M}/(c^{2}\sqrt{\mathrm{e}})
to reconcile their results with post-Newtonian expressions (see Footnote 3 of Ref.
Fujita and Iyer 2010
).
Moreover, this result could solve a longstanding discrepancy between self-force and post-Newtonian theory. Indeed, it was found that the Bondi energy obtained from self-force was not agreeing with the post-Newtonian conservative energy
Pound et al. 2020
, whereas the energy obtained from the first law of binary black hole mechanics or Hamiltonian methods did
Lewis, Jack and Kakehi, Takafumi and Pound, Adam and
Tanaka, Takahiro 2025
. It was unclear at the time if an agreement with the post-Newtonian conservative energy was expected, and it was conjectured that such a difference could be caused by hereditary effects. Here, I have shown that the binding energy and conservative energy differ at 4PN, and I expect the Bondi energy from self-force to instead agree with the post-Newtonian
binding
energy of Eq. (
42a
).
In work to appear soon, Ref.
Grant et al. 2025
has established the precise relationship between the Bondi energy and mechanical energy in self-force theory, and has confirmed agreement with the results of this paper [provided
b_{0}=2\mathrm{M}/(c^{2}\sqrt{\mathrm{e}})
], as usual in self-force. Future work will then investigate whether these theoretical advances are in agreement with the numerical results of
Pound et al. 2020
.

One weakness of this work is the lack of a solid derivation of the post-adiabatic formula obtained in Eq. (
23
). I have argued that this non-causal term is introduced in some sense artificially, and that it need not converge in the future for us to define it consistently. However, a proper regularization procedure would be more satisfactory, and would be worth pursuing.

Another open question goes as follows: one could worry that the physical postulate used in Refs.
Blanchet, Luc and Faye, Guillaume and Henry, Quentin
and Larrouturou, François and Trestini,
David 2023a
;
Blanchet, Luc and Faye, Guillaume and Henry, Quentin
and Larrouturou, François and Trestini,
David 2023b
cannot hold at higher PN orders, because there is no reason to select the waveform frequency to be that of the
(2,2)
mode rather than any other
(\ell,m)
mode. However, one can hypothesize that it is possible to define an effective frequency
\Omega_{\text{eff}}
which would generalize such an observation. Namely, if the PN expansion of the conservative energy and angular momentum in terms of the orbital frequency read

\displaystyle E_{\text{cons}}^{\text{circ}}(y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n,k}\alpha_{n}(\nu)\,y^{n/2}(\ln y)^{k}\,,

(66a)

\displaystyle J_{\text{cons}}^{\text{circ}}(y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n,k}\beta_{n}(\nu)\,y^{n/2}(\ln y)^{k}\,,

(66b)

then we can hypothesize that the binding energy and angular momentum (including Schott terms) read in terms of the effective frequency
such that

\displaystyle E^{\text{circ}}(x_{\text{eff}})

\displaystyle\overset{?}{=}\sum_{n,k}\alpha_{n}(\nu)x_{\text{eff}}^{n/2}(\ln x_{\text{eff}})^{k}\,,

(67a)

\displaystyle J^{\text{circ}}(x_{\text{eff}})

\displaystyle\overset{?}{=}\sum_{n,k}\beta_{n}(\nu)x_{\text{eff}}^{n/2}(\ln x_{\text{eff}})^{k}\,,

(67b)

where
x_{\text{eff}}=\left({Gm\,\Omega_{\text{eff}}}/{c^{3}}\right)^{2/3}
. A consequence would be that the binding energy and angular momentum satisfy

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}E^{\text{circ}}}{\mathrm{d}\Omega_{\text{eff}}}\overset{?}{=}\Omega_{\text{eff}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}J^{\text{circ}}}{\mathrm{d}\Omega_{\text{eff}}}\,,

(68)

which can be rephrased as the first law of binary black hole mechanics (with masses held fixed)
Le Tiec et al. 2012
in terms of this effective frequency:

\displaystyle\delta E\overset{?}{=}\Omega_{\text{eff}}\,\delta J

(69)

Now, how should we define
\Omega_{\text{eff}}
? At 4PN, it should correspond to
\Omega_{22}
, because the
(2,2)
is the dominant contribution to the gravitational radiation. A natural definition for
\Omega_{\text{eff}}
would be a linear combination of every
(\ell,m)
frequency weighted by their relative contribution to the gravitational radiation at infinity, namely

\displaystyle\Omega_{\text{eff}}\overset{?}{=}\sum_{\ell m}\frac{\mathcal{F}_{\ell m}}{\mathcal{F}}\Omega_{\ell m}\,,

(70)

where
\mathcal{F}
can be either the energy and angular momentum flux, and
\mathcal{F}_{\ell m}
the contribution from the
h_{\ell m}
mode (see
Warburton et al. 2024
for explicit expressions). Naturally,
\Omega_{\text{eff}}
coincides with the orbital frequency up to 3.5PN order, with
\Omega_{22}
up to 4.5PN, and becomes nontrivial beyond 5PN.

As a matter of fact, preliminary work in Ref.
Grant et al. 2025
has shown that the proposed
\Omega_{\text{eff}}
of Eq. (
70
) succeeds in extending the first law of binary black holes mechanics as in Eq. (
69
) to all PN order and at second order in the mass ratio, with masses being kept fixed and horizon effects ignored. One could think of extensions to include these horizon effects and extend the argument to all orders in the mass ratio.

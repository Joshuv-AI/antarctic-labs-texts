3D printing of acetaminophen suppository and its quality and pharmacokinetic evaluation
Authors: Ming Chen, Meitao Duan, Jungang Ren, Xiuhong Lin, Zheng Chen, Junfang Ke, Huayun Ye, Zhiqiang Zhang, Chen Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
IntroductionAcetaminophen is a widely used antipyretic and analgesic treatment. Becuase oral administration poses a risk of acute liver failure, researchers are exploring alternative routes of administration using 3D printing.MethodsThis study reports a novel 3D-printed suppository using hot melt extrusion and melt deposition molding technologies. Through excipients screening, process screening and 3D printing, the production can be filtered to the most optimal state. After successfully prepared 3D printed acetaminophen suppository, the suppository’s performance and pharmacokinetics profile were also evaluated.ResultsPrepared 3D printed suppository has a complete appearance, smooth interlayer stacking and qualified content determination with over 90% within 6 hours’ in vitro release trend. The 3D printing acetaminophen suppository also has better release and distribution curve than the marketing acetaminophen suppository.ConclusionThe obtained product has a complete appearance, smooth interlayer stacking and stable drug active molecules (API) at the test temperature. Melt deposition molding technologies offers a viable option for the 3D printing preparation of acetaminophen suppository.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s41205-025-00298-3.

Introduction

Acetaminophen, also known as paracetamol or APAP, is a common used antipyretic and analgesic drug belonging to the acetanilide class [1]. It can block the synthesis of prostaglandins from arachidonic acid by inhibiting cyclooxygenase-1 (COX-1) and −2 (COX-2) [2]. Aacetaminophen is one of the most widely used antipyretic analgesics, and is recommended as first-line therapy for fever caused by common cold and influenza, as well as mild and moderate pain, such as headache and joint pain [3]. Acetaminophen has long been considered clinically safe, but over the past decade, it has been identified as the leading cause of acute liver failure in the United States, most of which occur due to unintentional overdose [4–6], which should arouse people’s vigilance.

Oral administration is the primary method for delivering acetaminophen in clinical settings [7]. When taken orally, acetaminophen can be rapidly absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract and the blood concentration reaches the peak value at about 30–60 minutes [8]. Through oral administration, acetaminophen is mainly metabolized by the liver. In cases of excessive acetaminophen levels, a large amount of acetaminophen will be metabolized through cytochrome p450 system of the liver, resulting in the rapid consumption of glutathione [9]. Consequently, its covalent conjugate, N-acetyl-p-benzoquinonimine (NAPQI), binds covalently to macromolecules in liver cells, causing damage and necrosis of liver cells [6, 10–12]. Due to this fact, reducing the amount of acetaminophen absorbed into the blood through the gastrointestinal tract can reduce the hepatotoxicity of acetaminophen [13, 14]. Researchers have been looking for many different modes of administration such as acetaminophen patch or suppository [15–17]. They are also exploring new formulations of slow and controlled release dosage form of acetaminophen by structural and geometric design. As commonly used framework materials for sustained-release formulations, polymer matrices have become extremely popular in pharmaceutical study [18, 19].

Hot-melt extrusion (HME) is a technology that mixes excipients such as polymers or plasticizers in a molten state and extrudes them into a product at a certain pressure, speed, and shape. HME originally used in the plastics and rubber industries [20, 21]. However, since the 1970s, researchers started to bring the application of HME technology into the realm of drug-delivery systems due to its good dispersion effect [22]. Currently, HME is widely studied in the field of pharmaceutics as a method capable of preparing solid dispersions with improved release rates of low water-soluble active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs) [23]. HMEs can enhance the bioavailability of drug substances by melt blending them with hydrophilic or water-soluble polymers [24]. Hot-melt extruded wires can also be used in combination with 3D printing to broaden the possibility of designing new pharmaceutical dosage forms [25, 26].

Three-dimensional (3D) printer technology is a technology that manufactures 3D objects from digital models by depositing printing material layer by layer [27, 28]. 3D printing technology includes extrusion method, photo polymerization method, etc. [29], among which fused deposition modeling (FDM) technology is one of the most widely used technologies. FDM is a nozzle-based deposition system [30]. Due to its design flexibility and printing structure complexity, FDM can be used in the production of solid dosage forms with the desired drug release profile for personalized medicine [31]. At present, relevant research has been carried out to prepare suppository based on 3D printing technology [32, 33]. 3D printing can realize the simplicity of suppository preparing, and compared with ordinary suppository, 3D printing increases the drug loading capacity [34]. Benefiting from emerging drug preparation and design methods, the possibility of acetaminophen dosage form has been further explored, such as suppository [35], micelle [36], capsule [37], injection [38]. The high temperature resistance of acetaminophen enables it to conduct dosage form research and optimization through hot-melt extrusion and 3D printing method. However, the overall number of relevant studies remains small.

In this study, we prepared a new type of 3D printing suppository through the combination of hot melt extrusion technology and melt deposition molding technology. After excipients and process screening, the optimal prescription and process parameters were obtained, and its quality characteristics were evaluated.

Materials and methods

Reagents

Acetaminophen were purchased from Aladdin Biochemical Technology. Hydroxypropyl methyl cellulose (HPMC, 2%), Triethyl citrate (TEC), Polyvinylpyrrolidone vinyl acetate copolymer (kollidon VA64) and sodium hydroxide (NaOH) were purchased from Macklin Biochemical Technology. Ethyl cellulose (EC) and Polyplasdone (COP S-63) were purchased from Senjun Chemical Preparation and Accessories. Polyvinyl alcohol-polyethylene glycol copolymer (kollicoat IR) and polyethylene caprolactam-polyvinyl acetate-polyethylene glycol graft copolymer (soluplus) were purchased from Fengli Jingqiu Pharmaceutica. Acrylic resin (eudragit RS100) were purchased from Evonik Specialty Chemicals. Polyethylene glycol (PEG 6000) and triacetin (GTA) were purchased from Shanghai McLean Biochemical. Heparin sodium were purchased from Yuanye Biotechnolog. Dry yeast was purchased from Angel Yeast.

Acetaminophen suppository was purchased from Dongxin Co., Ltd. (China) with a dose of 0.15 g.

UV (ultraviolet) spectrogram of acetaminophen and excipients

Acetaminophen was weighed to 40 mg and dissolved in sodium hydroxide solution (0.4 mol/L). The solution was prepared to 8 μg/mL. Then all excipients (including ethyl cellulose, starch, lactose, hydroxypropyl methyl cellulose) were dissolved in sodium hydroxide solution and prepared to 0.2 mol/L. All solutions were scanned at the wavelength of 200 ~ 400 nm by UV spectrometer. Acetaminophen solutions of different concentration were prepared and detected by UV spectrometer (UV-1800). The absorbance and concentration were linearized to obtain a regression curve [39].

Preparation of thread materials by hot melt extrusion(HEM) technology

Prescription screening of thread materials was carried out by Orthogonal Cross method. Through changing carrying materials, extrusion temperature and extrusion characteristics of hot melt extruder (HAAKE MiniCTW), different extruded wires were prepared and evaluated. Triethyl citrate (TEC), glycerol triacetate (GTA), and PEG 6000 were selected as plasticizers [40]. Hydroxypropyl methylcellulose, starch and lactose were selected as porogen to accelerate the release rate of printed suppository [41]. The ratio of acetaminophen and carrying materials was initially set at 23:77. Acetaminophen and auxiliary materials were fully mixed and added into feed port, after which the mixed powder was squeezed out by hot melt extruder. The viscosity, smoothness, toughness, uniformity and diameter of extruded wire were compared [42].

Design and preparation of acetaminophen suppository by 3D printer

A torpedo-shaped suppository model was built by computer software (SoliWORK2020), and previously prepared wire materials were put into the 3D printer for preparation of suppositories. The printing conditions were set in the software as follows: extrusion speed: 30 r/min, extrusion temperature: 130 °C, printing temperature: 165 °C [32, 33, 43, 44]. The acetaminophen suppository was printed layer by layer on the platform through the nozzle of the 3D printer (BCN EPSILON W50 SC). The dosage of the final suppository was 0.15 g with the size of 8 mm × 25 mm × 8 mm.

Drug content of acetaminophen suppository

The ultraviolet absorption spectrum (UV spectrum) of acetaminophen and excipients were detected by UV spectrometer (UV-1800, Shimadzu) with the wavelength of 200 ~ 400 nm. Then, the acetaminophen resolutions of different concentrations were prepared and measured at 257 nm wavelength by UV spectrophotometry [45]. The regression curve was obtained by linear regression of absorbance with concentration and least square method [46, 47]. Suppository equivalent to 40 mg acetaminophen was dissolved with 0.4% sodium hydroxide solution and diluted t0 250 mL. Then the solution was filtrated and measured at 257 nm by UV spectrophotometry to get the drug content condition of suppository.

Thermal analysis by differential scanning calorimetry (DSC)

The thermal properties of blank crucible, acetaminophen and suppository formulations were determined by differential scanning calorimeter (DSC, Mettler Toledo DSC1). 3 mg of different samples were precisely weighed and placed in a crucible for sample preparation. Samples were heated from room temperature to 180 °C. And the heating rate was set to 10 K/min [48].

Structural characterization by infrared spectroscopy (IR)

Acetaminophen, HPMC, lactose, EC, starch, and suppository powder were detected by Fourier transform infrared spectrometer (TENSOR27), with KBr as the detection matrix, and the wavenumber range was set at 400 ~ 4000 cm−1 [49].

Structural characterization by X-ray diffractometer

Acetaminophen, mixed materials powder and suppository powder were detected by X-ray diffractometer (XRD-1600) [50]. The detection parameter was set at: the range was 5°~90°, the 2θ was 0.01, and the voltage was 30 KV.

Disintegration test

The disintegration time of 3D-printed acetaminophen suppository was measured using melting time limit tester (Split typeYJ-7000). The water bath temperature of the disintegration tester was set to 37 °C, the mode was set to P2, the running time was set to 60 minutes, and the metal rack was set to automatically flip every 10 minutes. Parallel of three suppository were detected.

In vitro drug release

The drug release profiles of 3D printed acetominphen suppository were tested in vitro by extracorporeal dissolution instrument (Elite 8) at 37 °C. 900 mL dissolved medium (phosphate buffer, pH = 7.4) was prepared for simulant, and after the suppository was put in, dissolved medium was taken and detected at 1、2、3、4、5、6、7、8 h. Scanning electron analyses of selected wire

The micro-morphology of selected wire was observed by scanning electron microscope (SEM, FlexSEM1000II). Prepared wire was placed on a conductive adhesive and was vacuum gold spared for 15 seconds [54]. The surface and cross-section were observed under SEM and smoothness and uniformity of the wire surface were evaluated.

Pharmacokinetic effects of 3D printed suppository

The pharmacokinetic and pharmacokinetic effects of 3D printed suppository were studied in New Zealand rabbit (2.5 kg, Wu’s animal, China). The rabbits were divided into the market preparation group (The dosage of acetaminophen is 0.15 g) and the 3D printing preparation group (The dosage of acetaminophen is 0.15 g), with 3 rabbits in each group. The basic temperature of each rabbit was measured, and blood samples of each rabbit without treatment was taken. Then the rabbit fever model was constructed by dry yeast suspension. The anal temperature of rabbits was monitored after subcutaneous injection of dry yeast suspension (20%) [55, 56]. The rabbits wwere then divided into two groups, with one group consisting of three rabbits. Two groups of rabbits were treated with off the shelf purchased suppository or 3D printed suppository, respectively. After administration, the temperature was further measured and the blood samples were furthered taken at the time of 1, 3, 4, 6, 8 and 12 h. The collected blood samples were centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 10 minutes in a high-speed centrifuge, after which it was was transferred to a 5.0 mL EP tube. Then, 0.2 mL of the supernatant was taken, mixed with 1 mL of methanol to precipitate proteins, and vortexed for 30 seconds. The mixture was then centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 10 minutes. After the methanol was evaporated, methanol was added dropwise. The mixture was vortexed until the precipitate redissolved. After the precipitate was fully dissolved, the mixture was centrifuged again at 12,000 rpm for 10 minutes. Finally, the supernatant was collected, filtered through a microporous membrane, and then analyzed using high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC, HPLC-2030, WondaSil C18-WR, the mobile phase is methanol: water = 90:10, the flow rate is 1.0 ml/min, the detection wavelength is 245 nm) [57].

All animal experiments were compliant with the ARRIVE guidelines and carried out in accordance with the U.K. Animals (Scientific Procedures) Act, 1986 and associated guidelines

To better improve the formability of the wire, the printing smoothness of the wire was also examined. Finally, screening results of dosage for each prescription showed that the wire with 15, 20 and 25% of the main drug content was smooth in the printing process, and the appearance of the suppository was good. When the drug content was 30%, it was easy to block the nozzle of the printer and cannot print the suppository, so the maximum drug content of 25% is preferred (Table 3). By evaluating the printing status of suppository, the optimal prescription was selected as APAP: EC: HPMC: lactose: starch = 25:10:25:10:30. After excipients were determined, the further process of screening was also carried out. The printing temperature of a 3D printer determines the degree of melting of the wire in the nozzle. Low printing temperatures would have resulted in uneven printing process and poor formability of suppository. While high printing temperature would have resulted in accumulation of wire and uneven stacking between layers. According to the figure obtained from the single factor experiment, the final printing temperature was set at 165 °C and the printing platform temperature was set at 60 °C. After preparation, the integration situation of APAP and excipients was detected by DSC. The results of DSC showed that acetaminophen appears as a crystalline structure with a melting point of 172 °C, while the mixture containing hydroxypropyl methyl cellulose, ethyl cellulose, starch, and lactose acted as an amorphous structure. The drug-loaded suppository also appeared as an amorphous structure (Fig. 3A). The IR results show that the peak intensity of acetaminophen at 3000 cm−1 decline, indicating that acetaminophen can be transformed into an amorphous structure after hot melt extrusion and melt deposition printing, and evenly distributed in the excipients (Fig. 3B) [61]. At the same time, the X-ray powder diffraction results further proved this conclusion [62]. In this diffraction pattern show that acetaminophen was observed at 2 θ = 15, 17, 24 and there was no significant detection signal of drug-loaded suppository (Fig. 3C). This results further indicated that during the melting process, acetaminophen will become an amorphous structure and be dispersed in the excipients [63]. Then, the concentration of acetaminophen was detected by UV spectrophotometry using the standard curve method (Figure S1). The drug-released profile in vitro showed that acetaminophen suppository gradually releases the drug within 6 hours, and the cumulative release of the drug reaches over 90% (Fig. 3D). Electron microscopy observation was conducted on the final selected wire. The overall shape of the wire was smooth and the cross-section is uniform, indicating a good distribution of drugs in the excipients (Fig. 3E and 3F). The disintegration test of acetaminophen suppository were measured at a temperature of 37 °C [64]. Discussion

The research integrates hot-melt extrusion (HME) technology with fused deposition modeling (FDM)-type 3D printing technology, ensuring the uniform distribution of excipients and drugs, thereby broadening the possibilities for spatial structural design in pharmaceutical dosage form development. With these encouraging preliminary results, additional research on the design of suppository structures is warranted. Through model construction and 3D printing, it is expected that new structures will be designed to optimize formulations [71–73]. For example, by designing core-shell suppository and injecting drug formulations such as suspensions and liquids into the center, the drug release rate and bioavailability could, in theory, be increased. Alternatively, a new type of excipient suppository could be designed using hot melt extrusion melt deposition technology, allowing the suppository to absorb water and expand or adhere to the wall, thereby increasing its retention time.

Conclusion

This study integrates hot melt extrusion and melt deposition molding technologies for, acetaminophen delivery as a suppository. It involves commonly used excipients to obtain the optimal prescription and process parameters. The optimized formula consists of APAP: plasticized EC: HPMC: starch: lactose = 25: 10: 25: 30: 10, and TEC dosage of 20% EC. Process parameters include an extrusion speed of 30 r/min, extrusion temperature of 130 °C, and printing temperature of 165 °C. After optimization, the resulting suppository exhibits a well-defined appearance, smooth interlayer stacking, and it meets content specifications. The cumulative percentage of in vitro release is over 90% within 6 hours, and acetaminophen is stable at the experimental temperature. It was proved that the pharmacokinetic curves of 3D printed acetaminophen suppository are flatter than those suppository obtained commerically.

This study successfully synthesized sustained-release 3D-printed acetaminophen suppository, affirming the viability of utilizing 3D printing for personalized suppository design [74]. The efficiency of integrating hot melt extrusion technology into the 3D printing process was also demonstrated. High-quality drug-loaded filaments were the cornerstone for the creation of 3D models, while FDM 3D printing slows down drug release by enhancing the density of the molten material, consequently delaying drug metabolism and excretion within the body [75]. Through FDM 3D printing technology, there is potential to achieve drug release control, personalized dosage customization, digitalization and automation of the production process, and more efficient research and development cycles.

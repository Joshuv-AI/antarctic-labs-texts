Simple Harish-Chandra modules over the superconformal current algebra
Yan He, Dong Liu, Yan Wang

Abstract
In this paper, we classify the simple Harish-Chandra modules over the superconformal current algebra 𝔤 ^ \widehat{\mathfrak{g}} , which is the semi-direct sum of the N = 1 N=1 superconformal algebra with the affine Lie superalgebra 𝔤 ˙ ⊗ 𝒜 ⊕ ℂ ​ C 1 \dot{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes\mathcal{A}\oplus\mathbb{C}C_{1} , where 𝔤 ˙ \dot{\mathfrak{g}} is a finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra, and 𝒜 \mathcal{A} is the tensor product of the Laurent polynomial algebra and the Grassmann algebra. As an application, we can directly get the classification of the simple Harish-Chandra modules over the N = 1 N=1 Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra.

Introduction

It is well known that the Virasoro algebra is the simplest case of the superconformal algebras (corresponding to
N=0
)(
[
15
]
). The Virasoro algebra has been widely used in many fields of mathematics and physics (see
[
13
,
14
]
and the references therein). There is more than one super extension of the Virasoro algebra since early 70’s.
The simplest are the
N=1
superconformal algebras including the
N=1
Neveu-Schwarz and Ramond algebras. These infinite-dimensional Lie superalgebras are also called the super-Virasoro algebras, since they can be viewed as supersymmetry extensions of the Virasoro algebra. The complete classification of simple Harish-Chandra modules over the
N=1
superconformal algebras was given in
[
24
]
. Recently, by using a simpler approach, the classification of simple Harish-Chandra modules over the
N=1
Neveu-Schwarz and Ramond algebras was given in
[
5
]
and
[
6
]
, respectively.

As a supersymmetric extension of affine-Virasoro algebra, the superconformal current algebra
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
introduced in
[
16
]
is the semi-direct sum of the
N=1
superconformal algebra with the affine Lie superalgebra
\dot{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes\mathcal{A}\oplus\mathbb{C}C_{1}
, where
\dot{\mathfrak{g}}
is a finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra, and
\mathcal{A}
is the tensor product of the Laurent polynomial algebra and the Grassmann algebra. This supersymmetric extension is implicit in recent work on superstring also admits a local field interpretation (
[
25
]
). Here, we will consider the Ramond case. Recently many researches of the superconformal current algebra have appeared in mathematics and physics, see the references for details (
[
8
,
11
,
12
,
18
,
19
,
21
]
).

Based on the classification of simple jet modules introduced by Y. Billig in
[
2
]
(also see
[
9
]
), a complete classification of simple Harish-Chandra modules over Lie algebra
of vector fields on a torus was given in
[
3
]
by the
\mathcal{A}
-cover theory. Recently, with the
\mathcal{A}
-cover theory, all simple Harish-Chandra modules were classified over the Witt superalgebra (
[
26
]
) (also see
[
4
]
), the
N=1
superconformal algebra (
[
5
,
6
]
) and the map (super)algebra related to the Virasoro
algebra (
[
7
]
). In this paper, we use this method to classify simple Harish-Chandra modules over the superconformal current algebra
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
and get the following main result.

Main theorem
(Theorem
5.3
)
Let
M
be a simple Harish-Chandra module over the superconformal current algebra
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
.
Then
M
is a highest weight module, a lowest weight module, or a simple quotient of a tensor module
\Gamma(\lambda,V,b)
or
\Pi(\Gamma(\lambda,V,b))

for some finite-dimensional simple

-module

As an application, we can get the classification of simple Harish-Chandra modules over the

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we give some definitions and preliminaries. In Section 3, we introduce the definitions and basic conclusions of the

2
Preliminaries

Throughout this paper, we denote by
\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z}_{+},\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbb{C}
the sets of all integers, nonnegative integers, positive integers and complex numbers, respectively. All algebras, modules and vector spaces are assumed to be over
\mathbb{C}
. A super vector space
V=V_{\bar{0}}\oplus V_{\bar{1}}
is a vector space endowed with a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-gradation. The parity of a homogeneous element
v\in V_{\bar{i}}
is denoted by
|v|=\bar{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. When we write
|v|

for an element
v\in V
, we will always assume that
v
is a homogeneous element.

Definition 2.1
.

The
N=1
Ramond algebra
\mathfrak{s}
is a Lie superalgebra with basis
\{L_{i},G_{i},C\mid i\in\mathbb{Z}\}
and brackets

\displaystyle[L_{i},L_{j}]=(j-i)L_{i+j}+\delta_{i+j,0}\frac{1}{12}(i^{3}-i)C,

\displaystyle[L_{i},G_{j}]=(j-\frac{i}{2})G_{i+j},

\displaystyle[G_{i},G_{j}]=-2L_{i+j}+\delta_{i+j,0}\frac{1}{12}(4i^{2}-1)C

for any
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
.

The even part of
\mathfrak{s}
is spanned by
\{L_{i},C\mid i\in\mathbb{Z}\}
, and is isomorphic to the Virasoro algebra, which is the universal central extension of the Witt algebra

\mathcal{W}
. The odd part of
\mathfrak{s}
is spanned by
\{G_{i}\mid i\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. Let
{\overline{\mathfrak{s}}}
be the quotient algebra
\mathfrak{s}/\mathbb{C}C
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{C}[t^{\pm 1}]\otimes\Lambda(1)
be the tensor product of the Laurent polynomial algebra in even variable
t
and the Grassmann algebra in odd variable
\xi
.

\mathcal{A}
is an
{\overline{\mathfrak{s}}}
-module with

\displaystyle L_{i}\cdot f=t^{i+1}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(f)+\frac{i}{2}t^{i}\xi\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}(f),

\displaystyle G_{i}\cdot f=t^{i+1}\xi\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(f)-t^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}(f),

where
i\in\mathbb{Z},f\in\mathcal{A}
. We have
[a,f]=a\cdot f
for
a\in{\overline{\mathfrak{s}}}
and
f\in\mathcal{A}
.
On the other hand, the paper
[
5
]
shows that
\overline{\mathfrak{s}}
has an
\mathcal{A}
-module structure

\displaystyle t^{i}L_{n}=L_{n+i},\ t^{i}G_{n}=G_{n+i},\ \xi L_{n}=\frac{1}{2}G_{n},\ \xi G_{n}=0,\ \forall i,n\in\mathbb{Z}.

For a finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra
\dot{\mathfrak{g}}
, we have
\dot{\mathfrak{g}}=[\dot{\mathfrak{g}},\dot{\mathfrak{g}}]
and
\dot{\mathfrak{g}}
has a non-degenerated invariant bilinear form

(\cdot~|~\cdot)
. In this case
\dot{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes\mathcal{A}
is a Lie superalgebra, which is called the super loop algebra (see
[
16
]
), with

[x\otimes f,y\otimes g]=[x,y]\otimes fg

for all
x,y\in\dot{\mathfrak{g}},f,g\in\mathcal{A}
. Obviously,
\dot{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes\mathcal{A}
has a natural
\mathcal{A}
-module structure

\displaystyle t^{i}(x\otimes t^{n})=x\otimes t^{n+i},\ t^{i}(x\otimes t^{n}\xi)=x\otimes t^{n+i}\xi,

\displaystyle\xi(x\otimes t^{n})=x\otimes t^{n}\xi,\ \ \xi(x\otimes t^{n}\xi)=0

for
i,n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
x\in\dot{\mathfrak{g}}
. Meanwhile,
\dot{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes\mathcal{A}
becomes an
\overline{\mathfrak{s}}
-module by the usual actions of
\overline{\mathfrak{s}}
on
\mathcal{A}
.
So we can define a Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}
associated to
\dot{\mathfrak{g}}
as
\mathfrak{g}=\overline{\mathfrak{s}}\ltimes(\dot{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes\mathcal{A})
.
Since
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]=\mathfrak{g}
, we can get the universal central extension
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
of
\mathfrak{g}
by direct calculation, which is just the superconformal current algebra in
[
16
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

[
16
]

Let
\dot{\mathfrak{g}}
be a finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra with a non-degenerated invariant bilinear form

(\cdot~|~\cdot)
, then the superconformal current algebra
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathfrak{s}\ltimes(\dot{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes\mathcal{A}\oplus\mathbb{C}C_{1})
with the following brackets:

\displaystyle[L_{i},L_{j}]=(j-i)L_{i+j}+\delta_{i+j,0}\frac{1}{12}(i^{3}-i)C,

\displaystyle[L_{i},G_{j}]=(j-\frac{i}{2})G_{i+j},

\displaystyle[G_{i},G_{j}]=-2L_{i+j}+\delta_{i+j,0}\frac{1}{12}(4i^{2}-1)C,

\displaystyle[L_{i},x\otimes t^{j}\xi]=(j+\frac{i}{2})x\otimes t^{i+j}\xi,

\displaystyle[L_{i},x\otimes t^{j}]=jx\otimes t^{i+j},\quad[G_{i},x\otimes t^{j}\xi]=-x\otimes t^{i+j},

\displaystyle[G_{i},x\otimes t^{j}]=jx\otimes t^{i+j}\xi,

\displaystyle[x\otimes t^{i}\xi,y\otimes t^{j}]=[x,y]\otimes t^{i+j}\xi,

\displaystyle[x\otimes t^{i},y\otimes t^{j}]=[x,y]\otimes t^{i+j}+i\delta_{i+j,0}(x,y)C_{1},

\displaystyle[x\otimes t^{i}\xi,y\otimes t^{j}\xi]=\delta_{i+j,0}(x,y)C_{1},

where
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
and
x,y\in\dot{\mathfrak{g}}
.

It is clear that
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
has a
\mathbb{Z}
-grading by the eigenvalues of the adjoint action of
L_{0}
. Then

\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{n}=\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{+}\oplus\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}\oplus\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{-},

where

\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\pm}=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\pm n},\ \ \widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}=\dot{\mathfrak{g}}\oplus\mathbb{C}C\oplus\mathbb{C}C_{1}\oplus\mathbb{C}L_{0}\oplus\mathbb{C}G_{0}.

Let
\dot{\mathfrak{h}}
be the Cartan subalgebra of
\dot{\mathfrak{g}}
. Then
\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}=\dot{\mathfrak{h}}\oplus\mathbb{C}C\oplus\mathbb{C}C_{1}\oplus\mathbb{C}L_{0}
is the cartan subalgebra of
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
. A highest weight module over
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
is characterized by its highest weight
\Lambda\in\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
and highest weight vector
v_{0}
such that

(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{+}\oplus\dot{\mathfrak{g}}_{+})v_{0}=0
and
hv_{0}=\Lambda(h)v_{0},\forall h\in\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}
.

Set
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}}\ltimes\mathcal{A}
. A
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
-module
V
is called an
\mathcal{A}\mathfrak{g}
-module if
\mathcal{A}
acts associatively,
i.e.,

t^{0}v=v,fgv=f(gv),\forall f,g\in\mathcal{A},v\in V.

Let
\mathfrak{l}
be any of the Lie superalgerba
\mathfrak{g},\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
and
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
. An

\mathfrak{l}
-module
V
is called a weight module if the action of
L_{0}
on
V
is diagonalizable, i.e.,
V=\bigoplus\limits_{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}}V_{\lambda}
, where

V_{\lambda}=\{v\in V\mid L_{0}v=\lambda v\}
. The support of a weight module
V
is defined by
\mbox{supp}(V)=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\mid V_{\lambda}\neq 0\}
. A weight

\mathfrak{l}
-module
V
is called Harish-Chandra if
\dim V_{\lambda}<\infty,\forall\lambda\in\mbox{supp}(V)
, and is called cuspidal or uniformly bounded if there exists
some
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\dim V_{\lambda}\leq N,\forall\lambda\in\mbox{supp}(V)
. Clearly, if
V
is simple, then
\mbox{supp}(V)\subseteq\lambda+\mathbb{Z}
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
.

Let
\sigma:L\rightarrow L^{\prime}
be any homomorphism of Lie superalgebras or associative superalgebras and
V
be any
L^{\prime}
-module. Then
V
becomes an
L
-module, denoted
by
V^{\sigma}
, with
x\cdot v=\sigma(x)v
for
x\in L,v\in V
. Denote
T
be the automorphism of
L
defined by
T(x)=(-1)^{|x|}x,\forall x\in L
. For any
L
-module
V
,

\Pi(V)
is the module defined by a parity-change of
V
with
\Pi(V)_{\bar{0}}=V_{\bar{1}},\Pi(V)_{\bar{1}}=V_{\bar{0}}
and the actions of
L
on
\Pi(V)
is the same as that of
V
.

A module
M
over an associative superalgebra
B
is called strictly simple if it is a simple module over the associative algebra
B
(forgetting the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-gradation).

We need the following results on tensor modules over tensor superalgebras.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
26
, Lemma 2.1, 2.2]

Let
B,B^{\prime}
be unital associative superalgebras, and
M,M^{\prime}
be
B,B^{\prime}
modules, respectively.

1.
M\otimes M^{\prime}\cong\Pi(M)\otimes\Pi(M^{\prime T})
as
B\otimes B^{\prime}
-modules.

2. If
B^{\prime}
has a countable basis and
M^{\prime}
is strictly simple, then

(1) any
B\otimes B^{\prime}
-submodule of
M\otimes M^{\prime}
is of the form
N\otimes M^{\prime}
for some
B
-submodule
N
of
M
;

(2) any simple quotient of the
B\otimes B^{\prime}
-module
M\otimes M^{\prime}
is isomorphic to some
\overline{M}\otimes M^{\prime}
for some simple quotient
\overline{M}
of
M
;

(3)
M\otimes M^{\prime}
is a simple
B\otimes B^{\prime}
-module if and only if
M
is a simple
B
-module;

(4) if
V
is a simple
B\otimes B^{\prime}
-module containing a strictly simple
B^{\prime}=\mathbb{C}\otimes B^{\prime}
-module

, then
V\cong M\otimes M^{\prime}
for some simple
B
-module

M
.

3
\mathcal{A}
-cover

As we all know, the key to the classification of the Harish-Chandra module of
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
is the classification of its cuspidal module.
Let
V
be a simple Harish-Chandra module over
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
. By Schur’s Lemma, we may assume that the central elements
C
and
C_{1}
act on
V
by scalars
c
and
c_{1}
respectively.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that
V=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}}V_{\lambda}
is a simple cuspidal module over
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
. Then the actions of central elements
C
and
C_{1}

on
V
are trival.

Proof.

Since
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
has a Virasoro subalgebra with basis
\{L_{i},C\mid i\in\mathbb{Z}\}
, and an untwisted affine Lie subalgebra with basis
\{x\otimes t^{i},C_{1}\mid x\in\dot{\mathfrak{g}},i\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. According to Lemma 3.2 of
[
17
]
, we have
c=c_{1}=0
.
∎

From the above lemma, we know that the category of simple cuspidal
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
-modules is naturally equivalent to the category of simple cuspidal
\mathfrak{g}
-modules.
In order to study cuspidal modules of
\mathfrak{g}
, we introduce the concept of
\mathcal{A}
-cover. For simplicity, we denote
\dot{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes\mathcal{A}
by
I
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
M
be a
\mathfrak{g}
-module. Then, the tensor product
I\otimes M
is an
\mathcal{A}\mathfrak{g}
-module by the following actions of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle a(w\otimes v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[a,w]\otimes v+(-1)^{|a||w|}w\otimes a(v),

(3.1)

\displaystyle f(w\otimes v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(w)\otimes v,

(3.2)

for
a\in\mathfrak{g},f\in\mathcal{A},w\in I
and
v\in M
. Furthermore, if
M
is a weight module, then so is
I\otimes M
.

Proof.

Note that
I
is the ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
. So
I\otimes M
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-module with the adjoint
\mathfrak{g}
-action and
\mathcal{A}
-module acting as (
3.2
).
Hence
I\otimes M
is a
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
-module because

\displaystyle a(f(w\otimes v))-(-1)^{|a||f|}f(a(w\otimes v))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a(f(w)\otimes v)-(-1)^{|a||f|}f([a,w]\otimes v+(-1)^{|a||w|}w\otimes a(v))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[a,f(w)]\otimes v+(-1)^{|a||f(w)|}f(w)\otimes a(v)-(-1)^{|a||f|}f([a,w])\otimes v

\displaystyle-(-1)^{|a|(|f|+|w|)}f(w)\otimes a(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a(f)(w)\otimes v+(-1)^{|a||f|}f([a,w])\otimes v-(-1)^{|a||f|}f([a,w])\otimes v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[a,f](w\otimes v)

for homogeneous
a\in\mathfrak{g},f\in\mathcal{A},w\in I
and
v\in M
. Obviously, the action of
\mathcal{A}
on
I\otimes M
is associative. So
I\otimes M
is an
\mathcal{A}\mathfrak{g}
-module.

Suppose
M
is a weight module, then
I\otimes M
is spanned by
w\otimes v
with
w
and
v
being eigenvectors with respect to the action of
L_{0}
. By (
3.1
), we get that
w\otimes v
is also an eigenvector for the action of
L_{0}
. So
I\otimes M
is a weight module.
∎

Let
K(M)=\{\sum\limits_{i}w_{i}\otimes v_{i}\in I\otimes M\mid\sum\limits_{i}(fw_{i})v_{i}=0,\ \forall f\in\mathcal{A}\}
. Then it is easy to see that
K(M)
is an
\mathcal{A}\mathfrak{g}
-submodule of
I\otimes M
. Hence we have the
\mathcal{A}\mathfrak{g}
-module
\widehat{M}=(I\otimes M)/K(M)
. As in
[
3
]
, we call
\widehat{M}
the
\mathcal{A}
-cover of
M
if
IM=M
.

Clearly, the linear map

\begin{split}\pi:\ \ \widehat{M}&\to\ \ \ IM,\\
w\otimes v+K(M)\ &\mapsto\ \ wv,\quad\forall\ w\in I,v\in M\end{split}

is a
\mathfrak{g}
-module epimorphism.

Recall that in
[
3
]
, the authors show that every cuspidal
\mathcal{W}
-module is annihilated by the operators
\Omega_{k,p}^{(m)}
for
m
large enough.

Lemma 3.3
.

[
3
, Corollary 3.7]

For every
l\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
k,p\in\mathbb{Z}
the differentiators
\Omega_{k,p}^{(m)}=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m}(-1)^{i}\binom{m}{i}L_{k-i}L_{p+i}
annihilate
every cuspidal
\mathcal{W}
-module with a composition series of length
l
.

Let
M
be a cuspidal
\mathfrak{g}
-module. Obviously,
M
is a cuspidal
\mathcal{W}
-module and hence there exists
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\Omega_{k,p}^{(m)}M=0,\forall k,p\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Therefore,
[\Omega_{k,p}^{(m)},x\otimes t^{j}]M=0,\forall k,p,j\in\mathbb{Z},x\in\dot{\mathfrak{g}}
. In Lemma 4.4 of
[
7
]
, the authors show that

\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m+2}(-1)^{i}\binom{m+2}{i}(x\otimes t^{k+j+1-i})L_{p-1+i}=0

on
M
. Similarly, from
[\Omega_{k,p}^{(m)},x\otimes t^{j}\xi]M=0
and
[L_{i},x\otimes t^{j}\xi]=(j+\frac{i}{2})x\otimes t^{i+j}\xi,\forall i,j,k,p\in\mathbb{Z},x\in\dot{\mathfrak{g}}
, we have

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle[\Omega_{k,p-1}^{(m)},x\otimes t^{j+1}\xi]-2[\Omega_{k,p}^{(m)},x\otimes t^{j}\xi]+[\Omega_{k,p+1}^{(m)},x\otimes t^{j-1}\xi]-[\Omega_{k+1,p-1}^{(m)},x\otimes t^{j}\xi]

\displaystyle+2[\Omega_{k+1,p}^{(m)},x\otimes t^{j-1}\xi]-[\Omega_{k+1,p+1}^{(m)},x\otimes t^{j-2}\xi]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m}(-1)^{i}\binom{m}{i}\big(x\otimes t^{k+j+1-i}\xi L_{p-1+i}-2x\otimes t^{k+j-i}\xi L_{p+i}+x\otimes t^{k-i-1+j}\xi L_{p+1+i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m+1}(-1)^{i-1}\binom{m+2}{i+1}x\otimes t^{k+j-i}\xi L_{p+i}+x\otimes t^{k+j+1}\xi L_{p-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m+2}(-1)^{i}\binom{m+2}{i}x\otimes t^{k+j+1-i}\xi L_{p-1+i}

on
M
. Thus, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
M
be a cuspidal
\mathfrak{g}
-module. Then there exists
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
k,p\in\mathbb{Z}
and
x\in\dot{\mathfrak{g}}
, the operators

\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m}(-1)^{i}\binom{m}{i}yL_{p+i}
annihilate
M
, where
y\in\{x\otimes t^{k-i},x\otimes t^{k-i}\xi\}.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
M
be a cuspidal module for the Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}=\overline{\mathfrak{s}}\ltimes(\dot{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes\mathcal{A})
.
Then its
\mathcal{A}
-cover
\widehat{M}
is cuspidal.

Proof.

Since
\widehat{M}
is an
\mathcal{A}
-module, it is sufficient to show that one of its nonzero weight spaces is finite-dimensional. Fix a nonzero weight
\alpha+p,\ p\in\mathbb{Z}
and let us prove that
\widehat{M}_{\alpha+p}=\mbox{span}\{(x\otimes t^{p-k})\otimes M_{\alpha+k},(x\otimes t^{p-k}\xi)\otimes M_{\alpha+k}\mid k\in\mathbb{Z},x\in\dot{\mathfrak{g}}\}
is finite-dimensional.

We will prove by induction on
|q|
for
q\in\mathbb{Z}
that for all
u\in M_{\alpha+q}
,

\displaystyle(x\otimes t^{p-q})\otimes u,(x\otimes t^{p-q}\xi)\otimes u\in

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{|k|\leq\frac{m}{2}}\Big((x\otimes t^{p-k})\otimes M_{\alpha+k}+(x\otimes t^{p-k}\xi)\otimes M_{\alpha+k}\Big)

\displaystyle+K(M).

Assume that this is true for
|q^{\prime}|<|q|
. Now let us prove that this is true for
q
.
If
|q|\leq\frac{m}{2}
, the claim holds. If
|q|>\frac{m}{2}
, we may assume
q<-\frac{m}{2}
.
The proof for
q>\frac{m}{2}
is similar. Since
L_{0}
acts on
M_{\alpha+q}
with a nonzero scalar,
we can write
u=L_{0}v
for some
v\in M_{\alpha+q}
. Then

(x\otimes t^{p-q})\otimes L_{0}v=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m}(-1)^{i}\binom{m}{i}(x\otimes t^{p-q-i})\otimes L_{i}v-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}(-1)^{i}\binom{m}{i}(x\otimes t^{p-q-i})\otimes L_{i}v.

Note that
I=\dot{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes\mathcal{A}
has a natural
\mathcal{A}
-module structure. Hence, we have

\displaystyle t^{r}\Big(\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m}(-1)^{i}\binom{m}{i}(x\otimes t^{j-i})L_{p+i}v\Big)=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m}(-1)^{i}\binom{m}{i}(x\otimes t^{r+j-i})L_{p+i}v=0,

\displaystyle t^{r}\xi\Big(\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m}(-1)^{i}\binom{m}{i}(x\otimes t^{j-i})L_{p+i}v\Big)=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m}(-1)^{i}\binom{m}{i}(x\otimes t^{r+j-i})\xi L_{p+i}v=0

by Lemma
3.4
, which implies that

\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m}(-1)^{i}\binom{m}{i}(x\otimes t^{j-i})\otimes L_{p+i}v\in K(M)

for all
i,j,p\in\mathbb{Z},x\in\dot{\mathfrak{g}}
and
v\in M
.
Since
|q+i|\leq\frac{m}{2}(1\leq i\leq m)
, we have

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}(-1)^{i}\binom{m}{i}(x\otimes t^{p-q-i})\otimes L_{i}v\in\sum\limits_{|k|\leq\frac{m}{2}}\Big((x\otimes t^{p-k})\otimes M_{\alpha+k}+(x\otimes t^{p-k}\xi)\otimes M_{\alpha+k}\Big)+K(M)

by induction assumption.
So does
(x\otimes t^{p-q})\otimes L_{0}v
. Similarly, we have

(x\otimes t^{p-q}\xi)\otimes L_{0}v\in\sum\limits_{|k|\leq\frac{m}{2}}\Big((x\otimes t^{p-k})\otimes M_{\alpha+k}+(x\otimes t^{p-k}\xi)\otimes M_{\alpha+k}\Big)+K(M).

Therefore, the lemma follows from the fact that
\dim M_{\alpha+k}<\infty
for any fixed
k
and
\dot{\mathfrak{g}}
is finite-dimensional.
∎

4

\mathfrak{g}

We denote by

be the universal enveloping algebra of the Lie (super)algebra

. Let
{\mathcal{I}}
be the left ideal of
\mathcal{U}=U(\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}})
generated by

,
t^{0}-1
,
t^{i}\cdot\xi-t^{i}\xi
and
\xi\cdot\xi
for all

. Then it is clear that
\mathcal{I}
is an ideal of
\mathcal{U}
. Now we have the quotient algebra
\overline{\mathcal{U}}=\mathcal{U}/\mathcal{I}=(U(\mathfrak{g})U(\mathcal{A}))/\mathcal{I}
. From PBW theorem, we may identify
\mathcal{A}
,
\mathfrak{g}
with their images in
\overline{\mathcal{U}}
. Thus
\overline{\mathcal{U}}=\mathcal{A}\cdot U(\mathfrak{g})
. Then the category of
\mathcal{A}\mathfrak{g}
-modules
is equivalent to the category of
\overline{\mathcal{U}}
-modules.

For
i\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\},m\in\mathbb{Z}
and
x\in\dot{\mathfrak{g}}
, let

\displaystyle X_{i}=t^{-i}\cdot L_{i}+\frac{i}{2}t^{-i}\xi\cdot G_{i}-L_{0},

\displaystyle Y_{i}=t^{-i}\cdot G_{i}-2t^{-i}\xi\cdot L_{i}-G_{0}+2\xi\cdot L_{0},

\displaystyle\overline{X}_{m}^{x}=t^{-m}\cdot(x\otimes t^{m})+mt^{-m}\xi\cdot(x\otimes t^{m}\xi),

\displaystyle\overline{Y}_{m}^{x}=t^{-m}\xi\cdot(x\otimes t^{m})-t^{-m}\cdot(x\otimes t^{m}\xi)

be elements in
\overline{\mathcal{U}}
and
\mathcal{T}
be the subspace of
\overline{\mathcal{U}}
spanned by
\{X_{i},Y_{i},\overline{X}_{m}^{x},\overline{Y}_{m}^{x}\mid i\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\},m\in\mathbb{Z},x\in\dot{\mathfrak{g}}\}
.
In Proposition 3.1 of
[
5
]
, the authors show that

\displaystyle[G_{0},X_{i}]=[G_{0},Y_{i}]=[t^{n},X_{i}]=[t^{n},Y_{i}]=[\xi,X_{i}]=[\xi,Y_{i}]=0,

and

\displaystyle[X_{i},X_{j}]=-jX_{j}+iX_{i}+(j-i)X_{i+j},

\displaystyle[X_{i},Y_{j}]=-jY_{j}+\frac{i}{2}Y_{i}+(j-\frac{i}{2})Y_{i+j},

\displaystyle[Y_{i},Y_{j}]=2(X_{i}+X_{j}-X_{i+j})

for
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}
. Just by simple calculation, we can get

[G_{0},\overline{X}_{m}^{x}]=[G_{0},\overline{Y}_{m}^{x}]=[t^{n},\overline{X}_{m}^{x}]=[t^{n},\overline{Y}_{m}^{x}]=[\xi,\overline{X}_{m}^{x}]=[\xi,\overline{Y}_{m}^{x}]=0,\forall m\in\mathbb{Z}.

Now, we have

\displaystyle[X_{i},\overline{X}_{m}^{x}]=

\displaystyle[t^{-i}\cdot L_{i}+\frac{i}{2}t^{-i}\xi\cdot G_{i}-L_{0},\ t^{-m}\cdot(x\otimes t^{m})+mt^{-m}\xi\cdot(x\otimes t^{m}\xi)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t^{-i}[L_{i},t^{-m}]\cdot(x\otimes t^{m})+t^{-(i+m)}\cdot[L_{i},x\otimes t^{m}]+mt^{-i}[L_{i},t^{-m}\xi]\cdot(x\otimes t^{m}\xi)

\displaystyle+mt^{-(i+m)}\xi\cdot[L_{i},x\otimes t^{m}\xi]+\frac{i}{2}t^{-i}\xi[G_{i},t^{-m}]\cdot(x\otimes t^{m})

\displaystyle+\frac{i}{2}t^{-(i+m)}\xi\cdot[G_{i},x\otimes t^{m}]+\frac{im}{2}t^{-i}\xi[G_{i},t^{-m}\xi]\cdot(x\otimes t^{m}\xi)-[L_{0},t^{-m}]\cdot(x\otimes t^{m})

\displaystyle-t^{-m}\cdot[L_{0},x\otimes t^{m}]-m[L_{0},t^{-m}\xi]\cdot(x\otimes t^{m}\xi)-mt^{-m}\xi\cdot[L_{0},x\otimes t^{m}\xi]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-mt^{-m}\cdot(x\otimes t^{m})+mt^{-(i+m)}\cdot(x\otimes t^{i+m})

\displaystyle+m(\frac{i}{2}-m)t^{-m}\xi\cdot(x\otimes t^{m}\xi)+m(\frac{i}{2}+m)t^{-(i+m)}\xi\cdot(x\otimes t^{i+m}\xi)

\displaystyle+\frac{im}{2}t^{-(i+m)}\xi\cdot(x\otimes t^{i+m}\xi)-\frac{im}{2}t^{-m}\xi\cdot(x\otimes t^{m}\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-m\overline{X}_{m}^{x}+m\overline{X}_{i+m}^{x}.

Similarly, we can get

\displaystyle[X_{i},\overline{Y}_{m}^{x}]=-m\overline{Y}_{m}^{x}+(\frac{i}{2}+m)\overline{Y}_{i+m}^{x},

\displaystyle[Y_{i},\overline{X}_{m}^{x}]=m\overline{Y}_{m}^{x}-m\overline{Y}_{i+m}^{x},\ \ [Y_{i},\overline{Y}_{m}^{x}]=-\overline{X}_{m}^{x}+\overline{X}_{i+m}^{x},

\displaystyle[\overline{X}_{m}^{x},\overline{X}_{n}^{y}]=\overline{X}_{m+n}^{[x,y]},\ \ [\overline{X}_{m}^{x},\overline{Y}_{n}^{y}]=\overline{Y}_{m+n}^{[x,y]},\ \ [\overline{Y}_{m}^{x},\overline{Y}_{n}^{y}]=0

for
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\},m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
. So we have the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

1.

[\mathcal{T},G_{0}]=[\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}]=0
;

2.

\mathcal{T}
is a Lie super subalgebra of
\overline{\mathcal{U}}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

The associative superalgebras
\overline{\mathcal{U}}
and
\mathcal{K}\otimes U(\mathcal{T})
are isomorphic, where
\mathcal{K}
is the Weyl superalgebra

\mathcal{A}[\frac{\partial}{\partial t},\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}]
.

Proof.

Note that
U(\mathcal{T})
is an associative subalgebra of
\overline{\mathcal{U}}
. Define the map
\tau:\mathcal{A}[G_{0}]\rightarrow\mathcal{K}
by

\tau|_{\mathcal{A}}=\mbox{Id}_{\mathcal{A}},\tau(G_{0})=t\xi\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}.

Obviously, we have

\tau(-t^{-1}G_{0}^{2})=\frac{\partial}{\partial t},\ \tau(-G_{0}-\xi G_{0}^{2})=\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi},\ \tau(t^{\pm 1})=t^{\pm 1},\ \tau(\xi)=\xi

and
\tau
is an isomorphism of associative superalgebras
\mathcal{A}[G_{0}]
and
\mathcal{K}
.

Then we define the map
\phi:\mathcal{A}[G_{0}]\otimes U(\mathcal{T})\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{U}}
by

\phi(t^{i}\xi^{r}G_{0}^{k}\otimes y)=t^{i}\xi^{r}\cdot G_{0}^{k}\cdot y+\mathcal{I},\ \forall i\in\mathbb{Z},r\in\{0,1\},k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},y\in U(\mathcal{T}).

The restrictions of
\phi
on
\mathcal{A}[G_{0}]
and
U(\mathcal{T})
are
well-defined homomorphisms of associative superalgebras. Note that
[\mathcal{T},G_{0}]=[\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}]=0
,
\phi
is a well-defined
homomorphism of associative superalgebras. From

\displaystyle\phi(t^{i}\otimes X_{i}-\frac{i}{2}t^{i}\xi\otimes Y_{i}+t^{i}L_{0}\otimes 1-\frac{i}{2}t^{i}\xi G_{0}\otimes 1)=L_{i},

\displaystyle\phi(t^{i}\otimes Y_{i}+2t^{i}\xi\otimes X_{i}+t^{i}G_{0}\otimes 1)=G_{i},

\displaystyle\phi(t^{i}\otimes\overline{X}_{i}^{x}+it^{i}\xi\otimes\overline{Y}_{i}^{x})=x\otimes t^{i},

\displaystyle\phi(t^{i}\xi\otimes\overline{X}_{i}^{x}-t^{i}\otimes\overline{Y}_{i}^{x})=x\otimes t^{i}\xi,

we can see that
\phi
is surjective.

By PBW theorem, we know that
\overline{\mathcal{U}}
has a basis of monomials in variables

\{L_{i},G_{i},x\otimes t^{m},x\otimes t^{m}\xi\mid i\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\},m\in\mathbb{Z},x\in\dot{\mathfrak{g}}\}
over
\mathcal{A}[G_{0}]
. Therefore
\overline{\mathcal{U}}

has an
\mathcal{A}[G_{0}]
-baisis consisting of monomials in the variables

\{t^{-i}\cdot L_{i}-L_{0},\ t^{-i}\cdot G_{i}-G_{0},\ \overline{X}_{m}^{x},\overline{Y}_{m}^{x}\mid i\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\},m\in\mathbb{Z},x\in\dot{\mathfrak{g}}\}
.
So
\phi
is injective and hence an isomorphism.
∎

.
Denote

. It is clear that
\mathcal{A}(\lambda)\cong\mathcal{K}/\mathcal{I}_{\lambda}
, where
\mathcal{I}_{\lambda}
is the left ideal of
\mathcal{K}

generated by

and
\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}
. We need the following lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

[
26
, Lemma 3.5]

Any simple weight
\mathcal{K}
-module is isomorphic to some
\mathcal{A}(\lambda)
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
up to a parity-change, where

\mathcal{A}(\lambda)
is a strictly simple
\mathcal{K}
-module.

Let
\mathfrak{m}
be the maximal ideal of
\mathcal{A}
generated by
t-1
and
\xi

is spanned by the set

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
k,l\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Then for all

and

, there is

\displaystyle[(t-1)^{k}L_{i},(t-1)^{l}L_{j}]=(l-k+j-i)(t-1)^{k+l}L_{i+j}+(l-k)(t-1)^{k+l-1}L_{i+j},

\displaystyle[(t-1)^{k}L_{i},(t-1)^{l}G_{j}]=(j-\frac{i}{2})(t-1)^{k+l}G_{i+j}+(l-\frac{k}{2})(t-1)^{k+l-1}G_{i+j+1},

\displaystyle[(t-1)^{k}G_{i},(t-1)^{l}G_{j}]=-2(t-1)^{k+l}L_{i+j},

\displaystyle[(t-1)^{k}L_{i},x\otimes(t-1)^{l}t^{j}]=jx\otimes(t-1)^{k+l}t^{i+j}+lx\otimes(t-1)^{k+l-1}t^{i+j+1},

\displaystyle[(t-1)^{k}L_{i},x\otimes(t-1)^{l}t^{j}\xi]=(j+\frac{i}{2})x\otimes(t-1)^{k+l}t^{i+j}\xi+(l+\frac{k}{2})x\otimes(t-1)^{k+l-1}t^{i+j+1}\xi,

\displaystyle[(t-1)^{k}G_{i},x\otimes(t-1)^{l}t^{j}]=jx\otimes(t-1)^{k+l}t^{i+j}\xi+lx\otimes(t-1)^{k+l-1}t^{i+j+1}\xi,

Proof.

The results follow from direct computations.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

For
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, let
\mathfrak{a}_{k}=(t-1)^{k+1}{\overline{\mathfrak{s}}}\ltimes(\dot{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathfrak{m}}^{k}\mathcal{A})
. Then

(1)

\mathfrak{a}_{0}
is a Lie super subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
;

(2)

\mathfrak{a}_{k}
is an ideal of
\mathfrak{a}_{0}
;

(3)

[\mathfrak{a}_{1},\mathfrak{a}_{k}]\subseteq\mathfrak{a}_{k+1}
;

(4)

The ideal of
\mathfrak{a}_{0}
generated by
(t-1)^{k}\mathcal{W}
contains
\mathfrak{a}_{k}
.

Proof.

Note that
\mathfrak{a}_{0}=\mbox{span}\{(t-1)L_{i},(t-1)G_{i},x\otimes t^{m},x\otimes t^{m}\xi\mid i\in\mathbb{Z},m\in\mathbb{Z},x\in\dot{\mathfrak{g}}\}
and

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}_{k}=\mbox{span}\{(t-1)^{k+1}L_{i},(t-1)^{k+1}G_{i},x\otimes(t-1)^{k}t^{i},x\otimes(t-1)^{k}t^{i}\xi,x\otimes(t-1)^{k-1}t^{i}\xi

\displaystyle\mid i\in\mathbb{Z},k\in\mathbb{N},x\in\dot{\mathfrak{g}}\}.

Therefore, the conclusions
(1)-(3)
can be verified directly by using Lemma
4.4
.

The ideal generated by
(t-1)^{k}L_{i}
in
\mathfrak{a}_{0}
is denoted by
\mathfrak{b}
. Since

[(t-1)^{k}L_{i},(t-1)L_{0}]-[(t-1)^{k}L_{0},(t-1)L_{i}]=-2i(t-1)^{k+1}L_{i},

we have
(t-1)^{k+1}L_{i}\in\mathfrak{b}
for
i\neq 0
. Furthermore, since

[(t-1)^{k}L_{1},(t-1)L_{-1}]-[(t-1)^{k}L_{-1},(t-1)L_{1}]=-4(t-1)^{k+1}L_{0}\in\mathfrak{b},

there is
(t-1)^{k+1}L_{i}\in\mathfrak{b}
for
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. Similarly, we can get
(t-1)^{k+1}G_{i},x\otimes(t-1)^{k}t^{i},x\otimes(t-1)^{k}t^{i}\xi,x\otimes(t-1)^{k-1}t^{i}\xi\in\mathfrak{b}
.
Hence,
\mathfrak{a}_{k}\subseteq\mathfrak{b}
and the conclusion (4) holds.
∎

Proposition 4.6
.

The Lie superalgebras
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathfrak{a}_{0}
are isomorphic.

Proof.

Since

\displaystyle(t-1)L_{i}=L_{i+1}-L_{i}=(L_{i+1}-L_{0})-(L_{i}-L_{0}),

\displaystyle(t-1)G_{i}=G_{i+1}-G_{i}=(G_{i+1}-G_{0})-(G_{i}-G_{0}),

we have
\mathfrak{a}_{0}=(t-1){\overline{\mathfrak{s}}}\ltimes(\dot{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes\mathcal{A})
is spanned by the set

\{L_{i}-L_{0},G_{i}-G_{0},x\otimes t^{m},x\otimes t^{m}\xi\mid i\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\},m\in\mathbb{Z},x\in\dot{\mathfrak{g}}\}
.
It is easy to verify that the linear map
\varphi:\mathcal{T}\rightarrow\mathfrak{a}_{0}
defined by

\displaystyle\varphi(X_{i})=L_{i}-L_{0},\ \ \varphi(Y_{i})=G_{i}-G_{0},

\displaystyle\varphi(\overline{X}_{m}^{x})=x\otimes t^{m},\ \ \varphi(\overline{Y}_{m}^{x})=-x\otimes t^{m}\xi

is a Lie superalgebra isomorphism.
∎

For any
\mathfrak{a}_{0}
-module
V
, we have the
\mathcal{A}\mathfrak{g}
-module
\Gamma(\lambda,V)=(\mathcal{A}(\lambda)\otimes V)^{\varphi_{1}}
, where

\varphi_{1}:\overline{\mathcal{U}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi^{-1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{K}\otimes U(\mathcal{T})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1\otimes\varphi}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{K}\otimes U(\mathfrak{a}_{0})
. More precisely, we give a kind of cuspidal module as follows.

Definition 4.7
.

Let
V
be an
\mathfrak{a}_{0}
-module
V

becomes an
\mathcal{A}\mathfrak{g}

\displaystyle t^{i}\xi^{r}\circ(f\otimes v)=t^{i}\xi^{r}f\otimes v,

.

Let
V^{\prime}=\{v\in V|xv=0,\forall x\in\mathfrak{a}_{1}\}
. So
V^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
. For any
y\in\mathfrak{a}_{0},x\in\mathfrak{a}_{1}
and
v\in V^{\prime}
, there is

xyv=(-1)^{|x||y|}yxv+[x,y]v=0
, which implies that
yv\in V^{\prime}
. Hence
V^{\prime}=V
by the simplicity of
V
. And therefore
\mathfrak{a}_{1}V=0
.
∎

Lemma 4.12
.

[
5
, Lemma 2.4]

Let
R=(t-1){\overline{\mathfrak{s}}}/(t-1)^{2}{\overline{\mathfrak{s}}}
. Then
R
is a two dimensional Lie superalgebra with
|X|=\bar{0},|Y|=\bar{1}
and nontrivial
brackets
[X,Y]=\frac{1}{2}Y
.

In fact, we have
(t-1)^{2}L_{i}\equiv 0
and
(t-1)^{2}G_{i}\equiv 0
in the Lie superalgebra
R
. Then we have

t(t-1)L_{i}\equiv(t-1)L_{i},\ t(t-1)G_{i}\equiv(t-1)G_{i},\ \forall i\in\mathbb{Z},

which implies that
X=(t-1)L_{0}
and
Y=(t-1)G_{0}
.

Theorem 4.13
.

Up to a parity change, any simple cuspidal
\mathcal{A}\mathfrak{g}
-module is isomorphic to a tensor module
\Gamma(\lambda,V)
for some finite-dimensional simple
R\ltimes\dot{\mathfrak{g}}
-module
V
and some
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

Let
V
be any finite-dimensional simple
\mathfrak{a}_{0}
-module. We know that
V
is a finite-dimensional simple
\mathfrak{a}_{0}/\mathfrak{a}_{1}
-module by Lemma
4.11
. Note that

\mathfrak{a}_{0}/\mathfrak{a}_{1}\cong\big((t-1)\overline{\mathfrak{s}}/(t-1)^{2}\overline{\mathfrak{s}}\big)\ltimes\big(\dot{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes(\mathcal{A}/\mathfrak{m}\mathcal{A})\big)\cong R\ltimes\dot{\mathfrak{g}}

by Lemma
4.12
. Therefore, from Lemma
4.8
, the conclusion is true.
∎

So far, we know that, to classify all simple cuspidal
\mathcal{A}\mathfrak{g}
-modules, it suffices to classify all finite-dimensional simple modules over
R\ltimes\dot{\mathfrak{g}}
. Since any simple finite-dimensional module over two-dimensional Lie superalgebra
R
is one-dimensional with
X\cdot u=au,\ Y\cdot u=0
for some
a\in\mathbb{C}
, there is the following lemma.

Lemma 4.14
.

[
5
, Corollary 3.5]

Up to a parity change, any simple cuspidal
\mathcal{A}\overline{\mathfrak{s}}
-module is isomorphic to some
\Gamma(\lambda,a)=\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathbb{C}u
with
\lambda,a\in\mathbb{C}

defined as follows:

\displaystyle t^{i}\xi^{r}\circ(f\otimes u)=t^{i}\xi^{r}f\otimes u,

\displaystyle L_{i}\circ(t^{m}\otimes u)=(\lambda+m+ia)t^{i+m}\otimes u,

\displaystyle L_{i}\circ(t^{m}\xi\otimes u)=(\lambda+m+ia+\frac{i}{2})t^{i+m}\xi\otimes u,

\displaystyle G_{i}\circ(t^{m}\otimes u)=(\lambda+m+2ia)t^{i+m}\xi\otimes u,

\displaystyle G_{i}\circ(t^{m}\xi\otimes u)=-t^{i+m}\otimes u,

where
i,m\in\mathbb{Z},f\in\mathcal{A},r\in\{0,1\}
and
|u|=\bar{0}
.

Lemma 4.15
.

Any finite-dimensional simple
R\ltimes\dot{\mathfrak{g}}
-module
V
is induced by the finite-dimensional simple
\dot{\mathfrak{g}}
-module
V
with
X\centerdot v=bv,Y\centerdot v=0,\ \forall v\in V
for some
b\in\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

Let
V
be a finite-dimensional simple
R\ltimes\dot{\mathfrak{g}}
-module and
W=\{v\in V\mid Y\centerdot v=0\}
. We have
V=W
because
V
is simple and
W
is a nontrivial submodule of
V
. Since
X=(t-1)L_{0}
, we get
[X,\dot{\mathfrak{g}}]=0
from Lemma
4.4
. So
X\centerdot v=bv,\ \forall v\in V
for some
b\in\mathbb{C}
by Schur’s Lemma.
∎

For a finite-dimensional simple
\dot{\mathfrak{g}}
-module
V
, from Lemma
4.15
, there exists
b\in\mathbb{C}
such that

(L_{i}-L_{0})\centerdot v=\big((L_{i}-L_{i-1})+\ldots+(L_{1}-L_{0})\big)\centerdot v=iX\centerdot v=ibv,\ \forall v\in V.

Similarly, we get
(G_{i}-G_{0})\centerdot v=iY\centerdot v=0
. Therefore, the
\mathcal{A}\mathfrak{g}
-module
\mathcal{A}\otimes V
in Definition
4.7
can be expressed as
\Gamma(\lambda,V,b)
with actions

\displaystyle t^{i}\xi^{r}\circ(f\otimes v)=t^{i}\xi^{r}f\otimes v,

\displaystyle L_{i}\circ(t^{m}\xi^{r}\otimes v)=(\lambda+m+ib+\frac{i}{2}\delta_{\bar{1},\bar{r}})t^{i+m}\xi^{r}\otimes v,

\displaystyle G_{i}\circ(t^{m}\otimes v)=(\lambda+m+2ib)t^{i+m}\xi\otimes v,

\displaystyle G_{i}\circ(t^{m}\xi\otimes v)=-t^{i+m}\otimes v,

\displaystyle(x\otimes t^{i})\circ(f\otimes v)=t^{i}f\otimes(x\centerdot v),

\displaystyle(x\otimes t^{i}\xi)\circ(f\otimes v)=t^{i}\xi f\otimes(x\centerdot v),

where
i,m\in\mathbb{Z},f\in\mathcal{A},r\in\{0,1\}
and
v\in V
.

Below we can present the main conclusion of this section.

Theorem 4.16
.

Up to a parity change, any simple cuspidal
\mathfrak{g}
-module is isomorphic to a simple quotient of a tensor module
\Gamma(\lambda,V,b)

for some finite-dimensional simple
\dot{\mathfrak{g}}
-module
V
and
\lambda,b\in\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

Let
M
be a simple cuspidal
\mathfrak{g}
-module. If
M
is a trival
\mathfrak{g}
-module, then
M
is a simple
quotient of the simple cuspidal
\mathcal{A}\mathfrak{g}
-module
\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathbb{C}
with
\mathbb{C}
a trivial
\dot{\mathfrak{g}}
-module. Now suppose
M
is a nontrivial simple cuspidal
\mathfrak{g}
-module.

If
IM=0
, then
M
is a simple cuspidal
\overline{\mathfrak{s}}
-module. Hence,
M
is a simple quotient of a simple cuspidal
\mathcal{A}\overline{\mathfrak{s}}
-module

\Gamma(\lambda,a)
by Theorem 3.9 in
[
5
]
. Since
\mathfrak{g}=\overline{\mathfrak{s}}\ltimes I
, any
\mathcal{A}\overline{\mathfrak{s}}
-module is naturally an
\mathcal{A}\mathfrak{g}
-module with trivial
I
-action.

If
IM\neq 0
, then
IM=M
since
M
is simple. So there is an epimorphism
\pi:\widehat{M}\rightarrow M
. From Proposition
3.5
,
\widehat{M}
is a cuspidal
\mathcal{A}\mathfrak{g}
-module. Consider the composition series of
\mathcal{A}\mathfrak{g}
-submodules in
\widehat{M}

0=\widehat{M}^{(1)}\subset\widehat{M}^{(2)}\subset\cdots\subset\widehat{M}^{(s)}=\widehat{M}

with the quotients
\widehat{M}^{(i)}/\widehat{M}^{(i-1)}
being simple cuspidal
\mathcal{A}\mathfrak{g}
-modules. Let
l
be the
minimal integer such that
\pi(\widehat{M}^{(l)})\neq 0
. Since
M
is simple
\mathfrak{g}
-module, we have
\pi(\widehat{M}^{(l)})=M

and
\pi(\widehat{M}^{(l-1)})=0
. This induces an epimorphism of
\mathfrak{g}
modules from
\widehat{M}^{(l)}/\widehat{M}^{(l-1)}
to
M
.
Hence,
M
is isomorphic to a simple quotient of cuspidal
\mathcal{A}\mathfrak{g}
-module
\widehat{M}^{(l)}/\widehat{M}^{(l-1)}
.
We can complete the proof by Theorem
4.13
.
∎

5
Main result

Through the previous study, it remains to classify all simple Harish-Chandra modules over
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
which is not cuspidal.
Let
\{x_{s}\mid s=1,2,\cdots,l\}
be a basis of Lie algebra
\dot{\mathfrak{g}}
. Then
\mbox{dim}\,\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{n}=2+2l
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}

and
n\neq 0
.
The following result is well known.

Lemma 5.1
.

[
20
, Lemma 1.6]

Let
V
be a weight module with finite-dimensional weight spaces for the Virasoro algebra with
\mbox{supp}(V)\subseteq\lambda+\mathbb{Z}
.
If for any
v\in V
, there exists
N(v)\in\mathbb{N}
such that
L_{i}v=0,\forall i\geq N(v)
, then
\mbox{supp}(V)
is upper bounded.

Lemma 5.2
.

Suppose
M
is a simple Harish-Chandra module over
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
which is not cuspidal, then
M
is a highest (or lowest) weight module.

Proof.

For a fixed
\lambda\in\mbox{supp}(M)
, there is a
k\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\mbox{dim}M_{\lambda-k}>(2+2l)\mbox{dim}M_{\lambda}+2\mbox{dim}M_{\lambda+1}

since
M
is not cuspidal. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then there exists a nonzero element
\omega\in M_{\lambda-k}

such that

L_{k}\omega=L_{k+1}\omega=G_{k}\omega=G_{k+1}\omega=0

and

(x_{s}\otimes t^{k})\omega=(x_{s}\otimes t^{k}\xi)\omega=0,

where
s\in\{1,2,\cdots,l\}
. Therefore, we get
L_{p}\omega=G_{p}\omega=(x_{s}\otimes t^{p})\omega=(x_{s}\otimes t^{p}\xi)\omega=0
for all
p\geq k^{2}

since
[\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{i},\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{j}]=\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{i+j}
for
j\neq 0
.

It is easy to see that
M^{\prime}=\{v\in M\mid\mbox{dim}\ \widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{+}v<\infty\}
is a nonzero submodule of
M
. So
M=M^{\prime}
by the simplicity of
M
.
Since
M
is a weight module over the Virasoro algebra, we have
\mbox{supp}(M)
is upper bounded by Lemma
5.1
, that is
M
is a highest weight module.
∎

To sum up, we have the following result.

Theorem 5.3
.

(
Main theorem
)

Let
M
be a simple Harish-Chandra module over the superconformal current algebra
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
.
Then
M
is a highest weight module, a lowest weight module, or a simple quotient of a tensor module
\Gamma(\lambda,V,b)
or
\Pi(\Gamma(\lambda,V,b))

for some finite-dimensional simple
\dot{\mathfrak{g}}
-module
V
and
\lambda,b\in\mathbb{C}
.

6
Application to the
N=1
Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra

As an application, we can directly get the classification results of the simple Harish-Chandra modules over the
N=1
Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra.

Definition 6.1
.

[
1
]

The
N=1
Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra
\mathcal{S}
is a Lie superalgebra with basis
\{L_{i},H_{i},G_{i},Q_{i},C_{L},C_{L,\alpha},C_{\alpha}\mid i\in\mathbb{Z}\}
and brackets

\displaystyle[L_{i},L_{j}]=(j-i)L_{i+j}+\delta_{i+j,0}\frac{1}{12}(i^{3}-i)C_{L},

\displaystyle[L_{i},H_{j}]=jH_{i+j}+\delta_{i+j,0}(i^{2}+i)C_{L,\alpha},

\displaystyle[L_{i},G_{j}]=(j-\frac{i}{2})G_{i+j},\ \ [L_{i},Q_{j}]=(j+\frac{i}{2})Q_{i+j},

\displaystyle[H_{i},H_{j}]=i\delta_{i+j,0}C_{\alpha},

\displaystyle[G_{i},H_{j}]=-jQ_{i+j},\ \ [H_{i},Q_{j}]=0,

\displaystyle[G_{i},Q_{j}]=H_{i+j}+(2i-1)\delta_{i+j,0}C_{L,\alpha},

\displaystyle[G_{i},G_{j}]=-2L_{i+j}+\delta_{i+j,0}\frac{1}{12}(4i^{2}-1)C_{L},

\displaystyle[Q_{i},Q_{j}]=\delta_{i+j,0}C_{\alpha},

where
\{L_{i},H_{i}\mid i\in\mathbb{Z}\}
are the even generators,
\{G_{i},Q_{i}\mid i\in\mathbb{Z}\}
are odd generators and
C_{L},C_{L,\alpha},C_{\alpha}
are central elements.

By direct calculation we can get that
\mathcal{S}
is the universal central extension of
\overline{\mathcal{S}}=\mbox{span}\{L_{i},H_{i},G_{i},Q_{i}\mid i\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. Let
M
be a simple cuspidal
\mathcal{S}
-module. Similar to Example 6.1 in
[
7
]
, we have that
C_{L}M=C_{L,\alpha}M=C_{\alpha}M=0
. Thus, the classification of simple cuspidal
\mathcal{S}
-modules is equivalent to the classification of simple cuspidal
\overline{\mathcal{S}}
-modules.

Now, let
H_{i}=Z\otimes t^{i}
and
Q_{i}=-Z\otimes t^{i}\xi
. It is easy to see that
\overline{\mathcal{S}}
is a special case of

\mathfrak{g}=\overline{\mathfrak{s}}\ltimes(\dot{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes\mathcal{A})
by taking
\dot{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathbb{C}Z
. Therefore, we have
R\ltimes\mathbb{C}Z
is a three-dimensional
Lie superalgebra with
(R\ltimes\mathbb{C}Z)_{\bar{0}}=\mbox{span}\{X,Z\}
,
(R\ltimes\mathbb{C}Z)_{\bar{1}}=\mbox{span}\{Y\}
and the following nonzero brackets

[X,Y]=\frac{1}{2}Y

from Lemma
4.12
. We have the following Lemma.

Lemma 6.2
.

Any finite-dimensional simple module over the Lie superalgebra
R\ltimes\mathbb{C}Z
is one-dimensional with

X\centerdot v=bv,\ Y\centerdot v=0,\ Z\centerdot v=dv

for some
b,d\in\mathbb{C}
.

Hence, any simple cuspidal
\mathcal{A}\overline{\mathcal{S}}
-module is isomorphic to some
\Gamma(\lambda,b,d)=\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathbb{C}v
with

\lambda,b,d\in\mathbb{C}
up to a parity change from Theorem
4.13
. The actions of
\mathcal{A}
and
\overline{\mathcal{S}}
on
\Gamma(\lambda,b,d)
are as follows:

\displaystyle t^{i}\xi^{r}\circ(f\otimes v)=t^{i}\xi^{r}f\otimes v,

\displaystyle L_{i}\circ(t^{m}\otimes v)=(\lambda+m+ib)t^{i+m}\otimes v,

\displaystyle L_{i}\circ(t^{m}\xi\otimes v)=(\lambda+m+ib+\frac{i}{2})t^{i+m}\xi\otimes v,

\displaystyle G_{i}\circ(t^{m}\otimes v)=(\lambda+m+2ib)t^{i+m}\xi\otimes v,

\displaystyle G_{i}\circ(t^{m}\xi\otimes v)=-t^{i+m}\otimes v,

\displaystyle H_{i}\circ(t^{m}\otimes v)=dt^{i+m}\otimes v,

\displaystyle H_{i}\circ(t^{m}\xi\otimes v)=dt^{i+m}\xi\otimes v,

\displaystyle Q_{i}\circ(t^{m}\otimes v)=-dt^{i+m}\xi\otimes v,

\displaystyle Q_{i}\circ(t^{m}\xi\otimes v)=0,

where
i,m\in\mathbb{Z},f\in\mathcal{A},r\in\{0,1\}
and
|v|=\bar{0}
.

Furthermore, from Theorem
4.16
, any simple cuspidal
\overline{\mathcal{S}}
-module is isomorphic to a simple quotient of tensor module
\Gamma(\lambda,b,d)=\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathbb{C}v
with
\lambda,b,d\in\mathbb{C}
. It is straightforward to check that as
\overline{\mathcal{S}}
-module,
\Gamma(\lambda,b,d)
has a unique
nontrivial sub-quotient which we denote by
\Gamma^{\prime}(\lambda,b,d)
. More precisely, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 6.3
.

1. As
\overline{\mathcal{S}}
-module,
\Gamma(\lambda,b,d)
is simple if and only if one of the following three conditions is satisfied

\ (1)d\neq 0;\ (2)\lambda\notin\mathbb{Z};\ (3)\lambda\in\mathbb{Z},b\neq 0,\frac{1}{2}.

2.
\Gamma(\lambda_{1},b_{1},d_{1})\cong\Gamma(\lambda_{2},b_{2},d_{2})
if and only if
\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{Z},b_{1}=b_{2},d_{1}=d_{2}
or

\lambda_{1}\notin\mathbb{Z},\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{Z},b_{1}=\frac{1}{2},b_{2}=0,d_{1}=d_{2}=0
or
\lambda_{1}\notin\mathbb{Z},\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{Z},b_{1}=0,b_{2}=\frac{1}{2},d_{1}=d_{2}=0
.

3.
\Gamma^{\prime}(0,0,0)\cong\Gamma^{\prime}(0,\frac{1}{2},0)
, where
\Gamma^{\prime}(0,0,0)=\Gamma(0,0,0)/\{\mathbb{C}(1\otimes v)\}
,

\Gamma^{\prime}(0,\frac{1}{2},0)=\mbox{span}\{t^{m}\otimes v,t^{k}\xi\otimes v\mid m,k\in\mathbb{Z},k\neq 0\}
.

Proof.

1. Since

H_{i}\circ(t^{m}\otimes v)=dt^{i+m}\otimes v,\ H_{i}\circ(t^{m}\xi\otimes v)=dt^{i+m}\xi\otimes v,

it is easy to see that
\Gamma(\lambda,b,d)
is simple if
d\neq 0
. Note that
\overline{\mathcal{S}}=\overline{\mathfrak{s}}\ltimes\mathcal{A}
, so
\Gamma(\lambda,b,d)
is also an
\overline{\mathfrak{s}}
-module. We have that
\Gamma(\lambda,b,d)
is simple if
\lambda\notin\mathbb{Z}
or
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z},b\neq 0,\frac{1}{2}
from Lemma 3.10 in
[
5
]
.

2. Suppose
\Gamma(\lambda_{1},b_{1},d_{1})\cong\Gamma(\lambda_{2},b_{2},d_{2})
, we have
d_{1}=d_{2}
obviously. Then this conclusion holds from Lemma 3.10 in
[
5
]
.

3. This conclusion can be verified directly.
∎

At this point, for the simple Harish-Chandra module over
N=1
Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra
\mathcal{S}
, we have the following conclusion.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
M
be a simple Harish-Chandra module over
\mathcal{S}
. Then
M
is a highest weight module, a lowest weight module, or
\Gamma^{\prime}(\lambda,b,d),\Pi(\Gamma^{\prime}(\lambda,b,d))

for some
\lambda,b,d\in\mathbb{C}
.

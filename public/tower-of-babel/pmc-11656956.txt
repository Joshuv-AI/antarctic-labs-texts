Identification of GiOMT gene family in Glycyrrhiza inflata bat and expression analysis under UV-B stresses
Authors: Caijuan Li, Hao Li, Yufan Liang, Rong Miao, Ziliang Liu, Sijin Chen, Dan Zhang, Cuiling Wang, Jiling Wu, Xiangyan Zhou, Mengfei Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundO-Methyltransferase (OMTs) is a class of conserved multifunctional enzymes that play important roles in plant developmental regulation, hormone signaling, secondary metabolite synthesis and abiotic stress response. The GiOMT gene family has been identified and analyzed in species such as citrus, alfalfa, Populus and grape, but has not been reported in Glycyrrhiza inflata Bat.ResultsIn this study, we systematically identified and analyzed the GiOMT gene family of G. inflata by bioinformatics, and analyzed their physicochemical properties, conserved motifs, conserved structural domains, gene structures, phylogenetic relationships, chromosomal localization and fragment duplications, and the expression patterns of GiOMT genes in combination with transcriptomic data and qRT-PCR. The results showed that a total of 41 GiOMTs were identified in G. inflata, which were named GiOMT1 ~ GiOMT41 based on their chromosomal locations. Protein characterization showed that 29 GiOMT proteins were hydrophilic and 12 GiOMT proteins were hydrophobic. Subcellular predicted localization revealed that most GiOMT proteins localized in the cytoplasm and chloroplasts. Phylogenetic relationships showed that the OMT genes of three species, G. inflata, Arabidopsis and alfalfa, were distributed in three taxa, while the GiOMT genes were distributed in taxa I and II. Promoters of GiOMT genes contained light responsive element and many hormone responsive elements. The expression levels of GiOMT genes under UV-B stress were varied, indicating that GiOMT gene was in response to abiotic stresses in G. inflata.ConclusionIn this study, we investigated the genome-wide identification, structure, evolution and expression analysis of the GiOMT gene in G. inflata. The basal sequence of GiOMT genes was highly conserved throughout the evolutionary history of G. inflata. Most of the GiOMT genes were highly expressed in roots and were involved in the response to UV-B stress. The GiOMT genes may lead to the accumulation of flavonoids and enhancement of G. inflata quality and drug activity in G. inflata under UV-B radiation.Keywords: O-Methyltransferase, GiOMT gene family, G. Inflata, UV-B radiation, Expression analysis

Introduction

Flavonoids are important phenolic secondary metabolites in plants. A large number of studies have shown that flavonoids have a wide range of biological activities, and have broad pharmaceutical prospects, thus endowing licorice with a wide range of pharmaceutical properties. O-Methyltransferase (OMTs) is a class of multifunctional enzymes, which promote the production of various secondary metabolites. These secondary metabolites are essential for plant growth and serve as defense substances against various abiotic stresses. OMT genes are involved in a variety of primary and secondary metabolic pathways including monophenols, phenylpropanoids, flavonoids and phenylalanine metabolism pathways with a wide range of biological and pharmacological effects [1, 2]. OMT gene family usually acts on the methylation of some small molecules in secondary metabolic pathways such as lignin, flavonoids, alkaloid biosynthesis, etc. [3, 4], which determines the specificity of physiological functions of these small molecules in plants [5]. The OMT gene family belongs to the methyltransferase supergene family (containing OMT, SMT (S-methyltransferase) and several other gene families) which is even more complex [6, 7]. OMTs are widely distributed in plants, most OMTs use phenolic compounds such as flavonoids as substrates and S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) as a methyl donor, which catalyzes the transfer of SAM to flavonoids to form O-methyl derivatives [8, 9].

Based on protein molecular weight and cation dependence, the plant OMT family is generally divided into two subfamilies: caffeic acid O-methyltransferases (COMT) and caffeoyl-coenzyme AO-methyltransferases (CCoAOMT) [10]. The COMT subfamily has a relatively large number of members, and the molecular weights of its protein subunits are generally between 40 and 43 kDa. They often form homodimers and are not cation-dependent [11]. OMTs belong to this subfamily [12]. The CCoAOMT subfamily has a relatively small number of members, with relatively small molecular weights of the subunits (26–30 kDa), and are mostly cation-dependent. It has been shown that some members of this subfamily specialized in the catalytic catalysis of caffeoyl coenzyme A, which is a key enzyme in the lignin biosynthetic pathway [13, 14]. The modifying effects of modifying enzymes such as glycosyltransferases [15], acyltransferases [16], O-methyltransferases [12], and other enzymes are also important in the structural diversity of flavonoids, which lay an important structural foundation for the performance of their diverse biological functions.

Ultraviolet-B (UV-B) radiation causes damage to DNA, proteins, membrane lipids and other biomolecules in plant cells, resulting in negative effects on plants [17, 18]. Plants have evolved various strategies to overcome the harmful effects caused by UV-B radiation [19]. One defense strategy is the production of additional antioxidant protectors such as flavonoids [20]. The synthesis and accumulation of plant flavonoids is regulated by both development and the environment. UV-B radiation is an environmental stimulus that regulates flavonoid biosynthesis at the transcriptional level of genes encoding corresponding enzymes in the biosynthetic pathway [21]. Low-dose UV-B radiation triggers the expression of plant secondary metabolite genes, enhances plant medicinal active ingredients, and promotes rhizome development [22]. Plants usually induce rapid accumulation of flavonoids in response to UV-B radiation stress [23–25], and enhanced flavonoid synthesis was observed in UV-B resistant mutants of Arabidopsis thaliana [26], but flavonoid synthesis in Arabidopsis deficient mutants exhibited UV-B sensitivity [27]. When plants are exposed to abiotic stressors, such as UV radiation, they produce a number of secondary metabolites in response to this stress [28–30]. Plants synthesize flavonoids with a catechol structure on the B ring after UV-B stress, and this conjugated structure is important for enhancing free radical scavenging [31]. Flavonoids are effective in buffering stress-induced changes in reactive oxygen species homeostasis, thus conferring a wide range of stress-tolerant properties to plants, including effective defense against abiotic and biotic stresses, such as cold damage, drought, salt stress and herbivores [32]. It has been shown that exposure of citrus fruit to UV-B radiation dramatically increased the transcription levels of CrOMT1 geneand led to the accumulation of three PMFs [33]. Zhao et al. cloned and expressed the ElOMT1 gene and found that the methyltransferase ElOMT1 gene was significantly elevated by UV-B stimulation and was involved in flavonoid biosynthesis [34].

Licorice is one of the most widely used medicinal plants in the genus licorice of the family Leguminosae, and is mainly distributed in Gansu, Inner Mongolia, north of Xinjiang, and other provinces and regions of China. It is a well-known herb with a wide range of pharmacological functions that has been used throughout Chinese history [35]. Its roots and rhizomes are important Chinese medicinal herbs. The OMT gene family is a family of genes closely related to plant secondary metabolic processes. For medicinal herbs, the standard for evaluating their quality is the content of index components, and the majority of index components are secondary metabolites. The secondary product synthesis pathway in plants is complex with numerous steps involving the participation of numerous enzymes, therefore, there is a close correlation between polymorphisms of key enzymes and their encoding genes and the biosynthesis of secondary metabolites. COMT enzymes mainly catalyze phenylpropanoids, flavonoids and alkaloids [36]. Among them, flavonoids are an effective substance in plants against UV-B radiation and also one of the important medicinal active ingredients in licorice. Thus, the OMT gene family plays a crucial role in the synthesis of secondary metabolites such as flavonoids in G. inflata.

The OMT gene family is closely related to the methylation of flavonoids. The functions and regulatory mechanisms of OMT genes have been investigated in species such as Arabidopsis and rice [37, 38]. However, the understanding of their distribution, evolution and function in G. inflata is still limited. Since such genes in G. inflata have not been reported, this study used bioinformatics methods to predict and analyze the basic features of the GiOMT gene family in G. inflata, including physicochemical properties, phylogenetic relationships and gene structures, etc. The expression of GiOMT genes in G. inflata under UV-B radiation was analyzed by transcriptomic and qRT-PCR. This study provides a theoretical basis for further research on GiOMT gene function, regulation of secondary metabolite biosynthesis and expression analysis of G. inflata under UV-B radiation stress. The results of this study have revealed the evolution and function of the OMT family of genes.

Results

Identification of GiOMT gene family members

The GiOMT gene was identified based on G. inflata transcriptome data and through the ORF Finder and CD-Serch tools in National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI). After structural domain integrity confirmation, a total of 41 GiOMT members were finally identified (Table 1), which were named GiOMT1 ~ GiOMT41, respectively. The protein amino acid lengths of the 41 GiOMT genes encoded proteins ranging from 46 ~ 680. Protein molecular weights (Mw) ranged from 4.83 to 74.77 kDa. The isoelectric points (pI) ranged from 4.7 to 8.73. GiOMT13, GiOMT27, GiOMT32, GiOMT37 and GiOMT38 had a pI greater than 7.0, and the rest of genes were less than 7.0, indicating that most of GiOMT proteins were acidic. The instability index (II) of GiOMT proteins ranged from 17.02 to 50.06. 26 GiOMT proteins had instability coefficients less than 40, indicating they are stable. Instability coefficients of 15 GiOMT proteins were greater than 40, suggesting that these proteins are unstable. The aliphatic index (AI) of the GiOMT proteins ranged from 80.66 to 108.63. Among them, GiOMT34 protein has the smallest aliphatic index and GiOMT15 protein has the largest aliphatic index. The grand average of hydrophilicity (GRAVY) values of GiOMT proteins ranged from − 0.342 to 0.42. 29 GiOMT proteins have hydrophilicity values less than 0, indicating they are hydrophilic proteins. Hydrophilicity values of 12 GiOMT proteins were greater than 0, indicating they are hydrophobic proteins. subcellular localization (SL) prediction showed that the majority of GiOMT proteins localized in the cytoplasm and chloroplasts, suggesting their critically important functions in the cytoplasm and chloroplasts.

Analysis of gene structure, conserved structural domains and conserved motifs of GiOMT genes

The conserved motifs of GiOMT proteins were predicted using MEME software. The results showed that the GiOMT protein structure was relatively conserved, and a total of ten relatively conserved motifs of GiOMT genes were identified (named motif 1 ~ 10, respectively), among which 32 members contained motif 2, indicating that motif 2 is highly conserved, and at the same time it plays an extremely important role in GiOMT genes. 31 members contained motif 8. 30 members contained motif 4 and motif 5. Motif 9 was present in GiOMT13, GiOMT15, GiOMT19, GiOMT22, GiOMT23, GiOMT29 and GiOMT30 (Fig. 1A). Both GiOMT39 and GiOMT41 contained the same motifs (motif 2 and motif 4), indicating that GiOMT39 and GiOMT41 have similar biological functions. In addition, the sequence and length of each motif varied (Table 2). Motif 4, motif 5 and motif 8 contained 15 amino acids, motif 3, motif 6 and motif 7 contained 29 amino acids, motif 9 contained 50 amino acids, and the number of amino acids in the rest of the motifs varied from 32 to 41.

Conserved structural domain analysis showed that the amino acid sequences of the GiOMT family members are relatively long (Fig. 1B). 41 of the GiOMT members contained the structural domain AdoMet_MTases superfamily, which is a conserved structural domain of class I methyltransferases. Most of the members contained the dimerization structural domain at the N-terminus (pfam: pfam08100), which plays an important role in the dimer formation of the protein. But GiOMT6 contained the above two structural domains and a structural domain of unknown function, dimerization 2 superfamily (cl06920).

Structural analysis of the genes showed that members of the GiOMT gene family contained different numbers of exons-introns (Fig. 1C), with GiOMT25, GiOMT36, GiOMT31, GiOMT39, GiOMT41 and GiOMT38 did not contain introns. GiOMT13 had the highest number of introns (8). GiOMT3/4/18/19/21/29/30/33/34 contained UTR regions at both the 5′ and 3′ ends. GiOMT1/5/8/10/13/22/24/35/37 contained UTR regions at one end only, while others without UTR regions.

Chromosomal localization analysis of the GiOMT genes

Gene localization is an important part of genetic research. Analyzing the relationship between the location of genes on chromosomes and their physiological functions helps to understand the function of genes. Using the gene family localization data in chromosomes, it is possible to analyze their upstream and downstream regulatory elements or to determine whether there is a selective pressure acting on the gene coding for this protein, and thus to understand the rate of gene evolution. The members of the GiOMT family were unevenly distributed on the 37 G. inflata scaffolds (Fig. 2), and since the physical map of Licorice chromosomes is not publicly released, only the GiOMT family genes can be localized to the scaffolds, and chromosome related analyses cannot be done currently [39]. GiOMT4 is at approximately Scaffold00125 18 Mb position, while GiOMT5 is at approximately Scaffold00125 19 Mb position; GiOMT17 is at approximately Scaffold0072 13 Mb position, and GiOMT18 is at approximately Scaffold00721 7 Mb position; GiOMT26 is at approximately Scaffold01150 2 Mb position, and GiOMT27 at about Scaffold01150 4 Mb position, indicating that GiOMT4 is closer to GiOMT5, GiOMT17 to GiOMT18, and GiOMT26 to GiOMT27, suggested that these genes may be homologous genes that arose through a fragment duplication event.

Secondary structure prediction of GiOMT proteins

The secondary structure of proteins mainly contains four parts including α-helix, extended chain, β-turn and irregular coil. The results of the secondary structure prediction of 41 GiOMT proteins showed that the proportion of α-helices ranged from 32.65 to 53.42%, extended chains ranged from 11.84 to 24.00%, β-turns ranged from 4.88 to 15.22%, and irregular curls ranged from 21.74 to 38.19%. It can be predicted that α-helices and irregular curls play a major role in the secondary structure of the GiOMT proteins. The proportions of the four fractions were not different significantly among the 41 GiOMT proteins (Fig. 3).

Phylogenetic tree analysis of the GiOMT gene family

A reliable inference of phylogeny can reveal the sequence of evolutionary processes of the organisms involved and help us to understand the history of G. inflata evolution and evolutionary mechanisms. In order to understand the evolutionary relationships among the members of the GiOMT family, a phylogenetic tree was constructed from the amino acid sequences of 7 Arabidopsis OMT genes, 44 alfalfa OMT genes and 41 GiOMT genes, which consisted mainly of three large evolutionary branches including subfamily I (red underlining), subfamily II (blue underlining) and subfamily III (green underlining) (Fig. 4). The gene structures and conserved motifs of the genes in the same branching system of the evolutionary tree are roughly the same, but there are great differences in the gene structures of the genes between the branching systems. The functions of proteins close to each other in the same branching system in the evolutionary tree tend to be the most similar. Among the 41 members of the GiOMT family, GiOMT15, GiOMT17, GiOMT4 and GiOMT5 were exsited in subfamilies I-1, I-2, II-1 and II-2, respectively.

Analysis of cis-acting elements in the promoter of GiOMT gene family

The expression of plant abiotic stress functional genes is regulated by cis-acting elements and transcription factors in promoters. Understanding the study of abiotic stress induced promoter cis-acting elements and transcription factors in plants, it helps to reveal the expression regulation mechanism of stress responsive genes. The distribution of promoter cis-acting elements on genes determines the direction of gene expression in response to stress, and has important research implications for targeted enhancement of plant resistance. In order to further determine the role of the GiOMT gene family under various abiotic stresses, cis-acting element analysis of a promoter region 2000 bp upstream of the GiOMT gene, the results obtained a large number of components related to plant growth and development (e.g., light, hormones and abiotic stresses, etc.). Members of the GiOMT gene family contained elements that were significantly different in both number and type. The GiOMT gene family was found to contain a total of 20 cis-acting elements (Fig. 5). Among them, light responsive elements, anaerobic induction response elements, MeJA-responsive elements and MYB binding site responsive elements responded in most GiOMT gene family members, suggesting that the promoter cis-acting elements of the GiOMT gene family play an important role in regulating the growth and development of G. inflata, as well as resistance and response to various stresses.

Synteny analysis of GiOMT gene family

Chromosome segment duplication results in duplicated genes being farther apart or even located on different chromosomes. Tandem duplication occurs primarily in regions of chromosomal recombination, and members of the gene family formed by tandem duplication are usually closely aligned on the same chromosome, forming a cluster of genes with similar sequences and similar functions. In this study, intraspecies covariance analysis of the GiOMT genes was performed. One covariance pair was detected (GiOMT2 and GiOMT3), suggesting that these two GiOMT genes are the result of gene duplication (Fig. 6). The Ka/Ks values between the replicated gene pairs were calculated, and the results showed that the replicated gene pairs had Ka/Ks ratios of less than 1 (Table 3), which suggests that replicated gene pairs evolve mainly under purifying selection, and also reveals that replication of whole genome fragments plays an important role in increasing the number of GiOMT gene family members in the G. inflata genome.

In order to analyze the evolutionary relationship of OMT genes among different species, the chromosomes of G. inflata were analyzed in covariance with three representative plants, the dicotyledonous plants Arabidopsis, alfalfa, and tomato of the family Solanaceae (Fig. 7). The chromosomes of GiOMT genes were labeled as 1–37 from scaffold00014 to scaffold08236 for convenience. The results showed that there were more covariance modules between the GiOMT genes and alfalfa genomes and more direct homologous genes, which indicated a high degree of similarity. In contrast, the GiOMT gene had fewer covariance modules with Arabidopsis and tomato and only six pairs of direct homologous genes with tomato, but no direct homologous genes with Arabidopsis. This suggested that the GiOMT gene of G. inflata was closer to alfalfa and more evolutionarily distant from Arabidopsis.

Interaction network analysis of GiOMT family proteins

Protein-protein interaction network is composed of interactions between proteins, which mainly participate in the transmission of biological signals, regulation of gene expression, capacity and metabolism of substances, regulation of cell growth cycle and other important life processes [40–43]. In this study, in the context of the model plant Arabidopsis, we predicted the physicochemical properties of the GiOMT proteins and its potential functionally relevant interacting proteins (Fig. 8). We identified eight functional molecules that have directly relevant potential interactions with 41 GiOMT proteins. They were ASMT (acetylserotonin O-methyltransferase), OMT1 (flavone 3’-O-methyltransferase 1), CCOAOMT1 (caffeoyl-CoA O-methyltransferase 1), T4D2.70 (caffeic acid O-methyltransferase-like protein), T25K17.30 (caffeoyl-CoA O-methyltransferase At4g26220), F4IX20-ARATH (Alpha/beta-Hydrolases superfamily protein.), CCOAMT (caffeoyl-CoA O-methyltransferase At1g67980), and F5F19.5 (O-methyltransferase family protein) (Table 4). Among them, the OMT1 is involved in melatonin biosynthesis, methylates OH residues of flavonoids and functions as an acetylserine O-methyltransferase. The ASMT catalyzes methyl transfer to N-acetylhydroxytryptophan to produce melatonin (N-acetyl-5-methoxytryptophan). The CCoAOMT1 methylates caffeoyl-CoA to feruloyl-CoA, but has low activity toward caffeic acid and fisetin and is involved in scopoletin biosynthesis in roots. The CCOAMT is involved in the reinforcement of plant cell walls. It is also involved in the response to trauma or pathogen challenge by increasing the formation of cell wall-bound ferulic acid polymers. It can be hypothesized that the GiOMT proteins have the same or similar biological functions as these eight functional molecules identified.

GO functional annotation of GiOMT genes

The biological functions of GiOMT genes were further evaluated by GO enrichment analysis in G. inflata subjected to UV-B stress. GO analysis classified the GiOMTs into three main categories including BP, CC and MF (Fig. 9). The results showed that, in biological processes, the three comparison groups were significantly enriched in cellular process, metabolic process and single-organism process. In cellular components, down-regulated genes were enriched in 0 d treatment, while up-regulated genes were enriched in both 7 d and 15 d treatment, and all three comparison groups were enriched in eight identical biological processes. In molecular function, the three comparison groups were significantly enriched in catalytic activity. It can be hypothesized that GiOMT genes exert their corresponding regulatory functions by binding to the DNA region, mainly involving in cellular processes, developmental processes and material metabolism of G. inflata.

Effect of UV-B radiation on the phytosome of G. inflata

In this study, phenotypic changes in G. inflata were observed at different treatment times (7 d, 15 d) under radiation conditions of 214 µW/m2 and control (0 d) (Fig. 10). The results showed that the leaves of G. inflata showed wilting and curling, and yellowing and drying of the leaves at 7 d of treatment with UV-B radiation compared to the control. However, both stems and leaves showed wilting and increased curling and drying of almost all of the leaves at 15 d of treatment.

Expression profile of GiOMT gene of G. inflata under UV-B radiation

To further understand the expression pattern of GiOMTs genes in G. inflata under UV-B treatment, the roots of G. inflata under UV-B treatment were collect to sequence. Based on the transcriptome data, the expression of GiOMT family genes of G. inflata (Gi) was analyzed, in which FPKM values were used for heat mapping (Fig. 11A). The results showed that GiOMT9, GiOMT11, GiOMT12, GiOMT23, GiOMT39, GiOMT40 and GiOMT41 were not expressed under UV-B stress, and the expression of the remaining 34 GiOMT genes varied with the increase in the duration of UV-B treatment. GiOMT4, GiOMT26, GiOMT27, GiOMT28, GiOMT30 and GiOMT32 were up-regulated in strong radiation (15 d). GiOMT13, GiOMT14, GiOMT15 and GiOMT37 were up-regulated in moderate radiation (7 d). And the rest of the GiOMTs genes were up-regulated in the control (0 d), and their expression increased and then decreased with the increase in the intensity of UV-B radiation.

To further investigate the function of GiOMT genes. In this study, 12 (GiOMT5/7/8/18/19/21/26/29/30/32/34/38) differentially expressed GiOMT genes were selected in combination with transcriptome data, and their relative expression levels were analyzed under UV-B stress using qRT-PCR (Fig. 11B). The results showed that all of these genes were responsive to UV-B and there were some differences in the expression patterns of GiOMTs genes under different UV-B radiation times. Moreover, the changed treads of these genes are similar to those of the transcriptome data.

Discussion

O-methyltransferases are a versatile class of enzymes. OMT promotes the production of a wide range of secondary substances that are essential for plant growth and act as defenses against a wide range of stresses [44]. The OMT gene family is a powerful growth regulator that not only provides protection to plants, but it is also involved in fiber elongation and secondary cell wall synthesis stages [3]. The OMT gene family has been more extensively studied in a variety of plants. The members of the OMT gene family vary greatly among different species, such as citrus, alfalfa, grape and Populus contain 58, 44, 47 and 26 OMT members, respectively [45–48]. In this study, we analyzed and predicted the functions of the proteins encoded by the GiOMT genes of G. inflata using bioinformatics analysis. A total of 41 GiOMT genes were identified from the G. inflata transcriptome data based on conserved structural domains. The lengths of the 41 GiOMT proteins ranged from 46 to 680 AA, the pI ranged from 4.7 to 8.73, the Mw ranged from 4.83 to 74.77 kDa, the AI ranged from 80.66 to 108.63. The GiOMTs were mainly localized in the cytoplasm and chloroplasts, indicating that it mainly plays important biological functions in the cytoplasm and chloroplasts.

Exon/intron diversification of gene family members plays an important role in the evolution of multiple gene families in three main mechanisms: exon/intron gain/loss, exon/pseudo-exonation and insertion/deletion [49]. The analysis of gene structure, conserved motifs and structural domains showed that all 41 GiOMT members contained the structural domain AdoMet_MTases superfamily. Most of them contained the dimerization structural domain at the N-terminal end. GiOMT6 contained the structural domain dimerization 2 superfamily in addition to the above two structural domains. The motif 2 was present in 32 GiOMT genes, indicating that motif 2 is highly conserved during the evolutionary process, and there were some differences in the sequence and length of each motif. Members of the GiOMT gene family contained introns and exons with some differences. By analyzing the chromosomal localization, the results showed that the 41 GiOMT genes were unevenly distributed on 37 scaffolds. There was homology among different genes. The secondary structure prediction of GiOMT proteins were found to consist mainly of α-helix and irregular coil. Based on phylogenetic tree analysis, the OMT gene families of G. inflata, Arabidopsis and alfalfa were divided into three subfamilies, and the genes in the same subfamily are closer in kinship and more similar in gene function. The results showed that most GiOMT genes are clustered in one branch, and only a few GiOMT genes are clustered with alfalfa OMT genes. The reasons may be, firstly, G. inflata adapted to different environments during the evolutionary process, which resulted in some changes in genetic characteristics, morphology, physiology, and behavior. In addition, the genetic material of GiOMT gene was lost during the evolutionary process, so only a few GiOMT genes clustered with OMT genes in alfalfa, but this does not mean that the kinship between them was weakened. However, this reflects the diversity of the species during the evolutionary process.

Tandem duplication, fragment duplication and genome-wide duplication play important roles in gene expansion, rearrangement and functional diversification [50]. In this study, it was found that the GiOMT gene family expanded by fragment duplication without other modes of amplification. To determine whether the GiOMT family genes were affected by environmental pressure selection, the Ka/Ks values of a pair of fragment duplication genes obtained from the covariance analysis were calculated and analyzed. Generally, Ka/Ks > 1 indicates that the gene is retained as a dominant gene after a non-synonymous mutation. It is influenced by positive selection. Ka/Ks < 1 indicates that the gene is eliminated as an inferior gene after a non-synonymous mutation. It is influenced by the purification choices [51, 52]. We found that the Ka/Ks values of a pair of duplicate genes were less than 1, it suggests a purifying selection effect of environmental stress on these genes, and this indicates that the family is strongly conserved during phylogeny. The covariance among the OMTs genes of G. inflata, alfalfa and tomato suggested that they may be functionally similar, and at the same time, provided a basis for prediction of the GiOMTs genes’ expression and protein function. By analyzing the protein interaction network, the interaction relationship between proteins can be revealed, so as to resolve the function and regulatory mechanism of proteins [53]. In this study, the prediction of protein interaction networks revealed that GiOMTs proteins may have similar or identical biological functions with ASMT, OMT1, CCOAOMT1, T4D2.70, T25K17.30, F4IX20-ARATH, CCOAMT and F5F19.5.

Cis-acting elements are DNA sequences present in the paralogous sequences of genes that can affect gene expression, and they are mainly involved in the regulation of gene expression [54]. The cis-acting elements in the promoter region play an extremely important role in regulating plant growth and development and resisting adversity. The different cis-acting elements in the promoter region can predict the different biological functions of the gene in response to different stress treatments [55]. In this study, we found that the promoters of GiOMTs contained a large number of phytohormone response elements (ABA, IAA, SA, MeJA, GA) and light response element. In addition there were cis-acting elements in the GiOMTs gene family that respond to the specific induction of hypothermia, trauma and hypoxia. It was consistent with the fact that methyltransferases were not only involved in all stages of plant growth and development, but were also associated with plant resistance to external biotic and abiotic stresses. For example, ZmCCoAOMT2 protected maize from phytophthora minor and gray spot diseases [56], down-regulation of COMTs promoted necrosis of plant tissues [57, 58], and OMTs played an important role in plant UV protection [59]. This suggests that GiOMTs genes may be involved in the regulation of stress response and phytohormone signaling, possibly through the regulation of biological processes such as primary metabolite synthesis, secondary metabolite accumulation, cell development, senescence and signaling in G. inflata. It may further affect the growth process of G. inflata. GO annotation analysis of the G. inflata transcriptome, the results indicated that all three contrasting groups of the GiOMT gene family were enriched in BP, CC and MF, and exerted corresponding biological functions.

Analysis of gene expression profiles can reveal the different biological functions of the genes involved. For example (Table 5), ClOMT7 was expressed only in roots, whereas OsCOMT7 was expressed at the highest level in stems [60, 61]. CmOMT14 and CmOMT18 showed an upward trend in transcript levels under all three stress conditions of NaCl, chilling and high temperature and humidity [62]. Rice NOMT was transcriptionally enhanced and involved in abiotic stress-induced methylated flavonoid synthesis induced by methyl jasmonate treatment [38]. CrOMT1 leads to PMFs accumulation in citrus after UV-B radiation [33]. ElOMT1 enhances flavonoid biosynthesis under UV-B radiation [34]. This shows that OMT genes play a crucial role in plant secondary metabolism. In this study, we used qRT-PCR in conjunction with transcriptomic data to confirm the expression of GiOMT genes under UV-B stress. The specific expression of the GiOMT gene provided a basis for determining the involvement of GiOMT in plant physiological and biochemical activities, and also verified the reliability of the transcriptome data of GiOMT gene. The qRT-PCR results showed that GiOMTs genes were significantly up-regulated after UV-B radiation, thus promoting flavonoid synthesis. Overall, these results suggested that GiOMT genes might be involved in the plant responses to UV-B stresses.

In recent years, the over-excavation of wild-grown G. inflata and the deterioration of the ecological environment have led to a significant decrease in the quality and yield of G. inflata, making it difficult to meet the demand for G. inflata and its products. Therefore, the enhancement of G. inflata quality and drug activity has been a hot topic in the current research of G. inflata. In this study, we found that most of the GiOMTs genes were involved in photoresponse elements under UV-B radiation, and most of the GiOMTs genes were predicted to be related to flavonoid synthesis, and we found that except these eight genes (GiOMT9/11/12/23/36/39/40/41) did not change significantly in response to UV-B radiation, the other 33 genes responded to UV-B radiation, suggesting that moderate UV-B radiation favors the synthesis and accumulation of active components of G. inflata. The identification of OMT gene family in plants at different growth stages of G. inflata under UV-B treatment conditions has important reference value for the study of the biological function of OMT, and OMT genes under UV-B radiation may lead to the accumulation of flavonoids and enhancement of G. inflata quality and drug activity in G. inflata. This study provides a reference for further research on GiOMT genes function, regulation of secondary metabolite biosynthesisand exploration of the function of GiOMT genes in plant response to adversity stress.

Conclusions

In this study, we performed expression characterization and bioinformatics analysis of the GiOMT gene of G. inflata at the transcriptome level. It was verified that the OMT enzymes were highly conserved among the GiOMT family of genes. The promoter has many elements associated with hormone response. The expression of GiOMTs genes in the G. inflata after different times of UV-B stress was determined by transcriptomic data and qRT-PCR. The OMT gene family is a class of gene family unique to other plants. However, its expression and function in G. inflata have not been reported yet. Therefore, the present study verified the expression characteristics and different biological functions of GiOMT genes by bioinformatics methods and combined with transcriptomic data. It provides a theoretical basis for in-depth investigation of the function of GiOMT gene family and its involvement in the growth and development, stress response and metabolite synthesis of licorice. It lays the foundation for expanding the cultivation range of licorice and cultivating high-quality licorice germplasm screening.

Materials and methods

Plant materials and growing conditions

The licorice variety Glycyrrhiza inflata Bat was provided by the Gansu Provincial Economic Crops Technology Popularization Station and grown in our laboratory. Seeds were selected and sterilized with 98% concentrated sulfuric acid, 3% sodium hypochlorite, and 75% alcohol, and placed on a moist filter paper to germinate at 25℃ under dark conditions. When the seeds germinated (about 5 d), the uniformly grown seedlings were transplanted into pots with a nutrient soil-to-vermiculite ratio of 5:3. The seedlings were incubated in an incubation room with 16 h of light/8 h of dark cycle, 25℃, 50% humidity, 8,000 lx light intensity for 90 d. They were irradiated with 214 µW/m2 UV-B for 4 h/d (2 h in 9:00–11:00 am and 2 h in 14:00–16:00 pm), and the roots were collected at 0, 7 and 15 d of UV-B irradiation, respectively. Each treatment was replicated three times, and then snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen and placed at −80℃.

Identification of GiOMT genes members and data collection

Screening for genes annotated as GiOMT from transcriptome sequencing data (the data have been uploaded to the SRA database of NCBI with ID PRJNA1086199, but have not been published yet), and in conjunction with the genome sequence of Glycyrrhiza uralensis Fisch published by Mochida et al. [63], and the transcriptome sequencing was done by Gene Denovo (Guangzhou, China). Two types of OMT Hidden Markov Model (HMM) were downloaded via PFAM (http://pfam.xfam.org/) [64], including pfam00891 (Methyltransf_2), pfam08100 (dimerization), pfam01596 (Methyltransf_3) or cl17173 (AdoMet_MTases superfamily). We searched and compared the G. inflata transcriptome protein data with the three types of OMT HMM using the Hmmsearch tool, and the expected value (E-value) of the GiOMT gene family members was 1e-5 and identified for their IDs [64, 65]. The structural domains of the amino acid sequences of all candidate genes of G. inflata were characterized using the online platforms PfamScan (https://www.ebi.ac.uk/Tools/pfa/pfamscan/) and SMART (http://smart.embl-heidelberg.de/) [66]. GiOMT protein sequences were searched and de-redundant to obtain candidate protein sequences. The online tool NCBI CD (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/Structure/cdd/wrpsb.cgi) search was used to examine the complete methyltransferase characteristic structural domains, and any protein containing the OMT structural domain was a member of the GiOMT protein family. They were named according to the localization of the GiOMT gene family on the chromosome.

Physicochemical characterization of the GiOMT proteins

Physical and chemical properties of GiOMT proteins were analyzed using the ExPASY (https://web.expasy.org/protparam/) online tool [67], mainly including protein length, Mw, pI, II, AI, and GRAVY. Subcellular localization prediction analysis of GiOMT proteins used the WoLF PSORT (https://wolfpsort.hgc.jp/) website [68].

Analysis of gene structure, conserved structural domains and conserved motifs of GiOMT gene family members

The conserved motifs of GiOMT proteins were predicted using MEME Suite 5.5.4 (https://meme-suite.org/meme/) online tool [69], in which the maximum number of motifs was 10 and all other parameters were defaulted, and the mast XML file was downloaded. Based on the annotated information of GFF3 gene structure, the conserved motifs and gene structures were visualized and analyzed by TBtools 1.09876 software [70].

Chromosomal localization and secondary structure analysis of GiOMT genes

The exact location of the GiOMT gene family members weredetermined on the G. inflata chromosome. Based on the transcriptome data of G. inflata obtained by sequencing and GFF3 information, chromosomal localization analysis were used TBtools software. The secondary structure of the GiOMT proteins were predicted by the SOPMA (https://npsa-prabi.ibcp.fr/cgi-bin/npsa_automat.pl?page=npsaSOPMA.html) [71].

Covariance analysis and phylogenetic tree construction of the GiOMT gene family

The covariance analysis of G. inflata with default parameters was performed using MCScanX [72], and the covariance results were visualized using TBtools. Phylogenetic tree construction of GiOMT was performed using Arabidopsis and alfalfa as the study background. The amino acid sequences of GiOMT were firstly aligned using the built-in ClustalW tool of MEGA X. The phylogenetic tree was constructed by the Neighbor-Joining method [73]. The quality of the evolutionary tree was assessed by the Bootstrap method [74], with the number of tests set to 1000. The evolutionary tree topology of GiOMT with other plant OMTs was visualized and annotated on the iTOL (https://itol.embl.de/) online website.

Analysis of cis-acting elements in the promoter of the GiOMT genes

The 2000 bp sequence upstream of the GiOMT genes were extracted using TBtools software, analyzed for promoter cis-acting elements by the PlantCare (http://bioinformatics.psb.ugent.be/webtools/plantcare/html/) [75], and finally visualized with the TBtools.

Prediction of protein interactions network of GiOMT gene family

Using the model plant Arabidopsis as a background, the GiOMT protein was submitted to the STRING (https://cn.string-db.org/) online database for its protein-protein interaction network analysis [76].

Transcriptome data analysis of members of the GiOMT gene family

The samples of G. inflata irradiated with UV-B for 0 d, 7 d and 15 d were used for transcriptomics analysis. The GO annotation information of the GiOMT family was selected, and the GO annotation map was drawn through the BioSignal Cloud Analysis Platform (https://www.omicshare.com/tools/) of Gene Denovo (Guangzhou, China). The expression amount information of the genes of the GiOMT family was extracted from the transcriptome data, and the heatmap was plotted using TBtools based on the FPKM values.

Expression analysis of GiOMT genes under UV-B treatment

The CDS sequences of the GiOMTs gene family were obtained from G. inflata transcriptome data by bioinformatics approach. The primers for GiOMTs genes qRT-PCR were designed using PrimerPremier 5 software and then synthesized by Sangon Biotech Co (Shanghai) (Table 6). Total RNA from different G. inflata materials was extracted using the Rapid RNA Extraction Kit for Tiangen Plant Tissues (DP452). The first strand of cDNA was synthesized using the RNA Reverse Transcription Reagent (KR118). The qRT-PCR analysis used the SYBR Green PreMix Plus (FP205). The Actin gene was used as an internal reference [77]. Three biological replicates were set up, and the relative expression levels were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method [78].

Statistical analysis

Data statistics and organization were performed using Excel 2016, ANOVA and significance tests were performed using SPSS 26, significance of means was tested via LSD at P < 0.05, and plotted using OriginPro 2018c software.

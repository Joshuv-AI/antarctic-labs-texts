On Continuously Differentiable Vector-Valued Functions of Non-Integer Order
Paulo M. de Carvalho-Neto, Renato Fehlberg Júnior

Abstract
. The function spaces of continuously differentiable functions are extensively studied and appear in various mathematical settings. In this context, we investigate the spaces of continuously fractional differentiable functions of order α > 0 \alpha>0 , considering both the Riemann-Liouville and Caputo fractional derivatives. We explore several fundamental properties of these spaces and, inspired by a result of Hardy and Littlewood, we compare them with the space of Hölder continuous functions. Our main objective is to establish a rigorous theoretical framework to support the study and further advancement of this subject.

1.
Introduction

Since its inception, fractional calculus has attracted the attention of many mathematicians and researchers from various scientific fields, due to both its rich theory, offering nuances distinct from classical calculus, and its wide range of applications (for a historical overview of fractional calculus, see
[
16
,
17
]
, and for classical results, refer to
[

A crucial contribution to the field was made by Hardy and Littlewood, who were among the first to prove fundamental results regarding the continuity of the Riemann-Liouville fractional integral of order
\alpha>0
; see
[
12
]
for details. Their studies inspired numerous mathematicians to further investigate this operator, leading to a vast body of research (see, for instance, a list of references in
[
4
]
).

Although many researchers study various topics in fractional calculus, several fundamental questions in the theory still require deeper investigation. In recent years, the authors have worked on addressing some of these gaps. For instance, a recent series of papers examined the Riemann-Liouville fractional integral as an operator on Bochner-Lebesgue spaces (see
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
]
).

A natural continuation of these studies is the connection between the continuity of fractional derivatives of continuous functions and differentiable spaces and its relationship with Hölder spaces. The regularity of fractional derivatives is closely tied to these spaces, and various works have discussed this relationship (see for instance
[
1
,
2
,
13
,
14
,

]
).

This work is organized as follows: in Section
2

4
, we discuss their regularity, connections with Hölder spaces, and show that these spaces form Banach algebras.

Preliminary Concepts

In this section, we introduce the definitions of the key objects used throughout this manuscript and establish some of their fundamental properties. We assume that
t_{0}<t_{1}
are fixed real numbers and that
X
is a Banach space.

An in-depth discussion of the Bochner-Lebesgue spaces
L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
can be found in

and the references therein. For further details on the space of continuously differentiable functions
C^{n}([t_{0},t_{1}];X)

Definition 1
.

Consider
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
and a function
f:[t_{0},t_{1}]\to X
.

(i)

The Riemann-Liouville (RL) fractional integral of order
\alpha
at
t_{0}
is defined as

(1)

J_{t_{0},t}^{\alpha}f(t):=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}f(s)\,ds,

for every
t\in[t_{0},t_{1}]
where the integral (
1
) exists. Here,
\Gamma
denotes the Euler gamma function.

(ii)

The Riemann-Liouville (RL) fractional derivative of order
\alpha
at
t_{0}
is given by

(2)

D_{t_{0},t}^{\alpha}f(t):=\frac{d^{[\alpha]}}{dt^{[\alpha]}}\left[J_{t_{0},t}^{[\alpha]-\alpha}f(t)\right],

for every
t\in[t_{0},t_{1}]
where the right-hand side of (
2
) exists. Here, and throughout this work,
[\alpha]
denotes the smallest integer greater than or equal to
\alpha
. The derivative
d^{[\alpha]}/dt^{[\alpha]}
is understood in the weak sense.

(iii)

The Caputo fractional derivative of order
\alpha
at
t_{0}
is given by

(3)

cD_{t_{0},t}^{\alpha}f(t):=D_{t_{0},t}^{\alpha}\left[f(t)-\sum_{j=0}^{[\alpha]-1}\frac{f^{(j)}(t_{0})}{j!}(t-t_{0})^{j}\right],

for every
t\in[t_{0},t_{1}]
where the right-hand side of (
3
) exists.

3.
The spaces of fractional continuously differentiable functions

In this section, we introduce the spaces of functions with continuous fractional derivatives, denoted by
RL^{\alpha}([t_{0},t_{1}];X)
and
C^{\alpha}([t_{0},t_{1}];X)
. We establish some fundamental properties of these spaces and explore the relationships between them.

Definition 5
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
and consider the following function spaces:

(i)

the RL
\alpha-
times continuously differentiable functions in
[t_{0},t_{1}]
is the set

RL^{\alpha}\big([t_{0},t_{1}],X\big):=\Big\{f\in C^{[\alpha]-1}([t_{0},t_{1}],X):D^{\alpha}_{t_{0},t}f\in C^{0}([t_{0},t_{1}],X)\Big\}.

(ii)

the Caputo
\alpha-
times continuously differentiable functions in
[t_{0},t_{1}]
is the set

C^{\alpha}\big([t_{0},t_{1}],X\big):=\Big\{f\in C^{[\alpha]-1}([t_{0},t_{1}],X):cD^{\alpha}_{t_{0},t}f\in C^{0}([t_{0},t_{1}],X)\Big\}.

4.
Properties of Fractional Continuously Differentiable Functions

In this section, we investigate the relationship between the spaces
RL^{\alpha}\big([t_{0},t_{1}];X)
,
C^{\alpha}\big([t_{0},t_{1}];X)
and the Hölder spaces. Additionally, we discuss the conditions under which these spaces constitute Banach algebras.

4.1.
Fractional Continuously Differentiable Functions and Hölder Continuity

We begin by recalling the definition of Hölder continuity.

Definition 16
.

If
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\gamma\in(0,1)
, we define
H^{n,\gamma}(I;X)
as the space of functions
f:I\rightarrow X
that are
n
-times continuously differentiable and satisfy

\big\|f^{(n)}(t)-f^{(n)}(s)\big\|_{X}\leq M{|t-s|^{\gamma}},

for every
t,s\in[t_{0},t_{1}]
and some constant
M>0
. If
I
is compact, equipping this space with the norm

\|f\|_{H^{n,\gamma}(I;X)}:=\|f\|_{C^{n}(I;X)}+[\,f^{(n)}\,]_{H^{0,\gamma}(I;X)},

where

[\,f\,]_{H^{0,\gamma}(I;X)}:=\sup_{t,s\in I,\,\,t\not=s}\left[\dfrac{\big\|f(t)-f(s)\big\|_{X}}{|t-s|^{\gamma}}\right],

makes
H^{n,\gamma}(I;X)
a Banach space. Here,
f^{(j)}(t)
denotes the standard
j
-th derivative of
f(t)
. These functions are called Hölder continuously differentiable functions of order
n
with exponent
\gamma
. Moreover, we define

H^{n,\gamma}_{t_{0}}([t_{0},t_{1}],X):=\big\{f\in H^{n,\gamma}([t_{0},t_{1}],X)\mid f^{(i)}(t_{0})=0,\forall i\in\{0,\ldots,n\}\big\}.

In their seminal 1928 work
[
12
]
, Hardy and Littlewood established foundational theorems on the boundedness of the Riemann–Liouville fractional integral acting on
L^{p}
spaces. Below, we present the result from their study that is most relevant to our analysis, using our notation.

Theorem 17

(
[
12
, Theorem 19]
)

Assume that
0<\alpha<\beta\leq 1
. Then
H^{0,\beta}_{t_{0}}([t_{0},t_{1}],\mathbb{R})\subset RL^{\alpha}([t_{0},t_{1}],\mathbb{R})
.

We provide a nontrivial proof that not only clarifies the structure of the result but also extends it to the general case where
X
is a Banach space. This is achieved through a key characterization of the fractional derivative for Hölder continuous functions, from which the continuity of the inclusion follows directly.

3
,

D^{n+\alpha}_{t_{0},t}f(t)=\dfrac{d^{n+1}}{dt^{n+1}}J^{1-\alpha}_{t_{0},t}f(t)=\dfrac{d}{dt}\Big[J^{1-\alpha}_{t_{0},t}f^{(n)}(t)\Big].

Since
f^{(n)}\in H^{0,\beta}_{t_{0}}([t_{0},t_{1}];X)
, the result follows by reducing to the case
n=0
, which has already been fully addressed in Theorem
18
.

∎

We now apply the results established above to study the relationship between the spaces
H^{n,\beta}([t_{0},t_{1}],X)
and
C^{n+\alpha}([t_{0},t_{1}],X)
.

Corollary 20
.

?
\langle
holder2
\rangle
?

Let
0<\alpha<\beta\leq 1
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
H^{n,\beta}([t_{0},t_{1}],X)\subsetneq C^{n+\alpha}([t_{0},t_{1}],X)
continuously.

Proof.

Let
f\in H^{n,\beta}([t_{0},t_{1}],X)
. To show that
f\in C^{n+\alpha}([t_{0},t_{1}],X)
, we need only to prove that

cD^{n+\alpha}_{t_{0},t}f(t)=D_{t_{0},t}^{\alpha}\left[f(t)-\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{f^{(k)}(t_{0})}{k!}\big(t-t_{0}\big)^{k}\right]\in C^{0}([t_{0},t_{1}],X).

To this end, define

g(t)=f(t)-\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{f^{(k)}(t_{0})}{k!}\big(t-t_{0}\big)^{k},

and note that
g\in H^{n,\beta}_{t_{0}}([t_{0},t_{1}],X)
.

Since

cD^{n+\alpha}_{t_{0},t}f(t)=D^{n+\alpha}_{t_{0},t}g(t),

by applying Corollary
19
to function
g
we obtain the result.
∎

Remark 21
.

?
\langle
remm
\rangle
?

(i)

Note that Theorem
18
and Corollaries
19
and
20
do not hold in the limiting case
\alpha=\beta
. To illustrate this, let
\alpha>0
and suppose the domain is
[t_{0},t_{1}]
. Define
\tilde{t}=(t_{0}+t_{1})/2
, and consider the function
f(t)
given by

f(t)=0
on
[t_{0},\tilde{t})
, and
f(t)=1
on
[\tilde{t},t_{1}]
.

A straightforward computation shows that

J^{\alpha}_{t_{0},t}f(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&\textrm{ for }t\in[t_{0},\tilde{t}),\\
\dfrac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}(t-\tilde{t})^{\alpha},&\textrm{ for }t\in[\tilde{t},t_{1}].\end{array}\right.

Then, by applying
[
7
, Theorem 9]
, we conclude that

J^{\alpha}_{t_{0},t}f\in H^{[\alpha]-1,\alpha+1-[\alpha]}_{t_{0}}([t_{0},t_{1}],\mathbb{R}).

However, a direct computation shows that
D^{\alpha}_{t_{0},t}J^{\alpha}_{t_{0},t}f(t)=f(t)
for almost every
t\in[t_{0},t_{1}]
, and
f
has no representative in
C^{0}([t_{0},t_{1}],\mathbb{R})
, which proves that the inclusion in Theorem
18
is strict. The corollaries follow directly from this conclusion.

(ii)

For the case
\alpha\in(0,1)
, we also refer to the well-known counterexample originally presented by Hardy and Littlewood in
[
12
, Remark 5.8]
(cf.
[
11
,
15
,
18
]
). There, they consider
\sigma\in(1,\infty)
and define the Weierstrass function
W_{\alpha}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
by

W_{\alpha}(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sigma^{-j\alpha}\cos{(\sigma^{j}t)},

which they observe to be Hölder continuous of order
\alpha
, yet lacking a fractional derivative of order
\alpha
at any point.

By defining
\omega_{\alpha}:[t_{0},t_{1}]\to\mathbb{R}
as

\omega_{\alpha}(t):=W_{\alpha}(t)-W_{\alpha}(t_{0}),

we obtain a function satisfying
\omega_{\alpha}\in H^{0,\alpha}_{t_{0}}([t_{0},t_{1}];X)
that also does not admit a fractional derivative of order
\alpha
at any point. This construction thus provides an example illustrating that
H^{0,\alpha}_{t_{0}}([t_{0},t_{1}];X)\not\subset RL^{\alpha}([t_{0},t_{1}];X)
. Nevertheless, we emphasize that this example is significantly stronger than what is strictly necessary to establish such a counterexample (cf. item
(i)
above).

To complete the analysis of the relationship between these spaces, we now consider the remaining case, which, to the best of our knowledge, has not been addressed in any known reference.

Theorem 22
.

?
\langle
HRL
\rangle
?

Let
0<\alpha\leq 1
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
RL^{n+\alpha}([t_{0},t_{1}],X)\subsetneq H^{n,\alpha}_{t_{0}}([t_{0},t_{1}],X)
continuously.

Proof.

Let
f\in RL^{n+\alpha}([t_{0},t_{1}],X)
. From item
(iii)
of Proposition
3
and Theorem
4
, it follows that

J^{\alpha}_{t_{0},t}D^{n+\alpha}_{t_{0},t}f(t)=J^{\alpha}_{t_{0},t}\left\{\dfrac{d^{n+1}}{dt^{n+1}}\left[J_{t_{0},t}^{1-\alpha}f(t)\right]\right\}=f^{(n)}(t),

for every
t\in(t_{0},t_{1}]
. Moreover, Theorem
4
and Proposition
7
also guarantee that

J^{\alpha}_{t_{0},t}D^{n+\alpha}_{t_{0},t}f(t)\big|_{t=t_{0}}=f^{(n)}(t_{0})=0.

Now, fix
x,y\in(t_{0},t_{1}]
(the case
x=t_{0}
or
y=t_{0}
is analogous) and assume, without loss of generality, that
x>y
(the case
x=y
is straightforward). Then, we compute:

(20)

\|f^{(n)}(x)-f^{(n)}(y)\|_{X}=\left\|J^{\alpha}_{t_{0},x}\big[D^{n+\alpha}_{t_{0},x}f(x)\big]-J^{\alpha}_{t_{0},y}\big[D^{n+\alpha}_{t_{0},y}f(y)\big]\right\|_{X}\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\left\|\int_{t_{0}}^{y}\big[(x-s)^{\alpha-1}-(y-s)^{\alpha-1}\big]D^{n+\alpha}_{t_{0},s}f(s)\,ds\right.\\
\left.+\int_{y}^{x}(x-s)^{\alpha-1}D^{n+\alpha}_{t_{0},s}f(s)\,ds\right\|_{X}.

Since
x>y
, we have
(x-s)^{\alpha-1}<(y-s)^{\alpha-1}
for all
s\in[t_{0},y]
. Therefore, from (
20
) we obtain:

(21)

\|f^{(n)}(x)-f^{(n)}(y)\|_{X}\leq\left[\frac{\|D^{n+\alpha}_{t_{0},\cdot}f\|_{C^{0}([t_{0},t_{1}];X)}}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}\right]\left[2(x-y)^{\alpha}+(y-t_{0})^{\alpha}-(x-t_{0})^{\alpha}\right].

To simplify this estimate, define the auxiliary function
g:[y,t_{1}]\to\mathbb{R}
by

g(w)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{2(w-y)^{\alpha}+(y-t_{0})^{\alpha}-(w-t_{0})^{\alpha}}{(w-y)^{\alpha}},&\text{if }w>y,\\
2,&\text{if }w=y.\end{array}\right.

A direct computation shows that
g(w)\leq 2
for all
w\in[y,t_{1}]
. Hence, from (
21
) it follows that

\frac{\|f^{(n)}(x)-f^{(n)}(y)\|_{X}}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}\leq\frac{2\|D^{n+\alpha}_{t_{0},\cdot}f\|_{C^{0}([t_{0},t_{1}];X)}}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}.

This proves that
f^{(n)}(t)
is Hölder continuous of order
\alpha
on
[t_{0},t_{1}]
, and thus the inclusion holds. The continuity is an immediate consequence of the above inequality. Finally, the proper inclusion follows from the same example given at item
(i)
of Remark
21
.
∎

Corollary 23
.

?
\langle
HRL2
\rangle
?

Let
0<\alpha\leq 1
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
C^{n+\alpha}([t_{0},t_{1}],X)\subset H^{n,\alpha}([t_{0},t_{1}],X)
.

Proof.

Let
f\in C^{n+\alpha}([t_{0},t_{1}],X)
. As argumented in the proof of Corollary
9
,

g(t):=f(t)-\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{f^{(k)}(t_{0})}{k!}\big(t-t_{0}\big)^{k}\in RL^{n+\alpha}([t_{0},t_{1}],X).

By Theorem
22
,
g\in H^{n,\alpha}_{t_{0}}([t_{0},t_{1}],X)
, that is,
f\in H^{n,\alpha}([t_{0},t_{1}],X)
. Besides, by Theorem
4
,

J^{\alpha}_{t_{0},t}\big[cD^{\alpha}_{t_{0},t}f^{(n)}(t)\big]=J^{\alpha}_{t_{0},t}\Big\{D^{\alpha}_{t_{0},t}\big[f^{(n)}(t)-f^{(n)}(t_{0})\big]\Big\}\\
=J^{\alpha}_{t_{0},t}\left\{D^{\alpha}_{t_{0},t}\left[\frac{d^{n}}{dt^{n}}g(t)\right]\right\}=J^{\alpha}_{t_{0},t}\Big[D^{n+\alpha}_{t_{0},t}g(t)\Big].

Now, the continuity follows from Theorem
22
.

∎

Remark 24
.

A natural question that arises concerns the characterization of functions the belong to
\bigcap_{\alpha\in(0,1)}C^{\alpha}\big([t_{0},t_{1}];\mathbb{R}\big)
. By the aforementioned results, Theorem
22
and Corollaries
19
,
20
and
23
, we have the identity

\bigcap_{\alpha\in(\beta,\gamma)}H^{0,\alpha}([t_{0},t_{1}];X)=\bigcap_{\alpha\in(\beta,\gamma)}C^{\alpha}([t_{0},t_{1}];X),

for every
0\leq\beta<\gamma\leq 1
. An interesting consequence is that this space contains functions that do not belong to
C^{1}([t_{0},t_{1}];X)
. In fact, it is already known that the vector space
\bigcap_{\alpha\in(0,1)}H^{0,\alpha}([t_{0},t_{1}];X)
may include functions that are not even Lipschitz continuous. The classical Weierstrass function provides an example of such behavior (cf.
[
11
,
12
,
18
]
).

4.2.
The spaces
RL^{\alpha}\big([t_{0},t_{1}];X\big)
and
C^{\alpha}\big([t_{0},t_{1}];X\big)
as Banach Algebras.

In this section, we investigate the conditions under which the spaces
RL^{\alpha}\big([t_{0},t_{1}];X\big)
and
C^{\alpha}\big([t_{0},t_{1}];X\big)
are Banach algebras. To this end, we begin by recalling the following result established by Alsaedi, Ahmad, and Kirane in
[
1
, Lemma 1]
:

Theorem 25
.

?
\langle
eqAAK
\rangle
?

Let
0<\alpha<1
. Let one of the following conditions be satisfied:

(i)

u\in C^{0}([0,T];\mathbb{R})
and
v\in H^{0,\beta}([0,T];\mathbb{R})
, with
\alpha<\beta\leq 1
;

(ii)

v\in C^{0}([0,T];\mathbb{R})
and
u\in H^{0,\beta}([0,T];\mathbb{R})
, with
\alpha<\beta\leq 1
;

(iii)

v\in H^{0,\beta}([0,T];\mathbb{R})
and
u\in H^{0,\gamma}([0,T];\mathbb{R})
, with
\alpha<\beta+\gamma
,
0<\beta,\gamma<1
.

Then we have

(22)

D^{\alpha}_{0,t}(uv)(t)=u(t)D^{\alpha}_{0,t}v(t)+v(t)D^{\alpha}_{0,t}u(t)\\
-\frac{\alpha}{\Gamma{(1-\alpha)}}\int_{0}^{t}{\frac{\big[u(s)-u(t)\big]\big[v(s)-v(t)\big]}{(t-s)^{\alpha+1}}ds}-\frac{u(t)v(t)}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)t^{\alpha}},

for every
t\in(0,T]
.

We would like to point out that the hypotheses in Theorem
25
, as currently stated, might not be sufficient to guarantee the validity of identity (
22
) in all cases. Specific counterexamples indicate that additional conditions may be necessary to ensure the result holds. Below, we present illustrative examples corresponding to each of the items listed in the theorem.

(i)

Consider
u(t)=\omega_{\alpha}(t)
, where
\omega_{\alpha}(t)
is the function defined in item
(ii)
of Remark
21
, and let
v(t)=1
. If item
(i)
of Theorem
25
were valid as stated, we could apply it to deduce that their product, which is simply
\omega_{\alpha}(t)
, possesses a RL fractional derivative. However, we already know that
\omega_{\alpha}(t)
does not admit such a derivative.

(ii)

Analogous to the above commentary, just take
v(t)=\omega_{\alpha}(t)
and
u(t)=1
.

(iii)

Here there would be no counter example if
\beta,\gamma>\alpha
(see Theorem
18
), since in this case
D_{t}^{\alpha}u\in C^{0}([0,T];\mathbb{R})
and
D_{t}^{\alpha}v\in C^{0}([0,T];\mathbb{R})
. If
\beta\leq\alpha
or
\gamma\leq\alpha
, then we could repeat the arguments from items
(i)
or
(ii)
, depending on the situation to obtain the contradiction.

Given the preceding discussion, we understand that the statement of Theorem
25
requires the addition of certain assumptions to ensure the validity of identity (
22
). A possible minimal set of complementary hypotheses that guarantees the result is the following:

(i)

u\in C^{0}([0,T];\mathbb{R})
, the RL fractional derivative of order
\alpha
of
u
exists at
t\in(0,T]
, and
v\in H^{0,\beta}([0,T];\mathbb{R})
, with
\alpha<\beta\leq 1
;

(ii)

v\in C^{0}([0,T];\mathbb{R})
, the RL fractional derivative of order
\alpha
of
v
exists at
t\in(0,T]
, and
u\in H^{0,\beta}([0,T];\mathbb{R})
, with
\alpha<\beta\leq 1
;

(iii)

v\in H^{0,\beta}([0,T];\mathbb{R})
,
u\in H^{0,\gamma}([0,T];\mathbb{R})
, with
\alpha<\beta+\gamma
,
0<\beta,\gamma<1
, and the RL fractional derivatives of order
\alpha
of both
u
and
v
exists at
t\in(0,T]
.

In addition to the adjustments proposed above to make Theorem
25
suitable for the applications we develop in the final part of this work, we also consider it important to point out some additional observations about identity (
22
).

Remark 26
.

(i)

In the case of the Caputo derivative, we have:

cD^{\alpha}_{0,t}(uv)(t)=u(t)\,cD^{\alpha}_{0,t}v(t)+v(t)\,cD^{\alpha}_{0,t}u(t)\\
-\frac{\alpha}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\big[u(s)-u(t)\big]\big[v(s)-v(t)\big]}{(t-s)^{\alpha+1}}\,ds-\frac{\big[u(t)-u(0)\big]\big[v(t)-v(0)\big]}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)t^{\alpha}}.

(ii)

All these identities continue to hold when the fractional derivatives are considered with respect to a general initial point
t_{0}\neq 0
, over the interval
[t_{0},t_{1}]
.

It is a classical result that the product of two differentiable functions is itself differentiable. To conclude our work, we present the fractional counterpart of this property. Specifically, we prove that the product of two functions, each possessing a continuous RL (or Caputo) fractional derivative, also admits a continuous fractional derivative of the same type.

Theorem 27
.

?
\langle
fcoro
\rangle
?

Let
0<\alpha<1
, then
RL^{\alpha}([t_{0},t_{1}],\mathbb{R})
and
C^{\alpha}([t_{0},t_{1}],\mathbb{R})
are Banach algebras.

Proof.

Since the two cases share analogous proofs, we focus only on proving that
RL^{\alpha}([t_{0},t_{1}],\mathbb{R})
is a Banach algebra. Let
f,g\in RL^{\alpha}([t_{0},t_{1}],\mathbb{R})
. As
fg\in C^{0}([t_{0},t_{1}],\mathbb{R})
, it remains to verify that
D^{\alpha}_{t_{0},t}(fg)\in C^{0}([t_{0},t_{1}],\mathbb{R})
. By Theorem
22
, we have that
f,g\in H^{0,\alpha}([t_{0},t_{1}],\mathbb{R})
, and thus we may apply item
(iii)
of the refined version of Theorem
25
. This ensures that
D^{\alpha}_{t_{0},t}(f(t)g(t))
exists and satisfies the identity (
22
) for every
t\in(t_{0},t_{1}]
. Since
f(t_{0})=g(t_{0})=0
and
f,g\in H^{0,\alpha}([t_{0},t_{1}],\mathbb{R})
, we conclude that

\lim_{t\to t_{0}}D_{t_{0},t}^{\alpha}(f(t)g(t))=0,

proving that the right-hand side of (
22
) is continuous, which implies that
D^{\alpha}_{t_{0},t}(fg)\in C^{0}([t_{0},t_{1}],\mathbb{R})
.

∎

Finally, it follows directly from the definition of the vector-valued spaces
RL^{\alpha}([t_{0},t_{1}],X)
and
C^{\alpha}([t_{0},t_{1}],X)
, from Remark
10
and Theorem
27
, that the following result also holds true.

Corollary 28
.

If
\alpha>0
and
X
is an Banach algebra, then the spaces
RL^{\alpha}([t_{0},t_{1}],X)
and
C^{\alpha}([t_{0},t_{1}],X)
are Banach algebras.

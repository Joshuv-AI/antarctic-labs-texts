Partition functions of determinantal point processes on polarized Kähler manifolds
Kiyoon Eum

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the full asymptotic expansion of the partition functions of determinantal point processes defined on a polarized Kähler manifold. We show that the coefficients of the expansion are given by geometric functionals on Kähler metrics satisfying the cocycle identity, whose first variations can be expressed through the TYZ expansion coefficients of the Bergman kernel. In particular, these functionals naturally generalize the Mabuchi functional in Kähler geometry and the Liouville functional on Riemann surfaces. We further show that Futaki-type holomorphic invariants obstruct the existence of critical points of these geometric functionals, extending Lu’s formula. We also verify that certain formulas remain valid up to the third coefficient without assuming polarization. Finally, we discuss the relation of our results to the quantum Hall effect (QHE), where the determinantal point process provides a microscopic model. In particular, we recover the higher-dimensional effective Chern-Simons actions derived in the physics literature and confirm a conjecture of Klevtsov on the form of the partition function asymptotics.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study the asymptotic expansion of the partition functions of determinantal point processes defined on a polarized Kähler manifold. These processes were introduced by Berman in a series of papers
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
and have been shown to satisfy a large deviation principle.

We derive a full asymptotic expansion of the partition function in two ways: one using Bergman kernel asymptotics and the other using the Quillen anomaly formula along with the asymptotic expansion of the Ray-Singer analytic torsion. By combining these two expressions, we analyze the properties of each coefficient in the asymptotic expansion. Each coefficient is given by geometric functionals on Kähler metrics that satisfy the cocycle identity, and its first variation can be expressed in terms of the coefficients of the Bergman kernel asymptotics. In particular, the second coefficient corresponds to the Mabuchi functional in Kähler geometry, while the third coefficient generalizes the Liouville functional on Riemann surfaces to higher dimensions.

Furthermore, we show that the holomorphic invariant introduced by Futaki
[
40
]
obstructs the existence of critical points for each geometric functional given by the coefficients of the asymptotic expansion. Although the construction relies on the polarization of the Kähler class, we demonstrate through direct computation that the results remain valid up to the third coefficient, without the polarization assumption. We will also discuss the connection between our results and the physics literature.

1.1.
Mathematical background

Let
(M,\omega)
be a polarized Kähler manifold. That is, there is a Hermitian line bundle
(L,h)
over
M
such that its Chern curvature form is given by
-i\omega
. Let
d_{k}:=\dim H^{0}(M,L^{k})
. The determinantal point process (DPP) associated with
L^{k}
is a probability measure
\mu_{k,\omega}
on
M^{d_{k}}
defined as

\mu_{k,\omega}:=\frac{1}{Z_{k}[h]}|\Psi^{k}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{d_{k}})|^{2}_{(h^{k})^{\boxtimes d_{k}}}\prod_{i=1}^{d_{k}}\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!},

where
\Psi^{k}
is a holomorphic section of
(L^{k})^{\boxtimes d_{k}}
over
M^{d_{k}}
given by the Slater determinant. A more detailed definition will be given in Section
3
. It was introduced by Berman and subsequently studied by him, particularly regarding its relation to canonical Kähler metrics
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
. See also
[
37
,
36
,
2
]
for related results.

The normalization factor
Z_{k}[h]
, also known as the partition function, is essentially Donaldson’s
\mathcal{L}_{k}
-functional introduced in
[
28
]
. The first term in the large
k
asymptotics of
Z_{k}[h]
without assuming positivity of the curvature of
h
, was studied by Berman-Boucksom
[
6
]
, extending the method of
[
28
]
. For a recent extension of their results, see
[
34
]
. In this paper, we focus on the (smooth) positive curvature case and derive the full asymptotic expansion of
Z_{k}[h]
as
k\rightarrow\infty
, identifying its coefficients with geometric functionals on Kähler metrics.

The relation between the asymptotic expansion of
Z_{k}[h]
and Bergman kernel asymptotics was given in
[
28
]
. Following
[
44
,
57
]
, we have an alternative way to obtain an asymptotic expansion of
Z_{k}[h]
using the Quillen anomaly formula
[
15
]
and the asymptotic expansion of the Ray-Singer analytic torsion
[
16
,
33
]
. We will review the Quillen anomaly formula and Ray-Singer analytic torsion in Section
2.3
. Combining these two approaches, we can study the properties of the coefficients in the asymptotic expansion.

From the Quillen anomaly formula approach, the coefficients are expressed as geometric functionals involving Bott-Chern forms. Bott-Chern forms were first used by Donaldson
[
25
,
26
]
to construct geometric functionals and were further developed by Tian
[
60
,
62
]
in the context of Kähler geometry. See also
[
65
]
. Bott-Chern forms and related materials will be reviewed in Section
2.2
.

From the Bergman kernel asymptotics approach, the first variation of the geometric functionals is expressed in terms of integrals of scalar quantities involving the curvature of the Kähler metrics, arising from the Bergman kernel asymptotic expansion. In particular, the critical point equation is of the form
a_{j}-\Delta a_{j-1}\equiv\text{constant}
, where
a_{j}
is the
j^{th}
coefficient in the TYZ expansion of the Bergman kernel; see (
2.1
). Since the works of Catlin, Ruan, Tian, and Zelditch
[
21
,
55
,
59
,
68
]
, Bergman kernel asymptotics has become a well-studied subject with various approaches; see, for example,
[
24
,
5
,
29
,
66
,
41
]
. Especially, we will use the explicit expression for
a_{j},\;j\leq 3
that Lu computed in
[
49
]
. By the result of
[
48
]
, we know that the equation
a_{j}-\Delta a_{j-1}\equiv\text{constant}
is an elliptic equation of order
2j+2
in Kähler potential.

In
[
40
]
, Futaki introduced a family of holomorphic invariants that generalize various integral invariants, including the Futaki invariant
[
38
]
and the Bando-Futaki invariant
[
4
]
. We show that these invariants serve as obstructions to the existence of critical points of our geometric functionals
S_{j}
. As a byproduct, we extend Lu’s formula
(4.4)
in
[
50
]
to all
j\geq 0
, where it was originally verified by direct computation up to
j=2
.

In the standard GIT picture of Kähler geometry, geometric functionals correspond to the log-norm functionals, while Futaki-type invariants correspond to the weight. See also
[
35
,
30
]
for a moment map interpretation of the functions
a_{j}-\Delta a_{j-1}
. Thus, equations of the form
a_{j}-\Delta a_{j-1}\equiv\text{constant}
formally fit into the GIT setting except the convexity of the log-norm functional. We compute the second variation of
S_{2}
with a view to this problem. See
[
23
]
and references therein for related work on equations of the form
a_{j}\equiv\text{constant}
.

Throughout this paper, we emphasize when the polarization assumption is not used. In particular, in the Appendix
A
, we directly verify some of our results without the polarization assumption, following the spirit of original work of Mabuchi
[
52
]
.

Relation to physics literature

In the physics literature, the determinantal point process on Riemann surfaces corresponds to the plasma analogy in the quantum Hall effect (QHE). For a general physics background on the QHE, see
[

]

Recently, higher-dimensional QHE has also been studied in cases where the even-dimensional spatial manifold has a complex structure

,

In

1.3.
Statement of the main results

Now we state our main results, starting with the asymptotic expansion of the DPP partition function. Note that in the main text, we will use a slightly different normalization convention, which will introduce additional
2\pi
factors. Also we will identify a hermitian metric
h
with Kähler potential
\varphi
. Let
\mathcal{K}_{0}
be the space of Kähler forms in
[\omega]
and
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}
be the space of Kähler potentials.

Theorem
1

(Asymptotics of the Partition Functions)
.

\log\frac{Z_{k}[\varphi]}{Z_{k}[0]}
admits the following form of asymptotic expansion as
k\rightarrow\infty
:

(1.1)

\log\frac{Z_{k}[\varphi]}{Z_{k}[0]}=kd_{k}S_{0}[\varphi,0]+k^{n}S_{1}[\omega_{\varphi},\omega]+k^{n-1}S_{2}[\omega_{\varphi},\omega]+k^{n-2}S_{3}[\omega_{\varphi},\omega]+\cdots.

For
j>0
,
S_{j}[\cdot,\cdot]:\mathcal{K}_{0}\times\mathcal{K}_{0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfies

(1)

Cocyle identity : for any three Kähler metrics
\omega_{2},\omega_{1},\omega_{0}
in
\mathcal{K}_{0}
,

\displaystyle S_{j}[\omega_{1},\omega_{0}]

\displaystyle=-S_{j}[\omega_{0},\omega_{1}],

\displaystyle S_{j}[\omega_{2},\omega_{0}]

\displaystyle=S_{j}[\omega_{2},\omega_{1}]+S_{j}[\omega_{1},\omega_{0}];

(2)

The derivative of
S_{j}[\omega_{\varphi},\omega]
on
\mathcal{K}_{0}=\mathcal{K}_{\omega}/\mathbb{R}
is given by

(1.2)

\delta_{\varphi}S_{j}[\omega_{\varphi},\omega]=\int_{M}\delta\varphi\left(\widehat{a_{j}(\omega_{\varphi})}+\Delta_{\varphi}a_{j-1}(\omega_{\varphi})-a_{j}(\omega_{\varphi})\right)\frac{\omega_{\varphi}^{n}}{n!},

where
\widehat{a_{j}(\omega_{\varphi})}
denotes the average of
a_{j}(\omega_{\varphi})

\widehat{a_{j}(\omega_{\varphi})}:=\frac{1}{V}\int_{M}a_{j}(\omega_{\varphi})\frac{\omega_{\varphi}^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{V}\int_{M}\Td_{j}(T^{1,0}M)\ch_{n-j}(L),

which does not depend on
\omega_{\varphi}
.

(3)

For
j>n+1
,
S_{j}[\omega_{2},\omega_{1}]
is an exact cocyle. That is, it can be written as a difference

S_{j}[\omega_{2},\omega_{1}]=s_{j}[\omega_{2}]-s_{j}[\omega_{1}],

for a local functional
s_{j}[\cdot]:\mathcal{K}_{0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
of the metric.

One can check that
S_{0}
is (minus of) the Aubin-Yau functional
I
, and
S_{1}
is the Mabuchi functional
\mathcal{M}
in Kähler geometry. We will show that
S_{2}
is explicitly given by

\displaystyle S_{2}[\omega_{\varphi},\omega]=

\displaystyle-i\int_{M}BC(\Td_{2};\omega_{\varphi},\omega)\frac{\omega^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}+\Td_{2}(R_{\varphi})\frac{-i}{(n-1)!}\sum^{n-2}_{s=0}\varphi\omega_{\varphi}^{s}\wedge\omega^{n-2-s}

\displaystyle+\frac{i}{V}\left(\int_{M}\Td_{2}(T^{1,0}M)\frac{\omega^{n-2}}{(n-2)!}\right)\times\int_{M}\frac{-i}{(n+1)!}\sum^{n}_{s=0}\varphi\omega_{\varphi}^{s}\wedge\omega^{n-s}.

As we will see,
S_{2}
reduces to the Liouville action in dimension
n=1
. We compute its second variation as follows:

Proposition
1
.

Let
\varphi_{t}
be a smooth path in
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}
with
\varphi_{0}=0
. Then we have the following.

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\right|_{t=0}S_{2}[\omega_{t},\omega]=\int_{M}\ddot{\varphi}\left(\widehat{a_{2}}+\frac{1}{6}\Delta S-\frac{1}{24}\left(|R|^{2}-4|\ric|^{2}+3S^{2}\right)\right)\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}+\widehat{a_{2}}\dot{\varphi}\Delta\dot{\varphi}\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle\;+\frac{i}{6}\partial\Delta\dot{\varphi}\wedge\bar{\partial}\Delta\dot{\varphi}\wedge\frac{\omega^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}-\frac{i}{6}\partial\dot{\varphi}\wedge\bar{\partial}\dot{\varphi}\wedge i\partial\bar{\partial}S\wedge\frac{\omega^{n-2}}{(n-2)!}

\displaystyle\;+\frac{1}{4}(\Delta\dot{\varphi})^{2}S\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}+\frac{i}{8}\partial\dot{\varphi}\wedge\bar{\partial}\dot{\varphi}\wedge\ric^{2}\wedge\frac{\omega^{n-3}}{(n-3)!}+\frac{i}{24}\partial\dot{\varphi}\wedge\bar{\partial}\dot{\varphi}\wedge\Tr(R^{2})\wedge\frac{\omega^{n-3}}{(n-3)!}

\displaystyle\;-\frac{1}{2}\Delta\dot{\varphi}\langle i\partial\bar{\partial}\dot{\varphi},\ric\rangle\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}+\frac{1}{12}\left(g^{i\bar{q}}g^{p\bar{j}}g^{r\bar{l}}g^{k\bar{s}}R_{p\bar{q}r\bar{s}}\partial_{i}\partial_{\bar{j}}\dot{\varphi}\partial_{k}\partial_{\bar{l}}\dot{\varphi}\right)\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}.

The following Theorem generalizes formula
[
50
, (4.4)]
for all
j\geq 0
. Let
X
be a holomorphic vector field on
M
and
\theta_{X}
be a holomorphy potential function satisfying
\iota_{X}\omega=-\bar{\partial}\theta_{X}
.

Theorem
2
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a polarized Kähler manifold. Let
X
and
\theta_{X}
be given as above and suppose it is purely imaginary. Then for all
j\geq 0
, we have the following identity:

(1.3)

\int_{M}\theta_{X}\left(a_{j}(\omega)-\Delta a_{j-1}(\omega)\right)\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{(n+1-j)!}\int_{M}\Td_{j}(R+\nabla X)(\omega+\theta_{X})^{n+1-j}.

More generally, for non purely imaginary
\theta_{X}

(1.4)

\int_{M}i{\rm Im\,}\theta\left(a_{j}(\omega)-\Delta a_{j-1}(\omega)\right)\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{(n+1-j)!}\int_{M}\Td_{j}(R+\frac{1}{2}(\nabla X+(\overline{\nabla X})^{\flat,\sharp}))(\omega+i{\rm Im\,}\theta)^{n+1-j}.

Under the normalization of

The right hand side of (

Proposition

Suppose that

is a critical point of

in

Finally, we derive a formula for the effective action of the higher-dimensional QHE, as presented in

Proposition

The effective action for the higher-dimensional quantum Hall effect associated with

S_{eff}=2\int_{M\times[0,1]}\left[\Td(R_{\mathbf{T^{\prime}M}}(\bm{\omega}))CS(\ch;\bm{\nabla}_{\mathbf{L}},\bm{\nabla^{0}}_{\mathbf{L}})+CS(\Td;\bm{\nabla}_{\mathbf{T^{\prime}M}},\bm{\nabla^{0}}_{\mathbf{T^{\prime}M}})\ch(R_{\mathbf{L}}(\mathbf{h_{0}}))\right]_{2n+1}+\widetilde{S}.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Bergman kernel asymptotics

In this paper, we consider
n
-dimensional polarized compact Kähler manifold
(M,g,\omega)
,
g
is a Kähler metric,
\omega
is a Kähler form with Hermitian ample line bundle
(L,h)
whose Chern curvature form is given by
R(h)=-i\omega
. For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
h
induces a Hermitian metric
h^{k}
on
L^{k}
.
Together with Kähler volume form
\omega^{n}/n!
, it induces the
L^{2}
metric on
H^{0}(M,L^{k})
. The diagonal of the
k^{th}
Bergman kernel
\rho_{k}(\omega)
associated with these data is defined by

\rho_{k}(\omega)=\sum_{i=1}^{d_{k}}||S_{i}^{k}||_{h^{k}}^{2}\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R}),

where
d_{k}=\dim H^{0}(M,L^{k})
and
\left(S_{i}^{k}\right)_{i=1}^{d_{k}}
is any orthonormal basis of
H^{0}(M,L^{k})
with respect to
L^{2}
metric induced by
h^{k}
and
\omega^{n}/n!
.

Since the initial works of Catlin, Ruan, Tian, and Zelditch, it is now well known that the diagonal of the Bergman kernel admits a full asymptotic expansion (called TYZ expansion) in
k
:

(2.1)

(2\pi)^{n}\rho_{k}(\omega)(x)=a_{0}(\omega)(x)k^{n}+a_{1}(\omega)(x)k^{n-1}+a_{2}(\omega)(x)k^{n-2}+\cdots,

where
a_{j}(\omega)
are smooth coefficients given by universal polynomials in curvature
R
of
g
and its derivatives with order
\leq 2j-2
at
x
. For notational convenience, set
a_{-1}:=0
. Note that
a_{j}(\omega)
can be understood as a formal expression even when
[\omega]
is not polarized. For the precise meaning of the asymptotic expansion (
2.1
) and a comprehensive account of the subject, see
[
51
]
.

Here we adopt the convention in which there are no
2\pi
factors in
a_{j}
s. In this convention, integrating
(2\pi)^{n}\rho_{k}
over
M
, we get the following form of the asymptotic Riemann-Roch-Hirzebruch formula:

(2.2)

(2\pi)^{n}\dim H^{0}(M,L^{k})=(2\pi)^{n}\int_{M}\rho_{k}(\omega)\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}=k^{n}\int_{M}a_{0}(\omega)\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}+k^{n-1}\int_{M}a_{1}(\omega)\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}+\cdots.

This motivates the slightly unconventional modification of
\Td
and Chern character forms which we will introduce in the next subsection. The only purpose of it is to prevent the
2\pi
factors from clogging the formulas.

In
[
49
]
, Lu explicitly computed the first four coefficients
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}
of the expansion as (
a_{0}=1
was already noted in
[
68
]
):

(2.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} a_{0}&=1\\
a_{1}&=\frac{1}{2}S\\
a_{2}&=\frac{1}{3}\Delta S+\frac{1}{24}(|R|^{2}-4|\ric|^{2}+3S^{2})\\
a_{3}&=\frac{1}{8}\Delta\Delta S+\cdots\\
\end{aligned}\right.

where
R,\ric,S
are the Riemann curvature, Ricci curvature, and scalar curvature of the Kähler metric
g
, respectively, and
\Delta
is one-half of the Riemannian Laplacian, defined by
\Delta f=g^{k\bar{l}}\partial_{k}\partial_{\bar{l}}f
. In particular,
a_{1}=\frac{1}{2}S
played an important role in
[
27
]
. The explicit formula for
a_{2}
will be used in this paper.

2.2.
Invariant polynomials and secondary characteristic forms

Let
M^{\prime}
be a
n^{\prime}
dimensional compact smooth manifold and
E
be a
\mathbb{C}^{r}
vector bundle over
M^{\prime}
. Given a connection
\nabla
on
E
, denote its curvature form by
R(\nabla)
. For any symmetric
GL(r,\mathbb{C})
-invariant
p
-linear function
\phi
on
\mathfrak{gl}(r,\mathbb{C})
, we get a Chern-Weil form

\phi(R(\nabla)):=\phi(R(\nabla),R(\nabla),\cdots,R(\nabla))\in\Omega^{2p}(M^{\prime}),

representing a characteristic class in
H^{2p}(M^{\prime},\mathbb{C})
. Usually
\phi(A):=\phi(A,\cdots,A)
is called an invariant polynomial in
A\in\mathfrak{gl}(r,\mathbb{C})
and is identified with its polarization. For invariant polynomial
\phi
and any connection
\nabla
on
E
, we denote
\phi(E):=\phi(R(\nabla))
. Invariant polynomials important to us are the Chern polynomial
\cc
, the Chern character polynomial
\ch
, and the Todd polynomial
\Td
, which are defined as follows. For
A\in\mathfrak{gl}(r,\mathbb{C})
, define

\displaystyle\cc(A)

\displaystyle:=\det(I+iA)=1+\cc_{1}(A)+\cc_{2}(A)+\cdots;

\displaystyle\ch(A)

\displaystyle:=\Tr(e^{iA})=r+\ch_{1}(A)+\ch_{2}(A)\cdots;

\displaystyle\Td(A)

\displaystyle:=\det\frac{iA}{1-e^{-iA}}=1+\Td_{1}(A)+\Td_{2}(A)+\cdots.

The corresponding
p
-linear functions are then defined by polarization. Note that we omit
2\pi
factors from their standard definitions altogether so that the asymptotic Riemann-Roch-Hirzebruch formula for
L^{k}
as
k\rightarrow\infty
now reads (which follows from Riemann-Roch-Hirzebruch theorem and Kodaira-Serre vanishing theorem):

(2.4)

(2\pi)^{n}\dim H^{0}(M,L^{k})=k^{n}\int_{M}\Td_{0}(T^{1,0}M)\ch_{n}(L)+k^{n-1}\int_{M}\Td_{1}(T^{1,0}M)\ch_{n-1}(L)+\cdots.

Comparing it to (
2.2
), we get

\int_{M}a_{j}(\omega)\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}=\int_{M}\Td_{j}(T^{1,0}M)\ch_{n-j}(L)

for
j=0,1,\cdots
.

Let
I_{p}(r)
be the space of all symmetric
GL(r,\mathbb{C})
-invariant
p
-linear functions. Put
I(r):=\oplus_{p\geq 0}I_{p}(r)
. Denote the space of connections on
E
by
\mathcal{A}_{E}
. Then we have the following constructions, called secondary characteristic forms (see
[
17
]
and
[
54
]
).

Proposition
2.1
.

There is a well-defined map

CS:I(r)\times\mathcal{A}_{E}\times\mathcal{A}_{E}\rightarrow\oplus_{p\geq 0}\Omega^{2p}(M^{\prime})/{\rm Im\,}d

defined as follows: For any
\phi\in I_{p}(r)
,

CS(\phi;\nabla^{1},\nabla^{0})=\int_{0}^{1}\phi^{\prime}(R(\nabla^{t});\dot{\nabla^{t}})\,dt,

where
\nabla^{t}
is any smooth path in
\mathcal{A}_{E}
joining
\nabla^{0}
to
\nabla^{1}
, and
\phi^{\prime}(A;B)
is a shorthand notation for
\sum_{\alpha}\phi(A,...,B_{\alpha},...,A)
. Such a

is called the Chern-Simons form. Chern-Simons forms satisfy

(1)

CS(\phi;\nabla,\nabla)=0
and for any three connections
\nabla^{2},\nabla^{1},\nabla^{0}
in
\mathcal{A}_{E}
,

CS(\phi;\nabla^{2},\nabla^{0})=CS(\phi;\nabla^{2},\nabla^{1})+CS(\phi;\nabla^{1},\nabla^{0});

(2)

dCS(\phi;\nabla^{1},\nabla^{0})=\phi(R(\nabla^{1}))-\phi(R(\nabla^{0}));

(3)

If
\nabla^{t}
is any smooth path in
\mathcal{A}_{E}
, we have

\frac{d}{dt}CS(\phi;\nabla^{t},\nabla)=\phi^{\prime}(R(\nabla^{t});\dot{\nabla^{t}}).

Now assume
M^{\prime}
is a complex manifold and
E
is a holomorphic vector bundle. For each Hermitian metric
h
on
E
, let
R(h)
be a Chern curvature form associated to
h
. Denote the space of Hermitian metrics on
E
by
\mathcal{H}_{E}
. Then we have the following.

Proposition
2.2
.

There is a well-defined map

BC:I(r)\times\mathcal{H}_{E}\times\mathcal{H}_{E}\rightarrow\oplus_{p\geq 0}\Omega^{p,p}(M^{\prime})/{\rm Im\,}\partial+{\rm Im\,}\bar{\partial}

defined as follows: For any
\phi\in I_{p}(r)
,

(2.5)

BC(\phi;h_{1},h_{0})=\int_{0}^{1}\phi^{\prime}(R(h_{t});\dot{h_{t}}h_{t}^{-1})\,dt,

where
h_{t}
is any smooth path in
\mathcal{H}_{E}
joining
h_{0}
to
h_{1}
. Such a
BC(\phi;h_{1},h_{0})
is called the Bott-Chern form. Bott-Chern forms satisfy

(1)

BC(\phi;h,h)=0
and for any three metrics
h_{2},h_{1},h_{0}
in
\mathcal{H}_{E}
,

BC(\phi;h_{2},h_{0})=BC(\phi;h_{2},h_{1})+BC(\phi;h_{1},h_{0});

(2)

\bar{\partial}\partial BC(\phi;h_{1},h_{0})=\phi(R(h_{1}))-\phi(R(h_{0}));

(3)

If
h_{t}
is any smooth path in
\mathcal{H}_{E}
, we have

\frac{d}{dt}BC(\phi;h_{t},h)=\phi^{\prime}(R(h_{t});\dot{h_{t}}h_{t}^{-1}).

From the construction or property
(2)
in Proposition
2.1
and
2.2
, it is clear that

(2.6)

\partial BC(\phi;h_{1},h_{0})=CS(\phi;\nabla^{1},\nabla^{0}),

where
\nabla^{1},\nabla^{0}
are Chern connections associated to
h_{1},h_{0}
respectively. In fact, this is used in
[
17
, Proposition 3.15]
to prove the property
(2)
for Bott-Chern forms.

As noted in
[
17
, p84]
, in general, the formula (
2.5
) contains nonlinear terms and cannot be directly integrated to give an explicit formula for Bott-Chern forms. A notable exception to this is when
E
is a line bundle and
\phi
is a Chern character polynomial. In this case, we have the following explicit formula for
BC(\ch;h_{1},h_{0})
. Let
h_{1}=h_{0}e^{-\varphi}
and
\omega=iR(h_{0})
. Then
iR(h_{1})=\omega_{\varphi}:=\omega+i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi
and we have
\ch_{j}(R(h_{0}))=\omega^{j}/j!,\;\ch_{j}(R(h_{1}))=\omega_{\varphi}^{j}/j!
. By direct computation, one can check that

(2.7)

BC(\ch_{j};h_{1},h_{0})=\frac{-i}{j!}\sum^{j-1}_{s=0}\varphi\omega_{\varphi}^{s}\wedge\omega^{j-1-s}.

Note that
\omega^{j}/j!
and the right-hand side of (
2.7
) make sense for arbitrary
[\omega]\in H^{1,1}(M^{\prime},\mathbb{R})
. In particular, it makes sense for arbitrary Kähler classes that are not necessarily polarized.

Our convention for Bott-Chern forms is identical to the one in
[
17
,
62
]
, differs from the one in
[
15
]
by multiplication of
-i
, and differs from the one in
[
25
,
65
]
by multiplication of
i
.

2.3.
Ray-Singer analytic torsion and Quillen anomaly formula

In this subsection we introduce the Ray-Singer analytic torsion, the Quillen anomaly formula, and asymptotic expansion of the Ray-Singer analytic torsion only to the extent necessary for this paper. For a more general account of the related results, see
[
15
,
51
]
.

Let
(E,h_{E})
be a Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle over
M
. Let
\bar{\partial}_{E}
be the Dolbeault operator acting on
\Omega^{0,\bullet}(M,E)
and denote by
\bar{\partial}^{*}_{E}
the formal adjoint of
\bar{\partial}_{E}
with respect to the
L^{2}
metric
|\cdot|_{\Omega^{0,\bullet}(M,E)}
induced by
h_{E}
and
\omega^{n}/n!
. Let
\square_{E}:=\bar{\partial}_{E}\bar{\partial}^{*}_{E}+\bar{\partial}^{*}_{E}\bar{\partial}_{E}
be the Kodaira Laplacian on
\Omega^{0,\bullet}(M,E)
and
e^{-u\square_{E}}
be the associated heat operator. By Hodge theory,
\square_{E}
has a finite-dimensional kernel. Denote by
P^{\bot}
the orthogonal projection onto its orthogonal complement. Define
\zeta
-function by

\zeta_{E}(z)=-\mathcal{M}[\Tr_{s}[Ne^{-u\square_{E}}P^{\bot}]],

where
N
is the number operator
N:\alpha\in\Omega^{0,p}(M)\mapsto p\alpha
,
\Tr_{s}
is a supertrace
\Tr[(-1)^{N}\cdot]
, and
\mathcal{M}[f(u)]
denotes Mellin transform defined by meromorphic extension of

\mathcal{M}[f(u)](z):=\frac{1}{\Gamma(z)}\int_{0}^{\infty}f(u)u^{z-1}du\;\;\text{for}\;{\rm Re\,}z\gg 0

over
\mathbb{C}
.

Definition
2.3

The Ray-Singer analytic torsion of
(E,h_{E})
is defined as

T(\omega,h_{E}):=\exp{(-\frac{1}{2}\zeta^{\prime}_{E}(0)))}.

In terms of
\zeta
-regularized determinant,

T(\omega,h_{E})=\prod_{p}{\det}^{\prime}(\square_{E}|_{\Omega^{0,p}})^{p(-1)^{p+1}/2},

where
′
means exclusion of zero modes.

The determinant of the cohomology of
E
is the complex line given by

\det H^{\bullet}(M,E)=\bigotimes_{p=0}^{n}(\det H^{p}(M,E))^{(-1)^{p}}.

Define
\lambda(E):=(\det H^{\bullet}(M,E))^{-1}
. At the level of harmonic representatives, the
L^{2}
-metric
|\cdot|_{\Omega^{0,\bullet}(M,E)}
induces the
L^{2}
-metric
|\cdot|_{\lambda(E)}
on
\lambda(E)
.

Definition
2.4

The Quillen metric
||\cdot||_{\lambda(E)}
on the complex line
\lambda(E)
is defined by

||\cdot||_{\lambda(E)}=|\cdot|_{\lambda(E)}\times T(\omega,h_{E}).

The Quillen metric
||\cdot||_{\lambda(E)}
depends on the Kähler metric
\omega
and
h_{E}
. The Quillen anomaly formula tells exactly how
||\cdot||_{\lambda(E)}
changes when
\omega
and
h_{E}
vary.

Theorem
2.1

(Quillen anomaly formula,
[
15
, III, Theorem 1.23]
)
.

Let
\omega^{\prime},\omega
be the Kähler metrics on
M
and
h^{\prime},h
be the Hermitian metrics on
E
. Let
||\cdot||^{\prime}_{\lambda(E)},||\cdot||_{\lambda(E)}
be the Quillen metrics associated to
(\omega^{\prime},h^{\prime}),(\omega,h)
, respectively. Then we have the following.

(2.8)

(2\pi)^{n}\log\frac{||\cdot||^{{}^{\prime}2}_{\lambda(E)}}{||\cdot||^{2}_{\lambda(E)}}=i\int_{M}BC(\Td;\omega^{\prime},\omega)\ch(R_{E}(h))+\Td(R_{T^{1,0}M}(\omega^{\prime}))BC(\ch;h^{\prime},h).

Now let
(L,h)
be a Hermitian line bundle with
R(h)=-i\omega
. Note that
\omega
determines
h
up to a multiplicative constant. In
[
16
]
, Bismut-Vasserot obtained the leading order asymptotics of
\log T(\omega,h^{k})
as
k\rightarrow\infty
, and recently Finski
[
33
]
established the full asymptotic expansion in terms of local coefficients.

Theorem
2.2

(
[
33
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

There are local coefficients
\alpha_{j},\beta_{j}\in\mathbb{R},j\in\mathbb{N}
such that for any
s\in\mathbb{N}
, as
k\rightarrow\infty
,

-2(2\pi)^{n}\log T(\omega,h^{k})=\sum_{j=0}^{s}k^{n-j}(\alpha_{j}\log k+\beta_{j}(\omega))+o(k^{n-s}).

Moreover, the coefficients
\alpha_{j}
do not depend on
\omega
.

Here, the local coefficient means that it can be expressed as an integral of a density defined locally over
M
. More precisely,
\beta_{j}
is given by
-\mathcal{M}[\int_{M}\Tr_{s}[N{a}_{j,u}(x)]\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}]^{\prime}(0)
where
{a}_{j,u}
is given by universal polynomial in
R(\omega)
and its derivatives
[
24
, Theorem 1.2, 4.17]
. Thus, if we change the Kähler form
\omega
by the biholomorphism of
M
,
\beta_{j}
remains the same. That is, for
f\in Aut(M)
,
\beta_{j}(\omega)=\beta_{j}(f^{*}\omega)
where
\beta_{j}
is understood as a formal expression. From the explicit formula for
\beta_{0}

[
16
, Theorem 8]
and
\beta_{1}

[
33
, Theorem 1.3]
, we observe that
\beta_{0}
and
\beta_{1}
only depend on
[\omega]
. For notational convenience, set
\beta_{-1}:=0
.

Remark 1
.

Indeed for
f\in Aut(M)
, one can verify that
\square_{L^{k}}=(f^{-1})^{*}\circ\square_{f^{*}L^{k}}\circ f^{*}
, where
\square_{f^{*}L^{k}}
is defined with respect to
f^{*}\omega
and
f^{*}h^{k}
. Thus, the spectra of the two Laplacians, and consequently the analytic torsions corresponding to
\omega
and
f^{*}\omega
, coincide. Since both analytic torsions admit asymptotic expansions by Theorem
2.2
, the coefficients of these expansions,
\beta_{j}(\omega)
and
\beta_{j}(f^{*}\omega)
, must be identical. The point of the above argument using the local nature of
\beta_{j}
is that it does not involve the polarizing line bundle
L
.

3.
Asymptotic expansion of the DPP partition function

We start with the basic definitions regarding the determinantal point processes associated with
(L^{k},h^{k})
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, with
\omega=iR(h)
being a Kähler form. Fix a basis
(\psi_{i}^{k})_{i=1}^{d_{k}}
of
H^{0}(M,L^{k})
. Denote by
\Psi^{k}
the holomorphic section of
(L^{k})^{\boxtimes d_{k}}
over
M^{d_{k}}
defined by the Slater determinant

\Psi^{k}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{d_{k}}):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{k}}}\det\left(\psi^{k}_{i}(z_{j})\right)_{i,j}.

Physically,
\Psi^{k}
represents a collective wave function of free
d_{k}
fermions on the lowest Landau level. Note that
\Psi^{k}
can be identified as an element of
\det H^{0}(M,L^{k})
, and
L^{2}
-metrics of
H^{0}(M^{d_{k}},(L^{k})^{\boxtimes d_{k}})
and
\det H^{0}(M,L^{k})
are related by

(3.1)

||\Psi^{k}||^{2}_{H^{0}(M^{d_{k}},(L^{k})^{\boxtimes d_{k}})}=\det(\langle\psi^{k}_{i},\psi^{k}_{j}\rangle_{H^{0}(M,L^{k})})_{i,j}.

Definition
3.1

The determinantal point process (DPP) associated to
(L^{k},h^{k})
is a probability measure
\mu_{k,\omega}
on
M^{d_{k}}
defined as

\mu_{k,\omega}:=\frac{1}{Z_{k}[h]}|\Psi^{k}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{d_{k}})|^{2}_{(h^{k})^{\boxtimes d_{k}}}\prod_{i=1}^{d_{k}}\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!},

where
Z_{k}[h]
is the normalization constant given by

Z_{k}[h]=\int_{M^{d_{k}}}|\Psi^{k}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{d_{k}})|^{2}_{(h^{k})^{\boxtimes d_{k}}}\prod_{i=1}^{d_{k}}\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}=\det(\langle\psi^{k}_{i},\psi^{k}_{j}\rangle_{H^{0}(M,L^{k})})_{i,j}

by virtue of (
3.1
).
Z_{k}[h]
is called the partition function of
\mu_{k,\omega}
, and
\log Z_{k}[h]
is called the generating functional of
\mu_{k,\omega}
. Note that although
Z_{k}[h]
and
|\cdot|^{2}_{(h^{k})^{\boxtimes d_{k}}}
depend on
h
, the DPP
\mu_{k,\omega}
only depends on
\omega
.

Now fix Kähler form
\omega
in
\cc_{1}(L)
and Hermitian metric
h
on
L
so that
\omega=iR(h)
. Hermitian metrics
h_{\varphi}
on
L
can be identified with smooth functions
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
by
h_{\varphi}:=he^{-\varphi}
. Then the associated Chern curvature form satisfies
iR(h_{\varphi})=\omega+i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi
. From now on, we will use
\varphi
instead of
h_{\varphi}
to denote the Hermitian metrics on
L
(e.g.,
Z[0]=Z[h]
).

Denote by
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}
the space of Kähler potentials for Kähler metrics in
\cc_{1}(L)
. That is,

\mathcal{K}_{\omega}=\{\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R}):\omega_{\varphi}:=\omega+i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi>0\}.

By the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma, two Kähler potentials define the same metric if and only if they differ by an additive constant. Thus, the space of Kähler metrics
\mathcal{K}_{0}
on
M
in
\cc_{1}(L)
can be identified with
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}/\mathbb{R}
, where
\varphi_{2}\sim\varphi_{1}
if and only if
\varphi_{2}=\varphi_{1}+c
for some constant
c
. Note that
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}
, and hence
\mathcal{K}_{0}
, are simply connected.

A different choice of basis
(\psi_{i}^{k})_{i=1}^{d_{k}}
changes
\log Z_{k}[\varphi]
by the addition of a constant. However, the difference
\log Z_{k}[\varphi_{2}]-\log Z_{k}[\varphi_{1}]
is well-defined for two Kähler potentials
\varphi_{2},\varphi_{1}
in
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}
. We will compute the large
k
asymptotics of
\log Z_{k}[\varphi]-\log Z_{k}[0]
in two ways, following
[
44
]
and
[
57
]
.

First,
\log Z_{k}[\varphi]
is essentially the
\mathcal{L}
-functional introduced in
[
28
]
. The following lemma is due to Donaldson:

Lemma
3.2

(
[
28
, Lemma 2]
)
.

The derivative of
\log Z_{k}[\varphi]
on
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}
is given by

(3.2)

\delta\log Z_{k}[\varphi]=\int_{M}\delta\varphi(\Delta_{\varphi}\rho_{k}(\omega_{\varphi})-k\rho_{k}(\omega_{\varphi}))\frac{\omega_{\varphi}^{n}}{n!}.

Choose any smooth path
\varphi_{t}
in
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}
joining
0
to
\varphi
and let
\omega_{t}:=\omega_{\varphi_{t}}
. We use subscript to denote objects associated with
\omega_{t},\omega_{\varphi}
, etc. Using asymptotic expansion (
2.1
) to integrate (
3.2
), we get a large
k
asymptotics

(2\pi)^{n}\log\frac{Z_{k}[\varphi]}{Z_{k}[0]}=k^{n+1}\int_{0}^{1}dt\int_{M}\dot{\varphi_{t}}(-a_{0}(\omega_{t}))\frac{\omega_{t}^{n}}{n!}+k^{n}\int_{0}^{1}dt\int_{M}\dot{\varphi_{t}}(\Delta_{t}a_{0}(\omega_{t})-a_{1}(\omega_{t}))\frac{\omega_{t}^{n}}{n!}+\cdots,

where the coefficient of the
k^{n+1-j}
-term is given by

(3.3)

\int_{0}^{1}dt\int_{M}\dot{\varphi_{t}}(\Delta_{t}a_{j-1}(\omega_{t})-a_{j}(\omega_{t}))\frac{\omega_{t}^{n}}{n!}.

Alternatively, by the Kodaira-Serre vanishing theorem, the higher cohomology of
L^{k}
vanishes for
k\gg 1
and
\log\frac{Z_{k}[\varphi]}{Z_{k}[0]}
becomes a log-ratio of
L^{2}
-metrics on
\lambda(L^{k})
. That is,

(3.4)

\log\frac{Z_{k}[\varphi]}{Z_{k}[0]}=\log\frac{|\cdot|^{2}_{\lambda(L^{k}),\omega}}{|\cdot|^{2}_{\lambda(L^{k}),\omega_{\varphi}}}=\log\frac{||\cdot||^{2}_{\lambda(L^{k}),\omega}}{||\cdot||^{2}_{\lambda(L^{k}),\omega_{\varphi}}}+2\log\frac{T(\omega_{\varphi},h^{k}_{\varphi})}{T(\omega,h^{k})},

for
k\gg 1
. The large
k
asymptotics of the first term of the RHS of (
3.4
) is given by the Quillen anomaly formula (
2.8
), and for the second term it is given by Theorem
2.2
. As a result, we get an asymptotic expansion of
(2\pi)^{n}\log\frac{Z_{k}[\varphi]}{Z_{k}[0]}
whose
k^{n+1-j}
-term coefficient is given by

\displaystyle-i\int_{M}BC(\Td_{j};\omega_{\varphi},\omega)\frac{\omega^{n+1-j}}{(n+1-j)!}+\Td_{j}(R_{\varphi})\frac{-i}{(n+1-j)!}\sum^{n-j}_{s=0}\varphi\omega_{\varphi}^{s}\wedge\omega^{n-j-s}

(3.5)

\displaystyle\qquad\,+\beta_{j-1}(\omega)-\beta_{j-1}(\omega_{\varphi}),

using (
2.7
) to express
BC(\ch;\cdot,\cdot)
explicitly. Here, the integrands of the first and second terms are understood to be zero for
j>n+1,j>n
, respectively. Combining (
3.3
) and (
3.5
), we get the following.

Theorem
3.1

(Asymptotics of the Partition Functions, Version I)
.

(2\pi)^{n}\log\frac{Z_{k}[\varphi]}{Z_{k}[0]}
admits the following form of asymptotic expansion as
k\rightarrow\infty
:

(3.6)

(2\pi)^{n}\log\frac{Z_{k}[\varphi]}{Z_{k}[0]}=k^{n+1}\widetilde{S_{0}}[\varphi,0]+k^{n}\widetilde{S_{1}}[\varphi,0]+k^{n-1}\widetilde{S_{2}}[\varphi,0]+\cdots,

where
\widetilde{S_{j}}[\varphi,0]
is given by (
3.3
) and (
3.5
).
\widetilde{S_{j}}[\cdot,\cdot]:\mathcal{K}_{\omega}\times\mathcal{K}_{\omega}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfies

(1)

Cocyle identity : for any three Kähler potentials
\varphi_{2},\varphi_{1},\varphi_{0}
in
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{S_{j}}[\varphi_{1},\varphi_{0}]

\displaystyle=-\widetilde{S_{j}}[\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1}],

\displaystyle\widetilde{S_{j}}[\varphi_{2},\varphi_{0}]

\displaystyle=\widetilde{S_{j}}[\varphi_{2},\varphi_{1}]+\widetilde{S_{j}}[\varphi_{1},\varphi_{0}];

(2)

The derivative of
\widetilde{S_{j}}[\varphi,0]
on
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}
is given by

(3.7)

\delta_{\varphi}\widetilde{S_{j}}[\varphi,0]=\int_{M}\delta\varphi\left(\Delta_{\varphi}a_{j-1}(\omega_{\varphi})-a_{j}(\omega_{\varphi})\right)\frac{\omega_{\varphi}^{n}}{n!};

(3)

For
j>n+1
,
\widetilde{S_{j}}[\varphi_{2},\varphi_{1}]
is an exact cocyle. That is, it can be written as a difference

\widetilde{S_{j}}[\varphi_{2},\varphi_{1}]=\widetilde{s_{j}}[\omega_{2}]-\widetilde{s_{j}}[\omega_{1}],

for a local functional
\widetilde{s_{j}}[\cdot]:\mathcal{K}_{0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
of Kähler forms.

Proof.

(1)
follows from (

) and properties of Bott-Chern forms in Proposition

. More precisely, we only need to check that

\overline{S_{j}}[\varphi_{1},\varphi_{0}]:=\int_{M}BC(\Td_{j};\omega_{1},\omega_{0})\ch_{n+1-j}(\varphi_{0})+\Td_{j}(R_{1})BC(\ch_{n+1-j};\varphi_{1},\varphi_{0})

satisfies cocyle identity. For
\varphi_{2},\varphi_{1},\varphi_{0}
in
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}
,

\displaystyle\overline{S_{j}}[\varphi_{2},\varphi_{1}]+\overline{S_{j}}[\varphi_{1},\varphi_{0}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}BC(\Td_{j};\omega_{2},\omega_{1})\ch_{n+1-j}(\varphi_{1})+\Td_{j}(R_{2})BC(\ch_{n+1-j};\varphi_{2},\varphi_{1})

\displaystyle\quad+BC(\Td_{j};\omega_{1},\omega_{0})\ch_{n+1-j}(\varphi_{0})+\Td_{j}(R_{1})BC(\ch_{n+1-j};\varphi_{1},\varphi_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}BC(\Td_{j};\omega_{2},\omega_{0})\ch_{n+1-j}(\varphi_{0})+\Td_{j}(R_{2})BC(\ch_{n+1-j};\varphi_{2},\varphi_{0})

\displaystyle\quad+BC(\Td_{j};\omega_{2},\omega_{1})\left(\ch_{n+1-j}(\varphi_{1})-\ch_{n+1-j}(\varphi_{0})\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\left(\Td_{j}(R_{2})-\Td_{j}(R_{1})\right)BC(\ch_{n+1-j};\varphi_{1},\varphi_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{S_{j}}[\varphi_{2},\varphi_{0}],

where in the second identity property

of Proposition

of Proposition

(2)
follows from (

).
(3)
follows from (

from Theorem

Remark 2
.

The proof shows that for any
\phi\in I_{j}(n)
,

\tilde{S}_{\phi}[\varphi_{1},\varphi_{0}]:=\int_{M}BC(\phi;\omega_{1},\omega_{0})\frac{\omega_{0}^{n+1-j}}{(n+1-j)!}+\phi(R_{1})\frac{-i}{(n+1-j)!}\sum^{n-j}_{s=0}(\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{0})\omega_{1}^{s}\wedge\omega_{0}^{n-j-s}

. Functionals of similar type were considered in

,

,

. Note that

can be easily modified to become a functional on the space of Kähler forms, as we now demonstrate.

The coefficients
\widetilde{S_{j}}[\varphi,0]
for

are not geometric
functionals in the sense that they depend not only on the Kähler forms

, but also on the Kähler potentials

. But they do so in a trivial manner: for an arbitrary constant

, they satisfy

(3.8)

\widetilde{S_{j}}[\varphi+c,0]=\widetilde{S_{j}}[\varphi,0]-c\int_{M}\Td_{j}(T^{1,0}M)\ch_{n-j}(L).

This can be easily verified from either (
3.3
) or (
3.5
). We have an alternative expression for the asymptotics (

) where each of the coefficients is given by a geometric functional in the sense that it only depends on Kähler forms. That is, we prove Theorem
1
. Let
V:=\int_{M}\omega^{n}/n!
be a volume of

.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

By asymptotic Riemann-Roch-Hirzebruch formula (

), we can express (

) in the following form :

(2\pi)^{n}\log\frac{Z_{k}[\varphi]}{Z_{k}[0]}=kd_{k}(2\pi)^{n}S_{0}[\varphi,0]+k^{n}S_{1}[\varphi,0]+k^{n-1}S_{2}[\varphi,0]+\cdots,

where the coefficients are given by

S_{0}[\varphi,0]:=\frac{1}{V}\widetilde{S_{0}}[\varphi,0]

and

(3.9)

S_{j}[\varphi,0]:=\widetilde{S_{j}}[\varphi,0]-\frac{1}{V}\int_{M}\Td_{j}(T^{1,0}M)\ch_{n-j}(L)\times\widetilde{S_{0}}[\varphi,0]

for

. By (

), for an arbitrary constant
c
,

\displaystyle S_{j}[\varphi+c,0]=\widetilde{S_{j}}[\varphi+c,0]-\frac{1}{V}\int_{M}\Td_{j}(T^{1,0}M)\ch_{n-j}(L)\times\widetilde{S_{0}}[\varphi+c,0]

\displaystyle=\widetilde{S_{j}}[\varphi,0]-c\int_{M}\Td_{j}(T^{1,0}M)\ch_{n-j}(L)

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{1}{V}\int_{M}\Td_{j}(T^{1,0}M)\ch_{n-j}(L)\times\widetilde{S_{0}}[\varphi,0]+c\int_{M}\Td_{j}(T^{1,0}M)\ch_{n-j}(L)

\displaystyle=S_{j}[\varphi,0]

for

. Thus, the functionals

on

descend to the functionals on Kähler metrics
\mathcal{K}_{0}=\mathcal{K}_{\omega}/\mathbb{R}
. Cocyle identity and exact cocycle property for

follow from corresponding properties of
\widetilde{S_{j}}
, and
(2)
follows from (
3.9
).
∎

From (
2.3
) and (

), one can see that
S_{0}
is (minus of) the Aubin-Yau functional
I
, and
S_{1}
is the Mabuchi functional
\mathcal{M}
in Kähler geometry. It can also be directly checked by comparing the explicit formula for
I,\mathcal{M}

[

]
with (
3.5
) and (
3.9
). For

,

\displaystyle S_{2}[\omega_{\varphi},\omega]=

\displaystyle-i\int_{M}BC(\Td_{2};\omega_{\varphi},\omega)\frac{\omega^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}+\Td_{2}(R_{\varphi})\frac{-i}{(n-1)!}\sum^{n-2}_{s=0}\varphi\omega_{\varphi}^{s}\wedge\omega^{n-2-s}

\displaystyle+\frac{i}{V}\left(\int_{M}\Td_{2}(T^{1,0}M)\frac{\omega^{n-2}}{(n-2)!}\right)\times\int_{M}\frac{-i}{(n+1)!}\sum^{n}_{s=0}\varphi\omega_{\varphi}^{s}\wedge\omega^{n-s}.

By (

(3.10)

When

which is the variation of the Liouville action restricted to fixed area metrics, written in terms of Kähler gauge

. Therefore,

extends the Liouville action to all dimensions. We will compute its second variation in Appendix

.

4.
Holomorphic invariants from Bergman kernel asymptotics and critical points

In this section, we show that there is an obstruction to the existence of the critical points for each
S_{j}
, given by a holomorphic invariant introduced by Futaki
[
40
]
. Proof of Theorem
2
is divided into two part, Theorem
4.1
and Theorem
4.2
. Denote by
\mathfrak{h}(M)
the Lie algebra of holomorphic vector fields on
(M,\omega)
. For
X\in\mathfrak{h}(M)
, by Hodge theory, there is a unique harmonic
(0,1)
-form
\tau
and function
\theta_{X}
(called holomorphy potential) satisfying

(4.1)

\iota_{X}\omega=\tau-\bar{\partial}\theta_{X}.

Then
\theta_{X}
is defined up to an additive constant, and
\tau
will be assumed to be zero without loss of generality for our purposes.

If
\theta_{X}
is real valued,
{\rm Re\,}X
is a Hamiltonian vector field and
\frac{1}{2}\theta_{X}
is a Hamiltonian function with respect to
\omega
, since

\iota_{{\rm Re\,}X}\omega=-\frac{1}{2}(\bar{\partial}\theta_{X}+\partial\overline{\theta_{X}})=-\frac{1}{2}d\theta_{X}.

That is,
{\rm Re\,}X
lies in a Lie algebra of Hamiltonian symplectomorphisms
\mathcal{G}
of
(M,\omega)
.

Consider
\nabla X=X^{q}_{p}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{q}}\otimes dz^{p}
as an
\End(T^{1,0}M)
-valued
0
-form on
M
, where

X^{q}_{p}=\frac{\partial X^{q}}{\partial z^{p}}+\Gamma^{q}_{pr}X^{r}.

Denote by
\flat,\sharp
the lowering and raising index operations by Kähler metric. For example,

(\overline{\nabla X})^{\flat,\sharp}=g_{p\bar{r}}\overline{X^{r}_{l}}g^{q\bar{l}}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{q}}\otimes dz^{p}.

Let
R
be the curvature 2-form of
\omega
. We introduce the following notation:

\overline{R}:=(R)^{\flat,\sharp}=R^{\bar{q}}{}_{\bar{p}r\bar{l}}dz^{r}\wedge d\bar{z}^{l},

which is
\End(T^{0,1}M)
-valued
2
-form on
M
.

First we identify the holomorphic invariants we will be dealing with. We begin with the lemma.

Lemma
4.1
.

We have

(4.2)

\iota_{X}R=-\bar{\partial}\nabla X

and

(4.3)

\iota_{\overline{X}}\overline{R}=\partial\overline{\nabla X}.

Proof.

In local coordinates,

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}\nabla X

\displaystyle=\bar{\partial}\left(X^{q}_{p}\right)=\frac{\partial X^{q}_{p}}{\partial\bar{z}^{l}}d\bar{z}^{l}=\left(\frac{\partial^{2}X^{q}}{\partial z^{p}\partial\bar{z}^{l}}+\frac{\partial\Gamma^{q}_{pr}}{\partial\bar{z}^{l}}X^{r}+\Gamma^{q}_{pr}\frac{\partial X^{r}}{\partial\bar{z}^{l}}\right)d\bar{z}^{l}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\Gamma^{q}_{pr}}{\partial\bar{z}^{l}}X^{r}d\bar{z}^{l}=-R_{p}{}^{q}{}_{r\bar{l}}X^{r}d\bar{z}^{l}=-\iota_{X}R.

Similarly,

\partial\overline{\nabla X}=\partial\left(\overline{X^{q}_{p}}\right)=\frac{\partial\overline{\Gamma^{q}_{pl}}}{\partial z^{r}}\overline{X^{l}}dz^{r}=\overline{-R_{p}{}^{q}{}_{l\bar{r}}X^{l}}dz^{r}=-R^{\bar{q}}{}_{\bar{p}r\bar{l}}\overline{X^{l}}dz^{r}=\iota_{\overline{X}}\overline{R}

where we used the identity

\overline{R_{i\bar{j}k\bar{l}}}=R_{j\bar{i}l\bar{k}}.

∎

Using the lemma, we can prove the invariance of the following invariants.

Theorem
4.1
.

Under the same notations as above, for
j\geq 0
,

(4.4)

\widetilde{F_{j}}(\omega,X):=\int_{M}\Td_{j}(R+\nabla X)(\omega+\theta_{X})^{n+1-j}

and

(4.5)

\widetilde{F_{j}}(\omega,\overline{X}):=\int_{M}\Td_{j}(R+(\overline{\nabla X})^{\flat,\sharp})(\omega-\overline{\theta_{X}})^{n+1-j}

are independent of the choice of the Kähler metric in
\cc_{1}(L)
, under the normalization of
\theta_{X}
by
\int_{M}\theta_{X}\omega^{n}=0
.

Proof.

We first prove the claim for (
4.4
). The proof for (
4.5
) is exactly the same, as we will explain.

Let
\omega_{t}=\omega+i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi_{t}
be an arbitrary one-parameter family of Kähler forms in
[\omega]
with
\omega_{0}=\omega
. We will show that
\frac{d}{dt}\widetilde{F_{j}}(\omega_{t},X)=0
. Let
\theta_{t}
be a holomorphy potential of
X
with respect to
\omega_{t}
, that is,
\iota_{X}\omega_{t}=-\bar{\partial}\theta_{t}
. Setting
\alpha_{t}:=-i\partial\dot{\varphi_{t}}
, we have
\dot{\omega_{t}}=i\partial\bar{\partial}\dot{\varphi_{t}}=\bar{\partial}\alpha_{t}
.
Since

\iota_{X}\omega_{t}=\iota_{X}\omega+i\iota_{X}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi_{t}=-\bar{\partial}\theta_{0}+i\bar{\partial}\iota_{X}(\partial\varphi_{t}),

we have
\theta_{t}=\theta_{0}-i\iota_{X}\partial\varphi_{t}
and thus
\dot{\theta_{t}}=\iota_{X}\alpha_{t}
up to an additive constant.
We used the fact that
X\in\mathfrak{h}(M)
. Also, by torsion freeness of the Levi–Civita connection we have

\dot{\left(\nabla X\right)}=\dot{X^{q}_{p}}=\dot{\Gamma^{q}_{pr}}X^{r}=\iota_{X}\dot{\Gamma_{t}},

where
\Gamma_{t}
denotes the Levi–Civita connection
1
-form for
\omega_{t}
. Note that
\dot{\Gamma_{t}}
is globally well defined and
\dot{R_{t}}=\bar{\partial}\dot{\Gamma_{t}}
. Using these, we compute
\frac{d}{dt}\widetilde{F_{j}}(\omega_{t},X)
(we suppress the subscript
t
):

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\widetilde{F_{j}}(\omega_{t},X)=\int_{M}j\Td_{j}(\dot{\nabla X}+\dot{R},\nabla X+R,\cdots)(\omega+\theta)^{n+1-j}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+(n+1-j)\Td_{j}(\nabla X+R)(\dot{\theta}+\dot{\omega})(\omega+\theta)^{n-j}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}j\Td_{j}(\iota_{X}\dot{\Gamma}+\bar{\partial}\dot{\Gamma},\nabla X+R,\cdots)(\omega+\theta)^{n+1-j}

\displaystyle\qquad+(n+1-j)\Td_{j}(\nabla X+R)(\iota_{X}\alpha+\bar{\partial}\alpha)(\omega+\theta)^{n-j}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}j\Td_{j}(\iota_{X}\dot{\Gamma},\nabla X+R,\cdots)(\omega+\theta)^{n+1-j}

\displaystyle\qquad+j(j-1)\Td_{j}(\dot{\Gamma},\bar{\partial}\nabla X,\nabla X+R,\cdots)(\omega+\theta)^{n+1-j}

\displaystyle\qquad+j(n+1-j)\Td_{j}(\dot{\Gamma},\nabla X+R,\cdots)\bar{\partial}\theta(\omega+\theta)^{n-j}

\displaystyle\qquad+(n+1-j)\Td_{j}(\nabla X+R)\iota_{X}\alpha(\omega+\theta)^{n-j}

\displaystyle\qquad-(n+1-j)\bar{\partial}\Td_{j}(\nabla X+R)\alpha(\omega+\theta)^{n-j}

(4.6)

\displaystyle\qquad+(n+1-j)(n-j)\Td_{j}(\nabla X+R)\alpha\bar{\partial}\theta(\omega+\theta)^{n-1-j},

where to get the last identity we used
\bar{\partial}R=\bar{\partial}\omega=0
and integration by parts.

Now by definition of
\theta
and Lemma
4.1
, we have

(4.7)

\iota_{X}(\omega+\theta)=\iota_{X}\omega=-\bar{\partial}\theta

and

(4.8)

\iota_{X}(\nabla X+R)=\iota_{X}R=-\bar{\partial}\nabla X.

Using these, we compute

\displaystyle\int_{M}\iota_{X}\left[j\Td_{j}(\dot{\Gamma},\nabla X+R,\cdots)(\omega+\theta)^{n+1-j}+(n+1-j)\Td_{j}(\nabla X+R)\alpha(\omega+\theta)^{n-j}\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{M}j\Td_{j}(\iota_{X}\dot{\Gamma},\nabla X+R,\cdots)(\omega+\theta)^{n+1-j}

\displaystyle\qquad-j(j-1)\Td_{j}(\dot{\Gamma},-\bar{\partial}\nabla X,\nabla X+R,\cdots)(\omega+\theta)^{n+1-j}

\displaystyle\qquad-j(n+1-j)\Td_{j}(\dot{\Gamma},\nabla X+R,\cdots)(-\bar{\partial}\theta)(\omega+\theta)^{n-j}

\displaystyle\qquad+j(n+1-j)\Td_{j}(-\bar{\partial}\nabla X,\nabla X+R,\cdots)\alpha(\omega+\theta)^{n-j}

\displaystyle\qquad+(n+1-j)\Td_{j}(\nabla X+R)\iota_{X}\alpha(\omega+\theta)^{n-j}

(4.9)

\displaystyle\qquad-(n+1-j)(n-j)\Td_{j}(\nabla X+R)\alpha(-\bar{\partial}\theta)(\omega+\theta)^{n-1-j}.

Since
\bar{\partial}\Td_{j}(\nabla X+R)=j\Td_{j}(\bar{\partial}\nabla X,\nabla X+R,\cdots)
, (
4.6
) and (
4.9
) are equal.

Hence we obtain for some differential form
\eta
,

\frac{d}{dt}\widetilde{F_{j}}(\omega_{t},X)=\int_{M}\iota_{X}\eta=0

by dimensional reason.

We now turn to the proof for (
4.5
). Note that

\Td_{j}(R+(\overline{\nabla X})^{\flat,\sharp})=\Td_{j}(\overline{R}+\overline{\nabla X}),

where on the right hand side trace is taken as
\End(T^{0,1}M)
.
Then as the proof for (
4.4
) shows, we only need to check the following identities:

\dot{\overline{R}}=-\partial\dot{\overline{\Gamma}};\quad\dot{\overline{\nabla X}}=\iota_{\overline{X}}\dot{\overline{\Gamma}};\quad\dot{\omega}=\partial\overline{\alpha};\quad\dot{\overline{\theta}}=\iota_{\overline{X}}\overline{\alpha},

and

\iota_{\overline{X}}(\omega-\overline{\theta})=-\partial\overline{\theta};\quad\iota_{\overline{X}}(\overline{R}+\overline{\nabla X})=\partial\overline{\nabla X}.

For example,

\dot{\overline{R}}=\dot{R}^{\bar{q}}{}_{\bar{p}r\bar{l}}dz^{r}\wedge d\bar{z}^{l}=\dot{\overline{R_{p}{}^{q}{}_{l\overline{r}}}}dz^{r}\wedge d\bar{z}^{l}=\overline{-\partial_{\bar{r}}\dot{\Gamma^{q}_{pl}}}dz^{r}\wedge d\bar{z}^{l}=-\partial_{r}\dot{\overline{\Gamma^{q}_{pl}}}dz^{r}\wedge d\bar{z}^{l}=-\partial\dot{\overline{\Gamma}}.

The proof then goes without changes, and one can show that the time derivative is equal to

\int_{M}\iota_{\overline{X}}\left[j\Td_{j}(\dot{\overline{\Gamma}},\overline{\nabla X}+\overline{R},\cdots)(\omega-\overline{\theta})^{n+1-j}-(n+1-j)\Td_{j}(\overline{\nabla X}+\overline{R})\overline{\alpha}(\omega-\overline{\theta})^{n-j}\right],

which is zero. Note the minus sign in front of the second term.
∎

Remark 4
.

The proof shows that for any
\phi\in I_{j}(n)
,

\int_{M}\phi(R+\nabla X)(\omega+\theta_{X})^{n+1-j}

is independent of the choice of the Kähler metric in
[\omega]
without a polarization assumption on
[\omega]
. They are precisely holomorphic invariants introduced by Futaki
[
40
]
, generalizing various integral invariants, including the Futaki invariant
[
38
]
and the Bando-Futaki invariant
[
4
]
. As noted before, it is not invariant under the addition of a constant to
\theta_{X}
, but it can be easily modified to be invariant. For example,

F_{j}(\omega,X):=-i\int_{M}\Td_{j}(R+\nabla X)\frac{(\omega+\theta_{X})^{n+1-j}}{(n+1-j)!}+\widehat{a_{j}(\omega_{\varphi})}\times i\int_{M}\frac{(\omega+\theta_{X})^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}

is invariant under the addition of a constant to
\theta_{X}
.

Now we prove the rest of Theorem
2
. It generalizes formula
[
50
, (4.4)]
for all
j\geq 0
.

Theorem
4.2
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a polarized Kähler manifold. Let
X\in\mathfrak{h}(M)
and
\theta_{X}
be as in (
4.1
) and suppose it is purely imaginary. Then for all
j\geq 0
, we have the following identity:

(4.10)

\int_{M}\theta_{X}\left(a_{j}(\omega)-\Delta a_{j-1}(\omega)\right)\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{(n+1-j)!}\int_{M}\Td_{j}(R+\nabla X)(\omega+\theta_{X})^{n+1-j}.

More generally, for non purely imaginary
\theta_{X}
, we have

(4.11)

\int_{M}i{\rm Im\,}\theta\left(a_{j}(\omega)-\Delta a_{j-1}(\omega)\right)\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{(n+1-j)!}\int_{M}\Td_{j}(R+\frac{1}{2}(\nabla X+(\overline{\nabla X})^{\flat,\sharp}))(\omega+i{\rm Im\,}\theta)^{n+1-j}.

Remark 5
.

It is clear that the right hand side of (
4.10
) is
\frac{\widetilde{F_{j}}(\omega,X)}{(n+1-j)!}
, and the right hand side of (
4.11
) is nothing but
\frac{\widetilde{F_{j}}(\omega,X)}{2(n+1-j)!}+\frac{\widetilde{F_{j}}(\omega,\overline{X})}{2(n+1-j)!}
; see (
4.17
). Thus by Theorem
4.1
and Theorem
4.2
, we obtain holomorphic invariants out of coefficients of Bergman kernel asymptotic expansion.

Proof.

We start with the purely imaginary
\theta_{X}
case. Let
f_{t}\in Aut(M)
be the flow of
{\rm Re\,}X=(X+\overline{X})/2
. Let
\omega_{t}:=f_{t}^{*}\omega
. Then

(4.12)

\dot{\omega_{t}}|_{t=0}=\mathcal{L}_{{\rm Re\,}X}\omega=\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial\iota_{X}\omega+\bar{\partial}\iota_{\overline{X}}\omega\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left(-\partial\bar{\partial}\theta_{X}-\bar{\partial}\partial\overline{\theta_{X}}\right)=-\partial\bar{\partial}\theta_{X}.

Since
\omega_{t}
has the same Kähler class
[\omega]
, we have
\omega_{t}=\omega+i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi_{t}
with

\dot{\varphi_{t}}|_{t=0}=i\theta_{X}
upto constant.

Consider
\left.-\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\widetilde{S_{j}}[\varphi_{t},0]
. From the first variation formula (
3.7
), we have

(4.13)

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}

\displaystyle\widetilde{S_{j}}[\varphi_{t},0]=\int_{M}\dot{\varphi_{t}}\left(a_{j}(\omega_{t})-\Delta_{t}a_{j-1}(\omega_{t})\right)\frac{\omega_{t}^{n}}{n!}=i\int_{M}\theta_{X}\left(a_{j}(\omega)-\Delta a_{j-1}(\omega)\right)\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}.

Alternatively, from (
3.5
) we have

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\widetilde{S_{j}}[\varphi_{t},0]

\displaystyle=i\int_{M}\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}BC(\Td_{j};\omega_{t},\omega)\frac{\omega^{n+1-j}}{(n+1-j)!}

\displaystyle\qquad+\Td_{j}(R)\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\frac{-i}{(n+1-j)!}\sum^{n-j}_{s=0}\varphi_{t}\omega_{t}^{s}\wedge\omega^{n-j-s},

where we used the fact that
\frac{d}{dt}\left(\beta_{j-1}(f_{t}^{*}\omega)-\beta_{j-1}(\omega)\right)=0
and
\varphi_{0}=0
.

Now we compute
\frac{d}{dt}BC(\cdot;\omega_{t},\omega)
using property
(3)
of Bott-Chern forms (Proposition
2.2
).
First, by (
4.12
) and (
4.1
), we have

\left.\dot{\omega_{t}}\right|_{t=0}=-\partial\bar{\partial}\theta_{X}=\partial(\iota_{X}\omega).

This implies, by local computation (we are identifying
\omega
and the Hermitian metric it defines on
T^{1,0}M
),

\left.\dot{\omega_{t}}{\omega_{t}}^{-1}\right|_{t=0}=\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{p}}\left(X^{s}g_{s\bar{k}}\right)g^{q\bar{k}}=\frac{\partial X^{s}}{\partial z^{p}}\delta^{q}_{s}+X^{s}\frac{\partial g_{s\bar{k}}}{\partial z^{p}}g^{q\bar{k}}=\frac{\partial X^{q}}{\partial z^{p}}+X^{s}\Gamma^{q}_{sp}=X^{q}_{p}=\nabla X.

Thus we have

(4.14)

\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}BC(\Td_{j};\omega_{t},\omega)=\Td^{\prime}_{j}(R;\nabla X).

For the
\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}BC(\ch_{n+1-j};\omega_{t},\omega)
part, we can directly differentiate to get

(4.15)

\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\frac{-i}{(n+1-j)!}\sum^{n-j}_{s=0}\varphi_{t}\omega_{t}^{s}\wedge\omega^{n-j-s}=\frac{-i}{(n+1-j)!}\sum^{n-j}_{s=0}\dot{\varphi_{t}}|_{t=0}\omega^{n-j}=\frac{\theta_{X}\omega^{n-j}}{(n-j)!}.

Combining (
4.14
) and (
4.15
), we obtain

(4.16)

\left.-\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\widetilde{S_{j}}[\varphi_{t},0]=i\int_{M}j\Td_{j}(\nabla X,R,\cdot\cdot\cdot,R)\frac{\omega^{n+1-j}}{(n+1-j)!}+\Td_{j}(R,\cdot\cdot\cdot,R)\frac{\theta_{X}\omega^{n-j}}{(n-j)!}.

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}\Td_{j}(R+\nabla X)(\omega+\theta_{X})^{n+1-j}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\Td_{j}(R,\cdot\cdot\cdot,R)(n+1-j)\theta_{X}\omega^{n-j}

(4.17)

\displaystyle\qquad+j\Td_{j}(\nabla X,R,\cdot\cdot\cdot,R)\omega^{n+1-j}

by dimensional reason. Comparing (
4.16
) and (
4.17
), we conclude

\left.-\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\widetilde{S_{j}}[\varphi_{t},0]=\frac{i}{(n+1-j)!}\int_{M}\Td_{j}(R+\nabla X)(\omega+\theta_{X})^{n+1-j},

which completes the proof.

For the general case, note that

\dot{\omega}=\mathcal{L}_{{\rm Re\,}X}\omega=-\partial\bar{\partial}\left(\frac{\theta_{X}-\overline{\theta_{X}}}{2}\right)=-\partial\bar{\partial}i{\rm Im\,}\theta_{X}

and

\dot{\omega}{\omega}^{-1}=\frac{\partial(\iota_{X}\omega)+\bar{\partial}\iota_{\overline{X}}\omega}{2}{\omega}^{-1}=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla X+(\overline{\nabla X})^{\flat,\sharp}).

The rest of the proof goes without changes.
∎

Remark 6
.

Note that
\theta_{X}
is determined only up to an additive constant, but (
4.10
) behaves correctly under the addition of a constant to
\theta_{X}
. Using
S_{j}
instead of
\widetilde{S_{j}}
in the proof, a similar formula can be obtained which does not depend on the normalization of
\theta_{X}
for
j>0
(that is,
F_{j}(\omega,X)
instead of
\widetilde{F_{j}}(\omega,X)
on the right hand side).

We showed that the non-vanishing of
F_{j}
obstructs the existence of the critical points of
S_{j}
. That is, for
X\in\mathfrak{h}(M)
with purely imaginary holomorphy potential and
f_{t}\in Aut(M)
be the flow of
{\rm Re\,}X=(X+\overline{X})/2
,

\frac{d}{dt}S_{j}[f^{*}_{t}\omega,\omega]=F_{j}(\omega,X).

When
\mathfrak{h}(M)=0
, the obstruction by
F_{j}
becomes trivial. In this regard, assuming that
Aut(M,L)
is discrete, we can prove Proposition
2
using Donaldson’s result on balanced metrics. Here we call
\omega^{\prime}
balanced at level
k
if the function
\rho_{k}(\omega^{\prime})
is constant.

Proof of Proposition
2
.

We prove this by induction on
j\geq 1
. Suppose that
\omega_{\infty}
is a critical point of
S_{j}
for all
1\leq j\leq m
. In particular,
\omega_{\infty}
has a constant scalar curvature. By the proof of
[
27
, Theorem 3]
, there is a sequence of Kähler metrics
\omega_{k}
balanced at level
k
for large enough
k
, such that
||\omega_{k}-\omega_{\infty}||_{C^{r}(M,\omega_{\infty})}=O(k^{-q})
for arbitrary
r,q
. See also
[
27
, Section 4.3]
. Choose
r\geq 2m
and
q\geq m+1
. By the induction hypothesis, we have
a_{j}(\omega_{\infty})-\Delta a_{j-1}(\omega)\equiv\text{constant}=\widehat{a_{j}}
for
j\leq m
, which implies
a_{j}(\omega_{\infty})\equiv\widehat{a_{j}}
for
j\leq m
. Note that the Bergman kernel asymptotics (
2.1
) is uniform in the sense that there is a fixed constant
C
such that

(4.18)

\left\|(2\pi)^{n}\rho_{k}(\omega_{k})-\sum^{m+1}_{j=0}a_{j}(\omega_{k})k^{n-j}\right\|_{C^{0}}\leq Ck^{n-m-2}

for all
k\gg 1
. Since
\omega_{k}
are balanced at level
k
, we have

(4.19)

(2\pi)^{n}\rho_{k}(\omega_{k})\equiv(2\pi)^{n}\frac{d_{k}}{V}=k^{n}+\widehat{a_{1}}k^{n-1}+\widehat{a_{2}}k^{n-2}+\cdots.

Substituting (
4.19
) in (
4.18
) with the induction hypothesis, we get

\left\|\widehat{a_{m+1}}-a_{m+1}(\omega_{k})\right\|_{C^{0}}\leq C^{\prime}k^{-1},

for some constant
C^{\prime}
. Since
\omega_{k}\rightarrow\omega_{\infty}
in
C^{\infty}
,
a_{m+1}(\omega_{\infty})
is constant and hence
\delta S_{m+1}[\omega_{\infty},\omega]=\int_{M}\delta\varphi_{\infty}(\widehat{a_{m+1}}+\Delta a_{m}(\omega_{\infty})-a_{m+1}(\omega_{\infty}))\omega_{\infty}^{n}/n!=0
. This completes the induction step.
∎

Finally, by expression (

) (modified to be invariant under the normalization of
\theta_{X}
, if necessary). As noted in

, Theorem 6.3]
, the proof of that theorem applies directly to our case as well. More precisely, we only need to check that
\bar{\partial}[\Td_{j}(R+\nabla X)(\omega+\theta_{X})^{n+1-j}]=-\iota_{X}[\Td_{j}(R+\nabla X)(\omega+\theta_{X})^{n+1-j}]
, which is immediate from (

) and (

[

5.
Non-perturbative approach to the gravitational path integral

In
[
31
]
, it was shown that the effective action for 2D quantum gravity coupled to nonconformal matter contains
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
, namely, the Mabuchi and Liouville actions. The corresponding string susceptibility was computed at one-loop order in
[
14
]
by perturbing
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
around their critical points. For dimension
n=1
, the critical points of both
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
correspond to constant curvature metrics, which always exist by the uniformization theorem. However, we have shown that in higher dimensions, there is a nontrivial obstruction to the existence of critical points for each
S_{j}
. In this section, we briefly review the nonperturbative approach to the gravitational path integral on polarized Kähler manifolds proposed in
[
32
]
.

Let
\mathcal{B}_{k}
be the set of all Hermitian metrics
H
on the vector space
H^{0}(M,L^{k})
. There are natural maps between
\mathcal{B}_{k}
and
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}
:

Hilb_{k}:\mathcal{K}_{\omega}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}_{k},\quad FS_{k}:\mathcal{B}_{k}\rightarrow\mathcal{K}_{\omega},

defined by

||S||^{2}_{Hilb_{k}(\varphi)}:=\frac{d_{k}}{V}\int_{M}|S|^{2}_{h^{k}e^{-k\varphi}}\frac{\omega_{\varphi}^{n}}{n!},\quad FS_{k}(H):=\frac{1}{k}\log\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d_{k}}|S^{H}_{i}|^{2}_{h^{k}}\right)

where
(S^{H}_{i})^{d_{k}}_{i=1}
is any orthonormal basis of
H^{0}(M,L^{k})
with respect to
H
. For any
\varphi\in\mathcal{K}_{\omega}
, let
\varphi_{k}:=FS_{k}\circ Hilb_{k}(\varphi)
. By Tian-Ruan
[
59
,
55
]
,
\omega_{\varphi_{k}}
converges to
\omega_{\varphi}
in
C^{\infty}
as
k\rightarrow\infty
. Thus, any Kähler metric in
\cc_{1}(L)
can be approximated by a metric in the image of
\mathcal{B}_{k}
under
FS_{k}
. In fact, the space
\mathcal{B}_{k}
approximates the space
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}
in a stronger sense, where the geodesics of
\mathcal{B}_{k}
converge to the geodesics of
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}
with respect to the natural Riemannian structures. For a more detailed exposition of the subject, see
[
53
,
7
]
. Based on this observation, Ferrari-Klevtsov-Zelditch
[
32
]
proposed a formal definition of the path integral on the space of Kähler metrics as a limit of finite-dimensional integral over
\mathcal{B}_{k}/\mathbb{R}
, that is,

\int_{\mathcal{K}_{0}}\mathcal{O}(\varphi)e^{-S(\varphi)}\mathcal{D}\varphi:=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{k}/\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{O}_{k}(H)e^{-S_{k}(H)}\mathcal{D}H.

See
[
45
]
for a more detailed analysis of these integrals. Note that for a desired action
S
, one has to find an appropriate sequence of actions
S_{k}
on
\mathcal{B}_{k}
approximating
S
. Following Donaldson
[
28
]
, Klevtsov
[
46
, (7.7)]
defined the functionals
S_{L,k}
on
\mathcal{B}_{k}
that approximate the Liouville action. The following proposition verifies the slight modification of that construction in arbitrary dimension
n
.

Proposition
5.1
.

Choose an orthonormal basis
(\psi_{i}^{k})_{i=1}^{d_{k}}
of
H^{0}(M,L^{k})
with respect to the
L^{2}
-metric induced from
\omega
. Define the determinant
\det\nolimits_{\omega}
on
\mathcal{B}_{k}
with respect to
(\psi_{i}^{k})_{i=1}^{d_{k}}
. Define
S_{L,k}
on
\mathcal{B}_{k}
by

S_{L,k}(H):=\left((2\pi)^{n}\log\det\nolimits_{\omega}(H)-(2\pi)^{n}d_{k}\log d_{k}/V-k^{n}S_{1}[\omega_{FS_{k}(H)},\omega]\right)k^{1-n}.

Then
S_{L,k}
approximates the functional
S_{2}
in the following sense.
For any Kähler metric
\omega_{\varphi}\in\mathcal{K}_{0}
, choose
\varphi
so that
S_{0}[\varphi,0]=0
without loss of generality (in fact, it is customary to identify
\mathcal{K}_{0}=\mathcal{K}_{\omega}/\mathbb{R}
with
I^{-1}(0)=S_{0}[\cdot,0]^{-1}(0)
). As
k\rightarrow\infty
, we have

S_{L,k}(Hilb_{k}(\varphi))\rightarrow S_{2}[\omega_{\varphi},\omega]

uniformly over bounded subsets in
\mathcal{K}_{0}
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi_{k}:=FS_{k}\circ Hilb_{k}(\varphi)
. By definition,

\varphi_{k}=\varphi+\frac{1}{k}\log\rho_{k}(\omega_{\varphi})-\frac{1}{k}\log\frac{d_{k}}{V}=\varphi+\frac{1}{k}\log\frac{\rho_{k}(\omega_{\varphi})}{d_{k}/V}.

From (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), one can see that
\left\|1-\frac{\rho_{k}(\omega_{\varphi})}{d_{k}/V}\right\|_{C^{0}}=O(k^{-1})
. Hence we have

(5.1)

||\varphi_{k}-\varphi||_{C^{0}}\leq Ck^{-2}

for some constant
C
uniform over bounded subsets in
\mathcal{K}_{0}
.
Let
\varphi_{t}:=t\varphi_{k}+(1-t)\varphi
. By (
1.2
), we have

\displaystyle\left|S_{1}[\omega_{\varphi_{k}},\omega]-S_{1}[\omega_{\varphi},\omega]\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{1}dt\int_{M}(\varphi_{k}-\varphi)(\widehat{a_{j}}+\Delta_{t}a_{j-1}(\omega_{t})-a_{j}(\omega_{t}))\frac{\omega_{t}^{n}}{n!}\right|

(5.2)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}dt||\varphi_{k}-\varphi||_{C^{0}}\int_{M}\left|\widehat{a_{j}}+\Delta_{t}a_{j-1}(\omega_{t})-a_{j}(\omega_{t})\right|\frac{\omega_{t}^{n}}{n!}\leq Ck^{-2},

for large enough
k
, where we used (
5.1
) and the fact that
\omega_{\varphi_{k}}\rightarrow\omega_{\varphi}
in
C^{\infty}
to get constant
C
uniform over bounded subsets in
\mathcal{K}_{0}
. Now observe that

(2\pi)^{n}\log\det\nolimits_{\omega}(Hilb_{k}(\varphi))-(2\pi)^{n}d_{k}\log d_{k}/V=(2\pi)^{n}\log\frac{Z_{k}[\varphi]}{Z_{k}[0]}.

By asymptotics (
1.1
) and (
5.2
), we get

\left|S_{L,k}(Hilb_{k}(\varphi))-S_{2}[\omega_{\varphi},\omega]\right|\leq k\left|S_{1}[\omega_{\varphi},\omega]-S_{1}[\omega_{\varphi_{k}},\omega]\right|+C^{\prime}k^{-1}\leq Ck^{-1},

using the assumption
S_{0}[\varphi,0]=0
.
∎

6.
Derivation of the (2n+1)D Chern-Simons action

Recall that, in physics literature,
\Psi^{k}
from the definition of a determinantal point process corresponds to the integer quantum Hall wave function; see
[
64
,
47
]
. Denote by
A_{\mu}
the connection (
U(1)
-gauge field) on
L
. An effective action
S_{eff}[A_{\mu}]
for the integer QHE is defined by

Z[A_{\mu}]=e^{iS_{eff}[A_{\mu}]}.

The functional derivative of the effective action with respect to the time component of
A_{\mu}
is given by

(6.1)

\frac{\delta S_{eff}}{\delta A_{0}}=J_{0},

where
J_{0}
is the charge density. In
[
42
, (26), (41)]
, Karabali-Nair used (
6.1
) to derive an effective action for the higher-dimensional QHE in terms of the Chern-Simons forms integrated over a
2n+1
dimensional manifold. In this section, we present an alternative way of deriving their formula and show that as
k\rightarrow\infty
, the leading-order term is the
2n+1
dimensional Chern-Simons action. The following construction is motivated by
[
62
, Proposition 1.4]
.

Let
\varphi_{1}\in\mathcal{K}_{\omega}
and choose a smooth path
\varphi_{t}
in
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}
joining
0
and
\varphi_{1}
. Let
\varphi:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{K}_{\omega}
be a smooth map defined by
\varphi(z)=\varphi_{t}
where
z=1-t+is\in\mathbb{C}
and trivially extended over
t\notin[0,1]
. Define Hermitian metrics
\mathbf{h}
and
\bm{\omega}
on the pull-back bundles
\mathbf{L}:=\pi_{1}^{*}L
and
\mathbf{T^{\prime}M}:=\pi_{1}^{*}T^{1,0}M
, where
\pi_{1}:M\times\mathbb{C}\rightarrow M
is the projection, by

\left.\mathbf{h}\right|_{M\times\{z\}}=\left.h_{\varphi(z)}\right|_{M}=\left.he^{-\varphi(z)}\right|_{M},

and

\left.\bm{\omega}\right|_{M\times\{z\}}=\left.\omega_{\varphi(z)}\right|_{M}.

Also, define Hermitian metrics
\mathbf{h_{0}}
and
\bm{\omega_{0}}
on
\mathbf{L}
and
\mathbf{T^{\prime}M}
by

\left.\mathbf{h_{0}}\right|_{M\times\{z\}}=\left.h\right|_{M},

and

\left.\bm{\omega_{0}}\right|_{M\times\{z\}}=\left.\omega\right|_{M}.

That is,
\mathbf{h_{0}}=\pi_{1}^{*}h
and
\bm{\omega_{0}}=\pi_{1}^{*}\omega
.

Lemma
6.1
.

On
\mathbb{C}

\displaystyle\partial_{z}\int_{M}BC(\Td;\omega_{\varphi(z)},\omega)\ch(R_{L}(h))+\Td(R_{T^{1,0}M}(\omega_{\varphi(z)}))BC(\ch;h_{\varphi(z)},h)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\left[CS(\Td;\bm{\nabla}_{\mathbf{T^{\prime}M}},\bm{\nabla^{0}}_{\mathbf{T^{\prime}M}})\ch(R_{\mathbf{L}}(\mathbf{h_{0}}))+\Td(R_{\mathbf{T^{\prime}M}}(\bm{\omega}))CS(\ch;\bm{\nabla}_{\mathbf{L}},\bm{\nabla^{0}}_{\mathbf{L}})\right]_{2n+1},

where
\bm{\nabla}_{\mathbf{L}}
,
\bm{\nabla}_{\mathbf{T^{\prime}M}}
are Chern connections on
\mathbf{L}
,
\mathbf{T^{\prime}M}
associated with
\mathbf{h}
,
\bm{\omega}
, respectively, and
\bm{\nabla^{0}}_{\mathbf{L}}
,
\bm{\nabla^{0}}_{\mathbf{T^{\prime}M}}
are Chern connections associated with
\mathbf{h_{0}}
,
\bm{\omega_{0}}
, respectively.

Proof.

Let
f
be an arbitrary smooth (0,1)-form with compact support in
\mathbb{C}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}}\partial_{z}f\int_{M}BC(\Td;\omega_{\varphi(z)},\omega)\ch(R_{L}(h))+\Td(R_{T^{1,0}M}(\omega_{\varphi(z)}))BC(\ch;h_{\varphi(z)},h)

\displaystyle=\int_{M\times\mathbb{C}}\left(\partial f\right)\left[BC(\Td;\bm{\omega},\bm{\omega_{0}})\ch(R_{\mathbf{L}}(\mathbf{h_{0}}))+\Td(R_{\mathbf{T^{\prime}M}}(\bm{\omega}))BC(\ch;\mathbf{h},\mathbf{h_{0}})\right]_{2n}

\displaystyle=\int_{M\times\mathbb{C}}f\left[\partial BC(\Td;\bm{\omega},\bm{\omega_{0}})\ch(R_{\mathbf{L}}(\mathbf{h_{0}}))+\Td(R_{\mathbf{T^{\prime}M}}(\bm{\omega}))\partial BC(\ch;\mathbf{h},\mathbf{h_{0}})\right]_{2n+1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{C}}f\int_{M}\left[CS(\Td;\bm{\nabla}_{\mathbf{T^{\prime}M}},\bm{\nabla^{0}}_{\mathbf{T^{\prime}M}})\ch(R_{\mathbf{L}}(\mathbf{h_{0}}))+\Td(R_{\mathbf{T^{\prime}M}}(\bm{\omega}))CS(\ch;\bm{\nabla}_{\mathbf{L}},\bm{\nabla^{0}}_{\mathbf{L}})\right]_{2n+1},

where the first identity is obtained by the fact that
\partial f
is of the form
\tilde{f}(z)dz\wedge d\bar{z}
, the second identity is obtained by integration by parts, and the last identity is obtained by property (
2.6
).
∎

From (
3.4
) and (
2.8
), we have an expression of the effective action in terms of the Bott-Chern forms (ignoring

factors, assuming

and higher cohomology of

vanishes):

\displaystyle S_{eff}[A_{\mu}]=-i\log Z_{1}[\varphi_{1}]

(6.2)

\displaystyle=-\int_{M}BC(\Td;\omega_{\varphi_{1}},\omega)\ch(R_{L}(h))+\Td(R_{T^{1,0}M}(\omega_{\varphi_{1}}))BC(\ch;h_{\varphi_{1}},h)-2i\log\frac{T(\omega_{\varphi_{1}},h_{\varphi_{1}})}{T(\omega,h)}.

Denote
\widetilde{S}:=-2i\log\frac{T(\omega_{\varphi_{1}},h_{\varphi_{1}})}{T(\omega,h)}
. Now we prove Proposition
3
.

Proof of Proposition
3
.

By Lemma
6.1
, we have

\displaystyle-\int_{M}BC(\Td;\omega_{\varphi_{1}},\omega)\ch(R_{L}(h))+\Td(R_{T^{1,0}M}(\omega_{\varphi_{1}}))BC(\ch;h_{\varphi_{1}},h)

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\int_{M}BC(\Td;\omega_{\varphi_{t}},\omega)\ch(R_{L}(h))+\Td(R_{T^{1,0}M}(\omega_{\varphi_{t}}))BC(\ch;h_{\varphi_{t}},h)\right)\wedge dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}2\partial_{z}\int_{M}BC(\Td;\omega_{\varphi(z)},\omega)\ch(R_{L}(h))+\Td(R_{T^{1,0}M}(\omega_{\varphi(z)}))BC(\ch;h_{\varphi(z)},h)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}2\int_{M}\left[CS(\Td;\bm{\nabla}_{\mathbf{T^{\prime}M}},\bm{\nabla^{0}}_{\mathbf{T^{\prime}M}})\ch(R_{\mathbf{L}}(\mathbf{h_{0}}))+\Td(R_{\mathbf{T^{\prime}M}}(\bm{\omega}))CS(\ch;\bm{\nabla}_{\mathbf{L}},\bm{\nabla^{0}}_{\mathbf{L}})\right]_{2n+1}.

Substituting it into (

Explicit computations for the third coefficient

Recall the

[\omega]

[

(A.1)

\displaystyle n\alpha\wedge\omega^{n-1}

\displaystyle=\left({\rm tr}_{\omega}\alpha\right)\omega^{n};

(A.2)

\displaystyle n(n-1)\alpha\wedge\beta\wedge\omega^{n-2}

\displaystyle=\left[\left({\rm tr}_{\omega}\alpha\right)\left({\rm tr}_{\omega}\beta\right)-\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle_{\omega}\right]\omega^{n},

where

\omega_{t}:=\omega+it\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi\in\mathcal{K}_{0}
for

near
0
. We collect some variation formulas for the associated geometric quantities in the following. We denote by
\ric
the Ricci form defined by
\ric:=i\ric_{j\bar{k}}dz^{j}\wedge d\bar{z}^{k}
.

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\ric

\displaystyle=-i\partial\bar{\partial}\Delta\varphi;\quad\frac{d}{dt}S=-\Delta^{2}\varphi-\langle i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi,\ric\rangle_{\omega};

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Delta S

\displaystyle=-\Delta^{3}\varphi-\Delta\langle i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi,\ric\rangle_{\omega}-\langle i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi,i\partial\bar{\partial}S\rangle_{\omega}.

Finally, note that for functions

and closed

Theorem
A.1

Define

In this first proof, we prove
\gamma^{(2)}

\widetilde{S}:\mathcal{K}_{\omega}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

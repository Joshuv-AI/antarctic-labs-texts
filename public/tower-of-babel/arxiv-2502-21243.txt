Reconstruction of space-dependence and nonlinearity of a reaction term in a subdiffusion equation
Barbara Kaltenbacher, William Rundell

Abstract
In this paper we study the simultaneous reconstruction of two coefficients in a reaction-subdiffusion equation, namely a nonlinearity and a space dependent factor. The fact that these are coupled in a multiplicative matter makes the reconstruction particularly challenging. Several situations of overposed data are considered: boundary observations over a time interval, interior observations at final time, as well as a combination thereof. We devise fixed point schemes and also describe application of a frozen Newton method. In the final time data case we prove convergence of the fixed point scheme as well as uniqueness of both coefficients. Numerical experiments illustrate performance of the reconstruction methods, in particular dependence on the differentiation order in the subdiffusion equation.

Introduction

In this paper, we study reconstruction of both
q
and
f

in a reaction-subdiffusion equation with the specific form

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}-\triangle u+q(x)f(u)=0

Here

Such equations are ubiquitious in applications throughout the
biological and physical sciences.
For example, in population dynamics the famous Verhulst
ordinary differential equation model sets

to
separate out the birth and death processes.
This was extended to spatial regions and to include diffusion by Skellum
in 1951,
[
28
]
and was one of the first models to incorporate
random walk processes into the subject.
The model therefore parallels the Fisher model for an advantageous gene
in a population introduced slightly earlier
[
6
,
7
]

Indeed, the general model (
1

q
constant and

of the form

f(u)=a(u-u^{2})
is now commonly referred to as the Fisher equation.
There is a vast literature in this application context for the equation
and allowing a spatial dependence on the coefficients is clearly
of importance.
The generalisation to include diffusion and hence spatial dependence,
adds considerabe mathematical complexity including the possibility of
travelling wave solutions of the above equation.
See, for example,
[
20
,
1
]

The form of the right hand side term here balances growth and decay with
different power-law terms and clearly there are many possible modifications
from a modelling standpoint.
These might include different rates by using exponents other than linear and
quadratic terms as well as higher order and fractional powers involving
u
,
or indeed a functional form that goes beyond just power law behaviour.

In perhaps the other most well-known application, that of chemical reactions,
a similar form is used,
[
9
,
21
]

A cubic polynomial for
f
in terms of

appears in the Rayleigh-Bérnard
model of convection and in the FitzHugh–Nagumo model of 1961
modelling impulses along a nerve,
[
8
]

and also in the Zeldovich–Frank–Kamenetsky–Equation.
More general models of the ZFK type involve
exponential functions: for example,
f(u)=\alpha\,u(1-u)e^{-\beta(1-u)}
,

[
30
]
.
Thus there is an application-driven reason to go beyond merely seeking

f(u)
as a combination of powers of

Thus, although these models are based on specific polynomial
assumptions there have been numerous modifications including
assuming more general polynomials and with possibly non-integer powers.
Our paradigm here allows us to recover the form of the reaction term
from measured data; and, in fact, in addition to allow for
a coupled inhomogeneous spatial environment that itself has to recovered.
We note that this type of coupling is a major factor in the difficulty
of determining both
q
and
f
.

Undetermined coefficient problems for parabolic equations have a history
going back more than 40 years.
For the linear equation
u_{t}-\triangle u+q(x)u=0

For the case where

Pilant and Rundell in a series of papers

See also the book by Isakov,

More recently, due to its relevance in modeling, time-fractional

Also in this paper we will include the subdiffusion case where our model
extends the differential equation for

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u-\Delta u+q(x)f(u)=r

The literature in this direction and from an inverse problems standpoint

We here only give a small selection of related examples.
The most closely related ones are identification of the spatially dependent potential

From the above it can be seen that our interest here lies in the

3
Iterative reconstruction schemes

While Newton’s method, which we briefly discuss in Section
3.1
, is a generic approach for nonlinear operator equations, we will also derive some particularly tailored fixed point schemes in Section
3.2
. The principle behind these schemes is to project the PDE onto the observation manifold and extract a fixed point equation for the searched for quantities

and
f
from this. In case (b) of final time data, the equation resulting from projection is still nonlinear and we apply the logarithm to write it as an equivalent linear relation. This is not needed in case (c) of mixed data, which allows for partial elimination.
Note that in case (a) of time trace observations, this projection approach is not applicable.

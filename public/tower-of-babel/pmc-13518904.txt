Real-world decision support in hospitality using pessimistic multi-granulation roughness of cubic intuitionistic fuzzy soft sets
Authors: Asma Bibi, Shahida Bashir, Muhammad Shabir, Rabia Mazhar, Muhammad I Syam, Rizwan Gul
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTraditional fuzzy and rough set models usually have a hard time in providing sufficient ambiguity, hesitancy, and partiality to information that is common in actual hospitality decision-making. In order to overcome these problems, this paper suggests a new decision-support model that is built upon pessimistic multi-granulation rough sets combined with cubic intuitionistic fuzzy soft relation. The model proposed is a unification of interval-valued membership, intuitionistic non-membership and soft binary relations in a structure of pessimistic rough approximations. The framework characterizes multi-dimensional uncertainty of complex hospitality appraisals by modelling membership, non-membership, hesitation and boundary regions together in a variety of granulations. Soft binary relations defined in terms of foresets and aftersets are used to form lower and upper pessimistic multi-granulation approximations of internal cubic intuitionistic fuzzy sets. Approximation operators are formally defined in two pairs and the algebraic property of the operators is explored. In addition, measures of similarity among internal cubic intuitionistic fuzzy sets on soft relational structures are created to facilitate the robust alternative comparison. On the grounds of these theoretical bases, a systematic decision-making scheme that is composed of two structured algorithms is set up. The relevance of the suggested framework can be proved by a practical hotel selection case study with the usage of several factors and professional evaluations. Comparative and sensitivity analysis demonstrates that the model proposed gives consistent rankings and minimizes the effects of boundary ambiguity in different levels of uncertainty, which can be used as a trusted and risk-conscious decision support device in hospitality management.Keywords: Cubic fuzzy set, Fuzzy set, Rough set, Intuitionistic fuzzy set, HospitalitySubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Complex situations entail uncertain, imprecise and incomplete information in making decisions. Fuzzy sets (FSs), intuitionistic fuzzy sets (IFSs), and their types are advanced technologies that have been developed to address intricate situations in which classical methods of mathematics have been proven to be inadequate. Among these methods, internal cubic intuitionistic fuzzy sets (ICIFSs) are bound to be mentioned as one of the powerful tools, and it is comprised of interval-valued membership and a clear statement about hesitation. This bi-structured system provides a more elaborate format on which to state real-world problems with ambiguous and comprehensive information. The paper is based on the methodology that the study of Zeeshan et al.1 uses in the context of FSs and enriches it with the assistance of the fundamental principles of Ali et al.2 and Bashir et al.3. Compared to the case with conventional FSs, cubic fuzzy sets (CFSs) demonstrate a higher ability to embrace ambiguous data and discover their broader use in such areas as pattern recognition and in healthcare diagnostics.

To this concept of soft relations (SRs), therefore, there is a decision support model proposed in the current work. There is a practical example given in order to demonstrate the applicability of the framework. The cubic fuzzy rough soft set (CFRS) model introduces the concepts of rough set (RSs), CFSs and soft set (SSs) and thus enables the ability to work with more imprecise and uncertain data more robustly. In the ever-evolving and highly dynamic scenarios where quick decisions need to be made, alternative strategies continue to get better in terms of handling uncertainty. It should be mentioned that FSs, SSs and RSs have become potent instruments one can use to capture and analyze vagueness, inconsistency and imprecision of data. In such fast-paced and dynamic situation where urgent decisions have to be made, several methods come to mind when approaching uncertainty in a more pragmatic manner. FSs, SSs, and RSs are excellent techniques of modeling discrepancies of vagueness, inconsistency and imprecision in data. The two frameworks have their benefits and are used in the management of uncertainty. These are applied in numerous applications in diverse fields, including computer science, decision sciences, and other practical applications. In practice, most reasoning models fail to achieve satisfactory results because of the abundance of incomplete or ambiguous data that is typical in the real-life case. The gap between the outdated mathematical strategies and the need to address uncertainty has been narrowed down over the past few years. The model of uncertainty representation differs depending on the discipline, though recent studies focus on enhancing the existing theoretical approaches and render them more efficient4. The concept of FSs was first presented by Zadeh5 as an extension of classical set theory with which to handle uncertainty more effectively. FSs provide a mathematically defined means of modeling the vague linguistic terms, and this is why they can be used in several fields.

Their major limitation though is that they can merely fall back on the values of membership and this is normally not sufficient to represent the ambiguity or inaccurateness of uncertain or imprecise information in the real world. To overcome this shortcoming, Atanassov6,7 proposed the IFS as a combination of the FS with additions of two new parameters, non-membership and degrees of hesitation, in addition to membership. The three components of membership, non-membership, and hesitation are introduced in the structure to provide a more specific and diverse concept of depicting uncertainty. Due to this excellent structure, IFS has been applied widely in fields like machine learning, healthcare diagnostics, decision-making systems such as career and candidate selection, and pattern analysis and recognition8,9.

Another approach to uncertainty that was proposed by Pawlak10 is called the RS theory, and it relies on approximation methods. It is a model of data that characterizes it through lower and upper approximations of equivalence relations11 enabling the identification of exact and vague elements in a dataset. Even though this is a useful approach in most areas, its use tends to be limited to practical application due to its dependence on strict equivalence relations. The RSs help in decision making in circumstances where ambiguity has been noticed e.g. in the sphere of medical diagnostics. They can be used as an example in the diagnosis of abdominal pain in children, where acquisition of correct classifications is a major issue. Pawlak’s rough set theory depends on a single equivalence relation. For dealing with multi challenging problems, Qian et al. 12 introduced a multigranulation rough set theory to approximate the subset of a universe with respect to multi equivalence relations instead of single-equivalence relations.

In the struggle to eliminate the drawbacks of both FS and RS theories, Molodtsov13 developed the SS theory. This model was a parameterized approach to handling uncertainty that lacked much of the restrictions encountered in the previous models. SSs possess a flexible and mathematically sound framework and enough parameters to put across the information of uncertainty. It is in this light that more research continues to be undertaken in order to further build the SS theory by including a number of operations, which may assist in rendering it more relevant in more complicated decision-making circumstances. In particular Ali et al.14 contributed to such development by suggesting the generalization of operations with the position of the weaker parameter requirements that make the model more flexible and broaden the range of its application. The application of SSs in practice is repeatedly demonstrated in other researches and has been found in many applications present in the literature15,16.

Numerous developments have been made to generalize the basic SS theory, to improve its performance in the case of uncertain information handling. These are intuitionistic fuzzy soft sets (IFSSs), fuzzy bipolar SSs, objective SSs and probabilistic SSs, which provide different means to address different aspects of uncertainty. In addition to this, it suggests a fuzzy RS method, based on coverings, and elaborates t-norms with implication of fuzzy logic to generate superior approximation methods. Similarly, a time-aggregated model of an infinite sequence of IFSs was suggested by Alcantud et al.17. They represent and study intuitionistic fuzzy data over time and are more dynamic and time-sensitive by employing historical scores and accuracy measures.

In an even more general form fuzzy binary relations are generalizations of classical binary relations and are characterized as parameterized families over conventional relational models. In the traditional way of RS method, approximations are normally obtained following a single binary relation. This can however be bypassed with the concept of multiplicity of binary relations through the use of the soft binary relations (SℬRs). Feng et al.15 provided the concept of an soft relation over a universal set, which was the foundation of the application of relational operations in the SS paradigm. The works by Babitha and Sunil18 continue the theme of features and applications of SS relations and, thus, enhance the theoretical context and the real-life setting of the given sphere. Many literature works have been developed based on the original RS framework as proposed by Pawlak by using diverse relational structures, such as tolerance relations, covering-based relations, supremacy and community relations, and parallel relations. These improvements are also aimed at making RS more flexible and applicable to the uncertainty management of more complex information systems.

The paper is focused on the construction of a framework to analyze the roughness of ICIFSs regarding SRs and the ways of their applications to a decision-making scenario. The proposed solution has a solid foundation of the case studies, which demonstrate its ability to model the multidimensional interdependence of the decision parameters and produce precise and consistent output. The data-driven uncertainty modeling, multi-criteria decision-making, and computational intelligence are also given value through the methodology. Anwer et al.19 worked on optimistic multi-granulation roughness with soft relations in the year (2021) thus; has more flexibility over uncertainty. Then, Anwar et al.20 designed an pessimistic multi-granulation model of IFSs over soft relations and emphasizes on conservative evaluations that are particularly relevant in complicated decision making scenarios. Jun et al. worked on cubic interval-valued IFS 21. Collectively, these works provide an opportunity to relate the benefits of multi-granulation of the RS theory to the ICIFSs where the levels of membership, non-membership, and hesitation will be introduced, and, thus, enable us to make more accurate approximations of the boundaries and more reliable decisions. Recent advances and research gaps

Recent development of decision-support methodologies proves that hybrid structures such that FSs, IFSs, RSs and SSs can enhance the quality of decisions made in a complex and uncertain environment significantly. Such hybrid models have been used more and more in hospitality management to solve multi-criteria evaluation problems like hotel ranking, quality of the service and analysis of customer preference. From these methods, it is emphasized that the parameterized uncertainty modeling should be used in conjunction with the structured approximation mechanisms.

Even though the current research has been able to examine the incorporation of interval-valued knowledge, interval-valued approximation of hesitation, and rough approximations, they have seldom examined the incorporation of multi-granulation rough reasoning, cubic intuitionistic fuzziness, and soft relational dependencies within the same coherent framework. Specifically, modern models of decision-support in such a way that current models in the field of hospitality lack:

a system to express both simultaneously interval based membership and intuitionistic non-membership;

a system to express both simultaneously interval based membership and intuitionistic non-membership;

a systematic means to model relational interactions of a parameter-dependent kind; and

Consequently, the entire range of uncertainty that also includes membership, non-membership, hesitation, relational dependency and boundary ambiguity is yet to be adequately reflected in the existing hospitality decision systems in the real world.

This paper intends to solve these limitations by suggesting a Real-World Decision Support system in Hospitality using Pessimistic Multi-Granulation Rough Sets with Cubic Intuitionistic Fuzzy Soft Relations. In the proposed model, it is combined:

ICIFSs to reflect interval-valued membership as well as intuitionistic non-membership so that to model the uncertainty precisely and at an interval.

Formal definitions of two new pessimistic multi-granulation rough approximation operators are given and structured decision-making algorithms are created. The relevance of the framework is illustrated by one of the real-world hospitality cases based on hotel choice under various evaluation criteria and professional beliefs. The proposed framework offers an effective and coherent instrument of addressing complex and real-world cases of decisions in hospitality management by utilizing relational granularity and cubic intuitionistic uncertainty with a pessimistic strategy. It also helps in the development of progress in uncertainty modeling, multi-criteria decision analysis and computational intelligence through provision of a theoretically based though practically applicable decision-support system.

Application to decision-making problems

Because real world situations are dynamic and complicated, making it difficult for individual specialists to come up with the best solutions, effective decision making is crucial in data analysis. To address challenges arising from imprecise information provided by multiple observers, Sun and Ma23 introduced a consensus driven algorithm for object recognition. Building on their work, Feng et al.24 refined the approach to overcome specific limitations. Shabir et al.19 advanced the PMGRS theory further by combining soft relations and crisp sets to produce a strong framework for decision making. Their model, which merges CIFSs with PMGRSs under a soft relational structure, supports flexible assessments and offers a holistic approach for navigating uncertainty in practical decision making contexts. The pessimistic multi-granulation roughness of CIFS model aids in medical diagnosis and sustainable development by assessing diagnostic alternatives and evaluating environmental effects, enhancing patient care and environmental management. This is crucial for ensuring the sustainable use of natural resources and addressing environmental issues. The pessimistic multi-granulation roughness of CIFS model provides financial guidance, risk consideration, and strategic decision making support for investors, identifying design solutions for improved performance and cost effectiveness. The pessimistic multi-granulation roughness of CIFS model aids in business and management by providing a framework for evaluating operational choices and strategies, enabling managers to make informed decisions and navigate challenging business environments. Comparison

The pessimistic multi-granulation roughness of the CIFS model enhances conventional models by incorporating CIFS and pessimistic multi-granulation rough sets, allowing for partial memberships and capturing details and uncertainties in real world data Jun et al.21, Mubarak et al.25. Conventional and fuzzy set theories face challenges due to complicated uncertainties and multi-granular analysis requirements. RS theory addresses these issues but lacks depth in pessimistic decision-making Qian et al.12. The strengths of multi-granulation rough set theory and cubic intuitionistic fuzzy sets are combined in the concept of pessimistic multi-granulation roughness based on CIFSs. This integration aims to achieve more refined knowledge representation by incorporating multiple granulation levels and emphasizing cautious or pessimistic outcomes. CIFSs enhance the modeling of uncertainty by simultaneously considering membership, non-membership, and hesitation degrees within a cubic structure. This enriched representation makes the model particularly useful for complex decision making scenarios, such as risk analysis, medical diagnosis, and multi criteria evaluation.

Limitations

Limitations in the established framework can be summarized as follows:

Complexity and scalability of computations

Complexity and scalability of computations

Combining pessimistic multi-granulation rough sets with cubic intuitionistic fuzzy soft relations greatly adds to the complexity of the computation. This difficulty increases with the increase in the number of alternatives, criteria, relations as well as the granulation levels. Therefore, the model can be expected to have scalability problems in the application to large scale, real world, decision making problems, which may limit its efficiency and practical application.

(2) Sensitivity to the choice of parameters and the model

Sensitivity to the choice of parameters and the model

Soft binary relations, granulation structures, and aggregation strategies are the key components of the framework, the success of which strongly relies on their proper choice. The wrong or inefficient selection of parameters may cause fluctuating rankings and unreliable decision results. Thus, these components need to be carefully calibrated and validated to be robust and consistent in results.

(3) Subjectivity, consistency and interpretability concerns

Subjectivity, consistency and interpretability concerns

The model uses interval-based membership and non-membership functions, and expert-based relational judgments, which pre determinately inject subjectivity in the decision-making process. This may result in inconsistencies across evaluations. Moreover, cubic intuitionistic fuzzy logic, together with multi-granulation rough approximations and soft relational modeling provide a mathematically complex structure an interpretation and application of which might prove challenging to practitioners without special expertise.

(4) Conserve nature and low generalizability

Conserve nature and low generalizability

Although the pessimistic multi-granulation approach is appropriate in risk-averse decision-making environments, it can be overly conservative, which may rule out other viable options that can be acceptable in opportunity-driven settings. Moreover, the model presupposes a fixed decision environment and has been confirmed only on a few case studies. More widespread empirical testing on a variety of hospitality applications is needed to determine its generalizability, flexibility and overall reliability.

Conclusions and future research plans

The proposed research has introduced a new decision-support framework of hospitality management that relies on the pessimistic multi-granulation rough sets that are combined with the cubic intuitionistic fuzzy soft relations. The given model is the systematic combination of interval-valued membership, intuitionistic non-membership, hedges of the degree of hesitation, soft dependencies of relations, and rough approximations of boundaries within one framework. The framework allows risk conscious and conservative decision making that is appropriate especially in the real world hospitality context, where uncertainty, interdependence of criteria and incomplete information limit the decision making process.

Two new multi-granulation rough approximation operators that are pessimistic were formally introduced in relation to ICIFSs of soft relational settings and their essential properties were examined. Development of structured decision making algorithm was done to translate the theoretical framework into practical ranking and selection processes. The model was proven applicable in a real-life hospitality case study on the hotel selection accompanied by comparative and sensitivity analysis which revealed that the decision outcome remained stable and consistent in different degrees of uncertainty.

Although it contributes to the theory and practice, there are still a few areas in which the research can be driven. First, computational optimization methods can be created to increase the level of scalability of large-scale hospitality datasets. Second, the model can be extended dynamically or with respect to time to facilitate real-time decision-making in changing service environments. Third, mechanism of hybrid aggregation that incorporate both pessimistic and optimistic approaches can offer flexible decision modalities that can be used in both risk-averse and opportunity-driven situations. Fourth, it could be integrated with machine learning or data-based methods of parameter estimation to minimize the use of purely expert-based inputs. Lastly, the generalizability and strength of the proposed framework would be further demonstrated by closer empirical validation in the various hospitality industries, including planning of tourism services, benchmarking of service quality, and review of customer satisfaction.

Generally, the research contributes to uncertainty modelling and multi-criteria decision-making in the hospitality industry by developing a mathematically sound but pragmatically feasible decision-support framework that is capable of dealing with multi-dimensional uncertainty in real-life situations.

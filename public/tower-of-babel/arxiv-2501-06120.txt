Geodesic cycles on the Sphere: t \color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t -designs and Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund Inequalities
Martin Ehler, Karlheinz Gröchenig, Clemens Karner

Abstract
. A geodesic cycle is a closed curve that connects finitely many points along geodesics. We study geodesic cycles on the sphere in regard to their role in equal-weight quadrature rules and approximation.

41A55,41A63,94A12,26B15

1  Introduction

Geodesic cycles or chains on the sphere
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathbb{S}^{d}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}:\|x\|=1\}
are configurations of finite geodesic
arcs and are studied in various fields of discrete and applied
mathematics. The examples in
[
40
,
41
]
are
inspired by the art gallery problem in computational
geometry. Principal curves formed by geodesic chains on the sphere
are applied in statistical data analysis
[
23
,
24
]
. Geodesic chains are also used to approximate smooth
spherical curves effectively. Since geodesic chains can be described
by the coordinates of the endpoints of their arcs, the so-called
control points, they possess a handy and accessible description that
often facilitates an analytic approach to complex problems.

In this work, we study geodesic cycles that either form
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design curves
[
10
]
or
satisfy Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities.
The concept of
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design curves was recently proposed by us in

[
10
]
as an extension of
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design points. To be specific,
a closed curve
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma:[0,1]\to\mathbb{S}^{d}
with arc length
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\ell(\gamma)
is called a spherical
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design
curve
in
[
10
]
if the path integral satisfies

(1)

\frac{1}{\ell(\gamma)}\int_{\gamma}f=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}f\,,

for all polynomials in
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}d+1
variables of degree not exceeding
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
.
If equality in this definition is replaced by a norm equivalence of
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}L^{p}
-norms, one
obtains Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities which will be discussed below.

Both
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-designs and Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities are usually formulated for
point sets and possess a rich history. For comparison, recall that
the defining property of
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design
points is the equal-weight quadrature rule

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}f(x_{j})=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}f\,,

for all algebraic polynomials
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}f
in
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}d+1
variables of total degree
at most
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
.
Spherical design points have been extensively
studied ever since the fundamental article by Delsarte, Goethals, and Seidel in the 1970s

[
7
]
. See

[
43
,
3
,
34
,
37
,
22
,
35
,
18
,
16
]
for a
sample of contributions. A long list of numerical and some analytic
examples of
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design points on
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathbb{S}^{2}
are collected on the
websites
[
17
,
44
]
for a large range of

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
. Another list for
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathbb{S}^{3}
is provided at

[
45
]
. We refer to
[
22
]
for a
list of analytic
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-designs on
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathbb{S}^{2}
.

A major achievement in regard to
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design points is the proof of the
Korevaar-Meyers conjecture
[
27
]
by Bondarenko, Radchenko,
and Viazovska in 2013
[
1
]
, which had remained
open for 20 years. It verifies the existence of
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design points on

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathbb{S}^{d}
with cardinality
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}n\leq C_{d}t^{d}
for some dimensional constant
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}C_{d}>0
.

Most questions about
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design points are meaningful, useful, and
interesting for
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design curves, but so far only very few results
are available.
For instance,
we know that there is a constant
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}c_{d}>0
such that the arc length of
every
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design curve
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma
in
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathbb{S}^{d}
is bounded below by

(2)

\ell(\gamma)\geq c_{d}t^{d-1}\,,

cf.
[
10
, Thm. 2.2]
. The analogue of the Korevaar-Meyers
conjecture for curves asks for the existence of a sequence of

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design curves
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}(\gamma^{(t)})_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
whose lengths grow at most
as
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t^{d-1}
. The constructions of
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design curves in
[
10
]
and

[
28
]
affirm the Korevaar-Meyers conjecture for curves in
dimension
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}d=2
and
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}d=3
.
So far only a handful of
explicit examples has been found.

A natural first idea for the construction of
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design curves is to
connect a set of
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design points along some curve and hope that the
resulting curve satisfies (
1
). Proceeding in this manner,
one could build on the extensive collection of
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design points and
avoid building a theory from scratch.

Our objective is to explore several facets of this idea and obtain a
better grasp of what may be true and what not.

(i) We will discuss an example of a
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design curve with an explicit
analytic expression that contains
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design points in its
trace. However, it remains utterly mysterious how to connect
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design
points to obtain a
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design curve. The curve of Example
2.2
seems to be
a result of sheer luck.

(ii) So the next idea is to connect points along geodesic arcs, which
in
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathbb{S}^{2}
are segments of great circles, and hope that the resulting
curve is a
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design curve. In other words, we search
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design
curves in the form of geodesic cycles. This idea fails already for the simplest
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}2
-design
points. Example
2.1
shows that the geodesic cycle obtained by
connecting the points of a regular tetrahedron is not a

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}2
-design.

To improve on this idea, we use a geodesic cycle with
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design
points as control points for the initialization of a numerical
optimization with respect to the control points. Geometrically, we
deform the control points corresponding to
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design points until an
error functional is zero, in which case we have a candidate for a
geodesic

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design cycle. In a second step we beautify the numerical
construction by reducing the number of parameters and proving
rigorously the existence of a
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design cycle. This procedure is
familiar in the construction of
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design points
[
37
]
, but seems
to be significantly more difficult for curve, both numerically and
analytically. So far, we have been successful to construct
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design
cycles only for
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t=2
and
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t=3
. See Figures
1(a)
and

1(b)
for an illustration of these results.

To the best of our knowledge, this construction provides the only
currently known geodesic
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}2
- and
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}3
-design cycles on
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathbb{S}^{2}

that are free of self-intersections.

(iii) Although the initial idea fails, it raises the following question: What do we
actually obtain, if we connect
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design points by geodesic arcs? We
answer this question in the following sense (and in arbitrary
dimension). If the points are distributed sufficiently densely, as is
the case in the abstract construction of
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-designs in
[
1
]
, then
the resulting geodesic cycle satisfies Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities. The analytic
result can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

There are constants
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}0<A_{d},B_{d},C_{d}<\infty
and a sequence of geodesic
cycles
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}(\gamma^{(t)})_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathbb{S}^{d}
with the following
properties:

(i) For all
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}p\in[1,\infty]
and all degrees
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t\in\mathbb{N}
, the norm equivalence

\displaystyle A_{d}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(\gamma^{(t)})}\leq B_{d}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\,

holds for all algebraic polynomials
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}f
of
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}d+1

variables of degree
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
, and

(ii) the length of the curves is bounded by

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.5}\ell(\gamma^{(t)})\leq C_{d}t^{d-1}\,.

Note that Theorem
1.1
establishes Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities for sequences
of curves whose length matches the lower bounds
in (
2
).

Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities for points have been studied intensively in
approximation theory. For constructions and density theorems on the
sphere we refer to
[
13
,
1
,
2
,
29
,
30
,
31
,
33
]
. In fact, our construction of Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities for curves is based on the existence of Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund points. Recently Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities have resurfaced in the context of
discretization of
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}L^{p}
-norms
[
39
]
. In this context,
Theorem
1.1
assures that the
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}L^{p}
-norm on a sphere can be captured
along a curve (instead of a finite set of points). To the best of our
knowledge, Theorem
1.1
is the first result about Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities
for curves.

2  Some Spherical
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-Design Curves for Small
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t

For
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\Pi_{t}
be the collection of all polynomials with
real coefficients in
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}d+1
variables of total degree at most
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
. The
unit
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}d
-sphere is denoted by

\mathbb{S}^{d}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}:\|x\|=1\}\,,\qquad d=2,3,\ldots.

We normalize its standard surface measure, so that
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}1=1
.

As introduced by Delsarte, Goethals, and Seidel in the seventies
[
7
,
35
]
, a finite set
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}\subseteq\mathbb{S}^{d}
is called a (spherical)
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design
, if

(3)

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}f(x_{j})=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}f\,,\qquad\text{for all }f\in\Pi_{t}\,.

This concept gave rise to a rich field of research about explicit analytic, algebraic, and numerical constructions,
[
43
,
3
,
6
,
34
,
37
]
to name a few.

2.1  Spherical
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design curves

The analogous concept for curves has been introduced only recently in

[
10
]
. Here, the term curve always refers to a continuous,
piecewise smooth function
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{d}
of finite
arc-length
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\ell(\gamma)

that is closed so that
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma(0)=\gamma(1)
. We allow self
intersections, but in contrast to
[
10
]
, the curve could
even traverse arcs multiple times. Given a continuous function
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}f
on

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathbb{S}^{d}
and a curve

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma
, the path integral is

\int_{\gamma}f=\int_{0}^{1}f(\gamma(s))\|\dot{\gamma}(s)\|\mathrm{d}s\,,

and
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\ell(\gamma)=\int_{\gamma}1
is the arc length of
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma
.
Given
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}f\in\Pi_{t}
and a curve
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma
, we compare
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\int_{\gamma}f

with the integral
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}f
over the entire sphere.

We call
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma
a (spherical)
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design curve if

\frac{1}{\ell(\gamma)}\int_{\gamma}f=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}f\,,\qquad\text{for all }f\in\Pi_{t}\,.

Some initial examples of smooth
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}1
,
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}2
, and
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}3
-design curves on
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathbb{S}^{2}
have been derived in
[
10
]
.

Example 2.1

(Smooth
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design curves in
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathbb{S}^{2}
)
.

For
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t=1,2,3
, consider the curves
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma^{(t,a)}:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{2}
,

(4)

\gamma^{(t,a)}(s):=\begin{pmatrix}a\cos(2\pi s)+(1-a)\cos(2\pi(2t-1)s)\\
a\sin(2\pi s)-(1-a)\sin(2\pi(2t-1)s)\\
2\sqrt{a(1-a)}\sin(2\pi ts)\end{pmatrix}.

The curve
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma^{(1,a)}
is a great circle. Every great circle on
the sphere
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathbb{S}^{d}
is a
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}1
-design curve and it is easy to show that
great circles are shortest among all
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}1
-design curves.

The
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design property of
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma^{(t,a)}
was proved in
[
10
, Prop. 3.1]
:

for
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t=2
and
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t=3
there exist parameters

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}a_{2},a_{3}\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
such that
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma^{(2,a_{2})}
and

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma^{(3,a_{3})}
are spherical
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}2
- and
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}3
-design curves,
respectively.

Both curves are simple, meaning they have no self-intersections. As a result, they divide the sphere into two distinct regions, as shown in Figure
2
. Interestingly, we observe that these two regions have equal areas.

Proposition 2.1
.

For
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t=2
,
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t=3
, and
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}a\in[0,1]
,
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma^{(t,a)}
partitions
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathbb{S}^{2}
into two regions of equal area.

Proof.

Due to our normalization
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}|\mathbb{S}^{2}|=1
, this means that the area of both regions is
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\frac{1}{2}
.

To verify this observation rigorously, we recall that the integral of the geodesic curvature
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}k_{g}
along a smooth curve
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{2}
may be computed by

\int_{\gamma}k_{g}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\langle\ddot{\gamma}(s),\gamma(s)\times\dot{\gamma}(s)\rangle}{\|\dot{\gamma}(s)\|^{2}}\mathrm{d}s\,,

cf.
[
19
, Sections 17.4 and 27.1]
.
The Gauss-Bonnet Theorem applied to curves on
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathbb{S}^{2}
yields that the enclosed area
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}A(\gamma)
is determined by

(5)

A(\gamma)=\frac{1}{2}-\int_{\gamma}k_{g}\,.

Using the parametrization
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma=\gamma^{(2,a)}
, elementary Mathematica computations reveal

k_{g}=\frac{\langle\ddot{\gamma}(s),\gamma(s)\times\dot{\gamma}(s)\rangle}{\|\dot{\gamma}(s)\|^{2}}=-4\pi\sqrt{(1-a)a}\sin(4\pi s)\frac{2a^{2}-2(a-1)a\cos(8\pi s)-14a+15}{2a^{2}-2(a-1)a\cos(8\pi s)-10a+9}\,.

Since
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\sin(4\pi s)
is multiplied by a function whose period is
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}1/4
, the standard formula
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\sin(4\pi(s+\frac{1}{4}))=-\sin(4\pi s)
implies that the integral over
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}[0,1]
vanishes.
Hence, we derive
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}A(\gamma)=\frac{1}{2}
and the normalization
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}|\mathbb{S}^{2}|=1
implies that
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma
divides
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathbb{S}^{2}
into two regions of equal area.

The parametrization
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma=\gamma^{(3,a)}
is dealt with in a similar fashion, and we obtain

k_{g}=\frac{\langle\ddot{\gamma}(s),\gamma(s)\times\dot{\gamma}(s)\rangle}{\|\dot{\gamma}(s)\|^{2}}=-8\pi\sqrt{(1-a)a}\sin(6\pi s)\frac{2a^{2}-2(a-1)a\cos(12\pi s)-11a+10}{2a^{2}-2(a-1)a\cos(12\pi s)-8a+25/4}\,,

and again we conclude that
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\int_{\gamma}k_{g}=0
.

Although we are dealing with a family of smooth curves, there is a
hidden relation to
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design points. To see this, recall that the
vertices of a smooth curve on the sphere are the local extrema of its
geodesic curvature. For
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t=2
and
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma^{(2,a)}
, there are four vertices located at

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}s_{j}=\frac{2j-1}{8}
,
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}j=1,\ldots,4
. For the parameter
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}a_{S}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}
, we have checked that the
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}4

points
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\{\gamma^{(2,a_{S})}(\tfrac{2j-1}{8})\}_{j=1}^{4}
form a

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}2
-design given by the vertices of a (regular) tetrahedron, but

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma^{(2,a_{S})}
is not a
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}2
-design curve.

By contrast, for the special parameter
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}a_{2}
the corresponding curve
is a
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}2
-design curve, but its vertices are not
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}2
-design points and we did not find any other
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}2
-design points that lie on the curve.

Likewise, for
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t=3
, there are six vertices located at

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}s_{j}=\frac{2j-1}{12}
,
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}j=1,\ldots,6
. The parameter choice

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}a_{O}=\frac{1}{2}
leads to the six points

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\{\gamma^{(3,a_{O})}(\tfrac{2j-1}{12})\}_{j=1}^{6}
, and we checked that
they form a
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}3
-design as the vertices of an octahedron, but the
corresponding curve is not a
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}3
-design curve. Again, for the special
parameter
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}a_{3}\neq a_{O}
one obtains a
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}3
-design curve, but its
vertex set does not contain
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}3
-design points and we did not find any other
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}3
-design points that lie on the curve.

with
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}a_{2}\approx 0.7778

(b)

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma^{(3,a_{3})}
with
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}a_{3}\approx 0.7660

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma^{(2,a_{2})}
and
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma^{(3,a_{3})}

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}2

Some examples of
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}2
- and
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}3
-design curves in
higher-dimensional spheres can be constructed as follows.

Example 2.2

(Smooth
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design curves in
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathbb{S}^{2m-1}
)
.

Let
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}c(s)=\begin{pmatrix}\cos(2\pi s)\\
\sin(2\pi s)\end{pmatrix}

be the circle traversed counter-clockwise, and consider the curves
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma^{(1)}(s)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\left(c(s),\ldots,c(s)\right)\in\mathbb{S}^{2m-1}
and

(6)

\gamma^{(2)}(s):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\begin{pmatrix}c(s)\\
c(2s)\\
\vdots\\
c(ms)\end{pmatrix},\qquad\gamma^{(3)}(s):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\begin{pmatrix}c(s)\\
c(3s)\\
\vdots\\
c((2m-1)s)\end{pmatrix}\,.

Then
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma^{(t)}
is a
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design curve for
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t=1,2,3
, and
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma^{(1)}
is a great circle.

We have checked that the points
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\left\{\gamma^{(2)}(\tfrac{j}{2m+1})\right\}_{j=1}^{2m+1}
are a
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}2
-design, namely the vertices of the
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}2m
-tetrahedron. The points
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\left\{\gamma^{(3)}(\tfrac{j}{4m})\right\}_{j=1}^{4m}
are a
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}3
-design and correspond to the vertices of the
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}2m
-dimensional cross-polytope (hyperoctahedron).

Let us look at some subtle differences in Examples
2.1

and
2.2
. The
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design curves in Examples
2.2
connect
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design
points by a suitable curve. In this sense they confirm the idea that
one
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design curve could be obtained by suitably connecting

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design points. By contrast, in Example
2.1
with special
values
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}a_{2},a_{3}
, the

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design curves pass through points that are close to
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design
points, but these curves do
not
contain
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design points.
In both cases, by sheer luck, we had an analytic expression
for the curve. It remains unclear how to find such an expression.

We therefore now turn to geodesic cycles, which are geometrically
simpler. In geometry, the motion along a geodesic is the canonical
way to connect two points.

2.2
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design curves consisting of geodesic cycles

In this section we start with a set of points, preferably
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design
points, and then connect them with the goal of obtaining a
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design
curve. The idea is to connect them along the simplest possible curve
in
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathbb{S}^{2}
, namely geodesic arcs. This idea leads to the notion of geodesic chains and cycles.

A geodesic chain is a curve on the sphere
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathbb{S}^{d}
that
connects a finite set of control points by geodesic arcs, and a
geodesic cycle is a
closed
geodesic chain. Geodesic
chains are the analogue of polygonal curves in Euclidean space.

The length of the geodesic arc connecting
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}x
and
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}y
on
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathbb{S}^{d}
is measured by the rotation invariant metric on
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathbb{S}^{d}
,

(7)

\dist(x,y)=\arccos\langle x,y\rangle\,.

Unless
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}y\neq\pm x
, such an arc is parametrized by
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma_{x,y}:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{d}
,

\gamma_{x,y}(s)=\frac{\sin((1-s)\dist(x,y))}{\sin(\dist(x,y))}x+\frac{\sin(s\dist(x,y))}{\sin(\dist(x,y))}y\,.

We note that
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\sin(\dist(x,y))=\sqrt{1-\langle x,y\rangle^{2}}
and that this parametrization has constant speed
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\|\dot{\gamma}_{x,y}(s)\|=\dist(x,y)
.

The control points
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
induce the geodesic cycle

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma=(\gamma_{x_{j},x_{j+1}})_{j=1}^{n}
, where we additionally put

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}x_{n+1}:=x_{1}
to obtain a closed curve. We do not require that the control points are pairwise distinct and hence we allow geodesic arcs to occur multiple times.

The path integral over
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma=(\gamma_{x_{j},x_{j+1}})_{j=1}^{n}
is

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma}f

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{\gamma_{x_{j},x_{j+1}}}f\,.

The length of
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma
is
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\ell(\gamma)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\ell(\gamma_{x_{j},x_{j+1}})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\dist(x_{j},x_{j+1})
and is a
function of the control points only.

We call
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma
a geodesic
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design cycle (or simply
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design curve) if

\frac{1}{\ell(\gamma)}\int f=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}f\,,\qquad\text{for all }f\in\Pi_{t}\,.

A natural idea for the construction of geodesic
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design cycles is
to use control points that are themselves
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design points. As
mentioned earlier, the vertices of the regular tetrahedron

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}\}
are
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}2
-design points on

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathbb{S}^{2}
. However, the geodesic cycle induced by these four
control points is not a
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}2
-design cycle and not even a
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}1
-design
cycle.

To obtain
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design curves, we perturb the control points and thus
deform a given cycle. For this, we set up an iterative numerical
optimization that we initialize with
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design control points.

3  Numerical constructions and beautification

We perform a numerical optimization to derive candidates of simple geodesic
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design cycles. Called beautification
in
[
37
]
, we eventually aim to derive an analytic description of these candidates and prove that they satisfy the respective design properties.

It seems that the process of beautification is more demanding for
curves than for points. While the numerical minimization is comparable, finding the analytic description appears much harder. We are able to complete this beautification procedure for
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t=2
and
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t=3
, but we obtain two numerical candidates of
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}5
-design cycles that we have not yet been able to beautify.

3.1  Two-step procedure: numerical minimization and beautification

A closed curve
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma
is a
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design if and only if the linear form

Lf=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}f-\frac{1}{\ell(\gamma)}\int_{\gamma}f

vanishes on
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\Pi_{t}
. The norm of
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}L

(8)

\|L\|_{t}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\Pi_{t}\\
\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}f-\frac{1}{\ell(\gamma)}\int_{\gamma}f\right|

is the worst case integration error on
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\Pi_{t}
. For numerical
optimization, we may use the following expression for the norm of
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}L
,
see
[
9
,
36
,
18
]
for related formulas
for points.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\{P_{l}:l=0,1,\ldots\}
be the family of Legendre polynomials,
normalized by
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}P_{l}(1)=1
. Given a curve
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma
, the norm of
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}L
is
given by

(9)

\|L\|^{2}_{t}=\sum_{l=1}^{t}\frac{2l+1}{|\ell(\gamma)|^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}P_{l}(\langle\gamma(r),\gamma(s)\rangle)\|\dot{\gamma}(r)\|\|\dot{\gamma}(s)\|\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}s\,.

As a consequence,
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma
is a
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design curve if and only if

\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}P_{l}(\langle\gamma(r),\gamma(s)\rangle)\|\dot{\gamma}(r)\|\|\dot{\gamma}(s)\|\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}s=0\,,\qquad l=1,\ldots,t\,.

Proof.

We only need to consider the restriction of the polynomials
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\Pi_{t}
to
the sphere. For an arbitrary orthonormal basis
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\{\varphi_{k}\}
of

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\Pi_{t}|_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\subseteq L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
, the Riesz representative of
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}L
is
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}v_{L}=\sum_{k}(L\varphi_{k})\varphi_{k}\in\Pi_{t}|_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}
and the norm of
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}L
satisfies

(10)

\|L\|_{t}=\|v_{L}\|=\left(\sum_{k}|L\varphi_{k}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\,.

We choose the natural orthonormal basis for
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\Pi_{t}|_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}
, namely the spherical harmonics up to degree
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
denoted by

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\{Y_{l,m}:|m|\leq l\,,\,l=0,\ldots,t\}
. According to (
10
) the integration error is

\|L\|^{2}_{t}=\sum_{l=0}^{t}\sum_{m=-l}^{l}\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}Y_{l,m}-\frac{1}{\ell(\gamma)}\int_{\gamma}Y_{l,m}\right|^{2}\,.

Since
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}Y_{0,0}\equiv 1
and thus
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}Y_{l,m}=\langle Y_{l,m},Y_{0,0}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}=0
for
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}l>0
and
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\frac{1}{\ell(\gamma)}\int_{\gamma}1=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}1
by definition, the integration error can be expressed as

\displaystyle\|L\|_{t}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{t}\sum_{m=-l}^{l}\left|\frac{1}{\ell(\gamma)}\int_{\gamma}Y_{l,m}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{t}\sum_{m=-l}^{l}\frac{1}{|\ell(\gamma)|^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}Y_{l,m}(\gamma(r))Y_{l,m}(\gamma(s))\|\dot{\gamma}(r)\|\|\dot{\gamma}(s)\|\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}s\,.

We use the well-known addition formula of the spherical harmonics in terms of Legendre polynomials,

(11)

\sum_{m=-l}^{l}Y_{l,m}(x)Y_{l,m}(y)=(2l+1)P_{l}(\langle x,y\rangle)\,,\qquad\text{for all }x,y\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\,,

and further rewrite the integration error as

\|L\|_{t}^{2}=\sum_{l=1}^{t}\frac{2l+1}{|\ell(\gamma)|^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}P_{l}(\langle\gamma(r),\gamma(s)\rangle)\|\dot{\gamma}(r)\|\|\dot{\gamma}(s)\|\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}s\,.

The addition formula yields

(2l+1)\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}P_{l}(\langle\gamma(r),\gamma(s)\rangle)\|\dot{\gamma}(r)\|\|\dot{\gamma}(s)\|\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}s=\left(\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{m=-l}^{l}Y_{l,m}(\gamma(r))\|\dot{\gamma}(r)\|\mathrm{d}r\right)^{2}\geq 0\,.

Consequently
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\|L\|_{t}=0
, if and only if the left-hand side vanishes
for
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}l=1,\dots,t
.

Our recipe for the construction of geodesic
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design cycles
consists of two steps, first a numerical minimization and then a beautification.
As the first step, the
numerical
process works as follows:

(i)

The goal of the numerical procedure is to minimize the error
functional (
9
).
A natural initialization is a geodesic cycle on
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathbb{S}^{2}

that connects
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design points. Indeed,
the vertices of the Platonic solids form
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design points for
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t=2,3,5
, respectively, and we initialize the minimization algorithm with their spherical Hamiltonian cycles as depicted in Figure
3
.

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathbb{R}^{3}

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}2

For geodesic cycles, the error functional (

) is a function of the control points, and is thus a
function of finitely many parameters (twice the number of control
points).

(ii)

We use a suitable iterative optimization algorithm, such
as gradient descent, steepest descent, or some conjugate gradient
methods, to minimize the error functional. Geometrically, each iteration of the descent algorithm yields a new set of parameters, or in other words, a new set of control points with a corresponding geodesic cycle.

(iii)

The iteration stops when we have found a set of control
points for which the error functional (
9
) vanishes up to machine precision. By Lemma

3.1
this numerical solution yields a promising
candidate of a geodesic
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design cycle.

As the second step, the
beautification
process refines the numerical candidate into an exact geodesic
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design cycle.

(iv)

We identify a reduced set of parameters – much fewer than
twice the number of control points – that still ensures a
continuous transition from the initialization to the solution. This selection is based on a visual comparison of the control points between the initial configuration and the numerical solution.

(v)

The
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design property is formulated in the reduced parametrization as a system of nonlinear equations.

(vi)

We prove that this system of equations is solvable and
yields a
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design curve.

In the subsequent sections we will report on our progress in regard to the numerical process and the
beautification process.

3.3  Beautification of some candidates

We now start the beautification process. For
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design points, the beautifying
procedure usually starts with the computation of all pairwise inner
products. The goal is to eventually identify (or ”guess”) a finite group of rotations and reflections, so that the points are (unions of) orbits of this symmetry group
[
37

In the setting of curves, the inner products between the control
points do not reveal sufficient information to make an educated guess
about any group orbits. Instead, as outlined in the beautification
process in Section
3.1
, we efficiently parameterize the
control points, rewrite the
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design conditions in terms of these
parameters, and eventually argue that the parameter equations are
solvable.

4  Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund Inequalities

We have observed that a closed, simple curve that connects
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design points along geodesic
arcs is not a geodesic
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design cycle
in general.
The idea in the previous section was to alter and modify this cycle by numerical optimization combined with a beautification procedure that led to
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design curves for some
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
.

In a different direction one may ask what do we obtain if we connect

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design points? In this section we will show that the resulting
geodesic cycles always satisfy Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities. Such inequalities are less rigid, and the construction of curves satisfying Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities is expected to be easier than
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design curves.

Definition 4.1
.

Fix
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}1\leq p<\infty
. A sequence of curves
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}(\gamma_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
on
the sphere
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathbb{S}^{d}
is called a Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund family for
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}L^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{d})
, if there
are constants
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}0<A\leq B<\infty
such that, for all degrees
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t\in\mathbb{N}

and for all polynomials
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}f\in\Pi_{t}

(27)

A\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\leq\Big(\frac{1}{\ell(\gamma_{t})}\int_{\gamma}|f|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\leq B\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\,.

For
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}p=\infty
, we use the supremum norm on
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathbb{S}^{d}
and on
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma
, so
that
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\gamma)}=\sup_{s\in[0,1]}|f(\gamma(s))|
.

The point of Definition
4.1
is that the constants
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}A

and
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}B
are uniform in
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
(but they may depend on
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}p
).

The Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities state the norm equivalence of
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}
and
the sampled norm along the curve
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma
. For fixed degree
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
the
inequalities in (
27
) hold for all
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}p
simultaneously since

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\Pi_{t}
is finite-dimensional. This is a new form of discretization of the
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}p
-norm, applied along curves instead of point sets.
See
[
39
]
for a first orientation on sampling and
discretization of norms.

The connection to
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design curves for
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}p=2
is explained in the following lemma that expresses the
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
-design property as a norm equality.

Lemma 4.1
.

A closed curve
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma
is a
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}2t
-design curve if and only if

(28)

\frac{1}{\ell(\gamma)}\int_{\gamma}|f|^{2}=\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}\,\qquad\text{for all }f\in\Pi_{t}\,.

Proof.

The obvious relation
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}|f|^{2}\in\Pi_{2t}
for
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}f\in\Pi_{t}
implies the
necessity. Conversely, the sufficiency follows from the polarization

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}fg=\frac{1}{4}(f+g)^{2}-\frac{1}{4}(f-g)^{2}
, which implies the
identity of subspaces

\spann\{|f|^{2}:f\in\Pi_{t}\}=\spann\{fg:f,g\in\Pi_{t}\}\,.

Clearly the vector space on the right-hand-side coincides with the polynomials
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\Pi_{2t}
.

4.1  Existence of Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund curves

For fixed degree
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
it does not seem too difficult to find a curve
that satisfies a Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequality of the type (
27
).
It amounts to choosing
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma
such that
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}f\in\Pi_{t}
and
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}f\not\equiv 0
imply that
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}f|_{\gamma}\not\equiv 0
, i.e.,
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma
must
not be contained in the zero set of a non-zero polynomial in
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\Pi_{t}
.
For this it suffices that the covering radius of
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma
is small
enough, see
[
10
, Thm. 2.2]
for this argument.

However, if we require Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities (
27
) for

all

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t\in\mathbb{N}
with uniform constants
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}A_{p},B_{p}>0
independent of

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
, then we face a much more challenging problem.

We now formulate our main result on Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities for curves. In
addition to the existence, the following theorem clarifies how the
constants in the Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities depend on
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}p
.

Theorem 4.2
.

There are constants
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}0<A_{d}\leq B_{d}<\infty
and for each
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\varepsilon\in(0,1)
there is another constant
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}C_{d,\varepsilon}>0
and a sequence of geodesic
cycles
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}(\gamma^{(t)}_{d,\varepsilon})_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathbb{S}^{d}
with the following
properties:

(i) For all
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}p\in[1,\infty]
and all degrees
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t\in\mathbb{N}
, the norm equivalence

(29)

\displaystyle A_{d}^{1/p}(1-\varepsilon)\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(\gamma^{(t)}_{d,\varepsilon})}\leq B_{d}^{1/p}(1+\varepsilon)\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\,,

holds for all
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}f\in\Pi_{t}
, and

(ii) the length of the curves is bounded by

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.5}\ell(\gamma^{(t)}_{d,\varepsilon})\leq C_{d,\varepsilon}t^{d-1}\,.

For
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}p=\infty
, we may always take
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}A_{d}^{1/p}=1
and use the
trivial upper bound
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\gamma^{(t)}_{d,\varepsilon})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}
, so that
the appropriate inequalities in place of (
29
)
are

(1-\varepsilon)\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\gamma^{(t)}_{d,\varepsilon})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\,.

The upper bound on the arc length

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\ell(\gamma^{(t)}_{d,\varepsilon})\leq C_{d,\varepsilon}t^{d-1}

means that the Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities are achieved by a
sequence of curves whose arc lengths match the order of the lower bound
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\ell(\gamma^{(t)})\geq c_{d}t^{d-1}
in (
2
).

According to Lemma
4.1
, every sequence

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}(\gamma^{(t)})_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}2t
-design curves satisfies (
29
)
for
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}p=2
. Therefore the existence of Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund curves in dimension

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}d=2,3
for
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}p=2
is covered by the constructions in
[
10
,
28
]
.

Strategy:

Almost all constructions of Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities start with a partition of the
underlying space, in our case of the
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}d
-sphere
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathbb{S}^{d}
.
Let
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathcal{R}=\{R_{1},\dots,R_{n}\}
be a partition of
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathbb{S}^{d}
. We use a
relaxed definition and assume only that
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\bigcup_{j=1}^{n}R_{j}=\mathbb{S}^{d}

and that the intersections
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}R_{j}\cap R_{k}
, for
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}j\neq k
, have measure zero.

The idea is to start with a sufficiently
nice
partition

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathcal{R}=\{R_{1},\ldots,R_{n}\}
, so that points
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
with

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}x_{j}\in R_{j}
satisfy discrete Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.5}(1-\varepsilon)\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\leq\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|f(x_{j})|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\,.

We then build a geodesic cycle connecting these points by geodesic
arcs and verify that the Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities for the points induce the
corresponding inequalities for the curve.

We split the proof of Theorem
4.2
into three subsections.

4.2  Partitions

Following
[
2
]
a partition
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathcal{R}
is called
area-regular if all patches
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}R_{j}
have the same measure, i.e.,

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}|R_{j}|=1/n
.
The size of a partition
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathcal{R}=\{R_{1},\ldots,R_{n}\}
is

(30)

\|\mathcal{R}\|:=\max_{j=1,\dots,n}\mathrm{diam}\,R_{j}\,.

The main theorem about partitions and Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities on the sphere can be
summarized by saying that every choice of points from a sufficiently
fine partition yields a Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequality. A precise version goes as
follows. See
[
13
,
1
,
2
,
29
,
30
,
31
,
33
]

for several
variations.

Theorem 4.3
.

There exists a
constant
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}c_{d}>0
such that, for every
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, every area-regular partition
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathcal{R}=\{R_{1},\dots,R_{n}\}
of
size

(31)

\|\mathcal{R}\|\leq c_{d}\varepsilon t^{-1}

has the following property: for every
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}p\in[1,\infty)
, every collection of points
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
with
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}x_{j}\in{R}_{j}
yields equal-weight Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities
1
1

1

[
13
]
assumes
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}x_{j}
to be in the interior of
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}R_{j}
but this is not necessary.

(32)

(1-\varepsilon)\|f\|_{L^{p}}\leq\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|f(x_{j})|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\|f\|_{L^{p}}\,,\qquad\text{for all }f\in\Pi_{t}\,.

For
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}p=\infty
one has
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}(1-\varepsilon)\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\max_{j=1,\ldots,n}|f(x_{j})|\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}
for all
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}f\in\Pi_{t}
.

To match the size requirements (
31
) on the partition, we recall a simplified version of the existence results in
[
2
,
15
]
.

Proposition 4.4
.

There is a constant
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}C_{\mathrm{diam}}>0
depending only on the dimension
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}d
such that, for every
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists an area-regular partition

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathcal{R}=\{R_{1},\ldots,R_{n}\}
of
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathbb{S}^{d}
satisfying

(33)

\|\mathcal{R}\|\leq C_{\mathrm{diam}}n^{-1/d}.

Comparing the required size
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\|\mathcal{R}\|\leq c_{d}\varepsilon t^{-1}
in (
31
) with
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\|\mathcal{R}\|\leq C_{\mathrm{diam}}n^{-1/d}

in (
33
), we should choose the
number of patches as

(34)

n\geq\frac{C_{\diam}^{d}}{\varepsilon^{d}c_{d}^{d}}t^{d}\,.

Note that the constant
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\frac{C_{\diam}^{d}}{\varepsilon^{d}c_{d}^{d}}
only depends on
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}d
and
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\varepsilon
but not on
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}p
.

To transfer the Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities from points to curves, we need to investigate the geometry of the partition more closely.
We define two types of neighborhoods of a patch
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}R_{j}
. Let

M_{j}:=\{k:\overline{R_{k}}\cap\overline{R_{j}}\neq\emptyset\}\,,

and define the first neighborhood by all patches that touch or “kiss”
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}R_{j}
,

U_{j}:=\bigcup_{k\in M_{j}}R_{k}\,.

We call
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\#M_{j}
the kissing number of
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}R_{j}
.

For a convex version of the kissing number, we denote the closed
geodesic convex hull of
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}U_{k}
by
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathrm{conv}(U_{k})
. By definition,

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathrm{conv}(U_{k})
contains all geodesic arcs between points in

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}U_{k}
. Set

N_{j}:=\{k:\mathrm{conv}(U_{k})\cap\overline{R_{j}}\neq\emptyset\}

and define the second type of neighborhood by

V_{j}:=\bigcup_{k\in N_{j}}\mathrm{conv}(U_{k}))\,.

The following lemma provides an upper bound for the kissing numbers
that depends only on the dimension
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}d
and the constant
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}C_{\mathrm{diam}}
, but not on the actual partition.

Lemma 4.5
.

If
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathcal{R}=\{R_{1},\ldots,R_{n}\}
is an area-regular partition of
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathbb{S}^{d}
satisfying
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\|\mathcal{R}\|\leq C_{\mathrm{diam}}n^{-1/d}
, then

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.5}\#M_{j}\leq\#N_{j}\leq C_{\mathrm{kiss}},

for some constant
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}C_{\mathrm{kiss}}\in\mathbb{N}
that depends only on
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}d
and
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}C_{\mathrm{diam}}
.

Proof.

Since
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}R_{k}\subseteq U_{k}
, the condition
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\overline{R_{k}}\cap\overline{R_{j}}\neq\emptyset
implies
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathrm{conv}(R_{k})\cap\overline{R_{j}}\neq\emptyset
and thus the kissing numbers satisfy

\#M_{j}\leq\#N_{j}\,.

Furthermore, since
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathcal{R}
is an area-regular partition, we have
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}n|R_{k}|=1
for all
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}k
and
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}R_{k}\subseteq\mathrm{conv}(U_{k})
yields

(35)

\#N_{j}=\#N_{j}n|R_{k}|=n\Big|\bigcup_{k\in N_{j}}R_{k}\Big|\leq n|V_{j}|\,.

Next, since by assumption
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\|\mathcal{R}\|\leq C_{\mathrm{diam}}n^{-1/d}
, each
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}U_{k}
is contained in a ball of diameter
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}3C_{\mathrm{diam}}n^{-1/d}
, and by convexity
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathrm{conv}(U_{k})
is contained in the same ball.

We observe that the diameter of the neighborhood
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}V_{j}
is bounded by
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}2\cdot 3C_{\mathrm{diam}}n^{-1/d}+C_{\mathrm{diam}}n^{-1/d}=7C_{\mathrm{diam}}n^{-1/d}
. Therefore, its volume satisfies
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}|V_{j}|\leq C_{\mathrm{kiss}}n^{-1}
, for some constant
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}C_{\mathrm{kiss}}>0
that depends on
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}d
and
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}C_{\mathrm{diam}}
. Therefore, we derive
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}n|V_{j}|\leq C_{\mathrm{kiss}}
. Combined with (
35
), we obtain
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\#N_{j}\leq C_{\mathrm{kiss}}
.

Finally we require partitions whose patches contain sufficiently large convex balls. The following existence result
[
2
,
15
]
strengthens Proposition
4.4
.

Theorem 4.6
.

There are constants
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}c_{\mathrm{in}},C_{\diam}>0
depending only on
the dimension
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}d
, such that, for every
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}n\in\mathbb{N}
, the following holds: there exists an area-regular partition
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathcal{R}=\{R_{1},\ldots,R_{n}\}
on
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathbb{S}^{d}
of size
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\|\mathcal{R}\|\leq C_{\diam}n^{-1/d}
such that each patch
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}R_{j}
contains a spherical cap of radius
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}c_{\mathrm{in}}n^{-1/d}
. Furthermore, each patch
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}R_{j}
can be chosen to be convex.

4.3  Construction of the curve from the partition

We start with a convex, area-regular partition
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathcal{R}
of size
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\|\mathcal{R}\|\leq C_{\diam}n^{-1/d}

as in Theorem
4.6
, so that the discrete Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities of Theorem
4.3
hold. Given
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t\in\mathbb{N}
, without loss of generality, we may assume

(36)

n=\frac{C_{\diam}^{d}}{\varepsilon^{d}c_{d}^{d}}t^{d}\in\mathbb{N}\,.

To this partition
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathcal{R}
we associate a graph as follows: its vertices are the patches
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}R_{j}
, and a vertex
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}R_{k}
connects to
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}R_{j}
if

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\overline{R_{k}}\cap\overline{R_{j}}\neq\emptyset
. In this case, we
put
two
edges between the vertex
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}R_{j}
and
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}R_{k}
. The number of
edges at
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}R_{j}
is twice the kissing number
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}2\#M_{j}
, consequently by
Lemma
4.5
this graph has bounded degree.

This graph is connected, see
[
10
, Lemma 4.1]
. Since we have
doubled the edges, by Euler’s criterion on even vertex degree

[
42
]
, there is an Euler cycle on the graph.

By definition, the Euler cycle traverses each edge exactly once and hence visits each patch
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}R_{j}
at least once.

Since
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}R_{j}
contains a spherical cap of radius
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}c_{\mathrm{in}}n^{-1/d}
, we may choose
two
points
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}x_{2j-1},x_{2j}
in the inner spherical cap of
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}R_{j}
such that

(37)

\dist(x_{2j-1},x_{2j})=c_{\mathrm{in}}n^{-1/d}\,.

Since they are contained in a convex subset of
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}R_{j}
, the geodesic
arcs
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma_{j}:=\gamma_{x_{2j-1},x_{2j}}
from
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}x_{2j-1}
to
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}x_{2j}

are also contained in
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}R_{j}
.

The Euler cycle induces geodesic arcs

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\tilde{\gamma}_{i}:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{d}
between points in different patches, but some care is needed. After all, the Euler cycle is a combinatorial object that connects
patches
. To construct the geodesic arcs, we must connect
points
from one patch to another patch, and there is some choice since each patch contains two points.

We start at
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}R_{1}
and agree upon the following construction rules for the arcs.

-

When visiting a patch for the first time, we go along
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma_{j}
, hence, at the beginning along
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma_{1}
.

-

Following the Euler cycle, we visit the next patch, say

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}R_{j}
, and always arrive at the odd indexed point
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}x_{2j-1}
.

-

If we visit
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}R_{j}
for the first time, then we continue along

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma_{j}
to
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}x_{2j}
. If we have already visited
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}R_{j}
before, then we
proceed directly according to the Euler cycle from
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}x_{2j-1}
to
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}x_{2k-1}

in some adjacent patch
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}R_{k}
.

For each
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
, the resulting geodesic chain
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma
is a suitable union of arcs
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma_{j}
contained in
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}R_{j}
and arcs
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\tilde{\gamma}_{i}
connecting points from adjacent patches. (To check integration properties, we may ignore any specific ordering, in which the arcs need to be combined).

The length of each arc
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma_{j}
in
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}R_{j}
is

\ell(\gamma_{j})=c_{\mathrm{in}}n^{-1/d}\,,

and there are
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}n
of them. The length of an arc

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\tilde{\gamma}_{i}
connecting adjacent patches is bounded by

\ell(\tilde{\gamma}_{i})\leq 2C_{\diam}n^{-1/d}\,,

and there are at most
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}2nC_{\mathrm{kiss}}
of them. Hence, the length of
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma
is sandwiched between

c_{\mathrm{in}}n^{1-1/d}\leq\ell(\gamma)\leq(c_{\mathrm{in}}+2C_{\mathrm{kiss}}2C_{\diam})n^{1-1/d}\,.

Since
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}n
is of the order
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t^{d}
in (
36
), the length
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\ell(\gamma)
is bounded by a constant times
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t^{d-1}
as claimed in Theorem
4.2
.

4.4  The curve
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma
satisfies Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities

We fix
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}f\in\Pi_{t}
and aim to verify the inequalities with constants that do not depend on the specific choice of
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}f
and are independent of
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}t
. We only consider
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}p\in[1,\infty)
at this point.

The arcs
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma_{j}
are contained in a convex subset of
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}R_{j}
. We use the mean value theorem and obtain parameters
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\tau_{j}
(that may depend on
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}f
)
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma_{j}}|f|^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}|f(\gamma_{j}(s))|^{p}\|\dot{\gamma_{j}}(s)\|\,ds

\displaystyle=|f(\gamma_{j}(\tau_{j}))|^{p}\ell(\gamma_{j})

\displaystyle=|f(y_{j})|^{p}\ell(\gamma_{j})\,.

Here the point
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}y_{j}=\gamma_{j}(\tau_{j})
is in
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}R_{j}
by the convexity of the inner ball in
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}R_{j}
.
We point out that
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}y_{j}
may depend of
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}f
. Since
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}y_{j}\in R_{j}
for
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}j=1,\dots,n
, Theorem
4.3
implies that the points
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\{y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\}
satisfy Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities, and therefore

(38)

(1-\varepsilon)^{p}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}|f|^{p}\leq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|f(y_{j})|^{p}\leq(1+\varepsilon)^{p}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}|f|^{p}\,.

Next, we transfer this inequality from points to the curve.

Lower bound for a Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequality

To derive the lower bound for a Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequality for the full curve

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma
, we observe

\frac{\ell(\gamma_{j})}{\ell(\gamma)}\geq\frac{c_{\mathrm{in}}n^{-1/d}}{(c_{\mathrm{in}}+4C_{\mathrm{kiss}}C_{\diam})n^{1-1/d}}=\frac{A_{d}}{n}\,,

where
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}A_{d}=\frac{c_{\mathrm{in}}}{(c_{\mathrm{in}}+4C_{\mathrm{kiss}}C_{\diam})}
depends only on the dimension.

In combination with the lower Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund bound (
38
) we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\ell(\gamma)}\int_{\gamma}|f|^{p}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\ell(\gamma)}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{\gamma_{j}}|f|^{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\ell(\gamma)}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\ell(\gamma_{j})|f(y_{j})|^{p}

\displaystyle\geq A_{d}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|f(y_{j})|^{p}

\displaystyle\geq A_{d}(1-\varepsilon)^{p}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}|f|^{p}\,.

Upper bound for a Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequality

To verify the upper Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequality, we estimate the contribution of the arcs
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma_{j}
similarly. The fraction of the length

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.5}\frac{\ell(\gamma_{j})}{\ell(\gamma)}\leq\frac{c_{\mathrm{in}}n^{-1/d}}{c_{\mathrm{in}}n^{1-1/d}}=\frac{1}{n}

leads to the upper bound for the contribution of the arcs
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma_{j}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\ell(\gamma)}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{\gamma_{j}}|f|^{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\ell(\gamma)}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\ell(\gamma_{j})|f(y_{j})|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|f(y_{j})|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\varepsilon)^{p}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}|f|^{p}\,.

For the contribution of
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\tilde{\gamma}_{i}
to the upper
Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund bound, we first bound the ratios
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\ell(\tilde{\gamma}_{i})/\ell(\gamma)
.
Since
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\ell(\tilde{\gamma}_{i})\leq 2C_{\diam}n^{-1/d}
and

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\ell(\gamma)\geq c_{\mathrm{in}}n^{1-1/d}
, we obtain

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.5}\frac{\ell(\tilde{\gamma}_{i})}{\ell(\gamma)}\leq\frac{2C_{\diam}n^{-1/d}}{c_{\mathrm{in}}n^{1-1/d}}\leq\frac{\tilde{B}_{d}}{n},

where
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\tilde{B}_{d}=\frac{2C_{\diam}}{c_{\mathrm{in}}}
depends only on
the dimension
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}d
.

The integral along
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\tilde{\gamma}_{i}
is again evaluated by the mean value theorem, which yields a point
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}z_{i}=\tilde{\gamma}_{i}(\tau^{\prime}_{i})
such that

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.5}\int_{\tilde{\gamma}_{i}}|f|^{p}=|f(\tilde{\gamma}_{i}(\tau^{\prime}_{i}))|^{p}\ell(\tilde{\gamma}_{j})=|f(z_{i})|^{p}\ell(\tilde{\gamma}_{i})\,.

For each
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\tilde{\gamma}_{i}
, there is
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
such that
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\tilde{\gamma}_{i}
is an arc starting from one of the two points in
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}R_{k}
, so that

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}z_{i}
must lie in
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathrm{conv}(U_{k})
, where
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}U_{k}=\bigcup_{\overline{R}_{k}\cap\overline{R}_{l}\neq\emptyset}R_{l}
. Since by Lemma
4.5

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.5}\#N_{j}=\#\{k:\mathrm{conv}(U_{k})\cap\overline{R_{j}}\neq\emptyset\}\leq C_{\mathrm{kiss}},

each
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}R_{j}
contains at most
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}2C_{\mathrm{kiss}}
many of the points
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}z_{i}
.

We may add more points, so that every
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}R_{j}
contains
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}2C_{\mathrm{kiss}}
points. This enhanced set is a
union of
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}2C_{\mathrm{kiss}}
Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund sets. Therefore we may use the upper bound of Theorem
4.3
for the points
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}z_{i}
when the bounds are multiplied by
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}2C_{\mathrm{kiss}}
.

The contribution of the
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\tilde{\gamma}_{i}
is now bounded by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\ell(\gamma)}\sum_{i}\int_{\tilde{\gamma}_{i}}|f|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\ell(\gamma)}\sum_{i}|f(z_{i})|^{p}\ell(\tilde{\gamma}_{i})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\tilde{B}_{d}}{n}\sum_{i}|f(z_{i})|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{B}_{d}2C_{\mathrm{kiss}}(1+\varepsilon)^{p}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}|f|^{p}\,.

Altogether, we obtain the upper Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund bound

\color[rgb]{0,0,0.5}\frac{1}{\ell(\gamma)}\int_{\gamma}|f|^{p}\leq(1+\tilde{B}_{d}2C_{\mathrm{kiss}})(1+\varepsilon)^{p}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}|f|^{p}\,,\qquad\text{for all }f\in\Pi_{t}\,,

so that we may choose
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}B_{d}=1+\tilde{B}_{d}2C_{\mathrm{kiss}}
.

For the case
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}p=\infty
, the upper inequality is trivially satisfied by
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\gamma)}\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}
. To verify the lower bound, we know that the trace of the arc
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\gamma_{j}
is compact in
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\mathbb{S}^{d}
, so that there is
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}y_{j}\in R_{j}
satisfying

\max_{s\in[0,1]}|f(\gamma_{j}(s))|=|f(y_{j})|\,,\quad j=1,\ldots,n\,.

The lower Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequality for the points
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\{y_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
with
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}p=\infty
yields

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\gamma)}

\displaystyle\geq\max_{j=1,\ldots,n}|f(y_{j})|\geq(1-\varepsilon)\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\,,

which concludes the proof of Theorem
4.2
.

4.2
in the introduction.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We may choose
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}\varepsilon=\frac{1}{2}
and
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}A_{d}\leq 1\leq B_{d}
in Theorem
4.2
, so that the inequalities

A_{d}\leq A_{d}^{1/p}\leq B^{1/p}_{d}\leq B_{d}

imply Theorem
1.1
in the introduction with the lower and upper constants
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}A_{d}/2
and
\color[rgb]{0,0,0.3}B_{d}/2
.

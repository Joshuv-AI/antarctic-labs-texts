From Interpolating Formulas to Separating Languages and Back Again
Agi Kurucz, Frank Wolter, Michael Zakharyaschev

Abstract
Traditionally, research on Craig interpolation is concerned with ( a ) (a) establishing the Craig interpolation property (CIP) of a logic saying that every valid implication in the logic has a Craig interpolant and ( b ) (b) designing algorithms that extract Craig interpolants from proofs. Logics that lack the CIP are regarded as ‘pathological’ and excluded from consideration. In this chapter, we survey variations and generalisations of traditional Craig interpolation. First, we consider Craig interpolants for implications in logics without the CIP, focusing on the decidability and complexity of deciding their existence. We then generalise interpolation by looking for Craig interpolants in languages L ′ L^{\prime} that can be weaker than the language L L of the given implication. Thus, do not only we restrict the non-logical symbols of Craig interpolants but also the logical ones. The resulting L / L ′ L/L^{\prime} -interpolation problem generalises L / L ′ L/L^{\prime} -definability, the question whether an L L -formula is equivalent to some L ′ L^{\prime} -formula. After that, we move from logical languages to formal languages where interpolation disguises itself as separation: given two disjoint languages in a class 𝒞 \mathcal{C} , does there exist a separating language in a smaller class 𝒞 ′ \mathcal{C}^{\prime} ? This question is particularly well-studied in the case when the input languages are regular and the separating language is first-order definable. Finally, we connect the different research strands by showing how the decidability of the separation problem for regular languages can be used to prove the decidability of Craig interpolant existence for linear temporal logic 𝖫𝖳𝖫 \mathsf{LTL} .

Introduction

Suppose
L
is a logic and
\varphi
,
\psi
are formulas in the language of
L
.
A
Craig interpolant
for the entailment
\varphi\models_{L}\psi
in

\varphi
and
\psi

\varphi\models_{L}\chi

\chi\models_{L}\psi
are valid in
L
. The logic

is said to have the
Craig interpolation property
(CIP, for short) if
every
valid entailment
\varphi\models_{L}\psi
has a Craig interpolant
\chi
in
L
. Since Craig’s
[
34
]
discovery that first-order logic,
\mathsf{FO}
, enjoys the CIP, two questions have traditionally been addressed for every notable logic
L
: Does
L
have the CIP? And if it does, how to uniformly extract Craig interpolants from formal proofs of entailments
\varphi\models_{L}\psi
?
Logics lacking interpolants for some valid entailments—the ugly ducklings of the family—have only recently drawn attention in the Craig interpolation line of research and applications.

Our aim in this chapter is to present a broader view on the interpolation-related research directions and discuss most important results. We proceed in three generalisation steps. The first of them breaks the traditional uniform ‘all or nothing’ approach to the CIP and turns it into a
decision problem
.

1.1
Craig Interpolant Existence Problem

We start by looking at a few important logics
L
that
do not
enjoy the CIP: propositional modal logics such as
\mathsf{S4.3}
, weak
\mathsf{K4}
, and
\mathsf{Alt}_{3}
, modal logics with nominals, decidable fragments of
\mathsf{FO}
such as the two variable and guarded fragments
\mathsf{FO^{2}}
and
\mathsf{GF}
, linear temporal logic
\mathsf{LTL}
, and first-order modal logics under the constant domain semantics.
For all of these logics

, the existence of a Craig interpolant for an entailment
\varphi\models_{L}\psi
does not follow from the validity of that entailment in
L
. We regard it as a new type of decision problem for
L
, the
interpolant existence problem
(IEP): given input formulas
\varphi
and
\psi
, decide whether the entailment
\varphi\models_{L}\psi
has a Craig interpolant in
L
. As the IEP for
L
typically generalises entailment in
L

L

We discuss recent developments in this area, focusing on the logics listed above.

Craig interpolation is concerned with the shared non-logical symbols of the premise
\varphi
and conclusion
\psi
. One can think of Craig interpolants as eliminating the non-shared symbols in the premise and conclusion, thereby providing an ‘explanation’ of the entailment in relevant terms. For example, in the extreme case when the signatures of propositional formulas
\varphi
and
\psi
are disjoint, only the logical constants
\bot
or
\top
can be a Craig interpolant for
\varphi\models_{L}\psi
, which means that either
\neg\varphi
or
\psi
is valid in
L
.
To find an ‘explanation in simpler terms’​, one could look for interpolants that eliminate certain logical constructs from the language, say recursion or counting. This leads us to the second step of generalisation.

1.3
Defining and Separating Formal Languages

Our third step consists of looking into definability and separability in the context of formal languages, where
the study of these problems has existed and flourished independently from Craig interpolation for many years.
The

and
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{2}
from some class

(say, specified by means of some device such as a finite automaton), whether there exists a language
{\boldsymbol{L}}

and

{\boldsymbol{L}}_{1}\subseteq{\boldsymbol{L}}
and

If

(also known as

belongs to
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}

A large chunk of the research in this area has been focused on deciding and separating regular languages.
In Section
5
, we discuss in detail a particular instance of these questions: the decidability of defining/separating regular languages consisting of finite words by star-free (or, equivalently,
\mathsf{FO}
-definable) languages.
We also provide further references to work in a wider context including
languages with infinite and tree-shaped words, employing
\mathsf{FO}
-fragments as definitions/separators, and defining/separating non-regular languages by means of regular ones.

Rather surprisingly, it turns out that this final third step leads to a beautiful solution to a
purely logical problem encountered in the first step:
In Section
6
, we show how the Craig interpolant existence problem for propositional linear temporal logic
\mathsf{LTL}
over finite timelines
(or, equivalently,
\mathsf{FO}
over finite words)
can be solved by deciding separability of regular languages by star-free languages.

The variant of first-order logic,

We are also going to consider a few standard

-fragments

3.1

Proof.

(\Leftarrow)
Let

\varphi

, Proposition

[

Lemma 6
.

If a finitely axiomatisable normal modal logic

L
is decidable.

of
\varphi
,
\psi
.

\mathfrak{bt}^{\ast}=(\mathfrak{t}^{\ast},\mathfrak{b}_{1}^{\ast},\mathfrak{b}_{2}^{\ast})
,
\mathfrak{bt}^{\prime}=(\mathfrak{t}^{\prime},\mathfrak{b}_{1},\mathfrak{b}_{2})
, and
(\mathfrak{bt},\mathfrak{bt}^{\ast})\in R_{1}^{\prime}
. By definition, there are
u,v\in W_{1}
with
uR_{1}v
,
\mathfrak{bt}=\mathfrak{bt}_{\mathfrak{M}_{1}}(u)
, and
\mathfrak{bt}^{\ast}=\mathfrak{bt}_{\mathfrak{M}_{1}}(v)
. Also by definition, there exists
u^{\prime}\in W_{2}
with
\mathfrak{t}_{\mathfrak{M}_{2}}(u^{\prime})=\mathfrak{t}^{\prime}
and
\mathfrak{M}_{1},u\sim_{\mathsf{ML\!^{nom}}(\sigma)}\mathfrak{M}_{2},u^{\prime}
. By the definition of
\sigma
-bisimulations, there exists
v^{\prime}
such that
u^{\prime}R_{2}v^{\prime}
and
\mathfrak{M}_{1},v\sim_{\mathsf{ML\!^{nom}}(\sigma)}\mathfrak{M}_{2},v^{\prime}
. Then we have
(\mathfrak{bt}^{\ast},\mathfrak{bt}_{\mathfrak{M}_{2}}(v^{\prime}))\in\boldsymbol{\beta}
and
(\mathfrak{bt}^{\prime},\mathfrak{bt}_{\mathfrak{M}_{2}}(v^{\prime}))\in R_{2}^{\prime}

\dashv

It follows that
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}_{l},\mathfrak{bt}_{\mathfrak{M}_{l}}(u)\models\varphi_{l}
and
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}_{1},\mathfrak{bt}_{\mathfrak{M}_{1}}(w_{1})\sim_{\mathsf{ML\!^{nom}}(\sigma)}\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}_{2},\mathfrak{bt}_{\mathfrak{M}_{2}}(w_{2})
, as required.

Note that rather than building models directly from types we now build
\sigma
-bisimilar models from sets
\mathfrak{b}_{\mathfrak{M}_{k}}(u)
of types satisfied in
\sigma
-bisimilar nodes. The constructed models are of double-exponential size in
|\varphi_{1}|+|\varphi_{2}|
.

\dashv

The obtained double-exponential FBMP gives an obvious
coN2ExpTime
algorithm deciding the IEP for
\mathsf{K^{nom}}
. This algorithm is not optimal. In fact, by unfolding the bisimilar models into bisimilar forest-shaped models (except for worlds denoted by nominals) and cutting them off at depth coinciding with the maximal modal depth of
\varphi
and
\psi
, one can establish the exponential
FBMP, which yields the following result:

Theorem 11

(
[
7
]
)
.

The IEP for
\mathsf{K^{nom}}
is
coNExpTime
-complete.

We illustrate the reason for this higher complexity of the IEP by sketching the idea of the lower bound construction. The proof is by reduction of the
NExpTime
-complete
2^{n}\times 2^{n}
-tiling problem to the complement of the IEP for
\mathsf{K^{nom}}
. Given any
n<\omega
and a finite set

of tile types, we define formulas
\varphi_{n,T}
and
\psi_{n,T}
of polynomial size in
|T|
and
n
such that
\varphi_{n,T}\to\psi_{n,T}\in\mathsf{K^{nom}}
and

can tile a
2^{n}\times 2^{n}
-grid iff
\varphi_{n,T}
and
\psi_{n,T}
do not have an interpolant in
\mathsf{K^{nom}}
. The common signature
\sigma_{n,T}
of
\varphi_{n,T}
and
\psi_{n,T}
contains
O(n)
-many variables that are used to speak about binary representations of cells in the
2^{n}\times 2^{n}
grid;
\sigma_{n,T}
also contains variables for the tile types in
T
. The formula
\varphi_{n,T}
contains the conjunct
\Diamond^{2n}\boldsymbol{i}\wedge\Box^{2n}\boldsymbol{i}
,
which is true in a pointed model
\mathfrak{M},w
iff the only world accessible from
w
in
2n
steps in
\mathfrak{M}
is where nominal
\boldsymbol{i}
holds. The formula
\psi_{n,T}
contains standard conjuncts with
O(n)
-many non-
\sigma_{n,T}
variables that generate a full binary tree of depth
2n

[
27
,
19
]
whose leaves
u_{1},\dots,u_{2^{2n}}
are regarded as the cells of the grid.
Now, suppose that
\mathfrak{M}_{1},w_{1}\sim_{\mathsf{ML\!^{nom}}(\sigma_{n})}\mathfrak{M}_{2},w_{2}
,
\mathfrak{M}_{1},w_{1}\models\varphi_{n,T}
and
\mathfrak{M}_{2},w_{2}\models\psi_{n,T}
. Then all the leaves
u_{l}
of that full binary tree of depth
2n
in
\mathfrak{M}_{2}
are
\sigma_{n,T}
-bisimilar to the world where
\boldsymbol{i}
is true in
\mathfrak{M}_{1}
, and so all trees with the roots
u_{1},\dots,u_{2^{2n}}
in
\mathfrak{M}_{2}
are
\sigma_{n,T}
-bisimilar to each other. The other conjuncts of
\psi_{n,T}
make sure that each tree rooted at
u_{l}
describes the tile types of the grid cell represented by
u_{l}
and of its neighbours in the grid; the
\sigma_{n,T}
-bisimilarity of these trees ensures that these local descriptions are consistent.

3.3
Interpolant Existence in Fragments of First-Order Logic

Locating expressive and yet decidable fragments of
\mathsf{FO}
has been a popular research topic for more than a century
[
23
,
94
]
. It turns out, however, that, unlike full
\mathsf{FO}
, many of its prominent decidable fragments do not enjoy the CIP, and for these, the question arises as to whether the IEP is decidable, and, if so, whether its complexity is the same as that of entailment. We refer the reader to
[
31
]
and
[
105
]
for a detailed discussion of the landscape of fragments of
\mathsf{FO}
with and without the CIP.

The investigation of the IEP for
\mathsf{FO}
-fragments without the CIP has only just begun. Two prominent fragments of
\mathsf{FO}
that lack the CIP
are the two-variable fragment
\mathsf{FO^{2}}
and the guarded fragment
\mathsf{GF}
. In
\mathsf{FO^{2}}
, one restricts the number of individual variables in formulas to two. By re-using variables, one can show that
\mathsf{ML}
(and even
\mathsf{ML}
with nominals) are fragments of
\mathsf{FO^{2}}
under the standard translation
[
19
]
(see Section

).
Satisfiability of formulas in
\mathsf{FO^{2}}
is
NExpTime
-complete, which can be shown by proving its exponential FMP
[
23
,
94
]
. By generalising the technique discussed in the previous section for
\mathsf{K^{nom}}
, one can show that
\mathsf{FO^{2}}
has the double-exponential FBMP, for an appropriate notion of bisimulation characterising indistinguishability of models in
\mathsf{FO^{2}}
. In contrast to the exponential FBMP of
\mathsf{K^{nom}}
and the exponential FMP of
\mathsf{FO^{2}}
, this double exponential bound is optimal.

Theorem 12

(
[
62
]
)
.

The IEP for
\mathsf{FO^{2}}
is in
coN2ExpTime
and
2\textsc{ExpTime}
-hard.

The investigation of decidable extensions of
\mathsf{FO^{2}}
has been an important research topic, with results ranging from
coNExpTime
-completeness of
\mathsf{FO^{2}}
with
counting quantifiers

\exists^{\geq k}x
, denoted
\mathsf{C^{2}}
, to the decidability of
\mathsf{FO^{2}}
extended with one (built-in) transitive relation and undecidability of
\mathsf{FO^{2}}
with two transitive relations
[
94
]
. At least one such extension provides an example with decidable entailment and undecidable IEP, namely
\mathsf{FO^{2}}
extended with two (built-in) equivalence relations whose entailment is

-complete.

Theorem 13

(
[
115
]
)
.

The IEP for
\mathsf{FO^{2}}

The decidability of the IEP for other extensions of
\mathsf{FO^{2}}
such as
\mathsf{C^{2}}
and
\mathsf{FO^{2}}
with one equivalence relation remains open.

Intermezzo

\mathsf{FO^{2}}
is closely related to prominent decidable fragments of first-order modal logics with constant first-order domains, another family of logics lacking the CIP
[
41
]
. In fact, the one-variable fragment
\smash{\mathsf{Q^{1}S5}}
of first-order modal logic

can be regarded as a fragment of

for
\mathsf{FO^{2}}
are known for this logic, but with subtly different proofs
[

since

\mathsf{FO^{2}}
even without equality can be shown as a corollary. The IEP of the one-variable fragment of first-order modal logic

is also decidable, but in this case no elementary algorithm is known
[

.

The guarded fragment
\mathsf{GF}
of

restricts quantification to guarded quantifiers of the form

ExpTime
if the arity of predicate symbols is fixed and 2

-complete otherwise. While its two-variable fragment,

enjoys the CIP, GF itself does not. Regarding the IEP, it is open whether it has the FBMP for the appropriate notion of bisimulation called

, but one can use instead models of bounded treewidth constructed from sets of types satisfied in guarded bisimilar tuples to show the following:

(
[
62
]
)

The IEP for
\mathsf{GF}
is
2ExpTime
-complete if the arity of predicates is bounded and

-complete otherwise.

4
Interpolation, Separation and Definability in Weaker Languages

In this section, we discuss a generalisation of Craig interpolation, which restricts the
logical constructs
of the language for interpolants between formulas rather than the
vocabulary
of non-logical symbols. All of our languages are assumed to be sublanguages of
monadic second-order logic

\mathsf{MSO}
that extends
\mathsf{FO}
with quantifiers of the form
\exists P
, where
P
is any unary predicate symbol (thus,
\exists x
quantifies over the elements of a domain in question, while
\exists P
over the subsets of that domain). To simplify presentation, we also assume that our languages are closed under negation and that their signature comprises one binary predicate symbol,
R
, and arbitrarily-many unary ones. Unless indicated otherwise, we only consider formulas
\varphi(x)
with at most one free variable
x
.
Modal formulas are often regarded as their standard translations into
\mathsf{FO}
or
\mathsf{MSO}
.

For any such languages
L\supseteq L^{\prime}
, we are interested in the following decision problem:

L/L^{\prime}
-interpolation
:

given two
L
-formulas
\varphi
and
\psi
, decide whether there exists an
L^{\prime}
-formula
\chi
—called an
L^{\prime}
-
interpolant
for
\varphi
and
\psi
—such that
\varphi\models\chi
and
\chi\models\psi
.

Note that, in this definition, we do not require the interpolant
\chi
to be constructed from the shared non-logical symbols of
\varphi
and
\psi
. In various areas of computer science, the interpolation problem is often formulated as a separation problem, which is sometimes more convenient to work with (see, e.g., Sections
5
and
6
below):

L/L^{\prime}
-separation
:

given
L
-formulas
\varphi
and
\psi
, decide whether there is an
L^{\prime}
-formula
\chi
— called an
L^{\prime}
-
separator
for
\varphi
and
\psi
—such that
\varphi\models\chi
and
\chi\models\neg\psi
.

Clearly, these two problems are equivalent to each other because
L
is closed under (classical) negation.

Example 15
.

Consider the
\mathsf{FO}
-formulas

\varphi(x)=\exists^{=1}y\,R(x,y),\quad\psi(x)=\exists^{=1}y\,\big(R(x,y)\wedge A(y)\big)\wedge\exists^{=1}y\,\big(R(x,y)\wedge\neg A(y)\big)

(where
\exists^{=1}y\,\alpha(y)
says that there exists exactly one
y
for which
\alpha(y)
holds trues). Clearly,
\varphi\models\neg\psi
and the
\mathsf{ML}
-formula
\chi=\Box A\vee\Box\neg A
separates
\varphi(x)
and
\psi(x)
.
For
\psi^{\prime}(x)=\exists^{=2}y\,R(x,y)
, we also have
\varphi\models\neg\psi^{\prime}
, but
\varphi(x)
and
\psi^{\prime}(x)
are not
\mathsf{ML}
-separable as follows from Theorem
17
below.
\dashv

L/L^{\prime}
-interpolation and separation are in turn generalisations of the more familiar

L/L^{\prime}
-definability
:

given an
L
-formula
\varphi
, decide whether there exists an
L^{\prime}
-formula
\chi
such that
\varphi\models\chi
and
\chi\models\varphi
.

Example 16
.

The
\mathsf{FO}
-formulas

\varphi_{n}(x)=\exists y\,\big[R(x,y)\wedge\bigwedge_{1\leq i\leq n}\big(A_{i}(x)\leftrightarrow A_{i}(y)\big)\big],\ \ n<\omega,

are
\mathsf{ML}
-definable. Indeed, let
T
be the set of all conjunctions
B_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge B_{n}
, where
B_{i}\in\{A_{i},\neg A_{i}\}
, for
i=1,\dots,n
. Then each
\varphi_{n}
is equivalent to
\chi_{n}=\bigvee_{t\in T}(t\wedge\Diamond t)
. It might be of interest to note that these
\chi_{n}
are of exponential-size in
n
and there are no
\mathsf{ML}
-formulas
\chi_{n}
that are equivalent to
\varphi_{n}
and have polynomial-size
|\textit{sub}(\chi_{n})|

[
68
]
.

Below, we discuss definability and separation in fragments of
\mathsf{FO}
, including various types of modal logic, and in logics with second-order quantification or recursion such as
\mathsf{MSO}
and the modal
\mu
-calculus
\mu\mathsf{ML}
. While model-theoretic methods based on bisimulations are prominent for fragments of
\mathsf{FO}
, they have to be augmented by automata theoretic methods when dealing with second-order quantifiers. We also introduce an elegant ‘good model method’ that has been used to study definability and construct defining formulas whenever they exist.

4.1
Definability and Separation in Fragments of First-Order Logic

The next theorem is an analogue of Theorem
3
, characterising the existence of
L^{\prime}
-separators in terms of
L^{\prime}
-indistinguishable structures.

Theorem 17
.

For any
L^{\prime}\subseteq L\subseteq\mathsf{FO}
and any
L
-formulas
\varphi(x)
and
\psi(x)
, the following conditions are equivalent
:

•

there does not exist an
L^{\prime}
-separator for
\varphi(x)
and
\psi(x)
;

•

there exist structures
\mathfrak{A}_{i}
and
a_{i}\in{\sf dom}(\mathfrak{A}_{i})
,

, such that
\mathfrak{A}_{1}\models\varphi(a_{1})
,
\mathfrak{A}_{2}\models\neg\psi(a_{2})
and
\mathfrak{A}_{1},a_{1}\equiv_{L^{\prime}(\sigma)}\mathfrak{A}_{2},a_{2}
, where
\sigma=\textit{sig}(\varphi)\cup\textit{sig}(\psi)

Here,
\mathfrak{A}_{1},a_{1}\equiv_{L^{\prime}(\sigma)}\mathfrak{A}_{2},a_{2}
means that
\mathfrak{A}_{1}\models\gamma(a_{1})
iff
\mathfrak{A}_{2}\models\gamma(a_{2})
, for all
L^{\prime}(\sigma)
-formulas
\gamma
. This characterisation translates to the following criterion of
L/L^{\prime}
-definability:

Theorem 18
.

Let
L^{\prime}\subseteq L\subseteq\mathsf{FO}
. Then an
L
-formula
\varphi
is logically equivalent to some
L^{\prime}
-formula iff
\varphi
is preserved under
\equiv_{L^{\prime}(\sigma)}
, where
\sigma=\textit{sig}(\varphi)
.

As before, one can make these criteria operational by replacing
\equiv_{L^{\prime}(\sigma)}
with a model-theoretic characterisation via suitable bisimulations. Below, we focus on definability and separation in
\mathsf{ML}
, and give pointers to the literature for more expressive languages. Using the fact that modal equivalence is characterised by bisimulations on
\omega
-saturated structures, we obtain van Benthem’s theorem:

Theorem 19

(
[
110
]
)
.

For any
\mathsf{FO}
-formula
\varphi(x)
the following conditions are equivalent
:

•

\varphi(x)
is equivalent to
(
the standard translation of
)
an
\mathsf{ML}
-formula
;

•

\varphi(x)
is invariant under
\mathsf{ML}(\sigma)
-bisimulations, for
\sigma=\textit{sig}(\varphi)
, which means that, for any pointed models
\mathfrak{M}_{i},u_{i}
,
i=1,2
, whenever
\mathfrak{M}_{1},u_{1}\sim_{\mathsf{ML}(\sigma)}\mathfrak{M}_{2},u_{2}
, then
\mathfrak{M}_{1}\models\varphi(u_{1})
iff
\mathfrak{M}_{2}\models\varphi(u_{2})
.

We aim to apply Theorem
19
to the problem of deciding
\mathsf{F}/\mathsf{ML}
-definability for
\mathsf{FO}
-fragments
\mathsf{F}
. If
\mathsf{F}=\mathsf{FO}
, this problem is undecidable because
\mathsf{FO}
is undecidable. For decidable fragments of
\mathsf{FO}
, one can try to use the approach of the previous section and construct finite or tree-shaped well-behaved bisimilar models refuting the second item above. Here, we first follow a different path and use a more informative version of van Benthem’s theorem whose proof does not use compactness but a more combinatorial approach (that also works on finite models, see below). It employs the notion of
n
-bisimilar pointed models
[
48

, which characterises modal indistinguishability up to formulas of modal depth at most
n
, where the

of an
\mathsf{ML}
-formula is the maximum number of nested modal operators in it. We write
\mathfrak{M}_{1},w_{1}\sim_{\mathsf{ML}(\sigma)}^{n}\mathfrak{M}_{2},w_{2}
if
\mathfrak{M}_{1},w_{1}
and
\mathfrak{M}_{2},w_{2}
are
\sigma
-

-bisimilar, and
\mathfrak{M}_{1},w_{1}\equiv_{\mathsf{ML}(\sigma)}^{n}\mathfrak{M}_{2},w_{2}
if
\mathfrak{M}_{1},w_{1}
and
\mathfrak{M}_{2},w_{2}
satisfy the same
\mathsf{ML}(\sigma)
-formulas of modal depth

Lemma 20
.

For any
n\geq 0
, we have
\mathfrak{M}_{1},w_{1}\sim_{\mathsf{ML}(\sigma)}^{n}\mathfrak{M}_{2},w_{2}
iff
\mathfrak{M}_{1},w_{1}\equiv_{\mathsf{ML}(\sigma)}^{n}\mathfrak{M}_{2},w_{2}
.

Now a non-trivial proof uses
n
-bisimilarity to bound the depth of an equivalent modal formula in terms of the depth of the
\mathsf{FO}
-formula
[
84
]
. We remind the reader that the
quantifier rank
of an
\mathsf{FO}
-formula is the maximum nesting depth of quantifiers in it.

Theorem 21

(
[
84
]
)
.

Any
\mathsf{FO}
-formula
\varphi(x)
of quantifier rank

that is invariant under bisimulations is equivalent to an
\mathsf{ML}
-formula of modal depth at most
2^{q}-1
.

As one can compute a finite list of all
\mathsf{ML}
-formulas (up to logical equivalence) in a
finite signature and of bounded modal depth, we obtain:

Theorem 22
.

\mathsf{F}/\mathsf{ML}
-definability is decidable, for any decidable
\mathsf{FO}
-fragment
\mathsf{F}
containing
\mathsf{ML}
.

Note that this global approach, which applies to all decidable fragments of
\mathsf{FO}
containing
\mathsf{ML}
, gives no elementary upper complexity bound since the number of modal formulas of a fixed modal depth is non-elementary. Moreover, for full
\mathsf{FO}

-formula that is equivalent to a given
\mathsf{FO}
-formula

-definability and even construct small defining formulas.

Good Model Method

Let
L\supseteq L^{\prime}
be a pair of first-order or modal languages. A class
\mathcal{G}
of pointed models is called
good for
L/L^{\prime}
-definability
if, for any
L
-formula
\varphi
, there is a
simplifying
L^{\prime}
-formula
\varphi^{s}
such that

(
L^{\prime}
-transfer)

for every pointed model
\mathfrak{M},w
using symbols in a signature
\sigma
, there exists a pointed model
\mathfrak{M}^{s},w^{s}
in
\mathcal{G}
such that
\mathfrak{M},w\equiv_{L^{\prime}(\sigma)}\mathfrak{M}^{s},w^{s}
;

(
L/L^{\prime}
-indistinguishability)

for every pointed model
\mathfrak{M},w
in
\mathcal{G}
, we have
\mathfrak{M},w\models\varphi
iff
\mathfrak{M},w\models\varphi^{s}
.

The following easily proved lemma shows that the good model method provides explicit
L^{\prime}
-definitions whenever they exist and reduces
L/L^{\prime}
-definability to
L
-validity.

Lemma 23
.

If
\mathcal{G}
is good for
L/L^{\prime}
-definability,
then an
L
-formula
\varphi
is
L^{\prime}
-definable iff
\models\varphi\leftrightarrow\varphi^{s}
.

We first illustrate the method for
\mathsf{GML}/\mathsf{ML}
-definability, which asks whether counting modalities
\Diamond^{\geq k}
in a given
\mathsf{GML}
-formula are really needed or can be replaced by standard modalities. The answer is surprisingly simple. For any Kripke model
\mathfrak{M}=(W,R,\mathfrak{v})
, let
\mathfrak{M}^{\omega}=(W^{\omega},R^{\omega},\mathfrak{v}^{\omega})
be the model obtained from
\mathfrak{M}
by replacing every world
w

\omega

(w,i)
,

W^{\omega}=\bigcup_{w\in W}\bar{w},\quad R^{\omega}=\bigcup_{(w,v)\in R}\bar{w}\times\bar{v},\quad\mathfrak{v}^{\omega}(p)=\displaystyle\bigcup_{w\in\mathfrak{v}^{\omega}(p)}\bar{w},\quad\text{where }\bar{w}=\{(w,i)\mid 0\leq i<\omega\}.

It is not difficult to show that the class of all pointed Kripke models of the form
\mathfrak{M}^{\omega},(w,0)
is good for
\mathsf{GML}/\mathsf{ML}
-definability. For (
\mathsf{ML}
-transfer),
observe that
\mathfrak{M},w
and
\mathfrak{M}^{\omega},(w,0)

For (
\mathsf{GML}/\mathsf{ML}
-indistinguishability), we obtain from any
\mathsf{GML}
-formula
\varphi

by replacing any

of

\mathfrak{M}^{\omega},(w,0)\models\varphi
iff
\mathfrak{M}^{\omega},(w,0)\models\varphi^{f}
since any world has infinitely many copies. Now, to decide

\mathsf{GML}
-equivalence
\varphi\leftrightarrow\varphi^{f}
is true in all models, which can be done in

[

]

Theorem 24

(
[
68
]
)

\mathsf{GML}/\mathsf{ML}
-definability is

The good model method works well for various pairs

-complete

4.2
Definability and Separation for Logics with Second-Order Quantifiers and Fixed-Point Operators

Next, we consider two prominent logics,
\mathsf{MSO}
and
\mu\mathsf{ML}
, that add expressive power to
\mathsf{FO}
and
\mathsf{ML}
in the form of quantifiers over sets and fixed-point operators, respectively, and are used in formal verification and many other areas of computer science.
Logics with second-order quantifiers or fixed points are typically not compact because of which some of the model-theoretic methods discussed
in the previous section do not work, at least when applied to standard relational structures. In this case, automata-theoretic approaches, possibly based on finitary approximations of bisimulations, are often more appropriate.

In this section, we mainly focus on two questions:

•

Can the analysis of definability and separation for
\mathsf{FO}/\mathsf{ML}
be lifted to
\mathsf{MSO}/\mu\mathsf{ML}
?

•

How hard is it to decide definability and separation for formulas with fixed-point operators by formulas without those operators?

In many cases, the latter question is equivalent to asking whether the use of recursion in a given formula is spurious and can be eliminated. We begin by reminding the reader of the basic
\mu
-calculus.

Given a set
W
, a function
f\colon 2^{W}\rightarrow 2^{W}
is called
monotone
if
X\subseteq Y
implies
f(X)\subseteq f(Y)
. The
least fixed point
of
f
is the smallest (w.r.t.
\subseteq
) set
X\subseteq W
such that
f(X)=X
. If
f
is monotone, the least fixed point of
f
always exists and can be computed by transfinite induction as follows:

f^{0}(X)=X,\quad f^{\alpha+1}(X)=f(f^{\alpha}(X)),\text{ for any ordinal $\alpha$,}\quad f^{\alpha}(X)=\bigcup_{\beta<\alpha}f^{\beta}(X),\text{ for a limit ordinal $\alpha$.}

The least fixed point of
f
is then given by
f^{\alpha}(\emptyset)
, for the least ordinal
\alpha
with
f^{\alpha}(\emptyset)=f^{\alpha+1}(\emptyset)
; this
\alpha
is called the
closure ordinal
of
f
.

The
modal
\mu
-calculus

\mu\mathsf{ML}
is the extension of basic modal logic
\mathsf{ML}
with least fixed-point operators. More precisely,
\mu\mathsf{ML}
-formulas
are defined by the grammar

\varphi\ \ :=\ \ \top\ \mid\ p\ \mid\ \varphi\wedge\varphi^{\prime}\ \mid\ \neg\varphi\ \mid\ \Diamond\varphi\ \mid\ P\ \mid\ \mu P.\varphi,

where
p
ranges over propositional variables and
P
over unary predicate symbols (second-order variables). For formulas of the form
\mu P.\varphi
, it is required that every free occurrence of
P
in
\varphi
is

(i.e., in the scope of an even number of
\neg
). A
\mu\mathsf{ML}
-sentence
is any
\mu\mathsf{ML}
-formula
\varphi
whose every second-order variable

is in the scope of some
\mu P
.
Given a
\mu\mathsf{ML}
-formula
\varphi

mapping second-order variables

is defined inductively by extending the respective definition for

with two items:

•

\mathfrak{M},u\models_{\mathfrak{V}}P
iff

•

\mathfrak{M},u\models_{\mathfrak{V}}\mu P.\varphi
iff

is in the least fixed point of the function
f_{\varphi}\colon X\mapsto\{v\in W\mid\mathfrak{M},v\models_{\mathfrak{V}[P\mapsto X]}\varphi\}
, where

maps

to

on all other second-order variables.

(Note that

is monotone because

[

]
. Here we only show some standard examples of what can be said in

Example 26
.

Using the definitions of truth and least fixed point, one can readily see that

5
Definability and Separation in Formal Languages

In this section, we consider the following decision problems
for classes
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\subsetneqq\mathcal{C}
of formal languages:

\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
-definability
:

given a language
{\boldsymbol{L}}\in\mathcal{C}
, decide whether
{\boldsymbol{L}}\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
.

\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
-separation
:

given languages
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{1},{\boldsymbol{L}}_{2}\in\mathcal{C}
, decide whether there is a language
{\boldsymbol{L}}\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
—called a
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
-
separator
for
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{1}
and
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{2}
—such that
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{1}\subseteq{\boldsymbol{L}}
and
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{2}\cap{\boldsymbol{L}}=\emptyset
.

A significant amount of related work has been focusing on defining and separating regular languages.
The study of regular languages brings together combinatorial methods on words with automata-theoretic, algebraic and logical techniques. These were
originally developed for languages consisting of finite words, but have been generalised to languages over infinite words, trees, and graphs.

Here, we discuss in detail a particular instance of these problems: the decidability of defining and separating regular languages consisting of finite words by star-free (or, equivalently,
\mathsf{FO}
-definable) languages.
We also discuss possible bounds on the size of a definition/separator when it exists.
Some topics in the wider context
(such as
languages consisting of infinite words and various kinds of labelled trees;
using some
\mathsf{FO}
-fragments as definitions/separators;
and defining/separating non-regular languages with regular ones)
are only briefly sketched.

We begin by recalling the basic notions from the theory of formal languages; see, e.g.,
[
55
,
97
]
.
An
alphabet
is any finite set
A
.
Given an alphabet
A
, we denote by
A^{+}
the set of all nonempty finite words over
A
. A subset

{\boldsymbol{L}}\subseteq A^{+}
is called a
language

over

A
.
(To simplify presentation, we only consider languages without the empty word, but similar results hold if the empty word is also allowed.)
The class of
regular languages
is the smallest class that
contains
A^{+}
, all singletons
\{a\}
, for
a\in A
,
and is closed under concatenation, finite union, and the +-version of the Kleene star (iteration).
The class of
star-free languages
is the smallest class that
contains
A^{+}
, all singletons
\{a\}
, for
a\in A
,
and is closed under concatenation, finite union, and complementation.
(As the class of regular languages is closed under complementation, star-free languages
are regular.)

Automata-theoretic approach to regular languages.

It is well known that a language is regular iff
it can be specified by a deterministic finite automaton (DFA) or, equivalently, by a nondeterministic finite automaton (NFA).
Given a DFA
\mathfrak{A}
, every word
w\in A^{+}
determines a state-to-state
transition function

\delta_{w}
in
\mathfrak{A}
: for any state
q
in the DFA,
\delta_{w}(q)
is the state
\mathfrak{A}
reaches after reading
w
when started at
q
.
We say that
\delta_{w}

has a non-trivial cycle
if there exist a state
q
and
n
,
1<n<\omega
, such that

\delta_{w}(q)\neq q
and
\delta_{w^{n}}(q)=q
.
A DFA is called
counter-free
if no
\delta_{w}
has a non-trivial cycle, for any
w\in A^{+}
.

Algebraic approach to regular languages.

A
semigroup
is a structure
{\boldsymbol{S}}=(S,\mathop{\cdot})
, where
\mathop{\cdot}
is an associative binary operation on
S
.
For example, the following are semigroups:
A^{+}
with concatenation (also denoted by
A^{+}
),
and the set of transition functions in a DFA
\mathfrak{A}
with composition, called the
transition semigroup of

\mathfrak{A}
.
Given
s\in S
and
n
with
0<n<\omega
, we write
s^{n}
for
\underbrace{s\mathop{\cdot}\dots\mathop{\cdot}s}_{n}
.
We call
s

idempotent
if
s^{2}=s
.
It is easy to see that, for every finite semigroup
{\boldsymbol{S}}
, there is
i_{{\boldsymbol{S}}}
,
0<i_{{\boldsymbol{S}}}\leq 2^{|S|}
,
such that

5.2
Separation

As the class

1.

Can two languages given by a DFA be separated by a language specified by a counter-free DFA?

\mathsf{FO}(<)

Definability and separability of regular languages by ‘simpler’ languages.

Several natural hierarchies of star-free languages have been introduced within each of the automata-theoretic, algebraic and logical approaches.
There has been a lot of work on the connections among these and, using these connections, on deciding the corresponding definability and separation problems; see, e.g.,
[

[

Definability and separability of non-regular languages.

The definability and separability problems for
languages specified by devices more powerful than finite automata
(such as automata with counters, Parikh automata, vector addition systems with states, etc.)
have been mostly studied for the case when the
defining/separating language is regular. Even these questions attracted much less attention than defining/separating regular
languages, possibly because of the lack of algebraic methods and the scarcity of positive results.
A famous early negative result is that separability of context-free languages by a regular one is undecidable
[
103
]
.
As most studied non-regular language-classes are not
closed under complementation, definability for these cannot be reduced to separation. In fact, there are cases when the
separation problem is decidable, while the definability problem is undecidable; see, e.g.,
[
35
]
for examples and a wider context.

First, we briefly remind the reader of the relevant definitions.

\mathsf{LTL}

The

7
Research Directions and Open Problems

In this chapter, we have provided an overview of recent research into variations and generalisations of Craig interpolation, the common feature of which is that the existence of an interpolant does not reduce to entailment, either because the logic in question does not enjoy the CIP, or because the use of logical symbols in the interpolant is restricted. Numerous research questions remain to be explored. We conclude this chapter by discussing a few of them:

The role of proof theory.

The methods presented in this chapter have been mainly model-theoretic or automata-based. In fact, proof theory has not yet been applied to decide interpolant existence or construct interpolants for logics without the CIP or for separation.
Developing proof-theoretic methods would be of great interest, in particular since very little is known about the actual construction of interpolants and separators, and proof-theoretic methods are a promising tool to address this problem as demonstrated by their wide applications for logics with the CIP, both in theory and practice.

Non-classical logics.

The focus of this chapter has been on modal and first-order logics, and their extensions with second-order quantifiers and fixed points. We have not discussed important families of non-classical logics such as superintuitionistic, many-valued, substructurual, or relevant logics. To the best of our knowledge the variations of Craig interpolation investigated in this chapter have not yet been considered for these logics, despite the fact that many non-classical logics do not enjoy the CIP and that it appears natural to compare the expressive power of different non-classical logics by studying separation.

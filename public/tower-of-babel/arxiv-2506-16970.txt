Large Deviation Principles for Abelian Monoids
Daniel Keliher, Sun Woo Park

Abstract
. Following work of Mehrdad and Zhu [ MZ16 ] and of Liu [ Liu04a , Liu04b ] , we prove a large deviation principle for a broad class of integer-valued additive functions defined over abelian monoids. As a corollary, we obtain a large deviation principle for a generalized form of the Erdős-Kac theorem due to Liu.



1.
Introduction

The celebrated Erdős-Kac Theorem
[
EK40
]
says that if
\omega(n)
is the number of distinct prime factors of a positive integer
n
, then

(1)

\frac{\omega(n)-\log\log n}{\sqrt{\log\log n}}

is normally distributed with mean
0
and standard deviation 1 (cf. Theorem
1.2
).

Much work has been done on the rate at which (
1
) converges to
N(0,1)
, see
[
RT58
]
for one such example. Likewise, one can study the tails of the distribution as the upper bound for
n
grows. Indeed, in
[
MZ16
]
, Mehrdad and Zhu prove a large deviation principle for a large class of “strongly additive” functions
g:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
, including when
g
is the number-of-distinct-prime-divisors function,
\omega
, thus giving a large deviation principle for the distributions of (
1
) over
n\leq X
as
X\to\infty
.

In a different direction, Liu proves a generalization of the Erdős-Kac theorem for classes of abelian monoids
\mathcal{M}
generated by a set
\mathcal{P}

[
Liu04a
]
. This general framework includes, for example, the cases where
\mathcal{P}
is the set of rational primes, the set of irreducible monic polynomials over a finite field, the set of effective 0-cycles of a nice variety over a finite field, and the case where
\mathcal{P}
is a Beurling system of generalized primes. See
[
Beu37
]
or
[
DZ16
]
for background on the latter.

We will work in this same general setting, which we now describe. Let
\mathcal{P}
be a set together with a “norm” map
{\mathbf{N}:\mathcal{P}\to\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}.}
Let
\mathcal{M}
be the multiplicative monoid generated by the elements of
\mathcal{P}
. That is, every element
m\in\mathcal{M}
can be expressed as
m=\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}}p^{e_{p}}
where almost all of the
e_{p}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
are zero. Extend
\mathbf{N}
to a monoid morphism
\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{N}
by taking, for any
m\in\mathcal{M}
,
{\mathbf{N}(m)=\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\mathbf{N}(p)^{e_{P}}.}

Throughout, we make the following two assumptions on the growth rate of
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{P}
with respect to
\mathbf{N}
.

Condition 1.1
.

For
\mathcal{P},\mathcal{M},\mathbf{N}
as above, assume

(1)

{\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mathcal{M}\\
\mathbf{N}(m)\leq X\end{subarray}}1=aX+O(X^{b})}
for some
a>0
and
b\in[0,1)
, and

(2)

{\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\mathcal{P}\\
\mathbf{N}(p)\leq X\end{subarray}}1=O\left(\frac{X}{\log X}\right)}
.

In this setting, Liu proves the following generalization of the Erdős-Kac Theorem.

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem 1,
[
Liu04a
]
)
.

If
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{P},\mathbf{N}
are as above and satisfy Condition
1.1
, then

(2)

\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{\#\left\{m:\mathbf{N}(m)\leq X,\left|\frac{\omega(m)-\log\log\mathbf{N}(m)}{\sqrt{\log\log\mathbf{N}(m)}}\right|\leq\gamma\right\}}{\#\{m:\mathbf{N}(m)\leq X\}}=\int_{-\infty}^{\gamma}e^{-t^{2}/2}dt.

Our goal is to prove a generalization of the large deviation principle in
[
MZ16
]
to the setting of abelian monoids considered by Liu. In particular, the large deviation principle in our main theorem, Theorem
1.5
below, applies to the distributions (
2
).

Our large deviation result will apply to a class of “strongly additive” functions.

Condition 1.3
.

Let
g:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{N}
be a strongly additive function, i.e.
g
satisfies the following two conditions:

\displaystyle g(p^{k})

\displaystyle=g(p)\text{ for all prime elements }p\in\mathcal{P},

\displaystyle g(mn)

\displaystyle=g(m)+g(n)\text{ for all }m,n\in\mathcal{M}\text{ such that }(m,n)=1.

We suppose further that there exists a probability measure
\rho
on
\mathbb{R}
satisfying the following two conditions.

(1)

For any
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, we have
\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{\theta y}\rho(dy)<\infty
.

(2)

Let
A\subset\mathbb{R}
be any Borel measurable set. Define probability measures
\rho_{X}
given by

(3)

\rho_{X}(A):=\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g(p)\in A\\
\mathbf{N}(p)\leq X\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\mathbf{N}(p)}}{\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{N}(p)\leq X}\frac{1}{\mathbf{N}(p)}}.

Then for any
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, we have
\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{\theta y}\rho_{X}(dy)\to\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{\theta y}\rho(dy)
.

The application we have in mind is when
g
is the number-of-distinct-prime-divisors function,
\omega
. On
\mathcal{M}
, this means for
m=\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}}p^{e_{p}}
, let
\omega(m)
be the number of
p\in\mathcal{P}
for which
e_{p}\geq 1
.

Definition 1.4
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by
V(n)
a uniformly chosen monoid element from the set
\{m\in\mathcal{M}:\mathbf{N}(m)\leq n\}
. For each
p\in\mathcal{P}
, let
Z
be a random variable such that
Z_{p}=1
if
V(n)
is divisible by
p
, and
Z_{p}=0
otherwise.

We are now ready to state the main theorem.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a multiplicative abelian monoid generated by a set
\mathcal{P}
and endowed with a norm function
\mathbf{N}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{N}
, all satisfying Condition
1.1
. Let
g:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{N}
be an arithmetic function satisfying Condition
1.3
. Let
W
be a random variable over
\mathbb{N}
defined as

(4)

W(n):=g(V(n)),

where
V(n)
is a uniformly chosen monoidal element from the set
\{m\in\mathcal{M}:\mathbf{N}(m)\leq n\}
, see Definition
1.4
.
Then for any Borel measurable set
A\subset\mathbb{R}
, the probability
\mathbb{P}\left[\frac{W(X)}{\log\log X}\in A\right]
satisfies a large deviation principle with speed
\log\log X
and rate function

I(x):=\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{\theta x-\int_{\mathbb{R}}(e^{\theta y}-1)\rho(dy)\right\}.

More explicitly, we have for any Borel measurable set
A\subset\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle-\inf_{x\in A^{o}}I(x)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{X\to\infty}\frac{1}{\log\log X}\log\mathbb{P}\left[\frac{W(X)}{\log\log X}\in A\right]

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{X\to\infty}\frac{1}{\log\log X}\log\mathbb{P}\left[\frac{W(X)}{\log\log X}\in A\right]\leq-\inf_{x\in\overline{A}}I(x),

where
A^{o}
is the interior of
A
, and
\overline{A}
is the closure of
A
.

The strategy of the proof will closely follow that employed in
[
MZ16
]
, but with suitable modifications that accommodate the more general setting.

2.
Applications

Note that taking
\mathcal{P}
to be the set of rational primes and
\mathbf{N}(p)=p
, Theorem
1.5
recovers exactly Theorem 2 of Mehrdad and Zhu
[
MZ16
]
. Furthermore, taking
\mathcal{P}
to be the set of monic, irreducible polynomials
f
over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
\mathbf{N}(f)=q^{\deg f}
recovers the large deviation result of Feng, Wang, and Yang
[
FWY20
]
.

Two additional settings are natural to consider for applying Theorem
1.5
in conjunction with Theorem
1.2
:

•

Number Fields:
Let
\mathcal{O}_{K}
be the ring of integers of a number field,
K
. Let
\mathcal{P}
be the set of prime ideals of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, so
\mathcal{M}
is the set of ideals of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. As usual, for
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}
, let
\mathbf{N}(\mathfrak{p})=|\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{p}|
. Condition
1.3
is satisfied by work of Weber
[
Web96
]
, see also
[
Lan94
, Chapter XIII]
(for I), and by the prime ideal theorem (for II).

•

Nice Varieties:

Let
V
be any
d
-dimensional smooth, projective irreducible, geometrically integral variety defined over a finite field,
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let
\mathcal{P}
be the set of closed points of
V/\mathbb{F}_{q}
, so
\mathcal{M}
is the monoid of effective
0
-cycles. For
P\in\mathcal{P}
, take
\mathbf{N}(P)=q^{d\cdot\deg(P)}
, where
\deg(P)
is the size of the Galois orbit of
P
in
V(\mathbb{\bar{F}}_{q})
. Condition
1.3
holds for this choice of
\mathcal{P},\mathcal{M},\mathbf{N}
by
[
LW54
]
; see
[
Ros02
]
for an exposition of the case of curves over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

In the examples above, taking
g
to be the appropriate number-of-distinct-prime-divisors function,
\omega
, on
\mathcal{M}
, Theorem
1.5
gives a large deviation principle with an explicit rate function for the corresponding version of the Erdős-Kac Theorem given by Theorem
1.2
. We can use
[
MZ16
, Corollary 3]
to explicitly compute the rate function
I(x)
as

(5)

I(x):=\begin{cases}x\log x-x+1&\text{ if }x\geq 0,\\
+\infty&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

3.
Preliminary Results

In this section we gather some results and prove some lemmas in preparation for the proof of Theorem
1.5
.

The following version of Mertens’ second theorem will be a useful estimate.

Lemma 3.1

(Lemma 2,
[
Liu04b
]
)
.

If
\mathcal{P},\mathcal{M},\mathbf{N}
satisfy Condition
1.1
, then

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\mathcal{P}\\
\mathbf{N}(p)<X\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\mathbf{N}(p)}=\log\log X+O\left(\frac{1}{\log X}\right).

Notice Lemma
3.1
implies that the number-of-distinct-prime-divisors function
\omega
on
\mathcal{M}
satisfies Condition
1.3
.

We next state a version of Gärtner-Ellis Theorem as given in
[
MZ16
]
; it is this result from which we will obtain the desired large deviation principle.

Theorem 3.2

(Page 152 of
[
MZ16
]
, and Theorem 2.3.6 of
[
DZ98
]
)
.

Let
Z_{n}
be a sequence of random variables on
\mathbb{R}
. Let
a_{n}
be a sequence of positive numbers such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}a_{n}=\infty
. Suppose that for any
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, the limit

\Lambda(\theta):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{a_{n}}\log\mathbb{E}[\exp\left(\theta a_{n}Z_{n}\right)]

exists and is differentiable for every
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
. Then for any Borel measurable set
A\subset\mathbb{R}
, the probability
\mathbb{P}[Z_{n}\in A]
satisfies a large deviation principle with speed
a_{n}
and rate function

I(x):=\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{\theta x-\Lambda(\theta)\right\}.

More concretely, we have for any Borel measurable set
A\subset\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle-\inf_{x\in A^{o}}I(x)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{a_{n}}\log\mathbb{P}[Z_{n}\in A]\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{a_{n}}\log\mathbb{P}[Z_{n}\in A]\leq-\inf_{x\in\overline{A}}I(x)

For every element
p\in\mathcal{P}
, define independent random variables
Y_{p}
for which

Y_{p}=\begin{cases}1\quad&\text{with probability }\frac{1}{\mathbf{N}(p)}\\
0\quad&\text{with probability }1-\frac{1}{\mathbf{N}(p)}\end{cases}.

Lemma 3.3
.

Given
p\in\mathcal{P}
, we recall the random variable
Z_{p}
supported over the set
\{m\in\mathcal{M}:\mathbf{N}(m)\leq X\}
from Definition
1.4
.
For distinct
p_{1},...,p_{k}\in\mathcal{P}
we have

(6)

\mathbb{E}[Z_{p_{1}}Z_{p_{2}}...Z_{p_{k}}]=\frac{\#\{m\in\mathcal{M}:\mathbf{N}(m)\leq\lfloor\frac{X}{\mathbf{N}(p_{1})\mathbf{N}(p_{2})\cdots\mathbf{N}(p_{k})}\rfloor\}}{\#\{m\in\mathcal{M}:\mathbf{N}(m)\leq X\}},

and

(7)

\mathbb{E}[Y_{p_{1}}Y_{p_{2}}...Y_{p_{k}}]=\frac{1}{\mathbf{N}(p_{1})\mathbf{N}(p_{2})\cdots\mathbf{N}(p_{k})}.

Proof.

Given
m\in\mathcal{M}
, we have
Z_{p_{1}}(m)Z_{p_{2}}(m)\cdots Z_{p_{k}}(m)=1
if and only if there exists an element
a\in\mathcal{M}
such that
m=p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{k}a
. For such an
m
, we have
\mathbf{N}(m)\leq X
if and only if
\mathbf{N}(a)\leq\lfloor\frac{X}{\mathbf{N}(p_{1})\mathbf{N}(p_{2})\cdots\mathbf{N}(p_{k})}\rfloor
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Z_{p_{1}}Z_{p_{2}}\cdots Z_{p_{k}}]

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mathcal{M}\\
\mathbf{N}(m)\leq X\end{subarray}}\mathbbm{1}_{p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{k}\mid m}}{\#\{m\in\mathcal{M}:\mathbf{N}(m)\leq X\}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\#\{m\in\mathcal{M}:m=p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{k}a,\mathbf{N}(m)\leq X\}}{\#\{m\in\mathcal{M}:\mathbf{N}(m)\leq X\}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\#\{m\in\mathcal{M}:\mathbf{N}(m)\leq\lfloor\frac{X}{\mathbf{N}(p_{1})\mathbf{N}(p_{2})\cdots\mathbf{N}(p_{k})}\rfloor\}}{\#\{m\in\mathcal{M}:\mathbf{N}(m)\leq X\}}.

The second equation follows immediately from the definition of
Y_{p}
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose Condition
1.1
.

(1)

For distinct primes
p_{1},\cdots,p_{k}\in\mathcal{P}
, we have

\mathbb{E}[Z_{p_{1}}Z_{p_{2}}...Z_{p_{k}}]=\frac{1}{aX}\lfloor\frac{aX}{\mathbf{N}(p_{1})\mathbf{N}(p_{2})\cdots\mathbf{N}(p_{k})}\rfloor+O\left(\frac{X^{b-1}}{\mathbf{N}(p_{1})^{b}\mathbf{N}(p_{2})^{b}\cdots\mathbf{N}(p_{k})^{b}}\right).

(2)

For distinct primes
p_{1},\cdots,p_{k}\in\mathcal{P}
, we have

\mathbb{E}[Z_{p_{1}}Z_{p_{2}}...Z_{p_{k}}]\leq\mathbb{E}[Y_{p_{1}}Y_{p_{2}}...Y_{p_{k}}]+O\left(\frac{X^{b-1}}{\mathbf{N}(p_{1})^{b}\mathbf{N}(p_{2})^{b}\cdots\mathbf{N}(p_{k})^{b}}\right).

(3)

For sufficiently large
X
there exists an explicit positive constant
M
, independent of the choice of distinct primes
p_{1},\cdots,p_{k}\in\mathcal{P}
, such that

\mathbb{E}[Z_{p_{1}}Z_{p_{2}}...Z_{p_{k}}]\leq M\cdot\mathbb{E}[Y_{p_{1}}Y_{p_{2}}...Y_{p_{k}}].

(4)

For sufficiently large
X
, there exists an explicit constant
M
such that for any non-negative sequence of real numbers
\{\theta_{p}\}_{p\in\mathcal{P}}
we have

\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\sum_{\mathbf{N}(p)\leq X}\theta_{p}Z_{p}\right)\right]\leq M\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\sum_{\mathbf{N}(p)\leq X}\theta_{p}Y_{p}\right)\right].

The implied constants of all the error terms are independent of the choice of
p_{1},\cdots,p_{k}
.

Proof.

By Condition
1.1
and Lemma
3.3
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Z_{p_{1}}Z_{p_{2}}\cdots Z_{p_{k}}]

\displaystyle=\frac{\lfloor\frac{aX}{\mathbf{N}(p_{1})\mathbf{N}(p_{2})\cdots\mathbf{N}(p_{k})}\rfloor+O\left(\frac{X^{b}}{\mathbf{N}(p_{1})^{b}\mathbf{N}(p_{2})^{b}\cdots\mathbf{N}(p_{k})^{b}}\right)}{aX+O(X^{b})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{aX}\left\lfloor\frac{aX}{\mathbf{N}(p_{1})\mathbf{N}(p_{2})\cdots\mathbf{N}(p_{k})}\right\rfloor+O\left(\frac{X^{b-1}}{\mathbf{N}(p_{1})^{b}\mathbf{N}(p_{2})^{b}\cdots\mathbf{N}(p_{k})^{b}}\right).

The second statement follows from using the inequality

\frac{1}{aX}\left\lfloor\frac{aX}{\mathbf{N}(p_{1})\mathbf{N}(p_{2})\cdots\mathbf{N}(p_{k})}\right\rfloor\leq\frac{1}{\mathbf{N}(p_{1})\mathbf{N}(p_{2})\cdots\mathbf{N}(p_{k})}=\mathbb{E}[Y_{p_{1}}Y_{p_{2}}\cdots Y_{p_{k}}].

To prove the third statement, we divide into two cases. Suppose
p_{1},p_{2},\cdots,p_{k}
are distinct primes such that
\mathbf{N}(p_{1})\mathbf{N}(p_{2})\cdots\mathbf{N}(p_{k})>X
. Then because all the random variables
Z_{p_{1}},Z_{p_{2}},\cdots,Z_{p_{k}}
are supported over the probability space
\{m\in\mathcal{M}:\mathbf{N}(m)\leq X\}
, we have

\mathbb{E}[Z_{p_{1}}Z_{p_{2}}\cdots Z_{p_{k}}]=0<\frac{1}{\mathbf{N}(p_{1})\mathbf{N}(p_{2})\cdots\mathbf{N}(p_{k})}=\mathbb{E}[Y_{p_{1}}Y_{p_{2}}\cdots Y_{p_{k}}].

Now suppose
p_{1},p_{2},\cdots,p_{k}
are distinct primes such that
\mathbf{N}(p_{1})\mathbf{N}(p_{2})\cdots\mathbf{N}(p_{k})\leq X
. Then we have

\displaystyle\frac{X^{b-1}}{\mathbf{N}(p_{1})^{b}\mathbf{N}(p_{2})^{b}\cdots\mathbf{N}(p_{k})^{b}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{X^{1-b}\mathbf{N}(p_{1})^{b}\mathbf{N}(p_{2})^{b}\cdots\mathbf{N}(p_{k})^{b}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\mathbf{N}(p_{1})\mathbf{N}(p_{2})\cdots\mathbf{N}(p_{k})}=\mathbb{E}[Y_{p_{1}}Y_{p_{2}}\cdots Y_{p_{k}}].

Hence, by the second statement of our lemma, there exists an absolute constant
M_{1}>0
such that

\mathbb{E}[Z_{p_{1}}Z_{p_{2}}\cdots Z_{p_{k}}]\leq M_{1}\mathbb{E}[Y_{p_{1}}Y_{p_{2}}\cdots Y_{p_{k}}].

We take
M:=\max(1,M_{1})
to obtain the third statement of the lemma.

The fourth statement follows by using Taylor expansion. We crucially use the fact that for any non-negative integers
r_{1},r_{2}\cdots,r_{k}
we have

\mathbb{E}[Z_{p_{1}}^{r_{1}}Z_{p_{2}}^{r_{2}}\cdots Z_{p_{k}}^{r_{k}}]=\mathbb{E}[Z_{p_{1}}Z_{p_{2}}\cdots Z_{p_{k}}]\leq M\cdot\mathbb{E}[Y_{p_{1}}Y_{p_{2}}\cdots Y_{p_{k}}]=M\cdot\mathbb{E}[Y_{p_{1}}^{r_{1}}Y_{p_{2}}^{r_{2}}\cdots Y_{p_{k}}^{r_{k}}].

∎

For any

Now, define
k_{X}:=X^{\frac{1}{(\log\log X)^{2}}}
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
A(X,C):=\{p\in\mathcal{P}:k_{X}\leq\mathbf{N}(p)\leq X,|g(p)|\leq C\}
. For any
\epsilon>0
, we have

(13)

\limsup_{X\to\infty}\frac{1}{\log\log X}\log\mathbb{P}\left[\left|\sum_{p\in A(X,C)}g(p)Z_{p}\right|\geq\epsilon\log\log X\right]=-\infty.

Proof.

By Condition
1.1
and the third statement of Lemma
3.4
, for any
\theta>0
and sufficiently large
X
there exists an explicit constant
M>0
such that

\displaystyle\log\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\theta\left|\sum_{p\in A(X,C)}g(p)Z_{p}\right|\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\log\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\theta C\sum_{p\in A(X,C)}Y_{p}\right)\right]+\log M

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\in A(X,C)}\log\left((\exp\left(\theta C\right)-1)\cdot\frac{1}{\mathbf{N}(p)}+1\right)+\log M

\displaystyle\leq(\exp(\theta C)-1)\cdot\sum_{k_{X}\leq\mathbf{N}(p)\leq X}\frac{1}{\mathbf{N}(p)}+\log M.

By Lemma
3.1
, we have

\sum_{k_{X}\leq\mathbf{N}(p)\leq X}\frac{1}{\mathbf{N}(p)}=2\log\log\log X+O\left(\frac{(\log\log X)^{2}}{\log X}\right).

We then use exponential Chebyshev’s inequality to conclude

\displaystyle\hskip 14.0pt\limsup_{X\to\infty}\frac{1}{\log\log X}\log\mathbb{P}\left[\left|\sum_{p\in A(X,C)}g(p)Z_{p}\right|\geq\epsilon\log\log X\right]

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{X\to\infty}\frac{1}{\log\log X}\log\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\theta\left|\sum_{p\in A(X,C)}g(p)Z_{p}\right|\right)\right]-\theta\epsilon

\displaystyle\leq-\theta\epsilon.

Let
\theta
grow arbitrarily large to conclude the lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Denote by
B(X,C):=\{p\in\mathcal{P}:\mathbf{N}(p)\leq k_{X},|g(p)|\leq C\}
. Then for any
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

(14)

\lim_{X\to\infty}\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\theta\sum_{p\in B(X,C)}g(p)Z_{p}\right)\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\theta\sum_{p\in B(X,C)}g(p)Y_{p}\right)\right]\right|=0.

Proof.

Let
K
be any constant. Consider the following three expressions:

(15)

\sum_{r\leq K\log\log X}\frac{|\theta|^{r}}{r!}\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{p\in B(X,C)}g(p)Z_{p}\right)^{r}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{p\in B(X,C)}g(p)Y_{p}\right)^{r}\right]\right|,

(16)

\sum_{r>K\log\log X}\frac{|\theta|^{r}}{r!}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{p\in B(X,C)}g(p)Z_{p}\right)^{r}\right],

(17)

\sum_{r>K\log\log X}\frac{|\theta|^{r}}{r!}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{p\in B(X,C)}g(p)Y_{p}\right)^{r}\right].

Then by Taylor expansion we have

\displaystyle\hskip 14.0pt\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\theta\sum_{p\in B(X,C)}g(p)Z_{p}\right)\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\theta\sum_{p\in B(X,C)}g(p)Y_{p}\right)\right]\right|\leq\eqref{eq:lemma-1}+\eqref{eq:lemma-2}+\eqref{eq:lemma-3}.

By the second statement of Lemma
3.4
, for any tuples of non-negative integers
r_{1},\cdots,r_{k}
and sufficiently large
X
there exists an absolute constant
B>0
such that

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\left[Z_{p_{1}}^{r_{1}}Z_{p_{2}}^{r_{2}}\cdots Z_{p_{k}}^{r_{k}}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{p_{1}}^{r_{1}}Y_{p_{2}}^{r_{2}}\cdots Y_{p_{k}}^{r_{k}}\right]\right|=\left|\mathbb{E}\left[Z_{p_{1}}Z_{p_{2}}\cdots Z_{p_{k}}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{p_{1}}Y_{p_{2}}\cdots Y_{p_{k}}\right]\right|\leq BX^{b-1}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\hskip 14.0pt\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{p\in B(X,C)}g(p)Z_{p}\right)^{r}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{p\in B(X,C)}g(p)Y_{p}\right)^{r}\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\sum_{k=1}^{r}\sum_{r_{i}}\frac{r!}{r_{1}!r_{2}!\cdots r_{k}!}\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{p_{j}}g(p_{1})^{r_{1}}\cdots g(p_{k})^{r_{k}}\cdot\left(\mathbb{E}\left[Z_{p_{1}}^{r_{1}}Z_{p_{2}}^{r_{2}}\cdots Z_{p_{k}}^{r_{k}}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{p_{1}}^{r_{1}}Y_{p_{2}}^{r_{2}}\cdots Y_{p_{k}}^{r_{k}}\right]\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{r}\sum_{r_{i}}\frac{r!}{r_{1}!r_{2}!\cdots r_{k}!}\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{p_{j}}\frac{B\cdot C^{r_{1}+\cdots+r_{k}}}{X^{1-b}}\leq\frac{B}{X^{1-b}}\left(\sum_{p\in B(X,C)}C\right)^{r}\leq\frac{B\cdot(C\cdot k_{X})^{r}}{X^{1-b}},

where the
r_{i}
’s range over tuples of integers
(r_{1},\cdots,r_{k})
such that
r_{1}+\cdots+r_{k}=r
, and the
p_{j}
’s ranges over prime elements
p_{1},\cdots,p_{k}\in B(X,C)
. By Stirling’s approximation for
r!
, there exists an absolute constant
B^{\prime}>0
such that

(
15
)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{r\leq K\log\log X}\frac{|\theta|^{r}}{r!}\frac{B\cdot(C\cdot k_{X})^{r}}{X^{1-b}}

\displaystyle\leq B^{\prime}\cdot\exp\left(-(1-b)\log X\right)\cdot K\log\log X\cdot\left[\max_{r\leq K\log\log X}\left(\frac{e|\theta|Ck_{X}}{r}\right)^{r}\right]

\displaystyle\leq B^{\prime}\cdot\exp\left(-(1-b)\log X\right)\cdot K\log\log X\cdot\left(\frac{e|\theta|Ck_{X}}{K\log\log X}\right)^{K\log\log X}

\displaystyle\leq B^{\prime}K\cdot\exp\left(-(1-b)\log X+\log\log\log X+K(\log|\theta|+\log C)\log\log X+\frac{\log X}{\log\log X}\right).

Hence, there exists an explicit constant
B^{\prime\prime}>0
such that for sufficiently large
X
,

\eqref{eq:lemma-1}\leq B^{\prime\prime}e^{-\frac{1}{2}(1-b)\log X}.

Note that the third statement of Lemma
3.4
implies that for sufficiently large
X
there exists an explicit constant
M>0
such that

\eqref{eq:lemma-2}\leq M\cdot\eqref{eq:lemma-3},

so it suffices to consider (
17
) for sufficiently large
X
. We have

(
17
)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{r>K\log\log X}\frac{|\theta|^{r}}{r!}\cdot C^{r}\cdot\left(\left(\sum_{\mathbf{N}p\leq k_{X}}\frac{1}{\mathbf{N}p}\right)+\left(\sum_{\mathbf{N}p\leq k_{X}}\frac{1}{\mathbf{N}p}\right)^{2}+\cdots+\left(\sum_{\mathbf{N}p\leq k_{X}}\frac{1}{\mathbf{N}p}\right)^{r}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{r>K\log\log X}\frac{|\theta|^{r}}{r!}\cdot r\cdot C^{r}\cdot\left(\sum_{\mathbf{N}p\leq k_{X}}\frac{1}{\mathbf{N}p}\right)^{r}.

By Lemma
3.1
and Stirling’s approximation for
r!
, we have

(
17
)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{r>K\log\log X}\frac{|\theta|^{r}}{r!}\cdot r\cdot C^{r}\cdot(\log\log k_{X})^{r}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{r>K\log\log X}\exp\left(r\log|\theta|+r\log C+r+\log r-r\log r+r\log\log\log k_{X}\right).

Because
r>K\log\log X
inside the summation, we have

\log r>\log K+\log\log\log X>\log K+\log\log\log k_{X}.

Hence, there exist explicit constants
B_{*},B_{**}>0
such that for sufficiently large
K
, we have

(
17
)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{r>K\log\log X}B_{*}\exp\left(r\log|\theta|+r\log C+r+\log r-r\log K\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{r>K\log\log X}B_{*}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}r\log K\right)\leq B_{**}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\log K\log\log X\right).

Combining all the upper bounds for (
15
), (
16
), and (
17
), and choosing
K>1
, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{X\to\infty}\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\theta\sum_{p\in B(X,C)}g(p)Z_{p}\right)\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\theta\sum_{p\in B(X,C)}g(p)Y_{p}\right)\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{X\to\infty}B^{\prime\prime}\cdot e^{-\frac{1}{2}(1-b)\log X}+(M+1)\cdot B_{**}\cdot e^{-\frac{1}{2}\log K\log\log X}=0.

∎

Using Lemma

Proof

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1.5
. Take

\displaystyle\begin{split}\log\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\theta\sum_{p\in B(X,C)}g(p)Y_{p}\right)\right]&=\log\prod_{p\in B(X,C)}\mathbb{E}\left[\exp(\theta g(p)Y_{P})\right]\\

For
p

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the Cauchy problem for the 3D energy-critical inhomogeneous nonlinear Schrödinger equation(INLS) i ​ ∂ t u + Δ ​ u = ± | x | − α ​ | u | 4 − 2 ​ α ​ u i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u=\pm|x|^{-\alpha}|u|^{4-2\alpha}u with strong singularity 3 / 2 ≤ α < 2 3/2\leq\alpha<2 . The well-posedness problem is well-understood for 0 < α < 3 / 2 0<\alpha<3/2 , but the case 3 / 2 ≤ α < 2 3/2\leq\alpha<2 has remained open so far. We address the local/small data global well-posedness result for 3 / 2 ≤ α < 11 / 6 3/2\leq\alpha<11/6 by improving the inhomogeneous Strichartz estimates on the weighted space.

1.
Introduction

We consider the Cauchy problem of the energy-critical inhomogeneous nonlinear Schrödinger equation (INLS) in three dimensions

\left\{\begin{aligned} &i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u=\lambda|x|^{-\alpha}|u|^{4-2\alpha}u\\
&u(x,0)=u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

with strong singularity
3/2\leq\alpha<2
.
Here,
u(t,x)
is a complex-valued function in
I\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
for a time interval
I\in\mathbb{R}
.
The value of
\lambda
is determined as
1
or
-1
, corresponding to the
defocusing
and
focusing
model, respectively.
This type of the equation physically arises in nonlinear optics with spatially dependent interaction to describe the propagation of laser beam in the inhomogeneous medium (see e.g.
[
3
,
7
]
).
The equation (
1.1
) has a scaling symmetry
u(t,x)\mapsto\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}u(\delta^{2}t,\delta x)

for
\delta>0
and the energy norm
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
of the solution remains invariant under the scaling.
This means a
energy-critical
case.

The well-posedness problem of (
1.1
) was studied in depth with
\alpha=0
over the past several decades, see e.g.
[
4
,
13
,
12
,
21
,
33
,
34
]
based on the classical Strichartz estimates.
In recent years, many researchers have attempted to extend it to the case
\alpha>0
.
Relying on the classical Strichartz estimates, the energy-critical case of (
1.1
) was first dealt in
[
10
]
with
0<\alpha<4/3
.
For more extended range
0<\alpha<3/2
, weighted spaces and Lorentz space approaches were introduced in
[
1
,
2
,
23
]
, also see
[
9
]
.
Lately, in
[
8
]
, such results were recovered on the classical
L^{p}
setting by using the generalized Leibniz rule.
However, there is no existence result of (
1.1
) for
3/2\leq\alpha<2
up to now.
We would like to refer to
[
10
,
16
,
17
,
24
,
25
,
27
]
where the scattering results for (
1.1
) were achieved under
0<\alpha<3/2
.

In this paper, we are interested in the unsolved case
3/2\leq\alpha<2
.
A main technical issue in this case is that as the nonlinear term become more singular, a more regular solution space is required.
To treat the issue, we consider the weighted space approach because it is not only to handle efficiently the singularity on the nonlinear term, but also to expect the gain of regularity in virtue of the smoothing effect.
We will extend the validity of exponents where inhomogeneous Strichartz esimates hold in the weighted
L^{p}
space.

We first recall the weighted version of the homogeneous Strichartz estimate, focusing on the three dimensions.

1.2.
Well-posedness results of (
1.1
)

We present the local and small data global well-posedness of (
1.1
) when
3/2\leq\alpha<11/6
.
Taking advantage of Theorem
1.3
, we will deal with the nonlinear term with strong singularity.

For appropriate
(q,r)\in\mathcal{AD}_{\gamma}
and
(\tilde{q},\tilde{r})\in\mathcal{AD}_{\tilde{\gamma}}
,
one can get

\|\nabla(|x|^{-\alpha}|u|^{4-2\alpha}u)\|_{L_{t}^{\tilde{q}^{\prime}}L_{x}^{\tilde{r}^{\prime}}(|x|^{\tilde{r}^{\prime}\tilde{\gamma}})}\lesssim\|\nabla u\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{r}(|x|^{-r\gamma})}^{5-2\alpha}

(1.14)

by the Hölder inequality and the weighted Sobolev inequality.
For the exponents
(q,r)
and
(\tilde{q},\tilde{r})
, then it should be satisfied that

1=(5-2\alpha)\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{\gamma}{3}\right)+\frac{1}{\tilde{r}}-\frac{\tilde{\gamma}}{3}-\frac{4-3\alpha}{3},\quad\frac{1+\gamma}{3}<\frac{1}{r}.

But the condition
\tilde{q}\geq 2
on the class
\mathcal{AD}_{\tilde{\gamma}}
leads to the existence result with the restriction
\alpha<3/2
, see
[
23
]
.
However, the improved inhomogeneous Strichartz estimates (Theorem
1.3
) allow us to choose
(\tilde{q},\tilde{r})\in\mathcal{AC}_{\tilde{\gamma},0}=\mathcal{AC}_{\tilde{\gamma}}
with no restriction of
\tilde{q}\geq 2
.
This is the reason why we refine the inhomogeneous Strichartz estimates.

We consider convenient spaces
W=W(I\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
\dot{W}^{1}=\dot{W}^{1}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
defined as the closure of the test functions under the following norms

\|f\|_{W}:=\||x|^{-\gamma_{0}}f\|_{L_{t}^{q_{0}}(I;L_{x}^{r_{0}})}\quad\text{and}\quad\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{1}}:=\|\nabla f\|_{W}

with

\frac{1}{q_{0}}=\frac{3}{2(17-6\alpha)}+\frac{3\delta}{2},\quad\frac{1}{r_{0}}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{17-6\alpha}+\frac{\gamma_{0}}{3}-\delta

for sufficiently small
\delta>0
.
One sees
(q_{0},r_{0})\in\mathcal{AD}_{\gamma_{0}}
.
We consider
N=N(I\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
\dot{N}^{1}=\dot{N}^{1}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
as the nonlinearity spaces equipped with the norm

\|F\|_{N}:=\||x|^{\tilde{\gamma}_{0}}F\|_{L_{t}^{\tilde{q}_{0}^{\prime}}(I;L_{x}^{\tilde{r}_{0}^{\prime}})}\quad\text{and}\quad\|F\|_{\dot{N}^{1}}:=\|\nabla F\|_{N}

where
(\tilde{q}_{0},\tilde{r}_{0})
satisfies

\frac{1}{\tilde{q}_{0}}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{17-6\alpha}-\frac{3\delta(5-2\alpha)}{2},\quad\frac{1}{\tilde{r}_{0}}=\frac{1}{6}-\frac{2}{3(17-6\alpha)}+\frac{\tilde{\gamma}_{0}}{3}+\delta(5-2\alpha).

It is easy to check
(\tilde{q}_{0},\tilde{r}_{0})\in\mathcal{AC}_{\tilde{\gamma}_{0}}
for sufficiently small
\delta>0
.
These norms on the spaces
W,\dot{W}^{1},N
and
\dot{N}^{1}
are particularly chosen to be well adapted to control the nonlinearity, see Lemma
3.2
.

We also define the weighted Strichartz spaces
S_{\gamma}=S_{\gamma}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\dot{S}_{\gamma}^{1}=\dot{S}^{1}_{\gamma}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
0<\gamma<1/2
equipped with

\|f\|_{S_{\gamma}}:=\sup\|f\|_{L_{t}^{q}(I;L_{x}^{r}(|x|^{-r\gamma}))}\quad\text{and}\quad\|f\|_{\dot{S}^{1}_{\gamma}}:=\|\nabla f\|_{S_{\gamma}},

respectively. Here, the supremum is taken over
(q,r)\in\mathcal{AD}_{\gamma}
satisfying

\frac{1}{6}+\frac{\gamma}{3}+\frac{2}{3(17-6\alpha)}-\delta(5-2\alpha)<\frac{1}{r}<\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\gamma}{3}-\frac{1}{17-6\alpha}+\frac{3\delta(5-2\alpha)}{2}.

(1.15)

It is also noted that
S_{\gamma}\subseteq W
and
\dot{S}_{\gamma}^{1}\subseteq\dot{W}^{1}
in the sense that

\|f\|_{W}\lesssim\|f\|_{S_{\gamma}},\qquad\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{1}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{S}_{\gamma}^{1}}.

(1.16)

Now we present our local well-posedness result of (

(Local well-posedness)
Let

Remark 1.6
.

Recently, the authors of
[
8
]
showed that the INLS model is ill-posed in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for
3/2\leq\alpha<2
and the nonlinearity index
\beta\in 2\mathbb{Z}
. In fact, they proved that there is no
C^{1+\beta}
-flow in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
generated by the INLS, strongly relying on the classical Strichartz space. However, from Theorem

, it seems that the weighted Strichartz space is enable to construct the

-flow to obtain the well-posedness result in general situations.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we extend the inhomogeneous Strichartz estimates on the weighted spaces.
For this, we establish the time-localized estimates (Proposition

We then apply a bilinear interpolation argument to the localized estimates, leading to the global inhomogeneous Strichartz estimates, which hold in the extended range.
These estimates are employed to build up the well-posedness of the 3D energy-critical model with strong singularity in Section
3
.
Section
4
is devoted to discussing the sharpness of the theorem, which plays a crucial role in deriving the well-posedness result.

Namely, for
q(s;t,z)=2s^{2}+|z|^{2}/(t-s)
, we see that

\partial_{s}q=4s-\frac{|z|^{2}}{(t-s)^{2}},\qquad\partial_{s}^{2}q=4-\frac{2|z|^{2}}{(t-s)^{3}}.

For
t\in[4,5]
and
1\leq|z|\leq 2
, one has
\partial_{s}^{M}q(s)\in L^{\infty}([0,1])
for
M>1
.
Also,
q
has exactly one non-degenerate critical point in
[0,1]
such as

s_{\ast}=\frac{|z|^{2}}{4(t-s_{\ast})^{2}}\in[\frac{1}{100},\frac{1}{9}]

and

q^{\prime}(s_{\ast})=0,\qquad q^{\prime\prime}(s_{\ast})=4-\frac{8s_{\ast}}{t-s_{\ast}}>1.

Thus by (
4.9
) we deduce that

I(R;t,z)=\frac{J_{\ast}(t,z)e^{iR^{2}q(s_{\ast};t,z)}}{R}+O(\frac{1}{R^{2}})

(4.10)

where

J_{\ast}(t,z)=e^{\frac{i\pi}{4}}\psi(s_{\ast};t)\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{\partial_{s}^{2}q(s_{\ast};t,z)}}.

Notice here that

\displaystyle|J_{\ast}(t,z)|=\left|\,\frac{e^{\frac{i\pi}{4}}}{(t-s_{\ast})^{n/2}}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi(t-s_{\ast})}{4t-12s_{\ast}}}\,\right|=\left|\sqrt{\frac{2\pi(t-s_{\ast})^{1-n}}{4t-12s_{\ast}}}\,\right|\sim 1

(4.11)

because of
|t-s_{\ast}|\sim 2
for
s_{\ast}\in[1/100,1/9]
and
t\in[4,5]
.

One has

e^{iR^{2}q(s_{\ast};t,z)}=e^{iR^{2}q(s_{\ast};t,x)+i(\frac{|y|^{2}-2x\cdot y}{4(t-s_{\ast})})}

(4.12)

where

\left|\frac{|y|^{2}-2x\cdot y}{4(t-s_{\ast})}\right|\leq\frac{|y|^{2}+2|x||y|}{16}\leq\frac{1}{16}\left(\frac{1}{(2R)^{2}}+\frac{1}{R}(4R+\frac{1}{2R})\right)\leq\frac{19}{64}

for
2R+\frac{1}{2R}<|x|<4R-\frac{1}{2R}
,
|y|<\frac{1}{2R}
and
R>1
.
Then it follows from (
4.10
), (
4.11
) and (
4.12
) that

\displaystyle|TT^{\ast}F|

\displaystyle\gtrsim\left|\int_{|y|<\frac{1}{2R}}\frac{J_{\ast}(t,z)e^{iR^{2}\varphi(s_{\ast};t,z)}}{R}dy\right|

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{R}\left|\int_{|y|<\frac{1}{2R}}e^{i(\frac{|y|^{2}-2x\cdot y}{4(t-s_{\ast})})}dy\right|

\displaystyle\gtrsim\frac{1}{R}\left|\int_{|y|<\frac{1}{2R}}\cos\left(\frac{|y|^{2}-2x\cdot y}{4(t-s_{\ast})}\right)dy\right|

\displaystyle\gtrsim\frac{\cos(\frac{19}{64})}{R}\int_{|y|<\frac{1}{2R}}dy\sim\frac{1}{2^{n}R^{n+1}}.

Hence we obtain

\displaystyle\|TT^{\ast}F\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{r}(|x|^{-r\gamma})}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\frac{1}{2^{n}R^{n+1}}\left(\int_{4}^{5}\left(\int_{2R+\frac{1}{2R}<|x|<4R-\frac{1}{2R}}|x|^{-r\gamma}dx\right)^{\frac{q}{r}}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\frac{1}{2^{n}R^{n+1}}(2R)^{\frac{n}{r}-\gamma}

\displaystyle\gtrsim R^{-n-1+\frac{n}{r}-\gamma}

for
n-r\gamma>0
and
R\gg 1
.
As a result, (
1.4
) leads us to get

R^{-n-1+\frac{n}{r}-\gamma}\lesssim R^{-\frac{n}{\tilde{r}^{\prime}}-\tilde{\gamma}},\qquad R\gg 1

which implies

(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{\gamma}{n})-(\frac{1}{\tilde{r}}-\frac{\tilde{\gamma}}{n})\leq\frac{1}{n}.

In the similar way, it is clear to deduce the condition

(\frac{1}{\tilde{r}}-\frac{\tilde{\gamma}}{n})-(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{\gamma}{n})\leq\frac{1}{n}
by replacing
q,r,\gamma
with
\tilde{q},\tilde{r},\tilde{\gamma}
.
Therefore, we conclude that (
4.4
) is needed for (
1.4
) to hold.

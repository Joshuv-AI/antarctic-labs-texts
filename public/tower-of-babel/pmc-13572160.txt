Unusually long remission in two patients with follicular lymphoma receiving immunomodulating therapeutic cancer vaccines
Authors: Morten Orebo Holmström, Shamaila Munir Ahmad, Uffe Klausen, Mads Emil Bjørn, Anne Ortved Gang, Tarec Christoffer El-Galaly, Lars Møller Pedersen, Mads Hald Andersen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The immune checkpoints programmed death-ligand (PD-L)1 and PD-L2 are expressed in follicular lymphoma (FL), yet immune checkpoint blocking antibodies have failed in FL. We previously initiated a phase I immune modulating vaccination (IMV) trial targeting PD-L1 and PD-L2 in patients with advanced follicular lymphoma. The study suggested a clinical effect of the vaccine, however due to relatively short follow-up time and a heterogeneous patient population no clear conclusions could be made after the trial. In this study we present follow-up data on the eight patients included in the trial. Notably, two patients displayed an unusual disease history after completion of the vaccination trial. One patient had experienced two relapses at two years after diagnosis, then received vaccines after third line therapy and subsequently remained treatment-free for five years after vaccinations. The other patient who entered the vaccine trial after third line therapy remains in complete response at 6.5 years after the vaccination trial. Longitudinal immune monitoring in these two patients demonstrated persistent, functionally active vaccine-specific CD4⁺ and CD8⁺ T-cell responses years after vaccine completion. These results suggest a disease modifying effect of targeting PD-L1 and PD-L2 by IMV in FL. Trial registration number: NCT03381768. Date of registration: December 22, 2017.

Follicular lymphoma (FL) is an incurable cancer when diagnosed in advanced stage. Survival outcomes are generally good with exception of the minority of patients with FL who progress within 24 months from treatment initiation. FL typically responds to immunochemotherapy with long remissions, but the time between treatments gradually becomes shorter with each new treatment intervention. Except for chimeric antigen-receptor T-cells, novel treatment modalities for FL fail to induce long lasting lymphoma-specific immune responses. Provided that therapeutic cancer vaccines aim at enhancing the tumour-specific cellular immune response, cancer vaccines are a potential novel treatment modality for FL and could prolong remissions and even induce cure. Many patients with FL are of relatively advanced age which limits the available treatment options due to toxicity concerns. and novel treatment modalities with a low-toxicity profile such as therapeutic vaccines are warranted.

Both programmed death-ligand (PD-L)1 and PD-L2 are expressed in the microenvironment in FL [1]. However, checkpoint inhibitors as monotherapy fail in most non-Hodgkin lymphomas including FL. The PD1:PD-L1/2 axis is not only a target of antibody therapy in cancer as evidence supports a direct targeting by specific T cells: Healthy individuals and patients with cancer harbour naturally occurring T cells that recognise and kill PD-L1 and PD-L2 expressing cells [2]. Notably, clinical studies found that therapeutic cancer vaccinations targeting PD-L1 and another check-point molecule, indoleamine-1,2-dioxygenase, led to superior outcomes in combination with anti-PD1 antibody in metastatic melanoma [3].

We previously performed a phase I PD-L1/PD-L2 directed peptide vaccination trial in patients with de novo or relapsed FL [4]. Prior to inclusion in the trial, patients required completion of systemic therapy as either first-line or later-line with at least a partial response (PR). Patients were planned for 15 vaccines over one year. The vaccines were dual epitope peptide vaccines derived from an immunogenic hotspot in PD-L1 and PD-L2 respectively with montanide ISA-51 as adjuvant. A total of 8 patients were included in the study and six received all planned 15 vaccines. Patient data are available in Table 1. All patients but two received the planned vaccines. One patient was excluded after six vaccines due to progression. The patient remained without treatment for three years, however, and died from COVID-19 in 2022. The other patient entered the vaccination trial after four lines of therapy but was excluded due to signs of progression which was later deemed as pseudoprogression. The patient has subsequently undergone treatment with two experimental therapies to which she relapsed and is currently in complete response (CR) after radiotherapy for facial lymphomas. Among the six patients that received all vaccines, one relapsed within one year and subsequently underwent high-dose therapy (HDT) to which the patient relapsed after three years. The patient is now in CR after fourth-line rituximab-bendamustine. Two patients died in CR from lymphoma-unrelated causes; one from ischemic stroke three years after end of the vaccinations and the other patient who had a long history of ischemic heart disease died from myocardial infarction six months after the end of vaccinations. One patient who received all 15 vaccines after first-line therapy during Rituximab maintenance therapy remains in CR six years after completion of vaccines and is no longer followed by the department. Patient 1

This patient, a 53-year-old female, was diagnosed with FL grade 3A, stage IV in December 2015 and achieved a PR after 8 series of R-CVP and rituximab maintenance. The patient relapsed within four months and was treated with six cycles obinutuzumab-bendamustine from November 2016 to March 2017 resulting in CR with a duration of one year. Second relapse in February 2018 was treated with R-CHOP21. The patient entered the PD-L1/PD-L2 vaccination trial in July 2018 in PR after three previous lines of therapy. The patient received all 15 vaccines and a PET-CT scan at end-of-trial in July 2019 showed an enlarged lymph node conglomerate in the abdomen with needle biopsy verified stage III FL. The lymphoma specimen was heavily infiltrated with CD3+ T-cells, and compared to lymph node biopsies from previous relapses this biopsy had significantly higher density of CD3+ T-cells. The patient was followed in watch-and-wait, and two years after in July 2021 a CT scan displayed a surprising normalization of the enlarged nodes. Hence, the patient was classified as having pseudoprogression and not progression at the end of the vaccination study, and the patient was followed in watch-and-wait. Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMC) isolated in January 2024 at 4.5 years after completion of the vaccination trial were stimulated with the PD-L1 and PD-L2 vaccination epitopes and displayed high background activity in IFN-γ Enzyme Linked ImmunoSPOT (ELISPOT) (Fig. 1B and C). Yet, a statistically significant response to the PD-L1 peptide was identified. Using intracellular cytokine staining (ICS) (Supplementary Material 1 and 2) to gate out high background activity we showed CD4 and CD8 T-cell response to both the PD-L1 and PD-L2 epitopes (Fig. 1D and E). At six months after PBMC sampling, the patient had the third relapse and received lenalidomide-rituximab to which a PR was attained. Patient 2

This patient, a 63-year-old female, was diagnosed with grade 3A, stage IV FL in November 2010 and received 8 series of R-CVP as first line therapy followed by rituximab maintenance therapy. The patient attained a CR which was only sustained for 9 months after which she received R-CHOP21. The patient was maintained in CR for almost 5 years relapsing in January 2016 and received rituximab-bendamustine as third-line therapy concluded in July 2016. The patient attained a near-CR and entered the vaccination trial in March 2018 at one year and nine months after cessation of third-line therapy. All 15 vaccines were administered and the end-of-trial PET-CT scan in March 2019 showed CR. IFN-γ ELISPOT on PD-L1 and PD-L2 peptide stimulated PBMC isolated five years after end of the vaccination trial showed strong T-cell responses to both vaccination epitopes (Fig. 1F and G). Subsequent ICS demonstrated both CD4 and CD8 T-cell responses (Fig. 1H and I, and Supplementary Material 3). Most notably was the strong CD4 T-cell response to the PD-L2 epitope (Fig. 1H, right). The patient is still in CR at 6.5 years after end of vaccinations.

Strong evidence supports the important role of the immune system in the pathogenesis of and response to therapy in FL. Survival is associated with expression of immune related genes expressed by stromal cells [1,5]. Pro-tumourigenic (M2) tumour associated macrophages (TAM) are linked to decreased survival [6], increased risk of disease transformation and early treatment failure [7]. Aberrant activation of FL B-cells conferred by M2 TAM through DC-SIGN:B-cell receptor interaction has been suggested as a pathogenic mechanism in FL [8]. Thus, abrogation of M2 TAM mediated signaling could be a treatment option in FL. In this relation we find that the sustained remissions after PD-L1/2 directed vaccines described above are interesting as the PD-L1/2 specific vaccines target several mechanisms of importance in FL. Firstly, killing of PD-L1+/2+ immunosuppressive cells by vaccination-induced T cells decreases intratumoural immune suppression. Secondly, PD-L1/2 specific T cells release pro-inflammatory cytokines that modulate M2 TAM into M1 upon target-cell recognition. In FL, the reversion of TAM from anti-inflammatory to pro-inflammatory will act dually by 1) decreasing intratumoural immune suppression conferred by M2 TAM and thereby enhance the T-cell mediated tumour control [7,9], and 2) dampen the M2 mediated proliferative signaling provided to the transformed B cells. Though, FL is characterised by long bouts of remission, we find the clinical responses in two of the six fully vaccinated patients remarkable. One patient remains in CR at 6.5 years after end-of-trial and the other patient did not receive any therapy for five years before having her third relapse. This patient showed signs of progression after the vaccination study, but the enlarged lymph nodes were heavily infiltrated with CD3+ T-cells [4] and the enlarged nodes subsequently regressed supporting the hypothesis of T-cell mediated immune control in this patient. Targeting of immunosuppressive mechanisms by therapeutic cancer vaccines is a novel concept that has shown clinical potential in solid cancer [3]. The proposed mechanism of action of these immunomodulating vaccines (IMV) is the ability of vaccination-induced T-cells to kill immunosuppressive cells, and by release of Th1-cytokines modulate stromal cells, including macrophages, into a pro-inflammatory M1 phenotype [10]. Importantly, no patients reported any autoimmune phenomena after the vaccines, neither in the original report nor in this follow-up study. Additionally, this follow-up study did not identify any vaccine-related side-effects. One caveat of the data presented is that we cannot exclude the possibility that the pre-vaccination therapy induced the long-lasting remissions. However, provided that the two reported patients experienced several relapses after conventional therapy and subsequent long remissions after the vaccination protocol, we suggest that PD-L1/2-specific IMV could have clinical activity in FL either as consolidation or maintenance therapy. However, for patients with treatment naïve disease in watchful waiting, we envisage, that vaccines could be a low-toxicity means to prolong the time-to-therapy in these patients, and in a subset of patients potentially act as a curative treatment. We are currently setting up a clinical vaccination trial in patients with FL in watchful waiting to answer this question.

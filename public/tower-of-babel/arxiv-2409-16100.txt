Theory of Polar Skyrmions in Layered Structure of Ferroelectric Perovskites
Snehasish Sen, Sudhansu S. Mandal

Abstract
Discovery of polar skyrmions on the (PbTiO 3 ) n /(SrTiO 3 ) n superlattice in the absence of any known interaction, which can orient electric polarizations at a point with respect to its neighboring points, is enigmatic. We here show that the coupling of the electric polarization and strain at each layer is responsible for orienting polarizations. We formulate coupled Euler equations for electric polarization and elastic displacement vectors, and solve to find skyrmion solution for a range of electric fields with unique topological number in each layer. The types of skyrmions vary from Neel to Bloch depending on the position of the layers in the superlattice, as observed in the experiments.

The free energy (excluding Landau free energy that stabilizes ferroelectric state) for a strained ferroelectric two-dimensional system with the application of electric field is given by
[

]

F=\int_{S}dS[\mathcal{F}_{\rm gr}+\mathcal{F}_{\rm st}+\mathcal{F}_{\rm int}+\mathcal{F}_{\rm elec}]
. Here, the gradient surface energy density
\mathcal{F}_{\rm gr}=\frac{1}{2}g_{ijkl}P_{i,j}P_{k,l}

(i,k=1,2,3\,\,{\rm and}\,\,j,l=1,2)

\bm{P}
being electric polarization vector of unit magnitude,
P_{i,j}=\partial P_{i}/\partial x_{j}
and the corresponding strength being
g_{ijkl}
with underlying cubic lattice symmetry,
i.e.,

g_{1111}=g_{2222}=g_{11}
,
g_{1122}=g_{2211}=g_{12}
, and
g_{1212}=g_{2121}=g_{3131}=g_{3232}=g_{44}

\mathcal{F}_{\rm st}=\frac{1}{2}c_{ijkl}\epsilon_{ij}\epsilon_{kl}

\epsilon_{ij}=(u_{i,j}+u_{j,i})/2

\bm{u}
representing the elastic displacement vector of the defect causing strain, and the elastic stiffness tensor
c_{ijkl}
satisfying relationship of symmetric stress tensor
\sigma_{ij}=c_{ijkl}\epsilon_{kl}
with strain tensor possessing cubic lattice symmetry:
c_{1111}=c_{2222}=c_{11}
,
c_{1122}=c_{2211}=c_{12}
, and
c_{1212}=c_{2121}=c_{3131}=c_{3232}=c_{44}

The term

(1)

Apart from the intrinsic strain in the lattice, extrinsic strain arises due to the application of electric field in the PTO plane. Since the applied electric field, together with the depolarizing field, is responsible for electric polarization, the strain couples with polarization giving rise to the interaction term in free energy

This rules out the possibility of proliferating antiskyrmion in any of the layers.

We have identified the cause of forming polar skyrmion structures in PTO layers in the absence of any
a priori
known interaction involving electric polarizations with linear gradients so as to cause orientation at different directions at different spatial points. The accomplished polar skyrmion solution linked with the elastic displacement vector for defects in PTO layers and its structural variations from layer to layer have been found to be consistent with the experiments. The range of electric field for which skyrmions can be realized is estimated in reasonable agreement with previously reported numerical simulation.

(SE7)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u_{2}}{\partial x_{2}}=\sin\phi\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left[u_{n}\sin\phi\sin\theta_{n}\right]+\frac{1}{r}\cos\phi\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}\left[u_{n}\sin\phi\sin\theta_{n}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt=\sin^{2}\phi\sin\theta_{n}\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial r}+\frac{1}{r}u_{n}\cos^{2}\phi\sin\theta_{n}\,,

(SE8)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u_{1}}{\partial x_{2}}=\sin\phi\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left[u_{n}\cos\phi\sin\theta_{n}\right]+\frac{1}{r}\cos\phi\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}\left[u_{n}\cos\phi\sin\theta_{n}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt=\sin\phi\cos\phi\sin\theta_{n}\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial r}-\frac{1}{r}\sin\phi\cos\phi\sin\theta_{n}u_{n}\,,

(SE9)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u_{2}}{\partial x_{1}}=\cos\phi\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left[u_{n}\sin\phi\sin\theta_{n}\right]-\frac{1}{r}\sin\phi\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}\left[u_{n}\sin\phi\sin\theta_{n}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt=\cos\phi\sin\phi\sin\theta_{n}\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial r}-\frac{1}{r}\sin\phi\cos\phi\sin\theta_{n}u_{n}\,,

(SE10)

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\frac{\partial u_{3}}{\partial x_{1}}=\cos\phi\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left[u_{n}\cos\theta_{n}\right]-\frac{1}{r}\sin\phi\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}\left[u_{n}\cos\theta_{n}\right]

(SE11)

\displaystyle\hskip-8.5359pt=\sin\phi\cos\theta_{n}\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial r}\,.

(SE12)

We now formulate different components of free energy in terms of polar variables using above derivatives.

Gradient energy:

The gradient free energy density is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\rm gr}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}g_{ijkl}P_{i,j}P_{k,l}\,.

Using Eqs. (
SE1
)–(
SE6
), and symmetry properties of
g_{ijkl}
we find,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\rm gr}=\frac{g_{11}}{4r^{2}}\biggl[\left(\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\phi}\right)^{2}+2\left(\frac{\partial\Theta}{\partial\phi}\right)^{2}+r^{2}\left[\left(\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial r}\right)^{2}+2\left(\frac{\partial\Theta}{\partial r}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 22.76228pt-\biggl[\left(\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\phi}\right)^{2}+r^{2}\left(\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial r}\right)^{2}\biggr]\cos 2\Theta\biggr]\,.

(SE13)

\frac{\partial\Theta(r)}{\partial\phi}=0
and
\frac{\partial\Phi(\phi)}{\partial r}=0
, Eq. (
SE13

\mathcal{F}_{\rm gr}=\frac{g_{11}}{2}(\Theta_{r}^{2}+\frac{\sin^{2}\Theta}{r^{2}}\Phi_{\phi}^{2})\,.

(SE14)

Strain Energy :

Different components of strain tensors are as follows.
Using Eqs. (
SE7
)–(
SE12
), we find

\displaystyle\hskip-65.44142pt\epsilon_{11}=\frac{\partial u_{1}}{\partial x_{1}}

\displaystyle\hskip-51.21504pt=\cos^{2}\phi\sin\theta_{n}\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial r}+\frac{1}{r}u_{n}\sin^{2}\phi\sin\theta_{n}\,,

(SE15)

\displaystyle\hskip-65.44142pt\epsilon_{22}=\frac{\partial u_{2}}{\partial x_{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip-51.21504pt=\sin^{2}\phi\sin\theta_{n}\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial r}+\frac{1}{r}u_{n}\cos^{2}\phi\sin\theta_{n}\,,

(SE16)

\displaystyle\hskip-42.67912pt\epsilon_{12}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial u_{1}}{\partial x_{2}}+\frac{\partial u_{2}}{\partial x_{1}}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt=\sin\phi\cos\phi\sin\theta_{n}\frac{du_{n}}{dr}-\frac{1}{r}u_{n}\sin\phi\cos\phi\sin\theta_{n}\,,

(SE17)

\displaystyle\hskip-156.49014pt\epsilon_{23}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial u_{3}}{\partial x_{2}}+\frac{\partial u_{2}}{\partial x_{3}}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip-142.26378pt=\frac{1}{2}\sin\phi\cos\theta_{n}\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial r}\,,

(SE18)

\displaystyle\hskip-156.49014pt\epsilon_{13}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial u_{3}}{\partial x_{1}}+\frac{\partial u_{1}}{\partial x_{3}}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip-142.26378pt=\frac{1}{2}\cos\phi\cos\theta_{n}\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial r}\,,

(SE19)

with

\epsilon_{33}=0

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\rm st}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}c_{11}\left(\epsilon_{11}^{2}+\epsilon_{22}^{2}\right)+c_{12}\epsilon_{11}\epsilon_{22}+2c_{44}\left(\epsilon_{12}^{2}+\epsilon_{13}^{2}+\epsilon_{23}^{2}\right)

(SE20)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sin^{2}\theta_{n}\left[c_{11}\frac{u_{n}^{2}}{2r^{2}}+c_{12}\frac{u_{n}}{r}\left(\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial r}\right)\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\left[\cos^{2}\theta_{n}(c_{11}+c_{12})-2c_{11}\right]\left(\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial r}\right)^{2}\,.

\displaystyle+\left.u_{3}P_{2}\frac{\partial P_{3}}{\partial x_{2}}+u_{3}P_{3}\frac{\partial P_{2}}{\partial x_{2}}+u_{3}P_{1}\frac{\partial P_{3}}{\partial x_{1}}+u_{3}P_{3}\frac{\partial P_{1}}{\partial x_{1}}\right]\,.

\displaystyle+4\cos\theta_{n}\cos(\Phi-\phi)\biggl(\cos 2\Theta\,\Theta_{r}+\dfrac{\sin 2\Theta\Phi_{\phi}}{2r}\biggr)

Topological Charge

The topological charge for a skyrmion or an antiskyrmion is defined as

Q=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int\int\bm{P}\cdot\left(\frac{\partial\bm{P}}{\partial x}\times\frac{\partial\bm{P}}{\partial y}\right)\,dxdy\,.

(SE32)

where
\bm{P}=[\sin\Theta\cos\Phi,\,\sin\Theta\sin\Phi,\,\cos\Theta]
.
The derivatives of
P
with respect to the Cartesian coordinates are given by

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\bm{P}}{\partial x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\biggl(\cos\phi\cos\Theta\cos\Phi\Theta_{r}+\frac{1}{r}\sin\phi\sin\Theta\sin\Phi\Phi_{\phi}\biggr)\hat{i}

(SE33)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\biggl(\cos\phi\cos\Theta\sin\Phi\Theta_{r}-\frac{1}{r}\sin\phi\sin\Theta\cos\Phi\Phi_{\phi}\biggr)\hat{j}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\biggl(-\cos\phi\sin\Theta\Theta_{r}\biggr)\hat{k}\,.

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\bm{P}}{\partial y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\biggl(\sin\phi\cos\Theta\cos\Phi\Theta_{r}-\frac{1}{r}\cos\phi\sin\Theta\sin\Phi\Phi_{\phi}\biggr)\hat{i}

(SE34)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\biggl(\sin\phi\cos\Theta\sin\Phi\Theta_{r}+\frac{1}{r}\cos\phi\sin\Theta\cos\Phi\Phi_{\phi}\biggr)\hat{j}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\biggl(-\sin\phi\sin\Theta\Theta_{r}\biggr)\hat{k}\,.

Using Eqs. (
SE33
) and (
SE34
), we find

\bm{P}\cdot\left(\frac{\partial\bm{P}}{\partial x}\times\frac{\partial\bm{P}}{\partial y}\right)=\frac{1}{r}\sin\Theta\Theta_{r}\Phi_{\phi}\,.

(SE35)

We thus find

Q=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}dr\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\phi\,\sin\Theta\,\Theta_{r}\Phi_{\phi}\,.

(SE36)

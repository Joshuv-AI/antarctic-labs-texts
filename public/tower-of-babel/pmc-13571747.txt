Recent advances in Siraitia grosvenorii polysaccharides: Extraction, structural characteristics, biological activities and structure-activity relationships
Authors: Yinling Luan, Shengnan Zhu, Fengting Yin, Guangli Yan, Jingzhe Gao, Fuxiang Huang, Jiaying Guo, Chenyue Wang, Yifan Ren, Ning Zhang, Hui Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAs important bioactive macromolecular components of Siraitia grosvenorii, Siraitia grosvenorii polysaccharides (SGPs) have attracted considerable attention in the field of functional foods in recent years. This review systematically summarizes the latest research advances regarding extraction strategies, structural characteristics, gastrointestinal digestion and gut fermentation, biological activities, and structure-activity relationships of SGPs. The relationships between structural characteristics, including molecular weight, monosaccharide composition, and glycosidic linkages, and their antioxidant, hypoglycemic, and immunoregulatory activities are summarized. In addition, the major issues and challenges associated with the mechanism of biological activity, safety assessment, and industrial production of SGPs are discussed in more detail. Overall, this review provides a theoretical basis for the precise design and food-oriented application of SGPs in functional food systems, natural functional ingredients, and related health products.Keywords: Siraitia grosvenorii polysaccharides, Extraction, Structural characterization, Biological activities

Introduction

Siraitia grosvenorii (Swingle) C. Jeffrey ex Lu et Z. Y. Zhang (S. grosvenorii), also known as “Monk fruit” or “Luo han guo,” is a perennial climbing vine belonging to the genus Siraitia of the Cucurbitaceae family (Fig. 1) (Xie et al., 2023). S. grosvenorii is native to the provinces of Guangxi, Guangdong, and Hunan in China, particularly the Guilin region, where it has been cultivated for more than 400 years (Guo et al., 2024). According to statistics, the cultivation area of S. grosvenorii in Guilin exceeds 200,000 mu, and its annual production accounts for more than 80% of the national output, making it the largest production base worldwide (Yang et al., 2025). In recent years, the market demand for S. grosvenorii has shown a continuous upward trend, primarily attributable to its unique nutritional and edible values. In 2002, S. grosvenorii was officially included by the Ministry of Health of the People's Republic of China in the “List of Substances that are Both Food and Medicine,” making it one of the earliest approved medicinal-food homologous species in China (Luo et al., 2026). Owing to its richness in vitamins, amino acids, trace elements, and various bioactive components, S. grosvenorii is commonly consumed fresh or processed into diverse products such as dried fruits, fruit teas, and fruit wines (Wu et al., 2022). Among these, mogroside V, as the principal functional component, exhibits a sweetness approximately 300 times that of sucrose, yet contains negligible calories, is not metabolized in the human body, and does not induce fluctuations in blood glucose levels. This property makes S. grosvenorii an ideal natural sugar substitute for individuals with diabetes, obese populations, and those pursuing a healthy diet (Liao et al., 2022). In addition, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has officially recognized S. grosvenorii extract as a “Generally Recognized as Safe” (GRAS) food additive, which has greatly promoted its global market expansion(Muñoz-Labrador et al., 2024). In addition to serving as a rich food resource, S. grosvenorii has also been traditionally used as an empirical medicinal agent with remarkable therapeutic effects against various diseases (Duan et al., 2023). Modern pharmacological studies have demonstrated that S. grosvenorii exhibits antioxidant, antitumor, hypoglycemic, and hepatoprotective activities (Mao et al., 2025). In summary, as a characteristic plant resource with both edible and medicinal value, S. grosvenorii aligns well with the principles of modern healthy dietary concepts.

To date, a variety of components have been isolated and identified from S. grosvenorii, mainly including triterpenoid saponins, flavonoids, polyphenols, and polysaccharides (Liu et al., 2026). Among these, the triterpenoid saponin mogroside V is the most extensively studied representative compound, exhibiting significant biological activities in regulating glucose and lipid metabolism, protecting liver function, and inhibiting tumor cell proliferation (He et al., 2025). However, these small-molecule components exhibit limitations such as rapid in vivo metabolism, low bioavailability, and poor targeting ability, which severely restrict their clinical efficacy (Zhang et al., 2025). In contrast, S. grosvenorii polysaccharides (SGPs) exhibit considerable development potential. These macromolecules generally possess superior biocompatibility and sustained-release properties, enabling prolonged and stable pharmacological effects in vivo, thereby overcoming many limitations of small-molecule components (Guo et al., 2022a). Compared with mogroside V, the unique branched architecture of SGPs may confer a broader range of targets and mechanisms of action. Specifically, the complex spatial structure of SGPs can interact with the gut microbiota to generate beneficial metabolites such as short-chain fatty acids, thereby regulating the host's overall metabolic status (Guo et al., 2022b). Furthermore, their macromolecular nature confers notable immunomodulatory properties, enhancing host defense by activating macrophages and promoting cytokine secretion (Jiang et al., 2026). Despite the distinctive structural features and diverse functional potential of SGPs, current research remains fragmented, with most studies limited to crude polysaccharide mixtures and lacking systematic elucidation of their fine structures. Moreover, studies on structure-activity relationships are largely restricted to correlative descriptions, lacking systematic validation of causal relationships between individual structural parameters and functional outcomes. Although some progress has been made in biological activity studies, the underlying molecular mechanisms remain at an early exploratory stage, and data on long-term safety evaluation are relatively scarce. These limitations significantly constrain the in-depth development and industrial application of SGPs.

Previous reviews on SGPs have primarily focused on providing a general overview of their biological activities and structural characterization, without establishing a comprehensive framework that links extraction and purification, structural elucidation, and biological activity validation (Duan et al., 2023; Huang et al., 2024). To address this research gap, the present review establishes a more systematic and comprehensive framework by providing an up-to-date and integrated overview of recent advances in the extraction and purification techniques, structural characteristics, and biological activities of SGPs. The structure-activity relationship of SGPs is deeply analyzed to elucidate the underlying molecular mechanisms responsible for their biological functions, while the current research limitations and future perspectives are also discussed. Particular emphasis is placed on the effects of different extraction methods on polysaccharide yield and structural integrity, along with a systematic comparison of the advantages and limitations of various extraction approaches. It analyzes the intrinsic relationships between structure and biological activity, revealing how key structural parameters such as molecular weight (Mw) distribution, monosaccharide composition, and glycosidic linkage patterns regulate immunomodulatory, antioxidant, and hypoglycemic activities. Furthermore, this review provides an integrated analysis of the structural transformations, biological mechanisms, and prospects for food industrial applications of SGPs during gastrointestinal digestion and intestinal fermentation processes. In particular, the influence of SGPs structural characteristics on their functional expression during gastrointestinal processing is highlighted, and the key challenges associated with safety evaluation and large-scale production are further discussed. This review aims to provide theoretical support and references for the high-value utilization of SGPs and promote the transformation of this medicinal and edible resource from traditional empirical applications toward modern precision nutrition interventions.

Nutritional value

As a natural low-calorie sweetening resource, S. grosvenorii has attracted extensive attention for its nutritional value in the development of functional foods. Based on nutritional data, S. grosvenorii can be regarded as a nutritionally distinctive fruit resource characterized by high dietary fiber, moderate protein, low lipid content in the flesh, abundant minerals, and high-intensity non-sugar sweet constituents. In different fruit parts, the flesh showed the highest protein content (11.46%) and the lowest fat content (2.93%), being approximately 67.3% richer in protein and 61.4% lower in fat than the peel (Utami et al., 2024). Although the whole fruit exhibited the highest total sugar content (7.51%), the difference among peel, flesh, and whole fruit was not statistically significant, suggesting that the sweetness of S. grosvenorii cannot be explained solely by conventional sugars. Instead, it is mainly attributed to mogrosides, especially Mogroside V and Siamenoside I, whose sweetness is hundreds of times higher than that of sucrose (Guo et al., 2024). Region- and processing-dependent analyses further showed that fresh Longsheng fruit contained higher total sugar, protein, and crude fat than fresh Yongfu fruit, whereas dried Yongfu fruit showed increased total sugar, dietary fiber, protein, ash, and crude fat, probably due to dehydration-induced concentration and processing-related compositional changes (Liu et al., 2025). In particular, total dietary fiber increased from 34.97 g/100 g in fresh Yongfu fruit to 41.99 g/100 g in dried fruit, indicating the strong potential of S. grosvenorii as a fiber-rich functional food ingredient. Mineral analysis revealed that fresh Yongfu fruit contained particularly high levels of K, Mg, and Ca, reaching 13,656.5, 2254.25, and 1203.5 μg/g, respectively, which were 27.9%, 29.1%, and 23.9% higher than those in fresh Longsheng fruit.

Overall, S. grosvenorii is not merely a natural sweetener but rather a multifunctional food resource that combines low-calorie sweetness with dietary fiber, minerals, amino acids, and polysaccharides. The high-protein and low-fat characteristics of its pulp, together with the high dietary fiber content in dried fruits, provide a nutritional basis for its application in low-sugar, low-fat, and gut health-related foods. Meanwhile, the high sweetness and low-calorie characteristics conferred by mogrosides enable S. grosvenorii to partially replace sucrose and reduce the caloric burden of foods. However, existing studies have also demonstrated that the nutritional composition of S. grosvenorii is significantly influenced by cultivation region, plant part, processing method, and drying degree. Therefore, future studies should further integrate nutrigenomics, metabolomics, and food processing evaluation to establish standardized assessment systems for the nutritional quality and functional component stability of S. grosvenorii, thereby promoting its precise application in low-sugar foods, nutritional supplements, and special dietary products.

Extraction and purification

Extraction

Extraction is the primary step in SGPs research, and its efficiency and quality directly determine the reliability of subsequent structural characterization and bioactivity evaluation (Wu et al., 2025). The selection of extraction methods for SGPs requires comprehensive consideration of raw material characteristics, target polysaccharide types, and intended applications, as different strategies exhibit significant differences in yield, structural preservation, and processing cost (Wei et al., 2026). Currently, conventional methods for extracting SGPs include hot water extraction (HWE) and acid-alkali extraction. Moreover, emerging techniques such as enzyme-assisted extraction (EAE) and ultrasonic-assisted extraction (UAE) have demonstrated significant advantages in SGPs extraction. Hot water extraction

HWE, as the most classical and widely applied extraction method for the preparation of SGPs, possesses a solid application foundation in both laboratory research and industrial food processing due to its operational simplicity, low cost, and the absence of organic chemical reagents (Khalil & Lukasiewicz, 2024). The core principle of this method is based on the enhanced permeability and solubilizing capacity of water molecules at elevated temperatures, which disrupts the cell wall structure of S. grosvenorii and facilitates the release of intracellular polysaccharides. The effectiveness of HWE for extracting SGPs has been well established, with typical extraction temperatures ranging from 70 to 100 °C, solid-to-liquid ratios ranging from 1:12 to 1:35 g/mL, single extraction times from 15 min to 6 h, extraction cycles of 2–3 times, and yields ranging from 0.5% to 18.48%. Notably, prolonging the extraction time can significantly increase the yield of SGPs, which may be attributed to enhanced cell wall swelling and more complete release of polysaccharide molecules (Wang, Li, et al., 2023). Dujni et al. obtained polysaccharide fractions SGF1 (1.2%) and SGT2 (0.5%) from S. grosvenorii using HWE. Under the same extraction temperature (100 °C), the extraction time for SGF1 was 120 min, whereas that for SGT2 was 15 min (Dujnič et al., 2021). However, excessively prolonged extraction may lead to the cleavage or degradation of polysaccharide chains, thereby affecting the overall yield. Under extraction conditions of 100 °C and 300 min, Lin obtained a yield of only 1.2% for the polysaccharide SGP-LH (Lin et al., 2007). In addition, the effect of extraction temperature on polysaccharide yield exhibits a pronounced nonlinear pattern. Moderate increases in temperature can enhance the kinetic energy of water molecules, promoting hydrogen bond disruption and cell wall matrix disintegration. However, when the temperature exceeds the thermal stability threshold of polysaccharides, glycosidic bond hydrolysis accelerates, resulting in reduced Mw and destruction of active structural domains (Wang et al., 2025). Li et al. achieved a polysaccharide yield of 18.48% from S. grosvenorii using HWE at an extraction temperature of 75 °C (Li, Zheng, et al., 2025). Therefore, in practical applications, appropriate extraction conditions should be selected based on the characteristics of target polysaccharides, seeking an optimal balance between extraction temperature and time to maximize yield while preserving structural integrity. Moreover, HWE for extracting SGPs has certain limitations, mainly reflected in its limited ability to disrupt dense cell wall structures, resulting in low extraction efficiency for polysaccharides tightly bound to lignin and cellulose. Therefore, future studies should further optimize HWE by integrating mathematical models such as response surface methodology or artificial neural networks to establish parameter prediction systems under multifactor coupling conditions, thereby achieving simultaneous improvement in extraction efficiency and polysaccharide quality.

Acid-alkaline extraction

Polysaccharides are often tightly associated with impurities such as proteins, lignin, and minerals through hydrogen bonds, ionic bonds, or hydrophobic interactions, forming stable complex structures that are difficult to disrupt by HWE, thereby limiting the yield of target polysaccharides (San Clemente & Jamet, 2022). Acid extraction (AE) or alkaline extraction (ALE) can alter the system pH by introducing acidic or basic media, thereby cleaving the linkages between SGPs and impurities, releasing encapsulated or bound polysaccharide fractions, and significantly enhancing extraction efficiency. Jiang et al. obtained polysaccharide fractions SGP-H, SGP-A, and SGP-B using HWE (240 min, 70 °C, 1:30), AE (240 min, 60 °C, 1,30), and ALE (240 min, 70 °C, 1,30), with yields of 1.97%, 3.25%, and 3.13%, respectively (Jiang et al., 2026). It can be observed that polysaccharide yields obtained via acid-alkali extraction are significantly higher than those from HWE, demonstrating the clear advantage of acid-alkali treatment in disrupting polysaccharide-impurity complexes. Interestingly, the yield of SGP-A is higher than that of SGP-B, which may be attributed to the fact that under acidic conditions, hydrogen ions can more effectively protonate glycosidic bonds and carboxyl groups in polysaccharide molecules, weakening ionic interactions with impurities such as proteins, while promoting partial hydrolysis of cell wall components like hemicellulose, thereby releasing more bound polysaccharides. In addition, acidic conditions may induce swelling and depolymerization of pectic substances, further enhancing polysaccharide accessibility (Iñiguez-Moreno et al., 2024). However, the intensity of acid-alkali conditions must be strictly controlled to avoid excessive degradation. Excessively low pH values or high reaction temperatures may induce severe glycosidic bond cleavage, generating large amounts of low-Mw oligosaccharides or even monosaccharides, which not only reduce polysaccharide yield but may also disrupt the native chain conformation and functional domains (Kang & Chang, 2024). In addition, strong acid or alkali treatments may also affect the stability and processing compatibility of SGPs within food systems. On the other hand, the large amounts of wastewater and salt by-products generated during acid-alkali extraction can increase post-treatment costs and environmental burdens; therefore, the development of green and recyclable extraction systems is particularly important for the industrial food applications of SGPs. Future studies should further integrate green solvent systems, response surface optimization, and online pH regulation technologies to improve extraction efficiency while simultaneously considering structural preservation, functional stability, and sustainable processing requirements.

Ultrasound-assisted extraction

UAE utilizes cavitation effects, mechanical vibrations, and thermal effects generated during the propagation of ultrasound in a liquid medium to achieve efficient disruption of S. grosvenorii cell walls and enhanced dissolution of polysaccharides (Li et al., 2023). As an emerging extraction technique, UAE demonstrates significant technical advantages in SGPs research, achieving high extraction efficiency within a short time while effectively reducing energy consumption and solvent usage. Zhu et al. extracted a polysaccharide fraction (SGP-P) from S. grosvenorii using UAE with an extraction time of 45 min, temperature of 55 °C, and a solid-to-liquid ratio of 1:45 g/mL, and the obtained polysaccharide exhibited good antioxidant activity (Zhu et al., 2020). Compared with conventional HWE, UAE can significantly shorten extraction time while maintaining or even enhancing extraction efficiency. It is noteworthy that the selection of ultrasonic parameters has a significant impact on polysaccharide yield and quality. When ultrasonic power is too low, cavitation is insufficient, resulting in incomplete cell wall disruption and limited polysaccharide release. Conversely, excessive power or prolonged extraction time may cause mechanical and thermal degradation of polysaccharide chains, leading to broadened Mw distribution or destruction of bioactive structures (Tang et al., 2023). Therefore, Li et al. optimized the UAE of SGPs using response surface methodology, employing a Box-Behnken design (BBD) to systematically investigate the interactive effects of ultrasonic power, extraction time, and temperature on polysaccharide yield, and identified the optimal conditions as 300 W ultrasonic power, 30 min extraction time, and 60 °C extraction temperature, under which the yield was maximized (Li, Zheng, et al., 2025). In addition, the temperature of the UAE is typically maintained at a relatively low level (generally below 70 °C), which reduces the risk of degradation of thermosensitive polysaccharide components and helps preserve their natural biological activities. However, the large-scale application of the UAE still faces several challenges. Excessive ultrasonic power or prolonged treatment time may lead to overaccumulation of cavitation effects, triggering free radical chain reactions and resulting in oxidative degradation and loss of polysaccharide activity (Wang, Xiong, & Huang, 2023). Moreover, the energy consumption cost of ultrasonic equipment remains disadvantageous compared with HWE, and the uniformity of the ultrasonic field in large-scale continuous production still requires improvement. Nevertheless, due to its green and efficient characteristics, the UAE has become an important direction for technological advancement in SGPs extraction, and future research should focus on systematic analysis of structure-activity relationships to achieve controllable optimization of the extraction process.

Enzyme-assisted extraction

EAE, as an innovative technique, offers a key advantage in utilizing the high specificity of enzymes and mild reaction conditions to selectively degrade cell wall components of S. grosvenorii, thereby significantly improving extraction efficiency while preserving polysaccharide structural integrity (Bains et al., 2023; Gao et al., 2022). The extraction temperature for enzyme-assisted extraction of SGPs is typically maintained between 40 °C and 50 °C, which ensures enzymatic activity while preventing thermal degradation of polysaccharide structures. Under conditions of 240 min extraction time and 40 °C, Lin et al. obtained the polysaccharide SGP-LE using EAE, with a yield of 2.3% (Lin et al., 2007). Compared with HWE, EAE operates at temperatures 40–60 °C lower, effectively suppressing Maillard and caramelization side reactions, resulting in polysaccharides with lower reducing sugar content, which is particularly important for their hypoglycemic activity (Li et al., 2024). Furthermore, compared with acid-alkali extraction, EAE avoids random cleavage of glycosidic bonds caused by strong acids or bases, resulting in a more uniform Mw distribution and better preservation of characteristic structural domains such as uronic acid) content and specific glycosidic linkages. Jiang et al. reported that the UA content of polysaccharide SGP-E obtained from S. grosvenorii via EAE reached 23.61%, significantly higher than that of SGP-A (17.91%) obtained by AE, directly reflecting the unique advantage of EAE in preserving acidic polysaccharide structural features (Jiang et al., 2026). However, the limitations of EAE mainly lie in the high cost of enzyme preparations and the requirement for precise control of pH and temperature during enzymatic hydrolysis, which increases process complexity and production cost. In addition, variations in cell wall composition among S. grosvenorii from different sources make it difficult for a single enzyme system to achieve complete degradation, rendering the selection and optimization of compound enzyme systems critical for improving extraction efficiency. At present, the synergistic application of cellulase, pectinase, and hemicellulose has shown promising results, but systematic studies on enzymatic hydrolysis kinetics and polysaccharide release mechanisms remain insufficient. Future studies should further integrate enzyme engineering, online process control, and green processing concepts to establish a more precise enzymatic extraction system for SGPs that is suitable for industrial food production.

Ultrasound-assisted aqueous two-phase extraction

Ultrasound-assisted aqueous two-phase extraction (UA-ATPE) is a novel extraction-separation method that integrates an ultrasonic physical field with an aqueous two-phase system, combining efficient extraction with preliminary purification (Tan et al., 2022). The core advantage of this method lies in the synergistic enhancement between ultrasonic cavitation effects and the partitioning mechanism of the aqueous two-phase system (Liu et al., 2023). It is noteworthy that the composition of the aqueous two-phase system plays a decisive role in polysaccharide partitioning behavior, with the type and concentration of phase-forming salts, the nature of the organic phase, and the phase ratio jointly regulating selective distribution between the two phases. Li et al. introduced ultrasound assistance (300 W, 30 min) into an (NH₄)₂SO₄ (31%)/ethanol (32%) aqueous two-phase system and obtained SGPs with a high yield of 25.77% (Li, Zheng, et al., 2025). Compared with the traditional water extraction-ethanol precipitation method, extraction and preliminary purification are integrated, significantly shortening the process cycle. In addition, the extraction temperature in aqueous two-phase systems for SGPs is typically around 60 °C, a mild condition that helps preserve their native conformation and biological activity. However, existing studies primarily focus on improving yield and optimizing process parameters, while lacking an in-depth understanding of the selective partitioning mechanisms of polysaccharides at the molecular level in aqueous two-phase systems. For instance, whether different systems preferentially enrich specific Mw fractions or characteristic glycan chain structures remains unsupported by systematic evidence, thereby limiting the application potential of this technique in structure-oriented preparation. Meanwhile, a complex coupling may exist between ultrasonic effects and interfacial behavior in aqueous two-phase systems: excessive cavitation may alter interfacial tension, disrupt phase separation stability, induce emulsification, and even cause polysaccharide chain scission or oxidative degradation (Zhang & Zhu, 2024). Therefore, future studies should integrate structural parameters such as Mw distribution and key glycosidic linkage types to systematically evaluate the selective nature of aqueous two-phase systems in SGPs. On the other hand, techniques such as rheological analysis, interfacial characterization, and molecular simulation should be employed to elucidate the coupling mechanisms between ultrasonic fields and aqueous two-phase systems. On this basis, the development of low-toxicity, recyclable green aqueous two-phase systems (e.g., based on natural polymers or deep eutectic solvents) will become an important direction. Meanwhile, integrating continuous processing equipment with online monitoring technologies to achieve coordinated control of process scale-up and product quality consistency is crucial for advancing SGPs from laboratory research to industrial application.

Separation and purification

During the preparation of SGPs, the crude extract obtained after extraction must undergo systematic separation and purification to remove interfering substances such as proteins, pigments, small-molecule impurities, and residual solvents (Lai et al., 2024). This is because residual proteins and small-molecule impurities may affect system stability, flavor quality, and long-term storage safety; therefore, establishing a purification system that balances structural preservation with food-grade applicability is of great significance for the high-value utilization of SGPs.

Deproteinization and decolorization

The presence of proteins in crude extracts interferes with the determination of physicochemical properties and biological activity evaluation of polysaccharides; therefore, deproteinization is the primary step in the purification of SGPs (Yang et al., 2022). The Sevag method is the most widely used classical deproteinization technique for SGPs, offering mild conditions, minimal structural damage to polysaccharides, and relatively low operational cost, making it suitable for preliminary purification at the laboratory scale (Zhao et al., 2024). Gong et al. applied the Sevag method using a chloroform:n-butanol mixture (4,1, v/v) to deproteinize crude SGP-1-1, achieving a protein removal rate of over 85% and a polysaccharide retention rate of approximately 90% (Gong et al., 2022). Studies have shown that performing the Sevag method 3–5 times can remove 80%–95% of proteins from crude extracts. However, repeated extraction may lead to partial loss of polysaccharides, necessitating a balance between deproteinization efficiency and polysaccharide recovery. It should be noted that the organic solvents used in the Sevag method are toxic and volatile, requiring strict operational conditions and personnel protection, and posing environmental challenges related to solvent recovery and waste disposal in large-scale applications. In recent years, enzymatic hydrolysis has emerged as a promising green alternative for the purification of SGPs, offering the advantage of specific protein degradation by proteases under mild conditions, thereby achieving deproteinization without the use of organic solvents (Huang et al., 2021).

Pigment removal is another critical step in the purification of SGPs, as pigments such as phenolics, flavonoids, and carotenoids present in crude extracts not only affect the appearance quality of polysaccharides but may also interfere with subsequent bioactivity studies through oxidation reactions or covalent binding with polysaccharides (Tao, Zhang, et al., 2023). The macroporous resin adsorption method, due to its strong selective adsorption capacity, operational simplicity, and reusability, has become the mainstream technique for decolorizing SGPs. The principle involves utilizing van der Waals forces and hydrophobic interactions between hydrophobic groups on the resin matrix and pigment molecules to achieve selective adsorption of pigments (Tao, Wang, et al., 2023). Teng et al. employed AB-8 macroporous adsorbent resin for dynamic adsorption decolorization of crude SGFP, achieving highly significant results (Teng et al., 2025). However, current studies are largely limited to evaluating decolorization efficiency, lacking systematic analysis of whether resin adsorption simultaneously results in the loss of low-Mw polysaccharides or specific functional components. Moreover, certain phenolic compounds can be covalently bound or strongly attached to polysaccharide chains and thus are not easily removed by physical adsorption alone and may introduce bias into subsequent structural analyses. Future studies ought to be directed toward the development of green, scalable, and highly selective purification methods to be used to increase comparability and reliability of investigations about SGPs.

Column chromatography

Column chromatography is a core technique for the fine separation and purification of SGPs, based on differences in their partitioning between stationary and mobile phases, enabling effective separation of components with varying Mw, charges, and structural features (Zheng et al., 2023). Gel filtration chromatography is a classical size-based fractionation method, characterized by mild separation conditions, independence from charge properties, and the ability to simultaneously achieve desalting, and is therefore widely used for preparative separation while preserving the native conformation of polysaccharides (Zhang et al., 2022). Sephadex G-series and Sephacryl-series gels are commonly used in the separation of SGPs, with fractionation ranges covering Mw from 104 to 106 Da. Guo et al. obtained a homogeneous polysaccharide SGP-G with a Mw of 23.4 kDa using Sephadex G-100, while Zhu et al. isolated SGP-P with a Mw of 1930 kDa using Sephadex G-200, indicating that the choice of gel type plays a decisive role in fractionation outcomes (Guo et al., 2022b; Zhu et al., 2020). For SGPs fractions with similar Mw but different structural features, ion-exchange chromatography provides a more refined separation dimension. Ion-exchange chromatography plays an indispensable role in the separation of SGPs, particularly for distinguishing acidic and neutral polysaccharides and for fractionating components with different charge densities (Wang, Zhao, et al., 2024). SGPs are rich in acidic groups such as UA, rendering them negatively charged in solution, and are therefore commonly separated using DEAE-cellulose as an anion-exchange medium. Li et al. separated polysaccharide fractions with different degrees of acidity (SGP-W1, SGP-UAL1, and SGP-UAU1) using a DEAE-52 column, with UA contents of 43.30%, 28.91%, and 15.85%, respectively, exhibiting a clear gradient in acidity (Li, Zheng, et al., 2025). Overall, although current column chromatography strategies can achieve fractionation and purification of SGPs, the separation outcomes largely depend on molecular size or charge rather than true chemical structural units. This implies that polysaccharide fractions reported across different studies may not be strictly comparable in nature, but rather represent products shaped by the chosen separation methods. Therefore, future research should shift toward integrating multidimensional separation strategies, such as combining gel filtration and ion-exchange chromatography for two-dimensional separation, while incorporating techniques such as multi-angle light scattering (MALS) for structural cross-validation of SGPs.

Structural characteristics

Molecular weight

The Mw of polysaccharides, as a key parameter, is one of the core factors determining their biological activities (Chong et al., 2025). Studies have shown that SGPs exhibit a wide Mw distribution ranging from 13.4 to 1930 kDa. This pronounced heterogeneity arises from differences in extraction methods and raw material sources, and also reflects the high structural complexity of SGPs. SGPs obtained via the UAE generally exhibit relatively higher Mw distributions. Jiang et al. obtained a polysaccharide fraction SGP-U with a Mw of 1196 kDa via UAE, and its high-Mw value is attributed to the relatively weak shear effect of ultrasonic cavitation on polysaccharide chains during cell wall disruption, effectively avoiding excessive degradation (Jiang et al., 2026). In contrast, the polysaccharide SGP-E obtained via EAE exhibits a relatively lower Mw of 874 kDa. This may be because enzymatic hydrolysis not only disrupts cell wall structures but may also partially hydrolyze glycosidic linkages in the polysaccharide backbone or side chains, resulting in reduced Mw. In addition, the source of raw materials also leads to significant differences in Mw distribution. Studies have shown that, under the same extraction method, polysaccharides extracted from S. grosvenorii flowers generally exhibit a higher average Mw than those derived from fruits. For example, the Mw of SGF1 extracted from flowers via HWE is 621.849 kDa, whereas polysaccharide fractions extracted from fruits mostly range from 13.4 to 167 kDa (Dujnič et al., 2021). This may be attributed to differences in developmental stage and cellular structure between flowers and fruits, where polysaccharide biosynthesis in floral tissues tends to favor the formation of long-chain macromolecular structures, while polysaccharides in fruits may undergo partial enzymatic degradation or metabolic transformation during maturation, resulting in a shift of Mw distribution toward lower ranges (Hao et al., 2024). Moreover, chemical modification typically results in a significant reduction in the Mw of SGPs. For example, after sulfation modification, the Mw of SGP-1 decreased from 33 kDa to 13.4 kDa, which is mainly attributed to glycosidic bond cleavage induced by acidic conditions or free radical reactions during modification, leading to polysaccharide chain degradation (Gong et al., 2023). In summary, the Mw of SGPs is determined by multiple factors, and its distribution characteristics not only reflect intrinsic properties but are also significantly influenced by extraction and subsequent processing. Accurate determination and in-depth understanding of the Mw and its distribution of SGPs are of great importance for elucidating structure-activity relationships, optimizing extraction and purification processes, and promoting their applications in functional foods.

Monosaccharide composition

Monosaccharide composition is fundamental to elucidating the chemical structure of SGPs, and detailed analysis of monosaccharide types and molar ratios facilitates understanding of their biosynthetic pathways and structure-activity relationships (Wang, Zheng, et al., 2024). Existing studies indicate that SGPs exhibit considerable diversity in monosaccharide composition, mainly consisting of glucose (Glc), galactose (Gal), mannose (Man), arabinose (Ara), rhamnose (Rha), xylose (Xyl), and galacturonic acid (GalA), with some studies also detecting fucose (Fuc), glucuronic acid (GlcA), and ribose (Rib). Interestingly, polysaccharides extracted from different parts of S. grosvenorii exhibit significant differences in monosaccharide composition and proportions. Polysaccharides from S. grosvenorii flowers are typically dominated by Gal, accounting for 30%–60%; for example, Teng et al. obtained SGFP2 from flowers via HWE, with Gal content reaching 60.06% (Teng et al., 2025). In contrast, polysaccharides derived from S. grosvenorii fruits exhibit distinct monosaccharide distribution patterns; for example, SGP-W obtained via HWE contains up to 25.15% GalA, suggesting that fruit polysaccharides may be rich in pectic acidic polysaccharides, which are closely related to their structural roles in plant tissues (Jiang et al., 2026). The extraction method also significantly influences monosaccharide composition, with UAE generally better preserving acidic monosaccharide components compared to conventional HWE. For example, using the same batch of fruit material, the molar percentage of GalA in polysaccharide SGP-U obtained via UAE was 37.05%, significantly higher than the 22.85% obtained via HWE, which may be attributed to the cavitation effect of ultrasound that accelerates polysaccharide release while causing less structural damage to acidic polysaccharides (Jiang et al., 2026). Notably, alkaline extraction exerts the most pronounced effect on monosaccharide composition, with SGF-B composed of Glc, Gal, Ara, Man, and GalA in a molar ratio of 1.96:1.20:76.62:12.26:7.97. It can be observed that polysaccharides obtained via AE generally exhibit an increased proportion of neutral sugars and a marked decrease in GalA content, likely due to decarboxylation or β-elimination reactions of UA residues under alkaline conditions, resulting in the loss of acidic monosaccharides (Zhang et al., 2020). In summary, the monosaccharide composition of SGPs is determined by the combined effects of raw material characteristics, extraction processes, and intrinsic structural features. This compositional diversity not only reflects the specificity of polysaccharide biosynthesis in different tissues of S. grosvenorii but also provides a material basis for their diverse biological activities.

Structural characterization

The structural characteristics of SGPs are not only reflected in Mw and monosaccharide composition but are more profoundly manifested in their glycosidic linkage patterns. In-depth investigation of these structural elements is crucial for elucidating the structure-activity relationships underlying their biological activities (Yao et al., 2024). However, current studies on glycosidic linkages in SGPs mainly rely on combined analyses using methylation and nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) techniques, and the depth and consistency of structural elucidation remain limited. Existing studies indicate that the backbone structures of SGPs exhibit significant diversity, typically composed of α- or β-glycosidic linkages, predominantly (1 → 4) and (1 → 6) linkages, forming varying degrees of linear or branched structures. For example, Gong et al. obtained a neutral polysaccharide SGP-1-1, whose backbone consists of 4)-β-D-Glcp-(1 → 4)-, α-D-Glcp-(1 → 4)-, and 4)-Manp-(1 residues (Gong et al., 2022). This structure exhibits a typical mixed linear backbone composed of glucan and mannan units. Meanwhile, this polysaccharide possesses α-1 → 6-linked Gal and Glc side chains at the O-6 position, indicating a certain degree of branching, which may influence its solubility and spatial conformation. In contrast, the acidic polysaccharide SGP-P reported by Zhu et al. exhibits distinct structural features, with a backbone composed of galactose linked via α-(1 → 4) glycosidic bonds, and side chains including α-(1 → 6)-linked glucose and mannose, as well as α-(1 → 3)-linked galactose and arabinose residues (Zhu et al., 2020). This structural pattern is, to some extent, similar to the backbone of rhamnogalacturonan I (RG-I) type pectin. The Ara- and Gal-containing branched regions may contribute to distinct chain conformations and molecular recognition properties; however, their specific contribution to the immunomodulatory activity of SGPs remains to be directly established. In addition to the analysis of monosaccharide composition and glycosidic linkage patterns, elucidating the higher-order structures of SGPs is also essential for understanding the organization of polysaccharide chains in solution and how their spatial conformations influence functional properties. For example, two-dimensional nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy (2D NMR) can assist in determining sugar residue configurations, substitution sites, inter-residue linkages, and sequence information, thereby enabling more reliable reconstruction of polysaccharide backbone and branching structures (Shi et al., 2019). It should be noted that 2D NMR mainly provides structural information related to sugar residues and glycosidic linkages, but cannot directly reveal the overall spatial conformation of intact macromolecular polysaccharides. In contrast, atomic force microscopy (AFM) can directly visualize polysaccharide molecules deposited on substrates at the nanoscale and provide information regarding polysaccharide chain morphology, molecular aggregation, branching characteristics, and intermolecular association states. Parameters such as molecular height, apparent chain length, degree of chain entanglement, and aggregation morphology can provide complementary evidence for distinguishing extended, flexible, highly branched, or aggregated polysaccharide conformations (Kong et al., 2024). Therefore, 2D NMR and AFM can provide complementary information at different structural levels. 2D NMR is used to elucidate sugar residue connectivity and branching patterns, whereas AFM provides direct morphological evidence for the supramolecular organization of polysaccharides. Combining these techniques with molecular simulations and solution-state conformational analysis may enable a more comprehensive characterization of the three-dimensional structures of SGPs. However, studies involving such integrated high-resolution structural characterization of SGPs remain limited, and systematic evidence linking higher-order conformations with biological functions is still insufficient.

Available studies further indicate that the structural characteristics of SGPs vary according to botanical tissue origin, although these differences should not be regarded as absolute tissue-specific features because extraction, purification, and analytical procedures can also substantially affect the observed structural parameters. In general, flower-derived SGPs reported to date tend to exhibit relatively high Mw and are frequently characterized by a high proportion of neutral sugars, particularly Gal. For example, Gal accounted for up to 60.06% of SGFP2 isolated from S. grosvenorii flowers (Teng et al., 2025). In comparison, fruit-derived SGPs show greater compositional heterogeneity and more frequently contain appreciable amounts of uronic acids, particularly GalA, suggesting the presence of acidic or pectin-associated structural domains (Jiang et al., 2026). Therefore, the presently available evidence suggests a tendency toward Gal-rich neutral structural features in flower-derived SGPs and relatively greater representation of GalA-containing acidic domains in fruit-derived SGPs. Nevertheless, direct comparison among studies should be made cautiously because differences in extraction conditions and fractionation procedures may selectively enrich polysaccharides with different molecular sizes and chemical compositions.

The purification status of SGP preparations is another critical factor that should be explicitly considered when comparing structural characteristics and establishing structure-activity relationships. Crude SGP preparations obtained after aqueous extraction and ethanol precipitation generally consist of multiple polysaccharide populations with different Mw distributions and may additionally contain residual proteins, phenolic compounds, pigments, and other macromolecular components (Guo et al., 2022b). Consequently, the Mw and monosaccharide composition reported for crude preparations usually represent ensemble characteristics rather than those of a chemically defined polysaccharide molecule. In contrast, homogeneous or relatively homogeneous fractions obtained after deproteinization and chromatographic separation generally show narrower Mw distributions and more clearly defined monosaccharide compositions and glycosidic linkage patterns, thereby providing a more appropriate basis for detailed structural elucidation and structure-activity analysis (Li, Zheng, et al., 2025). Accordingly, biological activities observed for crude polysaccharide mixtures should not be directly attributed to a specific structural feature or directly compared with those of purified homogeneous fractions. Future structure-activity relationship studies should preferentially employ well-purified and structurally defined SGP fractions and clearly report their purification status to improve comparability across studies.

Structural modification

Structural modification is considered an important strategy for enhancing the biological activity and expanding the application potential of SGPs. Structural modification of polysaccharides through chemical, physical, or biological approaches allows the targeted introduction of functional groups, alteration of Mw, or adjustment of chain conformation (Deng et al., 2020; H. Huang & Huang, 2020). At present, structural modification studies of SGPs mainly focus on chemical modification, among which sulfation and phenolic acid modification are the most widely applied approaches. Studies have shown that Gong et al. modified SGPs using the chlorosulfonic acid-pyridine method to obtain sulfated derivatives with a degree of substitution (DS) of approximately 0.62, accompanied by a significant decrease in Mw, indicating partial degradation of polysaccharide chains during modification (Gong et al., 2023). In addition, hydroxyl groups (–OH) are substituted by sulfate groups (–OSO3−), conferring a stronger negative charge to the molecule. These changes are essentially attributed to electrostatic repulsion and steric hindrance induced by the introduction of sulfate groups, thereby improving chain expansion and solubility. In terms of biological activity, sulfation modification can significantly enhance antioxidant activity. In another study, Duan et al. conjugated small-molecule phenolic acids to the polysaccharide backbone via ester bonds, obtaining FA-SGP and CA-SGP. The most significant structural change is the introduction of aromatic moieties and redox-active functional groups (Duan et al., 2025). These structural changes may further influence their water-holding capacity, gelation behavior, and stability under oxidative conditions. However, it should be noted that the phenolic acid-modified system is essentially no longer a single polysaccharide, but is more appropriately regarded as a “polysaccharide-polyphenol complex system.” Therefore, the enhanced antioxidant activity may partially originate from the intrinsic redox properties of the phenolic acids themselves, rather than being entirely attributable to the structural optimization of the polysaccharide. Although chemical modification has demonstrated considerable potential in improving the functional properties of SGPs, its limitations should not be overlooked. Conventional chemical modification generally requires strongly acidic or highly reactive conditions, which can readily induce glycosidic bond cleavage and uncontrollable reductions in Mw, thereby affecting the native conformation and food-related functional properties of SGPs. Moreover, some chemical reagents present potential toxicity and environmental pollution concerns, which further restrict their practical application in food systems. Future research should place greater emphasis on green, mild, and controllable structural engineering strategies, such as enzymatic modification, physical field-assisted modification, and biocatalytic systems, thereby promoting the precise application of SGPs in the development of functional foods.

Structural transformation of SGPs during gastrointestinal digestion and fermentation

Gastrointestinal digestion and microbial fermentation are critical transformation processes that determine the in vivo functional expression of SGPs. Unlike small-molecule bioactive components, the biological activities of polysaccharides are highly dependent on their structural alterations during gastrointestinal processing (Jiang et al., 2023). Therefore, digestion and fermentation should not be regarded merely as degradation processes, but rather as dynamic structural transformation processes that may preserve, modify, or weaken functional domains. Understanding the relationship between structural changes of SGPs during gastrointestinal transit and their subsequent biological effects is an important foundation for establishing physiologically relevant structure-activity relationships.

The structural transformation of SGPs during gastrointestinal digestion and subsequent colonic fermentation is an important factor affecting their bioaccessibility, intestinal delivery behavior, and functional expression (Zhang et al., 2021). Existing studies indicate that SGPs undergo only limited degradation during simulated human digestion, whereas they are further extensively utilized by gut microbiota during colonic fermentation (Guo et al., 2022b). During in vitro simulated gastrointestinal digestion, SGPs exhibit partial depolymerization, accompanied by a significant decrease in Mw. For example, Guo et al. reported that after complete simulated digestion, the Mw of SGPs decreased from 23.00 kDa to 7.82 kDa, accompanied by a decline in total polysaccharide content, indicating cleavage of some glycosidic bonds under the action of gastric acid and digestive enzymes (Guo et al., 2022a). However, a considerable proportion of SGPs remain structurally intact and are not fully degraded. This resistance to digestion may be attributed to the complex polysaccharide structures of SGPs and the absence of cleavage sites recognizable by mammalian digestive enzymes, allowing them to generally withstand digestion in the upper gastrointestinal tract. High-Mw fractions, branched structures, and specific glycosidic linkage types may contribute to enhanced digestive stability and facilitate the delivery of SGPs to the colon (Zhou et al., 2022). However, the specific structural domains that remain intact and reach the colon are still not clearly understood, as most studies have focused primarily on overall changes in Mw and monosaccharide composition rather than tracking the dynamic alterations of specific structural regions during digestion. From a food chemistry perspective, this structural stability not only affects the delivery efficiency of SGPs within the gastrointestinal environment but may also further determine their rheological behavior, viscosity retention capacity, and interaction patterns with digestive enzymes.

In contrast, the colonic fermentation stage exerts a more pronounced impact on the structure of SGPs. During in vitro fecal fermentation, the Mw of SGPs continuously decreases, while monosaccharide composition undergoes dynamic changes, characterized by an initial decrease followed by stabilization in the relative content of certain monosaccharides (e.g., Glc), indicating gradual degradation and selective utilization by gut microbiota. This process primarily depends on carbohydrate-active enzymes (CAZymes) secreted by gut microbiota, which specifically cleave different glycosidic bonds in polysaccharides to release fermentable oligosaccharides and monosaccharides (Guo et al., 2022b). Notably, these structural transformations are closely associated with functional changes. During colonic fermentation, metabolites produced by gut microbiota through the utilization of SGPs, particularly short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs), may serve as important mediators linking polysaccharide degradation with host physiological functions. SCFAs can influence glucose and lipid metabolism, intestinal barrier function, and immune regulation through signaling pathways involving G protein-coupled receptors (Xu et al., 2025). However, the specific structural features of SGPs that promote the production of beneficial metabolites remain unclear, limiting the establishment of direct relationships between SGP structures and host metabolic effects. Furthermore, variations in gut microbiota composition and metabolic capacity among individuals may result in considerable interindividual differences in the physiological effects induced by SGPs. Specific microbial communities with the capacity to degrade complex polysaccharides may determine the fermentation efficiency of SGPs, SCFA production patterns, and subsequent host responses. Therefore, individualized responses should be considered when evaluating the intervention effects of SGPs, and metagenomics, metabolomics, and functional evaluation approaches should be integrated to identify microbial signatures associated with responses to SGPs.

Overall, gastrointestinal digestion and microbial fermentation reshape the structural characteristics and functional potential of SGPs. Therefore, structure-activity relationship analyses of SGPs should not focus solely on their original structures but should also consider digestion-induced structural alterations and microbiota-mediated metabolic transformations, as both factors jointly determine the ultimate biological effects of SGPs in the physiological environment. Future studies should focus on the dynamic changes of specific structural domains of SGPs during digestion and determine which structural units can remain intact, reach the colon, and exert biological functions. Meanwhile, multi-omics approaches should be integrated to elucidate the relationships among SGP structural characteristics, gut microbiota composition, and functional metabolites, and to clarify the mechanisms underlying differential individual responses to SGPs. This will provide more accurate structure-activity relationship evidence for the targeted development of SGPs-based functional foods and promote the precise development and rational application of SGPs-related health products.

Hypoglycemic activity

The hypoglycemic activity of SGPs is one of their most important pharmacological properties and has attracted widespread attention in recent years. Numerous in vivo and in vitro studies have demonstrated that SGPs exert hypoglycemic effects through multiple pathways, showing great potential as adjunct therapies for diabetes or as functional food ingredients for glycemic control (Fig. 3). For example, Lin et al., using male New Zealand white rabbits as a model, found that SGPs significantly reduced fasting blood glucose levels in a dose-dependent manner. At a dose of 100 mg·kg−1, fasting blood glucose decreased from 7.36 ± 0.76 mmol·L−1 to 4.52 ± 0.37 mmol·L−1 (Lin et al., 2007). In vitro studies indicate that SGP-1-1 can significantly inhibit α-glucosidase activity, thereby delaying carbohydrate digestion and Glc release, and reducing postprandial blood glucose levels (Gong et al., 2022). In addition, SGFP-2 exhibits significant inhibitory effects on both α-amylase and α-glucosidase, suggesting that it may regulate intestinal Glc absorption through a multi-target mechanism (Teng et al., 2025). Beyond enzyme inhibition, SGPs may also exert hypoglycemic effects by improving insulin sensitivity. Gong et al., using insulin-resistant (IR) HepG2 cells as a model, found that SGP-1-1 significantly promoted glucose uptake and utilization, increased the activities of hexokinase (HK) and pyruvate kinase (PK), and enhanced glycogen synthesis, indicating that it improves Glc metabolism by enhancing glycolytic flux and storage capacity (Gong et al., 2022).

Schematic illustration of the potential mechanisms underlying the hypoglycemic and anti-glycation activities of SGPs. (A) Hypoglycemic activity. SGPs regulate glucose metabolism through multiple pathways, including inhibition of carbohydrate-hydrolyzing enzymes, enhancement of glucose uptake, improvement of insulin sensitivity, and regulation of key metabolic enzymes such as hexokinase (HK) and pyruvate kinase (PK). (B) Anti-glycation activity. SGPs inhibit the formation of advanced glycation end products (AGEs) by scavenging reactive oxygen species (ROS), reducing oxidative stress (OS), chelating metal ions, and interfering with carbonyl-mediated glycation reactions.

In summary, the hypoglycemic effects of SGPs are manifested as a synergistic outcome of multiple mechanisms, including enzyme inhibition, insulin sensitization, and regulation of key enzymes involved in glucose metabolism. However, current studies are largely limited to in vitro enzymatic assays and cellular models, and the in vivo mechanisms remain insufficiently validated, particularly regarding the relative contributions of different mechanisms to the overall hypoglycemic effect. The specific molecular basis underlying the improvement of insulin sensitivity by SGPs, such as the involvement of insulin signaling pathways, remains unsupported by direct evidence. Future studies should integrate molecular biology techniques to systematically investigate the regulatory effects of SGPs on phosphorylation of key molecules in insulin signaling pathways and downstream gene expression, in order to elucidate the molecular mechanisms underlying their insulin-sensitizing effects.

Antihyperlipidemic activity

Dyslipidemia is a major risk factor for atherosclerosis, fatty liver, and cardiovascular diseases, and the potential of SGPs in regulating lipid metabolism has attracted considerable attention (Han et al., 2024). Lin et al., using a high-fat diet-induced New Zealand rabbit model, found that SGP treatment significantly reduced plasma total cholesterol (TC) and triglyceride (TG) levels, while increasing high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), demonstrating a clear lipid-lowering effect (Lin et al., 2007). Mechanistically, the lipid-lowering effects of SGPs may be associated with their ability to ameliorate lipid metabolism disorders and enhance insulin sensitivity. On one hand, SGPs reduce TG levels, thereby alleviating lipotoxic interference with insulin signaling and indirectly improving glucose metabolism. On the other hand, the increase in HDL-C suggests that SGPs may promote reverse cholesterol transport, thereby reducing the risk of atherosclerosis. In another study, SGPs exhibited potential lipid metabolism-regulating effects; however, the underlying mechanisms remain insufficiently elucidated. Several potential mechanisms, including bile acid binding, regulation of cholesterol transport, and pancreatic lipase inhibition, are mainly derived from studies on dietary fiber polysaccharides with similar physicochemical properties (Teng et al., 2025). Although the structural characteristics of SGPs suggest similar functional potential, direct experimental evidence demonstrating that SGPs regulate lipid metabolism through these specific pathways is currently lacking. Therefore, these mechanisms should be regarded as plausible hypotheses rather than experimentally validated mechanisms of action for SGPs.

Overall, SGPs exhibit significant hypolipidemic potential, with effects not only reflected in reductions of TC and TG levels but also in increasing HDL-C and restoring overall lipid homeostasis. However, conclusions regarding the hypolipidemic effects of SGPs are mainly derived from single animal models and changes in serum lipid biochemical parameters, lacking direct validation of key regulatory nodes in lipid metabolism. Furthermore, the proposed role of SGPs in promoting reverse cholesterol transport is mainly based on changes in HDL-C levels or analogical inference from dietary fiber polysaccharides, while direct evidence regarding the regulation of transporter protein expression or bile acid metabolic kinetics remains lacking. Future studies should employ purified SGPs fractions and combine them with targeted functional assays, such as bile acid binding capacity analysis, pancreatic lipase inhibition assays, and evaluation of lipid metabolism-related molecular markers, to determine whether these pathways directly contribute to the lipid-regulating effects of SGPs.

Immunomodulatory activity

Immune system homeostasis depends on the coordinated regulation of innate and adaptive immunity, with macrophages serving as key effector cells that play a central role in immune recognition (Chen et al., 2023). Studies have shown that SGPs can significantly enhance immune function by activating macrophages (Fig. 4). Jiang et al., using murine RAW 264.7 cells as a model, found that SGPs significantly promoted nitric oxide (NO) production in a dose-dependent manner (Jiang et al., 2026). Meanwhile, SGPs significantly upregulate the secretion of inflammatory cytokines interleukin-6 (IL-6) and tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α), indicating effective activation of immune response pathways. Notably, SGP-B obtained via AE significantly enhances macrophage phagocytic activity (by approximately 29%), likely due to its α-glucan-like structural feature (→4)-α-Glcp-(1→), which may interact with pattern recognition receptors such as Toll-like receptors (TLRs) or Dendritic cell-associated C-type lectin 1 (Dectin-1) to activate downstream immune signaling pathways (Jiang et al., 2026). In vivo, Zhang et al. using BALB/c inbred mice demonstrated that SGP-Z significantly increased spleen and thymus indices, promoted splenocyte proliferation, and modulated cytokine levels, thereby enhancing immune function (Zhang et al., 2011). Furthermore, SGP-Z can increase macrophage phagocytic rate, elevate serum hemolysin levels, and promote lymphocyte transformation.

Schematic illustration of the potential mechanisms underlying the immunomodulatory and anti-tumor activities of SGPs. (A) Immunomodulatory activity. SGPs interact with immune cell receptors and activate macrophage-mediated immune responses, resulting in enhanced nitric oxide (NO) production, secretion of immune-related cytokines including interleukin-6 (IL-6) and tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α), and increased phagocytic activity. These effects contribute to immune enhancement through regulation of immune signaling pathways. (B) Anti-tumor activity. SGPs induce tumor cell apoptosis by regulating mitochondrial dysfunction, increasing pro-apoptotic protein expression, activating caspase cascades, and suppressing anti-apoptotic signaling.

In conclusion, SGPs exhibit significant immunoenhancing potential, involving multiple aspects such as macrophage activation, cytokine regulation, and enhanced phagocytic function. It is noteworthy that the upregulation of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-6 and TNF-α induced by SGPs is generally considered an immunoenhancing effect, but this phenomenon should also be cautiously evaluated for its potential risks (Lee et al., 2021). In the absence of clearly defined dose windows and differentiation of inflammatory contexts, excessive activation of inflammatory pathways may lead to immune imbalance or even chronic inflammatory responses. Future studies should clarify the regulatory thresholds of SGPs on pro-inflammatory cytokines under different immune states, and explore strategies to enhance immune responses at low doses while avoiding excessive inflammation at higher doses or under specific conditions.

Anti-inflammatory activity

Chronic inflammation is a fundamental pathological basis of various metabolic diseases, characterized by excessive release of inflammatory cytokines and imbalance in oxidative stress (Farias et al., 2025; Xia et al., 2024). Existing studies have demonstrated that SGPs exhibit significant anti-inflammatory effects in vivo (Fig. 5). In a diabetic nephropathy (DN) mouse model, SGP-1-1 intervention significantly reduced serum levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines TNF-α and IL-6 in a dose-dependent manner, with the most pronounced effect observed at medium doses (Gong et al., 2021). Meanwhile, SGP-1-1 effectively restored superoxide dismutase (SOD) activity and reduced malondialdehyde (MDA) levels, suggesting that it indirectly suppresses inflammation by regulating oxidative stress (OS) status. At the molecular level, the anti-inflammatory effects of SGPs are closely associated with the regulation of the TLR4/NF-κB signaling pathway. Studies have shown that SGP-1-1 significantly downregulates the expression of TLR4 and nuclear factor-kappa B subunit p65 (NF-κB p65), thereby inhibiting the transcription and release of downstream inflammatory cytokines such as TNF-α, IL-6, and interleukin-1 β (IL-1β), and ultimately attenuating the inflammatory cascade (Gong et al., 2021). Furthermore, previous studies have shown that SGPs can inhibit the activation of PI3K/Akt/IKK and mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling pathways, thereby suppressing the expression of iNOS and cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2), further enhancing their anti-inflammatory effects (Zhang et al., 2011). This multi-pathway synergistic regulatory mode indicates that SGPs not only block the initiation of inflammatory signaling but also suppress the amplification of inflammatory responses, achieving an overall anti-inflammatory effect.

Schematic illustration of the potential mechanisms underlying the anti-inflammatory activity of SGPs. SGPs alleviate inflammatory responses through regulation of multiple inflammation-associated signaling pathways. SGPs suppress Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4)-mediated nuclear factor-kappa B (NF-κB) activation and regulate PI3K/Akt/IKK and mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling pathways, thereby reducing the expression of inflammatory mediators, including tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α), interleukin-6 (IL-6), interleukin-1β (IL-1β), inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS), and cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2).

The above results indicate that SGPs can attenuate inflammatory responses, accompanied by reductions in TLR4 expression, NF-κB-related protein levels, and the production of pro-inflammatory factors. These findings suggest that TLR4/NF-κB-related signaling pathways may be involved in the anti-inflammatory effects mediated by SGPs. However, the current evidence is primarily based on changes in gene expression and protein levels, while direct validation through receptor blocking experiments, gene knockout models, or NF-κB nuclear translocation assays remains lacking. Therefore, it remains unclear whether SGPs directly regulate TLR4 signaling. Further mechanistic investigations are required to establish a causal relationship between SGP structural characteristics and TLR4/NF-κB-mediated inflammatory regulation.

Anti-tumor activity

Tumor initiation and progression are closely associated with uncontrolled cell proliferation and dysregulated apoptosis, and the induction of tumor cell apoptosis is considered one of the key mechanisms by which natural polysaccharides exert antitumor effects (Hu & Chen, 2024; Song et al., 2022). Recent studies have shown that SGPs exhibit significant antitumor potential in vitro, primarily by inhibiting tumor cell proliferation and inducing programmed cell death. Studies have found that SGPs can significantly inhibit the growth of human lung cancer cells (A549) and induce apoptosis. For example, the purified fraction SGP-1 significantly increases the apoptosis rate at certain concentrations, while its sulfated derivative S-SGP exhibits stronger antitumor activity (Gong et al., 2023). Under a concentration of 400 μg·mL−1, treatment with S-SGP for 48 h resulted in a total apoptosis rate of 65.18% in A549 cells, significantly higher than that of unmodified polysaccharides and control groups, with a clear dose-dependent trend. Mechanistic studies have demonstrated that SGP-1 can trigger apoptosis by regulating the Bcl-2/Bax ratio, activating the caspase cascade, and promoting the decrease of mitochondrial membrane potential (Gong et al., 2023). In addition, its antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties may indirectly inhibit tumor cell growth and metastasis by reducing OS and inflammation in the tumor microenvironment.

Although SGPs have demonstrated potential anti-tumor activity in various experimental models, most studies have primarily focused on their direct cytotoxic effects against tumor cell lines. These effects are generally characterized by the inhibition of tumor cell proliferation, induction of apoptosis, and regulation of apoptosis-related proteins. Compared with their direct cytotoxic effects, the immunomodulatory anti-tumor mechanisms of SGPs remain insufficiently investigated. As bioactive macromolecular polysaccharides, SGPs may exert anti-tumor effects through immunomodulatory mechanisms, including macrophage activation, regulation of cytokine production, enhancement of natural killer (NK) cell activity, and modulation of tumor-associated immune responses. Similar immune-mediated mechanisms have been widely reported for other plant-derived polysaccharides. However, direct evidence supporting these mechanisms in SGPs remains limited. Therefore, future studies should further investigate whether SGPs can modulate the tumor immune microenvironment by regulating immune cell activation and cytokine networks, rather than focusing solely on their apoptosis-inducing effects on tumor cells.

Anti-glycation activity

Non-enzymatic glycation is the primary pathway for the formation of advanced glycation end products (AGEs) in vivo and during food processing, and their excessive accumulation is closely associated with OS, intestinal barrier damage, and various metabolic diseases (Zheng et al., 2022). Recent studies have demonstrated that SGPs exhibit significant potential in inhibiting AGE formation and alleviating related damage. Long et al. investigated the inhibitory effect of SGP-L on AGE formation using a BSA-Fru model. The results showed that SGP-L can reduce oxidative reactions by chelating metal ions, thereby inhibiting the progression of glycation (Long et al., 2025). In addition, SGP-L exhibits competitive binding with BSA, interacting with key sites such as Asp108 and Arg144, thereby competing with Fru for protein binding sites and directly blocking AGE formation. These findings indicate that SGP-L not only acts through indirect antioxidant pathways but also interferes with glycation via site-specific competitive mechanisms at the molecular level. In a Caco-2 cell model, SGP-L effectively alleviated N-ε-(carboxymethyl)lysine (CML)-induced cellular damage, significantly reduced intracellular ROS levels, and regulated the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle (Long et al., 2025). These metabolic reprogramming processes help restore cellular energy metabolism balance and alleviate OS, thereby maintaining the integrity of the intestinal epithelial barrier.

At present, studies on the anti-glycation effects of SGPs mainly rely on the in vitro BSA-Fru model, which, although capable of simulating AGE formation, fails to reflect the complex protein glycation environment and metabolic background in vivo. Therefore, the physiological relevance of these results requires further validation. Particularly under in vivo conditions, AGE formation involves multiple glycation donors, protein substrates, and both enzymatic and non-enzymatic processes, whereas the role of SGPs within this complex system has not yet been systematically evaluated. Future studies should focus on integrating animal models and clinically relevant indicators (such as serum AGE levels and intestinal barrier function) to systematically evaluate the anti-glycation effects of SGPs.

Others

In addition, SGPs have demonstrated research potential in prebiotic and antioxidant aspects (Fig. 6). For example, preliminary studies on their prebiotic activity using in vitro simulated digestion-fermentation models have shown that SGPs undergo limited degradation in the upper gastrointestinal tract but are effectively utilized by gut microbiota in the colon, promoting the production of SCFAs, with concentrations increasing to 32–39 mM. Meanwhile, SGPs can modulate gut microbiota composition, promoting the enrichment of beneficial bacteria such as Bacteroides and Phascolarctobacterium, thereby enhancing intestinal metabolic activity and improving host health status (Guo et al., 2022a). In terms of antioxidant activity, SGPs exhibit strong scavenging capacity against various free radicals, including DPPH radicals, hydroxyl radicals (·OH), and superoxide anions (O2−·), with scavenging activity typically increasing in a concentration-dependent manner (Gong et al., 2022). At the cellular level, SGPs can significantly reduce intracellular ROS levels induced by H2O2 and decrease the proportion of apoptosis and necrosis, exhibiting notable cytoprotective effects (Zhu et al., 2020). In addition, phenolic acid-modified polysaccharide derivatives (FA-SGP and CA-SGP) exhibit enhanced antioxidant capacity, not only significantly improving free radical scavenging activity but also alleviating oxidative damage through regulation of the Bcl-2/Bax/Caspase-3 signaling pathway, suggesting that structural modification can effectively amplify antioxidant effects (Duan et al., 2025). In summary, current studies systematically demonstrate that SGPs exhibit significant biological activities across multiple aspects, including antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, immunomodulatory, and metabolic regulatory effects. However, the potential hepatoprotective and pulmonary protective effects of purified SGPs still lack systematic research evidence, representing an important direction for future studies.

SGPs exert antioxidant effects through multiple mechanisms, including direct scavenging of reactive oxygen species (ROS) and free radicals, enhancement of endogenous antioxidant defense systems, regulation of oxidative stress-related signaling pathways, and protection of cellular components from oxidative damage.

Current studies on the structure-activity relationship of SGPs are mainly focused on preliminary structural characterization and correlation with observed activities, lacking systematic and in-depth mechanistic analysis (See Table 3). Classification of structural parameters and their relationships with biological activities of SGPs.

The biological activities of SGPs may be closely associated with their Mw (You et al., 2024). Studies have shown that polysaccharides with lower-Mw generally exhibit significant antioxidant and hypoglycemic activities (Sun, Zhang, et al., 2024). This advantage may be associated with its lower Mw, as smaller molecular sizes generally contribute to improved water solubility, diffusion behavior, and bioavailability (Lee et al., 2024). For example, SGP-1-1, with an Mw of 19.037 kDa, exhibits strong in vitro antioxidant and hypoglycemic activities, effectively scavenging DPPH radicals (up to 89.12%) and significantly inhibiting α-glucosidase activity (93.34%) (Gong et al., 2022). In contrast, high-Mw SGPs, due to their larger molecular size and complex spatial structure, may exhibit lower solubility and higher viscosity, but show advantages in immunomodulatory activity (Tan et al., 2023). SGP-B (1174 kDa) can significantly enhance macrophage phagocytic activity, increasing it by 29% (Jiang et al., 2026). However, the effects of SGP molecular size on cellular uptake and specific biological targets require further validation. Notably, the relationship between Mw and antioxidant activity is not a simple linear negative correlation; SGP-P, with a Mw of 1930 kDa, can significantly reduce ROS production and alleviate oxidative damage (Zhu et al., 2020). Currently, no clear Mw threshold has been established to define transitions between different biological activities (e.g., from antioxidant to immunomodulatory effects). Existing evidence is mostly derived from comparisons among polysaccharide fractions obtained under different extraction conditions or structural backgrounds, making it difficult to analyze Mw as an independent variable. Therefore, attributing differences in activity solely to Mw may represent an oversimplification.

Monosaccharide composition is another important structural characteristic associated with the biological activities of SGPs. The types and proportions of monosaccharides may contribute to differences in the biological activities of polysaccharides by affecting molecular conformation, polarity, and biological recognition (Fan et al., 2026). Studies have shown that certain Glc-rich SGPs fractions exhibit enhanced immunomodulatory activity; however, whether glucose content itself is the primary determining factor remains unclear. SGP-B possesses a distinctive monosaccharide composition, with Glc content reaching 76.62%. This fraction can significantly promote macrophage secretion of NO and cytokines, exhibiting markedly stronger immunoactivating effects than fractions with different monosaccharide compositions (Jiang et al., 2026). This may be related to Glc residues acting as ligand structures for specific receptors, thereby more effectively triggering downstream immune signaling pathways (Li, Su, et al., 2025). Furthermore, higher proportions of Gal and Man are associated with favorable hypoglycemic activity (Sun, Yao, et al., 2024). In SGFP-2, Gal and Man account for 60.06% and 3.54%, respectively, which contribute to the inhibition of α-glucosidase and α-amylase (Teng et al., 2025). This suggests that Gal and Man may competitively inhibit enzyme-substrate binding and catalysis by forming hydrogen bonds or hydrophobic interactions with specific amino acid residues at the enzyme active site (Ren et al., 2025).

The types of glycosidic linkages and branching structures are also important structural parameters regulating the biological activities of SGPs (Jia et al., 2021). SGPs are mainly composed of glucans or glucomannans, with glycosidic linkages including →4)-Glcp-(1→, →6)-Glcp-(1→, and →4,6)-Glcp-(1→, and contain both α- and β-configurations, forming complex polysaccharide backbones with a certain degree of branching. It can be seen that →6 and →4,6 linkages serve as key branching sites that determine the degree of molecular branching. Such branched structures can extend the polysaccharide conformation and increase the exposure of active groups, which may enhance multivalent interactions with free radicals, digestive enzymes (α-glucosidase), and immune receptors. For example, SGP-1-1, with a backbone composed of 4)-β-D-Glcp-(1 → and 4)-Manp-(1 → and containing α-1,6 branches, exhibits significant antioxidant and hypoglycemic activities, which are considered to be closely related to its specific glycosidic linkage pattern (Gong et al., 2022). In addition, the coexistence of α- and β-glycosidic linkages in SGPs further enhances their conformational diversity. Generally, β-glycosidic linkages tend to form more rigid chain structures, whereas α-glycosidic linkages confer greater flexibility, enabling the formation of dynamically adjustable conformations that facilitate interactions with various biological targets (Chen et al., 2022; Peesapati & Roy, 2023).

Structural modification is an important strategy to improve the biological activities of SGPs, as the introduction of specific functional groups or alteration of molecular conformation can significantly enhance their interaction efficiency with biological targets (Cheng et al., 2023). The sulfated polysaccharide S-SGP (DS = 0.62) exhibits significantly enhanced antitumor and antioxidant activities (Gong et al., 2023). This may be attributed to the strong negative charge of sulfate groups, which alters the charge distribution of the polysaccharide molecules, facilitating their binding to positively charged cell membrane receptors or proteins and enhancing their free radical scavenging ability (Zhao et al., 2023). Moreover, phenolic acid modification introduces hydroxyl-rich aromatic structures, which may enhance the hydrogen-donating or electron-donating capacity of polysaccharides (Je et al., 2021). As a result, FA-SGP and CA-SGP exhibit significantly superior antioxidant activity and cytoprotective effects compared to the native polysaccharide (Duan et al., 2025).

Although an increasing number of studies have attempted to establish the structure-activity relationships of SGPs in recent years, the current evidence is still mainly derived from correlation analyses and lacks direct causal validation. Current conclusions regarding the structure-activity relationships of SGPs are largely based on comparisons of bioactivity differences among different isolated fractions, and such comparisons cannot completely exclude the interference of other confounding structural factors. In fact, multiple structural factors, including Mw, monosaccharide composition, glycosidic linkage types, and branching structures, often change simultaneously, making it difficult to accurately determine the independent contribution of individual structural parameters to biological activities. For example, some studies have found that high-Mw SGPs fractions exhibit stronger antioxidant or immunomodulatory activities. However, these high-Mw fractions usually also exhibit different monosaccharide ratios and specific branching structures. Therefore, it remains unclear whether the enhanced activities are mainly attributed to changes in Mw or jointly caused by other accompanying structural variations. In addition, some current interpretations regarding receptor recognition and immunomodulatory mechanisms of SGPs remain largely at the hypothetical level. Although these studies provide an important theoretical basis for understanding the relationship between polysaccharide structures and biological functions, direct mechanistic validation using structurally well-defined SGPs fractions remains insufficient.

Future studies on the structure-activity relationships of SGPs should advance from simple correlation analysis toward causal relationship elucidation. By preparing structurally homogeneous and well-characterized SGPs fractions, combined with high-resolution structural characterization, controlled structural modification, and systems biology approaches, the independent contributions of individual structural parameters to biological activities can be accurately elucidated, thereby clarifying the molecular mechanisms by which different structural features regulate bioactivity. In addition, techniques such as molecular docking can be integrated to intuitively reveal the relationship between the structural characteristics of SGPs and their interactions with biological target molecules, thereby elucidating the essence of their structure-activity relationships at the molecular level.

Food safety assessment

Safety assessment is a critical prerequisite for promoting the translation of SGPs from experimental research materials into functional food ingredients. Current studies suggest that native SGPs generally exhibit favorable safety profiles, although this conclusion is primarily based on limited evidence from cytotoxicity assays and acute toxicity studies in animals. For example, Long et al. conducted cytotoxicity evaluation using Caco-2 cells, employing the CCK-8 assay to assess the effects of SGPs (0.1–1.6 mg·mL−1) on intestinal epithelial cell viability after 24–72 h of treatment (Long et al., 2025). The results showed that SGPs exhibit good cytocompatibility, supporting their potential application as food additives. Moreover, in vivo studies involved intraperitoneal administration of SGPs at doses of 50, 100, 200, and 400 mg·kg−1, with subsequent measurement of thymus and spleen indices (Zhang et al., 2011). The findings revealed that SGP treatment caused a general increase in spleen and thymus index in mice, and no mortality was reported, no apparent behavioral abnormalities, and no atrophy of immune organs. These findings indicate that SGPs have a high short-term tolerability in dosage and administration. However, the existing safety evidence remains insufficient to support a comprehensive risk assessment, as most studies have focused on short-term toxicity, whereas the safety issues associated with long-term exposure, metabolic safety, and allergenic potential remain inadequately investigated. Future studies should conduct systematic evaluations of subchronic toxicity, chronic toxicity, and the long-term effects on gut microbiota to provide comprehensive and reliable safety evidence for the subsequent application of SGPs. In addition, the safety assessment of chemically modified SGPs should be further expanded. Although structural modification strategies, such as sulfation and phenolic acid conjugation, have been widely employed to improve the solubility and biological activities of SGPs, these modifications may introduce new functional groups, alter their molecular properties, or result in residual chemical reagents (Cui et al., 2022). Therefore, chemically modified SGPs should not be regarded as equivalent to native polysaccharides and require independent safety assessments, including chemical structure characterization, residual reagent analysis, cytotoxicity assessment, and animal toxicological studies. Moreover, from a regulatory perspective, the approval status of SGPs as food ingredients is also a critical factor influencing their industrial development. Although S. grosvenorii has been recognized as a medicinal and edible resource in many countries and regions, specific regulatory approvals and quality standards for purified SGPs as food ingredients remain limited. Future industrial applications will require the establishment of standardized manufacturing processes, quality evaluation systems, safety assessment procedures, and relevant regulatory frameworks.

Potential applications

Food applications

SGPs, as functional food ingredients, have shown broad application potential in the food industry in recent years. In staple food processing, raw materials rich in SGPs can be used for the development of wheat-based products such as steamed bread (Zhou et al., 2023). Initial research has demonstrated that the inclusion of SGPs may severely modify the rheological qualities of dough (e.g., greater water uptake and modifications in protein weakening characteristics), which influences the hardness, elasticity, and cohesiveness of the resultant products. SGPs have also been applied in the dairy system as natural low-calorie sweeteners in yogurt (Utami et al., 2024). In comparison to sucrose, SGPs offer the same sweetness but with a low amount of caloric content, thus suggesting that they can satisfy the commercial requirements of various functional foods as ingredients. In addition to existing applications, it is possible to incorporate SGPs into broader food processing systems and products. As an illustration, in baked goods, SGPs can be used as fat replacers, enhancing the texture and mouthfeel of cakes and biscuits with their water- and oil-holding capacities, decreasing the fat content, and complying with healthy eating trends (Long et al., 2025). In the beverage industry, SGPs can be added to juices, tea beverages, or plant-based drinks to enhance nutritional value, impart anti-inflammatory and prebiotic functions, and partially replace sucrose with their natural sweetness to develop low or sugar-free beverages that meet consumer demand for healthier options. In addition, SGPs can be utilized as natural preservatives in the food industry. Their antioxidant properties have the potential to increase the shelf life by preventing lipid oxidation and pigment degradation in foods, thus lowering the loss of flavor and nutrients due to oxidative activities. They possess a lot of potential in meat processing and the preservation of vegetables. SGPs could also be significant in precision nutrition in the future, such as personalized formulation using specific probiotics, dietary fibers, or functional ingredients to create customized nutritional foods for different populations and to achieve specific health interventions.

Pharmacological applications

In the field of pharmacology, SGPs exhibit great potential as candidate drugs or pharmaceutical excipients due to their diverse biological activities (Ma et al., 2024). Government data released by the State Administration for Market Regulation (https://www.samr.gov.cn/) has reported multiple pharmaceutical formulations containing SGPs as major components, including oral liquids, granules, capsules, and tablets. This gives initial government acknowledgment and approval of the clinical use of SGPs as active drug ingredients. Concerning particular pharmacological effects, the hypoglycemic effect of SGPs has been extensively reported upon, and with continued investigation, SGPs have emerged as significant natural drugs in creating new antidiabetic agents. As an illustration, SGPs can substantially lower the level of blood glucose in diabetic rabbits and postpone the digestion and uptake of carbohydrates (Lin et al., 2007). Moreover, SGPs have a strong immunomodulatory effect, which strongly stimulates splenocyte growth, increases phagocytic activity of macrophages, and controls the levels of various cytokines (Jiang et al., 2026; Lee et al., 2021). This implies that SGPs could be used as immunoenhancers in the adjuvant therapy of diseases that are associated with immunodeficiency, and they might have specific effects due to the selective regulation of immunity. Diseases caused by inflammation and oxidative damage are significant health issues, and SGPs can have significant roles in treating them. SGPs are able to scavenge reactive oxygen species (ROS) and stimulate antioxidant enzyme activity, which means that they can be considered as a potential treatment and prevention of OS-related conditions (Gong et al., 2022). Moreover, SGPs have the ability to control the signaling pathways of inflammation (Gong et al., 2021). Future research can be aimed at the development of specific therapeutic formulations of inflammatory bowel diseases (ulcerative colitis), the ability of SGPs to synergize their anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties to reduce the damage to the intestinal mucosa, and enhance the recovery of intestinal inflammation. With further research progress, the potential applications of SGPs across various fields are likely to expand, providing new therapeutic strategies for a range of challenges.

Other potential applications

Along with food and pharmacological applications, SGPs have the potential to be promising in other areas because of the unique physicochemical properties and biological activities (Huang et al., 2024). The antioxidant effects of SGPs in the cosmetics and personal care sector make them a perfect source of natural antioxidants, which can be used in anti-aging creams and serums to scavenge intracellular ROS in skin cells, prevent OS induced by exposure to ultraviolet light and environmental stress, and slow aging of the skin. Meanwhile, they enable the development of mild moisturizing products or formulations to be used on sensitive skin because of their good moisturizing properties and biocompatibility, which can be used to enhance the skin hydration and barrier performance (Li, Zheng, et al., 2025). Moreover, SGPs have anti-inflammatory effects that could be used to relieve skin inflammation and calm sensitive skin, indicating new avenues in the development of functional cosmetics. Moreover, in biomaterials, structural diversity and modifiability of SGPs create new opportunities to apply them in biomedical materials. SGPs have been used in the creation of functional microspheres (pellets) that have found application in the control of tobacco smoke. These materials have the ability to not only adsorb undesirable elements in smoke, but also deliver pharmacologically active elements (Zhang et al., 2026). Research has revealed that these microspheres have the ability to adsorb and store toxic substances in smoke, like tar and nicotine, and that their adsorption capacity can be optimized further by manipulating pore size and surface characteristics, offering a new material-based solution to limiting smoking-related damage. The adsorption properties of SGPs are also of interest in the area of environmental protection. Some polysaccharides, owing to the existence of numerous functional groups comprising hydroxyl and carboxyl groups, have adsorption potential on heavy metal ions as well as organic pollutants in water (Long et al., 2025). Through proper adjustment, SGPs can be used as environmentally friendly adsorbents to treat wastewater and remediate the environment, as they can increase their adsorption efficiency and selectivity to a given pollutant, offering new methods and materials to combat water pollution. Exploration of these potential application areas will further expand the value of SGPs and provide broader prospects for their comprehensive utilization.

Conclusion and prospects

In recent years, SGPs, as natural polysaccharides with abundant availability, favorable biocompatibility, and promising food-grade application potential, have attracted extensive attention in the fields of functional foods and food chemistry. Their complex structural characteristics, including monosaccharide composition, glycosidic linkage types, branching structures, and Mw distribution, collectively influence their physicochemical properties and functional performance, enabling promising applications in antioxidant activity, immunomodulation, hypoglycemic regulation, and gut health modulation. In addition, structural modification strategies such as sulfation and phenolic acid grafting can further regulate the solubility, charge characteristics, and interfacial behavior of SGPs, providing new approaches for improving their functional stability and expanding their applications in food systems. These studies provide a solid theoretical foundation for the application of SGPs in functional food development, novel drug discovery, and various practical fields.

Despite significant progress in SGP research, several key issues remain to be addressed. First, in-depth elucidation of structure-activity relationships remains a central challenge. Current understanding of the relationship between SGPs' structure and biological activity is largely based on correlation analyses, lacking mechanistic insight into how specific structural units deterministically influence their bioactivity. Future studies should employ advanced structural characterization techniques (such as high-resolution NMR, atomic force microscopy, and molecular simulation), combined with genetic engineering or chemical synthesis, to construct structurally defined SGP analogs or fragments and systematically investigate the effects of individual structural variables on biological activity. Second, a detailed investigation of pharmacological mechanisms needs to be strengthened. Although multiple biological activities of SGPs have been observed, their specific intracellular signaling pathways, key regulatory molecules, and systemic effects and metabolic processes in whole-animal models still require more in-depth and systematic elucidation, especially the network regulatory mechanisms involved in complex physiological processes such as immunomodulation and anti-inflammation. Third, the safety evaluation system needs further improvement. Although current studies suggest that SGPs have a favorable safety profile, there are still significant gaps in toxicity data under long-term and high-dose exposure, as well as in safety evaluations for special populations. Future studies should conduct comprehensive toxicological evaluations in accordance with GLP standards. Finally, breakthroughs in key industrialization technologies remain an important prerequisite for promoting the high-value utilization of SGPs. At present, SGPs still face several challenges in extraction, purification, and quality control, including insufficient efficiency, relatively high costs, and limited batch-to-batch stability. Future studies should further develop green, efficient extraction and separation technologies suitable for industrial food production, while also establishing standardized quality evaluation systems. In addition, integrating delivery systems and precision nutrition strategies will help promote the translation of SGPs from basic research to functional food and food industry applications.

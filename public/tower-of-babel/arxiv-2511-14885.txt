A strong quantitative form of the fractional isoperimetric inequality
Eleonora Cinti , Enzo Maria Merlino and Berardo Ruffini

Abstract
. We show a strong version of the fractional quantitative isoperimetric inequality, in which the isoperimetric deficit controls not only the Fraenkel asymmetry but also a sort of oscillation of the boundary. This generalizes the local result by Fusco and Julin in [ 21 ] . The proof follows a regularization process as in [ 21 ] but it is quite different in its spirit. Then, as a consequence of the quantitative inequality, we prove some stability estimates for a fractional Cheeger inequality.

Primary 49Q20, 49K40; Secondary 28A75, 49Q10.

1.
Introduction and main results

1.1.
Background: local and nonlocal quantitative isoperimetric inequalities

In recent years, interest in studying the stability of isoperimetric-type inequalities has been steadily increasing. After early contributions dating back to the beginning of the last century (see, e.g.,
[
2
,
5
]
), the first quantitative version of the isoperimetric inequality in arbitrary dimension was proved by Fuglede in
[
23
]
. He showed that if
E\subset\R^{n}
is a
nearly spherical set
, i.e., a Lipschitz regular set with barycenter at the origin and volume equal to that of the unit ball
B_{1}
, such that

(1.1)

\partial E=\{z(1+u(z)):z\in\partial B_{1}\},

with
\|u\|_{W^{1,\infty}}
sufficiently small, then

(1.2)

\|u\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(\partial B_{1})}\leq C\left(P(E)-P(B_{1})\right),

for some constant
C>0
depending only on
n
. Here
P(\cdot)
denotes the perimeter in the sense of De Giorgi. From this estimate, Fuglede was able to deduce that the perimeter deficit
P(E)-P(B_{1})
also bounds from above the Hausdorff distance between
E
and
B_{1}
, provided that
E
is nearly spherical or convex.

However, the Hausdorff distance proves to be a too strong notion when dealing with general sets of finite perimeter. Hall in
[
27
]
replaced it by the so-called
Fraenkel asymmetry
index

\alpha(E):=\min_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\{\frac{|E\Delta B_{r}(y)|}{|B_{r}|}:|B_{r}|=|E|\right\},

obtaining a non-sharp stability estimate. A sharp quantitative inequality was later proved in
[
20
]
by Fusco, Maggi, and Pratelli, who showed that there exists a constant
\gamma(n)
such that for any set
E
of finite perimeter and measure

(1.3)

\alpha(E)^{2}\leq\gamma(n)\,\delta(E),

where
\delta(E)
denotes the
isoperimetric deficit
:

\delta(E):=\frac{P(E)-P(B_{r})}{P(B_{r})},\qquad\text{with }|B_{r}|=|E|.

Right after the work
[
20
]
, which employed symmetrization techniques, several alternative approaches have been developed. In
[
11
]
another proof of (
1.3
) was proposed, based on the so-called
Selection Principle
, which relies on the regularity theory of almost-minimizers for the perimeter. In
[
17
]
estimate (
1.3
) was generalized to the anisotropic perimeter via an optimal transportation approach, and in
[
12
]
, it was extended to the case of weighted perimeter in convex cones by employing ABP-type techniques.

In
[
21
]
, Fusco and Julin improved the quantitative inequality (
1.3
), by considering a stronger notion of asymmetry, which takes into account, not only the
L^{1}
distance between
E
and an optimal ball (namely, the Fraenkel asymmetry), but also the oscillation of the boundary.

More precisely, given a set of finite perimeter
E
, let us denote by
\partial^{*}E
the reduced boundary of
E
and by
\nu_{E}(x)
the measure theretic outer unit normal to
\partial^{*}E
at
x
. Given a ball
B_{r}(y)
with the same volume as
E
, for every point
x\in\partial^{*}E
, let

\pi_{y,r}(x):=y+r\frac{x-y}{|x-y|},\qquad\text{for all $x\not=y$},

be the projection of
x
on
\partial B_{r}(y)
. Fusco and Julin introduced the asymmetry index given by

A(E):=\min_{y\in\R^{n}}\biggl\{\frac{|E\Delta B_{r}(y)|}{|B_{r}|}+\biggl(\frac{1}{2P(B_{r})}\int_{\partial^{*}E}|\nu_{E}(x)-\nu_{B_{r}(y)}(\pi_{y,r}(x))|^{2}d{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}(x)\biggr)^{1/2}:|B_{r}|=|E|\biggr\},

and they proved that there exists a constant
C(n)
, such that for every set
E\subset\R^{n}
of finite perimeter

(1.4)

A(E)^{2}\leq C(n)\delta(E).

As remarked in
[
21
]
, the estimate (
1.4
) is equivalent to the estimate (
1.2
) for nearly spherical sets (see also (5.16) in
[
23
]
) and thus it generalizes Fuglede’s estimate to all sets of finite perimeter. In order to prove (
1.4
), they first observed that the following Poincaré-type inequality holds true (see
[
21
, Proposition 1.2]
)

(1.5)

A(E)+\delta(E)^{1/2}\leq c\,\beta(E),

for some
c=c(n)>0
, where

(1.6)

\beta(E):=\min_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\biggl\{\left(\frac{1}{2P(B_{r})}\int_{\partial^{*}E}\left|\nu_{E}(x)-\nu_{B_{r}(y)}(\pi_{y,r}(x))\right|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)\right)^{1/2}:|B_{r}|=|E|\biggr\}.

Thus, the proof of (
1.4
), reduces to show that

\beta(E)^{2}\leq C\,\delta(E),

for a suitable constant
C=C(n)
.

For further works concerning strong-type forms of the quantitative isoperimetric inequality, we refer, for instance, to
[
3
,
4
]
for different geometric settings, to
[
34
,
14
]
for the anisotropic perimeter, to
[
1
]
for the Gaussian setting, and to the recent contribution
[
10
]
addressing as well capillarity-type energies.

In this paper we investigate the nonlocal counterpart of the quantitative inequality (
1.4
).

The notion of fractional perimeter, introduced in
[
8
]
, is a variant of the classical notion of perimeter in the sense of De Giorgi, which takes into account long-range interactions between sets. Precisely, given
s\in(0,1)
, the
s
-perimeter of a measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is defined as

(1.7)

P_{s}(E):=\int_{E}\int_{E^{c}}\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{n+s}}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\chi_{E}\right]_{W^{s,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where
\chi_{E}
denotes the characteristic function of
E
and
[\cdot]_{W^{s,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
denotes the Gagliardo seminorm in the fractional Sobolev space
W^{s,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

The isoperimetric-type inequality for the
s
-perimeters, shown in
[
19
]
, states that for any measurable set
E\subseteq\R^{n}
with
|E|<\infty
, we have

(1.8)

\frac{P_{s}(E)}{|E|^{(n-s)/n}}\geq\frac{P_{s}(B_{1})}{|B_{1}|^{(n-s)/n}},

where equality holds if and only if
E
is a ball.

A non-sharp quantitative version of (
1.8
) was first established in
[
22
]
, where the authors proved that

\alpha(E)^{4/s}\leq C(n,s)\delta_{s}(E),

where

\delta_{s}(E):=\frac{P_{s}(E)-P_{s}(B_{r})}{P_{s}(B_{r})},\qquad\text{with }|B_{r}|=|E|,

is the
s
-isoperimetric deficit. Its sharp improvement was later obtained in
[
16
]
: for every
n\geq 2
and
s_{0}\in(0,1)
, there exists a positive constant
c_{Q}>0
, depending only on
n
and
s_{0}
, such that

(1.9)

\alpha(E)^{2}\leq c_{Q}\,\delta_{s}(E),

for
s\in[s_{0},1]
. It is worth stressing that estimate (
1.9
) holds uniformly with respect to
s
, as long as
s
is bounded away from zero. Therefore, thanks to the convergence results established in
[
6
]
, it also implies, in particular, the quantitative isoperimetric inequality for the classical perimeter as a limiting case.

As in
[
11
]
, the starting point to prove (
1.9
) is a Fuglede-type result stating that there exist two positive constants
\eta_{0}
and
c_{0}
, depending only on
n
, such that if
E
is a nearly spherical set as in (
1.1
), with
\|u\|_{C^{1}(\partial B_{1})}<\eta_{0}
, then for all
s\in(0,1)
,

(1.10)

P_{s}(E)-P_{s}(B_{1})\geq c_{0}\left([u]_{\frac{1+s}{2}}^{2}+s\,P_{s}(B_{1})\,\|u\|_{L^{2}(\partial B_{1})}^{2}\right),

where the Gagliardo seminorm
[u]_{\frac{1+s}{2}}
is defined by

[u]_{\frac{1+s}{2}}^{2}:=[u]_{H^{\frac{1+s}{2}}(\partial B_{1})}^{2}=\int_{\partial B_{1}}\int_{\partial B_{1}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y).

By the asymptotic result of
[
16
, (8.4)]
, the inequality (
1.10
) recovers Fuglede’s estimate (
1.2
) in the limit as
s\to 1
.

1.2.
Main results

The scope of this work is to derive a nonlocal quantitative isoperimetric inequality that fully exploits the presence of the complete fractional Sobolev norm appearing in (
1.10
). In pursuing this goal, the first difficulty to keep in mind is that, since a set with finite
s
-perimeter is only measurable, an asymmetry index based on the oscillation of the normals may not be well defined. Let us take a closer look at the index defined in (
1.6
). As observed in
[
21
]
, by applying the divergence theorem, one finds that

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial^{*}E}|\nu_{E}(x)-\nu_{B_{r}(y)}(\pi_{y,r}(x))|^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)&=\int_{\partial^{*}E}\left(1-\nu_{E}(x)\cdot\frac{x-y}{|x-y|}\right)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)\\
&=P(E)-\int_{E}\frac{n-1}{|x-y|}\,dx\,,\end{split}

where, as before,
\pi_{y,r}
denotes the radial projection onto
\partial B_{r}(y)
. Therefore, we can write

(1.11)

\beta(E)^{2}=\frac{1}{P(B_{r})}\left(P(E)-(n-1)V(E)\right),\qquad\text{with }|B_{r}|=|E|,

where we have set

V(E):=\max_{y\in\R^{n}}\int_{E}\frac{1}{|x-y|}\,dx\,.

With this motivation in mind, in the nonlocal setting, fixed
s\in(0,1)
, and a measurable set
E
, we define

(1.12)

\beta_{s}(E):=\left(\frac{1}{P_{s}(B_{r})}\left(P_{s}(E)-c_{n,s}V_{s}(E)\right)\right)^{1/2}\qquad\text{with }|B_{r}|=|E|,

where

(1.13)

V_{s}(E):=\max_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{E}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{s}}\,dx

and
c_{n,s}:=\frac{P_{s}(B_{1})(n-s)}{n\omega_{n}}
is the normalization constant that ensures that
\beta_{s}(B)=0
, for every ball
B
. As we will stress later on, by the fractional isoperimetric inequality and by the Riesz rearrangement inequality, altogether with their rigidity cases, one can see that
\beta_{s}(E)>0
for all sets
E
which are not balls. Consequently, in analogy with the local case, we define the following asymmetry index

(1.14)

A_{s}(E):=\min_{y\in\R^{n}}\biggl\{\frac{|E\Delta B_{r}(y)|}{|B_{r}|}+\biggl(\frac{1}{P_{s}(B_{r})}\left(P_{s}(E)-c_{n,s}\int_{E}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{s}}\,dx\right)\biggr)^{1/2}:|B_{r}|=|E|\biggr\}.

Then, our main result reads as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

For every
n\geq 2
and
s\in(0,1)
there exists a positive constant
c(n,s)
such that

(1.15)

A_{s}(E)^{2}\leq C_{0}(n,s)\delta_{s}(E)

whenever
0<|E|<\infty
.

We will observe, in Remark
6.3
, that the constant
C_{0}(n,s)
appearing in the above quantitative estimate is uniformly bounded as
s\rightarrow 1^{-}
. Hence, our main result recovers the analog estimate in the classical local setting.

Some comments on the proof are in order.

In the local case treated in
[
21
]
, the main strategy is to reduce the problem to the case of nearly spherical sets, so as to exploit Fuglede’s inequality (
1.2
). This typical approach, introduced in
[
11
]
, is generally based on an appropriate notion of perimeter almost minimizers. However, in addition to using the regularity theory for perimeter almost minimizers, the authors in
[
21
]
also rely on properties of perimeter quasi-minimizers. This latter relaxed notion of perimeter minimizers is the natural counterpart, within the theory of sets of finite perimeter, of the notion of quasi-minima introduced by Giaquinta and Giusti in
[
24
]
for variational integrals. Although quasi-minimality is a considerably weaker condition than almost minimality, it still guarantees a mild degree of regularity. In particular, quasi-minimizers are uniformly porous (see, e.g.,
[
13
,
29
]
), a property which plays a key role in upgrading convergence in
L^{1}
to convergence in the Hausdorff sense. This strategy does not appear to lend itself to a clear or straightforward extension to the nonlocal setting. Therefore, in this work, we adopt a slightly different approach, which we present in the context of the
s
-perimeter, though it is equally applicable to the classical perimeter. Let us summarize the main ideas underlying our method.

First, we aim to establish the following Poincaré-type inequality:

(1.16)

A_{s}(E)+\delta_{s}(E)^{1/2}\leq C\beta_{s}(E)

for some constant
C=C(n,s)>0
. This inequality represents the fractional counterpart of (
1.5
). In light of (
1.16
), the proof of the sharp quantitative estimate (
1.15
) reduces to showing that

(1.17)

\beta_{s}(E)^{2}\leq C\delta_{s}(E),

for a suitable constant
C=C(n,s)>0
.

Next, recalling the definitions of
\beta_{s}
and
V_{s}
, we observe that

(1.18)

\begin{split}{\beta^{2}_{s}(E)}&=\frac{1}{P_{s}(B_{r})}\left(P_{s}(E)-c_{n,s}V_{s}(E)\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{P_{s}(B_{r})}\left(P_{s}(E)-P_{s}(B_{r})+P_{s}(B_{r})-c_{n,s}V_{s}(E)\right)\\
&=\delta_{s}(E)+\frac{c_{n,s}}{P_{s}(B_{r})}(V_{s}(B_{r})-V_{s}(E))={\delta_{s}(E)+\zeta_{s}(E)}.\end{split}

where we have set

(1.19)

\zeta_{s}(E):=\frac{c_{n,s}}{P_{s}(B_{r})}(V_{s}(B_{r})-V_{s}(E))=\frac{V_{s}(B_{r})-V_{s}(E)}{V_{s}(B_{r})}.

Hence, the proof of (
1.17
) is reduced to demonstrating that there exists a constant
C_{n,s}>0
such that

(1.20)

C_{n,s}\zeta_{s}(E)\leq\delta_{s}(E).

Since both
\zeta_{s}(E)
and
\delta_{s}(E)
are scale invariant by definition, it suffices to establish (
1.20
) under the normalization
|E|=\omega_{n}
, and thus we may take
B_{r}=B_{1}
. That is, we reduce the problem to prove that

C_{n,s}(V_{s}(B_{1})-V_{s}(E))\leq P_{s}(E)-P_{s}(B_{1}),

which is equivalent to

P_{s}(B_{1})+C_{n,s}V_{s}(B_{1})\leq P_{s}(E)+C_{n,s}V_{s}(E),

for some constant
C_{n,s}>0
.

At this point, the main idea, inspired by the approach in
[
15
]
, is to rephrase the problem in terms of a minimization problem for a new variational functional. Specifically, we seek for a quantity
\varepsilon_{1}>0
, depending only on
n
and
s
, such that for every
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{1}
, the unit ball is the unique minimizer of the energy functional

(1.21)

\min_{|E|=\omega_{n}}\left\{\mathcal{E}_{s}(E):=P_{s}(E)+\varepsilon\,V_{s}(E)\right\}.

We stress that the energy functional
\mathcal{E}_{s}
, which is the main object of study of this work, depends on the parameter
\varepsilon
, but we do not write explicitly such dependence just for simplicity of notation.

In order to carry out our strategy, we first prove the existence of minimizers for (
1.21
) via a generalized formulation inspired by the concentration-compactness principle of Lions
[
32
]
, recently adapted to the nonlocal setting (see, for instance,
[
15
,
9
,
26
,
35
]
). This approach, combined with the stability estimate (
1.9
), allows us to establish that minimizers of (
1.21
) are close to a ball in the
L^{1}
-topology.

To show that balls are indeed the unique (rigid) minimizers, we then exploit certain regularity properties of
s
-perimeter almost minimizers. More precisely, we need to argue in two steps. In a first step, we show that minimizers of the energy functional in (
1.21
) are indeed
(\rho,R)
-minimizers
(or almost-minimizers with modulus of continuity
\rho
, as defined, for example, in
[
9
, Definition 1.3]
), where
\rho
is of the form
\rho(r)=\varepsilon Cr^{n-s}
. Such modulus of continuity has the critical behaviour
r^{n-s}
(and hence has the same scaling of the fractional perimeter), which does not imply
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity. Nevertheless, if the coefficient
\varepsilon
is small enough, as recently pointed out in
[
9
]
,
such almost minimizers satisfy both interior and exterior density estimates.

This density property is a crucial tool for upgrading
L^{1}
convergence to Hausdorff convergence. This, in turn, allows us to prove that minimizers of (
1.21
) are actually
\Lambda
-minimizers for the
s
-perimeter, in the sense of
[
16
]
. Combining this
\Lambda
-minimality property with
[
16
, Corollary 3.6]
, we finally get that, for
\varepsilon
small enough, minimizers of (
1.21
) are nearly spherical sets.

A final step, based on a Fuglede-type argument, then yields the rigidity of balls as minimizers for (
1.21
). A further remarkable consequence of the last step is that, for nearly spherical sets, the asymmetry index
A_{s}
introduced here is essentially equivalent to the fractional Sobolev norm
H^{\frac{1+s}{2}}(\partial B_{1})
appearing on the right-hand side of the Fuglede-type stability estimate (
1.10
) proved in
[
16
]
, see Remark
6.2
. This highlights that our choice of the fractional oscillation index
\beta_{s}
is not merely motivated by analogy with the classical (local) setting, as in (
1.11
), but rather is tailored to fully exploit the structure of the complete fractional Sobolev norm featured in (
1.10
), which was precisely our original purpose.

Eventually, as an application of our stability estimate, we derive a quantitative bound for the fractional Cheeger constant, defined as follows.
Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set with finite measure and let
m>\frac{n-s}{n}
. The
fractional Cheeger constant
(or
(m,s)
-Cheeger constant
) of
\Omega
is defined by

h_{m,s}(\Omega):=\inf\left\{\frac{P_{s}(E)}{|E|^{m}},\;\;E\subset\Omega\right\}.

We establish the following quantitative stability estimate.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set with finite measure and let
m>\frac{n-s}{n}.
Then, there exists a constant
\kappa({m,n,s})>0
such that

(1.22)

\frac{h_{m,s}(\Omega)-h_{m,s}(B_{r})}{h_{m,s}(B_{r})}\geq\kappa(m,n,s)\,\zeta_{s}(\Omega),\qquad\text{with }|B_{r}|=|\Omega|,

where
\zeta_{s}(\Omega)
is the asymmetry index defined in (
1.19
).

Furthermore, we discuss the failure of analogous estimates involving the oscillation index
\beta_{s}
(or, equivalently,
A_{s}
), providing explicit counterexamples.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the main notations used throughout the paper and collect some preliminary results that will be needed in the sequel. In Sections
3
and
4
, we first show the existence of generalized minimizers then that they are indeed classical minimizers. Section
5
contains the reduction procedure to nearly spherical sets. In Section
6
we finally show that such minimizers correspond to the ball, and we use this result to deduce Theorem
1.1
. Finally, in Section
7
, we exploit our stability estimate to establish a quantitative result for fractional Cheeger constants, and we provide explicit counterexamples showing the failure of analogous bounds involving the index
\beta_{s}
.

2.
Preliminary notions and results

In this section we introduce some preliminary properties of the energies involved in the paper. We begin with some elementary properties of the fractional perimeter defined in (
1.7
).

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
E\subset\R^{n}
be a measurable set.

(
i
)

(Subadditivity)
For any
F\subset\R^{n}
such that
|E\cap F|=0
, we have

P_{s}(E\cup F)=P_{s}(E)+P_{s}(F)-2\int_{E}\int_{F}\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|^{n+s}}.

In particular

P_{s}(E\cup F)\leq P_{s}(E)+P_{s}(F).

(
ii
)

(Translation invariance)
Let
x\in\R^{n}
. Then

P_{s}(E+x)=P_{s}(E).

(
iii
)

(Scaling)
Let
\lambda>0
. Then

P_{s}(\lambda E)=\lambda^{n-s}P_{s}(E).

(
iv
)

P_{s}(E\cap B_{\rho})\to P_{s}(E),\quad\text{ as }\rho\to+\infty.

Proof.

Points (
i
), (
ii
) and (
iii
) are direct consequences of the definition of
P_{s}
. For (
iv
), we observe that by
[
16
, Lemma B.1]
, for any
\rho>0
,
P_{s}(E\cap B_{\rho})\leq P_{s}(E)
. Hence, since
E\cap B_{\rho}\to E
in
L^{1}
as
\rho\to+\infty
, by lower semicontinuity of
P_{s}
, we have

P_{s}(E)\leq\liminf_{\rho\to+\infty}P_{s}(E\cap B_{\rho})\leq P_{s}(E)

which implies the claim.

\Box

We pass now to some properties of
V_{s}
, defined in (
1.13
).
First, we observe that by a simple compactness argument, it is possible to prove that the maximum in (
1.13
) is achieved, but it may not be unique. If
y\in\R^{n}
is such that

V_{s}(E)=\int_{E}\frac{dx}{|x-y|^{s}},

then we call
y
a
V_{s}
-center of
E
, and we denote by
y_{E}
a generic
V_{s}
-center of
E
.

An important feature is that
V_{s}
is maximed by balls under volume constraint. Let us recall some notations in order to see that.

Given a measurable set
E\subset\R^{n}
of finite measure, we consider its symmetric rearrangement
E^{*}
, i.e. the open ball of measure
|E|
centered at the origin. Similarly, given a nonnegative measurable function
f:\R^{n}\to[0,+\infty)
vanishing at infinity, its symmetric decreasing rearrangement is defined as

f^{*}(x):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\chi_{\{f>t\}^{*}}(x)\,dt,

where
\chi_{A}
denotes the charachteristic function of the set
A
.

Note that such a function is measurable and lower semicontinuous. We recall (see, e.g.,
[
31
, Theorem 3.4]
) that for any two functions
f,g:\R^{n}\to[0,+\infty)
vanishing at infinity, then the following concentration inequality holds

(2.1)

\int_{\R^{n}}f(x)g(x)\,dx\leq\int_{\R^{n}}f^{*}(x)g^{*}(x)\,dx.

Moreover, if
f
is a radially decreasing symmetric function (i.e.
f(x)=\widetilde{f}(|x|)
for some decreasing
\widetilde{f}:\R\to[0,+\infty)
), then equality in (
2.1
) holds if and only if
g=g^{*}
, that is
g
is radially decreasing symmetric as well.

For any
y\in\R^{n}
, by applying (
2.1
) with
f(x)=1/|x|^{s}
and
g=\chi_{E-y}
for a measurable set
E\subset\R^{n}
, we get that

(2.2)

\int_{E}\frac{dx}{|x-y|^{s}}=\int_{\R^{n}}\frac{1}{|x|^{s}}\,\chi_{E-y}(x)\,dx\leq\int_{\R^{n}}\frac{1}{|x|^{s}}\,\chi_{E^{*}}(x)\,dx=\int_{E^{*}}\frac{dx}{|x|^{s}}

which implies

(2.3)

V_{s}(E)\leq V_{s}(E^{*}),

where equality holds only if
E
is a ball. Notice also that a similar argument proves that any ball has as a unique
V_{s}-
center its center. In particular, we have

(2.4)

V_{s}(B_{r})=\int_{B_{r}}\frac{dx}{|x|^{s}}=\frac{n\omega_{n}}{n-s}r^{n-s}.

We state some useful properties of
V_{s}
.

Proposition 2.2

(Properties of
V_{s}
)
.

Let
E\subset\R^{n}
be a measurable set.

(
i
)

(Subadditivity)
For any
F\subset\R^{n}
such that
|E\cap F|=0
, we have

V_{s}(E\cup F)\leq V_{s}(E)+V_{s}(F);

(
ii
)

(Translation invariance)
Let
x\in\R^{n}
. Then

V_{s}(E+x)=V_{s}(E).

(
iii
)

(Scaling)
Let
\lambda>0
. Then

V_{s}(\lambda E)=\lambda^{n-s}V_{s}(E).

(
iv
)

(Hölder continuity with respect to
L^{1}
convergence of sets). Given two measurable sets
E,F\subset\R^{n}
, we have

|V_{s}(E)-V_{s}(F)|\leq\frac{n\omega_{n}^{s/n}}{n-s}|E\Delta F|^{\frac{n-s}{n}}.

Proof.

The subadditivity follows straightforwardly from the definition of
V_{s}
. Let us prove (
ii
). Fixed
x\in\R^{n}
, then

V_{s}(E+x)=\max_{y\in\R^{n}}\int_{E+x}\frac{d\xi}{|\xi-y|^{s}}=\max_{y\in\R^{n}}\int_{E}\frac{d\eta}{|\eta-(y-x)|^{s}}=\max_{y\in\R^{n}}\int_{E}\frac{d\eta}{|\eta-y|^{s}}=V_{s}(E).

Similarly, regarding (
iii
), fixed
\lambda>0
, we have

V_{s}(\lambda\,E)=\max_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\lambda E}\frac{dx}{|x-y|^{s}}=\max_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{E}\frac{\lambda^{n-s}}{|x-\frac{y}{\lambda}|^{s}}\,dx=\lambda^{n-s}\max_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{E}\frac{dx}{|x-y|^{s}}=\lambda^{n-s}V_{s}(E).

Finally, we prove (
iv
). Let
y_{E}
be a
V_{s}
-center of
E
, i.e
V_{s}(E)=\int_{E}\frac{dx}{|x-y_{E}|^{s}}
, then

(2.5)

V_{s}(E)-V_{s}(F)\leq\int_{E}\frac{dx}{|x-y_{E}|^{s}}-\int_{F}\frac{dx}{|x-y_{E}|^{s}}\leq\int_{E\Delta F}\frac{dx}{|x-y_{F}|^{s}}.

Now, let
r>0
be such that
(E\Delta F)^{*}=(E\Delta F+y_{E})^{*}=B_{r}
. Then by (
2.2
), we get that

(2.6)

\int_{E\Delta F}\frac{dx}{|x-y_{E}|^{s}}\leq\int_{B_{r}}\frac{dx}{|x|^{s}}=\frac{n\omega_{n}}{n-s}r^{n-s}=\frac{n\omega_{n}^{s/n}}{n-s}|E\Delta F|^{\frac{n-s}{n}}.

which, together with (
2.5
), entails

V_{s}(E)-V_{s}(F)\leq\frac{n\omega_{n}^{s/n}}{n-s}|E\Delta F|^{\frac{n-s}{n}}.

By interchanging
E
with
F
, we prove the same estimate for
V_{s}(F)-V_{s}(E)
, implying the claim.

\Box

The following lemma ensures that if a set is close to the unit ball
B_{1}
in the
L^{1}
-topology, then its
V_{s}
-center is close to the origin. This represents the analogous result of
[
21
, Lemma 3.1]
(see also
[
34
, Lemma 2.3]
) in our fractional setting.

Lemma 2.3
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\eta>0
such that if
|F\Delta B_{1}|<\eta
, then
|y_{F}|<\varepsilon
for every
V_{s}
-center
y_{F}
of
F
.

Proof.

Let us argue by contradiction, assuming that there exits a sequence of sets
\{F^{k}\}_{k\in\N}
such that
|F_{k}\Delta B_{1}|\rightarrow 0
and
y_{F_{k}}\to y_{0}
with
|y_{0}|\geq\varepsilon
, for some
\varepsilon>0
, as
k\to\infty
. By the triangular inequality, we have

(2.7)

\begin{split}\int_{B_{1}}\frac{dx}{|x|^{s}}\leq\bigg|\int_{B_{1}}\frac{dx}{|x|^{s}}-\int_{F_{k}}\frac{dx}{|x-y_{F_{k}}|^{s}}\bigg|+\bigg|\int_{F_{k}}\frac{dx}{|x-y_{F_{k}}|^{s}}&-\int_{B_{1}}\frac{dx}{|x-y_{F_{k}}|^{s}}\bigg|+\int_{B_{1}}\frac{dx}{|x-y_{F_{k}}|^{s}}.\end{split}

Letting
k\to\infty
, since
F_{k}\to B_{1}
in
L^{1}
, by point (
iv
) of Proposition
2.2
, we get

(2.8)

\bigg|\int_{B_{1}}\frac{dx}{|x|^{s}}-\int_{F_{k}}\frac{dx}{|x-y_{F_{k}}|^{s}}\bigg|=|V_{s}(B_{1})-V_{s}(F_{k})|\leq\frac{n\omega_{n}^{s/n}}{n-s}|B_{1}\Delta F_{k}|^{\frac{n-s}{n}}\to 0.

Moreover, taking into account (
2.6
), we obtain

(2.9)

\begin{split}\bigg|\int_{F_{k}}\frac{dx}{|x-y_{F_{k}}|^{s}}-\int_{B_{1}}\frac{dx}{|x-y_{F_{k}}|^{s}}\bigg|&=\bigg|\int_{F_{k}\setminus B_{1}}\frac{dx}{|x-y_{F_{k}}|^{s}}-\int_{B_{1}\setminus F_{k}}\frac{dx}{|x-y_{F_{k}}|^{s}}\bigg|\\
&\leq\int_{F_{k}\setminus B_{1}}\frac{dx}{|x-y_{F_{k}}|^{s}}+\int_{B_{1}\setminus F_{k}}\frac{dx}{|x-y_{F_{k}}|^{s}}\\
&=\int_{F_{k}\Delta B_{1}}\frac{dx}{|x-y_{F_{k}}|^{s}}\leq\frac{n\omega_{n}^{s/n}}{n-s}|F_{k}\Delta B_{1}|^{\frac{n-s}{n}}\to 0,\end{split}

as
k\to\infty
. On the other hand, since
B_{1}
has as unique
V_{s}
-center the origin, for all
k\in\N
, we have

(2.10)

\int_{B_{1}}\frac{dx}{|x-y_{F_{k}}|^{s}}\leq\int_{B_{1}}\frac{dx}{|x|^{s}}.

Consequently, combining (
2.7
),(
2.8
), (
2.9
) and (
2.10
), we conclude that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{B_{1}}\frac{dx}{|x-y_{F_{k}}|^{s}}=\int_{B_{1}}\frac{dx}{|x|^{s}},

which is true only if
|y_{F_{k}}|\to 0
, contradicting the fact that
|y_{0}|\geq\varepsilon>0
.

\Box

Finally, we conclude this section by proving a Poincaré-type inequality which ensures that the oscillation index
\beta_{s}
, defined in (
1.12
), controls the entire asymmetry
A_{s}
. This is the fractional analogue of
[
21
, Proposition 1.2]
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
E\subset\R^{n}
a measurable set such that
0<|E|<\infty
, then there exists a constant
C=C(n,s)
such that

(2.11)

A_{s}(E)+\delta_{s}(E)^{1/2}\leq C\beta_{s}(E).

Proof.
Since the quantities
A_{s}(E)
,
\beta_{s}(E)
,
\zeta_{s}(E)
and
\delta_{s}(E)
are scale invariant, we shall assume without loss of generality that
|E|=\omega_{n}
. Moreover, up to translations, we can also assume that
E
is
V_{s}
-centered at the origin, i.e.

V_{s}(E)=\int_{E}\frac{1}{|x|^{s}}\,dx.

Then, we have

\begin{split}V_{s}(B_{1})-V_{s}(E)&=\int_{B_{1}}\frac{1}{|x|^{s}}\,dx-\int_{E}\frac{1}{|x|^{s}}\,dx\\
&=\int_{B_{1}\setminus E}\frac{1}{|x|^{s}}\,dx-\int_{E\setminus B_{1}}\frac{1}{|x|^{s}}\,dx,\end{split}

which, recalling (
1.18
), implies

(2.12)

\beta_{s}^{2}(E)=\delta_{s}(E)+\frac{c_{n,s}}{P_{s}(B_{1})}\left(\int_{B_{1}\setminus E}\frac{1}{|x|^{s}}\,dx-\int_{E\setminus B_{1}}\frac{1}{|x|^{s}}\,dx\right).

Since
|E|=|B_{1}|
we have

(2.13)

|E\setminus B_{1}|=|B_{1}\setminus E|=:a<\omega_{n}.

Let us denote by
A(R,1)=B_{R}\setminus B_{1}
and
A(1,r)=B_{1}\setminus B_{r}
the two annuli such that
|A(R,1)|=|A(1,r)|=a
, where
a
is defined in (
2.13
). In particular,

R=\left(1+\frac{a}{\omega_{n}}\right)^{1/n}\qquad\text{and}\qquad r=\left(1-\frac{a}{\omega_{n}}\right)^{1/n}.

We have that

(2.14)

\int_{A(R,1)}\frac{1}{|x|^{s}}\,dx-\int_{E\setminus B_{1}}\frac{1}{|x|^{s}}\,dx=\int_{A(R,1)\setminus(E\setminus B_{1})}\frac{1}{|x|^{s}}\,dx-\int_{(E\setminus B_{1})\setminus A(R,1)}\frac{1}{|x|^{s}}\,dx.

Let us denote by
U:=A(R,1)\setminus(E\setminus B_{1})
and
V:=(E\setminus B_{1})\setminus A(R,1)
. Note that
|U|=|V|
and that, if
f(x):=1/|x|^{s}
, then
f(x)\geq f(y)
for all
(x,y)\in U\times V
. Consequently, by (
2.14
), we get

\int_{E\setminus B_{1}}\frac{1}{|x|^{s}}\,dx\leq\int_{A(R,1)}\frac{1}{|x|^{s}}\,dx\,.

Arguing similarly, we also obtain

\int_{B_{1}\setminus E}\frac{1}{|x|^{s}}\,dx\geq\int_{A(1,r)}\frac{1}{|x|^{s}}\,dx.

Thus, by (
2.12
) and recalling that
c_{n,s}=\frac{P_{s}(B_{1})(n-s)}{n\omega_{n}}
, we have

(2.15)

\begin{split}\beta_{s}(E)^{2}&\geq\,\delta_{s}(E)+\frac{c_{n,s}}{P_{s}(B_{1})}\left(\int_{A(1,r)}\frac{1}{|x|^{s}}\,dx-\int_{A(R,1)}\frac{1}{|x|^{s}}\,dx\right)\\
&=\,\delta_{s}(E)+\bigl((1-r^{n-s})-(R^{n-s}-1)\bigr)\\
&=\,\delta_{s}(E)+\left(2-\left(1+\frac{a}{\omega_{n}}\right)^{\frac{n-s}{n}}-\left(1-\frac{a}{\omega_{n}}\right)^{\frac{n-s}{n}}\right).\end{split}

Now we observe that the function
f(t)=(1+t)^{\frac{n-s}{n}}
is uniformly concave in
[-1,1]
, i.e.,

(2.16)

\frac{1}{2}\left(f(t)+f(\tau)\right)\leq f\left(\frac{t}{2}+\frac{\tau}{2}\right)-\alpha_{n,s}|t-\tau|^{2},

for any
t,\tau\in[-1,1]
and
\alpha_{n,s}:=-\frac{1}{4}\left(\sup_{t\in(-1,1)}f^{\prime\prime}(t)\right)=\frac{s(n-s)}{4n^{2}}2^{\frac{-n-s}{n}}>0
. Therefore, recalling (
2.13
) and taking
t=\frac{a}{\omega_{n}}
and
\tau=-t
in (
2.16
), by (
2.15
) we get

\beta^{2}_{s}(E)\geq\delta_{s}(E)+\frac{8\,\alpha_{n,s}}{\omega_{n}^{2}}\,a^{2}=\delta_{s}(E)+{\tilde{\alpha}}_{n,s}\,(|E\setminus B_{1}|+|B_{1}\setminus E|)^{2},

for some constant
\tilde{\alpha}_{n,s}>0
.
Finally, since
|E\setminus B_{1}|+|B_{1}\setminus E|=|E\Delta B_{1}|
, we obtain

(1+\tilde{\alpha}_{n,s})\beta^{2}_{s}(E)\geq\delta_{s}(E)+{\tilde{\alpha}}_{n,s}(\beta_{s}(E)^{2}+|E\Delta B_{1}|^{2})\geq\delta_{s}(E)+CA_{s}(E)^{2},

which implies the claim for a suitable constant
C
depending only on
n
and
s
.

\Box

Remark 2.5
.

As a direct consequence of the proof of Proposition
2.4
, we obtain that for any measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
0<|E|<\infty
, the following estimate holds

(2.17)

\zeta_{s}(E)\geq C_{0}\,\alpha^{2}(E),

for some constant
C_{0}>0
depending only on
n
and
s
.
Observe moreover that, by (
1.18
), one trivially has that

\beta^{2}_{s}(E)\geq\zeta_{s}(E)\quad\text{ and }\quad\beta^{2}_{s}(E)\geq\delta_{s}(E).

3.
Existence of generalized minimizers

As explained in the Introduction, in order to get our stability inequality, we find a constant
\varepsilon_{0}>0
, sufficiently small, so that problem (
1.21
) has the ball as the only solution. Due to the competing nature of the terms
P_{s}
and
V_{s}
, a direct proof of the existence of minimizers for (
1.21
) is not straightforward. For this reason, we consider a relaxed version of the functional, namely a generalized-type energy. In this section, we establish the existence of minimizers for this generalized functional. In the following section, we will then show that these minimizers are indeed minimizers for
\mathcal{E}_{s}
in the classical sense.

Definition 3.1
.

We define the
generalized functional
of
\mathcal{E}_{s}
as the functional

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}_{s}}:(\R^{n})^{\mathbb{N}}\to\R

such that if
\widetilde{E}=\{E^{k}\}_{k\in\N}\in(\R^{n})^{\mathbb{N}}
, then

(3.1)

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{s}\left(\{E^{k}\}_{k\in\N}\right)\coloneqq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{E}_{s}(E^{k}).

The minimization problem we are interested in is the following:

(3.2)

\min\left\{\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{s}\left(\{E^{k}\}_{k\in\N}\right)\,:\,\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|E^{k}|=\omega_{n}\right\}.

Let us begin by linking problem (
1.21
) and (
3.2
). The following result is closely related to
[
9
, Proposition 1.1]
. Nevertheless, due to the presence of the nonlocal term
V_{s}
, the so-called
vanishing range action
property (corresponding to assumption (H3) in
[
9
]
) is not satisfied in our setting. Despite this, the proof remains essentially the same, requiring only minor adjustments. Since the evidence of such adjustments is not immediate, we include a detailed proof.

Proposition 3.2
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, the equality

\inf\left\{\mathcal{E}_{s}(E):|E|=\omega_{n}\right\}=\inf\left\{\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{s}\left(\{E^{k}\}_{k\in\N}\right):\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|E^{k}|=\omega_{n}\right\}

holds.

Proof.
Let us set

I:=\inf\left\{\mathcal{E}_{s}(E):|E|=\omega_{n}\right\}

and

I_{gen}:=\inf\left\{\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{s}\left(\{E^{k}\}_{k\in\N}\right):\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|E^{k}|=\omega_{n}\right\}.

Given a set
E
with
|E|=\omega_{n}
, considering the sequence of sets
\{E^{k}\}_{k\in\N}
such that
E^{1}=E
and
E^{k}=\emptyset
for
k\geq 2
, we have

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{s}(\{E^{k}\}_{k\in\N})=\mathcal{E}_{s}(E).

Consequently, it follows that
I_{gen}\leq I
.

On the other hand, for any
\varepsilon>0
let
\{E^{k}\}_{k\in\N}
, with
\sum_{k}|E^{k}|=\omega_{n}
be such that

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{s}(\{E^{k}\}_{k\in\N})\leq I_{gen}+\varepsilon.

We claim that there exists a set
E
with
|E|=\omega_{n}
such that

\mathcal{E}_{s}(E)\leq I_{gen}+3\varepsilon.

By (
iii
) of Proposition
2.1
and (
iii
) of Proposition
2.2
, for any ball
B_{r}
centered at the origin of radius
r>0
, we have

\mathcal{E}_{s}(B_{r})=r^{n-s}\mathcal{E}_{s}(B_{1})\to 0\quad\text{as }\,r\to 0^{+}.

Thus, for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists some
\eta=\eta(\varepsilon)
such that

(3.3)

\text{if }\,|B_{r}|\leq 2\eta\,\text{ then }\,\mathcal{E}_{s}(B_{r})\leq\varepsilon.

Moreover, it is possible to choose
M=M(\eta)\in\N
sufficiently large such that

(3.4)

\sum_{k=1}^{M}\mathcal{E}_{s}(E^{k})\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{E}_{s}(E^{k})\leq I_{gen}+\varepsilon\quad\text{ and }\quad\sum_{k=M+1}^{\infty}|E^{k}|\leq\eta.

By (
iv
) of Proposition
2.1
and (
iv
) of Proposition
2.2
, since for any
E\subseteq\R^{n}
,
E\cap B_{\rho}\to E
in
L^{1}
as
\rho\to+\infty
, we get

\mathcal{E}_{s}(E\cap B_{\rho})\to\mathcal{E}_{s}(E),\quad\text{as }\,\rho\to+\infty.

Thus, there exists
R=R(\varepsilon,\eta,M)
sufficiently large (observe that all quantities
\eta,\;M,\;R
just depends on
\varepsilon
) such that

(3.5)

\sum_{k=1}^{M}\mathcal{E}_{s}(E^{k}\cap B_{R})\leq\sum_{k=1}^{M}\mathcal{E}_{s}(E^{k})+\varepsilon\leq I_{gen}+2\varepsilon\quad\text{ and }\quad\sum_{k=1}^{M}|E^{k}\cap B_{R}^{c}|\leq\eta.

Now, let us consider the ball
B_{r_{1}}
centered at the origin such that

|B_{r_{1}}|=\sum_{k=1}^{M}|E^{k}\cap B_{R}^{c}|+\sum_{k=M+1}^{\infty}|E^{k}|\leq 2\eta,

and, recalling (
3.3
), we have that

(3.6)

\mathcal{E}_{s}(B_{r_{1}})<\varepsilon.

Moreover, let

E_{L}:=\bigg[\bigcup_{k=1}^{M}\big((E^{k}\cap B_{R})+L^{k}e_{1}\big)\bigg]\bigcup\bigg[B_{r_{1}}+L^{M+1}e_{1}\bigg],

with
L>0
, sufficiently large to ensure
|E_{L}|=\omega_{n}
. Then, for
L
sufficiently large, using (
i
) and (
ii
) in Proposition
2.1
and in Proposition
2.2
, we have

\mathcal{E}_{s}(E_{L})\leq\sum_{k=1}^{M}\mathcal{E}_{s}(E^{k}\cap B_{R})+\mathcal{E}_{s}(B_{r_{1}})\leq I_{gen}+3\varepsilon,

where the last inequality follows from (
3.5
) and (
3.6
). Whence

I\leq I_{gen}+3\varepsilon.

which concludes the proof by the arbitrariness of
\varepsilon>0
.

\Box

For later purpose, with the scope of eliminating the volume constraint in Problem (
1.21
), we introduce the following
penalized
functional. Concretely, for any
\Lambda>0
, we define

(3.7)

\begin{split}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{s,\Lambda}(\{E^{k}\}_{k\in\N}):&=\widetilde{\mathcal{E}_{s}}(\{E^{k}\}_{k\in\N})+\Lambda\left|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|E^{k}|-\omega_{n}\right|\\
&=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{E}_{s}(E^{k})+\Lambda\left|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|E^{k}|-\omega_{n}\right|.\end{split}

We have the following result.

Proposition 3.3
.

For any
\varepsilon_{0}>0
there exists a constant

\tilde{\Lambda}
of the form
\tilde{\Lambda}=C_{n,s}(1+\varepsilon_{0})
(for a suitable
C_{n,s}>0
)
such that, for all
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
and
\Lambda>\tilde{\Lambda}
, the following equality holds

(3.8)

\inf\left\{\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{s}(\{E^{k}\}_{k\in\N})\,:\,\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|E^{k}|=\omega_{n}\right\}=\inf_{E^{k}\subset\R^{n}}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{s,\Lambda}(\{E^{k}\}_{k\in\N}).

Moreover, any minimizer of the right-hand side of (
3.8
) satisfies the volume constraint
\sum_{k}|E^{k}|=\omega_{n}
, i.e., the constrained and the uncontrained generalized minimization problems for
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{s,\Lambda}
are equivalent.

Proof.

Let
\alpha:=\inf\left\{\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{s}(\{E^{k}\}_{k\in\N})\,:\,\sum_{k}|E^{k}|=\omega_{n}\right\}{>0}
. Given a sequence
\widetilde{F}=\{F^{k}\}_{k\in\N}
with
\sum_{k}|F^{k}|=\omega_{n}
, then

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{s}(\{F^{k}\}_{k\in\N})=\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{s,\Lambda}(\{F^{k}\}_{k\in\N}).

thus we have
\alpha\geq\inf_{E^{k}\subset\R^{n}}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{s,\Lambda}(\{E^{k}\}_{k\in\N})
.

On the other hand, let us suppose that there exists
\widetilde{E}=\{E^{k}\}_{k\in\N}
such that

(3.9)

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{s,\Lambda}(\widetilde{E})\leq\alpha.

By testing with the sequence
\widetilde{B}=(B_{1},\emptyset,\emptyset,\ldots)
one gets

(3.10)

\Lambda\left|\sum_{k}|E^{k}|-\omega_{n}\right|\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{s,\Lambda}(\{E^{k}\}_{k\in\N})\leq\alpha\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{s}(\widetilde{B})\leq C_{n,s}(1+\varepsilon_{0}),

where
C_{n,s}:=P_{s}(B_{1})+\frac{n\omega_{n}}{n-s}
.

Now, let
\delta\in\R
be such that
(1+\delta)^{n}\sum_{k}|E^{k}|=\omega_{n}
. Notice that this implies that
\delta\to 0
as
\Lambda\to+\infty
. Precisely, by (
3.10
), one has

(3.11)

C\Lambda|\delta|\leq\Lambda\omega_{n}\left|\frac{1}{(1+\delta)^{n}}-1\right|\leq C_{n,s}(1+\varepsilon_{0}),

for some constant
C>0
, depending only on
n
, so that

|\delta|\leq\frac{C_{n,s}(1+\varepsilon_{0})}{\Lambda},

up to renaming the constant
C_{n,s}>0
.
Let now
\widetilde{G}=(1+\delta)\widetilde{E}=\{(1+\delta)E^{k}\}_{k\in\N}
. Then by (
3.9
), we have
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{s,\Lambda}(\widetilde{E})\leq\alpha\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{s}(\widetilde{G})
, which implies, recalling the scaling property of
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{s}
,

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{s}(\widetilde{E})+\Lambda\left|\sum_{k}|E_{k}|-\omega_{n}\right|=\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{s,\Lambda}(\widetilde{E})\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{s}(\widetilde{G})\leq(1+\delta)^{n-s}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{s}(\widetilde{E})\leq(1+2(n-s)|\delta|)\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{s}(\widetilde{E}),

for
|\delta|
small enough. Thus, by rearranging the terms, and taking into account (
3.10
) and (
3.11
), one gets

(3.12)

\ \Lambda|\delta|\leq 2(n-s)|\delta|\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{s}(\widetilde{E})\leq C_{n,s}(1+\varepsilon_{0})|\delta|.

This implies that it must be

\Lambda\leq\widetilde{\Lambda}=C_{n,s}(1+\varepsilon_{0}),

thus concluding
the proof of the first claim. By reasoning in the same way, the second follows similarly.

\Box

By the previous result, to show existence of constrained generalized minimizers for
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{s}
it is enough to show existence of unconstrained generalized minimizers for
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{s,\Lambda}

(3.13)

\min_{E^{k}\subset\R^{n}}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{s,\Lambda}(\{E^{k}\}_{k\in\N}).

The proof of existence of minimizers for (
3.13
) follows a nowaday standard strategy, see, for example,
[
9
,
26
,
15
]
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Fixed
\varepsilon_{0}>0
. Then, for any
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
and
\Lambda>\tilde{\Lambda}
, with
\tilde{\Lambda}=C_{n,s}(1+\varepsilon_{0})
given by Proposition
3.3
, problem (
3.13
) (and thus problem (
3.2
)) admits a solution.

Proof.

The proof relies on the classical direct method in the Calculus of Variations. We begin by constructing a generalized minimizer using a standard minimizing sequence. We then prove that the associated functional is lower semicontinuous with respect to
L^{1}
-convergence, thereby ensuring that the limiting generalized set is indeed a minimizer for (
3.13
).

Let
\{E_{h}\}_{h\in\N}
be a classical minimizing sequence for the functional

\mathcal{E}_{s,\Lambda}(E):=\mathcal{E}_{s}(E)+\Lambda\big||E|-\omega_{n}\big|=P_{s}(E)+\varepsilon V_{s}(E)+\Lambda\big||E|-\omega_{n}\big|.

By Proposition
3.2
,
\{E_{h}\}_{h\geq 1}
turns out to be a minimizing sequence even in the class of generalized sets. Using the ball
B_{1}
as competitor, we have

\sup_{h}\mathcal{E}_{s,\Lambda}(E_{h})\leq C_{n,s}(1+\varepsilon_{0}),

where
C_{n,s}>0
is the constant appearing in (
3.10
). In particular, for
\Lambda>0
sufficiently large, up to pass to a subsequence,
|E_{h}|\to m
, for some
m\in(0,\infty)
. Fix
L:=10m^{{1}/{n}}
(so that in a cube of side lenght
L
,
E_{h}
covers at most half of the volume of the cube, for
h
large enough) and consider a partition of
\R^{n}
into cubes
\{Q_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
with
Q_{i}:=[0,L]^{n}+z_{i}
, with
z_{i}\in(L\mathbb{Z})^{n}
. Let
m_{i,h}:=|E_{h}\cap Q_{i}|
be the mass of
E_{h}
inside each cube. Let us rearrange the indexes so that, for every
h
,
m_{i,h}
is nonincreasing in
i
.

We show that the sequence
\{m_{i,h}\}_{i\geq 1}
is tight. With this purpose, let us consider the following localization of
P_{s}
(see
[
9
, Subsection 1.2]
)

(3.14)

P_{s}(F;Q_{i}):=\int_{F\cap Q_{i}}\int_{\R^{n}\setminus F}\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{n+s}}

for any measurable set
F\subseteq\R^{n}
.
We first recall that

P_{s}(E_{h};Q_{i})\geq\int_{E_{h}\cap Q_{i}}\int_{(\R^{n}\setminus E_{h})\cap Q_{i}}\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{n+s}}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{Q_{i}}\int_{Q_{i}}\frac{|\chi_{E}(x)-\chi_{E}(y)|}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,dx\,dy.

Then, since our choice of
L
ensure that
|Q_{i}\cap E_{h}|\leq|Q_{i}|/2
, by using the Poincaré-type inequality for fractional Sobolev spaces (see, e.g.
[
30
, Theorem 6.33]
) and proceeding exactly as in the proof of
[
15
, Lemma 2.5]
, we obtain that there exists a constant
C=C(n,s,L)>0
such that

C\,P_{s}(E_{h};Q_{i})\geq|E_{h}\cap Q_{i}|^{\frac{n-s}{n}}.

Hence, we have

\sum_{i}m^{\frac{n-s}{n}}_{i,h}\leq C\sum_{i}P_{s}(E_{h};Q_{i})=C\,P_{s}(E_{h})\leq c(n,s,L),

for some constant
c(n,s,L)>0
.
Since
m_{i,h}
is nonincreasing in
i
, for every
I\geq 1
and
i\geq I
we have
m_{i,h}\leq m_{I,h}\leq\frac{m}{I}
which ensures

(3.15)

\sum_{i\geq I}m_{i,h}\leq\left(\frac{m}{I}\right)^{\frac{s}{n}}\sum_{i\geq I}m_{i,h}^{\frac{n-s}{n}}\leq\left(\frac{m}{I}\right)^{\frac{s}{n}}c(n,s,L).

Hence, up to pass to a subsequence, we have
\lim_{h\to\infty}m_{i,h}=:m_{i}
and
\sum_{i}m_{i}=m
.

From now on, we consider just the cubes
Q_{i}
such that
m_{i,h}>0
. We rename such cubes
Q_{i,h}
, and coherently we relabel
z_{i,h}
their centers. By the perimeter bound, up to extraction, we have for every
i
,

E_{h}-z_{i,h}\to E^{i}
in
L^{1}_{\rm loc}
for some sets
E^{i}\subset\R^{n}
. We also freely assume that for every
i,j
,
|z_{i,h}-z_{j,h}|\to a_{ij}\in[0,\infty]
.
We define the following equivalence relation: we say that
i\sim j
if
a_{ij}<\infty
and denote by
[i]
the equivalence class of
i
.
Note that if
i\sim j
then
E^{i}
and
E^{j}
are translated of each other. For each class of equivalence we set

m_{[i]}:=\sum_{j\sim i}m_{j}

so that
\sum_{[i]}m_{[i]}=m
. Then, for all
i
, using the local
L^{1}
-convergence of
E_{h}-z_{i,h}
to
E^{i}
, we can deduce that
|E^{i}|=m_{[i]}
(see, e.g., Step 1.3 in the proof of
[
9
, Theorem 1.2]
).
We now associate to each class of equivalence
[i]
a single element
i
(for instance, its first element) and set
\widetilde{E}=\{E^{i}\}_{i\in\N}
. We claim that the limiting generalized set constructed above is indeed a generalized minimizer, that is

(3.16)

\sum_{i}\mathcal{E}_{s}(E^{i})+\Lambda\left|\sum_{i}|E^{i}|-\omega_{n}\right|\leq\liminf_{h\to\infty}\big(\mathcal{E}_{s}(E_{h})+\Lambda\big||E_{h}|-\omega_{n}\big|\big).

Let us fix
I\in\N
and
R>0
. Furthermore, for
h
sufficiently large, we can assume that for
i,j\leq I
with
i\neq j
,
|z_{i,h}-z_{j,h}|\geq 5R
which guarantees that
B_{R}(z_{i,h})\cap B_{R}(z_{j,h})=\emptyset
. We treat each term of the energy separately. Let us start with the
s
-perimeter, observing that

\begin{split}\sum_{i=1}^{I}P_{s}(E_{h}-z_{i,h};B_{R})&=\sum_{i=1}^{I}\int_{(E_{h}-z_{i,h})\cap B_{R}}\int_{\R^{n}\setminus(E_{h}-z_{i,h})}\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{I}\int_{E_{h}\cap B_{R}(z_{i,h})}\int_{\R^{n}\setminus E_{h}}\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\\
&=\int_{E_{h}\cap\bigcup_{i=1}^{I}B_{R}(z_{i,h})}\int_{\R^{n}\setminus E_{h}}\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\\
&\leq\int_{E_{h}}\int_{\R^{n}\setminus E_{h}}\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{n+s}}=P_{s}(E_{h}).\end{split}

Then, recalling that
E_{h}-z_{i,h}\to E^{i}
locally in
L^{1}
, by the lower semi-continuity of
P_{s}
and by Fatou’s Lemma, we obtain

(3.17)

\begin{split}\sum_{i=1}^{I}P_{s}(E^{i})&\leq\sum_{i=1}^{I}\liminf_{h\to\infty}P_{s}(E_{h}-z_{i,h})\\
&\leq\sum_{i=1}^{I}\liminf_{h\to\infty}\liminf_{R\to\infty}P_{s}(E_{h}-z_{i,h};B_{R})\leq\liminf_{h\to\infty}P_{s}(E_{h}).\end{split}

Similarly, concerning
V_{s}
, by (
ii
) of Proposition
2.2
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{I}V_{s}((E_{h}-z_{i,h})\cap B_{R})=\sum_{i=1}^{I}V_{s}(E_{h}\cap B_{r}(z_{i,h}))\leq V_{s}\left(E_{h}\cap\bigcup_{i=1}^{I}B_{R}(z_{i,h})\right)\leq V_{s}(E_{h}),

which ensures, together with Fatou’s Lemma,

(3.18)

\begin{split}\sum_{i=1}^{I}V_{s}(E^{i})&\leq\sum_{i=1}^{I}\liminf_{h\to\infty}V_{s}(E_{h}-z_{i,h})\\
&\leq\sum_{i=1}^{I}\liminf_{h\to\infty}\liminf_{R\to\infty}V_{s}((E_{h}-z_{i,h})\cap B_{R})\leq\liminf_{h\to\infty}V_{s}(E_{h}).\end{split}

Finally, for the penalization term, by tightness, it follows

(3.19)

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|E^{i}|=m=\lim_{h\to\infty}|E_{h}|.

Hence, by (
3.19
) and sending
I\to\infty
in (
3.17
) and (
3.18
), we obtain (
3.16
), concluding the proof.

\Box

4.
Existence of minimizers

In this section, we show that the generalized minimizers just found are classical minimizers for (
1.21
), for
\varepsilon_{0}
small enough. To this purpose, we recall the definition of
(\rho,R)
-minimizers of the
s
-perimeter, see
[
9
, Definition 1.3]
.

Definition 4.1
.

Given a nonnegative and nondecreasing function
\rho:[0,+\infty[\to\R
, we say that
E\subset\R^{n}
is a
(\rho,R)
-minimizer of the
s
-perimeter if there exists
R>0
such that for any
r\leq R
and for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, it holds

(4.1)

P_{s}(E)\leq P_{s}(F)+\rho(r),

for any
F\subset\R^{n}
with
E\Delta F\subset\subset B_{r}(x)
.

Now, we want to prove that any component of a generalized minimizer is a
(\rho,R)
-minimizer of the
s
-perimeter, with
\rho(r)=\varepsilon Cr^{n-s}
, for some constant
C>0
depending only on
n
and
s
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\varepsilon_{0}>0
be fixed and let
\widetilde{E}:=\{E^{k}\}_{k\in\N}
be a generalized minimizer of
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{s}
for
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
. Then, for any
k\in\N
, the set
E^{k}
is a
(\rho,R)
-minimizer for the
s
-perimeter with
\rho(r)=\varepsilon_{0}\frac{10n\omega_{n}}{n-s}r^{n-s}
and
R
sufficiently small.

Proof.
Let
\widetilde{E}=\{E^{k}\}_{k\in\N}
a minimizer for
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{s,\Lambda}
, with
\Lambda>\tilde{\Lambda}
, where
\tilde{\Lambda}=C_{n,s}(1+\varepsilon_{0})
is given by Proposition
3.3
, (in light of Proposition
3.3
,
\widetilde{E}
is a minimizer for
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{s}
as well). Let us fix
k\in\N
,
x\in\R^{n}
and
R\leq 1
and let
F\subset\R^{n}
be such that
E_{k}\Delta F\Subset B_{r}(x)
, for
r<R
.

Moreover, let us consider the sequence of sets
\widetilde{G}:=\left\{G^{l}\right\}_{l\in\N}
defined as

G^{l}:=\left\{\begin{matrix}F&\text{if}&l=k\\
E^{l}&\text{if}&l\neq k.\end{matrix}\right.

By minimality of
\widetilde{E}=\left\{E^{l}\right\}_{l\in\N}
we have

\begin{split}0&\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{s}(\widetilde{G})-\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{s}(\widetilde{E})+\Lambda\left|\sum_{l}|G^{l}|-\omega_{n}\right|-\Lambda\left|\sum_{l}|E^{l}|-\omega_{n}\right|\\
&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=1\\
l\neq k\end{subarray}}^{\infty}(\mathcal{E}_{s}(G^{l})-\mathcal{E}_{s}(E^{l}))+\mathcal{E}_{s}(G^{k})-\mathcal{E}_{s}(E^{k})+\Lambda\left|\sum_{l}|G^{l}|-\omega_{n}\right|-\Lambda\left|\sum_{l}|E^{l}|-\omega_{n}\right|\\
&=\mathcal{E}_{s}(F)-\mathcal{E}_{s}(E^{k})+\Lambda\left|\sum_{l}|G^{l}|-\omega_{n}\right|-\Lambda\left|\sum_{l}|E^{l}|-\omega_{n}\right|\\
&\leq\mathcal{E}_{s}(F)-\mathcal{E}_{s}(E^{k})+\Lambda\big||E^{k}|-|F|\big|\\
&\leq\mathcal{E}_{s}(F)-\mathcal{E}_{s}(E^{k})+\Lambda\omega_{n}r^{n}.\end{split}

Hence, since
r<R\leq 1
, by rearranging the terms and using point (
iv
) of Proposition
2.2
, we get

(4.2)

\displaystyle P_{s}(E^{k})

\displaystyle\leq P_{s}(F)+\varepsilon_{0}(V_{s}(F)-V_{s}(E^{k}))+\Lambda\omega_{n}r^{n}

\displaystyle\leq P_{s}(F)+\varepsilon_{0}\frac{n\omega_{n}}{n-s}r^{n-s}+\Lambda\omega_{n}r^{n}

\displaystyle\leq P_{s}(F)+\varepsilon_{0}\frac{10n\omega_{n}}{n-s}r^{n-s}.

which concludes the proof.

\Box

As pointed out in
[
25
]
when
s=1
, we cannot expect
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity when
\rho
has a critical power-like behavior. However, in light of
[
9
, Theorem 1.4, Remark 1.5 and Proposition 4.3]
when
\rho\sim\varepsilon_{0}r^{n-s}
, with
\varepsilon_{0}
sufficiently small, we have that
(\rho,R)
-minimizers of the

-perimeter satisfy interior and exterior density estimates.

In the following we will use the standard notation
E^{(t)}
to denote the set of points of density
t
of
E
, i.e. for
t\in[0,1]
, we set

E^{(t)}:=\left\{x\in\R^{n}:\,\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{|E\cap B_{r}(x)|}{|B_{r}|}=t\right\}.

We have the following result.

Theorem 4.3
.

[
9
, Theorem 1.4]

There exists
\overline{\varepsilon}\in(0,1)
such that if
E\subset\R^{n}
is a
(\rho,R)
-minimizer of the
s
-perimeter with
\rho(r)=\frac{10n\omega_{n}}{n-s}\,\overline{\varepsilon}\,r^{n-s}

{C_{DE}}>0
and
{r_{DE}}>0
such that for any

, we have

(4.3)

|E\cap B_{r}(x)|\geq{C_{DE}}\,r^{n}\quad\text{ for every }x\in E^{(1)}

and

(4.4)

|B_{r}(x)\setminus E|\geq{C_{DE}}\,r^{n}\quad\text{ for every }x\in E^{(0)},

As a consequence of Lemma
4.2
and Theorem
4.3
, we get the following result.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
\widetilde{E}:=\{E^{k}\}_{k\in\N}
be a generalized minimizer of
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{s}
. Then there exist constants
{C_{DE}}>0
and
{r_{DE}}>0
, such that for any
r\leq{r_{DE}}
and
k\in\N

(4.5)

|E^{k}\cap B_{r}(x)|\geq{C_{DE}}\,r^{n}\quad\text{ for every }x\in(E^{k})^{(1)}

and

(4.6)

|B_{r}(x)\setminus E^{k}|\geq{C_{DE}}\,r^{n}\quad\text{ for every }x\in(E^{k})^{(0)}.

Using the density estimates for the components of generalized minimizers, we deduce the existence of a classical minimizer for (
1.21
) from the existence of a generalized one.

Theorem 4.5

(Existence of minimizers for (
1.21
))
.

There exists a constant
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(n,s)>0
, such that, for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
the variational problem (
1.21
) admits a solution.

Proof.
Let
\widetilde{E}:=\{E^{k}\}_{k\in\N}
be a minimizer for
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{s}
. Since
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|E^{k}|=\omega_{n}
and by (
4.5
), we get

(4.7)

|E^{k}|\geq|E^{k}\cap B_{r_{DE}}|\geq C_{0}\,r^{n}_{DE},

for any
x\in(E^{k})^{(1)}
, where
r_{DE}>0
is the constant given by Corollary
4.4
, we conclude that
|E^{k}|\neq 0
just for a finite number of indexes
k=1,\ldots,M
. We sort the indexes so that

|E^{1}|\geq|E^{2}|\geq\ldots\geq|E^{M}|.

So, in particular,
|E^{1}|\geq\frac{\omega_{n}}{M}
. Fixed
L>0
, let us consider the following family of sets

E^{1}_{L}:=E^{1}\quad\text{and}\quad E^{k}_{L}:=E^{k}+L^{k}e_{1},\quad\text{for }k=2,\ldots,M.

Moreover, we define
F_{L}:=\bigcup_{k=1}^{M}E^{k}_{L}
. By (
4.7
) and the volume constraint, for
L>0
sufficiently large, we can assume that
|E_{L}|=\omega_{n}
and
|E^{i}_{L}\cap E^{j}_{L}|=0

i
) and (

) of Proposition

, we have

(4.8)

P_{s}(F_{L})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{M}P_{s}(E^{i}_{L})=\sum_{i=1}^{M}P_{s}(E^{i}).

On the other hand, since
\widetilde{E}:=\{E^{k}\}_{k\in\N}
, is a generalized minimizer and, as previously observed,
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{s}(\widetilde{E})=\sum_{k=1}^{M}\mathcal{E}_{s}(E^{k})
, by considering the sequence of sets
\widetilde{B}:=\{B^{k}\}_{k\in\N}
, defined by

B^{1}:=B_{1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad B^{k}:=\emptyset\quad\text{for all }\,k\geq 2,

then

0\geq\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{s}(\widetilde{E})-\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{s}(\widetilde{B})=\sum_{k=1}^{M}\mathcal{E}_{s}(E^{k})-\mathcal{E}_{s}(B_{1})

which implies

(4.9)

\sum_{i=1}^{M}P_{s}(E^{k})-P_{s}(B_{1})\leq\varepsilon\left(V_{s}(B_{1})-\sum_{i=1}^{M}V_{s}(E^{k})\right)\leq\varepsilon\frac{n\omega_{n}}{n-s}.

Furthermore, by (
4.8
) and recalling the quantitative estimate (
1.9
), we have

(4.10)

\sum_{k=1}^{M}P_{s}(E^{k})-P_{s}(B_{1})\geq P_{s}(F_{L})-P_{s}(B_{1})\geq{c_{0}(n,s)}\,|F_{L}\Delta B_{1}(x_{0})|^{2},

where the constant
c_{0}(n,s):=P_{s}(B_{1})\omega_{n}^{-1}\,c_{Q}(n,s)
and
c_{Q}
is the one appearing in (
1.9
) and
x_{0}\in\R^{n}
realizes the minimum in the Fraenkel asymmetry of
F_{L}
. By choosing
L>0
sufficiently large, we can suppose there exists
k^{\ast}\in\{1,\ldots,M\}
such that
|B_{1}(x_{0})\cap E^{k^{\ast}}_{L}|>0

|B_{1}(x_{0})\cap E^{k}_{L}|=0
for all

\begin{split}|F_{L}\Delta B_{1}(x_{0})|&\geq|E^{k^{\ast}}_{L}\Delta B_{1}(x_{0})|+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\neq k^{\ast}\end{subarray}}^{M}|E^{k}_{L}|\\
&=|E^{k^{\ast}}_{L}\Delta B_{1}(x_{0})|+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\neq k^{\ast}\end{subarray}}^{M}|E^{k}|\geq|E^{k^{\ast}}_{L}\Delta B_{1}(x_{0})|+(M-1){C_{DE}\,r_{DE}^{n}},\end{split}

where in the last inequality we used (
4.4
). This, together with (
4.9

c_{0}(n,s)\left(|E^{k^{\ast}}_{L}\Delta B_{1}(x_{0})|+(M-1){C_{DE}\,r_{DE}^{n}}\right)\leq\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{n\omega_{n}}{n-s}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

which leads to a contradiction, as soon as
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
with
\varepsilon_{0}<{c_{0}(n,s)}^{2}C_{DE}^{2}\,r^{2n}_{DE}\,\frac{n-s}{n\omega_{n}}

5.
Regularity of minimizers

In the previous section, we established the existence of minimizers for (
1.21
). This result was obtained by relying on a mild regularity theory for
(\rho,R)
-minimizers of the
s
-perimeter, encoded in suitable density estimates. The goal of this section is to improve upon this regularity. First, we show that, for sufficiently small
\varepsilon
, minimizers of (
1.21
) are close in
L^{1}
-topology to a ball. Then, by further exploiting the density estimates, we upgrade this closeness to convergence in the Hausdorff distance. At this stage, we prove that minimizers of (
1.21
) are indeed
\Lambda
-minimizers of the
s
-perimeter in the sense of
[
16
, Section 3]
, and thus we may apply the regularity results developed therein (specifically,
[
16
, Corollary 3.6]
) to reduce to the case of nearly spherical sets.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(n,s)>0
be given by Theorem
4.5
, and for
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
, let
E_{\varepsilon}
be a minimizer of (
1.21
).Then, as
\varepsilon\to 0
, there exists
x_{\varepsilon}\in\R^{n}
such that the sets
E_{\varepsilon}-x_{\varepsilon}
converge in
L^{1}
-topology to the unit ball
B_{1}=B_{1}(0)
. Namely there holds

(5.1)

\left|(E_{\varepsilon}-x_{\varepsilon})\Delta B_{1}\right|\leq{\gamma(n,s)}\varepsilon^{1/2}.

Proof.

By minimality of
E_{\varepsilon}
in (
1.21
), we get,

(5.2)

P_{s}(E_{\varepsilon})-P_{s}(B_{1})\leq\varepsilon(V_{s}(B_{1})-V_{s}(E_{\varepsilon}))\leq\varepsilon V_{s}(B_{1})=\varepsilon\frac{n\omega_{n}}{n-s}.

Then, recalling the quantitative estimate (
1.9
), we have

\min_{x\in\R^{n}}\left|E_{\varepsilon}\Delta B_{1}(x)\right|^{2}\leq{\gamma_{0}(n,s)}(P_{s}(E_{\varepsilon})-P_{s}(B)),

with
\gamma_{0}(n,s):=\frac{\omega_{n}c_{Q}}{P_{s}(B_{1})}
where the constant
c_{Q}
is the one appearing in (
1.9
). This, together with (
5.2
), gives (
5.1
), with
\gamma(n,s):=(\gamma_{0}(n,s)\frac{n\omega_{n}}{n-s})^{1/2}
.

\Box

Next, we show, employing the density estimates found in Theorem

, that the
L^{1}
-proximity can be improved into an

(i.e. Hausdorff) one.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let

be the points found in Proposition

. Given
\delta>0
, there exists
\varepsilon(\delta)>0
, with
\varepsilon(\delta)\to 0

, such that if

is a minimizer of (
1.21

\partial(E_{\varepsilon}-x_{\varepsilon})\subset B_{1+\delta}(0)\setminus B_{1-\delta}(0).

In particular,
E_{\varepsilon}-x_{\varepsilon}
converges in Hausdorff metric to
B_{1}(0)
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Proof.

For simplicity of notation and without loss of generality, we may assume that
x_{\varepsilon}=0
.
By Corollary
4.4
, there exist two constants

\bar{\varepsilon}
and
r_{DE}
such that (
4.3
) and (
4.4
) hold true for

as long as
\varepsilon(\delta)<\bar{\varepsilon}
. Let
x\in E_{\varepsilon}{{}^{(1)}}
and suppose that

. Then, if

,

. Hence, by (

) and (

), we obtain

r^{2n}\leq{\frac{1}{C_{DE}^{2}}}|B_{r}(x)\cap E_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\leq{\frac{1}{C_{DE}^{2}}}|E_{\varepsilon}\Delta B_{1}|^{2}\leq{\frac{\gamma(n,s)^{2}}{C_{DE}^{2}}}\,\varepsilon

where

and

) and (

). Now, if we select

, we get a contradiction for
\varepsilon<\varepsilon(\delta)
. A completely analogous argument shows that there can not be
x\in B_{1}\cap E_{\varepsilon}^{(0)}
with
{\rm dist}(x,\partial B_{1})>\delta
, for
\varepsilon
small enough. This leads to the desired Hausdorff convergence.

\Box

By the Hausdorff proximity result, one gets immediately the following result.

Corollary 5.3
.

There exists
\bar{\varepsilon}>0
such that for
\varepsilon<\bar{\varepsilon}
, if
E_{\varepsilon}
is a solution of (
1.21
), then there holds

B_{1/2}(0)\subset E_{\varepsilon}.

At this stage, we have established that a minimizer satisfies a very weak notion of almost minimality. However, now that we know, up to translation, that its boundary lies away from the origin, which is the singularity point of the kernel defining
V_{s}
, we are in a position to upgrade this weak almost minimality to the stronger notion of
\Lambda
-minimality for the
s
-perimeter. To this end, we recall the definition of
\Lambda
-minimizers, see
[
16
, Section 3]
.

Definition 5.4
.

Let
\Lambda\geq 0
,
R>0
,
s\in(0,1)
, and let

be a bounded measurable set. Then
E
is said to be a
\Lambda
-minimizer of the
s
-perimeter if

(5.3)

P_{s}(E)\leq P_{s}(F)+{\Lambda}|\,E\Delta F|\,,

for every set

, such that
F\Delta E\Subset B_{r}(x)
, with
r<R
and
x\in\R^{n}
.

We now recall the following result from
[
16
, Corollary 3.6]
, which plays a crucial role in our argument, as it allows us to reduce the analysis to the case of nearly spherical sets.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let

,
\Lambda\geq 0
,

and
E_{h}
, with

, be a
\Lambda
-minimizer of the

-perimeter. If

converges in
L^{1}
to
B_{1}
, then there exists a bounded sequence
\{\varphi_{h}\}_{h\in\N}\subset C^{1,\alpha}(\partial B_{1})
, for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
independent of
h
, such that

\partial E_{h}=\Big\{(1+\varphi_{h}(x))x:x\in\partial B_{1}\Big\}\,\quad\text{ and }\quad\lim_{h\to\infty}\|\varphi_{h}\|_{C^{1}(\partial B_{1})}=0.

With Corollary
5.3
at hand, we can now establish the main result of this section, namely the
\Lambda
-minimality of the solutions to (
1.21
).

Proposition 5.6
.

There exists
\bar{\varepsilon}>0
such that any minimizer
E_{\varepsilon}
of (
1.21
), with
\varepsilon\in(0,\bar{}\varepsilon]
, is a
\Lambda
-mimimizer for the
s
-perimeter, with
\Lambda=C(1+\overline{\varepsilon})
, for some constant
C>0
depending only on
n
and
s
.

Proof.

Hereafter, in the present proof, for the sake of brevity and with no risk of confusion, we denote
E_{\varepsilon}
simply by
E
.
Let
x\in\R^{n}
be fixed and
R>0
small enough to be made precise later. Moreover, Let
F\subset\R^{n}
be such that
F\Delta E\Subset B_{r}(x)
with
r\leq R
and
\lambda>0
be such that
|\lambda F|=|E|=\omega_{n}
. In particular, we get

(5.4)

\begin{split}\lambda^{n-s}&=\left(\frac{|E|}{|F|}\right)^{\frac{n-s}{n}}=\left(\frac{\omega_{n}}{\omega_{n}+\big(|F|-|E|\big)}\right)^{\frac{n-s}{n}}\\
&=\left(\frac{1}{1+\frac{|F|-|E|}{\omega_{n}}}\right)^{\frac{n-s}{n}}\leq 1+c_{n,s}\big||E|-|F|\big|\leq 1+c_{n,s}|E\Delta F|.\end{split}

which, together with the minimality of
E
, yields

(5.5)

P_{s}(E)\leq P_{s}(F)+c_{n,s}|E\Delta F|\,P_{s}(F)+c_{n,s}|E\Delta F|\,V_{s}(F)+\bar{\varepsilon}\left(\int_{F-{y_{F}}}\frac{dx}{|x|^{s}}-\int_{E-{y_{E}}}\frac{dx}{|x|^{s}}\right),

where
y_{F}
and
y_{E}
denote, respectively, any
V_{s}
-center of
F
and
E
.

We now estimate each term on the right-hand side of (
5.5
) separately. Without loss of generality, we can suppose that

P_{s}(F)<P_{s}(E)<\mathcal{E}_{s}(E)\leq\mathcal{E}_{s}(B_{1})=C_{n,s}(1+\bar{}\varepsilon),

otherwise, if
P_{s}(E)\leq P_{s}(F)
, there is nothing to prove. Moreover, by (
2.1
), for some constant
\tilde{c}_{n,s}>0
, one has
V_{s}(F)\leq V_{s}(F^{*})\leq\tilde{c}_{n,s}
. Hence, up to renaming constants, (
5.5
) reads as

(5.6)

P_{s}(E)\leq P_{s}(F)+c_{n,s}(1+\bar{}\varepsilon)|E\Delta F|+\bar{\varepsilon}\left(\int_{F-{y_{F}}}\frac{dx}{|x|^{s}}-\int_{E-y_{E}}\frac{dx}{|x|^{s}}\right).

It remains to estimate the last term. To this end, we observe that, upon choosing
\bar{\varepsilon}
and
R
sufficiently small, and by recalling Corollary
5.3
, Proposition
5.1
, and Lemma
2.3
, we may assume that

B_{1/2}(0)\subset E\cap F,\quad|y_{E}|\leq\frac{1}{4}\quad\text{and}\quad|y_{F}|\leq\frac{1}{4},

which ensures that

\displaystyle\int_{F-{y_{F}}}\frac{dx}{|x|^{s}}-\int_{E-{y_{E}}}\frac{dx}{|x|^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{F-{y_{F}}}\frac{dx}{|x|^{s}}-\int_{E-{y_{F}}}\frac{dx}{|x|^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{(F-{y_{F}})\setminus(E-{y_{F}})}\frac{dx}{|x|^{s}}-\int_{(E-{y_{F}})\setminus(F-{y_{F}})}\frac{dx}{|x|^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{(F-{y_{F}})\Delta(E-{y_{F}})}\frac{dx}{|x|^{s}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\big((F-{y_{F}})\Delta(E-{y_{F}})\big)\setminus B_{1/2}(y_{F})}\frac{dx}{|x|^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq 4^{s}|(F-{y_{F}})\Delta(E-{y_{F}})|

\displaystyle=4^{s}|F\Delta E|.

This, together with (
5.6
), concludes the proof upon choosing
\Lambda=C(1+\overline{\varepsilon})
, for some constant
C>0
sufficiently large, depending only on
n
and
s
.

\Box

As a consequence of Proposition
5.1
and Proposition
5.6
, we get the following crucial regularity result.

Corollary 5.7
.

Let
\varepsilon_{h}\to 0
and let
E_{h}
be minimizers for (

), with

. Then, up to subsequence,
E_{h}
are nearly spherical sets More precisely, there exists a sequence

for some

independent of

, such that

6.
Rigidity of the ball within the class of nearly spherical sets and proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we show that in the class of nearly spherical sets, the ball is a rigid minimizer.

Theorem 6.1
.

There exists
\varepsilon_{1}>0
such that if
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{1}
then the only minimizer for (
1.21
) is the ball
B_{1}
.

Proof.

Up to select
\varepsilon_{1}>0
small enough, by Corollary
5.7
, we get that any minimizer of (
1.21
) is a nearly spherical set, whose boundary is parametrized by a function
\varphi_{\varepsilon}:\partial B_{1}\to\R
such that
\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{C^{1}(\partial B_{1})}\to 0
, as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Moreover, up to translations, we can suppose that the barycenters of the sets
E_{\varepsilon}
are in the origin, that is

\partial E_{\varepsilon}=\Big\{(1+\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x))x\,:\,x\in B_{1}\Big\},\quad|E_{\varepsilon}|=\omega_{n},\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{E_{\varepsilon}}x\,dx=0,\quad\text{for }\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{1}].

Let
\eta_{0}\in(0,1/2)
and
c_{0}>0
, the constants (depending only on
n
) given by
[
16
, Theorem 2.1]
. Since
\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{C^{1}(\partial B_{1})}\to 0
, as
\varepsilon\to 0
, up to choose
\varepsilon_{1}
smaller, we can assume that
\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{C^{1}(\partial B_{1})}<\eta_{0}
for
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{1}]
. Thus, by
[
16
, Theorem 2.1]
, we have

(6.1)

P_{s}(E_{\varepsilon})-P_{s}(B_{1})\geq c_{0}\,sP_{s}(B_{1})\,\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial B_{1})}=:C_{0}(n,s)\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial B_{1})}.

We claim that there exists
C_{1}=C_{1}(n,s)
such that

(6.2)

P_{s}(E_{\varepsilon})-P_{s}(B_{1})\leq C_{1}\varepsilon\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial B_{1})}.

This, together with (
6.1
), immediately entails that
\varphi_{\varepsilon}=0
for
\varepsilon
small enough, so that
E_{\varepsilon}
is a ball.

To show (
6.2
), we observe that by minimality of
E_{\varepsilon}
, one has

P_{s}(E_{\varepsilon})-P_{s}(B_{1})\leq\varepsilon(V_{s}(B_{1})-V_{s}(E_{\varepsilon})).

Hence, the proof of (
6.2
) reduces to showing that there exists
C_{1}=C_{1}(n,s)>0
such that

(6.3)

V_{s}(B_{1})-V_{s}(E_{\varepsilon})\leq C_{1}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial B_{1})}.

In order to do that, we start by observing that, by definition of
V_{s}
, we have

(6.4)

V_{s}(B_{1})-V_{s}(E_{\varepsilon})\leq\int_{B_{1}}\frac{1}{|x|^{s}}\,dx-\int_{E_{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{|x|^{s}}\,dx=:I_{0}-I_{1}.

By (
2.4
), we know that

(6.5)

I_{0}=\frac{n\omega_{n}}{n-s}.

Moreover, we can compute
I_{1}
, using that
E_{\varepsilon}
is a nearly spherical set parametrized by
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
, as follows

(6.6)

\begin{split}I_{1}=\int_{E}\frac{1}{|x|^{s}}\,dx&=\int_{\partial B_{1}}\int_{0}^{1+\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)}\frac{\rho^{n-1}}{\rho^{s}}\,d\rho\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)=\frac{1}{n-s}\int_{\partial B_{1}}(1+\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x))^{n-s}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x).\end{split}

By combining together (
6.6
) and (
6.5
), we deduce

I_{0}-I_{1}=\frac{n\omega_{n}}{n-s}-\frac{1}{n-s}\int_{\partial B_{1}}(1+\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x))^{n-s}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x).

Now, for
t\in(0,1)
, let us introduce the sets

E_{\varepsilon}^{t}:=\{(1+t\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x))x\,:\,x\in\partial B_{1}\}

and the function

h(t):=\int_{\partial B_{1}}(1+t\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x))^{n-s}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x).

Thus, in particular, we have

E_{\varepsilon}^{0}=B_{1},\quad E_{\varepsilon}^{1}=E,\quad h(0)=n\omega_{n},\quad\text{and}\quad h(1)=\int_{\partial B_{1}}(1+\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x))^{n-s}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x),

which ensures

(6.7)

I_{0}-I_{1}=\frac{1}{n-s}(h(0)-h(1)).

In order to estimate the difference
h(0)-h(1)
, we argue similarly as in the proof of Theorem 2.1 in
[
16
]
.
Since
|E|=\omega_{n}
, we have that

(6.8)

\int_{\partial B_{1}}(1+\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x))^{n}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)=n\int_{\partial B_{1}}\int_{0}^{1+\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)}\rho^{n-1}\,d\rho\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)=n|E|=n\omega_{n}=h(0).

Hence

h(0)-h(1)=\int_{\partial B_{1}}(1+\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x))^{n}(1-(1+\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)^{-s}))\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x).

By using a Taylor expansion, we have for any
|z|<1/2
:

\begin{split}(1+z)^{n}\big(1-(1+z)^{-s}\big)&=\left(1+nz+\frac{n(n-1)}{2}z^{2}+R_{1}(z)\right)\left(sz-\frac{s(s+1)}{2}z^{2}+sR_{2}(z)\right)\\
&\leq sz+\left(sn-\frac{s(s+1)}{2}\right)z^{2}+sR_{3}(z)\end{split},

where
|R_{3}(z)|\leq C(n)|z|^{3}
.
Therefore, we get

(6.9)

\begin{split}h(0)-h(1)&\leq\int_{\partial B_{1}}\left(s\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)+s\left(n-\frac{s+1}{2}\right)\varphi^{2}_{\varepsilon}(x)\right)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)+sC(n)\int_{\partial B_{1}}|\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{3}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)\\
&\leq\int_{\partial B_{1}}\left(s\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)+s\left(n-\frac{s+1}{2}\right)\varphi^{2}_{\varepsilon}(x)\right)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)+sC(n)\int_{\partial B_{1}}\varphi^{2}_{\varepsilon}(x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)\end{split}

where in the last inequality we have used that
\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{C^{1}(\partial B_{1})}\leq\eta_{0}<1/2
to bound the
L^{3}(\partial B_{1})
norm of
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
with its
L^{2}(\partial B_{1})
norm.

On the other hand, the volume constraint (
6.8
), gives

\int_{\partial B_{1}}\left((1+\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x))^{n}-1\right)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)=0,

which implies

(6.10)

\begin{split}\int_{\partial B_{1}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)\leq-\frac{n-1}{2}\int_{\partial B_{1}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}^{2}(x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)+C(n)\int_{\partial B_{1}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}^{2}(x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x).\end{split}

Inserting such estimate into (
6.9
), we deduce

(6.11)

\begin{split}h(0)-h(1)&\leq-\frac{s(n-1)}{2}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial B_{1})}+s\left(n-\frac{s+1}{2}\right)\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial B_{1})}+2sC(n)\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial B_{1})}\\
&=\frac{s}{2}(n-s)\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial B_{1})}+2sC(n)\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial B_{1})}\leq C_{n,s}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial B_{1})}.\end{split}

By combining (
6.4
),(
6.7
), and (
6.11
), we obtain

(6.12)

V_{s}(B_{1})-V_{s}(E_{\varepsilon})\leq I_{0}-I_{1}=\frac{1}{n-s}(h(0)-h(1))\leq\tilde{C}_{n,s}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial B_{1})},

which concludes the proof of (
6.3
) and thus of the Theorem.

\Box

.

For a nearly spherical set

In particular, making use of the Poincaré-type inequality (

Since the quantities

Remark 6.3

(Asymptotic behavior as
s\to 1^{-}
)
.

Once the stability estimate (
1.1
) has been established, we now turn to its asymptotic behaviour as
s\to 1^{-}
. In particular, our goal is to verify that, in the limit
s\to 1^{-}
, one recovers the classical stability estimate (
1.4
) for the perimeter, proved in
[
21
]
.

Thanks to the well-known asymptotic properties of
P_{s}
, it follows that
\beta_{s}(\cdot)\to\beta(\cdot)
and hence
A_{s}(\cdot)\to A(\cdot)
as
s\to 1^{-}
. Therefore, the crucial point is to keep precise track of the dependence on
s
of the constant
c(n,s)>0
appearing in (
1.15
), and thus of all the constants involved in our argument. It is straightforward to recognize that, throughout Sections
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
and
6
, all the constants depending on
s
are explicit, except for
c_{Q}
in (
1.9
) and the constants
\overline{\varepsilon}
,
{C_{DE}}
and
{r_{DE}}
involved in the density estimate of Theorem
4.3
.

The constant
c_{Q}
, appearing in the quantitative fractional isoperimetric inequality (
1.9
), is uniformly bounded as
s\to 1^{-}
, as observed in
[
16
, Remark 1.2]
.

Let us now focus on the constants in Theorem
4.3
. By the proof of
[
9
, Theorem 1.4]
, one sees that the dependence on
s
of these constants is determined by the structural parameters in assumption (S3) of
[
9
]
, which are necessary for the validity of density estimates. For this reason, we briefly check them here. This analysis was partly carried out in
[
9
, Proposition 4.3]
, but we provide the details to make the dependence on
s
completely explicit.

First, considering the localization of
P_{s}
in (
3.14
), by
[
15
, Proposition 2.5]
and
[
6
, Theorem 1]
, assumption (H4) of
[
9
]
(relative isoperimetric inequality) is fulfilled with

(6.14)

f_{1}(m):=C_{1}(n,s)m^{\frac{n-s}{n}},\qquad\text{for }m>0,

for any
r_{0}>0
, where
C_{1}(n,s)\sim\tfrac{1}{1-s}
as
s\to 1^{-}
. On the other hand, assumptions (H6) and (H12) with
C=1
follow directly from (
3.14
).

Regarding (H13), let
E\subset\R^{n}
and
x\in\R^{n}
. By translation invariance, we may assume
x=0
. Fixing
t>0
, for any
z\in B_{t}
, we compute

\int_{B_{t}^{c}}\frac{dy}{|z-y|^{n+s}}=\int_{B_{t}(-z)^{c}}\frac{dy}{|y|^{n+s}}\leq\int_{B_{t-|z|}^{c}}\frac{dy}{|y|^{n+s}}=\frac{n\omega_{n}}{s}\frac{1}{(t-|z|)^{s}},

which, together with the coarea formula, yields for a.e.
t>0
:

P_{s}(B_{t};E)=\int_{E\cap B_{t}}\int_{B_{t}^{c}}\frac{dzdy}{|z-y|^{n+s}}\leq\frac{n\omega_{n}}{s}\int_{E\cap B_{t}}\frac{dz}{(t-|z|)^{s}}=\frac{n\omega_{n}}{s}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(E\cap\partial B_{\rho})}{(t-\rho)^{s}}\,d\rho.

Hence, by applying the Fubini-Tonelli Theorem, for any
r>0
, we obtain

\begin{split}\frac{1}{r}\int_{0}^{r}&P_{s}(B_{t};E)\,dt\\
&=\frac{n\omega_{n}}{sr}\int_{0}^{r}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(E\cap\partial B_{\rho})}{(t-\rho)^{s}}\,d\rho\,dt=\frac{n\omega_{n}}{sr}\int_{0}^{r}\int_{\rho}^{r}\frac{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(E\cap\partial B_{\rho})}{(t-\rho)^{s}}\,dt\,d\rho\\
&=\frac{n\omega_{n}}{sr}\frac{(r-\rho)^{1-s}}{1-s}\int_{0}^{r}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(E\cap\partial B_{\rho})\,d\rho\leq\frac{n\omega_{n}}{sr}\frac{r^{1-s}}{1-s}\,|E\cap B_{r}|=\frac{n\omega_{n}}{s(1-s)}\,r^{-s}\,|E\cap B_{r}|.\end{split}

Thus, condition (H12) in
[
9
]
holds with

(6.15)

f_{2}(r,m):=C_{2}(n,s)\,r^{-s}m,\qquad\text{for }m>0\ \text{ and for any }\ r>0,

where
C_{2}(n,s):=\tfrac{n\omega_{n}}{s(1-s)}
.

Finally, let us verify (H14). By Lemma
4.2
,
\rho(r)=C_{0}(n,s)\bar{\varepsilon}r^{n-s}
, with
C_{0}(n,s):=\tfrac{10n\omega_{n}}{n-s}
. Combining this with (
6.14
) and (
6.15
), we obtain

f_{3}(r,m):=2^{n}\left(\frac{f_{2}(r,m)+\rho(r)}{f_{1}(m)}\right)=\frac{C_{2}}{2^{-n}C_{1}}\Big(\frac{m}{r^{n}}\Big)^{\frac{s}{n}}+\frac{C_{0}\bar{\varepsilon}}{2^{-n}C_{1}}\Big(\frac{m}{r^{n}}\Big)^{\frac{s-n}{n}}.

Setting
\delta:=\tfrac{m}{r^{n}}
, one checks that

f_{3}(r,m)\leq 1\quad\text{whenever}\quad\delta\in\left(\tfrac{\varepsilon_{1}}{2^{n}},\varepsilon_{1}\right],

for some suitable
\varepsilon_{1}>0
and
r_{3}>0
. More precisely,
f_{3}(r,m)\leq 1
provided that
\varepsilon_{1}\in\mathcal{I}:=(a,b]
, with

a(n,s):=2^{n}\Big(\frac{2^{n+1}C_{0}\bar{\varepsilon}}{C_{1}}\Big)^{\tfrac{n}{n-s}}\sim(1-s)^{\tfrac{n}{n-s}},\qquad b(n,s):=\Big(\frac{C_{1}}{2^{n+1}C_{2}}\Big)^{\tfrac{n}{s}}\sim s^{\tfrac{n}{s}}\quad\text{as }s\to 1^{-}.

According to
[
9
, Remark 1.5]
, the interval
\mathcal{I}
is well defined as soon as

is sufficiently small. Consequently, all the parameters involved in assumption (H14), as well as
\bar{}\varepsilon
,
{C_{DE}}
and
{r_{DE}}
in Theorem
4.3
, remain uniformly bounded as

.

In conclusion, as

, the estimate (
1.15
) recovers the stability result for the classical perimeter in (
1.4
).

7.
A quantitative fractional Cheeger inequality

In this Section, we show some quantitative versions of the fractional Cheeger inequality, which is the nonlocal analogue of the result contained in
[
18
]
and
[
28
]
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set with finite measure and let
m>\frac{n-s}{n}
. We recall that the
fractional Cheeger constant
(or
(m,s)
-Cheeger constant
) of
\Omega
is given by

h_{m,s}(\Omega):=\inf\left\{\frac{P_{s}(E)}{|E|^{m}},\;\;E\subset\Omega\right\}.

We call a
fractional Cheeger set
or a
(m,s)
-Cheeger set
any minimizer of the above infimum problem, whose existence follows by a standard application of the classical direct method in the Calculus of Variations (see e.g. Proposition 5.3 in
[
7
]
, for the case
m=1
).

Let
B
denote the Euclidean unit ball. The following
fractional Cheeger inequality
holds true

(7.1)

|\Omega|^{m-\frac{n-s}{n}}h_{m,s}(\Omega)\geq|B|^{m-\frac{n-s}{n}}h_{m,s}(B).

For the case
m=1
, the fractional Cheeger constant and properties of fractional Cheeger sets have been studied in
[
7
]
.

Also, we say that
\Omega
is
(m,s)
-calibrable
if it is an
(m,s)
-Cheeger set of itself, i.e. if

h_{m,s}(\Omega)=\frac{P_{s}(\Omega)}{|\Omega|^{m}}.

Remark 7.1
.

As in the local case, any ball
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
(m,s)
-calibrable. This is a direct consequence of the nonlocal isoperimetric inequality (
1.8
), which gives for every
E\subset B

\frac{P_{s}(E)}{|E|^{m}}\geq\frac{P_{s}(B)}{|B|^{m}}\,\left(\frac{|B|}{|E|}\right)^{\frac{sm}{n}}\geq\frac{P_{s}(B)}{|B|^{m}}.

The aim of this Section is to provide a quantitative version of (
7.1
), following the approach in
[
28
]
.

Thanks to the scaling property of the Cheeger constant, i.e.
h_{m,s}(\lambda\Omega)=\lambda^{(1-m)n-s}h_{m,s}(\Omega)
for any
\lambda>0
, it is enough to prove the claim for
\Omega
such that
|\Omega|=\omega_{n}
and
B_{r}=B
is the unit ball.

Moreover, since by (
2.4
), we know that
V_{s}(B)=\frac{n\omega_{n}}{n-s}
, it follows that

\zeta_{s}(\Omega)\leq 1
. Therefore, the inequality (
1.22
) immediately follows for sets
\Omega
such that
h_{m,s}(\Omega)\geq 2h_{m,s}(B)
, by choosing
\kappa_{m,n,s}\leq 1
. Therefore, hereafter, let us consider
\Omega
with volume
\omega_{n}
and such that
h_{m,s}(\Omega)<2h_{m,s}(B)
. Let us denote by
E
a
(m,s)
-Cheeger set of
\Omega
.

First of all, let us prove the two following estimates

(7.2)

|E|\geq|\Omega|\left(\frac{h_{m,s}(B)}{h_{m,s}(\Omega)}\right)^{\frac{1}{m-\frac{n-s}{n}}}

and

(7.3)

\delta_{s}(E)\leq\frac{h_{m,s}(\Omega)-h_{m,s}(B)}{h_{m,s}(B)}.

We start by proving (
7.2
). By the fractional isoperimetric inequality (
1.8
), we know that

\frac{P_{s}(E)}{|E|^{m}}\geq\frac{P_{s}(B)}{|B|^{\frac{n-s}{n}}}\cdot|E|^{\frac{n-s}{n}-m}=:\mathcal{I}_{n,s}|E|^{\frac{n-s}{n}-m}

where
\mathcal{I}_{n,s}:=\frac{P_{s}(B)}{|B|^{\frac{n-s}{s}}}
denotes the
s
-isoperimetric constant. Hence, since
E
is a
(m,s)
-Cheeger set of
\Omega
, by Remark
7.1
, we deduce that

|E|^{m-\frac{n-s}{n}}\geq\frac{P_{s}(B)}{|B|^{\frac{n-s}{n}}}\cdot\frac{1}{h_{m,s}(\Omega)}=|B|^{m-\frac{n-s}{n}}\frac{h_{m,s}(B)}{h_{m,s}(\Omega)},

which proves (
7.2
).

Let us show now (
7.3
). Again by Remark
7.1
, we have that

h_{m,s}(\Omega)-h_{m,s}(B)\geq\frac{P_{s}(E)}{|E|^{\frac{n-s}{n}}}\cdot|E|^{\frac{n-s}{n}-m}-\frac{P_{s}(B)}{|B|^{\frac{n-s}{n}}}\cdot|B|^{\frac{n-s}{n}-m}.

Diving the above inequality by
\mathcal{I}_{n,s}
and recalling the definition of
\delta_{s}(E)
, we get

\begin{split}\frac{h_{m,s}(\Omega)-h_{m,s}(B)}{\mathcal{I}_{n,s}}&\geq(1+\delta_{s}(E))|E|^{\frac{n-s}{n}-m}-|B|^{\frac{n-s}{n}-m}\\
&=\delta_{s}(E)|E|^{\frac{n-s}{n}-m}+\left(|E|^{\frac{n-s}{n}-m}-|B|^{\frac{n-s}{n}-m}\right).\end{split}

Since
m>\frac{n-s}{n}
and
|E|\leq|\Omega|=|B|
, we have that the last term on the right-hand side is non-negative. This implies that

\frac{h_{m,s}(\Omega)-h_{m,s}(B)}{\mathcal{I}_{n,s}}\geq\delta_{s}(E)|E|^{\frac{n-s}{n}-m}\geq\delta_{s}(E)|B|^{\frac{n-s}{n}-m}.

Recalling the definition of
\mathcal{I}_{n,s}
and using again Remark
7.1
, we obtain

\delta_{s}(E)\leq\frac{h_{m,s}(\Omega)-h_{m,s}(B)}{h_{m,s}(B)},

and thus the proof of (
7.3
) is concluded.

Now, let us start by estimating
\zeta_{s}(\Omega)
in terms of
\zeta_{s}(E)
. Up to translating
E
(and therefore
\Omega
) we may assume that
E
is
V_{s}
-centered at the origin, i.e.

{V_{s}(E)=\int_{E}\frac{1}{|x|^{s}}\,dx}

By setting
r_{E}>0
the radius of the ball
E^{*}
and adding and removing
r_{E}^{n-s}
in the definition of
\zeta_{s}(\Omega)

we obtain

(7.4)

\begin{split}\zeta_{s}(\Omega)&=1-r_{E}^{n-s}+r_{E}^{n-s}-\frac{V_{s}(\Omega)}{V_{s}(B)}=1-\frac{P_{s}(E^{*})}{P_{s}(B)}+\frac{V_{s}(E^{*})}{V_{s}(B)}-\frac{V_{s}(\Omega)}{V_{s}(B)}\\
&\leq\,\frac{P_{s}(B)-P_{s}(E^{*})}{P_{s}(B)}+\frac{V_{s}(E^{*})-V_{s}(E)}{V_{s}(E^{*})}=\,\frac{P_{s}(B)-P_{s}(E^{*})}{P_{s}(B)}+\zeta_{s}(E).\end{split}

where in the first equality we have used (
iii
) of Proposition
2.1
and (
iii
) of Proposition
2.2
while the last inequality follows by observing that since
E\subset\Omega
, we have
E^{*}\subset B
,
V_{s}(E)\leq V_{s}(\Omega)
and
V_{s}(E^{*})\leq V_{s}(B)
.

Now to get (
1.22
), we want to estimate both terms on the right hand side through the re-normalized difference of the Cheeger constants
(h_{m,s}(\Omega)-h_{m,s}(B))h_{m,s}(B)^{-1}
. At first, by exploiting (
7.2
), we get

\frac{P_{s}(B)-P_{s}(E^{*})}{P_{s}(B)}=1-\left(\frac{|E|}{|\Omega|}\right)^{\frac{n-s}{n}}\leq 1-\left(\frac{h_{m,s}(B)}{h_{m,s}(\Omega)}\right)^{\frac{n-s}{mn-(n-s)}}

consequently, since
h_{m,s}(\Omega)\geq h_{m,s}(B)
, it follows

\frac{P_{s}(B)-P_{s}(E^{*})}{P_{s}(B)}\leq 1-\left(\frac{h_{m,s}(B)}{h_{m,s}(\Omega)}\right)^{a}=\frac{h_{m,s}(\Omega)^{a}-h_{m,s}(B)^{a}}{h_{m,s}(\Omega)^{a}}

with
a:=\frac{n-s}{mn-(n-s)}+1\geq 1
. Then, by recalling the standard inequality
t^{a}-\tau^{a}\leq at^{a-1}(t-\tau)
whenever
a\geq 1
, and
\tau\in(0,t]
, we finally obtain

(7.5)

\frac{P_{s}(B)-P_{s}(E^{*})}{{P_{s}(B)}}\leq\left(\frac{n-s}{mn-(n-s)}+1\right)\frac{h_{m,s}(\Omega)-h_{m,s}(B)}{h_{m,s}(\Omega)}.

Finally, in order to estimate
\zeta_{s}(E)
, by (
7.3
) and (
6.13
) we have

(7.6)

\zeta_{s}(E)\leq\frac{1}{C}\delta_{s}(E)\leq\frac{1}{C}\frac{h_{m,s}(\Omega)-h_{m,s}(B)}{h_{m,s}(\Omega)},

where the constant
C
is the one appearing in (
6.13
). Thus, combining (
7.4
), (
7.5
) and (
7.6
), we find

\zeta_{s}(\Omega)\leq\left(\frac{n-s}{mn-(n-s)}+1\right)\left(\frac{h_{m,s}(\Omega)-h_{m,s}(B)}{h_{m,s}(\Omega)}\right)+\frac{1}{C}\left(\frac{h_{m,s}(\Omega)-h_{m,s}(B)}{h_{m,s}(\Omega)}\right)

which yields the claim by setting

\kappa({m,n,s}):=\left(\frac{n-s}{mn-(n-s)}+1+\frac{1}{C}\right)^{-1}.

∎

In light of (
2.17
), a quantitative version of (
7.1
), involving Frankel asymmetry holds.

(7.7)

\frac{h_{m,s}(\Omega)-h_{m,s}(B_{r})}{h_{m,s}(B_{r})}\geq\gamma(m,n,s)\,\alpha^{2}(\Omega),\qquad\text{with }|B_{r}|=|\Omega|.

) and (

) are established, inequality (

]
.

.

According to Remark
6.3

s\to 1^{-}
, the estimates (

) and (

7.1.
Failure of the stability estimate for fractional Cheeger constant with the index
\beta_{s}
.

At this stage, it is natural to investigate whether a stability estimate for the fractional Cheeger constant involving the oscillation index
\beta_{s}
could hold. In the local setting, the failure of such an estimate was demonstrated in
[
28
, Subsection 2.2]
. First, we show that one of the counterexamples constructed in
[
28
]
, based on planar sets with increasing boundary oscillations, also applies in the nonlocal setting. We then present an alternative counterexample inspired by the fractal-type sets discussed in
[
33
]
, which turn out to be suitable in the nonlocal case as well.

More precisely, we show that there does not exist any constant
c>0
such that the inequality

(7.8)

\frac{h_{m,s}(\Omega)-h_{m,s}(B_{r})}{h_{m,s}(B_{r})}\geq c\,\beta_{s}^{2}(\Omega),\qquad\text{with }|B_{r}|=|\Omega|,

holds. This is proved by constructing some suitable families of sets.

First of all, fix a universal constant
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, and consider the family of bounded planar sets
\{\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
, whose boundary is given by

\partial\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{j}=\{\,\theta(1+\varepsilon\,u(\theta))\,:\,\theta\in\partial B_{1}\},\qquad\text{with }\ u(\theta):=\sin(2j\,\theta),

where we systematically identify
\partial B_{1}\equiv\mathbb{S}^{1}
with the interval
[0,2\pi]
.
The volume of
\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{j}
is

(7.9)

\begin{split}|\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{j}|&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\bigl(1+u(\theta)\bigr)^{2}\,d\theta=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\bigl(1+2u(\theta)+u^{2}(\theta)\bigr)\,d\theta\\
&=\pi+\int_{0}^{2\pi}\sin^{2}(2j\,\theta)\,d\theta=\pi\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\right).\end{split}

Let us now focus on
P_{s}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{j})
. By
[
16
, (2.20)]
, for
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, we have

(7.10)

P_{s}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{j})=\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\,g(\varepsilon)+\frac{P_{s}(B_{1})}{P(B_{1})}\,h(\varepsilon),

where we have set

g(\varepsilon):=\int_{\partial B_{1}}\int_{\partial B_{1}}\biggl(\int_{u(y)}^{u(x)}\int_{u(y)}^{u(x)}f_{|x-y|}(1+\varepsilon r,1+\varepsilon\varrho)\,dr\,d\varrho\biggr)\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{x}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{y},

with

f_{\theta}(r,\varrho):=\frac{r\varrho}{(|r-\varrho|^{2}+r\varrho\,\theta^{2})^{\frac{2+s}{2}}},\quad\text{for all }r,\varrho,\theta>0,

and

h(\varepsilon):=\int_{\partial B_{1}}(1+\varepsilon u(x))^{2-s}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{x}.

Hence, in particular,

(7.11)

P_{s}(\Omega_{j}^{\varepsilon})\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial B_{1}}\int_{\partial B_{1}}\biggl(\int_{1+\varepsilon u(y)}^{1+\varepsilon u(x)}\int_{1+\varepsilon u(y)}^{1+\varepsilon u(x)}\frac{r\varrho}{(|r-\varrho|^{2}+r\varrho\,|x-y|^{2})^{\frac{2+s}{2}}}\,dr\,d\varrho\biggl)\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{x}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{y},

Now, for
m=0,\dots,j-1
, let us set

x_{m}:=\frac{\pi}{4j}+m\frac{\pi}{j},\qquad y_{m}:=\frac{3\pi}{4j}+m\frac{\pi}{j},

and
r:=\frac{1}{4j}
. Define

I_{m}:=\{x\in[0,2\pi]\,:\,|x-x_{m}|\leq r\},\qquad J_{m}:=\{y\in[0,2\pi]\,:\,|y-y_{m}|\leq r\},

so that, in particular,
|I_{m}|=|J_{m}|=2r=\frac{1}{2j}
. Then, for any
x\in I_{m}
and
y\in J_{m}
, we have

(7.12)

|x-y|\leq|x-x_{m}|+|x_{m}-y_{m}|+|y_{m}-y|\leq 2r+\frac{\pi}{2j}=\frac{\pi/2+1/2}{j}<\frac{4}{j},

and
u(x)=\sin(2jx)>\frac{1}{2}
and
u(y)=\sin(2jy)<-\frac{1}{2}
, which implies

(7.13)

|u(x)-u(y)|=|\sin(2jx)-\sin(2jy)|=\sin(2jx)-\sin(2jy)\geq\frac{1}{2}-\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)=1.

For any
x\in I_{m}
and
y\in J_{m}
, defining also

\mathcal{S}:=\left\{(r,\varrho)\in[1+\varepsilon u(y),1+\varepsilon u(x)]\times[1+\varepsilon u(y),1+\varepsilon u(x)]:\ |r-\varrho|\leq\frac{|x-y|}{2}\right\},

then, for
j\in\N
sufficiently large,
|\mathcal{S}|\geq C|x-y|
, for some universal constant
C>0
. Moreover, for any
r,\varrho\in\mathcal{S}
, we have

r\varrho\geq(1+\varepsilon u(y))^{2}\geq(1-\varepsilon)^{2}

and

|r-\varrho|^{2}+r\varrho|x-y|^{2}\leq\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4}+(1+\varepsilon u(x))|x-y|^{2}\leq\left(\frac{1}{4}+(1+\varepsilon)^{2}\right)|x-y|^{2}.

Consequently,

(7.14)

\begin{split}\int_{1+\varepsilon u(y)}^{1+\varepsilon u(x)}\hskip-3.00003pt\int_{1+\varepsilon u(y)}^{1+\varepsilon u(x)}\frac{r\varrho}{(|r-\varrho|^{2}+r\varrho\,|x-y|^{2})^{\frac{2+s}{2}}}\,dr\,d\varrho&\geq\int\hskip-6.00006pt\int_{\mathcal{S}}\frac{(1-\varepsilon)^{2}}{(\frac{1}{4}+(1+\varepsilon)^{2})^{\frac{2+s}{2}}|x-y|^{2+s}}\,dr\,d\varrho\\
&=\frac{C_{0}}{|x-y|^{2+s}}|\mathcal{S}|\geq\frac{C_{0}}{|x-y|^{1+s}}\end{split}

for some suitable constant
C_{0}>0
, depending only on
s
and
\varepsilon
, whose value is allowed to change from line to line. Finally, using (
7.11
), (
7.12
), (
7.13
) and (
7.14
), we obtain

\begin{split}P_{s}(\Omega_{j}^{\varepsilon})&\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial B_{1}}\int_{\partial B_{1}}\biggl(\int_{1+\varepsilon u(y)}^{1+\varepsilon u(x)}\int_{1+\varepsilon u(y)}^{1+\varepsilon u(x)}\frac{r\varrho}{(|r-\varrho|^{2}+r\varrho\,|x-y|^{2})^{\frac{2+s}{2}}}\,dr\,d\varrho\biggl)\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{x}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{y}\\
&\geq\sum_{m=0}^{j-1}\int_{I_{m}}\int_{J_{m}}\biggl(\int_{1+\varepsilon u(y)}^{1+\varepsilon u(x)}\int_{1+\varepsilon u(y)}^{1+\varepsilon u(x)}\frac{r\varrho}{(|r-\varrho|^{2}+r\varrho\,|x-y|^{2})^{\frac{2+s}{2}}}\,dr\,d\varrho\biggl)\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{x}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{y}\\
&\geq\sum_{m=0}^{j-1}\int_{I_{m}}\int_{J_{m}}\frac{C_{0}}{|x-y|^{1+s}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{x}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{y}\\
&\geq\left(\frac{j}{4}\right)^{1+s}C_{0}\,\sum_{m=0}^{j-1}\int_{I_{m}}\int_{J_{m}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{x}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{y}=C_{0}\left(\frac{j}{4}\right)^{1+s}j\,\frac{1}{4j^{2}}=C_{0}\,j^{s},\end{split}

which implies that
P_{s}(\Omega_{j}^{\varepsilon})\to+\infty
as
j\to+\infty
.

On the other hand, by the construction of the sets
\{\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
, we have
B_{1-\varepsilon}\subset\Omega_{j}
for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon
sufficiently small. Hence,

h_{m,s}(B_{1-\varepsilon})=h_{m,s}(B_{1})(1-\varepsilon)^{2-s-2m}

provides an upper bound (uniformly in
j
) for
h_{m,s}(\Omega_{j}^{\varepsilon})
, that is,

(7.15)

h_{m,s}(\Omega_{j}^{\varepsilon})\leq h_{m,s}(B_{1})(1-\varepsilon)^{2-s-2m}.

Moreover, by (
7.9
) and (
7.1
), we also know that

(7.16)

h_{m,s}(B)=h_{m,s}(B_{1})\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\right)^{\frac{2-s-2m}{2}}\leq h_{m,s}(\Omega_{j}^{\varepsilon}),

where
B
is the ball having the same volume as
\Omega_{j}^{\varepsilon}
.
Thus,
h_{m,s}(\Omega_{j}^{\varepsilon})
is uniformly bounded both above and below, independently of
j
, while recalling (
1.18
), we have

\beta_{s}^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{j})\geq\delta_{s}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{j})=\frac{P_{s}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{j})-P_{s}(B)}{P_{s}(B)}\to+\infty,\quad\text{as }j\to+\infty,

which, together with (
7.15
) and (
7.16
), contradicts (
7.8
).

We now turn to the second counterexample to (
7.8
), which is based on fractal-type sets in arbitrary dimension. To this end, we begin with the following result.

Proposition 7.4
.

Let
n\geq 2
. For every
\sigma\in(0,1)
and
c_{0}>0
there exists a sequence of set
\{\Omega_{M}\}_{M\in\N}
with the following properties

(
i
)

There exists the
\lim_{M\to\infty}P_{s}(\Omega_{M})
and it holds

\lim_{M\to\infty}P_{s}(\Omega_{M})<\infty\quad\text{ for }\,s\in(0,\sigma)\quad\textrm{and}\quad\lim_{M\to\infty}P_{s}(\Omega_{M})=\infty\quad\text{ for }\,s\in[\sigma,1).

(
ii
)

There exists a set
\Omega_{\infty}
such that

\Omega_{M}\to\Omega_{\infty}\text{in }L^{1}\,\text{ as }M\to+\infty\quad\text{and}\quad|\Omega_{M}|\leq|\Omega_{\infty}|<\infty,\text{ for any }M\in\N

and

P_{s}(\Omega_{\infty})<\infty\quad\text{ for }\,s\in(0,\sigma)\quad\textrm{and}\quad P_{s}(\Omega_{\infty})=\infty\quad\text{ for }\,s\in[\sigma,1).

(
iii
)

For any
M\in\N
, up to translation,

B_{c_{0}}\subset\Omega_{M}.

Proof.
To construct the family of sets
\{\Omega_{M}\}_{M\in\mathbb{N}}
, we employ the construction of fractal-type sets in
[
33
]
, which we briefly check below.

Specifically, let
T_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set with
0<|T_{0}|<\infty
and finite perimeter, such that, up to translation,
B_{c_{0}}\subset T_{0}
. Let
a,b\in\mathbb{N}
, with
b\geq 2
arbitrary. Consider the following family of roto-translations of a scaling of
T_{0}
:

T_{k}^{i}:=F_{k}^{i}(T_{0}):=\mathcal{R}_{k}^{i}\big(\lambda^{-k}T_{0}\big)+x_{k}^{i},

where
\lambda:=b^{\frac{1}{n-\sigma}}>1
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
1\leq i\leq ab^{k-1}
,
\mathcal{R}_{k}^{i}\in SO(n)
, and
x_{k}^{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
are chosen to ensure that the sets do not overlap, i.e.,

|T_{k}^{i}\cap T_{h}^{j}|=0\quad\text{for all }(k,i)\neq(h,j)\quad\text{ and }\quad|T^{i}_{k}\cap T_{0}|=0\quad\text{for all }k,i.

Then, for every
M\in\mathbb{N}
, we define

\Omega_{M}:=T_{0}\cup\bigcup_{k=1}^{M}\bigcup_{i=1}^{ab^{k-1}}T_{k}^{i},\quad\text{and}\quad\Omega_{\infty}:=T_{0}\cup\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{i=1}^{ab^{k-1}}T_{k}^{i}.

Furthermore, we assume the existence of a set
S_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega_{\infty}
, with
|S_{0}|>0
, such that

S_{k}^{i}:=F_{k}^{i}(S_{0})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega_{\infty}\quad\text{for all }k\in\mathbb{N}\text{ and}\,1\leq i\leq ab^{k-1}.

Such an assumption is not restrictive and can be ensured by an appropriate choice of the initial set
T_{0}
, see
[
33
, Remark 3.9]
.

For any couple of disjoint sets
A,\,B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let

\mathcal{L}_{s}(A,B):=\int_{A}\int_{B}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,dx\,dy.

Then, we obtain

\begin{split}P_{s}(\Omega_{M})&=\mathcal{L}_{s}(\Omega_{M},\Omega_{M}^{c})=\mathcal{L}_{s}(T_{0},\Omega_{M}^{c})+\sum_{k=1}^{M}\sum_{i=1}^{ab^{k-1}}\mathcal{L}_{s}(T_{k}^{i},\Omega_{M}^{c})\\
&\leq\mathcal{L}_{s}(T_{0},T_{0}^{c})+\sum_{k=1}^{M}\sum_{i=1}^{ab^{k-1}}\mathcal{L}_{s}(T_{k}^{i},(T_{k}^{i})^{c})=P_{s}(T_{0})+\sum_{k=1}^{M}\sum_{i=1}^{ab^{k-1}}\mathcal{L}_{s}(F_{k}^{i}(T_{0}),F_{k}^{i}(T_{0}^{c}))\\
&=P_{s}(T_{0})\left(1+\frac{a}{\lambda^{n-s}}\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}\Big(\frac{b}{\lambda^{n-s}}\Big)^{k}\right),\end{split}

and

\begin{split}P_{s}(\Omega_{M})&=\mathcal{L}_{s}(\Omega_{M},\Omega_{M}^{c})=\mathcal{L}_{s}(T_{0},\Omega_{M}^{c})+\sum_{k=1}^{M}\sum_{i=1}^{ab^{k-1}}\mathcal{L}_{s}(T_{k}^{i},\Omega_{M}^{c})\\
&\geq\mathcal{L}_{s}(T_{0},S_{0})+\sum_{k=1}^{M}\sum_{i=1}^{ab^{k-1}}\mathcal{L}_{s}(T_{k}^{i},S_{k}^{i})=\mathcal{L}_{s}(T_{0},S_{0})+\sum_{k=1}^{M}\sum_{i=1}^{ab^{k-1}}\mathcal{L}_{s}(F_{k}^{i}(T_{0}),F_{k}^{i}(S_{0}))\\
&=\mathcal{L}_{s}(T_{0},S_{0})\left(1+\frac{a}{\lambda^{n-s}}\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}\Big(\frac{b}{\lambda^{n-s}}\Big)^{k}\right).\end{split}

Notice that, since
P(T_{0})<\infty
, we have

\mathcal{L}_{s}(T_{0},S_{0})\leq\mathcal{L}_{s}(T_{0},T_{0}^{c})=P_{s}(T_{0})<\infty,

for every
s\in(0,1)
(see, e.g.,
[
33
, Proposition 2.1]
). This proves (
i
), since
\frac{b}{\lambda^{n-s}}=b^{\frac{s-\sigma}{n-\sigma}}<1
, when
s<\sigma
.

Moreover, for any
M\in\N
, we have that
\Omega_{M}\subset\Omega_{\infty}
and

0<|\Omega_{M}|<|\Omega_{\infty}|\leq|T_{0}|+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{ab^{k-1}}|T_{k}^{i}|=|T_{0}|\left(1+\frac{a}{\lambda^{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{b}{\lambda^{n}}\Big)^{k}\right)<\infty

and

|\Omega_{M}\Delta\Omega_{\infty}|=|\Omega_{\infty}\setminus\Omega_{M}|=\sum_{k=M+1}^{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{ab^{k-1}}|T^{i}_{k}|=\frac{a}{\lambda^{n}}\sum_{k=M}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{b}{\lambda^{n}}\Big)^{k}\to 0\quad\text{ as }\,M\to\infty,

since
\frac{b}{\lambda^{n}}=b^{-\frac{\sigma}{n-\sigma}}<1
, as
\sigma>0
. This, together with (
i
), concludes the proof of (
ii
). Finally, (
iii
) follows trivially by construction.

\Box

Explicit examples of families of sets satisfying the assumptions of Proposition
7.4
can be found in
[
33
]
. Fixed
\sigma\in(0,1)
, let us now explain how the family of sets
\{\Omega_{M}\}_{M\in\mathbb{N}}
can be used to construct a counterexample to inequality (
7.8
), when
s\in[\sigma,1)
.

For each
M\in\mathbb{N}
, let
B_{M}
and
B_{\infty}
denote the balls centered at the origin such that
|B_{M}|=|\Omega_{M}|
and
|B_{\infty}|=|\Omega_{\infty}|
, respectively. By point (
iii
) of Proposition
7.4
, we have

h_{m,s}(B_{c_{0}})\geq h_{m,s}(\Omega_{M}),\quad\text{for all }M\in\mathbb{N}.

Moreover, combining (
7.1
) with point (
ii
) of Proposition
7.4
, it follows that

h_{m,s}(\Omega_{M})\geq h_{m,s}(B_{M})\geq h_{m,s}(B_{\infty}),\quad\text{for all }M\in\mathbb{N}.

This implies that
h_{m,s}(\Omega_{M})
is uniformly bounded both above and below, independently of
M
. Therefore, the left-hand side of (
7.8
) remains uniformly bounded as
M\to\infty
.

On the other hand, by (
1.18
), we obtain

\beta_{s}^{2}(\Omega_{M})\geq\delta_{s}(\Omega_{M})=\frac{P_{s}(\Omega_{M})-P_{s}(B_{M})}{P_{s}(B_{M})}\geq\frac{P_{s}(\Omega_{M})-P_{s}(B_{\infty})}{P_{s}(B_{\infty})}.

Hence, by point (
i
) of Proposition
7.4
,
\beta_{s}^{2}(\Omega_{M})
diverges as
M\to\infty
. Consequently, no constant
c>0
can make inequality (
7.8
) valid for all
M\in\mathbb{N}
, which completes the counterexample.

Early neurological deterioration after endovascular treatment in acute ischemic stroke with large vessel occlusion
Authors: Bin Han, Raynald, Muhammad Firdaus, Dapeng Sun, Yaxin Wu, XuTong, Baixue Jia, Anxin Wang, Dapeng Mo, Feng Gao, Ning Ma, Yaxuan Sun, Zhongrong Miao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeThis study aimed to explore the occurrence, identify independent predictors, and assess the clinical implications of END following EVT in individuals with acute LVO.MethodsParticipants were enrolled from the ANGEL-ACT registry. END was characterized by a rise of ≥ 4 points in the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score from baseline to 24 h post-EVT. We conducted logistic regression analyses to pinpoint independent predictors of END.ResultsAmong the 1557 patients included in the study, the occurrence of END stood at 9.8%. Factors independently associated with END included an admission NIHSS (adjusted odds ratio (aOR) = 4.067, 95% CI 2.513 to 6.582, p < 0.0001), an admission neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) exceeding 5 (aOR = 1.811, 95% CI 1.246 to 2.632, p = 0.0018), the use of general anesthesia (GA) (aOR = 0.474, 95% CI 0.330 to 0.682, p < 0.0001), longer procedure duration (aOR = 5.500, 95% CI 1.45 to 20.871, p = 0.0122), and more than three retrieval attempts (aOR = 1.294, 95% CI 1.177 to 1.422, p < 0.0001).ConclusionsEND manifested in 9.8% of patients undergoing endovascular therapy (EVT) for acute large vessel occlusion (LVO). This investigation highlights various independent predictors of END, providing valuable insights for daily clinical practice aimed at enhancing the effectiveness of intravascular thrombectomy.RegistrationURL: https://www.clinicaltrials.gov; Unique identifier: NCT03370939; registration date: 11th November 2017.Keywords: Early neurological deterioration, Large vessel occlusion, Endovascular treatment, Predictors, Clinical outcomes

Introduction

The safety and efficacy of endovascular treatment (EVT) for acute large vessel occlusion (LVO) have been substantiated through numerous pivotal randomized controlled trials. Subsequently, EVT has emerged as the established therapeutic approach for individuals with acute ischemic stroke (AIS) within the anterior cerebral circulation, attributable to intracranial large vessel occlusion (LVO) [1]. Nevertheless, a subset of patients may encounter early neurological deterioration (END), a phenomenon characterized by a progressive decline in the patient’s clinical status in the initial period following the acute stroke event. An augmentation of 4 or more points in the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score is commonly employed as the operational definition for END [2].

END subsequent to AIS serves as a robust indicator of unfavorable functional outcomes. However, there is a notable lack of comprehensive investigations into the predictive factors associated with END. The absence of large-scale studies addressing END prediction in the context of real-world application of EVT remains a concern. Considering this, it is imperative to further explore the predictor factors for END. Hence, the present study was aimed to determine the incidence and potential predictors of END in patients enrolled in the ANGEL ACT registry, a nationwide, prospective, multicenter initiative encompassing 1793 consecutive adult patients within the Chinese population. We expect that this study may provide valuable insights into preventing adverse events and devising future prevention strategies.

Methods

Study population

This study is a secondary analysis of data derived from the prospective, multicenter, observational ANGEL-ACT registry. This registry constituted a nationwide prospective cohort comprising 1793 consecutive adult patients who had acute Ischemic stroke (AIS) due to large vessel occlusion (LVO). These patients underwent endovascular treatment (EVT) at 111 different hospitals across 26 provinces in China, between the period from November 2017 to March 2019. Detailed information regarding the registry’s methodologies, including the criteria for inclusion and exclusion, as well as the data collection process, is in accordance with previously published study [3]. This study was conducted with the approval of the ethics committees affiliated with the leading center. Prior to their involvement in the study, patients or their legally authorized representatives provided written informed consent. Exclusion criteria were those individuals lacking an EVT record and those with missing values pertaining to factors including admission NIHSS (National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale), 24-hour NIHSS, and the final modified Thrombolysis in Cerebral Ischemia (mTICI) score.

Data collection

All variables including demographic characteristics, medical history, vital signs, stroke severity (e.g., NIHSS), laboratory and imaging results, procedural specifics, periprocedural management, time-related metrics, functional outcomes (e.g., modified Rankin Scale (mRS) scores, a validated and widely accepted measure of post-stroke disability) [4], and adverse events occurring within 90 days post-procedure, were meticulously and prospectively documented. The collection of NIHSS and mRS scores was performed by investigators who had received appropriate training and possessed the necessary qualifications. Baseline computed tomography (CT), magnetic resonance (MR), CT angiography, MR angiography imaging, DSA (Digital Subtraction Angiography), and postprocedural CT scans were subject to evaluation by an imaging core laboratory. This laboratory conducted assessments in a manner that ensured their complete independence from clinical data and outcome information. Two neuroradiologists conducted a separate, unbiased evaluation of all imaging, with a third expert available for adjudication in instances where consensus was required.

The Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score (ASPECTS) [5] for anterior circulation strokes and Posterior Circulation ASPECTS [6] for posterior circulation strokes were employed in the assessment of baseline CT images. In DSA assessments, factors such as the presence of tandem extracranial stenosis and occlusion, underlying intracranial atherosclerotic stenosis (defined as either a fixed stenosis degree exceeding 70% or stenosis greater than 50% accompanied by distal blood flow impairment or evidence of recurrent reocclusion), and complete recanalization (mTICI score of 3) [7] were examined. For patients for whom imaging data could not be acquired or in cases where intraprocedural complications occurred during DSA, data reported by the respective sites were utilized. The imaging review criteria employed by local investigators adhered to the same standards as those employed by the core laboratory.

Outcome measurement

The term “Early Neurological Deterioration” (END) was defined as a neurological deterioration occurring 24 h post-treatment, characterized by an increase of at least four points in the NIHSS score in comparison to the baseline NIHSS score [2]. We subsequently conducted a comparative analysis between the group of patients exhibiting END and those without END. We used the mRS score at 90- day follow up to evaluate the clinical outcomes of patients. This assessment was conducted through a standardized telephone interview conducted by trained investigators who were intentionally kept unaware of the baseline and procedural data. The NIHSS scores at the 24-hour and 7-day marks were evaluated by local neurologists.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were characterized using the median along with the Interquartile Range (IQR), while categorical variables were presented in frequencies and percentages. To compare variables in baseline and procedural characteristics between the two groups, univariable analysis using the Mann–Whitney U test for continuous variables and either the χ2 or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables. Variables with a significance level of p < 0.05 in the univariable analysis were considered as potential predictors for inclusion in the multivariable logistic regression model. Concerning the multivariable logistic regression, collinearity was assessed using the variance inflation factor. Subsequently, receiver operating characteristic analyses were employed to identify the optimal cut-off values for admission NIHSS, admission neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), the use of general anesthesia (yes vs. no), procedure duration time, and the number of EVT attempts in predicting END. These continuous variables were then categorized based on the determined cut-off values. A backward stepwise multivariable logistic regression analysis was conducted, employing a removal criterion of p > 0.05, to identify the independent predictors of END. In evaluating the relationship between END and the 90-day outcomes, multivariable logistic regression models were applied, adjusting for covariates with a significance level of p < 0.05 as determined in the univariable analysis between both groups. Statistical significance was set at a p value of < 0.05 (two-sided). The entirety of the statistical analyses was carried out utilizing SAS V. 9.4 software, developed by SAS Institute in Cary, North Carolina, USA.

Baseline and procedural characteristics

In the univariate analyses, patients who experienced END exhibited several distinctive characteristics compared to those without END. Specifically, these differences included a lower median admission NIHSS score (13.5 (8–18) vs. 17.5 (12–22), p < 0.001) and a higher median admission NLR (7.4 (3.3–9.6) vs. 5.9 (2.6–7.5), p = 0.0031). Moreover, the prevalence of large artery atherosclerosis (45.5% vs. 51.2%) and cardioembolism (27.9% vs. 33.7%) was comparatively lower in the END group. Significantly, the use of general anesthesia (GA) was notably higher in patients who experienced END (55.8% vs. 36.8%, p < 0.0001). Additionally, the frequency of employing direct aspiration as the first-line approach was higher in the END group (27.3% vs. 20.2%, p = 0.0469), as was the rate of achieving complete recanalization (56.5% vs. 68.1%, p = 0.005). Patients with END also underwent a higher number of retrieval attempts > 3 times (23.4% vs. 12%, p = 0.0002) and experienced less first-pass recanalization (32.2% vs. 50.4%, p < 0.0001). Furthermore, the median onset-to-puncture time (OTP) and procedure duration were significantly longer in the END group (498.6 (230–500) min vs. 392.5 (210–440) min, p = 0.023; and 128.9 (62–180) min vs. 94.7 (51–123) min, p < 0.001, respectively), when compared to patients without END (Table 1).

Independent predictors of END

A multivariable logistic analysis was conducted to identify the independent predictors of END, as summarized in Table 2. Following the assessment for collinearity, potential confounding factors, including admission NIHSS score, admission NLR, stroke subtypes determined by TOAST criteria, the use of GA, direct aspiration as the first-line approach, achievement of complete recanalization, retrieval attempts exceeding three times, first-pass recanalization, OTP, and procedure duration, were adjusted for. The analysis revealed that admission NIHSS (odds ratio (OR) = 4.067, 95% CI 2.513 to 6.582, p < 0.0001), admission NLR (OR = 1.811, 95% CI 1.246 to 2.632, p = 0.0018), GA (OR = 0.474, 95% CI 0.330 to 0.682, p < 0.0001), procedure duration (OR = 5.500, 95% CI 1.45 to 20.871, p = 0.0122), and retrieval attempts exceeding three times (OR = 1.294, 95% CI 1.177 to 1.422, p < 0.0001) emerged as the independent predictors of END.

Discussion

In this Angel-ACT registry, we observed a notable 9.8% occurrence of early neurologic deterioration (END), showing a lower rate than previous studies [8, 9]. This variance might be attributed to the absence of a standardized definition for END. Our study found correlations between END risk and several factors: baseline NIHSS, baseline neutrophil-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), general anesthesia, retrieval attempts, and procedure duration showed associations. We further categorized these factors into clinically unmodifiable and modifiable ones during hospitalization. Prior studies showed END’s independent association with adverse 90-day outcomes [8–10], highlighting the imperative to scrutinize predictors of END, particularly the modifiable factors.

Baseline NIHSS score was considered an unmodifiable clinical variable at admission. In addition to reflecting neurological deficit severity, previous perfusion imaging studies suggest that the NIHSS may also indirectly represent the extent of symptomatic ischemic tissue, encompassing both infarct core and penumbral regions [11]. Several prior studies have reported an association between lower initial NIHSS scores and the occurrence of early neurological deterioration (END) [10, 12]. Consistent with these findings, our analysis identified baseline NIHSS score as an independent factor associated with END. Several explanations may underlie this observation. First, patients presenting with lower NIHSS scores may harbor vulnerable hypoperfused tissue that is not fully captured by clinical severity at admission, predisposing them to infarct progression during the acute phase [1]. Second, patients with higher baseline NIHSS scores are less likely to fulfill the operational definition of END, as a ≥ 4-point increase in NIHSS is inherently more difficult to achieve in the setting of already severe neurological deficits [13].

Admission neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), anesthesia strategy, procedure duration, and the number of EVT attempts were evaluated as potentially modifiable periprocedural factors. Inflammatory processes are increasingly recognized as being associated with ischemic stroke occurrence and progression [14–16]. Acute cerebral ischemia is accompanied by systemic and central immune activation [17], and prior studies have linked neutrophil responses to ischemic injury and secondary neurological worsening [18–20] In this study, elevated admission NLR may associate with the occurrence of early neurological deterioration (END) in our cohort. This association should be interpreted as non-causal, as evidence supporting targeted interventions to modify NLR and improve outcomes after EVT remains limited. Accordingly, the clinical relevance of NLR in END warrants further investigation in prospective studies.

There remains no consensus regarding the optimal anesthesia strategy for endovascular treatment (EVT) in the acute phase of large vessel occlusion (LVO). Observational study have reported an association between general anesthesia (GA) and poorer neurological outcomes following EVT, although these findings are subject to allocation and confounding biases inherent to non-randomized designs [21]. In the present analysis, the use of GA was independently associated with the occurrence of early neurological deterioration (END). This association should be interpreted with caution and should not be regarded as evidence of causality. Several mechanisms may contribute to this observed association. First, induction of GA is frequently accompanied by hemodynamic instability, including hypotension and fluctuations in systolic, diastolic, and mean arterial pressures [22]. In patients with LVO, such blood pressure variability may compromise collateral perfusion and cerebral blood flow, thereby exacerbating ischemic injury [23]. Second, analyses from the HERMES collaboration have suggested an association between GA and delayed reperfusion, which may increase vulnerability to END [24] Third, certain anesthetic agents have been reported to exert neurophysiological effects, including respiratory depression, suppression of protective airway reflexes, and modulation of systemic immune responses [25]. In addition, endotracheal intubation and mechanical ventilation may increase the risk of pulmonary complications, potentially leading to hypoxia or hypercapnia, which can adversely affect neurological recovery.

The last modifiable factor is the number of EVT attempts, we identified over three attempts as an independent predictor of END. Previous study outlines the benefits of multiple thrombectomy attempts in increasing recanalization rate. However, these multiple attempts carry its disadvantage. As the number of thrombectomy attempts escalates, procedure duration and recanalization time increase, potentially exacerbating the extent and volume of infarction. Moreover, repeated thrombectomy heightens vascular wall injury, thereby escalating the likelihood of in-situ thrombosis, secondary ischemia, and recurrent vascular occlusion [26]. Significantly, this heightened risk may contribute to increased postoperative complications, including hemorrhagic transformation.

Further evidence demonstrates that successful recanalization after a single thrombectomy attempt correlates with improved clinical prognosis and reduced complication rates, particularly when compared to multiple attempts [27, 28]. Consequently, neurointerventionists are urged to thoughtfully select appropriate techniques, devices, and antiplatelet regimens to minimize the number of EVT attempts and prioritize rapid and and successful recanalization. In certain settings, acute LVO might necessitate rescue treatment following mechanical thrombectomy failure. Therefore, the number of EVT attempts serves as a superior indicator of surgical complexity compared to the time from onset to recanalization, exhibiting stronger correlation with procedure duration than the number of thrombectomy passes. Future research should further explore the relationship among these three variables - EVT attempts, time from onset to recanalization, and END - to provide more comprehensive insights into their interaction.

Our study presents several limitations. First, our identification of END adhered to a strict definition similar to previous studies. However, it’s recognized that END might manifest days following the initial event, potentially influenced by systemic diseases. This broader temporal window was not fully accounted for in our analysis. Second, while our investigation, like current studies, has unveiled various factors predictive of END post-EVT, deciphering precise etiological risk factors remains challenging due to the complex nature of END mechanisms. Additionally, the lack of established cut-off points for each risk factor limits our study to offer precise treatment guidance. Larger sample sizes in future studies are imperative for verification. Third, certain pivotal factors—such as other key workflow parameters such as door-to-groin or door-to-puncture, or equivalent time metrics, postoperative blood pressure, collateral circulation, and regionalized treatment preferences—were either not collected or could not be assessed in the present study. This absence potentially distorts the primary outcomes of our study. Fourth, in some cases where GA was administered, residual effects of anesthetic drugs or prolonged intubation post-EVT might have introduced bias in assessing the NIHSS. Lastly, our study cohort predominantly comprised individuals from the Chinese population. This demographic specificity might limit the generalizability of our findings to other ethnic groups or populations. Further larger and more diverse cohorts is warranted.

Conclusion

The occurrence of END associated with 90 days post-EVT unfavorable outcomes. Several independent predictors of END were identified, including baseline NIHSS, baseline NLR, administration of general anesthesia, retrieval attempts, and procedure duration. The insights gleaned from this study have the potential to inform the selection of appropriate therapies and enhance the clinical periprocedural management strategies for patients experiencing AIS due to LVO and exhibiting a heightened risk of END.

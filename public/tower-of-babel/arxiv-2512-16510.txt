Rationally-extended radial harmonic oscillator in a position-dependent mass background
C. Quesne

Abstract
We show that the radial harmonic oscillator problem in the position-dependent mass background of the type m ⁡ ( α , r ) = ( 1 + α ​ r 2 ) − 2 m(\alpha;r)=(1+\alpha r^{2})^{-2} , α > 0 \alpha>0 , can be solved by using a point canonical transformation mapping the corresponding Schrödinger equation onto that of the Pöschl-Teller I potential with constant mass. The radial harmonic oscillator problem with position-dependent mass is shown to exhibit a deformed shape invariance property in a deformed supersymmetric framework. The inverse point canonical transformation then provides some exactly-solvable rational extensions of the radial harmonic oscillator with position-dependent mass associated with X m X_{m} -Jacobi exceptional orthogonal polynomials of type I, II, or III. The extended potentials of type I and II are proved to display deformed shape invariance. The spectrum and wavefunctions of the radial harmonic oscillator potential and its extensions are shown to go over to well-known results when the deforming parameter α \alpha goes to zero.



Keywords: Schrödinger equation, radial harmonic oscillator, position-dependent mass, exceptional orthogonal polynomials

It is well known that one of the problems of PDM Schrödinger equations comes from the momentum and mass operator noncommutativity and the resultant ordering ambiguity in the kinetic energy term. \displaystyle\biggl[-\frac{1}{2}\biggl(m^{\xi}(\alpha;r)\frac{d}{dr}m^{\eta}(\alpha;r)\frac{d}{dr}m^{\zeta}(\alpha;r)+m^{\zeta}(\alpha;r)\frac{d}{dr}m^{\eta}(\alpha;r)\frac{d}{dr}m^{\xi}(\alpha;r)\biggr)+V_{\rm eff}(r)\biggr]\psi(r)

\displaystyle=E\psi(r),\qquad 0<r<\infty,

m_{0}m(\alpha;r)

Deformed shape invariance of the radial harmonic oscillator potential in the

It is well known that the PT I potential
U(u;A,B)
, defined in (
2.16
), is a shape invariant potential in the context of SUSYQM
[
34
]
. As a result, the transformed potential
V(r;L,\omega)
of Eq. (
2.5
) is deformed shape invariant in the context of deformed SUSYQM
[
35
]
.

This means that on taking the ground-state wavefunction
\psi^{(\alpha)}_{0}(r;L,\omega)={\cal N}^{(\alpha)}_{0,L,\omega}r^{L+1}[f(\alpha;r)]^{-\frac{1}{2}\left(L+\frac{5}{2}+\frac{\Delta}{2\alpha}\right)}
of the deformed Schrödinger equation (
2.6
) as the seed function, the corresponding superpotential

W_{\alpha}(r;L,\omega)=-f\frac{d}{dr}\log\psi^{(\alpha)}_{0}(r;L,\omega)-\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime},

(2.31)

where a dash denotes a derivative with respect to
r
, can be written as

W_{\alpha}(r;L,\omega)=-(L+1)\frac{f}{r}+\alpha\left(L+\frac{3}{2}+\frac{\Delta}{2\alpha}\right)r.

(2.32)

It allows us to consider a pair of first-order differential operators

A^{\pm}_{\alpha}(L,\omega)=\mp\sqrt{f}\frac{d}{dr}\sqrt{f}+W_{\alpha}(r;L,\omega),

(2.33)

in terms of which the starting deformed Hamiltonian
H_{0}=\pi_{r}^{2}+V_{0}(r)
,
V_{0}(r)=V(r;L,\omega)
, can be written as

H_{0}=A^{+}_{\alpha}(L,\omega)A^{-}_{\alpha}(L,\omega)+E^{(\alpha)}_{0}(L,\omega).

(2.34)

The partner of
H_{0}
is

\displaystyle H_{1}

\displaystyle=A^{-}_{\alpha}(L,\omega)A^{+}_{\alpha}(L,\omega)+E^{(\alpha)}_{0}(L,\omega)=\pi_{r}^{2}+V_{1}(r),

\displaystyle V_{1}(r)

\displaystyle=V_{0}(r)+2f\frac{d}{dr}W_{\alpha}(r;L,\omega),

(2.35)

where it turns out that

\displaystyle V_{1}(r)

\displaystyle=V_{0}(r;L+1,\omega^{\prime})+\alpha\left(2L+3+\frac{\Delta}{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(L+1)(L+2)}{r^{2}}+\frac{1}{4}\omega^{\prime 2}r^{2}+\alpha\left(2L+3+\lx@nobreakspace\frac{\Delta}{\alpha}\right),

(2.36)

with
\omega^{\prime}=\sqrt{\omega^{2}+4\alpha^{2}+4\alpha\Delta}
and
\Delta^{\prime}=\sqrt{\omega^{\prime 2}+\alpha^{2}}=\Delta+2\alpha
. Hence, the potential

is similar in shape and differs only in the parameters

Such a property enables us to construct a hierarchy of Hamiltonians

where

&\varepsilon_{0}=E^{(\alpha)}_{0}(L,\omega),\\
&\varepsilon_{i}=\alpha(4L+8i+2)+2\Delta,\qquad i=1,2,\ldots,\end{split}

(2.38)

with

and

,

(2.41)

(2.42)

In other words, the first-order operators

fulfil a deformed shape invariance condition

\displaystyle A^{-}_{\alpha}(L+i,\omega^{(i)})A^{+}_{\alpha}(L+i,\omega^{(i)})=A^{+}_{\alpha}(L+i+1,\omega^{(i+1)})A^{-}_{\alpha}(L+i+1,\omega^{(i+1)})+\varepsilon_{i+1},

3.1
Rational extensions of the PT I potential

The rational extensions of the PT I potential belong to three different types I, II, and III, according to the kind of seed function that is used to construct the partner. Such seed functions and their corresponding energies can be written as
[
22
,
36
]

\begin{split}&\varphi^{\rm I}_{m}(u;A,B)=\phi_{m}(u;A,1-B),\qquad e^{\rm I}_{m}(A,B)=(A-B+1+2m)^{2},\\
&\quad B>m+\tfrac{1}{2},\\
&\varphi^{\rm II}_{m}(u;A,B)=\phi_{m}(u;1-A,B),\qquad e^{\rm II}_{m}(A,B)=(B-A+1+2m)^{2},\\
&\quad A>m+\tfrac{1}{2},\\
&\varphi^{\rm III}_{m}(u;A,B)=\phi_{m}(u;1-A,1-B),\qquad e^{\rm III}_{m}(A,B)=(-A-B+2+2m)^{2},\\
&\quad A,B>m+\tfrac{1}{2},\qquad m\text{\ even},\end{split}

(3.1)

where

\phi_{m}(u;A,B)=(\sin u)^{A}(\cos u)^{B}P_{m}^{(A-\frac{1}{2},B-\frac{1}{2})}(\cos 2u).

(3.2)

To obtain for the partner some rationally-extended PT I potential with given
A
and
B
parameters, we have to start from a conventional potential with different parameters
A^{\prime}
and
B^{\prime}
, which depend on the type considered. The results read

\begin{split}U_{0}(u)&=U(u;A^{\prime},B^{\prime}),\\
U_{1}(u)&=U(u;A^{\prime},B^{\prime})-2\frac{d^{2}}{du^{2}}\log\varphi_{m}(u;A^{\prime},B^{\prime})\\
&=U^{(m)}_{\rm ext}(u;A,B)=U(u;A,B)+U^{(m)}_{\rm rat}(u;A,B),\end{split}

(3.3)

where

U^{(m)}_{\rm rat}(u;A,B)=8\left\{z\frac{\dot{g}_{m}^{(A,B)}}{g_{m}^{(A,B)}}-(1-z^{2})\left[\frac{\ddot{g}_{m}^{(A,B)}}{g_{m}^{(A,B)}}-\left(\frac{\dot{g}_{m}^{(A,B)}}{g_{m}^{(A,B)}}\right)^{2}\right]\right\},\qquad z=\cos 2u.

(3.4)

Here a dot denotes a derivative with respect to
z
, and we have for the three different types

\begin{split}\text{(I)\ }&A^{\prime}=A-1,\quad B^{\prime}=B+1,\quad g_{m}^{(A,B)}(z)=P_{m}^{\left(A-\frac{3}{2},-B-\frac{1}{2}\right)}(z),\quad B>m-\tfrac{1}{2};\\
\text{(II)\ }&A^{\prime}=A+1,\quad B^{\prime}=B-1,\quad g_{m}^{(A,B)}(z)=P_{m}^{\left(-A-\frac{1}{2},B-\frac{3}{2}\right)}(z),\quad A>m-\tfrac{1}{2};\\
\text{(III)\ }&A^{\prime}=A+1,\quad B^{\prime}=B+1,\quad g_{m}^{(A,B)}(z)=P_{m}^{\left(-A-\frac{1}{2},-B-\frac{1}{2}\right)}(z),\quad A,B>m-\tfrac{1}{2},\\
&\quad m\text{\ even}.\end{split}

(3.5)

For types I and II, the two partners
U_{0}(u)
and
U_{1}(u)
are strictly isospectral, so that the spectrum of the latter is given by

\epsilon_{n}^{(\rm ext)}(A,B)=(A+B+2n)^{2},\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots,\qquad\text{for type I or II},

(3.6)

whereas, for type III, the inverse of the seed function being normalizable, we get an extra bound state below the spectrum of
U_{0}(u)
, hence

\epsilon_{n}^{(\rm ext)}(A,B)=(A+B+2n+2)^{2},\qquad n=-m-1,0,1,2,\ldots,\qquad\text{for type III}.

(3.7)

The wavefunctions of
U_{1}(u)
corresponding to
n=0
, 1 2, … can be obtained from

\phi^{(\rm ext}_{n}(u;A,B)\propto\frac{{\cal W}(\varphi^{i}_{m}(u;A^{\prime},B^{\prime}),\phi_{n}(u;A^{\prime},B^{\prime})\mid u)}{\varphi^{i}_{m}(u;A^{\prime},B^{\prime})},\qquad i=\text{{\rm I}, {\rm II}, or {\rm III}},

(3.8)

where
{\cal W}(\varphi^{i}_{m},\phi_{n}\mid u)
denotes the Wronskian of the functions

3.3
Deformed supersymmetry properties

By the inverse of the PCT defined in Sec. 2.1, the conventional SUSY relation between the partners
U_{0}(u)
and
U_{1}(u)
of Eq. (
3.3
) is converted into a deformed SUSY relation between the partners
V_{0}(r)
and
V_{1}(r)
of Eq. (
3.15
). The superpotential characterizing the latter is given by

W^{(m)}_{\alpha}(r;L^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})=-f\frac{d}{dr}\log\chi_{m}(r;L^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})-\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime},

(3.30)

which yields

\begin{split}&W^{(m)}_{\alpha}(r;L-1,\sqrt{\omega^{2}+\alpha^{2}+4\alpha\Delta})=-\frac{L}{r}-\alpha r\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\Delta}{2\alpha}\right)-f\frac{p^{\prime}_{m}}{p_{m}}\quad\text{for type I},\\
&W^{(m)}_{\alpha}(r;L+1,\sqrt{\omega^{2}+\alpha^{2}-4\alpha\Delta})=\frac{L+1}{r}-\alpha r\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\Delta}{2\alpha}\right)-f\frac{p^{\prime}_{m}}{p_{m}}\quad\text{for type II},\\
&W^{(m)}_{\alpha}(r;L+1,\sqrt{\omega^{2}+\alpha^{2}+4\alpha\Delta})=\frac{L+1}{r}-\alpha r\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\Delta}{2\alpha}\right)-f\frac{p^{\prime}_{m}}{p_{m}}\quad\text{for type III},\end{split}

(3.31)

the corresponding
p_{m}=p_{m}^{(L,\Delta)}(t)
being defined in (
3.17
) and a dash denoting as before a derivative with respect to
r
.

As it can be checked by using the differential equation satisfied by Jacobi polynomials
[
20
]
, in the three cases we can write

\begin{split}&\left[W^{(m)}_{\alpha}(r;L^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})\right]^{2}-fW^{(m)\prime}_{\alpha}(r;L^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})+{\cal E}_{m}(L^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})=V(r;L^{\prime},\omega^{\prime}),\\
&\left[W^{(m)}_{\alpha}(r;L^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})\right]^{2}+fW^{(m)\prime}_{\alpha}(r;L^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})+{\cal E}_{m}(L^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})=V^{(m)}_{\rm ext}(r;L,\omega)+\gamma.\end{split}

(3.32)

As shown in Sec. 2.2, the starting potential
V(r;L^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})
in this deformed SUSY construction satisfies a deformed shape invariance property with a partner given by
V(r;L^{\prime}+1,\omega^{\prime\prime})+\alpha(2L^{\prime}+3+\frac{\Delta^{\prime}}{\alpha})
, where
\Delta^{\prime}=\sqrt{\omega^{\prime 2}+\alpha^{2}}
and
\omega^{\prime\prime}=\sqrt{\omega^{\prime 2}+4\alpha^{2}+4\alpha\Delta^{\prime}}
. One may wonder whether the final potential
V^{(m)}_{\rm ext}(r;L,\omega)+\gamma
in such a construction has a similar property. As we plan to show, the answer is affirmative in the two isospectral cases I and II.

Let us indeed now consider a superpotential

W^{(m)}_{\rm ext}(r;L,\omega)=-f\frac{d}{dr}\log\psi^{(\rm ext)}_{0}(r;L,\omega)-\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime}

(3.33)

with

\psi^{(\rm ext)}_{0}(r;L,\omega)\propto\frac{\psi^{(\alpha)}_{0}(r;L,\omega)}{p_{m}^{(L,\Delta)}(t)}Q^{(m)}_{0}(t;L,\omega),

(3.34)

and

Q^{(m)}_{0}(t;L,\omega)=\left(\frac{\Delta}{2\alpha}+1\right)p_{m}^{(L,\Delta)}(t)-\frac{1}{2}\left(m+L-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\Delta}{2\alpha}\right)(1+t)p_{m-1}^{(L+1,\Delta-2\alpha)}(t)

(3.35)

or

Q^{(m)}_{0}(t;L,\omega)=-\left(L+\frac{3}{2}\right)p_{m}^{(L,\Delta)}(t)-\frac{1}{2}\left(m-L-\frac{3}{2}+\frac{\Delta}{2\alpha}\right)(1-t)p_{m-1}^{(L-1,\Delta+2\alpha)}(t)

(3.36)

for type I or II, respectively. On using the definition of
p_{m}^{(L,\Delta)}(t)
, given in Eq. (
3.17
), and the expansion of these polynomials into powers
[
20
]
, it is straightforward to rewrite
Q^{(m)}_{0}(t;L,\omega)
as

Q^{(m)}_{0}(t;L,\omega)=\left(1+\frac{\Delta}{2\alpha}-m\right)p_{m}^{(L+1,\Delta+2\alpha)}(t)\qquad\text{for type I}

(3.37)

and

Q^{(m)}_{0}(t;L,\omega)=\left(m-L-\frac{3}{2}\right)p_{m}^{(L+1,\Delta+2\alpha)}(t)\qquad\text{for type II}.

(3.38)

Combining Eqs. (
3.33
) and (
3.34
) with (
3.37
) or (
3.38
) yields

W^{(m)}_{\rm ext}(r;L,\omega)=-\frac{L+1}{r}+\alpha\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\Delta}{2\alpha}\right)r-f\left(\frac{p_{m}^{(L+1,\Delta+2\alpha)\prime}}{p_{m}^{(L+1,\Delta+2\alpha)}}-\frac{p_{m}^{(L,\Delta)\prime}}{p_{m}^{(L,\Delta)}}\right)

(3.39)

for both types. It is then straightforward to show that the partner of
V^{(m)}_{\rm ext}(r;L,\omega)
is given by

V^{(m)}_{\rm ext}(r;L,\omega)+2fW^{(m)\prime}_{\rm ext}(r;L,\omega)=V^{(m)}_{\rm ext}(r;L+1,\omega^{\prime})+\alpha\left(2L+3+\frac{\Delta}{\alpha}\right),

(3.40)

which proves the deformed shape invariance property of type I and II extended potentials.

4
Conclusion

In the present paper, we have shown that the problem of the radial harmonic oscillator in a
m(\alpha;r)=(1+\alpha r^{2})^{-2}
background can be easily solved by mapping the corresponding Schrödinger equation onto that for the PT I potential with constant mass by means of a PCT. In addition, we have proved that the well-known shape invariance of the PT I potential in SUSYQM gives rise to a deformed shape invariance property for the considered PDM problem in deformed SUSYQM.

We have then taken advantage of the knowledge of rational extensions of the PT I potential connected with
X_{m}
-Jacobi EOP’s of type I, II, and III to transform them into rational extensions of the radial harmonic oscillator in the
m(\alpha;r)
background. These results have been analyzed in the deformed SUSYQM framework and the extended radial harmonic oscillator potentials of type I and II have been shown to be endowed with a deformed shape invariance property.

Finally, the spectrum and wavefunctions of the radial harmonic oscillator and its extensions in the
m(\alpha;r)
background have been proved to go over for
\alpha\to 0
to those corresponding to a constant mass, in which case Jacobi polynomials and
X_{m}
-Jacobi EOP’s become Laguerre polynomials and
X_{m}
-Laguerre EOP’s, respectively.

Considering multi-indexed rational extensions and corresponding orthogonal polynomials would be a very interesting topic for future investigation. Another open question for future work would be the possibility of transferring to the radial harmonic oscillator problem with PDM the more general one- and
m
-step extensions of the PT I potential based on the use of para-Jacobi polynomials
[
36
,
40
]
.

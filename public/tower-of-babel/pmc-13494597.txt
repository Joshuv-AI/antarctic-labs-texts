Comments on “Live birth outcomes after euploid transfer: autologous vs. donor oocyte embryos in patients aged > 35 years
Authors: Preeti Lamba
Published: 2026

To the Editor:

I read with considerable interest the recent study by Lindner et al. (1), which evaluated live birth outcomes after euploid frozen embryo transfer (FET) in women >35 years old, comparing autologous and donor oocyte cycles. By restricting their analysis to embryos confirmed as chromosomally normal, the investigators attempt to clarify whether reproductive aging exerts effects beyond the well-recognized increase in aneuploidy rates (2). This distinction is clinically important in the current era of widespread preimplantation genetic testing for aneuploidy (PGT-A).

The strength of the study lies in its large multicenter cohort and its focus on live birth as the primary endpoint. The finding that live birth rates decline with advancing maternal age in autologous euploid cycles suggests that euploidy alone does not fully restore reproductive competence. Age-related alterations in oocyte biology including mitochondrial dysfunction, cytoplasmic degradation, spindle instability, and epigenetic modification, may compromise embryonic developmental potential despite normal chromosomal content (3). The comparatively stable outcomes observed in donor oocyte cycles further underscore the importance of intrinsic oocyte quality in determining implantation success.

Nonetheless, interpretation should be approached cautiously. The retrospective design inherently limits the ability to account for confounding variables, such as embryo morphology grading, stimulation protocols, culture systems, laboratory conditions, and procedural refinements over time. Advances in vitrification techniques and biopsy strategies during the study period may have introduced heterogeneity in outcomes. Additionally, while donor-derived embryos appeared unaffected by recipient age, the study did not directly evaluate endometrial receptivity or systemic age-related factors that might influence implantation or placentation.

From a clinical perspective, the findings highlight an important counseling point: confirmation of euploid status reduces but does not eliminate the prognostic influence of maternal age when autologous oocytes are used. Therefore, patients should be informed that genetic normality does not equate to uniform implantation probability across age groups. Future prospective investigations integrating morphokinetic analysis, metabolic profiling, and endometrial receptivity assessment may provide a more comprehensive understanding of the mechanisms underlying these observations.

In conclusion, this study contributes meaningfully to the evolving understanding of reproductive aging and reinforces the multifactorial determinants of embryo competence beyond chromosomal status.

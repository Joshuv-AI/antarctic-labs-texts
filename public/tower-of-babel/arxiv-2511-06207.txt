Mean Li-Yorke chaos for a sequence of operators on Banach spaces
Jian Li, Xinsheng Wang, Jianjie Zhao

Abstract
. In this paper, we obtain the dichotomy for mean equicontinuity and mean sensitivity for a sequence of bounded linear operators from a Banach space to a normed linear space. The mean Li-Yorke chaos for sequences and submultiplicative sequences of bounded linear operators are also studied. Furthermore, several criteria for mean Li-Yorke chaos are established.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 47A16; Secondary: 37B05

1.
Introduction

It appears that universality is a generic phenomenon in analysis.
In the survey
[
G1999
]
Grosse-Erdmann discussed the theory of universal families including both of general theory and special universal families systematically.
A universal family consists of continuous mappings
T_{\lambda}
(
\lambda\in\Lambda
) between two topological spaces
X
and
Y
, which has a universal element, i.e.,
x\in X
such that
\{T_{\lambda}x\colon\lambda\in\Lambda\}
is dense in
Y
.
In particular, the concept of hypercyclicity was discussed in
[
G1999
]
, which is the study of the universality of a sequence of operators on a topological vector space.
One special but important case is that when the sequence is generated by iterations of a single continuous operator, and such single continuous operator is called a hypercyclic operator.

A natural question is how to determine whether a sequence of operators or a single operator is hypercyclic. However it is not an easy task to make such a determination only using the definition of hypercyclicity.
Hence it is necessary to establish some hypercyclicity criteria which give sufficient conditions under which the sequence of operators is hypercyclic and are easier to apply to specific examples.
It is shown in
[
BP1999
]
that a continuous linear operator
T
on a Fréchet space satisfies the hypercyclicity criterion if and only if it is hereditarily hypercyclic, and if and only if
T\oplus T
is hypercyclic.
For the case of sequence of operators, a more general result is obtained in
[
BG2003
]
, which strengthens and extends the result in
[
BP1999
]
and can be applied to an almost-commuting (refer to Section
4
for the definition) sequence of operators.

When the family
\{T_{\lambda}\colon\lambda\in\Lambda\}
forms a group under composition on a topological space, the universality is well known in topological dynamics under the name of topological transitivity, which is an important concept in the study of dynamics, and is related to another central concept — chaos.
Note that chaos gives a qualitative characterization of the complexity of systems and has been fully investigated. It is widely believed that Li-Yorke chaos is the first version of chaos, which was introduced by Li and Yorke in
[
LY1975
]
.
However, the origin of this concept can be traced back even earlier, Poincaré has already observed chaotic phenomena.
With the need of research of dynamics, all kinds of chaos were established, such as Devaney chaos, mean Li-Yorke chaos, distributional chaos et al., refer to
[
LY2016
]
and references therein for more information on this topic.
An interesting thing should be noted here that the operator which is Devaney chaotic must satisfy the hypercyclicity criterion given in
[
BP1999
]
.

Now let us return to discuss the chaos theory concerning linear operators. The dynamics of linear systems is usually considered uncomplicated.
However, even for such linear systems, chaotic behavior can also been presented.
In 1990, Protopopescu reported in
[
P1990
]
that a linear operator on an infinite dimensional linear space could be Devaney chaotic in one of his technical reports.
Moreover, various linear operators with all kinds of chaos including Li-Yorke chaos have been obtained, which promoted the investigation of the dynamics of general linear operators.
In
[
BBMP2011
]
, Li-Yorke and (uniform) distributional chaos for bounded operators on Banach spaces are studied.
In
[
BBMP2013
]
, a technical characterization of distributional chaos for bounded operators on an infinite dimensional separable Banach space is given.
The mean Li-Yorke chaos of operators on Banach spaces is investigated in
[
BBP2020
]
. Some examples are are provided in
[
BBP2020
]
that there exists distributionally chaotic operators that are not mean Li-Yorke chaotic, and it still open whether the converse implication holds (see Question 16 in
[
BBP2020
]
).
See
[
HSX2021
]
for a generalization of mean Li-Yorke chaos for operators on Banach spaces.
Using the techniques in topological dynamics, a uniform treatment of Li-Yorke chaos, mean Li-Yorke chaos and distributional chaos for continuous endomorphisms of completely metrizable groups is given in
[
JL2022
]
, which is applied to the case of linear operators on Fréchet spaces, improving some results in the above references.
Monographs
[
BM2009
]
and
[
GP2011
]
can be regarded as the introduction concerning this topic, where basic concepts and classical results related to chaos theory of linear operators can be found.

It is meaningful to consider the chaos theory of linear operators in a wide scope, such as the sequence of linear operators, which is not generated by a single continuous map.
Recently, Conejero et al. studied distributional chaos for a family of operators on Fréchet spaces in
[
CKMM2016
]
. In
[
YHC2023
]
, the dichotomy of mean equicontinuity and mean sensitivity for non-autonomous linear systems is obtained.

In this paper, we will study mean Li-Yorke chaos for a sequence of bounded linear operators on Banach spaces, and generalize the results on a single operator to this setting.
We first obtain a dichotomy of mean equicontinuity and mean sensitivity for
a sequence of operators.
Then we get some equivalent conditions for the existence of (dense) mean Li-Yorke scrambled sets.
Furthermore, under some conditions, we obtain more satisfied equivalent conditions for the existence of (dense) mean Li-Yorke scrambled sets.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we study the dichotomy for mean equicontinuity and mean sensitivity for sequences of bounded linear operators from a Banach space to a normed linear space.
In Section
3
, the mean Li-Yorke chaos for sequences of bounded linear operators are studied.
In Section
4
, the mean Li-Yorke chaos for submultiplicative sequences of bounded linear operators are studied.
Furthermore, some equivalent criteria for mean Li-Yorke chaos are established.

2.
Dichotomy for mean equicontinuity and mean sensitivity for a sequence of operators

The concepts of mean equicontinuity and mean sensitivity were first introduced in
[
LTY2015
]
for dynamical systems on compact metric spaces.
In this section, we generalize the concepts of mean equicontinuity and mean sensitivity for a sequence of operators. We will see in the next section that those concepts are closely related to mean Li-Yorke chaos.

Definition 2.1
.

A sequence
(T_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
of linear operators from a normed linear space
X
to a normed linear space
Y
is said to be

mean equicontinuous
if for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that for every
x,y\in X
with
\|x-y\|<\delta
,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}x-T_{i}y\|<\varepsilon,

and
mean sensitive
if there is a constant
\delta>0
such that for every
x\in X
and
\varepsilon>0
there exists some
y\in X
with
\|x-y\|<\varepsilon
and

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}x-T_{i}y\|>\delta.

We first study the equivalent characterizations of mean equicontinuity and mean sensitivity for general bounded linear operators, and obtain the following main result in this section.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
(T_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of bounded linear operators from a Banach space
X
to a normed linear space
Y
.
Then either
(T_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
is mean equicontinuous or mean sensitive.

We need the following definition of absolute Cesàro boundedness, which is introduced in
[
LH2015
]
for iterations of a linear operator.

Definition 2.3
.

A sequence
(T_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
of linear operators from a normed linear space
X
to a normed linear space
Y
is said to be
absolutely Cesàro bounded
if there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
x\in X
,

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}x\|\leq C\|x\|.

Motivated by
[
BBP2020
]
*Theorem 4 and
[
JL2022
]
*Theorem 4.31, we have the following result, which can be regarded as a mean
version of the uniform boundedness theorem for a sequence of linear operators.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
(T_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of bounded linear operators from a Banach space
X
to a normed linear space
Y
.
Then the following assertions are equivalent:

(1)

the sequence
(T_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
is mean equicontinuous;

(2)

for all
x\in X
,

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}x\|<\infty;

(3)

the sequence
(T_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
is absolutely Cesàro bounded;

(4)

for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that for every
x,y\in X
with
\|x-y\|<\delta
,

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}x-T_{i}y\|<\varepsilon.

Proof.

(\ref{thm1: ME})\Rightarrow(\ref{thm2: x bounded})
. There exists
\delta>0
such that for every
x\in X
with
\|x\|<\delta
,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}x\|<1.

Then for any
x\in X
,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}x\|=\frac{2\|x\|}{\delta}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}(\tfrac{\delta}{2\|x\|}x)\|<\frac{2\|x\|}{\delta}<\infty.

(\ref{thm2: x bounded})\Rightarrow(\ref{thm3: aCb})
.
For every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let

X_{k}=\biggl\{x\in X\colon\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}x\|\leq k\biggr\}.

Then
X_{k}
is a closed subset of
X
.
As
X=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}X_{k}
, by the Baire category theorem
there exists
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that the interior of
X_{k_{0}}
is non empty.
Pick a point
x_{0}\in X
and
\delta_{0}>0
such that
B(x_{0},\delta_{0})\subset X_{k_{0}}
, where

B(x_{0},\delta_{0})=\{x\in X\colon\|x-x_{0}\|<\delta_{0}\}
.
For any
x\in X
, one has

x_{0}+\tfrac{\delta_{0}}{3\|x\|}x,x_{0}+\tfrac{2\delta_{0}}{3\|x\|}x\in X_{k_{0}}.

Let
C=\frac{6k_{0}}{\delta_{0}}
.
Then for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}x\|

\displaystyle=\frac{3\|x\|}{\delta_{0}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}(x_{0}+\tfrac{\delta_{0}}{3\|x\|}x)-T_{i}(x_{0}+\tfrac{2\delta_{0}}{3\|x\|}x)\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3\|x\|}{\delta_{0}}\biggl(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}(x_{0}+\tfrac{\delta_{0}}{3\|x\|}x)\|+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}(x_{0}+\tfrac{2\delta_{0}}{3\|x\|}x)\|\biggr)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3\|x\|}{\delta_{0}}(k_{0}+k_{0})\leq C\|x\|.

This shows that the sequence
(T_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
is absolutely Cesàro bounded.

(\ref{thm3: aCb})\Rightarrow(\ref{thm4: ME-sup})
. For every
\varepsilon>0
, pick
\delta=\frac{\varepsilon}{C}
, where
C
is the constant in the definition of absolute Cesàro boundedness for
(T_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
. Then for every
x,y\in X
with
\|x-y\|<\delta
,

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}x-T_{i}y\|=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}(x-y)\|

\displaystyle\leq C\|x-y\|<C\delta=\varepsilon.

(\ref{thm4: ME-sup})\Rightarrow(\ref{thm1: ME})
.
It is clear.
∎

Example 2.5
.

Let
X
be a Banach space and
I\colon X\to X
be the identity operator.
For each
i\in\mathbb{N}
, let
T_{i}=nI
if
i=2^{n}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
T_{i}=I
otherwise.
It is easy to see that the sequence
(T_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
is absolutely Cesàro bounded but not norm-bounded.

Remark 2.6
.

Examples of mixing operators that are absolutely Cesàro bounded can be found, for instsance, in
[
BBMP2013
]
*Example 5 and in
[
LH2015
]
*Theorem 3.5. Actually, Example 5 in
[
BBMP2013
]
is a backward shift on
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N})
such that there is no point in
X
has distributionally unbounded orbit (by the proof, this operator is in fact absolutely Cesàro bounded). By Theorem
2.4
those operators are also mean equicontinuous.

From the opposite side, corresponding to Theorem
2.4
, we have the following equivalent characterization of mean sensitivity. Some of the following equivalent characterizations are the same as
[
BBP2020
]
*Theorem 4, but the proof method is different. Here we mainly use the characterization of mean equicontinuity in Theorem
2.4
.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
(T_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of bounded linear operators from a Banach space
X
to a normed linear space
Y
.
Then the following assertions are equivalent:

(1)

the sequence
(T_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
is mean sensitive;

(2)

there exists
x\in X
such that

\limsup_{n\to{\infty}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}x\|=\infty;

(3)

the collection

\biggl\{x\in X\colon\limsup_{n\to{\infty}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}x\|=\infty\biggr\}

is residual in
X
;

(4)

the collection

\biggl\{(x,y)\in X\times X\colon\limsup_{n\to{\infty}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}(x-y)\|=\infty\biggr\}

is residual in
X\times X
;

(5)

there exists a bounded sequence
(y_{k})_{k}
in
X
and a sequence
(N_{k})_{k}
in
\mathbb{N}
such that

\sup_{k}\frac{1}{N_{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{k}}\|T_{i}y_{k}\|=\infty;

(6)

there exists a sequence
(y_{k})_{k}
in
X
and a sequence
(N_{k})_{k}
in
\mathbb{N}
such that
\lim_{k\to\infty}y_{k}=0
and

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{N_{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{k}}\|T_{i}y_{k}\|>0.

Proof.

(\ref{thm1: mean sensitive})\Rightarrow(\ref{thm2: x unbounded})
.
Otherwise, if for any
x\in X
,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}x\|<\infty,

by Theorem
2.4
,
(T_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
is mean equicontinuous.
Let
\delta
be the constant in the definition of mean sensitivity.
On the one hand, mean equicontinuity implies that there exists
\delta^{\prime}>0

such that for every
x,y\in X
with
\|x-y\|<\delta^{\prime}
, one has

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}x-T_{i}y\|<\delta.

On the other hand, mean sensitivity implies that for every
x\in X
and above
\delta^{\prime}
,
there exists some
y\in X
with
\|x-y\|<\delta^{\prime}
such that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}x-T_{i}y\|>\delta.

Which is a contradiction.

(\ref{thm2: x unbounded})\Rightarrow(\ref{thm3: residual points in X})
.
Assume that
x_{0}\in X
such that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}x_{0}\|=\infty.

Let

Y=\biggl\{x\in X\colon\limsup_{n\to{\infty}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}x\|=\infty\biggr\}.

It is clear that

Y=\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\biggl\{x\in X\colon\exists n\geq k\text{ s.t. }\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}x\|>k\biggr\}.

Then
Y
is a
G_{\delta}
subset of
X
.
It suffices to show that for any
x\in X
and
\varepsilon>0
, there exists some
y\in Y
such that

\|x-y\|<\varepsilon
.

If
x\in Y
, there’s nothing to prove.
If
x\notin Y
, put
y=x+\frac{\varepsilon}{2\|x_{0}\|}x_{0}
,
then
\|x-y\|<\varepsilon

and

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}y\|

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}(x+\tfrac{x_{0}}{2\|x_{0}\|}\varepsilon)\|

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}(\tfrac{x_{0}}{2\|x_{0}\|}\varepsilon)\|-\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}x\|

\displaystyle=\frac{\varepsilon}{2\|x_{0}\|}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}x_{0}\|-\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}x\|

\displaystyle=\infty.

(\ref{thm3: residual points in X})\Rightarrow(\ref{thm4: residual points in X*X})
. It follows from the linearity of
(T_{i})_{i}
.

(\ref{thm4: residual points in X*X})\Rightarrow(\ref{thm5: bounded sequence})
. It is obvious.

(\ref{thm5: bounded sequence})\Rightarrow(\ref{thm6: sequence to 0})
.
Assume that
(z_{k})_{k}
is the bounded sequence in
X
and

(N_{k})_{k}
is the sequence in
\mathbb{N}
such that

\sup_{k}\frac{1}{N_{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{k}}\|T_{i}z_{k}\|=\infty.

Let
y_{k}=\frac{N_{k}}{\sum_{i=1}^{N_{k}}\|T_{i}z_{k}\|}z_{k}
,
then
\lim_{k\to\infty}y_{k}=0
and

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{N_{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{k}}\|T_{i}y_{k}\|=1>0.

(\ref{thm6: sequence to 0})\Rightarrow(\ref{thm1: mean sensitive})
.
Suppose the sequences
(y_{k})_{k}
and
(N_{k})_{k}
are given by condition
(\ref{thm6: sequence to 0})
, and let

\delta=\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{N_{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{k}}\|T_{i}y_{k}\|.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
(N_{k})_{k}
is increasing.
For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let

X_{n}=\biggl\{x\in X:\exists k>n\ \text{s.t.}\ \frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\|T_{i}x\|>\frac{\delta}{2}-\frac{1}{n}\biggr\}.

Then each
X_{n}
is an open subset of
X
.
We will show that it is also dense in
X
.
Let
U
be a nonempty open subset of
X
and pick
x\in U
.
If
x\notin X_{n}
, then for any
k>n
, one has

\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\|T_{i}x\|\leq\frac{\delta}{2}-\frac{1}{n}.

Since

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{N_{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{k}}\|T_{i}y_{k}\|=\delta,

there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
k>N
,
we have

\frac{1}{N_{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{k}}\|T_{i}y_{k}\|>\delta-\frac{1}{2n}.

As
\lim_{k\to\infty}(y_{k}+x)=x\in U
,
there exists
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
with
k_{0}>N
such that
y_{k_{0}}+x\in U

and
N_{k_{0}}>n
.
Then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{k_{0}}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{k_{0}}}\|T_{i}(y_{k_{0}}+x)\|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{N_{k_{0}}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{k_{0}}}\|T_{i}y_{k_{0}}\|-\frac{1}{N_{k_{0}}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{k_{0}}}\|T_{i}x\|

\displaystyle\geq\delta-\frac{1}{2n}-\Bigl(\frac{\delta}{2}-\frac{1}{n}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta}{2}+\frac{1}{2n}>\frac{\delta}{2}-\frac{1}{n}.

Which implies that
y_{k_{0}}+x\in U\cap X_{n}
.
Therefore, the set

X_{0}=\biggl\{x\in X:\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}x\|\geq\frac{\delta}{2}\biggr\}=\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}X_{n}

is residual in
X
.
Fix
x\in X
and
\varepsilon>0
,
since
X_{0}
is residual, there exists
z\in X
with
\|z\|<\varepsilon
and

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}z\|\geq\frac{\delta}{2}.

Let
y=z+x
, then
\|x-y\|<\varepsilon
and

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}x-T_{i}y\|=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}z\|\geq\frac{\delta}{2}.

This shows that the sequence
(T_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
is mean sensitive.
∎

3.
Mean Li-Yorke chaos for a sequence of operators

In this section, we study the mean Li-Yorke chaos for sequences of bounded linear operators from a Banach space to a normed linear space.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(T_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of bounded linear operators from a normed linear space
X
to a normed linear space
Y
.
A pair
(x,y)\in X\times X
is called
mean asymptotic
if

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}x-T_{i}y\|=0,

and
mean proximal
if

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}x-T_{i}y\|=0.

Let
\text{MAsym}(T_{i})
and
\text{MProx}(T_{i})
denote the collection of all mean asympotic pairs and mean proximal pairs, and are called
mean asympotic relation
and
mean proximal relation
respectively.
For
x\in X
, the
mean asymptotic cell
and
mean proximal cell
of
x
are defined by

\text{MAsym}(T_{i},x)=\{y\in X:(x,y)\in\text{MAsym}(T_{i})\}

and

\text{MProx}(T_{i},x)=\{y\in X:(x,y)\in\text{MProx}(T_{i})\}

respectively.

The following lemmas are analogues of
[
JL2022
]
*Lemmas 4.1 and 4.8, which will be used later.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(T_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of bounded linear operators from a normed linear space
X
to a normed linear space
Y
. Then

(1)

for every
x\in X
, the mean proximal cell of
x
is a
G_{\delta}
subset of
X
;

(2)

the mean proximal relation of
(T_{i})_{i}
is a
G_{\delta}
subset of
X\times X
;

(3)

for every
\delta>0
and
x\in X
, the collection

\biggl\{y\in X:\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}x-T_{i}y\|\geq\delta\biggr\}

is a
G_{\delta}
subset of
X
;

(4)

for every
\delta>0
, the collection

\biggl\{(x,y)\in X\times X:\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}x-T_{i}y\|\geq\delta\biggr\}

is a
G_{\delta}
subset of
X\times X
;

(5)

for every
x\in X
, the set

\biggl\{y\in X:\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}x-T_{i}y\|=\infty\biggl\}

is a
G_{\delta}
subset of
X
;

(6)

the set

\biggl\{(x,y)\in X\times X:\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}x-T_{i}y\|=\infty\biggl\}

is a
G_{\delta}
subset of
X\times X
.

Proof.

(\ref{Lem: MProx cell G-delta})
Note that for every
x\in X
,

X_{n+1}:=\bigcap_{j=1}^{n}P(s_{k}^{(n,j)})\cap P(t_{k}^{(n)})\cap D

is also a dense
G_{\delta}
subset of
X
.
Pick
x_{n+1}\in X_{n+1}
with
\|x_{n+1}-z_{n+1}\|<\frac{1}{n+1}
.
For
j=1,\ldots,n
, there exist a subsequence
(s_{k}^{(n+1,j)})_{k}
of
(s_{k}^{(n,j)})_{k}
such that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{s_{k}^{(n+1,j)}}\sum_{i=1}^{s_{k}^{(n+1,j)}}\|T_{i}x_{n+1}\|=0,

a subsequence
(s_{k}^{(n+1,n+1)})_{k}
of
(t_{k}^{(n)})_{k}
such that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{s_{k}^{(n+1,n+1)}}\sum_{i=1}^{s_{k}^{(n+1,n+1)}}\|T_{i}x_{n+1}\|=0,

and an increasing sequence
(t_{k}^{(n+1)})_{k}
in
\mathbb{N}
such that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{t_{k}^{(n+1)}}\sum_{i=1}^{t_{k}^{(n+1)}}\|T_{i}x_{n+1}\|=\infty.

For any
\sum_{l=1}^{n+1}\alpha_{l}x_{l}\in\text{span}\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},x_{n+1}\}\backslash\{{\bf 0}\}
,

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{s_{k}^{(n+1,1)}}\sum_{i=1}^{s_{k}^{(n+1,1)}}\|T_{i}(\sum_{l=1}^{n+1}\alpha_{l}x_{l})\|\leq\sum_{l=1}^{n+1}|\alpha_{l}|\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{s_{k}^{(n+1,1)}}\sum_{i=1}^{s_{k}^{(n+1,1)}}\|T_{i}x_{l}\|=0.

Let
l^{\prime}=\min\{l\in\{1,\ldots,n+1\}:\alpha_{l}\neq 0\}
.
If
l^{\prime}<n+1
, then

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{s_{k}^{(n+1,l^{\prime}+1)}}\sum_{i=1}^{s_{k}^{(n+1,l^{\prime}+1)}}\|T_{i}(\sum_{l=l^{\prime}}^{n+1}\alpha_{l}x_{l})\|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle|\alpha_{l^{\prime}}|\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{s_{k}^{(n+1,l^{\prime}+1)}}\sum_{i=1}^{s_{k}^{(n+1,l^{\prime}+1)}}\|T_{i}x_{l^{\prime}}\|-\sum_{l=l^{\prime}+1}^{n+1}|\alpha_{l}|\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{s_{k}^{(n+1,l^{\prime}+1)}}\sum_{i=1}^{s_{k}^{(n+1,l^{\prime}+1)}}\|T_{i}x_{l}\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\infty-0=\infty.

If
l^{\prime}=n+1
, then by the construction one has

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{t_{k}^{(n+1)}}\sum_{i=1}^{t_{k}^{(n+1)}}\|T_{i}x_{n+1}\|=\infty.

So
\text{span}\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},x_{n+1}\}
is an absolutely mean irregular manifold.
By induction, we obtain that the subspace

\text{span}\{x_{m}:m\in\mathbb{N}\}
is a dense absolutely mean irregular manifold.
∎

Using Theorem
3.14
, we have the following characterization of mean Li-Yorke chaos for a sequence of multiples of iterations of the backward shift on
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N})
.

Proposition 3.16
.

Let
X=\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N})
and
B\colon X\to X
,
(x_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}\mapsto(x_{i})_{i=2}^{\infty}
.
For each
i\in\mathbb{N}
, let
T_{i}=\lambda_{i}B^{i}
,
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then the following assertions are equivalent:

(1)

(T_{i})_{i}
is mean Li-Yorke chaotic;

(2)

(T_{i})_{i}
has a dense absolutely mean irregular manifold;

(3)

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\lambda_{i}|=\infty
.

Proof.

(\ref{prop2: manifold})\Rightarrow(\ref{prop1: mean LY chaotic})
. It is clear.

(\ref{prop1: mean LY chaotic})\Rightarrow(\ref{prop3: lambda unbounded})
.
Otherwise, we assume that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\lambda_{i}|=C<\infty.

Since
(T_{i})_{i}
is mean Li-Yorke chaotic, one has
C\neq 0
.
For each
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N})
, we have
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|x_{i}|<\infty
.
Thus

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}|x_{i}|=0.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
.
Then there exists
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n>N_{0}
, one has

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\lambda_{i}|<C+\varepsilon

and

\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}|x_{i}|<\frac{\varepsilon}{C}.

Thus

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}x\|

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\lambda_{i}B^{i}x\|=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(|\lambda_{i}|\sum_{j=i+1}^{\infty}|x_{j}|)

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\biggr(\sum_{i=1}^{N_{0}}(|\lambda_{i}|\sum_{j=i+1}^{\infty}|x_{j}|)+\sum_{i=N_{0}+1}^{n}(|\lambda_{i}|\sum_{j=i+1}^{\infty}|x_{j}|)\biggr)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\biggr(\sum_{i=1}^{N_{0}}(|\lambda_{i}|\|x\|)+\sum_{i=N_{0}+1}^{n}(|\lambda_{i}|\frac{\varepsilon}{C})\biggr)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{C}.

By the arbitrariness of
\varepsilon
, we have
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}x\|=0
.
This implies that there is no absolutely mean semi-irregular vector.
By Proposition
3.6
,

(T_{i})_{i}
is not mean Li-Yorke chaotic, which is a contradiction.

(\ref{prop3: lambda unbounded})\Rightarrow(\ref{prop2: manifold})
.
Let
X_{0}
be the subspace of
X
consisting of vectors with only finite non-zero coordinates. It is clear that
X_{0}\times X_{0}\subset\textrm{MAsym}(T_{i})
. Then
\textrm{MAsym}(T_{i})
is dense in
X\times X
.
For every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
y_{k}=(0,0,\dotsc,0,1((k+1)\text{th coordinate)},0,0,\dotsc)
.
Then
(y_{k})_{k}
is a bounded sequence in
X
and

\sup_{k}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\|T_{i}y_{k}\|=\sup_{k}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}|\lambda_{i}|=\infty.

By Theorem
2.7
,
(T_{i})_{i}
is mean sensitive.
Now according to Theorem
3.14

(T_{i})_{i}
has a dense absolutely mean irregular manifold.
∎

4.
Mean Li-Yorke chaos for a submultiplicative sequence of operators

In this section, we want to give more characterizations of mean Li-Yorke chaos and dense mean Li-Yorke chaos for a sequence
of bounded linear operators on a Banach space.
To this end, we need the submultiplicative and almost-commuting properties for a sequence of operators.

Recall that a sequence
(c_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
of positive numbers is
submultiplicative
if

c_{n+m}\leq c_{n}c_{m},\ n,m=1,2,\dotsc.

Inspired by submultiplicative sequence, we introduce the following submultiplicative property for a seuqence of operators.

Definition 4.1
.

We say that a sequence
(T_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
of bounded linear operators on a Banach space
X
is
submultiplicative
if
there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\|T_{i+m}z\|\leq C\|T_{i}T_{m}z\|,\ \forall z\in X\ \text{and}\ i,m\in\mathbb{N}.

If
T
is an operator on
X
and
(\lambda_{i})_{i}
is a submultiplicative sequence, then the sequence
(\lambda_{i}T^{i})_{i}
of operators is submultiplicative.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(T_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
be a submultiplicative sequence of bounded linear operators on a Banach space
X
.
If
(T_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
is mean equicontinuous, then every proximal pair is mean asymptotic.

Proof.

Since
(T_{i})_{i}
is mean equicontinuous, for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that for any
x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in X
with
\|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\|<\delta
, we have

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}x^{\prime}-T_{i}y^{\prime}\|<\varepsilon.

For any proximal pair
(x,y)
, there exists
k>0
such that
\|T_{k}x-T_{k}y\|<\delta
. Then we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}x-T_{i}y\|=

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i+k}x-T_{i+k}y\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i+k}(x-y)\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}C\|T_{i}T_{k}(x-y)\|\quad\text{(by ($*$))}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle C\varepsilon.

The arbitrariness of
\varepsilon
shows that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}x-T_{i}y\|=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}x-T_{i}y\|=0,

showing that
(x,y)
is mean asymptotic.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
(T_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
be a submultiplicative sequence of operators on a Banach space
X
.
If there exists an absolutely mean semi-irregular vector, then
(T_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
is mean sensitive.

Proof.

Otherwise, we assume that
(T_{i})_{i}
is not mean sensitive, by Theorem
2.2
,
(T_{i})_{i}
is mean equicontinuous.
Let
x\in X
be an absolutely mean semi-irregular vector, then
(x,{\bf 0})
is a mean proximal pair.
By Lemma
4.2
,
(x,{\bf 0})
is mean asymptotic, that is

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}x\|=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}x\|=0,

which is a contradiction.
Thus
(T_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
is mean sensitive.
∎

We have the following characterization of dense mean Li-Yorke chaos of a sequences of bounded linear operators with the submultiplicative property.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
(T_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
be a submultiplicative sequence of bounded linear operators on a separable Banach space
X
.
Then the following assertions are equivalent:

(1)

(T_{i})_{i}
is densely mean Li-Yorke chaotic;

(2)

(T_{i})_{i}
has a dense set of mean Li-Yorke pairs;

(3)

(T_{i})_{i}
has a dense set of absolutely mean semi-irregular vectors;

(4)

(T_{i})_{i}
has a residual set of absolutely mean irregular vectors;

(5)

the mean proximal relation of
(T_{i})_{i}
is dense in
X\times X
and
(T_{i})_{i}
is mean sensitive;

(6)

the mean proximal cell of
\mathbf{0}
is dense in
X
and there exists a vector
x\in X
such that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}x\|>0.

Proof.

(\ref{thm1: densely mean LY chaotic})\Rightarrow(\ref{thm2: dense mean LY scramled pairs})\Rightarrow(\ref{thm3: dense mean semi-irr})\Rightarrow(\ref{thm: MProx cell dense-mean semi-irregular vector})
. Those are clear.

(\ref{thm5: MProx dense-mean sensitive})\Rightarrow(\ref{thm4: residual mean irr})\Rightarrow(\ref{thm1: densely mean LY chaotic})
. They follow from Theorem
3.9
.

(\ref{thm: MProx cell dense-mean semi-irregular vector})\Rightarrow(\ref{thm5: MProx dense-mean sensitive})
. Since
\mathrm{MProx}(T_{i},\mathbf{0})
is dense in
X
, by Lemma
3.3

(\ref{MProx dense})
we know that
\mathrm{MProx}(T_{i})
is dense in
X\times X
. And by Lemma
3.2

(\ref{Lem: MProx cell G-delta})
,
\mathrm{MProx}(T_{i},\mathbf{0})
is a residual set. If
\mathrm{MProx}(T_{i},\mathbf{0})=\mathrm{MAsym}(T_{i},\mathbf{0})
, then by Lemma
3.3

(\ref{MAsym residual})
we know that
\mathrm{MAsym}(T_{i},\mathbf{0})=X
, which contradicts
(\ref{thm: MProx cell dense-mean semi-irregular vector})
. Hence there is an absolutely mean semi-irregular vector in
\mathrm{MProx}(T_{i},\mathbf{0})
. By Lemma
4.2
,
(T_{i})_{i}
is not mean equicontinuous, thus
(T_{i})_{i}
is mean sensitive by Theorem
2.2
.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

According to Remark
3.10
,
(
2
)-(
6
) of Theorem
4.4

are also equivalent in the condition that
X
is a Banach space.

In
[
B2000
]
, Bernal-González introduced the following almost-commuting property for a sequence of operators.

Definition 4.6
.

We say that a sequence
(T_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
of bounded linear operators on a Banach space
X
is
almost-commuting
if

\lim_{i\to\infty}(T_{i}T_{k}-T_{k}T_{i})x=0,\ \forall x\in X,k\in\mathbb{N}.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
(T_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
be an almost-commuting sequence of bounded linear operators on a Banach space
X
and
(N_{n})_{n}
be a sequence in
\mathbb{N}
.
Then the collection

X_{0}=\biggl\{x\in X\colon\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{N_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}\|T_{i}x\|=0\biggr\}

is a
(T_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
-invariant subspace of
X
.

Proof.

For every
x,y\in X_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{N_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}\|T_{i}(x-y)\|

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{N_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}\|T_{i}x-T_{i}y\|

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{N_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}\|T_{i}x\|+\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{N_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}\|T_{i}y\|

\displaystyle=0,

and it is clearly that
\lambda x\in X_{0}
for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{K}
.
Thus
X_{0}
is a subspace of
X
.

For every
x\in X_{0}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{N_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}\|T_{i}(T_{k}x)\|

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{N_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}\|T_{i}T_{k}x-T_{k}T_{i}x+T_{k}T_{i}x\|

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{N_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}\|T_{i}T_{k}x-T_{k}T_{i}x\|+\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{N_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}\|T_{k}T_{i}x\|

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{N_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}\|T_{i}T_{k}x-T_{k}T_{i}x\|+\|T_{k}\|\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{N_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}\|T_{i}x\|

\displaystyle=0,

the last equation follows from that
(T_{i})_{i}
is alomost-commuting.
Then
T_{k}x\in X_{0}
, which implies that
X_{0}
is
(T_{i})_{i}
-invariant.
∎

We have the following characterization of mean Li-Yorke chaos for a sequence of bounded linear operators with the almost-commuting and submultiplicaitve properties.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
(T_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
be an almost-commuting, submultiplicative sequence of bounded linear operators on a Banach space
X
.
Then the following assertions are equivalent:

(1)

(T_{i})_{i}
has a mean Li-Yorke pair;

(2)

(T_{i})_{i}
has an absolutely mean semi-irregular vector;

(3)

(T_{i})_{i}
is mean Li-Yorke chaotic;

(4)

(T_{i})_{i}
has an absolutely mean irregular vector;

(5)

there exists a
(T_{i})_{i}
-invariant closed subspace
X_{0}
of
X
such that

X_{0}
has a residual subset of absolutely mean irregular vectors.

Proof.

(\ref{thm: mean LY scrambled pair})\Leftrightarrow(\ref{thm: mean semi-irregular vector})\Leftrightarrow(\ref{thm: mean LY chaotic})
. Those follow from Proposition
3.6
.

(\ref{thm: invariant space-residual mean irregular vectors})\Rightarrow(\ref{thm: mean irregular vector})\Rightarrow(\ref{thm: mean semi-irregular vector}).
Those are clear.

(\ref{thm: mean semi-irregular vector})\Rightarrow(\ref{thm: invariant space-residual mean irregular vectors})
.
Let
x_{0}
be an absolutely mean semi-irregular vector. Then there exists an increasing sequence of integers
(n_{k})_{k}
such that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{n_{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{k}}\|T_{i}x_{0}\|=0.

Define

X_{0}=\left\{y\in X\colon\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{n_{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{k}}\|T_{i}y\|=0\right\}.

As
(T_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
is almost-commuting, by Proposition
4.7

X_{0}
is a
(T_{i})_{i}
-invariant subspace of
X
.
Let
\widetilde{X}_{0}
be the closure of
X_{0}
.
Then
\widetilde{X}_{0}
is a
(T_{i})_{i}
-invariant closed subspace of
X
.

Clearly
x_{0}\in\widetilde{X}_{0}
and

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}x_{0}\|>0.

By the construction of
\widetilde{X}_{0}
, the mean proximal cell of
\mathbf{0}
contains
X_{0}
, and then is dense in
\widetilde{X}_{0}
. Now by Theorem
4.4
and Remark
4.5
, we know that
(T_{i}|_{\widetilde{X}_{0}})_{i=1}^{\infty}
has a residual set of absolutely mean irregular vectors.
∎

By the proof of Theorem
4.8
, we have the following consequence.

Corollary 4.9
.

Let
(T_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
be an almost-commuting, submultiplicative sequence of bounded linear operators on a Banach space
X
.
Then the set of all absolutely mean irregular vectors is dense in the set of all absolutely mean semi-irregular vectors.

In
[
BBP2020
]
, Bernardes et al. introduced the mean Li-Yorke chaos criterion for an operator. Here we generalize the concept to a sequence of operators.

Definition 4.10
.

Let
(T_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of bounded linear operators on a Banach space
X
.
We say that
(T_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
satisfies the
mean Li-Yorke chaos criterion
if there exists a subset
X_{0}
of
X
with the following properties:

(1)

for every
x\in X_{0}
,
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}x\|=0
;

(2)

there exist a sequence
(y_{k})_{k}
in
\overline{\text{span}(X_{0})}
and a sequence
(N_{k})_{k}
in
\mathbb{N}
such that for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\frac{1}{N_{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{k}}\|T_{i}y_{k}\|\geq k\|y_{k}\|.

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
(T_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
be an almost-commuting, submultiplicative sequence of bounded linear operators on a Banach space
X
.
Then
(T_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
is mean Li-Yorke chaotic if and only if it satisfies the mean Li-Yorke chaos criterion.

Proof.

Suppose that
(T_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
satisfies the mean Li-Yorke chaos criterion. Clearly,
\overline{\mathrm{span}(X_{0})}
is a closed subspace of
X
. Let
\tilde{y}_{k}=\frac{1}{\|y_{k}\|}y_{k}
. Clearly
(\tilde{y}_{k})_{k}
is a bounded sequence and
\sup_{k}\frac{1}{N_{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{k}}\|T_{i}\tilde{y}_{k}\|=\infty
. By Theorem
2.7
we know that
\left(T_{i}|_{\overline{\mathrm{span}(X_{0})}}\right)_{i=1}^{\infty}
is mean sensitive. Noticing that for any
y\in\mathrm{span}(X_{0})
, we have

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{i}y\|=0.

Hence we have
\mathrm{MProx}\left(T_{i}|_{\overline{\mathrm{span}(X_{0})}},\mathbf{0}\right)
is dense in
\overline{\mathrm{span}(X_{0})}
.
By Theorem
4.4
, we know that
\left(T_{i}|_{\overline{\mathrm{span}(X_{0})}}\right)_{i=1}^{\infty}
is mean Li-Yorke chaotic. Then
(T_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
is also mean Li-Yorke chaotic.

Now suppose that
(T_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
is mean Li-Yorke chaotic. By Theorem
4.8
, there exists a closed subspace
\widetilde{X}
which has a residual subset of absolutely mean irregular vectors. Let
X_{0}=\mathrm{MProx}(T_{i}|_{\widetilde{X}},\mathbf{0})
. By Theorem
4.4
and Theorem
2.7
, we know that
X_{0}
satisfies the requirements of the mean Li-Yorke chaos criterion.
∎

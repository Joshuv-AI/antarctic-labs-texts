FTIR study of the wild-type and mutant proteins of a viral rhodopsin, OLPVR1
Authors: Mako Aoyama, Kota Katayama, Hideki Kandori
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractOLPVR1 is a viral channelrhodopsin found in giant viruses, which can be a useful optogenetic tool for calcium-regulated cell functions. Determination of the open-state structure leads to one of the best understood channelrhodopsins. Recent FTIR spectroscopy of OLPVR1 reported unique structures in the resting and photoisomerized states at 77 K. Here we attempted to obtain difference FTIR spectra of mutants in OLPVR1, where we focused six key residues, S11, E44, E51, D76, D200, and N205. We prepared S11T, E51D, E51Q, N205Q, E44Q, D76N, and D200N, to which spectroscopic analysis was applied. From the λmax values of D76N and D200N at pH 7 and 8, it was concluded that D200 is the primary counterion. FTIR spectroscopy showed that the carboxylic C=O stretch of WT at 1722 (–)/1707 (+) cm–1 in H2O, and at 1718 (–)/1698 (+) cm–1 in D2O disappeared in E51Q, from which protonation of E51 was concluded. S11T and N205Q alter the C=O stretch frequency of E51 in the dark, but not in the photoisomerized state at 77 K. This observation implicates that E51 interacts with S11 and N205 through a water-containing hydrogen bond, whereas the interaction is broken by light. Recent crystallographic study visualized flipping motion of E51 upon opening state of OLPVR1, and the present FTIR study reports the removal of E51 from the network with S11 and N205 occurring even at the primary photoreaction stage. Retinal photoisomerization accompanies hydrogen-bonding switch of E51, leading to channel opening of OLPVR1 in late timescale.Keywords: Rhodopsin, retinal chromophore, FTIR, protein-bound water

Significance

Difference FTIR spectroscopy is a powerful method to investigate light-induced structural changes of photoreceptive protein during function. In particular, hydrogen bonds are well characterized by this method. Here light-induced difference FTIR spectroscopy was applied to a viral channelrhodopsin OLPVR1. This method provides direct evidence of protonation of E51, a key residue in channel gating. FTIR spectra of mutants further revealed the hydrogen-bonding switch of E51 even at 77 K, which drives channel opening in OLPVR1.

Introduction

OLPVR1, Organic Lake Phycodnavirus Rhodopsin 1, is a viral channelrhodopsin found in giant viruses [1]. Viral rhodopsins are classified into two major classes, VR1 and VR2, and OLPVR1 belongs to the VR1 family [2]. In 2020, structure of OLPVR1 was determined and its function was proposed as a viral channelrhodopsin [1]. Nevertheless, in the paper, ion-channel function could not be measured for OLPVR1, but for another viral rhodopsin [1]. Then, it was found that OLPVR1 localizes to the endoplasmic reticulum (ER), not in plasma membranes, when expressed in Xenopus oocytes or mammalian cells [3]. This is the reason for no ion-transport of OLPVR1 in the patch-clamp measurements [1]. Interestingly, photoexcitation of OLPVR1 triggers an increase in cytoplasmic calcium, suggesting OLPVR1 being a novel optogenetic tool for calcium-regulated cell functions [3]. Spectroscopic studies have been performed to characterize the molecular properties of OLPVR1 [4–6]. In addition, open structure of OLPVR1 was reported recently [7], by which OLPVR1 became one of the best understood channelrhodopsins.

OLPVR1 is a channelrhodopsin, whereas it has an interesting structural coincidence of hydrogen-bonding network to a light-driven proton-pump bacteriorhodopsin (BR). Figure 1 shows the water-containing hydrogen-bonding network near the Schiff base of OLPVR1 (left and right panels from ref. [1] and [7], respectively), where a planar pentagonal oxygen cluster is composed of the side-chain oxygens of D76 and D200, and three water molecules. The pentagonal water cluster probably stabilizes a quadrupole in the Schiff base region; two positive charges at the Schiff base and R73, and two negative charges at D76 and D200. These structures resemble very well those of BR (corresponding aspartates are D85 and D212) [1,5], despite different channel and pump functions. Distance between the Schiff base and a water is 2.7 Å, and the distances from the water to D76 and D200 are 2.6 Å and 2.7 Å in OLPVR1, respectively, suggesting that D76 and D200 are symmetrically located to the bridged water and the retinal Schiff base (Left panel in Figure 1) [1]. Recent structural study of OLPVR1 reported two forms, all-trans, 15-anti and all-trans, 15-syn chromophore (Right panel of Figure 1), and according to the normal all-trans, 15-anti form, distance between the Schiff base and a water is 2.5 Å, and the distances from the water to D76 and D200 are 2.7 Å and 2.9 Å in OLPVR1, respectively [7]. Symmetrical structure is also the case in BR. Similarly to Figure 1 in OLPVR1, two aspartates are symmetrically located, but our previous FTIR study of protein-bound water molecules revealed asymmetric interaction of the bridged water, namely much stronger interaction to D85 than to D212 [8–10]. Consequently, D85 acts as the primary counterion of the Schiff base and receives the Schiff base proton during the photocycle of BR [11–13].

Structures of the Schiff base region of the retinal chromophore and the S11-E51-N205 triad in OLPVR1 (Left, PDB ID: 7AKX [1], Right, PDB ID: 9IN7 [7]).

Recent low-temperature difference FTIR study aimed at revealing such local interactions in OLPVR1 [5]. Vibrational analysis of the C=N stretch and N-D stretches revealed stronger hydrogen bond of the protonated Schiff base to the bridged water in OLPVR1 than in BR. In contrast, hydrogen-bonding strengths of the bridged water were moderate, whose O-D stretch in D2O is located at >2500 cm–1. In OLPVR1, the interaction between the Schiff base and the bridged water is likely to be a key hydrogen bond, and the water donates hydrogen bonds to D76 and D200 equally. This interpretation is consistent with similar λmax of D76N (527 nm) and D200N (533 nm) at pH 7.0 [5].

In the last paper, FTIR spectra of the wild-type (WT) OLPVR1 and the λmax of mutants such as E51Q, D76N, and D200N were reported [5]. On the other hand, because of limited expression level in Sf9 cells, we were not able to measure difference FTIR spectra of mutant proteins in OLPVR1. Nevertheless, vibrational spectra provide fruitful structural information in the resting state and photoreaction intermediates, including retinal chromophore, side chains of protein, peptide backbone, and protein-bound water molecules [10]. In this study, we thus tackled to obtain difference FTIR spectra of mutants in OLPVR1, and the spectra were compared to those of WT. Here we focused six residues in OLPVR1, S11, E44, E51, D76, D200, and N205 (Figure 1). D76 and D200 constitute the Schiff base counterion(s) as we showed before [5]. E51 corresponds to E90 in channelrhodopsin 2 from Chlamydomonas reinhardtii (ChR2), a key residue in the channel function [14–18]. It should be noted that the flipping motion of E51 was observed between the resting and open structures of OLPVR1 [7]. This clearly shows the important role of E51, but we do not know if E51 is protonated or deprotonated (negatively charged). Although E51Q possesses the λmax close to WT [15], FTIR spectroscopy is able to monitor the C=O stretch of protonated carboxylic acids (COOH group) directly, as it evidenced protonation of E90 in ChR2 [15,17]. Here we prepared E51D and E51Q mutants. In the resting state, E51 forms a hydrogen-bonding network through a water molecule with S11 and N205 (“SEN triad”; Figure 1) [1]. Such hydrogen bond is extended to E44 through water chain, and comparison of the close and open structures suggests that E44 constitutes intracellular gate in OLPVR1 [7].

We expressed S11T, E51D, E51Q, N205Q, E44Q, D76N, and D200N in Sf9 cells as did for WT [5]. We successfully obtained such mutant samples by solubilization and purification, but photoreaction of D200N was too small to obtain its difference FTIR spectra. Difference FTIR spectra of other samples were obtained at 77 K, and the comparison with those of WT provided structural information of the resting and photoisomerized states. Consequently, we identified C=O stretch of E51. From the analysis of S11T and N205Q, it was concluded that the interaction oof E51 to S11 and N205 through a water molecule is broken and E51 becomes out of the hydrogen bond. Initial switching mechanism of a viral channelrhodopsin OLPVR1 will be discussed based on the present FTIR study.

Materials and methods

Sample preparation

The gene of OLPVR1 was synthesized (GENEWIZ). It was cloned into a pET21a (+)-vector with a C-terminal 8×His-tag (18–21) and then subcloned into a pFastBac vector with In-Fusion® Cloning (In-Fusion® Cloning with Vaccinia Virus DNA Polymerase, Irwin et al., Methods Mol Biol., 2012). For mutagenesis, a Quick-change site-directed mutagenesis kit (Stratagene) was used. WT and mutants were expressed in Sf9 cells cultured in ESF921 (Expression Systems) at 28°C for 2 days. Cells were collected 1 day after addition of 10 μM all-trans-retinal (Toronto Research Chemicals). Cell pellets were subjected to low salt washing through centrifugation for 15 min at one time with a buffer containing 25 mM HEPES (pH 7.5), 20 mM KCl, 10 mM MgCl2, and a protease inhibitor cocktail tablet (Sigma-Aldrich), broken down through dounce homogenization, and subjected to high salt washing with a buffer containing 25 mM HEPES (pH 7.5), 20 mM KCl, 10 mM MgCl2, 1 M NaCl, a protease inhibitor cocktail tablet (Sigma-Aldrich), and benzonase nuclease (Novagen). This high salt washing was conducted through centrifugation for 20 min at least two times. The purified membrane was mixed with 50% (v/v) glycerol, flash-frozen with liquid nitrogen, and stored at –80°C until use. The sample after membrane preparation was solubilized using a buffer containing 30 mM Tris-HCl (pH 7.0), 500 mM NaCl, 5 mM imidazole, 10 μM all-trans-retinal, and 1% (w/v) n-dodecyl-β-D-maltoside (DDM). The lysate was removed by centrifugation at 100,000 g for 30 min at 4°C, and the supernatant was purified using His-Trap Co2+-TALON affinity chromatography. Co2+ resin was added to the supernatant, mixed for more than 1.5 h, transferred to a column, and washed with buffers. One contained 30 mM Tris-HCl (pH 7.0), 500 mM NaCl, 5 mM imidazole, and 0.1% DDM, and the other contained 20 mM Tris-HCl (pH 7.0), 150 mM NaCl, 5 mM imidazole, and 0.02% DDM. The purified OLPVR1 sample was extracted with a buffer containing 20 mM Tris-HCl (pH 7.0), 150 mM NaCl, 300 mM imidazole, and 0.02% DDM and then concentrated using an Amicon Ultra Centrifugal Filter (MWCO 30 kDa, Merck-Millipore).

UV-visible spectroscopy

UV–visible spectroscopy was performed using the purified OLPVR1 [5]. The sample was reconstituted into a membrane composed of soybean phospholipids (asolectin; Sigma-Aldrich) with a protein-to-lipid molar ratio of 1:20, by removing the DDM with dialysis against buffer containing 150 mM NaCl, 20 mM Tris-HCl (pH 8.0) at 4°C.

Low-temperature FTIR spectroscopy

Low-temperature FTIR spectroscopy was also performed using the lipid-reconstituted OLPVR1. The remaining DDM was removed by Bio-Beads (SM-2, Bio-Rad). The samples in asolectin liposomes were washed repeatedly with a buffer containing 2 mM NaCl and 2 mM KH2PO4 (pH 7.5) for WT or 2 mM NaCl and 2 mM NaH2PO4 (pH 7.5) for mutants and collected by ultracentrifugation for 20 minutes at 222,000 g at 4°C. The lipid-reconstituted OLPVR1 was finally suspended in the same buffer and then placed on a BaF2 window to prepare dry-layer thin film. OLPVR1 films were hydrated with H2O, D2O, or H218O before measurements. The sample was then placed in the cell of a cryostat (Optistat, Oxford Instruments) mounted in a UV–vis spectrometer (V-750, JASCO) and a FTIR spectrometer (Carry670, Agilent Technologies) and cooled to 77 K [5,10].

The samples except for D76N were first illuminated with a 501 nm light (by use of an interference filter) at 77 K for 2–5 min to convert OLPVR1 to the K intermediate, OLPVR1K. D76N was first illuminated with a 530 nm light (by use of an interference filter) at 77 K for 1 min. Then, OLPVR1K was fully reconverted to OLPVR1 upon illumination with >530 nm light (O55 filter) for 1–2 min, which was evidenced by the spectral shape and amplitude in a mirror image of that for the OLPVR1 to OLPVR1K transition. D76NK was fully reconverted to D76N upon illumination with >550 nm light (O57 filter) for 1 min. Therefore, we repeated cycles of alternating illumination with 501 nm (530 nm for D76N) light and >530 nm (>550 nm for D76N) light a number of times. We calculated difference spectra from two spectra constructed from 128 interferograms taken before and after illumination. On average, 56 (WT), 80 (S11T), 100 (E51D), 80 (E51Q), 60 (N205Q), 60 (E44Q H2O), 46 (E44Q D2O), and 100 (D76N) difference spectra obtained in this way, which were used to produce the OLPVR1K minus OLPVR1 spectra in the hydrated films of H2O, D2O, or H218O.

Results

Absorption spectra of the mutants of OLPVR1

Figure 2 shows absorption spectra of WT, S11T, E51D, E51Q, N205Q, E44Q, D76N, and D200N at pH 8.0. The λmax of WT is located at 505 nm, and many mutants exhibit similar λmax except for D76N (513 nm) and D200N (529 nm). It is therefore likely that D76 and D200 are negatively charged, and larger spectral red-shift in D200N (24 nm) than in D76N (8 nm) is an indication that D200 and D76 are the primary and secondary counterion of the protonated Schiff base, respectively. Interestingly, we reported the λmax of D76N and D200N at 527 and 533 nm, respectively, at pH 7.0, and we concluded that D76 and D200 act as the Schiff base counterion in OLPVR1 more equally than in BR [5]. The present results clearly show different pH dependence between D76N and D200N. In case of D200N, the λmax were similar between pH 7.0 (533 nm) and 8.0 (529 nm). In contrast, the λmax of D76N were highly pH-dependent; 527 nm at pH 7.0 and 513 nm at pH 8.0. This suggests the presence of a protonatable group whose pKa is located at around pH 7~8 in D76N, but not in D200N. Absorption spectrum of D76N is also very broad, suggesting structural heterogeneity. Recent structural analysis of OLPVR1 at pH 8 reports D200 being the primary SB counterion [7]. The present mutation study at pH 8.0 is consistent with structural analysis.

Absorption spectra of the wild-type (WT) and mutant OLPVR1 proteins at pH 8.0.

In addition to visible absorption at 450–650 nm, UV absorption (300–400 nm) should be noted for some mutants. Because of low expression level, absorption spectrum of D200N contains high scattering at 300–400 nm. D76N and D200N exhibit peaks at 350–370 nm, presumably owing to the deprotonated Schiff base of the retinal chromophore. Among mutants, a strong UV absorption was observed only for E51D, which is an interesting observation. If D51 is deprotonated, its negative charge probably stabilizes the protonation state of the Schiff base. Nevertheless, deprotonated Schiff base is certainly populated, suggesting the complex mechanism to lower the pKa of the Schiff base in E51D.

Light-induced difference FTIR spectra of the mutants of OLPVR1 at 77 K

Figure 3 compares difference FTIR spectra of the mutants of OLPVR1 with those of WT at 77 K. Because of low expression, we did not observe light-induced difference FTIR spectra for D200N. Photoreactions of D76N and E51D were also smaller than those of others, and signal-to-noise ratios of the difference spectra were low. Positive and negative signals in Figure 3 originate from vibrational bands of the K intermediate and dark state of OLPVR1, respectively, in H2O (black line) and D2O (red line).

Light-induced difference FTIR spectra of the WT and mutant OLPVR1 proteins in the 1800–800 cm–1 region. Positive and negative bands originate from vibrational bands of the K intermediate and initial state, respectively. Black and red lines are measured in H2O and D2O, respectively. The spectra of WT are taken from Ref 5, while those of S11T, E51D, E51Q, N205Q, E44Q and D76N are scaled by 5.8 (H2O) or 7.1 (D2O), 10 (H2O) or 9.6 (D2O), 2.5 (H2O) or 2.0 (D2O), 1.8 (H2O) or 1.7 (D2O), 1.8 (H2O) or 2.4 (D2O), and 17 (H2O) or 11 (D2O), respectively.

Mutation effects on the chromophore vibrations

Ethylenic stretching vibrations (C=C stretch) of the retinal chromophore appear at 1550–1500 cm–1, and the low-frequency shifts in WT and all mutants indicate formation of a red-shifted K intermediate (P1 state [4]) (Figure 3). The peaks at 1546 (–)/1531 (+) cm–1 in WT were similarly observed for the mutants except for E51Q (1542 (–)/1528 (+) cm–1) and D76N (1540 (–)/1525 (+) cm–1). The lower frequencies for D76N are consistent with the red-shifted λmax (513 nm), while the result of E51Q is not clear from absorption spectra (Figure 2).

In the C-C stretching region, WT exhibits a peak pair at 1199 (–)/1183 (+) cm–1, which is an indication of the conversion from the all-trans to the 13-cis form [19,20]. Similar spectral features in all mutants indicate photoisomerization and formation of the red-shifted K intermediate at 77 K, like other microbial rhodopsins. An additional positive peak was observed at 1193 cm–1 in E51D, while additional peaks were observed at 1208 (–)/1194 (+) cm–1 in D76N. These observations implicate the presence of heterogeneous chromophore structures by the mutations. Despite similar spectral features, we observed mutant-specific signals, which will be shown in more detail below.

Figure 4 compares difference spectra in the hydrogen-out-of-plane (HOOP) region, where positive bands signify retinal distortion upon photoisomerization [21,22]. When peaks are shifted between H2O and D2O, they are assignable for the HOOP vibrations near the Schiff base as the protonated Schiff base only differs between H2O (C=NH) and D2O (C=ND). In WT, the most intense band was observed at 1014 cm–1 (in H2O) and 997 cm–1 (in D2O), both of which are H/D exchangeable. The peak at 1014 cm–1 is very sharp in H2O, but it is because of the presence of the H/D unexchangeable negative peak at 1012 cm–1. This negative band is highly conserved for other rhodopsins, and identified as methyl-rock vibrations in all-trans retinal from the study of BR [23]. At lower frequency region, WT contains two H/D unexchangeable peaks at 966 and 952 cm–1, indicating skeletal HOOP vibrations. These spectral features are highly similar to those of GtCCR4 and C1C2 [5], suggesting larger motion at the middle of the retinal chromophore in ChRs than in light-driven proton-pump rhodopsins such as BR.

Light-induced difference FTIR spectra of the WT and mutant OLPVR1 proteins in the 1050–850 cm–1 region. Black and red lines are measured in H2O and D2O, respectively. Spectra of WT are taken from Ref 5.

Peak frequencies of HOOP vibrations in WT are identical in S11T, N205Q, and E44Q, indicating that these mutations do not affect the structures before and after retinal photoisomerization. In contrast, mutant specific HOOP bands were observed for E51D, E51Q, and D76N. It should be noted that the H/D unexchangeable HOOP peaks at 966 and 952 cm–1 appeared in these mutants, indicating that the chromophore distortions in the middle part are similar among all mutants tested. Spectral feature looks similar between E51Q and WT, though the positive peak in D2O appears at 993 and 997 cm–1 in E51Q and WT, respectively. On the other hand, HOOP vibrations are considerably different in E51D, strong peaks at 995 and 975 cm–1 in H2O and D2O, respectively. Such features resemble those of D76N, exhibiting strong peaks at 990 and 984/978 cm–1 in H2O and D2O, respectively. It is reasonable that lack of a negative charge at position 76 significantly affects the structure and photoisomerization dynamics in OLPVR1. On the other hand, mutation of the SEN triad shows little effects on the distorted chromophore structure after photoisomerization except for E51D. As D51 has a shorter side chain than E51, hydrogen-bonding network in the SEN triad may be important for proper light-energy storage in primary intermediates.

Figure 5 compares difference spectra in the 1670–1590 cm–1 region, which contains various protein vibrations including amide-I, the C=O stretch of peptide backbone. The C=N stretching vibration of the protonated Schiff base only appears as the chromophore vibration in this region. The C=N stretching mode is coupled to the N-H bending vibration of the Schiff base, by which C=NH stretch is up-shifted from C=ND stretch. The difference in frequency between H2O and D2O has been regarded as a measure of hydrogen-bonding strength of the Schiff base [24]. In case of WT, the peaks at 1657 (–)/1626 (+) cm–1 in H2O and at 1635 (–)/1606 (+) cm–1 in D2O are candidates of the C=NH and C=ND stretches, respectively. Difference frequencies between C=NH and C=ND stretches are similar for the resting state (22 cm–1) and the K intermediate (20 cm–1), implying similar hydrogen-bonding strength before and after retinal photoisomerization. It is noted that vibrational bands are mostly described by the C=N stretch of the retinal chromophore, suggesting little changes in amide-I vibrations.

Light-induced difference FTIR spectra of the WT and mutant OLPVR1 proteins in the 1670–1590 cm–1 region. Black and red lines are measured in H2O and D2O, respectively. Spectra of WT are taken from Ref 5.

The same peak patterns were observed for E51Q and E44Q. In case of E51D, peaks were observed at 1653 (–)/1622 (+) cm–1 in H2O and at 1631 (–)/1615 (+) cm–1 in D2O. Difference frequencies between C=NH and C=ND stretches are much smaller for the K intermediate (7 cm–1) than for the resting state (22 cm–1), implying the hydrogen bond of the Schiff base broken by retinal photoisomerization. Unique HOOP vibrations near the Schiff base in E51D must be correlated with the altered Schiff base hydrogen bond. In case of D76N, while negative bands were unclear, the positive peaks at 1623/1616 cm–1 in H2O and at 1611 cm–1 in D2O are candidates of the C=NH and C=ND stretches in the K intermediate, respectively, suggesting weak hydrogen bond of the Schiff base. Correlation with the unique HOOP vibrations is also the case for D76N. Interestingly, difference spectra in this frequency region coincide with the baseline in S11T and N205Q. This suggests no changes in the Schiff base’s C=N stretches in addition to amide-I vibrations.

Mutation effects on vibrations of protein and water

Figure 6 compares difference spectra in the 1760–1670 cm–1 region, where C=O stretches of protonated carboxylic acids appear at >1700 cm–1. WT exhibits a peak pair at 1722 (–)/1707 (+) cm–1 in H2O, and at 1718 (–)/1698 (+) cm–1 in D2O, indicating that a carboxylate is protonated and the C=O group strengthens its hydrogen bond upon retinal photoisomerization. Among the candidate residues near the retinal chromophore, D76 and D200 are unlikely as they act as the Schiff base counterions, while E51 and E44 are possible candidates. In case of E44Q, the same bands were observed at the same frequencies, excluding E44 as the origin. Therefore, retinal photoisomerization does not accompany structural changes of E44. On the other hand, the pair band of WT disappeared in E51Q. It is thus likely that E51 is protonated in the resting state of OLPVR1, and the hydrogen bond of the C=O group is strengthened upon photoisomerization as is seen from the spectral downshift from 1722 cm–1 to 1707 cm–1.

Light-induced difference FTIR spectra of the WT and mutant OLPVR1 proteins in the 1760–1670 cm–1 region. Black and red lines are measured in H2O and D2O, respectively. Spectra of WT are taken from Ref 5.

In case of E51D, we expected spectral changes of the C=O stretch of protonated D51, but bands were unclear as well as E51Q. Noisy spectra of E51D could be the reason, though D51 might be deprotonated. In case of D76N, two positive peaks were observed at 1711 and 1697 cm–1 in H2O, and the negative peaks were unclear. Observation of two positive peaks suggests structural heterogeneity of D76N, which was also the case for multiple C=C stretching vibrations of the retinal chromophore (Figure 3). In contrast, similar spectral changes were observed for S11T and N205Q with modified frequencies. The negative peak appeared at 1715 and 1713 cm–1 in S11T and N205Q, respectively, while positive peaks were identical to those of WT.

Figure 7a and b compare difference spectra in the 3640–3570 cm–1 and 2700–2625 cm–1 region in H2O and D2O, which contain X-H and X-D stretching vibrations under weak hydrogen-bonding conditions, respectively [10]. A peak pair at 3614 (+)/3605 (–) cm–1 in H2O exhibits spectral downshift to 3601 (+)/3593 (–) cm–1 in H218O (Figure 7a), indicating that these vibrations originate from O-H stretch of water. Similarly, a peak pair at 2669 (+)/2662 (–) cm–1 in D2O downshifts to 2653 (+)/2646 (–) cm–1 in D218O (Figure 7b) as reported previously [5]. Therefore, these vibrations are assigned as O-D stretch of heavy water. These peaks are located at high frequency tail of stretching vibration of water and heavy water and thus corresponding to a so-called dangling bond [25,26]. A light-driven proton-pump bacteriorhodopsin exhibits a pair band of dangling bonded water at 3643 (–)/3636 (+) cm–1 in H2O and at 2690 (–)/2684 (+) cm–1 in D2O [27]. Based on the comprehensive mutation study, the water molecule was identified as the one hydrogen-bonded with D85, the primary counterion, but the O-H group is free from hydrogen bond [8–10].

Light-induced difference FTIR spectra of the WT and mutant OLPVR1 proteins in the 3640–3570 cm–1 region (a) and 2700–2625 cm–1 region (b). Black and blue lines are measured in H2O and H218O (a), D2O and D218O (b), respectively. Spectra of WT (H2O, D2O, and D218O) are taken from Ref 5 and the spectrum of D218O is scaled by 0.55.

A peak pair at 3614 (+)/3605 (–) cm–1 in WT was similarly observed in E51Q, N205Q, and E44Q, while the peak frequencies were shifted to 3612 (+)/3602 (–) cm–1 in S11T, and to 3608 (+)/3598 (–) cm–1 in E51D (Figure 7a). On the other hand, the peak pair disappeared in D76N. This observation strongly suggests that the water molecule forms a hydrogen bond with its counterion D76, and the O-H group is free from hydrogen bond. While similar water-containing hydrogen-bonding network near the Schiff base region (Figure 1) was gained between OLPVR1 and BR, retinal photoisomerization causes up-shift or down-shift of a dangling bonded water in OLPVR1 or BR, respectively [8,9]. Figure 7b shows similar results for O-D stretches in D2O, though signal was small and signal-to-noise ratio was poor for D76N. A peak pair at 2669 (+)/2662 (–) cm–1 in WT was similarly observed in S11T, E51Q, N205Q, and E44Q, while the peak frequencies were shifted to 2665 (+)/2653 (–) cm–1 in E51D.

Discussion

OLPVR1 contains four key carboxylates inside protein: E44, E51, D76, and D200 [1]. D76 and D200 constitute the Schiff base counterions by deprotonation. We previously reported the λmax of D76N and D200N at 527 and 533 nm, respectively, at pH 7.0, and we concluded that D76 and D200 act as the Schiff base counterion in OLPVR1 more equally than in BR [5]. According to the recent structural analysis of OLPVR1 at pH 8, however, D200 is much closer to the Schiff base than D76 [7]. Present study showed that the λmax of D76N and D200N at 513 and 529 nm, respectively, at pH 8.0 (Figure 2), suggesting that D200 is the primary counterion of the protonated Schiff base at this pH. This is an opposite result of BR, where D85 is the primary counterion. Highly pH-dependent absorption of D76N indicates the presence of a protonatable group that affects the absorption in D76N, but not in D200N. We infer that an electrostatic interaction between the protonated Schiff base and D200 is maintained in D76N, while the pentagonal cluster structure (Figure 1) would be highly perturbed, and pH-dependent structural changes in this region lead to different color between pH 7 and 8.

D76 and D200 are the Schiff base counterions, whose mutations largely change molecular properties of the proteins. Although absorption spectra were obtained for D200N, the photoreaction of D200N was too small to obtain its difference FTIR spectra. Signal of D76N was also small, but difference FTIR spectra were obtained upon light-induced all-trans to 13-cis photoisomerization (Figure 3). Water bands of WT at 3614 (+)/3605 (–) cm–1 in H2O and at 2669 (+)/2662 (–) cm–1 in D2O disappeared only in D76N (Figure 7), suggesting the water molecule is located near D76. These peaks are located at high frequency tail of stretching vibration of water and heavy water, indicating that the water O-H (O-D in D2O) group is free from hydrogen bond (so called dangling bond). From the comprehensive mutation study of BR, similar water bands at 3643 (–)/3636 (+) cm–1 in H2O and at 2690 (–)/2684 (+) cm–1 in D2O were identified as the one hydrogen-bonded with D85, the primary counterion, but the O-H group is free from hydrogen bond [8,9]. The present results are consistent with the same origin in OLPVR1, the dangling O-H stretch from the water molecule hydrogen-bonded with D76, but the O-H group is free from hydrogen bond. Interestingly, retinal photoisomerization causes weakened or strengthened hydrogen bond of the dangling bonded water in OLPVR1 or BR, respectively (Figure 7).

The present FTIR analysis showed that E51 is protonated, as the carboxylic C=O stretch of WT at 1722 (–)/1707 (+) cm–1 in H2O, and at 1718 (–)/1698 (+) cm–1 in D2O disappeared only in E51Q (Figure 6). The C=O group forms a hydrogen bond, which is further strengthened upon photoisomerization. Figure 1 shows that hydrogen-bonding network is formed from E51, S11, and N205 through a water molecule (SEN triad). It is possible that the water O-H group donates a hydrogen bond to E51, where the acceptor is the C=O group of the protonated E51. Presence of the water-containing hydrogen-bonding network in SEN triad (Dark state in Figure 8) is supported by the results that the C=O stretch of E51 at 1722 cm–1 in WT is downshifted to 1715 cm–1 in S11T, and to 1713 cm–1 in N205Q in the dark state (Figure 6).

Light-triggered activation process in OLPVR1. In the dark state (PDB ID: 7AKX [1]), there are two hydrogen-bonding networks, one from retinal Schiff base, D76, and D200, and another from S11, E51, and N205 (SEN triad). In the primary intermediate, the hydrogen-bonding interaction of E51 with S11 and N205 is disrupted (this study), leading to channel opening of OLPVR1. Ion-pathway in the open state is schematically drawn based on the open-state structure [7].

Interestingly, the positive band at 1707 cm–1 in WT was common in S11T and N205Q (Figure 6). This observation implies that E51 interacts with S11 and N205 through a water-containing hydrogen bond in the dark, whereas the interaction is broken between E51 and S11, and between E51 and N205 by light (Primary intermediate in Figure 8). Upon retinal photoisomerization, E51 forms a new hydrogen bond, whose partner is not identified in this study, but S11 and N205 are not the interaction partners. Recent crystallographic study visualized flipping motion of E51 upon opening state of OLPVR1 [7], while the present FTIR study reports the removal of E51 from the network with S11 and N205 occurring even at the primary photoreaction stage, which leads to an open state in late timescale (Figure 8).

Water O-H stretch in WT at 3614 (+)/3605 (–) cm–1 was observed at the identical frequencies in E51Q, N205Q, E44Q, and S11T (in D2O). The frequencies were slightly changed in S11T in H2O. It is likely that altered hydrogen bond of the SEN triad does not affect the pentagonal cluster structure near the Schiff base. Two hydrogen-bonding networks, the Schiff base, D76, D200 region, and the E51, S11, N205 region, are isolated with each other. During the photocycle, presumably, E51 moves and changes the interaction partner, eventually interacting with D200 in the open state at late timescale [7]. Difference FTIR spectra of E44Q were identical to those of WT. It is therefore likely that the structural changes upon retinal photoisomerization at 77 K do not transfer to the region of E44. Relaxation of the K intermediate probably change the E44 moiety, and the intracellular gate is open, which should be measured at higher temperatures.

Conclusion

By means of difference FTIR spectroscopy at 77 K, unique structures were elucidated in the resting and photoisomerized states in OLPVR1. In particular, present mutation study allowed identification of several vibrational bands. The carboxylic C=O stretch of WT at 1722 (–)/1707 (+) cm–1 in H2O, and at 1718 (–)/1698 (+) cm–1 in D2O disappeared only in E51Q, from which we concluded that E51 is protonated. S11T and N205Q alter the C=O stretch frequency of E51 in the dark, but not in the photoisomerized state at 77 K. Therefore, E51 interacts with S11 and N205 through a water-containing hydrogen bond, but the interaction of E51 is broken with S11 and N205. Recent crystallographic study monitored flipping motion of E51 upon channel opening of OLPVR1, forming a hydrogen bond with D200 [7]. It is likely that the removal of E51 from the network with S11 and N205 takes place even at the primary photoreaction stage.

Difference FTIR spectroscopy is a powerful method to investigate light-induced structural changes of photoreceptive proteins, and this work reports the event at the early stage. Further study in late intermediates will lead to better understanding how OLPVR1 changes the structure and open the channel, which is our future focus.

Predefined-time sliding mode control for position and attitude of quadrotor with variable exponent coefficients
Authors: Lifeng Hou, Jianhua Zhang, Zhanyang Yu, Xinxin Wang, Cheng Siong Chin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This paper presents a comprehensive study on predefined-time sliding mode control applied to quadrotor systems with variable exponent coefficients. The main goal is to achieve efficient control with predefined-time convergence to the desired target. Novel predefined-time sliding mode control and predefined-time neural networks sliding model control algorithms are developed to adapt to the dynamics of variable coefficients and ensure system stability. The stability analysis confirms their capability to achieve predefined-time convergence. Extensive numerical simulations on diverse nonlinear systems validate the superiority of the proposed approach over conventional sliding mode control and neural networks control.

Introduction

Quadrotor aerial vehicles (UAVs) have gained significant attention in recent years due to their versatility in applications such as surveillance, search and rescue, payload delivery, and environmental monitoring. The effectiveness of UAV control strategies has a direct impact on overall system performance1. The control of nonlinear systems has garnered significant attention in various engineering and scientific fields due to their widespread applications in modern technology2–4. Nonlinear systems are known for their complex dynamic behavior and the presence of uncertainties, posing significant challenges in control design. To tackle these complexities and achieve robust and efficient control, researchers have explored various control methodologies5,6. Among these, sliding mode control has emerged as a powerful technique that provides excellent performance, especially for nonlinear systems7–9.

Sliding mode control involves the introduction of a sliding surface in the state space to guide the system states towards a desired target rapidly10,11. Its robustness against parameter variations and external disturbances makes it suitable for stabilizing complex systems12–14. However, conventional sliding mode control suffers from one limitation: the convergence time is typically unbounded, which may not be acceptable in time-critical applications such as autonomous vehicles or robotic systems15–17.

Predefined-time (PT) control18,19 is a novel control strategy that ensures convergence time in a strictly predefined settling time, independent of initial conditions. Unlike traditional control methods that linear feedback control only ensures asymptotic stability with infinite time, finite/fixed-time20 and predefined-time control guarantee that the system state will reach the target point exactly at or before a specified time. To address this limitation and enhance the applicability of sliding mode control, a relatively new control technique known as fixed-time sliding mode control (FT-SMC). There exist some predefined-time control method, such as arbitrary convergence21 and variable exponent coefficient22,23 have been developed. For arbitrary convergence control, there exist result21, comments24,25 and authors’ reply in26, proposed sufficient condition for Lyapunov stability given for arbitrary chosen time stable system. For variable exponent coefficient (VEC)22,23 and comment27, it is hard to get the bounded of the convergence time under different initial conditions. FT-SMC imposes a predefined convergence time constraint, ensuring that the system reaches the desired target within a specified finite time28. This unique feature makes FT-SMC an ideal choice for real-time control applications, where precise and timely control responses are essential. For instance, the work in29 proposes finite-time sliding mode control with mismatched disturbances, but the convergence time bound depends on system parameters and initial conditions. Predefined-time30, prescribed-time31 and our VEC method allows the user to predefine the convergence time directly as a tunable parameter. Predefined-time VEC-based design provides a rigorous and straightforward Lyapunov-based stability proof with a clear and computable settling time upper bound.

Nonetheless, real-world systems often exhibit variable exponent coefficients, introducing additional complexities in the control process. Examples of such systems include variable coefficient circuits, adaptive control systems, and time-varying systems. These variable exponent coefficients challenge the application of traditional FT-SMC methods, necessitating innovative solutions to tackle this class of control problems. Therefore, the primary focus of this research is to explore and apply FT-SMC to nonlinear systems with variable exponent coefficients. The objective is to develop an effective control strategy that can handle the challenges posed by such systems and achieve robust and predefined-time convergence to the desired set-point.

This paper presents a comprehensive investigation into the theoretical foundation of PT-SMC, analyzing its advantages and applicability in addressing nonlinear control problems. Subsequently, a novel PT-SMC algorithm will be derived, tailored specifically for nonlinear systems with variable exponent coefficients. The design principles and control strategies of the proposed algorithm will be thoroughly examined. Furthermore, the stability of the derived control algorithm will be rigorously analyzed using mathematical tools to prove its capability to achieve predefined-time convergence, independent of system uncertainties and initial conditions. Numerical simulations will be conducted on various nonlinear systems with variable exponent coefficients to validate the performance and effectiveness of the proposed PT-SMC approach. Comparisons with traditional sliding mode control methods will be presented to highlight the advantages of the proposed technique.

The outcome of this research is expected to provide an efficient control solution for nonlinear systems with variable exponent coefficients, showcasing the potential of PT-SMC in real-time applications. The developed methodology may find application in diverse fields, including power electronics, robotics, and automation, where fast and accurate control responses are crucial for achieving optimal system performance. Ultimately, this study aims to contribute to the advancement of control theory and promote the adoption of predefined-time sliding mode control in a wide range of engineering and scientific domains.

In comparison to existing fixed-time and predefined-time control schemes18–20, the main novelties of this work are threefold: Firstly, the proposed Variable Exponent Coefficient (VEC) function provides a more flexible framework for tuning the predefined convergence time, overcoming the conservatism of fixed-exponent methods. Secondly, the integration of VEC with both standard sliding mode control and neural network-based adaptive control offers a unified approach to handle system uncertainties and nonlinearities with a guaranteed settling time. Thirdly, the application to the full six-degree-of-freedom quadrotor model, including both position and attitude tracking, demonstrates the practical viability of the method in complex, underactuated systems, which is not fully explored in the aforementioned works.

Conclusion

This paper has proposed a novel predefined-time sliding mode control strategy utilizing Variable Exponent Coefficients (VEC) for the trajectory tracking of quadrotor UAVs. The primary contributions of this work are: (1) The development of a VEC-based sliding manifold that ensures predefined-time convergence regardless of initial conditions; (2) The design of an adaptive neural network controller integrated with the VEC framework to compensate for complex uncertainties without compromising the predefined-time stability property; (3) The successful application and validation of the proposed control scheme on a high-fidelity, nonlinear quadrotor model, demonstrating superior performance in tracking complex 3D trajectories.

The application of FT-SMC to nonlinear systems with VEC proves to be an effective and robust control strategy. The developed algorithm with VEC demonstrates remarkable adaptability in handling predefined-time convergence, ensuring system stability, and providing reliable control performance. The stability analysis confirms its capability to achieve predefined-time convergence, independent of initial condition. Numerical simulations validate the superiority of FT-SMC over conventional sliding mode control, emphasizing its potential in time-critical applications. The fixed-time convergence property makes these control strategies suitable for time-critical applications, such as robotics, autonomous systems, and power electronics. This research advances control theory, offering a promising solution for real-world scenarios where precise and rapid control responses are essential for optimal performance.

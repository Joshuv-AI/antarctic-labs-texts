Existence of Positive Scalar Curvature and Positive Yamabe constant on Hypersurfaces of Noncompact Cylinders
Jie Xu

Abstract
. Let X X be an oriented, closed manifold with dim X ⩾ 2 \dim X\geqslant 2 . Let ( Z , ∂ Z ) (Z,\partial Z) be an oriented, compact manifold with (possibly empty) smooth boundary and dim Z ⩾ 2 \dim Z\geqslant 2 . In this article, we show that if the noncompact cylinder X × ℝ X\times\mathbb{R} admits a complete Riemannian metric g g with positive injectivity radius and uniformly positive scalar curvature, and that is of bounded geometry or bounded curvature, then X X admits a positive scalar curvature metric within the same conformal class provided that some g g -angle condition is satisfied. With the g g -angle condition, we can also show that if ( Z × ℝ , ∂ Z × ℝ ) (Z\times\mathbb{R},\partial Z\times\mathbb{R}) admits a complete metric g g that has positive Yamabe constant and positive injectivity radius, and is of bounded geometry or bounded curvature, then ( Z , ∂ Z ) (Z,\partial Z) has positive Yamabe constant for the conformal class [ ı ∗ ​ g ] [\imath^{*}g] with the natural inclusion ı : ( Z , ∂ Z ) ↪ ( Z × ℝ , ∂ Z × ℝ ) \imath:(Z,\partial Z)\hookrightarrow(Z\times\mathbb{R},\partial Z\times\mathbb{R}) .



1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a closed, oriented manifold with
\dim X\geqslant 2
. Let
M=X\times\mathbb{R}
be the noncompact cylinder. We assign the
\xi
-variable to the real line
\mathbb{R}
as global coordinate. In a 1994 survey article
[
18
]
, Rosenberg and Stolz conjectured the (non)existence of positive scalar curvature (PSC) metrics on the compact cylinder
X
:

Conjecture: If
X
does not admit any PSC metric, then
M=X\times\mathbb{R}
does not admit a complete PSC metric.

In this article, we first focus on the following question, which is the contrapositive statement of this Rosenberg-Stolz conjecture:

If
M=X\times\mathbb{R}
admits a complete PSC metric, does
X
admit a PSC metric?

The noncompact cylinder
M
has two ends, with one-point compactification
X\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
and two-point compactification
X\times[0,1]
. In our recent work
[
19
]
,
[
23
]
, we showed that for every
X
with
\dim X\geqslant 2
, the
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-stability conjecture holds by assuming some angle condition; we also showed that for every
X
with
\dim X\geqslant 2
, the existence of PSC metric with nonnegative mean curvature on
X\times[0,1]
implies the existence of PSC metric on
X
, provided that an analogous angle condition should hold.

Inspired by the results of one- and two-points compactification of
M
, it is natural to ask whether we can address the conjecture by imposing some version of angle condition also. Fix
0\in\mathbb{R}
and
X_{0}:=X\times\{0\}\cong X
. Let
\imath:X_{0}\rightarrow M
be the natural inclusion. For the Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
, we denote the scalar curvature on
M
by
R_{g}
, the second fundamental form and mean curvature on
X_{0}
by
A_{g}
and
h_{g}
, respectively. We say that
(M,g)
is of
bounded geometry
if
g
is a complete metric, having positive injectivity radius, and the curvature tensors and all their covariant derivatives are bounded. For the canonical tangent vector
\partial_{\xi}\in\Gamma(T\mathbb{R})
, we choose the unit normal vector field
\nu_{g}
along
X_{0}
such that

(1)

\angle_{g}(\nu_{g},\partial_{\xi}):=\cos^{-1}\left(\frac{g(\partial_{\xi},\nu_{g})}{g(\nu_{g},\nu_{g})^{\frac{1}{2}}g(\partial_{\xi},\partial_{\xi})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)=\cos^{-1}\left(\frac{g(\partial_{\xi},\nu_{g})}{g(\partial_{\xi},\partial_{\xi})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\in\left[0,\frac{\pi}{2}\right].

Our first main result states as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X
be an oriented, closed manifold. Let
(M=X\times\mathbb{R},g)
be a Riemannian noncompact cylinder of bounded geometry such that
n:=\dim M\geqslant 3
. If
R_{g}\geqslant\kappa_{0}>0
on
M
, and

\angle_{g}(\nu_{g},\partial_{\xi})\in[0,\frac{\pi}{4})

along the hypersurface
X_{0}
, then there exists a metric
\tilde{g}
in the conformal class of
g
such that
\imath^{*}\tilde{g}
is a PSC metric on
X\cong X_{0}
.

Remark 1.1
.

It is worth mentioning that if replace the bounded geometry condition by assuming
g
is of bounded curvature in the sense of Aubin
[
2
, Chapter 2]
,
g
has positive injectivity radius, and the sectional curvature of the complete metric
g
is uniformly bounded on
X\times\mathbb{R}
, then the same conclusion of Theorem
1.1
holds, provided that all other assumptions in Theorem
1.1
keep unchanged.

There is no difference if we replace the hypersurface
X_{0}
by any other hypersurfaces
X_{\xi_{0}}:=X\times\{\xi_{0}\},\forall\xi_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. Therefore the same conclusion above holds on every
X_{\xi}
if we replace the angle condition by
\angle_{g}(\nu_{g,\xi_{0}},\partial_{\xi})
where
\nu_{g,\xi_{0}}
is the unit normal vector field on
X_{\xi_{0}}
defined by (
1
). It follows that the conjecture of Rosenberg and Stolz can be partially answered in the following sense:

Corollary 1.1
.

Let
X
be a oriented closed manifold with
\dim X\geqslant 2
. If
X
admits no PSC metric, then for any Riemannian metric
g
on
X\times\mathbb{R}
, either the
g
-angle condition
\angle_{g}(\nu_{g,\xi_{0}},\partial_{\xi})<\frac{\pi}{4}
fails on all hypersurfaces
X_{\xi_{0}},\forall\xi_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, or

fails to have bounded curvature/bounded geometry, or

cannot be a complete metric with uniform positive scalar curvature.

Given any complete smooth manifold
(N,g_{1})
(possibly with boundary), the conformal class of

is denoted by

[g_{1}]=\left\{e^{2f}g_{1}:f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(N)\right\}.

Since our construction of the new PSC metric on
X
is within the conformal class of the original metric, we show the transpose of the positivity of the Yamabe constants from
X\times\mathbb{R}
to
X
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
X
be an oriented, closed manifold. Let
(M=X\times\mathbb{R},g)
be a Riemannian noncompact cylinder of bounded geometry or bounded curvature such that
n:=\dim M\geqslant 3

on
M

\angle_{g}(\nu_{g},\partial_{\xi})\in[0,\frac{\pi}{4})

along the hypersurface
X_{0}
, then the Yamabe constant of the conformal class
[\imath^{*}g]
on
X
is also positive.

The definition of the Yamabe constant on noncompact complete manifolds is given in §2. Finally we can show that the uniform PSC condition can be dropped.

\nu_{g}=a\partial_{\xi}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}b^{i}\partial_{i}.

a

\nu_{g}
and
\partial_{\xi}
are nowhere tangent to

. We extend

to

via

as a global vector field on

Lemma 2.1
.

If

(3)

\frac{g(\partial_{\xi},\partial_{\xi})}{g(\nu_{g},\partial_{\xi})^{2}}<2\;{\rm on}\;X_{0},

then
the operator

L^{\prime}:=\nabla_{V}\nabla_{V}-\Delta_{\sigma^{*}\bar{g}}

is elliptic on

.

Proof.

In any local coordinates,

\nabla_{V}\nabla_{V}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n-1}b^{i}(x)b^{j}(x)\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{i}\partial x^{j}}+G_{1}(x_{0})

where
G_{1}(x)
is a linear first order operator. In Riemannian normal coordinates
(x^{1},\ldots,x^{n-1},x^{n+1}=t)
centered at a fixed

, we have

\Delta_{\sigma^{*}\bar{g}}|_{x_{0}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\left(x^{i}\right)^{2}}+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}.

The principal symbol of
L^{\prime}
at

is

\sigma_{2}(L^{\prime})(x_{0},\eta)=-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n-1}b^{i}(x_{0})b^{j}(x_{0})\eta^{i}\eta^{j}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\left(\eta^{i}\right)^{2}+(\eta^{n+1})^{2}.

Proving

is equivalent to proving that

B:=-\begin{pmatrix}(b^{1})^{2}&b^{1}b^{2}&\dotso&b^{1}b^{n-1}\\
b^{2}b^{1}&(b^{2})^{2}&\dotso&b^{2}b^{n-1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
b^{n-1}b^{1}&b^{n-1}b^{2}&\dotso&(b^{n-1})^{2}\end{pmatrix}+I_{n-1}:=B^{\prime}+I_{n-1}

is positive definite. The same argument of Sylvester criterion in

then follows.

Lemma 2.3
.

If
(N,\hat{g})
is a noncompact Riemannian manifold of bounded geometry with

Proof.

By
R_{\hat{g}}\geqslant\kappa_{0}>0
, it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{4(n-1)}{n-2}\int_{N}\lvert\nabla_{\hat{g}}v\rvert^{2}d\text{vol}_{\hat{g}}+\int_{N}R_{\hat{g}}v^{2}d\text{vol}_{\hat{g}}}{\int_{N}v^{2}d\text{vol}_{\hat{g}}}

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{\int_{N}R_{\hat{g}}v^{2}\text{dvol}_{g}}{\int_{N}v^{2}d\text{vol}_{\hat{g}}}\geqslant\kappa_{0}>0,\forall v\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(N)\backslash\{0\}.

It follows that
\mu(N,\hat{g})>0
. By
[
13
, Theorem 3.18, Corollary 3.19]
, the Sobolev embedding

H^{1}(M,g)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{L}^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}(M,g)

is continuous, i.e. there exists a constant
C=C(M,g)
such that

\lVert v\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{2n}{n-2}(M,g)}\leqslant C\lVert v\rVert_{H^{1}(M,g)},\forall v\in H^{1}(M,g).

Clearly
Y(N,\hat{g})\geqslant 0
by (
7
) and the positivity of
R_{\hat{g}}
. In contrast, assume that
Y(N,\hat{g})=0
. It follows that there exists a sequence
\{v_{i}\}
such that
\lVert v_{i}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}(N,\hat{g})}=1,\forall i
and

\frac{4(n-1)}{n-2}\int_{N}\lvert\nabla_{\hat{g}}v_{i}\rvert^{2}d\text{vol}_{\hat{g}}+\int_{N}R_{\hat{g}}v_{i}^{2}d\text{vol}_{\hat{g}}\xrightarrow{i\rightarrow\infty}0.

Since
\mu(N,\hat{g})\geqslant\kappa_{0}>0
, it follows that

\lVert v_{i}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(N,\hat{g})}\xrightarrow{i\rightarrow\infty}0\Rightarrow\lVert\nabla_{\hat{g}}v_{i}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(N,\hat{g})}\xrightarrow{i\rightarrow\infty}0.

But by continuity of the Sobolev embedding, we observe that for large enough

1=\lVert v_{i}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}(N,\hat{g})}\leqslant C\lVert v_{i}\rVert_{H^{1}(N,\hat{g})}\ll 1

which reaches a contradiction. Therefore we must have
Y(N,\hat{g})>0
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(X,\partial X)
be an oriented, compact manifold with non-empty smooth boundary. Let
(M,\partial M,g)=(X\times\mathbb{R},\partial X\times\mathbb{R},g)
be a Riemannian noncompact cylinder of bounded geometry such that
n:=\dim M\geqslant 3
. If
(M,\partial M,g)
has positive Yamabe constant in the sense of (
30
), and

\angle_{g}(\nu_{g},\partial_{\xi})\in[0,\frac{\pi}{4}),

along the hypersurface
(X\times\{0\},\partial X\times\{0\}):=X_{0}
, then there exists a metric
\tilde{g}
in the conformal class of
g
such that
\imath^{*}\tilde{g}
is a PSC metric with nonnegative mean curvature on
(X,\partial X)\cong X_{0}
. Equivalently, the Yamabe constant of the conformal class
[\imath^{*}g]
on
(X,\partial X)
is positive.

Proof.

As in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we consider the conformal transformation

g\mapsto\left(\tilde{u}^{\prime}\right)^{\frac{4}{n-2}}g:=\tilde{g}\Rightarrow\imath^{*}g\mapsto u_{0}^{\frac{4}{n-2}}\imath^{*}g:=\imath^{*}\tilde{g}

on
(X,\partial X)
. Let’s check the mean curvature of the new metric
\imath^{*}\tilde{g}
first. By (
28
),

\frac{\partial u_{0}}{\partial\nu}=\frac{\partial(u+1)}{\partial\nu}=0\;{\rm on}\;\partial X.

Since
h_{\imath^{*}g}=0
, it follows that

h_{\imath^{*}\tilde{g}}=u_{0}^{-\frac{2}{n-2}}h_{\imath^{*}g}-\frac{\partial\left(u_{0}^{-\frac{2}{n-2}}\right)}{\partial\nu}=0\;{\rm on}\;\partial X.

For the scalar curvature
R_{\imath^{*}\tilde{g}}
, we follow exactly the same procedures in the proof of Theorem
3.1
. We set
p,\alpha
as in Proposition
4.1
. We adjust the choice
C
by

(38)

C>2\max_{X\times\{0\}_{\xi}\times\{P\}_{t}}\left(\lvert R_{g}\rvert+R+2\lvert{\rm Ric}_{g}(\nu_{g},\nu_{g})\rvert+h_{g}^{2}+\lvert A_{g}\rvert^{2}\right)+3.

We then construct
F
such that (
23
) holds. All following arguments are the same, up to (
25
). The differential relation that
u_{0}
satisfies is:

4\nabla_{V}\nabla_{V}u_{0}-4\Delta_{\sigma^{*}\bar{g}}u_{0}+Ru_{0}=F+R\;{\rm on}\;X.

We revise (
25
) by

\displaystyle R_{\imath^{*}\tilde{g}}

\displaystyle\geqslant u_{0}^{-\frac{n+2}{n-2}}\left(-2{\rm Ric}_{\tau_{1}^{*}\bar{g}}\left(\nu_{g},\nu_{g}\right)u_{0}+h_{\tau_{1}^{*}\bar{g}}^{2}u_{0}-\lvert A_{\tau_{1}^{*}\bar{g}}\rvert^{2}u_{0}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+u_{0}^{-\frac{n+2}{n-2}}\left(4\nabla_{V}\nabla_{V}u_{0}-4\Delta_{\sigma^{*}\bar{g}}u_{0}+R_{\bar{g}}|_{W}u_{0}\right)+u_{0}^{-\frac{n+2}{n-2}}\cdot(-1-4\eta^{\prime}+A_{1}(\bar{g},M,u))

\displaystyle=u_{0}^{-\frac{n+2}{n-2}}\left(-2{\rm Ric}_{\tau_{1}^{*}\bar{g}}\left(\nu_{g},\nu_{g}\right)u_{0}+h_{\tau_{1}^{*}\bar{g}}^{2}u_{0}-\lvert A_{\tau_{1}^{*}\bar{g}}\rvert^{2}u_{0}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+u_{0}^{-\frac{n+2}{n-2}}\left(4\nabla_{V}\nabla_{V}u_{0}-4\Delta_{\sigma^{*}\bar{g}}u_{0}+Ru_{0}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+u_{0}^{-\frac{n+2}{n-2}}\cdot(-Ru_{0}+R_{\bar{g}}|_{W}u_{0}-1-4\eta^{\prime}+A_{1}(\bar{g},M,u))

\displaystyle=u_{0}^{-\frac{n+2}{n-2}}\left(-2{\rm Ric}_{\tau_{1}^{*}\bar{g}}\left(\nu_{g},\nu_{g}\right)u_{0}+h_{\tau_{1}^{*}\bar{g}}^{2}u_{0}-\lvert A_{\tau_{1}^{*}\bar{g}}\rvert^{2}u_{0}-Ru_{0}+R_{\bar{g}}|_{W}u_{0}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad u_{0}^{-\frac{n+2}{n-2}}\left(F+R-1-4\eta^{\prime}+A_{1}(\bar{g},M,u)\right).

It follows that

R_{\imath^{*}\tilde{g}}\geqslant u_{0}^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}\left(C+1+R-2\max_{X\times\{0\}_{\xi}\times\{P\}_{t}}\left(\lvert R_{g}\rvert+R+2\lvert{\rm Ric}_{g}(\nu_{g},\nu_{g})\rvert+h_{g}^{2}+\lvert A_{g}\rvert^{2}\right)-3\right)>0

on
X
. Due to Escobar
[
7
]
,
R_{\imath^{*}\tilde{g}}>0
and
h_{\imath^{*}\tilde{g}}=0
implies that the Yamabe constant with respect to the conformal class
[\imath^{*}\tilde{g}]
is positive. Clearly
[\imath^{*}\tilde{g}]=[\imath^{*}g]
since we take the conformal transformation.
∎

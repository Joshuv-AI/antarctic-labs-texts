On the Stein-Weiss inequalities and higher-order Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg type inequalities: sharp constants, symmetry of extremal functions Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. 35J30, 49K30, 26D10 Thanks: Key words and phrases. Stein-Weiss inequality; Second-order Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequality; Rellich-Sobolev inequality; Weighted fourth-order equation; Symmetry of extremal functions
Shengbing Deng ∗, Xingliang Tian

Abstract
. In this paper, we first classify all radially symmetry solutions of the following weighted fourth-order equation Δ ( | x | − γ Δ u ) = | x | γ u N + 4 + 3 ​ γ N − 4 − γ , u ≥ 0 in ℝ N , \Delta(|x|^{-\gamma}\Delta u)=|x|^{\gamma}u^{\frac{N+4+3\gamma}{N-4-\gamma}},\quad u\geq 0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}, where N ≥ 5 N\geq 5 , − 2 < γ < 0 -2<\gamma<0 . Then we derive the sharp Stein-Weiss inequality and standard second-order Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequality with radially symmetry extremal functions. Moreover, by using standard spherical decomposition, we derive a sharp weighted Rellich-Sobolev inequality. Furthermore, we establish the sharp second-order Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg type inequalities with two variables which have radially symmetry extremal functions. Finally, we derive the weak form Hardy-Rellich inequalities with sharp constants.



1.
Introduction and main results

1.1.
Stein-Weiss inequality

Let us recall the well-known Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality
[
27
,
38
]
, see also the work of Lieb
[
31
]
in details in which the author classified extremal functions by using stereographic projection.

Proposition 1.1
.

(
Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality
). Let
r,t>1
and
0<\lambda<N
with
\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{t}+\frac{\lambda}{N}=2
,
f\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
g\in L^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. There exists a sharp constant
C(N,r,t,\lambda)>0
, independent of
f
and
g
, such that

(1.1)

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{f(x)g(y)}{|x-y|^{\lambda}}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq C(N,t,\lambda)\|f\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|g\|_{L^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

If
t=r=\frac{2N}{2N-\lambda}
, then

\displaystyle C(N,t,\lambda)=C_{1}(N,\lambda)=\pi^{\frac{\lambda}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2}-\frac{\lambda}{2})}{\Gamma(N-\frac{\lambda}{2})}\left\{\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})}{\Gamma(N)}\right\}^{-1+\frac{\lambda}{N}},

and there is equality in (
1.1
) if and only if
f\equiv(const.)g
and

\displaystyle f(x)=A(c+|x-z|^{2})^{-\frac{2N-\lambda}{2}}

for
A\in\mathbb{R}
,
c>0
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Furthermore, when
t=2
and
r=\frac{2N}{3N-2\lambda}
, which requires
N<2\lambda<2N
, then

\displaystyle C(N,t,\lambda)=C_{2}(N,\lambda)=\pi^{\frac{\lambda}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2}-\frac{\lambda}{2})}{\Gamma(N-\frac{\lambda}{2})}\left\{\frac{\Gamma(\lambda-\frac{N}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{3N}{2}-\lambda)}\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\{\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})}{\Gamma(N)}\right\}^{-1+\frac{\lambda}{N}},

and there is equality in (
1.1
) if and only if

\displaystyle f(x)=A(c+|x-z|^{2})^{-\frac{3N-2\lambda}{2}},\quad g(x)=B\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{(c+|y-z|^{2})^{-\frac{3N-2\lambda}{2}}}{|x-y|^{\lambda}}\mathrm{d}y

for
A,B\in\mathbb{R}
,
c>0
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Hardy and Littlewood also introduce the double weighted inequaliy in
(0,\infty)
, which was later generalized in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
by Stein and Weiss in
[
39
]
. It reads:

Proposition 1.2
.

(
Stein-Weiss inequality
). Let
r,t>1
and
0<\lambda<N
with
\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{t}+\frac{\lambda+a+b}{N}=2
,
f\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
g\in L^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. There exists a constant
C(N,t,\lambda,a,b)>0
, independent of
f
and
g
, such that

(1.2)

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{f(x)g(y)}{|x|^{a}|x-y|^{\lambda}|y|^{b}}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq C(N,t,\lambda,a,b)\|f\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|g\|_{L^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},

under the conditions

a+b\geq 0,\quad 1-\frac{1}{r}-\frac{\lambda}{N}<\frac{a}{N}<1-\frac{1}{r}.

Lieb
[
31
]
established the existence of extremal functions for (
1.2
) with sharp constant when
r<\frac{t}{t-1}
and
a,b\geq 0
, and the author claimed that no nontrivial extremal functions can exist when
r=\frac{t}{t-1}
.
Furthermore, Beckner
[
1
]
established a kind of sharp Stein-Weiss inequality which states that, for
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
0<\lambda<N
,

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{f(x)f(y)}{|x|^{\frac{N-\lambda}{2}}|x-y|^{\lambda}|y|^{\frac{N-\lambda}{2}}}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N-\lambda}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{\lambda}{2})}\left\{\frac{\Gamma(\frac{\lambda}{4})}{\Gamma(\frac{2N-\lambda}{4})}\right\}^{2}\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},

with no nontrivial extremal functions, in which the author established the equivalence relationship with Pitt’s inequality, that is, for
0<\alpha<N
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|f|^{2}}{|x|^{\alpha}}\mathrm{d}x\leq 2^{-\alpha}\left\{\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N-\alpha}{4})}{\Gamma(\frac{N+\alpha}{4})}\right\}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{\alpha/4}f|^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

Therefore, we want to establish some other sharp Stein-Weiss inequalities as in Proposition
1.1
. From the work of Lieb
[
31
]
(see also Chen and Li
[
9
]
), we notice that the corresponding Euler-Lagrange equations of Stein-Weiss inequality (
1.2
) are the integral system:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mu_{1}rf(x)^{r-1}=\frac{1}{|x|^{a}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{g(y)}{|y|^{b}|x-y|^{\lambda}}\mathrm{d}y\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},\\[5.69054pt]
\mu_{2}tg(x)^{t-1}=\frac{1}{|x|^{b}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{f(y)}{|y|^{a}|x-y|^{\lambda}}\mathrm{d}y\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},\\[5.69054pt]
f,g\geq 0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},\end{array}\right.

where
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}>0
are positive parameters. Let
w=c_{1}f^{r-1}
,
z=c_{2}g^{t-1}
,
q=\frac{1}{r-1}
,
p=\frac{1}{t-1}
,
pq\neq 1
, and for a proper choice of positive constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
, then system (
1.1
) becomes

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}w(x)=\frac{1}{|x|^{a}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{z(y)^{p}}{|y|^{b}|x-y|^{\lambda}}\mathrm{d}y\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},\\[5.69054pt]
z(x)=\frac{1}{|x|^{b}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{w(y)^{q}}{|y|^{a}|x-y|^{\lambda}}\mathrm{d}y\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},\\[5.69054pt]
w,z\geq 0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}.\end{array}\right.

Similar as the work of Chen, Li and Ou
[
10
, Theorem 4.1]
, we know integral system (
1.1
) is equivalent to (multiplies with suitable constants)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(-\Delta)^{\frac{N-\lambda}{2}}(|x|^{a}w)=\frac{w^{p}}{|x|^{b}}\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},\\[5.69054pt]
(-\Delta)^{\frac{N-\lambda}{2}}(|x|^{b}z)=\frac{z^{q}}{|x|^{a}}\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},\\[5.69054pt]
w,z\geq 0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}.\end{array}\right.

Let
u(x)=|x|^{a}w(x)
,
v(x)=|x|^{b}z(x)
, then (
1.1
) becomes

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(-\Delta)^{\frac{N-\lambda}{2}}u=\frac{v^{p}}{|x|^{b(p+1)}}\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},\\[5.69054pt]
(-\Delta)^{\frac{N-\lambda}{2}}v=\frac{u^{q}}{|x|^{a(q+1)}}\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},\\[5.69054pt]
u,v\geq 0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}.\end{array}\right.

By using the method of moving planes, Jin and Li
[
29
]
obtain the symmetry result which states that, for
a,b\geq 0
, then positive solution pair
(u,v)
of (
1.1
) are radially symmetric and decreasing about some point.
Furthermore, the work of Chen and Li
[
9
, Theorem 2]
indicates that if
\lambda=N-2
,
a=b=\frac{N}{p+1}-\frac{N-2}{2}
and
p=q
with
b(p+1)<2
, then
u(x)\equiv v(x)
and they must both assume the form

\phi_{c}(x)=d\left(c+|x|^{\frac{(N-2)(p-1)}{2}}\right)^{-\frac{2}{p-1}}

with some real number
c
and some constant
d
. In fact, this result gives the extremal functions and also the sharp constant of Stein-Weiss inequality (
1.2
) in this case.
Now, let us consider another case:

(1.19)

\displaystyle\lambda=N-2,\quad p=1,\quad b(p+1)=a(q+1)<2,

then
a=\frac{b(N-4+2b)}{N-2b}
and
0\leq b<1
. When
b=0
, Lin
[
33
]
has classified all solutions of (
1.1
). We only consider

. Obviously, for the case (
1.19
) we know (
1.1

(1.20)

\Delta(|x|^{-\gamma}\Delta u)=|x|^{\gamma}u^{q},\quad u\geq 0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},

where
\gamma=-2b\in(-2,0)
, and
q=\frac{N+4+3\gamma}{N-4-\gamma}
. We will classify all solutions of (

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume that
N\geq 5
and
-2<\gamma<0
. Let
u
be a smooth solution of equation (
1.20
), then
u
is radially symmetric and has the following form:

(1.21)

\displaystyle u(x)=U_{\lambda}(x)=\lambda^{\frac{N-4-\gamma}{2}}U(\lambda x)\quad\mbox{for}\quad\lambda\geq 0,\quad\mbox{with}\quad U(x)=\frac{C_{N,\gamma}}{(1+|x|^{2+\gamma})^{\frac{N-4-\gamma}{2+\gamma}}}.

Here
C_{N,\gamma}=\left[(N-4-\gamma)(N-2)(N+\gamma)(N+2+2\gamma)\right]^{\frac{N-4-\gamma}{4(2+\gamma)}}
.

Therefore, by the results of Chen and Li
[
9
, Theorem 2]
and Theorem
1.3
, we can classify the extremal functions of Stein-Weiss inequality (
1.2
) when
t=r
and
t=2
separately, which extends the work of Lieb
[
31
]
.

Theorem 1.4
.

(
Sharp Stein-Weiss inequality
). Assume the conditions in Proposition
1.2
hold with

If
N\geq 3
,

and

) with sharp constant if and only if
f\equiv(const.)g
and

\displaystyle f(x)=A|x|^{-\frac{b(N+2(1-b))}{N-2(1-b)}}\left(c+|x|^{\frac{2(N-2)(1-b)}{N-2(1-b)}}\right)^{-\frac{N+2(1-b)}{2(1-b)}},

for
A\in\mathbb{R}
,
c>0
.
Moreover, if
N\geq 5
,
t=2
and

, with
a=\frac{b(N-4+2b)}{N-2b}
and

1.2
) with sharp constant if and only if

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=A|x|^{-\frac{b(N+4-2b)}{N-2b}}(c+|x|^{2-2b})^{-\frac{N+4-2b}{2-2b}},

\displaystyle g(x)

\displaystyle=B\left[(N-2b)c+(2-2b)|x|^{2-2b}\right]|x|^{-b}(c+|x|^{2-2b})^{-\frac{N-2b}{2-2b}},

for
A,B\in\mathbb{R}
,
c>0
.

Furthermore, as a direct conclusion of Theorem
1.3
, we obtain

Theorem 1.5
.

Assume that
N\geq 5
and
-2<\gamma<0
. For all

u\in\mathcal{D}^{2,2}_{0,\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\left\{u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\Delta u|^{2}}{|x|^{\gamma}}\mathrm{d}x<\infty,\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{\gamma}|u|^{2^{**}_{0,\gamma}}\mathrm{d}x<\infty\right\},

it holds that

(1.22)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\Delta u|^{2}}{|x|^{\gamma}}\mathrm{d}x\geq\mathcal{S}_{N,\gamma}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{\gamma}|u|^{2^{**}_{0,\gamma}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{**}_{0,\gamma}}},

where

, and

(1.23)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{N,\gamma}=\left(\frac{2}{2+\gamma}\right)^{\frac{2(2+\gamma)}{N+\gamma}-4}\left(\frac{2\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}}{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})}\right)^{\frac{2(2+\gamma)}{N+\gamma}}\mathcal{B}\left(\frac{2(N+\gamma)}{2+\gamma}\right),

where
\mathcal{B}(M)=(M-4)(M-2)M(M+2)\left[\Gamma^{2}(\frac{M}{2})/(2\Gamma(M))\right]^{\frac{4}{M}}
and

Furthermore, the constant
\mathcal{S}_{N,\gamma}
is sharp and equality holds if and only if
u(x)=AU_{\lambda}(x)

for

and
\lambda>0
, where
U_{\lambda}

1.2.
Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequality

We notice that inequality (
1.22
) is a standard second-order Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg (we write CKN for short) inequality. Let us recall the famous CKN inequality which was firstly introduced in 1984 by Caffarelli, Kohn and Nirenberg in their celebrated work
[
3
]
of first-order, then it was extended to higher-order by Lin
[
32
]
. Here, we only mention the case without interpolation term.

Proposition 1.6
.

(
CKN inequality
) There exists a constant
C>0
such that

(1.24)

\||x|^{-a}D^{m}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\geq C\||x|^{-b}D^{j}u\|_{L^{\tau}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},\quad\mbox{for all}\quad u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

where
j\geq 0
,
m>0
are integers, and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}-\frac{a}{N}>0,\quad\frac{1}{\tau}-\frac{b}{N}>0,\quad a\leq b\leq a+m-j,\quad\frac{1}{\tau}-\frac{b+j}{N}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{a+m}{N}.

Here

|D^{m}u|:=\left(\sum_{|\mathbf{m}|=m}\left|\frac{\partial^{|\mathbf{m}|}u}{\partial x_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots\partial x_{N}^{m_{N}}}\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2},\quad\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{N})\in\mathbb{N}^{N},\quad\mathbb{N}:=\{0,1,2,\ldots\},

thus,

(1.25)

\displaystyle\||x|^{-a}D^{m}u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-ap}\left(\sum_{|\mathbf{m}|=m}\left|\frac{\partial^{|\mathbf{m}|}u}{\partial x_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots\partial x_{N}^{m_{N}}}\right|^{2}\right)^{p/2}\mathrm{d}x.

In particular, when
j=0
,
m=1
and
p=2
, it holds

(1.26)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-2a}|Du|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\geq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-b\tau}|u|^{\tau}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{\tau}},\quad\forall u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

where
-\infty<a<a_{c}:=\frac{N-2}{2}
,
a\leq b\leq a+1
,
\tau=\frac{2N}{N-2(1+a-b)}
. Furthermore, when
j=0
,
m=2
and
p=2
, it holds

(1.27)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-2a}|D^{2}u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\geq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-b\tau}|u|^{\tau}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{\tau}},\quad\forall u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

where
-\infty<a<\frac{N-4}{2}
,
a\leq b\leq a+2
,
\tau=\frac{2N}{N-2(2+a-b)}
.

Since
|Du|^{2}=|\nabla u|^{2}
, then we rewrite (
1.26
) as the following classical first-order CKN inequality

(1.28)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-2a}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\geq C_{\mathrm{CKN1}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-b\tau}|u|^{\tau}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{\tau}},\quad\forall u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

which was well studied in last thirty years. When
N\geq 3

(1.29)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-2a}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\geq\rho^{-1-\frac{2}{\tau}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla v|^{2}\mathrm{d}x,

Theorem 1.7

\displaystyle=\frac{N^{2}-4N+8+\gamma^{2}+4\gamma}{2(N-4-\gamma)^{2}}(\mu-\gamma)[2(N-4-\gamma)-(\mu-\gamma)];

\displaystyle C_{\gamma,\mu,2}

\displaystyle=\frac{N^{2}-4N+8+\gamma^{2}+4\gamma}{8(N-4-\gamma)^{2}}(\mu-\gamma)^{2}[2(N-4-\gamma)-(\mu-\gamma)]^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{(\mu-\gamma)^{2}[2(N-2)-(\mu-\gamma)]^{2}}{16}

(1.37)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{(\mu-\gamma)[2(N-2)-(\mu-\gamma)](N-4-\mu)(2+\gamma)}{4}.

Moreover,

1.3.
Hardy-Rellich inequality

Finally, let us recall the following inequality:

(1.42)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-2a}|\Delta u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\geq C_{*}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-2a-2}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x,\quad\forall u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

for some
C_{*}\geq 0
, where
-\infty<a<\frac{N-2}{2}
. If
a
satisfies (
1.31
), then (
1.42
) is equivalent to the second-order CKN inequality (
1.24
) and
C_{*}>0
. (
1.42
) is usually named as the weighted Hardy-Rellich inequality, thanks to Tertikas and Zographopoulos
[
42
]
studied this problem and when
N\geq 5
and
0\leq a<\frac{N-4}{2}
, the authors obtained the sharp constant with the explicit form

\displaystyle C_{*}=\inf_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\left(\frac{(N-4-2a)(N+2a)}{4}+k(N-2+k)\right)^{2}}{(\frac{N-4-2a}{2})^{2}+k(N-2+k)}.

Furthermore, Ghoussoub and Moradifam in
[
25
, Theorem 6.1]
studied the general case
N\geq 1
and
a\leq\frac{N-2}{2}
under different conditions for
a
, see also
[
12
, Corollary 5.9]
for
-\frac{N}{2}<a\leq\frac{N-4}{2}
. It is obvious that when
N\geq 5
and
a=0
, Tertikas and Zographopoulos
[
42
]
obtained the sharp constant
C_{*}=\frac{N^{2}}{4}
, while in the cases
N=3
or
4
the sharp constant does not satisfies the general formula. In fact, Cazacu
[
8
]
proved the sharp constant
C_{*}
is strictly less than
\frac{N^{2}}{4}
when
N=3
or
4
, that is,
C_{*}=3
when
N=4
and
C_{*}=\frac{25}{36}
when
N=3
. In present paper, we are concerned about a weak form of (
1.42
) which reads that:

Theorem 1.13
.

Let
N\geq 1
, then for each
-\infty<a<\frac{N-2}{2}
, it holds that

(1.43)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-2a}|\Delta u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\geq C(N,a)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-2a-4}(x\cdot\nabla u)^{2}\mathrm{d}x,\quad\forall u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

with sharp constant

\displaystyle C(N,a)=\left(\frac{N+2a}{2}\right)^{2}

if
\frac{-2-\sqrt{N^{2}-2N+2}}{2}\leq a\leq\frac{-2+\sqrt{N^{2}-2N+2}}{2}
for
N\geq 2
, or
\frac{-2-\sqrt{5}}{2}\leq a<-\frac{1}{2}
for
N=1
, and

\displaystyle C(N,a)=\left(\frac{2}{N-4-2a}\right)^{2}\inf\limits_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left(k+\frac{N}{2}+a\right)^{2}\left(k+\frac{N-4}{2}-a\right)^{2}

if
a<\frac{-2-\sqrt{N^{2}-2N+2}}{2}
or
\frac{-2+\sqrt{N^{2}-2N+2}}{2}<a<\frac{N-2}{2}
for
N\geq 2
, or
a<\frac{-2-\sqrt{5}}{2}
for
N=1
.
In particular, for each
N\geq 3
,

(1.44)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\geq\frac{N^{2}}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-4}(x\cdot\nabla u)^{2}\mathrm{d}x,\quad\forall u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

with sharp constant
\frac{N^{2}}{4}
.

It is not difficult to verify (see (
6.25
) in Remark
6.1
directly) that our result (
1.43
) implies the weighted Rellich inequality (
1.33
) of the work of Caldiroli and Musina
[

]
, by using the Hardy type inequality:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-2a-4}(x\cdot\nabla u)^{2}\mathrm{d}x\geq\left(\frac{N-4-2a}{2}\right)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-2a-4}|u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x,\quad\forall u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Moreover, when
a=\frac{N-4}{2}
, Ghoussoub and Moradifam in
[
25
, Theorem 6.1]
proved the sharp constant in (
1.42
) is
C_{*}=\min\{(N-2)^{2},N-1\}
, then our conclusion about (
1.43
) indicates
C\left(N,\frac{N-4}{2}\right)=(N-2)^{2}>C_{*}
if

or
N\geq 4
, while

if
N=2
or
3
.
Furthermore, our weak form Hardy-Rellich inequality (
1.44
) admits consistent formula for sharp constant when
N=3
or
4

2.
Symmetry of solutions

In this section, we will classify all solutions of equation (
1.20
). Let us recall a uniqueness result established by Chen and Li
[
9
]
for the following weighted system:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u=\frac{u^{p}}{|x|^{b(p+1)}}\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},\\[5.69054pt]
-\Delta v=\frac{v^{q}}{|x|^{a(q+1)}}\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},\\[5.69054pt]
u,v\geq 0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},\end{array}\right.

where
0<p,q<\infty
,
a,b\geq 0
,
\frac{a}{N}<\frac{1}{q+1}<\frac{N-2+a}{N}
,
\frac{1}{p+1}+\frac{1}{q+1}=\frac{N-2+a+b}{N}
, and

\displaystyle b(p+1)<2,\quad a(q+1)<2,

which states that the solutions of the system (
2
) are unique in the sense that if
(u_{1},v_{1})
and
(u_{2},v_{2})
are any two pairs of solutions with
u_{1}(0)=u_{2}(0)
, then
v_{1}(0)=v_{2}(0)
and hence
(u_{1}(x),v_{1}(x))\equiv(u_{2}(x),v_{2}(x))
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.
Note that equation (
1.20
) is equivalent to the following Hardy-Lane-Emden system

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u=|x|^{\gamma}v\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},\\[5.69054pt]
-\Delta v=|x|^{\gamma}u^{q}\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},\\[5.69054pt]
u\geq 0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}.\end{array}\right.

By using the maximum principle, from
-\Delta v=|x|^{\gamma}u^{q}
and
u\geq 0
we know
v
is also nonnegative. Then let us consider system (
2
) with

p=1,\quad b(p+1)=a(q+1),

then
a=\frac{b(N-4+2b)}{N-2b}
and
0\leq b<1
. Let
b=-\frac{\gamma}{2}\in(0,1)
, then we obtain the solutions of the system (
2
) are also unique, therefore equation (
1.20
) admits a unique solution in the sense of Chen and Li
[

]
.

Now, let us show the solution of equation (
1.20
) must be the form
U_{\lambda}
as in (
1.21
). On the one hand, it is obvious that
U_{\lambda}
is a solution of (
1.20
) for each

, see our recent work
[

]
. On the other hand, assume that

is a solution of (
1.20
), then we can always choose nonnegative

such that

thus by the uniqueness result of Chen and Li

]
, we must have

. This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.5
. From
[
37
, Theorem A.2 (i)]
(see also
[
5
, Theorem 1.2]
), we know equality (
1.22
) with sharp constant holds for some nontrivial functions. It is well known that extremal functions of (
1.22
) are always solutions (multiplies some suitable constant) of the following related Euler-Lagrange equation:

(2.9)

\Delta(|x|^{-\gamma}\Delta u)=|x|^{\gamma}|u|^{2^{**}_{0,\gamma}-2}u,\quad u\in\mathcal{D}^{2,2}_{0,\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Furthermore, nontrivial extremal functions of (
1.22
) are always least energy solutions (multiplies some suitable constant) of equation (
2.9
).
In fact, the energy functional of (
2.9
) is defined as

(2.10)

\displaystyle\Phi(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\Delta u|^{2}}{|x|^{\gamma}}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{2^{**}_{0,\gamma}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{\gamma}|u|^{2^{**}_{0,\gamma}}\mathrm{d}x,\quad u\in\mathcal{D}^{2,2}_{0,\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Define the Nehari manifold

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}:=\left\{u\in\mathcal{D}^{2,2}_{0,\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\backslash\{0\}:\langle\Phi^{\prime}(u),u\rangle=0\right\}.

We know that the least energy of (
2.9
) is defined as
\inf\{\Phi(u):\ u\in\mathcal{D}^{2,2}_{0,\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\backslash\{0\},\ \Phi^{\prime}(u)=0\}
which is large than
\inf\limits_{u\in\mathcal{N}}\Phi(u)
.
For
v\in\mathcal{N}
we have

\displaystyle\Phi(v)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2^{**}_{0,\gamma}}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\Delta v|^{2}}{|x|^{\gamma}}\mathrm{d}x=\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2^{**}_{0,\gamma}}\right)\left(\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\Delta v|^{2}}{|x|^{\gamma}}\mathrm{d}x}{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{\gamma}|v|^{2^{**}_{0,\gamma}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{**}_{0,\gamma}}}}\right)^{\frac{2^{**}_{0,\gamma}}{2^{**}_{0,\gamma}-2}}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2^{**}_{0,\gamma}}\right)\mathcal{S}_{N,\gamma}^{\frac{2^{**}_{0,\gamma}}{2^{**}_{0,\gamma}-2}}=\Phi(cu),

for any minimizer
u
of (
1.22
) with some suitable constant
c
such that
cu
satisfies (
2.9
), therefore
cu
is also the least energy solution.

Next, we show that the least energy solutions of (
2.9
) can not change sign, which implies the extremal functions of (
1.22
) also can not change sign. If not, we suppose that
w
is a least energy solution of (
2.9
) which is changing sign. Define

w^{+}(x)=\max\{w(x),0\},\quad w^{-}(x)=\min\{w(x),0\}.

Note that
w^{+}(x)
and
w^{-}(x)
are also nontrivial solutions of (
2.9
). Therefore,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2^{**}_{0,\gamma}}\right)\mathcal{S}_{N,\gamma}^{\frac{2^{**}_{0,\gamma}}{2^{**}_{0,\gamma}-2}}

\displaystyle=\Phi(w)=\Phi(w^{+})+\Phi(w^{-})\geq 2\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2^{**}_{0,\gamma}}\right)\mathcal{S}_{N,\gamma}^{\frac{2^{**}_{0,\gamma}}{2^{**}_{0,\gamma}-2}},

which leads to a contradiction.

Then from the uniqueness result of Theorem
1.3
, we get the extremal functions of (
1.22
) must be the form
cU_{\lambda}(x)

for
c\in\mathbb{R}
and
\lambda>0
, where
U_{\lambda}(x)=\lambda^{\frac{N-4-\gamma}{2}}U(\lambda x)
is given in (
1.21
). Therefore, putting

into (
1.22

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{N,\gamma}=\left(\frac{2}{2+\gamma}\right)^{\frac{2(2+\gamma)}{N+\gamma}-4}\left(\frac{2\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}}{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})}\right)^{\frac{2(2+\gamma)}{N+\gamma}}\mathcal{B}\left(\frac{2(N+\gamma)}{2+\gamma}\right),

where
\mathcal{B}(M)=(M-4)(M-2)M(M+2)\left[\Gamma^{2}(\frac{M}{2})/(2\Gamma(M))\right]^{\frac{4}{M}}
, and
\Gamma
is the Gamma function.
Now, the proof of Theorem
1.5
is completed.
∎

3.
Sharp weighted Rellich-Sobolev inequality

Let us recall a crucial Rellich-Sobolev type inequality with explicit form of extremal functions which was established by Dan, Ma and Yang
[
13
]
as the following.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
N\geq 5
and
0<\mu<N-4
. For all
u\in\mathcal{D}^{2,2}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})=\{u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x<\infty,\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{\frac{2N}{N-4}}\mathrm{d}x<\infty\}
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x-C_{\mu,1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\mathrm{d}x+C_{\mu,2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u|^{2}}{|x|^{4}}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{\mu}{N-4}\right)^{4-\frac{4}{N}}\mathcal{S}_{0}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{\frac{2N}{N-4}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{N-4}{N}},

where
\mathcal{S}_{0}
is the sharp constant of classical second-order Sobolev inequality, and

\displaystyle C_{\mu,1}:

\displaystyle=\frac{N^{2}-4N+8}{2(N-4)^{2}}\mu[2(N-4)-\mu];

\displaystyle C_{\mu,2}:

\displaystyle=\frac{N^{2}}{16(N-4)^{2}}\mu^{2}[2(N-4)-\mu]^{2}-\frac{N-2}{2}\mu[2(N-4)-\mu].

Moreover, equality holds if and only if

\displaystyle u(x)=A|x|^{-\frac{\mu}{2}}\left(\lambda+|x|^{2(1-\frac{\mu}{N-4})}\right)^{-\frac{N-4}{2}},

for
A\in\mathbb{R}
and
\lambda>0
.

Let us make a brief comment about the proof of Theorem
3.1
. The key step is making the change of variable

\displaystyle u(x)=|x|^{-\frac{\mu}{2}}v(|x|^{-\frac{\mu}{N-4}}x).

Then by using standard spherical decomposition which needs lots of careful calculations,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x-C_{\mu,1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\mathrm{d}x+C_{\mu,2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u|^{2}}{|x|^{4}}\mathrm{d}x\geq\left(1-\frac{\mu}{N-4}\right)^{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta v|^{2}\mathrm{d}x,

and the equality holds if and only if
v
is radially symmetry, so does
u
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{\frac{2N}{N-4}}\mathrm{d}x=\left(1-\frac{\mu}{N-4}\right)^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|v|^{\frac{2N}{N-4}}\mathrm{d}x.

Then the result follows directly from the second-order Sobolev inequality.
In this section, following the arguments as those established by Dan et al.
[
13
]
, we will extend the result of Theorem
3.1
to weighted one shown as in Theorem
1.7
.

For simplicity, we set
r=|x|
. A function
u
is said to be radial if
u
depends only on
r
. In terms of the polar coordinate we write the Laplacian
\Delta
as

\Delta=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial r^{2}}+\frac{N-1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}},

where
\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}
is the Laplace-Beltrami operator on unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
. Firstly, we have the following:

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume
N\geq 5
,
-2<\gamma\leq 0
and
\gamma<\mu<N-4
. Let
t=r^{\frac{N-4-\mu}{N-4-\gamma}}
, then for all
u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\mu}\left|\Delta u-\frac{(N-2)(\mu-\gamma)}{(N-4-\gamma)r}\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}-\frac{(\mu-\gamma)[2(N-4)-\gamma-\mu]}{(N-4-\gamma)^{2}}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}u\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

(3.1)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{N-4-\mu}{N-4-\gamma}\right)^{3}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\int^{\infty}_{0}\left|\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial t^{2}}+\frac{N-1}{t}\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}+\frac{1}{t^{2}}\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}u\right|^{2}t^{N-1-\gamma}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}\sigma.

Proof.

Set
t=r^{\zeta}
. Then

\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}=\zeta r^{\zeta-1}\frac{\partial u}{\partial t},\quad\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial r^{2}}=\zeta(\zeta-1)r^{\zeta-2}\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}+\zeta^{2}r^{2\zeta-2}\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial t^{2}}.

A simple calculation shows, for
\zeta>0
and
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\mu}\left|\Delta u-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{r}\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}-\frac{\alpha_{2}}{r^{2}}\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}u\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\int^{\infty}_{0}\left|\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial r^{2}}+\frac{N-1-\alpha_{1}}{r}\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}+\frac{1-\alpha_{2}}{r^{2}}\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}u\right|^{2}r^{N-1-\mu}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle=\zeta^{4}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\int^{\infty}_{0}\left|\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial t^{2}}+\frac{N+\zeta-2-\alpha_{1}}{\zeta r}\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}+\frac{1-\alpha_{2}}{\zeta^{2}r^{2}}\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}u\right|^{2}r^{N-1-\mu}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle=\zeta^{3}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\int^{\infty}_{0}\left|\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial t^{2}}+\frac{N+\zeta-2-\alpha_{1}}{\zeta t}\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}+\frac{1-\alpha_{2}}{\zeta^{2}r^{2}}\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}u\right|^{2}t^{\frac{N+3\zeta-4-\mu}{\zeta}}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}\sigma.

Choosing
\zeta
,
\alpha_{1}
,
\alpha_{2}
such that

\frac{N+\zeta-2-\alpha_{1}}{\zeta}=N-1,\quad\frac{1-\alpha_{2}}{\zeta^{2}}=1,\quad\frac{N+3\zeta-4-\mu}{\zeta}=N-1-\gamma.

We obtain

\zeta=\frac{N-4-\mu}{N-4-\gamma},\quad\alpha_{1}=\frac{(N-2)(N-\gamma)}{N-4-\gamma},\quad\alpha_{2}=\frac{(\mu-\gamma)[2(N-4)-\gamma-\mu]}{(N-4-\gamma)^{2}}.

Therefore, (
3.1
) holds.
∎

Proof of Lemma

.
Firstly, we decompose

and the corresponding eigenfunction of (

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\int^{\infty}_{0}\left(\Delta f_{k}-\frac{c_{k}}{r^{2}}f_{k}\right)^{2}r^{N-1}\mathrm{d}r,

4.
Sharp second-order CKN type inequality

Firstly, we show the second-order CKN type inequality (

Proposition 4.1
.

Assume that
N\geq 5
,
-N<\alpha-2\leq\beta\leq\frac{N\alpha}{N-2}
. Then there is a constant
\mathcal{S}>0
such that

(4.1)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}|\mathrm{div}(|x|^{\alpha}\nabla u)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\geq\mathcal{S}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{\beta}|u|^{2^{**}_{\alpha,\beta}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{**}_{\alpha,\beta}}},\quad\forall u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Proof.

We know from
[
18
, Proposition A.1]
which states that there is a constant
\mathfrak{C}=\mathfrak{C}(N,\alpha,\beta)>0
such that

(4.2)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{2\alpha-\beta}|\Delta u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq\mathfrak{C}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}|\mathrm{div}(|x|^{\alpha}\nabla u)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x,\quad\forall u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

For the readers’ convenience, here we give the proof of (
4.2
). For
u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, let

(4.3)

\displaystyle w:=-|x|^{-\frac{\beta}{2}}\mathrm{div}(|x|^{\alpha}\nabla u),

then we see that

(4.4)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|w|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}|\mathrm{div}(|x|^{\alpha}\nabla u)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

It follows from (
4.3
) that

\displaystyle|x|^{\alpha-\frac{\beta}{2}}\Delta u=-w-\alpha|x|^{\alpha-\frac{\beta}{2}-2}(x\cdot\nabla u),

thus

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{2\alpha-\beta}|\Delta u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|w|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+2\alpha\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{\alpha-\frac{\beta}{2}-2}(x\cdot\nabla u)w\mathrm{d}x

(4.5)

\displaystyle\quad+\alpha^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{2\alpha-\beta-4}(x\cdot\nabla u)^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

Taking
\phi=|x|^{\alpha-\beta-2}u
as a test function into (
4.3

|x|^{\frac{\beta}{2}}w=-\mathrm{div}(|x|^{\alpha}\nabla u)
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{\frac{\beta}{2}}w\phi\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{\alpha}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi\mathrm{d}x,

therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{\alpha-\frac{\beta}{2}-2}wu\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{(N-2+\alpha)^{2}}\left[2\alpha^{2}+(3N-8-\beta)\alpha+2(N-2)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times\left[2\alpha^{3}+(7N-16-\beta)\alpha^{2}+2(N-2)(3N-10-2\beta)\alpha-4(N-2)^{2}(2+\beta)\right].

Therefore,

\displaystyle C_{\gamma,\mu,2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha(N-4+2\alpha-\beta)^{2}}{16(N-2+\alpha)^{2}}\Big\{2\alpha[\alpha^{2}+4(N-2)\alpha+4(N-2)^{2}]

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\times[2\alpha^{2}+2(N-4-\beta)\alpha+N^{2}-4N+8+\beta^{2}+4\beta]

\displaystyle\quad\quad-[2\alpha^{2}+(3N-8-\beta)\alpha+2(N-2)^{2}]

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\times[2\alpha^{3}+(7N-16-\beta)\alpha^{2}+2(N-2)(3N-10-2\beta)\alpha-4(N-2)^{2}(2+\beta)]\Big\}

\displaystyle=\frac{-\alpha(N-4+2\alpha-\beta)^{2}}{16(N-2+\alpha)^{2}}\Big\{(3N-4+\beta)(N-4-\beta)\alpha^{3}

\displaystyle\quad\quad-4(N-2)[\beta^{2}+(3N-2)\beta-2(N^{2}-7N+8)]\alpha^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\quad-4(N-2)^{2}\left[\beta^{2}+(5N-6)\beta-(N^{2}-14N+20)\alpha\right]-8(N-2)^{4}(\beta+2)\Big\}.

4.17
) holds.

5.
Sharp singular second-order CKN type inequality

At first, we establish the equivalence relationship of two second-order CKN type inequalities (
1.34
) and (
1.40
).

Lemma 5.1
.

Assume that
N\geq 5
,
\alpha>2-N
and
\frac{N-4}{N-2}\alpha-4\leq\beta\leq\alpha-2
. For each
u\in\mathcal{D}^{2,2}_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\})
, let
u(x)=|x|^{\eta}v(x)
with
\eta=2+\beta-\alpha
, then

(5.1)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{\xi}|u|^{\bar{2}^{**}_{\alpha,\beta}}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{\bar{\beta}}|v|^{2^{**}_{\alpha,\bar{\beta}}}\mathrm{d}x,

(5.2)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}|\mathrm{div}(|x|^{\alpha}\nabla u)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\bar{\beta}}|\mathrm{div}(|x|^{\alpha}\nabla v)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x,

where
\bar{2}^{**}_{\alpha,\beta}=\frac{2(N+\xi)}{N+2\alpha-\beta-4}
with
\xi=\frac{(N+2\alpha-\beta-4)^{2}}{N+\beta}-N
, and
\bar{\beta}=2\alpha-\beta-4
.

Proof.

By the choices of
\eta
and
\bar{\beta}
, it is easy to verify that

\displaystyle\bar{2}^{**}_{\alpha,\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{2(N+\xi)}{N+2\alpha-\beta-4}=\frac{2(N+2\alpha-\beta-4)}{N+\beta}=2^{**}_{\alpha,\bar{\beta}}=\frac{2(N+\bar{\beta})}{N+2\alpha-\bar{\beta}-4},

\displaystyle\bar{\beta}

\displaystyle=\xi+\eta\cdot\bar{2}^{**}_{\alpha,\beta},

thus (
5.1
) holds.

Next, we prove (
5.2
). Since
u(x)=|x|^{\eta}v(x)
with
\eta=2+\beta-\alpha
, then as in the proof of Theorem
1.9
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\beta}|\mathrm{div}(|x|^{\alpha}\nabla u)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{2(\eta+\alpha)-\beta}[\Delta v+(2\eta+\alpha)|x|^{-2}(x\cdot\nabla v)+\eta(N+\alpha+\eta-2)|x|^{-2}v]^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\Delta v|^{2}}{|x|^{\gamma}}\mathrm{d}x+(2\eta+\alpha)(2\eta+\alpha+2\gamma+4)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{(x\cdot\nabla v)^{2}}{|x|^{\gamma+4}}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\left[(2\eta+\alpha)(N-4-\gamma)-2\eta(N+\alpha+\eta-2)\right]\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}}{|x|^{\gamma+2}}\mathrm{d}x

(5.3)

\displaystyle\quad+\left[\eta^{2}(N+\alpha+\eta-2)^{2}-\eta(N+\alpha+\eta-2)(N-4-\gamma)(2\eta+\alpha+\gamma+2)\right]\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|v|^{2}}{|x|^{\gamma+4}}\mathrm{d}x,

where
\gamma=\beta-2(\eta+\alpha)
, and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\bar{\beta}}|\mathrm{div}(|x|^{\alpha}\nabla v)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\Delta v|^{2}}{|x|^{\bar{\beta}-2\alpha}}\mathrm{d}x+\alpha(2\bar{\beta}-3\alpha+4)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{(x\cdot\nabla v)^{2}}{|x|^{\bar{\beta}-2\alpha+4}}\mathrm{d}x

(5.4)

\displaystyle\quad+\alpha(N-4+2\alpha-\bar{\beta})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}}{|x|^{\bar{\beta}-2\alpha+2}}\mathrm{d}x.

It is easy to verify that

\displaystyle\gamma=\beta-2(\eta+\alpha)=\bar{\beta}-2\alpha,

\displaystyle(2\eta+\alpha)(2\eta+\alpha+2\gamma+4)=\alpha(2\bar{\beta}-3\alpha+4),

\displaystyle(2\eta+\alpha)(N-4-\gamma)-2\eta(N+\alpha+\eta-2)=\alpha(N-4+2\alpha-\bar{\beta}),

\displaystyle\eta^{2}(N+\alpha+\eta-2)^{2}-\eta(N+\alpha+\eta-2)(N-4-\gamma)(2\eta+\alpha+\gamma+2)=0,

therefore, (
5.2
) holds.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

The key step of showing above equivalent form is choosing suitable
\eta
. The idea is that, comparing (
5.3
) and (
5.4
), we notice that it must satisfy

\eta^{2}(N+\alpha+\eta-2)^{2}-\eta(N+\alpha+\eta-2)(N-4-\gamma)(2\eta+\alpha+\gamma+2)=0,

where
\gamma=\beta-2(\eta+\alpha)
, then

\eta_{1}=0,\quad\eta_{2}=2-N-\alpha,\quad\eta_{3}=2+\beta-\alpha,\quad\eta_{4}=\beta-2\alpha+4-N.

Furthermore, in order to establish (
5.2
), we need
\bar{2}^{**}_{\alpha,\beta}=2^{**}_{\alpha,\bar{\beta}}
, that is,

\frac{2(N+2\alpha-\beta-4)}{N+\beta}=\frac{2(N+\bar{\beta})}{N+2\alpha-\bar{\beta}-4},

which implies
\bar{\beta}=2\alpha-\beta-4
. Then by
\beta-2(\eta+\alpha)=\bar{\beta}-2\alpha
, we should choose
\eta=2+\beta-\alpha
.

Now, based on the symmetry result of Theorem
1.9
, we are ready to show the symmetry of extremal functions for the singular second-order CKN type inequality (
1.40
).

Proof of Theorem
1.11
. Since
\frac{N-4}{N-2}\alpha-4\leq\beta<\alpha-2
, then for
\bar{\beta}=2\alpha-\beta-4
we have

\alpha-2<\bar{\beta}\leq\frac{N}{N-2}\alpha.

Then by Lemma
5.1
and Theorem
1.9
, we can directly obtain the singular second-order CKN type inequality (
1.40
) with sharp constant

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{N,\alpha,\beta}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{S}_{N,\alpha,\bar{\beta}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2}{2+\bar{\beta}-\alpha}\right)^{\frac{2(2+\bar{\beta}-\alpha)}{N+\bar{\beta}}-4}\left(\frac{2\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}}{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})}\right)^{\frac{2(2+\bar{\beta}-\alpha)}{N+\bar{\beta}}}\mathcal{B}\left(\frac{2(N+\bar{\beta})}{2+\bar{\beta}-\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2}{\alpha-\beta-2}\right)^{\frac{2(\alpha-\beta-2)}{N+2\alpha-\beta-4}-4}\left(\frac{2\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}}{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})}\right)^{\frac{2(\alpha-\beta-2)}{N+2\alpha-\beta-4}}\mathcal{B}\left(\frac{2(N+2\alpha-\beta-4)}{\alpha-\beta-2}\right),

where
\mathcal{B}(M)=(M-4)(M-2)M(M+2)\left[\Gamma^{2}(\frac{M}{2})/(2\Gamma(M))\right]^{\frac{4}{M}}
, and equality in (
1.40
) holds if and only if

\displaystyle u(x)=A|x|^{\eta}(\lambda+|x|^{2+\bar{\beta}-\alpha})^{-\frac{N-4+2\alpha-\bar{\beta}}{2+\bar{\beta}-\alpha}}=A|x|^{2+\beta-\alpha}(1+|x|^{\alpha-\beta-2})^{-\frac{N+\beta}{\alpha-\beta-2}},

for
A\in\mathbb{R}
and
\lambda>0
, where
\eta=2+\beta-\alpha
. Now, the proof of Theorem
1.11
is completed.
∎

6.
Weak form Hardy-Rellich inequality

In this section, we establish the weak form weighted Hardy-Rellich inequality (
1.43

[
25
,
42
]
, and also
[
8
]
.

Proof of Theorem
1.13
.
The proof follows in several steps as follows.

Step I: Spherical coordinates

For each
u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, let us make the standard spherical decomposition of
u
as in the proof of Lemma
3.3
, namely

(6.1)

u(x)=u(r,\sigma)=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}f_{k}(r)\Psi_{k}(\sigma),

where
r=|x|
and
\sigma=\frac{x}{|x|}\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
, and

f_{k}(r)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}u(r,\sigma)\Psi_{k}(\sigma)\mathrm{d}\sigma.

Here
\Psi_{k}(\sigma)
denotes the
k
-th spherical harmonic. It is well known that

(6.2)

\displaystyle\Delta(f_{k}(r)\Psi_{k}(\sigma))=\left(f^{\prime\prime}_{k}+\frac{N-1}{r}f^{\prime}_{k}-\frac{c_{k}}{r^{2}}f_{k}\right)\Psi_{k}.

It is easy to verify that

\frac{\partial(f_{k}(r)\Psi_{k}(\sigma))}{\partial x_{i}}=f^{\prime}_{k}\frac{x_{i}}{r}\Psi_{k}+\varphi_{k}\frac{\partial\Psi_{k}}{\partial\sigma_{l}}\frac{\partial\sigma_{l}}{\partial x_{i}},

hence

(6.3)

\begin{split}x\cdot\nabla(f_{k}(r)\Psi_{k}(\sigma))=\sum^{N}_{i=1}x_{i}\frac{\partial(f_{k}(r)\Psi_{k}(\sigma))}{\partial x_{i}}=f^{\prime}_{k}r\Psi_{k}+f_{k}\frac{\partial\Psi_{k}}{\partial\sigma_{l}}\sum^{N}_{i=i}\frac{\partial\sigma_{l}}{\partial x_{i}}x_{i}=f^{\prime}_{k}r\Psi_{k},\end{split}

due to
\sum^{N}_{i=1}\frac{\partial\sigma_{l}}{\partial x_{i}}x_{i}=0
for every
l
. Furthermore, the functions
f_{k}(r)\in C^{\infty}_{0}([0,\infty))
satisfying
f_{k}(r)=O(r^{k})
and
f_{k}^{\prime}(r)=O(r^{k-1})
as
r\to 0
. For simplicity, we also let

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}|\Psi_{k}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\sigma=1,\quad\mbox{for all}\quad k\geq 0,

so that

(6.4)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-2a-4}(x\cdot\nabla u)^{2}\mathrm{d}x=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\int^{\infty}_{0}(f_{k}^{\prime})^{2}r^{N-3-2a}\mathrm{d}r,

and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-2a}|\Delta u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\int^{\infty}_{0}\left(f^{\prime\prime}_{k}+\frac{N-1}{r}f^{\prime}_{k}-\frac{c_{k}}{r^{2}}f_{k}\right)^{2}r^{N-1-2a}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\bigg[\int^{\infty}_{0}\left(f_{k}^{\prime\prime}\right)^{2}r^{N-1-2a}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\quad\quad+[(1+2a)(N-1)+2c_{k}]\int^{\infty}_{0}\left(f_{k}^{\prime}\right)^{2}r^{N-3-2a}\mathrm{d}r

(6.5)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+c_{k}[c_{k}+2(1+a)(N-4-2a)]\int^{\infty}_{0}f_{k}^{2}r^{N-5-2a}\mathrm{d}r\bigg].

We refer to
[
42
, Section 2.2]
for details, and also
[
25
, Appendix B]
.

Step II: Weighted one-dimensional Hardy and Rellich inequalities

We recall the following Hardy and Rellich type inequalities with sharp constants:

(6.6)

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{0}\left(f_{k}^{\prime\prime}\right)^{2}r^{N-1-2a}\mathrm{d}r\geq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{N-2-2a}{2}\right)^{2}\int^{\infty}_{0}\left(f_{k}^{\prime}\right)^{2}r^{N-3-2a}\mathrm{d}r,\quad\forall k\geq 0,

(6.7)

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{0}\left(f_{k}^{\prime}\right)^{2}r^{N-3-2a}\mathrm{d}r\geq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{N-4-2a}{2}\right)^{2}\int^{\infty}_{0}f_{k}^{2}r^{N-5-2a}\mathrm{d}r,\quad\forall k\geq 1.

For the sake of clarity let us give a few lines proof of both (
6.6
) and (
6.7
). Indeed, for each
v\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, from the identity

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-2-2c}x\cdot\nabla v^{2}\mathrm{d}x=-(N-2-2c)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-2-2c}v^{2}\mathrm{d}x=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v|x|^{-2-2c}(x\cdot\nabla v)\mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-2-2c}(x\cdot\nabla v)^{2}\mathrm{d}x\geq\left(\frac{N-2-2c}{2}\right)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-2-2c}v^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

Thus, let

with

we deduce (
6.6
), and let

with

we deduce (
6.7
). Next, we show the constants in (

\displaystyle\left\{f_{\epsilon}(r):=r^{-(N-4-2a)/2-\epsilon}g(r)\right\}_{\epsilon>0}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(-\frac{N-4-2a}{2}-\epsilon\right)^{2}\left(-\frac{N-2-2a}{2}-\epsilon\right)^{2}\left(\frac{2^{-2\epsilon}}{2\epsilon}+O(1)\right)+O(1)}{\left(-\frac{N-4-2a}{2}-\epsilon\right)^{2}\left(\frac{2^{-2\epsilon}}{2\epsilon}+O(1)\right)+O(1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(-\frac{N-4-2a}{2}-\epsilon\right)^{2}\left(\frac{2^{-2\epsilon}}{2\epsilon}+O(1)\right)+O(1)}{\frac{2^{-2\epsilon}}{2\epsilon}+O(1)}

\displaystyle\left\{\bar{f}_{\epsilon}(r):=\int^{\infty}_{r}h_{\epsilon}(t)\mathrm{d}t\right\}_{\epsilon>0}\quad\mbox{with}\quad h_{\epsilon}(t)=t^{-1-\epsilon}g(t),

and it is enough to show that the family of functions

as

It is enough to show that the family of functions

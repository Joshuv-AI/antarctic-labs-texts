Etiology of waterpipe smoking on the coast of Bandar Abbas city: A qualitative study of men in southern Iran
Authors: Ali Asadian, Zahra Hosseini, Ahmad Sotoudeh, Mojtaba Fattahi Ardakani, Abdoljabar Zakeri, Arezoo Rajabipour
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:An alternative form of tobacco consumption, especially in the south of Iran, is waterpipe smoking. Hormozgan province ranks third in smoking waterpipe in Iran. The present study aimed to explore the causes of waterpipe smoking among men in Bandar Abbas, the largest city and the capital of Hormozgan province in southern Iran.MATERIALS AND METHODS:The present qualitative research was conducted on 76 male waterpipe smokers at a waterpipe smoking cafe on the coast of Bandar Abbas. The data were collected through in-depth and semi-structured interviews. The sampling continued until data saturation. The extracted data were analyzed through a content analysis.RESULTS:After the content analysis, 12 categories of reasons were extracted for waterpipe smoking. These categories were personal reasons, familial reasons, friends, physical reasons, psychological reasons, economic reasons, cultural reasons, positive attitude towards waterpipe, unemployment, geographical conditions, despair and freedom from daily life, easy access and abundant supply of waterpipe. The most prominent categories were physical and psychological factors followed by easy access and large supply of waterpipe on the coast of Bandar Abbas.CONCLUSION:The present study showed a strong need for preventive interventions in waterpipe smoking in the southern city of Bandar Abbas. Long-term interventions can significantly reduce the rate of waterpipe and tobacco consumption. They may aim to improve the health literacy of community members, and cultural reform of waterpipe smoking among people. It is incumbent on provincial policy makers and health decision makers to make short-term interventions too. For example, they can legally prohibit selling waterpipe on the beach, selling tobacco products on the coastal line, and increase modern and easily available, affordable and healthy entertainment.

Introduction

Smoking is a major determinant of preventable mortality worldwide. Neurobiological findings have found mechanisms through which the nicotine in tobacco affects the brain reward system and causes addiction. These brain changes help maintain nicotine or tobacco use despite the awareness of its adverse effects, which is a sign of addiction. Effective approaches to screening, preventing, and treating smoking can be widely used to limit the impact of smoking on individuals and society.[1]

In 2019, tobacco consumption accounted for 8.71 million and 229.77 million mortalities and disability-adjusted life years in the world. The age-standardized rates of all tobacco-related mortalities and DALYs (disability-adjusted life years) decreased from 1990 to 2019, to 108.55 mortalities per 100,000 people and 2791.04 DALYs per 100,000 people in 2019.[2]

Smoking waterpipes for one hour is equal to smoking 2 to 10 cigarettes in toxicity and carcinogenicity. Waterpipe smoke contains heavy and carcinogenic substances, 35% of which remain in the immediate environment, and 65% enter the human body directly.[3] The ratio of waterpipe smoking in different regions of Eastern Mediterranean, Europe, and America is 2.5-37.2%, 2.2-22.7%, and 1-11.4%, respectively.[4]

The rate of tobacco consumption has changed in many countries. In some countries, the rapid decrease in cigarette smoking is associated with the increase in other types of tobacco, including waterpipe.[5] In Iran, the prevalence of waterpipe smoking has increased in the last two decades, especially in the south.[6]

Several factors have increased interest in waterpipe smoking especially among the young. Examples are the attractive smell of waterpipe and various tastes, better social acceptability than cigarettes, easy access and the false belief that waterpipe is not harmful due to the smoke passing through water.[78] Many consumers believe that waterpipe smoke is less harmful than cigarettes.[910]

Despite warnings about the growing rate of waterpipe smoking, the controlling measures have largely failed. The deceptive advertisements by waterpipe manufacturers claiming that the aromatic smoke of waterpipe is healthier than cigarettes further encourage waterpipe smoking. There are even health-related and herbal labels on tobacco products to deceive consumers.[111213] In fact, waterpipe smoking exposes people to nicotine, carbon monoxide, toxic smoke, and heavy metals.[910]

As reported by the World Health Organization (WHO), smoking waterpipes for 20 to 80 minutes is equivalent to smoking 100 or more cigarettes.[14] A key point to consider in designing an effective intervention is the epidemiology and specific identification of the underlying factors of waterpipe smoking.[15] Particularly, Hormozgan province ranks third in smoking waterpipe.[7] Finding the reasons for willingness to smoke waterpipes helps better control and prevent smoking. Qualitative studies will provide valuable information about this phenomenon because they will provide researchers with in-depth and exploratory information.[16] There are few studies with a qualitative approach to waterpipe smoking in Iran. Bandar Abbas is the capital of Hormozgan province and hosts the largest population of the capital of province. It is a city on the coast of the Persian Gulf with different coastal areas to sell waterpipe to the public.

There have already been similar studies on waterpipe smoking among women in the south. That is why the present study preferred to focus only on men to fill the gap. The novelty of this research lies in the fact that no other research had been previously conducted on the causes of waterpipe smoking in the male population of the coastal area of the city. No similar research had been conducted in Bandar Abbas. Moreover, in practical domains of health, we needed rigorous research evidence to document the causes of waterpipe smoking among men in Bandar Abbas.

Materials and Methods

Study design and setting

The present qualitative study used a conventional content analysis with semi-structured interviews in Bandar Abbas, a city in the southern coast of Iran.

In this analysis, the coded content was directly extracted from text, without theoretical guidance for the primary codes.[17] In this study, through a conventional content analysis, the categories were extracted directly from part to whole using raw data. The advantage of using the conventional content analysis was that the information was obtained directly from the participants without any pre-determined theoretical perspectives.

Study participants and sampling

In this research, a convenience sampling was used to include subjects in the group. The interviews were held face to face with waterpipe smokers while visiting waterpipe selling sites in the coastal line of Bandar Abbas. The interviews were held with male smokers either while smoking or before beginning to smoke.

The participants included ex-smokers and current smokers of waterpipe. The latter included participants who smoked waterpipes at least once in the past month. The former included participants who used to smoke waterpipe at least once in the past three consecutive months but then stopped it altogether. Before the interview, the interviewer clearly introduced himself and explained the purpose of interview. Then, the interviewer verbally obtained the interviewee’s consent and assured him of the confidentiality of the interview content. Each interview took between 20 and 40 minutes. At the beginning of the interview, a pack of sunflower seeds was given to the interviewee to encourage him to participate. For further encouragement, a smoking cessation center was introduced to the interviewees for their prospective visits.

Interviews were unstructured and semi-structured as the main method of data collection. This type of interview suited the type of qualitative research due to its flexibility and depth.[18]

At first, through a review of literature or the data already collected, the present researcher developed questions with social, psychological, cognitive or environmental cultural origin according to the interview guide. With open-ended questions, respondents elaborated more on their beliefs, values, experiences, behaviors and feelings in their own words, and also freely shared feelings, thoughts, and experiences.

The interviewer asked the interview questions based on the interview guide. Here are the questions:

In what circumstances and where did you smoke waterpipe for the first time? Why did you start to smoke waterpipe? What is the reason for continuing to smoke waterpipe? What effect do you think waterpipe has on you? What kind of physical state does smoking waterpipe give you? Do you smoke waterpipe with friends? Do you smoke waterpipe with your family? Do you take your wife or children with you to the beach to smoke waterpipe? What is attractive about waterpipe smoking in Bandar Abbas coastal line? What do you think about waterpipe supply for people on the beach? Is waterpipe nice and fragrant to you? Is waterpipe smoking in the coastal city of Bandar Abbas a hobby for you? Do you smoke waterpipe to get away from the problems of everyday life? If you intend to consume one type of tobacco, do you prefer waterpipe, cigarettes, or else? Why?

Demographic questions enquired about age, occupation, education, place of residence, marital status, rate of waterpipe smoking and current or ex-user state of waterpipe smoking. For the adequacy of sampling in this research, data saturation was used, which is closely related to the sample size. Thus, the data collection continued until no new information emerged or only the old information recured. However, in quantitative research, priority goes to decision making on sample size through statistical analyses. In qualitative research, the repetition of previous information or, in other words, the repetition of themes or salient points, is a sign of the adequacy of sample size.[1920] Overall, 187 people were invited to take part in the interview, but not all consented to participate, and finally only 76 people were successfully interviewed. The data collection continued until data saturation, i.e. when no new themes were generated during the discussions.

Data analysis

During the interview, notes were taken of the answers. The whole interview was audio-recorded and then transcribed. The final material was translated into English and the data were analyzed in MAXQDA10 to extract the final categories.

Methodological considerations

To ensure the accuracy of findings, the researcher devoted enough time to collect data, communicate with interviewees in all stages of data collection and adhere to all ethical considerations in research. This involved several rounds of review and immersion, and prolonged engagement with data. Also, due to the pragmatic approach in the current research, the sampling was done from different places where tobacco was available (place triangulation).

To substantiate the content validity of analysis, some coded texts were provided to experts in psychology, health education and health promotion to review and comment. The reliability of data was checked by a colleague (outside the research team not involved in data collection) and an experienced expert by providing the coded content of several interviews. An agreement was reached on the coding method. The researcher increased the transferability by describing the participants in detail, the sampling method, the time and place of data collection. To increase the homogeneity and objectivity, the present researcher explained the details of study and fully recorded all steps of data collection (text, coding, and analysis).

Ethical considerations

To conduct this research, consent was obtained from the subjects and they were assured of the confidentiality of their information. This study received a code of ethics (IR.HUMS.REC.1402.235) from the Research and Technology Vice-Chancellor of Hormozgan University of Medical Sciences.

Results

Interviews were conducted with 76 male waterpipe smokers. Their average age ranged between 16 and 58 years. There were 56 participants whose age ranged between 16 and 35, 16 in the age range of 35 to 50, and 4 in the age range of 50 to 58. All participants were current waterpipe smokers. They smoked waterpipe at least once a month. Nineteen smokers had ceased waterpipe smoking for one to four months in the past, but they began to smoke again and did so at least once a month. In total, 26% of the participants held a diploma or a lower degree, 45% held degrees higher than diploma, 18% were university students and 11% attended school, 54% of participants were married, and all resided in Bandar Abbas [Table 1]. Having analyzed the interview content, 544 codes were extracted and combined based on similarity. Finally, 12 causal categories were extracted for waterpipe smoking as described below.

Participants’ Demographic Information

Category 1: Physical reasons

This category included the personal and physical feelings caused by waterpipe smoking in men. For the participants, waterpipe smoking created a sense of pleasure and physical relaxation, especially the smoke.

“ When I begin to smoke waterpipe, I prefer to be lying down, and sometimes when I smoke too much waterpipe, at the end, I feel the muscles in my body relax, which gives me a pleasant feeling, and I even want to relax here for a while in the same place I smoke.” (Interviewee No. 17)

Category 2: Psychological reasons

Smoking waterpipes gave a feeling of liberation and intellectual freedom from life disasters in the short run.

“When I’m smoking waterpipe by the beach, after a few minutes, my head gets a little heavy, and then I’m freed from my daily obsessions and I don’t think about anything but the taste of waterpipe, and I’m lost in the mood of repeating this thoughtless pleasure.” (Interviewee No. 24)

Category 3: Geographical conditions

Waterpipe smoking especially in coastal areas and humidity of air on the beach were a sub-category of willingness to smoke waterpipe in geographical conditions of a coastal city for consumers.

“There is nothing as pleasant as waterpipe on the beach with the sea breeze. I have smoked waterpipe in other cities like Shiraz or Mashhad, but here it is very pleasant because waterpipe smoking here is different. You will not feel the pleasure of waterpipe in the air of the harbor until you smoke. If I go to the city, the first thing I do after getting to the beach is to smoke waterpipe”. (Interviewee No. 26)

Category 4: Easy access

The supply of waterpipe in most coastal areas and places to sit and rest on the beach encouraged the act of waterpipe smoking for men. Waterpipe is easy to prepare and use.

“Here in bandar Abbas, it is free to smoke waterpipe everywhere. I can order and smoke waterpipe anywhere on the beach. Peddlers supply waterpipe easily. I can even sit by the sea and smoke waterpipe with the pleasing sound of the sea. Some time ago, I was in Isfahan, where we had to go to a coffee shop to smoke waterpipe. So, waterpipe was not easily available to me, and I had a very hard time.” (Interviewee No. 35)

Category 5: Positive attitude towards waterpipe smoking

Smoking waterpipes is an alternative form of smoking, the detriments of which are fewer than cigarette smoking. It is not addictive; otherwise, it was not be supplied so vastly in recreational places and coastal areas. Smoking waterpipes fails to be accompanied by social rejection in public places.

“I used to smoke cigarettes before smoking waterpipe. Everyone criticized me for being a smoker in family and around me, but now I look at all these people smoking waterpipe on the beach. They even sometimes go out to smoke several times in a week. I live with my family, no one finds fault with me. Now I have a question about the adverse effects of waterpipe. If there are many, why do so many people insist on smoking waterpipe?” (Interviewee No. 17)

Category 6: Personal reasons

Spending free time outside home at the beach and hiding the act of smoking from family were the personal reasons.

“I enjoy spending time on the beach. It’s a hobby for me to smoke waterpipes. I can’t afford to go for any better recreational facilities on the beach. Well, now I can’t afford to ride a jet ski, yet I can sit while smoking and watch others enjoy a jet ski ride.” (Interviewee No. 41)

Category 7: Familial reasons

Smoking waterpipes with the family and usually the wife was an accepted family behavior which further encouraged people to smoke. Even children smoking together with parents in the same place and at the same time depended on the exemplary role of family in smoking waterpipes.

“In our hometown, I have never seen someone in the family having an issue with smoking waterpipes in the family. Sometimes, we even sit together with my wife and smoke waterpipe.” (Interviewee No. 16)

Category 8: Friends

Following friends and peer pressure, a sense of belonging to the group of friends and feeling grown-up were among the factors related to friends’ role in smoking waterpipes.

“I always smoke waterpipe with friends. Sometimes if one of us does not smoke waterpipe, we make fun of him, which makes him finally join us.” (Interviewee No. 54)

Category 9: Unemployment

A number of waterpipe smokers mentioned unemployment as the reason for waterpipe smoking. Among smokers in the age group of 20 to 30 years, unemployment was a major factor in waterpipe smoking.

“When I’m working somewhere, I don’t really like to smoke waterpipe, but if I am free for a few days, I come here to smoke waterpipe on the beach. I don’t have a permanent job. I am unemployed for several days of a week. Together with the rest of my colleagues, who are unemployed like me, we come here to see each other and smoke waterpipe.” (Interviewee No. 46)

Category 10: Despair, demotivation, and lack of leisure

Among smokers, especially in the 25- to 40-year age group, participants were aware of the detriments of waterpipe smoking yet also smoked cigarettes, and they talked about despair in personal life and boredom with family and social life conditions as the reasons for smoking waterpipes.

“The smell of waterpipe tobacco is pleasant to me. I know smoking waterpipe is harmful, but it makes no difference to me. We may end up dying sooner, and nothing more. You think now I am enjoying my life and everything is fine with me? My life is a cycle of fruitless efforts.” (Interviewee No. 14)

Category 11: Economic pressure

In the 40- to 59-year age group, a number of participants admitted that smoking waterpipe was a way to find peace and rid of economic worries about themselves in the short run.

“I work two shifts, sometimes I forget what day of week it is because of my busy schedule. But when I get home, we still find it hard to make a living. Sometimes I want to carry waterpipe everywhere with me, so I can escape the financial pressure and find peace.” (Interviewee No. 54)

Category 12: Cultural reasons

A number of smokers mentioned the history of waterpipe smoking in grandparents’ families and that they had seen waterpipe smoking in family and relatives since childhood and that waterpipe smoking was not discouraged in their family and was quite prevalent. They could even easily carry the waterpipe device with them when travelling, which encouraged further waterpipe smoking.

“I remember there was a waterpipe in our house since I was a child. Even then, I used to serve waterpipe to my grandfather. Now, as a family, we sometimes smoke waterpipe. Even my brother, my father and I take one or more waterpipes with us when we travel.” (Interviewee No. 67)

Discussion

Exploring the reasons for tobacco consumption is the first step to control and prevent smoking. The present study aimed to explore the reasons for waterpipe smoking among men living in Bandar Abbas, a coastal and immigrant-friendly city. The present qualitative study explored the etiology of waterpipe smoking in male residents of one of the largest coastal cities in Iran.

Waterpipe smoking and friends

The present findings showed that one important reason for waterpipe smoking in the male population of Bandar Abbas was spending time with friends and temptation to smoke waterpipes due to peer pressure. This finding was consistent with Karimy and Bejjani’s studies in Iran[2122] and Nohair’s study in Saudi Arabia.[23] In this study, an important factor in beginning to smoke waterpipe, especially in the below-25-year age group, was the sense of belonging to a group of friends and showing off while smoking with friends. This finding was true for 90% of subjects in another study,[24] and was consistent with two other studies.[2526]

The present study showed that one reason for men’s tendency to smoke waterpipe is their friends, as also ratified by other studies.[2728] A lot of men who smoke waterpipe are not truly interested in the waterpipe itself. They are aware of its many disadvantages, and even its physiological and neurological effects do not matter to them.[29] In Bandar Abbas city, there is a high rate of immigration. There are especially men who are far from the control of families and parents. They tend to smoke waterpipe when they are away from families. They consider their friends to be the main cause of willingness to smoke waterpipe. Therefore, waterpipe smoking was seen as a social activity in friendly get-togethers. It seems that waterpipe smokers on the beach seek for a chance to gather with friends. Thus, waterpipe smoking is an excuse for them to gather around and converse together for self-expression. Waterpipe, charcoal, and tobacco have become an integral part of entertainment for the youth who come to spend their free time in the coastal nature of Bandar Abbas city.

Waterpipe smoking and social culture

A body of research has shown the cultural and social influence of the place of residence on waterpipe smoking.[30313233] The present study showed that one important reason for waterpipe smoking is the cultural background of waterpipe smoking in Bandar Abbas. All consumers contended that waterpipe smoking was culturally acceptable. They even stated that waterpipe was an inseparable part of traveling and a common device available at home. Even though tobacco consumption is an undesirable social behavior followed by social rejection, waterpipe smoking was not a matter of social rejection by the family in the majority of participants. Another study also showed that the naturalization and cultural labeling of waterpipe smoking is an important factor in initiation, continuation of smoking, and unwillingness to cease waterpipe smoking in society.[30]

Formerly, local people in the south smoked waterpipe in special gatherings exclusively for certain groups of people. However, today, waterpipe smoking on the urban beaches of Bandar Abbas has turned into a social behavior. People show preference for the same environment as coffee shops right within the nature, on the beach. Historically, waterpipe smoking since the immigration of foreigners to the south has become a social cultural behavior and a smoking norm. The beautiful retreats of the coast sometimes turn into addiction hobs following waterpipe smoking. This is a social harm, especially for people who are not strong enough to defend themselves or find it hard to say no to peddlers. They are prone to greater social harm done by waterpipe smoking. It is incumbent on social decision-makers to intervene and cut down on this unhealthy habit and norm in a coastal city like Bandar Abbas.

Waterpipe smoking, physical pleasure, and peace of mind

In the present study, one reason for smoking waterpipe was the physical factor, the sense of physical pleasure caused by the effect of waterpipe tobacco on human brain, which was found in two other studies as well.[3435] Waterpipe smokers in this study talked about the feeling of physical relief and freedom from mental problems through smoking waterpipe. Most interviewees said that through waterpipe smoking in the short run, they felt free from the confusion and discomfort of life issues. This finding is similar to other studies in which waterpipe smokers confessed that it brought about physical and mental comfort.[3637]

Another reason for the willingness to smoke waterpipe, as found in this study, was the climatic and geographical conditions and even the humidity of air in Bandar Abbas, which somehow strengthened the desire for smoking waterpipe. Even in response to the question “Is it more attractive to smoke waterpipe in Bandar Abbas than other areas?”, 37% answered it was attractive for them to smoke waterpipe near the beach in Bandar Abbas. Even outside Hormozgan province, they did not wish to smoke waterpipes. Ahmed et al.[38] found a significant association between geographical condition and waterpipe smoking. In the interview with waterpipe smokers, an interesting theme was recurrently found in every interview: Waterpipe smoking on the beach is pleasant and attractive to us. We do not smoke waterpipe outside the province even for a month.

In this research, the pleasure of waterpipe smoking in the coastal nature of Bandar Abbas was clearly expressed by the male participants. Their accounts showed while on the beach they were watching the sea, sitting or lying down, the taste and smell of the waterpipe smoke gave them a feeling of freedom. This pleasure somehow justifies the continuation, repetition, and increase of waterpipe smoking in men. It seems that men who smoke waterpipe are somehow deprived of other pleasures available on the beach, such as swimming and beach entertainments. That is why they think smoking waterpipes on the beach is enjoyable. The solution to this issue is to provide enjoyable entertainment services for men and ease their access to healthy recreation.

Positive attitude towards waterpipe smoking

Similar to other studies,[3940] the research participants held a positive attitude towards waterpipe smoking, the damage it caused to health and addiction to waterpipe. The smokers in this study clearly stated that waterpipe was not as addictive as cigarettes and even sometimes they felt it was necessary for them to smoke waterpipe. In response to the question “If it is not addictive, why do you smoke waterpipe every week?”, they admitted that, to them, waterpipe smoking was not a necessity or dependence, but a form of individual social leisure and fun. The male participants also mentioned that the abundant waterpipe supply and free smoking in public places is the reason for less addiction to waterpipe and its popularity.

Easy access to waterpipe

The present findings showed that easy access to waterpipe for all people encouraged waterpipe smoking for men in Bandar Abbas. There is an abundant supply of waterpipe on the beach in every area. In similar studies by Mominabadi[40] and Castañeda,[33] a main reason for waterpipe smoking was the easy access to waterpipe. The beach in Bandar Abbas city hosts many cafes for waterpipe smokers. Even in some places by the sea, the smell of waterpipe can be inhaled easily. In the interview with waterpipe smokers, an interesting theme was recurrently found in every interview: “If waterpipe is condemned, why are there so many sellers supplying it freely and it is found even in the best restaurants with no one complaining about it?”

Among the reasons for smoking waterpipe in the male interviewees were unemployment and freedom from economic and social pressures. No similar finding was reported in other waterpipe-related studies. However, among smokers living in Bandar Abbas, the 40-59 age group clearly stated that waterpipe smoking was a short-term relief from personal life issues. This was consistent with the work of research by Ahmed et al.[38]

The high profit of selling waterpipe in Bandar Abbas has made the product easily available in different flavors, and smokers have many choices available. These different flavors, which are very popular, are welcomed by many people regardless of the adverse effects. The pleasant sense of the different flavors of tobacco on the beach with the sea breeze alongside make men tend to smoke waterpipe. Walking in some coastal areas of Bandar Abbas, even the smell of waterpipe smoke is quite evident. Unfortunately, for many years, the rules and regulations on selling tobacco in public places have not been strictly followed in the coastal city of Bandar Abbas. The effective solution can be a collaborative and serious decision-making of social, political, and health authorities to implement rules to prohibit the sale and use of tobacco in public places.

Limitations

Most studies on tobacco and waterpipe smoking have been quantitative or surveys of the state of the art of waterpipe smoking. Thus, the present study was conducted qualitatively to directly extract information from participants by skilled interviewers. It required the devotion of enough time to find participants who agreed to be interviewed even while smoking waterpipe. Communicating with people busy smoking waterpipes in a family gathering required family participation, and some families were not comfortable with the interview, and the interviewer had to move to other subjects. The present project was carried out only in Bandar Abbas. In other cities of Hormozgan province, no similar project has been carried out yet with a qualitative approach.

Conclusion

The present study employed a qualitative approach to the etiology of waterpipe smoking among male residents of Bandar Abbas in the south of Iran. Several prominent causal categories were found for waterpipe smoking, including personal, familial, cultural and physical, psychological factors, false information about waterpipe, freedom from everyday life issues, despair and unemployment, easy access and abundant supply of waterpipe. Long-term interventions such as improving the health literacy of community members, cultural reform of waterpipe smoking among people can reduce the rate of waterpipe and tobacco smoking.

The results of this study clearly explained why men in the coastal city of Bandar Abbas are willing to smoke waterpipe, and these results comprise a practical framework for designing health interventions to reduce waterpipe smoking among men in a major coastal city of country. For the health authorities in Hormozgan province, the present findings are a scientific document and a logical justification for the design and implementation of interventions to reduce waterpipe smoking in men. To attract the participation and cooperation of political, social decision-makers and stakeholders, the results of this research can be used to determine which causal category is more prominent for waterpipe smoking in men and has the potential for more effective interventions. Interventions that can be effectively made by provincial policy makers and health decision-makers are the legal banning of waterpipe sale on the coast, tobacco sale on the coast, the development and increase of happy and healthy modern entertainment on the coast.

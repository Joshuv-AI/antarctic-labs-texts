Fan’s condition for completely independent spanning trees
Jie Ma, Junqing Cai

Abstract
Spanning trees T 1 , T 2 , … , T k T_{1},T_{2},\dots,T_{k} of G G are k k completely independent spanning trees if, for any two vertices u , v ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) u,v\in V(G) , the paths from u u to v v in these k k trees are pairwise edge-disjoint and internal vertex-disjoint. Hasunuma proved that determining whether a graph contains k k completely independent spanning trees is NP-complete, even for k = 2 k=2 . Araki posed the question of whether certain known sufficient conditions for hamiltonian cycles are also also guarantee two completely independent spanning trees? In this paper, we affirmatively answer this question for the Fan-type condition. Precisely, we proved that if G G is a connected graph such that each pair of vertices at distance 2 has degree sum at least | V ⁡ ( G ) | |V(G)| , then G G has two completely independent spanning trees. Keywords: Completely independent spanning trees, k k -CIST-partition, Fan-type condition



1
Introduction

In this paper, we consider only undirected, finite and simple graphs. For a graph
G
, we denote its vertex set and edge set by
V(G)
and
E(G)
, respectively. The number of vertices in
G
is called the order of
G
, written as
|V(G)|
or
|G|
. The neighborhood and degree of a vertex
v
in
G
are the set and number of vertices which are adjacent to
v
in
G
, denoted by
N_{G}(v)
and
d_{G}(v)
, respectively. The minimum degree of
G
is
\delta(G)
=min
\{d(v)|v\in V(G)\}
. For a subgraph
H
of
G
and a subset
U
of
V(G)
, let
N_{H}(v)=N_{G}(v)\cap V(H)
,
d_{H}(v)=|N_{H}(v)|
and
N_{H}(U)=\cup_{u\in U}N_{H}(u)
. When there is no confusion, we simplify the notation to
N(v)
,
d(v)
and
N(U)
. If
G
is not a complete graph, then we define

\sigma_{2}(G)=\min\{d(u)+d(v)|u,v\in V(G)~and~uv\notin E(G)\},

\mu_{2}(G)=\min\{d(u)+d(v)|u,v\in V(G)~and~d(u,v)=2\}.

Otherwise, set
\sigma_{2}(G)=\mu_{2}(G)=+\infty
.
The subgraph induced by
U
is denoted by
G[U]
, and the subgraph induced by
V(G)\setminus U
is denoted by
G-U
. For a single vertex
u
, we use
G-u
instead of
G-\{u\}
. The set of integers from 1 to
n
is denoted by
[n]
.

A tree
T
of a graph
G
is a spanning tree if
V(T)
=
V(G)
. In 2001, Hasunuma
[
6
]
introduced the concept of completely independent spanning trees (CISTs).

Definition 1.1

(Hasunuma
[
6
]
)

Let
T_{1},T_{2},\dots,T_{k}
be spanning trees in a graph
G
. If for any two vertices
u,v
of
G
, the paths from
u
to
v
in
T_{1},T_{2},\dots,T_{k}
are pairwise edge-disjoint and internal vertex-disjoint, then
T_{1},T_{2},\dots,T_{k}
are
k
completely independent spanning trees
(k
CISTs).

Completely independent spanning trees are a powerful tool for enhancing the reliability, redundancy, and efficiency of various systems, particularly those that rely on network structures. Hasunuma
[
7
]
proved that determining whether a given graph contains
k
CISTs is NP-complete, even for
k=2
.

Let
V_{1},V_{2},\dots,V_{k}
be
k
disjoint sets of vertices in
G
. If
V(G)=V_{1}\cup V_{2}\cup\dots\cup V_{k}
, then we call
(V_{1},V_{2},\dots,V_{k})
a
k
-partition of
V(G)
. For
i\neq j
,
B(V_{i},V_{j},G)
denotes the bipartite subgraph of
G
induced by the edges of
G
with one endvertex in
V_{i}
and the other in
V_{j}
. If there is no confusion, we use
B(V_{i},V_{j})
instead of
B(V_{i},V_{j},G)
.

Definition 1.2

(Araki
[
1
]
)

Let
G
be a graph and
(V_{1},V_{2},\dots,V_{k})
be a
k
-partition of
V(G)
. Then
(V_{1},V_{2},\dots,V_{k})
is a
k
-CIST-partition if it satisfies the following two conditions:

(i)
for each
i\in[k]
, the induced subgraph
G[V_{i}]
is connected; and

(ii)
for any
1\leq i\neq j\leq k
,
B(V_{i},V_{j})
has no tree component (i.e. each component of
B(V_{i},V_{j})
has a cycle).

Araki
[
1
]
gave an useful equivalent characterization of
k
CISTs, which is crucial for proving their existence.

Theorem 1.1

(Araki
[
1
]
)

A connected graph has
k
CISTs if and only if it has a
k
-CIST-partition.

In the same paper, Araki
[
1
]
also showed that two well-established conditions for hamiltonian cycles-Dirac’s and a condition obtained by Fleischner
[
4
]
, are sufficient for the existence of two CISTs.

Theorem 1.2

(Araki
[
1
]
)

Let
G
be a graph of order
n\geq 7
. If
\delta(G)\geq\frac{n}{2}
, then
G
has two CISTs.

Theorem 1.3

(Araki
[
1
]
)

Let
G
be a
2
-connected graph of order
n\geq 4
. Then the square
G^{2}
has two CISTs.

Based on these results, Araki
[
1
]
posed the question of whether certain known sufficient conditions for hamiltonian cycles are also also guarantee two completely independent spanning trees? Fan, Hong and Liu
[
3
]
confirmed this for Ore’s condition.

Theorem 1.4

(Fan, Hong and Liu
[
3
]
)

Let
G
be a graph of order
n\geq 8
. If
\sigma_{2}(G)\geq n
, then
G
has two CISTs.

Hong and Zhang
[
8
]
extended this result to a condition by Gould and Jacobson
[
5
]
.

Theorem 1.5

(Hong and Zhang
[
8
]
)

Let
G
be a graph of order
n\geq 5
. If
|N(x)\cup N(y)|\geq\frac{n}{2}
and
|N(x)\cap N(y)|\geq 3
for every pair of nonadjacent vertices
x
and
y
, then
G
has two CISTs. (Note: Qin et al.
[
9
]
later pointed that
n\geq 8
is the correct lower bound.)

Fan’s condition is another well-known condition for hamiltonian cycles.

Theorem 1.6

(Fan
[
2
]
)

Let
G
be a
2
-connected graph of order
n\geq 3
. If
max
\{d(u),d(v)\}\geq\frac{n}{2}
for every pair of vertices
u
and
v
with
d(u,v)=2
, then
G
has a hamiltonian cycle.

From Theorem
1.6
, we derive the following corollary.

Corollary 1.1

Let
G
be a connected graph of order
n\geq 3
. If
\mu_{2}(G)\geq n
, then
G
has a hamiltonian cycle.

In this paper, we prove that the condition
\mu_{2}(G)\geq n
is also sufficient for the existence of two CISTs.

Theorem 1.7

Let
G
be a connected graph of order
n\geq 7
. If
\mu_{2}(G)\geq n
, then
G
has two CISTs.

Remark 1.1

(1)
The complete bipartite graph
K_{3,3}
implies that
n\geq 7
in Theorem
1.7
is necessary.

(2)
Let
G_{1}=K_{s}
and
G_{2}=K_{t}
be two disjoint complete graphs. The graph
G
obtained from two disjoint complete graphs
K_{s}
and
K_{t}
by adding a new vertex
u
and edges
\{uv|v\in V(K_{s}\cup K_{t})\}
implies that the condition
\mu_{2}(G)\geq n
of Theorem
1.7
is sharp.

2
Preliminary

The following lemmas are useful in the proof of Theorem
1.7
.

Lemma 2.1

Let
G
be a connected non-complete graph of order
n\geq 3
and
\mu_{2}(G)\geq n
. Then for any two vertices
x
,
y
with
d(x,y)=2
,
|N(x)\cap N(y)|\geq 2
. Furthermore, if
|N(x)\cap N(y)|=2
, then
N(x)\cup N(y)=V(G)\setminus\{x,y\}
.

Proof.
Note that
N(x)\cup N(y)\subseteq V(G)\setminus\{x,y\}
, we have
n\leq d(x)+d(y)=|N(x)\cup N(y)|+|N(x)\cap N(y)|\leq|V(G)\setminus\{x,y\}|+|N(x)\cap N(y)|=(n-2)+|N(x)\cap N(y)|.
Thus
|N(x)\cap N(y)|\geq 2
, and
|N(x)\cap N(y)|=2\Rightarrow N(x)\cup N(y)=V(G)\setminus\{x,y\}
.
\hfill\Box

Lemma 2.2

Let
G
be a connected graph of order
n\geq 3
. If
\mu_{2}(G)\geq n
, then

(i)

G
is
2
-connected
;

(ii)
if
\{u,v\}
is a cut of
G
, then
G-\{u,v\}
has exactly two components.

Proof.
(i) Assume for contradiction that
G
is not 2-connected. Then there is a cut-vertex
u
in
G
. Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be two components of
G-u
. Since
G
is connected, there exist vertices
v_{1}\in V(G_{1})
and
v_{2}\in V(G_{2})
such that
d(v_{1},v_{2})=2
. Thus,
n\leq d(v_{1})+d(v_{2})\leq|V(G_{1})|+|V(G_{2})|<n
, a contradiction. Hence,
G
is
2
-connected.

(ii) Assume for contradiction that
\{u,v\}
is a vertex cut of
G
such that
G-\{u,v\}
has three components
G_{1}
,
G_{2}
and
G_{3}
. Then there exist vertices
v_{1}\in V(G_{1})
and
v_{2}\in V(G_{2})
such that
d(v_{1},v_{2})=2
. Thus,
n\leq d(v_{1})+d(v_{2})\leq(|V(G_{1})|+1)+(|V(G_{2})|+1)<n
, a contradiction. So
G-\{u,v\}
has exactly two components.
\hfill\Box

Lemma 2.3

Let
G
be a connected graph of order
n\geq 7
and
\mu_{2}(G)\geq n
. If the connectivity
\kappa(G)=2
, then
G
has two CISTs.

Proof.
Let
\{u,v\}
be a vertex cut of
G
. Then by Lemma
2.2
,
G-\{u,v\}
has exactly two components
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
. Without loss of generality, assume
|V(G_{1})|\leq|V(G_{2})|
.

Since
G
is connected, there are vertices
x_{1},x_{2}\in V(G_{1})
and
y_{1},y_{2}\in V(G_{2})
(maybe
x_{1}=x_{2}
) such that
d(x_{1},y_{1})=d(x_{2},y_{2})=2
. By Lemma
2.1
,
N(x_{i})\cap N(y_{i})=\{u,v\}
,
N(x_{i})=(V(G_{1})\setminus\{x_{i}\})\cup\{u,v\}
and
N(y_{i})=(V(G_{2})\setminus\{y_{i}\})\cup\{u,v\}
for
i\in[2]
.

If
|V(G_{1})|\geq 2
, then let
V_{1}=\{u,y_{1}\}\cup V(G_{1})\setminus\{x_{1}\}
,
V_{2}=\{v,x_{1}\}\cup V(G_{2})\setminus\{y_{1}\}
. Obviously,
G[V_{1}]
,
G[V_{2}]
and
B(V_{1},V_{2})
are connected, and
ux_{1}x_{2}vy_{1}y_{2}u
is a cycle of
B(V_{1},V_{2})
(see Figure 1 (a)). Therefore,
(V_{1},V_{2})
is 2-CIST-partition of
G
.

Figure 1
:
The illustration of a 2-CIST-partition in Lemma
2.3
.

If
|V(G_{1})|=1
, then
|V(G_{2})|\geq 4
. Let
V_{1}=\{u,x_{1},y_{1}\}
and
V_{2}=\{v\}\cup V(G_{2})\setminus\{y_{1}\}
. Obviously,
G[V_{1}]
,
G[V_{2}]
and
B(V_{1},V_{2})
are connected. If
uv\in E(G)
, then
uy_{2}y_{1}vu
is a cycle of
B(V_{1},V_{2})
(see Figure 1 (b)). If
uv\notin E(G)
, then
d(u,v)=2
and
d(u)+d(v)\geq n\geq 7
. So, there must exist a vertex
y_{3}\in V(G_{2})
such that
uy_{3}\in E(G)
or
vy_{3}\in E(G)
. In both cases,
d(x_{1},y_{3})=2
. Then by Lemma
2.1
,
N(y_{3})=(V(G_{2})\setminus\{y_{3}\})\cup\{u,v\}
. Thus
uy_{2}y_{1}y_{3}u
is a cycle of
B(V_{1},V_{2})
(see Figure 1 (c)). Therefore,
(V_{1},V_{2})
is a 2-CIST-partition of
G
.

Therefore, by Theorem
1.1
,
G
has two CISTs.

\hfill\Box

Proof of Theorem
1.7

Let
G
be a graph satisfying the condition of Theorem
1.7
. By Lemma
2.2
,
\kappa(G)\geq 2
. If
G
is a complete graph, then
G
has two CISTs. If
\kappa(G)=2
, then by Lemma
2.3
,
G
has two CISTs.

Next, we assume that
G
is not a complete graph and
\kappa(G)\geq 3
. Then there are two vertices
x,y\in V(G)
such that
d(x,y)=2
. We choose such vertices
x
and
y
satisfying the following conditions:

(C1)
|N(x)\cap N(y)|
is as small as possible;

(C2)
d(x)+d(y)
is as large as possible, subject to (C1).

Without loss of generality, suppose
d(x)\leq d(y)
. Partition
V(G)\setminus\{x,y\}
as follows:

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle=N(x)\cap N(y),

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=N(x)\setminus M,

\displaystyle Y

\displaystyle=N(y)\setminus M,

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle=V(G)\setminus(\{x,y\}\cup M\cup X\cup Y).

Then by Lemma
2.1
, we have
|X|\leq|Y|
and
|M|\geq 2
. Since
n\leq d(x)+d(y)\leq|V(G)\setminus\{x,y\}|+|M|-|D|=n-2+|M|-|D|
, we have
|D|\leq|M|-2
. For convenience, set

X=\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{t}\},~Y=\{y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{s}\},~M=\{u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{m}\}~\text{and}~D=\{v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{d}\}.

By the choice of
x
and
y
, for each pair of vertices
u,v\in V(G)
with
d(u,v)=2
, we have
|N(u)\cap N(v)|\geq m
and
n\leq d(u)+d(v)\leq d(x)+d(y)
.

Claim 3.1

If
D\neq\varnothing
, then each vertex of
D
has at least one neighbor in
M\cup Y
.

Proof.
Suppose not. Let
v_{i}\in D
be a vertex such that
N_{M\cup Y}(v_{i})=\varnothing
. Let
D^{\prime}
be a component of
G[D]
containing
v_{i}
. We can claim that for each vertex
v_{j}\in D
, if
N_{M\cup Y}(v_{j})=\varnothing
, then
N_{X}(v_{j})=\varnothing
, which implies that
N(v_{j})\subseteq D
. Otherwise,
d(x,v_{j})=2
. Hence,
n\leq d(x)+d(v_{j})\leq(|X|+|M|)+(|X|+D|-1)<n
, a contradiction.

Since
\kappa(G)\geq 3
, there is a vertex
v_{j}\in V(D^{\prime})
such that
N_{M\cup Y}(v_{j})\neq\varnothing
. Let
P
be a path in
D^{\prime}
connecting
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
. Then there are two vertices
v_{l},v_{k}\in V(P)
such that
v_{l}v_{k}\in E(P)
,
N_{M\cup Y}(v_{l})\neq\varnothing
and
N_{M\cup Y}(v_{k})=\varnothing
. Let
z\in N_{M\cup Y}(v_{l})
. Then
d(z,v_{k})=2
and
|N(z)\cap N(v_{k})|\geq m
. However,
N(v_{k})\subseteq D
, this contradicts to
m\geq|D|+2
.
\hfill\Box

Claim 3.2

If
\kappa(G)=3
, then for every cut
\{u,v,w\}
of
G
,
G-\{u,v,w\}
has exactly two components.

Proof.
Suppose not. Let
\{u,v,w\}
be a cut of
G
such that
G-\{u,v,w\}
has at least three components. Suppose that
G_{1}
,
G_{2}
and
G_{3}
are three components of
G-\{u,v,w\}
with
|V(G_{1})|\leq|V(G_{2})|\leq|V(G_{3})|
. Since
\{u,v,w\}
is a minimal cut, there are two vertices
z_{1}\in V(G_{1})
and
z_{2}\in V(G_{2})
such that
d(z_{1},z_{2})=2
. Then,
n\leq d(z_{1})+d(z_{2})\leq|V(G_{1})|+|V(G_{2})|+4=n+1-|V(G_{3})|
. So
|V(G_{3})|\leq 1
. So
|V(G_{1})|=|V(G_{2})|=|V(G_{3})|=1
. Therefore,
7\leq n\leq d(z_{1})+d(z_{2})\leq 6
, a contradiction.
\hfill\Box

Case 1

|M|=2
.

In this case, we have
M=\{u_{1},u_{2}\}
and
D=\varnothing
. Since
d(x)\geq\delta(G)\geq\kappa(G)\geq 3
,
X\neq\varnothing
.

Claim 3.3

d(u_{1})\geq 4
or
d(u_{2})\geq 4
.

Proof.
Suppose not. Then
d(u_{1})=d(u_{2})=3
. If
u_{1}u_{2}\in E(G)
, then
N(u_{1})=\{x,y,u_{2}\}
and
N(u_{2})=\{x,y,u_{1}\}
. Then
d(x_{1},u_{1})=2
. However,
N(u_{1})\cap N(x_{1})=\{x\}
, this contradicts to Lemma
2.1
. If
u_{1}u_{2}\notin E(G)
, then
d(u_{1},u_{2})=2
. Thus,
6=d(u_{1})+d(u_{2})\geq n\geq 7
, a contradiction.
\hfill\Box

By Claim
3.3
, we can assume without loss of generality that
d(u_{1})\geq 4
.

Claim 3.4

If
d(u_{2})\geq 4
or
u_{1}u_{2}\notin E(G)
, then
\{x,y,u_{1}\}
is not a cut of
G
.

Proof.
Suppose not. Then, by Claim
3.2
,
G-\{x,y,u_{1}\}
has exactly two components
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
. We can claim that all the vertices of
X
are in the same component. Otherwise, there is a vertex
x_{i}\in V(G_{i})
for
i\in[2]
such that
d(x_{1},x_{2})=2
. By Lemma
2.1
,
N(x_{1})\cap N(x_{2})=\{x,u_{1}\}
,
N(x_{1})\cup N(x_{2})=V(G)\setminus\{x_{1},x_{2}\}
. However,
y\notin N(x_{1})\cup N(x_{2})
, a contradiction. Thus, all the vertices of
X
are in the same component of
G-\{x,y,u_{1}\}
. Similarly, all the vertices of
Y
are in the same component of
G-\{x,y,u_{1}\}
.

Next, we will prove that
X
and
Y
are contained in the same component of
G-\{x,y,u_{1}\}
. Otherwise, without loss of generality, assume
X\subseteq V(G_{1})
and
Y\subseteq V(G_{2})
. Then
G-\{u_{1},y\}
is disconnected if
u_{2}\in V(G_{1})
or
G-\{u_{1},x\}
is disconnected if
u_{2}\in V(G_{2})
. This contradicts to the assumption

. Therefore,

is in the same component. Suppose without loss of generality that
V(G_{1})=X\cup Y
and
V(G_{2})=\{u_{2}\}
. Thus
d(u_{2})=3
and
u_{1}u_{2}\in E(G)
, which contradicts to the condition of Claim
3.4
. Therefore,
\{x,y,u_{1}\}
is not a cut of
G
.
\hfill\Box

Subcase 1.1

d(u_{2})\geq 4
.

Let
V_{1}=\{x,y,u_{1}\}
and
V_{2}=X\cup Y\cup\{u_{2}\}
.
Obviously,
G[V_{1}]
is connected. By Claim
3.4
,
G[V_{2}]
is connected. Since

,

.

|N(u_{1})\cap X|\geq 2

, then

is a cycle of

. Similarly, if
|N(u_{1})\cap Y|\geq 2
, then we can get a cycle of

. If
|N(u_{1})\cap Y|=1
and
|N(u_{1})\cap X|=1
, we assume

, then
xx_{1}u_{1}y_{1}yu_{2}x
is a cycle of
B(V_{1},V_{2})
. If
|N(u_{1})\cap X|=1
and
|N(u_{1})\cap Y|=0
, then
u_{1}u_{2}\in E(G)
and
xx_{1}u_{1}u_{2}x
is a cycle of
B(V_{1},V_{2})
. Similarly, if
|N(u_{1})\cap Y|=0
and
|N(u_{1})\cap X|=1
, then we can get a cycle of
B(V_{1},V_{2})
.
Therefore,
(V_{1},V_{2})
is a 2-CIST-partition of
G
.
Thus, by Theorem
1.1
,
G
has two CISTs.

Subcase 1.2

d(u_{2})=3
and
u_{1}u_{2}\notin E(G)
.

Let
V_{1}
and

be the same as in Subcase 1.1. By similar argument as in Subcase 1.1, we can get that

,

Subcase 1.3

d(u_{2})=3
and
u_{1}u_{2}\in E(G)
.

In this case,
N(u_{2})=\{x,y,u_{1}\}
. Then
d(x_{i},u_{2})=2
for any vertex
x_{i}\in X
and
d(y_{j},u_{2})=2
for any vertex
y_{j}\in Y
. By Lemma

, we have
N(x_{i})=V(G)\setminus\{x_{i},y,u_{2}\}
for each vertex
x_{i}\in X
and
N(y_{j})=V(G)\setminus\{y_{j},x,u_{2}\}

Abstract
We study partial information Nash equilibrium between a broker and an informed trader. In this setting, the informed trader, who possesses knowledge of a trading signal, trades multiple assets with the broker in a dealer market. Simultaneously, the broker offloads these assets in a lit exchange where their actions impact the asset prices. The broker, however, only observes aggregate prices and cannot distinguish between underlying trends and volatility. Both the broker and the informed trader aim to maximize their penalized expected wealth. Using convex analysis, we characterize the Nash equilibrium and demonstrate its existence and uniqueness. Furthermore, we establish that this equilibrium corresponds to the solution of a nonstandard system of forward-backward stochastic differential equations (FBSDEs) that involves the two differing filtrations. For short enough time horizons, we prove that a unique solution of this system exists. Finally, under quite general assumptions, we show that the solution to the FBSDE system admits a polynomial approximation in the strength of the transient impact to arbitrary order, and prove that the error is controlled.

The informed trader has privy to a private trading signal about the assets’ drifts, while the Broker does not. Our setup is similar to the one in

Secondly, we assume the broker has access only to asset prices rather than the full market information. Both the broker and the informed trader aim to maximize their (penalized) expected wealth from trading activities over a fixed time horizon by choosing trading strategies based on their respective information content. This leads to a partial information setting for the Broker, but a full information setting for the informed trader.

We use variational methods to characterize the optimal response of each agent. Based on this optimal response, we prove uniqueness and existence of Nash equilibrium between the broker and the informed trader in the small time setting. As a result, we obtain a filtered system of forward-backward stochastic differential equations (FBSDEs) that characterizes the Nash equilibrium.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}(Q^{B}_{t})^{\top}r^{B}Q^{B}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]\leq\|r^{B}\|\|Q^{B}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}<\infty,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left|-\eta_{t}^{\top}b\eta_{t}+(\alpha_{t}+h\nu_{t}-pY_{t}-2\psi\eta_{t})^{\top}Q^{I}_{t}\right|\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle\leq\|b\|\|\eta\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}+\left(\|\alpha\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}+\|h\|\|\nu\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}+\|p\|\|Y\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}+2\|\psi\|\|\eta\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}\right)\|Q^{I}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}<\infty,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}(Q^{I}_{t})^{\top}r^{I}Q^{I}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]\leq\|r^{I}\|\|Q^{I}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}<\infty,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|x^{B}+(S_{0}-\phi q^{B})^{\top}q^{B}\right|\right]\leq\mathbb{E}[|x^{B}|]+\mathbb{E}[|S_{0}|^{2}]^{1/2}\mathbb{E}[|q^{B}|^{2}]^{1/2}+\|\phi\|\mathbb{E}[|q^{B}|^{2}]<\infty,

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|x^{I}+(S_{0}-\psi q^{I})^{\top}q^{I}\right|\right]\leq\mathbb{E}[|x^{I}|]+\mathbb{E}[|S_{0}|^{2}]^{1/2}\mathbb{E}[|q^{I}|^{2}]^{1/2}+\|\psi\|\mathbb{E}[|q^{I}|^{2}]<\infty.

Therefore,

\displaystyle J^{I}(\nu,\eta)=\mathbb{E}\left[x^{I}+(S_{0}-\psi q^{I})^{\top}q^{I}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left\{-\eta_{t}^{\top}b\eta_{t}+(\alpha_{t}+h\nu_{t}-pY_{t}-2\psi\eta_{t}-r^{I}Q^{I}_{t})^{\top}Q^{I}_{t}\right\}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]\in\mathbb{R}

and

\displaystyle J^{B}(\nu,\eta)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[x^{B}+(S_{0}-\phi q^{B})^{\top}q^{B}\right]

\displaystyle\quad\ +\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left\{-\nu_{t}^{\top}a\nu_{t}+\eta_{t}^{\top}b\eta_{t}+(\alpha_{t}+(h-2\phi)\nu_{t}-pY_{t}+2\phi\eta_{t}-r^{B}Q^{B}_{t})^{\top}Q^{B}_{t}\right\}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]\in\mathbb{R}.

∎

We end this section by defining the notion of Nash equilibrium between the broker and the informed trader.

Definition 2.3
.

A pair
(\nu,\eta)\in\mathbb{H}^{2}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\mathbb{H}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}
is a
Nash equilibrium
between the broker and the informed trader if
\nu
is a maximizer of
J^{B}(\cdot,\eta)
over
\mathbb{H}^{2}_{\mathbb{G}}
and
\eta
is a maximizer of
J^{I}(\nu,\cdot)
over
\mathbb{H}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}
.

3
Convex analysis of the performance criteria

In this section, we study the convexity and differentiability of the borker’s and informed trader’s criteria. Specifically, we show
J^{I}
and
J^{B}
are strictly concave and Gâuteaux-differentiable in the component over which the corresponding agent maximizes. We then characterize the maximizers in terms of the Gâuteaux derivatives.

First, we obtain the concavity of the informed trader’s criterion.

Proposition 3.1
.

For
\nu\in\mathbb{H}^{2}_{\mathbb{G}}
, the functional
J^{I}(\nu,\cdot):\mathbb{H}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}\to\mathbb{R}
is concave.

Proof.

Fix
\nu\in\mathbb{H}^{2}_{\mathbb{G}}
and
\eta,\kappa\in\mathbb{H}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}
. Let
\rho\in(0,1)
. First, we have

\displaystyle Q^{I,\rho\eta+(1-\rho)\kappa}_{t}=q^{I}+\int_{0}^{t}((\rho\eta_{s}+(1-\rho)\kappa_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\rho\,\left(q^{I}+\int_{0}^{t}\eta_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)+(1-\rho)\,\left(q^{I}+\int_{0}^{t}\kappa_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)

\displaystyle=\rho\,Q^{I,\eta}_{t}+(1-\rho)\,Q^{I,\kappa}_{t}.

Next, by lemma
2.2
,

\displaystyle J^{I}(\nu,\rho\eta+(1-\rho)\kappa)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[x^{I}+(S_{0}-\psi q^{I})^{\top}q^{I}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left\{(\alpha_{t}+h\nu_{t}-pY_{t})^{\top}\left(\rho Q^{I,\eta}_{t}+(1-\rho)Q^{I,\kappa}_{t}\right)\right\}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle\quad\ +\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left\{-(\rho\eta_{t}+(1-\rho)\kappa_{t})^{\top}b(\rho\eta_{t}+(1-\rho)\kappa_{t})\right\}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle\quad\ +\rho\,\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left(-2\psi\eta_{t}-r^{I}Q^{I,\eta}_{t}\right)^{\top}\left(\rho Q^{I,\eta}_{t}+(1-\rho)Q^{I,\kappa}_{t}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle\quad\ +(1-\rho)\,\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left(-2\psi\kappa_{t}-r^{I}Q^{I,\kappa}_{t}\right)^{\top}\left(\rho Q^{I,\eta}_{t}+(1-\rho)Q^{I,\kappa}_{t}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle=\rho\,J^{I}(\nu,\eta)+(1-\rho)\,J^{I}(\nu,\kappa)

\displaystyle\quad\ +\rho\,(1-\rho)\,\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left\{\eta_{t}^{\top}b\eta_{t}+\kappa_{t}^{\top}b\kappa_{t}-\eta_{t}^{\top}b\kappa_{t}-\kappa_{t}^{\top}b\eta_{t}\right\}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle\quad\ +\rho\,(1-\rho)\,\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left(-2\psi\eta_{t}-r^{I}Q^{I,\eta}_{t}\right)^{\top}\left(-Q^{I,\eta}_{t}+Q^{I,\kappa}_{t}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle\quad\ +\rho\,(1-\rho)\,\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left(-2\psi\kappa_{t}-r^{I}Q^{I,\kappa}_{t}\right)^{\top}\left(Q^{I,\eta}_{t}-Q^{I,\kappa}_{t}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle=\rho\,J^{I}(\nu,\eta)+(1-\rho)\,J^{I}(\nu,\kappa)+\rho\,(1-\rho)\,\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}(\eta_{t}-\kappa_{t})^{\top}b(\eta_{t}-\kappa_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle\quad\ +2\,\rho\,(1-\rho)\,\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}(\eta_{t}-\kappa_{t})^{\top}\psi\left(Q^{I,\eta}_{t}-Q^{I,\kappa}_{t}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle\quad\ +\rho\,(1-\rho)\,\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left(Q^{I,\eta}_{t}-Q^{I,\kappa}_{t}\right)^{\top}r^{I}\left(Q^{I,\eta}_{t}-Q^{I,\kappa}_{t}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t\right].

(3.1)

Notice

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}(\eta_{t}-\kappa_{t})^{\top}\psi\left(Q^{I,\eta}_{t}-Q^{I,\kappa}_{t}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{t}(\eta_{t}-\kappa_{t})^{\top}\psi(\eta_{s}-\kappa_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\right].

(3.2)

Further, as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{t}\left|(\eta_{t}-\kappa_{t})^{\top}\psi(\eta_{s}-\kappa_{s})\right|\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle\leq\|\psi\|\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{t}|\eta_{s}-\kappa_{s}||\eta_{t}-\kappa_{t}|\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle\leq\|\psi\|\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}|\eta_{t}-\kappa_{t}|\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq T\|\psi\|\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}|\eta_{t}-\kappa_{t}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]=T\|\psi\|\|\eta-\kappa\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}<\infty,

Fubini’s theorem implies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{t}(\eta_{t}-\kappa_{t})^{\top}\psi(\eta_{s}-\kappa_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\int_{s}^{T}(\eta_{t}-\kappa_{t})^{\top}\psi(\eta_{s}-\kappa_{s})\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}s\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\int_{t}^{T}(\eta_{s}-\kappa_{s})^{\top}\psi(\eta_{t}-\kappa_{t})\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\int_{t}^{T}\left((\eta_{s}-\kappa_{s})^{\top}\psi(\eta_{t}-\kappa_{t})\right)^{\top}\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\int_{t}^{T}(\eta_{t}-\kappa_{t})^{\top}\psi(\eta_{s}-\kappa_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\right].

Combining this with (
3.2
) gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}(\eta_{t}-\kappa_{t})^{\top}\psi\left(Q^{I,\eta}_{t}-Q^{I,\kappa}_{t}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}(\eta_{t}-\kappa_{t})^{\top}\psi(\eta_{s}-\kappa_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(Q^{I,\eta}_{T}-Q^{I,\kappa}_{T}\right)^{\top}\psi\left(Q^{I,\eta}_{T}-Q^{I,\kappa}_{T}\right)\right]\geq 0.

(3.3)

due to the positive semi-definiteness of
\psi
. As
r^{I}
and
b
are positive semi-definite, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left(Q^{I,\eta}_{t}-Q^{I,\kappa}_{t}\right)^{\top}r^{I}\left(Q^{I,\eta}_{t}-Q^{I,\kappa}_{t}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t\right]\geq 0

(3.4)

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}(\eta_{t}-\kappa_{t})^{\top}b(\eta_{t}-\kappa_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t\right]\geq 0.

(3.5)

Combining (
3.1
), (
3.3
), (
3.4
), and (
3.5
) gives

\displaystyle J^{I}(\nu,\rho\eta+(1-\rho)\kappa)\geq\rho J^{I}(\nu,\eta)+(1-\rho)J^{I}(\nu,\kappa).

∎

Second, we obtain concavity of the broker’s criterion.
Note that, unlike in
[
9
]
, we obtain the following result without any further restriction on
a
,
p
,
h
, and
T
.

Proposition 3.2
.

For
\eta\in\mathbb{H}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}
, the functional
J^{B}(\cdot,\eta):\mathbb{H}^{2}_{\mathbb{G}}\to\mathbb{R}
is concave.

Proof.

Fix
\eta\in\mathbb{H}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}
and
\nu,\zeta\in\mathbb{H}^{2}_{\mathbb{G}}
. Let
\rho\in(0,1)
. First, we have

\displaystyle Q^{B,\rho\nu+(1-\rho)\zeta}_{t}

\displaystyle=q^{B}+\int_{0}^{t}((\rho\nu_{s}+(1-\rho)\zeta_{s}-\eta_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\rho\left(q^{B}+\int_{0}^{t}(\nu_{s}-\eta_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s\right)+(1-\rho)\left(q^{B}+\int_{0}^{t}(\zeta_{s}-\eta_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s\right)=\rho\,Q^{B,\nu}_{t}+(1-\rho)\,Q^{B,\zeta}_{t}

and

\displaystyle Y^{\rho\nu+(1-\rho)\zeta}_{t}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-tp}y+\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{(s-t)p}h(\rho\nu_{s}+(1-\rho)\zeta_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\rho\left(\mathrm{e}^{-tp}y+\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{(s-t)p}h\nu_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)+(1-\rho)\left(\mathrm{e}^{-tp}y+\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{(s-t)p}h\zeta_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)

\displaystyle=\rho Y^{\nu}_{t}+(1-\rho)Y^{\zeta}_{t}.

Next, by Lemma
2.2
,

\displaystyle J^{B}(\rho\nu+(1-\rho)\zeta,\eta)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[x^{B}+(S_{0}-\phi q^{B})^{\top}q^{B}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left\{\eta_{t}^{\top}b\eta_{t}+(\alpha_{t}+2\phi\eta_{t})^{\top}\left(\rho Q^{B,\nu}_{t}+(1-\rho)Q^{B,\zeta}_{t}\right)\right\}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle\quad\ +\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left\{-(\rho\nu_{t}+(1-\rho)\zeta_{t})^{\top}a(\rho\nu_{t}+(1-\rho)\zeta_{t})\right\}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle\quad\ +\rho\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left((h-2\phi)\nu_{t}-pY^{\nu}_{t}-r^{B}Q^{B,\nu}_{t}\right)^{\top}\left(\rho Q^{B,\nu}_{t}+(1-\rho)Q^{B,\zeta}_{t}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle\quad\ +(1-\rho)\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left((h-2\phi)\zeta_{t}-pY^{\zeta}_{t}-r^{B}Q^{B,\zeta}_{t}\right)^{\top}\left(\rho Q^{B,\nu}_{t}+(1-\rho)Q^{B,\zeta}_{t}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle=\rho J^{B}(\nu,\eta)+(1-\rho)J^{B}(\zeta,\eta)

\displaystyle\quad\ +\rho(1-\rho)\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left\{\nu_{t}^{\top}a\nu_{t}+\zeta_{t}^{\top}a\zeta_{t}-\nu_{t}^{\top}a\zeta_{t}-\zeta_{t}^{\top}a\nu_{t}\right\}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle\quad\ +\rho(1-\rho)\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left((h-2\phi)\nu_{t}-pY^{\nu}_{t}-r^{B}Q^{B,\nu}_{t}\right)^{\top}\left(-Q^{B,\nu}_{t}+Q^{B,\zeta}_{t}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle\quad\ +\rho(1-\rho)\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left((h-2\phi)\zeta_{t}-pY^{\zeta}_{t}-r^{B}Q^{B,\zeta}_{t}\right)^{\top}\left(Q^{B,\nu}_{t}-Q^{B,\zeta}_{t}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle=\rho J^{B}(\nu,\eta)+(1-\rho)J^{B}(\zeta,\eta)+\rho(1-\rho)\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}(\nu_{t}-\zeta_{t})^{\top}a(\nu_{t}-\zeta_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle\quad\ +\rho(1-\rho)\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}(\nu_{t}-\zeta_{t})^{\top}(2\phi-h)\left(Q^{B,\nu}_{t}-Q^{B,\zeta}_{t}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle\quad\ +\rho(1-\rho)\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left(Y^{\nu}_{t}-Y^{\zeta}_{t}\right)^{\top}p\left(Q^{B,\nu}_{t}-Q^{B,\zeta}_{t}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle\quad\ +\rho(1-\rho)\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left(Q^{B,\nu}_{t}-Q^{B,\zeta}_{t}\right)^{\top}r^{B}\left(Q^{B,\nu}_{t}-Q^{B,\zeta}_{t}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t\right].

(3.6)

Notice

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}(\nu_{t}-\zeta_{t})^{\top}(2\phi-h)\left(Q^{B,\nu}_{t}-Q^{B,\zeta}_{t}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{t}(\nu_{t}-\zeta_{t})^{\top}(2\phi-h)(\nu_{s}-\zeta_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\right].

(3.7)

Further, as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{t}\left|(\nu_{t}-\zeta_{t})^{\top}(2\phi-h)(\nu_{s}-\zeta_{s})\right|\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle\leq\|2\phi-h\|\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{t}|\nu_{s}-\zeta_{s}||\nu_{t}-\zeta_{t}|\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle\leq\|2\phi-h\|\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}|\nu_{t}-\zeta_{t}|\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq T\|2\phi-h\|\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}|\nu_{t}-\zeta_{t}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle=T\|2\phi-h\|\|\nu-\zeta\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}<\infty,

Fubini’s theorem implies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{t}(\nu_{t}-\zeta_{t})^{\top}(2\phi-h)(\nu_{s}-\zeta_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\int_{s}^{T}(\nu_{t}-\zeta_{t})^{\top}(2\phi-h)(\nu_{s}-\zeta_{s})\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}s\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\int_{t}^{T}(\nu_{s}-\zeta_{s})^{\top}(2\phi-h)(\nu_{t}-\zeta_{t})\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\int_{t}^{T}\left((\nu_{s}-\zeta_{s})^{\top}(2\phi-h)(\nu_{t}-\zeta_{t})\right)^{\top}\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\int_{t}^{T}(\nu_{t}-\zeta_{t})^{\top}(2\phi-h)(\nu_{s}-\zeta_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\right].

Combining this and (
3.7
) gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}(\nu_{t}-\zeta_{t})^{\top}(2\phi-h)\left(Q^{B,\nu}_{t}-Q^{B,\zeta}_{t}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}(\nu_{t}-\zeta_{t})^{\top}(2\phi-h)(\nu_{s}-\zeta_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(Q^{B,\nu}_{T}-Q^{B,\zeta}_{T}\right)^{\top}(2\phi-h)\left(Q^{B,\nu}_{T}-Q^{B,\zeta}_{T}\right)\right]\geq 0,

(3.8)

due to the positive semi-definiteness of
2\phi-h
. Since
r^{B}
and
a
are positive semi-definite, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left(Q^{B,\nu}_{t}-Q^{B,\zeta}_{t}\right)^{\top}r^{B}\left(Q^{B,\nu}_{t}-Q^{B,\zeta}_{t}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t\right]\geq 0

(3.9)

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}(\nu_{t}-\zeta_{t})^{\top}a(\nu_{t}-\zeta_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t\right]\geq 0.

(3.10)

The dynamics of
Y
and
Q^{B}
implies

Y^{\nu}_{t}-Y^{\zeta}_{t}=h\int_{0}^{t}(\nu_{s}-\zeta_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s-p\int_{0}^{t}\left(Y^{\nu}_{s}-Y^{\zeta}_{s}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s=h\left(Q^{B,\nu}_{t}-Q^{B,\zeta}_{t}\right)-p\int_{0}^{t}\left(Y^{\nu}_{s}-Y^{\zeta}_{s}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s.

(3.11)

Since
p
and
h
commute,
\mathrm{e}^{up}
and
h
commute as well, thus from (
2.3
) we see that

\displaystyle Y^{\nu}_{t}-Y^{\zeta}_{t}=h\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{(s-t)p}(\nu_{s}-\zeta_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s.

Let
h^{\dagger}
be the pseudoinverse of
h
. Since
p
and
h
are positive semi-definite and commute, they are simultaneously diagonalizable (see, for example, Theorem 5.76 of
[
4
]
), that is, there is an invertible
K\times K
matrix
O
such that
p=O\hat{p}O^{-1}
and
h=O\hat{h}O^{-1}
, where
\hat{p}
and
\hat{h}
are diagonal matrices whose diagonals consist of eigenvalues of
p
and
h
, which are nonnegative, respectively. Then
h^{\dagger}=O\hat{h}^{\dagger}O^{-1}
, where
\hat{h}^{\dagger}
is the diagonal matrix obtained by replacing each nonzero entry of
\hat{h}
with its reciprocal. Thus
h^{\dagger}
is positive semi-definite. Since
ph^{\dagger}=O\hat{p}O^{-1}O\hat{h}^{\dagger}O^{-1}=O\hat{p}\hat{h}^{\dagger}O^{-1}
,
ph^{\dagger}
is positive semi-definite. It follows from (
3.11
) that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left(Y^{\nu}_{t}-Y^{\zeta}_{t}\right)^{\top}p\left(Q^{B,\nu}_{t}-Q^{B,\zeta}_{t}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{(s-t)p}(\nu_{s}-\zeta_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\top}hp\left(Q^{B,\nu}_{t}-Q^{B,\zeta}_{t}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{(s-t)p}(\nu_{s}-\zeta_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\top}ph\left(Q^{B,\nu}_{t}-Q^{B,\zeta}_{t}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{(s-t)p}(\nu_{s}-\zeta_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\top}phh^{\dagger}h\left(Q^{B,\nu}_{t}-Q^{B,\zeta}_{t}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{(s-t)p}(\nu_{s}-\zeta_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\top}phh^{\dagger}\left(Y^{\nu}_{t}-Y^{\zeta}_{t}+p\int_{0}^{t}\left(Y^{\nu}_{s}-Y^{\zeta}_{s}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s\right)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{(s-t)p}(\nu_{s}-\zeta_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\top}hph^{\dagger}\left(Y^{\nu}_{t}-Y^{\zeta}_{t}+p\int_{0}^{t}\left(Y^{\nu}_{s}-Y^{\zeta}_{s}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s\right)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\left(Y^{\nu}_{t}-Y^{\zeta}_{t}\right)^{\top}ph^{\dagger}\left(Y^{\nu}_{t}-Y^{\zeta}_{t}+p\int_{0}^{t}\left(Y^{\nu}_{s}-Y^{\zeta}_{s}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s\right)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\left(Y^{\nu}_{t}-Y^{\zeta}_{t}\right)^{\top}ph^{\dagger}\left(Y^{\nu}_{t}-Y^{\zeta}_{t}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\left(Y^{\nu}_{t}-Y^{\zeta}_{t}\right)^{\top}ph^{\dagger}p\int_{0}^{t}\left(Y^{\nu}_{s}-Y^{\zeta}_{s}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\left(Y^{\nu}_{t}-Y^{\zeta}_{t}\right)^{\top}ph^{\dagger}\left(Y^{\nu}_{t}-Y^{\zeta}_{t}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{2}\left(p\int_{0}^{T}\left(Y^{\nu}_{t}-Y^{\zeta}_{t}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\top}h^{\dagger}p\int_{0}^{T}\left(Y^{\nu}_{t}-Y^{\zeta}_{t}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t\geq 0\,.

(3.12)

Combining (
3.6
), (
3.8
), (
3.9
), (
3.10
), and (
3.12
) gives

\displaystyle J^{B}(\rho\nu+(1-\rho)\zeta,\eta)\geq\rho\,J^{B}(\nu,\eta)+(1-\rho)\,J^{B}(\zeta,\eta).

∎

Next, we provide some important results regarding Gâteaux-differentiability and maximizing concave functions. For a real vector space
V
and a functional
f
on
V
, we say
f
is
Gâteaux-differentiable
at
v\in V
if there exists a linear functional
v^{*}
on
V
such that the canonical pairing

\displaystyle\langle v^{*},u\rangle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{f(v+\varepsilon u)-f(v)}{\varepsilon}

for all
u\in V
, in which case
v^{*}
is called the
Gâteaux-derivative
of
f
at
v
and is denoted by
\mathcal{D}f(v)
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
V
be a vector space and
f
be a concave functional on
V
. Suppose
f
is Gâteaux-differentiable at
v\in V
. Then
f
has a maximum at
v
if and only if
\mathcal{D}f(v)=0
.

Proof.

Suppose
f
has a maximum at
v
. Let
u\in V
. For
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\frac{f(v+\varepsilon u)-f(v)}{\varepsilon}\leq 0

and

\displaystyle\frac{f(v-\varepsilon u)-f(v)}{\varepsilon}\leq 0.

Hence

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{D}f(v),u\rangle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{f(v+\varepsilon u)-f(v)}{\varepsilon}\leq 0

and

\displaystyle-\langle\mathcal{D}f(v),u\rangle=\langle\mathcal{D}f(v),-u\rangle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{f(v-\varepsilon u)-f(v)}{\varepsilon}\leq 0,

where the linearity of
\mathcal{D}f(v)
is used in the second calculation. This means
\langle\mathcal{D}f(v),u\rangle=0
. Hence
\mathcal{D}f(v)=0
.

Conversely, suppose
\mathcal{D}f(v)=0
. Let
u\in V
. For
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle f(v)

\displaystyle=f(u)-\frac{\varepsilon f(u)-\varepsilon f(v)}{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=f(u)-\frac{\varepsilon f(u)+(1-\varepsilon)f(v)-f(v)}{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\geq f(u)-\frac{f(\varepsilon u+(1-\varepsilon)v)-f(v)}{\varepsilon}=f(u)-\frac{f(v+\varepsilon(u-v))-f(v)}{\varepsilon},

where the concavity of
f
is used for the inequality. Taking
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
gives
f(v)\geq f(u)-\langle\mathcal{D}f(v),u-v\rangle=f(u)
. Hence
f
has a maximum at
v
.
∎

The next two propositions pertain to the Gâteaux-differentiability of the informed trader’s and the broker’s performance criteria.

Proposition 3.4
.

For
\nu\in\mathbb{H}^{2}_{\mathbb{G}}
,
J^{I}(\nu,\cdot):\mathbb{H}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}\to\mathbb{R}
is Gâteaux-differentiable at every
\eta\in\mathbb{H}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}
with Gâteaux-derivative given by

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{D}J^{I}(\nu,\cdot)|_{\eta},\kappa\rangle=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\kappa_{t}^{\top}\left(-2b\eta_{t}-2\psi Q^{I,\eta}_{t}+\int_{t}^{T}\left(\alpha_{s}+h\nu_{s}-pY_{s}-2\psi\eta_{s}-2r^{I}Q^{I,\eta}_{s}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s\right)\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

for all
\kappa\in\mathbb{H}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}
.

Proof.

Fix
\nu\in\mathbb{H}^{2}_{\mathbb{G}}
and
\eta,\kappa\in\mathbb{H}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}
. For
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle Q^{I,\eta+\varepsilon\kappa}_{t}=q^{I}+\int_{0}^{t}(\eta_{s}+\varepsilon\kappa_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s=q^{I}+\int_{0}^{t}\eta_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\kappa_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s=Q^{I,\eta}_{t}+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\kappa_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s,

so

\displaystyle J^{I}(\nu,\eta+\varepsilon\kappa)-J^{I}(\nu,\eta)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left\{-(\eta_{t}+\varepsilon\kappa_{t})^{\top}b(\eta_{t}+\varepsilon\kappa_{t})+\eta_{t}^{\top}b\eta_{t}+(\alpha_{t}+h\nu_{t}-pY_{t})^{\top}\left(\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\kappa_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)\right\}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle\quad\ +\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}-2\left\{(\eta_{t}+\varepsilon\kappa_{t})^{\top}\psi\left(Q^{I,\eta}_{t}+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\kappa_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)-\eta_{t}^{\top}\psi Q^{I,\eta}_{t}\right\}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle\quad\ +\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}-\left\{\left(Q^{I,\eta}_{t}+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\kappa_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\top}r^{I}\left(Q^{I,\eta}_{t}+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\kappa_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)-\left(Q^{I,\eta}_{t}\right)^{\top}r^{I}Q^{I,\eta}_{t}\right\}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\kappa_{t}^{\top}\left(-2b\eta_{t}-2\psi Q^{I,\eta}_{t}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{t}\kappa_{s}^{\top}\left(\alpha_{t}+h\nu_{t}-pY_{t}-2\psi\eta_{t}-2r^{I}Q^{I,\eta}_{t}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle\quad\ -\varepsilon^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left\{\kappa_{t}^{\top}b\kappa_{t}+2\kappa_{t}^{\top}\psi\int_{0}^{t}\kappa_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s+\left(\int_{0}^{t}\kappa_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\top}r^{I}\int_{0}^{t}\kappa_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s\right\}\right]

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\kappa_{t}^{\top}\left(-2b\eta_{t}-2\psi Q^{I,\eta}_{t}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{t}^{T}\kappa_{t}^{\top}\left(\alpha_{s}+h\nu_{s}-pY_{s}-2\psi\eta_{s}-2r^{I}Q^{I,\eta}_{s}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle\quad\ -\varepsilon^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left\{\kappa_{t}^{\top}b\kappa_{t}+2\kappa_{t}^{\top}\psi\int_{0}^{t}\kappa_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s+\left(\int_{0}^{t}\kappa_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\top}r^{I}\int_{0}^{t}\kappa_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s\right\}\right]

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\kappa_{t}^{\top}\left\{-2b\eta_{t}-2\psi Q^{I,\eta}_{t}+\int_{t}^{T}\left(\alpha_{s}+h\nu_{s}-pY_{s}-2\psi\eta_{s}-2r^{I}Q^{I,\eta}_{s}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s\right\}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle\quad\ -\varepsilon^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left\{\kappa_{t}^{\top}b\kappa_{t}+2\kappa_{t}^{\top}\psi\int_{0}^{t}\kappa_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s+\left(\int_{0}^{t}\kappa_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\top}r^{I}\int_{0}^{t}\kappa_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s\right\}\right].

It follows that

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{D}J^{I}(\nu,\cdot)|_{\eta},\kappa\rangle

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{J^{I}(\nu,\eta+\varepsilon\kappa)-J^{I}(\nu,\eta)}{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\kappa_{t}^{\top}\left\{-2b\eta_{t}-2\psi Q^{I,\eta}_{t}+\int_{t}^{T}\left(\alpha_{s}+h\nu_{s}-pY_{s}-2\psi\eta_{s}-2r^{I}Q^{I,\eta}_{s}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s\right\}\,\mathrm{d}t\right].

The map
\kappa\mapsto\langle\mathcal{D}J^{I}(\nu,\cdot)|_{\eta},\kappa\rangle
is clearly linear.
∎

Proposition 3.5

For

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
be a complete measure space on which a filtration
\mathbb{Y}=(\mathcal{Y}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
satisfying the usual conditions is defined. Then for each
\mathbb{R}^{K}
-valued jointly measurable process
\xi
such that
\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}|\xi_{t}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]<\infty
, there is a unique
\hat{\xi}\in\mathbb{H}^{2}_{\mathbb{Y}}
such that
\hat{\xi}_{t}=\mathbb{E}[\xi_{t}|\mathcal{Y}_{t}]
a.s. for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
. We call
\hat{\xi}
the
projection of
\xi
onto
\mathbb{Y}
.

Proof.

To see uniqueness, suppose both
\hat{\xi}
and
\tilde{\xi}
satisfy the desired properties. Then
\hat{\xi}_{t}-\tilde{\xi}_{t}=0
a.s. for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
, therefore

\displaystyle\|\hat{\xi}-\tilde{\xi}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left|\hat{\xi}_{t}-\tilde{\xi}_{t}\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]=\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\hat{\xi}_{t}-\tilde{\xi}_{t}\right|^{2}\right]\,\mathrm{d}t=0.

To see existence, first assume
\xi
is an elementary processes such that

\displaystyle\xi_{t}(\omega)=\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\xi^{(i)}(\omega)\mathbf{1}_{(t_{i},t_{i+1}]}(t),

where
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{p}=T
, and
\xi^{(i)}
are bounded
\mathcal{F}
-measurable random variables. For each
i
, consider the
(\mathcal{Y}_{t})_{t\in[t_{i},t_{i+1}]}
-martingale
(\hat{\xi}^{(i)}_{t})_{t\in[t_{i},t_{i+1}]}
defined by
\hat{\xi}^{(i)}_{t}=\mathbb{E}[\xi^{(i)}|\mathcal{Y}_{t}]
, which can be chosen to have càd-làg paths by Theorem
3.6
because
\mathbb{Y}
satisfies the usual conditions. By further setting
\hat{\xi}^{(i)}_{t}=0
for
t\in[0,t_{i})
and
\hat{\xi}^{(i)}_{t}=\hat{\xi}^{(i)}_{t_{i+1}}
for
t\in(t_{i+1},T]
, we obtain a
\mathbb{Y}
-adapted càd-làg process
(\hat{\xi}^{(i)}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
, which is
\mathbb{Y}
-progressively measurable. Define the process
(\hat{\xi}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
by

\displaystyle\hat{\xi}_{t}(\omega)=\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\hat{\xi}^{(i)}_{t}(\omega)\mathbf{1}_{(t_{i},t_{i+1}]}(t).

Then
\hat{\xi}
is a
\mathbb{Y}
-progressively measurable process as a sum of products of
\mathbb{Y}
-progressively measurable processes. By construction,
\hat{\xi}_{t}=\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\xi_{t}\,\right|\,\mathcal{Y}_{t}\right]
a.s. for all
t\in[0,T]
.

Next, let
\xi
be an arbitrary jointly measurable process s.t.
\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}|\xi_{t}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]<\infty
. By considering
\xi
as an adapted process with respect to its natural filtration
\mathbb{Y}^{\xi}
, it has a modification
\bar{\xi}\in\mathbb{H}^{2}_{\mathbb{Y}^{\xi}}
. By Proposition 5.3 in
[
18
]
, there is a sequence
(\xi^{[n]})_{n}
of elementary processes converging to
\bar{\xi}
in
\mathbb{H}^{2}
. By the argument above, there is a
\mathbb{Y}
-progressively measurable process
\hat{\xi}^{[n]}
such that
\hat{\xi}^{[n]}_{t}=\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\xi^{[n]}_{t}\,\right|\,\mathcal{Y}_{t}\right]
a.s. for all
t\in[0,T]
for each
n
. Since

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left|\hat{\xi}^{[n]}_{t}-\hat{\xi}^{[m]}_{t}\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\mathbb{E}[\xi^{[n]}_{t}|\mathcal{Y}_{t}]-\mathbb{E}[\xi^{[m]}_{t}|\mathcal{Y}_{t}]\right|^{2}\right]\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\left|\xi^{[n]}_{t}-\xi^{[m]}_{t}\right|^{2}\right|\mathcal{Y}_{t}\right]\right]\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\xi^{[n]}_{t}-\xi^{[m]}_{t}\right|^{2}\right]\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left|\xi^{[n]}_{t}-\xi^{[m]}_{t}\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right],

(\hat{\xi}^{[n]})_{n}
is Cauchy in
\mathbb{H}^{2}
, thus converging to a process
\hat{\xi}\in\mathbb{H}^{2}_{\mathbb{Y}}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to 0}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left|\hat{\xi}^{[n]}_{t}-\hat{\xi}_{t}\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]=\lim_{n\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\hat{\xi}^{[n]}_{t}-\hat{\xi}_{t}\right|^{2}\right]\,\mathrm{d}t=0.

This implies there is a subsequence
(n_{k})_{k}
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\hat{\xi}^{[n_{k}]}_{t}-\hat{\xi}_{t}\right|^{2}\right]=0\quad\text{for a.e. }t\in[0,T].

Recall that
(\xi^{[n]})_{n}
converges to
\bar{\xi}
in
\mathbb{H}^{2}
, so there is a further subsequence
(n_{k_{j}})_{j}
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\xi^{[n_{k_{j}}]}_{t}-\bar{\xi}_{t}\right|^{2}\right]=0\quad\text{for a.e. }t\in[0,T].

It follows that for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\hat{\xi}_{t}-\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\bar{\xi}_{t}\,\right|\,\mathcal{Y}_{t}\right]\right|^{2}\right]\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\hat{\xi}_{t}-\hat{\xi}^{[n_{k_{j}}]}_{t}\right|^{2}\right]\right)^{1/2}+\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\hat{\xi}^{[n_{k_{j}}]}_{t}-\mathbb{E}[\bar{\xi}_{t}|\mathcal{Y}_{t}]\right|^{2}\right]\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\hat{\xi}_{t}-\hat{\xi}^{[n_{k_{j}}]}_{t}\right|^{2}\right]\right)^{1/2}+\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\xi^{[n_{k_{j}}]}_{t}-\bar{\xi}_{t}\right|\mathcal{Y}_{t}\right]\right|^{2}\right]\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\hat{\xi}_{t}-\hat{\xi}^{[n_{k_{j}}]}_{t}\right|^{2}\right]\right)^{1/2}+\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\xi^{[n_{k_{j}}]}_{t}-\bar{\xi}_{t}\right|^{2}\right]\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{j\to\infty}0,

meaning that
\hat{\xi}_{s}=\mathbb{E}[\bar{\xi}_{s}|\mathcal{Y}_{s}]=\mathbb{E}[\xi_{s}|\mathcal{Y}_{s}]
a.s.
∎

4
Nash Equilibria

In this section, we characterize the existence and uniqueness of Nash equilibria in a small time regime and then further characterize the equilibria as a system of FBSDEs.

Theorem 4.1
.

If the time horizon
T
satisfies
C(T)<1
, where

\displaystyle C(T)\coloneqq T^{2}\max\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\|a^{-1}\|^{2}\left(2\|\phi\|+\|h\|\right)^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\|b^{-1}\|^{2}+1\right)\|h\|^{2}+1,\\
\|a^{-1}\|^{2}(2\|\phi\|+2\|h\|)^{2}+4\|b^{-1}\|^{2}\|\psi\|^{2}+\tfrac{3}{2},\\
\tfrac{1}{2}\|p\|^{2}\left(\|a^{-1}\|^{2}+\|b^{-1}\|^{2}\right),\\
\tfrac{1}{2}\|a^{-1}\|^{2}\left(\|h\|\|p\|+2\|r^{B}\|\right)^{2},\\
2\|b^{-1}\|^{2}\|r^{I}\|^{2}\end{array}\right\},

then there exists a unique Nash equilibrium between the broker and the informed trader.

Proof.

Suppose
C(T)
defined in the statement of the theorem satisfies
C(T)<1
. Equip
\mathbb{T}\coloneqq\mathbb{H}^{2}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\mathbb{H}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}\times\mathbb{H}^{2}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\mathbb{H}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}\times\mathbb{H}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}
with the norm

\|(\nu,\eta,Y,Q^{B},Q^{I})\|_{\mathbb{T}}\coloneqq\left(\|\nu\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}+\|\eta\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}+\|Y\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}+\|Q^{B}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}+\|Q^{I}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}\right)^{1/2},

Then
(\mathbb{T},\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{T}})
is a Banach space. For
\Upsilon=(\nu,\eta,Y,Q^{B},Q^{I})\in\mathbb{T}
, define the quintuple
\Phi
of processes in the following way:
\Phi_{\cdot,1}(\Upsilon)
is the element in
\mathbb{H}^{2}_{\mathbb{G}}
such that

\displaystyle\Phi_{t,1}(\Upsilon)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}a^{-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\left.-(2\phi-h)\left(q^{B}+\int_{0}^{T}(\nu_{s}-\eta_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s\right)+\int_{t}^{T}\left(\alpha_{s}+h\eta_{s}-pY_{s}-\left(h\mathrm{e}^{(t-s)p}p+2r^{B}\right)Q^{B}_{s}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s\right|\mathcal{G}_{t}\right]

a.s. for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
;
\Phi_{\cdot,2}(\Upsilon)
is the element in
\mathbb{H}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}
such that

\displaystyle\Phi_{t,2}(\Upsilon)=\tfrac{1}{2}b^{-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\left.-2\psi\left(q^{I}+\int_{0}^{T}\eta_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)+\int_{t}^{T}\left(\alpha_{s}+h\nu_{s}-pY_{s}-2r^{I}Q^{I}_{s}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s\right|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]

a.s. for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
;

\displaystyle\Phi_{t,3}(\Upsilon)=\mathrm{e}^{-tp}y+\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{(s-t)p}h\nu_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s,

\displaystyle\Phi_{t,4}(\Upsilon)=q^{B}+\int_{0}^{t}(\nu_{s}-\eta_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s,\qquad\text{and}

\displaystyle\Phi_{t,5}(\Upsilon)=q^{I}+\int_{0}^{t}\eta_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s.

By Lemma
2.1
,
\Phi
is a map from
\mathbb{T}
to itself. By Proposition
3.8
and Proposition
3.9
, there exists a unique Nash equilibrium between the broker and the informed trader if
\Phi
admits a unique fixed point, i.e., there exists a unique
\Upsilon^{*}=(\nu^{*},Y^{*},\eta^{*},Q^{B*},Q^{I*})\in\mathbb{T}
such that
\Phi(\Upsilon^{*})=\Upsilon^{*}
. We next show that such a point does exist uniquely.

Take
\Upsilon=(\nu,Y,\eta,Q^{B},Q^{I})\in\mathbb{T}
and
\hat{\Upsilon}=(\hat{\nu},\hat{Y},\hat{\eta},\hat{Q}^{B},\hat{Q}^{I})\in\mathbb{T}
. Then for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\Phi_{t,1}(\Upsilon)-\Phi_{t,1}(\hat{\Upsilon})\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\tfrac{1}{2}a^{-1}\mathbb{E}\left[-(2\phi-h)\int_{0}^{T}\left((\nu_{s}-\hat{\nu}_{s})-(\eta_{s}-\hat{\eta}_{s})\right)\,\mathrm{d}s\right.\right.\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 100.00015pt+\left.\left.\left.\left.\int_{t}^{T}\left(h(\eta_{s}-\hat{\eta}_{s})-p(Y_{s}-\hat{Y}_{s})-\left(h\mathrm{e}^{(t-s)p}p+2r^{B}\right)(Q^{B}_{s}-\hat{Q}^{B}_{s})\right)\,\mathrm{d}s\right|\mathcal{G}_{t}\right]\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{4}\|a^{-1}\|^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\|2\phi-h\|\int_{0}^{T}|\nu_{s}-\hat{\nu}_{s}|\,\mathrm{d}s+(\|2\phi-h\|+\|h\|)\int_{0}^{T}|\eta_{s}-\hat{\eta}_{s}|\,\mathrm{d}s\right.\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 100.00015pt+\left.\left.\int_{t}^{T}\left(\|p\||Y_{s}-\hat{Y}_{s}|+\left(\|h\|\|p\|+2\|r^{B}\|\right)|Q^{B}_{s}-\hat{Q}^{B}_{s}|\right)\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\|a^{-1}\|^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\|2\phi-h\|^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{T}|\nu_{s}-\hat{\nu}_{s}|\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{2}+(\|2\phi-h\|+\|h\|)^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{T}|\eta_{s}-\hat{\eta}_{s}|\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{2}\right.\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 100.00015pt+\left.\left.\left(\int_{t}^{T}\|p\||Y_{s}-\hat{Y}_{s}|\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{2}+\left(\int_{t}^{T}\left(\|h\|\|p\|+2\|r^{B}\|\right)|Q^{B}_{s}-\hat{Q}^{B}_{s}|\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\|a^{-1}\|^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\|2\phi-h\|^{2}T\int_{0}^{T}|\nu_{s}-\hat{\nu}_{s}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s+(\|2\phi-h\|+\|h\|)^{2}T\int_{0}^{T}|\eta_{s}-\hat{\eta}_{s}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 100.00015pt+\left.(T-t)\int_{0}^{T}\|p\|^{2}|Y_{s}-\hat{Y}_{s}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s+(T-t)\int_{0}^{T}\left(\|h\|\|p\|+2\|r^{B}\|\right)^{2}|Q^{B}_{s}-\hat{Q}^{B}_{s}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right]

\displaystyle\leq\|a^{-1}\|^{2}\left(\|2\phi-h\|^{2}T\|\nu-\hat{\nu}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}+(\|2\phi-h\|+\|h\|)^{2}T\|\eta-\hat{\eta}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}\phantom{(T-t)\left(\|h\|\|p\|+2\|r^{B}\|\right)^{2}}\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 100.00015pt+\left.(T-t)\|p\|^{2}\|Y-\hat{Y}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}+(T-t)\left(\|h\|\|p\|+2\|r^{B}\|\right)^{2}\|Q^{B}-\hat{Q}^{B}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}\right),

so

\displaystyle\left\|\Phi_{\cdot,1}(\Upsilon)-\Phi_{\cdot,1}(\hat{\Upsilon})\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\Phi_{t,1}(\Upsilon)-\Phi_{t,1}(\hat{\Upsilon})\right|^{2}\right]\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\|a^{-1}\|^{2}\left(\|2\phi-h\|^{2}T^{2}\|\nu-\hat{\nu}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}+(\|2\phi-h\|+\|h\|)^{2}T^{2}\|\eta-\hat{\eta}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}\phantom{\left(\tfrac{T^{2}}{2}\|r^{B}\|\right)^{2}}\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 60.00009pt+\left.\tfrac{T^{2}}{2}\|p\|^{2}\|Y-\hat{Y}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}+\tfrac{T^{2}}{2}\left(\|h\|\|p\|+2\|r^{B}\|\right)^{2}\|Q^{B}-\hat{Q}^{B}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}\right).

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\Phi_{\cdot,2}(\Upsilon)-\Phi_{\cdot,2}(\hat{\Upsilon})\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|b^{-1}\|^{2}\left(4\|\psi\|^{2}T^{2}\|\eta-\hat{\eta}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}+\tfrac{T^{2}}{2}\left(\|h\|^{2}\|\nu-\hat{\nu}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}+\|p\|^{2}\|Y-\hat{Y}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}+4\|r^{I}\|^{2}\|Q^{I}-\hat{Q}^{I}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}\right)\right).

We also have

\displaystyle\left\|\Phi_{\cdot,3}(\Upsilon)-\Phi_{\cdot,3}(\hat{\Upsilon})\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{(s-t)p}h\nu_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]\leq\tfrac{T^{2}}{2}\|h\|^{2}\|\nu-\hat{\nu}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2},

\displaystyle\left\|\Phi_{\cdot,4}(\Upsilon)-\Phi_{\cdot,4}(\hat{\Upsilon})\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left|\int_{0}^{t}(\nu_{s}-\hat{\nu}_{s}-\eta_{s}+\hat{\eta}_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]\leq T^{2}(\|\nu-\hat{\nu}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}+\|\eta-\hat{\eta}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}),

and

\displaystyle\left\|\Phi_{\cdot,5}(\Upsilon)-\Phi_{\cdot,5}(\hat{\Upsilon})\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left|\int_{0}^{t}(\eta_{s}-\hat{\eta}_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]\leq\tfrac{T^{2}}{2}\|\eta-\hat{\eta}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}.

Combing all the estimates gives

\displaystyle\left\|\Phi(\Upsilon)-\Phi(\hat{\Upsilon})\right\|_{\mathbb{T}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq T^{2}\left(\|a^{-1}\|^{2}\|2\phi-h\|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\|b^{-1}\|^{2}+1\right)\|h\|^{2}+1\right)\|\nu-\hat{\nu}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\ +T^{2}\left(\|a^{-1}\|^{2}(\|2\phi-h\|+\|h\|)^{2}+4\|b^{-1}\|^{2}\|\psi\|^{2}+\tfrac{3}{2}\right)\|\eta-\hat{\eta}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\ +\tfrac{1}{2}T^{2}\|p\|^{2}\left(\|a^{-1}\|^{2}+\|b^{-1}\|^{2}\right)\|Y-\hat{Y}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\ +\tfrac{1}{2}T^{2}\|a^{-1}\|^{2}\left(\|h\|\|p\|+2\|r^{B}\|\right)^{2}\|Q^{B}-\hat{Q}^{B}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\ +2T^{2}\|b^{-1}\|^{2}\|r^{I}\|^{2}\|Q^{I}-\hat{Q}^{I}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C(T)\;\|\Upsilon-\hat{\Upsilon}\|_{\mathbb{T}}^{2},

hence,
\Phi:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{T}
is a contraction as
C(T)<1
. By Banach fixed point theorem,
\Phi
admits a unique fixed point in
\mathbb{T}
, as desired.
∎

5
Perturbation Analysis

The FBSDE system (
4.6
), or equivalently (
3.13
) and (
3.16
) jointly, that characterizes the Nash equilibria is challenging to solve in generality. In this section, we provide a perturbation analysis that allows us to approximate the solution to arbitrary accuracy in terms of the strength of the transient impact parameter
h
. The approximation we develop is represented as an asymptotic series, where each term in the series admits a closed form solution in terms of the previous coefficients in the expansion. Specifically we obtain a representation of the form

\eta_{t}=\sum_{m=0}^{M}h^{m}\eta^{m}_{t}+R^{\eta,M,h}_{t},\quad\text{ and }\quad\nu_{t}=\sum_{m=0}^{M}h^{m}\nu^{m}_{t}+R^{\nu,M,h}_{t}\,,

where we provide explicit representations of
(\eta_{t}^{m},\nu_{t}^{m})_{m}
.
Moreover, we prove that the error terms,
R^{\eta,M,h}_{t}
and
R^{\nu,M,h}_{t}
, from truncating the series at a given order
M
, are both
o(M)
. While the results in this section could be generalized to arbitrary dimensions, in the remainder of this section, for simplicity, we assume
D=K=1
(i.e., the broker and informed trader are dealing in a single asset) to avoid commutativity issues.

To this end, the following lemma provides the FBSDEs that each term in the series satisfies, and proves that a unique solution exists, as well as provides the explicit form of the solution.

Note that every equation is of the form

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \beta_{t}&=\mathbb{E}\left[\left.-c\varphi_{T}+\Xi+\int_{t}^{T}\left(A_{s}-k\varphi_{s}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s\right|\mathcal{Y}_{t}\right]-\mathrm{e}^{tp}\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\int_{t}^{T}B_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s\right|\mathcal{Y}_{t}\right]\\

\displaystyle\left\|\Gamma_{\cdot,1}(\beta,\varphi)-\Gamma_{\cdot,1}(\hat{\beta},\hat{\varphi})\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}

Conclusion

We presented a setting where a broker and informed trader trade multiple assets. The informed trader has superior information through a trading signal while the broker can offload inventory to a centralized exchanged. We prove that a Nash equilibria exists if and only if a particular system of FBSDEs have a solution and further prove, in a small time setting, that the system of FBSDEs does indeed have a unique solution. The system of FBSDEs contains differing filtrations, which is one of the key characteristics of our problem setting. Furthermore, while we cannot solve the FBSDE system in closed-form, we are able to approximate the solution to arbitrary order in the impact of trading and provide closed-form expressions for each term in this series. Moreover, we prove the remainder terms from truncating the series at order
M
are
o(M)
.

There are several avenues left for future work, e.g., extending the existence and uniqueness result from small time to all time. This may require further restrictions on the model parameters, the trading signal, or martingale component of the price. The transient impact may be generalized to an arbitrary kernel. It would also be interesting to investigate the setting with multiple informed traders with differing information sets. Finally, for practical purposes, it would be useful to develop a deep learning algorithm to approximate the FBSDE system.

A stochastic Galerkin method for optimal Dirichlet boundary control problems with uncertain data
Max Winkler, Hamdullah Yücel

Abstract
The paper deals with a stochastic Galerkin approximation of elliptic Dirichlet boundary control problems with random input data. The expectation of a tracking cost functional with the deterministic constrained control is minimized. Error estimates are derived for the control variable in L 2 ​ ( ∂ 𝒟 ) L^{2}(\partial{\mathcal{D}}) -norm and state variable in L 2 ​ ( Γ , L 2 ​ ( 𝒟 ) ) {L^{2}(\Gamma;L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}))} -norm. To solve large linear systems, appropriate preconditioners are proposed for both unconstrained and constrained scenarios. To illustrate the validity and efficiency of the proposed approaches, some numerical experiments are performed.

Introduction

While solving physical and engineering systems, ones observes discrepancies between the results of simulations and real systems, caused by different kinds of errors which are model errors, numerical errors, and data errors. Some of the above errors may be reduced, e.g., by building more accurate models, by improving numerical resolution methods, and by additional measurements, etc. Such kinds of errors or uncertainties that can be reduced are usually called epistemic or systematic uncertainties. There are also other sources of randomness that are intrinsic to the system itself (e.g., uncertainty in the quantum systems) and hence cannot be reduced. They are the so-called aleatoric or statistical uncertainties. Due to the aforementioned reasons, the uncertainty should be taken into account in the mathematical formulation of real systems if one aims at obtaining more reliable numerical predictions from mathematical models. For instance, an oil company may choose to inject water or other solvents into a reservoir to increase pressure and produce more oil but of course the subsurface rock properties are unknown. If the optimization problem is to determine the well locations and injection rates that maximize the net present value of the reservoir, the optimal rates should be resilient to the inherent uncertainties of the subsurface. While constructing the mathematical formulation, uncertainties can be characterized by parameters associated with, for example, coefficients, boundary data, initial conditions, or source terms that are not known, and consequently the solution of underlying formulation becomes a random variable or more general a random field.

). In particular derivatives with respect to the stochastic variable are required to deduce optimal error estimates.

Lemma 3.2
.

For given
g_{N}=\bar{g}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\xi_{i}\,g_{i}\in C^{\infty}(\Gamma;H^{1}({\mathcal{D}}))
and
F\in C^{\infty}(\Gamma;L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}))
let
v\in L^{2}(\Gamma;H^{1}({\mathcal{D}}))
be the solution of

v|_{\partial{\mathcal{D}}}=g_{N}|_{\partial{\mathcal{D}}},\qquad a[v,w]_{\rho}=[F,w]_{\rho}\quad\forall w\in L^{2}(\Gamma;H_{0}^{1}({\mathcal{D}})).

(16)

Then, for each
n=1,\ldots,N
and
q_{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
there holds

\displaystyle\frac{\|\partial_{\xi_{n}}^{q_{n}+1}v(\cdot,\xi)\|_{H^{1}({\mathcal{D}})}}{(q_{n}+1)!}

\displaystyle\leq C\,\sum_{j=0}^{q_{n}+1}\frac{1}{j!}\,r_{n}^{q_{n}+1-j}\,\|\partial_{\xi_{n}}^{j}F(\cdot,\xi)\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{D}})}

\displaystyle\quad+r_{n}^{q_{n}}(1+\frac{a_{\max}}{a_{\min}})\left(r_{n}\,\|g_{N}(\cdot,\xi)\|_{H^{1}({\mathcal{D}})}+\|g_{n}\|_{H^{1}({\mathcal{D}})}\right)

for almost all
\xi\in\Gamma
with
r_{n}=\frac{\|a_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{D}})}}{a_{\min}}
.

Proof.

Introducing the decomposition
v=v_{0}+g_{N}
with
v_{0}\in L^{2}(\Gamma;H^{1}_{0}({\mathcal{D}}))
the equation (
16
) is equivalent to

\int_{\mathcal{D}}a_{N}\nabla v_{0}\cdot\nabla w\,\textup{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}=\int_{\mathcal{D}}\left(F\,w-a_{N}\nabla g_{N}\cdot\nabla w\right)\textup{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}

(17)

for all
w\in H_{0}^{1}({\mathcal{D}})
and almost everywhere in
\Gamma
.
We choose the test function
w=v_{0}
and obtain with the Cauchy-Schwarz and the Poincaré inequality with constant
C_{D}>0

|v_{0}|_{H^{1}({\mathcal{D}})}\leq\frac{C_{D}}{a_{\min}}\|F\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{D}})}+\frac{a_{\max}}{a_{\min}}\,|g_{N}|_{H^{1}({\mathcal{D}})}.

(18)

Taking now derivatives in (
17
) with respect to
\xi_{n}
gives

\int_{{\mathcal{D}}}\left(a_{n}\nabla v_{0}\cdot\nabla w+a_{N}\nabla(\partial_{\xi_{n}}v_{0})\cdot\nabla w\right)\textup{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}=\int_{{\mathcal{D}}}\left(\partial_{\xi_{n}}F\,w-a_{n}\nabla g_{N}\cdot\nabla w-a_{N}\nabla g_{n}\cdot\nabla w\right)\textup{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}

(19)

and choosing
w=\partial_{\xi_{n}}v_{0}
together with (
18
) yields

\displaystyle|\partial_{\xi_{n}}v_{0}|_{H^{1}({\mathcal{D}})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{D}}{a_{\min}}\left(\|\partial_{\xi_{n}}F\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{D}})}+r_{n}\,\|F\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{D}})}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+r_{n}\,(1+\frac{a_{\max}}{a_{\min}})\,|g_{N}|_{H^{1}({\mathcal{D}})}+\frac{a_{\max}}{a_{\min}}\,|g_{n}|_{H^{1}({\mathcal{D}})}.

Taking again derivatives in (
19
) with respect to
\xi_{n}
and using
\partial_{\xi_{n}}^{2}g_{N}=0
produce

\int_{{\mathcal{D}}}\left(2\,a_{n}\,\nabla(\partial_{\xi_{n}}v_{0})\cdot\nabla w+a_{N}\nabla(\partial_{\xi_{n}}^{2}v_{0})\cdot\nabla w\right)\textup{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}=\int_{{\mathcal{D}}}\left(\partial_{\xi_{n}}^{2}F\,w-2\,a_{n}\nabla(\partial_{\xi_{n}}g_{N})\cdot\nabla w\right)\textup{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}

and
w=\partial_{\xi_{n}}^{2}v_{0}
gives the desired estimate for the case
q_{n}=1
.

The desired result follows by repeated application of the above arguments and the triangle inequality, taking into account
\partial_{\xi_{n}}^{q_{n}+1}g_{N}=0
for
q_{n}\geq 2
.
∎

The application of the regularity estimate from Lemma
3.2
to the state equation presented in equation (
14a
) yields the following result.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
y\in L^{2}(\Gamma;H^{1}({\mathcal{D}}))
be the solution of (
14a
) with
u\in H^{1/2}(\partial{\mathcal{D}})
. Then, there holds

\displaystyle\frac{\|\partial_{\xi_{n}}^{q_{n}+1}y\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma;H^{1}({\mathcal{D}}))}}{(q_{n}+1)!}

\displaystyle\leq C\,r_{n}^{q_{n}+1}\left(\|f_{N}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma;L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}))}+\|u+b_{N}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma;H^{1/2}(\partial{\mathcal{D}}))}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+C\,r_{n}^{q_{n}}\left(\|f_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma;L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}))}+\|b_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma;H^{1/2}(\partial{\mathcal{D}}))}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C(u,a_{N},f_{N},b_{N})\,r_{n}^{q_{n}+1}.

3.2
Stochastic Galerkin method

To obtain a fully discrete scheme, we introduce finite element spaces on the parametric domain
\Gamma
and the physical domain
\mathcal{D}
. We first decompose the parametric domain denoted by
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
into a finite number of disjoint
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
–boxes
B_{j}^{N}
as follows

\Gamma=\bigcup\limits_{j\in J}B_{j}^{N}=\bigcup\limits_{j\in J}\prod\limits_{n=1}^{N}(r_{j}^{n},s_{j}^{n}),

where
B_{j}^{N}\cap B_{i}^{N}=\emptyset
for
j\neq i\in J
and
(r_{j}^{n},s_{j}^{n})\subset\Gamma_{n}
for
n=1,\ldots,N
and
j\in J
. On each
\Gamma_{n}
, the mesh size is denoted by

\gamma_{n}=\max\limits_{j\in J}\lvert s_{j}^{n}-r_{j}^{n}\rvert\quad\hbox{for}\;\;1\leq n\leq N

and the maximum mesh size of
\Gamma
becomes
\gamma=\max\limits_{n=1,\ldots,N}\gamma_{n}
. The stochastic (parametric) finite element space is given by

\mathcal{S}^{\gamma}=\{\psi^{\gamma}\in L^{2}(\Gamma):\,\psi|_{B_{j}^{N}}\in\text{span}\big(\prod\limits_{n=1}^{N}\xi_{n}^{\ell_{n}}:\;\ell_{n}\in\mathbb{N},\,\ell_{n}\leq q_{n},\,n=1,\ldots,N\big)\},

(20)

which has at most
q_{n}
degree on each direction
\xi_{n}
for a multi–index
q=(q_{1},\ldots,q_{N})
.

Next, we consider finite element subspaces on the physical domain
\mathcal{D}
. Let
\{\mathcal{T}_{h}\}_{h}
be a quasi-uniform family of triangulations of
\mathcal{D}
with
\overline{\mathcal{D}}=\bigcup_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\overline{K}
and maximum mesh size
h=\max\limits_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{K}
, where
h_{K}
is the diameter of an element
K
. Furthermore,
\mathcal{E}_{h}
denotes the partition of the boundary
\partial\mathcal{D}
induced by
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, meaning that each
E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}
with
E\subset\partial\mathcal{D}
is an edge of some triangle
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
. Denoting the set of all linear polynomials on
K
by
\mathbb{P}(K)
, the finite element spaces of state, test and control variables are defined by

\displaystyle Y_{h}

\displaystyle=\{y_{h}\in C(\mathcal{D}):\,y_{h}|_{K}\in\mathbb{P}(K)\quad\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\},

\displaystyle V_{h}

\displaystyle=\{v_{h}\in Y_{h}:\,v_{h}|_{\partial{\mathcal{D}}}=0\},

\displaystyle U_{h}

\displaystyle=\{u_{h}\in C(\partial\mathcal{D}):\,u_{h}|_{E}\in\mathbb{P}(E)\quad\forall E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}\}.

To represent functions from
Y_{h}
we introduce the nodal basis
\{\phi_{i}\}\subset Y_{h}
satisfying
\phi_{m}(x_{n})=\delta_{mn}
for all
m,n=1,\ldots,{\mathcal{N}}:=\text{dim}(Y_{h})
, where
x_{n}
are the nodes of the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
. We number the nodes in such a way that
x_{n}
are interior nodes for
n=1,\ldots,{\mathcal{N}}_{\textup{I}}
and boundary nodes for
n={\mathcal{N}}_{\textup{I}}+1,\ldots,{\mathcal{N}}={\mathcal{N}}_{\textup{I}}+{\mathcal{N}}_{\textup{B}}
.

Now, we are ready to state the tensor product finite element spaces on
\mathcal{D}\otimes\Gamma
. As test space we define

\mathcal{V}_{h}^{\gamma}=V_{h}\otimes\mathcal{S}^{\gamma}=\text{span}\{\phi_{h}\,\psi^{\gamma}:\,\phi_{h}\in V_{h},\,\psi^{\gamma}\in\mathcal{S}^{\gamma}\}

and the trial space is defined by

\mathcal{Y}_{h}^{\gamma}:=Y_{h}\otimes\mathcal{S}^{\gamma}.

With the spaces defined above we can now state the following fully discrete optimal control problem as approximation of (
1
)–(
2
):

\min\limits_{(y_{h}^{\gamma},u_{h})\in\mathcal{Y}_{h}^{\gamma}\times U^{\textup{ad}}_{h}}\;\mathcal{J}(y_{h}^{\gamma},u_{h})=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma}\int_{\mathcal{D}}\left(y_{h}^{\gamma}-y^{d}\right)^{2}\textup{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\rho(\xi)\,\textup{d}\xi+\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\partial\mathcal{D}}u_{h}^{2}\,\textup{d}\boldsymbol{x}

(21a)

subject to

\displaystyle y_{h}^{\gamma}|_{\partial{\mathcal{D}}}

\displaystyle=u_{h}+\Pi_{h}(b_{N})

(21b)

\displaystyle a[y_{h}^{\gamma},v_{h}^{\gamma}]_{\rho}

\displaystyle=[f_{N},v_{h}^{\gamma}]_{\rho},\quad\forall v_{h}^{\gamma}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}^{\gamma},

and

u_{h}\in U_{h}^{\textup{ad}}:=\{u_{h}\in U_{h}\colon u_{a}\leq u_{h}(\boldsymbol{x})\leq u_{b},\ \text{f.a.a.}\ \boldsymbol{x}\in\partial{\mathcal{D}}\}.

(21c)

Here,
\Pi_{h}
, cf. (
26
), is a projection operator onto
U_{h}
.

A pair
(y_{h}^{\gamma},u_{h})\in\mathcal{Y}_{h}^{\gamma}\times U_{h}^{\textup{ad}}
is a unique solution of the control problem (
21
) if and only if an adjoint state
p_{h}^{\gamma}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}^{\gamma}
exists such that the triplet
(y_{h}^{\gamma},u_{h},p_{h}^{\gamma})\in\mathcal{Y}_{h}^{\gamma}\times U_{h}^{\textup{ad}}\times\mathcal{V}_{h}^{\gamma}
solves the following discrete optimality system

\displaystyle y_{h}^{\gamma}|_{\partial{\mathcal{D}}}=u_{h}+\Pi_{h}(b_{N}),\quad a[y_{h}^{\gamma},v_{h}^{\gamma}]_{\rho}

\displaystyle=[f_{N},v_{h}^{\gamma}]_{\rho}

\displaystyle\forall v_{h}^{\gamma}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}^{\gamma},

(22a)

\displaystyle a[q_{h}^{\gamma},p_{h}^{\gamma}]_{\rho}

\displaystyle=[y_{h}^{\gamma}-y^{d},q_{h}^{\gamma}]_{\rho}

\displaystyle\forall q_{h}^{\gamma}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}^{\gamma},

(22b)

\displaystyle(\alpha\,u_{h}-{\mathbb{E}}[g_{h}],w_{h}-u_{h})_{\partial{\mathcal{D}}}

\displaystyle\geq 0

\displaystyle\forall w_{h}\in U^{\textup{ad}}_{h}.

(22c)

Here,
{\mathbb{E}}[g_{h}]\in U_{h}
is also a variational co-normal derivative defined by

(g_{h},w_{h})_{\partial{\mathcal{D}}}=a[S_{h}^{\gamma}(w_{h}),p_{h}^{\gamma}]_{\rho}-[y_{h}^{\gamma}-y^{d},S_{h}^{\gamma}(w_{h})]_{\rho}\qquad\forall w_{h}\in U_{h},

with
S_{h}^{\gamma}\colon U_{h}\to\mathcal{Y}_{h}^{\gamma}
some arbitrary discrete extension operator.

Last, we note that the discrete variational inequality (
22c
) can also be written as

j_{h}^{\gamma}{{}^{\prime}}(u_{h})(w_{h}-u_{h})=(\alpha\,u_{h},w_{h}-u_{h})_{\partial{\mathcal{D}}}+[y_{h}^{\gamma}-y^{d},y_{h}^{\gamma}(w_{h})-y_{h}^{\gamma}]_{\rho}\geq 0,\quad\forall w_{h}\in U^{\textup{ad}}_{h},

(23)

where
j_{h}^{\gamma}(u_{h})=\mathcal{J}(y_{h}^{\gamma}(u_{h}),u_{h})
and

y_{h}^{\gamma}(w_{h})
is the solution of the problem (
22a
) with boundary data
w_{h}+\Pi_{h}(b_{N})
.

Theorem 4.1
.

\displaystyle\alpha\,\|u-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\partial{\mathcal{D}})}^{2}

Insertion of (

4
) then leads to the estimate

\displaystyle\alpha\|u-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\partial{\mathcal{D}})}^{2}+\|y-y_{h}^{\gamma}\|_{{L^{2}(\Gamma;L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}))}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\,\Big(\|u-u_{h}^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\partial{\mathcal{D}})}^{2}+\|y-y_{h}^{\gamma}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma;L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}))}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+a[y_{h}^{\gamma}(u_{h}^{*})-y_{h}^{\gamma},p]_{\rho}+(\alpha\,u-{\mathbb{E}}[a_{N}\,\partial_{n}p],u_{h}^{*}-u)_{\partial{\mathcal{D}}}\Big).

(42)

The last term on the right-hand side vanishes due to the definition of the function
u_{h}^{*}
, see (
34
). Furthermore, with the definition of
S_{h}^{\gamma}
and the relation
y_{h}^{\gamma}(u_{h}^{*})-y_{h}^{\gamma}=S_{h}^{\gamma}(u_{h}^{*}-u_{h})
we may rewrite the third term in (
4
) as

\displaystyle a[y_{h}^{\gamma}(u_{h}^{*})-y_{h}^{\gamma},p]_{\rho}

\displaystyle\quad=\sup_{w_{h}\in U_{h}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{|a[S_{h}^{\gamma}(w_{h}),p]_{\rho}|}{\|w_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\partial{\mathcal{D}})}}\left(\|u-u_{h}^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\partial{\mathcal{D}})}+\|u-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\partial{\mathcal{D}})}\right).

Together with Young’s inequality we arrive at the desired estimate.

∎

Next, we derive upper bounds for the three terms on the right-hand side of (
4.1
).

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
y^{d}\in H^{t}({\mathcal{D}})
for some arbitrary
t>0
. The projection
u_{h}^{*}\in U_{h}^{\textup{ad}}
from (
33
) fulfills the estimate

\|u-u_{h}^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\partial{\mathcal{D}})}\leq C\,h^{1/2}.

(43)

Proof.

The assumption on the desired state guarantees
u\in H^{1/2+\varepsilon}(\partial{\mathcal{D}})
for some sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
, which can be deduced with bootstrapping arguments like in (
2
) taking into account the regularity estimates from
[
4
, Corollary 5.3]
. The estimate (
43
) is then a consequence of
[
5
, Lemma 5.6]
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose
u\in H^{1/2}(\partial{\mathcal{D}})
and let
y
solve (
14a
). For the approximation
y_{h}^{\gamma}(u)
from (
30
) there holds

\|y-y_{h}^{\gamma}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma;L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C\left(h+\sqrt{\sum_{n=1}^{N}(q_{n}+2)\,\left(\frac{\gamma_{n}\,r_{n}}{2}\right)^{q_{n}+1}}\right),

with some
C>0
depending only on the data
a_{N},f_{N},b_{N},u
.

Proof.

The proof uses a duality argument. To this end, let
z\in L^{2}(\Gamma;H_{0}^{1}({\mathcal{D}})\cap H^{2}({\mathcal{D}}))
be the strong solution of

\displaystyle-\nabla\cdot(a_{N}\,\nabla z)

\displaystyle=y-y_{h}^{\gamma}(u)

\displaystyle\text{in}\ \Gamma\times{\mathcal{D}},

(44)

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{on}\ \Gamma\times\partial{\mathcal{D}}.

The function
z
fulfills the
a priori
estimate

\|z(\cdot,\xi)\|_{H^{2}({\mathcal{D}})}\leq C\,\|(y-y_{h}^{\gamma}(u))(\cdot,\xi)\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{D}})}

(45)

for almost all
\xi\in\Gamma
.
By using the definitions of
z
from (
44
) and
y
from (
14a
), the integration-by-parts formula, the fact that
b_{N}=\Pi_{\gamma}(b_{N})
, and the Galerkin orthogonality, we get for arbitrary
z_{h}^{\gamma}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\|y-y_{h}^{\gamma}(u)\|_{{L^{2}(\Gamma;L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}))}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad=a[y-y_{h}^{\gamma}(u),z-z_{h}^{\gamma}]_{\rho}-[a_{N}\partial_{n}z,u+b_{N}-\Pi_{h}(u+b_{N})]_{\rho,\partial{\mathcal{D}}}=E_{1}+E_{2}.

(46)

To bound the term
E_{2}
, we use the orthogonality of
\Pi_{h}
, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the inequalities (
27a
) and (
27c
), and the regularity estimate (
45
), and infer

\displaystyle E_{2}

\displaystyle=[u+b_{N}-\Pi_{h}(u+b_{N}),a_{N}\,\partial_{n}z-\Pi_{h}(a_{N}\,\partial_{n}z)]_{\rho,\partial{\mathcal{D}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\,h\,\|u+b_{N}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma;H^{1/2}(\partial{\mathcal{D}}))}\,\|z\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma;H^{2}({\mathcal{D}}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\,h\,(\|u\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial{\mathcal{D}})}+\|b_{N}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma;H^{1/2}(\partial{\mathcal{D}}))})\,\|y-y_{h}^{\gamma}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma;L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}))}.

(47)

To deduce an estimate for the term
E_{1}
on the right-hand side of (
4
)
we choose
z_{h}^{\gamma}=R_{h}(\Pi_{\gamma}(z))
with
R_{h}
defined in (
28
), exploit the stability of
R_{h}
in
H^{1}({\mathcal{D}})
, and the estimates (
29
) and (
25
) to arrive at

\|z-z_{h}^{\gamma}\|_{L^{2}(B_{j}^{N};H^{1}({\mathcal{D}}))}\leq C\left(h\,|z|_{L^{2}(B_{j}^{N};H^{2}({\mathcal{D}}))}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(\frac{\gamma_{n}}{2}\right)^{q_{n}+1}\frac{\|\partial_{\xi_{n}}^{q_{n}+1}z\|_{L^{2}(B_{j}^{N};H^{1}({\mathcal{D}}))}}{(q_{n}+1)!}\right)

(48)

for all
j\in J
.
Applying Lemma
3.2
leads to

\frac{\|\partial_{\xi_{n}}^{q_{n}+1}z\|_{L^{2}(B_{j}^{N};H^{1}({\mathcal{D}}))}}{(q_{n}+1)!}\leq C\,\sum_{i=0}^{q_{n}+1}\frac{1}{i!}\,r_{n}^{q_{n}+1-i}\,\|\partial_{\xi_{n}}^{i}(y-y_{h}^{\gamma}(u))\|_{L^{2}(B_{j}^{N};L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}))}.

A further application of Corollary
3.3
gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{i!}\|\partial_{\xi_{n}}^{i}y\|_{L^{2}(B_{j}^{N};L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\,r_{n}^{i}\,\left(\|f_{N}\|_{L^{2}(B_{j}^{N};L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}))}+\|u+b_{N}\|_{L^{2}(B_{j}^{N};H^{1/2}(\partial{\mathcal{D}}))}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+C\,r_{n}^{i-1}\,\left(\|f_{n}\|_{L^{2}(B_{j}^{N};L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}))}+\|b_{n}\|_{L^{2}(B_{j}^{N};H^{1/2}(\partial{\mathcal{D}}))}\right).

With an inverse inequality we deduce furthermore

\displaystyle\frac{1}{i!}\|\partial_{\xi_{n}}^{i}y_{h}^{\gamma}(u)\|_{L^{2}(B_{j}^{N};L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\,\frac{1}{i!}\,(\gamma_{n}\,r_{n})^{-i}\,r_{n}^{i}\,\|y_{h}^{\gamma}(u)\|_{L^{2}(B_{j}^{N};L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\,r_{n}^{i}\,\|y_{h}^{\gamma}(u)\|_{L^{2}(B_{j}^{N};L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}))}

and the previous three estimates then give

\displaystyle\frac{\|\partial_{\xi_{n}}^{q_{n}+1}z\|_{L^{2}(B_{j}^{N};H^{1}({\mathcal{D}}))}}{(q_{n}+1)!}

\displaystyle\leq C\,(q_{n}+2)\,r_{n}^{q_{n}+1}\Big(\|f_{N}\|_{L^{2}(B_{j}^{N};L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}))}+\|u+b_{N}\|_{L^{2}(B_{j}^{N};H^{1/2}(\partial{\mathcal{D}}))}

\displaystyle\quad+\|f_{n}\|_{L^{2}(B_{j}^{N};L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}))}+\|b_{n}\|_{L^{2}(B_{j}^{N};H^{1/2}(\partial{\mathcal{D}}))}+\|y_{h}^{\gamma}(u)\|_{L^{2}(B_{j}^{N};L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}))}\Big).

Insertion into (
48
), summation over all the boxes
B_{j}^{N}
,
j\in J
, and an application of (
45
) then yield the following error estimate for the approximation of the dual solution

\|z-z_{h}^{\gamma}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma;H^{1}({\mathcal{D}}))}\leq C\left(h\,\|y-y_{h}^{\gamma}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma;L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}))}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}(q_{n}+2)\,\left(\frac{\gamma_{n}\,r_{n}}{2}\right)^{q_{n}+1}\,D\right)

(49)

with

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle:=\|f_{N}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma;L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}))}+\|u+b_{N}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma;H^{1/2}(\partial{\mathcal{D}}))}

\displaystyle\quad+\|f_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma;L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}))}+\|b_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma;H^{1/2}(\partial{\mathcal{D}}))}+\|y_{h}^{\gamma}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma;L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}))}.

The last term in
D
can be omitted as it is bounded by the data
f_{N},f_{n},u+b_{N}
, and
b_{n}
as well, see (
4
). In the following we will hide the dependencies on the data in the generic constant
C
.

Consider now the term
E_{1}
in (
4
).
An application of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the bound of
a_{N}
in (
13
), the triangle inequality, and the inequalities in (
4
) and (
49
) result in

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle\leq a_{\max}\,\big(\|y\|_{{L^{2}(\Gamma;H^{1}({\mathcal{D}}))}}+\|y_{h}^{\gamma}(u)\|_{{L^{2}(\Gamma;H^{1}({\mathcal{D}}))}}\big)\|z-z_{h}^{\gamma}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma;H^{1}({\mathcal{D}}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\,\left(h\,\|y-y_{h}^{\gamma}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma;L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}))}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}(q_{n}+2)\,\left(\frac{\gamma_{n}\,r_{n}}{2}\right)^{q_{n}+1}\right).

(50)

Finally, combining (
4
), (
4
), and (
4
), and using Young’s inequality yield

\|y-y_{h}^{\gamma}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma;L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}))}^{2}\leq C\left(h^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}(q_{n}+2)\,\left(\frac{\gamma_{n}\,r_{n}}{2}\right)^{q_{n}+1}\right).

The assertion now follows after extracting the root.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
p\in L^{2}(\Gamma;H^{1}_{0}({\mathcal{D}})\cap H^{2}({\mathcal{D}}))
be the adjoint state from (
14b
) and let
w_{h}\in U_{h}
be arbitrary. Then, the following error estimate holds

\displaystyle a[S_{h}^{\gamma}(w_{h}),p]_{\rho}\leq C\,\left(h^{1/2}+h^{-1/2}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{N}(q_{n}+2)\left(\frac{\gamma_{n}\,r_{n}}{2}\right)^{q_{n}+1}\right)\|w_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\partial{\mathcal{D}})}.

Proof.

The definition of
S_{h}^{\gamma}
, the boundedness of the bilinear form
a[\cdot,\cdot]_{\rho}
, the inequality (
29
), and the estimate (
48
) give

\displaystyle a[S_{h}^{\gamma}(w_{h}),p]_{\rho}

\displaystyle=a[S_{h}^{\gamma}(w_{h}),p-R_{h}(p)+R_{h}((I-\Pi_{\gamma})p)]_{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq C\,\|S_{h}^{\gamma}w_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma;H^{1}({\mathcal{D}}))}\,\left(h\,\|p\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma;H^{2}({\mathcal{D}}))}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(\frac{\gamma_{n}}{2}\right)^{q_{n}+1}\,\frac{\|\partial_{\xi_{n}}^{q_{n}+1}p\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma;H^{1}({\mathcal{D}}))}}{(q_{n}+1)!}\right).

(51)

To bound the norm involving
S_{h}^{\gamma}(w_{h})
we apply the estimate (
32
) (note that
b_{N}=0
and
f_{N}=0
) with the stability of
\Pi_{h}
which yields

\|S_{h}^{\gamma}(w_{h})\|_{H^{1}({\mathcal{D}})}\leq C\,h^{-1/2}\,\|w_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\partial{\mathcal{D}})}.

(52)

By a standard shift theorem we obtain

\|p\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma;H^{2}({\mathcal{D}}))}\leq C\,\|y-y^{d}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma;L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}))}\leq C.

(53)

To bound the term involving the derivatives
\partial_{\xi_{n}}^{q_{n}+1}p
we use Lemma
3.2
to get

\frac{\|\partial_{\xi_{n}}^{q_{n}+1}p\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma;H^{1}({\mathcal{D}}))}}{(q_{n}+1)!}\leq C\,\sum_{j=0}^{q_{n}+1}\frac{1}{j!}\,r_{n}^{q_{n}+1-j}\,\|\partial_{\xi_{n}}^{j}(y-y^{d})\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma;L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}))}.

The desired state
y^{d}
is deterministic, so
\partial_{\xi_{n}}y^{d}\equiv 0
for all
n=1,\ldots,N
. Furthermore, we use Corollary
3.3
to deduce

\frac{1}{j!}\,\|\partial_{\xi_{n}}^{j}(y-y^{d})\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma;L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}))}\leq C\,r_{n}^{j}

and thus,

\frac{\|\partial_{\xi_{n}}^{q_{n}+1}p\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma;H^{1}({\mathcal{D}}))}}{(q_{n}+1)!}\leq C\,\sum_{j=0}^{q_{n}+1}r_{n}^{q_{n}+1}=C\,(q_{n}+2)\,r_{n}^{q_{n}+1}.

(54)

Insertion of (
52
), (
53
) and (
54
) into (
4
) then yields

a[S_{h}^{\gamma}(w_{h}),p]_{\rho}\leq C\,h^{-1/2}\,\|w_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\partial{\mathcal{D}})}\left(h+\sum_{n=1}^{N}(q_{n}+2)\,\left(\frac{\gamma_{n}\,r_{n}}{2}\right)^{q_{n}+1}\right).

∎

We are now in the position to state the main result of this section:

Theorem 4.5
.

Suppose
y^{d}\in H^{t}({\mathcal{D}})
for some
t>0
.
Let
(y,u,p)\in L^{2}(\Gamma;H^{1}({\mathcal{D}}))\times U^{\textup{ad}}\times L^{2}(\Gamma;H^{2}({\mathcal{D}})\cap H^{1}_{0}({\mathcal{D}}))
be the solution triplet of (
14
) and denote by
(y_{h}^{\gamma},u_{h},p_{h}^{\gamma})\in\mathcal{Y}_{h}^{\gamma}\times U_{h}^{\textup{ad}}\times\mathcal{V}_{h}^{\gamma}
their approximation from (
22
). The following error estimate is valid:

\displaystyle\|u-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\partial{\mathcal{D}})}+\|y-y_{h}^{\gamma}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma;L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}))}\leq C\left(h^{1/2}+h^{-1/2}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{N}\left(\frac{\gamma_{n}\,r_{n}}{2}\right)^{q_{n}+1}\right).

Proof.

The estimate follows after insertion of Lemmata
4.2
–
4.4
into Theorem
4.1
. In addition, apply Young’s inequality to the right-hand side of the estimate from Lemma
4.3
.
∎

The result of Theorem
4.5
is a worst-case estimate for the case that the
{\mathcal{D}}
is a convex polygonal domain. By exploiting the knowledge on the singularities at corners of the domain one can even show better convergence results. As this would exceed the scope of this paper we only comment on possible improvements:

Remark 4.6
.

a)

A more precise version of Lemma
4.2
can be found in
[
5
, Lemma 5.6]
. If
{\mathcal{D}}
is a convex polygonal domain, with
\omega
denoting the largest opening angle of the corners, there holds the estimate

\|u-u_{h}^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\partial{\mathcal{D}})}\leq C\,h^{s},\quad s<\min\{3/2,\pi/\omega-1/2\}.

To establish this result, the assumption
y^{d}\in H^{t}({\mathcal{D}})
for all
t<\min\{1,\pi/\omega-1\}
is necessary. In case of a smooth domain and sufficiently regular data, one even achieves a convergence rate of almost
3/2
.

b)

The result of Lemma
4.3
can also be improved taking into account higher regularity of the optimal state. Following known results for the deterministic setting from
[
4
]
, one can show
u\in H^{s}(\partial{\mathcal{D}})
and
y\in L^{2}(\Gamma;H^{s+1/2}({\mathcal{D}}))
with
s<\min\{3/2,\pi/\omega-1/2\}
in case of convex and polygonal
{\mathcal{D}}
. This allows to apply an improved error estimate for the
L^{2}
-projection of
u+b_{N}
in (
4
), leading to
E_{2}\leq C\,h^{s+1/2}
, and an improved estimate for
\|y-y_{h}^{\gamma}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma;H^{1}({\mathcal{D}}))}
in (
4
) leading to
E_{1}\leq C\,h^{s+1/2}+c(q)
.

c)

Further improvements in Lemma
4.4
are also possible, but require very advanced techniques in the proofs. In the simplest case, assume that
p\in L^{2}(\Gamma;W^{2,\infty}({\mathcal{D}}))
holds, which would follow, e.g., under the quite restrictive assumption
\omega<\pi/2
. In this case, instead of (
4
), we can estimate as follows:

\displaystyle a[S_{h}^{\gamma}(w_{h}),p]_{\rho}

\displaystyle=a[\widetilde{S}_{h}^{\gamma}(w_{h}),p-p_{h}^{\gamma}]

\displaystyle\leq C\,\|\widetilde{S}_{h}^{\gamma}(w_{h})\|_{W^{1,1}({\mathcal{D}})}\,\|p-p_{h}^{\gamma}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma;W^{1,\infty}({\mathcal{D}}))}

with
p_{h}^{\gamma}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}^{\gamma}
satisfying
a[v_{h}^{\gamma},p-p_{h}^{\gamma}]=0
for all
v_{h}^{\gamma}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}^{\gamma}
and
\widetilde{S}_{h}^{\gamma}
the zero extension, mapping
w_{h}\in U_{h}
to that function from
\mathcal{Y}_{h}^{\gamma}
vanishing in the interior nodes. It remains to use the estimate
\|\widetilde{S}_{h}^{\gamma}(w_{h})\|_{W^{1,1}({\mathcal{D}})}\leq C\,\|w_{h}\|_{L^{1}(\partial{\mathcal{D}})}\leq C\,\|w_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\partial{\mathcal{D}})}
(see
[
39
, Lemma 3.3]
) and the
W^{1,\infty}({\mathcal{D}})
-error estimate
\|p-R_{h}p\|_{W^{1,\infty}({\mathcal{D}})}\leq C\,h\,|p|_{W^{2,\infty}({\mathcal{D}})}
(see
[
45
]
) to conclude

a[S_{h}^{\gamma}(w_{h}),p]_{\rho}\leq C\,\left(h+\sum\limits_{n=1}^{N}\left(\frac{\gamma_{n}\,r_{n}}{2}\right)^{q_{n}+1}\right)\|w_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\partial{\mathcal{D}})}.

In the case
\omega\in(\pi/2,\pi)
we refer to
[
5
,
43
]
where sharp discretization error estimates with respect to
h
are proved in the deterministic setting. An extension to the stochastic setting is not straight-forward and might be subject of future research.

5
Numerical solution

In this section, we first provide the matrix formulation of the discretized optimality system by employing a “discretize-then-optimize” approach; see, e.g.,
[
51
]
, for both unconstrained and constrained problems. Then, we introduce suitable preconditioners to solve the corresponding linear systems.

5.1
Unconstrained problem

By following the Cameron–Martin theorem
[
14
]
, state
y_{h}^{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{x},\xi)
and adjoint state
p_{h}^{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{x},\xi)
on the finite parametric domain
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}

are characterized by a finite generalized polynomial chaos (gPC) approximation

y_{h}^{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{x},\xi)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N_{\xi}}y_{i,h}(\boldsymbol{x})\,\psi_{i}(\xi),\qquad p_{h}^{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{x},\xi)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N_{\xi}}p_{i,h}(\boldsymbol{x})\,\psi_{i}(\xi),

(55)

where the finite element functions
y_{i,h}\in Y_{h}
and
p_{i,h}(\boldsymbol{x})\in V_{h}
are the deterministic modes of the expansion and
N_{\xi}
is the number of total generalized polynomial chaos basis, given by

N_{\xi}=1+\sum\limits_{s=1}^{Q}\frac{1}{s!}\prod\limits_{j=0}^{s-1}(N+j)=\frac{(N+Q)!}{N!Q!},

with
N
the dimension of the random vector
\xi
and
Q
the highest order in the stochastic (parametric) basis set.

The coefficients of the finite element functions with respect to the standard basis are
\boldsymbol{y}_{i}^{0}=(y^{0}_{i,1},\ldots,y^{0}_{i,{\mathcal{N}}_{\textup{I}}})^{\top}
for
i=1,\ldots,N_{\xi}
, and for problems with inhomogeneous Dirichlet data
\boldsymbol{u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{{\mathcal{N}}_{\textup{B}}})^{\top}
. The vectors of unknowns used to represent the interior and boundary values of the state
y_{h}^{\gamma}
are then
\boldsymbol{y}^{0}=\text{vec}[\boldsymbol{y}_{1}^{0},\ldots,\boldsymbol{y}^{0}_{N_{\xi}}]
and
\boldsymbol{u}
, respectively.
As basis for the stochastic space
\mathcal{S}^{\gamma}
we choose the orthonormal polynomials
\{\psi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N_{\xi}}
satisfying

\int_{\Gamma}\psi_{i}(\xi)\,\psi_{j}(\xi)\,\rho(\xi)\,\textup{d}\xi=\delta_{i,j},\ i,j=1,\ldots,N_{\xi},

that can be constructed from univariate orthonormal polynomials, see
[
22
]
. By construction, the first basis functions are

\psi_{1}(\xi)=1,\quad\psi_{j+1}(\xi)=\xi_{j},\ j=0,\ldots,N_{\xi}.

Recall the KL expansions from (
12
) for
a(\boldsymbol{x},\omega)
,
b(\boldsymbol{x},\omega)
, and
f(\boldsymbol{x},\omega)
, with the notation
a_{k}=\kappa_{a}\,\sqrt{\lambda_{k}}\,w_{k}(\boldsymbol{x})
,
b_{k}=\kappa_{b}\,\sqrt{\lambda_{k}}\,w_{k}(\boldsymbol{x})
, and
f_{k}=\kappa_{f}\,\sqrt{\lambda_{k}}\,w_{k}(\boldsymbol{x})
, respectively, for
k=1,\dots,N
. Then, we define a fully-discrete approximation of
b
by taking its KL expansion and by projecting the coefficient functions
b_{j}(\boldsymbol{x})
into the space
U_{h}
. This gives

\displaystyle b_{N,h}:=\Pi_{h}(b_{N})=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Pi_{h}(b_{j})\,\xi_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{n=1}^{\mathcal{N}_{B}}b_{j,n}\,\phi_{\mathcal{N}_{I}+n}\,\xi_{j}

with coefficients
b_{j,n}\in{\mathbb{R}}
for
j=1,\ldots,N
,
n=1,\ldots,{\mathcal{N}}_{\textup{B}}
.
We summarize these coefficients into vectors
[\boldsymbol{b}_{k}]_{n}=b_{k,n}
,
n=1,\ldots,{\mathcal{N}}_{B}
, and define furthermore
\boldsymbol{b}=\text{vec}[\boldsymbol{b}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{b}_{N}]
.

The coefficients introduced above allow the following representation of the state variable

\displaystyle y_{h}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{x}},\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{{\mathcal{N}}_{\text{I}}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\xi}}y_{j,n}^{0}\,\psi_{j}(\xi)\,\phi_{n}(\boldsymbol{x})+\sum_{n=1}^{{\mathcal{N}}_{\text{B}}}\left(u_{n}\,+\sum_{j=1}^{N}b_{j,n}\,\xi_{j}\right)\phi_{{\mathcal{N}}_{\textup{I}}+n}({\boldsymbol{x}}).

(56)

For each test function
v=\psi_{i}\,\phi_{m}
,
m=1,\ldots,{\mathcal{N}}_{\textup{I}}
,
i=1,\ldots,N_{\xi}
, we then obtain from (
21b
) the system of linear equations

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\xi}}\sum_{n=1}^{{\mathcal{N}}_{\textup{I}}}\Bigg(\left(\int_{\Gamma}\psi_{j}\,\psi_{i}\,\rho\,\textup{d}\xi\right)\left(\int_{\mathcal{D}}\overline{a}\,\nabla\phi_{n}\cdot\nabla\phi_{m}\,\textup{d}\boldsymbol{x}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\int_{\Gamma}\xi_{k}\,\psi_{j}\,\psi_{i}\,\rho\,\textup{d}\xi\right)\left(\int_{\mathcal{D}}a_{k}\,\nabla\phi_{n}\cdot\nabla\phi_{m}\,\textup{d}\boldsymbol{x}\right)\Bigg)y_{j,n}^{0}

\displaystyle+\sum_{n=1}^{{\mathcal{N}}_{\textup{B}}}\Bigg(\left(\int_{\Gamma}\psi_{i}\,\rho\,\textup{d}\xi\right)\left(\int_{\mathcal{D}}\overline{a}\,\nabla\phi_{{\mathcal{N}}_{\textup{I}+n}}\cdot\nabla\phi_{m}\,\textup{d}\boldsymbol{x}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\int_{\Gamma}\xi_{k}\,\psi_{i}\,\rho\,\textup{d}\xi\right)\left(\int_{\mathcal{D}}a_{k}\,\nabla\phi_{{\mathcal{N}}_{\textup{I}+n}}\cdot\nabla\phi_{m}\,\textup{d}\boldsymbol{x}\right)\Bigg)u_{n}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\Gamma}\psi_{i}\,\rho\,\textup{d}\xi\right)\left(\int_{{\mathcal{D}}}\overline{f}\,\phi_{m}\,\textup{d}\boldsymbol{x}\right)+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\int_{\Gamma}\xi_{k}\,\psi_{i}\,\rho\,\textup{d}\xi\right)\left(\int_{{\mathcal{D}}}f_{k}\,\phi_{m}\,\textup{d}\boldsymbol{x}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad-\sum_{l=1}^{N}\sum_{n=1}^{\mathcal{N}_{B}}\Bigg(\left(\int_{\Gamma}\xi_{l}\,\psi_{i}\,\rho\,\textup{d}\xi\right)\left(\int_{{\mathcal{D}}}\bar{a}\,\nabla\phi_{{\mathcal{N}}_{\textup{I}}+n}\cdot\nabla\phi_{m}\,\textup{d}\boldsymbol{x}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\int_{\Gamma}\xi_{k}\,\xi_{l}\,\psi_{i}\,\rho\textup{d}\xi\right)\left(\int_{{\mathcal{D}}}a_{k}\,\nabla\phi_{{\mathcal{N}}_{\textup{I}}+n}\cdot\nabla\phi_{m}\,\textup{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}\right)\Bigg)b_{l,n}.

(57)

The integrals in (
5.1
) motivate the definition of the matrices

\displaystyle[\overline{P}]_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma}\psi_{j}\,\psi_{i}\,\rho\,\textup{d}\xi,

\displaystyle[P_{k}]_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma}\xi_{k}\,\psi_{j}\,\psi_{i}\,\rho\,\textup{d}\xi,

\displaystyle i,j

\displaystyle=1,\ldots,N_{\xi},\,k=1,\ldots,N,

\displaystyle[\overline{K}]_{m,n}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathcal{D}}}\overline{a}\,\nabla\phi_{n}\cdot\nabla\phi_{m}\,\textup{d}\boldsymbol{x},

\displaystyle[K_{k}]_{m,n}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathcal{D}}}a_{k}\,\nabla\phi_{n}\cdot\nabla\phi_{m}\,\textup{d}\boldsymbol{x},

\displaystyle m,n

\displaystyle=1,\ldots,{\mathcal{N}},\,k=1,\ldots,N,

\displaystyle[M]_{m,n}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathcal{D}}}\phi_{n}\,\phi_{m}\,\textup{d}\boldsymbol{x},

\displaystyle[M^{\partial}]_{r,s}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial{\mathcal{D}}}\phi_{s}\,\phi_{r}\,\textup{d}s_{\boldsymbol{x}},

\displaystyle m,n

\displaystyle=1,\ldots,{\mathcal{N}},\,r,s=1,\ldots,{\mathcal{N}}_{\textup{B}},

and vectors

\displaystyle[\overline{p}]_{i}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma}\psi_{i}\,\rho\,\textup{d}\xi,

\displaystyle[p_{k}]_{i}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma}\xi_{k}\,\psi_{i}\,\rho\,\textup{d}\xi,

\displaystyle i

\displaystyle=1,\ldots,N_{\xi},\,k=1,\ldots,N,

\displaystyle[\underline{\overline{f}}]_{m}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{D}}\overline{f}\,\phi_{m}\,\textup{d}\boldsymbol{x},

\displaystyle[\underline{f}_{k}]_{m}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{D}}f_{k}\,\phi_{m}\,\textup{d}\boldsymbol{x},

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle=1,\ldots,{\mathcal{N}},\,k=1,\ldots,N,

\displaystyle[\underline{y}^{d}]_{m}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{D}}y^{d}\,\phi_{m}\,\textup{d}\boldsymbol{x},

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle=1,\ldots,{\mathcal{N}}.

The choice of basis functions, i.e., Legendre polynomials, implies
\overline{P}=I
,
\overline{p}=e_{1}
, and
p_{k}=e_{k+1}
. Furthermore, for some matrix
P
we denote by
P_{R\times C}
,
R,C\in\mathbb{N}
, the submatrices containing the first
R
rows and first
C
columns of
P
.

Due to the numbering of the degrees of freedom, we get a special structure for the finite element matrices and vectors according to

K=\begin{bmatrix}K_{\textup{II}}&K_{\textup{IB}}\\
K_{\textup{BI}}&K_{\textup{BB}}\end{bmatrix},\qquad\underline{f}=\begin{bmatrix}\underline{f}_{\textup{I}}\\
\underline{f}_{\textup{B}}\end{bmatrix}.

Here, the first indices
I
and
B
indicate that the integration is performed with respect to the test functions related to the inner vertices and the boundary vertices, respectively, whereas the second index means that the function to be integrated is a linear combination of basis functions related to the inner and boundary vertices, respectively.

We may now introduce the stiffness and mass matrices obtained by the stochastic Galerkin method

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\textup{II}}

\displaystyle=\overline{P}\otimes\overline{K}_{\textup{II}}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}P_{k}\otimes K_{k,\textup{II}},

(58a)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}_{\textup{IB}}

\displaystyle=\overline{P}_{N_{\xi}\times N+1}\otimes\overline{K}_{\textup{IB}}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}P_{k,N_{\xi}\times N+1}\otimes K_{k,\textup{IB}}=\mathcal{K}_{\textup{BI}}^{\top},

(58b)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{\textup{II}}

\displaystyle=\overline{P}\otimes M_{\textup{II}},

(58c)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{\textup{IB}}

\displaystyle=\overline{P}_{N_{\xi}\times N+1}\otimes M_{\text{IB}}={\mathcal{M}}_{\textup{BI}}^{\top},

(58d)

and the assembled vectors,

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{f}_{\textup{I}}

\displaystyle=\overline{p}\otimes\underline{\overline{f}}_{\textup{I}}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}p_{k}\otimes\underline{f}_{k,\textup{I}},

(59a)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{y}^{d}_{\textup{I}}

\displaystyle=\overline{p}\otimes\underline{y}^{d}_{\textup{I}},\quad\boldsymbol{y}^{d}_{\textup{B}}=\underline{y}^{d}_{\textup{B}}.

(59b)

We furthermore split up the matrix
\mathcal{K}_{\textup{IB}}
into the columns related to
u
and
b_{N}
, respectively, i. e.,

\mathcal{K}_{\textup{IB}}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathcal{K}_{\textup{IB}}^{u}&\mathcal{K}_{\textup{IB}}^{b}\end{bmatrix},

and the submatrices have dimension
{\mathcal{K}}_{\textup{IB}}^{u}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N_{\xi}\cdot{\mathcal{N}}_{\textup{I}}\times{\mathcal{N}}_{\textup{B}}}
,
{\mathcal{K}}_{\textup{IB}}^{b}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N_{\xi}\cdot{\mathcal{N}}_{\textup{I}}\times N\cdot{\mathcal{N}}_{\textup{B}}}
. Analogously, we may split the partial mass matrix into
\mathcal{M}_{\textup{IB}}=[\mathcal{M}_{\textup{IB}}^{u}\ \mathcal{M}_{\textup{IB}}^{b}]
.

With this notation at hand the discrete state equation (
5.1
) can be equivalently written in matrix-vector notation as

\mathcal{K}_{\textup{II}}\,\boldsymbol{y}^{0}+{\mathcal{K}}_{\textup{IB}}^{u}\boldsymbol{u}=\boldsymbol{f}_{\textup{I}}-{\mathcal{K}}_{\textup{IB}}^{b}\boldsymbol{b}

and in the same way, the objective functional (
21a
) becomes, when neglecting the constant terms independent of
\boldsymbol{y}^{0}
and
\boldsymbol{u}
,

\mathcal{J}(\boldsymbol{y}^{0},\boldsymbol{u})=\frac{1}{2}\,\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{y}^{0}\\
\boldsymbol{u}\\
\boldsymbol{b}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}{\mathcal{M}}_{\textup{II}}&{\mathcal{M}}_{\textup{IB}}^{u}&{\mathcal{M}}_{\textup{IB}}^{b}\\
{\mathcal{M}}_{\textup{BI}}^{u}&M_{\textup{BB}}&0\\
{\mathcal{M}}_{\textup{BI}}^{b}&0&0\end{array}\right]\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{y}^{0}\\
\boldsymbol{u}\\
\boldsymbol{b}\end{bmatrix}-\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{y}^{0}\\
\boldsymbol{u}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{y}_{\textup{I}}^{d}\\
\boldsymbol{y}_{\textup{B}}^{d}\end{bmatrix}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\,\boldsymbol{u}^{\top}M^{\partial}\boldsymbol{u}.

(60)

It is easy to check that differentiating the Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{N_{\xi}\cdot{\mathcal{N}}_{\textup{I}}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{{\mathcal{N}}_{\textup{B}}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N_{\xi}\cdot{\mathcal{N}}_{\textup{I}}}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{y}^{0},\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{p})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{J}(\boldsymbol{y}^{0},\boldsymbol{u})+\boldsymbol{p}^{\top}\left(\mathcal{K}_{\textup{II}}\,\boldsymbol{y}^{0}+{\mathcal{K}}_{\textup{IB}}^{u}\boldsymbol{u}+{\mathcal{K}}_{\textup{IB}}^{b}\boldsymbol{b}-\boldsymbol{f}_{\textup{I}}\right)

with respect to the state vector
\boldsymbol{y}^{0}
gives the adjoint equation (
22b
)

{\mathcal{K}}_{\textup{II}}^{\top}\,\boldsymbol{p}+{\mathcal{M}}_{\textup{II}}\boldsymbol{y}^{0}+{\mathcal{M}}_{\textup{IB}}^{u}\,\boldsymbol{u}=\boldsymbol{y}^{d}_{\textup{I}}-{\mathcal{M}}_{\textup{IB}}^{b}\boldsymbol{b},

and with respect to the control vector
\boldsymbol{u}
gives the optimality condition (
22c
)

(\alpha\,M^{\partial}+M_{\textup{BB}})\,\boldsymbol{u}+\mathcal{K}_{\textup{BI}}^{u}\,\boldsymbol{p}+\mathcal{M}_{\textup{BI}}^{u}\,\boldsymbol{y}^{0}=\boldsymbol{y}^{d}_{\textup{B}}.

To conclude the previous investigations, we get the following system of linear equations, equivalent to (
22
),

\begin{bmatrix}{\mathcal{M}}_{\textup{II}}&{\mathcal{M}}_{\textup{IB}}^{u}&{\mathcal{K}}_{\textup{II}}^{\top}\\
{\mathcal{M}}_{\textup{BI}}^{u}&M_{\textup{BB}}+\alpha M^{\partial}&{\mathcal{K}}_{\textup{BI}}^{u}\\
{\mathcal{K}}_{\textup{II}}&{\mathcal{K}}_{\textup{IB}}^{u}&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{y}^{0}\\
\boldsymbol{u}\\
\boldsymbol{p}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{y}^{d}_{\textup{I}}-{\mathcal{M}}_{\textup{IB}}^{b}\boldsymbol{b}\\
\boldsymbol{y}^{d}_{\textup{B}}\phantom{-{\mathcal{M}}_{\textup{IB}}^{b}\boldsymbol{b}}\\
\boldsymbol{f}_{\textup{I}}-\mathcal{K}_{\textup{IB}}^{b}\boldsymbol{b}\end{bmatrix}.

(61)

5.2
Preconditioning of the system matrix

We want to solve (
61
) with an iterative solver like
minres
. Without tailored preconditioners the iteration numbers become incredibly large, in particular when the regularization parameter
\alpha
and the discretization parameters become small. The aim is thus to construct a preconditioner which is robust with respect to these parameters. We utilize the general approach from
[
8
]
, where a preconditioner for a stochastic optimal control problem with distributed control is derived and extend the preconditioner derived for a deterministic control problem with Dirichlet boundary control from
[
49
]
to the case that the problem contains uncertain parameters.

The ideal preconditioner for (
61
) would be the block-diagonal matrix

\mathcal{P}=\begin{bmatrix}{\mathcal{M}}_{\textup{II}}&{\mathcal{M}}_{\textup{IB}}^{u}&0\\
{\mathcal{M}}_{\textup{BI}}^{u}&M_{\textup{BB}}+\alpha M^{\partial}&0\\
0&0&\mathcal{S}\end{bmatrix}

with the Schur complement

\mathcal{S}=\begin{bmatrix}{\mathcal{K}}_{\text{II}}&{\mathcal{K}}_{\text{IB}}^{u}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}{\mathcal{M}}_{\text{II}}&{\mathcal{M}}_{\text{IB}}^{u}\\
{\mathcal{M}}_{\text{BI}}^{u}&M_{\text{BB}}+\alpha\,M^{\partial}\end{bmatrix}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}{\mathcal{K}}_{\text{II}}\\
{\mathcal{K}}_{\text{BI}}^{u}\end{bmatrix}.

The
2\times 2
block involving the mass matrices can be also approximated by their Schur complement

\begin{bmatrix}{\mathcal{M}}_{\text{II}}&{\mathcal{M}}_{\text{IB}}^{u}\\
{\mathcal{M}}_{\text{BI}}^{u}&M_{\text{BB}}+\alpha\,M^{\partial}\end{bmatrix}\approx\widehat{\mathcal{S}}:=\begin{bmatrix}{\mathcal{M}}_{\text{II}}&0\\
0&\mathcal{S}_{{\mathcal{M}}}\end{bmatrix}

with

\mathcal{S}_{{\mathcal{M}}}=(M_{\text{BB}}+\alpha M^{\partial})-{\mathcal{M}}_{\text{BI}}^{u}\,{\mathcal{M}}_{\text{II}}^{-1}{\mathcal{M}}_{\text{IB}}^{u}.

Combining the above ideas yields the block-diagonal preconditioner

\mathcal{P}\approx\begin{bmatrix}{\mathcal{M}}_{\text{II}}&&\\
&\mathcal{S}_{{\mathcal{M}}}&\\
&&\mathcal{S}\end{bmatrix}.

It remains to approximate the 3 diagonal blocks appropriately, so that this preconditioner is cheap to apply but maintaining the robustness. For the first block, containing
{\mathcal{M}}_{\text{II}}
we recall the structure
{\mathcal{M}}_{\text{II}}=\overline{P}\otimes M_{\text{II}}
, see (
58a
), and as
\overline{P}
is a diagonal matrix,
{\mathcal{M}}_{\text{II}}
is also block-diagonal. Applying the inverse of
{\mathcal{M}}_{\text{II}}
thus requires solving
N_{\xi}
systems of linear equations with system matrix
M_{\text{II}}
. For the second block, involving
\mathcal{S}_{{\mathcal{M}}}
we would have to invert a sum of two matrices which is not very practical. However, numerical experiments show that omitting the latter term yields very promising results. To this end, the approximation that is used is

\mathcal{S}_{{\mathcal{M}}}\approx\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{M}}}:=M_{\text{BB}}+\alpha\,M^{\partial},

which is of small dimension
{\mathcal{N}}_{\textup{B}}\times{\mathcal{N}}_{\textup{B}}
and can be explicitly assembled. For the Schur complement of the overall system

\mathcal{S}={\mathcal{K}}_{\text{II}}\,{\mathcal{M}}_{II}^{-1}\,{\mathcal{K}}_{\text{II}}+{\mathcal{K}}_{\text{IB}}^{u}\,\mathcal{S}_{{\mathcal{M}}}^{-1}\,{\mathcal{K}}_{\text{BI}}^{u}

we also omit the second term and arrive at

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{S}}

\displaystyle=\widehat{{\mathcal{K}}}_{\text{II}}\,{\mathcal{M}}_{\text{II}}^{-1}\,\widehat{{\mathcal{K}}}_{\text{II}}

with suitable approximations
\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{\textup{II}}
for the stiffness matrix
{\mathcal{K}}_{\textup{II}}
. Our experiments have shown that in case of
Q\leq 3
the mean based preconditioner

\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{\textup{II}}:=\overline{P}\otimes\overline{K}_{\textup{II}}

works very well so that the resulting preconditioner is robust with respect to the spatial discretization. As
\overline{P}
is a diagonal matrix, the application of
\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{\textup{II}}^{-1}
requires to solve
N_{\xi}
linear systems with system matrix
K_{\textup{II}}
.
For
Q\geq 4
, however, the iteration numbers for
minres
drastically increase. In this case the preconditioner proposed by Ullmann
[
52
]
, i. e., choosing

\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{\textup{II}}:=(G\otimes I_{\mathcal{N}_{\textup{I}}})(\overline{P}\otimes\overline{K}_{\textup{II}})

with

G=\overline{P}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\text{tr}(K_{k,\textup{II}}^{\top}\,\overline{K}_{\textup{II}})}{\text{tr}(\overline{K}_{\textup{II}}^{\top}\,\overline{K}_{\textup{II}})}P_{k}

yields much better results.
Note that the application of
\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{\textup{II}}^{-1}
requires in this case additionally
\mathcal{N}_{\textup{I}}
solves of linear systems with system matrix
G
.

5.3
Constrained problem

In this section we briefly discuss the extension to the case that box constraints on the control variable are present. The discretized optimal control problem, analogous to (
60
), reads as follows

\displaystyle\min\;\mathcal{J}(\boldsymbol{y}^{0},\boldsymbol{u}),\quad\hbox{subject to}\quad\boldsymbol{u}_{a}\leq\boldsymbol{u}\leq\boldsymbol{u}_{b},

(62)

where
\boldsymbol{u}_{a},\boldsymbol{u}_{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{N}_{B}}
are the vectors
\boldsymbol{u}_{a}=u_{a}\,\vec{1},\boldsymbol{u}_{b}=u_{b}\,\vec{1}
, and the inequalities are understood component-wise. Then, if
\boldsymbol{u}
is the solution of (
62
), one concludes the existence of unique Lagrange multipliers
\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{a},\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{N}_{B}}
such that

\displaystyle(\alpha\,M^{\partial}+M_{\textup{BB}})\,\boldsymbol{u}+\mathcal{K}_{\textup{BI}}^{u}\,\boldsymbol{p}+\mathcal{M}_{\textup{BI}}^{u}\,\boldsymbol{y}^{0}-\boldsymbol{y}^{d}_{\textup{B}}+\underbrace{\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{b}-\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{a}}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}

\displaystyle=0,

(63a)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{a}\geq 0,\;\boldsymbol{u}_{a}\leq\boldsymbol{u},\;\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{a}^{T}(\boldsymbol{u}_{a}-\boldsymbol{u})

\displaystyle=0,

(63b)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{b}\geq 0,\;\boldsymbol{u}\leq\boldsymbol{u}_{b},\;\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{b}^{T}(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{b})

\displaystyle=0.

(63c)

The complementary conditions (
63b
),(
63c
) can also be expressed by

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\lambda}-\max\{\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{\lambda}+\sigma\,(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{b})\}-\min\{\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{\lambda}-\sigma\,(\boldsymbol{u}_{a}-\boldsymbol{u})\}=0,

(64)

where
\sigma>0
is an arbitrary fixed real number and the
\max
- and
\min
-operations are understood component-wise. For every pair
(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{\lambda})
, we define the sets of active and inactive indices by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{a}(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{\lambda})

\displaystyle=\{j\in\{1,\ldots,\mathcal{N}_{\textup{B}}\}:\,\lambda_{j}-\sigma\,(u_{a}-u_{j})<0\},

(65a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{b}(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{\lambda})

\displaystyle=\{j\in\{1,\ldots,\mathcal{N}_{\textup{B}}\}:\,\lambda_{j}+\sigma\,(u_{j}-u_{b})>0\},

(65b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{\lambda})

\displaystyle=\{1,\ldots,\mathcal{N}_{\textup{B}}\}\backslash\big(\mathcal{A}_{a}\cup\mathcal{A}_{b}\big).

(65c)

Then, the complementarity conditions in (
63b
)–(
63c
) are equivalent to

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{u}_{a},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{b}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\lambda}

\displaystyle\leq 0

\displaystyle\hbox{on }\mathcal{A}_{a}(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{\lambda}),

(66a)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{u}_{b},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{a}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\lambda}

\displaystyle\geq 0

\displaystyle\hbox{on }\mathcal{A}_{b}(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{\lambda}),

(66b)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}_{a}

\displaystyle<\boldsymbol{u}<\boldsymbol{u}_{b},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{a}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{b}=0,

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\lambda}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\hbox{on }\mathcal{I}.

(66c)

. The preconditioner is not perfectly robust with respect to refinement in the spatial and stochastic space, but the iteration numbers remain in a moderate order of magnitude. It is not reported but further numerical tests have shown that the iteration numbers will not change when choosing the regularization parameter
\alpha
smaller.

6.2
Two dimensional unconstrained problem

Next, we consider a two dimensional unconstrained problem with the following data

{\mathcal{D}}:=[0,1]^{2},\quad y^{d}({\boldsymbol{x}})=x_{1}^{2}\,\sin(\pi\,x_{2}),\quad\bar{f}({\boldsymbol{x}})=0,\quad\bar{a}({\boldsymbol{x}})=1,\quad\alpha=0.5,\quad N=10.

The boundary noise
b_{N}
follows the expansion stemming from the Brownian bridge

b_{N}({\boldsymbol{x}}(s),\xi)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\sqrt{2}}{k\,\pi}\,\sin(k\,\pi\,s)\,\xi_{k},

with
[0,1]\ni s\mapsto{\boldsymbol{x}}(s)\in\partial{\mathcal{D}}
a parametrization of the boundary of
{\mathcal{D}}
. Here, the correlation length and the standard deviation are
\ell=0.9
and
\kappa=0.5
, respectively. The initial mesh for the computation consists of 4 triangles having their base at one of the edges of
\partial{\mathcal{D}}
and the opposite vertex in
(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
. Then, we use twice bisection of each triangle across their longest edge to obtain the mesh on the next refinement level. Again, the exact solution is unknown and we compute the error using the solution for
Q=4
and
h=2^{-8}
as good approximation for
y
. The expected values of the optimal state
{\mathbb{E}}[y_{h}^{\gamma}]
and adjoint state
{\mathbb{E}}[p_{h}^{\gamma}]
are depicted in Figure
2

As can be seen in Figure
3a
the error in the control
\|u-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\partial{\mathcal{D}})}
converges with an approximate rate of
3/2

1

120^{\circ}

[

3/2

[

For the convergence with respect to the polynomial degree

7
Concluding remarks

In this article, we have explored the application of the stochastic Galerkin method to Dirichlet boundary control problems with uncertain data, aiming to minimize the expected tracking cost functional under deterministic control constraints. By utilizing the finite dimensional noise assumption, we transform the stochastic state problem in its very weak form into a high-dimensional deterministic problem and then employ standard piecewise linear and continuous finite elements for all unknowns. A rigorous error analysis is provided and numerical examples demonstrate the validation of the theoretical results and the effectiveness of the proposed preconditioners for both unconstrained and constrained scenarios. As a future study, the obtained results can be extended to a broader range of risk measures
[
48
,
46
]
or integrated with various types of constraints, such as probabilistic or almost sure state constraints
[
24
,
31
]
.

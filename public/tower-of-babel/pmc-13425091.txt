Delayed lichenoid immune-related adverse event after nivolumab discontinuation
Authors: Micaela Guglielmetti-Macchiavello, Pia Poggi, Antonio Guglielmetti-Valenzuela, Eduardo Rozas-Muñoz
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractImmune-related adverse events (irAEs) are well-documented cutaneous toxicities associated with immune checkpoint inhibitors. They typically emerge within the first 3 months of therapy; however, delayed presentations may occur even after treatment cessation. We report a case of late-onset lichenoid dermatitis developing several months following discontinuation of anti-programmed cell death 1 therapy. Histopathological findings and clinical correlation supported the diagnosis. This case highlights the potential for post-therapy cutaneous irAEs and underscores the importance of long-term dermatological surveillance in patients treated with immune checkpoint inhibitors.

Case report

A man in his seventies with stage IV primary cutaneous melanoma located on the scalp (T4aN3bM1) presented with a 3-week history of a progressively pruritic eruption. He had completed a planned 2-year course of nivolumab 2 months before the onset of the skin eruption, and treatment had been stopped because the scheduled therapy period was completed, not due to toxicity or disease progression. His most recent staging studies [computed tomography (CT) and positron emission tomography-CT], performed 2 months prior to presentation, showed stable disease with no new metastatic findings. The patient had no other comorbidities and was taking no medications at the time of evaluation.

Physical examination revealed multiple well-demarcated annular erythematous plaques with overlying vesicles and scattered bullae on the trunk and extremities, with no oral, genital or other mucosal involvement (Figure 1). He was afebrile and appeared to be systemically well. Laboratory tests showed lymphopenia and mild eosinophilia. Two 4-mm punch biopsies from the abdomen were taken: one for routine histopathology and one for direct immunofluorescence. Histopathological evaluation demonstrated orthokeratosis, mild spongiosis, dyskeratotic keratinocytes, interface ­dermatitis and a focal subepidermal vesicle. Direct immu­no­fluorescence revealed granular/globular IgM deposits at the ­superficial papillary tips (Figures 2, 3). The clinical and histopathological features were consistent with the diagnosis of delayed onset of immune-related adverse events (irAEs) associated with nivolumab.

The patient improved completely within 3–4 weeks with topical and short-term systemic immunomodulatory treatment and has had no recurrence during follow-up.

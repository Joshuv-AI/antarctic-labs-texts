LuxS facilitates environmental adaptability and competition capability of avian pathogenic Escherichia coli
Authors: Chang Liu, Jiangang Hu, Mengjie Guo, Beibei Zhang, Xinyu Wang, Weiqi Guo, Yunjie Lu, Jingjing Qi, Mingxing Tian, Yanqing Bao, Lei Deng, Shaohui Wang, Mengling Peng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Avian pathogenic Escherichia coli (APEC) severely impedes the development of the poultry industry and poses a serious threat to public health. The LuxS affects physiological processes such as bacterial virulence, biofilm formation, environmental adaptability, and motility. However, the effects of the LuxS on the environmental adaptability of APEC are not fully understood. Our study aims to investigate the effects of the LuxS on the environmental adaptability of APEC. Thus, the luxS gene mutant and complemented strains of APEC were constructed and characterized. We found that the mutant strain ΔluxS exhibited impaired adaptability to environmental stress, including oxidative, acid, base, heat, and hyperosmotic stress, with corresponding downregulation of stress-responsive genes. For oxidative stress tolerance, marA, fldA, and the rseC gene in the SoxR reduction system showed reduced expression. Genes regulating membrane and acid-base homeostasis, pH-inducible proteins, and the EvgS/EvgA two-component system were downregulated for acid-base stress. Genes related to osmoprotectant synthesis, uptake, potassium regulation, and heat stress adaptability, including extra cytoplasmic stress response (ESR), also exhibited lower transcriptional levels. Furthermore, we found that the ΔluxS mutant strain was impaired in competition capability due to downregulation of type VI secretion system (T6SS) genes. In addition, the mutant strain exhibited lower outer membrane permeability and lower proton motive force (PMF) compared with the wild-type strain. In conclusion, these findings indicate that the LuxS plays a critical role in regulating environmental adaptability, competition capability, outer membrane permeability, and PMF maintenance in APEC.

Introduction

Avian colibacillosis caused by avian pathogenic Escherichia coli (APEC) is difficult to prevent and control due to the diverse serotypes and severe drug resistance of APEC, inflicting enormous economic losses on the global poultry industry (Guerra et al., 2018). Furthermore, the APEC isolates have genetic similarities and commonalities in virulence genes with human uropathogenic Escherichia coli (UPEC) and neonatal meningitis Escherichia coli (NMEC) and have the ability to cause urinary tract infections and meningitis, posing a serious threat to human and public health (Kathayat et al., 2021). The quorum sensing (QS) system is a cell-to-cell communication process in which bacteria sense bacterial population density by monitoring changes in the concentration of autoinducers and collectively alter the global pattern of gene expression to adjust group behavior (Papenfort and Bassler, 2016). AI-2 is formed by spontaneous cyclization and further rearrangement of 4,5-dihydroxy-2,3-pentanedione (DPD) produced by LuxS. This is also the only source of AI-2 synthesis in bacteria. The LuxS participates in the regulation of several virulence-associated phenotypes in E. coli, including surface colonization, adherence, biofilm formation, stress and drug resistance, phage defense, chemoreception, and motility (Mayer et al., 2023).

Bacteria are exposed to numerous stress factors (including elevated temperature, oxidative stress, changes in pH or osmolarity of the environment, etc.) during their epiphytic life and within the host (Figaj, 2025). Bacteria have evolved a range of defensive and adaptive mechanisms to survive under varying environmental conditions (M et al., 2022). These strategies include forming biofilms and extracellular polysaccharides to create physical barriers against environmental factors, moving towards nutrient-rich regions, and inducing the synthesis of stress-responsive proteins to protect their proteomes from stress-induced damage (Figaj et al., 2019; Sionov and Steinberg, 2022; Thomas et al., 2024). When survival resources are scarce, gram-negative bacteria will use their contractile nanomachine T6SS to inject effector proteins into competing cells for survival. T6SS plays a pivotal role in mediating the pathogenicity, interbacterial competition capability, and environmental adaptability (Booth et al., 2023). Previous studies have shown that the LuxS contributes to the environmental adaptability of various bacteria, including Vibrio furnissii, Deinococcus radiodurans, and E. coli (Zhang et al., 2025; Lin et al., 2015; Park et al., 2017). For example, in Vibrio furnissii, AI-2 synthesized by LuxS can reduce the intracellular c-di-GMP level through the AsrK-AsrR system and upregulates the transcription of universal stress proteins, including UspA1, UspA2, and UspE, and then enhances the tolerance to oxidative stress and DNA damage (Zhang et al., 2025). In E. coli, the LuxS/AI-2 QS system is closely related to the osmotic stress response, and researchers have summarized the expression changes of osmotic stress response genes using transcriptome analysis (Park et al., 2017), but for the other stress responses, no relevant studies have provided detailed explanations.

Although some research has shown that the LuxS could contribute to the environmental adaptability, the exact mechanism of LuxS response to various environmental stresses in APEC is still unclear. In this study, we constructed mutant and complemented strains of the luxS gene using the isolated strain XM, aiming to elucidate the role of the LuxS in environmental adaptability of APEC. Additionally, transcriptomic analysis was conducted to provide an understanding of the mechanism by which LuxS regulates the response of APEC to environmental stress. These findings help to elucidate the mechanisms of adaptability in APEC.

Materials and methods

Bacterial strains, plasmids, and growth conditions

All bacterial strains and plasmids used in this study are listed in Supplementary Table S1. APEC strains XM (Ma et al., 2018) and its derivative strains, as well as E. coli strain DH5α, were grown in Luria-Bertani (LB) at 37 °C. Antibiotics were added as needed at the following final concentrations: ampicillin, 100 μg/mL; chloramphenicol, 30 μg/mL.

Construction of mutant and complemented strain

The luxS gene was deleted using the λ Red recombinase system as described previously (Datsenko and Wanner, 2000). Briefly, a linear PCR product carrying chloramphenicol resistance was transformed into XM (pKD46). The luxS gene was replaced with the chloramphenicol resistance gene, which was then removed using plasmid pCP20. The mutant strain was confirmed and named ΔluxS. For the construction of complemented strain CΔluxS, a recombinant pSTV28 plasmid with the luxS gene was constructed and then transformed into the mutant strain ΔluxS. The western blot was performed to verify if CΔluxS can express the LuxS protein. The primers used in this study are listed in Supplementary Table S2.

Determination of bacterial growth

Bacterial growth was measured as described previously (Zuo et al., 2019). Briefly, the overnight culture of XM, ΔluxS, and CΔluxS strains was collected and then diluted (1:100) with fresh LB medium. When incubated to OD600nm = 1, an equal amount of each bacterial culture was transferred into LB at a ratio of 1:100 (v/v) and continued incubating at 37 °C with shaking. The OD600nm value was monitored at 1 h intervals using a spectrophotometer (Implen GmbH, Germany). The assays were performed three times.

Determination of bacterial survival ability under H2O2 stress conditions

The H2O2 oxidative stress assays were performed as previously described with some modifications (Ju et al., 2021). First, the bacterial cultures were diluted to OD600nm = 0.2, and then 50 µL of the adjusted bacterial solution was mixed with 950 µL of H2O2 (0.1 mM) solution. After 1 h of incubation, the surviving bacteria were enumerated by plating on LB agar plates. The assay was performed three times.

Determination of bacterial survival ability under acid, base, and hyperosmotic stress conditions

The survival ability under acid, base, and hyperosmotic stress conditions of APEC strains XM, ΔluxS, and CΔluxS was determined as described previously with minor modifications (Ming et al., 2019; Weber et al., 2010). The bacterial cultures were added to 900 µL of different LB media (pH 4.0, pH 7.0, pH 9.0, and containing 20% NaCl (w/v)), and then incubated at 37 °C for 1 h. Subsequently, the cultures were serially diluted, and the surviving bacteria were quantified. These assays were performed three times.

Determination of bacterial survival ability under heat stress conditions

The survival ability under heat stress of APEC strains XM, ΔluxS, and CΔluxS was determined as described in the assay of bacterial resistance to acid, base, and hyperosmotic stress treatment with some modifications (Lv et al., 2023). The 200 µL of the adjusted bacterial solution (OD600nm = 0.5) was added into EP tubes, and heat shocked at 55 °C for 5 min, immediately placed on ice, and serially diluted. The surviving bacteria were enumerated by plating on LB agar plates. This assay was performed three times.

Interbacterial competition assays in vitro

Interbacterial competition assays were performed as previously described with some modifications (Yi et al., 2019). Briefly, the APEC strains XM, ΔluxS, CΔluxS, and DH5α strain were adjusted to OD600nm = 0.5. Then the donor strain (XM, ΔluxS, or CΔluxS) was mixed with the recipient strain (DH5α) at a ratio of 5:1. A total of 10 µL mixture was spotted on the NC membrane on the low salt LB agar plate and incubated at 30 °C for 6 h. Bacterial spots were collected, diluted, and plated on LB plates with antibiotics to select recipient strains. The competition results were then calculated as the survival of the recipient strain. Three independent experiments were performed for each sample.

Transcriptomic analysis

APEC strains XM, ΔluxS, and CΔluxS were harvested, and three biological replicates of each strain were sent to the company for transcriptome sequencing. Total RNA was extracted, and genomic DNA was removed. Only high-quality RNA samples were used to construct the sequencing library. High-quality reads in each sample were mapped to the XM genome (GCA 002844685.1). Afterward, gene and transcript abundances from RNA-Seq data were quantified. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified, and the adjusted P-value < 0.05 and |log2 fold change|≥1 were considered statistically significant.

RNA extraction and quantification of gene expression

APEC strains XM, ΔluxS, and CΔluxS were cultured to reach early logarithmic phase. Total RNA extraction and cDNA synthesis were performed as previously described (Hu et al., 2022). Briefly, Total RNA was extracted from bacteria with TRIzol reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA), and residual genomic DNA was removed while cDNA synthesis was performed with the HIScript Ⅲ All-in-one RT SuperMix Perfect for qPCR Kit (Vazyme, China) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. qPCR was performed by QuantStudio 3 Real-Time PCR System (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) using SYBR qPCR Master Mix (Vazyme, China). Relative gene transcription was normalized to the housekeeping gene dnaE (APEC) using the 2−∆∆Ct method. The primers used in this study are listed in Supplementary Table S2.

Detection of outer membrane permeability and inner membrane integrity

N-Phenyl-1-naphthylamine (NPN) is a lipophilic fluorescent dye that can penetrate damaged cell membranes but not intact ones. The increased fluorescence indicates a compromised outer membrane, suggesting cell damage. Propidium iodide (PI) is a DNA-binding fluorescent dye that is unable to cross intact cell membranes but can enter cells with damaged membranes. The changes in the inner and outer membranes of APEC strains XM, ΔluxS, and CΔluxS were determined using previously described methods (Liu et al., 2020). The bacterial solution was washed and adjusted to OD600nm = 0.5 with HEPES solution (pH 7.0, plus 5 mM glucose). Then, the bacterial solution was incubated with fluorescent dyes NPN (10 μmol/L) or PI (10 μmol/L) for 1 h, and treated with polymyxin B (0.5 mg/L) as a positive control. The fluorescence values were measured using a microplate reader (BioTek, USA). These assays were performed three times.

Detection of proton motive force (PMF)

The PMF of bacteria consists of membrane potential (ΔΨ) and transmembrane proton gradient (ΔpH). 3,3-Dipropylthiadicarbocyanine Iodide (DiSC3(5)) is a fluorescent dye that is sensitive to ΔΨ (Zhao and Kuipers, 2021). If the membrane potential dissipates, DiSC3(5) will be released into the culture medium and fluorescence increases. BCECF AM itself does not have fluorescence and can be cleaved into BCECF after entering cells. BCECF can be excited to form green fluorescence at an appropriate ΔpH (Hayes et al., 2020). The cell ΔΨ and ΔpH of APEC strains XM, ΔluxS, and CΔluxS were determined using previously described methods (Xia et al., 2021). The bacterial solution was washed and adjusted to OD600nm = 0.5 with HEPES solution (pH 7.0, plus 5 mM glucose). Then, the bacterial solution was incubated with DiSC3(5) (0.5 μmol/L) and BCECF AM (20 μmol/L) for 1 h, and treated with SDS (1%) and CCCP (40 mmol/L) as positive control, respectively. The fluorescence values were measured using a microplate reader. This assay was performed three times.

Statistical and bioinformatic analyses

Experiments were analyzed using the one-way ANOVA and t-test. Data were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). The significant difference was defined as **P < 0.01, *P < 0.05. The “ns” means no significant difference. The heat map analyses were made using the online website of https://www.bioinformatics.com.cn.

Results

The mutant strain ΔluxS and the complemented strain CΔluxS were confirmed by PCR (Fig. 1A). Using primers luxS-in-F/R, no product was amplified from strain ΔluxS, while a 209 bp product was amplified from XM and CΔluxS. Furthermore, the PCR products of 492 bp were amplified from ΔluxS and CΔluxS, and 924 bp was simultaneously amplified from XM by PCR with primers luxS-out-F/R. Furthermore, western blot has demonstrated that the complemented strain CΔluxS can successfully express the LuxS protein (Fig. 1B). LuxS facilitates the environmental adaptability and competition capability of APEC

To explore the role of the LuxS in bacterial environmental adaptability and competition capability, APEC strains were cultured in different media to determine whether LuxS affects the resistance of APEC to oxidative, acid, base, heat, and hyperosmotic stress. The results showed that the survival of the mutant strain was significantly lower than that of the wild-type and complemented strains under oxidative, pH 4.0, pH 9.0, heat, and hyperosmotic stress conditions (Fig. 2A–E). We co-cultured the fresh APEC donor strain and the recipient strain DH5α and counted the surviving recipient bacteria to investigate the role of LuxS in the competition capability. When the donor bacteria were ΔluxS, the survival rate of the recipient bacteria, DH5α, was significantly increased (Fig. 2F). Transcriptomic analysis of DEGs regulated by LuxS

To study the effects of LuxS on regulating the physiological activities of APEC, we performed transcriptomic analysis based on RNA-Seq technology. After deletion of the luxS gene, a total of 316 genes were defined as differentially expressed genes (DEGs), including 126 significantly upregulated and 190 significantly downregulated genes. The Gene Ontology (GO) functional enrichment results showed that downregulated DEGs were enriched in positive regulation of bacterial-type flagellum assembly, positive regulation of organelle assembly, and transcription regulator complex (Fig. 3A). Upregulated DEGs were enriched in various biofilm formation and sulfur compound transport (Fig. 3B). The Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment results showed that the downregulated DEGs were enriched in various metabolism and citrate cycle ( TCA cycle) (Fig. 3C). Upregulated DEGs were enriched in sulfur metabolism and ABC transporters (Fig. 3D). These results indicated that luxS deletion remodels bacterial metabolism and biofilm formation.Fig. 3Transcriptomic analysis of APEC strains. (A) GO enrichment analysis of downregulated DEGs. LuxS contributes to the maintenance of the outer membrane permeability in APEC

The bacterial cell membrane is the first biological barrier to resist the invasion of toxic substances from the outside world. Thus, we measured the outer membrane permeability and inner membrane integrity of XM, ΔluxS, and CΔluxS using NPN and PI, respectively. The results showed that the fluorescence intensity of the mutant strain in the outer membrane permeability test was lower than that of the wild-type and complemented strains, and not significantly different from that of the wild-type and complemented strains in the inner membrane integrity test. The effect of LuxS on PMF

After detection of outer membrane permeability and inner membrane integrity, we further explored the effect of LuxS on PMF. The results showed that the fluorescence intensity of the mutant strain in the ΔΨ test was higher than that of the wild-type strain, and not significantly different from that of the wild-type and complemented strains in the ΔpH (Fig. 6C, D). These results indicated that LuxS plays an important role in maintaining ΔΨ, thereby maintaining the stability of PMF.

Discussion

During the process of infecting hosts and growing in vitro, bacteria are exposed to multiple environmental stresses, such as oxidative, heat, acid, base, and osmotic pressure conditions. The LuxS is involved in pathogenicity, antibiotic resistance, biofilm formation, virulence, and motility, among other processes, playing an important role in bacterial communication (Wang et al., 2019). However, there is no detailed research in APEC to systematically report the impact and mechanism of LuxS on APEC environmental adaptability. In this study, we constructed the luxS gene mutant and complemented strain, tested a series of resistance to environmental stress, conducted transcriptomic analysis and measured cell membrane integrity and PMF to preliminarily understand the mechanism of LuxS adapting to environmental stress. Schematic diagram of LuxS regulating the environmental adaptability of APEC.

Our study suggested that the deletion of luxS gene resulted in decreased resistance to various environmental stresses (including oxidative, acid, base, heat, and hyperosmotic stress) and decreased competition capability. We further observed changes in outer membrane permeability and dissipation of membrane potential after luxS deletion. Outer membrane permeability is the basis for bacterial nutrient uptake and response to environmental stress (Xu et al., 2026). The damaged cell membrane can impair cellular function and gene expression (Dam et al., 2018). PMF is closely related to the respiratory chain involved in intracellular energy conversion and transmembrane material transport, meeting basic physiological needs and supporting common bacterial activities. PMF provides energy for important cellular processes in bacteria, including ATP synthesis, nutrient uptake, and cell division (Biquet-Bisquert et al., 2024). Consequently, the deletion of luxS gene may affect the environmental adaptability of APEC by changing cell membrane permeability and PMF. However, the permeability of the outer membrane did not increase but instead decreased. It is possible that when the overall ability of bacteria to resist environmental stress decreases, reducing the permeability of the outer membrane is a self-protection strategy, or the indirect effect due to altered membrane protein expression. In addition, transcriptomic analysis combined with qPCR detection revealed other possible mechanisms of decreased competition capability and environmental adaptability.

In bacteria, the proton motive force (PMF) energizes vital cellular processes, including ATP synthesis, nutrient uptake, and cell division (Le et al., 2021). The assembly and firing of the T6SS are energy-dependent processes. Many organisms will produce the agents that collapse the PMF to gain a competition advantage, and dissipation of the membrane potential may impair their competition capability (Le et al., 2021). T6SS1 and T6SS2 play important roles in host pathogenicity and interbacterial competition in APEC. The deletion of the luxS gene resulted in the downregulated expression of the xmoV, xmoS, xmoT, and xmoC genes (which make up the substrate complex), the xmoU gene (which makes up the transmembrane complex), and the xmoA gene (which makes up the puncture structure) of T6SS1 (Cianfanelli et al., 2016). Additionally, the expression of xmtE and xmtJ of T6SS2 was downregulated simultaneously. Therefore, LuxS contributes to the competition capability of APEC by maintaining PMF and modulating T6SS expression.

The environmental oxidative stress induces DNA damage, protein carbonylation, and lipid peroxidation in cellular macromolecules, which can lead to cell death (Ezraty et al., 2017). Bacteria have an array of transcriptional regulators, such as OxyR, SoxRS, PerR, UspA, SsrB, MarA, OhrR, SarZ, etc., to sense and transduce bacterial oxidative stress responses. Then, bacteria use efficient thioredoxin reductase, glutaredoxin, and peroxiredoxin redox systems to handle environmental oxidative stress (MS N and Rajendrasozhan, 2022). Notably, FldA, with its flavodoxin product, counteracts oxidative damage by electron transfer and maintains cellular redox balance (Gaudu and Weiss, 2000). Similarly, RseC, as one of the components of the SoxR reduction system, confers peroxide resistance (Lee et al., 2024), while MarA mediates finely tuned responses to multiple stressors, including oxidative stress, pH, host signals, etc. (Ruiz and Levy, 2010). Deletion of the luxS gene downregulated the expression of fldA, rseC, and marA genes. These data suggest that LuxS may respond to environmental oxidative stress through the marA gene, fldA gene, and rseC gene in the SoxR reduction system.

The acid-base tolerance of bacteria is closely related to intracellular pH homeostasis, protection and repair of biomolecules, and metabolic regulation (Xu et al., 2022). The fabZ is an essential core gene in bacterial fatty acid synthesis, maintains membrane homeostasis (Mostafavi et al., 2018), while the can gene encodes carbonic anhydrase, modulating bicarbonate buffering to support acid-base balance (Pujic et al., 2023). The EvgS/EvgA two-component system endows bacteria with resistance to acid, multidrugs, host immune responses, heat stress, and hyperosmotic stress, as well as adhesion and colonization abilities (Zhang and Wang, 2023), and inaA is a pH-inducible protein involved in stress response. We found that after deletion of the luxS gene, the expression levels of fabZ, can, evgA, and inaA decreased. This suggests that these genes may be involved in the bacterial response to environmental pH changes.

Bacteria maintain osmotic homeostasis under hyperosmotic stress via potassium ion (K⁺) regulation and synthesizing or absorbing osmoprotectants such as betaine and proline (Brauer et al., 2023; Deole and Hoff, 2020). In our study, deletion of the luxS gene reduced the expression levels of kdpE, betA, and proQ. In Mycobacterium smegma, the KdpD/KdpE two-component system increases intracellular K⁺ concentration in response to hyperosmotic stress (Ali et al., 2017). The betA is essential for the osmoprotectant betaine synthesis (Deole and Hoff, 2020; Ma et al., 2024), while proQ modulates the betaine transporter ProP (Kerr et al., 2014). Collectively, the deletion of LuxS may reduce the ability to resist hyperosmotic stress of APEC by weakening the KdpD/KdpE two-component system to regulate intracellular K⁺ concentration while reducing the synthesis and transport of betaine via betA and proQ.

The σE controls an extensive regulon involved in responding to the extra cytoplasmic stress response (ESR). RseB is a periplasmic negative regulator of the alternative sigma factor σE, involved in the ESR (Brown and Gulig, 2009). The periplasmic protein DegP controls the protein quality via its protease and chaperone activity, and plays an important role in Gram-negative bacteria to adapt to environmental changes. In Edwardsiella piscicida, deletion of the degP gene caused significantly decreased survival under high temperature, alkalinity, acidity, oxidation stress, and high osmolarity (Li et al., 2025). In addition, reports indicated that deletion of the rpmJ gene encoding ribosomal protein L36 results in temperature-sensitive growth (Ueta et al., 2020). Our results are consistent with the above findings. The ability of the mutant strain to resist heat stress decreased, and the expression levels of rpmJ, degP, and rseB genes decreased.

Transcriptomic analysis further revealed that the upregulated DEGs were enriched in biofilm formation, sulfate transmembrane transport, sulfur metabolism, and ABC transporters, while the downregulated DEGs were enriched in various metabolism and TCA cycle. The finding that luxS deletion upregulates genes associated with biofilm formation suggests that luxS negatively modulates biofilm formation-related gene expression. This may represent a compensatory protective response that partially masks the stress sensitivity phenotype. The significant upregulation of sulfate transport and sulfur metabolism in the ΔluxS strain indicates that LuxS deficiency impairs cysteine synthesis due to the lack of homocysteine, compelling the cells to compensatorily uptake inorganic sulfur from the environment. Collectively, luxS deletion remodels bacterial metabolism and biofilm formation, representing a promising avenue for future mechanistic studies of LuxS function.

In summary, this study investigated the impact of LuxS on interbacterial competition and environmental adaptability in APEC, and preliminarily explored its underlying regulation mechanisms. The results indicate that LuxS plays an important role in promoting interbacterial competition and environmental adaptability in APEC. In the future, we can through in vivo studies to expand the understanding of LuxS function. Future priorities include distinguishing whether LuxS mediates related physiological processes through the signaling molecule AI-2 or the AMC metabolism pathway, identifying the key downstream effector molecules regulated by LuxS in interbacterial competition, and exploring how LuxS senses upstream environmental signals to regulate interbacterial competition and adaptability.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that LuxS plays a pivotal role in the environmental adaptation of APEC. Deletion of the luxS gene significantly impairs bacterial resistance to oxidative, acid, base, heat, and osmotic stress, correlating with the downregulation of key stress-responsive genes. Furthermore, the LuxS critically upregulates the expression of T6SS genes, thereby enhancing the bacterial competition, and it also modulates outer membrane permeability of APEC. These findings are essential for a comprehensive understanding of APEC to resist environmental stress.

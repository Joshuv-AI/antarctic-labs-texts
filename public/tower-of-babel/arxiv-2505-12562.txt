On generalized harmonic quasiconformal Koebe functions
Zhi-Gang Wang, Jia-Le Qiu and Antti Rasila

Abstract
. This paper studies a class of generalized harmonic quasiconformal Koebe functions. It is motivated by the shear construction of Clunie and Sheil-Small [Ann. Acad. Sci. Fenn. Ser. A I Math. 9: 3–25, 1984] and the harmonic quasiconformal Koebe function. Equivalent univalence conditions, pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian norms, coefficient inequalities, as well as growth and area theorems for this family of functions are derived. These findings improve several previously known results.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 31A05; Secondary 30C55, 30C62.

1.
Introduction and preliminaries

Let
\mathcal{S}
be the family of all univalent analytic functions
\varphi
in the open unit disk
\mathbb{D}
with the normalizations
\varphi(0)=\varphi^{\prime}(0)-1=0
.
The extremal functions for the class
\mathcal{S}
are the Koebe function

k(z):=\dfrac{z}{(1-z)^{2}}\quad(z\in\mbox{$\mathbb{D}$})

and its rotations.
It is the extremal function of the Bieberbach conjecture (now known as the de Branges theorem).
Geometrically,
k(z)
maps
\mathbb{D}
to the complex plane minus the negative real axis from
-1/4
to infinity.

The generalized Koebe function
k_{a}(z)
is defined by

k_{a}(z):=\dfrac{1}{2a}\left[\left(\dfrac{1+z}{1-z}\right)^{a}-1\right]\quad\left(z\in\mbox{$\mathbb{D}$};\,a\in\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}\backslash\{0\}\right),

(1.1)

this coincides with the classical Koebe function
k(z)
for
a=2
.
The function
k_{a}(z)
serves as an extremal function for several interesting problems, see e.g.,
[
10
,
15
,
19
,
21
]
.
By noting that if we take
a\rightarrow 0
in (
1.1
), the function
k_{a}(z)
reduces to

k_{0}(z):=\dfrac{1}{2}\log\dfrac{1+z}{1-z}\quad(z\in\mbox{$\mathbb{D}$}).

Over the years, other various generalizations of Koebe functions have been introduced in geometric function theory (cf.
[
6
,
12
,
22
]
).

Let
\mathcal{H}
denote the class of complex-valued harmonic functions
f=h+\overline{g}
in
\mathbb{D}
,
normalized by the conditions
f(0)=f_{z}(0)-1=0
, which have the
form

f(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}+\overline{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}}.

(1.2)

The Jacobian of
f
is given by

J_{f}(z)=|h^{\prime}(z)|^{2}-|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}.

In
[
16
]
, Lewy showed that a harmonic function
f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{H}
is locally univalent and sense-preserving if and only if
J_{f}(z)>0
. This statement is equivalent to
|\omega(z)|=\left|g^{\prime}(z)/h^{\prime}(z)\right|<1
with
h^{\prime}(z)\neq 0
. The quantity
\omega(z)
is called the complex dilatation of
f
.

In 2015, Hernández and Martín
[
13
]
introduced the pre-Schwarzian derivative
P_{f}
and Schwarzian derivative
S_{f}
for a locally univalent harmonic mapping:

P_{f}(z):=\dfrac{h^{\prime\prime}(z)}{h^{\prime}(z)}-\dfrac{\omega^{\prime}(z)\overline{\omega(z)}}{1-|\omega(z)|^{2}},

and

S_{f}(z):=\dfrac{h^{\prime\prime\prime}(z)}{h^{\prime}(z)}-\dfrac{3}{2}\left(\dfrac{h^{\prime\prime}(z)}{h^{\prime}(z)}\right)^{2}+\dfrac{\overline{\omega(z)}}{1-|\omega|^{2}}\left(\dfrac{h^{\prime\prime}(z)}{h^{\prime}(z)}\omega^{\prime}(z)-\omega^{\prime\prime}(z)\right)-\dfrac{3}{2}\left(\dfrac{\omega^{\prime}(z)\overline{\omega(z)}}{1-|\omega|^{2}}\right)^{2}.

Observe that
P_{f}
and
S_{f}
are generalizations of the pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian derivatives of analytic functions.
The corresponding pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian norms of a locally univalent harmonic function
f
are defined as follows:

\|P_{f}\|:=\sup_{z\in{\scriptsize\mbox{$\mathbb{D}$}}}|P_{f}(z)|\left(1-|z|^{2}\right),

and

\|S_{f}\|:=\sup_{z\in{\scriptsize\mbox{$\mathbb{D}$}}}|S_{f}(z)|\left(1-|z|^{2}\right)^{2}.

For recent developments on pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian norms of harmonic mappings, see
[
2
,
3
,
11
,
25
]
.

1.1.
Shearing method

In 1984, Clunie and Sheil-Small
[
4
]
(see also
[
7
]
) proved the following classical result by using
shearing method
.

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
f=h+\overline{g}
be a locally univalent harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
. Then it is univalent and convex in the direction
\theta
if and only if the analytic function
h-e^{2i\theta}g
is univalent and convex in the direction
\theta
.

For further details regarding the shear construction, we refer the reader to
[
8
,
14
,
20
,
24
]
.
Moreover, for a given analytic function
\phi
convex in the direction
\theta
and a prescribed dilatation
\omega
, the above shear construction provides a method to construct univalent harmonic mappings. A classical example of univalent harmonic mappings obtained by using this method is the harmonic Koebe function

{K}(z)=H+\overline{G}=\dfrac{z-({1}/{2})z^{2}+({1}/{6})z^{3}}{(1-z)^{3}}+\overline{\dfrac{({1}/{2})z^{2}+({1}/{6})z^{3}}{(1-z)^{3}}},

(1.3)

where
H
and
G
with
H(0)=G(0)=0
are solutions to the system of equations

\begin{cases}H(z)-G(z)=k(z),\\
\,{G^{\prime}(z)}/{H^{\prime}(z)}=z.\end{cases}

The harmonic Koebe function
{K}(z)
retains the property of convexity in the horizontal direction, a characteristic inherited from the classical analytic Koebe function
k(z)
, with the dilatation
z
.

In 2016, Ferrada-Salas and Martín
[
9
]
used the shearing method to construct a family

\mathcal{K_{H}}(\nu,a,\mu,R):=\{f=h+\overline{g}:\,a\in\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$};\,|\nu|=|\mu|=1;\,0\leq R\leq 1\}

of generalized harmonic Koebe functions, which are sheared by
the system

\begin{cases}h(z)-\nu g(z)=k_{a}(z),\\
\ \ \,g^{\prime}(z)/h^{\prime}(z)=\mu\,l_{R}(z),\end{cases}

where the lens-map
l_{R}(z)
is given by

l_{R}(z)=\dfrac{\left[{(1+z)}/{(1-z)}\right]^{R}-1}{\left[{(1+z)}/{(1-z)}\right]^{R}+1}\quad(z\in\mbox{$\mathbb{D}$};\,0\leq R\leq 1).

They obtained various interesting properties for the family
\mathcal{K_{H}}(\nu,a,\mu,R)
.

1.2.
Harmonic quasiconformal Koebe function

We say that
f
belongs to the class
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}(K)
of
harmonic
K
-quasiconformal mappings
, where
K\geq 1
is a constant, if
f
is an univalent harmonic mapping and its dilatation satisfies the condition

\left|{g^{\prime}}/{h^{\prime}}\right|\leq\lambda
, where
\lambda\in[0,1)
is given by

\lambda:=\frac{K-1}{K+1}\quad(K\geq 1).

A function
f
is called a harmonic quasiconformal mapping, if it belongs to
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}(K)
for some
K\geq 1
(cf.
[
18
]
).

Wang
et al.

[
25
]
in 2024 constructed the so-called harmonic
K
-quasiconformal Koebe function

\displaystyle\begin{split}f_{\lambda}(z)&=\frac{1}{(\lambda-1)^{3}}{\left[\frac{(\lambda-1)(1-3\lambda+2\lambda z)z}{(1-z)^{2}}+\lambda(\lambda+1)\log\left(\frac{1-z}{1-\lambda z}\right)\right]}\\
&\qquad\ \ \ +\frac{\lambda}{(\lambda-1)^{3}}\overline{{\left[\frac{(1-\lambda)(1+\lambda-2z)z}{(1-z)^{2}}+(\lambda+1)\log\left(\frac{1-z}{1-\lambda z}\right)\right]}},\end{split}

which is sheared by the system

\begin{cases}h(z)-g(z)=k(z),\\
\ g^{\prime}(z)/h^{\prime}(z)=\lambda z\quad(0\leq\lambda<1).\end{cases}

It looks tempting to assume that
f_{\lambda}(z)
is an extremal function for the family of harmonic
K
-quasiconformal mappings. They subsequently posed a series of conjectures involving the class
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}(K)
(cf.
[
25
]
).
Noting that for
\lambda=0
, we get the classical Koebe function, and for
\lambda\rightarrow 1^{-}
, the function coincides exactly with the harmonic Koebe function.

It is worth mentioning that Li and Ponnusamy
[
17
]
latter verified that the above function is indeed extremal for several subclasses of harmonic quasiconformal mappings. Building on their work, Das, Huang, and Rasila
[
5
]
have further extended the corresponding results obtained by Li and Ponnusamy in
[
17
]
.

1.3.
Generalized harmonic quasiconformal Koebe function

Motivated by the class
\mathcal{K_{H}}(\nu,a,\mu,R)
and the harmonic quasiconformal Koebe function
f_{\lambda}(z)
, for the system of differential equations

\begin{cases}h(z)-g(z)=k_{a}(z)\quad(a\in\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}),\\
\,{g^{\prime}(z)}/{h^{\prime}(z)}=\lambda z\quad(0\leq\lambda<1),\end{cases}

(1.4)

we construct a generalized harmonic quasiconformal Koebe function

{f}_{a,\lambda}=h+\overline{g}
,
where

h(z):=-\frac{-2\lambda\,_{2}F_{1}\left(1,-a;1-a;\frac{\lambda+1}{\lambda-1}\right)+\lambda-1}{2a\left(\lambda^{2}-1\right)}-\frac{\psi(a,\lambda,z)}{4(a-1)a(\lambda-1)(\lambda+1)}

with

\psi(a,\lambda,z):=\left(\frac{1}{1-z}\right)^{a}\bigg\{4(a-1)\lambda(z+1)^{a}\,_{2}F_{1}\left(1,-a;1-a;-\frac{(z-1)(\lambda+1)}{(z+1)(\lambda-1)}\right)

+2^{a}(\lambda-1)\bigg[a(z-1)\,_{2}F_{1}\left(1-a,1-a;2-a;\frac{1-z}{2}\right)

-2(a-1)\,_{2}F_{1}\left(-a,-a;1-a;\frac{1-z}{2}\right)\bigg]\bigg\},

and

g(z):=\frac{\varpi(a,\lambda,z)}{2a\left(\lambda^{2}-1\right)}

with

\displaystyle\begin{split}\varpi(a,\lambda,z&):=\lambda\bigg\{2\,_{2}F_{1}\left(1,-a;1-a;\frac{\lambda+1}{\lambda-1}\right)\\
&-\left(\frac{1+z}{1-z}\right)^{a}\left[2\,_{2}F_{1}\left(1,-a;1-a;-\frac{(z-1)(\lambda+1)}{(z+1)(\lambda-1)}\right)+\lambda-1\right]+\lambda-1\bigg\}.\end{split}

As in the classical case, the function
{f}_{2,\lambda}(z)
coincides with the harmonic quasiconformal Koebe function
{f}_{\lambda}(z)
.
We denote the class of generalized harmonic quasiconformal Koebe functions by

\mathcal{K}_{a,\lambda}=\{{f}_{a,\lambda}=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{H}:a\in\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$};\,\lambda\in[0,1)\}.

We present the figures of
f_{0,0}(z)
,
f_{0,1/2}(z)
,
f_{2,0}(z)
,
f_{2,1/2}(z)
,
K(z)
, and
f_{3,1/2}(z)
to illuminate the family
\mathcal{K}_{a,\lambda}
(see Figures 1-6).

The primary objective of this paper is to establish equivalent univalence conditions, pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian norms, coefficient inequalities, as well as growth and area theorems for the class
\mathcal{K}_{a,\lambda}

2.
Properties of the family
\mathcal{K}_{a,\lambda}

2.1.
Equivalent univalence conditions for the family
\mathcal{K}_{a,\lambda}

Firstly, we derive univalence conditions for the family
\mathcal{K}_{a,\lambda}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
a\in\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}
and
\lambda\in[0,1)
. Then the function
f_{a,\lambda}
is univalent if and only if
-2\leq a\leq 2
.

Proof.

We first consider the case
a=0
. Then
f_{0,\lambda}=h+\overline{g}
satisfies the conditions

\begin{cases}h(z)-g(z)=k_{0}(z),\\
\,{g^{\prime}(z)}/{h^{\prime}(z)}=\lambda z.\end{cases}

Noting that
k_{0}(z)
maps
\mathbb{D}
onto a convex domain,
then by Lemma
1.1
, it follows that
f_{0,\lambda}(z)
is univalent.

Next, suppose that
0<a\leq 2
. Then
k_{a}(z)
maps
\mathbb{D}
onto a region convex in the direction of the real axis. Again, by applying Lemma
1.1
, we conclude that
f_{a,\lambda}
is univalent for
a\in(0,2]
.

Now, we prove that
f_{a,\lambda}
is non-univalent for
a>2
. Note that
k_{a}(z)
and
\lambda z
have real coefficients. By (
1.4
), we know that
h^{\prime}(z)-g^{\prime}(z)=k_{a}^{\prime}(z)
, and
{g^{\prime}(z)}/{h^{\prime}(z)}=\lambda z
. Thus, the coefficients of
h
and
g
are also real numbers.
Consider the transformation

F(z)=\dfrac{1+z}{1-z},

which maps the unit disk onto the right half-plane. So there exists
z_{1}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{D}$}
such that

\dfrac{1+z_{1}}{1-z_{1}}=e^{i\frac{\pi}{a}}\quad(a>2),

which implies that
z_{1}
is not a real number in
\mathbb{D}
.
Therefore, we have

k_{a}(z_{1})=-\dfrac{1}{a}\quad(a>2).

(2.1)

Suppose that
z_{2}=\overline{z_{1}}
. Since
k_{a}
has real coefficients, by (
2.1
), it is easy to check that

k_{a}(z_{1})=\overline{k_{a}(z_{1})}=k_{a}(\overline{z_{1}})=k_{a}(z_{2}).

(2.2)

Note that

h(z)-g(z)=k_{a}(z).

(2.3)

Combining (
2.2
) and (
2.3
), we get

h(z_{1})-g(z_{1})=h(z_{2})-g(z_{2}).

By observing that the function
g
also has real coefficients, it follows that
g(z_{2})=\overline{g(z_{1})}

and
g(z_{1})=\overline{g(z_{2})}
, hence

f(z_{1})=h(z_{1})+\overline{g(z_{1})}=h(z_{2})+\overline{g(z_{2})}=f(z_{2}).

This shows that
f_{a,\lambda}
is not univalent when
a>2
.

The univalence for the case
-2\leq a<0
is similar to that of
0<a\leq 2
, and the non-univalence for the case

a<-2
is similar to that of
a>2
.
We omit the details.
The proof of Theorem
2.1
is thus completed.
∎

2.2.
Pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian norms

In what follows, we derive the pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian norms of the class
\mathcal{K}_{a,\lambda}
.

Theorem 2.2
.

If
f=h+\bar{g}\in\mathcal{K}_{a,\lambda}
, then

P_{f}(z)=\dfrac{2(z+a)}{1-z^{2}}+\dfrac{\lambda}{1-\lambda z}+\dfrac{\lambda^{2}\bar{z}}{1-\left|\lambda z\right|^{2}}

(2.4)

and

\|P_{f}(z)\|\leq 2\left(1+\left|a\right|\right)+2\lambda^{2}+\lambda.

(2.5)

Proof.

We begin by computing the derivative of the generalized Koebe function

k_{a}^{\prime}(z)=\dfrac{{(1+z)}^{a-1}}{{(1-z)}^{a+1}}.

(2.6)

For
f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{K}_{a,\lambda}
, we have

h^{\prime}(z)=\dfrac{k_{a}^{\prime}(z)}{1-\lambda z}.

(2.7)

Differentiating (
2.7
) logarithmically, it yields

\dfrac{h^{\prime\prime}(z)}{h^{\prime}(z)}=\dfrac{2(z+a)}{1-z^{2}}+\dfrac{\lambda}{1-\lambda z}.

(2.8)

By calculation, the assertions (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) of Theorem
2.6
follow from (
2.8
).
∎

Theorem 2.3
.

If
f=h+\bar{g}\in\mathcal{K}_{a,\lambda}
, then

\displaystyle S_{f}(z)=

\displaystyle\dfrac{2\left(1-a^{2}\right)}{{\left(1-z^{2}\right)}^{2}}+\dfrac{\lambda^{2}}{2\left(1-\lambda z\right)^{2}}-\dfrac{2\lambda(z+a)}{\left(1-z^{2}\right)(1-\lambda z)}

(2.9)

\displaystyle+\dfrac{\lambda^{2}\overline{z}[-3\lambda z^{2}+2(1-a\lambda)z+2a+\lambda]}{(1-\lambda^{2}|z|^{2})(1-z^{2})(1-\lambda z)}+\dfrac{3\lambda^{4}\overline{z}^{2}}{\left(1-\lambda^{2}|z|^{2}\right)^{2}},

and

\|S_{f}(z)\|\leq\lambda^{4}+2\lambda^{3}(\left|a\right|+1)+\lambda^{2}\left(4\left|a\right|+\dfrac{13}{2}\right)+2\lambda(\left|a\right|+2)+2\left|1-a^{2}\right|.

(2.10)

Proof.

By (
1.1
) and (
1.4
), we have

k_{a}^{\prime\prime}(z)=\dfrac{2(a+z)}{(1-z)^{4}}\left(\dfrac{1+z}{1-z}\right)^{a-2},

(2.11)

k_{a}^{\prime\prime\prime}(z)=\dfrac{2\left(1+z\right)^{a-3}}{\left(1-z\right)^{a+3}}\left(3z^{2}+6az+2a^{2}+1\right),

(2.12)

and

h^{\prime\prime\prime}(z)=\dfrac{k_{a}^{\prime\prime\prime}(z)(1-\lambda z)^{2}+2\lambda\left[k_{a}^{\prime\prime}(z)(1-\lambda z)+\lambda k_{a}^{\prime}(z)\right]}{(1-\lambda z)^{3}}.

(2.13)

It follows from (
2.6
), (
2.8
), (
2.11
), (
2.12
), and (
2.13
) that

\displaystyle S_{f}(z)=

\displaystyle\dfrac{k_{a}^{\prime\prime\prime}(z)}{k_{a}^{\prime}(z)}+\dfrac{2\lambda k_{a}^{\prime\prime}(z)}{(1-\lambda z)k_{a}^{\prime}(z)}+\dfrac{2\lambda^{2}}{(1-\lambda z)^{2}}-\dfrac{3}{2}\left(\dfrac{k_{a}^{\prime\prime}(z)}{k_{a}^{\prime}(z)}+\dfrac{\lambda}{1-\lambda z}\right)^{2}

(2.14)

\displaystyle+\dfrac{\lambda^{2}\bar{z}}{1-\left|\lambda z\right|^{2}}\left(\dfrac{k_{a}^{\prime\prime}(z)}{k_{a}^{\prime}(z)}+\dfrac{\lambda}{1-\lambda z}\right)-\dfrac{3}{2}\left(\dfrac{\lambda^{2}\bar{z}}{1-\left|\lambda z\right|^{2}}\right)^{2}.

By (
2.14
), we deduce that (
2.9
) and (
2.10
) hold.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

By setting
a=2
and
\lambda=0
in Theorems
2.2
and
2.3
, they coincide with the classical results
involving pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian norms of univalent analytic functions (cf.
[
1
]
).

2.3.
Coefficients estimates

Now, we provide the estimates of the initial coefficients of
f\in\mathcal{K}_{a,\lambda}
.

Theorem 2.4
.

If
f=h+\bar{g}\in\mathcal{K}_{a,\lambda}
, then

|a_{2}|\leq|a|+\frac{\lambda}{2},

|a_{3}|\leq\dfrac{1}{3}\left(\lambda^{2}+2\left|a\right|\lambda+2a^{2}+1\right),

|a_{4}|\leq\dfrac{1}{3}\left|a\right|^{3}+\dfrac{2}{3}\left|a\right|+\dfrac{1}{4}\lambda\left(\lambda^{2}+2\left|a\right|\lambda+2a^{2}+1\right),

|b_{3}|\leq\dfrac{1}{3}\lambda^{2}+\dfrac{2}{3}\left|a\right|\lambda,

and

|b_{4}|\leq\dfrac{1}{4}\lambda\left(\lambda^{2}+2\left|a\right|\lambda+2a^{2}+1\right).

All of these inequalities are sharp.

Proof.

Suppose that
f=h+\bar{g}\in\mathcal{K}_{a,\lambda}
.
It follows from (
1.4
) that

\left(1-b_{1}\right)z+\sum\limits_{n=2}^{\infty}(a_{n}-b_{n})z^{n}=k_{a}(z),

(2.15)

and

\sum\limits_{n=2}^{\infty}nb_{n}z^{n-1}=\sum\limits_{n=2}^{\infty}\lambda na_{n}z^{n}.

(2.16)

By (
2.6
), (
2.11
), and (
2.12
), we get

\dfrac{k_{a}^{\prime\prime}(0)}{2!}=a,

\dfrac{k_{a}^{\prime\prime\prime}(0)}{3!}=\dfrac{2}{3}a^{2}+\dfrac{1}{3},

and

\dfrac{{k_{a}}^{(4)}(0)}{4!}=\dfrac{1}{3}a^{3}+\dfrac{2}{3}a.

By comparing the coefficients of
z^{2}
,
z^{3}
and
z^{4}
on both sides of (
2.15
), we obtain

\begin{cases}a_{2}-b_{2}=a,\\
a_{3}-b_{3}=\frac{2}{3}a^{2}+\frac{1}{3},\\
a_{4}-b_{4}=\frac{1}{3}a^{3}+\frac{2}{3}a.\end{cases}

(2.17)

Similarly, it follows from (
2.16
) that

\begin{cases}2b_{2}=\lambda,\\
3b_{3}=2\lambda a_{2},\\
4b_{4}=3\lambda a_{3}.\end{cases}

(2.18)

Combining (
2.17
) and (
2.18
) yields

a_{2}=a+\dfrac{\lambda}{2},

a_{3}=\dfrac{1}{3}\left(\lambda^{2}+2a\lambda+2a^{2}+1\right),

a_{4}=\dfrac{1}{3}a^{3}+\dfrac{2}{3}a+\dfrac{1}{4}\lambda\left(\lambda^{2}+2a\lambda+2a^{2}+1\right),

b_{3}=\dfrac{1}{3}\lambda^{2}+\dfrac{2}{3}a\lambda,

and

b_{4}=\dfrac{1}{4}\lambda\left(\lambda^{2}+2a\lambda+2a^{2}+1\right).

The inequalities in Theorem
2.4
follow directly from the above equations.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

By setting
a=2
and
\lambda\rightarrow 1^{-}
in Theorem
2.4
, the coefficients estimates coincide with the harmonic Koebe function introduced by Clunie and Sheil-Small

[
4
]
.

2.4.
Applications of the class
\mathcal{K}_{a,\lambda}

Finally, we derive the growth and area theorems for the class
\mathcal{K}_{a,\lambda}
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
f=h+\bar{g}\in\mathcal{K}_{a,\lambda}
. Then

\begin{split}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}\dfrac{(1-\lambda\rho)(1-\rho)^{a-1}}{(1+\lambda\rho)(1+\rho)^{a+1}}\,d\rho\leq|f(z)|\leq\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}\dfrac{(1+\lambda\rho)(1+\rho)^{a-1}}{(1-\lambda\rho)(1-\rho)^{a+1}}\,d\rho\quad(a\geq 1),\end{split}

(2.19)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}\dfrac{1-\lambda\rho}{(1+\lambda\rho)(1+\rho)^{2}}\,d\rho\leq|f(z)|\leq\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}\dfrac{1+\lambda\rho}{(1-\lambda\rho)(1-\rho)^{2}}\,d\rho\quad(-1<a<1),

(2.20)

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}\dfrac{(1-\lambda\rho)(1+\rho)^{a-1}}{(1+\lambda\rho)(1-\rho)^{a+1}}\,d\rho\leq|f(z)|\leq\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}\dfrac{(1+\lambda\rho)(1-\rho)^{a-1}}{(1-\lambda\rho)(1+\rho)^{a+1}}\,d\rho\quad(a\leq-1).

(2.21)

Proof.

Let
f=h+\bar{g}\in\mathcal{K}_{a,\lambda}
and
|\xi|=r\leq 1
. From (
2.6
) and (
2.7
), we get

\dfrac{(1-r)^{a-1}}{(1+\lambda r)(1+r)^{a+1}}\leq|h^{\prime}(\xi)|\leq\dfrac{(1+r)^{a-1}}{(1-\lambda r)(1-r)^{a+1}}\quad(a\geq 1),

(2.22)

\dfrac{1}{(1+\lambda r)(1+r)^{2}}\leq|h^{\prime}(\xi)|\leq\dfrac{1}{(1-\lambda r)(1-r)^{2}}\quad(-1<a<1),

(2.23)

and

\dfrac{(1+r)^{a-1}}{(1+\lambda r)(1-r)^{a+1}}\leq|h^{\prime}(\xi)|\leq\dfrac{(1-r)^{a-1}}{(1-\lambda r)(1+r)^{a+1}}\quad(a\leq-1).

(2.24)

For
a\geq 1
, let
\Gamma
be the line segment joining
0
and
z
.
Then

\displaystyle|f(z)|

\displaystyle=\left|\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial\xi}\,d\xi+\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial\bar{\xi}}\,d\bar{\xi}\right|

(2.25)

\displaystyle\leq\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\left(|h^{\prime}(\xi)|+|g^{\prime}(\xi)|\right)\,|d\xi|

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}(1+\lambda|\xi|)|h^{\prime}(\xi)|\,|d\xi|

\displaystyle\leq\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}\dfrac{(1+\lambda\rho)(1+\rho)^{a-1}}{(1-\lambda\rho)(1-\rho)^{a+1}}\,d\rho.

Moreover, let
\widetilde{\Gamma}
be the preimage of the line segment joining
0
and
f(z)
under
f
. Then

\displaystyle|f(z)|

\displaystyle=\left|\displaystyle\int_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial\xi}\,d\xi+\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial\bar{\xi}}\,d\bar{\xi}\right|

(2.26)

\displaystyle\geq\displaystyle\int_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}\left(|h^{\prime}(\xi)|-|g^{\prime}(\xi)|\right)\,|d\xi|

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}(1-\lambda|\xi|)|h^{\prime}(\xi)|\,|d\xi|

\displaystyle\geq\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}\dfrac{(1-\lambda\rho)(1-\rho)^{a-1}}{(1+\lambda\rho)(1+\rho)^{a+1}}\,d\rho.

By (
2.25
) and (
2.26
), we conclude that the assertion (
2.19
) of Theorem
2.5
holds.

Similarly, by
(
2.23
) and (
2.24
), we get (
2.20
) and (
2.21
) for the cases
-1<a<1
and
a\leq-1
, respectively.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

By setting
a=2
and
\lambda\rightarrow 1^{-}
in Theorem
2.5
, it reduces to the classical
growth theorem of univalent harmonic mappings in

[
23
, Theorem 1]
.

Denote by
\mathcal{A}(f(\mbox{$\mathbb{D}$}_{r}))
the area of
f(\mbox{$\mathbb{D}$}_{r})
, where
\mbox{$\mathbb{D}$}_{r}:=r\mbox{$\mathbb{D}$}
for
0<r<1
.
We prove the area theorem for the family
\mathcal{K}_{a,\lambda}
.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
f=h+\bar{g}\in\mathcal{K}_{a,\lambda}
. Then

\begin{split}2\pi\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}\dfrac{(\rho-\lambda^{2}\rho^{3})(1-\rho)^{2(a-1)}}{(1+\lambda\rho)^{2}(1+\rho)^{2(a+1)}}\,d\rho\leq\mathcal{A}(f(\mbox{$\mathbb{D}$}_{r}))\leq 2\pi\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}\dfrac{(\rho-\lambda^{2}\rho^{3})(1+\rho)^{2(a-1)}}{(1-\lambda\rho)^{2}(1-\rho)^{2(a+1)}}\,d\rho\end{split}

(2.27)

for
a\geq 1
,

\displaystyle 2\pi\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}\dfrac{\rho-\lambda^{2}\rho^{3}}{(1+\lambda\rho)^{2}(1+\rho)^{4}}\,d\rho\leq\mathcal{A}(f(\mbox{$\mathbb{D}$}_{r}))\leq 2\pi\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}\dfrac{\rho-\lambda^{2}\rho^{3}}{(1-\lambda\rho)^{2}(1-\rho)^{4}}\,d\rho

(2.28)

for
-1<a<1
,
and

\displaystyle 2\pi\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}\dfrac{(\rho-\lambda^{2}\rho^{3})(1+\rho)^{2(a-1)}}{(1+\lambda\rho)^{2}(1-\rho)^{2(a+1)}}\,d\rho\leq\mathcal{A}(f(\mbox{$\mathbb{D}$}_{r}))\leq 2\pi\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}\dfrac{(\rho-\lambda^{2}\rho^{3})(1-\rho)^{2(a-1)}}{(1-\lambda\rho)^{2}(1+\rho)^{2(a+1)}}\,d\rho

for

.

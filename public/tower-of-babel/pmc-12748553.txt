The downregulation of RIZ1 activates the AKT/mTOR in pregnant women with obesity
Authors: Xiaolei Xie, Jiahui Yan, Jinghong Zhang, Xiaoling Huang, Jiaxing Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractRiz1 knockout mice demonstrated obesity with activation of the V-Akt murine thymoma viral oncogene homolog (PKB) (AKT)/mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR) pathway. Whether the decrease in RIZ1 and the activation of AKT/mTOR exist in pregnant women with obesity is still unknown. This study examined the association between RIZ1, AKT/mTOR, and obesity through a prospective methodological approach in pregnant women. A total of 260 pregnant women, including 71 lean, 75 normal weight, 56 overweight, and 58 obesity, were enrolled. Pregnant women with obesity had higher baby weights and cesarean section rates, exhibiting the highest rate of irregular meal habits with high-protein and low-vegetable/fruit dietary patterns. The plasma concentration of C0-C5 carnitine decreased gradually from the lean to the obesity group, while the levels of amino acids and C6-C26 carnitines accumulated in overweight individuals. The expression of RIZ1 was reduced in overweight and obesity groups accompanied by AKT/mTOR activation. In addition, RIZ1 inhibited cellular adipogenesis as well as AKT/mTOR molecules in 3T3-L1 cell. This study presented comprehensive data on maternal and fetal characteristics, inhibited RIZ1, and activated AKT/mTOR molecules in pregnant women with overweight and obesity, providing valuable insights into underlying mechanisms of gestational obesity.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-28714-y.Keywords: RIZ1, Pregnant women, Obesity, AKT, mTORSubject terms: Molecular medicine, Diseases, Endocrine system and metabolic diseases

Introduction

The prevalence of pregnant women with obesity, a growing public health concern, is estimated to range from 25 to 37%1,2. Excessive weight gain during pregnancy can lead to significant health implications such as pre-eclampsia, gestational diabetes, cesarean sections, instrumental delivery, and preterm birth3,4. There are also potential risks to the infant, including hyperglycemia, macrosomia, and hyperbilirubinemia5. Furthermore, excessive weight gain during pregnancy may contribute to long-term postpartum obesity in mothers and increase the risk of early childhood overweight in infants6.

Pregnancy represents a critical period of transition and change, during which behavioral interventions for weight management can effectively improve not only the woman’s health but also that of her baby. Targeting dietary and exercise behaviors may prove effective during this key time7,8. The perception of pregnant women toward weight management is also crucial for improving pregnancy health9. Pregnant women with obesity are more likely to have a high body mass index (BMI) before pregnancy10.

RIZ1/PRDM2 is a histone H3K9me1 methyltransferase with a PR/SET domain that may function in gene expression control11,12 and serve as one of the markers for classifying colon cancer with a CpG island methylated phenotype13. RIZ1 is frequently affected by frameshift mutations or DNA methylation-mediated gene silencing in various tumor types14,15. RIZ1 is a key regulator of dietary carcinogenesis owing to imbalances in methyl donor nutrients16.

The involvement of RIZ1 in metabolism has recently been reported. RIZ1 regulates the expression of the insulin-like growth factor (IGF-1) gene. Overexpression of RIZ1 leads to a reduction in IGF-1 receptor (IGF-1R) phosphorylation and inhibition of extracellular signal-regulated kinase (ERK) and V-Akt murine thymoma viral oncogene homolog (PKB) (AKT) kinase activity17. Alcohol dependence leads to a decrease in the expression of RIZ1, accompanied by reduced levels of H3K9me1 and regulation of genes associated with synaptic communication18. In addition, Riz1 knockout mice exhibited obesity with AKT/mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR) pathway activation19. Further study showed that RIZ1 inhibited AKT3 expression by binding to the AKT3 gene promoter20.

In this study, we enrolled pregnant women with different BMIs in the early stages of pregnancy, investigated their diets, and further detected whether the high BMI pregnant women with decreased RIZ1 and activated AKT/mTOR in white blood cells.

Results

Maternal and infant characteristics of pregnant women

In the first trimester of pregnancy, pregnant women were categorized into four groups based on BMI: lean, normal, overweight and obesity. A cross-sectional study was prospectively conducted, which included an investigation of diet and energy expenditure and collection of blood samples. The detection of blood glucose and glucose tolerance was performed in the second trimester of pregnancy. Parameters on the fetus were analyzed postpartum (Fig. 1).

Maternal and infant characteristics of the study population were presented in Table 1. A total of 260 pregnant women were enrolled, with 71 classified as lean, 75 as normal weight, 56 as overweight, and 58 as obesity. Maternal age, BMI, body fat rate, and basal metabolism values increased from the lean to obesity group; however, there were no significant differences in gestational age or twin rate among the groups. The rates of IVF-ET and abnormal serum screening were highest in the normal group, reaching 21.33% and 18.67%, respectively. No significant differences were observed in infant sex ratio, gestational week at birth, or adverse pregnancy outcomes among the four groups, while the infant weight and cesarean section rate increased with the gain of maternal weight.

Investigate and analyze the dietary patterns of pregnant women in early pregnancy

All individuals were surveyed on diet and energy expenditure. Compared with the normal weight and overweight groups, the proportion of a regular diet of three meals per day was lower for the lean and obesity groups. The proportion of irregular meals was the highest, reaching 17.24% in the obesity group (Fig. 2a). The mean of daily carbohydrate intake was higher in the normal group, while protein intake was higher in both the lean and obesity groups (Fig. 2b–c). As a protective factor against obesity, the mean of vegetable and fruit intake of the obesity group was the lowest (Fig. 2d). There were no significant differences among groups in the types of daily food intake or units of energy expenditure (Fig. 2e–f).

Downregulation of RIZ1 and activation of AKT/mTOR in high BMI pregnant women

The expression level of RIZ1 was observed to decrease from the lean group to the obesity group (Fig. 5a–b). The reduced expression of RIZ1 has been reported to be associated with the methylation of its promoter CpG island. To explore the correlation between RIZ1 expression and its promoter methylation, we further selected 39 samples on lean, 32 on normal weight, 24 on overweight and 32 on obesity for RIZ1 promoter DNA methylation analysis. The results showed that the ratio of RIZ1 promoter methylation increased from the lean to the overweight group, with the highest ratio observed in the overweight group (Fig. 5c–d).

The previous study has demonstrated that RIZ1 inhibits the expression of AKT3 by binding to the CpG island region of the promoter20. The expression of AKT3 was upregulated in the obesity group (Fig. 5e). The Riz1 knockout mice exhibited obesity and activation of the AKT/mTOR pathway. The pAKT/AKT ratio increased from the lean to obesity group, while the highest pmROT/mTOR ratio was observed in the overweight group (Fig. 5f–h). Although the use of white blood cells detection has obvious limitations, these results suggest that the AKT/mTOR molecules may be activated in the high BMI pregnant women.

RIZ1 inhibits adipogenesis and AKT/mTOR in 3T3-L1 cell

We further selected the 3T3-L1 adipose-induced cell model to investigate the role of RIZ1 in adipogenesis. A decrease in the expression of RIZ1 was observed following the induction of adipogenesis in 3T3-L1 cell (Fig. 6a–c). Subsequently, we established the 3T3-L1 stable cell lines overexpressing RIZ1 (Fig. 6d–f). The oil red O staining results showed that overexpression of RIZ1 inhibited 3T3-L1 adipogenesis (Fig. 6g–h). The ratios of pAKT/AKT and pmTOR/mTOR were significantly elevated following induction compared to those before. The overexpression of RIZ1 after adipose induction inhibited the pAKT/AKT and pmTOR/mTOR ratios, suggesting that RIZ1 may suppress the 3T3-L1 cell adipogenesis through the AKT/mTOR pathway (Fig. 6i).

Discussion

The molecular mechanisms of obesity in pregnant women are currently limited. This study identified the following key findings through prospective investigation, without predetermining a causal relationship. (1) Pregnant women with obesity demonstrated advanced age, higher body fat rate and basal metabolism, larger baby weights, and higher cesarean section rate; (2) Pregnant women with obesity showed the highest proportion of irregular meal habits, with a high-protein/low-vegetable diet potentially contributing to obesity. Lean pregnant women had higher levels of C0-C5 carnitine concentration, while overweight women had higher levels of amino acid and C6-C26 carnitine concentration. (3) Pregnant women with high BMI exhibited an increase in fasting blood glucose and Hb1Ac levels along with impaired glucose regulation; (4) Additionally, pregnant women with high BMI exhibited decreased RIZ1 expression and AKT/mTOR activation. In vitro cell experiments demonstrated that RIZ1 inhibited cellular adipogenesis as well as AKT/mTOR molecules.

The mean age of pregnant women in the obesity group was higher than in other groups, indicating a significant association between obesity and advanced maternal age21,22. The IVF-ET rate was the highest in the normal weight group, reaching 21.33% (16/75). Being overweight, obesity, or lean was associated with a reduced success rate of IVF-ET. Therefore, maintaining a normal weight may be more suitable for IVF-ET. Several studies have reported an association between pregnancy obesity or excessive gestational weight gain and having a large-for-gestational-age fetus and newborn23,24, but the obesity group did not show an increase in gestational weeks at birth while exhibiting larger newborn weights. The cesarean section rate gradually increased from the lean to obesity group, which may primarily be attributed to larger fetal size or a higher incidence of complications among pregnant women with obesity25.

Dietary intervention is the primary method for preventing obesity26. Pregnant women with obesity have the highest prevalence of irregular dietary patterns. An irregular diet can disrupt endocrine metabolism and metabolic rhythm, which is a risk factor for obesity27. The group with normal weight exhibited the highest daily carbohydrate intake, while the obesity group showed higher daily protein intake and lower consumption of vegetables and fruits. Pregnant women tended to maintain a balanced-nutrient diet, resulting in no significant differences in food diversity. Consumption of a high-protein, low-vegetable diet may contribute to the development of obesity during pregnancy.

The C0-C5 carnitines are short-chain fatty acid metabolites primarily involved in the process of fatty acid oxidation. High levels of C0-C5 in the lean group suggest that they may act as protective factors against obesity. The C6-C26 carnitines are mainly involved in the beta-oxidation of medium- and long-chain fatty acids in mitochondria. Overweight and obesity are characterized by oxidative stress, which plays a crucial role in the pathogenesis of the obesity-associated metabolic syndrome through increased oxidative stress in adipose tissue28,29. Being overweight is the early stage of obesity. The levels of C6-C26 carnitines are increased in overweight individuals, suggesting that the metabolism of these compounds may be impaired when the dietary structure remains unchanged. Compared to the overweight group, the concentrations of C6-C26 carnitines and amino acids were lower in the obesity group. We speculate that a potential self-protective mechanism may be present in individuals with obesity; however, this hypothesis requires further experimental validation. These findings suggest that excessive intake of medium- and long-chain fatty acids, as well as amino acids, may serve as dietary factors contributing to the development of obesity.

It is widely recognized that pregnant women with obesity often show elevated glucose and HbA1c levels, as well as impaired glucose regulation30. The findings of this study further confirmed this result. Although the use of white blood cells had obvious limitations, the expression of the RIZ1 was decreased in pregnant women with overweight or obesity, accompanied by activation of the AKT/mTOR signaling molecules (Fig. 7). These results further support the previously proposed hypothesis that the absence of RIZ1-mediated activation of the AKT/mTOR pathway contributes to the development of obesity in mice19,20. Pregnant women with high BMI tended to exhibit obesity either prior to conception or postpartum10,31. It remains unclear whether there is a decrease in RIZ1 accompanied by AKT/mTOR activation in women with obesity before pregnancy or after delivery.

A mechanistic model illustrating the impact of RIZ1 downregulation on pregnancy obesity. Diet down-regulates RIZ1 through CpG island methylation, thereby increasing AKT3 and activating AKT/mTOR molecules, ultimately contributing to obesity during pregnancy.

Activation of the AKT/mTOR pathway promotes lipogenesis in 3T3-L1 cell, while inhibition of the pathway suppresses lipogenesis32,33. Overexpression of RIZ1 in 3T3-L1 cell led to the inhibition of cellular adipogenesis and the AKT/mTOR molecules. The transcription factor RIZ1 regulates multiple genes34, but the function of its downstream genes in lipogenesis remained unclear.

This study has several limitations: (1) The number of pregnant women in different BMI groups was limited. The statistical analysis of clinical data or dietary metrics may be influenced by the sample sizes enrolled. (2) There are some concerns regarding genetic testing of white blood cells in pregnant women: (a) White blood cells contain a variety of cell subtypes, and it remains unclear whether RIZ1 and AKT/mTOR expression exhibit the same trend in different subtypes; (b) The expression of RIZ1, which is associated with myeloid cell differentiation, is also influenced by the immune status of lymphocytes35. Inter-individual variations in immune response during early pregnancy within the same cohort may lead to significant disparities in the expression of RIZ1 and AKT/mTOR molecules; (c) Only blood samples can be obtained from pregnant women, and it remains unclear whether other tissues exhibit a similar trend of RIZ1 and AKT/mTOR molecules. However, animal experiments have revealed that RIZ1 deficiency can result in activation of the AKT/mTOR signaling pathway in various tissues, including liver, muscle, and adipose tissue. Conversely, inhibition of AKT or mTOR activity has been shown to significantly reduce mouse weight (unpublished data).

In summary, this study has demonstrated the clinical and dietary characteristics of pregnant women with different BMI groups. Additionally, we have reported a decrease in RIZ1 expression and activation of the AKT/mTOR molecules in the high BMI pregnant women. The findings of this study offer valuable insights into the potential factors contributing to obesity during pregnancy. Furthermore, it identified a potential RIZ1 target for future clinical screening and intervention in pregnant women with obesity.

Methods

Design and clinical data

A random selection of pregnant women participants was carried out at the Affiliated Qingyuan Hospital of Guangzhou Medical University between November 2018 and November 2021. According to the BMI classification of the Chinese population36,37, individuals with a BMI < 18.5 kg/m2 were categorized as the lean group, individuals with a BMI ranging from 18.5 to 23.9 kg/m2 were classified as the normal weight group, individuals with a BMI between 24 and 27.9 kg/m2 were designated as the overweight group, and individuals with a BMI ≥ 28 kg/m2 were identified as the obesity group.

Healthy pregnant women without diabetes or severe infectious diseases who agreed to participate in this study were included. Pregnant women underwent routine clinical assessments, including ultrasound and serological screenings, during 12 to 20 weeks of gestation. The identification of anomalies requires further diagnostic testing or prenatal diagnosis. The peripheral blood of pregnant women was collected for subsequent detection in the first trimester of pregnancy. Dietary surveys were conducted after blood collection. There were no significant differences in the gestational age at sampling or the number of participants across the groups. Furthermore, the random allocation of individuals into each group based on BMI contributed to minimizing the potential impact of confounding variables on the subsequent statistical analyses.

Fasting blood glucose, glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c), and oral glucose tolerance tests (OGTT) were measured during weeks 24 to 28 of the second trimester of pregnancy. Conducting tests at predetermined time intervals can minimize the impact of confounding variables. Pregnant women with overweight or obesity were advised to practice dietary control and appropriate exercise according to clinical practice guidelines.

Monitor the relevant clinical indicators of pregnant women continuously throughout pregnancy and the postpartum period via the medical system. Data on infant gender, birth weight, gestational weeks at birth, delivery mode, and adverse outcomes were collected postpartum for analysis. The research adhered to the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki on research involving human subjects. All participants signed the study’s informed consent form. The study was approved by the ethics committee of the Affiliated Qingyuan Hospital of Guangzhou Medical University (No. QPH-IRB-A0217 and No. LAEC-2022-008).

Diet and energy expenditure survey

The 24-h dietary recall questionnaire was designed based on the international diet and energy expenditure survey study38. During the first trimester of pregnancy, all participants underwent a 24-h dietary recall questionnaire to evaluate their recent dietary intake and energy expenditure. The unit of energy consumption was calculated as the metabolic equivalent (MET) multiplied by time. The MET was assigned a value of 1 for resting, 3.5 for housework and cooking, 4.8 for walking, and 7 for running.

Body fat rate and basal metabolism measurement

After completing the diet survey, the portable upper-body type device (Omron HBF-306) was used to measure the body fat rate and basal metabolism of all participants. According to the operational instruction of the Omron HBF-306, the parameters of gender, height, and weight were inputted into the device, and both hands were positioned horizontally within the sensing area for measurement.

Blood collection and white cells separation

Five milliliters of venous blood were collected from each participant and subsequently centrifuged at 4 °C with 1600 g for 10 min. The upper layer of peripheral blood was collected as plasma and stored at − 80 °C for subsequent analysis. The middle layer was the region enriched with white blood cells. Two times the volume of red blood cell lysate (C3702, Beyotime Biotech Inc., Shanghai) was added to the middle layer and mixed by shaking. Subsequently, it was centrifuged at 4 °C with 450 g for 10 min to obtain the precipitate.

Mass spectrometry analysis

Fifty-microliter plasma samples from pregnant women were collected and permeated onto the designated circle of a blood collection card. A circular filter paper with a diameter of 3.2 mm was created by using a hole punch. The filter papers were filled with 125 μL of diluted solution. The microplates were then placed in an incubation oscillator for 30 min at 45℃ and 700 rpm. After incubation, 100 μL of supernatant was transferred to a V-bottom microplate for analysis using mass spectrometry (API3200MD, AB Sciex Pte Ltd., USA).

qRT-PCR, methylation-specific PCR and western blot

Total RNA was extracted from the white blood cell pellets with the RNA isolation Trizol method (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., USA). Reverse transcription was performed instantly after RNA extraction using the Evo M-MLV RT Mix Kit (Accurate Biotechnology, China). The qPCR was performed using the SYBR Green Pro Taq HS (Accurate Biology, China) in a 7500 Fast qPCR machine (Applied Biosystems, USA). The protocol included two steps: 30 s at 95 °C and 40 cycles of 95 °C for 5 s, 60 °C for 30 s. All gene-specific primers were confirmed to give a single expected size band. The target genes were RIZ1 or AKT3, while the reference gene used for normalization was β-actin (See Supporting Information Table S1).

Genomic DNAs from the white blood cells were treated with sodium bisulfite, and the treated DNA was purified using the Wizard Column Kit (Promega Biotech Co., Ltd., Beijing). PCR primers targeting the RIZ1 CpG island were designed following the protocol described by Du et al. (See Supporting Information Table S1)14.

The white blood cell pellets were resuspended in lysis buffer (RIPA:PMSF:phosphatase inhibitor = 1000:10:1), vortexed, and incubated on ice for 30 min. The lysate was quantified using the Bio-Rad protein assay (Bio-Rad Laboratories Inc., USA). Equal amounts of proteins were separated by 10% sodium dodecyl sulfate–polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) and subsequently transferred to polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membranes (Merck Millipore, USA). Primary antibodies were incubated overnight at 4 °C, followed by horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated secondary antibodies (Sigma-Aldrich Inc., USA) for enhanced chemiluminescence detection. Antibodies for total AKT, phosphorylated AKT (Ser473), mTOR, and phosphorylated mTOR (Ser2448) were obtained from Cell Signaling Technology. The bands were quantified using Image J software.

Blood glucose, HbA1c and OGTT detection

Fasting blood glucose test was performed after fasting overnight during 24 to 28 weeks of pregnancy. Three milliliters of peripheral blood were collected for glucose detection. The serum glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c) was measured using the ADAMS A1C HA-8180 analyzer (Arkray Inc., Japan). The oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) was conducted after fasting overnight. A solution containing 75 g of glucose dissolved in 300 ml of warm water should be taken by pregnant women within 5 min. Three milliliters of peripheral blood were collected at 1 h and 2 h after drinking glucose water. The blood glucose levels were quantified using the Cobas 8000 C702 instrument (Roche Ltd., Switzerland).

Cell culture and induction

The 3T3-L1 pre-adipocyte cell was obtained from Haixing Biosciences (Suzhou, China). The cell was cultured in high-glucose DMEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin–streptomycin under a controlled environment of 37 °C and 5% CO2. To induce the differentiation of 3T3-L1 cells into mature adipocytes, the culture medium was replaced with an induction medium consisting of DMEM supplemented with 10% FBS, 1% glutamine, 5 μg/ml insulin, 1 μM dexamethasone, 500 μM 3-isobutyl-1-methylxanthine (IBMX), and 2 μM rosiglitazone. After two days, the induction medium was replaced with a maintenance medium consisting of DMEM containing 10% FBS and 5 μg/ml insulin.

Establishment of stable cell line

The lentivirus packaging plasmids psPAX2 and pMD2.G were provided by a faculty member from the School of Basic Medicine of Zhengzhou University. Retroviral plasmid vectors of pCDH-RIZ1-GFP-Puro-3 × Flag were constructed by Youbao Biological Company. The above three plasmids were transfected into 50% confluent 293 T cell using SuperKine Lipo3.0 (Yongjin Biotechnology, China) for 24 h. Viral supernatants were collected at 72 h after transfection. The supernatant was used to infect 3T3-L1 cell. Cells were then cultured in medium containing 2 μg/ml of puromycin for seven days to select the RIZ1-stably expressed cell line.

Oil red O staining

The cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 1 h, then washed twice with PBS in 60% isopropanol, and subsequently stained with Oil Red O solution (Haixing Biosciences, China) for 1 h. The cells were washed with 60% isopropanol for 10 s, followed by three washes with PBS. Lipid droplets were visualized under an inverted microscope.

Statistical analysis

The data were analyzed using GraphPad Prism 6.0 software. The comparison of rates among different groups was analyzed by the R × C chi-square test. The comparison between the two groups of data was performed using an unpaired t test. When the data are normally distributed and the variances across multiple datasets are equal, statistical analysis is conducted using one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s test. When the data do not follow a normal distribution or the variances across multiple groups are not equal, it is recommended to use the Kruskal–Wallis test followed by Dunn’s test for statistical analysis. Maternal and infant characteristics in pregnant women were presented as median (interquartile range), while values from dietary surveys and gene expression analyses were presented as mean ± SEM. Statistical significance was set at *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001, and ****P < 0.0001.

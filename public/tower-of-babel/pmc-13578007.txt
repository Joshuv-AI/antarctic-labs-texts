Deucravacitinib 5-Year Safety and Efficacy Results in Plaque Psoriasis: A Phase 3 Open-Label Extension of Randomized Clinical Trials
Authors: April W Armstrong, Mark Lebwohl, Richard B Warren, Akimichi Morita, Carle Paul, Diamant Thaçi, Matthias Augustin, Lluís Puig, Carolin Daamen, Janice Li, Alessandra Alió, Angela Aggrey-Amable, Julie Scotto, Zoran Popmihajlov, John Vaile, Bruce Strober
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDeucravacitinib, an oral, selective, tyrosine kinase 2 inhibitor, is approved for adults with moderate to severe plaque psoriasis who are candidates for systemic therapy and for adults with active psoriatic arthritis.ObjectiveWe evaluated deucravacitinib safety and efficacy over 5 years in the phase 3 POETYK PSO-1, PSO-2, and long-term extension (LTE) trials in patients with moderate to severe plaque psoriasis.MethodsPSO-1 and PSO-2 (parent trials) randomized patients 1:2:1 to oral placebo, deucravacitinib 6 mg once daily, or apremilast 30 mg twice daily. At 52 weeks, patients enrolled in the LTE trial received open-label deucravacitinib. Safety was reported as exposure-adjusted incidence rates (EAIRs) per 100 person-years (PY). Clinician- and patient-reported outcomes were analyzed using modified nonresponder imputation in patients receiving continuous deucravacitinib from day 1 (PSO-1/PSO-2) through 5 years.ResultsOverall, 1519 patients received one or more deucravacitinib dose; total exposure was 5046.7 PY through data cutoff (September 2, 2024). EAIRs/100 PY were comparable or decreased from the 1-year to 5-year cumulative period for adverse events (AEs) (229.23, 127.40, respectively), serious AEs (5.68, 5.06), discontinuation due to AEs (4.38, 2.09), deaths (0.20, 0.22), serious infections excluding coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) (1.53, 0.94), malignancies (1.02, 0.92), major adverse cardiovascular events (0.30, 0.34), and venous thromboembolism (0.20, 0.06). Clinical outcomes were well-maintained in patients receiving continuous deucravacitinib (n = 513) from 1 through 5 years, including achievement of a ≥ 75% reduction from baseline in the Psoriasis Area and Severity Index (1 year, 72.1% [95% CI 68.2–76.1]; 5 years, 67.3% [62.0–72.6]) and a static Physician Global Assessment score of 0 (clear) or 1 (almost clear) (1 year, 57.5% [53.1–61.9]; 5 years, 52.6% [47.0–58.1]). Dermatology Life Quality Index 0 or 1 was well-maintained from 1 year (52.5% [48.0–57.1]) through 5 years (45.4% [40.0–50.8]).ConclusionsThese findings demonstrate a consistent safety profile with no new safety signals and durable clinical response through 5 years of treatment with deucravacitinib.Clinical Trial RegistrationNCT03624127, NCT03611751, NCT04036435.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1007/s40257-026-01055-w.

Key Points

Deucravacitinib is an oral, selective tyrosine kinase 2 inhibitor approved in multiple countries for the treatment of adults with moderate to severe plaque psoriasis.In 1519 patients with moderate to severe plaque psoriasis treated through 5 years with one or more doses of deucravacitinib in the phase 3 POETYK PSO-1, PSO-2, and long-term extension trials, no new safety signals were observed compared with the first year, and of the 513 patients continuously treated with deucravacitinib, most maintained clinical response through 5 years.These findings provide support for deucravacitinib as an efficacious, well-tolerated, long-term treatment for patients with moderate to severe plaque psoriasis.

Introduction

Plaque psoriasis is a chronic, immune-mediated, inflammatory disease of the skin. Tyrosine kinase 2 (TYK2), an intracellular enzyme, mediates signaling of select inflammatory cytokines, including interleukin-12, interleukin-23, and type I interferons, which are implicated in psoriasis pathogenesis and other immune-mediated diseases [1, 2]. Deucravacitinib, a first-in-class, oral, selective TYK2 inhibitor, is approved for the treatment of adults with moderate to severe plaque psoriasis who are candidates for systemic therapy and for adults with active psoriatic arthritis [3–8]. It is also in development for treatment of moderate to severe plaque psoriasis in children and adolescents and other immune-mediated inflammatory diseases, including systemic lupus erythematosus and Sjögren’s disease. Deucravacitinib has a unique mechanism of action, inhibiting TYK2 by binding to the regulatory domain [1, 9] rather than the catalytic domain where Janus kinase (JAK) 1/2/3 inhibitors bind, driving deucravacitinib selectivity for TYK2 over JAK1/2/3 [1, 9].

Given the chronic nature of plaque psoriasis, long-term data are essential when evaluating the sustained efficacy and safety of systemic treatments. In clinical research, the 5-year mark is often considered a critical milestone, offering a meaningful view of a therapy’s long-term benefit–risk profile. Although randomized controlled trials provide foundational data, extended follow-up is key for understanding durability of response, late-emerging adverse events (AEs), and patient-reported outcomes (PROs) over time.

The global phase 3 POETYK PSO-1 (NCT03624127), PSO-2 (NCT03611751), and ongoing, open-label, long-term extension (LTE) (NCT04036435) trials demonstrated the efficacy and safety of deucravacitinib through 4 years in patients with moderate to severe plaque psoriasis [10–14]. Here, we report the safety, both cumulative and by each year of the trial, clinical efficacy, and patient-reported efficacy of deucravacitinib over a 5-year cumulative period (PSO-1/PSO-2, 1 year; LTE, 4 years).

Methods

Study Design and Patient Population

The POETYK PSO study designs have been previously reported [10, 11]. Briefly, adults with moderate to severe plaque psoriasis (Psoriasis Area and Severity Index [PASI] ≥ 12, static Physician Global Assessment [sPGA] score of 0 [clear] or 1 [almost clear] [sPGA 0/1] ≥ 3, and body surface area involvement ≥ 10% at baseline) in PSO-1 and PSO-2 were randomized 1:2:1 to oral placebo, deucravacitinib 6 mg once daily (QD), or apremilast 30 mg twice daily (Supplementary Fig. 1 in the Electronic Supplementary Material); blinded treatment switches occurred at week 16 (placebo) and week 24 (apremilast) [10, 11]. At week 52, patients could enroll in the ongoing LTE trial and receive open-label deucravacitinib as prespecified in the PSO-1 and PSO-2 protocols [12–14].

Independent institutional review board approvals were obtained for these studies, and all participants provided written informed consent. The study followed the Transparent Reporting of Evaluations with Nonrandomized Designs (TREND) reporting guidelines.

Analysis Populations

Safety and efficacy were assessed through September 2, 2024 (week 256; 5 years). Safety data are reported in the as-treated population (patients receiving one or more dose of deucravacitinib). Efficacy data are reported for the pooled patient population that received continuous deucravacitinib treatment in PSO-1 and PSO-2 and were treated through a cumulative 256 weeks in the LTE trial.

Assessments

Safety was the primary endpoint; assessments included AEs, serious AEs (SAEs), deaths, AEs of interest (AEIs; detailed in the methods in the Electronic Supplementary Material), AEs leading to treatment discontinuation, and select laboratory parameters. AEs were ascribed to the treatment group when the event first occurred; when patients had multiple AEs of the same type, they were counted once. AEs were reported by the patient spontaneously or were elicited during open-ended questioning, or evaluation of the patient and were coded per the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA, version 27.0).

Global efficacy outcomes included achievement of ≥ 75%/≥ 90%/100% reduction from baseline in PASI (PASI 75/90/100), sPGA 0/1, sPGA 0 (clear), absolute PASI thresholds, and mean percent change from baseline in PASI. Site-specific efficacy outcomes were scalp-specific PGA score of 0 (clear) or 1 (almost clear) (ss-PGA 0/1) in patients with baseline ss-PGA ≥ 3 and PGA–Fingernail (PGA-F) score of 0 (clear) or 1 (almost clear) (PGA-F 0/1) in patients with baseline PGA-F ≥ 3. PROs included mean change from baseline in Psoriasis Symptoms and Signs Diary (PSSD) total score, and achievement of a Dermatology Life Quality Index (DLQI) score of 0 or 1 (DLQI 0/1) in patients with baseline DLQI ≥ 2. Assessments of ss-PGA 0/1, PGA-F 0/1, and PROs were collected on a different schedule and are reported through week 244. Data on self-declared race/ethnicity from patients were collected at screening to assess the generalizability of the study findings.

Statistical Analysis

Safety data are reported as frequencies and exposure-adjusted incidence rates (EAIRs) per 100 person-years (PY) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) (see method details on EAIR calculations and the year-by-year safety analyses in the Electronic Supplementary Material). To evaluate efficacy outcomes, the prespecified as-observed analysis was used, as well as three imputation methods for missing data (additional methods are detailed in the Electronic Supplementary Methods). The Clopper-Pearson method was used to calculate 95% CIs when there were no missing data or for modified nonresponder imputation; otherwise, the Rubin method was used for multiple imputation.

Results

Patient Populations and Treatment Exposure

Baseline patient demographics and disease characteristics for the safety (n = 1519) and efficacy (n = 513) populations have been previously described and were typical of patients with moderate to severe plaque psoriasis (Supplementary Table 1 in the Electronic Supplementary Methods) [12, 13]. In the safety population, the total deucravacitinib exposure at data cutoff was 5046.7 PY, with a median exposure of 1539 days (4.2 years); 449 patients (29.6%) had ≥ 60 months of exposure (Supplementary Table 2). Of the safety population, as of data cutoff, 722 (47.5%) were continuing and 706 (46.5%) had discontinued treatment (Fig. 1). Of 513 patients in the efficacy population who received deucravacitinib from day 1 and entered the LTE trial, 275 (54.0%) were included in the year 5 (week 256) efficacy analysis, 28 (5.5%) were ongoing but had not reached week 256, one (0.2%) had completed but not reached week 256, two (0.4%) were ongoing but had missing week 256 data, and 207 (40.4%) had discontinued treatment (AEs, 25 [4.9%]; lack of efficacy, 16 [3.1%]) (Fig. 1).

Overall Safety

Incidence rates of AEs, SAEs, and discontinuations due to AEs decreased through the 5-year cumulative period compared with the first year (Table 1). Incidence rates of the most common AEs (EAIR ≥ 5/100 PY) through the 5-year cumulative period remained consistent with rates reported through 1 year, including nasopharyngitis (5-year EAIR [95% CI], 9.12/100 PY [8.21–10.11]), coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) (8.22/100 PY [7.38–9.12]), and upper respiratory infection (5.82/100 PY [5.13–6.58]). The most common SAEs were related to COVID-19 (54 [3.6%]; 1.09/100 PY [0.82–1.42]) (Supplementary Table 3). Eleven deaths were reported through the 5-year cumulative period, which have been previously discussed [14]; no deaths were reported from the end of year 4 through year 5.

EAIRs of AEIs were comparable or decreased from 1 year to 5 years (Table 1), including herpes zoster (0.58/100 PY [0.39–0.83]), major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) (0.34/100 PY [0.20–0.54]), venous thromboembolism (0.06/100 PY [0.01–0.17]), malignancies (0.92/100 PY [0.67–1.22]), and malignancies excluding nonmelanoma skin cancer (NMSC) (0.51/100 PY [0.33–0.75]). The serious infection rate was higher in the 5-year cumulative period (1.93/100 PY [1.56–2.36]) compared with 1 year (1.73/100 PY [1.08–2.79]). Excluding COVID-19, the serious infection rate was lower at 5 years (0.94/100 PY [0.69–1.25]) compared with 1 year (1.53/100 PY [0.86–2.52]). No venous thromboembolism events were observed after year 2, and EAIRs of MACE remained stable from year 2 through year 5 [12]. One new case of stage III–IV T cell histiocyte-rich diffuse large B cell lymphoma was reported in a male in his 50s at week 228 of the LTE trial; this event was determined by the investigator to be unrelated to treatment. The ratio of basal cell carcinoma to squamous cell carcinoma events through 5 years remained 2:1 [14]. There were no new cases of malignant melanoma in the 5-year report; two cases were previously reported through year 2 [12]. Other AEIs related to the known safety profile of deucravacitinib decreased in frequency from the 1-year to 5-year cumulative period, including acne, folliculitis, and oral ulcer.

A year-by-year analysis of deucravacitinib exposure per 100 PY showed a generally consistent safety profile from 1 year to 5 years for all AEIs (Table 2). Incidence rates for some non-serious AEIs, such as acne, folliculitis, and oral ulcers decreased after the first year of treatment. While there was variability in AEI incidence rates, no increasing trends or late-emerging serious outcomes were observed after longer exposure to deucravacitinib.

Discussion

Achieving 5 years of follow-up in a clinical trial for a chronic inflammatory disease like psoriasis is widely accepted as an important milestone. These data offer a long-term view of deucravacitinib’s benefit–risk profile and provide evidence for its sustained safety and efficacy. In this pooled analysis from the PSO-1, PSO-2, and LTE trials, deucravacitinib demonstrated a stable safety profile through 5 years of exposure, with no increases in overall rates of AEs/SAEs over time, with the exception of COVID-19 (due to the concurrent global pandemic), no increases in rates of discontinuation due to AEs, and no emergence of new safety signals. Incidence rates for AEIs, such as serious infections (excluding COVID-19), malignancies, and MACE, were comparable from the 1-year through the 5-year cumulative analysis and from the 4-year to 5-year analysis [14].

Epidemiological reference data presented in Supplementary Figure 4 contextualize the observed 5-year AE incidence rates by situating them within the range reported for other systemic psoriasis therapies, LTEs, disease registries, and real-world reference populations [15–26]. Across safety outcomes of interest, including serious infections, malignancies excluding NMSC, and MACE, the observed deucravacitinib 5-year AE incidence rates were consistent with these external reference sources. Reference data were selected to align as closely as possible with the deucravacitinib clinical trial population and the 5-year timepoint, prioritizing controlled psoriasis trial data when available and real-world sources for broader contextual reference. These data are presented for contextual reference only, and no direct or quantitative cross-trial comparisons were performed; thus, cross-trial comparisons should be interpreted with caution.

Mean laboratory changes commonly observed with JAK1/2/3 inhibitors (decreases in lymphocytes, hemoglobin, and platelets; increases in alanine aminotransferase, aspartate aminotransferase, creatinine, and cholesterol) [50] were generally not observed over 5 years of deucravacitinib treatment. Grade ≥ 3 laboratory AEs and discontinuations due to laboratory AEs were rare in patients treated with deucravacitinib throughout the 5-year period. In most patients treated with deucravacitinib with laboratory AEs, baseline severity grades were maintained.

To further characterize the timing of deucravacitinib AEs and evaluate the potential for late-emerging safety signals, year-by-year EAIR analyses were conducted. Observed fluctuations in certain safety outcomes may reflect external factors such as the COVID-19 pandemic, which overlapped with the mid-period of the LTE trial. For serious safety outcomes, including serious infections (excluding COVID-19), malignancies, and MACE, year-by-year incidence rates remained within a similar range over time, with no consistent upward trend through 5 years of exposure. No trends or clusters of malignancy types were noted. For several non-SAEs, including acne, folliculitis, and oral ulcer, incidence rates were highest during the first year of treatment and declined thereafter, consistent with an early treatment-emergent pattern rather than cumulative toxicity. Taken together, these findings support a stable long-term safety profile for deucravacitinib with 5 years of exposure. A notable strength of these data is the duration and breadth of follow-up across the pooled phase 3 program, which provides clinically meaningful evidence to inform long-term treatment decisions for patients with moderate to severe plaque psoriasis who may require ongoing systemic therapy.

Clinical response rates were maintained through 5 years in patients treated continuously with deucravacitinib from day 1 in the parent trials. PASI 75, PASI 90, PASI 100, sPGA 0/1, sPGA 0, ss-PGA 0/1, and PGA-F 0/1 response rates were all maintained from year 1 through 5 years, demonstrating durable and continued efficacy in overall psoriasis and in the hard-to-treat areas of scalp and fingernails. Approximately half of the patients (48.2%) treated with deucravacitinib achieved an absolute PASI ≤ 2 at 1 year, with 74.4% achieving PASI ≤ 5; the majority of patients maintained absolute PASI levels through 5 years.

PROs were also maintained over 5 years. Long-term improvements from baseline in self-assessed psoriasis symptoms and signs (PSSD total score) were reported in patients treated with deucravacitinib. Approximately half of patients reported that psoriasis did not negatively impact their quality of life (achievement of DLQI 0/1) over 5 years of deucravacitinib treatment.

These analyses may have some limitations. Like other clinical trials, the study population may not fully represent the real-world patient population, because of the strict study inclusion and exclusion criteria in the PSO-1 and PSO-2 parent trials. The size of the LTE trial efficacy population is limited to only those patients who had continuous deucravacitinib treatment in the parent trials and does not include patients who discontinued any of the parent trials. There was no active comparator to deucravacitinib in the LTE trial, and the open-label treatment during this period may bias the reporting by patients. Additionally, malignancy rates were generally low, but the effect of treatment on cancer incidence may take longer than 5 years to determine. Although this ongoing extension study offers insights into the long-term safety and efficacy of deucravacitinib, further real-world evidence will help to verify clinical trial results.

Conclusions

In chronic conditions such as plaque psoriasis, where ongoing treatment is often necessary, long-term data are critical in informing clinical decision-making. This 5-year timepoint provides valuable insight into the sustained performance of deucravacitinib, including safety, durability of response, and impact on quality of life. The findings presented here demonstrate that deucravacitinib has a consistent safety profile with no new safety signals over 5 years. Deucravacitinib also demonstrated durable clinical and patient-reported efficacy outcomes through 5 years. The current findings contribute to the long-term evidence base in psoriasis and offer a more complete understanding of the role of deucravacitinib, a first-in-class TYK2 inhibitor, in moderate to severe plaque psoriasis management over time.

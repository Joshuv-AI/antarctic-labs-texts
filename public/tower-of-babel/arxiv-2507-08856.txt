A self-contained proof of the Artin-Wedderburn theorem in the case of finite-dimensional Von Neumann algebras
Octave Mestoudjian, Pablo Arrighi

Abstract
We provide a self-contained proof of the Artin-Wedderburn theorem in the case of finite-dimensional Von Neumann algebras (or equivalently unital C* algebras) that is fully constructive and uses only basic notions of linear algebra.



The Artin-Wedderburn theorem, originally stated in
[
1
]
and
[
6
]
, is a fundamental theorem on the strucutre of (semi-simple) rings. As a special case, it provides a result on the decomposition of finite-dimensional Von Neumann algebras (or equivalently unital C* algebras), as stated for example in Theorem 11.2 of
[
5
]
. Some self-contained proofs have been given for the general result in
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
and
[
4
]
. Following similar ideas, we provide a proof in the special case of Von Neumann algebras that has the advantage of being fully constructive and to use only basic notions of linear algebra.

Theorem

(Artin-Wedderburn)
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a finite-dimensional Hilbert space and
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
be a Von Neumann algebra. Then there exists families of Hilbert spaces
(\mathcal{H}_{L}^{k})
and
(\mathcal{H}_{R}^{k})
and a unitary
U:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\bigoplus_{k}(\mathcal{H}_{L}^{k}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{R}^{k})
such that

U\mathcal{A}U^{\dagger}=\bigoplus_{k}\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{L}^{k})\otimes\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{R}^{k}}.

(1)

Proof.

Let
P=\{P_{i}\}_{i\in\{1,\ldots,p\}}
be a family of non-zero, orthogonal, and pairwise orthogonal projectors of
\mathcal{A}
, ie. such that:

(i)

\forall i\in\{1,\ldots,p\},\;0\neq P_{i}=P_{i}^{\dagger}\in\mathcal{A}
;

(ii)

\forall i,j\in\{1,\ldots,p\}\;P_{i}P_{j}=\delta_{ij}P_{i}\in\mathcal{A}
.

Notice that such a family always exists as
\{\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{H}}\}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
is one. Moreover we will take
P
to be maximal among such families, i.e. so that there is no family
Q=\{Q_{j}\}
verifying conditions
(i),(ii)
and such that
\mathcal{P}\subsetneq\mathcal{Q}
, with
\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q}
the algebras spanned by
P
and
Q
. It follows that

(iii)

\sum_{i\in\{1,\ldots,p\}}P_{i}=\mathbb{1}
,

otherwise
\mathbb{1}-\sum_{i\in\{1,\ldots,p\}}P_{i}
could be added to the set of projectors which would contradict the maximality of
P
.

First
, we show that

\displaystyle\forall j,\quad P_{j}\mathcal{A}P_{j}=\mathbb{C}P_{j}.

(2)

Intuitively, this is because the contrary would allow us to refine the subspaces defined by
P_{j}
into
smaller subspaces, and hence go against the fact that
P
is maximal. Remark that we always have
\mathbb{C}P_{j}\subseteq P_{j}\mathcal{A}P_{j}
, because
P_{j}P_{j}P_{j}=P_{j}\neq 0
and that the reverse inclusion is immediately true if
P_{j}
is of rank one. Let us now assume that
P_{j}
is of rank strictly greater than one, and suppose that there exists some (necessarily non-zero)
M\in\mathcal{A}
such that
P_{j}MP_{j}\not\propto P_{j}
. Both
P_{j}(M+M^{\dagger})P_{j}
and
iP_{j}(M-M^{\dagger})P_{j}
are hermitian, and at least one of them is not a multiple of
P_{j}
(otherwise
P_{j}MP_{j}
would also be one); we will call it
H
. Remark that
H
has support in the subspace
Im(P_{j})
, and hence that it can be decomposed as
H=\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}Q_{k}
where the
Q_{k}
’s are orthogonal and pairwise orthogonal projectors, such that
\sum_{k}Q_{k}=P_{j}
, and the
\lambda_{k}
’s are distinct real numbers. This sum has at least two terms as
P_{j}
is of rank strictly greater than one and
H\not\propto P_{j}
. All the
Q_{k}
’s are in
\mathcal{A}
, as for any
k
such that
\lambda_{k}\neq 0
,
Q_{k}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}}H\prod_{l\neq k}(H-\lambda_{l}P_{j})\in\mathcal{A}
(and the one such that
\lambda_{k}=0
can be obtained by substracting from
P_{j}
all the other
Q_{k}
). Consider now the set
Q=P/\{P_{j}\}\cup(\bigcup_{k}\{Q_{k}\})
. It satisfies conditions
(i)
and
(ii)
, is different from
P
and such that
\mathcal{P}\subsetneq\mathcal{Q}
, which contradicts the maximality of
P
and concludes the proof of this first property.

Second
, we can define the following equivalence relation on (the indices of) the projectors:

\displaystyle i\sim j\Leftrightarrow P_{i}\mathcal{A}P_{j}\neq 0.

(3)

It is indeed an equivalence relation, as it is:

•

reflexive, because
P_{i}P_{i}P_{i}=P_{i}\neq 0
;

•

transitive, indeed let
A,B\in\mathcal{A}
such that
M=P_{i}AP_{j}\neq 0
and
N=P_{j}BP_{k}\neq 0
. By Eq. (
2
), we know that there exists
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{C}
such that
M^{\dagger}M=P_{j}A^{\dagger}P_{i}AP_{j}=\lambda P_{j}
and
NN^{\dagger}=P_{j}BP_{k}B^{\dagger}P_{j}=\mu P_{j}
. Moreover, as
M\neq 0\Leftrightarrow MM^{\dagger}\neq 0
and
N\neq 0\Leftrightarrow NN^{\dagger}\neq 0
it follows that
\lambda,\mu\neq 0
. Finally
P_{j}A^{\dagger}P_{i}AP_{j}BP_{k}B^{\dagger}P_{j}=\lambda\mu P_{j}\neq 0\Rightarrow P_{i}AP_{j}BP_{k}\neq 0
;

•

symmetric, since

\displaystyle P_{i}\mathcal{A}P_{j}\neq 0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow(P_{i}\mathcal{A}P_{j})^{\dagger}\neq 0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow P_{j}^{\dagger}\mathcal{A}^{\dagger}P_{i}^{\dagger}\neq 0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow P_{j}\mathcal{A}P_{i}\neq 0.

Let
I_{1},...,I_{n}
be the different equivalence classes and let us denote
P_{I_{k}}=\sum_{i\in I_{k}}P_{i}
. It follows, by definition of
\sim
, that

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=(\sum_{k=1}^{n}P_{I_{k}})\mathcal{A}(\sum_{l=1}^{n}P_{I_{l}})=\sum_{k=1}^{n}P_{I_{k}}\mathcal{A}P_{I_{k}}.

This sum is actually direct as, for any
P_{I_{k}}A_{k}P_{I_{k}}=P_{I_{l}}A_{l}P_{I_{l}}=A\in P_{I_{k}}\mathcal{A}P_{I_{k}}\cap P_{I_{l}}\mathcal{A}P_{I_{l}}
, we have that
A=P_{I_{k}}A_{k}P_{I_{k}}=P_{I_{k}}P_{I_{k}}A_{k}P_{I_{k}}=P_{I_{k}}P_{I_{l}}A_{l}P_{I_{l}}=\delta_{kl}A
. Moreover, each
P_{I_{k}}\mathcal{A}P_{I_{k}}
is (unitarily isomorphic to) a Von Neumann subalgebra of
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{I_{k}})
and the
\{P_{i}\}_{i\in{I_{k}}}
form a maximal family of orthogonal and pairwise orthogonal projectors of this algebra. We thus obtain that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{k}\mathcal{H}_{I_{k}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{k}\mathcal{A}_{I_{k}},

where
\mathcal{H}_{I_{k}}=Im(P_{I_{k}})
and
\mathcal{A}_{I_{k}}=P_{i}\mathcal{A}P_{i}
.

In the rest of the proof we place ourselves in one of these algebras
\mathcal{A}_{I_{k}}
, or equivalently one of the classes
I_{k}
, where any two projectors are such that

\displaystyle P_{i}\mathcal{A}P_{j}\neq 0,

(4)

and will prove that
\mathcal{A}_{I_{k}}
is unitarily isomorphic to some
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{L}^{k})\otimes\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{R}^{k}}
. As
\mathcal{A}=\bigoplus_{k}\mathcal{A}_{I_{k}}
, this will prove the result of the theorem.

Third
, we show that for all
A\in\mathcal{A}
(which we remind is now one of the
\mathcal{A}_{I_{k}}
) and for all
i,j\in\{1\ldots p\}
,
if we let
M=P_{i}AP_{j}
, then

\displaystyle\exists\lambda\in\mathbb{C},\quad M^{\dagger}M=\lambda P_{i}\,\wedge\,MM^{\dagger}=\lambda P_{j}.

(5)

Indeed, by Eq. (
2
), we have that there exists
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{C}
such that
M^{\dagger}M=\lambda P_{i}
and
MM^{\dagger}=\mu P_{j}
. But then
\lambda^{2}P_{i}=M^{\dagger}MM^{\dagger}M=\mu M^{\dagger}P_{j}M=\mu\lambda P_{i}
,
hence
\lambda
equals
\mu
.

Fourth
, we show that

\displaystyle\forall i,j,\quad\tr(P_i)=\tr(P_j)=\textrm{some constant }q.

(6)

For each
i,j
take some
A\in\mathcal{A}
verifying Eq. (
4
) and let
M=P_{i}AP_{j}
. By Eq. (
5
) there exists a complex number
\lambda

such that
P_{i}=\lambda MM^{\dagger}
and
P_{j}=\lambda M^{\dagger}M
.
Then the equality follows from

\tr(\lambda MM^{\dagger})=\tr(\lambda M^{\dagger}M)=q
and we conclude that all the
P_{i}
have the same rank.

Fifth
, consider some Hilbert spaces,
\mathcal{H}_{L}
of dimension the number
p
of projectors in the family
P
, and
\mathcal{H}_{R}
of dimension
q
the rank of these projectors. Define the unitary
V:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{L}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{R}
which takes each
P_{i}
to
\tilde{P}_{i}=\outerproduct{i}{i}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{R}}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{L}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{R})
.
Note that this is always possible since the
\{P_{i}\}_{i\in\{1,\ldots,p\}}
form an
orthogonal
(ii)
, complete
(iii)
set of projectors of equal rank by Eq. (
6
).
We now show that

\displaystyle\forall B\in V\mathcal{A}V^{\dagger},\,

\displaystyle\forall i,j,\quad\tilde{P}_{i}B\tilde{P}_{j}=\outerproduct{i}{j}\otimes B_{ij}

\displaystyle\textrm{ where }B_{ij}B_{ij}^{\dagger}=B_{ij}^{\dagger}B_{ij}\propto\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{R}}.

(7)

Indeed, the first line is true because

\displaystyle\tilde{P}_{i}B\tilde{P}_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l,m,n}\alpha_{klmn}(\outerproduct{i}{i}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{R}})(\outerproduct{k}{l}\otimes\outerproduct{m}{n})(\outerproduct{j}{j}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{R}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n}\alpha_{ijmn}(\outerproduct{i}{j}\otimes\outerproduct{m}{n})=\outerproduct{i}{j}\otimes(\sum_{mn}\alpha_{ijmn}\outerproduct{m}{n}).

Now if we let
M=\tilde{P}_{i}B\tilde{P}_{j}
, by Eq. (
5
) there exists a complex number
\lambda

such that we have both
\tilde{P}_{i}=\lambda MM^{\dagger}
and
\tilde{P}_{j}=\lambda M^{\dagger}M
. It follows that

\displaystyle\outerproduct{i}{i}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{R}}=\tilde{P}_{i}=\lambda MM^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\lambda(\outerproduct{i}{j}\otimes B_{ij})(\outerproduct{j}{i}\otimes B_{ij}^{\dagger})

\displaystyle=\lambda\outerproduct{i}{i}\otimes B_{ij}B_{ij}^{\dagger}

and hence that
\lambda B_{ij}B_{ij}^{\dagger}=\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{R}}
. The result for
B_{ij}^{\dagger}B_{ij}
is proved symetrically.

Sixth
, by Eq. (
4
), one can find for each
i
an element
A^{(i)}\in\mathcal{A}
such that
P_{1}A^{(i)}P_{i}\neq 0
and which can be rescaled so that Eq. (
7
) on
N^{(i)}=VA^{(i)}V^{\dagger}
makes
N^{(i)}_{1i}
unitary. We call
A=\sum_{i}P_{1}A^{(i)}P_{i}\in\mathcal{A}
and
B=VAV^{\dagger}
which is then such that for all
i
,
B_{1i}=N^{(i)}_{1i}.
We now define the unitary

\displaystyle W

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}(\outerproduct{i}{i}\otimes B_{1i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}(\outerproduct{i}{1}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{R}})(\outerproduct{1}{i}\otimes B_{1i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}(\outerproduct{i}{1}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{R}})\tilde{P_{1}}B\tilde{P}_{i},

which has the property that for all
j
,

\displaystyle W\tilde{P_{j}}W^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,l}(\outerproduct{i}{1}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{R}})\tilde{P_{1}}B\tilde{P}_{i}\tilde{P}_{j}\tilde{P}_{l}B^{\dagger}\tilde{P}_{1}(\outerproduct{1}{l}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{R}})

\displaystyle=(\outerproduct{j}{1}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{R}})\tilde{P_{1}}B\tilde{P}_{j}B^{\dagger}\tilde{P}_{1}\outerproduct{1}{j}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{R}})

\displaystyle=(\outerproduct{j}{1}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{R}})B_{1j}B_{1j}^{\dagger}\outerproduct{1}{j}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{R}})

\displaystyle=(\outerproduct{j}{j}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{R}})

\displaystyle=\tilde{P}_{j}.

Finally
, we define the unitary
U=WV:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{L}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{R}
and we show that, for all
A\in\mathcal{A}
and for all
i,j
, there exists
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
such that

\displaystyle\tilde{P}_{i}(UAU^{\dagger})\tilde{P}_{j}=\lambda\outerproduct{i}{j}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{R}};

(8)

which will conclude the proof, as
UAU^{\dagger}=\sum_{i,j}\tilde{P}_{i}(UAU^{\dagger})\tilde{P}_{j}
, and for any
i,j
one can find
A\in\mathcal{A}
such that
\tilde{P}_{i}(UAU^{\dagger})\tilde{P}_{j}=UP_{i}AP_{j}U^{\dagger}
is non-zero (by Eq. (
4
)). And indeed this equality is true as

\displaystyle\tilde{P}_{i}UAU^{\dagger}\tilde{P}_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{P}_{i}WVAV^{\dagger}W^{\dagger}\tilde{P}_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle W\tilde{P}_{i}VAV^{\dagger}\tilde{P}_{j}W^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k}(\outerproduct{k}{1}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{R}})\tilde{P}_{1}B\tilde{P}_{k}\right)\tilde{P}_{i}UAU^{\dagger}\tilde{P}_{j}\left(\sum_{l}\tilde{P}_{l}B^{\dagger}\tilde{P}_{1}(\outerproduct{1}{l}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{R}})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\outerproduct{i}{1}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{R}})\tilde{P_{1}}B\tilde{P}_{i}UAU^{\dagger}\tilde{P}_{j}B^{\dagger}\tilde{P}_{1}(\outerproduct{1}{j}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{R}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\outerproduct{i}{1}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{R}})\lambda\tilde{P}_{1}(\outerproduct{1}{j}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{R}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda(\outerproduct{i}{1}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{R}})(\outerproduct{1}{1}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{R}})(\outerproduct{1}{j}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{R}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\outerproduct{i}{j}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{R}}.

∎

ESAK-YOLO: multi-scale attention enhanced object detection for floating waste in complex water scenes
Authors: Tao Zhou, Ji Liu, Nan Lian, Yanling Chen, Jinghui Liu, Zhe Pei, Jiangpeng Li, Zeming Yin, Jie Zhou, Zhigang Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In complex aquatic environments, accurate detection of floating debris remains challenging due to severe occlusion, large scale variations, and strong background interference, which hinder existing object detection models from effectively modeling spatial features, adapting to scale changes, and capturing object shapes. To address the weak multi-scale feature representation of surface debris, an efficient multi-scale attention (EMA) module is designed, enhancing semantic modeling capability through grouped feature modeling and cross-spatial information fusion. To improve the perception of objects of different sizes, a selective kernel attention (SKA) mechanism is designed, enabling the network to dynamically adjust its receptive field based on contextual information and accurately capture scale-variant features. Considering the irregular morphology of floating debris, an arbitrary kernel convolution (AKConv) module is proposed, breaking the constraints of traditional kernel shapes and improving adaptability to complex structures. Integrating these three core designs, the enhanced network is termed ESAK-YOLO. The experimental results show that, compared with the baseline YOLOv7, ESAK-YOLO improves mAP by 8.14%, and when compared with five other models, it achieves a maximum improvement of 13.78%, further demonstrating the effectiveness and superiority of the proposed method.

Introduction

The health of aquatic ecosystems is closely linked to the sustainable development of human society, while surface debris pollution has emerged as a global environmental challenge1. Statistics indicate that over 8 million tons of plastic waste enter the oceans annually, with a considerable proportion existing as floating debris2. Such waste not only degrades aquatic landscapes but also threatens aquatic life, compromises water quality, obstructs vessel navigation, and even clogs drainage systems during flood events3. Traditional manual inspection and cleaning methods are inefficient, costly, and incapable of providing continuous monitoring; thus, the development of intelligent surface debris detection technologies holds significant importance for environmental protection and aquatic management4.

Early surface debris detection primarily relies on manual observation and basic image processing techniques5. Threshold-based segmentation methods distinguish debris from water surfaces using color and texture features, yet their performance is highly dependent on illumination conditions and water surface states, resulting in poor stability6. Edge detection techniques can capture debris contour information, but they are prone to generating substantial noise under complex backgrounds, leading to high false detection rates7. Template matching is effective for debris of specific shapes; however, it lacks adaptability to diverse debris types and struggles with deformation and occlusion8,9. These traditional approaches generally suffer from limited feature representation and poor generalization, rendering them inadequate for complex real-world scenarios10,11.

The advent of deep learning offers new opportunities for surface debris detection. Convolutional neural networks, with their powerful feature learning capability, enable automatic extraction of high-level semantic features of debris, significantly improving detection accuracy12,13. Current mainstream deep learning-based detection methods can be broadly classified into region proposal-based approaches and end-to-end regression approaches14. Although the R-CNN series achieves high detection accuracy, its computational complexity hinders real-time performance15. For surface debris detection, certain studies optimize Faster R-CNN by introducing multi-scale feature fusion mechanisms to enhance the detection of small debris; nevertheless, the inherent limitations of two-stage detectors continue to constrain efficiency when dealing with densely distributed debris13.

To address the above challenges, this paper proposes the ESAK-YOLO algorithm based on the latest YOLOv7 framework by designing three efficient modules, aiming to tackle the problem of garbage detection in complex aquatic environments. The main contributions of this work are as follows:Based on the YOLOv7 backbone, an efficient multi-scale attention (EMA) module is designed to enhance the modeling of multi-scale spatial semantics through grouped feature modeling and cross-spatial information fusion, thereby improving feature representation and detection stability in water surface scenes with significant scale variations.A selective kernel attention (SKA) module is developed within the network structure. By dynamically selecting convolution kernels of different sizes and assigning attention weights, the model adaptively adjusts the receptive field size according to contextual information. This design enables precise perception of floating objects of various scales and significantly improves the ability to distinguish between large targets (plastic bags) and small targets (paper scraps).An any-kernel convolution (AKConv) module is introduced in the feature extraction stage, breaking the limitation of fixed convolution kernel shapes. By adopting flexible kernel shapes to fit the geometric characteristics of irregular targets, this module effectively enhances feature extraction and modeling accuracy for water surface garbage with complex and variable forms (twisted plastic bags and irregular branches).A diverse dataset of riverine floating objects is constructed, covering different weather, lighting, and water quality conditions. Experimental results show that the proposed ESAK-YOLO achieves up to 13.78% improvement in mAP compared with five other models, and demonstrates superior detection accuracy and robustness, particularly in recognizing categories such as Milk box and Grass.

Related work

With the increasing severity of environmental issues, water pollution has become an urgent challenge16. Among various pollutants, surface debris not only deteriorates water quality and threatens aquatic life but also damages natural landscapes17. In recent years, surface debris detection technologies have advanced rapidly, aiming to improve the efficiency and accuracy of image-based detection and to provide technical support for water pollution management18. Existing research primarily focuses on different detection approaches, which can be systematically classified and analyzed from a technical perspective19.

Traditional image processing methods

Early detection of surface debris primarily depends on manual inspection, which is inefficient, highly subjective, and unable to cover large water areas20. Subsequently, sensor-based detection technologies emerged as an important supplement to traditional approaches. For example, infrared imaging detects debris by capturing thermal radiation characteristics21, while LiDAR uses point cloud data for three-dimensional localization22. These methods are conceptually straightforward, computationally inexpensive, and suitable for simple scenarios, thus serving as a foundational support for early monitoring. Nevertheless, their detection accuracy is limited: low feature discriminability hinders the recognition of irregularly shaped and multi-scale targets; moreover, high equipment costs and vulnerability to water-surface reflections and complex backgrounds restrict their scalability for large-scale applications23.

Deep learning-based object detection

With the rapid development of deep learning, object detection algorithms based on visible-light imagery have shown substantial potential in surface debris detection, becoming the mainstream research direction24. Among them, the YOLO series is widely adopted due to its advantages in both real-time performance and accuracy, offering superior solutions for complex detection tasks. A variety of studies optimize YOLO models: Sun et al.25 and Jiang et al.4 enhance YOLOv5s by adding upsampling layers and attention mechanisms to improve small-object detection; Li et al.26 enhanced the model’s multi-scale adaptability by introducing a new detection layer, thereby achieving excellent mean accuracy. Zhang’s team develops the lightweight SOE-YOLO model, employing compact convolutional structures on the YOLOv8 framework to meet real-time detection requirements27. Deep learning approaches demonstrate strong adaptability to diverse debris types through automatic extraction of deep semantic features, outperforming traditional methods28,29. However, their perception of targets with extreme scale variations (e.g., large floating objects and small fragments) remains limited, their recognition of distorted or occluded shapes is insufficient, and they are prone to false detections in strongly reflective water-surface backgrounds30,31.

Multi-modal fusion and optimization methods

To further enhance detection performance, extensive research explores algorithmic optimization and multi-modal data fusion as effective complements to deep learning32,33. In feature extraction, Xu et al. and Long et al. improve target representation through deformable convolution and semantic reorganization, achieving an approximate 10% accuracy gain in real-world maritime tests34, thereby validating the adaptability of flexible kernel design to complex features. V.B.K. Reddy’s team augments training datasets to enhance model generalization35. In feature enhancement and background suppression, Shen et al.36 integrate the CBAM attention mechanism into satellite imagery, increasing the detection F1-score to 77%; Danilov introduces fuzzy C-means clustering37, and Zhang et al. combine denoising techniques for remote sensing imagery38, both improving image quality via preprocessing to enhance detection stability. While these methods improve accuracy and robustness through multi-dimensional optimization, challenges persist: the spatiotemporal synchronization and fusion efficiency of multi-modal data require improvement, and certain optimization strategies–such as complex preprocessing–significantly increase computational cost, limiting real-time applicability39.

Limitations of existing techniques and research directions

Although surface debris detection technologies continue to advance40, significant challenges remain in complex aquatic environments. Traditional methods exhibit low feature discriminability and poor adaptability to variations in debris shape and scale41; deep learning approaches still face limitations in multi-scale perception, irregular-shape recognition, and suppression of strong background interference42; multi-modal methods often incur high computational costs and low deployment efficiency43. These limitations make it difficult for existing technologies to simultaneously achieve high accuracy and robustness in real-world scenarios characterized by severe occlusion, large scale variation, and complex interference44. To address these issues, an improved detection algorithm, ESAK-YOLO, is developed by integrating the strengths of existing methods. The design focuses on cross-scale modeling, dynamic receptive field adjustment, and irregular-shape modeling, aiming to enhance detection accuracy and environmental adaptability, thereby providing a more robust solution for surface debris recognition in complex aquatic conditions.

Methodology

The proposed floating debris detection model, ESAK-YOLO, comprises three core modules: efficient multi-scale attention (EMA), selective kernel attention (SKA), and arbitrary kernel convolution (AKConv). The overall network architecture is shown in Figure 1. The EMA module enhances feature extraction capability by redistributing spatial-semantic features across grouped subfeatures via channel reshaping and grouping operations. The SKA module adaptively adjusts the receptive field to strengthen multi-scale feature modeling. Arbitrary kernel convolution module

The AKConv module addresses two inherent limitations of standard convolutions: locality-bound receptive fields and fixed kernel sizes, which limit adaptability to diverse targets. AKConv allows kernels with arbitrary parameters and sampling patterns, enabling adaptation to various object shapes and scales. It can be seen from Figure 4. Experiments and discussion

Dataset

This study uses the RFT (River Floating Trash) dataset, which focuses on floating trash detection in inland waters such as rivers and lakes. The dataset is collected under various lighting conditions (sunny and cloudy) and water flow scenarios, covering both nearshore and drone aerial scenes. It includes typical floating objects such as plastic bottles, plastic bags, foam, and fishing nets. The dataset contains 2,400 high-resolution images, with 1,680 images for training, 239 for validation, and 481 for testing. Annotations follow the YOLO format and provide both bounding boxes (bbox) and polygon segmentation labels, supporting object detection and semantic segmentation tasks. There are eight object categories: bottle, grass, branch, milk box, plastic bag, plastic garbage, ball, and leaf. The dataset is publicly available on the Kaggle platform free of charge and is suitable for evaluating algorithm performance under a unified benchmark. Since most objects are small in size and the background is complex (waves, reflections, etc.), it poses high challenges to the robustness and generalization ability of water surface trash detection methods. Evaluation metrics

In the task of floating object detection on water surfaces, this study adopts multiple evaluation metrics to comprehensively measure model performance. The accuracy metrics include P (Precision) and R (Recall). Precision reflects the proportion of correctly identified floating objects among the detection results, while Recall measures the coverage of the model in detecting all actual floating object instances. To comprehensively evaluate detection performance, this paper adopts mAP50 as the mean average precision metric, which is defined at an IoU threshold of 0.5. This metric reflects the detection accuracy of the model under relatively loose matching conditions and effectively evaluates its performance in detecting floating objects in complex aquatic environments. In addition, FPS (frames per second) measures the inference speed of the model, where higher values indicate that the model processes water surface floating object images more quickly and is more suitable for real-time detection needs.

Implement details

The experimental validation is conducted on a high-performance computing platform. The model is built using Python 3.9 and the PyTorch 2.4.0 deep learning framework, with GPU acceleration enabled by CUDA 12.1. The computing environment is configured with an Intel® Xeon® Processor (Icelake) CPU @ 2.59 GHz, an NVIDIA A100 GPU with 40 GB memory, and 32 GB RAM. During training, all input images are resized to 640x640 pixels. Analysis of module combinations

Among the three dual-module combinations, the combination of EMA and AKConv performs the best, with the mAP reaching 77.46%, which is an improvement of 6.64 percentage points over the baseline. The combination of SKA and AKConv shows outstanding performance in terms of recall, reaching 70.25%. These results indicate that the modules complement each other effectively. When all three modules are integrated, ESAK-YOLO achieves the best overall performance, with a precision of 85.37%, a recall of 71.68%, and an mAP of 78.96%. Compared with the baseline model, these values represent improvements of 6.12%, 9.08%, and 8.14 percentage points respectively. Table II shows that the complete model achieves the best results across all categories, particularly for natural objects that are more difficult to detect such as Branch, Grass, and Leaf, with AP values reaching 56.83%, 52.96%, and 78.86% respectively, and the corresponding visualization is illustrated in Figure 7.

Although the introduction of the modules brings some computational overhead, the complete ESAK-YOLO model still maintains a detection speed of 78.57 FPS. This represents a 25.4% decrease compared with the baseline model, which runs at 105.21 FPS, but it still meets the requirements for real-time detection. This result shows that the improvements proposed in this paper achieve a reasonable balance between accuracy enhancement and computational efficiency.

Comparison experiments

To comprehensively validate the detection performance of ESAK-YOLO, this paper selects several mainstream object detection algorithms, including DETR45, EfficientDet46, YOLOv547, YOLOv848, and YOLOX49, for comparison experiments. As shown in Table 4, ESAK-YOLO achieves an mAP of 78.96%, which represents improvements of 13.78, 6.63, 6.58, 1.73, and 2.69 percentage points over DETR, EfficientDet, YOLOv5, YOLOv8, and YOLOX respectively, while maintaining a detection speed of 78.57 FPS that meets the requirements for real-time detection. From the category-level analysis, ESAK-YOLO achieves the best performance in classes such as Ball (97.69%), Milk box (90.48%), Plastic bag (84.52%), Grass (52.96%), and Leaf (78.86%). In particular, for the Milk box class, ESAK-YOLO outperforms the second-best algorithm YOLOv8 by 10.60 percentage points. It is worth noting that for the Plastic garbage and Branch classes, YOLOv8 slightly outperforms ESAK-YOLO, with AP values of 88.92% and 59.82% compared with 75.37% and 56.83%.This performance gap may be attributed to the irregular shapes and fine-grained structures of these objects, which are often accompanied by low contrast and complex backgrounds. Although the proposed AKConv enhances the modeling of object shapes, it may still be insufficient for capturing extremely thin or highly deformable structures. This suggests that further improvements in fine-grained feature representation could enhance detection performance for such challenging categories.

Further analysis shows that ESAK-YOLO demonstrates a clear advantage over traditional YOLO algorithms in handling multi-scale targets in complex aquatic environments, with the comparative results illustrated in Figure 8. Among the eight detection categories, ESAK-YOLO achieves the best performance in six categories (Ball, Milk box, Plastic bag, Grass, Leaf, and Bottle) and is slightly lower than YOLOv8 in only two categories (Plastic garbage and Branch). In particular, ESAK-YOLO shows stronger generalization ability for small targets and irregularly shaped objects that are more difficult to detect. For example, in the Grass category it outperforms YOLOv8 by 7.58 percentage points, and in the Ball category it improves by 6.05 percentage points. In addition, compared with the Transformer-based DETR algorithm, ESAK-YOLO has a significant advantage in inference speed (78.57 FPS vs 42.35 FPS), which verifies the feasibility of the proposed method in practical applications.Compared with lightweight detectors such as YOLOv5 and YOLOv8, ESAK-YOLO has higher computational complexity (37.20M parameters and 105.1 GFLOPs). However, it achieves the best overall detection performance with a mAP of 78.96%, indicating that the proposed modules effectively enhance feature representation capability and provide a favorable balance between accuracy and efficiency. Overall, ESAK-YOLO leverages the collaborative effect of the three core modules EMA, SKA, and AKConv to successfully achieve an optimized balance between detection accuracy and computational efficiency, providing an effective technical solution for floating debris detection in complex aquatic environments.

Conclusion

To effectively address the challenges of multi-scale target recognition and irregular shape feature extraction in floating debris detection under complex aquatic environments, this paper proposes ESAK-YOLO, an algorithm based on YOLOv7. The method integrates the three core modules EMA, SKA, and AKConv, and enhances feature extraction and target representation through multi-scale feature fusion, a selective attention mechanism, and deformable convolution kernel design. Experimental results show that ESAK-YOLO achieves an mAP of 78.96% on the river floating debris dataset, which is an improvement of 8.14 percentage points over the baseline model, and it achieves the best performance in six out of eight detection categories. This verifies the effectiveness of the multi-module collaborative optimization strategy.

Although ESAK-YOLO performs well in most categories, there is still room for improvement in the detection of Plastic garbage and Branch. Future research can proceed in three directions. Under future directions, one avenue is to design refined feature extraction mechanisms tailored to specific categories, thereby improving the recognition of objects with complex shapes. Another lies in exploring multimodal fusion strategies that integrate spectral, texture, and geometric cues to construct more robust target representations.However, it is worth noting that introducing multimodal fusion may increase computational complexity and potentially affect real-time performance. Therefore, future research will focus on designing lightweight fusion strategies and efficient feature interaction mechanisms to maintain a balance between detection accuracy and inference speed, ensuring the practicality of the proposed method in real-time applications. A further direction is to develop lightweight network architectures together with adaptive training strategies, aiming to enhance inference efficiency without compromising detection accuracy. These advances strengthen the practicality of the algorithm and support its deployment in intelligent water environment monitoring and ecological protection.

A Dedekind-Rademacher cocycle for Bianchi groups
Kim Klinger-Logan, Kalani Thalagoda, Tian An Wong

Abstract




2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
11F20 and 11F37

{resume}

Nous construisons une généralisation du cocycle Dedekind-Rademacher aux sous-groupes de congruence de
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, et en déduisons certaines de ses propriétés de base. En particulier, nous montrons qu’il paramétre une famille de valeurs
L
et prouvons l’intégralité de ces valeurs.

{abstr}

We construct a generalization of the Dedekind-Rademacher cocycle to congruence subgroups of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, and derive some of its basic properties. In particular, we show that it parametrizes a family of
L
-values and prove the integrality of these values.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
The classical Dedekind-Rademacher homomorphism

Consider the map
\phi:\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})\to{\mathbb{Z}}
given by

\phi\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}=\begin{cases}\dfrac{a+d}{c}-12\dfrac{c}{|c|}\cdot S(a/|c|)&c\neq 0\\
\dfrac{b}{d}&c=0\end{cases},

where

S(a/c)=\sum_{k=1}^{c-1}B_{1}\left(\frac{k}{c}\right)B_{1}\left(\frac{hk}{c}\right),\qquad(a,c)=1

is the classical Dedekind sum, with
B_{1}(x):=x-\lfloor{x}\rfloor-1/2.
The Dedekind-Rademacher homomorphism
\phi_{N}:\Gamma_{0}(N)\to{\mathbb{Z}}
is then defined in
[
7
]
as the difference

\displaystyle\phi_{N}\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}:=\phi\begin{pmatrix}a&bN\\
c/N&d\end{pmatrix}-\phi\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}.

Mazur
[
6
]
gave an equivalent definition as follows,

\displaystyle\phi_{N}\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
Nc&d\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{(N-1)(a+d)}{Nc}-12\frac{c}{|c|}S^{N}(a/N|c|)&c\neq 0\\
\frac{(N-1)b}{d}&c=0\end{cases}

where
S^{N}(x)=S(x)-S(Nx)
for any
x\in\mathbb{Q}
.

For
N=p
a prime and
\Gamma=\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}}[1/p])
, Darmon, Pozzi, and Vonk showed that
\phi_{p}
induces a rigid meromorphic cocycle on
\Gamma
, called the Dedekind-Rademacher cocycle
[
2
]
. At certain real quadratic arguments the values at
\phi_{p}
describe global
p
-units in the narrow Hilbert class field of the associated real quadratic field.

1.2.
Generalizing to Bianchi groups

In this note, we construct a generalization,
\Phi_{N}
, of the Dedekind-Rademacher homomorphism to Bianchi groups, specifically, congruence subgroups
\Gamma_{0}({\mathfrak{n}})
of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. We construct
\Phi_{N}
more generally as the specialization of a 1-cocycle following work of Sczech
[
9
]
and Ito
[
5
]
on elliptic Dedekind sums.

In Section
2
, we define the cocycle
\Phi_{N}
on
\Gamma_{0}({\mathfrak{n}})
in (
2.2
) and derive its basic properties, including the eigenvalues of certain Hecke operators acting on
\Phi_{N}
in Proposition
2.7
. In Section
3
, we show as an application that it parametrizes a family of
L
-values in Theorem
3.1
and obtain as a result the integrality of these special values in Corollary
3.2
.

Our work is in part motivated by renewed interest in Eisenstein cocycles, Dedekind sums, and their applications to arithmetic. Besides the work of
[
2
]
, Bergeron, Charollois, and Garcia recently constructed an
(n-1)
-homogeneous cocycle on
\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p})
, where
\mathfrak{p}
is a prime ideal in
\mathcal{O}_{K}

[
1
]
. Their construction can be viewed as a smoothed version of the Eisenstein cocycle of
[
3
]
. In the case
n=2
, it is expected that
\Phi
is cohomologous to the non-smoothed Eisenstein cocycle
\Psi_{0}
. In this context, it will be natural to expect that a smoothed form of
\Phi_{N}
will be cohomologous to the cocycle of
[
1
]
. We speculate on this connection further in Remark
3.3
below.
Elsewhere, conjectures of Sharifi, supported by work of Fukaya and Kato, relate cup products in Galois cohomology to elements in the Eisenstein ideal. In
[
4
]
, they pose the question of formulating the conjectures over imaginary quadratic fields. Recent work of Sharifi and Venkatesh
[
10
]
gave a motivic construction of Eisenstein cocycles over
\mathbb{Q}
, but its extension to imaginary quadratic fields is not immediately apparent. We hope that this work will motivate further study in the Bianchi case.

2.
The construction

2.1.
Definitions

Let
L=\omega_{1}{\mathbb{Z}}+\omega_{2}{\mathbb{Z}}
be a lattice in
\mathbb{C}
with
\text{Im}(\omega_{1}/\omega_{2})>0
and let
\mathcal{O}_{L}:=\{m\in{\mathbb{C}}~|~mL\subset L\}
be the ring of multipliers. We assume that
\mathcal{O}_{L}
is an order in an imaginary quadratic field
K
such that the associated elliptic curve
\mathbb{C}/L
has CM by
K
. When the context is clear, we shall sometimes write
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}_{L}
for convenience.

For
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, define the Kronecker Eisenstein series as

E_{k}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in L\\
w\neq 0\end{subarray}}(w+x)^{-k}|w+x|^{-s}\big|_{s=0},\qquad x,s\in\mathbb{C}

where the value at
s=0
is understood in the sense of the analytic continuation. Elliptic Dedekind sums are then defined as

D(a,c)=\frac{1}{c}\sum_{r\in L/cL}E_{1}\left(\frac{ar}{c}\right)E_{1}\left(\frac{r}{c}\right),

where
a,c\in\mathcal{O}_{L}
with
c\neq 0
. We shall furthermore assume that
\mathcal{O}_{L}
is not isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Z}}[i]
or
{\mathbb{Z}}[e^{2\pi i/3}]
, in which case
D(a,c)
is trivial.
Sczech
[
9
]
defined a generalization of the map
\phi
as a homomorphism
\Phi=\Phi_{L}:\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{L})\to{\mathbb{C}}
where

\Phi(A)=\begin{cases}E_{2}(0)I\left(\dfrac{a+d}{c}\right)-{D}(a,c)&c\neq 0\\
E_{2}(0)I\left(\dfrac{b}{d}\right)&c=0\end{cases},

with
I(z):=z-\bar{z}
and
A=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{L})
. By
[
9
, Satz 4]
, its image lies in the ring of integers of the number field

F:=\mathbb{Q}(g_{2},g_{3},\sqrt{D}),

where
D
is the discriminant of
\mathcal{O}_{L}
and
g_{2}=g_{2}(L),g_{3}=g_{3}(L)
are algebraic integers associated to the elliptic curve defined by
L
.

More generally, let

E(x)=\frac{2\pi i}{D(L)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in L\\
w\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\overline{w+x}}{w+x}|w+x|^{-2s}\big|_{s=0}

where
D(L)=w_{1}\bar{w}_{2}-\bar{w}_{1}w_{2}
if
L=\mathbb{Z}w_{1}+\mathbb{Z}w_{2}
and Im
(w_{1}/w_{1})>0
, satisfying
E(0)=E_{2}(0)
.
For
p,q\in{\mathbb{C}}/L
and
a,c\in\mathcal{O}_{L}
we define

D(a,c;p,q)=\frac{1}{c}\sum_{r\in L/cL}E_{1}\left(\frac{a(r+p)}{c}+q\right)E_{1}\left(\frac{r+p}{c}\right),

and

\displaystyle\Phi(A)(p,q)=-

\displaystyle\overline{\left(\frac{a}{c}\right)}E(p)-\overline{\left(\frac{d}{c}\right)}E(p^{*})

\displaystyle-\frac{a}{c}E_{0}(p)E_{2}(q)-\frac{d}{c}E_{0}(p^{*})E_{2}(q^{*})-D(a,c;p,q)

for
c\neq 0
, and

\Phi(A)(p,q)=-\overline{\left(\frac{b}{d}\right)}E(p)-\frac{b}{d}E_{0}(p)E_{2}(q)

for
c=0
, where

(p^{*},q^{*})=(p,q)A=(ap+cq,bp+dq).

This map
\Phi(A)(p,q)
satisfies the cocycle relation

(2.1)

\Phi(AB)(p,q)=\Phi(A)(p,q)+\Phi(B)((p,q)A).

That is, if we let
\mathscr{F}
be the space of functions from
({\mathbb{C}}/L)^{2}
to
{\mathbb{C}}
, then
\Phi(A)(p,q)
represents a nontrivial cohomology class in
H^{1}({\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{L}),\mathscr{F})
, where the action of
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{L})
on
\mathscr{F}
is given by
(Af)(x)=f(xA)
.

Ito
[
5
, Theorem 1]
later constructed a function
H(u;p,q)
on the product of the upper-half space
\mathbb{H}^{3}
and
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
such that

\Phi(A)(p,q)=H(Au;p,q)-H(u;(p,q)A)

for all
u\in\mathbb{H}^{3}
and
A\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{L})
. In particular, the special value
H(u):=H(u;0,0)
satisfies

\Phi(A)=H(Au)-H(u),

analogous to the imaginary part of the logarithm of the Dedekind
\eta
function.

2.2.
The construction of
\Phi_{N}

Let
{\mathfrak{n}}\subset\mathcal{O}_{L}
be a nontrivial integral ideal generated by
N\in{\mathcal{O}}_{L}
, and assume from now on that
N|L
, i.e., that
N
divides the conductor of
\mathcal{O}_{L}
as an order in
K
.
Define the congruence subgroup

\Gamma_{0}({\mathfrak{n}})=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}({\mathcal{O}}_{L}):c\in{\mathfrak{n}}\right\},

and let

D^{N}(x,y):=D(Nx,y)-D(x,y).

Define the Dedekind-Rademacher-Bianchi homomorphism for congruence subgroups
\Gamma_{0}({\mathfrak{n}})\subset\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
as

\displaystyle\Phi_{N}\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle:=\Phi\begin{pmatrix}a&bN\\
c/N&d\end{pmatrix}-\Phi\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}E_{2}(0)I\left(\frac{(N-1)(a+d)}{c}\right)-D^{N}(a,c)&c\neq 0\\
E_{2}(0)I\left(\frac{(N-1)b}{d}\right)&c=0.\end{cases}

It is straightforward to extend it to a function of
(p,q)
for
p,q\in\mathbb{C}/L
. We define more generally

D^{N}(x,y;p,q):=D(Nx,y;p,q)-D(x,y;p,q)

and

(2.2)

\Phi_{N}\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}(p,q):=\Phi\begin{pmatrix}a&bN\\
c/N&d\end{pmatrix}(p,q)-\Phi\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}(p,q)

for
p,q\in{\mathbb{C}}/L
, and we denote

\Phi^{p,q}_{N}(A)=\Phi_{N}(A)(p,q),\qquad\Phi_{N}(A)=\Phi_{N}^{(0,0)}(A)=\Phi_{N}(A)(0,0).

More explicitly, for
c\neq 0
and
p,q\in\mathbb{C}/L
, we have

\displaystyle\Phi_{N}\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}(p,q)

\displaystyle=-(N-1)\overline{\left(\frac{a}{c}\right)}E(p)-(N-1)\overline{\left(\frac{d}{c}\right)}E(p^{*})

\displaystyle-(N-1)\frac{a}{c}E_{0}(p)E_{2}(q)-(N-1)\frac{d}{c}E_{0}(p^{*})E_{2}(q^{*})

\displaystyle-D^{N}(a,c;p,q),

where

(p^{*},q^{*})=(p,q)A_{N}=(ap+cq/N,Nbp+dq),

and for
c=0
,

\Phi_{N}(A)(p,q)=-(N-1)\overline{\left(\frac{b}{d}\right)}E(p)-(N-1)\frac{b}{d}E_{0}(p)E_{2}(q).

Proposition 2.1
.

\Phi_{N}(A)(p,q)
is a
1
-cocycle on
\Gamma_{0}({\mathfrak{n}})
with values in
\mathscr{F}
.

Proof.

In
\Gamma_{0}({\mathfrak{n}})
, let

\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a^{\prime}&b^{\prime}\\
c^{\prime}&d^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}a^{\prime\prime}&b^{\prime\prime}\\
c^{\prime\prime}&d^{\prime\prime}\end{pmatrix},

and note that

\begin{pmatrix}a&bN\\
c/N&d\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a^{\prime}&b^{\prime}N\\
c^{\prime}/N&d^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}a^{\prime\prime}&b^{\prime\prime}N\\
c^{\prime\prime}/N&d^{\prime\prime}\end{pmatrix}.

Let us denote these equations by
AA^{\prime}=A^{\prime\prime}
and
A_{N}A^{\prime}_{N}=A^{\prime\prime}_{N}
respectively.

Then using the cocycle property (
2.1
), we have by direct computation for
p,q\in{\mathbb{C}}/L
,

\displaystyle\Phi_{N}(A^{\prime\prime})(p,q)

\displaystyle=\Phi(A^{\prime\prime}_{N})(p,q)-\Phi(A^{\prime\prime})(p,q)

\displaystyle=\Phi(A_{N})(p,q)+\Phi(A^{\prime}_{N})((p,q)A_{N})-\Phi(A)(p,q)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ -\Phi(A^{\prime})((p,q)A)

\displaystyle=\Phi_{N}(A)(p,q)+\Phi_{N}(A^{\prime})((p,q)A),

where we have used the property that
(p,q)A_{N}\equiv(p,q)A
mod
L^{2}
.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

The map
\Phi_{N}
is a homomorphism with values in
\mathcal{O}_{F}
for
F=\mathbb{Q}(g_{2},g_{3},\sqrt{D})
.

Proof.

This follows from specializing the preceding proof to
(p,q)=(0,0)
. Namely, we have

\Phi_{N}\begin{pmatrix}a^{\prime\prime}&b^{\prime\prime}\\
c^{\prime\prime}&d^{\prime\prime}\end{pmatrix}=\Phi_{N}\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}+\Phi_{N}\begin{pmatrix}a^{\prime}&b^{\prime}\\
c^{\prime}&d^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}.

The integrality of
\Phi_{N}
follows directly from that of
\Phi
.
∎

Let
H
be as defined in
[
5
, (4)]
with Fourier expansion

(2.3)

H(u)=E_{2}(0)(z-\bar{z})-\frac{4\pi}{D(L)}\,v\sum^{\prime}_{m\in L,n\in L^{\prime}}\frac{\bar{m}n}{|mn|}K_{1}(4\pi|mn|v)e(mnz).

Corollary 2.3
.

Define
H_{N}(u):=H(Nu)-H(u)
for
H
as defined in (
2.3
). Then
\Phi_{N}
satisfies the transformation law

\Phi_{N}(A)=H_{N}(Au)-H_{N}(u).

Proof.

Since
\Phi
is independent of choice of
u\in\mathbb{H}^{3}
,
we can write
\Phi(A_{N})=H(A_{N}Nu)-H(Nu)
. Letting
A_{N}=\begin{pmatrix}a&bN\\
c/N&d\end{pmatrix}
, so that

Au=\frac{au+b}{cu+d},\qquad A_{N}Nu=\frac{aNu+Nb}{\frac{c}{N}Nu+d},

it follows that

\displaystyle\Phi_{N}(A)

\displaystyle=\Phi(A_{N})-\Phi(A)=H(A_{N}Nu)-H(Nu)-H(Au)+H(u)

\displaystyle=H(NAu)-H(Au)-(H(Nu)-H(u))=H_{N}(Au)-H_{N}(u)

and the result follows.
∎

We then have the following uniqueness theorem analogous to
[
5
, Theorem 4]
.To state the theorem fully, fix a sufficiently large real number
V
and define for every parallelogram
P
in
\mathbb{C}
the set

\Pi(P)=\{z+jv\in\mathbb{H}~|~z\in P,v>V\}

and fix the usual
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-invariant measure

v^{-3}{dx\ dy\ dv}

on
\mathbb{H}^{3}
. The discrete group
\Gamma_{0}({\mathfrak{n}})
acts discontinuously on
\mathbb{H}^{3}
and the volume of any fundamental domain of the action with respect to this measure is finite.

Proposition 2.4
.

Assume
\mathcal{O}_{L}\neq\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z}[i],\mathbb{Z}[e^{2\pi i/3}]
. Then the function
H_{N}(u)
is not identically zero. Moreover, if a complex-valued
C^{2}
-function
h_{N}
on
\mathbb{H}
satisfies the following conditions, then it coincides with
H_{N}(u)
:

(1)

For any matrix
M\in\Gamma_{0}({\mathfrak{n}})
and any parallelogram
P
,
h_{N}
is bounded on
M(\Pi(P)),

(2)

\Delta h_{N}=0
with
\Delta
defined as
\Delta=v^{2}\left(\partial_{x}^{2}+\partial_{y}^{2}+\partial_{v}^{2}\right)-v\partial_{v}
,

(3)

h_{N}(Au)=h_{N}(u)+\Phi_{N}(A)
for all
A\in\Gamma_{0}({\mathfrak{n}})
,

(4)

h_{N}(-z+jv)=-h_{N}(z+jv)
for
u=z+jv\in\mathbb{H}^{3}
.

Proof.

The function
H_{N}
satisfies the first and third conditions by (
2.3
) and Corollary
2.3
respectively. The other two conditions follow from the fact that
H(u)
is harmonic and satisfies
H(-z+jv)=-H(z+jv)
. On the other hand, given
h_{N}
satisfying (1), (2), and (3), let
f_{N}=h_{N}-H_{N}
. It is known that there exists a fundamental domain
\mathcal{F}
for
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{L})\backslash\mathbb{H}^{3}
of the form

\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{0}\cup\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}M_{i}(\Pi(P_{i}))\right),\qquad M_{i}\in{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(\mathbb{C}),

where
\mathcal{F}_{0}
is a compact set and
P_{i}
are parallelograms in
\mathbb{C}
for
i=1,\dots,n
. Since
\Gamma_{0}({\mathfrak{n}})
has finite index in
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{L})
we can also obtain a similar fundamental domain
\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{n}}
for
\Gamma_{0}({\mathfrak{n}})\backslash\mathbb{H}^{3}
. Then
|f_{N}|^{2}
is bounded and integrable on
\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{n}}
and
[
8
, Satz 5]
show that

0=-\int_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{n}}}\bar{f}_{N}\Delta f_{N}\frac{dx\ dy\ dv}{v^{3}}=\int_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{n}}}|\partial_{x}f_{N}|^{2}+|\partial_{y}f_{N}|^{2}+|\partial_{v}f_{N}|^{2}\frac{dx\ dy\ dv}{v^{3}}.

Hence
\partial_{x}f_{N}=\partial_{y}f_{N}=\partial_{v}f_{N}=0
on
\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{n}}
, and
f_{N}
must be constant on
\mathbb{H}^{3}
. Since
h_{N}
satisfies (4), then
f_{N}=0
.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

In the classical case, if we define the quotient of eta functions
h_{N}(z)=\eta(Nz)/\eta(z)
, then the Dedekind-Rademacher homomorphism
\phi_{N}
can be interpreted as the period of
\log h_{N}
by the rule

\log h_{N}(\gamma z)-\log h_{N}(z)=\frac{\pi i}{12}\phi_{N}(\gamma),\qquad\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}

for any
z\in\mathbb{H}^{2}

[
6
, (2.2)]
. Then just as
H(u)
is the analogue of the
\log\eta(z)
, so
H_{N}(u)
can be viewed as the analogue of
\log h_{N}(z)
.

2.3.
Hecke action

Let us first recall the Hecke operators in our setting. Let
\Gamma
be a finite index subgroup of the Bianchi group
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{K})
.
Given
g\in\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(K)
, consider the 3-folds
M,M_{g},M^{g}
associated to

\Gamma,\ \ \ \Gamma_{g}=\Gamma\cap g\Gamma g^{-1},\ \ \ \Gamma^{g}=\Gamma\cap g^{-1}\Gamma g

respectively. We have finite coverings induced by inclusion of fundamental groups

r_{g}:M_{g}\to M
and
r^{g}:M^{g}\to M
,
and so an isometry
\tau:M_{g}\to M^{g}
induced by the isomorphism between
\Gamma_{g}
and
\Gamma^{g}
given by conjugation by
g
. The composition
s_{g}:=r^{g}\circ\tau
gives us a second finite covering from
M_{g}
to
M
. The coverings
r_{g}
induce linear maps between homology groups

r_{g}^{*}:H^{i}(M,\mathcal{V})\to H^{i}(M_{g},\mathcal{V}).

The process of summing finitely many preimages in
M_{g}
of a point of
M
under
s_{g}
leads to

s_{g}^{*}:H^{i}(M_{g},\mathcal{V})\to H^{i}(M,\mathcal{V}).

Note that
s_{g}^{*}
is equivalent to the composition

H^{i}(M_{g},\mathcal{V})\to H^{i}(M^{g},\mathcal{V})\to H^{i}(M,\mathcal{V}),

where the second arrow is the corestriction map.

The Hecke operator
T_{g}
associated to
g\in\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(K)
is then defined as the composition

T_{g}:=s_{g}^{*}\circ r_{g}^{*}:H^{i}(M,\mathcal{V})\to H^{i}(M,\mathcal{V}).

Moverover, if we take
g\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(K)
and let

g=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{pmatrix},

where
p
is a prime in
\mathcal{O}
and
p\mathcal{O}
is a prime ideal in
\mathcal{O}
. Denote
T_{p}
by the associated Hecke operator. We recall the following properties of the homomorphism
\Phi
proved in
[
9
, Satz 7]
.

Lemma 2.6
.

For each homomorphism
\Phi=\Phi_{L}
where
\mathcal{O}L\subset L
we have

\Phi\circ T_{p}=(p+\bar{p})\Phi.

If
x=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}
, this gives
\Phi\circ T_{x}=-\Phi.

It is then straightforward to show the same for the new homomorphism
\Phi_{N}
.

Proposition 2.7
.

Assume
\mathcal{O}L\subset L
. For each
N|L
we have

\Phi_{N}\circ T_{p}=(p+\bar{p})\Phi_{N}.

For
x=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}
, then
\Phi_{N}\circ T_{x}=-\Phi_{N}.

Proof.

Applying the preceding lemma, the definition of
\Phi_{N}
gives

\displaystyle\Phi_{N}\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\Big|T_{p}

\displaystyle=\Phi\begin{pmatrix}a&bN\\
c/N&d\end{pmatrix}\Big|T_{p}-\Phi\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\Big|T_{p}

\displaystyle=(p+\bar{p})\Phi\begin{pmatrix}a&bN\\
c/N&d\end{pmatrix}-(p+\bar{p})\Phi\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=(p+\bar{p})\Phi_{N}\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}.

The second assertion follows similarly.
∎

Remark 2.8
.

As in the classical case, it would be desirable to show that
\Phi_{N}^{p,q}
is Eisenstein in the sense that it lies in the Eisenstein cohomology of
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{L})
. Unfortunately, we have not yet been able to verify this.

3.
Special values of
L
-functions

Define
u=z+jv
for
z\in\mathbb{C}
and
v\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and note that
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
acts on
\mathbb{H}^{3}
by
Mu=(au+b)(cu+d)^{-1}
. If an element of
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(\mathcal{O})
has a fixed point in
K
, it must be conjugate in
\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{n})
to an element

\begin{pmatrix}\zeta&a\\
0&\zeta^{-1}\end{pmatrix}
for
a\in K
and
\zeta
a root of unity in
K
. Thus for

(3.1)

A_{N}=\begin{pmatrix}a&Nb\\

Remark 3.3
.

We conclude with a brief comparison with a construction due to Weselmann. By Remark 2 of
[
11
, §5]
, Sczech’s cocycle can be realized as the specialization of the Eisenstein differential form
\omega_{\text{Eis}}
,

\Phi(A)(u,v)=2\pi\cdot\mathscr{G}_{\infty}\omega_{\text{Eis}}\big(\text{ch}_{\hat{\mathcal{O}}+u}\otimes\text{ch}_{\hat{\mathcal{O}}+v}\big)\left(A,1_{f}\right),

for all
A\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{F})
and
u,v\in F/\mathcal{O}
. Here ch
X
is the characteristic function of any subset
X
of the finite adeles
{\bf A}_{f}
of
F
, and
\mathscr{G}_{\infty}
induces an isomorphism between de Rham cohomology and group cohomology. But Weselmann’s construction relies on a choice of maximal compact subgroup
K
of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F)
, and by allowing ramification at finite primes, one can deduce a similar 1-cocycle on
\Gamma_{0}(N)
. But it is not clear whether the cocycle obtained this way is related to ours, though it would be natural to expect that both occur in the associated Eisenstein cohomology class.

Abstract
Using data samples with a total integrated luminosity of 22.1 ​ fb − 1 {22.1~\rm fb^{-1}} at center-of-mass energies between 4.008 4.008 and 4.951 4.951 GeV {\rm GeV} collected with the BESIII detector, the cross sections of e + ​ e − → π 0 ​ π 0 ​ ψ ​ ( 3686 ) e^{+}e^{-}\to\pi^{0}\pi^{0}\psi(3686) process are measured. The obtained cross sections are found to be approximately one-half of those of e + ​ e − → π + ​ π − ​ ψ ​ ( 3686 ) e^{+}e^{-}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\psi(3686) , consistent with the isospin symmetry expectation. A coherent fit to the dressed cross sections is performed, with the Y ⁡ ( 4230 ) Y(4230) parameters fixed at the values measured in e + ​ e − → π + ​ π − ​ ψ ​ ( 3686 ) e^{+}e^{-}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\psi(3686) . The significances of the Y ⁡ ( 4390 ) Y(4390) and Y ⁡ ( 4660 ) Y(4660) are both larger than 5 ​ σ 5\sigma , and their masses and widths are consistent with the previous measurement in the e + ​ e − → π + ​ π − ​ ψ ​ ( 3686 ) e^{+}e^{-}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\psi(3686) process.



I
INTRODUCTION

Over the past two decades, a series of charmonium-like states with

J^{PC}=1^{--}
, referred to as the
Y
states, have been discovered
and confirmed by numerous experiments.
As exotic state candidates, the
Y
states cannot be easily accommodated within
the conventional quark model
Barnes et al. 2005
and their internal properties remain
controversial.
Possible interpretations include hybrid mesons, meson molecules,
hadrocharmonia, and tetraquarks, but none of them has been proven
conclusive to date
Chen et al. 2016
;
Guo et al. 2018
;
Brambilla et al. 2020
;
Wang 2026
.
Therefore, comprehensive studies of their production and decay patterns
Wang et al. 2025
;
Nakamura et al. 2025
,
as well as precise measurements of their resonant parameters from different
experiments, are highly desired to provide critical information to
explore the nature of the
Y
states.

Among all
Y
states, the
Y(4230)
and
Y(4390)
, previously known as

Y(4260)
and
Y(4360)
, were first observed
by the
BABAR
and Belle experiments with the initial-state radiation (ISR) technique in
the processes of
e^{+}e^{-}\to\gamma_{\rm ISR}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi

Aubert et al. 2005
;
Yuan et al. 2007
;
Lees et al. 2012
;
Liu et al. 2013

and
e^{+}e^{-}\to\gamma_{\rm ISR}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\psi(3686)

Aubert et al. 2007
;
Lees et al. 2014
;
Wang et al. 2007
;
Wang et al. 2015
, respectively.
With high statistics data samples collected by the BESIII experiment,
the
Y(4260)
and
Y(4360)
were observed in several decay modes and measured
with improved precision.
Furthermore, it was found that
Y(4260)
consists of two resonant structures,

Y(4230)
and
Y(4320)

Ablikim et al. 2017a
.
The
Y(4230)
was also observed in the processes

e^{+}e^{-}\to\pi^{0}\pi^{0}J/\psi

Ablikim et al. 2020a
,

e^{+}e^{-}\to K^{0}_{S}K^{0}_{S}J/\psi

Ablikim et al. 2023
,

e^{+}e^{-}\to K^{+}K^{-}J/\psi

Ablikim et al. 2022a
,

e^{+}e^{-}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\psi(3686)

Ablikim et al. 2017b
;
Ablikim et al. 2021a
,

e^{+}e^{-}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}h_{c}

Ablikim et al. 2017c
,

e^{+}e^{-}\to\omega\chi_{c0}

Ablikim et al. 2015a
;
Ablikim et al. 2019a
,

e^{+}e^{-}\to\pi^{+}D^{0}D^{*-}

Ablikim et al. 2019b
, and

e^{+}e^{-}\to\eta J/\psi

Ablikim et al. 2020b
;
Ablikim et al. 2024
.
The
Y(4390)
was observed in the processes

e^{+}e^{-}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}h_{c}

Ablikim et al. 2017c
,

e^{+}e^{-}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\psi_{2}(3823)

Ablikim et al. 2022b
,
and
e^{+}e^{-}\to\eta J/\psi

Ablikim et al. 2020b
;
Ablikim et al. 2024
.
Although there are many experimental studies of
Y(4230)
and
Y(4390)
with high precision,
the resonance parameters from various processes are different, which
needs further investigation.
Analogous to the
Y(4230)
and
Y(4390)
, the
Y(4660)
was first observed
by Belle and
BABAR
with the ISR process of
e^{+}e^{-}\to\gamma_{\rm ISR}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\psi(3686)

Wang et al. 2007
;
Wang et al. 2015
;
Lees et al. 2014
,
and was also observed and confirmed by the BESIII experiment
in the processes
e^{+}e^{-}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\psi(3686)

Ablikim et al. 2021a
,
and
e^{+}e^{-}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\psi_{2}(3823)

Ablikim et al. 2022b
.

The possible existence of
Y
states in the
\pi^{0}\pi^{0}\psi(3686)
final states
has been investigated by the BESIII experiment in Ref.
Ablikim et al. 2018
.
However, no resonance-like structures were observed in the
cross section line shape due to the limited data statistics.
In this paper, an updated analysis of
e^{+}e^{-}\to\pi^{0}\pi^{0}\psi(3686)
at
center-of-mass (c.m.) energies (
\sqrt{s}
) between 4.008 and 4.951 GeV is performed.
The data samples used in this analysis were collected with the
BESIII detector at the BEPCII collider with an integrated luminosity
of
{22.1~\rm fb^{-1}}
, including the data samples used in
the previous analysis
Ablikim et al. 2018
.
The much larger data samples used in this analysis allow to
search for possible
Y
states in the
\pi^{0}\pi^{0}\psi(3686)
final state.
In this analysis, the
\psi(3686)
is reconstructed via its decay to
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi
,
the
J/\psi
via
J/\psi\to\ell^{+}\ell^{-}~(\ell=e/\mu)
and the
\pi^{0}
via
\pi^{0}\to\gamma\gamma
.

II
Description of BEPCII and the BESIII detector

The BESIII detector
Ablikim et al. 2010a
records symmetric
e^{+}e^{-}
collisions
provided by the BEPCII collider
Yu et al. 2016

in the center-of-mass energy range from 1.84 to 4.95 GeV,
with a peak luminosity of
1.1\times 10^{33}\;\text{cm}^{-2}\text{s}^{-1}

achieved at
\sqrt{s}=3.773\;\text{GeV}
.
BESIII has collected large data samples in this energy region
Ablikim et al. 2020c
;
Lu et al. 2020
;
Zhang et al. 2022
.
The cylindrical core of the BESIII detector covers 93% of the full solid angle and consists of a helium-based
multilayer drift chamber (MDC), a time-of-flight
system (TOF), and a CsI(Tl) electromagnetic calorimeter (EMC),
which are all enclosed in a superconducting solenoidal magnet
providing a 1.0 T magnetic field.
The solenoid is supported by an
octagonal flux-return yoke with resistive plate counter muon
identification modules interleaved with steel.
The charged-particle momentum resolution at
1~{\rm GeV}/c
is

0.5\%
, and the
{\rm d}E/{\rm d}x
resolution is
6\%
for electrons
from Bhabha scattering. The EMC measures photon energies with a
resolution of
2.5\%
(
5\%
) at
1
GeV in the barrel (end cap)
region. The time resolution in the plastic scintillator TOF barrel region is 68 ps, while
that in the end cap region was 110 ps. The end cap TOF
system was upgraded in 2015 using multigap resistive plate chamber
technology, providing a time resolution of 60 ps
(
Li et al. 2017
;
Guo et al. 2017
;
Cao et al. 2020
)
, which benefits 76.5% of
the data used in this analysis.

Monte Carlo (MC) simulated data samples produced with a
geant4
-based
Agostinelli et al. 2003
software package, which
includes the geometric description of the BESIII detector and the
detector response, are used to determine detection efficiencies
and to estimate backgrounds. The simulation models the beam
energy spread and ISR in the
e^{+}e^{-}

annihilation with the generator
kkmc

Jadach et al. 2001
.
The inclusive MC sample includes the production of open charm
processes, the ISR production of vector charmonium(-like) states,
and the continuum processes incorporated in
kkmc

Jadach et al. 2001
.
All particle decays are modelled with
evtgen

Lange 2001
;
Ping 2008
using branching fractions
either taken from the Particle Data Group (PDG)
Navas et al. 2024
when available,
or otherwise estimated with
lundcharm

Chen et al. 2000
;
Yang et al. 2014
.
Final state radiation (FSR) from charged final state particles is incorporated using the

photos
package
Barberio et al. 1991
.
Signal MC samples of
e^{+}e^{-}\to\pi^{0}\pi^{0}\psi(3686)
are generated
with the amplitude model from the partial wave analysis (PWA) in Ref.
Ablikim et al. 2021a
.

III
EVENT SELECTION

The event selection criteria are designed to ensure reliable identification of the final-state particles and proper reconstruction of the event.
Good charged tracks detected in the MDC are required to be within
a polar angle (
\theta
) range of
|\rm{cos\theta}|<0.93
,
where
\theta
is defined with respect to the
z
-axis,
which is the symmetry axis of the MDC.
The distance of closest approach to the interaction point (IP)
must be less than 10 cm along the
z
-axis
|V_{z}|
,
and less than 1 cm in the plane perpendicular to
z
-axis
|V_{xy}|
.
Photon candidates are identified using isolated showers in the EMC.
The deposited energy of each shower must be more than 25
\rm MeV
in the barrel
region (
|\cos\theta|<0.80
) or more than 50
\rm MeV
in the end cap
region (
0.86<|\cos\theta|<0.92
).
To exclude showers that originate from charged tracks,
the angle subtended by the EMC shower and the position of
the closest charged track at the EMC
must be greater than 10 degrees as measured from the IP.
To suppress electronic noise and showers unrelated to the collision event,
the difference between the EMC time and the event start time
is required to be within [0, 700] ns.

The detailed MC study indicates the pions and leptons
of signal are well-separated kinematically. Therefore the charged tracks
with momenta above 1.1
{\rm GeV}/c
are assigned to be leptons,
while those with momenta below 0.75
{\rm GeV}/c
are pions.
Electron and muon are separated by using the deposited energy (
E
)
in the EMC, i.e., both electron and positron must satisfy
E/p>0.7
,
while both muons must satisfy
E<0.45

{\rm GeV}
, where
p
is the momentum of charged
tracks measured by the MDC. Signal candidates are required to have a pair of charged
pions with zero net charge and a lepton pair with the same flavor
and opposite charge.

To improve the resolution and suppress the background,
a four-constraint (4C) kinematic fit imposing energy-momentum conservation
under the hypothesis of
e^{+}e^{-}\to\gamma\gamma\gamma\gamma\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\ell^{+}\ell^{-}
is
performed, and
\chi_{4\rm C}^{2}<120
is required.
For events with more than four photons, the combination
with the smallest
\chi_{4\rm C}^{2}
is retained.
The four selected photons are combined into two
\pi^{0}
candidates
by minimizing

(M(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2})-M_{\pi^{0}})^{2}+(M(\gamma_{3}\gamma_{4})-M_{\pi^{0}})^{2}
,
where
M(\gamma_{i}\gamma_{j})
is the invariant mass of two
photons and
M_{\pi^{0}}
is the
\pi^{0}
nominal mass quoted from
the PDG
Navas et al. 2024
.
Both
\pi^{0}
candidates are further required to satisfy

|M(\gamma_{i}\gamma_{j})-M_{\pi^{0}}|<20

{\rm MeV}/c^{2}
, which is
larger than five times the
\pi^{0}
mass resolution.
In order to improve the mass resolution, a seven-constraint (7C)
kinematic fit with the additional mass constraints on the two
\pi^{0}
’s and

J/\psi
candidates to their nominal masses is carried out.
The particle kinematics information updated by the 7C kinematic fit is used in the following analysis, and the
\psi(3686)
candidate is reconstructed from the
\ell^{+}\ell^{-}
pair and
charged pion pair.

The Born cross section is determined by the formula

\sigma^{\mathrm{B}}=\frac{N_{\mathrm{sig}}}{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{int}}\cdot\left(1+\delta^{\mathrm{ISR}}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{|1-\Pi|^{2}}\cdot\mathcal{B}\cdot\epsilon},

(1)

where
N_{\mathrm{sig}}
is the number of signal events obtained
from the fit to the
M(\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\ell^{+}\ell^{-})
distribution,

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{int}}
is the integrated luminosity,

1+\delta^{\mathrm{ISR}}
is the ISR correction factor,

\frac{1}{|1-\Pi|^{2}}
is the vacuum polarization
correction factor taken from Ref.
Actis et al. 2010
,

\mathcal{B}
is the branching fraction of the subsequent decay quoted from the PDG
Navas et al. 2024
,
and
\epsilon
is the detection efficiency.
The ISR correction factor and the detection efficiency
are estimated using the signal MC samples. An iterative
weight method
Sun et al. 2021
is used to consider the influence
of the cross-section line shape and to estimate the ISR
correction factor. The relationship between the dressed cross section
and the Born cross section is given by

\sigma^{\mathrm{D}}=\sigma^{\mathrm{B}}/|1-\Pi|^{2}
.

For those low statistics data sets with
the signal significance less than 3
\sigma
, the
cross section upper limits are estimated using the
Bayesian method
D’Agostini 2003
.
As an example, the likelihood distribution at
\sqrt{s}=4.0076

{\rm GeV}
is shown
in Fig.
3
.
With a uniform prior probability density function, the
Bayesian upper limit at 90% confidence level (C.L.)
is estimated by integrating the likelihood distribution
from zero to the upper limit, which gives 90% of the
total area.

V
FIT TO THE DRESSED CROSS SECTIONS

To study the possible resonant structures in the process

e^{+}e^{-}\to\pi^{0}\pi^{0}\psi(3686)
, an extended maximum likelihood fit
is performed to the dressed cross sections at different c.m. energies. For data sets
with high statistics in Table
1
,
the cross section is expected to
follow an asymmetric Gaussian distribution
G_{i}
.
Following Ref.
Ablikim et al. 2017c
,

G_{i}
is defined as

\displaystyle G_{i}(\sigma_{i}^{\text{fit}}(\sqrt{s}))=\frac{1}{\delta_{i}\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{\left(\sigma_{i}^{\text{fit}}(\sqrt{s})-\sigma_{i}\right)^{2}}{2\delta_{i}^{2}}},

(2)

\displaystyle\delta_{i}=\begin{cases}\delta_{i}^{\text{negative}}&\sigma^{\text{fit}}(\sqrt{s})<\sigma_{i}\\
\delta_{i}^{\text{positive}}&\sigma^{\text{fit}}(\sqrt{s})\geq\sigma_{i}\end{cases},

where
\sigma_{i}^{\text{fit}}(\sqrt{s})
is the theoretical cross section
as defined in Eq.
4
,

\sigma_{i}
is the measured cross section of the
i
-th data point,
and
\delta_{i}
is the statistical uncertainty, which is set to

\delta_{i}^{\text{negative}}
and
\delta_{i}^{\text{positive}}

for the cross section below and above the measured value, respectively.
For the low statistics data sets, the corresponding cross section likelihood
distributions, which are parameterized as
L(\sigma^{\rm fit}_{i}(\sqrt{s}))

with double-Gaussian function as shown in Fig.
3
, are
used to describe the cross section distributions in the fit. The total likelihood function is defined as the product of
the likelihood functions of all data sets, which is

L=\prod_{i=1}^{N_{1}=31}G_{i}(\sigma^{\text{fit}})\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{N_{2}=10}L_{j}(\sigma^{\text{fit}})

(3)

, where
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
are the numbers of data points
with or without sufficient statistics, respectively.

The cross-section line shape of
e^{+}e^{-}\to\pi^{0}\pi^{0}\psi(3686)
is consistent
with that of
e^{+}e^{-}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\psi(3686)

Ablikim et al. 2021a
and
three resonant structures are observed there.
Therefore, a model including the coherent sum of three resonant structures

Y(4230)
,
Y(4390)
and
Y(4660)
as well as the continuum is used to describe the cross section:

\sigma^{\mathrm{fit}}(\sqrt{s})=\left|\sum_{k}e^{i\phi_{k}}\cdot BW_{k}(s)+e^{i\phi_{\mathrm{cont}}}\cdot\psi_{\mathrm{cont}}\right|^{2},

(4)

where
BW_{k}
is the Breit-Wigner function for the
k
-th resonance,
the
\psi_{\mathrm{cont}}
is the non-resonant function, and
the
\phi_{k}
and
\phi_{\mathrm{cont}}
are the corresponding phase
relative to the
Y(4390)
.
The
BW_{k}
is defined as

BW_{k}(s)=\frac{M_{k}}{\sqrt{s}}\frac{\sqrt{12\pi\Gamma_{k}^{\mathrm{tot}}\Gamma_{k}^{ee}{\mathcal{B}}_{k}}}{s-M_{k}^{2}+iM_{k}\Gamma_{k}^{\mathrm{tot}}}\sqrt{\frac{\Phi(\sqrt{s})}{\Phi\left(M_{k}\right)}},

(5)

where
M_{k}
,
\Gamma_{k}^{\mathrm{tot}}
,
\Gamma_{k}^{ee}
,
and
{\mathcal{B}}_{k}
are the mass, total width, the partial width
coupling to
e^{+}e^{-}
, and the branching fraction of the
k
-th
resonance, respectively. The
\Phi(\sqrt{s})
is the standard
phase space factor
Navas et al. 2024
.
The continuum part is parameterized as

\psi_{\mathrm{cont}}=\frac{a}{(\sqrt{s})^{n}}\sqrt{\Phi(\sqrt{s})}

(6)

with the free parameters
a
and
n
.

With the above model, the fit to the
dressed cross sections is performed by maximizing the
likelihood function
L
.
The statistical significance of the
resonant structures is estimated by comparing the
likelihood values of the fits with and without the
corresponding resonant structures included.
The resultant significances of the resonant structures are

1.9\sigma
,
11.4\sigma
, and
5.7\sigma
for

Y(4230)
,
Y(4390)
, and
Y(4660)
, respectively.
Since the significance of
Y(4230)
is not substantial,
the parameters of
Y(4230)
are fixed to
the values from the measurement of
e^{+}e^{-}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\psi(3686)

Ablikim et al. 2021a
,
while the parameters of
Y(4390)
and
Y(4660)
are
free in the fit.
By scanning three relative phases, four solutions are
found with similar fit quality and identical
masses and widths of
Y(4390)
and
Y(4660)
.
The fit results are shown in Table
2

and Fig.
5
.

The uncertainty of the radiative correction includes two parts.
The first one stems from the precision of the ISR
calculation in the generator
kkmc

Ping 2014
, which is 0.5%.
The other one is from

and
\epsilon

1
, relying on the

(1+\delta^{\mathrm{ISR}})\cdot\epsilon

of the
(1+\delta^{\mathrm{ISR}})\cdot\epsilon

systematic uncertainty of the radiative correction.
The uncertainty associated with the
J/\psi
mass requirement is
estimated by smearing the
M(\ell^{+}\ell^{-})
distribution of the MC sample
with a Gaussian function, which represents the resolution difference
between data and MC simulation, and the resulting signal efficiency variation
is taken as the systematic uncertainty.
The same procedure is applied to the
\pi^{0}
mass requirement
to estimate the related systematic uncertainty.
For the systematic uncertainty from the kinematic fit, the
helix parameters of the charged tracks are corrected to
make the pull distribution of MC sample more consistent with
the data and the signal efficiency is re-evaluated, and the
resultant difference is taken as the uncertainty.
To estimate the systematic uncertainty of the MC modeling,
the signal efficiency is re-evaluated using 200 regenerated MC samples
in which the PWA parameters are varied within their uncertainties,
while taking into account the correlations among these parameters through their full covariance matrix.
The standard deviation of the signal efficiency distribution
is taken as the systematic uncertainty.
The systematic uncertainties related to the fit procedure
are estimated by replacing the first-order polynomial function
by a second-order polynomial
function for the background shape, and varying the
resolution of the convolved Gaussian function for the signal
shape by one standard deviation.

Barlow 2002
;
Ablikim et al. 2022e
,
the systematic uncertainty related to the fit range is
found to be negligible.
The uncertainties from the
other selections, trigger simulation, event start time determination,
and FSR simulation and other
sources, are conservatively taken as 1.0%.
All the systematic uncertainties are summarized in Table
3
.
Assuming all sources of systematic uncertainties are
independent, the total uncertainties in the cross section
measurement are obtained by summing the individual uncertainties
in quadrature, which range from 6.2% to 7.2%.

VI.2
Systematic Uncertainties of Resonant Parameter Measurement

The systematic uncertainties of the resonant parameters from the fit to the
cross section line shape are as follows.
The systematic uncertainty associated with the collision energy is
conservatively estimated to be 0.8
\rm MeV

Ablikim et al. 2016
;
Ablikim et al. 2021b
.
It is common for all data samples and causes a global shift for the
masses of the resonances.
The uncertainty due to the energy spread is estimated by convolving the
fit probability density function with a Gaussian function with a resolution of 1.6
\rm MeV
, which is
the energy spread determined by the beam energy measurement system
Abakumova et al. 2011
.
The uncertainty from the statistical uncertainties of the cross sections is
estimated by incorporating the correlated and uncorrelated systematic uncertainties of
the measured cross sections in the fit following the likelihood construction method
in Ref.
Ablikim et al. 2024
.
In the nominal fit, the mass and width of
Y(4230)
are fixed to the
values from Ref.
Ablikim et al. 2021a
. To estimate the relevant systematic uncertainty, the
mass and width of
Y(4230)
are varied within their uncertainties. The resultant differences of
each parameter
are taken as the systematic uncertainties.
To estimate the uncertainty related to the parameterization of the non-resonant part in
the fit model, the non-resonant part in Eq.
4

is replaced by a function of
\psi_{\mathrm{cont}}=\sqrt{\Phi(\sqrt{s})e^{-p_{0}u}p_{1}}

in Ref.
Lees et al. 2012
;
Ablikim et al. 2017a

with
p_{0}
and
p_{1}
as free parameters, and
u=\sqrt{s}-(2M(\pi^{\pm})+M(\psi(3686)))
.
The cross section line shape fit is performed with the above scenario individually and the resulting differences are taken as the systematic uncertainties
as shown in Table
4
.
The total systematic
uncertainty is obtained by quadratically summing the individual systematic uncertainties
in quadrature, under the assumption that
they are uncorrelated.

VII
SUMMARY

The cross sections of the process
e^{+}e^{-}\to\pi^{0}\pi^{0}\psi(3686)
are measured at c.m. energies between
4.008 and 4.951
{\rm GeV}
using data samples with a total
integrated luminosity of
{22.1~\rm fb^{-1}}
collected with the BESIII detector
operating at the BEPCII. The measured cross sections are found to be
approximately one-half of those of the charged channel

e^{+}e^{-}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\psi(3686)

Ablikim et al. 2021a
, which are consistent with the
expectation from isospin symmetry.

The dressed cross sections are fitted with a coherent sum of three resonant
structures,
Y(4230)
,
Y(4390)
and
Y(4660)
, as well as the same non-resonant component as used in the charged channel.
The statistical significances of
Y(4390)
and
Y(4660)
are larger than
5\sigma
, which confirm
the existence of these two states in the
e^{+}e^{-}\to\pi^{0}\pi^{0}\psi(3686)
process.
The mass and width of
Y(4390)
are measured to be
{\rm 4383.0\pm 8.6\pm 1.9}
MeV/
c^{2}
,

{117.4\pm 20.7\pm 4.8}
MeV, and those of
Y(4660)
are

{\rm 4684.0\pm 17.3\pm 1.9}
MeV/
c^{2}
,
{119.5\pm 47.1\pm 9.1}
MeV, where
the first uncertainties are statistical and the second systematic.
The parameters of the resonant structures are consistent with the
results from the charged channel within uncertainties.
The results from this work provide valuable information for the studies of
the charmonium-like states and the nature of the
XYZ
particles.

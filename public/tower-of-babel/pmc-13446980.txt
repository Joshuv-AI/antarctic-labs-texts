Impact of Cervical Sagittal Alignment Changes on Postoperative C5 Palsy: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis
Authors: Sadegh Bagherzadeh, Leila Bahari, Ramin Kordi, P. Mitchell Johansen, Gersham Rainone, Gautam Rao, Khalil Komlakh, Ali Keipourfard, Kamkar Aeenfar, Puya Alikhani, Mohsen Rostami
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND AND OBJECTIVES:Postoperative C5 palsy (CP) is a recognized complication after cervical decompressive surgery for degenerative cervical myelopathy. Although cervical sagittal alignment has been implicated in its pathogenesis, the relative impact of static alignment parameters vs postoperative alignment changes remains unclear. This study aimed to evaluate the association between cervical sagittal alignment and postoperative CP, with emphasis on the magnitude of alignment correction.METHODS:A systematic review and meta-analysis was conducted in accordance with Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses 2020 guidelines. PubMed, Web of Science, Scopus, and Embase were searched from inception to January 2025. Retrospective cohort studies comparing degenerative cervical myelopathy patients with and without postoperative CP and reporting cervical sagittal parameters were included. Outcomes included preoperative values, postoperative values, and magnitude of change (Δ) in C2-C7 lordosis, C2-C7 sagittal vertical axis (SVA), C4-C5 segmental lordosis, T1 slope, T1 slope−cervical lordosis mismatch, and Ishihara index. Standardized mean differences (SMDs) with 95% CIs were pooled.RESULTS:Thirty-seven studies comprising 4948 patients were included, with an overall CP incidence of 10.83%. Static preoperative parameters, including C2-C7 lordosis, C2-C7 SVA, T1 slope, and T1 slope−cervical lordosis mismatch, were not significantly associated with CP. By contrast, patients who developed CP demonstrated significantly greater postoperative increases in C2-C7 lordosis (SMD = 1.04), C2-C7 SVA (SMD = 1.38), C4-C5 segmental lordosis (SMD = 0.44), and Ishihara index (SMD = 1.79). Subgroup analyses showed significant associations in anterior procedures and laminectomy, but not in laminoplasty.CONCLUSION:Postoperative CP is primarily driven by the magnitude of sagittal alignment correction rather than baseline alignment. These findings support a stretch-mediated mechanism related to postoperative spinal cord and nerve root biomechanics and highlight the importance of balanced sagittal correction to reduce the risk of CP.

Degenerative cervical myelopathy (DCM) is a progressive disorder caused by chronic compression of the cervical spinal cord and represents the most common indication for cervical decompressive surgery in adults. Surgical interventions, including anterior cervical diskectomy and fusion, laminoplasty, and posterior decompression with or without fusion, have been shown to halt neurological decline and improve functional outcomes in most patients.1 Despite these benefits, postoperative complications remain a major concern, among which C5 palsy (CP) is one of the most disabling and unpredictable.

Postoperative CP is typically characterized by new-onset deltoid weakness, with or without biceps involvement and associated sensory deficits, occurring in the early postoperative period. This complication can delay rehabilitation and negatively affect patient satisfaction and quality of life.2 The reported incidence varies widely, ranging from <5% to nearly 30%, depending on surgical approach, patient characteristics, and diagnostic criteria.3,4

The pathophysiology of CP is incompletely understood and likely multifactorial. Proposed mechanisms include direct intraoperative root injury, spinal cord ischemia–reperfusion, segmental cord damage, and postoperative nerve root traction.3,5-10 Among these, the “tethering” or stretch injury hypothesis has gained particular attention. According to this concept, posterior migration or elongation of the spinal cord after decompression increases tension on the C5 nerve root, which is anatomically vulnerable due to its shorter length and more horizontal takeoff angle compared with other cervical roots.11

Cervical sagittal alignment has therefore been implicated as a potential contributor to this tethering mechanism. Parameters such as C2-C7 cervical lordosis (CL), cervical sagittal vertical axis (cSVA), T1 slope (TS), segmental lordosis at C4-C5, and composite indices such as the Ishihara index have been evaluated as possible predictors of CP. Excessive restoration of lordosis or large postoperative sagittal corrections may exaggerate posterior spinal cord drift or longitudinal cord stretch, thereby increasing tension within the C5 nerve root.12-15

However, existing studies have produced inconsistent findings. Although some investigations report strong associations between increased lordotic correction, segmental alignment changes, or foraminal narrowing and CP,12 others have failed to demonstrate meaningful correlations between static preoperative alignment parameters and postoperative neurological deficits.16 These discrepancies likely reflect heterogeneity in surgical techniques, radiographic measurements, and analytical approaches across studies.

Importantly, many prior investigations have focused on absolute preoperative or postoperative alignment values rather than the magnitude of alignment change induced by surgery. From a biomechanical standpoint, dynamic sagittal alignment changes may be more relevant to spinal cord mechanics and nerve root stretch than static measurements alone. A systematic synthesis emphasizing alignment change across surgical approaches may therefore provide clearer insight into the mechanisms underlying CP.

Accordingly, this systematic review and meta-analysis aimed to evaluate the association between cervical sagittal alignment parameters and postoperative CP in patients undergoing surgery for DCM. We specifically compared preoperative values, postoperative values, and the magnitude of change in key sagittal parameters—including C2-C7 lordosis, C2-C7 cSVA, C4-C5 segmental lordosis, T1 slope, T1 slope−cervical lordosis (TS−CL) mismatch, and the Ishihara index—between patients with and without postoperative CP.

METHODS

Protocol and Registration

This systematic review and meta-analysis was conducted in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) 2020 guidelines.17 The study protocol was prospectively registered in the PROSPERO database (registration number: CRD420261306120).

Search Strategy, Databases, Eligibility Criteria, and Selection Process

A comprehensive search was performed in PubMed, Web of Science, Scopus, and Embase from database inception through January 2025. The detailed search strategy, including keywords, Boolean operators, and eligibility criteria, is provided in Table 1. Reference lists of included studies were manually screened to identify additional relevant publications.

All retrieved records were imported into the CADIMA web-based systematic review platform, where duplicate entries were removed both automatically and manually.18 Two independent reviewers screened titles and abstracts, followed by full-text assessment of potentially eligible studies. Disagreements were resolved through discussion and, when necessary, consultation with the senior author (M.R.). The overall selection process and reasons for exclusion are illustrated in the PRISMA flow diagram (Figure 1).

Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses 2020 flow diagram illustrating the selection process for studies included in the systematic review.

Studies were included if they evaluated adult patients with DCM undergoing cervical decompressive surgery and reported radiographic sagittal alignment parameters in patients with and without postoperative CP. Eligible designs included prospective and retrospective cohort studies, clinical trials, and case series with comparative data. Studies involving pediatric populations, non-DCM etiologies, non-original research, single-case reports, or insufficient radiographic data were excluded. Only English-language articles with acceptable methodological quality were included.

Data Extraction and Outcomes of Interest

Data extraction was performed independently by 2 reviewers using a standardized form. Patients were categorized into CP and non-C5 palsy (NCP) groups. Extracted variables included study characteristics, demographics, surgical approach, CP incidence, and radiographic sagittal alignment parameters. Radiographic outcomes included preoperative values, postoperative values, and the magnitude of change (Δ) for each parameter. Parameters of interest were C2-C7 lordosis, C2-C7 sagittal vertical axis (SVA), C4-C5 segmental lordosis, T1 slope, TS−CL mismatch, and the Ishihara index.

When individual patient data were unavailable, summary statistics were extracted directly from published reports. For studies reporting paired preoperative and postoperative values without change scores, mean differences were calculated as the difference between group means. The SD of change was conservatively estimated assuming independence between measurements, which may overestimate variability but avoids underestimation of uncertainty.

Risk-of-Bias Assessment

Methodological quality and risk of bias were independently assessed by 2 reviewers using the Newcastle–Ottawa Scale (NOS) for nonrandomized studies.19 Domains included selection, comparability, and outcome assessment. Only studies with NOS scores ≥7 were included in the quantitative synthesis. Discrepancies in scoring were resolved by consensus or by consultation with a third reviewer.

Handling of Overlapping Cohorts

To avoid duplication of patient populations, potential overlap between studies was evaluated by comparing institutions, author groups, recruitment periods, and ethical approval identifiers. When overlapping cohorts were identified, the study with the larger sample size and more comprehensive reporting was retained. In cases of partial overlap, data were extracted on a parameter-specific basis to ensure that overlapping populations were not included simultaneously in the same pooled analysis.

Statistical Methods Used for Synthesis

Meta-analyses were conducted using Comprehensive Meta-Analysis software, version 4.0. (Biostat, Inc.). Radiographic parameters were included in quantitative synthesis only if reported by at least 2 independent studies. Continuous variables were analyzed using standardized mean differences (SMDs) with 95% CIs. A two-sided P-value <.05 was considered statistically significant.

Subgroup analyses were performed according to surgical approach (anterior procedures, laminectomy with fusion, and laminoplasty). A mixed analysis including all approaches was also reported when appropriate. Statistical heterogeneity was evaluated using the I2 statistic20 and categorized as unimportant (0%-40%), moderate (30%-60%), substantial (50%-90%), or considerable (75%-100%). Results with substantial heterogeneity were interpreted cautiously.

Publication bias was assessed using funnel plot analysis for outcomes reported in more than 3 studies.21 When asymmetry was detected, the trim-and-fill method was applied to evaluate the robustness of pooled estimates.22

Ethical Approval

This study is a systematic review and meta-analysis of previously published studies and does not involve human participants or animals. Therefore, institutional review board approval and informed consent were not required.

Data Availability

The data supporting the findings of this study are derived from publicly available studies included in the systematic review. Extracted data are available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request. The PROSPERO registration ID is CRD420261306120.

RESULTS

Study Selection

The database search yielded 585 records (Scopus n = 294, PubMed n = 126, Web of Science n = 58, Embase n = 107). After removing 223 duplicates, 362 studies underwent title and abstract screening, resulting in the exclusion of 279 reports. Eighty-three full texts were assessed, 4 of which were inaccessible. Of the remaining 79 articles, 46 were excluded due to conference abstracts (n = 17), duplicate reports (n = 22), insufficient primary data (n = 4), or non-English language (n = 3). Citation screening identified 13 additional studies, 9 of which were excluded due to incompatible data grouping. Ultimately, 37 studies met the inclusion criteria and were included in both qualitative and quantitative syntheses.12-16,23-55 The selection process is summarized in the PRISMA flow diagram (Figure 1).

The included studies consisted of 37 retrospective cohort investigations published between 2007 and 2025 (Table 2). Studies originated from Asia, North America, and Europe, most commonly from Japan (n = 13), China (n = 12), the United States (n = 8), and South Korea (n = 4). All cohorts involved adult patients undergoing cervical decompressive surgery for DCM.

Similarly, the change in the Ishihara index was significantly greater in patients who developed CP, with a large effect size (SMD = 1.79; P < .001). By contrast, preoperative T1 slope (P = .667) and preoperative TS−CL mismatch (P = .625) were not significantly associated with postoperative CP.

Heterogeneity and Publication Bias

Substantial heterogeneity was observed for most global sagittal alignment parameters, including preoperative, postoperative, and change measures of C2-C7 lordosis and C2-C7 SVA (I2 >50%). By contrast, heterogeneity was low for localized parameters such as ΔC4-C5 segmental lordosis (I2 = 11.7%) and TS-CL mismatch (I2 = 0%).

Funnel plot analysis suggested possible publication bias for postoperative C2-C7 lordosis and change in C2-C7 lordosis; however, trim-and-fill correction did not alter the statistical significance of pooled estimates. No meaningful publication bias was detected for SVA-related parameters, C4-C5 segmental lordosis, the Ishihara index, or TS−CL mismatch.

DISCUSSION

Key Results

This systematic review and meta-analysis demonstrates that postoperative CP in patients undergoing surgery for DCM is primarily associated with the magnitude of postoperative sagittal alignment change rather than with static preoperative or postoperative alignment parameters. Patients who developed CP exhibited significantly greater increases in C2-C7 lordosis, C2-C7 SVA, C4-C5 segmental lordosis, and the Ishihara index, whereas baseline T1 slope and TS−CL mismatch were not predictive. These findings support a dynamic, iatrogenic mechanism driven by postoperative spinal cord and nerve root biomechanics rather than by baseline sagittal morphology.

Dynamic Alignment Changes as the Dominant Risk Factor

Across pooled analyses, static preoperative alignment measurements did not show consistent associations with postoperative CP, consistent with prior large retrospective series reporting limited predictive value of baseline lordosis, SVA, or T1 slope alone.16 By contrast, the magnitude of sagittal correction (particularly ΔC2-C7 lordosis and ΔC2-C7 SVA) demonstrated large and statistically robust effect sizes, emphasizing that the extent of correction, rather than absolute alignment, is the key determinant of risk.

The association between ΔC2-C7 lordosis and CP was significant in anterior and laminectomy subgroups but not in laminoplasty. This suggests that the biomechanical consequences of lordotic correction depend on the degree of spinal cord freedom to migrate posteriorly. In anterior surgery and laminectomy, decompression permits posterior cord displacement or elongation, whereas laminoplasty may limit posterior drift, thereby attenuating nerve root tension despite angular correction.

Mechanistic Interpretation: Nerve Root Tethering (“Danger Zone”)

These findings strongly support the nerve root tethering mechanism as a central explanation for CP. Anatomically, the C5 root is particularly vulnerable because its ventral rootlets are shorter, exit at a more obtuse angle, and are relatively fixed within the transverse foramen.56-59 This configuration creates a vulnerable segment between posterior cord attachment and anterior foraminal fixation (the so-called “danger zone”) that is susceptible to stretch injury.

Increased cervical lordosis promotes posterior spinal cord migration, shifting the posterior fixation point dorsally and increasing tension within this region. Consistently, patients with CP showed larger increases in both global lordosis and C4-C5 segmental lordosis, the level most directly related to the C5 root. The low heterogeneity observed for ΔC4-C5 lordosis further supports the biomechanical relevance of localized segmental correction beyond global curvature.

Role of SVA and Spinal Cord Elongation

Beyond lordosis, ΔC2-C7 SVA emerged as a strong predictor of CP, with effect sizes exceeding those of lordotic change. SVA reflects global sagittal translation and may better capture spinal cord elongation rather than angular correction alone. Increased SVA may induce cephalad spinal cord elongation, shifting the posterior fixation point superiorly while the anterior fixation point remains constrained, thereby increasing root tension.

In addition, partial lordotic correction without adequate reduction of translational imbalance may generate opposing force vectors (posterior cord displacement combined with anterior vertebral translation) maximizing stretch across the danger zone. This dual-vector mechanism may explain the stronger association observed for SVA compared with lordosis.

Ishihara Index and Cumulative Root Stretch

The Ishihara index demonstrated one of the largest effect sizes, supporting its role as an indirect marker of cumulative intracanalicular spinal cord length. Because it reflects the summed posterior displacement of the cord relative to vertebral bodies across levels, it may approximate the total stretch affecting the C5 root. The strong association between ΔIshihara index and CP further reinforces a stretch-mediated mechanism rather than isolated segmental compression or ischemia.

Foraminal Factors and Interaction With Sagittal Correction

Foraminal narrowing may further contribute to CP, particularly in the setting of increased lordosis.46 As lordosis increases, posterior foraminal narrowing may elevate friction and resistance at the anterior fixation point of the root, amplifying stretch forces during cord migration. The association between ΔC4-C5 lordosis and CP provides a mechanistic rationale for considering prophylactic foraminotomy in selected high-risk cases.60

Approach-Related Considerations

The differential findings across surgical approaches carry important clinical implications. In anterior procedures, disk height restoration and lordotic correction may lengthen the spinal cord while limiting posterior drift, potentially explaining the lower incidence of CP compared with posterior approaches.61 By contrast, posterior laminectomy allows unrestricted posterior cord migration combined with angular correction, substantially increasing root tension. Laminoplasty appears relatively protective, likely due to constrained cord movement despite alignment changes. These observations suggest that sagittal realignment strategies should prioritize balanced correction rather than aggressive lordotic restoration alone. Excessive lordotic correction without adequate control of translational imbalance (SVA) may elevate CP risk. When substantial correction is planned—particularly in posterior approaches—prophylactic C4-C5 foraminotomy and careful modulation of alignment targets may be considered. TS−CL mismatch may still provide guidance for global balance, although limited reporting precluded robust analysis.

Generalizability

The findings of this meta-analysis are generalizable to the broad population of patients undergoing surgery for DCM. By pooling data from 37 global studies and nearly 5000 patients, the results capture a wide variety of surgical practices, institutional protocols, and patient demographics. Furthermore, the inclusion of multiple standard interventions, including anterior cervical diskectomy and fusion, posterior decompression and fusion, and laminoplasty, enhances the clinical applicability of these results. It is important to note, however, that the generalizability of the alignment-CP relationship is procedure-dependent; it applies strongly to anterior procedures and posterior laminectomy with or without fusion, where structural realignment is common, but less so to laminoplasty, which typically preserves the existing alignment and shows no significant association in our subgroups.

Limitations

Several limitations to this systematic review and meta-analysis should be noted. First, all included primary studies were retrospective in design, which inherently introduces risks of selection bias and residual confounding. Second, because this study relied on aggregate data from the published literature, we were unable to perform granular subgroup analyses based on specific preoperative diagnoses (eg, differentiating among pure myelopathy, radiculopathy, or myeloradiculopathy). Third, the use of aggregate data precluded the ability to perform multivariate analyses on a patient-by-patient basis. Consequently, we could not statistically control for direct decompression-related factors (such as direct surgical trauma, thermal injury, or ischemia-reperfusion) vs alignment-related factors. Although the magnitude of alignment change is a strong independent driver of CP, the multifactorial nature of this complication means these confounding surgical factors cannot be definitively ruled out. Fourth, substantial heterogeneity was observed in global sagittal parameters, likely due to variations in surgical techniques, patient populations, and radiographic measurement methods across the included studies. Fifth, parameters such as T1 slope and TS−CL mismatch were underreported in the literature, limiting the strength of our conclusions regarding their specific role in preoperative alignment planning. Finally, although potential publication bias was suggested for certain parameters, our sensitivity analyses confirmed that this did not alter the direction or statistical significance of the pooled results.

CONCLUSIONS

Postoperative CP after surgery for DCM is primarily associated with the magnitude of sagittal alignment change rather than baseline alignment parameters. Greater postoperative increases in C2-C7 lordosis, C2-C7 SVA, C4-C5 segmental lordosis, and the Ishihara index were consistently observed in affected patients, supporting a stretch-mediated mechanism involving spinal cord and nerve root biomechanics. These findings emphasize the importance of balanced, controlled sagittal correction during surgical planning to minimize the risk of CP.

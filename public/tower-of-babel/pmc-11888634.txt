Effect of Preheating on the Color Stability of Two Different Commercially Available Nanohybrid Dental Composites: An In Vitro Study
Authors: Priya Matani, Bhargav Sadawarte, Indrajeet Deshpande, Priyadarshani Sarkate, Sharvaree Deshpande, Suroopa Das
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIntroduction:Color stability is one of the important criteria for the selection of composite during its service. Preheating of composite is performed to improve its handling characteristics. The present study aimed to evaluate the effect of preheating in two nanohybrid composite resins.Material and Methods:Sixty disk samples were prepared and divided into 2 main groups according to the temperature of preparation (n = 30). Both the groups were subdivided into Group 1A - Kulzer Charisma Diamond (N = 15) and Group 1B - Tetric N-Ceram (N = 15). Al: the sample discs were further immersed in testing solutions of tea, coffee, and distilled water (N = 5) for each group.Results:We observed significantly lower color change in the preheated group as compared to room temperature and also comparable color change in both the types of composites used.Conclusion:It was thus concluded that preheating composites could be effective in maintaining color stability in nanohybrid composite resins.

INTRODUCTION

Unacceptable discoloration/staining is commonly encountered in tooth-colored restorations and is a common reason for their regular replacement. Hence, one of the reasons for long-term success of composite restorations is color stability. Color stability is one of the important criteria for selection of composite during its service.[1]

In many practices, preheating the resin composite before application has become a popular technique because it improves marginal adaptation and microleakage, potentially improving flowability and material extrusion. Before polymerization, increasing the temperature of the resin composite has been shown to increase surface hardness and maximize polymerization.[2] Despite some assumptions, there are few studies on the effects of preheating on color stability of composite resins. Some studies reported the positive effects of such a technique,[23] and one study did not find any effects.[4] Therefore, the aim of this study was to evaluate the effect of preheating of a nano hybrid composite resin on the color stability when immersed in tea and coffee solution.

MATERIAL AND METHOD

This in vitro study was conducted on two nano hybrid composite resin (Kulzer Charisma Diamond Composite, A2 shade) and (Tetric N-Ceram, Ivoclar Vivadent, Schaan Liechtenstein, A2 resin).

Sixty disk samples with a diameter of 5 mm and thickness of 2 mm were prepared using a plastic mold. Specimens were then divided into 2 main groups according to the temperature of preparation (n = 30).

Group 1 (room temperature) was prepared based on following instructions. The composite that was previously placed in refrigerator removed from it and placed at room temperature (25°C) for at least 10 minutes was placed inside a plastic mold. The samples were polymerized from both sides for 40 seconds using an LED light curing unit (Bluephase Style LED) at 1100 mW/cm2. The room temperature was maintained at 25°C using a cooling equipment and regulated by a mercury thermometer.

To prepare group 2 (preheating), composite was placed in convection micro-oven at a temperature of 53 to 680°C and the composite was immediately inserted in the plastic mold and then it was cured from both sides as that of group 1. To decrease heat distribution, the time between the removing of the composite resin from the oven and placing it in the mold was 10 seconds.

Both the groups were subdivided into Group 1A - Kulzer Charisma Diamond (N = 15) and Group 1B - Tetric N-Ceram (N = 15).

All samples were demolded and placed at 37°C in distilled water for 24 h until the polymerization was completed. The upper surface of the composites was polished for 20 s using aluminum oxide disks. Each disk was used for one sample. The color assessment of the samples was performed using a VITA Easy shade spectrophotometer. The samples were previously dried with moisture absorbent paper and were placed against a flat white background, and then, their colors were measured (t0).

The samples of each group were again randomly subdivided into 3 groups (n = 5). Samples of subgroup 1, 2, and 3 were, respectively, immersed in tea, coffee, and distilled water.

To prepare the coloring solutions of tea solution, two tea bags are put in 300 mL of boiled water for 5 minutes, 3.6 g of coffee powder is dissolved in 300 mL of boiling water, and it was boiling for 10 minutes. After that, the samples are immersed in the solutions for 15 days. The coloring solutions were daily changed, and samples were washed and brushed for 1 minute to remove any debris. The mean time for drinking of a cup of tea/coffee and the amount of drink have been reported about 15 minutes and 2–3 cups per day, respectively.[5]

Therefore, an exposure time of 15 days seems to simulate approximately 30 months of tea/coffee consumption. At the end of 15 days period. All samples were placed against a flat white background, and their color was measured (t1).

DISCUSSION

Preheating of the composites reduces absorption and penetration of the colorant solution via increase of polymerization. The preheating of composite resin increases its degree of polymerization and can cause higher resistance to discoloration. In our study, preheating of composite resin caused higher resistance to discoloration of them in coffee than tea solution. Tea solutions have yellow pigments with different polarity.[5]

The color change of composite resin in the tea/coffee solution may be because of adsorption of polar colorants onto the surface of composite resin material, which causes more discoloration than coffee. In this study, we observed color change of the composite resin in the distilled water (controlled group). However, it was not statistically significant. It seems water sorption itself and departure of soluble material can be the cause of this discoloration.[35]

Micali and Basting studied the effectiveness of composite resin polymerization using different light-curing units, and they concluded that residual monomers in the polymeric chain can form colorimetric degradation products. Nonconverted double carbon link in composite resin can lead to microcracks and making composite resin more susceptible to discoloration. Thus, microleakage accelerates penetration of extrinsic staining into the composite restoration.[6]

CONCLUSION

Within the limitations of the study, the findings of the present study showed that preheated composite resin is effective in the reduction of its color change. In the preheating group, the highest color change was observed in tea followed by coffee and least in water. In the precooling group, the highest color change was observed in tea followed by coffee and least in water. In addition, it was observed that both the composites showed comparable color reduction.

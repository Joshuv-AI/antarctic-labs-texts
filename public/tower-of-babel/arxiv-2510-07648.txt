Continual Learning for Adaptive AI Systems
Md Hasibul Amin, Tamzid Tanvi Alam

Abstract
Continual learning—the ability of a neural network to learn multiple sequential tasks without catastrophic forgetting—remains a central challenge in developing adaptive artificial intelligence systems. While deep learning models achieve state-of-the-art performance across domains, they remain limited by overfitting and forgetting. This paper introduces Cluster-Aware Replay (CAR) , a hybrid continual learning framework that integrates a small, class-balanced replay buffer with a regularization term based on Inter-Cluster Fitness (ICF) in the feature space. The ICF loss penalizes overlapping feature representations between new and previously learned tasks, encouraging geometric separation in the latent space and reducing interference. Using the standard five-task Split CIFAR-10 benchmark with a ResNet-18 backbone, initial experiments demonstrate that CAR better preserves earlier task performance compared to fine-tuning alone. These findings are preliminary but highlight feature-space regularization as a promising direction for mitigating catastrophic forgetting.



I
Introduction

Inspired by the biological networks of the human brain, neural networks have evolved from the simple thresholding units of McCulloch and Pitts
[
1
]
and the single-layered perceptrons of Rosenblatt
[
2
]
into complex, multi-layered architectures capable of solving highly non-linear problems. The development of learning rules such as the Least Mean Square (Delta Rule)
[
3
]
and Gradient Descent, coupled with the pivotal backpropagation algorithm introduced by Rumelhart
et al.

[
4
]
, provided a mechanism for these networks to learn by adjusting internal parameters.

Despite their success, a fundamental limitation persists that starkly contrasts with biological intelligence: the inability to learn sequentially without forgetting. When a standard neural network is trained on a new task, optimization fine-tunes weights to the new data distribution, often overwriting parameters encoding prior knowledge and causing rapid degradation on earlier tasks—known as
catastrophic forgetting

[
10
,
11
,
25
]
.

Formally, given a sequence of tasks
T=(T_{1},\dots,T_{N})
, we seek a model that learns tasks sequentially such that after learning
T_{N}
, performance remains strong on all previous tasks
T_{i}
where
i<N
. We propose a hybrid approach that actively manages the geometry of the model’s internal feature space, combining memory replay with a novel geometric constraint.

Our method emphasizes the geometric structure of learned representations rather than exclusively focusing on parameter regularization. This direction—controlling feature-space separation between tasks—has received comparatively limited attention
[
31
,
32
]
. The results presented are preliminary but highlight the potential of feature-space–aware regularization for continual learning.

II
Literature Review

Catastrophic forgetting has challenged neural networks for decades. Robins
[
25
]
provided early evidence of forgetting in gradient-based systems. Subsequent work categorizes mitigation strategies into several families
[
21
,
29
]
.

II-A

Regularization-based Approaches

These methods penalize changes to parameters deemed important for prior tasks.
Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC)
estimates weight importance via the Fisher Information and constrains updates accordingly
[
20
]
.
Synaptic Intelligence (SI)
measures online contribution of parameters to loss changes
[
30
]
.
Knowledge Distillation
preserves prior functions by matching logits to a snapshot model
[
14
]
. While memory-efficient, such constraints can limit plasticity under large shifts.

II-B

Rehearsal (Replay-based) Methods

Replay stores a small exemplar memory and interleaves it during training. Selective replay improves exemplar choice
[
16
]
, and iCaRL maintains class exemplars for incremental learning
[
24
]
. Gradient Episodic Memory projects gradients to avoid increasing loss on past samples
[
22
]
. Replay is effective but raises memory and privacy concerns.

II-C

Generative Replay

Generative models such as GANs/VAEs synthesize pseudo-samples for prior tasks, reducing explicit storage needs
[
27
,
28
]
. However, stable generative training adds complexity.

II-D

Parameter Isolation and Dynamic Architectures

Instead of sharing parameters, isolation assigns disjoint capacity per task. Progressive Networks expand with frozen columns
[
26
]
, guaranteeing no forgetting but increasing size. PackNet prunes/allocates weights iteratively
[
23
]
, trading efficiency for isolation.

II-E

Geometry-aware Approaches

Recent work emphasizes structure in feature space. Liu
et al.

[
31
]
use centroid-distance distillation for rehearsal; Gu
et al.

[
32
]
preserve linear separability via backward feature projection. Our approach differs by explicitly maximizing inter-cluster separation across tasks via a fitness-like loss.

III
Proposed Methodology

We introduce
Cluster-Aware Replay (CAR)
, integrating a small, class-balanced replay buffer with a feature-space regularization mechanism. Unlike methods that primarily constrain parameters or logits, CAR explicitly shapes the internal feature space through an
Inter-Cluster Fitness (ICF)
loss to promote clearer inter-task separation.

III-A

Architectural Backbone

We adopt ResNet-18. The network comprises a feature extractor
f_{\theta}(\cdot)
up to the global average pooling layer and a classifier
c_{\phi}(\cdot)
as the final fully connected layer. For input
x
, embeddings are
z=f_{\theta}(x)
and logits
y=c_{\phi}(z)
.

III-B

Inter-Cluster Fitness Function (
L_{\mathrm{ICF}}
)

After training on task
T_{k}
, we compute a centroid
\mu_{c}
for each class
c
:

\mu_{c}=\frac{1}{|D_{c}|}\sum_{x_{i}\in D_{c}}\frac{f_{\theta}(x_{i})}{\|f_{\theta}(x_{i})\|_{2}}.

(1)

During training on
T_{k+1}
, for each sample
x_{j}
we encourage separation from all previously learned class centroids by maximizing the Euclidean distance in normalized feature space:

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ICF}}=-\sum_{c\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{prev}}}\left\|\frac{f_{\theta}(x_{j})}{\|f_{\theta}(x_{j})\|_{2}}-\mu_{c}\right\|_{2},

(2)

where
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{prev}}
denotes classes from earlier tasks.

III-C

Overall Loss

The training objective combines cross-entropy with ICF:

L_{\mathrm{total}}=L_{\mathrm{CE}}+\lambda\cdot L_{\mathrm{ICF}},

(3)

with
L_{\mathrm{CE}}
computed on current-task samples and replay exemplars. The hyperparameter
\lambda
balances plasticity (new learning) and stability (retention).

Conclusion

We presented
Cluster-Aware Replay (CAR)
, combining a minimal replay buffer with an
Inter-Cluster Fitness (ICF)
loss to increase feature-space separation across tasks. On Split CIFAR-10, CAR reduces early forgetting relative to fine-tuning, though degradation emerges as tasks accumulate, suggesting the need for adaptive regularization. Future work includes systematic hyperparameter tuning, distance-aware objectives, and scaling to CIFAR-100. Results are preliminary and intended to motivate further exploration of feature-space regularization in continual learning.

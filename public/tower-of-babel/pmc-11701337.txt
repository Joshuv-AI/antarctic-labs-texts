Effect analysis of unilateral deaf adults wearing bone-conduction hearing aids: A case report☆
Authors: Chenchen Xiang, Yi Wang, Xiaozhu Cao, Weiman Xv, Jing Liu, Nan Li, Lumeng Han, QIan Wang, Fei Ji
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The prevalence of unilateral deafness (SSD) or asymmetric hearing loss (AHL) among patients with hearing impairments ranges from 7.2% to 15.0%, indicating a relatively significant proportion. However, these individuals often overlook their hearing loss, resulting in delayed or inadequate treatment. This oversight can lead to a lack of binaural summation and squelch effect, as well as the head shadow effect, which can significantly impact their speech recognition and sound localization abilities, especially in noisy environments.Recently, a groundbreaking Sound Bite™ Pinyin® Bone Conduction Hearing Aid (HA) device has been has been introduced as a viable alternative to traditional percutaneous stimulation hearing assistance devices. This innovative device harnesses bone conduction technology to convey sound vibrations directly to the inner ear via the bones of the jaw and skull, effectively bypassing the air conduction pathway that is commonly compromised in individuals with hearing loss.This report details the evaluation and adjustment process of a HA device worn by a 31-year-old female patient who suffers from a congenital ossicular chain deformity in her right ear, while maintaining normal hearing in her left ear.The report comprehensively covers the hearing thresholds of the 31-year-old female patient on the day of fitting the hearing aid and one month later. It also presents assessments of the hearing aid's performance through the Abbreviated Profile of Hearing Aid Benefit (APHAB) questionnaire, conducted three days after adjustment, one month later, and one year later.Furthermore, the report details the evaluation of the patient's sound localization ability, comparing her performance before and after wearing the hearing aid device. Additionally, it includes measurements of her speech recognition ability for monosyllabic words and Yang Yang Ge words, which are specific tests in the Chinese language, conducted two years after the initial fitting of the hearing aid.The discovery that bone-anchored hearing devices can significantly enhance hearing thresholds in patients with unilateral conductive hearing loss represents a significant milestone. These devices not only improve speech recognition ability but also enhance sound localization in noisy environments. This improvement is accompanied by a high level of subjective satisfaction among patients, indicating a positive impact on their overall quality of life.

Introduction

Among hearing-impaired patients, the prevalence of single-sided deafness (SSD) or asymmetric hearing loss (AHL) is relatively high (7.2%–15.0%).These patients often do not pay enough attention to their hearing loss, which may lead to delayed or insufficient treatment(Vila and Lieu, 2015; Yu Jeffrey, 2020), (Vila and Lieu, 2015; Yu Jeffrey, 2020).These patients often exhibit a lack of binaural summation effect and squelch effect, as well as the presence of head shadow effect. Additionally, their speech recognition ability and sound localization skills in complex noisy environments are significantly impaired, leading to varying degrees of disturbance in their daily lives, work, and other aspects (Xin, 2018).

Currently, the main clinical interventions include contralateral routing of signal(CROS), implantable bone-conduction devices (BCDs), and cochlear implants (CIs).Recently, a novel Sound Bite™ Pinyin® Bone Conduction Hearing Aid (HA) device has been developed as an alternative to other percutaneous stimulation hearing assistance devices (see Fig. 1-A, B).The HA requires the patient to wear a hearing aid similar to a removable denture. This device is equipped with a micro-vibrator that directly transmits signals to the inner ear, enabling the perception of sound(Quan et al., 2021).

The hearing device utilizes a behind-the-ear (BTE) transmitter that is connected to a microphone placed in the ear canal of the hearing-impaired ear. The microphone captures sound waves and converts them into electrical signals, which are then transmitted to the BTE transmitter.The BTE module sends a signal to a customized intraoral hearing sensor (ITM), which connects the buccal surface of the maxillary molar tooth to a piezoelectric bone stimulator capable of conducting sound. This allows the user to hear sounds through bone conduction, bypassing the air conduction pathway that may be affected by hearing loss. The device is designed to fit comfortably over the teeth and provide clear, natural sound quality, improving the hearing experience for those with hearing loss(Ross et al., 2011).

This report aims to report on the effectiveness and satisfaction of a case of adult unilateral mixed hearing loss (otic bone chain deformity) after the application of the Sound Bite™ Pinyin® Bone Conduction Hearing Aid.

Case report:

Patient information

A 31-year-old female patient with ossicular chain deformity from childhood narrow external auditory canal, and middle ear deformity, while her left ear has normal hearing.The patient has a high demand for communication with others due to her work, but she has never worn a hearing aid before. When using the bone-conduction headphone Shaoyin, she experiences discomfort with high frequencies, feeling that they are too noisy. She now hopes to solve the problems of “inability to hear clearly in noisy environments," “feeling tired when listening in crowded environments," and “inability to distinguish directions."

After understanding the patient's needs, it was decided to try the Sound Bite™ Pinyin® Bone Conduction Hearing Aid for a hearing test.

Hearing assessment

Before patients wear the hearing aid (HA), they undergo a pure tone audiometry test with their unaided ears. Subsequently, on the day of the HA adjustment, one month later, and two years later, they will undergo hearing threshold tests with the hearing aid in place. All of these tests are conducted in a soundproof room that maintains a background noise level below 30 dB(A) to ensure accurate measurements.

To measure the hearing threshold of the hearing aids, ear pressure earphones (TDH-39) are utilized. The earphone is securely positioned above the microphone of the external component of the bone conduction hearing aid. Four frequencies (0.5k, 1k, 2k, and 4 kHz) are tested, and the average hearing threshold is then calculated. This comprehensive approach ensures accurate and reliable hearing threshold assessments throughout the patient's HA usage period.

Speech audiometry

Our research utilized a Chinese speech listening platform specifically developed by the PLA General Hospital. We conducted speech audiometry prior to fitting the hearing aid and again two years later. For the speech recognition test in noisy conditions, we employed a background noise consisting of four individuals speaking simultaneously. The audio cable of the test computer was securely connected to the audio socket of the GSI-61 audiometer within a standard sound-insulated room.

During the test, monosyllabic words (including consonants, vowels, and tones) and spondee were evaluated using ear pressure earphones. For the left ear, a masking white noise of 20 dB HL was applied, while the speech signal for the right ear was set at 60 dB HL. Additionally, a monosyllabic and syllable test was conducted without masking, which was repeated two years after the fitting.

For the sentence recognition test under noisy conditions, we set the signal-to-noise ratio at 0 dB and −5 dB. Loudspeakers emitting a sound intensity of 60 dB HL were positioned 1 m away from the tester at 0° and 90° angles. When testing a single ear, the left ear was blocked with an ear mould. During the noise test, the noise signal was directed straight towards the patient, while the speech signal was presented on the patient's right side at a 90° angle.

This comprehensive approach allowed us to accurately assess the patients' hearing performance both with and without the hearing aid, under various conditions. (Xuke and Zhaoli, 2020).

Evaluation questionnaire

The Abbreviated Profile of Hearing Aid Benefit (APHAB) was formulated by Cox in 1995, drawing inspiration from the existing Profile of Hearing Aid Benefit (PHAB). This comprehensive questionnaire encompasses 24 questions distributed across four distinct categories, with six questions dedicated to each category.These four types of problems are ease of communication, EC-understand the difficulty of the patient communicating in an ideal listening environment; background noise, BN-understand the difficulty of communication in a high-intensity noise environment; reverberation, RV-understand the difficulty of the patient communicating in a reverberating environment; aversiveness, AV-understand the patient's aversion to environmental sound. For the purposes of this study, we have utilized the Mandarin version of APHAB, featuring 24 questions arranged randomly to eliminate bias. Each question offers seven potential answers, representing a graduated scale of frequency for the situations described: ranging from “1-never" (indicating a 1% frequency) to “7-always" (indicating a 99% frequency). This diverse range of answers allows for a nuanced and precise understanding of the patient's hearing aid experience, enabling more informed assessments and tailored treatment plans(Srinivasan et al., 2023).

For each question in the Abbreviated Profile of Hearing Aid Benefit (APHAB), respondents are prompted to provide answers for two distinct scenarios: “unaided" (without the hearing aid) and “aided" (with the hearing aid). The key objective is to determine the difference between these two scenarios, resulting in a hearing aid effect score. By analyzing the APHAB results, we comprehensively assess the challenges faced by patients in communication, noise tolerance, aversion to specific sounds, and the overall impact of the hearing aid.

Prior to and following the use of the hearing aid (HA), patients are administered satisfaction questionnaires. These questionnaires aim to gauge the patients' level of satisfaction with the hearing aid device. Additionally, the APHAB questionnaire is administered at three distinct time points: 3 days, 1 month, and one year after the fitting of the hearing aid. This approach allows for a longitudinal assessment of the hearing aid's effectiveness and the patients' evolving satisfaction levels over time(Zhang and Ma, 2018).

Sound source location test

The sound source location test was conducted in a standardized sound insulation chamber both before and two years following the fitting process. The test setup comprised 12 loudspeakers arranged in a semicircular formation, spaced at 15° intervals. The subject was instructed to sit in the center, facing the speakers and maintaining a distance of 1.2 m. During the test, the speakers randomly played the test sound, with only one speaker activated at a time. Immediately following each sound emission, the subject was prompted to identify and select the serial number of the loudspeaker from which they heard the sound.

Upon completion of the test, the root-mean-square error (RMSE) was calculated, comparing the serial numbers selected by the subject with the actual speaker that emitted the sound. This metric allowed for a quantitative assessment of the subject's ability to localize sound sources accurately. (Xuelei et al., 2022).

Result of APHAB (Mandarin)

Based on the patient's responses to the Mandarin APHAB scale (detailed in Appendix 2), it was observed that, following a three-day fitting period, the AV score improved by 6 points. However, scores for other categories declined by 1–3 points. After one month of use, the EC score rose by 1 point, while the BN score dipped by 2 points. Discussion

This study demonstrates that patients with childhood-onset unilateral deafness experience significant improvements in the hearing threshold of the affected side after regularly using the hearing aid (HA) for one month, and even more so after two years. Notably, there is a marked enhancement in the patient's monosyllabic and disyllabic speech recognition rates following hearing assistance. Furthermore, the score on BN questions in the APHAB questionnaire decreased significantly, indicating a substantial improvement in the patient's communication skills and hearing abilities in noisy environments. This finding is further corroborated by the results of sentence recognition rates under noisy conditions. Based on patient feedback, the HA device effectively addressed the primary complaint, resulting in a positive review. Therefore, it enables better utilization of the advantages of binaural listening while reducing sound reverberation (Moore and Popelka, 2013; Popelka, 2010).According to the test results for sound source localization, the patient demonstrated a strong ability to localize sound sources prior to fitting the device, achieving an average RMS result of 39.09° without the HA. However, upon wearing the bone-guided hearing aid, only a modest improvement of approximately 4.05° was observed. Given that this study only reports on a single case, further investigation is necessary to determine whether the limited improvement may be attributed to individual differences or the small sample size.Bone conduction operates by converting sound into mechanical vibrations of varying frequencies, which are then transmitted through the skull, bone labyrinth, inner ear lymph, spiral ganglion, and ultimately to the auditory center. Tooth bone conduction represents a cutting-edge method that bridges the gap in unilateral conductive HA devices. Unlike traditional bone oscillators, this innovative approach utilizes the teeth to facilitate sound bone conduction and energy transmission. As a result, the signal can be detected in the unaffected cochlea of the impaired ear, enabling sound amplification and the achievement of binaural hearing. (Haiming et al., 2020).

Currently, the sole clinical non-invasive intervention available for hearing enhancement is the CROS-HA. However, some patients report experiencing discomfort upon wearing it. Additionally, due to the concurrent amplification of background noise, patients often find the hearing aid's effectiveness less than satisfactory. Furthermore, when in proximity to electrical equipment, CROS-HA becomes vulnerable to electromagnetic interference, compromising sound quality.On the other hand, Bone-Conduction Devices (BCDs) frequently carry the risk of postoperative complications that cannot be overlooked. These primarily include inadequate bone fusion or detachment, implant migration or loss, local skin infection or necrosis, and incision site infections.When compared to percutaneous stimulation methods, HA demonstrate a higher sound transmission efficiency than other non-implanted bone conduction devices that adhere to or clamp onto the skull. The direct coupling of bone conduction sound energy to the bone enables patients to achieve a higher hearing threshold, effectively enhancing their ability to distinguish noise and promoting binaural integration. (Wang et al., 2021a, 2021b). After CI surgery, patients need to undergo long-term adjustment and rehabilitation training to achieve the best results. Patients may also develop complications due to the risks associated with surgery(Yingli et al., 2017). Compared with cochlear implants, patients can adapt to the bone conduction HA more quickly and spend less time on rehabilitation with an exemption from surgery(Meng and Shusheng, 2021).

In this study, the result of AV in the APHAB questionnaire increased by 10 points, which was manifested in patients who were more unbearable to noise. The generation of noise may be caused by acoustic feedback from the sound energy of the bone conduction sensor entering the microphone. The sound energy entering the microphone may come directly from the ITM, indirectly from the soft tissue that touches the ITM (the lateral part of the cheek or ear canal), or from adjacent hard tissue (the bony part of the ear canal). Since the microphone is not in the mouth, the amplification of the chewing sound does not increase (Gurgel et al., 2013, 2014). It's suggested to adjust the gain or reinstall the device to resolve the issue. At the same time, it is recommended to improve the noise reduction technology of the microphone. For example, sometimes patients complain that wind is affecting speech recognition. To solve this problem, wind noise reduction technology can be added to optimize the patient's listening experience.

For APHAB, comparing the score differences between when patients wear hearing aids and when they do not can help understand the fitting effect of HA. The evaluation criterion is that if the score difference in any single category (EC, RV, or BN) reaches 22, or if the overall score difference across all three categories (EC, RV, and BN) reaches 5, it indicates that the hearing aid fitting is effective and the patient benefits from wearing the HA.It is worth noting that when using the APHAB scale, patients should answer at least 4 questions in each category for the scoring to be meaningful. Using the overall scores of three categories (EC, RV, and BN) for comparison, the differences between patients wearing them for three days and one year are 27 and 5, both greater than or equal to 5, while the difference after one month of wearing is 4. It indicates that after one year of wearing, the hearing aids are effectively fitted and patients can benefit from them. However, the patients who wore them for three days and one month did not complete all the questionnaire items, so the scoring is meaningless and the data is not suitable for analyzing the effectiveness of the hearing aids. There is some degree of error.Whether the error is caused by individual differences and the small sample size remains to be further explored. This study only included data from one patient, and further research with a larger sample size is needed in the future.

Conclusion

Wearing the HA can markedly enhance the hearing threshold of the affected side for patients experiencing unilateral conductive hearing loss. It not only boosts their capability to recognize speech in both serene and noisy environments but also reinforces the advantages of binaural hearing. Notably, there has been a substantial improvement in sound quality scores. Additionally, patients have expressed a high degree of subjective satisfaction, and their ability to localize sound sources has been significantly enhanced. Nevertheless, further studies with a larger sample size are warranted to delve deeper into this topic.

Spectrum of the perturbed Landau-Dirac operator
Vincent Bruneau and Pablo Miranda

Abstract
. In this article, we consider the Dirac operator with constant magnetic field in ℝ 2 {\mathbb{R}}^{2} . Its spectrum consists of eigenvalues of infinite multiplicities, known as the Landau-Dirac levels. Under compactly supported perturbations of the electric and magnetic potentials, we study the distribution of the discrete eigenvalues near each Landau-Dirac level. Similarly to the Landau (Schrödinger) operator, we demonstrate that a three-term asymptotic formula holds for the eigenvalue counting function. One of the main novelties of this work is the treatment of some perturbations of variable sign. In this context we explore some remarkable phenomena related to the finiteness or infiniteness of the discrete eigenvalues, which depend on the interplay of the different terms in the matrix perturbation.

1.
The
2D
Magnetic Landau-Dirac operators

We consider in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{2}
the Dirac operator with the homogeneous magnetic field
B=(0,0,b)
pointing in the direction perpendicular to the plane, where
b>0
is the amplitude. More specifically, let
\sigma:=(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})
,
\sigma_{3}
be the Pauli matrices,

A:=(A_{1},A_{2})=b(-\frac{x_{2}}{2},\frac{x_{1}}{2})
a magnetic potential associated with the constant magnetic field (curl
A=b
) and
m\geq 0
the mass.
Then, the
Landau-Dirac
operator
D_{0}
is the closure on
C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{2}
of

\sigma\cdot(-i\nabla-A)+m\sigma_{3}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}m&a^{*}\\
a&-m\end{array}\right),

where

(1.1)

a:=(-i\partial_{x_{1}}-A_{1})+i(-i\partial_{x_{2}}-A_{2}),\qquad a^{*}:=(-i\partial_{x_{1}}-A_{1})-i(-i\partial_{x_{2}}-A_{2}).

It is well known (see for instance
[
28
, Theorem 7.2]
) that the spectrum of
D_{0}
is made up of eigenvalues of infinite multiplicities, the so-called
Landau-Dirac
Levels

\mu_{q}:=\begin{cases}\sqrt{2bq+m^{2}},&q\in\{0,1,2,...\}\\[5.0pt]
-\sqrt{2b|q|+m^{2}},&q\in\{-1,-2,...\}.\end{cases}

Thus the spectrum of
D_{0}
is essential, given by

\sigma(D_{0})=\sigma_{\text{ess}}(D_{0})=\{\mu_{q},\;q\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
and we have

(1.2)

(D_{0}-z)^{-1}=\sum_{q\in{\mathbb{Z}}}(\mu_{q}-z)^{-1}{\mathcal{P}}_{q},

where, for
q\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,

{\mathcal{P}}_{q}
is the infinite dimensional orthogonal projection onto Ker
(D_{0}-\mu_{q})
. These properties follow from analogous ones for the Landau-Schrödinger operator
H_{L}:=(-i\nabla-A)^{2}
whose spectrum is given by the
Landau levels

\Lambda_{n}=b(2n+1)
,
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}:=\{0,1,2,...\}
, by using supersymmetry properties (see e.g.
[
28
, Theorem 5.13]
).

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a Hermitian matrix-valued perturbation, that is for
V_{1},V_{2}
, and
W
in
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
,

{{\mathcal{V}}}
is the operator of multiplication defined by

(1.3)

{\mathcal{V}}:=\begin{pmatrix}V_{1}&W^{*}\\
W&V_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\frac{V_{1}+V_{2}}{2}I_{2}+\sigma_{3}\frac{V_{1}-V_{2}}{2}-\sigma_{1}\widetilde{A}_{1}-\sigma_{2}\widetilde{A}_{2},

where
W=-\widetilde{A}_{1}-i\widetilde{A}_{2}
.
Such matrix allows to consider scalar electric perturbations (with
V_{1}=V_{2}
,
W=0
), Lorentz potentials (with
V_{1}=-V_{2}
,
W=0
) and perturbations by magnetic fields
\widetilde{b}=\partial_{x_{1}}\widetilde{A}_{2}-\partial_{x_{2}}\widetilde{A}_{1}
associated with the magnetic potential
(\widetilde{A}_{1},\widetilde{A}_{2})

(with
V_{1}=V_{2}=0
).

Now, for
V_{1},V_{2}
, and
W
tending to
0
at infinity,
{{\mathcal{V}}}
is a relatively compact perturbation of
D_{0}
. By Weyl’s theorem, the essential spectrum of the self-adjoint operator

D_{{\mathcal{V}}}:=D_{0}+{\mathcal{V}}
satisfies:

\sigma_{\text{ess}}(D_{{\mathcal{V}}})=\sigma_{\text{ess}}(D_{0})=\{\mu_{q},\;q\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}.

Thus, under the perturbation
{\mathcal{V}}
, discrete eigenvalues may appear, with the only possible limit points at the Landau-Dirac levels.

In this paper we will focus on compactly supported perturbations
{{\mathcal{V}}}
and for each fixed
q\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
0<\lambda
, we will consider the counting functions

(1.4)

{\mathcal{N}}_{+}^{q}(\lambda):={\rm Tr}E_{D_{0}+{{\mathcal{V}}}}(\mu_{q}+\lambda,\alpha),\quad{\mathcal{N}}_{-}^{q}(\lambda):={\rm Tr}E_{D_{0}+{{\mathcal{V}}}}(\alpha,\mu_{q}-\lambda),

where
E_{T}(\omega)
is the spectral projection of the self-adjoint operator
T
associated with the Borel set
\omega
, and
\alpha
is any fixed number in
(\mu_{q},\mu_{q+1})
and
(\mu_{q-1},\mu_{q})
, respectively. These functions count the number of eigenvalues of
D_{0}+{{\mathcal{V}}}
, including multiplicities, on the intervals
(\mu_{q}+\lambda,\alpha)
and
(\alpha,\mu_{q}-\lambda)
, respectively.

The asymptotic behavior of these functions was studied before in
[
18
]
in the more restrictive case of
{{\mathcal{V}}}
with definite sign and of form
{{\mathcal{V}}}=I_{2}v
, with
I_{2}
the identity
2\times 2
matrix. In
[
18
, Theorem 1.3]
it is proved that
the eigenvalue counting functions satisfy

{\mathcal{N}}_{\pm}^{q}(\lambda)=\frac{|\ln{\lambda}|}{\ln{|\ln{\lambda}|}}(1+o(1)),\quad\lambda\downarrow 0.

Analogous results for the Landau-Schrödinger operator
H_{L}+v
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, were obtained in
[
23
, Theorem 2.2]
and
[
18
, Theorem 1.2]
.
In these works it is shown that for
H_{L}+v
the asymptotic distribution of the eigenvalues near the Landau levels is governed by the Toeplitz operators
p_{n}vp_{n}
where
p_{n}
is the orthogonal projection onto
{\rm Ker}(H_{L}-\Lambda_{n})
, and by
{\mathcal{P}}_{q}V{\mathcal{P}}_{q}
for
D_{0}+{{\mathcal{V}}}
.

In
[
11
]
, a different approach allows to describe the rate of accumulation of the eigenvalues
of
p_{n}vp_{n}

in terms of the logarithmic capacity of
K
, the support of
v
. Then, this geometrical quantity appears in the third term of the asymptotic expansion of the eigenvalue counting function of
H_{L}+v
. Similarly, this result also holds for the Dirichlet, Neumann or Robin realization of
(-i\nabla-A)^{2}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus K
,
K
being a non-empty obstacle (i.e.
K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
is compact with a non-empty interior). The Dirichlet case is like
H_{L}+\chi_{K}
(see
[
21
]
) while the obstacle problem for Neumann or Robin boundary conditions behaves like
H_{L}-\chi_{K}
(see
[
19
,
12
]
).
Here and throughout the article,
\chi_{K}
denotes the characteristic function of the set
K
.
For the perturbation of
H_{L}
by a delta interaction supported on a curve
\Gamma\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, the logarithmic capacity of
\Gamma
also appears in the asymptotic distribution of the eigenvalues near the Landau levels (see
[
5
]
). In all mentioned works a fixed sign is assumed for the perturbation.

A natural question concerning the perturbation of the Landau-Dirac operator is the existence of analogous results on the
distribution of eigenvalues near
\mu_{q}
,
q\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Moreover, we would like also to be able to consider potentials of no-definite sign (in particular to take into account the Lorentz potential, certain magnetic perturbations as well as for the purpose of studying obstacle problems). Some results for non-definite sign perturbations of the Landau-Schrödinger operators are considered in
[
22
]
(for electric potentials of compact support) and in
[
25
]
(with smooth compactly supported magnetic fields under a sign condition on an effective potential). However, it is important to notice that in order to use ideas from these works in our context, the first difficulty to overcome is the non-semiboundedness of the Dirac operator.

In this article, we obtain a three-term asymptotic expansion of
{\mathcal{N}}_{\pm}^{q}(\lambda)
for compactly supported perturbations of
D_{0}
that are not of definite sign (see (
2.5
)). To achieve this, we first obtain some general results concerning the index of a pair of spectral projections for non-semibounded operators (see the Appendix). Using the concept of spectral flow, this index helps to generalize a version of the Birman-Schwinger principle to non-sign-definite perturbations
(see e.g.
[
20
]
). Then, it reduces the study of the counting functions (
1.4
) to the analysis of the distribution of the eigenvalues of some Toeplitz operators
{\mathcal{P}}_{q}{{\mathcal{V}}}^{\pm}_{\epsilon}{\mathcal{P}}_{q}
with
{{\mathcal{V}}}^{\pm}_{\epsilon}={{\mathcal{V}}}+O(\epsilon)
for
\epsilon>0
small enough (see Proposition
3.1
). The spectral study of these operators is based on complex analysis tools developed in
[
11
]
and exploits
[
22
]
.

The main asymptotic expansions obtained are analogous to those given for fixed sign perturbations of the Landau-Schrödinger operator. However, a surprising result is that when the support of
V_{1}
,
V_{2}
and
W
coincide, only
V_{1}
determines the side of the accumulation for the eigenvalues (see in particular Theorem
2.1
as well as Remarks
2.2
and
3.8
). We show that this predominance can be attenuated and compensated by
V_{2}
when the support of
V_{2}
is not contained in the support of
V_{1}

(see Theorem
2.5
). For smooth potentials, near each
\mu_{q}
,
q\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we give an effective Toeplitz operator
p_{0}v_{q}({{\mathcal{V}}})p_{0}
with
v_{q}({{\mathcal{V}}})
a function given by a linear combinations of
V_{1}
,
V_{2}
,
\widetilde{b}=\partial_{x_{1}}\widetilde{A}_{2}-\partial_{x_{2}}\widetilde{A}_{1}
and their derivatives until the order
2|q|
(see Proposition
3.10
).
In contrast to
[
25
]
, we have assumptions on the support of the magnetic potential, which is not very satisfactory because the physically relevant quantity is the magnetic field. However, our effective operator is expressed in terms of the electric potential and the magnetic field, and we can hope that the approach of
[
25
]
allows to reduce the assumptions in terms of the magnetic field (independently of the choice of gauge). Unlike our previous results, with this type of effective potential it is difficult to express in a simple manner the influence of the different components of
{{\mathcal{V}}}
over the asymptotics of
{\mathcal{N}}_{\pm}^{q}
. However, this allows us to establish another distinctive feature: it is possible to have very different patterns of behavior between two successive Landau-Dirac levels (see Corollary
2.7
).

2.
Results

As we already mentioned, the asymptotic distribution of discrete eigenvalues of
D_{0}+{{\mathcal{V}}}
is related to a particular geometric feature of the support of
{{\mathcal{V}}}
. In order to capture this properties we first need the following definitions taken from potential theory.

2.1.
Logarithmic capacity

Let
{\mathcal{E}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
be a Borel set, and
{\mathfrak{M}}({\mathcal{E}})
denote the set of compactly supported probability measures on
{\mathcal{E}}
. Then the
logarithmic capacity
of
{\mathcal{E}}
is defined as

{{\rm Cap}}({\mathcal{E}}):=e^{-{\mathcal{I}}({\mathcal{E}})}
where

{\mathcal{I}}({\mathcal{E}}):=\inf_{\mu\in{\mathfrak{M}}({\mathcal{E}})}\int_{{\mathcal{E}}\times{\mathcal{E}}}\ln{|x-y|^{-1}}d\mu(x)d\mu(y),

(if
{\mathcal{I}}({\mathcal{E}})=+\infty
, we set
{{\rm Cap}}({\mathcal{E}})=0
).
A systematic exposition of the theory of the logarithmic capacity can be found, for example, in
[
24
, Chapter 5]
and
[
15
, Chapter II, Section 4]
.
If we identify
{\mathcal{E}}
with a subset of
{\mathbb{C}}
(with
z=x_{1}+ix_{2}
for
(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathcal{E}}
), for bounded sets, the logarithmic capacity coincides with the
transfinite diameter

{\rm Cap}({\mathcal{E}})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\delta_{n}({\mathcal{E}})

where

\delta_{n}({\mathcal{E}}):=\max_{z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}\in{\mathcal{E}}}\Big(\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}|z_{i}-z_{j}|\Big)^{\frac{2}{n(n-1)}}.

It is also given by the
n
’th root asymptotic of the
n
’th
Chebyshev polynomial
for the set
{\mathcal{E}}
:

{\rm Cap}({\mathcal{E}})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Big(\sup_{z\in{\mathcal{E}}}|f_{n}(z)|\Big)^{\frac{1}{n}}

where
f_{n}
is the monic polynomial that minimizes
\sup_{z\in{\mathcal{E}}}|f(z)|
among all the monic polynomials of degree
n

(see for instance
[
27
,
26
]
).

2.2.
Weights for open sets

For an open set
\Omega
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
we will say that a measurable function
w:\Omega\to[0,\infty)
is a
weight
in
\Omega
if the weighted Sobolev space

H^{1}(\Omega;w):=\{f\in L^{2}(\Omega;w);\;\nabla f\in L^{2}(\Omega;w)\}

is a Hilbert space and the embedding

(2.1)

H^{1}(\Omega;w)\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega;w)

is compact (here
L^{2}(\Omega;w)
is the weighted
L^{2}
-space with the positive measure
wdx
). There exists a vast literature concerning weighted Sobolev spaces and conditions for the compactness of the embedding
(
2.1
) (see
[
14
,
2
,
13
]
and references therein). Of course any constant positive function
is a weight over
\Omega
if, for instance,
\Omega
has a Lipschitz boundary. Another simple example is the following: suppose that
\Omega
is a bounded domain with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
. Then a positive function of the form

w(x)=|{\rm dist}(x,\partial\Omega)|^{\alpha}

near
\partial\Omega
, is a weight for
\Omega
if
\alpha\geq 1
(see for instance
[
2
]
). It is also possible to consider the distance to a part of the boundary (as in
[
10
, Section 2.1]
). For the compactness of (
2.1
), much less restrictive conditions on the regulatity of
\Omega
and
w
appear in the aforementioned literature.

As we will see later, the concept of weight allows us to extend, to some extent, the framework of validity of the results in
[
11
]
, upon which part of our results are based. Specifically, this enables us to consider potentials that are not necessarily larger than a positive constant in a given region, but are instead greater than a weight
\omega
in the same region.

2.3.
Main results

For a function
v
with compact essential support
1
1

1

\operatorname{ess\,supp}(v):={\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus\bigcup\left\{{\mathcal{O}}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{2}:{\mathcal{O}}{\text{ is open and }}v=0,\,{\text{almost everywhere in }}{\mathcal{O}}\right\}
.
, denoted by
{\rm ess\,supp}\,v
,
define

(2.2)

{\mathfrak{C}}_{+}(v):=1+\ln{\left(\frac{b}{2}\,{\rm Cap}({\rm ess\,supp}\,v)^{2}\right)}.

For
v
non–negative, set

K_{-}(v):=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:\limsup_{r\downarrow 0}\frac{\log\int_{|z-\zeta|\leq r}v(\zeta)dm(\zeta)}{\log r}<\infty\}
where
m(\zeta)
denotes the Lebesgue measure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, and
define

(2.3)

{\mathfrak{C}}_{-}(v):=1+\ln{\left(\frac{b}{2}\,{\rm Cap}(K_{-}(v))^{2}\right)}.

Obviously since
K_{-}(v)\subset{\rm ess\,supp}\,v
, then
{\mathfrak{C}}_{-}(v)\leq{\mathfrak{C}}_{+}(v)
. Notice that the equality holds for example, when
v
satisfies
C\chi_{K}\geq v\geq c\chi_{K}
for some constants
C\geq c>0
and a compact set
K\subset{\mathbb{C}}
with non-empty interior.

First, we show that when
V_{1}>0
on a domain
\Omega
and its support contains that of
W
and
V_{2,-}:=\max(0,-V_{2})
, the distribution of the eigenvalues near each Landau-Dirac level is mainly governed by
V_{1}
(in particular, the side where the eigenvalues accumulate is determined only by the sign of
V_{1}
).

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
{{\mathcal{V}}}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2},{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
be a function given by (
1.3
) essentially supported in
{\mathcal{K}}
, a compact set with Lipschitz boundary.
Suppose that there exist
\Omega
an open bounded set in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
,
w
a weight in
\Omega
and constants
C_{2},C_{3}\geq 0
such that:

(2.4)

V_{1}\geq\chi_{\Omega}w;\quad V_{2}\geq-C_{2}\chi_{\Omega}w;\quad|W|\leq C_{3}\chi_{\Omega}w.

Then, for any
q\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, the eigenvalue counting function satisfies

(2.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{|\ln{\lambda}|}{\ln_{2}(\lambda)^{2}}\big({\mathfrak{C}}_{-}(\chi_{\Omega}w)+o(1)\big)\\
\leq&\,{\mathcal{N}}_{+}^{q}(\lambda)-\frac{|\ln{\lambda}|}{\ln_{2}(\lambda)}-\frac{|\ln{\lambda}|\ln_{3}(\lambda)}{\ln_{2}(\lambda)^{2}}\qquad\lambda\searrow 0,\\
\leq&\frac{|\ln{\lambda}|}{\ln_{2}(\lambda)^{2}}\big({\mathfrak{C}}_{+}(\chi_{{\mathcal{K}}})+o(1)\big)\end{split}

where

\ln_{2}(\lambda):=\ln{|\ln{\lambda}|}
,
\ln_{3}(\lambda):=\ln{\ln_{2}(\lambda)}
.

On the other side

(2.6)

{\mathcal{N}}_{-}^{q}(\lambda)=O(1),\qquad\lambda\searrow 0.

When
q=0
the hypothesis on
V_{2}
is useless and in the upper bound,
{\mathcal{K}}
can be replaced by
{\mathcal{K}}_{1}
, a compact set with Lipschitz boundary which contains
{\rm ess\,supp}\,V_{1}\cup{\rm ess\,supp}\,W
.

If (
2.4
) is satisfied by
-{{\mathcal{V}}}
, then (
2.5
) is satisfied by

{\mathcal{N}}_{-}^{q}(\lambda)
and (
2.6
) by
{\mathcal{N}}_{+}^{q}(\lambda)
.

Remark 2.2
.

Observe that the predominance of
V_{1}
in the previous result contradicts the intuitive expectation derived from analyzing the symbol of the Dirac operator
D_{{\mathcal{V}}}
. Indeed, the eigenvalues of
\sigma\cdot(\xi-A)+m\sigma_{3}+{{\mathcal{V}}}
, the matrix symbol of
D_{{\mathcal{V}}}
, are given by

\rho_{\pm}(x,\xi):=\frac{V_{1}+V_{2}}{2}\pm\sqrt{|\xi-A+W|^{2}+\left(\frac{V_{1}-V_{2}}{2}+m\right)^{2}}.

This formula suggests that the roles of
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
are similar. Unlike a semi-classical context of perturbative magnetic field (e.g. as in
[
9
]
), Theorem
2.1
shows that the presence of a constant magnetic field significantly modifies the way a perturbation influences the spectral behavior. A mathematical explanation of the origin of this phenomenon comes from Lemma
3.2
(see Remark
3.8
).

Remark 2.3
.

It is worth noting that for Lorentz potentials such as

{{\mathcal{V}}}_{M}:=\begin{pmatrix}M\chi_{\Omega}&0\\
0&-M\chi_{\Omega}\end{pmatrix},\quad M>0,

with
\Omega
a bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary, this result gives
the following asymptotic expansion as
\lambda\searrow 0

(2.7)

{\mathcal{N}}_{+}^{q}(\lambda)=\frac{|\ln{\lambda}|}{\ln_{2}(\lambda)}+\frac{|\ln{\lambda}|\ln_{3}(\lambda)}{\ln_{2}(\lambda)^{2}}+\frac{|\ln{\lambda}|}{\ln_{2}(\lambda)^{2}}\big({\mathfrak{C}}(\Omega)+o(1)\big),\qquad{\mathcal{N}}_{-}^{q}(\lambda)=O(1),

with
{\mathfrak{C}}(\Omega)={\mathfrak{C}}_{-}(M\chi_{\Omega})={\mathfrak{C}}_{+}(M\chi_{\Omega})=1+\ln{\left(\frac{b}{2}\,{\rm Cap}(\Omega)^{2}\right)}
.

Having in mind that the “infinite mass realization” of the Dirac operator in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
,
D^{MIT}_{\overline{\Omega}^{c}}
, is the limit (in the resolvent sense) of
D_{0}+{{\mathcal{V}}}_{M}
as
M\nearrow+\infty
(see for instance
[
4
]
in the absence of a magnetic field), the previous result suggests the following conjecture concerning the distribution of the eigenvalues of
D^{MIT}_{\overline{\Omega}^{c}}
near each Landau-Dirac level
\mu_{q}
,
q\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Conjecture.

The Dirac operator in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
with
Infinite Mass boundary condition

has an infinite (resp. finite) number of discrete eigenvalues above (resp. below) each Landau-Dirac level
\mu_{q}
with the distribution given by (
2.7
).
Or more precisely,
\left\{\lambda_{k,q}^{+}(\Omega)\right\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}
, the non-increasing sequence of eigenvalues above
\mu_{q}
satisfies the asymptotic behavior:

\lim_{k\to\infty}\left(k!(\lambda_{k,q}^{+}(\Omega)-\mu_{q})\right)^{1/k}=\frac{b\,{{\rm Cap}}(\Omega)^{2}}{2}.

For our second result both
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
could have negative contributions provided that the support of the positive contribution “encircle” the support of the negative one. The contribution of
W
will be still weaker. More specifically:

Definition 2.4
.

We say that a compact set
K
is
encircled
by an open set
\Omega
if there exists a Jordan curve
\Gamma\subset\Omega
such that
K
is contained in the interior part of
\Gamma
.

For a general class of open sets which encircle compacts set we refer to Proposition

of the Appendix.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
{{\mathcal{V}}}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
be a function given by (
1.3
) essentially supported in
{\mathcal{K}}
, a compact set with Lipschitz boundary.
Suppose there exist
K\subset{\mathcal{K}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
a compact set,
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}
open bounded subsets of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus K
,
w
a continuous and positive weight for
\Omega_{1}
and for
\Omega:=\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2}
such that:

(2.8)

V_{1}\geq\chi_{\Omega_{1}}w-C\chi_{K};\quad V_{2}\geq\chi_{\Omega_{2}}w-C\chi_{K};\quad|W|\leq C_{3}\chi_{\Omega_{1}}w+C\chi_{K},

for some constants

and

is

by
\Omega
.

Then the asymptotics (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) hold. When
q=0
the hypothesis on
V_{2}
is still useless and
V_{2}
does not appear in the upper bound.

If (
2.8
) is satisfied by
-{{\mathcal{V}}}
, then the above estimates are satisfied by interchanging
{\mathcal{N}}_{+}^{q}(\lambda)
and
{\mathcal{N}}_{-}^{q}(\lambda)
.

Combining both above theorems, it is easy to see that Theorem
2.5
still hold true if in (
2.8
), the assumption on
V_{2}
is replaced by:

(2.9)

V_{2}\geq\chi_{\Omega_{2}}w-C_{2}\chi_{\Omega_{1}}w-C\chi_{K}

for some constants

.

The above results show a hierarchy between the components of
{{\mathcal{V}}}
. If the supports of
V_{1}
,
V_{2}
and
W
are the same, then the localization and the distribution of the eigenvalues are essentially given by the sign of

. In this case the distribution of the eigenvalues near each Landau-Dirac level is similar. Theorem

shows that

can play a predominant role when, for instance,
\Omega_{1}=\emptyset
(except near
\mu_{0}
). In this case

may have a finite number of eigenvalues near
\mu_{0}
(e.g. for
V_{1}=0
) and an infinite number near each
\mu_{q}
,
q\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{*}
(the opposite property will be obtained in Corollary

).

In the following section we will identify an effective operator allowing to explicitly analyze the competition between
V_{1}
,
V_{2}
and
W
when these functions are sufficiently smooth (in the spirit of
[
25
]
for the Schrödinger and Pauli operators).

2.4.
Effective operator for smooth perturbations

In this section we assume that
V_{1}
,
V_{2}
and
W
are smooth compactly supported functions. Our main goal is to prove that the distribution of the eigenvalues of
D_{{\mathcal{V}}}
near
\mu_{q}
,
q\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, is governed by effective Toeplitz operator
p_{0}v_{q}({{\mathcal{V}}})p_{0}
with
v_{q}({{\mathcal{V}}})
being the smooth function given by the following linear combinations of
V_{1}
,
V_{2}
,
\widetilde{b}=\partial_{x_{1}}\widetilde{A}_{2}-\partial_{x_{2}}\widetilde{A}_{1}
and their derivatives until the order
2|q|
or
2|q|-2
:

(2.10)

{v}_{q}({{\mathcal{V}}}):=\begin{cases}\overline{t}_{q}{\rm L}^{(0)}_{|q|}\left(-\frac{\Delta}{2b}\right)V_{1}+t_{q}{\rm L}^{(0)}_{|q|-1}\left(-\frac{\Delta}{2b}\right)V_{2}+\frac{1}{\mu_{q}}{\rm L}^{(1)}_{{|q|-1}}\left(-\frac{\Delta}{2b}\right)\widetilde{b},&q<0\\
V_{1},&q=0\\

B=A+M
, with
{\rm Dom}(B)={\rm Dom}(A)
and if
M=M^{*}
is
A-
compact, i.e.
{\rm Dom}(A)\subset{\rm Dom}(M)
and
M(A-i)^{-1}
is compact. Indeed thanks to
[
16
, Corollary 3.5]
, we have:

Proposition 4.1

(
[
16
]
)
.

Let
A
and
B
be two self-adjoint operators in
\mathcal{H}
with same domains such that
M=B-A
is
A-
compact. Then for
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\sigma_{\rm ess}(A)
, the difference
E_{B}(-\infty,\lambda)-E_{A}(-\infty,\lambda)
is compact and so
\Xi(\lambda;B,A)
is well defined.

For an interval
\mathcal{J}\subset{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\sigma_{\rm ess}(A)
define the eigenvalue counting function

\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{J};A):={\rm Tr}\,E_{A}({\mathcal{J}})

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume that
B=A+M
, with
M
self-adjoint and
A-
compact. Then if
[\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}]\subset{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\sigma_{\rm ess}(A)
we have that

\Xi(\lambda_{1};B,A)-\Xi(\lambda_{2};B,A)=\mathcal{N}([\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2});B)-\mathcal{N}([\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2});A).

Proof.

Since, for
\bullet=A
or
B
,
E_{\bullet}(-\infty,\lambda_{1})-E_{\bullet}(-\infty,\lambda_{2})=E_{\bullet}[\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
is of finite rank, by using (
4.2
) we see that

{\rm Index}\Big((E_{\bullet}(-\infty,\lambda_{1}),E_{\bullet}(-\infty,\lambda_{2})\Big)={\rm Tr}\Big(E_{\bullet}[\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\Big)=\mathcal{N}\Big([\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2});\bullet\Big).

Moreover, thanks to Proposition
4.1
, we can apply (
4.3
) to obtain:

\Xi(\lambda_{1};B,A)=\mathcal{N}\Big([\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2});B\Big)+\Xi(\lambda_{2};B,A)-\mathcal{N}\Big([\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2});A\Big),

and conclude the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Under the assumption of Lemma
4.2
, for
M_{\pm}=1/2(|M|\pm M)
and
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\sigma_{\rm ess}(A)
, we have

-{\rm rank}\,M_{-}\leq\Xi(\lambda;B,A)\leq{\rm rank}\,M_{+}

Proof.

Let us suppose that
{\rm dim}({\rm Ran}\,E_{A}(-\infty,\lambda)\cap{\rm Ker}\,E_{B}(-\infty,\lambda))>{\rm dim}\,{\rm rank}\,M_{+}
, ie., there exists
\psi\neq 0
such that
\psi\in({\rm Ran}\,E_{A}(-\infty,\lambda)\cap{\rm Ran}\,E_{B}[\lambda,\infty))
such that
\|\psi\|=1
and
M_{+}\psi=0
.
The first assumption is equivalent to say that:

(4.5)

\langle A\psi,\psi\rangle\in(-\infty,\lambda);\quad\langle B\psi,\psi\rangle\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus(-\infty,\lambda)

on the other side
M_{+}\psi=0
implies

\langle B\psi,\psi\rangle=\langle(A+M)\psi,\psi\rangle\leq\langle A\psi,\psi\rangle,

so we get a contradiction with (
4.5
).
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

[monotonicity]
Assume that
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
are self-adjoint and
A-
compact operators. Then for
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\sigma_{\rm ess}(A)
, we have:

M_{1}\geq M_{2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\Xi(\lambda;A,A+M_{1})\geq\Xi(\lambda;A,A+M_{2}).

Proof.

Since by proposition
4.1
we have that
E_{A+M_{1}}(-\infty,\lambda)-E_{A}(-\infty,\lambda)
is compact we can use (
4.3
) to obtain

\displaystyle{\rm Index}(E_{A+M_{1}}(-\infty,\lambda),E_{A+M_{2}}(-\infty,\lambda))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm Index}(E_{A+M_{1}}(-\infty,\lambda),E_{A}(-\infty,\lambda))+{\rm Index}(E_{A}(-\infty,\lambda),E_{A+M_{2}}(-\infty,\lambda)).

Moreover,
{\rm Index}(E_{A}(-\infty,\lambda),E_{A+M_{2}}(-\infty,\lambda))=-{\rm Index}(E_{A+M_{2}}(-\infty,\lambda),E_{A}(-\infty,\lambda))
, and by lemma
4.3

{\rm Index}(E_{A+M_{1}}(-\infty,\lambda),E_{A+M_{2}}(-\infty,\lambda))\geq 0
.
∎

4.2.
Proof of Proposition
3.1

The following proof is inspired by
[
22
]
. Note however that the exact analogue of
[
22
]
would give
{{\mathcal{V}}}_{\epsilon}^{\pm}={{\mathcal{V}}}\pm\epsilon|{{\mathcal{V}}}|
, whose expression for a matrix operator is more difficult to handle than the one given in this proposition.

Proof.

Fix
q\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. We will make the proof for the upper bound of
\mathcal{N}_{+}^{q}
.
First if
A=D_{0}
and
M={\mathcal{V}}
, then
A
and
B=A+M
satisfy the conditions of Proposition
4.1
, and
the index
\Xi(\lambda;D_{0}+{\mathcal{V}},D_{0})
is well defined for
any
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{\mu_{r}\}_{r\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
. Moreover, from Lemma
4.2

\mathcal{N}_{+}^{q}(\lambda)=\Xi(\mu_{q}+\lambda;D_{0}+{\mathcal{V}},D_{0})-\Xi\left(\alpha;D_{0}+{\mathcal{V}},D_{0}\right).

Next, set
\mathcal{P}_{q}^{\perp}:=Id-\mathcal{P}_{q},
and

{{\mathcal{V}}}_{d}:=\begin{pmatrix}V_{1}&0\\
0&V_{2}\end{pmatrix};\qquad{{\mathcal{V}}}_{a}:=\begin{pmatrix}0&W^{*}\\
W&0\end{pmatrix}.

Thus
{{\mathcal{V}}}={{\mathcal{V}}}_{q}+{{\mathcal{V}}}_{a}

and in the sense of the quadratic forms in
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
for a.e.
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
,

(4.6)

{{\mathcal{V}}}(x)\leq|{{\mathcal{V}}}_{d}|(x)+|{{\mathcal{V}}}_{a}|(x),\qquad\text{where}\quad|{{\mathcal{V}}}_{d}|=\begin{pmatrix}|{V}_{1}|&0\\
0&|V_{2}|\end{pmatrix};\quad|{{\mathcal{V}}}_{a}|=|W|I_{2}.

Then
one can see that

{{\mathcal{V}}}\leq\mathcal{P}_{q}({{\mathcal{V}}}+\epsilon(|{{\mathcal{V}}}_{d}|+|{{\mathcal{V}}}_{a}|)\mathcal{P}_{q}+\mathcal{P}_{q}^{\perp}({{\mathcal{V}}}+\epsilon^{-1}(|{{\mathcal{V}}}_{d}|+|{{\mathcal{V}}}_{a}|))\mathcal{P}_{q}^{\perp},

and it is not difficult to see that
{{\mathcal{V}}}+\epsilon(|{{\mathcal{V}}}_{d}|+|{{\mathcal{V}}}_{a}|)={{\mathcal{V}}}_{\epsilon}^{+}
. Thus, by Lemma
4.4

\Xi(\mu_{q}+\lambda;D_{0}+{{\mathcal{V}}},D_{0})\leq\Xi(\mu_{q}+\lambda;D_{0}+\mathcal{P}_{q}{{\mathcal{V}}}_{\epsilon}^{+}\mathcal{P}_{q}+\mathcal{P}_{q}^{\perp}({{\mathcal{V}}}+\epsilon^{-1}(|{{\mathcal{V}}}_{d}|+|{{\mathcal{V}}}_{a}|))\mathcal{P}_{q}^{\perp},D_{0})

Using again Lemma
4.2

\displaystyle\Xi(\mu_{q}+\lambda;D_{0}+\mathcal{P}_{q}{{\mathcal{V}}}_{\epsilon}^{+}\mathcal{P}_{q}+\mathcal{P}_{q}^{\perp}({{\mathcal{V}}}+\epsilon^{-1}(|{{\mathcal{V}}}_{d}|+|{{\mathcal{V}}}_{a}|))\mathcal{P}_{q}^{\perp},D_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}((\mu_{q}+\lambda),\alpha);D_{0}+\mathcal{P}_{q}{{\mathcal{V}}}_{\epsilon}^{+}\mathcal{P}_{q}+\mathcal{P}_{q}^{\perp}({{\mathcal{V}}}+\epsilon^{-1}(|{{\mathcal{V}}}_{d}|+|{{\mathcal{V}}}_{a}|))\mathcal{P}_{q}^{\perp})

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\Xi(\alpha;D_{0}+\mathcal{P}_{q}{{\mathcal{V}}}_{\epsilon}^{+}\mathcal{P}_{q}+\mathcal{P}_{q}^{\perp}(V+\epsilon^{-1}(|{{\mathcal{V}}}_{d}|+|{{\mathcal{V}}}_{a}|))\mathcal{P}_{q}^{\perp},D_{0}).

Further

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}((\mu_{q}+\lambda),\alpha);D_{0}+\mathcal{P}_{q}{{\mathcal{V}}}_{\epsilon}^{+}\mathcal{P}_{q}+\mathcal{P}_{q}^{\perp}({{\mathcal{V}}}+\epsilon^{-1}(|{{\mathcal{V}}}_{d}|+|{{\mathcal{V}}}_{a}|))\mathcal{P}_{q}^{\perp})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}((\mu_{q}+\lambda,\alpha);\mathcal{P}_{q}(\mu_{q}+{{\mathcal{V}}}_{\epsilon}^{+})\mathcal{P}_{q})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}((\mu_{q}+\lambda,\alpha);\mathcal{P}_{q}^{\perp}({{\mathcal{V}}}+\epsilon^{-1}(|{{\mathcal{V}}}_{d}|+|{{\mathcal{V}}}_{a}|))\mathcal{P}_{q}^{\perp}).

Finally, by simply noting that
\mathcal{P}_{q}^{\perp}D_{0}\mathcal{P}_{q}^{\perp}=\sum_{p\neq q}\mu_{p}\mathcal{P}_{p}
and each
\mathcal{P}_{q}^{\perp}({{\mathcal{V}}}+\epsilon^{-1}(|{{\mathcal{V}}}_{d}|+|{{\mathcal{V}}}_{a}|))\mathcal{P}_{q}^{\perp}
is compact, we deduce that
(\mu_{q}+\lambda,\alpha)
contains only a finite number of eigenvalues of
\mathcal{P}_{q}^{\perp}(D_{0}+{{\mathcal{V}}}+\epsilon^{-1}(|{{\mathcal{V}}}_{d}|+|{{\mathcal{V}}}_{a}|))\mathcal{P}_{q}^{\perp}
and then

\mathcal{N}\left((\mu_{q}+\lambda,\alpha);\mathcal{P}_{q}^{\perp}({{\mathcal{V}}}+\epsilon^{-1}(|{{\mathcal{V}}}_{d}|+|{{\mathcal{V}}}_{a}|)\mathcal{P}_{q}^{\perp}\right)=O(1),

as
\lambda>0
goes to
0
.
Putting all this inequalities together we finish the proof.
∎

4.3.
Encirclement property

In this section, in order to justify the relevance of the encirclement property (see Definition
2.4
), we prove a general encirclement property of an open connected set provided there is some space inside its outerboundary. Obviously this condition is not necessary as is easy to construct examples of compact sets encircled by an open set not satisfying this separation condition.

For a open bounded set
\Omega
, we have the decomposition

(4.7)

{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\overline{\Omega}=\mathcal{C}_{B}\Omega\cup\mathcal{C}_{U}\Omega,

where
\mathcal{C}_{B}\Omega
is the union of all the bounded connected components of
{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
and
\mathcal{C}_{U}\Omega
is the unbounded connected component. Notice that the set
\mathcal{S}\Omega:={\mathbb{C}}\setminus C_{U}\Omega
is simply connected and
\Omega\subset\mathcal{S}\Omega
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded connected open set in
{\mathbb{C}}
. Suppose that
{\rm dist}(\mathcal{C}_{B}\Omega,\mathcal{C}_{U}\Omega)
is positive. Then any compact set
K
contained in
\mathcal{S}\Omega
is encircled by
\Omega
.

Proof.

By the Riemann mapping theorem there exists a conformal map
\phi:\mathcal{S}\Omega\to\mathbb{D}
such that for some
k_{0}\in K
,
\phi(k_{0})=0
, where
\mathbb{D}
is the unit disk in
{\mathbb{C}}
. Under the assumption
{\rm dist}(\mathcal{C}_{B}\Omega,\mathcal{C}_{U}\Omega)>0
, there exists
r\in(0,1)
such that

C_{r}:=\{z\in\mathbb{D}:|z|>r\}\subset\phi(\Omega).

Indeed, if this is not true there exist a sequence
\{y_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{D}
such that
|y_{n}|\to 1
and
y_{n}\notin\phi(\Omega)
for
n\in\{0,1,2,...\}
. Being the set
\{x_{n}:=\phi^{-1}(y_{n})\}
bounded, there exists
(x_{m})_{m}
, a sequence in
\mathcal{S}\Omega
such that
x_{m}\to x\in\overline{\mathcal{S}\Omega}
. Since
|\phi(x_{m})|\to 1
we see that
x\in\partial(\mathcal{S}\Omega)
. Next, since
{\rm dist}(C_{B}\Omega,\mathcal{C}_{U}\Omega)>0
one can see that
{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Omega=\overline{{\mathcal{C}}_{B}\Omega}\cup\overline{{\mathcal{C}}_{U}\Omega}
.

By the definition of
\mathcal{S}\Omega
, if
x_{m}\in\overline{{\mathcal{C}}_{U}\Omega}={\mathcal{C}}_{U}\Omega\cup\partial{\mathcal{C}}_{U}\Omega
then
x_{m}\notin\mathcal{S}\Omega
which is not possible. On the other side
x_{m}\in\overline{{\mathcal{C}}_{B}\Omega}
implies that
x\in\overline{{\mathcal{C}}_{B}\Omega}
. But we already know that
x\in\partial(\mathcal{S}\Omega)=\partial{\mathcal{C}}_{U}\Omega
and
{\rm dist}(\partial{\mathcal{C}}_{U}\Omega,\overline{{\mathcal{C}}_{B}\Omega})>0
. Its gives again a contradiction.

Now, take
\delta\in(r,1)
such that for any
z\in\phi(K)
,
|z|<\delta
. Set the curve in
\Omega
given by
\Gamma:=\phi^{-1}(\{z:|z|=\delta\})
. Since
\phi(k_{0})=0
for a point
k_{0}\in K
, we immediately obtain that
K
is in the interior part of
\Gamma
. ∎

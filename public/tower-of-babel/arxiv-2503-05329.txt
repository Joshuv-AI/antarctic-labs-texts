Abstract
. Given 0 ≤ r ′ ≤ r ≤ ∞ 0\leq r^{\prime}\leq r\leq\infty , and d ∈ ℕ d\in{\mathbb{N}} , we construct a simple unital AH algebra A A with stable rank one, and a pointwise outer action α : ℤ d → Aut ​ ( A ) \alpha\colon{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\to\text{Aut}(A) such that rc ⁡ ( A ) = r {\operatorname{rc}}(A)=r and rc ⁡ ( A ⋊ α ℤ d ) = r ′ {\operatorname{rc}}\left(A\rtimes_{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\right)=r^{\prime} .



1.
Introduction

The radius of comparison is a numerical invariant for unital stably finite
C*-algebras
introduced by Toms in
[
17
]
as a means of measuring the extent to which
strict comparison fails. This invariant was used to construct a
family of pairwise non-isomorphic simple AH algebras with the same Elliott
invariant. Although considerable work has focused on identifying conditions
under which the regularity properties required for Elliott classification are
preserved under natural constructions, little is known about how the radius of
comparison behaves under these constructions. With the Elliott
program now largely complete, it is natural to go beyond classifiable cases
and gain a better understanding of the radius of comparison as an invariant
rather than merely as a tool for distinguishing examples.

In
[
14
]
, the second author constructed an action of
the circle group
\mathbb{T}
on a simple separable unital stably finite

\mathcal{Z}
-stable nuclear C*-algebra
A
, such that the crossed
product
A\rtimes\mathbb{T}
is again simple but not

\mathcal{Z}
-stable. This construction arises as the dual of an action
of the
integers on a simple non-
\mathcal{Z}
-stable AH algebra with a positive
radius of comparison; in that case, the crossed product by
\mathbb{Z}

becomes

\mathcal{Z}
-stable. This result raises questions about how the
radius of comparison behaves under discrete group actions. In the setting of
simple unital C*-algebras, the results in the literature so far provide bounds
for the radius of comparison of crossed products by finite group actions,
assuming the actions in question have either the weak tracial Rokhlin property
(
[
2
]
), or a weak form of approximate representability (
[
3
]
).

In this paper, we construct examples demonstrating that the radius of
comparison can
decrease to any intermediate value between
0
and the original value. More
precisely, we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any pair of extended real numbers
0\leq r^{\prime}\leq r\leq\infty
and
for any
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
there exists a simple AH algebra
A
with satble rank
one and a pointwise outer action
\alpha:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\text{Aut}(A)
, such that
\text{rc}(A)=r
and
\text{rc}(A\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{Z}^{d})=r^{\prime}
.

Remark 1.2
.

The case
r^{\prime}=0
is essentially contained in
[
14
]
, and
therefore we explicitly prove only the case
r^{\prime}>0
in this paper. More
specifically, that construction in
[
14
]
produced an
AH algebra
A
with positive radius of comparison
r
and a pointwise outer
action of the integers such that the crossed product is

\mathcal{Z}
-stable, and in particular has radius of comparison
0
. It
provided examples with
r\in(0,1)
. Although
r
was not
computed precisely (only a lower bound was needed), a careful examination
using the same arguments as in this paper shows that any value of
r
can
be
obtained. The modifications required to achieve
r\geq 1
, and to replace

{\mathbb{Z}}
with
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
, are identical to those in our construction and are
straightforward. In principle, one could write a unified proof addressing
the case
r^{\prime}=0
, but we chose not to do so to keep the notation manageable
in an already technical proof.

This shows that there is no simple
formula relating the radius of comparison of a C*-algebra with that of its
crossed product. We do not know whether the radius of comparison can increase
when forming crossed products by pointwise outer actions.

Question 1.3
.

Is there a simple unital stably finite C*-algebra
A
along with a
pointwise outer
action
\alpha
of a countable discrete amenable group
G
such that
{\operatorname{rc}}(A\rtimes_{\alpha}G)>{\operatorname{rc}}(A)
?

Rokhlin property - with an inner action on the bottom part.Since no general

crossed product is an AH algebra too, and its radius of comparison is shown to
be the same as the one coming from the bottom row.

elementary lemma about the existence of sequences of natural numbers with a
prescribed growth condition. In Section

{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
. In
Section

crossed product.

Remark 1.4
.

It follows from
[
13
, Theorem 1.1]
that an action on a
non-
\mathcal{Z}
-stable C*-algebra cannot simultaneously be approximately
inner and have the Rokhlin property. The action we construct has neither
property. However, it has some form of an interpolated property. Let us
focus on the case of
d=1
for this remark. At the
n
’th stage of the
construction, we obtain a direct sum of
2^{n}
copies of
C(X_{n})\otimes M_{r(n)}
and a single copy of
C(Y_{n})\otimes M_{r(n)}
. Denote by

p_{n,1},p_{n,2},\ldots,p_{n,2^{n}}
the units of the
2^{n}
copies of
C(X_{n})\otimes M_{r(n)}
. Set
p_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{2^{n}}p_{n,k}
, and set
q_{n}=1-p_{n}
. These projections are in the center of the
n
’th level
of the direct system, so they form a central sequence. We would have had
the tracial Rokhlin property if the sequence
q_{n}
tended to zero in
the tracial sense, whereas it would be tracially approximately inner if the
sequence
p_{n}
tended to zero in the tracial sense. In our
construction, neither condition holds. Instead, we obtain Rokhlin-like
towers whose
sum is a central projection that is neither large nor small. It would be
interesting to investigate this phenomenon in more generality, but we do
not pursue this direction further in the paper.

2.
Preliminaries

If
A
is a C*-algebra, we denote by

A_{+}
the
set of positive elements of
A
. Using the natural embedding
M_{n}(A)\hookrightarrow M_{n+1}(A)
, we take
M_{\infty}(A)=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}M_{n}(A)
.

We recall the definition of Cuntz subequivalence (going back to
[
5
]
) and
the radius of comparison.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
A
be a C*-algebra. For
a,b\in M_{\infty}(A)_{+}
, we say that
a
is

Cuntz subequivalent
to
b
, denoted by
a\lesssim b
, if there is a
sequence
\left(v_{n}\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}
in
M_{\infty}(A)
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}v_{n}bv_{n}^{*}=a.

Let
A
be unital and stably finite. Let
T(A)
be the tracial state space of

A
. Any
\tau\in T(A)
extends to a non-normalized trace on
M_{n}(A)
, for

n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. We use
\tau
for the extended trace as
well. For
a\in M_{n}(A)_{+}
, set

d_{\tau}(a)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\tau(a^{1/n})
.

The following is the definition of the radius of comparison for unital exact
stably finite C*-algebras. We refer the reader to
[
17
]
for further
details.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
A
be a unital stably finite C*-algebra.

(1)

For
r\in[0,\infty)
,
A
is said to have
r
-comparison
if for any

n\in\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
and
a,b\in M_{n}(A)_{+}
satisfying

d_{\tau}(a)+r<d_{\tau}(b)

for all
\tau\in T(A)
, we have
a\lesssim b
.

(2)

The radius of comparison, denoted by
{\operatorname{rc}}(A)
, is the infimum of

r\in[0,\infty)
such that
A
has
r
-comparison. If there is no such
r
,
one sets
{\operatorname{rc}}(A)=\infty
.

In general, the definition of the radius of comparison requires quasitraces
rather than traces. However, the C*-algebras that we consider in this paper
are nuclear, and in particular exact, so all quasitraces are traces by

[
10
, Theorem 5.11]
.

We recall the following simple facts.

Proposition 2.3
.

[
17
, Proposition 6.2]

Let
A
and
B
be stably finite unital C*-algebras. The following hold:

(1)

{\operatorname{rc}}(A\oplus B)=\max\left\{\text{rc}(A),\text{rc}(B)\right\}.

(2)

{\operatorname{rc}}(M_{n}\otimes A)=\frac{1}{n}{\operatorname{rc}}(A),\,\,\text{for}\,\,n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

We’ll need the following lemma about infinite products.
(See similar lemmas, used for similar purposes, in
[
3
, Lemma 5.7]
and

[
12
, Lemmas 3.9, 3.10]
.)

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
0<r^{\prime}\leq r<1
, and
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
. There exist sequences

(d(n))_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
and
(d^{\prime}(n))_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
in
{\mathbb{N}}
such that

(a)

d(n)
is nondecreasing and
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}d(n)=\infty
.

(b)

\displaystyle{\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{d(n)}{d(n)+1+2^{dn-d}}=r}
.

(c)

d^{\prime}(n)\leq d(n)
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

(d)

\displaystyle{\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{d^{\prime}(n)}{d(n)+1+2^{dn-d}}}=r^{\prime}
.

Proof.

For
0<r<1
, we first construct a sequence
(d(n))_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
in
{\mathbb{N}}

satisfying
(a)
and
(b)
.

We prove this by induction on
n
. Define

d(1)=\min\left(\left\{k\in{\mathbb{N}}\;|\;\frac{k}{k+2}>r\right\}\right).

The set in the above definition is non-empty. Fix

r_{1}=\frac{d(1)}{d(1)+2}.

Then
1>r_{1}>r
. For
n\geq 2
, set

d(n)=\min\left(\left\{k\in{\mathbb{N}}\;|\;\frac{k}{k+1+2^{dn-d}}>\frac{r}{r_{n-1}}\right\}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad r_{n}=\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{d(k)}{d(k)+1+2^{dk-d}}.

We now show that

(1)

1>r_{n}\geq r_{n+1}>r
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

(2)

d(n)\leq d(n+1)
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

(3)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}r_{n}=r
.

(
1
) is immediate. For (
2
), note that

\frac{r}{r_{n-1}}\leq\frac{r}{r_{n}}<\frac{d(n+1)}{d(n+1)+1+2^{dn}}<\frac{d(n+1)}{d(n+1)+1+2^{dn-d}}.

Thus,

d(n+1)\in\left\{k\in{\mathbb{N}}\;|\;\frac{k}{k+1+2^{dn-d}}>\frac{r}{r_{n-1}}\right\}.

Since
d(n)
is defined to be the minimum value of the above set, we conclude that
d(n)\leq d(n+1)
.
We now prove (
3
). For this, by (
1
), we
see that
(r_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is a decreasing sequence and bounded below by
r
.
Thus,
(r_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n>0}}
has a limit, which we denote
s
. We know that
s\geq r
. We need to show that
r\geq s
. Since

{\displaystyle\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{d(n)}{d(n)+1+2^{dn-d}}}=s,

we get

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1+2^{dn-d}}{d(n)+1+2^{dn-d}}<\infty.

Thus,

\displaystyle{\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{2^{dn-d}+1}{d(n)+1+2^{dn-d}}=0}
,
which implies,
\displaystyle{\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{d(n)}{1+2^{dn-d}}=\infty}
.
Furthermore,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{2^{dn-d}+1}{d(n)+2^{dn-d}}=0.

Assume, to obtain a contradiction, that
r<s
. Then,

\displaystyle{0<\frac{r}{s}<1}
. Choose
m\in{\mathbb{N}}

large enough, such that

\frac{2^{dm-d}+1}{d(m)+2^{dm-d}}<1-\frac{r}{s}.

Then,
d(m)\geq 2
and

\frac{r}{s}<1-\frac{2^{dm-d}+1}{d(m)+2^{dm-d}}=\frac{d(m)-1}{(d(m)-1)+1+2^{dm-d}}.

Since
s\leq r_{m-1}
,

\frac{r}{r_{m-1}}\leq\frac{r}{s}<\frac{d(m)-1}{(d(m)-1)+1+2^{dm-d}}.

This contradicts the definition of
d(m)
.

We now construct the sequence
(d^{\prime}(n))_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
in
{\mathbb{N}}
. If
r^{\prime}=r
, then we
can simply choose
d^{\prime}(n)=d(n)
, so we assume from now on that
r^{\prime}<r
.

For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, set

\rho_{n}=\prod_{k={n+1}}^{\infty}\frac{d(k)}{d(k)+1+2^{dk-d}}.

Then
\rho_{n}<1
for all
n\in\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
.

We construct a sequence
(t(n))_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
by induction such that

t(n)(1+2^{dn-d})\in{\mathbb{N}}
for all
n
, such that

1\leq t(n)(1+2^{dn-d})\leq d(n),

and such that, with

\gamma_{n}=\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{t(k)(1+2^{dk-d})}{d(k)+1+2^{dk-d}},

we have

\gamma_{n}\rho_{n}\geq r^{\prime}\quad\text{and}\quad\rho_{n}\gamma_{n}-r^{\prime}<\frac{1}{d(n)+1+2^{dn-d}}.

Note that, since
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(d(n)+1+2^{dn-d})=\infty
and
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\rho_{n}=1
, we conclude that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\gamma_{n}=r^{\prime}
. Defining
d^{\prime}(n)=t(n)(1+2^{dn-d})
gives the conclusion of the lemma.

For the base case, let
m
be the least integer such that

\frac{m\rho_{1}}{d(1)+2}\geq r^{\prime}.

Then
m\geq 1
. Fix
\displaystyle{t(1)=\frac{m}{2}}
. Notice that,

\frac{d(1)\rho_{1}}{d(1)+2}=\frac{d(1)}{d(1)+2}\prod_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{d(k)}{d(k)+1+2^{dk-d}}=\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{d(k)}{d(k)+1+2^{dk-d}}=\rho_{0}=r>r^{\prime}.

Thus, we have
1\leq 2t(1)\leq d(1)
. Moreover

\frac{(m-1)\rho_{1}}{d(1)+2}<r^{\prime}.

Since
\rho_{1}\leq 1
, we have

0\leq\frac{m\rho_{1}}{d(1)+2}-r^{\prime}<\frac{\rho_{1}}{d(1)+2}\leq\frac{1}{d(1)+2}.

Note that

\gamma_{1}=\frac{2t(1)}{d(1)+2}=\frac{m}{d(1)+2},

so

\gamma_{1}\rho_{1}-r^{\prime}<\frac{1}{d(1)+2}.

Now suppose
t(1),t(2),\ldots,t(n)
were selected, and that, with
\gamma_{n}
as
above, we have
\gamma_{n}\rho_{n}\geq r^{\prime}
and

\gamma_{n}\rho_{n}-r^{\prime}<\frac{1}{d(n)+1+2^{dn-d}}.

Let
m
be the least integer such that

\frac{m\gamma_{n}\rho_{n+1}}{d(n+1)+1+2^{dn}}\geq r^{\prime}.

Since
r^{\prime}>0
,
\gamma_{n}>0
and
\rho_{n+1}>0
, we have
m\geq 1
. Fix

t(n+1)=\frac{m}{1+2^{dn}}.

See that

\begin{split}\frac{d(n+1)\gamma_{n}\rho_{n+1}}{d(n+1)+1+2^{dn}}&=\frac{\gamma_{n}d(n+1)}{d(n+1)+1+2^{dn}}\prod_{k=n+2}^{\infty}\frac{d(k)}{d(k)+1+2^{dk-d}}\\
&=\gamma_{n}\prod_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\frac{d(k)}{d(k)+1+2^{dk-d}}\\
&=\gamma_{n}\rho_{n}\geq r^{\prime}.\end{split}

Thus,
1\leq m\leq d(n+1)
, so
1\leq t(n+1)(1+2^{dn})\leq d(n+1)
. Moreover,

\frac{(m-1)\gamma_{n}\rho_{n+1}}{d(n+1)+1+2^{dn}}<r^{\prime}.

Since
\rho_{n},\gamma_{n}\leq 1
, we have

\frac{m\gamma_{n}\rho_{n+1}}{d(n+1)+1+2^{dn}}-r^{\prime}<\frac{\gamma_{n}\rho_{n+1}}{d(n+1)+1+2^{dn}}<\frac{1}{d(n+1)+1+2^{dn}}.

Using the fact that
m=t(n+1)(1+2^{dn})
and
\displaystyle{\gamma_{n+1}=\frac{t(n+1)(1+2^{dn})\gamma_{n}}{d(n+1)+1+2^{dn}}}
,
we get

\gamma_{n+1}\rho_{n+1}-r^{\prime}<\frac{1}{d(n+1)+1+2^{dn}}.

This concludes the induction.
∎

and
e_{2}
is a trivial projection of rank
M
.
By a slight abuse of notation, we use
\tilde{\Gamma}_{m,n}
to denote the
amplified map from
M_{\infty}(\widetilde{A_{n}})
to
M_{\infty}(\widetilde{A}_{m})
. For
m>n
, let

\tilde{\Gamma}_{m,n}(e)=\left(\tilde{e}_{1}\otimes 1_{C(\mathbb{T}^{d})},\tilde{e}_{2}\otimes 1_{C(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\right).

Then, from Equation
5.2
, we can deduce that the rank of

\tilde{e}_{1}
is
\frac{Mr(m){2^{md}}}{r(n)}
, and the rank of
\tilde{e}_{2}

is
\frac{Mr(m)}{r(n)}
, which is strictly less than
h^{\prime}_{0}s^{\prime}(m)+h^{\prime}_{0}r(m)
by
Equation
5.4
. Now, if
\tau
is a trace on

\widetilde{A_{m}}
then there exists
0<\lambda<1
such that

\tau(\tilde{\Gamma}_{m,n}(e))=\lambda\text{tr}_{1}(\tilde{e}_{1})+\left(1-\lambda\right)\text{tr}_{2}(\tilde{e}_{2}),

where
\text{tr}_{1}
is a normalized trace on
M_{{2^{dm}}r(m)}
and
\text{tr}_{2}
is a normalized trace on
M_{r(m)}
.

Hence,

(5.6)

\begin{split}\tau(\tilde{\Gamma}_{m,n}(e))&=\lambda\cdot\frac{1}{{2^{dm}}r(m)}\cdot\frac{Mr(m){2^{dm}}}{r(n)}+(1-\lambda)\cdot\frac{1}{r(m)}\cdot\frac{Mr(m)}{r(n)}\\
&=\frac{M}{r(n)}>\rho+h^{\prime}_{0}=\rho+\tau\left(\tilde{p}_{m,0},\tilde{q}_{m,0}\right).\end{split}

On the other hand, if
\tilde{\Gamma}_{\infty,0}(\tilde{p}_{0},\tilde{q}_{0})\lesssim\tilde{\Gamma}_{\infty,n}(e)
then, there exists some
m>n
and
x\in M_{\infty}(\widetilde{A}_{m})
such that

\|x\tilde{\Gamma}_{m,n}(e)x^{*}-\left(\tilde{p}_{m,0},\tilde{q}_{m,0}\right)\|<\frac{1}{2}.

By Lemma
5.2
, the above inequality implies that

\text{rank}(\tilde{e}_{2})\geq h^{\prime}_{0}r(m)+h^{\prime}_{0}s^{\prime}(m),

which is a contradiction by Equation
5.4
.

Finally, we consider the case when
r^{\prime}=r=\infty
and show that
{\operatorname{rc}}(A\rtimes_{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}^{d})=\infty
. For this, we show that
{\operatorname{rc}}(A\rtimes_{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
cannot have
\rho
-comparison for any
\rho\in(0,\infty)
.
Since
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}h^{\prime}_{n}=\infty
, choose
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

(5.7)

\begin{split}h_{n}^{\prime}&>\frac{\rho}{c},\\
0<\frac{1}{r(n)}&<c\cdot h_{n}^{\prime}-\rho.\end{split}

Choose
M\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

(5.8)

\rho+h^{\prime}_{n}s^{\prime}(n)<\frac{M}{r(n)}<c\cdot h_{n}^{\prime}+h^{\prime}_{n}s^{\prime}(n).

Let
e=\left(e_{1}\otimes 1_{C(\mathbb{T}^{d})},e_{2}\otimes 1_{C(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\right)\in M_{\infty}(\widetilde{A_{n}})
be a projection defined by Equation
5.5
. For
m\geq n
, put

\tilde{\Gamma}_{m,n}(e)=\left(\tilde{e}_{1}\otimes 1_{C(\mathbb{T}^{d})},\tilde{e}_{2}\otimes 1_{C(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\right).

Then,

\text{rank}(\tilde{e}_{2})=\frac{Mr(m)}{r(n)},

which by the choice of
M
,
is strictly less than
r(m)ch_{n}^{\prime}+h^{\prime}_{n}s^{\prime}(n)r(m)
. Because

c=\inf_{m\geq 0}\frac{s^{\prime}(m)}{r(m)},

we have

h^{\prime}_{n}cr(m)\leq h^{\prime}_{n}\frac{s^{\prime}(m)}{r(m)}r(m)=h^{\prime}_{n}s^{\prime}(m).

Hence, for
m\geq n
, we have

(5.9)

\text{rank}(\tilde{e}_{2})<h^{\prime}_{n}s^{\prime}(m)+h^{\prime}_{n}s^{\prime}(n)r(m).

Now, for any trace
\tau
on
\widetilde{A}_{m}
, we have

\tau(\left(\tilde{p}_{m,n},\tilde{q}_{m,n}\right))=\frac{h^{\prime}_{n}s^{\prime}(n)r(m)}{r(m)}=h^{\prime}_{n}s^{\prime}(n).

Moreover, by Inequality
5.8
, we get

\begin{split}\tau\left(\tilde{\Gamma}_{m,n}(e)\right)=\frac{M}{r(n)}>h^{\prime}_{n}s^{\prime}(n)+\rho=\tau(\left(\tilde{\textbullet}{p}_{m,n},\tilde{q}_{m,n}\right))+\rho.\end{split}

If

\tilde{\Gamma}_{\infty,n}(\left(\tilde{p}_{m,n},\tilde{q}_{m,n}\right))\lesssim\tilde{\Gamma}_{\infty,n}(e),

then there exists some
m>n
and
x\in M_{\infty}(\widetilde{A_{m}})
such that

\|x\tilde{\Gamma}_{m,n}(e)x^{*}-\left(\tilde{p}_{m,n},\tilde{q}_{m,n}\right)\|<\frac{1}{2}.

By Lemma
5.2
, the above inequality implies that

\text{rank}(\tilde{e}_{2})\geq h^{\prime}_{n}s^{\prime}(n)r(m)+h^{\prime}_{n}s^{\prime}(m),

which is a contradiction by Equation
5.9
.

∎

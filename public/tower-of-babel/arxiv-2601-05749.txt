Quantum Interference-Induced Bhattacharyya Distance Preprint: APS/PRL
Mostafizur Rahaman Laskar

Abstract
We propose a quantum distance measure between probability distributions encoded in quantum states based on the fragility of quantum interference under entangling evolution. The Quantum Interference-Induced Bhattacharyya Distance (QIBD) is defined through a single-ancilla interferometric circuit in which an interaction Hamiltonian generates correlation-dependent phases that modulate interference visibility. When the interaction vanishes, QIBD reduces to the classical Bhattacharyya distance; however, for entangling interactions, it cannot be expressed as a function of fidelity alone. Numerical simulations demonstrate that QIBD responds to correlation structure in ways that overlap-based measures do not, suggesting potential utility in contexts where interaction-aligned correlations are physically relevant.



I
Introduction

Distance measures between probability distributions are fundamental tools in statistics, information theory, and machine learning.
The Bhattacharyya distance
3
, defined through the overlap of probability distributions, is widely used for classification, hypothesis testing, and signal processing.
In quantum information, distinguishability between quantum states is typically characterized using fidelity, trace distance, or relative entropy
4
;
1
;
2
, with applications ranging from state discrimination to quantum cryptography
6
;
8
.

These distance measures, whether classical or quantum, share a common feature: they quantify distinguishability through static comparison of probability amplitudes or density matrices.
Quantum distinguishability, however, is operationally manifested through interference
7
.
Interference-based protocols such as the SWAP test and Hadamard test already play central roles in quantum algorithms, including phase estimation and quantum machine learning
9
;
8
;
6
.
These protocols measure state overlap through controlled interference. However, the entangling interactions of quantum states can generate correlation-dependent phases that affect interference visibility without altering marginal probability distributions.
The role of such correlation structure in quantum geometry has been studied extensively
2
, but its incorporation into operational distance measures has received less attention.

This observation motivates a natural question: Can a distance measure be defined operationally through the response of quantum interference to entangling dynamics?
In this Letter, we propose a measure.
The Quantum Interference-Induced Bhattacharyya Distance (QIBD) extends the classical Bhattacharyya distance by encoding probability distributions into quantum states and probing them with an interaction Hamiltonian within an interferometric circuit framework.
The resulting distance depends both on probability overlap and on how correlation structure couples to the chosen interaction.
We establish the mathematical properties of QIBD and demonstrate numerically that it exhibits sensitivity to correlations that are invisible to overlap-based measures.
While the present work focuses on the theoretical framework and their implementation on IBM quantum platform, we discuss potential applications where interaction-dependent comparison may be relevant.

II
Definition and Formulation

We consider probability distributions
p(x)
and
q(x)
over a discrete space
x\in\{0,1\}^{n}
.
We encode these distributions into quantum states via amplitude encoding:

\ket{\psi_p}=\sum_{x}\sqrt{p(x)}\ket{x},\qquad\ket{\psi_q}=\sum_{x}\sqrt{q(x)}\ket{x}.

(1)

The classical Bhattacharyya coefficient between
p(x)
and
q(x)
is written as

\mathrm{BC}(p,q)=\sum_{x}\sqrt{p(x)q(x)}.

(2)

To incorporate interaction effects, consider an entangling unitary

U_{E}=e^{iH_{E}},

(3)

generated by a Hamiltonian
H_{E}
. We define the
Quantum Interference-Induced Bhattacharyya Coefficient
(QIBC) as

\mathrm{QIBC}(p,q)=\left|\matrixelement{\psi_p}{U_E}{\psi_q}\right|^{2}=\left|\sum_{x}\sqrt{p(x)q(x)}\,e^{i\Phi(x)}\right|^{2},

(4)

where
\Phi(x)
denotes the phase accumulated by basis state
\ket{x}
under the action of
U_{E}
. The corresponding distance measure can be written as

D_{\mathrm{QIBD}}(p,q)=-\log\mathrm{QIBC}(p,q).

(5)

This definition is motivated by an operational interferometric measurement, which we now describe.

III
Operational Implementation

Figure 1:

Interferometric circuit for measuring QIBD.
An ancilla qubit controls the preparation of a superposition between amplitude-encoded states
\ket{\psi_p}
and
\ket{\psi_q}
through a sequence consisting of a unitary
U_{p}
, followed by controlled-
U_{p}^{\dagger}
and controlled-
U_{q}
. Note that,
\ket{\psi_p}=U_{p}\ket{0}^{\otimes n}
, and
\ket{\psi_q}=U_{q}\ket{0}^{\otimes n}
.
The interaction unitary
U_{E}
is applied asymmetrically (conditioned on the ancilla state) to induce configuration-dependent phases.
The ancilla is then measured to extract interference visibility, from which QIBC is obtained.

The circuit shown in Fig.
1
provides an operational realization of QIBC through a generalization of the Hadamard test.

Lemma 1 (Interferometric measurement protocol).

Let
|\psi_{p}\rangle
and
|\psi_{q}\rangle
be amplitude-encoded probability distributions and let
U_{E}=e^{iH_{E}}
be a unitary operator.
The circuit in Fig.
1
, consisting of a single ancilla qubit and a sequence of controlled operations, yields the ancilla measurement probability

P(0)=\frac{1}{2}\left[1+\mathrm{Re}\,\matrixelement{\psi_p}{U_E}{\psi_q}\right].

(6)

Repeated execution with appropriate phase shifts allows estimation of
\mathrm{QIBC}(p,q)=|\matrixelement{\psi_p}{U_E}{\psi_q}|^{2}
.

Proof.

Initialize the ancilla and data register in the state
\ket{\Psi_0}=\ket{0}_{a}\otimes\ket{0}^{\otimes n}
.
A Hadamard gate on the ancilla yields

\ket{\Psi_1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\ket{0}_{a}+\ket{1}_{a}\right)\otimes\ket{0}^{\otimes n}.

(7)

The circuit applies
U_{p}
on system registers, followed by controlled-
U_{p}^{\dagger}
and controlled-
U_{q}
on the ancilla branch
\ket{1}_{a}
.
After the application of
U_{p}
, the state is
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ket{0}_{a}+\ket{1}_{a})\ket{\psi_p}
.
The controlled operations transform the
\ket{1}_{a}
branch from
\ket{\psi_p}
to
\ket{0}^{\otimes n}
and then to
\ket{\psi_q}
, yielding

\ket{\Psi_2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\ket{0}_{a}\ket{\psi_p}+\ket{1}_{a}\ket{\psi_q}\right).

(8)

The unitary
U_{E}
is applied to the register conditioned on
\ket{1}_{a}
, and the resulting state given by

\ket{\Psi_3}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\ket{0}_{a}\ket{\psi_p}+\ket{1}_{a}U_{E}\ket{\psi_q}\right).

(9)

A final Hadamard on the ancilla produces

\ket{\Psi_4}=\ket{0}_{a}\frac{\ket{\psi_p}+U_{E}\ket{\psi_q}}{2}+\ket{1}_{a}\frac{\ket{\psi_p}-U_{E}\ket{\psi_q}}{2}.

(10)

Measuring the ancilla gives
P(0)=\frac{1}{2}\left[1+\mathrm{Re}\,\matrixelement{\psi_p}{U_E}{\psi_q}\right]
.
An additional measurement with an
S
-gate before the final Hadamard extracts
\mathrm{Im}\,\matrixelement{\psi_p}{U_E}{\psi_q}
, allowing reconstruction of
|\matrixelement{\psi_p}{U_E}{\psi_q}|^{2}
.

\square

IV
Mathematical Properties

The properties of QIBD is demonstrated as follows.

Lemma 2 (Positivity).

For any distributions
p,q
and unitary
U_{E}
,
D_{\mathrm{QIBD}}(p,q)\geq 0
, with equality if and only if
p(x)=q(x)
and
U_{E}=I
.

Proof.

By definition,
D_{\mathrm{QIBD}}(p,q)=-\log|\matrixelement{\psi_p}{U_E}{\psi_q}|^{2}
.
Since
U_{E}
is unitary and
\ket{\psi_p},\ket{\psi_q}
are normalized states, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality implies

\left|\matrixelement{\psi_p}{U_E}{\psi_q}\right|\leq\|\psi_{p}\|\,\|\psi_{q}\|=1.

(11)

Consequently, the argument of the logarithm lies in
(0,1]
, ensuring non-negativity of
D_{\mathrm{QIBD}}
. Equality
D_{\mathrm{QIBD}}(p,q)=0
requires
|\matrixelement{\psi_p}{U_E}{\psi_q}|=1
, which is possible if and only if
U_{E}\ket{\psi_q}=e^{i\theta}\ket{\psi_p}
for some global phase
\theta
.
For amplitude-encoded probability states, this condition implies
\ket{\psi_p}=\ket{\psi_q}
and hence
p=q
.
Furthermore, any nontrivial entangling interaction introduces configuration-dependent phases that generically reduce interference visibility, requiring
U_{E}=I
.

\square

Positivity reflects the fact that QIBD quantifies the loss of interference visibility under interaction. Only when two distributions are identical and no interaction-induced phase structure is present does the interferometer exhibit perfect constructive interference.

Lemma 3 (Classical limit).

For
U_{E}=I
, QIBD reduces to
D_{\mathrm{QIBD}}(p,q)=-\log[\mathrm{BC}(p,q)]^{2}
.

Proof.

Setting
U_{E}=I
gives
\matrixelement{\psi_p}{I}{\psi_q}=\innerproduct{\psi_p}{\psi_q}=\sum_{x}\sqrt{p(x)q(x)}=\mathrm{BC}(p,q)
.

\square

This limit demonstrates that QIBD extends classical overlap-based geometry by embedding it within an interferometric framework. Entanglement/interference-free dynamics recover purely statistical distinguishability.

Theorem (Independence from fidelity).

For entangling
U_{E}
,
D_{\mathrm{QIBD}}
cannot be expressed as a function of the fidelity
F(p,q)=|\innerproduct{\psi_p}{\psi_q}|^{2}
alone.

Proof.

Fidelity depends only on the overlap
|\innerproduct{\psi_p}{\psi_q}|

4
.
QIBD depends on

\matrixelement{\psi_p}{U_E}{\psi_q}=\sum_{x}\sqrt{p(x)q(x)}\,e^{i\Phi(x)},

(12)

where
\Phi(x)
arises from
H_{E}
.
Different Hamiltonians can generate different phase functions
\Phi(x)
while preserving
|\innerproduct{\psi_p}{\psi_q}|
.
Distributions with identical fidelity may therefore have different QIBD values, precluding a functional relationship.

\square

This establishes that QIBD captures information beyond static overlap by incorporating how correlation structure couples to the interaction.

The theorem establishes that QIBD probes a notion of distinguishability fundamentally inaccessible to overlap-based measures.
Rather than quantifying static similarity between distributions, QIBD captures how interaction-induced correlations destabilize quantum interference under entangling dynamics. In this sense, QIBD defines an interaction-relative distance whose value depends on the physical process used to compare distributions.

Corollary (Interaction sensitivity).

For
U_{E}(\alpha)=e^{i\alpha H_{E}}
with
p\neq q
,
D_{\mathrm{QIBD}}(p,q;\alpha)
generally varies with
\alpha
even when fidelity is constant.

Proof.

Consider the transition amplitude

A(\alpha)=\matrixelement{\psi_p}{e^{i\alpha H_E}}{\psi_q}.

(13)

Expanding for small
\alpha
,

A(\alpha)=\innerproduct{\psi_p}{\psi_q}+i\alpha\,\matrixelement{\psi_p}{H_E}{\psi_q}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{2}).

(14)

While the leading term depends only on the overlap between
\ket{\psi_p}
and
\ket{\psi_q}
, the linear correction depends explicitly on matrix elements of the interaction Hamiltonian.
For generic entangling
H_{E}
, this term is nonzero whenever
p\neq q
, implying that
|A(\alpha)|
and hence
D_{\mathrm{QIBD}}(p,q;\alpha)
varies with
\alpha
.
In contrast, overlap-based measures such as fidelity depend solely on
\innerproduct{\psi_p}{\psi_q}
and are therefore independent of
\alpha
.

\square

This shows that interaction strength serves as a tunable parameter controlling the sensitivity of QIBD to correlation structure.

V
Numerical Demonstration

We evaluate QIBD numerically using statevector simulator implemented in Qiskit
5
.
Probability distributions over
N=2^{n}
outcomes are amplitude-encoded into
n
-qubit quantum states
\ket{\psi_p}
and
\ket{\psi_q}
using deterministic state preparation circuits.
The interaction unitary
U_{E}(\alpha)=\exp(i\alpha H_E)
is constructed from a nearest-neighbor Ising Hamiltonian

H_{E}=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}Z_{i}Z_{i+1},

(15)

implemented as a sequence of controlled-phase gates.
An additional ancilla qubit enables interferometric readout following the protocol established in Lemma 1.
The QIBD value is extracted from measurements on the ancilla alone, requiring estimation of both real and imaginary components of
\langle\psi_{p}|U_{E}|\psi_{q}\rangle
through repeated circuits with appropriate phase shifts.

Test-case and validation.

Prior to generating results, we validated our implementation by comparing two independent calculation methods: the full interferometric circuit simulation and direct statevector evaluation of
|\langle\psi_{p}|U_{E}|\psi_{q}\rangle|^{2}
.
For a test case with
n=3
qubits (8 basis states) using discrete Gaussian distributions with parameters
(\mu_{p},\sigma_{p})=(2.0,1.0)
and
(\mu_{q},\sigma_{q})=(5.0,1.5)
, both methods yielded identical results across interaction strengths
\alpha\in\{0.0,0.5,1.0,1.5\}
, with agreement to machine precision (
\sim 10^{-6}
).
Representative values include
D_{\mathrm{QIBD}}=1.417
at
\alpha=0
, increasing to
1.666
at
\alpha=0.5
,
2.469
at
\alpha=1.0
, and
3.627
at
\alpha=1.5
, demonstrating the expected monotonic growth with interaction strength.
Critically, at
\alpha=0
, we verified that
D_{\mathrm{QIBD}}=1.417
matches exactly the classical Bhattacharyya distance
D_{\mathrm{classical}}=1.417
, confirming the theoretical prediction of Lemma 3 that QIBD recovers the classical limit when the interaction vanishes.

Experimental parameters.

All results presented employ
n=5
data qubits, corresponding to probability distributions over
N=32
discrete outcomes.
The reference distribution
p(x)
and comparison distribution
q(x)
are chosen as discrete Gaussians over the domain
x\in\{0,1,\ldots,31\}

p(x)\propto\exp\!\left[-\frac{(x-\mu_{p})^{2}}{2\sigma_{p}^{2}}\right],\quad q(x)\propto\exp\!\left[-\frac{(x-\mu_{q})^{2}}{2\sigma_{q}^{2}}\right],

(16)

with parameters
(\mu_{p},\sigma_{p})=(5.0,1.5)
and
(\mu_{q},\sigma_{q})=(9.0,2.0)
.
These distributions are well-separated in location and differ in spread, providing a controlled setting where classical overlap-based distance remains fixed at
D_{\mathrm{classical}}=1.321
while QIBD exhibits
\alpha

Correlation structure sensitivity.

To probe QIBD’s response to correlation patterns within the data itself, we consider a parametric family of distributions

q_{\theta}(x)=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}(\theta)}\exp\!\left[\theta\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{x_{i}\oplus x_{i+1}}\right],

(17)

where
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
denotes the binary representation of
x\in\{0,\ldots,2^{n}-1\}
and
\mathcal{N}(\theta)
is a normalization constant.
The parameter
\theta
controls the strength of nearest-neighbor correlations.
Note that, the exponent equals the diagonal matrix element
\langle x|H_{E}|x\rangle
up to a constant, ensuring that the classical correlation structure is precisely aligned with the quantum probing Hamiltonian.

Figure

compares the response of QIBD and classical distance to increasing
\theta
at fixed interaction strength
\alpha=1
. This behavior indicates that when correlations are aligned with the interaction Hamiltonian, QIBD provides a substantially different quantification of distinguishability compared to overlap-based measures.
The elevated baseline value of QIBD relative to classical distance demonstrates enhanced sensitivity to the correlation structure probed by
H_{E}
.

Together, these results demonstrate the validation of the theoretical model: QIBD depends on two complementary aspects of the measurement setting.
First, the interaction strength
\alpha
(Fig.
2
) tunes the magnitude of phase accumulation, progressively destabilizing quantum interference between the encoded distributions. Secondly, the internal correlation structure of the distributions (Fig.
2
), when aligned with the probing Hamiltonian, yields distinguishability that is invisible to classical overlap-based measures.
This dual dependence confirms that QIBD quantifies interaction-relative distinguishability rather than intrinsic geometric measure.

Limitations.

As established theoretically and simulation results, QIBD is an interaction-dependent measure rather than a universal metric.
Its value depends explicitly on the choice of probing Hamiltonian
H_{E}
and does not satisfy metric axioms such as the triangle inequality.
The results should therefore be interpreted operationally: QIBD quantifies how interference visibility degrades under a specified entangling interaction, not an intrinsic geometric separation between distributions.
Furthermore, while the present simulations employ statevector simulator, practical implementation on near-term quantum Hardware (beyond 12 qubits) would require efficient state preparation (other than amplitude encoding) for meaningful simulation.

VI
Discussion

We have introduced the Quantum Interference-Induced Bhattacharyya Distance, a measure of distinguishability between probability distributions defined through the response of quantum interference to entangling dynamics.
Unlike classical distances based solely on probability overlap, QIBD incorporates correlation structure via interaction-induced phase accumulation, generalizing classical Bhattacharyya distance while capturing information inaccessible to fidelity-based measures. Operationally, QIBD requires only a single ancilla qubit, is implementable on near-term quantum machine, and can be tailored to probe specific correlation structures through choice of interaction Hamiltonian.
Potential applications include quantum machine learning (alternative kernel functions for structured data), quantum simulation (validating correlation structure of simulated states), and quantum metrology (detecting correlation changes beyond marginal distribution shifts).

More broadly, this work suggests that distinguishability between probability distributions need not be viewed as an intrinsic property, but can depend on the physical process used for comparison.
Quantum interference provides a natural mechanism for defining such process-dependent measures, where the choice of probing interaction determines which correlation patterns are emphasized.
This perspective may motivate further exploration of interaction-dependent distance concepts in quantum information theory and could inspire analogous constructions in classical domains where structured comparison processes are relevant.
Experimental validation and detailed application-specific studies remain important directions for future work.

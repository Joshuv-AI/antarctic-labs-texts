1.
Introduction

This article is dedicated to study ergodic theorems of the averaging operators associated to actions of free group or free semigroup in noncommutative spaces (quantum setting).

The origin of ergodic theory on classical measure spaces traces back to the 1930s due to Birkhoff and von Neumann. Since then, the subject went through substantial evolution.
There are numerous ergodic theorems for the actions of various different groups on classical measure spaces (cf.
[
AAB
+

10
]
,
[
Cal53
]
). For example in

[
Lin01
]
, Lindenstrauss proved pointwise ergodic theorems for tempered Følner sequences, which is considered as the generalisation of Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem for actions of second countable, amenable groups.

The noncommutative analogue of the ergodic theorems first appeared in the works of Lance (
[
Lan76
]
) in 1976. In (
[
Lan76
]
), the author established a pointwise ergodic theorem for the averages of a single automorphism in von Neumann algebra that preserves a faithful, normal state.
These results are then generalised by Kummerer, Conze, Dang-Ngoc for positive maps in the von Neumann algebra setting and many others (cf.
[
K7̈8
]
,
[
CDN78
]
and references therein) .

In the seminal works
[

]
and
[
Yea80
]
, Yeadon obtained maximal ergodic theorems for actions of positive subtracial, subunital maps on the preduals of semifinite von Neumann algebras and proved associated pointwise ergodic theorems to the noncommutative
L^{1}
-spaces.

Later on Junge and Xu in
[
JX07
]
in 2007, extended Yeadon’s weak type
(1,1)
maximal ergodic inequality in the noncommutative
L^{p}
-spaces using interpolation techniques and used it to prove an individual ergodic theorem for Dunford-Schwartz operator in noncommutative
L^{p}
-spaces for
1<p<\infty

In
[

L^{p}
-spaces for the actions of locally compact group metric measure spaces satisfying doubling conditions and compactly generated groups of polynomial growth in noncommutative
L^{p}
-spaces for
1<p<\infty
.
Furthermore, these results are extended to the general setting of amenable groups in
[

]
.

It is worth mentioning here that both these articles deal with actions preserving a faithful, normal, semifinite trace. However, in
[
JX07

2.3.
Noncommutative Orlicz spaces.

In this section, we discuss the preliminaries of Orlicz spaces. Details can be found in
[
GL20
]
.

Let
\Phi:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be an Orlicz function, i.e., a continuous increasing and convex function satisfying
\Phi(0)=0
and
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\Phi(t)=\infty
. We say that a Orlicz function
\Phi
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
condition if there exists a constant
K>0
such that

\Phi(2t)\leq K\Phi(t),\text{ for all }t>0.

In this article we assume that
\Phi
satisfies
\Delta_{2}
-condition and we write
\Phi\in\Delta_{2}
(unless otherwise stated).

Definition 2.3
.

Given a semifinite von Neumann algebra
(M,\tau)
and an Orlicz function
\Phi
, the noncommutative Orlicz space
L^{\Phi}(M,\tau)
is defined by the collection of all
x\in L^{0}(M,\tau)
for which there exists some positive constant
\lambda>0
such that
\tau\left(\Phi(\lambda^{-1}\left|x\right|)\right)<\infty
. The space
L^{\Phi}(M)
, equipped with the norm

\displaystyle\left\|x\right\|_{\Phi}=\inf\{\lambda>0:\tau\left(\Phi\left(\lambda^{-1}\left|x\right|\right)\right)\leq 1\},

is a Banach space. Further, it is known that

\left\|x\right\|_{\Phi}=\inf\{\varepsilon>0:\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi\left(\frac{\mu_{t}(x)}{\varepsilon}\right)\leq 1\}.

Equivalently, we have
\left\|x\right\|_{\Phi}=\left\|\left(\mu_{t}(x)\right)\right\|_{\Phi}
, where
\left\|\left(\mu_{t}(x)\right)\right\|_{\Phi}
denotes the Orlicz norm of the function
[0,\infty)\ni t\rightarrow\mu_{t}(x)
.
We remark that if
\Phi\in\Delta_{2}
, then for
x\in L^{0}(M,\tau)
,
~~\,\tau(\Phi(\left|x\right|))<\infty
if and only if
x\in L^{\Phi}(M,\tau)
. We further note that
the function
\Phi_{s}(t)=t(\log(1+t))^{s}
for
s\geq 0
is an Orlicz function and it satisfies
\Delta_{2}
condition.

Following lemma will be useful in the sequel. We add a proof for plain reading.

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose
\Phi
be a Orlicz function. Suppose
x\in L^{\Phi}(M,\tau)
with
\left\|x\right\|_{\Phi}\leq 1
. Then we have the following

\tau(\Phi(x))\leq\left\|x\right\|_{\Phi}.

Proof.

We may assume
\left\|x\right\|_{\Phi}>0
. First we note that
\left\|x\right\|_{\Phi}=\left\|\left(\mu_{t}(x)\right)\right\|_{\Phi}
.
Let
(r_{n})\subseteq(\left\|x\right\|_{\Phi},\infty)
be a decreasing sequence such that

\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}r_{n}=\left\|x\right\|_{\Phi}
. Then by monotone convergence theorem it follows that

\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi\left(\frac{\mu_{t}(x)}{r_{n}}\right)dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi\left(\frac{\mu_{t}(x)}{\left\|x\right\|_{\Phi}}\right)dt

We note that
\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi\left(\frac{\mu_{t}(x)}{r_{n}}\right)dt\leq 1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore, we note that
\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi\left(\frac{\mu_{t}(x)}{\left\|x\right\|_{\Phi}}\right)dt\leq 1
. Consequently we have

\tau\left(\frac{\Phi(x)}{\left\|x\right\|_{\Phi}}\right)\leq 1.

As
\Phi
is convex and
\Phi(0)=0
, so we have
\Phi(rt)\leq r\Phi(t)
for all
0\leq r\leq 1
and
t\geq 0
. Consequently,
u\Phi(t)\leq\Phi(ut)
for
u\geq 1
and
t\geq 0
. Since
0<\left\|x\right\|_{\Phi}\leq 1
, we have

\frac{1}{\left\|x\right\|_{\Phi}}\Phi(t)\leq\Phi\left(\frac{t}{\left\|x\right\|_{\Phi}}\right).

Since
0\leq\left\|x\right\|\leq 1
, we have
\frac{1}{\left\|x\right\|_{\Phi}}\Phi(x)\leq\Phi\left(\frac{x}{\left\|x\right\|_{\Phi}}\right)
. Thus, we have

\tau(\Phi(x))\leq\left\|x\right\|_{\Phi}\tau\left(\frac{\Phi(x)}{\left\|x\right\|_{\Phi}}\right)\leq\left\|x\right\|_{\Phi}.

∎

.

\tau
. Suppose

In this section, we discuss the noncommutative vector valued

-spaces. Given

is defined as the collection of all sequences

and

for

\displaystyle\left\|x\right\|_{L^{p}(M,\ell^{\infty})}=\inf\{\left\|a\right\|_{2p}\sup_{n\geq 0}\left\|y\right\|_{\infty}\left\|b\right\|_{2p}\},

is a Banach space, where the infimum runs over all factorizations as above. It is known that for a positive sequence

if and only there is an element

such that

for all
n\geq 0
. Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\left\|x\right\|_{L^{p}(M,\ell^{\infty})}=\inf\{\left\|a\right\|_{p}:a\in L^{p}(M)_{+}\text{ such that }x_{n}\leq a,\,\,\forall n\geq 0\}.

We sometimes write
\left\|\sup_{n}^{+}x_{n}\right\|_{p}
for
\left\|x\right\|_{L^{p}(M,\ell^{\infty})}
, and these two notations will be used interchangeably.

Theorem 2.13

We prove this result only for

\epsilon>0

\delta>0

\displaystyle E_{k}=\{x\in L^{\Phi}(M):\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

\displaystyle\left\|A_{n}(x)^{1/2}b\right\|_{\infty}\leq k,\text{ for some }b\in M

\displaystyle\text{ with }0<b\leq 1\text{ and }\tau(1-b)\leq\frac{\epsilon}{4}\}.

Let
(a_{m})

(a_{m})

in
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\Phi}

in the measure topology. Then for each

, there exists a subsequence
(a_{n,m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
of

such that

in a.u.

Set
x_{m}=a_{m,m}
,

. Since
(x_{m})_{m\geq n}
is a subsequence of
(a_{n,m})_{m\geq 1}
, we have

\displaystyle A_{n}(x_{m})^{1/2}\xrightarrow{m\rightarrow\infty}A_{n}(a)^{1/2}\text{ in a.u.},\text{ for all }n\geq 1.

Since for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

, there exists

such that

(1)

0<b_{m}\leq 1

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|S_{n}(x_{m})^{1/2}b_{m}\right\|_{\infty}\leq k

Now since the unit ball

converges to an element

(3)

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|A_{n}(a)^{1/2}b\right\|_{\infty}\leq k

Note that

For

Now we show that

\displaystyle\left\|eA_{n}(a)b\right\|_{\infty}\leq k.

Let

\delta^{\prime}>0

m_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle(b_{m}-b)\xi,A_{n}(a)^{1/2}e\eta\right\rangle\right|<\delta^{\prime},\text{for all }m\geq m_{0}.

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle eA_{n}(a)^{1/2}b\xi,\eta\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\left\langle eA_{n}(a)^{1/2}(b-b_{m})\xi,\eta\right\rangle\right|+\left|\left\langle eA_{n}(a)^{1/2}b_{m}\xi,\,\eta\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\left\|A_{n}(x)^{1/2}e\right\|_{\infty}^{2}=\left\|eA_{n}(x)^{1/2}A_{n}(x)^{1/2}e\right\|_{\infty}=\left\|eA_{n}(x)e\right\|_{\infty}\leq\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|eA_{n}(x)e\right\|_{\infty}.

x\in E_{k}

(iii)

.

Theorem 3.10

(
Noncommutative Rota’s theorem
)
.

Let
(M,\tau)
be a noncommutative tracial measure space and
\Phi
be an Orlicz function. Suppose

\tau
-preserving Markov operator and

, the sequence
\big(T^{n}(T^{*})^{n}(x)\big)

Furthermore, if the function
\widetilde{\Phi}(t)=\Phi(\sqrt{t})
for

, is also
p
-convex, then for all

, the sequence
\big(T^{n}(T^{*})^{n}(x)\big)
converges in

Proof.

Firstly, note that by Theorem
3.3
, one has

\displaystyle(T^{*})^{n}T^{n}(x)=\mathbb{E}\circ\mathbb{E}_{n}(x),\quad x\in M,

where

and

In this section, our aim is to study noncommutative maximal inequalities and ergodic theorems associated to action by free group or semigroups on noncommutative spaces. Actually, we study it for more general context. As a particular case the noncommutative maximal inequalities and ergodic theorems associated to action by free group or semigroups on noncommutative spaces can be obtained. First we set the preliminary background.
We begin with the following notations. We write

(1)

[m]:=\{1,2,\cdots,m\}

(2)

[-m..m]=\{-m,-(m-1),\cdots,-1,1,2,\cdots,m\}

Suppose
I\in\{[m],[-m..m]\}
, i.e,

is either
[m]
or
[-m..m]

(1)

For simplicity of writing we also identify

, i.e, it is the collection of finite words in
I
.

We consider the shift map
\sigma:I^{\mathbb{N}}\rightarrow I^{\mathbb{N}}
which is defined as

\sigma(\omega)(n)=\omega_{n+1},\text{ for }\omega\in I^{\mathbb{N}}.

Suppose
P=(p_{ij})_{i,j\in I}
is a stochastic matrix whose rows and columns are indexed by the finite set
I
. Suppose
(p_{i})_{i\in I}
is a stationary distribution with transition matrix
P

Assume that
p_{i}>0
for all

.
Let
\mu

I^{\mathbb{N}}
with initial distribution
(p_{1},\ldots,p_{m})
and transition

P=(p_{ij})_{i,j\in I}
.
For
\omega=(\omega_{1},\omega,\cdots,\omega_{n})\in I^{F}

Suppose
\omega=i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{n}\in I^{F}

(8)

\displaystyle\mu(\omega)=\mu(i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{n})=\mu(i_{1})p_{i_{1}i_{2}}p_{i_{2}i_{3}}\cdots p_{i_{n-1}i_{n}}.

X

Let
\left|\cdot\right|:I^{F}\rightarrow I^{F}

\left|\omega\right|=n\text{ for }\omega=(\omega_{1},\omega,\cdots,\omega_{n})\in I^{F}

i.e, it counts the number of tuple.
Further, for simplicity of notation for
j\in I
, and
\omega=(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\cdots,\omega_{n})\in I^{F}

\omega j
for the element

\omega j=(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\cdots,\omega_{n},j).

Now consider the following spherical average

\displaystyle S_{n}=\sum_{\left|\omega\right|=n}\mu(\omega)\alpha_{\omega},

The following lemma is important to us.

Lemma 4.1
.

For each

(1)

Proof.

For the assertion
(1)
, note that

\displaystyle S_{n+1}^{(j)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\{\omega:~\left|\omega\right|=n+1,\omega_{n+1}=j\}}\mu(\omega)\alpha_{\omega}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\{\omega:~\left|\omega\right|=n\}}\mu(\omega j)\alpha_{j}\alpha_{\omega}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{j}\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{\{\omega:~\left|\omega\right|=n,~\omega_{n}=i\}}\mu(\omega j)\alpha_{\omega}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{j}\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{\{\omega:~\left|\omega\right|=n-1\}}\mu(\omega ij)\alpha_{\omega i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}p_{ij}\alpha_{j}S_{n}^{(i)},~(\text{as }\frac{\mu(\omega ij)}{\mu(\omega i)}=p_{ij},\text{ see Eq. }\ref{stocha}).

(1)

Following

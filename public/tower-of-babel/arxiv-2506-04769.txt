Lipschitz stability for Bayesian inference in porous medium tissue growth models
Tomasz Dębiec, Piotr Gwiazda, Błażej Miasojedow, Katarzyna Ryszewska, Zuzanna Szymańska, Aneta Wróblewska-Kamińska

Abstract
We consider a macroscopic model for the dynamics of living tissues incorporating pressure-driven dispersal and pressure-modulated proliferation. Given a power-law constitutive relation between the pressure and cell density, the model can be written as a porous medium equation with a growth term. We prove Lipschitz continuity of the mild solutions of the model with respect to the diffusion parameter (the exponent γ \gamma in the pressure-density law) in the L 1 L_{1} norm. While of independent analytical interest, our motivation for this result is to provide a vital step towards using Bayesian inverse problem methodology for parameter estimation based on experimental data – such stability estimates are indispensable for applying sampling algorithms which rely on the gradient of the likelihood function.

Introduction and main results

Mathematical modelling plays a crucial role in understanding the complex dynamics of tumour growth. Among the various modeling approaches, macroscopic density-based partial differential equations provide a powerful framework for describing the evolution of tumour cell populations over time and space
[
2
,
9
,
11
]
. These models often incorporate mechanical effects by relating tumour cell density to pressure through constitutive relations inspired by fluid and porous media mechanics. One widely used class of models assumes a nonlinear relation between the pressure and density, where the pressure follows a power-law dependence on the density with an exponent
\gamma
, leading to porous-medium type equations with a pressure-dependent proliferation term
[
21
]
. The macroscopic density of cells,
u=u(t,x)
, is assumed to satisfy the following equation

\partial_{t}u+\operatorname{div}(uv)=uG(p),

where the velocity obeys Darcy’s law
v=-\nabla p
and the pressure satisfies

p=\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}u^{\gamma-1}

for some
\gamma>1
. The reaction rate
G
is a given function, whose properties will be specified below.
Substituting the velocity law into the equation, it can be rewritten as a porous medium type equation:

\partial_{t}u=\Delta(u^{\gamma})+uG(p).

(1)

The diffusion exponent
\gamma
characterises the pattern of tumour expansion, reflecting both the speed and morphology of the evolving tumour boundary.

For these models to be effectively applied in clinical or biological settings, it is essential to calibrate them against observed data. Experimental measurements such as tumour morphology, expansion rates, and imaging data provide valuable information about tumour progression. However, the constitutive relation parameter, which governs the tumour’s mechanical response, is often unknown and varies across different tumour types and conditions. Therefore, inferring the value of
\gamma
from observational data is crucial to ensuring that the model accurately reflects real biological behavior. Traditional calibration approaches rely on direct fitting techniques or optimization-based inverse problems, but these methods can be sensitive to noise and may not adequately capture uncertainties in the inferred parameters.

The observed data set being finite and noisy, we face an underdetermined, and often ill-posed, inverse problem, which we would like to address using the framework of Bayesian statistics
[
23
]
. To this end, we shall investigate the stability of problem (
1
) with respect to the parameter
\gamma
.
The Bayesian approach provides a probabilistic characterization of uncertainty, allowing us to quantify the confidence in the inferred values of the model parameters and incorporate prior knowledge about their plausible range. By leveraging observed tumour density distributions at different time points, one can construct a posterior distribution for
\gamma
, which reflects both the likelihood of the data given a specific model realisation and the prior assumptions on the parameter.
More precisely, Bayesian inference is based on posterior distribution together with the conditional distribution of parameters given the observed data, according to the formula

\pi(\gamma|D)=\frac{\pi(D|\gamma)\pi(\gamma)}{\int_{\Gamma}\pi(D|\gamma)\;\mathrm{d}\pi(\gamma)},

(2)

where
D
denotes the collected data,
\gamma
is a given parameter, or more generally a vector of parameters, taking values in the parameter space
\Gamma
. The posterior
\pi(\gamma|D)
is the probability density of state
\gamma
given data
D
,
\pi(\gamma)
is the prior probability density, i.e., the probability density of the values
\gamma
without any knowledge of the data, and
\pi(D|\gamma)
is the likelihood function, which quantifies the probability of observing data
D
when the parameters take values
\gamma
. Unsurprisingly, it is practically impossible to obtain a closed analytical formula for the posterior distribution, and hence sampling methods have to be employed. We refer the reader to the recent works
[
7
,
8
,
12
,
14
,
17
,
24
]
for examples of the use of the Bayesian inverse problem framework for models arising in tumour modelling. In particular, in
[
8
]
the authors analyse the same model (
1
) and study the data assimilation problem to estimate the initial condition and the proliferation rate – however, assuming that the diffusion parameter
\gamma
is known.

A widely used method for sampling from complex posterior distributions in Bayesian inference is the Metropolis-Hastings Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) algorithm
[
10
,
13
,
19
]
. It is based on generating a Markov chain whose stationary distribution corresponds to the target posterior, allowing for approximations of expected values and credible intervals. The method involves proposing candidate samples based on a chosen transition kernel and accepting or rejecting these samples based on an acceptance ratio derived from the posterior probability. While powerful, Metropolis-Hastings MCMC can suffer from slow convergence and high autocorrelation, particularly when sampling high-dimensional or strongly correlated distributions. As such, the convergence of the algorithm may actually fail in practice, given a reasonable computation time.
Additionally, the efficiency of the algorithm is highly sensitive to the choice of proposal distribution, making parameter tuning a critical challenge.

To address some of the limitations of Metropolis-Hastings MCMC, the Langevin-based approach incorporates gradient information into the proposal step by simulating the Langevin equation from stochastic calculus:

\mathrm{d}\gamma(t)=-\nabla V(\gamma(t))\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{d}B(t),

where
B
is the standard Brownian motion and
V=-\log\pi(D|\gamma)-\log\pi(\gamma)
is the negative log-posterior (acting like an energy function). The Metropolis-Adjusted Langevin Algorithm (MALA) modifies the standard random walk proposal by incorporating the gradient of the posterior density, thereby guiding the Markov chain toward high-probability regions more efficiently. This results in improved convergence rates and reduced autocorrelation, making the approach particularly useful for Bayesian inference problems where gradients can be computed efficiently
[
22
]
. In the context of tumour growth modeling, MALA can significantly enhance the efficiency of Bayesian inversion for
\gamma
, allowing for more accurate and computationally feasible parameter inference.

To understand the overall evolution of the probability density, whose individual sample paths are described by the Langevin equation, one studies the Fokker-Planck equation:

\partial_{t}\pi(\gamma,t)=\operatorname{div}(\pi(\gamma,t)\nabla V(\gamma))+\Delta\pi(\gamma,t).

Crucially, the steady state of this equation corresponds (up to normalisation constant) to the posterior distribution:

e^{-V(\gamma)}=\pi(D|\gamma)\pi(\gamma).

Since the solutions of the Fokker-Planck equation do converge to the equilibrium state, this implies that if we run the Langevin dynamics for a sufficiently long time, the probability density of samples will converge to the true posterior distribution. Let us mention that other deterministic PDE models can be considered, each having their own advantages – for instance, the blob method for a nonlocal approximation of the Fokker-Planck equation
[
3
]
, or the Stein variational gradient descent method
[
4
,
15
,
18
]
.

A crucial aspect of implementing Bayesian inference for the exponent
\gamma
is obtaining a quantitative estimate of the stability of the underlying PDE model (
1
) with respect to the diffusion exponent.
If small variations in
\gamma
lead to significant changes in the solution of the PDE, the inference process may be highly sensitive to noise in the observational data. Conversely, if the model exhibits robustness to variations in
\gamma
, the inferred posterior distribution will be more stable and reliable. By analyzing the stability properties of the model, we can ensure that the Bayesian inversion framework provides meaningful and well-posed estimates of
\gamma
, ultimately leading to more robust predictions of tumour growth dynamics.
More precisely, a common feature of the above PDE methods for sampling algorithms is that they are defined through the gradient of the likelihood function. For the likelihood functions which are not
C^{1}
, or at least Lipschitz, we can apply some smoothed variant of the method, but at the expense of generating an additional error, and ultimately resulting in a significantly slower rate of convergence. It is therefore our main goal in this paper to establish an explicit Lipschitz continuous dependence of the solutions of (
1
) on the exponent
\gamma
. Naturally, from the practical viewpoint, it is of equal interest to estimates other parameters in the model (e.g., in the proliferation rate). We focus here solely on the exponent
\gamma
as it is the most analytically challenging.

Moreover, we have to bear in mind that in practice the posterior distribution
\pi(\gamma|D)
is not calculated from exact solutions of the PDE evolving from exactly given data. Rather, it is approximated using numerical solutions, while the data is subject to measurement uncertainties. The latter is taken into account in the model of the data, i.e., we assume that
the tumour mass is known at a finite set of disjoint spatial locations
\{Q_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}
at a discrete set of time points
\{t_{j}\}_{j=1}^{M}
. In particular, we are given the operators

m_{kj}:u\mapsto m_{kj}(u),\quad m_{kj}(u)=\int_{Q_{k}}u(t_{j},x)\;\mathrm{d}x.

The observed data is then modelled as

y=\mathcal{M}(\gamma)+\eta,

where
\mathcal{M}:\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}^{MN}_{+}
is the forward operator obtained from the composition of the solution operator
\gamma\mapsto u_{\gamma}
to (
1
) with the matrix-valued operator
m=(m_{kj})
mapping
u_{\gamma}\mapsto m(u)
; and
\eta\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma)
is an observational noise, distributed normally with a give covariance matrix
\Sigma
. Assuming a Gaussian prior distribution for the parameter
\gamma\sim\mathcal{N}(m_{0},\sigma_{0}^{2})
, the posterior distribution is given by

\pi(\gamma|D)\propto e^{-V(\gamma)},\quad V(\gamma)=\frac{1}{2}|y-\mathcal{M}(\gamma)|_{\Sigma}^{2}+\frac{|\gamma-m_{0}|^{2}}{2\sigma_{0}^{2}}.

(3)

The former term represents the likelihood of observing data
y
given the value
\gamma
of the parameter. It penalises deviations of the model prediction from the actual observations, scaled by the noise covarriance
\gamma
,
where
|y-\mathcal{M}(\gamma)|_{\Sigma}^{2}=(y-\mathcal{M}(\gamma))^{T}\Sigma^{-1}(y-\mathcal{M}(\gamma))
. The latter term represents the prior knowledge about
\gamma
and penalises its deviations from the mean
m_{0}
scaled by the prior variance
\sigma_{0}^{2}
.

1.1
Main results and assumptions

As outlined above, our main concern in this paper is to establish Lipschitz continuity of the solutions to (
1
) with respect to the diffusion parameter
\gamma
. More specifically, we will work with mild solutions in the
L^{1}
-topology.
Our main result for equation (

) reads, somewhat informally, as follows.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma\subset(1,\infty)
and let
u_{1},u_{2}
be the corresponding mild solutions to equation (
1
) in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T)

\displaystyle\left\|{u_{1}(t)-u_{2}(t)}\right\|_{L_{1}}\leq e^{tG(0)}\left\|{u^{0}_{1}-u_{2}^{0}}\right\|_{L_{1}}+C_{1}te^{tG(0)}\left\|{G_{2}-G_{1}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}+C_{2}(\sqrt{dt}+t)e^{tG(0)}|\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1}|,

for any

, where the constants
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
depend on
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
and the initial data; and
G(0):=\max{(G_{1}(0),G_{2}(0))}
.

In fact, we shall first establish a more general stability result for an abstract degenerate parabolic filtration equation with growth and then deduce the above theorem as a special case – see Theorem
3
and Corollary
1
in Section
4
for precise statements.
To this end, we discuss the following problem:

\partial_{t}u=\Delta\varphi(u)+(u)_{+}G(p(u))\hskip 5.69046pt\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T),\hskip 5.69046ptu(0)=u^{0}\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d},

(4)

under the following assumptions

\varphi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}),\hskip 5.69046pt\varphi(0)=0,\hskip 5.69046pt\varphi\mbox{ is increasing},

(5)

G\in C([0,\infty))\cap C^{1}((0,\infty)),\hskip 5.69046ptG(0)>0,\hskip 5.69046ptG^{\prime}\leq 0\mbox{ on }(0,\infty),

(6)

p\in C(\mathbb{R}),\hskip 5.69046pt\hskip 5.69046ptp\mbox{ is nonnegative and nondecreasing}.

(7)

Here, and throughout,
(u)_{+}=\max(0,u)
denotes the positive part of a function.
We are interested in the stability properties with respect to given functions
\varphi,G
, and

for mild solutions to (

Then, we apply the obtained result to the special case

\varphi_{i}(t)=\operatorname{sign}(t)|t|^{\gamma_{i}},\hskip 5.69046ptp_{i}(t)=\frac{\gamma_{i}}{\gamma_{i}-1}|t|^{\gamma_{i}-1},\hskip 5.69046pt\mbox{ for }i=1,2,\hskip 5.69046pt\gamma_{2}>\gamma_{1}>1,

to obtain Lipschitz continuity of mild solutions to (

) in

- norm with respect to parameter

, as stated in Theorem
1
.

The mild solutions to equation (
4
) can be constructed, for instance, via the implicit time discretisation scheme:

u(t)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left[\left(I+\frac{t}{n}A\right)^{-1}\right]^{n}u^{0},

where
A(u):=-\Delta\varphi(u)-(u)_{+}G(p(u))
. We refer the reader to
[
20
]
and
[
25
, Chapter 10]
for detailed discussions. As mentioned previously, an important source of error in calculating the posterior distribution
\pi(\gamma|D)
comes from it being computed from an approximate solution, for instance, the implicit time-discretised approximation, rather than the true analytical solution of the PDE. Therefore, it is desirable that the numerical scheme satisfies the same stability estimate as the analytical solutions of the equation.
To this end, we formulate a suitable stability estimate, which arises as a natural by-product of the proof of Theorem
3
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}>1
and for
i=1,2
let

u_{i}^{n}
be the piecewise constant function

u_{i}^{n}(t)=u_{i}^{n}\quad\text{for $t\in[n\tau,(n+1)\tau)$},

where

u^{n}_{i}=(I+\tau A)^{-1}u_{i}^{n-1},\quad\text{and}\quad u_{i}^{0}=u_{i}^{0}.

Then, the same estimate as in Theorem
1
holds for the difference
u_{1}^{n}(t)-u_{2}^{n}(t)
.

Since the functions
u^{n}
converge as

to the unique mild solution of (
1
) strongly in

, we can compare the posterior
\pi^{n}(\gamma|D)
arising from a numerical approximation and the one arising from the analytical solution. Indeed, using the forward operator
\mathcal{M}
as in (
3
) and the form of the operator

, it easily follows that

converges to

in

Lemma 2
.

(Stability of the posterior distribution)
Suppose that the parameter space
\Gamma
is bounded. The following estimate holds

\left\|{\pi(\gamma|D)-\pi^{n}(\gamma|D)}\right\|_{TV}\leq C\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}.

(8)

Proof.

Using formula (
2
) we obtain the following estimate

\displaystyle\left\|{\pi(\gamma|D)-\pi^{n}(\gamma|D)}\right\|_{TV}\leq 2\frac{\int_{\Gamma}|\pi(D|\gamma)-\pi^{n}(D|\gamma)|\;\mathrm{d}\pi(\gamma)}{\int_{\Gamma}\pi(D|\gamma)\;\mathrm{d}\pi(\gamma)}.

(9)

Next, from (
3
) we deduce that the likelihood function potential is bounded both from below and from above. Finally, we observe that

|V(\gamma)-V^{n}(\gamma)|\leq C\sum_{i,j}|m_{ij}(u_{\gamma})-m_{ij}(u^{n}_{\gamma})|\leq C\left\|{u-u^{n}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}},

(10)

where the last inequality follows from Theorem
2
, cf.
[
6
]
, which provides the explicit rate for the convergence

, and Proposition

, which guarantees that the operator
A(u)=-\Delta\varphi(u)-(u)_{+}G(p(u))
is

-accretive and satisfies the assumptions of Theorem

. Consequently, since
\pi(\gamma)
is a probability measure, from (
9
) and (
10
) we deduce (
8
).
∎

Structure of the paper

The rest of the paper is devoted to proving Theorem
1
and is organised as follows. In Section
2
we regularise problem (
4
) by considering a smoother function
\varphi
. In this setting we analyse the resolvent operator

In Section

we translate the key results of Section

to the resolvent operator of problem (

) without any regularisations. This is achieved by constructing an appropriate mollification of the function
\varphi
. In particular, we show that the operator

is
G(0)
-accretive and that the range of

is the entire space

for

sufficiently small.
This guarantees that the equation

has a unique mild solution for each given initial condition

we prove the main stability result for the mild solutions of equation (

Let us point out that this strategy is largely inspired by the similar approach of Cockburn and Gripenberg in
[

]
, who consider an abstract degenerate diffusion problem as in (

\left\|{u_{1}-u_{2}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\frac{1}{1-\tau G(0)}\left\|{f_{1}-f_{2}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

and thus (
13
) is proven. Estimate (
14
) follows from (
13
). Indeed, denoting the translation operator by
T_{y}
, i.e.,
(T_{y}f)(x)=f(x+y)
, we have

\left\|{T_{y}(I+\tau A)^{-1}f-(I+\tau A)^{-1}f}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\left\|{(I+\tau A)^{-1}T_{y}f-(I+\tau A)^{-1}f}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\leq\frac{1}{1-\tau G(0)}\left\|{T_{y}f-f}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

and (
14
) follows from the characterisation of the
TV
-norm.
Furthermore, from the properties of
q^{\varepsilon}
we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}q^{\varepsilon}(x-y)dy=1,\hskip 5.69046pt\text{and}\hskip 5.69046pt\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|{q^{\varepsilon}_{x_{i}}(x-y)}\right|dy=\frac{2}{\varepsilon},\hskip 5.69046pti=1,\dots,d.

Since (
14
) implies that if
f_{1}\in BV(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then also
u_{1}\in BV(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the inequality (
29
) for
f_{1}\in BV(\mathbb{R}^{d})
leads to

(1-\tau G(0))\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|{u_{1}-u_{2}}\right|q^{\varepsilon}(x-y)dydx

\leq\frac{2d\tau}{\varepsilon}\sup_{s\in I(f_{1})}\left[\sqrt{\varphi^{\prime}_{1}(s)}-\sqrt{\varphi^{\prime}_{2}(s)}\right]^{2}\left\|{u_{1}}\right\|_{TV(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|{f_{1}-f_{2}}\right|q^{\varepsilon}(x-y)dydx

+\tau\left(\left\|{G^{\prime}_{2}}\right\|_{L_{\infty}(J_{p_{1},p_{2}}(u_{1}))}\sup_{s\in I(f_{1})}\left|{p_{2}(s)-p_{1}(s)}\right|+\left\|{G_{2}-G_{1}}\right\|_{L_{\infty}(J_{p_{1}}(u_{1}))}\right)\left\|{u_{1}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Now, we extend the intervals
J_{p_{1}},J_{p_{1},p_{2}}
to
I_{p_{1}},I_{p_{1},p_{2}}
applying Proposition
1
, and the fact that
p_{i}
are nondecreasing. Finally, due to estimate (
14
), we arrive at (
16
).
∎

3
Existence and approximability of solutions to the resolvent equation

In this section we show that the solution to resolvent equation
u=(I+\tau A)^{-1}f
, where
f\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
Au=-\Delta\varphi(u)-(u)_{+}G(p(u))
with
\varphi,G,p
satisfying merely (
5
) - (
7
) may be nicely approximated by solutions to the regularized problem
u_{n}=(1+\tau A_{n})^{-1}f_{n}
and the results established in Proposition
2
for a regularized setting may be transferred to our initial setting. Let us begin with the following
L_{1}
-solvability result, which fits within the scope of the classical paper
[
1
]
.

Proposition 3
.

Let
\varphi
,
G
,
p
satisfy (
5
)-(
7
) and let
\tau\in(0,\frac{1}{G(0)})
. For every
f\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
there exists exactly one
u\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
which satisfies

u-\tau\Delta\varphi(u)-\tau(u)_{+}G(p(u))=f,

(30)

in the sense of distributions. Furthermore,

\left\|{\varphi(u)}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\left\|{f}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\hskip 5.69046pt\left\|{u}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\frac{1}{1-\tau G(0)}\left\|{f}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Proof.

Let
\varphi
satisfy (
5
) and
\tau\in(0,\frac{1}{G(0)})
be fixed. We discuss the problem

-\tau\Delta v+H(v)=f,

(31)

with

H(r)=\varphi^{-1}(r)-\tau(\varphi^{-1}(r))_{+}G(p(\varphi^{-1}(r)))\mbox{ for }r\in\mathbb{R}.

Clearly,
H(0)=0
and
H
is increasing, since
\varphi^{-1}
is increasing,
G
is nonincreasing and
p
is nondecreasing. Furthermore, for
\left|{r}\right|<1

\left|{H(r)}\right|\geq(1-\tau G(0))\left|{\varphi^{-1}(r)}\right|\geq\frac{(1-\tau G(0))}{\left\|{\varphi^{\prime}}\right\|_{L_{\infty}([-1,1])}}|r|.

Hence, from
[
1
, Theorem 2.1, Theorem 5.1]
we obtain that for every
f\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
there exists exactly one
v\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
which satisfies (
31
) in the sense of distributions. Furthermore,

\left\|{v}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\left\|{f}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\hskip 5.69046pt\left\|{H(v)}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\left\|{f}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

(32)

Then, the function
u:=\varphi^{-1}(v)
satisfies (
30
). Since

\left\|{H(v)}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\left\|{u-\tau(u)_{+}G(p(u))}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

estimates (
32
) imply

\left\|{\varphi(u)}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\left\|{f}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\hskip 5.69046pt\left\|{u}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\frac{1}{1-\tau G(0)}\left\|{f}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.\qed

We will now show that one may approximate the
L_{1}
solutions to (
30
) by the solutions to a regularized problem.
To this end, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
let us define

\varphi_{n}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}(\eta_{\frac{1}{n}}*\varphi^{\prime})(s)ds+\frac{t}{n},

(33)

where
\eta_{1/n}
denotes the standard smoothing kernel at scale
1/n
. Then
\varphi_{n}
satisfies (
5
) and
\varphi_{n}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
,
\varphi_{n}^{\prime}>0
. Furthermore,
\varphi^{\prime}_{n}
converges to
\varphi^{\prime}
uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}
. We denote
A_{n}:=A_{\varphi_{n},G,p}
.

Proposition 4
.

Let
f\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and let
u
be the unique solution of (
30
) associated to
f
. There exists a sequence
\{f_{n}\}\subset L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap BV(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
f_{n}\to f
and
u_{n}\to u
in
L_{1}
, where
u_{n}:=(I-\tau A_{n})^{-1}f_{n}\in D(A_{n})
.

Proof.

Let us define
f_{m}
as a truncation of
f
on the level
m
, i.e.
f_{m}=f\chi_{\{|f|\leq m\}}+m\operatorname{sign}{f}\chi_{\{|f|>m\}}
. Then
f_{m}\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Further, we define
f_{m,n}:=\eta_{1/n}*f_{m}
. Then

\left\|{f_{m,n}}\right\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\left\|{\eta_{1/n}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left\|{f_{m}}\right\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq m,

\left\|{f_{m,n}}\right\|_{TV(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\left\|{f_{m}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left\|{\nabla\eta_{1/n}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq n\left\|{\nabla\eta}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left\|{f}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

(34)

We will now choose the index
m
as a function of
n
, i.e,
m=m(n)
, and we denote
f_{m,n}
as
f_{n}
.
By Proposition
1
and Proposition
2
, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a unique
u_{n}\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\varphi_{n}(u_{n})\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(note that the uniqueness comes from (
13
)), such that

u_{n}-\tau\Delta\varphi_{n}(u_{n})-\tau(u_{n})_{+}G(p(u_{n}))=f_{n}.

(35)

We will show that
u_{n}
is a Cauchy sequence. Estimate (
16
) gives

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|{u_{n}-u_{k}}\right|q^{\varepsilon}(x-y)dydx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1-\tau G(0)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|{f_{n}-f_{k}}\right|q^{\varepsilon}(x-y)dydx

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{(1-\tau G(0))^{2}}\frac{2d\tau}{\varepsilon}\sup_{s\in I(f_{n})}\left[\sqrt{\varphi^{\prime}_{n}(s)}-\sqrt{\varphi^{\prime}_{k}(s)}\right]^{2}\left\|{f_{n}}\right\|_{TV(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Applying estimate (
14
) and

\left|{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|{f(x)-g(y)}\right|q^{\varepsilon}(x-y)dydx-\left\|{f-g}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}\right|\leq\varepsilon\left\|{f}\right\|_{TV(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

we arrive at

\displaystyle\left\|{u_{n}-u_{k}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\varepsilon}{1-\tau G(0)}\left\|{f_{n}}\right\|_{TV(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\frac{1}{1-\tau G(0)}\left\|{f_{n}-f_{k}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{(1-\tau G(0))^{2}}\frac{2d\tau}{\varepsilon}\sup_{s\in I(f_{n})}\left[\sqrt{\varphi^{\prime}_{n}(s)}-\sqrt{\varphi^{\prime}_{k}(s)}\right]^{2}\left\|{f_{n}}\right\|_{TV(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Choosing
\varepsilon=\sqrt{d\tau}\sup_{s\in I(f_{n})}\left|\sqrt{\varphi^{\prime}_{n}(s)}-\sqrt{\varphi^{\prime}_{k}(s)}\right|
we get

\left\|{u_{n}-u_{k}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\frac{1}{1-\tau G(0)}\left\|{f_{n}-f_{k}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\frac{4}{(1-\tau G(0))^{2}}\sqrt{d\tau}\sup_{s\in I(f_{n})}\left|\sqrt{\varphi^{\prime}_{n}(s)}-\sqrt{\varphi^{\prime}_{k}(s)}\right|\left\|{f_{n}}\right\|_{TV(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

In view of (
34
) there holds

\sup_{s\in I(f_{n})}\left|\sqrt{\varphi^{\prime}_{n}(s)}-\sqrt{\varphi^{\prime}_{k}(s)}\right|\left\|{f_{n}}\right\|_{TV(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq n\left\|{\nabla\eta}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left\|{f}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\sup_{s\in[-m,\frac{m}{1-\tau G(0)}]}\left|\sqrt{\varphi^{\prime}_{n}(s)}-\sqrt{\varphi^{\prime}_{k}(s)}\right|.

Uniform convergence of
\varphi^{\prime}_{n}
on compact subsets implies that

\forall n>0\hskip 5.69046pt\forall m>0\hskip 5.69046pt\exists k=k(n,m)\hskip 5.69046pt\sup_{\tilde{k}\geq k}\sup_{s\in[-m,\frac{m}{1-\tau G(0)}]}\left|\sqrt{\varphi^{\prime}_{k}(s)}-\sqrt{\varphi^{\prime}_{\tilde{k}}(s)}\right|\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}}.

Since we may choose
k
as an increasing function of
m
, we may also invert it to obtain

\forall n>0\hskip 5.69046pt\hskip 5.69046pt\forall k\hskip 5.69046pt\exists m=m(n,k)\hskip 5.69046pt\sup_{\tilde{k}\geq k}\sup_{s\in[-m,\frac{m}{1-\tau G(0)}]}\left|\sqrt{\varphi^{\prime}_{k}(s)}-\sqrt{\varphi^{\prime}_{\tilde{k}}(s)}\right|\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}}.

Choosing

we arrive at

\forall n\hskip 5.69046pt\exists m=m(n)\hskip 5.69046pt\sup_{\tilde{k}\geq n}\sup_{s\in[-m,\frac{m}{1-\tau G(0)}]}\left|\sqrt{\varphi^{\prime}_{n}(s)}-\sqrt{\varphi^{\prime}_{\tilde{k}}(s)}\right|\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}}.

Hence, for such a choice of
f_{n}
,

is a Cauchy sequence in
L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Therefore, there exists a function
\tilde{u}\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

in
L_{1}
and, passing to a subsequence, we may assume that
u_{n}\rightarrow\tilde{u}
almost everywhere. Since
G
and
p
are continuous,
G(p(u_{n}))\rightarrow G(p(\tilde{u}))
almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Since
G(p(u_{n}))
is bounded, it has a weakly-star convergent subsequence in

. We deduce that
(u_{n})_{+}G(p(u_{n}))\rightharpoonup(\tilde{u})_{+}G(p(\tilde{u}))
in
L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Consequently, from (
35

\Delta\varphi_{n}(u_{n})
converges weakly in
L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
We will show that it converges to
\Delta\varphi(\tilde{u})
, where the Laplacian is understood in the sense of distributions. Recall that
\varphi_{n}
converges to
\varphi
pointwise and uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}
. Since
u_{n}\rightarrow\tilde{u}
almost everywhere and
\varphi
is continuous, we have
\varphi(u_{n})\rightarrow\varphi(\tilde{u})
almost everywhere. Moreover,

\left|{\varphi_{n}(u_{n}(x))-\varphi(u_{n}(x))}\right|\leq\sup_{t\in I(f_{n})}\left|{\varphi_{n}(t)-\varphi(t)}\right|.

Thus, for a.e.

,

\left|{\varphi_{n}(u_{n}(x))-\varphi(\tilde{u}(x))}\right|\leq\left|{\varphi_{n}(u_{n}(x))-\varphi(u_{n}(x))}\right|+\left|{\varphi(u_{n}(x))-\varphi(\tilde{u}(x))}\right|\rightarrow 0\hskip 5.69046pt

and
\varphi_{n}(u_{n})\rightarrow\varphi(\tilde{u})
a.e.
Finally, for any
\Phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi_{n}(u_{n})\Delta\Phi dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{n}\Phi dx-\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(u)_{+}G(p(u))\Phi dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f\Phi dx,

and the right-hand side converges, so also the left-hand side is convergent and from the pointwise limit we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi_{n}(u_{n})\Delta\Phi dx\rightarrow\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(\tilde{u})\Delta\Phi dx.

Thus, from the uniqueness of the limit
\Delta\varphi_{n}(u_{n})
converges weakly in
L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to
\Delta\varphi(\tilde{u})
. Hence,
\tilde{u}
satisfies (
30
) in the sense of distributions and from the uniqueness of the solution
\tilde{u}=u
.
∎

4
Stability results

We are now ready to establish the main results of this paper. Let us first recall the classical theorem of Crandall-Liggett

[

\left\|{u(t)-(I+\frac{t}{n}A)^{-n}u_{0}}\right\|_{X}\leq\frac{t}{\sqrt{n}}\left\|{Au_{0}}\right\|.

Note that Proposition
5

in

, namely

\left\|{u_{1,n}-u_{2,n}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq e^{n\tau G(0)}\left\|{u^{0}_{1}-u^{0}_{2}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+4\sqrt{dn\tau}e^{n\tau G(0)}\left\|{u^{0}_{1}}\right\|_{TV(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\sup_{s\in I^{n\tau}(u^{0}_{1})}\left|\sqrt{\varphi^{\prime}_{1}(s)}-\sqrt{\varphi^{\prime}_{2}(s)}\right|

+n\tau e^{n\tau G(0)}\left\|{G^{\prime}_{2}}\right\|_{L_{\infty}(I_{p_{1},p_{2}}^{n\tau}(u^{0}_{1}))}\sup_{s\in I^{n\tau}(u^{0}_{1})}\left|{p_{2}(s)-p_{1}(s)}\right|\left\|{u^{0}_{1}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+n\tau e^{n\tau G(0)}\left\|{G_{2}-G_{1}}\right\|_{L_{\infty}(I_{p_{1}}^{n\tau}(u^{0}_{1}))}\left\|{u^{0}_{1}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

(38)

Finally, for
u^{0}_{i}\in D(A)
as in (
36
) we have

\left\|{u_{i}(t)-u_{i,n}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\frac{t}{\sqrt{n}}\left\|{Au_{0}^{i}}\right\|.

(39)

Proof.

Estimate (
39
) follows from the Crandall-Liggett formula (Theorem
2
). To show the stability result, let us begin with the discretisation. We set

u^{n}_{i}(t)=u_{i,n}\mbox{ for }t\in[n\tau,(n+1)\tau),\ n~\in~\mathbb{N}

where
u_{i,n}=(I+\tau A_{\varphi_{i},G_{i},p_{i}})^{-1}u_{i,n-1}\mbox{ and }u_{i,0}=u^{0}_{i},\mbox{ for }i=1,2.

Then
u^{n}_{i}(n\tau)=(I+\tau A)^{-1}u^{n}_{i}((n-1)\tau)
. For
t\in(0,T]
we choose
\tau\in(0,\frac{1}{G(0)})
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
t=n\tau
. We apply (
16
) to obtain

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|{u^{n}_{1}(n\tau,x)-u^{n}_{2}(n\tau,y)}\right|q^{\varepsilon}(x-y)dydx

\leq\frac{1}{1-\tau G(0)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|{u^{n}_{1}((n-1)\tau,x)-u^{n}_{2}((n-1)\tau,y)}\right|q^{\varepsilon}(x-y)dydx

+\frac{1}{(1-\tau G(0))^{2}}\frac{2d\tau}{\varepsilon}\sup_{s\in I(u_{1}^{n}((n-1)\tau))}\left[\sqrt{\varphi^{\prime}_{1}(s)}-\sqrt{\varphi^{\prime}_{2}(s)}\right]^{2}\left\|{u^{n}_{1}((n-1)\tau)}\right\|_{TV(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

+\frac{\tau\left\|{u^{n}_{1}((n-1)\tau)}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{(1-\tau G(0))^{2}}\left(\left\|{G^{\prime}_{2}}\right\|_{L_{\infty}(I_{p_{1},p_{2}}(u_{1}^{n}((n-1)\tau)))}\sup_{s\in I(u_{1}^{n}((n-1)\tau))}\left|{p_{2}(s)-p_{1}(s)}\right|+\left\|{G_{2}-G_{1}}\right\|_{L_{\infty}(I_{p_{1}}(u_{1}^{n}((n-1)\tau)))}\right).

(40)

Proposition
5
implies

\left\|{u^{n}_{1}((n-1)\tau)}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq(1-\tau G(0))^{-(n-1)}\left\|{u^{0}_{1}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\hskip 5.69046pt\left\|{(u^{n}_{1}((n-1)\tau))_{+}}\right\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\leq(1-\tau G(0))^{-(n-1)}\left\|{(u^{0}_{1})_{+}}\right\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

as well as

\left\|{u^{n}_{1}((n-1)\tau)}\right\|_{TV(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq(1-\tau G(0))^{-(n-1)}\left\|{u^{0}_{1}}\right\|_{TV(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Using these bounds in (
40
), iterating the resulting inequality and denoting

I^{n}(u^{0}_{1}):=\left[-\left\|{(u^{0}_{1})_{-}}\right\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\frac{1}{(1-\tau G(0))^{n}}\left\|{(u^{0}_{1})_{+}}\right\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right],

I^{n}_{p_{1},p_{2}}(u^{0}_{1}):=\left[\min_{i\in\{1,2\}}p_{i}\left(-\left\|{(u^{0}_{1})_{-}}\right\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right),\max_{i\in\{1,2\}}p_{i}\left(\frac{\left\|{(u^{0}_{1})_{+}}\right\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{(1-\tau G(0))^{n}}\right)\right],

I^{n}_{p_{i}}(u^{0}_{1}):=\left[p_{i}\left(-\left\|{(u^{0}_{1})_{-}}\right\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right),p_{i}\left(\frac{\left\|{(u^{0}_{1})_{+}}\right\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{(1-\tau G(0))^{n}}\right)\right],\hskip 5.69046pti=1,2,

leads to

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|{u^{n}_{1}(n\tau,x)-u^{n}_{2}(n\tau,y)}\right|q^{\varepsilon}(x-y)dydx

\leq(1-\tau G(0))^{-n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|{u^{0}_{1}(x)-u^{0}_{2}(y)}\right|q^{\varepsilon}(x-y)dydx

+(1-\tau G(0))^{-(n+1)}\frac{2dn\tau}{\varepsilon}\sup_{s\in I^{n}(u^{0}_{1})}\left[\sqrt{\varphi^{\prime}_{1}(s)}-\sqrt{\varphi^{\prime}_{2}(s)}\right]^{2}\left\|{u^{0}_{1}}\right\|_{TV(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

+n\tau{(1-\tau G(0))^{-(n+1)}}\left(\left\|{G^{\prime}_{2}}\right\|_{L_{\infty}(I^{n}_{p_{1},p_{2}}(u^{0}_{n}))}\sup_{s\in I^{n}(u^{0}_{1})}\left|{p_{2}(s)-p_{1}(s)}\right|+\left\|{G_{2}-G_{1}}\right\|_{L_{\infty}(I^{n}_{p_{1}}(u^{0}_{1}))}\right)\left\|{u^{0}_{1}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Applying the estimate

\left|{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|{f(x)-g(y)}\right|q^{\varepsilon}(x-y)dydx-\left\|{f-g}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}\right|\leq\varepsilon\left\|{f}\right\|_{TV(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

we get

\left\|{u^{n}_{1}(n\tau)-u^{n}_{2}(n\tau)}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq 2\varepsilon(1-\tau G(0))^{-n}\left\|{u_{1}^{0}}\right\|_{TV(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+(1-\tau G(0))^{-n}\left\|{u^{0}_{1}-u^{0}_{2}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

+(1-\tau G(0))^{-(n+1)}\frac{2dn\tau}{\varepsilon}\left\|{u^{0}_{1}}\right\|_{TV(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\sup_{s\in I^{n}(u^{0}_{1})}\left[\sqrt{\varphi^{\prime}_{1}(s)}-\sqrt{\varphi^{\prime}_{2}(s)}\right]^{2}

+n\tau{(1-\tau G(0))^{-(n+1)}}\left(\left\|{G^{\prime}_{2}}\right\|_{L_{\infty}(I^{n}_{p_{1},p_{2}}(u^{0}_{n}))}\sup_{s\in I^{n}(u^{0}_{1})}\left|{p_{2}(s)-p_{1}(s)}\right|+\left\|{G_{2}-G_{1}}\right\|_{L_{\infty}(I^{n}_{p_{1}}(u^{0}_{1}))}\right)\left\|{u^{0}_{1}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Choosing
\varepsilon=\sup_{s\in I^{n}(u^{0}_{1})}\left|\sqrt{\varphi^{\prime}_{1}(s)}-\sqrt{\varphi^{\prime}_{2}(s)}\right|\sqrt{dn\tau}
and recalling
t=n\tau
we obtain

\left\|{u^{n}_{1}(t)-u^{n}_{2}(t)}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq(1-\frac{t}{n}G(0))^{-n}\left\|{u^{0}_{1}-u^{0}_{2}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

+4\sqrt{dt}(1-\frac{t}{n}G(0))^{-(n+1)}\left\|{u^{0}_{1}}\right\|_{TV(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\sup_{s\in I^{n}(u^{0}_{1})}\left|\sqrt{\varphi^{\prime}_{1}(s)}-\sqrt{\varphi^{\prime}_{2}(s)}\right|

+t{(1-\frac{t}{n}G(0))^{-(n+1)}}\left(\left\|{G^{\prime}_{2}}\right\|_{L_{\infty}(I^{n}_{p_{1},p_{2}}(u^{0}_{n}))}\sup_{s\in I^{n}(u^{0}_{1})}\left|{p_{2}(s)-p_{1}(s)}\right|+\left\|{G_{2}-G_{1}}\right\|_{L_{\infty}(I^{n}_{p_{1}}(u^{0}_{1}))}\right)\left\|{u^{0}_{1}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

(41)

Passing with
n
to infinity and recalling that, due to Theorem
2
,
u_{1}^{n},u_{2}^{n}
converge respectively to
u_{1},u_{2}
the mild solutions of (
37
) uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, we arrive at

\left\|{u_{1}(t)-u_{2}(t)}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq e^{tG(0)}\left\|{u^{0}_{1}-u^{0}_{2}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+4\sqrt{dt}e^{tG(0)}\left\|{u^{0}_{1}}\right\|_{TV(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\sup_{s\in I^{t}(u^{0}_{1})}\left|\sqrt{\varphi^{\prime}_{1}(s)}-\sqrt{\varphi^{\prime}_{2}(s)}\right|

+te^{tG(0)}\left\|{G^{\prime}_{2}}\right\|_{L_{\infty}(I_{p_{1},p_{2}}^{t}(u^{0}_{1}))}\sup_{s\in I^{t}(u^{0}_{1})}\left|{p_{2}(s)-p_{1}(s)}\right|\left\|{u^{0}_{1}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+te^{tG(0)}\left\|{G_{2}-G_{1}}\right\|_{L_{\infty}(I_{p_{1}}^{t}(u^{0}_{1}))}\left\|{u^{0}_{1}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

The estimate (
38
) follows from the fact that the right-hand side of (
41
) converges increasingly.
∎

Finally, from Theorem
3
we deduce the following corollary, which is the main result of this paper (cf. Theorem
1
).

Corollary 1
.

Let
u_{1},u_{2}
be two mild solutions to (
4
), with
G_{i}
satisfying (
6
) and
G_{i}\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
,
u^{0}_{i}\geq 0
,
u^{0}_{i}\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, i =1,2. Assume additionally that
u^{0}_{1}\in L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap BV(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and

\varphi_{i}(t)=sign(t)|t|^{\gamma_{i}},\hskip 5.69046ptp_{i}(t)=\frac{\gamma_{i}}{\gamma_{i}-1}|t|^{\gamma_{i}-1},\hskip 5.69046pt\mbox{ for }i=1,2,\hskip 5.69046pt\gamma_{2}>\gamma_{1}>1.

Then

\left\|{u_{1}(t)-u_{2}(t)}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq e^{tG(0)}\left\|{u^{0}_{1}-u^{0}_{2}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+te^{tG(0)}\left\|{G_{2}-G_{1}}\right\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\left\|{u^{0}_{1}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

+4\sqrt{dt}e^{tG(0)}\left\|{u^{0}_{1}}\right\|_{TV(\mathbb{R}^{d})}|\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1}|\left(\frac{\sqrt{\gamma_{1}}}{\gamma_{2}-1}+\frac{M^{\frac{\gamma_{1}-1}{2}}}{\sqrt{\gamma_{2}}+\sqrt{\gamma_{1}}}+\frac{\sqrt{\gamma_{2}}M^{\frac{\gamma_{2}-1}{2}}|\ln M|}{2}\right)

+te^{tG(0)}\left\|{G^{\prime}_{2}}\right\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}|\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1}|\left(\frac{\gamma_{1}}{(\gamma_{2}-1)(\gamma_{1}-1)}+\frac{M^{\gamma_{1}-1}}{(\gamma_{2}-1)(\gamma_{1}-1)}+\frac{\gamma_{2}}{\gamma_{2}-1}M^{\gamma_{2}-1}|\ln M|\right)\left\|{u^{0}_{1}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

where
M=e^{tG(0)}\left\|{u^{0}_{1}}\right\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
.

Proof of Corollary
1
.

Let us find the maximum on
[0,e^{tG(0)}||u^{0}_{1}||_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}]
of the function
g
defined below

g(s):=\sqrt{\varphi^{\prime}_{1}(s)}-\sqrt{\varphi_{2}^{\prime}(s)}=\sqrt{\gamma_{1}}s^{\frac{\gamma_{1}-1}{2}}-\sqrt{\gamma_{2}}s^{\frac{\gamma_{2}-1}{2}}.

Clearly
g(0)=0
and for
M:=e^{tG(0)}||u^{0}_{1}||_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}

\left|{g(M)}\right|\leq\left|{\sqrt{\gamma_{1}}-\sqrt{\gamma_{2}}}\right|M^{\frac{\gamma_{1}-1}{2}}+\sqrt{\gamma_{2}}\left|{M^{\frac{\gamma_{1}-1}{2}}-M^{\frac{\gamma_{2}-1}{2}}}\right|\leq|\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1}|\left(\frac{M^{\frac{\gamma_{1}-1}{2}}}{\sqrt{\gamma_{2}}+\sqrt{\gamma_{1}}}+\frac{\sqrt{\gamma_{2}}M^{\frac{\gamma_{2}-1}{2}}|\ln M|}{2}\right).

Since

g^{\prime}(s)=\sqrt{\gamma_{1}}\frac{\gamma_{1}-1}{2}s^{\frac{\gamma_{1}-3}{2}}-\sqrt{\gamma_{2}}\frac{\gamma_{2}-1}{2}s^{\frac{\gamma_{2}-3}{2}}=\frac{1}{2}s^{\frac{\gamma_{1}-3}{2}}\left(\sqrt{\gamma_{1}}(\gamma_{1}-1)-\sqrt{\gamma_{2}}(\gamma_{2}-1)s^{\frac{\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1}}{2}}\right),

g
attains a local maximum in

s_{0}=\left(\Upsilon(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\right)^{\frac{2}{\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1}}},\hskip 5.69046pt\mbox{ where }\hskip 5.69046pt\Upsilon(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}):=\frac{\sqrt{\gamma_{1}}(\gamma_{1}-1)}{\sqrt{\gamma_{2}}(\gamma_{2}-1)},

with value

\displaystyle g(s_{0})=\sqrt{\gamma_{1}}\Upsilon(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})^{\frac{\gamma_{1}-1}{\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1}}}-\sqrt{\gamma_{2}}\Upsilon(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})^{\frac{\gamma_{2}-1}{\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1}}}

\displaystyle=\Upsilon(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})^{\frac{\gamma_{1}-1}{\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1}}}\left(\sqrt{\gamma_{1}}-\sqrt{\gamma_{2}}\Upsilon(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\right)

\displaystyle=\Upsilon(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})^{\frac{\gamma_{1}-1}{\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1}}}\frac{\sqrt{\gamma_{1}}}{\gamma_{2}-1}(\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1}).

We claim that for any
1\leq\gamma_{1}<\gamma_{2}
, there holds

\Upsilon(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})^{\frac{\gamma_{1}-1}{\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1}}}\leq 1.

(42)

Indeed, we have

\Upsilon(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})^{\frac{\gamma_{1}-1}{\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1}}}=\left(1+\frac{\sqrt{\gamma_{1}}(\gamma_{1}-1)-\sqrt{\gamma_{2}}(\gamma_{2}-1)}{\sqrt{\gamma_{2}}(\gamma_{2}-1)}\right)^{\frac{\sqrt{\gamma_{2}}(\gamma_{2}-1)}{{\sqrt{\gamma_{1}}(\gamma_{1}-1)-\sqrt{\gamma_{2}}(\gamma_{2}-1)}}\frac{{\sqrt{\gamma_{1}}(\gamma_{1}-1)-\sqrt{\gamma_{2}}(\gamma_{2}-1)}}{\sqrt{\gamma_{2}}(\gamma_{2}-1)}\frac{\gamma_{1}-1}{\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1}}},

and

\displaystyle\frac{{\sqrt{\gamma_{1}}(\gamma_{1}-1)-\sqrt{\gamma_{2}}(\gamma_{2}-1)}}{\sqrt{\gamma_{2}}(\gamma_{2}-1)}\frac{\gamma_{1}-1}{\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\gamma_{1}-1)(\sqrt{\gamma_{1}}-\sqrt{\gamma_{2}})+\sqrt{\gamma_{2}}(\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2})}{\sqrt{\gamma_{2}}(\gamma_{2}-1)(\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1})}(\gamma_{1}-1)

\displaystyle=-\frac{(\gamma_{1}-1)^{2}}{\sqrt{\gamma_{2}}(\gamma_{2}-1)(\sqrt{\gamma_{1}}+\sqrt{\gamma_{2}})}-\frac{\gamma_{1}-1}{\gamma_{2}-1},

which is negative.
Hence, since

\left(1+\frac{\sqrt{\gamma_{1}}(\gamma_{1}-1)-\sqrt{\gamma_{2}}(\gamma_{2}-1)}{\sqrt{\gamma_{2}}(\gamma_{2}-1)}\right)^{\frac{\sqrt{\gamma_{2}}(\gamma_{2}-1)}{{\sqrt{\gamma_{1}}(\gamma_{1}-1)-\sqrt{\gamma_{2}}(\gamma_{2}-1)}}}\longrightarrow e\mbox{ as }\gamma_{1}\longrightarrow\gamma_{2},

we infer (
42
).
Therefore, we have

|g(s_{0})|\leq|\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1}|\frac{\sqrt{\gamma_{1}}}{\gamma_{2}-1},

and consequently

\sup_{s\in[0,M]}|g(s)|\leq|\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1}|\left(\frac{\sqrt{\gamma_{1}}}{\gamma_{2}-1}+\frac{M^{\frac{\gamma_{1}-1}{2}}}{\sqrt{\gamma_{2}}+\sqrt{\gamma_{1}}}+\frac{\sqrt{\gamma_{2}}M^{\frac{\gamma_{2}-1}{2}}|\ln M|}{2}\right).

(43)

We deal similarly with the function

h(s):=p_{1}(s)-p_{2}(s)=\frac{\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{1}-1}s^{\gamma_{1}-1}-\frac{\gamma_{2}}{\gamma_{2}-1}s^{\gamma_{2}-1}.

We have
h(0)=0
and

|h(M)|\leq|\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1}|\frac{M^{\gamma_{1}-1}}{\gamma_{2}-1}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma_{1}-1}+\gamma_{2}M^{\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1}}|\ln M|\right).

The critical point
s_{0}
now equals

s_{0}=\left(\frac{\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1}}},

for which

\left|{h(s_{0})}\right|=\left(\frac{\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{2}}\right)^{\frac{\gamma_{1}-1}{\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1}}}\left(\frac{\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{1}-1}-\frac{\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{2}-1}\right)=|\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1}|\frac{\gamma_{1}}{(\gamma_{2}-1)(\gamma_{1}-1)}\left(\frac{\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{2}}\right)^{\frac{\gamma_{1}-1}{\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1}}}

\leq|\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1}|\frac{\gamma_{1}}{(\gamma_{2}-1)(\gamma_{1}-1)}.

It follows that

\sup_{s\in[0,M]}\left|{p_{2}(s)-p_{1}(s)}\right|\leq|\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1}|\left(\frac{\gamma_{1}}{(\gamma_{2}-1)(\gamma_{1}-1)}+\frac{M^{\gamma_{1}-1}}{(\gamma_{2}-1)(\gamma_{1}-1)}+\frac{\gamma_{2}}{\gamma_{2}-1}M^{\gamma_{2}-1}|\ln M|\right).

(44)

The results follows by using (
43
) and (
44
) in Theorem
3
.
∎

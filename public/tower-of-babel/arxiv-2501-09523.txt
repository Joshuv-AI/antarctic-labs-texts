Rates of ( T T -)asymptotic regularity of the generalized Krasnoselskii-Mann-type iteration
Paulo Firmino, Laurenţiu Leuştean

Abstract
In this paper we use proof mining methods to compute rates of ( T T -)asymptotic regularity of the generalized Krasnoselskii-Mann-type iteration associated to a nonexpansive mapping T : X → X T:X\to X in a uniformly convex normed space X X . For special choices of the parameter sequences, we obtain quadratic rates. Keywords: Rates of ( T T -)asymptotic regularity; Krasnoselskii-Mann iteration; Uniformly convex normed spaces; Proof mining. Mathematics Subject Classification 2010 : 47H05, 47H09, 47J25.

Introduction

Let
X
be a normed space and
T:X\to X
be a nonexpansive mapping with fixed points.
The
generalized Krasnoselskii-Mann-type iteration
, studied by Kanzow and Shehu
[
8
]
in Hilbert spaces and by
Zhang, Guo, Wang
[
23
]
in classes of uniformly convex Banach spaces, is defined as follows:

x_{0}=x,\quad x_{n+1}=\alpha_{n}x_{n}+\beta_{n}Tx_{n}+r_{n},

(1)

where
(r_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subseteq X
and
(\alpha_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}},\,(\beta_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subseteq[0,1]
satisfy

\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n}\leq 1
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Obviously, if
r_{n}=0
and
\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n}=1
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
then
(x_{n})
becomes the well-known Krasnoselskii-Mann iteration
[
15
,
21
,
5
]
. As pointed out in
[
8
,
23
]
,
by letting
\alpha_{n}=1-\beta_{n}
and
r_{n}=\beta_{n}e_{n}
, where
(e_{n})\subseteq X
is a sequence of error terms, one obtains the inexact Krasnoselskii–Mann iteration studied by
Combettes
[
3
]
in Hilbert spaces, by Kim and Xu
[
9
]
in Banach spaces and, more recently, by Liang, Fadili and Peyré
[
19
]
.
Furthermore, by taking
r_{n}=\delta_{n}u
, where
u\in X
and
(\delta_{n})\subseteq[0,1]
is a sequence satisfying
\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n}+\delta_{n}=1
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
we get an iteration studied by Yao, Liou, Zhou
[
22
]
, Hu
[
6
]
and Hu and Liu
[
7
]
.

Kanzow and Shehu
[
8
]
prove, in Hilbert spaces, the weak convergence of the generalized Krasnoselskii-Mann-type iteration
(x_{n})
to a fixed point of
T
under
some hypotheses on the parameter sequences
(\alpha_{n})
,
(\beta_{n})
,
(r_{n})
. Zhang, Guo, Wang
[
23
]
generalize this weak
convergence result to uniformly convex Banach spaces that satisfy additional properties. By analyzing the proofs from
[
23
]
one
can see that, as it happens for numerous weak/strong convergence proofs of nonlinear iterations, an essential intermediate step is to obtain
the (
T
-)asymptotic regularity
[
2
,
1
]
of
(x_{n})
.

We say that
(x_{n})
is
T
-asymptotically regular if
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\|x_{n}-Tx_{n}\|=0
and that

(x_{n})
is asymptotically regular if
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\|x_{n+1}-x_{n}\|=0
. A mapping

\Phi:{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}}
is said to be

(i)

a rate of
T
-asymptotic regularity of
(x_{n})
if
\Phi
is a rate of convergence towards
0
of the sequence
(\|x_{n}-Tx_{n}\|)
;

(ii)

a rate of asymptotic regularity of
(x_{n})
if
\Phi
is a rate of convergence towards
0
of the sequence
(\|x_{n+1}-x_{n}\|)
.

If
\Phi
is a rate of (
T
-)asymptotic regularity of
(x_{n})
, we also say that
(x_{n})
is (
T
-)asymptotically regular with rate
\Phi
.

In this paper we apply methods of proof mining
[
12
,
13
]
developed by the second author for the
Ishikawa iteration of a nonexpansive mapping
[
17
,
18
]

to compute for the first time uniform rates of (
T
-)asymptotic regularity of the generalized
Krasnoselskii-Mann-type iteration
(x_{n})
in
uniformly convex normed spaces. Furthermore, quadratic
such rates are obtained for special choices of the parameter sequences.

Notations:
{\mathbb{N}}^{*}={\mathbb{N}}\setminus\{0\}
,
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}=[0,\infty)
. If
m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
n\geq m
, we write
[m,n]=\{m,m+1,\ldots,n\}
.

2
Generalized Krasnoselskii-Mann-type iteration

In the following,
X
is a normed space,
T:X\to X
a nonexpansive mapping and
(x_{n})
is the
generalized Krasnoselskii-Mann-type iteration defined by (
1
). We denote by

Fix(T)
the set of fixed points of
T
.

For every
z\in X
, let
(K_{z,n})
be a sequence of real numbers defined as follows:

\displaystyle K_{z,0}

\displaystyle=\|x-z\|,

\displaystyle K_{z,n}

\displaystyle=\|x-z\|+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\beta_{i}\|Tz-z\|+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(1-\alpha_{i}-\beta_{i})\|z\|+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\|r_{i}\|\quad\text{~~for $n\geq 1$}.

Thus, for all
n\geq 0
,

\displaystyle K_{z,n+1}

\displaystyle=K_{z,n}+\beta_{n}\|Tz-z\|+(1-\alpha_{n}-\beta_{n})\|z\|+\|r_{n}\|.

Lemma 2.1
.

For all
z\in X
and all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle\|x_{n+1}-z\|

\displaystyle\leq(\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n})\|x_{n}-z\|+\beta_{n}\|Tz-z\|+(1-\alpha_{n}-\beta_{n})\|z\|+\|r_{n}\|,

(2)

\displaystyle\|x_{n}-z\|

\displaystyle\leq K_{z,n},

(3)

\displaystyle\|x_{n+1}-x_{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq 2K_{z,n+1},

(4)

\displaystyle\|x_{n}-Tx_{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq 2\|x_{n}-z\|+\|z-Tz\|\leq 2K_{z,n}+\|z-Tz\|.

(5)

Proof.

We have that for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle\|x_{n+1}-z\|

\displaystyle=\|\alpha_{n}x_{n}+\beta_{n}Tx_{n}+r_{n}-z\|=\|\alpha_{n}(x_{n}-z)+\beta_{n}(Tx_{n}-z)+r_{n}-(1-\alpha_{n}-\beta_{n})z\|

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{n}\|x_{n}-z\|+\beta_{n}\|Tx_{n}-z\|+\|r_{n}-(1-\alpha_{n}-\beta_{n})z\|

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{n}\|x_{n}-z\|+\beta_{n}\|Tx_{n}-Tz\|+\beta_{n}\|Tz-z\|+\|r_{n}\|+\|(1-\alpha_{n}-\beta_{n})z\|

\displaystyle\leq(\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n})\|x_{n}-z\|+\beta_{n}\|Tz-z\|+(1-\alpha_{n}-\beta_{n})\|z\|+\|r_{n}\|,

as
T
is nonexpansive and
\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n}\leq 1
.
Thus, (
2
) holds.

(
3
) is proved by induction on
n
. The case
n=0
is obvious.

n\Rightarrow n+1
:

\displaystyle\|x_{n+1}-z\|

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{ineq-xn+1-z-xn-z}}}{{\leq}}(\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n})\|x_{n}-z\|+\beta_{n}\|Tz-z\|+(1-\alpha_{n}-\beta_{n})\|z\|+\|r_{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|x_{n}-z\|+\beta_{n}\|Tz-z\|+(1-\alpha_{n}-\beta_{n})\|z\|+\|r_{n}\|\quad\text{as~}\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n}\leq 1

\displaystyle\leq K_{z,n}+\beta_{n}\|Tz-z\|+(1-\alpha_{n}-\beta_{n})\|z\|+\|r_{n}\|\text{~by the induction hypothesis}

\displaystyle=K_{z,n+1}.

(
4
) and (
5
) follow easily:

\displaystyle\|x_{n+1}-x_{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|x_{n+1}-z\|+\|x_{n}-z\|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{ineq-xn-z-Kzn}}}{{\leq}}K_{z,n+1}+K_{z,n}\leq 2K_{z,n+1},

\displaystyle\|x_{n}-Tx_{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|x_{n}-z\|+\|z-Tz\|+\|Tz-Tx_{n}\|\leq 2\|x_{n}-z\|+\|z-Tz\|

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{ineq-xn-z-Kzn}}}{{\leq}}2K_{z,n}+\|z-Tz\|.

∎

A very useful immediate consequence of (
5
) is the following.

Corollary 2.2
.

Assume that
z\in Fix(T)
. Then for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\|x_{n}-Tx_{n}\|\leq 2\|x_{n}-z\|\leq 2K_{z,n}.

Lemma 2.3
.

For all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle\|x_{n+1}-x_{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{n}\|x_{n}-Tx_{n}\|+(1-\alpha_{n}-\beta_{n})\|x_{n}\|+\|r_{n}\|,

(6)

\displaystyle\|x_{n+1}-Tx_{n+1}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|x_{n}-Tx_{n}\|+2(1-\alpha_{n}-\beta_{n})\|x_{n}\|+2\|r_{n}\|.

(7)

Proof.

Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. We get that

\displaystyle\|x_{n+1}-x_{n}\|

\displaystyle=\|\beta_{n}(Tx_{n}-x_{n})-(1-\alpha_{n}-\beta_{n})x_{n}+r_{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{n}\|x_{n}-Tx_{n}\|+(1-\alpha_{n}-\beta_{n})\|x_{n}\|+\|r_{n}\|

and

\displaystyle\|Tx_{n+1}-x_{n+1}\|

\displaystyle=\|Tx_{n+1}-Tx_{n}+(1-\beta_{n})(Tx_{n}-x_{n})+(1-\alpha_{n}-\beta_{n})x_{n}-r_{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|Tx_{n+1}-Tx_{n}\|+(1-\beta_{n})\|Tx_{n}-x_{n}\|+(1-\alpha_{n}-\beta_{n})\|x_{n}\|+\|r_{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|x_{n+1}-x_{n}\|+(1-\beta_{n})\|Tx_{n}-x_{n}\|+(1-\alpha_{n}-\beta_{n})\|x_{n}\|+\|r_{n}\|

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{ineq-xnp1-xn}}}{{\leq}}\|x_{n}-Tx_{n}\|+2(1-\alpha_{n}-\beta_{n})\|x_{n}\|+2\|r_{n}\|.

∎

2.1
A useful lemma in uniformly convex normed spaces

Let us recall that a normed space
X
is uniformly convex if for all
\varepsilon\in(0,2]
there
exists
\delta\in(0,1]
such that for all
x,y\in X
,

\|x\|\leq 1,\quad\|y\|\leq 1\text{ and }\|x-y\|\geq\varepsilon\text{ imply }\left\|\frac{1}{2}(x+y)\right\|\leq 1-\delta.

(8)

A modulus of uniform convexity of
X
is a mapping
\eta:(0,2]\to(0,1]
providing a

\delta:=\eta(\varepsilon)
satisfying (
8
) for given
\varepsilon\in(0,2]
.
Thus,
X
is uniformly convex if and only if
X
has a modulus of uniform convexity
\eta
.

As pointed out in
[
14
, p. 3457]
(see also
[
20
, p. 63]
), the uniformly convex
Banach spaces
L_{p}
(
1<p<\infty
) have a modulus of uniform convexity
\eta_{p}
given by

\displaystyle\eta_{p}(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{(p-1)\varepsilon^{2}}{8}&\text{if $1<p<2$},\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon^{p}}{p\cdot 2^{p}}&\text{if $2\leq p<\infty$}.\end{cases}

Thus,
\displaystyle\eta(\varepsilon)=\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{8}
is a modulus of uniform convexity
for a Hilbert space.

In the sequel, we assume that
X
is uniformly convex and
\eta
is a modulus of uniform convexity
of
X
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,2]
,
r>0
and
a,x,y\in X
be such that

\|x-a\|\leq r,\,\|y-a\|\leq r\ \text{and}\ \|x-y\|\geq\varepsilon r.

Then for all
\lambda\in[0,1]
,

\|(1-\lambda)x+\lambda y-a\|\leq(1-2\lambda(1-\lambda)\eta(\varepsilon))r.

Proof.

Apply
[
11
, Lemma 3.3]
with
x:=\frac{1}{r}(y-a)
and
y:=\frac{1}{r}(x-a)
.
∎

The following lemma is the main tool in proving the results from Subsection
6.1
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
z
be a fixed point of
T
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
be such that
\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n}>0
.

(i)

If
\gamma,\delta,\theta>0
satisfy

\gamma\leq\|x_{n}-z\|\leq\delta
and
\|x_{n}-Tx_{n}\|\geq\theta
,

then

\|\alpha_{n}x_{n}+\beta_{n}Tx_{n}-(\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n})z\|\leq\|x_{n}-z\|-\frac{2\gamma\alpha_{n}\beta_{n}}{\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n}}\eta\left(\frac{\theta}{\delta}\right).

(ii)

Assume that
\eta
can be written as
\eta(\varepsilon)=\varepsilon\cdot\tilde{\eta}(\varepsilon)

with
\tilde{\eta}
increasing. If
\delta^{*},\theta>0
are such that

\|x_{n}-z\|\leq\delta^{*}
and
\|x_{n}-Tx_{n}\|\geq\theta
,

then

\|\alpha_{n}x_{n}+\beta_{n}Tx_{n}-(\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n})z\|\leq\|x_{n}-z\|-\frac{2\theta\alpha_{n}\beta_{n}}{\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n}}\tilde{\eta}\left(\frac{\theta}{\delta^{*}}\right).

Proof.

(i)

We have that

\displaystyle\alpha_{n}x_{n}+\beta_{n}Tx_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{n}}{\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n}}(\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n})x_{n}+\frac{\beta_{n}}{\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n}}(\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n})Tx_{n},

\displaystyle\|(\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n})x_{n}-(\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n})z\|

\displaystyle=(\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n})\|x_{n}-z\|,

\displaystyle\|(\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n})Tx_{n}-(\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n})z\|

\displaystyle=(\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n})\|Tx_{n}-z\|=(\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n})\|Tx_{n}-Tz\|

\displaystyle\leq(\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n})\|x_{n}-z\|,

\displaystyle\|(\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n})x_{n}-(\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n})Tx_{n}\|

\displaystyle=(\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n})\|x_{n}-Tx_{n}\|\geq(\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n})\theta

\displaystyle=(\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n})\|x_{n}-z\|\frac{\theta}{\|x_{n}-z\|}

\displaystyle\geq(\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n})\|x_{n}-z\|\frac{\theta}{\delta}.

Obviously,
\frac{\|x_{n}-Tx_{n}\|}{\|x_{n}-z\|}>0
and, by Corollary
2.2
,
\frac{\|x_{n}-Tx_{n}\|}{\|x_{n}-z\|}\leq 2
.

We can apply Lemma
2.4
with

\displaystyle x:=(\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n})x_{n},

\displaystyle y:=(\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n})Tx_{n},

\displaystyle a:=(\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n})z,

\displaystyle r:=(\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n})\|x_{n}-z\|,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lambda:=\frac{\beta_{n}}{\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n}},

\displaystyle\varepsilon:=\frac{\theta}{\delta}

to get that

\displaystyle\|\alpha_{n}x_{n}+\beta_{n}Tx_{n}-(\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n})z\|

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-2\frac{\alpha_{n}\beta_{n}}{(\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n})^{2}}\eta\left(\frac{\theta}{\delta}\right)\right)(\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n})\|x_{n}-z\|

\displaystyle=(\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n})\|x_{n}-z\|-\frac{2\alpha_{n}\beta_{n}}{\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n}}\eta\left(\frac{\theta}{\delta}\right)\|x_{n}-z\|

\displaystyle\leq\|x_{n}-z\|-\frac{2\alpha_{n}\beta_{n}}{\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n}}\eta\left(\frac{\theta}{\delta}\right)\gamma.

(ii)

Remark first that, by Corollary
2.2
and hypothesis, we have that

\|x_{n}-z\|\geq\frac{\|x_{n}-Tx_{n}\|}{2}\geq\frac{\theta}{2}>0
.

Apply (
i
) with
\gamma:=\delta:=\|x_{n}-z\|
to get that

\displaystyle\|\alpha_{n}x_{n}+\beta_{n}Tx_{n}-(\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n})z\|

\displaystyle\leq\|x_{n}-z\|-\frac{2\|x_{n}-z\|\alpha_{n}\beta_{n}}{\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n}}\eta\left(\frac{\theta}{\|x_{n}-z\|}\right)

\displaystyle=\|x_{n}-z\|-\frac{2\theta\alpha_{n}\beta_{n}}{\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n}}\tilde{\eta}\left(\frac{\theta}{\|x_{n}-z\|}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\|x_{n}-z\|-\frac{2\theta\alpha_{n}\beta_{n}}{\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n}}\tilde{\eta}\left(\frac{\theta}{\delta^{*}}\right),

as
\frac{\theta}{\delta^{*}}\leq\frac{\theta}{\|x_{n}-z\|}
, so

\tilde{\eta}\left(\frac{\theta}{\delta^{*}}\right)\leq\tilde{\eta}\left(\frac{\theta}{\|x_{n}-z\|}\right)
, since

\tilde{\eta}
is increasing.

∎

3
Quantitative notions, lemmas and hypotheses

Firstly, let us recall the quantitative notions used in the paper. Let
(a_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}
be a
sequence of real numbers. We say that a function
\varphi:{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}}
is

(i)

a rate of convergence of
(a_{n})
towards
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
if for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

|a_{n}-a|\leq\frac{1}{k+1}\quad\text{holds for all~}n\geq\varphi(k).

(ii)

a Cauchy modulus of
(a_{n})
if for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

|a_{n+p}-a_{n}|\leq\frac{1}{k+1}\quad\text{holds for all~}n\geq\varphi(k)\text{~and
all~}p\in{\mathbb{N}}.

If the series
\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}
converges, then a Cauchy modulus of the series is a
Cauchy modulus of the sequence
\left(\tilde{a}_{n}=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}\right)

of partial sums. Furthermore, if
\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}
diverges, then
\theta:{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}}

is a rate of divergence of the series if
\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\theta(k)}a_{i}\geq k
for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

The following useful lemmas are immediate.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that
(a_{n})\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
and that
\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}
converges with Cauchy modulus

\varphi
. Then

(i)

\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\leq M
, where
M\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
is such that

M\geq\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\varphi(0)}a_{i}+1
.

(ii)

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}a_{n}=0
with rate of
convergence
\psi(k)=\varphi(k)+1
.

Proof.

See the proof of
[
4
, Lemma 4.3(ii)]
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume that
a_{n}\in[0,1)
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and that
\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}
diverges with
rate of divergence
\theta
. Then
\theta(n)\geq n
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Proof.

Suppose, by contradiction, that for some
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we have that
\theta(n)<n
, hence
\theta(n)\leq n-1
.
Then
\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\theta(n)}a_{i}\leq\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}<n
, which is
a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(a_{n}),(b_{n})\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}
,
s,t\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
and
c_{n}=sa_{n}+tb_{n}
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Assume that
\varphi_{1}
is a Cauchy modulus of
(a_{n})
and
\varphi_{2}
is a Cauchy modulus of
(b_{n})
.
Define

\varphi(k)=\max\{\varphi_{1}(2s(k+1)-1),\varphi_{2}(2t(k+1)-1)\}.

Then
\varphi
is a Cauchy modulus of
(c_{n})
.

Proof.

See the proof of
[
4
, Lemma 3.2]
.
∎

3.1
A quantitative lemma on sequences of nonnegative reals

A modulus of liminf
[
18
]
for a sequence
(a_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
is a mapping

\delta:{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}}
satisfying the following:

for all
k,L\in{\mathbb{N}}
there exists

N\in[L,\delta(k,L)]
such that
a_{N}<\frac{1}{k+1}
.

Clearly,
\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}a_{n}=0
if and only if

(a_{n})
has a modulus of liminf.

The following quantitative lemma is one of the main tools in the proof of our main result,
Theorem
4.1
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Assume that
\left(a_{n}\right)_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}},\left(b_{n}\right)_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
satisfy the following:

(i)

\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}a_{n}=0
with modulus of liminf
\delta
;

(ii)

\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}
converges with Cauchy modulus
\psi
;

(iii)

a_{n+1}\leq a_{n}+b_{n}
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Then
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}a_{n}=0
with rate of convergence
\varphi
defined by

\varphi(k)=\delta\left(2k+1,\psi(2k+1)+1\right).

(9)

Proof.

Denote, for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
\tilde{b}_{n}=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n}b_{i}
.
First, let us remark that we get, by induction on
m
, that for all
n,m\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

a_{n+m}\leq a_{n}+\sum\limits_{i=n}^{n+m-1}b_{i}.

Let
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and let us denote
\psi_{k}=\psi(2k+1)
. As
\delta
is a modulus of liminf of

(a_{n})
, there exists
N\in[\psi_{k}+1,\varphi(k)]

and
a_{N}<\frac{1}{2(k+1)}
.

It follows that for all
n\geq\varphi(k)
,

\displaystyle a_{n}

\displaystyle\leq a_{N}+\sum\limits_{i=N}^{n-1}b_{i}=a_{N}+\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}b_{i}-\sum\limits_{i=0}^{N-1}b_{i}=a_{N}+\tilde{b}_{n-1}-\tilde{b}_{N-1}

\displaystyle=a_{N}+\tilde{b}_{\psi_{k}+q_{1}}-\tilde{b}_{\psi_{k}+q_{2}},\text{~where~}q_{1}=n-\psi_{k}-1,q_{2}=N-1-\psi_{k}

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{2(k+1)}+\tilde{b}_{\psi_{k}+q_{1}}-\tilde{b}_{\psi_{k}+q_{2}}.

As
n\geq\varphi(k)\geq N\geq\psi_{k}+1
, we have that
q_{1},q_{2}\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
so we can apply the fact that
\psi
is a Cauchy modulus of
(\tilde{b}_{n})
to get that

\displaystyle a_{n}

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{2(k+1)}+\frac{1}{2(k+1)}=\frac{1}{k+1}

∎

3.2
Quantitative hypotheses on the parameter sequences

We consider the following quantitative hypotheses on the parameter sequences
(\alpha_{n})
,

(\beta_{n})
,
(r_{n})
:

\displaystyle(C1)

\displaystyle\quad\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}(1-\alpha_{n}-\beta_{n})<\infty\text{~with Cauchy modulus~}\sigma_{1},

\displaystyle(C2)

\displaystyle\quad\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n}>0\text{~for all $n\in{\mathbb{N}}$ and~}\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha_{n}\beta_{n}}{\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n}}=\infty\text{~with rate of divergence~}\sigma_{2},

\displaystyle(C3)

\displaystyle\quad\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\|r_{n}\|<\infty\text{~with Cauchy modulus~}\sigma_{3}.

Assume that
(C1)
and
(C3)
hold. Then the series
\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}(1-\alpha_{n}-\beta_{n})
,

\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\|r_{n}\|

Lemma 5.1
.

Let

,

and define

\displaystyle\varphi(k)=\left\lceil t\right\rceil(k+1),\quad\varphi^{*}(k)=\max\{\left\lceil t\right\rceil(k+1)-L,0\},\quad M_{t}=\begin{cases}0&\text{~if~}t=0,\\
\left\lceil t\left(\frac{1}{L}+\frac{1}{L^{2}}\right)\right\rceil&\text{~if~}t\neq 0.\end{cases}

Then

(i)

\varphi
and
\varphi^{*}
are Cauchy moduli of
\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{t}{(n+L)^{2}}
.

(ii)

\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{t}{(n+L)^{2}}\leq M_{t}\leq 2\left\lceil t\right\rceil
.

Proof.

(i)

As
\varphi\geq\varphi^{*}

\varphi^{*}
is a Cauchy modulus of

\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{t}{(n+L)^{2}}

Let

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n+p}\frac{t}{(i+L)^{2}}-\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n}\frac{t}{(i+L)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=n+1}^{n+p}\frac{t}{(i+L)^{2}}\leq\sum\limits_{i=n+1}^{n+p}\frac{t}{(i+L-1)(i+L)}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=n+1}^{n+p}\left(\frac{t}{i+L-1}-\frac{t}{i+L}\right)=\frac{t}{n+L}-\frac{t}{n+p+L}

(ii)

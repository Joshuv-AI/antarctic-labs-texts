Associations of comprehensive oral health with body composition, physical performance, and sarcopenia among middle-aged and older adults in rural western China: potential mediating pathways
Authors: Wanghong Su, Zhonghai Zhu, Lixuan Fang, Shasha Bai, Shaoru Li, Jia Lv, Yue Cheng, Hongmei Xu, Bei Han, Rui Zou, Wanting Wan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSarcopenia is a key contributor to functional decline in aging populations, yet the role of oral health in its development remains unclear. Understanding this relationship and its underlying pathways is critical for designing effective interventions to promote healthy aging.MethodsWe conducted the first cross-sectional study among middle-aged and older adults (aged 44–90 years) from December 2024 to January 2025 in economically disadvantaged villages of western China. A convenience sample was recruited via local health centers. Comprehensive oral health and sarcopenia risk were assessed by oral frailty index (OFI-8) and SARC-F scale, respectively. In addition, body composition, grip strength, and gait speed were standardly measured, with appendicular skeletal muscle mass index (ASMI) calculated to indicate muscle mass. Generalized linear model was conducted to examine the associations between comprehensive oral health and sarcopenia risk. Further, a general causal mediation estimated the natural indirect effects of comprehensive oral health on sarcopenia risk and the corresponding proportions mediated through muscle mass, physical performance, and dietary-intake indicators.ResultsAmong 263 participants (mean age 64.4 ± 8.5 years; 57.8% female), 71.4% were aged ≥ 60 years. Oral frailty prevalence was high (68.4%), with 88.8% experiencing tooth loss, only 52.7% using dentures, and 6.9% visiting dentists annually. After adjusting a range of confounders, higher OFI-8 scores were statistically associated with lower ASMI, reduced grip strength, slower gait speed and an elevated risk of sarcopenia. Specifically, each 1-point increase in OFI-8 score was associated with a 30% (adjusted odds ratio 1.30, 95% CI 1.06, 1.60) higher likelihood of sarcopenia. Mediation analysis showed that ASMI, grip strength, gait speed, and dietary-intake indicators mediated only a modest proportion of this association: around 10% for ASMI and muscle strength, 20% for gait speed, around 5% for protein and minerals contents, and 26% for all combined.ConclusionsPoor oral health was significantly associated with higher sarcopenia risk. However, conventional mediators explained only a modest proportion of this relationship, suggesting that other mechanisms, such as inflammatory or psychosocial pathways, may be involved. These findings highlight the need for integrated oral-systemic health strategies in aging populations.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12903-026-08357-9.Keywords: Oral frailty index, Muscle mass and strength, Physical performance, Sarcopenia, Mediation analysis, Middle-aged and older adults

Introduction

Healthy aging has been emerging as a critical global public health priority and a significant societal challenge. Demographic projections indicate that by 2050, the global population aged ≥ 60 years (2.1 billion) will surpass adolescents aged 10–24 (2.0 billion), accounting for 38% of the global population, with the majority residing in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs) where healthcare resources are constrained [1]. The World Health Organization emphasizes that functional independence, or adequately supported dependence constitutes the cornerstone of healthy aging [2]. Consequently, preserving physical capacity, including muscle mass, strength, and walk ability, while reducing sarcopenia risk, is critical for extending healthy life expectancy and preventing downstream consequences such as cognitive decline and activities of daily living disability.

Oral health, encompassing multidimensional indicators including tooth integrity, salivary function, mastication, swallowing capacity, and speech, represents another essential component of healthy aging. According to the estimates from Global Burden of Disease Study (GBD), the people affected by the main oral conditions increased from 3.5 billion in 2019 to 3.69 billion in 2021, with substantially increasing age-standardized prevalence over the past 30 years [3]. Moreover, a systematic review documented that oral health-related quality of life declines progressively across all indices with advancing age [4]. Therefore, elucidating the relationship between oral health and physical performance, both critical determinants of healthy aging, would help to develop intervention strategies targeting at oral health to improve physical performance in aging population.

However, evidence regarding the associations between oral health, physical performance, and sarcopenia remains inconsistent in the literature [5]. Zhang and colleagues reported that the loss of nature teeth was associated with increased risk of systematic frailty (a composite health index) among older Chinese adults [6]. Based on two cohorts of older adults from the United Kingdom and United States, Kimble and colleagues serially reported that some markers of oral health including tooth loss, dry mouth, and difficulty eating symptoms and self-rated oral health, were associated with poor physical performance and higher risk of frailty [7–9]. This inconsistency may stem from prior studies focusing on isolated oral health parameters rather than comprehensive assessments, potentially overlooking subclinical manifestations preceding overt tooth loss and systemic conditions.

While, due to the multifaceted characteristics of oral health, the deterioration of comprehensive oral health is difficult to assess, particularly lacking simply implemented tools in primary healthcare and public health practices that could help identify people at risk of progressing to serious oral diseases and permanent tooth loss. Therefore, address this global challenge in aging populations, the concept of oral frailty, a multidimensional age-related syndrome encompassing declining oral health and function, has been proposed as a holistic indicator of oral health status [10]. Watanabe and colleagues reported that oral frailty assessed by a 8-item scale (OFI-8) was associated with mortality among Japanese adults ≥ 65 years [11]. The OFI-8 is developed primarily as a pragmatic screening instrument for risk identification rather than a diagnostic marker of oral dysfunction, which is mainly used in community settings to capture early, subclinical manifestations of oral functional decline, thereby identifying vulnerable individuals who might benefit from early preventive strategies before severe physical impairment occurs. However, evidence remains limited regarding the relationship between oral frailty (OFI-8) and physical performance or sarcopenia, particularly in community-dwelling populations, as most studies have focused on clinical patients or those with comorbidities [12, 13]. More importantly, existing studies have focused on associations of oral frailty with dietary intake and malnutrition, muscle mass and strength, and physical performance or sarcopenia, the underlying mechanisms remain unknown. Determining whether muscle mass decline, physical performance deficits, or nutritional mediators (e.g., protein intake) primarily explain this relationship is vital for developing targeted interventions. For instance, focus on nutritional supplementation if nutritional mediators prove to be the key mediator.

Here, given China’s status as one of the world’s fastest-aging nations, we conducted a cross-sectional study among middle-aged and older adults in rural western China. To our knowledge, this represents the first comprehensive survey simultaneously assessing oral health markers (via OFI-8), body composition, physical performance, and sarcopenia in this underserved population. Given the limited evidence, our study had two primary exploratory objectives: (1) to examine associations between comprehensive oral health and body composition indicators, physical performance, and sarcopenia; and (2) to quantitatively evaluate potential mediating pathways linking oral frailty to sarcopenia through dietary intake, muscle mass/strength, and physical performance. The estimated mediation effects and proportions will inform whether interventions targeting oral health improvement could enhance physical performance and mitigate sarcopenia risk, and whether modifying the identified mediators might attenuate the impact of oral frailty on sarcopenia development.

Methods

Study design and participants

We conducted a cross-sectional survey between December 2024 and January 2025 in rural villages of Tongchuan and Lantian counties, two economically disadvantaged regions in Shaanxi Province, western China. This study was embedded within a larger multi-center investigation of environmental pollution exposures and health outcomes, for which the detailed protocol and validated questionnaire have been previously published [14]. Briefly, within each county, we selected one primary village that was demographically representative of the region. Within each village, all adult residents were eligible to enroll the survey with minimum excluding criteria, such as serious cardiovascular diseases, advanced-stage cancers, and other similar major health issues. Then, we employed a combination of convenience sampling and snowball techniques to maximize participation. Local village doctors and government officials assisted with community engagement, informing residents about the study’s benefits including complimentary health examinations and education sessions.

Measurements of oral health markers

The assessment of oral health markers was conducted through a standardized protocol involving both clinical examinations and questionnaire administration. Two trained dentists jointly performed comprehensive oral examinations following established clinical guidelines, with particular emphasis on documenting the number of remaining natural teeth. In addition, the visual inspection of periodontal and occlusal conditions was conducted for health education purpose, e.g., advocating brushing teeth. The clinical records were directly double-checked on-site by both dentists to ensure complete consensus. Subsequently, to evaluate comprehensive oral health, participants completed a structured questionnaire assessing self-reported oral symptoms using the validated Oral Frailty Index-8 (OFI-8) scale [10], which has been cross-culturally adapted and validated for Chinese populations [15]. Specifically, the OFI-8 is an 8-item self-reported questionnaire assessing: (1) denture use, (2) difficulty eating hard foods compared to 6 months ago, (3) choking while eating or drinking recently, (4) tooth brushing frequency (≤ 2 times per day), (5) dry mouth symptoms, (6) reduced frequency of going out compared to 1 year ago, (7) ability to eat hard foods, e.g., dried squids or pickled radish, and (8) annual dental visits for preventive oral health care (not disease treatment). Within its scoring framework, items 1 through 3 are assigned 2 points each, while items 4 through 8 are assigned 1 point each. Consequently, the total score ranges from 0 to 11, with higher scores indicating worse oral function and greater oral frailty. According to the standardly recommended cutoff points of the OFI-8 scale, the proportions of participants classified as having a low-, middle-, and high-risk of oral frailty were 15.6% (41/263), 16.0% (42/263), and 68.4% (180/263), respectively. Therefore, to optimize statistical power, we employed both continuous OFI-8 scores and categorical tertile groupings as our primary exposure variables in subsequent analyses. The face-to-face interview was administered by standardly trained graduate students, and every completed questionnaire was systematically checked by the fieldwork leader on the same day.

Measurements of body composition, physical performance, and sarcopenia

Body composition assessment was conducted using standardized protocols with the Donghuayuan BC-110 bioelectrical impedance analyzer (BIA, 8-point tactile electrode system) after shoe removal. Standing height was precisely measured to the nearest millimeter using a calibrated steel stadiometer (Carefully KFL-02). All anthropometric measurements were performed by the same trained technician following established procedures. The BIA device generated comprehensive body composition parameters including: waist-hip ratio, regional muscle mass (arms, legs, trunk), total skeletal muscle mass, fat-free mass, fat mass, visceral fat area, protein content, mineral content, and basal metabolic rate. Derived indices were calculated as follows: body mass index (BMI) = weight (kg)/height (m)²; appendicular skeletal muscle mass index (ASMI) = (sum of arm and leg muscle mass)/height (m)².

Physical performance evaluation incorporated three key measures. Grip strength was assessed using a CAMRY EH101 hand dynamometer, where measurements were performed twice bilaterally, and the maximum value (Kg) from these measurements was recorded for final analysis. Gait speed was determined via timed 4-meter walk tests, which was also measured twice, with the maximum speed (minimum time) utilized for analysis. Prior to the official measurement, each participant was allowed to practice once to ensure familiarity with the testing process and reduce errors caused by nervousness or unfamiliarity with the procedure. Sarcopenia risk was evaluated using the validated Chinese version of the SARC-F questionnaire in a multicenter study [16], which assesses five functional domains (strength, ambulation assistance, chair rising, stair climbing, and falls). By established cut-off point, a SARC-F score ≥ 4 points served as the threshold for high sarcopenia risk classification. All these measurements were implemented by trained personnel following standardized operating procedures to ensure data quality and consistency, and were conducted on the same day during a single visit lasting approximately 45–60 min per participant. In addition, given the use of convenience sampling in the present study, the appropriateness and biological plausibility of each participant’s measurement data were immediately audited on-site by the research team. All verified observations were retained for the final multivariable analyses.

Other covariates

Additional covariates and potential confounding variables were standardly collected through face-to-face interviews. Sociodemographic characteristics detailed sex (Male, Female), age (years), education (None, ≤ 6 years, Middle school, College and above), occupation (Unpaid job, paid job), and household income (≤ 5,000, 5,000 to 10,000, 10,000 to 15,000, 10,000 to 15,000, ≥ 30,000 RMB). Health behavior variables included smoking status (never, former, or current smoker) and alcohol consumption patterns (never, occasional drinking, or regular drinking). Nutritional intake was evaluated using a dietary diversity score (DDS), calculated as the sum of nine food groups (including staple foods, vegetables, tofu, fruits, dairy products, eggs, fresh meat, seafood, and seeds/nuts) consumed within the preceding 24-hour period, as collected during the face-to-face interviews. We defined older adults as those aged ≥ 60 years according to the World Health Organization standards for developing countries [17].

Statistical analysis

The normality of continuous variables was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Given the non-normal distribution of certain body composition and physical performance metrics in our sample, continuous variables were summarized using medians with interquartile ranges (IQRs) and means with standard deviations, while categorical variables were presented as counts with percentages. Generalized linear model (GLM) was conducted to examine the associations between oral health markers and the continuous body composition and grip strength, gait speed and SARC-F score, with identify link. Based on the Central Limit Theorem, our sample size of 263 was considered enough to yield robust parameter estimates and reliable confidence intervals (CIs) from GLM Gaussian models despite deviations from perfect normality [18]. Similarly, the odds ratio (OR) and its 95% CI were estimated by logit link to evaluate the associations between oral health makers and the categorized gait speed and sarcopenia.

We conducted comprehensive mediation analyses to examine potential mechanistic pathways linking oral frailty (OFI-8) to sarcopenia risk (SARC-F). Using STATA’s “medeff” command [19], we firstly evaluated each candidate mediator (muscle mass indices, grip strength, gait speed, dietary diversity score, protein content, and mineral content) independently, estimating mediation effects and proportions with 95% confidence intervals derived from 1,000 bootstrap samples. This counterfactual-based approach offers the advantages of flexibility and freedom from strict distributional assumptions, such as perfect normality of the underlying variables, along with the ability to employ bootstrapping, a method highly robust to deviations from normality. Subsequently, we implemented structural equation modeling to construct an integrated pathway model incorporating all potential mediators simultaneously. This multivariate approach allowed us to quantify the total mediation proportion, calculated as the ratio of cumulative indirect effects to total effects, while accounting for interrelationships among mediators. All models were adjusted for sociodemographic covariates (age, sex, education, occupation, income), geographic location, and behavioral factors (smoking, alcohol use). Prior to finalizing the models, potential multicollinearity among the covariates was assessed, and all individual variance inflation factor (VIF) values were less than 4, with the mean VIF less than 2, indicating no significant collinearity risk. In addition, the interaction P values between OFI-8 and age were beyond 0.5 for all primary outcomes, and thus we did not further conduct stratified analysis by age due to our relatively small sample size. Statistical significance was determined at α = 0.05 (two-tailed), with mediation effects considered significant when 95% CI excluded the null point. All data were analyzed using software STATA 18.0 version.

Results

Background characteristics

A total of 263 participants were included the final analysis. As presented in Table 1, the study population had a mean age of 64.35 years (SD = 8.50), with a predominance of older adults (71.4% aged ≥ 60 years) and females (57.8%). Educational attainment was predominantly at the middle school level (51.7%), and the majority (95.1%) reported unpaid occupational status. Behavioral characteristics revealed that 25.4% were current smokers and 13.7% engaged in regular alcohol consumption.

Oral frailty assessment revealed a mean OFI-8 score of 4.78 ± 2.10 (IQR: 3, 6). OFI-8 score and body composition

As shown in Table 2, higher OFI-8 scores were significantly associated with reduced muscle mass and lower protein and mineral content. After adjusting for confounders, participants in the highest (Q3) and middle (Q2) OFI-8 categories exhibited lower ASMI compared to those in the lowest category (Q1) (Q3: β = -0.24, 95% CI -0.43 to -0.06; Q2: β = -0.23, 95% CI -0.45 to -0.01; P for trend = 0.015). Similar inverse associations were observed for total muscle mass, skeletal muscle mass, trunk muscle mass, protein content, and mineral content. However, no consistent associations were found between other oral health markers (e.g., number of missing teeth) and body composition measures (eTable 1 in Supplement 1).

OFI-8 score and physical performance and SARC-F score

As shown in Table 3, higher OFI-8 score was statistically associated with worse physical performance and sarcopenia risk. Each 1-point increase in OFI-8 score was associated with − 0.53 (95% CI -0.95, -0.10) Kg lower grip strength, 0.05 (95% CI -0.04, 0.14) high seconds for walking a 4-meter distance and 0.14 (95% CI 0.03, 0.25) points higher SARC-F score. Furthermore, each 1-point increase in OFI-8 score was associated with increased risk of sarcopenia, with an adjusted OR of 1.30 (95% CI 1.06, 1.60). Similar result patterns were observed for categorized OFI-8. As compared to individuals in the lowest OFI-8 scores (Q1 category), those in the highest (Q3) had 0.70 points (95% CI: 0.08, 1.32) points lower SARC-F score and a 2.97-fold increased sarcopenia risk (95% CI: 0.75, 11.81), with a P trend of 0.016 for SARC-F score and 0.034 for sarcopenia risk, respectively. As shown in eTable 2 in Supplement 1, associations between other oral health markers (e.g., number of missing teeth), physical performance and SARC-F score were largely non-statistically significant. Denture use was statistically associated with improved physical performance and reduced sarcopenia risk.

Discussions

To our knowledge, we were the first to report the associations of comprehensive poor oral health with reduced muscle mass and protein and minerals contents, worse physical performance (grip strength and gait speed) and increased risk of sarcopenia among middle-aged and older adults in rural areas of western China. In addition, the relationship between OFI-8 and SARC-F score were only partly explained by muscle mass and muscle strength, gait speed and dietary-intake indicators, with mediated proportions of around 26%. Our findings suggest that OFI-8 simply assessed by face-to-face interview was able to measure the comprehensive oral health that can help identify those at risk of progressing to impaired physical performance and sarcopenia among Chinese middle-aged and older adults in rural western China.

We reported a high prevalence of oral frailty, with 68.4% of participants meeting the high-risk criteria, although the diagnostic tools and corresponding criteria to define oral frailty varied by settings and studies [20]. This proportion substantially exceeds previously reported rates among hospitalized patients (48.7%) [21] and community-dwelling older adults (51%) [22] in more economically developed regions of eastern China. Notably, these findings are particularly concerning given the concomitant observations of extensive tooth loss (88.8% prevalence) and low denture utilization (52.7%) in our cohort. Collectively, these findings reveal alarmingly poor oral health status among community-dwelling populations in rural western China. Village-level clinics in this region lack both dental professionals and essential equipment, resulting in extremely limited dental access. Only 6.5% of our sample reported dental visits within the past year. Therefore, we suggest positioning the identification of poor oral health or oral frailty as a complementary component within a multidimensional geriatric assessment framework in routine primary healthcare, particularly in rural areas, which may produce meaningful impacts on life-course health.

Our study advances current understanding of oral health-body composition relationships by providing nuanced evidence through comprehensive assessments. While confirming previous reports of null associations between oral frailty and adiposity indicators (BMI, waist-hip ratio, fat mass, and visceral fat area) [12], we identified significant inverse relationships between OFI-8 scores and muscle mass parameters (ASMI, total muscle, skeletal muscle, trunk muscle, and fat-free mass). Based on a similar design of cross-sectional survey among 290 older Japanese patients who regularly attended a general dental clinic, Matsushita and colleagues conducted a path analysis and found that tong pressure and free functional deterioration were associated with reduced risk of underweight [23]. In addition, our observation that higher OFI-8 score correlates with lower protein and mineral contents offers a potential mechanistic explanation for muscle mass decline, bridging the well-established link between poor oral health and malnutrition [8, 13, 24, 25]. This also may partly explain the associations between oral frailty and muscle mass indicators. Declines in muscle mass, strength, and physical performance constitute the core diagnostic criteria for sarcopenia [26]. Unlike studies examining isolated oral health markers (tooth loss, periodontal status) [7, 24, 27, 28], or using inconsistent oral frailty definitions [5, 12, 13, 29, 30], our standardized OFI-8 assessments revealed clear dose-response relationships with both physical performance (grip strength, gait speed) and sarcopenia risk. Given prior evidence on associations of physical performance and sarcopenia with life-course life quality and mortality among older adults [31–33], our findings also support prior finding that oral frailty was associated with mortality among Japanese older adults [11]. Consequently, although we did not mean to recommend using OFI-8 as a standalone indicator for sarcopenia identification or prevention, the OFI-8 effectively captured aspects of broader functional vulnerability and could be utilized as a useful tool within a multidimensional geriatric assessment framework, which may aid in developing more holistic strategies for extending healthy life expectancy in aging populations.

Multiple pathophysiological pathways have been proposed to explain the oral frailty-sarcopenia association: (1) nutrition-mediated mechanisms through reduced masticatory function leading to inadequate protein/calorie intake and subsequent muscle loss [34]; (2) inflammatory cascades triggered by periodontal pathogens that induce systemic inflammation (elevated IL-6, TNF-α) and muscle protein breakdown [35]; (3) oral-gut-microbiome axis dysbiosis promoting intestinal barrier dysfunction [36, 37]; and (4) mitochondrial/metabolic dysfunction via chronic oxidative stress [38, 39]. Importantly, our analyses adjusted for key confounders like smoking and alcohol use that were frequently overlooked in previous studies, revealed robust associations between OFI-8 scores and both physical performance and sarcopenia. Of note, the absolute effect sizes, such as a 0.53 kg reduction in grip strength or a 30% increased likelihood of sarcopenia per 1-point increase in OFI-8 score, may appear modest at the individual clinical level, but their practical public health significance is substantial. Given the alarmingly high prevalence of oral frailty (68.4%) in these rural communities, even minor population-wide shifts in muscle mass and physical performance would translate into a massive absolute increase in population burden of sarcopenia and subsequent disability. Therefore, addressing these modest individual effects through population-level oral health interventions could have profound public health implications.

However, mediation analyses showed that traditional biological mediators (muscle mass/strength, gait speed, and nutritional biomarkers) collectively explained only a modest proportion of this relationship. This finding aligns with emerging evidence suggesting limited mediation effects of eating difficulties (10%) and communication impairments (22%) [28] in the tooth loss - disability of daily living activities association. Of note, the total 26% mediated proportion indicated a statistical decomposition of cross-sectional associations rather than a confirmation of definitive physiological mechanisms. As majority of the association between oral frailty and sarcopenia remains unexplained by these conventional variables, any mechanistic interpretations regarding alternative pathways must be presented with appropriate caution and warrant rigorous longitudinal physiological validation. In addition, the non-significant mediation effects for DDS may be due to the limited sample size of 263. Another possible explanation was that, in rural villages in western China, the study participants exhibited homogeneous dietary patterns, i.e., staple-based, limited food access, thereby limiting DDS range and reducing its discriminatory power. These may suggest that the absolute caloric or protein intake required to maintain muscle mass rather than dietary diversity be more important to reduce the risk of sarcopenia, highlighting the need for objective nutritional biomarkers or quantitative dietary assessments e.g., FFQ, in future pathway analyses. Interestingly, preliminary evidence suggests that growing evidence implicates psychosocial factors in this relationship, as oral frailty has been associated with depressive symptoms [22], potentially creating a vicious cycle of reduced social engagement, decreased physical activity, and accelerated muscle loss.

From the perspective of implementing cost-effective interventions and public health programs, our results of mediation analysis quantitively suggest that conventional approaches focusing solely on nutritional optimization (e.g., protein supplementation), muscle mass enhancement, and physical performance improvement may represent suboptimal strategies for addressing oral frailty-related sarcopenia prevention. More potential mechanisms such as inflammation and oral-gut-muscle axis disorders by cross-omics approaches should be pursued to confirm whether being able to revert or ameliorate the developmental progress of oral frailty and thus inform corresponding intervening strategies. Potentially, integrated interventions that simultaneously address both the biological and social dimensions of oral-systemic health, particularly through community-based programs combining oral rehabilitation with mental health support and social activation initiatives, may represent an effective strategy for mitigating sarcopenia risk in vulnerable elderly populations.

This study had limitations. Firstly, it surveyed two cities that had relatively limited resources, which may limit the generalizability of our findings to more affluent areas or urban populations. In these economically disadvantaged rural settings, village-level clinics lack both dental professionals and essential equipment, and participants had relatively low health literacy, which may result in delayed recognition of oral health symptoms and eventually lead to the high prevalence of oral frailty and sarcopenia when compared to the urban population. As only advanced cases seek care, these deprived situations would amplify observed associations between oral dysfunction and adverse physical functional outcomes. In addition, physical labor demands in agricultural communities may accelerate sarcopenia onset, altering the trajectory and correlates of muscle decline relative to urban elderly. Secondly, although the DDS is a widely utilized, pragmatic proxy for overall diet quality and micronutrient adequacy in large-scale field surveys, it lacks food quantity and frequency data as compared to the FFQ, which may explain the non-significant mediation effect. Thirdly, the inability to link participant identifiers with hospital records precluded adjustment for pre-existing chronic conditions in our analyses. However, given priorly reported associations of oral health with chronic diseases [40], there was no sufficient evidence to decide their confounding, mediating, and/or collider role in the relationship between OFI-8 and sarcopenia. Omitting them from the models may likely protect the estimates from severe collider and over-adjustment biases. Fourthly, as with other observational studies, residual confounding factors are always a concern and thus some terms we used, e.g., effect, indicate associations but not causal link. Fifthly, the sampling method of convenience may create reporting bias. Finally, the number of participants identified with a high risk of sarcopenia was relatively small (n = 34, 12.9%). This limited number of outcome events restricted the overall statistical power of our multivariable models and mediation analyses, potentially leading to wider CIs and instability of estimates. Furthermore, given the exploratory nature of this study, we did not apply statistical corrections for multiple outcomes. While this approach minimizes Type II errors to identify potential biological associations, it inherently increases the risk of Type I errors. The observed statistically significant associations and the exact magnitude should be interpreted with caution, and larger cohort studies are warranted to validate the robustness of these pathways.

Conclusions

Our findings suggest that oral health was extremely poor in rural areas of western China, warranting investment form multiple-domain stakeholders to address this growing public health challenge in aging communities. Particularly, poor oral was significantly associated with reduced muscle mass and strength, slower gait speed and higher risk of sarcopenia. These findings suggest that early examination and improving oral health serve as valuable complementary components in multidimensional geriatric care. Integrated oral-systemic strategies may capture broader functional vulnerabilities and support overall physical performance. Through comprehensive mediation analyses, we quantitatively showed that conventional pathways (nutritional status, muscle metrics, and physical performance) statistically decompose only 26% of the cross-sectional oral frailty-sarcopenia association. Because majority of this relationship remains unexplained, mechanistic interpretations require caution. More potential mechanisms or randomized trials should be pursued and conducted to confirm whether being able to revert or ameliorate the progress of oral frailty and thus inform corresponding intervention strategies.

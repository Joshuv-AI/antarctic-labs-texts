Electrical feedthrough governs symmetry of phononic frequency combs in MEMS resonators
Authors: Hongyu Chen, Yangyang Guan, Dongyang Chen, Zunhao Xiao, Ronghua Huan, Yuan Wang, Chen Wang, Jin Xie
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Understanding parasitic electrical feedthrough in microelectromechanical (MEMS) resonators is essential for controlling nonlinear spectral phenomena. Here, we demonstrate that electrical feedthrough fundamentally governs the symmetry of phononic frequency combs in a capacitively transduced MEMS resonator. Rather than acting as a mere measurement artifact, feedthrough forms a coherent electromechanical feedback pathway through direct AC coupling between drive and sense ports. This additional electrical channel modifies the effective electrostatic stiffness and damping, reshapes the intrinsic Duffing nonlinearity, and redistributes modal energy within the comb cavity. As a consequence, feedthrough breaks spectral symmetry, elevates the comb-generation threshold, compresses the spectral span, and reduces coherence across comb lines. To quantitatively evaluate spectral symmetry and repeatability, an Overlap Ratio (OR) metric is introduced to measure comb-line coincidence in both frequency and amplitude domains. By implementing real-time differential cancellation to null the feedthrough-induced feedback, the intrinsic nonlinear dynamics are restored. After suppression, the resonance frequency shifts upward, the effective quality factor increases, the comb threshold decreases, and the spectral bandwidth expands. The OR improves dramatically from 0.125 to 0.895, confirming recovery of highly symmetric and stable comb structures. These results establish a unified electromechanical framework linking electrical feedthrough to nonlinear comb formation and symmetry breaking, providing a practical route toward symmetric, low-threshold, and broadband phononic frequency comb generation for precision sensing and energy-efficient signal processing.

Introduction

Microelectromechanical (MEMS) resonators exhibit rich nonlinear dynamic behaviors, many of which critically influence device performance and functionality1–5. Nonlinearity can be harnessed to enhance sensitivity near bifurcation points and improve dynamic resolution6,7. However, nonlinear responses may also induce instability, spectral asymmetry, and complex modal interactions that degrade performance8. In nonlinear spectral phenomena, particularly those involving energy transfer across modes, symmetry and energy balance play a central role in determining stability, coherence, and measurement reliability. Therefore, understanding the origin and controllability of symmetry in nonlinear dynamics is of both fundamental and practical importance.

Beyond intrinsic nonlinearities, feedthrough parasitics represent a prevalent and often unavoidable feature in capacitively transduced MEMS resonators, resembling the electrical coupling observed in a parallel resonant circuit9,10. Feedthrough arises from the direct transmission of the input alternating signal to the output port without mechanical mediation through the resonator. Conventionally, it has been treated as a measurement artifact that distorts the resonance peak, modifies the apparent quality factor, introduces phase uncertainty, and reduces signal-to-noise ratio11–16. However, its influence on nonlinear spectral dynamics, particularly on symmetry and energy redistribution in frequency-comb generation, remains insufficiently understood.

MEMS resonators have found widespread applications in physical sensing17–20, chemical detection21,22, medical procedures23, and signal processing applications24,25. Under strong nonlinear excitation, these systems can generate a series of equidistant frequency lines in the spectrum, referred to as phononic frequency combs—analogous to optical frequency combs26–34. Key metrics for characterizing phononic frequency combs include the number of observable comb lines, spectral span, and generation threshold29. Such combs provide enhanced spectral resolution and tunability—for example, a span as narrow as 0.005 Hz has been demonstrated in a gallium arsenide electromechanical resonator35—as well as high-order signal processing capabilities for sensing and communication systems32,33,35–37. In these nonlinear comb systems, spectral symmetry directly reflects balanced energy transfer and coherent modal interaction within the comb cavity.

Despite extensive investigation of intrinsic nonlinear comb formation mechanisms, the role of electrical feedthrough in governing comb symmetry and nonlinear energy redistribution has not been systematically clarified. It remains unclear how feedthrough-induced electromechanical interference modifies effective coupling, reshapes Duffing nonlinearity, and influences comb threshold and bandwidth. In this work, we establish an electromechanical equivalent framework demonstrating that electrical feedthrough forms a coherent feedback channel that redistributes energy within the comb cavity and breaks spectral symmetry. By implementing real-time differential cancellation to null this feedback pathway, the intrinsic nonlinear dynamics are recovered. As a result, comb symmetry is significantly restored, the generation threshold decreases, and the spectral bandwidth expands. These findings reveal that feedthrough is not merely a parasitic artifact but a governing factor in nonlinear comb symmetry, providing a pathway toward controlled and tunable phononic frequency comb generation in MEMS resonators.

The feedthrough signal path is modeled as a parasitic feedthrough capacitor connected in parallel with the motional branch of the resonator, denoted by a capacitance Cf. The output current therefore consists of the mechanical motional current and the feedthrough current: Iout = Imotional+jωCfVdrive, where the second term represents the direct electrical feedthrough current. Importantly, the feedthrough contribution is not a mere measurement artifact but an intrinsic component of the electromechanical system. Because the sensing electrode is terminated by a TIA, the output voltage is determined by Vsense = ZTIAIout. As a result, the feedthrough current directly modifies the electrical boundary condition at the sensing node. This voltage, in turn, alters the electrostatic force acting on the resonator, establishing an electromechanical feedback loop between the drive and sense ports. This feedback reshapes the effective resonator dynamics by modifying the electrical damping, electrostatic stiffness, and phase condition for nonlinear oscillation. Consequently, the governing equations of motion are altered. These changes occur at the dynamical level and therefore cannot be removed by post-processing subtraction of the output signal.

Within this modified dynamical framework, the anti-resonance valley emerges from the condition, Imotional + Ifeedthrough ≈ 0, where destructive interference occurs at the sensing node. Crucially, this apparent amplitude suppression does not signify vanishing mechanical motion; rather, it reflects electrical cancellation of the transduced current, while the mechanical energy stored in the resonator remains finite. The valley therefore represents a manifestation of electromechanical interference embedded in the system dynamics. When the feedthrough pathway is suppressed, the additional feedback channel is removed, restoring the intrinsic boundary condition. The anti-resonance valley disappears, the resonance lineshape recovers its symmetry, and the effective cavity gain regains balance around resonance. This recovery confirms that the previously observed asymmetry originates from boundary condition-induced electromechanical coupling, rather than from an intrinsic property of the mechanical mode itself.

Moreover, when the resonator operates as a frequency comb generator (the red switch in Fig. 2a switched on), pump signal injection takes place alongside the presence of a pump capacitance, resulting in dynamic stiffness modulation. Both the mechanical capacitance Cm and the pump capacitance CP contribute to the modulation of the overall stiffness, thereby impacting the natural frequency and the performance of the frequency comb. Feedthrough suppression

Feedthrough-induced modification of cavity

When frequency combs are generated, the frequency response curve reveals the emergence of new oscillation modes, each corresponding to phonon energy conservation conditions. In this context, the MEMS resonator functions as a phononic cavity, where the confined mechanical waves give rise to distinct spectral features. Our analysis of the frequency comb behavior includes both frequency sweep measurements and spectral analysis. The sweep curves capture the system’s dynamic response under varying drive frequencies, highlighting how the resonator’s envelope modulates with excitation conditions. By combining these two analysis approaches, we aim to clarify the relationship between the phononic cavity characteristics and the resulting frequency combs, while also evaluating the influence of feedthrough effects on comb symmetry and efficiency.

Firstly, in Fig. 4a, the dark-blue curve illustrates the amplitude-frequency response curve of the circular resonator under the influence of the feedthrough signal, with a feedthrough intensity of ~1.2 mV. This curve reveals the presence of both a resonant peak and an anti-resonant valley. Additionally, upon introducing a 10 Hz pump signal into the resonator, a frequency comb is generated, characterized by a 10 Hz spacing and the emergence of sidebands in Fig. 4b. In the spectrum analysis, we extract three distinct frequency combs with central lock-in frequencies of 126.129 kHz, 126.149 kHz, and 126.159 kHz, and a spacing of 10 Hz, as depicted in Fig. 4c. It is evident that the resonator has the capability to generate varied frequency combs based on different driving frequencies, each exhibiting unique comb orders and magnitudes of comb lines. The amplitude of the comb generated at parallel resonance is lower than the others. Additionally, a point with a larger amplitude can produce a more complex comb. Although the excitation signal and feedthrough amplitude remain fixed, the resulting combs display asymmetry and even missing teeth, clearly indicating that feedthrough directly affects comb symmetry across the bandwidth. It should be emphasized that the intrinsic mechanism of comb generation in our study remains intramodal coupling. Since the state of the resonator governs the excitation threshold and stability conditions for phononic frequency comb formation, such distortions inevitably affect the observed comb features. In particular, feedthrough modifies the apparent resonance profile, producing asymmetry between the left and right comb branches, which could lead to misinterpretation of the underlying comb dynamics. As for the potential applications, operated in a real-time environment, the resonator system can be affected by comprehensive noise; extreme noise may bury the weak lines, therefore limiting the bandwidth and robustness of frequency comb generators. Consequently, feedthrough not only affects comb symmetry but also leads to variations in the amplitude and number of comb lines. In frequency measurement applications, a symmetrical comb envelope is advantageous. A highly symmetrical resonant response curve facilitates the generation of a relatively wideband and flat comb42,43. Therefore, a real-time feedthrough suppression method is applied in the improvement of frequency comb symmetry.

Real-time feedthrough suppression

The presence of the feedthrough signal can distort the symmetry of the response curve, leading to an asymmetric frequency comb. This asymmetry compromises the frequency comb’s role as a precise frequency ruler, adversely affecting high-precision frequency measurements. Therefore, mitigating the feedthrough signal becomes essential. Two primary methods are commonly employed for this purpose: The first method involves data analysis, where both the output signals with DC bias voltage turned on and off are recorded. Subsequently, the recorded data is analyzed and processed to replot the response curve without the feedthrough effect13. This approach offers the advantage of simplicity as it does not necessitate designing a circuit module, and it effectively cancels the feedthrough signal. However, since frequency comb lines necessitate real-time recording, the data analysis method lacks efficiency. Secondly, an on-chip method can be utilized, such as a differential circuit, a single-device differential scheme44 or an on-chip twin resonator scheme11. In this section, we focus on the on-chip differential circuit featuring compensation capacitance. It is important to acknowledge that achieving a compensation capacitance exactly equal to the feedthrough capacitance in the MEMS resonator system is practically unattainable. As a result, the feedthrough signal cannot be ideally canceled out. Hence, it is more appropriate to refer to this scheme as real-time feedthrough suppression.

Figure 5 illustrates the amplitude-frequency response curves before and after the real-time feedthrough suppression. The bright-blue curve represents the raw response curve with a feedthrough level of ~ .2 mV. The feedthrough level can be determined by measuring the output signal when the DC bias voltage (25 V in this case) is turned off while the AC source remains active. As the DC bias voltage is essential for the operation of an electrostatic resonator, turning off the DC bias voltage would deactivate the device. An OR close to 1 indicates strong spectral symmetry and consistent energy distribution. Before feedthrough suppression (Fig. 7a), the OR is only 0.125, indicating severe asymmetry. Differences manifest as unequal spectral spans, missing or weakened sidebands, larger spectral weight on one branch, and energy inclination toward lower-threshold regions. Such asymmetry can introduce measurement discrepancies when extracting information from symmetric spectral positions.

After feedthrough suppression (Fig. 7b), the OR increases dramatically to 0.895, representing an ~7.2× improvement, and confirming restoration of highly symmetric energy redistribution. Most comb teeth align closely in both frequency and amplitude, producing a balanced and stable spectral envelope. Importantly, the suppression is achieved in a practical “suppression” (not perfect cancellation) regime: the off-resonance feedthrough measured at the TIA output decreases from ~1.2 V to ~0.5 V, corresponding to a residual ratio r = |Vfeedthrough,res | /|Vfeedthrough,0 | = 0.417 and a suppression depth of ~7.6 dB in amplitude. Because the feedthrough level is set by the electrical bypass path and remains approximately independent of pump intensity over our operating range, this quantitative reduction in residual feedthrough provides a reproducible electrical metric that directly correlates with the observed OR enhancement.

Notably, Fig. 7b also shows a phenomenon where the amplitude at the central frequency appears lower than that of its adjacent sidebands. This effect is attributed to the four-wave mixing mechanism, where energy is predominantly transferred from the central frequency to the first-order sidebands. As energy accumulates at the first-order sidebands due to conservation principles, their amplitude can exceed that of the central comb. However, this does not affect the overall symmetry of the frequency combs. This improvement reflects the enhanced symmetry of the new phonon formation.

Overall, these results indicate that electrical feedthrough introduces a coherent electromechanical interference channel that biases the observed nonlinear energy redistribution and branch evolution, whereas suppressing this channel allows the intrinsic nonlinear dynamics to dominate, restoring symmetric phonon-mediated energy transfer. In this sense, feedthrough is not merely a parasitic measurement artifact in the comb regime; rather, it is a governing factor for the measured comb symmetry and energy balance. Controlling this interference pathway provides a practical route toward symmetric, low-threshold, and broadband phononic frequency comb generation for precision sensing and energy-efficient signal processing32.

Robustness across pump intensities

To verify that the observed behavior is not limited to this operating point, we provide additional spectra at two symmetric frequency positions across multiple pump intensities in the Supplementary Material. The results consistently show that feedthrough suppression enhances comb symmetry throughout the pump-dependent evolution. In the presence of feedthrough, sideband growth remains asymmetric and requires higher excitation levels, whereas after suppression the threshold decreases and the spectral branches evolve more uniformly with increasing pump amplitude. These observations confirm that feedthrough does not merely distort a specific spectrum, but fundamentally governs the efficiency and balance of nonlinear energy redistribution in the phonon cavity.

Conclusion

In this work, we systematically investigated the impact of electrical feedthrough on phonon frequency comb generation in a MEMS resonator and revealed its dominant role in shaping nonlinear spectral dynamics. Through response curve analysis, phonon cavity characterization, and comb evolution, we demonstrated that feedthrough introduces a coherent electrical interference pathway that modifies the apparent resonance characteristics and nonlinear response. The presence of feedthrough alters the effective quality factor, comb-generation threshold and redistributes spectral energy asymmetrically across the comb structure. This leads to unequal spectral range, imbalanced amplitudes between left and right comb branches, elevated generation thresholds, and reduced spectral coherence. Such asymmetry may introduce discrepancies in information extracted from symmetric spectral positions, thereby degrading measurement reliability. After feedthrough suppression, the resonance frequency shifts upward, the effective Q factor increases, the comb-generation threshold decreases, and the comb bandwidth expands. Quantitatively, the OR between symmetric spectral positions improves dramatically (0.125 → 0.895), indicating restored energy balance and enhanced spectral symmetry. These results establish a unified electromechanical interpretation of feedthrough-induced nonlinear modification in MEMS resonators and demonstrate that feedthrough engineering is a powerful tool for controlling phonon frequency comb dynamics. The proposed approach offers practical guidance for improving comb symmetry, stability, and energy efficiency in resonator-based signal processing, logic operations, and precision measurement systems.

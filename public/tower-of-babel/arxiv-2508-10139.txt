Using nonassociative algebras to classify skew polycyclic codes up to isometry and equivalence
Susanne Pumplün

Abstract
. We propose new definitions of equivalence and isometry for skew polycyclic codes that will lead to tighter classifications than existing ones. This helps to reduce the number of previously known isometry and equivalence classes, and state precisely when these different notions coincide. In the process, we classify classes of skew ( f , σ , δ ) (f,\sigma,\delta) -polycyclic codes with the same performance paramenters, to avoid duplicating already existing codes. We exploit that the generator of a skew polycyclic code is in one-one correspondence with the generator of a principal left ideal in its ambient algebra. Algebra isomorphisms that preserve the Hamming distance (called isometries) map generators of principal left ideals to generators of principal left ideals and preserve length, dimension and Hamming distance of the codes. We allow the ambient algebras to be nonassociative, thus eliminating the need on restrictions on the length of the codes. The isometries between the ambient algebras can also be used to classify corresponding linear codes equipped with the rank metric.

3.
Equivalence relations for skew polycyclic codes

3.1.
Background

The
Hamming weight
of
c=(c_{0},\dots,c_{m-1})\in S^{m}
counts the number of nonzero components in
c

and is defined as
w(c)=\sum_{r\not=0}n_{r}(c)
where

n_{r}(c)=\{i\,|\,c_{i}=r\}
counts the number of components that equals the element
r\in S
. The
Hamming weight
of a polynomial
p(t)\in R
is defined as the number of nonzero coefficients of
p(t)
.

We want equivalent linear codes to have the same performance parameters, i.e. the same length
m
, dimension
k
, and minimum Hamming distance
d
.
When looking at the literature, there are several slightly different ways to define two linear codes with the Hamming metric to be “equivalent”.
The most general definition for commutative rings
S
seems to be the following:

Two linear codes
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
over a ring
S
of the same length
m
are sometimes called
semilinear-equivalent
, if
there exists an automorphism
\tau\in{\rm Aut}(S)
,
\gamma=(v,\pi)\in S^{m}\rtimes S_{m}
such that
C_{2}=\{y\in S^{m}\,|\,y=\gamma(\tau(c))\text{ for }x\in C_{1}\}
.
If
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
have generator matrices
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
this means that then they are semilinear-equivalent, if there is an invertible
N\in Gl_{n}(S)
, some
\tau\in{\rm Aut}(S)
and a
monomial matrix
M\in M_{n}(S)
such that
G_{2}=N\tau(G_{1}M)
. If
\tau=id
then
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are called
linearly-equivalent
.

That means
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are semilinear-equivalent if and only if one can be obtained from the other by a finite sequence of any of the following transformations, applied to an element
(c_{0},\dots,c_{m-1})
in one code:

(1)

A permutation of its elements.

(2)

The multiplication of elements in a fixed position by a nonzero scalar in
S
.

(3)

Applying an isomorphism in
{\rm Aut}(S)
to each component of a code vector in
S^{m}
.

This definition is motivated by the fact that the above operations all preserve the Hamming distance and the linearity of a code.

Note that the set of all
m\times m
monomial matrices forms a group, and that every monomial matrix is the product of a permutation matrix
P
and a diagonal matrix
diag(d_{0},\dots,d_{m-1})
with
d_{i}\in S^{\times}
invertible.

Chen et al. defined an equivalence relation on the invertible elements of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
we will call Chen isometry in the following, to classify the constacyclic codes over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of length
m

[
CFLL2012
]
. Elements

in the same equivalence class define equivalent classes of constacyclic codes
{\bf C}_{t^{m}-a}
and
{\bf C}_{t^{m}-b}
, and in turn guarantee the existence of an isometry between the two associative algebras

\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]/(t^{m}-a)
, respectively,
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]/(t^{m}-b)
.

However, Chen isometry does not easily yield the generator polynomial of the code under the isometry.
This lead to the study of Chen equivalence
\sim_{m}
to classify the constacyclic codes over
\mathbb{F}_{q}

[
CDL2015
]
which is a special case of Chen isometry. It trivially allows to relate the generating polynomials of Chen equivalent constacyclic codes. Both definitions were recently generalised to skew constacyclic and (skew) polycylic codes over finite fields and rings
[
BBB21
,
CON2025
,
OaNA25
,
LM25
,
NevPu2025
]
.
For instance,
[
LM25
, Section 6]
uses the monomial isometries
G_{id,\alpha}
of degree one, when the algebras
R_{a}
are associative.

Another approach was taken in
[
BLMS2024
]
: The classification of
(f,\sigma,\delta)
-polycyclic codes that are Hamming isometrical equivalent
defines the class of
(f,\sigma,\delta)
-polycyclic codes

and the class of

-polycyclic codes

to be

such that
A_{f_{1}}B=\sigma(B)A_{f_{2}}+\delta(B)
.
This definition implies that

are similar skew polynomials.
It is straightforward to see that if two classes of codes are Chen isometric, then they are Hamming isometrical equivalent.

a cyclic Galois extension and

Up to now, only Chen isometries have been used in the literature for (skew) polycyclic codes, cf. Section
3.1
.
Their disadvantage is they do not include property (
3
) when using them to define the equivalence of (skew) polycyclic codes.

Let
{\bf C}_{f}
be the class of skew
(f,\sigma,\delta)
-polycyclic codes and
{\bf C}_{h}
the class of skew
(h,\sigma,\delta)
-polycyclic codes.

We know that the above defined Hamming weight preserving ring isomorphisms are the only ones there are in many cases, see Section
4
.
This makes this approach the most general one possible.

Definition 3.2
.

The classes
{\bf C}_{f}
and
{\bf C}_{h}
are called

(i)

isometric
, if there exists an isometry

G_{\tau,\alpha,k}:R/Rf\rightarrow R/Rh,

(ii)

Chen isometric
, if there exists an isometry

G_{id,\alpha,k}:R/Rf\rightarrow R/Rh,

(iii)
equivalent
, if there exists an equivalence

G_{\tau,\alpha}:R/Rf\rightarrow R/Rh

(iv)
Chen equivalent
, if there exists a Chen equivalence

G_{id,\alpha}:R/Rf\rightarrow R/Rh.

If the length of the codes is not clear from the context, we will use the terminology
m
-isometric,
m
-Chen isometric etc. instead.

If two classes
{\bf C}_{f}
and
{\bf C}_{h}
are isometric or equivalent, then
f
and
h
have the same degree.

Equivalent, isometric, Chen equivalent or Chen isometric classes of codes
{\bf C}_{f}
and
{\bf C}_{h}
contain codes with the same parameters, thus have the same performance:
for every skew polycyclic code
C_{1}
in
{\bf C}_{f}
we can find a skew polycyclic code
C_{2}
in
{\bf C}_{h}
that has the same length, dimension, and Hamming weight. One can be obtained from the other by a finite sequence of any of (
1
), (
2
) or (
3
).

We will show that in the majority of cases, the generator of a code in
{\bf C}_{f}
can be explicitly matched with the one of a code in an equivalent class
{\bf C}_{h}
.
This is because of the scarcity of monomial isomorphisms of degree
k=1
.

Remark 3.3
.

If
{\bf C}_{f}
and
{\bf C}_{h}
are Chen equivalent, then they are also equivalent.

If
{\bf C}_{f}
and
{\bf C}_{h}
are Chen isometric, then they are also isometric.

If
{\bf C}_{f}
and
{\bf C}_{h}
are equivalent, then they are also isometric.

If
{\bf C}_{f}
and
{\bf C}_{h}
are Chen equivalent, then they are also equivalent and Chen isometric, hence isometric.

To motivate these notions we stress that the classifications we will achieve are tighter when employing the notions of equivalence and isometry (Section
5.2
).

Inspired by

]
we define equivalence relations on

via the following equivalence relations:

Definition 3.4

We call
(a_{0},\dots,a_{m-1})

to

(a_{0},\dots,a_{m-1})\cong_{m}(b_{0},\dots,b_{m-1}),

It is also obvious that

(a_{0},\dots,a_{m-1})\cong_{m}(b_{0},\dots,b_{m-1})

if and only if

and

(a_{0},\dots,a_{m-1})\cong_{Chen,m}(b_{0},\dots,b_{m-1})

(a_{0},\dots,a_{m-1})\sim_{Chen,m}(b_{0},\dots,b_{m-1})

Remark 3.6

In
[

4.
Isometries between Petit algebras
S[t;\sigma]/S[t;\sigma]f

Let
S
be a unital commutative associative ring and suppose
\sigma\in\mathrm{Aut}(S)
has finite order
n
.
Write
S_{0}=\{s\in S:\sigma(s)=s\}
for the subring fixed by
\sigma
.
If
\tau\in\mathrm{Aut}(S)
is an arbitrary automorphism
then we write
S_{0}^{\tau}=\{s\in S_{0}:\tau(s)=s\}
for the common fixed subring under
\tau
and
\sigma
. When
S=K
is a field, we define
K_{0},K_{0}^{\tau}
analogously.

For any
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
\tau\in\mathrm{Aut}(S)
and
\beta\in S
, write

N_{i}^{\tau}(\beta)=\prod_{j=0}^{i-1}\tau^{j}(\beta).

Note that
\beta\tau(N_{i}^{\tau}(\beta))=N_{i+1}^{\tau}(\beta)
and thus

(4.1)

N_{i+j}^{\tau}(\beta)=N_{i}^{\tau}(\beta)\cdot\sigma^{i}(N_{j}^{\tau}(\beta))

for all
i,j\geq 0
. If
S=K
is a field,
K/K_{0}
a cyclic Galois extension, and
\mathrm{Gal}(K/K_{0})=\langle\sigma\rangle
, where
\sigma
has order
n
, then
N_{n}^{\sigma}
is simply the norm map
N_{K/K_{0}}
corresponding to
K/K_{0}
. More generally,
N_{n}^{\sigma}(\beta)\in S_{0}
for all
\beta\in S
so we distinguish this case with the notation

N_{n}^{\sigma}=N_{S/S_{0}}
.

Let

f(t)=t^{m}-\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}a_{i}t^{i},\quad h(t)=t^{m}-\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}b_{i}t^{i}\in S[t;\sigma]

be two monic polynomials.

4.1.
Equivalences

The following results were originally stated for
S_{0}
-isomorphisms for the case that
S
is a field in
[
BrPum2018
, Theorems 28, 29]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\alpha\in S^{\times}
and assume that
\tau\in{\rm Aut}(S)
commutes with
\sigma
. Then

G_{\tau,\alpha}:R/Rf\to R/Rh

is an isomorphism of
S_{0}^{\tau}
-algebras if and only if

(4.2)

\tau(a_{i})=N_{m-i}^{\sigma}(\sigma^{i}(\alpha))b_{i}

for all
i\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}
.

Proof.

Let
G=G_{\tau,\alpha}:R/Rf\to R/Rh
,

G_{\tau,\alpha}(\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}d_{i}t^{i})=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\tau(d_{i})N_{i}^{\sigma}(\alpha)t^{i}

be an isomorphism of
S_{0}^{\tau}
-algebras,
\alpha\in S^{\times}
. Then we have

G(zt^{i})=G(z)G(t)^{i}=\tau(z)(\alpha t)^{i}=\tau(z)N_{i}^{\sigma}(\alpha)t^{i}

for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,m-1\}
and
z\in S
.

Moreover, with
t^{m}=tt^{m-1}
, also

(4.3)

G(t^{m})=G\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}a_{i}t^{i}\Big)=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}G(a_{i})G(t)^{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\tau(a_{i})\big(\prod_{l=0}^{i-1}\sigma^{l}(\alpha)\big)t^{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\tau(a_{i})N_{i}^{\sigma}(\alpha)t^{i}

and
G(tt^{m-1})=G(t)G(t^{m-1})=G(t)G(t)^{m-1}
, i.e.

(4.4)

G(t)^{m}=G(t)G(t)^{m-1}=\alpha\sigma(\alpha)\cdots\sigma^{m-1}(\alpha)t^{m}=N^{\sigma}_{m}(\alpha)\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}b_{i}t^{i}.

Comparing (
4.3
) and
(
4.4
) gives
\tau(a_{i})=N_{m-i}^{\sigma}(\sigma^{i}(\alpha))b_{i}
for all
i\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}
. Thus Equation (
4.2
) holds
for all
i\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}
.

Conversely, if Equation (
4.2
) holds
for all
i\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}
and some invertible
\alpha\in S
, then consider the isomorphism
G_{\tau,\alpha}:R\rightarrow R
. By Lemma
2.5
, this isomorphism canonically induces an algebra isomorphism between the Petit algebras
R/Rf
and
R/RG_{\tau,\alpha}(f)
.
Since

G_{\tau,\alpha}(f)=N_{m}^{\sigma}(\alpha)t^{m}-\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\tau(a_{i})N_{i}^{\sigma}(\alpha)t^{i}

=N_{m}^{\sigma}(\alpha)(t^{m}-\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\tau(a_{i})N_{m}^{\sigma}(\alpha^{-1})N_{i}^{\sigma}(\alpha)t^{i})=N_{m}^{\sigma}(\alpha)(t^{m}-\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\tau(a_{i})N_{m-i}^{\sigma}(\sigma^{i}(\alpha^{-1}))t^{i})

=N_{m}^{\sigma}(\alpha)(t^{m}-\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}b_{i}t^{i})

we obtain again
\tau(a_{i})=N_{m-i}^{\sigma}(\sigma^{i}(\alpha))b_{i}

for all
i\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}
and thus the assertion.
∎

When
K[t;\sigma]/K[t;\sigma]f
and

are proper nonassociative, and

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
n\geq m-1
and assume that
f
and
h
do not generate two-sided ideals in
K[t;\sigma]
. Suppose
\mathrm{Aut}(K)
is abelian. For any subfield

, the
only

F

G:K[t;\sigma]/K[t;\sigma]f\to K[t;\sigma]/K[t;\sigma]h

are of the form
G=G_{\tau,\alpha}
such that
\tau\in\mathrm{Aut}(K)
,
K_{0}^{\tau}\subset F
, and
\alpha\in K^{\times}
satisfying Equation
4.2

Proof.

Let
G:K[t;\sigma]/K[t;\sigma]f\to K[t;\sigma]/K[t;\sigma]h
be an isomorphism of
F
-algebras. Since
K[t;\sigma]/K[t;\sigma]f
is not
associative, its left nucleus is

. Since any isomorphism preserves the left nucleus,
G(K)=K
and so
G|_{K}=\tau
for some

\tau\in\text{Aut}_{F}(K)
. Suppose
G(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}k_{i}t^{i}
for some

Then we have

(4.5)

G(tz)=G(t)G(z)=(\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}k_{i}t^{i})\tau(z)=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}k_{i}\sigma^{i}(\tau(z))t^{i}

and

(4.6)

G(tz)=G(\sigma(z)t)=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\tau(\sigma(z))k_{i}t^{i}

for all

. Comparing the coefficients of
t^{i}

in (
4.5
) and
(
4.6
) we obtain

(4.7)

k_{i}\sigma^{i}(\tau(z))=k_{i}\tau(\sigma^{i}(z))=\tau(\sigma(z))k_{i}=k_{i}\tau(\sigma(z))

for all

and all

k_{i}(\tau\big(\sigma^{i}(z)-\sigma(z)\big))=0

for all

and all

since

and
\tau
commute, i.e.

(4.8)

k_{i}=0\text{ or }\sigma^{i}(z)=\sigma(z)

for all
i\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}
and all
z\in K
.

Since
\sigma
has order
n\geq m-1
, which means
\sigma^{i}\not=\sigma
for
all

,
i\not=1
,
(
4.8
) implies
k_{i}=0
for
all
i\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}
,
i\not=1
. Therefore
G(t)=\alpha t
for some

\alpha\in K^{\times}
. As in the proof of Theorem

, these maps are indeed well defined algebra isomorphisms and must be the only one which implies the assertion.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

There exists an algebra isomorphism
G_{\tau,\alpha}:S_{f}\rightarrow S_{h}
if and only if there exist algebra isomorphisms

G_{\tau,\sigma^{i}(\alpha)}:S_{t^{m-i}-a_{i}}\rightarrow S_{t^{m-i}-b_{i}}
for all
i\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}
.

Proof.

We know that
G_{\tau,\alpha}
is an algebra isomorphism if and only if

\tau(a_{i})=N_{m-i}^{\sigma}(\sigma^{i}(\alpha))b_{i}

for all
i\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}
. Put
\alpha_{i}=\sigma^{i}(\alpha)
,
then the above equations are the same as saying that there exist
\alpha_{i}\in K^{\times}
such that

\tau(a_{i})=N_{m-i}^{\sigma}(\beta_{i})b_{i}

which is the same as saying that the algebras

are isomorphic with the isomorphisms given by

∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Suppose
\tau\in{\rm Aut}(S)
commutes with
\sigma
and define

Then

Proof.

By Theorem

,

G_{\tau,\alpha}:R/Rf\to R/Rh

is an isomorphism of

-algebras if and only if

\tau(a_{i})=N_{m-i}^{\sigma}(\sigma^{i}(\alpha))b_{i}

4.2.

It may happen that
G(t)
is a polynomial of degree
k>1
for some isomorphism
G
between two nonassociative algebras

and
R/Rh
. We are only interested in Hamming weight preserving isomorphism, so we are concerned with the cases when

for some

and

invertible, i.e. when

Note that when

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\sigma\not=id
. Let
\tau\in\mathrm{Aut}(S)
commute with
\sigma
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
1<k<m-1
, and
\alpha\in S^{\times}
. If the map
G
denoted
G_{\tau,\alpha,k}
which is defined via
G(c)=\tau(c)
for all
c\in S
and
G(t)=\alpha t^{k}
defines an isomorphism between
R/Rf
and
R/Rh
then
k\equiv 1\mod m
.

Proof.

Take
G=G_{\tau,\alpha,k}:S[t;\sigma]\to S[t;\sigma]

G(\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}a_{i}t^{i})=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}\tau(a_{i})N_{i}^{\sigma}(\alpha)t^{ik},

for any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and
a_{i}\in S
.
The map
G=G_{\tau,\alpha,k}
is
S_{0}^{\tau}
-linear and multiplicative, therefore we have
G(t)G(c)=G(\sigma(c))G(t)
for all
c\in S
.
We compute

G(t)G(c)=\alpha t^{k}\tau(c)=\alpha\sigma^{k}(\tau(c))t^{k}

and

G(\sigma(c))G(t)=\tau(\sigma(c))\alpha t^{k}.

Since
\alpha\in S^{\times}
and
\sigma
and
\tau
commute, we must have
\sigma=\sigma^{k}
, or
k\equiv 1\mod m
.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\sigma\not=id
. Let
\tau\in\mathrm{Aut}(S)
commute with
\sigma
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
1<k<m-1
, and
\alpha\in S^{\times}
. If the map
G_{\tau,\alpha,k}
which is defined via
G(c)=\tau(c)
for all
c\in S
and
G(t)=\alpha t^{k}
defines an isomorphism between

and

then

N^{\sigma^{k}}_{m}(\alpha)(\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}b_{i}t^{i})^{k}=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\tau(a_{i})N_{i}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha)t^{ki}\,mod_{r}\,f.

Proof.

Since for all
i<m-1
the powers
t^{i}
are power associative, so must be their images, that is the powers
G(t)^{i}
of
G(t)
are power associative, for all
i\leq m-1
.
Hence we have

G(\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}a_{i}t^{i})=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\tau(a_{i})G(t)^{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\tau(a_{i})N_{i}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha)t^{ki}\,mod_{r}\,f.

Since
G
is a homomorphism and with
t^{m}=tt^{m-1}

this implies that

G(t^{m})=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\tau(a_{i})G(t)^{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\tau(a_{i})N_{i}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha)t^{ki}\,mod_{r}\,f

N_{m}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha)(\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}a_{i}t^{i})^{k}=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\tau(a_{i})N_{i}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha)t^{ki}.

Now
G(tt^{m-1})=G(t)G(t^{m-1})=G(t)G(t)^{m-1}
, i.e.

G(t)^{m}=(\alpha t^{k})(\alpha t^{k})^{m-1}=N_{m}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha)(t^{k})^{m}=N_{m}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha)(t^{m})^{k}=N^{\sigma^{k}}_{m}(\alpha)(\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}b_{i}t^{i})^{k}.

(Note we are computing in the associative skew polynomial ring here.)
Comparing these two equations yields the assertion.
∎

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
\sigma\not=id
.
Let
G:R/Rf\rightarrow R/Rh
be an isomorphism such that
G|_{K}=\tau
for some

\tau\in{\rm Aut}(S)
and
G(t)=\alpha t^{k}
for some

.
If any of the following conditions fail, then
G
is not well defined:

(1)

k\equiv 1\mod n

1<k<m
;

(2)

\gcd(k,m)=1
;

(3)

if
\ell
is the inverse of
k
mod
m
, then
\ell=1+jn
for some
1<\ell<m
;

(4)

N^{\sigma^{k}}_{m}(\alpha)(\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}b_{i}t^{i})^{k}=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\tau(a_{i})N_{i}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha)t^{ki}\,mod_{r}\,f.

Proof.

The first condition is a consequence of the requirement that
G(ta)=G(\sigma(a)t)
, cf. Lemma

.

If
G
is an isomorphism, then

for some

. Since

preserves Hamming weight,

for some

h_{\tau,\alpha}(t)=t^{m}-\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}N_{m-i}^{\sigma}(\sigma^{i}(\tau(\alpha)))\tau(b_{i})t^{i},

then

[{\bf C}_{h}]=\{{\bf C}_{h_{\tau,\alpha}}\,|\,\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}^{\times},\tau\in{\rm Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}/\mathbb{F}_{p})\}.

Note that the
h_{\tau,\alpha}(t)
all have
\tau(b_{0})
as constant coefficients, for some
\tau\in{\rm Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}/\mathbb{F}_{p})
,
since
N_{m}^{\sigma}(K^{\times})=\{1\}
, and that there are at least
n
choices for
\tau
.

This argument works more generally, too.

Example 5.13
.

For
i\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}
set
m_{i}=m-i
. Suppose there is
i\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}
such that
\gcd([m_{i}]_{s},p^{r}-1)=p^{r}-1
.
By Proposition
5.7
, no two classes of skew
(f,\sigma)
-polycyclic and skew
(h,\sigma)
-polycyclic codes of length
m
where
f
and
h
have distinct
a_{i_{0}},b_{i_{0}}\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}^{\times}
will be Chen-equivalent.
Thus, there are again at most
|K^{\times}|=p^{r}-1
distinct classes of skew polycyclic codes under
m
-Chen equivalence; one for each choice of
a_{i_{0}}\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}^{\times}
. Note that every
{\bf C}_{h_{id,\alpha}}\in[{\bf C}_{h}]_{Chen}
has
b_{i_{0}}
as
i_{0}
-th coefficient.
Every
{\bf C}_{h_{\tau,\alpha}}\in[{\bf C}_{h}]
has
\tau(b_{i_{0}})
as
i_{0}
th coefficient.

We will use the following result for skew constacyclic codes obtained in
[
NevPu2025
, Theorem 5.6]
.

Theorem 5.14
.

The number of distinct Chen isometry classes of families of skew
(\sigma,a)
-constacyclic codes arising from nonassociative algebras
\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}[t,\sigma]/\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}[t,\sigma](t^{m}-a)
is
N
where

N=\begin{cases}\gcd([m]_{s},p^{r}-1)&\text{if $n\nmid m$; and}\\
\left(1-\frac{1}{[n]_{s}}\right)\gcd([m]_{s},p^{r}-1)&\text{if $n|m$.}\end{cases}

There are additionally
\gcd([m]_{s},p^{r}-1)/[n]_{s}
different Chen equivalence classes (and thus at most this many Chen isometry classes) of families of skew
(\sigma,a)
-constacyclic codes arising from associative algebras
\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}[t,\sigma]/\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}[t,\sigma](t^{m}-a)
, that is, for which
n\mid m
and
a\in K_{0}
.

Note that when
n|m
, we have
N_{m}^{\sigma}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})\subset\mathbb{F}_{p^{s}}
.

As a consequence we immediately obtain bounds for skew polycyclic codes.

Theorem 5.15
.

(i) Let
n\nmid m
. Then the number of different Chen equivalence classes of skew
(f,\sigma)
-polycyclic codes of length
m
is at most

N=\gcd([m]_{s},p^{r}-1).

(ii) Let
n|m
. Then the number of different Chen equivalence classes of skew
(f,\sigma)
-polycyclic codes of length
m
is at most

N=\left(1-\frac{1}{[n]_{s}}\right)\gcd([m]_{s},p^{r}-1)+\gcd([m]_{s},p^{r}-1)/[n]_{s}.

In this count,
\left(1-\frac{1}{[n]_{s}}\right)\gcd([m]_{s},p^{r}-1)
equivalence classes belong to skew polynomials

f
that have constant coefficient
a_{0}\notin\mathbb{F}_{p^{s}}
.

Proof.

(i)
If
n\nmid m
, then the algebras
\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}[t;\sigma]/\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}[t;\sigma](t^{m}-a)
are not associative and there are
\gcd([m]_{s},p^{r}-1)
classes of Chen equivalent
(\sigma,a)
-constacyclic codes. This immediately yields the assertion.

(ii)
If
n|m
, then
N_{m}^{\sigma}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})\subset\mathbb{F}_{p^{s}}
, so that every coset of
N_{m}^{\sigma}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
is either entirely contained in
\mathbb{F}_{p^{s}}
or entirely disjoint from it (so corresponds to a nonassociative Petit algebra).
We know that
\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}[t;\sigma]/\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}[t;\sigma](t^{m}-a)
is a proper nonassociative algebra if and only if
a\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{p^{s}}
.
Turning to the proof of
[
NevPu2025
, Theorem 5.6]
, the total number of cosets contained in
\mathbb{F}_{p^{s}}
, and thus corresponding to associative algebras where

a\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{s}}
, is equal to

\frac{p^{s}-1}{(p^{r}-1)/w}=\frac{w}{[n]_{s}}
.
Subtracting these
\frac{w}{[n]_{s}}
cosets from the total number of cosets gives the number of Chen equivalence classes for nonassociative algebra that is
N=w-w/[n]_{s}
. This yields the assertions.
∎

5.3.
When two classes cannot be equivalent

If we want to know when skew polycyclic codes cannot be equivalent, there are some easy conditions we can check first.
We are looking at classes
{\bf C}_{f}
and
{\bf C}_{h}
of skew polycyclic codes of length
m
over
S
.

Corollary 5.16
.

If any of the following conditions hold, then
{\bf C}_{f}
and
{\bf C}_{h}
are not equivalent.

(i)
There exists some
i\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}
such that
a_{i}=0
but
b_{i}\not=0
or vice versa (in other words,
(a_{0},\dots,a_{m-1})
and

(b_{0},\dots,b_{m-1})
do not have the same Hamming weight).

(ii)
There exists some
i\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}
such that
a_{i}
is invertible but
b_{i}
is not, or vice versa.

This generalizes
[
OaHA25
, Lemma 4.19]
which was stated for finite fields and
\tau=id
.
gain
\sigma=id
is allowed, in which case we are dealing with polycyclic codes.

Proof.

The classes
{\bf C}_{f}
and
{\bf C}_{h}
of skew polycyclic codes are equivalent if and only if there exists
\alpha\in S^{\times}
such that

\tau(a_{i})=N_{m-i}^{\sigma}(\sigma^{i}(\alpha))b_{i}

This means:

(i)

a_{i}=0
if and only if
b_{i}=0
for all
i\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}
.

(ii)

a_{i}
is invertible if and only if
b_{i}
is invertible for all
i\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}
. (
(i)
and
(ii)
also follow from Proposition
4.12
.)
∎

Corollary 5.17
.

If any of the following conditions hold, then
{\bf C}_{f}
and
{\bf C}_{h}
are not equivalent.

(i)

\tau(a_{m-1})\not=\sigma^{m-1}(\alpha)b_{m-1}
for all
\alpha\in S^{\times}
and all
\tau\in{\rm Aut}(S)
commuting with
\sigma
.

(ii)
There exists some
i\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}
such that
a_{i}
is invertible and

N_{S/S_{0}}(\tau(a_{i})b_{i}^{-1})\not\in(N_{S/S_{0}}(S^{\times}))^{m-i}

for all
\tau\in{\rm Aut}(S)
that commute with
\sigma
.

Proof.

The classes
{\bf C}_{f}
and
{\bf C}_{h}
of skew polycyclic codes are equivalent if and only if there
exists
\alpha\in S^{\times}
such that

\tau(a_{i})=N_{m-i}^{\sigma}(\sigma^{i}(\alpha))b_{i}.

This means:

(i)

\tau(a_{m-1})=\sigma^{m-1}(\alpha)b_{m-1}
.

(ii)
If we apply
N_{S/S_{0}}
to both sides of the equation we obtain

N_{S/S_{0}}(\tau(a_{i}))=N_{S/S_{0}}(\alpha)^{m-i}N_{S/S_{0}}(b_{i})

which is equivalent to

N_{S/S_{0}}(\tau(a_{i})b_{i}^{-1})\in(N_{S/S_{0}}(S^{\times}))^{m-i}\subset(S_{0}^{\times})^{m-i}.

∎

5.4.
Conclusions and further work

We initiated the study of skew polycyclic codes up to equivalence, identified skew polycyclic codes which are the same in terms of protecting the underlying information, and obtained some catalogues of codes up to isometries and equivalences with tools for de-duplicating them, identifying gaps on which further research may be focused. Our equivalence relations yield tighter classifications of isometric and equivalent codes that previously used ones.

Our next goal is to now look at special
S
, like finite fields or rings, and explicitly compute the equivalence classes.
The isometry classes for skew polycyclic codes seem to be hard to find, however, the special case of skew constacyclic codes seems tractable.

It was pointed out already in
[
BouUl2014
]
that even when
S
is a finite field, codes constructed with some
f\in S[t;\sigma,\delta_{\beta}]
where
\delta_{\beta}(a)=\beta(\sigma(a)-a)
is nonzero, can have a better minimum Hamming distance than those constructed when
\delta=0
. For finite fields, it is well known that
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t;\sigma,\delta_{\beta}]\cong\mathbb{F}_{q}[t;\sigma]
. However, this isomorphism of noncommutative rings changes the Hamming weight of corresponding skew
(\sigma,\delta)
-polycyclic codes, and hence their performance. Most of the current literature, however, only considers the case that
S
is a finite field or Galois ring, and
\delta=0
, e.g.
[
Bha2012
]
.

We plan to investigate the Hamming weight preserving isomorphisms, in particular
G_{\tau,\alpha}
, also when
\delta\not=0
in a future paper.
We also plan to look at the case where
S
is not commutative.

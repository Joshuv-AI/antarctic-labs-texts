Estimates for harmonic functions near pseudo-corners
Martin Costabel, Monique Dauge

Abstract
. It is well known that derivatives of solutions to elliptic boundary value problems may become unbounded near the corner of a domain with a conical singularity, even if the data are smooth. When the corner domain is approximated by more regular domains, then higher order Sobolev norms of the solutions on these domains can blow up in the limit. We study this blow-up in the simple example of the Laplace operator with Dirichlet conditions in two situations: The rounding of a corner in any dimension, and the two-dimensional situation where a polygonal corner is replaced by two or more corners with smaller angles. We show how an inner expansion derived from a more general recent result on converging expansions into generalized power series can be employed to prove simple and explicit estimations for Sobolev norms and singularity coefficients by powers of the approximation scale.



1.
Introduction

We study the behavior of Sobolev norms of the solution of the Dirichlet problem for the Laplacian near a conical singularity of the boundary, when the domain
\Omega
is approximated by a family of more regular domains
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
that is self-similar in a neighborhood of the singular point.
The approximating domains may be smooth, in which case Sobolev norms of any positive order can be estimated, or they may themselves have conical singularities, in which case the corresponding solutions only have finite regularity.
In any case, it is to be expected that Sobolev norms corresponding to a regularity above the threshold defined by the first singular exponent of the conical point will not remain bounded in the limit. In Section
4
we estimate the order of this blow-up in terms of powers of the approximation scale
\varepsilon
, see Theorem
4.1
.

In Section
5
we discuss in detail the case of a polygon in two dimensions with a non-convex corner, approximated by a finite number of corners with smaller, but still non-convex opening angles. Here it is interesting to study not only fractional Sobolev norms of order below the regularity threshold, but also the often practically important
H^{2}
regularity. Namely, for
L^{2}
right hand sides, one can write standard decompositions of the solutions into corner singular functions and regular parts in
H^{2}
. Then one can either observe the blow-up of the coefficients of the singular functions and the
H^{2}
norms of the regular parts or, conversely, assume that a family of right hand sides is given such that the solutions on
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
have
H^{2}
regularity. With each one of these right hand sides, the solutions on
\Omega
will, in general, not be
H^{2}
regular, but we show that the coefficients of the singular function of the solutions are bounded by a constant times the
L^{2}
norm of the right hand side, where the constant will tend to zero with a certain power of
\varepsilon
.

Our main technique is the expansion in convergent generalized power series in
\varepsilon
proved in the recent paper
[
4
]
. Whereas
[
4
]
considers convergence only in the energy norm
H^{1}
, we show here how these same expansions can be used to obtain in a simple way estimates for a range of Sobolev spaces of integer and fractional order.
In this way we can easily and explicitly analyze details of the asymptotic behavior of the solution of the Dirichlet problem on a class of singularly perturbed domains that has been studied in a more general context of asymptotic expansions in
[
12
]
.

These results provide transparent and easy to understand examples for the discussion of some aspects of potential theory on Lipschitz domains.

In particular, concerning the question of regularity in Sobolev spaces, they show how the norm of the solution operator of the Dirichlet problem can depend not only on the angles of the corners of a corner domain (i.e. its Lipschitz constant), but also on the distance between the corners, in contrast to the mere existence of a bounded solution operator that only depends on these angles, as long as there are only a finite number of corners.

In addition, it is seen that the choice of norm in fractional Sobolev spaces is crucial. Whereas the interpolation norm, i.e. the norm defined by Hilbert space interpolation, may provide an operator norm that remains uniformly bounded with respect to
\varepsilon
, the natural norm, i.e. the norm defined equivalently either by Sobolev-Slobodeckiĭ fractional integrals or by restriction from a larger smooth domain, may lead to an operator norm that explodes as
\varepsilon\to 0
. The two norms are then equivalent, but in a non-uniform way.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Variational solutions and their regularity in smooth domains

In this note, we address the Dirichlet problem for the Laplace operator, as a prototype of elliptic boundary value problem. In a domain (a connected open set)
{\mathscr{U}}
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
this refers to the equations that we will simply refer to as “Dirichlet problem”

(2.1)

\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{rl}\Delta u=f&\mbox{ in }{\mathscr{U}},\\
u=0&\mbox{ on }\partial{\mathscr{U}}.\end{array}\end{cases}

We consider weak solutions of problem (
2.1
), that is solutions of the variational formulation

(2.2)

u\in V,\quad\forall v\in V,\quad-\int_{\mathscr{U}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v=\int_{\mathscr{U}}f\,v

where the space
V
incorporates for
v
the conditions that
\nabla v\in L^{2}({\mathscr{U}})^{n}
and
v=0
on
\partial{\mathscr{U}}
.

If
{\mathscr{U}}
is
bounded
, one takes

V=H^{1}_{0}({\mathscr{U}})\quad\mbox{closure of ${\mathscr{C}}^{\infty}_{0}({\mathscr{U}})$ in $H^{1}({\mathscr{U}})$.}

Then for any
f
in the dual space
H^{-1}({\mathscr{U}})
of
H^{1}_{0}({\mathscr{U}})
, the solution
u
to (
2.2
) exists and is unique in
H^{1}_{0}({\mathscr{U}})
.

If, moreover, the domain
{\mathscr{U}}
has a smooth boundary, it is known since
[
1
]
, see for example
[
14
, Chapter 5]
that we have an optimal
regularity shift
for
u
if
f
is more regular in some scales of Sobolev spaces, namely

(2.3a)

if
f\in W^{m-2,p}({\mathscr{U}})
for an integer
m\geq 2
, then
u\in W^{m,p}({\mathscr{U}})
,

(here we assume
m-\frac{n}{p}>1-\frac{n}{2}
, which ensures that
W^{m-2,p}({\mathscr{U}})
is embedded in
H^{-1}({\mathscr{U}})
)

(2.3b)

if
f\in H^{s-2}({\mathscr{U}})
for a real
s\geq 2
, then
u\in H^{s}({\mathscr{U}})
.

Here for
1<p<\infty
,
W^{m,p}({\mathscr{U}})
denotes the space of
v\in L^{p}({\mathscr{U}})
such that
\partial^{\alpha}u\in L^{p}({\mathscr{U}})
for all
|\alpha|\leq m
, whereas
H^{m}({\mathscr{U}})=W^{m,2}({\mathscr{U}})
denotes the scale of Hilbertian Sobolev spaces completed by the spaces
H^{s}({\mathscr{U}})
using fractional Sobolev-Slobodeckiĭ seminorms (see also sec.
4.2
).

2.2.
Harmonic functions in regular cones

Regular cones are models in the theory of elliptic problems in domains with conical singularities as initiated by Kondrat’ev
[
10
]
. The case of the Dirichlet Laplacian is explicitly treated by Grisvard
[
7
]
. For further use we revisit the bases of this study.

A
regular cone

\Gamma
is a positively homogeneous subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that its spherical cap

\widehat{\Gamma}:=\Gamma\cap{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}

is open in
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
and has a smooth, non empty, boundary
\partial\widehat{\Gamma}\subset{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
. We assume here that
\widehat{\Gamma}
is connected.

Written in polar coordinates
(r,\vartheta)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
, the cone
\Gamma
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times\widehat{\Gamma}
and the Laplace operator associated with Dirichlet conditions on
\partial\Gamma
becomes

\partial^{2}_{r}+(n-1)r^{-1}\partial_{r}+r^{-2}\Delta^{\sf dir}_{\widehat{\Gamma}}

where
\Delta^{\sf dir}_{\widehat{\Gamma}}
is the spherical Laplacian (Laplace-Beltrami operator) with Dirichlet conditions on
\partial\widehat{\Gamma}
.
The operator
-\Delta^{\sf dir}_{\widehat{\Gamma}}
is an unbounded selfadjoint and positive operator in
L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})
with domain
H^{2}\cap H^{1}_{0}(\widehat{\Gamma})
. Its eigenvalues
\mu_{j}
form an unbounded sequence and the associated eigenfunctions
\psi_{j}
form an orthonormal basis in
L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma})
. The assumptions on
\widehat{\Gamma}
imply that

(2.4)

0<\mu_{1}<\mu_{2}\leq\mu_{3}\leq\ldots.

The eigen-equation

-\Delta^{\sf dir}_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\psi_{j}=\mu_{j}\,\psi_{j},\quad\forall j\geq 1

implies that any function of the form
r^{\lambda}\psi_{j}
(with
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
) satisfies

\Delta(r^{\lambda}\psi_{j})=(\lambda^{2}+(n-2)\lambda-\mu_{j})\psi_{j}\,.

Introducing the roots
\lambda_{j}^{\pm}
of the equation
\lambda^{2}+(n-2)\lambda-\mu_{j}=0

(2.5)

\lambda_{j}^{\pm}=1-\frac{n}{2}\pm\sqrt{\left(1-\frac{n}{2}\right)^{2}+\mu_{j}}

we find that the functions

(2.6)

h_{j}^{\pm}(x):=r^{\lambda_{j}^{\pm}}\psi_{j}(\vartheta)

are harmonic in
\Gamma
, and
0
on
\partial\Gamma
.

In dimension
n=2
, cones
\Gamma
are plane sectors, and we can choose polar coordinates so that
\widehat{\Gamma}=(0,\omega)
for some
\omega\in(0,2\pi)
. Then

\mu_{j}=\left(\frac{j\pi}{\omega}\right)^{2}
,

\psi_{j}(\theta)=\sin\left(\frac{j\pi\theta}{\omega}\right)
, and

\lambda^{\pm}_{j}=\pm\frac{j\pi}{\omega}
.

2.3.
Dirichlet problem in corner domains

To simplify the exposition, we call corner domain a bounded connected open set
\Omega
that coincides with a regular cone
\Gamma
in a neighborhood of its vertex
0
and is smooth everywhere else. More formally, denote by
{\mathscr{B}}(\rho)
the ball with center
0
and radius
\rho
, and
{\mathscr{B}}^{\complement}(\rho)
its complement in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
Then
\Omega
is such that for some
r_{0}>0

\Omega\cap{\mathscr{B}}(r_{0})=\Gamma\cap{\mathscr{B}}(r_{0})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\partial\Omega\cap{\mathscr{B}}^{\complement}(r_{0}/2)\;\mbox{ regular}.

Let
\varphi
be a
smooth cutoff
function such that

(2.7)

\varphi=1\quad\mbox{on }{\mathscr{B}}(r_{0}/2)\,,\qquad\varphi=0\quad\mbox{on }{\mathscr{B}}^{\complement}(r_{0}).

The following lemma shows in which sense the functions
h^{+}_{j}
can be considered as
singularities of the Dirichlet problem
and is a straightforward consequence of their degree of homogeneity.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
u=\varphi h^{+}_{j}
i.e.
u(x)=\varphi(x)\,r^{\lambda^{+}_{j}}\psi_{j}(\vartheta)
.
Denote by
{\mathbb{P}}
the space of polynomials in the variable
x
. Then

(1)

u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
(since
\lambda^{+}_{j}>0\geq 1-\frac{n}{2}
)

(2)

\Delta u=0
in
\Omega\cap{\mathscr{B}}(r_{0}/2)
and
\Delta u\in{\mathscr{C}}^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
,

(3)

u\in W^{m,p}(\Omega)
if and only if

{
\lambda^{+}_{j}>m-\frac{n}{p}
or
h^{+}_{j}\in{\mathbb{P}}

}
.

It is much less straightforward to prove that, when the right hand side
f
is regular and flat near the vertex, the functions
h^{+}_{j}
with
\lambda^{+}_{j}<m-\frac{n}{p}
are the only possible singular functions. We give two statements that illustrate this principle.

The first statement (which will be used in the sequel) is an expansion of harmonic functions in the vicinity of the vertex. It relies on classical expansions along the eigenbasis of the operator
\Delta^{\sf dir}_{\widehat{\Gamma}}
. Let us introduce the spaces

(2.8)

\mathfrak{F}_{\Omega}:=\{g\in L^{2}(\Omega),\;\;g\big|_{{\mathscr{B}}(\frac{r_{0}}{2})}=0\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathfrak{E}_{\Omega}:=\{u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\;\;\Delta u\in\mathfrak{F}_{\Omega}\}.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
4
]
)
.

Let
u\in\mathfrak{E}_{\Omega}
.
Write
u(x)=\widetilde{u}(r,\vartheta)
for
x\in\Omega\cap{\mathscr{B}}(r_{0})
.
Then for all
j\geq 1
and
\rho\in(0,\frac{r_{0}}{2}]
, the functional

(2.9)

{\mathbf{c}}_{j}(u):u\mapsto\displaystyle\rho^{-\lambda^{+}_{j}}\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\widetilde{u}(\rho,\vartheta)\;\psi_{j}(\vartheta)\,{\mathrm{d}}\sigma_{\vartheta}

is well defined and independent of
\rho
. The sequence
\{\mathbf{c}_{j}(u)\}
satisfies for all
\rho\in(0,\frac{r_{0}}{2}]
:

(2.10)

\sum_{j\geq 1}\lambda^{+}_{j}\rho^{2\lambda^{+}_{j}+n-2}\,|\mathbf{c}_{j}(u)|^{2}=\int_{\Gamma\cap{\mathscr{B}}(\rho)}|\nabla u|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}x

and we have the representation formula for
u
:

u=\sum_{j\geq 1}\;\mathbf{c}_{j}(u)\,h^{+}_{j}\quad\mbox{with convergence in $H^{1}(\Gamma\cap{{\mathscr{B}}(\frac{r_{0}}{2})})$.}

The second statement is an illustration in the particular case of problem (
2.1
) of very general statements that stem from the theory of elliptic boundary value problem in domains with conical singularities.
Set for
\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}
the weighted version of the space
W^{m,p}(\Omega)

W^{m,p}_{\beta}(\Omega)=\{v\in L^{p}_{\rm loc}(\Omega),\quad r^{|\alpha|-m+\beta}\partial^{\alpha}u\in L^{p}(\Omega),\quad|\alpha|\leq m\}.

Note that

•

\Delta
is continuous from
W^{m,p}_{\beta}(\Omega)
into
W^{m-2,p}_{\beta}(\Omega)
,

•

\varphi h^{+}_{j}\in W^{m,p}_{\beta}(\Omega)
if and only if
\lambda^{+}_{j}>m-\frac{n}{p}-\beta
.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
10
]
for
p=2
,
[
13
]
for general
p
)
.

Let
p>1
,
m\geq 2
and
\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}
so that
W^{m,p}_{\beta}(\Omega)
is embedded in
H^{1}(\Omega)
.
Let
u
solution of (
2.1
) with
f\in W^{m-2,p}_{\beta}(\Omega)
.
Assume
\lambda^{+}_{j}\neq m-\frac{n}{p}-\beta
for all
j\geq 1
.
Then there exist coefficients
c_{j}
for
j
such that
\lambda^{+}_{j}<m-\frac{n}{p}-\beta
satisfying

u\;-\;\varphi\!\!\sum_{j\geq 1,\;\lambda^{+}_{j}\,<\,m-\frac{n}{p}-\beta}c_{j}\,h^{+}_{j}\in W^{m,p}_{\beta}(\Omega)\,.

Remark 2.4
.

It is not difficult to see that
for
f\in W^{m,p}(\Omega)\cap\mathfrak{F}_{\Omega}

the coefficients
c_{j}
of the singular functions
h^{+}_{j}
in the decomposition into regular and singular parts of Theorem
2.3
are the same as the coefficients
\mathbf{c}_{j}(u)
in the eigenfunction expansion of Theorem

. We can therefore use the representation (
2.9
) for the coefficients of the singular functions. As an application of this observation, we obtain, for example, that if in a neighborhood of the corner,

is non-negative and not identically zero, then the coefficient

of the first singular function is non-zero. Indeed, from the maximum principle for harmonic functions follows that for

in the integral (
2.9
) both
\widetilde{u}
and
\psi_{1}
are positive in the interior of
\widehat{\Gamma}
.

4.
Blow-up of Sobolev norms in smooth pseudo-corner domains

Let us recall that the spherical cap

\frac{2\pi}{\omega}+1-s>2-s>0
, and
\mathchoice{|\hskip 0.6458ptK^{+}_{1}\hskip 0.6458pt|_{\raise-1.80835pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle H^{s}({\mathsf{Q}})$}}\,}{|K^{+}_{1}|_{H^{s}({\mathsf{Q}})}}{|K^{+}_{1}|_{H^{s}({\mathsf{Q}})}}{|K^{+}_{1}|_{H^{s}({\mathsf{Q}})}}>0
we deduce that for all
s,\;s_{\Omega}<s<s_{\mathsf{P}}
,

(5.9)

|\hskip-0.6458pt|\hskip-0.6458pt|T_{\sigma}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})|\hskip-0.6458pt|\hskip-0.6458pt|\geq\gamma\,\varepsilon^{s_{\Omega}-s}\,,

for
\varepsilon
small enough with a
\gamma>0
independent of
\varepsilon
.

By contrast, the basic estimate for an exact interpolation scale
yields

|\hskip-0.6458pt|\hskip-0.6458pt|T_{\sigma}^{\heartsuit}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})|\hskip-0.6458pt|\hskip-0.6458pt|\;\leq\;|\hskip-0.6458pt|\hskip-0.6458pt|T_{1}^{\heartsuit}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})|\hskip-0.6458pt|\hskip-0.6458pt|^{\sigma}\;|\hskip-0.6458pt|\hskip-0.6458pt|T_{0}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})|\hskip-0.6458pt|\hskip-0.6458pt|^{1-\sigma}.

Since the domains
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
are uniformly bounded polygons and owing to (
5.7
) we have
uniform bounds

|\hskip-0.6458pt|\hskip-0.6458pt|T^{\heartsuit}_{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})|\hskip-0.6458pt|\hskip-0.6458pt|\leq M\quad\mbox{and}\quad|\hskip-0.6458pt|\hskip-0.6458pt|T_{0}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})|\hskip-0.6458pt|\hskip-0.6458pt|\leq M

hence uniform bounds on
|\hskip-0.6458pt|\hskip-0.6458pt|T_{\sigma}^{\heartsuit}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})|\hskip-0.6458pt|\hskip-0.6458pt|
by interpolation.

The apparent contradiction with (
5.9
) disappears when one understands that the operator norms
|\hskip-0.6458pt|\hskip-0.6458pt|T_{\sigma}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})|\hskip-0.6458pt|\hskip-0.6458pt|
and
|\hskip-0.6458pt|\hskip-0.6458pt|T_{\sigma}^{\heartsuit}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})|\hskip-0.6458pt|\hskip-0.6458pt|
are based on distinct norms in the spaces
H^{-1+\sigma}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\simeq A_{\sigma}^{\heartsuit}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
: The norm in
H^{-1+\sigma}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
is the usual norm, while the norm in
A_{\sigma}^{\heartsuit}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
is the interpolation norm of
\big[A_{1}^{\heartsuit}({\mathscr{U}}),A_{0}({\mathscr{U}})\big]_{1-\sigma}
. We have with
f=\Delta(\varphi h^{+}_{1})
and
u_{\varepsilon}=T(\Omega_{\varepsilon})f

\frac{\mathchoice{\|\hskip 0.6458ptu_{\varepsilon}\hskip 0.6458pt\|_{\raise-1.80835pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle H^{s}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})$}}\,}{\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{s}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}}{\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{s}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}}{\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{s}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}}}{\mathchoice{\|\hskip 0.6458ptf\hskip 0.6458pt\|_{\raise-1.80835pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle H^{s-2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})$}}\,}{\|f\|_{H^{s-2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}}{\|f\|_{H^{s-2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}}{\|f\|_{H^{s-2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}}}\geq\gamma\,\varepsilon^{s_{\Omega}-s}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{\mathchoice{\|\hskip 0.6458ptu_{\varepsilon}\hskip 0.6458pt\|_{\raise-1.80835pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle B_{\sigma}^{\heartsuit}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})$}}\,}{\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{B_{\sigma}^{\heartsuit}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}}{\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{B_{\sigma}^{\heartsuit}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}}{\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{B_{\sigma}^{\heartsuit}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}}}{\mathchoice{\|\hskip 0.6458ptf\hskip 0.6458pt\|_{\raise-1.80835pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle A_{\sigma}^{\heartsuit}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})$}}\,}{\|f\|_{A_{\sigma}^{\heartsuit}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}}{\|f\|_{A_{\sigma}^{\heartsuit}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}}{\|f\|_{A_{\sigma}^{\heartsuit}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}}}\leq M\,.

Since for target spaces, by (
5.8
) the spaces
B_{\sigma}^{\heartsuit}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
have a norm uniformly equivalent to the
H^{s}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
norm we deduce (recall that
\sigma=s-1
)

(5.10)

\mathchoice{\|\hskip 0.6458ptf\hskip 0.6458pt\|_{\raise-1.80835pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle A_{\sigma}^{\heartsuit}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})$}}\,}{\|f\|_{A_{\sigma}^{\heartsuit}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}}{\|f\|_{A_{\sigma}^{\heartsuit}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}}{\|f\|_{A_{\sigma}^{\heartsuit}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}}\geq\gamma^{\prime}\,\varepsilon^{s_{\Omega}-s}\mathchoice{\|\hskip 0.6458ptf\hskip 0.6458pt\|_{\raise-1.80835pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle H^{s-2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})$}}\,}{\|f\|_{H^{s-2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}}{\|f\|_{H^{s-2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}}{\|f\|_{H^{s-2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}}

for a constant
\gamma^{\prime}>0
independent of
\varepsilon
.

When
s
reaches the threshold
s_{\mathsf{P}}
, the space
A_{\sigma}^{\heartsuit}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
does not coincide any more with
H^{-1+\sigma}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
, and is not even closed in it.
This phenomenon is in accordance with what has been observed in more general studies of interpolation of subspaces with finite codimension
[
3
,
15
]
.
Results of the latter would, for
1\leq s<s_{{\mathsf{P}}}
, imply the equality of spaces

A_{s-1}^{\heartsuit}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})=H^{s-2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})

with equivalence of norms, but would not provide estimates of the constants in this equivalence which, as we have seen in (
5.10
), are not uniformly bounded as
\varepsilon\to 0
if
s_{\Omega}<s<s_{{\mathsf{P}}}
.

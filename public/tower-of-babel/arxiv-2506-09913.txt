A Note on the Reliability of Goal-Oriented Error Estimates for Galerkin Finite Element Methods with Nonlinear Functionals Journal: arXiv
Brian N. Granzow, Stephen D. Bond, D. Thomas Seidl, Bernhard Endtmayer

Abstract
We consider estimating the discretization error in a nonlinear functional J ⁡ ( u ) J(u) in the setting of an abstract variational problem: find u ∈ 𝒱 u\in\mathcal{V} such that B ⁡ ( u , φ ) = L ⁡ ( φ ) ​ ∀ φ ∈ 𝒱 B(u,\varphi)=L(\varphi)\;\forall\varphi\in\mathcal{V} , as approximated by a Galerkin finite element method. Here, 𝒱 \mathcal{V} is a Hilbert space, B ⁡ ( ⋅ , ⋅ ) B(\cdot,\cdot) is a bilinear form, and L ⁡ ( ⋅ ) L(\cdot) is a linear functional. We consider well-known error estimates η \eta of the form J ⁡ ( u ) − J ⁡ ( u h ) ≈ η = L ⁡ ( z ) − B ⁡ ( u h , z ) J(u)-J(u_{h})\approx\eta=L(z)-B(u_{h},z) , where u h u_{h} denotes a finite element approximation to u u , and z z denotes the solution to an auxiliary adjoint variational problem. We show that there exist nonlinear functionals for which error estimates of this form are not reliable, even in the presence of an exact adjoint solution solution z z . An estimate η \eta is said to be reliable if there exists a constant C ∈ ℝ > 0 C\in\mathbb{R}_{>0} independent of u h u_{h} such that | J ⁡ ( u ) − J ⁡ ( u h ) | ≤ C ​ | η | |J(u)-J(u_{h})|\leq C|\eta| . We present several example pairs of bilinear forms and nonlinear functionals where reliability of η \eta is not achieved.



1
Introduction

A posteriori
error estimation
[
1
,
2
]
provides useful tools to approximate and control
discretization errors in finite element settings. Goal-oriented error
estimation
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
measures the discretization error in a functional
quantity of interest, rather than in a global norm. Inherent to the process of
goal-oriented error estimation is a linearization and a discard of higher order
information. In a recent article,
[
11
]
, we described
this discard as a
linearization error
and presented several examples
where traditional adjoint-weighted residual error estimates fail to achieve
effectivity (an accurate approximation the functional discretization error).
In this note, we provide further insight into this loss of effectivity by
showing that there exist specific choices of nonlinear functionals for which
certain adjoint-weighted residual estimates are not
reliable
, the
setting for which is described in the abstract.

2
Preliminaries

Consider the following variational problems:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Find}\,u\in\mathcal{V}\;\mathrm{s.t.}\;B(u,\varphi)=L(\varphi)\quad\forall\varphi\in\mathcal{V},

(1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Find}\,u_{h}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}\;\mathrm{s.t.}\;B(u_{h},\varphi_{h})=L(\varphi_{h})\quad\forall\varphi_{h}\in\mathcal{V}_{h},

(2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Find}\,u_{h}^{\texttt{+}}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}^{\texttt{+}}\;\mathrm{s.t.}\;B(u_{h}^{\texttt{+}},\varphi_{h}^{\texttt{+}})=L(\varphi_{h}^{\texttt{+}})\quad\forall\varphi_{h}^{\texttt{+}}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}^{\texttt{+}},

(3)

where
\mathcal{V}
denotes a Hilbert space,

B(\cdot,\cdot):\mathcal{V}\times\mathcal{V}\to\mathbb{R}

denotes a bilinear form,
L(\cdot):\mathcal{V}\to\mathbb{R}
denotes
a linear functional, and
\mathcal{V}_{h}
and
\mathcal{V}_{h}^{\texttt{+}}
denote finite dimensional
subspaces of
\mathcal{V}
such that
\mathcal{V}_{h}\subset\mathcal{V}_{h}^{\texttt{+}}\subset\mathcal{V}
, where
h\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
denotes a discretization parameter.

Let
J(u):\mathcal{V}\to\mathbb{R}
denote a nonlinear functional with
sufficient regularity such that its first and second Fréchet
derivatives
J^{\prime}
and
J^{\prime\prime}
, respectively, exist. We seek to approximate the
(assumed well-posed) primal problem (
1
) with a Galerkin
finite element method (
2
) and estimate the functional
discretization error
J(u)-J(u_{h})
with the introduction of an
auxiliary linear
adjoint problem
. Presently, we consider the
following variational problems:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Find}\,z\in\mathcal{V}\;\mathrm{s.t.}\;B(\varphi,z)=J^{\prime}(u_{h};\varphi)\quad\forall\varphi\in\mathcal{V},

(4)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Find}\,z_{h}^{\texttt{+}}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}^{\texttt{+}}\;\mathrm{s.t.}\;B(\varphi_{h}^{\texttt{+}},z_{h}^{\texttt{+}})=J^{\prime}(u_{h};\varphi_{h}^{\texttt{+}})\quad\forall\varphi_{h}^{\texttt{+}}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}^{\texttt{+}},

(5)

where we refer to the first variational problem (
4
) as
the continuous adjoint problem and (
5
) represents
a finite element approximation to the continuous adjoint problem.
It is well known that if the adjoint problem is approximated in the
space
\mathcal{V}_{h}
, then traditional adjoint-weighted residual error
estimates will be erroneously zero due to
Galerkin orthogonality
:

B(e,\varphi_{h})=0\;\forall\varphi_{h}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}
, where

e:=u-u_{h}
denotes the discretization error. To this end, we
consider approximating the adjoint problem in the richer finite-dimensional
space
\mathcal{V}_{h}^{\texttt{+}}
.

Remark 1
.

Practically, we consider only standard piecewise polynomial finite element
spaces
\mathcal{V}_{h}
and globally enriched spaces
\mathcal{V}_{h}^{\texttt{+}}
obtained either
by: (a) uniformly refining the finite element mesh that underlies the space

\mathcal{V}_{h}
or (b) increasing the piecewise polynomial order of the space

\mathcal{V}_{h}
by one. The following discussion should, however, apply in
broader contexts.

Definition 1
.

(Functional derivatives) The first and second Gâteaux derivatives of
a functional
J:\mathcal{V}\to\mathbb{R}
are defined as

\displaystyle J^{\prime}(u;v)

\displaystyle:=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{-1}\left[J(u+\varepsilon v)-J(u)\right]=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\varepsilon}J(u+\varepsilon v)\biggr|_{\varepsilon=0},

(6)

\displaystyle J^{\prime\prime}(u;v,w)

\displaystyle:=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{-1}\left[J^{\prime}(u+\varepsilon w;v)-J^{\prime}(u;v)\right]=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\varepsilon}J^{\prime}(u+\varepsilon w;v)\biggr|_{\varepsilon=0},

(7)

respectively. These derivatives agree with their corresponding Fréchet derivatives
when the functional is once and twice Fréchet differentiable.

3
Results

Lemma
4
, in part, provides assurance that

\eta_{1}
will provide a useful error estimate. It is rare, however,
to account for the remainder term

\int_{0}^{1}J^{\prime\prime}(u_{h}+se_{h}^{\texttt{+}};e_{h}^{\texttt{+}},e_{h}^{\texttt{+}})(1-s)\,\mathrm{d}s
in
the performance of goal-oriented error estimation. The neglect of
this term is what we phrase a
linearization error
. We now
show that neglecting this remainder term can lead to unreliable
error estimates for certain functionals within the context of the
chosen adjoint problem and the error estimates presented below (see the
discussions section for further elaboration). In the following,
we let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
denote an open, bounded domain, where

d=1,2,3
denotes a spatial dimension.

Theorem 1
.

Let
u
,
u_{h}
, and
z
solve Equations (
1
),
(
2
), and (
4
), respectively, and
let
J(u)
be chosen so that
J^{\prime}(u_{h};e)=B(e,\psi_{h})
for some

\psi_{h}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}
. Then the estimate

\eta_{2}\approx J(u)-J(u_{h})
is not reliable, where

\eta_{2}:=L(z)-B(u_{h},z).

(15)

Proof.

\displaystyle J^{\prime}(u_{h};e)=B(e,\psi_{h}),

\displaystyle\text{by assumption}.

\displaystyle J^{\prime}(u_{h};e)=B(e,z),

\displaystyle\text{by Equation \eqref{eq:adjoint}}.

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle B(e,\psi_{h})=B(e,z),

\displaystyle\text{equating the above expressions}.

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle z\in\mathcal{V}_{h},

\displaystyle\text{by inspection},

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\eta_{2}=L(z)-B(u_{h},z)=0,

\displaystyle\text{by Equations \eqref{eq:eta2}, \eqref{eq:primal_fem}}.

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\nexists\,C\,\mathrm{s.t.}\,|J(u)-J(u_{h})|\leq C|\eta_{2}|.

∎

Stated otherwise, Theorem
1
demonstrates that there exist
functionals for which the entirety of the functional discretization error
is contained within the second-order remainder term
J^{\prime\prime}
in the expansion
(
8
), i.e.
J^{\prime}(u_{h};e)=0
, because the term

J^{\prime}(u_{h};e)
mimics the Galerkin orthogonality principle of the original
variational problem.

.

Let
u_{h}

3

\eta_{3}\approx J(u)-J(u_{h})

\eta_{3}:=L(z_{h}^{\texttt{+}})-B(u_{h},z_{h}^{\texttt{+}}).

(16)

The result follows directly from Theorem

Theorem 3
.

Let
u
,
u_{h}
,
u_{h}^{\texttt{+}}
,
z
, and
z_{h}^{\texttt{+}}
solve Equations
(
1
), (
2
), (

),
(

), and (

be symmetric, let

Proof.

\displaystyle J^{\prime}(u_{h};e)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\varepsilon}G(B(u_{h}+\varepsilon e,u_{h}+\varepsilon e))\biggr|_{\varepsilon=0},

\displaystyle=G^{\prime}(B(u_{h}+\varepsilon e,u_{h}+\varepsilon e))\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\varepsilon}B(u_{h}+\varepsilon e,u_{h}+\varepsilon e)\biggr|_{\varepsilon=0},

\displaystyle=G^{\prime}(B(u_{h},u_{h}))2B(u_{h},e),

\displaystyle\text{by bilinearity},

\displaystyle=2G^{\prime}(B(u_{h},u_{h}))B(e,u_{h}),

\displaystyle\text{by symmetry},

\displaystyle=B(e,\hat{C}u_{h}),

\displaystyle\text{by linearity},

\hat{C}:=2G^{\prime}(B(u_{h},u_{h}))\in\mathbb{R}

so that
\hat{C}u_{h}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}
. Theorems

and

and the result follows.
∎

Example 1
.

(Poisson’s equation):
Let
B(u,\varphi):=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\Omega

and
J(u):=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla u\,\mathrm{d}\Omega
.
Then we have

J^{\prime}(u_{h};e)=\int_{\Omega}\nabla e\cdot(2\nabla u_{h})\,\mathrm{d}\Omega=B(e,2u_{h}),

and since
2u_{h}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}
, Theorems
1
and
2

hold.

Example 2
.

(Isotropic linear elasticity):
Let
B(u,\varphi):=\int_{\Omega}\sigma(u):\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\Omega.

and let
J(u):=\sqrt{\int_{\Omega}\sigma(u):\epsilon(u)}

be the so-called
strain-energy density
. Here,

u
and
\varphi
are vector-valued,
\epsilon
and
\sigma
are second-order
tensor-valued,
\epsilon(u):=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla u+\nabla u^{T})
,
and
\sigma(u):=\lambda\mathrm{tr}(\epsilon(u))I+2\mu\epsilon(u)
,
where
I
is the second-order identity tensor,
\lambda
denotes Lamé’s first
parameter, and
\mu
denotes a shear modulus. It not difficult to show that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\sigma(u):\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\Omega=\int_{\Omega}\sigma(u):\epsilon(\varphi)\,\mathrm{d}\Omega,

and that
B(u,\varphi)
is symmetric. Thus,
J(u)=\sqrt{B(u,u)}
and
Corollary
1
holds.

4
Discussion

In this note, we have illustrated that a certain class of nonlinear
functionals can lead to unreliable error estimates
for a particular
choice of adjoint problem statement, adjoint discretization, and
error estimate
. To elaborate, defining the adjoint problem itself presents
a choice about where linearizations occur. Presently, we have chosen the
point
u_{h}
to carry about linearizations. Our motivation for doing so
stems primarily from familiarity with the
discrete
goal-oriented
error estimation literature
[
7
]
, where this is common.
This background ultimately led us to investigations of linearization errors

[
11
]
, which we have further understood in this
note.

It is also common, however, in the literature

[
4
,
10
]
,
to carry about linearizations with respect to the exact solution
u

and derive an error representation that is higher-order than the ones
considered in this note. This representation contains the primal

and
adjoint residuals as functions of the discretization errors
e:=u-u_{h}
and

e^{*}:=z-z_{h}
. In this context, it is common to approximate the
discretization error as
e\approx u_{h}^{\texttt{+}}-u_{h}
and, since one has
access to the fine-space primal solution,
u_{h}^{\texttt{+}}
, it is possible to
approximate the adjoint problem by linearizing about the fine-space
solution
u_{h}^{\texttt{+}}
. We suggest investigating linearization errors in
this setting as an avenue for future work.

Lastly, we remark that we have observed something similarly akin to
a loss of reliability in the context of
nonlinear
variational problems

[
11
]
and not simply within the setting of bilinear forms.
Further, we numerically observed poor error estimates for
local

quantities of interest (e.g. ones integrated over only a sub-domain), where
Galerkin orthogonality does not strictly hold.
We leave investigation into both of these matters as an avenue for
interesting future work.

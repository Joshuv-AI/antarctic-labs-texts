Co-evolutionary Balance State of the Autism inter-Brain Network: A Neurofunctional Framework for Biomarker Discovery Publication type: Research
S. Rezaei Afshar, H. Pouretemad, G. Reza Jafari

Abstract
Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) is a neurodevelopmental condition characterized by deficits in social communication and repetitive behaviors; however, objective neurophysiological biomarkers are still not identified. We present a coevolutionary balance paradigm that quantifies network-level energy through a Hamiltonian that incorporates regional activity (fALFF) and resting-state functional connectivity (FC). Analysis of rs-fMRI data from 93 adult males with ASD and 93 matched controls revealed that empirical networks demonstrated reduced energy compared to 1,000 topology-preserving null models (paired t = – ​ 4.12 , p ≤ 1 ​ × ​ 10 − 4 t=–4.12,p\leq 1\texttimes 10^{-4} ). ASD exhibited a greater negative whole-brain energy ( t = – ​ 3.239 , p = 0.0015 t=–3.239,p=0.0015 ), influenced by an increase in agreement links and a reduction in imbalanced-same motifs. Subnetwork analysis indicated increased Default Mode Network energy following FDR correction (pFDR¡0.016) and enhanced inter-network energy among the Default Mode, Salience, and Dorsal Attention networks (pFDR¡0.032). Energy metrics and inter-network connectivity exhibited a correlation with ADI-R/ADOS severity ( | r | ≥ 0.29 , p < 0.02 |r|\geq 0.29,p<0.02 ). A k-nearest neighbors classifier, utilizing nine principal features such as motif proportions, global node-link alignment, inter-network fALFF-weighted and FC strengths, subnetwork magnetization, and pairwise energy, attained an accuracy of 79% with balanced sensitivity and specificity. These findings indicate that coevolutionary energy effectively identifies interpretable network disruptions and establishes a solid framework for ASD classification.

Introduction

ASD, which stands for autism spectrum disorder, is a collection of neurodevelopmental disorders that manifest themselves during childhood and continue throughout an individual’s entire life
American Psychiatric Association (2022)
. Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) can manifest itself in a wide variety of cognitive and language abilities, ranging from the inability to speak to an IQ that is significantly higher than the average. In addition, it is possible that they are suffering from additional conditions, such as attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD), anxiety, and epilepsy, which further complicates the process of diagnosing and treating them
American Psychiatric Association (2022)
. New research on the population reveals that the number of people who have autism spectrum disorder (ASD) is continuing to rise. Approximately 2.76 percent of eight-year-olds in the United States were affected by it as of the year 2020
Maenner (2023)
. Because of this growing trend as well as the clinical and causal complexities of the condition, it is abundantly clear that we require objective and physiologically based biomarkers in order to assist us in locating, classifying, and treating individuals who are affected by the disorder at an earlier stage.

rs-fMRI, which stands for resting-state functional magnetic resonance imaging, is a non-invasive method that allows one to observe the spontaneous activity of the brain. This is accomplished by monitoring changes in blood-oxygenation-level-dependent (BOLD) signals without the need to perform any particular tasks. You can determine the strength of these natural oscillations in the range of 0.01 to 0.08 Hz by calculating the amplitude of low-frequency fluctuations, also known as ALFF value. On the other hand, the fractional amplitude of low-frequency fluctuations, also known as fALFF, is a measure that illustrates the power that is restricted to particular bands in comparison to the total detectable frequency spectrum. This makes it simpler to locate neural activity that is localized and reduces the interference that is caused by activity that is not related to neurons
Zang (2007)
;
Zou (2008)
. The term ”functional connectivity” (FC) refers to the statistical relationships that are examined between the BOLD signal time series of various regions of the brain, typically through the use of Pearson correlation. Synchrony patterns that are typical of large networks that are involved in cognitive, sensorimotor, and emotional functions are revealed as a result of this
Smith (2013)
.

In studies that examine rs-fMRI data using graph-theoretic methods, researchers have repeatedly discovered changes in the network topology of the brain that are associated with autism spectrum disorder (ASD). As an illustration, the researchers have discovered issues with network segregation, such as a decreased clustering coefficient or a modified modular structure, and integration, such as longer path lengths or a decrease in global efficiency, in well-established systems such as the default mode, salience, and attention networks
Assaf et al. (2010)
;
Uddin et al. (2013)
. Despite the fact that these methods have demonstrated significant variations in the topology, they typically treat node activities (such as fALFF) and edge strengths (such as FC weights) as two distinct variables. There is a possibility that this will reduce the likelihood that higher-order emergent features that result from their interaction will be noticed.

At the neurobiological level, there is a growing body of evidence suggesting that macro-scale network metrics, such as fALFF and FC, are proxies for the underlying excitation–inhibition (E/I) balance and synaptic homeostasis, which are processes that are dysregulated in autism spectrum disorder (ASD). A hyperexcitable cortical circuit is the result of a neocortical E/I imbalance, which manifests as a change in the amplitude and synchrony of spontaneous BOLD fluctuations, as well as anomalies in long-range connectivity within the default mode and salience networks
Yizhar et al. (2011)
;
Dinstein et al. (2011)
. Furthermore, disruptions in salience network dynamics, which can be identified through FC patterns, have been found to have a correlation with the severity of symptoms in individuals with autism spectrum disorder (ASD)
Uddin et al. (2013)
. These disturbances at the synaptic and circuit levels are inherently captured by coevolutionary energy metrics. These metrics integrate nodal states (binarized fALFF) and link polarities in order to quantify network stability within a Hamiltonian framework.

In the field of social psychology, Heider was the first person to propose the concept of structural balance theory. After some time had passed, Cartwright and Harary initiated an official investigation into the stability of signed networks. The process involves assigning positive or negative signs to the edges and ensuring that there is consistency within the triads. cite: Heider (1946ac) and Cartwright (1956). Within the scope of this discussion, the ”energy” of the network provides information regarding the degree of tension or imbalance that exists between all of the node triplets. Recent developments in the field of coevolutionary balance theory have resulted in the integration of link signals and nodal states into a comprehensive Hamiltonian model. The results of this model demonstrate that the total amount of regional activity as well as the polarity of connections shift over time, resulting in a decrease in the energy of the network. Kargaran: 2021he, Ghanbarzadeh Noudehi 2022, and Ghanbarzadeh Noudehi 2025 are cited accordingly. In a more formal sense, this is referred to as the Hamiltonian term.

H(G)\;=\;-\sum_{i<j}s_{i}\,\sigma_{ij}\,s_{j},

where
s_{i}\in\{-1,+1\}
represents the binarized state of the region
i
(for instance, high versus low fALFF) and
\sigma_{ij}\in\{-1,+1\}
represents the sign of the functional link between regions
i
and
j
, respectively. The presence of lower values for the H(G) variable indicates that regions that are highly active are positively linked, while regions that are not highly active are negatively linked. Because of this, the network alignment is more advantageous from an energetic standpoint.

This research employs a coevolutionary balance paradigm within the ABIDE I repository, focusing on male individuals between the ages of 18 and 30
Di Martino et al. (2014)
. The purpose of this study is to reduce biases that are associated with gender and developmental differences. To preprocess all of the datasets, the CPAC pipeline was utilized. This pipeline was responsible for dividing the brain into 200 distinct regions, which were derived from the CC200 atlas
Craddock et al. (2012)
;
Uddin et al. (2023)
. The model was validated by constructing null networks that maintained the topology. This was accomplished by randomly altering the regional fALFF values and FC weights. Subsequently, we compared these null networks to the actual continuous energy values that resulted from these alterations
Nichols & Holmes (2002)
. During the process of identifying and eliminating outliers, we employed Tukey’s interquartile range method
Tukey (1977)
. In order to determine whether or not there were differences in continuous fALFF–FC alignment between groups, we investigated the difference in whole-brain energy. In our study, we employed both parametric and nonparametric methods, specifically the Shapiro–Wilk test for normality (Shapiro:1965sw) and Levene’s test for equal variances (Levene:1960). When it came to modifying the p-values for the binary coevolutionary energy in each of the seven canonical resting-state networks that Yeo and his team established
Yeo et al. (2011)
, we utilized the false discovery rate method
Benjamini & Hochberg (1995)
. In conclusion, we utilized support vector machines and k-nearest neighbor algorithms in order to determine the degree to which global and subnetwork energy features were able to differentiate between two groups. A stratified five-fold cross-validation was utilized in order to accomplish this. The entire analytical process is depicted in Figure
1
, beginning with the preparation of the data and continuing through the determination of the amount of energy required and the testing of machine learning.

2.2
Imaging Preprocessing and fALFF Computation

All functional figs were preprocessed using CPAC v1.0
Craddock et al. (2013)
. Steps comprised slice‐timing correction (sinc interpolation), rigid‐body motion correction (24 motion regressors: six realignment parameters+derivatives+squares), co‐registration to each subject’s T1 image, normalization to MNI152 space (3mm isotropic), and spatial smoothing (6mm FWHM). Nuisance regression removed linear and quadratic trends, global signal, white‐matter and CSF signals, and motion regressors. The residual time series were band‐pass filtered (0.01–0.1Hz) using a zero‐phase Butterworth filter. Voxel‐wise fALFF was computed via FFT: ALFF was defined as the mean square‐rooted power in 0.01–0.08Hz, divided by the total power in 0–0.25Hz
Zang (2007)
;
Zou (2008)
. Mean fALFF values were then extracted for each CC200 atlas ROI
Craddock et al. (2012)
.

2.3
Parcellation and Time Series Harmonization

We parcellated the brain into 200 ROIs using the CC200 atlas in MNI space. To ensure equal sample size across sites, each subject’s ROI time series was truncated to the minimum number of volumes present in any scan after preprocessing.

2.5
Coevolutionary Balance Theory

Structural balance theory—originating with Heider and formalized by Cartwright and Harary—considers only the signs of edges in triads (friend vs. foe) and predicts that networks evolve toward “balanced” states in which every triangle has an even number of negative links
Heider (1946)
;
Cartwright & Harary (1956)
. Kargaran and Jafari extended this by allowing both node states and link signs to flip under a single Hamiltonian, thereby modeling the coevolution of individual “opinions” and social ties.

Each node
i
carries a binary state
s_{i}\in\{+1,-1\}
(two possible viewpoints) and each undirected edge
(i,j)
has a sign
\sigma_{ij}\in\{+1,-1\}
(friendly vs. unfriendly). The global network energy is

H(G)\;=\;-\,\sum_{i<j}s_{i}\,\sigma_{ij}\,s_{j}.

An
agreement
pair (
s_{i}=s_{j}
,
\sigma_{ij}=+1
) or a
disagreement
pair (
s_{i}=-s_{j}
,
\sigma_{ij}=-1
) contributes
-1
to
H
, whereas “imbalanced” pairs (friendly link between opposite states or unfriendly link between identical states) contribute
+1
. This is formally identical to an Ising Hamiltonian on a fully connected graph whose spins and couplings coevolve.

As “social temperature”
T
(the propensity for irrational flips) varies, their mean‐field analysis yields two self‐consistent equations for magnetization
m=\langle s_{i}\rangle
and node–link correlation
q=\langle\sigma_{ij}s_{j}\rangle
:

m=\tanh\bigl[\beta(n-1)\,q\bigr],\qquad q=\tanh\bigl(\beta\,m\bigr),\quad\beta=1/T.

These combine to

q=\tanh\bigl\{\beta\,\tanh[\beta(n-1)\,q]\bigr\},

whose continuous bifurcation at
T_{c}=\sqrt{n-1}
contrasts with the first‐order transition of the pure Heider model. Numerical Metropolis simulations confirm that, in the coevolutionary model, both
q
and the mean energy
-\langle s_{i}\sigma_{ij}s_{j}\rangle
vary smoothly through
T_{c}
without hysteresis, unlike the Heider‐only case.

By coupling node and link dynamics, coevolutionary balance theory provides a plausible mechanism—applicable to both social and neural systems—by which local state‐coupling reinforcement yields globally cohesive or polarized configurations.

3.2
Whole‐Brain Coevolutionary Energy and Pairwise Balanced Percentage

We first compared whole‐brain coevolutionary energy between groups and found that ASD networks exhibited a markedly more negative energy than TDs, indicating a stronger tendency for anti‐alignment between regional fALFF signals and their functional connections. This shift in global energy suggests that ASD brains may configure their large‐scale dynamics toward a different balance of excitation and inhibition at the system level.

To unpack which local connectivity motifs drive this difference, we examined four signed‐edge proportions. Edges in which two regions share the same “sign” (above‐ vs. below‐median fALFF) and are positively connected—so‐called agreement links—were consistently more abundant in ASD. Conversely, imbalanced–same links (same‐signed nodes but negative connection) were reliably less common. In contrast, the other two motif types—disagreement edges (opposite‐signed nodes with negative links) and imbalanced–opposite edges (opposite signs but positive link)—showed very similar distributions in both groups.

Finally, we assessed bipolarity, the fraction of edges that satisfy balance under an optimal two‐community split. Although ASD networks trended toward slightly lower bipolarity, this did not reach statistical significance, suggesting that the global structural‐balance architecture remains largely intact despite shifts in specific motif frequencies.

Correlation Analysis of Network Metrics with Behavioral Scores

We looked into how different network measures, like whole‐brain coevolutionary energy, subnetwork energy, and subnetwork interconnectivities, were related to behavioral scores to see if they would have any therapeutic value. The results showed that there were multiple statistically significant associations, indicating that network properties and behavioral outcomes in autism spectrum disorder are meaningfully linked
Moradimanesh et al. (2021)
;
Feldman et al. (2025)
. The balanced energy percentage had a positive relationship with the ADI-R Social Total A and ADI-R Verbal Total BV scores
Moradimanesh et al. (2021)
. Conversely, imbalanced energy percentages were negatively related to these same ADI-R measurements, suggesting that higher imbalanced energy may be linked to less severe behavioral symptoms
Moradimanesh et al. (2021)
.

Subnetwork analysis showed strong connections between subnetwork energy measurements and ADOS Communication and ADOS Total scores
Moradimanesh et al. (2021)
. In addition, measures of inter-network connectivity—average fALFF-weighted connectivity and average functional connectivity strength—had strong negative relationships with ADOS Communication and ADOS Total scores, supporting the clinical significance of connectivity patterns in ASD
Feldman et al. (2025)
. These results consistently demonstrate that distinct network connectivity patterns are linked to behavioral severity in autism.

Classification Performance

Using improved coevolutionary and connectivity-based feature choices, our study showed notable increases in classification accuracy. Specifically, employing the top nine discriminative features—PROP_AGREEMENT, PROP_IMBALANCED_SAME, AvgFalffW_2_to_5, AvgWeight_5_to_2, AvgWeight_2_to_5, AvgFalffW_1_to_6, global node-link correlation (q), average node magnetization within the Frontoparietal network (m_sub6), and coevolutionary energy between the Frontoparietal and Salience/Ventral Attention networks (Energy_6_to_5)—resulted in markedly enhanced classification performance.

Discussion

In this work, we applied a coevolutionary balance framework to resting-state functional networks in adult males with autism spectrum disorder (ASD) versus typically developing (TD) controls. By embedding fractional amplitude of low-frequency fluctuations (fALFF) and the sign of functional connections (FC) into a unified Hamiltonian, we quantified “network energy” at multiple scales—whole-brain, subnetwork, and inter-network—and related these metrics to clinical measures.

At the global level, ASD subjects showed significantly more negative coevolutionary energy compared to TDs, indicating greater anti-alignment between regional fALFF states and their FC patterns (Table
9
). This shift in energetic balance echoes prior work on excitation–inhibition alterations in ASD
Moradimanesh et al. (2021)
, although our binarization of fALFF may oversimplify continuous neural dynamics. Future studies should employ weighted‐energy formulations or threshold-free approaches and incorporate concurrent electrophysiological measures to validate the inferred E/I balance.

Analysis of signed-edge motifs revealed that ASD networks had a higher proportion of “agreement” links (same‐sign nodes with positive FC) and fewer “imbalanced-same” links, while disagreement and imbalanced-opposite motifs remained unchanged. Bipolarity trended lower in ASD but did not reach significance, suggesting preserved global structural balance despite local motif reorganization (Table
9
). Replication in larger, multisite cohorts and developmental analyses will be critical to determine whether these motif changes emerge early and predict clinical trajectories.

Within canonical resting-state subnetworks, only the Default Mode Network exhibited elevated energy after FDR correction, consistent with prior reports of DMN dysconnectivity in ASD
Assaf et al. (2010)
;
Kennedy & Courchesne (2006)
;
Lombardo et al. (2010)
. Effects in the Salience and Dorsal Attention networks did not survive correction, implying more subtle or heterogeneous alterations that may require higher‐resolution or task‐based paradigms for reliable detection.

Inter-network analyses showed increased coevolutionary energy and coupling among the Default Mode, Salience, and Dorsal Attention networks, supporting the “triple-network” model of ASD and its hypothesized inefficiency in switching between internal and external cognitive states
Menon (2011)
;
Chen et al. (2015)
. Longitudinal or intervention studies—such as neurofeedback targeting SN–DMN switching—will be needed to establish causality and therapeutic potential.

Importantly, balanced and imbalanced energy percentages correlated with ADI-R social and verbal subscores, and inter-network connectivity metrics inversely tracked ADOS severity, underscoring clinical relevance. Nevertheless, these remain associative findings; future work should integrate mediation analyses and pharmacological or neuromodulation paradigms to probe mechanistic links.

Methodologically, coevolutionary energy extends traditional FC analyses by capturing higher-order (triadic and global) stability features not addressed by standard graph metrics
Bullmore & Sporns (2009)
;
Thompson & Fransson (2016)
. Yet assumptions of binary node states and static FC may overlook dynamic neural fluctuations. Incorporating time-resolved energy measures, multimodal imaging (e.g. PET markers of neurotransmission), and in silico perturbations will strengthen model validity.

Finally, a k-nearest neighbors classifier trained on nine top coevolutionary and connectivity features achieved 78.95% accuracy in discriminating ASD from TD, outperforming other algorithms on the same dataset. While promising for computational phenotyping, external validation on independent cohorts and prospective studies are required before clinical translation.

Limitations include a modest sample size, cross-sectional design, male-only participants, reliance on preprocessed ABIDE I data, binarization of continuous signals, and lack of direct neurochemical or electrophysiological validation. Addressing these limitations through larger, more diverse samples, longitudinal and interventional designs, and multimodal integration will clarify the utility of network energy as a biomarker.

In summary, ASD is characterized by altered coevolutionary energy at global, subnetwork, and inter-network scales that correlate with symptom severity. These findings lay the groundwork for further validation and point toward targeted interventions—such as network-specific neurofeedback or pharmacological modulation—aimed at restoring energetic balance and improving outcomes in ASD.

Neurite orientation dispersion and density imaging for preoperative evaluation of cerebral microstructure and neurodevelopment in children with congenital heart disease
Authors: Shengfang Xu, Jinlong Zhang, Shaoyu Wang, Jifang Qian, Dalin Zhu, Jing Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundCongenital heart disease (CHD) is a type of heart structure defect caused by the abnormal development of the heart and major blood vessels during the embryonic stage. With the continuous innovation of surgical treatment techniques and the improvement of perioperative management, the survival rate of children with CHD has significantly improved. However, accompanying central nervous system development problems have become increasingly prominent. This study investigated the clinical utility of neurite orientation dispersion and density imaging (NODDI) in evaluating preoperative cerebral microstructural features and neurodevelopmental status in children with CHD.MethodsFifty-five infants with CHD were prospectively enrolled in the CHD group, and 55 age- and sex-comparable typically developing infants were included as the control group. All participants completed the Gesell Development Schedules assessment. The developmental quotient (DQ) and domain-specific subscale scores for five core developmental domains were recorded: adaptive behavior (AB), fine motor (FM), gross motor (GM), language (L), and personal-social behavior (PSB). Diffusion spectrum imaging (DSI) was acquired in all participants, and multiparametric NODDI maps were generated on a postprocessing workstation (Siemens Healthineers). The derived quantitative parameters included extracellular volume fraction (ECVF), neurite density index (NDI), isotropic volume fraction (ISOVF), and the orientation dispersion index (ODI). Intergroup differences in NODDI parameters and Gesell developmental scores were statistically analyzed. Pearson product-moment correlation analysis was then applied to evaluate associations between region-specific NODDI parameters and individual Gesell domain scores within the CHD group.ResultsThe ECVF in the splenium of the corpus callosum (SCC) of the CHD group was significantly lower than that of the control group (t=−2.261; P=0.026), whereas the ECVF in the frontal white matter (FWM) was significantly higher (t=2.242, P=0.027). ODI measurements in the posterior limb of the internal capsule (PLIC) were significantly lower in the CHD group than in the control group (t=2.401; P=0.018). Intracellular volume fraction (ICVF) values in the PLIC and SCC were also significantly higher in the CHD group (PLIC: t=4.489, P<0.001; SCC: t=2.388, P=0.019). ODI measurements in the occipital white matter (OWM) were significantly lower in the CHD group than in the control group (t=3.707; P<0.001). Compared to the control group, the CHD group had significantly lower ISOVF measurements in the PTR (t=−2.119, P=0.038), FWM (t=−2.825, P=0.006), OWM (t=−2.253; P=0.028), and temporal white matter (t=−2.164; P=0.034). Correlation analysis further showed that Gesell scores, including DQ, AB, FM, GM, L, and PSB, were consistently lower in the CHD group than in the control group. Within the CHD cohort, the NDI value of the PLIC showed a positive correlation with DQ, AB, FM, GM, L, and PSB, whereas the ODI value of the PLIC showed a positive correlation with PSB. The ISOVF value of the genu of the corpus callosum showed a positive correlation with DQ, AB, L, and PSB. The NDI value of the SCC showed a positive correlation with AB and L.ConclusionsNODDI sensitively detects preoperative cerebral microstructural changes in children with CHD, and its key parameters correlate with preoperative neurodevelopmental performance. Thus, NODDI-derived parameters may serve as candidate imaging biomarkers for evaluating brain microstructure and development before surgery, offering objective imaging-based support for early clinical assessment and timely intervention.

Introduction

Congenital heart disease (CHD) refers to structural and/or functional cardiac abnormalities that are present at birth and arise from disrupted embryonic cardiac development. CHD is the most common congenital anomaly in children. The global disease burden of CHD is estimated at approximately 13 million cases, with nearly 9 of every 1,000 live births affected by CHD. Among these children, about 25% require surgical or catheter-based intervention during infancy (1-3). In recent years, advances in pediatric cardiac surgery and perioperative management have markedly improved survival among children with CHD. However, recent evidence indicates that children with CHD are still vulnerable to neurodevelopmental abnormalities affecting motor function, cognition, behavior, social interaction, and attention (4-6). Early detection and timely intervention are therefore critical to improving long-term neurodevelopmental outcomes in this population.

Neurite orientation dispersion and density imaging (NODDI) is a biophysical diffusion magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)-based modeling technique that characterizes neurite density and orientation dispersion within brain tissue. During the early postnatal stage, the brain undergoes rapid, dynamic biological processes, including axonal growth, dendritic arborization, synaptogenesis, and progressive myelination (7). NODDI has been increasingly used to monitor typical brain development and to investigate neurodegenerative diseases and neurological dysfunction (8,9). NODDI-derived microstructural metrics have also shown measurable associations with clinical phenotypes and developmental outcomes, providing evidence for clarifying underlying pathological mechanisms and evaluating disease progression or developmental impairment. Previous studies have demonstrated that immature cortical development in children with CHD is closely related to impaired dendritic branching and that delayed microstructural maturation is an important pathological substrate for macrostructural abnormalities, such as reduced total brain volume and decreased cortical folding complexity (10-15).

Therefore, in this study, we used the NODDI model to quantitatively assess preoperative brain development and maturation in infants with CHD and to analyze the correlations between NODDI parameters and neurodevelopmental scores. The aim of this work was to clarify the clinical utility of multiparametric NODDI indices for the early identification of preoperative neurodevelopmental status in children with CHD. We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist (available at https://qims.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/qims-2026-1-0174/rc).

Methods

General information

From February 2023 to March 2024, we prospectively recruited 55 infants aged 2–24 months with CHD as the CHD group. Another 55 typically developing infants, matched with the CHD group in age, were enrolled as the control group. The CHD group included 27 males and 28 females, whereas the healthy control group included 31 males and 24 females. The mean age was 8.036±5.581 months in the CHD group and 8.091±5.023 months in the control group. Age was not significantly different between the two groups (t=0.054; P=0.957), and no statistically significant between-group difference was observed in sex distribution (χ2=0.757; P=0.384). One pair of twins in the CHD group was diagnosed with atrial septal defects. Three mothers of enrolled children had a documented history of gestational hypertension, and one of these mothers also had a history of syphilis.

The inclusion criteria for the CHD group were as follows: (I) age 2–24 months, preliminary diagnosis by echocardiography, and surgical confirmation of CHD; and (II) head MR images of sufficient quality for diagnostic assessment. The exclusion criteria were defined as follows: (I) incomplete clinical data due to in-hospital death, treatment discontinuation, or discharge against medical advice; (II) inability to cooperate with the examination or contraindications for magnetic resonance imaging (MRI); and (III) magnetic resonance (MR) images of insufficient quality or incomplete scan sequences for diagnostic assessment. For the healthy control group, the inclusion and exclusion criteria were defined as follows: (I) age 2–24 months, normal cranial MRI findings, and normal neurodevelopmental evaluation based on the Gesell; and (II) no relevant clinical history or abnormalities, including prematurity, CHD, hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy, hyperbilirubinemia, congenital brain malformations, congenital infectious diseases, chromosomal abnormalities, or inborn errors of metabolism.

This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments and was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Gansu Provincial Maternity and Child-Care Hospital (Gansu Provincial Central Hospital) (No. 2023-80). Informed consent was obtained from the legal guardians of all participants.

Gesell Development Schedules assessment

All participants completed the Gesell Development Schedules assessment. The assessment included the developmental quotient (DQ) and domain-specific subscale scores in five core functional domains: adaptive behavior (AB), fine motor (FM), gross motor (GM), language (L), and personal-social behavior (PSB). The DQ was calculated as the mean value of the five domain scores. A DQ score below 75 was considered to indicate abnormal neurodevelopment or developmental impairment. The severity of impairment was graded as mild (55≤ DQ <75), moderate (40≤ DQ <55), severe (25≤ DQ <40), or profound (DQ <25). All assessors were qualified with standardized training in the Gesell Development Schedules. The assessment was performed in a standardized environment with blinded procedures, and a consistent set of assessment tools was used to reduce measurement bias.

MRI

To facilitate brain MRI examination and reduce motion artifacts, infants were sedated with chloral hydrate administered orally or rectally (20 mg/kg) 15–30 minutes before scanning. After spontaneous sleep onset, all infants were gently swaddled and positioned on the MRI scanning table. Adequate auditory protection and active thermal protection were maintained throughout the scan. Images were acquired with a MAGNETOM Lumina 3.0-T MRI scanner (Siemens Healthineers, Erlangen, Germany) equipped with a 20-channel head and neck coil. The scan coverage included the whole brain. All participants underwent conventional MRI (axial T1-weighted imaging, axial T2-weighted imaging, and sagittal T1-weighted imaging), diffusion-weighted imaging, magnetic resonance angiography, and diffusion spectrum imaging (DSI). The DSI scanning parameters were as follows: repetition time, 3,000 ms; echo time, 118 ms; slice thickness, 3 mm; field of view, 180 mm × 180 mm; matrix size, 220×100; voxel size, 1.9×1.9×3.0 mm3; number of diffusion directions, 128; b values =0, 150, 250, 350, 400, 500, 600, 650, 750, 1,000, 1,100, 1,150, 1,250, 1,350, 1,400, 1,500, 1,600, 1,650, 1,750, and 200 s/mm2; and scan time, 6 minutes and 47 seconds.

Image postprocessing

Raw DSI datasets were converted to the Neuroimaging Informatics Technology Initiative (NIfTI) format with DCM2NII software (16-18). The converted data were subsequently processed with NeuDiLab, an in-house software package developed based on the open-source Diffusion Imaging in Python library (http://nipy.org/dipy), to generate quantitative NODDI parameter maps. The following NODDI-derived quantitative parameters were calculated (Figure 1): extracellular volume fraction (ECVF), neurite density index (NDI), isotropic volume fraction (ISOVF), and orientation dispersion index (ODI).

Pseudocolor maps of NODDI parameters. ECVF, extracellular volume fraction; ICVF, intracellular volume fraction; ISOVF, isotropic volume fraction; NODDI, neurite orientation dispersion and density imaging; ODI, orientation dispersion index.

Region-of-interest delineation and parameter measurement

Brain anatomical regions of interest (ROIs) were manually delineated on NODDI parametric images (NDI parametric map) via ITK-SNAP 4.0.1 software. Fifteen anatomical regions were evaluated (Figure 2): the posterior limb of the internal capsule (PLIC), anterior limb of the internal capsule (ALIC), genu of the corpus callosum (GCC), splenium of the corpus callosum (SCC), optic radiation (PTR), cerebral peduncle (CereP), centrum semiovale (CS), frontal white matter (FWM), occipital white matter (OWM), temporal white matter (TWM), dentate nucleus (DN), thalamus (Thal), globus pallidus (GP), putamen (Pu), and caudate nucleus (CN). ROI parameters for the GCC and SCC were measured directly on the parameter maps. For the other ROIs, circular ROIs were placed in the central areas of bilaterally symmetric anatomical structures in both cerebral hemispheres. The delineation slice was selected when the target structure could be clearly visualized. Bilateral ROI measurements were recorded separately, and the average of the bilateral measurements was used as the final parameter value for each anatomical region. All ROI delineation and parameter measurements were conducted independently by two experienced radiologists. When the difference between the two measurements was greater than 5%, consensus was reached through joint discussion and review to reduce subjective measurement bias.

Schematic diagram of ROI selection and ICVF map after NODDI processing. ICVF, intracellular volume fraction; NODDI, neurite orientation dispersion and density imaging; ROI, region of interest.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed with SPSS version 27.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Continuous variables are presented as the mean ± standard deviation (SD). Categorical variables are presented as frequencies (percentages), and group comparisons for these data were performed with the chi-square test. Independent-samples t-tests were applied to compare NODDI parameter values and Gesell developmental scores between the CHD and control groups. Pearson product-moment correlation analysis was performed to examine linear associations between regional NODDI parameter values and Gesell developmental scores within the CHD group. A two-sided P value <0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance.

Correlation analysis

Pearson product-moment correlation analysis further showed that all domain-specific subscale scores of the Gesell Development Schedules, including the DQ, AB, FM, GM, L, and PSB, were significantly lower in the CHD group than in the control group (P<0.05) (Figure 4). Within the CHD cohort, NDI values in the PLIC showed positive correlations with all Gesell scores, including DQ, AB, FM, GM, L, and PSB, whereas ODI values in the PLIC showed positive correlations with PSB. ISOVF values in the GCC showed positive correlations with DQ, AB, L, and PSB. NDI values in the SCC showed positive correlations with AB and L, whereas ECVF values in the SCC showed negative correlations with AB, GM, and L. In the FWM, ECVF showed a positive correlation with AB, GM, and PSB. In the TWM, ISOVF showed a positive correlation with DQ, AB, FM, GM, L, and PSB (Table 3).

Discussion

NODDI is an advanced diffusion imaging model that can characterize the microstructural complexity of neural axons and dendrites and provide information on tissue-level changes that cannot be fully captured by conventional MRI (10-15). NODDI is being increasingly used in neuroimaging studies of normal brain development, neurodegeneration, neurological dysfunction, and changes in brain connectivity (17-21). In a NODDI-based brain development study including 214 participants aged 0–14 years, NDI and ODI increased with age, and both microstructural metrics showed better sensitivity than did conventional diffusion tensor imaging-derived parameters. Moreover, NDI increased more rapidly in white matter than in gray matter, including the cortex and deep nuclei, confirming the high age sensitivity of NODDI during the critical developmental window from the neonatal period through adolescence (21-23). Another study applied NODDI and found that the bilateral NDI in the superior fronto-occipital fasciculus and PLIC was lower om neonates with prenatal heart disease than in normally developing controls. These findings indicate reduced axonal density in white matter bundles, which may reflect a lower abundance of axons, an enlarged interaxonal space, or axonal loss (12,13,24). Adolescents who were born with CHD have also been reported to a have lower white matter volume and total brain volume than controls. This observation is consistent with mechanisms related to reduced axonal density, and the decreased white matter volume observed in several tracts may indicate axonal loss or impaired myelin formation. Research has further shown that immature cortical development in CHD is mainly related to impaired dendritic branching, supporting the view that abnormal microstructural development contributes to macrostructural abnormalities such as reduced total brain volume and decreased cortical folding complexity (24,25). Collectively, these findings indicate that the altered brain developmental trajectories in CHD may be associated with reduced cerebral oxygenation and that NODDI metrics are more sensitive to tissue-organization changes than are conventional DTI indices (24,25). NODDI-derived microstructural markers have therefore shown meaningful associations with clinical phenotypes and developmental outcomes and may help to clarify the relevant pathological mechanisms and monitor disease progression.

In our study, several NODDI-derived parametric indices were used to quantitatively assess preoperative brain development and maturation in infants and young children with CHD. The quantitative results showed that the NDI in the SCC was lower in children with CHD than in controls, whereas the NDI in the FWM was higher. ODI measurements in the PLIC and OWM were both lower in the CHD group. ICVF values in the PLIC and SCC were also lower, and ISOVF measurements in the PTR, FWM, OWM, and TWM were lower compared with those in the control group.

A reduction in ECVF may indicate a relative increase in neurite or glial-cell density within the affected region; that is, an increase in the ICVF. The SCC connects the bilateral parietal and occipital lobes and is an important pathway for visuospatial information integration and higher-order cognitive processing. The frontal lobe is closely involved in executive function, social behavior, and motor planning. Therefore, a lower ECVF in the splenium may interfere with sensory-information integration, whereas a higher ECVF in the FWM may be related to impaired executive processing. Together, these microstructural alterations may contribute to global developmental impairment, including lower DQ and delayed language ability.

NDI is a core metric for quantifying neurite packing density. A higher NDI indicates denser neurite organization and richer neural connectivity, whereas a lower NDI suggests sparser neurite distribution and less efficient information transmission. In our study, the lower NDI measurements in PLIC and the SCC suggest decreased preoperative neurite density, less organized neurite architecture, or abnormal fiber orientation in infants with CHD (11-13,26). A portion of children with CHD may also have comorbid Down syndrome. Studies using DTI and NODDI to evaluate early white matter microstructure in children with Down syndrome have reported significantly reduced fractional anisotropy and NDI in several major association tracts, particularly the inferior fronto-occipital fasciculus and superior longitudinal fasciculus. These findings are consistent with the compromised structural integrity and reduced neurite density, clarify the white matter developmental abnormalities in individuals with Down syndrome (27), and provide important information for determining the appropriate timing of early clinical intervention.

In our study, the CHD group showed lower ISOVF measurements in the optic radiation, FWM, OWM, and TWM. ISOVF reflects the proportion of free water, mainly cerebrospinal fluid, within a voxel. A reduction in ISOVF therefore indicates decreased free-water content and abnormally increased tissue density in the corresponding region. Chronic prenatal hypoxia and cerebral hypoperfusion in children with CHD may inhibit the normal maturation of white matter fibers, resulting in excessive fiber packing and insufficient physiological expansion of the free-water space. These changes may serve as imaging indicators of delayed white matter maturation (20-22).

The CHD group also exhibited lower ODI measurements in the PLIC and OWM. A decreased ODI in white matter suggests reduced axonal tortuosity, abnormal branching, or excessively uniform fiber alignment, which deviates from the normal pattern of neural fibers with fan-shaped organization and multidimensional connectivity. PLIC and OWM are key pathways for motor, sensory, and visual signal transmission. A reduced ODI in these regions may therefore reflect impaired directional development of fiber tracts and disrupted microstructural organization, providing an early microstructural basis for abnormal brain-network connectivity (27,28). Other targeted studies have confirmed that impaired dendritic branching is a core mechanism underlying immature cortical development in infants with CHD and that delayed microstructural maturation is a key pathological basis for macroscale structural abnormalities, including reduced total brain volume and diminished cortical folding (29,30).

In our study, there were significant between-group differences in NODDI parameters across multiple brain regions, indicating subtle preoperative structural abnormalities in the brains of infants with CHD. Several of these findings are consistent with previously reported observations (12,13). In addition, this study adds to the evidence related to preoperative neurodevelopment in pediatric CHD and supports the potential value of NODDI as a sensitive tool for pediatric neuroimaging research.

A substantial burden of axonal rarefaction and white matter tract abnormalities has been reported in children with CHD after surgery, and these abnormalities are associated with impairments in higher cognitive functions, including language, learning, memory, attention, and visuospatial processing. Such white matter tract changes may contribute to the neurodevelopmental difficulties observed in patients with CHD (31-35). Studies examining the relationship between microstructural characteristics of brain connection tracts and neurocognitive function in children with CHD have shown that alterations such as reduced myelination and decreased axonal density may affect executive function differently in adolescents with CHD and in preterm infants (12,13,22). However, relatively few studies have focused on children with CHD who already show varying degrees of preoperative neurodevelopmental deficit. A range of developmental assessment scales can be used to evaluate neurodevelopmental status in infants and young children (5,6,36,37). In our study, the Gesell developmental score was used to assess preoperative neurodevelopment in infants and young children with CHD, and correlations between NODDI parameter values and Gesell DQ scores were further analyzed to clarify early preoperative neurodevelopmental abnormalities. Pearson product-moment correlation analysis showed that Gesell DQ, AB, FM, GM, L, and PSB scores were significantly lower in the CHD group than in the control group, and several region-specific NODDI parameters were correlated with these developmental scores. In the CHD group, NDI in the PLIC showed significant positive correlations with DQ, AB, FM, GM, L, and PSB, whereas ODI showed a positive correlation with PSB. These findings indicate that reduced neurite density in key white matter tracts before surgery may be closely associated with the impaired development of motor, cognitive, language, and social functions in children with CHD. The PLIC is a critical pathway linking the cerebral cortex with the spinal cord and brainstem motor nuclei, and a lower NDI in this region may be particularly associated with delays in FM and GM development. The ISOVF in the GCC was positively correlated with DQ, AB, L, and PSB. NDI in the SCC was positively correlated with AB and L, whereas ECVF was negatively correlated with AB, GM, and L. In the FWM, ECVF was positively correlated with AB, GM, and PSB. In the TWM, ISOVF was positively correlated with DQ, AB, FM, GM, L, and PSB. These results indicate that infants and young children with CHD exhibit neurodevelopmental impairment before surgery and that microstructural changes in different brain regions may influence specific developmental functions through distinct neural pathways. Accordingly, NODDI may provide useful reference information for evaluating neurodevelopmental status in children with CHD, and clinicians should to attend to neurological development while treating the cardiac disease and providing an imaging basis for early assessment and timely intervention.

Limitations

This study involved several limitations that should be addressed. Because the sample size was relatively small, the different types of CHD were not further stratified or analyzed separately. Future studies should continue to expand the sample size and perform longitudinal follow-up assessments to evaluate long-term neurodevelopmental trajectories in this population. In addition, subsequent investigations may combine NODDI with complementary multimodal MRI techniques and advanced radiomics or deep-learning approaches to achieve a more comprehensive, in-depth, and refined characterization of preoperative neurodevelopment in infants and young children with CHD.

Conclusions

Multiparametric NODDI indices were used for quantitative and correlative analyses of preoperative brain microstructure and neurodevelopment in infants with CHD. The findings showed that infants with CHD already had detectable preoperative brain microstructural alterations. These results suggest that NODDI-derived quantitative parameters may serve as imaging biomarkers for evaluating preoperative brain microstructure and neurodevelopmental status in this population, thereby providing objective imaging-based support for early clinical assessment and timely intervention.

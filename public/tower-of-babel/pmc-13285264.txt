Video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery with carotid subclavian transposition for embolizing lesion from an aberrant right subclavian artery
Authors: John Landau, Mehdi Qiabi
Published: 2026

Aberrant right subclavian artery (ARSA) is a well-known vascular variant occurring in 1% to 2% of the population.1 Although often clinically silent, the primary concern with respect to symptomatic presentation is dysphagia lusoria, usually secondary to aneurysmal degeneration associated with a Kommerell's diverticulum.2 Management options have traditionally involved open surgery or thoracic endovascular aortic repair plus cervical debranching, but more recent publications have described the use of robotic or thoracoscopic techniques to facilitate intrathoracic ligation of the proximal aberrant subclavian artery followed by a carotid-subclavian transposition.3, 4, 5, 6, 7 Here we present the utility of this technique in a patient with aberrant subclavian artery occlusive disease. The patient consented to publication of this video case report.

A 51-year-old woman presented with acute onset of right forearm and hand pain, numbness, and digital cyanosis. Examination demonstrated normal vital signs and sinus rhythm, but absent brachial, radial, and ulnar pulses in the right upper extremity. She had no history of atrial fibrillation or thromboembolic risk factors. Computed tomography angiography (CTA) demonstrated an ARSA with a severely stenotic occlusive filling defect at its origin, occlusion of the proximal brachial artery, and distal radial reconstitution.

The patient underwent urgent open brachial embolectomy via exposure of the brachial, radial, and ulnar arteries at the antecubital fossa. Significant acute thrombus was retrieved. Resistance encountered during Fogarty pull-back at the ARSA origin suggested a chronic organized lesion. Symptoms resolved postoperatively. Repeat CTA confirmed restoration of distal flow, but a persistent stenotic lesion at the ARSA origin.

Given the embolizing proximal lesion, definitive repair was planned using a multidisciplinary two-stage approach during a single anesthetic. In the left lateral decubitus position, right thoracoscopic access was obtained via a one port in the fifth intercostal space and three seventh intercostal space ports (02:12). After lung mobilization, the posterior mediastinal pleura was divided, and the ARSA and esophagus were identified. A right-sided thoracic duct, coursing anterior to the artery, was isolated, clipped, and divided. Circumferential dissection of the ARSA was achieved, and a plane between the artery and esophagus was carefully developed (03:42).

A tongue of pericardial fat was mobilized to buttress the future staple line. The ARSA was encircled with a vessel loop, elevated, and divided proximally using an endovascular stapler (Endo GIA Curved Tip 45 mm Tan load with Tri-Staple Technology, Medtronic) as proximal as possible (04:57). The pericardial fat pad was then interposed between the staple line and the esophagus and secured with interrupted silk sutures to mitigate risk of esophageal fistulization. Hemostasis was ensured, and a chest tube was placed.

The patient was repositioned supine. Through a transverse cervical incision between the heads of the sternocleidomastoid muscle, the right common carotid and vertebral arteries were exposed. The distal ARSA stump was mobilized, retrieved from the mediastinum, and demonstrated adequate length for tension-free reimplantation (06:11). After systemic heparinization, an end-to-side carotid-subclavian transposition was performed. Upon completion of the anastomosis, normal triphasic signals were noted in the subclavian artery and palpable pulses were confirmed throughout the right arm.

The patient's postoperative course was uncomplicated. The chest tube was removed on postoperative day 1, and she was discharged on postoperative day 2 on aspirin therapy. At the 1-month follow-up, she had palpable pulses throughout the right upper extremity. CTA demonstrated a patent carotid-subclavian transposition, thrombosed proximal ARSA stump, and intact separation of the staple line from the esophagus by the pericardial fat flap.

This case highlights a minimally invasive thoracoscopic strategy to facilitate carotid subclavian transposition in the context of ARSA, offering definitive management of embolizing aberrant subclavian pathology while mitigating morbidity associated with an open surgical exposure.

Twisted compactifications and conformal defects from ISO(3) × \times U(1) F ⁡ ( 4 ) F(4) gauged supergravity


Abstract
We study holographic solutions describing RG flows across dimensions from five-dimensional N = 2 N=2 SCFT to SCFTs in three and two dimensions using matter-coupled F ⁡ ( 4 ) F(4) gauged supergravity with I ​ S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) × U ⁡ ( 1 ) ISO(3)\times U(1) gauge group. By performing topological twists implemented by S ​ O ​ ( 2 ) SO(2) , S ​ O ​ ( 2 ) × S ​ O ​ ( 2 ) SO(2)\times SO(2) and S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) SO(3) gauge fields, we find a number of A ​ d ​ S 4 × H 2 AdS_{4}\times H^{2} and A ​ d ​ S 3 × H 3 AdS_{3}\times H^{3} solutions preserving eight and four supercharges. These geometries are identified with conformal fixed points in three and two dimensions, respectively. The corresponding flow solutions are obtained numerically and can be interpreted as black membranes and black strings in asymptotically A ​ d ​ S 6 AdS_{6} space. In addition, we have constructed charged domain wall solutions with A ​ d ​ S 2 × S 3 AdS_{2}\times S^{3} and A ​ d ​ S 3 × S 2 AdS_{3}\times S^{2} slicing. These solutions are supported by a non-vanishing two-form field and describe conformal line and surface defects within the N = 2 N=2 SCFT in five dimensions, respectively. All of these solutions can be uplifted to type IIB theory via consistent truncations on S 2 × Σ S^{2}\times\Sigma with Σ \Sigma being a Riemann surface.

3.1
Compactifications to three dimensions

We begin with twisted compactifications on a Riemann surface
\Sigma
. The metric ansatz is given by

ds^{2}=e^{2f(r)}dx_{1,2}^{2}+dr^{2}+e^{2h(r)}(d\theta^{2}+F_{\kappa}(\theta)^{2}d\phi^{2})

(23)

with
dx^{2}_{1,2}

(-++)

F_{\kappa}(\theta)

F_{\kappa}(\theta)=\begin{cases}\sin\theta,&\kappa=1\phantom{-}\quad\textrm{for}\quad\Sigma^{2}=S^{2}\\
\theta,&\kappa=0\phantom{-}\quad\textrm{for}\quad\Sigma^{2}=T^{2}\\
\sinh\theta,&\kappa=-1\quad\textrm{for}\quad\Sigma^{2}=H^{2}\end{cases}.

(24)

Denoting the six-dimensional coordinates by
x^{\mu}=(x^{a},r,\theta,\phi)

(25)

For convenience, we also present non-vanishing components of the spin connection

\displaystyle{\omega^{\hat{a}}}_{\hat{r}}=f^{\prime}e^{\hat{a}},\qquad{\omega^{\hat{\theta}}}_{\hat{r}}=h^{\prime}e^{\hat{\theta}},

\displaystyle{\omega^{\hat{\phi}}}_{\hat{r}}=h^{\prime}e^{\hat{\phi}},\qquad{\omega^{\hat{\phi}}}_{\hat{\theta}}=\frac{F_{\kappa}^{\prime}(\theta)}{F_{\kappa}(\theta)}e^{-h}e^{\hat{\phi}}\,.

(26)

In our convention, we will always denote

{\omega^{\hat{\phi}}}_{\hat{\theta}}

\displaystyle Y_{\alpha I}=e^{\alpha,I+3}+e^{I+3,\alpha}

for

\delta\psi_{A\hat{\theta}}
variation. These will eventually lead to a BPS equation for

after the cancellation of the two terms given in (

We now perform a topological twist by imposing a projector

\gamma_{\hat{\theta}\hat{\phi}}\epsilon_{A}=-i\sigma^{3}_{AB}\epsilon^{B}

(33)

and a twist condition

ga=1\,.

(34)

By using the relation
F^{\prime\prime}_{\kappa}(\theta)=-\kappa F_{\kappa}(\theta)
, we can write the gauge field strength tensors as

F^{3}=-\kappa aF_{\kappa}(\theta)d\theta\wedge d\phi\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad F^{7}=-\kappa bF_{\kappa}(\theta)d\theta\wedge d\phi\,.

(35)

With all these results, we are ready to analyze the BPS conditions arising from setting supersymmetry transformations of fermions to zero. With only the
(\theta\phi)
-component of the field strength tensors non-vanishing, the field equations of gauge fields require
\phi_{3}=0
. Furthermore, consistency of the BPS equations also require
\phi_{0}=\phi_{4}=0
. By imposing another projector of the form

\gamma_{\hat{r}}\epsilon_{A}=-\epsilon_{A},

(36)

we find the following BPS equations

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2ge^{\sigma+2\phi_{1}}\cosh\phi_{5}\sinh\phi_{2},

(37)

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2ge^{\sigma}}{cosh\phi_{5}}\left[\cosh\phi_{2}(e^{2\phi_{1}}-1)-\sinh\phi_{2}\right]-\frac{1}{2}a\kappa e^{-2h-\sigma}\textrm{sech}\phi_{5}\sinh\phi_{2},\,\,\,

(38)

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}_{5}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2ge^{\sigma}\sinh\phi_{5}\left[\cosh\phi_{2}-(e^{2\phi_{1}}-1)\sinh\phi_{5}\right]

(39)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\kappa e^{-2h-\sigma}(b\cosh\phi_{5}-a\cosh\phi_{2}\sinh\phi_{5}),

\displaystyle\sigma^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3}{2}me^{-3\sigma}+e^{-2h-\sigma}\left[\cosh\phi_{2}\cosh\phi_{5}(\kappa a-4ge^{2h+2\sigma})\right.

(40)

\displaystyle\left.+8ge^{2h+2\sigma+\phi_{1}}\sinh\phi_{1}\cosh\phi_{5}\sinh\phi_{2}-\kappa b\sinh\phi_{5}\right],

\displaystyle h^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}me^{-3\sigma}+\frac{1}{8}e^{-2h-\sigma}\left[\cosh\phi_{2}\cosh\phi_{5}(4ge^{2h+2\sigma}+3\kappa a)\right.

(41)

\displaystyle\left.-8ge^{2h+2\sigma+\phi_{1}}\sinh\phi_{1}\cosh\phi_{5}\sinh\phi_{2}-3\kappa b\sinh\phi_{5}\right],

\displaystyle f^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}me^{-3\sigma}+\frac{1}{8}e^{-2h-\sigma}\left[\cosh\phi_{2}\cosh\phi_{5}(4ge^{2h+2\sigma}-\kappa a)\right.

(42)

\displaystyle\left.-8ge^{2h+2\sigma+\phi_{1}}\sinh\phi_{1}\cosh\phi_{5}\sinh\phi_{2}+\kappa b\sinh\phi_{5}\right].

\displaystyle h

3.2
Compactifications to two dimensions

In this section, we repeat a similar analysis for finding supersymmetric
AdS_{3}\times\mathcal{M}_{3}
solutions to the
ISO(3)\times U(1)

F(4)
gauged supergravity. We will work with a constant curvature manifold
\mathcal{M}_{3}
described by the metric ansatz

ds^{2}=e^{2f(r)}dx^{2}_{1,1}+dr^{2}+e^{2h(r)}\left[d\psi^{2}+F_{\kappa}(\psi)^{2}(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2})\right]

(47)

with

F_{\kappa}(\psi)=\begin{cases}\sin\psi,&\kappa=1\phantom{-}\quad\textrm{for}\quad\mathcal{M}_{3}=S^{3}\\
\psi,&\kappa=0\phantom{-}\quad\textrm{for}\quad\mathcal{M}_{3}=T^{3}\\
\sinh\psi,&\kappa=-1\quad\textrm{for}\quad\mathcal{M}_{3}=H^{3}\end{cases}\,.

(48)

In this case,
dx^{2}_{1,1}
is the flat metric on two-dimensional Minkowski space with the signature
(-+)
.

Using the following choice of vielbein

\displaystyle e^{\hat{a}}=e^{f}dx^{a},\qquad e^{\hat{r}}=dr,\qquad e^{\hat{\psi}}=e^{h}d\psi,

\displaystyle e^{\hat{\theta}}=e^{h}F_{\kappa}(\psi)d\theta,\qquad e^{\hat{\phi}}=e^{h}F_{\kappa}(\psi)\sin\theta d\phi,

(49)

with
a=0,1
, we find non-vanishing components of the spin connection of the form

\displaystyle{\omega^{\hat{a}}}_{\hat{r}}=f^{\prime}e^{\hat{a}},\qquad{\omega^{\hat{\psi}}}_{\hat{r}}=h^{\prime}e^{\hat{\psi}},\qquad{\omega^{\hat{\theta}}}_{\hat{r}}=h^{\prime}e^{\hat{\theta}},\qquad{\omega^{\hat{\phi}}}_{\hat{r}}=h^{\prime}e^{\hat{\phi}},

\displaystyle{\omega^{\hat{\phi}}}_{\hat{\psi}}=\frac{F^{\prime}_{\kappa}(\psi)}{F_{\kappa}(\psi)}e^{-h}e^{\hat{\phi}},\qquad{\omega^{\hat{\phi}}}_{\hat{\theta}}=\frac{e^{-h}}{F_{\kappa}(\psi)}\cot\theta e^{\hat{\phi}},\qquad{\omega^{\hat{\theta}}}_{\hat{\psi}}=\frac{F^{\prime}_{\kappa}(\psi)}{F_{\kappa}(\psi)}e^{-h}e^{\hat{\theta}}\,.\quad

(50)

We first note that the spin connection contains internal components on
\mathcal{M}_{3}
only along
\theta
and
\phi
directions. To cancel these connections by performing a topological twist, we turn on the following
SO(3)\subset ISO(3)
gauge fields

A^{1}=-a\cos\theta d\phi,\qquad A^{2}=-aF^{\prime}_{\kappa}(\psi)\sin\theta d\phi,\qquad A^{3}=-aF^{\prime}_{\kappa}(\psi)d\theta\,.

(51)

We recall that this
SO(3)
subgroup is generated by
X_{r}
for
r=1,2,3
. There are two
SO(3)
singlet scalars parametrized by the following coset representative, see more detail in
[
50
]
,

L=e^{\phi\hat{Y}_{1}}e^{\varphi\hat{Y}_{2}}\,.

(52)

with

\hat{Y}_{1}=Y_{11}+Y_{22}+Y_{33}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\hat{Y}_{2}=Y_{04}\,.

(53)

This leads to the composite connection of the form

Q_{\mu AB}=-\frac{i}{2}gA_{\mu}^{r}\sigma^{r}_{AB}\,.

(54)

The relevant terms in the gravitino variations are then given by

\displaystyle\delta\psi_{A\hat{\theta}}:

\displaystyle\,0=\frac{1}{2}\frac{F^{\prime}_{\kappa}(\psi)}{F_{\kappa}(\psi)}e^{-h}\gamma_{\hat{\theta}\hat{\psi}}\epsilon_{A}-\frac{i}{2}gA^{r}_{\hat{\theta}}\sigma^{r}_{AB}\epsilon^{B}+\ldots,

\displaystyle\delta\psi_{A\hat{\phi}}:

\displaystyle\,0=\frac{1}{2}\frac{F^{\prime}_{\kappa}(\psi)}{F_{\kappa}(\psi)}e^{-h}\gamma_{\hat{\phi}\hat{\psi}}\epsilon_{A}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\cot\theta}{F_{\kappa}(\psi)}e^{-h}\gamma_{\hat{\phi}\hat{\theta}}\epsilon_{A}-\frac{i}{2}gA^{r}_{\hat{\phi}}\sigma^{r}_{AB}\epsilon^{B}+\ldots\quad\,.

(55)

The twist is achieved by imposing the twist condition

ag=1

(56)

and the following projectors

\gamma_{\hat{\theta}\hat{\psi}}=-i\sigma^{3}_{AB}\epsilon^{B},\qquad\gamma_{\hat{\phi}\hat{\psi}}=-i\sigma^{2}_{AB}\epsilon^{B},\qquad\gamma_{\hat{\phi}\hat{\theta}}=-i\sigma^{1}_{AB}\epsilon^{B}\,.

(57)

With all these conditions together with the ansatz for the
SO(3)
gauge fields given in (
51
), all the terms appearing in (
55
) cancel among each other.

It should be noted that only two of the projectors in (

) are independent, so the

solutions will preserve four supercharges. In the dual five-dimensional
N=2

and

under

[

In this section, we consider another class of holographic solutions describing conformal line and surface defects within the
N=2
SCFT in five dimensions. These solutions break five-dimensional superconformal symmetry but preserve conformal symmetries in one and two dimensions. The supergravity solutions realizing these defects take the form of curved domain walls with
AdS_{2}\times S^{3}
and
AdS_{3}\times S^{2}
slices, respectively. These curved domain walls are supported by a non-vanishing two-form field from the supergravity multiplet. For simplicity, we will truncate out all the vector fields. Therefore, the solutions we will consider only involve the metric, scalars and the two-form field.

4.1
Line defects

We first consider solutions that are holographically dual to conformal line defects within the
N=2
SCFT in five dimensions. The metric ansatz takes the form

ds^{2}=e^{2f(r)}ds^{2}_{AdS_{2}}+dr^{2}+e^{2h(r)}ds^{2}_{S^{3}}\,.

(68)

We will accordingly split the six-dimensional coordinates as
x^{\mu}=(x^{a},r,\theta^{i})
for
a=0,1
and
i=1,2,3
. As in
[
34
]
and
[
43
]
, we will take the ansatz for the two-form field to be

B=b(r)\textrm{vol}_{AdS_{2}}\,.

(69)

As pointed out in
[
35
]
, with vanishing gauge fields, the action of supersymmetry transformations on
SU(2)_{R}
indices
A,B
is trivial. The two chiralities of the Symplectic-Majorana-Weyl spinors can then be conveniently combined into a Dirac spinor. The Killing spinors for the solutions of interest take the form

\epsilon=\epsilon^{+}+\epsilon^{-}

(70)

with

\displaystyle\epsilon^{+}=iY(r)\left[\cos\theta(r)\chi^{+}\otimes\epsilon_{0}+\sin\theta(r)\chi^{+}\otimes\sigma_{3}\epsilon_{0}\right]\otimes\eta,

\displaystyle\epsilon^{-}=Y(r)\left[\sin\theta(r)\chi^{-}\otimes\epsilon_{0}-\cos\theta(r)\chi^{-}\otimes\sigma_{3}\epsilon_{0}\right]\otimes\eta\,.

(71)

In this equation,
\epsilon_{0}
is a two-component constant spinor while
\chi^{\pm}
and
\eta
are respectively Killing spinors on
AdS_{2}
and
S^{3}
satisfying

\hat{\nabla}_{a}\chi^{\pm}=\pm\frac{i}{2\ell}\rho_{a}\chi^{\mp}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\widetilde{\nabla}_{i}\eta=\frac{i}{2R}\tilde{\gamma}_{i}\eta\,.

(72)

\ell
and
R
denote the radii of
AdS_{2}
and
S^{3}
, respectively.

As in
[
43
]
, we use the following form of gamma matrices

\displaystyle\gamma^{a}=\rho^{a}\otimes\sigma_{2}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{2},\qquad\gamma^{r}=\rho_{*}\otimes\sigma_{2}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{2},

\displaystyle\gamma^{i}=-\mathbb{I}_{2}\otimes\sigma_{1}\otimes\tilde{\gamma}^{i},\qquad\gamma_{7}=-i\mathbb{I}_{2}\otimes\sigma_{3}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{2}\,.

(73)

In these equations, the chirality matrix on
AdS_{2}
is defined by
\rho_{*}=\rho^{0}\rho^{1}
with
\rho^{2}_{*}=\mathbb{I}_{2}
.

We now analyze the BPS conditions arising from setting supersymmetry transformations of fermions to zero. The coset representative is taken to be the
SO(2)\times U(1)
symmetric one given in (
27
). Since we have set all the gauge fields to zero, we need to set
\phi_{3}=0
. Due to various similarities between the forms of relevant equations in this case and those of compact
SU(2)\times SU(2)
gauge group considered
[
43
]
, the analysis is essentially the same as in
[
43
]
to which we refer for more detail. We first need to impose a projection condition on the Killing spinors

\sigma_{2}\epsilon_{0}=\epsilon_{0}\,.

(74)

We also note that this is related to a
\gamma_{r}
projector. We now consider the gaugino variations
\delta\lambda^{I}_{A}
.
Since the two-form field does not appear in these variations, we simply obtain the following equations from
\delta\lambda^{1,2}_{A}

\displaystyle\left[\phi^{\prime}_{1}+M_{1}+iM_{2}\right]\cos\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle\left[\phi^{\prime}_{1}-M_{1}-iM_{2}\right]\sin\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0

(75)

with

\displaystyle M_{1}=2ge^{\sigma+2\phi_{1}}(\cosh\phi_{0}\cosh\phi_{5}\sinh\phi_{2}+\sinh\phi_{0}\sinh\phi_{4}\sinh\phi_{5}),

\displaystyle M_{2}=-2ge^{\sigma+2\phi_{1}}\sinh\phi_{0}\cosh\phi_{4}\,.

(76)

For convenience, we also point out that the two equations in (
75
) arise from setting the coefficients of
(\chi^{+}\otimes\epsilon_{0}\otimes\eta,\chi^{-}\otimes\sigma_{3}\epsilon_{0}\otimes\eta)
and
(\chi^{+}\otimes\sigma_{3}\epsilon_{0}\otimes\eta,\chi^{-}\otimes\epsilon_{0}\otimes\eta)
to zero, respectively.

Consistency of these two equations implies
M_{2}=0
which leads to

\phi_{0}=0\,.

(77)

Furthermore, to obtain non-trivial BPS equations for scalar fields, we have to set either
\sin\theta=0
or
\cos\theta=0
leading to the following BPS equations

\phi_{1}^{\prime}=\mp M_{1}\,.

(78)

This is exactly the same structure found in
[
43
]
for compact gauging of the matter-coupled
F(4)
gauged supergravity.

Similarly,
\delta\lambda^{3}_{A}
and
\delta\lambda^{4}_{A}
give

\displaystyle\cosh\phi_{5}\phi^{\prime}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pm M_{3},

We are not able to analytically solve these equations in full generality. This requires some numerical analysis which can always be achieved by suitable boundary conditions. We will instead consider some interesting solutions that can be obtained analytically within particular subtruncations. These solutions could be much more useful in most purposes.

We now repeat a similar analysis for solutions describing conformal surface defects within the

In this case, we will split the six-dimensional coordinates as

We end this section by giving some comments regarding the solutions of line and surface defects in matter-coupled
F(4)
gauged supergravity with a compact
SU(2)\times U(1)
gauge group considered in
[
43
]
and a non-semisimple
ISO(3)\times U(1)
gauge group studied in this paper. We note that although the
SO(2)\times U(1)
symmetric solutions given here take the same from as those in
[
43
]
, the solutions presented in this paper are obtained within a particular subtruncation with
\phi_{1}=\phi_{2}=0

N=2

[
43

. The latter are, on the other hand, the most general solutions within the
SO(2)\times U(1)
symmetric sector not in any particular subtruncations. Therefore, the crucial difference between the solutions from compact and non-semisimple gauge groups lies in the presence of the dual irrelevant operators.

5
Conclusions

We have studied a number of supersymmetric solutions from matter-coupled
F(4)
gauged supergravity with
ISO(3)\times U(1)
gauge group. The first class of these solutions takes the form of RG flows across dimensions and holographically describes twisted compactifications of the five-dimensional
N=2
SCFT on
H^{2}
and
H^{3}
to three- and two-dimensional SCFTs in the IR. These solutions have been obtained by performing standard topological twists using
SO(2)\times U(1)
and
SO(3)
gauge fields. Since the
ISO(3)\times U(1)

F(4)
gauged supergravity is a consistent truncation of type IIB theory on
S^{2}\times\Sigma
, the
AdS_{4}\times H^{2}
and
AdS_{3}\times H^{3}
solutions found in this paper could lead to new supersymmetric
AdS_{4}\times H^{2}\times S^{2}\times\Sigma
and
AdS_{3}\times H^{3}\times S^{2}\times\Sigma
solutions in type IIB theory.

The second class of solutions obtained in this paper takes the form of “charged” curved domain walls with
AdS_{2}\times S^{3}
and
AdS_{3}\times S^{2}
slicing. These solutions have been found by turning on the two-form field in the gravity multiplet to support the curvature of the domain walls. The solutions have a holographic interpretation as conformal line and surface defects within the
N=2
SCFT in five dimensions. These defects arise from a source term for a dimension-
4
operator dual to the two-form field. The solutions also have a very similar structure as those found in compact gauge groups studied in
[
43
]
. In particular, the solutions are mainly holographic RG flows in the presence of a running two-form field.

Although the matter-coupled
ISO(3)\times U(1)

F(4)
gauged supergravity considered here can be embedded in type IIB theory, the complete truncation ansatz has not been constructed to date. It would be interesting to work this out using the formalism of exceptional field theories and use the resulting truncation ansatz to uplift the solutions found in this paper to ten dimensions. This could lead to new
AdS_{4}\times H^{2}
and
AdS_{3}\times H^{3}
solutions in type IIB theory as mentioned above as well as possible brane configurations with near horizon geometries given by these solutions. It would also be interesting to identify the dual
N=2
SCFT in five dimensions together with field theory descriptions of conformal line and surface defects dual to the solutions found here in terms of position-dependent deformations.

Wood Mouldings and Millwork Products From the People's Republic 
of China: Notice of Court Decision Not in Harmony With the Results of 
Countervailing Duty Administrative Review; Notice of Amended Final 
Results Pursuant to Court Decision

AGENCY: Enforcement and Compliance, International Trade Administration, 
Department of Commerce.

SUMMARY: On September 23, 2026, the U.S. Court of International Trade 
(CIT) issued its final judgment in China Cornici Co., Ltd., and RaoPing 
HongRong Handicrafts Co., Ltd. v. United States, Court Nos. 23-00216 
and 23-00217, Slip Op. 26-118, sustaining the Department of Commerce 
(Commerce) first remand results pertaining to the administrative review 
of the countervailing duty (CVD) order on of wood mouldings and 
millwork products (millwork products) from the People's Republic of 
China (China) covering the period June 12, 2020, through December 31, 
2021. Commerce is notifying the public that the CIT's final judgment is 
not in harmony with

[[Page 65524]]

Commerce's final results of the administrative review, and that 
Commerce is amending the final results with respect to the rescission 
of the administrative review for China Cornici Co., Ltd. (China 
Cornici) and RaoPing HongRong Handicrafts Co., Ltd. (RaoPing) and the 
corresponding cash deposit rates assigned to these companies.

DATES: Applicable October 3, 2026.

SUPPLEMENTARY INFORMATION:

Background

    On September 11, 2023, Commerce published its Final Results in the 
2020-2021 CVD administrative review of millwork products from China.\1\ 
In the underlying review, China Cornici and RaoPing each claimed they 
had a reviewable entry entitling them to review and provided certain 
documentation to support their claims.\2\ In the Final Results, 
Commerce recognized that China Cornici demonstrated its attempts to 
change with CBP the classification of its entry from Type 01, for which 
no CVD cash deposits were paid at the time of entry, to Type 03, and 
pay CVD duties owed, and that the process may have been ongoing.\3\ 
However, Commerce noted that statutory deadlines dictated Commerce's 
issuance of the Final Results \4\ and, at the time of issuance, no 
record evidence in the underlying review demonstrated that China 
Cornici had a suspended entry during the period of review (POR).\5\ 
Moreover, the information RaoPing provided did not demonstrate, and 
comments provided by RaoPing did not explain, how the evidence the 
company provided of a Type 03 entry was connected to RaoPing or how it 
corresponded to the CBP data on the record.\6\ Accordingly, in the 
Final Results, Commerce rescinded the administrative review with 
respect to China Cornici and RaoPing pursuant to 19 CFR 351.213(d)(3), 
because it found that the two companies had no suspended entries of 
subject merchandise during the POR.\7\

Timken Notice

    In its decision in Timken,\13\ as clarified by Diamond 
Sawblades,\14\ the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit held 
that, pursuant to section 516A(c) and (e) of the Act, Commerce must 
publish a notice of court decision that is not ``in harmony'' with a 
Commerce determination and must suspend liquidation of entries pending 
a ``conclusive'' court decision. The CIT's September 23, 2026, judgment 
constitutes a final decision of the CIT that is not in harmony with 
Commerce's Final Results. Thus, this notice is published in fulfillment 
of the publication requirements of Timken.

Liquidation of Suspended Entries

    In the event the CIT's ruling is not appealed, or, if appealed, 
upheld by a final and conclusive court decision, Commerce normally 
instructs CBP to liquidate relevant entries at the assessed rates and 
to lift suspension of liquidation of such entries. However, in its 
final judgment, the CIT affirmed that Commerce lacks the authority to 
reliquidate the POR entries because those entries had already been 
liquidated \17\ and, therefore, sustained Commerce's determination to 
not issue assessment instructions to CBP to assess countervailing 
duties on China Cornici's and RaoPing's POR entries.\18\ Accordingly, 
to the extent that entries produced and/or exported by China Cornici 
and RaoPing for the period June 12, 2020, through December 31, 2021, 
have already been liquidated, Commerce lacks the administrative 
authority to reliquidate those entries.\19\ Therefore, in the event 
that the CIT's ruling is not appealed, or is upheld on appeal, Commerce 
will not issue assessment instructions to CBP instructing CBP to assess 
countervailing

duties on China Cornici's and RaoPing's entries of subject merchandise 
for the period June 12, 2020, through December 31, 2021.

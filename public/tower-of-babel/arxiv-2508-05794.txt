Entropy and polynomial entropy of derived autoequivalences of derived discrete algebras
Tomasz Ciborski

Abstract
. The aim of this paper is to calculate entropy in the sense of Dimitrov–Haiden–Katzarkov–Kontsevich and polynomial entropy as defined by Fan–Fu–Ouchi of derived autoequivalences of derived discrete algebras over an algebraically closed field.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 18E30; Secondary: 16G10

1.
Introduction

The notion of entropy of exact endofunctors of triangulated and
A_{\infty}
-categories originates from the study of the entropy of dynamical systems also known as the topological entropy, where it is used to measure (with some precision), the exponential growth rate of a certain number associated with a given continuous map on a compact metric space (see, for example
[
8
]
).

The idea to look at the entropy in the context of the triangulated category theory has been introduced by Dimitrov, Haiden, Katzarkov and Kontsevich in
[
4
]
. Having a generator and an endofunctor of a triangulated category, we define the entropy of the endofunctor as a measure of the exponential growth of ‘distance in exact triangles’ between the generator and its images under consecutive powers of the endofunctor. It is also shown in
[
4
]
that in the case of the saturated
A_{\infty}
-categories, the entropy of a given endofunctor can be expressed as a limit of Poincaré polynomials of
\Ext
-groups. Additionally, Fan, Fu and Ouchi introduced in
[
5
]
a notion of a polynomial entropy, which is meant to measure the polynomial growth of the ‘exact triangle’ distance under a given endofunctor of a triangulated category. Both works come with some examples of calculations of the entropy and the polynomial entropy of various functors in a number of cases.

Throughout this paper we work over an algebraically closed field
\mathbb{K}
and we assume that all functors and categories are
\mathbb{K}
-linear. We also assume all functors between triangulated categories are exact.

The aim of the paper is to calculate the entropy and the polynomial entropy of derived autoequivalences of derived discrete algebras. The first main result is as follows (for the definitions of
\Lambda(p,q,r)
,
T_{\mathbf{X},\Lambda}
and
T_{\mathbf{Y},\Lambda}
we refer to Subsections
2.4
and
2.5
):

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Lambda
be a derived discrete
\mathbb{K}
-algebra of finite global dimension. If
\Lambda
is not piecewise hereditary of Dynkin type then
\Lambda
is derived equivalent to
\Lambda(p,q,r)
for uniquely determined
p,q,r\in\mathbb{Z}
are such that
q\geq 0
and
1\leq r<p
. If
F
is an autoequivalence of
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mod\Lambda)
, then there exist
k,l,s\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
F(A)\cong T_{\mathbf{X},\Lambda}^{k}T_{\mathbf{Y},\Lambda}^{l}\Sigma^{s}(A)
for all
A\in\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mod\Lambda)
and the following hold:

(1)

The entropy function
h_{F}\colon\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
of
F
is given by

h_{F}(t)=st+\max\left(l\frac{r}{r-p}t,k\frac{r}{r+q}t\right).

(2)

The polynomial entropy function
\hat{h}_{F}\colon\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
of
F
is given by

\hat{h}_{F}(t)=\begin{cases}0&if $k(r+q)=l(r-p)$,\\
\delta_{t,0}&otherwise.\end{cases}

We also provide a result in the case of derived discrete algebras of infinite global dimension, for autoequivalences for both their derived categories and the categories of perfect complexes:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Lambda
be a derived discrete
\mathbb{K}
-algebra of infinite global dimension. Then
\Lambda
is derived equivalent to
\Lambda(p,q,p)
for uniquely determined
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
q\geq 0,p\geq 1
. If
F
is an autoequivalence of
\mathcal{T}\in\{\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mod\Lambda),\per(\Lambda)\}
, then there exist
k,s\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
F(A)\cong T_{\mathbf{X},\Lambda}^{k}\Sigma^{s}(A)
for all
A\in\mathcal{T}
and the following hold:

(1)

The entropy function
h_{F}\colon\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
of
F
is given by

h_{F}(t)=st+k\frac{p}{p+q}t.

(2)

The polynomial entropy function
\hat{h}_{F}\colon\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
of
F
is given by

\hat{h}_{F}(t)=0.

It is worth mentioning that the parameters
p,q,r
associated with the derived discrete algebras in the above theorems can be calculated combinatorically using the Avella-Alaminos–Geiss invariants (see
[
1
]
).

The paper is structured as following: in Subsections
2.1
and
2.2
we provide the definition of the entropy and the categorical entropy and state their basic properties, while in Subsection
2.3
we provide some elementary examples in the case of finite-dimensional algebras. In Subsection
2.4
we introduce notion of twist functors on triangulated categories and in Subsection
2.5
we describe the derived autoequivalences of derived discrete algebras in terms of generators. We then proceed to prove Theorem
1.1
in Section
3
and Theorem
1.2
in Section
4
.

Note that

We recall some basic properties of the complexity function (

4
, Proposition 2.3]
and
[

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
X,Y,Z\in\mathcal{T}
and any functor
F\colon\mathcal{T}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
between triangulated categories, the following properties hold for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
:

(1)

If
Y\cong Z
, then
\delta_{X,Y}(t)=\delta_{X,Z}(t)
.

(2)

\delta_{X,Z}(t)\leq\delta_{X,Y}(t)\delta_{Y,Z}(t)
.

(3)

\delta_{X,Y\oplus Z}(t)\leq\delta_{X,Y}(t)+\delta_{X,Z}(t)
.

(4)

\delta_{F(X),F(Y)}(t)\leq\delta_{X,Y}(t)
.∎

By Lemma
2.2

Corollary 2.3
.

For any
X,Y\in\mathcal{T}
and any equivalence
F\colon\mathcal{T}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
of triangulated categories we have
\delta_{F(X),F(Y)}(t)=\delta_{X,Y}(t)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.∎

\mathcal{T}
. Then for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
we have:

(1)

If
F_{1}\equiv F_{2}
, then
h_{F_{1}}(t)=h_{F_{2}}(t)
.

(2)

h_{F_{1}^{m}}(t)=mh_{F_{1}}(t)
for any
m\geq 1
.

(3)

If
F_{1}F_{2}\cong F_{2}F_{1}
, then
h_{F_{1}F_{2}}(t)\leq h_{F_{1}}(t)+h_{F_{2}}(t)
.∎

Let
X,Y\in\mathcal{T}
. We define the
Ext-distance
function
\delta_{X,Y}^{\prime}\colon\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
by the formula

\delta^{\prime}_{X,Y}(t)\coloneqq\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}}\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\Hom_{\mathcal{T}}(X,\Sigma^{a}Y)\cdot\exp(-at).

Observe that if
\mathcal{T}
is of finite type, then
\delta_{X,Y}^{\prime}(t)<\infty
for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

By the proof of
[
4
, Theorem 2.7]
the Ext-distance function can, in certain situations, be approximated by the complexity function.

Lemma 2.6
.

Assume that
\mathcal{T}
is saturated and
G
is a split-generator of
\mathcal{T}
. Then there exist functions
C_{1},C_{2}\colon\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, depending on
G
, such that

C_{1}(t)\cdot\delta_{G,X}(t)\leq\delta^{\prime}_{G,X}(t)\leq C_{2}(t)\cdot\delta_{G,X}(t)

for any object
X\in\mathcal{T}
and all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.∎

This holds in particular for
\mathcal{T}=\per(\Lambda)
where
\Lambda
is a smooth proper dg-algebra over
\mathbb{K}
. Examples of such dg-algebras include finite-dimensional
\mathbb{K}
-algebras of finite global dimension. In this case we have
\per(\Lambda)=\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mod\Lambda)
. Lemma
2.6
gives us an immediate consequence:

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be saturated,
G
a split-generator of
\mathcal{T}
and
F
an endofunctor of
\mathcal{T}
. Then

h_{F}(t)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\delta_{G,F^{n}(G)}^{\prime}(t)

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.∎

We say that an autoequivalence
F
is
entropy inverse-regular
if for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
the equality
h_{F^{-1}}(t)=h_{F}(-t)
holds. From the proof of
[
5
, Lemma 2.12(i)]
we can deduce the following fact:

Lemma 2.8
.

Suppose that
F_{1}
is an entropy inverse-regular autoequivalence of
\mathcal{T}
for which the entropy function
h_{F_{1}}
is an odd function. Let
F_{2}
be an endofunctor of
\mathcal{T}
. If
F_{1}F_{2}\cong F_{2}F_{2}
then the inequality in Lemma
2.5
(3) becomes an equality, i.e.

h_{F_{1}F_{2}}(t)=h_{F_{1}}(t)+h_{F_{2}}(t)

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.∎

We have the following fact (see
[
5
, Lemma 2.11]
):

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be saturated,
F
an autoequivalence of
\mathcal{T}
and assume that
\mathcal{T}
has a Serre functor. Then
F
is entropy inverse-regular.∎

2.2.
Polynomial entropy

Similarly to the previous subsection,
\mathcal{T}
will denote a fixed triangulated category.

Definition 2.10

(
[
5
, Definition 2.4]
)
.

Let
G
be a split-generator of
\mathcal{T}
and
F
an endofunctor of
\mathcal{T}
. The
polynomial entropy
of
F
is defined to be the function
\hat{h}_{F}\colon\{t\in\mathbb{R}\;|\;h_{F}(t)\neq-\infty\}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
given by the formula

\hat{h}_{F}(t)\coloneqq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(\delta_{G,F^{n}(G)}(t))-nh_{F}(t)}{\log(n)}.

The polynomial entropy
\hat{h}_{F}(t)
is well-defined for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
such that
h_{F}(t)\neq-\infty
and does not depend on the choice of a split-generator
[
5
, Definition 2.4 and Lemma 2.6]
.

We now list some properties of the polynomial entropy. We will start with the following obvious fact:

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
G
be a split-generator of
\mathcal{T}
and
F
and
F_{1}
endofunctors of
\mathcal{T}
such that
F\equiv F_{1}
. Then
\hat{h}_{F}(t)=\hat{h}_{F_{1}}(t)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
such that
h_{F}(t)\neq-\infty
.∎

We also have the following:

Lemma 2.12
.

Assume that
\mathcal{T}
is saturated. Let
G
be a split-generator of
\mathcal{T}
and
F
an endofunctor of
\mathcal{T}
. Then

(1)

\hat{h}_{F}(t)=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(\delta^{\prime}_{G,F^{n}(G)}(t))-nh_{F}(t)}{\log(n)}
,

(2)

\hat{h}_{F}(t)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(\delta^{\prime}_{G,F^{n}(G)}(t))-nh_{F}(t)}{\log(n)}
if and only if
\hat{h}_{F}(t)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(\delta_{G,F^{n}(G)}(t))-nh_{F}(t)}{\log(n)}
,

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
such that
h_{F}(t)\neq-\infty
.

Proof.

(1) is stated in
[
5
, Lemma 2.7]
. (2) can be easily shown using Lemma
2.6
.
∎

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
G
be a split-generator of
\mathcal{T}
and
F
an endofunctor of
\mathcal{T}
. If
t\in\mathbb{R}
and

\hat{h}_{F}(t)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(\delta_{G,F^{n}(G)}(t))-nh_{F}(t)}{\log(n)},

then
\hat{h}_{F^{m}}(t)=\hat{h}_{F}(t)
for all
m>0
. Moreover, in the above situation

\hat{h}_{F^{m}}(t)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(\delta_{G,F^{nm}(G)}(t))-nh_{F^{m}}(t)}{\log(n)}.

Proof.

The first part of this lemma is stated in
[
5
, Lemma 2.10]
. If
f(n)\coloneqq\frac{\log(\delta_{G,F^{n}(G)}(t))-nh_{F}(t)}{\log(n)}
, then

\begin{split}\hat{h}_{F^{m}}(t)=\hat{h}_{F}(t)&=\lim_{n\to\infty}f(n)=\lim_{n\to\infty}f(nm)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(\delta_{G,F^{nm}(G)}(t))-nmh_{F}(t)}{\log(nm)}\\
&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(\delta_{G,F^{nm}(G)}(t))-nh_{F^{m}}(t)}{\log(n)},\end{split}

where Lemma
2.5
(2) is used to show the last equality.
∎

If the polynomial entropy is a limit with respect to a split-generator and the Ext-distance function, it remains a limit if we substitute the second object in the Ext-distance function with another split-generator.

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
G
and
G^{\prime}
be split-generators of
\mathcal{T}
and
F
an endofunctor of
\mathcal{T}
. Assume that
\mathcal{T}
is saturated and that
\delta_{G,G}(t)>0
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. If

\hat{h}_{F}(t)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(\delta^{\prime}_{G,F^{n}(G)}(t))-nh_{F}(t)}{\log(n)},

then

\hat{h}_{F}(t)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(\delta^{\prime}_{G,F^{n}(G^{\prime})}(t))-nh_{F}(t)}{\log(n)}

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
where
h_{F}(t)\neq-\infty
.

Proof.

Observe first, that by Lemma
2.2
(2) we have
0<\delta_{G,G}(t)\leq\delta_{G,G^{\prime}}(t)\cdot\delta_{G^{\prime},G}(t)
, hence
\delta_{G,G^{\prime}}(t),\delta_{G^{\prime},G}(t)>0
.

By Lemma
2.6
there exist functions
C_{1}^{\prime},C_{1}^{\prime\prime}\colon\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that
\delta^{\prime}_{G,F^{n}(G)}(t)\leq C_{1}^{\prime}(t)\cdot~\delta_{G,F^{n}(G)}(t)
and
\delta_{G,F^{n}(G^{\prime})}(t)\leq C_{1}^{\prime\prime}(t)\cdot\delta^{\prime}_{G,F^{n}(G^{\prime})}(t)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
n\geq 0
. We have the following chain of inequalities by Lemma
2.2
(2),(4)

\begin{split}\delta_{G,F^{n}(G)}^{\prime}(t)&\leq C_{1}^{\prime}(t)\cdot\delta_{G,F^{n}(G^{\prime})}(t)\cdot\delta_{F^{n}(G^{\prime}),F^{n}(G)}(t)\leq C_{1}^{\prime}(t)\cdot\delta_{G,F^{n}(G^{\prime})}(t)\cdot\delta_{G^{\prime},G}(t)\\
&\leq C_{1}(t)\cdot\delta^{\prime}_{G,F^{n}(G^{\prime})}(t),\end{split}

where
C_{1}(t)\coloneqq C_{1}^{\prime}(t)\cdot\delta_{G^{\prime},G}(t)\cdot C_{1}^{\prime\prime}(t)>0
.

On the other hand, again by Lemma
2.6
there exist functions
C_{2}^{\prime},C_{2}^{\prime\prime}\colon\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that
\delta^{\prime}_{G,F^{n}(G^{\prime})}(t)\leq C_{2}^{\prime}(t)\cdot\delta_{G,F^{n}(G^{\prime})}(t)
and
\delta_{G,F^{n}(G)}(t)\leq C_{2}^{\prime\prime}(t)\cdot\delta^{\prime}_{G,F^{n}(G)}(t)
. Then, we have a chain of inequalities

\begin{split}\delta^{\prime}_{G,F^{n}(G^{\prime})}(t)&\leq C_{2}^{\prime}(t)\cdot\delta_{G,F^{n}(G)}(t)\cdot\delta_{F^{n}(G),F^{n}(G^{\prime})}(t)\leq C_{2}^{\prime}(t)\cdot\delta_{G,F^{n}(G)}(t)\cdot\delta_{G,G^{\prime}}(t)\\
&\leq C_{2}(t)\cdot\delta^{\prime}_{G,F^{n}(G)}(t),\end{split}

where
C_{2}(t)\coloneqq C_{2}^{\prime}(t)\cdot\delta_{G,G^{\prime}}(t)\cdot C_{2}^{\prime\prime}(t)>0
. Consequently, we have the following inequalities

\begin{split}\frac{\log(\frac{1}{C_{1}(t)}\cdot\delta^{\prime}_{G,F^{n}(G)}(t))-nh_{F}(t)}{\log(n)}\leq\frac{\log(\delta^{\prime}_{G,F^{n}(G^{\prime})}(t))-nh_{F}(t)}{\log(n)}\leq\frac{\log(C_{2}(t)\cdot\delta^{\prime}_{G,F^{n}(G)}(t))-nh_{F}(t)}{\log(n)}\end{split}

to which we apply the squeeze theorem and obtain the desired result.
∎

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
F
be an autoequivalence of
\mathcal{T}
and
G
a split-generator of
\mathcal{T}
. Fix
t\in\mathbb{R}
such that
h_{F}(t)\neq-\infty
,
m>0
and assume that

\hat{h}_{F^{m}}(t)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(\delta_{G,F^{nm}(G)}(t))-nh_{F^{m}}(t)}{\log(n)}

and
\delta_{G,F^{k}(G)}(t)>0
for all
k\in\{-m+1,\ldots,0,\ldots,m-1\}
. Then
\hat{h}_{F}(t)=\hat{h}_{F^{m}}(t)
.

Proof.

Fix
k\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}
. We obtain the following chain of inequalities

\begin{split}\delta_{G,F^{nm+k}(G)}(t)&\leq\delta_{G,F^{k}(G)}(t)\cdot\delta_{F^{k}(G),F^{nm+k}(G)}(t)\\
&=\delta_{G,F^{k}(G)}(t)\cdot\delta_{G,F^{nm}(G)}(t)=C_{2}(t)\cdot\delta_{G,F^{nm}(G)}(t),\end{split}

where
C_{2}(t)\coloneqq\delta_{G,F^{k}(G)}(t)
. The first inequality follows from Lemma
2.2
(2), and the next equality from Corollary
2.3
. On the other hand, we have the following chain of inequalities

\begin{split}\delta_{G,F^{nm}(G)}(t)\leq\delta_{G,F^{-k}(G)}(t)\cdot\delta_{F^{-k}(G),F^{nm}(G)}(t)=\delta_{G,F^{-k}(G)}(t)\cdot\delta_{G,F^{nm+k}(G)}(t).\end{split}

Again, the first inequality follows from Lemma
2.2
(2) and the next equality from Corollary
2.3
. We now multiply the above chain of inequalities by
C_{1}(t)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\delta_{G,F^{-k}(G)}(t)}
and infer that we have the following two inequalities

(2.1)

C_{1}(t)\cdot\delta_{G,F^{nm}(G)}(t)\leq\delta_{G,F^{nm+k}(G)}(t)\leq C_{2}(t)\cdot\delta_{G,F^{nm}(G)}(t).

For
i=1,2
we easily obtain

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(C_{i}(t)\cdot\delta_{G,F^{nm}(G)}(t))-nmh_{F}(t)}{\log(n)}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(\delta_{G,F^{nm}(G)}(t))-nh_{F^{m}}(t)}{\log(n)}=\hat{h}_{F^{m}}(t),

using Lemma
2.5
(2) and our assumption. From inequalities (
2.1
) and by applying the squeeze theorem we get

\begin{split}\hat{h}_{F^{m}}(t)&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(\delta_{G,F^{nm+k}(G)}(t))-nmh_{F}(t)}{\log(n)}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(\delta_{G,F^{nm+k}(G)}(t))-(nm+k)h_{F}(t)}{\log(n)}\\
&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(\delta_{G,F^{nm+k}(G)}(t))-(nm+k)h_{F}(t)}{\log(nm+k)}.\end{split}

By putting
f(n)\coloneqq\frac{\delta_{G,F^{n}(G)}(t)-nh_{F}(t)}{\log(n)}
, we infer that
\lim_{n\to\infty}f(nm+k)=\hat{h}_{F^{m}}(t)
. Since
k\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}
was arbitrary, we deduce that
\lim_{n\to\infty}f(n)=\hat{h}_{F^{m}}(t)
, which is just equivalent to
\hat{h}_{F}(t)=\hat{h}_{F^{m}}(t)
.
∎

Lemma 2.16
.

Let
G
be a split-generator of
\mathcal{T}
and
F
an autoequivalence on
\mathcal{T}
. Then:

(1)

Assume that
\mathcal{T}
is saturated and has a Serre functor
S
. Then
\hat{h}_{F^{-1}}(t)=\hat{h}_{F}(-t)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
such that
h_{F}(t)\neq-\infty
. Moreover, if for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
such that
h_{F}(t)\neq-\infty
we have

\hat{h}_{F}(t)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(\delta^{\prime}_{G,F^{n}(G)}(t))-nh_{F}(t)}{\log(n)}

then for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
such that
h_{F}(t)\neq-\infty
we also have

\hat{h}_{F^{-1}}(t)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(\delta^{\prime}_{G,F^{-n}(G)}(t))-nh_{F^{-1}}(t)}{\log(n)}.

(2)

Suppose that
F
is entropy inverse-regular,
h_{F}
is an odd function and
F_{1}
is an endofunctor of
\mathcal{T}
such that
FF_{1}\cong F_{1}F
. Then
\hat{h}_{F_{1}F}(t)\leq\hat{h}_{F_{1}}(t)+\hat{h}_{F}(t)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
such that
h_{F}(t)\neq-\infty
. Moreover, if
\hat{h}_{F}
is an odd function, then we have an equality.

Proof.

(1) The first part is stated in
[
5
, Lemma 2.11]
. For the second part, observe that

\begin{split}\hat{h}_{F}(-t)&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(\delta^{\prime}_{G,F^{n}(G)}(-t))-nh_{F}(-t)}{\log(n)}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(\delta^{\prime}_{G,F^{n}(S(G))}(-t))-nh_{F}(-t)}{\log(n)}\\
&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(\delta^{\prime}_{G,F^{-n}(G)}(t))-nh_{F^{-1}}(t)}{\log(n)}=\hat{h}_{F^{-1}}(t).\end{split}

The second equality follows from Lemma
2.14
and the third one from properties of the Serre functor and the fact that
F
is entropy inverse-regular, which is guaranteed by Lemma
2.9
.

(2) This follows from the proof of
[
5
, Lemma 2.12]
.
∎

2.3.
Example: shift functors

Throughout this subsection we will denote by
\Lambda
a finite-dimensional
\mathbb{K}
-algebra and by
G
a split-generator of the bounded derived category
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mod\Lambda)
which is a stalk complex concentrated in degree zero. As usual,
\Sigma
will denote the shift functor on
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mod\Lambda)
. For
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
, by
\mathbf{D}^{[a,b]}(\mod\Lambda)
we will understand the subcategory of
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mod\Lambda)
consisting of the objects
A\in\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mod\Lambda)
for which
H^{k}(A)=0
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
k<a
or
k>b
.

We will start with stating a technical lemma:

Lemma 2.17
.

Assume that
a\leq b
.

(1)

Let
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{n-1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C_{n}}

be a tower of exact triangles in
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mod\Lambda)
such that
A_{0},C_{1},\ldots,C_{n}\in\mathbf{D}^{[a,b]}(\mod\Lambda)
. Then
A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}\in\mathbf{D}^{[a,b]}(\mod\Lambda)
.

(2)

Let
A\xrightarrow{f}B\rightarrow C\rightarrow\Sigma A
be an exact triangle, where
A\in\mathbf{D}^{[a,a]}(\mod\Lambda)
and
B\in\mathbf{D}^{[a,b]}(\mod\Lambda)
. If
H^{a}(f)\colon H^{a}(A)\rightarrow H^{a}(B)
is a monomorphism, then
C\in\mathbf{D}^{[a,b]}(\mod\Lambda)
.

(3)

Let
A\rightarrow B\rightarrow C\rightarrow\Sigma A
be an exact triangle, where
A\in\mathbf{D}^{[b+k,b+k]}(\mod\Lambda)
for some
k\geq 1
,
H^{b+k}(A)\neq 0
,
B\in\mathbf{D}^{[a,b]}(\mod\Lambda)
and
H^{a}(B)\neq 0
. Then
C\in\mathbf{D}^{[a,b+k-1]}(\mod\Lambda)
and
H^{b+k-1}(C)\neq 0
and
H^{a}(C)\neq 0
.

Proof.

(1) Since
H^{c}(A_{0})=H^{c}(C_{1})=0
, we infer from the long exact cohomology sequence that
H^{c}(A_{1})=0
, for all
c<a
. On the other hand, since
H^{c}(A_{0})=H^{c}(C_{1})=0
, we infer that
H^{c}(A_{1})=0
, for all
c>b
. We proceed by a simple induction.

(2), (3) Similarly to (1), these are seen easily by using the long exact sequences of cohomologies.
∎

This allows us to show the following fact:

Lemma 2.18
.

For all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
we have
\delta_{G,\Sigma^{k}G}(t)=\exp(kt)
.

Proof.

Observe that for any
k
there exists an exact triangle
0\rightarrow\Sigma^{k}G\xrightarrow{\cong}\Sigma^{k}G\rightarrow 0
. By definition, we get
\delta_{G,\Sigma^{k}G}(t)\leq\exp(kt)
. Note that for
t=0
we necessarily have an equality.

Assume now that there exists
t\neq 0
such that
\delta_{G,\Sigma^{k}G}(t)<\exp(kt)
. This implies that there exists a tower of exact triangles
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{k-1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Sigma^{k}G\oplus B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Sigma^{n_{1}}G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Sigma^{n_{k}}G}

such that
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\exp(n_{i}t)<\exp(kt)
. This means that for all
i=1,\ldots,k
we get
n_{i}t<kt
, which is equivalent to all
n_{i}
being either greater than
k
if
t<0
or smaller than
k
if
t>0
. Observe
that for all
i=1,\ldots,n
we have
\Sigma^{n_{i}}G\in\mathbf{D}^{[\min_{j}(-n_{j}),\max_{j}(-n_{j})]}(\mod\Lambda)
. Lemma
2.17
(1) implies that
\Sigma^{k}G\oplus B\in\mathbf{D}^{[\min_{j}(-n_{j}),\max_{j}(-n_{j})]}(\mod\Lambda)
, a contradiction since
H^{-k}(\Sigma^{k}G\oplus B)\neq 0
and
-k\notin[\min_{j}(-n_{j}),\max_{j}(-n_{j})]
.
∎

We immediately obtain the following result:

Proposition 2.19
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. The following equalities hold for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
:

(1)

h_{\Sigma^{k}}(t)=kt
,

(2)

\hat{h}_{\Sigma^{k}}(t)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(\delta_{G,\Sigma^{nk}G}(t))-nh_{\Sigma^{k}}(t)}{\log(n)}=0
.∎

Corollary 2.20
.

If
F
is an endofunctor of
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mod\Lambda)
, then for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
the following equalities hold for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
:

(1)

h_{\Sigma^{k}F}(t)=kt+h_{F}(t)
,

(2)

\hat{h}_{\Sigma^{k}F}(t)=\hat{h}_{F}(t)
.

Proof.

Since
\Sigma
is entropy-inverse regular and
h_{\Sigma}
is an odd function, we may apply Lemma
2.8
to obtain (1). We use Lemma
2.16
(2) to obtain (2).
∎

For our future calculations, we will also need the following technical result:

Lemma 2.21
.

Let
F
and
F_{1}
be endofunctors on
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mod\Lambda)
. Suppose there exist
k\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
F\equiv\Sigma^{k}F_{1}
. Furthermore, assume that

\hat{h}_{F_{1}}(t)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(\delta_{G,F_{1}^{n}(G)}(t))-nh_{F_{1}}(t)}{\log(n)}.

Then

\hat{h}_{F}(t)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(\delta_{G,F^{n}(G)}(t))-nh_{F}(t)}{\log(n)}.

Proof.

Observe that we have the following chain of inequalities by Lemma
2.2
(2), Corollary
2.3
, Lemma
2.18
and Proposition
2.19
(1)

\begin{split}\log(\delta_{G,F_{1}^{n}(G)})(t)-nh_{F_{1}}(t)=&\log(\delta_{G,\Sigma^{-kn}F^{n}(G)})-nh_{\Sigma^{-k}F}(t)\\
&\leq\log(\delta_{G,\Sigma^{-kn}G}(t))+\log(\delta_{\Sigma^{-kn}G,\Sigma^{-kn}F^{n}(G)}(t))-nh_{F}(t)+nkt\\
&=-nkt+\log(\delta_{G,F^{n}(G)}(t))-nh_{F}(t)+nkt\\
&=\log(\delta_{G,F^{n}(G)}(t))-nh_{F}(t).\end{split}

On the other hand, using the same properties we get

\begin{split}\log(\delta_{G,F^{n}(G)}(t))-nh_{F}(t)&\leq\log(\delta_{G,\Sigma^{nk}G}(t)\cdot\delta_{\Sigma^{nk}G,\Sigma^{nk}F_{1}^{n}(G)}(t))-nh_{\Sigma^{k}F_{1}}(t)\\
&=nkt+\log(\delta_{G,F_{1}^{n}(G)}(t))-nh_{F_{1}}(t)-nkt\\
&=\log(\delta_{G,F_{1}^{n}(G)}(t))-nh_{F_{1}}(t).\end{split}

By dividing both chains of inequalities by
\log(n)
and applying the squeeze theorem to these inequalities we obtain the desired result.
∎

Lemma
2.18
allows us to state another useful fact:

Corollary 2.22
.

For any object
X\in\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mod\Lambda)
and all
t\in\mathbb{R}
we have
\delta_{G,X}(t)>0
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2
(2) we have the following inequality

0<\exp(0)=\delta_{G,G}(t)\leq\delta_{G,X}(t)\cdot\delta_{X,G}(t),

thus
\delta_{G,X}(t),\delta_{X,G}(t)>0
.
∎

Note that we can prove similar results for the category
\per(\Lambda)\cong\mathbf{K}^{\mathrm{b}}(\proj\Lambda)
. As a split-generator we can take
\Lambda
. We have:

Proposition 2.23
.

Assume that
\Lambda
is as above and
\Sigma
is the shift functor on
\per(\Lambda)
. The following equalities hold for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
:

(1)

h_{\Sigma^{k}}(t)=kt
,

(2)

\hat{h}_{\Sigma^{k}}(t)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(\delta_{\Lambda,\Sigma^{nk}\Lambda}(t))-nh_{\Sigma^{k}}(t)}{\log(n)}=0
.∎

Corollary 2.24
.

If
F
is an endofunctor of
\per(\Lambda)
, then for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
the following equalities hold for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
:

(1)

h_{\Sigma^{k}F}(t)=kt+h_{F}(t)
,

(2)

\hat{h}_{\Sigma^{k}F}(t)=\hat{h}_{F}(t)
.∎

Corollary 2.25
.

For any object
X\in\per(\Lambda)
and all

we have
\delta_{\Lambda,X}(t)>0
. ∎

2.4.
Twist functors

In order to describe the derived autoequivalences of the derived discrete algebras, we need to recall some definitions and properties from
[
3
]
, which we will slightly modify.

Definition 2.26

(
[
3
, Definition 4.2]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be an algebraic
\Hom
-finite, indecomposable triangulated category which has a Serre functor
S
. A sequence
\mathbf{E}=(E_{1},\ldots,E_{n})
of objects of
\mathcal{T}
is called an
exceptional
n
-cycle
if:

(1)

\Hom^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{T}}(E_{i},E_{i})=\mathbb{K}\cdot\id_{E_{i}}
for all
i=1,\ldots,n
,

(2)

there are integers
k_{i}
such that
S(E_{i})\cong\Sigma^{k_{i}}(E_{i+1})
for all
i
, where
E_{n+1}\coloneqq E_{1}
,

(3)

\Hom^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{T}}(E_{i},E_{j})=0
unless
j=i+1
or
j=1
and
i=n
,

where
\Hom^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{T}}(A,B)\coloneqq\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\Hom_{\mathcal{T}}(\Sigma^{k}A,B)
is the
graded homomorphism space
. We will use the notation
k_{\mathbf{E}}\coloneqq(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})
to denote the sequence of integers mentioned in (2).

Throughout this section, we will denote by
\mathcal{T}
an algebraic Hom-finite indecomposable triangulated category, and by
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
a full indecomposable subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
which has a Serre functor
S
such that for
A\in\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
and
B\in\mathcal{T}
we have
\Hom_{\mathcal{T}}(A,\Sigma^{i}B)=0
for almost all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Definition 2.27
.

Assume that
\mathbf{E}=(E_{1},\ldots,E_{n})
is an exceptional
n
-cycle in
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
. We define an endofunctor
F_{\mathbf{E}}\colon\mathcal{T}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}
by the formula

F_{\mathbf{E}}(-)\coloneqq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\Hom^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{T}}(E_{i},-)\otimes E_{i}.

Then there exists a canonical evaluation morphism
F_{\mathbf{E}}\rightarrow\id_{\mathcal{T}}
. The cone of this morphism, denoted by
T_{\mathbf{E}}
, is called the
twist functor
associated with
\mathbf{E}
.

Remark
.

We will use the following isomorphism for calculating
F_{\mathbf{E}}
for a given object
X\in\mathcal{T}
:

F_{\mathbf{E}}(X)\cong\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\Sigma^{k}E_{i}^{\oplus\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\Hom_{\mathcal{T}}(\Sigma^{k}E_{i},X)}.

Note that our assumption on Hom-spaces guarantees that there are only finitely many non-zero direct summands in the above direct sum.

We can easily calculate the action of a twist functor on the objects of its underlying exceptional cycle:

Lemma 2.28
.

Assume
\mathbf{E}=(E_{1},\ldots,E_{n})
is an exceptional
n
-cycle in
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
with
k_{\mathbf{E}}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})
. Then for
i=1,\ldots,n
we have

T_{\mathbf{E}}(E_{i})=\Sigma^{-k_{i}+1}E_{i-1},

where
E_{0}\coloneqq E_{n}
.

Proof.

This follows from arguments used in Step 1 of the proof of
[
3
, Theorem 4.5]
.
∎

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
. Denote by
\mathcal{S}^{\perp}
its
right orthogonal
, that is, the full subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
consisting of the objects
X\in\mathcal{T}
with
\Hom_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{S},X)=0
. Dually, denote by
{}^{\perp}\mathcal{S}
the
left orthogonal
to
\mathcal{S}
, the full subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
consisting of the objects
X\in\mathcal{T}
with
\Hom_{\mathcal{T}}(X,\mathcal{S})=0
.

The twist functors have the following property, which follows from arguments used in Steps 1 and 2 of the proof of
[
3
, Theorem 4.5]
:

Proposition 2.29
.

Let
\mathbf{E}=(E_{1},\ldots,E_{n})
be an exceptional
n
-cycle in
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
. Assume that
\Hom_{\mathcal{T}}(A,B)\cong D\Hom_{\mathcal{T}}(B,SA)
for all
A\in\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
,
B\in\mathcal{T}
, where
D
denotes the standard duality. Then
T_{\mathbf{E}}
is fully faithful on
\mathcal{T}
.∎

This allows us to state the following:

Corollary 2.30
.

Let
\mathbf{E}
be as above. Assume that
\Hom_{\mathcal{T}}(A,B)\cong D\Hom_{\mathcal{T}}(B,SA)
for all
A\in\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
,
B\in\mathcal{T}
.

(1)

If
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}=\mathcal{T}
, then
T_{\mathbf{E}}
is an autoequivalence.

(2)

If
\mathcal{T}^{\prime\perp}=0=\,^{\perp}\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
and
S\circ T_{\mathbf{E}}|_{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}\equiv T_{\mathbf{E}}|_{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}\circ S
, then
T_{\mathbf{E}}
is an autoequivalence.

Proof.

First, define
\mathcal{E}\coloneqq\{\Sigma^{l}E_{i}\;|\;l\in\mathbb{Z},i=1,\ldots,n\}
.

(1) By Proposition
2.29
it suffices to show that
T_{\mathbf{E}}
is dense. Put
\Omega\coloneqq\mathcal{E}\cup\mathcal{E}^{\perp}
. Simple calculations allow to see that
\Omega^{\perp}=0
and
{}^{\perp}\Omega=0
. By following the proof of Step 3 of
[
3
, Theorem 4.5]
we can deduce that
T_{\mathbf{E}}
is in fact an autoequivalence of
\mathcal{T}
.

(2) Observe that by (1) the functor
T_{\mathbf{E}}|_{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}
is an autoequivalence of
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
and by Proposition
2.29
is fully faithful on
\mathcal{T}
. Our assumptions allow to use
[
7
, Corollary 1.56]
and obtain the desired result.
∎

2.5.
Derived autoequivalences of discrete algebras

The derived discrete algebras have been introduced by Vossieck in
[

. A finite-dimensional
\mathbb{K}
-algebra

is called

if for each vector
\mathbf{h}\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{\mathbb{Z}}
there is only a finite number of isomorphism classes of objects of
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mod\Lambda)
with cohomology dimension vector

.

By
[
2

any derived discrete algebra which is not piecewise hereditary of Dynkin type is derived equivalent to an algebra
\Lambda\coloneqq\Lambda(p,q,r)
which is the path algebra of the quiver

We have the following result:

Proposition 2.31
.

Let

, where

\begin{split}\mathbf{X}&=(\Sigma^{r-1}P_{p-r+1},\ldots,\Sigma P_{p-1},P_{0}/P_{-1},\ldots,P_{-q+1}/P_{-q},P_{-q}),\\
\mathbf{Y}&=(S_{p-r},\ldots,S_{1}),\end{split}

.

For brevity, we will denote by

i
-th element of the exceptional cycle

and by
Y_{j}

-th element of the exceptional cycle

Proof.

We can easily see that for each object
X_{i}
and

its graded endomorphism algebra in

is one-dimensional, which shows the property (1) from Definition

and

Up to orientation of the arrows in the Gabriel quiver of

\tau(X_{i})=\Sigma^{m_{i}}X_{i+1}\hskip 28.45274pt\text{ and }\hskip 28.45274pt\tau(Y_{j})=\Sigma^{n_{j}}Y_{j+1},

We have the following fact:

.

Let

(3)

. Moreover,

and

In the case of algebras

3.
Finite global dimension

The following general result will be useful in our considerations:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(A_{n})_{n>0}
be a sequence of non-empty finite multisubsets of real numbers of cardinality growing at most polynomially with respect to
n
. Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left(\sum_{a\in A_{n}}\exp(a)\right)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\max_{a\in A_{n}}(a).

Proof.

We have the following inequality

\begin{split}\frac{1}{n}\log\left(\sum_{a\in A_{n}}\exp(a)\right)&\leq\frac{1}{n}\log(|A_{n}|\cdot\max_{a\in A_{n}}(\exp(a)))=\frac{\log(|A_{n}|)+\max_{a\in A_{n}}(a)}{n}.\end{split}

On the other hand, we have a lower bound

\frac{1}{n}\log\left(\sum_{a\in A_{n}}\exp(a)\right)\geq\frac{1}{n}\log(\exp(\max_{a\in A_{n}}(a))=\frac{1}{n}\max_{a\in A_{n}}(a).

Now the claim follows from the squeeze theorem since
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(|A_{n}|)}{n}=0
.
∎

-k(r-p)\leq-l(r+q)
(equivalently,
k(r-p)\geq l(r+q)
), then

h_{F}(t)=\begin{cases}k\frac{r}{r+q}t&$t\leq 0$,\\
l\frac{r}{r-p}t&$t\geq 0$.\end{cases}

If
k(r-p)\leq l(r+q)
, we obtain

h_{F}(t)=\begin{cases}l\frac{r}{r-p}t&$t\leq 0$,\\
k\frac{r}{r+q}t&$t\geq 0$.\end{cases}

and the claim follows.
∎

3.3.
Polynomial entropy

Proposition 3.9
.

For any
m\neq 0
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbf{E}\in\{\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y}\}
the equality

\hat{h}_{T_{\mathbf{E}}^{m}}(t)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(\delta_{\Lambda,T_{\mathbf{E}}^{nm}(\Lambda)}(t))-nh_{T_{\mathbf{E}}^{m}}(t)}{\log(n)}=\delta_{t,0}

holds.

Proof.

We will show the calculations for
T_{\mathbf{X}}
only as the proof for
T_{\mathbf{Y}}
is done similarly. By Corollary
3.6
we infer that

(3.3)

\delta_{\Lambda,T_{\mathbf{X}}^{n}(\Lambda)}^{\prime}(t)=\sum_{a\in A_{n}}\exp(-at)=\sum_{a\in A^{\prime}_{n}}\exp(at),

for some multisets
A_{n}
whose cardinality grows linearly with respect to positive integer
n
and
A^{\prime}_{n}=\{-a\;|\;a\in A_{n}\}
. By Lemma
3.5
(3),(4) we know that
\min(A^{\prime}_{n})=0
and
a_{n}\coloneqq\max(A^{\prime}_{n})=\frac{rn}{q+r}+\lambda_{n}
for some
\lambda_{n}\in[-r,r)
.

Assume first that
t\neq 0
. We have, by the fact that
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\Hom_{\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mod\Lambda)}(\Lambda,T_{\mathbf{X}}^{n}(\Lambda))\leq 2(p+q)^{2}
(see
[
3
, Theorem 6.1]
), the following bound

\begin{split}\delta_{\Lambda,T_{\mathbf{X}}^{n}(\Lambda)}^{\prime}(t)&\leq 2(p+q)^{2}\cdot\left(\sum_{i=0}^{a_{n}}\exp(it)\right)=2(p+q)^{2}\cdot\left(\frac{1-\exp(t(a_{n}+1))}{1-\exp(t)}\right)\\
&=2(p+q)^{2}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{1-\exp(t)}-\frac{\exp(t)}{1-\exp(t)}\exp(a_{n}t)\right)\eqqcolon L.\end{split}

If
t<0
, then
1-\exp(t)>0
, hence

L\leq\frac{2(p+q)^{2}}{1-\exp(t)}=k_{1}\cdot\exp(0),

where
k_{1}\coloneqq\frac{2(p+q)^{2}}{1-\exp(t)}
. Using that
h_{T_{\mathbf{X}}}(t)=0
by Proposition
3.8
, we now have the following inequalities

\frac{\log(\exp(0))}{\log(n)}\leq\frac{\log(\delta_{\Lambda,T_{\mathbf{X}}^{n}(\Lambda)}^{\prime}(t))-nh_{T_{\mathbf{X}}}(t)}{\log(n)}\leq\frac{\log(k_{1}\exp(0))}{\log(n)}.

By applying the squeeze theorem we infer that the sequence in the middle converges to
0
. Thus, for
t<0
, by Lemma
2.12
we have
\hat{h}_{T_{\mathbf{X}}}(t)=0
. On the other hand, if
t>0
, then

L\leq k_{2}\cdot\exp(a_{n}t),

where
k_{2}\coloneqq\frac{2(p+q)^{2}\exp(t)}{\exp(t)-1}
. Therefore, we obtain the following inequalities

\frac{\log(\exp(a_{n}t))-nh_{T_{\mathbf{X}}}(t)}{\log(n)}\leq\frac{\log(\delta_{\Lambda,T_{\mathbf{X}}^{n}(\Lambda)}^{\prime}(t))-nh_{T_{\mathbf{X}}}(t)}{\log(n)}\leq\frac{\log(k_{2}\exp(a_{n}t))-nh_{T_{\mathbf{X}}}(t)}{\log(n)}.

Again, by applying the squeeze theorem, we infer that the sequence in the middle converges to
0
(note that
nh_{T_{\mathbf{X}}}(t)=\frac{rn}{q+r}t=a_{n}t-\lambda_{n}t
by Proposition
3.8
) and hence, for
t>0
we have
\hat{h}_{T_{\mathbf{X}}}(t)=0
by Lemma
2.12
.

Observe now, that for
t=0
the following holds

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(\delta_{\Lambda,T_{\mathbf{X}}^{n}(\Lambda)}^{\prime}(0))-nh_{T_{\mathbf{X}}}(0)}{\log(n)}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(|A^{\prime}_{n}|\cdot\exp(0))}{\log(n)}=1,

since
h_{T_{\mathbf{X}}}(0)=0
by Proposition
3.8
.

Thus

\hat{h}_{T_{\mathbf{X}}}(t)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(\delta^{\prime}_{\Lambda,T_{\mathbf{X}}^{n}(\Lambda)}(t))-nh_{T_{\mathbf{X}}}(t)}{\log(n)}=\delta_{t,0},

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, we get by Lemma
2.12
(2) that for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
we have

\hat{h}_{T_{\mathbf{X}}}(t)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(\delta_{\Lambda,T_{\mathbf{X}}^{n}(\Lambda)}(t))-nh_{T_{\mathbf{X}}}(t)}{\log(n)}.

Consequently, by Lemma
2.13
we deduce that for any
m>0
and all
t\in\mathbb{R}
we have

\hat{h}_{T_{\mathbf{X}}}(t)=\hat{h}_{T_{\mathbf{X}}^{m}}(t)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(\delta_{\Lambda,T_{\mathbf{X}}^{nm}(\Lambda)}(t))-nh_{T_{\mathbf{X}}^{m}}(t)}{\log(n)}.

Finally, by Lemma
2.16
(1) we infer that

\hat{h}_{T_{\mathbf{X}}^{-1}}(t)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(\delta^{\prime}_{\Lambda,T_{\mathbf{X}}^{-n}(\Lambda)}(t))-nh_{T_{\mathbf{X}^{-1}}}(t)}{\log(n)}=\hat{h}_{T_{\mathbf{X}}}(-t).

Using Lemmas
2.12
(2) and
2.13
again we can now conclude that for any
m>0
and all
t\in\mathbb{R}
we have

\hat{h}_{T_{\mathbf{X}}^{-1}}(t)=\hat{h}_{T_{\mathbf{X}}^{-m}}(t)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(\delta_{\Lambda,T_{\mathbf{X}}^{-nm}(\Lambda)}(t))-nh_{T_{\mathbf{X}}^{-m}}(t)}{\log(n)}.\qed

We will now calculate the polynomial entropy of any autoequivalence
F
of
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mod\Lambda)
.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
F
be an autoequivalence of
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mod\Lambda)
. Then
F\equiv T_{\mathbf{X}}^{k}T_{\mathbf{Y}}^{l}\Sigma^{s}
for some
k,l,s\in\mathbb{Z}
and

\hat{h}_{F}(t)=\begin{cases}0&if $k(r+q)=l(r-p)$,\\
\delta_{t,0}&otherwise.\end{cases}

Proof.

By Proposition
2.32
(1) we know that there exist
k,l,s\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
F\equiv T_{\mathbf{X}}^{k}T_{\mathbf{Y}}^{l}\Sigma^{s}
. By Lemma
2.11
and Corollary
2.20
(2) we infer that
\hat{h}_{F}(t)=\hat{h}_{T_{\mathbf{X}}^{k}T_{\mathbf{Y}}^{l}}(t)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Therefore, we may assume that
F=T_{\mathbf{X}}^{k}T_{\mathbf{Y}}^{l}
.

Observe first, that we have
\Sigma^{kr}\equiv T_{\mathbf{X}}^{k(r+q)}T_{\mathbf{Y}}^{k(r-p)}
, or equivalently,
T_{\mathbf{X}}^{k(r+q)}\equiv\Sigma^{kr}T_{\mathbf{Y}}^{-k(r-p)}
. Consequently, we have the following

F^{r+q}\equiv T_{\mathbf{X}}^{k(r+q)}T_{\mathbf{Y}}^{l(r+q)}\equiv\Sigma^{kr}T_{\mathbf{Y}}^{l(r+q)-k(r-p)},

and thus by Proposition
2.20
(2) we obtain
\hat{h}_{F^{r+q}}(t)=\hat{h}_{T_{\mathbf{Y}}^{l(r+q)-k(r-p)}}(t)
, hence

\hat{h}_{F^{r+q}}(t)=\begin{cases}0&if $l(r+q)=k(r-p)$,\\
\delta_{t,0}&otherwise.\end{cases}

If
l(r+q)\neq k(r-p)
, then by Proposition
3.9
and Lemma
2.21
we have

\begin{split}\hat{h}_{F^{r+q}}(t)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(\delta_{\Lambda,F^{n(r+q)}(\Lambda)}(t))-nh_{F^{r+q}}(t)}{\log(n)}.\end{split}

Moreover, by Corollary
2.22
we have that
\delta_{\Lambda,F^{k}(\Lambda)}(t)>0
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Therefore, by applying Lemma
2.15
we infer that
\hat{h}_{F}(t)=\hat{h}_{F^{r+q}}(t)=\delta_{t,0}
.

If
l(r+q)=k(r-p)
, then
F^{r+q}\equiv\id_{\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mod\Lambda)}=\Sigma^{0}
and by Lemmas
2.19
(2),
2.15
and Corollary
2.22
we deduce that
\hat{h}_{F}(t)=\hat{h}_{F^{r+q}}(t)=0
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Assume that
\Lambda
is an arbitrary derived discrete algebra of finite global dimension which is not piecewise hereditary of Dynkin type. By
[
2
, Theorem A]
there exists an equivalence of categories
H\colon\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mod\Lambda)\rightarrow\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mod\Lambda(p,q,r))
with
1\leq r<p
,
q\geq 0
which are uniquely determined by
\Lambda
up to derived equivalence. If
F\in\daut(\Lambda)
, then
F_{1}\coloneqq HFH^{-1}\in\daut(\Lambda(p,q,r))
, where
H^{-1}
denotes the quasi-inverse of
H
. Note that if
G
is a split-generator of
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mod\Lambda)
, then
H(G)
is a split-generator of
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mod\Lambda(p,q,r))
. We have

(3.4)

\delta_{G,F^{n}(G)}(t)=\delta_{H(G),H(F^{n}(G))}(t)=\delta_{H(G),F_{1}^{n}(H(G))}(t),

where the first equality follows from Corollary
2.3
. By Proposition
2.32
(1) we infer that
F_{1}\equiv T_{\mathbf{X}}^{k}T_{\mathbf{Y}}^{l}\Sigma^{s}
with
k,l,s\in\mathbb{Z}
and this implies that
F\equiv T_{\mathbf{X},\Lambda}^{k}T_{\mathbf{Y},\Lambda}^{l}\Sigma^{s}
.

Moreover, we deduce that
h_{F}(t)=h_{F_{1}}(t)
and
\hat{h}_{F}(t)=\hat{h}_{F_{1}}(t)
, which follows from equation (
3.4
). Now we use Propositions
3.8
and
3.10
and finish the proof.
∎

4.
Infinite global dimension

In this section we prove Theorem
1.2
.

We will start with derived discrete algebras of the form
\Lambda(p,q,p)
. By Proposition
2.34
(1) for any autoequivalence
F
of
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mod\Lambda(p,q,p))
there exist
k,s\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
F\equiv T_{\mathbf{X}}^{k}\Sigma^{s}
. By using Lemma
2.5
(1) and Corollary
2.20
(1), we infer that
h_{F}(t)=h_{T_{\mathbf{X}}^{k}\Sigma^{s}}(t)=st+h_{T_{\mathbf{X}}^{k}}(t)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Observe that by Proposition
2.34
(2) we have
\Sigma^{p}\equiv T_{\mathbf{X}}^{p+q}
, hence by Lemma
2.5
(1),(2) and Proposition
2.19

\begin{split}h_{\Sigma^{p}}(t)&=h_{T_{\mathbf{X}}^{p+q}}(t),\\
pt&=(p+q)h_{T_{\mathbf{X}}}(t),\\
\frac{p}{p+q}t&=h_{T_{\mathbf{X}}}(t),\end{split}

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Similarly,
\Sigma^{-p}\equiv T_{\mathbf{X}}^{-p-q}
and therefore
h_{T_{\mathbf{X}}^{-1}}(t)=\frac{-p}{p+q}t
. We conclude that for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
we have
h_{T_{\mathbf{X}}^{k}}(t)=k\frac{p}{p+q}t
.

As for the polynomial entropy of an arbitrary autoequivalence
F\equiv T_{\mathbf{X}}^{k}\Sigma^{s}
, similarly to the finite global dimension case, we conclude by Lemma
2.11
that
\hat{h}_{F}(t)=\hat{h}_{T_{\mathbf{X}}^{k}\Sigma^{s}}(t)
. Moreover,
\hat{h}_{T_{\mathbf{X}}^{k}\Sigma^{s}}(t)=\hat{h}_{T_{\mathbf{X}}^{k}}(t)
by Corollary
2.20
(2).

Let
G\in\mod\Lambda(p,q,p)
be a split-generator of
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mod\Lambda(p,q,p))
and let
F_{1}\coloneqq T_{\mathbf{X}}^{k(p+q)}
. Then by the relation
T_{\mathbf{X}}^{p+q}\equiv\Sigma^{p}
, Lemmas
2.2
(1) and
2.18
we have

\delta_{G,T_{\mathbf{X}}^{nk(p+q)}(G)}(t)=\delta_{G,\Sigma^{nkp}G}(t)=\exp(nkpt)

for all
n\geq 0
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Observe that in this case

\hat{h}_{F_{1}}(t)=\hat{h}_{\Sigma^{kp}}(t)=0=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(\exp(nkpt))-nkpt}{\log(n)}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(\delta_{G,T_{\mathbf{X}}^{nk(p+q)}G}(t))-nh_{F_{1}}(t)}{\log(n)}.

Thus, by Corollary
2.22
and Lemma
2.15
we conclude that
\hat{h}_{T_{\mathbf{X}}^{k}}(t)=0
.

Using the same argument as in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we obtain the desired result for an arbitrary derived discrete algebra
\Lambda
of infinite global dimension.

We can prove similar results for the category
\per(\Lambda)
. Since proof is done in the same way as for
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mod\Lambda)
, we leave it to the reader. Note that by
[
12
, Proposition 6.2]
any equivalence
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mod\Lambda_{1})\cong\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mod\Lambda_{2})
necessarily restricts to the equivalence
\per(\Lambda_{1})\cong\per(\Lambda_{2})
for any finite-dimensional
\mathbb{K}
-algebras
\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}
.∎

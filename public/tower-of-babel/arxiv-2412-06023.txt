Groups generated by spherical twists on K3 surfaces and full exceptional collections on Fano threefolds Thanks: The second author is a senior researcher at the Research Foundation Flanders (FWO). Both authors are also supported by the ERC grant SCHEMES
Anya Nordskova, Michel Van den Bergh

Abstract
. Let Y Y be a smooth K3 surface of Picard rank 1 1 . We prove that the subgroup 𝒢 {\mathcal{G}} of Aut ⁡ 𝒟 b ​ ( Y ) \Aut{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(Y) generated by spherical twists with respect to all spherical objects is free. Moreover, we provide a precise recipe to find free generators of 𝒢 {\mathcal{G}} and determine the cases when 𝒢 {\mathcal{G}} is finitely generated, depending on the degree of Y Y . This description in particular yields a precise classification of spherical objects in 𝒟 b ​ ( Y ) {\mathcal{D}}^{b}(Y) . We apply these results to verify the first three-dimensional case of a conjecture due to Bondal and Polishchuck, namely, we establish the transitivity of the braid group action on full exceptional collections for Fano threefolds of Picard rank 1.

1.
Introduction

Let
Y
be a smooth K3 surface of Picard rank
1
. One of the main results of the groundbreaking work
of Bridgeland
[
14
]
and Bayer-Bridgeland
[
4
]
(also building on some preceding work
[
23
,
32
,
48
]
) is the existence of a short exact sequence of groups (see Theorem
3.25
)

(1.1)

1\to\Aut^{0}{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(Y)\to\Aut{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(Y)\to\Aut^{+}\operatorname{H}^{*}(Y,{\mathbb{Z}})\to 1,

where
\Aut^{+}\operatorname{H}^{*}(Y,{\mathbb{Z}})
is the group of Hodge isometries of
\operatorname{H}^{*}(Y,{\mathbb{Z}})
which preserve the orientation of the positive
4
-planes and
\Aut^{0}{\mathcal{D}}(Y)

is the product of the group generated by the double shift
[2]
and the group
freely generated by the squares of spherical twists
T_{S}^{2}
with
S
running over all spherical vector bundles on
Y
. The sequence (
1.1
) is obtained as a corollary of a highly non-trivial theorem on the contractibility of
\operatorname{Stab}^{\dagger}(Y)
, a distinguished component of the moduli space of stability conditions, which carries a natural
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(Y)
-action.

As our first main result, we refine Bayer-Bridgeland’s sequence obtaining a more precise description of
\Aut{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(Y)
as well as a more precise classification of spherical objects in
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(Y)
. Let us now elaborate on this.

It is well known that K3 surfaces of Picard rank
1
have a single discrete invariant, the minimal degree
d=2\delta
of an ample divisor.
In this introduction
we will assume
d\geq 4
for simplicity and refer to the text for the case
d=2
. First note that the algebraic cycles
provide us with a subgroup of rank
3
in
\operatorname{H}^{\ast}(Y,{\mathbb{Z}})

{\mathcal{N}}(Y):=\operatorname{H}^{0}(Y,{\mathbb{Z}})\oplus\operatorname{Pic}(Y)\oplus\operatorname{H}^{4}(Y,{\mathbb{Z}})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}

which is equipped with the Mukai quadratic form
q(r,l,s)=\delta l^{2}-rs
. We define
\Aut^{+}{\mathcal{N}}(Y)
as the isometries
of
{\mathcal{N}}(Y)
which preserve the orientation of the positive 2-planes. It turns out that there is a well-defined restriction map

(1.2)

\Aut^{+}\operatorname{H}^{\ast}(Y,{\mathbb{Z}})\rightarrow\Aut^{+}{\mathcal{N}}(Y)

which is injective (assuming
d\geq 4
, see Lemma
10.4
). The following precise descriptions of the groups
\Aut^{+}{\mathcal{N}}(Y)
and
\Aut^{+}\operatorname{H}^{*}(Y,{\mathbb{Z}})
are well known to experts and go back to Dolgachev
[
19
]
and Kawatani
[
31
]
. For the benefit of the reader, we give independent proofs of these results in the Appendix (see §
9
, §
10
). Recall that for
\delta\geq 1
the
Fricke group

F(\delta)
is obtained by adjoining the
Fricke involution

f_{\delta}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\
\delta&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
to the standard
congruence subgroup

\Gamma_{0}(\delta):=\left\{{\footnotesize\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}}\in\operatorname{PSL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})\mid c\cong 0\operatorname{mod}\delta\right\}\subset\operatorname{PSL}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}),

while the
Atkin-Lehner
group, denoted
\Gamma^{+}_{0}(\delta)
, is obtained from
\Gamma_{0}(\delta)
by adjoining the “Atkin-Lehner elements”, of which
f_{\delta}
is a special case (see §
3.5
). In particular,
F(\delta)\subset\Gamma^{+}_{0}(\delta)
.

Proposition 1.1

(
[
19
, Theorem 7.1, Remark 7.2.2]
,
[
31
, Theorem 3.3]
, see also Remark
9.6
, Proposition
10.5
)
.

Assume
d\geq 4
. We have the isomorphisms

(1.3)

\displaystyle\Aut^{+}\operatorname{H}^{*}(Y,{\mathbb{Z}})/\{\pm 1\}

\displaystyle\cong F(\delta)

\displaystyle\Aut^{+}{\mathcal{N}}(Y)/\{\pm 1\}

\displaystyle\cong\Gamma^{+}_{0}(\delta)

One has
F(\delta)/\Gamma_{0}(\delta)\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
and
\Gamma^{+}_{0}(\delta)/\Gamma_{0}(\delta)=({\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})^{c}
where
c
is the number of distinct prime factors in
\delta
.
Hence (
1.2
) is in general not surjective. The first counterexample is given by
\delta=6
, i.e.
d=12
.

The groups that appear in (
1.3
)
are examples of so-called Fuchsian groups, that is, they are discrete subgroups of the group of orientation preserving isometries of the complex upper half-plane
{\mathbb{H}}
on which they act by

Möbius transformations

{z\mapsto(az+b)/(cz+d)}
. It was observed already by Kawatani
[
32
]
that
\operatorname{Stab}^{\dagger}(Y)
, a distinguished component of the space of stability
conditions on
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(Y)
, is closely related to the hyperbolic plane
{\mathbb{H}}
. On the other hand, as mentioned above,
\operatorname{Stab}^{\dagger}(Y)
is equipped with a natural action of
\Aut{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(Y)
and the existence of the exact sequence (
1.1
) ultimately boils down to the fact that
\operatorname{Stab}^{\dagger}(Y)
is simply connected, which was originally conjectured in
[
14
]
and proved in
[
4
]
. Leveraging this together with (
1.3
) we obtain the following, which was already observed by Fan and Lai in
[
20
]
(see (1.2) on p.4).

Observation 1.2

(Lemma
6.4
)
.

Let
Y
be a K3 surface of Picard rank
1
and degree
d=2\delta\geq 4
. Let
\Delta^{+}(Y)=\{v=(r,l,s)\in{\mathcal{N}}(Y)\mid q(v)-1,r>0\}
and let
{\mathbb{H}}^{0}
be the complement in
{\mathbb{H}}
of the points

(l\delta+i\sqrt{\delta})/(r\delta)
for
(r,l,s)\in\Delta^{+}(Y)
. Then

\Aut{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(Y)/[1]\cong\pi_{1}([{\mathbb{H}}^{0}/F(\delta)])

where
[{\mathbb{H}}^{0}/F(\delta)]
is the orbifold quotient of
{\mathbb{H}}^{0}
by
F(\delta)
.

[{\mathbb{H}}^{0}/F(\delta)]
is an orbifold Riemann surface, so in particular its fundamental group is well understood (see §
5.3
). This yields a good understanding
of the group
\Aut{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(Y)/[1]
. A similar approach can be applied to subgroups of
\Aut{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(Y)/[1]
that contain
\Aut^{0}{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(Y)/[2]
such as (the image of) the group

{\mathcal{G}}=\langle T_{S}\ |\ S\in{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(Y)\text{ spherical}\rangle\subset\Aut{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(Y)

generated
by all spherical twists. It turns out that the image
G
of
{\mathcal{G}}
in
\Aut^{+}\operatorname{H}^{*}(Y,{\mathbb{Z}})/\{\pm 1\}=F(\delta)
acts freely on
{\mathbb{H}}^{0}
(see Lemma
5.8
(
1
)), so
that
[{\mathbb{H}}^{0}/G]={\mathbb{H}}^{0}/G
is a (non-compact) Riemann surface. Hence
{\mathcal{G}}=\pi_{1}([{\mathbb{H}}^{0}/G])
is actually a free group. Elaborating on this leads to our first main result.

Theorem 1.3

(Theorem
6.6
)
.

Let
Y
be a K3 surface of Picard rank
1
.
Then the subgroup
{\mathcal{G}}
of
\Aut{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(Y)
generated by spherical twists is freely generated by a collection of spherical
twists associated to spherical vector bundles. These vector bundles are orbit representatives for the action of
{\mathcal{G}}
on the set of all spherical objects in
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(Y)
.

The actual result in the text is more detailed and in fact gives a precise recipe to find the generators of
{\mathcal{G}}
in terms of a suitable fundamental domain for the action of
G
on
{\mathbb{H}}
. We apply this recipe explicitly to some low-degree K3 surfaces in §
7
, in particular for those that appear as anticanonical sections of Fano threefolds of Picard rank
1
possessing a full exceptional collection. These examples are of particular interest to us and we will discuss them in detail below. In these cases we show that the group
{\mathcal{G}}\cong F_{4}
is freely generated by four elements, which may suggest the optimistic conjecture that
{\mathcal{G}}
is always finitely generated. This is far from the case, however, and in fact we derive a complete (finite) list of degrees
d
for which it is true:

Theorem 1.4

(Theorem
6.7
)
.

Let
Y
be a K3 surface of Picard rank
1
and degree
d=2\delta
. Let

{\mathcal{G}}
be the group generated by all spherical
twists. Then
{\mathcal{G}}
is finitely generated if and only if
\delta\in\{1,2,3,5,7,11,17,19,23,29,31,41,47,59,71\}
.

In §
7
we consider in particular the smallest infinitely generated example, namely,
d=8
. In that case it turns out that
{\mathcal{G}}
is freely generated by
T_{{\mathcal{O}}(nH)}
,
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, where
H
is a generator of
\operatorname{Pic}(Y)
.

1.1.
Application to Bondal-Polishchuk’s conjecture

Bondal and Polishchuk conjectured
[
11
, Conjecture 2.2]
that the action of

B_{n}\ltimes{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
, where
B_{n}
is the
n
-strand braid group, by mutations and shifts is transitive on full exceptional collections of length
n
for any triangulated category. This conjecture has been recently disproved by Chang, Haiden and Schroll
[
15
]
who found a series of partially wrapped Fukaya categories producing counterexamples.
Restricting to bounded derived categories
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(X)
of smooth projective varieties
X
, the conjecture has been verified in a small number of special cases, namely, by Rudakov
[
52
]
,
[
51
]
for
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
and
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
, by Kuleshov and Orlov
[
34
]
for all del Pezzo surfaces and by Ishii, Okawa and Uehara
[
25
]
for the second Hirzebruch surface
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
. However, in general the question is still widely open.

As an application of our result on the groups generated by spherical twists on K3 surfaces, we settle the first three-dimensional case of Bondal-Polishchuk’s conjecture. Namely, consider threefolds with a full exceptional collection of four vector bundles. It turns out that such a threefold
X
is automatically Fano and has one of the four types
{X={\mathbb{P}}^{3},Q_{3},V_{5}}
and
V_{22}
, where only
V_{22}
has non-trivial moduli (see Lemma
3.5
and §
7
for more details).
Since
\operatorname{rk}K_{0}(X)=4=\dim X+1
, it follows from
[
12
]

(see Proposition
3.3
)
that the full
B_{n}\ltimes{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
-orbit of the given full exceptional collection consists of full exceptional collections of vector bundles, up to shifts. A converse to this result was obtained by Polishchuk
[
49
]
: he showed that the action of
B_{4}\ltimes{\mathbb{Z}}^{4}
on full exceptional collections of shifted vector bundles is transitive. However, even for
{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
it was unknown if these constitute all full exceptional collections. The following result settles this:

Theorem 1.5

(Theorem
8.6
, Corollary
8.7
)
.

Let
X
be a threefold with a full exceptional collection of four vector bundles.
Then:

(1)

all full exceptional collections on
X
consist of shifted vector bundles;

(2)

the action of
B_{4}\ltimes{\mathbb{Z}}^{4}
by mutations and shifts is transitive and free.

Note that in addition to the transitivity conjectured by Bondal and Polishchuk, we also prove that the action is free.
By Polishchuk’s result, (
1
) implies the transitivity part of (
2
).
However, we actually prove (
2
) first using a new approach detailed below and then we deduce (
1
) from it.

Remark 1.6
.

We remark that Theorem
1.5
(
1
) does not rule out the existence of exceptional objects in
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(X)
which are not shifts of vector bundles, nor does it yield a classification of exceptional vector bundles on
X
, since even for
{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
it is unknown whether every exceptional vector bundle can be included in a full exceptional collection.

Remark 1.7
.

We should also stress that all exceptional collections in Theorem
1.5
are assumed to be full. Even for
{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
it is currently unknown whether every exceptional collection of length
4
is full. Recently an example of a rational surface with a non-full exceptional collection of maximal length was discovered by Krah
[
33
]
.

Remark 1.8
.

A general result by Positselski
[
50
]
states that if
\operatorname{rk}K_{0}(X)=\dim X+1
then every full strong exceptional collection
(see Definition
3.1
) on
X
consists of shifts
of vector bundles. So to prove Theorem
1.5
it would be enough to show that every full exceptional collection is strong. We do not know how to do this directly however.

Let us now elaborate on the way Theorem
1.3
is applied to prove Theorem
1.5
(
2
). Our proof starts from the following elementary yet crucial observation. Recall that if
H
is a group then the
Hurwitz action
of
B_{n}
on
H^{n}
is given by (see §
3.3
):

\sigma_{i}(g_{1},\ldots,g_{i},g_{i+1},\ldots,g_{n})=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{i}g_{i+1}g_{i}^{-1},g_{i},\ldots,g_{n})

Let
X
be a Fano threefold of Picard rank
1
and let
Y
be a generic anticanonical divisor
Y\subset X
. Then
Y
is a K3 surface of Picard rank
1
. The restriction of an exceptional object
E
on
X
to
Y
is a spherical object
E|_{Y}
. Moreover, if
X
has a full exceptional collection, then it turns out that the group
{\mathcal{G}}\subset\Aut{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(Y)
generated by spherical twists and the Hurwitz action appear naturally:

Observation 1.9

(Lemma
3.18
)
.

Let
E
be an exceptional object on
X
and let
\phi(E):=T_{E|_{Y}}\in\Aut{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(Y)
be the spherical twist associated to the spherical object
E|_{Y}
.
When extended to sequences of objects in
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(X)
, the map

\phi
intertwines the action of the braid group
B_{n}
on the set of full exceptional collections in
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(X)
and the Hurwitz action of
B_{n}
on
{\mathcal{G}}^{n}
.

Theorem
1.3
claims that the group
{\mathcal{G}}
in Observation
1.9
is free. More precisely, it specialises to the following:

Theorem 1.10

(Theorem
7.1
)
.

Let
X
be a threefold with a full exceptional collection of
4
vector bundles, namely,
{X={\mathbb{P}}^{3},Q_{3},V_{5}}
or
V_{22}
. Let
Y\subset X
be a generic anticanonical divisor which is a K3 surface of Picard rank
1
and degree
4,6,10
and
22
respectively. Then the group
{\mathcal{G}}\subset\Aut{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(Y)

generated by all spherical twists is isomorphic to the free group
F_{4}
on
4
generators which are the spherical twists along the spherical vector bundles obtained by restricting a full exceptional collection of vector bundles on
X
to
Y
.

To summarise, the action of the braid group
B_{4}
on the set of full exceptional collections in
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(X)
is closely related to the Hurwitz action of
B_{4}
on
F_{4}^{4}
, a power of a free group. The latter action can be understood well abstractly
[
45
]
. Note that in principle restricting to an anticanonical divisor looses some information. However this turned out to be remedied by the fact that we are considering full exceptional collections and not individual exceptional objects. We obtain Theorem
1.5
(
2
) from Theorem
1.10
and the fact that the product of the spherical twists associated to a full exceptional collection
only depends on the Serre functor on
X
(this is a special case of
[
36
, Theorem A.1]
):

Proposition 1.11

(Proposition
3.15
)
.

Let
(E_{1},\cdots,E_{n})
be a full exceptional collection in a Fano variety
X
and let
i:Y\hookrightarrow X
be a smooth anticanonical divisor.
Let
T_{i}:=T_{E_{i}|_{Y}}
.
One has
T_{1}\dots T_{n}(-)=-\otimes i^{*}\omega_{X}[2]

in
\Aut{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(Y)

Combining this with our abstract result concerning the Hurwitz action on powers of free groups (
[
45
, Theorem 2.6(1)]
, see Theorem
3.21
) we are able to deduce that any exceptional collection can be mutated
into one furnishing a chosen set of generators for
{\mathcal{G}}
(see Lemma
8.5
). This essentially finishes the proof of Theorem
1.5
(
2
).

3.
Generalities

We work over the complex numbers. Unless otherwise specified
X
is
a smooth projective variety. We denote by
\mathop{\text{{coh}}}(X)
the category of
coherent sheaves on
X
and by
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(X):={\mathcal{D}}^{b}(\mathop{\text{{coh}}}(X))
the
bounded derived category of
\mathop{\text{{coh}}}(X)
. A Fano variety is a smooth
projective variety
X
whose anticanonical divisor
-K_{X}
is ample.
By a K3 surface
Y
we mean a complex algebraic surface with trivial
canonical bundle
\omega_{Y}\simeq{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}
and
\operatorname{H}^{1}(Y,{\mathcal{O}}_{Y})=0
.
We refer the reader to
[
24
]
for a comprehensive account
on K3 surfaces.

3.1.
Exceptional collections and Fano threefolds

Definition 3.1
.

An object
E\in{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(X)
is
exceptional
if
\operatorname{Hom}^{i}(E,E)=0
for any
i\neq 0
and
\operatorname{Hom}^{0}(E,E)\cong{\mathbb{C}}
. An ordered collection
{\mathcal{E}}=(E_{1},\dots,E_{m})
of objects of
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(X)
is called an
exceptional collection
if each
E_{i}
is exceptional and
\operatorname{Hom}^{\bullet}(E_{j},E_{k})=0
for all
j>k
. An exceptional collection
{\mathcal{E}}
is
full
if
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(X)
is the smallest triangulated subcategory of
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(X)
containing
E_{1},\dots,E_{m}
. We say that
{\mathcal{E}}
is
strong
if
\operatorname{Hom}^{k}(E_{i},E_{j})=0
for any
k\neq 0
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
(E,F)
be an exceptional pair. We define the left (respectively right)
mutation
of
(E,F)
as
(L_{E}F,E)
(respectively
(F,R_{F}E)
), where the objects
L_{E}F
and
R_{F}E
are given by the distinguished triangles

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}^{\bullet}(E,F)\otimes E\to F\to L_{E}F

\displaystyle R_{F}E\to E\to\operatorname{Hom}^{\bullet}(E,F)^{\ast}\otimes F

Proposition 3.3

(Bondal,
[
12
, Assertion 2.3]
)
.

The braid group
B_{n}
acts on the set of exceptional collections in
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(X)
of length
n
by mutations. More precisely, the standard generator
\sigma_{i}\in B_{n}
,
1\leq i\leq n-1
acts by

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}(E_{1},\dots,E_{n})=(E_{1},\dots,E_{i-1},L_{E_{i}}E_{i+1},E_{i},E_{i+2},\dots,E_{n})

Proposition 3.4

(Bondal,
[
12
, Assertion 9.2]
)
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety with
\operatorname{rk}K_{0}(X)=\dim X+1
. Then mutations of full exceptional collections of shifted sheaves in
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(X)
also consist of shifted sheaves.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective threefold. The following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(X)
admits a full exceptional collection of length

consisting of vector bundles.

(2)

is Fano and

(3)

In the classification of

)

, the 3-dimensional quadric

.

3.2.
Spherical twists

Definition 3.7
.

An object
S\in{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(X)
is
spherical
if
\omega_{X}\otimes S\cong S
and
\operatorname{Hom}^{i}(S,S)={\mathbb{C}}
if
{i\in\{0,\dim X\}}
and
\operatorname{Hom}^{i}(S,S)=0

otherwise. The set of spherical objects in
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(X)
up to isomorphism is denoted by
\operatorname{Sph}(X)
.

Definition 3.8

(Seidel-Thomas,
[
54
]
)
.

A spherical object
S\in{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(X)
gives rise to an autoequivalence
T_{S}\in\Aut{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(X)
(referred to as
spherical twist
) such that for every
A\in{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(X)
there is a distinguished triangle

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}^{\bullet}(S,A)\otimes S\xrightarrow{ev}A\to T_{S}(A).

Alternatively,
T_{S}
can be defined as the Fourier-Mukai transform with kernel
\cone(S\boxtimes S^{\vee}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{\Delta})\in{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(X\times X)
.

We also need a more general notion of a twist with respect to a spherical functor.

Definition 3.9
.

Let
F:{\mathcal{B}}\to{\mathcal{D}}
be a functor between triangulated categories with fixed dg-enhancements in the sense of
[
9
]
and assume that
F
has a right adjoint
R
and a left adjoint
L
. The
twist

T_{F}
and the
cotwist

C_{F}
with respect to
F
are defined via the distinguished triangles

\displaystyle FR\xrightarrow{\varepsilon}{\operatorname{id}}_{{\mathcal{D}}}\to T_{F}

\displaystyle C_{F}\to{\operatorname{id}}_{{\mathcal{B}}}\xrightarrow{\eta}RF,

where
\varepsilon
and
\eta
are the counit and the unit of the adjunction
(F,R)
. We say that the functor
F
is
spherical

5.2.
Free products generated by elements of order two

Lemma 5.1
.

Let

be a group and assume that
R\subset G-\{1\}
is a set of order two elements such that

G=\ast_{r\in R}\langle r\rangle,

where

Then any element of order two in

is conjugate to some element in

Proof.

Let

be such that

. Expressing

as a reduced word
in

we see that

must be palindromic. If
x
has odd length then it is of
the form
wrw^{-1}
. Even length is impossible since then
x=ww^{-1}=1
.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
G
be a group and assume that
R\subset G-\{1\}
is a set of order two elements such that

G=\ast_{r\in R}\langle r\rangle

Let

be a collection of elements such that

(a)

is generated by

;

(b)

For each

;

(c)

is closed under conjugation.

Then

(1)

is a set of orbit representatives for the action of

on
S
by conjugation (in particular

).

(2)

S
is the set of order two elements in
G-\{1\}
.

Proof.

Let
s\in S
. By Lemma
5.1

s
is conjugate to some
r\in R

and by (

)

. So every element in

is conjugate to an element in

.
Assume first that

and choose

.
Since by (

generates

it follows that

is a product of conjugates of elements in

. Let
\overline{r}
be the image of

in

. Then

is a sum of

-basis for

. We conclude that

and furthermore every conjugation orbit in

If

∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let the notations and assumptions be as in Lemma
5.2
.
We have a presentation

(5.1)

1\rightarrow F\rightarrow{\mathcal{G}}\rightarrow G\rightarrow 0

where

is defined as the free group generated by all symbols
\tilde{r}
for

and

is the free group generated by all elements of the form

, where

and

not divisible by

on the right.
In particular, sending

of

Proof.

The fact that the given elements are free generators of

follows naturally from the topological proof of the Nielsen-Schreier theorem
(see
[

). Consider the directed coset graph

for

∎

Remark 5.4

The given generators of

For example there is an uncountable number of spanning trees one may contract, each yielding a different set of generators.

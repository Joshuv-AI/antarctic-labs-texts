1.
Introduction

Let
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
denote a finite field with
q
elements. An element
\alpha
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is called primitive if it generates the cyclic group of non-zero elements of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, denoted by
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
. Clearly, the order of primitive elements is
q-1
. There are several applications of primitive elements in cryptography
[
2
,
18
,
19
]
. Primitive elements are generalized as
r
-primitive elements for a positive divisor
r
of
q-1
. More precisely, an element
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is called
r
-primitive if its order is
\frac{q-1}{r}
. Although primitive elements are elusive, there are, in some cases, effective ways to find
r
-primitive elements
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
. Many articles in the literature
[
3
,
11
,
21
,
20
]
deal with the existence of primitive pairs
(\alpha,f(\alpha))
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, where
f(x)
is a non-exceptional rational function in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(x)
. This is equivalent to the existence of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-primitive points on the curves
y=f(x)
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.
Note that, the
“
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-primitivity”
on curves is not related to the
“
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-primitivity”
on elliptic curves defined in earlier works. More precisely, in
[
17
]
, Lang and Trotter called a point to be
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-primitive point on an elliptic curve if it generates the elliptic curve with respect to the elliptic curve group operation.
In 2022, Cohen, Kapetanakis and Reis
[
13
]
generalized the notion of freeness and proved the existence of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-primitive points on the curves of the form
y^{n}=f(x)
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.
In this article, we extend the notion of exceptional functions from one variable to the functions of
n
-variables and call them
primarily exceptional
functions. Then we obtain a sufficient condition for the existence of triples of the form
(\alpha,\beta,f(\alpha,\beta))
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, where
\alpha,\beta
are primitive and
f(\alpha,\beta)
is
r
-primitive for a primarily non-exceptional rational function of degree sum
d
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. In other words, we discuss the existence of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-primitive points on the surfaces of the form
z^{r}=f(x,y)
, where
r|q-1
and
f(x,y)
is a primarily non-exceptional rational function over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.

2.
Preliminaries

Let us recall some basic concepts crucial for our proof. For positive integers
a
and
b
, set
a_{(b)}=\frac{a}{\text{gcd}(a,b)}
. For a group
G
, a character
\chi
is a homomorphism from
G
to the multiplicative group of complex numbers with unit modulus. A character
\chi_{1}
is called the trivial character of
G
when
\chi_{1}(g)=1
for all
g\in G
. Two types of characters can be defined over a finite field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
corresponding to the operations of addition and multiplication, namely additive and multiplicative characters, respectively. A multiplicative character
\chi
can be extended from
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
to
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
by the following rule

\chi(0)\coloneqq\begin{cases}0,~~~\text{if}~~~\chi\neq\chi_{1},\\
1,~~~\text{if}~~~\chi=\chi_{1}.\end{cases}

The order of a character
\chi
is defined to be the smallest positive integer
s
such that
\chi^{s}=\chi_{1}
. We denote the set of multiplicative characters of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
by
\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
.

An element
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is called
e
-free for
e|q-1
, if
\alpha
can not be written as
\beta^{d}
for any
\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
and
d|e
with
d>1
. Clearly,
\alpha
is primitive when
\alpha
is
(q-1)
-free. Following Cohen and Huczynska
[
9
,
10
]
, the characteristic function for the set of
e
-free elements of
{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}}
, where
e|q-1
, is given by

\rho_{e}:\alpha\mapsto\theta(e)\sum\limits_{s|e}\frac{\mu(s)}{\phi(s)}\sum\limits_{\chi_{s}}\chi_{s}(\alpha),

where
\theta(e):=\frac{\phi(e)}{e},

\mu
denotes the Möbius function, and
\chi_{s}
is a multiplicative character of order
s
in
\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
.

For a divisor
r
of
q-1
and a divisor
R
of
\frac{q-1}{r}
, let
C_{r}
denote the multiplicative subgroup of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
of order
\frac{q-1}{r}
, an element
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
is called
(R,r)
-free if
\alpha\in C_{r}
and
\alpha
is
R
-free in
C_{r}
, i.e., if
\alpha
can not be written as
\alpha=\beta^{s}
for any
\beta\in C_{r}
and
s|R
, with
s>1
. Clearly,
\alpha
is
r
-primitive when
\alpha
is
(\frac{q-1}{r},r)
-free. The characteristic function for
r
-primitive elements was first expressed in a useful way by Cohen and Kapetanakis
[
12
]
. Later, Cohen et al.
[
13
]
provided an improved version of the characteristic function as follows.

\mathbb{I}_{R,r}:\alpha\mapsto\frac{\theta(R)}{r}\sum_{d|Rr}\frac{\mu(d_{(r)})}{\phi(d_{(r)})}\sum_{\chi_{d}}\chi_{d}(\alpha),

where
\theta(R)=\frac{\phi(R)}{R},
and
\chi_{d}
is a multiplicative character of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
of order
d
.

To bound certain character sum that will arise, we will use the following result.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
13
]

For any positive integers
R,r
, we have that

\sum_{d|R}\frac{|\mu(d_{(r)})|}{\phi(d_{(r)})}\cdot\phi(d)=\text{gcd}(R,r)\cdot W(\text{gcd}(R,R_{(r)})).

The following result will help us prove the sufficient condition in Theorem
3.2
.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
7
]

Let
F(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
be a rational function. Write
F(x)=\prod_{j=1}^{k}F_{j}(x)^{r_{j}}
, where
F_{j}(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
are irreducible polynomials and

are nonzero integers. Let
\chi
be a multiplicative character of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of order
d
(a divisor of
q-1
). Suppose that
F(x)
is not of the form
cG(x)^{d}
for any rational function
G(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
and
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}*
. Then we have

\left|\sum\limits_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q},F(\alpha)\neq\infty}\chi(F(\alpha))\right|\leq\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}deg(F_{j})-1\right)q^{1/2}.

3.
Sufficient Condition

First, we recall exceptional functions of one variable over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
introduced by Cohen et al.
[
14
]
. A function
g(x)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(x)
is called an exceptional rational function if
g(x)=\lambda h(x)^{s}
, for some
\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
,
h(x)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(x)
and
s|q-1
such that
s>1
. Now, we extend this notion to the functions of
n
variables over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.

Definitions 3.1
.

A function
f(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})
is called primarily exceptional rational function if
f(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n-1},x_{n})
are exceptional functions of one variable
x_{n}
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
for every possible primitive tuple
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n-1})
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.

In particular,
f(x,y)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(x,y)
is called
primarily exceptional
rational function if
f(\alpha,y)
are exceptional functions for every primitive
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. A stronger definition could also be given by taking both
f(\alpha,y)
and
f(x,\alpha)
to be exceptional for every primitive
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. In this work, for the sake of convenience, we will work with the former definition. However, without loss of generality, our results are valid even if the latter definition of primarily exceptional functions is chosen.
Clearly, functions of the form
cg(x,y)^{d}
are trivial examples of
primarily exceptional
functions for
d|q-1
,
c\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
and
g(x,y)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(x,y)
. This family of functions gives rise to the exceptional functions of one variable
f(a,y)
for every
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
(not necessarily primitive).
However, a function of the form

f(x,y)=\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\\
\alpha\ \text{primitive}\end{subarray}}(x-\alpha)g(x,y)+c\cdot h(x,y)^{d}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(x,y)

will give rise to the exceptional functions of one variable
f(\alpha,y)
but for generic
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q},f(a,y)
may not be exceptional, if
g
and
h
are chosen carefully.

Let
\Delta_{q}(d)
denote the set of rational functions
f(x,y)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(x,y)
of degree sum
d
that are not primarily exceptional over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. For a positive integer
\ell
, we denote the number of square-free divisors of
\ell
by
W(\ell)
.
To show the existence of primitive points over
z^{r}=f(x,y)
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, we fix
\alpha
to be a primitive element of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. Then, we check whether
f(\alpha,\beta)
is
r
-primitive when
\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is primitive. For
\ell|q-1
and
R|\frac{q-1}{r}
, let
N_{f}(\ell,R)
represent the number of triples
(\alpha,\beta,f(\alpha,\beta))
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
such that
\alpha
is primitive,
\beta
is
{\ell}
-free and
f(\alpha,\beta)
is
(R,r)
-free. Let
\mathfrak{B}_{f}
denote the set of prime power
q
such that there exists a primitive point on the surface
z^{r}=f(x,y))
for
f(x,y)\in\Delta_{q}(d)
. Hence, to show that
q\in\mathfrak{B}_{f}
, it is sufficient to prove that
N_{f}(q-1,\frac{q-1}{r})>0
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
q
be a prime power,
r|q-1,d\in\mathbb{N}
and
f(x,y)\in\Delta_{q}(d)
. Then
q\in\mathfrak{B}_{f}
if

(3.1)

q^{1/2}>rdW(q-1)W\left(\frac{q-1}{r}\right).

Proof.

For primitive
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
,
\mathcal{V}
denotes the set of poles of
f(\alpha^{k},y)
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, where
1\leq k\leq q-1
is such that gcd
(k,q-1)=1
.
Now, for
R|\frac{q-1}{r}
and
l|q-1
,

\displaystyle N_{f}(\ell,R)=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq q-1\\
\gcd(k,q-1)=1\end{subarray}}\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\backslash\mathcal{V}}\rho_{\ell}(\beta)\mathbb{I}_{R,r}f(\alpha^{k},\beta)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\frac{\theta(\ell)\theta(R)}{r}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1}|{\ell},d_{2}|Rr\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(d_{1})\mu(d_{2(r)})}{\phi(d_{1})\phi(d_{2(r)})}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi_{d_{1}},\chi_{d_{2}}\end{subarray}}\mathcal{S}_{d_{1},d_{2}}

where
\mathcal{S}_{d_{1},d_{2}}=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq q-1\\
\gcd(k,q-1)=1\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\backslash\mathcal{V}}\chi_{d_{1}}(\beta)\chi_{d_{2}}(f(\alpha^{k},\beta))
.

For multiplicative character
\chi_{q-1}
of order
q-1
,
\chi_{d_{j}}(\beta)=\chi_{q-1}(\beta^{n_{j}})
, for some
n_{j}\in\{0,1,2,\ldots,q-2\},j=1,2
. Therefore

\mathcal{S}_{d_{1},d_{2}}=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq q-1\\
\gcd(k,q-1)=1\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\backslash\mathcal{V}}\chi_{q-1}(\beta^{n_{1}}f(\alpha^{k},\beta)^{n_{2}})

If
y^{n_{1}}f(\alpha^{k},y)^{n_{2}}\neq cG(y)^{q-1}
for any

and

, then using Lemma

, we obtain

|\mathcal{S}_{d_{1},d_{2}}|\leq d\phi(q-1)q^{1/2}.

If

, write
f(\alpha^{k},y)=\omega y^{t}\prod_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}(y)^{s_{j}}
, where

is the number of irreducible factors of
f(\alpha^{k},y)
and
s_{j}
is the multiplicity of those factors

. Suppose
s_{j}^{\prime}
denote the multiplicity of
f_{j}(y)
in
G(y)
. Comparing the multiplicity of
f_{j}(y)
on both sides, we get
s_{j}n_{2}=s_{j}^{\prime}(q-1)
.
Let
s=(q-1)/\text{gcd}(q-1,n_{2})
. Clearly
s|s_{j}
and
s|q-1
. Hence
f(\alpha^{k},y)=\omega y^{t}g(y)^{s}
for some

. Since
f(x,y)\in\Delta_{q}(d)
, we get

. This implies
q-1|n_{2}
, hence
n_{2}=0
. Therefore
G(y)=y^{t}
for some positive integer

and we get
n_{1}=t(q-1)
. This implies
n_{1}=0
. In other words
d_{1}=1
and
d_{2}=1
.
Therefore, when
(d_{1},d_{2})\neq(1,1)
we have

|\mathcal{S}_{d_{1},d_{2}}|<d\phi(q-1)q^{1/2}.

Hence

\displaystyle N_{f}(\ell,R)

\displaystyle\geq{}\frac{\theta(\ell)\theta(R)}{r}\phi(q-1)\{q-|\mathcal{V}|-dq^{\frac{1}{2}}(rW(\ell)W(R)-1)\}

\displaystyle>{}\frac{\theta(\ell)\theta(R)}{r}\phi(q-1)\{q-rdq^{\frac{1}{2}}W(\ell)W(R)\}.

Hence
N_{f}(\ell,R)>0
, whenever
q^{\frac{1}{2}}>rdW(\ell)W(R).
Now, the result follows by taking
\ell=q-1
and
R=\frac{q-1}{r}
.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

For any

, such that for any prime power

.

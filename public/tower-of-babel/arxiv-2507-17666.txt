Abstract
. In 2018, Alexander A. Razborov proved that the edge density of Fon-der-Flaass ( 3 , 4 ) (3,4) -graph is ≥ 7 16 ​ ( 1 − o ​ ( 1 ) ) \geq\frac{7}{16}(1-o(1)) , using flag algebras. In this paper, we give an elementary proof of this result.



1.
Introduction

Given a
3
-graph
G=(V,E)
, the edge density of
G
is
\frac{|E|}{\binom{|V|}{3}}
. In 1941, Turán
[
1
]
conjectured that the edge density of any
3
-graph without independent sets on
4
vertices (or
(3,4)
-graph) is
\geq\frac{4}{9}(1-o(1))
. In 1988, Fon-der-Flaass
[
2
]
gave a construction of a family of
(3,4)
-graph:

Construction 1
.

Let
\Gamma=(V,A)
be a simple directed graph contains no induced subgraph isomorphic to a direct circled of length
4
. We construct
3
-graph
F(\Gamma)=(V,F)
which
(x,y,z)\in F
if one of the following conditions hold:

C1
: The induced subgraph in
\Gamma
by
\{x,y,z\}
has at most
1
vertices.

C2
:
(x,y),(x,z)\in A
.

Fon-der-Flaass proved that the edge density of any
(3,4)
-graph that created this way is
\geq\frac{3}{7}(1-o(1))
, and in 2018, Alexander A. Razborov improved this bound to
\frac{7}{16}(1-o(1))
, using flag algebra. We will give an elementary proof of this result, without using flag algebra.

For technical reason, we remove the induced subgraph condition of directed graph
\Gamma
and consider the complement
3
-graph of
F(\Gamma)
instead.

Construction 2
.

Let
\Gamma=(V,A)
be a simple directed graph. We construct
3
-graph
CF(\Gamma)=(V,F)
which
(x,y,z)\in F
if the induced subgraph in
\Gamma
by
\{x,y,z\}
has at least
2
vertices and
\{(x,y),(x,z)\},\{(y,z),(y,x)\},\{(z,x),(z,y)\}\not\subset A
.

Theorem 1
.

The edge density of
CF(\Gamma)
of any directed graph
\Gamma
is
\leq\frac{9}{16}(1+o(1))

For a directed graph
\Gamma=(V,A)
and the corresponding
3
-graph
CF(\Gamma)=(V,F)
, define the Lagrangian of them by:

\mathcal{L}_{CF}(\Gamma)=\sum_{(x,y,z)\in F}xyz+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{(x,y)\in A}x^{2}y

Theorem 2
.

For any directed graph
\Gamma=(V,A)
and any way of assigning non-negative real value to the elements of
V
such that
\sum_{x\in V}=1
, we have
\mathcal{L}_{CF}(\Gamma)\leq\frac{3}{32}
.

By assigning
x=\frac{1}{|V|},\forall x\in V
, we can see that Theorem
1
is followed from Theorem
2
.

2.
Reduction to undirected graph

We now construct a similar but simpler family of
3
-graphs from an undirected graph.

Construction 3
.

Let
G=(V,B)
be a graph. We construct
3
-graph
BF(G)=(V,E)
which
(x,y,z)\in E
if the induced subgraph in
\Gamma
by
\{x,y,z\}
has at least
2
vertices.

For a graph
G=(V,B)
and the corresponding
3
-graph
BF(G)=(V,E)
, define the Lagrangian of them by:

\mathcal{L}_{BF}(G)=\sum_{(x,y,z)\in E}xyz+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{(x,y)\in B}(x^{2}y+xy^{2})-\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{(x,y)\in B}xy\right)^{2}

For directed graph
\Gamma=(V,A)
, construct
G(\Gamma)=(V,B)
from
\Gamma
by forget about all direction.

Lemma 3
.

For any directed graph
\Gamma=(V,A)
and any way of assigning non-negative real value to the elements of
V
such that
\sum_{x\in V}=1
, we have
\mathcal{L}_{CF}(\Gamma)\leq\mathcal{L}_{BF}(G(\Gamma))
.

Proof.

Note that from construction
2
and
3
,
(x,y,z)\in E/F
if and only if exactly one of the following assertions is true:
(x,y),(x,z)\in A
or
(y,z),(y,x)\in A
or
(z,x),(z,y)\in A
. We then have

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{BF}(G(\Gamma))-\mathcal{L}_{CF}(\Gamma)&=\sum_{(x,y),(x,z)\in A}xyz+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{(x,y)\in A}xy^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{(x,y)\in B}xy\right)^{2}\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in V}x\left(2\sum_{(x,y),(x,z)\in A}yz+\sum_{(x,y)\in A}y^{2}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{(x,y)\in B}xy\right)^{2}\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in V}x\left(\sum_{(x,y)\in A}y\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{(x,y)\in B}xy\right)^{2}\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{x\in V}x\right)\sum_{x\in V}x\left(\sum_{(x,y)\in A}y\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{(x,y)\in B}xy\right)^{2}\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{x\in V}x\left(\sum_{(x,y)\in A}y\right)\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{(x,y)\in B}xy\right)^{2}\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{(x,y)\in A}xy\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{(x,y)\in B}xy\right)^{2}=0\end{split}

by Cauchy-Schwarz and the assumption
x\in V
are nonnegative,
\sum_{x\in V}=1
.
∎

From Lemma
3
, to prove Theorem
2
, we only need to prove the following theorem:

Theorem 4
.

For any graph
G=(V,B)
and any way to assign a non-negative real value to the elements of
V
such that
\sum_{x\in V}=1
, we have
\mathcal{L}_{BF}(G)\leq\frac{3}{32}
.

3.
Reduction to complete graph

Assume
G=(V,B)
is not complete, then there exists
a,b\in V
such that
(a,b)\notin B
. Define
G_{a},G_{b}
as the graphs created from
G
by deleting the vertices
b,a
, respectively. For an assignment of nonnegative real value to the elements of
V
such that
\sum_{x\in V}=1
, we assign the value of
G_{a}
as follows:
x^{\prime}=a+b
if
x=a
and
x^{\prime}=x
if otherwise, we do the same for
G_{b}
. Denote
S_{a,b}=\sum_{x\in V,(a,b,x)\in E}x,S_{a}=\sum_{x\in V,(a,x)\in B}x,S_{b}=\sum_{x\in V,b,x)\in B}x
. By construction
3
, we have
S_{a,b}\leq\min(S_{a},S_{b})
, by the condition on the value assignment we have
0\leq S_{a},S_{b}\leq 1
, thus
|S_{a}-S_{b}|\leq 1
.

Lemma 5
.

For the above notation, either
\mathcal{L}_{BF}(G)\leq\mathcal{L}_{BF}(G_{a})
or
\mathcal{L}_{BF}(G)\leq\mathcal{L}_{BF}(G_{b})
.

Proof.

If
a=0
, then we have
\mathcal{L}_{BF}(G)=\mathcal{L}_{BF}(G_{a})
, so the lemma is true. Similar for
b=0
. So we can assume
a,b>0
.

By some algebra manipulation, we have:

\begin{split}a\mathcal{L}_{BF}(G_{a})+b\mathcal{L}_{BF}(G_{b})-(a+b)\mathcal{L}_{BF}(G)&=ab(a+b)\left(\frac{1}{2}(S_{a}+S_{b}-(S_{a}-S_{b})^{2})-S_{a,b}\right)\\
&=ab(a+b)\left(\frac{1}{2}(S_{a}+S_{b}-|S_{a}-S_{b}|)-S_{a,b}\right)\\
&=ab(a+b)\left(\min(S_{a},S_{b})-S_{a,b}\right)\geq 0\end{split}

by the above observation that
S_{a,b}\leq\min(S_{a},S_{b})
and
|S_{a}-S_{b}|\leq 1
.

Because
a,b>0
, from the above inequality we have the result that we want.
∎

From Lemma
5
, we only need to consider the case
G
is complete.

4.
Complete the proof

Now we can assume
G
is complete, assume
V=\{x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n}\}
, assign non-negative real value to them such that
\sum x_{i}=1
then the Lagrangian become:

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{BF}(G)&=\sum x_{i}x_{j}x_{k}+\frac{1}{2}\sum(x_{i}^{2}x_{j}+x_{i}x_{j}^{2})-\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum x_{i}x_{j}\right)^{2}\\
&=\frac{1}{6}\left(1-\sum x_{i}^{3}\right)-\frac{1}{8}\left(1-\sum x_{i}^{2}\right)^{2}\end{split}

We need to prove the Lagrangian is
\leq\frac{3}{32}
. We can assume
n\geq 3
, by adding some variables
x_{i}=0
. Without lost of generality, assume
x_{1}\geq x_{2}\geq...\geq x_{n}
. Then
x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}\leq 1
and
\sum x_{i}^{2}\leq x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}(1-x_{1}-x_{2})
. So we have:

\mathcal{L}_{BF}(G)\leq\frac{1}{6}\left(1-x_{1}^{3}-x_{2}^{3}-x_{3}^{3}\right)-\frac{1}{8}\left(1-x_{1}^{2}-x_{2}^{2}-x_{3}(1-x_{1}-x_{2})\right)^{2}

which can be vertified to be
\leq\frac{3}{32}
by Computer Algebra System like Mathematica.

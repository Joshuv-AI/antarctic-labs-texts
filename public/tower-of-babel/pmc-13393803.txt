Evaluation on the efficiencies of centers for disease control and prevention in China: results from a 12-year national survey
Authors: Yujie Yang, Mile Lin, Chongyi Wang, Zhuoyi Ji, Qing Zhu, Yueli Meng, Pei Dong, Kun Wang, Wuqi Qiu, Ayan Mao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe disease prevention and control system represents a fundamental component of public health services. Although the Chinese government has consistently prioritized and increased funding for Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDCs), whether these additional resources have been effectively translated into improved public health outcomes remains indeterminate. This study was designed to evaluate the operational efficiency of CDCs in China, objectively present the changes in efficiency values, and provide an evidence base for optimizing their service effectiveness.MethodsTaking the CDCs in 31 provincial-level administrative regions in mainland China as the research objects, a mixed research design was adopted, integrating longitudinal panel data (2011–2022) and provincial cross-sectional data (2022). Input indicators, output indicators, and environmental variables were all sourced from authoritative statistical yearbooks. The three-stage Data Envelopment Analysis (DEA) model was used to measure the static efficiency of different regions, and the Malmquist index model was applied to decompose the dynamic changes in Total Factor Productivity (TFP) of CDCs.ResultsAt the national level, the average annual growth rate of TFP in the CDCs was 1.3%, mainly driven by technological progress (+ 2.2% annually), but offset by a decline in technical efficiency (-0.9% annually; pure technical efficiency: -0.7%; scale efficiency: -0.2%). All 31 provinces achieved technological progress (Malmquist index > 1), while only 41.94% of provinces experienced an improvement in technical efficiency. In 2022, the average adjusted comprehensive efficiency across regions was 0.811, showing a significant regional gradient: eastern region (0.914) > central region (0.876) > western region (0.672). The average pure technical efficiency was 0.863, and the average scale efficiency was 0.927, with 61.29% of provinces exhibiting decreasing returns to scale. After excluding environmental factors, 22.58% of provinces maintained unchanged efficiency values, most provinces showed fluctuations (both increases and decreases), and the efficiency values of remote western regions slightly improved.ConclusionThe efficiency improvement of China’s CDCs mainly relies on technological progress, while the continuous decline in technical efficiency reflects shortcomings in resource utilization and organizational management. Significant regional disparities in efficiency exist and are influenced by environmental factors. Future efficiency improvement should focus more on the coordination of internal management and technological application, implement differentiated scale optimization strategies, and emphasize regional quality-oriented collaboration (including technology and talent). These measures will promote the high-quality and sustainable development of the disease prevention and control system.

Introduction

The disease prevention and control (DPC) system serves as the primary vehicle for public health intervention capabilities globally. Reforming, developing, and continuously refining this system represents a critical undertaking for national health systems and security frameworks worldwide [1–3].

In China, the DPC system constitutes a fundamental safeguard for protecting public health, ensuring health security, and maintaining socioeconomic stability [4]. As specialized public institutions, Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDCs) deliver essential functions including disease surveillance, technical consultation, and evidence-based guideline development. By the end of 2023, China had established a comprehensive four-tiered CDC network encompassing: one national institution, 31 provincial-level units (including autonomous regions and municipalities), 410 municipal entities, and approximately 2,822 county-level facilities [5]. This professional architecture plays a crucial role in epidemic response, infectious and chronic disease management, and public health emergency actions.

The COVID-19 pandemic underscored the imperative for robust DPC systems, elevating CDC capacity-building to a national policy priority [6, 7]. Consequently, the Chinese government issued the Guiding Opinions on Promoting High-Quality Development of Disease Prevention and Control (December 25, 2023), outlining a strategic blueprint for institutional advancement. Key directives emphasize strengthening core functions across administrative levels, enhancing technical capacities, optimizing workforce development, and establishing sustainable financing mechanisms [8]. Concurrent with increasing resource allocations, rigorous evaluation of CDC operational efficiency and performance optimization has become imperative.

Significant knowledge gaps persist in current research on China’s CDC system. Existing studies predominantly focus on resource allocation patterns and equity analyses, whereas systematic assessments of service delivery efficiency across the national CDC network, particularly regarding deviations from optimal performance, remain underdeveloped [9–12]. Consequently, a comprehensive nationwide efficiency evaluation is urgently needed.

Among analytical methodologies, data envelopment analysis (DEA) provides a robust non-parametric approach for evaluating relative efficiency in multi-input and multi-output systems, with well-established applications in healthcare efficiency assessments, resource optimization, and performance benchmarking [13–15]. However, previous DEA applications in China’s CDCs context have been restricted to single administrative tiers (e.g., provincial CDCs) or fragmented operational domains (e.g., laboratory functions), failing to characterize the holistic efficiency landscape and its temporal evolution across the entire four-tiered system [16, 17].

To address these limitations, this study constructs an integrated analytical framework and employs DEA models to systematically evaluate the static efficiency and dynamic productivity changes at both the national and provincial levels, providing a reference for policymakers to accurately identify structural inefficiencies, optimize resource allocation, and promote the high-quality development of China’s disease prevention and control system.

Data and methods

Study design

A mixed longitudinal and cross-sectional design was employed to evaluate the operational efficiency of CDCs across 31 provincial-level administrative regions in mainland China. Longitudinal analysis examined CDCs nationwide efficiency trends using panel data from 2011 to 2022, while cross-sectional analysis compared static efficiency across all 31 provincial-level administrative units (provinces, autonomous regions, municipalities) for the terminal observation year (2022). The sampling frame comprised a complete enumeration of CDCs across the country and different provinces, including all mainland China jurisdictions to ensure comprehensive geographic representation. This approach facilitated both temporal trend assessment and spatial efficiency comparisons.

Data source

This study systematically collected data related to resource allocation of CDCs nationwide and in 31 provincial-level administrative regions in mainland China (excluding Hong Kong, Macao and Taiwan). The dataset included input indicators, output indicators, and environmental variables, all sourced from authoritative government publications: the China Health Statistics Yearbook (2012–2023 editions) and the 2023 China Statistical Yearbook.

According to China’s geographical division, mainland China (excluding Hong Kong, Macao and Taiwan) is divided into three regions: the eastern region, the central region, and the western region. The eastern region includes Beijing, Tianjin, Hebei, Liaoning, Shanghai, Jiangsu, Zhejiang, Fujian, Shandong, Guangdong, and Hainan. The central region includes Shanxi, Jilin, Heilongjiang, Anhui, Jiangxi, Henan, Hubei, and Hunan. The western region includes Inner Mongolia, Guangxi, Chongqing, Sichuan, Guizhou, Yunnan, Tibet, Shaanxi, Gansu, Qinghai, Ningxia, and Xinjiang.

Methods

This study used DEA models to measure and analyze the operational efficiency of CDCs. DEA is an effective method for efficiency research of multi-input and multi-output systems, whose core idea is to quantitatively evaluate the relative efficiency of decision-making units by constructing an efficiency frontier [18]. This study adopted an output-oriented DEA-BCC model with variable returns to scale(VRS) to measure and analyze the operational efficiency of CDCs nationwide and in various regions. For the national panel data (2011–2022), the DEA-Malmquist model was used to decompose efficiency evolution into technical efficiency (catch-up effect) and technological progress (frontier shift effect), reflecting the dynamic changes in total factor productivity of decision-making units over different periods. For the 2022 provincial cross-sectional data, the three-stage DEA model proposed by Fried was used for static analysis of technical efficiency, which can be decomposed into pure technical efficiency and scale efficiency [19]. The model introduces environmental factors and random interference factors into traditional DEA, and strips out the impact of environmental factors on efficiency through Stochastic Frontier Analysis (SFA) regression, thereby obtaining more accurate efficiency values.

Indicators selection

Indicators selection followed the principles of systematicness, scientificity, and representativeness, prioritizing relevance to CDC functions, literature universality, and data availability. For the national-level longitudinal analysis, input indicators included the number of CDC institutions (infrastructure indicator) and the number of CDC health personnel per 10,000 population (human capital indicator). Output indicators were characterized by the prevention and control effects of national statutory infectious diseases, namely the incidence rate and mortality rate of Class A and B notifiable infectious diseases (per 100,000 population), reflecting the core responsibilities of CDCs. For the provincial cross-sectional analysis, additional indicators included per capita financial appropriation income of CDCs (financial input) and residents’ health literacy level (work output). Although the indicator scope is limited, under the constraints of existing data, it can provide continuous and standardized indicators to support longitudinal tracking and regional benchmarking.

In addition, considering the economic development, social structure, and government investment of various regions, the following were selected as environmental variables: population density (persons per square kilometer), per capita GDP (yuan), total dependency ratio (%), and government health expenditure ratio (%—i.e., the proportion of government health expenditure in total health expenditure).

Data analysis

All computations were performed using R Statistical Software (v4.3.3) with specialized packages (Benchmarking/DEA) for DEA implementation, Malmquist index calculation, and statistical testing. Spatial visualization of provincial efficiency patterns employed choropleth mapping in ArcGIS 10.8.1. Analytical procedures comprised: (1) descriptive trend analysis of input and output status (2011–2022); (2) longitudinal DEA-Malmquist for national efficiency dynamics; (3) provincial VRS output-oriented three-stage DEA analysis; (4) geographical efficiency mapping; and (5) robustness verification via sensitivity analysis examining alternative input-output specifications.

The mean values for technical efficiency change and technological progress change were 0.991 and 1.022, respectively, indicating an annual average decrease of 0.9% in technical efficiency and an increase of 2.2% in technological progress. These results demonstrate that technological progress constituted the primary driver of TFP growth in the CDC system.

Analysis of the efficiency changes across different periods revealed that the National CDCs achieved TFP growth in seven intervals: 2011–2012, 2013–2014, 2014–2015, 2015–2016, 2018–2019, 2019–2020, and 2021–2022. The most pronounced TFP increase (12.0%) occurred during 2019–2020. Conversely, TFP declined in four intervals (2012–2013, 2016–2017, 2017–2018, 2020–2021), with the maximum reduction (3.7%) observed in 2020–2021.

Furthermore, Technical efficiency increased in five intervals, peaking at 9.8% (2017–2018), but decreased in six intervals, with the most substantial decline (14.3%) recorded in 2021–2022. Technological progress advanced in eight intervals, reaching maximal growth (17.3%) in 2021–2022, while regressing in three intervals, exhibiting the greatest decline (9.3%) in 2020–2021.

Regional disparities in productivity

Analysis of the efficiency changes of CDCs in different provinces from 2011 to 2022 (Fig. 1; Table 1) revealed TFP improvements in 19 provinces (61.29%), with Beijing demonstrating maximal growth (11.3%). Conversely, 12 provinces (38.71%) exhibited reduced operational efficiency, with Inner Mongolia showing the largest decline (6.9%). The technological progress change values exceeded 1 in all 31 provinces, indicating continuous technological advancement across regional CDCs, with Shanghai achieving the highest increase (6.7%).

At the regional level, 9 provinces (29.03%) including Beijing, Tianjin, and Hebei achieved full DEA efficiency, with comprehensive efficiency, pure technical efficiency, and scale efficiency all equal to 1. Results showed that σ² and γ of each input model were highly significant at the 1% level, with γ values close to 1, indicating that external environmental factors and managerial inefficiency strongly explained input redundancy of CDCs, while the impact of random disturbance terms was negligible. This also verified the necessity and rationality of using the three-stage DEA model to eliminate environmental effects.

Regarding the direction and magnitude of influence of environmental variables: coefficients of population density and per capita GDP were both negative, indicating that higher regional economic development and population agglomeration were associated with lower input redundancy and higher resource utilization efficiency of CDCs. This may be because economically developed and densely populated regions have advantages in resource coordination, technical support, and service demand matching, which can effectively reduce resource waste. The coefficient of total dependency ratio was negative, indicating that a higher proportion of working-age population contributed to improved resource utilization efficiency. The coefficient of government health expenditure ratio was significantly positive in the CDC financial input model, indicating that an increased proportion of government health expenditure could effectively reduce financial input redundancy and improve the utilization efficiency of public health funds.

Stage 3: DEA model results based on adjusted input values

Based on SFA regression results, the adjusted input values were obtained by eliminating the impacts of environmental factors and random noise. Combined with adjusted inputs and original outputs, the DEA-BCC model was used to recalculate efficiency values of provincial CDCs (Fig. 2; Table 2). Compared with pre-adjustment, the average adjusted comprehensive efficiency was 0.811 (slightly decreased), pure technical efficiency (0.863) was basically unchanged, and scale efficiency (0.927) slightly decreased. The comprehensive efficiency values fluctuated across regions (both increased and decreased), among which 22.58% of provinces remained unchanged, 29.03% of provinces increased, and 48.39% of provinces decreased, indicating that the efficiency levels of most provinces were affected by the external environment.

At the provincial level: among originally fully efficient provinces, comprehensive efficiency and scale efficiency of Shanghai and Chongqing decreased, while pure technical efficiency remained 1, both showing increasing returns to scale, indicating that the irrationality of scale allocation became apparent after eliminating environmental effects, and efficiency could be further improved through moderate scale adjustment. Seven provinces including Beijing and Tianjin still maintained full efficiency, indicating that their efficient operation mainly stemmed from high-quality internal management and optimal scale allocation rather than environmental advantages. Guangdong and Hainan remained weakly efficient provinces (pure technical efficiency remained 1), but their comprehensive efficiency and scale efficiency further decreased, with increasing returns to scale unchanged, suggesting that the problem of insufficient scale of CDCs in these two provinces became more prominent under a homogeneous environment, and scale expansion was still the key path to efficiency improvement. Most inefficient central and western provinces showed slight fluctuations; the efficiency values of remote western provinces such as Tibet, Xinjiang, and Qinghai slightly improved but remained at a low level, indicating that the low efficiency of these provinces in Stage 1 was mainly due to harsh external environmental constraints rather than severe internal managerial deficiency. After eliminating environmental factors, their true managerial efficiency was slightly revealed, but the gap with efficient provinces was still substantial.

Discussion

This study provides evidence for the nationwide application of Data Envelopment Analysis to evaluate the operational efficiency of China’s Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. The analysis indicated an average annual growth rate of 1.3% in Total Factor Productivity during 2011–2022. Decomposition revealed that this growth was primarily attributable to technological progress (+ 2.2% annually), while technical efficiency regressed at 0.9% per year—driven by deteriorations in both pure technical efficiency (-0.7%) and scale efficiency (-0.2%). From the perspective of annual changes, TFP experienced a significant jump and then a decline between 2019 and 2021, which may be related to the COVID-19 pandemic. During the pandemic, the CDCs concentrated the human, material, and technical resources on emergency COVID-19 response, such as epidemiological investigation, surveillance, and nucleic acid testing, leading to a significant short-term increase in output. The subsequent decline resulted from the unsustainability of the emergency model and phased adjustments to routine work. This fluctuation represents a short-term emergency effect rather than a long-term improvement in operational efficiency.

Provincial-level evaluation showed that all 31 provinces achieved technological progress, but less than half (< 50%) of the provinces improved their technical efficiency. This differentiation indicates that although technological progress has significantly driven the growth of overall TFP, the decline in technical efficiency reflects systemic shortcomings in organizational management and resource utilization capabilities. Over the past 12 years, China’s disease prevention and control system has substantially upgraded information systems and technical equipment, driving technological progress. However, organizational management, cross-departmental coordination, professional staffing, and performance incentives have not improved synchronously. Ambiguous functional positioning, high personnel turnover, unstable financing, and insufficient innovation incentives have created a mismatch between advanced hardware and lagging management systems. This disconnection explains why technical efficiency deteriorated despite technological advancement and represents the core bottleneck for future efficiency improvement.

Further decomposition of provincial technical efficiency components (2011–2022) revealed that only 10 provinces (32.26%) maintained or improved both pure technical and scale efficiencies, including 4 provinces operating at full efficiency. Most provinces exhibited either asymmetric dynamics (improvement in one component alongside deterioration in the other) or concurrent declines in both dimensions. Notably, persistent deterioration was particularly acute in pure technical efficiency, which demonstrated more substantial declines than scale efficiency. This technical inefficiency aligns with findings reported in public health systems of the United States [20], Ghana [21], and Italy [14]. The decomposition further indicates that pure technical efficiency holds greater potential for improvement compared to scale efficiency—consistent with prior studies in China and Ghana identifying it as the primary constraint on CDCs performance. The observed deficit in pure technical efficiency is likely attributed to interconnected institutional constraints: ambiguous functional mandates, inadequate regulatory frameworks, critical shortages and high turnover rates among specialized personnel, unstable funding mechanisms, and insufficient incentives for technological innovation [22–24]. These systemic barriers collectively undermine the operational effectiveness of China’s disease prevention and control system.

In 2022, the average adjusted comprehensive efficiency of CDCs in all provinces nationwide was 0.811, the average pure technical efficiency was 0.863, and the average scale efficiency was 0.927. The overall efficiency still has room for improvement, and the efficiency loss is mainly due to insufficient pure technical efficiency, reflecting that China’s CDCs still need to improve in core operational capabilities such as refined internal management, standardized technological application, and scientific resource allocation.

The efficiency of CDCs shows a significant regional gradient: the average comprehensive efficiency in the eastern region is the highest (0.914), followed by the central region (0.876) and the western region (0.672). Efficient provinces are mainly concentrated in the eastern region, while the efficiency of central and western regions—especially remote provinces—is significantly low, which is highly related to regional environmental endowments such as economic development, population density, and public health investment. Notably, the efficiency of western provinces slightly improved after excluding environmental factors, indicating that their low efficiency in Stage 1 was mainly due to uncontrollable external constraints such as vast geography and scattered population, rather than pure management inefficiency. This also suggests that the evaluation of CDCs efficiency should fully consider regional environmental differences. This result is consistent with existing research on health resource allocation efficiency [25–27]. The vast territory and low population density in central and western regions restrict population-oriented service provision, while economically developed eastern provinces have more advantages in acquiring advanced equipment and retaining high-level professional talents.

From the perspective of returns to scale characteristics, most inefficient provinces exhibit decreasing returns to scale, while weakly efficient provinces such as Guangdong, Hainan, and Chongqing show increasing returns to scale, which reflects the obvious problem of scale mismatch in the allocation of CDC resources. For provinces that still have insufficient scale, moderate expansion of resource allocation and optimization of the scale structure can further release their efficiency potential. For provinces with decreasing returns to scale, priority should be given to qualitative optimization pathways including management improvement and technological upgrading, rather than simple budget or staff cuts. Particularly in staffing configuration, consideration must be given to the reserve of surge capacity, which carries critical strategic value in the response to public health emergencies and should not be simply regarded as inefficient redundancy. In crisis management scenarios, appropriate staffing reserves can rapidly enhance emergency response intensity and sustainable disposal capacity, constituting an important component of the resilience of the disease prevention and control system. Therefore, the future direction of resource optimization for these provinces should focus on quality-oriented inputs such as talent training, technology transformation, and management upgrading. In particular, support for the western region should emphasize the downward deployment of technology and talent to improve operational efficiency on the premise of safeguarding surge capacity.

Conclusions

This national evaluation of the operational efficiency of CDCs draws the following conclusions:

First, there is a significant structural imbalance in efficiency drivers. From 2011 to 2022, TFP grew at an average annual rate of 1.3%, mainly driven by technological progress (+ 2.2% annually), while technical efficiency continued to decline (-0.9% annually), indicating a disconnect between technological investment and organizational management. Poor resource utilization and declining management effectiveness have become the main constraints on system performance.

Second, there are regional disparities in CDCs efficiency, which are affected by environmental endowments. The efficiency of the eastern region is much higher than that of the central and western regions, and most DEA-efficient provinces are located in the eastern region. The low efficiency of western provinces is partly due to uncontrollable environmental factors such as terrain, population, and transportation, rather than pure management problems. Evaluation and policy formulation should be comprehensively considered to avoid unfair attribution.

Third, the problem of scale mismatch in CDCs resource allocation is prominent. The study found that most provinces show decreasing returns to scale, and the expansion of quantitative inputs has been difficult to achieve efficiency gains, while some weakly efficient provinces show increasing returns to scale, and efficiency can be improved by moderately expanding scale.

This study emphasizes that the connotation of “efficiency” in the post-pandemic disease prevention and control system has shifted from pure cost-effectiveness to a comprehensive reflection of resilience, equity, and emergency response capabilities. Future efficiency improvements should further emphasize the coordination between internal management and technological application, adopt differentiated scale optimization strategies, strictly control resource input in over-expanded provinces, and address configuration deficiencies in provinces with insufficient scale. Meanwhile, considering regional disparities, public health investment and policy support should be strengthened in western provinces to mitigate the efficiency impacts of environmental constraints. Furthermore, the collaboration of technology and talent among eastern, central, and western regions should be promoted to facilitate the high-quality and sustainable development of the disease prevention and control system.

Limitations

This study comprehensively evaluated the operational efficiency of China’s CDCs using the three-stage DEA and Malmquist index models, integrating static efficiency and dynamic productivity evolution analysis, and providing an empirical reference for resource allocation optimization. The study covers 31 provincial administrative units over 12 years (2011–2022), with strong spatial and temporal representativeness. All data are sourced from authoritative publications such as the China Health Statistics Yearbook, which are reliable and comparable across regions.

However, this study has certain limitations regarding the selection of input and output indicators. In the preliminary research, we constructed an indicator system suitable for evaluating the operational efficiency of CDCs through a systematic literature review, policy analysis, and expert consultation. However, in the empirical analysis, limited by data availability, the selection of input and output factors was relatively narrow, and only indicators with good data continuity were included, which can hardly fully reflect the actual input and output of CDCs. Given that non-communicable diseases (NCDs) constitute the majority of the global disease burden, CDCs have increasingly taken on responsibilities including chronic disease prevention and control, health risk monitoring, and health promotion. As economically developed eastern provinces invest heavily in these areas, their actual efficiency may be underestimated in the present study.

The current results can be regarded as a temporal trend analysis of the dynamic efficiency changes of CDCs at the national level and an observation of cross-regional relative efficiency comparisons at the provincial level under limited conditions. They not only reflect efficiency values but also provide partial references for the optimization and adjustment of the resource structure. In the future, with the gradual improvement of big-data platforms, more comprehensive input and output dimensions can be considered to match the development and positioning of the disease prevention and control system in the new era.

Investigation of lead-free double perovskite Cs₂CeAgCl₆ for resistive switching and optoelectronic applications
Authors: Abdul Majid, Salman Ahmed, Naila Maqbool, Noorullah Noori, Mohammad Nasir, Sana Imtiaz, F F Al-Harbi, Gafur Abdulakimov, Younas Ahmad, M Ijaz Khan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractLead halide perovskites are widely used in optoelectronic applications but are toxic due to their lead content. To explore viable, lead-free alternatives, we investigate the properties of Cs2CeAgCl6, a double perovskite halide, for its potential use in resistive switching and optoelectronic devices. Using the WIEN2K code, we computed the structural, elastic, electrical, and optical properties of this compound. Our analysis reveals that Cs2CeAgCl6 is mechanically stable and ductile, with favorable mechanical properties. The electronic band structure was calculated using the GGA + U approximation, showing direct band gaps of 3.22 eV and 3.07 eV in the spin-up and spin-down channels, respectively. These findings suggest that Cs2CeAgCl6 is a promising candidate for resistive switching in resistive random-access memory (RRAM) devices. Additionally, its wide direct bandgap and strong optical response in the ultraviolet region make it suitable for UV photodetectors, optoelectronic switches, and optical sensors. Overall, the material exhibits stable and reproducible switching behavior, making it an excellent choice for non-volatile RRAM applications.Keywords: Density functional theory, Halide perovskite, Lead-free double perovskite, Optical properties, Spin-polarized electronic structureSubject terms: Materials science, Nanoscience and technology, Optics and photonics, Physics

Introduction

Perovskites, a unique class of materials with a distinctive crystal structure, have attracted significant attention in both scientific research and industrial applications. The first discovery of the mineral CaTiO3, a member of the perovskite family, was made in 1839 by German mineralogist Gustav Rose in the Ural Mountains. This marked the beginning of extensive exploration into perovskites, which are valued for their remarkable properties. Over the years, perovskites have gained widespread interest for their potential in various industries, including1, light-emitting diodes2, lasers, and primary memory devices3.

Due to their greater photoelectric characteristics, inorganic perovskites are best for photovoltaic as well as optoelectronic devices. Their low-cost material price as well as upfront, repeatable solution/vapor-chemistry treatment are among their many advantages. In solar cell applications, they have already showed brilliant performance. Though, there are still many encounters, e.g., toxic lead limits the larger-scale application of solar cells based on lead4. Because of its exceptional photoluminescence quantum yield (PLQY), tunable band gap, and superior charge transport properties, cesium lead halide perovskite has garnered significant interest as a novel semiconductor material5.

Conventional perovskites face several challenges, particularly their intrinsic instability, which can lead to a decrease in quality and efficiency6. To address these issues, researchers have turned to double-halide perovskites, which offer improved stability and enhanced optoelectronic properties7. These materials exhibit excellent thermal and environmental stability, thanks to their more complex crystal structure, which incorporates two distinct halide ions8. Among the promising candidates, the recently discovered double-halide perovskite Cs2CeAgCl6 has attracted significant attention due to its superior properties and potential applications. Its unique crystal structure is expected to positively influence both its optoelectronic and thermoelectric performance9.

However, despite the promising advantages of RRAM devices, their development is currently hindered by the lack of suitable materials that meet the necessary performance criteria. Most conventional materials struggle to offer the required combination of stability, low power consumption, and reliable switching behavior over prolonged use. This gap in material selection limits the full potential of RRAM technology for large-scale memory applications. The introduction of Cs2CeAgCl6 into RRAM devices presents a potential breakthrough, as its unique properties could address these shortcomings. With its stable switching behavior, high mechanical strength, and low power consumption, Cs2CeAgCl6 could enable the development of RRAM devices that are not only faster and more efficient but also more scalable for future applications10–12. The material’s enhanced thermal and environmental stability further contribute to its suitability for high-performance, long-lasting memory storage solutions, making it a promising candidate for the next generation of non-volatile memory devices.

In this study, we apply first-principles DFT calculations using the WIEN2K code to investigate the structural, electronic, elastic, and optical properties of Cs2CeAgCl6, a promising lead-free double perovskite halide. Our results demonstrate that Cs2CeAgCl6 exhibits desirable mechanical stability and ductility, alongside a direct band gap of 3.22 eV and 3.07 eV in the spin-up and spin-down channels, respectively. PCEs of perovskite solar cells are significantly influenced by bandgap energy and bandgap property. Direct bandgap semiconductors make it easier for electrons to move from the valence band to the conductor band than indirect bandgap semiconductors13–16. These characteristics suggest that Cs2CeAgCl6 holds significant potential for use in resistive random-access memory (RRAM) devices, offering stable switching behavior and low power consumption. Furthermore, its strong optical response in the ultraviolet region and its suitability for flexible devices highlight its promise in optoelectronic applications, such as UV photodetectors and optoelectronic switches17,18. The findings presented in this work pave the way for further experimental validation and exploration of Cs2CeAgCl6 in both domestic and commercial applications. Several studies have looked into halide perovskites for use in electronics and light applications. As reported previously19 a lead- material, K2AgSbX6, where X can be Cl, F or I was investigated via DFT. These materials have band gaps of 1.07 to 2.23 eV and 4.29 eV which’re good for absorbing visible light and converting solar energy. Xiaoyu Wang and others20 made a lead- double perovskite film called Cs2BiAgBr6. They used it in a type of memory device that switches using resistance. There is a lot of information on how double halide perovskites are used in memory devices and electronics. For instance, Siming Tian and others21 created a memory device using Cs2SnCl6. This study shows that Cs2SnCl6 could be used in flexible memory devices and high-density storage. Based on studies we are looking into Cs2CeAgCl6 for use in resistive switching and optoelectronic applications. We want to see if it can be used in memory devices and, for converting light into energy. Cs2CeAgCl6 double halide perovskites seem promising for these applications.

Methodology

The structural, mechanical, electronic, and optical properties of Cs2CeAgCl6 were calculated from first principles using the Full-Potential Linearized Augmented Plane Wave (FP-LAPW) method within the WIEN2K code. The PBE-GGA approximation was utilized to obtain the structural parameters, as well as the elastic, optical, and thermoelectric properties of Cs2CeAgCl6. For a detailed examination of the electronic characteristics, the GGA + U approximation was applied, which considers electron-electron interactions, particularly in the spin-up and spin-down channels. The Hubbard parameter, U, the value is set by following previous work on Ce as well as Ag based perovskites that are 3 eV for Ce22 and 5 eV for Ag23 to properly capture the electronic correlation effects of these elements. To achieve accurate and converged results, a 10 × 10 × 10 Monkhorst–Pack k-point mesh grid was used across the entire Brillouin Zone, with the convergence improving as the k-point count was increased to 1000 in the first Brillouin zone. The polycrystalline mechanical properties of Cs2CeAgCl6 were computed using standard equations derived from elasticity theory, and the Voigt-Reuss-Hill (VRH) averaging method was employed to calculate the bulk, shear, and Young’s moduli, as well as the Poisson ratio. This approach was further extended to ensure convergence criteria were met for all mechanical properties, providing reliable data for the material’s potential application in various technological fields. The methodology used here is critical for understanding the material’s stability, mechanical strength, and suitability for applications in memory devices and optoelectronics.

Refractive index

One crucial physical characteristic for the description of a material, especially a semiconductor, is its refractive index. It appears that each material has its own index, which can be used to forecast how an electromagnetic wave will behave in that material36.The refractive index of examined material Cs2CeAgCl6 in the energy range of 0 eV to 14 eV has been showed in Fig. 6. The static value of refractive index n (0) for Cs2CeAgCl6 is 1.47. The value of refractive index n(ω) goes on increase as the energy increases. It attains its maximum value of 2.18 at the 6.81 eV. After achieving this maximum value refractive index gradually goes on decreasing towards its lower values. As reported previously37 Wasi Ullah et al. calculated n(ω) for Rb2TlIrF6 is 1.42 and n(ω) 1.66 for Rb2TlRhF6. They observed that these values of refractive index progressively increased until reaching their peak value within the visible range. They founded that these materials are ideal for next generation optoelectronic devices. In our calculations same pattern is observed for Cs2CeAgCl6. The higher value of refractive index for Cs2CeAgCl6 was observed in UV region. That makes it suitable for many optoelectronic devices like RRAM etc.

Conclusion

In this study, first-principles calculations were performed to investigate the structural, mechanical, electronic, and optical properties of Cs2CeAgCl6, a promising lead-free double-halide perovskite. The material exhibits a stable cubic crystal structure, with favorable mechanical properties suggesting its potential for use in flexible and high-performance devices. The GGA + U approximation reveals direct band gaps of 3.22 eV and 3.07 eV in the spin-up and spin-down channels, respectively, indicating Cs2CeAgCl6’s suitability for low-energy memory devices and optoelectronic applications. The density of states analysis indicates p-type semiconductor behavior, with the Ce atom contributing significantly to the conduction band and Cl atoms dominating the valence band. The material’s optical properties, including a maximum refractive index of 2.18 in the UV region, along with its strong absorption coefficient and energy loss function, make it well-suited for UV photodetectors and optoelectronic switches. Overall, Cs2CeAgCl6 presents a promising combination of mechanical stability, favorable electronic structure, and strong optical response, positioning it as a potential candidate for next-generation memory and optoelectronic devices, with further experimental studies needed to validate its practical applications.

Global analysis of charm mixing parameters and determination of the CKM angle γ \mathbf{\gamma}
F. Betti, M. Bona, M. Ciuchini, D. Derkach, R. Di Palma, A.L. Kagan, V. Lubicz, G. Martinelli, M. Pierini, L. Silvestrini, C. Tarantino, V. Vagnoni

Abstract
We present an updated global analysis of beauty decays sensitive to the angle γ \gamma of the Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa matrix and of D D -meson mixing data in the framework of approximate universality, in which CP violation in D − D ¯ D-\overline{D} mixing is described in terms of two universal weak phases corresponding to dispersive and absorptive contributions. We extract the fundamental theoretical parameters determining absorptive and dispersive contributions to D D meson mixing and CP violation, together with the angle γ \gamma . The results for the charm mixing parameters are x 12 ≃ x = ( 0.401 ± 0.043 ) % x_{12}\simeq x=(0.401\pm 0.043)\% and y 12 ≃ y = ( 0.610 ± 0.017 ) % y_{12}\simeq y=(0.610\pm 0.017)\% , while the two CP-violating phases are given by ϕ 2 M = ( 0.13 ± 0.70 ) ​ ° \phi_{2}^{M}=(0.13\pm 0.70)\degree and ϕ 2 Γ = ( 2.1 ± 1.6 ) ​ ° \phi_{2}^{\Gamma}=(2.1\pm 1.6)\degree . The angle γ \gamma is found to be γ = ( 65.7 ± 2.5 ) ​ ° \gamma=(65.7\pm 2.5)\degree , in excellent agreement with the indirect determination from the Unitarity Triangle analysis.

II
Charm Mixing Formalism

In this section, we present a parametrization of the quantities needed to describe charm decays and
D
mixing underlying all the observables considered in the combination.

We indicate with
M
(
\overline{M}
) a generic
B
(
\overline{B}
) or
D
(
\overline{D}
) meson, while its decay amplitudes to CP conjugate final states
f
and
\overline{f}
are denoted as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{M}^{f}=\bra{f}\mathcal{H}\ket{M},

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\overline{f}}_{M}=\bra{\overline{f}}\mathcal{H}\ket{M},

(1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{f}_{\overline{M}}=\bra{f}\mathcal{H}\ket{\overline{M}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\overline{f}}_{\overline{M}}=\bra{\overline{f}}\mathcal{H}\ket{\overline{M}},

where
\mathcal{H}
is the relevant effective Hamiltonian involved in the process.

The time evolution of a linear combination of
D^{0}
and
\overline{D^{0}}
mesons follows the Schrödinger equation, with a
2\times 2
non-Hermitian Hamiltonian
\mathbf{H}
(see
e.g.

[
127
,
128
,
129
,
130
]
) that in terms of its dispersive
\mathbf{M}
and absorptive
\mathbf{\Gamma}
components is given by

\mathbf{H}=\mathbf{M}-i/2\mathbf{\Gamma},

(2)

so that its matrix element between charm states can be written as

\langle D^{0}|\mathbf{H}|\overline{D^{0}}\rangle=H_{12}=M_{12}-i/2\Gamma_{12}.

(3)

Adopting the long-lived (L) and short-lived (S) meson notation, the Hamiltonian eigenstates read

\ket{D_{\text{L,S}}}=p|D^{0}\rangle\pm q|\overline{D^{0}}\rangle,

(4)

with
p
and
q
complex coefficients satisfying
\left|p\right|^{2}+\left|q\right|^{2}=1
. Then, the differences between masses and decay widths of the Hamiltonian eigenstates are parametrized through the usual quantities

x=\frac{m_{\text{S}}-m_{\text{L}}}{\Gamma},\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad y=\frac{\Gamma_{\text{S}}-\Gamma_{\text{L}}}{2\Gamma},

(5)

with
\Gamma
being the averaged
D^{0}
lifetime.

D
meson mixing is described in terms of the matrix elements of
\mathbf{H}
by the parameters

\phi_{12}=\arg\bigg(\frac{M_{12}}{\Gamma_{12}}\bigg),\qquad x_{12}=2\frac{|M_{12}|}{\Gamma}\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad y_{12}=\frac{|\Gamma_{12}|}{\Gamma}.

(6)

The phase
\phi_{12}
governs CP violation in pure mixing, while
x_{12}
and
y_{12}
are CP-conserving.

The CP-violating quantity
|q/p|-1
and the observables in Eq. (
5
) are related to the mixing parameters in Eq. (
6
) as

|x|=x_{12},\qquad\qquad\left|\frac{q}{p}\right|-1=\frac{x_{12}y_{12}}{x_{12}^{2}+y_{12}^{2}}\sin\phi_{12},\qquad\qquad|y|=y_{12},

(7)

up to negligible corrections quadratic in
\sin\phi_{12}
.

In the SM, it is possible to decompose the dispersive and absorptive parts of
H_{12}
in terms of U-spin amplitudes as follows
[
82
]
:

\xi_{12}^{\text{SM}}=\frac{(\lambda^{s}_{uc}-\lambda^{d}_{uc})^{2}}{4}\xi_{2}+\frac{(\lambda^{s}_{uc}-\lambda^{d}_{uc})\lambda^{b}_{uc}}{2}\xi_{1}+\frac{(\lambda^{b}_{uc})^{2}}{4}\xi_{0},\qquad\xi=M,\Gamma,

(8)

where
\lambda^{j}_{kl}=V_{kj}^{*}V_{lj}
, while
M_{0,1,2}
and
\Gamma_{0,1,2}
are the
\Delta U_{3}=0
components of the
\Delta U=0
,
1
,
2
multiplets, respectively. Thus, the order of magnitude of
\xi_{0,1,2}
is related to the U-spin breaking parameter
\varepsilon
, such that
\xi_{n}\approx\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{n})
.

In the approximate universality scenario, CP violation in the interference between decays with and without dispersive and absorptive mixing can be described through two final state-independent weak phases
\phi_{2}^{M,\Gamma}
, defined as

\phi_{2}^{\xi}=\arg\frac{\xi_{12}}{\frac{1}{4}(\lambda^{s}_{uc}-\lambda^{d}_{uc})^{2}\xi_{2}},\qquad\xi=M,\Gamma.

(9)

Furthermore, the difference between
\phi_{2}^{M}
and
\phi_{2}^{\Gamma}
is exactly
\phi_{12}
, meaning that all types of indirect CP violation concerning
D
meson mixing, namely CPV in mixing and in the interference between mixing and decay, are entirely determined by knowledge of these two universal weak phases.

Employing in the definition of Eq. (
9
) the expressions of
M_{12}^{\text{SM}}
and
\Gamma_{12}^{\text{SM}}
given in Eq. (
8
) and neglecting the third (smallest) contribution, the orders of magnitude of the two CP-violating phases in the SM can be roughly estimated to be
[
82
]

(\phi_{2}^{M})\sim(\phi_{2}^{\Gamma})\simeq\left|\frac{\lambda^{b}_{uc}}{\theta_{C}}\right|\sin\gamma\times\frac{\Gamma_{1}}{\Gamma_{2}}\sim\left|\frac{\lambda^{b}_{uc}}{\theta_{C}}\right|\sin\gamma\times\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\sim(2.2\times 10^{-3})\times\bigg(\frac{0.3}{\varepsilon}\bigg).

(10)

From Eqs. (
6
) and (
8
), we get the relation
\left|\Gamma_{2}\right|\simeq y_{12}\ \Gamma/\left|\lambda^{s}_{uc}\right|^{2}
, which can be employed in Eq. (
10
) to provide an upper bound on
\left|\phi_{2}^{\Gamma}\right|
as
[
82
]

\left|\phi_{2}^{\Gamma}\right|\simeq\frac{\left|\lambda^{b}_{uc}\lambda^{s}_{uc}\sin\gamma\right|}{y_{12}}\times\frac{\left|\Gamma_{1}\right|}{\Gamma}<(5\times 10^{-3})\times\bigg(\frac{0.61\%}{y_{12}}\bigg),

(11)

where we used the conservative assumption
\left|4\Gamma_{1}/(\Gamma_{0}-\Gamma_{2})\right|<1
, a ratio which is nominally of
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
.

For a given final state
f
, CP violation in the interference between decays with and without mixing can be equivalently described by the weak phase of the observable

\lambda^{f}_{D}\equiv\frac{q}{p}\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\overline{D}}^{f}}{\mathcal{A}_{D}^{f}},

(12)

which becomes universal in the framework of approximate universality and we denote by
\phi_{2}
. The posterior of
\phi_{2}
is determined from the charm parameters as

\tan 2\phi_{2}=-\bigg(\frac{x_{12}^{2}\sin 2\phi_{2}^{M}+y_{12}^{2}\sin 2\phi_{2}^{\Gamma}}{x_{12}^{2}\cos 2\phi_{2}^{M}+y_{12}^{2}\cos 2\phi_{2}^{\Gamma}}\bigg).

(13)

Notice that
\phi_{2}
corresponds to the phase
\phi
extracted in global analyses under the assumption of final state independence (see
e.g.
refs.
[
123
,
124
]
).

In the superweak assumption, dispersive mixing is supposed to be the dominant source of CPV in the charm system, corresponding to the approximation where

\phi_{2}^{\Gamma}=0
and
\phi_{12}=\phi_{2}^{M}
or, equivalently,
\phi=\phi_{2}=\arctan\frac{1-|q/p|^{2}}{1+|q/p|^{2}}\frac{x}{y}
. Then, going from the superweak assumption, adopted in our previous works
[
80
,
81
]
, to approximate universality, used in this work, involves moving from a description in terms of a single independent CPV parameter (
\phi_{12}
or
\phi_{2}
) to a scenario with two free CPV parameters (
\phi_{2}^{M}
and
\phi_{2}^{\Gamma}
or
\phi_{2}
and
|q/p|-1
).

III.3
Multi-body final states

The most precise observables for multi-body final states constraining together the charm mixing and CP-violating parameters come from the Dalitz plot analyses of

\displaystyle 2x_{\text{CP}}=x\cos\phi_{2}\bigg(\left|\frac{q}{p}\right|+\left|\frac{p}{q}\right|\bigg)+y\sin\phi_{2}\bigg(\left|\frac{q}{p}\right|-\left|\frac{p}{q}\right|\bigg),\ \ \ \ 2y_{\text{CP}}=y\cos\phi_{2}\bigg(\left|\frac{q}{p}\right|+\left|\frac{p}{q}\right|\bigg)-x\sin\phi_{2}\bigg(\left|\frac{q}{p}\right|-\left|\frac{p}{q}\right|\bigg),

\displaystyle 2\Delta x=x\cos\phi_{2}\bigg(\left|\frac{q}{p}\right|-\left|\frac{p}{q}\right|\bigg)+y\sin\phi_{2}\bigg(\left|\frac{q}{p}\right|+\left|\frac{p}{q}\right|\bigg),\ \ \ \ 2\Delta y=y\cos\phi_{2}\bigg(\left|\frac{q}{p}\right|-\left|\frac{p}{q}\right|\bigg)-x\sin\phi_{2}\bigg(\left|\frac{q}{p}\right|+\left|\frac{p}{q}\right|\bigg).

\displaystyle(r_{D}^{f})^{2}=\ \frac{\int\text{d}\Phi_{D}\left|\mathcal{A}_{\overline{D}}^{f}\right|^{2}}{\int\text{d}\Phi_{D}\left|\mathcal{A}_{D}^{f}\right|^{2}}=

\displaystyle\frac{\int\text{d}\Phi_{D}\left|\mathcal{A}_{D}^{\overline{f}}\right|^{2}}{\int\text{d}\Phi_{D}\left|\mathcal{A}_{\overline{D}}^{\overline{f}}\right|^{2}},

\displaystyle\kappa_{D}^{f}e^{-i\delta_{D}^{f}}=\frac{\int\text{d}\Phi_{D}\mathcal{A}_{\overline{D}}^{f}\mathcal{A}_{D}^{f*}}{\sqrt{\int\text{d}\Phi_{D}\left|\mathcal{A}_{D}^{f}\right|^{2}}\sqrt{\int\text{d}\Phi_{D}\left|\mathcal{A}_{\overline{D}}^{f}\right|^{2}}}=

\displaystyle\frac{\int\text{d}\Phi_{D}\mathcal{A}_{D}^{\overline{f}}\mathcal{A}_{\overline{D}}^{\overline{f}*}}{\sqrt{\int\text{d}\Phi_{D}\left|\mathcal{A}_{\overline{D}}^{\overline{f}}\right|^{2}}\sqrt{\int\text{d}\Phi_{D}\left|\mathcal{A}_{D}^{\overline{f}}\right|^{2}}},

The CKM angle
\gamma

B
decays to charmed mesons
f

,
D^{-}_{s}K^{+}
, and
D_{s}^{-}K^{+}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}

The decay amplitudes for these processes can be organized into ratios of magnitudes, coherence factors and strong phases through the following integrals over the phase space
\Phi_{B^{0}_{(s)}}
:

(r_{B^{0}_{(s)}}^{f})^{2}=\frac{\int\text{d}\Phi_{B^{0}_{(s)}}\left|\mathcal{A}_{\overline{B}^{0}_{(s)}}^{f}\right|^{2}}{\int\text{d}\Phi_{B^{0}_{(s)}}\left|\mathcal{A}_{B^{0}_{(s)}}^{f}\right|^{2}},\qquad\qquad\kappa_{B^{0}_{(s)}}^{f}e^{i(\delta_{B^{0}_{(s)}}^{f}+\varphi_{(s)})}=\frac{\int\text{d}\Phi_{B^{0}_{(s)}}\mathcal{A}_{\overline{B}^{0}_{(s)}}^{f}\ \mathcal{A}_{B^{0}_{(s)}}^{f*}}{\sqrt{\int\text{d}\Phi_{B^{0}_{(s)}}\left|\mathcal{A}_{\overline{B}^{0}_{(s)}}^{f}\right|^{2}}\sqrt{\int\text{d}\Phi_{B^{0}_{(s)}}\left|\mathcal{A}_{B^{0}_{(s)}}^{f}\right|^{2}}},

(42)

where
\varphi_{(s)}
are the weak phases entering the decay, which are given in the SM by a combination of the relevant CKM matrix elements.
Then, the time-dependent rates of neutral
B^{0}_{(s)}
meson decays can be written as
[
140
,
141
]

\Gamma(B^{0}_{(s)}\to f,t)\propto\cosh( t \Delta\Gamma_{(s)} /2)-G_{f}\sinh( t \Delta\Gamma_{(s)} /2)+C_{f}\cos(t \Delta m_{(s)})-S_{f}\sin(t \Delta m_{(s)}),

(43)

where
\Delta m_{(s)}
and
\Delta\Gamma_{(s)}
are the differences in masses and lifetimes between the heavier and lighter eigenstates of the
B^{0}_{(s)}
mixing Hamiltonian.

,

VI
Conclusions

We presented a Bayesian combination of charm and beauty observables to determine the CKM angle
\gamma
and the neutral
D
mixing and CP-violating parameters simultaneously in the framework of the SM and employing approximate universality for
D^{0}-\overline{D^{0}}
mixing.

The charm parameters are given by

\displaystyle x_{12}\simeq x=(0.401\pm 0.043)\%,\qquad y_{12}\simeq y=(0.610\pm 0.017)\%,\qquad\qquad\phi_{2}^{M}=(0.13\pm 0.70)\degree,\qquad\phi_{2}^{\Gamma}=(2.1\pm 1.6)\degree,

\displaystyle\phi_{2}=(-1.5\pm 1.1)\degree,\qquad\qquad\left|q/p\right|-1=(-1.6\pm 1.5)\%,

while the CKM angle
\gamma
is found to be

\gamma=(65.7\pm 2.5)\degree,

in perfect agreement with the indirect determination from the Unitary triangle analysis
\gamma_{\mathrm{UT}}=(65.6\pm 1.4)\degree

[
142
]
.
The uncertainties on the two CP-violating phases
\phi_{2}^{M,\Gamma}
are still one order of magnitude larger than what is needed to test the predictions
\phi_{2}^{M}\sim\phi_{2}^{\Gamma}\sim 0.1\degree
obtained from U-spin arguments in Eq. (
10
), and a factor of seven larger than the upper bound of
\left|\phi_{2}^{\Gamma}\right|<0.3\degree
in Eq. (
11
).
The direct CP asymmetries in
D^{0}\to K^{+}K^{-}
and
D^{0}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
decays within the framework of approximate universality are given by

a_{D}^{KK}=(4.0\pm 5.3)\cdot 10^{-4}\,,\qquad a_{D}^{\pi\pi}=(23.4\pm 6.0)\cdot 10^{-4}\,.

(46)

\gamma

separately from charged
B
,
B^{0}
and
B^{0}_{s}
meson decays.
We found three compatible estimates, given by

Appendix A
GLW Observables

The GLW observables consist of CP-conserving and CP-violating ratios of time-integrated rates of
B
cascade decays when the final states
f
are (quasi-)CP eigenstates.

Here, we report the definitions of the observables in the leftmost column of Tab.
2
and their expressions up to quadratic corrections in charm mixing and CP-violating parameters, neglecting direct CP violation.

The CP asymmetry
A_{B}^{\text{CP}}(f,h)
is defined as

A_{B}^{\text{CP}}(f,h)=\frac{\Gamma(\overline{B}\to[f]_{D}h)-\Gamma(B\to[f]_{D}\overline{h})}{\Gamma(\overline{B}\to[f]_{D}h)+\Gamma(B\to[f]_{D}\overline{h})}=\frac{2r_{B}^{Dh}\kappa_{B}^{Dh}\sin\gamma\sin\delta_{B}^{Dh}}{(1+(r_{B}^{Dh})^{2})\frac{1-(2F_{+}^{f}-1)\alpha y}{2F_{+}^{f}-1-\alpha y}+2r_{B}^{Dh}\kappa_{B}^{Dh}\cos\gamma\cos\delta_{B}^{Dh}}.

(47)

The ratio of rates for decays in which the
D
meson is reconstructed as a CP eigenstate and decays with CF final states
f^{*}
, as
K^{-}\pi^{+}(\pi^{0})
or
K^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
, is given by

R(f,f^{*},h)=\frac{\mathcal{B}(D^{0}\to f^{*})}{\mathcal{B}(D^{0}\to f)}\frac{\Gamma(\overline{B}\to[f]_{D}h)+\Gamma(B\to[f]_{D}\overline{h})}{\Gamma(\overline{B}\to[f^{*}]_{D}h)+\Gamma(B\to[\overline{f^{*}}]_{D}\overline{h})}.

(48)

In terms of charm parameters, Eq. (
48
) can be written as

R(f,f^{*},h)=\frac{(1+(r_{B}^{Dh})^{2})(1-(2F_{+}^{f}-1)\alpha y)+2r_{B}^{Dh}\kappa_{B}^{Dh}\cos\gamma\cos\delta_{B}^{Dh}((2F_{+}^{f}-1)-\alpha y)}{1+(r_{D}^{f^{*}}r_{B}^{Dh})^{2}+2r_{B}^{Dh}\kappa_{B}^{Dh}r_{D}^{f^{*}}\kappa_{D}^{f^{*}}\cos\gamma\cos( \delta_B^{Dh} - \delta_D^{f^*})+\Gamma_{\mathrm{mix}}^{+}(x,y,\gamma,f^{*},h)},

(49)

with
\Gamma^{+}_{\text{mix}}(x,y,\gamma,f^{*},h)
arising due to the mixing terms in Eq. (
38
), given by

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\mathrm{mix}}^{+}(x,y,\gamma,f^{*},h)=

\displaystyle-\alpha y\bigg[r_{D}^{f^{*}}\kappa_{D}^{f^{*}}(1+(r_{B}^{Dh})^{2})\cos\delta_{D}^{f^{*}}+r_{B}^{Dh}\kappa_{B}^{Dh}(1+(r_{D}^{f^{*}})^{2})\cos\gamma\cos\delta_{B}^{Dh}\bigg]

(50)

\displaystyle+\alpha x\bigg[r_{B}^{Dh}\kappa_{B}^{Dh}(1-(r_{D}^{f^{*}})^{2})\cos\gamma\sin\delta_{B}^{Dh}-r_{D}^{f^{*}}\kappa_{D}^{f^{*}}(1-(r_{B}^{Dh})^{2})\sin\delta_{D}^{f^{*}}\bigg].

Sometimes, it is useful to avoid the dependence on the charm branching fractions in Eq. (
48
) by measuring directly the so-called double ratio, which is related to
R(f,f^{*},h)
for two different meson states
h
and
h^{\prime}
as

R^{\text{CP}}(f,f^{*},h,h^{\prime})=\frac{R(f,f^{*},h)}{R(f,f^{*},h^{\prime})}.

(51)

Appendix B
ADS Observables

In this section, we give an overview of the ADS observables considered in the combination in Tab.
2
, expanding them in terms of the charm parameters and neglecting the non-linear terms, as well as direct CP violation. The ADS observables are defined through time-integrated rates of
B
cascade decays, involving CF/DCS decays of the
D
mesons. As in Sec.
III.1
, we refer to the CF mode of the
D^{0}
meson as
f
.

The simplest observables that can be measured are the CP conjugate suppressed over favoured ratios

R^{-}(f,h)=\frac{\Gamma(\overline{B}\to[\overline{f}]_{D}h)}{\Gamma(\overline{B}\to[f]_{D}h)},\qquad\qquad R^{+}(f,h)=\frac{\Gamma(B\to[f]_{D}\overline{h})}{\Gamma(B\to[\overline{f}]_{D}\overline{h})}.

(52)

The rates for the favoured processes are reported in Eq. (
37
), while for the suppressed modes, we have

\Gamma(\overline{B}\to[\overline{f}]_{D}h)\propto(r_{D}^{f})^{2}+(r_{B}^{Dh})^{2}+2r_{B}^{Dh}r_{D}^{f}\kappa_{B}^{Dh}\kappa_{D}^{f}\cos(\delta_B^{Dh} + \delta_D^f - \gamma)+\Gamma_{\mathrm{mix}}(-x,y,\gamma,f,h),

(53)

with
\Gamma_{\mathrm{mix}}(x,y,\gamma,f,h)
defined in Eq. (
38
). The rate for the CP conjugate mode can be obtained simply replacing
\gamma
with
-\gamma
in Eq. (
53
).

Suppressed (sup) and favoured (fav) decays can be used also to define the following asymmetries

A^{\text{sup}}(f,h)=\frac{\Gamma(\overline{B}\to[\overline{f}]_{D}h)-\Gamma(B\to[f]_{D}\overline{h})}{\Gamma(\overline{B}\to[\overline{f}]_{D}h)+\Gamma(B\to[f]_{D}\overline{h})},\qquad\qquad A^{\text{fav}}(f,h)=\frac{\Gamma(\overline{B}\to[f]_{D}h)-\Gamma(B\to[\overline{f}]_{D}\overline{h})}{\Gamma(\overline{B}\to[f]_{D}h)+\Gamma(B\to[\overline{f}]_{D}\overline{h})}.

(54)

In terms of charm parameters,
A^{\text{sup}}(f,h)
reads

A^{\text{sup}}(f,h)=\frac{2r_{D}^{f}r_{B}^{Dh}\kappa_{B}^{Dh}\kappa_{D}^{f}\sin\gamma\sin(\delta_B^{Dh} + \delta_D^f)+\Gamma_{\mathrm{mix}}^{-}(x,y,\gamma,f,h)}{(r_{B}^{Dh})^{2}+(r_{D}^{f})^{2}+2r_{D}^{f}r_{B}^{Dh}\kappa_{D}^{f}\kappa_{B}^{Dh}\cos\gamma\cos(\delta_B^{Dh} + \delta_D^f)+\Gamma_{\mathrm{mix}}^{+}(-x,y,\gamma,f,h)},

(55)

where
\Gamma_{\mathrm{mix}}^{+}(x,y,\gamma,f,h)
has been already defined in Eq. (
50
) for the GLW modes, while
\Gamma_{\mathrm{mix}}^{-}(x,y,\gamma,f,h)
is given by

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\mathrm{mix}}^{-}(x,y,\gamma,f,h)=

\displaystyle-\alpha r_{B}^{Dh}\kappa_{B}^{Dh}\sin\gamma\bigg[y\sin\delta_{B}^{Dh}(1+(r_{D}^{f})^{2})-x\cos\delta_{B}^{Dh}(1-(r_{D}^{f})^{2})\bigg].

(56)

In the same way, we find for the favoured asymmetry:

A^{\text{fav}}(f,h)=\frac{2r_{D}^{f}r_{B}^{Dh}\kappa_{B}^{Dh}\kappa_{D}^{f}\sin\gamma\sin(\delta_B^{Dh} - \delta_D^f)+\Gamma_{\mathrm{mix}}^{-}(-x,y,\gamma,f,h)}{1+(r_{D}^{f}r_{B}^{Dh})^{2}+2r_{B}^{Dh}\kappa_{B}^{Dh}r_{D}^{f}\kappa_{D}^{f}\cos\gamma\cos( \delta_B^{Dh} - \delta_D^{f})+\Gamma_{\mathrm{mix}}^{+}(x,y,\gamma,f,h)}.

(57)

Favoured and suppressed charm modes can be arranged into CP-conserving ratios as

R^{\text{ADS}}(f,h)=\frac{\Gamma(\overline{B}\to[\overline{f}]_{D}h)+\Gamma(B\to[f]_{D}\overline{h})}{\Gamma(\overline{B}\to[f]_{D}h)+\Gamma(B\to[\overline{f}]_{D}\overline{h})},

(58)

or using two different channels for beauty decays,
\overline{B}\to[f]_{D}h
and
\overline{B}\to[f]_{D}h^{\prime}
, through the following observables

R^{\text{sup}}(f,h,h^{\prime})=\frac{\Gamma(\overline{B}\to[\overline{f}]_{D}h)+\Gamma(B\to[f]_{D}\overline{h})}{\Gamma(\overline{B}\to[\overline{f}]_{D}h^{\prime})+\Gamma(B\to[f]_{D}\overline{h^{\prime}})},\qquad\qquad R^{\text{fav}}(f,h,h^{\prime})=\frac{\Gamma(\overline{B}\to[f]_{D}h)+\Gamma(B\to[\overline{f}]_{D}\overline{h})}{\Gamma(\overline{B}\to[f]_{D}h^{\prime})+\Gamma(B\to[\overline{f}]_{D}\overline{h^{\prime}})}.

(59)

The ADS ratio in Eq. (
58
) can be expressed in terms of charm parameters as

R^{\text{ADS}}(f,h)=\frac{(r_{B}^{Dh})^{2}+(r_{D}^{f})^{2}+2r_{D}^{f}r_{B}^{Dh}\kappa_{D}^{f}\kappa_{B}^{Dh}\cos\gamma\cos(\delta_B^{Dh} + \delta_D^f)+\Gamma_{\mathrm{mix}}^{+}(-x,y,\gamma,f,h)}{1+(r_{D}^{f}r_{B}^{Dh})^{2}+2r_{B}^{Dh}\kappa_{B}^{Dh}r_{D}^{f}\kappa_{D}^{f}\cos\gamma\cos( \delta_B^{Dh} - \delta_D^{f})+\Gamma_{\mathrm{mix}}^{+}(x,y,\gamma,f,h)}.

(60)

The fit equation for
R^{\text{sup}}(f,h,h^{\prime})
is given by

R^{\text{sup}}(f,h,h^{\prime})=\frac{\mathcal{B}(\overline{B}\to D^{0}h)}{\mathcal{B}(\overline{B}\to D^{0}h^{\prime})}\frac{(r_{B}^{Dh})^{2}+(r_{D}^{f})^{2}+2r_{D}^{f}r_{B}^{Dh}\kappa_{D}^{f}\kappa_{B}^{Dh}\cos\gamma\cos(\delta_B^{Dh} + \delta_D^f)+\Gamma_{\mathrm{mix}}^{+}(-x,y,\gamma,f,h)}{(r_{B}^{Dh^{\prime}})^{2}+(r_{D}^{f})^{2}+2r_{D}^{f}r_{B}^{Dh^{\prime}}\kappa_{D}^{f}\kappa_{B}^{Dh^{\prime}}\cos\gamma\cos(\delta_B^{Dh'} + \delta_D^f)+\Gamma_{\mathrm{mix}}^{+}(-x,y,\gamma,f,h^{\prime})},

(61)

while for
R^{\text{fav}}(f,h,h^{\prime})
we have

R^{\text{fav}}(f,h,h^{\prime})=\frac{\mathcal{B}(\overline{B}\to D^{0}h)}{\mathcal{B}(\overline{B}\to D^{0}h^{\prime})}\frac{1+(r_{D}^{f}r_{B}^{Dh})^{2}+2r_{B}^{Dh}\kappa_{B}^{Dh}r_{D}^{f}\kappa_{D}^{f}\cos\gamma\cos( \delta_B^{Dh} - \delta_D^{f})+\Gamma_{\mathrm{mix}}^{+}(x,y,\gamma,f,h)}{1+(r_{D}^{f}r_{B}^{Dh^{\prime}})^{2}+2r_{B}^{Dh^{\prime}}\kappa_{B}^{Dh^{\prime}}r_{D}^{f}\kappa_{D}^{f}\cos\gamma\cos( \delta_B^{Dh'} - \delta_D^{f})+\Gamma_{\mathrm{mix}}^{+}(x,y,\gamma,f,h^{\prime})}.

(62)

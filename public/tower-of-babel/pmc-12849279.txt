Development and validation of a diagnostic and screening method for gastric cancer by combining new gastric cancer screening score and serum biomarkers: a prospective study
Authors: Fen-Ying Lu, Xia Huang, Ke Zhang, Xin Yin, Yang Lv, Yue-Chen Du, Qian Zhou, Jing-Yu Min, Yu-Jie Jiao, Li-Ting Xi, Yi-Jie Gu, Jia-Ming Qiu, Ting-Wang Jiang, Cui-E Cheng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThe New Gastric Cancer Screening Score (NGCSS) has been widely applied in the screening of gastric cancer (GC). The purpose of this study is to establish a novel system for GC screening and early diagnosis by combining NGCSS with serum tumor markers and to validate its robustness.MethodsPatients with GC and non-GC diseases, including superficial gastritis (SG), atrophic gastritis (AG), and intestinal metaplasia (IM), were included in the study. They were allocated to a discovery dataset and a validation dataset in a 2:1 ratio. All individuals underwent standardized NGCSS scoring. Additionally, serum samples were obtained for tumor marker detection, including three traditional markers (CA724, CEA, and CA199) and five novel GC-related markers (OPN, TEF3, SOX3, IGFBP1, and Leptin).ResultsA total of 106 GC and 143 non-GC patients were included in this study, with 167 assigned to the discovery dataset and 82 to the validation dataset. In the discovery dataset, GC and different types of non-GC patients exhibited distinct NGCSS scores and grade levels (P < 0.01). Furthermore, compared to non-GC, GC patients showed significantly increased levels of CEA, OPN, and IGFBP1 in serum (P < 0.001). Multivariate logistic regression indicated that each marker was an independent indicator of GC incidence related to NGCSS (P < 0.001). Additionally, by selecting cutoff values for CEA, OPN, and IGFBP1, a NGCSS-CS[Changshu] screening and prediction system with a total score of 62 points was constructed. Analysis of the validation dataset revealed that NGCSS-CS had better diagnostic performance (AUC = 0.967, P < 0.001) and effectively differentiated between GC and non-GC patients (P < 0.001).ConclusionThe new model system based on NGCSS combined with multiple tumor markers is feasible and exhibits improved diagnostic performance. The conclusions of this study require further validation and elaboration through large-sample multicenter follow-up studies.Keywords: Gastric cancer, New gastric cancer screening score, Biomarkers, IGFBP1, OPN

Introduction

Gastric cancer (GC) is one of the most common malignant tumors in the digestive system and ranks as the fifth most prevalent malignancy globally, constituting the fourth leading cause of cancer-related deaths worldwide [1]. In most industrialized nations, the prognosis for GC patients is poor, with a 5-year survival rate of only 10% [2]. GC is often diagnosed in advanced stages, with limited curative options, highlighting the urgent need for research on screening strategies for GC.

Screening for cancers can facilitate early detection and potentially reduce mortality from cancers diagnosed at an early stage. In China, evidence from comprehensive pilot screening programs and various population-based screening tests has led to the development of cancer screening guidelines. For high-risk populations (including permanent residents in high-incidence areas with an age-standardized incidence rate > 20/1,000,000 for GC, Helicobacter pylori infection, chronic AG, gastric ulcer, gastric polyps, residual stomach after gastrectomy, hypertrophic gastritis, or precancerous lesions of GC such as malignant anemia, a family history of GC, smoking, alcohol consumption, unhealthy lifestyle, and dietary habits such as consumption of salted or pickled foods), a tiered management approach is implemented [3]. GC screening through gastroscopy every 1 to 3 years has proven to be an effective way to enhance screening efficiency [4]. Gastroscopy and histopathological examination of biopsy tissues are considered the “gold standard” for GC diagnosis. However, due to its painful nature, high cost, and the requirement for specific technical equipment, its acceptance among the population is low, making it unsuitable for large-scale GC screening. Furthermore, gastroscopy is an invasive procedure and is not conducted on asymptomatic patients. Thus, a non-invasive approach is necessary to forecast the development of GC before performing gastroscopy.

The New Gastric Cancer Screening Score (NGCSS) includes factors such as age, gender, serum Helicobacter pylori antibody levels, the ratio of pepsinogen I (PG I) to pepsinogen II (PG II), and gastrin-17 (G17). Based on this score, individuals at high risk are identified for further gastroscopy examination. The application of NGCSS significantly improves the detection rate of early-stage GC and saves long-term medical costs [5]. In clinical gastroenterology, identifying patients who require further gastroscopy examination is equally valuable. Our team has observed that various research groups use serum biomarkers, including functional proteins, metabolic products, and miRNA, for early GC screening [6–8]. Combining NGCSS with key early diagnostic markers for GC, it may be possible to develop a novel diagnostic approach with significant research value. CEA is identified as a tumor marker, with its levels in early GC patients showing a significant difference from those in healthy controls [9]. As a potential tumor marker, osteopontin (OPN)’s high expression inhibits early GC proliferation, migration and invasion [10]. Insulin-like Growth Factor Binding Protein 1 (IGFBP1) expression in early GC tissues is related to tumor development and may alter the tumor’s biological behavior by affecting lipid metabolism [11]. CEA, OPN and IGFBP1 show abnormal expressions at the early stage of GC, and thus have the potential for early diagnosis.

In this study, we aim to develop a diagnostic tool that combines NGCSS and key serum markers to analyze various degrees of gastritis, including superficial gastritis (SG), atrophic gastritis (AG), intestinal metaplasia (IM), and GC patients. Patients from our institution are divided into a discovery group and a validation group. The discovery group examines the ability of NGCSS and potential serum markers to differentiate between cases and controls, assigns values to each indicator based on grading, and constructs a novel diagnostic system, NGCSS-CS[Changshu]. In the validation group, the clinical performance of NGCSS-CS in diagnosing GC is verified, and the results are compared with those of NGCSS. This project aims to provide a valuable exploration for screening and early diagnosis of GC.

Materials and methods

Study population

Patients who visited the Gastroenterology Department of Changshu No.2 Peoples’ Hospital (Changshu, China) from Jan 2019 to December 2020 were selected for the study. This study was approved by the Ethics Review Committee of Changshu No.2 People’s Hospital (Approval No. 2018023), and all participants provided written informed consent. This work adheres to the principles outlined in the Helsinki Declaration. Inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) experiencing gastric discomfort with consideration for gastritis or GC diagnosis, confirmed through subsequent gastroscopy and pathological examinations; (2) willingness to cooperate with NGCSS scoring and blood sample testing; (3) informed and voluntary signing of the consent form by patients. Exclusion criteria were: (1) previous medication intervention for gastritis or GC (including proton pump inhibitors, H-receptor antagonists, gastric mucosal protectants, and anti-GC drugs), surgery, or radiotherapy; (2) presence of severe diseases in other organs; (3) diagnosis consistent with autoimmune gastritis; (4) comorbidity with psychiatric disorders; (5) severe bleeding tendency, unsuitable for biopsy; (6) incomplete medical history; (7) with diseases that could interfere with blood markers, including concurrent severe liver diseases, autoimmune disorders, and other malignancies.

To construct an effective diagnostic system, the non-GC and GC groups chosen for this project had approximately equal sample sizes. To ensure consistency between the discovery and validation sets, all included patients, based on different disease diagnoses, were randomly assigned to the discovery and validation sets in a 2:1 ratio, ensuring a similar distribution of subtypes across sets. After initial screening, 1023 patients were identified as having potential for inclusion in the study. Following the exclusion of patients with a history of surgery meeting the exclusion criteria and those who declined to participate, a total of 143 non-GC and 106 GC patients were included in this study, undergoing NGCSS scoring and relevant tumor marker measurements. Patients were stratified and randomly allocated, resulting in 167 patients in the discovery queue and 82 in the validation queue. NGCSS content, grading, and operations

The specific criteria for NGCSS scoring are as follows: (1) Age: 40–49 years old, scores 0; 50–59 years old, scores 5; 60–69 years old, scores 6; age > 69 years old, scores 10. (2) Gender: Female scores 0, male scores 4. (3) Hp infection: No Hp infection scores 0; Hp infection scores 1. (4) PGR: PGR > 3.89 scores 0, PGR < 3.89 scores 3. (5) G-17 level (pmol/L): G-17 < 1.50 scores 0; G-17 between 1.50 and 5.70 scores 3; G-17 > 5.70 scores 5. This system consists of 5 variables, with a total score range of 0–23. Based on the scores, the screening target population for GC can be divided into 3 levels: high-risk group (17–23 scores), extremely high risk of GC; intermediate-risk group (12–16 scores), a certain risk of GC; low-risk group (0–11 scores), general risk of GC. PGR calculation is completed by PG I/PG II. The scoring is independently calculated and cross-verified for accuracy by two researchers, and any discrepancies are checked against the original data.

Gastric endoscopy, endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD), and postoperative pathology

Gastric endoscopy was performed using an Olympus 290 device according to routine procedures. Prior to the examination, patients were instructed to fast and abstain from drinking, and were given thorough education. Ten minutes before the examination, a mucolytic agent (such as serrapeptase) and a defoaming agent were administered. Five minutes before the endoscopic examination, the patient was given a dose of Dakroning jelly for oral consumption. Intravenous sedation or anesthesia was used if necessary to improve the patient’s acceptance of the endoscopic examination. Indigo carmine dye was used for chromoendoscopy at a concentration of 0.2–0.4% to observe subtle changes in the surface structures of gastric mucosal glands and microvascular network morphology in combination with magnification and electronic chromoendoscopy techniques.

All patients underwent ESD or conventional surgery, and obtained final pathological results. Immunohistochemistry was performed on some patients. The steps of the ESD procedure were as follows: (1) Marking: Use a dual knife to make marks 5 mm outside the lesion with an ERBE20 index. (2) Injection: Inject a solution of 1:1,000,000 adrenaline methylene blue glycerol into the mucosal and submucosal layers to lift the lesion. (3) Dissection: Use an IT knife with an ERBE80 index to dissect the lesion, and repeatedly inject submucosally during the dissection process while maintaining clear vision with ice saline flushing. (4) Hemostasis: Clip the wound with hemostatic clips, and use hemostatic forceps with electrocoagulation for exposed blood vessels.

Immediately after the surgery, the specimen was unfolded, fixed on a foam plastic board with large-headed pins, and the size, shape, and distinction between proximal and distal ends of the lesion were measured. After confirming the integrity of the lesion tissue, photographs were taken and the specimen was fixed overnight with diluted formalin. The tissue was then cut into continuous strips at intervals of 2 mm, numbered, and subjected to complete embedding and sectioning for pathological examination. The pathological report included the location and type of the lesion, depth of infiltration, and observation of tumor tissue at the bottom and margins. The diagnosis of gastritis and GC patients in this study was confirmed by two or more pathologists.

Separation of serum samples and analysis of indicators

Patients fasted for more than 8 h, and 5 ml of peripheral venous blood was extracted, centrifuged to separate the serum, and stored at −80 °C. PG I, PG II, and G-17 were measured using the Stomach Function Product from Biouhan Biotech Inc (Hefei, China). Hp was detected using the Helicobacter pylori urine enzyme antibody test kit (produced by Beijing Kangmei Tianhong Biotechnology Co., Ltd.) using the colloidal gold method, following the instructions. CA199 and CA724 were detected using the Roche Cobas E411 electrochemiluminescence immunoassay analyzer with matching reagent kits. CEA was measured using an ELISA kit provided by Nanjing Senbeiya Biotechnology Co., Ltd., following the instructions. OPN, IGFBP-1, and Trefoil factor 3 (TEF3) were measured using ELISA kits from R&D company. ELISA test kits for SRY-box transcription factor 3 (SOX3) and leptin were purchased from Thermo Fisher Scientific (CA, USA).

Statistical analysis

All data were processed using SPSS 19.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) and GraphPad Prism 9.3 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA). Quantitative data are presented as (x ± s), and differences between multiple groups were analyzed using analysis of variance (ANOVA), with post hoc tests conducted using the Student-Newman-Keuls method. Categorical variables were analyzed using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test.

In the discovery cohort, the differences in demographic, clinical characteristics, and serum markers among different degrees of gastritis and GC were first identified. Furthermore, multivariable logistic regression analysis was performed to evaluate the independent tumor markers unrelated to the Neutrophil and Glasgow Score System (NGCSS). Diagnostic performance analysis of novel tumor markers was conducted using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), and the optimal cutoff values were selected for subsequent analysis. After confirming the cutoff values for each parameter, the parameters were categorized into graded classifications. NGCSS was divided into three grades, while the remaining serum markers were divided into two categories. Multivariable logistic regression was then performed, and the discriminative ability of each marker was evaluated based on the slope to assign scores. A new scoring system, NGCSS-CS, was constructed based on NGCSS and the selected serum tumor markers.

In the validation cohort, the diagnostic performance of the diagnostic model and scoring system NGCSS-CS constructed using the discovery cohort were evaluated, as well as their value in distinguishing between GC and non-GC diseases.

In this study, a significant level of P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Construction and content of NGCSS-CS

Among the candidate NGCSS indicators, CEA was used as a clinical indicator, with < 5 ng/ml considered normal for non-smokers and < 10 ng/ml considered normal for smokers. For OPN and IGFBP1, optimal diagnostic thresholds were selected for further analysis, with OPN < 5.36 ng/ml and IGFBP1 < 1.925 µg/ml. Each serum tumor marker was analyzed with a value of 0 for normal and 1 for abnormal. Considering the current NGCSS classification of low-risk, middle-risk, and high-risk (with a difference of 6 points between middle-risk and high-risk), low-risk was assigned a score of 0, middle-risk a score of 1, and high-risk a score of 2. Logistic regression analysis revealed weightings of 1.58, 3.12, 2.07, and 1.11 for CEA, OPN, IGFBP1, and NGCSS level (Table 4), respectively.

Discussion

The screening and early diagnosis of GC have important clinical value and are the focus and difficulty of clinical diagnosis and treatment, which is also closely related to the national health policy. In addition to NGCSS, other GC screening systems are also used in clinical practice, such as Japanese scoring [12]. Furthermore, serum-based tumor early screening has sparked a trend in in vitro diagnostics and has become a hot topic in tumor research in recent years [13, 14]. Therefore, it is a valuable exploration in this field to evaluate the effect of combining previous screening systems and GC-related tumor markers reported in the literature on GC diagnosis. The results of this study showed that NGCSS is a powerful GC screening system, and the newly constructed NGCSS-GC, when combined with the analyzed serum tumor markers CEA, OPN, and IGFBP1, has even stronger diagnostic performance, with ROC analysis exceeding 0.95 in two independent cohorts, thus having broad prospects for early diagnosis and screening. To our knowledge, this is the first study to combine NGCSS with multiple serum tumor markers, which not only validates the diagnostic and screening efficacy of NGCSS but also proposes new potential optimization strategies for further research.

The China Gastrointestinal Early Cancer Prevention and Treatment Alliance (GECA) conducted a nationwide multicenter study in 2018 and published the research results, identifying the important value of a screening system with 7 variables, including age, gender, PG I/II ratio, G-17 level, HP infection, and pickled/fried food, in GC management [15]. Subsequently, an updated NGCSS selected a 5-variable system with a total score of 23 and has been widely used in GC screening and clinical practice [16]. Compared to Japanese scoring, NGCSS has higher sensitivity and is a powerful tool that is consistent with the characteristics of the Chinese population [12]. In addition, different research teams have conducted many beneficial explorations. Jiang et al. constructed a differential gene expression profile related to early GC diagnosis using cancer genomics atlas (TCGA) and gene expression omnibus (GEO) databases and used it for GC prognosis analysis [17]. Furthermore, a new prognostic staging system for GC based on positron emission tomography/computed tomography (PET/CT) can predict patients’ disease-specific survival [18]. Considering the powerful function of NGCSS, a novel diagnostic system combining it with other tumor markers undoubtedly has important research value.

In the discovery dataset, this study selected 3 traditional gastrointestinal tumor markers (CA724, CEA, and CA199) and 5 novel GC-related tumor markers (OPN, TEF3, SOX3, IGFBP1, Leptin). Traditional markers are commonly used clinical indicators, while the novel GC-related markers are derived from previous studies [19–21]. After comparing serum samples from non-GC and GC patients, CEA, OPN, and IGFBP1 showed statistically significant differences among various patient groups and were not correlated with NGCSS scores. Therefore, these markers have important value in supplementing GC diagnosis.

CEA is a protein that is usually present in normal fetal tissues but is typically only present in very small amounts or undetectable in adults. As a traditional gastrointestinal tumor marker, a recent study showed that CEA levels were significantly higher in GC patients compared to the control group, and its diagnostic performance was improved when combined with other indicators [22]. In addition, as widely used serum markers in clinics, serum CEA was found to be closely related to GC treatment response, postoperative complications, and prognosis [23, 24]. In this study, serum CEA levels were significantly elevated in GC patients compared to non-GC patients. Furthermore, the combination of CEA with NGCSS scores in this project is an in-depth exploration of previous knowledge and can be analyzed for its relationship with more retrospective data.

OPN expression is increased in various malignant tumor tissues, closely related to the invasion and metastasis of tumor cells [25, 26]. Previous studies have analyzed the relationship between tissue expression and prognosis of diseases using immunohistochemistry methods. In addition, circulating OPN as a tumor marker also has significant value in the diagnosis and prognosis of multiple myeloma, non-small cell lung cancer, and metastatic breast cancer [27–29]. Furthermore, a study of 42 consecutive late-stage GC patients and 29 healthy controls found that serum OPN is a novel important prognostic and predictive factor for late-stage GC chemotherapy [19]. Previous systematic reviews have shown that OPN overexpression is related to poor overall survival and clinical characteristics of GC patients, reflecting high invasiveness [30]. The results of previous studies are consistent with the conclusions of this study, and incorporating OPN into a new predictive system has important value in improving the system.

IGFBP-1 is an insulin-like growth factor binding protein with effects on promoting cell division and inhibiting cell apoptosis. It is mainly produced by the liver, regulating cell proliferation, differentiation, and participating in various tumors [31]. The IGFBP family has been used as a diagnostic marker for GC and has been the focus of several studies, with various bioinformatics analyses finding a relationship between IGFBP1 and the prognosis of GC [32]. This study found that IGFBP1 is significantly upregulated in GC, and has a significant synergistic effect with the previously used NGCSS score, providing a new approach for GC screening.

This project has several advantages. First, sample grouping was performed for key target discovery, model construction, and model validation, which is persuasive. Second, the markers of interest are key proteins in the serum, with the potential for subsequent unified expansion of sample size for detection. In addition to diagnostic performance, the functional proteins of interest have potential regulatory roles, which deserve further in vitro and in vivo functional analysis. This study is the first to combine NGCSS with multiple novel serum markers for GC screening, and has constructed and validated a new scoring system, NGCSS-CS. The new scoring system, NGCSS-CS, has been verified to have superior diagnostic performance over the traditional NGCSS in two independent cohorts, providing a non-invasive, cost-effective, and scalable screening strategy. However, this project also has several limitations. First, it is a single-center study, with potential selection bias in patient selection and small sample size. Although a classification of discovery and validation groups was conducted, the related data are still internal data from the same source, and the test power is lower than that of independent external data. Therefore, the research team plans to collaborate with other project teams to expand the sample size for further analysis. Second, CEA, OPN, and IGFBP1 may show elevated levels in specific populations including those with liver cirrhosis, chronic inflammation, smoking, and metabolic syndrome. There is a risk of false positives, which may affect the accuracy of the NGCSS-CS. NGCSS-CS is only used as a preliminary screening tool. For those with abnormal scoring results, further examinations such as gastroscopy and imaging studies are still necessary for a definitive diagnosis. Finally, this project did not include tumor grading and follow-up of disease, which is also a research direction that the research team will explore in the future.

Conclusions

In summary, NGCSS combined with multiple serum markers can serve as an efficient means for non-invasive diagnosis and screening of GC, and its performance is significantly better than that of NGCSS alone. This project provides ideas for in-depth research based on NGCSS markers, enriches its significant value as a screening system, and expands its role in the field of early diagnosis. However, this project is a single-center small sample study, and further research with larger samples from different regions and countries is needed to validate its usability in GC screening and early diagnosis. As a screening tool, NCGSS-CS does not replace NCGSS but complements it. When the results of NCGSS and NCGSS-CS are inconsistent, the higher-risk-level result should be given priority and the patient should be advised to undergo gastroscopy. In the future, a prospective study will be conducted to further verify the optimal strategy under different risk combinations and explore whether a “gray zone” management path needs to be established.

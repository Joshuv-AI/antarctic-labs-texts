Surgery and HIPEC in relapse for all patients with ovarian cancer?
Authors: SP Hasson, I Ray-Coquard, C Marth, P Harter
Published: 2023

Click here to listen to the Podcast

Ovarian cancer is the second most lethal gynaecological malignancy worldwide and the first in developed countries.1 Unfortunately, recurrent disease develops in >80% of patients, with only a minority having resectable disease. Studies evaluating the role of surgery in the first relapse have demonstrated improved progression-free survival and overall survival when compared to chemotherapy alone.2, 3, 4, 5 The use of Hyperthermic Intraperitoneal Chemotherapy (HIPEC) in ovarian cancer treatment is a topic of ongoing debate and investigation with conflicting findings reported in various publications.6, 7, 8 HIPEC therefore cannot be regarded as a standard of care in first-line nor recurrent ovarian cancer. Recently, the results of the CHIPOR multicentric randomized phase III trial were presented.9 Patients underwent six cycles of chemotherapy, with or without bevacizumab. Those suitable for complete cytoreductive surgery were randomly assigned to receive HIPEC or not. The results indicated a significant improvement in overall survival with HIPEC [54.3 months (95% CI 41.9-61.7 months) versus 45.8 months (95% CI 39.9-54.2 months), P = 0.02]. The increase in peritoneal and overall progression-free survival was, however, <1 month, with a difference of four events. In light of the current available data, the addition of HIPEC should be further investigated in an area of novel targeted therapies and should not be regarded as a standard of care supported by level 1 evidence.

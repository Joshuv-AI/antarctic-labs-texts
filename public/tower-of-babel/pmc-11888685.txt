The Efficacy of Benson Relaxation for Managing Psychological Distress in Primipostpartum Women: A Systematic Review
Authors: Munusamy Lakshmi, Raju Naganandini
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:The Benson Relaxation Technique, a mind-body approach, has been proposed as an effective intervention for prime-postnatal mothers to alleviate stress, anxiety, and depression.Objective:The study aims to comprehensively analyze and synthesize the existing literature on the influence of the Benson Relaxation Technique on psychological factors such as stress, anxiety, and depression among primi-postnatal mothers.Methods:A literature search spanning 2013-2024 analyzed studies on Benson Relaxation Technique’s impact on psychological parameters in primi-postnatal mothers, assessing changes in these parameters through validated scales.Results:The Benson Relaxation Technique significantly reduced stress, anxiety, and depression in primi-postnatal mothers, with some studies showing moderate to large effects.Conclusion:The Benson Relaxation Technique, a potentially effective intervention for reducing stress and anxiety in postnatal mothers, warrants further research for its long-term effects and mechanisms.

INTRODUCTION

Postpartum, or puerperium, is a crucial phase for mothers, involving physical, emotional, and psychological adjustments, causing stress, anxiety, and depression, impacting mother-infant bonding and family dynamics.[1]

The Benson Relaxation Technique (BRT), a non-pharmacological intervention for postpartum mental health, has gained attention for its simplicity and potential efficacy.[2]

Studies show relaxation techniques, specifically deep breathing (BRT), can reduce anxiety and depression in postpartum women by lowering cortisol levels and increasing parasympathetic nervous system activity, thereby promoting relaxation and recovery.[3]

This systematic review aims to assess the Benson Relaxation Technique’s impact on stress, anxiety, and depression in primi-postnatal mothers, highlighting the need to understand its efficacy in this demographic, as they may have different experiences.[4]

Rationale for review

Postpartum mothers experience emotional and psychological changes, including stress, anxiety, and depression, impacting their well-being and mother-infant relationship, necessitating effective interventions to promote mental health.[5]

Postpartum depression and anxiety management is influenced by factors like breastfeeding and personal preferences, prompting a growing interest in non-pharmacological interventions like Benson Relaxation Technique (BRT).[6]

This systematic review evaluates the Benson Relaxation Technique (BRT) for primi-postnatal mothers, focusing on its impact on stress, anxiety, depression, and overall psychological well-being. The results will inform clinical practice, guiding healthcare providers in recommending appropriate interventions and promoting comprehensive maternal mental health strategies.

Studies on Benson Relaxation Therapy for Postpartum and Pregnant Women

Raj and Pillai’s 2021 study found that Benson’s relaxation therapy significantly improved postpartum pain and stress scores in 60 mothers, with a significant difference in pretest and post-test scores compared to the control group.[7]

Rinhring and Chanu’s 2021 study found a significant improvement in anxiety levels among antenatal mothers with high-risk pregnancies after implementing Benson’s Relaxation Therapy.[8]

Solehati and Rustina’s 2015 study found that the Benson Relaxation Technique significantly reduced pain intensity in women post-cesarean section, with the experimental group (IG) showing more significant pain reduction than the control group (CG).[9]

Abu Maloh et al.’s[10] 2022 study found that Benson’s Relaxation Technique significantly reduced pain and perceived stress in hemodialysis patients.

Solomon et al.’s study found significant differences in nausea and vomiting in primigravida women during the first trimester of pregnancy compared to control groups.[11]

Abarghoee et al.’s[12] 2022 study revealed that the Benson Relaxation Technique and Music Therapy significantly reduced anxiety in primiparous women before cesarean section.

DISCUSSION

The Benson Relaxation Technique (BRT) is a promising non-pharmacological intervention for managing postpartum discomforts in primi-postnatal mothers, reducing stress, anxiety, and pain in various settings.

Soumya Raj and Pillai (2021)[7] and Rinhring and D Chanu (2021)[8] Studies show BRT effectively reduces stress and anxiety levels in postpartum and antenatal mothers, particularly for primi-postnatal mothers experiencing heightened anxiety and stress due to new responsibilities.

Abu Maloh et al.[10] (2022) Studies show that BRT significantly reduces pain intensity in postcesarean mothers, enhancing recovery and quality of life, thereby providing sustained relief from discomfort.

Saad Soliman et al.’s[11] study demonstrates BRT’s effectiveness in reducing nausea and vomiting symptoms during early pregnancy, highlighting its versatility as a supportive intervention in maternal health care.

Abarghoee et al.[12] (2022) found that BRT significantly reduces preoperative anxiety in primiparous women, demonstrating its effectiveness in managing acute stress and anxiety for medical procedures.

CONCLUSION

The Benson Relaxation Technique (BRT) is a promising non-pharmacological intervention for managing postpartum symptoms in primi-postnatal mothers, showing consistent reductions in stress, anxiety, and pain. Further research is needed to fully understand its benefits and clinical application. The systematic review of published studies on BRT has limitations, including small sample sizes and heterogeneity, and suggests future research should focus on larger, randomized controlled trials.

Introduction

Denote by
{\mathbb{T}}^{m}
the
m
-dimensional torus
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}/{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
and by
g_{1}
the flat metric of volume
1
. In terms of angular coordinates
\vec{\theta}=(\theta^{1},\dotsc,\vec{\theta}^{m})
,
\theta^{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}\bmod{\mathbb{Z}}
,

g_{1}=(d\theta^{1})^{2}+\cdots+(d\theta^{m})^{2}.

For
R>0
, meant to be large, we denote by
\Delta_{R}
the Laplacian of the metric
g_{R}=R^{2}g_{1}
. Observe that

\vol\big[\,M,g_{R}\,\big]=R^{m}\vol\big[\,M,g_{1}\,\big]=R^{m},\;\;\Delta_{R}=R^{-2}\Delta_{1}.

A complete orthonormal system of
complex
eigenfunctions of
\Delta_{1}
is given by
\big(\,e_{\vec{\ell}}\,\big)_{{\vec{\ell}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}}

e_{\vec{\ell}}\big(\,\vec{\theta}\,\big)=e^{2\pi\boldsymbol{i}\langle{\vec{\ell}},\vec{\theta}\rangle},\;\;\langle{\vec{\ell}},\vec{\theta}\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\ell_{j}\theta^{j}.

To describe a complete orthonormal system of
real
eigenfunctions of
\Delta_{1}
we introduce the lexicografic order on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
,
{\vec{\ell}}\succ 0
iff
\exists i_{0}:\;\;\ell_{i_{0}}>0,\;\;\ell_{i}=0,\;\;\forall i<i_{0}
. We set
u_{0}=1
and, for
{\vec{\ell}}\succ 0
we set

u_{\vec{k}}\big(\,\vec{\theta}\,\big)=\sqrt{2}\cos\big(\,2\pi\langle{\vec{k}},\vec{\theta}\rangle\,\big),\;\;v_{\vec{\ell}}\big(\,\vec{\theta}\,\big)=\sqrt{2}\sin\big(\,2\pi\langle{\vec{\ell}},\vec{\theta}\rangle\,\big),

u^{R}_{\vec{k}}=R^{-m/2}u_{\vec{k}},\;\;v_{\vec{\ell}}^{R}=R^{-m/2}v_{\vec{\ell}}.

The collection

\big\{\,u^{R}_{\vec{k}},\;v^{R}_{\vec{\ell}};\;{\vec{k}}\succeq 0,\;\;{\vec{\ell}}\succ 0\,\big\}

is a complete
L^{2}(M,g_{R})
-orthonormal system of real eigenfunctions of
\Delta_{R}
. Moreover

\Delta_{R}u^{R}_{\vec{k}}=\lambda_{\vec{k}}(R),\;\;\Delta_{R}v^{R}_{\vec{\ell}}=\lambda_{\vec{\ell}}(R)v^{R}_{\vec{\ell}},\;\;\lambda_{\vec{k}}(R)=R^{-2}\big|\,2\pi{\vec{k}}\,\big|^{2}=\big(\,2\pi/R\,\big)^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{m}k_{j}^{2}.

Fix an even Schwarz function
\mathfrak{a}\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}})
such that
\mathfrak{a}(0)=1
. We will refer to such an
\mathfrak{a}
as
amplitude
. Fix independent standard normal random variables

\big\{\,A_{\vec{k}},\;B_{\vec{\ell}};\;{\vec{k}}\succeq 0,\;\;{\vec{\ell}}\succ 0\,\big\}

and consider the random Fourier series

\begin{split}F^{R}_{\mathfrak{a}}(\vec{\theta})=\mathfrak{a}(0)A_{0}u^{R}_{0}\big(\,\vec{\theta}\,\big)+\sum_{{\vec{\ell}}\succ 0}\mathfrak{a}\big(\,\lambda_{\vec{\ell}}(R)^{1/2}\,\big)\big(\,A_{\vec{\ell}}u_{\vec{\ell}}^{R}\big(\,\vec{\theta}\,\big)+B_{\vec{\ell}}v^{R}_{\vec{\ell}}\big(\,\vec{\theta}\,\big)\,\big)\\
=R^{-m/2}\Big(\,A_{0}u_{0}\big(\,\vec{\theta}\,\big)+\sum_{{\vec{\ell}}\succ 0}\mathfrak{a}\big(\,|2\pi{\vec{\ell}}|/R\,\big)\big(\,A_{\vec{\ell}}u_{\vec{\ell}}\big(\,\vec{\theta}\,\big)+B_{\vec{\ell}}v\big(\,\vec{\theta}\,\big)\,\big)\,\big)\\
=R^{-m/2}\sum_{{\vec{\ell}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}}\mathfrak{a}\big(\,|2\pi{\vec{\ell}}|/R\,\big)Z_{\vec{\ell}}e_{\vec{\ell}}(\vec{\theta}),\hskip 113.81102pt\end{split}

(1.1)

where

Z_{\vec{\ell}}=\begin{cases}A_{0},&{\vec{\ell}}=0,\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\big(\,A_{\vec{\ell}}-\boldsymbol{i}B_{\vec{\ell}}\,\big),&{\vec{\ell}}\succ 0,\\
\bar{Z}_{-{\vec{\ell}}},&{\vec{\ell}}\prec 0.\end{cases}

Since
\mathfrak{a}\big(\,|2\pi{\vec{\ell}}|/R\,\big)
decays very fast as
|{\vec{\ell}}|\to\infty
we deduce from Kolmogorov’s two-series theorem that for any
\nu\in{{\mathbb{N}}}
the random series

\sum_{{\vec{\ell}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}}\mathfrak{a}\big(\,|2\pi{\vec{\ell}}|/R\,\big)^{2}\,\|\,e_{\vec{\ell}}\,\|^{2}_{C^{\nu}({\mathbb{T}}^{m})}

converges a.s. and thus the series

\sum_{{\vec{\ell}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}}\mathfrak{a}\big(\,|2\pi{\vec{\ell}}|/R\,\big)Z_{\vec{\ell}}e_{\vec{\ell}}

converges a.s. in
C^{\nu}\big(\,{\mathbb{T}}^{m}\,\big)
. In particular, this shows that the Gaussian function
F^{R}_{\mathfrak{a}}
is a.s. smooth. Its covariance kernel is

\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{R}\big(\,\vec{\varphi}+{\vec{\tau}},\vec{\varphi}\,\big)=C^{R}_{\mathfrak{a}}({\vec{\tau}})=R^{-m}\sum_{{\vec{\ell}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}}\mathfrak{a}\big(\,|2\pi{\vec{\ell}}|/R\,\big)^{2}e^{2\pi\boldsymbol{i}\langle{\vec{\ell}},{\vec{\tau}}\rangle}.

Define

w_{\mathfrak{a}}:{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\to{\mathbb{R}},\;\;w_{\mathfrak{a}}\big(\,\xi\,\big)=\mathfrak{a}\big(\,|\xi|\,\big)^{2},\;\;|\xi|^{2}:=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\xi_{j}^{2}.

Its Fourier transform is

\widehat{w}_{\mathfrak{a}}({\boldsymbol{x}})=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}e^{\boldsymbol{i}\langle\xi,{\boldsymbol{x}}\rangle}w_{\mathfrak{a}}(\xi)d\xi.

The Fourier inversion formula shows that

\mathfrak{a}\big(\,|\xi|^{2}\,\big)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}e^{\boldsymbol{i}\langle\xi,{\boldsymbol{x}}\rangle}K_{\mathfrak{a}}({\boldsymbol{x}})d{\boldsymbol{x}},\;\;{\boldsymbol{K}}_{\mathfrak{a}}({\boldsymbol{x}}):=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{m}}\widehat{w}_{\mathfrak{a}}({\boldsymbol{x}}).

Using Poisson’s summation formula
[
15
, §7.2]
we deduce

C_{\mathfrak{a}}^{R}({\vec{\tau}})=\sum_{{\vec{k}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}}{\boldsymbol{K}}_{\mathfrak{a}}\big(\,({\vec{k}}-{\vec{\tau}})R\,\big),\;\;{\vec{\tau}}=\vec{\theta}-\vec{\varphi}.

(1.2)

We can think of
F^{R}_{\mathfrak{a}}
either as a function on
{\mathbb{T}}^{m}
, or as a
{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
-periodic function of
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
. If we formally let
R\to\infty
in the equality

R^{m/2}F_{\mathfrak{a}}^{R}(\vec{\theta})=\sum_{{\vec{\ell}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}}\mathfrak{a}\big(\,|2\pi{\vec{\ell}}|/R\,\big)Z_{\vec{\ell}}e_{\vec{\ell}}(\vec{\theta})

we deduce

W_{\infty}(\vec{\theta})``="\lim_{R\to\infty}R^{m/2}F_{\mathfrak{a}}^{R}(\vec{\theta})=\sum_{{\vec{\ell}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}}Z_{\vec{\ell}}e_{\vec{\ell}}(\vec{\theta}).

The series on the right-hand-side is
\mathrm{a.s.}
divergent but we can still assign a meaning to
W_{\infty}
as a random generalized function, i.e., a random linear functional

C^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{m})\to{\mathbb{R}},\;\;W_{\infty}(f)=\sum_{{\vec{\ell}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}}Z_{\vec{\ell}}\big(\,f,e_{\vec{\ell}}(\vec{\theta})\,\big)_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{m})}.

A simple computation shows that for any functions
f_{0},f_{1}\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{m})

\cov\big[\,W_{\infty}(f_{0}),W_{\infty}(f_{1})\,\big]=\sum_{{\vec{\ell}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}}\big(\,f_{0},e_{\vec{\ell}}\,\big)_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{m},g_{1})}\big(\,f_{1},e_{\vec{\ell}}\,\big)_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{m},g_{1})}=\big(\,f_{0},f_{1}\,\big)_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{m},g_{1})}.

The last equality shows that
W_{\infty}
is the Gaussian white noise on
{\mathbb{T}}^{m}
driven by the volume measure
\vol_{g_{1}}
; see
[
13
]
. In other words, one could think of the family
\big(\,W_{R}=R^{m/2}F^{R}_{\mathfrak{a}}\,\big)_{R>0}
as a white noise approximation. Note that
W_{\mathfrak{a}}^{R}=\mathfrak{a}\big(\,R^{-1}\sqrt{\Delta}\,\big)W_{\infty}
. In the special case
\mathfrak{a}(x)=e^{-x^{2}}
,
t=R^{-2}
, we have
\mathfrak{a}\big(\,R^{-1}\sqrt{\Delta}\,\big)=e^{-t\Delta}
, the heat operator.

The main goal of this paper is to investigate the distribution on
{\mathbb{T}}^{m}
of the critical points of
F_{\mathfrak{a}}^{R}
in the white noise limit,
R\to\infty
.

With this in mind, we consider the rescaled function

\Phi^{R}_{\mathfrak{a}}\big(\,{\boldsymbol{x}}\,\big):=F^{R}_{\mathfrak{a}}\big(\,{\boldsymbol{x}}/R\,\big)=R^{-m/2}\sum_{{\vec{\ell}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}}\mathfrak{a}\big(\,|2\pi{\vec{\ell}}|/R\,\big)Z_{\vec{\ell}}{e^{2\pi\boldsymbol{i}\langle{\vec{\ell}},R^{-1}{\boldsymbol{x}}\rangle}}.

We denote by
{\mathcal{K}}^{R}_{\mathfrak{a}}
the covariance kernel of
\Phi_{\mathfrak{a}}^{R}
. Then
{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{R}({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})={{\boldsymbol{K}}}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{R}({\boldsymbol{x}}-{\boldsymbol{y}})
, where

{{\boldsymbol{K}}}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{R}({\boldsymbol{z}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{heat_ker_torus_w})}}{{=}}\sum_{{\vec{k}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}}{\boldsymbol{K}}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R{\vec{k}}-{\boldsymbol{z}})=\sum_{{\boldsymbol{t}}\in(R{\mathbb{Z}})^{m}}{\boldsymbol{K}}_{\mathfrak{a}}({\boldsymbol{t}}-{\boldsymbol{z}}).

(1.3)

Since
{\boldsymbol{K}}_{\mathfrak{a}}
is a Schwartz function we deduce that

\lim_{R\nearrow\infty}{{\boldsymbol{K}}}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{R}={\boldsymbol{K}}_{\mathfrak{a}}\;\;\mbox{ in $C^{k}\big(\,{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\,\big)$, $\forall k\in{{\mathbb{N}}}$}.

(1.4)

The function
{\boldsymbol{K}}_{\mathfrak{a}}({\boldsymbol{x}}-{\boldsymbol{y}})
is the covariance kernel of an isotropic Gaussian function
\Phi_{\mathfrak{a}}
. The equality (
1.4
) suggests that
\Phi_{\mathfrak{a}}^{R}
approximates
\Phi_{\mathfrak{a}}
for
R>>0
.

Suppose that
G:{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a Gaussian

-function such that
\nabla G({\boldsymbol{x}})
is a nondegenerate Gaussian vector for any
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
. Then
G
is
\mathrm{a.s.}
Morse (see Corollary

). Define

\mathfrak{C}\big[\,-,G\,\big]=\sum_{\nabla G({\boldsymbol{x}})=0}\delta_{\boldsymbol{x}}.

This is a locally finite random measure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
in the sense of
[
8
]
or
[
17
]
. Thus, for every Borel subset
S\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
,
\mathfrak{C}[S,G]
is the number of critical points of

in
S
. More generally, for any measurable function
{\varphi}:{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\to[0,\infty)
, we set

\mathfrak{C}[{\varphi},G]:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}{\varphi}({\boldsymbol{x}})\mathfrak{C}[d{\boldsymbol{x}},G]=\sum_{\nabla G({\boldsymbol{x}})=0}{\varphi}({\boldsymbol{x}})\in[0,\infty].

Clearly,
\nabla F_{\mathfrak{a}}^{R}({\boldsymbol{y}})=0
iff
\nabla\Phi_{\mathfrak{a}}^{R}(R{\boldsymbol{y}})=0
so, for any box
B=[a,b]^{m}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
, and any

, we have

\mathfrak{C}\big[\,B,\Phi_{\mathfrak{a}}^{R}\,\big]=\mathfrak{C}\big[\,RB,F_{\mathfrak{a}}^{R}\,\big],\;\;\mathfrak{C}\big[\,f,F_{\mathfrak{a}}^{R}\,\big]=\mathfrak{C}\big[\,f_{R},\Phi_{\mathfrak{a}}^{R}\,\big]

where
f_{R}({\boldsymbol{x}})=f\big(\,R^{-1}{\boldsymbol{x}}\,\big)
.

When the support of
f
is contained in a fundamental domain of the
{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
-action on
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
we can identify
f
canonically with a function
\bar{f}
on
{\mathbb{T}}^{m}
and we have

\mathfrak{C}\big[\,f,F_{\mathfrak{a}}^{R}\,\big]=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\boldsymbol{x}}\in{\mathbb{T}}^{m},\\
\nabla F_{\mathfrak{a}}^{R}({\boldsymbol{x}})=0\end{subarray}}\bar{f}({\boldsymbol{x}})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\boldsymbol{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m},\\
\nabla\Phi_{\mathfrak{a}}^{R}({\boldsymbol{x}})=0\end{subarray}}f({\boldsymbol{x}}).

In
[
19
,
23
]
it is shown that the random function

is
\mathrm{a.s.}
Morse and there exists an explicit positive constant

that depends only on

and

and any

we have

{\mathbb{E}}\big[\,\mathfrak{C}[B,\Phi_{\mathfrak{a}}]\,\big]=C_{m}(\mathfrak{a})\vol\big[\,B\,\big],

(1.5a)

{\mathbb{E}}\big[\,\mathfrak{C}[f,\Phi_{\mathfrak{a}}]\,\big]=C_{m}(\mathfrak{a})\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}f({\boldsymbol{x}}){\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\big[\,dx\,\big],

(1.5b)

where
{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
denotes the Lebesgue measure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.

Set
C_{1}:=[0,1]^{m}
. In
[

the second author proved that there exists a constant

such that

\lim_{R\to\infty}R^{-m}\var\big[\,\mathfrak{C}[C_{1},F_{\mathfrak{a}}^{R}]\,\big]=C^{\prime}_{m}(\mathfrak{a}).

(1.6)

The proof of (

The first result of this paper is a functional version of (
1.6
). We achieve this using a less computational, more robust and more conceptual technique. One consequence of this asymptotic estimate functional strong law of large numbers concerning the random measures
\mathfrak{C}[-,F_{\mathfrak{a}}^{N}]
,
N\in{{\mathbb{N}}}
. Let us provide some more details.

First some notation. Denote by
|-|
the Euclidean norm on
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
and by
|-|_{\infty}
the sup-norm on
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
. For
{\boldsymbol{x}}_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
and
r>0
we set

B_{r}(x_{0}):=\big\{\,{\boldsymbol{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m};\;\;|{\boldsymbol{x}}|\leq r\,\big\},\;\;B^{\infty}_{r}(x_{0}):=\big\{\,{\boldsymbol{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m};\;\;|{\boldsymbol{x}}|_{\infty}\leq r\,\big\}.

Clearly
B_{r}(x_{0})\subset B^{\infty}_{r}(x_{0})
.

The function
F^{R}_{\mathfrak{a}}
is
{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
-periodic and for
r\in(0,1/2)
the ball
B^{\infty}_{r}(0)
is contained in the interior of a fundamental domain of the
{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
-action since
|{\boldsymbol{x}}-{\boldsymbol{y}}|_{\infty}\leq 2r<1
and
|{\vec{\ell}}|_{\infty}\geq 1
,
\forall{\vec{\ell}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}\setminus 0
. This reflects the fact that the injectivity radius of the flat torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{m}={\mathbb{R}}^{m}/{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
is
\leq\frac{1}{2}
so
B_{r}(0)
can be viewed as a geodesic ball. We can now state the main technical result of this paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix an amplitude
\mathfrak{a}
, a positive integer
m\in{{\mathbb{N}}}
, a radius
r_{0}\in(0,1/2)
and a nonnegative function
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\to{\mathbb{R}}
with support contained in
B_{r_{0}}(0)
. Then the following hold.

(i)

\lim_{R\to\infty}R^{-m}{\mathbb{E}}\big[\,\mathfrak{C}[f,F^{R}_{\mathfrak{a}}]\,\big]={\mathbb{E}}\big[\,\mathfrak{C}[f,\Phi_{\mathfrak{a}}]\,\big]=C_{m}(\mathfrak{a})\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}f({\boldsymbol{x}}){\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\big[\,dx\,\big].

(1.7)

(ii)

There exists a constant
V_{m}(\mathfrak{a})\geq 0
that depends only on
m
and
\mathfrak{a}
such that

\lim_{R\to\infty}R^{-m}\var\big[\,\mathfrak{C}[f,F^{R}_{\mathfrak{a}}]\,\big]=V_{m}(\mathfrak{a})\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}f({\boldsymbol{x}})^{2}d{\boldsymbol{x}}.

(1.8)

If we consider the normalized random measures

\bar{\mathfrak{C}}_{R}:=\frac{1}{R^{m}}\mathfrak{C}[-,F_{\mathfrak{a}}^{R}],\;\;R>0

then we deduce that for any nonnegative
f\in C^{0}_{\mathrm{cpt}}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
,
\supp f\in B_{r_{0}}(0)
, we have

\lim_{R\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\big[\,\bar{\mathfrak{C}}_{R}[f]\,\big]=C_{m}(\mathfrak{a})\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}f({\boldsymbol{x}}){\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\big[\,dx\,\big],

(1.9)

and

\var\big[\,\bar{\mathfrak{C}}_{R}[f]\,\big]\sim VR^{-m}\;\;\mbox{as $R\to\infty$}.

(1.10)

Using finite partitions of unity we deduce from (
1.10
) that for any nonnegative
f
with compact support

\var\big[\,\bar{\mathfrak{C}}_{R}[f]\,\big]=O\big(\,R^{-m}\,\big)\;\;\mbox{as $R\to\infty$}.

If
m\geq 2
, then

\sum_{N\in{{\mathbb{N}}}}\frac{1}{N^{m}}<\infty

Borel-Cantelli and (
1.10
) imply that for any nonnegative
f\in C^{0}_{\mathrm{cpt}}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}\bar{\mathfrak{C}}_{N}[f]=C_{m}(\mathfrak{a})\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}f({\boldsymbol{x}}){\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\big[\,dx\,\big]\;\;\mbox{a.s. and in $L^{2}$}.

(1.11)

Thus, in the white noise limit (
R\to\infty
), the critical points of
F_{\mathfrak{a}}^{R}
will equidistribute with probability
1
. In the case
m=1
, this law of large numbers is proved in the recent work of L. Gass
[
11
, Thm. 1.6]
.

To put (
1.11
) in its proper context we need to recall a few facts about the convergence of random measures. For proofs and details we refer to
[
8
, Chap. 11]
and
[
17
, Chap. 4]
.

We denote by
\Prob({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
the space of Borel probability measures on
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
, by
\Meas({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
the space of finite probability measures on
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
and by
\Meas_{{\mathrm{loc}}}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
the space of locally finite Borel measures on
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
, i.e., Borel measures
\mu
such that
\mu\big[\,S\,\big]<\infty
for any bounded Borel set
S\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
. Any such set
S
defines an additive map

L_{S}:\Meas_{\mathrm{loc}}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})\to{\mathbb{R}},\;\;\Meas_{\mathrm{loc}}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})\ni\mu\mapsto L_{S}(\mu)=\mu\big[\,S\,\big]\in{\mathbb{R}}.

The
vague
topology on the space
\Meas_{\mathrm{loc}}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
is the smallest topology such that all the maps
L_{S}
,
S
bounded Borel, are continuous. The space
\Meas_{\mathrm{loc}}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
equipped with the vague topology is a Polish space, i.e., it is separable and the topology is induced by a complete (Prokhorov-like) metric.

A random locally finite measure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
is a measurable map

\mathfrak{M}:\big(\,\Omega,\mathcal{S},{{\mathbb{P}}}\,\big)\to\Meas_{\mathrm{loc}}\big(\,{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\,\big),

where
\big(\,\Omega,\mathcal{S},{{\mathbb{P}}}\,\big)
is a probability space. Its distribution is a Borel probability measure
{{\mathbb{P}}}_{\mathfrak{M}}
on
\Meas_{\mathrm{loc}}\big(\,{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\,\big)
. It’s mean intensity is the measure
\bar{\mathfrak{M}}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}

\bar{\mathfrak{M}}[S]={\mathbb{E}}\big[\,\mathfrak{M}[S]\,\big]

for any Borel subset
S\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
. We say that
\mathfrak{M}
is locally integrable if its mean intensity is locally finite.

A sequence of random measures
\mathfrak{M}_{N}:\big(\,\Omega,\mathcal{S},{{\mathbb{P}}}\,\big)\to\Meas_{\mathrm{loc}}\big(\,{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\,\big)
is said to converge vaguely a.s. to the random measure
\mathfrak{M}
if

{{\mathbb{P}}}\Big[\,\big\{\,\omega;\;\mathfrak{M}_{N}(\omega)\to\mathfrak{M}(\omega)\;\;\mbox{vaguely in}\;\Meas_{\mathrm{loc}}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})\,\big\}\,\Big]=1.

One can show that the following statements are equivalent

•

\mathfrak{M}_{N}\to\mathfrak{M}
vaguely a.s..

•

\mathfrak{M}_{N}[S]\to\mathfrak{M}[S]
, a.s., for any bounded Borel
S\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.

•

\mathfrak{M}_{N}[f]\to\mathfrak{M}[f]
, a.s.,
\forall f\in C^{0}_{{\mathrm{cpt}}}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
.

Similarly we say that
\mathfrak{M}_{N}\to\mathfrak{M}
vaguely
L^{p}
if the following equivalent conditions hold.

•

\mathfrak{M}_{N}[S]\to\mathfrak{M}[S]
,
L^{p}
, for any bounded Borel
S\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.

•

\mathfrak{M}_{N}[f]\to\mathfrak{M}[f]
,
L^{p}
,
\forall f\in C^{0}_{{\mathrm{cpt}}}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
.

We can rephrase the equality (
1.11
) as a law as large numbers.

Corollary 1.2

(Strong Law of Large Numbers)
.

Suppose that
m\geq 2
. In white noise limit (
N\to\infty
) the
random
measures
\frac{1}{N^{m}}\mathfrak{C}\big[\,-,F^{N}_{\mathfrak{a}}\,\big]
converge vaguely a.s. and
L^{2}
to the
deterministic
measure
C_{m}(\mathfrak{a}){\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
. In particular, for any bounded Borel subset
S\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{m}}\mathfrak{C}\big[\,S,F^{N}_{\mathfrak{a}}\,\big]=C_{m}(\mathfrak{a}){\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\big[\,S\,\big]

a.s. and
L^{2}
.

\hbox to0.0pt{$\sqcap$\hss}\sqcup

In
[
20
]
the second author proved that in white noise limit the
random
measures
\mathfrak{C}\big[\,-,F^{N}_{\mathfrak{a}}\,\big]
also satisfy a Central Limit Theorem. More precisely, for any

\frac{1}{N^{m/2}}\Big(\,\mathfrak{C}[B^{\infty}_{r/2},F_{\mathfrak{a}}^{N}]-{\mathbb{E}}\big[\,\mathfrak{C}[B^{\infty}_{r/2},F_{\mathfrak{a}}^{N}]\,\big]\,\Big)

converges in distribution to a centered normal random variable with nonzero variance.

The random functions
F_{\mathfrak{a}}^{N}
are stationary and so the random measures
\mathfrak{C}\big[\,-,F^{N}_{\mathfrak{a}}\,\big]
are stationary as well, i.e., their distributions are invariant with respect to the natural action of
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
by translations on
\Meas_{\mathrm{loc}}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
.

As discussed in
[
8
, Chap.12]
or
[
17
, Chap.5]
, every
stationary
random locally finite measure
\mathfrak{M}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
with locally finite mean intensity has an
asymptotic intensity

\widehat{\mathfrak{M}}
. This is an integrable random variable

The random measure

.
We set

Corollary

\mathrm{a.s.}

we relate the critical points of

Let first recall a few things about Gaussian vectors. For details and proofs we refer to
[
24
]
.

Suppose that
{\boldsymbol{X}}
is a finite dimensional real Euclidean space with inner product
(-,-)
. An
{\boldsymbol{X}}
-valued
Gaussian vector
is a measurable map
X:\big(\,\Omega,\mathcal{S},{{\mathbb{P}}}\,\big)\to{\boldsymbol{X}}
such that, for any
\xi\in{\boldsymbol{X}}
, the random variable
\big(\,\xi,X\,\big)
is Gaussian. Above and in the sequel
\big(\,\Omega,\mathcal{S},{{\mathbb{P}}}\,\big)
denotes a probability space. The Gaussian vector
X
is called
centered
if
\big(\,\xi,X\,\big)
has mean zero,
\forall\xi\in{\boldsymbol{X}}
.

If
X
is a centered Gaussian random vector valued in the finite dimensional Euclidean space
{\boldsymbol{X}}
, then its distribution is uniquely determined by its
variance
. This is the symmetric nonnegative operator
\var\big[\,X\,\big]:{\boldsymbol{X}}\to{\boldsymbol{X}}
uniquely determined by the equality

{\mathbb{E}}\big[\,e^{\boldsymbol{i}(\xi,X)}\,\big]=e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\,\var[X]\xi,\xi\,)},\;\;\forall\xi\in{\boldsymbol{X}}.

The Gaussian vector
X
is called nondegenerate if
\var\big[\,X\,\big]
is nonsingular.

Suppose that
X_{1},X_{2}
are centered Gaussian vectors valued in the Euclidean spaces
{\boldsymbol{X}}_{1}
and respectively
{\boldsymbol{X}}_{2}
. If
X_{1},X_{2}
are
jointly Gaussian,
i.e.,
X_{1}\oplus X_{2}
is also Gaussian, then the variance of
X_{1}\oplus X_{2}
has the block decomposition

\var\big[\,X_{i}\oplus X_{2}\,\big]=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\var\big[\,X_{1}\,\big]&\cov\big[\,X_{1},X_{2}\,\big]\\
\cov\big[\,X_{2},X_{1}&\var\big[\,X_{2}\,\big]\end{array}\right]

where the
covariance

\cov\big[\,X_{1},X_{2}\,\big]
is a linear map
{\boldsymbol{X}}_{2}\to{\boldsymbol{X}}_{1}
and

\cov\big[\,X_{1},X_{2}\,\big]=\cov\big[\,X_{1},X_{2}\,\big]^{*}.

If
X_{1},X_{2}
are jointly Gaussian and
X_{1}
is nondegenerate, then
{\mathbb{E}}\big[\,X_{2}\,\|\,X_{1}\,\big]
, the conditional expectation of
X_{2}
given
X_{1}
, is an explicit linear function of
X_{1}
. Moreover, for any continuous function
f:{\boldsymbol{X}}_{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}
with at most polynomial growth at
\infty
we have the
regression formula
(see
[
4
, Prop. 12]
or
[
11
, Sec. 2.3.2]
)

{\mathbb{E}}\big[\,f(X_{2})\big|\,X_{1}=0\,\big]={\mathbb{E}}\big[\,f(Z)\,\big]

where
Z=X_{2}-{\mathbb{E}}\big[\,X_{2}\,\|\,X_{1}\,\big]
is a centered Gaussian vector with variance

\Delta_{X_{2},X_{1}}=\Var\big[\,X_{2}\,\big]-\cov\big[\,X_{2},X_{1}]\Var[X_{1}]^{-1}\cov\big[\,X_{1},X_{2}\,\big].

(2.1)

Suppose that
{\boldsymbol{U}}
and
{\boldsymbol{V}}
are finite dimensional real Euclidean spaces and
\mathscr{V}\subset{\boldsymbol{V}}
is an open set. For a map

we denote by

the
k
-order differential of
F
at
v
. It is an element of the vector space

consisting of symmetric
k

\underbrace{{\boldsymbol{V}}\times\cdots\times{\boldsymbol{V}}}_{k}\to{\boldsymbol{U}}.

The
k
-th jet
of

at

is the vector

J_{k}F({\boldsymbol{v}}):=F({\boldsymbol{v}})\oplus F^{\prime}({\boldsymbol{v}})\oplus\cdots\oplus F^{(v)}({\boldsymbol{v}}).

The
Jacobian of
F
at

is

J_{F}(v)=\sqrt{\det\big(\,F^{\prime}(v(F^{\prime}(v)^{*}\,\big)}.

When
{\boldsymbol{U}}={\boldsymbol{V}}
we have

J_{F}(v)=\big|\,\det F^{\prime}(v)\,\big|

A
{\boldsymbol{U}}
-valued
random field
on
\mathscr{V}
is a family of random variables

F(v):\big(\,\Omega,\mathcal{S},{{\mathbb{P}}}\,\big)\to{\boldsymbol{U}},\;\;\Omega\ni\omega\mapsto F_{\omega}({\boldsymbol{v}})\in{\boldsymbol{U}},\;\;{\boldsymbol{v}}\in\mathscr{V}.

We will work with measurable random fields , i.e., random fields

such that the the map

\Omega\times\mathscr{V}\to{\boldsymbol{U}},\;\;(\omega,{\boldsymbol{v}})\mapsto F_{\omega}({\boldsymbol{v}})

is measurable with respect to the product sigma-algebra on
\Omega\times\mathscr{V}
. The maps
F_{\omega}:\mathscr{V}\to{\boldsymbol{U}}
are called the

of the random field

. The random field is called

if all its sample maps belong to

.

The random field is called

if, for any

, and any

, the random vector

\big(\,F({\boldsymbol{v}}_{1}),\dotsc,F({\boldsymbol{v}}_{n})\,\big)\in{\boldsymbol{U}}^{n}

is centered,
\forall{\boldsymbol{v}}\in\mathscr{V}
.

If

, the the covariance kernel of

\mathcal{K}_{F}:\mathscr{V}\times\mathscr{V}\to\End\big(\,{\boldsymbol{U}}\,\big),\;\mathcal{K}_{F}({\boldsymbol{v}}_{1},{\boldsymbol{v}}_{0})=\cov\big[\,F({\boldsymbol{v}}_{1}),F{\boldsymbol{v}}_{0})\,\big]

We begin we an abstract result that will be our main tool for detecting ample Gaussian fields.

Proposition A.1
.

Let
{\boldsymbol{X}}
be a separable Banach space with norm
\|-\|
. Let
(x_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a sequence in
{\boldsymbol{X}}
and
(c_{n})_{n\geq 0}
a sequence of positive real numbers such that

\sum_{n\geq 1}c_{n}\|x_{n}\|<\infty.

Denote by
{\boldsymbol{Y}}
the closure of the span of
(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
. Let
(A_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence of independent standard normal random variables defined on the probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{S},{{\mathbb{P}}})
. Then the following hold.

(i)

There exists a negligible subset
\mathcal{N}\in\mathcal{S}
such that the series

\sum_{n\geq 1}A_{n}(\omega)c_{n}x_{n}

converges in

to an element in
{\boldsymbol{Y}}
for any

(ii)

The map

0,&\omega\in\mathcal{N}\end{cases}

is Borel measurable and the push-forward
\Gamma_{S}:=S_{\#}{{\mathbb{P}}}
is a nondegenerate Gaussian measure on
{\boldsymbol{Y}}
.

(iii)

For any nonempty open subset
\mathcal{O}\subset{\boldsymbol{Y}}
,
{{\mathbb{P}}}\big[\,S\in\mathcal{O}\,\big]>0
.

Proof.

(i) We will show that the random scalar series

\sum_{n}|A_{n}|c_{n}\|x_{n}\|

is
\mathrm{a.s.}
convergent. According to Kolmogorov’s two-series theorem this happens if the positive random variables
X_{n}=|A_{n}|\cdot c_{n}\|x_{n}\|
satisfy

\sum_{n\geq 1}{\mathbb{E}}\big[\,X_{n}\,\big]<\infty\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\sum_{n\geq 1}{\mathbb{E}}\big[\,X_{n}^{2}\,\big]<\infty.

Now observe that

{\mathbb{E}}\big[\,|A_{n}|\,\big]=2\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}xe^{-x^{2}/2}dx=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}},

\sum_{n\geq 1}{\mathbb{E}}\big[\,X_{n}\,\big]=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\sum_{n\geq 1}c_{n}\|x_{n}\|<\infty

and

\sum_{n\geq 1}{\mathbb{E}}\big[\,X_{n}^{2}\,\big]=\sum_{n\geq 1}c_{n}^{2}\|x_{n}\|^{2}<\infty.

(ii) Define
S_{n}:\Omega\to{\boldsymbol{Y}}

S_{n}(\omega)=\begin{cases}\sum_{k=1}^{n}A_{k}(\omega)c_{k}x_{k},&\omega\in\Omega\setminus\mathcal{N},\\
0,&\omega\in\mathcal{N}.\end{cases}

The maps
S_{n}
are measurable since the addition operation on a separable Banach space is a measurable map. The map
S
is measurable since for any
\xi\in{\boldsymbol{Y}}^{*}
the function
\langle\xi,S\rangle
is measurable as limit of the measurable functions
\langle\xi,S_{n}\rangle
.

To see that
\Gamma_{S}
is a Gaussian map let
\xi\in{\boldsymbol{Y}}^{*}
. Then

\langle\xi,S(\omega)\rangle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle\xi,S_{n}(\omega)\rangle.

The random variables

\langle\xi,S_{n}\rangle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}A_{n}c_{n}\langle\xi,x_{n}\rangle

are Gaussian as sum of independent Gaussians. Since the limit of Gaussian random variables is also Gaussian we deduce that
\langle\xi,S\rangle
is Gaussian with variance

v[\xi]=\sum_{n\geq 1}c_{n}^{2}\big|\,\langle\xi,x_{n}\rangle\,\big|^{2}.

Since
(x_{n})
spans a dense subspace of
{\boldsymbol{Y}}
, we deduce that for any
\xi\in{\boldsymbol{Y}}^{*}\setminus 0
there exists

is nondegenerate. Part (iii) now follows from the Support Theorem
[

=\frac{1}{|\det A|\sqrt{\det\big(\,2\pi\var[F(v_{0})\oplus\Xi({\underline{v}}))])}}=r({\underline{v}})^{-m}p_{F(v_{0})\oplus\Xi({\underline{v}})}(0).

We deduce that for any
\uu\in B^{2}_{*}
we have

\rho^{(2)}_{F}({\underline{v}}):=r({\underline{v}})^{-m}{\mathbb{E}}\big[\,|\det F^{\prime}(v_{0})\det F^{\prime}(v_{1})|\;\big|\,Z({\underline{v}})=0\,\big]p_{F(v_{0})\oplus\Xi({\underline{v}})}(0).

(B.4)

Lemma B.2
.

There exists a constant
C=C(m,\vol[B],r)>0
depending only on
m
and
\vol[B]
and
r<\dist(B,\partial\mathscr{V})
such that, for
i=0,1
, and any
{\underline{v}}\in B^{2}_{*}

\big|\,{\mathbb{E}}\big[\,|\det F(v_{i})|^{2}\big|\,Z({\underline{v}})=0\,\big]\,\big|\leq C(m,\vol[B],r)\|\mathcal{K}_{F}\|^{m+2}_{C^{6}(S\times S)}r({\underline{v}})^{2}.

Proof.

It suffices to consider only the case
i=0
since

F(v_{0})=\Xi({\underline{v}})=0\,{\Longleftrightarrow}\,F(v_{1})=\Xi({\underline{v}})=0.

Set

\nu=\nu({\underline{v}}):=\frac{1}{r({\underline{v}})}\big(\,v_{1}-v_{0}\,\big),\;\;Z=Z({\underline{v}}).

Let
f(t)=F(v_{0}+t\nu)
. Since
F(v)
is
\mathrm{a.s.}

C^{2}
we deduce from the first order Taylor approximation with integral remainder that

F(v_{1})-F(v_{0})=f(r)-f(0)=rf^{\prime}(0)+\int_{0}^{r}f^{\prime\prime}(t)(r-t)dt=\partial_{\nu}F(v_{0})+\underbrace{\int_{0}^{r}f^{\prime\prime}(t)(r-t)dt}_{=:W}.

Hence

r\partial_{\nu}F(v_{0})=F(v_{0})-F(v_{1})-W

Hence, for any
p\geq 1
we have

{\mathbb{E}}\big[\,|r\partial_{\nu}F(v_{0})|^{p}\,\big|\,Z=0\,\big]={\mathbb{E}}\big[\,|F(v_{0})-F(v_{1})-W|^{p}\big|\,Z=0\,\big]={\mathbb{E}}\big[\,|W|^{p}\,\big|\,Z=0\,\big].

The random variable
W
is a centered
{\boldsymbol{U}}
-valued Gaussian vector. We deduce that for any
p\geq 1
we have

\big|\,{\mathbb{E}}\big[\,|\partial_{\nu}F(v_{0})|^{p}\big|\,Z=0\,\big]\,\big|=\frac{1}{r^{p}}{\mathbb{E}}\big[\,|W|^{p}\,\big|\,Z=0\,\big]^{p}.

Note that

|W|\leq\int_{0}^{r}\|f^{\prime\prime}(t)\|_{{\boldsymbol{U}}}(r-t)dt\leq\frac{r^{2}}{2}\|F\|_{C^{2}(B)}.

We deduce that

\big\|\,\Var\big[\,W\,\big]\,\big\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\frac{r^{4}}{4}{\mathbb{E}}\big[\,\|F\|^{2}_{C^{2}(B)}\,\big].

Using Corollary
2.8
we deduce that

{\mathbb{E}}\big[\,|W|^{p}\,\big|\,Z=0\,\big]\leq C(m,p)r^{2p}{\mathbb{E}}\big[\,\|F\|^{2}_{C^{2}(B)}\,\big]^{p/2},

where
C(m,p)
is a universal constant that depends only on the dimension
m
and on
p
. We will continue to denote by the same symbol
C(m,p)
various positive constants that depend only on
m
and
p
. We deduce

{\mathbb{E}}\big[\,\big|\,\partial_{\nu}F(v_{0})\,\big]^{p}\big|\,Z=0\,\big]\leq C(m,p)r^{p}{\mathbb{E}}\big[\,\|F\|^{2}_{C^{2}(B)}\,\big]^{p/2}.

(B.5)

Extend
\nu
to an orthonormal basis
\{\nu={\boldsymbol{e}}_{1},{\boldsymbol{e}}_{2},\dotsc{\boldsymbol{e}}_{m}\}
of
{\boldsymbol{V}}
. Using Hadamard’s inequality
[
16
, Cor. 7.8.2]
we deduce

\big|\,\det F^{\prime}(v_{0})\,\big|=\big|\,\det\big(\,\partial_{{\boldsymbol{e}}_{1}}F(v_{0}),\partial_{{\boldsymbol{e}}_{2}}F(v_{0}),\dotsc,\partial_{{\boldsymbol{e}}_{m}}F(v_{0})\,\big)\,\big|

\leq\big|\,\partial_{{\boldsymbol{e}}_{1}}F(v_{0})\,\big|\prod_{k=2}^{m}\big|\,\partial_{{\boldsymbol{e}}_{k}}F(v_{0})\,\big|.

Using Hölder’s inequality we deduce

{\mathbb{E}}\big[\,\big|\,\det F^{\prime}(v_{0})\,\big|^{2}\,\big|Z=0\;\,\big]\leq\prod_{k=1}^{m}{\mathbb{E}}\big[\,\big|\,\partial_{{\boldsymbol{e}}_{k}}F(v_{0})\,\big|^{2m}\,\big|\;\big|\,Z=0\,\big]\,\big]^{\frac{1}{m}}.

For
k=2,\dotsc,m
we have

\Var\big[\,\partial_{{\boldsymbol{e}}_{k}}F(v_{0})\,\big|\,Z=0\,\big]\leq\Var\big[\,\partial_{{\boldsymbol{e}}_{k}}F(v_{0})\,\big]

and

\big\|\,\Var\big[\,\partial_{{\boldsymbol{e}}_{k}}F(v_{0})\,\big]\,\big\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq C(m)\|\mathcal{K}_{F}\|_{C^{2}(B\times B)}.

Using again Corollary
2.8
we deduce that for
k=2,\dotsc,m
we have

{\mathbb{E}}\big[\,\big|\,\partial_{{\boldsymbol{e}}_{k}}F(v_{0})\,\big|^{2m}\,\big|\;\big|\,Z=0\,\big]^{\frac{1}{m}}\leq C(m)\|\mathcal{K}_{F}\|_{C^{2}(B\times B)}.

Using (
B.5
), we deduce that

{\mathbb{E}}\big[\,\big|\,\det F^{\prime}(v_{0})\,\big|^{2}\,\big|\;Z=0\,\big]\leq C(m)r^{2}{\mathbb{E}}\big[\,\|F\|^{2}_{C^{2}(B)}\,\big]\|\mathcal{K}_{F}\|^{m-1}_{C^{2}(B\times B)}.

Invoking (
2.2
) we conclude that

{\mathbb{E}}\big[\,\|F\|^{2}_{C^{2}(B)}\,\big]\leq C(m,\vol[B],r)\|K\|^{3}_{C^{6}(S\times S)}.

This completes the proof of Lemma
B.2
.

\begin{split}\leq{\mathbb{E}}\big[\,\big|\,\det F^{\prime}(v_{0})\,\big|^{2}\,\big|Z({\underline{v}})=0\,\big]\,\big|^{1/2}{\mathbb{E}}\big[\,\big|\,\det F^{\prime}(v_{1})\,\big|^{2}\,\big|\;Z({\underline{v}})=0\,\big]\,\big|^{1/2}\end{split}

B.1

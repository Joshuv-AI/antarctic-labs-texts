Quasi parton distributions of pions at large longitudinal momentum
Dao-yu Zhang, Chuang Huang, Wei-jie Fu

Abstract
In this paper, we develop an approach to calculate the valence-quark quasi parton distribution amplitude (quasi-PDA) and quasi parton distribution function (quasi-PDF) for the pion with a large longitudinal momentum with the functional renormalization group (fRG). This is demonstrated in a low energy effective theory (LEFT) with four-quark scatterings. In the study of the complex structure of quasi-PDA, we introduce a deformed integration contour in the calculations of quasi-PDA or quasi-PDF, which allows us to obtain correct integrals for all momentum fractions. It is found that the pion light-front PDA extrapolated from quasi-PDA based on the large momentum effective theory (LaMET) in the LEFT is comparable with lattice QCD and Dyson-Schwinger equation. This work paves the way to study the PDA and PDF within the fRG approach to first-principles QCD.

Introduction

The Standard Model of particle physics describes all known fundamental interactions and elementary particles. For the mass of visible matter, only 1-2% originates from the Higgs mechanism in the Standard Model, while approximately 98% comes from the strong interactions described by Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD), see
[

The pion parton distribution amplitude (PDA) and parton distribution function (PDF) are the leading-twist physical quantities. The PDA describes the momentum distribution amplitude of the valence quark-antiquark inside the hadron, which is, e.g., a key input for the studies of relevant
B

[
3
,
4
,
5

[
6
,
7
,
8
]
. Measurement of the pion and kaon PDFs is also one of the main scientific goals in future electron-ion collider experiments
[

,
10
,
11
,
12

The direct computation of pion PDA or PDF requires to be done at the light cone, which usually is very challenging and difficult. They arise from the inherently complex structure of these observables, and one needs correlation functions in the complex plane as input to calculate them. Even though, significant progress was made in the direct calculation of PDA and PDF within effective model
[
13
]
. However, there is still a long way to go before a realistic calculation in QCD is feasible.

Alternatively, we can also adopt indirect methods to compute the dependence of PDA or PDF on the momentum fraction of partons, usually denoted by
x

,

and the subsequent establishment of the Large Momentum Effective Theory (LaMET), see
[

The LaMET allows us to extract the light-cone PDA or PDF from the Euclidean quasi parton distribution amplitude (quasi-PDA) or quasi parton distribution function (quasi-PDF) with a finite large longitudinal momentum by extrapolating to the limit of momentum infinity. The quasi-PDA method has already been applied in lattice QCD to compute the light-front wave function and PDA for the pion and kaon
[
30
,
31
,
32
,
33
]
.

II
Quasi parton distributions at large longitudinal momentum

In this Section, we discuss the quasi-light-front framework, which is used here to calculate the valence-quark parton distribution amplitude (PDA) and the parton distribution function (PDF) for the pions. This formalism is based on the Large Momentum Effective Theory (LaMET)
[
27
,
28
]
, see also
[
29
]
. We will show that in the limit of large longitudinal momentum, the quasi-PDA returns to the analytical results of PDA obtained from the light-front calculations by using the pointlike and asymptotic cases as examples.

We start with the unamputated Bethe-Salpeter amplitude for the pion,

\displaystyle\chi_{\pi}(p;P)=G_{q}(p_{+})\,\Gamma_{\pi}(p;P)\,G_{q}(p_{-})\,,

(1)

with

\displaystyle p_{\pm}=p\pm P/2\,,

(2)

and the pion BS amplitude

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\pi}(p;P)=\mathrm{i}\gamma_{5}h_{\pi}(p;P)\,.

(3)

Here
p_{\pm}
denotes the quark momenta and
P
is the momentum of pion. The quark propagator is given by

\displaystyle G_{q}(p)=\big[-\mathrm{i}\gamma\cdot p+M_{q}(p)\big]\,\Delta\big(p^{2},M_{q}(p)\big)\,,

(4)

with

\displaystyle\Delta\big(p^{2},M_{q}(p)\big)=\frac{1}{Z_{q}(p)}\frac{1}{p^{2}+M^{2}_{q}(p)}\,,

(5)

being a scalar function. In
5
,
Z_{q}(p)
and
M_{q}(p)
stand for the quark wave function and the quark mass function, respectively. The unamputated pion BS amplitude in
1
is also shown diagrammatically in
Figure

1
.

\displaystyle\parbox[c]{68.99895pt}{\includegraphics[width=13.79958pt]{V3-quark-meson}}=\chi_{\pi}(p;P)

Figure 1:
Diagrammatic representation of the unamputated Bethe-Salpeter amplitude for the pion.

The four-vector momentum
p
, which is both in the light-front and quasi-light-front formalisms, reads

\displaystyle p_{\mu}=(p_{0},p_{3},p_{1},p_{2})=(p_{0},p_{3},p_{\perp})\,.

(6)

In
6
, the transverse momentum is given by
p_{\perp}=(p_{1},p_{2})
. As for the pion momentum, there is a slight difference. In the light-front formalism, the on-shell momentum of the pion in a rest frame is given by

\displaystyle P_{{}_{\mathrm{LF}},\mu}=(\mathrm{i}m_{\pi},0,0,0)\,,

(7)

with
P_{{}_{\mathrm{LF}}}^{2}=-m_{\pi}^{2}
. While in the quasi-light-front formalism, a nonzero longitudinal momentum for the pion, here denoted by
P_{z}
, is necessary. Thus, one arrives at

\displaystyle P_{\mu}=(\mathrm{i}E_{\pi},P_{z},0,0)\,,\qquad E_{\pi}=\sqrt{P_{z}^{2}+m_{\pi}^{2}}\,,

(8)

with the Lorentz invariant
P^{2}=-m_{\pi}^{2}
.

A twist-two quasi-parton distribution amplitude (quasi-PDA or qPDA) of pion is given by

\displaystyle\phi_{\pi}(x,P_{z})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{f_{\pi}}\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathrm{CD}}\bigg[\int\frac{d^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}\delta(\tilde{n}\cdot p_{+}-x\tilde{n}\cdot P)\gamma_{5}\gamma\cdot\tilde{n}\chi_{\pi}(p;P)\bigg]\,,

(9)

with the pion decay constant
f_{\pi}
and an Euclidean vector
\tilde{n}=(0,1,0,0)
, where the subscript
CD
indicates that the trace operates in the color and Dirac space. The variable
x
stands for the fraction of pion longitudinal momentum carried by the quark, that is implemented by the delta function. Note that if the vector
\tilde{n}
is replaced with the lightlike vector
n=(\mathrm{i},1,0,0)
, the quasi-PDA in
9
would return to the PDA on the light front. Moreover, the PDA can also be obtained from the quasi-PDA in the limit of large longitudinal momentum, i.e.,
P_{z}\to\infty
, based on the LaMET
[
27
,
29
]
, which is demonstrated and proved analytically in two simple cases in
Appendices

A
and
B
. If the transverse momentum in
9
was not integrated and kept untouched, one would obtain instead the quasi-light-front wave function (quasi-LFWF) of pion, denoted by
\psi_{\pi}(x,P_{z},p_{\perp})
. Obviously, the quasi-PDA is related to the quasi-LFWF through the relation, as follows

\displaystyle\phi_{\pi}(x,P_{z})=\frac{1}{16\pi^{3}}\int d^{2}p_{\perp}\psi_{\pi}(x,P_{z},p_{\perp})\,.

(10)

In the same way, one can also obtain the valence-quark quasi-parton distribution function (quasi-PDF) for the pion
[
20
,
21
,
50
]
, as follows

\displaystyle u_{\pi}(x)=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathrm{CD}}\bigg[\int\frac{d^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}\delta(\tilde{n}\cdot p_{+}-x\tilde{n}\cdot P)

\displaystyle\times\Big(\tilde{n}\cdot\partial_{p}H_{+}(p;P)\Big)H_{-}(p;P)\bigg]-\mathcal{S}(x)\,,

(11)

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(x)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathrm{CD}}\bigg[\int\frac{d^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}\delta(\tilde{n}\cdot p_{+}-x\tilde{n}\cdot P)

\displaystyle\times\tilde{n}\cdot\partial_{p}\Big(H_{+}(p;P)H_{-}(p;P)\Big)\bigg]\,,

(12)

where one has
\tilde{n}\cdot\partial_{p}=\tilde{n}_{\mu}(\partial/\partial p_{\mu})
and

\displaystyle H_{+}(p;P)

\displaystyle=\bar{\Gamma}_{\pi}(p;-P)G_{q}(p_{+})\,,

(13)

\displaystyle H_{-}(p;P)

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{\pi}(p;P)G_{q}(p_{-})\,,

(14)

with
\bar{\Gamma}_{\pi}(p;P)=C^{\dagger}\Gamma_{\pi}(p;P)^{\mathrm{T}}C
, where
C
is the charge conjugation matrix.

III
Quark propagator and Bethe-Salpeter amplitude

Figure 2:
Diagrammatic representation of the four-quark vertices, where all momenta are incoming.

Apparently, a pion BS amplitude as well as the quark propagator in the complex plane of momenta is required to compute the quasi-PDA in
9
or the quasi-PDF in
11
discussed in the section above. Recently, we have developed an approach of multi-quark flows within the functional renormalization group, that is well suited for the studies of bound state properties
[
47
,
48
,
49
]
. It is found that the flow of quark self-energy, i.e., the two-quark correlation function, and the flow of four-quark vertex play a similar role as the quark gap equation and Bethe-Salpeter equation for the four-quark scattering kernel, respectively. Note that the self-consistency between the two-quark and four-quark flows is guaranteed naturally by the fact that these two flows are derived from the same effective action, independent of truncations used.

In this work, we would like to calculate the pion quasi parton distributions based on the pion BS amplitude and quark propagator obtained from the infrared dynamics of four-quark scatterings. This low-energy effective theory (LEFT) within the fRG is introduced in
[
48
]
, which can be directly embedded in the QCD, see
[
49
]
. This also indicates that our approach can be directly applied to QCD.

In this section, we briefly introduce the four-quark formalism and the related notations firstly, and then present the results of observables to be used. For more details, we refer to the works of series
[
47
,
48
,
49
]
. Let us begin with the effective action of a two-flavor LEFT, that is,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k}[q,\bar{q}]=\Gamma_{\mathrm{kin},k}[q,\bar{q}]+\Gamma_{4q,k}[q,\bar{q}]\,,

(15)

being purely fermionic, where
k
denotes the infrared cutoff, i.e., the renormalization group (RG) scale, in the flows of fRG. The kinetic term reads

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\mathrm{kin},k}=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}Z_{q}(p)\,\bar{q}(-p)\Big[\mathrm{i}\not{p}+M_{q}(p)\Big]\,q(p)\,.

(16)

with
\not{p}=\gamma_{\mu}p_{\mu}
. The four-quark term is given by

\displaystyle\Gamma_{4q,k}=

\displaystyle-\int\frac{d^{4}p_{1}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\cdots\frac{d^{4}p_{4}}{(2\pi)^{4}}(2\pi)^{4}\delta(p_{1}+p_{2}+p_{3}+p_{4})

\displaystyle\times\lambda_{\alpha}(\bm{p})\,{\mathcal{T}}^{(\alpha)}_{ijlm}(\bm{p})\,\bar{q}_{i}(p_{1})q_{j}(p_{2})\bar{q}_{l}(p_{3})q_{m}(p_{4})\,,

(17)

where a Fierz-complete tensor basis of ten elements
{\mathcal{T}}^{(\alpha)}_{ijlm}
for two-flavor quarks is used, and
\lambda_{\alpha}
is the relevant four-quark dressing of tensor
\alpha
. A sum over the index
\alpha
is assumed in
17
.

Figure 3:
Schematic demonstration of the decomposition of the four-quark vertex into the two quark-meson vertices connected by a meson propagator, when the
t
-channel momentum is near the pole mass of meson. The direction of external quark momenta is along with that of fermion flows.

The four-quark vertex is depicted in
Figure

2
, where the momentum notations are also shown. In our previous work
[
48
]
, a three-momentum approximation for the four-quark vertices has been established, viz.,

\displaystyle\lambda_{\alpha}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},p_{4})\approx

\displaystyle\lambda_{\alpha}(s,t,u)\,,

(18)

where the Mandelstam variables read

\displaystyle s=(\bar{p}+\bar{p}^{\prime})^{2},\quad t=P^{2},\quad u=(\bar{p}-\bar{p}^{\prime})^{2}\,.

(19)

The reliability of the approximation in
18
was investigated in detail in
[
48
]
, and it was found that the resulting error does not exceed 1.5%. Being compatible with the three-momentum approximation, the momentum configuration of the four-quark vertex is chosen as

\displaystyle P_{\mu}=

\displaystyle\sqrt{P^{2}}\,\Big(1,\,0,\,0,\,0\Big)\,,

\displaystyle\bar{p}_{\mu}=

\displaystyle\sqrt{p^{2}}\,\Big(1,\,0,\,0,\,0\Big)\,,

\displaystyle\bar{p}^{\prime}_{\mu}=

\displaystyle\sqrt{p^{2}}\,\Big(\cos\theta,\,\sin\theta,\,0,\,0\Big)\,,

(20)

which are obviously in one-by-one correspondence to the Mandelstam variables
(s,t,u)
. When the
t
-channel momentum is on-shell, that is, the momentum is near the pole mass of mesons, e.g.,
P^{2}\approx-m_{\pi}^{2}
for the pion channel, the strength of four-quark dressing increases remarkably, which indicates there is a resonance of emergent mesonic degrees of freedom. Consequently, in the resonance the four-quark vertex can be approximated by the two quark-meson vertices connected by a meson propagator as shown in
Figure

3
. In order to extract the BS amplitude appropriately, we choose another momentum configuration, as follows

\displaystyle P_{\mu}=

\displaystyle\sqrt{P^{2}}\,\Big(1,\,0,\,0,\,0\Big)\,,

\displaystyle\bar{p}_{\mu}=

\displaystyle\sqrt{p^{2}}\,\Big(\cos\theta,\,\sin\theta,\,0,\,0\Big)\,,

\displaystyle\bar{p}^{\prime}_{\mu}=

\displaystyle-\sqrt{p^{2}}\,\Big(\cos\theta,\,\sin\theta,\,0,\,0\Big)\,.

(21)

Equation

21
is a bit different in comparison to
20
. Here we have used
\bar{p}_{\mu}=-\bar{p}^{\prime}_{\mu}\equiv p_{\mu}
, which is in accordance with the definition of the BS amplitude in
Figure

1
.

Note that the computation above is done in the Euclidean regime, i.e.,
t=P^{2}\geq 0
. The Euclidean data of the four-quark dressings are continued analytically into the Minkowski regime based on the Padé approximants, which are discussed in detail in
[
48
]
. The pion pole mass is obtained from the zero point of the inverse of the four-quark coupling, viz.,

\displaystyle\lambda_{\pi}^{-1}(P^{2}=-m_{\pi}^{2})=0\,.

(22)

The relevant BS amplitude just corresponds to the residue of the four-quark coupling at the pole, i.e.,

\displaystyle h_{\pi}(p,\cos\theta)=\lim_{P^{2}\to-m_{\pi}^{2}}\Big[\lambda_{\pi}(P^{2},p,\cos\theta)\,(P^{2}+m_{\pi}^{2})\Big]^{1/2}\,.

(23)

As a short conclusion, we briefly introduced the LEFT with the fRG, which describes the infrared dynamics of four-quark scatterings, including its interaction truncations and momentum-dependent approximations. Based on this framework, we obtained the momentum-dependent quark mass function and the BS amplitude in Euclidean space. They will be applied in the following quasi-PDA and quasi-PDF calculations.

, leading to more precise PDA and PDF calculations, which are also shown in

Deformed integration contour for the

-integral

Now it is ready to compute the quasi-PDA in
9
. It is straightforward to integrate the longitudinal momentum
p_{3}
by resorting to the delta function. Inserting
2
and
8
, one is immediately led to

\displaystyle p_{3}=\Big(x-\frac{1}{2}\Big)P_{z}\,,

(24)

and the pion quasi-PDA

\displaystyle\phi_{\pi}(x,P_{z})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{f_{\pi}}\frac{4N_{c}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\int dp_{\perp}^{2}dp_{0}\,h_{\pi}(p,\cos\theta)P_{z}

\displaystyle\times\frac{1}{Z_{q}(p_{+})Z_{q}(p_{-})}\frac{1}{p_{+}^{2}+M_{q}^{2}(p_{+})}\frac{1}{p_{-}^{2}+M_{q}^{2}(p_{-})}

\displaystyle\times\Big[xM_{q}(p_{-})+(1-x)M_{q}(p_{+})\Big]\,,

(25)

with

\displaystyle\cos\theta=\frac{p_{0}}{p},\qquad p=\sqrt{p_{0}^{2}+p_{3}^{2}+p_{\perp}^{2}}\,.

(26)

Before we integrate the variable
p_{0}
, it is necessary to analyze the analytic structure of
Equation

25
. To that end, in this section, we assume for the moment that both the BS amplitude
h_{\pi}
and the quark mass
M_{q}
are constants, which allows us to do analytic calculations. Obviously from the propagators of the quark and anti-quark in
25
, one readily finds four poles on the purely imaginary axis. For the quark, the two poles are labeled as

\displaystyle p_{0,\mathrm{pole}\,1}=

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\bigg[-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{P_{z}^{2}+m_{\pi}^{2}}-\sqrt{p_{\perp}^{2}+(xP_{z})^{2}+M_{q}^{2}}\bigg]\,,

\displaystyle p_{0,\mathrm{pole}\,2}=

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\bigg[-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{P_{z}^{2}+m_{\pi}^{2}}+\sqrt{p_{\perp}^{2}+(xP_{z})^{2}+M_{q}^{2}}\bigg]\,.

(27a)

The other two poles for the anti-quark read

\displaystyle p_{0,\mathrm{pole}\,3}=

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\bigg[\frac{\sqrt{P_{z}^{2}+m_{\pi}^{2}}}{2}-\sqrt{p_{\perp}^{2}+\big((1-x)P_{z}\big)^{2}+M_{q}^{2}}\bigg]\,,

\displaystyle p_{0,\mathrm{pole}\,4}=

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\bigg[\frac{\sqrt{P_{z}^{2}+m_{\pi}^{2}}}{2}+\sqrt{p_{\perp}^{2}+\big((1-x)P_{z}\big)^{2}+M_{q}^{2}}\bigg]\,.

(27b)

It can be easily found that the first pole is always in the lower half complex plane and the fourth pole always in the upper half. What is intriguing is that the second pole, supposed to be in the upper half plane, might cross over the
x
-axis and enter into the lower half if the transverse momentum
p_{\perp}
is small while
P_{z}
is large. In the same way, the third pole
could move from the lower half to the upper half plane. This behavior is clearly shown in the left panel of
Figure

4
, where the imaginary parts of the four poles in

are depicted as functions of the momentum fraction

. Here, we have chosen
p_{\perp}=0
,
P_{z}=1
GeV,
M_{q}=0.35
GeV,
m_{\pi}=0.14
GeV for illustrative purpose. One can see that the second pole enters into the lower half plane in the regime of
-0.5<x<0.5
and the third pole into the upper half plane in the regime of
0.5<x<1.5
.

Note that when the crossing takes place, the
p_{0}
-integral, being originally along the real axis, should be modified accordingly. Otherwise the contribution of quark or anti-quark to
Equation

25
could not be taken into account. This is demonstrated clearly in the right panel of
Figure

4
, where the quasi-LFWF of pion on the right side of

is presented. The blue dashed line denotes the results with the

-integral along the real axis. In the two disconnected crossing regions with

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}\,p_{0,\mathrm{pole}\,1}

\displaystyle<C<\mathrm{Im}\,p_{0,\mathrm{pole}\,2},

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}\,p_{0,\mathrm{pole}\,3}

\displaystyle<C<\mathrm{Im}\,p_{0,\mathrm{pole}\,4}\,,

(29)

where

is the shifted magnitude in

\displaystyle C=\frac{\mathrm{Im}\,p_{0,\mathrm{pole}\,2}+\mathrm{Im}\,p_{0,\mathrm{pole}\,3}}{2}\,,

(30)

as shown by the black solid line in the left panel of

, which obviously satisfies the constraints in

IV.2
Taylor expansion of the Bethe-Salpeter amplitude and quark mass in the complex plane

As we have discussed in
Section

IV.1
, the shift

\displaystyle p_{0}\to p_{0}+\mathrm{i}C\,,

(31)

is required in the
p_{0}
-integral, which allows us to take into account the contributions from the poles of quark and anti-quark properly. This entails that the momentum
p
in the BS amplitude in
23
has the form as follows

\displaystyle p_{\mu}=\Big(p_{0}+\mathrm{i}C,(x-1/2)P_{z},p_{\perp}\Big)\,,

(32)

where we have used the relation in
24
arising from the delta function in
9
. In the same way, the momenta of quark and anti-quark as shown in
Figure

1
or
25
read

\displaystyle p_{+\mu}=

\displaystyle\Big(p_{0}+\mathrm{i}\big(C+\sqrt{P_{z}^{2}+m_{\pi}^{2}}/2\big),xP_{z},p_{\perp}\Big)\,,

\displaystyle p_{-\mu}=

\displaystyle\Big(p_{0}+\mathrm{i}\big(C-\sqrt{P_{z}^{2}+m_{\pi}^{2}}/2\big),(x-1)P_{z},p_{\perp}\Big)\,.

(33)

Evidently, the zeroth components in all the momenta from
32
and
33
are complex-valued. In this work, we use the Taylor expansion to extend the momentum dependence to the complex plane, by means of a series of powers of the imaginary part. This leads us to

\displaystyle h_{\pi}(p,\cos\theta)=

\displaystyle h_{\pi}(\bar{p},\cos\theta)+\sum_{m=1}^{n}\frac{1}{m!}\frac{\partial^{m}h_{\pi}}{\partial p_{0}^{m}}(\Delta p_{0})^{m}

\displaystyle+\mathcal{O}\big((\Delta p_{0})^{m+1}\big)\,,

(34)

with

\displaystyle\bar{p}_{\mu}=\Big(p_{0},(x-1/2)P_{z},p_{\perp}\Big)\,,\quad\Delta p_{0}=\mathrm{i}C\,,

(35)

for the pion BS amplitude, where it is expanded to the
n
-th order. Similarly one finds the quark mass

\displaystyle M_{q}(p_{+})=

\displaystyle M_{q}(\bar{p}_{+})+\sum_{m=1}^{n}\frac{1}{m!}\frac{\partial^{m}M_{q}}{\partial p_{0}^{m}}(\Delta p^{+}_{0})^{m}

\displaystyle+\mathcal{O}\big((\Delta p^{+}_{0})^{m+1}\big)\,,

\displaystyle M_{q}(p_{-})=

\displaystyle M_{q}(\bar{p}_{-})+\sum_{m=1}^{n}\frac{1}{m!}\frac{\partial^{m}M_{q}}{\partial p_{0}^{m}}(\Delta p^{-}_{0})^{m}

\displaystyle+\mathcal{O}\big((\Delta p^{-}_{0})^{m+1}\big)\,,

(36)

with

\displaystyle\bar{p}_{+\mu}=\Big(p_{0},xP_{z},p_{\perp}\Big)\,,\quad\bar{p}_{-\mu}=\Big(p_{0},(x-1)P_{z},p_{\perp}\Big)\,,

(37)

and

\displaystyle\Delta p^{+}_{0}=

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\big(C+\sqrt{P_{z}^{2}+m_{\pi}^{2}}/2\big)\,,

VI
Conclusions and outlook

In this work, we have developed a method within the functional renormalization group approach to calculate the valence quark quasi-PDA and quasi-PDF for the pion with a large longitudinal momentum. This method is applied in a low-energy effective theory with four-quark scatterings. The calculated quasi-PDA or quasi-PDF is used to extrapolate the light-front PDA or PDF based on the large momentum effective theory (LaMET).

The flows of the quark propagator and the four-quark vertices constitute a closed set of self-consistent equations, which can be regarded as analogues of the gap equation for the quark propagator and the Bethe-Salpeter equation for the four-quark scattering. Based on this observation and related advantages, we have constructed a framework of multi-quark flows within the fRG to study properties of bound states
[
47
,
48
,
49
]
. More specifically, taking the pion for example, one is able to obtain its pole mass and Bethe-Salpeter amplitude by resolving the pole of the four-quark vertex of pion channel and the residue at the pole.

When one adopts the LaMET method to calculate the light-front PDA or PDF by extrapolating the data of quasi-PDA or quasi-PDF from finite
P_{z}
to
P_{z}\to\infty
, where
P_{z}
is the longitudinal momentum of pion, there is a key point, that is, can one push
P_{z}
to a value as large as possible? However, in realistic calculations the maximal
P_{z}
, below which the computation of quasi-PDA or quasi-PDF is applicable, is usually constrained by our knowledge of ingredients of parton distribution, e.g., the quark propagator and the pion BS amplitude, on the complex plane. In order to incorporate analytic information of parton distributions as much as possible, we have employed two methods in this work. One is the deformed integration contour that allows us to encode the pole contribution of quark propagators correctly. The other one is the Taylor expansion for the BS amplitude and quark mass.

We find that the pion light-front PDA extracted from the quasi-PDA in the low energy effective theory in this work is in qualitative agreement with the lattice QCD within errors. Moreover, the quadratic cumulant of pion PDA obtained in this work is comparable with lattice QCD and Dyson-Schwinger equation.

As discussed in
Section

III
, the approach introduced in this work is also applicable to the inputs of other momentum-dependent quark two-point functions and BS amplitudes. Based on our current first-principles QCD calculations with fRG
[
49
]
, we hope to report on the results of PDAs or PDFs of pion or other hadrons in QCD in the near future.

Appendix A
Quasi-PDA in the Point-like limit

In the appendix, we would like to derive the pion PDA in the point-like pion and asymptotic limits in the quasi-light-front framework analytically, using the integration shift discussed in
Section

IV.1
.

First of all, we begin with the point-like limit. In this limit, the momentum dependence of quark mass and BS amplitude is neglected, and here we take
h_{\pi}=1
. Note that the constant BS amplitude also corresponds to the chiral limit
m_{\pi}=0
, see
[
55
]
. Then the quasi-LFWF is given by

\displaystyle\psi_{\pi}(x,P_{z},p_{\perp})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi f_{\pi}}\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathrm{CD}}\bigg[\int dp_{0}dp_{3}\delta(\tilde{n}\cdot p_{+}-x\tilde{n}\cdot P)\gamma_{5}\gamma\cdot\tilde{n}\chi_{\pi}(p;P)\bigg]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4N_{c}}{\pi f_{\pi}}\int dp_{0}P_{z}M_{q}\frac{1}{(p_{0}+\mathrm{i}P_{z}/2)^{2}+(xP_{z})^{2}+p_{\perp}^{2}+M_{q}^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\frac{1}{(p_{0}-\mathrm{i}P_{z}/2)^{2}+(1-x)^{2}P_{z}^{2}+p_{\perp}^{2}+M_{q}^{2}}\,,

(49)

where the quark wave function is assumed to be
Z_{q}=1
. Hence, the poles in
27
are reduced to

\displaystyle p_{0,\mathrm{pole}\,1}=

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\bigg[-\sqrt{p_{\perp}^{2}+(xP_{z})^{2}+M_{q}^{2}}-\frac{P_{z}}{2}\bigg]\,,

\displaystyle p_{0,\mathrm{pole}\,2}=

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\bigg[\sqrt{p_{\perp}^{2}+(xP_{z})^{2}+M_{q}^{2}}-\frac{P_{z}}{2}\bigg]\,,

\displaystyle p_{0,\mathrm{pole}\,3}=

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\bigg[-\sqrt{p_{\perp}^{2}+\big((1-x)P_{z}\big)^{2}+M_{q}^{2}}+\frac{P_{z}}{2}\bigg]\,,

\displaystyle p_{0,\mathrm{pole}\,4}=

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\bigg[\sqrt{p_{\perp}^{2}+\big((1-x)P_{z}\big)^{2}+M_{q}^{2}}+\frac{P_{z}}{2}\bigg]\,.

(50)

According to the shifted
p_{0}
-integral in
28
with
29
, it is equivalent to compute the residues at
p_{0,\mathrm{pole}\,1}
and
p_{0,\mathrm{pole}\,3}
or
p_{0,\mathrm{pole}\,2}
and
p_{0,\mathrm{pole}\,4}
. Then one arrives at

\displaystyle\psi_{\pi}(x,P_{z},p_{\perp})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2N_{c}}{f_{\pi}}M_{q}P_{z}\bigg\{\Big[P_{z}\big(p_{\perp}^{2}+(xP_{z})^{2}+M_{q}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle-xP_{z}^{2}\big(p_{\perp}^{2}+(xP_{z})^{2}+M_{q}^{2}\big)^{1/2}\Big]^{-1}

\displaystyle+(1-x)P_{z}^{2}\big(p_{\perp}^{2}+((1-x)P_{z})^{2}+M_{q}^{2}\big)^{1/2}\Big]^{-1}

\displaystyle-\Big[P_{z}\big(p_{\perp}^{2}+((1-x)P_{z})^{2}+M_{q}^{2}\big)\bigg\}\,.

(51)

It is not difficult to find that the first term in the curly bracket in
51
behaves as
\sim P_{z}^{-1}
as
P_{z}\to\infty
and the second term
\sim P_{z}^{-3}
. Therefore, the second can be neglected safely, and one finally finds the pion light-front wave function

\displaystyle\psi_{\pi}^{\mathrm{LF}}(x,p_{\perp})\equiv\psi_{\pi}(x,P_{z}\to\infty,p_{\perp})=

\displaystyle\frac{4N_{c}}{f_{\pi}}\frac{M_{q}}{p_{\perp}^{2}+M_{q}^{2}}\,.

(52)

Evidently, the LFWF above is independent of the momentum fraction
x
, and so is the PDA, which reads

\displaystyle\varphi_{\pi}(x)=1\,,

(53)

for the point-like case. In this case, the pion can be regarded as a point-like particle without inner structure.

Appendix B
Quasi-PDA in the asymptotic limit

The asymptotic limit corresponds to the ultraviolet high energy regime of
p\gg\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}
. In this regime, the quark mass can be treated as a constant, and the pion meson mass can be neglected as such
m_{\pi}=0
. Here we employ the first-order Nakanishi-like representation for the BS amplitude, see also
[
19
]
. Since the PDA would be normalized finally, one can ignore constant prefactors in the BS amplitude as well as the quasi-LFWF in
49
for the moment. Inserting the quasi-light-front BS amplitude as follows

\displaystyle h_{\pi}(p;P)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}dw\,\frac{1-w^{2}}{(p_{0}+\mathrm{i}wP_{z}/2)^{2}+(p_{3}+wP_{z}/2)^{2}+p_{\perp}^{2}+M_{q}^{2}}\,,

(54)

one finds for the quasi-LFWF

\displaystyle{\psi}_{\pi}(x,P_{z},p_{\perp})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M_{q}P_{z}\int dp_{0}\int_{-1}^{1}dw(1-w^{2})

\displaystyle\times\frac{1}{(p_{0}+\mathrm{i}wP_{z}/2)^{2}+(w+2x-1)^{2}P_{z}^{2}/4+p_{\perp}^{2}+M_{q}^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\frac{1}{(p_{0}+\mathrm{i}P_{z}/2)^{2}+x^{2}P_{z}^{2}+p_{\perp}^{2}+M_{q}^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\frac{1}{(p_{0}-\mathrm{i}P_{z}/2)^{2}+(1-x)^{2}P_{z}^{2}+p_{\perp}^{2}+M_{q}^{2}}\,.

(55)

Note that there are two other poles from the BS amplitude besides the four poles in
50
, which are denoted by

\displaystyle p_{0,\mathrm{pole}\,5}=

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\bigg[-\sqrt{p_{\perp}^{2}+\frac{(w+2x-1)^{2}P_{z}^{2}}{4}+M_{q}^{2}}-\frac{wP_{z}}{2}\bigg]\,,

\displaystyle p_{0,\mathrm{pole}\,6}=

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\bigg[\sqrt{p_{\perp}^{2}+\frac{(w+2x-1)^{2}P_{z}^{2}}{4}+M_{q}^{2}}-\frac{wP_{z}}{2}\bigg]\,.

(56)

One is able to integrate
p_{0}
in
55
as before by computing the residues at
(p_{0,\mathrm{pole}\,1},\,p_{0,\mathrm{pole}\,3},p_{0,\mathrm{pole}\,5}\,)
or
(p_{0,\mathrm{pole}\,2},\,p_{0,\mathrm{pole}\,4},p_{0,\mathrm{pole}\,6}\,)
. Then, the result is given by

\displaystyle\psi_{\pi}(x,P_{z},p_{\perp})=

\displaystyle\pi M_{q}P_{z}\int_{-1}^{1}dw\Bigg[\frac{1+w}{2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{p_{\perp}^{2}+(xP_{z})^{2}+M_{q}^{2}}}\frac{1}{\Big(P_{z}\sqrt{p_{\perp}^{2}+(xP_{z})^{2}+M_{q}^{2}}-xP_{z}^{2}\Big)^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1-w}{2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{p_{\perp}^{2}+\big((1-x)P_{z}\big)^{2}+M_{q}^{2}}}\frac{1}{\Big(P_{z}\sqrt{p_{\perp}^{2}+\big((1-x)P_{z}\big)^{2}+M_{q}^{2}}+(1-x)P_{z}^{2}\Big)^{2}}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\sqrt{p_{\perp}^{2}+(w+2x-1)^{2}P_{z}^{2}/4+M_{q}^{2}}}\frac{1}{\Big(P_{z}\sqrt{p_{\perp}^{2}+(w+2x-1)^{2}P_{z}^{2}/4+M_{q}^{2}}-(w+2x-1)P_{z}^{2}/2\Big)^{2}}\Bigg]\,.

(57)

It is not difficult to find that the first term in the square bracket in
57
has the leading behavior
\sim P_{z}^{-1}
in the limit of
P_{z}\to\infty
, the second term
\sim P_{z}^{-5}
that would not contribute to the light-front PDA. For the third term, we have to distinguish two cases: One is
w+2x-1>0
and it has the leading behavior
\sim P_{z}^{-1}
that contributes to PDA. The other is
w+2x-1<0
, which leads to
\sim P_{z}^{-5}
and thus can be neglected. Taking all these analyses into account, one is led to

\displaystyle\psi_{\pi}^{\mathrm{LF}}

\displaystyle(x,p_{\perp})=4\pi\frac{M_{q}}{(p_{\perp}^{2}+M_{q}^{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\Big(\int_{-1}^{1}dw\frac{1+w}{2}x-\int_{1-2x}^{1}dw\frac{2x-1+w}{2}\Big)\,.

(58)

Finally, we arrive

\displaystyle\psi_{\pi}^{\mathrm{LF}}(x,p_{\perp})=4\pi\frac{M_{q}}{(p_{\perp}^{2}+M_{q}^{2})^{2}}x(1-x)\,.

(59)

In consequence, the light-front PDA of the pion in the asymptotic limit reads

\displaystyle\varphi_{\pi}(x)=6x(1-x)\,,

(60)

which has been normalized.

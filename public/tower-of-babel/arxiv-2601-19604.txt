Microscopic theory of an atomic spin diode
William J. Huddie, Rembert A. Duine

Abstract
We present a microscopic theory of an atomic spin diode. Our proposed system consists of two magnetic adatoms deposited on the surface of a two-dimensional electron gas with Rashba spin-orbit coupling. A local s-d type coupling between the local spins and the spins of the electrons induces a non-local Ruderman-Kittel-Kazuya-Yoshida type interaction and a Dzyalonshinskii-Moriya interaction, in addition to dissipative interactions, between the spins. We derive the effective action for the spins using the Keldysh formalism. From the effective action, we also derive equations of motion for the spins which are shown to be of Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert (LLG) type, and give expressions for the effective field and Gilbert damping which appear in this equation. From our microscopic theory, we find that for an in-plane magnetic field perpendicular to the vector connecting the two atoms, the magnitude of the field and the distance between the atoms can always be tuned to engender perfectly diodic coupling. Our findings may pave the way to experimental realisation of atomic spin diodes.



Introduction

The field of spintronics is concerned with the design and engineering of new devices which are based on magnetic materials, with the aim that these will eventually replace conventional electronics
4
;
12
;
27
.
Such electronic (CMOS–based) devices rely on the transistor, which only allows transport of charge across it when a gate voltage is applied. The device which underlies the operation of the transistor is called a pn junction, or semiconducting diode, which allows the flow of electric current across it in one direction, but strongly suppresses flow in the opposite direction – this is an example of a non-reciprocal phenomenon
42
;
45
.

In spintronics, however, one is interested in the transport of information using the spin of the electron as opposed to its charge. Such devices may transport information either using spin-polarised electric currents, or using magnons, the quanta of spin waves, which have the advantage that the propagation of such quasiparticles generates much less heat than is lost to Joule heating in ordinary electronics
8
. One may imagine, for example, a so–called magnon transistor, allowing the transmission of magnons across it only under certain conditions. The design of such a device would also necessarily have to include some diode component, facilitating this non-reciprocal behaviour. Non-reciprocal spin waves in certain systems have been known to exist since the 1960s
9
, while recent experimental and theoretical works have demonstrated a variety of ways in which one may construct such a magnon transistor
7
;
25
;
54
;
56
.

Ref.
54
demonstrates, phenomenologically at least, the existence of a perfectly diodic (unidirectional) coupling in an archetypal synthetic antiferromagnet (SAF) structure, in which the two ferromagnetic layers are coupled via a coherent magnetic interaction (e.g. the Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction (DMI)), and an incoherent interaction (e.g. spin pumping across the spacer layer). The authors show that, under certain assumptions, the magnetisation dynamics of each magnetic layer are qualitatively different; that is, when one layer is driven with a microwave field, the other layer undergoes its dynamics in response, but not vice-versa.
This phenomenon has subsequently also been corroborated using the Lindblad master equation formalism
56
. The key ingredients for the perfectly diodic behaviour emphasised in Refs.
54
;
56
are DMI and dissipative interactions, which precisely cancel one another for a specific field. This is an implementation in a magnetic system of the ideas put forward in Ref.
32
for coupling photonic cavities.

In this work, we consider a rather different system from that considered in
54
;
56
, but which still possesses all the key ingredients to realise non-reciprocal spin dynamics. The setup, which is depicted in Fig.
1
we consider a system of two atomic spins (magnetic adatoms) deposited on a two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG) substrate. Rashba-type spin-orbit coupling is present, and we assume a
s-d
type coupling between the localised spins and the spins of the electron gas.

Several groups have recently demonstrated the experimental feasibility of constructing systems similar to the one we consider here by use of scanning-tunnelling microscopy (STM) techniques
22
;
21
;
6
. Not only are such one-dimensional atomic spin chains relatively straightforward to construct, it is also possible to measure their spin-wave dispersion by similar techniques
43
.

Using a Keldysh approach to determine the effective dynamics of the atomic spins, we find that these dynamics are of Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert (LLG) type. The effective equations of motion contain non-local coherent and dissipative interactions, as well as local Gilbert damping. Moreover, examining the magnon susceptibility, we find that for an in-plane magnetic field perpendicular to the vector connecting the two atoms, the magnitude of the field and the distance between the atoms can always be tuned to engender unidirectional coupling. These findings may prove beneficial in the future experimental realisation of atomic spin diodes.

The outline of this work is as follows. In Se.
I
, we give a description of the model system and Hamiltonian, and summarise our key results.

In Sec.
II
, we develop the action of this system along the Keldysh contour. Using perturbation theory in the
s-d
coupling constant, we integrate out the electronic bath and derive an effective action on the spins.

In Sec.
III
, we expand the electronic response function to first order in frequency, allowing us to derive exact expressions for the former.

In Sec.
IV
, we relate the real part of the retarded and advanced components of this response function to the non-local Ruderman–-Kittel–-Kasuya–-Yosida(RKKY)–type coupling between the spins (including an antisymmetric part, which is identified as the DMI), while the imaginary part is related to the Gilbert damping. We show that the equations of motion of the spins are exactly in LLG form, and study the linearised forms of these equations.
We end with a short conclusion and discussion of our results, and provide some outlook on possible continuations of our work.

II
Equations of motion

In this section, we derive the equations of motion for the semiclassical spin dynamics of the system. We begin with a brief overview of the Keldysh path integral formalism, which is a widely-used method for applying the techniques of quantum field theory to non-equilibrium systems, and finds diverse applications in a variety of areas of physics. We then develop the combined action of the system and bath, integrate out the latter, and derive the equations of motion from the resulting effective action on the former. We find that these equations of motion are in the form of a generalised Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert equation.

\displaystyle=S\begin{pmatrix}\sin\theta^{\text{cl}}_{i}\cos\phi^{\text{cl}}_{i}\\
\sin\theta^{\text{cl}}_{i}\sin\phi^{\text{cl}}_{i}\\
\cos\theta^{\text{cl}}_{i}\end{pmatrix},

(35)

\displaystyle{\bm{\mathrm{S}}}^{\text{q}}_{i}

\displaystyle=S\begin{pmatrix}\theta^{\text{q}}_{i}\cos\theta^{\text{cl}}_{i}\cos\phi^{\text{cl}}_{i}-\phi^{\text{q}}_{i}\sin\theta^{\text{cl}}_{i}\sin\phi^{\text{cl}}_{i}\\
\theta^{\text{q}}_{i}\sin\theta^{\text{cl}}_{i}\cos\phi^{\text{cl}}_{i}+\phi^{\text{q}}_{i}\cos\theta^{\text{cl}}_{i}\sin\phi^{\text{cl}}_{i}\\
-\theta^{\text{q}}_{i}\sin\theta^{\text{cl}}_{i}\end{pmatrix},

(36)

giving us the useful relation

{\bm{\mathrm{S}}}^{\text{q}}_{i}=\theta^{\text{q}}_{i}\frac{\partial{\bm{\mathrm{S}}}^{\text{cl}}_{i}}{\partial\theta^{\text{cl}}_{i}}+\phi^{\text{q}}_{i}\frac{\partial{\bm{\mathrm{S}}}^{\text{cl}}_{i}}{\partial\phi^{\text{cl}}_{i}}

(37)

Performing the Keldysh rotation of
\mathcal{S}_{\text{int}}
is no more difficult, though slightly more involved, as this term contains a double time integral along the Keldysh contour. We give some more details of this calculation in the Appendix. We obtain:

\displaystyle{\mathrm{i}}\mathcal{S}_{\text{int}}

\displaystyle={\mathrm{i}}\sum_{ij}\int{\mathrm{d}}t\int{\mathrm{d}}t^{\prime}\ \left[\right.{\bm{\mathrm{S}}}^{\text{q}}_{i}(t)\cdot\left[{\bm{\mathrm{\Pi}}}^{R}_{ij}(t-t^{\prime}){\bm{\mathrm{S}}}^{\text{cl}}_{j}(t^{\prime})\right]

\displaystyle+{\mathrm{i}}\sum_{ij}\int{\mathrm{d}}t\int{\mathrm{d}}t^{\prime}\ {\bm{\mathrm{S}}}^{\text{cl}}_{j}(t)\cdot\left[{\bm{\mathrm{\Pi}}}^{A}_{ij}(t-t^{\prime}){\bm{\mathrm{S}}}^{\text{q}}_{i}(t^{\prime})\right]

\displaystyle+{\mathrm{i}}\sum_{ij}\int{\mathrm{d}}t\int{\mathrm{d}}t^{\prime}\ \frac{1}{2}{\bm{\mathrm{S}}}^{\text{q}}_{i}(t){\bm{\mathrm{\Pi}}}^{K}_{ij}(t-t^{\prime}){\bm{\mathrm{S}}}^{\text{q}}_{j}(t^{\prime})\left.\right]

(38)

where the retarded, advanced, and Keldysh components of the response function are given by

\displaystyle{\bm{\mathrm{\Pi}}}^{R(A)}(t-t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\pm\Theta(\pm(t-t^{\prime}))\left({\bm{\mathrm{\Pi}}}^{>}(t-t^{\prime})-{\bm{\mathrm{\Pi}}}^{<}(t-t^{\prime})\right);

(39)

\displaystyle{\bm{\mathrm{\Pi}}}^{K}(t-t^{\prime})

\displaystyle={\bm{\mathrm{\Pi}}}^{>}(t-t^{\prime})+{\bm{\mathrm{\Pi}}}^{<}(t-t^{\prime}).

(40)

Having converted the contour integrals into real-time integrals, we are in a position to study the dynamics of the spins under the influence of the coupling to the electron bath. The derivation of these equations is the subject of the following section.

II.3
Equations of motion

The third term in (
38
) is quadratic in quantum components of the spins. In order to remove this term, we perform a Hubbard-Stratonovich transformation
16
;
44
, which exactly cancels this term at the cost of introducing a pair of auxilliary fields
{\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}}_{1}
and
{\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}}_{2}
. The partition function of the system becomes

\displaystyle Z[{\bm{\mathrm{S}}}_{1},{\bm{\mathrm{S}}}_{2},t]

\displaystyle=\int\mathcal{D}[{\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}}_{1}]\int\mathcal{D}[{\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}}_{2}]\int\mathcal{D}[g_{1}]\int\mathcal{D}[g_{2}]

\displaystyle\times\operatorname{exp}\left({\mathrm{i}}\mathcal{S}_{\text{eff}}\left[\hat{{\bm{\mathrm{S}}}}_{1},\hat{{\bm{\mathrm{S}}}}_{2},{\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}}_{1},{\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}}_{2}\right]\right),

(41)

with the effective action

\displaystyle{\mathrm{i}}\mathcal{S}_{\text{eff}}

\displaystyle={\mathrm{i}}S\sum_{i}\int{\mathrm{d}}t\ \left[\phi^{\text{q}}_{i}\dot{\theta}^{\text{cl}}_{i}\sin\theta^{\text{cl}}_{i}-\theta^{\text{q}}_{i}\left(B_{i}+\dot{\phi}^{\text{cl}}_{i}\right)\sin\theta^{\text{cl}}_{i}\right]-\frac{{\mathrm{i}}}{2}\sum_{ij}\int{\mathrm{d}}t\ {\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}}_{i}(t)\left(\left({\bm{\mathrm{\Pi}}}^{K}_{ij}\right)^{-1}*{\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}}_{j}\right)(t)

\displaystyle+{\mathrm{i}}\sum_{ij}\int{\mathrm{d}}t\ {\bm{\mathrm{S}}}^{\text{cl}}_{i}(t)\cdot\left[\left({\bm{\mathrm{\Pi}}}^{R}_{ij}*{\bm{\mathrm{S}}}^{\text{cl}}_{i}\right)(t)\right]+{\bm{\mathrm{S}}}^{\text{q}}_{j}(t)\cdot\left[\left({\bm{\mathrm{\Pi}}}^{A}_{ij}*{\bm{\mathrm{S}}}^{\text{cl}}_{i}\right)(-t)\right]

\displaystyle+{\mathrm{i}}\sum_{ij}\int{\mathrm{d}}t\ {\bm{\mathrm{S}}}^{\text{q}}_{i}(t)\cdot{\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}}_{j}(t),

(42)

where we rewrote the integral over
t^{\prime}
as a convolution. Eq. (
42
) shows us that the fluctuating magnetic fields have a noise spectrum defined by the Keldysh component of the magnetic field, namely

\Braket{{\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}}_{i}(t){\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}}_{j}(t^{\prime})}=-{\mathrm{i}}{\bm{\mathrm{\Pi}}}^{K}_{ij}(t-t^{\prime}),

(43)

and also allows us to identify the Lagrangian of the system as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(t)

\displaystyle=S\sum_{i}\left[\phi^{\text{q}}_{i}\dot{\theta}^{\text{cl}}_{i}\sin\theta^{\text{cl}}_{i}-\theta^{\text{q}}_{i}\left(B_{i}+\dot{\phi}^{\text{cl}}_{i}\right)\sin\theta^{\text{cl}}_{i}\right]

\displaystyle+\sum_{ij}{\bm{\mathrm{S}}}^{\text{q}}_{i}(t)\cdot\left({\bm{\mathrm{\Pi}}}^{R}_{ij}*{\bm{\mathrm{S}}}^{\text{cl}}_{i}\right)(t)+{\bm{\mathrm{S}}}^{\text{q}}_{j}(t)\cdot\left({\bm{\mathrm{\Pi}}}^{A}_{ij}*{\bm{\mathrm{S}}}^{\text{cl}}_{i}\right)(-t)

\displaystyle+\sum_{ij}{\bm{\mathrm{S}}}^{\text{q}}_{i}(t)\cdot{\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}}_{j}(t).

(44)

From the Lagrangian, we obtain the equations of motion by imposing the constraints

\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\phi^{\text{q}}_{k}}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\theta^{\text{q}}_{k}}=0\quad k=1,2.

(45)

Note that any dependence on the time derivatives of
\theta^{\text{q}}
,
\phi^{\text{q}}
is removed by the expansion we made to first order in the quantum components.

We define the friction kernel:

{\bm{\mathrm{\Gamma}}}_{kj}(t-t^{\prime})={\bm{\mathrm{\Pi}}}^{R}_{kj}(t-t^{\prime})+{\bm{\mathrm{\Pi}}}^{A}_{jk}(t^{\prime}-t).

If we make use of Eq. (
37
) and rearrange a bit, we find that these constraints give us the equations of motion for
\phi^{\text{cl}}_{k},\theta^{\text{cl}}_{k}
, namely

\dot{\theta}_{k}=-\frac{1}{S\sin\theta_{k}}\frac{\partial{\bm{\mathrm{S}}}_{k}(t)}{\partial\phi_{k}}\cdot\sum_{j}\left[\left({\bm{\mathrm{\Gamma}}}_{kj}*{\bm{\mathrm{S}}}_{j}\right)(t)+{\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}}_{j}(t)\right]

(46)

and

\dot{\phi}_{k}=-B_{k}+\frac{1}{S\sin\theta_{k}}\frac{\partial{\bm{\mathrm{S}}}_{k}(t)}{\partial\theta_{k}}\cdot\sum_{j}\left[\left({\bm{\mathrm{\Gamma}}}_{kj}*{\bm{\mathrm{S}}}_{j}\right)(t)+{\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}}_{j}(t)\right],

(47)

where we now omitted the subscript
cl
for the sake of conciseness. Following an approach similar to that which was used in Ref.
49
, we show that these equations of motion are in fact exactly of the form of a generalised Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert equation, namely

\dot{{\bm{\mathrm{S}}}}_{k}(t)={\bm{\mathrm{S}}}_{k}(t)\times\left[{\bm{\mathrm{B}}}^{\text{ext}}+\sum_{j}({\bm{\mathrm{\Gamma}}}*{\bm{\mathrm{S}}}_{j})(t)+{\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}}_{j}(t)\right].

(48)

It is well-known that the retarded and advanced components of the response function
{\bm{\mathrm{\Pi}}}
are related as
20

\left[{\bm{\mathrm{\Pi}}}^{R}(t-t^{\prime})\right]^{\dagger}={\bm{\mathrm{\Pi}}}^{A}(t^{\prime}-t),

where
\dagger
denotes complex conjugation and exchange of the time arguments, as well as the transpose with respect to both site and spin indices. One immediately concludes that the low-frequency expansion of
{\bm{\mathrm{\Gamma}}}
must give, in the frequency domain:

{\bm{\mathrm{\Gamma}}}(\omega)={\bm{\mathrm{K}}}+{\mathrm{i}}\omega{\bm{\mathrm{\alpha}}},

(49)

where we defined the reactive and dissipative parts of the friction kernel as

\displaystyle{\bm{\mathrm{K}}}

\displaystyle\equiv 2\lim_{\omega\to 0}{\text{Re}}\left[{\bm{\mathrm{\Pi}}}^{R}(\omega)\right];

(50)

\displaystyle{\bm{\mathrm{\alpha}}}

\displaystyle\equiv 2\lim_{\omega\to 0}\frac{{\text{Im}}\left[{\bm{\mathrm{\Pi}}}^{R}(\omega)\right]}{\omega},

(51)

such that Eq. (
13
) reduces to Eq. (
14
), as claimed.

One now sees that, in order to make further progress in understanding the bath-induced dynamics of the spins, we need to proceed with evaluating the real and imaginary parts of
{\bm{\mathrm{\Pi}}}^{R}
. This calculation forms the subject of the following section.

III
Calculation of the response function

III.1
Response functions in the frequency domain

To make progress with the evaluation of the response function, we first extract its greater and lesser parts, and apply the following identities:

G^{\gtrless}({\bm{\mathrm{q,}}}\pm(t-t^{\prime}))=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int{\mathrm{d}}\omega\ {\mathrm{e}^{\mp{\mathrm{i}}\omega(t-t^{\prime})}}G^{\gtrless}({\bm{\mathrm{q}}},\omega);

(52)

\displaystyle{\mathrm{i}}G^{>}_{s}({\bm{\mathrm{q}}},\omega)

\displaystyle=A_{s}({\bm{\mathrm{q}}},\omega)\left(1-N_{\text{F}}(\omega-\mu)\right)

(53)

\displaystyle{\mathrm{i}}G^{<}_{s}({\bm{\mathrm{q}}},\omega)

\displaystyle=-A_{s}({\bm{\mathrm{q}}},\omega)N_{\text{F}}(\omega-\mu),

(54)

where
N_{\text{F}}(x)=1/(e^{\beta x}+1)
is the Fermi-Dirac distribution function, and
A_{s}({\bm{\mathrm{q}}},\omega)
is the spectral function of electrons with momentum
{\bm{\mathrm{q}}}
and energy
\omega
in the Rashba band
s
, defined by

A_{s}({\bm{\mathrm{q}}},\omega)\equiv{\mathrm{i}}\left[G^{R}_{s}({\bm{\mathrm{q}}},\omega)-G^{A}_{s}({\bm{\mathrm{q}}},\omega)\right],

(55)

which may be taken to have a
\delta
-function form if the finite lifetime of electrons in the bath is neglected:

A_{s}({\bm{\mathrm{q}}},\omega)=2\pi\delta({\varepsilon}_{{\bm{\mathrm{q}}}s}-\omega).

(56)

Using the definitions from Eqs. (
39
-
40
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left({\bm{\mathrm{\Pi}}}^{R(A)}\right)^{\mu\nu}_{ij}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\pm\Theta\left(\pm\tau\right)\frac{{\mathrm{i}}J_{sd}^{2}}{2}\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\omega^{\prime}}{2\pi}\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\omega^{\prime\prime}}{2\pi}{\mathrm{e}^{-{\mathrm{i}}(\omega^{\prime}-\omega^{\prime\prime})\tau}}\left(N_{\text{F}}(\omega^{\prime}-\mu)-N_{\text{F}}(\omega^{\prime\prime}-\mu)\right)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{ss^{\prime}}\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}^{2}{\bm{\mathrm{k}}}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}^{2}{\bm{\mathrm{k^{\prime}}}}}{(2\pi)^{2}}{\mathrm{e}^{-{\mathrm{i}}({\bm{\mathrm{k}}}-{\bm{\mathrm{k^{\prime}}}})\cdot{\bm{\mathrm{r}}}_{ij}}}\mathcal{M}^{\mu\nu}_{ss^{\prime}}({\bm{\mathrm{k}}},{\bm{\mathrm{k^{\prime}}}})A_{s}({\bm{\mathrm{k}}},\omega^{\prime\prime})A_{s^{\prime}}({\bm{\mathrm{k^{\prime}}}},\omega^{\prime}),

(57)

and

\displaystyle\left({\bm{\mathrm{\Pi}}}^{K}\right)^{\mu\nu}_{ij}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{{\mathrm{i}}J_{sd}^{2}}{2}\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\omega^{\prime}}{2\pi}\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\omega^{\prime\prime}}{2\pi}{\mathrm{e}^{-{\mathrm{i}}(\omega^{\prime}-\omega^{\prime\prime})\tau}}\left[N_{\text{F}}(\omega^{\prime\prime}-\mu)+N_{\text{F}}(\omega^{\prime}-\mu)-2N_{\text{F}}(\omega^{\prime\prime}-\mu)N_{\text{F}}(\omega^{\prime}-\mu)\right]

\displaystyle\times\sum_{ss^{\prime}}\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}^{2}{\bm{\mathrm{k}}}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}^{2}{\bm{\mathrm{k^{\prime}}}}}{(2\pi)^{2}}{\mathrm{e}^{-{\mathrm{i}}({\bm{\mathrm{k}}}-{\bm{\mathrm{k^{\prime}}}})\cdot{\bm{\mathrm{r}}}_{ij}}}\mathcal{M}^{\mu\nu}_{ss^{\prime}}({\bm{\mathrm{k}}},{\bm{\mathrm{k^{\prime}}}})A_{s}({\bm{\mathrm{k}}},\omega^{\prime\prime})A_{s^{\prime}}({\bm{\mathrm{k^{\prime}}}},\omega^{\prime}),

(58)

where we defined
\tau\equiv t-t^{\prime}
for brevity. The Fourier transform yields

\displaystyle\left({\bm{\mathrm{\Pi}}}^{R(A)}\right)^{\mu\nu}_{ij}(\omega)

\displaystyle=-\frac{J_{sd}^{2}}{2}\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\omega^{\prime}}{2\pi}\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\omega^{\prime\prime}}{2\pi}\frac{1}{\omega-(\omega^{\prime}-\omega^{\prime\prime})\pm{\mathrm{i}}0}\left(N_{\text{F}}(\omega^{\prime\prime}-\mu)-N_{\text{F}}(\omega^{\prime}-\mu)\right)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{ss^{\prime}}\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}^{2}{\bm{\mathrm{k}}}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}^{2}{\bm{\mathrm{k^{\prime}}}}}{(2\pi)^{2}}{\mathrm{e}^{-{\mathrm{i}}({\bm{\mathrm{k}}}-{\bm{\mathrm{k^{\prime}}}})\cdot{\bm{\mathrm{r}}}_{ij}}}\mathcal{M}^{\mu\nu}_{ss^{\prime}}({\bm{\mathrm{k}}},{\bm{\mathrm{k^{\prime}}}})A_{s}({\bm{\mathrm{k}}},\omega^{\prime\prime})A_{s^{\prime}}({\bm{\mathrm{k^{\prime}}}},\omega^{\prime});

(59)

\displaystyle\left({\bm{\mathrm{\Pi}}}^{K}\right)^{\mu\nu}_{ij}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{{\mathrm{i}}J_{sd}^{2}}{2}\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\omega^{\prime}}{2\pi}\int{\mathrm{d}}\omega^{\prime\prime}\delta(\omega-(\omega^{\prime}-\omega^{\prime\prime}))\left[N_{\text{F}}(\omega^{\prime\prime}-\mu)+N_{\text{F}}(\omega^{\prime}-\mu)-2N_{\text{F}}(\omega^{\prime\prime}-\mu)N_{\text{F}}(\omega^{\prime}-\mu)\right]

\displaystyle\times\sum_{ss^{\prime}}\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}^{2}{\bm{\mathrm{k}}}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}^{2}{\bm{\mathrm{k^{\prime}}}}}{(2\pi)^{2}}{\mathrm{e}^{-{\mathrm{i}}({\bm{\mathrm{k}}}-{\bm{\mathrm{k^{\prime}}}})\cdot{\bm{\mathrm{r}}}_{ij}}}\mathcal{M}^{\mu\nu}_{ss^{\prime}}({\bm{\mathrm{k}}},{\bm{\mathrm{k^{\prime}}}})A_{s}({\bm{\mathrm{k}}},\omega^{\prime\prime})A_{s^{\prime}}({\bm{\mathrm{k^{\prime}}}},\omega^{\prime})

(60)

Before we proceed any further, we note an important relation between the Keldysh component and the retarded/advanced components. Specifically, one has:

\left({\bm{\mathrm{\Pi}}}^{K}\right)^{\mu\nu}_{ij}(\omega)=\coth\left(\frac{\omega}{2T}\right)\left[\left({\bm{\mathrm{\Pi}}}^{R}\right)^{\mu\nu}_{ij}(\omega)-\left({\bm{\mathrm{\Pi}}}^{A}\right)^{\mu\nu}_{ij}(\omega)\right].

(61)

This equation holds generally whether one performs a low-frequency expansion of the response function or not, and constitutes a statement of the fluctuation-dissipation theorem: it relates the Keldysh component, determining the correlations of the
fluctuating
magnetic fields
{\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}}_{i}(t)
, to the imaginary part of
{\bm{\mathrm{\Pi}}}^{R}
, which, as we shall see, determines the Gilbert damping, i.e. the dissipation.

We now continue with the calculation. First, we evaluate the momentum space integrals which appear in Eqs. (
59
–
60
). As shown in Appendix
A
, these integrals have the following matrix structure:

\displaystyle\sum_{ss^{\prime}}\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}^{2}{\bm{\mathrm{k}}}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}^{2}{\bm{\mathrm{k^{\prime}}}}}{(2\pi)^{2}}{\mathrm{e}^{-{\mathrm{i}}({\bm{\mathrm{k}}}-{\bm{\mathrm{k^{\prime}}}})\cdot{\bm{\mathrm{r}}}_{ij}}}\mathcal{M}^{\mu\nu}_{ss^{\prime}}({\bm{\mathrm{k}}},{\bm{\mathrm{k^{\prime}}}})A_{s}({\bm{\mathrm{k}}},\omega^{\prime\prime})A_{s^{\prime}}({\bm{\mathrm{k^{\prime}}}},\omega^{\prime})

(62)

\displaystyle=\frac{m^{2}}{2}\sum_{ss^{\prime}}\frac{k_{0s}(\omega^{\prime\prime})k_{0s^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})}{k_{0}(\omega^{\prime})k_{0}(\omega^{\prime\prime})}\mathds{J}^{\mu\nu}_{ss^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime},\omega^{\prime\prime};{\bm{\mathrm{r}}}_{ij})\Theta(\omega^{\prime\prime}-{\varepsilon}_{0s})\Theta(\omega^{\prime}-{\varepsilon}_{0s^{\prime}}),

(63)

where the matrix
\mathds{J}^{\mu\nu}_{ss^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime},\omega^{\prime\prime};{\bm{\mathrm{r}}}_{ij})
has components

\displaystyle\mathds{J}^{xx}_{ss^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime},\omega^{\prime\prime};{\bm{\mathrm{r}}}_{ij})

\displaystyle=J_{0}(k_{0s^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})R)J_{0}(k_{0s}(\omega^{\prime\prime})R)-ss^{\prime}\cos(2\theta)J_{1}(k_{0s^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})R)J_{1}(k_{0s}(\omega^{\prime\prime})R);

\displaystyle\mathds{J}^{yy}_{ss^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime},\omega^{\prime\prime};{\bm{\mathrm{r}}}_{ij})

\displaystyle=J_{0}(k_{0s^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})R)J_{0}(k_{0s}(\omega^{\prime\prime})R)+ss^{\prime}\cos(2\theta)J_{1}(k_{0s^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})R)J_{1}(k_{0s}(\omega^{\prime\prime})R);

\displaystyle\mathds{J}^{zz}_{ss^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime},\omega^{\prime\prime};{\bm{\mathrm{r}}}_{ij})

\displaystyle=J_{0}(k_{0s^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})R)J_{0}(k_{0s}(\omega^{\prime\prime})R)-ss^{\prime}J_{1}(k_{0s^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})R)J_{1}(k_{0s}(\omega^{\prime\prime})R);

\displaystyle\mathds{J}^{xy}_{ss^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime},\omega^{\prime\prime};{\bm{\mathrm{r}}}_{ij})

\displaystyle=\mathds{J}^{yx}_{ss^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime},\omega^{\prime\prime};{\bm{\mathrm{r}}}_{ij})=-\frac{ss^{\prime}}{2}\sin(2\theta)J_{1}(k_{0s^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})R)J_{1}(k_{0s}(\omega^{\prime\prime})R);

\displaystyle\mathds{J}^{xz}_{ss^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime},\omega^{\prime\prime};{\bm{\mathrm{r}}}_{ij})

\displaystyle=-\mathds{J}^{zx}_{ss^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime},\omega^{\prime\prime};{\bm{\mathrm{r}}}_{ij})=-\left(s^{\prime}J_{1}(k_{0s^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})R)J_{0}(k_{0s}(\omega^{\prime\prime})R)+sJ_{0}(k_{0s^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})R)J_{1}(k_{0s}(\omega^{\prime\prime})R)\right)\cos\theta;

\displaystyle\mathds{J}^{yz}_{ss^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime},\omega^{\prime\prime};{\bm{\mathrm{r}}}_{ij})

\displaystyle=-\mathds{J}^{zy}_{ss^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime},\omega^{\prime\prime};{\bm{\mathrm{r}}}_{ij})=\left(s^{\prime}J_{1}(k_{0s^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})R)J_{0}(k_{0s}(\omega^{\prime\prime})R)+sJ_{0}(k_{0s^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})R)J_{1}(k_{0s}(\omega^{\prime\prime})R)\right)\sin\theta,

where
{\bm{\mathrm{r}}}_{ij}=(R\cos\theta,R\sin\theta)
,
J_{n}(x)
is the Bessel function of the first kind at order

and we defined

\displaystyle k_{0}({\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\sqrt{q_{R}^{2}+2m{\varepsilon}},

\displaystyle k_{0s}({\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=-sq_{R}+k_{0}({\varepsilon}).

The step functions appearing in Eq. (

III.2
Keldysh component

Our aim is to perform a low-frequency expansion of the expressions (

;

;

The Keldysh component becomes, to zeroth order in

\displaystyle\left({\bm{\mathrm{\Pi}}}^{K}\right)^{\mu\nu}_{ij}(\omega)

We first split the energy denominator in Eq. (

\displaystyle{\bm{\mathrm{\alpha}}}^{\mu\nu}_{ij}

\Braket{{\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}}_{i}(t){\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}}_{j}(t^{\prime})}=T{\bm{\mathrm{\alpha}}}_{ij}\delta(t-t^{\prime}).

(71)

For the principal value part (

Conclusion

In this work, we developed a model for an atomic spin diode, consisting of two localised spins (magnetic adatoms), deposited on the surface of a two-dimensional electron gas. We derived the semi–classical equations of motion for the steady-state spin dynamics using the Keldysh path integral formalism, and showed that these are very similar in form to the phenomenological equations which were the starting point of Ref.
54
. Our microscopic approach gives exact expressions for both the local and non–local magnetic exchange and Gilbert damping parameters which enter into these equations of motion. Under certain assumptions, these all have analytical expressions.

The results we obtain rest on several key assumptions and approximations. Firstly, we treated the
s-d
coupling constant perturbatively in order to integrate out the bath. Further, we assumed that the temperature of the system is much smaller than the Fermi temperature of the bath. These first two are the only two approximations that are necessary to arrive at an analytical expression for the damping; their validity is discussed in Sec.
II
and
III
, respectively.

To obtain the quoted expression for the exchange tensor, we needed to make two further approximations in order to evaluate the frequency integrals in Eq. (
). The validity of these approximations is discussed in Appendix
A
.

As was emphasised in Ref.
54
, a system which possesses both DMI, long–known to give non-reciprocal effects in spin-wave physics, and a non-local Gilbert damping, can exhibit unidirectional transmission of spin between the two magnetic subsystems, provided symmetric exchange is absent. To this end, we also studied the magnetic susceptibility, which is obtained by linearising the equations of motion and taking the Fourier transform. By tuning of the exchange and damping parameters, and carefully choosing the strength of the applied magnetic field, we find that the transmission of angular momentum between spin
1
and spin
2
(or vice versa) can always be made unidirectional.

In constrast to the work of Ref.
54
, the symmetric exchange (denoted as
J_{0}
in this work) is generally non-zero, and we also have a chiral damping
\alpha_{1}
, which was absent in the work of Yuan.
et. al.
. Nevertheless, we were able to derive two conditions on both the external magnetic field
B_{0}
and the interatomic spacing
R
to realise such unidirectionality. The oscillatory natures of the magnetic parameters we calculated provide for high tunability, allowing our derived conditions to be satisfied independent of the underlying microscopic properties of the bath, such as the Fermi energy, Rashba spin splitting, and effective electron mass; these quantities only determine the relative magnitudes of the effective magnetic parameters and do not have an impact on the existence of the conditions (
84
–
86
).

Our work provides the first derivation, starting from a microscopic model, of the conditions for unidirectional spin-wave propagation which were laid down phenomenologically in Ref.
54
. We emphasise that the conditions we derived are generic, though for the experimental parameters that we were able to infer, we found that the magnetic fields required to satisfy the conditions (
84
–
85
) are currently too large to realise experimentally
30
;
39
;
34
;
15
;
29
;
23
;
19
;
28
. Nevertheless, the field of magnetic adatoms on surfaces is a very active one, and we hope that our work motivates further study of experimental setups in which realising these conditions is more feasible. We also foresee several other extensions to our work, which could include: going beyond the semi–classical approximation, using a more realistic form of the s-d interaction constant, explicitly considering the crystalline structure of the electronic substrate, or considering a system of more than two spins.

Comparison of the hemodynamic effects of ropivacaine versus bupivacaine for elective cesarean delivery under combined spinal–epidural anesthesia: a randomized controlled trial
Authors: Fanshu Ni, Haiyin Ji, Ping Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundWomen with term pregnancies undergoing cesarean delivery under combined spinal and epidural anesthesia (CSEA) usually present with hemodynamic variability and a high incidence of hypotension. Among the commonly used anesthetic drugs for CSEA, ropivacaine reduces the incidence of hypotension compared with bupivacaine. However, most studies have focused on blood pressure and heart rate measurements to evaluate the effects of these two anesthetic drugs on parturients. This study aimed to compare maternal cardiac output and hemodynamic parameters, such as cardiac preload and afterload, of ropivacaine and bupivacaine using transthoracic echocardiography.MethodsEighty-two parturients scheduled for elective caesarean deliveries under CSEA anesthesia were randomly assigned to receive 0.5% bupivacaine 7.5–8.5 mg (n = 41) or 0.5% ropivacaine 11-12.5 mg (n = 41) with different medication doses used for different height-ranges. Cardiac output, stroke volume (SV), ejection fraction, end-diastolic volume, end-diastolic volume, and systemic vascular resistance (SVR) were measured using transthoracic echocardiography before anesthesia; at 1, 5, and 10 min after CSEA; at delivery; and 1–2 min after the oxytocin administration. The primary outcome was the change in cardiac output (CO) at 5 min. Secondary outcomes included maternal hemodynamic, anesthetic efficacy, maternal side effects and fetal outcome data.ResultsNormalized cardiac output was higher in the ropivacaine group at 5 min after CSEA (92.7%; 95% confidence interval (CI): 87.1–98.3%) than in the bupivacaine group (79.8%; 95% CI: 74.2–85.3%) (P = 0.001). CO, SV, end-diastolic volume, and blood pressure were greater, systemic vascular resistance and phenylephrine dosage were lower, and the incidence of bradycardia was lower in the ropivacaine group. The 1-minute Apgar score was higher in the ropivacaine group (P = 0.043). Other neonatal outcomes were similar between the groups.ConclusionsCompared to bupivacaine, ropivacaine demonstrated higher cardiac output (CO), stroke volume (SV), blood pressure, and an improved 1-minute neonatal Apgar score in elective cesarean deliveries under combined spinal-epidural anesthesia (CSEA).Trial registrationRegistration number: ChiCTR220006454. Introduction

The hemodynamic changes after CSEA in cesarean delivery is very common, and hypotension can lead to maternal nausea, vomiting, dyspnea, and uteroplacental hypoperfusion [1–3]. Among the factors that can reduce the risk of spinal-induced hypotension, studies have mostly focused on intravenous vasopressors and volume preloading, but rarely on anesthetic drugs. Bupivacaine is the most commonly used local anesthetic for cesarean delivery [4]. However, this medication causes a deep and prolonged motor block, which may reduce the postoperative patient experience, and can lead to a relatively high incidence of hypotension (30%-80%) [5, 6]. Ropivacaine is a newer amide local anesthetic that is, similar to bupivacaine in its structure and mechanism of action. However, its analgesic potency is lower than that of bupivacaine. Owing to its less toxic effects on the central nervous and cardiovascular systems [7], it has gradually gained widespread use. Although some studies found no significant difference between the two drugs during cesarean Sects [8, 9]., many studies agree that, compared to bupivacaine, ropivacaine helps maintain blood pressure (BP), results in less hypotension and requires lower dosages of vasoconstrictor medication [10–13], suggesting that ropivacaine may have certain advantages in hemodynamic changes.

BP and heart rate (HR) remain the most widely used indicators during cesarean section; however, changes in BP are not consistent with changes in cardiac output (CO). As “CO = mean arterial pressure (MAP) – central venous pressure (CVP) /systemic vascular resistance (SVR),” MAP is only proportional to CO if vascular tone and volume are constant, which is seldom the case during CSEA anesthesia for cesarean Sect [14]. Nevertheless, CO correlates more closely with organ and placental perfusion and oxygen delivery, and recent research has revealed that CO-guided hemodynamic management may improve the postoperative outcomes [15, 16]. Among the techniques available to monitor CO, transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) is a modality that has been employed for an extended period and has been reported to be an reliable method for CO estimation [17]. TTE not only demonstrate the change in CO, but also evaluate changes in cardiac preload, afterload, and systolic function, which is beneficial for comprehensively analyzing the mechanisms of hemodynamic changes. Compared with other devices (LiDCO, Ultrasound Cardiac Output Monitor®, et al.), TTE offers the advantages of being non-invasive and providing accurate measurements throughout all stages of pregnancy in parturient [18, 19].

This study aimed to investigate the maternal cardiovascular stability of ropivacaine and bupivacaine by using TTE. The primary outcome was the change in CO at 5 min. We hypothesized that ropivacaine would maintain a higher CO during cesarean delivery as well as decrease the incidence of maternal hypotension, and result in a slower heart rate as well as higher stroke volume and SVR other than ropivacaine.

Methods

The study was approved by the local ethics committee of the Shengjing Hospital of China Medical University Ethics Committee, Shenyang, China (2022PS716K), and registered in the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2200064541). All enrolled patients provided written informed consent before inclusion. This manuscript adheres to the CONSORT guidelines. This study was conducted between October 2022 and January 2023. All pregnant women met the following inclusion criteria: physical status II–III according to the American Society of Anesthesiologists; age ≥ 18 years; singleton pregnancy; term pregnancy; and scheduled for cesarean delivery with standard CSEA. The exclusion criteria were allergy to any study medication, known fetal abnormalities, cardiac or cerebrovascular disease, hypertension, preeclampsia, sepsis, placental abnormalities, weight < 50 or > 100 kg, and height < 150 or > 180 cm.

Patients were randomized using block randomization with an unstratified block size of four to assign patients in a 1:1 ratio to the bupivacaine (n = 41) or ropivacaine group (n = 41). Randomization assignments were sealed in opaque envelopes. The study solution was prepared by an anesthetist who was not involved in data collection, following the protocol. The investigators who collected the data and the patients were blinded to the contents of the study solutions.

Patients were fasted from midnight and administered 500 ml of 5% glucose solution via intravenous infusion before surgery. They were placed on the operating table in a supine position with a 15° left lateral tilt upon entering the operating room. Routine HR, BP, and oxygen saturation (SpO2) monitoring was performed. A 20-gauge intravenous cannula was inserted into the dorsal hand or forearm veins. Baseline BP was defined as the average of two consecutive measurements with systolic arterial pressure (SAP) differences not exceeding 10%, if the difference was more than 10%, SAP measurements were repeated until the readings were within 10% of each other. The baseline HR was defined as the mean HR value during these measurements. The baseline CO was measured using an ultrasound scanning technique (M9 & UMT-500; Mindray, Shenzhen, China) by a well-trained investigator (H.J.) to ensure reliability of the results. These results were independently confirmed by another anesthesiologist (F.N.), who had undergone TTE training. Both anesthesiologists were blinded to the treatment allocation. During the measurement, the ultrasound probe was placed on the patient’s chest in advance to obtain images at the appropriate time, and the data was calculated after the surgery. The transducer was positioned at the third or fourth intercostal space directly to the left of the sternum, perpendicular to the chest wall, to obtain the parasternal long-axis image. After obtaining a clear image, left ventricular end-diastolic (Dd) and end-systolic (Ds) diameters were measured using the M-mode, and HR was obtained from monitor data simultaneously (Fig. 1). M-mode measurements in the parasternal long-axis view. Electronic calipers were placed at the interface between the myocardial wall and cavity and the interface between the wall and pericardium

CSEA was performed with the patient in the right lateral position using a 25-gauge/17-gauge needle (BD Durasafe, Suzhou, China) and 19-gauge epidural catheter. After confirming the free flow of cerebrospinal fluid, the study solution (1.5–1.7 mL 0.5% bupivacaine or 2.2–2.5 mL 0.5% ropivacaine, Bupivacaine dosage was determined by patient height: 1.5 mL for 1.5–1.6 m, 1.6 mL for 1.6–1.7 m, and 1.7 mL for > 1.7 m. Ropivacaine dosage was set at 1.5 times the corresponding bupivacaine dose.) was injected intrathecally at a rate of approximately 0.1 mL/s, with the opening of the needle facing cephalad. Any volume differences in the bupivacaine group were supplemented with normal saline. Immediately after the intrathecal injection, a 15-mL/kg/h lactated Ringer’s solution infusion was initiated intravenously. After the intrathecal injection, the patients assumed the supine position with left uterine displacement using a standard firm wedge, which was removed at the beginning of the operation.

After spinal block, BP, HR, and SpO2 were measured every minute until delivery and every 3 min thereafter. Additional measurements were obtained and recorded 1 min after fetal delivery and oxytocin administration. CO, SV, and other ultrasonic variables were measured at 1, 5, and 10 min after intrathecal injection, at fetal delivery and 1–2 min after the oxytocin administration(iv.). Hypotension (defined as SAP < 80% of baseline) was treated with phenylephrine (50 µg), with additional supplementation administered as necessary. Atropine (0.5 mg) was administered to treat bradycardia (HR < 60 bpm). The total volume of the vasopressor solution was recorded.

Bilateral sensory level (analgesia to pinprick with a 25-gauge needle) and motor block (modified Bromage scale: 0 = full movement; 1 = inability to raise extended leg, can bend knee; 2 = inability to bend knee, can flex ankle; 3 = no movement) were assessed every 5 min until 20 min after the CSEA. If sensory blockade of T8 was not achieved within 10 min, a 5–10-mL bolus of 2% lidocaine was administered via the epidural catheter by the anesthesiologist. When the attending anesthesiologist deemed the block sufficient, the operation was commenced. The highest level of analgesia (sensory block level at 20 min) was recorded. The obstetric data collected included the intrathecal injection time, skin incision time, delivery time, Apgar scores at 1 and 5 min, and umbilical arterial blood gases. The patients used a numeric rating scale ranging from 1 (unsatisfactory) to 10 (excellent) to assess the overall level of analgesia at the end of the operation. Adverse events such as hypotension, bradycardia, nausea, vomiting, and shivering were recorded.

Statistical analyses

The primary outcome was the CO at 5 min post-spinal injection. Based on our pretesting data, we expected a 0.59 (standard deviation [SD], 0.81) L/min decrease in CO 5 min after intrathecal injection of bupivacaine and a 0.17 (SD, 0.36) L/min decrease with ropivacaine. A previous sample size calculation determined that a minimum of 37 women in each group would provide 80% power, with significance set at P = 0.05. The sample size was increased by 10% to allow for probable dropouts, resulting in a final sample of 41 patients per group.

Results

The patient recruitment and flow are shown in Fig. 2. Eighty-two patients were enrolled in the study, 41 in the bupivacaine group and 41 in the ropivacaine group. Owing to insufficient analgesia, one patient in the bupivacaine group and one patient in the ropivacaine group received epidural lidocaine. We could not obtain clear images at all time points for one patient in each of the bupivacaine and ropivacaine groups. We could not obtain a clear image 1 min after anesthesia for one patient in the bupivacaine group and 5 min after anesthesia for one patient in the ropivacaine group. The time from intrathecal injection to delivery in one patient in the bupivacaine group was < 10 min. All patients had measurable data according to the protocol and were included in the intention-to-treat analysis.

The patient characteristics are presented in Table 1. The maternal characteristics were balanced between the groups. The patients were aged an average of 32 years and were in the 38th week of gestation. In the bupivacaine and ropivacaine groups, 11 (26.8%) and 10 (24.4%) patients, respectively, had a history of cesarean section. In both groups, CO increased after reaching a trough at 5 min after the administration of the study drug. At the lowest point, the bupivacaine group reached 79.8% of the baseline value (95% confidence interval [CI], 74.2–85.3%), whereas the value was significantly higher in the ropivacaine group (92.7%, 95% CI, 87.1–98.3%, P = 0.001). At each time point, CO was significantly higher in the ropivacaine group compared to the bupivacaine group (P < 0.05) (Fig. 3). From baseline to 5 min after CSEA, SV significantly decreased in both groups and increased thereafter (Fig. 3). After oxytocin administration, the SV in both groups declined slightly. The ropivacaine group was statistically higher at 5 min after CSEA (85.4%, 95% CI, 80.1–90.6% vs. 73.8%, 95% CI, 68.6–79.0%, P = 0.002).

The results of the per-protocol analysis, excluding patients with missing CO measurements, were similar to those of the intention-to-treat analysis. The CO at 5 min after spinal injection was 92.7% of baseline (95% CI, 87.1–98.3%) in the ropivacaine group and 79.8% (95% CI, 74.1–85.4%) in the bupivacaine group. The CO at each time point in the bupivacaine group was lower than that in the ropivacaine group. EDV did not differ significantly between the groups, and apart from 5 min, SVR was also higher in the bupivacaine group at delivery. The remaining results were similar to those of the main analysis. For missing data on neonatal outcomes, the mean imputation method was used for normally distributed data, whereas the median imputation method was used for non-normally distributed data. However, the results remain unchanged. Additionally, we repeated the analysis after removing missing data; the results remained unchanged.

Discussion

The results of this study revealed that compared with intrathecal bupivacaine, intrathecal ropivacaine was associated with higher CO. Additionally, ropivacaine was associated with higher SV, EDV, BP, and 1-minute Apgar score, as well as lower SVR, incidence of hypotension, and phenylephrine dosage.

The analgesic efficacy of ropivacaine is slightly lower than that of bupivacaine, most investigations to date have revealed that ropivacaine is 30%-40% less potent than bupivacaine [7, 20–22]. We intended to use 11–12.5 mg ropivacaine and 7.5–8.5 mg bupivacaine based on our experience and the aforementioned study [8, 10, 11]. The results revealed that the maximum block level and maternal satisfaction in both the groups were similar.

To our knowledge, this study is the first to compare detailed CO and hemodynamic evaluation between ropivacaine and bupivacaine for cesarean delivery under CSEA. Previous studies have primarily focused on comparing the sensory and motor block effects of the two drugs, with hemodynamics as secondary outcomes. In our study, no severe adverse maternal or neonatal outcomes were observed; however, the higher CO with ropivacaine than with bupivacaine suggests that ropivacaine use in patients with unstable or altered hemodynamics may reduce severe hemodynamic fluctuations and improve maternal and infant outcomes. These findings provide evidence for future research. As the HR was similar between the groups, the greater CO in the ropivacaine group was primarily due to better preservation of SV. Significant differences were observed in SV at 5 min. No significant difference was observed in SV between the two groups at 1 min and 10 min; however, the ropivacaine group had a higher SV than the bupivacaine group. The greater EDV in the ropivacaine group and the similar EF between the two groups indicated that the higher SV and CO observed in the ropivacaine group were related to a higher venous return and cardiac preload, rather than any alteration in the systolic function. The longer onset time of ropivacaine [10, 23, 24], may delay the sympathetic blockade induced by spinal anesthesia, thereby reducing the magnitude of hemodynamic fluctuations. In our study, the ropivacaine group had a longer onset time to T6, which is consistent with the results of previous studies. The reason for the higher CO level in the ropivacaine group after delivery remains unclear.

Many studies have suggested that after CSEA, SVR decreases rapidly with a compensatory increase in CO [25–28]. However, in our study, the increase in CO and decrease in SVR were not evident. Our study did not involve continuous measurements; thus, rapid changes could not be promptly observed, which may explain the lack of an observed increase. Furthermore, some studies have reported that CO at 5 min after spinal injection or other time points is lower than preanesthesia levels, which is consistent with our findings [26, 29–31]. After administering oxytocin, we observed a significant increase in CO, which is consistent with other studies [32, 33] and is primarily attributed to an increase in HR.

Some studies found that the BP of the ropivacaine group was higher than that of the bupivacaine group [10, 12, 13], which is consistent with our results. Other researchers observed no significant difference in BP between the two drugs [8, 9, 23]. In studies that discovered a significant difference in BP or vasopressor used, the intervals between blood pressure measurements were shorter. In addition, strict criteria for defining hypotension may lower the reported incidence and affect results. We found the ropivacaine group required less vasopressors than the bupivacaine group, consistent with previous studies [11, 12] However, our phenylephrine dosage was lower [18, 34, 35], possibly due to the lower anesthetic dose and lower phenylephrine bolus (50 µg/ml vs. 100 µg/ml). Additionally, our median induction-to-delivery time (12 min) was shorter than previously reported, which may also have reduced vasopressor requirements.

We observed a lower 1-minute Apgar score in the bupivacaine group compared with the ropivacaine group, which contradicts previous data [10–12, 24, 36]. However, the Apgar scores of all newborns were within the normal range; therefore, the clinical significance of this finding remains unclear. In addition, no significant differences were observed in the umbilical cord gases. Neonatal outcome was our second outcome, and our interval from anesthesia to delivery was short; therefore, further study is needed to prove or disprove this finding.

This study had some limitations. First, it was a single-center study, which may have limited the generalizability of the results. Second, we only included normal singleton pregnancies. Further studies should investigate the effects of bupivacaine and ropivacaine on parturients with twin pregnancies, gestational hypertension, and other cardiovascular diseases. Third, owing to time constraints, we chose the two-dimensional echocardiography-guided M-mode approach instead of the left ventricular outflow tract cross-sectional area–velocity time integral method to obtain CO, although the latter is the most commonly used method. The limitation of the M-mode measurements is that it assumes the left ventricular shape as a prolate ellipsoid; therefore, abnormal ventricular morphology and segmental wall motion abnormalities can reduce the accuracy of the measurement. In addition, beam orientation being frequently off-axis may also affect the results [37]. All measurements were conducted by the same well-trained anesthesiologist, and the results were standardized. Moreover, the included patients had no cardiac disease before surgery; thus, they were considered to have a normal cardiac structure and function, suggesting that our results were reliable. Fourth, as the HR of most patients increased significantly after CSEA, phenylephrine was chosen to treat the hypotension. However, phenylephrine is conventionally believed to negatively affect HR and CO [29]. In our study, the number of patients requiring phenylephrine and the total dose of phenylephrine were significantly higher in the bupivacaine group, which may have affected the CO and HR results. However, phenylephrine was mostly administered for ≥ 5 min. When we included the phenylephrine and group*phenylephrine into the fixed effect, only the ESV results demonstrated a significant difference in phenylephrine among all the indicators, and we made statistical adjustments. Fifth, during delivery, obstetricians typically apply pressure to the lower uterus and the region beneath the xiphoid process, which may elevate uterine pressure on the inferior vena cava, resulting in a reduction in CO and SV, with an increase in SVR. The brief period between induction and delivery may influence the 10-minute measurement. Randomization may mitigate this effect; however, the results obtained within 10 min should be carefully evaluated. Finally, we assumed a consistent CVP of 5 cm H2O for all individuals when calculating SVR, and considering the impact of the phenylephrine, results based on the SVR should be interpreted with some caution.

In conclusion, in CSEA for cesarean section, ropivacaine maintained higher CO, BP, and 1-minute Apgar score, indicating that ropivacaine was beneficial for maintaining hemodynamic stability in parturients. Further studies should be conducted to determine the effects of bupivacaine and ropivacaine in patients with unstable or altered hemodynamics, such as preeclampsia, other forms of hypertension, and maternal cardiac diseases.

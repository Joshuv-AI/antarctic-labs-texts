FedSER-XAI: PSO-optimized multi-stream cross-attention transformer with graph features for explainable federated speech emotion recognition
Authors: Eman Abdulrahman Alkhamali, Arwa Abdulaziz Allinjawi, Rehab Bahaaddin Ashari, Mohammed Tawfik
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractFederated learning for speech emotion recognition faces fundamental challenges in simultaneously achieving high performance, privacy preservation, and model interpretability. This paper introduces FedSER-XAI, a novel framework that integrates Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO)-based feature selection, multi-stream cross-attention mechanisms, and graph-based feature extraction within an explainable federated learning architecture. Our approach combines Vision Transformer processing of mel-spectrograms with temporal-spatial graph convolutional networks to capture both contextual and structural speech relationships. The PSO algorithm achieves 78.1% dimensionality reduction (22850 features) while improving discriminative power. The multi-stream architecture processes traditional acoustic features alongside novel graph-based representations derived from visibility and correlation graphs, fused through Transformer-based cross-attention mechanisms. Extensive evaluation on EMODB and SAVEE datasets demonstrates exceptional performance: 99.9% and 97.2% accuracy in centralized settings, with remarkable federated performance achieving global model accuracies of 99.7% (EMODB) and 97.2% (SAVEE) across 8 emotion-specialized clients, representing only 0.2% and 0.0% degradation compared to centralized training. The framework achieves rapid convergence within 10 communication rounds, representing minimal performance degradation (0.2% for EMODB) while preserving privacy. Cross-dataset evaluation on CREMA-D yields 68% accuracy, demonstrating reasonable generalization. The comprehensive explainability framework using SHAP and LIME provides global and local interpretations, validating that graph-based features contribute significantly to emotion discrimination. FedSER-XAI represents the first explainable federated speech emotion recognition system, advancing trustworthy AI for sensitive healthcare and human-computer interaction applications.Keywords: Speech emotion recognition, Federated learning, Explainable AI, Cross-attention mechanisms, Graph neural networks, Particle swarm optimization, Privacy preservationSubject terms: Computational biology and bioinformatics, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Speech emotion recognition (SER) has emerged as a critical component in next-generation human-computer interaction systems, with applications spanning healthcare monitoring, personalized AI assistants, and mental health assessment1. The increasing deployment of emotion-aware systems in sensitive domains has intensified demands for both privacy preservation and model interpretability, creating a fundamental tension between data protection and system transparency. Recent advances in federated learning offer promising solutions for privacy-preserving SER by enabling collaborative model training without centralized data sharing1. However, current federated SER systems achieve only 92–95% accuracy on multimodal tasks while suffering from 15–30% computational overhead when privacy protections are applied2. Specifically, existing federated approaches demonstrate significant performance gaps: Latif et al. achieve 54.8% UAR12, Simić et al. reach 89.27%1, Gahlan & Sethia obtain 88.3%14, and FENG et al. achieve 68.2% WA15, representing deficits of 10%–45% compared to state-of-the-art centralized methods. Furthermore, no existing federated SER system provides model explainability-a critical requirement for clinical deployment7-and most require excessive communication rounds (200+12), creating substantial overhead.

Contemporary SER research has witnessed remarkable progress through the integration of transformer architectures and sophisticated attention mechanisms. Vision Transformer (ViT) approaches treating mel-spectrograms as images have achieved exceptional performance, with Akinpelu et al. demonstrating 98% accuracy on TESS dataset and 91% on EMODB using lightweight architectures4. Cross-attention mechanisms have proven particularly effective in multimodal settings, with the Cross Attention Network achieving 2.66–3.18.66.18% relative improvements in weighted/unweighted accuracy on IEMOCAP through aligned audio-text processing5. Concurrently, graph-based feature extraction methods have shown significant promise, with visibility graph theory approaches yielding 18% UAR improvements over traditional methods by capturing structural interrelations in speech signals6. The emergence of explainable AI frameworks specifically for SER has begun addressing interpretability challenges, with recent work achieving 99.4% accuracy on TESS dataset while providing SHAP-based feature explanations7. However, privacy-preserving federated learning implementations have lagged behind, with novel Property-Indistinguishability techniques only recently demonstrating superior privacy-utility trade-offs compared to traditional differential privacy approaches8.

Despite these advances, several critical research gaps persist that limit the practical deployment of federated SER systems. First, existing federated SER approaches lack comprehensive explainability mechanisms, with the first explainable SER framework only published in 2024, leaving federated implementations without interpretability capabilities essential for clinical applications7. Second, current multi-stream architectures have not been effectively integrated with federated learning paradigms, as transformer-based cross-attention mechanisms showing significant performance improvements remain confined to centralized settings9,10. Third, graph-based feature extraction methods, despite achieving 8–18% performance improvements through structural speech analysis, have not been explored in privacy-preserving federated contexts6,11. Additionally, existing privacy protection mechanisms either compromise model performance significantly or fail to maintain interpretability, with Property-Indistinguishability being the only recent approach showing promise but lacking integration with explainable architectures8. The absence of standardized evaluation frameworks for privacy-utility trade-offs in explainable federated SER systems further impedes progress toward practical deployment.

Related work

The subsequent section provides a comprehensive overview of existing research in emotion recognition, with particular emphasis on federated learning approaches and attention mechanism-based methods. We systematically review the literature across three main areas: federated learning for emotion recognition, attention mechanisms and transformer architectures, and advanced deep learning techniques for emotion recognition.

Federated learning for emotion recognition

The application of federated learning (FL) to emotion recognition has gained significant attention due to growing privacy concerns and the need for decentralized learning systems. Latif et al.12 pioneered the implementation of FL for speech emotion recognition (SER), employing CNN and LSTM architectures with the FedAvg algorithm on the IEMOCAP dataset. Their LSTM-based model achieved a UAR of 54.8% over 200 communication rounds, demonstrating the feasibility of privacy-preserving SER despite slightly lower performance compared to centralized methods (60.91% UAR using variational autoencoders).

Building upon this foundation, Simić et al.1 proposed AVER (Audiovisual Emotion Recognition), a lightweight multimodal CNN-based system enhanced with FL. The framework employs a dual-stream architecture using pre-trained MobileNetV2 for video (0.37M trainable parameters, 10.3 MB) and custom 2D CNN for audio (6.1M parameters, 23.3 MB). Spectrograms are extracted from 1-second audio segments with 50% overlap and resized to 128170 pixels. On the eNTERFACE’05 dataset with 42 speakers divided into three non-overlapping client subsets, AVER+FL achieved multimodal accuracy of 89.27%, representing a 2.34% improvement over the non-federated baseline (86.93%). The audio modality performance increased from 46.16% to 50.82% at the spectrogram level and from 88.19% to 92.86% after voting.

Addressing data heterogeneity challenges, Chawla et al.13 developed a lightweight FL ecosystem using Bi-LSTM models for verbal emotion recognition across four emotions (angry, happy, sad, normal). Their system demonstrated exceptional performance on both IID and non-IID data distributions. Using RAVDESS, CREMA-D, TESS, and SAVEE datasets with MFCC features extracted through STFT, the Bi-LSTM-based FL model achieved remarkable results in the non-IID scenario: 99.97% validation accuracy, precision, and recall with 0.00 validation loss. In the IID scenario, it achieved 99.71% accuracy, 99.73% precision, and 99.69% recall.

Gahlan and Sethia14 introduced AFLEMP (Attention-based Federated Learning for Emotion recognition using Multi-modal Physiological data), integrating Transformer-based self-attention mechanisms with scaled weighted federated averaging. Using EEG, ECG, and GSR signals for three-dimensional emotion classification (Valence, Arousal, Dominance), AFLEMP achieved 88.3% average accuracy on AMIGOS (40 participants, 16 videos) and 84.1% on DREAMER (23 participants, 18 videos), significantly outperforming traditional FL methods including FedAvg (77.51%, 69.20%), FedPer (77.91%, 70.89%), and FedBoost (81.52%, 74.92%).

Recent advances have explored semi-supervised and cross-modal approaches. FENG et al.15 proposed MvPL (Multi-view Pseudo-Labeling), a semi-supervised FL framework combining consistency-based pseudo-labeling with SCAFFOLD variance-reduction. The model uses 1D CNN feature extraction followed by Transformer encoder for both IEMOCAP and MSP-IMPROV datasets. On IEMOCAP, MvPL achieved 68.2% WA and 65.7% UA using only 20–30% labeled data, outperforming FedAvg (62.1% WA, 59.8% UA) and SemiFed (64.5% WA, 61.9% UA).

Bano et al.16 introduced FedCMD (Federated Cross-Modal Distillation) for multimodal emotion recognition on the SEWA dataset. Using OpenSMILE for audio features (6373 functionals) and OpenFace 2.0 for visual features, the framework achieved 97.50% test accuracy and 97.48% F1-score under non-IID conditions with 20% client dropout. Even at 90% client dropout rate, it maintained 96.11% accuracy and 96.09% F1-score, demonstrating strong resilience.

Privacy-preserving mechanisms have been enhanced through advanced encryption. Qi et al.17 developed EmoFed for video-based emotion recognition using ViT-B/32 feature extraction with homomorphic encryption for personalization layers. Evaluated on MEAD (8-class), AFEW (6-class), IMIGUE (2-class), and LIRIS-ACCEDE (regression), EmoFed achieved 71.47% total accuracy on MEAD, 66.71% on AFEW, 59.15% on IMIGUE, and 0.019 MSE on LIRIS-ACCEDE, outperforming seven state-of-the-art baselines.

Raju et al.18 combined SMOTE with Federated 3D-CNN for multimodal emotion recognition on RAVDESS, AFEW, and eNTERFACE’05 datasets. Using MFCC features for audio and 9696 grayscale facial images, the framework achieved 95.72% prediction accuracy with real-time inference capabilities (20ms inference time, 50ms response time). Wang et al.19 proposed FedMer for micro-expression recognition, marking the first FL application to MER. Using STSTNet, RCNA, and FeatRef methods on the MEGC 2019 composite dataset (SMIC-HS, CASME II, SAMM), FedMer_FeatRef achieved a UF1 of 0.7115 versus 0.7838 in the original FeatRef.

Innovative quantum computing approaches have emerged with Qu et al.20 introducing QFSM (Quantum Federated Speech Emotion Recognition) for 5G-IoV environments. Using only 4 qubits and three quantum modules on CASIA, RAVDESS, and EMO-DB datasets, QFSM demonstrated faster convergence than QLSTM and QGRU while maintaining performance close to centralized training under non-IID conditions.

Attention mechanisms and transformer architectures

The integration of attention mechanisms and Transformer architectures has revolutionized emotion recognition by enabling models to capture long-range dependencies and focus on emotionally salient features. Akinpelu et al.4 proposed ViTSER, a lightweight Vision Transformer for SER using mel-spectrogram representations with 3232 patch size, 128-dimensional MLP head, and only 4,166,151 trainable parameters. ViTSER achieved 98% accuracy on TESS, 91% on EMODB, and 93% on the combined TESS-EMODB dataset using 5-fold cross-validation.

Hybrid architectures have shown remarkable results. Alkhamali et al.21 developed an ensemble model integrating CNN, LSTM, and Transformer architectures for processing STFT-based spectrograms (2242243 RGB format). The model achieved state-of-the-art accuracies of 99.86% on EMO-DB, 96.3% on RAVDESS, 96.5% on SAVEE, 85.3% on IEMOCAP, and 83% on SHEIE dataset, with F1-scores of 95.2% (SAVEE) and 95.9% (RAVDESS).

Multi-modal fusion through attention has been extensively explored. Khan et al.22 introduced CMT (Contextual Multi-modal Transformer) employing four fusion strategies (CLS, MAX, MEAN, MIN) on the MELD dataset. The MIN fusion strategy achieved the best performance with 64.18% accuracy, 62.52% F1-score, 63.82% precision, and 64.18% recall. Wang et al.9 proposed MSA-Transformer integrating audio (MFCCs, pitch, energy) and text (BERT embeddings) modalities through Multi-Head Self-Attention on IEMOCAP, achieving 81.85% weighted average accuracy and 81.33% unweighted accuracy.

Nasersharif and Namvarpour23 leveraged Wav2vec 2.0 with a Dimension Reduction Module (DRM) consisting of attentive average pooling, maxout activation, and AAM-Softmax loss. Their attention-based feature fusion achieved 94.80% UA on EMODB, 74.00% on IEMOCAP, and 80.60% on ShEMO. Abuain24 proposed a hybrid LSTM-Transformer framework achieving perfect performance (100% precision, recall, F1-score) for multiple emotions on TESS-DB through dual-path processing with global average pooling. Graph-based approaches have also shown promise for emotion recognition. Li et al.11 proposed Speech-Graph with top-K neighborhood selection achieving 65.39% UA on IEMOCAP, while Pentari et al.6 demonstrated visibility graph theory with k-NN graphs achieving 77.8% UAR on EMODB, outperforming traditional ResNet baselines by 18%. These structural representations validate our design choice of incorporating graph features (visibility and correlation graphs) within the federated architecture, though their application to privacy-preserving scenarios remains unexplored.

Multi-modal fusion approaches and robustness in emotion recognition

Beyond FL and attention mechanisms, innovative approaches have emerged for emotion recognition. Zhang et al.25 developed TCA-JSL (Transfer Component Analysis with Joint Subspace Learning) for cross-corpus SER using Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) in reproducing kernel Hilbert space. The method achieved superior domain adaptation: CASIAeNTERFACE (0.4950±0.0152), eNTERFACECASIA (0.4533±0.0151), CASIASAVEE (0.4521±0.0176), and SAVEECASIA (0.4987±0.0175).

Graph-based methods have shown promising results. Pentari et al.6 proposed a graph-based speaker-aware framework using k-NN graphs with statistical and structural features from Mel-spectrograms. Using Leave-One-Subject-Out Cross-Validation, the method achieved 77.8% UAR on EMO-DB, 79.1% on DEMoS, and 70% on AESDD, significantly outperforming eGeMAPS (48.49%) and Mel-spectrogram+ResNet (59.89%) baselines. Li et al.11 extended this with Top-K neighborhood selection (K=10) and weighted global pooling (0.3 max, 0.3 mean, 0.3 sum), achieving 65.39% UA and 71.83% WA on IEMOCAP, and 68.65% UA and 68.11% WA on MSP-IMPROV.

Min and Kim26 addressed class imbalance through sparse learning with the Ranking Magnitude Method (RMM), combining 2D CNN-SEN (0.256M parameters, 1.104B FLOPs) and MLSTM-FCN. The fusion model achieved 97.82% accuracy on RAVDESS and 98.33% on SAVEE, with consistent F1-scores above 95%.

Multi-modal fusion approaches and robustness in emotion recognition

Recent advances in multimodal emotion recognition have emphasized sophisticated fusion strategies and robustness mechanisms that complement federated learning paradigms. Xie and Mao27 proposed PGIF (Personality-Guided Iterative Feedback Graph Network), which incorporates personality information as a specialized modality to enhance emotional inference. Their iterative feedback fusion mechanism explicitly simulates emotional interactions between feature-level and decision-level modalities, achieving improvements of 1.94% and 1.42% over state-of-the-art methods on IEMOCAP and MELD datasets respectively. This personality-guided approach demonstrates the value of integrating auxiliary information streams, conceptually aligned with our graph-based feature extraction methodology.

Building upon pairwise fusion strategies, Fan et al.28 introduced a framework that explicitly models two-modality interactions (audio-video, text-audio, text-video) through spectral graph convolutional networks, achieving 69.34% F1 on IEMOCAP and 66.05% F1 on MELD. Their dedicated density loss (L1-norm on fused features) plus L2-regularization suppresses redundancy, outperforming 11 strong baselines including COSMIC and MMGCN by up to 4% F1. Similarly, Wang et al.29 developed CIME (Contextual Interaction-based Multimodal Emotion Analysis), which fuses text, audio, and video through a text-centric cross-attention module and Relation-enhanced Graph Convolutional Network, achieving 71.7% accuracy and 71.4% macro-F1 on IEMOCAP six-emotion task. These graph-based fusion approaches validate our design choice of employing cross-attention mechanisms with graph features for emotion recognition.

Robustness under challenging conditions has emerged as a critical requirement for practical deployment. Zhu et al.30 introduced RMER-DT, which fuses diffusion-based imputation with hierarchical transformer architecture to tackle random modality missingness, achieving state-of-the-art weighted accuracy of 72.03% on IEMOCAP and 67.85% on MELD under 10%−70% modality dropout. Wang et al.31 proposed RAFT (Robust Adversarial Fusion Transformer), which adversarially trains a noise generator to simulate real-world corruptions such as channel dropout and signal distortion, attaining 80.17% binary accuracy on MOSEI while maintaining robustness under various missingness patterns. These robustness mechanisms address complementary challenges to our privacy-preservation focus in federated settings.

Contrastive learning has proven effective for multimodal emotion recognition. Xie et al.32 proposed a Dual Contrastive Learning Framework (DCLF) that addresses label-replication-induced emotion-shift blindness through Context-Aware Contrastive Learning (CACL) and Modality-Contribution Contrastive Learning (MCCL), achieving weighted F1 improvements of 4.98% on IEMOCAP and 5.89% on MELD when integrated with existing architectures. This demonstrates the potential of contrastive objectives for preserving modality-specific expertise-a principle that aligns with our multi-stream architecture design where specialized pathways process different feature representations before fusion.

Cross-domain applications have extended multimodal fusion beyond traditional emotion recognition. Zhang et al.33 introduced MATADOR for multimodal traffic accident prediction, fusing driver-centric physiological signals with environmental data through iTransformer and cross-modal attention, achieving 95.34% F1 with dynamic emotion-accident coupling. Xiao et al.34 proposed Atlantis for joint multimodal aspect-based sentiment analysis, incorporating image aesthetic cues alongside visual-textual alignment to achieve 67.3% F1 on Twitter-2015. Zhu et al.35 developed a client-server multimodal mental state assessment system integrating voice, text, facial expression, and body movement, achieving 92.01% WAR in speech and 94.5% in behavior recognition for clinical applications. These diverse applications demonstrate the broader applicability of multimodal fusion techniques, though their centralized architectures lack the privacy guarantees essential for sensitive healthcare domains that our federated approach addresses.

Beyond emotion recognition, recent work has explored advanced optimization strategies for deep learning. Wei et al.37,38 developed bio-inspired meta-heuristic algorithms (QIESOA and HGAO) for adaptive hyperparameter tuning in CNNs, achieving superior accuracies across multiple image classification benchmarks. While these optimization techniques target different domains, the principle of intelligent parameter selection resonates with our PSO-based feature selection methodology. Additionally, Huang et al.36 proposed dynamic spatiotemporal graph frameworks for multi-city forecasting, demonstrating the effectiveness of graph-based representations for capturing complex dependencies-conceptually similar to our visibility and correlation graph construction. Tawfik39 introduced ensemble learning with advanced feature selection for IoT intrusion detection, highlighting the importance of dimensionality reduction in resource-constrained environments, which parallels our federated learning scenario where communication efficiency is paramount.

The reviewed literature establishes that while sophisticated fusion mechanisms, robustness techniques, and optimization strategies have advanced multimodal emotion recognition significantly, existing approaches predominantly operate in centralized settings and lack comprehensive explainability frameworks. Our FedSER-XAI framework uniquely integrates these advances-multi-stream cross-attention fusion, graph-based feature extraction, intelligent feature selection, and explainable AI-within a privacy-preserving federated learning paradigm, addressing the critical gap between high-performance emotion recognition and trustworthy deployment in sensitive applications.

Methodology

This section presents the comprehensive methodology of the proposed FedSER-XAI framework, which integrates Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) for intelligent feature selection, multi-stream cross-attention mechanisms with Triple-Stream CNN, and graph-based feature extraction in a privacy-preserving federated learning paradigm while maintaining explainability1,2. Our approach addresses four fundamental challenges in federated speech emotion recognition: intelligent feature selection from high-dimensional multimodal representations, effective feature fusion for heterogeneous audio representations, privacy preservation without significant utility loss, and model interpretability in distributed settings3,4.

Conclusion

This paper presents FedSER-XAI, a novel framework that successfully addresses the fundamental challenge of achieving high-performance speech emotion recognition while simultaneously preserving privacy and maintaining model interpretability. Through the innovative integration of PSO-based feature selection, multi-stream cross-attention mechanisms with graph-based features, and federated learning with explainable AI, our approach represents a significant advancement toward trustworthy emotion recognition systems suitable for sensitive applications.

The experimental results demonstrate exceptional performance across multiple datasets, achieving 99.9% accuracy on EMODB and 97.5% on SAVEE in centralized settings, with remarkable stability in federated configurations (99.8% and 97.6% respectively). The PSO-based feature selection achieves 78.1% dimensionality reduction while improving accuracy by 2.7–3.8.7.8% over baseline methods, challenging the conventional trade-off between feature dimensionality and discriminative power. The multi-stream architecture with graph-based feature integration captures complementary temporal and structural information, contributing 1.7% performance improvement through visibility and correlation graph analysis.

Our federated learning implementation with emotion-specialized clients addresses critical non-IID data distribution challenges while converging within 10 communication rounds-representing a 50–60% reduction in communication overhead compared to standard approaches. The comprehensive explainability framework using SHAP and LIME provides both global and local interpretations with consistent feature importance patterns across datasets, validating that the model learns generalizable emotional representations rather than dataset-specific artifacts.

The theoretical contributions include rigorous mathematical justification for PSO optimization advantages, multi-stream representation benefits, and convergence guarantees for enhanced federated learning with property-indistinguishability. Statistical validation with large effect sizes (Cohen’s d > 0.8) and comprehensive reproducibility assessment establishes new standards for experimental rigor in federated emotion recognition research.

While limitations exist regarding dataset size sensitivity and cross-linguistic generalization, the demonstrated capabilities address pressing real-world challenges in healthcare AI, mental health monitoring, and human-computer interaction. The successful combination of privacy preservation, explainability, and high performance provides a pathway toward regulatory compliance for clinical AI systems where understanding model decisions is essential for practitioner trust and patient safety.

Future research directions include cross-linguistic evaluation, enhanced privacy mechanisms through differential privacy, and temporal explainability for longitudinal mental health monitoring. The methodological innovations in intelligent feature selection, multi-stream architecture design, and federated optimization contribute broadly to machine learning research with applications extending beyond speech emotion recognition to other sensitive data domains requiring trustworthy AI solutions.

FedSER-XAI represents a crucial step toward practical deployment of emotion recognition systems that respect privacy, provide interpretable decisions, and maintain the performance necessary for real-world applications, thereby advancing the state-of-the-art in trustworthy artificial intelligence for sensitive domains.

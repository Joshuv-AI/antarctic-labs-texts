Development of a prognostic model for postoperative survival in appendiceal pseudomyxoma peritonei based on clinical indicators
Authors: Jie Jiao, Feng Li, Qi Liu, Houjun Zhang, Guanying Yu, Wenbin Yu, Yan Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPseudomyxoma peritonei (PMP) is a rare peritoneal malignancy primarily originating from appendiceal mucinous neoplasms. Prognosis is influenced by histologic subtype, tumor markers, and the completeness of cytoreduction. Cytoreductive surgery (CRS) combined with hyperthermic intraperitoneal chemotherapy (HIPEC) is the standard treatment. This study aims to identify key factors affecting long-term prognosis in appendiceal PMP patients and develop a dynamic nomogram for postoperative survival prediction.MethodsThis study retrospectively analyzed the clinical data of 153 patients with appendiceal-origin PMP confirmed by postoperative pathology from Jinan Central Hospital between October 2017 and December 2024. The primary endpoint was postoperative overall survival (OS). Patients were randomly divided into modeling (107 cases) and validation (46 cases) groups. Nonlinear relationships between continuous variables and survival time were assessed using restricted cubic splines (RCS) and ANOVA. Univariate Cox regression identified risk factors for postoperative OS, while Lasso regression generated a new variable, the LASSO-derived inflammation–nutrition score (LINS), from selected hematological indices. Multivariate Cox regression analysis identified independent prognostic factors, including histological type, preoperative peritoneal cancer index (PCI), CA125, LINS, and Completeness of Cytoreduction (CC) score (P < 0.05). A nomogram was developed to predict 1-, 3-, and 5-year postoperative OS. Model performance was validated using time-dependent ROC curves and calibration curves. Additionally, a dynamic nomogram was created using the Shiny application framework to predict survival probabilities in real-time.ResultsThe 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival rates for the 153 patients were 82.54%, 66.00%, and 56.76%, respectively. The multivariate Cox regression identified LINS, CA125, preoperative PCI, histological type, and CC score as independent prognostic factors (P < 0.05). The nomogram demonstrated good predictive ability, with time-dependent ROC AUC values for the modeling group of 0.945, 0.823, and 0.736 at 1, 3, and 5 years. Calibration curves showed good agreement between predicted and observed survival. A dynamic survival prediction tool was developed and is accessible via a Shiny app ( https://lq1999.shinyapps.io/Dynamic/).ConclusionHistological type, preoperative PCI, CA125, LINS, and CC score are independent prognostic factors in appendiceal PMP. The developed nomogram offers accurate survival predictions and can be updated with new data for improved clinical decision-making.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12957-026-04363-x.Keywords: Appendiceal-Origin Pseudomyxoma Peritonei, CRS, HIPEC, Dynamic Prognostic Model, Nomogram, Survival Analysis

Introduction

Pseudomyxoma peritonei (PMP) is a rare peritoneal surface malignancy, most often arising from appendiceal mucinous neoplasms. It is characterized by progressive intraperitoneal mucin accumulation. The clinical course is frequently indolent, yet recurrent abdominal distension, bowel involvement, and chronic nutritional depletion impose a sustained burden on quality of life and may ultimately compromise survival. Population-based studies suggest a low incidence (approximately 2–4 cases per million), with increasing clinical detection in recent years [1, 2]. Cytoreductive surgery (CRS) combined with hyperthermic intraperitoneal chemotherapy (HIPEC) is the current standard of care for PMP. Maximal macroscopic tumor removal followed by heated intraperitoneal chemotherapy targets residual microscopic disease and has been associated with improved long-term outcomes, particularly in appendiceal PMP. Current guidelines endorse CRS plus HIPEC as the recommended treatment pathway, supporting a more standardized management approach [3–5].

Despite broadly similar treatment strategies, prognosis in appendiceal PMP remains highly heterogeneous. Prior studies consistently link long-term survival to histologic subtype/grade, tumor markers (CEA, CA19-9, CA-125), peritoneal cancer index (PCI), and completeness of cytoreduction (CC score) [2]. Large cohorts have quantified the impact of Completeness of Cytoreduction (CC) score and developed nomograms integrating clinicopathologic variables [2, 6]. Room for improvement remains in variable coverage, interpretability, and clinical usability. Potentially informative biological features, including immune–inflammatory status and nutrition-related indices, are often not incorporated. Static nomograms can be cumbersome in routine care and do not readily support rapid bedside estimation, communication, and longitudinal updating.

Clinical prediction models are intended to translate multidimensional data into individualized risk estimates that can inform follow-up intensity, treatment planning, and patient counseling. Nomograms are widely used for prognostic assessment because they are intuitive and actionable. Practical utility depends on more than discrimination and calibration; it also relies on variable robustness, reproducibility, and tool accessibility [7].

In this study, we retrospectively analyzed a single-center cohort of patients with appendiceal PMP to identify key factors associated with postoperative overall survival. Variable selection was performed with methods including least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) to reduce overfitting risk. We developed and validated a postoperative survival prediction model, then implemented it as a dynamic nomogram to enable rapid estimation of 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival probabilities using up-to-date patient data, supporting risk stratification and follow-up management.

Materials and methods

Study design and participants

This study performed a single-centre retrospective cohort study at Jinan Central Hospital. The protocol was approved by the institutional ethics committee (No. 20240305005) and complied with the Declaration of Helsinki. Consecutive patients with pathologically confirmed appendiceal-origin PMP who underwent CRS plus HIPEC between 1 October 2017 and 31 December 2024 were eligible. Patients aged ≥ 14 years with complete baseline and follow-up data were included. Exclusion criteria were concomitant malignancies (including other peritoneal surface malignancies), end-stage organ failure, prior neoadjuvant chemotherapy or radiotherapy, substantial missing data, and non-surgical management or palliative surgery only.

Data collection and variable definitions

Demographic, clinical, surgical, and pathological data were extracted from electronic medical records. Laboratory indices and tumour markers were collected within 3 days before CRS/HIPEC. Variables included age, sex, BMI, American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) score, NRS2002 score, CEA, CA19-9, CA125, haemoglobin, albumin, and inflammatory/nutritional indices neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR), systemic immune-inflammation index (SII), prognostic nutritional index (PNI) and hemoglobin–albumin–lymphocyte–platelet score (HALP). Disease burden and surgical completeness were assessed using the PCI and CC score, recorded from intraoperative documentation and postoperative records. Histological subtype was obtained from pathology reports.

CRS and HIPEC procedure

CRS aimed for maximal macroscopic cytoreduction in line with institutional practice based on Sugarbaker principles. HIPEC was delivered immediately after CRS, with perfusate temperature maintained at 43 ± 0.1 °C for 60–90 min. Postoperative intraperitoneal chemotherapy was administered for 3–4 cycles at 24-h intervals according to institutional protocol.

Outcome and follow-up

The primary endpoint was overall survival (OS), defined as the time from CRS/HIPEC to death from any cause. Follow-up was scheduled at 1 and 3 months postoperatively, then every 3 months thereafter. Assessments included imaging (ultrasound and/or CT) and tumour markers (CEA, CA19-9, CA125); subsequent treatments were recorded when applicable. The last follow-up date was 15 January 2025.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables are presented as mean ± s.d. or median (IQR); categorical variables as n (%). Variables with > 20% missing values were excluded; remaining missing data were handled using multiple imputation (m = 5). The cohort was randomly split into training and validation sets (7:3). Restricted cubic splines were used to assess non-linearity of continuous predictors. Candidate predictors were screened using univariable Cox models. LASSO Cox regression with 10-fold cross-validation was used to derive a composite LASSO score from eligible haematologic/inflammatory indicators and reduce multicollinearity. To limit overfitting and capture the joint prognostic signal of correlated biomarkers while preserving predictor parsimony, this study constructed a single composite variable, the LASSO-derived inflammation–nutrition score (LINS). Independent prognostic factors were identified using multivariable Cox regression, and a nomogram was constructed to estimate 1-, 3-, and 5-year OS. Discrimination was evaluated with time-dependent ROC curves (AUC); calibration was assessed using calibration plots. A dynamic nomogram was implemented as a Shiny web application for individualized prediction. Analyses were conducted in R (version 4.3.3) and SPSS (version 26.0). Two-sided P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Population baseline

Between October 1, 2017, and December 31, 2024, 153 patients with appendiceal-origin PMP who underwent CRS combined with HIPEC were included. Among them, 63 (41.18%) were male and 90 (58.82%) were female. The median age was 59 years (52, 65). The mean BMI was 23.46 ± 3.47 kg/m². Regarding residence, 87 (56.86%) were from rural areas and 66 (43.14%) from urban areas. Preoperative tumor markers showed that 85 (55.56%) had elevated CEA, 70 (45.75%) had elevated CA199, and 72 (47.06%) had elevated CA125. Preoperatively, 120 (78.43%) had ASA score I/II, and 33 (21.57%) had ASA score III/IV. The mean surgical time was 270 min (180, 340 min), and median intraoperative blood loss was 100 mL (10, 300 mL). Pre-PCI mean was 18.64 ± 11.55, indicating extensive peritoneal metastasis in most patients. For CC score, 48 (31.37%) had score 0, 43 (28.10%) had score 1, 39 (25.49%) had score 2, and 23 (15.03%) had score 3. Histologically, 74 (48.37%) were disseminated peritoneal adenomucinosis (DPAM), 76 (49.67%) were peritoneal mucinous carcinomatosis (PMCA), and 3 (1.96%) were peritoneal mucinous carcinomatosis with signet ring cells (PMCA-S). Regarding postoperative complications, 61 (39.87%) had Clavien-Dindo score ≥ II (Supplementary Table 1).

Comparison of baseline characteristics between the modeling and validation groups

Among the 153 patients, the 1-year survival rate was 82.54%, the 3-year survival rate was 66.00%, and the 5-year survival rate was 56.76%, with a median follow-up time of 20 months (range: 0–87 months) (Fig. 1). In the modeling group (107 patients), the 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year survival rates were 82.43%, 64.96%, and 55.60%, respectively. In the validation group (46 patients), the 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year survival rates were 83.19%, 68.38%, and 58.92%, respectively (Fig. 2). We designated the 1- and 3-year survival rates as the primary endpoints for interpretation, while the 5-year survival rate was considered a secondary, exploratory outcome. There were no statistically significant differences in baseline characteristics between the two groups, including age, sex, residence, histological type, NRS2002 score, ASA score, preoperative PCI, CC score, and Clavien-Dindo grade II or higher complications (P > 0.05), indicating comparability between the groups (Table 1).

Univariable and multivariable cox regression analysis in the modeling group

For Cox regression analysis of continuous variables, the linear relationship between predictors and survival time was tested using restricted cubic splines (RCS), and non-linear effects were assessed by ANOVA. The results showed that continuous variables such as BMI, hemoglobin, albumin, NLR, PLR, SII, PNI, HALP, and surgical time had no significant non-linear relationship with survival time (P > 0.05) (Supplementary Table 2). Additionally, restricted cubic splines showed that with increasing values of BMI, HALP, PNI, and albumin, the mortality risk decreased, while increasing values of NLR, PLR, SII, intraoperative blood loss, and surgical time were associated with an increased mortality risk. Hemoglobin levels initially increased the mortality risk before decreasing it (Supplementary Fig. 1). Therefore, these variables were used for univariable Cox regression analysis.

The results of univariable Cox regression analysis in the modeling group (n = 107) showed that albumin, CA125, CEA, CA199, NRS2002 score, NLR, PLR, SII, PNI, HALP, histological type, stoma, Pre-PCI, Post-PCI, and CC score all significantly affected the postoperative overall survival (OS) of appendiceal PMP patients (P < 0.05) (Table 2). To prevent overfitting of the model and ensure the independence of the predictors, LASSO regression was used to select prognostic markers. Variables such as PLR, SII, and PNI were shrunk to zero in the LASSO model (Fig. 3). Based on the following formula, a new variable, LINS, was calculated: LINS = (0.15982494 × NLR) + (-0.01581141 × HALP) + (0 × PLR) + (0 × SII) + (0 × PNI). The results of the restricted cubic splines showed no significant non-linear relationship between LINS and survival time (P > 0.05) (Supplementary Table 2). As the LASSO score increased, the mortality risk increased (Supplementary Fig. 1). The final LASSO regression score calculation can be expressed as: LINS = 0.15982494 × (Pre-NLR) − 0.01581141 × ((Pre-Hb × Pre-ALB × Pre-Lymphocytes) / Pre-PLT). Multivariable Cox regression analysis using 11 variables, including LINS, albumin, CA125, CEA, CA199, NRS2002 score, histological type, stoma, Pre-PCI, Post-PCI, and CC score, showed that LINS, CA125, Pre-PCI, histological type, and CC score were independent prognostic factors for postoperative OS (P < 0.05) (Table 3).

Survival prediction model construction and validation

Based on the results of multivariable Cox regression analysis, we developed a nomogram model to estimate the 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival probabilities of patients with appendiceal-origin PMP after surgery (Fig. 4). This model evaluates the patient’s survival risk by calculating the total score based on various clinical factors. The higher the score, the lower the patient’s survival probability. For example, for a patient with a preoperative neutrophil count of 4.9 (×10⁹/L), preoperative lymphocyte count of 1.2 (×10⁹/L), preoperative hemoglobin level of 92 (g/L), preoperative albumin level of 35 (g/L), preoperative platelet count of 227 (×10⁹/L), CC score of 2, histological type of DPAM, preoperative PCI of 25, CA125 of 200 (U/mL), and LINS of 0.383476, the model predicts a 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival rate of 84.72%, 60.75%, and 50.19%, respectively.

Nomogram model for predicting long-term postoperative prognosis in appendiceal-origin pseudomyxoma peritonei patients

In the validation of the long-term prognostic model, the time-dependent ROC curve results for both the modeling and validation groups showed that the model has good predictive ability. In the modeling group, the AUC for 1-year survival was 0.945 (95% CI: 0.846–0.999), for 3-year survival was 0.823 (95% CI: 0.630–0.994), and for 5-year survival was 0.736 (95% CI: 0.431–0.999). In the validation group, the AUC for 1-year survival was 0.922 (95% CI: 0.850–0.995), for 3-year survival was 0.929 (95% CI: 0.853–0.999), and for 5-year survival was 0.843 (95% CI: 0.695–0.991). These results suggest that the model demonstrates good stability and discrimination in both the modeling and validation groups (Fig. 5).

The calibration curve validated by 1,000 bootstrap clinical predictions shows that the observed and predicted survival curves are well aligned, indicating that the predicted 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival probabilities are relatively close to the actual outcomes. However, in both the modeling and validation groups, when the 1-year survival probability is between 20% and 40%, the actual survival rate is higher. This may be due to the overall higher 1-year survival probability in appendiceal-origin PMP patients and the smaller sample size during the model construction (Fig. 6).

Development and application of the dynamic nomogram model

To further improve the accuracy of long-term prognosis prediction in patients with appendiceal-origin PMP, a dynamic nomogram model was developed based on the results of multivariable Cox regression analysis. The model incorporates various clinical features of the patient, including preoperative neutrophil count, preoperative hemoglobin, preoperative albumin, preoperative lymphocyte count, preoperative platelet count, preoperative CA125, preoperative PCI, histological type, and CC score. The model allows for the calculation of survival probabilities at different time points (1-year, 3-year, and 5-year). To facilitate clinical use, the nomogram was designed as a dynamic web application, allowing users to calculate and predict long-term prognosis based on specific clinical data (Access link: https://lq1999.shinyapps.io/Dynamic/).

Discussion

PMP is a rare malignant peritoneal tumour, most often arising from appendiceal mucinous neoplasms, characterized by progressive intraperitoneal mucin accumulation and diffuse peritoneal implants [8]. Although it typically follows an indolent course, patients often develop abdominal distension, pain, ascites, and bowel obstruction [9]. CRS combined with HIPEC is the standard treatment, aiming to remove macroscopic disease and treat residual microscopic deposits [10]. Compared with peritoneal metastases from other primaries, PMP is generally less aggressive and patients may achieve longer survival after CRS and HIPEC [11, 12]. Postoperative OS has been linked to completeness of cytoreduction, PCI, histological subtype/grade, tumour markers, and patient factors, yet guidance for early recurrence risk stratification and subsequent management remains limited [2, 9, 13]. Nomograms can translate these variables into individualized survival estimates, but their clinical utility depends on robust predictors and practical implementation. Using a single-centre 7-year cohort, we evaluated outcomes after CRS plus HIPEC in appendiceal-origin PMP and developed a nomogram to support clinical decision-making.

In this study, elevated CA125, higher preoperative PCI, CC score, PMCA or PMCA-S histology, and LINS were associated with poorer OS. CA125 (MUC16) is a glycoprotein encoded by the MUC16 gene. It is widely used as a tumour marker and is often elevated in ovarian cancer, though it can also rise in non-ovarian settings, limiting specificity [14, 15]. Experimental and clinical data link MUC16 to tumour biology relevant to peritoneal disease, including immune evasion, invasion, dissemination, motility changes, and reduced sensitivity to genotoxic agents [15, 16]. In appendiceal malignancies, CA125 has also shown prognostic value. A large analysis reported that elevated CA125 was associated with worse OS and supported its inclusion in baseline assessment [16]. Another study in CRS/HIPEC-treated PMP found that normal CA125 was associated with successful CRS, while CA19-9 elevation independently predicted worse progression-free survival [17]. The peritoneal cancer index (PCI) quantifies intraperitoneal tumour burden. It guides the extent of peritonectomy and helps judge the feasibility of optimal CRS. Higher PCI is often linked to worse outcomes, including shorter progression-free survival [18]. In appendiceal PMP, PCI has prognostic value for both survival and recurrence. In extensive disease, meaningful long-term survival remains achievable in selected patients, though outcomes are generally inferior to less extensive disease. PCI also informs resectability. A large cohort suggested different PCI cut-offs by grade, with higher thresholds in high-grade disease [19]. Completeness of cytoreduction is a major determinant of outcome across peritoneal surface malignancies, including PMP. CC scoring provides an operational measure of residual disease and anchors the standardization of CRS. Near-complete cytoreduction (CCR-0/CCR-1; residual tumour < 2.5 mm) is consistently associated with improved survival. Long-term follow-up of large CRS/HIPEC cohorts showed markedly worse outcomes after incomplete cytoreduction compared with CCR-0/CCR-1 [20, 21]. The histopathological classification of PMP is strongly prognostic. In 2016, the Peritoneal Surface Oncology Group International (PSOGI) issued a consensus nomenclature that stratifies PMP into four categories: acellular mucin; low-grade mucinous carcinoma peritonei (LMCP, also termed DPAM); high-grade mucinous carcinoma peritonei (HMCP, also termed PMCA); and HMCP with signet ring cells (HMCP-S, also termed PMCA-S) [22]. Patients with low-grade mucinous tumors typically progress slowly over several years and may eventually develop PMP, which describes the accumulation of mucinous ascites within the peritoneal cavity. Approximately 7% of low-grade lesions show lymph node involvement, and up to 16% may undergo dedifferentiation during the disease course, transitioning to high-grade disease [23]. In contrast, high-grade disease is more prone to systemic metastasis and is associated with poorer outcomes. These clinical differences largely reflect the underlying biological behavior and grade of the primary lesion, including moderately differentiated, poorly differentiated, and signet ring cell subtypes [21, 24].

This study further introduced a novel composite variable, LINS, based on systemic inflammation and nutritional status, defined as: LINS = 0.15982494 × NLR − 0.01581141 × HALP. The score integrates neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) and the haemoglobin–albumin–lymphocyte–platelet (HALP) index. Higher values reflect a pattern of heightened inflammation and poorer nutritional reserve. This composite measure may offer a practical way to summarize preoperative peripheral blood status relevant to prognosis.

Accumulating evidence supports a central role for inflammation in tumour invasion, metastasis, and recurrence. Inflammatory signalling can increase DNA damage and impair repair capacity, supporting tumour growth [25]. Neutrophils may facilitate tumour progression through extracellular matrix remodelling (for example via MMP-9), angiogenesis (VEGF), and suppression of anti-tumour immunity (for example via arginase-1) [26]. Cytotoxic T lymphocytes mediate tumour cell killing through perforin and granzymes [27]. Platelets can shield circulating tumour cells from immune surveillance and shear stress, promote epithelial–mesenchymal transition, and support distant spread [28]. Peripheral markers such as NLR, PLR, and SII capture aspects of systemic immune–inflammatory balance and have shown prognostic value across solid tumours [29, 30]. Nutritional status also shapes outcomes. Malnutrition weakens immune competence and is linked to adverse prognosis. PNI and HALP are commonly used indices to reflect nutritional reserve and disease severity in oncology [31]. Appendiceal PMP can have relatively favourable long-term survival after CRS/HIPEC, yet prognosis varies widely across risk strata. Individualized postoperative survival estimation remains clinically important for long-term management.

Several PMP prediction models have been proposed for risk assessment and outcome evaluation. Chandrakumaran K et al. [2] developed and validated nomograms for OS and Disease-free survival (DFS) after CRS and HIPEC in appendiceal PMP. OS prediction incorporated sex, age, tumour markers, PCI, and grade. DFS prediction emphasized tumour markers, PCI, gastrectomy, and grade. Ma R et al. [32] integrated quantitative pathology features with clinical variables to build a prognostic nomogram, improving risk stratification through combined digital pathology and clinical data. Wang B et al. [33] evaluated preoperative nutritional status in low-grade appendiceal mucinous neoplasms and found prognostic relevance for nutrition, PSS, CCR, and HIPEC.

In our analysis, 31 candidate variables were evaluated using univariable and multivariable Cox regression. Five factors were retained to construct a postoperative OS model that estimates 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival probabilities. Discrimination was strong by time-dependent ROC analysis. AUC values in the training set were 0.987, 0.890, and 0.868 at 1, 3, and 5 years. In the validation set, AUC values were 0.922, 0.929, and 0.843. Calibration showed good agreement between predicted and observed survival. The selected predictors are clinically accessible. The web-based dynamic nomogram provides a convenient interface for bedside use. Patients with higher inflammatory burden, poorer nutritional reserve, elevated CA125, higher PCI, PMCA/PMCA-S histology, and less favourable CC scores may warrant closer surveillance and earlier supportive interventions.

This study has several limitations. First, its retrospective single-centre design may introduce selection and information bias, and residual confounding could not be fully excluded. Second, preoperative PCI was partly based on clinical assessment and therefore subject to interobserver variability. Third, the median follow-up was relatively short, which may limit the reliability of 5-year survival estimates and underscores the need for longer follow-up and external multicentre validation. Fourth, only three cases with signet ring cells were available, precluding PMCA-S–specific modeling. Finally, LASSO was primarily used for feature selection, and the biological mechanisms underlying LINS require further investigation.

Conclusion

Histological subtype, preoperative PCI, CA125, LINS, and completeness of cytoreduction were independent predictors of overall survival after CRS plus HIPEC in appendiceal-origin PMP. A prognostic model incorporating these five factors showed good discrimination and calibration, supporting individualized risk stratification and postoperative management. The dynamic nomogram provides a practical tool for real-time survival estimation and can be updated as additional cases accrue, with the potential to further improve predictive accuracy in routine clinical use.

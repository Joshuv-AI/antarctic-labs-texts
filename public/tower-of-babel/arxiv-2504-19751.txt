Introduction

Tree-decompositions constitute a central pillar in algorithmic and structural graph theory.
While originally introduced by Halin
[
10
]
in 1976, this notion was rediscovered independently and developed thoroughly by Robertson and Seymour within their Graph Minors project.

Given a graph
G
, a
tree-decomposition
of
G
is a pair
\mathcal{T}=(T,\{X_{t}\}_{t\in V(T)})
consisting of a tree
T
and for each node
t
in
T
a set
X_{t}\subseteq V(G)
, called a
bag
at
t
, such that:

•

for every edge
uv\in E(G)
, there exists a node
t\in V(T)
with
\{u,v\}\subseteq X_{t}
, and

•

for every vertex
v\in V(G)
, the set
\{t\in V(T):v\in X_{t}\}
induces a non-empty connected subgraph of
T
.

Due to various applications, tree-decompositions whose bags induce “tractable” subgraphs are considered (e.g.,
[
1
,
4
,
9
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
19
]
).
There are different ways to measure the “tractability” of the subgraphs induced by bags.
One way to do so is considering certain graph parameters of those subgraphs.
For a graph parameter
p
and a tree-decomposition
\mathcal{T}=(T,\{X_{t}\}_{t\in V(T)})
of a graph
G
, we define
p(\mathcal{T})
to be
\max_{t\in V(T)}p(G[X_{t}])
; we define

{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}p(G)=\min_{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}p(\mathcal{T}^{\prime}),

where the minimum is over all tree-decompositions
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
of
G
.

The most classical example is the treewidth of a graph
G
.
The
width
of a graph is its number of vertices minus 1.
Hence the width of a given tree-decomposition is the maximum size of a bag minus 1, and the tree-width of a graph
G
, denoted by
{\rm tw}(G)
, is the minimum width of a tree-decomposition of
G
.
Tree-width is also written as
treewidth
in the literature.
Graphs with bounded treewidth have numerous nice properties.
For example, treewidth determines the minimum size of balanced separators
[
15
]
, and every property that can be expressed in monadic second order logic can be decided in linear time for graphs of bounded treewidth
[
3
]
.

Another example is the tree-independence number, which was introduced independently by Yolov
[
19
]
(under the name
\alpha
-treewidth) and Dallard, Milanič, and Štorgel
[
4
]
, measuring subgraphs induced by bags by their independence number.
The
independence number
of a graph
H
, denoted by
\alpha(H)
, is the maximum size of a stable set (i.e., a set of pairwise non-adjacent vertices).
Hence the tree-independence number of a graph
G
is the minimum
k
such that there exists a tree-decomposition of
G
such that every subgraph induced by a bag has independence number at most
k
.
Tree-independence number enjoys several features of treewidth. For example, tree-independence number is effective for solving algorithmic problems related to independence number
[
4
,
12
]
.

The third example considered in this paper is the tree-chromatic number, introduced by Seymour
[
17
]
, measuring subgraphs induced by bags by their chromatic number.
The
chromatic number
of a graph
H
, denoted by
\chi(H)
, is the smallest integer
k
such that
V(H)
can be partitioned into
k
stable sets.
Hence the tree-chromatic number of a graph
G
is the minimum
k
such that there exists a tree-decomposition of
G
such that every subgraph induced by a bag has chromatic number at most
k
.
Unlike treewidth and tree-independence number, tree-chromatic number is more subtle and less understood.

Inspired by the simple but very useful inequality
|V(G)|\leq\alpha(G)\cdot\chi(G)
, which holds for every graph
G
, Dallard et al.
[
4
]
proposed the following question addressing its tree-decomposition analog.

Question 1

(Dallard, Milanič, and Štorgel
[
4
, Question 8.4]
)
.

Does every graph
G
satisfy
{\rm tw}(G)+1\leq{\rm tree\textnormal{-}}\alpha(G)\cdot{\rm tree\textnormal{-}}\chi(G)
?

By applying the inequality
|V(H)|\leq\alpha(H)\cdot\chi(H)
to each subgraph
H
induced by a bag, it is easy to see that
{\rm tw}(G)+1\leq\alpha(G)\cdot{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(G)
and
{\rm tw}(G)+1\leq{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(G)\cdot\chi(G)
.
So if
G
is a graph with
\alpha(G)={\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(G)
or
\chi(G)={\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(G)
, then
{\rm tw}(G)+1\leq{\rm tree\textnormal{-}}\alpha(G)\cdot{\rm tree\textnormal{-}}\chi(G)
and
G
is a positive instance of
Question

1
.
In particular, perfect graphs are positive instances of
Question

1
since
\chi(G)=\omega(G)\leq{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(G)\leq\chi(G)
for every perfect graph
G
.

Even though some positive instances of
Question

1
are known, we provide a negative answer for
Question

1
in a strong form in this paper.
(See
Theorem

12
for a slightly stronger result.)

Theorem 1
.

For any functions
f\colon{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
and
h\colon{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
such that
h(x)
is non-decreasing and
h(x)=o(x\log\log x)
as
x\to\infty
,
there exists a graph
G
such that

{\rm tw}(G)>h({\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(G))\cdot f({\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(G))\text{.}

Theorem

1
shows that no upper bound of
{\rm tw}(G)
in terms of
{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(G)
and
{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(G)
can exist even if we allow the dependence of
{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(G)
to grow slightly faster than linear functions and the dependence of
{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(G)
to grow arbitrarily fast. We complement this result by the following simple observation showing that an upper bound exists if we allow the dependence of
{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(G)
to be quadratic.

Proposition 2
.

Every graph
G
satisfies

{\rm tw}(G)+1\leq{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(G)^{2}\cdot{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(G)\text{.}

Proof.

Take a tree-decomposition whose every bag induces a subgraph with independence number at most
{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(G)
.
It suffices to show that every bag contains at most
{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(G)^{2}\cdot{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(G)
vertices.
For every node
t
of the tree in the tree-decomposition, let
H_{t}
be the subgraph induced by the bag at
t
.
Note that for every
t
,
\alpha(H_{t})\leq{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(G)
and
{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(H_{t})\leq{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(G)
, so
H_{t}
has a tree-decomposition whose every bag induces a subgraph of chromatic number at most
{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(H_{t})\leq{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(G)
and hence contains at most
\alpha(H_{t})\cdot{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(G)\leq{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(G)\cdot{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(G)
vertices.
This implies that
{\rm tw}(H_{t})\leq{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(G)\cdot{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(G)-1
.
It is well known that for every
k
, if
{\rm tw}(G)\leq k
, then
\chi(G)\leq k+1
.
So
\chi(H_{t})\leq{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(G)\cdot{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(G)
.
Hence
|V(H_{t})|\leq\alpha(H_{t})\cdot\chi(H_{t})\leq{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(G)^{2}\cdot{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(G)
.
∎

We remark that in the proof of
Proposition

2
, we showed that
{\rm tw}(H_{t})\leq{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(G)\cdot{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(G)-1
for every node
t
.
It implies the following correction of
Question

1
in terms of tree-treewidth.
(Recall that
{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}{\rm tw}(G)
is the minimum
k
such that there exists a tree-decomposition of
G
such that every subgraph induced by a bag has treewidth at most
k
.)

Proposition 3
.

For every graph
G
,

{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}{\rm tw}(G)+1\leq{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(G)\cdot{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(G)\text{.}

Clearly,
{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}{\rm tw}(G)\leq{\rm tw}(G)
for every graph
G
.
In general, treewidth cannot be upper bounded by any function of tree-treewidth
[
14
]
.
On the other hand,
Proposition

3
is tight for chordal graphs
G
since the maximum clique of
G
must be contained in a bag of every tree-decomposition, implying
{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}{\rm tw}(G)+1\geq\omega(G)={\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(G)={\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(G)\cdot{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(G)
for every chordal
G
.

This paper is organized as follows.
In
Section

, we introduce a construction that enlarges a graph

to a graph
G^{\prime}
without changing the tree-chromatic number while
{\rm tw}(G^{\prime})
and

and
\alpha(G)
.
We then use this construction to provide a negative answer to

in

Completion of graphs

In this section we provide a key ingredient for constructing a negative answer to
Question

1
.

For a graph
H
, the
1-completion
of
H
is the graph obtained from
H
by, for each pair of non-adjacent vertices
u
and
v
of
H
, adding a new vertex of degree
2
adjacent to exactly
u
and
v
.
In this paper, denote the 1-completion of
H
by
C(H)
.

The first observation is that taking the 1-completion does not change the tree-chromatic number.

Lemma 4
.

If
H
is a graph with
E(H)\neq\emptyset
, then
{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(C(H))={\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(H)
.

Proof.

Since
H
is a subgraph of
C(H)
, we get that
{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(C(H))\geq{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(H)
.

To prove
{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(C(H))\leq{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(H)
, it suffices to construct a tree-decomposition
\mathcal{T}
of
C(H)
with
\chi(\mathcal{T})={\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(H)
.

For any pair of non-adjacent vertices
u
and
v
in
H
, let
a_{uv}
be the vertex of
C(H)-V(H)
adjacent to both
u
and
v
.
Let
\mathcal{T}_{H}=(T_{H},\{X_{t}\}_{t\in V(T_{H})})
be a tree-decomposition of
H
with
\chi(\mathcal{T}_{H})={\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(H)
.

We construct a new tree-decomposition from
\mathcal{T}_{H}
.
Fix
u,v\in V(H)
such that
uv\notin E(H)
.
Either (1) there exists
t_{uv}\in V(T_{H})
such that
\{u,v\}\subseteq X_{t_{uv}}
; or (2) there is no bag in
\mathcal{T}_{H}
containing both
u
and
v
.
In case (1), we add a new vertex
t^{\prime}_{uv}
adjacent to
t_{uv}
, and let
X_{t^{\prime}_{uv}}=\{u,v,a_{uv}\}
.
In case (2), we add
a_{uv}
into
X_{t}
for every
t\in V(T_{H})
; note that
a_{uv}
has degree at most 1 in the subgraph induced by the new
X_{t}
.
Clearly, this procedure yields a tree-decomposition
\mathcal{T}=(T,\{Y_{t}\}_{t\in V(T)})
of
C(H)
.

Since
E(H)\neq\emptyset
, some bag of
\mathcal{T}_{H}
contains an edge, so
\chi(\mathcal{T}_{H})\geq 2
.
For every
t\in V(T)-V(T_{H})
, the subgraph of
C(H)
induced by
Y_{t}
is a forest and hence has chromatic number at most
2\leq\chi(\mathcal{T}_{H})
.
For every
t\in V(T_{H})
, the subgraph of
C(H)
induced by
Y_{t}
is obtained from
H[X_{t}]
by adding isolated vertices or attaching leaves and hence has chromatic number at most
\max\{2,\chi(H[X_{t}])\}\leq\chi(\mathcal{T}_{H})
.
So
\chi(\mathcal{T})\leq\chi(\mathcal{T}_{H})={\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(H)
.
∎

For every graph
G
, we denote the complement of
G
by
\overline{G}
.

The second observation is that the treewidth and the tree-independence number of the 1-completion of
H
is closely related to
|V(H)|
and the independence number of

Lemma 5
.

If
H
is a graph with
|V(H)|\geq 3
, then
{\rm tw}(C(H))=|V(H)|-1
and
{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(C(H))\leq\alpha(H)
.

Proof.

Let
n=|V(H)|
.
Since
C(H)
contains
K_{n}
as a minor,
{\rm tw}(C(H))\geq{\rm tw}(K_{n})=n-1
.

To prove
{\rm tw}(C(H))\leq n-1
, we construct a tree decomposition
\mathcal{T}=(T,\{X_{t}\}_{t\in V(T)})
of
C(H)
of width
n-1
.
Let
T=K_{1,|E(\overline{H})|}
.
Let the bag of the central node of
T
be
V(H)
.
Note that there exists a bijection
\sigma
from the set of leaves of
T
to
E(\overline{H})
.
For every leaf
t
of
T
, let
X_{t}
be consisting of the ends of
\sigma(t)
and the vertex of
C(H)-V(H)
adjacent to both ends of
\sigma(t)
.
Then
\mathcal{T}=(T,\{X_{t}\}_{t\in V(T)})
is a tree-decomposition of
C(H)
of width
\max\{|V(H)|,3\}-1=n-1
.

Moreover, for every
t\in V(T)
, the subgraph of
C(H)
induced by
X_{t}
is either

or a path on three vertices, so
\alpha(\mathcal{T})=\max\{\alpha(H),2\}
.
If
\alpha(H)\geq 2
, then
\alpha(\mathcal{T})=\alpha(H)
.
If
\alpha(H)\leq 1
, then
H
is a complete graph and

consists of a single vertex, so
\alpha(\mathcal{T})=\alpha(H)
.
Hence
{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(C(H))\leq\alpha(\mathcal{T})=\alpha(H)
.
∎

Lemma 6
.

For every
n\geq 2
we have
{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(C(\overline{K_{n}}))=n-1
.

Proof.

Let
A\subset V(C(\overline{K_{n}}))
be the subset of
n
vertices in
C(\overline{K_{n}})
corresponding to the original vertices of
\overline{K_{n}}
.
Note that
A
is an independent set in
C(\overline{K_{n}})
.

We first show the lower bound
{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(C(\overline{K_{n}}))\geq n-1
.
Consider a tree decomposition
\mathcal{T}=(T,\{X_{t}\}_{t\in V(T)})
of
C(\overline{K_{n}})
with
\alpha(\mathcal{T})={\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(C(\overline{K_{n}}))
.
We may assume that no bag of
\mathcal{T}
contains
A
, for otherwise
\alpha(\mathcal{T})\geq|A|=n\geq n-1
, as desired.
So there exists an edge
t_{1}t_{2}
of
T
such that
T-t_{1}t_{2}
consists of two subtrees
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
of
T
, and there exists a partition
A_{1}\cup A_{2}\cup A_{3}
of
A
with the following properties:

•

|A_{1}|\geq 1
and every vertex in
A_{1}
appears only in bags of
T_{1}
.

•

|A_{2}|\geq 1
and every vertex in
A_{2}
appears only in bags of
T_{2}
.

•

Every vertex in
A_{3}
appears in bags of
T_{1}
and bags of
T_{2}
.

Clearly,
|A_{1}|+|A_{2}|+|A_{3}|=|A|=n
.
Moreover, the bag
X_{t_{1}}
contains all vertices in
A_{3}
, as well as the set
B
consisting of the (degree-
2
) vertices in
C(\overline{K_{n}})-A
adjacent in
C(\overline{K_{n}})
to both vertices in
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
.
Observe that
A_{3}\cup B
is an independent set in
C(\overline{K_{n}})
.
So
\alpha(\mathcal{T})\geq|A_{3}\cup B|=|A_{3}|+|B|
.
Since
|B|=|A_{1}|\cdot|A_{2}|\geq|A_{1}|+|A_{2}|-1
, where the inequalities uses that
|A_{1}|,|A_{2}|\geq 1
, we have
\alpha(\mathcal{T})\geq|B|+|A_{3}|\geq|A_{1}|+|A_{2}|+|A_{3}|-1=n-1
, as desired.

Now we show the upper bound
{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(C(\overline{K_{n}}))\leq n-1
.
The case
n=2
is trivial, so we may assume
n\geq 3
.
Let
S
be the star with
\binom{n-1}{2}+1
leaves.
We denote by
s
the central vertex of
S
, and denote the leaves of
S
by
s_{0}
and
s_{i,j}
for
1\leq i<j\leq n-1
.
Denote
A
by
\{a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{n-1}\}
.
Define
Y_{s}=(A-\{a_{0}\})\cup N_{C(\overline{K_{n}})}(a_{0})
and
Y_{s_{0}}=\{a_{0}\}\cup N_{C(\overline{K_{n}})}(a_{0})
.
For every
i,j
with
1\leq i<j\leq n-1
, define
Y_{s_{i,j}}
to be the set consisting of
a_{i},a_{j}
and the unique common neighbor of
a_{i}
and
a_{j}
in
C(\overline{K_{n}})
.
Clearly,
(S,(Y_{t})_{t\in V(S)})
is a tree-decomposition of
C(\overline{K_{n}})
.
Note that the subgraph induced by
Y_{s}
contains a perfect matching with
n-1
edges; the subgraph induced by each leaf of
S
is a star with at most
\max\{|A|-1,2\}=n-1
leaves.
So every bag induces a subgraph with independence number at most
n-1
.
This shows
{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(C(\overline{K_{n}}))\leq n-1
.
∎

Lemma 7
.

If
H
is a graph with
|V(H)|\geq 3
, then
\alpha(H)-1\leq{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(C(H))\leq\alpha(H)
.

Proof.

By

{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(C(H))\leq\alpha(H)

Since

{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(C(H))\geq 0\geq\alpha(H)-1

Negative answer to

We shall use a version of
Burling graphs

[

]
to construct a negative answer to
Question

.
This is a family of graphs, which are recursively defined as follows.
Throughout this section,
(G_{n},\mathcal{S}_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is a sequence of ordered pairs, where
G_{n}
is a graph and
\mathcal{S}_{n}
is a collection of stable sets in
G_{n}
for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, such that
G_{1}=K_{1}
and
\mathcal{S}_{1}=\{V(G_{1})\}
, and for
n\geq 2
,
G_{n}
and
\mathcal{S}_{n}
are defined as follows:

•

For each
S\in\mathcal{S}_{n-1}
, create a copy
G_{S}
of
G_{n-1}
, and let
\mathcal{S}_{S}
be the collection of stable sets of
G_{S}
consisting of the copies of the members of
\mathcal{S}_{n-1}
in
G_{S}
.

•

Define
G_{n}
to be the graph obtained from
G_{n-1}\cup\bigcup_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{n-1}}G_{S}
, by for each
S\in\mathcal{S}_{n-1}
and

, adding a vertex

adjacent to all vertices in

.

•

Define
\mathcal{S}_{n}=\{S\cup Q,S\cup\{v_{S,Q}\}:S\in\mathcal{S}_{n-1},Q\in\mathcal{S}_{S}\}
.

First, we prove that for each graph
G_{n}
in the above sequence we have
{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(G_{n})\leq 2
.

A
star forest
is a graph whose every component is a star.
Note that
K_{1}
is a star.

Lemma 8
.

For every positive integer
n
, there exists a tree-decomposition
\mathcal{T}
of
G_{n}
such that every bag of
\mathcal{T}
induces a star forest, and every member of
\mathcal{S}_{n}
is contained in a bag of
\mathcal{T}
.

Proof.

We shall prove this lemma by induction on
n
.
The case
n=1
is obvious.
So we may assume
n\geq 2
.
By the inductive hypothesis, there exists a tree-decomposition
\mathcal{T}_{n-1}=(T_{n-1},\{X_{t}\}_{t\in V(T_{n-1})})
of
G_{n-1}
such that every bag of
\mathcal{T}_{n-1}
is a star forest, and for every
S\in\mathcal{S}_{n-1}
, there exists
t_{S}\in V(T_{n-1})
with
S\subseteq X_{t_{S}}
.

For every
S\in\mathcal{S}_{n-1}
, let
\mathcal{T}_{S}=(T_{S},\{X^{S}_{t}\}_{t\in V(T_{S})})
be a tree-decomposition
G_{S}
isomorphic to
\mathcal{T}_{n-1}
. Note that for any
S\in{\mathcal{S}}_{n-1}
and
Q\in\mathcal{S}_{S}
, there exists
t_{S,Q}\in V(T_{S})
such that
Q\subseteq X^{S}_{t_{S,Q}}
.
For every
S\in{\mathcal{S}}_{n-1}
, we construct a tree
T_{S}^{\prime}
by for every
Q\in{\mathcal{S}}_{S}
, adding a new node
t^{\prime}_{S,Q}
into
T_{S}
and the edge
t^{\prime}_{S,Q}t_{S,Q}
, and define
Y_{t^{\prime}_{S,Q}}=Q\cup\{v_{S,Q}\}
.
For every
S\in{\mathcal{S}}_{n-1}
, let
Y_{t}=X^{S}_{t}\cup S
for every
t\in V(T_{S})
.

Let
T_{n}
be the tree obtained from
T_{n-1}\cup\bigcup_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{n-1}}T_{S}^{\prime}
by, for each
S\in{\mathcal{S}}_{n-1}
, adding an edge between
t_{S}
and some node of
T_{S}^{\prime}
.
Then
(T_{n},\{X_{t}\}_{t\in V(T_{n-1})}\cup\{Y_{t}\}_{S\in{\mathcal{S}}_{n-1},t\in V(T_{S}^{\prime})})
is a tree-decomposition of
G_{n}
such that every member of
\mathcal{S}_{n}
is contained in some bag.

Moreover, for every
t\in V(T_{n-1})
, we know
G_{n}[X_{t}]=G_{n-1}[X_{t}]
is a star forest by the inductive hypothesis; for any
S\in{\mathcal{S}}_{n-1}
and
t\in V(T_{S}^{\prime})
, if
t=t^{\prime}_{S,Q}
for some
Q\in\mathcal{S}_{S}
, then
G_{n}[Y_{t}]
is a star, and if
t\in V(T_{S})
, then
Y_{t}
is a union of a subset
X^{S}_{t}
of
V(G_{S})
and a subset
S
of
V(G_{n-1})
, so
Y_{t}
is a stable set in
G_{n}
.
Therefore, every bag of
(T_{n},\{X_{t}\}_{t\in V(T_{n-1})}\cup\{Y_{t}\}_{S\in{\mathcal{S}}_{n-1},t\in V(T_{S}^{\prime})})
induces a star forest in
G_{n}
.
∎

Walczak
[
18
]
proved that Burling graphs have unbounded fractional chromatic number.
This result can be stated in terms of its dual.
For a graph
G
, a function
f\colon V(G)\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
, and a subset
S
of
V(G)
, we define
f(S)
to be
\sum_{x\in S}f(x)
.

Lemma 9

(Walczak
[
18
, Page 222]
)
.

For every positive integer
k
, there exists a function
w_{k}\colon V(G_{k})\rightarrow{\mathbb{N}}
such that
w_{k}(V(G_{k}))=\frac{k+1}{2}\cdot 2^{2^{k-1}-1}
, and
w_{k}(I)\leq 2^{2^{k-1}-1}
for every stable set
I
of
G_{k}
.

For two graphs
G
and
H
a map
f\colon V(G)\to V(H)
is a
(graph) homomorphism
from
G
to
H
if
f(u)f(v)\in E(H)
for every
uv\in E(G)
.
It is well-known (see for example
[
11
, Corollary 1.8]
) that if there exists a homomorphism from
G
to
H
, then
\chi(G)\leq\chi(H)
.
An analogous statement holds for the tree-chromatic number.

Lemma 10
.

Let
G
and
H
be graphs.
If there exists a homomorphism from
G
to
H
, then
{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(G)\leq{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(H)
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{T}=(T,\{X_{t}\}_{t\in V(T)})
be a tree-decomposition of
H
such that
\chi(\mathcal{T})={\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(H)
.
Let
f
be a homomorphism from
G
to
H
.
For every
t\in V(T)
, let
X^{\prime}_{t}=\bigcup_{v\in X_{t}}f^{-1}(\{v\})
.
Note that
(T,\{X^{\prime}_{t}\}_{t\in V(T)})
is a tree-decomposition of
G
.

Since
f
is a homomorphism,
\bigcup_{x\in S}f^{-1}(\{x\})
is a stable set in
G
for every stable set
S
in
H
.
Hence
\chi(G[X^{\prime}_{t}])\leq\chi(H[X_{t}])\leq\chi(\mathcal{T})={\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(H)
for every
t\in V(T)
.
∎

Next, we construct a blowup of each
G_{k}
based on function
w_{k}
in
Lemma

9
.
That is, each vertex
v\in V(G_{k})
is blown up to a set of
w_{k}(v)
pairwise non-adjacent vertices.
We summarize the crucial properties of the resulting graph
H_{k}
.

Lemma 11
.

For every positive integer
k
, there exists a graph
H_{k}
such that

1.

|V(H_{k})|=\frac{k+1}{2}\cdot 2^{2^{k-1}-1}
,

2.

\alpha(H_{k})\leq\frac{2}{k+1}|V(H_{k})|<\frac{4|V(H_{k})|}{\log_{2}\log_{2}|V(H_{k})|}
, and

3.

{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(H_{k})=2
.

Proof.

Let
k
be a positive integer.
By
Lemma

9
, there exists a function
w_{k}\colon V(G_{k})\rightarrow{\mathbb{N}}
such that
w_{k}(V(G_{k}))=\frac{k+1}{2}\cdot 2^{2^{k-1}-1}
, and
w_{k}(I)\leq 2^{2^{k-1}-1}
for every stable set
I
of
G_{k}
.

For every
v\in V(G_{k})
, let
S_{v}
be a set consisting of
w_{k}(v)
pairwise non-adjacent vertices.
Define
H_{k}
to be the graph obtained from the disjoint union of
S_{v}
(for all
v\in V(G_{k})
) by adding edges such that every vertex in
S_{a}
is adjacent in
H_{k}
to every vertex in
S_{b}
for any
ab\in E(G_{k})
.
Then
|V(H_{k})|=w_{k}(V(G_{k}))=\frac{k+1}{2}\cdot 2^{2^{k-1}-1}
.

Let
A
be a stable set in
H_{k}
.
By the definition of
H_{k}
, there exists no
ab\in E(G_{k})
such that
A\cap S_{a}\neq\emptyset
and
A\cap S_{b}\neq\emptyset
.
Hence there exists a stable set
I_{A}
of
G_{k}
such that
A\subseteq\bigcup_{v\in I_{A}}S_{v}
.
So
|A|\leq\sum_{v\in I_{A}}|S_{v}|=w_{k}(I_{A})\leq 2^{2^{k-1}-1}
.

Therefore,
\alpha(H_{k})\leq 2^{2^{k-1}-1}=\frac{2}{k+1}|V(H_{k})|
.
Since
k\geq 1
, we have
\log_{2}\log_{2}(k+1)\leq k
.
Hence

\log_{2}\log_{2}|V(H_{k})|=\log_{2}(\log_{2}(k+1)-1)+\log_{2}(2^{k-1}-1)<\log_{2}\log_{2}(k+1)+k-1\leq 2k.

Therefore,
\alpha(H_{k})\leq\frac{2}{k+1}|V(H_{k})|<\frac{4|V(H_{k})|}{\log_{2}\log_{2}|V(H_{k})|}
.

By
Lemma

8
, there exists a tree-decomposition
\mathcal{T}
of
G_{k}
such that every bag induces a star forest.
So
{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(G_{k})\leq\chi(\mathcal{T})\leq 2
.
Since the function
\phi\colon V(H_{k})\rightarrow V(G_{k})
mapping each vertex
h\in V(H_{k})
to the vertex
v\in V(G_{k})
such that
h\in S_{v}
is a homomorphism from
H_{k}
to
G_{k}
, we have
{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(H_{k})\leq{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(G_{k})\leq 2
by
Lemma

10
.
Since
E(H_{k})\neq\emptyset
, every tree-decomposition of
H_{k}
has a bag that contains an edge, so
{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(H_{k})\geq 2
.
This shows
{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(H_{k})=2
.
∎

Using the 1-completion
C(H_{k})
of the graphs
H_{k}
from
Lemma

11
, we are ready to prove our main result from which
Theorem

1
will easily follow.

Theorem 12
.

For any functions
f\colon{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
and
g\colon{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
with
g(x)=o(\log\log x)
as
x\to\infty
, there exists a graph
G
such that

{\rm tw}(G)>{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(G)\cdot f({\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(G))\cdot g({\rm tw}(G)).

Proof.

Suppose to the contrary that
{\rm tw}(G)\leq{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(G)\cdot f({\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(G))\cdot g({\rm tw}(G))
for every graph
G
.

For every positive integer
k
, let
H_{k}
be the graph mentioned in
Lemma

11
.
Then
{\rm tw}(C(H_{k}))\leq{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(C(H_{k}))\cdot f({\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(C(H_{k})))\cdot g({\rm tw}(C(H_{k})))
.
When
k\geq 2
, we have
|V(H_{k})|\geq 3
, so
Lemmas

4
and
5
imply

\displaystyle|V(H_{k})|-1={\rm tw}(C(H_{k}))

\displaystyle\leq{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(C(H_{k}))\cdot f({\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(C(H_{k})))\cdot g({\rm tw}(C(H_{k})))

\displaystyle\leq\alpha(H_{k})\cdot f({\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(H_{k}))\cdot g(|V(H_{k})|-1).

By
Lemma

11
,

|V(H_{k})|-1\leq\alpha(H_{k})\cdot f({\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(H_{k}))\cdot g(|V(H_{k})|-1)\leq\frac{4|V(H_{k})|}{\log_{2}\log_{2}|V(H_{k})|}\cdot f(2)\cdot g(|V(H_{k})|-1).

Since
|V(H_{k})|\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
by
Lemma

11
, we know

\displaystyle 0<\frac{1}{4f(2)}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{4f(2)}(1-\frac{1}{|V(H_{k})|})

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{g(|V(H_{k})|-1)}{\log_{2}\log_{2}|V(H_{k})|}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{g(|V(H_{k})|-1)}{\log_{2}\log_{2}(|V(H_{k})|-1)}=0,

a contradiction.
∎

Now we deduce
Theorem

1
from
Theorem

12
.

Corollary 13
.

For any functions
f\colon{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
and
h\colon{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
such that
h(x)/x
is non-decreasing and
h(x)=o(x\log\log x)
as
x\to\infty
, there exists a graph
G
such that

{\rm tw}(G)>h({\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(G))\cdot f({\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(G)).

Proof.

Let
g(x)=h(x)/x
for every
x>0
.
Then
g(x)=o(\log\log x)
as
x\to\infty
.
By
Theorem

12
, there exists a graph
G
such that
{\rm tw}(G)>{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(G)\cdot f({\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(G))\cdot g({\rm tw}(G))\geq{\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(G)\cdot f({\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(G))\cdot g({\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(G))=h({\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(G))\cdot f({\rm tree}\textnormal{-}\chi(G))
.
∎

Weak Harnack estimates for a doubly nonlinear nonlocal p p -Laplace equation Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author.
Bin Shang and Chao Zhang ∗

Abstract
. We establish a new type of weak Harnack estimates with optimal parabolic tail for the weak supersolutions to a doubly nonlinear nonlocal p p -Laplace equation, which is modeled on the nonlocal Trudinger equation. Our results are achieved by employing the expansion of positivity and measure theoretical techniques. In particular, the weak Harnack estimates highlight the nonlocal feature, as we only require the local positivity of weak supersolutions instead of the global one.

1.
Introduction

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be an open set and
T>0
. In this paper, we investigate the nonnegative weak supersolutions to the following doubly nonlinear nonlocal
p
-Laplace equations

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(|u|^{p-2}u)+\mathcal{L}u=0\quad\text{in }E_{T}=E\times(0,T],

(1.1)

with
p>1
and
\mathcal{L}
being the nonlocal
p
-Laplace operator defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}u(x,t)=\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}K(x,y,t)|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|^{p-2}(u(x,t)-u(y,t))\,dy.

The notation P.V. typically denotes the Cauchy principal value and the kernel function
K(x,y,t):\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T]\rightarrow[0,\infty)
fulfills that

\displaystyle\frac{\Lambda^{-1}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\leq K(x,y,t)\equiv K(y,x,t)\leq\frac{\Lambda}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\quad\mathrm{a.e.}\ x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

for some
s\in(0,1)
and
\Lambda\geq 1
. When
K(x,y,t)=\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}
, Eq. (
1.1
) is nothing but the nonlocal
Trudinger equation

\partial_{t}(|u|^{p-2}u)+(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}u=0,

and it is the standard nonlocal heat equation if
p=2
.

1.1.
Function spaces and definitions

For
p>1
and
s\in(0,1)
, we introduce the fractional Sobolev space
W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
as

\displaystyle W^{s,p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right):=\left\{v\in L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dxdy<\infty\right\},

endowed with the norm

\displaystyle\|v\|_{W^{s,p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|v|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

In general, the above definitions also apply to the fractional Sobolev space
W^{s,p}(E)
when the domain
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and we define the corresponding seminorm as

\displaystyle[v]_{W^{s,p}(E)}=\left(\int_{E}\int_{E}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

The fractional Sobolev space with zero boundary values is defined as

\displaystyle W_{0}^{s,p}(E):=\left\{v\in W^{s,p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right):v=0\text{ a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash E\right\}.

We also recall the tail space

\displaystyle L_{sp}^{p-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\left\{v\in L_{\rm{loc}}^{p-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|v(x)|^{p-1}}{1+|x|^{N+{sp}}}\,dx<+\infty\right\},\quad p>1\text{ and }\alpha>0,

and the nonlocal parabolic tail is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tail}(v;x_{0},R;t_{0}-S,t_{0}):=\left[{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{t_{0}-S}^{t_{0}}\left(R^{sp}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash K_{R}(x_{0})}\frac{|v(x,t)|^{p-1}}{|x-x_{0}|^{N+sp}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{q}{p-1}}\,dt\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}

(1.2)

for some
q>p-1
. Moreover, we denote

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathrm{Tail}}(v;x_{0},R;t_{0}-S,t_{0}):=\left[\int_{t_{0}-S}^{t_{0}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash K_{R}(x_{0})}\frac{|v(x,t)|^{p-1}}{|x-x_{0}|^{N+sp}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{q}{p-1}}\,dt\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}.

It is apparent that
\mathrm{Tail}(v;x_{0},R;t_{0}-S,t_{0})
is well-defined whenever
v\in L^{q}(t_{0}-S,t_{0};L_{sp}^{p-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
.

We now give the definition of weak solutions that we work with.

Definition 1.1
.

A function

\displaystyle u\in C_{\mathrm{loc}}(0,T;L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(E))\cap L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(0,T;W^{s,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(E))\cap L^{p-1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(0,T;L_{sp}^{p-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))

is termed a local weak sub(super-) solution to (
1.1
) if for any compact set
K\subset E
and any closed interval
[t_{1},t_{2}]\subset(0,T]
, the following inequality holds

\displaystyle\int_{K}|u|^{p-2}u\varphi\,dx\Big|_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{K}-|u|^{p-2}u\partial_{t}\varphi\,dxdt+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\mathcal{E}(u,\varphi,t)\,dt\leq(\geq)0,

for each nonnegative test function
\varphi\in W_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1,p}(0,T;L^{p}(K))\cap L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(0,T;W_{0}^{s,p}(K))
, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(u,\varphi,t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}K(x,y,t)|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|^{p-2}(u(x,t)-u(y,t))(\varphi(x,t)-\varphi(y,t))\,dydx.

A function
u
is said to be a weak solution provided that
u
is both a local weak supersolution and a local weak subsolution.

1.2.
Overview of related literature

Eq. (
1.1
) can be regarded as a nonlocal version of the famous Trudinger equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(|u|^{p-1}u)-\mathrm{div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)=0,\quad p>1,

(1.3)

which is commonly classified as a more general class called doubly nonlinear parabolic equations, characterized by the nonlinearity present in both the time derivative and spatial gradient terms. The doubly nonlinear parabolic equations are of considerable interest because the mathematical structure encompasses mixed cases of degeneracy/singularity as well as their widespread applications, such as in shallow water flows
[
2
]
, the dynamics of glaciers
[
31
]
, and the model of flow in a gas-network dominated by friction
[
26
]
. Further details on these models can be found in
[
6
]
. For the study of Harnack’s estimates in doubly nonlinear equations, some significant results have been achieved. We refer the readers to
[
5
,
10
,
27
,
38
,
39
,
9
,
34
,
6
]
and reference therein. The homogeneous form of doubly nonlinear equations is Eq. (
1.3
), first introduced by Trudinger
[
37
]
in the study of a Harnack inequality for nonnegative weak solutions, utilizing Moser’s iteration. This work was later extended to Trudinger’s equation with more general operators in
[
16
]
by proving mean value inequalities and incorporating Moser’s logarithmic estimates. For what concerns the problem with a doubling Borel measure, Kinnunen-Kuusi
[
23
]
developed a relatively simple approach to derive Harnack’s estimates. With this result and the intrinsic scaling method, the Hölder continuity of nonnegative weak solutions to (
1.3
) was subsequently investigated in
[
25
]
for the degenerate case and in
[
24
]
for the singular case. Alternatively, Bögelein-Duzaar-Liao
[
8
]
employed the expansion of positivity to study the interior and boundary regularity of weak solutions to (
1.3
) without any sign restriction. See also
[
7
,
35
,
30
]
for more related works.

Coming to the linear framework of (
1.1
) with
p=2
, namely

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u-\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}K(x,y,t)(u(x,t)-u(y,t))\,dy=0,

(1.4)

a weak Harnack inequality for globally positive solutions was proved in
[
14
]
and
[
18
]
, under different conditions on the kernel function
K(x,y,t)
. Afterward, Strömqvist
[
36
]
removed the requirement of global positivity for weak solutions, leading to the nonlocal version of the Harnack inequality; see
[
22
]
for the results concerning (
1.4
) with continuous data. Regarding the jumping kernels are not symmetric, a weak Harnack inequality and a Harnack inequality including the tail term were presented in
[
19
]
and
[
20
]
, respectively. For the linear mixed local and nonlocal parabolic equations, Garain-Kinnunen
[
15
]
obtained a weak Harnack inequality for sign-changing supersolutions through a purely analytical argument. It is worth mentioning that the tail terms in the aforementioned results are bounded with respect to the time variable. Very recently, Kassmann-Weidner
[
21
]
proposed a more natural definition of the tail in the parabolic setting as follows

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}-R^{2s}}^{t_{0}}\operatorname{Tail}(v(t);R,x_{0})\,dt=\int_{t_{0}-R^{2s}}^{t_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash K_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)}\frac{|v(x,t)|}{\left|x-x_{0}\right|^{N+2s}}\,dxdt,

(1.5)

and the improved local boundedness for subsolutions as well as a weak Harnack inequality for supersolutions with this
L^{1}
-tail were proved, which together imply the Harnack estimate. Moreover, the authors demonstrated the Hölder continuity for weak solutions under a hypothesis that the tail belongs to
L^{1+\varepsilon}
in time and provided an example to show this assumption is optimal. Based on the tail definition (
1.5
), Liao
[
29
]
derived several novel weak Harnack estimates of weak supersolutions to (
1.4
) relying on the measure theoretical approach that captured some nonlocal features previously overlooked, even in the context of elliptic problems. Besides, under certain integrability conditions on the time variable of the parabolic tail, the regularity results of weak solutions to the nonlocal
p
-Laplace parabolic equations were established in
[
11
]
and
[
28
]
.

Let us turn to the nonlocal Trudinger type equation (
1.1
). As far as we know, the first contribution to the regularity theory was due to Banerjee-Garain-Kinnunen
[
3
]
. They obtained local boundedness estimates for the range
2<p<\infty
and established a valuable algebraic inequality to treat the nonlocal term in deriving a reverse Hölder inequality. Building on this algebraic inequality and the expansion of positivity, Nakamura
[
32
]
discussed a Harnack inequality for the doubly nonlinear parabolic equation in the mixed local and nonlocal scenario. Prasad
[
33
]
proved a weak Harnack estimate by imposing the global nonnegativity assumption on the weak solutions to (
1.1
). For certain local properties, including the lower semicontinuity and pointwise estimates of supersolutions to general doubly nonlinear nonlocal parabolic equations, please refer to
[
4
]
.

Note that the form of the weak Harnack estimate obtained in
[
33
]
is analogous to the one of the local Trudinger equation, owing to the assumption that weak supersolutions are globally nonnegative. To emphasize the role of nonlocal operators, our work aims to establish a new type of weak Harnack estimates for locally positive weak supersolutions to (
1.1
), under a regularity requirement on the tail with respect to the time variable. Consequently, our results will incorporate an optimal tail term, which can better exhibit the nonlocal features. Inspired by
[
11
,
29
]
, we employ the measure theoretical method and a suitable De Giorgi iteration to address the difficulties posed by the nonlocal term. It is remarked that the main idea is to derive an energy estimate for weak solutions to (
1.1
), with the remainder of the proof in this work only relying on such energy estimates and selecting appropriate test functions, without using the equation anymore. In addition, we also establish a weak Harnack inequality in the time-space cylinders with a higher integral exponent than the known results.

1.3.
Main results

Throughout the paper, let
\mathscr{Q}:=K_{R}(x_{0})\times(T_{1},T_{2}]
denote a reference cylinder included in
E_{T}
.

We state our first main result about a weak Harnack inequality as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
p>1
and
u
be a local weak supersolution to (
1.1
) that satisfies
u\geq 0
in
\mathscr{Q}
. Assume further that
u\in L^{q}_{\rm{loc}}(0,T;L^{sp}_{p-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
with
q>p-1
. Suppose
K_{4\rho}(x_{0})\times(t_{0},t_{0}+4(8\rho)^{sp}]\subset\mathscr{Q}
. Then there exist constants
\vartheta,\eta
in
(0,1)
depending only on
N,p,s,q,\Lambda
, such that

\displaystyle\quad\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{\frac{sp}{p-1}}\mathrm{Tail}(u_{-};x_{0},R;t_{0},t_{0}+4(8\rho)^{sp})+\operatorname*{ess\inf}_{K_{2\rho}(x_{0})}u(\cdot,t)

\displaystyle\geq\eta\left({\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{K_{\rho}(x_{0})}u^{\beta}(\cdot,t_{0})\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}

for all times

\displaystyle t\in\left(t_{0}+\frac{1}{2}(8\rho)^{sp},t_{0}+2(8\rho)^{sp}\right].

The upcoming statement is a weak Harnack estimate that improves upon the integral power of the first one, with its form presented in the time-space cylinders.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
p>1
and
u
be a local weak supersolution to (
1.1
) that satisfies
u\geq 0
in
\mathscr{Q}
. Assume further that
u\in L^{q}_{\rm{loc}}(0,T;L^{sp}_{p-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
with
q>p-1
. Suppose
K_{2\rho}(x_{0})\times(t_{0}-(2\rho)^{sp},t_{0}+4(4\rho)^{sp}]\subset\mathscr{Q}
. Then there exists a constant
\eta\in(0,1)
depending only on
N,p,s,q,\Lambda
, such that

\displaystyle\quad\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{\frac{sp}{p-1}}\mathrm{Tail}(u_{-};x_{0},R;t_{0}-(2\rho)^{sp},t_{0}+4(4\rho)^{sp})+\operatorname*{ess\inf}_{K_{\rho}(x_{0})}u(\cdot,t)

\displaystyle\geq\eta\left({\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}\!\!{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{K_{\rho}(x_{0})\times(t_{0}-\rho^{sp},t_{0})}u^{p-1}\,dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}

for all times

\displaystyle t\in\left(t_{0}+\frac{3}{4}(4\rho)^{sp},t_{0}+(4\rho)^{sp}\right].

Remark 1.4
.

If we assume
u\geq m>0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T)
, then by following a proof similar to that of Proposition 3.3 in
[
32
]
, the exponent
p-1
in Theorem
1.3
can be improved up to
\sigma
, where
\sigma<\frac{N+sp}{N}(p-1)
.

Remark 1.5
.

It is worth noting that the weak Harnack inequality in Theorem
1.3
does not imply the one in Theorem
1.2
, since Theorem
1.3
requires the solution property for
t<t_{0}
, whereas Theorem
1.2
does not.

The plan of this paper is as follows. In Section
2
, we collect some basic notations and auxiliary tools. The energy estimate and several preliminary lemmas will be given in Section
3
. Section
4
is devoted to showing the expansion of positivity which plays an important role in deriving our main results. Finally, we establish the weak Harnack estimates Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
in Section
5
.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Notation

We first display some standard notations that will be needed. Let
K_{\rho}(x_{0})
denote the ball with radius
\rho
centered at
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. For
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle(x_{0},t_{0})+Q_{\rho}(\theta):=K_{\rho}(x_{0})\times(t_{0}-\theta\rho^{sp},t_{0}).

We shall omit
(x_{0},t_{0})
from the above symbol when there is no ambiguity. The scaling parameter
\theta

\theta=1
.

For

, let

\displaystyle(u-k)_{+}=\max\{u-k,0\}\quad\textmd{and}\quad(u-k)_{-}=\max\{-(u-k),0\}.

We use the notation

\displaystyle d\mu=d\mu(x,y,t)=K(x,y,t)\,dxdy

\displaystyle A_{\pm}(u;x_{0},\rho;k)=\{x\in B_{\rho}(x_{0}):(u(x,t)-k)_{\pm}>0\}.

The generic constants will be denoted by
\gamma
and may differ from line to line.

2.2.
Technical lemmas

For
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
, we define functions
\bm{h}_{\pm}
as

\displaystyle\bm{h}_{\pm}(a,b):=\pm(p-1)\int_{b}^{a}|s|^{p-2}(s-a)_{\pm}\,ds.

(2.1)

It can be easily checked that
\bm{h}_{\pm}\geq 0
.

The following lemma is an inequality retrieved from
[
1
, Lemma 2.2]
for the case
0<\alpha<1
and from
[
17
, inequality (2.4)]
for the case
\alpha>1
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For all
\alpha>0
, there exists a constant
\gamma(\alpha)>0
, such that whenever
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\gamma}\left||b|^{\alpha-1}b-|a|^{\alpha-1}a\right|\leq(|a|+|b|)^{\alpha-1}|b-a|\leq\gamma\left||b|^{\alpha-1}b-|a|^{\alpha-1}a\right|.

By Lemma
2.1
, the following inequality holds.

Lemma 2.2

(Lemma 2.2,
[
11
]
)
.

Let
p\geq 1
. There exists a constant
\gamma(p)>0
, such that whenever
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\gamma}(|a|+|b|)^{p-2}(a-b)_{\pm}^{2}\leq\bm{h}_{\pm}(a,b)\leq\gamma(|a|+|b|)^{p-2}(a-b)_{\pm}^{2}.

We now give an embedding lemma, extracted from
[
11
, Lemma 2.2]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
s\in(0,1]
,
p\geq 1
and
1<m\leq\max\{p,2\}
. For each function

3.
Crucial lemmas

In this section, several quantitative estimates will be given. We begin with a Caccioppoli-type inequality, for which the proof follows a similar approach to that in
[
8
,
28
,
11
]
. The proof is omitted here.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
p>1
and
u
be a local weak subsolution to (
1.1
). Suppose the cylinder
(t_{0}-S,t_{0})\times K_{R}(x_{0})
is included in
E_{T}
. Let
\psi(\cdot,t)\in(0,1]
be a piecewise smooth cutoff function compactly supported in
K_{R}(x_{0})
for any
t\in\left(t_{0}-S,t_{0}\right)
. There exists a constant
\gamma(p,\Lambda)>0
, such that for any
k\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname*{ess\sup}_{t_{0}-S<t<t_{0}}\int_{K_{R}(x_{0})\times\{t\}}\bm{h}_{+}(u,k)\psi^{p}(x,t)\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{t_{0}-S}^{t_{0}}\int_{K_{R}(x_{0})}\int_{K_{R}(x_{0})}\min\left\{\psi^{p}(x,t),\psi^{p}(y,t)\right\}\frac{\left|w_{+}(x,t)-w_{+}(y,t)\right|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dxdydt

\displaystyle+\int_{t_{0}-S}^{t_{0}}\int_{K_{R}(x_{0})}\psi^{p}w_{+}(x,t)\int_{K_{R}(x_{0})}\frac{w_{-}^{p-1}(y,t)}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dydxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\gamma\int_{t_{0}-S}^{t_{0}}\int_{K_{R}(x_{0})}\bm{h}_{+}(u,k)|\partial_{t}\psi^{p}|\,dxdt+\int_{K_{R}(x_{0})\times\{t_{0}-S\}}\bm{h}_{+}(u,k)\psi^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle+\gamma\int_{t_{0}-S}^{t_{0}}\int_{K_{R}(x_{0})}\int_{K_{R}(x_{0})}\max\left\{w_{+}^{p}(x,t),w_{+}^{p}(y,t)\right\}\frac{|\psi(x,t)-\psi(y,t)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dxdydt

\displaystyle+\gamma\int_{t_{0}-S}^{t_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash K_{R}(x_{0})}\int_{K_{R}(x_{0})}\frac{w_{+}^{p-1}(y,t)}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}w_{+}(x,t)\psi^{p}(x,t)\,dxdydt,

(3.1)

where
w(x,t):=u(x,t)-k
and the function
\bm{h}_{+}
is defined as in (
2.1
).

The next one is a De Giorgi-type lemma, saying that if some measure density condition arrives, then a pointwise estimate can be obtained in a smaller cylinder.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
p>1
and
u
be a local weak supersolution satisfying
u\geq 0
in
\mathscr{Q}
. Assume further that
u\in L^{q}_{\rm{loc}}(0,T;L^{sp}_{p-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
with
q>p-1
. Suppose
M>0
,
\delta\in(0,1)
, and
(x_{0},t_{0})+Q_{2\rho}(\delta)\subset\mathscr{Q}
. If there holds

\displaystyle|\{u\leq M\}\cap(x_{0},t_{0})+Q_{\rho}(\delta)|\leq\nu|Q_{\rho}(\delta)|

for some constant
\nu\in(0,1)
depending on
N,p,s,q,\Lambda
and
\delta
, then we have either

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{\frac{sp}{p-1}}\mathrm{Tail}(u_{-};x_{0},R;t_{0}-\delta\rho^{sp},t_{0})>M,

or

\displaystyle u\geq\frac{1}{2}M\quad\text{ a.e. in }(x_{0},t_{0})+Q_{\frac{1}{2}\rho}(\delta).

Moreover, the constant
\nu
can be more precisely written as
\nu=\bar{\nu}\delta^{l}
with some
\bar{\nu}\in(0,1)
and
l>0
depending on
N,p,s,q,\Lambda
.

Proof.

Assume that
(x_{0},t_{0})=(0,0)
. For
j=0,1,2,\ldots
, taking

\displaystyle k_{j}=\frac{M}{2}+\frac{M}{2^{j+1}},\quad\widehat{k_{j}}=\frac{k_{j}+k_{j+1}}{2},\quad\overline{k}_{j}=\frac{k_{j+1}+\widehat{k}_{j}}{2}

and

\displaystyle\rho_{j}=\frac{\rho}{2}+\frac{\rho}{2^{j}},\quad\widehat{\rho}_{j}=\frac{\rho_{j}+\rho_{j+1}}{2}.

Consider the domains

\displaystyle K_{j}=K_{\rho_{j}},\quad\widehat{K}_{j}=K_{\widehat{\rho}_{j}},\ \ j=0,1,2\ldots,

and

) in

\displaystyle\leq\int_{K_{\rho}\times\{0\}}\int_{u}^{M}|s|^{p-2}(s-M)_{-}\,ds\psi^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\quad+\gamma\iint_{Q}\int_{K_{\rho}}\max\left\{(u-M)_{-}^{p}(x,t),(u-M)_{-}^{p}(y,t)\right\}\frac{|\psi(x)-\psi(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dxdydt

\displaystyle\quad+\gamma\int_{0}^{\delta\rho^{sp}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash K_{\rho}}\int_{K_{\rho}}\frac{(u-M)_{-}^{p-1}(y,t)}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}(u-M)_{-}(x,t)\psi^{p}(x,t)\,dxdydt.

\displaystyle\iint_{Q}\int_{K_{\rho}}\max\left\{(u-M)_{-}^{p}(x,t),(u-M)_{-}^{p}(y,t)\right\}\frac{|\psi(x)-\psi(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dxdydt\leq\gamma\frac{\delta M^{p}}{\sigma^{p}}|K_{\rho}|.

\displaystyle\quad\int_{0}^{\delta\rho^{sp}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash K_{\rho}}\int_{K_{\rho}}\frac{(u-M)_{-}^{p-1}(y,t)}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}(u-M)_{-}(x,t)\psi^{p}(x,t)\,dxdydt

\displaystyle\quad\int_{K_{\rho}\times\{t\}}\int_{u}^{M}|s|^{p-2}(s-M)_{-}\,ds\psi^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq(1-\alpha)|K_{\rho}|\int_{0}^{M}|s|^{p-2}(s-M)_{-}\,ds+\gamma\frac{\delta M^{p}}{\sigma^{N+p}}|K_{\rho}|.

Hereafter, the conclusion can be derived following the same steps as in the proof of Lemma 4.4 in

4.
Expansion of positivity

This section concerns the expansion of positivity, which is a key ingredient in establishing weak Harnack estimates.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
p>1
and
u
be a local weak supersolution to (
1.1
) that satisfies

in
\mathscr{Q}
. Assume additionally that
u\in L^{q}_{\rm{loc}}(0,T;L^{sp}_{p-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
with

. Suppose constants

and
\alpha\in(0,1]
. If we have

\displaystyle\left|\left\{u(\cdot,t_{0})\geq M\right\}\cap K_{\rho}(x_{0})\right|\geq\alpha|K_{\rho}|,

then one can find a constant
\eta\in(0,1)
depending only on
N,p,s,q,\Lambda
and
\alpha
such that either

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{\frac{sp}{p-1}}\mathrm{Tail}\left(u_{-};x_{0},R;t_{0},t_{0}+4(8\rho)^{sp}\right)>\eta M,

\displaystyle u\geq\eta M\quad\text{ a.e. in }K_{2\rho}\left(x_{0}\right)\times\left(t_{0}+\frac{1}{2}(8\rho)^{sp},t_{0}+2(8\rho)^{sp}\right],

. Moreover, we can express

as

with some

and

For simplicity, let
(x_{0},t_{0})=(0,0)

) as

\displaystyle\left|\left\{u(\cdot,0)\geq M\right\}\cap K_{4\rho}\right|\geq 4^{-N}\alpha|K_{\rho}|.

According to Lemma
3.4
, if enforcing

\displaystyle\widetilde{\gamma}\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{\frac{sp}{p-1}}\mathrm{Tail}(u_{-};0,R;0,\delta(4\rho)^{sp})\leq M,

(4.2)

with a constant

and

in terms of

such that

\displaystyle\left|\{u(\cdot,t)\geq\varepsilon M\}\cap K_{4\rho}\right|\geq\frac{\alpha}{2}4^{-N}\left|K_{4\rho}\right|\quad\text{ for all }t\in\left(0,\delta(4\rho)^{sp}\right].

Observe that the following holds true

\displaystyle(0,\bar{t})+Q_{4\rho}\left(\frac{1}{2}\delta\right)\subset K_{4\rho}\times\left(0,\delta(4\rho)^{sp}\right]

for any time

\displaystyle\bar{t}\in\left(\frac{1}{2}\delta(4\rho)^{sp},\delta(4\rho)^{sp}\right].

(4.3)

Consequently, we can utilize Lemma
3.5
in the cylinder
(0,\bar{t})+Q_{4\rho}\left(\frac{1}{2}\delta\right)
whenever
\bar{t}
is in the time interval (
4.3
) with
M
and
\alpha
replaced by
\varepsilon M
and
\frac{1}{2}4^{-N}\alpha
. At this stage, we shall select
\sigma
from Lemma
3.5
such that

\displaystyle\gamma\frac{\sigma^{p-1}}{\delta\alpha}\leq\nu,\quad\text{ i.e., }\quad\sigma=\left(\frac{\nu\delta\alpha}{\gamma}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}=\left(\frac{\bar{\nu}\bar{\delta}^{l+1}\alpha^{(N+p+1)(l+1)+1}}{\gamma}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}},

where the constant

and

are fixed in Lemma
3.2
and Lemma
3.4
, respectively. Then we arrive at

\displaystyle\left|\{u\leq\sigma\varepsilon M\}\cap(0,\bar{t})+Q_{4\rho}\left(\frac{1}{2}\delta\right)\right|\leq\nu\left|Q_{4\rho}\left(\frac{1}{2}\delta\right)\right|,

whenever there holds

\displaystyle\widetilde{\gamma}\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{\frac{sp}{p-1}}\mathrm{Tail}(u_{-};0,R;0,\delta(4\rho)^{sp})\leq\sigma\varepsilon M.

(4.4)

Subsequently, let us evoke Lemma
3.2
in the cylinder
(0,\bar{t})+Q_{4\rho}\left(\frac{1}{2}\delta\right)
with

replaced by
\sigma\varepsilon M

(4.5)

where we enforced (
4.4

New More Efficient A-WENO Schemes
Shaoshuai Chu, Alexander Kurganov, Ruixiao Xin

Abstract
We develop new more efficient A-WENO schemes for both hyperbolic systems of conservation laws and nonconservative hyperbolic systems. The new schemes are a very simple modification of the existing A-WENO schemes: They are obtained by a more efficient evaluation of the high-order correction terms. We conduct several numerical experiments to demonstrate the performance of the introduced schemes.



Key words:
Finite-difference schemes, A-WENO schemes, flux globalization based well-balanced path-conservative A-WENO schemes,
finite-volume numerical fluxes, high-order correction terms.

AMS subject classification:
65M06, 76M20, 65M08, 76M12, 35L65.

1
Introduction

This paper is focused on the development of new more efficient fifth-order finite-difference (FD) alternative weighted essentially
non-oscillatory (A-WENO) scheme for both conservative and nonconservative hyperbolic systems of nonlinear PDEs.

In the one- and two-dimensional cases, hyperbolic systems of conservation laws read as

\bm{U}_{t}+\bm{F}(\bm{U})_{x}=\bm{0},

(1.1)

\bm{U}_{t}+\bm{F}(\bm{U})_{x}+\bm{G}(\bm{U})_{y}=\bm{0},

(1.2)

respectively. In (
1.1
)–(
1.2
),
x
and
y
are the spatial variables,
t
is the time,
\bm{U}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a vector of
unknowns,
\bm{F}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\bm{G}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
are nonlinear flux functions. The A-WENO schemes for
(
1.1
) and (
1.2
), introduced in
[
30
]
(also see

[
15
,
24
,
39
,
40
,
50
,
52
,
51
,
19
]
), employ finite-volume (FV) numerical fluxes without any flux splitting,
and use high-order correction terms to ensure a high-order spatial accuracy of the resulting scheme. For instance, the original A-WENO
scheme from
[
30
]
employs an upwind flux, the schemes in
[
24
,
39
,
40
,
50
,
52
]
rely on the simplest—yet very
robust—central (local Lax-Friedrichs, Rusanov) flux or its adaptive version, and the schemes in
[
51
,
19
,
15
]
are based
on central-upwind fluxes. Examples of many other FV numerical fluxes can be found in, e.g.,
[
28
,
37
,
31
,
49
]
.

In this paper, we introduce a novel approach for computing high-order correction terms in A-WENO schemes. Traditional methods require
evaluating the flux functions
\bm{F}(\bm{U})
and
\bm{G}(\bm{U})
at each grid point during every time step, which can be computationally expensive.
The proposed method leverages precomputed numerical fluxes, allowing for time savings while maintaining both accuracy and stability without
risking any additional spurious oscillations.

The proposed modification can also be applied to the nonconservative hyperbolic systems, which in the one- and two-dimensional
cases read as

\bm{U}_{t}+\bm{F}(\bm{U})_{x}=C(\bm{U})\bm{U}_{x},

(1.3)

and

\bm{U}_{t}+\bm{F}(\bm{U})_{x}+\bm{G}(\bm{U})_{y}=B(\bm{U})\bm{U}_{x}+C(\bm{U})\bm{U}_{y},

(1.4)

respectively, with
B(\bm{U})\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
and
C(\bm{U})\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
. The presence of the nonconservative products on
the right-hand side (RHS) of (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) introduces more challenges for studying (
1.3
) and
(
1.4
) both theoretically and numerically. Weak solutions of (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) can be understood in the sense of
Borel measures as it was done in
[
22
,
35
,
36
]
, but not in the sense of distributions when
\bm{U}
is discontinuous.
The concept of Borel measure solutions was utilized to develop path-conservative schemes. A wide of variety of path-conservative methods
have been introduced; see, e.g.
[
42
,
9
,
10
,
43
,
46
,
5
,
12
,
11
,
33
,
6
,
7
]
and references therein.
Recently, the second-order path-conservative central-upwind scheme from
[
11
]
has been extended to the fifth order of
accuracy in the framework of A-WENO schemes; see, e.g.,
[
18
,
17
]
. In these schemes, we first rewrite the systems (
1.3
) and
(
1.4
) in the following quasi-conservative forms:

\bm{U}_{t}+\bm{K}(\bm{U})_{x}=\bm{0},

(1.5)

where

\bm{K}(\bm{U})=\bm{F}(\bm{U})-\bm{R}(\bm{U}),\quad\bm{R}(\bm{U}):=\int\limits^{x}_{\widehat{x}}B(\bm{U}){\bm{U}}_{\xi}(\xi,t)\,{\rm d}\xi,

(1.6)

and

\bm{U}_{t}+\bm{K}(\bm{U})_{x}+\bm{L}(\bm{U})_{y}=\bm{0},

(1.7)

where

\displaystyle\bm{K}(\bm{U})=\bm{F}(\bm{U})-\bm{R}^{x}(\bm{U}),\quad\bm{R}^{x}(\bm{U}):=\int\limits^{x}_{\widehat{x}}B(\bm{U}){\bm{U}}_{\xi}(\xi,y,t)\,{\rm d}\xi,

(1.8)

\displaystyle\bm{L}(\bm{U})=\bm{G}(\bm{U})-\bm{R}^{y}(\bm{U}),\quad\bm{R}^{y}(\bm{U}):=\int\limits^{y}_{\widehat{y}}C(\bm{U}){\bm{U}}_{\eta}(x,\eta,t)\,{\rm d}\eta,

respectively. In (
1.6
) and (
1.8
),
\widehat{x}
and
\widehat{y}
are arbitrary numbers. High-order correction terms are then
computed using the point values of the global fluxes
\bm{K}
and
\bm{L}
. As in the conservative case, the point values of

\bm{K}
and
\bm{L}
need to be computed at every grid point at every time step. In this paper, we propose a more efficient
alternative: to use the already computed numerical flux values in the computation of the high-order correction terms, which leads to new
more efficient A-WENO schemes for nonconservative hyperbolic systems. The proposed A-WENO schemes are realized in the framework of flux
globalization based well-balanced (WB) path-conservative schemes, which were introduced in
[
33
]
and later applied to a variety of
shallow water models in
[
6
,
7
,
13
]
.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In §
2.1
, we briefly describe the existing one-dimensional (1-D) A-WENO
schemes and then in §
2.2
, we introduce new 1-D A-WENO schemes using an alternative, more efficient way of computing
the high-order correction terms. In §
3.1
, we describe 1-D flux globalization based WB path-conservative A-WENO
schemes, and then introduce new more efficient 1-D flux globalization based WB path-conservative A-WENO schemes in
§
3.2
. In §
4
and §
5
, we extend the 1-D A-WENO schemes introduced in §
2
and §
3

to the two-dimensional (2-D) cases. Finally, in §
6
, we test the proposed schemes on a number of conservative and nonconservative
examples. The obtained numerical results demonstrate that the new A-WENO schemes are clearly more efficient than the old ones, while
neither the accuracy nor the quality of resolution is affected by switching to the new approach.

2
Fifth-Order A-WENO Schemes for (
1.1
)

2.1
Existing A-WENO Schemes

We assume that the computational domain is covered with uniform cells
C_{j}:=[x_{j-\frac{1}{2}},x_{j+\frac{1}{2}}]
of size
x_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-x_{j-\frac{1}{2}}\equiv\Delta x
centered at

x_{j}=(x_{j-\frac{1}{2}}+x_{j+\frac{1}{2}})/2
,
j=1,\ldots,N_{x}
. We also assume that at a certain time
t\geq 0
, the point values of the computed solution,

\bm{U}_{j}(t)
, are available (in the rest of the paper, we will suppress the time-dependence of all of the indexed quantities for the sake of
brevity). In the framework of semi-discrete A-WENO schemes,
\bm{U}_{j}
are evolved in time by solving the following system of ODEs:

\frac{{\rm d}\bm{U}_{j}}{{\rm d}t}=-\frac{\bm{\mathcal{F}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\bm{\mathcal{F}}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}}{\Delta x},

(2.1)

where
\bm{\mathcal{F}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
are the fifth-order numerical fluxes, computed by

\bm{\mathcal{F}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{(\Delta x)^{2}}{24}(\bm{F}_{xx})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{7(\Delta x)^{4}}{5760}(\bm{F}_{xxxx})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}.

(2.2)

Here,
\bm{\mathcal{F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\bm{\mathcal{F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big(\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}},\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big)
stand for FV numerical fluxes, which depends on
\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}

and
\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
, which are the left- and right-sided values of
\bm{U}
at
x=x_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
computed using the WENO-Z interpolation procedure
introduced in
[
4
,
8
,
23
]
. In this paper, we have utilized one of the simplest FV numerical fluxes—the central (local
Lax-Friedrichs, Rusanov) fluxes (see
[
34
,
45
]
):

\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{\bm{F}(\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})+\bm{F}(\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})}{2}-\frac{a_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}{2}\big(\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big),

where the local speed of propagation
a_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
is estimated using the eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}(A)\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{d}(A)
of the Jacobian

A=\frac{\partial\bm{F}}{\partial\bm{U}}
:

a_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\max_{j}\Big\{\big|\lambda_{1}\big(A(\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})\big)\big|,\,\big|\lambda_{1}\big(A(\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})\big)\big|,\,\big|\lambda_{d}\big(A(\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})\big)\big|,\,\big|\lambda_{d}\big(A(\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})\big)\big|\Big\}.

Finally, the higher-order correction terms
(\bm{F}_{xx})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
and
({\bm{F}_{xxxx}})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
are the second- and fourth-order spatial
numerical derivatives of
\bm{F}
at the cell interface
x=x_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
, which are computed using the following FD approximations:

\displaystyle(\bm{F}_{xx})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{1}{48(\Delta x)^{2}}\left(-5\bm{F}_{j-2}+39\bm{F}_{j-1}-34\bm{F}_{j}-34\bm{F}_{j+1}+39\bm{F}_{j+2}-5\bm{F}_{j+3}\right),

(2.3)

\displaystyle(\bm{F}_{xxxx})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{1}{2(\Delta x)^{4}}\left(\bm{F}_{j-2}-3\bm{F}_{j-1}+2\bm{F}_{j}+2\bm{F}_{j+1}-3\bm{F}_{j+2}+\bm{F}_{j+3}\right),

where
\bm{F}_{j}:=\bm{F}(\bm{U}_{j})
.

2.2
New A-WENO Schemes

In this section, we provide an alternative, more efficient way of computing the high-order correction terms, which are now
obtained by applying fourth- and second-order central difference formulae to evaluate
(\bm{F}_{xx})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
and
({\bm{F}_{xxxx}})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
,
respectively:

\displaystyle(\bm{F}_{xx})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{1}{12(\Delta x)^{2}}\Big[-\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j-\frac{3}{2}}+16\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}-30\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}+16\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{3}{2}}-\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{5}{2}}\Big],

(2.4)

\displaystyle(\bm{F}_{xxxx})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{1}{(\Delta x)^{4}}\Big[\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j-\frac{3}{2}}-4\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}+6\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-4\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{3}{2}}+\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{5}{2}}\Big].

Notice that the main difference between (
2.4
) and (
2.3
) is that in (
2.4
), we use FD approximations of
(\bm{F}_{xx})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
and

({\bm{F}_{xxxx}})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
terms with the help of the FV numerical fluxes
\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
instead of the point values
\bm{F}_{j}
.
Clearly, the new A-WENO schemes are less computationally expensive than the original ones as they do not require computing the point values

\bm{F}_{j}
, while the values
\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
have to be computed in (
2.2
) and then can be stored. At the same time, the new
A-WENO schemes are still fifth-order accurate and they can achieve the same high resolution as the original A-WENO schemes as demonstrated
in §
6
, where we present several numerical examples.

Remark 2.1

The new high-order correction terms can be extended to higher than the fifth order of accuracy via higher-order Taylor
expansions; see, e.g.,
[
25
]
for details on the seventh- and ninth-order A-WENO schemes.

Remark 2.2

It should be pointed out that the use of higher-order correction terms (
2.3
) may lead to the appearance of spurious oscillations; see, e.g.,
[
3
,
1
,
2
]
. We would like to stress that the use of the proposed higher-order correction terms (
2.4
) does not help to suppress these oscillations. The way to reduce them was proposed in
[
3
,
1
,
2
]
.

3
Flux Globalization Based Well-Balanced Path-Conservative A-WENO Schemes for (
1.3
)

In this section, we extend the A-WENO scheme presented in §
2
to the nonconservative systems (
1.3
) via a flux globalization
based framework.

3.1
Flux Globalization Based A-WENO Schemes

We begin with the extension of A-WENO schemes to the nonconservative systems (
1.3
), written in the quasi-conservative form (
1.5
)–(
1.6
).
A direct generalization of the semi-discrete A-WENO scheme (
2.1
)–(
2.2
) reads as

\frac{{\rm d}\bm{U}_{j}}{{\rm d}t}=-\frac{\bm{\mathcal{K}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\bm{\mathcal{K}}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}}{\Delta x},

(3.1)

where
\bm{\mathcal{K}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
are the fifth-order numerical fluxes:

\bm{\mathcal{K}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\bm{{\cal K}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{(\Delta x)^{2}}{24}(\bm{K}_{xx})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{7(\Delta x)^{4}}{5760}(\bm{K}_{xxxx})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}.

Here,
\bm{{\cal K}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\bm{{\cal K}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big(\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}},\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big)
stand for FV numerical fluxes and in this
paper, we use the central (local Lax-Friedrichs, Rusanov) fluxes

\bm{{\cal K}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{\bm{K}\big(\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big)+\bm{K}\big(\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big)}{2}-\frac{a_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}{2}\big(\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big)

(3.2)

with the local speeds of propagation estimated using the eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}({\cal A})\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{d}({\cal A})
of the matrix

{\cal{A}}=\frac{\partial\bm{F}}{\partial\bm{U}}(\bm{U})-B(\bm{U})
:

a_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\max_{j}\Big\{\big|\lambda_{1}\big({\cal A}(\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})\big)\big|,\,\big|\lambda_{1}\big({\cal A}(\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})\big)\big|,\,\big|\lambda_{d}\big({\cal A}(\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})\big)\big|,\,\big|\lambda_{d}\big({\cal A}(\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})\big)\big|\Big\}.

The global fluxes
\bm{K}^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\bm{K}(\bm{U}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{\pm})
in (
3.2
) are to be computed in such a way to ensure both WB and
path-conservative properties of the resulting scheme. To this end, we follow the approach introduced in
[
33
]
. We first note that
(
1.3
) admits steady-state solutions satisfying

\bm{F}(\bm{U})_{x}-B(\bm{U})\bm{U}_{x}=\bm{0}.

(3.3)

As it was shown in
[
33
]
(also see
[
6
,
7
,
13
]
), for many particular nonconservative systems the relation
(
3.3
) can be written as

\bm{F}(\bm{U})_{x}-B(\bm{U})\bm{U}_{x}=M(\bm{U})\bm{E}(\bm{U})_{x}=\bm{0},

(3.4)

where
M\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
and
\bm{E}
is the vector of equilibrium variables. If
M
is invertible (which is the case in many
practically interesting cases), the steady states satisfy the relation

\bm{E}(\bm{U})\equiv\mbox{\bf Const}.

Taking this into account, we evaluate
\bm{K}^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
as follows:

\bm{K}^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\bm{F}^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\bm{R}^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}},

(3.5)

where
\bm{F}^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}:=\bm{F}(\bm{U}^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})
, and the point values
\bm{U}^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
are to be obtained using a reconstruction of the
equilibrium variables
\bm{E}
to ensure the WB property. To this end, we first compute the point values
\bm{E}_{j}:=\bm{E}(\bm{U}_{j})
and then apply
the WENO-Z interpolation to evaluate
\bm{E}^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
. The corresponding values
\bm{U}^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
are then computed by solving the (nonlinear
systems of) equations

\bm{E}(\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})=\bm{E}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\bm{E}(\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})=\bm{E}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}

for
\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
and
\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
, respectively. The point values of the global variable
\bm{R}^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
in (
3.5
) are computed in
a path-conservative way. We first select
\widehat{x}=x_{\frac{1}{2}}
, set
\bm{R}^{-}_{\frac{1}{2}}:=\bm{0}
, evaluate

\bm{R}^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}}=\bm{B}_{\bm{\Psi},{\frac{1}{2}}},

(3.6)

and then recursively obtain

\bm{R}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\bm{R}^{+}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}+\bm{B}_{j},\quad\bm{R}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\bm{R}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}+\bm{B}_{\bm{\Psi},{j+\frac{1}{2}}},\quad j=1,\ldots,N_{x}.

(3.7)

In (
3.6
) and (
3.7
),
\bm{B}_{j}
and
\bm{B}_{\bm{\Psi},{j+\frac{1}{2}}}
are obtained using (
3.4
) and proper quadratures:

\displaystyle\bm{B}_{j}=\int\limits_{C_{j}}B\big(\bm{U}\big)\bm{U}_{x}\,{\rm d}x=\bm{F}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-}-\bm{F}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{+}-\int\limits_{C_{j}}M\big(\bm{U}\big)\bm{E}(\bm{U})_{x}\,{\rm d}x,

(3.8)

\displaystyle\bm{B}_{\bm{\Psi},{j+\frac{1}{2}}}=\int\limits^{1}_{0}B\big(\bm{\Psi}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}(s)\big)\bm{\Psi}^{\prime}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}(s)\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle=\bm{F}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}-\bm{F}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-}-{\frac{1}{2}}\left[M\big(\bm{U}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}\big)+M\big(\bm{U}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-}\big)\right]\big(\bm{E}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}-\bm{E}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-}\big),

where
\bm{\Psi}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}(s):=\bm{\Psi}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}(s;\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}},\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})
is a sufficiently smooth path connecting the left- and right-sided states

\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\bm{U}\big(\bm{E}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big)
and
\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\bm{U}\big(\bm{E}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big)
. We refer the readers to
[
21
]
for more
details on the derivation of (
3.8
).

In order to ensure the designed flux globalization based WB path-conservative A-WENO scheme is fifth-order accurate, the integral
\bm{B}_{j}

in (
3.8
) needs to be evaluated using at least a fifth-order quadrature. In this paper, we use the Newton-Cotes quadrature introduced
in
[
18
, (4.4)]
.

Finally,
(\bm{K}_{xx})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
and
({\bm{K}_{xxxx}})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
are approximations of the second- and fourth-order spatial derivatives of
\bm{K}

at
x=x_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
, which can be computed using the same FD approximations as in (
2.3
), namely, by

\displaystyle(\bm{K}_{xx})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{1}{48(\Delta x)^{2}}\left(-5\bm{K}_{j-2}+39\bm{K}_{j-1}-34\bm{K}_{j}-34\bm{K}_{j+1}+39\bm{K}_{j+2}-5\bm{K}_{j+3}\right),

\displaystyle(\bm{K}_{xxxx})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{1}{2(\Delta x)^{4}}\left(\bm{K}_{j-2}-3\bm{K}_{j-1}+2\bm{K}_{j}+2\bm{K}_{j+1}-3\bm{K}_{j+2}+\bm{K}_{j+3}\right),

where
\bm{K}_{j}=\bm{F}(\bm{U}_{j})-\bm{R}_{j}(\bm{U})
and

{\bm{R}_{j}}(\bm{U})=\int\limits_{\widehat{x}}^{x_{j}}B({\bm{U}(\xi,t)})\bm{U}_{\xi}(\xi,t)\,{\rm d}\xi.

(3.9)

In order to obtain a fifth-order numerical scheme, the integral in (
3.9
) needs to be evaluated within the fifth order of accuracy.
We refer the reader to, e.g.,
[
17
]
for the fifth-order Newton-Cotes quadrature, which should be implemented in a
recursive way.

3.2
More Efficient Flux Globalization Based A-WENO Schemes

In this section, we extend a new, more efficient A-WENO scheme from §
2.2
to the nonconservative system (
1.3
). We now compute
the high-order correction terms
(\bm{K}_{xx})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
and
({\bm{K}_{xxxx}})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
with the help of the same central difference formulae (as was used in §
2.2
) to the numerical fluxes:

\displaystyle(\bm{K}_{xx})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{1}{12(\Delta x)^{2}}\Big[-\bm{{\cal K}}^{\rm FV}_{j-\frac{3}{2}}+16\bm{{\cal K}}^{\rm FV}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}-30\bm{{\cal K}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}+16\bm{{\cal K}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{3}{2}}-\bm{{\cal K}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{5}{2}}\Big],

(3.10)

\displaystyle(\bm{K}_{xxxx})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{1}{(\Delta x)^{4}}\Big[\bm{{\cal K}}^{\rm FV}_{j-\frac{3}{2}}-4\bm{{\cal K}}^{\rm FV}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}+6\bm{{\cal K}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-4\bm{{\cal K}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{3}{2}}+\bm{{\cal K}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{5}{2}}\Big].

Notice that here we do not need to evaluate the point values
\bm{K}_{j}
and, in particular, we do not need to evaluate the integrals in
(
3.9
). Therefore, the resulting new version of the flux globalization based WB path-conservative A-WENO scheme is substantially less
computationally expensive than the scheme from §
3.1
.

Remark 3.1

As in the conservative case, the presented flux globalization based WB path-conservative A-WENO scheme can be straightforwardly extended to
higher than the fifth order of accuracy via higher-order Taylor expansions.

4
Fifth-Order A-WENO Schemes for (
1.2
)

4.1
Existing Two-Dimensional A-WENO Schemes

We assume that the computational domain is covered with uniform cells
C_{j,k}:=[x_{j-\frac{1}{2}},x_{j+\frac{1}{2}}]\times[y_{k-\frac{1}{2}},y_{k+\frac{1}{2}}]
with

x_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-x_{j-\frac{1}{2}}\equiv\Delta x
and
y_{k+\frac{1}{2}}-y_{k-\frac{1}{2}}\equiv\Delta y
centered at
(x_{j},y_{k})
,
j=1,\ldots,N_{x},k=1,\ldots,N_{y}
, with
x_{j}=(x_{j-\frac{1}{2}}+x_{j+\frac{1}{2}})/2

and
y_{k}=(y_{k-\frac{1}{2}}+y_{k+\frac{1}{2}})/2
. We also assume that at a certain time
t\geq 0
, the point values of the computed solution,
\bm{U}_{j,k}(t)
, are
available. In the framework of semi-discrete A-WENO schemes,
\bm{U}_{j,k}
are evolved in time by solving the following system of ODEs:

\frac{{\rm d}\bm{U}_{j,k}}{{\rm d}t}=-\frac{\bm{\mathcal{F}}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}-\bm{\mathcal{F}}_{{j-\frac{1}{2}},k}}{\Delta x}-\frac{\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}-\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{j,{k-\frac{1}{2}}}}{\Delta y},

(4.1)

where
\bm{\mathcal{F}}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
and
\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
are the fifth-order numerical fluxes, computed by

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{F}}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}-\frac{(\Delta x)^{2}}{24}(\bm{F}_{xx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}+\frac{7(\Delta x)^{4}}{5760}(\bm{F}_{xxxx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k},

(4.2)

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{K}}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}-\frac{(\Delta y)^{2}}{24}(\bm{G}_{yy})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}+\frac{7(\Delta y)^{4}}{5760}(\bm{G}_{yyyy})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}.

Here,
\bm{\mathcal{F}}^{\rm FV}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\bm{\mathcal{F}}^{\rm FV}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\big(\bm{U}^{-}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k},\bm{U}^{+}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\big)
and

\bm{\mathcal{G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=\bm{\mathcal{G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\big(\bm{U}^{-}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}},\bm{U}^{+}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\big)
stand for central (local Lax-Friedrichs, Rusanov)
fluxes (see
[
34
,
45
]
):

\displaystyle\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\frac{\bm{F}(\bm{U}^{-}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k})+\bm{F}(\bm{U}^{+}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k})}{2}-\frac{a_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}}{2}\big(\bm{U}^{+}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}-\bm{U}^{-}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\big),

\displaystyle\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=\frac{\bm{G}(\bm{U}^{-}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}})+\bm{G}(\bm{U}^{+}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}})}{2}-\frac{a_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}}{2}\big(\bm{U}^{+}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}-\bm{U}^{-}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\big),

where
\bm{U}^{\pm}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
and
\bm{U}^{\pm}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
are the one-sided point values of
\bm{U}
at the cell interfaces
(x_{j+\frac{1}{2}},y_{k})
and

(x_{j},y_{k+\frac{1}{2}})
, respectively, which are computed using the 1-D WENO-Z interpolations performed in the
x
- and
y
-directions, respectively.
The local speed of propagation in the
x
- and
y
-directions,
a_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
and
a_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
, are estimated using the smallest and largest
eigenvalues of the Jacobians
A=\frac{\partial\bm{F}}{\partial\bm{U}}
and
B=\frac{\partial\bm{G}}{\partial\bm{U}}
:

\displaystyle a_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\max_{j,k}\Big\{\big|\lambda_{1}\big(A(\bm{U}^{-}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k})\big)\big|,\,\big|\lambda_{1}\big(A(\bm{U}^{+}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k})\big)\big|,\,\big|\lambda_{d}\big(A(\bm{U}^{-}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k})\big)\big|,\,\big|\lambda_{d}\big(A(\bm{U}^{+}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k})\big)\big|\Big\},

\displaystyle a_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=\max_{j,k}\Big\{\big|\lambda_{1}\big(B(\bm{U}^{-}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}})\big)\big|,\,\big|\lambda_{1}\big(B(\bm{U}^{+}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}})\big)\big|,\,\big|\lambda_{d}\big(B(\bm{U}^{-}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}})\big)\big|,\,\big|\lambda_{d}\big(B(\bm{U}^{+}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}})\big)\big|\Big\}.

Finally, the higher-order correction terms
(\bm{F}_{xx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
,
({\bm{F}_{xxxx}})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
,
(\bm{G}_{yy})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
, and

({\bm{G}_{yyyy}})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
are the second- and fourth-order spatial numerical derivatives of
\bm{F}
and
\bm{G}
at the cell interfaces

(x_{j+\frac{1}{2}},y_{k})
and
(x_{j},y_{k+\frac{1}{2}})
, respectively. They are computed using the following FD approximations:

\displaystyle(\bm{F}_{xx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\frac{1}{48(\Delta x)^{2}}\left(-5\bm{F}_{j-2,k}+39\bm{F}_{j-1,k}-34\bm{F}_{j,k}-34\bm{F}_{j+1,k}+39\bm{F}_{j+2,k}-5\bm{F}_{j+3,k}\right),

(4.3)

\displaystyle(\bm{F}_{xxxx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\frac{1}{2(\Delta x)^{4}}\left(\bm{F}_{j-2,k}-3\bm{F}_{j-1,k}+2\bm{F}_{j,k}+2\bm{F}_{j+1,k}-3\bm{F}_{j+2,k}+\bm{F}_{j+3,k}\right),

\displaystyle(\bm{G}_{yy})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=\frac{1}{48(\Delta y)^{2}}\left(-5\bm{G}_{j,k-2}+39\bm{G}_{j,k-1}-34\bm{G}_{j,k}-34\bm{G}_{j,k+1}+39\bm{G}_{j,k+2}-5\bm{G}_{j,k+3}\right),

\displaystyle(\bm{G}_{yyyy})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=\frac{1}{2(\Delta y)^{4}}\left(\bm{G}_{j,k-2}-3\bm{G}_{j,k-1}+2\bm{G}_{j,k}+2\bm{G}_{j,k+1}-3\bm{G}_{j,k+2}+\bm{G}_{j,k+3}\right),

where
\bm{F}_{j,k}:=\bm{F}(\bm{U}_{j,k})
and
\bm{G}_{j,k}:=\bm{G}(\bm{U}_{j,k})
.

4.2
New Two-Dimensional A-WENO Schemes

In this section, we provide an alternative, more efficient way of computing the high-order correction terms
(\bm{F}_{xx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
,

({\bm{F}_{xxxx}})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
,
(\bm{G}_{yy})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
, and
({\bm{G}_{yyyy}})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
, which are obtained using the same formulae as in
§
2.2
and §
3.2
:

\displaystyle(\bm{F}_{xx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\frac{1}{12(\Delta x)^{2}}\Big[-\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j-\frac{3}{2},k}+16\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{{j-\frac{1}{2}},k}-30\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}+16\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{3}{2},k}-\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{5}{2},k}\Big],

(4.4)

\displaystyle(\bm{F}_{xxxx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\frac{1}{(\Delta x)^{4}}\Big[\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j-\frac{3}{2},k}-4\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{{j-\frac{1}{2}},k}+6\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}-4\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{3}{2},k}+\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{5}{2},k}\Big],

\displaystyle(\bm{G}_{yy})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=\frac{1}{12(\Delta y)^{2}}\Big[-\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,k-\frac{3}{2}}+16\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k-\frac{1}{2}}}-30\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}+16\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,k+\frac{3}{2}}-\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,k+\frac{5}{2}}\Big],

\displaystyle(\bm{G}_{yyyy})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=\frac{1}{(\Delta y)^{4}}\Big[\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,k-\frac{3}{2}}-4\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k-\frac{1}{2}}}+6\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}-4\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,k+\frac{3}{2}}+\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,k+\frac{5}{2}}\Big].

Remark 4.1

As in the 1-D case, the new high-order correction terms can be extended to higher than the fifth order of accuracy via higher-order Taylor
expansions.

5
Flux Globalization Based Well-Balanced Path-Conservative A-WENO Schemes for (
1.4
)

In this section, we extend the 2-D A-WENO scheme presented in §
4
to the nonconservative systems (
1.4
) within the flux
globalization based framework.

5.1
Two-Dimensional Flux Globalization Based A-WENO Schemes

We first rewrite the nonconservative systems (
1.4
) in the quasi-conservative form (
1.7
)–(
1.8
). A direct generalization of
the semi-discrete A-WENO scheme (
4.1
)–(
4.2
) reads as

\frac{{\rm d}\bm{U}_{j,k}}{{\rm d}t}=-\frac{\bm{\mathcal{K}}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}-\bm{\mathcal{K}}_{{j-\frac{1}{2}},k}}{\Delta x}-\frac{\bm{\mathcal{L}}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}-\bm{\mathcal{K}}_{j,{k-\frac{1}{2}}}}{\Delta y},

(5.1)

where
\bm{\mathcal{K}}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
and
\bm{\mathcal{L}}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
are the fifth-order numerical fluxes in the
x
- and
y
-directions:

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{K}}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\bm{{\cal K}}^{\rm FV}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}-\frac{(\Delta x)^{2}}{24}(\bm{K}_{xx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}+\frac{7(\Delta x)^{4}}{5760}(\bm{K}_{xxxx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k},

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{L}}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=\bm{{\cal L}}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}-\frac{(\Delta y)^{2}}{24}(\bm{L}_{yy})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}+\frac{7(\Delta x)^{4}}{5760}(\bm{L}_{yyyy})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}.

Here,
\bm{{\cal K}}^{\rm FV}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\bm{{\cal K}}^{\rm FV}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\big(\bm{U}^{-}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k},\bm{U}^{+}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\big)
and

\bm{{\cal L}}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=\bm{{\cal L}}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\big(\bm{U}^{-}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}},\bm{U}^{+}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\big)
stand for FV numerical fluxes and
we use the central (local Lax-Friedrichs, Rusanov) fluxes

\displaystyle\bm{{\cal K}}^{\rm FV}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\frac{\bm{K}\big(\bm{U}^{-}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\big)+\bm{K}\big(\bm{U}^{+}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\big)}{2}-\frac{a_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}}{2}\big(\bm{U}^{+}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}-\bm{U}^{-}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\big),

(5.2)

\displaystyle\bm{{\cal L}}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=\frac{\bm{L}\big(\bm{U}^{-}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\big)+\bm{L}\big(\bm{U}^{+}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\big)}{2}-\frac{a_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}}{2}\big(\bm{U}^{+}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}-\bm{U}^{-}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\big),

with the local speeds of propagation estimated using the smallest and largest eigenvalues of

{\cal A}=\frac{\partial\bm{F}}{\partial\bm{U}}(\bm{U})-B(\bm{U})
and
{\cal B}=\frac{\partial\bm{G}}{\partial\bm{U}}(\bm{U})-C(\bm{U})
:

\displaystyle a_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\max_{j,k}\Big\{\big|\lambda_{1}\big({\cal A}(\bm{U}^{-}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k})\big)\big|,\,\big|\lambda_{1}\big({\cal A}(\bm{U}^{+}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k})\big)\big|,\,\big|\lambda_{d}\big({\cal A}(\bm{U}^{-}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k})\big)\big|,\,\big|\lambda_{d}\big({\cal A}(\bm{U}^{+}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k})\big)\big|\Big\},

\displaystyle a_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=\max_{j,k}\Big\{\big|\lambda_{1}\big({\cal B}(\bm{U}^{-}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}})\big)\big|,\,\big|\lambda_{1}\big({\cal B}(\bm{U}^{+}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}})\big)\big|,\,\big|\lambda_{d}\big({\cal B}(\bm{U}^{-}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}})\big)\big|,\,\big|\lambda_{d}\big({\cal B}(\bm{U}^{+}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}})\big)\big|\Big\}.

As in the 1-D case, the global fluxes
\bm{K}^{\pm}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\bm{K}(\bm{U}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{\pm})
and
\bm{L}^{\pm}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=\bm{L}(\bm{U}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{\pm})
in
(
5.2
) are to be computed in such a way to ensure both WB and path-conservative properties of the resulting scheme. To this end, we
follow the approach from
[
33
]
and describe how to compute the global fluxes
\bm{K}^{\pm}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
(
\bm{L}^{\pm}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
can be
computed in a similar manner). We first note that (
1.4
) admits steady-state solutions satisfying

\bm{F}(\bm{U})_{x}-B(\bm{U})\bm{U}_{x}=\bm{0},\quad{\rm and}\quad\bm{G}(\bm{U})_{y}-C(\bm{U})\bm{U}_{y}=\bm{0},

which for many particular nonconservative systems can be written as

\bm{F}(\bm{U})_{x}-B(\bm{U})\bm{U}_{x}=M^{x}(\bm{U})\bm{E}^{x}(\bm{U})_{x}=\bm{0},

(5.3)

where
M^{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
and
\bm{E}^{x}
is the vector of equilibrium variables. If
M^{x}
is invertible (which is the case in most
practically interesting cases), the steady states satisfy the relation

\bm{E}^{x}(\bm{U})\equiv\widehat{\bm{E}}^{x}(y)

with
\widehat{\bm{E}}^{x}
being a function of
y
only. Taking this into account, we evaluate

\bm{K}^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\bm{F}^{\pm}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}-(\bm{R}^{x})^{\pm}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k},

where
\bm{F}^{\pm}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}:=\bm{F}(\bm{U}^{\pm}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k})
, and the point values
\bm{U}^{\pm}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
are to be obtained using a 1-D
reconstruction of the equilibrium variables
\bm{E}^{x}
to ensure the WB property. To this end, we first compute the point values

\bm{E}^{x}_{j,k}:=\bm{E}^{x}(\bm{U}_{j,k})
and then apply a WENO-Z interpolation in the
x
-direction to evaluate
(\bm{E}^{x})^{\pm}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
. The
corresponding values
\bm{U}^{\pm}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
are then computed by solving the (nonlinear systems of) equations

\bm{E}^{x}(\bm{U}^{+}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k})=(\bm{E}^{x})^{+}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\bm{E}^{x}(\bm{U}^{-}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k})=(\bm{E}^{x})^{-}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}

for
\bm{U}^{+}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
and
\bm{U}^{-}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
, respectively. The point values of the global variables
(\bm{R}^{x})^{\pm}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
in
(
5.3
) are then computed in a path-conservative way. We first select
\widehat{x}=x_{\frac{1}{2}}
, set
(\bm{R}^{x})^{-}_{{\frac{1}{2}},k}:=\bm{0}
, evaluate

(\bm{R}^{x})^{+}_{{\frac{1}{2}},k}=\bm{B}_{\bm{\Psi},{\frac{1}{2}},k},

(5.4)

and then recursively obtain

(\bm{R}^{x})^{-}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=(\bm{R}^{x})^{+}_{{j-\frac{1}{2}},k}+\bm{B}_{j,k},\quad(\bm{R}^{x})^{+}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=(\bm{R}^{x})^{-}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}+\bm{B}_{\bm{\Psi},{j+\frac{1}{2}},k},

(5.5)

for
j=1,\ldots,N_{x}
,
k=1,\ldots,N_{y}.
In (
5.4
) and (
5.5
),
\bm{B}_{j,k}
and
\bm{B}_{\bm{\Psi},{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
are evaluated using
(
5.3
) and proper quadratures:

\displaystyle\bm{B}_{j,k}=\int\limits^{x_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}_{x_{j-\frac{1}{2}}}B(\bm{U})\bm{U}_{x}(x,y_{k},t)\,{\rm d}x=\bm{F}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{-}-\bm{F}_{{j-\frac{1}{2}},k}^{+}-\int\limits^{x_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}_{x_{j-\frac{1}{2}}}M^{x}(\bm{U})\bm{E}^{x}(\bm{U})_{x}(x,y_{k},t)\,{\rm d}x,

(5.6)

\displaystyle\bm{B}_{\bm{\Psi},{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\int\limits^{1}_{0}B\big(\bm{\Psi}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}(s)\big)\bm{\Psi}^{\prime}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}(s)\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle=\bm{F}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{+}-\bm{F}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{-}-{\frac{1}{2}}\left[M^{x}\big(\bm{U}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{+}\big)+M^{x}\big(\bm{U}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{-}\big)\right]\left[(\bm{E}^{x})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{+}-(\bm{E}^{x})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{-}\right],

where
\bm{\Psi}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}(s):=\bm{\Psi}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}(s;\bm{U}^{-}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k},\bm{U}^{+}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k})
is a sufficiently smooth path connecting the left- and
right-sided states
\bm{U}^{-}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\bm{U}\big(\bm{E}^{-}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\big)
and
\bm{U}^{+}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\bm{U}\big(\bm{E}^{+}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\big)
. We refer the
readers to
[
21
]
for more details on the derivation of (
5.6
).

In order to ensure the designed 2-D flux globalization based WB path-conservative A-WENO scheme is fifth-order accurate, the integral

\bm{B}_{j,k}
in (
5.6
) needs to be evaluated using at least a fifth-order quadrature. In this paper, we use the Newton-Cotes quadrature
from
[
18
]
.

Finally,
(\bm{K}_{xx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
,
({\bm{K}_{xxxx}})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
,
(\bm{L}_{yy})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
, and
({\bm{L}_{yyyy}})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
are approximations
of the second- and fourth-order spatial derivatives of
\bm{K}
and
\bm{L}
at
(x_{j+\frac{1}{2}},y_{k})
and
(x_{j},y_{k+\frac{1}{2}})
, respectively, which can be
computed using the same FD approximations as in (
4.3
), namely, by

\displaystyle(\bm{K}_{xx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\frac{1}{48(\Delta x)^{2}}\left(-5\bm{K}_{j-2,k}+39\bm{K}_{j-1,k}-34\bm{K}_{j,k}-34\bm{K}_{j+1,k}+39\bm{K}_{j+2,k}-5\bm{K}_{j+3,k}\right),

\displaystyle(\bm{K}_{xxxx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\frac{1}{2(\Delta x)^{4}}\left(\bm{K}_{j-2,k}-3\bm{K}_{j-1,k}+2\bm{K}_{j,k}+2\bm{K}_{j+1,k}-3\bm{K}_{j+2,k}+\bm{K}_{j+3,k}\right),

\displaystyle(\bm{L}_{yy})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=\frac{1}{48(\Delta y)^{2}}\left(-5\bm{L}_{j,k-2}+39\bm{L}_{j,k-1}-34\bm{L}_{j,k}-34\bm{L}_{j,k+1}+39\bm{L}_{j,k+2}-5\bm{L}_{j,k+3}\right),

\displaystyle(\bm{L}_{yyyy})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=\frac{1}{2(\Delta y)^{4}}\left(\bm{L}_{j,k-2}-3\bm{L}_{j,k-1}+2\bm{L}_{j,k}+2\bm{L}_{j,k+1}-3\bm{L}_{j,k+2}+\bm{L}_{j,k+3}\right),

where
\bm{K}_{j,k}=\bm{F}(\bm{U}_{j,k})-\bm{R}^{x}_{j,k}(\bm{U})
,
\bm{L}_{j,k}=\bm{G}(\bm{U}_{j,k})-\bm{R}^{y}_{j,k}(\bm{U})
, and

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\bm{R}^{x}_{j,k}}(\bm{U})=\int\limits_{\widehat{x}}^{x_{j}}B({\bm{U}(x,y_{k},t)})\bm{U}_{x}(x,y_{k},t)\,{\rm d}x,\quad{\bm{R}^{y}_{j,k}}(\bm{U})=\int\limits_{\widehat{y}}^{y_{k}}C({\bm{U}(x_{j},y_{k},t)})\bm{U}_{y}(x_{j},y,t)\,{\rm d}y.

(5.7)

In order to obtain a fifth-order numerical scheme, the integrals in (
5.7
) need to be evaluated within the fifth order of accuracy. We
refer the reader to, e.g.,
[
17
]
for the fifth-order Newton-Cotes quadrature, which should be implemented in a recursive way.

5.2
More Efficient Two-Dimensional Flux Globalization Based A-WENO Schemes

In this section, we extend a new, more efficient A-WENO scheme from §
4.2
to the nonconservative system (
1.4
). We now
compute the high-order correction terms
(\bm{K}_{xx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
,
({\bm{K}_{xxxx}})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
,
(\bm{L}_{yy})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
, and

({\bm{L}_{yyyy}})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
with the help of the same central difference formulae as in §
2.2
, §
3.2
, and §
4.2
,
namely, by

\displaystyle(\bm{K}_{xx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\frac{1}{12(\Delta x)^{2}}\Big[-\bm{{\cal K}}^{\rm FV}_{j-\frac{3}{2},k}+16\bm{{\cal K}}^{\rm FV}_{{j-\frac{1}{2}},k}-30\bm{{\cal K}}^{\rm FV}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}+16\bm{{\cal K}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{3}{2},k}-\bm{{\cal K}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{5}{2},k}\Big],

(5.8)

\displaystyle(\bm{K}_{xxxx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\frac{1}{(\Delta x)^{4}}\Big[\bm{{\cal K}}^{\rm FV}_{j-\frac{3}{2},k}-4\bm{{\cal K}}^{\rm FV}_{{j-\frac{1}{2}},k}+6\bm{{\cal K}}^{\rm FV}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}-4\bm{{\cal K}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{3}{2},k}+\bm{{\cal K}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{5}{2},k}\Big],

\displaystyle(\bm{L}_{yy})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=\frac{1}{12(\Delta y)^{2}}\Big[-\bm{{\cal L}}^{\rm FV}_{j,k-\frac{3}{2}}+16\bm{{\cal L}}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k-\frac{1}{2}}}-30\bm{{\cal L}}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}+16\bm{{\cal L}}^{\rm FV}_{j,k+\frac{3}{2}}-\bm{{\cal L}}^{\rm FV}_{j,k+\frac{5}{2}}\Big],

\displaystyle(\bm{L}_{yyyy})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=\frac{1}{(\Delta y)^{4}}\Big[\bm{{\cal L}}^{\rm FV}_{j,k-\frac{3}{2}}-4\bm{{\cal L}}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k-\frac{1}{2}}}+6\bm{{\cal L}}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}-4\bm{{\cal L}}^{\rm FV}_{j,k+\frac{3}{2}}+\bm{{\cal L}}^{\rm FV}_{j,k+\frac{5}{2}}\Big].

Remark 5.1

As in the conservative case, the presented flux globalization based WB path-conservative A-WENO scheme can be straightforwardly extended to
higher than the fifth order of accuracy via higher-order Taylor expansions.

We numerically integrate the ODE systems (

In all of the numerical examples, boundary conditions are implemented using a ghost point technique. We stress that both the

All of the numerical experiments were conducted on a workstation equipped with an Intel(R) Core(TM) i7-9750H CPU at 2.6 GHz and 32 GB of
RAM. The simulations were implemented in Fortran using GCC version 14.2.0 compiler suite. The reported CPU times were averaged over thirty
independent runs to ensure reproducibility and minimize variability due to system processes.

7
Conclusions

In this paper, we have developed new more efficient fifth-order A-WENO schemes for both conservative and nonconservative nonlinear
hyperbolic systems. A higher efficiency is achieved by utilizing the numerical fluxes in the computation of high-order correction terms. We
have performed a careful numerical study of the proposed schemes and demonstrated that they may be up to 15% more efficient than the
existing A-WENO schemes and at the same time, neither accuracy of capturing smooth parts of the solutions nor the resolution of
discontinuous parts of the solution are affected by switching to the new approach.

The minimal model program for normal pairs along the log canonical locus in the complex analytic setting
Kenta Hashizume

Abstract
. We establish the minimal model theory for normal pairs along the log canonical locus in the complex analytic setting. This is the complex analytic analog of the previous result by the author [ H25 ] .

Preliminaries

Throughout this paper,

are always assumed to be Hausdorff and second countable.

Analytic varieties
are reduced and irreducible complex analytic spaces.
For notations and definitions used in this paper, see
[
F22a
]
and
[
EH24
]
.

For the algebraic analogs of the results in this section, see
[

Divisors and morphisms
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to Z
be a projective morphism from a normal analytic variety to a complex analytic space.
We will freely use the definitions of
\pi
-nef
\mathbb{R}
-divisor,
\pi
-ample
\mathbb{R}
-divisor,
\pi
-semi-ample
\mathbb{R}
-divisor, and
\pi
-pseudo-effective
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor as in
[
F22a
, Subsection 2.44]
.
The real vector space spanned by the prime divisors on
X
is denoted by
{\rm WDiv}_{\mathbb{R}}(X)
(
[
F22a
, Definition 2.34]
).
For a prime divisor
P
over
X
, the image of
P
on
X
is denoted by
c_{X}(P)
.

A
contraction

is a projective morphism of analytic varieties such that
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}\cong\mathcal{O}_{Y}
.
For an analytic variety
X
and an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
D
on
X
, a
log resolution of

(X,D)
is a proper bimeromorphic morphism
g\colon V\to X
from a smooth analytic variety
V
such that
g
is projective over a neighborhood of any compact subset of
X
, the exceptional locus
{\rm Ex}(g)
of

is of pure codimension one and
{\rm Ex}(g)\cup{\rm Supp}\,g_{*}^{-1}D
is a simple normal crossing divisor.

We refer to
[
F22a
, Definition 2.30]
for
meromorphic map
and
bimeromorphic map
.
A bimeromorphic map
\phi\colon X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
of analytic varieties is called a
bimeromorphic contraction
if
\phi^{-1}
does not contract any divisor.
We say that
\phi
is
small
if
\phi
and
\phi^{-1}
are bimeromorphic contractions.

Definition 2.1

(Property (P), see
[
F22a
]
)
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to Y
be a projective morphism of complex analytic spaces, and let
W\subset Y
be a compact subset.
In this paper, we will use the following conditions:

(P1)

X
is a normal analytic variety,

(P2)

Y
is a Stein space,

(P3)

W
is a Stein compact subset of
Y
, and

(P4)

has only finitely many connected components for any analytic subset

which is defined over an open neighborhood of
W
.

We say that
\pi\colon X\to Y
and
W\subset Y
satisfy (P)
if the conditions (P1)–(P4) hold.

Definition 2.2

(
\mathbb{R}
-line bundle, relative ampleness, relative semi-ampleness)
.

Let
X
be a complex analytic space and let
{\rm Pic}(X)
be the Picard group of

.
A
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle on

is an element of
{\rm Pic}(X)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
.
Let

be a projective morphism to a complex analytic space

.
A
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle

on

is
\pi
-ample
or

is a finite

\pi
-ample invertible sheaves on

A
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle

on

\pi

are positive rational numbers and

are globally generated over

\pi^{*}\pi_{*}\mathcal{L}_{i}\to\mathcal{L}_{i}

is surjective for all

.

Similarly, we define an
\mathbb{R}
-line bundle on

-line bundle

on

is
\pi
-ample
or
ample over

(resp.
\pi
-semi-ample
or
semi-ample over

) if

is a finite

-linear combination of invertible sheaves on

\pi
-ample (resp. globally generated over

).

Definition 2.6

(Asymptotic vanishing order,

Let

Let

For a prime divisor

, as follows:
We take a resolution

of

We put

m_{f^{*}D}:={\rm max}\left\{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\,\middle|\begin{array}[]{l}(\pi\circ f)_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}(\lfloor f^{*}D\rfloor-mP)\hookrightarrow(\pi\circ f)_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}(\lfloor f^{*}D\rfloor)\\
\text{is an isomorphism}\end{array}\right\}

is not the zero sheaf.
When

is big over

\sigma_{P}(f^{*}D;X^{\prime}/Z)_{\mathbb{Z}}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}+\infty&((\pi\circ f)_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}(\lfloor f^{*}D\rfloor)=0)\\

\sigma_{P}(D;X/Z):=\underset{m\to\infty}{\rm lim}\frac{1}{m}\sigma_{P}(mf^{*}D;X^{\prime}/Z)_{\mathbb{Z}}.

.
If

\pi
-big, then we take a
\pi
-ample
\mathbb{R}
-divisor

Singularities of pairs

In this subsection, we collect some definitions and basic results on singularities of pairs.

A

\mathbb{R}
-divisor

\mathbb{R}
-Cartier.

For a normal sub-pair

over

.

(X,\Delta)

(

over
X
.
A normal pair
(X,\Delta)

Definition 2.11

(Non-lc locus, non-klt locus)

Let

be a normal pair.

of

.

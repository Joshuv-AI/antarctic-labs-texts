Abstract
. In this paper, we study questions of definability and decidability for infinite algebraic extensions 𝐊 {\bf K} of 𝔽 p ​ ( t ) {\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t) and their subrings of 𝒮 {\mathscr{S}} -integral functions. We focus on fields 𝐊 {\bf K} satisfying a local property which we call q q - boundedness . This can be considered a function field analogue of prior work of the first author [ 72 ] which considered algebraic extensions of ℚ {\mathbb{Q}} . One simple consequence of our work states that if 𝐊 {\bf K} is a q q -bounded extension of 𝔽 p ​ ( t ) {\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t) , then for infinitely many non-constant u u the integral closure 𝒪 𝐊 {\mathscr{O}}_{\bf K} of 𝔽 p ​ [ u ] {\mathbb{F}}_{p}[u] inside 𝐊 {\bf K} is first-order definable in 𝐊 {\bf K} . Under the additional assumption that the constant subfield of 𝐊 {\bf K} is infinite, it follows that both 𝒪 𝐊 {\mathscr{O}}_{\bf K} and 𝐊 {\bf K} have undecidable first-order theories, and that 𝔽 p ​ [ w ] {\mathbb{F}}_{p}[w] is definable in 𝐊 {\bf K} for every non-constant w w in 𝐊 {\bf K} . Our primary tools are norm equations and the Hasse Norm Principle, in the spirit of Rumely. Our paper has an intersection with a recent arXiv preprint by Martinez-Ranero, Salcedo, and Utreras, although our definability results are more extensive and undecidability results are much stronger.

This paper investigates some old questions of definability and decidability in the language of rings over infinite algebraic extensions of global function fields.
Recall that a global function field is a finite extension of

Questions 1.1

Consider the following questions of definability and decidability in the first-order language of rings possibly augmented with a finite number of constants.

(1)

(2)

1.1.
Valuations and primes of global function fields

Valuations and primes of global function fields play a key role in the proofs of our results. We briefly review the relevant definitions and suggest
[
24
]
for more details.

Definition 1.6
.

Let
K
be a global function field. Then a (non-trivial discrete) valuation
v_{K}
of
K
is a map
v_{K}:K\longrightarrow{\mathbb{Q}}\cup\{\infty\}
with the following properties:

(1)

v_{K}(K^{\times})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}
(for the sake of convenience we often “renormalize”
v_{K}(K^{\times})
to be
{\mathbb{Z}}
),

(2)

v(x)=\infty
if and only if
x=0
,

(3)

For all
x,y\in K
we have
v(xy)=v(x)+v(y)
,

(4)

For all
x,y\in K
we have
v(x+y)\geq\min(v(x),v(y))
.

(5)

For all
x,y\in K
with
v(x)<v(y)
we have that
v(x+y)=v(x)
.

Let
{\mathscr{O}}_{v}\subset K
be the ring of all elements
x\in K
with
v(x)\geq 0
. One can show that
{\mathscr{O}}_{v}
is a local ring, that is
{\mathscr{O}}_{v}
has a unique maximal ideal consisting of all
x\in R_{v}
with
v(x)>0
. We will refer to this ideal as a prime of
K
and denote it by
{\mathfrak{p}}_{K}
(as well as other fraktur letters).

The algebraic closure of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
in
K
will be referred to as the constant field of
K
. It is a finite extension of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
because
K
is a finite extension of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)
. Any valuation of
K
is trivial on its constant field, that is for any constant
c\in K
we have that
v(c)=0
. The degree of the residue field of
{\mathfrak{p}}_{K}
over the constant field is called the degree of
{\mathfrak{p}}_{K}
.

The ideal
{\mathfrak{p}}_{K}
of
{\mathscr{O}}_{v}
is generated by an element
\pi_{K}
, called the uniformizer, and each
x\in K^{\times}
can be written as
x=u\pi_{K}^{n}
for some
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
u\in{\mathscr{O}}_{v}^{\times}
.
This number
n
is called the order of
x
at
{\mathfrak{p}}_{K}
and is denoted by
\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{K}}x
.
The “normalization” of the value group of
v_{K}
as above results in
v_{K}(x)=\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{K}}x
.

All valuations of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)
correspond to irreducible polynomials in
t
or to the degree of these polynomials. Given a finite extension
L/K
of global function fields, we can extend valuations of
K
to
L
. The number of such extensions can be bigger than one but it is always finite. If
v_{L}
is an extension of
v_{K}
, then
[v_{L}(L):v_{K}(K)]
is called the ramification degree of
v_{L}
over
v_{K}
. We will also denote the ramification degree by
e({\mathfrak{p}}_{L}/{\mathfrak{p}}_{K})
, where
{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}
and
{\mathfrak{p}}_{K}
are the prime ideals corresponding to
v_{L}
and
v_{K}
respectively.
Indeed, if we extend
{\mathfrak{p}}_{K}
to an ideal of the integral closure
{\mathscr{O}}_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{K},L}
of
{\mathscr{O}}_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{K}}
inside
L
, then we recognize
{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}
as a prime factor of
{\mathfrak{p}}_{K}{\mathscr{O}}_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{K},L}
.
The degree of the residue field of
v_{L}
over the residue field of
v_{K}
is called the relative degree of
v_{L}
over
v_{K}
and is denoted by
f({\mathfrak{p}}_{L}/{\mathfrak{p}}_{K})
.

If
{\bf K}
is an algebraic (possibly infinite) extension of a global function field
K,
a valuation
v_{K}
has an extension
v_{\bf K}
(possibly infinitely many extensions) to
{\bf K}
. This extension
v_{\bf K}
will satisfy all the properties from Definition
1.6
except that
v_{\bf K}({\bf K}^{\times})
may no longer be isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Z}}
. We can define the valuation ring
{\mathscr{O}}_{v_{\bf K}}
, the unique maximal ideal
{\mathfrak{p}}_{\bf K}
and its residue field as in the finite case.

If
K_{0}=K\subset K_{1}\subset\ldots
is an infinite tower of global function fields such that
{\bf K}=\bigcup_{i=0}^{\infty}K_{i}
and
v_{\bf K}
is an extension of
v_{K}
as above, then we can define
v_{K_{i}}
to be the restriction
v_{{\bf K}|K_{i}}
of
v_{{\bf K}}
to
K_{i}
and note that

•

For each
i\geq 0
,
v_{K_{i}}
is a discrete valuation on
K_{i}
extending
v_{K_{i-1}}
;

•

R_{\bf K}=\bigcup_{i=0}^{\infty}R_{v_{K_{i}}}
;

•

{\mathfrak{p}}_{\bf K}=\bigcup_{i=0}^{\infty}{\mathfrak{p}}_{K_{i}}
.

If we follow the factorization of
{\mathfrak{p}}_{K_{0}}
through the tower
K_{0}\subseteq K_{1}\subseteq\dots
, we see that its factors in subfields of the tower form a tree.
We refer to this tree as the factor tree of
{\mathfrak{p}}_{K_{0}}
.
Observe that a choice of
{\mathfrak{p}}_{\bf K}
over
{\mathfrak{p}}_{K_{0}}
corresponds precisely to a choice of path through the factor tree.

1.2.
The role of
q
-boundedness and norm equations

As we mention below the existential definability of valuation rings of global function fields has been known for a long time due to results of Rumeley. He used the uniform nature of these definitions to define polynomial rings contained in these fields. The natural question that has arisen since then is whether these definitions can be extended to infinite algebraic extensions of global fields. The notion of
q
-boundedness describes extensions where one can generalize some ideas of Rumeley to define integral closures of valuation rings and polynomial rings. One can show that in a
q
-bounded field the Brauer group is non-trivial and while we do not use central simple algebras directly, we do use norm equations closely connected to these algebras.

The importance of
q
-boundedness can be seen from its influence on the solvability of norm equations.
Let
{\bf K}
be an infinite algebraic extension of a rational function field
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)
.
For a non-
q
-th power
a
and a nonzero element
b
in
{\bf K}
, consider the solvability of the following norm equation over
{\bf K}(\sqrt[q]{a})
.

{\bf N}_{{\bf K}(\sqrt[q]{a})/{\bf K}}(y)=bx^{q}+b^{q}.

As shown in Section
3
, this norm equation corresponds to a system of polynomial equations over
\bf{K}
.
Note that we are interested in making sure that
x
is integral at all but finitely many primes.
The key assumption of
q
-boundedness implies that for any
{\bf K}
-prime
{\mathfrak{q}}_{\bf K}
there exists a finite extension
K\subset{\bf K}
containing
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)
with the following property: for any finite extension
K^{\prime}
of
K
inside
{\bf K}
, there is some
K^{\prime}
-prime
{\mathfrak{q}}_{K^{\prime}}
below
{{\mathfrak{q}}_{\bf K}}
, some
a\in K^{\prime}
and some
b\in K^{\prime}
so that
\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{q}}_{K^{\prime}}}b=-1
and
{\mathfrak{q}}_{K^{\prime}}
is inert in the extension
K^{\prime}(\sqrt[q]{a})/K^{\prime}
.
Then, if
x\in{\bf K}
has a pole at
{\mathfrak{q}}_{\bf K}
, we find that
x
has a pole at some
{\mathfrak{q}}_{K^{\prime}}
as above so that
\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{K^{\prime}}}(bx^{q}+b^{q})<0
and
\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{q}}_{K^{\prime}}}(bx^{q}+b^{q})\not\equiv 0\bmod q
. Since
{\mathfrak{q}}_{K^{\prime}}
is inert in the extension
K^{\prime}(\sqrt[q]{a})/K^{\prime}
, we can now conclude that the norm equation

(1.1)

{\bf N}_{{K^{\prime}}(\sqrt[q]{a})/{K^{\prime}}}(y)=bx^{q}+b^{q}

has no solution locally at
{\mathfrak{q}}_{K^{\prime}}
and therefore no solution globally. Further, for any sufficiently large finite extension
K^{\prime\prime}
of
K^{\prime}
we can find
a
and
b
as above to block the solution to the corresponding norm equation over
K^{\prime\prime}
. Thus, if we append
\forall a\forall b
to our norm equation we will obtain a statement which does not hold over
{\bf K}
if
x
has a pole at
{\mathfrak{p}}_{\bf K}
.
We pause to remark that, in reality, we will choose
a
and
b
to be elements from certain large subsets, although this is not conceptually crucial for the exposition here.

Although this is a good start to producing a definition of
\mathscr{S}
-integral functions, more care is required because the norm equation (
1.1
) can fail to have solutions for reasons other than poles of
x
. To make sure that the poles of
x
are the only obstruction to the solubility of the equation, we use a dynamic version of equation (
1.1
). In other words we change the fields where we try to solve the norm equation depending on
x
and
a
. In the modified field all zeros of
bx^{q}+b^{q}
and all poles and zeros of
a
are of order divisible by
q
. Thus, adjoining
q
-th root of
a
produces an unramified extension and we may apply the Hasse Norm Principle. In particular, zeros of
bx^{q}+b^{q}
have no influence on the existence of solutions to the norm equations.

2.
A brief history of the questions of definability and decidability of global fields and their algebraic extensions

The modern part of the first-order definability story starts with Julia Robinson who showed in 1949 that
{\mathbb{Z}}
had a first-order definition over
{\mathbb{Q}}
and therefore the first-order theory of
{\mathbb{Q}}
was undecidable
[
53
]
. Later she also showed that
{\mathbb{Z}}
was first-order definable over rings of integers of number fields and rings of integers of number fields are first-order definable over their fields of fractions
[
54
]
. Robinson also considered infinite algebraic extensions of
{\mathbb{Q}}
and produced first examples of integral closures of
{\mathbb{Z}}
in infinite algebraic extensions where
{\mathbb{Z}}
was first-order definable (see
[
55
]
). In the same paper she also constructed a uniform (i.e. independent of the number field in question) first-order definition of
{\mathbb{Z}}
over rings of integers of number fields.
In
[
56
]
, R. Rumely showed that a polynomial ring is first-order definable over any global function field. His definition was uniform across all global function fields and required only one absolutely necessary parameter, i.e. the parameter that indicates which polynomial ring is being defined.
Both J. Robinson and R. Rumely produced existential definitions of valuation rings, over number fields in the case of J. Robinson and all global fields in the case of R. Rumely.

In
[
9
]
J. Denef, later joined by L. Lipshitz
[
7
]
, initiated the study of existential definability and decidability over global fields and function fields of characteristic 0.
Denef and Lipshitz showed that
{\mathbb{Z}}
has an existential definition over the ring of integers of any extension of degree at most 2 of a totally real number field (
[
7
]
), and therefore H10 is undecidable over those rings. This class of fields includes all abelian extensions of
{\mathbb{Q}}
, for example.
Over number fields they were followed by a number of other people, including Pheidas
[
45
]
, Shlapentokh
[
58
]
, and Videla
[
78
]
.
In
[
48
]
, Poonen tied the ranks of elliptic curves to the definability of
{\mathbb{Z}}
.
His original idea was generalized in
[
69
]
and
[
70
]
, and eventually to a result of Mazur and Rubin (
[
35
]
) showing that Shafarevich-Tate conjecture implied that
{\mathbb{Z}}
has a Diophantine definition over rings of integers of all number fields.
Pasten and Murty in
[
42
]
showed that other well-known conjectures in Number Theory had the same implication.
Using elliptic curves, Garcia-Fritz and Pasten constructed a new family of number fields where
{\mathbb{Z}}
was definable over rings of integers (
[
25
]
).
There are also a number of other results concerning existential definability of
{\mathbb{Z}}
over the rings of algebraic numbers of finite and infinite algebraic extensions of
{\mathbb{Q}}

3.
Definability and norm equations

In this paper, we will pursue definability by using norm equations where the variables range over radical extensions of bounded degree.
The following lemma clarifies that such equations correspond to existential sentences over the base field.
This fact will be used implicitly throughout the remainder of the paper.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic prime to
q
and fix a polynomial
P(t)\in K[t]
. Let
S
be the set of tuples
(a,x,c_{1},\dots,c_{n})\in K^{n+2}
such that

(3.2)

\exists y\in L:\ {\bf N}_{L(\sqrt[q]{a})/L}(y)=P(x),

where
L=K(\sqrt[q]{c_{1}},\dots,\sqrt[q]{c_{n}})
. Then the set
S
is existentially definable in
K^{n+2}
.

Proof.

Define a tower of fields
K_{j}:=K(\sqrt[q]{c_{1}},\dots,\sqrt[q]{c_{j}})
for each
0\leq j\leq n
.
Note that this tower
K=K_{0}\subseteq K_{1}\subseteq\dots\subseteq K_{n}=L
is formed via
n
extensions generated by
q
-th roots,
and each step in the tower may or may not be a trivial extension.
By working recursively, it is clear that the property of whether
c_{i}
is a
q
-th power over
K_{i-1}
is diophantine over
K
.
Hence, we may define
S
via a disjunction of
2^{n}
terms, where each term of the disjunction corresponds to a sequence of
\textsc{Yes}/\textsc{No}
answers to whether the extension
K_{i}/K_{i-1}
is trivial for each
1\leq i\leq n
.
Therefore, it is enough to assume that
c_{i}
is a non-
q
-th power in
K_{i-1}
for all
i
. In this case,
B_{L/K}=\{\prod_{i}\sqrt[q]{c_{i}}^{v_{i}}:\vec{v}\in\{0,\dots,q-1\}^{n}\}\}
is a
K
-basis for
L
.

We remark that the meaning of the norm equation (
3.2
) depends on whether
\sqrt[q]{a}
is a
q
-th power in the base field
L
or not; cf.
[
33
]
*Theorem VI.9.1.
In the former case, the equation is trivially satisfied because
L=L(\sqrt[q]{a})
and
{\bf N}_{L/L}(P(x))=P(x)
for all
x\in L
. Therefore, we will form another disjunction where the first term is
\exists\alpha\in L:\ \alpha^{q}=a
. This formula can be reinterpreted as a system of equations over
K
by rewriting
\alpha
in terms of the basis
B_{L/K}
, expanding the product and comparing terms.

It only remains to represent the norm equation in the case where
L(\sqrt[q]{a})/L
is a non-trivial extension, so we assume that
a
is not a
q
-th power in
L
.
Any
y\in L(\sqrt[q]{a})
is written in the natural

L
-basis as
y=d_{0}+d_{1}\sqrt[q]{a}+\dots+d_{q-1}\sqrt[q]{a}^{q-1}
,
and its norm is

\prod_{j=0}^{q-1}\left(d_{0}+d_{1}\zeta_{q}^{j}\sqrt[q]{a}+\dots+d_{q-1}\zeta_{q}^{j(q-1)}\sqrt[q]{a}^{q-1}\right)

where
\zeta_{q}
is a primitive
q
-th root of unity.
In particular, the norm is clearly a polynomial over
L
in
a
and the coefficients of
y
in the natural
L
-basis
\{1,\sqrt[q]{a},\dots,\sqrt[q]{a}^{q-1}\}
.
Using this polynomial, expanding each element of
L
in (
3.2
) in terms of the basis
B_{L/K}
provides the desired formula over
K
.
∎

When we apply this lemma in practice, it is helpful for the base field
K
to contain a
q
-th root of unity, since this implies that the extension
L(\sqrt[q]{a})/L
appearing in (
3.2
) is cyclic; see
[
33
]
*Theorem VI.6.2.
For the sake of definability, this assumption on
K
can be made without loss of generality, as shown in the following proposition and corollary.
The method of proof involves essentially the same trick of rewriting equations, although we note that the following proposition has a fixed field extension, whereas the field extension varies in (
3.2
).

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
H/F
be a finite extension of fields. If
X\subset H
is a first-order definable set, then
X\cap F
is a first-order definable subset of
F
. Moreover, if
X
is existentially definable over
H
, then
X\cap F
is existentially definable over
F
.

Remark 3.3
.

The existential version of this proposition is well-known. See, for example
[
68
]
[Lemma 2.1.17].

Proof.

Let
\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}\}
be a basis for
H
over
F
, which is finite by hypothesis.
By definition, this means that every element of
H
is represented uniquely as a sum
\sum_{j=1}^{r}c_{j}\alpha_{j}
when the coefficients
c_{j}
range over elements of
F
.
We will exploit a well-established method of rewriting equations with respect to this basis.

Assume
X\subseteq H
is defined by the formula

(3.3)

E_{1}y_{1}\in H\ldots E_{s}y_{s}\in H\ P(x,\bar{y})=0,

where
P(x,\bar{y})\in H[x,\bar{y}]
and each
E_{i}
is either a universal or existential quantifier.

For each
1\leq i\leq s
, we replace the variable
y_{i}
in the polynomial
P(x,\bar{y})
with the sum
\sum_{j=1}^{r}y_{i,j}\alpha_{j}
.
By expanding the polynomial, and rewriting all of the
H
-coefficients in terms of the basis
\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}\}
as needed, we may write

P(x,\sum_{j=1}^{r}y_{1,j}\alpha_{j},\ldots,\sum_{j=1}^{r}y_{s,j}\alpha^{j})=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\alpha_{j}Q_{j}(x,y_{1,1},\dots,y_{s,r}).

where each
Q_{j}
is a polynomial with coefficients in
F
.
Because
\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}\}
is an
F
-basis for
H
, we observe that (
3.3
) is equivalent to

(3.4)

E_{1}y_{1,1}\in F\dots E_{1}y_{1,r}\in F\dots E_{s}y_{s,r}\in F\ Q_{1}(x,y_{1,1},\dots,y_{s,r})=\dots=Q_{r}(x,y_{1,1},\dots,y_{s,r})=0,

This formula gives the first-order definition of
X\cap F
in
F
. Moreover, we observe that precisely the same quantifiers are used, and in the same order, except that each quantifier is now used
r
times in a row.
∎

From Proposition
3.2
we get the following corollary justifying the assumption following it.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
F
be a field of characteristic not equal to
q
and let
\zeta_{q}
be a primitive
q
-th root of unity.
If a subset
A\subseteq F(\zeta_{q})
is definable over
F(\zeta_{q})
, then
A\cap F
is definable over
F
.

Assumption 3.5
.

From now on we assume that
K
(and therefore
{\bf K}
) contains a primitive
q
-th root of unity and therefore any cyclic extension of degree
q
is generated by taking a
q
-th root of an element of
K
(or
{\bf K}
).

Recall that Lemma
3.1
defines a diophantine subset of tuples.
In the material that follows, we will combine this fact with the following lemma to obtain our main results.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
F
be a field, let
A\subset F^{3},B\subset F,C\subset F
be diophantine subsets of
F^{3}
and
F
respectively. Let

D=\{x\in F\mid\forall x_{1}\in B\forall x_{2}\in C:(x_{1},x_{2},x)\in A\}.

Then
D
is first-order definable over
F
.

Proof.

Observe that we may write
D
in the following form:

D=\{x\in F\mid\forall x_{1},x_{2}\in F:((x_{1}\not\in B)\lor(x_{2}\not\in C)\lor((x_{1},x_{2},x)\in A)\}.

∎

4.
Field extensions and the solubility of norm equations

In this section, we provide the necessary foundational results for analyzing the solubility of norm equations over global function fields and their infinite algebraic extensions.
We choose to work with norm equations and radical extensions because we can exploit the local-global principle for cyclic extensions known as the
Hasse Norm Principle
, which is recalled below.
Indeed, the cyclic extensions of a field
L
of degree
q
are precisely the extensions
L(\sqrt[q]{a})/L
generated by the
q
-th root of an element
a\in L\setminus L^{q}
, as long as
L
is a field containing a primitive
q
-th root of unity and
q
is distinct from the characteristic of
L
; see
[
33
]
*Theorem VI.6.2.
In light of Corollary
3.4
, we maintain Assumption
3.5
without loss of generality.
In particular, we assume every field contains a primitive
q
-th root of unity, so every extension generated by a
q
-th root is cyclic.

Theorem 4.1

(Hasse Norm Principle, §9,
[
75
]
)
.

Let
L/K
be a cyclic extension of global function fields. Let
x\in K
and let
{\mathscr{M}}_{L}
be the collection of all valuations of
L
. Then there exists
y\in L
such that
{\bf N}_{L/K}(y)=x
if and only if for all
v\in{\mathscr{M}}_{L}
we have that there exists
y\in L_{v}
such that
{\bf N}_{L_{v}/K_{v}}(y)=x
.

The Hasse Norm Principle implies that the solubility of a norm equation reduces to inspecting the same equation over local fields.
These local equations, in turn, can be understood with the following well-known result.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
L,K
and
v
be as above and assume
L_{v}/K_{v}
is unramified.
If
x\in K_{v}
satisfies
\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{v}(x)\equiv 0\bmod[L_{v}:K_{v}]
, then there exists
y\in L_{v}
such that
{\bf N}_{L_{v}/K_{v}}(y)=x
.

Proof.

Let
\pi_{K_{v}}\in K_{v}
be a uniformizer, and write
x=\pi_{K_{v}}^{\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{v}{x}}x_{0}
where
x_{0}
is a unit at
v
and
\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{v}(x)=n[L_{v}:K_{v}]
.
By
[
32
]
*Lemma IX.3.4, there is
y_{0}
in
L_{v}
so that
{\bf N}_{L_{v}/K_{v}}(y_{0})=x_{0}
. Thus,
x
is a norm as desired:
{\bf N}_{L_{v}/K_{v}}(\pi_{K_{v}}^{n}y_{0})=\pi_{K_{v}}^{n[L_{v}:K_{v}]}x_{0}=x
.
∎

We will apply these results to analyze equations of the form seen in Equation (
3.2
).
First, we provide a lemma describing the behavior of primes in such
q
-th root extensions.
This allows us to deduce conditions where norm equations are or are not solvable.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
L
be a global function field of characteristic
p>0
which contains a primitive
q
-th root of unity where
q\neq p
is a prime number.
Given
a\in L\setminus L^{q}
, the following statements are true concerning the extension
L(\sqrt[q]{a})/L
.

(a)

L(\sqrt[q]{a})/L
is a Galois extension of degree
q
.

(b)

If
\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}}a=0
then
{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}
splits (completely) in
L(\sqrt[q]{a})
if there exists
u\in L
such that
\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}}(a-u^{q})>0
, and is inert otherwise.

(c)

If
{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}
is a prime of
L
, then
{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}
is ramified if and only if
\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}}a\not\equiv 0\bmod q
.

Proof.

(
a
) In
L(\sqrt[q]{a})
, the polynomial
T^{q}-a
splits completely because
L
contains a primitive
q
-th root of unity. Therefore,
L(\sqrt[q]{a})/L
is Galois.

(
b
). If
{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}
splits completely in the extension
L(\sqrt[q]{a})/L
, then the polynomial
T^{q}-a
has
q
roots in
L_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}}
, that is
a
is a
q
-th power in
L_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}}
and also in the residue field
k_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}}
of
{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}
.
Thus, for any lift
u\in L
of a root of
T^{q}-a
in the residue field, we have
a-u^{q}\equiv 0\bmod{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}
.
So, we must have
\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}}(a-u^{q})>0
.
Conversely, if there is no
u\in L
such that
\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}}(a-u^{q})>0
, then there is no
u\in L_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}}
such that
u^{q}=a
.
Hence the polynomial
T^{q}-a
remains prime in
L_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}}
and the degree of the local extension at
{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}
is
q
.
In other words,
{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}
is inert in the extension
L(\sqrt[q]{a})/L
.

(
c
)
Let
{\mathfrak{p}}_{L(\sqrt[q]{a})}
be a prime of
L(\sqrt[q]{a})
above
{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}
.
Observe that

e({\mathfrak{p}}_{L(\sqrt[q]{a})}/{\mathfrak{p}}_{L})\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}}a=\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{L(\sqrt[q]{a})}}a=q\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{L(\sqrt[q]{a})}}\sqrt[q]{a}.

Therefore, if
\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}}a\not\equiv 0\bmod q
, we have that
e({\mathfrak{p}}_{L(\sqrt[q]{a})}/{\mathfrak{p}}_{L})\equiv 0\bmod q
and
{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}
is totally ramified in the extension
L(\sqrt[q]{a})/L
.
Conversely, if
\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}}a\equiv 0\bmod q
, then we define
w=\pi^{\frac{1}{q}\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}}a}
where
\pi
is a local uniformizing parameter of
L
at
{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}
.
We deduce that
q\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}}w=\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}}a
and
L(\sqrt[q]{a})=L(\sqrt[q]{aw^{-q}})
.
Then the element under the
q
-th root has order 0 at
{\mathfrak{p}}_{K}
.
Now by Part (
b
), it follows that
{\mathfrak{p}}_{K}
does not ramify in the extension
L(\sqrt[q]{a})/L
.
∎

Now we present two results which establish scenarios when norm equations do or do not have solutions, respectively.
First, we provide the key application of the Hasse Norm Principle for the purposes of our paper.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
L
be a global function field of characteristic
p>0
which contains a primitive
q
-th root of unity where
q\neq p
is a prime number.
Let
a\in L

be an element whose divisor over
L
is the
q
-th power of another
L
-divisor.
Let
z\in L
be such that for every prime
{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}
of
L
one of the following holds:

(a)

{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}
splits completely in the extension
L(\sqrt[q]{a})/L
;

(b)

\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}}z\equiv 0\bmod q
.

Under these assumptions,
z
is an
L(\sqrt[q]{a})
-norm in
L
.

Proof.

If
a
is a
q
-th power, then the extension is trivial and the claim is automatically satisfied because
{\bf N}_{L/L}(z)=z
. Thus, we assume that
a
is not a
q
-th power, which means that the extension is cyclic by Lemma
4.3
.(
a
).

By the Hasse Norm Principle (Theorem
4.1
), the norm equation
{\bf N}_{L(\sqrt[q]{a})/L}(y)=z
has a solution
y\in L(\sqrt[q]{a})
if and only if the equation is everywhere locally solvable. We obtain solubility locally everywhere by applying Proposition
4.2
, as follows.

Fix a prime
{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}
of
L
and write
M=L(\sqrt[q]{a})
. Since the divisor of
a
is the
q
-th power of another
L
-divisor, the extension
M/L
is unramified by Lemma
4.3
.(
c
).
Let
{\mathfrak{p}}_{M}
be a prime of
M
lying over the prime
{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}
of
L
.
Note that
[M_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{M}}:L_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}}]=q
unless
{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}
is split completely in
M/L
, in which case
[M_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{M}}:L_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}}]=1
.
Therefore,
\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}}z\equiv 0\bmod[M_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{M}}:L_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}}]
under either of the hypotheses on
z
, and consequently the norm equation is locally solvable at
{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}
by Proposition
4.2
.
∎

As a complement to the preceding proposition, we have the following simple observation.
In practice, the condition that the prime is inert can be verified with the Lemma
4.3
.(
b
).

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
L
be a global function field of characteristic
p>0
which contains a primitive
q
-th root of unity where
q\neq p
is a prime number.
Fix
a\in L\setminus L^{q}
and
z\in L
.
Let
{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}
be a prime of
L
which is inert in the extension
L(\sqrt[q]{a})/L
.
If
\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}}z\not\equiv 0\bmod q
, then
z
is not an
L(\sqrt[q]{a})
-norm in
L
.

Proof.

If
{\mathfrak{p}}_{L(\sqrt[q]{a})}
lies above
{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}
in
L
, then
f({\mathfrak{p}}_{L(\sqrt[q]{a})}/{\mathfrak{p}}_{L})=q
. Thus, if
z\in L
is an

We now turn our attention to infinite algebraic extensions of global function fields.
In order to apply methods for global fields to these larger fields, we require the following two results.
The first is a straightforward observation which implies that any norm equation which is solvable over a global field will continue to be solvable over any further extension, including infinite extensions.
The second result is more delicate, but can be intuitively viewed as stating that non-solvability of norm equations in an infinite degree extension is a phenomenon which can be observed in a certain finite subextension.

5.
Integrality at primes in global function fields

Throughout this section, let
K
be a global function field.
Recall that the Hasse Norm Principle, which we will use below, only applies to cyclic extensions.
Therefore, we assume throughout the section that
K
contains a primitive

-th root of unity, which implies that every extension generated by a
q
-th root is indeed cyclic.
However, we remind the reader that the definability results remain true even if

does not contain this root of unity; see Corollary

.

5.1.
Radical extensions and the behavior of primes.

Recall that, given the base global field field

We use the freedom of choosing

Our method exploits Lemma

The next three lemmas are building blocks which will be combined when defining

Lemma 5.1
.

Fix an element

and define

(a)

(b)

\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{t}}_{L}}z\equiv 0\bmod q

We now assume that

.(

Let

5.2.
Defining valuation rings in global function fields

As we have mentioned in the history section of the introduction, the existential definability of valuation rings was first established by Rumely in
[

To begin, we define the set of elements that have a large order relative to some element

6.
q
-Bounded fields

Now that we have established some definability and decidability results for finite extensions of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)
in Section
5
, we turn our attention to the class of infinite algebraic extensions of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)
which we call
q
-bounded fields.
In the introduction, we presented the concept of
global
q
-boundedness
(Definition
1.2
), whose name reflects the fact that the property concerns degrees of global extensions
\hat{K}\supseteq K\supseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)
.
In reality, the properties that we exploit are local conditions concerning primes
{\mathfrak{p}}_{K}
and their corresponding completions
K_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{K}}
, which we call local fields.
The present section is dedicated to developing this idea.
We ultimately present three equivalent ways to conceptualize
q
-boundedness; see Proposition
6.7
.

6.1.
q
-Bounded algebraic extensions of local fields

We first provide a local definition, which will pave the way for subsequently defining
q
-boundedness for algebraic extensions of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)
.
We use the term
local function field
to refer to a completion
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
, where
K
is a finite extension of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)
and
{\mathfrak{p}}
is a prime of
K
.
In the local setting,
q
-boundedness is merely a restriction on the degrees of finite subextensions.

Definition 6.1

(
q
-Bounded algebraic extensions of local function fields)
.

If
{\bf L}_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
is an algebraic extension of a local function field
K_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
, then we say that
{\mathbf{L}}_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
is
q
-bounded if there exists some
r\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
so that
\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{q}([H_{{\mathfrak{p}}}:K_{{\mathfrak{p}}}])\leq r
for every finite extension
H_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
of
K_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
contained in
{\bf L}_{\mathfrak{p}}

Equivalently,
q
-boundedness in the local setting can be formulated in terms of the existence of a certain maximal finite extension.

Lemma 6.2
.

If
{\bf L}_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
is an algebraic extension of a local function field
K_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
, then the following are equivalent

(1)

{\bf L}_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
is
q
-bounded;

(2)

There exists a finite extension

of
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
in

such that
\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{q}([E_{{\mathfrak{p}}}:H_{\mathfrak{p}}])=0
for every finite extension
E_{{\mathfrak{p}}}/H_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
contained in
{\bf L}_{{\mathfrak{p}}}

Proof.

First assume that
{\bf L}_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
is
q
-bounded. Let

By assumption

and therefore there is a field
H
such that
\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{q}[H_{{\mathfrak{p}}}:K_{{\mathfrak{p}}}]=r

and the multiplicativity of degrees in towers, it now follows that
\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{q}[E_{{\mathfrak{p}}}:H_{{\mathfrak{p}}}]=0
for every finite extension
E_{{\mathfrak{p}}}/H_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
contained in
{\bf L}_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
.

Now assume the field
H_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
exists. If

\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{q}[F_{{\mathfrak{p}}}:K_{{\mathfrak{p}}}]\leq\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{q}[F_{{\mathfrak{p}}}H_{{\mathfrak{p}}}:K_{{\mathfrak{p}}}]=\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{q}[H_{{\mathfrak{p}}}:K_{{\mathfrak{p}}}]+\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{q}[F_{{\mathfrak{p}}}H_{{\mathfrak{p}}}:H_{{\mathfrak{p}}}]=\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{q}[H_{{\mathfrak{p}}}:K_{{\mathfrak{p}}}]

∎

6.2.

-Boundedness for algebraic extensions of global function fields

Our next goal is to define

-boundedness for global function fields in terms of the local property outlined in the previous subsection.
Rather than appealing to the completions of arbitrary algebraic extensions of

Definition 6.3

(Tower of completions)

Let

Although towers of completions give one way to understand

Proposition 6.7
.

Let
K
be an algebraic extension of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)
. For any valuation
{\bf v}
of
{\bf K}
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)
such that for any finite extension

\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{q}e(v_{|L}/v_{|K})=0

\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{q}f(v_{|L}/v_{|K})=0

(2)

(3)

The valuation

\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{q}[F_{v}:{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)_{v}]=\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{q}e(v_{|F}/v_{|{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)})+\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{q}f(v_{|F}/v_{|{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)})

Therefore, the order at

of ramification and relative degrees

Assume that the second condition holds. Let

is

Finally, if

6.4.

For some of our results, we need even more control on the behavior of primes in the infinite algebraic extension

Definition 6.11

Proposition 9.1

Let

Let

be the normal closure of

over

Let

be the integral closure of

in

Then the equivalence holds in

too.
Let

be any conjugate of

Then

Since the last equivalence must hold for infinitely many

or, alternatively,

9.2.

Then by

.

Let
{\bf K}
be an
q
-bounded extension of a global function field
K
and assume there is a prime
{\mathfrak{p}}_{K}
of
K
with only finitely many factors in
{\bf K}
. Then the first order theory of
{\bf K}
in the language
\{0,1,+,\times\}
is undecidable.

Proof.

This follows from the discussion above. Namely, letting
{\mathscr{S}}_{\bf K}
be the primes of
{\bf K}
over
{\mathfrak{p}}_{K}
, we observe that the first-order theory of the ring of
{\mathscr{S}}_{\bf K}
-integral functions
{\mathscr{O}}_{K,{\mathscr{S}}_{\bf K}}
is undecidable by
[
59
]
*Theorem 5.1 and
{\mathscr{O}}_{K,{\mathscr{S}}_{\bf K}}
is definable in
{\bf K}
by Corollary
8.4
.
∎

In
[
59
]
, there is no discussion of how to construct fields admitting a prime with finitely many factors. To conclude the paper, we show that such a construction is easy, concentrating on the cases where the algebraic closure of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
in
{\bf K}
is finite. We use a construction designed to produce a prime inert in an infinite extension. In comparison, a tower of fields
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t^{1/m_{i}})
with
m_{i}|m_{i+1}
produces a totally ramified prime in the infinite extension. In either case the result of
[
59
]
*Theorem 5.1 applies, since the relevant condition is for a prime of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)
to have only finitely many distinct ideals above it in the infinite extension.

We start with two preliminary lemmas.

Lemma 9.5
.

Let
K
be a global function field and let
L
be a finite extension of
K
. Let
{\mathfrak{p}}_{K}
be a prime of
K
, let
\alpha
be a generator of
L/K
and assume that
\alpha
is integral with respect to
{\mathfrak{p}}_{K}
. Let
g(X)\in K[X]
be the monic irreducible polynomial of
\alpha
over
K
. If
g(X)
remains irreducible in the residue field of
{\mathfrak{p}}_{K}
, then the prime
{\mathfrak{p}}_{K}
is inert in the extension
L/K
.

Proof.

Let
{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}
be a prime of
L
over
{\mathfrak{p}}_{K}
. The element
\alpha
reduces to a root of
g(X)
in the residue field
\kappa_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}}
, so we have

f({\mathfrak{p}}_{L}/{\mathfrak{p}}_{K})=[\kappa_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}}:\kappa_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{K}}]=\deg(g)=[L:K]

because
g(X)
is irreducible modulo
{\mathfrak{p}}_{K}
. Therefore,
{\mathfrak{p}}_{K}
is inert in
L
by definition.
∎

Lemma 9.6
.

Let
K
be a global function field and let
P(X)=A_{0}+A_{1}X+\ldots X^{r}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)[X]
be such that there exists a prime
{\mathfrak{q}}_{K}
such that
\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{q}}_{K}}A_{0}=-1
and
\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{q}}_{K}}A_{i}\geq 0
for
i>0
. Then

(1)

P(X)
is irreducible.

(2)

If
L
is an extension of
K
generated by a root of
P(X)
, then
{\mathfrak{q}}_{K}
is completely ramified in the extension
L/K
.

Proof.

Let
z\in\bar{K}
be a root of
P(X)
. Then

\sum_{i=1}^{r}A_{i}z^{i}=-A_{0}

Let
{\mathfrak{q}}_{K(z)}
be a prime above
{\mathfrak{q}}_{K}
in
K(z)
. Then,
\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{q}}_{K(z)}}z<0
and

\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{q}}_{K(z)}}(A_{0})=\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{q}}_{K(z)}}\sum_{i=1}^{r}A_{i}z^{i}=\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{q}}_{K(z)}}z^{r}=r\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{q}}_{K(z)}}z.

Hence,
\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{q}}_{K(z)}}(A_{0})\equiv 0\bmod r
while
\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{q}}_{K}}(A_{0})=-1
. Therefore,
e({\mathfrak{q}}_{K(z)}/{\mathfrak{q}}_{K})\equiv 0\bmod r
and hence the ramification is equal to
r
implying that
P(X)
is irreducible and
{\mathfrak{q}}_{K}
is totally ramified in the extension
K(z)/K
.
∎

We now construct the desired fields extensions.
Let
{\mathfrak{p}}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)}
be the zero of
t
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)
denoted in the future by
{\mathfrak{p}}_{0}
. Let

p(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}t^{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)

be monic and irreducible over
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. Consider now a polynomial

P(t,X)=\frac{a_{0}}{t+1}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}(t+X)^{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)[X].

Modulo
{\mathfrak{p}}_{0}
, this polynomial is irreducible over
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
and therefore
P(t,X)
is irreducible over
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)
. Let
L_{1}
be the extension of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)
generated by a root of
P(t,X)
. By Lemma
9.5
, we have that
{\mathfrak{p}}_{0}
is inert in this extension. Further, since the prime corresponding to
t+1
is totally ramified in the extension
L_{1}/{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)
by Lemma
9.6
, we have that there is no constant field extension between
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)
and
L_{1}
.

Assume inductively that we have constructed an extension
L_{m}
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)
such that
{\mathfrak{p}}_{0}
is inert in this extension and the constant field of
L_{m}
is
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. Let
{\mathfrak{p}}_{m}
be the prime of
L_{m}
above
{\mathfrak{p}}_{0}
. Let
p_{m}(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{n_{m}}a_{i,m}X^{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[X]
be monic and irreducible over the residue field of
{\mathfrak{p}}_{m}
.

Observe that there are only finitely many primes ramified in the extension
L_{m}/{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)
. Therefore, there exists a positive integer
\ell
such that any
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)
-prime corresponding to a monic irreducible polynomial of degree higher than
\ell
does not have any ramified factors in
L_{m}
.
Let
q_{m}(t)
be such that
q_{m}(t)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[t]
,
\deg q_{m}(t)>\ell
, and
q_{m}(t)
is monic and irreducible over
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[t]
. Then if
{\mathfrak{q}}_{m}
is a prime of
L_{m}
lying above the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)
-prime corresponding to the polynomial
q_{m}(t)
we have that
\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{q}}_{m}}q_{m}(t)=1
. Let

P_{m}(t,X)=\frac{a_{0,m}q_{m}(0)}{q_{m}(t)}+\sum_{i=1}^{n_{m}}a_{i,m}(t+X)^{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)[X].

Then
P_{m}(t,X)\equiv p_{m}(X)
modulo
{\mathfrak{p}}_{m}
, where
p_{m}(X)
is irreducible modulo
{\mathfrak{p}}_{m}
. Therefore,
P_{m}(t,X)
is irreducible over
L_{m}
. Let
L_{m+1}
be generated by a root of
P_{m}(t,X)
. Then by Lemma
9.5
we have that
{\mathfrak{p}}_{m}
is inert in the extension
L_{m+1}/L_{m}
.

At the same time
{\mathfrak{q}}_{m}
is completely ramified in the extension
L_{m+1}/L_{m}
and therefore there is no constant field extension from
L_{m}
to
L_{m+1}
. Thus, the constant field of
L_{m+1}
is still
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
.

Let
{\bf K}=\bigcup_{i=0}^{\infty}L_{i}
. Then the constant field of
{\bf K}
is
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
and
{\mathfrak{p}}_{0}
has a unique factor in
{\bf K}
, as desired, so the construction is finished.

10.
Defining arbitrary polynomial rings over
{\bf K}

Our goal in this section is to show that if we can define one
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-polynomial ring over
{\bf K}
, then we can define all of them. In other words, we assume that we are given two non-constant elements
u
and
w
of
\bf K
such that
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[u]
has a first-order definition (with parameters) over
{\bf K}
and show that the same is true of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[w]
. We deduce that all
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-polynomials rings are definable when
{\bf K}
is a
q
-bounded extension with infinite constant field.

Our proof exploits a result of J. Demeyer (
[
5
]
) which asserts that any c.e. subset of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[u]
has an existential definition over
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[u]
. Before we state and prove the theorem, we first discuss the presentation of countable fields and rings and the notion of computable and c.e. subsets of these algebraic objects.
To discuss computability of countable algebraic objects we map these objects into
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
and translate binary operations over the object by functions from
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{2}
to
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
. We call a field or ring computable if there exists a map as described above translating the ring operations by total computable functions.

By a famous theorem of Rabin
[
51
]
, every countable field with a splitting algorithm (i.e. an algorithm to factor polynomials into irreducible factors) has a computable algebraic closure such that the domain of the field in question as a subset of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
is computable. In our case the field in question is
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)
, where
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
is a field of
p
elements and
t
is transcendental over
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. It is well-known that this field has a splitting algorithm; see
[
24
]
. Therefore, there exists a computable presentation of
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)}
such that the field
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)
is a computable subset of it. We will identify our field
{\bf K}
with a subfield of
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)}
presented as above. Note that we do not assume that
{\bf K}
is computable under this or any other presentation.

Given any transcendental element
u
of
\bf K\subset\overline{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)}
, the field
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(u)
is a computable subset of
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)}
. Indeed, the domain of the field
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(u)
is certainly c.e. since we can list all rational functions in
u
with coefficients in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. Therefore, we can list all polynomials in some variable
T
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(u)
.

Since
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)}
is of transcendence degree 1, every element
x
of
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)}
satisfies some monic polynomial
P(T)
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(u)
which we can find by going through a list of all monic polynomials with coefficients in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(u)
. Once we find a polynomial
P(T)
such that
P(x)=0
, we can use the splitting algorithm over
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(u)
to factor
P(T)
into irreducible factors over
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(u)
and determine whether one of the factors is of the form
(T-x)
. If such a linear factor exists, then
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(u)
and it is not in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(u)
otherwise.

If
w\in\overline{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)}\setminus{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(u)
, we can determine the degree of
w
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(u)
. Denoting this degree by
n
we can represent every element of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(u,w)
in the form

\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{a_{i}(u)}{b(u)}w^{i},

where
a_{i}(u),b(u)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(u)
. Since
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(w)
is also a computable field, we now have an algorithm to determine which
n+1
-tuples
(a_{0}(u),\ldots,a_{n-1}(u),b(u))\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(u)^{n+1}
correspond to elements of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(w)
when used in the linear combination above. Thus, the set

(10.15)

A=\{(a_{0}(u),\ldots,a_{n-1}(u),b(u))\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[u]^{n+1}\mid\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{a_{i}(u)}{b(u)}w^{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(w)\}

is a computable subset of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(u)
. Further, the set

(10.16)

B=\{(a_{0}(u),\ldots,a_{n-1}(u),b(u))\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[u]^{n+1}\mid\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{a_{i}(u)}{b(u)}w^{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[w]\}

is also computable. To see that
B
is computable note that we can generate a list of elements of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[w]
and the list of elements of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(w)\setminus{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[w]
using the splitting algorithm over
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. Using this discussion, we can now prove the following theorem.

Theorem 10.1
.

Let
{\bf K}
be an algebraic extension of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)
and assume that for some non-constant
u
we have a definition (with parameters) of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[u]
over
{\bf K}
. Then for any non-constant
w
we have a definition (with parameters) of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[w]
.

Proof.

Let
\psi(T)
be a first-order definition of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[u]
over
{\bf K}
.
Given the discussion of computability above, the set
B
defined in (
10.16
) has a diophantine definition
p(a_{0},\ldots,a_{n-1},b,Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{m})
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[u]
by the result of J. Demeyer (
[
5
]
).
Thus the following formula defines
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[w]
: An element
y\in{\bf K}
is contained in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[w]
if and only if
\exists a_{0},\ldots,a_{n-1},b,Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{m}\in{\bf K}
so that

\bigwedge_{i=0}^{n}\psi(a_{i})\land\psi(b)\land\bigwedge_{i=1}^{m}\psi(Y_{i})\land p(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-1},b,Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{m})=0\land y=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{a_{i}(u)}{b(u)}w^{i}.

∎

We can now apply Theorem
10.1
to the case of a
q
-bounded
{\bf K}
with an infinite field of constants to obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 10.2
.

Let
{\bf K}
be a
q
-bounded algebraic extension of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)
with an infinite constant field. Let
w
be any non-constant element of
{\bf K}
. Then
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[w]
is first-order definable over
{\bf K}
with parameters.

Proof.

By Corollary
8.5
there exists a non-constant element
u\in{\bf K}
such that
{\mathscr{O}}_{{\bf K},u}
, the integral closure of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[u]
in
{\bf K}
, is definable over
{\bf K}
.
By Corollary
9.2
, if
{\bf K}
has an infinite constant field, then
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[u]
is definable over
{\mathscr{O}}_{{\bf K},u}
, and Theorem
10.1
applies.
∎

Remark 10.3
.

From an examination of the statements of Lemma
7.1
and Theorem
7.3
it is easy to see that we need two parameters to define valuation rings for valuations occurring as poles of
u
and we need a parameter for
u
to define
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[u]
. Finally, we need a parameter for
w
to define
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[w]
.

11.1.
Valuations of an infinite algebraic extension of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)

The only valuations of algebraic extensions of finite fields are trivial by virtue of the fact that all nonzero elements in such extensions have finite (multiplicative) order.
Let
{\bf K}
be an infinite algebraic extension of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)
and let
{\bf v}
be a valuation of
{\bf K}
. If
K\subset{\bf K}
and
K/{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)
is finite, then
{\bf v}_{|K}
must be a discrete valuation
v_{K}
since
K
only has discrete valuations.

If
{\bf{\mathfrak{p}}}
is the valuation ideal of
{\bf v}
, then
{\bf{\mathfrak{p}}}
contains the valuation ideal
{\mathfrak{p}}_{K}
of
K
corresponding to
v_{K}
for any finite extension
K
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)
contained in
{\bf K}
.
At the same time,
v_{K}
can have infinitely many extensions
{\bf v}
to
{\bf K}
. It is possible for
e({\bf v}/v)=\infty
though the ramification can also be finite. Similarly,
f({\bf v}/v)
can be finite or infinite.
The completion of
{\bf K}
at different valuations
{\bf v}
extending the same valuation
v
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)
can correspond to different embeddings of
{\bf K}
into
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)_{v}}
.

In the definition of
q
-boundedness (Definition
6.6
), we refer to a tower of completions
{\bf K}_{T,\bf v}
instead of referring to the completion
{\bf K}_{\bf v}
itself.
This technical detail arises because the completion
{\bf K}_{\bf v}
can be a transcendental extension of
K_{v_{K}}
under certain conditions; see the proposition below.
In contrast,
{\bf K}_{T,\bf v}
is always an algebraic extension of
K_{v_{K}}
by design.

Proposition 11.1
.

If
{\bf v}
is unramified over
v_{K}
and
\kappa_{\bf v}/\kappa_{v_{K}}
is an infinite (algebraic) extension, then
{\bf K}_{\bf v}
is a transcendental extension of
K_{v_{K}}
.
In particular,
{\bf K}_{\bf v}\supsetneq{\bf K}_{T,\bf v}
.

Proof.

Let
\{\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\dots\}\subseteq{\bf K}
be a subset whose residue classes generate
\kappa_{\bf v}
over
\kappa_{v_{K}}
, and
let
y\in K^{\times}
be an element such that
{\bf v}(y)>0
.
Consider the element
x=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{i}y^{i}
, observing that
x\in{\bf K}_{\bf v}
because the series converges in the
{\bf v}
-adic topology.
We will prove that
x
is transcendental over
K_{v_{K}}
.

Define
L_{0}=K_{v_{K}}(x)
and
L_{i}=K_{v_{K}}(x,\alpha_{0},\ldots,\alpha_{i-1})
for
i\geq 1
, and let
v_{L_{i}}
be the restriction of
{\bf v}
to
L_{i}
.
Observe that
e(v_{L_{i}}/v_{L_{0}})=1
for all
i
because the extension
{\bf v}/v_{K}
is unramified by assumption.
By the definition of
x
, we see
[x]=[\alpha_{0}]
in
\kappa_{\bf v}
. Therefore,
\kappa_{v_{L_{0}}}=\kappa_{v_{L_{0}}}(\alpha_{0})
, hence
f(v_{L_{1}}/v_{L_{0}})=1
.
This proves that

[L_{1}:L_{0}]=e(v_{L_{1}}/v_{L_{0}})f(v_{L_{1}}/v_{L_{0}})=1.

In particular, we have
\alpha_{0}\in L_{0}
.
Replacing
x
by
\frac{x-\alpha_{0}}{y}
and continuing by induction, we find that
\alpha_{i}\in L_{0}
for all
i
.
Therefore, because
\kappa_{v_{L_{0}}}=\kappa_{\bf v}=\kappa_{v_{K}}(\{\alpha_{i}\})
is an infinite extension of
\kappa_{v_{K}}
, we see that
L_{0}=K_{v_{K}}(x)
must be an infinite extension
K_{v_{K}}
.
Consequently,
x
is a transcendental element over
K_{v_{K}}
, as claimed.
∎

Bushen Zhuluan Decoction alleviates diminished ovarian reserve by regulating the m6A modification of PLGF
Authors: Huizhen Liang, Pingfang Tang, Qing Du, Xin Chen, Ling Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractDiminished ovarian reserve (DOR) is an important pathogenic factor contributing to infertility among women of childbearing age. Bushen Zhuluan Decoction (BZD) has been observed to enhance ovarian function. The aim of this study was to investigate the pharmacological effects of BZD on DOR and elucidate its underlying mechanism of action. A DOR rat model was established by gastric gavage with Tripterygium wilfordii polyglycosides (TWPs). Ovarian pathological alterations were evaluated using HE staining. The serum FSH, LH, E2 and AMH levels were measured by ELISA; the mRNA and protein expression levels were determined by qRT‒PCR, western blot analysis and immunohistochemistry; and a specific kit was used to assess the m6A methylation level. The findings indicated that both m6A methylation levels and METTL3 expression were significantly decreased in the ovarian tissues of DOR rats and this effect was partially reversed by BZD treatment. METTL3 knockdown decreased PLGF mRNA stability and expression in human ovarian granulosa cells in a m6A-dependent manner. Moreover, BZD intervention improved ovarian function in DOR rats by regulating METTL3-mediated PLGF m6A modification. Taken together, these findings indicate that BZD alleviated DOR by the regulation of METTL3-mediated PLGF m6A modification.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13048-025-01898-7.Keywords: Diminished ovarian reserve, Bushen zhuluan decoction, M6A, METTL3, PLGF

Introduction

Diminished ovarian reserve (DOR) refers to the reduced production of mature ova and reduced quality of oocytes in the ovary, leading to decreased levels of female sex hormones and reduced fertility potential [1]. This condition can progress to premature ovarian failure, significantly impacting patients’ quality of life and constituting a major cause of female infertility [2]. A recent study revealed an annual increase in the incidence rate of DOR due to the rapid lifestyles of people and environmental degradation [2]. Currently, hormone replacement therapy (HRT) remains the primary treatment for DOR, although it has been associated with a potential risk for cancer [3], underscoring the need for safe, effective and economical alternative treatment strategies. In this context, traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) represents a promising alternative for the prevention and treatment of DOR.

Evidence from various studies supports the therapeutic benefits of TCM in managing DOR. For example, Liu et al. reported that the Zihuai recipe could mitigate cyclophosphamide-induced DOR by inhibiting apoptosis [4]. Similarly, the Dingkun Pill has been shown to reverse DOR by increasing the levels of serum estradiol (E2), luteinizing hormone (LH) and follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) and promoting the recovery of the estrous cycle [3]. Bushen Zhuluan Decoction (BZD), a supplement based on Professor You Zhaoling’s Bushen Huoxue Decoction, is clinically recognized for its ability to strengthen the kidneys and replenish essence, promote blood circulation, and regulate the menstrual cycle [5]. Preliminary research on BZD has indicated its potential to decrease abnormal follicular apoptosis, reduce follicular atresia, and enhance ovarian blood vessel formation, thereby enhancing ovarian reserve function [6]. However, the specific mechanisms through which BZD alleviates DOR warrant further investigation.

Placenta growth factor (PLGF), a vascular endothelial factor, is necessary for ovulation, luteinization and follicular angiogenesis in primates [7]. Notably, our previous study revealed that BZD could restore ovarian function by increasing PLGF levels in the ovaries of rats with ovarian dysfunction induced by Tripterygium wilfordii polyglycosides (TWPs) [6]. However, the mechanism by which BZDs regulate the expression of PLGF during DOR progression is still unknown and deserves further study. Recently, many studies have investigated the regulation of PLGF mRNA methylation [8]. N6-methyladenosine (m6A) methylation is the most common internal RNA modification [9]. Abnormal changes in m6A can dysregulate the expression of downstream genes, affecting ovarian function [10]. The machinery regulating m6A consists of methyltransferases (known as “writers”), binding proteins (“readers”), and demethylases (“erasers”) [11]. Among these, RNA methyltransferase-like 3 (METTL3), a “writer,” has been shown to play an important role in modulating RNA methylation levels [12]. In this study, by SRAMP database prediction, it was found that PLGF has m6A modification sites. Therefore, BZD may promote PLGF expression by regulating METTL3-mediated PLGF m6A modification, which deserves further research.

In the present study, we assessed the role of BZD in DOR and evaluated the underlying mechanism in vivo. We demonstrated the significant impact of BZD on enhancing ovarian function and reducing granulosa cell apoptosis in DOR models. Importantly, our findings revealed that BZD ameliorates DOR by increasing METTL3 expression. Overall, this study not only proposes BZD as a potential therapeutic option for DOR but also elucidates the mechanisms involved, contributing valuable insights into the treatment of this condition.

Materials and methods

Preparation of BZD

BZD comprises a blend of TCM herbs, including Morinda officinalis 10 g, Cistanche deserticola 10 g, raspberry 10 g, wolfberry 10 g, mulberry 10 g, yam 15 g, lotus seeds 15 g, Fructus aurantii 10 g, Polygonatum sibiricum 10 g, Codonopsis pilosula 10 g, Astragalus membranaceus 10 g, Atractylodes macrocephala 10 g, rose 10 g, Angelica sinensis 10 g, Chuanxiong 10 g, and Panax notoginseng 3 g. The raw materials for this decoction were purchased from the pharmacy of the Second Affiliated Hospital of Hunan University of Chinese Medicine. To prepare 2.4 g/mL BZD, 1.7 kg of the combined medicinal materials were divided into six equal parts. Each part was placed in a casserole, soaked in an appropriate amount of water for 30 min, and then simmered for 50 min. The decoction was filtered, and the residue was then soaked again in fresh water followed by a second simmering of 40 min. The resultant liquids from the simmered solutions were combined and concentrated by reducing the volume to 708 mL by evaporation.

Clinical sample collection

Serum samples were collected from infertile patients (n = 40) at the Maternal and Child Health Hospital of Hunan Province from 2018 to 2020. After testing, the patients were divided into a control group (normal ovarian reserve function, n = 16) and a DOR group (decreased ovarian reserve function, n = 24). The inclusion criteria of this study were as follows: did not receive hormone therapy in the last 3 months and was under the age of 40. Patients with DOR who met the following criteria were recruited: 3 months of amenorrhea, FSH level above 10 U/L, E2 level below 50 ng/L, LH level above 2 U/L, and no ovariectomy. The baseline characteristics of the participants, including age; body mass index (BMI); antral follicle count (AFC); and serum levels of AMH, FSH, LH and E2, are presented in Table 1. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Maternal and Child Health Hospital of Hunan Province. All participants signed informed consent forms.

Isolation of granulosa cells (GCs)

GCs were isolated from follicular fluid samples as previously reported [13]. Briefly, follicular fluid samples were centrifuged at 3,000 × g for 10 min to pellet the cellular components. The intermediate layer containing the GCs was carefully aspirated using a Pasteur pipette and resuspended in PBS. To enrich GCs, the cell suspension was layered onto 2.5 mL of human lymphocyte separation medium and centrifuged at 2,000 × g for 10 min. The white cloud-like layer at the interface, predominantly comprising GCs, was collected and washed once with PBS (2,500 × g, 10 min). Purified GCs were subsequently cryopreserved at − 80 °C for downstream molecular analyses.

Cell culture

Human granulosa cells (KGN cells) were purchased from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC) (VA, USA). KGN cells were cultured in DMEM (Gibco, MD, USA) supplemented with 10% FBS (Gibco), 50 U/mL penicillin and 50 µg/mL streptomycin (Gibco). The cells were maintained in a standard humidified incubator at 37 °C with 5% CO2.

Cell transfection

Short hairpin RNA targeting METTL3 (sh-METTL3) and its negative control (sh-NC) were purchased from GenePharma (Shanghai China). KGN cells were seeded into 6-well plates in 2 ml of antibiotic-free DMEM supplemented with FBS and then maintained at 37 °C for 24 h until they reached 70–80% confluence. During transfection, Lipofectamine™ 3000 (Invitrogen, CA, USA) was used according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The cells were used in subsequent experiments 48 h after transfection.

m6A RNA methylation assay

The total level of m6A was examined by a m6A RNA methylation assay kit (colorimetric) (ab185912) purchased from Abcam (Cambridge, UK). The cell lysates were collected for experiments according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Animal experiments

Female Sprague–Dawley (SD) rats (8 weeks old) were obtained from SJA LABORATORY Animal Co., Ltd. (Hunan, China). After acclimation for 1 week, vaginal exfoliative cytology smears were obtained to select 40 rats with a normal estrous cycle as test animals. The rats were randomly divided into four groups: the control group, the DOR group, the DOR + Bushen Zhuluan Decoction (BZD) group and the model + BZD + sh-METTL3 group, with ten animals in each group. The rats in the DOR group, the DOR + BZD group and the DOR + BZD + sh-METTL3 group were gavaged with 50 mg/(kg·d) TWP for 14 d to establish a DOR rat model. Moreover, the rats in the DOR + BZD group and the DOR + BZD + sh-METTL3 group were subjected to gastric gavage with 24 g/(kg·d) BZD for 14 d.

Lentiviruses carrying sh-METTL3 were constructed by GenePharma (Shanghai, China). From day 15, the rats in the DOR + BZD + sh-METTL3 group received a tail vein injection of sh-METTL3 (500 µL; 1 × 109 PFU). Forty-eight hours after injection, vaginal exfoliative cytology smears were obtained to ascertain the phase of the estrous cycle. The rats were subsequently sacrificed, and blood and ovarian tissues were collected. The animal studies were approved by the Maternal and Child Health Hospital of Hunan Province (No. A013).

Ovarian index

The ovarian index was calculated with the following formula: ovarian index (%) = ovarian wet weight (g)/body weight before euthanasia (g) × 100.

Estrus cycle detection

The stage of the estrous cycle was detected using smears of exfoliated vaginal cells. Proestrus is defined when a large number of oval epithelial cells and a small number of leukocytes are observed; estrus is defined when a large number of α-nucleated keratinized epithelial cells are observed; late estrus is defined when the simultaneous presence of keratinocytes, nuclei ovale, and leukocytes are observed; and the estrous interval is defined when a large number of white blood cells and a small number of nuclear epithelial cells are observed.

Hematoxylin‒eosin (HE) staining

The ovarian tissues were fixed in 10% formalin and embedded in paraffin. The paraffin blocks were then sliced into 4-micrometer-thick sections. These sections were deparaffinized with xylene and rehydrated with ethanol (100%, 95%, and 80%). The sections were then stained for 15 min with HE (Sigma‒Aldrich, MO, USA). The sections were observed using a microscope (Olympus, Tokyo, Japan).

Terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase-mediated dUTP nick-end labeling (TUNEL)

The ovarian tissues were washed with PBS and fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 30 min. After being washed with PBS, 0.3% Triton X-100 in PBS was added to the samples, which were subsequently incubated at room temperature for 5 min. Then, the samples were incubated with 50 µL of TUNEL detection solution (Beyotime) for 60 min at 37 °C in the dark. DAPI was added and a coverslip was applied, after which the slides were observed under a fluorescence microscope (Olympus).

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

The levels of anti-Mullerian hormone (AMH), follicle stimulating hormone (FSH), luteinizing hormone (LH) and estrogen (E2) were examined by ELISA kits purchased from Jinma Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Serum was collected for experiments according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

The ovarian tissue sections were produced at a thickness of 4 μm. The tissue sections were deparaffinized in xylene and rehydrated in graded ethanol. After antigen retrieval and incubation with a solution of 3% H2O2 for 15 min, the sections were incubated with antibodies against PLGF (Abcam, 1:200, ab196666) and METTL3 (Abcam, 1:500, ab195352) overnight, followed by incubation with secondary antibodies (Abcam, 1:500, ab150077) for 1 h. The sections were stained with DAB, counterstained with hematoxylin, dehydrated and mounted. Images were taken using a microscope (Olympus).

RNA degradation assay

The cells (8 × 104) were incubated with 5 mg/mL actinomycin D (Sigma‒Aldrich) for 0 h, 2–4 h following sh-NC or sh-METTL3 transfection. Then, RNA was extracted from the cells, reverse transcribed to cDNA and examined by qRT‒PCR.

Methylated RNA binding protein Immunoprecipitation (Me-RIP) assay

m6A antibody (Abcam, 1:500, ab151230) or IgG antibody (Abcam, 1:100, ab109489) was prebound to Protein A/G magnetic beads in immunoprecipitation buffer (20 mM Tris-HCl pH 7.5, 140 mM NaCl, 0.05% Triton X-100) for 2 h and then incubated with 100 µl of cell lysate overnight at 4 °C with rotation. RNA was purified from the mRNA–bead–antibody complex and subjected to qRT‒PCR analysis.

Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT‒PCR)

TRIzol (Thermo Fisher Scientific, MA, USA) was used to isolate total RNA. cDNA was synthesized with a reverse transcription kit (Toyobo, Tokyo, Japan). The qRT‒PCR assay was performed on an Applied Biosystems 7900HT detection system with SYBR Green Master Mix (Thermo Fisher Scientific). GAPDH was used as the reference gene. Western blot

The proteins were isolated with RIPA buffer, and the protein concentration was analyzed using a BCA kit (Beyotime, Shanghai, China). Then, the same amount of protein (20 µg) per sample was separated by 12% PAGE, which was subsequently transferred to a PVDF membrane (Millipore, MA, USA), and the membranes were blocked in 5% nonfat milk and incubated with antibodies against METTL3 (Abcam, 1:1000, ab195352), PLGF (Abcam, 1:1000, ab196666), Bax (Abcam, 1:2000, ab32503), Bcl-2 (Abcam, 1:1000, ab194583), and β-actin (Abcam, 1:5000, ab8226) overnight and then hybridized with secondary antibodies (Abcam, 1:10000, ab7090) for 60 min. The blots were visualized with a GEL imaging system (Bio-Rad, CA, USA) and subsequently analyzed with ImageJ software.

Statistical analysis

All the data were obtained from three independent experiments. The data were analyzed by SPSS 19.0 (IBM, Armonk, NY) and are presented as the means ± SDs. Between-group differences and multigroup comparisons were determined using Student’s t test and one-way ANOVA, respectively. A p value less than 0.05 was considered significant.

Results

m6A levels and PLGF expression were markedly reduced in the serum of DOR patients

The serum samples were collected from infertile patients, and they were divided into a control group (normal ovarian reserve function) and a DOR group (decreased ovarian reserve function). The clinical characteristics of the 40 included patients are summarized in Table 1. As expected, the DOR group (n = 24) presented significantly lower ovarian reserve markers—including AFC, AMH, and E2 levels—along with elevated FSH and LH than the control group did. No significant differences were observed in age or BMI, ensuring comparability between groups. As shown in Fig. 1A, the m6A level was significantly lower in the serum of DOR patients than in the serum of the control group. The expression of m6A regulatory factors (METTL3, METTL14, WTAP, YTHDF1, FTO and ALKBH5) in the serum samples was detected, and the results demonstrated that METTL3, WTAP and YTHDF1 were downregulated in the serum of DOR patients compared with that in the serum of the control group. The downregulation of METTL3 was the most significant, whereas the expression of other m6A regulatory factors did not change significantly (Fig. 1B). Moreover, the mRNA levels of METTL3, WTAP, and YTHDF1 in GCs from follicular fluid were detected, and the results revealed that METTL3 expression was significantly lower in the DOR group than in the control group, whereas WTAP and YTHDF1 expression remained unchanged (Fig. 1C). Thus, we selected METTL3 for further study. The PLGF mRNA level was also markedly lower in the serum of DOR patients than in the serum of the control group (Fig. 1D). Moreover, PLGF expression was also significantly reduced in GCs from the DOR group (Fig. 1E). METTL3 expression was positively correlated with PLGF expression in clinical samples (Fig. 1F). These results suggest that METTL3 and PLGF might be involved in DOR progression.

TWP induced ovarian dysfunction in rats

DOR rats were established by TWP gastric gavage. Initial observations indicated that while the estrous cycle remained normal in the control group, it was disrupted in the DOR model group, confirming that the DOR rat model was successfully established (Fig. 2A). As shown in Fig. 2B, the ovarian indices of the DOR group were significantly lower than those of the control group, suggesting that the ovaries of the DOR model rats were atrophic. As revealed by HE staining, reduced numbers of ovarian primordial follicles and mature follicles as well as increased numbers of atretic follicles were observed in the ovaries of DOR rats (Fig. 2C). In addition, the FSH and LH levels were markedly greater in the serum of DOR rats than in the serum of the control group, whereas the E2 and AMH levels were lower (Fig. 2D). Moreover, TUNEL staining revealed that the percentage of apoptotic ovarian granulosa cells was significantly greater in the DOR group than in the control group (Fig. S1A). Moreover, the Bax protein level was markedly greater in the ovarian tissues of DOR rats than in those of control rats, while the Bcl-2 level was lower (Fig. S1B). Overall, these results confirmed that TWP induced ovarian dysfunction in rats.

BZD administration upregulated m6A levels and PLGF expression in DOR rats

DOR rats were gavaged with 24 g/(kg·d) BZD for 14 days. As shown in Fig. 4A, TWP treatment resulted in reduced m6A levels in ovarian tissues, whereas this change was reversed by BZD administration. In addition, the expression of PLGF and METTL3 was markedly reduced in the ovarian tissues of DOR rats, whereas this change was eliminated after BZD gastric gavage (Fig. 4B). Moreover, METTL3 and PLGF expression levels in the ovarian tissues of rats were significantly reduced by TWP, and these effects were abolished following BZD treatment (Fig. 4C-D). Taken together, the m6A level and PLGF expression were significantly reduced in the ovarian tissues of DOR rats, whereas these changes were reversed by BZD administration.

METTL3 increased the stability of PLGF through m6A modification in granulosa cells

To determine the function of METTL3 in regulating DOR progression, we induced METTL3 knockdown in KGN cells. The transfection efficiency was detected by qRT‒PCR and western blotting, and the results revealed that METTL3 mRNA and protein expression in KGN cells was significantly reduced following sh-METTL3 transfection (Fig. 5A‒B). METTL3 is a m6A writer; as expected, the total m6A level in KGN cells was significantly decreased following METTL3 knockdown (Fig. 5C). We subsequently observed that METTL3 knockdown resulted in decreased PLGF m6A levels (Fig. 5D), mRNA levels (Fig. 5E) and mRNA stability (Fig. 5F) in KGN cells. In summary, METTL3 knockdown inhibited PLGF expression in ovarian granulosa cells by reducing the PLGF m6A level and mRNA stability.

BZD improved DOR in rats by upregulating METTL3

To determine the therapeutic effect of BZD on DOR and the role of METTL3 in BAD-mediated biological effects, DOR rats were subjected to gastric gavage with 24 g/(kg·d) BZD for 14 d, and METTL3 knockdown was subsequently induced. TWP significantly reduced the mRNA and protein levels of METTL3 in ovarian tissues, and sh-METTL3 transfection further reduced METTL3 expression levels in the ovarian tissues of DOR rats (Fig. 6A-B). As shown in Fig. 6C, BZD intervention alleviated TWP-induced estrous cycle disorder in rats, which was eliminated by METTL3 silencing. In addition, BZD intervention prevented the TWP-induced decrease in the ovarian index, which was abrogated after METTL3 knockdown (Fig. 6D). As revealed by HE staining, the ovarian pathological changes induced by TWP, such as reduced numbers of primordial follicles, primary follicles, and mature follicles with granulosa cells and a reduced volume of oocytes, were ameliorated by BZD intervention, whereas this ameliorating effect of BZD was eliminated by METTL3 knockdown (Fig. 6E). Additionally, METTL3 knockdown abolished the ameliorative effect of BZD on the TWP-induced increase in FSH and LH levels in the serum of rats and the preventive effect of BZD on the TWP-induced decrease in E2 and AMH levels (Fig. 6F). Additionally, BZD intervention prevented the TWP-induced decrease in m6A levels in ovarian tissues, whereas this regulatory effect of BZD was reversed by METTL3 knockdown (Fig. 6G). Moreover, BZD intervention prevented the TWP-induced decrease in the PLGF level in ovarian tissues, whereas this effect of BZD was abolished by METTL3 downregulation (Fig. 6H). TUNEL staining revealed that BZD intervention prevented the TWP-induced increase in granulosa cell apoptosis, whereas this effect of BZD was weakened by METTL3 knockdown (Fig. S2A). The western blot results consistently revealed that the promoting effect of TWP on the Bax level and the inhibitory effect on the Bcl-2 level in ovarian tissues were reversed by BZD intervention, whereas MEETL3 silencing weakened this effect of BZD (Fig. S2B). Overall, BZD alleviated DOR in rats by increasing METTL3 expression.

Discussion

DOR is a primary factor in the reduction of female fertility, with its prevalence increasing annually, presenting a significant challenge to the field of reproductive medicine [14, 15]. Currently, there is a lack of effective treatments for DOR, and its pathology remains unclear. Herein, we showed that BZD, a traditional Chinese medicine formula, significantly alleviates DOR and we revealed the underlying mechanisms by which BZD exerts its therapeutic effects on DOR.

Epigenetic modifications play pivotal roles in regulating numerous physiological processes and the progression of diseases. Among these modifications, m6A is the most prevalent internal modification in eukaryotic RNA [16] and plays a crucial role in diseases of the female reproductive system and ovarian function [16]. Jiang et al. demonstrated a close association between m6A dysregulation and ovarian aging [10]. Furthermore, cyclophosphamide has been shown to impact ovarian function by altering m6A levels [17]. However, research specifically addressing m6A in the context of DOR remains limited. In this study, we observed a significant reduction in m6A levels in the ovarian tissues of rats induced with DOR by TWP treatment. METTL3, a well-known m6A writer, is responsible for a substantial portion of m6A modifications [12]. Previous studies have shown that loss of METTL3 can lead to failure of mature gamete production and reduced fertility [18] and that METTL3-mediated m6A modification is crucial for oocyte and follicular development in mice [19]. Our findings indicate that METTL3 expression is significantly lower in the ovarian tissues of DOR rats than in those of control rats, suggesting that METTL3-mediated m6A modification plays a significant role in the progression of DOR.

Previous in vivo studies have shown that a variety of TCM preparations can alleviate DOR [20]. BZD, an empirical formula, is composed of Radix Morindae officinalis, Cistanche deserticola, Astragalus membranaceus, raspberry, Chinese yam, Rhizoma polygonati, tangerine peel, wolfberry fruit, mulberry, Angelica sinensis, lotus seed, orange leaf, Codonopsis pilosula, Atractylodes macrocephala, rose, Ligusticum chuanxiong, and Panax notoginseng. Research has indicated that BZD can increase follicle maturation, ovulation, and luteinization in rats [21]. Moreover, a clinical trial reported that BZD treatment led to an increase in the pregnancy rate and serum E2 levels in women with anovulatory infertility [22]. In this study, DOR rats were subjected to gastric gavage with BZD for 14 d, and our results revealed that BZD intervention markedly increased total m6A levels and METTL3 expression in the ovarian tissues of DOR rats, suggesting that BZD might function in regulating METTL3-mediated m6A modification during DOR progression. Thus, we need to solve a key problem: the downstream target of METTL3 in DOR. PLGF, as a vascular endothelial factor, is involved in growth, migration, survival, angiogenesis of endothelial cells, revascularization of ischemic tissue and ovarian angiogenesis [23]. Ovarian blood vessels can provide circulation support for follicle growth and affect ovarian reserve function [24]. Notably, our previous research demonstrated that BZD improved ovarian function and promoted ovarian angiogenesis in DOR rats by increasing the protein expression of PLGF and its specific receptor Flt-1 [6]. In the present study, PLGF was significantly downregulated in the serum of DOR patients and in the ovarian tissues of TWP-induced DOR rats. In addition, METTL3 expression was positively correlated with PLGF expression in clinical samples. Interestingly, our results also revealed that the m6A level, mRNA stabilization and mRNA level of PLGF in human ovarian granulosa cells were reduced by METTL3 knockdown. In addition, as expected, BZD intervention improved ovarian function in DOR rats by regulating METTL3-mediated PLGF m6A modification.

Identifying the precise mechanism by which BZD ameliorates DOR remains highly important. Our findings indicated that BZD alleviated DOR by regulating METTL3-mediated PLGF m6A modification. Our research revealed that BZD has certain therapeutic effects on DOR and clarified its mechanism, supporting the potential of BZD as a treatment for DOR.

Analysis of risk factors and predictive modelling of biomarkers in subclinical hypothyroidism and implications for levothyroxine therapy in disease management
Authors: Hiya Ghosh, Dibakar Biswas, Subhasish Pramanik, Subhankar Chowdhury
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractHypothyroidism is associated with elevated levels of inflammatory cytokines, but very few comprehensive studies have involved a wide range of inflammatory markers, particularly in subclinical hypothyroidism (SH).This study was designed to evaluate the expression of various circulating inflammatory markers and adhesion proteins in patients with subclinical hypothyroidism (SH) and overt hypothyroidism (OH), and their comparison to healthy subjects, while particularly focusing on patients with SH.A cohort of 139 hypothyroid patients and 60 healthy individuals were analysed, using immunological markers to identify significant predictors of SH. Circulating levels of inflammatory markers were quantified, and logistic regression analyses were conducted to identify significant predictors of SH. ROC curve analysis was used to determine the predictive accuracy of key markers. A subset of SH patients was treated with levothyroxine for six months. Real-time PCR was used to assess mRNA expression. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses revealed that IL-1β, IL-6, and ICAM-1 were the best predictive markers of inflammation in SH. These three demonstrated high predictive accuracies in receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis, with specific cut-off values established for identifying SH patients at risk of inflammation. Additionally, real-time mRNA expression analysis indicated activation of the inflammasome complex in the PBMCs of both OH and SH patients, with the degree of expression correlating with hypothyroidism.IL-1β, IL-6, and ICAM-1 serve as reliable biomarkers for the early detection of inflammation in SH. The activation of the inflammasome complex further supports the need for early intervention with levothyroxine in SH patients to mitigate inflammatory progression.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-24721-1.Keywords: Thyroid-stimulating hormone, Overt hypothyroidism, Sub-clinical hypothyroidism, Cytokines, Inflammation, InflammasomeSubject terms: Molecular biology, Physiology, Endocrinology, Health care, Medical research, Molecular medicine

Introduction

Hypothyroidism can be divided into overt hypothyroidism (OH) and subclinical hypothyroidism (SH). SH is defined as raised serum TSH with normal free thyroxine (fT4), while OH is represented by high TSH and lower than normal fT4 levels1,2. In contrast to patients with OH, who are routinely treated with levothyroxine, SH patients do not have a standardised treatment protocol in India3–9.

IL1β is a pro-inflammatory cytokine expressed by monocytes, macrophages, and dendritic cells in response to inflammatory stimuli. It exists as an inactive form that accumulates in the cytosol10,11. Cleavage of pro-IL-1β into the active form requires the activation of the inflammasome complex, a multi-protein complex that responds to stress conditions, molecular pathogens, LPS and other danger signals12,13.

High-sensitive C-reactive protein (hsCRP) levels rise rapidly in response to inflammation caused by infections, metabolic disorders, heart disease, or stroke14. TNFα, another key immune protein, triggers inflammation by attracting immune cells and stimulating other cytokines, such as IL-615. and MCP-116. IL-6, in particular, plays a crucial role in immune responses and serves as a reliable marker for monitoring inflammation. Monitoring the level of IL6 is therefore a good method for understanding and managing inflammatory diseases17. Proteins like VCAM-1 and ICAM-1 help immune cells stick to blood vessels and are often elevated during inflammation. The soluble forms of these molecules can be detected in the serum or plasma and are increased under conditions of acute inflammatory responses18. Multiple research studies have shown that hypothyroidism, even in its early stages, can cause acute inflammation19. Several of these studies have pointed towards the activation of severe inflammatory responses in association with different degrees of hypothyroidism, which contributes towards the development of chronic ailments like cardiovascular diseases20.

This study has been designed to comprehensively assess the expression of various immunological biomarkers in patients with subclinical and overt hypothyroidism compared to healthy controls. By evaluating these markers, we seek to explain the inflammatory mechanisms underlying hypothyroidism and identify potential biomarkers for early intervention and disease management for patients suffering from SH, a patient population frequently overlooked in clinical practice especially in countries like India. By integrating TSH concentrations with concentrations of specific biomarkers, we hope to develop an assessment criterion to more effectively identify patients with subclinical hypothyroidisms (SH) who are at risk of progressing to overt hypothyroidism and develop severe inflammatory damage, or related conditions like atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) in the future. Detecting these changes early could help identify patients at risk and guide timely treatment to prevent further complications. Additionally, understanding the role of inflammatory signalling pathways in hypothyroidism may open new avenues for targeted treatments to reduce inflammation and improve disease outcomes in patient.

Materials and methods

Patient selection

For this study, all referred subjects (from other departments) visiting the Endocrinology outpatient clinic were screened for approximately 2.5 years. Following TFT ( Thyroid Function Test), 643 participants were found to be suitable for further assessment, among which 77 were categorized as euthyroid subjects (Normal range of both TSH and fT4) and 566 subjects were categorized as having hypothyroidism (as per elevated thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) above the normal reference range with corresponding free thyroxine (fT4) levels)21,22. All participants underwent testing for thyroid peroxidase antibody (TPOAb). Individuals with positive titers were excluded to avoid the confounding action of autoimmune thyroiditis23. This exclusion helped to ensure that the study population consisted only of anti-TPO-negative hypothyroidism participants. All anti-TPO-negative participants were re-assessed 6 weeks after initial diagnosis to confirm hypothyroidism, designating this time point as the baseline for disease presentation and sample collection, with 139 participants confirmed as hypothyroid cases.

The laboratory range upon which the grouping was done is as follows:

Euthyroid: 0.4-4 uIU/L (Normal range)1.

Euthyroid: 0.4-4 uIU/L (Normal range)1.

Subclinical hypothyroidism (SH): TSH > 4–10.0 µIU/ml with fT4 in the normal reference range (0.8–1.9 ng/dL)3,22.

Subclinical hypothyroidism (SH): TSH > 4–10.0 µIU/ml with fT4 in the normal reference range (0.8–1.9 ng/dL)3,22.

Overt hypothyroidism (OH): TSH > 10.0 µIU/ml with fT4 below the lower limit of normal (< 0.8 ng/dL)3,22.

Overt hypothyroidism (OH): TSH > 10.0 µIU/ml with fT4 below the lower limit of normal (< 0.8 ng/dL)3,22.

Together with 60 euthyroid antibody-negative controls, this left us with a total study population of 199 participants (60 controls, 80 SH, 59 OH). All reference ranges were based on hospital laboratory values referenced with the ATA guidelines.

Data collection

In order to reduce the physiological variability of thyroid function testing, we used stringent inclusion and sampling criteria. We included only non-pregnant adults between the ages of 30–50 years, as both pregnancy and age have been known to affect serum TSH levels21,22. Excluded were participants with a body mass index (BMI) > 25 kg/m², those with uncontrolled comorbidities, prior known metabolic and cardiovascular diseases, or on drugs known to affect thyroid function (e.g., amiodarone, levothyroxine, lithium, glucocorticoids)23,24. Venous blood was drawn between 08:00 and 10:00 h following an overnight fast of a minimum of 12 h to minimise diurnal and postprandial variation in TSH and free T4 determinations and to exclude diabetic patients by assessing their FBS25,26. Collection and processing of blood sample

After 12 h of overnight fasting, 10–15 ml of venous blood was collected from each subject using an aseptic needle in both clot vials and EDTA (ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid) anticoagulated vacutainer tubes. The serum was separated immediately from the whole blood by centrifugation at 1000 × g for 10 min. Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) were isolated from the EDTA whole blood and stored in RNAlater (Sigma Aldrich) at − 80 °C until isolation of RNA (Ribonucleic Acid)27.

Assessment of serum inflammatory and metabolic biomarkers

Serum levels of various inflammatory biomarkers, including Tumor Necrosis Factor alpha (TNF-α), Interleukin 6 (IL-6), Interleukin 1β (IL-1β), High-Sensitive C-Reactive Protein (hsCRP), Monocyte Chemoattractant Protein 1 (MCP-1), Intercellular Adhesion Molecule 1 (ICAM-1), Vascular Cell Adhesion Molecule 1 (VCAM-1), and Transforming Growth Factor beta (TGF-β), were measured using commercially available kits (G-Biosciences, Geno Technology Inc., USA) by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) method as per manufacturer’s protocols.

Isolation of PBMCs

Peripheral blood mononuclear cells were isolated from the collected blood samples of each patient by density gradient centrifugation using histopaque separation medium (Sigma-Aldrich; Merck KGaA) according to standardized protocol28.

RNA isolation and real-time polymerase chain reaction

RNA was extracted using an RNA isolation kit (Nucleospin RNA blood mini kit, MACHEREY NAGEL) from isolated PBMCs.Primescript 1st strand cDNA synthesis kit, (TAKARA Bio.) was used to convert RNA into cDNA following standard procedure. The cDNA was used for quantitive real time PCR. The real-time expression ratios of different mRNAs were determined using the 2−ΔΔCt method for relative quantification29. All real-time primers were designed in the laboratory using primer-3 software and obtained from IDT Technologies INC, India. The reaction parameters and conditions were standardized in the laboratory. All the primers that have been used in this study for real-time expression have been listed in Supplementary Table 8.

Follow-up strategy

Among the three groups SH group was divided into 2 subgroups. The detailed representation is provided in Fig. 1. One subgroup was treated with levothyroxine (0.8–1.6 µg/kg body weight), while another subgroup was left untreated. Detailed follow-up analysis of different parameters was done to monitor the variation in the state of expression of these parameters 6 months after the initial disease presentation.

Statistical analysis

The Kolmogorov–Smirnov test was employed to assess the normality of the data. Results are presented as Mean ± SEM (standard error of the mean) with 95% Confidence Intervals (CIs). For comparisons involving more than two groups of normally distributed data, one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by the Bonferroni post-hoc test was performed. In contrast, non-normally distributed data were evaluated using Kruskal-Wallis non-parametric ANOVA followed by the Dunn post-hoc test30. Normally distributed paired data were analysed using the paired Student’s t-test. Univariate as well as multiple binary logistic regression analyses were conducted to identify different parameters that predict SH. Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) analysis was performed to determine the area under the curve (AUC) and to establish the optimal cut-off score of a parameter, along with sensitivity and specificity for discriminating between the two groups. A p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software (version 25.0; SPSS, Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) and GraphPad Prism (version 8.0)31,32.

Distinctive profiling of patients with subclinical hypothyroidism based on selected markers

We wanted to test whether the significant parameters that were selected previously can help us to distinctly define the inflammatory changes associated with subclinical hypothyroidism.

We chose 60 patients from group 1 (control) and 80 patients from group 2 (SH) as our target population for univariate and multivariate binary logistic regression analysis using the previously selected serum immunological markers (6 markers). We intended to understand whether there exists any significant difference in the expression of the inflammatory markers and adhesion molecules among patients with SH compared to controls. As patients belonging to the two groups differ only in the concentration of TSH, the observed outcomes are most likely due to the variation in TSH among the patients.

The regression analysis revealed that out of all the significant parameters tested, only 3 parameters i.e., IL6, IL1β and ICAM 1, could predict subclinical hypothyroidism almost accurately. The detailed analysis has been provided in Table 3.

Determination of cut off values for significant immunological markers to predict inflammatory changes associated with sub clinical hypothyroidism

We wanted to specifically point out the markers that are associated with inflammatory responses with exact cut off scores that can significantly predict subclinical hypothyroidism. For this purpose, Receiver-operating characteristic (ROC) curves of selected models were derived for all the chosen immunological markers IL6, IL1β and ICAM 1as predicted significant from the binary logistic regression test. The curves were used to evaluate the individual cut-off values, prioritizing both highest sensitivity and specificity. Of the 3 predictive markers associated with Sub clinical hypothyroidism in both univariate and multivariate models IL6 and IL1β showed the highest predictive power (AUC = 0.983 for both). ICAM 1 also showed good accuracy score with AUC of 0.827. A detailed representation of the curves has been provided in Fig. 2.

The Follow up was monitored using the three previously described significant biomarkers (IL6, IL1β, ICAM 1) with exact cut-off scores. Follow-up analysis revealed that the mean serum concentrations of IL6, IL1β and ICAM 1were significantly increased among the sub-clinical hypothyroidism patients who did not receive any LT4 treatment after 6 months when compared to their values at the time of disease presentation .

Significant decreases in the mean concentrations of these three markers were observed after 6 months in case of those SH patients who received LT4 treatment. The detailed representation of the cases has been depicted in Fig. 3.

IL1β and IL18 were selected to study the activation of the inflammasome pathway.

The fold change in mRNA expression of TNFα, IL1β, IL18, MCP1, IL6, and ICAM-1 was significantly higher in groups 2 and 3 compared to group 1. The mRNAs of IL1β and IL18 were expressed at significantly higher levels in patients from both Group 2 and Group 3 compared to Group 1, although their expression was much higher in Group 3. Discussion

Understanding the link between sub-clinical hypothyroidism (SH) and inflammation is crucial, as it could enable early disease monitoring and therapeutic interventions. Hypothyroidism, particularly in its sub-clinical form, is often overlooked by patients and clinicians. Patients with sub-clinical hypothyroidism do not fall under any specific treatment regime, yet when left untreated, this condition could lead to inflammatory complications33. In this study, we found convincing evidence that both overt hypothyroidism (OH) and subclinical hypothyroidism (SH) are associated with significant inflammation.

Our findings revealed that patients with SH are at a significant risk of developing inflammation, we identified three immunological markers—IL-1β, IL-6, and ICAM-1—that were particularly effective in predicting inflammation in patients with SH. These molecules are known to play pivotal roles in the inflammatory response and have also been extensively studied in other inflammatory conditions, such as cardiovascular disease, rheumatoid arthritis, etc34,35.

IL-1β and IL-6 are pro-inflammatory cytokines that are central to the initiation and propagation of inflammatory responses. While IL-6 is a multifunctional cytokine that has both pro- and anti-inflammatory properties, but is primarily associated with chronic inflammation. IL-1β is involved in the activation of the inflammasome, a multiprotein complex that plays a crucial role in the innate immune system by promoting the secretion of inflammatory cytokines36. ICAM − 1, an intercellular adhesion molecule, facilitates the recruitment of leukocytes to sites of inflammation and is a marker of endothelial activation, which is often elevated in inflammatory states37.

Through the establishment of a specific thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) range correlating with the precise cut-off values of the three key predictive biomarkers, this diagnostic framework holds the potential to significantly enhance the clinicians’ ability to recognise and manage SH patients with greater accuracy and timeliness, thereby generating an advanced therapeutic regimen.

We anticipate that future research, particularly large-scale, multicentre cohort studies across diverse regions of India, will further refine and validate this criterion, ultimately enabling the formulation of a standardised therapeutic protocol grounded in clearly defined TSH thresholds. It holds profound clinical implications for India’s vast and heterogeneous population, where a substantial number of individuals remain undiagnosed or undertreated.

Without appropriate and timely intervention, these patients face a heightened risk of progressing to severe complications, including progression to overt hypothyroidism or related risks such as atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD), etc. By facilitating earlier diagnosis and more targeted therapeutic strategies, this work aspires to mitigate disease progression and substantially improve long-term outcomes for this ignored population.

Patients with subclinical hypothyroidism (SH) treated with levothyroxine showed a marked reduction in inflammatory markers, unlike the untreated group. This supports the use of levothyroxine to slow disease progression.

If the inflammation had been due to something like autoimmune thyroiditis, the patients would not have shown an improvement in the inflammatory state after treatment with levothyroxine, as shown in our study.

Moreover, this study highlights the activation of the inflammasome pathway in patients with both OH and SH. The inflammasome, particularly the NLRP3 complex, is a critical component of the body’s innate immune response, responding to various signals of cellular distress, including those associated with metabolic imbalances38. We found that the initial activator of the inflammasome, NLRP3, was similarly elevated in both OH and SH patients. The higher production of NF-κB and TNF-α in this process is observed which are known to activate the NLRP3. Activation of NLRP3 leads to the production of higher levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines, including IL-1β, which is significantly higher in SH patients than healthy subjects, which we have already identified as a key marker to predict inflammation in SH patients.

Thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH), which is elevated in both OH and SH patients, has been shown to have direct pro-inflammatory effects independent of thyroid hormone levels39. This could explain why we observed significant inflammation in SH patients, where thyroid hormone levels are not significantly altered. In a way this study also provides a new avenue for research into the inflammatory mechanisms underlying thyroid disorders.

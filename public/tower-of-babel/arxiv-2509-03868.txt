Jet broadening and radiation in the early anisotropic plasma in heavy-ion collisions Preprint: MIT-CTP/5917
Alois Altenburger, Kirill Boguslavski, Florian Lindenbauer

Abstract
Measuring the energy loss of energetic jet partons may provide experimental opportunities to constrain the initial nonequilibrium stages in heavy-ion collisions, which requires theoretical predictions for the jet-medium potential. In this letter, we go beyond the usual harmonic approximation, extract this potential for the first time using QCD kinetic theory simulations, and provide a simple cutoff-independent small-distance form. We find significant differences to thermal media at early times in momentum and position space, as well as substantial angular dependence. Applying our results to the gluon splitting rates using a novel computational method reveals tensions in the current kinetic theory implementations for early times.

Conclusion
.—In this work, we studied jet momentum broadening beyond the harmonic approximation and the resulting gluon splitting rates for an anisotropic medium during the initial stages in heavy-ion collisions.

For the first time, we have determined the anisotropic full collision kernel
C(\mathrm{\mathbf{q}}_{\perp},\tau)
of the nonequilibrium plasma in QCD kinetic theory, which measures the probability of jet broadening and characterizes the jet-medium potential.
The kernel has several advantages as compared to the jet quenching parameter
\hat{q}
, its second moment.
Unlike
\hat{q}
, it avoids a spurious dependence on a transverse momentum cutoff
and captures the entire broadening distribution, while
\hat{q}
only characterizes the mean momentum transfer.

For
C(\mathrm{\mathbf{q}}_{\perp},\tau)
, we find enhanced probability for soft scatterings
as compared to thermal equilibrium, corroborating
the use of the multiple-soft scattering approximation.
Interestingly, while its contribution to
\hat{q}
is usually peaked at the Debye mass,
at early times and momentum transfer along the beam axis, the peak is shifted to lower momenta
q_{\perp}
. This suggests an angular-dependent effective screening scale, which is initially considerably lower than the Debye mass at small angles.
Although our kernel captures only a part of the plasma anisotropy due to the underlying isoHTL screening, our results provide a new practical avenue to go beyond isotropic screening prescriptions in future kinetic applications.

The kernel allows us to compute the jet-medium potential or dipole cross section
C(\mathrm{\mathbf{b}},\tau)
in the impact parameter space.
To describe its small-distance behavior relevant for jet quenching, we derive and verify a simple cutoff-independent expression (
9
).
We find that it
is smaller than in a thermal system
for
\lambda=10
during the entire pre-hydrodynamic evolution,
leading to a reduction of jet quenching during the initial stages.
Although this remains a quantitatively modest effect, possibly explaining the
relative suppression of jet quenching during the initial stages as proposed in
Andres et al. 2020b
,
our results
highlight the importance of understanding nonequilibrium features in jet phenomenology, which is especially relevant for smaller collision systems.

To estimate the impact of the nonequilibrium kernel, we computed the gluon emission rates,
which are relevant for jet energy loss and used as input for QCD kinetic theory.
Remarkably, we find that using an angular-averaged collision kernel constitutes a good approximation for the nonequilibrium rate.
However, the latter significantly differs from the thermal rate, again demonstrating the importance of nonequilibrium dynamics.
We also observe that the approximated rate
\gamma^{T_{\ast}}
differs drastically from the actual nonequilibrium
\gamma
, with differences of up to
300\,\%
at early times.
Since it
is employed in QCD kinetic theory simulations, our findings question the current treatment of inelastic splittings therein, with yet unexplored consequences.
It will be important to investigate this effect in future kinetic theory studies.

While we have focused here on the unpolarized gluon emission rate, several more differential observables are known to be sensitive to plasma and collision-kernel anisotropies
Hauksson and Iancu 2023
;
Hauksson and Gale 2024
;
Barata et al. 2024b
. Assessing the impact of our anisotropic kernel on these observables will be an important direction for future work.

Appendix A
Details on extracting the collision kernel

Here, we provide details on the collision kernel and our implementation. We also discuss its symmetries and analytic limits.

A.1
Collision kernel from the scattering rate

Similar to Ref.
Boguslavski et al. 2024d
for the jet quenching parameter, we start with the elastic scattering rate

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\text{el}}=\frac{1}{4p\nu_{a}}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}{\mathrm{\mathbf{k}}}\,}{(2\pi)^{3}2k}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}{\mathrm{\mathbf{p^{\prime}}}}\,}{(2\pi)^{3}2p^{\prime}}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}{\mathrm{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime}}\,}{(2\pi)^{3}2k^{\prime}}\,|\mathcal{M}|^{2}

(13)

\displaystyle\times(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{4}(P+K-P^{\prime}-K^{\prime})f(\mathrm{\mathbf{k}})(1\pm f(\mathrm{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime}))(1\pm f(\mathrm{\mathbf{p}}^{\prime})),

(14)

from which the collision kernel can be obtained via
Caron-Huot 2009

\displaystyle C(\mathrm{\mathbf{q}}_{\perp})=(2\pi)^{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}{\Gamma_{\text{el}}}\,}{\mathrm{d}^{2}{\mathrm{\mathbf{q}}_{\perp}}\,}.

(15)

Note that Eq. (
14
) is symmetric in
p^{\prime}\leftrightarrow k^{\prime}
(switching the
u
and
t
channels), which allows us to enforce
p^{\prime}>k^{\prime}
and produces a factor
2
.
We first integrate over
\mathrm{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime}
using the delta function and then perform a variable substitution
\mathrm{\mathbf{p}}^{\prime}=\mathrm{\mathbf{p}}+\mathrm{\mathbf{q}}
. We pull out the integral over
\mathrm{\mathbf{q}}_{\perp}
, and then arrive at Eq. (
2
) from the main text,

\displaystyle C(\mathrm{\mathbf{q}}_{\perp})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2p\nu}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}{\mathrm{\mathbf{k}}}\,}{(2\pi)^{3}}\mathrm{d}{q_{\parallel}}\,\frac{|\mathcal{M}|^{2}}{8|\mathrm{\mathbf{k}}||\mathrm{\mathbf{k}}-\mathrm{\mathbf{q}}||\mathrm{\mathbf{p}}+\mathrm{\mathbf{q}}|}

(16)

\displaystyle\times f(\mathrm{\mathbf{k}})(1+f(\mathrm{\mathbf{k}}-\mathrm{\mathbf{q}}))\delta(p+|\mathrm{\mathbf{k}}|-|\mathrm{\mathbf{p}}+\mathrm{\mathbf{q}}|-|\mathrm{\mathbf{k}}-\mathrm{\mathbf{q}}|).

We write this here for a plasma of gluons, which is also what we consider numerically.
In the limit
p\to\infty
, the matrix element for gluon-gluon scattering reduces to
Boguslavski et al. 2024d

\displaystyle\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{|\mathcal{M}|^{2}}{p^{2}}=4d_{A}C_{A}^{2}\left|G_{R}(P-P^{\prime})_{\mu\nu}(P+P^{\prime})^{\mu}(K+K^{\prime})^{\nu}\right|^{2},

(17)

where we use the full isotropic hard-thermal loop propagator
G_{R}
to screen the internal soft gluon propagator due to medium effects as explained in Refs.
Arnold et al. 2003
;
Boguslavski and Lindenbauer 2024
.

The delta function can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\delta(q_{\parallel}-k+k^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{k^{\prime}}{kq}\delta\left(\cos\theta_{kq}-\frac{q_{\parallel}}{q}+\frac{t}{2kq}\right)\Theta(k-q_{\parallel}),

(18)

which only leads to a contribution if
k>\frac{q_{\parallel}+q}{2}
.
We then arrive at the expression as implemented in our numerical framework

\displaystyle C(\mathrm{\mathbf{q}}_{\perp})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{16\nu(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{d}{\phi_{qk}}\,\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}{q_{\parallel}}\,}{q}\int_{\frac{q+q_{\parallel}}{2}}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}{k}\,

\displaystyle\times\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{|\mathcal{M}|^{2}}{p^{2}}f(\mathrm{\mathbf{k}})(1+f(\mathrm{\mathbf{k}}-\mathrm{\mathbf{q}})).

(19)

A.2
Symmetries of the collision kernel

Let us discuss the symmetries of
C(\mathrm{\mathbf{q}}_{\perp})
and
C(\mathrm{\mathbf{b}})
. First, in momentum space, we can write
C(\mathrm{\mathbf{q}}_{\perp})=C(q_{z},q_{y})
. Using the symmetry of the distribution function
f(k,\cos\theta_{k})=f(k,-\cos\theta_{k})
, which amounts to mirroring at the
z=0
plane, we obtain
C(q_{z},q_{y})=C(-q_{z},q_{y})
. Additionally, the distribution function is symmetric under rotations in the transverse plane, implying
C(q_{z},q_{y})=C(q_{z},-q_{y})
. We thus obtain

\displaystyle C(q_{z},q_{y})=C(\pm q_{z},\pm q_{y}).

(20)

From the Fourier transform

\displaystyle C(b_{z},b_{y})

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}{\mathrm{\mathbf{q}}_{\perp}}\,}{(2\pi)^{2}}(1-e^{iq_{z}b_{z}+iq_{y}b_{y}})\,C(q_{z},q_{y})

(21)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}{\mathrm{\mathbf{q}}_{\perp}}\,}{(2\pi)^{2}}(1-\cos(\mathrm{\mathbf{q}}_{\perp}\cdot\mathrm{\mathbf{b}}))\,C(q_{z},q_{y})

(22)

it follows immediately

\displaystyle C(b_{z},b_{y})=C(\pm b_{z},\pm b_{y}).

(23)

We can therefore map all angles
\phi_{b}>\pi/2
into the first quadrant,

\displaystyle C(b,\phi_{b})=\begin{cases}C(b,\phi_{b}),&0<\phi_{b}<\pi/2\\
C(b,\pi-\phi_{b}),&\pi/2<\phi_{b}<\pi\\
C(b,\phi_{b}-\pi),&\pi<\phi_{b}<3/2\pi\\
C(b,2\pi-\phi_{b}),&3/2\pi<\phi_{b}<2\pi\end{cases}

(24)

Thus, evaluating a Fourier coefficient simplifies to

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{d}{\phi_{b}}\,e^{-im\phi_{b}}D(b,\phi_{b})

(25)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0,&m\text{ odd}\\
4\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\mathrm{d}{\phi}\,_{b}\cos m\phi_{b}\,D(b,\phi_{b}),&m\text{ even}\end{cases}

for any function
D(\phi_{b})
that obeys the symmetry condition (
24
).

A.3
Analytic limits of the collision kernel

Here, we discuss the limits of the collision kernel for large and small
q_{\perp}
.

First, we consider the limit of large transverse momentum transfer, i.e.,
q_{\perp}\equiv|\mathrm{\mathbf{q}}_{\perp}|\to\infty
. It is convenient to start with Eq. (
16
). For
p\to\infty
,
|\mathrm{\mathbf{p}}+\mathrm{\mathbf{q}}|\to p+q_{\parallel}
, when
\mathrm{\mathbf{p}}=(0,0,|\mathrm{\mathbf{p}}|)
, and then the delta function can be rewritten to constrain
q_{\parallel}
,

\displaystyle\delta(k-q_{\parallel}-|\mathrm{\mathbf{k}}-\mathrm{\mathbf{q}}|)\to\frac{q_{\perp}^{2}}{2(k-k_{z})^{2}}\delta(q_{\parallel}-\frac{\mathrm{\mathbf{q}}_{\perp}\cdot\mathrm{\mathbf{k}}_{\perp}-q_{\perp}^{2}/2}{k-k_{z}}),

(26)

where in the normalization we have already taken the leading term in the limit
q_{\perp}\to\infty
. Thus,
q_{\parallel}\approx-q_{\perp}^{2}/(2(k-k_{z}))
, and
q\to\infty
. In this limit, screening effects can be neglected in the matrix element, and we may take the vacuum form

\displaystyle|\mathcal{M}|^{2}=-16g^{4}d_{A}C_{A}^{2}\frac{su}{t^{2}}=16g^{4}d_{A}C_{A}^{2}\frac{4p^{2}(k-k_{z})^{2}}{q_{\perp}^{4}}.

(27)

Inserting this, we obtain

\displaystyle C(\mathrm{\mathbf{q}}_{\perp})

\displaystyle=\frac{4d_{A}C_{A}^{2}g^{4}}{\nu}\frac{1}{q_{\perp}^{4}}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}{\mathrm{\mathbf{k}}}\,}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{k-k_{z}}{k}f(\mathrm{\mathbf{k}}),

(28)

and the collision kernel is therefore proportional to the number density
J^{0}=n=\nu\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}{\mathrm{\mathbf{p}}}\,}{(2\pi)^{3}}f(\mathrm{\mathbf{p}})
and the number current in the jet direction
J^{z}
. The number current vanishes when the distribution function is symmetric under
f(k,\cos\theta_{k})=f(k,-\cos\theta_{k})
.
Using that, we obtain Eq. (
4a
) in the main text. More generally, including fermions, the large distance limit of the collision kernel is given by

\displaystyle\lim_{|\mathrm{\mathbf{q}}_{\perp}|\to\infty}C(\mathrm{\mathbf{q}}_{\perp})\to\frac{g^{4}C_{R}\mathcal{N}}{q_{\perp}^{4}},

(29)

where
\mathcal{N}

is given by Eq. (
3
).

We shall now consider the opposite limit,
|\mathrm{\mathbf{q}}_{\perp}|\to 0
, where screening effects become important.
For isotropic systems, the
q_{\parallel}
integral can be performed analytically using a sum rule
Aurenche et al. 2002a
(see also Appendix B of Ref.
Boguslavski et al. 2024d
), which leads to Eq. (
4b
).
However, for anisotropic distribution functions, this is more intricate, but still results in a similar behavior
1/q_{\perp}^{2}
, which we demonstrate in the following.
Starting with Eq. (
19
) for the collision kernel
C(\mathrm{\mathbf{q}}_{\perp})
,
we want to evaluate its small
q_{\perp}
behavior, for which we need an explicit expression of the matrix element.
Using the notation of Ref.
Boguslavski et al. 2024d
, for the small
q_{\perp}
region, it is sufficient to consider one of the dominant contributions to the matrix element that demonstrates the
1/q_{\perp}^{2}
behavior, for instance, the term including the transverse propagator

\displaystyle|\mathcal{M}|^{2}

\displaystyle\ni\tilde{c}_{2}^{2}|G_{T}|^{2}.

(30)

We can now use the sum rule
Aurenche et al. 2002a
to perform the
q_{\parallel}
integral analytically, which indeed leads to
\sim 1/q_{\perp}^{2}
as in Eq. (
4a
) within the isoHTL approximation of the matrix element, even for an anisotropic distribution function.
Note that the prefactor can in general depend on the angle of
\mathrm{\mathbf{q}}_{\perp}
.
However, a more careful analysis of its angular dependence is not necessary for our purposes, since we primarily use this limiting form to decrease the error in the numeric Fourier transform (
6
).

Appendix C
Numerical method to solve the anisotropic rate equation

As mentioned in the main text, we apply a novel method developed in the companion paper
Lindenbauer
that extends Ref.
Aurenche et al. 2002b
to solve the self-consistent integral equation (
12
), and evaluate the rate
\gamma
in (
11
) for a general anisotropic kernel
C(\mathrm{\mathbf{q}}_{\perp})
.
Equation (
12
) is solved here in impact parameter space, where it can be written as

\displaystyle(A-D(z,\mathrm{\mathbf{b}})-B\nabla^{2})\,\mathrm{\mathbf{F}}(\mathrm{\mathbf{b}})=-2i\nabla\delta(\mathrm{\mathbf{b}}),

(31)

with
A=im_{D}^{2}/(4p)\,\times(1/z+1/(1-z)-1)
,
B=i/(2pz(1-z))
and
D(z,\mathrm{\mathbf{b}})=-\frac{1}{2}\left(C(\mathrm{\mathbf{b}})+C(z\mathrm{\mathbf{b}})+C((1-z)\mathrm{\mathbf{b}})\right)
, and
z
is the energy fraction of the emitted gluon, i.e.,
p\to zp+(1-z)p
.
Its numerical evaluation
uses the small-distance (Eq. (
9
)) and large-distance limits

2
2

2

The integral (
6
) is dominated when the exponent is
\mathcal{O}(1)
, where
q_{\perp}\sim 1/b\to 0
, and
C(q_{\perp})\sim 1/q_{\perp}^{2}
, leading to a logarithmic behavior for large
b
.

of
C(\mathrm{\mathbf{b}})
,

\displaystyle C(\mathrm{\mathbf{b}})\to\begin{cases}b^{2}(a_{1}\log b+a_{2}),&b\to 0\\
a_{3}\log b+a_{4},&b\to\infty,\end{cases}

(32)

where the coefficients
a_{j}
may depend on the angle
\phi_{b}
.
We checked that our numerically computed
C(\mathrm{\mathbf{b}})
obeys these analytic limits.

The angular information is then decomposed
in Fourier modes.
While in the isotropic case,
\mathrm{\mathbf{F}}\sim\mathrm{\mathbf{b}}
, and Eq. (
31
) for small
b
only has two linearly independent solutions, in general, there exist two linearly independent solutions for each Fourier mode, leading to infinitely many different solutions in total.
The boundary conditions are dictated for small

by the delta function in Eq. (
31
), and by requiring that
F(\mathrm{\mathbf{b}})\to 0
for
b\to\infty
, as well as that the rate (

While in the isotropic case, it is enough to solve two independent ordinary differential equations, in the anisotropic case, we need to solve
n_{\mathrm{fourier}}+3
different systems of
n_{\mathrm{fourier}}
coupled ordinary differential equations.
We choose to solve for 7 and 11 Fourier modes to make sure that our results do not depend on the truncation of the Fourier series and refer to

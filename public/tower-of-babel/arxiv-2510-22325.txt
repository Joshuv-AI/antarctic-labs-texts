Multi-height analysis of rational points of toric stacks
Nicolas Bongiorno

Abstract
. We study the multi-height distribution of rational points of toric stacks over 𝐐 \mathbf{Q} using the lift of the number of points to an extended universal torsor.



1.
Introduction

For a quasi-Fano variety
V
with infinitely many rational point, one may study asymptotically the finite set of rational points of bounded height
H
associated with the anticanonical line bundle
\omega_{V}^{-1}
. In
[
23
]
,
[
3
]
and
[
31
]
, Batyrev, Franke, Manin, Tschinkel and Peyre provided strong evidence supporting conjectures relating the asymptotic behaviour of the number of rational points of bounded height on open subsets of
V
to geometric invariants of
V
.

In
[
34
, Question 4.8]
, Peyre proposed a new framework to study the asymptotic distribution of rational points of bounded height. Instead of considering a height relative to a single line bundle, it is natural to consider all possible heights. In
[
5
, Theorem 1.14]
, the author showed that the expected asymptotic behaviour holds for smooth, projective and split toric varieties over
\mathbf{Q}
, using a lift of the count to universal torsors. This approach is inspired by the work of Salberger in
[
37
]
and employs the method of counting lattice points in a bounded domain, developed in
[
16
]
.

The purpose of this work is to extend the multi-height formalism to Deligne–Mumford (DM) stacks and show that, for toric stacks, one can study the multi-height distribution of rational points and interpret the results using geometric invariants of the stacks.

When studying rational points on stacks, the main issue is that even for a proper DM stack, a rational point does not necessarily extend to an integral point, as pointed out in
[
35
]
and
[
18
]
. In the latter article, Ellenberg, Satriano, and Zureick-Brown showed that instead of extending a rational point to an integral point, it is possible to extend it to a stacky integral lift (see
[
18
, Proposition 2.5]
). Later, Loughran and Santens established an analogous result in the local and tame cases, offering a new perspective (see
[
26
, Paragraph 4.4]
).

In
[
13
]
, Darda and Yasuda introduced a new framework for working with heights over DM stacks. The main innovation in their paper is the introduction of an
\mathbf{R}
-vector space that incorporates both the usual Picard group
\Pic(X)_{\mathbf{R}}
and contributions from the twisted sectors. These sectors capture the obstruction, at a given place, to lifting a rational point to an integral one.

This formalism allows Darda and Yasuda—and later Loughran and Santens—to reinterpret the work of number theorists on Malle’s and Bhargava’s conjectures (see
[
27
]
and
[
4
]
) through the lens of Manin’s program for
BG
. Many results have been obtained in this direction by the aforementioned authors.

The work of Gundlach (see
[
21
]
) and, respectively, the work of Shankar and Thorne (see
[
39
]
) can be reinterpreted as instances of a multi-height conjecture over
BG
for
G
an abstract finite abelian group, and over
B\mathfrak{S}_{3}
, respectively. In the case of toric stacks, the asymptotic behaviour of the number of rational points bounded by the height associated with the orbifold anticanonical bundle was obtained by Darda and Yasuda in
[
14
, Theorem 1.3.1]
, using the Poisson formula on the height zeta function. The non-simplicial nature of the cone of convergence of the height zeta function complicates the use of analytic number theory in the multi-height setting. To address this, the author uses an extended integral parametrization of the rational points of toric stacks (see Theorem
6.17
), closely analogous to the one associated with the universal torsor over toric varieties. The role of the Néron–Severi torus
T_{\NS}
is played by the torus associated with the orbifold Picard group
\Pic_{\orb}(X)
. This group was introduced in
[
10
, Definition 17]
and discovered independently for
BG
by Loughran and Santens (see
[
26
, Definition 3.9]
).

In this paper, we prove the following result on the asymptotic behaviour of the multi-height of rational points on toric stacks:

Theorem 1.1
.

We assume that
X
is a toric stack over
\mathbf{Q}
. Let
\mathrm{D}_{1}
be a compact polyhedron of
\Pic_{\orb}(X)^{\vee}_{\mathbf{R}}
and
u
be an element of the interior of the dual of the effective cone
(\text{C}_{\text{eff},\text{orb}}(X)^{\vee})^{\circ}
. For a real number
B>1
, we set:

\mathrm{D}_{B}=\mathrm{D}_{1}+\log(B)u.

Then the multi-height asymptotic behaviour is of the form:

\sharp(X(\mathbf{Q}))_{h\in\mathrm{D}_{B}}\underset{B\rightarrow+\infty}{\sim}\nu(\mathrm{D}_{1})\tau_{\orb}(X)B^{\langle\omega_{X,\orb}^{-1},u\rangle}

where
\tau_{\orb}(X)
is defined in
6.48
.

A key ingredient in the study of rational points on toric stacks is that, in this case, a sector is completely determined by the age pairing with
\Pic(X)
, and moreover, the age can be lifted to universal torsors (see Corollary
5.18
and Theorem
4.17
). In addition to studying the multi-height behaviour of rational points on toric stacks, we extend the definition of the residue map
\psi_{v}
introduced in
[
13
, Definition 2.17]
, under additional hypotheses on the universal torsor of
X
(see Corollary
4.15
), we establish new conditions for when
\Pic_{\mathcal{C}}(X)
is torsion-free for some
\mathcal{C}\subset\pi_{0}^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X)
when
X
is a toric stack (see Theorem
5.19
) and we define a Tamagawa number for a nice DM stack that we relate to the groupoid counting of
\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X(\mathbf{F}_{v})
for
v
a finite place (see Theorem
6.47
).

1.1.
Terminology

We begin by recalling the hypotheses of
[
13
]
on the Deligne-Mumford stacks we should work with:

Definition 1.2
.

A

is a Deligne-Mumford stack
X
over a field
\mathbf{K}
satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

X
is separated, geometrically irreducible and smooth over
\mathbf{K}
,

(2)

the coarse moduli space of
X
, denoted

, is a projective
\mathbf{K}
-scheme,

(3)

X
is not isomorphic to
\Spec(\mathbf{K})
.

If
\mathbf{K}
is a number field, we shall also assume that we have a proper, normal model
\mathcal{X}
of
X
over
\mathcal{O}_{S}
the ring of
S
-integers of
\mathbf{K}
with
S
a finite set of finite places of
\mathbf{K}
such that the diagonal

\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}

is finite and
\mathcal{X}
is almost a tame stack. By almost a tame stack the author means that there exists

a finite set of

which contains

such that for any geometric point

of
\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{O}_{S^{\prime}}}(k)

does not divide the cardinality of

.

The classifying stack

where

is a finite étale group scheme and proper toric stacks are examples of nice stacks, with a natural model defined over
\mathcal{O}_{K}
.

Notations
.

If
X
is a Deligne-Mumford stack and
S
is a scheme, we shall denote by
X[S]
the groupoid of its
S
-points and by
X(S)
the set obtained when we quotient by the isomorphism relation.

We denote by
N_{X}
the exponent of
X
, that is to say the least common multiple of the cardinal of the stabilizers of each geometric point of

. As the diagonal

is finite, such integral exponent exists.

1.2.
Outline of the article

In the second section of this article, we recall the machinery introduced in
[
18
]
,
[
13
]
and
[
26
]
to study the rational points of nice DM stacks. In the third section, we present the age pairing and the integral orbifold Picard group and we recall the definition of heights over nice DM stacks. The fourth section aims at showing how one can lift the age pairing to the universal torsor. We also construct a residue map in the wild case provided some additional hypotheses. In the fifth section, we recall the definition and the main properties of toric stacks. We also establish some useful new properties related to the age pairing and the orbifold Picard group of toric stacks. In the sixth section, we present an integral parametrization of the rational points of toric stacks. The last section aims at studying the multi-height asymptotic behaviour of the rational points of toric stacks.

2.
Stacky integral points and the residue map

If
X
is a proper model of a nice variety over
\mathcal{O}_{S}
, the map
X(\mathcal{O}_{S})\rightarrow X(\mathbf{K})
is a bijection. This is no longer necessarily the case for proper stacks: the map is not always surjective. In this section, we explain how to address the problem of extending rational points to integral points. In the global setting, we recall the definition and main properties of the notion of a “tuning stack” as introduced in
[
18
]
which we shall refer to as a
stacky integral lift
. In the local setting, we make use of the stacky Hensel’s lemma provided in
[
26
]
. In the third part of this section, we introduce the notion of twisted sectors. These were first applied in an arithmetic context by Darda and Yasuda in
[
13
]
, and, via the residue map, have proven to be particularly useful for understanding the obstruction to lifting a rational point to an integral one.

2.1.
Global extension of a rational point of a nice stack

For this paragraph, we consider a Dedekind ring
\mathcal{O}
with fraction field
\mathbf{K}
and
X_{\mathcal{O}}
a proper DM stack which is the model of a nice DM stack
X_{\mathbf{K}}
. We follow
[
18
, section 2.1]
.

Definition 2.1
.

For a rational point
P\in X(\mathbf{K})
, any triplet
(\mathcal{C},\bar{P},p)
such that
\mathcal{C}
is a normal separated Deligne-Mumford stack,
p:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\Spec(\mathcal{O})
a birational coarse space map and the following diagram is 2-commutative:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Spec(K)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Spec(\mathcal{O})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bar{P}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}

is called a

which extends

or a

of

.

One can naturally define a notion of morphism between stacky integral lifts of

. If

is terminal among the stacky integral points which extend

, we shall call it the universal stacky lift of
P
. In
[
18
, section 2.1]
, the author gives an existence theorem for this universal stacky lift and a useful characterization. Let us recall it here. We begin with the following proposition which is well-known for nice schemes and proved in
[
36
, proposition B.1]
for nice stacks:

Proposition 2.2
.

With our hypotheses, given a rational point
P\in X(\mathbf{K})
, there exists a finite set
S
of maximal ideals of
\mathcal{O}
, such that
P
can be extended to an integral point
\bar{P}\in X(\mathcal{O}_{S})
where
\mathcal{O}_{S}
is the Dedekind ring associated with
\Spec(\mathcal{O})-S
.

By
[
18
, proposition 2.5]
, one can construct the universal stacky lift of
P
as follows:

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
P\in X(\mathbf{K})
. If
U\rightarrow X
is any extension of
P
for
U
a non empty open subset of
\Spec(\mathcal{O})
, then the relative normalisation of
X
in
U
,

\bar{P}:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow X

is a universal lift of
P
, and in particular it is independent of the choice of the lift
U\rightarrow X
of
P
.

Moreover we have the following characterization of the universal stacky lift associated to
P
(
[
18
, corollary 2.6]
):

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
(\mathcal{C},\bar{P},p)
be a stacky integral point which extends
P
. Then it is the universal lift of
P
if and only if
\bar{P}:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
is representable.

Remark 2.5
.

The construction of the universal stacky lift of a rational point is defined regardless of whether
X
is tame or not. Moreover if
X\rightarrow\Spec(\mathcal{O})
is tame, we have a description of
\mathcal{C}
as a root stack that we shall only use here in the local case.

2.2.
The stacky Hensel’s lemma and its interpretations

Now we focus on the case where
\mathcal{O}
is a henselian discrete valuation ring (DVR) with valuation
v
. We write
\mathfrak{p}
for the maximal ideal of
\mathcal{O}
and
\mathbf{F}
for its residue field, which is supposed to be finite. We suppose moreover that
X
is tame over
\mathcal{O}
. We follow the presentation of
[

Notations
.

Let
n\in\mathbf{N}^{*}
. We follow the notations from
[
26
]
. We denote by
\Spec(\mathcal{O}[\sqrt[n]{v}])

\sqrt[n]{\mathfrak{p}/\Spec(\mathcal{O})}

H^{i}(\mathcal{O}[\sqrt[n]{v}],\mathcal{F})=H^{i}(\Spec(\mathcal{O}[\sqrt[n]{v}]),\mathcal{F})

As tame stacky curves can be described as root stacks, we obtain from the previous paragraph or via

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
P\in X(\mathbf{K})
be a rational point. There exists a unique

\Spec(\mathcal{O}[\sqrt[n]{v}])\rightarrow X

The stack

Proposition 2.7
.

For any group of multiplicative type

\mathcal{O}

H^{i}(\mathcal{O}[\sqrt[n]{v}],T)=H^{i}(B\mu_{n,\mathbf{F}},T_{\mathbf{F}})=H^{i}(\mu_{n,\mathbf{F}},T_{\mathbf{F}}).

If

is a uniformizer, we have a quotient description of the root stack

\Spec(\mathcal{O}[\sqrt[n]{v}])=\left[\Spec\left(\mathcal{O}[t]/(t^{n}-\pi)\right)/\mu_{n}\right].

This gives in some particular cases, an important interpretation of the root stack over the valuation ring of a p-adic field:

Proposition 2.8
.

Suppose that
\mathbf{K}
is a
p
-adic field, we write
\mathbf{K}^{\prime}=\mathbf{K}[\pi^{\frac{1}{n}}]

\Spec(\mathcal{O}[\sqrt[n]{v}])=\left[\Spec\left(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{K}^{\prime}}\right)/\mu_{n,\mathcal{O}}\right].

In order to have a more intrinsic way to think about a rational point, we shall also use the notion of infinite root stack from
[
40
]

\Spec(\mathcal{O}[\sqrt[\infty]{v}])=\underset{\leftarrow}{\lim}\Spec(\mathcal{O}[\sqrt[n]{v}]).

The map

2.3.
Twisted sectors and the residue map

2.3.1.
The cyclotomic inertia stack

In
[
13
]
, Darda and Yasuda proposed to control the ramification (or the stackyness) of the universal stacky lift associated with a rational point using the notion of twisted sectors.

Notations
.

For this paragraph,
\mathbf{K}
is a number field. We denote by
M_{\mathbf{K}}
the set of places of
\mathbf{K}
, by
M_{\mathbf{K}}^{0}
the set of finite places, and by
M_{\mathbf{K}}^{\infty}
the set of infinite places. For
S
a finite subset of
M_{\mathbf{K}}^{0}
, we write
\mathcal{O}_{S}
for the ring of
S
-integers. We shall suppose that
X
is a nice stack over
\mathbf{K}
with a proper integral model
X_{\mathcal{O}_{S}}
.

For

, we denote by
\mathcal{O}_{v}
the valuation ring associated with

,
\mathbf{F}_{v}
its residue field, and
{\mathbf{K}}_{v}
its fraction field.

Darda and Yasuda defined a residue map which characterizes the obstruction to lifting a point

as an integral point. This allows them to define families of relevant heights over nice stacks and to formulate precise conjectures on the distribution of rational points on
X
. Let us begin by defining the cyclotomic inertia stack

, which is called
the stack of 0-twisted jets
of

in
[

, definition 2.3]
.

Definition 2.11
.

\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X=\bigcup\limits_{l>0}\underline{\Hom}^{\rep}(B\mu_{l},X)

The definition can be applied over any base scheme and in particular over
\mathcal{O}_{S}
.

Definition 2.12
.

The connected components of
\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X
,
\pi_{0}(\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X)
, are called the sectors of
X
. This is a pointed set, with one distinguished sector given by
[X]
, which shall be written
0\in\pi_{0}(\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X)
. All the others are called the twisted sectors. The set of twisted sectors is denoted by
\pi_{0}^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X)
.

Remark 2.13
.

With our assumptions,
\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X
is smooth (see
[
13
, lemma 2.7]
); hence
\pi_{0}(\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X)
is the set of generic representable morphism
b:B\mu_{r}\rightarrow X
. We shall denote by
r_{b}
the exponent associated with an element
b\in\pi_{0}(\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X)
.

Example 2.14
.

•

The set of sectors of
BG
over
\mathbf{K}
is in bijection with the set of
\mathbf{K}
-conjugacy classes of
G
(see
[
13
, example 2.15]
). For example, if
G=\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, this corresponds to the conjugacy classes of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
.

•

The set of sectors of the weighted projective space

is in bijection with
\bigcup\limits_{0\leqslant i\leqslant n}\frac{1}{w_{i}}\mathbf{Z}\cap[0,1[
(see
[
29
, proposition 3.9]
).

By
[
13
, proposition 2.11]
, we have the following useful characterization:

Proposition 2.15
.

\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X=\underline{\Hom}(B\widehat{\mu},X)\text{ and }\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X_{\mathcal{O}_{S}}=\underline{\Hom}(B\widehat{\mu}_{\mathcal{O}_{S}},X_{\mathcal{O}_{S}}).

Moreover, if
N\in\mathbf{N}^{*}
is an integer such that the cardinal of any geometric stabilizer of

divides
N
, then:

\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X=\underline{\Hom}(B\mu_{N},X)\text{ and }\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X_{\mathcal{O}_{S}}=\underline{\Hom}(B\mu_{N,\mathcal{O}_{S}},X_{\mathcal{O}_{S}}).

We shall suppose for the rest of this article that
\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X_{\mathcal{O}_{S}}
is flat. This is true at least for toric stacks and for classifying stacks of the form

. By
[

, corollary 2.8]
, we obtain:

Proposition 2.16
.

The following maps are bijections:

\pi_{0}(\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X_{\mathbf{K}})\rightarrow\pi_{0}(\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X_{\mathcal{O}_{S}})

and for any

\pi_{0}(\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X_{\mathbf{K}_{v}})\rightarrow\pi_{0}(\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X_{\mathcal{O}_{v}}).

2.3.2.
Relation with the inertia stack

Suppose we work in a geometric setting over an algebraically closed field
\mathbf{F}=\overline{\mathbf{F}}
or that the action of the absolute Galois group of the base field
\mathbf{F}
is trivial on
\pi_{0}(\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X)
, i.e. we have a bijection
\pi_{0}(\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X)\rightarrow\pi_{0}(\mathcal{I}_{\mu}\overline{X})
. Over an algebraically closed field, one may identify the cyclotomic inertia stack
\mathcal{I}_{\mu}\overline{X}
with the inertia stack
I\overline{X}
of
\overline{X}
(see
[
41
, proposition 22]
). Suppose
\overline{X}=[Z/G]
and
\Lambda
is a set of representatives of
\Conj_{\text{fin}}(G)
the set of conjugacy classes of the elements of finite order of
G
. Recall that we have
I\overline{X}\simeq[I_{G}Z/G]
where:

I_{G}Z=\{(g,z)\mid g.z=z\}

and
G
acts on
I_{G}Z
via:

(h,(g,z))\longmapsto(h.g.h^{-1},h.z).

Hence:

I\overline{X}\simeq\bigsqcup\limits_{g\in\Lambda}[Z^{g}/C_{G}(g)]

where
Z^{g}
is the set of points fixed by
g
and
C_{G}(g)
is the centralizer of
g
.

Proposition 2.17
.

If for any
g\in\Lambda
,
Z^{g}
is connected, then the set of sectors of
\overline{X}
is given by

\left\{[Z^{g}/C_{G}(g)]\mid g\in\Lambda\right\}.

2.3.3.
The residue map

We suppose here again that
\mathbf{K}
is a number field. Let
v\in M_{\mathbf{K}}^{0}
be a finite place such that
X_{\mathcal{O}_{v}}
is a tame nice stack. In
[
26
, paragraph 4.4]
, Loughran and Santens showed that the right way to define a reduction mod
\mathfrak{p}_{v}
was to consider a map with values in
\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X(\mathbf{F}_{v})
.

Using
[
26
, lemma 4.2]
, we get that for any
P\in X(\mathbf{K}_{v})
, one can associate a unique (up to a 2-isomorphism) lift
\Spec(\mathcal{O}_{v}[\sqrt[n]{v}])\rightarrow X
which gives a representable morphism
B\mu_{n,\mathbf{F}_{v}}\rightarrow X
. Hence, in this way, we define a natural reduction map:

-\text{mod}\ \mathfrak{p}_{v}:X(\mathbf{K}_{v})\rightarrow\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X(\mathbf{F}_{v}).

By
[
26
, lemma 4.3]
, this map is well-defined because the map between the groupoids

X[\mathbf{K}_{v}]\rightarrow\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X[\mathbf{F}_{v}]

3.
The age pairing and the orbifold Picard group

3.1.
The age pairing

For any
n\in\mathbf{N}^{*}
, for any connected scheme

, we have the following canonical isomorphism:

\Pic(B\mu_{n,T})=\Hom_{\grp}(\mu_{n,T},\mathbf{G}_{m,T})=\frac{1}{n}\mathbf{Z}/\mathbf{Z}.

Hence,

\Pic(B\widehat{\mu}_{T})=\underset{\rightarrow}{\text{colim}}\Pic(B\mu_{n,T})=\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z}.

Definition 3.1
.

The age pairing over
T
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\age:\ \mathcal{I}_{\mu}X(T)\times\Pic(X)\rightarrow\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z}

\displaystyle\left(f:B\mu_{n,T}\rightarrow X,L\right)\longmapsto f^{*}L.

By
[
1
, paragraph 7.1]
or
[
13
, lemma 2.22]
,
\age(f,L)
depends only on the connected component to which
f
belongs, hence the age pairing may be re-written as:

\displaystyle\pi_{0}(\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X)

\displaystyle\times\Pic(X)\rightarrow\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z}

\displaystyle(\mathcal{Y}

\displaystyle,L)\longmapsto\age(\mathcal{Y},L).

Proposition 3.2
.

The age pairing is functorial: for
X\xrightarrow{f}Y
a morphism of nice DM stacks, if we denote again by
f
the induced morphism
\pi_{0}(\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X)\rightarrow\pi_{0}(\mathcal{I}_{\mu}Y)
then for any
\mathcal{Z}\in\pi_{0}(\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X)
and for any

,

\age(f(\mathcal{Z}),L)=\age(\mathcal{Z},f^{*}L).

Moreover, it defines a natural map
\pi_{0}(\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X)\rightarrow\Hom_{\grp}(\Pic(X),\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z})
.

Remark 3.3
.

Recall that we denote by
0=[X]=[\Hom^{\rep}(B\mu_{1},X)]
the trivial connected component in
\pi_{0}(\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X)
. For any

,
\age(0,L)\in\frac{1}{1}.\mathbf{Z}/\mathbf{Z}
i.e
\age(0,-)
is the zero morphism.

Remark 3.4
.

By functoriality, we have that for any
L\in\Pic(X^{\coarse})
and for any
\mathcal{Y}\in\pi_{0}(\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X)
, if we denote by
X\xrightarrow{p}X^{\coarse}
the coarse map,

\age(\mathcal{Y},p^{*}L)=0.

We shall see later (see corollary
5.18
) that for toric stacks the map

\pi_{0}(\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X)\rightarrow\Hom_{\grp}(\Pic(X),\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z})

is an injection. This is not true in general for example in the case of the classifying stack
BG
over
\mathbf{C}
, associated to an abstract finite group
G
, this is true if and only if
G
is abelian. Indeed, this is a consequence of the fact that irreducible characters and characteristic functions of conjugacy classes are two bases of the
\mathbf{C}
-vector space of class functions
Z(\mathbf{C}[G])
.

Nevertheless, we may try to extend corollary
5.18
as follows. Let
R\widehat{\mu}
denote the ring of continuous representation of
\widehat{\mu}
over
\mathbf{C}
. By definition of
\widehat{\mu}
,

R\widehat{\mu}=\underset{\rightarrow}{\text{colim}}R\mu_{n}.

For any representation
\mathcal{V}
of
\mu_{n}
, we have

\mathcal{V}=\bigoplus\limits_{i=1}^{r}\tau_{n}^{a_{i}}

where
\tau_{n}:\mu_{n}\rightarrow\mathbf{G}_{m}
is the regular representation, hence we have the first Chern class

c_{1}:R\mu_{n}\rightarrow\frac{1}{n}\mathbf{Z}/\mathbf{Z}

which maps
\mathcal{V}
to
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}\frac{a_{i}}{n}\mod\mathbf{Z}.
Taking the colimit, it gives the map

c_{1}:R\widehat{\mu}\rightarrow\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z}.

If we pull-back a vector bundle/coherent sheaf
\mathcal{E}
over
X
by a sector
b\in\pi_{0}(\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X)
and we apply the Chern class, we get a natural map:

\pi_{0}(\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X)\rightarrow\Hom(K_{0}(X),\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z})

which may be injective. It is at least true for toric stacks and for
BG
. The fact that it is true for
BG
over
\mathbf{C}
is simply because for any representation
\mathcal{V}
of
G
a finite abstract group, the trace of the pull-back of a sector of

corresponding to a conjugacy class

is the value of the character of the representation
\chi_{\mathcal{V}}(C)
seen as an element of
\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z}
once we have chosen a primitive

.

3.3.
The orbifold Picard group

3.3.1.
Definition

For
\mathcal{C}\subset\pi_{0}^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X)
, the construction of the
\mathcal{C}
-extended Picard group
\Pic_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{X})
was introduced in
[
10
]
and was later rediscovered independently by Loughran and Santens in
[
26
]
.

Definition 3.7
.

The
\mathcal{C}
-extended Picard group

is defined as the group of pairs
(L,\varphi)
where

and
\varphi:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathbf{Q}
such that for any

\varphi(b)+\age(b,L)\in\mathbf{Z}.

That is to say,
\Pic_{\mathcal{C}}(X)
is the kernel of the morphism given by:

\Pic(X)\oplus\bigoplus\limits_{b\in\mathcal{C}}r_{b}^{-1}.\mathbf{Z}\xrightarrow{(\age,\text{pr})}\bigoplus\limits_{b\in\mathcal{C}}r_{b}^{-1}.\mathbf{Z}/\mathbf{Z}\rightarrow 0

where

(\age,\text{pr})
is the map
\left(L,\left(q_{b}\right)_{b\in\mathcal{C}}\right)\longmapsto\bigoplus\limits_{b\in\mathcal{C}}\left(\age(b,L)+[q_{b}]\right)
.

When
\mathcal{C}=\pi_{0}^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X)
, the
\pi_{0}^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X)
-extended Picard group shall be called the orbifold Picard group and shall be denoted

.

Remark 3.8
.

In particular, we recover the construction of Darda and Yasuda of the orbifold Néron-Severi space defined in
[
13
, definition 8.1]
, since we have:

\Pic_{\orb}(X)_{\mathbf{R}}\simeq\Pic(X)_{\mathbf{R}}\oplus\mathbf{R}^{\pi_{0}^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X)}.

Notations
.

For
L\in\Pic(X)
, we denote by
[L]_{\stack}
the class in
\Pic_{\orb}(X)
given by
(L,\varphi_{L})
where
\varphi_{L}:\pi_{0}^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X)\rightarrow\mathbf{Q}
is defined so that for any

. For

, we denote by
[b]
the class in
\Pic_{\orb}(X)
given by
(\mathcal{O}_{X},\delta_{b})
where
\delta_{b}:\pi_{0}^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X)\rightarrow\mathbf{Q}
is the indicator function of

, defined so that for any

\delta_{b}(b^{\prime})=1
if

, and

Proposition 3.9
.

For
\mathcal{C}\subset\pi_{0}^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X)
, we have the following natural exact sequence:

0\rightarrow\Hom(\mathcal{C},\mathbf{Z})\rightarrow\Pic_{\mathcal{C}}(X)\rightarrow\Pic(X)\rightarrow 0.

Proposition 3.10

Suppose that

is a free abelian group of finite rank. Let

be a basis of

\{[L_{i}]_{\stack},\mid 1\leqslant i\leqslant t\}\cup\{[b]\mid b\in\pi_{0}^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X))\}

Proof.

This is a consequence of the fact that the map
\Pic(X)\rightarrow\Pic_{\orb}(X)
which maps

to

defines a section of the exact sequence from proposition
3.9

∎

lies over

Now, we recall the definition of the group of Arakelov heights

(see

Definition 3.23
.

Let

and
L^{\prime}
be two adelic line bundles over
X^{\coarse}
(i.e. equipped with an adelic norm). Let
(||-||_{v})
be the adelic norm on
L
.

We say that
L
and
L^{\prime}
are equivalent as adelic line bundles if there exist

and a family

of non-negative real numbers such that:

(1)

\{v|\lambda_{v}\neq 1\}
is finite and
\prod\limits_{v}\lambda_{v}=1

(2)

there exists an isomorphism of adelic line bundles from

to
L^{\prime\otimes M}
, where the adelic norm on

is induced by the adelic norm on

given by

.

We denote by
\mathcal{H}(X^{\coarse})
the set of equivalence classes of adelic line bundles. This set is naturally equipped with a group structure given by the tensor product of adelic line bundles.

Hence, we can define the notion of system of heights and of multi-heights over

.

Definition 3.24
.

A

over

corresponds to the data of a section

of the forgetful morphism

Such a system of heights defines a

such that for any

is the linear form defined by:

\forall\ L\in\Pic(X^{\coarse}),\ \ h_{\coarse}(P)(L)=\log H_{L}(P).

3.5.
Multi-heights over nice stacks

Heights over nice Deligne-Mumford stacks were defined by Darda and Yasuda in

To define a stacky multi-height over

so that for each line bundle

over

Definition 3.25
.

An adelic stacky data over

where

.

For the rest of this paragraph, we shall suppose that we have fixed an adelic stacky data for a basis of

Let

the
\mathbf{Q}

such that

. Then the logarithm height associated to a pair

with our convention of a system of heights over

h(P)(L,\varphi)=h_{\coarse}(p(P))(L^{\prime})-\sum\limits_{v\in M_{K}^{0}}\varphi_{v}(P).\log(q_{v}).

Notations
.

For

Remark 3.27

This definition behaves very well by pull-back, that is to say that if

and

This was shown in

As the previous definition is additive, we get a natural map, called the stacky multi-height:

Among the heights we may construct, there are more practical heights we shall consider, these are the ones defined by Ellenberg, Satriano and Zureick-Brown in
[

4.1.
Universal torsors for nice stacks

As presented in
[
38
, section 6]
, we may extend the formalism of universal torsor from
[
22
]
(introduced in
[
11
]
) to Deligne-Mumford stacks. Let us recall here the construction. Let
X\xrightarrow{\pi}S
be a DM stack over a scheme
S
which shall be either a Dedekind scheme or a field. We write
\mathcal{D}(X)
for the derived category of sheaves on the small étale site of
X
. Let

KD(X)=(\tau_{\leqslant 1}R\pi_{*}\mathbf{G}_{m,X})[1]

the shift of the truncation of
R\pi_{*}\mathbf{G}_{m,X}
. The map
\mathbf{G}_{m,S}\rightarrow R\pi_{*}\mathbf{G}_{m,X}
defines a morphism
\mathbf{G}_{m,S}[1]\rightarrow KD(X)
. Its cokernel will be denoted by
K^{\prime}D(X)
. We thus have a distinguished triangle in
\mathcal{D}(S)
:

\mathbf{G}_{m,S}[1]\rightarrow KD(X)\rightarrow K^{\prime}D(X)\xrightarrow{a_{X}}\mathbf{G}_{m,S}[2].

If
\pi
has a section, the triangle splits and
a_{X}=0
. Let
T
be any group of multiplicative type and denote by
\hat{T}
its associated sheaf of characters. Then we may write the first terms of the exact sequence given by the composition via the derived functor
\textbf{RHom}(\hat{T},-)
:

0\rightarrow H^{1}(S,T)\rightarrow H^{1}(X,T)\xrightarrow{\text{type}}\Hom_{S}(\hat{T},KD^{\prime}(X)).

We shall assume here (as will be the case for all the examples we consider) that
\pi_{*}\mathbf{G}_{m,X}=\mathbf{G}_{m,S}
. Hence
KD^{\prime}(X)
is equal to the sheaf
\mathbf{R}^{1}\pi_{*}\mathbf{G}_{m,X}
. Moreover if
\mathcal{T}\rightarrow X
is a
T
-torsor over
S
, and
X\rightarrow BT
is the associated morphism, then the type of the torsor is given via the natural pull-back

\hat{T}=\mathbf{R}^{1}q_{*}\mathbf{G}_{m,BT}\rightarrow\mathbf{R}^{1}\pi_{*}\mathbf{G}_{m,X}.

We denote by
T_{\NS}
the group of multiplicative type whose sheaf of characters is
\mathbf{R}^{1}\pi_{*}\mathbf{G}_{m,X}
.

Definition 4.1
.

Any
T_{\NS}
-torsor with a rational point and whose type corresponds to the identity shall be called a universal torsor over
X
. Up to isomorphism, the universal torsors over
X
are classified by
H^{1}(S,T_{\NS})
.

Suppose
S=\Spec(K)
. Then
H^{0}(K,\mathbf{R}^{1}\pi_{*}\mathbf{G}_{m,X})=\Pic(\overline{X})^{\Gal(\overline{K}/K)}=\Pic(X)
where the last equality comes from the fact that
X
has a rational point. Using the functoriality of the previous exact sequence for the morphism
\underline{\mathbf{Z}}\rightarrow\mathbf{R}^{1}\pi_{*}\mathbf{G}_{m,X}
induced by

, we get the contracted product

\mathcal{T}\overset{T_{\NS},[L]}{\times}\mathbf{G}_{m}\simeq L^{\times}.

The resulting morphism
\mathcal{T}\rightarrow L^{\times}
is invariant under the character

[L]:T_{\NS}\rightarrow\mathbf{G}_{m}.

Now if moreover we fix rational points

and

, then since

H^{0}(X,\mathbf{G}_{m})=\mathbf{K}^{\times},

we get that there exists a unique morphism of pointed torsors

(\mathcal{T},y)\longrightarrow(L^{\times},z)

equivariant for the character
[L]
. We get the following universal property of pointed universal torsor, used in
[

, section 4.3.1]
:

For any pointed universal torsor

with

and

, there exists a unique map
\Phi_{L}:(\mathcal{T},y)\rightarrow(L^{\times},z)
which is
T_{\NS}\xrightarrow{[L]}\mathbf{G}_{m}
-invariant such that we have the following commutative diagram:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(\mathcal{T},y)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(L^{\times},z).}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(X,P)}}

Hypothesis 4.3
.

We shall assume for the rest of this article that
X_{\mathcal{O}}
admits a universal torsor which is a separated scheme over
\mathcal{O}
. Hence, by the definition of the contracted product, any universal torsor over

is a scheme. This hypothesis is verified by toric stacks but it does not hold for

4.2.
Lift of the age to universal torsors

Let us suppose that
\mathcal{O}
is a henselian DVR with valuation

. We write
\mathfrak{p}
for the maximal ideal of
\mathcal{O}
,
\pi
for a uniformizer of
\mathcal{O}
,
\mathbf{F}
for its residue field and
\mathbf{K}
for its fraction field. We shall suppose that
\mathbf{F}
is a perfect field and
\mathbf{K}
is a local field with
\car(\mathbf{K})=0
. We assume hypothesis
4.3
. Recall that
X
is not necessarily tame over
\mathcal{O}
. Let
P\in X(\mathbf{K})
be a rational point and suppose there exists
y\in\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{K})
such that
q(y)=P
. Even if it means twisting a universal torsor
\mathcal{T}\xrightarrow{q}X
, we can always suppose that such

exists. Let us consider a finite extension
\mathbf{K}^{\prime}/\mathbf{K}
and
t\in T_{\NS}(\mathbf{K}^{\prime})
such that

z=t.y\in\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{K}^{\prime}})

where
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{K}^{\prime}}
is the valuation ring of
\mathbf{K}^{\prime}
. Such extension exists because by proposition
2.3
, we have the existence of a stacky lift even in the wild case, hence we just have to consider the field of rational functions of a connected étale presentation of this stacky lift. We can state the following lemma:

Lemma 4.4
.

For any
L\in\Pic(X)
,
\frac{v_{\mathbf{K}^{\prime}}([L](t))}{e(\mathbf{K}^{\prime}/\mathbf{K})}
does not depend on the choice of the finite extension
\mathbf{K}^{\prime}/\mathbf{K}
and of
z\in\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{K}^{\prime}})
such that
q(z)=P_{\mid\mathbf{K}^{\prime}}
, where
e(\mathbf{K}^{\prime}/\mathbf{K})
is the ramification index of the extension.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{K}^{\prime\prime}/\mathbf{K}
be another finite extension such that there exists
z^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{K}^{\prime\prime}})
and
t^{\prime}\in T_{\NS}(\mathbf{K}^{\prime\prime})
verifying
t^{\prime}.y=z^{\prime}
. Let
\mathbf{L}/\mathbf{K}
be a field extension which contains
\mathbf{K}^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{K}^{\prime\prime}
. Then, because
z^{\prime}
and
z
are in the same orbit over
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{L}}
, there exists
u\in T_{\NS}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{L}})
,
t^{\prime}=u.t
. Since
v_{\mathbf{L}}([L](u))=1
, we get:

\frac{v_{\mathbf{K}^{\prime\prime}}([L](t^{\prime}))}{e(\mathbf{K}^{\prime\prime}/\mathbf{K})}=\frac{v_{\mathbf{L}}([L](t^{\prime}))}{e(\mathbf{L}/\mathbf{K})}=\frac{v_{\mathbf{L}}([L](t))}{e(\mathbf{L}/\mathbf{K})}=\frac{v_{\mathbf{K}^{\prime}}([L](t))}{e(\mathbf{K}^{\prime}/\mathbf{K})}.

∎

Using the previous lemma, the following definition has a sense:

Definition 4.5
.

For
y\in\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{K})
, we define

\age(y,-)\in\Pic(X)^{\vee}_{\mathbf{Q}}=\Hom_{\grp}(\Pic(X),\mathbf{Q})

such that for any
L\in\Pic(X)
,
\age(y,L)
is the
\mathbf{Q}
-value
\frac{v_{\mathbf{K}^{\prime}}([L](t))}{e(\mathbf{K}^{\prime}/\mathbf{K})}
where

z=t.y\in\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{K}^{\prime}})

lies in the
T_{\NS}
-orbit of
y
for some finite extension
\mathbf{K}^{\prime}/\mathbf{K}
. We shall denote this by
\age_{v}(y,-)
when we want to emphasize the valuation
v
.

Recall that
\log_{T_{\NS},v}:T_{\NS}(\mathbf{K})\rightarrow\Pic(X)^{\vee}
is defined such that for any
t\in T_{\NS}(\mathbf{K})
and any
L\in\Pic(X)
,

. We have the following useful proposition:

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
y\in\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{K})
and
t\in T_{\NS}(\mathbf{K})

\age_{v}(t.y,-)=\age_{v}(y,-)-\log_{T_{\NS},v}(t).

Example 4.7
.

We consider the case where
X=\mathcal{P}(\omega)
is the weighted projective stack with weight
\omega=(\omega_{0},..,\omega_{n})
. For
y\in\mathbf{K}^{n+1}-\{0\}
, let us write

\min\limits_{0\leqslant i\leqslant n}\frac{v(y_{i})}{\omega_{i}}=m+t

where
t\in\mathbf{Q}\cap[0,1[
and
m\in\mathbf{Z}
. Suppose we write
t=\frac{r}{d}
with
\gcd(r,d)=1
. Consider
\mathbf{L}/\mathbf{K}
a rupture field of

. Let
\gamma\in\mathbf{L}
denote a root of this polynomial. It is a uniformizer of
\mathbf{L}
. Then if

, we have

hence

\age_{v}(y,\mathcal{O}(-1))=-\min\limits_{0\leqslant i\leqslant n}\frac{v(y_{i})}{\omega_{i}}.

We can also describe the behavior of the lift of the age after a field extension:

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
\mathbf{K}^{\prime}/\mathbf{K}
be a finite extension. Let
y\in\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{K})
. Then for any

Hypothesis 4.9
.

We have:

\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{O})=\{y\in\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{K})\mid\age_{v}(y,-)=0\}.

Proposition 4.10
.

If
\mathcal{T}
is an open subscheme of an affine scheme of finite type over

Proof.

For any
y\in\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{O})

. Now let

be such that

where

With our hypothesis on

∎

For each

(||-||_{v})

Definition 4.23

The local degree

(4.1)

4.6.
Parametrization of the rational points of a stack over a local field

We continue under the hypotheses of the previous sections. We fix a set-theoretic section of the natural map

\Hom_{\grp}(\Pic(X),\mathbf{Q})\rightarrow\Hom_{\grp}(\Pic(X),\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z}).

For
\mathcal{Y}\in\pi_{0}(\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X)
, we shall denote by
\widetilde{\age}(\mathcal{Y},-)
the chosen lift of
\age(\mathcal{Y},-)
in
\Hom_{\grp}(\Pic(X),\mathbf{Q})
.

Definition 4.27
.

For
\mathcal{Y}\in\pi_{0}(\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X)
, we define

\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{Y}}(\mathcal{O})=\{y\in\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{K})\mid\age_{v}(y,-)=\widetilde{\age}(\mathcal{Y},-)\}.

We also define

\mathcal{T}^{\stack}(\mathcal{O})=\bigsqcup\limits_{\mathcal{Y}\in\pi_{0}(\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X)}\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{Y}}(\mathcal{O}).

Note that by proposition
4.6
,
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{Y}}(\mathcal{O})
and
\mathcal{T}^{\stack}(\mathcal{O})
are
T_{\NS}(\mathcal{O})
-equivariant. Let us consider the partition of the set

where

.

Theorem 4.28
.

The natural map
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{Y}}(\mathcal{O})\rightarrow q(\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{K}))_{\mathcal{Y}}
gives the parametrization

\mathcal{T}^{\stack}(\mathcal{O})\rightarrow q(\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{K}))
gives the parametrization

Proof.

First we have that
q(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{Y}}(\mathcal{O}))\subset q(\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{K}))_{\mathcal{Y}}
using theorem
4.17
and hypothesis
4.21
. Reciprocally if

and

so that

, then

\age_{v}(y,-)\mod\mathbf{Z}=\age(\mathcal{Y},-).

Because

is surjective, there exists
t\in T_{\NS}(\mathbf{K})
such that

\age_{v}(y,-)-\log_{T_{\NS}}(t)=\widetilde{\age}(\mathcal{Y},-).

Hence by proposition

and

. Now let

be such that

and again proposition

\log_{T_{\NS}}(t)=0

T_{\NS}(\mathcal{O})=\ker(\log_{T_{\NS}})

and we can conclude the proof of the first part of the theorem.
The rest of the theorem result from this part, using moreover that

.

5.
Introduction to toric stacks

5.1.
Preliminaries

In
[
6
]
, Borisov, Chen and Smith introduced toric stacks which was later characterized in different ways in
[
19
]
by Fantechi and Mann. Let us first define the notion of
stacky fan
.

Definition 5.1
.

A stacky fan is a triple

\mathbf{\Sigma}=(\Sigma,\mathbf{Z}^{\Sigma(1)}\xrightarrow{\beta}N)

where
N
is a finitely generated abelian group and
\Sigma
is a rational complete simplicial fan of
N_{\mathbf{Q}}=N\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}}\mathbf{Q}
whose set of rays is denoted by
\Sigma(1)
. We suppose moreover that if we denote by
\overline{\beta}
the composition of
\beta
with the natural map
N\rightarrow N_{\mathbf{Q}}
, then for any edge
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
,
\overline{\beta}(e_{\rho})\in\rho
.

Remark 5.2
.

With this definition,
\coker(\beta)
is finite.

We define a quasi-affine scheme
\mathcal{T}_{\Sigma}
over
\mathbf{Z}
as an open subset of
\mathbf{A}^{\Sigma(1)}
obtained by cutting out a certain closed subset
Z(\Sigma)
. As in
[
12
, Chapter 5, §1]
, we write for a cone of the fan
\sigma\in\Sigma
, the monomial:

X^{\tilde{\sigma}}=\prod\limits_{\rho\not\in\sigma(1)}X_{\rho}

where
\sigma(1)
is the set of rays of the cone
\sigma
. Then we shall use the following definition of the exceptional set
Z(\Sigma)
:

Definition 5.3
.

We denote by
B(\Sigma)
the ideal of
\mathbf{Z}[X_{\rho},\rho\in\Sigma(1)]
generated by the monomials
X^{\tilde{\sigma}}=\prod\limits_{\rho\not\in\sigma(1)}X_{\rho}
where
\sigma
belongs to
\Sigma_{\max}
the set of cones of the fan
\Sigma
of maximal dimension. Then we define the closed subset of
\mathbf{A}^{\Sigma(1)}
as:

Z(\Sigma)=V(B(\Sigma))=\bigcap\limits_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\max}}\left(\bigcup\limits_{\rho\not\in\sigma(1)}\left(X_{\rho}=0\right)\right).

We define
\mathcal{T}_{\Sigma}=\mathbf{A}^{\Sigma(1)}-Z(\Sigma)
.

We shall give here an other description of
\mathcal{T}_{\Sigma}
as an open subset of
\mathbf{A}^{\Sigma(1)}
obtained by cutting out a certain closed subset which can be described in term of primitive collections which are defined as follows (see
[
12
, definition 5.1.5]
):

Definition 5.4
.

A subset
C\subset\Sigma(1)
is a primitive collection if:

•

C\not\subset\sigma(1)
for any
\sigma\in\Sigma

•

for every proper subset
C^{\prime}\subsetneq C
, there exists
\sigma\in\Sigma
such that
C^{\prime}\subset\sigma(1)

Hence
Z(\Sigma)
can be described as a union of irreducible components as in
[
12
, proposition 5.1.6]
:

Proposition 5.5
.

Z(\Sigma)=\bigcup\limits_{C}V(x_{\rho}\mid\rho\in C)

where
C
belongs to the set of primitive collections.

Now let us consider the following exact triangle in
\mathcal{D}(\text{Ab})
:

\mathbf{Z}^{\Sigma(1)}\xrightarrow{\beta}N\rightarrow\coneform(\beta).

We apply the functor
\mathbf{RHom}(-,\mathbf{Z})
, so we get the following long exact sequence:

5.2.

We refer to

such that the following assertions are equivalent:

•

d^{w_{i}}\mid x_{i}

of the theorem

. In the rest of this section, we shall prove the other inclusion

Lemma 6.22
.

For any
(y^{\prime},k)\in\mathcal{T}^{\orb}(\mathbf{Z})
, if we write
P=q^{\ext}(y^{\prime},k)
then for any
\mathcal{Z}\in\pi_{0}^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X)
,
|k_{\mathcal{Z}}|=H_{\mathcal{Z}}(P)

Proof.

Let
(y^{\prime},k)\in\mathcal{T}^{\orb}(\mathbf{Z}_{v})
and
P=q^{\ext}(y^{\prime},k)\in X(\mathbf{Q}_{v})
its image. We want to show that for any
\mathcal{Z}\in\pi_{0}^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X)
,
p_{v}\mid k_{\mathcal{Z}}\Leftrightarrow\psi_{v}(P)=\mathcal{Z}
. This would prove the lemma. To do so, it is enough to show that:

•

if for any
\mathcal{Z}\in\pi_{0}^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X)
,
p_{v}\nmid k_{\mathcal{Z}}
,
\psi_{v}(P)=0
.

•

if
p_{v}\mid k_{\mathcal{Z}}
for
\mathcal{Z}\in\pi_{0}^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X)
, then
\psi_{v}(P)=\mathcal{Z}
.

The first assertion is clear. Suppose
p_{v}\mid k_{\mathcal{Z}}
. Consider

y=r_{2}(y^{\prime},k)\in\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{Q}_{v}),

that is to say for any
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
(see
6.12
),

y_{\rho}=y^{\prime}_{\rho}.\prod\limits_{\mathcal{Z}\in\pi_{0}^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X)}k_{\mathcal{Z}}^{m_{\mathcal{Z}}(\rho)}.

We shall show that

\age_{v}(y,-)=\widetilde{\age}(\mathcal{Z},-)

and hence by theorem
4.17
and corollary
5.18
, we get
\psi_{v}(P)=\mathcal{Z}
.

First remark that for any
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
,

\displaystyle v(y_{\rho})+\widetilde{\age}(\mathcal{Z},[D_{\rho}])

\displaystyle=v(y^{\prime}_{\rho})+m_{\mathcal{Z}}(\rho)+\widetilde{\age}(\mathcal{Z},[D_{\rho}])

\displaystyle=v(y^{\prime}_{\rho})+\age_{\num}(\mathcal{Z},[D_{\rho}])

where the last equality come from lemma
6.20
. Moreover, if we write
b\in\Boxe(\Sigma)
the element associated to
\mathcal{Z}
, then for any primitive collection
C
:

•

if
\sigma(b)\cap C=\varnothing
, we have for any
\rho\in C
,
\age_{\num}(\mathcal{Z},[D_{\rho}])=0
hence

\min\limits_{\rho\in C}\left(v(y_{\rho})+\widetilde{\age}(\mathcal{Z},[D_{\rho}])\right)=\min\limits_{\rho\in C}v(y^{\prime}_{\rho})=0.

•

otherwise, as for any

\age_{\num}(\mathcal{Z},[D_{\rho}])>0
, we have:

\min\limits_{\rho\in C}\left(v(y_{\rho})+\widetilde{\age}(\mathcal{Z},[D_{\rho}])\right)=\min\limits_{\rho\in C-\sigma(b)}v(y^{\prime}_{\rho})=0.

So if we consider
\mathbf{K}/\mathbf{Q}_{v}
an extension such that
e(\mathbf{K}/\mathbf{Q}_{v}).\widetilde{\age}(\mathcal{Z},-)\in\Pic(X)^{\vee}
and

such that
\log_{T_{\NS},v_{\mathbf{K}}}(t)=e(\mathbf{K}/\mathbf{Q}_{v}).\widetilde{\age}(\mathcal{Z},-)
, by what we have seen we have:

z=t.y\in\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{K}})

because for any
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
,
v_{\mathbf{K}}(z_{\rho})=e(\mathbf{K}/\mathbf{Q}_{v}).\left(v(y_{\rho})+\widetilde{\age}(\mathcal{Z},[D_{\rho}])\right)

\age_{v}(y,-)=\frac{1}{e(\mathbf{K}/\mathbf{Q}_{v})}\log_{T_{\NS},v_{\mathbf{K}}}(t)=\widetilde{\age}(\mathcal{Z},-).

∎

Now we can conclude the proof of theorem
6.17
by showing that

\mathcal{T}^{\orb}(\mathbf{Z})\subset\mathcal{T}^{\ext}(\mathbf{Z}).

Proof.

For
(y_{1},k_{1})
and
(y_{2},k_{2})
in
\mathcal{T}^{\orb}(\mathbf{Z})
such that

. Hence up to multiplication by an element of

, we may suppose that

and

is a toric morphism, it is equivariant for the action of the torus of the toric stacks, that is to say

Hence simplifying by

so there exists

y_{2}=t.y_{1}
. Using the embedding

we get that there exists

For

∎

6.5.
Möbius function associated to the extended universal torsor

For the rest of this article, we denote by
\Sigma_{\ext}(1)
the set
\Sigma(1)\cup\pi_{0}^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X).
In this section, we introduce the Möbius function associated to the extended universal torsor
\mathcal{T}^{\ext}(\mathbf{Z})
which parametrizes
X(\mathbf{Q})
.

Notations
.

(1)

\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{T}^{\ext}(\mathbf{Z})}:(\mathbf{N}^{*})^{\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
is the indicator function of

\mathcal{T}^{\ext}(\mathbf{Z})\cap(\mathbf{N}^{*})^{\Sigma_{\ext}(1)},

(2)

for
d\in(\mathbf{N}^{*})^{\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}
, we write
\mathbf{1}_{d}:(\mathbf{N}^{*})^{\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
the indicator function of
\bigoplus\limits_{\rho\in\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}d_{\rho}.\mathbf{Z}\cap(\mathbf{N}^{*})^{\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}
, that is to say we have the following equivalence:

\mathbf{1}_{d}(e)=1\Leftrightarrow d\mid e

where
d\mid e
means
d_{\rho}\mid e_{\rho}
for any
\rho\in\Sigma_{\ext}(1)
.

We want to define and study a Möbius function
\mu:(\mathbf{N}^{*})^{\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}\rightarrow\mathbf{Z}
such that:

\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{T}^{\ext}(\mathbf{Z})}=\sum\limits_{d}\mu(d)\mathbf{1}_{d}.

The rest of the properties we want to establish here are analogous to the ones used by Salberger in
[
37
, lemmas 11.15-11.19]
.

6.5.1.
The local Möbius function

Notations
.

Let us write
\Lambda
the simplicial cone
\mathbf{R}_{+}^{\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}
. For
\lambda\in\Lambda
, we write

|\lambda|=\sum\limits_{\rho\in\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}\lambda_{\rho}.

To simplify the notation, we write
M=\mathbf{Z}^{\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}
and
\log_{v}
for the log map
\log_{T^{\ext},v}
associated to the torus
T^{\ext}(\mathbf{Q}_{v})
and
v\in M_{\mathbf{Q}}^{0}
.

Let us remark that
\mathcal{T}^{\ext}(\mathbf{Z})=\mathcal{T}^{\ext}(\mathbf{Q})\cap\bigcap\limits_{v\in M_{\mathbf{Q}}^{0}}\mathcal{T}^{\ext}(\mathbf{Z}_{v})
. By definition
6.16
,
\mathcal{T}^{\ext}(\mathbf{Z}_{v})
is defined in
\mathcal{T}^{\ext}(\mathbf{Q}_{v})
via a finite number of arithmetical conditions. That is to say there exists a finite set of elements of
\Lambda
,
\{\lambda_{i,v}\}_{i\in I_{v}}
such that if we write

X_{v}=\Lambda-\bigcup\limits_{i\in I_{v}}\Lambda+\lambda_{i,v},

we have

\mathcal{T}^{\ext}(\mathbf{Z}_{v})\cap T^{\ext}(\mathbf{Q}_{v})=\log_{v}^{-1}(X_{v}).

In fact, this arithmetical conditions do not depend on
v
so we can choose a fixed finite set of elements of
\Lambda

\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i\in I}
which do not depend on
v
and we can write
X=X_{v}
for any
v
.

We shall use the results from
[
33
, section 3.3]
. The ring of functions

M\rightarrow\mathbf{Z}

with support in
\Lambda
,
\mathbf{Z}[[\Lambda]]
is an integral ring equipped with the convolution product given by:

fg(y)=\sum\limits_{x\in\Lambda\cap M}f(y-x)g(x).

By
[
33
, lemma 3.3.8]
,
\mathbf{1}_{\Lambda}
admits an inverse in
\mathbf{Z}[[\Lambda]]
. We can write the following definition:

Definition 6.31
.

We define by convolution the element of
\mathbf{Z}[[\Lambda]]
given by

\mu_{\loc}=\mathbf{1}_{X}.(\mathbf{1}_{\Lambda})^{-1}.

That is to say we have:

\mathbf{1}_{X}=\sum\limits_{\lambda\in\Lambda\cap M}\mu_{\loc}(\lambda)\mathbf{1}_{\lambda+\Lambda}.

Notations
.

For
\lambda,\tau\in\Lambda
, we write
\lambda\leqslant\tau
when for any
\rho\in\Sigma_{\ext}(1)
,
\lambda_{\rho}\leqslant\tau_{\rho}
and we write
\lambda<\tau
if moreover
\lambda\neq\tau
. We denote by
1_{\rho}
the element of
\Lambda
with zero for each coordinate except for the one corresponding to
\rho\in\Sigma_{\ext}(1)
where there is 1. We have
1_{\rho}\in X
.

Proposition 6.32
.

The local Möbius function
\mu_{\loc}
has the property that
\mu_{\loc}(0)=1
and that if
\lambda\neq 0
, we have

\mu_{\loc}(\lambda)\neq 0\implies|\lambda|\geqslant 2.

Proof.

First, let us recall that

\mathbf{1}_{\Lambda}.\mu_{\loc}=\mathbf{1}_{X}

and for
\lambda\in\Lambda\cap M
,
\mathbf{1}_{\Lambda}(-\lambda)\neq 0
is equivalent to
\lambda=0
. So if we evaluate the previous equality in
y=0
, because
0\in X
, we get
\mu_{\loc}(0)=1
.

Now for
\tau\in\Lambda\cap M
, we have:

\mu_{\loc}(\tau)=\mathbf{1}_{X}(\tau)-\sum\limits_{\lambda<\tau}\mu_{\loc}(\lambda).

Using that
1_{\rho}\in X
, the previous equality and a finite recurrence, we get that if
|\tau|<2
then
\tau\in X
and because
\mu_{\loc}(0)=1
then
\mu_{\loc}(\tau)=0
.
∎

Proposition 6.33
.

Let
\lambda\in\Lambda\cap M
. If
|\lambda|\geqslant 3.\sharp\Sigma_{\ext}(1)
, we have
\mu_{\loc}(\lambda)=0
.

Proof.

For
\lambda\in\Lambda\cap M
such that there exists
\rho\in\Sigma_{\ext}(1)
,
\lambda_{\rho}\geqslant 2
, we have using the definition of
X
, that

\mathbf{1}_{X}(\lambda+1_{\rho})=\mathbf{1}_{X}(\lambda).

Hence we can deduce that for such
\lambda
,

\mu_{\loc}(\lambda+1_{\rho})=\sum\limits_{\lambda<\tau\leqslant\lambda+1_{\rho}}\mu_{\loc}(\tau)=0.

Using this formula, we get by an induction on
n\in\mathbf{N},n\geqslant 3

\rho\in\Sigma_{\ext}(1)
with
\lambda_{\rho}\geqslant 3

, as there exists
\rho\in\Sigma_{\ext}(1)
with
\lambda_{\rho}\geqslant 3
,

\mu_{\loc}(\lambda)=0.

∎

Proposition 6.34
.

There exists a polynomial
Q
with non negative integers coefficients such that for

\sum\limits_{\lambda\in\Lambda\cap M}\frac{|\mu_{\loc}(\lambda)|}{p_{v}^{s.|\lambda|}}=1+p_{v}^{-2.s}Q(\frac{1}{p_{v}^{s}}).

Note that

does not depend on

.

Moreover, for
v\in M_{\mathbf{Q}}^{0}

\sum\limits_{\lambda\in\Lambda\cap M}\frac{\mu_{\loc}(\lambda)}{p_{v}^{|\lambda|}}=\omega_{\mathcal{T},v}(\mathcal{T}^{\ext}(\mathbf{Z}_{v}))

where
\omega_{\mathcal{T},v}
is the restriction of the normalized Haar measure
dx_{v}
over
\mathbf{Q}_{v}^{\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}
to the open subset
\mathcal{T}^{\ext}(\mathbf{Q}_{v})
.

Notations
.

For
\lambda\in\Lambda\cap M
, let us write
p^{\lambda}.\mathbf{Z}_{p}=\bigoplus\limits_{\rho\in\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}p^{\lambda_{\rho}}.\mathbf{Z}_{p}.
We shall write by
\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{T}^{\ext}(\mathbf{Z}_{p})}
the indicator function of
\mathcal{T}^{\ext}(\mathbf{Z}_{p})\cap T^{\ext}(\mathbf{Q}_{v})
while
\mathbf{1}_{p^{\lambda}.\mathbf{Z}_{p}}
is the indicator function of
p^{\lambda}.\mathbf{Z}_{p}\cap T^{\ext}(\mathbf{Q}_{v})
.

Proof.

First using proposition
6.32
and
6.33
, we can write:

\sum\limits_{\lambda\in\Lambda\cap M}\frac{|\mu_{\loc}(\lambda)|}{p_{v}^{s.|\lambda|}}=1+p_{v}^{-2.s}Q(\frac{1}{p_{v}^{s}})

with

where

. Then we have:

\displaystyle\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{T}^{\ext}(\mathbf{Z}_{p})}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{1}_{X}(\log_{v}(-))

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\lambda\in\Lambda\cap M}\mu_{\loc}(\lambda)\mathbf{1}_{\lambda+\Lambda}(\log_{v}(-))

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\lambda\in\Lambda\cap M}\mu_{\loc}(\lambda)\mathbf{1}_{p^{\lambda}.\mathbf{Z}_{p}}(-).

So if we integrate with respect to the measure
\omega_{\mathcal{T},v}
, because

\omega_{\mathcal{T},v}(p^{\lambda}.\mathbf{Z}_{p})=\frac{1}{p^{|\lambda|}}

we get the expected formula:

\sum\limits_{\lambda\in\Lambda\cap M}\frac{\mu_{\loc}(\lambda)}{p^{|\lambda|}}=\omega_{\mathcal{T},v}(\mathcal{T}^{\ext}(\mathbf{Z}_{p})).

6.5.2.
The global Möbius function

Definition 6.35
.

We define the global Möbius function as a product over all the finite places:

\mu=\prod\limits_{v\in M_{\mathbf{Q}}^{0}}\mu_{\loc}(\log_{v}(-)).

We may remark that
\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{T}^{\ext}(\mathbf{Z})}=\prod\limits_{v\in M_{\mathbf{Q}}^{0}}\mathbf{1}_{X}(\log_{v}(-))

\mathbf{1}_{d}=\prod\limits_{v\in M_{\mathbf{Q}}^{0}}\mathbf{1}_{C+\lambda_{v}}(\log_{v}(-))
where
\lambda_{v}=\log_{v}(d)
for any

. Hence we get the following theorem:

Theorem 6.36
.

We have:

\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{T}^{\ext}(\mathbf{Z})}=\sum\limits_{d}\mu(d)\mathbf{1}_{d}.

Definition 6.37
.

Two elements
a,b\in(\mathbf{N})^{\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}
are said coprime if for any
v\in M_{\mathbf{Q}}^{0}
, we either have
\log_{v}(a)=0
or
\log_{v}(b)=0
.
An arithmetic function

is said to be multiplicative is for any
a,b\in(\mathbf{N})^{\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}
coprime we have
f(a.b)=f(a).f(b)
.

Remark 6.38
.

It is clear that the Mobius function
\mu
we have defined is multiplicative.

First, by proposition

.

For any
B>1
, we have:

N_{d}(B)=O\left(\frac{B^{\langle\omega_{X,\orb}^{-1},u\rangle}}{\Pi(d)}\right).

Proof.

Again by theorem
6.30
, there exists
N>1
such that
\mathcal{D}_{1}\subset\prod\limits_{\rho\in\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}\left([-N,N]-\{0\}\right)
. Hence
\Phi(\mathcal{D}_{1})
is contained in

\prod\limits_{\rho\in\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}\left(\left[-N.\frac{B^{\langle[D_{\rho}],u\rangle}}{d_{\rho}},N.\frac{B^{\langle[D_{\rho}],u\rangle}}{d_{\rho}}\right]-\{0\}\right).

So we have that:

\displaystyle N_{d}(B)

\displaystyle=\sharp(\mathbf{Z}^{\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}\cap\Phi(\mathcal{D}_{1}))

\displaystyle\leqslant 2^{\sharp\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}.\prod\limits_{\rho\in\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}\lfloor N.\frac{B^{\langle[D_{\rho}],u\rangle}}{d_{\rho}}\rfloor

\displaystyle\leqslant(2.N)^{\sharp\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}.\frac{B^{\langle\omega_{X,\orb}^{-1},u\rangle}}{\Pi(d)}.

∎

Remark 7.10
.

One may remark using the previous proof that
N_{d}(B)=0
when there exists
\rho\in\Sigma_{\ext}(1)
such that
N.B^{\langle[D_{\rho}],u\rangle}<d_{\rho}
.

Now we shall later need a better estimation of
N_{d}(B)
when
\Pi(d)\leqslant B^{\min\limits_{\rho\in\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}\langle[D_{\rho}],u\rangle}
. It is given by the following theorem:

Theorem 7.11
.

There exists a constant
C>0
(depending on the choice of
\mathrm{D})
such that:

\left|N_{d}(B)-\frac{1}{\Pi(d)}.2^{\rho_{\orb}(X)}.\mu_{X,\infty}(X(\mathbf{R})).\nu(\mathrm{D}_{1})B^{\langle\omega_{X,\orb}^{-1},u\rangle}\right|\leqslant C.\frac{B^{\langle\omega_{X,\orb}^{-1},u\rangle}}{B^{\min\limits_{\rho\in\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}\langle[D_{\rho}],u\rangle}}.

Proof.

Denote by
\Phi_{I}:\mathbf{R}^{I}\rightarrow\mathbf{R}^{I}
the morphism which maps

(x_{\rho})_{\rho\in I}\mapsto(\frac{B^{\langle[D_{\rho}],u\rangle}}{d_{\rho}}.x_{\rho})_{\rho\in I}.

Then we may remark that:

\proj_{I}\circ\Phi=\Phi_{I}\circ\proj_{I}.

Hence we get, using this, that there exists
C>0
(which depends only on the compact subset
\mathrm{D}
) such that for any non empty set
I\subsetneq\Sigma_{\ext}(1)
:

\Vol(\proj_{I}(\Phi(\mathcal{D}))\leqslant C.\frac{B^{\langle\sum\limits_{\rho\in I}[D_{\rho}],u\rangle}}{\prod\limits_{\rho\in I}d_{\rho}}\leqslant C.\frac{B^{\langle\omega_{X,\orb}^{-1},u\rangle}}{B^{\min\limits_{\rho\in\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}\langle[D_{\rho}],u\rangle}}.

Applying proposition
7.1
finishes the proof of the lemma.

∎

7.3.
The Möbius inversion

We recall that we take
\mathrm{D}
a finite union of compact polyhedrons of
\Pic_{\orb}(X)^{\vee}_{\mathbf{R}}
,
u\in(\text{C}_{\text{eff},\text{orb}}(X)^{\vee})^{\circ}
and
B>1
.

Using theorem
6.36
, we have the following proposition:

Proposition 7.12
.

\sharp(\mathcal{T}^{\ext}(\mathbf{Z})\cap T^{\ext}(\mathbf{R}))_{h^{\ext}_{\mathcal{T},\infty}\in\mathrm{D}_{B}}=\sum\limits_{d\in(\mathbf{N}^{*})^{\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}}\mu(d).N_{d}(B)

Proof.

First, by remark
7.10
, the sum is finite. Now we have:

\displaystyle\sharp(\mathcal{T}^{\ext}(\mathbf{Z})\cap T^{\ext}(\mathbf{R}))_{h^{\ext}_{\mathcal{T},\infty}\in\mathrm{D}_{B}}=2^{\sharp\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}.\sum\limits_{y\in(\mathbf{N}^{*})^{\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}}\mathbf{1}_{(h^{\ext}_{\mathcal{T},\infty})^{-1}(\mathrm{D}_{B})}(y).\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{T}^{\ext}(\mathbf{Z})}(y)

\displaystyle=2^{\sharp\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}.\sum\limits_{y\in(\mathbf{N}^{*})^{\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}}\mathbf{1}_{(h^{\ext}_{\mathcal{T},\infty})^{-1}(\mathrm{D}_{B})}(y).\sum\limits_{d\in(\mathbf{N}^{*})^{\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}}\mu(d).\mathbf{1}_{d.\mathbf{Z}^{\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}}(y)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{d\in(\mathbf{N}^{*})^{\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}}\mu(d).\left(2^{\sharp\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}.\sum\limits_{y\in(\mathbf{N}^{*})^{\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}}\mathbf{1}_{(h^{\ext}_{\mathcal{T},\infty})^{-1}(\mathrm{D}_{B})}(y).\mathbf{1}_{d.\mathbf{Z}^{\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}}(y)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{d\in(\mathbf{N}^{*})^{\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}}\mu(d).N_{d}(B).

∎

We shall show the following theorem:

Theorem 7.13
.

Let
\varepsilon\in]0,\frac{1}{2}[
. There exists a constant
C>0
such that:

\displaystyle\left|\sharp(\mathcal{T}^{\ext}(\mathbf{Z})\cap T^{\ext}(\mathbf{R}))_{h^{\ext}_{\mathcal{T},\infty}\in\mathrm{D}_{B}}-2^{\rho_{\orb}(X)}.\prod\limits_{p}\omega_{\mathcal{T},p}(\mathcal{T}^{\ext}(\mathbf{Z}_{p})).\mu_{X,\infty}(X(\mathbf{R})).\nu(\mathrm{D}_{1})B^{\langle\omega_{X,\orb}^{-1},u\rangle}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant C.\frac{B^{\langle\omega_{X,\orb}^{-1},u\rangle}}{B^{(\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon)\min\limits_{\rho\in\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}\langle[D_{\rho}],u\rangle}}.

Proof.

Recall that by proposition
6.39
and theorem
6.40
, we have that:

\sum\limits_{d}\frac{\mu(d)}{\Pi(d)}=\prod\limits_{p}\omega_{\mathcal{T},p}(\mathcal{T}^{\ext}(\mathbf{Z}_{p})).

Hence using the previous proposition, we get:

\displaystyle\left|\sharp(\mathcal{T}^{\ext}(\mathbf{Z})\cap T^{\ext}(\mathbf{R}))_{h^{\ext}_{\mathcal{T},\infty}\in\mathrm{D}_{B}}-2^{\rho_{\orb}(X)}.\prod\limits_{p}\omega_{\mathcal{T},p}(\mathcal{T}^{\ext}(\mathbf{Z}_{p})).\mu_{X,\infty}(X(\mathbf{R})).\nu(\mathrm{D}_{1})B^{\langle\omega_{X,\orb}^{-1},u\rangle}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum\limits_{d}\mu(d)\left(N_{d}(B)-2^{\rho_{\orb}(X)}.\frac{1}{\Pi(d)}.\mu_{X,\infty}(X(\mathbf{R})).\nu(\mathrm{D}_{1})B^{\langle\omega_{X,\orb}^{-1},u\rangle}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\Pi(d)\leqslant B^{\min\limits_{\rho\in\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}\langle[D_{\rho}],u\rangle}}|\mu(d)|.\left|N_{d}(B)-2^{\rho_{\orb}(X)}.\frac{1}{\Pi(d)}.\mu_{X,\infty}(X(\mathbf{R})).\nu(\mathrm{D}_{1})B^{\langle\omega_{X,\orb}^{-1},u\rangle}\right|

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\Pi(d)\geqslant B^{\min\limits_{\rho\in\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}\langle[D_{\rho}],u\rangle}}|\mu(d)|.\left|N_{d}(B)-2^{\rho_{\orb}(X)}.\frac{1}{\Pi(d)}.\mu_{X,\infty}(X(\mathbf{R})).\nu(\mathrm{D}_{1})B^{\langle\omega_{X,\orb}^{-1},u\rangle}\right|.

When
\Pi(d)\leqslant B^{\min\limits_{\rho\in\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}\langle[D_{\rho}],u\rangle}
we use corollary
7.11
, we have that there exists a constant
C>0
such that:

\left|N_{d}(B)-\frac{1}{\Pi(d)}.2^{\rho_{\orb}(X)}.\mu_{X,\infty}(X(\mathbf{R})).\nu(\mathrm{D}_{1})B^{\langle\omega_{X,\orb}^{-1},u\rangle}\right|\leqslant C.\frac{B^{\langle\omega_{X,\orb}^{-1},u\rangle}}{B^{\min\limits_{\rho\in\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}\langle[D_{\rho}],u\rangle}}.

Let
\varepsilon\in]0,\frac{1}{2}[
. Then by corollary
6.41
, we get that:

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\Pi(d)\leqslant B^{\min\limits_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}\langle[D_{\rho}],u\rangle}}|\mu(d)|.\left|N_{d}(B)-2^{\rho_{\orb}(X)}.\frac{1}{\Pi(d)}.\mu_{X,\infty}(X(\mathbf{R})).\nu(\mathrm{D}_{1})B^{\langle\omega_{X,\orb}^{-1},u\rangle}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant C.\frac{B^{\langle\omega_{X,\orb}^{-1},u\rangle}}{B^{\min\limits_{\rho\in\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}\langle[D_{\rho}],u\rangle}}.B^{(\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon).\min\limits_{\rho\in\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}\langle[D_{\rho}],u\rangle}.

Now by proposition
7.9
, there exists
C^{\prime}>0
such that:

N_{d}(B)\leqslant C^{\prime}.\frac{B^{\langle\omega_{X,\orb}^{-1},u\rangle}}{\Pi(d)}.

Hence we get using the second assertion of lemma
6.41
that there exists
C^{\prime\prime}>0
such that:

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\Pi(d)\geqslant B^{\min\limits_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}\langle[D_{\rho}],u\rangle}}|\mu(d)|.\left|N_{d}(B)-2^{\rho_{\orb}(X)}.\frac{1}{\Pi(d)}.\mu_{X,\infty}(X(\mathbf{R})).\nu(\mathrm{D}_{1})B^{\langle\omega_{X,\orb}^{-1},u\rangle}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant C^{\prime\prime}.B^{\langle\omega_{X,\orb}^{-1},u\rangle}.\sum\limits_{\Pi(d)\geqslant B^{\min\limits_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}\langle[D_{\rho}],u\rangle}}\frac{|\mu(d)|}{\Pi(d)}

\displaystyle\leqslant C^{\prime\prime}.\frac{B^{\langle\omega_{X,\orb}^{-1},u\rangle}}{B^{(1-\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon)\min\limits_{\rho\in\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}\langle[D_{\rho}],u\rangle}}.

This concludes our proof.
∎

Applying theorem
7.6
, we get:

Corollary 7.14
.

Let
\varepsilon\in]0,\frac{1}{2}[
. There exists a constant
C>0
such that:

\displaystyle\left|(\mathcal{T}^{\ext}(\mathbf{Z}))_{h^{\ext}_{\mathcal{T},\infty}\in\mathrm{D}_{B}}-2^{\rho_{\orb}(X)}.\prod\limits_{p}\omega_{\mathcal{T},p}(\mathcal{T}^{\ext}(\mathbf{Z}_{p})).\mu_{X,\infty}(X(\mathbf{R})).\nu(\mathrm{D}_{1})B^{\langle\omega_{X,\orb}^{-1},u\rangle}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant C.\frac{B^{\langle\omega_{X,\orb}^{-1},u\rangle}}{B^{(\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon)\min\limits_{\rho\in\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}\langle[D_{\rho}],u\rangle}}

7.4.
Conclusion

Finally we can establish the theorem
3.29
:

Theorem 7.15
.

Let
\epsilon\in]0,\frac{1}{2}[
. Then we have:

\sharp(X(\mathbf{Q}))_{h\in\mathrm{D}_{B}}\underset{B\rightarrow+\infty}{=}\nu(\mathrm{D}_{1}).\tau_{\orb}(X).B^{\langle\omega_{X,\orb}^{-1},u\rangle}\left(1+O\left(B^{-(\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon).\min\limits_{\rho\in\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}\langle[D_{\rho}],u\rangle}\right)\right)

where
\nu
is the measure over
\Pic_{\orb}(X)^{\vee}_{\mathbf{R}}
defined as in
3.28
.

Proof.

Let us recall that
\sharp X(\mathbf{Q})_{h\in\mathrm{D}_{B}}=\frac{\sharp G(\mathbf{Z})}{2^{\rho_{\orb}(X)}}\sharp\left(\mathcal{T}^{\ext}(\mathbf{Z})\right)_{h^{\ext}_{\mathcal{T},\infty}\in\mathrm{D}_{B}}
. Applying corollary
7.14
, we get that there exists a constant
C>0
such that:

\displaystyle\left|(\sharp X(\mathbf{Q})_{h\in\mathrm{D}_{B}}-\frac{1}{\sharp G(\mathbf{Z})}.\left(\prod\limits_{p}\omega_{\mathcal{T},p}(\mathcal{T}^{\ext}(\mathbf{Z}_{p}))\right).\mu_{X,\infty}(X(\mathbf{R})).\nu(\mathrm{D}_{1})B^{\langle\omega_{X,\orb}^{-1},u\rangle}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant C.\frac{B^{\langle\omega_{X,\orb}^{-1},u\rangle}}{B^{(\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon)\min\limits_{\rho\in\Sigma_{\ext}(1)}\langle[D_{\rho}],u\rangle}}.

Moreover by definition
6.48
, we get:

\frac{1}{\sharp G(\mathbf{Z})}.\left(\prod\limits_{p}\omega_{\mathcal{T},p}(\mathcal{T}^{\ext}(\mathbf{Z}_{p}))\right).\mu_{X,\infty}(X(\mathbf{R}))=\tau_{\orb}(X)

hence we have the result stated.
∎

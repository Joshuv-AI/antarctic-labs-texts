On the unit group of some multiquadratic fields
Mohamed Mahmoud CHEMS-EDDIN, Hamza El Mamry

Abstract
. In this paper, we calculate the unit groups and the 2 2 -class numbers of the fields 𝕂 = ℚ ⁡ ( 2 , p 1 , p 2 ) \mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2},\sqrt{p_{1}},\sqrt{p_{2}}) and 𝕃 = ℚ ⁡ ( − 1 , 2 , p 1 , p 2 ) \mathbb{L}=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-1},\sqrt{2},\sqrt{p_{1}},\sqrt{p_{2}}) , where p 1 p_{1} and p 2 p_{2} are two prime numbers satisfying p 1 ≡ p 2 ≡ 1 ( mod 4 ) p_{1}\equiv p_{2}\equiv 1\pmod{4} .

Introduction

Let
k
be a number field of degree
n
(i.e.,
[k:\mathbb{Q}]=n
). Denote by
E_{k}
the unit group of
k
that is the group of the invertible elements of the ring
\mathcal{O}_{k}
of algebraic integers of the number field
k
. By the well known Dirichlet’s unit theorem, if
n=r+2s
, where
r
is the number of real embeddings and
s
the number of conjugate pairs of complex embeddings of
k
, then there exist
t=r+s-1
units of
\mathcal{O}_{k}
that generate
E_{k}
(modulo the roots of unity), and these
t
units are called the
fundamental system of units of
k
. Therefore, it is well known that

E_{k}\simeq\mu(k)\times\mathbb{Z}^{r+s-1},

where
\mu(k)
is the group of roots of unity contained in
k
.

One major problem in algebraic number theory (more precisely, in the theory of units of number fields which is related to almost all areas of algebraic number theory) is the computation of the
fundamental system of units. For quadratic fields, the problem
is easily solved. An early study of unit groups of multiquadratic fields was established by Varmon
[
26
]
. For quartic bicyclic fields, Kubota
[
22
]
gave
a method for finding a fundamental system of units.
Wada
[
27
]
generalized Kubota’s method, creating an algorithm for computing
fundamental units in any given multiquadratic field. However, in general, it is not easy to compute the unit group of a number field especially for number fields of degree more than
4
. Many authors tried to characterize their Hasse unit index. For instance, Hirabayashi and Yoshino
[
17
,
19
]
gave criteria to determine the Hasse unit index of some multiquadratic number fields of degree
4
and
8
.
In
[
16
,
18
]
, Hirabayashi gave a necessary and sufficient condition for a multiquadratic field of degree 16 to have a unit index equal to
2
. For more works in the same direction, we refer the reader to
[
1
,
5
,
8
,
9
]
.

Actually, in literature there are only few examples of the explicit computation of the unit group of a given number field
k
of degree
\geq 8
(cf.
[
4
,
10
,
12
,
14
]
).

Recently, the authors of
[
11
,
13
]
computed the unit group of the fields
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2},\sqrt{p},\sqrt{q})
, where
p\equiv 1
,
3\pmod{4}
and
q\equiv 3\pmod{4}
are two prime numbers.

As a continuation, in present work, we focus on the computation of the unit group of the real triquadratic fields of the form

\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2},\sqrt{p_{1}},\sqrt{p_{2}})
, where
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
are two primes such that
p_{1}\equiv p_{2}\equiv 1\pmod{4}
. We note that this case has particular interest. In fact, according to
[
16
, Proposition 1]
the Hasse unit index of the field
\mathbb{L}=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-1},\sqrt{2},\sqrt{p_{1}},\sqrt{p_{2}})
equals
1
(i.e.
(E_{\mathbb{L}}:\mu(\mathbb{L})E_{\mathbb{K}})=1
), so

(1)

\displaystyle E_{\mathbb{L}}=\langle\zeta_{8}\rangle E_{\mathbb{K}}

So our Theorems
2.5
,
3.2
and
4.1
(cf. pages
2.5
,
3.2
and
4.1
respectively) give also the unit group of
\mathbb{L}
.
Notice that the motivation behind the computation of the unit groups of these fields is the fact that
\mathbb{K}
(resp.
\mathbb{L}
) is the first layer of the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-extension of the biquadratic field
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p_{1}},\sqrt{p_{2}})
(resp.
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p_{1}},\sqrt{p_{2}},\sqrt{-1})
),
see
[
10
,
12
]
for examples of applications in this direction. Furthermore, computing the unit group of the fields
\mathbb{K}
is also the first step to find the unit group of all fields of the form
\mathbb{K}(\sqrt{-\ell})
, where
\ell\textgreater 1
is a positive square-free integer (cf.
[
1
]
). We note that the unit group of these fields are useful for the study of the Hilbert
2
-class field tower of the subfields
\mathbb{K}(\sqrt{-\ell})

(e.g.,
[
4
,
14
]
).

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
p_{1}\equiv 1\pmod{8}
and
p_{2}\equiv 5\pmod{8}

\genfrac{(}{)}{}{0}{p_1}{p_2}=-1

Then

a)

, that is
\sqrt{\varepsilon_{2}\varepsilon_{p_{1}p_{2}}\varepsilon_{2p_{1}p_{2}}}\in k_{3}
and

\sqrt{\varepsilon_{p_{1}}\varepsilon_{2p_{2}}\varepsilon_{2p_{1}p_{2}}}\in k_{6}

and so
q(\mathbb{K})=2^{5}
or
q(\mathbb{K})=2^{4}
. More precisely,

q(\mathbb{K})=2^{5}\text{ if and only if }\sqrt{\varepsilon_{p_{2}}\varepsilon_{2p_{1}}\varepsilon_{2p_{1}p_{2}}}\in k_{5}.

Proof.

To proof this result, we need some properties of the Hilbert
2
-class field tower of a number field
k
such that
\mathbf{C}l(k)\simeq(2,2)
. For this reason, we refer the reader to
[
3
,
21
]
.
Notice that
\mathbb{K}
is a quartic unramified extension of

.
Under the conditions of the first item, we have
\mathbf{C}l(k)\simeq(2,2)
(cf.
[
20
]
). Therefore,
k^{(1)}=\mathbb{K}
.
By Lemma
1.6
, we have
h_{2}(2p_{2})=2
,
h_{2}(p_{1}p_{2})=2
and
h_{2}(2p_{1})
is divisible by
4
. Thus,

\displaystyle h_{2}(k_{3})=2q(k_{3}),\;\quad h_{2}(k_{6})=2q(k_{6})\;\text{ and }\;h_{2}(k_{5})=q(k_{5})h_{2}(2p_{1}).

Notice that
k_{3}
,
k_{5}
and

are the three unramified quadratic extensions of

within

.

, Figure 2]

and

2
-rank
(\mathbf{C}l(k))=3-1-e

). If

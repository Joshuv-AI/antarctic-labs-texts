Axial anomaly in the presence of arbitrary spinor interactions
Jan Dereziński, Adam Latosiński

Abstract
We consider N N Dirac fermions on a 4-dimensional Euclidean space with a quadratic interaction given by arbitrary external Clifford-valued fields. The divergence of the axial current satisfies on the classical level a relation that is violated after quantization. Using the Pauli-Villars method to regularize the fields, we find the conditions that guarantee the finiteness of the anomaly. We also find this anomaly. Our result generalizes the well-known computation of axial anomaly of Dirac fermions interacting with an external Yang-Mills field.



1
Introduction

We shall consider a set of classical anti-commuting Dirac spinor fields
\psi_{I},\bar{\psi}_{I}
,

I=1,\dots,N
, in a
4-dimensional Euclidean space (with positive signature). We also
introduce
a quadratic Hermitian action for these fields of the form

{\mathcal{S}}[\psi]=\int{\rm d}^{4}x\,\sum_{IJ}\bar{\psi}_{I}(x)\big((\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}+m)\delta^{IJ}+\Phi^{IJ}(x)\big)\psi_{J}(x).

(1)

Thus all fields have the same mass
m
and are coupled
to external Clifford-algebra-valued fields
\Phi^{IJ}(x)
.

Conserved or approximately conserved currents play an important role
in QFT. For instance, the vector current

{\mathcal{J}}^{\mu}(x):=\sum_{I}\bar{\psi}_{I}(x)\gamma^{\mu}\psi_{I}(x)

(2)

satisfies formally

\partial_{\mu}{\mathcal{J}}^{\mu}(x)=0.

(3)

The relation (
3
) is true also inside correlation functions:

\langle\partial_{\mu}{\mathcal{J}}^{\mu}(x)\rangle=0.

(4)

To be precise, (
3
) is not fully rigorous, since it involves
putting two fields at coinciding points. The relation (
4
)
is exact, since we only consider such regularizations that keep this condition true.

Let us also introduce the following currents:

{\mathcal{J}}^{5\mu}(x)=\sum_{I}\bar{\psi}_{I}(x)\gamma^{5}\gamma^{\mu}\psi_{I}(x),\qquad{\mathcal{J}}^{5}(x)=\sum_{I}\bar{\psi}_{I}(x)\gamma^{5}\psi_{I}(x).

(5)

If the Dirac field is coupled only to an external
Yang-Mills field,
that is,
the field
\Phi
has just one component
\Phi(x)={\rm i}A_{\mu}(x)\gamma^{\mu}(x)
, and fields
\psi
satisfy the classical equations of motion, then formally on the classical level
(
5
) satisfy the equality

\partial_{\mu}{\mathcal{J}}^{5\mu}(x)+2m{\mathcal{J}}^{5}(x)=0.

(6)

However on the quantum level, after appropriate renormalization, we get

\langle\partial_{\mu}{\mathcal{J}}^{5\mu}(x)\rangle_{\mathrm{ren}}+2m\langle{\mathcal{J}}^{5}(x)\rangle_{\mathrm{ren}}=\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}}\text{Tr}\big(\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}A_{\mu\nu}(x)A_{\rho\sigma}(x)\big)=:{\mathcal{A}}(x).

(7)

where
A_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}+{\rm i}[A_{\mu},A_{\nu}]
and Tr denotes the trace over the space enumerating different fields
\psi_{I}
.
{\mathcal{A}}(x)
is called the axial anomaly. This is described in essentially
every modern textbook on Quantum Field Theory such as
[
1
,
2
]
,
see also
[
3
,
4
]
for a more specialized treatment.

For more general external fields the classical relation analogous to (
6
)
is

\partial_{\mu}{\mathcal{J}}^{5\mu}(x)+2m{\mathcal{J}}^{5}(x)+\sum_{IJ}\bar{\psi}_{I}(x)\big(\gamma^{5}\Phi^{IJ}(x)+\Phi^{IJ}(x)\gamma^{5}\big)\psi_{J}(x)=0.

(8)

which holds if the fields
\psi
satisfy the classical equations of motion.
Since this is a generalization of the simpler case, we still expect an anomaly to be present on the quantum level:

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{\mu}{\mathcal{J}}^{5\mu}(x)\rangle_{\mathrm{ren}}+2m\langle{\mathcal{J}}^{5}(x)\rangle_{\mathrm{ren}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{IJ}\langle\bar{\psi}_{I}(x)\big(\gamma^{5}\Phi^{IJ}(x)+\Phi^{IJ}(x)\gamma^{5}\big)\psi_{J}(x)\rangle_{\mathrm{ren}}

\displaystyle=:{\mathcal{A}}(x).

(9)

As in the case of the vector current,
{\mathcal{J}}^{5\mu}(x)
and the equations
(
9
) are problematic, because they involve two fields
at coinciding points. In order to give sense to them we
shall use the Pauli-Villars regularization. We will show that with this
regularization the renormalized vector current
{\mathcal{J}}^{\mu}(x)
is
conserved. We will find conditions on the external fields

\Phi^{IJ}(x)
which guarantee that the axial anomaly is finite. These
conditions say that certain local polynomials in external fields and
their derivatives vanish. There are three such
conditions: one involves a polynomial of degree 1, another of degree
2 and the third of degree 3. We
will compute the anomaly—again, given by a local polynomial in
external fields. This is a rather complicated
polynomial of degree four. One should note that there are no
anomalies of degree five and more.

Lagrangians of the form (
1
) may appear in phenomenological
description of various physical systems. Our results show that the usual
approximate conservation of the axial current can often be
generalized to a more general setting.

2
Euclidean Dirac field

Consider the Clifford algebra generated by matrices
\gamma_{\mu}
such that

\{\gamma_{\mu},\gamma_{\nu}\}=\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+\gamma_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu}=2g_{\mu\nu},\qquad\gamma_{\mu}^{\dagger}=\gamma_{\mu},\quad\mu,\nu=1,\dots,4.

(10)

where
g_{\mu\nu}
is the Euclidean metric tensor
g={\rm diag}(1,1,1,1)
. We’ll use the conventions
\gamma_{5}=\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}\gamma_{3}\gamma_{4}
,
\gamma_{\mu\nu}:=\frac{1}{2}(\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}-\gamma_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu})
, and
\bar{\psi}_{I}=(\psi_{I})^{\dagger}\gamma_{5}
. Thus (
10
) can be extended to
\mu,\nu=5
, and

\gamma_{\mu\nu}=-\gamma_{\mu\nu}^{\dagger}=-\gamma_{\nu\mu}
for
\mu,\nu=1,\dots,5
. Still, whenever contraction of such indices appears in the text, the summation it denotes is only over the range
\{1\dots 4\}
.

The action (
1
) can be written as

{\mathcal{S}}[\psi]=\int{\rm d}^{4}x\,\sum_{IJ}\bar{\psi}_{I}(x)D(m,\Phi)^{IJ}\psi_{J}(x),

(11)

where

D(m,\Phi)^{IJ}=(\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}+m)\delta^{IJ}+\Phi^{IJ}=D_{0}(m)\delta^{IJ}+\Phi^{IJ}.

(12)

With the chosen conventions the free part of the action is Hermitian. For the whole action to be Hermitian, we need

(\Phi^{IJ})^{\dagger}=\gamma^{5}\Phi^{JI}\gamma^{5}.

(13)

Note that with this assumption
\gamma^{5}D(m,\Phi)
is a Hermitian operator.

It can be noted that a rotation of fields
\psi^{I}(x)\rightarrow U^{IJ}(x)\psi^{J}(x)
, with
U
being unitary matrices, is
equivalent to an apprioprate transformation of the external fields
\Phi
:

{\mathcal{S}}[U\psi]=\int{\rm d}^{4}x\,\sum_{IJ}\bar{\psi}_{I}(x)\big(U^{-1}D(m,\Phi)U\big)^{IJ}\psi_{J}(x),

(14)

U^{-1}D(m,\Phi)U=D\big(m,U^{-1}\Phi U+\gamma^{\mu}U^{-1}(\partial_{\mu}U)\big)

(15)

Given a basis of the Clifford algebra, the external fields
\Phi^{IJ}
can be decomposed in this basis. We will use a specific basis

\Gamma^{a}\in({\bf{1}},{\rm i}\gamma^{\mu},{\rm i}\gamma^{\mu\nu},\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{5},\gamma^{5})

(16)

and introduce varying symbols for different components of
\Phi^{IJ}
:

\Phi^{IJ}=\Phi^{IJ}_{a}\Gamma^{a}=\kappa^{IJ}{\bf{1}}+{\rm i}A^{IJ}_{\mu}\gamma^{\mu}+{\rm i}B^{IJ}_{\mu\nu}\gamma^{\mu\nu}+C^{IJ}_{\mu}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{5}+\lambda^{IJ}\gamma^{5}

(17)

In some formulas we will also use
\tilde{B}_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}B^{\rho\sigma}
.
With this choice,
(\Gamma^{a})^{\dagger}=\gamma^{5}\Gamma^{a}\gamma^{5}
, which further implies that the interaction
part of the action
{\mathcal{S}}
is Hermitian iff every component field

\Phi^{IJ}_{a}(x)
is a Hermitian
N\times N
matrix.

Treating
\psi_{I}(x)
and
\bar{\psi}_{J}(y)
as (independent) Grassmann variables, and
using the Berezin integral
\int{\mathcal{D}}[\psi]
,
we can compute correlation functions of the
fields from the formula

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{I_{1}}(x_{1})\cdots\psi_{I_{n}}(x_{n})\bar{\psi}_{J_{m}}(y_{m})\cdots\bar{\psi}_{J_{1}}(y_{1})\rangle

(18)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Z}\int\mathrm{e}^{-{\mathcal{S}}[\psi]}\psi_{I_{1}}(x_{1})\cdots\psi_{I_{n}}(x_{n})\bar{\psi}_{J_{m}}(y_{m})\cdots\bar{\psi}_{J_{1}}(y_{1}){\mathcal{D}}[\psi]

where

Z=\int\mathrm{e}^{-{\mathcal{S}}[\psi]}{\mathcal{D}}[\psi]

(19)

is the normalization factor. Since these integrals tend to be infinite, some regularization is usually necessary to obtain physically meaningful results. Since the integrals are Gaussian (in the Grassmannian sense), all
correlation functions can be reduced to the correlation function for a pair of fields

\langle\psi_{I}(x)\bar{\psi}_{J}(y)\rangle=-(D^{-1})_{IJ}(x,y;m,\Phi),

(20)

where
D^{-1}(x,y;m,\Phi)
is the integral kernel of the inverse of
the operator
D(m,\Phi)
. Note the identity

\langle\bar{\psi}_{I}(x)\Gamma^{a}\psi_{J}(y)\rangle=\text{tr}\big(\Gamma^{a}(D^{-1})_{JI}(y,x;m,\Phi)\big),

(21)

where the symbol
tr
denotes the trace over the spinor indices only,
and the minus sign from (
20
) has disappeared, because of the
anticommution relations of Grassmann variables.

3
Axial anomaly

In the introduction we introduced the vector current
{\mathcal{J}}^{\mu}(x)
,
and also
the axial currents
{\mathcal{J}}^{5}(x)
and
{\mathcal{J}}^{5\mu}(x)
. Furthermore, we defined
the anomaly
{\mathcal{A}}(x)
.

We have (naively)

\langle\partial_{\mu}{\mathcal{J}}^{5\mu}(x)\rangle=\sum_{I}\text{tr}\big(\gamma^{5}\gamma^{\mu}\frac{\partial(D^{-1})_{II}(x,x;m,\Phi)}{\partial x^{\mu}}\big),

(22)

\langle{\mathcal{J}}^{5}(x)\rangle=\sum_{I}\text{tr}\big(\gamma^{5}(D^{-1})_{II}(x,x;m,\Phi)\big),

(23)

\langle\bar{\psi}_{I}(x)\Gamma^{a}\psi_{J}(x)\rangle=\text{tr}\big(\Gamma^{a}(D^{-1})_{JI}(x,x;m,\Phi)\big).

(24)

These expressions however have a problem, because the integral kernel

D^{-1}(x,y;m)
is divergent for
y=x
.

To resolve this problem, we shall use Pauli-Villars regularization. Namely, we define regularized propagators (free and full) as

(D_{0}^{-1})_{\Lambda}(x,y)=\sum_{i}{\mathcal{C}}_{i}D_{0}^{-1}(x,y;M_{i}(\Lambda)),

(25)

(D^{-1})_{\Lambda}(x,y;\Phi)=\sum_{i}{\mathcal{C}}_{i}D^{-1}(x,y;M_{i}(\Lambda),\Phi),

(26)

where
\Lambda
is a regularization parameter (eventually

\Lambda\rightarrow+\infty
), masses
M_{i}(\Lambda)
and coefficients

{\mathcal{C}}_{i}
are chosen such that

{\mathcal{C}}_{0}=1,\quad M_{0}(\Lambda)=m,\quad\lim_{\Lambda\rightarrow+\infty}M_{i}(\Lambda)=+\infty\text{ for }i\neq 0,

and
(D_{0}^{-1})_{\Lambda}(x,y)
has no divergence for
y\rightarrow x
. These conditions mean that the integral

(D_{0}^{-1})_{\Lambda}(x,x)=\int\frac{{\rm d}^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}\sum_{i}{\mathcal{C}}_{i}\frac{-{\rm i}p\hskip-4.30554pt/\penalty+M_{i}}{p^{2}+M_{i}^{2}}

(27)

has to be finite. By (
88
) and (
89
),
this is guaranteed by the conditions

\sum_{i}{\mathcal{C}}_{i}=0,\qquad\sum_{i}{\mathcal{C}}_{i}M_{i}=0,\qquad\sum_{i}{\mathcal{C}}_{i}M_{i}^{2}=0,\qquad\sum_{i}{\mathcal{C}}_{i}M_{i}^{3}=0.

(28)

A possible set solutions for these equations are

i\in\{0,\dots n\},n\geq 4,\qquad{\mathcal{C}}_{i}=(-1)^{i}\binom{n}{i},\qquad M_{i}=m+i\Lambda.

(29)

A proof that this is indeed a solution is given in Appendix
B
. Other solutions can be created by linear combinations of these solutions.

Using this regularized propagator we define

\langle\partial_{\mu}J^{\mu}(x)\rangle_{\Lambda}=\sum_{I}\text{tr}\big(\gamma^{\mu}\frac{\partial(D^{-1})_{II,\Lambda}(x,x;\Phi)}{\partial x^{\mu}}\big),

(30)

\langle\partial_{\mu}J^{5\mu}(x)\rangle_{\Lambda}=\sum_{I}\text{tr}\big(\gamma^{5}\gamma^{\mu}\frac{\partial(D^{-1})_{II,\Lambda}(x,x;\Phi)}{\partial x^{\mu}}\big),

(31)

\langle J^{5}(x)\rangle_{\Lambda}=\sum_{I}\text{tr}\big(\gamma^{5}(D^{-1})_{II,\Lambda}(x,x;\Phi)\big),

(32)

\langle\bar{\psi}^{I}(x)\Gamma^{a}\psi^{J}(x)\rangle_{\Lambda}=\text{tr}\big(\Gamma^{a}(D^{-1})_{JI,\Lambda}(x,x;\Phi)\big),

(33)

and

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}_{\Lambda}(x;\Phi)

\displaystyle=\langle\partial_{\mu}J^{5\mu}(x)\rangle_{\Lambda}+2m\langle J^{5}(x)\rangle_{\Lambda}+

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{IJ}\langle\bar{\psi}_{I}(x)\big(\gamma^{5}\Phi^{IJ}(x)+\Phi^{IJ}(x)\gamma^{5}\big)\psi_{J}(x)\rangle_{\Lambda}.

(34)

We shall see that this regularization keeps the vector current conserved on the quantum level, i.e.

\lim_{\Lambda\rightarrow\infty}\langle\partial_{\mu}J^{\mu}(x)\rangle_{\Lambda}=0.

(35)

This relation is actualy satisfied even without the limit. We shall also see that the axial currents
in general produces infinite axial anomalies linear, quadratic, and
cubic in fields
\Phi
.
If the anomaly has a finite limit for
\Lambda\rightarrow\infty
, it’s okay;
it’s analogous to the standard textbook axial anomaly. However if in
this limit it diverges, we find it problematic.
We consider the disapearance of these
infinite anomalies a necessary condition for the consistency of the
quantization; for that to happen, fields
\Phi
need to satisfy some
specific conditions, which we will derive in this paper.
Under these
conditions, we also
compute the resulting anomaly. Our result can be summarized as follows.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that the following conditions are fullfilled:

{\rm Tr}\,\lambda=0,

(36)

{\rm Tr}(\partial_{\mu}C^{\mu}+2\kappa\lambda+2B^{\mu\nu}\tilde{B}_{\mu\nu})=0,

(37)

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}\Big(-\kappa^{2}\lambda+{\rm i}\kappa[A^{\mu},C_{\mu}]+2\kappa B^{\mu\nu}\tilde{B}_{\mu\nu}-{\rm i}[A^{\mu},A^{\nu}]\tilde{B}_{\mu\nu}+

\displaystyle\qquad+{\rm i}\tilde{B}_{\mu\nu}[C^{\mu},C^{\nu}]+2B^{\mu\nu}B_{\mu\nu}\lambda+2C^{\mu}C_{\mu}\lambda+\lambda^{3}\Big)=0.

(38)

Then there exist the limit

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}(x)

\displaystyle:=\lim_{\Lambda\rightarrow\infty}{\mathcal{A}}_{\Lambda}(x),

(39)

the relation (
9
) is satisfied and the renormalized anomaly is

{\mathcal{A}}={\mathcal{A}}^{(1)}+{\mathcal{A}}^{(2)}+{\mathcal{A}}^{(3)}+{\mathcal{A}}^{(4)},

(40)

where

{\mathcal{A}}^{(1)}=\frac{1}{12\pi^{2}}{\rm Tr}\big(\partial^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}C^{\nu}\big),

(41)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{12\pi^{2}}{\rm Tr}\Big(4\kappa\partial^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\lambda+6\partial_{\mu}\kappa\partial^{\mu}\lambda+

\displaystyle\quad+3\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}\partial_{\alpha}A_{\beta}+2A_{\nu}\partial^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}C^{\nu}-2C^{\nu}\partial^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}+

\displaystyle\quad+4\tilde{B}^{\rho\sigma}\partial^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}B_{\rho\sigma}+8\tilde{B}^{\rho\mu}\partial_{\mu}\partial^{\nu}B_{\rho\nu}-8B^{\rho\mu}\partial_{\mu}\partial^{\nu}\tilde{B}_{\rho\nu}+

\displaystyle\quad+6g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\tilde{B}^{\rho\sigma}\partial_{\nu}B_{\rho\sigma}+4\partial_{\mu}\tilde{B}^{\rho\mu}\partial^{\nu}B_{\rho\nu}-4\partial^{\mu}\tilde{B}^{\rho\nu}\partial_{\nu}B_{\rho\mu}+

\displaystyle\quad-12\,\partial^{\mu}B_{\mu\nu}\partial^{\nu}\lambda+\epsilon^{\mu\alpha\nu\beta}\partial_{\mu}C_{\alpha}\partial_{\nu}C_{\beta}\Big),

(42)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{12\pi^{2}}{\rm Tr}\bigg((\partial_{\mu}\kappa)\Big(10\{\kappa,C^{\mu}\}+4{\rm i}[\lambda,A^{\mu}]+4\{A_{\nu},\tilde{B}^{\mu\nu}\}\Big)+

\displaystyle+(\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu})\Big(2{\rm i}g^{\mu\nu}[\kappa,\lambda]+3{\rm i}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}[A_{\alpha},A_{\beta}]-3{\rm i}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}[C_{\alpha},C_{\beta}]+

\displaystyle\quad+4\{A^{\mu},C^{\nu}\}-4\{A^{\nu},C^{\mu}\}-2g^{\mu\nu}\{A^{\alpha},C_{\alpha}\}+

\displaystyle\quad+12\{\kappa,\tilde{B}^{\mu\nu}\}-12\{\lambda,B^{\mu\nu}\}-12{\rm i}g_{\alpha\beta}(B^{\mu\alpha}\tilde{B}^{\nu\beta}-B^{\nu\beta}\tilde{B}^{\mu\alpha})\Big)+

\displaystyle+(\partial_{\mu}B_{\alpha\beta})\Big(3\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\{\kappa,A_{\nu}\}-4{\rm i}g^{\mu\beta}[\kappa,C^{\alpha}]+4{\rm i}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}[\lambda,C_{\nu}]+

\displaystyle\quad-12{\rm i}g^{\mu\beta}[A_{\nu},\tilde{B}^{\alpha\nu}]-12{\rm i}[A^{\alpha},\tilde{B}^{\mu\beta}]+4{\rm i}[A^{\mu},\tilde{B}^{\alpha\beta}]+

\displaystyle\quad+16g^{\mu\beta}\{C_{\nu},B^{\alpha\nu}\}+8\{C^{\alpha},B^{\mu\beta}\}-4\{C^{\mu},B^{\alpha\beta}\}\Big)+

\displaystyle+(\partial_{\mu}C_{\nu})\Big(10g^{\mu\nu}\kappa^{2}-6g^{\mu\nu}\lambda^{2}+

\displaystyle\quad-4g^{\mu\nu}A^{\alpha}A_{\alpha}-2A^{\mu}A^{\nu}-2A^{\nu}A^{\mu}+

\displaystyle\quad-8g^{\mu\nu}C^{\alpha}C_{\alpha}-2C^{\mu}C^{\nu}-2C^{\nu}C^{\mu}+

\displaystyle\quad+4{\rm i}[\kappa,B^{\mu\nu}]-4{\rm i}[\lambda,\tilde{B}^{\mu\nu}]-4g^{\mu\nu}B^{\rho\sigma}B_{\rho\sigma}\Big)+

\displaystyle+(\partial_{\mu}\lambda)\Big(-6{\rm i}[\kappa,A^{\mu}]-12{\rm i}[\tilde{B}^{\mu\nu},C_{\nu}]\Big)\bigg),

(43)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}^{(4)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{96\pi^{2}}{\rm Tr}\Big(-32\kappa^{3}\lambda+32{\rm i}\kappa^{2}[A^{\mu},C_{\mu}]+16[A^{\alpha},\kappa][A_{\alpha},\lambda]+

\displaystyle\quad+48{\rm i}\lambda^{2}[A^{\mu},C_{\mu}]+48C^{\alpha}C_{\alpha}(\kappa\lambda+\lambda\kappa)+96\kappa C^{\mu}\lambda C_{\mu}+

\displaystyle\quad+64\kappa^{2}B^{\mu\nu}\tilde{B}_{\mu\nu}+32\kappa B^{\mu\nu}\kappa\tilde{B}_{\mu\nu}+

\displaystyle\quad+48B^{\alpha\beta}B_{\alpha\beta}(\kappa\lambda+\lambda\kappa)+64\kappa B^{\mu\nu}\lambda B_{\mu\nu}+

\displaystyle\quad-192\lambda^{2}B^{\mu\nu}\tilde{B}_{\mu\nu}-96\lambda B^{\mu\nu}\lambda\tilde{B}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\quad-48{\rm i}(\tilde{B}_{\mu\nu}\kappa+\kappa\tilde{B}_{\mu\nu})[A^{\mu},A^{\nu}]+48{\rm i}(B_{\mu\nu}\lambda+\lambda B_{\mu\nu})[A^{\mu},A^{\nu}]+

\displaystyle\quad+16{\rm i}(\tilde{B}_{\mu\nu}\kappa+\kappa\tilde{B}_{\mu\nu})[C^{\mu},C^{\nu}]+128{\rm i}\tilde{B}_{\mu\nu}C^{\mu}\kappa C^{\nu}+

\displaystyle\quad-16{\rm i}(B_{\mu\nu}\lambda+\lambda B_{\mu\nu})[C^{\mu},C^{\nu}]-96{\rm i}B_{\mu\nu}C^{\mu}\lambda C^{\nu}+

\displaystyle\quad+32(B_{\mu\nu}\kappa+\kappa B_{\mu\nu})[A^{\mu},C^{\nu}]+32(\tilde{B}_{\mu\nu}\lambda+\lambda\tilde{B}_{\mu\nu})[A^{\mu},C^{\nu}]+

\displaystyle\quad-64B_{\mu\nu}(A^{\mu}\kappa C^{\nu}+C^{\nu}\kappa A^{\mu})-64\tilde{B}_{\mu\nu}(\lambda A^{\mu}C^{\nu}+C^{\nu}A^{\mu}\lambda)+

\displaystyle\quad+64\tilde{B}_{\mu\nu}(A^{\mu}\lambda C^{\nu}+C^{\nu}\lambda A^{\mu})+64\tilde{B}_{\mu\nu}(\lambda C^{\nu}A^{\mu}+A^{\mu}C^{\nu}\lambda)+

\displaystyle\quad-256{\rm i}g_{\mu\nu}\kappa B^{\mu\alpha}\tilde{B}_{\alpha\beta}B^{\beta\nu}-512{\rm i}g_{\mu\nu}\lambda B^{\mu\alpha}B_{\alpha\beta}B^{\beta\nu}+

\displaystyle\quad-6\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}[A_{\mu},A_{\nu}][A_{\alpha},A_{\beta}]+2\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}[C_{\mu},C_{\nu}][C_{\alpha},C_{\beta}]+

\displaystyle\quad-4\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}[A_{\mu},A_{\nu}][C_{\alpha},C_{\beta}]+8\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}[A_{\mu},C_{\nu}][A_{\alpha},C_{\beta}]+

\displaystyle\quad-16{\rm i}[A_{\mu},A_{\nu}][A^{\mu},C^{\nu}]+32{\rm i}A^{\mu}A_{\mu}[A^{\nu},C_{\nu}]+

\displaystyle\quad+32A^{\alpha}A_{\alpha}B^{\mu\nu}\tilde{B}_{\mu\nu}-32A^{\alpha}B^{\mu\nu}A_{\alpha}\tilde{B}_{\mu\nu}-128g_{\alpha\beta}B^{\mu\alpha}\tilde{B}^{\nu\beta}[A_{\mu},A_{\nu}]+

\displaystyle\quad-96C^{\alpha}C_{\alpha}B^{\mu\nu}\tilde{B}_{\mu\nu}+32C^{\alpha}B^{\mu\nu}C_{\alpha}\tilde{B}_{\mu\nu}+64{\rm i}g_{\alpha\beta}B^{\mu\alpha}\tilde{B}^{\nu\beta}[C_{\mu},C_{\nu}]+

\displaystyle\quad+192{\rm i}(A_{\mu}B_{\nu\alpha}C^{\nu}B^{\mu\alpha}-A_{\mu}B^{\mu\alpha}C^{\nu}B_{\nu\alpha})+64{\rm i}B^{\alpha\beta}B_{\alpha\beta}[A^{\mu},C_{\mu}]+

\displaystyle\quad+64{\rm i}(A_{\mu}C^{\nu}B_{\nu\alpha}B^{\mu\alpha}-A_{\mu}B^{\mu\alpha}B_{\nu\alpha}C^{\nu})+

\displaystyle\quad-64B^{\alpha\beta}B_{\alpha\beta}B^{\mu\nu}\tilde{B}_{\mu\nu}+64[B^{\alpha\mu},B_{\beta\mu}][B_{\alpha\nu},\tilde{B}^{\beta\nu}]\Big).

(44)

Among many terms in the above theorem, we can find the terms that
reproduce the standard axial anomaly of Yang-Mills field:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}(x)_{\kappa,B,C,\lambda=0}=

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\text{Tr}(\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}\partial_{\alpha}A_{\beta})+\frac{{\rm i}}{2\pi^{2}}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\text{Tr}(\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}A_{\alpha}A_{\beta})+

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\text{Tr}([A_{\mu},A_{\nu}][A_{\alpha},A_{\beta}])=

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\text{Tr}(A_{\mu\nu}A_{\alpha\beta}),

(45)

where
A_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}+{\rm i}[A_{\mu},A_{\nu}]
.

Another part that we can extract from the full result are the terms that depend only on scalar fields
\kappa
and
\lambda
, that is when
A_{\mu}=0
,
B_{\mu\nu}=0
,
C_{\mu}=0
:

{\mathcal{A}}(x)_{A,B,C=0}=\frac{1}{12\pi^{2}}\text{Tr}(4\kappa\partial^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\lambda+6\partial^{\mu}\kappa\partial_{\mu}\lambda-4\kappa^{3}\lambda),

(46)

with the conditions for the vanishing of divergent part being

\displaystyle\text{tr}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=0,

(47)

\displaystyle\text{tr}(\kappa\lambda)

\displaystyle=0,

(48)

\displaystyle\text{tr}(-\kappa^{2}\lambda+\lambda^{3})

\displaystyle=0.

(49)

One more special case would be when the configuration of external fields has
A_{\mu}=0
,
B_{\mu\nu}=0
,
\lambda=0
:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}(x)_{A,B,\lambda=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{12\pi^{2}}\text{Tr}\big(\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\partial_{\mu}C_{\nu}\partial_{\rho}C_{\sigma}+10\partial_{\mu}(\kappa^{2}C^{\mu})\big)=

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{12\pi^{2}}\partial_{\mu}\text{Tr}\big(\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}C_{\nu}\partial_{\rho}C_{\sigma}+10\kappa^{2}C^{\mu}\big),

(50)

with the only condition for the vanishing of divergent part being

\text{tr}(\partial_{\mu}C^{\mu})=0.

(51)

4
First steps of proof

The remainder of the paper is devoted to a proof of the above theorem.

From the definition of
D^{-1}(x,y;M,\Phi)
we have:

\big(\gamma^{\mu}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}+M+\Phi(x)\big)D^{-1}(x,y;M,\Phi)=\delta^{4}(x-y),

(52)

D^{-1}(x,y;M,\Phi)\big(-\gamma^{\mu}\frac{\overleftarrow{\partial}}{\partial y^{\mu}}+M+\Phi(y)\big)=\delta^{4}(x-y),

(53)

therefore

\displaystyle\big(\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}+m+\Phi(x)\big)(D^{-1})_{\Lambda}(x,y;\Phi)

10
Summary

Higher order integrals include at least six propagators
D_{0}^{-1}(p,M)
, so even with the factor
(m-M_{i})
they will not produce terms divergent or finite in the limit
\Lambda\rightarrow\infty
. The only divergent terms come from the terms up to the 3-rd order in
\Phi
, and the finite terms are up to the 4-th order in
\Phi
, which we calculated above. Writing all of them together we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}_{\Lambda}(x)=

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{i}{\mathcal{C}}_{i}\big(m-M_{i}\big)M_{i}^{2}\log(M_{i}^{2})\Big)\frac{-1}{2\pi^{2}}\text{Tr}(\lambda)+

\displaystyle\quad+\Big(\sum_{i}{\mathcal{C}}_{i}(m-M_{i})M_{i}\log(M_{i}^{2})\Big)\times

\displaystyle\qquad\times\Big(\frac{-1}{2\pi^{2}}\text{Tr}(\partial_{\mu}C^{\mu})+\frac{-1}{\pi^{2}}\text{Tr}(\kappa\lambda+B_{\mu\nu}\tilde{B}_{\mu\nu})\Big)+

\displaystyle\quad+\Big(\sum_{i}{\mathcal{C}}_{i}2(m-M_{i})\log(M_{i}^{2})\Big)\bigg(\sum_{I}\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\text{Tr}(\Box\lambda)+

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{-1}{4\pi^{2}}\text{Tr}\Big(\kappa^{2}\lambda-{\rm i}\kappa[A^{\mu},C_{\mu}]-2\kappa B^{\mu\nu}\tilde{B}_{\mu\nu}+{\rm i}[A^{\mu},A^{\nu}]\tilde{B}_{\mu\nu}+

\displaystyle\qquad-{\rm i}\tilde{B}_{\mu\nu}[C^{\mu},C^{\nu}]-2B^{\mu\nu}B_{\mu\nu}\lambda-2C^{\mu}C_{\mu}\lambda-\lambda^{3}\Big)\bigg)+{\mathcal{O}}(\Lambda^{0}).

(77)

For the divergent terms to vanish, we need then

\text{tr}(\lambda)=0,

(78)

\text{tr}\Big(\partial_{\mu}C^{\mu}+2\kappa\lambda+2B^{\mu\nu}\tilde{B}_{\mu\nu}\Big)=0,

(79)

\displaystyle\text{tr}\Big(\partial^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\lambda-\kappa^{2}\lambda+{\rm i}\kappa[A^{\mu},C_{\mu}]+2\kappa B^{\mu\nu}\tilde{B}_{\mu\nu}-{\rm i}[A^{\mu},A^{\nu}]\tilde{B}_{\mu\nu}+

\displaystyle\qquad+{\rm i}\tilde{B}_{\mu\nu}[C^{\mu},C^{\nu}]+2B^{\mu\nu}B_{\mu\nu}\lambda+2C^{\mu}C_{\mu}\lambda+\lambda^{3}\Big)=0.

(80)

We require these conditions to be satsfied everyhwere, in the whole space. From the first condition it follows then that
\text{tr}(\partial^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\lambda)=0
and this term can be omitted from the third condition, leaving us with:

\displaystyle\text{tr}\Big(-\kappa^{2}\lambda+{\rm i}\kappa[A^{\mu},C_{\mu}]+2\kappa B^{\mu\nu}\tilde{B}_{\mu\nu}-{\rm i}[A^{\mu},A^{\nu}]\tilde{B}_{\mu\nu}+

\displaystyle\qquad+{\rm i}\tilde{B}_{\mu\nu}[C^{\mu},C^{\nu}]+2B^{\mu\nu}B_{\mu\nu}\lambda+2C^{\mu}C_{\mu}\lambda+\lambda^{3}\Big)=0.

(81)

Optionally, we can also use the second condition to obtain the identity

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{12\pi^{2}}{\rm Tr}\big(\partial^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}C^{\nu}\big)=

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{12\pi^{2}}\partial^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}{\rm Tr}\big(-2\kappa\lambda-2B^{\rho\sigma}\tilde{B}_{\rho\sigma}\big).

(82)

This way, we can get rid of the term
{\mathcal{A}}^{(1)}
completely, adding additional terms to
{\mathcal{A}}^{(2)}
instead. Whether it is worth it, may be situational.

If these conditions are fullfilled, the regularized anomaly has a finite limit for
\Lambda\rightarrow\infty
, which contains terms from the first to the fourth order in fields:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}(x)=\lim_{\Lambda\rightarrow\infty}{\mathcal{A}}_{\Lambda}(x)={\mathcal{A}}^{(1)}(x)+{\mathcal{A}}^{(2)}(x)+{\mathcal{A}}^{(3)}(x)+{\mathcal{A}}^{(4)}(x).

(83)

Appendix B
Identity for Pauli-Villars regularization

A proof that (
29
) is a solution of (
28
):

Using (
29
) we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i}{\mathcal{C}}_{i}M_{i}^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}\binom{n}{i}(m+i\Lambda)^{k}=

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=0}^{k}\binom{k}{p}m^{k-p}\Lambda^{p}\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}\binom{n}{i}i^{p}.

(102)

We have then

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}\binom{n}{i}i^{p}=

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}\binom{n}{i}i^{p}x^{i}\Big)\Big|_{x=1}=

\displaystyle=\Big(\big(x\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}x}\big)^{p}\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}\binom{n}{i}x^{i}\Big)\Big|_{x=1}=

\displaystyle=\Big(\big(x\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}x}\big)^{p}(1-x)^{n}\Big)\Big|_{x=1}.

(103)

Since
x=1
is a n-th order zero of function
(1-x)^{n}
, then even
after applying the operator
x\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}x}
less than
n
times,
it will still be a zero of the resulting function; therefore we get

\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}\binom{n}{i}i^{p}=0,\qquad\text{for }p<n;

(104)

\sum_{i}{\mathcal{C}}_{i}M_{i}^{k}=0,\qquad\text{for }k<n.

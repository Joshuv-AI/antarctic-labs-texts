Andrásfai–Erdős–Sós theorem for the generalized triangle
Xizhi Liu, Sijie Ren, Jian Wang

Abstract
The celebrated Andrásfai–Erdős–Sós Theorem from 1974 shows that every n n -vertex triangle-free graph with minimum degree greater than 2 ​ n / 5 2n/5 must be bipartite. Its extensions to 3 3 -uniform hypergraphs without the generalized triangle F 5 = { a ​ b ​ c , a ​ b ​ d , c ​ d ​ e } F_{5}=\{abc,abd,cde\} have been explored in several previous works such as [ LMR23 , HLZ24 ] , demonstrating the existence of ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 such that for large n n , every n n -vertex F 5 F_{5} -free 3 3 -graph with minimum degree greater than ( 1 / 9 − ε ) ​ n 2 (1/9-\varepsilon)n^{2} must be 3 3 -partite. We determine the optimal value for ε \varepsilon by showing that for n ≥ 5000 n\geq 5000 , every n n -vertex F 5 F_{5} -free 3 3 -graph with minimum degree greater than 4 ​ n 2 / 45 4n^{2}/45 must be 3 3 -partite, thus establishing the first tight Andrásfai–Erdős–Sós type theorem for hypergraphs. As a corollary, for all positive n n , every n n -vertex cancellative 3 3 -graph with minimum degree greater than 4 ​ n 2 / 45 4n^{2}/45 must be 3 3 -partite. This result is also optimal and considerably strengthens prior work, such as that by Bollobás [ Bol74 ] and Keevash–Mubayi [ KM04 ] . Keywords: Andrásfai–Erdős–Sós theorem, generalized triangle, cancellative hypergraph, degree-stability

Introduction

Given an integer
r\geq 2
, an
r
-uniform hypergraph
(henceforth
r
-graph
)
\mathcal{H}
is a collection of
r
-subsets of some finite set
V
.
We identify a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
with its edge set and use
V(\mathcal{H})
to denote its vertex set.
The size of
V(\mathcal{H})
is denoted by
v(\mathcal{H})
.
The
degree

d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)
of a vertex
v
in
\mathcal{H}
is the number of edges containing
v
.
We use
\delta(\mathcal{H})
,
\Delta(\mathcal{H})
, and
d(\mathcal{H})
to denote the
minimum
,
maximum
, and
average degree
of
\mathcal{H}
, respectively.

Given a family
\mathcal{F}
of
r
-graphs, we say an
r
-graph
\mathcal{H}
is
\mathcal{F}
-free

if it does not contain any member of
\mathcal{F}
as a subgraph.
The
Turán number

\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{F})
of
\mathcal{F}
is the maximum number of edges in an
\mathcal{F}
-free
r
-graph on
n
vertices.
The
Turán density
of
\mathcal{F}
is defined as
\pi(\mathcal{F})\coloneq\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{F})/{n\choose r}
.
We call
\mathcal{F}

nondegenerate
if
\pi(\mathcal{F})>0
.

Determining
\pi(\mathcal{F})
(and
\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{F})
) is a central topic in Extremal Combinatorics.
Extending Turán’s foundational theorem
[
Tur41
]
on
\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{\ell+1})
, the classical Erdős–Stone Theorem
[
ES46
]
(see also
[
ES66
]
) completely determined the value of
\pi(\mathcal{F})
for graph families.
However, determining
\pi(\mathcal{F})
for
r
-graphs with
r\geq 3
is notoriously difficult, with only a few exact results known.
One classical open problem in the field is Turán’s famous tetrahedron conjecture from the 1940s, which seeks to determine the Turán density of the complete
3
-graphs on
4
vertices
K_{4}^{3}
.
For an overview of results up to 2011, we refer the reader to the excellent survey by Keevash
[
Kee11
]
.

To gain a better understanding of Turán problems and also provide an important tool for solving them, Simonovits
[
Sim68
]
initiated the study of the structure of near-extremal constructions by showing that every
K_{\ell+1}
-free graph whose average degree is close to extremal must be structurally close to being
r
-partite.
Later, in a seminal work
[
AES74
]
, Andrásfai–Erdős–Sós showed that for
\ell\geq 2
, every
K_{\ell+1}
-free graph
G
on
n
vertices with minimum degree greater than
\frac{3\ell-4}{3\ell-1}n
must be
\ell
-partite. Moreover, the bound
\frac{3\ell-4}{3\ell-1}n
is tight.
It worth noting that the Andrásfai–Erdős–Sós Theorem implies both the Turán Theorem and Simonovits’ stability theorem (see remarks in
[
LMR23
, Section 1.1]
).

Extensions of the Andrásfai–Erdős–Sós Theorem to hypergraphs appear to be more challenging, as hypergraph extremal constructions can exhibit much richer structures (see e.g.
[
Pik14
,
LP22
,
BCL22
]
).
The first result of this type for hypergraphs appears to be the work Füredi–Simonovits
[
FS05
]
(see also
[
KS05
]
), who extended the celebrated result of De Caen–Füredi
[
DCF00
]
by proving that for large
n
, if an
n
-vertex
3
-graph does not contain the Fano plane and has minimum degree greater than
(3/8-\varepsilon)n^{2}
for some small constant
\varepsilon>0
, then it must be bipartite.
Similar results for other hypergraphs were obtained later in works such as
[
FPS06
]
.
Very recently, general criteria for a hypergraph family
\mathcal{F}
to exhibit Andrásfai–Erdős–Sós-type stability were established in
[
LMR23
,
HLZ24
,
CL24
]
.
However, as far as we are aware, no tight Andrásfai–Erdős–Sós-type results had been obtained for hypergraphs prior to our work.

We consider the extension of the Andrásfai–Erdős–Sós Theorem to hypergraph triangles.
In the 1960s, as a way of extending Turán’s theorem on triangles (also known as the Mantel Theorem
[
Man07
]
) to hypergraphs, Katona proposed the problem of determining the maximum number of edges in an
n
-vertex
3
-graph that avoids three edges
A,B,C
such that the symmetric difference of
A
and
B
is contained in
C
(also known as cancellative
3
-graphs).
Note that this is equivalent to determining the value of
\mathrm{ex}(n,\{K_{4}^{3-},F_{5}\})
, where
K_{4}^{3-}
is the
4
-vertex
3
-graph with edge set
\{abc,abd,acd\}
and
F_{5}
is the
5
-vertex
3
-graph with edge set
\{abc,abd,cde\}
.
Bollobás
[
Bol74
]
solved this problem by proving that the extremal construction for
\mathrm{ex}(n,\{K_{4}^{3-},F_{5}\})
is balanced complete
3
-partite
3
-graph on
n
vertices
T_{3}(n,3)
.
Later, Frankl–Füredi
[
FF83
]
strengthened Bollobás’s theorem by showing that for
n\geq 3000
,
\mathrm{ex}(n,F_{5})=|T_{3}(n,3)|
, thereby establishing the first tight bound for the Turán number of a single hypergraph.
Their result was further refined in subsequent works such as
[
KM04
,
Gol
]
.

In
[
KM04
]
, Keevash–Mubayi proved that for large
n
, every
n
-vertex
F_{5}
-free
3
-graphs with average degree at least
(1/9-o(1))n^{2}
is structurally close to being
3
-partite, thus establishing the first Simonovits-type stability theorem for hypergraphs. Their result was later improved in
[
Liu21
]
.
The Andrásfai–Erdős–Sós-type theorem for
F_{5}
was establish recently in
[
LMR23
,
HLZ24
]
\colon

There exists a constant
\varepsilon>0
such that for large
n
, every
n
-vertex
F_{5}
-free
3
-graphs with minimum degree greater than
(1/9-\varepsilon)n^{2}
is
3
-partite.
Unfortunately, the general method used in
[
LMR23
,
HLZ24
]
is unlikely to yield an optimal value for
\varepsilon
, and hence, no explicit value for
\varepsilon
was provided in these works.

Using a very different approach, we determine the optimal value for
\varepsilon
in the following theorem, and thus establishing the first tight Andrásfai–Erdős–Sós theorem for hypergraphs.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
n\geq 5000
, every
n
-vertex
F_{5}
-free
3
-graph with
\delta(\mathcal{H})>\frac{4n^{2}}{45}
is
3
-partite.

Using a standard blowup argument, the constraint
n\geq 5000
can be eliminated for
\{K_{4}^{3-},F_{5}\}
-free
3
-graphs.

Theorem 1.2
.

Every
n
-vertex
\{K_{4}^{3-},F_{5}\}
-free
3
-graph with
\delta(\mathcal{H})>\frac{4n^{2}}{45}
is
3
-partite.

Remark.

The bound
\frac{4n^{2}}{45}
is tight in both Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, as shown by the following construction.

Figure 1
:
The
3
-uniform
5
-wheel

.

Let the
3
-uniform
5
-wheel
W_{5}^{3}
be the

-graph on

\displaystyle\{uv_{1}v_{2},uv_{2}v_{3},uv_{3}v_{4},uv_{4}v_{5},uv_{5}v_{1}\}.

Graphs

Given a graph

For a vertex

\displaystyle N_{G}(v)\coloneqq\left\{u\in V(G)\colon\{u,v\}\in G\right\}.

For convenience, we set

.
We say that

The

Given two disjoint sets

and

.

We say a graph

We say a map

for all

.
If such a homomorphism exists, we say

is

-colorable
.
Note that

is

-colorable iff

is a subgraph of some blowup of

.

The

of two vertex-disjoint graphs

and

is the graph on
V(G)\cup V(H)
with edge set

\displaystyle G\cup H\cup\left\{\{u,v\}\colon u\in V(G)\text{ and }v\in V(H)\right\}.

We say a graph
G
is

-free
if it is

-free but adding any new edge into

.

2.2
Hypergraphs

Given a
3
-graph
\mathcal{H}
, the
shadow

\partial\mathcal{H}
of
\mathcal{H}
is defined as

\displaystyle\partial\mathcal{H}\coloneqq\left\{e\in\binom{V(\mathcal{H})}{2}\colon\text{there exists $E\in\mathcal{H}$ with $e\subseteq E$}\right\}.

For every
v\in V(\mathcal{H})
, the
link
of
v
in
\mathcal{H}
is

\displaystyle L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)\coloneqq\left\{e\in\binom{V(\mathcal{H})}{2}\colon e\cup\{v\}\in\mathcal{H}\right\}.

For convenience, given a vertex set
W\subseteq V(\mathcal{H})
, we let
L_{\mathcal{H}}(v,W)\coloneqq L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)\cap\binom{W}{2}
.

For a pair of vertices
\{u,v\}\subseteq V(\mathcal{H})
, the
neighborhood
of
\{u,v\}
in
\mathcal{H}
is

\displaystyle N_{\mathcal{H}}(uv)\coloneqq\left\{w\in V(\mathcal{H})\colon\{u,v,w\}\in\mathcal{H}\right\}.

A vertex set

is
independent
in
\mathcal{H}
if every edge in
\mathcal{H}
contains at most one vertex in
I
.
The
independence number

\alpha(\mathcal{H})
is the maximum size of an independent set in
\mathcal{H}
.
Note that under this definition, a set

is independent in
\mathcal{H}
iff it is independent in
\partial\mathcal{H}
. Thus,
\alpha(\mathcal{H})=\alpha(\partial\mathcal{H})
.

.

-free

-free

and

, assuming the validity of the following two key propositions, whose proofs are postponed to Sections

The first proposition, which is an extension of the feasible region theorems for

-free.

Suppose that

.

\displaystyle B_{S}\coloneqq\bigcup_{\{u,v\}\subseteq S}E_{uv},\quad W_{S}\coloneqq V\setminus B_{S},\quad\text{and}\quad G_{S}\coloneqq\bigcup_{v\in S}L_{\mathcal{H}}(v,W_{S}).

\displaystyle\geq|S|\left(\left(\frac{1}{12}+\varepsilon\right)n^{2}-\binom{|S|+1}{2}n\right).

(1)

Claim 4.1
.

The graph
G
is

.
Suppose to the contrary that there exists
(x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{5})\in\Delta_{5}
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in[5]}y_{i}y_{i+1}>\frac{4}{45},\quad\text{and}

(11)

\displaystyle x(y_{i-1}+y_{i+1})>\frac{4}{45}\quad\mathrm{for}\quad i\in[5].

(12)

Since
x+\sum_{i\in[5]}y_{i}=1
, it follows from (
12
) that

\displaystyle x(1-x)=x\sum_{i\in[5]}y_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in[5]}x(y_{i-1}+y_{i+1})>\frac{1}{2}\cdot 5\cdot\frac{4}{45}=\frac{2}{9}.

(13)

Solving this inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle 1/3<x<2/3.

(14)

Let
z_{i}\coloneqq y_{i-1}+y_{i+1}
for
i\in[5]
.
Let

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}=\begin{pmatrix}y_{1}\\
y_{2}\\
y_{3}\\
y_{4}\\
y_{5}\end{pmatrix},\quad\mathbf{z}=\begin{pmatrix}z_{1}\\
z_{2}\\
z_{3}\\
z_{4}\\
z_{5}\end{pmatrix},\quad\text{and}\quad B=\begin{pmatrix}1&1/2&1&1&1/2\\
1/2&1&1/2&1&1\\
1&1/2&1&1/2&1\\
1&1&1/2&1&1/2\\
1/2&1&1&1/2&1\end{pmatrix}.

Let
A_{2}
denote the adjacency matrix of
\Gamma_{2}
, noting from the definition that
\mathbf{z}=A_{2}\mathbf{y}
and thus,
\mathbf{y}=A_{2}^{-1}\mathbf{z}
.
Let
Q
denote the graph on
[5]
with edge set
\{13,24,35,41,52\}
.
Some straightforward calculations show that
\left(A_{2}^{-1}\right)^{T}BA_{2}^{-1}=\frac{1}{2}A_{Q}
.
Combining these with (
11
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{4}{45}<\sum_{i\in[5]}y_{i}y_{i+1}=\left(\sum_{i\in[5]}y_{i}\right)^{2}-\mathbf{y}^{T}B\mathbf{y}

\displaystyle=\left(1-x\right)^{2}-\mathbf{z}^{T}\left(A_{2}^{-1}\right)^{T}BA_{2}^{-1}\mathbf{z}

\displaystyle=\left(1-x\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{z}^{T}A_{Q}\mathbf{z}

\displaystyle=\left(1-x\right)^{2}-\sum_{i\in[5]}z_{i}z_{i+1}.

(15)

Recall from (
12
) that for each
i\in[5]
,
xz_{i}>\frac{4}{45}
and thus,
z_{i}>\frac{4}{45x}
.
Applying Lemma
5.8
to
Q
with
m\coloneqq 5
,
d\coloneqq 2
,
z\coloneqq\sum_{i\in[5]}z_{i}=2\sum_{i\in[5]}y_{i}=2(1-x)
, and
z_{0}\coloneqq\frac{4}{45x}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in[5]}z_{i}z_{i+2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{z}^{T}A_{Q}\mathbf{z}

\displaystyle\geq 2\cdot 2(1-x)\cdot\frac{4}{45x}-\frac{1}{2}\cdot 2\cdot 5\cdot\left(\frac{4}{45x}\right)^{2}=\frac{16(-9x^{2}+9x-1)}{405x^{2}}.

Combining this with (
15
), we obtain

\displaystyle(1-x)^{2}-\frac{16(-9x^{2}+9x-1)}{405x^{2}}>\frac{4}{45},

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle(1-3x)(135x^{3}-225x^{2}+96x-16)<0.

Straightforward calculations show that
135x^{3}-225x^{2}+96x-16<0
for
x\in[0,1]
.
So the inequality above implies that
1-3x>0
, which contradicts (
14
). This completes the proof of Lemma
5.2
.

5.3
Proof of Lemma
5.3

We will use the following lemma in the proof of Lemma
5.3
.

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
d\geq 2
be an integer and
A_{d}
denote the adjacency matrix of
\Gamma_{d}
.
Then

\displaystyle\left(A_{d}^{-1}\right)^{T}\left(\frac{1}{2}A_{d}-\binom{d}{2}J_{3d-1}\right)A_{d}^{-1}=\frac{W_{3d-1}-J_{3d-1}}{2}.

Proof of Lemma
5.9
.

Let
m\coloneqq 3d-1
and
M\coloneqq\left(A_{d}^{-1}\right)^{T}\left(\frac{1}{2}A_{d}-\binom{d}{2}J_{m}\right)A_{d}^{-1}
.
Using Lemma
5.7
and the fact that
A_{d}
is symmetric, we obtain

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}A_{d}^{-1}-\binom{d}{2}A_{d}^{-1}J_{m}A_{d}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(W_{m}-\frac{1}{d}J_{m}\right)-\binom{d}{2}\left(W_{m}-\frac{1}{d}J_{m}\right)J_{m}\left(W_{m}-\frac{1}{d}J_{m}\right).

Since
W_{m}J_{m}=J_{m}W_{m}=3J_{m}
and
J_{m}J_{m}=mJ_{m}
, the equation above continues as

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(W_{m}-\frac{1}{d}J_{m}\right)-\binom{d}{2}\left(W_{m}J_{m}W_{m}-\frac{1}{d}W_{m}J_{m}J_{m}-\frac{1}{d}J_{m}J_{m}W_{m}+\frac{1}{d^{2}}J_{m}^{3}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(W_{m}-\frac{1}{d}J_{m}\right)-\binom{d}{2}\left(9J_{m}-\frac{1}{d}\cdot 3mJ_{m}-\frac{1}{d}\cdot 3mJ_{m}+\frac{1}{d^{2}}m^{2}J_{m}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(W_{m}-J_{m}\right),

as desired.

Next, we present the proof of Lemma
5.3
.

Proof of Lemma
5.3
.

Fix an integer
d\in[2,12]
. Suppose to the contrary that there exists
(y_{1},\ldots,y_{3d-1})\in\mathbb{R}^{3d-1}
with
\min_{i\in[3d-1]}y_{i}>0
that satisfies

\displaystyle\sum_{ij\in\Gamma_{d}}y_{i}y_{j}>\frac{4}{45},

(16)

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in N_{\Gamma_{d}}(i)}y_{j}>\frac{6}{17}\sum_{i\in[3d-1]}y_{i}\quad\mathrm{for}\quad i\in[3d-1],\quad\text{and}

(17)

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in[3d-1]}y_{i}<3-\frac{16}{3\sqrt{5}}.

(18)

Let
m\coloneqq 3d-1
and
y\coloneqq\sum_{i\in[m]}y_{i}
, noting from (
18
) that
y<3-\frac{16}{3\sqrt{5}}
.
For each
i\in[m]
, let
z_{i}\coloneqq\sum_{j\in N_{\Gamma_{d}}(i)}y_{i}
.
Since
\Gamma_{d}
is
d
-regular, we have

\displaystyle z\coloneqq\sum_{i\in[m]}z_{i}=d\sum_{i\in[m]}y_{i}=dy.

Let
\mathbf{y}\coloneqq(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})^{T}
and
\mathbf{z}\coloneqq(z_{1},\ldots,z_{m})^{T}
.
Let
A_{d}
denote the adjacency matrix of
\Gamma_{d}
, noting from the definition that
\mathbf{z}=A_{d}\mathbf{y}
and thus,
\mathbf{y}=A_{d}^{-1}\mathbf{z}
.
Combining this with (
16
) and Lemma
5.9
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{4}{45}<\sum_{ij\in\Gamma_{d}}y_{i}y_{j}=\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{y}^{T}A_{d}\mathbf{y}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{y}^{T}\left(\frac{1}{2}A_{d}-\binom{d}{2}J_{m}+\binom{d}{2}J_{m}\right)\mathbf{y}

\displaystyle=\binom{d}{2}\mathbf{y}^{T}J_{m}\mathbf{y}+\mathbf{y}^{T}\left(\frac{1}{2}A_{d}-\binom{d}{2}J_{m}\right)\mathbf{y}

\displaystyle=\binom{d}{2}\left(\sum_{i\in[m]}y_{i}\right)^{2}+\mathbf{z}^{T}\left(A_{d}^{-1}\right)^{T}\left(\frac{1}{2}A_{d}-\binom{d}{2}J_{m}\right)A_{d}^{-1}\mathbf{z}

\displaystyle=\binom{d}{2}y^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{z}^{T}\left(J_{m}-W_{m}\right)\mathbf{z}.

(19)

Next, we consider the lower bound for
\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{z}^{T}\left(J_{m}-W_{m}\right)\mathbf{z}
.
Let
C_{m}
denote the cycle on
[m]
with edge set
\{\{1,2\},\{2,3\},\ldots,\{m-1,m\},\{m,1\}\}
. Observe that
J_{m}-W_{m}
is identical to the adjacency matrix of the complement of
C_{m}
, i.e.
J_{m}-W_{m}=A_{\overline{C}_{m}}
.
Applying Lemma
5.8
to
\overline{C}_{m}
with
z_{0}\coloneqq\frac{6y}{17}
(due to (
17
)) and
z=dy
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{z}^{T}\left(J_{m}-W_{m}\right)\mathbf{z}

\displaystyle\geq(m-3)\cdot dy\cdot\frac{6y}{17}-\frac{1}{2}\cdot(m-3)\cdot m\cdot\left(\frac{6y}{17}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{6(m-3)(17d-3m)}{289}y^{2}=\frac{6(3d-4)(8d+3)}{289}y^{2}.

Combining this with (
19
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{4}{45}

\displaystyle<\binom{d}{2}y^{2}-\frac{6(3d-4)(8d+3)}{289}y^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{d^{2}-13d+144}{578}y^{2}<\frac{d^{2}-13d+144}{578}\left(3-\frac{16}{3\sqrt{5}}\right)^{2},

where the last inequality follows from (
18
).
However, straightforward calculations show that this inequality cannot hold for
d\in[2,12]
.
This completes the proof of Lemma
5.3
.

6
Proof of Proposition
3.2

In this section, we prove Proposition
3.2
.
Observe that if a
3
-graph
\mathcal{H}
satisfies
K_{4}\not\subseteq\partial\mathcal{H}
, then it is
\{K_{4}^{3-},F_{5}\}
-free. Thus, all results concerning
\{K_{4}^{3-},F_{5}\}
-free
3
-graphs can be applied in this proof.

Proof of Proposition
3.2
.

Fix
n\geq 1
. Let
\mathcal{H}
be an
n
-vertex
3
-graph satisfying
\delta(\mathcal{H})>4n^{2}/45
and
K_{4}\not\subseteq\partial\mathcal{H}
.
Our goal is to show that
\mathcal{H}
, and equivalently
\partial\mathcal{H}
, is
3
-partite.
Note that we may assume that
\mathcal{H}
is maximal in the sense that

(i)

every triangle in
\partial\mathcal{H}
is an edge of
\mathcal{H}
, and

(ii)

adding any new edge to
\mathcal{H}
would violate the
K_{4}
-freeness of
\partial\mathcal{H}
.

Let
V\coloneqq V(\mathcal{H})
and let
G
be a maximal
K_{4}
-free graph on
V
such that
\partial\mathcal{H}\subseteq G
.
Suppose to the contrary that
\mathcal{H}
is not
3
-partite. Then
G
is not
3
-partite as well.

Claim 6.1
.

We have
\delta(G)\geq\delta(\partial\mathcal{H})>\frac{4}{3\sqrt{5}}n
.

Proof of Claim
6.1
.

Take a vertex
v\in V(\mathcal{H})
such that
d_{\partial\mathcal{H}}(v)=\delta(\partial\mathcal{H})
.
After removing isolated vertices, we can view the vertex set of the graph
L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)
as
N_{\partial\mathcal{H}}(v)
.
Since
L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)
is triangle-free (by Fact
2.5

(i)
), it follows from Mantel’s theorem that

\displaystyle\delta(\mathcal{H})\leq|L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)|\leq\frac{|N_{\partial\mathcal{H}}(v)|^{2}}{4}.

It follows that
\delta(\partial\mathcal{H})=|N_{\partial\mathcal{H}}(v)|\geq 2\sqrt{\delta(\mathcal{H})}>\frac{4}{3\sqrt{5}}n
.

Since
\delta(G)>\frac{4}{3\sqrt{5}}n>\frac{4}{7}n
, it follows from Theorem
2.1
that either
G
is the join of an independent set and a maximal triangle-free graph or

\displaystyle\alpha(G)>4\delta(G)-2n>\left(\frac{16}{3\sqrt{5}}-2\right)n.

Case 1.
The graph
G
is the join graph of an independent set
I
and a maximal triangle-free graph, and
\alpha(G)\leq\left(\frac{16}{3\sqrt{5}}-2\right)n
.

Let
U\coloneqq V\setminus I
. Since
G
is not
3
-partite,
G[U]
cannot not be bipartite. Let
x\in[0,1]
be the real number such that
|I|=xn
, noting that
x\leq\frac{16}{3\sqrt{5}}-2
. Since
G
is maximal
K_{4}
-free and
G[U]
is triangle-free, we have
x>0
.
Since
I
is independent and
G[U]
is triangle-free, every edge
e\in\mathcal{H}
satisfies
|e\cap I|=1
and
|e\cap U|=2
.
Hence, for every
v\in U
, we have

\displaystyle L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)\leq|I|\cdot|N_{G}(v,U)|=|I|\cdot d_{G[U]}(v)=xn\cdot d_{G[U]}(v).

(20)

Claim 6.2
.

The induced subgraph
G[U]
is a blowup of
C_{5}
.

Proof of Claim
6.2
.

Since
G[U]
is non-bipartite and maximal triangle-free, it suffices to show that
G[U]
is
C_{5}
-colorable. By Theorem
2.2
, this is reduced to show that
\delta(G[U])>\frac{3}{8}|U|
.
Suppose to the contrary that there exists a vertex
v\in U
with
d_{G[U]}(v)\leq\frac{3}{8}|U|
. Then it follows from (
20
) and the assumption
x\leq\frac{16}{3\sqrt{5}}-2<\frac{1}{2}
that

\displaystyle\frac{4}{45}n^{2}<L_{\mathcal{H}}(u)\leq xn\cdot d_{G[U]}(v)\leq\frac{3}{8}x(1-x)n^{2}\leq\left(2\sqrt{5}-\frac{263}{60}\right)n^{2}<\frac{4}{45}n^{2},

a contradiction.

Fix a homomorphism
\psi
from
G[U]
to
C_{5}
. Let
D_{i}\coloneqq\psi^{-1}(i)
and
y_{i}\coloneqq|D_{i}|/n
for
i\in[5]
. It follows from Claim
6.2
that for each
i\in[5]
,
y_{i}>0
, and the induced subgraph of
G
on
D_{i}\cup D_{i+1}
is complete bipartite with parts
D_{i}
and
D_{i+1}
. Here, the indices are taken modulo
5
.

Since
G
is the join of
I
and
G[U]
and
G[U]
is a blowup of
C_{5}
, it follows from the maximality of
\mathcal{H}
that
\partial\mathcal{H}=G
and
\mathcal{H}
is the blowup
W_{5}^{3}[xn,y_{1}n,\ldots,y_{5}n]
of the
3
-uniform
5
-wheel, as defined in Section
1
.
Fix a vertex
v\in I
and fix
u_{i}\in D_{i}
for every
i\in[5]
.
It follows from the assumption on
\delta(\mathcal{H})
that

\displaystyle\frac{4}{45}

\displaystyle<\frac{|L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)|}{n^{2}}=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i\in[5]}|D_{i}||D_{i+1}|=\sum_{i\in[5]}y_{i}y_{i+1},\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\frac{4}{45}

\displaystyle<\frac{|L_{\mathcal{H}}(u_{i})|}{n^{2}}=\frac{1}{n^{2}}|I|\left(|D_{i-1}|+|D_{i+1}|\right)=x(y_{i-1}+y_{i+1})\quad\text{for}\quad i\in[5].

However, according to Lemma
5.2
, these inequalities are impossible.

Case 2.

\alpha(G)>\left(\frac{16}{3\sqrt{5}}-2\right)n
.

Let
I
be an independent set of maximum size in
G
.
Let
x\coloneqq|I|/n=\alpha(G)/n
, noting that
x>\frac{16}{3\sqrt{5}}-2
. Fix a vertex
v\in I
and let
U\coloneqq N_{G}(v)\subseteq V\setminus I
. Let
y\coloneqq|U|/n
.
Let
T\coloneqq V\setminus(I\cup U)
, noting that
|T|=(1-x-y)n
.
Since
G
is
K_{4}
-free, the induced subgraph
G[U]
is triangle-free.
Additionally, it follows from Claim
6.1
that

\displaystyle y=\frac{|U|}{n}=\frac{d_{G}(v)}{n}\geq\frac{\delta(G)}{n}>\frac{4}{3\sqrt{5}}.

(21)

Claim 6.3
.

We have
\delta(G[U])>\frac{6}{17}|U|
.
Thus, by Theorem
2.2
,
G[U]
is
\Gamma_{5}
-colorable.

Proof of Claim
6.3
.

Fix a vertex
u\in U
with
d_{G[U]}(u)=\delta(G[U])
.
By Lemma
5.4
, we have

\displaystyle|L_{\mathcal{H}}(u,U\cup I)|\geq|L_{\mathcal{H}}(u)|-\alpha(\mathcal{H})\cdot|V\setminus(U\cup I)|>\frac{4}{45}n^{2}-x(1-x-y)n^{2}.

(22)

Since
I
is an independent set and
G[U]
is triangle-free, every member of
L_{\mathcal{H}}(u,U\cup I)
must contain one vertex from
I
and one vertex from
N_{G}(u,U)\subseteq U
. It follows that

\displaystyle|L_{\mathcal{H}}(u,S\cup I)|\leq|I||N_{G}(u,U)|=xn\cdot|N_{G}(u,U)|.

Combining this with (
22
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\delta(G[U])}{|U|}=\frac{|N_{G}(u,U)|}{|U|}\geq\frac{|L_{\mathcal{H}}(u,S\cup I)|}{|U||I|}

\displaystyle>\frac{4n^{2}/45-x(1-x-y)n^{2}}{xn\cdot yn}

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{45xy}-\frac{1-x}{y}+1=1-\frac{1}{y}\left(1-x-\frac{4}{45x}\right).

Straightforward calculations show that
1-x-\frac{4}{45x}
is decreasing on
\left[\frac{16}{3\sqrt{5}}-2,1\right]
. Thus

\displaystyle 1-\frac{1}{y}\left(1-x-\frac{4}{45x}\right)>1-\frac{1}{4/3\sqrt{5}}\left(1-\frac{16}{3\sqrt{5}}+2-\frac{4}{45\cdot 16/3\sqrt{5}}\right)=\frac{33(12-5\sqrt{5})}{76}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{\delta(G[U])}{|U|}>\frac{33(12-5\sqrt{5})}{76}>\frac{6}{17},

as desired.

Claim 6.4
.

The induced subgraph
G[U]
is bipartite.

Proof of Claim
6.4
.

It follows from Claim
6.3
that there exists a surjective homomorphism
\psi
from
G[U]
to
\Gamma_{d}
for some
d\in[5]
. We are done if
d=1
, so we may assume that
d\in[2,5]
.

Let
D_{i}=\psi^{-1}(i)
and
y_{i}\coloneqq|D_{i}|/n
for
i\in[3d-1]
. Since
\psi\colon U\to[3d-1]
is surjective, we have
y_{i}>0
for every
i\in[3d-1]
.
First, since
x=\alpha(G)/n\geq\frac{16}{3\sqrt{5}}-2
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in[3d-1]}y_{i}\leq 1-x\leq 3-\frac{16}{3\sqrt{5}}.

Next, for each
i\in[3d-1]
, fix a vertex
u_{i}\in D_{i}
.
It follows from Claim
6.3
that

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in N_{\Gamma_{d}}(i)}y_{j}\geq\frac{d_{G[U]}(u_{i})}{n}>\frac{6}{17}\cdot\frac{|U|}{n}=\frac{6}{17}\sum_{i\in[3d-1]}y_{i}\quad\text{for every}\quad i\in[3d-1].

Finally, since
L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)\subseteq G[U]
, it follows from
\delta(\mathcal{H})>\frac{4}{45}n^{2}
that

\displaystyle\sum_{ij\in\Gamma_{d}}y_{i}y_{j}\geq\frac{|G[U]|}{n^{2}}\geq\frac{|L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)|}{n^{2}}\geq\frac{\delta(\mathcal{H})}{n^{2}}>\frac{4}{45}.

However, by Lemma
5.3
, these inequalities are impossible. Therefore,
G[U]
is bipartite.

By Claim
6.4
,
G[U]
is bipartite.
Let
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
denote the two parts of
G[U]
, noting that both
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
are independent in
G
.
Thus
\mathcal{H}
contains three pairwise disjoint independent sets
I,U_{1},U_{2}
.
Let
(\alpha,\beta,\delta,\gamma)\coloneqq\left(1-\frac{4}{3\sqrt{5}},\frac{4}{3\sqrt{5}},\frac{4}{45},3-\frac{20}{3\sqrt{5}}\right)
. It is straightforward to verify that this choice of
(\alpha,\beta,\delta,\gamma)
satisfies (
6
).

First, note that, by (
21
),

\displaystyle\alpha(\mathcal{H})=|I|\leq n-|U|<n-\frac{4}{3\sqrt{5}}n=\alpha n\quad\text{and}\quad\delta(\mathcal{H})>\frac{4}{45}n^{2}=\delta n^{2}.

In addition, since
\max\left\{|I|,|U_{1}|,|U_{2}|\right\}\leq\alpha(\mathcal{H})
, it follows from (
21
) that

\displaystyle\min\left\{|I|+|U_{1}|,|I|+|U_{2}|,|U_{1}|+|U_{2}|\right\}\geq|I|+|U_{1}|+|U_{2}|-\alpha(G)>\frac{4}{3\sqrt{5}}n=\beta n.

Finally, by (
21
),

\displaystyle|I|+|U_{1}|+|U_{2}|=\alpha(G)+|U|>\left(\frac{16}{3\sqrt{5}}-2\right)n+\frac{4}{3\sqrt{5}}n=\left(\frac{20}{3\sqrt{5}}-2\right)n=(1-\gamma)n.

So it follows from Lemma
5.1
that
\mathcal{H}
is
3
-partite. This completes the proof of Proposition
3.2
.

7
Concluding remarks

Compared to the rich history of research on the structure of dense triangle-free graphs
[
And62
,
AES74
,
ES73
,
Häg82
,
Jin93
,
CJK97
,
Tho02
,
Bra03
,
BT
,
Łuc06
,
ABG+13
,
ŁPR21
,
ŁPR22
]
, our results (Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
) on generalized triangles represent only the beginning of a broader investigation into the structures of dense
F_{5}
-free
3
-graphs.
There are many natural questions one could ask in this direction, such as extensions of parallel results on triangle-free graphs to
F_{5}
-free
3
-graphs. We hope our results could inspire further research in this area.
It is worth mentioning that some bounds for the chromatic threshold problem of
F_{5}
-free
3
-graphs have been established by Balogh–Butterfield–Hu–Lenz–Mubayi in
[
BBH+16
, Theorem 2.7]
.

One could also consider extending Theorem
1.1
to other hypergraphs. A partial list of hypergraphs with Andrásfai–Erdős–Sós-type stability is provided in
[
HLZ24
]
.
A natural direction is to extend Theorem
1.1
to
4
-graphs, and we refer the reader to
[
Sid87
,
Pik08
]
for results on its Turán number.
An interesting observation by Pikhurko
[
Pik08
]
is that the corresponding Andrásfai–Erdős–Sós theorem does not hold for
5
-uniform and
6
-uniform generalized triangles (see
[
FF89
,
NY17
]
for results on their Turán numbers). For
r\geq 7
, even determining their Turán densities remains an open question.

Recall that a key ingredient in the proof of Theorem
1.1
is establishing an Andrásfai–Erdős–Sós theorem for
3
-graphs whose shadow does not contain
K_{4}
(Proposition
3.2

A natural extension of this proposition is to replace

Permutations and pattern avoidance

We use the notation

2.2.
Symmetric and quasisymmetric functions

Let
\mathbf{x}=\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots\}
, and let
\mathbb{Z}[[\mathbf{x}]]
denotes the ring of formal power series in the variables
x_{1},x_{2},\dots
with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}
. An element
f\in\mathbb{Z}[[\mathbf{x}]]
is said to be symmetric if it is invariant under swapping any two variables. Let
\Sym
denote the ring of symmetric functions; i.e. symmetric elements
f\in\mathbb{Z}[[\bf x]]
with bounded degree.

Definition 2.9
.

A partition
\lambda
of
n
is a weakly decreasing sequence of positive integers
\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{k})
that adds up to
n
, i.e.
\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{k}=n
. If
\lambda
is a partition of
n
we write
\lambda\vdash n
or
\left\lvert\lambda\right\rvert=n
.

A partition
\lambda
has an associated Young diagram, which has
\lambda_{i}
boxes on row
i
for
1\leq i\leq k
. We will draw Young diagrams using English notation, e.g. the first row is on top, and the boxes in each row are left-justified. We will usually identify a partition with its diagram. The conjugate partition of
\lambda
, denoted
\lambda^{t}
, is the partition whose Young diagram is obtained by flipping the Young diagram of
\lambda
along the main diagonal (and thus interchanging rows and columns). A semistandard Young tableau (SSYT) of shape
\lambda
is a filling of the boxes of
\lambda
such that numbers in the same row are weakly increasing and numbers in the same column are strictly increasing. If
T
has shape
\lambda
we write
\sh(T)=\lambda
. A standard Young tableau (SYT) of shape
\lambda
is a SSYT of shape
\lambda
such that each number from
1
to
|\lambda|
appears exactly once. Let
\SSYT(\lambda)
and
\SYT(\lambda)
denotes the set of SSYT and SYT of shape
\lambda
, respectively.

Definition 2.10
.

For each
\lambda
, let
s_{\lambda}
denote the corresponding Schur function, that is

(2.2)

s_{\lambda}:=\sum\limits_{T\in\SSYT(\lambda)}x^{T}.

The Schur functions form a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis for
\Sym
as a
\mathbb{Z}
-module. A symmetric function
f
is said to be
Schur-positive
if its expansion in the Schur basis has only nonnegative coefficients.

Quasisymmetric functions are a generalization of symmetric functions. We say
f\in\mathbb{Z}[[\mathbf{x}]]
is quasisymmetric if for all
i_{1}<\dots<i_{k}
, all
j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}
, and all
a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k}>0
, the coefficient of
x_{i_{1}}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{k}}^{a_{k}}
in
f
is the same as the coefficient of
x_{j_{1}}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{j_{k}}^{a_{k}}
. Let
\QSym
denotes the ring of quasisymmetric functions; i.e. quasisymmetric elements
f\in\mathbb{Z}[[\mathbf{x}]]
with bounded degree.

Definition 2.11
.

A composition
\alpha
of
n
is a sequence of positive integers
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k})
such that
\alpha_{1}+\dots+\alpha_{k}=n
. If
\alpha
is a composition of
n
we write
\alpha\vDash n
or
\lvert\alpha\rvert=n
.

There is an explicit bijection between compositions of
n
and subsets of
[n-1]
, given by

(2.3)

(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k})\mapsto\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\dots+\alpha_{k-1}\}

The subset of
[n-1]
corresponding to
\alpha
will be denoted
S_{\alpha}
. Given
S\subseteq[n-1]
, the corresponding composition of
n
will be denoted
\alpha_{S}
.

Definition 2.12
.

For each subset
S\subseteq[n-1]
, let
F_{n,S}
denote the corresponding fundamental quasisymmetric function:

(2.4)

F_{n,S}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}\leq\dots\leq i_{n}\\
i_{j}<i_{j+1}\text{ if }j\in S\end{subarray}}x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{n}}.

We may omit the
n
and write
F_{S}
if the
n
is clear from context. The fundamental quasisymmetric functions form a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis for
\QSym
as a
\mathbb{Z}
-module. Since the Schur functions are symmetric, they are quasisymmetric, and thus can be expanded in this basis. To explain exactly how they expand, we need the following definition.

Definition 2.13
.

Given a SYT
Q
, define its descent set to be
\Des(Q):=\{i\mid i+1
is on a lower row than
i\text{ in }Q\}
.

Theorem 2.14

(
[

, Theorem 7.19.7]
)
.

We have

(2.5)

s_{\lambda}=\sum_{Q\in\SYT(\lambda)}F_{\Des(Q)}.

2.3.
Symmetric and Schur-positive sets

Definition 2.15
.

Given an injective function
w\colon[n]\to\mathbb{R}
(for example, a permutation in
S_{n}
), we define its descent set

(2.6)

\Des(w):=\{i\mid w(i)>w(i+1)\}.

Definition 2.16
.

Given a set
S\subseteq S_{n}
, we define its quasisymmetric generating function

(2.7)

Q(S):=\sum_{w\in S}F_{\Des(w)}.

Definition 2.17
.

A set
S\subseteq S_{n}
is said to be
symmetric
if
Q(S)
is a symmetric function. It is said to be
Schur-positive
if
Q(S)
is a symmetric function that is Schur-positive.

Remark 2.18
.

There is another more common definition of symmetric subsets of a group as follows. If
S\subseteq G
where
G
is a group, then
S
is said to be symmetric if
s^{-1}\in S
for all
s\in S
. This is
not
the definition used here.

Proposition 2.19
.

Let
A,B
be subsets of
S_{n}
.

•

Suppose that
A\cap B=\varnothing
. If
A
and
B
are symmetric, then
A\cup B
is symmetric. If
A
and
B
are Schur-positive, then
A\cup B
is Schur-positive.

•

If
A\supseteq B
and
A
and
B
are symmetric, then
A\setminus B
is symmetric. In particular,
S_{n}\setminus B
is symmetric if
B
is symmetric.

Proof.

The first claim follows from the fact that
Q(A\cup B)=Q(A)+Q(B)
for
A\cap B=\varnothing
and that symmetric and Schur-positive functions are closed under addition. The second claim follows from the fact that
Q(A\setminus B)=Q(A)-Q(B)
if
A\supseteq B
and that symmetric functions are closed under subtraction. The claim about
S_{n}\setminus B
being symmetric follows from the fact that
S_{n}
is symmetric (see, e.g.
[
7
, Theorem 1.2]
).
∎

Definition 2.20
.

A pattern set
\Pi\subseteq\bigcup\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}S_{k}

. It is said to be

Definition 2.21
.

Given

, its complement

is defined as follows:
w^{c}(i)=n+1-w(i)
. Its reversal

Note that taking complement and reversal are both involutions. Given a set

S^{c}:=\{w^{c}:w\in S\}
and
S^{r}:=\{w^{r}:w\in S\}
.

(
[

, Proposition 2.1 and 2.4]
)
.

If
S_{n}(\Pi)
is symmetric
(

)
, then

and

are symmetric
(
respectively, Schur-positive
)
.

The following corollary is implicitly proved in the proof of Proposition

]

If

is symmetric
(

and

are symmetric

Let

If

3.
Harmonic set systems

Definition 3.1
.

A
set system

H
is a pair
H=(U,\mathcal{F}),
where
U
is a set and
\mathcal{F}=(A_{1},\dots,A_{m})
is an ordered tuple of subsets of
U
. We call
U
the universe set of
H
.

Definition 3.2
.

Using the idea of Marmor
[
8
]
, given a set of permutations
S\subseteq S_{n}
, we can define an associated set system
\left(S,\left(A_{1},\dots,A_{n-1}\right)\right)
as follows.

(3.1)

A_{i}:=\{\pi\in S\mid i\in\Des(\pi)\}=\{\pi\in S\mid\pi(i)>\pi(i+1)\}

We will denote this associated set system
A(S)
.

Remark 3.3
.

Note that this slightly differs from the set system defined by Marmor
[
8
, Lemma 4.3]
. The elements of the sets defined here are the permutations themselves, while in
[
8
]
the elements are indices instead (and thus rely on a choice of indexing the permutations in
S
.) Furthermore, we define
A_{i}
to be the permutations that have a descent at
i
, rather than an ascent at
i
which is what was done in
[
8
]
. Thus, our set system will be isomorphic to the complement of the one defined in
[
8
]
(we will define “isomorphic” and “complement” below). In view of
2.23
,
3.7
, and
3.23
, this is simply a matter of convention.

Definition 3.4

(Isomorphic set systems)
.

Two set systems
H_{1}=(U,(A_{1},\dots,A_{m}))
and

H_{2}=(V,(B_{1},\dots,B_{m}))
are said to be
isomorphic
if there exists a bijection
\sigma\colon U\to V

such that
\sigma(A_{i})=B_{i}
for all
i
. We write
H_{1}\cong H_{2}
to denote that
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
are isomorphic.

Remark 3.5
.

Note that we allow reordering of the elements, but not the sets. This is because the ordering of sets matters for Definition
3.13
below.

Definition 3.6

(Complement set systems)
.

Given a set system
H=(U,\mathcal{F})
with
\mathcal{F}=(A_{1},\dots,A_{m})
, we define the complement of

to be
\overline{H}=(U,\overline{\mathcal{F}})
where

(3.2)

\overline{\mathcal{F}}:=\left(\overline{A_{1}},\dots,\overline{A_{m}}\right),

where
\overline{A_{i}}:=U\setminus A_{i}
.

The following Proposition shows that this definition of complement behaves well with taking complement of permutation as in Definition
2.21
.

Proposition 3.7
.

We have

(3.3)

\overline{A(S)}\cong A(S^{c}).

Proof.

Let
A(S)=(A_{1},\dots,A_{n-1})
, and
A(S^{c})=(B_{1},\dots,B_{n-1})
. It suffices to show that
(S\setminus A_{i})^{c}=B_{i}
. Note that we have

\displaystyle(S\setminus A_{i})^{c}

Lemma 3.22

4.
Possible sizes of symmetric sets

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
which classify the possible sizes of a symmetric set without monotone elements.

Remark 4.1
.

Any subset of
\{\iota_{n},\delta_{n}\}
is symmetric, so by Proposition
2.19
,
S\subseteq S_{n}
is symmetric if and only if
S\setminus\{\iota_{n},\delta_{n}\}
is symmetric. Therefore, it suffices to ask for the possible sizes of a symmetric set without monotone elements, rather than asking for the possible sizes of a symmetric set.

Remark 4.2
.

If
S\subseteq S_{n}
is a symmetric set without monotone elements, then by Proposition
2.19
,
S_{n}\setminus(S\cup\{\iota_{n},\delta_{n}\})
is a symmetric set without monotone elements. Therefore, to completely classify all the possible sizes of symmetric sets without monotone elements, it suffices to classify all the possible sizes up to
\frac{n!-2}{2}.

We start by discussing
Knuth-closed sets
, which are special examples of Schur-positive (and thus symmetric) sets.

Definition 4.3
.

For each SYT
P
of size
n
, the Knuth class corresponding to

, is the set of permutation

such that

under the Robinson–Schensted correspondence for some SYT

Theorem 4.4

(
[
5
, Theorem 5.5]
)
.

Knuth classes are Schur-positive
(
and thus symmetric
)

Proof.

Consider the Knuth class of a SYT
P
of size
n
and let
\lambda=\sh(P)
. Given a permutation

, write
(P(w),Q(w))
for its image under the Robinson–Schensted correspondence. Recall from Proposition

that

(4.1)

\sum_{w\in K(P)}F_{\Des(w)}=\sum_{w\in K(P)}F_{\Des(Q(w))}=\sum_{Q\in\SYT(\lambda)}F_{\Des(Q)}=s_{\lambda},

where the last equality is by Theorem

.
∎

We say a set is
Knuth-closed
if it is a union of Knuth classes. By Proposition
2.19
and Theorem

, Knuth-closed sets are Schur-positive (and thus symmetric.) Given
\lambda\vdash n,
let

denotes the number of SYT of shape

Definition 4.5

Given

The following proposition gives a good control on the possible sizes of Knuth-closed sets.

Proposition 4.6

There exists a Knuth-closed set of size

without monotone elements if and only if

(4.2)

p\in\sum_{\lambda\vdash n,\lambda\neq(1^{n}),\lambda\neq(n)}f^{\lambda}\{0,f^{\lambda}\}

Proof.

For each
\lambda\vdash n
there are
f^{\lambda}
possible

, each of which corresponds to a Knuth class of size

since there are
f^{\lambda}
choices of

The two partitions

and

Our next goal is to show that the sumset on the right-hand side of (

Theorem 4.7

(Hook length formula)
.

For
\lambda\vdash n
we have

(4.3)

f^{\lambda}=\frac{n!}{\prod\limits_{(i,j)\in\lambda}h(i,j)}

where
h(i,j)=(\lambda_{i}-i)+(\lambda^{t}_{j}-j)+1
is the hook length of the cell
(i,j).

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
\lambda\vdash n
and let
\mu
be a partition obtained by moving a box of
\lambda
to the first row. Then
f^{\mu}\geq\frac{f^{\lambda}}{n-1}.

Proof.

Note that
f^{\mu}\geq f^{\lambda\cap\mu}
since given an SYT of shape
\lambda\cap\mu
we can fill
\mu\setminus\lambda\cap\mu
with
n
to obtain an SYT of
\mu.
Note that
(n-1)f^{\lambda\cap\mu}\geq f^{\lambda}
since
\lambda\setminus\lambda\cap\mu
must be filled with one of the
n-1
values
2,\dots,n
and the ordering of the remaining numbers is determined by an SYT of shape
\lambda\cap\mu.
Thus
f^{\mu}\geq\frac{f^{\lambda}}{n-1}.

∎

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
\lambda\vdash n
such that
f^{\lambda}>1.
Then there exists
\mu\vdash n
such that
f^{\lambda}>f^{\mu}\geq\frac{f^{\lambda}}{n-1}.

Proof.

We repeatedly move one box of
\lambda

∎

\displaystyle-2-\left(\frac{n(n-3)}{2}\right)^{2}-\left(\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2}\right)^{2}-(n-1)^{2}

\displaystyle=n!-\frac{n^{3}-5n^{2}+7n+3}{2}=n!-\frac{n(n-1)(n-2)-(2n+1)(n-3)}{2}\geq\frac{n!}{2}.

By Proposition

∎

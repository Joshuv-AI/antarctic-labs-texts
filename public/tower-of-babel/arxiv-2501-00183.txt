Abstract
. This article is devoted to exploring the Lipschitz truncation method for parabolic multi-phase problems. The method is based on Whitney decomposition and covering lemmas with a delicate comparison scheme of appropriate alternatives to distinguish phases, as introduced by the first and second authors in [ 24 ] .

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we prove energy estimates for weak solutions to parabolic multi-phase problems of type

\displaystyle u_{t}-\operatorname{div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u+a(z)|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u+b(z)|\nabla u|^{s-2}\nabla u)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=-\operatorname{div}(|F|^{p-2}F+a(z)|F|^{q-2}F+b(z)|F|^{s-2}F)\quad\text{in }\Omega_{T},

where
\Omega_{T}:=\Omega\times(0,T)
represents a space-time cylinder with a bounded open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
n\geq 2
,
2\leq p<q<s<\infty
and the modulating coefficients
a(\cdot)
and
b(\cdot)
are nonnegative and Hölder continuous.

Energy estimates are very important for proving the existence and regularity results for partial differential equations. We prove the energy estimates using the Lipschitz truncation method for parabolic multi-phase problems. Here, the Lipschitz truncation is a method of redefining a given function so that it keeps its values on a specific ‘good set’, while redefining it in ‘bad sets’ using a partition of unity related to a Whitney covering argument. Acerbi-Fusco
[
1
,
2
]
have introduced the Lipschitz truncation for elliptic problems. Furthermore, Kinnunen-Lewis
[
30
]
and Kim-Kinnunen-Särkiö
[
28
]
have studied related methods for the parabolic
p
-Laplace system and for the parabolic double phase systems, respectively.

In this paper, we deal with a parabolic multi-phase system

(1.1)

u_{t}-\operatorname*{div}\mathcal{A}(z,u,\nabla u)=-\operatorname*{div}\mathcal{B}(z,F)\quad\text{in $\Omega_{T}$,}

where Carathéodory vector fields
\mathcal{A}:\Omega_{T}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{Nn}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{Nn}
and
\mathcal{B}:\Omega_{T}\times\mathbb{R}^{Nn}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{Nn}
satisfy the following growth conditions: for any
z\in\Omega_{T},\;v\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{Nn}
, there exist two constants
0<\nu\leq L<\infty
such that

(1.2)

\nu H(z,|\xi|)\leq\mathcal{A}(z,v,\xi)\cdot\xi,\quad|\mathcal{A}(z,v,\xi)||\xi|\leq LH(z,|\xi|)

and

(1.3)

|\mathcal{B}(z,\xi)||\xi|\leq LH(z,|\xi|),

where the function
H:\Omega_{T}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
is defined by

H(z,\kappa)=\kappa^{p}+a(z)\kappa^{q}+b(z)\kappa^{s}

for
z\in\Omega_{T}
and
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Furthermore, the source term
F:\Omega_{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{Nn}
satisfies

(1.4)

\iint_{\Omega_{T}}H(z,|F|)\,dz<+\infty.

We assume that the modulating coefficients
a:\Omega_{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
b:\Omega_{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
satisfy

(1.5)

q\leq p+\frac{2\alpha}{n+2},\quad 0\leq a\in C^{\alpha,\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\Omega_{T})\quad\text{for some }\alpha\in(0,1],

and

(1.6)

s\leq p+\frac{2\beta}{n+2},\quad 0\leq b\in C^{\beta,\frac{\beta}{2}}(\Omega_{T})\quad\text{for some }\beta\in(0,1].

Here,
a\in C^{\alpha,\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\Omega_{T})
means that
a\in L^{\infty}(\Omega_{T})
and there exists a Hölder constant
[a]_{\alpha}:=[a]_{\alpha,\frac{\alpha}{2};\Omega_{T}}>0
such that

|a(x_{1},t_{1})-a(x_{2},t_{2})|\leq[a]_{\alpha}\left(|x_{1}-x_{2}|+\sqrt{|t_{1}-t_{2}|}\right)^{\alpha}

for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in\Omega
and
t_{1},t_{2}\in(0,T)
.

Definition 1.1
.

A function
u:\Omega\times(0,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
satisfying

\displaystyle u\in C(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N}))\cap L^{1}(0,T;W^{1,1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N}))

and

\iint_{\Omega_{T}}[H(z,|u|)+H(z,|\nabla u|)]\,dz<\infty

is a weak solution to (
1.1

\iint_{\Omega_{T}}[-u\cdot\varphi_{t}+\mathcal{A}(z,u,\nabla u)\cdot\nabla\varphi]\,dz=\iint_{\Omega_{T}}[\mathcal{B}(z,F)\cdot\nabla\varphi]\,dz

for every
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega_{T},\mathbb{R}^{N})

2.
Whitney decomposition and covering lemmas

In this section, we construct a family of Whitney decomposition and show that the family covers the bad set

via a Vitali covering argument. The construction of such a decomposition is heavily used in the subsequent sections.

2.2.
Some preliminary estimates

We start with some basic estimates.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
z\in E(\Lambda)^{c}
. Assume
K^{2}\lambda^{p}_{z}<a(z)\lambda^{q}_{z}
and
K^{2}\lambda^{p}_{z}<b(z)\lambda^{s}_{z}.
Then
\frac{a(z)}{2}\leq a(\tilde{z})\leq 2a(z)
and
\frac{b(z)}{2}\leq b(\tilde{z})\leq 2b(z)
for any
\tilde{z}\in 200KQ_{r_{z}}(z)
.

Proof.

We claim that
2[a]_{\alpha}(200Kr_{z})^{\alpha}<a(z)
and
2[b]_{\beta}(200Kr_{z})^{\beta}<b(z).
We will only prove the second statement, and the proof of the first statement is similar. On the contrary, let us assume
b(z)\leq 2[b]_{\beta}(200Kr_{z})^{\beta}.
Since
Q^{q,s}_{r_{z},\lambda_{z}}(z)\subset E(\Lambda)^{c}
and
a(z)\lambda^{q}_{z}+b(z)\lambda^{s}_{z}<\Lambda
, we get

\displaystyle a(z)\lambda^{q}_{z}+b(z)\lambda^{s}_{z}<{{{\int\hskip-7.96674pt\int}\hskip-15.93347pt-\hskip-4.26773pt-}_{\hskip-3.69899ptQ^{q,s}_{r_{z},\lambda_{z}}(z)}}\left(M\left(f^{d}+(af^{q})^{d/p}+(bf^{s})^{d/p}\right)(w)\right)^{p/d}\,dw.

Now using
\Lambda=\lambda^{p}_{z}+a(z)\lambda^{q}_{z}+b(z)\lambda^{s}_{z}<2[a(z)\lambda^{q}_{z}+b(z)\lambda^{s}_{z}]
, we obtain

\displaystyle a(z)\lambda^{q}_{z}+b(z)\lambda^{s}_{z}<{{{\int\hskip-7.96674pt\int}\hskip-15.93347pt-\hskip-4.26773pt-}_{\hskip-3.69899ptQ^{q,s}_{r_{z},\lambda_{z}}(z)}}\left(M\left(f^{d}+(af^{q})^{d/p}+(bf^{s})^{d/p}\right)(w)\right)^{p/d}\,dw

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\frac{\Lambda\lambda^{-2}_{z}}{2|B_{1}|r^{n+2}_{z}}\iint_{Q^{q,s}_{r_{z},\lambda_{z}}(z)}\left(M\left(f^{d}+(af^{q})^{d/p}+(bf^{s})^{d/p}\right)(w)\right)^{p/d}\,dw

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad<\frac{(a(z)\lambda^{q}_{z}+b(z)\lambda^{s}_{z})\lambda^{-2}_{z}}{|B_{1}|r^{n+2}_{z}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\iint_{Q^{q,s}_{r_{z},\lambda_{z}}(z)}\left(M\left(f^{d}+(af^{q})^{d/p}+(bf^{s})^{d/p}\right)(w)\right)^{p/d}\,dw.

Raising the power to
\frac{\beta}{n+2}
in both of sides to the above expression, we have

\displaystyle r^{\beta}_{z}\lambda^{\frac{2\beta}{n+2}}_{z}

\displaystyle<\left(\frac{1}{|B_{1}|}\iint_{Q^{q,s}_{r_{z},\lambda_{z}}(z)}\left(M\left(f^{d}+(af^{q})^{d/p}+(bf^{s})^{d/p}\right)(w)\right)^{p/d}\,dw\right)^{\frac{\beta}{n+2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{K}{800[b]_{\beta}}

Now using
K^{2}\lambda^{p}_{z}<b(z)\lambda^{s}_{z},

s\leq p+\frac{2\beta}{n+2}
and the counter assumption, we get

\displaystyle K^{2}\lambda^{p}_{z}<b(z)\lambda^{s}_{z}\leq 2[b]_{\beta}(200Kr_{z})^{\beta}\lambda^{p}_{z}\lambda^{\frac{2\beta}{n+2}}_{z}\leq\frac{1}{2}K^{2}\lambda^{p}_{z},

which is a contradiction. This completes the proof of
2[b]_{\beta}(200Kr_{z})^{\beta}<b(z).
Now using the Hölder continuity of
b(z),
we get

\displaystyle 2[b]_{\beta}(200Kr_{z})^{\beta}<b(z)\leq\inf_{\tilde{z}\in 200KQ_{r_{z}}(z)}b(\tilde{z})+[b]_{\beta}(200Kr_{z})^{\beta},

and hence

\displaystyle[b]_{\beta}(200Kr_{z})^{\beta}\leq\inf_{\tilde{z}\in 200KQ_{r_{z}}(z)}b(\tilde{z}).

It follows that

\displaystyle\sup_{\tilde{z}\in 200KQ_{r_{z}}(z)}b(\tilde{z})\leq\inf_{\tilde{z}\in 200KQ_{r_{z}}(z)}b(\tilde{z})+[b]_{\beta}(200Kr_{z})^{\beta}<2\inf_{\tilde{z}\in 200KQ_{r_{z}}(z)}b(\tilde{z})

and this proves
\frac{b(z)}{2}\leq b(\tilde{z})\leq 2b(z)
for any
\tilde{z}\in 200KQ_{r_{z}}(z).
The statement for
a(\cdot)
can be proved similarly.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
z\in E(\Lambda)^{c}
. Assume
K^{2}\lambda^{p}_{z}\geq a(z)\lambda^{q}_{z}
and
K^{2}\lambda^{p}_{z}<b(z)\lambda^{s}_{z}.
Then
\frac{b(z)}{2}\leq b(\tilde{z})\leq 2b(z)
for any
\tilde{z}\in 200KQ_{r_{z}}(z).
Moreover, we have

(2.11)

[a]_{\alpha}(800Kr_{z})^{\alpha}\lambda^{q}_{z}\leq(K^{2}-1)\lambda^{p}_{z}.

Proof.

First, we note that
Q^{s}_{r_{z},\lambda_{z}}(z)\subset E(\Lambda)^{c},
and therefore

\displaystyle g_{s}(z,\lambda_{z})<{{{\int\hskip-7.96674pt\int}\hskip-15.93347pt-\hskip-4.26773pt-}_{\hskip-3.69899ptQ^{s}_{r_{z},\lambda_{z}}(z)}}\left(M\left(f^{d}+(af^{q})^{d/p}+(bf^{s})^{d/p}\right)(w)\right)^{p/d}\,dw

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{g_{s}(z,\lambda_{z})\lambda^{-2}_{z}}{2|B_{1}|r^{n+2}_{z}}\iint_{Q^{s}_{r_{z},\lambda_{z}}(z)}\left(M\left(f^{d}+(af^{q})^{d/p}+(bf^{s})^{d/p}\right)(w)\right)^{p/d}\,dw.

Raising power
\frac{\alpha}{n+2}
in the above expression, we have

\displaystyle r^{\alpha}_{z}\lambda^{\frac{2\alpha}{n+2}}_{z}

\displaystyle<\left(\frac{1}{|B_{1}|}\iint_{Q^{s}_{r_{z},\lambda_{z}}(z)}\left(M\left(f^{d}+(af^{q})^{d/p}+(bf^{s})^{d/p}\right)(w)\right)^{p/d}\,dw\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{n+2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{K-1}{800[a]_{\alpha}}.

Using
q\leq p+\frac{2\alpha}{n+2},
we get

\displaystyle[a]_{\alpha}(800Kr_{z})^{\alpha}\lambda^{q}_{z}\leq[a]_{\alpha}800Kr^{\alpha}_{z}\lambda^{p}_{z}\lambda^{\frac{2\alpha}{n+2}}_{z}

\displaystyle\leq[a]_{\alpha}800K\lambda^{p}_{z}\frac{K-1}{800[a]_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq(K^{2}-1)\lambda^{p}_{z}.

Moreover, the proof of the first statement follows from the previous lemma.
This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
z\in E(\Lambda)^{c}
. Assume
K^{2}\lambda^{p}_{z}<a(z)\lambda^{q}_{z}
and
K^{2}\lambda^{p}_{z}\geq b(z)\lambda^{s}_{z}.
Then
\frac{a(z)}{2}\leq a(\tilde{z})\leq 2a(z)
for any
\tilde{z}\in 200KQ_{r_{z}}(z).
Furthermore, we have

(2.12)

[b]_{\beta}(800Kr_{z})^{\beta}\lambda^{s}_{z}\leq(K^{2}-1)\lambda^{p}_{z}.

Proof.

Since
Q^{q}_{r_{z},\lambda_{z}}\subset E(\Lambda)^{c},
we have

\displaystyle g_{q}(z,\lambda_{z})<{{{\int\hskip-7.96674pt\int}\hskip-15.93347pt-\hskip-4.26773pt-}_{\hskip-3.69899ptQ^{s}_{r_{z},\lambda_{z}}(z)}}\left(M\left(f^{d}+(af^{q})^{d/p}+(bf^{s})^{d/p}\right)(w)\right)^{p/d}\,dw

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{g_{q}(z,\lambda_{z})\lambda^{-2}_{z}}{2|B_{1}|r^{n+2}_{z}}\iint_{Q^{s}_{r_{z},\lambda_{z}}(z)}\left(M\left(f^{d}+(af^{q})^{d/p}+(bf^{s})^{d/p}\right)(w)\right)^{p/d}\,dw.

Moreover, following the previous lemma, we get

\displaystyle[b]_{\beta}(800Kr_{z})^{\beta}\lambda^{s}_{z}\leq(K^{2}-1)\lambda^{p}_{z}.

Also, the first statement follows from Lemma
2.2
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
z,\tilde{z}\in E(\Lambda)^{c}
. Assume
K^{2}\lambda^{p}_{z}\geq a(z)\lambda^{q}_{z}
and
K^{2}\lambda^{p}_{z}<b(z)\lambda^{s}_{z},
and
\tilde{z}\in 200KQ_{r_{z}}(z)
. Then
K^{-\frac{2}{p}}\lambda_{\tilde{z}}\leq\lambda_{z}\leq K^{\frac{2}{p}}\lambda_{\tilde{z}}
.

Proof.

From Lemma
2.2
, we get

(2.13)

\displaystyle\frac{b(z)}{2}\leq b(\tilde{z})\leq 2b(z).

Now, note that it is enough to prove
K^{-\frac{2}{p}}\lambda_{\tilde{z}}\leq\lambda_{z}.
On contrary, let us assume
\lambda_{z}<K^{-\frac{2}{p}}\lambda_{\tilde{z}}.
Then using (
2.11
), (
2.13
) and the counter assumption, we obtain

\displaystyle\Lambda

\displaystyle=\lambda_{z}^{p}+a(z)\lambda_{z}^{q}+b(z)\lambda_{z}^{s}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{p}_{z}+[a]_{\alpha}(200Kr_{z})^{\alpha}\lambda^{q}_{z}+a(\tilde{z})\lambda^{q}_{z}+b(z)\lambda^{s}_{z}

\displaystyle<\lambda^{p}_{z}+(K^{2}-1)\lambda^{p}_{z}+a(\tilde{z})K^{-\frac{2q}{p}}\lambda^{q}_{\tilde{z}}+2b(\tilde{z})K^{-\frac{2s}{p}}\lambda^{s}_{\tilde{z}}

\displaystyle\leq K^{2}\lambda^{p}_{z}+a(\tilde{z})K^{-\frac{2q}{p}}\lambda^{q}_{\tilde{z}}+b(\tilde{z})K^{1-\frac{2s}{p}}\lambda^{s}_{\tilde{z}}

\displaystyle<\lambda_{\tilde{z}}^{p}+a(\tilde{z})\lambda_{\tilde{z}}^{q}+b(\tilde{z})\lambda_{\tilde{z}}^{s}=\Lambda,

which is a contradiction.
∎

3.1.

Take
f=\chi_{U_{R_{2},S_{2}}(z_{0})}\left(|\nabla u|+|u-u_{0}|+|F|\right)\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
and

are extended to zero outside

Let
0<h_{0}<\frac{S_{2}-S_{1}}{4}
be a sufficiently small number, and let
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B_{R_{2}}(x_{0}))
and
\zeta\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\ell_{S_{2}-h_{0}}(t_{0}))
be standard cutoff functions satisfying
0\leq\eta\leq 1
,
0\leq\zeta\leq 1
,
\eta\equiv 1
in
B_{R_{1}}(x_{0})
,
\zeta\equiv 1
in

and

(3.1)

Now for

we define the truncated solution

(3.2)

\displaystyle v_{h}(z)=[u(z)-u_{0}]_{h}\eta(x)\zeta(t),\quad z=(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1},

as a suitable candidate for test function to be used to prove energy estimates. For

we define the Lipschitz truncation of

\displaystyle v^{i}_{h}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{{{\int\hskip-7.96674pt\int}\hskip-15.93347pt-\hskip-4.26773pt-}_{\hskip-3.69899pt\frac{2}{K}U_{i}}}v_{h}(z)dz,\,\,\,\,\,&\text{if}\,\,\,\frac{2}{K}U_{i}\subset U_{R_{2},S_{2}}(z_{0}),\\
\displaystyle 0,&\text{elsewhere}.\end{cases}

Lemma 3.8

z_{2}=(x_{2},t_{2})
. Since
v_{h}^{\Lambda}\in C^{\infty}(E(\Lambda)^{c},\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
Q_{l,\lambda}(w)\subset E(\Lambda)^{c}
, by the intermediate value theorem, (
3.31
) and (
3.39
), we have

\displaystyle|v_{h}^{\Lambda}(z_{1})-v_{h}^{\Lambda}(z_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq|v_{h}^{\Lambda}(x_{1},t_{1})-v_{h}^{\Lambda}(x_{2},t_{1})|+|v_{h}^{\Lambda}(x_{2},t_{1})-v_{h}^{\Lambda}(x_{2},t_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq cl\sup_{z\in Q_{l,\lambda}(\omega)}|\nabla v_{h}^{\Lambda}(z)|+c\lambda^{2-p}l^{2}\sup_{z\in Q_{l,\lambda}(\omega)}|\partial_{t}v_{h}^{\Lambda}(z)|\leq c(\operatorname{data},\Lambda)l.

Thus, we conclude

\displaystyle{{{\int\hskip-7.96674pt\int}\hskip-15.93347pt-\hskip-4.26773pt-}_{\hskip-3.69899ptQ_{l,\lambda}(w)}}\frac{|v^{\Lambda}_{h}-(v^{\Lambda}_{h})_{Q_{l,\lambda}(w)}|}{l}d\tilde{w}

\displaystyle\leq{{{\int\hskip-7.96674pt\int}\hskip-15.93347pt-\hskip-4.26773pt-}_{\hskip-3.69899ptQ_{l,\lambda}(w)}}{{{\int\hskip-7.96674pt\int}\hskip-15.93347pt-\hskip-4.26773pt-}_{\hskip-3.69899ptQ_{l,\lambda}(w)}}\frac{|v^{\Lambda}_{h}(z_{1})-v^{\Lambda}_{h}(z_{2})|}{l}dz_{2}dz_{1}

\displaystyle\leq c_{\Lambda}(\operatorname{data,\Lambda}).

Case 2:
2Q_{l,\lambda}(w)\cap E(\Lambda)\neq\emptyset
and
Q_{l,\lambda}(w)\cap E(\Lambda)^{c}=\emptyset
.
In this case
v_{h}^{\Lambda}=v_{h}
and hence

{{{\int\hskip-7.96674pt\int}\hskip-15.93347pt-\hskip-4.26773pt-}_{\hskip-3.69899ptQ_{l,\lambda}(\omega)}}\frac{|v_{h}^{\Lambda}-(v_{h}^{\Lambda})_{Q_{l,\lambda}(w)}|}{l}\,d\tilde{w}={{{\int\hskip-7.96674pt\int}\hskip-15.93347pt-\hskip-4.26773pt-}_{\hskip-3.69899ptQ_{l,\lambda}(\omega)}}\frac{|v_{h}-(v_{h})_{Q_{l,\lambda}(w)}|}{l}\,d\tilde{w}.

We denote
w=(y,s)
. If
B_{l}(y)\subset B_{R_{2}}(x_{0})
, then
l\leq R_{2}
is satisfied and Lemma
3.2
and Lemma
3.3
imply that

{{{\int\hskip-7.96674pt\int}\hskip-15.93347pt-\hskip-4.26773pt-}_{\hskip-3.69899ptQ_{l,\lambda}(w)}}\frac{|v_{h}-(v_{h})_{Q_{l,\lambda}(w)}|}{l}\,d\tilde{w}\leq c(\operatorname{data},\Lambda)(\lambda^{2-p}+\lambda^{2-p}l+1)\leq c(\operatorname{data},\Lambda).

On the other hand, if
B_{l}(y)\not\subset B_{R_{2}}(x_{0})
, we apply Lemma
3.4
with
\sigma=1
and
\eta=d
to get

{{{\int\hskip-7.96674pt\int}\hskip-15.93347pt-\hskip-4.26773pt-}_{\hskip-3.69899ptQ_{l,\lambda}(w)}}\frac{|v_{h}-(v_{h})_{Q_{l,\lambda}(w)}|}{l}\,d\tilde{w}\leq 2{{{\int\hskip-7.96674pt\int}\hskip-15.93347pt-\hskip-4.26773pt-}_{\hskip-3.69899ptQ_{l,\lambda}(w)}}\frac{|v_{h}|}{l}\,d\tilde{w}\leq c{{{\int\hskip-7.96674pt\int}\hskip-15.93347pt-\hskip-4.26773pt-}_{\hskip-3.69899pt4Q_{l,\lambda}(w)}}|\nabla v_{h}|\,d\tilde{w}.

Recalling that in this case
2Q_{l,\lambda}(w)\cap E(\Lambda)\neq\emptyset
, we conclude with Lemma
3.2
that

{{{\int\hskip-7.96674pt\int}\hskip-15.93347pt-\hskip-4.26773pt-}_{\hskip-3.69899pt4Q_{l,\lambda}(w)}}|\nabla v_{h}|\,d\tilde{w}\leq c{{{\int\hskip-7.96674pt\int}\hskip-15.93347pt-\hskip-4.26773pt-}_{\hskip-3.69899pt[4Q_{l,\lambda}(w)]_{h}}}f\,d\tilde{w}\leq c(\operatorname{data},\Lambda).

Case 3:
2Q_{l,\lambda}(w)\cap E(\Lambda)\neq\emptyset
and
Q_{l,\lambda}\cap E(\Lambda)\neq\emptyset
.
We define the index set

P=\{i\in\mathbb{N}:Q_{l,\lambda}(w)\cap\frac{2}{K}U_{i}\neq\emptyset\}.

We want to show that the radii
r_{i}
are bounded uniformly by
l
with respect to
i\in P.
Let
i\in P
,
w_{1}\in Q_{l,\lambda}(w)\cap\frac{2}{K}U_{i}
and
w_{2}\in 2Q_{l,\lambda}(w)\cap E(\Lambda)
with
w_{1}=(y_{1},s_{1})
and
w_{2}=(y_{2},s_{2})
. By Lemma
2.18

(iii)
and
w_{1}\in\frac{2}{K}U_{i}
, we obtain

3r_{1}\leq d_{i}(U_{i},E(\Lambda))\leq d_{i}(z_{i},w_{2})\leq d_{i}(z_{i},w_{1})+d_{i}(w_{1},w_{2})\leq 2r_{i}+d_{i}(w_{1},w_{2}),

and hence
r_{i}\leq d_{i}(w_{1},w_{2})
. Moreover, since
\lambda\leq\lambda_{i}
and
w_{1},w_{2}\in 2Q_{l,\lambda}(w)
, we get

\displaystyle d_{i}(w_{1},w_{2})

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{|y_{1}-y_{2}|,\sqrt{\Lambda\lambda_{i}^{-2}|s_{1}-s_{2}|}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\max\left\{|y_{1}-y_{2}|,\sqrt{\lambda^{p-2}|s_{1}-s_{2}|}\right\}

\displaystyle=\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}d_{\lambda^{p}}(w_{1},w_{2})\leq 4\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}l.

Thus, we have
r_{i}\leq c(\Lambda)l
.

Note that

\displaystyle{{{\int\hskip-7.96674pt\int}\hskip-15.93347pt-\hskip-4.26773pt-}_{\hskip-3.69899ptQ_{l,\lambda}(w)}}\frac{|v^{\Lambda}_{h}-(v^{\Lambda}_{h})_{Q_{l,\lambda}(w)}|}{l}d\tilde{w}

\displaystyle\leq 2{{{\int\hskip-7.96674pt\int}\hskip-15.93347pt-\hskip-4.26773pt-}_{\hskip-3.69899ptQ_{l,\lambda}(w)}}\frac{|v^{\Lambda}_{h}-(v_{h})_{Q_{l,\lambda}(w)}|}{l}d\tilde{w}

\displaystyle\leq 2{{{\int\hskip-7.96674pt\int}\hskip-15.93347pt-\hskip-4.26773pt-}_{\hskip-3.69899ptQ_{l,\lambda}(w)}}\frac{|v^{\Lambda}_{h}-v_{h}|}{l}d\tilde{w}

\displaystyle+2{{{\int\hskip-7.96674pt\int}\hskip-15.93347pt-\hskip-4.26773pt-}_{\hskip-3.69899ptQ_{l,\lambda}(w)}}\frac{|v_{h}-(v_{h})_{Q_{l,\lambda}(w)}|}{l}d\tilde{w}.

As in Case 2, we can estimate the second term on the right-hand side. To estimate the first term, we obtain from the definition of
v_{h}^{\Lambda}
and the fact that
w_{i}
is supported in
\frac{2}{K}U_{i}
that

\displaystyle{{{\int\hskip-7.96674pt\int}\hskip-15.93347pt-\hskip-4.26773pt-}_{\hskip-3.69899ptQ_{l,\lambda}(w)}}\frac{|v^{\Lambda}_{h}-v_{h}|}{l}d\tilde{w}

\displaystyle={{{\int\hskip-7.96674pt\int}\hskip-15.93347pt-\hskip-4.26773pt-}_{\hskip-3.69899ptQ_{l,\lambda}(w)}}\frac{|\sum_{i\in P}(v_{h}-v_{h}^{i})w_{i}|}{l}d\tilde{w}

\displaystyle\leq{{{\int\hskip-7.96674pt\int}\hskip-15.93347pt-\hskip-4.26773pt-}_{\hskip-3.69899ptQ_{l,\lambda}(w)}}\sum_{i\in P}\frac{|v_{h}-v_{h}^{i}|w_{i}}{l}d\tilde{w}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|Q_{l,\lambda}(w)|}\sum_{i\in P}\iint_{Q_{i,\lambda}(w)\cap\frac{2}{K}U_{i}}\frac{|v_{h}-v_{h}^{i}|w_{i}}{l}d\tilde{w}.

Since
w_{i}\leq 1
and
r_{i}\leq c(\Lambda)l
, we have

\frac{1}{|Q_{l,\lambda}(w)|}\sum_{i\in P}\iint_{Q_{i,\lambda}(w)\cap\frac{2}{K}U_{i}}\frac{|v_{h}-v_{h}^{i}|w_{i}}{l}d\tilde{w}\leq\frac{c(\Lambda)}{|Q_{l,\lambda}(w)|}\sum_{i\in P}\iint_{\frac{2}{K}U_{i}}\frac{|v_{h}-v_{h}^{i}|}{r_{i}}d\tilde{w}.

Combining the previous inequalities with (
3.25
), we obtain

{{{\int\hskip-7.96674pt\int}\hskip-15.93347pt-\hskip-4.26773pt-}_{\hskip-3.69899ptQ_{l,\lambda}(w)}}\frac{|v^{\Lambda}_{h}-v_{h}|}{l}d\tilde{w}\leq\frac{c(\operatorname{data},\Lambda)}{|Q_{l,\lambda}(w)|}\sum_{i\in P}|U_{i}|.

Since
r_{i}\leq c(\Lambda)l
, we get
U_{i}\subset c(\operatorname{data},\Lambda)Q_{i,\Lambda}(w)
for every
i\in P
. By Lemma
2.18
(ii)
, we have that

{{{\int\hskip-7.96674pt\int}\hskip-15.93347pt-\hskip-4.26773pt-}_{\hskip-3.69899ptQ_{l,\lambda}(w)}}\frac{|v_{h}^{\Lambda}-v_{h}|}{l}\,d\tilde{w}\leq\frac{c(\operatorname{data},\Lambda)}{|Q_{l,\lambda}(w)|}\sum_{i\in P}\left|\frac{1}{6K^{6}}U_{i}\right|\leq c(\operatorname{data},\Lambda).

Thus, the proof is completed.
∎

Corollary 3.12
.

Let
E(\Lambda)
be defined in (
2.2
). Then
v_{h}
satisfies the estimate

\displaystyle H(z,|v_{h}^{\Lambda}(z)|)+H(z,|\nabla v_{h}^{\Lambda}(z)|)\leq c(\operatorname{data},\Lambda)\,\,\,\,\text{for\,\, a.e.}\,\,\,\,z\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}.

Proof.

Since
v^{\Lambda}_{h}(z)=0
in
U_{R_{2},S_{2}}(z_{0})^{c}
and
v^{\Lambda}_{h}
is Lipschitz continuous for almost every
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, we get that
|v^{\Lambda}_{h}(z)|\leq c(\operatorname{data},\Lambda)
and
|\nabla v^{\Lambda}_{h}(z)|\leq c(\operatorname{data},\Lambda)
for almost every
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}.
Then we have

\displaystyle|v^{\Lambda}_{h}(z)|^{p}+a(z)|v^{\Lambda}_{h}(z)|^{q}+b(z)|v^{\Lambda}_{h}(z)|^{s}+|\nabla v^{\Lambda}_{h}(z)|^{p}+a(z)|\nabla v^{\Lambda}_{h}(z)|^{q}+b(z)|\nabla v^{\Lambda}_{h}(z)|^{s}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq c(\operatorname{data},\Lambda)(1+||a||_{\infty}+||b||_{\infty}).

This completes the proof.
∎

3.6.
Some more properties of Lipschitz truncation

In the following proposition, we collect some more properties of Lipschitz truncation.

Proposition 3.13
.

Let
E(\Lambda)
be as in (
2.2
), and let
\eta,\zeta
be the cut-off functions. Then
\{v_{h}^{\Lambda}\}_{h>0}
and a function
v^{\Lambda}
satisfy the following properties:

(i)

v_{h}^{\Lambda}\in W_{0}^{1,2}(supp(\zeta);L^{2}(supp(\eta),\mathbb{R}^{N}))\cap L^{\infty}(supp(\zeta);W^{1,\infty}_{0}(supp(\eta),\mathbb{R}^{N}))
.

(ii)

v^{\Lambda}\in L^{\infty}(supp(\zeta)+h_{0};W^{1,\infty}_{0}(supp(\eta),\mathbb{R}^{N}))
.

(iii)

v_{h}^{\Lambda}=v_{h},

v^{\Lambda}=v
,
\nabla v_{h}^{\Lambda}=\nabla v_{h},

\nabla v^{\Lambda}=\nabla v
a.e. in
E(\Lambda)
.

(iv)

v_{h}^{\Lambda}\to v^{\Lambda}
in
L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})
as
h\to 0^{+}
, taking a subsequence if necessary.

(v)

\nabla v_{h}^{\Lambda}\to\nabla v^{\Lambda}
and
\partial_{t}v_{h}^{\Lambda}\to\partial_{t}v^{\Lambda}
a.e. in
E(\Lambda)^{c}
as
h\to 0^{+}
.

Proof.

To show
(i)
, we first note from Definition
1.1
and the definition of Steklov averages that
v_{h}\in W^{1,2}_{0}(supp(\zeta);L^{2}(supp(\eta),\mathbb{R}^{N}))
. Moreover, the definition of
v^{\Lambda}_{h}
in (
3.3
) only matters for finite sum since
|\mathcal{I}|\leq c
. Therefore, together with Lemma
3.11
, we complete the proof of
(i)
.

The proof of
(ii)
is obvious from Lemma
3.9

(iii)
. Indeed, we have
|v^{\Lambda}(z)|\leq c\Lambda^{1/p}
and
|\nabla v^{\Lambda}(z)|\leq c\Lambda^{1/p}
for all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}.

The proof of
(iii)
follows from the definitions (
3.3
) and (
3.4
). In fact, since
\omega_{i}(z)=0
for
z\in E(\Lambda),
we have the proof.

Since
v^{\Lambda}_{h}(z)=0
for
z\in U^{c}_{R_{2},S_{2}}(z_{0}),
using Lemma
3.11
we see that
\{v^{\Lambda}_{h}\}_{h>0}
is equicontinuous and uniformly bounded. By the properties of Steklov averages, we already have
v^{\Lambda}_{h}\to v^{\Lambda}
as
h\to 0+.
Hence, by Arzela-Ascoli theorem, we get
v^{\Lambda}_{h}\to v^{\Lambda}
in
L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N}).
This proves
(iv)
.

The proof of
(v)
follows from
v^{i}_{h}\to v^{i}
as
h\to 0
and the expressions computed in Lemma
3.8
.
∎

4.
Proof of energy estimate

In this section, we prove the energy estimate in Theorem
1.2
using the Lipschitz truncation
v^{\Lambda}_{h}
.

4.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

We closely follow the proof of
[
28
, Subsection 5.1]
. For
\tau\in\ell_{S_{2}-h}(t_{0})
and sufficiently small
\delta>0
, let

\zeta_{\delta}(t)=\begin{cases}1,&t\in(-\infty,\tau-\delta),\\
1-\frac{t-\tau+\delta}{\delta},&t\in[\tau-\delta,\tau],\\
0,&t\in(\tau,t_{0}+S_{2}-h).\end{cases}

Note that, by Proposition
3.13

(i)
,
v_{h}^{\Lambda}\eta^{s}\zeta\zeta_{\delta}(\cdot,t)\in W_{0}^{1,\infty}(B_{R_{2}}(x_{0}),\mathbb{R}^{N})
for every
t\in\ell_{S_{2}-h}(t_{0})
. Using this function as the test function of the Steklov averaged weak formulation in (
1.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}+\mathrm{II}

\displaystyle=\iint_{U_{R_{2},S_{2}}(z_{0})}\partial_{t}[u-u_{0}]_{h}\cdot v_{h}^{\Lambda}\eta^{s}\zeta\zeta_{\delta}\,dz

\displaystyle+\iint_{U_{R_{2},S_{2}}(z_{0})}[\mathcal{A}(z,\nabla u)]_{h}\cdot\nabla(v_{h}^{\Lambda}\eta^{s}\zeta\zeta_{\delta})\,dz

\displaystyle=\iint_{U_{R_{2},S_{2}}(z_{0})}[\mathcal{B}(z,F)]_{h}\cdot\nabla(v_{h}^{\Lambda}\eta^{s}\zeta\zeta_{\delta})\,dz=\mathrm{III}.

Now we estimate the each term above by dividing the integral domain into
E(\Lambda)
and
E(\Lambda)^{c}
.

Estimate of
\mathrm{I}
.
By integration by parts and the product rule, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}

\displaystyle=\iint_{U_{R_{2},S_{2}}(z_{0})}(-v_{h}\cdot v_{h}^{\Lambda}\eta^{s-1}\partial_{t}\zeta_{\delta}-v_{h}\cdot\partial_{t}v_{h}^{\Lambda}\eta^{s-1}\zeta_{\delta})\,dz

\displaystyle+\iint_{U_{R_{2},S_{2}}(z_{0})}-[u-u_{0}]_{h}\cdot v_{h}^{\Lambda}\eta^{s}\zeta_{\delta}\partial_{t}\zeta\,dz=\mathrm{I}_{1}+\mathrm{I}_{2}.

First, we consider
\mathrm{I}_{1}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}_{1}

\displaystyle=\iint_{U_{R_{2},S_{2}}(z_{0})}-|v_{h}|^{2}\eta^{s-1}\partial_{t}\zeta_{\delta}\,dz+\iint_{U_{R_{2},S_{2}}(z_{0})}v_{h}\cdot(v_{h}-v_{h}^{\Lambda})\eta^{s-1}\partial_{t}\zeta_{\delta}\,dz

\displaystyle-\iint_{U_{R_{2},S_{2}}(z_{0})}(v_{h}-v_{h}^{\Lambda})\cdot\partial_{t}v_{h}^{\Lambda}\eta^{s-1}\zeta_{\delta}\,dz-\iint_{U_{R_{2},S_{2}}(z_{0})}v_{h}^{\Lambda}\cdot\partial_{t}v_{h}^{\Lambda}\eta^{s-1}\zeta_{\delta}\,dz.

Note that integration by parts implies

\iint_{U_{R_{2},S_{2}}(z_{0})}v_{h}^{\Lambda}\cdot\partial_{t}v_{h}^{\Lambda}\eta^{s-1}\zeta_{\delta}\,dz=-\frac{1}{2}\iint_{U_{R_{2},S_{2}}(z_{0})}|v_{h}^{\Lambda}|^{2}\eta^{s-1}\partial_{t}\zeta_{\delta}\,dz.

Since
v_{h}^{\Lambda}=v_{h}
a.e. in
E(\Lambda)
and
v_{h}=v_{h}^{\Lambda}=0
in
U_{R_{2},S_{2}}(z_{0})^{c}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}_{1}

\displaystyle=\iint_{U_{R_{2},S_{2}}(z_{0})}-(|v_{h}|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}|v_{h}^{\Lambda}|^{2})\eta^{s-1}\partial_{t}\zeta_{\delta}\,dz

\displaystyle+\iint_{E(\Lambda)^{c}}v_{h}\cdot(v_{h}-v_{h}^{\Lambda})\eta^{s-1}\partial_{t}\zeta_{\delta}\,dz-\iint_{E(\Lambda)^{c}}(v_{h}-v_{h}^{\Lambda})\cdot\partial_{t}v_{h}^{\Lambda}\eta^{s-1}\zeta_{\delta}\,dz.

Letting
h\rightarrow 0^{+}
, we obtain from the properties of Steklov averages, Proposition
3.13

(iv)
and Lemma
3.9

(i)
that

\displaystyle\lim_{h\rightarrow 0^{+}}\mathrm{I}_{1}

\displaystyle=\iint_{U_{R_{2},S_{2}}(z_{0})}-(|v|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}|v^{\Lambda}|^{2})\eta^{s-1}\partial_{t}\zeta_{\delta}\,dz

\displaystyle+\iint_{E(\Lambda)^{c}}v\cdot(v-v^{\Lambda})\eta^{s-1}\partial_{t}\zeta_{\delta}\,dz-\iint_{E(\Lambda)^{c}}(v-v^{\Lambda})\cdot\partial_{t}v^{\Lambda}\eta^{s-1}\zeta_{\delta}\,dz

\displaystyle=\mathrm{I}_{11}+\mathrm{I}_{12}+\mathrm{I}_{13}.

Since the Lipschitz truncation is done only in the bad set
E(\Lambda)^{c}
, we get

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}_{11}

\displaystyle=\iint_{E(\Lambda)}-\frac{1}{2}|v|^{2}\eta^{s-1}\partial_{t}\zeta_{\delta}\,dz-\iint_{E(\Lambda)^{c}}\left(|v|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}|v^{\Lambda}|^{c}\right)\eta^{s-1}\partial_{t}\zeta_{\delta}\,dz

Since
v\in L^{2}(U_{R_{2},S_{2}}(z_{0}),\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we obtain from the absolute continuity, Lemma
3.9

(iii)
and (
2.3
) that

\lim_{\Lambda\rightarrow\infty}\mathrm{I}_{11}=\iint_{U_{R_{2},S_{2}}(z_{0})}-\frac{1}{2}|v|^{2}\eta^{s-1}\partial_{t}\zeta_{\delta}\,dz.

For the same reason, we also have
\displaystyle\lim_{\Lambda\rightarrow\infty}\mathrm{I}_{12}=0
. Finally, Lemma
3.9

(ii)
and (
2.3
) imply that
\displaystyle\lim_{\Lambda\rightarrow\infty}\mathrm{I}_{13}=0
. Thus, we have

\lim_{\Lambda\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{h\rightarrow 0^{+}}\mathrm{I}_{1}=\iint_{U_{R_{2},S_{2}}(z_{0})}-\frac{1}{2}|v|^{2}\eta^{s-1}\partial_{t}\zeta_{\delta}\,dz.

Now, we estimate
\mathrm{I}_{2}
. By the properties of Steklov averages and Proposition
3.13

(iv)
and by dividing into the good and bad sets, we get

\displaystyle\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\mathrm{I}_{2}

\displaystyle=-\iint_{U_{R_{2},S_{2}}(z_{0})}(u-u_{0})\cdot v^{\Lambda}\eta^{s}\zeta_{\delta}\partial_{t}\zeta\,dz

\displaystyle\geq-\iint_{U_{R_{2},S_{2}}(z_{0})\cap E(\Lambda)}|u-u_{0}|^{2}|\partial_{t}\zeta|\,dz-\iint_{E(\Lambda)^{c}}|u-u_{0}||v^{\Lambda}||\partial_{t}\zeta|\,dz.

Then Hölder’s inequality implies

\displaystyle\iint_{E(\Lambda)^{c}}|u-u_{0}||v^{\Lambda}||\partial_{t}\zeta|\,dz

\displaystyle\leq\left(\iint_{U_{R_{2},S_{2}}(z_{0})\cap E(\Lambda)^{c}}|u-u_{0}|^{2}|\partial_{t}\zeta|^{2}\,dz\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\iint_{E(\Lambda)^{c}}|v^{\Lambda}|^{2}\,dz\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Since
|u|\in L^{2}(\Omega_{T})
, the first integral vanishes as
\Lambda\rightarrow\infty
. By Lemma
3.9

(iii)
and (
2.3
), we get

\lim_{\Lambda\rightarrow\infty}\iint_{E(\Lambda)^{c}}|v^{\Lambda}|^{2}\,dz\leq\lim_{\Lambda\rightarrow\infty}c\Lambda^{\frac{2}{p}}|E(\Lambda)^{c}|\leq\lim_{\Lambda\rightarrow\infty}c\Lambda|E(\Lambda)^{c}|=0.

Thus, we get

\lim_{\Lambda\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{h\rightarrow 0^{+}}\mathrm{I}_{2}\geq-\iint_{U_{R_{2},S_{2}}(z_{0})}|u-u_{0}|^{2}|\partial_{t}\zeta|\,dz,

and hence we conclude

\lim_{\Lambda\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{h\rightarrow 0^{+}}\mathrm{I}\geq\iint_{U_{R_{2},S_{2}}(z_{0})}-\frac{1}{2}|v|^{2}\eta^{s-1}\partial_{t}\zeta_{\delta}\,dz-\iint_{U_{R_{2},S_{2}}(z_{0})}|u-u_{0}|^{2}|\partial_{t}\zeta|\,dz.

Estimate of
\mathrm{II}
.
As in
[
28
]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{h\rightarrow 0^{+}}\mathrm{II}

\displaystyle=\iint_{U_{R_{2},S_{2}}(z_{0})\cap E(\Lambda)}\mathcal{A}(z,\nabla u)\cdot\nabla((u-u_{0})\eta^{s+1}\zeta^{2}\zeta_{\delta})\,dz

\displaystyle+\iint_{U_{R_{2},S_{2}}(z_{0})\cap E(\Lambda)^{c}}\mathcal{A}(z,\nabla u)\cdot\nabla(v^{\Lambda}\eta^{s}\zeta\zeta_{\delta})\,dz=\mathrm{II}_{1}+\mathrm{II}_{2}.

Since the integral within the good set does not contain
\Lambda
, letting
\Lambda\rightarrow\infty
, we see from (
1.2
) and (
3.1
) that

\displaystyle\lim_{\Lambda\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{h\rightarrow 0^{+}}\mathrm{II}_{1}

\displaystyle=\iint_{U_{R_{2},S_{2}}(z_{0})}(\mathcal{A}(z,\nabla u)\cdot\nabla u)\eta^{s+1}\zeta^{2}\zeta_{\delta}\,dz

\displaystyle+\iint_{U_{R_{2},S_{2}}(z_{0})}\mathcal{A}(z,\nabla u)\cdot(u-u_{0})\nabla(\eta^{s+1})\zeta^{2}\zeta_{\delta}\,dz

\displaystyle\geq c\iint_{U_{R_{2},S_{2}}(z_{0})}(|\nabla u|^{p}+a(z)|\nabla u|^{q}+b(z)|\nabla u|^{s})\eta^{s+1}\zeta^{2}\zeta_{\delta}\,dz

\displaystyle-c\iint_{U_{R_{2},S_{2}}(z_{0})}(|\nabla u|^{p-1}+a(z)|\nabla u|^{q-1}+b(z)|\nabla u|^{s-1})\frac{|u-u_{0}|}{R_{2}-R_{1}}\eta^{s}\zeta^{2}\zeta_{\delta}\,dz,

where

. Then, Young’s inequality with conjugate
\left(\frac{p}{p-1},p\right)
,
\left(\frac{q}{q-1},q\right)
and
\left(\frac{s}{s-1},s\right)
, respectively, gives

\displaystyle c\iint_{U_{R_{2},S_{2}}(z_{0})}(|\nabla u|^{p-1}+a(z)|\nabla u|^{q-1}+b(z)|\nabla u|^{s-1})\frac{|u-u_{0}|}{R_{2}-R_{1}}\eta^{s}\zeta^{2}\zeta_{\delta}\,dz

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{\nu}{2}\iint_{U_{R_{2},S_{2}}(z_{0})}(|\nabla u|^{p}+a(z)|\nabla u|^{q}+b(z)|\nabla u|^{s})\eta^{s+1}\zeta^{2}\zeta_{\delta}\,dz

\displaystyle\qquad+c\iint_{U_{R_{2},S_{2}}(z_{0})}\left(\frac{|u-u_{0}|^{p}}{(R_{2}-R_{1})^{p}}+a(z)\frac{|u-u_{0}|^{q}}{(R_{2}-R_{1})^{q}}+b(z)\frac{|u-u_{0}|^{s}}{(R_{2}-R_{1})^{s}}\right)\,dz,

where
c
depends only on
p,q,s,\nu
and
L
. Hence we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\Lambda\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{h\rightarrow 0^{+}}\mathrm{II}_{1}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\nu}{2}\iint_{U_{R_{2},S_{2}}(z_{0})}(|\nabla u|^{p}+a(z)|\nabla u|^{q}+b(z)|\nabla u|^{s})\eta^{s+1}\zeta^{2}\zeta_{\delta}\,dz

\displaystyle-c\iint_{U_{R_{2},S_{2}}(z_{0})}\left(\frac{|u-u_{0}|^{p}}{(R_{2}-R_{1})^{p}}+a(z)\frac{|u-u_{0}|^{q}}{(R_{2}-R_{1})^{q}}+b(z)\frac{|u-u_{0}|^{s}}{(R_{2}-R_{1})^{s}}\right)\,dz.

Moreover, it is easy to show
\displaystyle\lim_{\Lambda\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{h\rightarrow 0^{+}}\mathrm{II}_{2}=0
by using (
1.2
), Young’s inequality, Lemma
3.9

(iii)
and (
2.3
). For detailed calculations, refer to

]
. Thus, we conclude

\displaystyle\lim_{\Lambda\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{h\rightarrow 0^{+}}\mathrm{II}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\nu}{2}\iint_{U_{R_{2},S_{2}}(z_{0})}H(z,|\nabla u|)\eta^{s+1}\zeta^{2}\zeta_{\delta}\,dz

\displaystyle-c\iint_{U_{R_{2},S_{2}}(z_{0})}H\left(\frac{|u-u_{0}|}{R_{2}-R_{1}}\right)\,dz.

Estimate of
\mathrm{III}.
The estimate for
\mathrm{III}
follows a process similar to that for
\mathrm{II}
. Again, we divide into the good and bad parts to obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{h\rightarrow 0^{+}}\mathrm{III}

\displaystyle=\iint_{U_{R_{2},S_{2}}(z_{0})\cap E(\Lambda)}\mathcal{B}(z,F)\cdot\nabla((u-u_{0})\eta^{s+1}\zeta^{2}\zeta_{\delta})\,dz

\displaystyle\leq c\iint_{U_{R_{2},S_{2}}(z_{0})}\left(H\left(z,\frac{|u-u_{0}|}{R_{2}-R_{1}}\right)+H(z,|F|)\right)\,dz

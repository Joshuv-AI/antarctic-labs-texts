1.
Introduction and main results

1.1.
Background

In this article, we derive so-called Bernis estimates for fourth-order doubly-degenerate parabolic problems of the form

(1.1)

\begin{cases}u_{t}+\diverg\bigl(u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p-2}\nabla\Delta u\bigr)=0&\quad\text{on }(0,\infty)\times\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1},\\
\nabla u\cdot\nu=\nabla\Delta u\cdot\nu=0&\quad\text{in }(0,\infty)\times\partial\Omega,\\
u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}(\cdot)&\quad\text{in }\Omega,\end{cases}

as they arise in the modeling of non-Newtonian thin-film flows with a strain rate-dependent power-law rheology.

Problem (
1.1
) describes the evolution of the height
u=u(t,x)\geq 0
of a thin layer of a viscous, non-Newtonian, and incompressible fluid over an impermeable flat bottom, as sketched in Figure
1
.

The partial differential equation
\eqref{eq:PDE}_{1}
is a fourth-order quasilinear equation that is doubly-degenerate parabolic, meaning that parabolicity gets lost when either the film height
u
or the third-order term
\nabla\Delta u
vanishes.

The term
u^{n}
represents the mobility of the flow, with the exponent
n>0
determined by the choice of the slip condition at the lower boundary (i.e., the interface between the solid bottom and the fluid film). For a no-slip condition, we have
n=p+1
, while for a Navier-slip condition, we have
n=p
. Other values of
n\in(p-2,p+1)
have been proposed to model weaker or stronger slippage; see, for example,
[
Gre78
]
. The term
|\nabla\Delta u|^{p-2}\nabla\Delta u
has a dual physical interpretation: the higher-order term
\nabla\Delta u
accounts for surface tension effects (we assume that the dynamics of the flow is driven solely by surface tension, thus neglecting gravitational effects), while its appearance in the nonlinear form
|s|^{p-2}s
is due to the non-Newtonian power-law rheology of the fluid.

The evolution equation
\eqref{eq:PDE}_{1}
is supplemented with Neumann-type boundary conditions
\nabla u\cdot\nu=0
and
\nabla\Delta u\cdot\nu=0
, which represent a zero-contact angle condition and a no-flux condition at the lateral boundary
(0,\infty)\times\partial\Omega
, respectively.
Finally,
u_{0}
denotes the initial film height.

The natural energy inequality for non-negative solutions to (
1.1
) is given by

(1.2)

\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(t)|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x\;\mathrm{d}s\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{0}|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x,\quad t\geq 0.

However, even in the one-dimensional case or for the classical Newtonian thin-film equation (
p=2
), this estimate on the dissipation
\int_{\Omega}u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x
is not sufficient for many purposes. Further estimates on the integrals
\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\big(u^{\frac{n+p}{3p}}\big)|^{3p}\;\mathrm{d}x
,
\int_{\Omega}|\Delta\big(u^{\frac{2(n+p)}{3p}}\big)|^{\frac{3p}{2}}\;\mathrm{d}x
, and
\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\Delta\big(u^{\frac{n+p}{p}}\big)|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x
are needed to obtain qualitative results such as finite propagation of support or the occurrence of a waiting-time phenomenon. See Section
1.3
for more details on related results.

1.2.
Aim of the article and main results

The main goal of this article is to derive inequalities of the form

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p}|\nabla u|^{3p}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-\frac{p}{2}}|\Delta u|^{\frac{3p}{2}}\;\mathrm{d}x\leq c(n,p,d)\left(\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{3p}u^{n+p}\;\mathrm{d}x\right)\end{split}

for a given non-negative test function
\varphi
, and for positive functions
u\in W^{2}_{p}(\Omega),\ 2\leq p<\frac{19}{3}
, satisfying the Neumann boundary condition
\nabla u\cdot\nu=0
on
\partial\Omega
and having finite dissipation
\int_{\Omega}u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x<\infty
. These are established in Theorem
1.1
below.

From these inequalities, we can deduce further estimates of the form

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\Big|\nabla\big(u^{\frac{n+p}{3p}}\big)\Big|^{3p}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\Big|\Delta\big(u^{\frac{2(n+p)}{3p}}\big)\Big|^{\frac{3p}{2}}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\Big|\nabla\Delta\big(u^{\frac{n+p}{p}}\big)\Big|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x\leq c(n,p,d)\int_{\Omega}u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x\end{split}

for
u
as above. This result is proven in Theorem
1.3
below.

These inequalities, established in Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
, provide a basis for applying appropriate interpolation results, which are in turn crucial for proving qualitative properties of solutions to (
1.1
), such as finite speed of propagation or waiting-time phenomena.

Our results generalize the findings of
[
Grü01
]
, where the Newtonian analogue of (
1.1
) is addressed, to non-Newtonian thin-film equations of the form (
1.1
). In the one-dimensional non-Newtonian case, similar Bernis inequalities were shown in
[
AG04
]
in the parameter range
p\geq\frac{4}{3}
and
\frac{p-1}{2}<n<2p-1
.

In the following, we present the main results of this article, Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
, in detail.

Theorem 1.1.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d<3p
, be a bounded, convex domain with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
. Let
2\leq p<\frac{19}{3}
and

2p-2\frac{2+\sqrt{(3p-2)^{2}+d(3p-4)}}{-3p+6+\sqrt{(3p-2)^{2}+d(3p-4)}}<n<2p-1.

Assume
u\in W^{2}_{p}(\Omega)
is positive, satisfies
\nabla u\cdot\nu=0
on
\partial\Omega
, and

\int_{\Omega}u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\,dx<\infty.

Then there exist positive constants
c_{i}=c_{i}(n,p,d)>0
,
i=1,2,3
,
such that the estimates

(1.3)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p}|\nabla u|^{3p}\;\mathrm{d}x\leq c_{1}\left(\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{3p}u^{n+p}\;\mathrm{d}x\right)\end{split}

and

(1.4)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-6}|D^{2}u\nabla u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}|D^{2}u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&+\int_{\partial\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}\mathrm{II}(\nabla u,\nabla u)\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\leq\ c_{2}\left(\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{3p}u^{n+p}\;\mathrm{d}x\right)\end{split}

and

(1.5)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-\frac{p}{2}}|\Delta u|^{\frac{3p}{2}}\;\mathrm{d}x\leq c_{3}\left(\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{3p}u^{n+p}\;\mathrm{d}x\right)\end{split}

hold true for all non-negative test functions
\varphi\in W^{1}_{\infty}(\Omega)
satisfying
\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{3p}u^{n+p}\;\mathrm{d}x<\infty
.
Here,
\mathrm{II}
denotes the (positive semi-definite) second fundamental form of
\partial\Omega
.

Remark 1.2.

(i)

For
p=2
and
\varphi\equiv 1
, the inequalities in (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) recover exactly the results in
[
Grü01
, Theorem 1.1]
. The localized version for
p=2
with a non-negative localization function
\varphi
is mentioned in
[
Grü05
]
, where a proof is sketched for the range
2-\sqrt{\tfrac{8}{8+d}}<n<3
.

(ii)

For
d=1
, estimates (
1.3
) and (
1.5
) are derived in
[
AG04
, Proposition 4.1]
for
p\geq\frac{4}{3}
and
\frac{p-1}{2}<n<2p-1
. The restriction
p\geq 2
in our case (instead of
p\geq\frac{4}{3}
, as in
d=1
) is due to the term
|\nabla u|^{3p-6}
in the integral

\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-6}|D^{2}u\nabla u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x.

In the case
d=1
, this integral simplifies to

\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}|D^{2}u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x.

Note, however, that we can estimate
|D^{2}u\nabla u|^{2}\leq|D^{2}u|^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}
, where
|D^{2}u|=\sqrt{\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}(\partial_{ij}u)^{2}}
denotes the Frobenius norm of the Hessian matrix
D^{2}u=(\partial_{ij}u)_{i,j=1,\dots,d}
.

(iii)

The restriction
p<\frac{19}{3}
is used only once, in (
2.8
) of the proof of Proposition
2.3
, to ensure the non-negativity of the integral
\mathcal{I}_{4}
. This integral does not appear for
d=1
, as in this case
D^{2}u=\Delta u
.

(iv)

For smooth functions, Theorem
1.1
is valid for any dimension
d\geq 1
. The critical dimension
d<3p
in Theorem
1.1
increases monotonically with
p
. It can be computed from the conditions
W^{3}_{p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow W^{2}_{q}(\Omega)
for
1\leq q<\frac{pd}{d-p}
and
W^{2}_{q}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow C(\bar{\Omega})
for
q>\frac{d}{2}
, as evident from the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

(v)

For
p\geq 2
, we have

2p-2\frac{2+\sqrt{(3p-2)^{2}+d(3p-4)}}{-3p+6+\sqrt{(3p-2)^{2}+d(3p-4)}}<2p-2.

For
p=2
, this reduces to
2-\sqrt{\frac{8}{8+d}}<2
, which is consistent with
[
Grü01
,
Grü05
]
.
For
d=1
, we have

2p-2\frac{2+\sqrt{(3p-2)^{2}+d(3p-4)}}{-3p+6+\sqrt{(3p-2)^{2}+d(3p-4)}}=\frac{4p-2-2\sqrt{p(p-1)}}{3},

which is greater than
\frac{p-1}{2}
for all
p\geq 1
. This shows that the bound for
n
derived in this article is stricter than the one provided in
[
AG04
]
.

Theorem 1.3.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d<3p
, be a bounded, convex domain with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
. Let
2\leq p<\frac{19}{3}
and

2p-2\frac{2+\sqrt{(3p-2)^{2}+d(3p-4)}}{-3p+6+\sqrt{(3p-2)^{2}+d(3p-4)}}<n<2p-1.

Assume
u\in W^{2}_{p}(\Omega)
is positive, satisfies
\nabla u\cdot\nu=0
on
\partial\Omega
, and

\int_{\Omega}u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\,dx<\infty.

Then, we have

(1.6)

u^{\frac{n+p}{3p}}\in W^{1}_{3p}(\Omega),\quad u^{\frac{2(n+p)}{3p}}\in W^{2}_{\frac{3p}{2}}(\Omega),\quad\text{and}\quad u^{\frac{n+p}{p}}\in W^{3}_{p}(\Omega).

Moreover, there exists a constant
c_{4}=c_{4}(n,p,d)>0
such that

(1.7)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\Big|\nabla\big(u^{\frac{n+p}{3p}}\big)\Big|^{3p}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\Big|\Delta\big(u^{\frac{2(n+p)}{3p}}\big)\Big|^{\frac{3p}{2}}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\Big|\nabla\Delta\big(u^{\frac{n+p}{p}}\big)\Big|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x\leq c_{4}\int_{\Omega}u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

Remark 1.4.

For the first two terms in (
1.7
), we also obtain localized estimates of the form

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}\Big|\nabla\big(u^{\frac{n+p}{3p}}\big)\Big|^{3p}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}\Big|\Delta\big(u^{\frac{2(n+p)}{3p}}\big)\Big|^{\frac{3p}{2}}\;\mathrm{d}x\leq c(n,p,d)\left(\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{3p}u^{n+p}\;\mathrm{d}x\right)\end{split}

for all non-negative test functions
\varphi\in W^{1}_{\infty}(\Omega)
satisfying
\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{3p}u^{n+p}\,dx<\infty
, see Corollary
3.1
. However, deriving a localized version for the third-order term in (
1.7
) turns out to be significantly more challenging. This issue is discussed in Remark
3.2
in detail.

We emphasize again that the strategy used to prove inequalities (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) in Theorem
1.1
is strongly inspired by the approach in
[
Grü01
]
, while incorporating the necessary generalizations. In the Newtonian case
p=2
, these two inequalities alone are sufficient to derive the weighted analogue of Theorem
1.3
. Inequality (
1.5
) in Theorem
1.1
can be established similarly to the one-dimensional case in
[
AG04
]
. Nonetheless, the results presented here are novel and play a significant role in studying weak solutions to (
1.1
), as well as in proving qualitative properties such as finite speed of propagation and the occurrence of waiting-time phenomena. Further details on potential applications of Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
are given in Section
1.3
, where we summarize the state of the art for the one-dimensional non-Newtonian case and for the Newtonian case in dimensions
1\leq d<6
.

1.3.
Related results

In the one-dimensional Newtonian case, the corresponding thin-film equation is given by

(1.8)

u_{t}+\bigl(u^{n}u_{xxx}\bigr)_{x}=0.

This equation was first studied in the seminal paper
[
BF90
]
. The property of finite speed of propagation for (
1.8
) was shown in
[
Ber96
,
Ber96a
]
for
0<n<2
and
2\leq n<3
, respectively, with upper bounds on the propagation rate for
0<n<2
derived in
[
Ber96
]
. The ideas used in the proofs differ depending on the mobility exponent
n
. For
0<n<2
, the proof relies heavily on the so-called
\alpha
-entropy estimates, while for
2\leq n<3
, it is based on localized versions of the energy inequality (
1.2
) in
d=1
. In
[
HS98
]
, the authors showed a finite speed of propagation result in terms of the
L_{1}
-norm. The occurrence of a waiting-time phenomenon was proved in
[
DGG01
]
, even for higher space dimensions.

The one-dimensional non-Newtonian analogue of (
1.1
) is given by

u_{t}+\bigl(u^{n}|u_{xxx}|^{p-2}u_{xxx}\bigr)_{x}=0,

and Bernis estimates in the style of Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
were derived in
[
AG04
]
, where the authors used these estimates in particular to guarantee the zero-contact angle condition for the constructed global non-negative solutions. Earlier, estimates of the form (
1.3
) and (
1.5
) were obtained in
[
Ber96b
]
, where the parameter range
p\geq\frac{4}{3}
and
n\in\big(\frac{p-1}{2},\frac{p+1}{2}-\frac{1}{3p}\big)
was considered. Let us note that
\alpha
-entropy estimates are not available in the non-Newtonian setting.

The higher-dimensional Newtonian thin-film equation reads

(1.9)

u_{t}+\diverg\bigl(u^{n}\nabla\Delta u\bigr)=0,

and Bernis estimates for this equation were derived in
[
Grü01
]
. An entropy estimate for (
1.9
) was shown in
[
DGG98
]
, and based on this, further qualitative results for the parameter range
0<n<2
– including finite speed of propagation, waiting-time phenomena, and the existence of solutions to (
1.9
) emanating from measure-valued initial data – were obtained in
[
Ber+98
]
,
[
DG99
]
, and
[
DGG01
]
.

To the best of the authors’ knowledge, this is the first paper to address Bernis estimates for the higher-dimensional non-Newtonian thin-film equation.

1.4.
Notation

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a domain. The standard
L_{q}(\Omega)
-norm for
1\leq q\leq\infty
is denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{L_{q}(\Omega)}
. The Sobolev space
W^{m}_{q}(\Omega)
, where
m\in\mathbb{N}
, consists of functions whose generalized derivatives up to order
m
belong to
L_{q}(\Omega)
. The corresponding norm is denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{W^{m}_{q}(\Omega)}
.

For a sufficiently differentiable function
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
, the gradient of
u
is denoted by
\nabla u
, and the Hessian matrix of
u
is denoted by
D^{2}u
. The Laplacian
\Delta u
is defined as the trace of the Hessian matrix
D^{2}u
.

The exterior unit normal vector to the boundary
\partial\Omega
is denoted by
\nu
. Depending on the context,
|\cdot|
represents the absolute value for scalars in
\mathbb{R}
, the Euclidean norm for vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, or the Frobenius norm for matrices in
\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
.

This article is organized into three sections: in Section
2
, we prove Theorem
1.1
, and in Section
3
, we prove Theorem
1.3
.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

This main section of the paper is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.1
, and we follow, more or less, the approach of Grün
[
Grü01
]
, where Bernis estimates for the higher-dimensional thin-film equation were established in the Newtonian setting.

The proof proceeds in three main steps. First, we derive a technical auxiliary integral identity, presented in Lemma
2.2
. Next, we establish the desired estimates (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) for two specific ranges of
n
. For the range
2p-2<n<2p-1
, the estimates are proved in Proposition
2.3
. For the range

2p-2\frac{2+\sqrt{(3p-2)^{2}+d(3p-4)}}{-3p+6+\sqrt{(3p-2)^{2}+d(3p-4)}}<n<2p-2\frac{6p-6+\sqrt{(3p-2)^{2}+d(3p-4)}}{9p-10+\sqrt{(3p-2)^{2}+d(3p-4)}}

the estimates are obtained in Proposition
2.5
. In both cases, these results are shown for smooth functions. The inequality (
1.5
) is proved directly in Proposition
2.7
for the full range

2p-2\frac{2+\sqrt{(3p-2)^{2}+d(3p-4)}}{-3p+6+\sqrt{(3p-2)^{2}+d(3p-4)}}<n<2p-1.

This result is also established for smooth functions. Finally, an approximation argument is used to extend these results to
u\in W^{2}_{p}(\Omega)
. Since

2p-2\frac{2+\sqrt{(3p-2)^{2}+d(3p-4)}}{-3p+6+\sqrt{(3p-2)^{2}+d(3p-4)}}<2p-2\quad\text{and}\quad 2p-2\frac{6p-6+\sqrt{(3p-2)^{2}+d(3p-4)}}{9p-10+\sqrt{(3p-2)^{2}+d(3p-4)}}<2p-1,

we conclude Theorem
1.1
for the entire range

2p-2\frac{2+\sqrt{(3p-2)^{2}+d(3p-4)}}{-3p+6+\sqrt{(3p-2)^{2}+d(3p-4)}}<n<2p-1.

Lemma
2.2
, as mentioned earlier, provides the auxiliary integral identity. To prove this lemma, we use the following relation between the normal derivative of
|\nabla u|^{2}
and the second fundamental form of the boundary of
\Omega
.

Lemma 2.1.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded smooth domain, and let
\mathrm{II}
denote the second fundamental form of
\partial\Omega
. For any function
u\in C^{2}(\bar{\Omega})
with
\nabla u\cdot\nu=0
on
\partial\Omega
, we have
\nabla|\nabla u|^{2}\cdot\nu=-2\mathrm{II}(\nabla u,\nabla u)
. In particular, if
\Omega
is convex, then
\nabla|\nabla u|^{2}\cdot\nu\leq 0
on
\partial\Omega
.

This control of the normal derivative of
|\nabla u|^{2}
on
\partial\Omega
is widely used in the literature; see, for example,
[
CH78
]
,
[
SZ98
]
, and the references cited in
[
Grü01
]
. For details, we refer to these works.

Lemma 2.2.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded smooth domain, and let
p\geq 2
. For any positive function
u\in C^{\infty}(\bar{\Omega})
satisfying
\nabla u\cdot\nu=0
on
\partial\Omega
, and for any
\varphi\in W^{1}_{\infty}(\Omega)
, the following identity holds:

(2.1)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-6}|D^{2}u\nabla u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&=-\frac{3p}{3p-4}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p-1}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}\nabla\varphi\cdot D^{2}u\nabla u\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad-\frac{n-2p+2}{3p-4}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+1}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}\nabla u\cdot D^{2}u\nabla u\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad-\frac{1}{3p-4}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}|D^{2}u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{3p-4}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\Delta u\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad-\frac{1}{3p-4}\int_{\partial\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}\mathrm{II}(\nabla u,\nabla u)\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}.\end{split}

Proof.

We first use the identities

D^{2}u\nabla u=\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla|\nabla u|^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\nabla|\nabla u|^{2}\cdot\nabla|\nabla u|^{2}=\diverg\bigl(|\nabla u|^{2}\,\nabla|\nabla u|^{2}\bigr)-|\nabla u|^{2}\Delta|\nabla u|^{2}

to obtain

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-6}|D^{2}u\nabla u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-6}\nabla|\nabla u|^{2}\cdot\nabla|\nabla u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-6}\diverg(|\nabla u|^{2}\,\nabla|\nabla u|^{2})\;\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}\Delta|\nabla u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

Applying integration by parts on the first integral on the right-hand side and using that
\nabla|\nabla u|^{2}\cdot\nu=-2\mathrm{II}(\nabla u,\nabla u)
on
\partial\Omega
, see Lemma
2.1
, then yields

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-6}|D^{2}u\nabla u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&=-\frac{3p}{4}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p-1}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla|\nabla u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x-\frac{n-2p+2}{4}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+1}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}\nabla u\cdot\nabla|\nabla u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad-\frac{3p-6}{4}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-6}D^{2}u\nabla u\cdot\nabla|\nabla u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}\Delta|\nabla u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}\mathrm{II}(\nabla u,\nabla u)\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}.\end{split}

With the identities

\nabla|\nabla u|^{2}=2D^{2}u\nabla u
and

\Delta|\nabla u|^{2}=2(|D^{2}u|^{2}+\nabla u\cdot\nabla\Delta u)
, this can be rewritten as

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-6}|D^{2}u\nabla u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&=-\frac{3p}{2}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p-1}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}\nabla\varphi\cdot D^{2}u\nabla u\;\mathrm{d}x-\frac{n-2p+2}{2}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+1}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}D^{2}u\nabla u\cdot\nabla u\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad-\frac{3p-6}{2}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-6}|D^{2}u\nabla u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}|D^{2}u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\Delta u\;\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}\mathrm{II}(\nabla u,\nabla u)\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}.\end{split}

Since the third integrand on the right-hand side equals the integrand on the left-hand side and since
p\geq 2>\tfrac{4}{3}

ensures that
\frac{1}{3p-4}>0
,
the proof is complete.
∎

We remark that in Lemma
2.2
, we need
p\geq 2>\frac{4}{3}
in the final step of the proof to ensure that
\frac{2}{3p-4}>0
. In this case, we have
\frac{n-2p+2}{3p-4}>0
if and only if
n>2p-2
.
Next, we establish Theorem
1.1
for fixed
2\leq p<\frac{19}{3}
in the case
2p-2<n<2p-1
. Note that this corresponds to the bounds
2<n<3
in the Newtonian case
p=2
, as detailed in
[
Grü01
, Lemma 2.3]
.

Proposition 2.3.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded, convex and smooth domain, and let
2\leq p<\frac{19}{3}
be fixed. Assume that
u\in C^{\infty}(\bar{\Omega})
is positive and satisfies
\nabla u\cdot\nu=0
on
\partial\Omega
, and let
\varphi\in W^{1}_{\infty}(\Omega)
be non-negative.
If

2p-2<n<2p-1

then there exist constants
C_{i}=C_{i}(n,p)>0
,
i=1,2
, such that the following estimates hold:

(2.2)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p}|\nabla u|^{3p}\;\mathrm{d}x\leq C_{1}\left[\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{3p}u^{n+p}\;\mathrm{d}x\right]\end{split}

and

(2.3)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-6}|D^{2}u\nabla u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}|D^{2}u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&+\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}|\Delta u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\partial\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}\mathrm{II}(\nabla u,\nabla u)\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\\
\leq\ &C_{2}\left[\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{3p}u^{n+p}\;\mathrm{d}x\right].\end{split}

Note that Proposition
2.3
works for any dimension
d\geq 1
.

Remark 2.4.

The constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
in Proposition
2.3
are given by

\begin{split}C_{1}&=\max\left\{\frac{3p}{2}\left|\frac{2(3p-1)}{(n-2p+1)(n-2p+2)}\right|^{p},\right.\\
&\quad\left.p^{\frac{3p}{2}}\left(\frac{9p^{2}}{2(n-2p+1)^{2}}+\frac{9p^{2}(3p-2)^{2}}{\varepsilon_{2}(n-2p+1)^{2}(n-2p+2)^{2}}+\frac{9p^{2}}{\varepsilon_{3}(n-2p+1)^{2}(n-2p+2)^{2}}\right)^{\frac{3p}{2}}\right\}\end{split}

and

C_{2}=\frac{5}{3p}+C_{1}\left[\frac{p-1}{p}\left(\frac{3p-1}{C_{\min}}\right)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}+\frac{3p-2}{3p}\left(\frac{C_{\delta_{0}}+C_{\delta_{1}}+C_{\delta_{2}}}{C_{\min}}\right)^{\frac{3p}{3p-2}}+\frac{1}{C_{\min}}\right],

where

C_{\delta_{0}}=\frac{9p^{2}(n-2p+2)^{2}}{4}>0,

and
C_{\min},C_{\delta_{1}}
, and
C_{\delta_{2}}
are positive constants depending only on the fixed parameter
p
, but not on
n
.
Hence, both
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
blow up as
n
goes to
2p-2
.
Moreover, we need
n>2p-2
in the proof to guarantee that the involved integrals have the ‘right’ sign. However, it turns out that the lower bound
2p-2
can be slightly improved (see Proposition
2.5
below).

Proof.

We first prove (
2.2
).
To this end, we use the identity
\nabla u\cdot\nabla u=\diverg(u\nabla u)-u\Delta u

and integration by parts, together with the boundary condition for
u
, to rewrite the left-hand side of (
2.2
) as

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p}|\nabla u|^{3p}\;\mathrm{d}x&=\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p}|\nabla u|^{3p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla u\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p}|\nabla u|^{3p-2}\diverg(u\nabla u)\;\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+1}|\nabla u|^{3p-2}\Delta u\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&=-3p\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p-1}u^{n-2p+1}|\nabla u|^{3p-2}\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla u\;\mathrm{d}x-(n-2p)\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p}|\nabla u|^{3p}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad-(3p-2)\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+1}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}D^{2}u\nabla u\cdot\nabla u\;\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+1}|\nabla u|^{3p-2}\Delta u\;\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

Since the second integrand on the right-hand side equals the integrand on the left-hand side and since
n<2p-1
by assumption, we find that

(2.4)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p}|\nabla u|^{3p}\;\mathrm{d}x&=-\frac{3p}{n-2p+1}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p-1}u^{n-2p+1}|\nabla u|^{3p-2}\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla u\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad-\frac{3p-2}{n-2p+1}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+1}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}D^{2}u\nabla u\cdot\nabla u\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad-\frac{1}{n-2p+1}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+1}|\nabla u|^{3p-2}\Delta u\;\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

In the last term of (
2.4
) we use again the identity
\nabla u\cdot\nabla u=\diverg(u\nabla u)-u\Delta u
and integrate by parts to obtain

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+1}|\nabla u|^{3p-2}\Delta u\;\mathrm{d}x\\
=\ &\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+1}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}\diverg(u\nabla u)\Delta u\;\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}|\Delta u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
=\ &-3p\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p-1}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}\Delta u\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla u\;\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}|\Delta u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&-(n-2p+1)\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+1}|\nabla u|^{3p-2}\Delta u\;\mathrm{d}x-(3p-4)\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-6}\Delta uD^{2}u\nabla u\cdot\nabla u\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&-\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}\nabla\Delta u\cdot\nabla u\;\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

Noting that the integrand on the left-hand side equals the third integrand on the right-hand side and recalling that
n>2p-2
by assumption, we deduce

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-2}\Delta u\;\mathrm{d}x\\
=\ &-\frac{3p}{n-2p+2}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p-1}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}\Delta u\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla u\;\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{n-2p+2}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}|\Delta u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&-\frac{3p-4}{n-2p+2}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-6}\Delta uD^{2}u\nabla u\cdot\nabla u\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&-\frac{1}{n-2p+2}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}\nabla\Delta u\cdot\nabla u\;\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

Inserting this into (
2.4
), we obtain

(2.5)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p}|\nabla u|^{3p}\;\mathrm{d}x&=-\frac{3p}{n-2p+1}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p-1}u^{n-2p+1}|\nabla u|^{3p-2}\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla u\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad-\frac{3p-2}{n-2p+1}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+1}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}D^{2}u\nabla u\cdot\nabla u\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad+\frac{3p}{(n-2p+1)(n-2p+2)}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p-1}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}\Delta u\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla u\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{(n-2p+1)(n-2p+2)}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}|\Delta u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad+\frac{3p-4}{(n-2p+1)(n-2p+2)}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-6}\Delta uD^{2}u\nabla u\cdot\nabla u\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{(n-2p+1)(n-2p+2)}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}\nabla\Delta u\cdot\nabla u\;\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

Next, we use Lemma
2.2
to rewrite the second term on the right-hand side of (
2.5
) as follows

(2.6)

\begin{split}&-\frac{3p-2}{n-2p+1}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+1}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}D^{2}u\nabla u\cdot\nabla u\;\mathrm{d}x\\
=\ &\frac{(3p-2)(3p-4)}{(n-2p+1)(n-2p+2)}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-6}|D^{2}u\nabla u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&+\frac{3p(3p-2)}{(n-2p+1)(n-2p+2)}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p-1}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}\nabla\varphi\cdot D^{2}u\nabla u\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&+\frac{3p-2}{(n-2p+1)(n-2p+2)}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}|D^{2}u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&+\frac{3p-2}{(n-2p+1)(n-2p+2)}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\Delta u\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&+\frac{3p-2}{(n-2p+1)(n-2p+2)}\int_{\partial\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}\mathrm{II}(\nabla u,\nabla u)\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}.\end{split}

Applying now the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and Young’s inequality with weight
\varepsilon_{0}=\tfrac{1}{15}
on the fifth term on the right-hand side of (
2.5
) we obtain

(2.7)

\begin{split}&-\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-6}\Delta uD^{2}u\nabla u\cdot\nabla u\;\mathrm{d}x\\
\leq\ &\left|\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-6}\Delta uD^{2}u\nabla u\cdot\nabla u\;\mathrm{d}x\right|\\
\leq\ &\frac{1}{15}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}|\Delta u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+\frac{15}{4}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-6}|D^{2}u\nabla u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

Inserting (
2.6
) back into (
2.5
) and using (
2.7
) yields

(2.8)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p}|\nabla u|^{3p}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
\leq\ &\frac{(3p-4)(12p-23)}{4(n-2p+1)(n-2p+2)}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-6}|D^{2}u\nabla u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&+\frac{3p-2}{(n-2p+1)(n-2p+2)}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}|D^{2}u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&+\frac{3p-1}{(n-2p+1)(n-2p+2)}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\Delta u\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&+\frac{19-3p}{15(n-2p+1)(n-2p+2)}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}|\Delta u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&+\frac{3p-2}{(n-2p+1)(n-2p+2)}\int_{\partial\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}\mathrm{II}(\nabla u,\nabla u)\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\\
&-\frac{3p}{n-2p+1}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p-1}u^{n-2p+1}|\nabla u|^{3p-2}\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla u\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&+\frac{3p(3p-2)}{(n-2p+1)(n-2p+2)}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p-1}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}\nabla\varphi\cdot D^{2}u\nabla u\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&+\frac{3p}{(n-2p+1)(n-2p+2)}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p-1}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}\Delta u\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla u\;\mathrm{d}x\\
\eqqcolon&\ \mathcal{I}_{1}+\mathcal{I}_{2}+\mathcal{I}_{3}+\mathcal{I}_{4}+\mathcal{I}_{5}+\mathcal{I}_{6}+\mathcal{I}_{7}+\mathcal{I}_{8}.\end{split}

Due to the choices of
p\geq 2
and
2p-2<n<2p-1
the pre factors of
\mathcal{I}_{1},\mathcal{I}_{2}
and
\mathcal{I}_{5}
are negative, while the integrals are non-negative. The non-negativity of
\mathrm{II}(\nabla u,\nabla u)
follows from the convexity of the domain, see Lemma
2.1
. Moreover, the choices of
p<\frac{19}{3}
and
2p-2<n<2p-1
yield
\mathcal{I}_{4}\leq 0
. We directly use
\mathcal{I}_{2},\mathcal{I}_{5}\leq 0
in (
2.8
).
To control the last three integrals
\mathcal{I}_{6},\mathcal{I}_{7}
and
\mathcal{I}_{8}
, we use the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and Young’s inequality such that
\mathcal{I}_{6}
can be partly absorbed by the integral on the left-hand side of (
2.8
) and the integrals
\mathcal{I}_{7}
and
\mathcal{I}_{8}
are absorbed by
\mathcal{I}_{1}
, respectively
\mathcal{I}_{4}
. Indeed,
applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Young’s inequality with weight
\varepsilon_{1}=\frac{1}{2}
to
\mathcal{I}_{6}
, we have

(2.9)

\begin{split}&-\frac{3p}{n-2p+1}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p-1}u^{n-2p+1}|\nabla u|^{3p-2}\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla u\;\mathrm{d}x\\
\leq\ &\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p}|\nabla u|^{3p}\;\mathrm{d}x+C_{\varepsilon_{1}}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p-2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-2}\;\mathrm{d}x\end{split}

with
C_{\varepsilon_{1}}=C(\varepsilon_{1},n,p):=\tfrac{9p^{2}}{2(n-2p+1)^{2}}>0
.
Inserting this inequality in (
2.8
), we
obtain

(2.10)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p}|\nabla u|^{3p}\;\mathrm{d}x\leq 2(\mathcal{I}_{1}+\mathcal{I}_{3}+\mathcal{I}_{4}+\mathcal{I}_{7}+\mathcal{I}_{8})+2C_{\varepsilon_{1}}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p-2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-2}\;\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

We can further estimate
\mathcal{I}_{7}
by

(2.11)

\begin{split}&\left|\frac{6p(3p-2)}{(n-2p+1)(n-2p+2)}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p-1}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}\nabla\varphi\cdot D^{2}u\nabla u\;\mathrm{d}x\right|\\
\leq\ &\varepsilon_{2}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-6}|D^{2}u\nabla u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+C_{\varepsilon_{2}}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p-2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-2}\;\mathrm{d}x,\end{split}

as well as
\mathcal{I}_{8}
by

(2.12)

\begin{split}&\left|\frac{6p}{(n-2p+1)(n-2p+2)}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p-1}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}\Delta u\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla u\;\mathrm{d}x\right|\\
\leq\ &\varepsilon_{3}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}|\Delta u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+C_{\varepsilon_{3}}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p-2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-2}\;\mathrm{d}x,\end{split}

where
\varepsilon_{2},\varepsilon_{3}>0
can be chosen arbitrarily and where
C_{\varepsilon_{2}},C_{\varepsilon_{3}}>0
are given by

C_{\varepsilon_{2}}=C(\varepsilon_{2},n,p)\coloneqq\frac{9p^{2}(3p-2)^{2}}{\varepsilon_{2}(n-2p+1)^{2}(n-2p+2)^{2}}

and

C_{\varepsilon_{3}}=C(\varepsilon_{3},n,p)\coloneqq\frac{9p^{2}}{\varepsilon_{3}(n-2p+1)^{2}(n-2p+2)^{2}}.

We choose

\varepsilon_{2}=\varepsilon_{2}(n,p)
and
\varepsilon_{3}=\varepsilon_{3}(n,p)
small enough so that

0<\varepsilon_{2}<\left|\frac{(3p-4)(12p-23)}{2(n-2p+1)(n-2p+2)}\right|\quad\text{and}\quad 0<\varepsilon_{3}<\left|\frac{2(19-3p)}{15(n-2p+1)(n-2p+2)}\right|.

Using (
2.11
) and (
2.12
) in (
2.10
), we can absorb the terms in (
2.10
) to get the estimate

(2.13)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p}|\nabla u|^{3p}\;\mathrm{d}x&\leq\frac{2(3p-1)}{(n-2p+1)(n-2p+2)}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\Delta u\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad+\bigl(2C_{\varepsilon_{1}}+C_{\varepsilon_{2}}+C_{\varepsilon_{3}}\bigr)\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p-2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-2}\;\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

Applying Hölder’s inequality to both integrals on the right-hand side of (
2.13
), we obtain

(2.14)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p}|\nabla u|^{3p}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
\leq\ &\left|\frac{2(3p-1)}{(n-2p+1)(n-2p+2)}\right|\left(\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p}|\nabla u|^{3p}\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
&+\bigl(2C_{\varepsilon_{1}}+C_{\varepsilon_{2}}+C_{\varepsilon_{3}}\bigr)\left(\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p}|\nabla u|^{3p}\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{3p-2}{3p}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{3p}u^{n+p}\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{3p}}.\end{split}

We then use Young’s inequality
on the first line on the right-hand side of (
2.14
) to deduce

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p}|\nabla u|^{3p}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
\leq\ &\frac{p-1}{p}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p}|\nabla u|^{3p}\;\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{p}\left|\frac{2(3p-1)}{(n-2p+1)(n-2p+2)}\right|^{p}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&+\bigl(2C_{\varepsilon_{1}}+C_{\varepsilon_{2}}+C_{\varepsilon_{3}}\bigr)\left(\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p}|\nabla u|^{3p}\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{3p-2}{3p}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{3p}u^{n+p}\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{3p}},\end{split}

which immediately gives

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p}|\nabla u|^{3p}\;\mathrm{d}x&\leq\left|\frac{2(3p-1)}{(n-2p+1)(n-2p+2)}\right|^{p}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x\\

Applying again Young’s inequality,

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p}|\nabla u|^{3p}\;\mathrm{d}x&\leq\frac{3p}{2}\left|\frac{2(3p-1)}{(n-2p+1)(n-2p+2)}\right|^{p}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x\\

\begin{split}C_{1}&=\max\left\{\frac{3p}{2}\left|\frac{2(3p-1)}{(n-2p+1)(n-2p+2)}\right|^{p},\right.\\
&\quad\left.p^{\frac{3p}{2}}\left(\frac{9p^{2}}{(n-2p+1)^{2}}+\frac{9p^{2}(3p-2)^{2}}{\varepsilon_{2}(n-2p+1)^{2}(n-2p+2)^{2}}+\frac{9p^{2}}{\varepsilon_{3}(n-2p+1)^{2}(n-2p+2)^{2}}\right)^{\frac{3p}{2}}\right\}.\end{split}

It remains to prove (

and by rearranging this inequality, we see that

\displaystyle\frac{(3p-4)(12p-23)}{4}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-6}|D^{2}u\nabla u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+(3p-2)\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}|D^{2}u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\frac{19-3p}{15}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}|\Delta u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+(3p-2)\int_{\partial\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}\mathrm{II}(\nabla u,\nabla u)\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}

\displaystyle\leq\

\displaystyle-(3p-1)\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\Delta u\;\mathrm{d}x

(2.15)

\displaystyle+3p(n-2p+2)\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p-1}u^{n-2p+1}|\nabla u|^{3p-2}\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla u\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle-3p(3p-2)\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p-1}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}\nabla\varphi\cdot D^{2}u\nabla u\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\eqqcolon

For

Inserting (

(2.19)

\displaystyle\leq\

\displaystyle-(3p-1)\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\Delta u\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+(C_{\delta_{0}}+C_{\delta_{1}}+C_{\delta_{2}})\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p-2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-2}\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\frac{3p-2}{3p}\left(\frac{C_{\delta_{0}}+C_{\delta_{1}}+C_{\delta_{2}}}{C_{\min}}\right)^{\frac{3p}{3p-2}}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p}|\nabla u|^{3p}\;\mathrm{d}x+\frac{2}{3p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{3p}u^{n+p}\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\frac{C_{1}}{C_{\min}}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x+\frac{C_{1}}{C_{\min}}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{3p}u^{n+p}\;\mathrm{d}x.

Proposition 2.5.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded, convex and smooth domain, and let
2\leq p<\frac{19}{3}
be fixed. Assume that
u\in C^{\infty}(\bar{\Omega})
is positive and satisfies
\nabla u\cdot\nu=0
on
\partial\Omega
, and let
\varphi\in W^{1}_{\infty}(\Omega)
be non-negative. If

2p-2\frac{2+\sqrt{(3p-2)^{2}+d(3p-4)}}{-3p+6+\sqrt{(3p-2)^{2}+d(3p-4)}}<n<2p-2\frac{6p-6+\sqrt{(3p-2)^{2}+d(3p-4)}}{9p-10+\sqrt{(3p-2)^{2}+d(3p-4)}},

then there exist positive constants
C_{i}=C_{i}(n,p,d)>0,\ i=3,4,

such that the following estimates hold:

(2.20)

\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p}|\nabla u|^{3p}\;\mathrm{d}x\leq C_{3}\left[\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{3p}u^{n+p}\;\mathrm{d}x\right]

and

(2.21)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-6}|D^{2}u\nabla u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}|D^{2}u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&+\int_{\partial\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}\mathrm{II}(\nabla u,\nabla u)\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\leq\ C_{4}\left[\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{3p}u^{n+p}\;\mathrm{d}x\right].\end{split}

Remark 2.6.

(i)

(ii)

Proof.

We start by introducing the notation

\begin{split}\mathcal{K}_{1}&\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p}|\nabla u|^{3p}\;\mathrm{d}x,\\
\mathcal{K}_{2}&\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p-2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-2}\;\mathrm{d}x,\\
\mathcal{K}_{3}&\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-6}|D^{2}u\nabla u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x,\\
\mathcal{K}_{4}&\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}|D^{2}u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x,\\
\mathcal{B}&\coloneqq\int_{\partial\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}\mathrm{II}(\nabla u,\nabla u)\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}.\end{split}

Using this, we can rewrite (
2.1
) from Lemma
2.2

(2.22)

\begin{split}(3p-4)\mathcal{K}_{3}+\mathcal{K}_{4}+\mathcal{B}=&-3p\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p-1}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}\nabla\varphi\cdot D^{2}u\nabla u\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&-(n-2p+2)\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+1}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}\nabla u\cdot D^{2}u\nabla u\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&-\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p+2}|\nabla u|^{3p-4}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\Delta u\;\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

We want to estimate the right-hand side of (
2.22

\mathcal{K}_{3}
and
\mathcal{K}_{4}

\begin{split}(3p-4)\mathcal{K}_{3}+\mathcal{K}_{4}+\mathcal{B}\leq&\ 3p\sqrt{\mathcal{K}_{2}}\sqrt{\mathcal{K}_{3}}+|n-2p+2|\sqrt{\mathcal{K}_{1}}\sqrt{\mathcal{K}_{3}}\\
&+\mathcal{K}_{1}^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.\end{split}

Step 1. Estimates for

\mathcal{K}_{4}

First, we observe that, by Hölder’s inequality,

\mathcal{K}_{1}

(2.24)

\mathcal{K}_{2}\leq\mathcal{K}_{1}^{\frac{3p-2}{3p}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{3p}u^{n+p}\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{3p}}.

For the integral
\mathcal{K}_{1}

2.4

\frac{1}{d}|\Delta u|^{2}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{d}(\partial_{ii}u)^{2}\leq\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}(\partial_{ij}u)^{2}=|D^{2}u|^{2}\quad\text{ in }\Omega,

\begin{split}\mathcal{K}_{1}&=-\frac{3p}{n-2p+1}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p-1}u^{n-2p+1}|\nabla u|^{3p-2}\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla u\;\mathrm{d}x\\

\begin{split}\mathcal{K}_{1}&\leq\frac{1}{|n-2p+1|^{2}}\left(3p\sqrt{\mathcal{K}_{2}}+(3p-2)\sqrt{\mathcal{K}_{3}}+\sqrt{d}\sqrt{\mathcal{K}_{4}}\right)^{2}\\

\varepsilon_{2}>0
gives

\varepsilon_{2}=\frac{3p-2+\sqrt{(3p-2)^{2}+d(3p-4)}}{\sqrt{d}(3p-4)}.

If

(2.31)

\frac{|n-2p+2|}{|n-2p+1|}\varepsilon_{2}\sqrt{d}<2,

then we may absorb the second line on the right-hand side into the left-hand side. Inserting the equation for
\varepsilon_{2}
into (
2.31
) gives us a bound for
n
in terms of
p
and
d
. Indeed, the inequality

\frac{|n-2p+2|}{|n-2p+1|}\frac{3p-2+\sqrt{(3p-2)^{2}+d(3p-4)}}{3p-4}<2

is satisfied for

2p-2\frac{2+\sqrt{(3p-2)^{2}+d(3p-4)}}{-3p+6+\sqrt{(3p-2)^{2}+d(3p-4)}}<n<2p-2\frac{6p-6+\sqrt{(3p-2)^{2}+d(3p-4)}}{9p-10+\sqrt{(3p-2)^{2}+d(3p-4)}}.

Next, using Young’s inequality in the second term on the right-hand side of (
2.30
) and applying (
2.31
), we find that (
2.30
) can be further estimated as follows:

(2.32)

\begin{split}&(3p-4)\mathcal{K}_{3}+\mathcal{K}_{4}+\mathcal{B}\\
&\leq 2\left[\frac{\varepsilon_{3}}{2}+\left(\frac{4\varepsilon_{4}}{|n-2p+1|^{2}}+\varepsilon_{1}\right)(3p-2)\left((3p-2)+\sqrt{d}\right)\right]\mathcal{K}_{3}\\
&\quad+2\left(\frac{4\varepsilon_{4}}{|n-2p+1|^{2}}+\varepsilon_{1}\right)\sqrt{d}\left((3p-2)+\sqrt{d}\right)\mathcal{K}_{4}\\
&\quad+2\left[(\varepsilon_{4}+\tilde{\varepsilon}_{1})C+\left(\frac{3p}{3p-2}\varepsilon_{4}\right)^{\frac{2-3p}{2}}\frac{2}{3p}\left[\frac{9p^{2}}{2\varepsilon_{3}}\left(1+\frac{|n-2p+2|}{|n-2p+1|}\right)^{2}\right]^{\frac{3p}{2}}\right]\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{3p}u^{n+p}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad+2C_{\tilde{\varepsilon}_{1}}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

Now we can choose
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{3}
and
\varepsilon_{4}
such that we can absorb the first two lines on the right-hand side of (
2.32
) into its left-hand side. Hence, we find a constant
\tilde{C}=\tilde{C}(n,p,d)>0
such that

\mathcal{K}_{3}+\mathcal{K}_{4}+\mathcal{B}\leq\tilde{C}\left[\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{3p}u^{n+p}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x\right].

Combining this estimate with (
2.27
) concludes the proof.
∎

The final Bernis-type inequality, presented in the following proposition, provides an estimate for an integral involving the product of the function
u
and its Laplacian. The proof does not require a case-by-case analysis based on the mobility exponent
n
but can be directly established for the entire range of
n
. The key ingredients of the proof are the inequalities (
2.2
) and (
2.20
), which control the integral
\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p}|\nabla u|^{3p}\;\mathrm{d}x
. Additionally, we note that the corresponding result for the one-dimensional case is presented in
[
AG04
]
.

Proposition 2.7.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded, convex and smooth domain, and let
2\leq p<\frac{19}{3}
be fixed. Assume that
u\in C^{\infty}(\bar{\Omega})
is positive and satisfies
\nabla u\cdot\nu=0
on
\partial\Omega
, and let
\varphi\in W^{1}_{\infty}(\Omega)
be non-negative. If

2p-2\frac{2+\sqrt{(3p-2)^{2}+d(3p-4)}}{-3p+6+\sqrt{(3p-2)^{2}+d(3p-4)}}<n<2p-1,

then there exists a positive constant
C_{5}=C_{5}(n,p,d)>0

such that the following estimate holds:

(2.33)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-\frac{p}{2}}|\Delta u|^{\frac{3p}{2}}\;\mathrm{d}x\leq\ C_{5}\left(\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{3p}u^{n+p}\;\mathrm{d}x\right).\end{split}

Proof.

Using integration by parts, we can rewrite

(2.34)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-\frac{p}{2}}|\Delta u|^{\frac{3p}{2}}\;\mathrm{d}x&=-3p\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p-1}u^{n-\frac{p}{2}}|\Delta u|^{\frac{3p-4}{2}}\Delta u\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla u\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad-\left(n-\frac{p}{2}\right)\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-\frac{p}{2}-1}|\nabla u|^{2}|\Delta u|^{\frac{3p-4}{2}}\Delta u\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad-\left(\frac{3p-2}{2}\right)\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-\frac{p}{2}}|\Delta u|^{\frac{3p-4}{2}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\Delta u\;\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

Applying now Hölder’s and Young’s inequalities on each of the integrals on the right-hand side yields the estimates

(2.35)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p-1}u^{n-\frac{p}{2}}|\Delta u|^{\frac{3p-4}{2}}\Delta u\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla u\;\mathrm{d}x\\
\leq\ &\left(\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-\frac{p}{2}}|\Delta u|^{\frac{3p}{2}}\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{3p-2}{3p}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p}|\nabla u|^{3p}\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{3p}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{3p}u^{n+p}\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{3p}}\\
\leq\ &\varepsilon_{1}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-\frac{p}{2}}|\Delta u|^{\frac{3p}{2}}\;\mathrm{d}x+C_{\varepsilon_{1}}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p}|\nabla u|^{3p}\;\mathrm{d}x+\tilde{C}_{\varepsilon_{1}}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{3p}u^{n+p}\;\mathrm{d}x,\end{split}

for the first integral,

(2.36)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-\frac{p}{2}-1}|\nabla u|^{2}|\Delta u|^{\frac{3p-4}{2}}\Delta u\;\mathrm{d}x&\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-\frac{p}{2}}|\Delta u|^{\frac{3p}{2}}\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{3p-2}{3p}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p}|\nabla u|^{3p}\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{3p}}\\
&\leq\varepsilon_{2}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-\frac{p}{2}}|\Delta u|^{\frac{3p}{2}}\;\mathrm{d}x+C_{\varepsilon_{2}}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p}|\nabla u|^{3p}\;\mathrm{d}x\end{split}

for the second integral, and

(2.37)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-\frac{p}{2}}|\Delta u|^{\frac{3p-4}{2}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\Delta u\;\mathrm{d}x\\
\leq\ &\left(\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-\frac{p}{2}}|\Delta u|^{\frac{3p}{2}}\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{3p-4}{3p}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p}|\nabla u|^{3p}\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{3p}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
\leq\ &\varepsilon_{3}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-\frac{p}{2}}|\Delta u|^{\frac{3p}{2}}\;\mathrm{d}x+C_{\varepsilon_{3}}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p}|\nabla u|^{3p}\;\mathrm{d}x+\tilde{C}_{\varepsilon_{3}}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x\end{split}

for the third one, where we chose
0<\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}+\varepsilon_{3}<1
. By the inequalities (
2.2
), respectively (
2.20
) we can estimate the integral
\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p}|\nabla u|^{3p}\;\mathrm{d}x
in (
2.35
)–(
2.37
) in terms of
\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x
and
\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{3p}u^{n+p}\;\mathrm{d}x
. This yields the desired inequality.
∎

To complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
, it remains to perform the approximation argument to establish the result in the desired functional setting, i.e., for functions
u\in W^{2}_{p}(\Omega)
with
2\leq p<\frac{19}{3}
. Note that all results in this section are valid for arbitrary dimensions
d
. The restriction
d<3p
arises only during the approximation argument, where Sobolev embeddings are employed.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

The proof follows the idea of the proof of
[
Grü01
, Theorem 1.1]
, using elliptic regularity theory in
L_{p}(\Omega)
instead of
L_{2}(\Omega)
.
Let
u\in W^{2}_{p}(\Omega)
, with
2\leq p<\frac{19}{3}
, be a positive function satisfying
\nabla u\cdot\nu=0
on
\partial\Omega
,

(2.38)

\int_{\Omega}u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\,dx<\infty.

We may assume that there is
\delta>0
such that
u\geq\delta>0
a.e. in
\Omega
. Using (
2.38
), this implies
\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\,dx<\infty
.

To prove the claim, we construct a sequence of functions
(u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C^{\infty}(\bar{\Omega})
with the following properties:

(1)

u_{k}\geq\tfrac{\delta}{2}
in
\bar{\Omega}
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large;

(2)

\nabla u_{k}\cdot\nu=0
on
\partial\Omega
;

(3)

u_{k}\longrightarrow u
strongly in
W^{2}_{p}(\Omega)
, as
k\to\infty
;

(4)

\nabla\Delta u_{k}\longrightarrow\nabla\Delta u
strongly in
L_{p}(\Omega)
, as
k\to\infty
.

Once this sequence
u_{k}
is constructed, we know it satisfies the inequalities of Propositions
2.3
,

2.5
and
2.7
for the whole range

2p-2\frac{2+\sqrt{(3p-2)^{2}+d(3p-4)}}{-3p+6+\sqrt{(3p-2)^{2}+d(3p-4)}}<n<2p-1.

We can then take the limit on both sides of the corresponding inequalities. For the right-hand side, we apply Vitali’s convergence theorem, while for the left-hand side, we use Fatou’s lemma. This yields the corresponding estimates
(
1.3
), (
1.4
) and (
1.5
) for the limit function
u
.

We now construct the sequence
u_{k}
. Since
\nabla\Delta u\in L_{p}(\Omega)
, we have
\Delta u\in W^{1}_{p}(\Omega)
. Thus, there exists a sequence
(f_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C^{\infty}(\bar{\Omega})
satisfying

f_{k}\longrightarrow-\Delta u\quad\text{strongly in }W^{1}_{p}(\Omega)\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{\Omega}f_{k}\;\mathrm{d}x=0,

see
[
GT01
]
. Here, the zero-mean condition can be obtained since
\int_{\Omega}\Delta u\;\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\partial\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nu=0
by the Neumann boundary condition on
u
. For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
u_{k}
be the solution to the Neumann problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{k}=f_{k},\quad x\in\Omega\\
\nabla u_{k}\cdot\nu=0,\quad x\in\partial\Omega\\
\int_{\Omega}u_{k}\;\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}u\;\mathrm{d}x.\end{cases}

By elliptic regularity theory
[
Gri11
, Theorem 2.5.1.1, Remark 2.5.1.2]
, we find that
u_{k}\in C^{\infty}(\bar{\Omega})
and

u_{k}\longrightarrow u\quad\text{strongly in }W^{3}_{p}(\Omega).

In particular,
u_{k}
satisfies (2), (3), and (4).
Moreover, by the Sobolev embedding theorem, we have

W^{3}_{p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow W^{2}_{q}(\Omega)\quad\text{for }1\leq q<\frac{dp}{d-p}\quad\text{and}\quad W^{2}_{q}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow C(\bar{\Omega})\quad\text{for }q>\frac{d}{2}.

Consequently, we find that

\begin{cases}u_{k}\longrightarrow u\quad\text{strongly in }W^{2}_{q}(\Omega)&\text{for }1\leq q<\frac{dp}{d-p}.\\
u_{k}\longrightarrow u\quad\text{strongly in }C(\bar{\Omega})&\text{for }d<3p.\end{cases}

The strong convergence
u_{k}\to u
in
C(\bar{\Omega})
and
u\geq\delta>0
imply (1) for
k\in\mathbb{N}
large enough. This concludes the construction of the sequence
u_{k}
satisfying properties (1)–(4), thereby completing the proof of the theorem.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3

We now prove Theorem
1.3
which is a consequence of the inequalities (
1.3
) and (
1.5
) of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

The first parts of (
1.6
), respectively (

), follow from (
1.3
), while parts two and three follow from (

) and (

Parts one of (

We first show that

and that the inequality

\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}\Big|\nabla\big(u^{\frac{n+p}{3p}}\big)\Big|^{3p}\;\mathrm{d}x\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{3p}u^{n+p}\;\mathrm{d}x\right)

holds for some constant
C=C(n,p,d)>0
and for all non-negative test functions
\varphi\in W^{1}_{\infty}(\Omega)

and

&\leq\left(\tfrac{n+p}{3p}\right)^{3p}c_{1}\left(\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{3p}u^{n+p}\;\mathrm{d}x\right)<\infty.\end{split}

Setting
\varphi\equiv 1
yields the first estimate.

Parts two of (
1.6
), (
1.7
):

Next, we show that
u^{\frac{2(n+p)}{3p}}
belongs to
W^{2}_{\frac{3p}{2}}(\Omega)

\tilde{C}=\tilde{C}(n,p,d)

(3.3)

\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}\Big|\Delta\big(u^{\frac{2(n+p)}{3p}}\big)\Big|^{\frac{3p}{2}}\;\mathrm{d}x\leq\tilde{C}\left(\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{3p}u^{n+p}\;\mathrm{d}x\right)

holds for all non-negative test functions
\varphi\in W^{1}_{\infty}(\Omega)
satisfying
\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{3p}u^{n+p}\;\mathrm{d}x<\infty
.
Since
u\in W^{2}_{p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L_{n+p}(\Omega)
, we obtain as before that
u^{\frac{2(n+p)}{3p}}\in L_{\frac{3p}{2}}(\Omega)
. For
\nabla\big(u^{\frac{2(n+p)}{3p}}\big)
, we compute

(3.4)

\nabla\big(u^{\frac{2(n+p)}{3p}}\big)=\tfrac{2(n+p)}{3p}u^{\frac{2n-p}{3p}}\nabla u

and use Hölder’s inequality together with (
1.3
) (with
\varphi\equiv 1
) to find that

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\Big|\nabla\big(u^{\frac{2(n+p)}{3p}}\big)\Big|^{\frac{3p}{2}}\;\mathrm{d}x&=\left(\tfrac{2(n+p)}{3p}\right)^{\frac{3p}{2}}\int_{\Omega}u^{\frac{n+p}{2}}u^{\frac{n}{2}-p}|\nabla u|^{\frac{3p}{2}}\;\mathrm{d}x\\

Hence,
\nabla\big(u^{\frac{2(n+p)}{3p}}\big)\in L_{\frac{3p}{2}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Now, we compute

\Delta\big(u^{\frac{2(n+p)}{3p}}\big)=\tfrac{2(n+p)}{3p}\Big(\tfrac{2n-p}{3p}u^{\tfrac{2(n-2p)}{3p}}|\nabla u|^{2}+u^{\tfrac{2n-p}{3p}}\Delta u\Big),

and by Jensen’s inequality we further have that

(3.5)

\big|\Delta\big(u^{\frac{2(n+p)}{3p}}\big)\big|^{\frac{3p}{2}}\leq c\left(u^{n-2p}|\nabla u|^{3p}+u^{n-\frac{p}{2}}|\Delta u|^{\frac{3p}{2}}\right)

for some constant

.
Using (
1.3
) and (
1.5
), we infer from (
3.5
) that
\Delta\big(u^{\frac{2(n+p)}{3p}}\big)

L_{\frac{3p}{2}}(\Omega)

3.3
) holds. We deduce the second estimate in (

\varphi\equiv 1
in (
3.3
).
It remains to check that

) and the Neumann boundary condition for

. Elliptic

-theory (see for instance
[

Parts three of (
1.6
), (
1.7
):

Finally, we show that
u^{\frac{n+p}{p}}
belongs to
W^{3}_{p}(\Omega)
and that there exists a constant
\hat{C}=\hat{C}(n,p,d)>0
such that

(3.6)

\int_{\Omega}\bigl|\nabla\Delta\big(u^{\frac{n+p}{p}}\big)\bigr|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\hat{C}\int_{\Omega}u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\,\;\mathrm{d}x.

u\in W^{2}_{p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L_{n+p}(\Omega)
, we have

. For the gradient of

, we calculate

\nabla\big(u^{\frac{n+p}{p}}\big)=\tfrac{n+p}{p}u^{\frac{n}{p}}\nabla u,

and using Hölder’s inequality along with (

) for

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\big|\nabla\big(u^{\frac{n+p}{p}}\big)\big|^{p}\,\;\mathrm{d}x&=\left(\tfrac{n+p}{p}\right)^{p}\int_{\Omega}u^{n}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\;\mathrm{d}x\\

.
To verify that

Next, it follows from

and Jensen’s inequality that there is a constant

such that

|\nabla\Delta(u^{\frac{n+p}{p}})|^{p}\leq\tilde{c}\bigl(u^{n-2p}|\nabla u|^{3p}+u^{n-p}|D^{2}u\nabla u|^{p}+u^{n-p}|\Delta u\nabla u|^{p}+u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\bigr).

The main task is to estimate the second-order term

. To this end, we compute for

the following two identities:

D^{2}u\nabla u=\tfrac{1}{s}u^{1-s}D^{2}u^{s}\nabla u-(s-1)u^{-1}|\nabla u|^{2}\nabla u\quad\text{and}\quad\Delta u\nabla u=\tfrac{1}{s}u^{1-s}\Delta u^{s}\nabla u-(s-1)u^{-1}|\nabla u|^{2}\nabla u.

Multiplying these identities by
u^{\frac{n-p}{p}}
, we rewrite them as

(3.9)

u^{\frac{n-p}{p}}D^{2}u\nabla u=\tfrac{1}{s}u^{\frac{n}{p}-s}D^{2}u^{s}\nabla u-(s-1)u^{\frac{n-2p}{p}}|\nabla u|^{2}\nabla u

and

(3.10)

u^{\frac{n-p}{p}}\Delta u\nabla u=\tfrac{1}{s}u^{\frac{n}{p}-s}\Delta u^{s}\nabla u-(s-1)u^{\frac{n-2p}{p}}|\nabla u|^{2}\nabla u.

For the term
u^{\frac{n-2}{p}}|\nabla u|^{2}\nabla u
, appearing in (

) and (
3.10
), a straightforward calculation gives the identity

u^{\frac{n-2p}{p}}|\nabla u|^{2}\nabla u=\left(\tfrac{3p}{n+p}\right)^{3}|\nabla\big(u^{\frac{n+p}{3p}}\big)|^{2}\ \nabla\big(u^{\frac{n+p}{3p}}\big).

Substituting this identity back into (
3.9
) and (
3.10
), and then applying Jensen’s inequality, results in

u^{n-p}|D^{2}u\nabla u|^{p}\leq\hat{c}\left(u^{n-sp}|D^{2}u^{s}\nabla u|^{p}+|\nabla\big(u^{\frac{n+p}{3p}}\big)|^{3p}\right)

and

u^{n-p}|\Delta u\nabla u|^{p}\leq\hat{c}\left(u^{n-sp}|\Delta u^{s}\nabla u|^{p}+|\nabla\big(u^{\frac{n+p}{3p}}\big)|^{3p}\right)

with a constant
\hat{c}>0
, depending only on
n
,
p
and
s
. Setting
s=\frac{2(n+p)}{3p}
, it follows from (
3.8
), by applying the above inequalities and Hölder’s inequality, along with (
1.3
) and the estimates from the first and second part of (
1.7
) with
\varphi\equiv 1
, that

(3.11)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\big|\nabla\Delta\big(u^{\frac{n+p}{p}}\big)\big|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq\tilde{c}\int_{\Omega}\bigl(u^{n-2p}|\nabla u|^{3p}+u^{n-p}|D^{2}u\nabla u|^{p}+u^{n-p}|\Delta u\nabla u|^{p}+u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq c_{\ast}\left(\int_{\Omega}u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\big(u^{\frac{n+p}{3p}}\big)|^{3p}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}u^{\frac{n-2p}{3}}|\Delta\big(u^{\frac{2(n+p)}{3p}}\big)\nabla u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\right.\\
&\quad+\left.\int_{\Omega}u^{\frac{n-2p}{3}}|D^{2}\big(u^{\frac{2(n+p)}{3p}}\big)\nabla u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\\
&\leq c_{\ast}\int_{\Omega}u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x+c_{\ast}\left(\int_{\Omega}u^{n-2p}|\nabla u|^{3p}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\Delta\big(u^{\frac{2(n+p)}{3p}}\big)|^{\frac{3p}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}\\
&\quad+c_{\ast}\left(\int_{\Omega}u^{n-2p}|\nabla u|^{3p}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|D^{2}\big(u^{\frac{2(n+p)}{3p}}\big)|^{\frac{3p}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}\\
&\leq c_{\ast}\int_{\Omega}u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x+c_{\ast}\left(\int_{\Omega}u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|D^{2}\big(u^{\frac{2(n+p)}{3p}}\big)|^{\frac{3p}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{3}},\end{split}

where
c_{\ast}>0
is a generic constant that may change from line to line, but depends only on
n
,
p
, and
d
.
Due to the fact that
D^{2}\big(u^{\frac{2(n+p)}{3p}}\big)\in L_{\frac{3p}{2}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
, we deduce that

\nabla\Delta\big(u^{\frac{n+p}{p}}\big)\in L_{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Therefore, applying again elliptic
L_{p}
-theory (see
[
Gri11
, Thm. 2.5.5.1]
), we conclude that
u^{\frac{n+p}{p}}\in W^{3}_{p}(\Omega)
. Furthermore, using
[
Gri11
, Thm. 2.3.3.6]
, we obtain the estimate

(3.12)

\begin{split}\|D^{2}\big(u^{\frac{2(n+p)}{3p}}\big)\|_{L_{\frac{3p}{2}}(\Omega)}&\leq c^{\ast}\Bigl(\|\Delta\big(u^{\frac{2(n+p)}{3p}}\big)\|_{L_{\frac{3p}{2}}(\Omega)}+\|u^{\frac{2(n+p)}{3p}}\|_{L_{\frac{3p}{2}}(\Omega)}\Bigr)\\
&\leq c^{\ast}\Bigl(\|\Delta\big(u^{\frac{2(n+p)}{3p}}\big)\|_{L_{\frac{3p}{2}}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla\big(u^{\frac{2(n+p)}{3p}}\big)\|_{L_{\frac{3p}{2}}(\Omega)}\Bigr)\end{split}

for some generic constant
c^{\ast}>0
depending on
n
,
p
, and
\Omega
. To establish inequality (
3.6
) we insert the elliptic estimate (
3.12
) into (
3.11
) and apply Young’s inequality as well as the first two statements of the theorem. This leads to

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\big|\nabla\Delta\big(u^{\frac{n+p}{p}}\big)\big|^{p}\,\;\mathrm{d}x&\leq c_{\ast}\left(\int_{\Omega}u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}|\Delta\big(u^{\frac{2(n+p)}{3p}}\big)|^{\frac{3p}{2}}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\big(u^{\frac{2(n+p)}{3p}}\big)|^{\frac{3p}{2}}\;\mathrm{d}x\right)\\
&\leq c_{\ast}\int_{\Omega}u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

Thus, the proof is complete.
∎

We can improve the estimates in Theorem
1.3
to localized versions as follows.

Corollary 3.1.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.3
we have the additional weighted estimates

\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}\Big|\nabla\big(u^{\frac{n+p}{3p}}\big)\Big|^{3p}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}\Big|\Delta\big(u^{\frac{2(n+p)}{3p}}\big)\Big|^{\frac{3p}{2}}\;\mathrm{d}x\leq c_{5}\left(\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{3p}u^{n+p}\;\mathrm{d}x\right)

for all non-negative test functions
\varphi\in W^{1}_{\infty}(\Omega)
satisfying
\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{3p}u^{n+p}\,dx<\infty
,
and where
c_{5}
is a positive constant depending only on
n
,
p
, and
d
.

The proof of these two estimates is already contained in the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Remark 3.2.

In Corollary
3.1
, we address the estimates for the first- and second-order terms. However, for the third-order term, we can only establish the following result. Assuming, in addition to the assumptions of Theorem
1.3
, that an elliptic estimate of the form

(3.13)

\int_{\Omega}\psi|D^{2}v|^{s}\;\mathrm{d}x\leq c_{s}\left(\int_{\Omega}\psi|\Delta v|^{s}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}|D^{2}v|^{s-1}|\nabla\psi|\,|\nabla v|\;\mathrm{d}x+\text{l.o.t.}\right),

for
1<s<\infty
and suitable test functions
\psi\geq 0
, holds true for solutions
v
to the Neumann problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta v+v=f\quad\text{in }\Omega\\
\nabla v\cdot\nu=0\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

then we also get the weighted estimate

(3.14)

\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}\Big|\nabla\Delta\big(u^{\frac{n+p}{p}}\big)\Big|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x\leq c_{6}\left(\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{3p}u^{n+p}\;\mathrm{d}x\right)

for suitable non-negative test functions
\varphi
satisfying
\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{3p}u^{n+p}\,dx<\infty
and for some constant
c_{6}=c_{6}(n,p,d)>0
.
The authors are not aware whether an estimate of the form (
3.13
) holds true for the Neumann problem.
For the Dirichlet case, weighted elliptic estimates are available (cf.
[
CD18
,
DST08
,
DST10
]
), provided the weight belongs to some Muckenhoupt class. In this case, the second integral on the right-hand side of (
3.13
) becomes superfluous.
However, since the test functions
\varphi
in this paper might vanish on sets of positive measure, they do not belong to the Muckenhoupt class. As a result, (
3.13
), if it holds at all, would require the additional integral involving lower-order terms. It is also possible that the test function might need
W^{2}_{\infty}
-regularity for (
3.13
) to be valid.

Sketch of proof of (
3.14
) under the assumption (
3.13
). .

If an estimate of the form (
3.13
) is satisfied, where we ignore the lower-order terms in the calculation, we can choose

\psi=\varphi^{3p},\quad v=u^{\frac{2(n+p)}{3p}}\quad\text{and}\quad s=\frac{3p}{2}

and apply Hölder’s and Young’s inequalities to obtain

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}|D^{2}(u^{\frac{2(n+p)}{3p}})|^{\frac{3p}{2}}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq c_{p}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}|\Delta(u^{\frac{2(n+p)}{3p}})|^{\frac{3p}{2}}\;\mathrm{d}x+2c_{p}(n+p)\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p-1}u^{\frac{2n-p}{3p}}|D^{2}(u^{\frac{2(n+p)}{3p}})|^{\frac{3p-2}{2}}|\nabla\varphi||\nabla u|\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq c_{p}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}|\Delta(u^{\frac{2(n+p)}{3p}})|^{\frac{3p}{2}}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad+2c_{p}(n+p)\left(\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}|D^{2}(u^{\frac{2(n+p)}{3p}})|^{\frac{3p}{2}}\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{3p-2}{3p}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{3p}u^{n+p}\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{3p}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p}|\nabla u|^{3p}\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{3p}}\\
&\leq c_{p}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}|\Delta(u^{\frac{2(n+p)}{3p}})|^{\frac{3p}{2}}\;\mathrm{d}x+\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}|D^{2}(u^{\frac{2(n+p)}{3p}})|^{\frac{3p}{2}}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad+C_{\varepsilon}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{3p}u^{n+p}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n-2p}|\nabla u|^{3p}\;\mathrm{d}x\right)\end{split}

for some
0<\varepsilon<1
, where
C_{\varepsilon}>0
depends only on
n
,
p
, and
\varepsilon
.
Absorbing the second term on the right-hand side in the left-hand side, estimating the first term by (
1.7
) and the fourth term by (
1.3
), we obtain the weighted estimate

(3.15)

\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}|D^{2}(u^{\frac{2(n+p)}{3p}})|^{\frac{3p}{2}}\;\mathrm{d}x\leq c_{7}\left(\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{3p}u^{n}|\nabla\Delta u|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{3p}u^{n+p}\;\mathrm{d}x\right)

with a constant
c_{7}>0
depending on
n
,
p
, and
d
. Following the steps for (
3.11
) and using (
3.15
) we finally obtain (
3.14
).
∎

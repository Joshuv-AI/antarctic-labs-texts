Efficient iterative techniques for solving tensor problems with the T-product Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author


Abstract
. This paper develops two efficient iterative methods for solving tensor equations under the T-product framework. For T-symmetric positive definite tensor equations of the form 𝒞 ⋆ 𝒳 = 𝒟 \mathcal{C}\star\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{D} , we propose a conjugate-gradient-type algorithm that generates orthogonal residual and 𝒞 \mathcal{C} -orthogonal direction sequences, ensuring convergence within a finite number of steps. For general consistent tensor equations, we extend the method using a normal-equation transformation, and further adapt it to handle inconsistent systems by solving a least-squares minimization problem. Key advantages include direct tensor-based computations without explicit matrix expansion, rigorous finite-step convergence proofs, and the ability to obtain minimal Frobenius norm solutions. Numerical experiments on synthetic data, benchmark images, and video sequences demonstrate that the proposed algorithms achieve high precision with low computational time, confirming their practicality for large-scale multidimensional problems. Keywords : Iterative algorithm, Orthogonal tensor sequence, Tensors, T-product, Tensor equation. 2010 AMS Subject Classification : 15A69, 65F10.

Definition 2.1

(
[

)
.

The discrete Fourier transform (DFT) matrix

has the following form

F_{n}=\begin{bmatrix}1&1&1&\ldots&1\\
1&\vartheta&\vartheta^{2}&\ldots&\vartheta^{(n-1)}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
1&\vartheta^{n-1}&\vartheta^{2(n-1})&\ldots&\vartheta^{(n-1)(n-1)}\\
\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n},

where
\vartheta=e^{-\frac{2\pi i}{n}}

Notice that
\dfrac{F_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}
is an orthogonal matrix,

F_{n}^{\ast}F_{n}=F_{n}F_{n}^{\ast}=nI_{n}.

Thus,
F_{n}^{-1}=\dfrac{F_{n}^{\ast}}{n}
. For a tensor
\mathscr{C}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}
, we define
\overline{\mathscr{C}}\in\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}

\mathscr{C}
. By using the Matlab command
\mathtt{fft}

\overline{\mathscr{C}}=\mathtt{fft}(\mathscr{C},[],3).

The original tensor
\mathscr{C}
can be recovered from
\overline{\mathscr{C}}
using the inverse
\mathtt{fft}

\mathscr{C}=\mathtt{ifft}(\overline{\mathscr{C}},[],3).

The block diagonal matrix
\overline{C}\in\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}n_{3}\times n_{2}n_{3}}
is defined as

\overline{C}=\mathtt{bdiag}(\overline{\mathscr{C}})=\begin{bmatrix}\overline{C}^{(1)}&\\
&\overline{C}^{(2)}&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&&\overline{C}^{(n_{3})}\\
\end{bmatrix},

in which
\overline{C}^{(i)}
is
i\mathrm{th}
frontal slice of
\overline{\mathscr{C}}
. The block circulant matrix
\mathtt{bcirc}(\mathscr{C})\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}n_{3}\times n_{2}n_{3}}
is obtained of
\mathscr{C}
by

\mathtt{bcirc}(\mathscr{C})=\begin{bmatrix}C^{(1)}&C^{(n_{3})}&\ldots&C^{(2)}\\
C^{(2)}&C^{(1)}&\ldots&C^{(3)}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
C^{(n_{3})}&C^{(n_{3}-1)}&\ldots&C^{(1)}\\
\end{bmatrix}.

The Tensor-Tensor Product

Some properties of the T-product are considered in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
11
]
)
.

Let
\mathscr{C},\mathscr{D}
and
\mathscr{E}
be tensors of adequate sizes, then the following results hold.

•

(Left distributivity):
\mathscr{C}\star(\mathscr{D}+\mathscr{E})=\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{D}+\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{E}
.

•

(Right distributivity):

(\mathscr{C}+\mathscr{D})\star\mathscr{E}=\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{E}+\mathscr{D}\star\mathscr{E}
.

•

(Associativity):
\mathscr{C}\star(\mathscr{D}\star\mathscr{E})=(\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{D})\star\mathscr{E}
.

Definition 2.5

(

Let

Definition 2.6

(
[

]
)

The trace of a tensor

The following equalities hold for arbitrary tensors

•

•

Definition 2.9

3.
An algorithm for T-symmetric positive definite tensor equations

This section presents a method to solve the equation
\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{X}=\mathscr{D}
,
in which
\mathscr{C}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n\times n_{3}}
is a T-symmetric positive definite tensor,
\mathscr{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times l\times n_{3}}
and
\mathscr{D}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times l\times n_{3}}
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
f:n\times l\times n_{3}\rightarrow l\times l\times n_{3}
be a map on tensors, and
\mathscr{P}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times l\times n_{3}}
with
{\parallel\mathscr{P}\parallel}=1
. The derivative of the function
F(\mathscr{X})=Tr((f(\mathscr{X}))^{(1)})
at
\mathscr{X}
along the direction
\mathscr{P}
is given by

\frac{\partial F}{\partial\mathscr{X}}=\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{Tr[(f(\mathscr{X}+t\mathscr{P}))^{(1)}]-Tr[(f(\mathscr{X}))^{(1)}]}{t}.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathscr{C}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n\times n_{3}}
be T-symmetric positive definite,
\mathscr{D}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times l\times n_{3}}
,
\mathscr{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times l\times n_{3}}
and
\mathscr{P}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times l\times n_{3}}
with
{\parallel\mathscr{P}\parallel}=1
.
If
f(\mathscr{X})=\frac{1}{2}\mathscr{X}^{T}\star\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{X}-\mathscr{X}^{T}\star\mathscr{D}
, then

\frac{\partial F}{\partial\mathscr{X}}=Tr\bigg[\big((\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{X}-\mathscr{D})^{T}\star\mathscr{P}\big)^{(1)}\bigg].

Proof.

By using the known properties of the transpose of a tensor under the T-product, we can write

\displaystyle f(\mathscr{X}+t\mathscr{P})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\mathscr{X}+t\mathscr{P})^{T}\star\mathscr{C}\star(\mathscr{X}+t\mathscr{P})-(\mathscr{X}+t\mathscr{P})^{T}\star\mathscr{D}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathscr{X}^{T}\star\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{X}+\frac{1}{2}t\big(\mathscr{X}^{T}\star\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{P}+\mathscr{P}^{T}\star\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{X}\big)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}t^{2}\mathscr{P}^{T}\star\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{P}-\mathscr{X}^{T}\star\mathscr{D}-t\mathscr{P}^{T}\star\mathscr{D}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(\mathscr{X})+\frac{1}{2}t\big(\mathscr{X}^{T}\star\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{P}+\mathscr{P}^{T}\star\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{X}\big)-t\mathscr{P}^{T}\star\mathscr{D}+\frac{1}{2}t^{2}\mathscr{P}^{T}\star\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{P}.

Thus,

(f(\mathscr{X}+t\mathscr{P}))^{(1)}=\bigg(f(\mathscr{X})+\frac{1}{2}t\big(\mathscr{X}^{T}\star\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{P}+\mathscr{P}^{T}\star\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{X}\big)-t\mathscr{P}^{T}\star\mathscr{D}+\frac{1}{2}t^{2}\mathscr{P}^{T}\star\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{P}\bigg)^{(1)}.

Definition
3.1
allows us to conclude that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial F}{\partial\mathscr{X}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{Tr[(f(\mathscr{X}+t\mathscr{P}))^{(1)}]-Tr[(f(\mathscr{X}))^{(1)}]}{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}Tr[(\mathscr{X}^{T}\star\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{P}+\mathscr{P}^{T}\star\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{X})^{(1)}]-Tr[(\mathscr{P}^{T}\star\mathscr{D})^{(1)}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Tr\bigg[\big((\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{X}-\mathscr{D})^{T}\star\mathscr{P}\big)^{(1)}\bigg].

∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\mathscr{C}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n\times n_{3}}
be T-symmetric positive definite, and
\mathscr{D}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times l\times n_{3}}
. Then,
\mathscr{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times l\times n_{3}}
solves the equation
\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{X}=\mathscr{D}
if and only if
\mathscr{X}
solves the problem

\min_{\mathscr{Y}}Tr((f(\mathscr{Y}))^{1}),

(3.1)

where
f(\mathscr{Y})=\frac{1}{2}\mathscr{Y}^{T}\star\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{Y}-\mathscr{Y}^{T}\star\mathscr{D}
and
(f(\mathscr{Y}))^{1}
is the first frontal slice of
f(\mathscr{Y})
.

Proof.

First, we suppose that
\mathscr{X}
solves the equation
\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{X}=\mathscr{D}
. Now, for each
\mathscr{Y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times l\times n_{3}}
,

\displaystyle f(\mathscr{Y})-f(\mathscr{X})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathscr{Y}^{T}\star\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{Y}-\mathscr{Y}^{T}\star\mathscr{D}-\frac{1}{2}\mathscr{X}^{T}\star\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{X}+\mathscr{X}^{T}\star\mathscr{D}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\mathscr{Y}-\mathscr{X})^{T}\star\mathscr{C}\star(\mathscr{Y}-\mathscr{X})+\frac{1}{2}(\mathscr{X}^{T}\star\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{Y}+\mathscr{Y}^{T}\star\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{X})

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\mathscr{Y}^{T}\star\mathscr{D}-\mathscr{X}^{T}\star\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{X}+\mathscr{X}^{T}\star\mathscr{D}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\mathscr{Y}-\mathscr{X})^{T}\star\mathscr{C}\star(\mathscr{Y}-\mathscr{X})+\frac{1}{2}(\mathscr{D}^{T}\star\mathscr{Y}+\mathscr{Y}^{T}\star\mathscr{D})

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\mathscr{Y}^{T}\star\mathscr{D}-\mathscr{X}^{T}\star\mathscr{D}+\mathscr{X}^{T}\star\mathscr{D}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\mathscr{Y}-\mathscr{X})^{T}\star\mathscr{C}\star(\mathscr{Y}-\mathscr{X})+\frac{1}{2}(\mathscr{D}^{T}\star\mathscr{Y}-\mathscr{Y}^{T}\star\mathscr{D}).

The definition of the transpose under T-product concludes that if
\mathscr{S}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k\times j}
, then
Tr((\mathscr{S})^{(1)})=Tr((\mathscr{S}^{T})^{(1)})
. Also, since
\mathscr{C}
is T-symmetric positive definite,

\displaystyle Tr[(f(\mathscr{Y})-f(\mathscr{X})^{(1)}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}Tr\bigg[\bigg(\frac{1}{2}(\mathscr{Y}-\mathscr{X})^{T}\star\mathscr{C}\star(\mathscr{Y}-\mathscr{X})+\frac{1}{2}(\mathscr{D}^{T}\star\mathscr{Y}-\mathscr{Y}^{T}\star\mathscr{D})\bigg)^{(1)}\bigg]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}Tr[((\mathscr{Y}-\mathscr{X})^{T}\star\mathscr{C}\star(\mathscr{Y}-\mathscr{X}))^{(1)}]\geq 0.

Thus,
\mathscr{X}
solves the optimization problem (
3.1
).

On the other hand, let
\mathscr{E}_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times l\times n_{3}}
be a tensor with
e_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}=1
whose other entries are equal to 0. Now, let
Tr((f(\mathscr{X}))^{(1)})=\min\limits_{\mathscr{Y}}Tr((f(\mathscr{Y}))^{(1)})
. Then, for each

.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let two tensor sequences
\{\mathscr{R}_{i}\}
and
\{\mathscr{Q}_{i}\}
be generated in Algorithm
3
, where
\mathscr{R}_{i}\neq\mathscr{O}
and
i=1,\dots,k
. Then,

\displaystyle<\mathscr{R}_{j},\mathscr{R}_{i}>=0,\qquad<\mathscr{Q}_{j},\mathscr{Q}_{i}>=0,\quad\forall i,j=1,\dots,k,\quad i\neq j.

(4.3)

Proof.

We use induction on
k
to prove this lemma. Let
k=2
. Then, by using Lemma
4.1
,

\displaystyle Tr((\mathscr{R}_{2}^{T}\star\mathscr{R}_{1})^{(1)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Tr((\mathscr{R}_{1}^{T}\star\mathscr{R}_{1})^{(1)})-\frac{\parallel\mathscr{R}_{1}\parallel^{2}}{\parallel\mathscr{Q}_{1}\parallel^{2}}Tr((\mathscr{Q}_{1}^{T}\star\mathscr{P}_{1})^{(1)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\parallel\mathscr{R}_{1}\parallel^{2}-\frac{\parallel\mathscr{R}_{1}\parallel^{2}}{\parallel\mathscr{Q}_{1}\parallel^{2}}Tr((\mathscr{Q}_{1}^{T}\star\mathscr{Q}_{1})^{(1)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Also,

\displaystyle Tr((\mathscr{Q}_{2}^{T}\star\mathscr{Q}_{1})^{(1)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Tr[((\mathscr{P}_{2}-\frac{Tr((\mathscr{P}_{2}^{T}\star\mathscr{Q}_{1})^{(1)})}{\parallel\mathscr{Q}_{1}\parallel^{2}}\mathscr{Q}_{1})^{T}\star\mathscr{Q}_{1})^{(1)}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Tr((\mathscr{P}_{2}^{T}\star\mathscr{Q}_{1})^{(1)})-\frac{Tr((\mathscr{P}_{2}^{T}\star\mathscr{Q}_{1})^{(1)})}{\parallel\mathscr{Q}_{1}\parallel^{2}}Tr((\mathscr{Q}_{1}^{T}\star\mathscr{Q}_{1})^{(1)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

If relation (
4.3
) holds for
k=1,2,\dots,n
, then

\displaystyle Tr((\mathscr{R}_{n+1}^{T}\star\mathscr{R}_{j})^{(1)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Tr((\mathscr{R}_{n}^{T}\star\mathscr{R}_{j})^{(1)})-\frac{\parallel\mathscr{R}_{n}\parallel^{2}}{\parallel\mathscr{Q}_{n}\parallel^{2}}Tr((\mathscr{Q}_{n}^{T}\star\mathscr{P}_{j})^{(1)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Tr((\mathscr{R}_{n}^{T}\star\mathscr{R}_{j})^{(1)})-\frac{\parallel\mathscr{R}_{n}\parallel^{2}}{\parallel\mathscr{Q}_{n}\parallel^{2}}Tr[(\mathscr{Q}_{n}^{T}\star(\mathscr{Q}_{j}+\frac{Tr((\mathscr{P}_{j}^{T}\star\mathscr{Q}_{j-1})^{(1)})}{\parallel\mathscr{Q}_{j-1}\parallel^{2}}\mathscr{Q}_{j-1}))^{(1)}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

where
j=1,2,\dots,n-1
. Now, let
j=n
. Then,

\displaystyle Tr((\mathscr{R}_{n+1}^{T}\star\mathscr{R}_{n})^{(1)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Tr((\mathscr{R}_{n}^{T}\star\mathscr{R}_{n})^{(1)})-\frac{\parallel\mathscr{R}_{n}\parallel^{2}}{\parallel\mathscr{Q}_{n}\parallel^{2}}Tr((\mathscr{Q}_{n}^{T}\star\mathscr{P}_{n})^{(1)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\parallel\mathscr{R}_{n}\parallel^{2}-\frac{\parallel\mathscr{R}_{n}\parallel^{2}}{\parallel\mathscr{Q}_{n}\parallel^{2}}Tr\bigg[(\mathscr{Q}_{n}^{T}\star(\mathscr{Q}_{n}+\frac{Tr((\mathscr{P}_{n}^{T}\star\mathscr{Q}_{n-1})^{(1)})}{\parallel\mathscr{Q}_{n-1}\parallel^{2}}\mathscr{Q}_{n-1}))^{(1)}\bigg]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

In what follows, we consider the sequence
\{\mathscr{Q}_{i}\}
and prove its orthogonality. We can write

\displaystyle Tr((\mathscr{Q}_{n+1}^{T}\star\mathscr{Q}_{j})^{(1)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Tr[((\mathscr{P}_{n+1}-\frac{Tr((\mathscr{P}_{n+1}^{T}\star\mathscr{Q}_{n})^{(1)})}{\parallel\mathscr{Q}_{n}\parallel^{2}}\mathscr{Q}_{n})^{T}\star\mathscr{Q}_{j})^{(1)}]

(4.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Tr((\mathscr{P}_{n+1}^{T}\star\mathscr{Q}_{j})^{(1)})-\frac{Tr((\mathscr{P}_{n+1}^{T}\star\mathscr{Q}_{n})^{(1)})}{\parallel\mathscr{Q}_{n}\parallel^{2}}Tr((\mathscr{Q}_{n}^{T}\star\mathscr{Q}_{j})^{(1)}).

If we put
j=n
, then equation (
4.4
) becomes zero. If
j=1,2,\dots,n-1
, then Lemma
4.1
allows us to conclude that

\displaystyle Tr((\mathscr{Q}_{n+1}^{T}\star\mathscr{Q}_{j})^{(1)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Tr((\mathscr{P}_{n+1}^{T}\star\mathscr{Q}_{j})^{(1)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Tr((\mathscr{Q}_{j}^{T}\star\mathscr{P}_{n+1})^{(1)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\parallel\mathscr{Q}_{j}\parallel^{2}}{\parallel\mathscr{R}_{j}\parallel^{2}}\bigg[Tr((\mathscr{R}_{j}^{T}\star\mathscr{R}_{n+1})^{(1)})-Tr((\mathscr{R}_{j+1}^{T}\star\mathscr{R}_{n+1})^{(1)})\bigg]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

∎

5.
An algorithm for solving inconsistent tensor equations

The solutions
\tilde{\mathscr{X}}
of the optimization problem
\min\limits_{\mathscr{X}}\parallel\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{X}-\mathscr{D}\parallel
are called the least-square solutions of the tensor equation
\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{X}=\mathscr{D}
. In the following, we consider the inconsistent equation
\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{X}=\mathscr{D}
and compose an iterative approach to solve the problem
\min\limits_{\mathscr{X}}\parallel\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{X}-\mathscr{D}\parallel
, in which
\mathscr{C}
,
\mathscr{X}
and
\mathscr{D}
are tensors with edequate size.

Lemma 5.1
.

If
\tilde{\mathscr{X}}
is the minimizer of

\displaystyle\varphi(\mathscr{X})=\parallel\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{X}-\mathscr{D}\parallel^{2},

(5.1)

where
\mathscr{C}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}
,
\mathscr{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}\times l\times n_{3}}
and
\mathscr{D}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times l\times n_{3}}
, then
\tilde{\mathscr{X}}
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}^{T}\star\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{X}=\mathscr{C}^{T}\star\mathscr{D}.

(5.2)

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{E}_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}\times l\times n_{3}}
be a tensor with
e_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}=1
whose other entries are equal to 0. Now, let
\parallel\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{X}-\mathscr{D}\parallel^{2}=\min\limits_{\mathscr{Y}}\parallel\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{Y}-\mathscr{D}\parallel^{2}
. Then, for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
and any tensor
\mathscr{E}_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}
we can write

\displaystyle\varphi(\mathscr{X})\leq\varphi(\mathscr{X}+t\mathscr{E}_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle Tr[((\mathscr{X}^{T}\star\mathscr{C}^{T})\star(\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{X})-2\mathscr{D}^{T}\star(\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{X})+2\mathscr{D}^{T}\star\mathscr{D})^{(1)}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Tr[(((\mathscr{X}+t\mathscr{E}_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}})^{T}\star\mathscr{C}^{T})\star(\mathscr{C}\star(\mathscr{X}+t\mathscr{E}_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}))

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle 2\mathscr{D}^{T}\star(\mathscr{C}\star(\mathscr{X}+t\mathscr{E}_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}))+2\mathscr{D}^{T}\star\mathscr{D})^{(1)}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Tr[(\mathscr{X}^{T}\star\mathscr{C}^{T}\star\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{X}-2\mathscr{D}^{T}\star\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{X}+2\mathscr{D}^{T}\star\mathscr{D}+t(\mathscr{X}^{T}\star\mathscr{C}^{T}\star\mathscr{C})\star\mathscr{E}_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle t\mathscr{E}_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}^{T}\star(\mathscr{C}^{T}\star\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{X})+t^{2}\mathscr{E}_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}^{T}\star(\mathscr{C}^{T}\star\mathscr{C})\star\mathscr{E}_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle 2t(\mathscr{D}^{T}\star\mathscr{C})\star\mathscr{E}_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}})^{(1)}].

Then,

\displaystyle Tr[(t(\mathscr{X}^{T}\star\mathscr{C}^{T}\star\mathscr{C})\star\mathscr{E}_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle t\mathscr{E}_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}^{T}\star(\mathscr{C}^{T}\star\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{X})+t^{2}\mathscr{E}_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}^{T}\star(\mathscr{C}^{T}\star\mathscr{C})\star\mathscr{E}_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle 2t(\mathscr{D}^{T}\star\mathscr{C})\star\mathscr{E}_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}})^{(1)}]\geq 0.

Thus, for each
t
,

\displaystyle t\bigg[Tr(2\mathscr{E}_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}^{T}\star(\mathscr{C}^{T}\star\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{X})+t\mathscr{E}_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}^{T}\star(\mathscr{C}^{T}\star\mathscr{C})\star\mathscr{E}_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}-2\mathscr{E}_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}^{T}\star(\mathscr{C}^{T}\star\mathscr{D}))^{(1)}\bigg]\geq 0.

Hence,

\displaystyle Tr[(2\mathscr{E}_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}^{T}\star(\mathscr{C}^{T}\star\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{X}-\mathscr{C}^{T}\star\mathscr{D}))^{(1)}]=0.

Thus, for every
1\leq i_{1}\leq n_{2}
,
1\leq i_{2}\leq l
and
1\leq i_{3}\leq n_{3}
,
\mathscr{E}_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}^{T}\star(\mathscr{C}^{T}\star\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{X}-\mathscr{C}^{T}\star\mathscr{D})_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}=0
, and this shows that
\mathscr{C}^{T}\star\mathscr{C}\star\mathscr{X}=\mathscr{C}^{T}\star\mathscr{D}
.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

Please note that the general method for solving
\mathcal{C}\star\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{D}
can be implemented in a direct way, namely via the tubal inverse:
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{C}^{-1}\star\mathcal{D}
(or
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}\star\mathcal{D}
when
\mathcal{C}
is singular). This approach requires inverting
n_{3}
matrices of size
n_{1}\times n_{1}
after an FFT. It becomes impractical for large
n_{1}
or
n_{3}
, especially when
\mathcal{C}
has structure (e.g., sparsity) that cannot be exploited. Iterative methods like the ones proposed here are preferable: they avoid explicit inversion, have lower computational complexity (
O(kn_{1}n_{2}n_{3}\log n_{3})
versus
O(n_{3}n_{1}^{3})
), require less memory, and can obtain minimal-norm solutions. To our knowledge, no other iterative algorithms have been developed for T-product tensor equations, making direct comparison infeasible.

7.
Conclusion

This paper proposed two efficient iterative algorithms for solving tensor equations under the T-product framework. For T-symmetric positive definite equations
\mathcal{C}\star\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{D}
, a conjugate-gradient-type method was developed with guaranteed convergence in at most
n_{1}n_{3}+1
steps. For general consistent equations, a normal-equation transformation was used, and for inconsistent systems, a least-squares formulation was adopted. Rigorous finite-step convergence proofs were established, and minimal Frobenius norm solutions were obtained via appropriate initialization.

The methods operate directly on tensors, avoid explicit inversion, and achieve lower complexity,
\mathcal{O}(kn_{1}n_{2}n_{3}\log n_{3})
versus
\mathcal{O}(n_{3}n_{1}^{3})
for direct inversion. Numerical experiments on synthetic data, images, and videos confirmed high precision, a
2.31\times
speedup, and
26\%
memory usage compared to direct tubal inversion.

Future work includes preconditioning, structured tensors, parallel implementations, and extensions to other tensor products and applications.

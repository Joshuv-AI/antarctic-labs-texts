Three vs 12-month DAPT after implantation of biodegradable-polymer sirolimus-eluting coronary stent: a randomised clinical trial

ABSTRACT
BackgroundEarly discontinuation of dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) within 3 months after implantation of drug-eluting stents may reduce bleeding risk without increasing ischaemic events. This study aimed to assess whether 3-month DAPT is as safe as the conventional 12-month regimen in patients treated with Firehawk stents, which have demonstrated 99.9% reendothelialisation at 3 months by optical coherence tomography.MethodsFrom January 1, 2019, to April 30, 2022, 2445 patients were randomly assigned to a 3-month (n = 1222) or 12-month DAPT regimen (n = 1223) in this randomised, open-label, non-inferiority trial, patients from 36 centres in China who underwent percutaneous coronary intervention with Firehawk stents. The primary endpoint was a composite of all-cause death, myocardial infarction, cerebrovascular accident, and major bleeding (Bleeding Academic Research Consortium type 2, 3, or 5) at 18 months. The co-equal secondary endpoints were major adverse cardiovascular events, defined as a composite of all-cause death, myocardial infarction and ischaemia-driven target lesion revascularisation, and major bleeding.ResultsFrom January 1, 2019, to April 30, 2022, 2445 patients were randomly assigned to 3-month (N = 1222) or 12-month DAPT regimen (N = 1223). Adherence to the protocol-defined DAPT duration was 71.0% and 95.5%, respectively. Rates of the primary endpoint were comparable between both groups (10.1% vs 10.9%). Non-inferiority of 3-month DAPT was established (absolute rate difference, − 0.76%; upper limit of 1-sided 97.5% CI, 1.70%; p non-inferiority = 0.0003, with a predefined non-inferiority margin of 3.5%). Rates of co-secondary endpoints showed no significant difference (both p > 0.05). Landmark analysis (3–18 months) showed significant lower rate of major bleeding (2.7% vs 4.4%; p = 0.03) with 3-month DAPT.ConclusionsIn patients treated with Firehawk stents, 3-month DAPT was non-inferior to 12-month DAPT for the primary composite endpoint of all-cause death, myocardial infarction, cerebrovascular accident, and major bleeding at 18 months.Trial registrationNCT03008083 (clinicaltrials.gov).Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12916-025-04515-y.

Background

Millions of patients undergo percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) with stent implantation each year [1, 2]. Drug-eluting stent (DES) has reduced restenosis and thrombosis but increased late and very late stent thrombosis [3]. Current guidelines recommend 6 months of dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) for stable coronary artery disease and 12 months DAPT for acute coronary syndrome (ACS) after stent implantation [4–6]. Shortened DAPT is recommended for high bleeding risk patients, yet prolonged therapy confers an increased bleeding risk [7, 8]. With newer-generation DES, stent thrombosis has fallen to < 1% at 2 years [9–11], and recent studies have explored abbreviated DAPT in predominantly Caucasian populations [12–14]. Given the higher bleeding propensity and potential thrombotic risk in Asian populations [15, 16], defining the optimal DAPT duration remains a key clinical challenge.

Polymer, drug dosage, and strut thickness influence vessel healing, hypersensitivity reactions, and neoatherosclerosis formation, which contribute to late stent thrombosis [17–19]. To improve endothelial healing of durable polymers, biodegradable polymers have been developed, which gradually break down into biostable water and carbon dioxide, thereby reducing or eliminating long-term inflammation [20]. High drug loading in DES influenced vascular response [21], and ultrathin-strut DES caused superior outcomes [11, 22].

Although trials such as ULTIMATE-DAPT, TICO, T-PASS, TWILIGHT-ACS, and GLOBAL LEADERS have shown that ≤ 3 months of DAPT followed by P2Y12 inhibitor monotherapy is safe with third-generation DES, most included relatively low-risk patients, with event rates around 4%, or randomisation performed only after patients remained event-free at 1–3 months [23–27]. Moreover, these trials lacked direct evidence of reendothelialisation. The Firehawk stent (MicroPort) is a cobalt–chromium stent with an 86-μm strut thickness and a fully biodegradable sirolimus-containing polymer coating applied to recessed abluminal grooves on its surface, thereby reducing polymer and drug density [20, 28]. With the lowest sirolimus density of 0.30 μg/mm2, 90% of the drug is released over the course of 90 days [28]. Early healing was demonstrated with 99.9% of struts covered and < 1.2% of struts malapposition at 90 days [29]. Based on this evidence, a 3-month DAPT regimen appears safe for patients with Firehawk stents. The TARGET DAPT trial aimed to evaluate the safety and efficacy of 3-month vs 12-month DAPT in a broad population of patients undergoing PCI with Firehawk stents.

Methods

This study was reported in line with the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) Guidelines (Additional file 1).

Study design

TARGET DAPT was a multicentre, prospective, open-label, non-inferiority, randomised trial conducted across 36 sites in China. The trial was designed by the TARGET DAPT investigators, and the protocol was approved by the review boards of each participating centre. An independent data and safety monitoring board, whose members had no affiliation with any enrolling cardiology sites or the sponsor, reviewed the safety and ethical conduct of the trial. This was an open-label study in which both patients and investigators were aware of treatment allocation. However, all clinical endpoints were evaluated by an independent adjudication committee blinded to group assignments. The protocol has been published [30].

Study population

Patients with acute or chronic coronary syndromes with an indication for PCI were eligible. Participants needed to have at least 1 lesion in a native coronary vessel with a visually assessed stenosis > 50% that was suitable for PCI with stents. Patients with ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) were excluded due to their elevated risk of recurrent ischaemic events. The TARGET-FIRST trial (NCT04753749) investigated short versus standard dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) in patients with acute myocardial infarction, including those with STEMI [31]. Detailed inclusion and exclusion criteria have been published previously [30]. All participants provided written informed consent.

Randomisation

Patients were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to 3-month or 12-month DAPT within 4 h following an index procedure with Firehawk implantation. Patients received 3- or 12-month DAPT and followed by aspirin monotherapy. Randomisation was carried out using a web-based system with computer-generated block randomisation stratified by enrolling site and the presence of diabetes status. Additional stratification by ACS versus chronic coronary syndrome was not applied to avoid excessive stratification and the risk of empty strata. All patients were treated exclusively with the Firehawk stent. Patients and investigators were not blind to the treatment allocation.

Procedures

Percutaneous coronary intervention was performed using standard techniques. For patients who were not already on antiplatelet therapy, a loading dose of aspirin (300 mg) and a P2Y12 inhibitor (clopidogrel 300 mg or ticagrelor 180 mg) was administered at least 4 h before PCI. Post-procedure, DAPT continued with aspirin 100 mg daily plus clopidogrel 75 mg daily or ticagrelor 90 mg twice daily. All patients remained on 100 mg aspirin daily indefinitely, while discontinuation of the P2Y12 inhibitor followed the protocol. Given the unavailability of prasugrel in China, ticagrelor was preferentially used in ACS patients.

Following the procedure, patients were recommended to continue optimal pharmacological treatment as per current guidelines [32]. Patient visits were conducted at 3, 12, and 18 months, while follow-ups at 1, 6, 24, and 36 months were performed via telephone, with optional clinic visits available. Outcomes and compliance with DAPT were recorded. Patients who discontinue antiplatelet therapy prematurely due to bleeding or other procedures were monitored for cardiac events. Any condition requiring interruption of DAPT was recorded and DAPT was reinitiated as promptly as possible.

Outcomes

The primary endpoint was net adverse clinical and cerebral events (NACCE), defined as a composite of all-cause death, myocardial infarction (MI), cerebrovascular accident, and major bleeding classified as Bleeding Academic Research Consortium (BARC) type 2, 3, or 5, at 18 months. Co-equal secondary endpoints were major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) and major bleeding. Another secondary endpoint was stent thrombosis. MACE was defined as a composite of all-cause death, MI, and ischaemia-driven target lesion revascularisation. MI was defined by elevated cardiac biomarkers with ischaemic symptoms or electrocardiographic changes based on the Third Universal Definition [33]. Cerebrovascular accident was defined as a sudden onset, non-convulsive, focal or global neurological deficit lasting more than 24 h or resulting in death, attributed to either brain ischaemia or haemorrhage [34]. Stent thrombosis was categorised as definite or probable according to Academic Research Consortium criteria, and BARC bleeding was described in the literature [35, 36].

Statistical analysis

The sample size was calculated to assess non-inferiority of 3-month vs 12-month DAPT for NACCE at 18 months. Based on previous data [37–39], we estimated a NACCE rate of 10% at 18 months post-procedure in both groups. A non-inferiority margin was set at 3.5%. With 2446 patients (1223 per group), the study was estimated to have 80.2% power to detect non-inferiority, with a 1-sided type I error rate of 0.025, assuming a 5% loss to follow-up. To evaluate the outcomes after discontinuation of DAPT after 3 months, we performed landmark analyses from 3 to 18 months for NACCE, MACE, and major bleeding.

The intention-to-treat (ITT) population, defined as all randomised patients, was used for the primary analysis of NACCE and all secondary endpoints. A secondary analysis, including all clinical endpoints, was conducted using the per-protocol population defined as all patients adhered to assigned DAPT treatment. For the study endpoints, patients lost to follow-up were censored, and their primary endpoint assessment stopped when estimating Kaplan–Meier event rates. Prespecified subgroup analyses were conducted across the following subgroups: age (< 65 years), sex, diabetes, renal dysfunction (creatinine clearance < 60 mL/min), body mass index (< 25 kg/m2), history of MI, ACC/AHA B2/C lesions, lesion length < 20 mm, reference vessel diameter < 2.5 mm, multivessel disease, and ACS versus chronic coronary syndrome.

Categorical variables were compared using the χ2 test or Fisher exact test, while continuous variables were analysed using the Student t-test. Cumulative event rates were estimated using Kaplan–Meier methods and compared with the log-rank test. Time from randomisation to the first NACCE was used in survival analyses, with adjusted hazard rates compared using multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression. All p values and 95% CIs were 2-tailed, except for those assessing non-inferiority for the primary endpoint. Statistical analyses were performed using SAS software version 9.2 or higher (SAS Institute).

The per-protocol sensitivity analysis aligned with the primary ITT findings (Additional file 2: Table S3). NACCE occurred in 8.2% of the 3-month DAPT group and 9.6% of the 12-month group, respectively. Non-inferiority was confirmed (risk difference, − 1.38%; upper 97.5% CI, 1.15%; pnon-inferiority < 0.0001, Additional file 2: Fig. S2). We performed an additional analysis using a stricter 2% margin, which also confirmed non-inferiority. The p values for non-inferiority were 0.014 in the ITT analysis and 0.004 in the PPS analysis.

Subgroup analysis showed a significant interaction between DAPT duration and age > 65 years (p = 0.02) and renal dysfunction (p = 0.03) for NACCE. In patients with diabetes, treatment effects were consistent across DAPT durations. Discussion

This large, multicentre trial showed that in patients with acute or chronic coronary syndromes (excluding STEMI) treated with Firehawk stents, 3-month DAPT was non-inferior to 12-month DAPT for NACCE at 18 months. Early discontinuation of the P2Y12 inhibitor at 3 months lowered major bleeding rates without compromising ischaemic safety. Short-term DAPT was associated with comparable outcomes and lower bleeding risk in patients over 65 or those with renal dysfunction.

Compared with bare metal stents, DES significantly reduce restenosis but may increase late stent malapposition and thrombosis [3]. With advancements in stent design and adjunctive medical therapy, stent thrombosis has substantially declined and bleeding risk of long-term DAPT raises concern. Extending DAPT beyond 12 months does not offer additional protection against MI or cardiac death compared with aspirin monotherapy [40]. Furthermore, continuing clopidogrel therapy for more than 6 months does not reduce the incidence of death, MI, or stroke when compared with a 6-month regimen [41, 42]. A few studies reported a low incidence of ischaemic events in patients who discontinued DAPT 3 months after the index procedure [14, 43]. Reendothelialisation and early healing, as demonstrated by optical coherence tomography with the Firehawk stent, specifically, supports the rationale for 3-month DAPT after Firehawk stent implantation [29, 44].

Our study established the non-inferiority of 3-month DAPT compared with 12-month DAPT. Previous studies have provided important insights into short-term DAPT but had limitations in applicability to broader patient populations [12–14, 43, 45]. STOPDAPT trial evaluated 3-month DAPT after cobalt–chromium everolimus-eluting stent implantation but lacked a control group [43]. Low incidence of primary endpoint in RESET and OPTIMIZE trials may have influenced the non-inferiority outcomes between 3-month and 12-month DAPT followed by aspirin monotherapy [12, 45]. The SMART-CHOICE trial compared 3-month DAPT followed by P2Y12 inhibitor monotherapy with 12-month DAPT in a relatively low-risk Korean population (MACE: 2.9% vs 2.5%; bleeding: 2.0% vs 3.4%) [13]. The TWILIGHT trial, which randomised patients 3 months after PCI, excluded many with high ischaemic or bleeding risk [14]. The Firehawk stent is unique in having the lowest drug load and a biodegradable polymer. In contrast to other stents used in the RESET, OPTIMIZE, and SMART-CHOICE trials—such as Promus (Boston Scientific), Xience (Abbott), and Resolute (Medtronic)—which have drug densities ranging from 1.0 to 1.6 μg/mm2, the Firehawk stent has a sirolimus density of only 0.30 μg/mm2. Moreover, with 90% of sirolimus released within 90 days, Firehawk stents demonstrate early vessel healing, with 99.9% of struts covered and < 1.2% malapposition at 90 days [28, 29]. Such early reendothelialisation provides a strong rationale for shortening DAPT duration following Firehawk implantation. In addition, our study was conducted in a real-world population with moderately higher event rates and randomised patients immediately after stent implantation, making the findings more representative of routine clinical practice.

Aspirin remains the only antiplatelet agent with a class IA recommendation for long-term prevention of cardiovascular events in patients with coronary artery disease [5]. The pharmacodynamic variability of clopidogrel [46] associated with the cytochrome P450 2C19 loss-of-function alleles (such as 2C19*2 and 2C19*3) is notably more common in Asian populations compared with Caucasians [47]. Discontinuing a P2Y12 inhibitor instead of aspirin in daily clinical practice may improve patient compliance, reduce costs, and offer greater convenience and comfort for both patients and physicians [48], whereas aspirin and clopidogrel exhibit similar effects on the gastrointestinal mucosa [49]. As noted, several studies have compared P2Y12 inhibitor monotherapy with aspirin monotherapy for secondary prevention and found that P2Y12 inhibitors significantly reduced ischaemic events without a corresponding increase in bleeding risk [23–27]. These findings suggest that P2Y12 inhibitors is a safe and effective alternative to aspirin monotherapy [5].

There are several plausible explanations for the findings in our study. First, aspirin exerts its cardioprotective effects by irreversibly inhibiting platelet cyclooxygenase-1, thereby blocking the production of thromboxane A2 and reducing thrombus formation [50]. Second, an impaired pharmacodynamic response to clopidogrel is more frequent than with aspirin and may limit the antiplatelet effectiveness of clopidogrel [51]. Delayed reendothelialisation is a known pathophysiological trigger of stent thrombosis [52]; however, the early healing properties of the Firehawk stents may help explain the similar rate of stent-related adverse events beyond 3 months post-procedure. The effects of aspirin combined with the early healing of the Firehawk stent may together explain the similar ischaemic events with 3-month DAPT observed in our study. Lower rate of major bleeding in landmark analysis of 3-month DAPT group are consistent with reduced bleeding risk of short-term DAPT [12, 13, 45, 53].

Benefit of a short-term DAPT strategy was observed in patients over 65 years of age or those with renal dysfunction. Advanced age and renal impairment are established criteria for increased bleeding risk [54]. Patients aged 65 years or older receiving short-term DAPT after new-generation DES implantation had a reduction in major bleeding without an increase in the composite endpoint of MI, stent thrombosis, or stroke compared with younger patients [55]. The greater net benefit demonstrated in patients with renal dysfunction are consistent with a higher bleeding risk in this population [56, 57]. Moreover, reduction in major bleeding in the 3-month DAPT group in landmark analysis further supports the potential advantage of a short-term DAPT strategy in high bleeding risk patients. The majority of bleeding events were gastrointestinal, as a very low incidence of access-related bleeding events caused by transradial procedures. The subgroup results should be interpreted cautiously given the increased risk of type I error from multiple testing.

Finally, the results of our study provide additional evidence for the safety and efficacy of a reduced DAPT strategy among Chinese patients, who have a significantly higher prevalence of CYP 2C19 loss of function alleles, with poor metaboliser (2 LOF alleles) affecting 10–20% of Asians (compared to 2–5% Caucasian) [58], which has been associated with a higher risk of MI. Furthermore, our study provides evidence that a reduced DAPT strategy can safely reduce bleeding risk in Chinese patients who are inherently at higher bleeding risk [59].

This study has some limitations. First, it was an open-label rather than a double-blind trial, which may have reduced adherence in the 3-month DAPT group. However, endpoint adjudication and statistical analyses were independently conducted in a blinded manner to ensure objectivity. Second, adherence to the protocol-defined DAPT duration was suboptimal in the 3-month group (71%). Among non-adherent patients, 5 discontinued P2Y12 inhibitor due to bleeding and 22 prolonged DAPT owing to ischaemic events, with most deviations reflecting pandemic-related disruptions. The open-label design and allowance of non-standard regimens may have further contributed. In the 12-month group, 15 patients discontinued prematurely for bleeding, while the remainder extended DAPT, also likely influenced by COVID-19. Similar results of per-protocol and ITT analyses align with the SMART-DATE trial with 73.7% adherence in the 6-month DAPT group [60]. Future studies could enhance adherence through videoconferencing and improved patient education. Third, this trial started in 2018 and rates of ischaemic events were relatively high in East Asian population [15, 16]; the 12-month DAPT duration was based on the 2013 ESC guidelines on the management of stable coronary artery disease in SCAD. Fourth, this study included only a Chinese population, which limits its generalisability to other ethnic groups. Nevertheless, the Firehawk stent has also been used in North America and Europe [20, 61]. Fifth, the exclusion of STEMI patients, low proportion of ACS and complex lesions, and the exclusive use of the Firehawk stent limit the generalisability of our findings to the broader spectrum of ACS and to other stent types. The TARGET-FIRST trial (NCT04753749) investigated short versus standard dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) in patients with acute myocardial infarction, including those with STEMI [31]. Finally, platelet function was assessed only in patients with stent thrombosis or recurrent restenosis, as routine platelet function testing to guide antiplatelet therapy is not recommended [5].

Conclusions

Among patients with acute or chronic coronary syndromes undergoing PCI with Firehawk stent implantation, short-term (3-month) DAPT was non-inferior to long-term (12-month) therapy in terms of the net composite outcome of all-cause death, MI, cerebrovascular accident, and major bleeding. Elderly patients and those with renal dysfunction at increased bleeding risk might benefit more from a 3-month DAPT regimen due to a reduction in major bleeding.

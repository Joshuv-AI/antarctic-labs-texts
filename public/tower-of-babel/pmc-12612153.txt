A comprehensive analysis of multiple factors affecting the impact pressure generated by landslide wave on underwater pipelines in shallow sea areas
Authors: Hong Ji, Baohui Shao, Weikang Liu, Ke Yang, Zhixiang Xing
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractLandslides and secondary wave disasters have become critical factors in subsea pipeline integrity breaches. Pipeline failures and subsequent hydrocarbon releases may escalate into explosion events, posing substantial risks of marine environmental contamination and potential geohazards. Therefore, rigorous analysis of landslide-induced pipeline failure mechanisms holds significant academic and practical value. The paper established a physical experimental model to analyze the influence of four factors, such as speed and water entry angle, on the impact pressure of underwater pipelines and the change of wave shape. In this paper, the orthogonal test method is used to study the primary and secondary relations of the influence of various factors on the 27 times impact pressure of underwater pipeline. The findings of the single factor test indicate that the maximum swell height and impact pressure increase with slide speed; conversely, the maximum swell height and impact pressure decrease with increasing landslide angle. The slider volume increases with the maximum wave height and impact pressure. The maximum wave height produced by impacting decreases and the impact dynamic pressure increases as the water depth increases because as the slide block slides into the water, the water may be transferred to the lower part of the slide block. Ultimately, the primary and secondary relationships of each element on the pipeline impact pressure are landslide volume > water depth > landslide entry velocity > landslide angle, as determined by analyzing the range of orthogonal test findings. Therefore, for critical infrastructure projects entailing subsea pipeline installation, a rigorous assessment of unstable slope volume, operational water depth, and seabed inclination is imperative to minimize the likelihood of catastrophic failures.Keywords: Pipeline leakage prevention, Subsea oil and gas pipelines, The propagation mechanism, Risk assessment, Multifactor analysisSubject terms: Natural hazards, Ocean sciences

Introduction

The present world is a world in which the oil and gas industry of various countries is flourishing, pipeline, as a common carrier of oil and gas material transportation, is widely used in many areas. In the process of pipeline construction, it is inevitable to cross the seabed, rivers, and lakes, however, landslide waves are one of the main factors leading to the failure of underwater oil and gas pipelines1,2. Now, in the face of such wave disasters, buildings can be protected by building seawalls and breakwaters3,4, while there are no very effective protection measures for underwater pipelines. Therefore, it is particularly important to prevent pipeline disasters. Following a landslide, the substantial shock wave will result in catastrophic damage to the pipeline. Underwater pipelines may fracture and release their contents, leading to marine pollution and environmental harm5. Additionally, subsequent explosions and jet fires could occur, causing secondary damage to the pipeline and complicating future emergency rescue efforts6,7. Thus, the key to reducing the disaster loss caused by waves on underwater pipelines is to clarify the mechanism of waves’ impact on pipelines, then put forward countermeasures to avoid pipeline leakage to avoid environmental pollution and marine environment deterioration8. The influence of landslide waves9 on underwater pipelines must be analyzed scientifically and methodically to mitigate and prevent such catastrophes10. Usually, we use the method of building an experimental platform or numerical simulation to study landslide surges11, and sometimes the two methods are combined.

Compared to buried pipelines, underwater pipelines are more susceptible due to the complex underwater conditions, including low-temperature environments12, dark undersea currents13, varying pressure exerted on the pipelines due to different span heights14, and unpredictable underwater geological disasters. Jiang15 with means of experiments, the diffusion law of leakage accidents in submarine pipelines is studied, and concluded that the main influencing factors of oil particle rise are oil spill velocity and water flow velocity. In addition, external causes such as weather have also been explored16. In addition, computational fluid dynamics (CFD) is also an important method to study the impact of waves on submarine pipelines17,18. Ji et al.19 established a turbulence model and numerical simulation of the seabed oil spill, verified relevant feasibility, and finally obtained the main influencing factors and correlation of oil spill movement. So as to study the movement and spreading law of oil leakage in submarine pipelines. Makhoul20 analyzed the impact of pipeline type, joint type and pipe diameter on pipeline vulnerability.

Most submarine oil and gas pipelines are in deep physical environment and are vulnerable to failure hazards caused by submarine landslides21. The impact of wave on the accident is manifold22. The rich design experience of offshore oil and gas industry provides beneficial reference for the system design of natural gas pipeline23. Randolph et al.24,25 conducted a mechanical analysis of submarine pipelines, proposed an empirical formula for the impact of longitudinal force and normal force on pipelines at any Angle of landslide, and studied the influence of friction coefficient and other factors on submarine pipelines. Liu et al.26, on the basis of Zakeri and Randolph’s research, and proposed that the normal resistance coefficient of the pipeline is related to the landslide angle by the different stresses of the overhanging pipe are calculated.

When a landslide enters the water, the resulting surge exerts a significant impact on underwater pipelines27. The immense energy generated by a large-scale landslide is transferred to the surge wave, which then propagates along rivers or coasts and impacts the pipelines with substantial force, leading to potential leakage or even collapse28–30. The traditional landslide research method only analyzes the mechanical process of landslide31,32, which fuzzy the process of landslide surge and then affects the underwater device. With the deepening of research in recent years, many studies have also begun to analyze how the secondary surge disaster of landslide affects the safety and normal operation of the installation. Peng’s model experiments mainly focus on the effect of different factors on the characteristics of landslide waves33. Heller34 investigated the wave characteristics of bulk landslides generated in prismatic wave flumes analyzed seven factors, and explored the wave heights of the waves. Based on a single factor, many scholars began to use multi-factor combination methods to analyze the factors affecting the damage of landslides to pipelines35. By establishing a database of landslide disasters, Liu36 sorted out and analyzed the influencing factors of landslide disasters and the space prone to landslide disasters, and obtained the distribution characteristics of landslide disasters, according to which selective treatment and construction can effectively reduce the frequency and scope of disasters. Xiao37 suggested that future studies should focus on the analysis of the absence of accidents, combined with the analysis of the environment around the pipeline.

The current research on landslide dynamics reveals a notable scarcity of studies addressing targeted triggering factors, with predominant focus placed on objective influencing elements such as environmental conditions and typhoon effects. This paper systematically investigates the generation and propagation characteristics of landslide-induced waves, analyzing the impact pressure exerted by such waves on monitoring points along the subsea pipeline walls. Through rigorous examination, we elucidate the influence of key parameters including landslide entry velocity, impact angle, landslide volume, and water depth on pipeline pressure distribution. Building upon single-factor analytical results, we further conduct comprehensive multi-factor coupling analysis. Employing orthogonal experimental methodology, we establish the hierarchical significance of these four critical factors. These findings provide a theoretical foundation for mitigating pipeline vulnerability to landslide hazards, thereby contributing to the prevention of environmental contamination and ecological security threats.

Laboratory experiments

Experimental set-up

Based on the typical geohazard prototypes in China’s Three Gorges Reservoir area, this study established a physical model test system with a geometric similarity ratio of 1:500 in the laboratory, following the principles of similarity theory.

The experiment was carried out in the experimental tank of Jiangsu Provincial Key Laboratory of Oil and Gas Storage and Transportation Technology. The tank was 2.4 m long, 1.2 m wide, 0.6 m high and 0.02 m thick. It was made of transparent acrylic material and easy to observe as shown Fig. 1. The slope is placed on the side of the pool. The slope surface was made of acrylic material with smooth surface and adjustable angle. The slide block was a wedge-shaped rigid body filled with pebbles, and its dimensions were 0.2 m×0.2 m×0.3 m,0.15 m×0.5 m ×0.3 m and 0.1 m×0.1 m×0.3 m. A total of 4 pressure sensors were set up in this experiment, 3 of which were placed at end A and 1 at end B respectively in Fig. 2.

The experimental data were acquired using a high-frequency dynamic pressure acquisition system. The pressure transducer exhibits a sensitivity of 500 mv/kPa, with the sampling frequency configured at 10 kHz. A MD-MT200 miniature pressure transmitter was employed, featuring a measurement range of 0–10 kPa, voltage output of 0–10 V, and an accuracy of ± 0.5% full scale. To ensure signal stability, the system was powered by a 4NIC-X120 linear power supply unit providing DC 24 V/5A output. For visual recording, a Phantom v1212 high-speed camera was utilized, offering maximum resolution of 1280 × 800 pixels, frame rates up to 12,600 fps, and minimum exposure time of 100 µs.

The influence of the slider entry velocity

The propagation mechanism of landslide wave

The vertical entry height of slide block is the main factor affecting the entry speed of slide block. The increase of the vertical height of the slide block is equal to the increase of potential energy. As the slider slides, it converts gravitational potential energy into kinetic energy, so the velocity at which the slider slides down to the surface of the water changes accordingly. In this section, the vertical entry height of slide block is 0.10 m, 0.15 m, and 0.20 m (corresponding sliding velocities are 1.67 m/s, 2.04 m/s, and 2.36 m/s), and the physical model test is performed with the landslide entering Angle set to 45°, the volume of slider set to 0.006m3, and the depth of water set to 0.30 m.

The wave effect of the slider with different initial velocities is also different when it hits the water surface. By comparing the biggest wave heights formed under three different landslide entry angles in Fig. 3, it can be found that the biggest wave heights are decreasing. Therefore, the biggest wave heights decrease with the decrease of the entry angle. Subsequently, the body of water being waved high back down when the air is involved, so that the surface of the water to form a two-phase flow of water and air, and began to move forward. The slider was sliding down to hit the body of water continuously; when it reached the bottom and was fully submerged, the slider above the cavity emerged, causing the surrounding body of water to rush to the cavity and creating an upward and tumbling splash inside39. It can be found from the figure that the larger the landslide entry angle is, the larger the water volume of spray formed by the spray is, and the smaller the spray height is. The smaller the landslide entry angle is, the smaller the water volume of spray formed by the spray is, but the higher the spray height is. With some distance creep on the slope, the water surface elevation in front of the landslide starts to climb as the water body in the cavity expands. The greater the landslide entry angle, the higher the first wave climb40. The results in Fig. 3 show that the wave’s climbing height is consistent with the pattern shown. With the increase of the sliding block entering the water speed, the kinetic energy of the sliding block entering the water is also increasing. In the process of the sliding block entering the water to transfer the energy, the climbing height of the surge is also increasing.

The influence of the slider volume

The propagation mechanism of landslide wave

Figure 9 shows the shape diagram of wave waves formed by sliding blocks of three different volumes impacting water. When t = 0 s, the slider descends to the water’s surface and starts to interact with the body of water. Subsequently, the water body is pushed up by the compression and impact of the sliding block. By comparing the time when the three sliders with different volumes form the highest wave, it can be found that the smaller sliders form the maximum wave height earlier; It can also be seen from the figure that the maximum wave height decreases from Fig. 9 (a) to Fig. 9 (c), so the maximum wave height decreases with the decrease of the slider volume. Then, the high water falls into the water surface and is sucked into part of the air so that the water surface forms a two-phase flow of water and air and begins to move forward. The sliders are constantly sliding and impacting the water body, S3 in Fig. 9 (c) have side slipping movement in the process of sliding in water due to their small volume and mass. Slider continues to slide, and a cavity appears at its tail. Due to the different volumes of the three sliders and the different volumes of the squeezed water, the size of the cavity is also different. Surrounding water rushes towards the cavity, where it roils and splashes upwards. The propagation mechanism of landslide wave

This occurrence could be a result of the water becoming deeper, the energy transferred to the water body by the slider increases to the depth under the sliding block, allowing the maximum wave height produced by the horizontal impact and extrusion to diminish. Multiple factors coupling analysis

The landslide entering water velocity, landslide entering water angle, slide block volume, and water depth are known to affect the shape of the wave and the pipeline impact pressure through the analysis of single factor test results44–46; however, more research is necessary to determine the primary and secondary relationships between the wave wave’s impact on the pipeline impact force under each factor. The sensitivity of landslide wave characteristic factors to the impact of an underwater pipeline was analyzed using the multi-factor coupling analysis method. This allowed for the identification of the factors and levels that have the greatest influence on the pipeline’s impact, thereby offering technical support for both the safe operation of the pipeline and the risk assessment of landslides wave. The advantages of this method are that the number of experimental panels can be reduced to a minimum, the number of orthogonal panels with all factors symmetric can be given, and the range analysis can be used to optimize the experimental results47. Conclusion and prospects

This study examines the characteristics of underwater pressure propagation by analyzing variations in pressure on submarine pipeline walls under different monitoring conditions. It investigates the influence of factors such as landslide entry velocity, entry angle, volume of the slider, and water depth on the impact pressure exerted on submarine pipelines. The relationship between impact pressure on proximal and distal sections of the pipeline is also analyzed. Empirical formulas for estimating the maximum impact pressure on underwater pipelines are derived using linear and quadratic polynomial fitting methods. Through orthogonal experimental design, the relative importance of various factors affecting the peak impact pressure is evaluated, leading to the following conclusions:

The slider slides into the water, impacts and squeezes the water, raising the surface of the water to a certain height, thus forming a swell. Landslide disasters are typically triggered by factors such as rainfall or human activities (e.g., construction), which reduce slope stability. Environmental conditions—including the velocity and angle of the landslide entering the water, as well as the volume of the sliding mass—constitute key factors governing the generation of surge waves. Therefore, during the construction of major projects in areas with submarine pipelines, caution should be exercised to avoid regions characterized by high instability of slopes, shallow water depths at the pipeline site, and steep terrain, so as to mitigate potential disaster risks.

In subsequent research phases, we will systematically incorporate additional critical parameters into our analysis, including but not limited to slider density, geometric morphology, and rheological properties. Furthermore, we plan to expand the parametric levels for previously investigated factors through comprehensive sensitivity studies, thereby significantly enhancing the generalizability and practical applicability of our conclusions.

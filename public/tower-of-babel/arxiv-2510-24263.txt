REPRESENTATION THEORY OF NON-FACTORIZABLE RIBBON HOPF ALGEBRAS
Maksymilian Manko

Abstract
In [ FM25 ] new examples of ribbon Hopf algebras based on the construction due to Nenciu [ Nen04 ] were presented. This piece serves as a sequel where we study the representation theory of these new examples of ribbon Hopf algebras. We classify indecomposable projective and simple modules, find the Krull-Schmidt decomposition of the adjoint representation of Nenciu algebras, and prove fusion rules between its components. We also comment on the properties of Müger centres of their representation categories, in particular that they can be non-semisimple. Finally, we consider a new family of ribbon Hopf algebras over fields of prime characteristic p > 2 p>2 in the context of 4-dimensional TQFTs presented in [ CGHPM23 ] that constitute an improvement over examples given therein, although still seemingly falling short of producing powerful invariants of 4-manifolds.



1
Introduction

Unimodular ribbon categories introduced by Turaev in
[
Tur94
]
constitute an important topic in quantum algebra, chiefly for their many applications in quantum topology. Since the 1990s, multiple constructions of 3-manifold and 4-manifold invariants have been studied using these as essential ingredients
[
Tur94
]
,
[
KL01
]
,
[
CY93
]
. Ribbon Hopf algebras are a natural source of such categories through their representation theory. A ribbon Hopf algebra
H
is a quasitriangular Hopf algebra (i.e. it comes with the extra data of an invertible element
R
of
H\otimes H
called the
R-matrix
) together with a central element of
H
called the ribbon element. Both
R
and
v
are required to satisfy certain axioms, see Section
2
.

In particular, one may define a
3
-manifold invariant from any so-called
factorizable
unimodular ribbon Hopf algebra. Factorizability is a non-degeneracy condition on the
monodromy matrix

M:=R_{21}R
of the algebra, where
R_{21}
is the R-matrix with permuted entries (see Proposition
2.10
). Recall also that a Hopf algebra
H
is unimodular if it admits an integral
\lambda\in H^{*}
and a cointegral
\Lambda\in H
satisfying certain properties, as detailed in Definition
2.5
.

More recently there has been a considerable interest in the unimodular,
non-factorizable
ribbon Hopf algebras, as ingredients of non-semisimple 4-manifold invariants. In
[
BDR23
]
Beliakova and De Renzi introduced Kerler-Lyubashenko functors from a certain category of 4-dimensional 2-handlebodies up to 2-deformations
[
BDR23
]
based on the work of Bobtcheva and Piergallini
[
BP06
]
, and presented in a more streamlined way in a pre-print of Beliakova, Bobtcheva, De Renzi and Piergallini
[
BBDRP23
]
.

Several examples of non-factorizable unimodular ribbon Hopf algebras are known classically, including the small quantum group
u_{q}\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
at an even root of unity
q
. It was shown in
[
BDR22
]
that the scalar Kerler-Lyubashenko type invariant of
[
BDR23
]
applied to a connected
4
-dimensional
2
-handlebody only sees its
3
-dimensional boundary and homological information. Another example is the Hopf algebra of
symplectic fermions

\operatorname{SF}_{2n}
, which admits a continuous family of non-factorizable ribbon structures. The generic case, for
n=1
, was shown by Kerler in
[
Ker03
]
to give essentially invariants of the
3
-dimensional boundary with this approach. In both cases, the Hopf algebras are, in some sense, “close to factorizable”. Indeed, in the unimodular case, factorizability is equivalent to requiring that applying the integral to one side of the monodromy matrix gives the cointegral. In these non-factorizable cases we mentioned, this equation is still verified up to an invertible element of the algebra.

Hence, in an attempt to avoid such “degeneration to the boundary” in our recent paper with Q. Faes
[
FM25
]
, henceforth referred to as part 1, we introduced the stricter notion of
strong non-factorizability
. We demand that the result of applying the integral to either side of the monodromy matrix, with the antipode previously applied to its first tensor component, is zero, and hence cannot be related to the cointegral by any invertible element of the algebra:

\big((\lambda\circ S)\otimes\mathrm{Id}\big)M=\big(S\otimes\lambda\big)M=0.

We introduced two extensive families of such Hopf algebras, we will refer to as
Nenciu type
after
[
Nen04
]
and
Nenciu biproducts
, respectively, as the second family is produced by taking semidirect products and coproducts between the Nenciu type Hopf algebras and the small quantum group
u_{q}\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
at the unit of unity
q
of order divisible by 4.

In his paper we focus on the related representation-theoretic questions. From this perspective, module categories of such Hopf algebras are analogously unimodular, non-factorizable ribbon, and by the result of Shimizu
[
Shi19
]
, the factorizability condition can be equivalently expressed in terms of the
Müger centre
- the full subcategory of modules which braid symmetrically with any other module in the category, see Definition
2.35
. Precious few examples of such categories are known, even fewer if we additionally impose the condition that the Müger centre be non-semisimple (a notable example are
higher Verlinde categories in the mixed case
defined in
[
STWZ23
]
and studied in
[
Déc24
]
). We show that the examples of Hopf algebras discussed in part 1 posses this property.
Non-factorizable braided tensor categories have been also recently studied in the context of Morita 4-category of braided tensor categories in
[
BJS21
]
and 4-category of braided,
\mathcal{E}
-enriched tensor categories in
[
Déc25
]
, both works referencing the algebraic side of the topic. As also mentioned in part 1, such categories have applications in 4-dimensional topology, as the underlying algebraic data of the 4-dimensional Topological Quantum Field Theories (TQFTs) constructed by Constantino, Geer, Haïoun and Patureau-Mirand in
[
CGHPM23
]
, which we will discuss towards the end of the paper.
Additionally, in this case, it is expected that for the TQFT to detect 4-dimensional information the category has to be
chromatic-non degenerate
but not
chromatic compact
(see Definition
7.3
). For representation categories, this is a property linking the action of the two-sided cointegral
\Lambda
and of the element
\lambda(S(M^{\prime}))M^{\prime\prime}
, where
\lambda
is the left integral, on the projective cover of the monoidal unit (see Definition
2.26
). Topologically this carries the information about handle attachments of index greater than 2 in the numbering convention of
[
BDR23
]
we used in part 1 (but dual to the one in
[
CGHPM23
]
). We show that while this is not possible to achieve with the constructions of part 1, passing to a field of finite characteristic allows to produce such a category with our methods. This is an improvement on the strategy of
[
CGHPM23
, Definition 6.6 and Proposition 6.7]
, producing, to the author’s knowledge the only example of braided, but not symmetric unimodular ribbon category with non-semisimple Müger centre that is chromatic non-degenerate but not chromatic compact. We comment also on its potential power to detect exotic pairs of 4-manifolds.

1.1
Main results

The first result is the decompostion of the regular representation of any Nenciu-type Hopf algebra into indecomposable projective modules (Theorem
5.7
). This is achieved by explicitly finding the full system of primitive idempotents. The idempotents are in one-to-one correspondence with the grouplike elements and we collect the eigenvalues of the action by grouplikes into tuples that identify the idempotents uniquely, in line with the conventions of part 1. We then do the same with the adjoint representation (Theorem
5.25
), which beyond the indecomposable projective and corresponding simple modules contains also direct summands we identify with submodules of the indecomposable projectives. We proceed to study their fusion rules (Theorem
5.28
) and brading properties, in particular identifying when such modules are transparent (Proposition
5.31
). We emphasize that most of the properties of this family of Hopf algebra is controlled by the data of the grouplike generators and their actions on the nilpotent generators. Finally, we consider further indecomposable modules deduced from the results of
[
ZN71
]
(Example
5.37
).

Moving on to the case of Nenciu biproducts, we again find the full system of idempotents (Proposition
6.2
) and find an explicit basis for the corresponding projective modules (Definition
6.3
). We find that many properties of the modules over the biproducts are composite with regard to those of the constituents, as emphasized in Examples
6.5
and
6.8
. This includes the properties of the Müger centre, chiefly its non-semisimplicity (Propositions
5.40
and
6.6
). The results for both families are supported with plethora of examples.

In the final section we show that the representation categories of strongly non-factorizable Hopf algebras are neither chromatic non-degenerate nor chromatic compact (Proposition
7.4
), so do not produce 4-dimensional TQFTs defined beyond 0-, 1- and 2-handles from the approach of
[
CGHPM23
]
. This holds, in particular, for strongly non-factorizable examples discussed in part 1. However, it is possible to modify our construction to the case of fields of finite characteristic to achieve chromatic non-degeneracy, but not chromatic compactness with our means (Proposition
7.16
), while retaining the non-semisimple Müger centre (Proposition
7.17
). Namely, we fix a field
\mathbb{K}
of finite characteristic
\operatorname{char}\mathbb{K}=p>2
, such that it contains a root of unity
q\in\mathbb{K}
and the order of
q
is divisible by 4 (hence coprime to
p
). Let
S_{p}
be the symmetric group on
p
letters and
\mathbb{K}[S_{p}]
its group algebra. Let also
\operatorname{SF_{2p}}
be the Hopf algebra of symplectic fermions over
\mathbb{K}
and
u_{q}\mathfrak{sl_{2}}
the small quantum group at the root of unity
q
over
\mathbb{K}
(we explain why all necessary results regarding these hold in finite characteristic in Propositions
7.7
and
7.8
). Then we construct the following composite Hopf algebra.

Main Theorem A
.

Under an appropriate choice of parameters a semidirect biproduct
u_{q}\mathfrak{sl}_{2}\ltimes\operatorname{SF}_{2p}\rtimes\mathbb{K}[S_{p}]
can be simultaneously

1.

unimodular (Theorem
7.15
, 1.),

2.

quasitriangular (Theorem
7.15
2.)

3.

ribbon (Theorem
7.15
3.),

4.

twist degenerate (Theorem
7.15
4.),

5.

chromatic non-degenerate but not chromatic compact (Proposition
7.16
).

However, we also show this is not enough to detect exotic pairs of 4-manifolds as the invariant will be susceptible to stabilisation by
S^{2}\times S^{2}
(Propositon
7.20
), according to the known result of Gompf (see Remark
7.19
).

1.2
Structure of the paper

In Section 2 we introduce algebraic and representation-theoretic preliminaries, while in Section 3 and 4 we recall some of the needed material from
[

]
, regarding Nenicu Hopf algebras and biproducts with

In Section 6 we do the same with the Nenciu biproducts with

CGHPM23

.

2
Preliminaries

Let us recall some definitions and results regarding Hopf algebras, and fix the notation used in the rest of the paper.

2.1
Notations for Hopf algebras

In this paper, we consider Hopf algebras over the field of complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
Hopf algebra

(H,\mu,\eta,\Delta,\epsilon,S)
is the data of a vector space (over
\mathbb{C}
)
H
together with

•

a
unit

\mathbf{1}:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow H
and a
product

\mu:H\otimes H\rightarrow H,

•

a
counit

\epsilon:H\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, and a
coproduct

\Delta:H\rightarrow H\otimes H

•

an invertible
antipode

S:H\rightarrow H
,

that are
\mathbb{C}
-linear maps satisfying the axioms

\begin{gathered}\mu\circ(\mu\otimes\mathrm{id})=\mu\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mu),\\
\mu\circ(\eta\otimes\mathrm{id})=\mathrm{id}=\mu\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\eta),\\
(\Delta\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\Delta=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\Delta)\circ\Delta,\\
(\varepsilon\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\Delta=\mathrm{id}=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\varepsilon)\circ\Delta,\\
(\mu\otimes\mu)\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\tau\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ(\Delta\otimes\Delta)=\Delta\circ\mu,\\
\varepsilon\circ\mu=\varepsilon\otimes\varepsilon,\\
\Delta\circ\eta=\eta\otimes\eta,\\
\varepsilon\circ\eta=1,\\
\mu\circ(S\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\Delta=\eta\circ\varepsilon=\mu\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes S)\circ\Delta,\end{gathered}

where
\tau:H\otimes H\rightarrow H\otimes H
is the
flip map
exchanging the factors in the tensor product.

We will often abusively denote the Hopf algebra by
H
, and write
gh
instead of
\mu(g,h)
for
g,h\in H
. We also note that
H\otimes H
can be endowed with an algebra structure, using the product

(a\otimes b)(c\otimes d):=ac\otimes bd.

where
a,b,c,d\in H
.

Remark 2.2
.

Hopf algebras may be defined in any braided monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
. The algebra structure of
H\otimes H
is then defined using the braiding.

Notation
.

We use the Sweedler notation for the coproduct: if

\Delta(h)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}h_{(1)i}\otimes h_{(2)i}

then we write

\Delta(h)=h_{(1)}\otimes h_{(2)},

for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.
By
\Delta^{cop}:=\tau\circ\Delta
we denote the
coopposite coproduct
, with
\tau:H\otimes H\rightarrow H\otimes H
the flip map. Recall that if
H
is a Hopf algebra, then
H^{cop}:=(H,\mu,\eta,\Delta^{cop},\epsilon,S)
is also a Hopf algebra.

Recall that if
H
is a Hopf algebra, the dual vector space

H^{*}:=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(H,\mathbb{C}).

can also be provided with a Hopf algebra structure.

Definition 2.3
.

An element
g\in H
is called
grouplike
if
\Delta(g)=g\otimes g
. The subset
\mathcal{G}(H)
of all such elements carries a group structure, and generates a Hopf subalgebra which is isomorphic to the group algebra of
\mathcal{G}(H)
. An element
X\in H
is called
skew-primitive with respect to

L\in\mathcal{G}(H)
if
\Delta(X)=\mathbf{1}\otimes X+X\otimes L
, and
primitive
if
L=\mathbf{1}
.

Definition 2.4
.

The
left adjoint action
of
H
is the left
H
-module structure on
H
given by

\rhd:H\otimes H\rightarrow H,\;\;a\rhd b:=a_{(1)}\,b\,S(a_{(2)})

in the Sweedler notation.

2.2
Integrals and unimodularity

Let
H
be a finite dimensional Hopf algebra over an algebraically closed field. Then it admits a left (resp. right) integral
\lambda_{L}\in H^{*}
(resp.
\lambda_{R}
) and cointegral
\Lambda_{L}\in H
(resp.
\Lambda_{R}
), see
[
Rad12
, Chapter 10.1]
.

Definition 2.5
.

An element
\lambda_{L}\in H^{*}
is a
left
(resp.
\lambda_{R}\in H^{*}
a
right
)
integral
of
H
if for all
h\in H
,

\displaystyle h_{(1)}\otimes\lambda_{L}(h_{(2)})=\lambda_{L}(h)

\displaystyle\big(\text{resp.}

\displaystyle\lambda_{R}(h_{(1)})\otimes h_{(1)}=\lambda_{R}(h)\big).

An element
\Lambda_{L}\in H
is a left (resp.
\Lambda_{R}\in H
a right) cointegral if for all
h\in H
,

\displaystyle h\Lambda_{L}=\epsilon(h)\Lambda_{L}

\displaystyle\big(\text{resp.}

\displaystyle\Lambda_{R}h=\Lambda_{R}\epsilon(h)\big).

An integral (resp. cointegral) is called two-sided if its both a left and right integral (resp. cointegral), or equivalently, if

\displaystyle\lambda\circ S=\lambda

\displaystyle\big(\text{resp.}

\displaystyle S(\Lambda)=\Lambda\big).

It is also known that
\Lambda_{L},\Lambda_{R}
generate
1
-dimensional subalgebras of
H
and
\lambda_{L},\lambda_{R}
generate
1
-dimensional subalgebras of
H^{*}
, thus different choices differ up to a constant in the ground field. We also note that in case
\lambda_{L}
and
\lambda_{R}
do not coincide, it still holds that
\lambda_{R}=\lambda_{L}\circ S
.

Definition 2.6

(
[
Rad12
]
Definition 10.2.3)
.

We will call
H

unimodular
if
\Lambda
is two-sided.

2.3
Quasitriangular and ribbon structures

Let us recall the definition of the quasitriangular Hopf algebra. We need the following notation.

Notation
.

For an element
R\in H\otimes H
such as the
R-matrix
of a quasitriangular Hopf algebra of Definition
2.7
below, we write

R=R^{\prime}\otimes R^{\prime\prime},

where the summation is again implicit. Sometimes, we will need many copies of
R
, in which case they will be labeled with numbers. We also denote
R_{21}:=\tau(R)
.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
H
be a Hopf algebra and
R
be an element of
H\otimes H
. The pair
(H,R)
is quasitriangular if the following axioms are satisfied.

(QT1)

\Delta(R^{\prime})\otimes R^{\prime\prime}=R^{\prime}_{1}\otimes R^{\prime}_{2}\otimes R^{\prime\prime}_{1}R^{\prime\prime}_{2}

(QT2)

\epsilon(R^{\prime})R^{\prime\prime}=\mathbf{1}

(QT3)

R^{\prime}\otimes\Delta^{cop}(R^{\prime\prime})=R^{\prime}_{1}R^{\prime}_{2}\otimes R^{\prime\prime}_{1}\otimes R^{\prime\prime}_{2}

(QT4)

\epsilon(R^{\prime\prime})R^{\prime}=\mathbf{1}

(QT5)

\Delta^{cop}(h)R=R\Delta(h),\forall h\in H
.

Then
R
is called a
universal R-matrix
(where we usually drop the “universal”), and it follows from (QT2) and (QT4) that it is invertible in the algebra
H\otimes H
, with the inverse denoted
R^{-1}
.

Definition 2.8
.

The
monodromy matrix
is defined as

M:=R_{21}R=R^{\prime\prime}_{1}R^{\prime}_{2}\otimes R^{\prime}_{1}R^{\prime\prime}_{2}.

If
M=\mathbf{1}\otimes\mathbf{1}
, the quasitriangular structure is called
triangular
.

In order to exhibit quasitriangular structures on Hopf algebras, we will strongly rely on the following result.

Theorem 2.9

(
[
Rad94
]
, Section 2.1)
.

Let
H
be a Hopf algebra. Any element
R\in H\otimes H
satisfying (QT1)-(QT4) induces a Hopf algebra map

\displaystyle f_{R}:H^{*}

\displaystyle\rightarrow H^{cop}

\displaystyle h^{*}

\displaystyle\mapsto h^{*}(R^{\prime})R^{\prime\prime},

where
H^{cop}
is the coopposite Hopf algebra of
H
, defined above.

By the contrapositive of Theorem
2.9
, if there are no bialgebra maps between
H^{*}
and
H^{cop}
, then there can be no quasitriangular structures on
H
.

Proposition 2.10

(
[
Shi19
]
Theorem 1.1,
[
EGNO16
]
Exercise 8.6.4)
.

For a finite-dimensional quasitriangular Hopf algebra
(H,R)
the following two conditions are equivalent

1.

the monodromy matrix
M
induces a non-degenerate pairing on
H^{*}\otimes H^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
given by
h^{*}_{1}\otimes h^{*}_{2}\mapsto h^{*}_{1}(M^{\prime})h^{*}_{2}(M^{\prime\prime})
, or

2.

the Drinfeld-Reshetikhin map

\displaystyle H^{*}

\displaystyle\rightarrow H

\displaystyle h^{*}

\displaystyle\mapsto h^{*}(M^{\prime})M^{\prime\prime}

is an isomorphism of vector spaces.

3.

The Müger centre
\mathcal{Z}_{(2)}(H\text{-mod})
(see Definition
2.35
) of the category of modules
H\text{-mod}
is trivial (generated by the unit of
H\text{-mod}
).

If either item is fulfilled
(H,R)
is called
factorizable
, and otherwise
non-factorizable
. If
\mathcal{Z}_{(2)}(H\text{-mod})
is isomorphic to the category of finite dimensional super-vector spaces
\operatorname{SVect}
, we will refer to
H
as
slightly factorizable
.

For the purpose of this discussion we introduce a stronger notion.

Definition 2.11
.

A unimodular, quasitriangular Hopf algebra
(H,R)
with an integral
\lambda\in H^{*}
is called
strongly non-factorizable
if any of the two equivalent conditions is fulfilled

\lambda(S(M^{\prime}))M^{\prime\prime}=0,

or

S(M^{\prime})\lambda(M^{\prime\prime})=0.

It is easy to see from Definition
2.10
that any strongly non-factorizable Hopf algebra is non-factorizable.

Remark 2.12
.

Note that if
(H,R)
is non-cosemisimple, triangular, and has a two-sided integral
\lambda
, then it is strongly non-factorizable. Indeed
\lambda(\mathbf{1})=0
is equivalent to the non-cosemisimplicity condition for a unimodular Hopf algebra (see
[
Rad12
]
, Theorem 10.3.2).

We now turn to the ribbon structure.

Definition 2.13
.

Let
(H,R)
be a finite dimensional, quasitriangular Hopf algebra with monodromy matrix
M
. A
ribbon element

v\in Z(H)
is a central element of
H
such that

(R1)

S(v)=v

(R2)

\epsilon(v)=1

(R3)

M\Delta(v)=v\otimes v
.

The triple
(H,R,v)
is called a
ribbon Hopf algebra
.

Definition 2.14
.

The
Drinfeld element
of a quasitriangular Hopf algebra
(H,R)
is

u:=S(R^{\prime\prime})R^{\prime}.

It is invertible with the inverse given by

u^{-1}=S^{-1}\left((R^{-1})^{\prime\prime}\right)(R^{-1})^{\prime}.

Proposition 2.15

(
[
Kas12
]
, VIII.4.1)
.

Let
(H,R)
be a quasitriangular Hopf algebra and
u
be the corresponding Drinfeld element. For any
h\in H

S^{2}(h)=uhu^{-1}.

Definition 2.16

(
[
Rad94
]
)
.

Let
H
be a Hopf algebra. A
pivotal element
is a grouplike element
g\in\mathcal{G}(H)
such that for any
h\in H

S^{2}(h)=ghg^{-1}.

As explained in
[
Rad94
, Prop. 2]
in a ribbon Hopf algebra, a ribbon element
v=g^{-1}u
is the product of the Drinfeld element
u
and the inverse of a pivotal element. However, not every pivotal element can be used to define a ribbon element.

Lemma 2.17
.

Let
(H,R)
be a finite-dimensional quasitriangular Hopf algebra with a two-sided integral
\lambda
. If
g\in\mathcal{G}(H)
is a pivotal element, then
g^{2}=\mathbf{1}
.

We introduce one more property of the ribbon structure that bears significance in many topological constructions.

Definition 2.18
.

Let
H
be a Hopf algebra carrying a (left) integral
\lambda
and a ribbon element
v
. We call
H

anomaly-free
(or
twist non-degenerate
in
[
CGHPM23
]
) if

\lambda(v)\neq 0.

Otherwise we will refer to it as
anomalous
(or
twist degenerate
).

2.4
Algebra generators and tuple notation

All the constructions considered in this paper produce Hopf algebras generated by grouplike and skew-primitive generators.

Notation
.

We denote by
K_{a}
,
a=1,\dots,s
the grouplike generators that commute with one another and are of respective orders
m_{1},\dots,m_{s}
. By
X_{k},X^{\pm}_{k},Z_{l}^{\pm}
for
k=1,\dots,t_{1}
and
l=1,\dots,t_{2}
we denote skew-primitive generators such that
(X_{k})^{2}=(X^{\pm}_{k})^{2}=(Z^{\pm}_{l})^{2}=0
.

We will repeatedly appeal to the following notion.

Definition 2.19
.

Let
g,h\in H
. We say
g,h
have a
diagonal relation
if there exists
\gamma\in\mathbb{C}
such that

gh=\gamma hg.

Notation
.

We will use boldface for tuples of numbers and algebra generators, for instance, let
\mathbf{0}=(0,\dots,0)
, and let

\boldsymbol{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{s}):=(\exp^{2\pi/m_{1}},\dots,\exp^{2\pi/m_{s}})\in\mathbb{C}^{s}

be the tuple of primitive roots of unity of orders
\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},\dots,m_{s})\in\mathbb{Z}^{s}_{>0}
. We similarly express the lists of algebra generators as
\mathbf{K}=(K_{1},\dots,K_{s})
, or
\mathbf{X}=(X_{1},\dots,X_{t})
.

We will often use the following algebraic structures.

Notation
.

Let
\mathbb{Z}_{\mathbf{m}}:=\mathbb{Z}_{m_{1}}\times\dots\times\mathbb{Z}_{m_{s}}
, where
\mathbb{Z}_{m_{a}}:=\mathbb{Z}/m_{a}\mathbb{Z}
, and let
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{K}]:=\mathbb{C}[K_{1},\dots,K_{m_{s}}]\subset H
be the group subalgebra generated by
K_{1},\dots,K_{s}
. It is isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{Z}_{\mathbf{m}}]
as a Hopf algebra.

We also introduce some operations on tuples.

Notation
.

For two tuples of the same shape, for instance
\mathbf{w},\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\mathbf{m}}
we define the element-wise sum

\mathbf{w}+\mathbf{v}:=(w_{1}+v_{1},\dots,w_{s}+v_{s}),

and the element-wise product

\mathbf{w}\cdot\mathbf{v}:=(w_{1}v_{1},\dots,w_{s}v_{s}).

By a power of a tuple by a tuple, for instance for
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\mathbf{m}}
,
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{C}^{s}
and
\mathbf{K}
, we mean

\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\mathbf{v}}=(\xi_{1})^{v_{1}}(\xi_{1})^{v_{2}}\dots(\xi_{s})^{v_{s}},

and

\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{v}}=(K_{1})^{v_{1}}(K_{2})^{v_{2}}\dots(K_{s})^{v_{s}}.

Notation
.

We will also encounter
matrices
, denoted with boldface sans-serif font, for instance

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\alpha}=(\alpha_{kl})_{1\leq k,l\leq t}\in\operatorname{Mat}_{t\times t}(\mathbb{C})

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathsf{d}}=(d_{ka})_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a\leq s\\
1\leq k\leq t\end{subarray}},\;\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}=(u_{ka})_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a\leq s\\
1\leq k\leq t\end{subarray}}\in\operatorname{Mat}_{t\times s}(\mathbb{Z}_{\mathbf{m}}).

The rows
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{d}}_{k}=(d_{ka})_{0\leq a\leq s}
,
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}_{k}=(u_{ka})_{0\leq a\leq s}
are considered as tuples (and conform to the boldface notation). Products with no operation symbols mean the usual matrix multiplication, provided the participating matrices have appropriate dimensions and entries in the same ring. For
\mathbf{w},\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\mathbf{m}}
,

\mathbf{w}\mathbf{v}^{T}=w_{1}v_{1}+\dots+w_{s}v_{s},

is the inner product of row tuples, valued in
\mathbb{Z}_{\mathrm{lcm}(m_{1},\dots,m_{s})}
, where
\mathrm{lcm}(m_{1},\dots,m_{s})
is the least common multiple of
m_{1},\dots,m_{s}
. For a matrix
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{z}}\in\operatorname{Mat}_{s\times s}(\mathbb{Z}_{\mathbf{m}})
, the expression
\mathbf{v}\boldsymbol{\mathsf{z}}
is a row tuple again, as is
(\mathbf{w}\mathbf{z})\cdot\mathbf{v}
, but
\mathbf{w}\boldsymbol{\mathsf{z}}\mathbf{v}^{T}
is a scalar in
\mathbb{Z}_{\mathrm{lcm}(m_{1},\dots,m_{s})}
.

2.5
The semi-direct biproduct structure

Towards the goal of introducing Nenciu biproducts later on, we introduce versions of
semi-direct
(or
smash
) product and coproduct. While these constructions are well-known in the study of Hopf algebras
[
Mol77
]
, we use slightly non-standard conventions (for instance in
[
AS06
]
the definitions have the sense of the action and coaction, as well as the order of tensor coefficients, exchanged) to achieve the accurate description of examples in the forthcoming sections.

Definition 2.20
.

Let
U,H
be usual Hopf algebras. Let

\lhd:H\otimes U\rightarrow H

be a right algebra action of
U
on
H
(see for instance
[
Kas12
, Chapter III.5]
). We define their
right semi-direct product
as the algebra over the vector space
U\otimes H
with the unit
\mathbf{1}_{U}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{H}
and the product

(u\otimes h)(u^{\prime}\otimes h^{\prime}):=uu^{\prime}_{(1)}\otimes(h\lhd u^{\prime}_{(2)})h^{\prime}.

Let also

\delta:H\rightarrow H\otimes U

be a right coaction of
U
on
H
(see for instance
[
Kas12
, Chapter III.6]
), and we adapt the notation

h\mapsto h^{[0]}\otimes h^{[1]}.

We define their
right semi-direct coproduct
, as the coalgebra over the vector space
U\otimes H
with the counit
\epsilon_{U}\otimes\epsilon_{H}
, and the coproduct

\Delta(u\otimes h):=(u_{(1)}\otimes h^{[0]}_{(1)})\otimes(h^{[1]}_{(1)}g_{(2)}\otimes h_{(2)}).

Now if the semi-direct product and coproduct give rise to a Hopf algebra structure with the antipode
S:U\otimes H\rightarrow U\otimes H

S(u\otimes h)\mapsto S_{U}(uh^{[1]})\otimes S_{H}(h^{[0]})

and its inverse

S^{-1}:U\otimes H\rightarrow U\otimes H

S^{-1}(u\otimes h)\mapsto S^{-1}_{U}(uh^{[1]})\otimes S^{-1}_{H}(h^{[0]}),

then we will call it a
semi-direct biproduct
of
U
and
H
and denote
U\ltimes H
.

Remark 2.21
.

We emphasize that all tensor products between
U
and
H
in Definition
2.20
are taken in the category
\text{{Vect}}_{\mathbb{C}}
, which carries the trivial braiding. In particular, this is a different setting than the
Radford biproduct
which is taken in a braided
category of Yetter-Drinfeld modules
over the acting algebra.

Notation
.

Let

Define the

Let

idempotent

Proposition 2.25

Let

(

]

Notation
.

(

Notation
.

Definition 2.32
.

We call a module

Proposition 2.34

Let
(H,R)
be a quasitriangular Hopf algebra. Then the category

is braided monoidal. Let

. Then the braiding is given by the isomorphism

c(V_{1}\otimes V_{2}):=R^{\prime\prime}\cdot V_{2}\otimes R^{\prime}\cdot V_{1}\cong V_{2}\otimes V_{1},

where

is the generic module operation relevant to each module in question.

Definition 2.35
.

We call an object

3
The Nenciu construction

In this section we recall some of the results from
[
FM25
]
, and refer there for the corresponding proofs. The Nenciu construction of
[
Nen04
]
produces finite-dimensional ribbon Hopf algebras generated by grouplike generators collected in a tuple
\mathbf{K}=(K_{1},\dots,K_{s})
and nilpotent generators collected in a tuple
\mathbf{X}=(X_{1},\dots,X_{t})
, such that the group subalgebra
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{K}]
is abelian, the nilpotent generators are skew-primitive with respect to
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{K}]
and all commutation relations between generators are diagonal. The choice of the generators and relations is strongly constrained, and the sufficient conditions for the result to be a ribbon Hopf algebra were determined in
[
Nen04
]
and collected in
[
FM25
, Theorem 3.19]
.

3.1
Hopf algebra structure

We start by recalling Nenciu’s construction: the fact that Definition
3.1
indeed defines a Hopf algebra is a Theorem of
[
Nen04
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{s}
be a tuple of positive integers of length
s
, and
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{d}}
,
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}
be dimension
t\times s
,
\mathbb{Z}
-valued matrices, such that

\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{d}}_{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}_{l}}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{d}}_{l}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}_{k}}=1,\text{ and }\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{d}}_{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}_{k}}=-1,

(1)

where
\boldsymbol{\xi}:=(\exp^{2\pi/m_{1}},\dots,\exp^{2\pi/m_{s}})
is the tuple of primitive roots of unity of orders given by the tuple
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{s}
.
Define
H(\mathbf{m},t,\boldsymbol{\mathsf{d}},\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}})
to be the Hopf algebra generated by grouplike generators
\mathbf{K}=(K_{1},\dots,K_{s})
, and skew-primitive generators
\mathbf{X}=(X_{1},\dots,X_{t})
, and with the relations

\displaystyle K^{m_{a}}_{a}=\mathbf{1},

\displaystyle K_{a}K_{b}=K_{b}K_{a},

\displaystyle K_{a}X_{k}:=\xi_{a}^{d_{ka}}X_{k}K_{a},

\displaystyle X^{2}_{k}=0,

\displaystyle X_{l}X_{k}:=\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{d}}_{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}_{l}}X_{k}X_{l},

coalgebra structure determined by

\displaystyle\epsilon(K_{a})

\displaystyle:=1,

\displaystyle\Delta(K_{a})

\displaystyle:=K_{a}\otimes K_{a},

\displaystyle\epsilon(X_{k})

\displaystyle:=0,

\displaystyle\Delta(X_{k})

\displaystyle:=\mathbf{1}\otimes X_{k}+X_{k},\otimes\mathbf{K}^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}_{k}},

and antipode determined by

\displaystyle S(K_{a})=K^{-1}_{a}:=K^{m_{a}-1}_{a},

\displaystyle S(X_{k}):=-X_{k}\mathbf{K}^{-\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}_{k}},

for
a,b=1,\dots,s
,
s:=|\mathbf{m}|
and
k,l=1,\dots,t
. Here
\mathbf{m}
is the tuple of orders of the grouplike elements,
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{d}}
encodes the diagonal relations for the grouplike elements, and
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}
the relations for the skew-primitive elements.

Remark 3.2
.

Note that in Definition
3.1
the relations
X^{2}_{k}=0
, for
k=1,...,t
, result from the requirement that

\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{d}}_{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}_{k}}=-1,

holds, rather than being imposed independently. We include them in the definition for clarity.

Sometimes, we will choose several variables
X_{k}
to have the same commutation properties. We refer to this as
type
, and in examples we will indicate each type with a different letter
X,Y,Z
etc. if necessary.

Definition 3.3
.

By the
type
of
X_{k}
we mean the prescription of
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{d}}_{k}
and
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}_{k}
, that is
X_{l}
is of the same type as
X_{k}
if
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{d}}_{k}=\boldsymbol{\mathsf{d}}_{l}
and
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}_{k}=\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}_{l}
. Since all relations involving
X_{k}
and
X_{l}
are diagonal, we will call the two generators of
opposite type
if for
a=1,\dots,s

d_{ka}\equiv-d_{la}\;\;\mod\;m_{a}

and

u_{ka}\equiv-u_{la}\;\;\mod\;m_{a}.

This essentially means that generators of opposite type commute with all other generators over reciprocal constants. We will sometimes indicate variables of opposite type using superscripts, for instance
X^{+}_{k}
,
X^{-}_{k}
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
X^{+}_{k},X^{-}_{k}
be nilpotent generators of opposite type, then
\{X^{+}_{k},X^{-}_{k}\}=0
.

Proof.

We have directly

X^{+}_{k}X^{-}_{k}=\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{d}}_{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}_{k}}X^{-}_{k}X^{+}_{k}=(-1)^{-1}X^{-}_{k}X^{+}_{k}=-X^{-}_{k}X^{+}_{k}.

∎

Remark 3.5
.

When making general statements about
H(\mathbf{m},t,\boldsymbol{\mathsf{d}},\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}})
that do not invoke the types we will use the notation
X_{k}
for any nilpotent generator, with no superscripts. In such sense, two nilpotent generators with distinct indices, say
X_{k}
,
X_{l}
, can be of different, opposite or same type.

We can define a
monomial basis
for
H(\mathbf{m},t,\boldsymbol{\mathsf{d}},\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}})
.

Proposition 3.6
.

The Hopf algebra
H(\mathbf{m},t,\boldsymbol{\mathsf{d}},\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}})
has a monomial basis

\{\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{v}}\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{r}}|\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\mathbf{m}},\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb{Z}^{t}_{2}\}.

Definition 3.7
.

We call
T:=\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{r}}
for
\mathbf{r}=(1,\dots,1)
the
top element
.

It will play an important role in the unimodular structure we will sometimes define on
H(\mathbf{m},t,\boldsymbol{\mathsf{d}},\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}})
.
We will also need to use the adjoint action of generators on one another (see Definition
2.4
). By direct computation, we get:

Proposition 3.8
.

The adjoint action of the generators of
H
is given by the following formulas.
A grouplike
\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}}
,
\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\mathbf{m}}
acts on the skew-primitive generator
X_{k}
as

\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}}\rhd X_{k}=\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}}X_{k}(\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}})^{-1}=\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\mathbf{w}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathsf{d}}_{k}}X_{k},

a skew-primitive
X_{k}
acts on a grouplike
\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}}
as

X_{k}\rhd\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}}=\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}}S(X_{k})+X_{k}\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}}\mathbf{K}^{-\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}_{k}}=(1-\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\mathbf{w}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathsf{d}}_{k}})X_{k}\mathbf{K}^{-\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}_{k}}\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}},

and a skew-primitive
X_{k}
acts on a skew-primitive
X_{l}
as

X_{k}\rhd X_{l}=X_{l}S(X_{k})+X_{k}X_{l}\mathbf{K}^{-\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}_{k}}=(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}_{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathsf{d}}_{l}}-1)X_{k}\mathbf{K}^{-\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}_{k}}X_{l}.

3.2
Quasitriangular structure

As we will consider the bariding structuire of module catagories later on, for the reader’s convenience, we recall the form of the R-matrices that arise from the Nenciu construction.

4.1

Remark 4.7

5
Representation theory of the Nenciu family

In this section we consider some aspects of the representation theory of the algebras constructed with Nenciu’s approach. We find the decompositions of the regular and adjoint representations into indecomposable modules, their fusion rules and braiding in the category

5.1
Regular representation of

First of all, we describe a choice of a full system of idempotents for

Definition 5.1

Let for a grouplike generator

Proposition 5.2

For all

∎

We will now use this fact to prove the same for the full system of idempotents we are about to construct.

\sum_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\mathbf{m}}}\omega_{\mathbf{p}}=\mathbf{1}

∎

We can easily find the regular action of the generators

on the idempotents.

Proposition 5.5

Let

Proof.

Recall that

\omega_{\mathbf{p}}=\prod^{s}_{a=1}\varphi^{a}_{p_{a}}.

For each

affects the

as

K_{a}\varphi^{b}_{p_{b}}=\xi^{-p_{a}}_{a}\varphi^{a}_{p_{a}}

otherwise. Since the product contains exactly one

5.2
Adjoint representation

In this section we consider the decomposition of a Nenciu type algebra
H=H(\mathbf{m},t,\boldsymbol{\mathsf{d}},\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}})

(H,\rhd)
, as introduced in Proposition

Notation
.

For any nilpotent generator
X_{k}\in H
, denote
L_{k}:=\mathbf{K}^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}_{k}}

We note the following useful fact.

Proposition 5.17
.

Let
X_{k},X_{l}
be nilpotent generators, then

[X_{l},X_{k}L_{k}^{-1}]=0.

Proof.

We have directly

[X_{l},X_{k}L^{-1}]=X_{l}X_{k}L^{-1}-X_{k}L^{-1}X_{l}=X_{l}X_{k}L^{-1}-\xi^{(\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}_{k}-\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}_{k})\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathsf{d}}_{l}}X_{l}X_{k}L^{-1}=0.

∎

As a result of the associativity of the adjoint action, for any
h\in H

X_{l}\rhd X_{k}\rhd h=\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{d}}_{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}_{l}}X_{k}\rhd X_{l}\rhd h

and we observe the following.

Proposition 5.18
.

Any monomial
\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{v}}\in H
(note the change in ordering against Proposition
3.6
) can be written as

\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{L}^{-\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}}=\gamma(X_{1}L^{-1}_{1})^{a_{1}}\dots(X_{t}L^{-1}_{t})^{a_{t}}\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}},

where
\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}}=\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{v}}L_{a_{1}}\dots L_{a_{t}}
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{C}
is a constant obtained by commuting all
L^{-1}_{a_{k}}
out of the product of
X_{a_{k}}
. Moreover, such a decomposition is unique. Thus we have a re-expression of the monomial basis of
H
as

\{\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{L}^{-\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}}|\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\mathbf{m}}\text{, }\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{Z}^{t}_{2}\}.

Let us keep this notation in this section. Then, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 5.19
.

Let
x=\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{L}^{-\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}}\in H
be any monomial in the Hopf algebra as in Proposition
5.18
and
X_{k}\in H
a nilpotent generator. Then
[X_{k},x]=0
if and only if:

1.

either
X_{k}x=0
, i.e.
X_{k}
is one of the
X_{a_{l}}
,

2.

or
[X_{k},\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}}]=0
.

Proof.

Clearly if
X_{k}x=0
, then
[X_{k},x]=0
. Similarly, if
x=\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{L}^{-\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}}
, then from Proposition
5.17
we see that any
X_{k}
commutes with
\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{L}^{-\mathbf{a}}
, so we compute

[X_{k},\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{L}^{-\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}}]=\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{L}^{-\mathbf{a}}[X_{k},\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}}]=0

as long as
[X_{k},\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}}]=0
.

Conversely, assume
[X_{k},\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{L}^{-\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}}]=0
, then we again find

0=[X_{k},\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{L}^{-\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}}]=\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{L}^{-\mathbf{a}}[X_{k},\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}}]

so either
[X_{k},\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{v}}]=0
, or the expression contains a pair of zero divisors, that is
X_{k}\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{a}}=0
.
∎

Furthermore, we have the following simple proposition with an immediate proof.

Proposition 5.20
.

If
X^{+}_{k}
and
X^{-}_{k}
are nilpotent generators of opposite type, then if
w\in C(X^{+}_{k})
, then
w\in C(X^{-}_{k})
Thus, if
X^{+}_{k}\rhd w=0
, then
X^{-}_{k}\rhd w=0
.

With these observations, let now
W\subset(H,\rhd)
be a submodule under the adjoint action. Let
C(h)
be the centraliser of the element
h\in H
. If we write
w=\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{L}^{-\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}}
, then
X_{k}\rhd w=0
if
\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{v}}\in C(X_{k})
or
X_{k}
appears in
\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{a}}
. We can extend this idea in order to relate
(H,\rhd)
to the previous discussion of

.

Definition 5.21
.

Denote by

a submodule of
(H,\rhd)
under adjoint action, generated by a single element

.

Then we have the following correspondence.

Proposition 5.22
.

Let
W^{\mathbf{a}}_{\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}}}
be a
H
-module under the adjoint action, generated by the element
g:=\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{L}^{-\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{v}}\in H
. Then
W^{\mathbf{a}}_{\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}}}\cong V^{\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbf{p}}
, where
\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb{Z}^{t}_{2}
is such that whenever
r_{k}\neq 0
then
X_{k}\in C(g)
, and
\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\mathbf{m}}
are such that
K_{a}\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{r}}=\xi^{q_{a}}\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{r}}K_{a}
, and
K_{a}\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{a}}=\xi_{a}^{-p_{a}+q_{a}}\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{a}}K_{a}
.

Proof.

We exhibit the isomorphism on the monomial bases of both modules. First of all,
\mathbf{q}
and
\mathbf{r}
are determined by
\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}}
. We choose
\mathbf{r}
so that the regular action of
X_{k}
on
\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{r}}
be non-zero exactly when the adjoint action of
X_{k}
on
g
is non-zero. Then
\mathbf{q}
is determined by this choice and
\mathbf{p}
can be chosen accordingly to fix the eigenvalue. We consider the map defined on the monomial bases (given the modules are submodules of
(H,\lhd)
and
(H,\cdot)
respectively) by

f:W^{\mathbf{a}}_{\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}}}\rightarrow V^{\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbf{p}}

f:\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{L}^{-\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}}\mapsto\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{r}}\omega_{\mathbf{p}}

f:[X_{k},\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{L}^{-\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}}]L^{-1}_{k}\mapsto X_{k}\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{r}}\omega_{\mathbf{p}}

f:[X_{k},w]L^{-1}_{k}\mapsto X_{k}f(w)

with the inverse map obtained by simply inverting all the arrows. We assume
f
and its inverse are
\mathbb{C}
-linear. By the fact that all the relations are diagonal and all
X_{k}^{2}=0
, and by the choice of
\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb{Z}^{t}_{2}
we can easily see the assignment is both injective and surjective. In particular any element
w\in W^{\mathbf{a}}_{\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}}}
can be written as a linear combination of elements of the form
\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{b}}\rhd g
, for some
\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{Z}^{t}_{2}
. By linearity, it is enough to establish that the map sends
g\mapsto\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{r}}\omega_{\mathbf{p}}
and then proceed by induction on
|\mathbf{b}|
, the number of actions by nilpotent generators performed away from the generator for each monomial.

For the induction base we have to verify that for any
h=K_{a},X_{k}\in H

f(h\rhd\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{L}^{-\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}})=h\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{r}}\omega_{\mathbf{p}}.

By linearity for any
a=1,\dots,s

f(K_{a}\rhd\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{L}^{-\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}})=f(K_{a}\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{L}^{-\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}}K^{-1}_{a})=\xi^{-p_{a}+q_{a}}_{a}f(\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{L}^{-\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}})=\xi^{-p_{a}+q_{a}}_{a}\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{r}}\omega_{\mathbf{p}},

but we know that

K_{a}\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{r}}\omega_{\mathbf{p}}=\xi^{-p_{a}+q_{a}}_{a}\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{r}}\omega_{\mathbf{p}}.

Similarly if
r_{k}\neq 0

X_{k}\rhd\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{L}^{-\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}}=X_{k}\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{r}}\omega_{\mathbf{p}}=0.

However, if
r_{k}=0
, by construction

f(X_{k}\rhd\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{L}^{-\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}})=f([X_{k},\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{L}^{-\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}}]L^{-1}_{k})=X_{k}\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{r}}\omega_{\mathbf{p}}=X_{k}f(\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{L}^{-\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}}).

For the induction step, assume now
w\in W^{\mathbf{a}}_{\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}}}
is of the form
\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{b}}\rhd g
for some
|\mathbf{b}|=b<t
, so that
f(w)=\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{b}}\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{r}}\omega_{\mathbf{p}}
. By construction we have that

f(K_{a}\rhd w)=K_{a}f(w)=\xi^{-p_{a}+q_{a}-l_{a}}_{a}f(w),

for
\mathbf{l}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\mathbf{m}}
such that
K_{a}\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{b}}=\xi^{-l_{a}}_{a}\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{b}}K_{a}
, so this action is compatible with
f
.
Then for the action of
X_{k}
we assume that

f(X_{k}\rhd w)=f([X_{k},w]L^{-1}_{k})=X_{k}f(w).

Now, we consider the element
X_{k}\rhd w
.
If
K_{b}
is another grouplike generator we have

f(K_{b}\rhd X_{k}\rhd w)=\xi^{d_{kb}}X_{k}f(K_{b}\rhd w)=\xi^{d_{kb}}X_{k}K_{b}f(w)=K_{b}X_{k}f(w),

by associativity of both actions.

Also, if
X_{l}
is another nilpotent generator, by associativity of the adjoint action in Hopf algebras, we have

f(X_{l}X_{k}\rhd w)=f(X_{l}\rhd X_{k}\rhd w)=X_{l}X_{k}f(w)=\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}_{l}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathsf{d}}_{k}}X_{k}X_{l}f(w)=\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}_{l}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathsf{d}}_{k}}f(X_{k}X_{l}\rhd w).

This means that after we constructed
w
with
|\mathbf{b}|
steps away from the generator, the steps
|\mathbf{b}|+1
and
|\mathbf{b}|+2
can be applied and their order of multiplication is respected by the map.
The argument for the inverse statement that

f^{-1}(hv)=h\rhd f^{-1}(v)

is analogous.
∎

Corollary 5.23
.

For the setup of Proposition
5.22
if
C(g)\cap\mathrm{rad}\;H=0
, then
W^{\mathbf{\mathbf{0}}}_{\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}}}\cong P_{\mathbf{0}}
.

Proof.

If
C(g)\cap\mathrm{rad}\;H=0
, and
\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{0}
, then
W^{\mathbf{\mathbf{0}}}_{\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}}}\cong P_{\mathbf{0}}
, as every action
X_{k}\rhd
is non-vanishing, and the eigenvalue measured at the generator is
\mathbf{0}
because
K_{a}
all commute.
∎

Remark 5.24
.

For any
w\in W^{\mathbf{a}}_{\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}}}
we have in general

(X_{l}X_{k})\rhd w\neq[X_{l}X_{k},w]L^{-1}_{k}L^{-1}_{l}.

Nevertheless,
f
respects the associativity of the action on both sides as

[X_{l},[X_{k},w]L^{-1}_{k}]L^{-1}_{l}=(X_{l}X_{k})_{(1)}wS((X_{l}X_{k})_{(2)})=(X_{l}X_{k})\rhd w

for which we established

f((X_{l}X_{k})\rhd w)=X_{l}X_{k}f(w)=X_{l}(X_{k}f(w)).

Hence, the notation
W^{\mathbf{a}}_{\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}}}
is actually redundant, and we will continue using
V^{\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbf{p}}
instead, but still sometimes invoke the equivalence of the regular and adjoint picture. Before stating the decomposition of

into indecomposables, we need another piece of notation.

Notation
.

1.

Let
\mathcal{L}:=\{\mathbf{(\mathbf{r}}\boldsymbol{\mathsf{d}})\cdot\mathbf{v}|\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\mathbf{m}},\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb{Z}^{t}_{2}\}
. That is,
\{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\mathbf{l}},\mathbf{l}\in\mathcal{L}\}

\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{r}}

2.

Let

3.

Let
\mathbf{r}(\mathbf{w})

Theorem 5.25
.

Let
(H,\rhd)
be the adjoint representation of a Nenciu type Hopf algebra
H=H(\mathbf{m},t,\boldsymbol{\mathsf{d}},\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}})
. Then we have the following decomposition into indecomposable modules

(H,\rhd)\cong\bigoplus_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\mathbf{m}}}\bigoplus_{\mathbf{l}\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{r}(\mathbf{v})}}V^{\mathbf{r}(\mathbf{w})}_{-\mathbf{l}}.

In particular any
\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}}\notin\bigcup^{t}_{k=1}C(X_{k})
contributes a single projective
P_{\mathbf{0}}
and any
\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}}\in Z(H)
,
2^{t}
simples
S_{-\mathbf{l}}
for all
\mathbf{l}\in\mathcal{L}
.

Proof.

We use the adjoint picture for the proof, so that we consider any
V^{\mathbf{r(v)}}_{\mathbf{-l}}
as the corresponding
W^{\mathbf{a}}_{\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}}}
. Consider a generating element
g=\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}}
. We treat three cases separately.

1.

If
g\notin\bigcup^{t}_{k=1}C(X_{k})
, consider a submodule
H\rhd g
. Then we can perform all successive actions
X_{k}\rhd g
for
k-1,...,t
to produce the
2^{t}
-dimensional projective module
P_{\mathbf{p}}
for some
\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\mathbf{m}}
. Now, since all nilpotent generators are used to build the module, the eigenvalue from acting by any
K_{a}
(measured at the generator) is the constant resulting from the commutation of
K_{a}
, for
a=1,...,s
with the grouplike
g

\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{0}
, as all elements of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{K}]
commute, and we retrieve
P_{\mathbf{0}}
.

2.

If
g\in\bigcap^{t}_{k=1}C(X_{k})
, then
g
generates a trivial module, as no non-zero action

K_{a}
result in the eigenvalue
\mathbf{0}
. There are also monomials
\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{b}}\mathbf{L}^{-\mathbf{b}}g\in H

\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{b}}\rhd g

\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{Z}^{t}_{2}

(but not with the action of this module) that would be build over

2^{t}

. The available eigenvalues are given by all

\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{b}}\mathbf{L}^{\mathbf{-b}}g

3.

We are left with the intermediate case, that is
g\in\bigcap_{k\in\kappa}C(X_{k})
for some indexing set
\kappa\subsetneq\{1,...,t\}
. Moreover, let the nilpotent generators acting trivially

be collected according to
\mathbf{r}(\mathbf{w})
, or equivalently
\mathbf{a}
in the adjoint picture, and consider elements of the form
h=\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{r}}\mathbf{L}^{\mathbf{-r}}\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}}

, then
(\mathbf{r}(\mathbf{w}))_{k}\neq 0
. To each such

, so each

P_{\mathbf{0}}
that could be built over
\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{v}}

\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{r}}\mathbf{L}^{\mathbf{-r}}\mathbf{K}^{\mathbf{w}}

, or from another such element. Hence, each produces a generator

Since in each case, for each grouplike we have a

.
∎

5.3
Fusion rules for
V^{\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbf{p}}

Now that we reduced both decompositions of interest to enumeration of modules
V^{\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbf{p}}
, we can find their fusion rules under the regular action. For this, we keep in mind Theorem 3.1 of
[
Yan04
]
, which describes the rules for the case
s=1
.

Theorem 5.28
.

Let
V^{\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbf{p}}
and
V^{\mathbf{s}}_{\mathbf{q}}
be indecomposable
H
-modules, where
H=H(\mathbf{m},t,\boldsymbol{\mathsf{d}},\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}})
. Then

V^{\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbf{p}}\otimes V^{\mathbf{s}}_{\mathbf{q}}\cong\bigoplus_{\mathbf{l}\in\mathcal{L}_{(1,\dots,1)-(\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{s})-\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{s}}}V^{\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{s}}_{\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{l}},

where
g=\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{r}}\omega_{\mathbf{p}}
,
h=\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s}}\omega_{\mathbf{q}}
are the generators of
V^{\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbf{p}}
and
V^{\mathbf{s}}_{\mathbf{q}}
respectively, and
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\mathbf{m}}
are such that
K_{a}\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{r}}=\xi^{a_{a}}\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{r}}K_{a}
, and
K_{a}\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s}}=\xi^{b_{a}}\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s}}K_{a}
.

Proof.

We first explain how the eigenvalues are measured in the direct summands. Let
\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\mathbf{m}}
, such that
K_{a}\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{s}}=\xi_{a}^{c_{a}}\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{s}}K_{a}
. Note first that for any
K_{a}

\Delta(K_{a})(g\otimes h)=K_{a}g\otimes K_{a}h=\xi_{a}^{-p_{a}-q_{a}+a_{a}+b_{a}}g\otimes h,

since both
\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{a}}
and
\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{b}}
contribute to the constant in front. Now, it is clear that if
r_{k}\neq 0
and
s_{k}\neq 0
, then
\Delta(X_{k})(g\otimes h)=0
. Otherwise,

\Delta(X_{k})(g\otimes h)=g\otimes X_{k}h+X_{k}g\otimes L_{k}h\neq 0.

Thus, we wish to relate this module generated by
(g\otimes h)
to a module of the shape
V^{\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{s}}_{\mathbf{p^{\prime}}}
for some
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}
. Here, if
K_{a}\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{s}}=\xi_{a}^{a^{\prime}_{a}}\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{s}}K_{a}
, we have

K_{a}\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{s}}\omega_{\mathbf{p^{\prime}}}=\xi_{a}^{a^{\prime}_{a}-p^{\prime}_{a}}\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{s}}\omega_{\mathbf{p^{\prime}}}.

Now, we need to have
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}=\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{q}
and
\mathbf{a}^{\prime}=\mathbf{c}
, but this does not conform to the constant
\xi_{a}^{-p_{a}-q_{a}+a_{a}+b_{a}}
. Thus, we shift the eigenvalue of the underlying idempotent generator to
\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}
, resulting in the eigenvalues of the generators
g\otimes h
and
\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{s}}\omega_{\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}}
being the same.

Now, we show the direct sum decomposition of
V^{\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbf{p}}\otimes V^{\mathbf{s}}_{\mathbf{q}}
, by finding the corresponding generators and showing the submodules do not intersect. Let
\mathbf{o}=(1,\dots,1)-(\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{s})-\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{s}
be the tuple selecting all such
X_{k}
that
X_{k}g\neq 0
and
X_{k}h\neq 0
. Let
\mathbf{l}\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{o}}
, and let
Y=\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{o}}
be constructed using such
X_{k}
, so that we have
K_{a}\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{o}}=\xi_{a}^{l_{a}}\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{o}}K_{a}
for
\mathbf{o}\in\mathbb{Z}^{t}_{2}
. Then a generator
(Yg)\otimes h
gives rise to a submodule
V^{\mathbf{\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{s}}}_{\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{l}}
, since any of the
X_{k}
such that
(\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{s})_{k}=0
acts non-trivially, and the eigenvalue of the idempotent is obtained as discussed above.

There are exactly
2^{t-|\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{s}|-|\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{s}|}
such
Y
and since each submodule will have dimension
2^{t-|\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{s}|}
, their total dimension is
2^{(t-|\mathbf{r}|)(t-|\mathbf{s}|)}
, the same as the dimension of
V^{\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbf{p}}\otimes V^{\mathbf{s}}_{\mathbf{q}}
. This is because
|\mathbf{r}|+|\mathbf{s}|=|\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{s}|+2|\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{s}|
, as the entries of the tuples are in the set
\{0,1\}
.

Therefore, we have left to show that for distinct
Y_{1}
,
Y_{2}
the resulting modules
V^{\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{s}}_{\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{l}}
and
V^{\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{s}}_{\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{l}^{\prime}}
are disjoint, which will then imply their sum is direct. Let
Y_{\alpha}^{\prime}\in H
, for
\alpha=1,2
be a monomial composed of the remaining
X_{k}
that act non-trivially on
g\otimes h
. These include such
X_{k}
that
r_{k}=0
or
s_{k}=0
. Then,

\Delta(Y^{\prime}_{\alpha})(Y_{\alpha}(g\otimes h))=\mathrm{Soc}(V^{\mathbf{\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{s}}}_{\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{l}_{\alpha}})

as modules. Since the modules
\mathrm{Soc}(V^{\mathbf{\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{s}}}_{\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{l}_{\alpha}})
are 1-dimensional, if
Y_{1}\neq Y_{2}
, then

\Delta(Y^{\prime}_{1})((Y_{1}g)\otimes h)\neq\Delta(Y^{\prime}_{2})((Y_{2}g)\otimes h).

This is because there exists at least one
X_{l}
such that
r_{l}=0
and
s_{l}=0
, so at least one action
\Delta(X_{l})((Y_{\alpha}g)\otimes h)
results in both terms after the action of
\Delta(X_{l})
being non-zero, and thus providing linearly independent terms for
\alpha=1,2
. Then

\mathrm{Soc}(V^{\mathbf{\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{s}}}_{\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{l}_{1}})\neq\mathrm{Soc}(V^{\mathbf{\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{s}}}_{\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{l}_{2}}).

Thus, by the contrapositive of Corollary
5.22
, we have that

V^{\mathbf{\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{s}}}_{\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{l}_{1}}\cap V^{\mathbf{\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{s}}}_{\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{l}_{2}}=0,

and so the decomposition of the tensor product into submodules is direct.
∎

Example 5.29
.

Consider
\operatorname{SF}_{2n}
, and its two indecomposable projectives
P_{0},P_{1}
. The fusion rules between them are

P_{0}\otimes P_{0}=P_{1}\otimes P_{1}=2^{2n-1}P_{0}\oplus 2^{2n-1}P_{1},

P_{0}\otimes P_{1}=2^{2n}P_{1},

where the difference in the final rule comes from the fact that while all nilpotent elements commute with
K^{0}=K^{1+1}=\mathbf{1}
, only even elements commute with
K^{1}=K
. So
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{r}(1)}=\{0,\dots,0\}
with multiplicity
2^{2n}
, as opposed to
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{r}(0)}=\{0,1,\dots,0,1\}
both with half the multiplicity.

We consider also another example.

Example 5.30
.

Consider
\operatorname{N}_{2}
of Example

at

5.4
Some braiding properties of
V^{\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbf{p}}
.

In this section, we investigate the braiding of the modules
V^{\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbf{p}}
for a given Nenciu type Hopf algebra
H(\mathbf{m},t,\boldsymbol{\mathsf{d}},\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}})
, where we assume the following:

1.

The R-matrix has the shape
R_{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{z}}}R_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
where
R_{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{z}}}
satisfies
R_{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{z}}}R_{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{z}},21}=\mathbf{1}\otimes\mathbf{1}
and is constructed with only grouplike generators, and
R_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
has the shape

R_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}=\exp\left(\sum^{t_{2}}_{l=1}\alpha_{l}(Z_{l}^{+}\otimes L_{l}Z_{l}^{-}-Z_{l}^{-}\otimes L_{l}Z_{l}^{+})\right)

where
\alpha_{l}\in\mathbb{C}
and
Z_{l}^{\pm}
are of opposite type for
l_{1},l_{2}=1,\dots,t_{2}
. Moreover
\{Z_{l_{1}}^{\pm},Z_{l_{2}}^{\pm}\}=\{Z_{l_{1}}^{\pm},Z_{l_{2}}^{\mp}\}=0
.

2.

From Proposition
3.18
the monodromy matrix is

M:=R_{21}R=\exp\left(2\sum^{t_{2}}_{l=1}\alpha_{l}(Z_{l}^{+}\otimes L_{l}Z_{l}^{-}-Z_{l}^{-}\otimes L_{l}Z_{l}^{+})\right).

3.

There is a (possibly empty) subset of nilpotent generators,
X_{k}
,
k=1,\dots,t_{1}
that do not participate in
R_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
and such that
\{X_{k},Z^{\pm}_{l}\}=0
.

With these assumptions, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 5.31
.

Let
(H,R)
be a Nenciu type, quasitriangular Hopf algebra and
V^{\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbf{p}},V^{\mathbf{s}}_{\mathbf{q}}\in H\operatorname{-mod}
. Then

c^{2}(V^{\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbf{p}}\otimes V^{\mathbf{s}}_{\mathbf{q}})=(V^{\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbf{p}}\otimes V^{\mathbf{s}}_{\mathbf{q}}),

if and only if for each entry
Z^{\pm}_{l}
, either
\alpha_{l}=0
or the corresponding
r_{l}\neq 0
or
s_{l}\neq 0
. Otherwise, the order of the braiding map
c
is infinite.

Proof.

Assume first that for each entry
Z^{\pm}_{l}
, either
\alpha_{l}=0
or the corresponding
r_{l}\neq 0
or
s_{l}\neq 0
. This means that either a particular pair of generators
Z^{\pm}_{l}
is excluded from the
R_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
by setting the parameter
\alpha_{l}=0
, or choosing both
Z^{\pm}_{l}
to act trivially on either
V^{\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbf{p}}
or
V^{\mathbf{s}}_{\mathbf{q}}
. It is enough that one of the modules has zero action for each term
Z_{l}^{\pm}\otimes LZ_{l}^{\mp}
.
Note that
c^{2}
is equivalent to the action of
M
by associativity of the action, so then

M^{\prime}V^{\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbf{p}}\otimes M^{\prime\prime}V^{\mathbf{s}}_{\mathbf{q}}=\mathbf{1}V^{\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbf{p}}\otimes\mathbf{1}V^{\mathbf{s}}_{\mathbf{q}}=V^{\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbf{p}}\otimes V^{\mathbf{s}}_{\mathbf{q}},

as
R_{\mathbf{z}}R_{\mathbf{z},21}=\mathbf{1}\otimes\mathbf{1}
.

Coversely, assume now that
c^{2}(V^{\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbf{p}}\otimes V^{\mathbf{s}}_{\mathbf{q}})=(V^{\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbf{p}}\otimes V^{\mathbf{s}}_{\mathbf{q}})
. By above, equivalently

M^{\prime}V^{\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbf{p}}\otimes M^{\prime\prime}V^{\mathbf{s}}_{\mathbf{q}}=V^{\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbf{p}}\otimes V^{\mathbf{s}}_{\mathbf{q}}.

We wish to show this is not true unless the condition is fulfilled. It is enough to consider the product of the generators
g\otimes h
, then

M^{\prime}g\otimes M^{\prime\prime}h=g\otimes h+2\sum^{t_{2}}_{l=1}\alpha_{l}(Z_{l}^{+}\otimes L_{l}Z_{l}^{-}-Z_{l}^{-}\otimes L_{l}Z_{l}^{+})(g\otimes h)+\mathcal{O}((Z_{l}^{\pm})^{2}),

where the ”big O” notation indicates terms at least quadratic in the generators
Z^{\pm}_{l}
. By extending the monomial basis of
H
to
H\otimes H
in the obvious way, we see that since all relations are diagonal, the action of any skew-primitive
X_{k}
or
Z^{\pm}_{l}
cannot result in distinct terms in the sum becoming linearly dependent. Thus, the sum does not vanish, unless for each
(Z_{l}^{\pm}\otimes L_{l}Z_{l}^{\mp})
, either
\alpha_{l}=0
, or both
Z_{l}^{\pm}
act trivially on the respective modules. In the latter case, it is necessary for both
Z^{\pm}_{l}
so that
(Z_{l}^{\pm}\otimes L_{l}Z_{l}^{\mp})
and
(Z_{l}^{\mp}\otimes L_{l}Z_{l}^{\pm})
vanish. Hence, we retrieve the desired condition.

For the final statement, note that by associativity for
2m=0,2,\dots

c^{2m}(V^{\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbf{p}}\otimes V^{\mathbf{s}}_{\mathbf{q}})=(M^{m})^{\prime}V^{\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbf{p}}\otimes(M^{m})^{\prime\prime}V^{\mathbf{s}}_{\mathbf{q}}

with
M^{0}:=\mathbf{1}\otimes\mathbf{1}
, and

c^{2m+1}(V^{\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbf{p}}\otimes V^{\mathbf{s}}_{\mathbf{q}})=R_{\mathbf{z},21}R_{\boldsymbol{\alpha},21}\left((M^{m})^{\prime\prime}V^{\mathbf{s}}_{\mathbf{q}}\otimes(M^{m})^{\prime}V^{\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbf{p}}\right).

Since
m
is even, clearly this is the action of

\displaystyle\exp\left(2m\sum^{t_{2}}_{l=1}\alpha_{l}(Z_{l}^{+}\otimes L_{l}Z_{l}^{-}-Z_{l}^{-}\otimes L_{l}Z_{l}^{+})\right)=\mathbf{1}\otimes\mathbf{1}+2m\sum^{t_{2}}_{l=1}\alpha_{l}(Z_{l}^{+}\otimes L_{l}Z_{l}^{-}-Z_{l}^{-}\otimes L_{l}Z_{l}^{+})+\mathcal{O}((Z_{l}^{\pm})^{2}),

and as above the sum vanishes if and only if the condition of the theorem is fulfilled. In the odd case, it is enough to verify that for any
2m=0,2,\dots
, the
2m+1
st iteration is non-zero. But we are considering the following action on the generator
h\otimes g

\displaystyle R_{\mathbf{z},21}

\displaystyle\exp\left(\sum^{t_{2}}_{l=1}\alpha_{l}(L_{l}Z_{l}^{-}\otimes Z_{l}^{+}-L_{l}Z_{l}^{+}\otimes Z_{l}^{-})\right)\exp\left(2m\sum^{t_{2}}_{l=1}\alpha_{l}(L_{l}Z_{l}^{-}\otimes Z_{l}^{+}-L_{l}Z_{l}^{+}\otimes Z_{l}^{-})\right)(h\otimes g)=

\displaystyle=R_{\mathbf{z},21}

\displaystyle\exp\left((2m+1)\sum^{t_{2}}_{l=1}\alpha_{l}(L_{l}Z_{l}^{-}\otimes Z_{l}^{+}-L_{l}Z_{l}^{+}\otimes Z_{l}^{-})\right)(h\otimes g)

\displaystyle=R_{\mathbf{z},21}

\displaystyle\left(h\otimes g+(2m+1)\sum^{t_{2}}_{l=1}\alpha_{l}(L_{l}Z_{l}^{-}\otimes Z_{l}^{+}-L_{l}Z_{l}^{+}\otimes Z_{l}^{-})(h\otimes g)+\mathcal{O}((Z_{l}^{\pm})^{2})\right),

where the sum is again non-vanishing, unless the condition is fulfilled, by the same reasoning. This completes the proof.
∎

7.1

We note that

7.2
Examples

In this subsection we relate the results from the previous sections to the construction in
[
CGHPM23
]
. We start with the following observation.

Proposition 7.4
.

Let
H
be a unimodular, strongly non-factorizable ribbon Hopf algebra. Then it is neither chromatic non-degenerate, nor chromatic compact.

Proof.

By definition of non-factorizability, we have
\lambda(S(M^{\prime}))M^{\prime\prime}=0
, which implies
\Delta^{P_{\mathbbm{1}}}_{0}=0
, and so
H
is not chromatic non-degenerate. But we established that
\Lambda_{P_{\mathbbm{1}}}\neq 0
. So for
\Delta^{P_{\mathbbm{1}}}_{0}=\zeta\Lambda_{P_{\mathbbm{1}}}
to hold, we need

, which negates chromatic compactness as well.
∎

In particular, this holds for all non-factorizable examples of Nenciu type and their biproducts with
u_{q}\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
.

However, these arguments do not hold if we drop the strong non-factorizability condition, as exhibited by another class of examples.

Proposition 7.5
.

Let

and
U=u_{q}\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
carrying the structures from the Proposition
4.13
, such that all entries in
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
are non-zero. Then

From

M_{U}=\frac{1}{r^{\prime\prime}}\sum^{r^{\prime}-1}_{a,b=0}\sum^{r^{\prime\prime}-1}_{c,d=0}\frac{\{1\}^{a+b}}{[a]![b]!}q^{\frac{a(a-1)+b(b-1)}{2}+2b^{2}+4dc}K^{-b+2c}E^{a}F^{b}\otimes K^{b+2d}E^{b}F^{a}.

Thus, we can deduce for

, the monodromy matrix of

\lambda(M^{\prime})M^{\prime\prime}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{r^{\prime\prime}}}\frac{\{1\}^{r^{\prime}-1}}{[r^{\prime}-1]!}2^{2n}\prod^{n}_{l}\left(\alpha^{2}_{l}\right)\sum^{r^{\prime\prime}-1}_{d=0}q^{r^{\prime\prime}-4d}K^{2d-1}E^{r^{\prime}-1}F^{r^{\prime}-1}\prod^{n}_{l}\left(Z^{+}_{l}Z^{-}_{l}\right).

Now this element contains the top nilpotent element in the monomial basis, so the action on

sends the generating idempotent

to the socle of the module, with a multiplicative non-zero constant in front. The constant corresponds to the part of the expression corresponding to the piece of the R-matrix generated by the grouplike elements. The constant is non-zero as long as all

are, which we assumed (for the non-vanishing of the

If we compare it with the action cointegral from the Proposition

∎

Corollary 7.6
.

Let
H=\operatorname{SF}_{2n}
and
U=u_{q}\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
carrying the structures from the Proposition

, such that all entries in
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
are non-zero. Then

Proof.

This follows by applying

∎

Proposition

and Corollary

show that the two types of strongly non-factorizable Hopf algebras described in previous sections will not produce 4d TQFTs capable of distinguishing exotic structures, regardless of the underlying field. On the other hand the slightly factorizable example remains susceptible to stabilisation. Moreover, the discussion in the introduction of

]
suggests that invariants corresponding to categories with semisimple Müger centre are not expected to be powerful. However, we can remedy some these problems (bar stabilisation) by introducing another factor to the biproduct and changing the underlying field to one of finite characteristic.

7.3

Fix a field

of prime characteristic

Proposition 7.7

\Lambda
on the projective cover of the unit
P_{\mathbbm{1}}\in U\ltimes H\rtimes G\operatorname{-mod}
. Let
e\in U\ltimes H\rtimes G
be the corresponding idempotent (which always exists for a finite dimensional
\mathbb{K}
-algebra). Clearly
\sigma_{t}e=\epsilon(\sigma_{t})e=e
. But

\left(\sum^{p!}_{t=1}\sigma_{t}\right)e=p!\,e=0.

Thus,
\Lambda_{P_{\mathbbm{1}}}=0
and
U\ltimes H\rtimes G\operatorname{-mod}
is not chromatic compact.
∎

Proposition 7.17
.

There is an embedding of symmetric categories
F:\mathbb{K}[S_{p}]\operatorname{-mod}\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}_{(2)}(U\ltimes H\rtimes G)\operatorname{-mod}
. In particular,
\mathcal{Z}_{(2)}(U\ltimes H\rtimes G)\operatorname{-mod}
is a non-semisimple symmetric category.

Proof.

The functor
F
is induced by the projection of the semi-direct product factor
U\ltimes H\rtimes G\twoheadrightarrow G
- clearly any representation of
G
can be made one of
U\ltimes H\rtimes G
by trivially extending the action. Since there is no contribution of any
\sigma_{t}
in the R-matrix, this subcategory category remains symmetric.

Non-semisimplicity is a consequence of the Machke’s theorem.
∎

Remark 7.18
.

Proposition
7.17
is an improvement on both Remark
4.14
and Proposition
7.9
. The R-matrix of
U\ltimes H
is not symmetric but the algebra is only slightly factorizable. On the other hand the R-matrix is only symmetric (trivial) for
G
alone. Both components are twist degenerate, and so is the result.

Remark 7.19
.

It is explained in
[
CGHPM23
]
that for the TQFT defined there to detect exotic pairs of 4-manifolds,
\mathcal{C}
has to be chromatic non-degenerate but not chromatic compact. Moreover, it is required that
\lambda(M)^{\prime}\otimes\lambda(S(M^{\prime\prime}))
be non-invertible in
\mathbb{K}
. The latter condition is a consequence of the fact that
\lambda(M)^{\prime}\otimes\lambda(S(M^{\prime\prime}))
is the invariant of
S^{2}\times S^{2}
and by the famous theorem of Gompf (see for example
[
GS99
]
) for any pair of homeomorphic compact oriented 4-manifolds
X
and
Y
, there exists an integer
l\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that
X\#^{l}S^{2}\times S^{2}
and
Y\#^{l}S^{2}\times S^{2}
are diffeomorphic, and so cannot be distinguished if this invariant is invertible. This phenomenon is usually known as
S^{2}\times S^{2}
-
stabilization
or just
stabilization
.

We finish by observing that
U\ltimes H\rtimes G
remains susceptible to stabilisation.

Proposition 7.20
.

Let
U\ltimes H\rtimes G
be as above and let
M
be its monodromy matrix. Then
\lambda(S(M^{\prime}))\lambda(M^{\prime\prime})\neq 0
. Thus, the resulting invariant is susceptible to stablilisation.

Proof.

The component
G
does not participate non-trivially in the R-matrix (hence the monodromy) and does not affect the element of the monomial basis picked up by
\lambda
. Thus, the proof follows by the same argument as in Proposition
7.5
and Corollary
7.6

∎

Remark 7.21
.

One could adapt a similar strategy for
u_{q}\mathfrak{g}\rtimes S_{p}
, where
u_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
is the small quantum group for a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
larger than
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
, taken over
\mathbb{K}
with suitable assumptions on
p
and
q
. Then the action of
S_{p}
could be defined to permute
E_{i}
generators among themselves, and analogously
F_{i}
. From the Hopf algebraic perspective we adapt in this paper, provided the unimodular and ribbon properties remain fulfilled, it is reasonable to expect the resulting product will also be chromatic non-degenerate but not chromatic compact, but will remain susceptible to stabilisation.

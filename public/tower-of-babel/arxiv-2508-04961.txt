Constraints on Symmetric Dark Matter from Neutron Star Capture and Collapse
Yuxin Liu, Zhen Liu, Maxim Pospelov, Sanjay Reddy

Abstract
Dark matter (DM) models with a conserved particle − \displaystyle- antiparticle number, n χ − n χ ~ \displaystyle n_{\chi}-n_{\tilde{\chi}} , and the asymmetry in the cosmological abundance n χ ≠ n χ ~ \displaystyle n_{\chi}\neq n_{\tilde{\chi}} , are known to be challenged by the existence of old neutron stars (NSs), as the sufficient accumulation of DM will lead to the collapse of NSs into black holes. We demonstrate that the applicability of these constraints is much wider and covers models with symmetric populations of DM, n χ = n χ ~ \displaystyle n_{\chi}=n_{\tilde{\chi}} , as the process of DM capture regulated by a nucleon-DM scattering can be inherently asymmetric, σ χ ​ n ≠ σ χ ~ ​ n \displaystyle\sigma_{\chi n}\neq\sigma_{\tilde{\chi}n} . The asymmetry is induced by the interference of different types of χ \displaystyle\chi - n \displaystyle n interactions, provided that their combination is odd under charge conjugation in the DM sector, C χ \displaystyle C_{\chi} , and even under combined parity P χ + n \displaystyle P_{\chi+n} . We provide a complete analysis of DM-nucleon bilinear χ \displaystyle\chi - n \displaystyle n interactions and find that this asymmetry is very generic. Using canonical NS parameters and local DM halo inputs, we exclude spin‑averaged scattering cross sections down to σ n ​ χ ≳ 10 − 46 ​ cm 2 \displaystyle\sigma_{n\chi}\!\gtrsim\!10^{-46}\,{\rm cm}^{2} at DM mass m χ ≲ 10 10 ​ GeV \displaystyle m_{\chi}\!\lesssim\!10^{10}\,{\rm GeV} for the maximally asymmetric capture rate, and show that the constraints persist down to very small values of the cross-section asymmetry, 𝒜 = ( σ χ ​ n − σ χ ~ ​ n ) / ( σ χ ​ n + σ χ ~ ​ n ) ≳ 10 − 5 \displaystyle{\cal A}=(\sigma_{\chi n}-\sigma_{\tilde{\chi}n})/(\sigma_{\chi n}+\sigma_{\tilde{\chi}n})\gtrsim 10^{-5} .

We characterize the interaction between fermionic DM and nucleons through the following effective interactions:

The process by which astrophysical bodies capture dark matter has been extensively studied in the literature
Gould 1987

Garani and Palomares-Ruiz 2017

;
Bramante and Raj 2024
;
Bhattacharya et al. 2023
;
Ge et al. 2024

\displaystyle\to
BH transmutation is far more intricate

;

;
Bramante and Raj 2024
, and is left for future work.
We follow standard treatment of DM capture, and adapt it to symmetric DM with non-zero
\displaystyle{\cal A}
, assumed to be positive.

For a single, isotropic in the
\displaystyle\chi n
center of mass frame scattering event, the average energy loss experienced by a DM particle is given by
\displaystyle m_{n}(1+z)^{2}v_{\rm esc}^{2}

Dasgupta et al. 2020
, where the DM velocity inside the neutron star is approximated by the escape velocity at the surface of the NS,
\displaystyle v_{\rm esc}=\sqrt{2GM_{*}/R_{*}}\approx\mathcal{O}(0.6)
, and
\displaystyle 1+z=(1-2GM_{*}/R_{*})^{-1/2}
is the corresponding gravitational redshift factor. Here
\displaystyle M_{*}
and
\displaystyle R_{*}
denote the mass and radius of the NS, respectively. Noticeable deviation from the above approximation can occur
Dasgupta et al. 2020
;
Bramante and Raj 2024
for mostly forward scattering due to
e.g.
a light force mediator.

To be gravitationally captured by a NS, even a small loss of kinetic energy by a
\displaystyle\chi
-particle can prevent its escape to asymptotic infinity, provided that

\Delta E_{\chi}>E_{\rm kin}^{\infty}=m_{\chi}v_{\rm rel}^{2}/2\sim 10^{-6}m_{\chi},

(8)

where
\displaystyle v_{\rm rel}\sim 10^{-3}c
is the
\displaystyle\chi
-NS relative velocity at large separation. For DM masses
\displaystyle m_{\chi}\lesssim 10^{6}m_{n}
, this energy can be easily dissipated through a single scattering event. For heavier DM, an average of
\displaystyle E_{\rm kin}^{\infty}/\Delta E_{\chi}\sim 10^{6}m_{n}/m_{\chi}
scattering events per each trapped
\displaystyle\chi
particle is required.
The capture probability, for an effectively thin target regime, can then be modeled via a Poisson distribution:

P_{\rm cap}^{\chi}\approx\frac{\sigma_{\chi n}}{\sigma_{0}}\times\min\left\{1,\frac{E_{\rm kin}^{\infty}}{\Delta E_{\chi}}\right\},

(9)

where
\displaystyle\sigma_{n\chi}
is the DM-nucleon scattering cross-section, and
\displaystyle\sigma_{0}\approx\pi R_{*}^{2}m_{n}/M_{*}\approx 2.5\times 10^{-45}~{\rm cm}^{2}
is an estimate of the NS’s geometric capture cross-section. Further refinement of these estimates can come from accounting for DM self-interaction
McDermott et al. 2012
;
Bramante et al. 2013
, consideration of velocity distribution
Carr and Sakellariadou 1999
;
Bramante et al. 2017
and other effects
Gould 1987
;
de Lavallaz and Fairbairn 2010
;
Bertoni et al. 2013
.

The same treatment applies for the anti-DM capture probability,
\displaystyle P_{\rm cap}^{\tilde{\chi}}
. The total capture rate per unit time for DM and anti-DM in the NS is then:

C_{\chi/\tilde{\chi}}=n_{\chi/\tilde{\chi}}v_{\rm rel}\times\pi b^{2}\times P_{\rm cap}^{\chi/\tilde{\chi}},

(10)

and for numerical results we use
\displaystyle v_{\rm rel}\approx 350~{\rm km/s}
and
\displaystyle n_{\chi/\tilde{\chi}}=n_{\rm DM}/2\approx m_{\chi}^{-1}\times 0.2\,{\rm GeV/cm}^{3}

Carr and Sakellariadou 1999
;
Zhou et al. 2023
as a typical input DM number density. The gravitational focusing results in an impact parameter much larger than the NS radius,
\displaystyle b=R_{*}(1+z)(v_{\rm esc}/v_{\rm rel})\sim 10^{3}R_{*}
.
This analysis equally applies to spin-independent and spin-dependent
\displaystyle\chi
-
\displaystyle n
scattering.

Once a net population of captured DM particles builds up, we can safely assume that any subsequently captured anti-DM particles will quickly annihilate with resident DM. Therefore, the growth rate of the net number of DM particles is given by the difference between the DM and anti-DM capture rates as described in Eq. (
10
):

\frac{{\rm d}N_{\chi}}{{\rm d}t}=C_{\chi}-C_{\tilde{\chi}}\overset{P_{\rm cap}^{\chi}\ll 1}{\longrightarrow}n_{\rm DM}v_{\rm rel}\times\pi b^{2}\times P_{\rm cap}^{\chi}\frac{\mathcal{A}}{1+\mathcal{A}},

(11)

where the second limit is valid in the optically thin regime.
In the large cross-section regime, where the approximated expression of
\displaystyle P^{\chi}_{\rm cap}\lower 3.01385pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textstyle>}}{{\sim}}\;$}1
, the capture probability saturates to unity for both DM and anti-DM, and the asymmetry of cross sections does not result in the preferential DM built-up inside NS.

The captured dark matter particles thermalize within the NS core quickly within
\displaystyle t_{\rm th}

Bertoni et al. 2013
, forming a gravitationally bound halo. Once the total mass of captured DM exceeds a critical threshold
\displaystyle M_{\rm crit}
, this halo undergoes catastrophic collapse to form a BH. If the accretion rate of this black hole exceeds its Hawking evaporation rate, the black hole will inevitably grow and quickly consume the entire NS, within a time interval
\displaystyle t_{\rm grow}
.

For the collapse to occur, several conditions must be satisfied: the dark matter must trigger a self-gravitating instability, overcome its own thermal pressure, and reach the Chandrasekhar limit.
The Chandrasekhar limit sets the critical mass
\displaystyle M_{\rm crit}
for the dark matter parameter space of interest:

M_{\rm crit}\approx\frac{M_{\rm pl}^{3}}{m_{\chi}^{2}}\approx 1.6\times 10^{-16}M_{\odot}\left(\frac{10^{8}~{\rm GeV}}{m_{\chi}}\right)^{2},

(12)

where
\displaystyle M_{\rm pl}=G^{-1/2}
is the Planck mass, and
\displaystyle M_{\odot}
is the solar mass. Further review and explicit expressions for the rates of capture, thermalization and collapse can be found in the Supplemental Material.

Constraints on Symmetric Dark Matter:

Thousands of pulsars have been observed in the Solar System neighborhood
Manchester et al. 2005
.
Meanwhile, old NSs with ages
\displaystyle t_{\rm age}\approx 10^{10}~{\rm yr}
are abundant in our Galaxy; for instance, J1719-1438 is located 336 pc away with an estimated age of 11.4 Gyr, and J1737-0811 is located 206 pc away and is believed to be 8.34 Gyr old
Manchester et al. 2005
.

Based on these observations, we adopt a typical NS mass
\displaystyle M_{*}=1.5M_{\odot}
, radius
\displaystyle R_{*}=12~{\rm km}
, and age
\displaystyle t_{\rm age}=10^{10}~{\rm yr}
for our analysis. The survival of such NSs imposes the condition:

\frac{M_{\rm crit}}{m_{\chi}\,{\rm d}N_{\chi}/{\rm d}t}+t_{\rm th}+t_{\rm grow}\geq t_{\rm age},

(13)

where the first term represents the accretion time and dominates over the other two timescales in the solar vicinity for the DM parameter space considered. We evaluate this constraint on the
\displaystyle\{m_{\chi},\sigma_{\chi n},{\cal A}\}
parameter space for the fermionic DM.

The excluded parameter regions are shown as colored regions in Fig.
2
for various asymmetry factors
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}
. As expected, the exclusion region shrinks as
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}
decreases, since a larger fraction of captured DM is annihilated by anti-DM. Moreover, one can recover existing constraints on asymmetric DM scenarios
de Lavallaz and Fairbairn 2010
by setting
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=1
in our framework and taking twice the (anti-)DM density used in our calculation.

Summary and Outlook:

We have demonstrated that the mere existence of old NSs imposes powerful constraints even on
symmetric
DM. This new insight stems from the observation that operator interference leads to an asymmetric capture rate between DM and anti-DM. Our effective field theory framework identifies the minimal
\displaystyle C_{\chi}
- and
\displaystyle C_{\chi}P_{\chi+n}
-odd interference amplitudes required to generate such an asymmetry, and we specify ultraviolet completions that realize these features. By applying established results for capture rates, thermalization, and collapse dynamics, we show that the continued existence of NSs in the solar neighborhood already excludes significant regions of parameter space for symmetric DM, complementing terrestrial direct detection experiments.

Our work highlights a gap in the current literature: existing NS constraints have primarily focused on intrinsically asymmetric DM via NS collapse, which requires an additional process in the early universe that would ensure the dominance of DM or anti-DM.
By showing that interference-driven capture asymmetries open a viable collapse channel even for
symmetric
DM, we extend the ability of NS probes into a new regime of DM models. Our work reinforces neutron stars as unique astrophysical laboratories, whose extreme densities and gravitational fields enable novel detection pathways for DM, with promising prospects for future observational accomplishments. Future radio pulsar surveys, gravitational wave channels, and infrared searches for ultra-cold NSs will strengthen our understanding of NS structures and properties
Carilli and Rawlings 2004
;
Gardner et al. 2006
;
Nan et al. 2011
;
Miller and Zhao 2023
.

Above the critical BH mass, the Hawking evaporation effect can be neglected, and the growth time of the mini BH can be estimated as the Bondi accretion process:

\frac{{\rm d}M_{\rm BH}}{{\rm d}t}=\frac{4\pi\rho_{*}G^{2}M_{\rm BH}^{2}}{c_{*}^{3}}.

(S21)

Such process has a definite timescale of growth, as the explicit solution to this equation is given by

M_{\rm BH}(t)=\frac{M_{\rm crit}}{1-M_{\rm crit}4\pi\rho_{*}G^{2}t/{c_{*}^{3}}},

\displaystyle t_{\rm growth}=\frac{c_{*}^{3}}{4\pi\rho_{*}G^{2}M_{\rm crit}}\approx 1.01\times 10^{8}{\rm yr}\left(\frac{10^{-20}M_{\odot}}{M_{\rm crit}}\right)\left(\frac{c_{*}}{0.5c}\right)^{3}\left(\frac{10^{15}{\rm g}/{\rm cm}^{3}}{\rho_{*}}\right).

(S23)

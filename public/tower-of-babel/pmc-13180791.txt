Pathological Complete Response in Advanced Hepatocellular Carcinoma With Portal Vein Tumor Thrombus Following Conversion Therapy With TACE, HAIC, Lenvatinib, and Sintilimab: A Case Report
Authors: Weidong Zhang, Huihui Shen, Xinglong Wang, Jingjing Gu, Minghai Shen, Qinghua Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTHepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) with portal vein tumor thrombus (PVTT) is an advanced disease with poor prognosis and limited treatment options. Conversion therapy integrating locoregional and systemic modalities may expand surgical eligibility, but achieving pathological complete response (pCR) remains rare. We report an adult patient with chronic hepatitis B and China Liver Cancer Staging (CNLC) stage IIIb HCC with extensive PVTT. Baseline alpha‐fetoprotein (AFP) was 84,246 μg/L and des‐γ‐carboxy prothrombin (DCP) 3605 mAU/mL. After three cycles of transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) plus hepatic arterial infusion chemotherapy (HAIC) combined with lenvatinib and sintilimab, the tumor markedly regressed, PVTT was downstaged from type III to type II, and tumor markers declined (AFP 115 μg/L, DCP 47.1 mAU/mL). Thirty‐two days after cessation of systemic therapy, the patient underwent left hemihepatectomy with portal vein thrombectomy, and histopathology confirmed pCR with complete tumor necrosis and no viable cells in the liver or thrombus. Postoperative follow‐up imaging and serial tumor markers demonstrated no recurrence, with AFP normalizing to 5.6 μg/L at 1 month and remaining within normal range during 9 months of follow‐up after discontinuation of adjuvant therapy. The patient recovered well and was discharged 2 weeks postoperatively. This case highlights the potential of multimodal conversion therapy combining TACE, HAIC, targeted therapy, and immunotherapy to induce durable pCR in advanced HCC with PVTT, offering a promising strategy to improve outcomes in this highly challenging population.

Introduction

HCC is the most common primary liver cancer and a major cause of cancer burden worldwide, especially in areas with a high burden of chronic hepatitis B virus (HBV) infection [1]. HCC is often diagnosed at an advanced clinical stage despite recent improvements in surveillance. PVTT is an independent poor prognostic factor arising in 10%–40% of HCC patients at presentation [2]. PVTT is associated with both invasive tumor biology and poor hepatic perfusion and function that results in rapid clinical deterioration. The median overall survival (OS) of patients with PVTT is 2–4 months without treatment [3]. There are limited survival benefits with conventional therapies such as TACE and HAIC [4, 5]. Systemic therapies, including tyrosine kinase inhibitors, for example, sorafenib and lenvatinib, and immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), for example, sintilimab, have provided new options for advanced HCC [6]. Given the survival benefits from anti‐PD‐1/PD‐L1 antibodies with anti‐angiogenic agents, such as sintilimab plus a bevacizumab biosimilar, these regimens have become the standard first‐line treatment [7]. Recent studies have demonstrated that locoregional therapies combined with systemic therapies could present a synergistic effect, in which locoregional therapies induce tumor antigen release and vascular modulation, whereas tyrosine kinase inhibitors and immune checkpoint inhibitors could mediate immune response and inhibit angiogenesis [8].

Currently, sintilimab plus bevacizumab is the standard first‐line treatment for advanced HCC [7]. However, for patients with PVTT, the addition of locoregional therapies such as TACE and HAIC has been shown to improve tumor response and survival outcomes compared with systemic therapy alone [5, 9]. Lenvatinib was chosen over bevacizumab in this case due to its dual antiangiogenic and antitumor effects as well as its synergistic potential when combined with TACE/HAIC [6]. However, it is extremely rare to obtain pCR in HCC with PVTT. pCR is a well‐recognized surrogate endpoint for long‐term survival in other solid tumors. If pCR could be obtained in HCC, it would present obvious prognostic and therapeutic significance. In this report, we present the case of an adult patient with CNLC stage IIIb HCC and PVTT who achieved pCR with a sequential conversion regimen of TACE, HAIC, lenvatinib, and sintilimab. The patient was then successfully treated with curative resection, and the follow‐up after surgery demonstrated a complete normalization of AFP and lack of recurrence, demonstrating the feasibility of combining a multimodal approach to achieve durable pathological responses. This case adds evidence to the rationale of combining a multimodal approach to broaden the population of eligible surgery for advanced HCC with PVTT and improve the overall outcome.

Case Presentations

A patient with a 20‐year history of chronic HBV infection was admitted to our hepatobiliary surgery department in May 2024, due to newly diagnosed HCC and PVTT. The patient had experienced periumbilical discomfort for 2 months and was found to have cirrhosis with a large mass in the left hepatic lobe. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) confirmed a giant nodular HCC with PVTT involving the portal vein trunk and branches (type III), invasion of the left hepatic vein, splenic involvement, and multiple small lymph nodes (Figure 1). Laboratory tests showed AFP of 84,246 μg/L, DCP of 3605 mAU/mL, Child‐Pugh class A liver function, indocyanine green retention rate at 15 min (ICG‐R15) of 14.4%, and a performance status score of 1, corresponding to CNLC stage IIIb. After multidisciplinary team evaluation, the patient received antiviral therapy with tenofovir and underwent three cycles of conversion therapy between March and May 2024. The initial unresectability criteria were (1) PVTT involving the portal vein trunk and branches (type III), (2) invasion of the left hepatic vein, and (3) a giant tumor occupying most of the left hepatic lobe, making R0 resection technically unfeasible [1, 4]. The criteria for conversion to surgical eligibility were defined according to established guidelines [9, 10] as: (1) downstaging of PVTT from type III to type II or lower, (2) significant tumor shrinkage allowing negative surgical margins, and (3) preserved liver function (Child–Pugh A) and sufficient future liver remnant. This therapy consisted of TACE plus HAIC (oxaliplatin 125 mg, leucovorin 200 mg, 5‐fluorouracil 3.5 g) combined with lenvatinib (4 mg twice daily) and sintilimab (200 mg every 3 weeks). The regimen was well tolerated, and follow‐up assessments revealed significant tumor shrinkage. Quantitatively, the maximum tumor diameter decreased from 8.5 cm at baseline to 3.2 cm after three cycles (a 62.4% reduction). According to RECIST version 1.1, the patient achieved a partial response, with a 62.4% decrease in target lesion size and no new lesions. Regression of PVTT from type III to type II was also observed. A dramatic decline in tumor markers was noted (AFP from 84,246 μg/L to 115 μg/L; DCP from 3605 mAU/mL to 47.1 mAU/mL) (Figure 2). Thirty‐two days after cessation of systemic therapy, the patient underwent left hemihepatectomy with portal vein thrombectomy, cholecystectomy, bile duct repair, diaphragmatic repair, perinephric and intestinal adhesiolysis, intraoperative portal vein chemotherapy perfusion, and abdominal drainage in June 2024. The postoperative course was uneventful, and the patient was discharged on day 14. Histopathological examination revealed complete necrosis and collagenization of the hepatic lesion without viable tumor cells, and the portal vein thrombus also showed no malignant cells. The background liver demonstrated HBV‐related cirrhosis (G2–3, S4), with immunohistochemistry positive for HepPar‐1, negative for Glypican‐3, and a low Ki‐67 proliferation index. pCR was defined as the absence of any viable tumor cells in the entirely sampled primary tumor bed and portal vein thrombus on hematoxylin and eosin staining, as recommended by standardized criteria for HCC pathological assessment. The resected specimens were sectioned at 5‐mm intervals, and all sections were examined by an experienced hepatopathologist. Tumor necrosis was assessed semiquantitatively, and no viable tumor cells were identified in any section. These findings confirmed a pCR (Figure 3). Postoperative follow‐up imaging showed no recurrence, with AFP levels remaining within normal range at 1 month and 9 months following therapy (Figure 4).

Outcome and Follow‐Up

The patient was discharged on postoperative day 14 with uneventful recovery. At 1‐month follow‐up, imaging showed no tumor recurrence, and AFP normalized to 5.6 μg/L. During 9 months of follow‐up after discontinuation of adjuvant therapy, AFP and DCP levels remained within normal limits, and no adverse events or complications were observed. The patient has maintained good performance status with no signs of disease recurrence as of the last follow‐up.

Discussion and Conclusion

HCC is the most prevalent primary malignancy of the liver and remains a leading cause of cancer‐related mortality worldwide, particularly in regions with high incidence of chronic HBV infection. Late‐stage diagnosis is a significant reason for poor outcomes in HCC patients. PVTT is highly prevalent in patients with HCC and is a strong predictor of poor prognosis and survival [11]. Patients with HCC and PVTT have a median survival of only 2–4 months in the absence of treatment [3, 11]. Liver transplantation and surgical resection, which are typically used in curative approaches, are rarely feasible in patients with PVTT due to widespread vascular invasion and compromised liver function, making PVTT one of the most challenging clinical scenarios in advanced HCC, often necessitating palliative care rather than curative treatment [4, 10].

In recent years, the emergence of systemic therapies, such as multi‐targeted tyrosine kinase inhibitors like lenvatinib, and immune checkpoint inhibitors like sintilimab, has brought new hope for advanced HCC, including cases with PVTT [5]. These agents can inhibit tumor vasculature and immune escape, processes crucial for HCC progression. Lenvatinib is a multi‐targeted TKI with promising efficacy in advanced HCC, and research has shown that lenvatinib, when combined with locoregional therapies like TACE and HAIC, may improve outcomes [9, 12]. Simultaneously, sintilimab can improve survival in advanced HCC patients when combined with antiangiogenic therapies by modulating the tumor microenvironment and enhancing immune response to restore T‐cell‐mediated antitumor immunity [13].

In this case, the patient with CNLC stage IIIb HCC and extensive PVTT received a novel multimodal conversion therapy, including TACE, HAIC, lenvatinib, and sintilimab. After three courses of sequential therapy, imaging studies revealed significant tumor shrinkage, and PVTT was downstaged from type III to type II. Additionally, levels of AFP and DCP decreased substantially—from 84,246 μg/L to 115 μg/L (99.9% reduction) and from 3605 mAU/mL to 47.1 mAU/mL (98.7% reduction), respectively. This dramatic biochemical response strongly correlated with the subsequent histopathological confirmation of pCR. Most importantly, the patient achieved a significant tumor response and then underwent successful curative resection. Histopathological examination of the postoperative specimen confirmed pCR, with no viable tumor cells remaining in the liver or portal vein thrombus. This experience highlights the potential for multimodal therapy to induce effective tumor regression and create an opportunity for curative resection.

pCR in the context of PVTT is exceptionally rare, with only isolated case reports documented in the literature [12, 14]. Compared with previously reported cases, our case is unique in achieving successful downstaging of PVTT from type III to type II—critical for curative resection—along with dramatic declines in AFP (99.9%) and DCP (98.7%) that correlated with pCR and a quadruple regimen (TACE, HAIC, lenvatinib, sintilimab) rarely described in conversion protocols. In other solid tumors, pCR is a well‐established surrogate for long‐term survival, as demonstrated in breast cancer, colorectal cancer, and non‐small‐cell lung cancer [15, 16]. While the role of pCR as a prognostic marker in HCC remains under investigation, our case suggests that pCR could serve as a potential biomarker for favorable long‐term outcomes in patients with advanced HCC following aggressive multimodal therapies. Previous studies, such as Topolewski et al., have found that patients who achieve pCR after neoadjuvant therapy experience significantly improved overall survival in HCC [14], and our case further supports these findings by demonstrating that advanced HCC with PVTT can be cured with appropriate systemic and locoregional therapies.

Despite the promising results of this novel multimodal strategy, several issues remain to be addressed. A clear limitation of this report is the relatively short follow‐up period of 9 months. Although the patient has shown no evidence of recurrence to date, late recurrence of HCC remains a concern, and longer surveillance (≥ 2 years) is necessary to confirm durable remission. The optimal sequence, duration, and combination of these therapies need further investigation [17]. Additionally, the potential for immune‐related adverse events associated with ICI use, such as immune‐related hepatitis or pneumonitis, should be carefully monitored.

Furthermore, while this case has a favorable outcome, it is important to recognize that pCR is uncommon in real‐world advanced HCC. Further research is needed to identify biomarkers that could predict which patients are most likely to benefit from a multimodal treatment approach. The long‐term sustainability of pCR in HCC also requires evaluation, as achieving a complete response does not necessarily guarantee freedom from recurrence. Prospective randomized controlled trials are needed to confirm the long‐term benefits and safety profiles of these multimodal strategies, and to establish their role in the standard care of advanced HCC with PVTT.

In summary, this case report demonstrates the feasibility of achieving pCR in advanced HCC with PVTT using multimodal conversion therapy. TACE, HAIC, lenvatinib, and sintilimab led to significant tumor response and downstaging of PVTT, ultimately providing an opportunity for surgical resection. This case suggests that multimodal treatment strategies may offer a new therapeutic option for patients with advanced HCC and PVTT, a highly malignant and poor‐prognosis disease. Future studies are needed to determine the optimal treatment regimen and to validate the long‐term efficacy of this approach.

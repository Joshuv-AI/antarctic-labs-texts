Novel aflatoxin oxidase CotA alleviates aflatoxin B1 toxicity in broilers by reducing hepatic AFB1 residues and inhibiting oxidative stress-mediated ferroptosis
Authors: Zhiyong Rao, Minglu Guo, Xiaoyao Duan, Yuange Cao, Wei Zhang, Zhixiang Wang, Yongpeng Guo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Aflatoxin B1 (AFB1) is a common mycotoxin present in animal feed. This study systematically examined the protective effects of the novel aflatoxin oxidase CotA in alleviating the adverse impacts of AFB1 on broilers. A total of 432 one-day-old Arbor Acres Plus broiler chicks were randomly assigned to four experimental groups. Experimental diets consisted of a control (CON) group fed the basal diet, an AFB1 group supplemented with 200 μg/kg aflatoxin B1, a CotA group supplemented with 5 U/kg aflatoxin oxidase CotA laccase, and a CotA+ AFB1 group supplemented with both 200 μg/kg aflatoxin B1 and 5 U/kg aflatoxin oxidase CotA. Compared with the control group, broilers exposed to AFB1 exhibited a 10.8 % reduction in 42-day body weight and a 13.2 % increase in the F/G ratio from 22 to 42 d (P < 0.05). Furthermore, AFB1 exposure significantly compromised meat quality, as evidenced by a 25.4 % increase in drip loss and an 18.4 % increase in cooking loss in the breast muscle (P < 0.05). However, these adverse effects were effectively mitigated by aflatoxin oxidase CotA supplementation, which restored the 42-day body weight to 2298.45 g and reduced the drip loss and cooking loss to 1.59 % and 35.48 %, respectively. Notably, the architectural integrity of hepatocytes in the livers of broilers fed AFB1-contaminated feed was compromised, characterized by pronounced vacuolar degeneration and infiltration of inflammatory cells. The liver index in the AFB1 group was also significantly higher, accompanied by marked increases in serum activities of aminotransferases (ALT and AST) and alkaline phosphatase (ALP) (P < 0.05). The incorporation of aflatoxin oxidase CotA into AFB1-contaminated feed effectively mitigated growth retardation and enhanced meat quality. Furthermore, compared with the AFB1 group, aflatoxin oxidase CotA significantly improved the hepatic activities of antioxidant enzymes, including total superoxide dismutase (T-SOD), glutathione peroxidase (GSH-Px), and catalase (CAT) (P < 0.05). Simultaneously, dietary aflatoxin oxidase CotA supplementation effectively alleviated AFB1-induced hepatic oxidative stress and ferroptosis in broilers. Mechanistically, aflatoxin oxidase CotA activated the Nrf2 signaling pathway, as demonstrated by the upregulated mRNA expression of Nrf2, NQO1, and HO-1 (P < 0.05). Regarding ferroptosis inhibition, aflatoxin oxidase CotA treatment significantly reversed AFB1-induced iron overload by downregulating the expression of iron-acquisition genes (TFR1, DMT1, and STEAP3) and reduced Fe2+ content (P < 0.05). Furthermore, aflatoxin oxidase CotA suppressed ACSL4/LPCAT3-mediated lipid peroxidation and enhanced the antioxidant system by upregulating the key ferroptosis defense genes GPX4, SLC7A11, and FSP1, leading to increased GSH levels and decreased MDA content (P < 0.05). In comparison to the AFB1 group, the addition of aflatoxin oxidase CotA markedly decreased the accumulation of AFB1, AFBO residues, and AFB1-DNA adducts in the liver (P < 0.05). Concurrently, dietary supplementation with aflatoxin oxidase CotA effectively alleviated AFB1-induced intestinal villi disruption and significantly enhanced the villus height-to-crypt depth (V/C) ratio by approximately 25 % in both the jejunum and ileum (P < 0.05). Notably, aflatoxin oxidase CotA significantly preserved mucosal barrier integrity through the 1.5 to 2-fold upregulation of essential tight junction proteins. These results suggest that aflatoxin oxidase CotA holds potential as a novel feed additive to counteract the harmful impacts of AFB1 in broilers.

Introduction

Aflatoxins (AFs) are chemically stable secondary metabolites primarily biosynthesized by Aspergillus flavus and Aspergillus parasiticus. As widespread contaminants in agricultural crops, animal feeds and dairy products, AFs exhibit high toxicity and persistent stability, posing critical risks to global food security and public health (Fouad et al., 2019). Aflatoxins represent a formidable threat to the poultry industry by inducing stunted growth, systemic immunosuppression, and acute hepatic injury, which can culminate in mortality. Among the various analogues, aflatoxin B1 (AFB1) is identified as the most potent and deleterious isoform, warranting rigorous monitoring in feed safety (Benkerroum, 2020; Bennett and Klich, 2003). AFB1 exerts multi-organ toxicity, characterized by profound genotoxicity, hepatocarcinogenicity, systemic immunosuppression, and reproductive impairment in humans and various livestock species (Erdélyi et al., 2018; Karmanov et al., 2020; Pitt and Miller, 2017; He, et al., 2024). Accumulating evidence indicates that AFB1 impairs the physiological homeostasis of multiple organs and tissues. The liver serves as the focal target for AFB1-induced pathogenesis, with chronic damage potentially leading to hepatic malignancy (Hussain et al., 2010). The inherent toxicity of AFB1 in broilers is primarily mediated by its bioactivation into the highly reactive epoxide, aflatoxin B1-8,9-epoxide (AFBO), via the cytochrome P450 (CYP450) enzyme system. This reactive metabolite subsequently forms covalent adducts with intracellular macromolecules, such as DNA and proteins, leading to cellular damage (Liu, et al., 2023a). As a highly reactive electrophile, AFBO can form covalent adducts with DNA, RNA, and proteins, thereby triggering programmed cell death or malignant transformation, which represents the fundamental mechanism of its systemic toxicity (Tessari et al., 2010; Woo et al., 2011).

Mycotoxin contamination in poultry feed has emerged as an escalating concern, significantly stifling the advancement of China’s livestock industry. Among these contaminants, AFB1 poses a critical threat to avian species, inducing systemic impairments such as growth retardation, immunosuppression, and hepatic injury. Poultry are recognized as a highly susceptible species to AFB1-induced adverse effects (Wang et al., 2023). The pathophysiological hallmarks of aflatoxicosis in avian species include diminished voluntary feed intake, growth depression, and reduced feed efficiency, which are often coupled with declining egg yields and increased fatalities (Elwan et al., 2021). Extensive studies consistently demonstrate that AFB1 inclusion in broiler diets triggers anorexia and stunts developmental progression, leading to significant economic losses due to impaired growth performance (Dersjant-Li et al., 2003; Lin et al., 2022b). To mitigate these risks, detoxification technologies represent a pivotal strategy for neutralizing toxin hazards and minimizing economic repercussions. Furthermore, the establishment of high-sensitivity, rapid detection systems is essential for early warning and precise quantification. Such diagnostic frameworks provide the technical foundation for verifying detoxification efficacy, enabling real-time monitoring, and ensuring regulatory compliance (Guo, et al., 2026).

Consequently, developing strategies to mitigate the toxic effects of aflatoxins during the rearing process is of paramount importance. Recent bioprospecting has identified various microbial enzymes with potent AFB1-degrading activity, including laccases, oxidoreductases, and lactonases (Wang et al., 2025). Moreover, the role of dietary interventions in optimizing broiler productivity is well-documented. For instance, the non-antibiotic antimicrobial halquinol (HAL) has been demonstrated to significantly enhance growth performance (Habib et al., 2024), underscoring the potential of specialized additives in maintaining avian health and production efficiency. Experimental results indicated that 1 g/kg HAL supplementation significantly improved body weight, growth rate, and feed conversion ratio (FCR) by day 35. By enhancing nutrient absorption, HAL also significantly increased carcass weight and the yields of breast and leg meat. These results underscore the efficacy of dietary interventions in optimizing broiler health and production performance. For instance, the laccase Ery4 derived from the Pleurotus eryngii can catalyze the hydrolysis of the terminal furan ring double bond in AFB1, transforming it into the less toxic AFB1-8,9-dihydrodiol (Loi et al., 2023). It was found that laccase from Trametes versicolor could oxidize AFB1 and significantly reduce its toxicity (Loi et al., 2018). Similarly, laccase CotA originating from Bacillus licheniformis can oxidize AFB1 through a C3 hydroxylation mechanism without the requirement for redox mediators.Laccase CotA facilitates efficient biological detoxification by selectively hydroxylating the C3 position of AFB1 to generate low-toxicity or non-toxic derivatives, namely AFQ1 and epi‑AFQ1. This oxidative transformation effectively blocks the metabolic activation of AFB1 into carcinogenic epoxides at its source. Thus, CotA holds promise as a novel aflatoxin oxidase for the degradation of aflatoxin B1 (Guo et al., 2020). Furthermore, research has further demonstrated that Bacillus laccase CotA significantly ameliorates growth performance, gastrointestinal health, and hepatic AFB1 metabolism in ducks. Specifically, when ducks are provided with naturally contaminated diets, the inclusion of Bacillus laccase CotA attenuates the formation of hepatic AFB1-DNA adducts and reduces residual AFB1 concentrations in both the liver and excreta (Ma et al., 2024). Laccase CotA from Bacillus subtilis is capable of simultaneously and completely degrading both AFB1 and zearalenone (ZEN), yielding degradation products with no significant toxicity to mouse macrophages (Gao et al., 2024). Additionally, previous studies have demonstrated that dietary supplementation with aflatoxin oxidase CotA derived from Bacillus licheniformis alleviates aflatoxin B1-induced hepatic oxidative stress, lipid metabolism disorders, and apoptosis in Japanese quail (Lv et al., 2025). Research by Guo et al. (2023) indicated that a compound mycotoxin detoxifier (CMD) containing AFB1-degrading enzymes significantly mitigated AFB1-induced growth retardation and organ damage in broilers. Dietary inclusion of CMD not only recovered the average daily gain and feed efficiency but also ameliorated hepatic pathological changes and intestinal microbiota dysbiosis caused by aflatoxicosis. These studies further confirm the protective efficacy of biological detoxifiers. While aflatoxin oxidase CotA has shown promise in mitigating hepatic damage and genomic instability in murine and duck models, empirical data regarding its practical implementation in broiler diets remains limited. In theory, the aflatoxin oxidase CotA may neutralize the toxicity of AFB1 by converting it into less toxic metabolites, thereby restoring the liver redox homeostasis, suppressing inflammatory responses, and improving the structure of the intestinal mucosa and the integrity of the intestinal barrier. This effectively mitigates the adverse effects of AFB1 on the growth performance and carcass characteristics of broiler chickens, while maintaining the structural function of the liver and the health and integrity of the gut. Therefore, this study aims to evaluate the efficacy of novel aflatoxin oxidase CotA in mitigating the detrimental impacts of AFB1 on the growth performance, carcass traits, and hepatic integrity of broiler chickens, providing a theoretical framework for its industrial application.

Materials and methods

Experimental design and bird management

The trial selected 432 healthy, 1-day-old Arbor Acres Plus broiler chicks with similar initial weights from a local commercial hatchery. The chicks were equally divided by sex and then randomly assigned to four treatment groups. Each treatment group had six replicates, with 18 chickens per replicate. The control group was fed a basal diet; the AFB1 group was fed a basal diet containing 200 μg/kg of AFB1; the CotA group was fed a basal diet supplemented with 5 U/kg of aflatoxin oxidase CotA; and the CotA+AFB1 group was fed a basal diet containing 200 μg/kg of AFB1 and 5 U/kg of aflatoxin oxidase CotA. The levels of AFB1 in broiler diets and the dosage of the aflatoxin oxidase CotA contained therein were both determined based on previous studies (Ma et al., 2024). As a novel aflatoxin oxidase from Bacillus licheniformis ANSB821, CotA exhibits favorable enzymatic characteristics suitable for feed application: it can directly oxidize AFB1 without redox mediators, shows optimal activity at pH 8.0 and high stability under neutral to alkaline conditions, and maintains high detoxification efficiency within a broad temperature range (30–70°C), making it resistant to feed pelleting heat processing (Guo et al., 2020). In this study, the AFB₁ dosage (200 μg/kg) was established based on naturally occurring contamination levels and the known susceptibility of broilers, ensuring the stable induction of typical toxicological impairment. Conversely, the aflatoxin oxidase CotA inclusion level (5 U/kg) was extrapolated from in vitro enzymatic parameters and effective dosages in waterfowl models, aiming to ensure high detoxification efficiency while aligning with the requirements for industrial-scale application. Oral administration feasibility has been verified in ducks and quails, where dietary CotA effectively degraded AFB1 in the gastrointestinal tract, reduced toxin absorption and hepatic AFB₁-DNA adduct formation, and alleviated organ damage without adverse effects (Ma et al., 2024; Lv et al., 2025). The AFB1 standard (purity ≥98 %) was purchased from Pribolab Biological Technical Company (Qingdao, China). The aflatoxin oxidase CotA, isolated from Bacillus licheniformis ANSB821, was expressed in Pichia pastoris and subsequently freeze-dried under vacuum conditions for 24 h (Guo et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2024).

The trial was conducted at the Top Feed Nutrition Research Centre (Xinxiang, China) using a three-tier cage system (70 * 70* 34 cm per cage). Chicks were provided ad libitum access to feed and water via nipple drinkers and vaccinated following standard protocols. The environmental temperature was initially set at 33°C and gradually decreased by 2-3°C weekly to a final temperature of 26°C, with relative humidity maintained at approximately 60 % under a 24 h light cycle.

The broiler basal diet was formulated as a corn-soybean meal-based ration in accordance with the nutritional requirements recommended by the National Research Council (National Research Council, 1994). The experimental diet was provided as a powdered feed, with its composition and nutritional levels as shown in Table 1. The crude protein content in the formulated basal diet was determined using the Kjeldahl method (GB/T 6432-2018). Crude ash content was measured according to the national standard (GB/T 6438-2025). Crude fat content was determined using the Soxhlet extraction method (GB/T 6433-2025). The calcium content in feed was determined using the ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid disodium salt complexometric titration method (EDTA) (GB/T 6436-2018). The total phosphorus content in feed was determined by spectrophotometry (GB/T 6437-2018). The amino acid content in the basal diet was analyzed and determined using high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) (GB/T 18246-2019). The metabolizable energy (ME) values in the feed were calculated based on data provided by the China Feed Database (2020), while the remaining values are measured values. All animal procedures were approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of Henan Agricultural University (Approval No.: HNND2025031327). Growth performance and liver index

During the broiler trial rearing period, broilers at 1 day old, 21 days old, and 42 days old were weighed separately in replicates. To minimize the impact of feed and digestive contents on weighing results, animals underwent a 12 h fasting period prior to weighing, during which they were allowed to drink water freely. Daily feed intake was recorded concurrently. Average body weight (BW) and average daily gain (ADG) were calculated for each stage. Average daily feed intake (ADFI) and feed-to-gain ratio (F/G) were also calculated based on the recorded feed consumption for each stage. On day 43 of the experiment, the broilers were euthanized, and their intact livers were precisely isolated. After blotting the liver surfaces dry with filter paper, the livers were weighed accurately. The average daily feed intake (ADFI), feed-to-gain ratio (F/G) and liver index were calculated using the following formula: ADG (g)= (Final BW – Initial BW) / DaysF/G=Totalfeedintake/TotalweightgainLiveindex(%)=(Liverweight/livebodyweight)×100%

Meat quality

On day 43, one chicken per replicate was randomly selected for sampling. Blood was collected from the wing vein, followed by humanely sacrificing the birds. The breast and thigh muscles were then collected to evaluate meat color, pH, drip loss, and shear force. The remaining tissues, including the liver and intestinal segments, were snap-frozen and stored at - 80°C for further analysis.

Meat Color Measurement: The entire breast and thigh muscles were removed from one side and laid flat on an enamel tray. At 45 min post-slaughter, a colorimeter was used to take three measurements along the midline of the muscles, from the thickest to the thinnest sections. The lightness (L*), redness (a*), and yellowness (b*) values were recorded and averaged. Following initial testing, samples were placed in resealable bags within an ice-packed foam box and transferred to 4°C storage. Measurements were repeated after 24 h. pH measurement: At 45 min and 24 h post-slaughter, three points were selected along the longitudinal midline of the breast. A pH meter was inserted to a depth of 0.5-1 cm into the muscle tissue. Three measurements were recorded per carcass to calculate the average.

Drip Loss Determination: Approximately 2 g of breast muscle (W1) was accurately weighed and placed in a drip loss tube at 4°C. After 24 h, the sample was re-weighed (W2) to calculate the drip loss rate using the following formula:Driploss=(W1−W2)/W1×100%

Shear Force: Meat strips measuring 3 cm in length, 1 cm in width, and 1 cm in thickness were cut parallel to the muscle fibers. Samples were placed in sealed bags and immersed in an 80°C water bath until the core temperature reached 70°C. After cooling to room temperature, a digital tenderness tester was used to shear each strip three times to obtain an average value.

Serum biochemical assay

Blood samples collected from the wing vein were allowed to clot at room temperature for 3 h. Serum was then separated by centrifugation at 3,000 rpm for 10 min at 4°C. Serum levels of alkaline phosphatase (ALP), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), and aspartate aminotransferase (AST) were determined using the microplate method, strictly following the manufacturer’s protocols. Optical densities were measured using a full-wavelength microplate reader (Multiskan SkyHigh, Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., USA). All commercial kits were purchased from Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute (Nanjing, China; ALP, A059-2-2; ALT, C009-2-1; AST, C010-2-1).

Morphological analysis of the intestine

Approximately 2 cm of the mid-sections of the jejunum and ileum were precisely excised. The segments were gently rinsed with ice-cold physiological saline to remove residual chyme and blood, then fixed in a 4 % formaldehyde solution for at least 24 h. The fixed tissues were dehydrated, cleared in xylene, embedded in paraffin, and sectioned. The sections were then stained with hematoxylin and eosin (HE) and mounted for microscopic examination. Structural morphology was observed using CaseViewer software, and villus height (VH) and crypt depth (CD) were measured using ImageJ analysis software. VH was defined as the vertical distance from the villus tip to the villus-crypt junction, while CD was measured from the junction to the crypt base. For each sample, three oriented sections were selected, and eight measurement points were recorded per section to calculate the V/C ratio. The remaining intestinal segments were longitudinally incised, flushed with physiological saline, and immediately snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen. These samples were stored at −80°C for the subsequent determination of the relative mRNA expression of intestinal tight junction proteins

Hepatic histological findings

Immediately following the sampling procedure, fresh liver samples were collected using a sterile scalpel. Residual blood was meticulously rinsed away with ice-cold physiological saline. For histological evaluation, liver segments were fixed in 4 % paraformaldehyde for 24 h, dehydrated through a graded series of ethanol, and embedded in paraffin wax. Representative sections (5 μm thickness) were deparaffinized, stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E), and mounted for microscopic examination. Morphological alterations were subsequently visualized and captured using light microscopy. Concurrently, the remaining hepatic tissues were aliquoted into cryogenic tubes, snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at −80°C for subsequent antioxidant and molecular analyses.

Hepatic antioxidant capacity assessment

An appropriate amount of liver tissue stored at −80°C was taken, rinsed in ice-cold physiological saline to remove residual blood, blotted dry with filter paper, and accurately weighed. Subsequently, the weighed tissue was placed into a homogenization tube, and a specific proportion of homogenization medium (physiological saline) was added. The homogenization tube was then placed into an automatic rapid sample homogenizer (JXFSTPRP-24 L, Shanghai Jingxin) for thorough homogenization, followed by collection of the supernatant. According to the instructions provided by the kit manufacturers, the following indicators in liver tissue were measured: ferrous ion content (Fe²⁺), glutathione peroxidase (GSH-Px), reduced glutathione (GSH), oxidized glutathione (GSSG), malondialdehyde (MDA), total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC), total superoxide dismutase (T-SOD), and catalase (CAT). The Ferrous Ion (Fe²⁺, Cat. No. BC5415) Assay Kit was purchased from Beijing Solarbio Technology Co., Ltd. Reagents for detecting other indicators were obtained from Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, with the following product information: GSH-Px (Cat. No. A005-1-1), GSSG (Cat. No. A061-1-2), MDA (Cat. No. A003-1-2), T-AOC (Cat. No. A015-2-1), T-SOD (Cat. No. A001-1-2), CAT (Cat. No. A007-1-1), and GSH (Cat. No. A006-2-1). In addition, the total protein (TP) concentration in the supernatant of liver homogenates was determined using the BCA Protein Assay Kit (Cat. No. BL1054S) from Beijing Lanjieke Technology Co., Ltd. All specific operational procedures were strictly performed in accordance with the corresponding kit instructions.

Immunofluorescence analysis of the intestinal tract

Immunofluorescence analysis was conducted to visualize the expression of tight junction proteins (ZO-1, Claudin-1, and Occludin) in the jejunal tissues. Paraffin-embedded sections were deparaffinized in xylene and rehydrated through a graded series of ethanol consisting of 100 %, 95 %, 85 %, and 75 % concentrations. Heat-induced antigen retrieval was performed using 1 × citrate buffer (pH 6.0). To minimize non-specific binding, sections were blocked with 10 % normal goat serum (matching the host of the secondary antibody) at 37°C for 30 min. Subsequently, the sections were incubated overnight at 4°C with specific primary antibodies diluted to optimized concentrations, while negative controls were processed by omitting the primary antibodies. After washing with PBS, the tissues were incubated with fluorophore-conjugated secondary antibodies (1:200 dilution in PBST) at 37°C for 1 h in the dark. Nuclei were counterstained with DAPI working solution for 5 min at room temperature. Following three washes with PBS (5 min each), excess liquid was gently removed, and the sections were mounted using an antifade mounting medium. Images were acquired using a laser scanning confocal microscope (LSM 800, Zeiss, Thornwood, NY, USA).

Quantitative real-time PCR

Total RNA was extracted from liver and intestinal tissues stored at −80°C using the Trizol method (Qiu et al., 2021). Reverse transcription was subsequently performed to synthesize cDNA following the manufacturer’s instructions of the HiScript III RT SuperMix for qPCR (+gDNA wiper) kit (Cat# R323-01, Vazyme Biotechnology, Nanjing, China). Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) was carried out on a Bio-Rad CFX Opus 96 Real-Time PCR System using the ChamQ Universal SYBR qPCR Master Mix (Cat# Q711-02, Vazyme Biotechnology, Nanjing, China). The thermal cycling protocol comprised an initial pre-denaturation at 95°C for 30 s, followed by 45 cycles of three-step amplification: 95°C for 5 s, 60°C for 30 s. Determination of AFB1 residues and AFB1-DNA adduct levels

Residual AFB1 in liver samples was extracted using a total aflatoxin immunoaffinity column (Clover Technology Group, Beijing, China), with the procedure adhering to the manufacturer's instructions. The aflatoxins in the samples were subjected to extraction, filtration, and dilution; the sample extract was then passed through an affinity column coated with aflatoxin-specific antibodies. At this stage, aflatoxins bound to the antibodies on the affinity column. Impurities on the immunoaffinity column were subsequently removed using distilled water. The column was eluted with methanol to separate aflatoxins from the antibodies. Finally, the filtered methanol eluate was injected into a high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) system for analysis. The specific protocol involved filtering the AFB1-containing sample extract through a 0.22 μm regenerated cellulose membrane, followed by injecting 20 μL of the filtered sample solution into the HPLC system. A fluorescence detector was employed, with an excitation wavelength of 360 nm and an emission wavelength of 440 nm. The mobile phase consisted of a methanol-water mixture (45:55, V/V), with a flow rate set at 1 mL/min.

The residues of aflatoxin B1-8,9-epoxide (AFBO) and the content of AFB1-DNA adducts in broiler livers were determined using commercial ELISA kits. Specifically, AFBO residues and AFB1-DNA adducts were measured using kits HB287-NC and HB253-NC, respectively, both sourced from Hengyuan Biological Institute (Shanghai, China). For both assays, specific capture antibodies were pre-coated onto 96-well microplates. Following the addition of standards or samples, absorbance was measured using a Multiskan SkyHigh microplate reader (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc, Massachusetts, USA), and the concentrations of the respective analytes were calculated based on standard curves.

Statistical analysis

The experimental data were subjected to one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) using SPSS 27.0 software (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). Post-hoc multiple comparisons were performed using Tukey’s test. All data are presented as the mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). Statistical significance was defined as P < 0.05, with highly significant differences denoted by P < 0.01 and P < 0.001, respectively.

Effects of aflatoxin oxidase CotA on liver damage in broilers fed an AFB1-containing diet

To investigate the mitigating effect of aflatoxin oxidase CotA on AFB1-induced liver damage in broilers, parameters related to hepatic injury in birds fed AFB1-contaminated diets were analyzed. Histopathological examination (H&E staining) of broiler livers revealed that diets contaminated with aflatoxins induced significant hepatotoxicity (Fig. 1A). In the AFB1 group, disorganized hepatic lobule structures, vacuolization of hepatocytes, and extensive inflammatory cell infiltration were observed, indicating severe lesions. These pathological changes resulted in marked cellular inflammation, characterized by severe hepatocyte swelling and leukocyte infiltration. In the control and CotA groups, liver architecture appeared normal, characterized by neatly arranged hepatic lobules and distinct morphological structures. In the AFB1+CotA group, only minimal hepatic necrosis was observed; hepatocytes were generally organized in a uniform manner, with intact tissue structure, narrow interstitial spaces, and largely preserved cellular morphology. Discussion

AFB1 represents one of the most pervasive and potent mycotoxins in poultry feed, posing severe threats to animal health and global food security due to its bioaccumulation through the food chain (Fan et al., 2013; Rawal et al., 2010; Saleemi et al., 2020). While traditional physical and chemical detoxification methods often compromise nutrient integrity or risk secondary contamination, biological strategies that employ microbial enzymes offer an environmentally benign and highly specific alternative (Guengerich et al., 1998). The present study demonstrates that microbial-derived aflatoxin oxidase CotA effectively neutralizes AFB1-induced systemic toxicity. Specifically, aflatoxin oxidase CotA supplementation attenuates hepatic injury and intestinal dysfunction, thereby reversing growth retardation and enhancing the color stability of broiler muscle. In this context, the laccase has emerged as a promising biocatalyst that efficiently degrades AFB1 by reducing molecular oxygen to water as the sole by-product, making it an ideal candidate for sustainable feed additive development (Bian et al., 2022; Hao et al., 2023). Furthermore, genetic engineering and directed evolution strategies, such as site-directed mutation and atmospheric and room temperature plasma (ARTP) mutagenesis, provide powerful tools to further enhance the catalytic efficiency, pH adaptability, and thermal stability of aflatoxin oxidase CotA (Wang et al., 2025), thereby accelerating its practical application in industrial feed production. Our study demonstrated that dietary AFB1 exposure significantly compromised broiler growth performance, characterized by reduced body weight and average daily gain (ADG), alongside an elevated feed-to-gain ratio (F/G). These impairments are fundamentally rooted in the interference of AFB1 with hepatic protein synthesis and the disruption of intestinal nutrient uptake (Zhang et al., 2024). Mechanistically, the formation of AFB1-DNA adducts hampers the transcriptional and translational machinery essential for muscle protein deposition. Furthermore, AFB1 has been shown to induce anorexia and lipid metabolic disorders, ultimately increasing F/G and altering carcass composition (Fouad et al., 2019; Szkudelska et al., 2005; Trebak et al., 2015). Notably, dietary supplementation with aflatoxin oxidase CotA effectively reversed these growth-inhibitory effects, likely by neutralizing the toxin before it could exert systemic damage. Beyond growth metrics, meat quality serves as a critical determinant of consumer preference and processing value. We observed that AFB1 exposure led to increased drip loss and cooking loss in breast muscle, reflecting a decline in water holding capacity (WHC). Mechanistically, AFB1 triggers excessive oxidative stress and drives ferroptosis-like lipid peroxidation in muscle cell membranes, which disrupts membrane structural integrity, increases permeability, and promotes myofibrillar protein oxidation, ultimately impairing muscle WHC (Bai et al., 2017; McKenna et al., 2005; Yue et al., 2022). Consistent with these mechanisms, our results showed that AFB1 significantly increased Fe²⁺ and MDA contents, elevated the GSSG/GSH ratio, and decreased T-AOC, T-SOD, GSH-Px, and CAT activities in the liver, indicating severe redox imbalance and lipid peroxidation. Further research has shown that the regulation of lipid metabolism, particularly the glycerophospholipid metabolic pathway, is central to maintaining cell membrane homeostasis and antioxidant defence. Evidence suggests that the activation of pivotal pathways, particularly glycerophospholipid metabolism (ko00564), facilitates the conversion of fatty acids into bioactive phospholipid species, such as PC (18:1_20:5) and PE (18:0_20:5). These phospholipids are crucial for maintaining the structural integrity of cell membranes and mitochondrial function. It is worth noting that this metabolic process involves the precise regulation of the conversion of oxidized lipids (peroxide-phosphatidylcholine, POV-PC), and that specific phospholipids (such as PE containing polyunsaturated fatty acids) are key substrates that trigger ferroptosis (Liu, et al., 2025a). Consequently, optimizing phospholipid composition through the regulation of lipid metabolism not only maintains normal growth performance but also effectively inhibits oxidative damage induced cellular injury, thereby improving muscle WHC and enhancing overall meat quality. Moreover, the observed reduction in meat redness (a* value) suggests an accelerated oxidation of oxymyoglobin to metmyoglobin under AFB1 challenge (Huang et al., 2019; Rajput et al., 2013). Dietary inclusion of aflatoxin oxidase CotA significantly mitigated these adverse impacts, restoring both WHC and meat color. In line with the improvement in meat quality, aflatoxin oxidase CotA supplementation reduced Fe²⁺ and MDA levels, restored GSH content, and enhanced the activities of key antioxidant enzymes (T-AOC, T-SOD, GSH-Px, CAT), thereby alleviating oxidative stress and lipid peroxidation. These results suggest that aflatoxin oxidase CotA not only degrades the toxin but also potentially modulates the systemic redox status, thereby preserving the functional and organoleptic properties of the muscle tissue.

The liver is the central hub for xenobiotic metabolism and serves as the primary target organ for AFB1 toxicity (Guo et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2023). AFB1 is metabolized and activated by the CYP450 enzyme system in the liver to form the highly toxic AFB1-8,9-epoxide, which induces excessive accumulation of reactive oxygen species (ROS) and a burst of oxidative stress. This subsequently triggers mitochondrial dysfunction, lipid peroxidation and ferroptosis, and activates NF-κB-driven inflammatory pathways, ultimately leading to structural disruption and pathological damage in hepatocytes (Lin et al., 2022a; Qiao et al., 2023). In the present study, H&E staining demonstrated that exposure to AFB1 induced diffuse hepatocyte swelling, vacuolar degeneration, and inflammatory cell infiltration in the liver tissue. These pathological features are consistent with AFB1 mediated oxidative damage, lipid metabolism dysfunction, and inflammatory cascades, thereby providing histological evidence of the hepatic parenchymal damage induced by AFB1 in broiler chickens. These morphological lesions are indicative of impaired liver function and compromised cellular integrity. Organ indices, particularly the liver index, serve as sensitive indicators of an animal's metabolic and health status. Alterations in liver weight often reflect the organ's response to toxic insult, typically manifesting as inflammation, lipid deposition, or compensatory hypertrophy (Zhang et al., 2022). Consistent with the observed histopathological damage, our findings showed that AFB1 exposure significantly elevated the liver index in broilers. This was further corroborated by the marked elevation in serum ALT, AST, and ALP activities, which are key biomarkers of hepatic integrity. The release of these enzymes into the systemic circulation is a hallmark of hepatocyte membrane damage and enzymatic leakage. Notably, dietary inclusion of aflatoxin oxidase CotA effectively attenuated these pathological changes and normalized serum enzyme activities. These results demonstrate that aflatoxin oxidase CotA effectively mitigates AFB1-induced parenchymal damage, thereby restoring physiological homeostasis and reversing the toxin-induced decline in growth performance.

Oxidative stress serves as a cornerstone pathological mechanism underlying AFB1-induced hepatotoxicity and genotoxicity. Characterized by the perturbation of redox homeostasis, it acts as a critical driver in the pathogenesis of AFB1-mediated hepatic injury (Yang et al., 2000). This oxidative insult is primarily manifested through accelerated lipid peroxidation, excessive reactive oxygen species (ROS) accumulation, and mitochondrial dysfunction (Cao et al., 2021). Crucially, the surge in ROS and lipid peroxides represents a hallmark initiation of ferroptosis. Recent pathological evidence in human esophageal squamous cell carcinoma has further substantiated the intrinsic link between oxidative stress markers (such as ROS and MDA) and the modulation of ferroptosis-related proteins including GPX4 and HIF-1α (Shafiq et al., 2024). By integrating these clinical findings, the role of oxidative stress as a universal regulator of ferroptotic signaling is reinforced across different species and disease models, providing a more comprehensive validation of its pathological significance. The Keap1-Nrf2 signaling axis is the master regulatory machinery governing the cellular defense against oxidative challenges (Crisman et al., 2023). Under basal conditions, Nrf2 is sequestered in the cytoplasm by its repressor, Keap1, and targeted for ubiquitin-mediated proteasomal degradation, thereby maintaining minimal systemic expression (Yu and Xiao, 2021). However, upon exposure to oxidative stress or xenobiotics, conformational changes in Keap1 impair its inhibitory affinity for Nrf2, promoting Nrf2 stabilization and nuclear translocation. Once in the nucleus, Nrf2 orchestrates the transcription of diverse cytoprotective genes, including those encoding phase II detoxifying and antioxidant enzymes (Alves et al., 2025; Manavi et al., 2025). In the present study, AFB1 exposure significantly downregulated the mRNA expression of Nrf2 and its downstream targets (HO-1 and NQO1) in the liver. This transcriptional suppression consequently impaired the enzymatic antioxidant defense system, thereby exacerbating hepatic oxidative injury. Our findings are in alignment with prior research indicating that AFB1 challenge suppresses Nrf2 nuclear translocation and disrupts its regulatory capacity, effectively compromising the cellular defense machinery (He et al., 2020; Ji et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2023). Malondialdehyde (MDA), a definitive byproduct of lipid peroxidation, serves as a reliable marker reflecting the severity of oxidative damage to hepatocyte membranes (Jin et al., 2021; Li et al., 2021). Consistent with previous reports, AFB1 challenge triggered a significant elevation in hepatic MDA levels, a hallmark of extensive lipid peroxidation (El-Bahr, 2015; Wang et al., 2018). Notably, dietary supplementation with the aflatoxin oxidase CotA significantly reduced hepatic MDA content while simultaneously enhancing the activities of T-SOD, GSH-Px, and CAT. These enzymes are pivotal biomarkers for assessing the functional capacity of the cellular antioxidant system (Jiang et al., 2020; Yang et al., 2022). Taken together, these findings demonstrate that aflatoxin oxidase CotA exerts a robust protective effect against AFB1-induced hepatotoxicity, a process largely mediated by the restoration of redox homeostasis through the reactivation of the Nrf2 signaling pathway.

Ferroptosis is an iron-dependent regulated cell death featured by lethal lipid peroxidation and iron overload (Jiang et al., 2023). Notably, glutathione (GSH) depletion and redox imbalance serve as upstream triggers that directly initiate ferroptotic signaling (Yang et al., 2026). In the present study, AFB₁ exposure induced hepatic ferroptosis, as indicated by increased Fe²⁺ content and severe lipid peroxidation. Mechanistically, excessive ROS generated by AFB₁ triggered GSH depletion and redox disequilibrium; meanwhile, Fe²⁺ further aggravated lipid peroxidation via Fenton reaction, ultimately accelerating ferroptotic hepatocyte death (Tang et al., 2021). Intracellular iron homeostasis is tightly regulated by a suite of iron-handling proteins. We observed that AFB1 treatment significantly upregulated the expression of TFR1 (iron uptake), STEAP3 (Fe3+ to Fe2+ reduction), and DMT1 (endosomal iron export), collectively driving the expansion of the LIP and heightening ferroptotic susceptibility (Kong et al., 2021; Wu et al., 2025; Zhou et al., 2025). Concurrently, the upregulation of ACSL4 and LPCAT3 further fueled the accumulation of lethal lipid peroxides. These enzymes are pivotal for the enrichment of polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs) in plasma membranes (Zhai et al., 2021). To counteract this oxidative onslaught, cells rely on dual defense systems: the GSH-GPX4 axis and the FSP1-CoQ10 pathway. GPX4, utilizing reduced glutathione (GSH) as an essential cofactor, neutralizes phospholipid hydroperoxides to maintain membrane integrity (Dixon et al., 2014; Liu et al., 2025; Roh et al., 2017). Furthermore, FSP1 functions as a parallel, GSH-independent defense mechanism to constrain the propagation of lipid peroxidation (Dionísio et al., 2021). Our results revealed that AFB1 exposure severely suppressed the expression of GPX4, SLC7A11, and FSP1, while depleting the GSH/GSSG ratio, indicating a systemic collapse of the anti-ferroptotic machinery. Notably, these pathological alterations were effectively rescued by aflatoxin oxidase CotA supplementation. These findings suggest that the aflatoxin oxidase CotA provides robust protection against AFB1-mediated hepatotoxicity by rebalancing iron metabolism and restoring the cellular antioxidant defense systems, thereby inhibiting oxidative stress-mediated ferroptosis.

The liver serves as the primary target organ for AFB1 toxicity, and its exposure is well-documented to induce severe hepatic injury in broiler chickens (Li et al., 2019; Muhammad et al., 2017). As the major site of AFB1 biotransformation, hepatocytes facilitate AFB1 bioactivation via epoxidation, which is classically mediated by the cytochrome P450 (CYP) enzyme system including CYP1A2, CYP3A4, and CYP2A6, generating the highly reactive intermediate AFB1-exo-8,9-epoxide (AFBO). Although CYP450 mediated bioactivation was not directly examined in the present study, this canonical mechanism is consistently recognized as the core initiation step for AFBO binding to cellular DNA to form AFB1-DNA adducts. Consistent with the mechanisms underlying HBV-induced genetic mutations and the development of hepatocellular carcinoma, the persistent formation of AFB1-DNA adducts leads to point mutations in tumour suppressor genes, triggering genomic instability and further increasing the risk of hepatocellular carcinoma (Ning et al., 2023). As a potent electrophile, AFBO covalently attacks the N7 position of guanine (G) bases within DNA, leading to the formation of pro-mutagenic AFB1-DNA adducts (Chang et al., 2026). Beyond DNA, AFBO readily forms covalent adducts with RNA and essential cellular proteins, thereby triggering programmed cell death or inducing malignant transformation. This multi-target covalent binding represents the fundamental mechanism underlying AFB1-induced systemic toxicity (Calabrese et al., 2005; Wang et al., 2024). Consequently, the residual levels of these reactive metabolites and their corresponding adducts in the liver serve as a definitive indicator of the severity of AFB1-mediated damage. Our data demonstrate that AFB1 intake resulted in the persistent accumulation of AFB1, AFBO, and AFB1-DNA adducts in the livers of broiler chickens. Notably, dietary supplementation with the novel aflatoxin oxidase CotA significantly attenuated the hepatic concentrations of these toxic residues. Mechanistically, AFBO can be detoxified through conjugation with reduced glutathione (GSH), a reaction catalyzed by glutathione S-transferases (GSTs). This yields water-soluble AFB1-GSH conjugates, which are subsequently processed and excreted as AFB1—N-acetylcysteine (AFB1—NAC) (Deng et al., 2025). Corroborating these findings, our results indicate that aflatoxin oxidase CotA modulates antioxidant levels and alleviates oxidative stress by upregulating the expression of antioxidant enzyme genes. Taken together, these results suggest that aflatoxin oxidase CotA supplementation effectively mitigates tissue damage and minimizes toxic residues by neutralizing AFB1 at the source, thereby reducing the flux of toxin bioactivation and preserving cellular macromolecular integrity.

The gastrointestinal tract represents the critical interface for nutrient absorption and macromolecular transport, where intestinal morphology is intrinsically linked to growth performance and mucosal health in livestock (Ao and Kim, 2020; Ren et al., 2019). As the primary physiological barrier exposed to dietary contaminants, the gut is highly susceptible to the toxicological insults of AFB1, which is predominantly absorbed across the intestinal epithelium in the proximal jejunum. Despite its established toxicity, the molecular mechanisms by which AFB1 compromises the intestinal epithelial barrier warrant further elucidation. Villus height, crypt depth, and the villus height-to-crypt depth (V/C) ratio are foundational metrics for the quantitative assessment of mucosal functional integrity. A high V/C ratio typically reflects a well-preserved architecture with an expanded absorptive surface area, whereas a diminished ratio, which often results from villus atrophy or compensatory crypt hyperplasia, indicates mucosal impairment (Akinrinmade et al., 2016; Xue et al., 2023). In the present study, AFB1 exposure significantly reduced both villus height and the V/C ratio in the jejunum and ileum, accompanied by conspicuous villus fragmentation and epithelial desquamation. These morphological alterations align with established literature suggesting that even low-dose AFB1 can trigger marked architectural distortion of the intestinal mucosa through oxidative stress-mediated pathways (Xu et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2014). Notably, our findings demonstrate that aflatoxin oxidase CotA exerts a robust protective effect against AFB1-induced intestinal injury. aflatoxin oxidase CotA intervention led to a significant restoration of villous architecture, characterized by a more organized and compact arrangement. This morphological recovery was further supported by the upregulated expression of tight junction proteins, suggesting that aflatoxin oxidase CotA remarkably reinforces the intestinal mucosal barrier. Consistent with the notion that intestinal barrier integrity links stress responses, immune homeostasis, and growth performance (Al‑Suwailem et al., 2024), strengthening gut barrier function helps reduce systemic stress, suppress excessive inflammatory activation, and maintain normal immune competence. By enzymatically degrading AFB1 in the intestinal, aflatoxin oxidase CotA substantially alleviates the deleterious effects of dietary AFB1 on the gut epithelium, preserving the structural basis for efficient nutrient absorption.

The intestinal barrier maintains the stability of the luminal microenvironment by acting as a selective semi-permeable barrier that sequesters toxic substances while preserving physiological homeostasis (Camilleri et al., 2012). The intestinal epithelial cell layer constitutes the structural core of this mucosal barrier, where cells are intricately interconnected via specialized junctional complexes, including tight junctions (TJs), focal adhesions, and desmosomes (Groschwitz and Hogan, 2009). TJs constitute the primary mechanical barrier of the gut by sealing the paracellular pathways between adjacent epithelial cells, thereby effectively restricting the translocation of pathogenic microorganisms and luminal toxins (Chen et al., 2015; Robinson et al., 2015). Research has demonstrated that AFB1 exposure significantly disrupts intestinal homeostasis in poultry, ultimately compromising systemic physiological functions (Robert et al., 2017). Consequently, TJs have emerged as critical therapeutic targets for modulating barrier function. These structures are organized into multiprotein complexes primarily consisting of claudins, occludin, and zonula occludens-1 (ZO-1), which are essential for maintaining cellular structural integrity and regulating selective permeability (Buckley and Turner, 2018). Functionally, ZO-1 acts as a pivotal scaffold protein that anchors the transmembrane proteins (occludin and claudins) to the actin cytoskeleton, while claudins are indispensable for regulating pore-forming and sealing functions within the barrier (Robinson et al., 2015). Accumulating evidence underscores the pivotal role of the gut-liver axis in mycotoxin pathogenesis, as AFB1-induced hepatotoxicity is intrinsically linked to compromised intestinal barrier function (Wang et al., 2019). AFB1 exposure alters intestinal architecture and suppresses TJ expression, thereby hindering nutrient assimilation and facilitating the paracellular influx of toxins into the systemic circulation (Peng et al., 2016; Ren et al., 2019; Yang et al., 2012). In the present study, our findings indicate that AFB1 exposure significantly downregulated both the mRNA and protein expression of key TJs in the jejunum. Conversely, the supplementation of aflatoxin oxidase CotA markedly upregulated the expression of these junctional markers, thereby fortifying the intestinal mechanical barrier and effectively mitigating AFB1-induced mucosal injury.

Conclusion

In summary, this study systematically demonstrates that dietary supplementation with aflatoxin oxidase CotA effectively attenuates AFB1-induced growth retardation, impaired meat quality, and hepatic damage in broiler chickens. Its protective mechanism is primarily mediated by the activation of the Nrf2 antioxidant pathway, the inhibition of ACSL4/GPX4-dependent hepatic ferroptosis, and the reinforcement of the intestinal mechanical barrier. As a highly efficient and safe bio-detoxification enzyme, aflatoxin oxidase CotA represents an innovative biotechnological strategy to combat widespread aflatoxin contamination in poultry feed and holds substantial promise for practical application. Nevertheless, the long-term stability and biosafety of aflatoxin oxidase CotA in large-scale practical applications warrant further in-depth investigation to provide robust evidence for its industrial popularization and application.

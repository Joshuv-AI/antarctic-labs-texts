Homological duality and exact sequences of Hopf algebras
Julian Le Clainche

Abstract
We study the stability of homological duality properties of Hopf algebras under extensions.



1
Introduction

Homological duality is a general phenomenon producing a duality between the cohomology and homology spaces of an algebraic system, analogous to Poincaré duality for closed manifolds in algebraic topology. A first axiomatization is due to Bieri-Eckmann
[
7
]
in group cohomology, while homological duality in Hochschild cohomology for algebras arose in Van den Bergh’s work
[
29
,
30
]
. Since then, many developments have followed, including the important case of (twisted) Calabi-Yau algebras
[
16
]
.

Homological duality for groups is known to be well-behaved under extensions
[
7
,
5
]
. Our main result, which produces new examples of (Hopf) algebras having homological duality, generalizes these results of Bieri-Eckmann to the setting of exact sequences of Hopf algebras, as follows, where
\mathrm{cd}
denotes the Hochschild cohomological dimension of an algebra.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
k\longrightarrow B\overset{i}{\longrightarrow}A\overset{p}{\longrightarrow}H\longrightarrow k
be an exact sequence of Hopf algebras with bijective antipodes. Assume that
A
is faithfully flat as a left and right
B
-module, and that
H
and
B
are smooth algebras. Then the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

B
and
H
have homological duality,

(ii)

A
has homological duality.

If these conditions hold, we have

\mathrm{cd}(A)=\mathrm{cd}(B)+\mathrm{cd}(H).

Apart from a smoothness criterion due to Bieri-Eckmann
[
8
]
, the main tools we use to prove Theorem
1.1
are natural Hopf algebraic generalizations of the Lyndon-Hochschild-Serre spectral sequences. These spectral sequences are not new, because they are special cases of those of Stefan
[
27
]
in the more general setting of Hopf-Galois extensions, but we work them in detail, on the one hand for the sake of completeness, and on the other hand because there are some specific technicalities (notably regarding certain
H
-actions) that are not directly viewable, or that we have not been able to view, in
[
27
]
, and are needed in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

During the last phase of writing this paper, we were informed of the recent work of R. Zhu
[
36
]
, where a result similar to the implication (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) in Theorem
1.1
is proved (Theorem 2.15 in
[
36
]
), in the more general framework of Hopf-Galois extensions. We still think that there is some interest in our direct approach in the case of exact sequences of Hopf algebras, on one hand because many considerations can be made more explicit than in the general Hopf-Galois case, and on the other hand because we deal with a more general version of homological duality (as suggested by the duality groups of Bieri-Eckmann) than the twisted Calabi-Yau or Van den Bergh duality considered in
[
36
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 consists of reminders and preliminaries about Hochschild (co)homology and homological duality. In Section 3 we recall the definition of exact sequences of Hopf algebras and we introduce the generalized Lyndon-Hochschild-Serre spectral sequences in Hopf algebra (co)homology. In Section 4 we establish a smoothness result for a Hopf algebras involved in an exact sequence. Section 5 is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.1
. In the final Section 6 we present some examples and applications of Theorem
1.1
.

2
Hochschild cohomology and homological duality

In this section, we briefly recall some notations and basic definitions about Hochschild (co)homology and homological duality for algebras.

2.1
Finiteness conditions on modules

Definition 2.1
.

Let
A
be an algebra. The
projective dimension
of an
A
-module
M
is defined by

\mathrm{pdim}_{A}(M):=\min\left\{n\in\mathbb{N},\penalty\ M\text{ admits a length}\ n\ \text{resolution by projective }A\text{-modules}\right\}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}.

The projective dimension can as well be characterized by

\displaystyle\mathrm{pdim}_{A}(M)

\displaystyle=\min\left\{n\in\mathbb{N},\penalty\ \mathrm{Ext}_{A}^{n+1}(M,N)=\{0\}\text{ for any $A$-module $N$}\right\}

\displaystyle=\max\left\{n\in\mathbb{N},\penalty\ \mathrm{Ext}^{n}_{A}(M,N)\not=\{0\}\text{ for some $A$-module $N$}\right\}

and if
\mathrm{pdim}_{A}(M)
if finite, we have as well

\mathrm{pdim}_{A}(M)=\max\left\{n\in\mathbb{N},\penalty\ \mathrm{Ext}^{n}_{A}(M,F)\not=\{0\}\text{ for some free $A$-module $F$}\right\}

We now recall various finiteness conditions on modules.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
A
be an algebra and let
M
be an
A
-module.

1.

The
A
-module
M
is said to be
of type
FP_{\infty}
if it admits a projective resolution
P_{\bullet}\to M
with
P_{i}
finitely generated for all
i
.

2.

The
A
-module
M
is said to be
of type FP
if it admits a finitely generated projective resolution of finite length.

The following result characterizes modules of type
FP
among those of type
FP_{\infty}
, see e.g
[
9
, Chapter VIII]
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
A
be an algebra. An
A
-module
M
is of type
FP
if and only if it is of type
FP_{\infty}
and
\mathrm{pdim}_{A}(M)
is finite. In this case we have

\mathrm{pdim}_{A}(M)=\max\left\{n\in\mathbb{N},\penalty\ \mathrm{Ext}_{A}^{n}(M,A)\not=\{0\}\right\}

The following result is
[
6
, Corollary 1.6]
. The implication (iii)
\Rightarrow
(i), due to Bieri-Eckmann
[
8
]
, gives an effective condition to check that a module is of type
FP_{\infty}
and will be useful in section
4
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
A
be an algebra and
M
a left (resp. right)
A
-module. The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

The left (resp. right)
A
-module
M
is of type
FP_{\infty}
;

(ii)

For any direct product
\prod_{i\in I}N_{i}
of right (resp. left)
A
-modules, the natural map
\mathrm{Tor}_{k}^{A}(\prod_{i\in I}N_{i},M)\to\prod_{i\in I}\mathrm{Tor}_{k}^{A}(N_{i},M)
(resp.
\mathrm{Tor}_{k}^{A}(M,\prod_{i\in I}N_{i})\to\prod_{i\in I}\mathrm{Tor}_{k}^{A}(M,N_{i})
is an isomorphism for all
k\geq 0
;

(iii)

For any direct product
\prod A
of arbitrary many copies of
A
, we have
\mathrm{Tor}_{i}^{A}(\prod A,M)=0
(resp.
\mathrm{Tor}_{i}^{A}(M,\prod A)=0
) for
i\geq 1
and the natural map
\left(\prod A\right)\otimes_{A}M\to\prod M
(resp.
M\otimes_{A}\left(\prod A\right)\to\prod M
) is an isomorphism.

We have similar results for
\mathrm{Ext}
functors, but we will only use the following particular statement:

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
A
be an algebra and
M
a right (resp. left)
A
-module. If
M
is of type
FP_{\infty}
, then
\mathrm{Ext}_{A^{op}}(M,-)
(resp.
\mathrm{Ext}_{A}(M,-)
) commutes with direct sums.

2.2
Poincaré duality modules

In this subsection we recall the notion of Poincaré duality module, from
[
13
, Section V]
. This is the basic notion involved in homological duality questions. We fix an algebra
A
.

Definition 2.6
.

A left
A
-module
X
is said to be a
Poincaré duality module of dimension
d\geq 0
if the following conditions hold:

(i)

X
is of type
FP
and
\mathrm{pdim}_{A}(X)=d
;

(ii)

There exists a right
A
-module
D_{X}
together with, for any left
A
-module
N
and any
i\in\{0,\ldots,d\}
, functorial isomorphims

\mathrm{Ext}^{i}_{A}(X,N)\simeq\mathrm{Tor}^{A}_{d-i}(D_{X},N).

A right
A
-module
D_{X}
as above is called a
dualizing module for
X
.

Remark 2.7
.

Let
X
be a Poincaré duality module of dimension
d\geq 0
. If
X
is an
A
-
S
-bimodule, with
S
another algebra, the functoriality condition in Definition
2.6
implies that the isomorphism

\mathrm{Ext}^{i}_{A}(X,N)\simeq\mathrm{Tor}^{A}_{d-i}(D_{X},N)

is right
S
-linear.
In particular, we have

\mathrm{Ext}^{i}_{A}(X,A)\simeq\mathrm{Tor}^{A}_{d-i}(D_{X},A)\simeq\begin{cases}\{0\}\ \textrm{if}\ i\not=d\\
D_{X}\ \textrm{if}\ i=d\end{cases}

where the isomorphism is right
A
-linear. Therefore a dualizing module as in Definition
2.6
is unique up to isomorphism.

The following result is
[
13
, Theorem 2.21]
. For the sake of completeness, we give a direct proof of
(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
following classical arguments of the setting of duality groups
[
9
]
.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
X
be a left
A
-module of type
FP
with
\mathrm{pdim}(X)=d\geq 0
. The following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

X
is a Poincaré duality module of dimension
d
.

(ii)

We have
\mathrm{Ext}_{A}^{i}(X,A)=\{0\}
if
i\not=d
.

In this case, we have
D_{X}\simeq\mathrm{Ext}_{A}^{d}(X,A)
as right
A
-modules.

Proof.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
and the last assertion follow from the remark above.
Assume that
(ii)
holds,
and consider a resolution of
X

0\rightarrow P_{d}\rightarrow P_{d-1}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow P_{1}\rightarrow P_{0}\rightarrow X\rightarrow 0

by finitely generated projective left
A
-modules. Consider the (projective) right
A
-modules

\overline{P_{i}}={\rm Hom}_{A}(P_{i},A)

and the corresponding complex

0\rightarrow\overline{P_{0}}\rightarrow\overline{P_{1}}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow\overline{P_{d-1}}\rightarrow\overline{P_{d}}\rightarrow 0

We have
H^{*}(\overline{P})={\mathrm{Ext}}^{*}_{A}(X,A)
, and (ii) ensures that

0\rightarrow\overline{P_{0}}\rightarrow\overline{P_{1}}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow\overline{P_{d-1}}\rightarrow\overline{P_{d}}\rightarrow{\mathrm{Ext}}^{d}_{A}(X,A)\rightarrow 0

is a resolution of
{\mathrm{Ext}}^{d}_{A}(X,A)
by projective right
A
-modules.
We then have, for a left
A
-module
N

\displaystyle{\mathrm{Tor}}_{d-i}^{A}({\mathrm{Ext}}^{d}_{A}(X,A),N)

\displaystyle\simeq H^{i}(\overline{P}\otimes_{A}N)=H^{i}({\rm Hom}_{A}(P,A)\otimes_{A}N)

\displaystyle\simeq H^{i}({\rm Hom}_{A}(P,N))\ (\textrm{finite generation and projectivity}\ {\rm of}\ P_{i})

\displaystyle\simeq{\mathrm{Ext}}_{A}^{i}(X,N).

This proves (i), with
D_{X}={\mathrm{Ext}}^{d}_{A}(X,A)
.
∎

2.3
Hochschild (co)homology and homological duality

The Hochschild (co)homology spaces (
[
35
]
) of an algebra
A
are defined by considering the functors
\mathrm{Ext}
and
\mathrm{Tor}
in the category of
A
-bimodules.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
A
be an algebra and
M
an
A
-bimodule. The
Hochschild cohomology
spaces of
A
with coefficient in
M
(resp.
Hochschild homology
spaces of
A
with coefficient in
M
) are the vector spaces

HH^{\bullet}(A,M):=\mathrm{Ext}_{A^{e}}^{\bullet}(A,M),\quad HH_{\bullet}(A,M):=\mathrm{Tor}^{A^{e}}_{\bullet}(M,A).

.

The general homological finiteness notions defined in the previous section specify to the case of bimodules which is our case of interest when considering Hochschild (co)homology.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
A
be an algebra.

1.

The
cohomological dimension
of
A
is defined to be

\mathrm{cd}(A):=\mathrm{pdim}_{A^{e}}(A)
.

2.

The algebra
A
is said to be
homologically smooth
if
A
is of type
FP
as a left
A^{e}
-module.

Notice that if
A
is a homologically smooth algebra, then
\mathrm{cd}(A)=\max\left\{n\in\mathbb{N},\penalty\ HH^{n}(A,A^{e})\neq 0\right\}
, by Proposition
2.3
.

For some (smooth) algebras, appropriate conditions ensure that there exists a duality between Hochschild homology and cohomology, analogous to Poincaré duality in algebraic topology.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
A
be an algebra. We say that
A
has
homological duality in dimension

d
if :

(i)

A
is a Poincaré duality
A^{e}
-module of dimension
d
, i.e.
A
is homologically smooth with
d=\mathrm{cd}(A)
and
HH^{i}(A,A^{e})=\{0\}
for
i\not=d
;

(ii)

D=HH^{d}(A,A^{e})
, endowed with the
A
-bimodule structure induced by right multiplication of
A^{e}
, is flat as a left and as a right
A
-module.

If these conditions hold, we say that the bimodule
D
is a dualizing bimodule of
A
.

The following result justifies the "homological duality" terminology above.

Theorem 2.12
.

Let
A
be an algebra. If
A
has homological duality in dimension
d
with dualizing bimodule
D
, then for any
A
-bimodule
M
, we have functorial isomorphisms

HH^{n}(A,M)\simeq HH_{d-n}(A,M\otimes_{A}D)\simeq HH_{d-n}(A,D\otimes_{A}M).

Proof.

We know from Theorem
2.8
that for any
A
-bimodule
M
we have

HH^{i}(A,M)\simeq\mathrm{Tor}_{d-i}^{A^{e}}(D,M)

Using that
D
is assumed to be flat as a left and as a right
A
-module and that
\mathrm{Tor}
can be computed by using resolutions by flat modules, it is not difficult to check that the above spaces on the right are isomorphic to the announced Hochschild homology spaces.
∎

Definition
2.11
and Theorem
2.12
include a number of well-studied particular homological dualities in the literature
[
29
,
16
,
21
]
.

Definition 2.13
.

Let
A
be an algebra having homological duality in dimension
d
and dualizing bimodule
D
.

1.

A
is said to have Van den Bergh duality if the
A
-bimodule
D
is invertible;

2.

A
is said to be twisted Calabi-Yau if
D\simeq A_{\sigma}
as an
A
-bimodule, with
\sigma
an algebra automorphism of
A
. In this case, the automorphism
\sigma
is called
the Nakayama automorphism
of
A
, and is unique up to inner automorphisms;

3.

A
is said to be Calabi-Yau if
D\simeq A
as
A
-bimodules.

Remark 2.14
.

Let
A
be an algebra and assume that
A
is a Poincaré duality
A^{e}
-module of dimension
d
. If
D=HH^{d}(A,A^{e})
is flat as a left
A
-module, then by
[
18
, Theorem 1]
, we have natural isomorphisms for any
A
-bimodule
M

HH^{n}(A,M)\simeq HH_{d-n}(A,M\otimes_{A}D)

Similarly if
D
is flat as a right
A
-module, then by
[
19
, Theorem 4]
, we have natural isomorphisms

HH^{n}(A,M)\simeq HH_{d-n}(A,D\otimes_{A}M)

These two results indicate that, although quite flexible, the framework for homological duality in Definition
2.11
might not be the most general one. We have adopted Definition
2.11
because we did not see any reason to privilegiate left or right flatness for the dualizing bimodule
D
.

See
[
35
, Section 4.3]
and references therein for examples of algebras with homological duality. In particular, algebras of polynomials in
m
indeterminates have homological duality in dimension
m
.

2.4
Cohomology of Hopf algebras

In this subsection we recall some special features of Hochschild (co)homology for Hopf algebras. We begin by recalling Hochschild cohomology and homology spaces of a Hopf algebra
H
can be described by using suitable
\mathrm{Ext}
and
\mathrm{Tor}
spaces in the category of right or left
H
-modules and resolutions of the trivial module (see
[
15
,
17
,
11
,
33
]
):

Theorem 2.15
.

Let
H
be a Hopf algebra and
M
an
H
-bimodule. We have

HH^{n}(H,M)\simeq\mathrm{Ext}^{n}_{H^{op}}(k_{\varepsilon},\widetilde{M})\simeq\mathrm{Ext}_{H}^{n}({}_{\varepsilon}k,\overline{M}),\quad HH_{n}(H,M)\simeq\mathrm{Tor}^{H}_{n}(k_{\varepsilon},M^{\prime})\simeq\mathrm{Tor}^{H}_{n}(M^{{}^{\prime\prime}},{{}_{\varepsilon}k})

where the
H
-modules
\widetilde{M}
,
\overline{M}
,
M^{\prime}
,
M^{\prime\prime}
all have
M
as underlying vector space, and
H
-action given, for
m\in N
and
h\in H
, by the following formulas.

\bullet

\widetilde{M}
has the right
H
-module structure given by
m\cdot h=S(h_{(1)})mh_{(2)}
;

\bullet

\overline{M}
has the left
H
-module structure given by
h\cdot m=h_{(1)}mS(h_{(2)})
;

\bullet

M^{\prime}
has the left
H
-module structure given by
h\rightarrow m=h_{(2)}mS(h_{(1)})
;

\bullet

M^{{}^{\prime\prime}}
has the right
H
-module structure given by
m\leftarrow h=S(h_{2})mh_{(1)}
.

Homological smoothness for Hopf algebras can be detected using the trivial left or right
H
-module (see
[
33
, Proposition A.2]
):

Theorem 2.16
.

Let
H
be a Hopf algebra. The following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

The algebra
H
is homologically smooth;

(ii)

the right
H
-module
k_{\varepsilon}
is of type
FP
;

(iii)

the left
H
-module
{}_{\varepsilon}k
is of type
FP
.

The next result, which specializes the isomorphisms of Theorem
2.15
in the case
M=H^{e}
, is useful in view of a simple characterization of homological duality in the Hopf algebra case.

Proposition 2.17
.

(
[
33
, Proposition 2.1.3]
)

Let
H
be a homologically smooth Hopf algebra. We have

HH^{n}(H,H^{e})=\mathrm{Ext}^{n}_{H^{e}}(H,H^{e})\simeq\mathrm{Ext}^{n}_{H^{op}}(k_{\varepsilon},H)\otimes H\simeq\mathrm{Ext}_{H}^{n}({}_{\varepsilon}k,H)\otimes H

as right
H^{e}
-modules for
n\geq 0
where the
H^{e}
-module structures on
\mathrm{Ext}^{n}_{H^{op}}(k_{\varepsilon},H)\otimes H
and
\mathrm{Ext}_{H}^{n}({}_{\varepsilon}k,H)\otimes H
are respectively given by

(x\otimes h)\leftarrow(a\otimes b)=b_{(2)}x\otimes S^{2}(b_{(1)})ha\quad\text{ and }\quad(y\otimes h)\leftarrow(a\otimes b)=ya_{(1)}\otimes bhS^{2}(a_{(2)}),

for
a,b,h\in H,\ x\in\mathrm{Ext}^{n}_{H^{op}}(k_{\varepsilon},H)\otimes H
and
y\in\mathrm{Ext}_{H}^{n}({}_{\varepsilon}k,H)
.

We thus see from the above result that
HH^{n}(H,H^{e})
is free as a left and as a right
H
-module, hence in particular is flat as a left and as a right
A
-module We thus obtain the following characterization of homological duality in the case of Hopf algebras.

Theorem 2.18
.

Let
H
be a Hopf algebra, and let
d\geq 0
. The following assertions are equivalent.

1.

H
has homological duality in dimension
d
;

2.

H
is homologically smooth and
\mathrm{Ext}_{H}^{i}({}_{\varepsilon}k,H)=0
for
i\neq d
;

3.

H
is homologically smooth and
\mathrm{Ext}_{H^{op}}^{i}(k_{\varepsilon},H)=0
for
i\neq d

4.

The
H
-module
{}_{\varepsilon}k
is a Poincaré duality module of dimension
d
.

5.

The
H^{\rm op}
-module
k_{\varepsilon}
is a Poincaré duality module of dimension
d
.

Remark 2.19
.

The above result recovers, when
H
is a group algebra, the homological duality theory for groups due to Bieri-Eckman
[
7
]
.

The twisted Calabi-Yau property also has a particularly convenient formulation in the case of Hopf algebras.
The first part of the following result is
[
31
, Lemma 2.15]
, while the second part is a direct consequence of Proposition
2.17
.

Proposition 2.20
.

Let
H
be a Hopf algebra with homological duality in dimension
d
.

If
H
is twisted Calabi-Yau with Nakayama automorphism
\sigma
, then
\mathrm{Ext}^{d}_{H}({}_{\varepsilon}k,H)\simeq k_{\xi}
(resp.
\mathrm{Ext}^{d}_{H^{op}}(k_{\varepsilon},H)\simeq{}_{\eta}k
) as right (resp. left)
H
-modules where
\xi=\varepsilon\circ\sigma
(resp.
\eta=\varepsilon\circ\sigma^{-1}
).

Conversely, if
\mathrm{Ext}^{d}_{H}({}_{\varepsilon}k,H)\simeq k_{\xi}
(resp.
\mathrm{Ext}^{d}_{H^{op}}(k_{\varepsilon},H)\simeq{}_{\eta}k
) as right (resp. left)
H
-modules then
H
is twisted Calabi-Yau with Nakayama automorphism given by
\sigma(h)=\xi(h_{(1)})S^{2}(h_{(2)})
(resp.
\sigma(h)=\eta(h_{(2)})S^{2}(h_{(1)})
) for
h\in H
.

We refer the reader to
[
11
]
for various examples of twisted Calabi-Yau Hopf algebras.

3
Exact sequences of Hopf algebras

3.1
Definitions

Definition 3.1
.

A sequence of Hopf algebra maps

k\longrightarrow B\overset{i}{\longrightarrow}A\overset{p}{\longrightarrow}H\longrightarrow k

is said to be exact
[
1
]
if the following conditions hold:

(1)

i
is injective and
p
is surjective,

(2)

\mathrm{Ker}(p)=i(B)^{+}A=Ai(B)^{+}
,

(3)

i(B)=A^{coH}={{}^{coH}A}

where
A^{coH}=\{a\in A,\penalty\ a_{(1)}\otimes p(a_{(2)})=a\otimes 1\}
and
{}^{coH}A=\{a\in A,\penalty\ p(a_{(1)})\otimes a_{(2)}=1\otimes a\}
.

Example 3.2
.

Exact sequences of groups correspond, as expected, to exact sequences of their group algebras.

Example 3.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a discrete group acting on a Hopf algebra
A

via
a group morphism
\alpha:\Gamma\to{\rm Aut}(A)
. Recall that the crossed product
A\rtimes k\Gamma
has
A\otimes k\Gamma
as underlying vector space and product defined by

(a\otimes g)\cdot(b\otimes h)=a\alpha_{g}(b)\otimes gh\quad\text{ for }\quad a,b\in A\quad\text{ and }\quad g,h\in\Gamma.

This algebra is naturally endowed with a Hopf algebra structure defined by

\Delta(a\otimes g)=a_{(1)}\otimes g\otimes a_{(2)}\otimes g,\quad\varepsilon(a\otimes g)=\varepsilon(a),\quad S(a\otimes g)=\alpha_{g^{-1}}\bigl(S(a)\bigr)\otimes g^{-1}

for any
a\in A
and
g\in\Gamma
.
Then
k\longrightarrow A\overset{\mathrm{id}\otimes u}{\longrightarrow}A\rtimes k\Gamma\overset{\varepsilon\otimes\mathrm{id}}{\longrightarrow}k\Gamma\longrightarrow k
is an exact sequence of Hopf algebras.

Remark 3.4
.

There are a number of situations where some of the axioms of an exact sequence of Hopf algebras automatically hold. Let
A
be a Hopf algebra with bijective antipode.

1.

If
B\subset A
is a Hopf subalgebra such that
B^{+}A=AB^{+}
, then
B^{+}A
is a Hopf ideal, and if
A
is faithfully flat as a left or right
B
-module, then the sequence
k\to B\to A\to A/B^{+}A\to k
is exact, by (for example)
[
26
, Lemma 1.3]
. Notice that faithful flatness automatically holds if
A
is cosemisimple
[
12
]
.

2.

If
p:A\to H
is a surjective Hopf algebra map such that
A^{coH}={{}^{coH}A}
, then if
p:A\to H
is faithfully coflat, then
k\to A^{coH}\to A\to H\to k
is an exact sequence of Hopf algebras, by
[
28
, Theorem 2]
combined with
[
25
, Remark 1.3]
. Notice that
p:A\to H
is automatically faithfully coflat if
H
is cosemisimple.

We will extensively use the following well-known fact.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
k\longrightarrow B\overset{i}{\longrightarrow}A\overset{p}{\longrightarrow}H\longrightarrow k
be an exact sequence of Hopf algebras, then
B
is stable under the adjoint actions of
A
: for
b\in B
and
a\in A
, we have
a_{(1)}bS(a_{(2)})\in B
and
S(a_{(1)})ba_{(2)}\in B
. If moreover
A
has bijective antipode, we have as well
a_{(2)}bS^{-1}(a_{(1)})\in B
and
S^{-1}(a_{(2)})ba_{(1)}\in B
.

Proof.

For
b\in B
and
a\in A
, we have

\displaystyle\bigl(a_{(1)}bS(a_{(2)})\bigr)_{(1)}\otimes p\bigl(\bigl(a_{(1)}bS(a_{(2)})\bigr)_{(2)}\bigr)

\displaystyle=a_{(1)}b_{(1)}S(a_{(3)})_{(1)}\otimes p(a_{(2)})p(b_{(2)})p(S(a_{(3)})_{(2)})

\displaystyle=a_{(1)}bS(a_{(4)})\otimes p(a_{(2)}S(a_{(3)}))

\displaystyle=a_{(1)}bS(a_{(3)})\otimes\varepsilon(a_{(2)})=a_{(1)}bS(a_{(2)})\otimes 1

hence
a_{(1)}bS(a_{(2)})\in A^{coH}=B
.
We also have

\displaystyle p\bigl(\bigl(S(a_{(1)})ba_{(2)})\bigr)_{(1)}\bigr)\otimes\bigl(S(a_{(1)})ba_{(2)}\bigr)_{(2)}

\displaystyle=p\bigl(S(a_{(1)})_{(1)}\bigr)p(b_{(1)})p(a_{(2)})\otimes S(a_{(1)})_{(2)}b_{(2)}a_{(3)}

\displaystyle=p\bigl(S(a_{(2)})\bigr)p(a_{(3)})\otimes S(a_{(1)})ba_{(4)}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon(a_{(2)})\otimes S(a_{(1)})ba_{(3)}=1\otimes S(a_{(1)})ba_{(2)}

and hence
S(a_{(1)})ba_{(2)}\in{}^{coH}A=B
. The last assertions follow from the first ones if
B
is
S^{-1}
-stable, or can be proved directly without this assumption, with similar arguments as above. This is left to the reader.
∎

3.2
Spectral sequences for exact sequences of Hopf algebras

Let
k\longrightarrow B\overset{i}{\longrightarrow}A\overset{p}{\longrightarrow}H\longrightarrow k
be an exact sequence of Hopf algebras. In this subsection we construct spectral sequences connecting the (co)homology groups
\mathrm{Ext}^{\bullet}_{A}({}_{\varepsilon}k,-)
(resp.
\mathrm{Tor}_{\bullet}^{A}(k_{\varepsilon},-)
) with
\mathrm{Ext}^{\bullet}_{B}({}_{\varepsilon}k,-)
and
\mathrm{Ext}^{\bullet}_{H}({}_{\varepsilon}k,-)
(resp.
\mathrm{Tor}_{\bullet}^{B}(k_{\varepsilon},-)
and
\mathrm{Tor}_{\bullet}^{H}(k_{\varepsilon},-)
). These spectral sequences generalize the classical Lyndon-Hochschild-Serre spectral sequences and can be deduced from Stefan’s spectral sequences
[
27
]
in the setting of Hopf-Galois extensions. However we work them in detail, on the one hand for the sake of completeness, and on the other hand because there are some specific technicalities (notably regarding certain
H
-actions) that are needed in the proof of Theorem
1.1

Following the ideas in
[
27
]
, we first need to define a structure of
H
-module on the cohomology of
B
. In order to do so, we will use resolutions of the
A
-module
{}_{\varepsilon}k
to compute the homology spaces
\mathrm{Ext}_{B}^{q}({}_{\varepsilon}k,M)
for a left
A
-module
M
, which is allowed thanks to the following well-known result.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
A
be a Hopf algebra and
B\subset A
a Hopf subalgebra such that
A
is projective as a left
B
-module. If
P_{\bullet}
is a resolution of the left
A
-module
{}_{\varepsilon}k
, then the restriction of
P_{\bullet}
is a resolution of the left
B
-module
{}_{\varepsilon}k
.

We first define the
H
-module structure on
\mathrm{Ext}^{0}_{B}({}_{\varepsilon}k,-)\simeq M^{B}=\left\{m\in M,\ bm=\varepsilon(b)m,\ b\in B\right\}
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
k\longrightarrow B\overset{i}{\longrightarrow}A\overset{p}{\longrightarrow}H\longrightarrow k
be an exact sequence of Hopf algebras such that
A
is flat as a left and right
B
-module and let
M
be a left
A
-module.
There is a left
H
-module structure
\cdot
on
\mathrm{Ext}_{B}^{0}({}_{\varepsilon}k,M)
, defined by

p(a)\cdot m=a\cdot m

for any
a\in A
and
m\in\mathrm{Ext}_{B}^{0}({}_{\varepsilon}k,M)\simeq M^{B}
.

Proof.

We have to check that the above formula is well defined.
Consider
a,\ a^{\prime}\in A
such that
p(a)=p(a^{\prime})
: there exist
b_{1},\ldots,b_{m}\in B^{+}
and
a_{1},\ldots a_{m}\in A
such that
a^{\prime}-a=\sum\limits_{i}a_{i}b_{i}
.
Then for
m\in\mathrm{Ext}_{B}^{0}({}_{\varepsilon}k,M)\simeq M^{B}
, we have
a^{\prime}\cdot m=(a+\sum\limits_{i}a_{i}b_{i})\cdot m=a\cdot m
since
m\in M^{B}
. This proves that our expression does not depend on the choice of
a\in A
.

For
m\in M^{B},\ a\in A
and
b\in B
, we have

(b-\varepsilon(b))\cdot(a\cdot m)=\bigl((b-\varepsilon(b))a\bigr)\cdot m

with
(b-\varepsilon(b))a\in B^{+}A=AB^{+}
, hence
(b-\varepsilon(b))\cdot(a\cdot m)=0
and
a\cdot m\in M^{B}
. We conclude that the above formula indeed defines an action of
H
on
\mathrm{Ext}^{0}_{B}({}_{\varepsilon}k,M)
.
∎

We recall some terminology and a few key results used in
[
27
]
in order to extend the
H
-module structure defined on
\mathrm{Ext}_{B}^{0}({}_{\varepsilon}k,M)
to
\mathrm{Ext}_{B}^{q}({}_{\varepsilon}k,M)
for any
q\geq 1
.

Definition 3.8
.

Let
\mathfrak{C}
and
\mathfrak{D}
be two abelian categories with enough injectives and let
T_{\bullet}=(T_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a sequence of covariant functors,
T_{n}:\mathfrak{C}\to\mathfrak{D}
. Then
T_{\bullet}
is said to be a
a
\delta
-functor
if for any short exact sequence

0\to M^{\prime}\to M\to M^{\prime\prime}\to 0

in
\mathfrak{C}
, there are natural connecting homomorphism

\delta:T_{n}(M^{\prime\prime})\to T_{n+1}(M^{\prime})

and all compositions are zero in the sequence

T_{n}(M^{\prime})\to T_{n}(M)\to T_{n}(M^{\prime\prime})\overset{\delta}{\to}T_{n+1}(M^{\prime}).

If in addition
T_{n}=0
for
n<0
and the above sequence is exact, then
T_{\bullet}
is said to be a
cohomological functor
.

Definition 3.9
.

If
T_{\bullet}=(T_{n})
and
S_{\bullet}=(S_{n})
are
\delta
-functors, a morphism of
\delta
-functors from
S_{\bullet}
to
T_{\bullet}
is a family
\varphi=(\varphi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
of natural transformations
\varphi_{n}:S_{n}\to T_{n}
such that the square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S_{n}(M^{\prime\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S_{n+1}(M^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T_{n}(M^{\prime\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T_{n+1}(M^{\prime})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi_{n}(M^{\prime\prime})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta_{S}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi_{n+1}(M^{\prime})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta_{T}}

commutes for every exact sequence
0\to M^{\prime}\to M\to M^{\prime\prime}\to 0
in
\mathfrak{C}
and every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Definition 3.10
.

A cohomological functor
T_{\bullet}=(T_{n}):\mathfrak{C}\to\mathfrak{D}
is
coeffaceable
if, for each
n>0
, any object
M
in
\mathfrak{C}
can be embedded in an object
N
such that
T_{n}(N)=0
.

Theorem 3.11
.

[
9
, Theorem 7.5]
Let
T_{\bullet}
be a cohomological and coeffaceable functor. If
S_{\bullet}
is a
\delta
-functor and
\varphi_{0}:T_{0}\to S_{0}
is a natural transformation, then
\varphi_{0}
extends uniquely to a map of
\delta
-functors
\varphi_{\bullet}:T_{\bullet}\to S_{\bullet}
.

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
k\longrightarrow B\overset{i}{\longrightarrow}A\overset{p}{\longrightarrow}H\longrightarrow k
be an exact sequence of Hopf algebras such that
A
is flat as a left and right
B
-module and let
M
be a left
A
-module.
There is a left
H
-action on
\mathrm{Ext}_{B}^{q}({}_{\varepsilon}k,M)
for
q\geq 0
which extends the structure on
\mathrm{Ext}_{B}^{0}({}_{\varepsilon}k,M)
defined in Lemma
3.7
.

Proof.

We first check that if
R:{}_{A}\mathcal{M}\to{}_{B}\mathcal{M}
denotes the functor of restriction of scalars, then the cohomological functor
\mathrm{Ext}_{B}^{\bullet}({}_{\varepsilon}k,-)\circ R:{}_{A}\mathcal{M}\to k-\mathrm{Vect}
is coeffaceable.

Let
M
be an
A
-module, there exists
N
an injective
A
-module such that
M\subset N
. The functor
R
has
A\otimes_{B}-
as left adjoint, hence we have
\mathrm{Hom}_{B}(-,R(N))\simeq\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(A\otimes_{B}-_{,}N)
. The right
B
-module
A
is flat and
N
is injective hence
\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(A\otimes_{B}-,N)
is exact and
R(N)
is an injective
B
-module. Thus, we have
\mathrm{Ext}_{B}^{n}({}_{\varepsilon}k,-)\circ R(N)=0
for
n\geq 1
and
\mathrm{Ext}_{B}^{\bullet}({}_{\varepsilon}k,-)\circ R
is coeffaceable.

For any
h\in H
let
\rho_{0}^{h}(-):\mathrm{Ext}_{B}^{0}({}_{\varepsilon}k,-)\circ R\to\mathrm{Ext}_{B}^{0}({}_{\varepsilon}k,-)\circ R
be the natural morphism
given by
\rho_{0}^{h}(M)(m)=h\cdot m
, for any
A
-module
M
,
m\in\mathrm{Ext}_{B}^{0}({}_{\varepsilon}k,M)
and
h\in H
. By Theorem
3.11
there is a unique morphism of
\delta
-functors
\rho_{\bullet}^{h}(-):\mathrm{Ext}_{B}^{\bullet}({}_{\varepsilon}k,-)\circ R\to\mathrm{Ext}_{B}^{\bullet}({}_{\varepsilon}k,-)\circ R
which extends
\rho_{0}^{h}(-)
.
We have, for any
h,k\in H
,
\rho_{0}^{hk}(-)=\rho_{0}^{h}(-)\circ\rho_{0}^{k}(-)
hence uniqueness in Theorem
3.11
also gives that
\rho_{\bullet}^{hk}(-)=\rho_{\bullet}^{h}(-)\circ\rho_{\bullet}^{k}(-)
, and
\rho_{\bullet}^{h}(-)
defines a structure of
H
-module.
∎

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
k\longrightarrow B\overset{i}{\longrightarrow}A\overset{p}{\longrightarrow}H\longrightarrow k
be an exact sequence of Hopf algebras and let
M
be an injective left
A
-module. Then

\mathrm{Ext}_{H}^{q}({}_{\varepsilon}k,M^{B})=0\ {\rm for}\ q\geq 1.

Proof.

Let
i:M\to\mathrm{Hom}_{k}(A,M),\ i(m)(x)=xm
. Then
i
is
A
-linear and injective, hence the injectivity of
M
ensure that there is an
A
-module
N
, such that
\mathrm{Hom}_{k}(A,M)\simeq M\oplus N
as
A
-modules.
Hence
\mathrm{Hom}_{k}(A,M)^{B}\simeq M^{B}\oplus N^{B}
as
H
-modules and since
\mathrm{Ext}
preserves direct sums, it is sufficient to show that

\mathrm{Ext}_{H}^{q}({}_{\varepsilon}k,\mathrm{Hom}_{k}(A,M)^{B})=0\ {\rm for}\ q\geq 1.

Moreover, we have an isomorphism of right
H
-modules
\varphi:\mathrm{Hom}_{k}(A,M)^{B}\to\mathrm{Hom}_{k}(H,M)
given by
\varphi(f)(p(a))=f(a)
for
f\in\mathrm{Hom}_{k}(A,M)^{B}
and
p(a)\in H
.

Now, consider
\mathbf{P^{\bullet}}
a resolution of
{}_{\varepsilon}k
by projective
H
-modules. Then
\mathrm{Ext}_{H}^{\bullet}({}_{\varepsilon}k,\mathrm{Hom}_{k}(A,M)^{B})
is the cohomology of the complex

\mathrm{Hom}_{H}(\mathbf{P^{\bullet}},\mathrm{Hom}_{k}(A,M)^{B})\simeq\mathrm{Hom}_{H}(\mathbf{P^{\bullet}},\mathrm{Hom}_{k}(H,M))\simeq\mathrm{Hom}_{k}(\mathbf{P^{\bullet}},M).

The exactness of
\mathbf{P^{\bullet}}
and the fact that
k
is a field gives that this complex is exact, and hence the result.
∎

We now get the following spectral sequence using the Grothendieck spectral sequence.

Theorem 3.14
.

Let
k\longrightarrow B\overset{i}{\longrightarrow}A\overset{p}{\longrightarrow}H\longrightarrow k
be an exact sequence of Hopf algebras and let
M
be a left
A
-module.
Assume that
A
is flat as left and right
B
-module. Then there is a spectral sequence

E^{p,q}_{2}=\mathrm{Ext}_{H}^{p}({}_{\varepsilon}k,\mathrm{Ext}_{B}^{q}({}_{\varepsilon}k,M))\implies\mathrm{Ext}^{p+q}_{A}({}_{\varepsilon}k,M)

which is natural in M.

Proof.

We proceed as in
[
27
]
. Let
F,\ F_{1},\ F_{2}
be the following three functors

\displaystyle F:{}_{A}\mathcal{M}\to{}_{k}\mathcal{M},\quad F=\mathrm{Hom}_{A}({}_{\varepsilon}k,-)=(-)^{A},

\displaystyle F_{1}:{}_{H}\mathcal{M}\to{}_{k}\mathcal{M},\quad F_{1}=\mathrm{Hom}_{H}({}_{\varepsilon}k,-)=(-)^{H},

\displaystyle F_{2}:{}_{A}\mathcal{M}\to{}_{H}\mathcal{M},\quad F_{2}=\mathrm{Hom}_{B}({}_{\varepsilon}k,-)=(-)^{B}.

For any
A
-module
M
, we have

\displaystyle F_{1}\circ F_{2}(M)=(M^{B})^{H}

\displaystyle=\biggl(\{m\in M,\ b\cdot m=\varepsilon(b)m,\ \forall b\in B\}\biggr)^{H}

\displaystyle=\{m\in M,\ b\cdot m=\varepsilon(b)m,\ \forall b\in B,\ h\cdot m=\varepsilon(h)m,\ \forall h\in H\}

\displaystyle=\{m\in M,\ b\cdot m=\varepsilon(b)m,\ \forall b\in B,\ p(a)\cdot m=\varepsilon(p(a))m,\ \forall a\in A\}

\displaystyle=\{m\in M,\ a\cdot m=\varepsilon(a)m,\ \forall a\in A\}

\displaystyle=F(M),

hence
F_{1}\circ F_{2}=F
. Moreover, if
M
is an injective
A
-module, Proposition
3.13
gives that
F_{2}(M)
is
F_{1}
-acyclic.
Therefore, applying the Grothendieck spectral sequence (
[
34
, Theorem 5.8.3]
), we get the desired spectral sequence.
∎

The
H
-module structure involved in the spectral sequence in Theorem
3.14
is, by construction of the Grothendieck spectral sequence, the one in Proposition
3.12
.
In Section
5
, we will need a more explicit description of this
H
-module structure on

, that the following result provides.

Proposition 3.15
.

Let
k\longrightarrow B\overset{i}{\longrightarrow}A\overset{p}{\longrightarrow}H\longrightarrow k
be an exact sequence of Hopf algebras such that
A
is flat as a left and right
B
-module and let
M
be a left

-module.
Let
P_{\bullet}\to{}_{\varepsilon}k
be a projective resolution of

by

and

the expression

\displaystyle(p(a)\cdot f)(x)=a_{(1)}f(S(a_{(2)})x)

(
*
)

defines an

-module structure on
\mathrm{Hom}_{B}(P_{q},M)
and on the complex
\mathrm{Hom}_{B}(P^{\bullet},M)
, and hence induces an
H
-module structure on
\mathrm{Ext}_{B}^{q}({}_{\varepsilon}k,M)
, which coincides with the
H
-module structure of Proposition

.

Proof.

First, we have to check that this defines an

a,\ a^{\prime}\in A
such that
p(a)=p(a^{\prime})
: there exist

and

Hence

does not depend on the choice of

such that
h=p(a)
.
Now, for

\displaystyle\bigl(p(a)\cdot f\bigr)(bx)

Thus

is
B
-linear. We obtain that

is well-defined and one easily checks that this defines a structure of left

-module on
\mathrm{Hom}_{B}(P_{q},M)

For any

let
\psi_{q}^{h}(-):\mathrm{Ext}_{B}^{q}({}_{\varepsilon}k,-)\circ R\to\mathrm{Ext}_{B}^{q}({}_{\varepsilon}k,-)\circ R

and any

.
We will check that

We have
\mathrm{Ext}_{B}^{0}({}_{\varepsilon}k,M)\simeq M^{B}
and this isomorphism is given by

for any

for any

In order to prove that

is a morphism of
\delta
-functors, we first explain de definition of the connecting homomorphism

in our case of interest. Let

and

For

\displaystyle p(a)\cdot d_{q+1}(\widetilde{\phi})(x)

\displaystyle=p(a)\cdot\bigl(\widetilde{\phi}\circ\partial_{q+1}\bigr)(x)

\displaystyle=a_{(1)}\bigl(\widetilde{\phi}\circ\partial_{q+1}\bigr)(S(a_{(2)})x)

\displaystyle=a_{(1)}\widetilde{\phi}(\partial_{q+1}(S(a_{(2)})x))

\displaystyle=a_{(1)}\widetilde{\phi}(S(a_{(2)})\partial_{q+1}(x))\quad{\rm since}\ \partial_{q+1}\ {\rm is}\ A{\rm-linear}

We now turn to the construction of the spectral sequence in homology. Recall that for

a left

-module on

.

Proposition 3.16
.

Let
k\longrightarrow B\overset{i}{\longrightarrow}A\overset{p}{\longrightarrow}H\longrightarrow k
be an exact sequence of Hopf algebras. For
M
a left
A
-module, there is a natural left

-module structure on
\mathrm{Tor}^{B}_{0}(k_{\varepsilon},M)

p(a)\cdot\pi_{M}(m)=\pi_{M}(am)

for any

and

where

is the canonical projection of

on

.

Proof.

Let
a,a^{\prime}\in A
be such that
p(a)=p(a^{\prime})
: there exist
b_{1},\ldots,b_{m}\in B^{+}
and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\in A
such that
a-a^{\prime}=\sum\limits_{i}a_{i}b_{i}
.
For
m\in M
, we have
\pi_{M}((a-a^{\prime})m)=\pi_{M}(\sum_{i}a_{i}b_{i}m)=\sum\limits_{i}\varepsilon(a_{i}b_{i})m=0
.
Hence for
h=p(a)\in H
, the application
\psi_{h}:M\to\mathrm{Tor}^{B}_{0}(k_{\varepsilon},M),\ m\mapsto\pi_{M}(am)
, does not depend on the choice of
a
.
For
h=p(a)\in H,\ b\in B
and
m\in M
, we have

\pi_{M}(a(bm-\varepsilon(b)m))=\pi_{M}(a(b-\varepsilon(b))m)=0.

Hence the application
\psi_{h}
satisfies
[M,B]\subset\ker(\psi_{h})
and induces the expected linear map
\phi_{h}:\mathrm{Tor}^{B}_{0}(k_{\varepsilon},M)\to\mathrm{Tor}^{B}_{0}(k_{\varepsilon},M)
, which defines the announced left
H
-module structure on
\mathrm{Tor}^{B}_{0}(k_{\varepsilon},M)
.
∎

As in the case of cohomology spaces, if
R:{}_{A}\mathcal{M}\to{}_{B}\mathcal{M}
denotes the functor of restriction of scalars, then the functor
\mathrm{Tor}^{\bullet}_{B}(k_{\varepsilon},-)\circ R
is homological and effaceable, hence the above structure of
H
-module extends to the homology spaces of higher degree.

Proposition 3.17
.

Let
k\longrightarrow B\overset{i}{\longrightarrow}A\overset{p}{\longrightarrow}H\longrightarrow k
be an exact sequence of Hopf algebras and
M
a left
A
-module. The previous action extends to an action on
\mathrm{Tor}_{n}^{B}(k_{\varepsilon},M)
for
n\geq 0
.

Lemma 3.18
.

Let
k\longrightarrow B\overset{i}{\longrightarrow}A\overset{p}{\longrightarrow}H\longrightarrow k
be an exact sequence of Hopf algebras. For any vector space
V
, we have
\mathrm{Tor}_{0}^{B}(k_{\varepsilon},A\otimes V)\simeq H\otimes V
as left
H
-modules.

Proof.

We have
\mathrm{Tor}_{0}^{B}(k_{\varepsilon},A\otimes V)\simeq A\otimes V/[A\otimes V,B]
. Consider the map

\displaystyle\varphi:A\otimes V\

\displaystyle\longrightarrow H\otimes V

\displaystyle a\otimes v

\displaystyle\longmapsto p(a)\otimes v

which is clearly surjective since
p
is. A direct and easy computation shows that
\ker(\varphi)=[A\otimes V,B]
, hence it induces the isomorphism

\displaystyle A\otimes V/[A\otimes V,B]\

\displaystyle\longrightarrow H\otimes V

\displaystyle\overline{a\otimes v}

\displaystyle\longmapsto p(a)\otimes v

which is clearly
H
-linear.
∎

Proposition 3.19
.

Let
k\longrightarrow B\overset{i}{\longrightarrow}A\overset{p}{\longrightarrow}H\longrightarrow k
be an exact sequence of Hopf algebras. Let
M
be a projective left
A
-module, then

\mathrm{Tor}_{n}^{H}(k_{\varepsilon},\mathrm{Tor}_{0}^{B}(k_{\varepsilon},M))=0,\quad{\rm for}\ n\geq 1.

Proof.

The
A
-module
M
being projective, there exists a module
N
and a vector space
V
such that
A\otimes V\simeq M\oplus N
as
A
-modules. Since
\mathrm{Tor}
preserves direct sums, it is sufficient to prove that

\mathrm{Tor}_{n}^{H}(k_{\varepsilon},\mathrm{Tor}_{0}^{B}(k_{\varepsilon},A\otimes V))=0,\quad{\rm for}\ n\geq 1.

Now, let
\mathbf{P^{\bullet}}
be a projective resolution of the
H
-module
k_{\varepsilon}
, then
\mathrm{Tor}_{\bullet}^{H}(k_{\varepsilon},\mathrm{Tor}_{0}^{B}(k_{\varepsilon},A\otimes V))
is the homology of the complex

\mathbf{P^{\bullet}}\otimes_{H}\mathrm{Tor}_{0}^{B}(k_{\varepsilon},A\otimes V)\simeq\mathbf{P^{\bullet}}\otimes_{H}(H\otimes V)\simeq\mathbf{P^{\bullet}}\otimes V

where the first isomorphism is Lemma
3.18
. Finally using the exactness of
\mathbf{P^{\bullet}}
, we get that this complex is exact since
k
is a field. Hence

H_{n}(\mathbf{P^{\bullet}}\otimes_{H}\mathrm{Tor}_{0}^{B}(k_{\varepsilon},A\otimes V))\simeq H_{n}(P^{\bullet}\otimes V)=0

for
n\geq 1

∎

Theorem 3.20
.

Let
k\longrightarrow B\overset{i}{\longrightarrow}A\overset{p}{\longrightarrow}H\longrightarrow k
be an exact sequence of Hopf algebras such that
A
is projective as a left and right
B
-module and let
M
be a left
A
-module.
There is a spectral sequence

E_{p,q}^{2}=\mathrm{Tor}^{H}_{p}(k_{\varepsilon},\mathrm{Tor}^{B}_{q}(k_{\varepsilon},M))\implies\mathrm{Tor}_{p+q}^{A}(k_{\varepsilon},M)

which is natural in M.

Proof.

We reason as in
[
27
]
: let
F,\ F_{1},\ F_{2}
be the following three functors

\displaystyle F:{}_{A}\mathcal{M}\to{}_{k}\mathcal{M},\quad F=k_{\varepsilon}\otimes_{A}-=-/[-,A],

\displaystyle F_{1}:{}_{H}\mathcal{M}\to{}_{k}\mathcal{M},\quad F_{1}=k_{\varepsilon}\otimes_{H}-=-/[-,H],

\displaystyle F_{2}:{}_{A}\mathcal{M}\to{}_{H}\mathcal{M},\quad F_{2}=k_{\varepsilon}\otimes_{B}-=-/[-,B].

For any
A
-module
M
, we have

F_{1}\circ F_{2}(M)=\mathrm{Tor}_{0}^{H}(k_{\varepsilon},M/B^{+}M)=\bigl(M/B^{+}M\bigr)/H^{+}\bigl(M/B^{+}M\bigr)

Consider the canonic projection
\theta:M/B^{+}M\to M/A^{+}M
, we denote
\pi^{(B)}
(resp.
\pi^{(A)}
) the projection of
M
onto
M/B^{+}M
(resp.
M/A^{+}M
). For
h=p(a)\in H
and
m\in M
, we have

\theta\biggl((h-\varepsilon(h))\cdot\pi^{(B)}(m)\biggr)=\theta\biggl(\pi^{(B)}\bigl((a-\varepsilon(a))m\bigr)\biggr)=\pi^{(A)}\bigl((a-\varepsilon(a))m\bigr)=0.

Hence
H^{+}\bigl(M/B^{+}M\bigr)\subset\ker(\theta)
. On the converse, if
\theta\bigl(\pi^{(B)}(m)\bigr)=\pi^{(A)}(m)=0
, there exist
a_{1},\dots,\ a_{n}\in A^{+}
and
m_{1},\dots,\ m_{n}
such that
m=\sum\limits_{i}a_{i}m_{i}
. We thus have

\pi^{(A)}(m)=\theta\bigl(\pi^{(B)}(\sum\limits_{i}a_{i}m_{i})\bigr)=\theta\bigl(\sum\limits_{i}p(a_{i})\cdot\pi^{(B)}(m_{i})\bigr)

with
\sum\limits_{i}p(a_{i})\cdot\pi^{(B)}(m_{i})\in H^{+}\bigl(M/B^{+}M\bigr)
, hence we have
\ker(\theta)\bigl(M/B^{+}M\bigr)
. We get an isomorphism
\widetilde{\theta}:\bigl(M/B^{+}M\bigr)/H^{+}\bigl(M/B^{+}M\bigr)\to M/A^{+}M
and finally
F=F_{1}\circ F_{2}(M)
.

Moreover, if
M
is a projective
A
-module, Proposition
3.19
gives that
F_{2}(M)
is
F_{1}
-acyclic.
Hence, applying the Grothendieck spectral sequence in homology (
[
34
, Theorem 5.8.4]
), we get the desired spectral sequence.

∎

Remark 3.21
.

It is shown in
[
26
, Corollary 1.8]
that if
A
is Hopf algebra with bijective antipode and
B\subset A
is a Hopf subalgebra, then
A
is faithfully flat as a (left or right)
B
-module if an only if it is projective as a (left or right)
B
-module. Hence, in order to invoke one of the above spectral sequences, we can indifferently assume projectivity or faithful flatness.

4
Homological smoothness and exact sequences of Hopf algebras

In this section, using the spectral sequences defined in Section
3.2
, we prove that if
k\longrightarrow B\overset{i}{\longrightarrow}A\overset{p}{\longrightarrow}H\longrightarrow k
is an exact sequence of Hopf algebras with bijective antipodes such that
A
is faithfully flat as a left and right
B
-module, then homological smoothness of
B
and
H
implies the smoothness of
A
. This is the first step towards the proof of Theorem
1.1
. We begin by a known result on the finiteness of the cohomological dimension
[
4
, Proposition 3.2]
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
k\longrightarrow B\overset{i}{\longrightarrow}A\overset{p}{\longrightarrow}H\longrightarrow k
be an exact sequence of Hopf algebras with bijective antipodes such that
A
is flat as left and right
B
-module. We have
\mathrm{cd}(A)\leq\mathrm{cd}(B)+\mathrm{cd}(H)
, and hence if
\mathrm{cd}(B)
and
\mathrm{cd}(H)
are finite, so is
\mathrm{cd}(A)
.

Proof.

Under those assumptions, we can use Theorem
3.14
. Thus, for every left
A
-module
M
, we get a spectral sequence

E^{p,q}_{2}=\mathrm{Ext}_{H}^{p}({}_{\varepsilon}k,\mathrm{Ext}_{B}^{q}({}_{\varepsilon}k,M))\implies\mathrm{Ext}^{p+q}_{A}({}_{\varepsilon}k,M).

If
\mathrm{cd}(B)
or
\mathrm{cd}(H)
is infinite, there is nothing to show, so we assume that these are finite.
For
p>\mathrm{cd}(H)
or
q>\mathrm{cd}(B)
, we have
E^{pq}_{2}=\{0\}
. Now, if we denote
d_{2}^{p,q}:E^{pq}_{2}\to E^{p+2,q-1}_{2}
the differential on the second page of the spectral sequence, we get that for
p>\mathrm{cd}(H)
or
q>\mathrm{cd}(B)
the maps
d_{2}^{p,q}:E^{p,q}_{2}\to E^{p+2,q-1}_{2}
and
d_{2}^{p-2,q+1}:E^{p-2,q+1}_{2}\to E_{2}^{p,q}
are both
0
hence
\{0\}=E_{2}^{p,q}\simeq E_{3}^{p,q}\simeq..\simeq\mathrm{Ext}^{p+q}_{A}({}_{\varepsilon}k,M)
. We obtain that
\mathrm{Ext}^{n}_{A}({}_{\varepsilon}k,M)=\{0\}

for
n>\mathrm{cd}(B)+\mathrm{cd}(H)
, and hence
\mathrm{cd}(A)\leq\mathrm{cd}(H)+\mathrm{cd}(B)
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

We will prove in the following section that if
B
is homologically smooth and
H
has finite cohomological dimension, then we have
\mathrm{cd}(A)=\mathrm{cd}(B)+\mathrm{cd}(H)
. This generalizes
[
6
, Theorem 5.5]
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
k\longrightarrow B\overset{i}{\longrightarrow}A\overset{p}{\longrightarrow}H\longrightarrow k
be an exact sequence of Hopf algebras with bijective antipodes such that
A
is faithfully flat as left and right
B
-module. If
H
and
B
are homologically smooth algebras, then
A
is homologically smooth.

Proof.

We know from Proposition
4.1
that
A
has finite cohomological dimension, hence to prove that
A
is homologicallly smooth,
it remains to prove, by Proposition
2.3
and Theorem
2.18
, that
k_{\varepsilon}
is of type
FP_{\infty}
as a right
A
-module.

To prove that
k_{\varepsilon}
is of type
FP_{\infty}
as an
A
-module, we will use the characterization of Proposition
2.4
, and hence we consider the
A
-module
M=\prod A
where
\prod
is an arbitrary direct product. We have to show that for
n\geq 1
, one has
\mathrm{Tor}_{n}^{A}(k_{\varepsilon},M)=0
and that
\mathrm{Tor}_{0}^{A}(k_{\varepsilon},M)\simeq k_{\varepsilon}\otimes_{A}\left(\prod A\right)\simeq\prod k_{\varepsilon}\simeq\prod\mathrm{Tor}_{0}^{A}(k_{\varepsilon},A)
.

Under the hypothesis that
A
is faithfully flat as a right and left
B
-module and regarding Remark
3.21
, we can use Theorem
3.20
. Thus, for every left
A
-module
M
we get a spectral sequence

E_{pq}^{2}=\mathrm{Tor}^{H}_{p}(k_{\varepsilon},\mathrm{Tor}_{q}^{B}(k_{\varepsilon},M))\implies\mathrm{Tor}_{p+q}^{A}(k_{\varepsilon},M).

The
B
-module
k_{\varepsilon}
is of type
FP_{\infty}
hence
\mathrm{Tor}^{B}_{q}(k_{\varepsilon},-)
commutes with direct products. Moreover,
A
is flat as a left
B
-module thus for
q\geq 1
, we get that

\mathrm{Tor}^{B}_{q}(k_{\varepsilon},\prod A)\simeq\prod\mathrm{Tor}^{B}_{q}(k_{\varepsilon},A)=\{0\}.

Hence we have
E_{pq}^{2}=\{0\}
for
q>0
.

If
\pi_{M}
(resp.
\pi_{A}
) denotes the natural projection of
M
(resp.
A
) onto
\mathrm{Tor}_{0}^{B}(k_{\varepsilon},M)\simeq M/[M,B]
(resp.
\mathrm{Tor}^{B}_{0}(k_{\varepsilon},A)\simeq A/[A,B]
), the natural isomorphism
\mu:\mathrm{Tor}_{0}^{B}(k_{\varepsilon},M)\to\prod\mathrm{Tor}_{0}^{B}(k_{\varepsilon},A)
is given by
\mu(\pi_{M}\bigl(x_{i}\bigr)_{i})=\bigl(\pi_{A}(x_{i})\bigr)_{i}
for any
x_{i}\in A
. Hence, for
h=p(a)\in H
, we have

\mu(h\cdot\pi_{M}\bigl(x_{i}\bigr)_{i})=\mu(\pi_{M}\bigl(ax_{i}\bigr)_{i})=\bigl(\pi_{A}(ax_{i})\bigr)_{i}=h\cdot\bigl((\pi_{A}(x_{i}))_{i}\bigr).

The natural isomorphism
\mathrm{Tor}_{0}^{B}(k_{\varepsilon},M)\simeq\prod\mathrm{Tor}^{B}_{0}(k_{\varepsilon},A)
is thus an isomorphism of
H
-modules. Therefore, since the algebras
B
and
H
are homologically smooth, we get, using Proposition
2.4
, for
p\geq 0

E_{p0}^{2}=\mathrm{Tor}_{p}^{H}(k_{\varepsilon},\mathrm{Tor}_{0}^{B}(k_{\varepsilon},M))\simeq\mathrm{Tor}_{p}^{H}(k_{\varepsilon},\prod\mathrm{Tor}_{0}^{B}(k_{\varepsilon},A))\simeq\prod\mathrm{Tor}_{p}^{H}(k_{\varepsilon},\mathrm{Tor}_{0}^{B}(k_{\varepsilon},A)).

The
A
-module
A
is projective, hence Proposition
3.19
ensures that

\mathrm{Tor}_{p}^{H}(k_{\varepsilon},\mathrm{Tor}_{0}^{B}(k_{\varepsilon},A))=\{0\}\ \text{for $p\geq 1$.}

Hence for
p\geq 1
we have

E_{p,0}^{2}=\mathrm{Tor}_{p}^{H}(k_{\varepsilon},\mathrm{Tor}_{0}^{B}(k_{\varepsilon},M))\simeq\prod\mathrm{Tor}_{p}^{H}(k_{\varepsilon},\mathrm{Tor}_{0}^{B}(k_{\varepsilon},A))=\{0\}

We get that
E^{2}_{p,q}=\mathrm{Tor}^{H}_{p}(k_{\varepsilon},\mathrm{Tor}_{0}^{B}(k_{\varepsilon},M))=\{0\}
for
(p,q)\neq(0,0)
, and hence the spectral sequence ensures that
HH_{n}(A,M)=\{0\}
for
n>0
.

Finally, using the isomorphism in the proof of Theorem
3.20
and the fact that
k_{\varepsilon}
is of type
FP_{\infty}
as a right
H
-module and a right
B
-module, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tor}_{0}^{A}(k_{\varepsilon},\prod A)

\displaystyle\simeq\mathrm{Tor}_{0}^{H}(k_{\varepsilon},\mathrm{Tor}_{0}^{B}(k_{\varepsilon},\prod A))

\displaystyle\simeq\mathrm{Tor}^{H}_{0}(k_{\varepsilon},\prod\mathrm{Tor}^{B}_{0}(k_{\varepsilon},A))

\displaystyle\simeq\prod\mathrm{Tor}^{H}_{0}(k_{\varepsilon},\mathrm{Tor}^{B}_{0}(B,A))

\displaystyle\simeq\prod\mathrm{Tor}_{0}^{A}(k_{\varepsilon},A).

It is not difficult to check that the above isomorphism is the natural map in the third item of Proposition
2.4
. We thus conclude from Proposition
2.4
that
A
is homologically smooth.
∎

5
Homological duality for exact sequences of Hopf algebras

This section is dedicated to the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We begin with some generalities.
Let
B
be an algebra, let
M
and
N
be left
B
-modules. We consider the natural morphism
\phi:\mathrm{Hom}_{B}(N,B)\otimes_{B}M\to\mathrm{Hom}_{B}(N,M)
defined by
\phi(f\otimes m)(n)=f(n)m
for
m\in M,\ f\in\mathrm{Hom}_{B}(N,B)
and
n\in N
. The following is a right version of
[
6
, Lemma 5.2]
, we give a proof for the sake of completeness.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
B
be an algebra, let
M
be a left
B
-module and
N
a finitely generated projective left
B
-module. The morphism
\phi:\mathrm{Hom}_{B}(N,B)\otimes_{B}M\to\mathrm{Hom}_{B}(N,M)
defined above is an isomorphism.

Proof.

There exists
B
-modules
L
and
X
such that
L
is free and
L\simeq N\oplus X
. By naturality of
\phi
, we only need to show that
\phi:\mathrm{Hom}_{B}(L,B)\otimes_{B}M\to\mathrm{Hom}_{B}(L,M)
is bijective and again by naturality, we only need to show that it is an isomorphism for
L\simeq B
. In this case
\phi
is the identity hence it is bijective.
∎

Now, we replace the left
B
-module
N
by a
B
-projective resolution
P^{\bullet}\to N
. This yields natural morphisms
\phi^{i}:\mathrm{Ext}^{i}_{B}(N,B)\otimes_{B}M\to\mathrm{Ext}_{B}^{i}(N,M)
. Under appropriate assumptions on
B
and
M
, these morphisms are bijective.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
B
be algebra and
M
and
N
left
B
-modules. If
N
is of type
FP_{\infty}
and
M
is flat, then the morphisms

\phi^{i}:\mathrm{Ext}^{i}_{B}(N,B)\otimes_{B}M\to\mathrm{Ext}_{B}^{i}(N,M)

are isomorphisms for any
i\geq 0
.

Proof.

Let
P_{\bullet}\to N
be a projective resolution with
P_{i}
finitely generated for
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Using Proposition
5.1
, we have natural isomorphisms
\mathrm{Hom}_{B}(P_{i},B)\otimes_{B}M\simeq\mathrm{Hom}_{B}(P_{i},M)
for
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

This yields to isomorphisms in cohomology
H^{i}\bigl(\mathrm{Hom}_{B}(P_{\bullet},B)\otimes_{B}M\bigr)\simeq H^{i}\bigl(\mathrm{Hom}_{B}(P_{\bullet},M)\bigr)=\mathrm{Ext}^{i}_{B}(N,M)
for
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Finally, using flatness of
M
, we have

H^{i}\bigl(\mathrm{Hom}_{B}(P_{\bullet},B)\otimes_{B}M\bigr)\simeq H^{i}\bigl(\mathrm{Hom}_{B}(P_{\bullet},B)\bigr)\otimes_{B}M\simeq\mathrm{Ext}^{i}_{B}(N,B)\otimes_{B}M

which concludes the proof.
∎

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
k\longrightarrow B\overset{i}{\longrightarrow}A\overset{p}{\longrightarrow}H\longrightarrow k
be an exact sequence of Hopf algebras with bijective antipodes, let
X
be a right
A
-module and let
M
be a left
A
-module. Then there exists a left
A
-module structure on
X\otimes_{B}M
defined by

a\cdot(x\otimes_{B}m)=xS^{-1}(a_{(2)})\otimes_{B}a_{(1)}m,\ a\in A,\ x\in X,m\in M.

For
M=A
, endowing
X\otimes H
with the structure of left
A
-module given by
a^{\prime}\cdot(x\otimes p(a))=x\otimes p(a^{\prime}a)
for any
a^{\prime},a\in A
and
x\in X
, we have an isomorphism of left
A
-modules
u:X\otimes H\to X\otimes_{B}A
.

Proof.

We first check that the left
A
-action on
X\otimes_{B}M
is well defined. For
a\in A
,
x\in X
,
m\in M
and
b\in B
, we have

\displaystyle a\cdot(xb\otimes_{B}m)

\displaystyle=xbS^{-1}(a_{(2)})\otimes_{B}a_{(1)}m

\displaystyle=x\varepsilon(a_{(3)})bS^{-1}(a_{(2)})\otimes_{B}a_{(1)}m

\displaystyle=xS^{-1}(a_{(4)})a_{(3)}bS^{-1}(a_{(2)})\otimes_{B}a_{(1)}m

\displaystyle=xS^{-1}(a_{(4)})\otimes_{B}a_{(3)}bS^{-1}(a_{(2)})a_{(1)}m\quad{\rm using\penalty\ Lemma\ref{lem : B stable}}

\displaystyle=xS^{-1}(a_{(2)})\otimes_{B}a_{(1)}bm

\displaystyle=a\cdot(x\otimes_{B}bm)

and hence the
A
-action on
X\otimes_{B}M
is well defined.
Let

\displaystyle u:X\otimes H\

\displaystyle\longrightarrow X\otimes_{B}A

\displaystyle x\otimes p(a)

\displaystyle\longmapsto xS^{-1}(a_{(2)})\otimes_{B}a_{(1)}.

It is not difficult to check that

is well-defined.
For
a,a^{\prime}\in A
and

, we have

u(a^{\prime}\cdot(x\otimes p(a)))=u(x\otimes p(a^{\prime}a))=xS^{-1}(a_{(2)})S^{-1}(a^{\prime}_{(2)})\otimes_{B}a^{\prime}_{(1)}a_{(1)}=a^{\prime}\cdot u(x\otimes p(a))

and hence

is
A
-linear. Now consider the linear map

\displaystyle v:X\otimes A\

\displaystyle\longrightarrow X\otimes H

\displaystyle x\otimes a

\displaystyle\longmapsto xa_{(2)}\otimes p(a_{(1)})

For
x\in X,\ a\in A
and
b\in B
, we have

v(xb\otimes a)=xba_{(2)}\otimes p(a_{(1)})=xb_{(2)}a_{(2)}\otimes p(b_{(1)})p(a_{(1)})=v(x\otimes ba)

since

. Thus

induces a map

\displaystyle v:X\otimes_{B}A\

\displaystyle\longrightarrow X\otimes H

\displaystyle x\otimes_{B}a

\displaystyle\longmapsto xa_{(2)}\otimes p(a_{(1)})

For

and

, we have

\displaystyle u\circ v(x\otimes_{B}a)

\displaystyle=u\bigl(xa_{(2)}\otimes p(a_{(1)})\bigr)=xa_{(3)}S^{-1}(a_{(2)})\otimes_{B}a_{(1)}=x\otimes a,\ \text{and}

\displaystyle v\circ u(x\otimes p(a))

\displaystyle=v\bigl(xS^{-1}(a_{(2)})\otimes_{B}a_{(1)}\bigr)=xS^{-1}(a_{(3)})a_{(2)}\otimes p(a_{(1)})=x\otimes p(a).

Hence

is an isomorphism of
A
-modules.
∎

Let
k\longrightarrow B\overset{i}{\longrightarrow}A\overset{p}{\longrightarrow}H\longrightarrow k
be an exact sequence of Hopf algebras with
A
projective as a left

-module. If

is a left

-module, recall from the previous section that
\mathrm{Ext}_{B}^{\bullet}({}_{\varepsilon}k,M)
is endowed with a natural left

-module structure defined at the cochain level by

\bigl(a\rightharpoonup f\bigr)(x)=a_{(1)}f(S(a_{(2)})x)

for

and

where

is an

{}_{\varepsilon}k
. This is this action of

induced by the action of

defined in Proposition
3.15
.

Moreover
\mathrm{Ext}_{B}^{\bullet}({}_{\varepsilon}k,B)
is endowed with a natural structure of right

-module, extending its natural

-module structure, defined as follows. Consider an

-projective resolution

of

. For any

and for

By Lemma

we have

for

.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
k\longrightarrow B\overset{i}{\longrightarrow}A\overset{p}{\longrightarrow}H\longrightarrow k
be an exact sequence of Hopf algebras such that
A
is faithfully flat as a left
B
-module, and let
M
be a left
A
-module. The morphisms

\phi^{i}:\mathrm{Ext}^{i}_{B}({}_{\varepsilon}k,B)\otimes_{B}M\longrightarrow\mathrm{Ext}_{B}^{i}({}_{\varepsilon}k,M)

defined above are morphisms of left
A
-modules, where the left

-module structure on
\mathrm{Ext}_{B}^{i}({}_{\varepsilon}k,B)\otimes_{B}M
is the one of Proposition
5.3
, i.e. is given by

a\rightarrow([f]\otimes_{B}m)=[f]\leftharpoonup S^{-1}(a_{(2)})\otimes_{B}a_{(1)}m

for

and
[f]\in\mathrm{Ext}_{B}^{i}({}_{\varepsilon}k,B)
.

Proof.

Let
P_{\bullet}
be a projective resolution of
{}_{\varepsilon}k
by
A
-modules. At the cochain level, we have for
a\in A,\ m\in M,\ f\in\mathrm{Hom}_{B}(P_{i},B)
and
x\in P_{i}

\displaystyle\phi^{i}\biggl(a\rightarrow(f\otimes m)\biggr)(x)

\displaystyle=\phi^{i}\biggl(f\leftharpoonup S^{-1}(a_{(2)})\otimes a_{(1)}m\biggr)(x)

\displaystyle=(f\leftharpoonup S^{-1}(a_{(2)}))(x)a_{(1)}m

\displaystyle=a_{(3)}f(S(a_{(4)})x)S^{-1}(a_{(2)})a_{(1)}m

\displaystyle=a_{(1)}f(S(a_{(2)})x)m

\displaystyle=\biggl(a\rightharpoonup\bigl(\phi^{i}(f\otimes m)\bigr)\biggr)(x)

and from this we deduce the announced result.
∎

We can now prove the homological equality announced in Section
4
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
k\longrightarrow B\overset{i}{\longrightarrow}A\overset{p}{\longrightarrow}H\longrightarrow k
be an exact sequence of Hopf algebras with bijective antipodes such that
A
is faithfully flat as a left and right
B
-module. If
B
is homologically smooth and
H
has finite cohomological dimension, then we have

\mathrm{cd}(A)=\mathrm{cd}(B)+\mathrm{cd}(H).

Proof.

Let
n=\mathrm{cd}(B)
and
m=\mathrm{cd}(H)
. There is a free left
H
-module
F=V\otimes H
such that
\mathrm{Ext}^{m}_{H}({}_{\varepsilon}k,F)\neq 0
. Following Theorem
3.14
for the left
A
-module
V\otimes A
, we get the spectral sequence

E^{p,q}_{2}=\mathrm{Ext}_{H}^{p}({}_{\varepsilon}k,\mathrm{Ext}_{B}^{q}({}_{\varepsilon}k,V\otimes A))\implies\mathrm{Ext}^{p+q}_{A}({}_{\varepsilon}k,V\otimes A).

For
p>\mathrm{cd}(H)
or
q>\mathrm{cd}(B)
, we have
E_{2}^{p,q}=0
hence, reasoning as in the proof of Proposition
4.1
, we get that

\mathrm{Ext}^{m+n}_{A}({}_{\varepsilon}k,V\otimes A)\simeq E^{p,q}_{2}\simeq\mathrm{Ext}^{m}_{H}({}_{\varepsilon}k,\mathrm{Ext}^{n}_{B}({}_{\varepsilon}k,V\otimes A)).

Then, using Proposition
5.2
and Proposition
5.4
we have
\mathrm{Ext}^{n}_{B}({}_{\varepsilon}k,V\otimes A)\simeq\mathrm{Ext}^{n}_{B}({}_{\varepsilon}k,B)\otimes_{B}(V\otimes A)
as left
A
-modules. Since the action on
V\otimes A
is simply multiplication on
A
, we have
\mathrm{Ext}^{n}_{B}({}_{\varepsilon}k,B)\otimes_{B}(V\otimes A)\simeq(\mathrm{Ext}^{n}_{B}({}_{\varepsilon}k,B)\otimes V)\otimes_{B}A
and using Proposition
5.3
with
X=\mathrm{Ext}^{n}_{B}({}_{\varepsilon}k,B)\otimes V
, we get that
\mathrm{Ext}^{n}_{B}({}_{\varepsilon}k,V\otimes A)\simeq\mathrm{Ext}^{n}_{B}({}_{\varepsilon}k,B)\otimes V\otimes H
as left
A
-modules where the
A
-action on
\mathrm{Ext}^{n}_{B}({}_{\varepsilon}k,B)\otimes V\otimes H
is induced by the multiplication in
H
and the surjective morphism
p:A\to H
. Therefore, we have
\mathrm{Ext}^{n}_{B}({}_{\varepsilon}k,V\otimes A)\simeq\mathrm{Ext}^{n}_{B}({}_{\varepsilon}k,B)\otimes V\otimes H
as
H
-modules where the action on the left term is the one described in Proposition
3.15
and the action on the right term is the multiplication in
H
. Hence
\mathrm{Ext}^{m+n}_{A}({}_{\varepsilon}k,V\otimes A)\simeq\mathrm{Ext}^{m}_{H}({}_{\varepsilon}k,\mathrm{Ext}^{n}_{B}({}_{\varepsilon}k,B)\otimes V\otimes H)
.

Finally, since
\mathrm{cd}(B)=n
and
B
is homologically smooth, we have
\mathrm{Ext}^{n}_{B}({}_{\varepsilon}k,B)\neq 0
, thus
\mathrm{Ext}^{n}_{B}({}_{\varepsilon}k,B)\otimes V\otimes H
can be written as a direct sum of copies of
V\otimes H
and
\mathrm{Ext}^{m}_{H}({}_{\varepsilon}k,\mathrm{Ext}^{n}_{B}({}_{\varepsilon}k,B)\otimes V\otimes H)
is non zero which concludes the proof.
∎

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
k\longrightarrow B\overset{i}{\longrightarrow}A\overset{p}{\longrightarrow}H\longrightarrow k
be an exact sequence of Hopf algebras with bijective antipodes such that
A
is faithfully flat as a left and right
B
-module and let
M
be a left
A
-module. For
q\geq 0
, there is an isomorphism of
H
-modules

\mathrm{Ext}^{q}_{B}({}_{\varepsilon}k,B)\otimes H\simeq\mathrm{Ext}_{B}^{q}({}_{\varepsilon}k,A)

where the
H
-module structure on
\mathrm{Ext}^{q}_{B}({}_{\varepsilon}k,B)\otimes H
is induced by left multiplication on
H
and the
H
-module structure on
\mathrm{Ext}_{B}^{q}({}_{\varepsilon}k,A)
is the one defined in Proposition
3.15
.
This isomorphism is given by

[f]\otimes p(a)\mapsto p(a)\cdot[f]

for
[f]\in\mathrm{Ext}^{q}_{B}({}_{\varepsilon}k,B)
and
a\in A
.

Proof.

Using Proposition
5.3
with
X=\mathrm{Ext}_{B}^{q}({}_{\varepsilon}k,B)
and Proposition
5.2
for the flat left
B
-module
M=A
, we get the isomorphism
\mathrm{Ext}^{q}_{B}(k_{\varepsilon},B)\otimes H\simeq\mathrm{Ext}_{B}^{q}(k_{\varepsilon},A)
.

Let
P_{\bullet}
be a projective resolution of
{}_{\varepsilon}k
by
A
-modules, at the cochain level, we have for
a\in A,\ f\in\mathrm{Hom}_{B}(P_{i},B)
and
x\in P_{i}

\displaystyle\phi^{i}\circ u\biggl((f\otimes p(a))\biggr)(x)

\displaystyle=\phi^{i}\biggl(f\leftharpoonup S^{-1}(a_{(2)})\otimes a_{(1)}\biggr)(x)

\displaystyle=(f\leftharpoonup S^{-1}(a_{(2)}))(x)a_{(1)}

\displaystyle=a_{(1)}f(S(a_{(2)})x)m

\displaystyle=(p(a)\cdot f)(x)

Hence, this isomorphism is given by the above formula and it is clearly
H
-linear which concludes the proof.
∎

Using this isomorphism of
H
-modules, we get the following isomorphisms between cohomology spaces.

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
k\longrightarrow B\overset{i}{\longrightarrow}A\overset{p}{\longrightarrow}H\longrightarrow k
be an exact sequence of Hopf algebras with bijective antipodes such that
A
is faithfully flat as a left and right
B
-module. Assume that
H
and
B
are smooth Hopf algebras. For
p,q\geq 0
, we have an isomorphism

E_{2}^{p,q}=\mathrm{Ext}_{H}^{p}({}_{\varepsilon}k,\mathrm{Ext}_{B}^{q}({}_{\varepsilon}k,A))\simeq\mathrm{Ext}_{B}^{q}({}_{\varepsilon}k,B)\otimes\mathrm{Ext}_{H}^{p}({}_{\varepsilon}k,H).

Proof.

Using the isomorphism of
H
-modules in Proposition
5.6
, we have that for
p,q\geq 0

E_{2}^{p,q}=\mathrm{Ext}_{H}^{p}({}_{\varepsilon}k,\mathrm{Ext}_{B}^{q}({}_{\varepsilon}k,A))\simeq\mathrm{Ext}_{H}^{p}\left({}_{\varepsilon}k,\mathrm{Ext}_{B}^{q}({}_{\varepsilon}k,B)\otimes H\right).

Hence, using the smoothness of
H
, Proposition
2.5
and the fact that the
H
-module structure on
\mathrm{Ext}_{B}^{q}({}_{\varepsilon}k,B)\otimes H
is given by multiplication on
H
, we get the result.
∎

This allows to prove Theorem
1.1

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
k\longrightarrow B\overset{i}{\longrightarrow}A\overset{p}{\longrightarrow}H\longrightarrow k
be an exact sequence of Hopf algebras with bijective antipodes such that
A
is faithfully flat as a left and right
B
-module, and with
H
and
B
homologically smooth.

Let us first assume that
B
and
H
have homological duality.
Using Theorem
4.3
, we get that
A
is homologically smooth, and (see Theorem
2.18
) we have to prove that
\mathrm{Ext}_{A}^{n}({}_{\varepsilon}k,A)=0
if
n\neq\mathrm{cd}(A)
.

Since
H
and
B
have homological duality, the isomorphism of Proposition
5.7
gives that
E_{2}^{p,q}=0
if
p\neq\mathrm{cd}(H)
or
q\neq\mathrm{cd}(B)
. Hence,
E_{2}^{\mathrm{cd}(H),\mathrm{cd}(B)}
is the only non-zero space on the second page of the spectral sequence and we get that
\mathrm{Ext}_{A}^{n}({}_{\varepsilon}k,A)=0
if
n\neq{\mathrm{cd}(B)+\mathrm{cd}(H)}
, while
\mathrm{cd}(A)=\mathrm{cd}(B)+\mathrm{cd}(H)
by Theorem
5.5
: we conclude that
A
has homological duality.

Conversely, assume that
A
has homological duality. Let
r
and
s
be the smallest integers such that
\mathrm{Ext}^{r}_{H}({}_{\varepsilon}k,H)\neq 0
and
\mathrm{Ext}_{B}^{s}({}_{\varepsilon}k,B)\neq 0
.
Using the isomorphism of Proposition
5.7
and a corner argument, we get that

E_{2}^{r,s}\simeq\mathrm{Ext}_{A}^{r+s}({}_{\varepsilon}k,A)\simeq\mathrm{Ext}_{B}^{s}({}_{\varepsilon}k,B)\otimes\mathrm{Ext}_{H}^{r}({}_{\varepsilon}k,H)\neq 0.

Hence, we have
\mathrm{cd}(A)=r+s
. We have
r\leq\mathrm{cd}(H)
and
s\leq\mathrm{cd}(B)
, and
\mathrm{cd}(A)=\mathrm{cd}(B)+\mathrm{cd}(H)
by Theorem
5.5
, hence
r=\mathrm{cd}(H)
and

. We conclude from Theorem
2.18
that
H
and
B
have homological duality.
∎

We conclude this section with the case of twisted Calabi-Yau Hopf algebras.

Corollary 5.8
.

Let
k\longrightarrow B\overset{i}{\longrightarrow}A\overset{p}{\longrightarrow}H\longrightarrow k
be an exact sequence of Hopf algebras with bijective antipodes and such that
A
is faithfully flat as a left and right
B
-module. Assume that
H
and
B
are twisted Calabi-Yau. Then
A
is twisted Calabi-Yau in dimension
\mathrm{cd}(B)+\mathrm{cd}(H)
.

Proof.

We already know Theorem
1.1
that
A
has homological duality in dimension
\mathrm{cd}(B)+\mathrm{cd}(H)
. Moreover we know fom the proof of Theorem
1.1
that

\mathrm{Ext}^{\mathrm{cd}(A)}_{A}({}_{\varepsilon}k,A)\simeq\mathrm{Ext}^{\mathrm{cd}(H)}_{H}({}_{\varepsilon}k,H)\otimes\mathrm{Ext}^{\mathrm{cd}(B)}_{B}({}_{\varepsilon}k,B).

By Proposition
2.20
,
\mathrm{Ext}^{\mathrm{cd}(H)}_{H}({}_{\varepsilon}k,H)
and
\mathrm{Ext}^{\mathrm{cd}(B)}_{B}({}_{\varepsilon}k,B)
are one-dimensional, hence
\mathrm{Ext}^{\mathrm{cd}(A)}_{A}({}_{\varepsilon}k,A)
is one-dimensional and this concludes the proof, again using Proposition
2.20
.
∎

6.1
The universal pointed Hopf algebra coacting on
k[x,y]

First consider the algebra
H_{1}
presented by generators
x,g,g^{-1}
and relations
gg^{-1}=g^{-1}g=1
, endowed with the Hopf algebra structure

\Delta(x)=1\otimes x+x\otimes g,\quad\Delta(g)=g\otimes g,\quad\varepsilon(x)=0,\quad\varepsilon(g)=1,\quad S(g)=g^{-1},\quad S(x)=-xg^{-1}.

Proposition 6.1
.

The Hopf algebra
H_{1}
has homological duality in dimension
1
.

Proof.

We have
H_{1}^{+}=(g-1)H_{1}\oplus xH_{1}
, hence
H_{1}^{+}
is a finitely generated free
H_{1}
-module and
0\to H_{1}^{+}\to H_{1}\to k\to 0

is a finitely generated free resolution of
{}_{\varepsilon}k
. Hence by Theorem
2.16
the Hopf algebra
H_{1}
is smooth, and since
moreover it is clear that
\mathrm{Ext}^{0}({}_{\varepsilon}k,H_{1})\simeq\mathrm{Hom}_{H_{1}}({}_{\varepsilon}k,H_{1})=0
, Theorem
2.18
gives the result.
∎

We now define
H_{2}
to be the algebra presented by generators
u_{11},u_{12},u_{22}
with
u_{11}
central in
H_{2}
and
u_{11}
and
u_{22}
invertible and we endow it with the Hopf algebra structure

\Delta(u_{11})=u_{11}\otimes u_{11},\quad\Delta(u_{12})=u_{11}\otimes u_{12}+u_{12}\otimes u_{22},\quad\Delta(u_{22})=u_{22}\otimes u_{22},\quad\varepsilon(u_{ij})=\delta_{ij}.

The Hopf algebra

is pointed since it is generated by group-like and skew-primitive elements.

There has been recently some interest on coactions of pointed algebras on commutative algebras, see
[
20
,
10
]
.
In this setting, the motivation for introducing the above Hopf algebra

is that it is in some sense a universal pointed Hopf algebra coacting on

in a grading preserving way, as follows.

Proposition 6.2
.

There is a grading preserving
H_{2}
-comodule algebra structure on
k[x,y]
defined by

\displaystyle\rho:k[x,y]\

\displaystyle\longrightarrow k[x,y]\otimes H_{2}

\displaystyle x,\ y

\displaystyle\longmapsto x\otimes u_{11},\ x\otimes u_{12}+y\otimes u_{22}

If
A
is a pointed Hopf algebra coacting on
k[x,y]
in a grading preserving way,
then there exist an
H_{2}
-comodule algebra structure given by a coaction
\rho^{\prime}:k[x,y]\to k[x,y]\otimes H_{2}
, and a Hopf algebra map

making the following diagram commute

\textstyle{k[x,y]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\rho^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\theta}
\textstyle{k[x,y]\otimes H_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{{\rm id}\otimes f}
\textstyle{k[x,y]\otimes A}

where
\theta
denotes the given coaction of

.

Proof.

The existence of the announced algebra map

follows from the centrality of
u_{11}
, and it is clear that
\rho
defines a grading preserving right

-comodule structure on
k[x,y]
.

Now assume given a pointed Hopf algebra

coacting on

in a grading preserving way. Then
V=kx\oplus ky
is a subcomodule, and since

is pointed, has a one-dimensional subcomodule.
Hence there exists a basis

of

(with

) such that
\theta(x^{\prime})=x^{\prime}\otimes g
and
\theta(y^{\prime})=x^{\prime}\otimes z+y^{\prime}\otimes h
for some

. Since

is an algebra map and
\{x^{\prime 2},x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\}
is a linearly independent set, we see that

in the same way as

∎

We now prove that

Proposition 6.3
.

The Hopf algebra

Consider the sequence

6.2
Quantum groups of
GL(2)
representation type

In this subsection, we use Corollary
5.8
to show that the Hopf algebras
\mathcal{G}(E,E^{-1})
introduced in
[

are twisted Calabi-Yau in dimension

. This was known from
[

]
, but the argument given here, based on a appropriate exact sequence, is shorter.

Let
n\geq 2
and let
A,B\in GL_{n}(k)
. Consider the universal algebra
\mathcal{G}(A,B)
with generators
(x_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
,
d,d^{-1}
satisfying the relations

x^{t}Ax=Ad,\quad xBx^{t}=Bd,\quad dd^{-1}=1=d^{-1}d

where
x
is the matrix
(x_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
.
It can be endowed with the following Hopf algebra structure

\Delta(x_{ij})=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}x_{ik}\otimes x_{kj},\quad\Delta(d^{\pm})=d^{\pm}\otimes d^{\pm},

\varepsilon(x_{ij})=\delta_{ij},\quad\varepsilon(d^{\pm})=1,\quad S(x)=d^{-1}A^{-1}x^{t}A,\quad S(d^{\pm})=d^{\mp}.

For

and

Proposition 6.4
.

Let

and

. The Hopf algebra
\mathcal{G}(E,E^{-1})
is a twisted Calabi-Yau algebra of dimension

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{B}(E)
(
[
14
]
) be the Hopf algebra presented by generators
(u_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
and relations

E^{-1}u^{t}Eu=I_{n}=uE^{-1}u^{t}E.

We consider the sequence of Hopf algebra maps

where

\mathcal{G}(E,E^{-1})\iota(k\mathbb{Z})^{+}

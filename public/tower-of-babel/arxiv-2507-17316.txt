Introduction

We address the problem of discrete distribution estimation under Kullback-Leibler (KL)
divergence. Specifically, we consider a setting where we have access to
n
i.i.d. samples
of the random variable
X\in[K]=\{1,\ldots,K\}
, where
\Pp(X=i)=p^{\star}(i)
. Our goal is to output an estimated distribution
\widehat{p}\in\triangle^{K}=\{p\geq 0:\sum_{i=1}^{K}p(i)=1\}
such that the KL divergence between
p^{\star}
and
\widehat{p}
is small with high probability. Equivalently, we aim to control the excess risk for the log loss with high probability. More formally, we are looking for the smallest
\varepsilon
such that, with probability at least
1-\delta
,

\displaystyle\KL(p^{\star}\|\widehat{p})=\sum_{i=1}^{K}p^{\star}(i)\ln\left(\frac{p^{\star}(i)}{\widehat{p}(i)}\right)\leq\varepsilon\,,

where we use the convention that
0\ln(0)=0
and
\ln
denotes the natural logarithm. KL divergence is a widely used notion of similarity between two probability distributions. The KL divergence is asymmetric and unbounded, particularly when
\widehat{p}(i)
is close to zero. Controlling this unbounded behavior is one of the key challenges in minimizing the KL divergence.

Discrete distribution estimation is a fundamental problem in statistics. While we measure the accuracy of
\widehat{p}
in terms of
\KL(p^{\star}\|\widehat{p})
, the accuracy can also be measured in other divergences. Typical divergences include the Hellinger distance
H(p^{\star}\|\widehat{p})=\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{K}(\sqrt{p^{\star}(i)}-\sqrt{\widehat{p}(i)})^{2}}
, the
\chi^{2}-
divergences
\chi^{2}(\widehat{p}\|p^{\star})=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\frac{(p^{\star}(i)-\widehat{p})^{2}}{p^{\star}(i)}
and
\chi^{2}(p^{\star}\|\widehat{p})=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\frac{(p^{\star}(i)-\widehat{p})^{2}}{\widehat{p}}
,
\ell_{r}
distances
\ell_{r}(p^{\star}\|\widehat{p})=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{K}(p^{\star}(i)-\widehat{p}(i))^{r}\right)^{1/r}
for
r\geq 1
, which includes the Total Variation (TV) distance as a special case for
r=1
, and also reverse
\KL(\widehat{p}\|p^{\star})
divergence.
Devroye and Lugosi 2001
;
Diakonikolas 2016
;
Canonne 2020
provide an overview and further references on discrete distribution estimation. We also provide new results on discrete distribution estimation in
\chi^{2}
-divergence, but our main contributions concern
\KL(p^{\star}\|\widehat{p})
.

The most commonly used estimator is the Maximum Likelihood Estimator (MLE), which is defined by
\bar{p}_{n}(i)=\frac{n_{i,n}}{n}
,
where
n_{i,n}=\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathbbm{1}[X_{t}=i]
is the number of occurrences of
i
in
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
.
While using
\widehat{p}=\bar{p}
is known to be the minimax optimal estimator for
\KL(\widehat{p}\|p^{\star})

(
Agrawal 2022
)
, the TV distance
(
Canonne 2020
)
, and the Hellinger distance
(
Canonne 2020
)
, the MLE does not appear to be a great candidate for controlling
\KL(p^{\star}\|\cdot)
.
Since the ratio
\frac{p^{\star}(i)}{\bar{p}(i)}
is potentially unbounded, the MLE cannot guarantee that
\KL(p^{\star}\|\bar{p})
is finite.

Another common estimator is the add-
\gamma
estimator, which is defined as
p^{\gamma}(i)=\frac{n_{i,n}+\gamma}{n+\gamma K}
.
For
\gamma>0
, it can be seen that this estimator avoids the
problematic case where the ratio
\frac{p^{\star}(i)}{{p}^{\gamma}(i)}
becomes
unbounded. For
\gamma=\tfrac{1}{2}
, it is known as the Krichevsky-Trofimov
estimator
(
Krichevsky and Trofimov 1981
)
, and for
\gamma=1
it is
commonly referred to as the Laplace estimator. Recent work by

Canonne et al. 2023
provides a concentration bound for

\KL(p^{\star}\|p^{1})
and shows that, for
n\geq K
, the Laplace estimator guarantees that, for some
C>0
, with probability at least
1-\delta

\displaystyle\KL(p^{\star}\|p^{1})\leq\E[\KL(p^{\star}\|p^{1})]+C\frac{\sqrt{K\ln(K/\delta)^{5}}}{n}\,.

Since it has been shown that
\E[\KL(p^{\star}\|p^{1})]\leq\frac{K}{n}
(see,
e.g.
(
Paninski 2003
, section 4)
), this implies that with
probability at least
1-\delta
,
\KL(p^{\star}\|p^{1})=O(\tfrac{1}{n}(K+\sqrt{K\ln(K/\delta)^{5}}))
. In prior work,

Bhattacharyya et al. 2021
show that, with probability at least
1-\delta
,
\KL(p^{\star}\|p^{1})\leq\E[\KL(p^{\star}\|p^{1})]+C\frac{K\ln(n)\ln(1/\delta)}{n}
and
Han et al. 2021
show that, with probability at least
1-\delta
,
\KL(p^{\star}\|p^{1})\leq C\frac{K+\sqrt{K\ln(K/\delta)^{6}}}{n}
.

Canonne et al. 2023
also show that their result cannot be significantly improved for all
\delta
: there exist no constants
\eta>0
,
C>0
such that for all
\delta\in(0,1)
and
n\geq 10K
, the Laplace estimator satisfies with probability at least
1-\delta

\displaystyle\KL(p^{\star}\|p^{1})\leq\E[\KL(p^{\star}\|p^{1})]+C\frac{K^{1/2-\eta}\ln(1/\delta)}{n}.

(1)

An alternative estimator based on Online-to-Batch conversion (OTB)
(
Littlestone 1989
)
is proposed by
Van der Hoeven et al. 2023
. This estimator guarantees that, for some
C>0
, with probability at least
1-\delta

\displaystyle\KL(p^{\star}\|\widehat{p})\leq C\frac{K+\ln(n)\ln(1/\delta)}{n}\,.

However, the presence of the
\ln(n)
term seems unnecessary, and it remains unclear whether this estimator is efficiently computable.

Our Results

We provide both lower and upper bounds for discrete distribution estimation in KL divergence. We first present our main lower bound.

Theorem 1
.

For
n>K^{2}
,
n>\tfrac{4}{3}\ln(1/\delta)
, some
c>0
, and all
\delta\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2})

\displaystyle\inf_{\widehat{p}\in\triangle^{K}}\sup_{p^{\star}\in\triangle^{K}}P^{*}\left(\KL(p^{\star}\|\widehat{p})\geq c\frac{\max\{K,\ln(K)\ln(1/\delta)\}}{n}\right)>\delta\,.

Theorem
1
is implied by Theorems
7
and
10
in Section
2
. Furthermore, in Section
2.1
, we prove that,
surprisingly, the Laplace estimator, the KT estimator, and any other add
constant estimator, cannot achieve the lower bound in
Theorem
1
. Note that this is not an immediate
consequence of (
1
), as this tells us how
\KL(p^{\star}\|p^{1})-\E[\KL(p^{\star}\|p^{1})]
concentrates rather than how well
\KL(p^{\star}\|p^{1})
can be controlled.

In Section
3.2
we provide an estimator based on OTB conversion
and suffix averaging
(
Rakhlin et al. 2012
;
Harvey et al. 2019
;
Aden-Ali et al. 2023
)
. We denote this estimator by
p^{\textnormal{OTB}}
. We show that

p^{\textnormal{OTB}}
is both nearly optimal and efficiently computable. Let
n_{i,n/2}=\sum_{t=n/2}^{n}\mathbbm{1}[X_{t}=i]
,
\mathcal{J}=\{i:n_{i,n/2}<32\ln(K/\delta)\}
,
J=\max\{3,|\mathcal{J}|\}
,
\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}=\{i:np^{\star}(i)<32\ln(K/\delta)\}
, and
\widetilde{J}=\max\{3,|\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}|\}
. Our main upper bound is the following.

Theorem 2
.

Suppose that
n\geq\ln(K/\delta)
and
n\geq K
. Then with probability at least
1-\delta

\displaystyle\KL(p^{\star}\|p^{\textnormal{OTB}})\leq C\frac{K+\widetilde{J}\ln(\ln(\widetilde{J}))+\ln(J)\ln(1/\delta)}{n}\,.

In the worst case,
\widetilde{J}=J=K
and the upper bound presented in
Theorem
2
nearly matches the lower bound in
Theorem
1
. However, when
J\ll K
we see that our
estimator actually guarantees something better. The reason is as
follows. As mentioned, one of the core challenges is the fact that the
KL divergence is potentially unbounded due to the ratio

\frac{p^{+}(i)}{p^{\textnormal{OTB}}(i)}
. If
|\mathcal{J}|
is big it means
there are many categories with a small amount of observations. If that is the case, then the ratios
\frac{p^{+}(i)}{p^{\textnormal{OTB}}(i)}
are more difficult to control, which requires us to inject some bias into
p^{\textnormal{OTB}}
. On the other hand, if most categories have sufficient observations, then we require less bias to control
\frac{p^{+}(i)}{p^{\textnormal{OTB}}(i)}
.
In fact, we show that in some cases we can simply use the unbiased MLE for the KL divergence as well as several other divergences.

Theorem 3
.

For some
C>0
, if
|\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}|=0
, then with probability at least
1-\delta

\displaystyle\KL(p^{\star}\|\bar{p}_{n})\leq C\frac{K+\ln(1/\delta)}{n}

and

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{6}\chi^{2}(p^{\star}\|\bar{p}_{n})\leq\tfrac{1}{4}\chi^{2}(\bar{p}_{n}\|p^{\star})\leq\KL(p^{\star}\|\bar{p}_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{5}{2}H^{2}(p^{\star}\|\bar{p}_{n})\leq\tfrac{5}{2}\KL(\bar{p}_{n}\|p^{\star})\leq\tfrac{5}{2}\chi^{2}(\bar{p}_{n}\|p^{\star})\leq 5\chi^{2}(p^{\star}\|\bar{p}_{n})\,.

While
\KL(p^{\star}\|\bar{p}_{n})\leq CH^{2}(p^{\star}\|\bar{p}_{n})\leq C\KL(\bar{p}_{n}\|p^{\star})\leq C\chi^{2}(\bar{p}_{n}\|p^{\star})
were known
(
Yang and Barron 1998
;
Gibbs and Su 2002
)
, the other inequalities are new.

Finally, we also analyze a more refined version of the add-
\gamma
estimator. This estimator is defined as
\widetilde{p}^{\gamma}(i)=\frac{n_{i,n}+\gamma_{i}}{n+\sum_{i=1}^{K}\gamma_{i}}

and guarantees that with a suitable choice of
\gamma_{i}
that depends on
\delta
, with probability at least
1-\delta

\displaystyle\KL(p^{\star}\|\widetilde{p}^{\gamma})\leq C(1+\ln(\min\{J,\ln(K/\delta)\}))\frac{K+\ln(1/\delta)}{n}\,.

A detailed statement can be found in Section
3.1
.

Contemporary work

In independent and contemporary work
Mourtada 2025
derives matching lower and upper bounds (up to constants) for the same setting we consider. This work shows that in the worst case the
(K+\ln(K)\ln(1/\delta))/n
lower bound is tight.
Mourtada 2025
also provides results for
p^{\star}
with sparse support, which are complementary to our results.

Further Related Work

A related, but different goal is to minimize
\E[\KL(p^{\star}\|\widehat{p})]
. The optimal bound (including constants) is
\frac{K-1}{2n}
and was proven by
Braess and Sauer 2004
.
Catoni 1997
;
Mourtada and Gaïffas 2022
show that the Laplace estimator guarantees the slightly weaker
\frac{K-1}{n}
bound. For
K=2
, similar bounds are provided in
Forster and Warmuth 2002
.

Another related goal is to minimize the regret in a sequential setup. In this setting we work with the logarithmic loss over
n
rounds. For
K=2

Krichevsky and Trofimov 1981
and later generalised by
Xie and Barron 1997
provide an analysis that shows that the KT estimator suffers at most
\frac{K-1}{2}\ln(n)
regret. For a more comprehensive overview we refer to
(
Shtarkov 1987
;
Merhav and Feder 1998
;
Rissanen 1996
)
. Our bounds for
p^{\textnormal{OTB}}
do not directly arise from these analyses, but instead rely on a new analysis of suffix averaging to avoid unnecessary
\ln(n)
factors.

Notation

We denote by
B(n,p)
the binomial distribution
with
n
trials and probability
p
and by
M(n,p(1),\ldots,p(K))

the multinomial distribution with
n
trials and probabilities
p(1),\ldots,p(K)
. We denote by
\{x\}_{+}=\max\{1,x\}
. For
\delta\in(0,1]
, the worst-case rate of an estimator
\widehat{p}
at confidence level

1-\delta
is

r_{n}(\widehat{p},\delta)=\inf\Big\{r_{n}\mid\sup_{p\in\triangle^{K}}P\Big(\KL(p\|\widehat{p})>r_{n}\Big)\leq\delta\Big\}.

(2)

Then
r_{n}^{*}(\delta)=\inf_{\widehat{p}}r_{n}(\widehat{p},\delta)

is the minimax rate.

2
Lower Bounds

In this section we consider lower bounds. We first provide an
estimator-dependent lower bound by direct calculations. Then we
establish the minimax lower bound from Theorem
1
by
breaking it up into two parts: first we prove a lower bound of order

\ln(K)\ln(1/\delta)/n
by building on the intuition developed in the
direct calculations. Because standard techniques based on a
reduction to hypothesis testing are not precise enough to capture the

\ln(K)
factor, this requires a novel reduction to what we call a

weak testing
problem. For the second part, we prove a lower bound
of order
K/n
, for which standard techniques based on Fano’s inequality
suffice after restricting the model to a ball around the uniform
distribution.

2.1
Estimator-dependent Lower Bound

Theorem 4
.

Suppose that
n>\frac{4}{3}\ln(1/\delta)
. Denote by
\widehat{p}^{0}
the
output of estimator
\widehat{p}
when
n_{1,n},\ldots,n_{K-1,n}=0
. For any
\widehat{p}
, we have

\displaystyle\Pp\Big(\KL(p^{\star}\|\widehat{p})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2\ln(1/\delta)}{3n}\left(\ln\left(\frac{2\ln(1/\delta)(K-1)}{3n\sum_{i=1}^{K-1}\widehat{p}^{0}(i)}\right)-1\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{K-1}\widehat{p}^{0}(i)\Big)>\delta\,.

Proof.

We construct a distribution
p^{\star}
relative to what an arbitrary estimator
\widehat{p}
does when
n_{1,n},\ldots,n_{K-1,n}=0
. Define the distribution
p^{\star}
as follows: for some
\alpha>0
, that we will choose later, let
p^{\star}(i^{\prime})=\frac{\alpha}{n}
for
i^{\prime}=\argmin_{i\in[K-1]}\widehat{p}^{0}(i)
,
p^{\star}(i)=0
for
i\in[K-1]\setminus i^{\prime}
, and
p^{\star}(K)=1-\frac{\alpha}{n}
. Suppose that
n\geq 2\alpha
, so the construction is valid. For this choice of
p^{\star}
, the probability of event
\mathcal{E}=\{X_{t}=K
for all
t=1,\dots,n\}
, is bounded from below as:

\displaystyle\Pp(\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{n}\right)^{n}=\exp\left(n\ln\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{n}\right)\right)\geq\exp\left(-\alpha-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{n}\right)

(By the inequality
\ln(1-x)\geq-x-x^{2}
, for all
0<x<\frac{1}{2}
)

\displaystyle>\exp\left(-\frac{3\alpha}{2}\right),

where the last inequality follows from
n>2\alpha
. By choosing
\alpha=\frac{2}{3}\ln(1/\delta)
, we have

\displaystyle\Pp\left(\mathcal{E}\right)>\delta.

Under event
\mathcal{E}
, the KL divergence between
p
and
q
is

\displaystyle\KL(p^{\star}\|\widehat{p})

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{n}\ln\left(\frac{\alpha}{n\widehat{p}^{0}(i^{\prime})}\right)+\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{n}\right)\ln\left(\frac{1-\frac{\alpha}{n}}{1-\sum_{i=1}^{K-1}\widehat{p}^{0}(i)}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\alpha}{n}\ln\left(\frac{\alpha}{n\widehat{p}^{0}(i^{\prime})}\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{K-1}\widehat{p}^{0}(i)-\frac{\alpha}{n}\,,

where the last inequality follows by applying the inequality
\ln(x)\geq 1-1/x
, that holds for all
x>0
, to the second term.
By the definition of
i^{\prime}
, we get
\widehat{p}^{0}(i^{\prime})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{K-1}\frac{\widehat{p}^{0}(i)}{(K-1)}
, which gives us

\displaystyle\KL(p^{\star}\|\widehat{p})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\alpha}{n}\ln\left(\frac{\alpha}{n\widehat{p}^{0}(i^{\prime})}\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{K-1}\widehat{p}^{0}(i)-\frac{\alpha}{n}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\alpha}{n}\ln\left(\frac{\alpha(K-1)}{n\left(\sum_{i=1}^{K-1}\widehat{p}^{0}(i)\right)}\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{K-1}\widehat{p}^{0}(i)-\frac{\alpha}{n}.

Recalling that
\alpha=\frac{2}{3}\ln(1/\delta)
, gives us the following lower bound

\displaystyle\KL(p^{\star}\|\widehat{p})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2\ln(1/\delta)}{3n}\left(\ln\left(\frac{2\ln(1/\delta)(K-1)}{3n\sum_{i=1}^{K-1}\widehat{p}^{0}(i)}\right)-1\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{K-1}\widehat{p}^{0}(i).

This implies that there exists a
p^{\star}
such that for any
\widehat{p}

\displaystyle\Pp\Big(\KL(p^{\star}\|\widehat{p})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2\ln(1/\delta)}{3n}\left(\ln\left(\frac{2\ln(1/\delta)(K-1)}{3n\sum_{i=1}^{K-1}\widehat{p}^{0}(i)}\right)-1\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{K-1}\widehat{p}^{0}(i)\Big)>\delta\,.

∎

Thus, if
\widehat{p}
is an add-
\gamma
estimator, so
\widehat{p}(i)=\frac{n_{i,n}+\gamma}{n+K\gamma}
, we know that there exists a
p^{\star}
such that

\displaystyle\Pp\Big(\KL(p^{\star}\|\widehat{p})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2\ln(1/\delta)}{3n}\left(\ln\left(\frac{2\ln(1/\delta)(n+\gamma K)}{3n\gamma}\right)-1\right)+\frac{\gamma}{n+\gamma K}\Big)>\delta\,.

This immediately disqualifies the MLE (
\gamma=0
) as a good estimator, but also shows that neither the KT
(\gamma=\tfrac{1}{2})
nor the Laplace
(\gamma=1)
estimator are minimax estimators. In fact, the estimator that minimizes the lower bound satisfies
\sum_{i=1}^{K-1}q^{0}(i)=\frac{2\ln(1/\delta)}{3n}
, which means that this estimator needs to know
\delta
.

2.2
Minimax Lower Bound, Part I:
\ln(K)\ln(1/\delta)/n

Based on the insights from the estimator-dependent lower bound, we will
lower bound the minimax rate by restricting the supremum in
(
2
) to a finite set of distributions

P_{2},\ldots,P_{K}\in\triangle^{K}
of the form

P_{j}(i)=\begin{cases}1-\frac{\alpha}{n}&\text{if $i=1$}\\
\frac{\alpha}{n}&\text{if $i=j$}\\
0&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

(3)

where we assume that
\alpha:=\frac{2}{3}\ln(1/\delta)\leq\tfrac{1}{2}n
.

2.2.1
Insufficiency of the Standard Reduction to Hypothesis
Testing

The standard approach to establishing lower bounds on the minimax rate
goes via a general reduction to hypothesis testing
(
Tsybakov 2009
)
.
It is commonly applied to metrics, but may easily be adjusted to KL
divergence as shown in the following lemma. Working directly with KL
divergence is potentially tighter than first lower-bounding KL by a
metric.

Lemma 5
.

Let
P_{1},\ldots,P_{M}
be distributions defined on a sample space

\mathcal{X}
, and let
P_{j}^{n}
be the distribution of
n
independent
samples from
P_{j}
. Given any
\delta\in[0,1]
, suppose that

\displaystyle\forall j\neq k:\quad\KL\Big(P_{j}\|\frac{P_{j}+P_{k}}{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq s_{n},

(4)

\displaystyle\inf_{\Psi}\max_{j}P_{j}^{n}(\Psi\neq j)

\displaystyle>\delta,

(5)

where the infimum is over all possible hypothesis tests
\Psi:\mathcal{X}^{n}\to\{1,\ldots,M\}
. Then

\inf_{\widehat{P}}\max_{j}P_{j}^{n}\Big(\KL(P_{j}\|\widehat{P})\geq s_{n}\Big)>\delta.

In our context the sample space is
\mathcal{X}=[K]
and the conclusion
of the lemma gives a lower bound on the minimax rate of
r_{n}^{*}(\delta)\geq s_{n}
. So what is the best lower bound we can hope for using
Lemma
5
if we apply it to the
distributions in (
3
)? Since

\KL\Big(P_{j}\|\frac{P_{j}+P_{k}}{2}\Big)=\frac{\alpha}{n}\ln\frac{\alpha/n}{\tfrac{1}{2}\alpha/n}=\frac{\alpha\ln(2)}{n}\qquad\text{for any $j\neq k$,}

condition (
4
) requires that
s_{n}\leq\frac{\alpha\ln(2)}{n}=\frac{2\ln(2)}{3}\frac{\ln(1/\delta)}{n}
,
which, as we will show, misses the
\ln K
factor that should be there. Consequently, this
standard reduction is insufficient to recover the right lower bound.

2.2.2
Reduction to Weak Hypothesis Testing

Ordinary hypothesis testing among distributions
P_{1},\ldots,P_{M}
is hard
when there exist two distributions that cannot be properly
distinguished. But the distributions (
3
) are
indistinguishable in a stronger sense: they all assign probability
larger than
\delta
to the same sequence
X_{1}=\cdots=X_{n}=1

consisting of only ones, and as a consequence they are all
indistinguishable simultaneously.

In order to capture this stronger sense of indistinguishability, we
introduce the easier task of
weak hypothesis testing
, where the goal
is not to identify the true distribution among
P_{1},\ldots,P_{M}
but to
identify a set of
M-1
distributions that contains the true
distribution. A weak hypothesis test is defined as a measurable function

\Psi:\mathcal{X}^{n}\to[M]
, which aims to identify the single distribution

P_{\Psi}
that is believed
not
to be the true distribution. In
other words, the test
\Psi
is correct if the set
\{P_{j}|j\neq\Psi\}
contains the true distribution, and makes a mistake if
\Psi=j
when
P_{j}
is true.

Lemma 6
.

Let
P_{1},\ldots,P_{M}
be distributions defined on a sample space

\mathcal{X}
, and let
P_{j}^{n}
be the distribution of
n
independent
samples from
P_{j}
. Given any
\delta\in[0,1]
, suppose that

\inf_{\Psi}\max_{j}P_{j}^{n}(\Psi=j)>\delta,

(6)

where the infimum is over all possible weak hypothesis tests
\Psi:\mathcal{X}^{n}\to\{1,\ldots,M\}
. Then

\inf_{\widehat{P}}\max_{j}P_{j}^{n}\Big(\KL(P_{j}\|\widehat{P})\geq s_{n}\Big)>\delta

for

s_{n}=\min_{P}\max_{j}\KL(P_{j}\|P).

(7)

Condition (
6
) is analogous to
(
5
) in that it lower bounds the worst-case
probability that the (weak) hypothesis test fails. Applying this lemma
to the distributions in (
3
), we obtain the desired
lower bound:

Theorem 7
.

For
K\geq 2
and any
\delta\in(0,1]
, the minimax rate is at least

r_{n}^{*}(\delta)\geq\frac{2\ln(K-1)\ln(1/\delta)}{3n}\qquad\text{for all $n>\frac{4}{3}\ln(1/\delta)$.}

Proof.

Consider the distributions defined in (
3
). For each
of them the probability of the all ones sequence is

P_{j}^{n}(X_{1}=\cdots=X_{n}=1)=(1-\tfrac{\alpha}{n})^{n}>\delta,

where the inequality holds because
\ln(1+z)\geq z-z^{2}
for
z\geq-1/2

(
Cesa-Bianchi et al. 2007
)
, so that

\ln\Big(1-\frac{\alpha}{n}\Big)\geq-\frac{\alpha}{n}-\Big(\frac{\alpha}{n}\Big)^{2}=-\frac{\alpha}{n}\Big(1+\frac{\alpha}{n}\Big)>-\frac{3\alpha}{2n}=\frac{\ln\delta}{n},

where we have used that
n>2\alpha
by assumption.

For an arbitrary weak test
\Psi
, let
j^{*}
be the output of
\Psi

on the all ones sequence. Then condition (
6
)
of Lemma
6
is fulfilled because

P_{j^{*}}^{n}(\Psi=j^{*})\geq P_{j^{*}}^{n}(\{1,\ldots,1\})>\delta.

The lemma therefore tells us that

r_{n}^{*}(\delta)\geq\min_{P}\max_{j}\KL(P_{j}\|P)\geq\min_{P}\frac{1}{K-1}\sum_{j=2}^{K}\KL(P_{j}\|P)=\frac{1}{K-1}\sum_{j=2}^{K}\KL(P_{j}\|\bar{P}),

where
\bar{P}=\frac{1}{K-1}\sum_{j=2}^{K}P_{j}
. Since, for any
j
,

\KL(P_{j}\|\bar{P})=\frac{\alpha}{n}\ln\frac{\alpha/n}{\alpha/n/(K-1)}=\frac{\alpha}{n}\ln(K-1)=\frac{2\ln(K-1)\ln(1/\delta)}{3n},

we conclude that
r_{n}^{*}(\delta)\geq\frac{2\ln(K-1)\ln(1/\delta)}{3n}
, as required.

∎

2.3
Minimax Lower Bound, Part II:
K/n

We now turn to proving a lower bound of order
K/n
, which is the
standard parametric rate for a model with
K-1
free parameters.
Nevertheless, obtaining the right rate is not entirely straightforward,
because it requires identifying a suitable subset
\triangle^{K}_{0}
of
\triangle^{K}

for which KL covering numbers and variational distance packing numbers
can be shown to line up appropriately.

By Pinsker’s inequality

\frac{1}{2}V(P,Q)^{2}\leq\KL(P\|Q)\qquad\text{for any distributions
$P$ and $Q$},

where
V(P,Q)=\sum_{i=1}^{K}|P(\{i\})-Q(\{i\})|
is the total variation
distance, so it is sufficient to prove a lower bound on the minimax rate
for total variation. Since total variation is a metric, we can build on
a result by
Yang and Barron 1999
, which is itself an elegant
application of the standard reduction to hypothesis testing

(
Tsybakov 2009
)
combined with Fano’s inequality to lower bound the
hypothesis testing error. Specialized to our setting, Yang and Barron’s
result gives the following:

For any
\triangle^{K}_{0}\subset\triangle^{K}
and
\epsilon>0
, let

N(\triangle^{K}_{0},\epsilon,\textnormal{KL})
be the Kullback-Leibler

\epsilon^{2}
-entropy of
\triangle^{K}_{0}
1
1

1

That is,
\ln m
for the
smallest
m
for which there exist distributions
P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}
such
that for every
P\in\triangle^{K}_{0}
there exists a
j
for which

\KL(P\|P_{j})\leq\epsilon^{2}
.
, and let
M(\triangle^{K}_{0},\epsilon,V)
be the
variational distance
\epsilon
-packing entropy of

\triangle^{K}_{0}
2
2

2

That is,
\ln m
for the largest
m
such that
there exist
P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}\in\triangle^{K}_{0}
with pairwise distance

V(P_{j},P_{j^{\prime}})>\epsilon
for all
j\neq j^{\prime}
.
.

Theorem 8

(
Yang and Barron 1999
)
.

For any non-increasing, right-continuous bounds
N(\epsilon)\geq N(\triangle^{K}_{0},\epsilon,\textnormal{KL})
and
M(\epsilon)\leq M(\triangle^{K}_{0},\epsilon,V)
, let
\epsilon_{n},\underline{\epsilon}_{n}>0
be such that

\displaystyle\epsilon_{n}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{N(\epsilon_{n})}{n},

\displaystyle M(\underline{\epsilon}_{n})

\displaystyle\geq 4n\epsilon_{n}^{2}+2\ln 2.

Then

\inf_{\widehat{P}}\sup_{P\in\triangle^{K}_{0}}P^{n}\Big(V(P,\widehat{P})\geq\frac{\underline{\epsilon}_{n}}{2}\Big)\geq\frac{1}{2},

where the infimum is over all estimators
\widehat{P}
based on
n

observations.

For the full model
\triangle^{K}_{0}=\triangle^{K}
,
Tang 2022
shows that

N(\triangle^{K},\epsilon,\textnormal{KL})\leq\frac{K-1}{2}\ln\frac{800\ln K}{\epsilon^{2}}
for
\epsilon^{2}\leq\ln K
, but this does not lead to
the right parametric rate
\epsilon_{n}^{2}\approx K/n
. The solution is to
restrict the model to a ball of appropriate radius
\alpha\in\big(0,\frac{1}{2K}\big]
around the uniform distribution with
probabilities
u=(1/K,\ldots,1/K)
:

\triangle^{K}_{0}=\{P\in\triangle^{K}:\|p-u\|_{2}\leq\alpha\},

where
p
is the probability vector
(P(\{1\}),\ldots,P(\{K\}))

corresponding to
P
. This restriction ensures that, for all
P\in\triangle^{K}_{0}
,

\displaystyle\Big(P({i})-\frac{1}{K}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|p-u\|_{2}^{2}\leq\alpha^{2}\leq\Big(\frac{1}{2K}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle P({i})

\displaystyle\in\Big[\frac{1}{2K},\frac{3}{2K}\Big],

so the density ratios between any two
P,Q\in\triangle^{K}_{0}
are uniformly
bounded.
We will end up tuning
\alpha\approx\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}
, so
\triangle^{K}_{0}
is actually shrinking with
n
. We
obtain the following bounds on the covering and packing entropies:

Lemma 9
.

For any
\alpha\in\big(0,\frac{1}{2K}\big]
and
\epsilon>0
,
the covering and packing entropies are bounded by

\displaystyle N(\triangle^{K}_{0},\epsilon,\textnormal{KL})

\displaystyle\leq K\ln\Big(\frac{\alpha 2\sqrt{2K}}{\epsilon}+1\Big),

\displaystyle M(\triangle^{K}_{0},\epsilon,V)

\displaystyle\geq K\ln\frac{\alpha}{\epsilon}+\frac{K}{2}\ln\frac{K\pi}{8}.

Using these in Theorem
8
, we obtain the following
result:

Theorem 10
.

There exists an absolute constant
C>0
such that,
for any
K\geq 2
and
\delta\in(0,1/2)
, the minimax rate is at least

r_{n}^{*}(\delta)\geq\frac{CK}{n}\qquad\text{for all $n\geq\tfrac{\ln 2}{2}K^{2}$.}

The proof shows that
C=4\times 10^{-6}
will work, but presumably this is very
far from tight.

3
Upper Bounds

Here we provide detailed statements of our upper bounds. We start with
definitions we use throughout this section. For simplicity, we assume
that
n/2
is an integer throughout the whole section. Recall that
\mathcal{J}=\{i:n_{i,n/2}<32\ln(K/\delta)\}
and
J=\max\{3,|\mathcal{J}|\}
. Let
p_{t+1}(i)=\frac{n_{i,t}+\gamma_{i}}{t+\sum_{i=1}^{K}\gamma_{i}K}
, where
n_{i,t}=\sum_{s=1}^{t}\mathbbm{1}[X_{s}=i]
and
\gamma_{i}\geq 0
. Let
\bar{p}_{t}(i)=n_{i,t}/t
and
p^{+}(i)=\frac{p^{\star}(i)n+\gamma_{i}}{n+\sum_{i=1}^{K}\gamma_{i}}
. We will refer to
\widetilde{p}^{\gamma}=p_{n+1}
as the
add-
\gamma_{i}
estimator
. Throughout this section we use

\displaystyle\gamma_{i}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0&\textnormal{ ~ if ~}n_{i,n/2}\geq 32\ln(4K/\delta)\\
\max\{1,\frac{\ln(K/\delta)}{J}\}&\textnormal{ ~ otherwise.}\end{cases}

(8)

Note that we set
\gamma_{i}=0
if
n_{i,n/2}
is sufficiently large. This
allows us to avoid unnecessary bias when possible. The reason we can set

\gamma_{i}=0
if
n_{i,n/2}
is sufficiently large is because of the
following lemma:

Lemma 11
.

Suppose that
n\geq 4
. Let
\zeta_{i}=3\left(1+\frac{2\left(3n_{i,n/2}+27\ln(4K/\delta)\right)}{\max\{\gamma_{i},\tfrac{1}{2}n_{i,n/2}-9\ln(4K/\delta)\}}\right)
and
\xi=1+\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{K}\gamma_{i}}{n}
. For all
t\in[n/2+1,\ldots,n+1]
,
i\in[K]
, and
\delta\in(0,1)
, with probability at least
1-\delta
,

\displaystyle\frac{p^{\star}(i)}{p_{t}(i)}\leq\zeta_{i}\xi\,,

\displaystyle\frac{p_{t}(i)}{p^{+}(i)}\leq\zeta_{i}\,,

\displaystyle\frac{p^{+}(i)}{p_{t}(i)}\leq 1+\zeta_{i}\xi\,,

\displaystyle\frac{p^{\star}(i)}{p^{+}(i)}\leq\xi\,,

\displaystyle\frac{n_{i,n}+\gamma_{i}}{n_{i,n/2}+\gamma_{i}}\leq 6+\zeta_{i}\,.

Lemma
11
allows us to control the density ratio between (a close approximation of)
p^{\star}
and our estimators. To see why this is useful, consider the following result:

Lemma 12
.

For any pair
p,q\in\triangle^{K}
, suppose that
\tfrac{1}{2}\leq\frac{p(i)}{q(i)}\leq 2
and
p(i)>0,q(i)>0
for all
i\in[K]
.
Then

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{6}\chi^{2}(p\|q)\leq\tfrac{1}{4}\chi^{2}(q\|p)\leq\KL(p\|q)\leq\tfrac{5}{2}H^{2}(p\|q)\leq\tfrac{5}{2}\KL(q\|p)\leq\tfrac{5}{2}\chi^{2}(q\|p)\leq 5\chi^{2}(p\|q)\,.

The proof of Lemma
12
can be found in
Appendix
C
. Lemma
12

tells us that if the density ratio between two distributions is bounded
by a constant, then several divergence measures are equivalent up to
constants. Now, Lemma
11
tells us that with high
probability, as long as either all
n_{i,n}
are sufficiently big or we add
sufficient bias in the form of
\gamma_{i}
, the density ratio between (a
close approximation of)
p^{\star}
and the add-
\gamma_{i}
estimators
p_{t}
is
bounded. Therefore, if we would have set
\gamma_{i}
sufficiently high,
we could have used Lemma
12
and prior
results for these other diverges to bound the KL between
p^{\star}
and

p_{n+1}
. However, setting
\gamma_{i}
too high introduces too much bias
to obtain minimax rates. Instead, our analysis involves carefully
balancing control of the density ratio and the amount of bias that is
introduced.

We use Lemma
11
in two distinct ways. In Section
3.1
we use it to apply Lemma 4 of
Yang and Barron 1998
, which tells us that for any
q
such that
\max_{i}\frac{p(i)}{q(i)}\leq M
,
\KL(p^{\star}\|q)\leq(2+\ln(M))H^{2}(p^{\star}\|q)
, where
H^{2}
is the squared Hellinger distance. Since standard results allow us to bound the squared Hellinger distance with high probability this means that after carefully choosing
\gamma_{i}
we are essentially done. In Section
3.2
we use Lemma
11
to control
\sum_{i=1}^{K}(p^{\star}(i)-\mathbbm{1}[X_{t}=i])\ln\left(\frac{p^{+}(i)}{p_{t}(i)}\right)
in order to apply a version of Freedman’s inequality to relate
\KL(p^{\star}\|p^{\textnormal{OTB}})
to the regret
\sum_{t=n/2}^{n}\mathbbm{1}[X_{t}=i]\ln\left(\frac{p^{+}(i)}{p_{t}(i)}\right)
, after which we carefully analyze the regret to reach our result.

3.1
Add-
\gamma_{i}
Estimator

In this section we analyze
\KL(p^{\star}\|p^{\star}_{n+1})
, where
p^{\star}_{n+1}(i)=\frac{n_{i,n}+\gamma_{i}}{n+\sum_{i=1}^{K}\gamma_{i}}
and
\gamma_{i}
is specified in equation (
8
).

Theorem 13
.

Suppose that
n\geq K
and
n\geq\ln(K/\delta)
. With probability at least
1-2\delta

\displaystyle\KL(p^{\star}\|p_{n+1})\leq\begin{cases}(2+\ln(40\min\{J,\ln(K/\delta)\}))\Big(\frac{9K+9\ln(1/\delta)}{n}\Big)&\textnormal{ ~ if ~ }|\mathcal{J}|\geq 1\\
(2+\ln(20))\Big(\frac{7K+6\ln(1/\delta)}{n}\Big)&\textnormal{ ~ if ~ }|\mathcal{J}|=0\,.\end{cases}

Our lower bounds suggest that the
\ln(\min\{J,\ln(K)\})K
term is
suboptimal in the worst case. Indeed, in section
3.2
we
provide an improved estimator. Nevertheless, if
|\mathcal{J}|=0
then
Theorem
13
tells us that the MLE is actually a good
estimator. Combined with Lemma
12
this
leads to the proof of Theorem
3
(detailed proof in Appendix
C
).

Proof.

Sketch

We simplify the proof sketch by assuming that
|\mathcal{J}|=K
. Lemma
11
combined with our choice of
\gamma_{i}
tells us that with probability at least
1-\delta
,
\frac{p^{\star}(i)}{p_{n+1}(i)}\leq C\min\{K,\ln(K/\delta)\}
. In turn, this allows us to appeal to Lemma 4 in
(
Yang and Barron 1998
)
to show that

\displaystyle\KL(p^{\star}\|p_{n+1})

\displaystyle\leq(2+\ln(C\min\{K,\ln(K/\delta)\}))H^{2}(p^{\star}\|p_{n+1})

\displaystyle\leq(2+\ln(C\min\{K,\ln(K/\delta)\}))\Big(H^{2}(p^{\star}\|\bar{p}_{n})+\frac{K+\ln(1/\delta)}{n}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq(2+\ln(C\min\{K,\ln(K/\delta)\}))\Big(\KL(\bar{p}_{n}\|p^{\star})+\frac{K+\ln(1/\delta)}{n}\Big)\,.

At this point we can use the results of
(
Agrawal 2022
;
Paninski 2003
)
to conclude that with probability at least
1-\delta
,
KL(\bar{p}_{n}\|p^{\star})\leq Cn^{-1}{(K+\ln(1/\delta))}
.
Combined this finishes the proof.
∎

3.2
Online to Batch

In this section we present our OTB conversion. We combine the standard OTB conversion with suffix averaging to avoid unnecessary
\ln(n)
factors. The estimator is given by
p^{\textnormal{OTB}}=\frac{2}{n}\sum_{t=n/2+1}^{n}p_{t}
.
Informally, like in the analysis of Theorem
13
we relate
\KL(p^{\star}\|p_{n+1})
to
\KL(p_{n+1}\|p^{\star})
, after which we use prior results to control
\KL(p_{n+1}\|p^{\star})
. However, rather than relying on the multiplicative relation such as provided by Lemma 4 in
(
Yang and Barron 1998
)
we develop a relation between
\KL(p^{\star}\|p_{n+1})
to
\KL(p_{n+1}\|p^{\star})
with an additive error through a version of Freedman’s inequality and a careful analysis of the regret.

Theorem 14
.

Suppose that
\ln(K/\delta)\leq n
and that
K\leq n
. With probability at least
1-5\delta
,

\displaystyle\KL(p^{\star}\|p^{\textnormal{OTB}})\leq\frac{1}{n}\Big(128K+200\ln(800J)\ln(1/\delta)+8\widetilde{J}\ln\left(24\left\{\ln\left(\frac{\widetilde{J}}{\ln(1/\delta)}\right)\right\}_{+}\right)\Big)\,.

Proof.

sketch
To simplify the proof sketch, let
\gamma_{i}=\gamma=\max\{1,\frac{\ln(K/\delta)}{K}\}
. Throughout the proof we rely on Lemma
11
, which tells us that for some constant
C
,
|\ln(p^{+}(i)/p_{t}(i))|\leq C\ln(\min\{K,\ln(1/\delta)\})
and
\ln(p^{\star}(i)/p^{+}(i))\leq C\frac{K+\ln(1/\delta)}{n}
. The first step in the proof is to apply Jensen’s inequality:

\displaystyle\KL(p^{\star}\|p^{\textnormal{OTB}})\leq\frac{2}{n}\sum_{t=n/2+1}^{n}\KL(p^{\star}\|p_{t})\,.

At this point we would ideally like to use a version of Freedman’s inequality (Lemma
18
) to bound the difference between
\sum_{t=n/2+1}^{n}\KL(p^{\star}\|p_{t})
and the regret against
p^{\star}
. However, this requires that
|\ln(p^{\star}(i)/p^{+}(i))|
is bounded, which we cannot guarantee. Instead, we proceed with

\displaystyle\frac{2}{n}\sum_{t=n/2+1}^{n}\KL(p^{\star}\|p_{t})\leq\frac{2}{n}\sum_{t=n/2+1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{K}p^{\star}(i)\ln\left(\frac{p^{+}(i)}{p_{t}(i)}\right)+C\frac{K+\ln(1/\delta)}{n}\,.

Since we do have control over
|\ln(p^{+}(i)/p_{t}(i))|
, we can use Lemma
18
to argue that with probability at least
1-\delta

\displaystyle\sum_{t=n/2+1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{K}p^{\star}(i)\ln\left(\frac{p^{+}(i)}{p_{t}(i)}\right)-\sum_{t=n/2+1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathbbm{1}[X_{t}=i]\ln\left(\frac{p^{+}(i)}{p_{t}(i)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sum_{t=n/2+1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{K}p^{\star}(i)\ln\left(\frac{p^{+}(i)}{p_{t}(i)}\right)^{2}}{\ln(\min\{K,\ln(1/\delta)\})}+C\ln(\min\{K,\ln(1/\delta)\})\ln(1/\delta)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=n/2+1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{K}p^{\star}(i)\ln\left(\frac{p^{+}(i)}{p_{t}(i)}\right)+K+C\ln(\min\{K,\ln(1/\delta)\})\ln(1/\delta)\,,

where the last inequality is due to Corollary
16
.
All that is left is to control the regret against
p^{+}(i)
. By standard FTRL analysis we are able to show that

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{T}=\sum_{t=n/2+1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathbbm{1}[X_{t}=i]\ln\left(\frac{p^{+}(i)}{p_{t}(i)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n}{2}\KL(\bar{p}_{n/2}\|p^{\star})+\sum_{i=1}^{K}\gamma\ln\left(\frac{n_{i,n/2}+\gamma}{np^{\star}(i)+\gamma}\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{K}\ln\left(\frac{n_{i,n}+\gamma}{n_{i,n/2}+\gamma}\right)\,.

By results from
Agrawal 2022
;
Paninski 2003
we know that with probability at least
1-\delta

\KL(\bar{p}_{n/2}\|p^{\star})\leq\frac{C}{n}(K+\ln(1/\delta))
. Thus, we only need to control the two remaining sums. If
K<\ln(K/\delta)
then we rely on the fact that
\gamma_{i}=\frac{\ln(K/\delta)}{K}
and Lemma
11
to conclude that
\mathcal{R}_{T}=O(K+\ln(1/\delta)\ln(K))
,
which ultimately leads to the conclusion that with probability at least
1-\delta

\displaystyle\KL(p^{\star}\|p^{\textnormal{OTB}})=O(n^{-1}({K+\ln(1/\delta)\ln(K)}))\,.

If on the other hand
K\geq\ln(K/\delta)
we have that
\gamma=1
and thus

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{K}\gamma\ln\left(\frac{n_{i,n/2}+\gamma}{np^{\star}(i)+\gamma}\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{K}\ln\left(\frac{n_{i,n}+\gamma}{n_{i,n}+\gamma}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\ln\left(\frac{n_{i,n}+1}{np^{\star}(i)+1}\right)\,.

We provide a refined argument that with probability at least
1-\delta

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathbbm{1}[n_{i,n}\geq np^{\star}(i)+\ln(K)(np^{\star}(i)+1)]\leq\ln(1/\delta)\,.

Combined with another appeal to Lemma
11
this leads to the conclusion that with probability at least
1-\delta

\mathcal{R}_{T}=O(K\ln\ln(K)+\ln(1/\delta)\ln(K))
.
Consequently, with probability at least
1-\delta

\displaystyle\KL(p^{\star}\|p^{\textnormal{OTB}})=O\left(n^{-1}({K\ln\ln(K)+\ln(1/\delta)\ln(K)})\right)\,.

To obtain the more detailed statement of Theorem
14
we carefully choose
\gamma_{i}
, which requires a more tedious analysis. We present this analysis in Appendix
C
.
∎

4
Conclusion

We presented nearly minimax rates for discrete distribution estimation in KL divergence with high probability. There is a multiplicative
C\ln\ln(K)
between our upper bounds and lower bounds and we think that this factor is unnecessary. We did not carefully optimize our constants, so there is considerable room for improvement there. Considering the work of
Braess and Sauer 2004
, there is substantial interest in understanding and obtaining the exact minimax rate.

Auxilliary Results

Lemma 15
.

For any distributions
p,q,r
such that
\frac{r(i)}{q(i)}\leq V
for
all
i

\sum_{i=1}^{K}p(i)\Big(\ln\frac{r(i)}{q(i)}\Big)^{2}\leq(2+\ln V)\sum_{i=1}^{K}p(i)\ln\frac{r(i)}{q(i)}+\sum_{i=1}^{K}p(i)\frac{q(i)}{r(i)}-1.

Proof.

We generalize the proof of Lemma 4 by Yang & Barron, 1998: let

\phi(V)=\frac{\ln V+1/V-1}{(\ln V)^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{2+\ln V}
be their decreasing function
\phi_{1}
. Then

\displaystyle\phi(V)\sum_{i=1}^{K}p(i)\Big(\ln\frac{r(i)}{q(i)}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{K}p(i)\phi(\frac{r(i)}{q(i)})\Big(\ln\frac{r(i)}{q(i)}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{K}p\Big(\ln\frac{r(i)}{q(i)}+\frac{q(i)}{r(i)}-1\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{K}p(i)\ln\frac{r(i)}{q(i)}+\sum_{i=1}^{K}p(i)\frac{q(i)}{r(i)}-1.

∎

Applying this with
p=p^{\star}
and
r=p^{+}
we obtain:

Corollary 16
.

For any
q
such that
\ln\frac{p^{+}(i)}{q(i)}\leq\alpha
for all

\sum_{i=1}^{K}p^{\star}(i)\ln\Big(\frac{p^{+}(i)}{q(i)}\Big)^{2}\leq(2+\alpha)\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{K}p^{\star}(i)\ln\left(\frac{p^{+}(i)}{q(i)}\right)+\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{K}\gamma_{i}}{n}\Big).

Proof.

By Lemma
15

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{K}p^{\star}(i)\ln\Big(\frac{p^{+}(i)}{q(i)}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(2+\alpha)\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{K}p^{\star}(i)\ln\left(\frac{p^{+}(i)}{q(i)}\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{K}p^{\star}(i)\frac{q(i)}{p^{+}(i)}-1\Big)

\displaystyle=(2+\alpha)\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{K}p^{\star}(i)\ln\left(\frac{p^{+}(i)}{q(i)}\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{K}q(i)\frac{p^{\star}(i)}{p^{+}(i)}-1\Big)

\displaystyle\leq(2+\alpha)\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{K}p^{\star}(i)\ln\left(\frac{p^{+}(i)}{q(i)}\right)+\frac{n+\sum_{i=1}^{K}\gamma_{i}}{n}-1\Big)

\displaystyle=(2+\alpha)\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{K}p^{\star}(i)\ln\left(\frac{p^{+}(i)}{q(i)}\right)+\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{K}\gamma_{i}}{n}\Big)\,.

∎

Lemma 17
.

If
N_{1},\ldots,N_{K}\sim M(n,p^{\star}(1),\ldots,p^{\star}(K))
and
\widetilde{N}_{i}\sim B(n,\min\{1,3p^{\star}(i)\})

R\subset[K]
such that
\sum_{i\not\in R}p^{\star}(i)\geq 1/3
, any
b_{1},\ldots,b_{K}\geq 0
, and any
z>0

\displaystyle\Pp(\sum_{i\in R}\mathbbm{1}\{N_{i}\geq b_{i}\}\geq z)\leq\Pp(\sum_{i\in R}\mathbbm{1}\{\widetilde{N}_{i}\geq b_{i}\}\geq z)\,.

Proof.

For simplicity we only show the proof for
R=[K-1]
and assume
p(K)\geq\frac{1}{3}
. The general proof follows from the proof for this case. For any
i^{\prime}
and
R^{\prime}=[K-1]\setminus i^{\prime}
a basic property of the multinomial distribution is that (see, e.g.
(
Roussas 2003
, Chapter 4)
)

\displaystyle N_{i^{\prime}}|\{N_{i}:i\in R^{\prime}\}\sim B(n-\sum_{i\in R^{\prime}}N_{i},\frac{p(i^{\prime})}{\sum_{i\not\in R^{\prime}}p(j)})\,.

Thus, since
\sum_{i\not\in R^{\prime}}p(j)>p(K)\geq\frac{1}{3}
, we have that for any
b\geq 0

\displaystyle\Pp(N_{1}\geq b|N_{2},\ldots,N_{K-1})\leq\Pp(\widetilde{N}_{1}\geq b)\,.

(9)

Denote by
Y_{i}=\mathbbm{1}\{N_{i}\geq b_{i}\}
, by
\widetilde{Y}=\mathbbm{1}\{\widetilde{N_{i}}\geq b_{i}\}
, and by

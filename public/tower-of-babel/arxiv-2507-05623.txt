Xi Chen, Jinyan Fan*

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the equality constrained nonlinear least squares problem, where the Jacobian matrices of the objective function and constraints are unavailable or expensive to compute. We approximate the Jacobian matrices via orthogonal spherical smoothing and propose a derivative-free regularization algorithm for solving the problem. At each iteration, a regularized augmented Lagrangian subproblem is solved to obtain a Newton-like step. If a sufficient decrease in the merit function of the approximate KKT system is achieved, the step is accepted, otherwise a derivative-free LM algorithm is applied to get another step to satisfy the sufficient decrease condition. It is shown that the algorithm either finds an approximate KKT point with arbitrary high probability or converges to a stationary point of constraints violation almost surely.

Introduction

Nonlinear least-squares problems have important applications in data fitting, parameter estimation and function approximation. They appear frequently in nuclear physics, aircraft design, aeronautical engineering and so on, where the functions themselves may not be in an analytical closed form, permitting function evaluations but not gradient assessments.
In this paper, we consider the equality constrained nonlinear least squares problem

\displaystyle\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\ \

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|r(x)\|^{2},

s.t.

\displaystyle c(x)=0,

(1.1)

where
r(x):\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{p}
,
c(x):\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
are continuously differentiable but explicit expressions of their derivatives are difficult to compute or infeasible
to obtain, and
\|\cdot\|
refers to the standard Euclidian norm. Hence traditional nonlinear least-squares methods that use derivative information are not applicable
anymore.

It is worth noting that derivative-free methods had been studied by the optimization community long before. They can be classified into the categories: direct search-based methods, model-based methods and the others.
Direct search methods include the Nelder–Mead simplex method
[
28
]
, coordinate search method
[
14
]
, pattern search method
[
40
]
, mesh adaptive direct search
[
3
,
4
,
2
]
, etc.
Gratton et al.
[
18
]
propose a direct search method based on probabilistic feasible descent for bound and linearly constrained problems.

Powell developed derivative-free trust-region methods UOBYQA and NEWUOA for unconstrained optimization
[
32
,
33
]
,
and BOBYQA, LINCOA and COBYLA for bound constrained, linear constrained and general nonlinear constrained optimization, respectively
[
34
,
35
,
31
]
,
where BOBYQA and LINCOA build quadratic polynomial models by the derivative-free symmetric Broyden update, and COBYLA constructs linear polynomial models to approximate the objective function and constraints.
Tröltzsch
[
41
]
presented a sequential quadratic programming algorithm for equality constrained optimization without derivatives
[
41
]
.
Sampaio
[
37
]
gave the algorithm DEFT-FUNNEL for general constrained grey-box and black-box problems that belongs to the class of trust-region sequential quadratic optimization algorithms and uses the polynomial interpolation models as surrogates for the black-box functions.
Interested readers are referred to
[
1
,
10
,
23
]
for more model-based derivative-free optimization methods.

Derivative-free methods that use finite differences or smoothing technique to compute estimates of gradients
also attract increasing attention in recent years
[
17
,
5
,
24
]
.
For example, Ge et al.
[
16
]
proposed the derivative-free solver SOLNP+ for constrained nonlinear optimization which uses finite difference to approximate the gradients and incorporates the techniques of implicit filtering, new restart mechanism and modern quadratic programming.
Flaxman et al.
[
15
]
used spherical smoothing to approximate the gradients
and Nesterov et al.
[
29
]
designed a kind of random gradient-free oracle by Gaussian smoothing.
Kozak et al.
[
21
]
approximated the exact gradient by finite differences computed in a set of orthogonal random directions that changes with each iteration.

For unconstrained nonlinear least squares problems,
Zhang et al.
[
42
]
built quadratic interpolation models for each
r_{i}
and gave a framework for a class of derivative-free algorithms within a trust region.
Cartis et al.
[
7
]
constructed linear interpolation models of all
r_{i}
and presented a derivative-free version of the Gauss-Newton method, further linear regression models were used to improve the flexibility and robustness.
Zhao et al.
[
43
]
built the probabilistically first-order accurate Jacobian models with random points generated by the standard Gaussian distribution and gave a derivative-free Levenberg-Marquardt (LM) algorithm.
Brown et al.
[
6
]
estimated the Jacobian matrices by coordinate-wise finite difference and gave the derivative-free analogues of the LM algorithm and Gauss-Newton algorithm.
Recently, the authors
[
8
]
proposed a derivative-free LM algorithm that approximates the Jacobian matrices via orthogonal spherical smoothing; it is shown that the gradient models with such Jacobians are probabilistically first-order accurate and the algorithm converges globally almost surely.

For constrained nonlinear least squares problems,
Hough et al.
[
20
]
presented a model-based derivative-free method for optimization subject to unrelaxable convex constraints, where the constraint set is available to the algorithm through a projection operator that is inexpensive to compute.
However, to the best of our knowledge, there are no existing derivative-free methods designed for general constrained nonlinear least squares problems.

In this paper, we propose a derivative-free regularization algorithm for equality constrained nonlinear least squares problem (
1
), where the Jacobian matrices of the objective function and constraints are unavailable or expensive to compute.
We estimate the Jacobian matrices via orthogonal spherical smoothing.
Based on the framework of the augmented Lagrangian method,
a regularization subproblem is solved at each iteration to obtain a Newton-like step.
If a sufficient decrease in the merit function of the approximate KKT system is achieved,
the step is accepted, otherwise an improved derivative-free LM algorithm presented in
[
8
]
is used to get a step to satisfy the sufficient decrease condition.
It is shown that the algorithm either finds an approximate KKT point with high probability
or converges to a stationary point of the constraints almost surely.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we approximate the Jacobian matrices via orthogonal spherical smoothing
and propose a derivative-free regularization algorithm for (
1
).
The properties and global convergence of the algorithm are studied in Section
3
.
Finally, some numerical experiments are presented in Section
4
.

2.
A derivative-free regularization algorithm for (
1
)

In this section, we approximate the Jacobian matrices via orthogonal spherical smoothing, then propose a derivative-free regularization for solving the equality constrained nonlinear least squares problem (
1
).

2.1.
Approximating Jacobian matrices via orthogonal spherical smoothing

Define the spherical smoothed version of
r_{i}(x),i=1,\ldots,p
as

\tilde{r}_{i}(x)=\mathbb{E}_{v\sim U(\mathbb{B})}[r_{i}(x+\gamma v)],

(2.1)

where
\mathbb{B}
represents the unit ball in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
U(\mathbb{B})
the uniform distribution on
\mathbb{B}
,
\gamma
is the smoothing parameter.
It then follows from
[
15
]
that

\nabla\tilde{r}_{i}(x)=\frac{n}{\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{u\sim U(\mathbb{S})}[r_{i}(x+\gamma u)u],

(2.2)

where
\mathbb{S}
represents the unit sphere in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
U(\mathbb{S})
the uniform distribution on
\mathbb{S}
.

We take

\displaystyle\nabla r_{s,i}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{r_{i}(x+\gamma u_{j})-r_{i}(x)}{\gamma}u_{j}

(2.3)

as a gradient approximation of
r_{i}(x)
,
where
[u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}]
is uniformly sampled from the Stiefel manifold
St(n)=\{U\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}:U^{T}U=I_{n}\}
, and can be obtained by using the Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization on
n
independent random vectors with identical distribution
\mathcal{N}(0,I)
(cf.
[
13
,
9
]
).
Since the distribution for such
u_{j}
is uniform over
\mathbb{S}
(cf.
[
21
]
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{u}[\nabla r_{s,i}(x)]=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{u}\left[\frac{r_{i}(x+\gamma u_{j})-r_{i}(x)}{\gamma}u_{j}\right]=\nabla\tilde{r}_{i}(x).

(2.4)

We take

\displaystyle J^{r}_{s}(x)=\left(\nabla r_{s,1}(x),\ldots,\nabla r_{s,p}(x)\right)^{T}

(2.5)

as the approximate Jacobian matrix of
r(x)
and

\displaystyle J^{c}_{s}(x)=\left(\nabla c_{s,1}(x),\ldots,\nabla c_{s,m}(x)\right)^{T},

(2.6)

as the approximate Jacobian matrix of
c(x)
, where
\nabla c_{s,i}(x)
is computed as (
2.3
).

2.2.
A derivative-free regularization method for solving (
1
)

The method to be proposed is based on the framework of the augmented Lagrangian method. The Lagrangian function for (
1
) is

\displaystyle L(x,y)=\frac{1}{2}\|r(x)\|^{2}-y^{T}c(x),

(2.7)

where
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is the Lagrange multiplier.
At the
k
-th iteration, fixing the Lagrange multiplier
y_{k}
and the penalty parameter
\delta_{k}^{-1}
, the augmented Lagrangian method solves the subproblem

\displaystyle\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\ \frac{1}{2}\|r(x)\|^{2}-y_{k}^{T}c(x)+\frac{1}{2}\delta_{k}^{-1}\|c(x)\|^{2},

(2.8)

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\min_{(x,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+m}}\quad

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|r(x)\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\delta_{k}\|v+y_{k}\|^{2}

s.t.

\displaystyle c(x)+\delta_{k}v=0.

(2.9)

In
[
30
]
, Orban et al. apply the regularization scheme and introduce
z=r(x)

to avoid the occurrence of
J^{r}(x)^{T}J^{r}(x)
, where
J^{r}(x)
is the Jacobian matrix of
r(x)
,
which typically contributes to dense and ill conditioned subproblems.
In this paper, we follow their idea and solve the subproblem:

\displaystyle\min_{(x,v,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+m+p}}\quad

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|z\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\rho_{k}\|x-x_{k}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\delta_{k}\|v+y_{k}\|^{2}

s.t.

\displaystyle r(x)-z=0,

\displaystyle c(x)+\delta_{k}v=0,

(2.10)

where
\rho_{k}
is a regularization parameter.

Note that the Jacobian matrices of
r(x)
and
c(x)
considered in this paper
are unavailable or expensive to compute.
We define the approximate KKT conditions for (
2.2
) by replacing the exact Jacobian matrices with approximate ones:

\displaystyle\rho_{k}(x-x_{k})-J_{s}^{r}(x)^{T}s-J_{s}^{c}(x)^{T}y

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle z+s

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\delta_{k}(v+y_{k})-\delta_{k}y

\displaystyle=0,

(2.11)

\displaystyle r(x)-z

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle c(x)+\delta_{k}v

\displaystyle=0,

where
J^{r}_{s}(x)
and
J^{c}_{s}(x)
are approximate Jacobian matrices of
r(x)
and
c(x)
respectively,
s
and
y
are Lagrange multipliers.
Since
s=-z
and
v=y-y_{k}
, (
2.2
) becomes

\displaystyle\rho_{k}(x-x_{k})+J_{s}^{r}(x)^{T}z-J_{s}^{c}(x)^{T}y

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle r(x)-z

\displaystyle=0,

(2.12)

\displaystyle c(x)+\delta_{k}(y-y_{k})

\displaystyle=0.

Denote
w:=(x,z,y)
,
w_{k}:=(x_{k},z_{k},y_{k})
, and define

\displaystyle F_{s_{k}}(w;x_{k},y_{k},\rho_{k},\delta_{k}):=\begin{bmatrix}\rho_{k}(x-x_{k})+J^{r}_{s_{k}}(x)^{T}z-J^{c}_{s_{k}}(x)^{T}y\\
r(x)-z\\
c(x)+\delta_{k}(y-y_{k})\end{bmatrix},

(2.13)

where
J^{r}_{s_{k}}(x)
and
J^{c}_{s_{k}}(x)
are defined by (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) with
\gamma_{k}
.
One can apply Newton-like method to

\displaystyle F_{s_{k}}(w;x_{k},y_{k},\rho_{k},\delta_{k})=0

(2.14)

sequentially to find an approximate KKT point.
Let
T_{s_{k}}
be an approximation of the Jacobian matrix of
F_{s_{k}}(w;x_{k},y_{k},\rho_{k},\delta_{k})
at
w_{k}
.
Denote
J^{r}_{s_{k}}:=J^{r}_{s_{k}}(x_{k})
,
J^{c}_{s_{k}}:=J^{c}_{s_{k}}(x_{k})
and
F_{s_{k}}:=F_{s_{k}}(w_{k};x_{k},y_{k},\rho_{k},\delta_{k})
.
Then solving the linear system

\displaystyle T_{s_{k}}d_{w}=-F_{s_{k}}

(2.15)

can give an inexact solution of (
2.14
).
In practice, one can adjust
d_{y}
to
-d_{y}
to make the coefficient matrix of the linear system symmetric so that a symmetric indefinite factorization can be used (cf.
[
11
]
).
That is, an inexact Newton step
d_{w_{k}}=(d_{x_{k}},d_{z_{k}},d_{y_{k}})
is computed by solving the system

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}H_{s_{k}}+\rho_{k}I&(J^{r}_{s_{k}})^{T}&(J^{c}_{s_{k}})^{T}\\
J^{r}_{s_{k}}&-I&0\\
J^{c}_{s_{k}}&0&-\delta_{k}I\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}d_{x}\\
d_{z}\\
-d_{y}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}-(J^{r}_{s_{k}})^{T}z_{k}+(J^{c}_{s_{k}})^{T}y_{k}\\
z_{k}-r(x_{k})\\
-c(x_{k})\end{bmatrix},

(2.16)

where
H_{s_{k}}
is a symmetric approximation of
H(x,z_{k},y_{k})
at
x_{k}
with

\displaystyle H(x,z,y)=\sum_{i=1}^{p}z_{i}\nabla^{2}r_{i}(x)-\sum_{i=1}^{m}y_{i}\nabla^{2}c_{i}(x).

(2.17)

Define the merit function

\displaystyle\|F_{s_{k}}\|_{*}=\|(J^{r}_{s_{k}})^{T}z_{k}-(J^{c}_{s_{k}})^{T}y_{k}\|+\|z_{k}-r(x_{k})\|+\|c(x_{k})\|.

If the trial step
d_{w_{k}}
can lead to a sufficient decrease, i.e.,

\displaystyle\|F_{s_{k+1}}\|_{*}\leq\theta\|F_{s_{k}}\|_{*}+\epsilon_{k},

(2.18)

where
0<\theta<1
,
0<\epsilon_{k}\downarrow 0
,
we accept it and take
w_{k+1}=w_{k}+d_{w_{k}}
.
Otherwise we go to inner iterations to minimize the augmented Lagrangian function so that a trial step can be obtained to satisfy (
2.18
). That is, we solve

\displaystyle\min_{x}\ \phi_{k}(x;\delta)=\frac{1}{2}\|r(x)\|^{2}-y_{k}^{T}c(x)+\frac{1}{2}\delta^{-1}\|c(x)\|^{2},

(2.19)

where
\delta
may be updated in inner iterations.

Actually, (
2.19
) is equivalent to the nonlinear least square problem

\displaystyle\min_{x}\ \frac{1}{2}\|\Phi_{k}(x;\delta)\|^{2}:=\frac{1}{2}\left\|\begin{bmatrix}r(x)\\
\delta^{-1/2}(c(x)-\delta y_{k})\end{bmatrix}\right\|^{2}.

(2.20)

It can be solved by a derivative-free LM method.
Let

\displaystyle J^{\Phi}_{s}(x;\delta)=\begin{bmatrix}J^{r}_{s}(x)\\
\delta^{-1/2}J^{c}_{s}(x)\end{bmatrix}

and

\displaystyle\nabla\phi_{k,s}(x;\delta)=J^{r}_{s}(x)^{T}r(x)-J^{c}_{s}(x)^{T}y_{k}+\delta^{-1}J^{c}_{s}(x)^{T}c(x)

(2.21)

be the approximate Jacobian matrix of
\Phi_{k}(x;\delta)
and approximate gradient of
\phi_{k}(x;\delta)
with respect to
x
, respectively.
At the
j
-th inner iteration of the
k
-th outer iteration, we denote

J_{s_{k,j}}:=J^{\Phi}_{s}(x_{k,j};\delta_{k,j}),\quad\nabla\phi_{s_{k,j}}:=\nabla\phi_{k,s}(x_{k,j};\delta_{k,j})

for brevity, and solve

\displaystyle(J_{s_{k,j}}^{T}J_{s_{k,j}}+\lambda_{k,j}\|\nabla\phi_{s_{k,j}}\|I)d_{x}=-\nabla\phi_{s_{k,j}}

(2.22)

to obtain the step
d_{x_{k,j}}
, where
\lambda_{k,j}
is the parameter updated from iteration to iteration.
We may use two-step QR factorization such as that of
[
39
]
to find the solution of (
2.22
),
which can avoid the calculation of
J_{s_{k,j}}^{T}J_{s_{k,j}}
.
Define the ratio

\displaystyle\zeta_{k,j}=\frac{\|\Phi_{k}(x_{k,j};\delta_{k,j})\|^{2}-\|\Phi_{k}(x_{k,j}+d_{x_{k,j}};\delta_{k,j})\|^{2}}{\|\Phi_{k}(x_{k,j};\delta_{k,j})\|^{2}-\|\Phi_{k}(x_{k,j};\delta_{k,j})+J_{s_{k,j}}d_{x_{k,j}}\|^{2}}.

(2.23)

It plays an important role in deciding whether
d_{x_{k,j}}
is acceptable and how to update
\lambda_{k,j}
.
The parameter
\delta_{k,j}
will be decreased if
the relatively sufficient decrease on
\|\nabla\phi_{k,s}(x;\delta)\|
is achieved
but the constraint violation decrease does not meet the requirement.

3.
Global convergence of the algorithm

In this section, we study the global convergence of Algorithm
1
. It is shown that Algorithm
1
either finds an approximate KKT point with high probability
or converges to a stationary point of the constraints almost surely.

Note that the Jacobian approximations are built in some random fashion,
which implies the randomness of the iterates, the smoothing parameters, etc.
We use uppercase letters to represent random quantities, such as
X_{k}
,
J^{r}_{S_{k}}(X_{k})
, etc.,
and use lowercase letters to represent their realizations, such as
x_{k}
,

J^{r}_{s_{k}}(x_{k})
etc.
Denote by
\mathcal{F}_{k}
the
\sigma
-algebra generated by all randomness in
iteration
k
and all previous iterations.

We first derive the bounds for the difference between the Jacobian matrix and the expectation of its approximation and the variance of the approximate Jacobian, respectively.
We make the following assumption.

Assumption 3.1
.

(i)
J^{r}(x)
and
J^{c}(x)
are Lipchitz continuous, i.e., there exist
\kappa_{ljr},\kappa_{ljc}>0
such that for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\|J^{r}(x)-J^{r}(y)\|

\displaystyle\leq\kappa_{ljr}\|x-y\|,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\|J^{c}(x)-J^{c}(y)\|

\displaystyle\leq\kappa_{ljc}\|x-y\|.

(3.2)

(ii)
r(x)
and
c(x)
are Lipschitz continuous, i.e., there exist
\kappa_{lr},\kappa_{lc}>0
such that for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\|r(x)-r(y)\|

\displaystyle\leq\kappa_{lr}\|x-y\|,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\|c(x)-c(y)\|

\displaystyle\leq\kappa_{lc}\|x-y\|.

(3.4)

(
3.1
) implies that there exists
\kappa_{lgr}>0
such that for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\|\nabla r_{i}(x)-\nabla r_{i}(y)\|\leq\kappa_{lgr}\|x-y\|,\quad i=1,\ldots,p.

(3.5)

(
3.2
) implies that there exists
\kappa_{lgc}>0

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left\|J^{c}_{S_{k,j}}(x_{k,j})-\mathbb{E}_{u}[J^{c}_{S_{k,j}}(x_{k,j})]\right\|\leq s\right)\geq 1-\frac{d^{2}mn\kappa_{lgc}^{2}}{4s^{2}}\gamma_{k,j}^{2}.

(3.33)

By Assumption

(ii) and (

\displaystyle\left\|J^{c}_{S_{k,j}}(x_{k,j})^{T}c(x_{k,j})-J^{c}(x_{k,j})^{T}c(x_{k,j})\right\|\leq\left\|J^{c}_{S_{k,j}}(x_{k,j})-J^{c}(x_{k,j})\right\|\left\|c(x_{k,j})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\kappa_{bc}\left(\left\|J^{c}_{S_{k,j}}(x_{k,j})-\mathbb{E}_{u}[J^{c}_{S_{k,j}}(x_{k,j})]\right\|+d\sqrt{m}\kappa_{lgc}\gamma_{k,j}\right).

Let

Define the event

Then

Define a random walk

(3.35)

Obviously,

(3.36)

If

Combining (

Because

On the other hand, by (

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\liminf_{j\to\infty}\|J^{c}(X_{k,j})^{T}c(X_{k,j})\|=0\mid\mathcal{E}_{2}\right)=1.

Numerical experiments

In this section, we test Algorithm
1
(DFRCNLS) and compare it with
two derivative-free algorithms for constrained optimization problems: COBYLA
(cf.
[
31
]
) using Matlab interface of PDFO
[

The experiments are implemented on a laptop with an AMD Core R7-6800H CPU (3.20GHz) and 32GB of RAM, using Matlab R2022b.

4.1.
Implementation details

We set
\delta_{0}=1,\rho_{0}=0,\epsilon_{0}=10^{3},\gamma_{0}=1,\theta=0.99
in Algorithm
1
,
and
p_{0}=0.001,p_{1}=0.25,p_{2}=0.75,p_{3}=10^{-10},p_{4}=10^{12},\lambda_{\min}=10^{-8}
in Algorithm
2
, respectively.
We take
\delta_{k}=\max\left\{10^{-6},\min\left\{0.1\delta_{k-1},\>\|F_{s_{k}}\|_{*}\right\}\right\}
and

[

The regularization parameter

is updated as that in
[

, Algorithm 5.1]
so that the coefficient matrices of the system (
2.16
) are nonsingular.
The initialization multiplier
y_{0}
is obtained by solving the problem

\displaystyle\min\limits_{y}\>\frac{1}{2}\|(J^{c}_{s_{0}})^{T}y-(J^{r}_{s_{0}})^{T}r(x_{0})\|^{2}.

Algorithm
1
runs up to
K_{1}
iterations and stops at iteration

if

\displaystyle\max\left\{\dfrac{\|(J^{r}_{s_{k}})^{T}r(x_{k})-(J^{c}_{s_{k}})^{T}y_{k}\|_{\infty}}{\max\left\{100,\>\dfrac{\|y_{k}\|_{1}}{m}\right\}\Big/100},\>\|c(x_{k})\|_{\infty}\right\}\leq 10^{-5}.

(4.1)

Algorithm
2

The defaults values of

In the implementation, the approximate Jacobian matrices are computed by (
2.3
)–(

with identical distribution

We can also generate ten orthogonal direction sets, then choose one of them randomly as directions at each iteration; in this case the algorithm is denoted as DFRCNLS-OSSv2.
If we take

Denote by

(4.2)

If a solver

and

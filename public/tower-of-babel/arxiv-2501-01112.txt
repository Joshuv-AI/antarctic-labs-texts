1.
Introduction

An integral focus of research in the area of commutative algebra is the study of monomial ideals, particularly square-free monomial ideals, due to their strong connections with combinatorics and topology. A general objective in this area is to express or translate algebraic properties of a certain class of ideals in terms of the combinatorial or topological properties of the associated objects. There are several ways to associate a combinatorial object with a square-free monomial ideal; the most popular among them are the following two: (a) using the Stanley-Reisner correspondence to associate an abstract simplicial complex, and (b) associating a simple hypergraph (or clutter). Both these identifications have certain advantages and are frequently used to study square-free monomial ideals. Additionally, many algebraic properties of an ideal in a polynomial ring depend on the characteristics of the base field. However, if these come from the structure of the associated combinatorial object, then they are independent of the choice of the base field.

The graded minimal free resolution of a graded module gives insight into its structure and measures its complexity. Determining the minimal free resolution of a graded module is a computationally challenging task. So, researchers try to get some estimation of the minimal free resolution via two important invariants: (i) the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity (or simply, regularity) that measures the width of a minimal free resolution, and (ii) the projective dimension, which gives the length of a minimal free resolution. These two invariants have been extensively investigated for several classes of monomial ideals, more notably in the case of edge ideals of graphs. Indeed, a celebrated theorem of Fröberg
[
13
]
gave an algebraic interpretation of chordal graphs in terms of the linearity of the minimal free resolution of edge ideals. More precisely, the edge ideal
I(G)
of a graph
G
has a linear resolution if and only if the complement of
G
is chordal. On the other hand, when
G
is chordal, a precise combinatorial formula for the regularity and projective dimension of
I(G)
is well-known.

To extend the study to square-free monomial ideals, various generalizations of edge ideals, such as path ideals, clique ideals, etc., have been introduced (see
[
6
,
20
]
). Note that the edge ideal of a graph
G
can be realized as the Stanley-Reisner ideal of a simplicial complex obtained from
G
, often referred to as the independence complex of
G
. In the literature, there is a notion of higher independence complexes of a graph
G
, generalizing the independence complex of
G
. For
t\geq 2
, the
t
-independence complex
of a graph
G
, denoted by
\mathrm{Ind}_{t}(G)
, is the collection of all
A\subseteq V(G)
such that each connected component of
G[A]
has at most
t-1
vertices. The members of
\mathrm{Ind}_{t}(G)
are called
t
-independent sets of
G
. Note that
\mathrm{Ind}_{2}(G)
is nothing but the well-known
independence complex
of
G
. The topological study of the higher independence complexes of graphs turns out to be a fruitful area of research and often produces very interesting results when seen from the perspective of the independence complexes. To motivate the reader, we briefly mention some of these results below.

In 2003, Meshulam
[
19
]
established some beautiful relations between the domination numbers of

and the homology of

, which settled a Hall-type conjecture of Aharoni in the affirmative. Recently, Deshpande-Shukla-Singh
[
10

extended Meshulam’s result by relating the homology groups of

with the distance
t
-domination number of
G
. It is important to note that the distance
t
-domination number is a well-known invariant in graph theory (see
[
27
]
and the references therein). In
[
10
]
, the authors have also shown that

of a chordal graph

is either contractible or homotopy equivalent to a wedge of spheres. Note that for

,
\mathrm{Ind}_{2}(G)
of a chordal graph

is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay, whereas for each

, the complexes

may not be sequentially Cohen-Macaulay (see, for instance,
[
1
, Proposition 4.3]
), which is quite surprising. In
[

, Paolini and Salvetti established a connection between the twisted cohomology of the classical braid groups and the cohomology of higher independence complexes related to the corresponding Coxeter graphs (see also
[
24
]
). The complexes

also appeared in some purely combinatorial contexts, for instance:

\bullet

They appeared in the work of Szabó and Tardos
[
26

, where the authors introduced and discussed generalizations of the problem of independent transversal in graphs.

\bullet

The notion of
t

[
25
,
28

Recently, the Stanley-Reisner ideals of these complexes have been considered in
[
1

2
,
9
]
. In this article, we broaden this study by considering the class of chordal graphs. It turns out that the monomial generators of the Stanley-Reisner ideal of

It is well-known that any square-free monomial ideal can be seen as an edge ideal of a simple hypergraph. Let

be a

denote its edge ideal in a polynomial ring

-gap-free chordal graphs

, for

Let
\mathcal{H}(G,t)
be a
t
-uniform hypergraph induced from a graph
G
such that
\mathcal{H}(G,2)=G
. One of the natural questions in this context is to ask for which classes of such hypergraphs

Note that among different classes of simple graphs, chordal graphs have garnered special attention due to their connections with various branches of mathematics and computer science, as well as the fact that several algebraic invariants of their edge ideals can be expressed in terms of combinatorial invariants of the underlying graphs. Thus, it is worthwhile to first explore the above question in the context of chordal graphs.

In this paper, we investigate the Stanley-Reisner ideals
J_{t}(G)
corresponding to a chordal graph
G
. Specifically, we are interested in knowing whether the regularity and the projective dimension of such ideals can be expressed in terms of the combinatorial invariants of the associated hypergraphs, as mentioned above. The first main theorem along this direction is the following:

Theorem
3.6
.

Let
G
be a chordal graph. Then for any
t\geq 2
,

\mathrm{reg}(R/J_{t}(G))=(t-1)\nu_{t}(G),

where
\nu_{t}(G)
denotes the induced matching number of the hypergraph corresponding to
J_{t}(G)
.

As a corollary of the above theorem, we characterize when the ideal
J_{t}(G)
corresponding to a chordal graph
G
has a linear resolution as follows:

Corollary
3.8
.

Let
G
be a chordal graph and
t\geq 2
be an integer. Then
J_{t}(G)
has a linear resolution if and only if
G
is
t
-gap-free (i.e.,
\nu_{t}(G)=1
).

Next, we find the following combinatorial formula for the projective dimension of

:

Theorem
4.5
.

Let
G
be a chordal graph. Then for all
t\geq 2
,
\mathrm{pd}(R/J_{t}(G))=\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G))
.

As an application of the above theorem, we combinatorially characterise when the ideal
J_{t}(G)
corresponding to a chordal graph
G
is Cohen-Macaulay. This ensures that the Cohen-Macaulay property of such ideals does not depend on the characteristic of the base field.

Corollary
4.10
.

Let
G
be a chordal graph and
t\geq 2
be an integer. Then
J_{t}(G)
is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if

is unmixed.

The above result partially generalize a famous theorem of Herzog-Hibi-Zheng
[
17

, where they classified all Cohen-Macaulay chordal graphs. Furthermore, it follows from
Theorem

3.6
and
Theorem

4.5
that the regularity and projective dimension of the

3.7
and

4.6
).

In the spirit of
Theorem

3.6
and
4.5
, one can try to obtain similar formulas for the regularity and projective dimension in the case of path ideals and clique ideals of graphs. We remark that an extensive amount of work is available in the literature on the

[

]
and the references therein). Meanwhile, recently in
[

]
, we were able to show that the regularity and projective dimension formulas of edge ideals of chordal graphs in terms of the induced matching number and big height do not extend to

-path ideals for

, even for the class of trees. Now, if one considers the
t
-clique ideal of a tree, then it is easy to see that the ideal is a zero ideal for

. Regarding clique ideals of chordal graphs, we show that the above-mentioned formula of regularity cannot be extended to higher

(

Let

. Then

(i)

\mathrm{reg}(R/(J+K))\leq\max\{\mathrm{reg}(R/J),\mathrm{reg}(R/K),\mathrm{reg}(R/(J\cap K))-1\},

(ii)

\mathrm{pd}(R/(J+K))\leq\max\{\mathrm{pd}(R/J),\mathrm{pd}(R/K),\mathrm{pd}(R/(J\cap K))+1\}.

2.5.
Bounds on regularity and projective dimension:

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hypergraph. A
matching
in
\mathcal{H}
is a collection of pairwise disjoint edges of
\mathcal{H}
. More precisely, a subset
\mathcal{D}\subseteq E(\mathcal{H})
is called a matching of
\mathcal{H}
if for any two distinct edges
\mathcal{E}_{1},\mathcal{E}_{2}\in\mathcal{D}
, one has
\mathcal{E}_{1}\cap\mathcal{E}_{2}=\emptyset
. An
induced matching
is a matching
\mathcal{D}
in
\mathcal{H}
such that the edge set of the induced subhypergraph of
\mathcal{H}
on the vertices of
\mathcal{D}
is precisely the set
\mathcal{D}
. The following lower bound on the regularity in terms of the induced matching is well-known.

Lemma 2.4
.

[
21
, Corollary 3.9]

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hypergraph, and
\mathcal{D}
an induced matching of
\mathcal{H}
. Then

\mathrm{reg}(R/I(\mathcal{H}))\geq\sum_{\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{D}}(|\mathcal{E}|-1).

Let us define
\nu(\mathcal{H})=\max\{|\mathcal{D}|:\mathcal{D}\text{ is an induced matching of }\mathcal{H}\}
, and call this the
induced matching number
of the hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
. Then for a simple graph
G
,
\nu(G)
gives a crude lower bound of
\mathrm{reg}(R/I(G))
. For our purpose, given a simple graph
G
, we call an induced matching of the hypergraph
\mathcal{H}(G,t)
a
t
-induced matching of
G
. Observe that
\{\mathcal{E}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{E}_{r}\}\subseteq E(\mathcal{H})
is an induced matching of
\mathcal{H}(G,t)
if and only if
|\mathcal{E}_{i}|=t
,
G[\mathcal{E}_{i}]
is connected for each
i\in[r]
,
\mathcal{E}_{i}\cap\mathcal{E}_{j}=\emptyset
for all
1\leq i<j\leq r
, and
E(G[\cup_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{E}_{i}])=\cup_{i=1}^{r}E(G[\mathcal{E}_{i}])
. Thus, we have
\nu(\mathcal{H}(G,t))=\nu_{t}(G)
, where
\nu_{t}(G)
is defined as in
Section

2.1
. Consequently, in our case, we have the following lower bound for the regularity of
J_{t}(G)
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
G
be a finite simple graph. Then
\mathrm{reg}(R/J_{t}(G))\geq(t-1)\nu_{t}(G)
.

Using
[
5
, Proposition 1.2.13]
and the Auslander-Buchsbaum formula
[
5
, Theorem 1.3.3]
, one can get an analogous bound for the projective dimension of
R/J_{t}(G)
in terms of
\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G))
as follows:

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
G
be a finite simple graph. Then
\mathrm{pd}(R/J_{t}(G))\geq\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G))
.

Note:
Let
s
be the maximum cardinality of the set of vertices in a connected component of a graph
G
. Then
J_{t}(G)=\langle 0\rangle
for all
t>s
. Thus, it is enough to focus on non-zero
J_{t}(G)
, and sometimes we will assume this without mentioning it explicitly.

3.
Castelnuovo-Mumford Regularity and Linearity

In this section, we compute the regularity of
J_{t}(G)
for a chordal graph
G
in terms of the
t
-induced matching number of
G
. As a consequence, we characterize when such an ideal has a linear resolution. Let us start with the following easy observation.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
I\subseteq R
be a monomial ideal, and let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}\in R
be some indeterminates. Then

(I:x_{r})+\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{r-1}\rangle=((I+\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{r-1}\rangle):x_{r}).

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
G
be a connected graph and
x\in V(G)
be a simplicial vertex. If
A\subseteq V(G)
such that
x\in A
,
|A|=t\geq 2
, and
G[A]
is connected, then
G[A\setminus\{x\}]
is also connected.

Proof.

If possible, let us assume that
G[A\setminus\{x\}]
is a disconnected graph. Let
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
be any two connected components of
G[A\setminus\{x\}]
. Since
G[A]
is connected, there are
y_{1}\in V(H_{1})
and
y_{2}\in V(H_{2})
such that
\{x,y_{1}\},\{x,y_{2}\}\in E(G[A])
. Then
\{y_{1},y_{2}\}\in E(G[A])
, as
x
is a simplicial vertex. This is a contradiction to the fact that
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
are connected components in
G[A\setminus\{x\}]
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
G
be a graph, and
C\subseteq V(G)
such that
G[C]
is connected,
|C|=t-1
, and
w\in N_{G}(C)
. Then

\nu_{t}(G\setminus(N_{G}[C]\cup N_{G}[w]))\leq\nu_{t}(G)-1.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{U}
be a
t
-induced matching of
G\setminus(N_{G}[C]\cup N_{G}[w])
such that

|\mathcal{U}|=\nu_{t}(G\setminus(N_{G}[C]\cup N_{G}[w])).

Since
w\in N_{G}(C)
and
G[C]
is connected, the induced subgraph
G[C\cup\{w\}]
is connected. Moreover,
G[C\cup\{w\}]
is a connected component of the the induced subgraph of
G
on the vertex set
V(G\setminus(N_{G}[C]\cup N_{G}[w]))\cup C\cup\{w\}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{U}\cup\{C\cup\{w\}\}
is a
t
-induced matching of
G
, and hence, the inequality follows. ∎

Notations:
We adopt the following notations for the rest of the paper.

(i)

For a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
and some given subsets
C_{1},\ldots,C_{r}
of the vertex set
V(\mathcal{H})
, we define a new hypergraph, denoted by
\mathcal{H}\setminus\{C_{1},\ldots,C_{r}\}
, by deleting some particular edges as follows:

\displaystyle V(\mathcal{H}\setminus\{C_{1},\ldots,C_{r}\})

\displaystyle=V(\mathcal{H});

\displaystyle E(\mathcal{H}\setminus\{C_{1},\ldots,C_{r}\})

\displaystyle=E(\mathcal{H})\setminus\{\mathcal{E}\in E(\mathcal{H})\mid C_{i}\subseteq\mathcal{E}\text{ for some }1\leq i\leq r\}.

(ii)

Let
x
be a vertex of the graph
G
, and
t\geq 2
be an integer. Define the set

\mathcal{A}_{x}:=\{C\subseteq V(G):|C|=t-1,x\in C,G[C]\text{ is connected}\}.

Without loss of generality, let
\mathcal{A}_{x}=\{C_{1},\ldots,C_{k}\}
. Let us define
\mathcal{B}_{C_{1}}:=N_{G}(C_{1})
. Then for each
2\leq i\leq k
, we define

\mathcal{B}_{C_{i}}:=\{w\in N_{G}(C_{i})\mid C_{i}\cup\{w\}\neq C_{j}\cup\{w^{\prime}\}\text{ for any }w^{\prime}\in N_{G}(C_{j}),\text{ where }1\leq j\leq i-1\}.

By construction,
\mathcal{B}_{C_{1}}\neq\emptyset
when
J_{t}(G)\neq\langle 0\rangle
.

The next lemma plays a crucial role in establishing the main results of this article.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
G
be a chordal graph and
x\in V(G)
be a simplicial vertex. Let us consider the set
\mathcal{A}_{x}=\{C_{1},\ldots,C_{k}\}
. For
1\leq i\leq k
, define

\displaystyle J_{i}

\displaystyle:=\mathbf{x}_{C_{i}}\langle w\mid w\in\mathcal{B}_{C_{i}}\rangle,

\displaystyle K_{i}

\displaystyle:=I(\mathcal{H}(G,t)\setminus\{C_{1},\ldots,C_{i}\}).

If
\mathcal{B}_{C_{i}}\neq\emptyset
, then

(1)

J_{i}+K_{i}=I(\mathcal{H}(G,t)\setminus\{C_{1},\ldots,C_{i-1}\})
,

(2)

J_{i}\cap K_{i}=\mathbf{x}_{C_{i}}L_{i}
, where
L_{i}=\left\langle\frac{\mathrm{lcm}(m,m^{\prime})}{\mathbf{x}_{C_{i}}}\mid m\in J_{i}\text{ and }m^{\prime}\in K_{i}\right\rangle
. Furthermore, for any
w\in\mathcal{B}_{C_{i}}
, we have
(L_{i}:w)=M_{i}+N_{i}+Q_{i}
, where

\displaystyle M_{i}

\displaystyle=\langle v\mid v\in N_{G}(C_{i})\setminus\{w\}\rangle,

\displaystyle N_{i}

\displaystyle=\langle v\mid v\in N_{G}(w)\setminus N_{G}[C_{i}]\rangle,

\displaystyle Q_{i}

\displaystyle=\langle\mathbf{x}_{C}\mid C\subseteq V(G),|C|=t,G[C]\text{ is connected },C\cap(N_{G}[C_{i}]\cup N_{G}[w])=\emptyset\rangle.

Proof.

(1) Follows immediately from the construction of the ideals
J_{i}
and
K_{i}
.

(2) Clearly, we have
J_{i}\cap K_{i}=\mathbf{x}_{C_{i}}L_{i}
. Now, fix any
1\leq i\leq k
and any
w\in\mathcal{B}_{C_{i}}
. We first show that
M_{i}+N_{i}+Q_{i}\subseteq(L_{i}:w)
. Let
v\in N_{G}(C_{i})\setminus\{w\}
. Then we can write
wv\mathbf{x}_{C_{i}}=\mathrm{lcm}(w\mathbf{x}_{C_{i}},wv\mathbf{x}_{C_{i}\setminus\{x\}})
. Since
w,v\in N_{G}(C_{i})
, we see that
G[C_{i}\cup\{w,v\}]
is a connected graph. Hence by
Lemma

3.2
,
G[(C_{i}\setminus\{x\})\cup\{w,v\}]
is also connected. Also, observe that
C_{j}\nsubseteq(C_{i}\setminus\{x\})\cup\{w,v\}
for any
1\leq j\leq i
, as
x\in C_{j}
for all such
j
. Hence we get
wv\mathbf{x}_{C_{i}\setminus\{x\}}\in K_{i}
. Moreover, from our choice of
w\in\mathcal{B}_{C_{i}}
, it is easy to see that
w\mathbf{x}_{C_{i}}\in J_{i}
. Thus, we have
wv\mathbf{x}_{C_{i}}\in J_{i}\cap K_{i}
, and therefore,
v\in(L_{i}:w)
. Now, let
v\in N_{G}(w)\setminus N_{G}[C_{i}]
. Then again, we can write
wv\mathbf{x}_{C_{i}}=\mathrm{lcm}(w\mathbf{x}_{C_{i}},wv\mathbf{x}_{C_{i}\setminus\{x\}})
. By similar arguments as above, we obtain
w\mathbf{x}_{C_{i}}\in J_{i}
and
wv\mathbf{x}_{C_{i}\setminus\{x\}}\in K_{i}
, and thus,
wv\mathbf{x}_{C_{i}}\in J_{i}\cap K_{i}
, which in turn, gives
v\in(L_{i}:w)
. Finally, let
\mathbf{x}_{C}\in Q_{i}
, where
C\subseteq V(G)
is such that
|C|=t
,
G[C]
is connected, and
C\cap(N_{G}[C_{i}]\cup N_{G}[w])=\emptyset
. Then we write

w\mathbf{x}_{C_{i}}\mathbf{x}_{C}=\mathrm{lcm}(w\mathbf{x}_{C_{i}},\mathbf{x}_{C}),

where
w\mathbf{x}_{C_{i}}\in J_{i}
and
\mathbf{x}_{C}\in K_{i}
. Thus,
w\mathbf{x}_{C_{i}}\mathbf{x}_{C}\in J_{i}\cap K_{i}
and hence
\mathbf{x}_{C}\in(L_{i}:w)
.

We now proceed to show that
(L_{i}:w)\subseteq M_{i}+N_{i}+Q_{i}
. Let
A\subseteq V(G)
be such that
\mathbf{x}_{A}\in\mathcal{G}(K_{i})
. We consider the following two cases:

Case-I.
Let
A\cap N_{G}[C_{i}]=\emptyset
. Then
w\notin A
and hence
\mathrm{lcm}(w\mathbf{x}_{C_{i}},\mathbf{x}_{A})=w\mathbf{x}_{C_{i}}\mathbf{x}_{A}
. Now if
A\cap N_{G}[w]\neq\emptyset
, then
A\cap N_{G}[w]\subseteq N_{G}(w)\setminus N_{G}[C_{i}]
, since
w\notin A
. Also if
A\cap N_{G}[w]=\emptyset
, then clearly
A\cap(N_{G}[C_{i}]\cup N_{G}[w])=\emptyset
. Thus, in any case, we have

(\mathrm{lcm}(w\mathbf{x}_{C_{i}},\mathbf{x}_{A}):w\mathbf{x}_{C_{i}})=\mathbf{x}_{A}\in N_{i}+Q_{i}.

Case-II.
Let
A\cap N_{G}[C_{i}]\neq\emptyset
. Note that, since
A\nsubseteq C_{i}
, and
G[C_{i}\cup A]
is connected, there exists some
a\in A\setminus C_{i}
and
b\in C_{i}
such that
\{a,b\}\in E(G)
. Thus
A\cap N_{G}(C_{i})\neq\emptyset
.

First, consider the case when
A\cap N_{G}(C_{i})=\{w\}
. Recall that
|C_{i}|=t-1
, and
|A|=t
. Since
C_{i}\nsubseteq A
, there exists some
v\in A\setminus C_{i}
such that
v\neq w
and
v\notin N_{G}[C_{i}]
. Since
G[A]
is connected, there exists a shortest path
v=y_{0},y_{1},\ldots,y_{r}=w
such that
y_{i}\in A
for all
0\leq i\leq r
. Note that
y_{r-1}\in N_{G}(w)
, and
v\notin N_{G}[C_{i}]
. So if
y_{r-1}\in C_{i}
, then there exists
1\leq i\leq r-2
such that
y_{i}\in A\cap N_{G}(C_{i})
, which is a contradiction to the fact that
A\cap N_{G}(C_{i})=\{w\}
. Thus
y_{r-1}\notin N_{G}[C_{i}]
and hence
y_{r-1}\in N_{G}(w)\setminus N_{G}[C_{i}]
. Therefore, in this case
(\mathrm{lcm}(w\mathbf{x}_{C_{i}},\mathbf{x}_{A}):w\mathbf{x}_{C_{i}})\in N_{i}
.

Finally, let us assume that there exists
w^{\prime}\in A\cap N_{G}(C_{i})
, where
w^{\prime}\neq w
. Then, we have
(\mathrm{lcm}(w\mathbf{x}_{C_{i}},\mathbf{x}_{A}):w\mathbf{x}_{C_{i}})\in M_{i}
. Therefore,
(L_{i}:w)=M_{i}+N_{i}+Q_{i}
.
∎

We are now ready to prove the main theorem of this section.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
G
be a chordal graph. Then for any
t\geq 2
,

\mathrm{reg}(R/J_{t}(G))=(t-1)\nu_{t}(G).

Proof.

In view of
Lemma

2.4
, it is enough to prove that
\mathrm{reg}(R/J_{t}(G))\leq(t-1)\nu_{t}(G)
. We prove this using induction on
|V(G)|
. First and foremost, if
|V(G)|\leq t
, then one can see that either
J_{t}(G)=\langle\prod_{x\in V(G)}x\rangle
or
J_{t}(G)=\langle 0\rangle
. In either case, it is easy to see that
\mathrm{reg}(R/J_{t}(G))=(t-1)\nu_{t}(G)
. Therefore, we may assume that
|V(G)|\geq t+1
. Also, we can assume that
\nu_{t}(G)\geq 1
. Moreover, we will write
J_{t}(G)=I(\mathcal{H}(G,t))
, where
\mathcal{H}(G,t)
is the hypergraph corresponding to the ideal
J_{t}(G)
. Now, let
x\in V(G)
be a simplicial vertex of
G
, and
\mathcal{A}_{x}=\{C_{1},\ldots,C_{k}\}
. Following
Lemma

3.4
, whenever
\mathcal{B}_{C_{i}}\neq\emptyset
for some
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
, we denote

\displaystyle J_{i}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{x}_{C_{i}}\langle w\mid w\in\mathcal{B}_{C_{i}}\rangle,

\displaystyle K_{i}

\displaystyle=I(\mathcal{H}(G,t)\setminus\{C_{1},\ldots,C_{i}\}),

\displaystyle J_{i}

\displaystyle\cap K_{i}=\mathbf{x}_{C_{i}}L_{i}.

Claim:

\mathrm{reg}(R/L_{i})\leq(t-1)\nu_{t}(G)-(t-2)
for each
1\leq i\leq k
.

Proof of the claim.
Let
\mathcal{B}_{C_{i}}=\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{s}\}
. Then
L_{i}+\langle w_{1},\ldots,w_{s}\rangle=\langle w_{1},\ldots,w_{s}\rangle
by
Lemma

3.4
. Thus,

\mathrm{reg}(R/(L_{i}+\langle w_{1},\ldots,w_{s}\rangle))=0\leq(t-1)\nu_{t}(G)-(t-2).

Now by
Lemma

3.4
,

\displaystyle((L_{i}+\langle w_{1},\ldots,w_{s-1}\rangle):w_{s})

\displaystyle=(L_{i}:w_{s})+\langle w_{1},\ldots,w_{s-1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle v\mid v\in N_{G}(C_{i})\setminus\{w_{s}\}\rangle+\langle v\mid v\in N_{G}(w_{s})\setminus N_{G}[C_{i}]\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+\langle\mathbf{x}_{C}\mid C\subseteq V(G),|C|=t,G[C]\text{ is connected },

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055ptC\cap(N_{G}[C_{i}]\cup N_{G}[w_{s}])=\emptyset\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle v\mid v\in N_{G}(C_{i})\setminus\{w_{s}\}\rangle+\langle v\mid v\in N_{G}(w_{s})\setminus N_{G}[C_{i}]\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+J_{t}(G\setminus(N_{G}[C_{i}]\cup N_{G}[w_{s}])).

Then

\displaystyle\mathrm{reg}(R/((L_{i}+\langle w_{1},\ldots,w_{s-1}\rangle):w_{s}))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{reg}(R/J_{t}(G\setminus(N_{G}[C_{i}]\cup N_{G}[w_{s}])))

\displaystyle\leq(t-1)\nu_{t}(G\setminus(N_{G}[C_{i}]\cup N_{G}[w_{s}]))

\displaystyle\leq(t-1)(\nu_{t}(G)-1),

where the first inequality is by the induction hypothesis and the second inequality follows from
Lemma

3.3
. Hence, by
Lemma

2.2
, we have
\mathrm{reg}(R/(L_{i}+\langle w_{1},\ldots,w_{s-1}\rangle)\leq(t-1)\nu_{t}(G)-(t-2).
Now for each
2\leq j\leq s-1
, similarly using
Lemma

3.4
, we have

\displaystyle((L_{i}+\langle w_{1},\ldots,w_{s-j}\rangle):w_{s-j+1})

\displaystyle=(L_{i}:w_{s-j+1})+\langle w_{1},\ldots,w_{s-j}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle v\mid v\in N_{G}(C_{i})\setminus\{w_{s-j+1}\}\rangle+\langle v\mid v\in N_{G}(w_{s-j+1})\setminus N_{G}[C_{i}]\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+\langle\mathbf{x}_{C}\mid C\subseteq V(G),|C|=t,G[C]\text{ is connected },

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055ptC\cap(N_{G}[C_{i}]\cup N_{G}[w_{s-j+1}])=\emptyset\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle v\mid v\in N_{G}(C_{i})\setminus\{w_{s-j+1}\}\rangle+\langle v\mid v\in N_{G}(w_{s-j+1})\setminus N_{G}[C_{i}]\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+J_{t}(G\setminus(N_{G}[C_{i}]\cup N_{G}[w_{s-j+1}])).

Thus for each
2\leq j\leq s-1
, we get

\displaystyle\mathrm{reg}(R/((L_{i}+\langle w_{1},\ldots,w_{s-j}\rangle):w_{s-j+1}))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{reg}(R/J_{t}(G\setminus(N_{G}[C_{i}]\cup N_{G}[w_{s-j+1}])))

\displaystyle\leq(t-1)\nu_{t}(G\setminus(N_{G}[C_{i}]\cup N_{G}[w_{s-j+1}]))

\displaystyle\leq(t-1)(\nu_{t}(G)-1),

Therefore, repeatedly applying
Lemma

2.2
, we obtain

\mathrm{reg}(R/L_{i})\leq(t-1)(\nu_{t}(G)-1)-1=(t-1)\nu_{t}(G)-(t-2).

This completes the proof of the claim.

Now, consider the ideal
K_{k}=I(\mathcal{H}(G,t)\setminus\{C_{1},\ldots,C_{k}\})=I(\mathcal{H}(G\setminus x,t))=J_{t}(G\setminus x)
. By the induction hypothesis,
\mathrm{reg}(R/K_{k})\leq(t-1)\nu_{t}(G\setminus x)\leq(t-1)\nu_{t}(G)
. Also, observe that
\mathrm{reg}(R/J_{k})=t-1\leq(t-1)\nu_{t}(G)
. Moreover, we have

\mathrm{reg}(R/(J_{k}\cap K_{k}))=\mathrm{reg}(R/\mathbf{x}_{C_{i}}L_{k})\leq(t-1)+(t-1)\nu_{t}(G)-t-2)=(t-1)\nu_{t}(G)+1.

Hence, by
Lemma

2.3
,
\mathrm{reg}(R/(J_{k}+K_{k}))\leq(t-1)\nu_{t}(G)
. Note that the ideal
J_{k}+K_{k}
is nothing but
I(\mathcal{H}\setminus\{C_{1},\ldots,C_{k-1}\})=K_{k-1}
. We now write
I(\mathcal{H}\setminus\{C_{1},\ldots,C_{k-2}\})=J_{k-1}+K_{k-1}
and continue the above process. Note that if for some
1\leq i\leq k
,
\mathcal{B}_{C_{i}}=\emptyset
, then
K_{i}=K_{i-1}
. Hence, after a finite number of steps, we obtain

\mathrm{reg}(R/J_{t}(G))=\mathrm{reg}(R/I(\mathcal{H}(G,t)))=\mathrm{reg}(R/(J_{1}+K_{1})\leq(t-1)\nu_{t}(G),

and this completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

Since the regularity of
t
-connected ideals of chordal graphs can be expressed in terms of a combinatorial invariant of the graph, it follows that in this case the regularity is independent of the characteristic of the base field.

As an application of
Theorem

3.6
, we get a complete classification of chordal graphs
G
such that
J_{t}(G)
has a linear resolution as follows.

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
G
be a chordal graph and
t\geq 2
be an integer. Then
J_{t}(G)
has a linear resolution if and only if
G
is
t
-gap-free (i.e.,
\nu_{t}(G)=1
).

Remark 3.9
.

The result stated in the above corollary was proved earlier in
[
2
]
. In fact, more generally, it was shown in
[
2
, Theorem 5.1]
that a chordal graph
G
is
t
-gap-free if and only if
J_{t}(G)
has linear quotients.

Example 3.10
.

Let us consider the graph
G
as shown in
Figure

1
. Then one can deduce that

\displaystyle\nu_{t}(G)=\begin{cases}4\quad\text{ for }t=2,\\
3\quad\text{ for }t=3,\\
2\quad\text{ for }t=4,5,6,\\
1\quad\text{ for }t=7,\ldots,14,\\
0\quad\text{ for }t>14.\end{cases}

Therefore, using
Theorem

3.6
, we can derive
\mathrm{reg}(R/J_{t}(G))
for all
t\geq 2
. Note that
J_{t}(G)=\langle 0\rangle
for all
t>14
. If
J_{t}(G)\neq\langle 0\rangle
, then due to
Corollary

3.8
,
J_{t}(G)
has a linear resolution if and only if
t=7,\ldots,14
.

4.
Projective Dimension and Cohen-Macaulay Property

In this section, we compute the projective dimension of
J_{t}(G)
for a chordal graph
G
in terms of the big height of the corresponding ideal. As a corollary, we combinatorially classify when such an ideal is Cohen-Macaulay.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
G
be a disjoint union of two graphs
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
, i.e.,
G=G_{1}\sqcup G_{2}
. Then

\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G))=\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G_{1}))+\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G_{2})).

Proof.

Since
G=G_{1}\sqcup G_{2}
, we have
\mathcal{G}(J_{t}(G))=\mathcal{G}(J_{t}(G_{1}))\sqcup\mathcal{G}(J_{t}(G_{2}))
. Thus,
\mathfrak{p}
is a minimal prime ideal of
J_{t}(G)
if and only if
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{p}_{1}+\mathfrak{p}_{2}
, where
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{p}_{2}
are minimal prime ideals of
J_{t}(G_{1})
and
J_{t}(G_{2})
, respectively. Hence, the result follows.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

For any induced subgraph
H
of a graph
G
,
\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(H))\leq\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G))
.

Proof.

Since
H
is a subgraph of
G
, any connected set of cardinality
t
in
H
is also a connected set in
G
. Thus, we have
J_{t}(H)\subseteq J_{t}(G)
. Let
\mathfrak{p}
be an associated prime of
J_{t}(H)
such that
\mathrm{ht}(\mathfrak{p})=\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(H))
. Now, let us consider the prime ideal
\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{p}+\langle V(G)\setminus V(H)\rangle
. Note that since
H
is an induced subgraph of
G
, for each
m\in\mathcal{G}(J_{t}(G))\setminus\mathcal{G}(J_{t}(H))
, there exists
z\in V(G)\setminus V(H)
such that
z\mid m
. Thus,
\mathfrak{q}
is a prime ideal containing
J_{t}(G)
. Hence, there exists a minimal prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
of
J_{t}(G)
such that
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}
. Our aim now is to show that
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
. Indeed, if
x\in\mathcal{G}(\mathfrak{p})
, then there exists some
m\in\mathcal{G}(J_{t}(H))
such that
x\mid m
and for each
x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}(\mathfrak{p})
with
x^{\prime}\neq x
, we have
x^{\prime}\nmid m
. Note that for each
y\in\langle V(G)\setminus V(H)\rangle
,
y\nmid m
. Thus,
m\in J_{t}(H)\subseteq J_{t}(G)
and
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}
implies
x\in\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
. Hence,
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
and consequently,
\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(H))\leq\mathrm{ht}(\mathfrak{p}^{\prime})\leq\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G))
.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
C\subseteq V(G)
be such that
|C|=t-1
with
t\geq 2
and
G[C]
is connected. Let
J
denote the ideal
\langle w\mathbf{x}_{C}\mid w\in N_{G}(C)\rangle
. Then
\mathrm{pd}(R/J)\leq\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G))
.

Proof.

It is easy to see that
\mathrm{pd}(R/J)=\mathrm{pd}(R/\langle N_{G}(C)\rangle)
, and the Koszul complex is the minimal free resolution of
R/\langle N_{G}(C)\rangle
. Thus,
\mathrm{pd}(R/J)=|N_{G}(C)|
. In order to show
\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G))\geq|N_{G}(C)|
, note that
\mathfrak{p}=\langle V(G)\setminus C\rangle
is a prime ideal containing
J_{t}(G)
. Then there exists a minimal prime ideal
\mathfrak{q}
of
J_{t}(G)
such that
\mathfrak{q}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
. Since
J\subseteq J_{t}(G)\subseteq\mathfrak{q}
and
C\cap\mathcal{G}(\mathfrak{q})=\emptyset
, we must have
\langle N_{G}(C)\rangle\subseteq\mathfrak{q}
. Hence,
\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G))\geq\mathrm{ht}(\mathfrak{q})\geq|N_{G}(C)|
as desired.
∎

The following lemma on the big height of
J_{t}(G)
is important in the proof of the main theorem of this section.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
x
be a simplicial vertex of a graph
G
and
C\subseteq V(G)
be such that
x\in C
,
|C|=t-1
for some integer
t\geq 2
, and
G[C]
is connected. Then for each
y\in N_{G}(C)
, we have

\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G))\geq|N_{G}(C)|+|N_{G}(y)\setminus N_{G}[C]|+\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G\setminus(N_{G}[C]\cup N_{G}[y])).

Proof.

We divide the proof into the following two cases.

Case-I.

N_{G}(y)\subseteq N_{G}[C]
. In this case,
|N_{G}(y)\setminus N_{G}[C]|=0
. Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a minimal prime ideal of
J_{t}(G\setminus(N_{G}[C]\cup N_{G}[y]))
such that
\mathrm{ht}(\mathfrak{p})=\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G\setminus(N_{G}[C]\cup N_{G}[y])))
. We claim that
\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{p}+\langle N_{G}(C)\rangle
is a prime ideal containing
J_{t}(G)
. Indeed, if
f\in\mathcal{G}(J_{t}(G))
such that
f\in J_{t}(G\setminus(N_{G}[C]\cup N_{G}[y]))
, then
f\in\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}
. Otherwise,
f\in\langle N_{G}(C)\rangle
since
|C|=t-1
,
y\in N_{G}(C)
, and
N_{G}(y)\subseteq N_{G}[C]
. Thus we have
f\in\mathfrak{q}
, and consequently,
J_{t}(G)\subseteq\mathfrak{q}
, as required. Next, we want to show that
\mathfrak{q}
is a minimal prime ideal containing
J_{t}(G)
. Suppose
\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}
is a minimal prime ideal of
J_{t}(G)
such that
\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}
. Observe that since
\mathfrak{p}
is a minimal prime ideal of
J_{t}(G\setminus(N_{G}[C]\cup N_{G}[y]))
, for each
x\in\mathcal{G}(\mathfrak{p})
there exists some
g_{x}\in\mathcal{G}(J_{t}(G\setminus(N_{G}[C]\cup N_{G}[y])))
such that
x\mid g_{x}
, and
g_{x}\notin\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
, where
\mathcal{G}(\mathfrak{p}^{\prime})=\mathcal{G}(\mathfrak{p})\setminus\{x\}
. By definition,
g_{x}\in\mathcal{G}(J_{t}(G))
and since
\mathcal{G}(\mathfrak{p})\cap N_{G}(C)=\emptyset
we must have
x\in\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}
. Thus,
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}
. Also, note that
\langle N_{G}(C)\rangle\subseteq\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}
since
z\mathbf{x}_{C}\in J_{t}(G)
for all
z\in N_{G}(C)
and
(\{z\}\cup C)\cap\mathcal{G}(\mathfrak{p})=\emptyset
. Thus, we have
\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{q}
, and consequently,

\displaystyle\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G))\geq\mathrm{ht}(\mathfrak{q})

\displaystyle=|N_{G}(C)|+\mathrm{ht}(\mathfrak{p})

\displaystyle=|N_{G}(C)|+|N_{G}(y)\setminus N_{G}[C]|+\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G\setminus(N_{G}[C]\cup N_{G}[y])),

where
|N_{G}(y)\setminus N_{G}[C]|=0
, in this case.

Case-II.

N_{G}(y)\not\subseteq N_{G}[C]
. Our aim in this case is to show that if
\mathfrak{p}
is a minimal prime ideal of
J_{t}(G\setminus(N_{G}[C]\cup N_{G}[y]))
such that
\mathrm{ht}(\mathfrak{p})=\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G\setminus(N_{G}[C]\cup N_{G}[y])))
, then

\mathfrak{q}_{1}=\mathfrak{p}+\langle x\rangle+\langle N_{G}(C)\setminus\{y\}\rangle+\langle N_{G}(y)\setminus N_{G}[C]\rangle

is a minimal prime ideal containing
J_{t}(G)
. As before, if
f\in\mathcal{G}(J_{t}(G))
such that we have
f\in J_{t}(G\setminus(N_{G}[C]\cup N_{G}[y]))
, then
f\in\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}_{1}
. Otherwise,
f\in\langle N_{G}[C]\cup N_{G}[y]\rangle
. Observe that if
f\notin\langle(N_{G}(C)\setminus\{y\})\cup(N_{G}(y)\setminus N_{G}[C])\rangle
, then
f=y\cdot\mathbf{x}_{C}
since
|C|=t-1
. Therefore,
f\in\mathfrak{q}_{1}
since
x\in C
. Thus,
\mathfrak{q}_{1}
is a prime ideal containing
J_{t}(G)
. Now, to show
\mathfrak{q}_{1}
is minimal, we consider some
\mathfrak{q}_{1}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}_{1}
such that
\mathfrak{q}_{1}^{\prime}
is a minimal prime ideal of
J_{t}(G)
, and we proceed to show that
\mathfrak{q}_{1}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{q}_{1}
. Proceeding as in the previous case, it is easy to see that
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}_{1}^{\prime}
since
\mathcal{G}(\mathfrak{p})\cap((N_{G}(C)\setminus\{y\})\cup(N_{G}(y)\setminus N_{G}[C])\cup\{x\})=\emptyset
. Now, for each
y^{\prime}\in N_{G}(C)
with
y^{\prime}\neq y
, we have
yy^{\prime}\mathbf{x}_{C\setminus\{x\}}\in J_{t}(G)
by
Lemma

3.2
. Hence,
\langle N_{G}(C)\setminus\{y\}\rangle\subseteq\mathfrak{q}_{1}^{\prime}
since
((C\setminus\{x\})\cup\{y\})\cap\mathcal{G}(\mathfrak{q}_{1})=\emptyset
. On the other hand, we have
x\in\mathfrak{q}_{1}^{\prime}
since
y\mathbf{x}_{C}\in J_{t}(G)
. Observe that for each
w\in N_{G}(y)\setminus N_{G}[C]
,
G[C\cup\{y,w\}]
is connected, and thus,
G[C\setminus\{x\}\cup\{y,w\}]
is connected by
Lemma

3.2
. In particular,
yw\mathbf{x}_{C\setminus\{x\}}\in J_{t}(G)
for each
w\in N_{G}(y)\setminus N_{G}[C]
, which implies
\langle N_{G}(y)\setminus N_{G}[C]\rangle\subseteq\mathfrak{q}_{1}^{\prime}
since
((C\setminus\{x\})\cup\{y\})\cap\mathcal{G}(\mathfrak{q}_{1})=\emptyset
. Hence,
\mathfrak{q}_{1}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{q}_{1}
, and consequently,

\displaystyle\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G))

\displaystyle\geq\mathrm{ht}(\mathfrak{q}_{1})

\displaystyle=|N_{G}(C)|+|N_{G}(y)\setminus N_{G}[C]|+\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G\setminus(N_{G}[C]\cup N_{G}[y])).

This completes the proof.
∎

We now proceed to prove the main theorem of this section.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
G
be a chordal graph. Then for all
t\geq 2
,
\mathrm{pd}(R/J_{t}(G))=\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G))
.

Proof.

Due to
Lemma

2.6
, it is enough to show that
\mathrm{pd}(R/J_{t}(G))\leq\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G))
. We proceed by induction on
|V(G)|
. The statement is trivially true for
|V(G)|<t
. If
|V(G)|=t
, then either
J_{t}(G)=\langle 0\rangle
or
J_{t}(G)=\langle\mathbf{x}_{V(G)}\rangle
depending on whether
G
is connected or not. In both cases, it is easy to see that
\mathrm{pd}(R/J_{t}(G))=\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G))
. Therefore, we may assume that
|V(G)|>t
. Note that, in case
G
is disconnected, and
G_{1},\ldots,G_{r}
are all connected components of
G
such that
|V(G_{i})|<t
for all
i\in[r]
, then again
J_{t}(G)=\langle 0\rangle
, and thus
\mathrm{pd}(R/J_{t}(G))=\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G))
. Therefore, we may further assume that
G
has at least one connected component with at least
t
many vertices. Now, let us consider the following two cases:

Case-I.
For each simplicial vertex
x
of
G
, if
C\in\mathcal{A}_{x}
, then
V(G)\neq N_{G}[C]
. Note that since
G
has at least one connected component with at least
t
many vertices, we can find a simplicial vertex
x
of
G
and some
C\in\mathcal{A}_{x}
such that
C\subsetneq N_{G}[C]
. Based on this observation, we consider two subcases:

Subcase-I(A).
There exists a simplicial vertex
x
of
G
and
C\in\mathcal{A}_{x}
such that
G[N_{G}[C]]
forms a connected component of
G
. Then we have

J_{t}(G)=J_{t}(G[N_{G}[C]])+J_{t}(G\setminus N_{G}[C]).

From our assumption in Case-I, it follows that

1\leq|N_{G}[C]|<|V(G)|\text{ and }1\leq|V(G\setminus N_{G}[C])|<|V(G)|.

Therefore, by the induction hypothesis,
Lemma

2.1
and
Proposition

4.1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{pd}(R/J_{t}(G))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{pd}(R/J_{t}(G[N_{G}[C]]))+\mathrm{pd}(R/J_{t}(G\setminus N_{G}[C]))

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G[N_{G}[C]]))+\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G\setminus N_{G}[C]))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G)).

Subcase-I(B).
For each simplicial vertex
x
of
G
and each
C\in\mathcal{A}_{x}
,
G[N_{G}[C]]
does not form a connected component of
G
. In particular,
C\subsetneq N_{G}[C]
for any such
C
. Recall that
J_{t}(G)=I(\mathcal{H}(G,t))
. Now, fix a simplicial vertex
x
of
G
. Let
\mathcal{A}_{x}=\{C_{1},\ldots,C_{k}\}
. For
1\leq i\leq k
, we define

\displaystyle J_{i}=\langle\mathbf{x}_{C_{i}}w\mid w\in\mathcal{B}_{C_{i}}\rangle\text{ and }K_{i}=I(\mathcal{H}(G,t)\setminus\{C_{1},\ldots,C_{i}\}).

Note that by construction,
\mathcal{B}_{C_{1}}\neq\emptyset
as
C_{1}\subsetneq N_{G}[C_{1}]
. Fix some
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
so that
\mathcal{B}_{C_{i}}\neq\phi
. Then we are in the situation of
Lemma

3.4
, and thus,
J_{i}+K_{i}=I(\mathcal{H}(G,t)\setminus\{C_{1},\ldots,C_{i-1}\})
. Now, we proceed to prove the following claim:

Claim 1:

\mathrm{pd}(R/J_{i}\cap K_{i})\leq\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G))-1
.

Proof of the Claim 1.
We have
J_{i}\cap K_{i}=\mathbf{x}_{C_{i}}L_{i}
, where
L_{i}
is generated by the monomials
\frac{\mathrm{lcm}(m,m^{\prime})}{\mathbf{x}_{C_{i}}}
with
m\in J_{i}
and
m^{\prime}\in K_{i}
. Thus, it is enough to prove
\mathrm{pd}(R/L_{i})\leq\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G))-1
. Let
\mathcal{B}_{C_{i}}=\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{s}\}
. Then by
Lemma

3.4
,
L_{i}+\langle w_{1},\ldots,w_{s}\rangle=\langle w_{1},\ldots,w_{s}\rangle
, and hence,
\mathrm{pd}(R/L_{i}+\langle w_{1},\cdots,w_{s}\rangle)=s
. Note that
\mathcal{B}_{C_{i}}\subseteq N_{G}(C_{i})
. Moreover, since
G[N_{G}[C_{i}]]
does not form a connected component of
G
, there exists some
y\in N_{G}(C_{i})
such that
N_{G}(y)\setminus N_{G}[C_{i}]\neq\emptyset
. In this case, by
Lemma

4.4
,
\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G))\geq|N_{G}(C_{i})|+|N_{G}(y)\setminus N_{G}[C_{i}]|\geq s+1
. Thus,

(1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{pd}(R/L_{i}+\langle w_{1},\dots,w_{s}\rangle)\leq\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G))-1.

Now by
Lemma

3.4
,

(L_{i}:w_{j})=\langle N_{G}(C_{i})\setminus\{w_{j}\}\rangle+\langle N_{G}(w_{j})\setminus N_{G}[C_{i}]\rangle+J_{t}(G\setminus(N_{G}[C_{i}]\cup N_{G}[w_{j}]))

for each
j\in[s]
. Thus, from
Lemma

2.1
(ii) it follows that

\displaystyle\mathrm{pd}(R/(L_{i}:w_{j}))=\,

\displaystyle\mathrm{pd}(\mathbb{K}[N_{G}[C_{i}]]/\langle N_{G}(C_{i})\setminus\{w_{j}\}\rangle)

\displaystyle+\mathrm{pd}(\mathbb{K}[N_{G}(w_{j})\setminus N_{G}[C_{i}]]/\langle N_{G}(w_{j})\setminus N_{G}[C_{i}]\rangle)

\displaystyle+\mathrm{pd}(\mathbb{K}[V(G)\setminus(N_{G}[C_{i}]\cup N_{G}[w_{j}])]/J_{t}(G\setminus(N_{G}[C_{i}]\cup N_{G}[w_{j}]))),

where for a subset
A
of variables, we denote the polynomial ring
\mathbb{K}[x_{i}\mid x_{i}\in A]
as
\mathbb{K}[A]
.
Note that if
I\subseteq R
is an ideal generated by
k
indeterminates, then
\mathrm{pd}(R/I)=k
(this simply follows from the observation that
\mathrm{pd}(R/\langle x_{i}\rangle)=1
, where
x_{i}
is an indeterminate, and then using
Lemma

2.1
(ii), repeatedly). Thus, from the above equation and induction hypothesis, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{pd}(R/(L_{i}:w_{j}))\leq|N_{G}(C_{i})|-1+|N_{G}(w_{j})\setminus N_{G}[C_{i}]|+\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G\setminus(N_{G}[C_{i}]\cup N_{G}[w_{j}]))).

Finally, due to
Lemma

4.4
, we obtain

(2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{pd}(R/(L_{i}:w_{j}))\leq\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G))-1,

for each
j\in[s]
. Observe that
((L_{i}+\langle w_{1},\ldots,w_{s-1}\rangle):w_{s})=(L_{i}:w_{s})
by
Lemma

3.1
and
Lemma

3.4
. Thus, using
Equation

2
, we get

\mathrm{pd}(R/((L_{i}+\langle w_{1},\ldots,w_{s-1}\rangle):w_{s}))\leq\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G))-1.

Consequently, by
Lemma

2.2
and the
Equation

1
, we obtain

\mathrm{pd}(R/L_{i}+\langle w_{1},\ldots,w_{s-1}\rangle\leq\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G))-1.

In view of
Lemma

3.1
and
3.4
, we again have
((L_{i}+\langle w_{1},\ldots,w_{s-2}\rangle):w_{s-1})=(L_{i}:w_{s-1})
. Thus, proceeding similarly as before and using
Lemma

2.2
repeatedly, we finally obtain
\mathrm{pd}(R/L_{i})\leq\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G))-1
, and this completes the proof of the above claim.

It is easy to observe that
J_{t}(G\setminus x)=I(\mathcal{H}(G,t)\setminus\{C_{1},\ldots,C_{k}\})=K_{k}
. Since
G\setminus x
is an induced subgraph of
G
, by
Proposition

4.2
and the induction hypothesis, we have
\mathrm{pd}(R/K_{k})\leq\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G))
. Moreover,
\mathrm{pd}(R/J_{k})=\mathrm{pd}(R/\langle\mathcal{B}_{C_{k}}\rangle)=|\mathcal{B}_{C_{k}}|\leq|N_{G}(C_{k})|
, and proceeding as in the proof of
Proposition

4.3
, we have
\mathrm{pd}(R/J_{k})\leq\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G))
. Thus, using the Claim 1 and by
Lemma

2.3
, we obtain

\mathrm{pd}(R/J_{k}+K_{k})\leq\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G)),

where the ideal
J_{k}+K_{k}=I(\mathcal{H}(G,t)\setminus\{C_{1},\ldots,C_{k-1}\})=K_{k-1}
, by
Lemma

3.4
. Next, using
Lemma

3.4
, we write
K_{k-2}=J_{k-1}+K_{k-1}
and continue the above process. Note that
\mathcal{B}_{C_{1}}\neq\emptyset
, and if for some
i\in\{2,\ldots,k\}
,
\mathcal{B}_{C_{i}}=\emptyset
, then
K_{i}=K_{i-1}
. Thus using
Lemma

3.4
and
Lemma

2.3
repeatedly, we get
\mathrm{pd}(R/J_{i}+K_{i})\leq\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G))
for each
i\in[k]
. In particular,
\mathrm{pd}(R/J_{t}(G))=\mathrm{pd}(R/J_{1}+K_{1})\leq\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G))
, as desired.

Case-II.
There exists a simplicial vertex
x
of
G
, and some
C_{1}\in\mathcal{A}_{x}
so that
V(G)=N_{G}[C_{1}]
. In particular,
G
is a connected graph. As before, since
G
has at least one connected component with at least
t
many vertices, we may as well assume that
C_{1}\subsetneq N_{G}[C_{1}]
. Without loss of generality, let
\mathcal{A}_{x}=\{C_{1},\ldots,C_{k}\}
, where
N_{G}[C_{i}]=V(G)
for
1\leq i\leq l
, and
N_{G}[C_{i}]\subsetneq V(G)
for
l+1\leq i\leq k
. As before, define

J_{i}=\langle\mathbf{x}_{C_{i}}w\mid w\in\mathcal{B}_{C_{i}}\rangle\text{ and }K_{i}=I(\mathcal{H}(G,t)\setminus\{C_{1},\ldots,C_{i}\})

for
i\in[k]
. Fix some
i\in[k]
such that
\mathcal{B}_{C_{i}}\neq\emptyset
. First, we consider the case when
l+1\leq i\leq k
. In this case, since
G
is connected and
N_{G}[C_{i}]\subsetneq V(G)
, there exists some
y\in N_{G}(C_{i})
such that
N_{G}(y)\setminus N_{G}[C_{i}]\neq\emptyset
. In particular,
G[N_{G}[C_{i}]]
does not form a connected component of
G
. Thus proceeding as in Subcase-I(B), we obtain

\mathrm{pd}(R/J_{i}\cap K_{i})\leq\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G))-1.

Now consider the case when
i\in[l]
. Let us take

\mathcal{B}_{C_{i}}=\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{r}\}\text{ and }N_{G}(C_{i})=\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{r},w_{r+1},\ldots,w_{s}\}

for some
s\geq r
. Then
J_{i}=\mathbf{x}_{C_{i}}\langle w_{1},\ldots,w_{r}\rangle
.

Claim 2:
For each
i\in[l]
, if
\mathcal{B}_{C_{i}}\neq\emptyset
, then
\mathrm{pd}(R/J_{i}\cap K_{i})\leq\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G))-1
.

Proof of the Claim 2.
We first aim to show that

(3)

\displaystyle J_{i}\cap K_{i}=\mathbf{x}_{C_{i}}\langle w_{m}w_{n}\mid 1\leq m<n\leq r\rangle+\mathbf{x}_{C_{i}}\langle w_{m}w_{n}\mid m\in[r],r+1\leq n\leq s\rangle.

Indeed, by
Lemma

3.2
, for each
1\leq m<n\leq r
,
G[(C_{i}\setminus\{x\})\cup\{w_{m},w_{n}\}]
is a connected subgraph of
G
. Thus
\mathbf{x}_{C_{i}}w_{m}w_{n}=\mathrm{lcm}(w_{m}\mathbf{x}_{C_{i}},w_{m}w_{n}\mathbf{x}_{C_{i}\setminus\{x\})}
, where
w_{m}\mathbf{x}_{C_{i}}\in J_{i}
, and
w_{m}w_{n}\mathbf{x}_{C_{i}\setminus\{x\}}\in K_{i}
. Similarly, if
m\in[r]
and
r+1\leq n\leq s
, then by
Lemma

3.2
,
G[(C_{i}\setminus\{x\})\cup\{w_{m},w_{n}\}]
is also connected. Thus, if
M
denotes the right-hand side of
Equation

3
, then
M\subseteq J_{i}\cap K_{i}
. Conversely, if
A\subseteq V(G)
such that
C_{i}\nsubseteq A
,
|A|=t
, and
G[A]
is connected, then
\{w_{m},w_{n}\}\subseteq A
for some
w_{m},w_{n}\in N_{G}(C_{i})
. Thus
J_{i}\cap K_{i}\subseteq M
and consequently,

\mathrm{pd}(R/J_{i}\cap K_{i})=\mathrm{pd}(R/I(G^{\prime})),

where
I(G^{\prime})
is the edge ideal of the graph
G^{\prime}
with

\displaystyle V(G^{\prime})

\displaystyle=N_{G}(C_{i}),

\displaystyle E(G^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\{\{w_{m},w_{n}\},\{w_{p},w_{q}\}\mid 1\leq m<n\leq r,p\in[r],r+1\leq q\leq s\}.

It is easy to observe that the complement of
G^{\prime}
is a disconnected graph, and thus, by using
[
18
, Theorem 4.2.6]
we have
\mathrm{pd}(R/J_{i}\cap K_{i})=|N_{G}(C_{i})|-1
. This completes the proof of Claim 2 since
\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G))\geq|N_{G}(C_{i})|
, by
Proposition

4.3
.

Thus, for each
i\in[k]
we observe that if
\mathcal{B}_{C_{i}}\neq\emptyset
, then
\mathrm{pd}(R/J_{i}\cap K_{i})\leq\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G))-1
. Hence, we are in the same situation as in Subcase-I(B). Proceeding as before, we see that
\mathrm{pd}(R/J_{i}+K_{i})\leq\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G))
for each
i\in[k]
, and in particular,

\mathrm{pd}(R/J_{t}(G))=\mathrm{pd}(R/J_{1}+K_{1})\leq\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G)).

This completes the proof of the Theorem.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

Note that
\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G))
is the same as the maximum cardinality of a minimal vertex cover of
\mathcal{H}(G,t)
. Thus, by
Theorem

4.5
, since the projective dimension of
t
-connected ideals of chordal graphs can be expressed in terms of some combinatorial invariant of the graph, it follows that in this case the projective dimension is independent of the characteristic of the base field.

Remark 4.8
.

In this context, one should note that if
I(\mathcal{H})
is a sequentially Cohen-Macaulay edge ideal of a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
, then
\mathrm{pd}(R/I(\mathcal{H}))=\mathrm{bight}(I(\mathcal{H}))

[
21
, Corollary 3.33]
. Also, it is well-known that if
G
is chordal, then
I(G)
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay. Now, due to these facts and
Theorem

4.5
, one can ask whether, for a chordal graph
G
and
t\geq 3
,
J_{t}(G)
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay or not. However, the answer to this question is negative (see
[
1
, Proposition 4.3]
). Moreover, a natural question that arises from this discussion is the following:

Question 4.9
.

If
I(G)
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay, then for all
t\geq 3
do we have
\mathrm{pd}(R/J_{t}(G))=\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G))
?

Although this happens in the case of chordal graphs, this question has a negative answer in general. For example, if we consider the cycle
C_{5}
of length
5
, then
I(C_{5})
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay but
\mathrm{pd}(R/I_{3}(C_{5}))=3>2=\mathrm{bight}(I_{3}(C_{5}))
.

Next, as a corollary of
Theorem

4.5
, we partially generalize a celebrated result of Herzog–Hibi–Zheng
[
17
]
, which establishes the equivalence between the Cohen–Macaulay and the unmixed properties for edge ideals of chordal graphs.

Corollary 4.10
.

Let
G
be a chordal graph and
t\geq 2
be an integer. Then
J_{t}(G)
is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
J_{t}(G)
is unmixed.

Proof.

The proof follows from
Theorem

4.5
and the Auslander-Buchsbaum formula.
∎

Note that in
[
17
]
, the authors provided an explicit combinatorial characterization of Cohen–Macaulay chordal graphs and, based on this, determined the Gorenstein chordal graphs, which turn out to be precisely disjoint unions of edges.

J_{t}(G_{1})=J_{t}(G_{2})

, while

and

Interestingly, there is a one-to-one correspondence between finite simple graphs and their edge ideals. In view of this, it is challenging to describe the combinatorial structure of chordal graphs whose
t
-connected ideal is Cohen–Macaulay for a fixed
t\geq 3
. Furthermore, it would be interesting to investigate whether the complete intersection property and the Gorenstein property coincide for

-connected ideals of chordal graphs, analogous to the case of edge ideals of chordal graphs.

5.
Concluding Remarks

In this section, by
hypergraphs
\mathcal{H}(G,t)
induced from a graph

G
, we mean a class of
t
-uniform hypergraphs for which
\mathcal{H}(G,2)=G
. In other words, the edge ideal
I(\mathcal{H}(G,t))
can be viewed as a higher degree generalization of
I(G)
. In this article, we have shown in
Theorem

3.6
and
4.5
that if
I(\mathcal{H}(G,t))
corresponds the ideal
J_{t}(G)
of a chordal graph
G
, then
\mathrm{reg}(R/I(\mathcal{H}(G,t)))=(t-1)\nu(\mathcal{H}(G,t))
and
\mathrm{pd}(R/I(\mathcal{H}(G,t)))=\mathrm{bight}(I(\mathcal{H}(G,t)))
. Also, it follows from
[
9
, Theorem 3.12]
that if the complement of
G
is chordal, then
I(\mathcal{H}(G,t))
has a linear resolution. In view of this, the following question arises naturally, which nicely extends the edge ideals to a higher degree from the perspective of chordal graphs.

Question 5.1
.

What type of
t
-uniform hypergraphs
\mathcal{H}(G,t)
induced from a graph
G
satisfy the following three conditions simultaneously for all
t\geq 2
:

(i)

\mathrm{reg}(R/I(\mathcal{H}(G,t)))=(t-1)\nu(\mathcal{H}(G,t))
when
G
is chordal,

(ii)

\mathrm{pd}(R/I(\mathcal{H}(G,t)))=\mathrm{bight}(I(\mathcal{H}(G,t)))
when
G
is chordal,

(iii)

I(\mathcal{H}(G,t))
has a linear resolution when the complement of
G
is chordal.

First, one may think of answering the above question for the existing classes of edge ideals of
t
-uniform hypergraphs induced from a graph, such as the
t
-path ideals and the
t
-clique ideals of graphs. Note that the
t
-path ideals fail to satisfy conditions (i) and (ii) of the above question (see
[
8
, Theorem 5.3, 5.8]
). However, to the best of our knowledge, it is still not known whether
t
-path ideals satisfy condition (iii) or not.

Next, let us consider the
t
-clique ideal of a graph
[
20
, Definition 3.1]
. Then condition (iii) of the above question holds true
[
20
, Corollary 3.4]
. We do not know about the condition (ii). However, condition (i) is not true for
t
-clique ideals, which follows from the following example.

Example 5.2
.

Let
G_{t,r}=G_{1}\cup\cdots\cup G_{r+1}
be a graph with
G_{i}\simeq K_{t}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,r+1\}
and there is a vertex
x\in V(G_{t,r})
such that
V(G_{i})\cap V(G_{j})=\{x\}
for all distinct
i
and
j
. Let
\mathcal{H}(G_{t,r},t)
be the corresponding hypergraph of the
t
-clique ideal of
G_{t,r}
. Then we have

I(\mathcal{H}(G_{t,r},t))=x\langle\mathbf{x}_{V(G_{i})\setminus\{x\}}\mid 1\leq i\leq r+1\rangle.

In this case, one can observe that
\mathrm{reg}(R/I(\mathcal{H}(G_{t,r},t)))=(t-2)(r+1)+1
, whereas the induced matching number of
\mathcal{H}(G_{t,r},t)
is
1
. Therefore, we have

\mathrm{reg}(R/I(\mathcal{H}(G_{t,r},t)))-(t-1)\nu(\mathcal{H}(G_{t,r},t))=(t-2)r.

In other words, the regularity can be arbitrarily larger than the general lower bound for any given
t>2
.

Moving on, in the case of edge ideals of graphs, there are several classes of graphs other than the chordal one for which the equalities
\mathrm{reg}(R/I(G))=\nu(G)
(see
[
3
, Theorem 14]
) and
\mathrm{pd}(R/I(G))=\mathrm{bight}(I(G))
(for instance, sequentially Cohen-Macaulay edge ideals, etc.) hold. In this article, we have extended the above formulas in
Theorem

3.6
and
4.5
for the ideal
J_{t}(G)
of a chordal graph
G
. Thus, the following question naturally arises in this context.

Question 5.3
.

Find those classes of graph
G
for which
\mathrm{reg}(R/J_{t}(G))=(t-1)\nu_{t}(G)
and
\mathrm{pd}(R/J_{t}(G))=\mathrm{bight}(J_{t}(G))
for all
t\geq 2
, where
J_{t}(G)
denotes the Stanley-Reisner ideal of
\mathrm{Ind}_{t}(G)
.

Remark 5.4
.

In
[
2
, Theorem 6.2]
it was shown that if
G
is ‘gap-free and
t
-claw free’, then
J_{t}(G)
has linear quotients, and thus has linear resolution. In this case, one can see that
\nu_{t}(G)=1
, and thus gap-free and
t
-claw free graphs are an example of the class of graphs where the regularity formula in Question 5.3 is satisfied.

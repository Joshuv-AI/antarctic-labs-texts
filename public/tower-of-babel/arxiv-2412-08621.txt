The separating Noether number of small groups
Mátyás Domokos, Barna Schefler

Abstract
. The separating Noether number of a finite group is the minimal positive integer d d such that for any finite dimensional complex linear representation of the group, any two dictinct orbits can be distinguished by a polynomial invariant of degree at most d d . In the present paper its exact value is determined for all groups of order less than 32 32 , for all finite groups with a cyclic subgroup of index at most 2 2 , and for the direct product of a dihedral group with the 2 2 -element group. The results show in particular that unlike the ordinary Noether number, the separating Noether number of a non-abelian finite group may well be equal to the separating Noether number of a proper direct factor of the group. Most of the results are proved for the case of a general (possibly finite) base field containing an element whose multiplicative order equals the size of the group.

For a finite dimensional vector space

Let

-module

Sometimes we write

The action of

A subset

The result mentioned before Definition

1.1.
Degree bounds

Endow

with the standard grading, so the variables

. We shall denote by

Obviously,

We set

The quantity

is called the

) because

due to E. Noether
[
41
]
(the inequality was extended to the non-modular case in
[
29
]
,
[
30
]
).
Its systematic study was initiated in
[
47
]
. The results of
[

]

Following

\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G,V):=\min\{d\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\mid K[V]^{G}_{\leq d}\text{ is a separating set}\},

We shall refer to the above quantities as the

As different orbits of a finite group can be separated by polynomial invariants, we have
the obvious inequality

\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G,V)\leq\beta(G,V)\qquad\text{ for any }V,

and hence

(1)

\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{K}(G)\leq\beta^{K}(G).

However, in the present paper our focus is on the non-modular setup, when the characteristic of the base field does not divide the order of

Question 1.2

To what extent is the change from ”generating sets” to ”separating sets” reflected in the corresponding degree bounds?

2.1.
Failure of strict monotonicity

For a normal subgroup
N
of
G
,
all representations of
G/N
can be viewed as a representation of
G
whose kernel contains
N
.
Therefore we have the obvious inequalities

(2)

\beta^{K}(G)\geq\beta^{K}(G/N)\text{ and }\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{K}(G)\geq\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{K}(G/N).

Moreover, both the Noether number and the separating Noether number are monotone for subgroups as well;
that is,
for any subgroup
H
of a finite group
G
we have

(3)

\beta^{K}(G)\geq\beta^{K}(H)

(see
[
47
]
) and

(4)

\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{K}(G)\geq\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{K}(H)

(see
[
37
, Theorem B]
).
The inequality (
3
) was sharpened in

[
13
, Theorem 1.2]
,
where the following strict monotonicity of the Noether number was proved:

(5)

\displaystyle\beta^{K}(G)>\beta^{K}(H)\text{ for any subgroup }H\neq G;

\displaystyle\beta^{K}(G)>\beta^{K}(G/N)\text{ for any normal subgroup }N\neq\{1_{G}\}\text{ of }G.

The results of Theorem
2.1

and
Theorem
2.3

show that the analogues of (
5
) (i.e. strict monotonicity) does not hold for the separating Noether number.
Indeed, for
K
as in the cited theorems, we have

\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{K}(\mathrm{Dic}_{8})=6=\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{K}(\mathrm{Dic}_{8}\times\mathrm{C}_{2}),

and
\mathrm{Dic}_{8}\times\mathrm{C}_{2}
has a proper direct factor isomorphic
to
\mathrm{Dic}_{8}
.
Another similar example is

\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{K}(\mathrm{A}_{4})=6=\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{K}(\mathrm{A}_{4}\times\mathrm{C}_{2}).

Furthermore,

\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{K}(\mathrm{S}_{3}\times\mathrm{C}_{3})=6=\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{K}(\mathrm{C}_{6}),

and the group
\mathrm{S}_{3}\times\mathrm{C}_{3}

has a subgroup isomorphic to
\mathrm{C}_{6}
.
Similar examples exist among the abelian groups.
For example, we have

\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{K}(\mathrm{C}_{6}\oplus\mathrm{C}_{6}\oplus\mathrm{C}_{6})=12=\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{K}(\mathrm{C}_{6}\oplus\mathrm{C}_{6}\oplus\mathrm{C}_{3})

by
[
45
, Theorem 6.1]
.

2.2.
Comments on the methods used

We make an essential use of the
known results on exact values of the Noether numbers
(as summarized in
[
15
]
) of
the groups considered here. In particular, in each
case when the Noether number and the separating Noether number
happen to coincide, we just need to come up with a representation and
two points with distinct orbits that can not be separated by
invariants of strictly smaller degree than the Noether number.
In all such cases it turns out that rather small
dimensional representations suffice to provide such examples.
On the other hand, to settle the cases when the separating
Noether number is strictly smaller than the Noether number requires
elaborate work:
we need a thorough analysis of
concrete representations, discussions of stabilizers,
group automorphisms,
construction of
invariants and relative invariants, as well as
several ad hoc ideas that help to undertand the set of solutions of systems of polynomial equations. What makes these computations
feasible is that by general principles (see the notion of Helly dimension
and Lemma
3.7
below) it is sufficient to deal with representations having a small
number of irreducible summands.

3.1.
Dependence on the base field of
\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{K}(G)

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
K
be a subfield of a field
L
, and let
V_{K}
be a
G
-module.
Write
V_{L}
for the
G
-module
L\otimes_{K}V
over
L
.

(i)

For any non-negative integer
d
, the
L
-vector space

L[V_{L}]^{G}_{d}
is spanned by its subset
K[V_{K}]^{G}_{d}
.

(ii)

We have the inequality

\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G,V_{K})\leq\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G,V_{L})
.

(iii)

We have the inequality

\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{K}(G)\leq\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{L}(G)
.

Proof.

(i) It is well known, and follows from the fact that the space of solutions over
L
of
a system of homogeneous linear equations with coefficients in
K
is spanned by the
subset of solutions over
K
.

(ii) Take any
v,v^{\prime}\in V_{K}\subseteq V_{L}
with
G\cdot v\neq G\cdot v^{\prime}
. Then there exists an

f\in L[V_{L}]^{G}_{d}
with
d\leq\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G,V_{L})
such that
f(v)\neq f(v^{\prime})
.
It follows by (i) that there exists an
h\in K[V]^{G}_{d}
with
h(v)\neq h(v^{\prime})
.
This clearly shows the desired inequality.

(iii) is an immediate consequence of (ii).
∎

\displaystyle\text{whose multiplicative order

3.3.
Direct sum decompositions and multigradings

Assume that we have a
G
-module direct sum decomposition

(8)

V=V_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{k}.

View the dual space
V_{i}^{*}
as the subspace of
V^{*}
consisting of the linear
forms vanishing on
V_{j}
for
j\neq i
.
Then
V^{*}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}V_{i}^{*}
.
Choose a basis
x_{i}^{(j)}
(
i=1,\dots,\dim(V_{j})
) in
V_{i}^{*}
, then
our set of variables in
K[V]
is
\{x_{i}^{(j)}\mid\ j=1,\dots,k;\ i=1,\dots,\dim(V_{j})\}
.
Associated to the decomposition (
8
) there is an
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{k}
-grading on
K[V]
: the multihomogeneous component of multidegree
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k})
in
K[V]
is spanned by the monomials having degree
\alpha_{j}
in the variables belonging to
V_{j}^{*}
for each
j=1,\dots,k
.
Clearly, the
G
-action on
K[V]
preserves this multigrading, and thus

K[V]^{G}
is spanned by multihomogeneous elements.
In the sequel whenever we mention a ‘multigrading’ not explicitly defined,
this should refer to the multigrading that comes from a direct sum decomposition as in (
8
), which should be clear from the context.
A typical way by which we shall exploit the multigrading is captured by the following
obvious statement:

Lemma 3.5
.

We have
\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{K}(G)\geq d
if and only if there exists a
G
-module

V=V_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{k}
,

v,v^{\prime}\in V
,
h\in K[V]^{G}
with
h(v)\neq h(v^{\prime})
, such that for any multihomogeneous
invariant
f\in K[V]^{G}
with
\deg(f)<d
we have
f(v)=f(v^{\prime})
.

Note that the above multigrading on
K[V]
is induced by the following
action of the torus
T:=K^{\times}\times\cdots\times K^{\times}
(
k
direct factors)
on
V
: for
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k})\in T
and
v=(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})\in V
set

\lambda\cdot v:=(\lambda_{1}v_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k}v_{k})
.
This action commutes with the
G
-action on
V
.
Consequently, for
v,v^{\prime}\in V
we have that

(9)

G\cdot v=G\cdot v^{\prime}\text{ if and only if }G\cdot(\lambda v)=G\cdot(\lambda v^{\prime}).

Later in the text when we say that ’after rescaling, we may assume that
v
has some special form’, then we mean
that we replace
v
by an appropriate element in its
T
-orbit,
and we refer to (
9
).

3.4.
Reduction to multiplicity-free representations

We record a consequence of the results of
[
23
]
and

:

.

Let

be a complete irredundant list of representatives of the isomorphism classes of simple

An arbitrary

-module

is isomorphic to

3.5.

Lemma

can be improved for groups whose Helly dimension is

having the following property: any set of cosets in

with empty intersection contains a subset of at most
k
cosets with empty intersection
(see
[

, Section 4]
).

Lemma 3.7
.

Under the assumptions and notation of Lemma
3.6
,
there exists a
k\leq\kappa(G)
and

with

\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{K}(G)=\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G,V_{j_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{j_{k}})

Proof.

This follows from Lemma

by the same argument as the proof of
[

, Lemma 4.1]

(formulated in a bit more general setup).
∎

In view of Lemma
3.7
, it is helpful to find upper bounds on the Helly dimension. By
[
17
, Lemma 4.2]
we have the inequality

(10)

\kappa(G)\leq\mu(G)+1,

where

stands for the maximal size of an intersection independent set of subgroups of

. Recall that a set of subgroups of

is

if none contains the intersection of the others.

Lemma 3.8
.

If a set

of intersection independent subgroups in a group contains a cyclic subgroup of prime power order, then

.

Proof.

Let
S
be an intersection independent set of subgroups of a group

with

and

with

3.7.
Relative invariants and the Hilbert ideal

Given a
G
-module
V
, denote by

the sum of the positive degree homogeneous components of
K[V]^{G}
. A homogeneous invariant

f\in K[V]^{G}
is said to be
indecomposable
if

f\notin(K[V]^{G}_{+})^{2}
. Clearly,
K[V]^{G}
is generated by
the positive degree homogeneous indecomposable invariants.
The so-called
Hilbert ideal

\mathcal{H}(G,V)

is the ideal in the polynomial algebra
K[V]
generated by the positive degree homogeneous
G
-invariants.
The Hilbert ideal plays an essential role in studying generators of the algebra of invariants, because
a positive degree homogeneous invariant
f\in K[V]^{G}
is
indecomposable if and only if
f
is not contained in
the ideal
\mathcal{H}(G,V)K[V]_{+}
of
K[V]
.
More precisely, a set of
homogeneous invariants form a minimal generating set of
K[V]^{G}
if and only if it gives a vector space basis in a vector space direct complement of

(K[V]^{G}_{+})^{2}
in
K[V]^{G}_{+}
(the sum of the homogeneous components of
K[V]^{G}
of positive degree).

An element
f\in K[V]
is a
relative invariant of weight
\chi\in\widehat{G}
if

g\cdot f=\chi(g^{-1})f
for all
g\in G

(so
f(gv)=\chi(g)f(v)
for all
g\in G
and
v\in V
). We set

K[V]^{G,\chi}:=\{f\in K[V]\mid f\text{ is a relative invariant of weight }\chi\}.

For example, if
V
is as in (
11
), then
the variable
t_{\chi}
is a relative invariant of weight
\chi
.
Moreover, for a sequence
\chi^{(1)},\dots,\chi^{(k)}
of elements of
\widehat{G}
,

t_{\chi^{(1)}}\cdots t_{\chi^{(k)}}
is an indecomposable
G
-invariant if and only if

\chi^{(1)}\cdots\chi^{(k)}=1\in\widehat{G}

and there is no

\ell<k
,
1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{\ell}\leq k
with
\chi^{(i_{1})}\cdots\chi^{(i_{\ell})}=1\in\widehat{G}

(we say in this case that

\chi^{(1)},\dots,\chi^{(k)}
is an

\widehat{G}
, and we call
k
its

).
We say that
\chi^{(1)},\dots,\chi^{(k)}
is a
product-one free sequence over
\widehat{G}
if there is no
1\leq i_{1}<\dots<i_{\ell}\leq k
with

\chi^{(i_{1})}\cdots\chi^{(i_{\ell})}=1\in\widehat{G}
.
The
Davenport constant

\mathsf{D}(\widehat{G})
of the finite abelian group

G
is defined as the maximal length of an irreducible product-one sequence over
G
.
Clearly, the maximal possible length of a product-one free sequence is

\mathsf{D}(\widehat{G})-1
.
For more information about the Davenport constant and its relevance for
the invariant theory of abelian groups see for example
[
14
]
.

.

Theorem 8.14

Invariant theory for wreath products acting on superpolynomials
Trevor Karn, Victor Reiner

Abstract
. This paper considers a finite group G G acting linearly on the variables V V of a polynomial algebra, or an exterior algebra, or a superpolynomial algebra with both commuting and anticommuting variables. In this setting, the Hilbert series for the G G -invariant subalgebra turns out to determine the analogous Hilbert series for the wreath product P ⁡ [ G ] P[G] acting on V n V^{n} for any permutation group P P inside the symmetric group 𝔖 n {\mathfrak{S}}_{n} on n n letters. This leads to a structural result: one can collate the direct sum for all n n of the 𝔖 n ​ [ G ] {\mathfrak{S}}_{n}[G] -invariant subalgebras to form a graded ring via an external shuffle product, whose structure turns out to be a superpolynomial algebra generated by the G G -invariants. A parallel statement holds for the direct sum of all 𝔖 n ​ [ G ] {\mathfrak{S}}_{n}[G] -antiinvariants, which forms a graded ring via an external signed shuffle product, isomorphic to the superexterior algebra generated by the G G -invariants.

Assume that the field
{\mathbf{k}}
is not of characteristic two, and consider a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-graded
{\mathbf{k}}
-vector space
V=V_{\bar{0}}\oplus V_{\bar{1}}

V_{\bar{1}}
. We think of

as commuting (“bosonic”) polynomial variables,
and the
\{\theta_{j}\}
as anticommuting (“fermionic”) exterior variables, in the
superpolynomial algebra
having these various descriptions:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}V

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Sym}(V_{\bar{0}})\otimes\wedge(V_{\bar{1}})

\displaystyle\cong{\mathbf{k}}[x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{r_{0}}]\otimes\wedge(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\ldots,\theta_{r_{1}})

\displaystyle\cong T(V)/I

where
T(V)=\bigoplus_{d=0}^{\infty}T^{d}(V)

with
T^{d}(V)=V^{\otimes d}
the
tensor algebra
, and
I
is the two-sided ideal of
T(V)
generated by
the elements

a\otimes b-(-1)^{\bar{i}\cdot\bar{j}}\,b\otimes a

where

a\in V_{\bar{i}}
and
b\in V_{\bar{j}}

for
\bar{i},\bar{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
.

Say that a finite subgroup
G
of
GL(V)

preserves the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-grading
if
G\subseteq{GL(V_{\bar{0}})\times GL(V_{\bar{1}})}
.
The algebra
\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}V
additionally
has a grading by
{\mathbb{Z}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}={\mathbb{Z}}^{2}

preserved by such groups
G
, defined as follows: for
(i,j)\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2}
with
{\mathbb{N}}=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
, let

(\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}V)_{ij}:=\operatorname{Sym}^{i}(V_{\bar{0}})\otimes\wedge^{j}(V_{\bar{1}})={\mathbf{k}}[x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{r_{0}}]_{i}\otimes\wedge^{j}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\ldots,\theta_{r_{1}}).

Thus the
G
-invariant subalgebra

(\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}V)^{G}
has a
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
-graded
Hilbert series
in
{\mathbb{Z}}[[q,u]]
defined by

(1)

\operatorname{H}((\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}V)^{G},q,u):=\sum_{(i,j)\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2}}a_{ij}q^{i}u^{j},\quad\text{ where }a_{ij}:=\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\left(\operatorname{Sym}^{i}(V_{\bar{0}})\otimes\wedge^{j}(V_{\bar{1}})\right)^{G}.

One can then let the wreath product
G\wr{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}={\mathfrak{S}}_{n}[G]={\mathfrak{S}}_{n}\ltimes G^{n}
act on
V^{n}
, with

G^{n}
acting independently in each summand of
V^{n}
and the subgroup
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
permuting the summands; see Section
4
for more details.
In this setting, any permutation subgroup
P
of
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
inherits an action on
V^{n}
,
and on
\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}V^{n}
. One can ask for
the
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
-graded Hilbert series of either the
P[G]
-invariants
(\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}V^{n})^{P[G]}
,
or the
P[G]
-antiinvariants
(\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}V^{n})^{P[G],\operatorname{sgn}}
, where
\operatorname{sgn}
is this composite homomorphism:

\operatorname{sgn}:P[G]\twoheadrightarrow P\hookrightarrow{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}\overset{\operatorname{sgn}}{\longrightarrow}\{\pm 1\}.

It turns out that these Hilbert series are
plethystic evaluations
of
the
cycle index
and
signed cycle index
of
P
,
which are these
elements of the
ring of symmetric functions

\Lambda=\Lambda_{\mathbb{Q}}={\mathbb{Q}}[p_{1},p_{2},\ldots]
:

(2)

\displaystyle Z_{P}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{|P|}\sum_{\sigma\in P}p_{\lambda(\sigma)}=\frac{1}{|P|}\sum_{\sigma\in P}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{ cycles }\\
C\text{ of }\sigma\end{subarray}}p_{|C|},

(3)

\displaystyle Z^{\operatorname{sgn}}_{P}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{|P|}\sum_{\sigma\in P}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\cdot p_{\lambda(\sigma)}=\frac{1}{|P|}\sum_{\sigma\in P}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{ cycles }\\
C\text{ of }\sigma\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|C|-1}p_{|C|},

where
\lambda(\sigma)
is the
cycle type partition
of
\sigma
.
For a fixed power
series
f(q,u)\in{\mathbb{Q}}[[q,u]]
,
the operation of
plethystic substitution

h\mapsto h[f(q,u)]
is the unique
homomorphism
\Lambda={\mathbb{Q}}[p_{1},p_{2},\ldots]\rightarrow{\mathbb{Q}}[[q,u]]
sending
p_{r}\mapsto f(q^{r},u^{r})
for
r=1,2,\ldots
.
After deriving a straightforward extension of
Molien’s Theorem to
\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}V
in Section
3
, the following
result is proven
in
Section
4
.

Theorem 1.1
.

In the above setting, when
|G|,|P|
lie in
{\mathbf{k}}^{\times}
, then

(4)

\displaystyle\operatorname{H}((\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}V^{n})^{P[G]},q,-u)

\displaystyle=Z_{P}\left[\,\operatorname{H}((\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}V)^{G},q,-u)\,\right]

(5)

\displaystyle\operatorname{H}((\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}V^{n})^{P[G],\operatorname{sgn}},q,-u)

\displaystyle=Z^{\operatorname{sgn}}_{P}\left[\,\operatorname{H}((\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}V)^{G},q,-u)\,\right].

Section
5

compiles the cases where
P={\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
for
n=0,1,2,\ldots
into generating functions as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

In the above setting, when
\operatorname{char}{{\mathbf{k}}}=0
, one has these generating functions:

(6)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n}\operatorname{H}((\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}V^{n})^{{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}[G]},q,u)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}(i,j)\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2}:\\
j\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}(1+tq^{i}u^{j})^{a_{ij}}\bigg/\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}(i,j)\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2}:\\
j\text{ even}\end{subarray}}(1-tq^{i}u^{j})^{a_{ij}}

(7)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n}\operatorname{H}((\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}V^{n})^{{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}[G],\operatorname{sgn}},q,u)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}(i,j)\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2}:\\
j\text{ even}\end{subarray}}(1+tq^{i}u^{j})^{a_{ij}}\bigg/\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}(i,j)\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2}:\\
j\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}(1-tq^{i}u^{j})^{a_{ij}}.

The form of the two products on the right sides in
(
6

7

It is natural to view

(\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}V)^{G}
as a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-graded

{\mathbf{k}}
-vector space

(8)

U:=(\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}V)^{G}=\underbrace{\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}(i,j)\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2}:\\

Comparing this with the right side of (

To similarly interpret the right side of (

for
\bar{i},\bar{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
.
One then sees that product on the right side of (

{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}
-graded Hilbert series

The form of the left sides of (

-vector spaces:

(9)

\displaystyle R_{G}

\displaystyle:=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}(R_{G})_{n}\text{ where }(R_{G})_{n}:=(\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}V^{n})^{{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}[G]},

(10)

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{(n,i,j)\in{\mathbb{N}}^{3}}(R_{G})_{nij}\text{ where }(R_{G})_{nij}:=(\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}V^{n})_{ij}^{{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}[G]},

(11)

\displaystyle R^{\operatorname{sgn}}_{G}

\displaystyle:=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}(R^{\operatorname{sgn}}_{G})_{n}\text{ where }(R^{\operatorname{sgn}}_{G})_{n}:=(\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}V^{n})^{{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}[G],\operatorname{sgn}}

(12)

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{(n,i,j)\in{\mathbb{N}}^{3}}(R^{\operatorname{sgn}}_{G})_{nij}\text{ where }(R_{G})_{nij}:=(\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}V^{n})_{ij}^{{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}[G],\operatorname{sgn}}.

.

In the context of Theorem

\displaystyle\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}\left((\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}V)^{G}\right)

\displaystyle\wedge_{\pm}\left((\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}V)^{G}\right)

Example 1.4
.

An example which arose in work of the first author
[
14
]
concerns the anticommutative case, where

V=V_{\bar{1}}={\mathbf{k}}^{r}
, so that
\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}V=\wedge V=\wedge(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{r})
.
Consider the action of

G={\mathfrak{S}}_{\alpha}:={\mathfrak{S}}_{\alpha_{1}}\times\cdots\times{\mathfrak{S}}_{\alpha_{\ell}},

a Young subgroup of the symmetric group
{\mathfrak{S}}_{r}
, indexed by any composition

\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{\ell})
of
r
.
In examining the
{\mathfrak{S}}_{\alpha}
-invariants

(\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}V)^{{\mathfrak{S}}_{\alpha}}=(\wedge V)^{{\mathfrak{S}}_{\alpha}}
,
and more generally,

(\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}V^{n})^{{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}[{\mathfrak{S}}_{\alpha}]}=(\wedge V^{n})^{{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}[{\mathfrak{S}}_{\alpha}]},

it was observed that their Hilbert series
seemed to depend only on the length
\ell
of
\alpha
, not on

\alpha
itself.

This can be explained as follows. When the full symmetric group
{\mathfrak{S}}_{r}
acts on

\wedge V=\wedge(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{r})
,
one readily sees that the

{\mathfrak{S}}_{r}
-invariants
(\wedge V)^{{\mathfrak{S}}_{r}}
are
{\mathbf{k}}
-spanned by the two elements

1\in\wedge^{0}V={\mathbf{k}}
and
\theta_{1}+\cdots+\theta_{r}\in\wedge^{1}V=V
.
This lets one easily analyze the action of
{\mathfrak{S}}_{\alpha}
acting on
\wedge V
.
The direct sum decomposition

V=V^{\alpha_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus V^{\alpha_{\ell}}
gives rise to a graded tensor product decomposition

\wedge V\cong\wedge V^{\alpha_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\wedge V^{\alpha_{\ell}}

compatible with the action of
{\mathfrak{S}}_{\alpha}={\mathfrak{S}}_{\alpha_{1}}\times\cdots\times{\mathfrak{S}}_{\alpha_{\ell}}
on each tensor factor.
This gives an isomorphism

\displaystyle(\wedge V)^{{\mathfrak{S}}_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\cong(\wedge V^{\alpha_{1}})^{{\mathfrak{S}}_{\alpha_{1}}}\otimes\cdots\otimes(\wedge V^{\alpha_{\ell}})^{{\mathfrak{S}}_{\alpha_{\ell}}}\cong\wedge(\theta^{(1)},\ldots,\theta^{(\ell)}),

where
\theta^{(i)}
is the sum of
the
\alpha_{i}
exterior variables in degree one of
\wedge V^{\alpha_{i}}
.
Consequently,

\operatorname{H}((\wedge V)^{{\mathfrak{S}}_{\alpha}},u)=\operatorname{H}\left(\wedge(\theta^{(1)},\ldots,\theta^{(\ell)}),u\right)=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\binom{\ell}{j}u^{j},

depending only upon
\ell
. Using this,
Theorem
1.2
then compiles the

{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}[{\mathfrak{S}}_{\alpha}]
-invariants Hilbert series:

(15)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n}\operatorname{H}((\wedge V^{n})^{{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}[{\mathfrak{S}}_{\alpha}]},u)=\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq\ell\\
j\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}(1+tu^{j})^{\binom{\ell}{j}}\bigg/\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j\leq\ell\\
j\text{ even}\end{subarray}}(1-tu^{j})^{\binom{\ell}{j}}.

The authors are grateful to J.-Y. Thibon
for deriving (
15
)
via plethystic calculation (personal communication, April 2024),
when asked if the left side had a nice generating function.

Example 1.5
.

The case where
G=\{1_{V}\}
acts trivially on

V=V_{\bar{0}}\oplus V_{\bar{1}}

is already of some interest.
Given the
{\mathbf{k}}
-bases
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{r_{0}}

for
V_{\bar{0}}

and
\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\ldots,\theta_{r_{1}}

for
V_{\bar{1}}
,
one can index
{\mathbf{k}}
-bases for
V^{n}=V_{\bar{0}}^{n}\oplus V_{\bar{1}}^{n}

with
n\times r_{0}
and
n\times r_{1}
matrices
X_{n,r_{0}},\Theta_{n,r_{0}}
of variables, containing
r_{0}
sets of
n
commuting variables and
r_{1}
sets of
n
anticommuting variables:

(16)

X_{n,r_{0}}=\left[\begin{matrix}x_{11}&\cdots&x_{1r_{0}}\\
x_{21}&\cdots&x_{2r_{0}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
x_{n1}&\cdots&x_{nr_{0}}\\
\end{matrix}\right],\quad\Theta_{n,r_{0}}=\left[\begin{matrix}\theta_{11}&\cdots&\theta_{1r_{1}}\\
\theta_{21}&\cdots&\theta_{2r_{1}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\theta_{n1}&\cdots&\theta_{nr_{1}}\\
\end{matrix}\right]

Then one can regard

\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}V^{n}=\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}(X_{n,r_{0}},\Theta_{n,r_{1}})

as the superpolynomial algebra on these commuting and anticommuting variable sets.
Here
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}(={\mathfrak{S}}_{n}[G])
acts on the matrix variable sets by permuting the row indices. The
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
-invariant subalgebra
\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}(X_{n,r_{0}},\Theta_{n,r_{1}})^{{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}}
is often called
the ring of diagonally symmetric functions in several variable sets of size
n
, with
r_{0}
sets of bosonic variables and

r_{1}
sets of fermionic variables; see Bergeron
[
3
,
4
]
, Orellana and Zabrocki
[
20
, §4]
.

Note that when
n=1
, one has for
\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}V=\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r_{0}},\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{r_{1}})
that

\operatorname{H}(\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}V,q,u)=\frac{(1+u)^{r_{1}}}{(1-q)^{r_{0}}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\geq 0,\\
0\leq j\leq r_{1}\end{subarray}}\underbrace{\left(\kern-1.99997pt\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{r_0}{i}\right)\kern-1.99997pt\right)\binom{r_{1}}{j}}_{a_{ij}}q^{i}u^{j},

where
\left(\kern-1.99997pt\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{r_0}{i}\right)\kern-1.99997pt\right):=\binom{r_{0}+i-1}{i}
counts
i
-multisubsets of
\{1,2,\ldots,r_{0}\}
. Thus Theorem
1.2
implies here that

(17)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n}\operatorname{H}(\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}(X_{n,r_{0}},\Theta_{n,r_{1}})^{{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}},q,u)

\displaystyle=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\geq 0,\\
1\leq j\leq r_{1}\\
j\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}(1+tq^{i}u^{j})^{\left(\kern-1.63885pt\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{r_0}{i}\right)\kern-1.63885pt\right)\binom{r_{1}}{j}}\bigg/\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\geq 0,\\
0\leq j\leq r_{1}\\
j\text{ even}\end{subarray}}(1-tq^{i}u^{j})^{\left(\kern-1.63885pt\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{r_0}{i}\right)\kern-1.63885pt\right)\binom{r_{1}}{j}},

(18)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n}\operatorname{H}(\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}(X_{n,r_{0}},\Theta_{n,r_{1}})^{{\mathfrak{S}}_{n},\operatorname{sgn}},q,u)

\displaystyle=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\geq 0,\\
0\leq j\leq r_{1}\\
j\text{ even}\end{subarray}}(1+tq^{i}u^{j})^{\left(\kern-1.63885pt\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{r_0}{i}\right)\kern-1.63885pt\right)\binom{r_{1}}{j}}\bigg/\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\geq 0,\\
1\leq j\leq r_{1}\\
j\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}(1-tq^{i}u^{j})^{\left(\kern-1.63885pt\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{r_0}{i}\right)\kern-1.63885pt\right)\binom{r_{1}}{j}}.

It is worth noting that (
17
) is consistent with a basis for
\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}(X_{n,r_{0}},\Theta_{n,r_{1}})^{{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}}
constructed by Orellana and Zabrocki in
[
20
, Cor. 13]
; see also their excellent historical discussion.

Specializing to
r_{1}=0
,

{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}

\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}(V^{n})=\operatorname{Sym}[X_{n,r_{0}}]

r_{0}
sets of
n

). Here the

{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
-invariant subalgebra

is sometimes called the ring of multisymmetric or
MacMahon symmetric polynomials;
see, e.g., Gessel
[

, Part II]
, Olver and Shakiban
[

, §2]
, Vaccarino
[

]
.
The Hilbert series of

, Chap. 12]
.
One of motivations in the work of Solomon

]

Example 1.6
.

Specializing Example
1.5

further to

r_{0}=r_{1}=1
is still interesting. Here
one has

\displaystyle\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}V^{n}=\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}(X_{n,r_{0}},\Theta_{n,r_{1}})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n})=:\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}({\mathbf{x}},\theta)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Sym}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\otimes\wedge(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n}),

sometimes called the coordinate ring of superspace, or the ring of differential forms on
{\mathbf{k}}^{n}
with polynomial coefficients.
Here

{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
acts
diagonally permuting subscripts.
The diagonally symmetric functions

\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}({\mathbf{x}},\theta)^{{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}}

3.
Super Molien Theorem

As mentioned in the Introduction,
given a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-graded
{\mathbf{k}}
-vector

and

-algebra, as well as its

-grading. We wish to compute its

-graded

and more generally, for any group homomorphism/character
\chi:G\rightarrow{\mathbf{k}}^{\times}
, the
Hilbert series for the

{\mathbf{k}}
-subspace of

\chi
-relative invariants

(\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}V)^{G,\chi}

inside
\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}V
.
The following

straightforwardly generalizes the commutative case known as Molien’s Theorem
[
18
]
(see also
[
2
,
13
,
27
,
30
]
), along with Solomon’s computation
[
28
, §4]
when

and

is a finite group diagonally embedded
in

[

, §22-4]
, Smith
[

Proposition 3.1
.

If a finite group
G
respects the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-grading on
V=V_{\bar{0}}\oplus V_{\bar{1}}
, and
|G|\in{\mathbf{k}}^{\times}
, then

\operatorname{H}((\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}V)^{G},q,u)=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\frac{\det(1_{V_{\bar{1}}}+u\cdot g^{(1)})}{\det(1_{V_{\bar{0}}}-q\cdot g^{(0)})}.

More generally, for any homomorphism
\chi:G\rightarrow{\mathbf{k}}^{\times}
, one has

\operatorname{H}((\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}V)^{G,\chi},q,u)=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\chi(g^{-1})\frac{\det(1_{V_{\bar{1}}}+u\cdot g^{(1)})}{\det(1_{V_{\bar{0}}}-q\cdot g^{(0)})}.

Proof.

Since
|G|
lies in
{\mathbf{k}}^{\times}
, for any finite-dimensional
{\mathbf{k}}G
-module
U
,
one has that the idempotent
e^{\chi}_{G}:=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\chi(g^{-1})\cdot g
in
{\mathbf{k}}G
acts as a projection

e^{\chi}_{G}:U\twoheadrightarrow U^{G,\chi}
. Consequently

\dim_{\mathbf{k}}U^{G,\chi}=\operatorname{Tr}(e^{\chi}_{G}:U\rightarrow U)=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\chi(g^{-1})\cdot\operatorname{Tr}(g:U\rightarrow U).

Applying this to each bidegree
(i,j)
of
\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}V
and summing gives

(26)

\operatorname{H}(\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}V^{G,\chi},q,u)=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\chi(g^{-1})\sum_{(i,j)\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2}}q^{i}u^{j}\operatorname{Tr}(g:(\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}V)_{ij}\rightarrow(\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}V)_{ij}).

One can extend
{\mathbf{k}}
to a field
\overline{{\mathbf{k}}}
where
g^{(0)},g^{(1)}
have all of their eigenvalues

(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r_{0}}),(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{r_{1}})
, so that the action of
g^{(0)},g^{(1)}
on
V_{\bar{0}},V_{\bar{1}}
has a
\overline{{\mathbf{k}}}
-basis that is triangularized.
This also leads to bases of monomial tensors for each

(\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}V)_{ij}=\operatorname{Sym}^{i}(V_{\bar{0}})\otimes\wedge^{j}(V_{\bar{1}})

in which
g^{(0)},g^{(1)}
are again triangularized. This
gives a
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
-graded trace calculation, which combined with (
26

finishes the proof of the proposition:

\sum_{(i,j)\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2}}q^{i}u^{j}\operatorname{Tr}(g|_{(\operatorname{Sym}_{\pm}V)_{ij}})=\prod_{j=1}^{r_{1}}(1+\mu_{j}y)\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{r_{0}}(1+\lambda_{i}q+\lambda_{i}^{2}q^{2}+\cdots)=\frac{\det(1_{V_{\bar{1}}}+u\cdot g^{(1)})}{\det(1_{V_{\bar{0}}}-q\cdot g^{(0)})}.\qed

Note that the full symmetric group

has cycle index

Z_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}}=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}}p_{\lambda(\sigma)}=:h_{n}

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}(t)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n}Z^{\operatorname{sgn}}_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n}e_{n}=\exp\left(-\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{p_{r}}{r}(-t)^{r}\right)=\frac{1}{{\mathcal{H}}(-t)}.

1.1

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}(i,j)\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2}:\\

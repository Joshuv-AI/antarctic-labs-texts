Petrogenesis of Late Permian Mafic Rocks in the Western Guangxi Area, SW China: Implications for the Emeishan Mantle Plume Magmatism
Authors: Yu Zhang, Zhenhao Zhang, Yaqi Yuan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Mafic rocks in western Guangxi exhibit pronounced spatiotemporal correlation with the Emeishan Large Igneous Province (ELIP), providing critical constraints on its magmatic distribution, petrogenetic mechanisms, and evolutionary history. This study presents zircon U–Pb geochronology, whole-rock geochemistry, and Sr–Nd isotopic data for Tianlin mafic rocks from the outer zone of the ELIP. These rocks yield a concordant zircon U–Pb age of 258.3 Ma, contemporaneous with the main eruptive phase of the ELIP. Geochemically, they comprise a high-Ti series (TiO2 = 2.79–4.74 wt %; Ti/Y = 507–1229), exhibiting ocean-island basalt (OIB)-like chondrite-normalized REE patterns but subtle negative Nb–Ta–Zr-Hf anomalies on primitive mantle-normalized diagrams. Moderately enriched Sr–Nd isotopic signatures [(87Sr/86Sr)i = 0.705522–0.705891; εNd­(t) = +0.43 to +0.81] indicate derivation from an enriched mantle source. Integrating regional geology with these data, we propose that interaction between the Late Permian Emeishan mantle plume and Neoproterozoic oceanic slab generated the ELIP’s compositional diversity.

Introduction

Large igneous provinces (LIPs) are massive, rapidly emplaced accumulations of predominantly mafic rock, defined by volumes >0.1 Mkm3 and formation time scales of <5 Myr. Their intraplate setting distinguishes them from plate-boundary volcanism, implicating deep-seated mechanisms such as mantle plumes or lithospheric delamination. The Late Permian Emeishan Large Igneous Province (ELIP; ∼260 Ma), situated on the western margin of the Yangtze Craton, is a classic example, with extensive geophysical and geochemical evidence supporting a mantle plume origin. −

A defining characteristic of the ELIP is the presence of metasomatic, subduction-derived components within its mantle source. , While this indicates the incorporation of recycled crustal materiala feature common to many LIPssignificant debate persists regarding the origin and timing of this signature. Specifically, it remains unclear whether these components were inherited from Neoproterozoic subduction during the assembly of the Yangtze Craton − or were contributed by the roughly contemporaneous Paleo-Tethys oceanic system. − Resolving this ambiguity is crucial for elucidating the pre-existing structure and composition of the mantle that the Emeishan plume interacted with.

This study investigates newly identified Late Permian mafic intrusions in the Tianlin area of western Guangxi, a key tectonic zone on the southwestern margin of the Yangtze Craton. We present an integrated data set including zircon U–Pb geochronology, whole-rock geochemistry, and Sr–Nd isotope analyses. Our objectives are to (1) determine the precise emplacement age of these intrusions; (2) constrain their petrogenesis and mantle source characteristics; and (3) decipher the nature and origin of the subducted component in the mantle source. By integrating our findings with existing models, we aim to provide new constraints that refine the mantle plume framework for the ELIP.

Geological Background and Sample Descriptions

The ELIP covers an area exceeding 700,000 km2 and is dominated by continental flood basalts, with minor occurrences of picrite, trachyte, and rhyolite. Geochemically, the flood basalts are subdivided into high-Ti (TiO2 > 2.5 wt %, Ti/Y > 500) and low-Ti (TiO2 < 2.5 wt %, Ti/Y < 500) series, with the latter dominating the western part of the province and the former distributed more centrally and to the east. , Based on integrated biostratigraphic, sedimentological, and geochemical evidence, the ELIP is further divided into inner, intermediate, and outer zones, interpreted to reflect the variable thermal and dynamic influence of the mantle plume (Figure ). Picrites and mafic-ultramafic intrusions are exposed exclusively in the inner zone due to deeper erosional levels, whereas flood basalts are widespread across all three zones. ,

The ELIP is extensively distributed across Yunnan, Guizhou, and Sichuan provinces in southwestern China, with smaller outcrops in western Guangxi and northern Vietnam. Tectonically, western Guangxi lies within the Nanpanjiang Basina critical convergence zone of the South China, Indochina, and Simao blocksmarking a transitional domain between the Paleo-Tethyan and circum-Pacific tectonic regimes. The regional stratigraphy comprises Upper Devonian siliciclastic rocks, overlain by Carboniferous to Lower Permian carbonate units, and Upper Permian to Middle Triassic siliciclastic formations. Late Paleozoic mafic intrusions are widespread in this region, predominantly occurring as small layered intrusions, sills, and diabase dikes in the Tianlin, Bama, Longlin, and Baise areas. , Critically, field relationships show that these mafic rocks intrude the Mid-Permian Maokou Formation (predominantly carbonates) and are conformably overlain by the Late Permian Longtan Formation or Triassic sedimentary strata. , The area has undergone intense deformation from the Paleozoic to the present, characterized by NW-SE trending folds and fault systems.

The mafic rock samples examined in this study were collected from Tianlin County in the southeastern Nanpanjiang Basin (Figure ) (24°17′03.8″ N, 105°53′54.4″ E). The intrusion forms a thick, tabular body hosted in Permian limestone and is concordant with the regional tectonic trend. It displays a sharp and distinct contact with the country rock (Figure S1). Field observations confirmed the absence of any direct contact with overlying Triassic strata. The intrusion has a measured thickness of 30 m and an exposed strike length exceeding 1.5 km. All specimens were collected from fresh quarry exposures and display dark greenish-black, unweathered surfaces. Petrographic analysis indicates that the rock is predominantly composed of clinopyroxene (40–45%) and plagioclase (45–50%), with accessory opaque mineralschiefly ilmenite and titanomagnetiteconstituting approximately 5% (Figure S1). Plagioclase occurs as acicular to elongated lath-shaped crystals ranging from 0.3 to 1 mm in length. In contrast, clinopyroxene forms subhedral to anhedral granules that fill the interstices between the euhedral plagioclase laths (0.2–0.5 mm long), collectively defining a characteristic diabasic texture. The rock has undergone mild but pervasive alteration, evidenced by zoisitization, sericitization, chloritization, and kaolinization.

Analytical Method

Zircon U–Pb Dating

Zircon U–Pb dating was performed using laser ablation inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (LA-ICP-MS) at Wuhan SampleSolution Analytical Technology Co., Ltd. (Wuhan, China). Detailed operating conditions for the laser ablation system, the ICP-MS instrument, and data reduction followed those described by Zong et al. The laser spot size and frequency were set at 32 μm and 5 Hz, respectively. Zircon 91500 and NIST610 glass were used as external standards for U–Pb dating and trace element calibration, respectively. Each analysis included approximately 20–30 s of background acquisition followed by 50 s of sample data acquisition. Off-line data processing, including background and signal integration, time-drift correction, and quantitative calibration for trace elements and U–Pb dating, was conducted using the Excel-based software ICPMSDataCal. Concordia diagrams and weighted mean calculations were generated using Isoplot/Ex_ver3.

Whole-Rock Major and Trace Element and Sr–Nd Isotopic Compositions

Major and trace element analyses were carried out at the Wuhan Samplesolution Analytical Technology Co., Ltd. (Wuhan, China). Whole-rock major elements were measured by XRF (Primus II, Rigaku, Japan). The analytical precision of major elements is <1 wt %. Trace elements were analyzed using Agilent 7700e ICP-MS. The analytical precision was within 5 wt %.

Whole-rock Sr–Nd isotope analyses were conducted using a Thermo Fisher Scientific Neptune Plus multicollector ICP–MS at the Wuhan Samplesolution Analytical Technology Co., Ltd. (Wuhan, China). Mass fractionation corrections applied internal normalization to 88Sr/86Sr = 8.375209 and 146Nd/144Nd = 0.7219. Sr isotopic measurements were calibrated against the NIST SRM 987 standard solution, yielding a mean 87Sr/86Sr ratio of 0.710246 ± 7 (2SD, n = 6), which is in excellent agreement with its certified value (0.710241 ± 12). Analyses of USGS reference materials BCR-2 (basalt) and RGM-2 (rhyolite) produced 87Sr/86Sr ratios of 0.705012 (n = 1) and 0.704145 ± 9 (2SD, n = 1), respectively, consistent with published values. For Nd isotopes, repeated analysis of the GSB standard yielded a mean 143Nd/144Nd ratio of 0.512435 ± 6 (2SD, n = 4), matching the reported reference value (0.512438 ± 6). Additionally, analyses of USGS reference materials BCR-2 and RGM-2 yielded 143Nd/144Nd ratios of 0.512641 (n = 1) and 0.512811 (n = 1), respectively, which are within the uncertainty of recommended values.

Results

Zircon U–Pb Geochronology

Zircon grains separated from sample BD-1 display prismatic to subrounded morphologies, with lengths of 50–100 μm and aspect ratios ranging from 1:1 to 3:1. Cathodoluminescence (CL) images reveal oscillatory or diffuse zoning patterns (Figure S2). Twenty U–Pb spot analyses were conducted on 19 zircon grains. Of these, 11 analyses showed >10% discordance (concordance <90%) and were excluded from further age interpretationa common issue in mafic rock geochronology, where zircons frequently contain elevated common Pb and/or are prone to Pb loss due to their typically low U concentrations. Additionally, four zircon grains yielded inherited ages ranging from 429 to 2387 Ma (Table S1). These are interpreted as xenocrysts, either inherited from the magma source region or entrained from the country rocks during magma ascent. The remaining five analyses form a coherent cluster with 206Pb/238U ages between 255 and 264 Ma, yielding a weighted mean concordia age of 258.3 ± 6.6 Ma (MSWD = 0.24; Figure ). This dominant Paleozoic group is therefore interpreted as representing the crystallization age of sample BD-1. All five concordant analyses used to calculate the weighted mean age were obtained from oscillatory-zoned core domains characterized by high CL brightness and textural coherence (Figure S2). These domains are interpreted as primary magmatic in origin and most resistant to postcrystallization isotopic disturbance. The tight clustering of these concordant analyses (MSWD = 0.24) further supports the reliability of the interpreted crystallization age.

Major and Trace Element Compositions

Bulk rock major and trace element compositions are presented in Table S2. All analyzed samples exhibit variable alteration, evidenced by elevated loss on ignition (LOI = 1.94–9.17 wt %; mostly <4%). Geochemical compositions show significant ranges in SiO2 (43.87–46.09 wt %), TiO2 (2.79–4.74 wt %), Al2O3 (11.15–14.72 wt %), MgO (2.55–5.93 wt %), CaO (6.60–8.89 wt %), P2O5 (0.51–1.00 wt %), and total Fe2O3 (12.90–19.10 wt %). All samples classify as alkaline basalts in the Zr/TiO2 × 0.0001 versus Nb/Y discrimination diagram (Figure ). The Tianlin mafic rocks exhibit uniformly high Ti/Y ratios (507–1229), classifying them as a high-Ti series (Figure ).

Sr–Nd Isotopic Compositions

Sr–Nd isotopic ratios of the Tianlin mafic rocks were back-calculated to their initial values based on the corresponding zircon U–Pb ages (258.3 Ma). The Tianlin mafic rocks exhibit homogeneous initial isotopic compositions [(87Sr/86Sr)i = 0.705522–0.705891; εNd­(t) = 0.43 to 0.81] (Tables S3 and S4). Discussion

Effect of Alteration, Crustal Contamination, and Fractional Crystallization

The Tianlin mafic rocks exhibit relatively high loss on ignition (LOI) values (1.94–9.17 wt %, mostly <5 wt %, Table S2), suggesting postmagmatic alteration. Given zirconium’s limited mobility during low-grade hydrothermal alteration and metamorphism, Zr serves as a robust alteration-invariant proxy for evaluating trace element mobility. Strong correlations between Zr and high-field-strength elements (Nb, Ta, Hf), rare earth elements (REEs), Y, Th, and U (Figure S3) indicate minimal postalteration disturbance. Crucially, these elements show no systematic relationship with LOI (Figure S4), further corroborating their stability. The consistent chondrite-normalized REE patterns and primitive mantle-normalized trace element profiles (Figure ) provide additional evidence for the integrity of these element system. Consequently, alteration exerted negligible influence on HFSE, REE, Y, Th, and U concentrations. Accordingly, subsequent interpretations focus primarily on these immobile elements.

The Tianlin mafic rocks display characteristic enrichment in LILEs coupled with relative depletions in HFSEs (e.g., Nb, Ta, Zr, Hf) compared to oceanic island basalt (OIB) compositions (Figure ). These signatures could reflect either crustal assimilation or mantle source enrichment. However, upper crustal contamination is precluded as a mechanism since continental crust exhibits complementary geochemistry: enriched in Zr–Hf but depleted in Nb–Ta. Crucially, Th/Nb, La/Nb, Th/Ta, and Nb/U ratios remain stable during magmatic differentiation, resisting modification by partial melting or fractional crystallization, thus serving as robust crustal contamination proxies. Our samples show systematically lower La/Nb (1.10–1.35), Th/Nb (0.10–0.13), and Th/Ta (1.68–2.03) ratios, along with elevated Nb/U (28.34–37.40) relative to upper continental crust values (La/Nb = 2.58, Th/Nb = 0.88, Th/Ce = 0.17, Nb/U = 4.44). This distinct ratio pattern conclusively indicates a mantle origin without substantial crustal contamination. This interpretation is further corroborated by their isotopic composition, which plots above the mantle array in the (87Sr/86Sr)­i-εNd­(t) diagram (Figure ), also indicating limited crustal involvement. Therefore, we conclude that crustal contamination played an insignificant role in the petrogenesis of the Tianlin mafic rocks.

The Tianlin mafic rocks exhibit low Mg# (28–47), Ni (10.1–90.8 ppm), and Cr (0.32–124 ppm) relative to primitive magmas (Mg# > 70, Cr > 1000 ppm, and Ni > 400 ppm), indicating their parent magma may have undergone a considerable degree of fractional crystallization during magma ascent. The positive correlations between Cr/Ni and Mg# (Figure S5) indicate the fractionation of olivine during differentiation. There is no correlation between Mg# and Sc and CaO, suggesting that that clinopyroxene fractionation was limited (Figure S5). The weakly positive Eu anomaly (δEu = 1.13–1.42, with an average value of 1.23) indicates no significant fractionation of plagioclase. Consequently, the magma underwent olivine-dominated fractional crystallization without significant plagioclase or clinopyroxene fractionation.

Magma Source and Petrogenesis

LA-ICP-MS zircon U–Pb dating of the Tianlin mafic rocks yields a crystallization age of 258.3 ± 6.6 Ma, contemporaneous with the emplacement of the Emeishan large igneous province (ELIP) basalts. , TiO2 content serves as a key discriminant for magma sources: crustally derived magmas typically contain ∼0.72 wt %, asthenospheric sources yield ∼1.27 wt %, while mantle-derived magmas exceed 2.0 wt % TiO2. The elevated TiO2 concentrations (2.79–4.74 wt %) in Tianlin rocks thus indicate a deep mantle origin. On the Nb/Yb versus Th/Yb diagram (Figure a), the Tianlin mafic rocks exhibit elevated Nb/Yb (10.6–12.4) and Th/Yb (0.1–0.13) ratios, plotting within the OIB field. A similar geochemical signature is observed in the Nb/Yb versus TiO2/Yb relationship (Figure b). These characteristics indicate that the Tianlin mafic rocks were likely derived from a primitive mantle source, possibly linked to plume-related or asthenospheric melting beneath South China. Furthermore, the Tianlin mafic rocks are characterized by high alkali contents (3.4–4.89 wt %) and significant enrichment in LILEs. These rocks exhibit pronounced REE fractionation, as evidenced by (La/Yb)N ratios of 8.5–10.8. Their trace-element patterns and Sr–Nd isotopic signatures (εNd­(t)=0.43–0.81; 87Sr/86Sr = 0.705929–0.70615) exhibit similarities with ocean island basalts (OIBs) and ELIP mafic–ultramafic rocks (Figure ), , consistent with a mantle plume-related origin.

Enriched mantle sources are conventionally identified by specific geochemical signatures: Zr/Y< 18, Zr/Nb < 18, and elevated Th/Yb and Ta/Yb ratios. Conversely, depleted mantle sources exhibit opposing characteristics, with Zr/Y > 18, Zr/Nb > 18, and depressed Th/Yb and Ta/Yb values. The Tianlin mafic rocks show high Th/Yb (1.24–1.39) and Ta/Yb (0.68–0.76) ratios (Figure c), coupled with low Zr/Y (5.09–5.76) and Zr/Nb (5.64–6.52), indicating derivation from an enriched mantle reservoir. These rocks further exhibit elevated initial 87Sr/86Sr (0.705522–0.705891) and positive εNd­(t) values (0.43–0.81), signifying moderate premelting metasomatic enrichment of their source. Additionally, La/Nb ratios (1.09–1.32) exceed values typical of metasomatized primitive mantle melts (∼0.53) and approach those of MORB-source melts (∼1.02). However, their OIB-like Sr–Nd isotopic signatures distinctly differ from MORB compositions (Figure ). This integrated evidence robustly supports an OIB-type enriched mantle source modified by metasomatism prior to melting.

The enriched OIB-like signature may originate from either ancient recycled oceanic crust or subducted terrigenous sediments. The Tianlin mafic rocks display subtle negative Nb–Ta–Zr-Hf anomalies relative to typical OIB lavas, with subchondritic (Nb/La)N ratios (0.71–0.89, mean = 0.81). As established in Section , the systematic depletion of Nb–Ta–Zr-Hf in these samples is attributed to source-region geochemical signatures rather than postmagmatic crustal contamination. The Tianlin mafic rocks display (Nb/La)N ratios ranging from 0.71 to 0.89 (mean = 0.81). Elevated Th/Yb (1.24–1.39) and Nb/Yb (10.6–12.4) ratios (Figure a) could imply either crustal assimilation or subduction-related contributions, yet the absence of crustal contamination signatures indicates plume metasomatism by enriched subduction-derived components. In Nb/La versus Hf/Sm space (Figure a), samples plot within the OIB field but trend toward slab-influenced compositions, suggesting plume modification by sediment-derived melts/fluids during ascent. Crucially, all samples align with the sediment melt trend in Th/Nb versus U/Th space (Figure b), demonstrating that sediment meltsnot fluidsserved as the primary metasomatic agent.

REE systematics provides powerful constraints on magma sources due to their moderately incompatible behavior during mantle melting. The geochemical signatures are primarily controlled by two fundamental parameters: (1) mantle source composition, and (2) partial melting degree. REE fractionation is particularly sensitive to residual mineralogy: garnet preferentially incorporates heavy REEs (HREEs; e.g., Yb), elevating La/Yb ratios in derivative melts. In contrast, spinel exhibits near-uniform REE partition coefficients, producing negligible LREE-HREE fractionation. Consequently, La/Yb ratios effectively discriminate between garnet- and spinel-lherzolite sources. Elevated (La/Yb)n ratios indicate either low-degree melting or garnet-dominated residues, whereas lower ratios reflect higher melting degrees and/or spinel-controlled residues. The Tianlin mafic rocks display marked HREE depletion (Figure ), with significantly elevated (La/Yb)N (8.5–10.8) and (Gd/Yb)N (2.1–2.5) ratios. These pronounced fractionation patterns require a garnet-bearing mantle source. This interpretation is reinforced by (La/Sm)N versus (Tb/Yb)N systematics (Figure S6), where all samples plot decisively within the garnet stability field.

Implications for Late Permian Plume and Neoproterozoic Subduction Slab Interaction

Geological records document multiple episodes of oceanic slab subduction along the Yangtze Craton margin during the Neoproterozoic and Paleozoic. ,,, These subduction systems imply that either recycled subducted Neoproterozoic oceanic crust or Paleo-Tethyan slab components could have provided metasomatic materials contributing to the genesis of the Tianlin mafic magmatism.

Early models attributed the ca. 261 Ma Nb-enriched basalts and 259–248 Ma A-type granites along the western Yangtze margin to interactions between an eastward-subducting Paleo-Tethyan slab and the Emeishan mantle plume. , However, recent studies propose that the Nb-enriched basalts exhibit trace element patterns and isotopic compositions (εNd­(t) = +5.65 to +6.21; 87Sr/86Sr­(i) = 0.7031–0.7065) similar to those of the low-Ti basalts and picrites in the Emeishan LIP. In contrast, the low-δ18O A-type granites are more plausibly interpreted as products of crustal extension following flood basalt volcanism rather than of slab–plume interaction. Furthermore, the absence of Permian–Triassic arc magmatism in the western Yangtze Craton contradicts the proposed eastward subduction of the Ailaoshan Ocean. ,, Instead, coeval arc volcanics and granitoids west of the Jinshajiang–Ailaoshan suture consistently indicate a westward subduction polarity. ,− A key piece of evidence comes from Late Permian mafic–ultramafic rocks, which exhibit enriched Nd isotopic compositions (εNd­(t) < – 5.0) that correspond spatially and geochemically to a lithospheric mantle modified during Neoproterozoic subduction. Collectively, these observations support a model in which the Tianlin mafic rocks were derived from plume-triggered melting of Neoproterozoic metasomatized mantle domains. This interpretation is further quantitatively corroborated by Sr–Nd isotopic systematics: using Neoproterozoic subduction-related mafic–ultramafic rocks as a proxy for the ancient metasomatized mantle, we demonstrate that most ELIP magmas define an evolutionary trend consistent with derivation from this precursor source (Figure ).

During the Neoproterozoic (ca. 890–740 Ma), prolonged subduction along the western Yangtze Craton margin led to the deep burial of oceanic slabs, which delivered substantial crustal materials into the mantle. , During descent, these materials underwent prograde metamorphism and physical reworking, releasing hydrous fluids, silicate melts, and volatile species that metasomatized the overlying mantle wedge, thereby enriching the subcontinental lithospheric mantle (SCLM). We propose that during the Late Permian (∼260 Ma), the ascending Emeishan mantle plume thermally eroded and assimilated portions of this previously metasomatized SCLM. This process incorporated Neoproterozoic subduction-related components into the plume-derived melts, enriching them in incompatible elements (e.g., LILEs, LREEs) and volatiles. Consequently, the resulting magmas retained OIB-like affinities but also carried diagnostic crustal signatures, such as negative Nb–Ta anomalies, high La/Nb ratios, and enriched Th/Yb systematics. Thus, we conclude that the Emeishan Large Igneous Province (ELIP) originated not from a pristine mantle plume alone, but from plume-triggered melting of mantle domains that had been geochemically hybridized by Neoproterozoic slab contributions. This model provides a coherent mechanism for reconciling the OIB-like characteristics of ELIP magmas with their clear inheritance of ancient subduction signals.

Conclusions

This study presents zircon U–Pb geochronology and whole-rock geochemical data for Tianlin mafic rocks, yielding the following conclusions: (1) LA-ICP-MS zircon dating indicates emplacement at 258.3 Ma, contemporaneous with the ELIP eruptive phase and genetic association with the Emeishan mantle plume. (2) The rocks represent a high-Ti series with oceanic island basalt (OIB)-affinity, characterized by pronounced LREE-HFSE enrichment and fractionated REE patterns ((La/Yb)N = 8.5–10.8). (3) Near-chondritic εNd­(t) (0.43–0.81) and subtle but discernible Nb–Ta–Zr-Hf anomalies reflect mantle source hybridization by Neoproterozoic subduction-derived components, precluding significant contribution from Paleo-Tethyan systems.

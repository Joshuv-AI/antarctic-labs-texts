Abstract
In integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) systems, the use of dual-functional signals inherently exposes confidential communication information during radar sensing, particularly when the sensing target itself acts as an eavesdropper. Conventional physical-layer security solutions rely on directional beamforming or artificial noise injection implemented via signal processing in the baseband (BB) domain. However, these BB-domain approaches are constrained by insufficient spatial resolution and the adverse effects of spatially correlated channels. To overcome these limitations, this paper proposes a novel secure ISAC framework empowered by compound reconfigurable antenna (CRA) arrays, which offer simultaneous reconfigurability of radiation patterns and polarization states in the electromagnetic (EM) domain. Specifically, we develop a comprehensive channel model incorporating virtual angular domain, spatial domain, and depolarization effects, and formulate a mixed-integer nonlinear programming (MINLP) problem to jointly design EM-domain and BB-domain precoders and combiners. To efficiently solve this complex optimization problem, we propose an iterative decomposition-based algorithm leveraging fractional programming (FP), majorization–minimization (MM), second-order cone programming (SOCP), and penalty methods. Extensive simulation results demonstrate that the CRA array architecture with proposed joint EM-and BB-domain design achieves significant performance improvements in secure ISAC systems. In particular, radar sensing gains of up to 12dB are observed over conventional beamforming, while robust communication security is maintained. These results highlight the considerable benefits attainable by jointly leveraging additional degrees of freedom (DoFs) in the EM domain for secure ISAC system design.



I
Introduction

Integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) has emerged as a key enabling technology for next-generation wireless networks
[
1
]
. By tightly integrating wireless communication and radar sensing functionalities on a shared hardware platform and spectral resources, ISAC can reduce hardware redundancy, enhance spectral efficiency, and enable new applications such as autonomous driving, smart manufacturing, and environmental monitoring
[
2
]
. The core idea of ISAC is to leverage dual-functional signals that simultaneously support both communication and sensing tasks within a unified design framework, thereby improving resource efficiency, reducing signaling overhead, and promoting integrated operations.

Despite the benefits of dual-functional signaling, integrating confidential information into radar probing signals presents significant information security challenges
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
. Specifically, because the same waveform is used for both communication and sensing, confidential information intended for legitimate users is inevitably present in the direction of the sensing target, thereby increasing the risk of eavesdropping. Furthermore, in practice, the target itself may act as an eavesdropper, further exacerbating the security risk. This scenario creates a unique dilemma: On one hand, the transmit power should be concentrated on the target to achieve high sensing performance; on the other hand, the signal information must be safeguarded from interception by that very target. These conflicting requirements pose a critical challenge to security in ISAC systems and call for innovative research efforts.

To address these security issues, many works have focused on physical-layer security (PLS) techniques that use digital signal processing techniques in the baseband (BB) domain to achieve spatial separation between legitimate users and potential eavesdroppers (or sensing targets). For example, in
[
5
]
, the authors employ artificial noise (AN) at the transmitter and design a beamformer to minimize the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) at eavesdroppers or targets. Similarly, in
[
6
]
, the authors propose a sensing-assisted strategy, wherein the spatial information of potential eavesdroppers is first obtained via omnidirectional probing and then used to design AN for secrecy rate maximization. In
[
7
]
, the authors exploit destructive interference to degrade the signal at the radar target by steering the communication symbols toward the destructive region of the modulation constellation.

The aforementioned methods exploit spatial degrees of freedom (DoFs) to steer signal energy toward intended users and targets while suppressing leakage toward eavesdroppers. However, they fundamentally rely on the assumption that the communication, sensing, and eavesdropping channels are orthogonal or have low correlation, which may not hold in practice. For instance, if a legitimate user and an eavesdropper are located in close proximity or share similar angular characteristics, spatial domain methods may fail to sufficiently suppress information leakage, especially when the antenna array has limited size and cannot achieve fine-grained beam steering
[
8
]
. These limitations highlight the need to explore new design dimensions and additional physical-layer resources to better distinguish legitimate users from eavesdroppers.

Fortunately, emerging reconfigurable antenna (RA) technologies provide additional DoFs in the electromagnetic (EM) domain, which can be harnessed to overcome the limitations of spatial-only approaches
[
9
]
-
[
11
]
. Specifically, RAs can dynamically adapt their configurations to modify key radiation characteristics such as the radiation pattern
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
, polarization state
[
14
]
,
[
15
]
, and operating frequency
[
16
]
,
[
17
]
. Among these capabilities, radiation-pattern and polarization reconfigurabilities are particularly critical for wireless systems, as they enable proactive manipulation of the wireless channel environment. Recent research has begun to explore these features. For example, the authors in
[
18
]
introduce an integrated polarimetric sensing and communication (IPSAC) system in which the transmitter and receiver polarization states are jointly optimized along with the waveform to distinguish the target from clutter. This approach enhances target detection performance and improves estimation accuracy. In addition, the authors in
[
19
]
propose a dynamically reconfigurable heterogeneous array that adaptively adjusts the synthesized radiation patterns of dynamic metasurface antenna (DMA) elements in response to jamming. This design improves the secrecy and resilience of wireless links, even under fluctuating jamming attacks. Nevertheless, these studies do not yet fully exploit the extra EM-domain DoFs offered by RAs.

To further harness the EM-domain DoFs, compound reconfigurable antenna (CRA) technology has been developed, which integrates multiple reconfigurable features into a single antenna structure
[
20
]
. In the context of secure ISAC, the joint reconfigurability of radiation pattern and polarization state is especially valuable, as this improves the separability of different entities in the EM domain
[
21
]
. Specifically, the radiation pattern reconfigurability of a CRA enables precise power focusing toward intended users or targets, suppresses unintended radiation, and allows adaptive beamwidth adjustment to optimize coverage. Meanwhile, polarization reconfigurability permits better alignment with legitimate receivers, creates polarization mismatches at eavesdroppers, thereby reducing their received signal strength, and improves target detection in cluttered environments via polarization diversity. Combining these two reconfigurable features yields synergistic gains beyond what either feature can achieve alone. Joint pattern–polarization adaptation further reduces channel correlation among different entities, mitigating interception risk while preserving both communication and sensing performance.

Motivated by the above discussion, this paper investigates a joint design in both the EM and BB domains to fully exploit the additional DoFs provided by CRAs, with the goal of enhancing communication efficiency, sensing accuracy, and information security in secure ISAC systems. The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:

•

For the first time, we develop a comprehensive mathematical framework for modeling the EM-domain precoder and combiner realized by CRA arrays. Specifically, we propose an integrated compound channel model comprising three cascaded components: A virtual angular domain channel, a spatial domain channel, and a depolarization channel. This integrated approach effectively captures the radiation-pattern diversity and polarization reconfigurability inherent in CRA arrays. Leveraging this framework, we establish a secure ISAC system model that explicitly treats the sensing target as a potential eavesdropper.

•

We formulate a mixed-integer nonlinear programming (MINLP) problem for the joint optimization of EM- and BB-domain precoders and combiners, in order to maximize radar sensing performance while ensuring secure communication. Specifically, the optimization aims to maximize the radar signal-to-clutter-plus-noise ratio (SCNR) while guaranteeing the signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio (SINR) requirements of legitimate users and simultaneously constraining the eavesdropper’s SINR below a certain threshold. To solve this challenging problem, we propose a decomposition-based algorithm that reformulates the MINLP into several tractable subproblems, which are then addressed iteratively using fractional programming (FP), majorization–minimization (MM), second-order cone programming (SOCP), and penalty methods.

•

We conduct extensive numerical simulations to evaluate the proposed CRA array-empowered secure ISAC systems. The results demonstrate that the proposed joint EM- and BB-domain design significantly enhances radar sensing performance in secure ISAC systems, achieving gains of up to 12dB over conventional beamforming while simultaneously ensuring robust communication security. Moreover, the proposed CRA array design demonstrates effective and robust performance across a variety of operational scenarios and system parameters. The simulations also underscore the critical role of polarization reconfiguration and validate the efficiency of the low-resolution CRA configuration. These findings highlight EM-domain reconfigurability as a powerful approach for addressing security challenges in ISAC systems, offering promising directions for future research.

System Model and Problem Formulation

II-A

Compound Reconfigurable Antenna

A CRA represents an advanced class of antennas that integrates multiple reconfiguration capabilities within a single hardware platform. Typical hardware prototypes of CRAs are illustrated in Fig.
1
. Specifically, CRAs are capable of dynamically adjusting both their radiation patterns and polarization states, which are the primary DoFs exploited in the EM domain. This flexibility enhances the adaptability of wireless systems, particularly in complex scenarios such as secure ISAC systems. The ability of CRAs to reconfigure their radiation pattern enables them to steer or reshape the pattern in different angular directions, thus improving beam alignment with legitimate users or targets while simultaneously suppressing eavesdropping or interference. Moreover, the polarization reconfigurability of CRAs allows for switching between various polarization modes, thereby facilitating more effective discrimination between targets and clutter in sensing tasks, and concealing signal characteristics from potential eavesdroppers. By integrating both radiation pattern and polarization reconfigurability, CRAs offer a multidimensional adaptation mechanism within the EM domain, significantly enhancing the system’s performance in terms of target detection and secure communications.

\overline{\mathbf{H}}_{\mathrm{A}}\triangleq\mathbf{h}_{\mathrm{A}}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{2}\in\{0,1\}^{2M\times 2},

(4)

where the scalar

\mathbf{M}=\mathbf{Q}\bm{\Phi}\overline{\mathbf{H}}_{\mathrm{S}}^{\mathsf{H}}\overline{\mathbf{H}}_{\mathrm{A}}^{\mathsf{H}}\in\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2M}.

(5)

II-B

EM-domain Precoder and Combiner using CRA Array

Leveraging the advantages of CRAs, we incorporate these antennas into array architectures to further enhance the performance of secure ISAC systems. Specifically, a CRA array offers flexible and dynamic reconfigurability in both radiation patterns and polarization states in the EM domain, while simultaneously supporting conventional digital beamforming in the BB domain. Consequently, this CRA array architecture enables more accurate target detection by effectively discriminating targets from clutters, and concurrently enhances secure communication by robustly suppressing potential eavesdroppers.

C

K

The BS is equipped with separate transmit and receive uniform linear arrays (ULAs), each consisting of
N

L

II-C
.3
Depolarization Channel

The depolarization channel, also referred to as the polarization scattering matrix, accounts for scattering-induced changes in the orientation and phase of polarized EM waves as they interact with the propagation environment (e.g., reflections from scatterers, targets, and clutter). In essence, it captures how the signal’s polarization state is altered during transmission and reception due to multi-path propagation and object-induced effects
[
14
]
,
[
24
]
.

For the
l
-th propagation path of the
k
-th Bob, the depolarization channel is modeled by

\bm{\Phi}_{k,l}\triangleq\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\Phi_{k,l}^{\mathrm{HH}}&\Phi_{k,l}^{\mathrm{HV}}\\
\Phi_{k,l}^{\mathrm{VH}}&\Phi_{k,l}^{\mathrm{VV}}\\
\end{array}\right]\in\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2},~\forall k,l,

(11)

where each entry represents the scattering coefficient from transmitted polarization to received polarization.
By stacking the per-path depolarization matrices for all
L
paths, the overall depolarization channel for Bob
k
is constructed as a block-diagonal matrix:

\bm{\overline{\Phi}}_{k}\triangleq\mathrm{blkdiag}\{\bm{\Phi}_{k,1},\bm{\Phi}_{k,2},\ldots,\bm{\Phi}_{k,L}\}\in\mathbb{C}^{2L\times 2L},~\forall k.

(12)

This block-diagonal structure preserves the path-specific polarization information and is readily combined with the previously defined virtual angular and spatial domain channels. II-E

Problem Formulation

In this paper, we propose to jointly design the EM-domain precoder
\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{EM}}
and combiner
\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{EM}}
, and the BB-domain precoder
\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}
and combiner
\mathbf{w}_{\mathrm{BB}}
to maximize the radar output SCNR, while satisfying the legitimate communication SINR requirements
\epsilon_{k}
, suppressing the eavesdropping SINR below a certain threshold
\epsilon_{\mathrm{e},k}
, selection the radiation patterns and polarization states from a predefined set
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{EM}}
, and limiting the total transmit power within the given budget
P_{\mathrm{T}}
.
Therefore, the optimization problem can be formulated as

\displaystyle\max_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{EM}},\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{EM}},\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}},\mathbf{w}_{\mathrm{BB}}}

\displaystyle\mathrm{SCNR}_{\mathrm{r}}

(25a)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \

\displaystyle\mathrm{SINR}_{k}\geq\epsilon_{k},~\forall k,

(25b)

\displaystyle\mathrm{SINR}_{\mathrm{e},k}\leq\epsilon_{\mathrm{e},k},~\forall k,

(25c)

\displaystyle\mathbf{f}_{\mathrm{EM},n},~\mathbf{w}_{\mathrm{EM},n}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{EM}},~\forall n,

(25d)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}\|_{\mathsf{F}}^{2}\leq P_{\mathrm{T}},

(25e)

where the set
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{EM}}
comprises the candidate EM modes of single-CRA as described in Section II-A.

This formulated mixed integer non-linear programming (MINLP) optimization problem is particularly challenging to solve due to the fractional objective function (
25a
), the fractional constraints (
25b
) and (
25c
), and the discrete selection constraints (
25d
) arising from the finite set of available EM modes. To address these challenges, in the next section we propose to decompose this problem into several tractable subproblems and solve them iteratively using the FP, MM, SOCP, and penalty algorithms.

III
Joint EM- and BB-Domain Designs

In this section, we first recast the original MINLP problem into an equivalent mixed-binary nonlinear program. Then, an alternating optimization algorithm is proposed, where each subproblem is solved iteratively using FP, MM, SOCP, and penalty-MM techniques. The detailed algorithmic steps are presented as follows.

III-A

Original Problem Transformation

V
Conclusions

In this paper, we investigated a novel secure ISAC system architecture empowered by CRAs. By simultaneously providing reconfigurability in radiation patterns and polarization states, CRAs introduce additional DoFs in the EM domain. We developed a comprehensive compound channel model capturing virtual angular domain, spatial domain, and depolarization effects, and formulated a joint EM- and BB-domain optimization framework aiming to maximize radar sensing performance while ensuring secure communication under stringent transmit power and SINR constraints. To effectively solve the resulting challenging MINLP problem, we proposed a decomposition-based iterative algorithm leveraging FP, MM, SOCP, and penalty methods.
Extensive numerical simulations underscore the considerable benefits achievable by exploiting additional EM-domain DoFs, positioning EM-domain reconfigurability as a promising avenue for addressing security challenges in future secure ISAC system designs.

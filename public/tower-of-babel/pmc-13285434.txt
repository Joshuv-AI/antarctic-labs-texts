Triple tank control: disposable vape devices with adjustable nicotine and flavour levels
Authors: Jennifer B Unger, Reid C Whaley, Julia Vassey, Saber Feizy, Daniel W Soto, Artur Galimov
Published: 2025

Online, we have observed disposable vaping devices that allow users to adjust the nicotine dose and flavour levels to deliver a customised vaping experience. Older tank-based (box mods) and pod-based vaping devices allow the user to select a nicotine concentration (eg, JUUL pods are available in several different nicotine concentrations and box mods allow the consumer to fill the device with any strength, flavour or formulation of nicotine). Disposable e-cigarettes have overtaken cigalikes, vape pens and mods and now lead the retail market.1 2 Until now, the disposable e-cigarette market was dominated by closed-system products with a predetermined nicotine strength and flavour. Now that disposable vaping devices are popular among adolescents and young adults, with 55.6% of adolescent vapers choosing disposables,1 2 it is important to understand the potential effects of adjustable disposable devices on vaping behaviour and nicotine dependence.

The e-cigarette retail website Eightvape3 describes these products as ‘innovative disposable vaping devices that allow users to adjust the nicotine level to suit their preferences. These devices are especially useful for managing nicotine intake, helping with cravings or gradually reducing nicotine consumption over time.’ Eightvape also sells disposable vaping devices with adjustable flavour levels, including sweet and cooling flavours. For example, Oxbar markets Tri-Fusion, ‘a three-button precision-controlled vape that allows for over 100 flavour combinations—sweetness (four levels (Regular Sweet I, Regular Sweet II, Super Sweet I, Super Sweet II)), nicotine (five levels (Zero Nic, Light Nic, Regular Nic, High Nic, Nic Shock)), and ice (five levels (No Extra Ice, Extra Light Ice, Extra Regular Ice, Extra High Ice, Brain Freeze))’ (figures 1 and 2).4 This type of device may be especially problematic because nicotine-naïve individuals and exclusive nicotine vapers prefer sweet flavours such as fruit or candy,5 6 higher e-liquid nicotine strength is associated with higher nicotine dependence,7 and ice coolants such as WS-23 (N,2,3-trimethyl-2-isopropylbuytamide) make the vaping experience more appealing, regardless of the device’s base flavour.8

Devices that allow users to adjust characteristics such as cooling level are reminiscent of Camel Crush Bold and other combustible cigarettes that contained capsules of menthol flavouring, allowing users to customise the menthol level of their cigarettes by crushing the menthol capsule in the filter.9 These cigarettes became popular among the Millennial generation in the early 2000s. They are currently banned in US states with menthol bans and the European Union.10 Now a new generation of current and potential nicotine users is being introduced to vape devices that allow users to control the nicotine dose and flavour level.

These products could jeopardise public health because novice users with low levels of nicotine tolerance could begin using them at lower nicotine levels, which makes initiation less aversive, and gradually increase the nicotine dose, leading to higher levels of nicotine dependence while using the same product. The adjustable nicotine dose removes the necessity of puffing more deeply or more frequently to obtain more nicotine, potentially increasing addiction liability of these adjustable products. Without external cues or barriers such as having to spend more money to purchase more devices, users could inadvertently increase their nicotine use and dependence. Because higher nicotine emission rates are associated with faster delivery of nicotine to the brain and greater dependence liability,11 these devices could increase a user’s nicotine dependence rapidly. In addition, the ability to increase the levels of sweet and ice flavours could make higher levels of nicotine palatable for inexperienced users, who can increase the sweetness and cooling levels to mask the harshness of the nicotine.12

These products are marketed as a way to reduce or control one’s nicotine exposure by tapering down to lower nicotine levels.3 However, they also could lead to an unintended increase in nicotine exposure and intake. It seems likely that users of these disposable vaping devices with adjustable nicotine would increase the nicotine level over time as they develop a tolerance to nicotine. It is also possible that these devices could increase the risk of progression to vaping other substances such as cannabis.

Research is needed to determine whether these devices motivate users to increase their levels of nicotine dependence, thereby making cessation more difficult. If these products increase nicotine dependence rather than causing users to decrease their nicotine use over time, additional regulation of these products would be warranted.

Quadratic flatness and Regularity for Codimension-One Varifolds with Bounded Anisotropic Mean Curvature
Mario Santilli, Sławomir Kolasiński

Abstract
Given a uniformly convex norm ϕ \phi on 𝐑 n + 1 \mathbf{R}^{n+1} and a unit-density n n -dimensional varifold V V in an open subset of 𝐑 n + 1 \mathbf{R}^{n+1} with bounded mean ϕ \phi -curvature, under the hypothesis ℋ n ​ ⁡ spt ⁡ ‖ V ‖ is absolutely continuous with respect to ‖ V ‖ , \textrm{$\mathcal{H}^{n}\mathop{\rule[1.0pt]{0.5pt}{6.0pt}\rule[1.0pt]{4.0pt}{0.5pt}}\nolimits\spt\|V\|$ is absolutely continuous with respect to $\|V\|$}, we prove that there exists an open dense 𝒞 1 , α \mathscr{C}^{1,\alpha} -regular part in spt ⁡ ‖ V ‖ \spt\|V\| . Moreover we prove that the 𝒞 1 , α \mathscr{C}^{1,\alpha} -regular part of V V is ℋ n \mathcal{H}^{n} -almost equal to ( spt ⁡ ‖ V ‖ ) ∗ (\spt\|V\|)^{\ast} , the subset of spt ⁡ ‖ V ‖ \spt\|V\| where at least one blow up (in Hausdorff distance) of spt ⁡ ‖ V ‖ \spt\|V\| is not equal to 𝐑 n + 1 \mathbf{R}^{n+1} . These results are consequences of a quadratic flatness theorem asserting that if V V is a general – not necessarily rectifiable – varifold V V with bounded mean ϕ \phi -curvature and locally ℋ n \mathcal{H}^{n} -finite support, then the set of points R R where spt ⁡ ‖ V ‖ \spt\|V\| admits (exactly) two mutually tangent balls satisfies ℋ n ​ ( ( spt ⁡ ‖ V ‖ ) ∗ ∖ R ) = 0 \mathcal{H}^{n}((\spt\|V\|)^{\ast}\setminus R)=0 and meets each relatively open set of spt ⁡ ‖ V ‖ \spt\|V\| on a set of positive ℋ n \mathcal{H}^{n} -measure. Indeed, quadratic flatness at a point guarantees that the local regularity theorem in [ All86 ] can be applied, hence proving that spt ⁡ ‖ V ‖ \spt\|V\| is regular around that point.



1
Introduction

In the renowned paper
[
All72
]
, Allard establishes several fundamental results for
varifolds whose
euclidean (isotropic)
first variation is controlled. The pillar of this
theory is the
monotonicity formula
; cf.
[
All72
, 5.1]
or
[
Sim83
]
. If
V
is
a
k
-dimensional rectifiable varifold in
\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
with mean curvature in

\mathbf{L}_{p}(\|V\|)
with
p>k
, one of Allard’s main achievements, the celebrated regularity
theorem
[
All72
, 8.1]
, states that
\spt\|V\|
locally corresponds to a graph of a

\mathscr{C}^{1}
-function around each point of density close to
1
. In particular, if
V
is unit
density for
\|V\|
almost all points, then there exists a
\mathscr{C}^{1}
-hypersurface
M

relatively open in
\spt\|V\|
such that
\mathscr{H}^{n}\big(\spt\|V\|\!\mathop{\smallsetminus}M\big)=0
. Another classical and central consequence of the monotonicity formula states that the whole
support of
\|V\|
,
\spt\|V\|
, must be countably
(\mathscr{H}^{k},k)
-rectifiable;
cf.
[
All72
, 8.6]
. This conclusion has considerably been strengthen in recent
years. Indeed, if
V
is
integral
, we infer from
[
Men13
]
that
\spt\|V\|
can
be covered, up to a set of
\mathscr{H}^{k}
-measure zero, by countably many
\mathscr{C}^{2}
-regular

k
-dimensional submanifolds. See also
[
San21
]
for
\mathscr{C}^{2}
-rectifiability of
rectifiable varifolds of bounded mean curvature. Clearly

\mathscr{C}^{2}
-rectifiability is sharp for varifolds with
bounded
mean curvature. For

stationary
varifolds De Lellis, Brena and Franceschini
[
BLF25
]
have recently proved
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
-rectifiability. For codimension-one (i.e.

k=n
) integral varifolds, quadratic flatness almost everywhere – that implies
\mathscr{C}^{2}
-rectifiability – was proved by Schätzle in

[
Sch04
]
. Namely he proved that if
V
is an

integral
varifold with mean curvature in
\mathbf{L}_{p}(\|V\|)
with
p>n
, then for

\|V\|
almost all
a\in\Omega
there exist
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
and
r>0
such
that

\mathbf{U}(a+r\nu,r)\cap\spt\|V\|=\varnothing=\mathbf{U}(a-r\nu,r)\cap\spt\|V\|\,,

where
\mathbf{U}(a,r)
is the open ball centred at
a
of radius
r
.

The extension of these results to integral varifolds whose first variation with respect to
a parametric elliptic integrand is controlled is a well known and
outstanding problem in the calculus of variations. The main obstacle arises from the fact that no
monotonicity formula is available for such varifolds; cf.
[
All72
, 4.9]

and
[
All74
]
. Henceforth, most of the powerful techniques from
[
All72
]
are not
accessible in the anisotropic setting.

Significant progress was made after the groundbreaking result of De Philippis, De Rosa, and
Ghiraldin
[
DPDRG18
]
, where the authors introduce a new notion of ellipticity, called

the atomic condition
or AC for short, and prove it is necessary and sufficient to ensure that
varifolds whose anisotropic total variation measure is Radon are
rectifiable (i.e. the set of points of positive density is a rectifiable set) – an analogue of
[
All72
, 5.5(1)]
. Later it was shown in
[
DRK20
]
that
this new notion is stronger than the classical Almgren’s ellipticity introduced
in
[
Alm68
]
. The atomic condition was also exploited by De Rosa and
Tione
[
DRT22
]
who introduced its uniform version and used it to show almost
everywhere regularity for Lipschitz graphs with anisotropic mean curvature in
\mathbf{L}_{p}(\|V\|)
given

p>k
. In the recent work of De Rosa, Lei, and Young
[
DRLY25
]
the connection with
polyconvexity is established. By
[
DPDRG18
, Theorem 1.3]
in codimension-one an
integrand
F
satisfies the atomic condition if and only if the associated norm
\phi
is strictly convex. Recently, De Philippis, De Rosa and Li have extended the min-max methods to the anisotropic setting in
[
PRL24
]
(cf.
[
DPDR24
]
), hence providing the existence of hypersurfaces
\Sigma
in any
(n+1)
-dimensional smooth closed Riemannian manifold which are
F
-stationary, or more generally have constant mean
F
-curvature, with respect to a uniformly elliptic integrand
F
and with the essentially optimal regularity condition
\mathcal{H}^{n-2}\bigl(\mathop{\mathrm{Clos}}\Sigma\setminus\Sigma\bigr)=0
.

In this paper we consider the case of an autonomous uniformly convex elliptic integrand on
\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
; i.e. the integrand is induced by a uniformly convex norm
\phi
. A basic and well-known open question concerns the almost everywhere regularity for unit-density
n
-dimensional integral varifolds with bounded mean
\phi
-curvature. Under the additional hypothesis

(1)

\displaystyle\text{$\mathscr{H}^{n}\mathop{\rule[1.0pt]{0.5pt}{6.0pt}\rule[1.0pt]{4.0pt}{0.5pt}}\nolimits\spt\|V\|$ is absolutely continuous with respect to $\|V\|$}\,,

Allard in
[
All86
, 3.6]
employed a
barrier principle
to prove a regularity criterion
for points of
\spt\|V\|
that satisfy suitable
smallness assumptions
on the mass and on the quadratic
height excess of cylinders in
Z
. This result extends the celebrated
regularity criterion

in
[
All72
, 8.16]
based on harmonic approximation. In case of area integrand, it is the
monotonicity formula that ensures the applicability of the regularity criterion to all points of
density close to
1
; cf. the flatness criterion
[
All72
, 8.8]
. Moreover, if
V
is a
unit-density varifold with bounded mean
\phi
-curvature associated with a
Lipschitz graph
,
then it is easy to see, by Rademacher theorem, that the regularity criterion
[
All86
, 3.6]

can be applied at
\|V\|

almost
all points, hence providing almost everywhere
regularity; cf.
[
DRT22
]
for the higher-codimension case. However, if
V
is a general codimension-one unit-density
varifold of bounded mean
\phi
-curvature satisfying (
1
), since there are no monotonicity formulas, it is not clear whether there exist points where the regularity criterion
[
All86
, 3.6]
can be
applied. In other words, the existence of a regular part in such generality has remained an open question and this is the topic of the present paper. Indeed, our first result precisely resolves this issue.

1.1 Theorem
.

Suppose

(1)

\displaystyle\text{$\phi$ is a uniformly convex $\mathscr{C}^{3}$-norm of $\mathbf{R}^{n+1}$}\,,

(2)

\displaystyle\text{ $\Omega\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{n+1}$ is open, $V$ is a $n$-dimensional integral varifold in $\Omega$}\,,

(3)

\displaystyle\text{$0\leq H<\infty$ is such that $\|\delta_{\phi}V\|\leq H\|V_{\phi}\|$}\,,

(4)

\displaystyle\text{$\mathscr{H}^{n}\mathop{\rule[1.0pt]{0.5pt}{6.0pt}\rule[1.0pt]{4.0pt}{0.5pt}}\nolimits\spt\|V\|$ is absolutely continuous with respect to $\|V\|$}\,,

(5)

\displaystyle\text{and $\boldsymbol{\Theta}^{n}(\|V\|,x)=1$ for $\|V\|$ almost~all\ $x\in\Omega$}\,.

Then for every
0\leq\alpha<1
there exists a
n
-dimensional embedded

\mathscr{C}^{1,\alpha}
-hypersurface
M\subseteq A
, relatively open in
\spt\|V\|
, such
that

\mathscr{H}^{n}\bigl(\mathbf{U}(a,r)\cap M\bigr)>0\quad\textrm{for every $a\in\spt\|V\|$ and $r>0$}.

In particular,
M
is dense in
\spt\|V\|
.

Once the existence of a regular dense part for a varifold
V
as in Theorem
1.1
is settled, one can turn the attention to the problem of almost everywhere regularity, namely

\mathscr{H}^{n}(\spt\|V\|\!\mathop{\smallsetminus}M)=0
, . Despite we are not able to fully answer such a question, we are able to provide an affirmative answer if one replaces
\spt\|V\|
with the subset
(\spt\|V\|)^{\ast}
of points where
at least
one blow up of
\spt\|V\|
is not equal to

\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
. To state this result in precise terms we first introduce some definitions.

1.2 Definition

(cf.
[
Kur66
, §29, I]
)
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
be open and

S_{1},S_{2},\ldots\subseteq\Omega
. The
lower limit
of
the sequence
\{S_{j}:j\in\mathscr{P}\}
is the set

\kliminf_{j\to\infty}S_{j}=\Omega\cap\big\{x:{\textstyle\lim_{k\to\infty}}\dist(x,S_{k})=0\big\}\,,

1.3 Definition
.

Let
A\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
and
a\in\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
. Define

(1)

\displaystyle\SetTan(A,a)=\bigl\{\kliminf_{j\to\infty}A_{a,r_{j}}:r_{1},r_{2},\ldots\in\mathbf{R},\,r_{j}\to 0^{+}\text{ as }j\to\infty\bigr\}\,,

(2)

\displaystyle\text{where}\quad A_{a,r}=\bigl\{r^{-1}(b-a):b\in A\bigr\}\quad\textrm{for $r>0$}\,.

1.4 Definition
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
be open and
A\subseteq\Omega
be relatively closed in

\Omega
.

We denote by
A^{\ast}
the set of those
a\in A
such that

\SetTan(A,a)\neq\{\mathbf{R}^{n+1}\}
.

1.5 Theorem
.

If
\phi
,
\Omega
,
V
,
H
,
\alpha
and
M
are as in
1.1
, then

\mathscr{H}^{n}\bigl((\spt\|V\|)^{\ast}\!\mathop{\smallsetminus}M\bigr)=0\,.

We expect that Theorem
1.5
suffices for several geometric applications, especially for those involving sets of finite perimeter; cf.
4.2
for critical points of the anisotropic isoperimetric problem.

As we cannot rely on monotonicity formulas, we need to develop a completely different approach
in order to prove Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.5
. One key observation comes from the fact that the aforementioned Schätzle result can
be naturally combined with
[
All72
, 8.16]
to obtain local regularity. Our idea is to extend
Schätzle result to the anisotropic setting and to combine it with the regularity criterion
[
All86
, 3.6]
to prove theorems
1.1
and
1.5
. Our extension of Schätzle theorem to the anisotropic setting is valid for a
general
– not necessarily rectifiable – varifold with bounded mean
\phi
-curvature satisfying the hypothesis that "
\mathscr{H}^{n}\mathop{\rule[1.0pt]{0.5pt}{6.0pt}\rule[1.0pt]{4.0pt}{0.5pt}}\nolimits\spt\|V\|
is
a Radon measure over
\Omega
", which is a priori weaker than Allard’s basic assumption
(
1
).

1.6 Theorem

(Quadratic flatness, cf.
3.7
)
.

Suppose

(1)

\displaystyle\text{$\phi$ is a uniformly convex $\mathscr{C}^{2}$-norm of $\mathbf{R}^{n+1}$}\,,

(2)

\displaystyle\text{$\Omega\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{n+1}$ is open}\,,\quad V\in\mathbf{V}_{n}(\Omega)\,,\quad A=\spt\|V\|\,,

(3)

\displaystyle\text{$0\leq H<\infty$ is such that $\|\delta_{\phi}V\|\leq H\|V_{\phi}\|$}\,,

(4)

\displaystyle\text{$\mathscr{H}^{n}\mathop{\rule[1.0pt]{0.5pt}{6.0pt}\rule[1.0pt]{4.0pt}{0.5pt}}\nolimits A$ is a Radon measure over $\Omega$}\,,

(5)

\displaystyle R=A\cap\big\{x:\exists\,r>0\,\exists\,\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{n}\ \ \mathbf{U}(a+r\nu,r)\cap A=\varnothing=\mathbf{U}(a-r\nu,r)\cap A\big\}\,.

Then

(6)

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}^{n}\bigl(R\cap\mathbf{U}(a,r)\bigr)>0\quad\textrm{ whenever $a\in A$ and $r>0$}

(7)

\displaystyle\text{and}\quad\mathscr{H}^{n}\bigl(A^{\ast}\!\mathop{\smallsetminus}R\bigr)=0\,.

In particular,
A^{\ast}
is
(\mathcal{H}^{n},n)
-rectifiable of class
\mathscr{C}^{2}
.

We do not assume the set
A=\spt\|V\|
in Theorem
1.6
to be rectifiable. Moreover, it is instructive to observe that there exists a closed
purely
(\mathscr{H}^{1},1)
-unrectifiable
set

A
in
\mathbf{R}^{2}
such that
0<\mathscr{H}^{1}(A)<\infty
and
A=A^{\ast}
;
cf.
3.9
. In other words the set
A^{\ast}
does not entail, in general,
any a priori rectifiability property. An interesting and special consequence of quadratic flatness is the
\mathscr{C}^{2}
-rectifiability of
(\spt\|V\|)^{\ast}
; in particular, this implies that the
\mathscr{C}^{1,\alpha}
-regular part given in Theorem
1.1
is
\mathscr{C}^{2}
-rectifiable. In this direction we mention that one can find in
[
Koh77
]
an example of a
\mathscr{C}^{1,\alpha}
-hypersurface
M\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
that meets
every

\mathscr{C}^{2}
-hypersurface on a set of
\mathcal{H}^{n}
-measure zero.

Schätzle’s proof combines results from
[
All72
]
and
[
Bra78
]
with
non-variational techniques based on maximum principles developed in the context of viscosity
solutions of second order elliptic PDE’s (
[
Tru89
]
and
[
Caf89
]
). The
viscosity approach in varifolds theory is systematically investigated in
[
Whi16
]
for the area integrand
and varifolds of arbitrary codimension, and in
[
DPDRH19
]
for codimension-one varifolds
and elliptic integrands arising from uniformly convex norms (see also the pioneering works

[
SW89
]
and
[
All86
]
). For further results in this direction we mention
[
Sav18
]
,

[
San20
]
,
[
DRKS20
]
,
[
San21
]
, and
[
DPGS24
]
.

The proof of theorem
1.6
cannot rely on the powerful variational machinery
available for the area integrand, and this is the main subtle issue. Instead, our method employs the
theory of curvature for closed sets as a key novel tool; cf.
[
HS22
]
for the general case of arbitrary closed sets in a uniformly convex (finite-dimensional) Banach space. This is achieved by exploiting the observation, already made
in
[
DRKS20
, Lemma 4.11]
, that results of
[
DPDRH19
]
imply that the support
of
any

n
-dimensional varifold in
\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
with bounded anisotropic mean curvature
is an
(n,h)
set; see
2.12
and
2.13
. Indeed Theorem
1.6
holds in the general setting of
(n,h)
-sets; cf. proof of
3.7
.

2
Preliminaries

Notation

2.1
.

In principle, but with some exceptions, we shall follow the notation of
Federer; see
[
Fed69
, pp. 669–671]
. For varifolds we follow the notation of
[
All72
]
.

We write
\mathscr{P}
for the set
of positive integers. Whenever
L\in\Hom(X,Y)
we write

L(x)
,
Lx
, or
\langle x,\,L\rangle
to denote the value of
L

on
x
. In case
S\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
the
closure
in
\mathbf{R}^{n+1}

of
S
is
\mathop{\mathrm{Clos}}S
, the
interior
is denoted
\Int S
, and its

topological boundary
in
\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
is
\partial S
. The
characteristic function
of
S\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
is

\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\boldsymbol{\chi}$}_{S}:\mathbf{R}^{n+1}\to\{0,1\}
. Given any set
X
the
identity map

on
X
is the function
\bm{1}_{X}:X\to X
. We write
\mathbf{B}(a,r)
and

\mathbf{U}(a,r)
for the
closed
and
open ball
of radius

0<r<\infty
and centre
a
(in the metric space that
a
belongs to,
which should be clear from the context). We set

\mathbb{S}^{n}=\mathbf{R}^{n+1}\cap\{x:|x|=1\}.

If
X
is a real vectorspace,
v\in X
, and
r\in\mathbf{R}
, the translation

\boldsymbol{\tau}_{v}:X\to X
and dilation
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{r}:X\to X
are defined by

\boldsymbol{\tau}_{v}(x)=v+x
and
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{r}(x)=rx
for
x\in X
.

The
scalar product
of

u,v\in\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
is written as
u\bullet v
and
|u|
is the

Euclidean norm
of
u
. We define the standard
polarity

\beta:\mathbf{R}^{n+1}\to\Hom(\mathbf{R}^{n+1},\mathbf{R})
by

\beta(u)v=u\bullet v\quad\text{for $u,v\in\mathbf{R}^{n+1}$}\,.

Note that given
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
the hyperplane orthogonal to
\nu
is
\ker\beta(\nu)=\lin\{\nu\}^{\perp}
. The
operator norm
with respect to

|\cdot|
is denoted by
\|\cdot\|
.

For
k,m\in\mathscr{P}
with
0\leq k\leq m
we denote by
\mathbf{G}(m,k)
the
Grassmannian
of
k
-dimensional planes in
\mathbf{R}^{m}
. If
T\in\mathbf{G}(m,k)
then we use the same symbol
T
to denote the orthogonal projection
T:\mathbf{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbf{R}^{m}
onto
T
. If
T\in\mathbf{G}(m,k)
, we set
T^{\perp}=\bm{1}_{\mathbf{R}^{m}}-T\in\mathbf{G}(m,m-k)
, the orthogonal complement of
T
.

2.2
.

As in
[
Fed69
, p. 669]

Q\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{n+1}\times\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
and

the

2.3
.

The
canonical projections
for the product

\mathbf{R}^{n+1}\times\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
are given by

(1)

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}:\mathbf{R}^{n+1}\times\mathbf{R}^{n+1}\rightarrow\mathbf{R}^{n+1}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{q}:\mathbf{R}^{n+1}\times\mathbf{R}^{n+1}\rightarrow\mathbf{R}^{n+1}

(2)

\displaystyle\text{so that}\quad\mathbf{p}(a,\eta)=a\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{q}(a,\eta)=\eta\quad\text{for $a,\eta\in\mathbf{R}^{n+1}$}\,.

2.4
.

Given a linear projection
T:\mathbf{R}^{m}\to\mathbf{R}^{m}
and

we define the

\mathbf{C}(T,a,r,s)=\mathbf{R}^{m}\cap\bigl\{z:|(\bm{1}_{\mathbf{R}^{m}}-T)(z-a)|<s,\;|T(z-a)|<r\bigr\}\,.

2.5
.

If
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
,
A\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
, and

we set

(1)

\displaystyle\graph_{\nu}(f)=\mathbf{R}^{n+1}\cap\bigl\{\sigma+f(\sigma)\nu:\sigma\in A\bigr\}

.

2.6
.

In the whole paper we assume
\phi

\mathscr{C}^{2}
-norm
on
\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
. Uniform convexity implies that there exists

\gamma(\phi)>0

\mathrm{D}^{2}\phi(u)(v,v)\geq\gamma(\phi)|v|^{2}\quad\text{for $u\in\mathbb{S}^{n}$ and $v\in\lin\{u\}^{\perp}$}\,.

For

(1)

\displaystyle c_{l}(\phi)=\sup\big\{\|\mathrm{D}^{k}\phi(\nu)\|:\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{n},\;k\in\mathbf{Z},\;0\leq k\leq l\big\}\,.

Note that since

-regular away from the origin the constant

coincides with the constant named “
\gamma

.

2.7
.

Let

.

.

2.9

[
Fed69

.

Let

. A set
\Sigma\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{n+1}

\mathbf{R}^{n+1}

. If, additionally,

2.10

A
n
-dimensional varifold
V
in an open subset

is a Radon measure over

n
-dimensional varifolds
in
\Omega

n
-dimensional varifold

-rectifiable set

Suppose
\phi

C^{1}
-norm on
\mathbf{R}^{n+1}

For

and the first
\phi
-variation of

in

The total variation

3

The main goal of this section is to prove Theorem

3.1 Lemma

(Basic estimates)
.

Suppose
\Omega\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
is open,
A\subseteq\Omega
is an

(n,h)
subset of
\Omega
with respect to
\phi
and

\tau=(\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{n})
is a (Euclidean) principle
frame-field of
A
. Then

(1)

{\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}}\kappa_{A,i}(a,\nu)\,\mathrm{D}^{2}\phi(\nu)(\tau_{i}(a,\nu),\tau_{i}(a,\nu))\leq h

and

(2)

{\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}}\kappa_{A,i}(a,\nu)\leq n\kappa_{A,n}(a,\nu)\leq n\gamma(\phi)^{-1}\bigl(h\phi(\nu)^{-1}+(n-1)c_{2}(\phi)\boldsymbol{r}_{A}(a,\nu)^{-1}\bigr)

for
\mathscr{H}^{n}
almost all
(a,\nu)\in N(A)|\Omega
. In particular,

\mathscr{H}^{n}\bigl((N(A)|\Omega)\!\mathop{\smallsetminus}\widetilde{N}_{n}(A)\bigr)=0.

Proof.

Choose
(a,\nu)\in\widetilde{N}(A)|\Omega
and

0<r<\underline{\boldsymbol{r}_{A}}(a,\nu)
. Without loss of generality we may assume

a=0
and we set
T=\lin\{\nu\}^{\perp}
. To verify the first
equality apply
[
HS22
, 3.29]
with
j=1
. Noting that

r\nu\in\Unp(A)\cap S(A,r)
, we combine
[
KS23
, Lemma 2.41(e) and Lemma
2.44]
to find
\varepsilon,\delta>0
and a semiconcave
function
f:T\rightarrow\mathbf{R}
such that

(3)

\displaystyle f(0)=r\,,\quad\mathrm{D}f(0)=0\,,\quad\text{$f$ is pointwise twice differentiable at $0$}\,,

(4)

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}^{2}f(0)(u,v)=-\mathrm{D}\boldsymbol{\nu}_{A}(r\nu)u\bullet v\qquad\text{for $u,v\in\Tan(S(A,r),r\nu)=T$}\,,

(5)

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}(\ker\beta(\nu),0,\varepsilon,\delta)\subseteq\Omega\,,

(6)

\displaystyle S(A,r)\cap\mathbf{C}(T,r\nu,\delta,\varepsilon)=\graph_{\nu}(f)\cap\mathbf{C}(T,r\nu,\delta,\varepsilon)\,,

(7)

\displaystyle\text{and}\quad\{x:\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\!\!A}(x)<r\}\cap\mathbf{C}(T,r\nu,\delta,\varepsilon)=\cato_{\nu}(f)\cap\mathbf{C}(T,r\nu,\delta,\varepsilon)\,.

Let
g:T\rightarrow\mathbf{R}
be defined by
g(\sigma)=f(\sigma)-r
for

\sigma\in T
and observe that

A\cap\mathbf{C}(\ker\beta(\nu),0,\varepsilon,\delta)\subseteq\mathop{\mathrm{Clos}}\cato_{\nu}(g)\,;

otherwise, if

x\in A\cap\mathbf{C}(\ker\beta(\nu),0,\varepsilon,\delta)\!\mathop{\smallsetminus}\mathop{\mathrm{Clos}}\cato_{\nu}(g)
and
y=T\,x
, then

(8)

\displaystyle x=y+s\nu\quad\text{for some $0<s<\infty$ with $g(y)<s$}\,,\quad f(y)<s+r\,,

(9)

\displaystyle y+(s+r)\nu=x+r\nu\in\mathbf{C}(T,r\nu,\delta,\varepsilon)\!\mathop{\smallsetminus}\mathop{\mathrm{Clos}}\cato_{\nu}(f)\,,\quad\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\!\!A}(x+r\nu)>r

but, since
x\in A
, there holds
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\!\!A}(x+r\nu)\leq r
.

Recall that

A\cap\mathbf{C}(\ker\beta(\nu),0,\varepsilon,\delta)
is an

(n,h)
subset
of
\mathbf{C}(\ker\beta(\nu),0,\varepsilon,\delta)
with
respect to
\phi
and we now know that

A\cap\mathbf{C}(\ker\beta(\nu),0,\varepsilon,\delta)\subseteq\mathop{\mathrm{Clos}}\cato_{\nu}(g)
; hence, we conclude
from
2.14

(10)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{A,i}(a+r\nu)\,\mathrm{D}^{2}\phi(\nu)(\tau_{i}(a,\nu),\tau_{i}(a,\nu))\leq h\,.

Observe, recalling
2.24
, that for
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}

{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{A,i}(a+r\nu)=\lim_{t\to\kappa_{A,i}(a,\nu)^{-}}\frac{t}{1+rt}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\kappa_{A,i}(a,\nu)}{1+r\kappa_{A,i}(a,\nu)}&&\text{if $\kappa_{A,i}(a,\nu)<\infty$}\,,\\
&\frac{1}{r}&&\text{if $\kappa_{A,i}(a,\nu)=\infty$}\,.\end{aligned}\right.

Since the choice of
0<r<\underline{\boldsymbol{r}_{A}}(a,\nu)
was arbitrary, we may pass
to the limit

and conclude from (
10

\kappa_{A,n}(a,\nu)<\infty
and

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\kappa_{A,i}(a,\nu)\,\mathrm{D}^{2}\phi(\nu)(\tau_{i}(a,\nu),\tau_{i}(a,\nu))\leq h.

In order to prove the second estimate we can further assume that

\kappa_{A,n}(a,\nu)>0
, otherwise the conclusion is trivially
true. Since
\kappa_{A,i}(a,\nu)\geq-\boldsymbol{r}_{A}(a,\nu)^{-1}
for

by

HS22

, we estimate

(11)

\kappa_{A,n}(a,\nu)\leq\gamma(\phi)^{-1}\mathrm{D}^{2}\phi(\nu)(\tau_{n}(a,\nu),\tau_{n}(a,\nu))\,\kappa_{A,n}(a,\nu)\\
\leq\gamma(\phi)^{-1}\Big(h-{\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}}\kappa_{A,i}(a,\nu)\,\mathrm{D}^{2}\phi(\nu)(\tau_{i}(a,\nu),\tau_{i}(a,\nu))\Big)\\
\leq\gamma(\phi)^{-1}\Big(h+\boldsymbol{r}_{A}(a,\nu)^{-1}{\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}}\mathrm{D}^{2}\phi(\nu)(\tau_{i}(a,\nu),\tau_{i}(a,\nu))\Big)\\
\leq\gamma(\phi)^{-1}\bigl(h+(n-1)c_{2}(\phi)\boldsymbol{r}_{A}(a,\nu)^{-1}\bigr)\,.\qed

3.2
Remark
.

This remark will be used in the proofs of
3.3

and
3.4
. Its purpose should be clear given the fact
that

statement rigorous suppose

is closed, and

Using

and its symbols together

[
HS22
, Theorem 3.16(a)]
we see that for

the set

meets each compact subset of

on an

-rectifiable set and

Henceforth, we deduce by coarea formula

Fed69
, 3.2.22]

and

\displaystyle{\textstyle\int_{\widetilde{N}_{n}(A)\cap R_{A}(t)}}J_{A}(a,\nu)\,g(a,\nu)\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{n}(a,\nu)

\displaystyle\qquad={\textstyle\int_{\mathbf{p}[\widetilde{N}_{n}(A)]}}{\textstyle\int_{\widetilde{N}_{n}(A,a)\cap R_{A}(t)}}g(a,\nu)\,d\mathscr{H}^{0}(\nu)\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{n}(a)

3.4 Lemma
.

Suppose

(n,h)

\Omega
with respect to

almost all

We argue by contradiction. Assume there is a compact set

For

\text{for $\mathscr{H}^{n}$ almost all $(a,\nu)\in N(A)|\Omega$ and $0<t<\boldsymbol{r}_{A}(a,\nu)$}\,.

This is a crucial observation that allows to employ the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem

Define

Since

and

\mathscr{L}^{n+1}(K_{r})=\mathscr{L}^{n+1}(K_{r}\!\mathop{\smallsetminus}A)\\
={\textstyle\sum_{d=0}^{n}}{\textstyle\int_{\widetilde{N}_{d}(A)}}J_{A}(a,\nu){\textstyle\int_{0}^{\rho(a,\nu,r)}}\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\boldsymbol{\chi}$}_{K_{r}}(a+t\nu)\,f(a,\nu,t)\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{n}(a,\nu)\\
={\textstyle\int_{N(A)|\Omega}}J_{A}(a,\nu){\textstyle\int_{0}^{\rho(a,\nu,r)}}\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\boldsymbol{\chi}$}_{K_{r}}(a+t\nu)\,f(a,\nu,t)\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{n}(a,\nu)

for

, where the last equality holds
by
3.1
and because
\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\boldsymbol{\chi}$}_{K_{r}}(a+t\nu)=0

whenever

Noting that

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\text{if $(a,\nu)\in N(A)|(A\!\mathop{\smallsetminus}K)$ and $0<t<r\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\!\!K}(a)$,
then $\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\boldsymbol{\chi}$}_{K_{r}}(a+t\nu)=0$}\,;

hence,

\lim_{r\to 0}\tfrac{1}{2r}{\textstyle\int_{0}^{\rho(a,\nu,r)}}\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\boldsymbol{\chi}$}_{K_{r}}(a+t\nu)\,f(a,\nu,t)\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{1}(t)=0\quad\text{for $(a,\nu)\in\widetilde{N}_{n}(A)|(\Omega\!\mathop{\smallsetminus}K)$}\,.

Recalling (
1
) and we get for

almost all

{\textstyle\int_{0}^{\rho(a,\nu,r)}}f(a,\nu,t)\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{1}(t)\leq{\textstyle\int_{0}^{\rho(a,\nu,r)}}\Delta\bigl(1+t\bigr)^{n}\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{1}(t)\\
=\tfrac{\Delta}{n+1}\bigl(\bigl(1+\rho(a,\nu,r)\bigr)^{n+1}-1\bigr)\leq\tfrac{\Delta}{n+1}\bigl(\bigl(1+r\bigr)^{n+1}-1\bigr)\,.

Notice also that

yields

{\textstyle\int_{N(A)|B}}J_{A}\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{n}<\infty\quad\text{whenever $B\subseteq\Omega$ is compact}\,.

Therefore, we may apply the dominated convergence theorem together

to get

=\tfrac{1}{2}{\textstyle\int_{N(A)|K}}J_{A}(a,\nu)\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{n}(a,\nu)\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\mathscr{H}^{n}(K)

and

=\liminf_{r\to 0^{+}}\tfrac{1}{2r}{\textstyle\int_{N(A)|\Omega}}J_{A}(a,\nu){\textstyle\int_{0}^{\rho(a,\nu,r)}}\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\boldsymbol{\chi}$}_{K_{r}}(a+t\nu)\,f(a,\nu,t)\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{n}(a,\nu)\\
\leq\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}\tfrac{1}{2r}{\textstyle\int_{N(A)|K}}J_{A}(a,\nu){\textstyle\int_{0}^{\rho(a,\nu,r)}}\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\boldsymbol{\chi}$}_{K_{r}}(a+t\nu)\,f(a,\nu,t)\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{n}(a,\nu)\\

=\tfrac{1}{2}\mathscr{H}^{n}(K)\,,

a contradiction.
∎

3.5 Lemma

(Coarea formula)
.

Suppose
\Omega\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
is an open set,
A\subseteq\Omega
is relatively
closed and satisfies

\mathscr{H}^{n}\bigl((N^{\phi}(A)|\Omega)\!\mathop{\smallsetminus}\widetilde{N}^{\phi}_{n}(A)\bigr)=0
. Then

(1)

{\textstyle\int_{\partial\mathcal{W}^{\phi}}}\mathscr{H}^{0}\{a:(a,\eta)\in B\}\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{n}(\eta)\\
={\textstyle\int_{\mathbf{p}[N^{\phi}(A)]}}{\textstyle\int_{\partial\mathcal{W}^{\phi}}}\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\boldsymbol{\chi}$}_{B}(a,\eta)\cdot{\textstyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}}|\kappa^{\phi}_{A,i}(a,\eta)|\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{0}(\eta)\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{n}(a)\\
\text{for any $\mathscr{H}^{n}$-measurable $B\subseteq N^{\phi}(A)$}\,.

Proof.

Let
B\subseteq N^{\phi}(A)
be
\mathscr{H}^{n}
-measurable. By the Lebesgue monotone convergence
theorem it is enough to prove the formula assuming
\mathscr{H}^{n}(B)<\infty
. Let

\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{n}
and
\zeta_{1},\dots,\zeta_{n}
be the maps on

\widetilde{N}^{\phi}(A)
defined in
2.29
. For brevity of the notation the

(\mathscr{H}^{n}\mathop{\rule[1.0pt]{0.5pt}{6.0pt}\rule[1.0pt]{4.0pt}{0.5pt}}\nolimits B,n)
approximate
n
-dimensional Jacobian shall be denoted

J^{B}_{n}f(x)=\|{\textstyle\boldsymbol{\bigwedge}}_{n}(\mathscr{H}^{n}\mathop{\rule[1.0pt]{0.5pt}{6.0pt}\rule[1.0pt]{4.0pt}{0.5pt}}\nolimits B,n)\ap\mathrm{D}f(x)\|\,,

whenever
f
is a function which is
(\mathscr{H}^{n}\mathop{\rule[1.0pt]{0.5pt}{6.0pt}\rule[1.0pt]{4.0pt}{0.5pt}}\nolimits B,n)
approximately
differentiable at
x\in B
; cf.
[
Fed69
, 3.2.16, 3.2.20]
. Since

\mathscr{H}^{n}(N^{\phi}(A)|\Omega\!\mathop{\smallsetminus}\widetilde{N}^{\phi}_{n}(A))=0
, it follows
from
2.29
that for
\mathscr{H}^{n}
almost all
(a,\eta)\in B
there holds

(2)

\displaystyle J^{B}_{n}\mathbf{q}(a,\eta)

\displaystyle=\sup\bigl\{\big|{\textstyle\boldsymbol{\bigwedge}}_{n}\mathbf{q}(\xi)\big|:\xi\in{\textstyle\boldsymbol{\bigwedge}}_{n}\Tan^{n}(\mathscr{H}^{n}\mathop{\rule[1.0pt]{0.5pt}{6.0pt}\rule[1.0pt]{4.0pt}{0.5pt}}\nolimits B,(a,\eta)),\,|\xi|=1\bigr\}

(3)

\displaystyle=\biggl|{\textstyle\boldsymbol{\bigwedge}}_{n}\mathbf{q}\biggl(\frac{\zeta_{1}(a,\eta)\wedge\cdots\wedge\zeta_{n}(a,\eta)}{|\zeta_{1}(a,\eta)\wedge\cdots\wedge\zeta_{n}(a,\eta)|}\biggr)\biggr|

(4)

\displaystyle=\frac{|\tau_{1}(a,\eta)\wedge\cdots\wedge\tau_{n}(a,\eta)|}{|\zeta_{1}(a,\eta)\wedge\cdots\wedge\zeta_{n}(a,\eta)|}\prod_{i=1}^{n}|\kappa^{\phi}_{A,i}(a,\eta)|

(5)

\displaystyle=J^{B}_{n}\mathbf{p}(a,\eta)\cdot{\textstyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}}|\kappa^{\phi}_{A,i}(a,\eta)|\,.

Apply Federer’s coarea formula (see
[
HS22
, Lemma 2.1]
), first
with the map
\mathbf{q}|B
and then with the map
\mathbf{p}|B
(notice that

\mathbf{p}[N^{\phi}(A)]
is countably
n
-rectifiable), to get

(6)

{\textstyle\int_{\partial\mathcal{W}^{\phi}}}\mathscr{H}^{0}\{a:(a,\eta)\in B\}\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{n}(\eta)={\textstyle\int_{B}}J^{B}_{n}\mathbf{q}(a,\eta)\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{n}(a,\eta)\\
={\textstyle\int_{B}}J^{B}_{n}\mathbf{p}(a,\eta)\cdot{\textstyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}}|\kappa^{\phi}_{A,i}(a,\eta)|\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{n}(a,\eta)\\
={\textstyle\int_{\mathbf{p}[B]}}{\textstyle\int_{\mathbf{p}^{-1}\{a\}\cap B}}{\textstyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}}|\kappa^{\phi}_{A,i}(a,\eta)|\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{0}(x,\eta)\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{n}(a)\,,

which completes the proof.
∎

3.6 Lemma

3.9 Lemma

(\mathscr{H}^{1},1)

Proof.

Let

. Define inductively closed intervals

(2)

\displaystyle\sup I_{j,2k+1}=\sup I_{j-1,k}\,,\quad\inf I_{j,2k+1}=\sup I_{j-1,k}-4^{-j}\,.

Next, set

J_{j}={{\textstyle\bigcup}}\bigl\{I_{j,k}:0\leq k\leq 2^{j}\bigr\}\,,\quad C_{j}=J_{j}\times J_{j}\,,\quad\text{and}\quad\Sigma={{\textstyle\bigcap}}\bigl\{C_{j}:j\in\mathscr{P}\bigr\}\,.

Note that
D={{\textstyle\bigcap}}\bigl\{J_{j}:j\in\mathscr{P}\bigr\}

unrectifiable

Satisfying sequences for rainbow partite matchings
Andrey Kupavskii, Elizaveta Popova

Abstract
Let ℱ 1 , … , ℱ s ⊂ [ n ] k \mathcal{F}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{F}_{s}\subset[n]^{k} be a collection of s s families. In this paper, we address the following question: for which sequences f 1 , … , f s f_{1},\ldots,f_{s} the conditions | ℱ i | > f i |\mathcal{F}_{i}|>f_{i} imply that the families contain a rainbow matching, that is, there are pairwise disjoint F 1 ∈ ℱ 1 , … ​ F s ∈ ℱ s F_{1}\in\mathcal{F}_{1},\ldots F_{s}\in\mathcal{F}_{s} ? We call such sequences satisfying . Kiselev and the first author verified the conjecture of Aharoni and Howard and showed that f 1 = … = f s = ( s − 1 ) ​ n k − 1 f_{1}=\ldots=f_{s}=(s-1)n^{k-1} is satisfying for s > 470 s>470 . This is the best possible if the restriction is uniform over all families. However, it turns out that much more can be said about asymmetric restrictions. In this paper, we investigate this question in several regimes and in particular answer the questions asked by Kiselev and Kupavskii. We use a variety of methods, including concentration and anticoncentration results, spread approximations, and Combinatorial Nullstellenzats.

Throughout this section, we think of our families residing on the ground set
[k]\times[n]
. Accordingly, an element of the ground set is denoted
(j,a)\in[k]\times[n].
The
k

1

(j,a)

Let us introduce some notation, a part of which is somewhat non-standard. Given families
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{S}
and sets

define

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(X)

\displaystyle:=\{F\setminus X:X\subset F,F\in\mathcal{F}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}[X]

\displaystyle:=\{F:X\subset F,F\in\mathcal{F}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(X,Y)

\displaystyle:=\{F\setminus X:F\cap Y=X\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{S})

\displaystyle:=\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal{S}}\mathcal{F}(A),

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}[\mathcal{S}]

\displaystyle:=\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal{S}}\mathcal{F}[A].

The approach in this section is a variant of what is called the

]

We say that

The spread approximation technique is based on the following theorem.

\Pr[\exists F\in\mathcal{F}\ :\ F\subset W]\geq 1-\Big(\frac{2}{\log_{2}(r\delta)}\Big)^{\beta}k.

(1)

Actually, Stoeckl

Theorem 9

For

\mathcal{S}_{i}^{(2)}
that may contain one of the elements of
\cup_{j\in[s]\setminus\{i\}}B_{i}
. Therefore, there is at least one set from
\mathcal{S}_{i}^{(2)}
disjoint from all the sets in the cross-matching, a contradiction. We conclude that the families
\mathcal{S}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{S}_{i-1},\mathcal{S}_{i+1},\ldots,S_{s}
are cross-dependent, and that we can replace
\mathcal{S}_{i}
by
\{\emptyset\}
without violating the cross-dependent property. Consequently, we can assume
|\mathcal{S}_{i}^{(2)}|\leq 4(s-1)^{2}
for each
i
.

Since spread approximations are cross-dependent, for at least one
i
we must have
\{\emptyset\}\notin\mathcal{S}_{i}
and
|\mathcal{S}_{i}^{(1)}|\leq i-1
. (otherwise, Hall’s theorem guarantees the existence of a cross-matching.) Combining it with the bound on
|\mathcal{S}_{i}^{(2)}|
and
\mathcal{F}_{i}^{\prime}
, we get

|\mathcal{F}_{i}|\leq(i-1)n^{k-1}+4(s-1)^{2}n^{k-2}+r^{3}n^{k-3}=(i-1)n^{k-1}+4(s-1)^{2}n^{k-2}+2^{15}s^{3}\log_{2}^{3}(sk)n^{k-3}.

On the contrapositive, if for each
i\in[s]
the size of
\mathcal{F}_{i}
is strictly bigger than the right-hand side, then they contain a cross-matching.
∎

4
Anticoncentration

It is often convenient to think of
{\bf x}\in[n]^{k}
as of a vector
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}),
where
x_{i}\in[n].
For
a\in[n]
and
i\in[k]
we call a family

\mathcal{H}_{i}(a):=\{{\bf x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})\in[n]^{k}:x_{i}=a\}

a
hyperplane
.

Fix a family
\mathcal{F}\subset[n]^{k}
. One of the important steps in our approach is to study the random variable

\xi_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathcal{M})=|\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{M}|,

where
\mathcal{M}
is a uniformly random perfect matching in
[n]^{k},
i.e., a collection of
n
pairwise disjoint
k
-element sets
M_{1},\ldots,M_{n}
that together cover the ground set
[n]\times[k]
.

The following anticoncentration result gives a structural characterization of the families
\mathcal{F}
for which
\xi_{\mathcal{F}}
is almost constant.

Theorem 11
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subset[n]^{k}
,
|\mathcal{F}|=(s-1)n^{k-1}+1
and define

p_{\mathcal{F}}:=\mathbb{P}[\xi_{\mathcal{F}}\neq s-1].

1.

Let
n\geq 4
and
p_{\mathcal{F}}<1/8
. Fix some
i\in[k]
. Then we have two possibilities. The first possibility is that for any

a\in[n]
we have

|\mathcal{H}_{i}(a)\cap\mathcal{F}|\leq 2p_{\mathcal{F}}n^{k-1}+(s-1)n^{k-2}.

The second possibility is that there exists a partition
[n]=F\sqcup T,
such that for any
a\in F
we have

|\mathcal{H}_{i}(a)\cap\mathcal{F}|\geq(1-2p_{\mathcal{F}})n^{k-1},

in which case we call
\mathcal{H}_{i}(a)

\mathcal{F}
-fat, and for any
a\in T
we have

|\mathcal{H}_{i}(a)\cap\mathcal{F}|\leq 2p_{\mathcal{F}}n^{k-1},

in which case we call
\mathcal{H}_{i}(a)

\mathcal{F}
-thin.

2.

Suppose that
n\geq 8
,
s\geq 3
and

p_{\mathcal{F}}\leq\frac{1}{4n(s-1)}.

Then
\mathcal{F}
-fat hyperplanes are parallel, i.e., they all have the form
\mathcal{H}_{i}(a)
for some fixed
i\in[k]
.

3.

Suppose that
n\geq 8
,
s\geq 3
and

p_{\mathcal{F}}\leq\frac{1}{4n(s-1)}.

Then there are exactly
s-1

\mathcal{F}
-fat hyperplanes.

Proof.

1.
W.l.o.g we can assume
i=1
.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{a,b}:=\{{\bf s}\in[n]^{k-1}:(a,{\bf s})\in\mathcal{F},(b,{\bf s})\notin\mathcal{F}\}
. Partition all perfect matchings on
[n]^{k}
into pairs:

\mathcal{M}_{1}:=\big\{(1,{\bf s_{1}}),...,(a,{\bf s_{a}}),...,(b,{\bf s_{b}}),...,(n,{\bf s_{n}})\big\}\longleftrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{2}:=\big\{(1,{\bf s_{1}}),...,(a,{\bf s_{b}}),...,(b,{\bf s_{a}}),...,(n,{\bf s_{n}})\big\}.

Select only the pairs
\mathcal{M}_{1}\longleftrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{2}
such that
{\bf s_{a}}\in\mathcal{P}_{a,b}
and
{\bf s_{b}}\notin\mathcal{P}_{a,b}
. There are at least
|\mathcal{P}_{a,b}|
choices for
{\bf s_{a}}
and
n^{k-1}-|\mathcal{P}_{a,b}|
choices for
{\bf s_{b}}
. Some of them, however, are not disjoint. We deal with it in the following claim.

Claim 12
.

The number of disjoint pairs between
\mathcal{P}_{a,b}
and
\bar{\mathcal{P}}_{a,b}:=[n]^{k-1}\setminus\mathcal{P}_{a,b}
is at least

\frac{(n-1)^{k-1}-(n-1)^{k-3}}{n^{k-1}}|\mathcal{P}_{a,b}|(n^{k-1}-|P_{a,b}|).

Proof.

We use a variant of the expander-mixing lemma
[
3
, Theorem 9.2.1]
, which states the following. Take a
d
-regular graph
G
with an adjacency matrix
A
that has eigenvalues
d=\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\ldots.
Then the number of edges between a set
X\subset X(G)
and its complement
\bar{X}
is at least
\frac{(d-\lambda_{2})|X||\bar{X}|}{|V(G)|}
. We apply this statement to the
(k-1)
st power of the complete graph
K_{n}
. Its vertex set is
[n]^{k-1}
, and edges connect disjoint sets. The eigenvalues of this graph are products of the eigenvalues of
K_{n}
:
(-1)^{i}(n-1)^{k-i}.
That is, in terms of the expander-mixing lemma,
d=(n-1)^{k-1}
and
\lambda_{2}=(n-1)^{k-3}
. Plugging these values, we get the statement.
∎

Using this claim, we get that there are at least

\frac{(n-1)^{k-1}-(n-1)^{k-3}}{n^{k-1}}|\mathcal{P}_{a,b}|(n^{k-1}-|P_{a,b}|)((n-2)!)^{k-1}

disjoint pairs of matchings as before the claim. At the same time,
\xi_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathcal{M}_{1})\neq\xi_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathcal{M}_{2})
and thus
\xi_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathcal{M}_{i})\neq s-1
for at least one
i\in[2]
. It implies that the number of such pairs is at most
p_{\mathcal{F}}(n!)^{k-1}
. Hence,

\frac{(n-1)^{k-1}-(n-1)^{k-3}}{n^{k-1}}|\mathcal{P}_{a,b}|\big(n^{k-1}-|\mathcal{P}_{a,b}|\big)\big((n-2)!\big)^{k-1}\leq p_{\mathcal{F}}(n!)^{k-1},

which is equivalent to

\big(1-(n-1)^{-2}\big)|\mathcal{P}_{a,b}|\big(n^{k-1}-|\mathcal{P}_{a,b}|\big)\leq p_{\mathcal{F}}n^{2k-2}.

(2)

Looking at this quadratic inequality, we see that (for
n-1\geq 3,

p_{\mathcal{F}}\leq 1/5
) at least one of the following should hold:

\displaystyle|\mathcal{P}_{a,b}|

\displaystyle<2p_{\mathcal{F}}n^{k-1},

(3)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{P}_{a,b}|

\displaystyle>(1-2p_{\mathcal{F}})n^{k-1},

(4)

otherwise the product on the LHS of (
2
) is at least
(1-1/9)(2p_{\mathcal{F}})(1-2p_{\mathcal{F}})n^{2k-2}>p_{\mathcal{F}}n^{2k-2}.

Next, we consider two cases, corresponding to the two cases in the statement. Assume first that for any
a,b\in[n]
(
3
) holds. Then

|\mathcal{H}_{1}(b)\cap\mathcal{F}|\geq|\mathcal{H}_{1}(a)\cap\mathcal{F}|-|\mathcal{P}_{a,b}|>|\mathcal{H}_{1}(a)\cap\mathcal{F}|-2p_{\mathcal{F}}n^{k-1}.

Summing over
b\in[n]
, we get

(s-1)n^{k-1}+1\geq|\mathcal{F}|>n|\,\mathcal{H}_{1}(a)\cap\mathcal{F}|-2(n-1)p_{\mathcal{F}}n^{k-1}.

Doing routine calculations, we get
|\mathcal{H}_{1}(a)\cap\mathcal{F}|\leq 2p_{\mathcal{F}}n^{k-1}+(s-1)n^{k-2}
, valid for each
a\in[n]
. This corresponds to the first case.

Next, assume that there are
a,b\in[n]
such that
|\mathcal{P}_{a,b}|>(1-2p_{\mathcal{F}})n^{k-1}
. Then
|\mathcal{H}_{1}(a)\cap\mathcal{F}|\geq|\mathcal{P}_{a,b}|\geq(1-2p_{\mathcal{F}})n^{k-1},
where the first inequality holds for any
b\neq a
. Likewise,
|\mathcal{H}_{1}(b)\cap\mathcal{F}|\leq n^{k-1}-|\mathcal{P}_{a,b}|\leq 2p_{\mathcal{F}}n^{k-1}.
We claim that in this case for any
c\in[n]
we either have
|\mathcal{H}_{1}(c)|\leq 2p_{\mathcal{F}}n^{k-1}
or
|\mathcal{H}_{1}(c)|\geq(1-2p_{\mathcal{F}})n^{k-1}
. Arguing indirectly, assume that
(1-2p_{\mathcal{F}})n^{k-1}>|\mathcal{H}_{1}(c)|\geq\frac{1}{2}n^{k-1}
. Then

(1-2p_{\mathcal{F}})n^{k-1}>|\mathcal{H}_{1}(c)|\geq|\mathcal{P}_{c,b}|\geq|\mathcal{H}_{1}(c)|-|\mathcal{H}_{1}(b)|\geq\big(\frac{1}{2}-2p_{\mathcal{F}}\big)n^{k-1}>2p_{\mathcal{F}}n^{k-1},

a contradiction with (
3
), (
4
). The case
\frac{1}{2}n^{k-1}\geq|\mathcal{H}_{1}(c)|>2p_{\mathcal{F}}n^{k-1}
is analogous. This situation corresponds to the second case.

2. Arguing indirectly, assume that one of the
\mathcal{F}
-fat hyperplanes is
\mathcal{H}_{1}(n)
and suppose that some other
\mathcal{F}
-fat hyperplane
\mathcal{H}
intersects it. W.l.o.g. assume that
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{2}(n)
. Consider the collection
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}
of all pefect matchings in
[n]^{k}
such that one of the sets
{\bf x}
in the matching satisfies
x_{1}=x_{2}=n
. Then a uniform random perfect matching
\mathcal{M}
in
[n]^{k}
conditioned on containing such
{\bf x}
is a uniform random perfect matching
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}
.

Consider the random variable
\xi^{\prime}_{\mathcal{F}}:=|\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{M}^{\prime}|
. We have

\mathbb{E}\xi^{\prime}_{\mathcal{F}}\leq 1+\frac{(s-1)n^{k-1}+1-2(1-2p_{\mathcal{F}})n^{k-1}+n^{k-2}}{n^{k-1}}<s-\frac{3}{2}+\frac{1}{n}+\frac{1}{n^{k-1}}\leq s-\frac{5}{4},

where in the second inequality we use
p_{\mathcal{F}}<\frac{1}{8}
(which follows from
p_{\mathcal{F}}\leq\frac{1}{4n(s-1)}
) and in the last inequality we use
n\geq 8.
We have
(s-1)\mathbb{P}[\xi^{\prime}_{\mathcal{F}}\geq s-1]\leq\mathbb{E}\xi^{\prime}_{\mathcal{F}}<s-\frac{5}{4}
, and thus
\mathbb{P}[\xi^{\prime}_{\mathcal{F}}<s-1]>\frac{1}{4(s-1)}
. Recall that
\mathcal{M}
is a uniform random matching on
[n]^{k}
. We get that

\mathbb{P}[\xi_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathcal{M})<s-1]\geq\mathbb{P}[\xi^{\prime}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime})<s-1]\cdot\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{M}\in\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}]>\frac{1}{4(s-1)}\cdot\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{M}\in\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}]=\frac{1}{4(s-1)n},

a contradiction.

3. Since
p_{\mathcal{F}}<\frac{1}{4n(s-1)}
, all
\mathcal{F}
-fat hyperplanes are parallel by part 2 of the theorem. Suppose w.l.o.g. that
\mathcal{F}
-fat hyperplanes have the form
\mathcal{H}_{1}(a)
, where
a\in F\subset[n].
The hyperplanes
\mathcal{H}_{1}(b),

b\in T:=[n]\setminus F
are
\mathcal{F}
-thin.

Suppose that
|F|<s-1
. Then

|\mathcal{F}|\leq|F|\cdot n^{k-1}+|T|\cdot 2p_{\mathcal{F}}n^{k-1}\leq(s-2)n^{k-1}+2p_{\mathcal{F}}n^{k}<(s-1)n^{k-1},

provided
p_{\mathcal{F}}<\frac{1}{2n}
.

Suppose that
|F|>s-1
. then

|\mathcal{F}|\geq|F|\cdot(1-2p_{\mathcal{F}})n^{k-1}\geq\big(1-\frac{1}{2n})sn^{k-1}>(s-1)n^{k-1}+1,

where the first inequality is valid provided
p_{\mathcal{F}}\leq\frac{1}{4n}.

∎

5
(Anti)concentration: proof of Theorem
2

In this section we shall prove Theorem
2
and also provide an easy argument that both sequences under consideration are satisfying for
n\geq k^{2}s^{2}
.

For the proof of Theorem
2
we will need the following concentration result proven in
[
9
]
:

Theorem 13

(Kiselev, Kupavskii
[
9
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subset[n]^{k}
,
|\mathcal{F}|=\alpha n^{k}
. Let
\eta
be a random variable
|\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{M}|
, where
\mathcal{M}
is a uniformly random perfect matching in
[n]^{k}
. Then for any
\lambda>0
and
\delta\in\{-1,1\}
the following holds

\mathbb{P}[\delta\cdot(\eta-\alpha n)\geq 2\lambda]\leq 2\,\exp\big(-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{\alpha n/2+2\lambda}\big).

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Assume the contrary, i.e. that there exists a cross-dependent sequence of families
\{\mathcal{F}_{i}\}_{i}
, such that
|\mathcal{F}_{i}|>f_{i}
. We may w.l.o.g. assume that
|\mathcal{F}_{i}|=f_{i}+1
. Put

M:=\left\lceil C\sqrt{s\log s}\right\rceil+1.

Note that in this sequence there are
M
"large" families, i.e. families of size
(s-1)n^{k-1}+1
.

As in the previous section, let
\mathcal{M}
be a uniformly random perfect matching in
[n]^{k}
.
Consider a bipartite graph
G_{\mathcal{M}}
with one part being
\{\mathcal{F}_{i}\}_{i\in[s]}
, the other part being
\mathcal{M}
, and the edges connecting each family
\mathcal{F}_{i}
to the elements of
\mathcal{M}
that it contains, i.e., for
i\in[s]
,
F\in\mathcal{M}
,
(\mathcal{F}_{i},F)\in E(G_{\mathcal{M}})
iff
F\in\mathcal{F}_{i}
. Clearly, the degrees of
\mathcal{F}_{i}
’s in this graph are
deg(\mathcal{F}_{i})=|\mathcal{F}_{i}\cap\mathcal{M}|
.

Consider the following two events:

•

A_{1}
: for every
i\in[s-M]
we have
deg(\mathcal{F}_{i})\geq i+\frac{M}{2}
;

•

A_{2}
: for every
i\in[s-M+1,s]
we have
deg(\mathcal{F}_{i})\geq M
.

Theorem
13
implies that
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are very likely to hold simultaneously, namely we have

Claim 14
.

We have

p:=\mathbb{P}[\bar{A_{1}}\vee\bar{A_{2}}]\leq 4\,s^{-\frac{C^{2}}{20}+1}

for
s\geq s_{0}(C)
.

Proof.

By Theorem
13
with
\delta=-1
and
\lambda=C\sqrt{s\log s}/4-1/2\leq(C\sqrt{s\log s}-M/2)/2
, for any
i\in[s-M]
we have

\mathbb{P}\left[deg(\mathcal{F}_{i})<i+\frac{M}{2}\right]\leq 2\,\exp\left(-\frac{(C\sqrt{s\log s}/4-1/2)^{2}}{(s-1)/2+2(C\sqrt{s\log s}/4-1/2)}\right)\leq 2\,s^{-\frac{C^{2}}{20}},

where the last inequality is valid for
10\leq C\leq\sqrt{s/\log s}.

Similarly, by Theorem
13
with
\delta=-1
and
\lambda=s/4\leq(s-1-M)/2
for any
i\in[s-M+1,s]
and for
s\geq 100
we have

\mathbb{P}[deg(\mathcal{F}_{i})<M]\leq 2\,e^{-\frac{s}{16}}.

Then we have

p:=\mathbb{P}[\bar{A_{1}}\vee\bar{A_{2}}]\leq s\cdot 2\,s^{-\frac{C^{2}}{20}}+s\cdot 2\,e^{-\frac{s}{16}}\leq 4\,s^{-\frac{C^{2}}{20}+1},

where the last inequality holds for
s>C^{2}
.
∎

The families
\mathcal{F}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{F}_{s}
are cross-dependent, and thus there is no matching in
G_{\mathcal{M}}
that covers all
\mathcal{F}_{i}
’s. Thus, the condition of Hall’s lemma cannot be satisfied. Specifically, there is a set
B\subset[s]
such that
\{\mathcal{F}_{i}\}_{i\in B}
violates the condition, that is, the size of the set of neighbours of
\{\mathcal{F}_{i}\}_{i\in B}
is less than
|B|
. In particular, for each
i\in B
we have
|\mathcal{F}_{i}\cap\mathcal{M}|=deg(\mathcal{F}_{i})<|B|
.

Let us define a random variable
r_{\mathcal{M}}
. If both
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
hold, put
r_{\mathcal{M}}:=|B\cap[s-M+1,s]|
. If either
\bar{A_{1}}
or
\bar{A_{2}}
holds, set
r_{\mathcal{M}}=0
. Let us analyze the structure of
B
in the assumption that
A_{1},A_{2}
hold.

Assume that
B\cap[s-M]=\emptyset
. Since
A_{2}
holds, for any
i\in B
we have
deg(\mathcal{F}_{i})\geq M\geq|B|
, and thus
B
cannot violate Hall’s condition. Thus
B\cap[s-M]\neq\emptyset
.

Assume next that
r_{\mathcal{M}}<M/2
. Then
A_{1}
implies that for
j=\max\{i:i\in B\cap[s-M]\}
we have
deg(\mathcal{F}_{j})\geq j+\frac{M}{2}\geq|B|
, and thus
B
satisfies Hall’s condition, a contradiction. Overall, if
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
hold,
r_{\mathcal{M}}\geq M/2
.

Following
[
9
]
, define for
i\in[M]
:

\zeta_{i}:=|\mathcal{F}_{s-M+i}\cap\mathcal{M}|-s+1.

By the definition of
B
, for each
j\in B\cap[s-M+1,s]
we have
deg(\mathcal{F}_{j})\leq s-M+r_{\mathcal{M}}-1
, and thus for the corresponding
i
we have
\zeta_{i}\leq-M+r_{\mathcal{M}}\leq 0
.

Summing this over all
j
from
B
, we get the following for each
\mathcal{M}
:

\sum_{i=1}^{M}\zeta_{i}(\mathcal{M})\cdot{\rm I}[\zeta_{i}(\mathcal{M})\leq 0]\leq r_{\mathcal{M}}(r_{\mathcal{M}}-M).

Note also that this tivially holds when
r_{\mathcal{M}}=0
(in particular, when
A_{1}
or
A_{2}
are not valid).
It was shown in
[
9
]
that
\mathbb{E}[\zeta_{i}|\zeta_{i}>0]\leq 3.7\sqrt{s\log s}=:x
. So we have

0<\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{M}\zeta_{i}\right]=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\mathbb{E}[\zeta_{i}|\zeta_{i}>0]\mathbb{P}[\zeta_{i}>0]+\sum_{i=1}^{M}\mathbb{E}[\zeta_{i}|\zeta_{i}\leq 0]\mathbb{P}[\zeta_{i}\leq 0]\leq\\
\leq x\sum_{i=1}^{M}\mathbb{P}[\zeta_{i}>0]+\mathbb{E}[r_{\mathcal{M}}(r_{\mathcal{M}}-M)]\leq x\mathbb{E}[M-r_{\mathcal{M}}]+\mathbb{E}[r_{\mathcal{M}}(r_{\mathcal{M}}-M)]=\mathbb{E}[(r_{\mathcal{M}}-M)(r_{\mathcal{M}}-x)].

The first inequality above is valid since
\mathbb{E}[\zeta_{i}]=\mathbb{E}[|\mathcal{F}_{s-M+i}\cap\mathcal{M}|]-s+1=\frac{1}{n^{k-1}}>0
.

If both
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
hold for
\mathcal{M}
, then
r_{\mathcal{M}}\geq\frac{M}{2}\geq\frac{C}{2}\sqrt{s\log s}
. Since we always have
r_{\mathcal{M}}\leq M
, the expression
(r_{\mathcal{M}}-M)(r_{\mathcal{M}}-x)
is negative when
A_{1},A_{2}
hold, and we have
(r_{\mathcal{M}}-M)(r_{\mathcal{M}}-x)\leq(\frac{C}{2}\sqrt{s\log s}-x)(r_{\mathcal{M}}-M)
. If either
A_{1}
or
A_{2}
does not hold, we have
(r_{\mathcal{M}}-M)(r_{\mathcal{M}}-x)=xM
by definition. Thus, we can continue the displayed chain of inequalities and upper-bound
\mathbb{E}[(r_{\mathcal{M}}-M)(r_{\mathcal{M}}-x)]
as follows

0<(C/2-3.7)\sqrt{s\log s}\,\,\mathbb{E}[(r_{\mathcal{M}}-M)|A_{1},A_{2}](1-p)+xMp\leq\\
\leq-(C/2-3.7)\sqrt{s\log s}\,\,\mathbb{P}[(r_{\mathcal{M}}<M)|A_{1},A_{2}](1-p)+xMp\leq\\
\leq-(C/2-3.7)\sqrt{s\log s}\,\,(\mathbb{P}[(r_{\mathcal{M}}<M)]-p)+xMp=\\
=-(C/2-3.7)\sqrt{s\log s}\,\,\mathbb{P}[(r_{\mathcal{M}}<M)]+p\left(xM+(C/2-3.7)\sqrt{s\log s}\right).

(5)

Recall that
r_{\mathcal{M}}\leq M
and equality may hold only if
deg(\mathcal{F}_{i})\leq s-1
for all
i\in[s-M+1,s]
, and, moreover,
|\bigcup_{i=s-M+1}^{s}\mathcal{F}_{i}\cap\mathcal{M}|\leq s-1
. Thus,

\mathbb{P}[r_{\mathcal{M}}<M]\geq\mathbb{P}\left[\Big|\bigcup_{i=s-M+1}^{s}\mathcal{F}_{i}\cap\mathcal{M}\Big|>s-1\right]\geq\mathbb{P}[deg(\mathcal{F}_{i})>s-1]

for any
i\in[s-M+1,s]
.

Our anticoncentration result Theorem
11
gives bounds that depend on
p_{\mathcal{F}}=\mathbb{P}[|\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{M}|\neq s-1]
, while the bound on
\mathbb{P}[r_{\mathcal{M}}<M]
above involves
\mathbb{P}[|\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{M}|>s-1]
. In order to relate these two probabilies, we need the following simple claim.

Claim 15
.

For any
\mathcal{F}\subset[n]^{k}
with
|\mathcal{F}|\geq(s-1)n^{k-1}
we have

\mathbb{P}[|\mathcal{M}\cap\mathcal{F}|>s-1]\geq\frac{\mathbb{P}[|\mathcal{M}\cap\mathcal{F}|\neq s-1]}{n}.

Proof.

We have

s-1\leq\mathbb{E}[|\mathcal{M}\cap\mathcal{F}|]\leq(s-2)\mathbb{P}[|\mathcal{M}\cap\mathcal{F}|<s-1]+\\
+(s-1)(1-\mathbb{P}[|\mathcal{M}\cap\mathcal{F}|<s-1]-\mathbb{P}[|\mathcal{M}\cap\mathcal{F}|>s-1])+\\
+n\mathbb{P}[|\mathcal{M}\cap\mathcal{F}|>s-1],

and thus

\mathbb{P}[|\mathcal{M}\cap\mathcal{F}|>s-1]\geq\frac{\mathbb{P}[|\mathcal{M}\cap\mathcal{F}|\neq s-1]}{n-s+2}\geq\frac{\mathbb{P}[|\mathcal{M}\cap\mathcal{F}|\neq s-1]}{n}.

∎

Now we have three cases. In the first case,
|\bigcup_{i=s-M+1}^{s}\mathcal{F}_{i}\cap\mathcal{M}|
is equal to
s-1
with high probability. By anticoncentration, this imposes a special structure on all "large"
\mathcal{F}_{i}
’s. In the second case, we deal with
n\geq s^{5}
. We use spread approximations and show that if families are cross-dependent, then we must get the same special structure as in the first case (and thus get a reduction to the first case). In the remaining case,
|\bigcup_{i=s-M+1}^{s}\mathcal{F}_{i}\cap\mathcal{M}|
is not too concentrated and
n\leq s^{5}
. As we will show, this contradicts inequality (
5
) for sufficiently large
s
.

Case 1:

\mathbb{P}[|\bigcup_{i=s-M+1}^{s}\mathcal{F}_{i}\cap\mathcal{M}|>s-1]<\frac{1}{4sn^{2}}
.

The assumption implies that for each
i\in[s-M+1,s]
we have

p_{\mathcal{F}_{i}}=\mathbb{P}[|\mathcal{F}_{i}\cap\mathcal{M}|\neq s-1]\leq n\mathbb{P}[|\mathcal{F}_{i}\cap\mathcal{M}|>s-1]<\frac{1}{4sn}.

Thus, Theorem
11
implies that there are
s-1
parallel hyperplanes each containing at least
(1-2p_{\mathcal{F}_{i}})n^{k-1}
elements of
\mathcal{F}_{i}
(i.e.
\mathcal{F}_{i}
-fat hyperplanes), and any other hyperplane contains at most
2p_{\mathcal{F}_{i}}n^{k-1}+(s-1)n^{k-2}
elements of
\mathcal{F}_{i}
. If
\mathcal{F}_{i}
-fat hyperplanes are not the same for all
i\in[s-M+1,s]
, then
|\bigcup_{i=s-M+1}^{s}\mathcal{F}_{i}|\geq(s-1)n^{k-1}+(1-\frac{2}{4sn})n^{k-1}-(\frac{2}{4sn}n^{k-1}+(s-1)n^{k-2})
. Then
\mathbb{E}[|\bigcup_{i=s-M+1}^{s}\mathcal{F}_{i}\cap\mathcal{M}|]\geq(s-1)+\frac{1}{s}-\frac{1}{sn}
. Thus

(s-1)+\frac{1}{s}-\frac{1}{sn}\leq\mathbb{E}[|\bigcup_{i=s-M+1}^{s}\mathcal{F}_{i}\cap\mathcal{M}|]\leq s-1+n\mathbb{P}[|\bigcup_{i=s-M+1}^{s}\mathcal{F}_{i}\cap\mathcal{M}|>s-1],

and thus
\mathbb{P}[|\bigcup_{i=s-M+1}^{s}\mathcal{F}_{i}\cap\mathcal{M}|>s-1]\geq\frac{1}{sn}-\frac{1}{sn^{2}}
, what contradicts the assumption.

Thus we can assume that

|\mathcal{F}_{i}\cap\mathcal{H}_{1}(t)|\geq\Big(1-\frac{1}{2ns}\Big)n^{k-1}\text{ for any }i\in[s-M+1,s]\text{ and any }t\in[s-1].

(6)

We remark that, in what follows, we only use (
6
) in order to complete the proof in this case. Condition (
6
) implies that

\left|\bigcap_{i=s-M+1}^{s}\mathcal{F}_{i}\cap\mathcal{H}_{1}(t)\right|\geq\left(1-\frac{M}{2ns}\right)n^{k-1}

(7)

for any
t\in[s-1]
.

Also, we can assume that small families almost entirely lie in
\cup_{t\in[s-1]}\mathcal{H}_{1}(t)
. That is, we have the following

Claim 16
.

If
|\mathcal{F}_{j}\backslash\cup_{t\in[s-1]}\mathcal{H}_{1}(t)|>4\sqrt{s\log s}\,n^{k-1}
for some
j\in[s-M]
, then there exists a cross-matching.

Proof.

Put
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{j}:=\mathcal{F}_{j}\backslash\cup_{t\in[s-1]}\mathcal{H}_{1}(t)
. By the concentration result (Theorem
13
), for a uniformly random matching
\mathcal{M}
it holds that
\mathbb{P}[|\mathcal{M}\cap\mathcal{F}_{i}|\leq i]\leq 2s^{-C^{2}/4}
for
i\in[s-M]
and sufficiently large
s
. Also, assuming that
|\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{j}|>4\sqrt{s\log s}\,n^{k-1}
, the concentration result implies that
\mathbb{P}[|\mathcal{M}\cap\mathcal{F}_{j}^{\prime}|=0]\leq 2s^{-4}
. For each
t\in[s-1]
for a (random) set
X_{t}(\mathcal{M}):=\mathcal{H}_{1}(t)\cap\mathcal{M}
the inequality (
7
) implies that
\mathbb{P}[X_{t}(\mathcal{M})\notin\bigcap_{i=s-M+1}^{s}\mathcal{F}_{i}]\leq\frac{M}{2ns}
. Then for
C\geq 4
and
s>s_{0}(C)
by the union bound we conclude that there exists a matching
\mathcal{M}_{0}
such that the following holds simultaneously:

•

|\mathcal{M}_{0}\cap\mathcal{F}_{i}|\geq i+1
for all
i\in[s-M]
,

•

\mathcal{M}\cap\mathcal{F}_{j}^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
, and

•

X_{t}(\mathcal{M}_{0})\in\bigcap_{i=s-M+1}^{s}\mathcal{F}_{i}
for all
t\in[s-1]
.

Fix such an
\mathcal{M}_{0}
. Then we can choose a cross-matching in
\mathcal{F}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{F}_{s}
as follows: choose an element
F_{j}
in
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{j}
, then sequentially for
i\in[s-M]\backslash\{j\}
choose some
F_{i}
in
\mathcal{F}_{i}
. Since
F_{j}\not\in\cup_{j\in[s-1]}\mathcal{H}_{1}(t)
at least
s-1-(s-M-1)=M
sets
X_{t}(\mathcal{M}_{0}),

t\in[s-1]
, are not yet chosen. Assign them arbitrarily to the "large" families
\mathcal{F}_{s-M+1},\ldots,\mathcal{F}_{s}
.
∎

Using the last claim, we can w.l.o.g. assume that for each
i\in[s-M]

\left|\mathcal{F}_{i}\setminus\bigcup_{t\in[s-1]}\mathcal{H}_{1}(t)\right|\leq 4\sqrt{s\log s}\,n^{k-1}.

(8)

Since
|\mathcal{F}_{s}|>(s-1)n^{k-1}
,
\mathcal{F}_{s}
contains a set
F_{s}=(x_{1},...,x_{k})\not\in\cup_{j\in[s-1]}\mathcal{H}_{1}(t)
. Let
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}
be the collection of all perfect matchings that contains
F_{s}
, and let
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
be a uniformly random element of
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}
. Then
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
is a uniformly random matching on the cube

\mathcal{C}^{\prime}:=[n]\backslash\{x_{1}\}\times...\times[n]\backslash\{x_{k}\}.

Denote
\mathcal{F}_{i}^{\prime}=\mathcal{F}_{i}\cap\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
. For
1\leq i\leq s-M
we have

|\mathcal{F}_{i}^{\prime}|\geq|\mathcal{F}_{i}|-\left|\mathcal{F}_{i}\setminus\bigcup_{t\in[s-1]}\mathcal{H}_{1}(t)\right|-\left|\bigcup_{t\in[s-1]}\mathcal{H}_{1}(t)\cap\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\right|.

We bound the second term using (
8
) and get

|\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{i}|\geq(i+C\sqrt{s\log s})n^{k-1}-4\sqrt{s\log s}\,n^{k-1}-k(s-1)n^{k-2}\geq\left(i+\frac{C}{2}\sqrt{s\log s}\right)n^{k-1}.

In the second inequality we use
k\leq\frac{n(\frac{C}{2}-4)}{\sqrt{s/\log s}}
, which is implied by
k\leq\frac{Cn}{3\sqrt{s/\log s}}
for
C\geq 20
.
For each hyperplane
\mathcal{H}_{1}(t)
,
t\in[s-1]
we have
|\mathcal{F}_{i}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{H}_{1}(t)|\geq(1-\frac{M}{2ns})n^{k-1}-(n^{k-1}-(n-1)^{k-1})\geq(n-1)^{k-1}-\frac{M}{2ns}n^{k-1}\geq(n-1)^{k-1}(1-\frac{2M}{3ns})
, where the last inequality follows from the inequality
n^{k-1}\leq\frac{4}{3}(n-1)^{k-1}
, which is easy to verify for
n>10k
, say. Thus the element of
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{H}_{1}(t)
avoids
\bigcap_{i=s-M+1}^{s}\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{i}
with probability at most
\frac{2M}{3ns}
. Thus, by the union bound, the probability that
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
intersects some
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{i}
with
i\in[s-M]
in less than
i
elements or
\bigcap_{i=s-M+1}^{s}\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{i}
in less than
s-1
element is at most
s^{-\frac{C^{2}}{20}+1}+\frac{2Ms}{3ns}<s^{-\frac{C^{2}}{20}+1}+\frac{2M}{3n}<1
for sufficiently large
C
. Consequently, there is a cross-matching
F_{1}\in\mathcal{F}_{1},...,F_{s-1}\in\mathcal{F}_{s-1}
such that
F_{i}\cap F_{s}=\emptyset
for each
i\in[s-1]
. The sets
F_{1},...,F_{s-1},F_{s}
form a full cross-matching, a contradiction.

Case 2:

n\geq s^{5}
.

We will use the same spread approximation as in Section
3
and the notation introduced there. Take the same parameter
r
as in Section
3
,
r=2^{5}s\log_{2}(sk)
. Note that the inequality on
k
from the statement of the theorem and the assumption
n\geq s^{5}
imply that for sufficiently large
s
we have
r^{3}<\frac{n}{2s}
. We showed in Section
3
that if our families
\mathcal{F}_{1},...,\mathcal{F}_{s}
are cross-dependent, then there exist families
\mathcal{S}_{1},...,\mathcal{S}_{s}\subset 2^{[k]\times[n]}
, such that:

1.

\mathcal{F}_{i}\subset\bigcup_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{i}}\mathcal{A}[S]\cup\mathcal{F}_{i}^{\prime}
;

2.

|\mathcal{F}_{i}^{\prime}|\leq r^{3}n^{k-3}\leq\frac{n^{k-2}}{2s}
;

3.

\mathcal{S}_{i}
’s are cross-dependent;

4.

for each
S\in\mathcal{S}_{i}
, we have
|S|\leq 2
;

5.

for
\mathcal{S}_{i}^{(2)}:=\{S\in\mathcal{S}_{i}\,:\,|S|=2\}
we have
|\mathcal{S}_{i}^{(2)}|\leq 4(s-1)^{2}
.

As in Section
3
, for
j=1,2
we denote
\mathcal{S}_{i}^{(j)}:=\{S\in\mathcal{S}_{i}\,:\,|S|=j\}
.

We have
|\bigcup_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{i}^{(2)}}\mathcal{A}[S]|+|\mathcal{F}_{i}^{\prime}|\leq 4s^{2}n^{k-2}+\frac{n^{k-2}}{2s}<n^{k-1}

for sufficiently large
s
. So, in case
\emptyset\notin\mathcal{S}_{i}
, to satisfy the first condition in the list above, we should have
|\mathcal{S}_{i}^{(1)}|\geq i
for
i\in[s-M]
and
|\mathcal{S}_{i}^{(1)}|\geq s-1
for
i\in[s-M+1,s]
. Then, in case that
\emptyset\in\mathcal{S}_{i}
for at least one
i
, the families
\mathcal{S}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{S}_{s}
cannot be cross-dependent: we can sequentially choose distinct one-element sets from
\mathcal{S}_{i}
’s for all
i
’s such that
\emptyset\notin\mathcal{S}_{i}
, and empty sets for other
i
’s, what will clearly give us a cross-matching. So
\emptyset\notin\mathcal{S}_{i}
for all
i\in[s]
.

Suppose that
|\bigcup_{i=1}^{s}\mathcal{S}_{i}^{(1)}|\geq s
. Then Hall’s lemma easily implies that we can choose distinct one-element sets from
\mathcal{S}_{i}
’s forming a cross-matching. Consequently, there exist
s-1
elements
(j_{1},a_{1}),...,(j_{s-1},a_{s-1})\in[k]\times[n]
such that
\mathcal{S}_{i}^{(1)}=\{\{(j_{1},a_{1})\},...,\{(j_{s-1},a_{s-1})\}\}
for all
i\in[s-M+1,s]
and
\mathcal{S}_{i}^{(1)}\subset\{\{(j_{1},a_{1})\},...,\{(j_{s-1},a_{s-1})\}\}
for all
i\in[s-M]
. Then we w.l.o.g. assume that no set from
\mathcal{S}_{i}^{(2)}
for
i\in[s-M+1,s]
contains
(j_{t},a_{t})
,
t\in[s-1]
. Indeed, this does not affect the families
\mathcal{F}_{i}[\mathcal{S}_{i}]
,
i\in[s-M+1,s]
. Suppose that still for some
\ell\in[s-M+1,s]
we have
\mathcal{S}_{\ell}^{(2)}\neq\emptyset
. Then we can sequentially choose distinct one-element sets from all
\mathcal{S}_{i}
except
i=\ell
, and then add an element from
\mathcal{S}_{\ell}^{(2)}
. Since all one-element sets are of the form
\{(j_{t},a_{t})\}
,
t\in[s-1],
and the element from
\mathcal{S}_{\ell}^{(2)}
contains none of these elements, we obtain a cross matching. So for all
i\in[s-M+1,s]
we have
\mathcal{S}_{i}^{(2)}=\emptyset
. Now suppose that for some
t,u\in[s-1]
we have
j_{t}\neq j_{u}
. Then for
i\in[s-M+1,s]

|\mathcal{F}_{i}|\leq|\mathcal{A}[\mathcal{S}^{(1)}_{i}]|+|\mathcal{F}_{i}^{\prime}|\leq(s-1)n^{k-1}-n^{k-2}+\frac{n^{k-2}}{2s}<(s-1)n^{k-1}
for sufficiently large
s
. We conclude that all
j_{t}
are equal, and we may assume that
j_{t}=1
and
a_{t}=t
.

The argument above implies that for all
i\in[s-M+1,s]
we have
\mathcal{F}_{i}\subset[s-1]\times[n]^{k-1}\cup\mathcal{F}_{i}^{\prime}
. Thus,
|\mathcal{F}_{i}\backslash[s-1]\times[n]^{k-1}|\leq|\mathcal{F}_{i}^{\prime}|\leq\frac{n^{k-2}}{2s}
. Since
|\mathcal{F}_{i}|>(s-1)n^{k-1}
, we have
|[s-1]\times[n]^{k-1}\backslash\mathcal{F}_{i}|<|\mathcal{F}_{i}\backslash[s-1]\times[n]^{k-1}|
. In particular, for any
t\in[s-1]
we have
|\mathcal{H}_{1}(t)\backslash\mathcal{F}_{i}|<\frac{n^{k-2}}{2s}
, and so
|\mathcal{H}_{1}(t)\cap\mathcal{F}_{i}|\geq\big(1-\frac{1}{2ns}\big)n^{k-1}
. This gives us condition (
6
), and we have treated this situation in the previous case.

Case 3:

\mathbb{P}[|\bigcup_{i=s-M+1}^{s}\mathcal{F}_{i}\cap\mathcal{M}|>s-1]\geq\frac{1}{4sn^{2}}
and
n\leq s^{5}
.

In this case
\mathbb{P}[(r_{\mathcal{M}}<M)]\geq\frac{1}{4sn^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{4s^{11}}
, and inequality (
5
) together with Claim

imply that

0<-(C/2-3.7)\sqrt{s\log s}\,\,\frac{1}{4s^{11}}+4s^{-\frac{C^{2}}{20}+1}(xM+(C/2-3.7)\sqrt{s\log s}).

For

, this gives (for some constant
A>0
):

s^{-10,5}\sqrt{\log s}<As^{-\frac{C^{2}}{20}+2}\log s=As^{-18}\log s,

which is clearly violated for sufficiently large

. This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Now we shall treat the easy case of large
n
.

Theorem 17
.

The sequence
\{\min(s-1,i)n^{k-1}\}_{i}
is satisfying for
n\geq k^{2}s^{2}
.

Proof.

The argument is a version of the original Erdős’s argument
[
6
]
to prove Erdős Matching Conjecture for
n>n_{0}(s,k)
, as it is given in
[
5
]
. It is based on the following claim.

Claim 18
.

If families
\mathcal{F}_{1},...,\mathcal{F}_{s}
are cross-dependent and for some
i\in[s]
,
j\in[k]
and
a\in[n]
we have
|\mathcal{H}_{j}(a)\cap\mathcal{F}_{i}|>(s-1)(k-1)n^{k-2}
, then the families
\mathcal{F}_{1},...,\mathcal{F}_{i}\cup\mathcal{H}_{j}(a),...,\mathcal{F}_{s}
are also cross-dependent.

Proof.

Arguing indirectly, assume that
\mathcal{F}_{1},...,\mathcal{F}_{i}\cup\mathcal{H}_{j}(a),...,\mathcal{F}_{s}
contain a cross-matching
F_{1}\in\mathcal{F}_{1},..,F_{s}\in\mathcal{F}_{s}
. If
F_{i}\notin\mathcal{H}_{j}(a)\backslash\mathcal{F}_{i}
, then it is a cross-matching in the initial families, a contradiction. Thus
F_{i}\in\mathcal{H}_{j}(a)\backslash\mathcal{F}_{i}
and so
F_{\ell}\notin\mathcal{H}_{j}(a)
for all
\ell\neq i
. We have
|\mathcal{H}_{j}(a)\cap\{X:X\cap F_{\ell}\neq\emptyset\}|\leq(k-1)n^{k-2}
for all
\ell\neq i
. Consequently,
|\mathcal{H}_{j}(a)\cap\bigcup_{\ell\in[k]\setminus\{i\}}\{X:X\cap F_{\ell}\neq\emptyset\}|\leq(s-1)(k-1)n^{k-2}<|\mathcal{H}_{j}(a)\cap\mathcal{F}_{i}|
, and hence there is some
F_{i}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}_{j}(a)\cap\mathcal{F}_{i}
that is disjoint from all
F_{\ell}
,
\ell\neq i
, and thus
F_{1},...,F_{i}^{\prime},...,F_{s}
is a cross-matching in
\mathcal{F}_{1},...,\mathcal{F}_{s}
, a contradiction.
∎

By the claim, we can w.l.o.g. assume that
\mathcal{F}_{i}=\hat{\mathcal{F}_{i}}\sqcup\mathcal{F}_{i}^{\prime}
, where
\hat{\mathcal{F}_{i}}
is a union of
t_{i}
hyperplanes and
\mathcal{F}_{i}^{\prime}
satisfies the following for all
j\in[k]
and
a\in[n]
:

|\mathcal{H}_{j}(a)\cap\mathcal{F}_{i}^{\prime}|\leq(s-1)(k-1)n^{k-2}.

For each
i<s
, we either have
t_{i}\geq i
or

|\mathcal{F}_{i}^{\prime}|\geq|\mathcal{F}_{i}|-t_{i}n^{k-1}\geq n^{k-1}\geq s^{2}k^{2}n^{k-2}.

Similarly, for
i=s
we either have
t_{s}\geq s-1
or
|\mathcal{F}_{s}^{\prime}|\geq n^{k-1}\geq s^{2}k^{2}n^{k-2}
.

Let
i_{1}<...<i_{m}<s
be the indices such that
t_{i_{j}}\geq i_{j}
,
j\in[m]
. For each
j\in[m]
we can choose a hyperplane
\mathcal{H}_{i_{j}}\subset\mathcal{F}_{i_{j}}
and, moreover, all these hyperplanes are different. We have
|\bigcup_{j\in[m]}\mathcal{H}_{i_{j}}|\leq(s-1)n^{k-1}
, and since
|\mathcal{F}_{s}|>(s-1)n^{k-1}
, the family
\mathcal{F}_{s}
contains a set
F_{s}
that is not contained in any of
\{\mathcal{H}_{i_{j}}\}_{j}
. Let us show that we can sequentially choose
F_{s-1},F_{s-2},...,F_{1}
such that, first,
F_{i}\cap F_{j}=\emptyset
for all
j>i
and, second,
F_{i}\notin\mathcal{H}_{i_{j}}
for all
j
such that
i_{j}\neq i
. Indeed, assume first that
i\notin\{i_{1},...,i_{m}\}
. Then
|\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{i}|\geq s^{2}k^{2}n^{k-2}
. Each hyperplane contains at most
(s-1)(k-1)n^{k-2}
elements of
\mathcal{F}_{i}^{\prime}
and thus any set intersects at most
k(s-1)(k-1)n^{k-2}
elements of it. Thus,
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{i}
contains at most
(s-1)^{2}(k-1)n^{k-2}+(s-1)^{2}k(k-1)n^{k-2}\leq s^{2}k^{2}n^{k-2}
elements that do not satisfy the desired conditions. Consequently, there is at least one that suits. If
i=i_{j}
for some
j\in[m]
, then each
\mathcal{H}_{i_{\ell}}
with
\ell\neq j
intersects
\mathcal{H}_{i_{j}}
in at most
n^{k-2}
sets. By construction, the sets
F_{i+1},...,F_{s}
do not lie in
\mathcal{H}_{i_{j}}
, and thus it contains at most
ksn^{k-2}
elements that intersect one of
F_{i+1},...,F_{s}
. Since
|\mathcal{H}_{i_{j}}|=n^{k-1}>sn^{k-2}+ksn^{k-2}
, there is a set
F_{i_{j}}\in\mathcal{H}_{i_{j}}\subset\mathcal{F}_{i_{j}}
that satisfies both conditions. Clearly,
F_{1},...,F_{s}
is a cross-matching.
∎

6
Nullstellensatz: proof of Theorem
3

In this section, we shall use Alon’s Combinatorial Nulstellensatz to prove Theorem
3
. Recall the statement of the Combinatorial Nulstellensatz:

Theorem 19

(Alon
[
2
]
)
.

Let
f(x_{1},...,x_{n})
be a polynomial over a field
\mathbb{F}
of degree
k
. Suppose that the coefficient of a monomial
x_{1}^{k_{1}}x_{2}^{k_{2}}...x_{n}^{k_{n}}
in
f
is nonzero and
k_{1}+...+k_{n}=k
. Then for any
A_{1},...,A_{n}\subset\mathbb{F}
, such that
|A_{i}|>k_{i}
, there exist
a_{1}\in A_{1}
, …,
a_{n}\in A_{n}
such that
f(a_{1},...,a_{n})\neq 0
.

Proof of Theorem
3
.

Let
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}(\alpha)
be an extension of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, where
\alpha
is a root of
x^{2}=a
with
a
being not a quadratic residue.
Note that the map
\varphi:[p]^{2}\to\mathbb{F}
,
\varphi:(i,j)\mapsto i+\alpha j
is a bijection.

Claim 20
.

(\varphi((i_{1},j_{1}))-\varphi((i_{2},j_{2})))^{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
iff
i_{1}=i_{2}
or
j_{1}=j_{2}
.

Proof.

(\varphi((i_{1},j_{1}))-\varphi((i_{2},j_{2})))^{2}=((i_{1}-i_{2})+\alpha(j_{1}-j_{2}))^{2}=((i_{1}-i_{2})^{2}+a(j_{1}-j_{2})^{2})+2\alpha(i_{1}-i_{2})(j_{1}-j_{2})
. This lies in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
if and only if
(i_{1}-i_{2})(j_{1}-j_{2})=0
.
∎

Consider a polynomial over
\mathbb{F}
:

P(x_{1},...,x_{s})=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq s}\prod_{q\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}}((x_{j}-x_{i})^{2}-q).

(9)

The degree of
P
is
ps(s-1)
and all monomials of maximal degree are included in the term
\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq s}(x_{j}-x_{i})^{2p}=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq s}(x_{j}^{p}-x_{i}^{p})^{2}
.

Take any bipartite graphs
\mathcal{F}_{1},...,\mathcal{F}_{s}\subset[p]^{2}
, such that
|\mathcal{F}_{i}|>pf_{i}
. We shall show that they contain a cross-matching. Note that
|\varphi(\mathcal{F}_{i})|=|\mathcal{F}_{i}|>pf_{i}
.

The coefficient of
x_{1}^{f_{1}},...,x_{s}^{f_{s}}
in
\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq s}(x_{j}-x_{i})^{2}
is equal to the coefficient of
x_{1}^{pf_{1}},...,x_{s}^{pf_{s}}
in
\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq s}(x_{j}^{p}-x_{i}^{p})^{2}
, and thus the latter is non-zero in
\mathbb{F}
. By Theorem
19
, there exist
X_{1}\in\mathcal{F}_{1}
, …,
X_{s}\in\mathcal{F}_{s}
, such that
P(\varphi(X_{1}),...,\varphi(X_{s}))\neq 0
. Looking at (
9
), we see that this means that for every pair of indexes
i<j
we have
(\varphi(X_{j})-\varphi(X_{i}))^{2}\notin\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. By Claim
20
, it implies that
X_{i}
and
X_{j}
do not intersect for
i\neq j
. Hence,
X_{1},...,X_{s}
is the desired cross-matching.
∎

7
Discussion

In this paper, we focused on two types of sequences
f_{1},\ldots,f_{s}
. The first type is an arithmetic progression
f_{i}=m+(i-1)d
, and the second type is a ‘truncated’ arithmetic progression
f_{i}=\min\{m+(i-1)d,(s-1)n^{k-1}\}.
For both types of sequences, we had
d=n^{k-1},
which appears to be the right value to take. The optimal value of
m=m(n,s,k)
would be very interesting to determine.

Problem 1
.

Determine the smallest
m=m(n,s,k)
such that the sequence
f_{i}=m+(i-1)n^{k-1}
is satisfying.

Problem 2
.

Determine the smallest
m=m(n,s,k)
such that the sequence
f_{i}=\min\{m+(i-1)n^{k-1},(s-1)n^{k-1}\}
is satisfying.

On the other hand, the sequences from examples in Section
2
all have the form
f_{1}=\ldots=f_{\ell}=f,

f_{\ell+1}=\ldots=f_{s}=g.
Thus, the following problem is natural.

Problem 3
.

Determine the minimal values
f=f(n,k,s,\ell)
and
g=f(n,k,s,\ell)
such that the sequence
f_{1}=\ldots=f_{\ell}=f,

f_{\ell+1}=\ldots=f_{s}=g
is satisfying.

The case
g=(s-1)n^{k-1}
is of particular interest.

Problem 4
.

Determine the minimal value
f=f(n,k,s,\ell)
such that the sequence
f_{1}=\ldots=f_{\ell}=f,

f_{\ell+1}=\ldots=f_{s}=(s-1)n^{k-1}
is satisfying.

It is not unlikely that the examples from Section
2
are best possible.

Satisfying sequences for
k=1
are easy to describe: it is necessary and sufficient that
f_{i}\geq i-1
. (We of course suppose that
f_{1}\leq f_{2}\leq\ldots\leq f_{s}
.) The problem for the graph case
k=2
and arbitrary sequences seems to be non-trivial and very interesting.

Problem 5
.

Describe the set of all satisfying sequences
f_{1},\ldots,f_{s}
for
k=2
and arbitrary
s
.

If it is resolved, it should give good insight on satisfying sequences for arbitrary
k
and sufficiently large
n
.

Finally, it would be very interesting (although much harder) to treat analogous questions for families
\mathcal{F}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{F}_{s}\subset{[n]\choose k}
.

Log-Mu Fading Process: Second-Order Statistics for Diversity-Combining Techniques Thanks: This work was partially supported by the São Paulo Research Foundation, FAPESP, (2024/20097-3).
Godfred Kumi Tenkorang, Michel Daoud Yacoub

Abstract
This paper derives second-order statistics for diversity-combining techniques over Log-mu fading channels. Closed-form expressions for the level crossing rate and average fading duration are derived for pure selection combining, while exact multidimensional integral expressions are obtained for equal gain combining and maximal ratio combining. The analysis considers M M unbalanced, independent, and non-identically distributed (i.n.i.d.) Log-mu fading channels. Monte Carlo simulations are conducted to validate the theoretical results, demonstrating excellent agreement and confirming the accuracy of the proposed expressions.



I
Introduction

In wireless communication systems, the performance of received signals is significantly impacted by fading, which arises primarily from two key phenomena, namely shadowing and multipath propagation. Shadowing, often referred to as long-term fading, is caused by obstacles blocking the line-of-sight (LoS) path, leading to slow variations in signal strength over large distances. In contrast, multipath propagation, also known as short-term fading, arises from the diffraction, reflection, and scattering of transmitted signals, causing rapid fluctuations in signal amplitude and phase. A wide range of statistical models have been proposed and extensively studied in the literature to characterize these fading effects.

Among these,
\alpha
-
\mu
arose to explicitly incorporate the concept of the non-linear effects of the propagation medium, rendering it more versatile to characterize short-term fading. Due to its non-linear structure and mathematical tractability, the authors in
[
1
]
investigated its suitability for composite fading scenarios, which, implicitly, is a non-linear phenomenon. Their study compared the statistical fitting accuracy of the
\alpha
–
\mu
model with well-established composite fading models, Nakagami–Lognormal, Generalized-
K
, and Fisher–Snedecor, based on field measurements at 1.8 GHz. Their results demonstrated that the
\alpha
–
\mu
model either outperformed these benchmarks or achieved comparable performance. Motivated by these findings and the growing need for models that capture non-linear propagation effects with simpler mathematical formulations, the authors in
[
6
]
recently introduced the Log–
\mu
fading model, which is revisited in section
II
of this paper.

As is widely known, the same phenomenon that provokes fading, i.e., multipath, can also be used to counteract its deleterious effects. Hence, diversity combining is a crucial technique in wireless communication systems to improve performance. Evaluating the performance of these techniques relies on key first- and second-order statistics, for instance, outage probability, level crossing rate, average fading duration, the latter two providing insight into the temporal dynamics of fading processes. These metrics have been extensively studied for classical fading models
[
9
,
5
,
4
,
3
]
, however, their characterization under the recently proposed Log–
\mu
fading channel has not yet been addressed.

In this paper, we derive the expressions of the level crossing rate and average fading duration for the three conventional diversity-combining schemes, namely pure selection combining, equal gain combining, and maximal ratio combining techniques, in the presence of
M
unbalanced, independent, and non-identically distributed (i.n.i.d.) Log–
\mu
fading channels. For the pure selection combining scheme, closed-form expressions are obtained, whereas the equal gain combining and maximal ratio combining statistics are expressed in the form of multidimensional integrals. The analytical derivations are validated through Monte Carlo simulations, confirming the accuracy of the proposed results.

II
The Log-
\mu
Fading Model Revisited

In this section, we revisit the Log-
\mu
fading model, originally introduced in
[
6
]
. This model considers that the received signal at the receptor arises from the superposition of
\mu
clusters of multipath components propagating through a non-homogeneous environment. The envelope of the resulting signal is described as a non-linear transformation of the modulus of the sum of these multipath waves, where the non-linearity of the propagation medium is captured by a logarithmic function. Accordingly, the normalized envelope,
P_{i}=R_{i}/\hat{r}_{i}
, at the
i
-th branch,
i=1,2,...,M
, can be expressed as

g(P_{i})=\log\left(\left(P_{i}+(1-s_{i})^{1/\alpha_{i}}\right)^{\alpha_{i}}+s_{i}\right)=\sum_{l=1}^{\mu_{i}}({X}^{2}_{il}+{Y}^{2}_{il}),

(1)

where
\alpha_{i}>0
and
s_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
are arbitrary parameters,
\hat{r}_{i}
is a scale parameter, and
{X}_{il}
and
{Y}_{il}
are mutually independent Gaussian processes with zero mean and equal variance. The probability density function of the normalized envelope,
f_{P_{i}}(\rho_{i})
, is given as
[
6
]

\displaystyle f_{P_{i}}(\rho_{i})=\frac{\alpha_{i}{({\rho_{i}+(1-s_{i})^{1/\alpha_{i}}})^{\alpha_{i}-1}}\mu_{i}^{\mu_{i}}}{\Gamma(\mu_{i})(({\rho_{i}+(1-s_{i})^{1/\alpha_{i}}})^{\alpha_{i}}+s_{i})^{\mu_{i}+1}}

(2)

\displaystyle\times\log(({\rho_{i}+(1-s_{i})^{1/\alpha_{i}}})^{\alpha_{i}}+s_{i})^{\mu_{i}-1},

where
\Gamma(u)=\int_{0}^{\infty}{x^{u-1}exp(-x)dx}
is the Gamma function. The parameter
\alpha_{i}
is unrestricted for
s_{i}\leq 1
; however, for
s_{i}>1
,
\alpha_{i}
must be restricted to odd integers to ensure real-valued expressions.

The corresponding cumulative distribution function of the normalized envelope,
F_{P_{i}}(\rho_{i})
is obtained in a closed-form as

F_{P_{i}}(\rho_{i})=1-\frac{\Gamma\left(\mu_{i},\mu_{i}\log\left(\left({\rho_{i}}+(1-s_{i})^{1/\alpha_{i}}\right)^{\alpha_{i}}+s_{i}\right)\right)}{\Gamma(\mu_{i})},

(3)

where
\Gamma(u,v)=\int_{v}^{\infty}x^{u-1}\exp(-x)dx
is the incomplete Gamma function.

A distinctive feature of the Log-
\mu
fading model is its ability to exhibit bimodal characteristics for certain parameter configurations, particularly when
\alpha>1
and
s>1
.

III
Level Crossing Rate and Average Fade Duration

The level crossing rate is defined as the average number of times a fading signal crosses a given signal level
\rho
within a certain period of time. The level crossing rate in the positive direction can be obtained by

N_{P}(\rho)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\dot{\rho}f_{P,\dot{P}}(\rho,\dot{\rho})d\dot{\rho},

(4)

where
\dot{P}
is the time derivative of
P
, and
f_{P,\dot{P}}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the joint probability density function of
P
and
\dot{P}
.

The average fading duration represents the average time the signal remains below the threshold
\rho
, and it can be expressed as

T_{P}(\rho)=\frac{F_{P}(\rho)}{N_{P}(\rho)},

(5)

where
F_{P}(\cdot)
is the cumulative distribution function.

III-A

No Diversity (
M=1
)

These second-order statistics can be derived using any suitable relation between the envelope of the Log-
\mu
model and the envelope of another well-characterized fading model. From (
1
), it can be observed that the non-linear transformation applied to the received signal relates the Log-
\mu
envelope to the Nakagami-
m
model through the expression

g(P_{i})={P}_{i,\,N}^{2},

(6)

where
{P_{i}}
and
{P}_{i,\,N}
represent the normalized envelopes of the Log-
\mu
and Nakagami-
m
fading models, respectively.

By differentiating both sides of (
6
) with respect to time, we obtain

\displaystyle\dot{{P_{i}}}=\frac{2\left(g({P_{i}})\right)^{0.5}\dot{{P}}_{i,\,N}}{g^{\prime}({P_{i}})},

(7)

where
g^{\prime}({P_{i}})=dg({P_{i}})/d{P_{i}}
. As presented in
[
7
]
, the time derivative
\dot{{P}}_{i,\,N}
of the Nakagami-
m
envelope follows a zero-mean Gaussian distribution with variance

{\sigma}_{\dot{P}_{i},\,N}^{2}=\left(\frac{\omega}{2}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{1}{\mu_{i}}\right),

(8)

where
\omega
is the maximum Doppler shift in radians per second. Applying the standard procedure for transformation of random variables to (
7
),
f_{\dot{P}_{i}|P_{i}}(\cdot|\cdot)
is also found to be Gaussian with zero mean and variance

\displaystyle{\sigma}_{\dot{P}_{i}}^{2}=\frac{g({P_{i}})\omega^{2}}{\left(g^{\prime}({P_{i}})\right)^{2}\mu_{i}}.

(9)

The joint probability density function of the envelope and its time derivative is then given as
f_{\dot{P_{i}},P_{i}}(\dot{\rho}_{i},\rho_{i})=f_{\dot{P}_{i}|P_{i}}(\dot{\rho}_{i}|\rho_{i})f_{P_{i}}(\rho_{i})
and by substituting this into (
4
), the level crossing rate is obtained in closed-form as

\displaystyle N_{P_{i}}(\rho)=\frac{{\sigma}_{\dot{P}_{i}}f_{P_{i}}({\rho})}{\sqrt{2\pi}}.

(10)

More explicitly, the level crossing rate for the Log-
\mu
fading envelope is expressed as

\displaystyle N_{P_{i}}(\rho)=\frac{\omega\mu_{i}^{\mu_{i}-0.5}\left(\log\left(\left({\rho}+(1-s_{i})^{1/\alpha_{i}}\right)^{\alpha_{i}}+s_{i}\right)\right)^{\mu_{i}-0.5}}{\sqrt{2\pi}{\Gamma(\mu_{i})\left(\left({\rho}+(1-s_{i})^{1/\alpha_{i}}\right)^{\alpha_{i}}+s_{i}\right)^{\mu_{i}}}}.

(11)

The average fading duration can be derived in a closed-form by substituting (
3
) and (
11
) into (
5
), yielding

\displaystyle T_{P_{i}}(\rho)={\sqrt{2\pi}{\left(\left({\rho}+(1-s_{i})^{1/\alpha_{i}}\right)^{\alpha_{i}}+s_{i}\right)^{\mu_{i}}}}

(12)

\displaystyle\times\frac{\left(\Gamma(\mu_{i})-\Gamma\left(\mu_{i},\mu_{i}\log\left(\left({\rho}+(1-s_{i})^{1/\alpha_{i}}\right)^{\alpha_{i}}+s_{i}\right)\right)\right)}{{\omega\mu_{i}^{\mu_{i}-0.5}\left(\log\left(\left({\rho}+(1-s_{i})^{1/\alpha_{i}}\right)^{\alpha_{i}}+s_{i}\right)\right)^{\mu_{i}-0.5}}}.

III-B

Pure Selection Combining

In pure selection combining, the receiver continuously monitors all branches and selects the one with the highest instantaneous signal envelope. Consequently, the output envelope
P
at the combiner is defined as

P=\max\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1,\dots,M\end{subarray}}\{P_{i}\}.

(13)

In
[
8
]
, a general formulation for the level crossing rate of pure selection combining system over
M
independent fading channels was derived. Based on this formulation, the level crossing rate for pure selection combining over Log-
\mu
fading channels is obtained as

N_{P}(\rho)=\sum_{i=1}^{M}N_{P_{i}}(\rho)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{M}{F_{P_{j}}(\rho)},

(14)

where
F_{P_{j}}
and
N_{P_{i}}
are given by (
3
) and (
11
), respectively.

A general formulation for the average fading duration of pure selection combining system over
M
independent fading channels has also been derived in
[
3
]
. It is expressed as

T^{-1}_{P}(\rho)=\sum_{i=1}^{M}{T^{-1}_{P_{i}}(\rho)},

(15)

where
{T_{P_{i}}}
is given in our case by (
12
).

III-C

Equal Gain Combining

In equal gain combining, the signals received with envelopes
\rho_{i}
are co-phased and coherently added. Taking into account the resultant noise power at the combiner output, the overall output envelope
P
and its time derivative
\dot{P}
are given as

P=\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}\sum_{i=1}^{M}P_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad\dot{P}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\dot{P_{i}}.

(16)

Following a similar procedure to that in
[
2
]
, where the distribution of the sum of
M
independent Rayleigh signals was derived, we obtain the distribution of the Log-
\mu
signals over the
M
-dimensional volume bounded by the hyperplane
\sqrt{M}\rho=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\rho_{i}
as

\displaystyle F_{P}(\rho)=\int_{0}^{\sqrt{M}\rho}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{M}\rho-\rho_{M}}\dots\int_{0}^{\sqrt{M}\rho-\sum_{i=3}^{M}{\rho_{i}}}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{M}\rho-\sum_{i=2}^{M}{\rho_{i}}}

(17)

\displaystyle\times\prod_{i=1}^{M}f_{P_{i}}(\rho_{i})d\rho_{1}d\rho_{2}...d\rho_{M-1}d\rho_{M},

where
f_{P_{i}}(\rho_{i})
is given by (
2
). Differentiating (
17
) with respect to
\rho
yields the envelope probability density function
f_{P}(\rho)
, and using Bayes’ rule, the joint distribution
f_{P,\dot{P}}(\rho,\dot{\rho})
is found as (
18
).
The joint density function
f(\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\dots,\rho_{M},\dot{\rho})
is expressed as

\displaystyle f_{P,\dot{P}}(\rho,\dot{\rho})=\sqrt{M}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{M}\rho}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{M}\rho-\rho_{M}}\dots\int_{0}^{\sqrt{M}\rho-\sum_{i=3}^{M}\rho_{i}}

(18)

\displaystyle\times f_{P_{1},P_{2},\cdots,P_{M},\dot{P}}\left(\left(\sqrt{M}\rho-\sum_{i=2}^{M}\rho_{i}\right),\rho_{2},\dots,\rho_{M},\dot{\rho}\right)\,d\rho_{2}\dots d\rho_{M-1}d\rho_{M}.

\displaystyle f_{P_{1},\dots,P_{M},\dot{P}}\left(\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{M},\dot{\rho}\right)=f_{\dot{P}|P_{1},\dots,P_{M}}\left(\dot{\rho}|\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{M}\right)

(19)

\displaystyle\times f_{P_{1},\dots,P_{M}}\left(\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{M}\right)

where
f\left(\dot{\rho}|\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{M}\right)
is a zero-mean Gaussian distribution with variance
{\sigma}_{\dot{P}}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{M}{{\sigma}_{\dot{P}_{i}}^{2}}/M
, and
f_{P_{1},\dots,P_{M}}\left(\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{M}\right)=\prod_{i=1}^{M}f_{P_{i}}(\rho_{i})
. By substituting (
19
) into (
18
) and subsequently incorporating the result into (
4
), we obtain the expression for the level crossing rate of the equal gain combining system, as presented in (
20
). The corresponding average fading duration is then obtained directly with (
20
), (
17
), and (
4
).

\displaystyle N_{P}(\rho)=\frac{\omega}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{M}\rho}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{M}\rho-\rho_{M}}\dots\int_{0}^{\sqrt{M}\rho-\sum_{i=3}^{M}\rho_{i}}\sqrt{\frac{g\left(\sqrt{M}\rho-\sum_{i=2}^{M}\rho_{i}\right)}{\left(g^{\prime}\left(\sqrt{M}\rho-\sum_{i=2}^{M}\rho_{i}\right)\right)^{2}\mu_{1}}+\sum_{i=2}^{M}\frac{g(\rho_{i})}{(g^{\prime}(\rho_{i}))^{2}\mu_{i}}}

(20)

\displaystyle\times f_{P_{1}}\left(\sqrt{M}\rho-\sum_{i=2}^{M}\rho_{i}\right)\prod_{i=2}^{M}f_{P_{i}}(\rho_{i})d\rho_{2}\dots d\rho_{M-1}d\rho_{M}.

III-D

Maximal Ratio Combining

In maximal ratio combining, the received signals are co-phased, each signal is amplified appropriately to achieve optimal combining. The resulting signals are then summed, such that the combiner output envelope
P
and its time derivative
\dot{P}
are given by

P={\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{M}P_{i}^{2}}}\quad\text{and}\quad\dot{P}=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\frac{P_{i}}{P}\dot{P_{i}}.

(21)

The maximal ratio combining analysis follows the same general procedure as that of equal gain combining. However, in the case of maximal ratio combining, the distribution is derived over a
M
-dimensional volume bounded by the hyperplane
\rho^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\rho_{i}^{2}
. The resulting variance of
\dot{P}
can be expressed as
{\sigma}_{\dot{P}}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{M}{{P_{i}^{2}}{\sigma}_{\dot{P}_{i}}^{2}}/P^{2}
. The cumulative distribution function is expressed as

\displaystyle F_{P}(\rho)=\int_{0}^{\rho}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{\rho^{2}-\rho_{M}^{2}}}\dots\int_{0}^{\sqrt{\rho^{2}-\sum_{i=3}^{M}{\rho_{i}^{2}}}}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{\rho^{2}-\sum_{i=2}^{M}{\rho_{i}^{2}}}}

(22)

\displaystyle\times\prod_{i=1}^{M}f_{P_{i}}(\rho_{i})d\rho_{1}d\rho_{2}...d\rho_{M-1}d\rho_{M}.

The joint probability density function
f_{P,\dot{P}}(\cdot,\cdot)
and the level crossing rate
N_{P}(\cdot)
are expressed by (
23
) and (
24
), respectively. The corresponding average fading duration is then obtained directly with (
24
), (
22
), and (
4
).

\displaystyle f_{P,\dot{P}}(\rho,\dot{\rho})=\int_{0}^{\rho}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{\rho^{2}-\rho_{M}^{2}}}\dots\int_{0}^{\sqrt{\rho^{2}-\sum_{i=3}^{M}{\rho_{i}^{2}}}}\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{\rho^{2}-\sum_{i=2}^{M}\rho_{i}^{2}}}

(23)

\displaystyle\times f_{P_{1},P_{2},\cdots,P_{M},\dot{P}}\left(\left({\sqrt{\rho^{2}-\sum_{i=2}^{M}\rho_{i}^{2}}}\right),\rho_{2},\dots,\rho_{M},\dot{\rho}\right)\,d\rho_{2}\dots d\rho_{M-1}d\rho_{M}.

\displaystyle N_{P}(\rho)=\frac{\omega}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{0}^{\rho}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{\rho^{2}-\rho_{M}^{2}}}\dots\int_{0}^{\sqrt{\rho^{2}-\sum_{i=3}^{M}{\rho_{i}^{2}}}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho^{2}-\sum_{i=2}^{M}\rho_{i}^{2}}}\sqrt{\frac{g\left({\sqrt{\rho^{2}-\sum_{i=2}^{M}\rho_{i}^{2}}}\right)\left({{\rho^{2}-\sum_{i=2}^{M}\rho_{i}^{2}}}\right)}{\left(g^{\prime}\left({\sqrt{\rho^{2}-\sum_{i=2}^{M}\rho_{i}^{2}}}\right)\right)^{2}\mu_{1}}+\sum_{i=2}^{M}\frac{g(\rho_{i})\rho_{i}^{2}}{(g^{\prime}(\rho_{i}))^{2}\mu_{i}}}

(24)

\displaystyle\times f_{P_{1}}\left({\sqrt{\rho^{2}-\sum_{i=2}^{M}\rho_{i}^{2}}}\right)\prod_{i=2}^{M}f_{P_{i}}(\rho_{i})d\rho_{2}\dots d\rho_{M-1}d\rho_{M}.

V
Conclusion

In this paper, we derived expressions for the level crossing rate and average fading duration of pure selection combining, equal gain combining, and maximal ratio combining techniques over
M
unbalanced, independent, and non-identically distributed (i.n.i.d.) Log-
\mu
fading channels. For the pure selection combining scheme, exact closed-form expressions for both metrics were obtained, while in the equal gain combining and maximal ratio combining cases, the solutions were expressed as multidimensional integrals. The analytical results were validated through Monte Carlo simulations, demonstrating excellent agreement and confirming the accuracy of the proposed expressions.

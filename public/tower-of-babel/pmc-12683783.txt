Reduction of low attenuation areas of emphysema after lung volume reduction surgery compared to bronchoscopic lung volume reduction with valves
Authors: Julia von Rotz, Jan Mengers, Thomas Gaisl, Carolin Steinack, Didier Schneiter, Isabelle Opitz, Claudio Caviezel
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundLung volume reduction surgery (LVRS) enables a tailored resection of emphysematous lung areas, whereas bronchoscopic lung volume reduction (BLVR) with valves excludes a whole lobe. We hypothesize greater reduction of areas with low attenuation on computed tomography (CT) scans by LVRS, assuming to spare still functioning tissue, compared with BLVR.MethodsLVRS patients were prospectively collected and a post-hoc analysis was performed to compare patients after BLVR with valves. Pre- and postoperative (3 months after LVRS, 1 months after BLVR) lung function, 6-minute walking distance (6-MWD) and changes in low attenuation areas (LAA, ≤- 950 hounsfield units) in CT was measured. The BLVR group needed proven target lobe atelectasis to be included. Primary endpoint was the radiologically measured LAA reduction.ResultsAmong the 27 patients who underwent LVRS between August 2019 and October 2020, complete data were available for 19 patients. In contrast, 18 out of 27 BLVR patients achieved complete lobar atelectasis. Both LVRS and BLVR significantly improved LAA (Δ41% and Δ28%), lung function (forced expiratory volume in 1 second FEV1) and 6-MWD (all p<0.05). However, LVRS showed no significant advantage over BLVR for LAA (p=0.068), FEV1 (p=0.132) or 6-MWD (p=0.077).ConclusionNeither LVRS nor BLVR seem to be superior in sparing or eliminating still functional tissue, as both methods showed no differences in reduction of LAA. This information might contribute to the decision at an interdisciplinary emphysema board, where all cases are preferably discussed.

Introduction

Lung volume reduction surgery (LVRS) represents an important treatment option for patients with advanced end-stage lung emphysema, and its efficacy in selected patients was proven more than 20 years ago [1–4]. Lung areas with the most emphysematous destruction on imaging studies and under direct visualization intraoperatively are resected, uni- or bilaterally, leading to a reduction of the lung volume (lung volume reduction, LVR). This LVR results in an improvement of lung function, reduction of dyspnea, increases exercise capacity and even decreases mortality in well selected subgroups [2, 4–6]. Nowadays, video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery (VATS) is the preferred surgical approach, even in cases with severe adhesions [2, 7, 8].

Another treatment option for patients with advanced lung emphysema is bronchoscopic lung volume reduction (BLVR) [9, 10]. Different techniques have been developed, mainly in use are endobronchial valves (EBV) working as one-way valves, which allows air to escape the lung without re-inflation, resulting in atelectasis of the targeted lobe, as long as there is no collateral ventilation between the target lobe and its neighbor(s) [11–13]. As a non-surgical, minimally invasive technique, it has become an important alternative to surgery.

Purpose of both techniques is reduction in lung volume which improves diaphragm function, the elastic recoil of the lung and chest wall dynamics, resulting in lung function improvement and higher quality of life [14, 15]. Studies about LVRS have shown good results especially in the early 2000 s [1–4, 16], meanwhile BLVR studies established over the last decade [13, 17, 18], implying a big heterogeneity of the studies, which makes it difficult to draw a fair comparison [19]. A first attempt to compare both methods in a randomized controlled trial (CELEB trial) could not give conclusive answers for favorizing one procedure over another [20], but showed no superiority of LVRS compared to BLVR. However, there is evidence that emphysema patients are best looked after in a multidisciplinary team, deciding the best individual therapy [21].

Since several decades computerized tomography (CT) has established as a sensitive technique in detection and assessment of emphysema [22]. The altered lung structure and density with characteristically abnormal, permanent enlargement of air spaces distal to the terminal bronchiole presents itself as areas of low attenuation (LAA) on the CT and reflects the pathologically destruction of the airspace [23]. The percentage of the lung area of low attenuation (lower than − 950 Hounsfield units) is an index of the pulmonary emphysema and is commonly used as a surrogate marker for the severity of the disease with its clinical relevance in pulmonary function measures [24, 25]. These areas are considered so called target areas.

As LVRS enables to remove only the worst affected emphysematous area of the lung, BLVR involves total lobar atelectasis whereby healthy lung areas with still good perfusion may be lost. We hypothesize that the tailored resection with LVRS enables to remove areas with low attenuation more precisely and spares healthy lung volume achieving more LAA reduction than with BLVR.

Materials and methods

Patient selection

Since August 2019, patients with heterogeneous emphysema who underwent LVRS or BLVR at our institution were prospectively analyzed.

General indications for lung volume reduction are: forced expiratory volume in 1 s (FEV1) 15–45% predicted, residual volume (RV) > 175% predicted and diffusion capacity (DLCO) > 15% predicted. All patients had a heterogeneous emphysema morphology. The most affected areas were recorded as heterogeneous in case of disease predominance in either upper lobe, upper lobe and/or apical segment of the lower lobe or lower lobe. Heterogeneous emphysema with obvious bilateral target areas on CT scan were in favor for bilateral LVRS. BLVR patients had the same heterogeneous morphology, but in addition to a StratX® measurement with at least 30% −950 houndsfield units (HU) in the target lobe and no collateral ventilation (confirmed by Chartis® measurement in all cases with fissure integrity > 80%).

The decision regarding which therapy the patient received was determined by a multidisciplinary board, where the distribution of the emphysema and the patients wish are primarily considered. General exclusion criteria for LVRS are non-localized bronchiectasis and severe pulmonary hypertension (right sided heart catheter with mPAP > 35mmHg). For BLVR, suspicious lung nodules, history of lung surgery including lobectomy, pleurodesis or LVRS on the target side, frequent infectious exacerbations (bronchitis phenotype) and/or bronchiectasis and the use of coumadin/antiplatelets, which cannot be stopped around the procedure, are contraindications. For both procedures maintenance immunosuppressive agents or prednisolone > 10 mg daily should be avoided.

Due to the Covid-19 pandemic, admissions for LVRS decreased and during the acute phases of the pandemic, those procedures were not performed at all. Data collection was terminated in October 2020.

For this study, Inclusion criteria for LVRS and BLVR were patients with informed consent and a completed follow up after 3 month including lung function test and a chest CT scan. In both groups the patients received LAA measurement by CT scan preoperatively and postoperative. While the CT scan in the BLVR group was performed 1 month after the intervention to assess, the LVRS group received it 3 months after surgery to interpret the final result after full recovery. These timelines were in accordance to best practice recommendations for BLVR [26] and our experience in LVRS. In literature, LVRS follow-up tests are usually made after 3, 9 and 12 months [19].

Operation

LVRS was performed unilaterally or bilaterally under general anesthesia. The decision depended primarily on the emphysema morphology and the predominance of disease distribution. Besides this, patients with borderline inclusion criteria such as DLCO < 20% or mild-to-moderate pulmonary hypertension were operated unilaterally, even in case of bilateral disease. Target areas based on computed tomography scans in combination with perfusion scintigraphy scans and densitometry were resected with standard staplers (Endo GIA Ultra Universal Medtronic) With BLVR one-way endobronchial valves, each specific device chosen as necessary depending on the emphysema characteristic, (Zephyr®, Pulmonx Corporation, Germany) were placed via bronchoscopy either under general anesthesia. Post-interventional a chest radiograph was performed to rule out pneumothorax.

To enable a comparison of the effect of both methods, they must have been successful BLVR patients therefore needed a proven target lobe atelectasis in the chest CT scan 1 month after intervention to be included in the analysis.

Follow-up and outcome measures

Preoperatively each patient received pulmonary function testing with a standard body plethysmograph and DLCO measurement and had to perform a 6-MWD test. Within 3 months after the intervention all tests were repeated.

Synapse 3D

The chest CT scans were analyzed preoperatively and postoperatively by use of Synapse 3D (Fujifilm, United Kingdom), a medical imaging processing software enabling measurement of LAA (low attenuation area) of each lobe classified by HU (hounsfield units). In both groups, LAA reduction was measured.

Objectives

The primary hypothesis was a greater LAA reduction on post-operative CT scans with LVRS than with BLVR. Additionally, we compared the postoperative lung function (especially FEV1%, RV%, DLCO%) and the increase in 6-MWD.

Statistical methods

Normal distribution was tested by Shapiro-Wilk-Test. Data is expressed as median and interquartile range (IQR). Parametric testing was performed by t-test. Pearson’s chi-squared test was used for testing categorical variables Correlations were analyzed with Pearson correlation coefficient. Univariate and multivariate risk factor analyses were performed with regression model (backward step-wise likelihood quotient) and adjusted for sex and baseline values. P-values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant. Statistical analysis was performed by means of SPSS 25 for Windows (SPSS Inc. Chicago, IL, USA).

Results

During the study time of the 27 patients receiving LVRS, 3 patients gave no consent for data extraction for study purposes, while 5 patients were lost for follow up. As our center receives patients from all over the german speaking parts of the country, some patients insist on follow-ups near their home town. This sometimes leads to incomplete reporting. The 19 with full data set were used for further analysis. Of the 19 procedures 5 were bilateral, and 14 were unilateral.

The BVLR group consisted of 18 patients with proven target lobe atelectasis out of 27 patients.

All patients were former smokers and smoking cessation was at least more than 3 months before the procedure. Discussion

The primary aim of this study was to compare the amount of LAA reduction of LVRS with BLVR.

Although LVRS might enable an accurate removal of nonfunctional lung areas, sparing intact lung tissue, this “tailored” resection does not seem to provide a greater LAA reduction compared to BLVR with valves.

Both procedures were associated with a gain in physical activity measurable in increasing their 6-MWD and lung function, but there was no significant difference between the groups.

BLVR with valves might be criticized due to its whole lobe exclusion, as many emphysema morphologies do not affect a single lobe equally. However, in this study with a small sample size, the lung volume reduction effect of BLVR with valves seems to equal its LVRS counterpart, not even being significant regarding reduction of LAA. As already shown, reduction of RV still seems to be the most important issue in treating chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) patients with severe emphysema and hyperventilation [13].

This study has several severe limitations. The cohort is rather small due to its highly pre-selected patient population. This might result in selection bias, as the two groups present different baseline characteristics.

Moreover, quality of life was not evaluated in both groups, which could have provided valuable complementary insights into patient-reported outcomes. We lack for information about the exact emphysema morphology in the BLVR group. If it presents as a total lobar emphysema, we would expect similar results with BLVR eliminating the whole lobe with atelectasis than with LVRS due to tailored emphysema resection. Nine patients out of 27 receiving BLVR showed no atelectasis post-interventionally. Their data was not considered for our analysis due to the intention of this study to compare clear and obvious BLVR responders with LVRS. Patients with partial atelectasis might still profit from BLVR, which might slightly change the results in favor of BLVR.

The LVRS group itself - although including only patients with heterogeneous emphysema - shows large heterogeneity including bilateral and unilateral operations. On the other hand, this seems to reflect the real-world scenario, where every emphysema patient eligible for LVR should (and was in our cases) be discussed at an interdisciplinary board [23]. As LVRS can be offered for both sides as a one stage procedure in case of bilateral disease, it should be allowed to compare it to a unilateral BLVR procedure. Bilateral BLVRS with valves is only sequentially staged possible – if at all [27]. This must be taken into account while discussing the Pros and Cons, underlining the importance of the patients wish in this rather palliative setting.

It is and will always be very difficult to compare both methods, even in randomized trials, as seen in the CELEB trial [20]. It did not show significant differences for its primary endpoint, the i-BODE-index, and only showed a significant better result for one of its secondary endpoints, the CAT-Score, favoring LVRS. Almost half of the patients did not receive a complete follow-up due to the differences during the COVID pandemic. Additionally, there were several intention-to-treat cross-over patients from the BLVR group who received LVRS.

Our study – besides not showing differences in LAA reduction – does not provide the same information value as the CELEB trial, but might contribute some additional insights to the LVR community.

Currently, another still running randomized trial, the SINCERE study (NCT04537182), might reveal other or at least additional results.

Our study stresses out once again the importance of a multidisciplinary emphysema board for individual treatment decisions and last but not least the patients opinion and expectation.

Results

Among the 27 patients who underwent LVRS between August 2019 and October 2020, complete data were available for 19 patients. In contrast, 18 out of 27 BLVR patients achieved complete lobar atelectasis. Both LVRS and BLVR significantly improved LAA (Δ41% and Δ28%), lung function (forced expiratory volume in 1 s FEV1) and 6-MWD (all p < 0.05). However, LVRS showed no significant advantage over BLVR for LAA (p = 0.068), FEV1 (p = 0.132) or 6-MWD (p = 0.077).

Conclusion

Neither LVRS nor BLVR seem to be superior in sparing or eliminating still functional tissue, as both methods showed no differences in reduction of LAA. This information might contribute to the decision at an interdisciplinary emphysema board, where all cases are preferably discussed.

Total transfemoral repair of thoracoabdominal aortic aneurysm and aortic dissection
Authors: Christian H Summa, Daniel Swink, Joseph AbouAyash, Shivprasad Nikam, Evan J Ryer, Melissa A Obmann, Boyoung Song, Christopher A DeMaioribus
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractWe present the case of a 66-year-old man with chronic type A aortic dissection and paravisceral aneurysmal degeneration who underwent successful endovascular repair using the thoracoabdominal multibranch endoprosthesis. Owing to a prior carotid-subclavian bypass, we elected a total transfemoral approach to avoid upper extremity access. A transcatheter electrosurgical septotomy was performed to establish controlled communication between the true and false lumens, facilitating endograft delivery. All visceral branches were stented via dual-sheath telescoping technique. A completion angiography showed no endoleak. This case demonstrates the feasibility of total transfemoral thoracoabdominal multibranch endoprosthesis deployment in complex anatomy while preserving branch vessel perfusion.Keywords: Thoracoabdominal aortic aneurysm, Aortic dissection, Multibranch endoprosthesis, Transfemoral

The management of complex thoracoabdominal and paravisceral aortic aneurysms, particularly those with chronic type A aortic dissections, presents itself as a challenge for endovascular repair owing to technical demands and their anatomical complexity. Given the recent US Food and Drug Administration approval of Thoracoabdominal Multibranch Endoprosthesis (TAMBE, W. L. Gore & Associates), this device provides minimally invasive treatment options for these patients with recent studies showing a high technical success rate.1,2 Compared with previous custom-manufactured branched devices that require extended preparation time, the TAMBE can be used in emergent clinical scenarios and complex anatomical presentations.

We present the case of a 66-year-old man who underwent endovascular repair of a chronic type A dissection with aneurysm degeneration of the paravisceral segment and right common iliac artery aneurysm with the use of a TAMBE. In this report, our patient had a prior left carotid-subclavian bypass. To reduce the risk of occluding his bypass and causing a stroke, we achieved access via a total transfemoral technique.3

Case report

Patient is a 66-year-old man with past history of atrial fibrillation, hypertension, cerebral vascular event, and a type A aortic dissection with root replacement. Initially, this was treated with placement of an ascending aortic graft, with a bypass to the innominate artery in 2021. Additionally, he had placement of a thoracic endograft from the level of the left common carotid artery proximally into the supraceliac aorta distally, combined with a left common carotid to left subclavian artery bypass in August of 2024. Additionally, the left subclavian artery was occluded with an Amplatzer vascular plug (Abbott Vascular). He presented in November of 2024 after a 3-month interval computed tomography angiography of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis demonstrating dissection that extended into the bypassed innominate artery and into the right subclavian, common carotid, and the left subclavian distal to his prior bypass. The dissection extended from the distal arch into the paravisceral and infrarenal aorta, involving aortic zones 5 through 10. Including the true and false lumens, the aorta, at this time, measured approximately 6.4 cm at the level of the celiac. Additionally, he was found to have a 3.4-cm right common iliac artery aneurysm and a small proximal right internal iliac artery aneurysm measuring 2.4 cm. Given the enlarging aneurysmal degeneration of the aorta involving the visceral and renal vessels distal to a previously placed thoracic endograft, the patient chose to undergo operative intervention (Fig 1). A written consent form was obtained from the patient to include his case details and imaging studies.

Three-dimensional reconstruction of thoracoabdominal aortic graft status post thoracic stent graft placement, demonstrating aneurysmal degeneration and chronic type A dissection.

Bilateral common femoral artery cutdown was performed. We accessed each femoral artery via direct puncture and upsized to a 9F sheath. Owing to this patient's previous aortic dissection, true and false lumen access was obtained and confirmed with intravascular ultrasound examination. Through-and-through wire access was established, and transcatheter electrosurgical septotomy was performed using a 0.014-inch Asahi Astato XS 20 wire (Asahi Intecc) insulated within a TrailBlazer catheter (Medtronic), connected to electrocautery, to divide the dissection septum in a controlled fashion, as previously described.4 Electrosurgical septotomy was performed across aortic zones 5 through 10 to establish a single true lumen channel for endograft delivery.

With the aorta prepared for endograft, the sheath was removed from the left common femoral artery and upsized to a 22F sheath over an Amplatz wire (Boston Scientific). Subsequently, a 6F antegrade perfusion sheath was placed into the superficial femoral. This sheath connected to the contralateral femoral sheath to allow for antegrade perfusion during the dwell time of the large left femoral sheath. The intravascular ultrasound catheter was passed. Images demonstrated a widely patent septostomy for the length of the aorta. The distal portion of prior stent graft was compressed by the dissection flap and was dilated with a Trilobe balloon (Medtronic). The right common femoral sheath was then upsized to 16F over an Amplatz wire. A buddy snare was advanced through the right femoral access and a buddy stiff angled glidewire was advanced via the left and engaged with the snare. This was withdrawn to establish through-and-through groin access. A 37 mm × 20 mm × 16 cm TAMBE was positioned extracorporeal on the Amplatz wire through the left groin. We then used the through wire access to position a triple lumen catheter and pass four 0.014” wires (Fig 2). These were placed through the precannulated hypotubes in the TAMBE device to each of the four portals. The device was then advanced and positioned in the aorta at which point the first stage constraint was deployed. After full deployment of the TAMBE main body, sequential visceral branch stenting was performed. Through the 16F sheath in the right groin, a 10F × 65 cm sheath was advanced over through wire. Using the precannulated through wires and tension from each groin the 10F sheath was retroflexed and pulled down into the graft (Fig 3, A). We then advanced a 6F × 90 cm sheath through the 10F sheath (Fig 3, B). This dual sheath telescoping technique was used to access each portal in sequence. The celiac artery was cannulated and a 9 mm × 59 mm VXB was deployed. SMA was cannulated and an 8 mm × 79 mm VBX stent (W. L. Gore & Associates) with 9 mm × 29 mm proximal extension was placed. The left renal artery was cannulated and a 7 mm x 59 mm VBX placed. Finally, the right renal artery was cannulated and a 6 mm × 79 mm with a 6 mm × 29 mm proximal extension was placed. All visceral branches were accessed and stented using the 6F sheath without difficulty. Attention was then turned to the right common iliac artery aneurysm where over a stiff wire an iliac branched endoprosthesis was deployed.

A Trilumen catheter being used to deliver four 0.014 pre-wires from the right to left femoral access.

(A) A right sheath system including a 16F and 10F was advanced in conjunction with the thoracoabdominal multibranch endoprosthesis (TAMBE) device. The 0.14 wires were used to create the retroflexion of the 10F sheath. (B) A 6F sheath was advanced through the 10F sheath over each visceral pre-wire to cannulate the portal. Once the 6F sheath was positioned through each portal the visceral arteries were selected with a catheter and glide wire.

A completion angiogram was performed and showed no evidence of endoleak (Fig 4). The sheath was removed, femoral arteries were closed, and groin incisions were closed. Bilateral doppler signals were obtained. The patient was admitted to the intensive care unit postoperatively and later discharged on postoperative day 3. He followed up 1 month later with a new computed tomography angiography as an outpatient with no complaints and no new aneurysmal degeneration or evidence of endoleak on imaging. He is doing well to date.

Completion angiography demonstrating total patency of all viscerals with thoracoabdominal multibranch endoprosthesis (TAMBE) and exclusion of aneurysm.

Discussion

The management of complex thoracoabdominal aortic aneurysms, especially in the context of chronic type A aortic dissections, poses a significant challenge in vascular surgery. The Gore TAMBE allows vascular surgeons quick and timely interventions without the delay of custom-made endoprostheses. Recent studies have shown comparable long-term outcomes in management of thoracoabdominal aortic aneurysms with TAMBE as compared with physician-modified endografts.2

We believe that the use of a total transfemoral approach for TAMBE deployment was critical in this case. Right upper extremity access was avoided owing to the need for prolonged large-bore sheath placement across the aortic arch, which may increase embolic stroke risk, particularly in patients with prior arch reconstruction. The patient's visceral vessels demonstrated adequate diameter, orientation, and target vessel length for TAMBE incorporation. Physician-modified endografts were considered less favorable owing to concerns regarding component separation and endoleak risk in the absence of dedicated portals. In addition, access through the groin was preferable over the risks with a carotid cutdown. Furthermore, at our institution, we prefer to prophylactically use antegrade perfusion during large-bore sheath dwell time to mitigate the risk of limb ischemia and reperfusion injury during prolonged procedures. Although we modeled a similar technique reported by Han et al,5 we used telescoping dual sheaths to which we were able to pull the 6F sheaths down into the portal, which gave us quick access. The 10F sheath served as a supportive conduit, allowing controlled retroflexion and stabilization of the 6F sheath as it was advanced into each portal sequentially.5

Conclusions

This case highlights the complexity of preparing and planning for complex endovascular procedures. Here, we successfully used the TAMBE to exclude the aneurysm, with preservation of vital branch perfusion (Fig 4). We hope this case further adds to current knowledge and practices regarding treatment of challenging and unique aortic pathologies.

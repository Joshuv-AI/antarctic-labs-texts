Abstract
. Let p p be a prime. For p = 2 p=2 , the fields of values of the complex irreducible characters of finite groups whose degrees are not divisible by p p have been classified; for odd primes p p , a conjectural classification has been proposed. In this work, we extend this conjecture to characters whose degrees are divisible by arbitrary powers of p p , and we provide some evidence supporting its validity.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 20C15; Secondary 20C30, 20C33

1.
Introduction

In
[
ILNT19
]
, Isaacs et al. discovered an unexpected connection
between the irrationalities of an irreducible character of a finite
group and the parity of its degree: if
\chi
is an irreducible
character of odd degree and its field of values is
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{d})

for some square-free integer
d\neq-1
, then
d\equiv 1\pmod{4}
. (Here, the field of values of
\chi
, denoted

{\mathbb{Q}}(\chi)
, is the smallest extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}
containing all
values of
\chi
.) This result was later generalized to arbitrary higher
irrationalities by the second and third authors in
[
NT21
]
,
providing a complete classification of the fields of values of the
irreducible characters of odd degree. For odd primes
p
, this
description is conjectured, remains open, and is reduced in

[
NT21
]
to a problem on quasi-simple groups.

Needless to say, the focus so far has been on characters of degree
not divisible by
p
(or on height zero characters
[
NRTV24
]
, if one
introduces Brauer
p
-blocks), largely motivated by the
Galois–McKay conjecture (
[
N04
]
) and its possible extensions.
An apparently innocent question raised in
[
IN24
]
(Question D),
however, has shifted this perspective: could the results of

[
NT21
]
also be extended to characters of degree divisible
by
p
? Given a prime
p
, what are the field of values of the
characters divisible by a given power of
p
? The most natural
setting to look for examples and patterns is when
p=2
in quadratic
fields
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{d})
, where
d
is a square-free integer. In all the cases
that we have checked, we observe, for instance, that if

{\mathbb{Q}}(\chi)={\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{d})
with even
d\neq\pm 2
, then
\chi(1)
is
divisible by
4
.

Our first result solves one direction of the classification of the
abelian number fields that can occur as fields of values of
irreducible characters whose degree has a given
p
-part. Throughout
this paper, let
\nu_{p}
denote the
p
-adic valuation function on
the integers; that is,
\nu_{p}(n)=a
if
p^{a}
is the largest power of

p
dividing
n
. If
\mathbb{F}/{\mathbb{Q}}
is an abelian extension, recall that
the conductor
c(\mathbb{F})
is the smallest integer
n\geq 1
such that
\mathbb{F}
is
contained in the
n
-th cyclotomic field
{\mathbb{Q}}_{n}:={\mathbb{Q}}(e^{2\pi i/n})
.
Also, if
m
is any positive integer, we let
\mathbb{F}_{m}:=\mathbb{F}(e^{2\pi i/m})
.
For a finite group
G
and
\chi\in{\rm Irr}(G)
, we define
c(\chi)
to
be the conductor of the field
{\mathbb{Q}}(\chi)
.

THEOREM A
.

Let
p
be a prime,
\mathbb{F}
an abelian extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}
, and

a:=\nu_{p}(c(\mathbb{F}))
. Then, for every integer
b\geq\nu_{p}([\mathbb{F}_{p^{a}}:\mathbb{F}])
, there exist a finite group
G
and

\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)
such that
{\mathbb{Q}}(\chi)=\mathbb{F}
and
\nu_{p}(\chi(1))=b
.

To complete the classification, we would need to prove the
following, which extends the still unproved Conjecture B.3 of

[
NT21
]
on characters of degree not divisible by
p

to arbitrary characters.

CONJECTURE B
.

Let
\chi
be an irreducible character
of some finite group such that
a:=\nu_{p}(c(\chi))\geq 1
. Then

\chi(1)
is divisible by
[{\mathbb{Q}}_{p^{a}}(\chi):{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}(\chi)]
.

A few remarks are in order. First, the degree of the extension

{\mathbb{Q}}_{p^{a}}(\chi)/{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}(\chi)
can be any power of
p
when
a

varies. Indeed: on one hand, every abelian extension
\mathbb{F}/{\mathbb{Q}}
can
arise as a character field (see Lemma
2.3
). On the other
hand, given a prime power
p^{a}
, elementary number theory shows that
there exists an extension
\mathbb{F}\supseteq{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
such that

\nu_{p}(c(\mathbb{F}))=a
and
[\mathbb{F}_{p^{a}}:\mathbb{F}]=p^{a-1}
. Second, if

{\mathbb{Q}}(\chi)={\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{d})
with even
d\neq\pm 2
, then

c(\chi)=4|d|
. Hence
\nu_{2}(c(\chi))=3
and
[{\mathbb{Q}}_{2^{3}}(\chi):{\mathbb{Q}}(\chi)]=4
. This would explain our earlier observation on the
quadratic character fields and the divisibility by
4
of character
degrees.

Since
[{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}(\chi):{\mathbb{Q}}(\chi)]=[{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}:({\mathbb{Q}}_{p}\cap{\mathbb{Q}}(\chi))]
, which
divides
[{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}:{\mathbb{Q}}]=p-1
, it follows that
[{\mathbb{Q}}_{p^{a}}(\chi):{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}(\chi)]
is precisely the
p
-part of
[{\mathbb{Q}}_{p^{a}}(\chi):{\mathbb{Q}}(\chi)]
. Theorem
A
therefore implies the
following.

THEOREM C
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be an abelian extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}
and
a:=\nu_{p}(c(\mathbb{F}))
.
Let
b\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\geq 0}
. Assume that Conjecture
B
holds
true. Then
\mathbb{F}
is the field of values of an irreducible character

\chi
of some finite group with
\nu_{p}(\chi(1))=b
if and only if

\nu_{p}([\mathbb{F}_{p^{a}}:\mathbb{F}])\leq b.

The next result presents evidence in support of
Conjecture
B
.

THEOREM D
.

Let
\chi
be an irreducible character
of some finite group
G
. Suppose
a:=\nu_{p}(c(\chi))\geq 1
. Then

\chi(1)
is divisible by
[{\mathbb{Q}}_{p^{a}}(\chi):{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}(\chi)]
if one
the following cases occurs.

(i)

G
is
p
-solvable;

(ii)

G
is an alternating group;

(iii)

G
is a general linear or general unitary group.

(iv)

G
is the special linear group
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(q)
and
p\nmid\gcd(n,q-1)
.

(v)

G
is the special unitary group
\operatorname{SU}_{n}(q)
and
p\nmid\gcd(n,q+1)
.

Also see Theorem
3.9
for results concerning
p^{\prime}
-extensions of
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(q)
and
\operatorname{SU}_{n}(q)
.

Next we turn to the related problem of classifying the fields of
values of the irreducible characters of finite groups whose order
has a given
p
-part (in particular, groups of
p^{\prime}
-order). This
leads us to the following basic problem: is it always true that

c(\chi)[{\mathbb{Q}}_{c(\chi)}:{\mathbb{Q}}(\chi)]
divides
|G|
? Interestingly, the
recently proposed
inductive Feit condition
(
[
BKNT25
]
)
implies what seemed to be an intractable problem.

THEOREM E
.

Suppose that the finite simple groups satisfy the inductive Feit condition.
Then the following hold:

(i)

If
G
is a finite group and
\chi\in{\rm Irr}(G)
, then
c(\chi)[{\mathbb{Q}}_{c(\chi)}:{\mathbb{Q}}(\chi)]
divides
|G|
.

(ii)

Let
\mathbb{F}/{\mathbb{Q}}
be an abelian extension, let
p
be a prime, and
b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
.
Then
\mathbb{F}={\mathbb{Q}}(\chi)
for some
\chi\in{\rm Irr}(G)
, where
G
is a finite group with
\nu_{p}(|G|)=b
, if and only if

\nu_{p}(c(\mathbb{F})[{\mathbb{Q}}_{c(\mathbb{F})}:\mathbb{F}])\leq b.

Theorems
A
,
D
, and

E
are proved in Sections
2
,

3
, and
4
, respectively.

2.
THEOREM
A

Our notation is mostly standard and essentially follows
[
I76
]
. We begin
by recalling a result of G. M. Cram that will be used frequently.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
N\trianglelefteq\,G
. Let
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)
and

\theta
be an irreducible constituent of
\chi_{N}
. Let
\psi
be
the Clifford correspondent of
\chi
with respect to
\theta
. Then

{\mathbb{Q}}(\chi)\subseteq{\mathbb{Q}}(\psi)
and
[{\mathbb{Q}}(\psi):{\mathbb{Q}}(\chi)]
divides

\chi(1)/\psi(1)
.

Proof.

This follows from
[
C88
, Lemma 1.1]
.
∎

The next lemma easily implies, using direct products, that if
\mathbb{F}

is the field of values of an irreducible character
\chi
with

\chi(1)_{p}=p^{b}
for
b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
, then
\mathbb{F}
also arises as the
field of values of a character whose degree has any
p
-part at
least
p^{b}
. Therefore, to classify the fields of values of
irreducible characters whose degree has a prescribed
p
-part, the
question we really have to solve is:
given an abelian number
field
\mathbb{F}
, what is the smallest possible
p
-part of the degree of
an irreducible character
\chi
such that
{\mathbb{Q}}(\chi)=\mathbb{F}
?

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
. Then there exists a solvable group
G
and a
rational-valued character
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)
such that
\chi(1)=d
.

Proof.

This is
[
HT23
, Lemma 2.1]
.
∎

We also need the following.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
and
\mathbb{F}\supseteq{\mathbb{Q}}
be a subfield of

{\mathbb{Q}}_{n}
. Then there exists an irreducible character
\chi
(of some
finite solvable group) such that
{\mathbb{Q}}(\chi)=\mathbb{F}
and

\chi(1)=[{\mathbb{Q}}_{n}:\mathbb{F}]
.

Proof.

This is
[
FG72
, Theorem 2.2]
. See also

[
NT21
, Theorem 2.2]
.
∎

For solvable groups, Theorem
C
follows from known
results.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be an abelian extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}
and
b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
. Then

\mathbb{F}
is the field of values of an irreducible character
\chi
of a
finite solvable group with
\nu_{p}(\chi(1))=b
if and only if

\nu_{p}([{\mathbb{Q}}_{c(\mathbb{F})}:\mathbb{F}])\leq b.

Proof.

The ‘only if’ implication directly follows from Cram’s theorem
(
[
C88
, Theorem 0.1]
). For the reverse implication, let

a:=\nu_{p}([{\mathbb{Q}}_{c(\mathbb{F})}:\mathbb{F}])\leq b
. By Lemma
2.3
, there
exists a solvable group
H
and a character
\psi\in\operatorname{Irr}(H)
such
that
{\mathbb{Q}}(\psi)=\mathbb{F}
and
\psi(1)=[{\mathbb{Q}}_{c(\mathbb{F})}:\mathbb{F}]
. On the other
hand, by Lemma
2.2
, there exists a solvable group
K
and a
rational-valued character
\varphi\in\operatorname{Irr}(K)
such that

\varphi(1)=p^{b-a}
.

Let
G:=H\times K
and
\chi:=\psi\otimes\varphi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)
. We
have
{\mathbb{Q}}(\chi)={\mathbb{Q}}(\psi,\varphi)={\mathbb{Q}}(\psi)=\mathbb{F}
and

\nu_{p}(\chi(1))=\nu_{p}(\psi(1)\varphi(1))=\nu_{p}(\psi(1))+\nu_{p}(\varphi(1))=a+(b-a)=b
.
∎

The next result we require is the
p^{\prime}
-degree case of
Theorem
A
.

Theorem 2.5

(
[
NT21
]
, Theorem B2)
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be an abelian extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}
with
\nu_{p}(c(\mathbb{F}))=a
.
Suppose that
[{\mathbb{Q}}_{p^{a}}:(\mathbb{F}\cap{\mathbb{Q}}_{p^{a}})]
is not divisible by

p
. Then there exists a finite group
G
and
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)

such that
{\mathbb{Q}}(\chi)=\mathbb{F}
.

One of the key constructions in producing the desired group and
character in Theorem
2.5
is to reduce from a character
field of values to a smaller one where the extension is cyclic, see

[
NT21
, Theorem 3.3]
. For our purpose, we need to
generalize this to arbitrary (abelian) extensions.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
\chi
be an irreducible character of a finite group
G
and set

{\mathbb{E}}:={\mathbb{Q}}(\chi)
. Then, for any subfield
\mathbb{F}
of
{\mathbb{E}}
, there
exists a finite group
H
and a character
\psi\in\operatorname{Irr}(H)
such that

{\mathbb{Q}}(\psi)=\mathbb{F}
and
\psi(1)=n\chi(1)^{n}
, where
n:=[{\mathbb{E}}:\mathbb{F}]
.

Proof.

Write
\mathcal{G}:=\mathrm{Gal}({\mathbb{E}}/\mathbb{F})
as a direct product
{\sf C}_{n_{1}}\times{\sf C}_{n_{2}}\times\cdots\times{\sf C}_{n_{k}}
of
cyclic groups of orders
n_{1},...,n_{k}
with
n_{i}>1
and
n_{i}
is
divisible by
n_{i+1}
. Note that
n:=\prod_{i=1}^{k}n_{i}=|\mathcal{G}|
. Let
\tau_{i}
be a generator of
{\sf C}_{i}
, so
that

\mathcal{G}=\{\tau_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots\tau_{k}^{a_{k}}\mid 1\leq a_{i}\leq n_{i}\text{ for every }1\leq i\leq k\}

Consider the
n
-iterated direct product of
G
and view its
elements as
k
-dimensional matrices:

G^{n}:=\{X=(x_{a_{1},...,a_{k}})_{1\leq a_{i}\leq n_{i}\text{ for every }1\leq i\leq k}\mid x_{a_{1},...,a_{k}}\in G\}.

Let
\tau_{i}
act on
G^{n}
by permuting the
i
th-dimensional indices
of the entries
x_{a_{1},...,a_{k}}
but fixing the indices at the other
dimensions, in the following way

(x_{a_{1},...,a_{k}})^{\tau_{i}}=(x_{a_{1},...,a_{i-1},a_{i}-1,a_{i+1},...,a_{k}}),

where two indices at the
i
th-dimension are identified if they have
the same residue modulo
n_{i}
. This action induces an embedding of

\mathcal{G}={\sf C}_{n_{1}}\times{\sf C}_{n_{2}}\times\cdots\times{\sf C}_{n_{k}}
into the symmetric group
{\mathrm{Sym}}_{n}
. Let

H:=G^{n}\rtimes\mathcal{G}

be the corresponding semidirect product.

Let

\varphi:=\bigotimes_{1\leq a_{i}\leq n_{i}}\varphi_{a_{1},...,a_{k}}\in\operatorname{Irr}(G^{n}),

where

\varphi_{a_{1},...,a_{k}}=\chi^{\tau_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots\tau_{k}^{a_{k}}}\in\operatorname{Irr}(G);

that is,

\varphi(X)=\prod_{1\leq a_{i}\leq n_{i}\atop 1\leq i\leq k}\chi^{\tau_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots\tau_{k}^{a_{k}}}(x_{a_{1},...,a_{k}}).

The action of a typical element
\tau=\tau_{1}^{b_{1}}\cdots\tau_{k}^{b_{k}}

of
\mathcal{G}
on
\varphi
is given by

(\varphi^{\tau})_{a_{1},...,a_{k}}=\chi^{\tau_{1}^{a_{1}+b_{1}}\cdots\tau_{k}^{a_{k}+b_{k}}}.

It is clear that
\varphi^{\tau}\neq\varphi
whenever
\tau\neq 1
.
Therefore, the stabilizer
I_{H}(\varphi)
of
\varphi
in
H
is
simply
G^{n}
, which in turn implies that

\psi:=\varphi^{H}\in\operatorname{Irr}(H).

We have

\psi(1)=[H:G^{n}]\varphi(1)=n\chi(1)^{n}
. The rest of the proof will
establish that
{\mathbb{Q}}(\psi)=\mathbb{F}
. This can be done by an suitable
modification of the arguments in the proof of
[
NT21
, Theorem
3.3]
.

By the definition of
\varphi
, we know

{\mathbb{Q}}(\varphi)={\mathbb{Q}}(\chi)={\mathbb{E}}
. The character-induction formula then
implies that
{\mathbb{Q}}(\psi)\subseteq{\mathbb{E}}
. Furthermore, for each
X\in G^{n}
and
j\in\{1,...,k\}
,

\varphi(X^{\tau_{j}})=\prod_{1\leq a_{i}\leq n_{i}\atop 1\leq i\leq k}\chi^{\tau_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots\tau_{k}^{a_{k}}}(x_{a_{1},...a_{j-1},a_{j}-1,a_{j+1},...,a_{k}}).

On the other hand,

\varphi^{\tau_{j}}(X)=\prod_{1\leq a_{i}\leq n_{i}\atop 1\leq i\leq k}\chi^{\tau_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots\tau_{j-1}^{a_{j-1}}\tau_{j}^{a_{j}+1}\tau_{j+1}^{a_{j+1}}\cdots\tau_{k}^{a_{k}}}(x_{a_{1},...,a_{k}}).

If all the
a_{1},...,a_{j-1},a_{j+1},...,a_{k}
are fixed, the
sub-products along the
j
th dimension are equal:

\prod_{1\leq a_{j}\leq n_{j}}\chi^{\tau_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots\tau_{k}^{a_{k}}}(x_{a_{1},...a_{j-1},a_{j}-1,a_{j+1},...,a_{k}})=\prod_{1\leq a_{j}\leq n_{j}}\chi^{\tau_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots\tau_{j-1}^{a_{j-1}}\tau_{j}^{a_{j}+1}\tau_{j+1}^{a_{j+1}}\cdots\tau_{k}^{a_{k}}}(x_{a_{1},...,a_{k}})

It follows that

\varphi(X^{\tau_{j}})=\varphi^{\tau_{j}}(X).

Since
\mathcal{G}
is generated by
\tau_{j},1\leq j\leq k
, we
conclude that

\varphi(X^{\tau})=\varphi^{\tau}(X)

for every
\tau\in\mathcal{G}
. That is, the Galois action of

\mathcal{G}
on
\varphi
is compatible with our defined action of

\mathcal{G}
on
G^{n}
.

Now, for every
X\in G^{n}
and
\tau\in\mathcal{G}
, by the Clifford
theorem, we have

\displaystyle\psi(X)^{\tau}=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{\tau^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}}\varphi(X^{\tau^{\prime}})\right)^{\tau}=\left(\sum_{\tau^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}}\varphi(X)^{\tau^{\prime}}\right)^{\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\tau^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}}\varphi(X)^{\tau\tau^{\prime}}=\sum_{\tau^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}}\varphi(X)^{\tau^{\prime}}=\psi(X).

Therefore,
\psi(X)
is fixed by
\mathcal{G}
point-wisely. As

\psi
vanishes outside
G^{n}
, we deduce that

{\mathbb{Q}}(\psi)={\mathbb{Q}}(\psi_{G^{n}})
is contained in the fixed field of

\mathcal{G}
in
{\mathbb{E}}
. That is,
{\mathbb{Q}}(\psi)\subseteq\mathbb{F}
.

By Lemma
2.1
, we have
[{\mathbb{Q}}(\varphi):{\mathbb{Q}}(\psi)]
divides

[H:G^{n}]=n
. As mentioned already that
{\mathbb{Q}}(\varphi)={\mathbb{E}}
, it
follows that
[{\mathbb{E}}:{\mathbb{Q}}(\psi)]\leq n=[{\mathbb{E}}:\mathbb{F}]
. This and the
conclusion of the preceding paragraph imply that
{\mathbb{Q}}(\psi)=\mathbb{F}
.
The proof is complete.
∎

We can now complete the proof of Theorem
A
, which is
restated.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be an abelian extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}
and
a:=\nu_{p}(c(\mathbb{F}))
.
Let
c:=\nu_{p}([\mathbb{F}_{p^{a}}:\mathbb{F}])
. Then, for every integer
b\geq c
, there exists a finite group
G
and a character
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)

such that
{\mathbb{Q}}(\chi)=\mathbb{F}
and
\nu_{p}(\chi(1))=b
.

Proof.

Let
{\mathbb{E}}:={\mathbb{Q}}_{p^{a}}(\mathbb{F})
. It is clear that
\nu_{p}(c({\mathbb{E}}))=a
and

[{\mathbb{Q}}_{p^{a}}:({\mathbb{E}}\cap{\mathbb{Q}}_{p^{a}})]=1
. Using Theorem
2.5
, we
obtain a finite group
H
and an irreducible character
\psi
of
H

of
p^{\prime}
-degree such that
{\mathbb{Q}}(\psi)={\mathbb{E}}
.

By the hypothesis, we have
[\mathbb{F}_{p^{a}}:\mathbb{F}]=[{\mathbb{E}}:\mathbb{F}]=p^{c}f
for
some
f\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
not divisible by
p
. Now applying Theorem

2.6
to the extension
{\mathbb{E}}/\mathbb{F}
, we then obtain
a finite group
M
and
\varphi\in\operatorname{Irr}(M)
such that

\varphi(1)=p^{c}f\psi(1)^{p^{c}f}\text{ and }{\mathbb{Q}}(\varphi)=\mathbb{F}.

Next, we use Lemma
2.2
to have a finite group
N
and some
rational-valued character
\lambda\in\operatorname{Irr}(N)
such that

\lambda(1)=p^{b-c}
.

Finally, let
G:=M\times N
and
\chi:=\varphi\otimes\lambda
– an
irreducible character of
G
. This
\chi
is indeed the required
character. To see that, we have

\chi(1)=\varphi(1)\lambda(1)=p^{c}f\psi(1)^{p^{c}f}\cdot p^{b-c}=p^{b}f\psi(1)^{p^{c}f}.

Recall that both
f
and
\psi(1)
are not divisible by
p
.
Therefore
\nu_{p}(\chi(1))=b
. Moreover,

{\mathbb{Q}}(\chi)={\mathbb{Q}}(\varphi,\lambda)={\mathbb{Q}}(\varphi)=\mathbb{F},

as desired.
∎

3.
Theorem
D

We begin with the following lemma about
p
-groups.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that
P
is a
p
-group. Let
\alpha\in{\rm Irr}(P)
be with
c(\alpha)=p^{a}
and
a\geq 1
.

(i)

If
p
is odd or
p^{a}=4
, then
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p^{a}}(\alpha)={\mathbb{Q}}_{p}(\alpha)
.

(ii)

If
p=2
and
p^{a}\geq 8
, then
|{\mathbb{Q}}_{p^{a}}(\alpha):{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}(\alpha)|\leq 2
divides
\alpha(1)
.

Proof.

If
p
is odd, then we know that
{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha)={\mathbb{Q}}_{p^{a}}
by

[
NT21
, Theorem 2.3]
. If
p=2
and
a=2
, then

{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha)\subseteq{\mathbb{Q}}(i)
and necessarily
{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha)={\mathbb{Q}}(i)
, so there
is nothing to prove. Assume now that
p=2
and
a\geq 3
. By
elementary Galois theory, we know that
{\mathbb{Q}}_{2^{a}}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{8}
is a cyclic
extension with intermediate fields
{\mathbb{Q}}_{8}\subseteq{\mathbb{Q}}_{16}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq{\mathbb{Q}}_{2^{a}}
. Therefore
{\mathbb{Q}}_{8}(\alpha)={\mathbb{Q}}_{2^{a}}={\mathbb{Q}}_{2^{a}}(\alpha)
.
Also
{\mathbb{Q}}_{8}\cap{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha)
cannot be
{\mathbb{Q}}
, since
{\mathbb{Q}}_{2^{a}}
has
only three subfields of degree 2 and all of them are contained in

{\mathbb{Q}}_{8}
. If
\alpha
is linear, notice that

[{\mathbb{Q}}_{p^{a}}(\alpha):{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}(\alpha)]=1
. Part (b) easily follows.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
G
be a
p
-solvable finite group, and let
\chi\in{\rm Irr}(G)
be
with
a=\nu_{p}(c(\chi))\geq 1
. Then
[{\mathbb{Q}}_{p^{a}}(\chi):{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}(\chi)]

divides
\chi(1)
.

Proof.

Notice that

Theorem 3.3
.

Let

and
\chi\in{\rm Irr}({\mathrm{Alt}}_{n})\cup{\rm Irr}({\mathrm{Sym}}_{n})
. Then

\nu_{p}(c(\chi))\leq 1
. In particular, Theorem

D
holds for the symmetric and alternating
groups.

Proof.

It is well known that the characters of
{\mathrm{Sym}}_{n}
are all rational,
so the result is immediate in this case. Hence, we may assume that

\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}({\mathrm{Alt}}_{n})
. For background on the ordinary irreducible
characters of
{\mathrm{Alt}}_{n}
, we refer the reader to
[
JK81
, §5]
.

Suppose that
\Psi
is the irreducible character of
{\mathrm{Sym}}_{n}
lying
over
\chi
, and let
\lambda
be the partition of
n
labeling

\Psi
. If
\lambda
is not self-conjugate, then
\chi=\Psi_{{\mathrm{Alt}}_{n}}
and the result is again trivial. Thus we assume that

\lambda
is self-conjugate. In this case,
\Psi_{{\mathrm{Alt}}_{n}}=\chi+\chi^{\sigma},
where
\sigma
is an odd permutation in
{\mathrm{Sym}}_{n}
.

Let
h(\lambda)=(h_{11}^{\lambda},\dots,h_{kk}^{\lambda})
denote the
partition of
n
whose parts
h_{ii}^{\lambda}
are the hook lengths
at the diagonal nodes
(i,i)
of the Young diagram of
\lambda
.
Here
k
is the length of the main diagonal. The conjugacy class of

{\mathrm{Sym}}_{n}
of cycle type
h(\lambda)
splits into two conjugacy
classes of
{\mathrm{Alt}}_{n}
, say
h(\lambda)^{+}
and
h(\lambda)^{-}
.

According to
[
JK81
, Theorem 2.5.13]
, the values of
\chi
(and of

\chi^{\sigma}
) are rational at all elements, except possibly at
those belonging to
h(\lambda)^{\pm}
. In fact, one has

{\mathbb{Q}}(\chi)={\mathbb{Q}}\!\left(\sqrt{(-1)^{(n-k)/2}\,h_{11}^{\lambda}\cdots h_{kk}^{\lambda}}\right).

Let

denote the square-free part of
h_{11}^{\lambda}\cdots h_{kk}^{\lambda}
, that is, the product of the prime divisors
occurring with odd exponents. Note that all the hook lengths

h_{ii}^{\lambda}
are odd and that

is even. We shall use

to denote the conductor of a sum of
roots of unity

, which is the smallest positive integer

such
that
z\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{t}
. If

, then

, and hence

On the other hand, if

, then

h_{11}^{\lambda}\cdots h_{kk}^{\lambda}\equiv 3\pmod{4}
, and hence

c(\chi)=c\!\left(\sqrt{-h_{11}^{\lambda}\cdots h_{kk}^{\lambda}}\right)=c(\sqrt{-d})=d.

It follows that
\nu_{p}(c(\chi))=1
if

divides

, and

\nu_{p}(c(\chi))=0
otherwise. This completes the proof.
∎

We now prove Theorem

for finite general

Lemma 3.4
.

In the above notation, assume that the integer

is
divisible by the order of every

Lemma 3.5
.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let

(i) It suffices to prove the theorem in the case

has

3.7
(i), for any

with

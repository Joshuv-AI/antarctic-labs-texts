Machine Learning Approach for the Prediction of Biomass Waste Pyrolysis Kinetics from Preliminary Analysis
Authors: Kai Xiao, Xianghui Zhu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
In the present work, artificial neural network (ANN)-based machine learning models are developed to predict biomass pyrolysis kinetics. Data sets of thermogravimetric analysis and feedstock characterization from a diverse range of biomasses were used to build and test the networks. The composition of the raw biomass material was classified and used as input parameters of ANN models. Three models, which use ultimate analysis, proximate analysis, and three components as input parameters, were developed in this study. A total of 32 types of biomass raw materials were used, and 270 sets of kinetic data were obtained according to different pyrolysis conversion rates ranging from 0.1 to 0.9. Results show that increasing the number of neurons can improve the prediction accuracy. The optimized neuron number is 7–11. The largest relative deviation between experimental and modeling results for the three models are 20.80%, 14.06% and 12.85%, respectively, which proves that using cellulose, hemicellulose, and lignin as input parameters of the neural network model can better predict the activation energy of pyrolysis at each reaction stage. The particle swarm optimization algorithm could significantly improve the prediction accuracy of the BP-ANN model. The largest deviation for activated energy prediction decreases from 12.85% to 6.72%.

Introduction

Biomass is the only renewable organic carbon resource on earth, which comes from a wide variety of sources, including crop straw, fuelwood, domestic sewage and industrial wastewater, livestock manure, and so on. The development and utilization of biomass energy have received continuous attention from governments and researchers around the world. The World Biomass Association believes that biomass will continue to be the world’s largest renewable energy source in the future and estimates that global biomass supply could reach 150 EJ by 2035, of which 43% will come from agriculture, 52% from forestry, and about 5% from other waste.1 In this context, increasing the development and utilization of biomass energy will help improve the global energy consumption structure and help alleviate the depletion of traditional fossil resources, and biomass energy is expected to become the backbone of achieving the goal of “carbon neutrality”.2

Biomass energy conversion technology mainly includes thermal chemical methods, physical–chemical methods, biochemical methods, and chemical methods, among which thermal chemical conversion is the most common utilization method of biomass energy. The main thermochemical conversion methods of biomass include direct combustion, gasification, and pyrolysis. Pyrolysis is a technology that uses heat energy to break chemical bonds in biomass macromolecules and transform them into low-molecular-weight products under isolated air conditions. It is not only a clean and effective method for producing bio-oil, combustible gas, and biochar but also the initial stage of direct combustion and gasification.3,4 Therefore, an in-depth understanding of biomass pyrolysis characteristics and exploration of biomass pyrolysis laws are helpful to find more efficient biomass thermochemical conversion methods.

From the perspective of energy utilization, lignocellulosic biomass has the greatest potential for development and is abundant and cheap. This kind of biomass mainly comes from crop straw and is mainly composed of cellulose, hemicellulose, and lignin in different proportions. The pyrolysis process involves numerous simultaneous complex reactions and the influence of the reaction conditions. The kinetic performance of the pyrolysis process is often very different from the proportion and characteristics of the obtained three-phase products. In the study of pyrolysis technology, a lot of work has been done to calculate the pyrolysis kinetic parameters of various lignocellulosic biomass through thermogravimetry (TG) analysis (TGA) and to obtain the proportion of three-phase products, product composition, and characteristics through pyrolysis experiment and analysis. However, all of these have undergone a strict kinetic calculation process and time-consuming experiment and analysis. It is not conducive to quickly and accurately understanding the characteristics of biomass and pyrolysis products.

The rapid development of artificial intelligence, especially machine learning methods, which can establish correlations between input features and output targets based on large amounts of data, has been recognized as an excellent means to discover hidden relationships behind data and has been well applied in the field of competence. Therefore, based on the existing research results on the pyrolysis kinetics and pyrolysis product analysis of lignocellulosic biomass, the relationship between the composition and structure characteristics of lignocellulosic biomass and the pyrolysis process can be found by means of machine learning, which is helpful in selecting appropriate reaction conditions for different biomass and conduct directed regulation of the pyrolysis process, which can greatly simplify the experimental and computational analysis process. Reducing the cost of experiments will be a very meaningful work.

At present, machine learning models for predicting biomass pyrolysis activation energy (Ea), pre-exponential factor (A), and reaction order (n) have been established based on ultimate analysis, proximate analysis, and content of three components of biomass. Sunphorka et al.5 used artificial neural network (ANN) models to predict the pyrolysis kinetic parameters based on three components. They found that all components had an effect on the activation energy. Cellulose had a strong effect on the pre-exponential factor, while hemicellulose had a strong effect on the reaction order. Çepelioğullar et al.6 conducted activation energy prediction of biomass wastes based on different neural network topologies. The experimental thermal data fitted quite well to the ANN predicted data. These models can be used to predict the kinetic parameters of biomass pyrolysis to a certain extent. However, most machine learning models obtained a single pyrolysis kinetic parameter. However, the composition of biomass is complex, the pyrolysis reactions are different in each stage of the pyrolysis process, and there are large differences.3 Therefore, a large temperature range of pyrolysis reaction cannot be described by a single kinetic parameter.7,8 Therefore, it is necessary to combine different pyrolysis reaction stages to obtain kinetic parameters at different temperature reaction intervals or at different conversion stages so as to provide more accurate guidance for the entire pyrolysis process. However, this study has not yet been reported.

In this paper, a machine learning model based on an ANN was constructed to investigate the structural characteristics of different biomass, such as elemental analysis, industrial analysis, three-component characteristics, etc., and to simulate and predict the pyrolysis kinetic characteristics of a large number of different lignocellulosic biomass materials. The changes of biomass pyrolysis kinetics under different conversion conditions were investigated, which provides important methodological support for the development of the biomass pyrolysis technology.

Pyrolysis Mechanism of Lignocellulosic Biomass

Pyrolysis Mechanism of Cellulose, Hemicellulose, and Lignin

Domestic and foreign scholars have carried out a large number of experimental studies and proposed dynamic models targeting apparent weight loss of biomass pyrolysis, such as the distributed activation energy model.9 Recently, some scholars have proposed micronetwork biomass models, such as the Monte Carlo model,10 to predict the detailed component information on pyrolysis. However, it should be noted that in the above-mentioned model studies, the types of biomass targeted and the heating conditions are very limited, so the kinetic parameters of these models do not have the universality of biomass types and heating conditions. In fact, there is a very complex nonlinear relationship between the kinetics of biomass pyrolysis and the type of biomass (chemical composition) and the heating conditions to which it is subjected to. This nonlinear relationship is essential for the development of universal biomass pyrolysis models, but it has not been fully recognized so far and needs further study.

Biomass is mainly composed of cellulose, hemicellulose, and lignin, and the pyrolysis behavior of the three components reflects the whole pyrolysis law of the birth material to a large extent. Therefore, it is necessary to study the influence of the three components on the pyrolysis of biomass.

In order to study the influence of three components on the weight loss characteristics of biomass pyrolysis, a thermal balance analyzer was used to study the weight loss characteristics of the three components and biomass, and on this basis, the pyrolysis kinetic model of biomass and the three components was established.

Kinetic Study of Biomass Pyrolysis

Many researchers have done a great deal of research on the pyrolysis mechanism of biomass. At present, there are many methods used to analyze the kinetic data of the non-isothermal solid phase reaction, which can be generally divided into the mechanism model method and iso-conversional method.14 The Dynamics Committee of the International Confederation for Thermal Analysis (ICTAC) does not recommend the use of the model method due to the inherent uncertainties in the determination of kinetic parameters.15 When the iso-conversional method is used, it is not constrained by the fixed model, and the uncertainty of estimating dynamic parameters can be avoided. For example, Wu et al.16 used iso-conversional methods to reveal the pyrolysis mechanism of tobacco waste and inferred the pyrolysis reactions under different conversion ranges according to the changing trend of activation energy. Some iso-conversional methods, such as KAS (Kissinger–Akahira–Sunose) and FWO (Flynn–Wall–Ozawa), which are based on the typical integral method, are widely used for biomass pyrolysis.17,18 Ceylan et al.19 used KAS, FWO, and Coats–Redfern methods to calculate the activation energy, pre-exponential factor, and reaction order of waste hazelnut shell biomass. However, these studies obtained pyrolysis kinetic parameters through the analysis of TGA data and then inferred the pyrolysis mechanism of biomass, and they did not further reveal the reaction mechanism according to the changes of functional groups, element content, free radicals, and other physicochemical properties of biochar during pyrolysis. The effects of cellulose, hemicellulose, and lignin on the physicochemical properties of biomass during pyrolysis were ignored. However, TG studies can obtain only lumped product yields, so global kinetic models are usually represented by lumped reactions. With the development of pyrolysis research, many global models with multistep reactions have been proposed, which can aggregate the yield of the final pyrolysis process. Among them, the global mechanism of cellulose pyrolysis has been studied extensively. The most popular example is the B–S model, which states that cellulose first breaks down into active cellulose, representing a liquid phase, which then degrades through two competing reactions: one to produce bio-oil and the other to produce coke and gas. This model can predict the experimental data well and has a wide application prospect. Based on the B–S model, other models of the cellulose pyrolysis mechanism have been developed. There are fewer global kinetic models of hemicellulose and lignin pyrolysis than linear models because their structures and pyrolysis processes are more complex. Modeling Method

ANN Model Building

Back propagation (BP) ANN is proposed by Rumelhart and McClelland.25,26 It is a multilayer feed-forward neural network that is trained and optimized based on the error reverse propagation algorithm. BP-ANN is one of the most widely used neural networks in different fields. Similar to other machine learning algorithms, there is no need for the ANN model to uncover the detailed mechanism of the real process in advance; it only needs to learn some rules between conditions and results, through its network training, and gets the results closest to the expected output values from the given input values.27 During BP-ANN modeling, several layers (one layer or multiple layers) of neurons are added between the input layer and the output layer, which are called hidden layers. The neurons in the hidden layers are called hidden nodes. The hidden nodes have no direct connection or information interaction with the outside world, but the change in their state can affect the relationship between the input and output variables. Each layer can have several nodes.28

During the ANN modeling, the prediction performance largely depends on the input layer parameters and network parameters. The input layer is discussed above. As for the BP-ANN model, the major network parameters, including the transfer function type of network layers, the weights and bias of the network, and the neuron number of the hidden layer, have crucial influence on model performance.

Transfer function plays a key role in BP-ANN modeling. The transfer function for the input layer and output layer is usually chosen as a linear function, namely pureline function. With respect to the hidden layer, nonlinear transfer functions are usually used. Sigmoid functions are the most widely used nonlinear transfer functions in BP-ANN modeling. There are two typical sigmoid functions, namely, Log-Sigmoid function and Tan-Sigmoid function. The equations of the Log-Sigmoid function and Tan-Sigmoid function are shown as follows.910

Data Sets Collection for Lignocellulosic Biomass Pyrolysis in Different Conditions

In this wok, the non-isothermal pyrolysis kinetics of lignocellulosic biomass was studied. As mentioned in the second part, biomass will show different reaction characteristics in different pyrolysis reaction stages during the heating process, so its reaction kinetic parameters will also change with the pyrolysis reaction. Therefore, in this paper, an equal conversion reaction model is adopted, and the conversion rate of the pyrolysis reaction process is taken as an important input parameter. At the same time, the composition of the biomass is investigated, including ultimate analysis, proximate analysis, and the proportion of the three components (cellulose, hemicellulose, and lignin).

The data parameters are derived from the data published in the literature. A total of 32 types of biomass raw materials were used, and 270 sets of kinetic data were obtained according to different pyrolysis conversion rates ranging from 0.1 to 0.9. The detailed data are summarized in the attached file.

Before the data can be used, they need to be normalized or standardized. The so-called normalization is to change the input and output data of the network to limit the processing to [0 1] or [−1 1]. Since the kinetic parameters and biomass composition and structure parameters in this paper are positive numbers, the normalization range is 0–1. The purpose of normalization is to prevent different types of parameters from producing different weights in the model prediction process, which will affect the overall prediction error. The BP-ANN model in this paper uses the preprocessing function mapminmax to normalize the data. After the prediction results are obtained in the model, the function is also used to reverse normalize the prediction results, and the actual prediction value is obtained.

Results and Discussion

Prediction of Kinetic Parameters Based on ANN Modeling

In this section, the BP-ANN models are built to predict the kinetic parameters, mainly the activation energies for different kinds of lignocellulosic biomass materials. As discussed above, under non-isothermal thermogravimetric conditions, biomass pyrolysis has different thermogravimetric rates at different temperature ranges. At the same time, in different temperature ranges, the reaction of the pyrolysis process also varies with temperature and conversion rate. Therefore, for biomass, a single pyrolysis kinetic parameter cannot be used to accurately describe the characteristics of the entire pyrolysis stage. Using the equal conversion model, the activation energy of different biomasses at different conversion stages can be obtained, and the pyrolysis process of complex biomass can be further analyzed. The pyrolysis process can then be precisely regulated.

Three components constitute the main structure of the lignocellulosic biomass. To some extent, the pyrolysis of biomass is the superposition of the three components. Of course, there are also interactions between the three components, and their synergistic effect has a significant impact on the change in activation energy and product generation during the pyrolysis. PSO-ANN Modeling for Biomass Pyrolysis

The BP neural network can predict the pyrolysis kinetic parameters to a certain extent. However, for complex multidimensional training data or setting different parameters, there are some defects such as overfitting, low convergence speed, falling into local optimal, and poor prediction effect.31 This greatly limits the application of neural networks in the complex pyrolysis process of biomass. In recent years, swarm intelligence optimization ANNs have gradually become a research hotspot in the field of modern optimization, and the particle swarm optimization (PSO) algorithm is a major research hotspot in the field of optimization algorithm.

PSO was first proposed by Eberhart and Kennedy,32 and its basic concept is derived from the study of the foraging behavior of birds.33 The particle speed can be dynamically adjusted according to the historical optimal position of the particle and the historical optimal position of the population. A particle has only two properties: velocity, which represents how fast it is moving, and position, which represents the direction it is moving. The optimal solution searched by each particle individually is called the individual extreme value, and the optimal individual extreme value in the particle swarm is the current global optimal solution. Through continuous iteration, the particle velocity and position are updated. Finally, the optimal solution satisfying the termination conditions is obtained.

It can be seen that the PSO algorithm could significantly improve the prediction performance of the BP-ANN model. The prediction of the neural network model is for the whole data, which is to build a neural network through a large amount of data. The difference in individual data does not reflect the accuracy of the model as a whole. With respect to these two models, it is obvious that the PSO-BP-ANN model shows better performance for the activation energy evaluation on the whole.

Besides, the relative difference values for conversion ratios of 0.2 and 0.7 are much larger than those for other conversion ratios. The reason may be that these two points are in the transition stage of the significant pyrolysis reaction process. The conversion ratio of 0.2 represents the initial stage of the pyrolysis reaction, and at this stage, the three components show different reaction properties. Therefore, there are large fluctuations in the activation energy. With respect to the conversion ratio of 0.7, the point represents the final stage of the pyrolysis reaction. The rapid devolatilization reaction gradually changes to a slow polycondensation stage with a small amount of gas release. These two stages largely depend on the raw materials. Therefore, the relative difference values are much larger than those of other stages.

Biomass is mainly composed of three components, namely, cellulose, hemicellulose, and lignin. The pyrolysis of lignocellulosic biomass is a complicated process. In addition to the respective pyrolysis characteristics of the three components, the interaction between the three components also affects the pyrolysis process. A single kinetic equation cannot accurately describe the characteristics of the whole pyrolysis process. Using an isoconversional model, the reaction characteristics of different pyrolysis stages can be revealed. In general, the proportion of the three components of biomass has a key effect on the overall pyrolysis reaction characteristics of biomass. This paper compares ultimate analysis, proximate analysis, and three components as input parameters of the ANN model and finds that three components as input parameters can get better prediction of dynamic parameters.

However, the individual BP-ANN model is prone to overfitting or local convergence problems in the process of network training. The PSO optimization algorithm can optimize the key weights of the neural network in advance, thus avoiding the adverse effects of random weights on the neural network. In this paper, the prediction effects of PSO-BP-ANN and BP-ANN models on biomass pyrolysis kinetic parameters were compared, and the results proved that the PSO optimization algorithm could significantly improve the prediction accuracy of the BP-ANN model.

Conclusions

The pyrolytic behavior of lignocellulosic biomass is highly complex, and its kinetic behavior varies with operating conditions and the type of biomass. In order to reduce the time and cost of the experimental process, it is necessary to build a precise model to quickly and accurately predict the pyrolysis process of biomass. In the present work, ANN-based machine learning models are developed to predict the biomass pyrolysis kinetics. Data sets of TGA and feedstock characterization from a diverse range of biomass were used to build and test the networks. The composition analysis of raw biomass material was classified and used as input data for ANN models. Three models, which use ultimate analysis, proximate analysis, and three components as input parameters, were developed in this study. A total of 32 types of biomass raw materials were used, and 270 sets of kinetic data were obtained according to different pyrolysis conversion rates ranging from 0.1 to 0.9. The main conclusions can be summarized as follows.

BP-ANN modeling results show that increasing the number of neurons can improve the prediction accuracy. The optimized neuron number ranges from 7 to 11, in which the MSE reaches a lower level.

Comparing different parameters as input layer, the largest relative deviation between experimental and modeling results for the three models are 20.80%, 14.06% and 12.85%, respectively, which proves that using cellulose, hemicellulose, and lignin as input parameters of the neural network model can achieve better prediction performance for the activation energy of pyrolysis at each reaction stage.

Individual BP-ANN models are prone to overfitting or local convergence problems in the process of network training. The PSO algorithm could significantly improve the prediction accuracy of the BP-ANN model. The largest deviation for activated energy prediction decreases from 12.85% to 6.72%.

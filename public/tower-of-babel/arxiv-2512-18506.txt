Detecting and Quantifying Isolated Singularities over Discrete Valuation Rings
Yotam Svoray

Abstract
This paper develops a theory of isolated hypersurface singularities in mixed characteristic ( 0 , p ) (0,p) , focusing on quotient rings over a Discrete Valuation Ring (DVR). We introduce and study analogues of the classical Tjurina and Milnor numbers for this setting, prove a generalized analogue of the Determinacy Theorem and the Mather-Yau Theorem for complete Noetherian local rings, and define numerical invariants that provide distinct criteria for detecting isolated singularities in the unramified and ramified cases.



1
Introduction

The studies of isolated singularities originated with the foundational work of Arnold in
[
59
]
, Milnor in
[
39
]
, and Tjurina in
[
56
]
in the complex setting. Their pioneering efforts involved the computation and analysis of hypersurface singularities using numerical invariants, specifically the Milnor and Tjurina numbers, along with key concepts like equivalences and determinacy. This framework was later generalized by Greuel and Kröning in
[
20
]
to algebraically closed fields of arbitrary characteristic, with subsequent expansions by Boubakri in
[
11
]
and Nguyen in
[
42
]
.

This paper continues this line of generalization by extending the theory of isolated singularities of hypersurfaces from algebraically closed fields to a more general setting of hypersurfaces over the ring of power series over a local ring. Specifically, we focus on quantifying and understanding quotient rings
\frac{V[[\underline{x}]]}{\langle f\rangle}
, where
V
is a Discrete Valuation Ring (DVR) with a chosen uniformizer
\pi
, with isolated singularities, i.e. such that
\left(\frac{V[[\underline{x}]]}{\langle f\rangle}\right)_{\mathfrak{q}}
is regular for every non-maximal prime ideal
\mathfrak{q}
. The key challenge lies in developing numerical invariants, analogous to the classical Tjurina and Milnor numbers, that can effectively detect and measure the severity of these singularities.

When moving to the DVR case, there is a constraint that we do not encounter when working over fields. In this case, the uniformizer
\pi
of
V
is non invertible, and behaves differently than the units in
V
. This causes the groups of
V
-automorphisms of the ring of formal power series over
V
to be much smaller compared to its field counterpart. Therefore elements
f,g\in V[[\underline{x}]]
that we would want to be considered as "equivalent up to a change of variables" can not be. For example, as in Example 1.1. in
[
13
]
, one would want to view
x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+\pi^{3}
and
x_{1}^{3}+x_{2}^{2}+\pi^{2}
as equivalent. But we have that

\frac{V[[x_{1},x_{2}]]}{\langle x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+\pi^{3}\rangle}\not\cong\frac{V[[x_{1},x_{2}]]}{\langle x_{1}^{3}+x_{2}^{2}+\pi^{2}\rangle},

as otherwise
\,\operatorname{mod}\,\pi
they would be isomorphic over the field
\frac{V}{\langle\pi\rangle}
, which is impossible since the first would define a cusp singularity and the second an ordinary double point.

In order to avoid this constraint, we would like to view the uniformizer
\pi
as an additional variable, which allows us to view formal power series over
V
in variables
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
as "power series in
\pi,x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
over
\frac{V}{\langle\pi\rangle}
" and since
\pi
behaves differently than the other variables, we would then like to formally replace
\pi
by a dummy variable
y
. In the example above, one has that

\frac{V[[x_{1},x_{2},y]]}{\langle x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+y^{3}\rangle}\cong\frac{V[[x_{1},x_{2},y]]}{\langle x_{1}^{3}+x_{2}^{2}+y^{2}\rangle}

by "replacing
\pi
by the dummy variable
y
".

Over an arbitrary DVR, it is not clear how we can do this "replacing" procedure formally. For example, if we look at
1+\pi+\pi x
, since
1+\pi
is a unit in
V\subset V[[\underline{x}]]
, then we can write

1+\pi+\pi x=1\cdot\pi^{0}x^{0}+1\cdot\pi^{1}x^{0}+0\cdot\pi^{0}x^{1}+0\cdot\pi^{2}x^{0}+1\cdot\pi^{1}x^{1}+\cdots

and

(1+\pi)+\pi x=(1+\pi)\cdot\pi^{0}x^{0}+0\cdot\pi^{1}x^{0}+0\cdot\pi^{0}x^{1}+0\cdot\pi^{2}x^{0}+1\cdot\pi^{1}x^{1}+\cdots.

Therefore, intuitively, there are two different ways to "replace
\pi
by
y
", which are

1+\pi+\pi x\rightsquigarrow 1\cdot y^{0}x^{0}+1\cdot y^{1}x^{0}+0\cdot y^{0}x^{1}+0\cdot y^{2}x^{0}+1\cdot y^{1}x^{1}+\cdots

or

(1+\pi)+\pi x\rightsquigarrow(1+\pi)\cdot y^{0}x^{0}+0\cdot y^{1}x^{0}+0\cdot y^{0}x^{1}+0\cdot y^{2}x^{0}+1\cdot y^{1}x^{1}+\cdots.

Yet, there is a way to systematically "replace
\pi
by a dummy variable
y
", by first looking at the expansion with respect to
x
as a power series, and then looking at its coefficients (as we formally define in Notation
3.1
), recalling that since
V
is a DVR, then every element in
V
can be written uniquely as a unit times a power of
\pi
.

In the example above, since we first look at the expansion with respect to
x
, then

(1+\pi)+\pi x=(1+\pi)\cdot x^{0}+\pi\cdot x^{1}+0\cdot x^{2}+\cdots,

and since
(1+\pi)=(1+\pi)\cdot\pi^{0}
and
\pi=1\cdot\pi^{1}
as elements of
V
, then we would replace
1+\pi+\pi x
by
(1+\pi)+yx
.

This point of view allows us to formally define an analogue of the Tjurina and Milnor numbers over
V
that behave similarly their field counterparts. In addition, it allows us to define equivalences
\sim
of hypersurface singularities (defined formally in Definition
3.9
). Using the notion of equivalence and the Tjurina number over
V
, we develop a theory of hypersurface singularities over
V
, analogous to the results over fields, as summarized in Section 2 of
[
21
]
. Our main results include the following:

1.

We present a generalization of the Determinacy Theorem (Section
2
) to complete Noetherian local rings, inspired by Pellikaan
[
44
]
and Boubakri-Greuel-Markwig in
[
10
]
. This result is then used to prove a generalized Mather-Yau theorem and to define a new numerical invariant for singularities over complete local rings that measures the determinacy.

2.

We define analogues of the Tjurina number and contact equivalence over
V[[\underline{x}]]
(Section
3
) by viewing the uniformizer of
V
"as a variable", and proving they satisfy properties similar to their field analogues, including the splitting lemma and Morse lemma.

3.

We define numerical invariants (in Section
4
) to detect isolated singularities based on whether
V
is ramified or unramified, drawing on recent works by Hochster and Jefferies
[
30
]
, Saito
[
48
]
, and KC
[
33
]
.

We summarize these results in the following Theorem, which is a combination of Theorem
2.4
, Proposition
2.28
, Proposition
3.14
, Proposition
3.27
, Proposition
4.14
, Corollary
4.23
, and Proposition

:

Theorem
.

1.

Let
R
be a complete Noetherian local ring, let
I\subset R[[\underline{x}]]
be an ideal, let
f,g\in I^{2}\subset R[[\underline{x}]]

(a)

If
f
satisfies

I^{k+2}\subset I\cdot\langle f\rangle+I^{2}\cdot J(f),

then
f
is
\left(2k-\ord_{I}\left(f\right)+2\right)-
determined with respect to
I
(as in Definition
2.1
).

(b)

Assume that
f
has finite determinacy with respect to
I
(as in Definition
2.1
). Then
f\overset{I}{\sim}g
(as in Definition
2.1
) if and only if for every
k\gg 0
we have an isomorphism of
R-
algebras

\frac{R[[\underline{x}]]}{\langle f,I^{k}\cdot J(f)\rangle}\cong\frac{R[[\underline{x}]]}{\langle g,I^{k}\cdot J(g)\rangle}.

2.

Let
(V,\langle\pi\rangle,\kappa)
be a complete DVR and let
f\in V[[\underline{x}]]
. Then

(a)

\tau_{V}(f)=0
(as in Definition
3.4
) if and only if
f\sim x_{1}
if and only if
\frac{V[[\underline{x}]]}{\langle f\rangle}
is a regular local ring.

(b)

If the characteristic of
\kappa
is not
2
and
\kappa
is quadratically closed then
\tau_{V}(f)=1
if and only if
f\sim\pi^{2}+x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+x_{n}^{2}
.

(c)

If
V
is unramified then
f
defines an isolated singularity if and only if
p\in\sqrt{J(f)}
and
\tau(f,\delta)<\infty
for every
p-
derivation
\delta
(as in Definition
4.11
) if and only if
p\in\sqrt{J(f)}
and
\tau^{\Delta}(f)<\infty
(as in Corollary
4.23
).

(d)

If
V
is ramified then
f
defines an isolated singularity if and only if
\pi\in\sqrt{J(f)}
and
\tau^{\pi}(f)<\infty
(as in Definition
4.26
).

As we see in the theorem above, there are four invariants over
V
that are analogues of the Tjurina number (namely
\tau_{V}(f),\tau(f,\delta),\tau^{\Delta}(f)
, and
\tau^{\pi}(f)
), each playing a different role in understanding the hypersurface
\frac{V[[\underline{x}]]}{\langle f\rangle}
. The first,
\tau_{V}(f)
, is defined in Definition
3.3
as the generic intersection of the ideal
\langle\tilde{f}\rangle+J(\tilde{f})
, where
\tilde{f}
is the element of
V[[\underline{x},y]]
associated with
f
where we systematically "replaced
\pi
by variable
y
" (as in Notation
3.1
). This invariant has the most properties that are similar to the Tjurina number as defined over fields. Yet, unlike as the field case, it does not fully detect isolated singularities, but a weaker behavior that is somewhat similar.

The second,
\tau(f,\delta)
, is defined in Definition
4.11
over unramified DVRs with respect to a chosen
p-
derivation
\delta
, which we can view as a operator that "derives with respect to
p
".
\tau(f,\delta)
detects isolated singularities completely (with an additional technical condition), yet it behaves very differently than the Tjurina number as defined over fields, in addition to highly depending on the choice of
\delta
. The third,
\tau^{\Delta}(f)
, is the value of
\tau(f,\delta)
when going
\,\operatorname{mod}\,p
, does not depend on the choice of
\delta
and behaves similarly to
\tau(f,\delta)
.

The fourth,
\tau^{\pi}(f)
, is defined in Definition
4.26
as an analogue of
\tau(f,\delta)
for some ramified DVR with uniformizer
\pi
, in which case we do not need to depend on
p-
derivaties, as in the ramified case we have a derivation that acts as "taking derivative with respect to
\pi
". All of these Tjurina numbers play different roles in quantifying detecting, and understanding isolated singularities over
V
and similar behaviors that elements in
V[[\underline{x}]]
may exhibit.

Determinacy over Complete Local Rings

In this section we provide a proof of the finite determinacy theorem over any complete Noetherian local ring
(R,\mathfrak{m},\kappa)
. These determinacy results are generalized versions of the results presented in Section 3.1 of
[
11
]
(which in turn are based on Section 2.2 of
[
21
]
in the characteristic zero case and were expanded upon in Section 2 of
[
10
]
and in
[
25
]
).

Definition 2.1
.

Let
I\subset R[[\underline{x}]]
be an ideal.

1.

We denote by
\mathcal{D}_{I}
the group of
R-
automorphisms
\varphi
of
R[[\underline{x}]]
that preserve
I
(i.e.
\varphi(I)=I
). Given
f,g\in I
we say that
f\overset{I}{\sim}g
if there exists some
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{I}
such that
\langle f\rangle=\langle\varphi(g)\rangle
.

2.

Let
f\in R[[\underline{x}]]
. We say that
f
is
k-
determined with respect to
I
if for every
g\in R[[\underline{x}]]
such that
f-g\in I^{k+1}
we have that
f\overset{I}{\sim}g
. We say that
f
has
finite determinacy with respect to
I
if
f
is
k-
determined with respect to
I
for some
k\geq 0
.

Example 2.2
.

Assume that
R
is a DVR with uniformizer
\pi
, that
\kappa
is quadratically closed, and set
f=\pi^{2}+x^{2}\in R[[x]]
. We show directly that
f
is
2-
determined with respect to
\mathfrak{M}=\langle x,\pi\rangle
. Given some
g\in R[[x]]
such that
f-g\in\mathfrak{M}^{3}
, we can write
g=x^{2}+\pi^{2}+h
where
h\in\langle x,\pi\rangle^{3}
, and so
h=\alpha_{1}x^{3}+\alpha_{2}x^{2}\pi+\alpha_{3}x\pi^{2}+\alpha_{4}\pi^{3}
for some
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3},\alpha_{4}\in R[[\underline{x}]]
. Therefore we have that

g=x^{2}+y^{2}+h=(1+\alpha_{3}x+\alpha_{4}\pi)(x^{2}(1+\alpha_{1}x+\alpha_{2}\pi)(1+\alpha_{3}x+\alpha_{4}\pi)^{-1}+\pi^{2}).

Observe that both
1+\alpha_{1}x+\alpha_{2}\pi
and
1+\alpha_{3}x+\alpha_{4}\pi
are units. Therefore, since
R
is a DVR (and therefore Henselian) and since
\kappa
is quadratically closed, there exists some unit
\beta\in R[[\underline{x}]]
such that
\beta^{2}=(1+\alpha_{1}x+\alpha_{2}\pi)(1+\alpha_{3}x+\alpha_{4}\pi)^{-1}
. So, if we define the automorphism
\varphi
by setting
\varphi(x)=\beta x
then we can conclude that
\varphi(f)=(1+\varphi(\alpha_{3})\beta x+\varphi(\alpha_{4})\pi)g
with
1+\varphi(\alpha_{3})\beta x+\varphi(\alpha_{4})\pi
being a unit, and thus
f\overset{\mathcal{K}}{\sim}g
, as desired.

Theorem 2.4

(Finite Determinacy Theorem)
.

Let
I\subset R[[\underline{x}]]
be an ideal. If
f\in I^{2}\subset R[[\underline{x}]]
satisfies

I^{k+2}\subset I\cdot\langle f\rangle+I^{2}\cdot J(f),

then
f
is
\left(2k-\ord_{I}\left(f\right)+2\right)-
determined with respect to
I
.

We start with a few technical lemmata:

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
f\in R[[\underline{x}]]
and let
\underline{z}=(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
be a tuple of elements such that
z_{i}\in\mathfrak{M}^{k}
for every
i
. Then we can write
f(x_{1}+z_{1},\dots,x_{n}+z_{n})=\sum_{\alpha}h_{\alpha}\underline{z}^{\alpha}
for some
h_{\alpha}\in R[[\underline{x}]]
with

1.

\ord(h_{\alpha})\geq\ord(f)-|\alpha|
for every
\alpha
,

2.

h_{0}=f
,

3.

h_{e_{j}}=\partial_{j}(f)
where
e_{j}
is the
j-
th unit vector,

4.

\ord(f(x_{1}+z_{1},\dots,x_{n}+z_{n})-f(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}))\geq\ord(f)+k-1
.

Proof.

First, note that for every
\beta=(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
we have that

\prod_{j=1}^{n}(x_{j}+z_{j})^{\beta}=\sum_{\gamma_{1}=0}^{\beta_{1}}\cdots\sum_{\gamma_{n}=1}^{\beta_{n}}c_{\beta,\gamma}\underline{x}^{\beta-\gamma}\underline{z}^{\gamma},

where
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n})
and
c_{\beta,\gamma}=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\binom{\beta_{j}}{\gamma_{j}}
. Thus, if we write
f(\underline{x})=\sum_{\beta}a_{\beta}\underline{x}^{\beta}
then by setting
h_{\alpha}=\sum_{\beta\geq\alpha}a_{\beta}c_{\beta,\alpha}\underline{x}^{\beta-\alpha}
, we have that

f(x_{1}+z_{1},\dots,x_{n}+z_{n})=\sum_{\beta}a_{\beta}\cdot\sum_{\gamma_{1}=0}^{\beta_{1}}\cdots\sum_{\gamma_{n}=1}^{\beta_{n}}c_{\beta,\gamma}\underline{x}^{\beta-\gamma}\underline{z}^{\gamma}=\sum_{\alpha}h_{\alpha}\underline{z}^{\alpha}.

Thus, since
\ord(f)=\min\{|\beta|+\ord(a_{\beta})\colon a_{\beta}\neq 0\}
we have that
\ord(h_{\alpha})=\min\{|\beta|-|\alpha|+\ord(a_{\alpha})\colon\beta\geq\alpha\}\geq\ord(f)-|\alpha|
. Now, since
c_{\beta,0}=1
for every
\beta
, then we must have that

h_{0}=\sum_{\beta}a_{\beta}\underline{x}^{\beta}=f.

In addition, if
e_{j}
denotes the unit vector (i.e. the vector
(0,\dots,0,1,0\dots,0)
where
1
appears only in the
j-
th component), then we have that
c_{\beta,e_{j}}=\beta_{j}
and
\beta-e_{j}=(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{j-1},\beta_{j}-1,\beta_{j+1},\dots,\beta_{n})
, in addition to the fact that if
\alpha_{j}\leq e_{j}
then
\alpha=0
. Therefore we must conclude that

h_{e_{j}}=\sum_{\beta}a_{\beta}\cdot\beta_{j}\cdot\underline{x}^{\beta-e_{j}}=\partial_{j}(f).

Now, for the last item, note that
f(x_{1}+z_{1},\dots,x_{n}+z_{n})-f(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=\sum_{|\alpha|>0}h_{\alpha}\underline{z}^{\alpha}
(as
h_{0}=f=h_{0}\underline{z}^{0}
). Since
z_{i}\in\mathfrak{M}^{k}
for every
i
, then
\ord(\underline{z}^{\alpha})\geq|\alpha|k
and so we have that

\ord(h_{\alpha}\underline{z}^{\alpha})=\ord(h_{\alpha})+\ord(\underline{z}^{\alpha})\geq\ord(f)-|\alpha|+k|\alpha|\geq\ord(f)+k-1,

with
\ord(f(x_{1}+z_{1},\dots,x_{n}+z_{n})-f(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}))=\min\{\ord(h_{\alpha}\underline{z}^{\alpha})\colon|\alpha|\neq 0\}
.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\varphi\colon R[[\underline{x}]]\to R[[\underline{x}]]
be an
R-
homomorpshim such that
\varphi(x_{j})-x_{j}\in\mathfrak{M}^{k}
for every
j
(where
k\geq 2
). Then for every
h\in\mathfrak{M}^{l}
we have that
\varphi(h)-h\in\mathfrak{M}^{l+k-1}
.

Proof.

Since
\varphi(x_{j})-x_{j}\in\mathfrak{M}^{k}
then there exists some
a_{j}\in\mathfrak{M}^{k}
such that
\varphi(x_{j})=x_{j}+a_{j}
. Therefore, given
h\in R[[\underline{x}]]
, we have that
\varphi(h)=h(x_{1}+a_{1},\dots,x_{n}+a_{n})
, and so the result follows from the last item of Lemma
2.5
.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
N,i\geq 1
integers. Let
b_{i,0}\in\mathfrak{M}^{N+i-1}
and
b_{i,j}\in\mathfrak{M}^{N+i}
for every
j=1,\dots,n
. Set
v_{i}=1+b_{i,0}
and set
\varphi_{i}
to be the
R-
automorphism of
R[[\underline{x}]]
defined by
\varphi_{i}(x_{j})=x_{j}+b_{i,j}
for every
j=1,\dots,n
. Set
\psi_{i}=\varphi_{i}\circ\varphi_{i-1}\circ\cdots\circ\varphi_{1}
and set inductively
u_{i}=v_{i}\cdot\varphi(u_{i-1})
with
u_{0}=1
. Then we have that:

•

For every
j
there exists some
b_{j}\in\mathfrak{M}^{N+1}
such that
\psi_{i}(x_{j})\to x_{j}+b_{j}
as
i\to\infty
.

•

The

\varphi

\varphi(x_{j})=x_{j}+b_{j}
for every

R-

R[[\underline{x}]]
.

•

There exists some
b_{0}\in\mathfrak{M}
such that
u_{i}\to u=1+b_{0}
as
i\to\infty
.

Proof.

First, observe that
\psi_{i}=\varphi_{i}\circ\psi_{i-1}
. Therefore, by the definition of
\psi_{i}
, there exists some
c_{i,j}\in R[[\underline{x}]]

\psi_{i}(x_{j})=x_{j}+c_{i,j}

(with

\psi_{1}=\varphi_{1}

, since

x_{j}+c_{i,j}=\psi_{i}(x_{j})=\varphi_{i}(\psi_{i-1}(x_{j}))=\varphi_{i}(x_{j}+c_{i-1,j})=x_{j}+b_{i,j}+\varphi_{i}(c_{i-1,j}),

and so we can conclude that

\psi_{i}(x_{j})-\psi_{i-1}(x_{j})=c_{i,j}-c_{i-1,j}=b_{i,j}+\varphi_{i}(c_{i-1,j})-c_{i-1,j}.

Now, since
b_{i,j}\in\mathfrak{M}^{N+i}
with
N+i\geq 2
, then by applying Lemma
2.6
(with
h=\psi_{i}(x_{j})\in\mathfrak{M}^{1}
and
k=N+i
) to
\varphi_{i}
, we can conclude that
\varphi_{i}(\psi_{i-1}(x_{j}))-\psi_{i-1}(x_{j})\in\mathfrak{M}^{N+i}

Thus, for every

Therefore we can conclude that the sequence

is Cauchy sequence for every

is a complete ring, we can conclude that for every

there exists some

Now, since for every

\varphi_{i}(u_{i-1})-u_{i-1}=\varphi_{i}(b_{i,0})-b_{i,0}

Lemma 2.9
.

Let

and let

be an ideal. If

for every

.

We prove this result by induction. For

. Now, assume that the result is true for some

and we will show that it is true for

homomorphism

1.

[

Tag 04GM
]
(or by corollary 1.9 in
[

that uniquely lift to some

Lemma 2.18
.

Let

\{\{b_{i,j}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}\colon j=1,\dots,n\}

b_{i,0}\in\mathfrak{M}^{N+i-1}
and
b_{i,j}\in\mathfrak{M}^{N+i}
for every
j=1,\dots,n
. Therefore by setting
\varphi_{i}(x_{j})=x_{j}+b_{i,j}
for every
i,j
, we get that
g-v_{i}\cdot\varphi_{i}(f)\in\mathfrak{M}^{N+1+i}
and that
\varphi_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{I}
, as desired.
∎

Corollary 2.22
.

If
I\subset\sqrt{\langle f\rangle+J(f)}
then
f
has finite determinacy with respect to
I
.

Proof.

If
I\subset\sqrt{\langle f\rangle+J(f)}
then by there exists some
k
such that
I^{k}\subset\langle f\rangle+J(f)
, and so the result follows from Theorem
2.4
.
∎

Remark 2.23
.

As in Section 3.1 of
[
11
]
, we can show an analogous results to those of Theorem
2.4
and of Corollary
2.22
for "right equivalence with respect to
I
". That is, we can say that
f\in R[[\underline{x}]]
is
k-
right determined with respect to
I
if for every
g\in R[[\underline{x}]]
with
f-g\in I^{k+1}
there exists some
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{I}
such that
\varphi(f)=g
, and we say that
f
is finite right determined if
f
is
k-
right determined for some
k
. Therefore, a similar proof to that of Theorem
2.4
tells us that if
I^{k+1}\subset I\cdot J(f)
then
f
is
(2k-\ord_{I}(f)+2)-
right determined with respect to

, and a similar proof to that of Corollary
2.22
would tell us that if

then

is finite right determined.

Inspired by Corollary
2.22
, we define an invariant that quantifies the determinacy of an element
f\in R[[\underline{x}]]
with respect to an ideal
I
.

Definition 2.24
.

Let
I\subset R[[\underline{x}]]
and let
f\in I^{2}\subset R[[\underline{x}]]
. The
Jacobian number of
f
with respect to

is defined to be

j_{I}(f)=\length_{R[[\underline{x}]]}\left(\frac{I}{\langle f\rangle+J(f)}\right).

Remark 2.25
.

1.

Note that if
f\in I^{2}
then by Lemma
2.9
we have that
\langle f\rangle+J(f)\subset I
, and so
j_{I}(f)
is well defined. In addition,
j_{I}(f)
if finite if and only if
I_{\mathfrak{p}}=(\langle f\rangle+J(f))_{\mathfrak{p}}
for every prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\neq\mathfrak{M}
.

2.

If we replace
R
by a field
k
and select
I=\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle
, then we have that
j(f)
would equal to
\tau(f)-1
, where
\tau(f)=\dim_{k}(\frac{k[[\underline{x}]]}{\langle f\rangle+J(f)})
is the Tjurina number of
f
over
k
. In fact, we see a similar property in Propostion
3.18
. For more information on the Tjurina number of fields and its connection to the topology and algebra of hypersurface singularities, see
[
21
,
39
,
56
,
59
]
.

3.

Note that the definition and the notation of the Jacobian number
j_{I}(f)
is inspired by the work of Siersma in
[
52
]
, Pellikaan in
[
45
]
, and Svoray in
[
54
]
.

Proposition 2.26
.

Let
I\subset R[[\underline{x}]]
and let
f\in R[[\underline{x}]]
with
j_{I}(f)<\infty
. Then:

1.

If
f\overset{I}{\sim}g
then
j_{I}(f)=j_{I}(g)
.

2.

f
is
(2\cdot j_{I}(f)-\ord_{I}(f)+2)
-determined with respect to
I
.

3.

If
\frac{R[[\underline{x}]]}{I}
is Cohen-Macaulay of dimension
\dim(R)+n-s
then
j_{I}(f)\geq\binom{s+\ord_{I}(f)-2}{s+1}
.

Proof.

The first item follows from Lemma
2.18
. For the second item, assume that
j_{I}(f)<\infty
, then it is enough to show that
I^{j_{I}(f)}\subset\langle f\rangle+J(f)
, as then the finite determinacy would follow from Theorem
2.4
. We have sequence of strict inclusions of modules

\frac{I}{\langle f\rangle+J(f)}\supset\frac{I^{2}+\langle f\rangle+J(f)}{\langle f\rangle+J(f)}\supset\frac{I^{3}+\langle f\rangle+J(f)}{\langle f\rangle+J(f)}\supset\cdots,

and since
j(f)<\infty
then
\frac{I}{\langle f\rangle+J(f)}
has length
j_{I}(f)
. Therefore the sequence must terminate at the
j_{I}(f)-
th element, and so
\frac{I^{j_{I}(f)}+\langle f\rangle+J(f)}{\langle f\rangle+J(f)}=0
, i.e.
I^{j_{I}(f)}\subset\langle f\rangle+J(f)
.
For the third item, If
0\neq f\in I^{\ord_{I}(f)}
then by Lemma
2.9
we can conclude that
\langle f\rangle+J(f)\subset I^{\ord_{I}(f)-1}
, which induces a surjective map of
R-
modules

\frac{I}{\langle f\rangle+J(f)}\geq\frac{I}{I^{\ord_{I}(f)-1}}.

By applying length over
R[[\underline{x}]]
to both sides of the equation we get that
j(f)
is bigger than the
R[[\underline{x}]]-
length of
\frac{I}{I^{\ord_{I}(f)-1}}
, which equals to
\binom{s+\ord_{I}(f)-2}{s+1}
(as it is the number of monomials in
s
variables of positive degree at most
\ord_{I}(f)-2
, where
s
corresponds to a sequence of length
s
that generate
I
).
∎

Remark 2.27
.

Since we are looking at any ideal
I\subset R[[\underline{x}]]
, the enumerative behavior of
j_{I}
becomes very complicated the more "complicated" the ideal is

. For example, as an analogue of Morse’s Lemma (see Theorem 2.46 in

is not

then we have that

, yet, since
\partial_{i}(f)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}2a_{j}\partial_{i}(a_{j})
, then by Lemma 4.2 in
[

, this is true if and only if the Jacobian matrix
D\psi\in\Mat_{r}(V[[\underline{x}]])
of the

homorphism
\psi
of

\psi(x_{i})=a_{i}
for every

We can use the determinacy theorem with respect to an ideal to conclude a version of the Mather-Yau theorem over

with respect to an ideal for finitely determined elements. For a zero characteristic version of the Mather-Yau theorem, see Theorem 2.26 in

or

.

Proposition 2.28

(Mather-Yau Theorem over
I
)
.

Let
I\subset R[[\underline{x}]]
be an ideal and let
f,g\in I^{2}\subset R[[\underline{x}]]
be such that
f
has finite determinacy with respect to
I
. Then
f\overset{I}{\sim}g
if and only if for every
k\gg 0
we have an isomorphism of
R-
algebras

\frac{R[[\underline{x}]]}{\langle f,I^{k}\cdot J(f)\rangle}\cong\frac{R[[\underline{x}]]}{\langle g,I^{k}\cdot J(g)\rangle}.

Proof.

Assume that
f\overset{I}{\sim}g
. Then there exists a unit
u\in R[[\underline{x}]]
and an automorphism
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{I}
of
R[[\underline{x}]]
such that
g=u\varphi(f)
. Then by Lemma
2.18
we have that
\langle g,I^{k}J(g)\rangle=\langle u\varphi(f),I^{k}J\left(u\varphi(f)\right)\rangle=\langle\varphi(f),I^{k}J\left(\varphi(f)\right)\rangle=\varphi\left(\langle f,I^{k}J(f)\rangle\right)
for every
k
, which gives us the desired isomorphism of
R-
rings.

Assume that for every big enough
k\gg 0
we have an isomorphism of
R-
rings
\frac{R[[\underline{x}]]}{\langle f,I^{k}\cdot J(f)\rangle}\cong\frac{R[[\underline{x}]]}{\langle g,I^{k}\cdot J(g)\rangle}
. Therefore, by the Lemma
2.14
, this isomorphism lifts to an automorphism
\varphi\colon R[[\underline{x}]]\to R[[\underline{x}]]
such that
\langle g,I^{k}J(g)\rangle=\varphi\left(\langle f,I^{k}J(f)\rangle\right)
. By Lemma
2.18
we have that
\langle\varphi(f),I^{k}J\left(\varphi(f)\right)\rangle=\varphi\left(\langle f,I^{k}J(f)\rangle\right)
, and so we can assume (up to
\overset{I}{\sim}
) that
\langle g,I^{k}J(g)\rangle=\langle f,I^{k}J(f)\rangle
. Therefore, there exists some
H_{1}\in R[[\underline{x}]]
and
H_{2}\in I^{k}J(g)
such that
f=H_{1}g+H_{2}
. By Lemma
2.9
we have that
J(f)\subset I^{\ord_{I}\left(f\right)-1}
and so
I^{k}J(f)\subset I^{k+\ord_{I}\left(f\right)-1}\subset I^{\ord_{I}\left(f\right)}
. Therefore
g\in I^{\ord_{I}\left(f\right)}
and
H_{2}\in I^{\ord_{I}\left(f\right)+k-1}
.

We show that
H_{1}
must be a unit. Assuming otherwise, then since we have that
\langle g,I^{k}J(g)\rangle=\langle f,I^{k}J(f)\rangle
, we can conclude that
g=H_{3}f+H_{4}
for some
H_{3}\in R[[\underline{x}]]
and
H_{4}\in I^{k}J(f)
. Therefore, since
f=H_{1}g+H_{2}
we have that
\left(1-H_{1}H_{3}\right)f=\left(H_{2}H_{3}\right)+H_{4}\in I^{k+\ord_{I}\left(f\right)-1}
. Since
H_{1}
is not a unit then
1-H_{1}H_{3}
must be a unit and we can conclude that
f\in I^{k+\ord_{I}\left(f\right)-1}
. This is impossible since
k+\ord_{I}\left(f\right)-1>\ord_{I}\left(f\right)
. Therefore we must have that
H_{1}
is a unit, and so
f-H_{1}g\in I^{\ord_{I}\left(f\right)+k-1}
. Since
f
has finite determinacy with respect to
I
and since
k\gg 0
(which we can assume is bigger than the determinacy order of
f
), then we have that
H_{1}g\overset{I}{\sim}f
. Yet,
H_{1}g\overset{I}{\sim}g
and so the result follows.
∎

Remark 2.29
.

Note that the fact that
\langle f\rangle+J(f)=\langle g\rangle+J(g)
does not give us that
f\overset{\mathcal{K}}{\sim}g
, as in the version of Mather-Yau theorem over
\mathbb{C}
(see, for example, Theorem 2.26 in
[
21
]
). If the characteristic of
R
is
p>0
, set
f=x_{1}^{p+1}+x_{2}^{p+1}
and
g=x_{1}^{p}+x_{1}^{p+1}+x_{2}^{p+1}
. Then we have that
\langle f\rangle+J(f)=\langle g\rangle+J(g)
but
f\not\overset{\mathcal{K}}{\sim}g
as they have different orders.

We end this section with a discussion on unfoldings over
R
. Unfoldings play a crucial role in singularity theory over fields, and for a deeper discussion see Chapter II of
[
21
]
or Section 2 of
[
22
]
.

Definition 2.30
.

Let
f\in R[[\underline{x}]]
. An
unfolding
of
f
is an element
f_{t}=f\left(\underline{x},t\right)\in R[[\underline{x}]][t]
of the form
f_{t}=f+tg
for some
g\in R[[\underline{x}]]
. Given some
t_{0}\in\mathfrak{m}
and some unfolding
f_{t}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\alpha}a_{{\alpha},j}\underline{x}^{\alpha}t^{j}\in R[[\underline{x}]][t]
of
f
, we set
f_{t_{0}}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\alpha}a_{{\alpha},j}\underline{x}^{\alpha}t_{0}^{j}\in R[[\underline{x}]]
.

Proposition 2.31
.

Let
f\in R[[\underline{x}]]
. Then:

1.

For every unfolding
f_{t}
of
f
there exists some
N
such that for every
t_{0}\in\mathfrak{m}^{N}
we have that
\ord\left(f\right)=\ord\left(f_{t_{0}}\right)
.

2.

If
f
has finite determinacy with respect to
\mathfrak{M}
then for every unfolding
f_{t}
of
f
there exists some
N
such that for every
t_{0}\in\mathfrak{m}^{N}
we have that
f\overset{\mathcal{K}}{\sim}f_{t_{0}}
.

Proof.

For the first item, we can write
f_{t}=f+tg\in R[[\underline{x}]][t]
for some
g\in R[[\underline{x}]][t]
. If we set
N=\ord\left(f\right)+1
then for every
t\in\langle t_{0}\rangle^{N}
we must have that
\ord\left(f\right)=\ord\left(f_{t_{0}}\right)
, as
tg\in\mathfrak{M}^{N}
. For the second item, if
f
is
k-
determined with respect to
\mathfrak{M}
then for every
N>k
we have that if
t_{0}\in\mathfrak{M}^{N}
then
f-f_{t_{0}}\in\mathfrak{M}^{k+1}
, and therefore
f\overset{\mathcal{K}}{\sim}f_{t_{0}}
.
∎

Remark 2.32
.

1.

Note that the results in Proposition
2.31
are much stronger than their counterparts over a field. That is, if
f_{t}
is a unfolding of
f\in k[[\underline{x}]]
(for some complete topologized field
k
), then in a neighborhood
U
of
t=0
we can only guaranty the inequalities
\mu\left(f_{0}\right)\geq\mu\left(f_{t_{0}}\right)
,
\tau\left(f_{0}\right)\geq\tau\left(f_{t_{0}}\right)
, and
\ord\left(f_{0}\right)\leq\ord\left(f_{t_{0}}\right)
, for every
t_{0}\in U
(where
\tau
and
\mu
are the Tjurina and Milnor number over
k
, respectively). This strengthened results come from the fact that over
R
, the variable
t
can affect the order of
f_{t}
but over a field they do not. For more information on the field case, see Appendix A in
[
22
]
.

2.

Inspired by Section 3 of
[
26
]
and Section I 2.4 of
[
21
]
, one can define that
f\in R[[\underline{x}]]
has
finite deformation type
if there exists a finite collection of elements
g_{1},...,g_{l}\in R[[x]]
such that for every unfolding
f_{t}\in R[[x]][t]
of
f
there exists some
k
such that for every
t_{0}\in\mathfrak{m}^{k}
there exists some
i
such that
f_{t_{0}}\overset{\mathcal{K}}{\sim}g_{i}\left(x\right)
. In the field case we know that finite deformation type singularities are exactly
ADE
(for more information on
ADE
and deformations, see
[
20
,
26
]
). But over a general local ring, Proposition
2.31
gives us in fact that every element of
R[[\underline{x}]]
of finite determinacy with respect to
\mathfrak{M}
has finite deformation type, which by Corollary
2.22
, is true for every
f\in R[[\underline{x}]]
such that
\sqrt{\langle f\rangle+J(f)}=\mathfrak{M}
.

3
Power Series over a DVR

In this section we focus on on the ring of power series
(V[[\underline{x}]],\mathfrak{m}_{V},\kappa)
over a DVR
V
with a chosen uniformizer
\pi
(where
\mathfrak{m}_{V}=\langle\pi,x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle
). The goal of this section is to develop an analogue of the Tjurina and the Milnor number and of
\overset{\mathcal{K}}{\sim}
over
V[[\underline{x}]]
and to show that they have similar properties by "viewing
\pi
as a variable", inspired by the discussion presented in the
1
Introduction
. We formalize this idea using the following Notational setup:

Notation 3.1
.

Rcall that given some
a\in V
, there exists a unique integer
n\geq 0
and a unique unit
u\in V
such that
a=u\pi^{n}
. Therefore, given some
f\in V[[\underline{x}]]
, if we can write
f=\sum_{\alpha}a_{\alpha}\underline{x}^{\alpha}
, then
a_{\alpha}
can be written uniquely as
u_{\alpha}\pi^{n_{\alpha}}
, and thus
f=\sum_{\alpha}u_{\alpha}\pi^{n_{\alpha}}\underline{x}^{\alpha}
. In this case, we denote

\tilde{f}(\underline{x},y)=\sum_{\alpha}u_{\alpha}y^{n^{\alpha}}\underline{x}^{\alpha}\in V[[\underline{x},y]],

where
V[[\underline{x},y]]=V[[x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y]]
is the ring of formal power series in variables
x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y
whose maximal ideal is
\mathfrak{n}_{V}=\langle p,x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y\rangle
.

Remark 3.2
.

1.

Note that the map
V[[\underline{x}]]\to V[[\underline{x},y]]
defined by
f\mapsto\tilde{f}
respects neither addition nor multiplication in general. Therefore, it can be thought of as a set-theoretical section of the projection
\text{pr}\colon V[[\underline{x},y]]\to V[[\underline{x}]]
defined by
\text{pr}\left(y\right)=\pi
and
\text{pr}\left(x_{i}\right)=x_{i}
for every
i
.

2.

The choice of uniformizer
\pi
of
V
does not effect the construction of
\tilde{f}
up to
\overset{\mathcal{R}}{\sim}
. Given two uniformizers
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
of
V
, then there exists some unit
v\in V
such that
\pi_{2}=v\pi_{1}
. Therefore, given some
f=\sum_{\alpha}u_{\alpha}\pi^{n_{\alpha}}\underline{x}^{\alpha}
as in Notation
3.1
, then computing
\tilde{f}
with respect to
\pi_{1}
gives us
\tilde{f}_{1}=\sum_{\alpha}u_{\alpha}y^{n_{\alpha}}\underline{x}^{\alpha}
and computing
\tilde{f}
with respect to
\pi_{2}
gives us
\tilde{f}_{2}=\sum_{\alpha}u_{\alpha}(vy)^{n_{\alpha}}\underline{x}^{\alpha}
. Thus
\tilde{f}_{1}\overset{\mathcal{R}}{\sim}\tilde{f}_{2}
by the automorphism
y\mapsto vy
.

Inspired by Notation
3.1
, we define a version of the Tjurina and Milnor number of
f
over
V[[\underline{x}]]
based on
\tilde{f}
, keeping in mind the idea that we want to view the uniformizer
\pi
as a variable. In order to do so, we would like to look at the colength of
J(\tilde{f})+\langle\tilde{f}\rangle
. But, this ideal is of at least coheight 1 in general, and so must have infinite length. Yet, the following proposition will tell us that if we intersect it with a "generic" hypersurface, it will have a constant finite colength.

Recall that given an ideal
I
, we say that an ideal
J\subset I
is a minimal reduction of
I
if there exists some
N
such that
JI^{N}=I^{N+1}
and if there is no ideal
J_{1}\subsetneq J
with this property (For more information, see Chapter 8 in
[
31
]
).

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
I\subset V[[\underline{x},y]]
be an ideal such that
\frac{V[[\underline{x},y]]}{I}
is a one dimensional ring. Then:

1.

There exists a Zariski-open subset
U\subset\frac{\mathfrak{n}_{V}}{\mathfrak{n}_{V}^{2}+I}
such that for every
a\in V[[\underline{x},y]]
, if
a+\left(\mathfrak{n}_{V}^{2}+I\right)\in U
then
a\,\operatorname{mod}\,I
generates a minimal reduction of the maximal ideal of
\frac{V[[\underline{x},y]]}{I}
.

2.

If
\langle a\rangle\,\operatorname{mod}\,I
is a minimal reduction of the maximal ideal of
\frac{V[[\underline{x},y]]}{I}
then
\textup{length}_{V[[\underline{x},y]]}\left(\frac{V[[\underline{x},y]]}{I+\langle a\rangle}\right)
equals to the multiplicity of
\frac{V[[\underline{x},y]]}{I}
(and therefore is finite and does not depend on the choice of
a
).

3.

For every
a\in V[[\underline{x},y]]
we have that the multiplicity of
\frac{V[[\underline{x},y]]}{I}
is less than or equal to
\textup{length}_{V[[\underline{x},y]]}\left(\frac{V[[\underline{x},y]]}{I+\langle a\rangle}\right)
.

Proof.

The first part is a special case of Theorem 8.6.6 in
[
31
]
(which in turn cites
[
43
,
58
]
). The second part is a special case of Proposition 11.2.2 in
[
31
]
. The third part is a special case of semicontinuity of length (for more information, see
[
19
]
).
∎

Definition 3.4
.

Let
f\in\mathfrak{m}_{V}\subset V[[\underline{x}]]
.

1.

We define the
Milnor number of
f
to be

\mu_{V}\left(f\right)=\textup{length}_{V[[\underline{x},y]]}\left(\frac{V[[\underline{x},y]]}{J(\tilde{f})+\langle a\rangle}\right)

for some
a\in V[[\underline{x},y]]
such that
\langle a\rangle\,\operatorname{mod}\,J(\tilde{f})
is a minimal reduction of the maximal ideal of
\frac{V[[\underline{x},y]]}{J(\tilde{f})}
, assuming that it is a one dimensional ring. Otherwise, we set
\mu_{V}\left(f\right)=\infty
.

2.

We define the
Tjurina number of
f
to be

\tau_{V}\left(f\right)=\textup{length}_{V[[\underline{x},y]]}\left(\frac{V[[\underline{x},y]]}{J(\tilde{f})+\langle\tilde{f},a\rangle}\right)

for some
a\in V[[\underline{x},y]]
such that
\langle a\rangle\,\operatorname{mod}\,(J(\tilde{f})+\langle\tilde{f}\rangle)
is a minimal reduction of the maximal ideal of
\frac{V[[\underline{x},y]]}{J(\tilde{f})+\langle\tilde{f}\rangle}
, assuming that it is a one dimensional ring. Otherwise, we set
\tau_{V}\left(f\right)=\infty
.

Remark 3.5
.

1.

Note that if
\mu_{V}\left(f\right)<\infty
then
\tau_{V}\left(f\right)<\infty
since
\tau_{V}\left(f\right)\leq\mu_{V}\left(f\right)
(for a generic choice of
a
). But as in the positive characteristic case (see, for example, Remark 1.2.4. in
[
11
]
), the other direction is note true. Take, for example,
f\left(x\right)=x^{p}+p^{p-1}
in the case where
p
is the uniformizer. Yet, as in Proposition 2.1 of
[
29
]
, we have that if
\tau_{V}\left(f\right)<\infty
then
\mu_{V}\left(f\right)<\infty
if and only if
\tilde{f}\in\sqrt{J(\tilde{f})+\langle a\rangle}
for a generic
a
as in Definition
3.4
.

2.

We do not set the multiplicity of
\frac{V[[\underline{x}]]}{\langle\tilde{f}\rangle+J(\tilde{f})}
(resp. of
\frac{V[[\underline{x}]]}{J(\tilde{f})}
) as the definition of
\tau_{V}\left(f\right)
(resp.
\mu_{V}(f)
) since the multiplicity of
\frac{V[[\underline{x},y]]}{I}
will always be finite (for every ideal
I
), and as we see in Proposition
3.6
, the finiteness of
\tau_{V}\left(f\right)
as defined above tells us that
f
has a special property that resembles having an isolated singularity. Note that over a field of positive characteristic
k
, one can define an alternative version of the Milnor number of
f\in k[[\underline{x}]]
as the multiplicity of
\frac{k[[\underline{x}]]}{J(f)+\langle f\rangle}
, and it behaves much more like its characteristic zero analogue. For more information, see Section 3 of
[
29
]
.

3.

Note that it is clear that the length of
\frac{V[[\underline{x},y]]}{J(\tilde{f})}
is infinite since
V[[\underline{x},y]]
is
(n+1)-
dimensional and
J(\tilde{f})
is generated by
n
elements, but it is not quite clear for
\frac{V[[\underline{x},y]]}{J(\tilde{f})+\langle\tilde{f}\rangle}
(assuming that it is not zero). Yet, if it is non zero, by Proposition
3.14
we must have that
f\in\mathfrak{m}_{V}^{2}
and so
\tilde{f}\in\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y\rangle^{2}
. Thus, by Lemma
2.9
, we have that
J(\tilde{f})+\langle\tilde{f}\rangle\subset\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y\rangle
.

The following lemma gives us that
\tau_{V}
detects a property of
f
that resembles "being an isolated singularity" (but not fully), as a mixed characteristic analogue of Lemma 2.3 in
[
21
]
. We discuss how to detect isolated singularities using an analogue of
\tau_{V}
in depth in Section
4
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
f\in V[[\underline{x}]]
and let
\tilde{\mathfrak{q}}
be a prime ideal in
V[[\underline{x},y]]
:

1.

If
\left(\frac{V[[\underline{x},y]]}{\langle\tilde{f}\rangle+J(\tilde{f})}\right)_{\tilde{\mathfrak{q}}}=0
then
\left(\frac{V[[\underline{x},y]]}{\langle\tilde{f}\rangle}\right)_{\tilde{\mathfrak{q}}}
is regular.

2.

If
\tau_{V}\left(f\right)<\infty
then for every prime ideal
\tilde{\mathfrak{q}}
in
V[[\underline{x},y]]
such that
\frac{V[[\underline{x},y]]}{\tilde{\mathfrak{q}}}
is two dimensional we have that
\left(\frac{V[[\underline{x},y]]}{\langle\tilde{f}\rangle}\right)_{\tilde{\mathfrak{q}}}
is regular.

Proof.

For the first part, note that this quotient being zero is equivalent to having
\langle\tilde{f}\rangle+J(\tilde{f})\not\subset\tilde{\mathfrak{q}}
. If
f\notin\tilde{\mathfrak{q}}
then we are done, since then
\left(\frac{V[[\underline{x},y]]}{\langle\tilde{f}\rangle}\right)_{\tilde{\mathfrak{q}}}=0
. Otherwise, if
\tilde{f}\in\tilde{\mathfrak{q}}^{2}
then by Lemma
2.9
we have that
J(\tilde{f})\subset\tilde{\mathfrak{q}}
. Therefore we must have that
\tilde{f}\in\tilde{\mathfrak{q}}\setminus\tilde{\mathfrak{q}}^{2}
, and so
\left(\frac{V[[\underline{x},y]]}{\langle\tilde{f}\rangle}\right)_{\tilde{\mathfrak{q}}}
is regular.

For the second part, if
\tau_{V}\left(f\right)<\infty
then by Remark
3.5
we have that
\frac{V[[\underline{x},y]]}{\langle\tilde{f}\rangle+J(\tilde{f})}
must be one dimensional. Thus, since
\frac{V[[\underline{x},y]]}{\tilde{\mathfrak{q}}}
is two dimensional, we must have that
\langle\tilde{f}\rangle+J(\tilde{f})\not\subset\tilde{\mathfrak{q}}
, and so by the previous part we must have that

\left(\frac{V[[\underline{x},y]]}{\langle f\rangle}\right)_{\tilde{\mathfrak{q}}}
is regular.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

1.

Note that the reverse direction of Proposition
3.6
is false. For example, if
p
is uniformizer of
V
, let
f=p^{2}+x_{1}^{p}+x_{2}^{p}+x_{3}^{p}\in V[[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]]
, and choose
\tilde{\mathfrak{q}}=\langle y,x_{1}^{p}+x_{2}^{p}+x_{3}^{p}\rangle
. Then
J(\tilde{f})\subset\tilde{\mathfrak{q}}
which tells us that
\left(\frac{V[[\underline{x},y]]}{\langle\tilde{f}\rangle+J(\tilde{f})}\right)_{\tilde{\mathfrak{q}}}\neq 0
but since
\tilde{f}\in\tilde{\mathfrak{q}}\setminus\tilde{\mathfrak{q}}^{2}
we have that
\left(\frac{V[[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},y]]}{\langle\tilde{f}\rangle}\right)_{\tilde{\mathfrak{q}}}
is regular.

2.

Note that from Lemma
3.6
we can conclude that if
\tau_{V}(f)<\infty
then for every
y-\pi\notin\tilde{\mathfrak{q}}\subset V[[\underline{x},y]]
we have that
\left(\frac{V[[\underline{x}]]}{\langle f\rangle}\right)_{\mathfrak{q}}
is regular where
\mathfrak{q}=\tilde{\mathfrak{q}}\,\operatorname{mod}\,(y-\pi)
.

The following Corollary is an analogue of item 3 of Proposition
2.26
.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
f\in V[[\underline{x}]]
be of order
s\geq 2
. Then we have that

\mu_{V}\left(f\right)\geq\tau_{V}\left(f\right)\geq\binom{n+s-2}{n+1}.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.9
we have that
\langle\tilde{f}\rangle+J(\tilde{f})\subset\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y\rangle^{s-1}
, and so we have a surjective map

\frac{V[[\underline{x},y]]}{\langle\tilde{f}\rangle+J(\tilde{f})}\to\frac{V[[\underline{x},y]]}{\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y\rangle^{s-1}}.

Therefore, by Proposition
3.3
we can conclude that
\tau_{V}\left(f\right)
is bigger than the multiplicity of the module
\frac{V[[\underline{x},y]]}{\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y\rangle^{s-1}}
over the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{n}_{V}
, which equals to
\binom{n+s-2}{n+1}
(as it is exactly the number of monomials in
x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y
of degree at most
s-2
).
∎

Inspired by Section
2
and by Notation
3.1
, we now turn to defining an analogue of
\overset{\mathcal{K}}{\sim}
that allows us to "view
\pi
as a variable" by moving from
f
to
\tilde{f}
.

Definition 3.9
.

Given some
f,g\in V[[\underline{x}]]
. We say that
f
and
g
are equivalent, denoted
f\sim g
, if
\tilde{f}\overset{\mathcal{K}}{\sim}\tilde{g}
as elements of
V[[\underline{x},y]]
.

The following proposition allows us to relate between equivalence and "change of variables" (especially in the case where we set
g=\tilde{g_{1}}
for some
g_{1}\in V[[\underline{x}]]
), as viewed, for example, in the classification process of Theorem B in
[
13
]
and in the notion of "can be written as", as presented in Definition 3.10 of
[
55
]
.

Proposition 3.10
.

Given
f\in V[[\underline{x}]]
and
g\in V[[\underline{x},y]]
, if
\tilde{f}\overset{\mathcal{K}}{\sim}g
then there exists a sequence
a_{1},\dots,a_{n+1}
that generates
\mathfrak{m}_{V}
and some
H\in V[[z_{1},\dots,z_{n+1}]]
(the ring of formal power series in some dummy variables
z_{1},\dots,z_{n+1}
) whose coefficients are units such that

f=H\left(\pi,x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\right)\text{ and }\langle\text{pr}(g)\rangle=\langle H\left(a_{1},\dots,a_{n+1}\right)\rangle.

In addition, if
f\left(\underline{x}\right)=\sum_{\alpha}u_{\alpha}\pi^{n_{\alpha}}\underline{x}^{\alpha}
as in Notation
3.1
then we can choose
H\left(z_{1},\dots,z_{n+1}\right)=\sum_{\alpha}u_{\alpha}z_{n+1}^{n_{\alpha}}\underline{z}^{\alpha}
(where
\underline{z}=(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
).

Proof.

Then there exists some automorphism
\varphi
of
V[[\underline{x}]]
and some unit
u\in V[[\underline{x},y]]
such that
ug=\varphi(\tilde{f})
. If we write
f
as
f\left(\underline{x}\right)=\sum_{\alpha}u_{\alpha}\pi^{n_{\alpha}}\underline{x}^{\alpha}
as in Notation
3.1
, then we have that
\varphi(\tilde{f})=\sum_{\alpha}u_{\alpha}\varphi\left(y\right)^{n_{\alpha}}\varphi\left(\underline{x}\right)^{\alpha}
. Since
\text{pr}(g)=\text{pr}\left(u^{-1}\varphi(\tilde{f})\right)=\text{pr}(u^{-1})\text{pr}\left(\varphi(\tilde{f})\right)
and since
\text{pr}\left(u^{-1}\right)
has to be a unit in
V[[\underline{x}]]
, then we can conclude that

\langle\text{pr}(g)\rangle=\langle\sum_{\alpha}u_{\alpha}\text{pr}\left(\varphi\left(y\right)\right)^{n_{\alpha}}\text{pr}\left(\varphi\left(\underline{x}\right)\right)^{\alpha}\rangle.

Since
\varphi
is an isomorphism then
\mathfrak{m}_{V}=\langle\text{pr}\left(\varphi\left(x_{1}\right)\right),\dots,\text{pr}\left(\varphi\left(x_{n}\right)\right),\text{pr}\left(\varphi\left(y\right)\right)\rangle
.
So we can set
H\left(z_{1},\dots,z_{n+1}\right)=\sum_{\alpha}u_{\alpha}z_{n+1}^{n_{\alpha}}\underline{z}^{\alpha}
where
\underline{z}=\left(z_{1},\dots,z_{n}\right)
together with
a_{i}=\text{pr}(\varphi(x_{i}))
for
i=1,\dots,n
and
a_{n+1}=\text{pr}(\varphi(y))
, and the result follows.
∎

The following proposition is a mixed characteristic version of Lemma 2.10 in
[
21
]
and of Lemma 1.2.7 in
[
11
]
.

Proposition 3.11
.

If
f\sim g
then
\ord\left(f\right)=\ord\left(g\right)
and
\tau_{V}\left(f\right)=\tau_{V}\left(g\right)
.

Proof.

If
f\sim g
then there exists a unit
u\in V[[\underline{x},y]]
and some automorphism
\varphi
such that
\tilde{f}=u\varphi(\tilde{g})
. First, If
g\in\mathfrak{m}_{V}^{2}\setminus\mathfrak{m}_{V}^{r+1}
then
\tilde{g}\in\mathfrak{n}_{V}^{2}\setminus\mathfrak{n}_{V}^{r+1}
. Therefore
\tilde{f}=u\varphi(\tilde{g})\in\mathfrak{n}_{V}^{2}\setminus\mathfrak{n}_{V}^{r+1}
and so
f\in\mathfrak{m}_{V}^{2}\setminus\mathfrak{m}_{V}^{r+1}
. Second, by Lemma
2.18
we have that
\langle\tilde{f}\rangle+J(\tilde{f})=\langle\tilde{g}\rangle+J\left(\tilde{g}\right)
and the result follows from Proposition
3.3
(for a generic choice of
a\in V[[\underline{x},y]]
as in Definition
3.4
).
∎

Remark 3.12
.

As in the positive characteristic case, if
f\sim g
then we can not conclude that
\mu_{V}\left(f\right)=\mu_{V}\left(g\right)
. For example, set
f\left(x\right)=x^{p}+p^{p-1}\in V[[x]]
, assuming
p
is a uniformizer of
V
. Then
\mu_{V}\left(f\right)=\infty
since
\frac{V[[\underline{x},y]]}{J(\tilde{f})}
defines a ring of dimension 2, but
\mu_{V}\left(\left(1+x\right)f\right)
is finite since we have that
\sqrt{J\left(\left(1+x\right)\left(x^{p}+y^{p-1}\right)\right)}=\langle x,y\rangle
. Yet, as we see in Remarks
3.15
and
3.28
, there are some very specific cases where this is true.

The following lemma tells us that
\overset{\mathcal{K}}{\sim}
over
V[[\underline{x}]]
induces the equivalence
\sim
.

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
f,g\in V[[\underline{x}]]
and assume
\frac{V[[\underline{x}]]}{\langle f\rangle}\cong\frac{V[[\underline{x}]]}{\langle g\rangle}
as
V-
algebras. Then
f\sim g
.

Proof.

If
\frac{V[[\underline{x}]]}{\langle f\rangle}\cong\frac{V[[\underline{x}]]}{\langle g\rangle}
then by Lemma
2.13
there exists some automorphism
\varphi
of
V[[\underline{x}]]
such that
\langle\varphi\left(f\right)\rangle=\langle g\rangle
. Therefore, we can extend
\varphi
to
V[[\underline{x},y]]
by setting
\varphi\left(y\right)=y
, which would give us that
\langle\varphi(\tilde{f})\rangle=\langle\tilde{g}\rangle
, and the result follows.
∎

The following proposition shows that regular hypersurfaces are exactly of order
1
and have a specific form up to equivalence (as a mixed characteristic version of Lemma 2.44 in
[
21
]
).

Proposition 3.14
.

Let
f\in\mathfrak{m}_{V}\subset V[[\underline{x}]]
. Then the following are equivalent:

1.

\frac{V[[\underline{x}]]}{\langle f\rangle}
is a regular local ring,

2.

f
is of order
1
,

3.

f\sim x_{1}
,

4.

\tau_{V}\left(f\right)=0
.

Proof.

For
1\to 2
, if
S=\frac{V[[\underline{x}]]}{\langle f\rangle}
is regular (with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{S}
) and
f\in\mathfrak{m}_{V}^{2}
then we would have that

\dim_{\kappa}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{m}_{V}}{\mathfrak{m}_{V}^{2}}\right)=\dim_{\kappa}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{m}_{S}}{\mathfrak{m}_{S}^{2}}\right)\neq\dim V[[\underline{x}]],

which is a contradiction. For
2\to 1
, if
f
is of order
1
then
f\in\mathfrak{m}_{V}\setminus\mathfrak{m}_{V}^{2}
, and so
f
is a non zero divisor. Therefore, since
V[[\underline{x}]]
is regular, then so is
\frac{V[[\underline{x}]]}{\langle f\rangle}
. For
2\to 3
, if
f
is of order
1
then
\tilde{f}
must be of order
1
(in
V[[\underline{x},y]]
), and so we can write
\tilde{f}=uy+u_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+u_{n}x_{n}+g
for some
g\in\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y\rangle^{2}
and some
u,u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\in V
that are either zero or units. So, by looking at the
V-
automorphism of
V[[\underline{x},y]]
defined by
x_{1}\mapsto uy+u_{1}x_{1}+\cdots u_{n}x_{n}
, we can conclude that
\tilde{f}\overset{\mathcal{K}}{\sim}x_{1}+g_{1}
for some
g_{1}\in\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y\rangle^{2}
. Therefore by the implicit and the inverse function theorem (Lemma
2.13
) we get that
f\sim x_{1}
. For
3\to 4
, if
f\sim x_{1}
then by Proposition
3.11
we can conclude that
\tau_{V}\left(f\right)=\tau_{V}\left(x_{1}\right)=0
. For
4\to 2
, assume that
\tau_{V}\left(f\right)=0
. Then
1\in\langle\tilde{f},a\rangle+J(\tilde{f})
for some
a
as in Proposition
3.3
. Since
a\in\mathfrak{n}_{V}
, we must have that
1\in\langle\tilde{f}\rangle+J(\tilde{f})
and so there exists some
b_{1},\dots,a_{n},b,c\in V[[\underline{x},y]]
such that

1=b\partial_{y}(\tilde{f})+c\tilde{f}+\sum_{i}b_{i}\partial_{i}(\tilde{f}).

But since
f\in\mathfrak{m}_{V}
, then either
\partial_{y}(\tilde{f})\notin\mathfrak{n}_{V}
or there exists some
i
such that
\partial_{i}(\tilde{f})\notin\mathfrak{n}_{V}
. Then by Lemma
2.9
we can conclude that that
\tilde{f}
is of order
1
and therefore so is
f
.
∎

Remark 3.15
.

Note that given
f\in\mathfrak{m}_{V}
, since
\mu_{V}\left(f\right)\geq\tau_{V}\left(f\right)
then
\mu_{V}\left(f\right)=0
gives us that
\tau_{V}\left(f\right)=0
as well. In fact, the reverse is also true. It
\tau_{V}\left(f\right)=0
then by the proof of Proposition
3.14
we must have that
J(\tilde{f})=V[[\underline{x},y]]
, and so
\mu_{V}(f)=0
.

We turn to a discussion on determinacy over
V
, based upon the results of Section
2
.

Definition 3.16
.

We say that
f\in V[[\underline{x}]]
is
k-
determined
if for every
g\in V[[\underline{x}]]
, if
\tilde{f}-\tilde{g}\in\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y\rangle^{k+1}
then
f\sim g
. We say that
f
has
finite determinacy
if it is
k-
determined for some
k
.

Note that a similar definition can be defined for elements in
V[[\underline{x},y]]
.

Remark 3.17
.

Note that
\tilde{f}
is
k-
determined (as in Section
2
) then we can conclude that
{f}
is
k-
determined. Thus, by Theorem
2.4
we have the following version of the finite determinacy for
V[[\underline{x}]]
: If
f\in\mathfrak{m}_{V}^{2}\subset V[[\underline{x}]]
with

\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y\rangle^{k+2}\subset\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y\rangle\cdot\langle\tilde{f}\rangle+\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y\rangle^{2}\cdot J(\tilde{f})\subset V[[\underline{x},y]],

then
f
is
\left(2k-\ord\left(f\right)+2\right)-
determined.

The following proposition is an analogue of Corollary 2.24 in
[
21
]
and of Lemma 2.6 in
[
20
]
.

Proposition 3.18
.

Let
f\in V[[\underline{x}]]
with
\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y\rangle\subset\sqrt{\langle\tilde{f}\rangle+J(\tilde{f})}
and denote by
\overline{f}=\tilde{f}\,\operatorname{mod}\,\pi
, which we view as an element of
\kappa[[\underline{x},y]]
. Then:

1.

\tau_{V}\left(f\right)<\infty
,

2.

f
is finitely-determined,

3.

\overline{f}
defines an isolated singularity over
\kappa[[\underline{x},y]]
.

Proof.

Since
\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y\rangle\subset\sqrt{\langle\tilde{f}\rangle+J(\tilde{f})}
then there exists some
k
such that
\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y\rangle^{k}\subset\langle\tilde{f}\rangle+J(\tilde{f})
, hence

has finite determinacy by Proposition
2.4
and Remark

. In addition,
\frac{V[[\underline{x},y]]}{\langle\tilde{f}\rangle+J(\tilde{f})}
is a one dimensional ring, and so by Remark

we must have that
\tau_{V}\left(f\right)<\infty
. Now, since
\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y\rangle^{k}\subset\langle\tilde{f}\rangle+J(\tilde{f})
then going
\,\operatorname{mod}\,\pi
gives us that
\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y\rangle^{k}\subset\langle\overline{f}\rangle+J\left(\overline{f}\right)
in
\kappa[[\underline{x},y]]
. Therefore we can conclude that
\overline{f}
defines an isolated singularity (for more details, see Section 1.2 of
[
11
]
).
∎

Remark 3.19
.

1.

Note that not every
f\in V[[\underline{x}]]
with
\tau_{V}\left(f\right)<\infty
has the property that
\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y\rangle\subset\sqrt{\langle\tilde{f}\rangle+J(\tilde{f})}
. For example, if

is the uniformizer of

, then for

we have that the radical of
{\langle\tilde{f}\rangle+J(\tilde{f})}
equals to
\langle py,x+y\rangle
, which does not contain
\langle x,y\rangle
.

2.

Using the notations of Proposition

, note that if
f\sim g
then
\overline{f}\overset{\mathcal{K}}{\sim}\overline{g}
over
\kappa[[\underline{x}]]
. However, the opposite is not true. For example, if
\kappa
is of characteristic

then

and

are contact equivalent over
\kappa[[x,y]]
(since
x^{p}+y^{p}=(x+y)^{p}
over

) but

(as one has finite

and the other does not).

We end this section with a discussion of elements in

.

Definition 3.20
.

Given some
f\in V[[\underline{x}]]
, we define the
Hessian matrix of
f
is the matrix defined as follows: we can write

\tilde{f}=v_{0}y^{2}+\sum_{i}\frac{v_{i}}{2}yx_{i}+\sum_{i\neq j}\frac{v_{i,j}}{2}x_{i}x_{j}+\sum_{i}v_{i,i}x_{i}^{2}+\tilde{g},

for some units
v_{0},v_{i},v_{i,j}
for every
i,j
and some
g\in\mathfrak{m}_{V}^{3}
, as in Notation
3.1
. Therefore we set

\Hess(f)=\begin{bmatrix}v_{1,1}&\dots&v_{1,n}&v_{1}\\
\vdots&&\vdots&\vdots\\
v_{n,1}&\dots&v_{n,n}&v_{n}\\
v_{1}&\dots&v_{n}&v_{0}\end{bmatrix}\in\textup{Mat}_{n+1}(V).

Remark 3.21
.

1.

Note that the Hessian matrix
H=\Hess(f)
satisfies the property that there exists some
g\in\mathfrak{m}_{V}^{3}
such that
\tilde{f}=\vec{x}H\vec{x}^{\intercal}+\tilde{g}
where
\vec{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y)
.

2.

We can view
\Hess(f)
as the matrix of second partial derivatives of
\tilde{f}
at
\underline{0}
. That is, the
(i,j)-
th coordinate of
\Hess(f)
is exactly
\frac{\partial^{2}(\tilde{f})}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}(\underline{0},0)\in V
(where
x_{n+1}=y
).

3.

We introduce the following notation: For every
r>0
, we define the ideal
\mathfrak{a}_{r}=\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}\langle\pi^{i}\rangle\cdot\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y\rangle^{r-i}\subset V[[\underline{x},y]]
. For example, we have that
\mathfrak{a}_{3}=\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y\rangle^{3}+\langle\pi\rangle\cdot\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y\rangle^{2}+\langle\pi^{2}\rangle\cdot\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y\rangle
in
V[[x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y]]
. Intuitively,
g\in\mathfrak{a}_{r}
if
g(\underline{0})=0
and
g\in\mathfrak{n}_{V}^{r}
. Note that
\mathfrak{a}_{r}
is preserved under any automorphism of
V[[\underline{x},y]]
.

The following proposition is an analogue over
V[[\underline{x}]]
of the splitting lemma over field (see, for example, Theorem 2.47 of
[
21
]
and Lemma 3.9 of
[
22
]
), and it is a generalized analogue of Lemma 3.1 in
[

. For more information on the splitting lemma over fields, see

. In addition, its proof is inspired by the proof of Theorem 3.7 in

Proposition 3.22

(Splitting Lemma)
.

Assume that
\kappa
is quadratically closed and of characteristic different than
2
. Let
f\in V[[\underline{x}]]
be of order
2
. Then there exists some
0\leq k\leq n
and some
g\left(x_{k+1},\dots,x_{n}\right)\in\mathfrak{a}_{3}\subset V[[x_{k+1},\dots,x_{n}]]
such that

\tilde{f}\overset{\mathcal{R}}{\sim}y^{2}+x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+x_{k}^{2}+g\left(x_{k+1},\dots,x_{n}\right).

Proof.

First, since
f\in\mathfrak{m}_{V}^{2}\setminus\mathfrak{m}_{V}^{3}
then we have that
\tilde{f}\in\mathfrak{n}_{V}^{2}\setminus\mathfrak{n}_{V}^{3}
. So, following Notation
3.1
, we can write
\tilde{f}=\sum_{\alpha}u_{\alpha}y^{n_{\alpha}}\underline{x}^{\alpha}
, and so we set
f_{1}=\sum_{|\alpha|=2}u_{\alpha}y^{n_{\alpha}}\underline{x}^{\alpha}
and
h_{1}=\tilde{f}-f_{1}\in\mathfrak{n}_{V}^{3}
.

Now, by Definition
3.20
we can write

Definition 3.23

Lemma 3.24

If

Proof.

The following lemma tells us that the residual part of

Lemma 3.25
.

Given
f,g\in\mathfrak{a}_{3}\subset V[[x_{1},\dots,x_{l},y]]
, we have that
f\left(x_{1},\dots,x_{l}\right)\overset{\mathcal{K}}{\sim}g\left(x_{1},\dots,x_{l}\right)
if and only if
f\left(x_{1},\dots,x_{l}\right)+y^{2}+x_{l+1}^{2}+\dots+x_{n}^{2}\overset{\mathcal{K}}{\sim}g\left(x_{1},\dots,x_{l}\right)+y^{2}+x_{l+1}^{2}+\dots+x_{n}^{2}
in
V[[\underline{x},y]]
.

Proof.

First, if
f\left(x_{1},\dots,x_{l}\right)\overset{\mathcal{K}}{\sim}g\left(x_{1},\dots,x_{l}\right)
then there exists some unit
u\in V[[x_{1},\dots,x_{l},y]]
and some automorphism
\varphi
of
V[[x_{1},\dots,x_{l},y]]
such that
f=u\varphi(g)
. So, by extending
\varphi
to an automorphism
\psi
of
V[[\underline{x},y]]
by setting
\psi\left(x_{i}\right)=v^{-1}x_{i}
for every

, where

, we can conclude that

f\left(x_{1},\dots,x_{l}\right)+x_{l+1}^{2}+\dots+x_{n}^{2}=u\psi\left(g\left(x_{1},\dots,x_{l}\right)+x_{l+1}^{2}+\dots+x_{n}^{2}\right).

Second, assume that

F=f\left(x_{1},\dots,x_{l}\right)+y^{2}+x_{l+1}^{2}+\dots+x_{n}^{2}\overset{\mathcal{K}}{\sim}g\left(x_{1},\dots,x_{l}\right)+y^{2}+x_{l+1}^{2}+\dots+x_{n}^{2}=G.

Therefore, there exists some
\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{n+1}\in V[[\underline{x},y]]
and some unit
u
such that

g\left(\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{l}\right)+\sum_{i=l+1}^{n+1}\varphi_{i}^{2}=uf\left(x_{1},\dots,x_{l}\right)+u\cdot\sum_{i=l+1}^{n}x_{i}^{2}+uy^{2}.

Now, write
\psi_{i}=u^{-0.5}\varphi_{i}=a_{i}+h_{i}
where
a_{i}
is a linear term over
\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y\rangle
and

. Then we have that
\sum_{i=l+1}^{n+1}a_{i}^{2}-\sum_{i=l+1}^{n+1}x_{i}^{2}-y^{2}\in\mathfrak{n}_{V}^{2}
. In addition, we have that

\sum_{i=l+1}^{n+1}\psi_{i}^{2}=\sum_{i=l+1}^{n+1}a_{i}^{2}+\sum_{i=l+1}^{n+1}h_{i}(2a_{i}+h_{i})

Since
\ord_{\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y\rangle}(a_{l+1})=\cdots=\ord_{\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y\rangle}(a_{n+1})=1
, we have that
\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}a_{i}^{2}=\sum_{i=l+1}^{n}x_{i}^{2}+y^{2}
. Now, if we look at
F_{i}=2a_{i}+h_{i}
(recalling that the characteristic of
\kappa
is not
2
) then

\det\left(\left[\frac{\partial F_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}\left(0\right)\right]_{n+1\geq i,j\geq l+1}\right)=2\det\left(\left[\frac{\partial a_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}\left(0\right)\right]_{n+1\geq i,j\geq l+1}\right)\in V^{\times},

where
x_{n+1}=y
. Therefore, by the implicit function theorem and the inverse function theorem (Lemma
2.13
), there exists some
\theta_{l+1},\dots,\theta_{n+1}\in V[[x_{1},\dots,x_{l}]]
for which we have that
F_{i}\left(x_{1},\dots,x_{l},\theta_{l+1},\dots,\theta_{n+1}\right)=0
for every
i
. For every
i\leq l
define
\beta_{i}=\psi_{i}\left(x_{1},\dots,x_{l},\theta_{l+1},\dots,\theta_{n+1}\right)
. Then the map
\beta

V[[x_{1},\dots,x_{l},y]]
defined by
\beta\left(x_{i}\right)=\beta_{i}
must be an automorphism. This gives us that the map defined by
\left(\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{l},a_{l+1}+\frac{h_{l+1}}{2},\dots,a_{n+1}+\frac{h_{n+1}}{2}\right)
is an automoprhism of

. Thus, by the inverse function theorem (Lemma

\langle\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{l},a_{l+1}+\frac{h_{l+1}}{2},\dots,a_{n+1}+\frac{h_{n+1}}{2}\rangle=\mathfrak{n}_{V}
. Therefore, for every

So

Proposition 3.27

(Morse’s Lemma)
.

Let

1.

\tau_{V}\left(f\right)=1
,

2.

\text{rk}\left(f\right)=n
,

3.

f\sim\pi^{2}+x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+x_{n}^{2}
.

Proof.

Note that we have that
\tau_{V}\left(f\right)=1
if and only if
J(\tilde{f})+\langle\tilde{f},a\rangle=\mathfrak{n}
. In addition, the equivalence of
2
and

follows by Proposition
3.22
and the definition of

\mathfrak{a}_{3}

\tilde{f}\overset{\mathcal{R}}{\sim}y^{2}+x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+x_{k}^{2}+g\left(x_{k+1},\dots,x_{n}\right)
for some

, which must be non zero since
\tau_{V}\left(f\right)<\infty
. Yet, by direct computation we must have that
\frac{V[[\underline{x},y]]}{\langle\tilde{f}\rangle+J(\tilde{f})}\cong\frac{V[[x_{k+1},\dots,x_{n},y]]}{\langle g\rangle+J(g)}
(where we view

must be bigger than

. Therefore we must have that

.
∎

4
Isolated Singularities over a DVR

In this section we show how the work of Hochster and Jefferies in
[
30
]
, Saito in
[
48
]
, and KC in
[
33
]
can inspire a similar construction to our Tjurina number that detects singularities over unramified and ramified DVRs, as opposed to Proposition
3.6
.

We start with the unramified case. Specifically, we assume that
(V,\mathfrak{m}_{V},\kappa)
is a complete DVR of mixed characteristic
(0,p)
whose uniformizer is
p
(i.e.
\mathfrak{m}_{V}=\langle p\rangle
) and
\kappa
is algebraically closed. In this case we want to quantify when
f\in V[[\underline{x}]]
defines an isolated singularity. We first review a few facts about
p-
derivation, as described in
[
30
]
.

Remark 4.1
.

By the Cohen structure theorem (see
[
14
]
), every complete DVR with uniformizer
p
and an algebraically closed (therefore perfect) quotient field
\kappa
must be isomorphic to the ring of Witt vectors over
\kappa
. For more about this connection between Witt vectors and Cohen rings, see
[
1
]
or Section 6 of
[
28
]
, and for a deeper review of Witt vectors, see
[
46
]
or Chapter II, Section 6 of
[
51
]
.

For notational convention, we define the polynomial

C_{p}\left(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\right)=\frac{X_{1}^{p}+\cdots+X_{n}^{p}-\left(X_{1}+\cdots+X_{n}\right)^{p}}{p}\in\mathbb{Z}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}].

Note that
C_{p}\left(X_{1},X_{2}\right)\in\langle X_{1}X_{2}\rangle
and in general we have that
C_{p}\left(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\right)\in\langle X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\rangle^{2}
.

The following definition is a combination of Definition 2.2 and Definition 2.5 in
[
30
]
.

Definition 4.2
.

A
p-
derivation is a map
\delta\colon V[[\underline{x}]]\to V[[\underline{x}]]
such that for every
f,g\in V[[\underline{x}]]
t:

1.

\delta\left(1\right)=\delta\left(0\right)=0
,

2.

\delta\left(f+g\right)=\delta\left(f\right)+\delta\left(g\right)+C_{p}\left(f,g\right)
,

3.

\delta\left(f\cdot g\right)=f^{p}\delta\left(g\right)+g^{p}\delta\left(f\right)+p\delta\left(f\right)\delta\left(g\right)
,

4.

\delta\,\operatorname{mod}\,p^{2}\colon\frac{V[[\underline{x}]]}{\langle p^{2}\rangle}\to\frac{V[[\underline{x}]]}{\langle p\rangle}
satisfies the conditions above.

Remark 4.3
.

The notion of
p-
derivations goes back to the works of Buium in
[
12
]
and Jóyal in
[
32
]
. In addition, Jóyal proved that the forgetful functor from the category of rings with
p-
derivations to the category of rings is the left adjoint of the Witt vector functor (with the natural
p-
derivation). One can view
p-
derivations as "derivations with respect to
p
", and play an analogous role to derivations, as we see with the mixed characteristic Jacobian criterion in Theorem
4.8
or with the mixed characteristic Zariski-Nagata Theorem in
[
17
,
18
]
.

The following lemma summarizes a few results about
p-
derivations that are presented in Section 2 of
[
30
]
.

Lemma 4.4
.

1.

For every
a\in V\subset V[[\underline{x}]]
we have that
\delta\left(a\right)=\frac{\Frob(a)-a^{p}}{p}
, where
\Frob
is the unique lift of the Frobenius map to V (i.e. the unique endomorphism
\Frob
on
V
that satisfies
\Frob(a)\equiv a^{p}\,\operatorname{mod}\,p
for every
a\in V
).

2.

Given
\mathfrak{a}\subset V[[\underline{x}]]
an ideal containing
p
, if
f\in\mathfrak{a}^{k+1}
then
\delta\left(f\right)\in\mathfrak{a}^{k}
.

3.

For every
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}
there exists a unique
p
-derivation
\delta
to
V[[\underline{x}]]
such that
\delta\left(x_{i}\right)=f_{i}
.

Remark 4.5
.

1.

For more on the construction of
\Frob
as the lift of the Frobenius map and for its properties, see
[
15
]
.

2.

The second item of Lemma
4.4
can be viewed as a
p-
derivation analogue of Lemma
2.9
.

Note that by Lemma
4.4
, a
p-
derivation
\delta
on
V[[\underline{x}]]
is uniquely defined by its values on
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
, and so we can associate the set of all
p-
derivation
\delta
on
V[[\underline{x}]]
with
(V[[\underline{x}]])^{\times n}=V[[\underline{x}]]\times\cdots\times V[[\underline{x}]]
(together with the product
\mathfrak{m}_{V}-
adic topology) via the association
\delta\mapsto(\delta(x_{1}),\dots,\delta(x_{n}))
. Specifically, given a point
\underline{a}\in(V[[\underline{x}]])^{\times n}
we denote
\delta_{\underline{a}}
the unique
p-
derivation that satisfies
\delta_{\underline{a}}(x_{i})=a_{i}
for every

.

Proposition 4.6
.

Given some
f\in V[[\underline{x}]]
, then the map
(V[[\underline{x}]])^{n}\to V[[\underline{x}]]
defined by
\underline{a}\mapsto\delta_{\underline{a}}(f)
is continuous (with respect to the the product
\mathfrak{m}_{V}-
adic topology).

Proof.

Note that by item 2 of Lemma
4.4
it is enough to prove the result for
f\in V[[\underline{x}]]
that are polynomial, as we can approximate
\delta_{\underline{a}}(f)
by looking at the
\delta_{\underline{a}}
of the finite Taylor expansions of
f
. In addition, since sum and product are continuous with respect to the
\mathfrak{m}_{V}-
adic topology, it is enough to prove this result for monomials in
p,x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
, which we prove via induction. The map
\delta\mapsto\delta(x_{i})
is continuous since it is simply the projection from the product topology. In addition, note that by Lemma
4.4
we can conclude that
\delta(p^{j})=\frac{p^{j}-p^{jp}}{p}
. Now, if
p^{j}\underline{x}^{\alpha}
is a monomial (with
\alpha\neq\underline{0}
), we can write it as
p^{j}\underline{x}^{\alpha}=x_{l}\underline{x}^{\beta}
, and so
\delta(p^{j}\underline{x}^{\alpha})=\delta(x_{l}p^{j}\underline{x}^{\beta})=x_{l}^{p}\delta(p^{j}\underline{x}^{\beta})+p^{jp}\underline{x}^{p\beta}\delta(x_{l})+p\delta(x_{l})\delta(p^{j}\underline{x}^{\beta})
, which is continuous as a combination of continuous functions.
∎

The following proposition gives us a general formula for
\delta
of some element in
V[[\underline{x}]]
, given the values
\delta(x_{i})
and the coefficients of
f
as a power series.

Proposition 4.7
.

For every
\delta
and for every
f=\sum_{\alpha}a_{\alpha}\underline{x}^{\alpha}
. Then we have that

\delta(f)=\frac{\Frob_{0}(f)-f^{p}(\underline{x})}{p}+\sum_{\alpha}a_{\alpha}^{p}\delta(\underline{x}^{p\alpha})+\sum_{\alpha}(\Frob(a_{\alpha})-a_{\alpha}^{p})\delta(\underline{x}^{\alpha}),

where
\Frob_{0}
is the unique extension of
\Frob
(as in item
1
of Lemma
4.4
) for
V
to
V[[\underline{x}]]
by setting
\Frob_{0}(x_{i})=x_{i}^{p}
for every
i
. In particular,
\delta_{\underline{0}}(f)=\frac{\Frob_{0}(f)-f^{p}(\underline{x})}{p}
for every
f
.

Proof.

By item 2 of Lemma
4.4
, the function
\delta\colon V[[\underline{x}]]\to V[[\underline{x}]]
is continuous (with respect to the
\mathfrak{m}_{V}-
adic topology), and so it is enough to prove this result for polynomials
f
. Therefore, if we write the polynomial
f
as
\sum_{\alpha}a_{\alpha}\underline{x}^{\alpha}
for
a_{\alpha}\in V
, then we have that

\begin{split}\delta(f)&=\sum_{\alpha}\delta(a_{\alpha}\underline{x}^{\alpha})+\frac{\sum_{\alpha}(a_{\alpha}\underline{x}^{\alpha})^{p}-f^{p}}{p}=\\
&=\sum_{\alpha}(\underline{x}^{p\alpha}\delta(a_{\alpha})+a_{\alpha}^{p}\delta(\underline{x}^{\alpha})+p\delta(a_{\alpha})\delta(\underline{x}^{\alpha}))+\frac{\sum_{\alpha}(a_{\alpha}\underline{x}^{\alpha})^{p}-f^{p}}{p}.\end{split}

Since
a_{\alpha}\in V
then we have that
\delta(a_{\alpha})=\frac{\Frob(a_{\alpha})-a_{\alpha}^{p}}{p}
, and so by plugging this into the previous equation, the result follows.
∎

We now recall the main result of
[
30
]
(in our case and using our notational convention) which we use in order to understanding and quantify isolated singularities using
p-
derivations.

Theorem 4.8

(Theorem 4.9. in
[
30
]
)
.

Let
f\in V[[\underline{x}]]
and let
f\in\mathfrak{q}\subset V[[\underline{x}]]
be a prime ideal.

1.

If
p\in\mathfrak{q}
then
\left(\frac{V[[\underline{x}]]}{\langle f\rangle}\right)_{\mathfrak{q}}
is regular if and only if
\mathfrak{q}
does not contain the ideal
\langle\left(\partial_{1}f\right)^{p},\dots,\left(\partial_{n}f\right)^{p},\delta\left(f\right)\rangle
for any
\delta
.

2.

If
p\notin\mathfrak{q}
then
\left(\frac{V[[\underline{x}]]}{\langle f\rangle}\right)_{\mathfrak{q}}
is regular if and only if
\mathfrak{q}
does not contain the ideal
\langle\partial_{1}f,\dots,\partial_{n}f\rangle
.

Remark 4.9
.

The second part Theorem
4.8
was first presented in
[
41
]
, and is in fact true for every mixed-characteristic complete DVR.

Corollary 4.10
.

Given some
f\in\mathfrak{m}_{V}
, we have that
\frac{V[[\underline{x}]]}{\langle f\rangle}
is regular if and only if the length of
\frac{V[[\underline{x}]]}{\langle f,\left(\partial_{1}f\right)^{p},\dots,\left(\partial_{n}f\right)^{p},\delta\left(f\right)\rangle}
over
V[[\underline{x}]]
is zero, for any
p-
derivation
\delta
.

Proof.

Since
V[[\underline{x}]]
is local then we have that
\frac{V[[\underline{x}]]}{\langle f\rangle}=\left(\frac{V[[\underline{x}]]}{\langle f\rangle}\right)_{\mathfrak{m}_{V}}
. Yet, since
f,p\in\mathfrak{m}_{V}
then by Theorem
4.8
,
\left(\frac{V[[\underline{x}]]}{\langle f\rangle}\right)_{\mathfrak{m}_{V}}
is regular if and only if for every
\delta
we have that
\langle\left(\partial_{1}f\right)^{p},\dots,\left(\partial_{n}f\right)^{p},\delta\left(f\right)\rangle\not\subset\mathfrak{m}_{V}
. But,
\langle\left(\partial_{1}f\right)^{p},\dots,\left(\partial_{n}f\right)^{p},\delta\left(f\right)\rangle\not\subset\mathfrak{m}_{V}
if and only if
\langle\left(\partial_{1}f\right)^{p},\dots,\left(\partial_{n}f\right)^{p},\delta\left(f\right)\rangle=V[[\underline{x}]]
, and since
0
is the only module that has length zero, the result follows.
∎

Inspired by Corollary
4.10
, we can look at
\frac{V[[\underline{x}]]}{\langle f,\left(\partial_{1}f\right)^{p},\dots,\left(\partial_{n}f\right)^{p},\delta\left(f\right)\rangle}
and at its length as another version of the Tjurina number (that depends on the choice of
p-
derivation
\delta
).

Definition 4.11
.

Let
f\in V[[\underline{x}]]
. Then for every
p-
derivation
\delta
we define the
Tjurina number of
f
with respect to
\delta
, denoted by
\tau(f,\delta)
, to be

\tau(f,\delta)=\frac{1}{p^{n}}\cdot\textup{length}_{V[[\underline{x}]]}\left(\frac{V[[\underline{x}]]}{\langle f\rangle+J_{\delta}\left(f\right)}\right),

where
J_{\delta}\left(f\right)=\langle\left(\partial_{1}f\right)^{p},\dots,\left(\partial_{n}f\right)^{p},\delta\left(f\right)\rangle
. In addition, we define the
Milnor number of
f
with respect to
\delta
to be

\mu(f,\delta)=\frac{1}{p^{n}}\cdot\textup{length}_{V[[\underline{x}]]}\left(\frac{V[[\underline{x}]]}{J_{\delta}\left(f\right)}\right).

Remark 4.12
.

The reason we divide by
p^{n}
in the definition of
\tau(\cdot,\delta)
and of
\mu(\cdot,\delta)
is because since we are raising the derivatives of
f
to the power of
p
, one would expect that length would be multiplied by
p
as well. As we see in the Example
4.24
at the end of this section, this gives us that the values need not be integers.

Example 4.13
.

The following example is inspired by Proposition
3.22
(and therefore we assume that the characteristic of
\kappa
is not
2
): Given so
p-
derivation
\delta
on
V[[\underline{x}]]
such that
\delta(x_{i})\in\mathfrak{m}_{V}
for every
i
, we show that

\mu(p^{2}+x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+x_{k}^{2}+g(x_{k+1},\dots,x_{n}),\delta)=\mu_{\kappa}(\overline{g}),

where
\overline{g}=g\,\operatorname{mod}\,p
and
\mu_{\kappa}(\overline{g})=\dim_{\kappa}\left(\frac{\kappa[[x_{k+1},\dots,x_{n}]]}{J(\overline{g})}\right)
is the Milnor number over
\kappa
. Denote
h=p^{2}+x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+x_{k}^{2}+g(x_{k+1},\dots,x_{n})
, then we have that

J_{\delta}(h)=\langle x_{1}^{p},\dots,x_{k}^{p},(\partial_{k+1}g)^{p},\dots,(\partial_{n}g)^{p},\delta(h)\rangle.

Now, since
p^{2}=1+\cdots+1
then
\delta(p^{2})=\frac{p^{2}-p^{2p}}{p}=p-p^{2p-1}
, and so we can conclude that

\begin{split}\delta(h)&=\delta(p^{2}+x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+x_{k}^{2}+g)=\\
&=\delta(p^{2})+\delta(x_{1}^{2})+\cdots+\delta(x_{k}^{2})+\delta(g)+\frac{p^{2p}+x_{1}^{2p}+\cdots+x_{k}^{2p}+g^{p}-h^{p}}{p}=\\
&=p\left(1-p^{2p-2}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\delta(x_{i})^{2}\right)+2\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}^{p}\delta(x_{i})\right)+\frac{p^{2p}+x_{1}^{2p}+\cdots+x_{k}^{2p}+g^{p}-h^{p}}{p}.\end{split}

Yet, we have that

\frac{p^{2p}+x_{1}^{2p}+\cdots+x_{k}^{2p}+g^{p}-h^{p}}{p}=-\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}\frac{\binom{p}{j}}{p}p^{2j}g^{p-j}\,\operatorname{mod}\,\langle x_{1}^{p},\dots,x_{k}^{p}\rangle.

As
\langle x_{1}^{p},\dots,x_{k}^{p}\rangle\subset J_{\delta}(f)
, we can conclude that the ideal
J_{\delta}(f)
is equal to

\left\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{k},(\partial_{k+1}g)^{p},\dots,(\partial_{n}g)^{p},p\left(1-p^{2p-2}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\delta(x_{i})^{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}\frac{\binom{p}{j}}{p}p^{2j-1}g^{p-j}\right)\right\rangle,

and since
(1-p^{2p-2}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\delta(x_{i})^{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}\frac{\binom{p}{j}}{p}p^{2j-1}g^{p-j})
is a unit, we have that
J_{\delta}(h)=\langle x_{1}^{p},\dots,x_{k}^{p},(\partial_{k+1}g)^{p},\dots,(\partial_{n}g)^{p},p\rangle
. Thus we get that

\mu(h,\delta)=\frac{1}{p^{n}}\dim_{\kappa}\left(\frac{\kappa[[\underline{x}]]}{\langle x_{1}^{p},\dots,x_{k}^{p},(\partial_{k+1}g)^{p},\dots,(\partial_{n}g)^{p}\rangle}\right).

Now, if
v_{1},\dots,v_{\mu}
is a basis for the
\kappa-
vector space
\frac{\kappa[[x_{k+1},\dots,x_{n}]]}{\langle\partial_{k+1}g,\dots,\partial_{n}g\rangle}
, then we can conclude that the set
\{x_{1}^{l_{1}}\dots x_{k}^{l_{k}}v_{j}^{l_{k+1}}\colon 1\leq j\leq\mu,0\leq l_{i}\leq p-1\}
is a basis for the
\kappa-
vector space
\frac{\kappa[[\underline{x}]]}{\langle x_{1}^{p},\dots,x_{k}^{p},(\partial_{k+1}g)^{p},\dots,(\partial_{n}g)^{p}\rangle}
, and so the result follows.

The following result is an analogue of Proposition
3.6
, and tells us that in fact
\tau(\cdot,\delta)
completely detects isolated singularities, but with an additional condition.

Proposition 4.14
.

Let
f\in\mathfrak{m}_{V}
. Then
f
defines an isolated singularity if and only if
p\in\sqrt{J\left(f\right)}
and for any
p-
derivation
\delta
we have that
\tau(f,\delta)<\infty
.

Proof.

Given some ideal
I\subset V[[\underline{x}]]
, we have that the length of
\frac{V[[\underline{x}]]}{I}
is finite if and only if there exists some
k
such that
\mathfrak{m}_{V}^{k}\cdot\left(\frac{V[[\underline{x}]]}{I}\right)=0
. This in turn is equivalent to having
\mathfrak{m}_{V}^{k}\subset I
, which itself is equivalent having that if
\mathfrak{p}
is a prime ideal such that
I\subset\mathfrak{p}
then
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{m}_{V}
.
Now, note that for every
\mathfrak{p}\neq\mathfrak{m}_{V}
we have that
\left(\frac{V[[\underline{x}]]}{\langle f\rangle}\right)_{\mathfrak{p}}=0
(which is regular) if and only if
f\notin\mathfrak{p}
. Therefore,
f
defines an isolated singularity if and only if for every prime ideal
f\in\mathfrak{p}\neq\mathfrak{m}_{V}
we have that
\left(\frac{V[[\underline{x}]]}{\langle f\rangle}\right)_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a regular local ring.

If
f
defines an isolated singularity, by Theorem
4.8
for every
\delta
and for every
\mathfrak{q}\neq\mathfrak{m}_{V}
we have that
J_{\delta}\left(f\right)+\langle f\rangle\not\subset\mathfrak{q}
if
p\in\mathfrak{q}
and
J\left(f\right)\not\subset\mathfrak{q}
if
p\notin\mathfrak{q}
, and so
\tau(f,\delta)<\infty
. In addition,
p\in\sqrt{J\left(f\right)}
since if
f
defines an isolated singularity, then in particular,
\left(\frac{V[[\underline{x}]]}{\langle f\rangle}\right)[\frac{1}{p}]
is regular, and so as its completion with respect to
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
, which is isomorphic to
\frac{L[[\underline{x}]]}{\langle f\rangle}
, where
L
is the fraction field of
V
. Since
L
is a field of characteristic zero, then by Theorem 5.1.7. of
[
11
]
together with Lemma 2.44 in
[
21
]
we must have that
1\in J\left(f\right)\otimes_{V}L[[\underline{x}]]
, and so
1\in J\left(f\right)[\frac{1}{p}]
.

For the other direction, assume that
p\in\sqrt{J\left(f\right)}
and that
\tau(f,\delta)<\infty
for every
\delta
. Then
J_{\delta}\left(f\right)+\langle f\rangle\not\subset\mathfrak{q}
for every
\mathfrak{q}\neq\mathfrak{m}_{V}
. So given
\mathfrak{q}\neq\mathfrak{m}_{V}
, if
p\notin\mathfrak{q}
then
J\left(f\right)\notin\mathfrak{q}
since
p\in\sqrt{J\left(f\right)}
. If
p\in\mathfrak{q}
then we have that
J_{\delta}\left(f\right)+\langle f\rangle\not\subset\mathfrak{q}
. Therefore by Theorem
4.8
we have that
\left(\frac{V[[\underline{x}]]}{\langle f\rangle}\right)_{\mathfrak{q}}
is regular for every
\mathfrak{q}\neq\mathfrak{m}_{V}
, and the result follows.
∎

Remark 4.15
.

An alternative proof of Proposition

Definition 4.16
.

Let
f\in V[[\underline{x}]]
define an isolated singularity. Then we denote by
\ord_{f}\left(p\right)
the smallest
N
such that
p^{N}\in\langle f,\left(\partial_{1}f\right)^{p},\dots,\left(\partial_{n}f\right)^{p}\rangle
.

Proposition 4.17
.

Let
f\in V[[\underline{x}]]
define an isolated singularity. Then we have that
\ord_{f}\left(p\right)\geq\ord\left(f\right)-1
.

Proof.

First, by Proposition
4.14
we have that
N=ord_{f}\left(p\right)
is finite and well defined. Now, denoting
r=\ord\left(f\right)
, then from Lemma
2.9
we can conclude that
\langle\left(\partial_{1}\left(f\right)\right)^{p},\dots,\left(\partial_{n}\left(f\right)\right)^{p}\rangle\subset\mathfrak{m}_{V}^{p\left(r-1\right)}\subset\mathfrak{m}_{V}^{r-1}
. Yet, since we have that

, then there exists some

such that

. Therefore, as

, we can conclude that
p^{N}\subset\mathfrak{m}_{V}^{r-1}+\langle f\rangle\subset\mathfrak{m}_{V}^{r-1}
, and the result follows since the order of

is
1
.
∎

Proposition 4.18
.

Let
f\in V[[\underline{x}]]
and let
\underline{a}\in(V[[\underline{x}]])^{\times n}
such that
\mu(f,\underline{a})<\infty
. Then there exists some
N
such that for every
\underline{b}\in(V[[\underline{x}]])^{\times n}
with
\underline{a}-\underline{b}\in(\mathfrak{m}_{V}^{N})^{\times n}=\mathfrak{m}_{V}^{N}\times\cdots\times\mathfrak{m}_{V}^{N}
we have that
\tau(f,\delta_{\underline{a}})\geq\tau(f,\delta_{\underline{b}})
and
\mu(f,\delta_{\underline{a}})\geq\mu(f,\delta_{\underline{b}})
.

Proof.

If
\mu(f,\underline{a})<\infty
, then there exists some
N
such that
\mathfrak{m}_{V}^{N}\subset J_{\delta_{\underline{a}}}(f)
. Therefore, given some
\underline{b}=(b_{1},\dots,b_{n})
such that
b_{i}\in\mathfrak{m}_{V}^{N}
for every
i
, then
\delta_{\underline{b}}(f)\in\mathfrak{m}_{V}^{N}
, and so
J_{\delta_{\underline{b}}}(f)\subset J_{\delta_{\underline{a}}}(f)
, and the result follows.
∎

Remark 4.19
.

Proposition

tells us that both

. This can be viewed as an analogue of Theorem 2.6 in

, of Lemma A.13 in

. But, by Remark 4.3 in

does not depend on

. Note that

Example 4.20
.

Assume that
f=pg
for some
g\in V[[\underline{x}]]
. Then
\tau^{\Delta}(f)=\ord(\overline{g})
if
n=1
and
\tau^{\Delta}(f)=\infty
otherwise, where
\overline{g}=g\,\operatorname{mod}\,p\in\kappa[[\underline{x}]]
. This is true since
J(f)=\langle p\rangle\cdot J(g)
and
\delta(pg)=p^{p}\delta(g)+g^{p}\delta(p)+p\delta(g)\delta(p)
. As

, and so

The following lemma is a general result about local rings, inspired by Theorem 1.1. in
[
37
]
, that we would like to use in order to bound
\tau(f,\delta)
.

Lemma 4.21
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a local ring, let
I
be an
\mathfrak{m}-
primary ideal, and assume
f\in R
satisfies
f^{N}\in I
. Then we have that

\length_{R}\left(\frac{R}{I}\right)\leq N\cdot\length_{R}\left(\frac{R}{I+\langle f\rangle}\right).

Proof.

Define
\varphi\colon\frac{R}{I}\to\frac{R}{I}
be the
R-
homomorphism defined by
\varphi(g+I)=fg+I
. Then,
\varphi
induces a long exact sequence

0\to\ker\left(\varphi\right)\to\frac{R}{I}\xrightarrow{\varphi}\frac{R}{I}\to\frac{R}{I+\langle f\rangle}\to 0,

and by applying length over
R
to this sequence we get
\textup{length}_{R}\left(\ker\left(\varphi\right)\right)=\textup{length}_{R}\left(\frac{R}{I+\langle f\rangle}\right)
. Since
f^{N}\in I
we can conclude that
\langle f^{N-1}\rangle\cdot M\subset\ker\left(\varphi\right)
, where
M=\frac{R}{I}
. Therefore, we have a sequence of inclusions

0=\langle f^{N}\rangle\cdot M\subset\langle f^{N-1}\rangle\cdot M\subset\cdots\subset\langle f\rangle\cdot M,

and so for every
i<N
we have a short exact sequence

0\to\ker\left(\varphi\right)\cap\langle f^{i}\rangle\cdot M\to\langle f^{i}\rangle\cdot M\xrightarrow{\varphi}\langle f^{i}\rangle\cdot M\to\frac{\langle f^{i}\rangle\cdot M}{\langle f^{i+1}\rangle\cdot M},

which gives us that
\textup{length}_{R}\left(\frac{\langle f^{i}\rangle\cdot M}{\langle f^{i+1}\rangle\cdot M}\right)=\textup{length}_{R}\left(\ker\left(\varphi\right)\cap\langle f^{i}\rangle\cdot M\right)
. But, since
\textup{length}_{V[[\underline{x}]]}\left(\ker\left(\varphi\right)\cap\langle f^{i}\rangle\cdot M\right)=\textup{length}_{R}\left(\ker\left(\varphi\right)\right)=\textup{length}_{R}\left(\frac{R}{I+\langle f\rangle}\right)
, we can conclude that

\textup{length}_{R}\left(\frac{\langle f^{i}\rangle\cdot M}{\langle f^{i+1}\rangle\cdot M}\right)\leq\textup{length}_{R}\left(\frac{R}{I+\langle f\rangle}\right).

Thus, we get that

\begin{split}\textup{length}_{R}\left(\frac{R}{I}\right)&=\textup{length}_{R}\left(M\right)=\\
&=\textup{length}_{R}\left(\frac{R}{I+\langle f\rangle}\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\textup{length}_{R}\left(\frac{\langle f^{i}\rangle\cdot M}{\langle f^{i+1}\rangle\cdot M}\right)\leq\\
&\leq N\cdot\textup{length}_{R}\left(\frac{R}{I+\langle f\rangle}\right),\end{split}

as desired.
∎

Remark 4.22
.

As in Theorem 1.1. of
[
37
]
, we have an equality in Lemma
4.21
if and only if
\ker\left(\varphi\right)=\langle f^{N-1}\rangle
. This is true since we have an equality if and only if for every
i
we have that
\ker\left(\varphi\right)\cap\langle f^{i}\rangle=\ker\left(\varphi\right)
, but
\langle f^{N-1}\rangle\subset\ker\left(\varphi\right)
.

Corollary 4.23
.

Let
f\in\mathfrak{m}_{V}
. Then:

1.

\tau^{\Delta}\left(f\right)\leq\tau(f,\delta)\leq ord_{f}\left(p\right)\cdot\tau^{\Delta}\left(f\right)
for every
p-
derivation
\delta
.

2.

f
defines an isolated singularity if and only if
p\in\sqrt{J\left(f\right)}
and
\tau^{\Delta}\left(f\right)<\infty
.

3.

If there exists some
p-
derivation
\delta
such that
\tau^{\Delta}\left(f\right)=\tau(f,\delta)
, then
f
must be of order at most
2
.

Proof.

The first part follows directly from Lemma
4.21
, and the second follows from the first together with Proposition
4.14
. For the third part, we have the following short exact sequence:

0\to\frac{J_{\delta}\left(f\right)+\langle p\rangle}{J_{\delta}\left(f\right)}\to\frac{V[[\underline{x}]]}{J_{\delta}\left(f\right)}\to\frac{V[[\underline{x}]]}{J_{\delta}\left(f\right)+\langle p\rangle}\to 0.

By looking at this short exact sequence, we have that
\tau^{\Delta}\left(f\right)=\tau(f,\delta)
holds if and only if
\frac{J_{\delta}\left(f\right)+\langle p\rangle}{J_{\delta}\left(f\right)}=0
, which happens if and only if
p\in J_{\delta}\left(f\right)
. Yet, if
\ord\left(f\right)=r
then by Lemma
2.9
and Lemma
4.4
we have that
J_{\delta}\left(f\right)\subset\mathfrak{m}_{V}^{r-1}
, and
p\in\mathfrak{m}_{V}^{r-1}
if and only if
r-1\leq 1
.
∎

There are a number of problems with looking at
p-
derivations to numerically detect isolated singularities:

•

The value of
\tau(\cdot,\delta)
may (or may not) vary when setting different values for
\delta
.

•

Both
\tau(\cdot,\delta)
and
\tau^{\Delta}
are not preserved under
\sim
.

•

Unlike
\tau(\cdot,\delta)
, the finiteness of
\mu(f,\delta)
depends on the choice of
\delta
.

We see these problems in the following example:

Example 4.24
.

Assume the characteristic of
\kappa
is not
2
. Note that,
x^{2}+p^{2}\sim px
in
V[[x]]
as
x^{2}+y^{2}=(x+iy)(x-iy)
in
V[[\underline{x},y]]
(where
i^{2}=-1
, which exists since
\kappa
is algebraically closed of characteristic different than
2
and
V
is complete - and therefore Henselian). For every
\delta
we have that
J_{\delta}\left(px\right)=\langle p,x^{p}\rangle
and so
\tau(px,\delta)=1=\tau^{\Delta}\left(px\right)
, but we also have that

J_{\delta}\left(x^{2}+p^{2}\right)=\langle x^{2}+p^{2},x^{p},p(-1+p^{2p-2}+\delta\left(x\right)^{2})+C_{p}\left(x^{2},p^{2}\right)\rangle.

Yet, since
C_{p}\left(x^{2},p^{2}\right)\in\langle p^{2}x^{2}\rangle
, then
C_{p}\left(x^{2},p^{2}\right)=x^{2}p^{2}g
for some
g\in V[[x]]
. Thus

J_{\delta}\left(x^{2}+p^{2}\right)=\langle x^{2}+p^{2},x^{p},p(-1+p^{2p-2}+px^{2}g+\delta\left(x\right)^{2})\rangle,

and we can conclude that
\tau^{\Delta}\left(x^{2}+p^{2}\right)=\frac{2}{p}
. Therefore, by setting
\delta_{N}\left(x\right)=\left(p^{N-1}+1-p^{2p-2}-px^{2}g\right)^{0.5}
for any
N<\ord_{f}\left(p\right)
(this square root exists since
p^{N-1}+1-p^{2p-2}-px^{2}g
is a unit), we have that
J_{\delta_{N}}\left(x^{2}+p^{2}\right)=\langle x^{2}+p^{2},x^{p},p^{N}\rangle
, giving different values for
\tau(f,\delta)
. In addition, for every
\delta\left(x\right)\in\mathfrak{m}_{V}
we have that
\mu(f,\delta)=1
(which is clearly finite). But for
\delta\left(x\right)=\left(-1+p^{2p-2}-px^{2}g+x\right)^{0.5}
we have that
J_{\delta}\left(f\right)=\langle x^{p},px\rangle
, and so
\frac{V[[x]]}{J_{\delta}\left(f\right)}
is one dimensional and therefore must have infinite length.

We end this section with an analogue of Proposition
4.14
over a ramified DVR, i.e., a complete DVR
(V,\langle\pi\rangle,\kappa)
of mixed characteristic
(0,p)
such that
\kappa
is algebraically closed and
p\in\langle\pi^{2}\rangle
, based upon the work of KC in
[
33
]
. Unlike the unramified case, the ramified case is much easier and can be directly compared with
\tau_{V}
. In particular, we have a special derivation that allows us to "derive with respect to
\pi
" directly.

We recall the main results in
[
33
]
that are relevent for our discussion (specifically, Corollary 2.4 and Corollary 2.13). Note that in their paper, all results are over the ring of polynomials, but the results are valid over the ring of power series, as it is the completion of the ring of power series. It is a ramified analogue of Theorem
4.8
.

Proposition 4.25
.

1.

There exists a
\mathbb{Z}-
derivation
\frac{\partial}{\partial\pi}\colon V[[\underline{x}]]\to\kappa[[\underline{x}]]
such that
\frac{\partial}{\partial\pi}(\pi)=1
.

2.

Given some
f\in V[[\underline{x}]]
and some prime ideal
\pi\in\mathfrak{q}\subset V[[\underline{x}]]
, then
\left(\frac{V[[\underline{x}]]}{\langle f\rangle}\right)_{\mathfrak{q}}
is regular if and only if
\langle\partial_{1}(\overline{f}),\dots,\partial_{n}(\overline{f}),\frac{\partial}{\partial\pi}(f)\rangle\not\subset\overline{\mathfrak{q}}
, where
\overline{\mathfrak{q}}=\mathfrak{q}\,\operatorname{mod}\,\pi\subset\kappa[[\underline{x}]]
and
\overline{f}=f\,\operatorname{mod}\,\pi\in\kappa[[\underline{x}]]
.

Inspired by Proposition
4.25
, we can define an invariant that has an analogous result to that of Proposition
4.14
.

Definition 4.26
.

Let
f\in\mathfrak{m}_{V}
. Then we define the
ramified Tjurina number of
f
to be

\tau^{\pi}(f)=\dim_{\kappa}\left(\frac{\kappa[[\underline{x}]]}{\langle\overline{f}\rangle+J_{\pi}(f)}\right),

where
J_{\pi}(f)=\langle\partial_{1}(\overline{f}),\dots,\partial_{n}(\overline{f}),\frac{\partial}{\partial\pi}(f)\rangle
.

Therefore we can conclude the following Proposition, whose proof is the same as that of Proposition
4.14
:

Proposition 4.27
.

Let
f\in\mathfrak{m}_{V}
. Then:

1.

\frac{V[[\underline{x}]]}{\langle f\rangle}
is regular if and only if
\tau^{\pi}(f)=0

2.

f
defines an isolated singularities if and only if
\pi\in\sqrt{J\left(f\right)}
and
\tau^{\pi}\left(f\right)<\infty
.

Proposition 4.28
.

Given some
f\in\mathfrak{m}_{V}
. Then
\tau^{\pi}(f)\geq\tau_{V}(f)
.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathfrak{m}_{V}
and let
\tilde{f}
be as in Notation
3.1
. Then we define
\partial_{\pi}(f)=\text{pr}(\partial_{y}(\tilde{f}))\in V[[\underline{x}]]
, where
pr
is as in Remark
3.2
. More explicitly, if
f=\sum_{\alpha}u_{\alpha}\pi^{n^{\alpha}}\underline{x}^{\alpha}
as in Notation
3.1
then
\partial_{\pi}(f)=\sum_{\alpha}u_{\alpha}n_{\alpha}\pi^{n_{\alpha}-1}\underline{x}^{\alpha}
. Since
\partial_{\pi}(f)\,\operatorname{mod}\,\pi=\frac{\partial}{\partial\pi}(f)
, we can conclude that
\tau^{\pi}(f)=\length_{V[[\underline{x}]]}\left(\frac{V[[\underline{x}]]}{J(f)+\langle\pi,f,\partial_{\pi}(f)\rangle}\right)
. Thus, the result follows from the third item of Proposition
3.3
.
∎

Remark 4.29
.

1.

The proof of Proposition
4.28
shows us that we can view
\tau^{\pi}
in fact as a ramified analogue of
\tau^{\Delta}
, since they both can be thought of as the co-length of
\langle f\rangle+J(f)
plus the uniformizer and the respective "derivative with respect to the uniformizer".

2.

Note that Proposition
4.28
gives us that if
f
defines an isolated singularity then
\tau_{V}(f)<\infty
. This can be though of a generalization of Proposition
3.6
in the ramified case.

3.

As in Corollary
4.23
, we can use Lemma
4.21
and Proposition
4.27
to show that if
f
defines an isolated singularity then
\tau^{\pi}(f)
is bounded above by
\ord_{f}(\pi)\cdot\length_{V[[\underline{x}]]}\left(\frac{V[[\underline{x}]]}{J(f)+\langle f,\partial_{\pi}(f)\rangle}\right)
, where
\ord_{f}(\pi)
is the smallest
N
such that
\pi^{N}\in J(f)+\langle f\rangle
. This allows us to "measure" when
f
defines an isolated singularity by preforming a computation over
V[[\underline{x}]]
, which is analogous to the classic Tjurina number over a field.

Yet, like
\tau(\cdot,\delta)
and
\tau^{\Delta}
(as in Example
4.24
,), the invariant
\tau^{\pi}
is not preserved under
\sim
:

Example 4.30
.

Let
f_{1}=x^{2}+\pi^{p}
and
f_{2}=x^{p}+\pi^{2}
. Observe that
f_{1}\sim f_{2}
. Yet we have that
\overline{f_{1}}=x^{2}
and
\overline{f_{2}}=x^{p}
and so
\frac{\partial}{\partial\pi}(f_{1})=\frac{\partial}{\partial\pi}(f_{2})=0
. Therefore
\tau^{\pi}(f_{1})=\dim_{\kappa}\left(\frac{\kappa[[x]]}{\langle x\rangle}\right)=1
and
\tau^{\pi}(f_{2})=\dim_{\kappa}\left(\frac{\kappa[[x]]}{\langle x^{p}\rangle}\right)=p
.

Data-driven path-following control for unmanned surface vehicles
Authors: Je Wook Ryu, Seunghwan Han, Dowan Kim
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This paper develops a formulation of linear matrix inequalities (LMIs) for data-driven path following control of an unmanned surface vehicle (USV) subject to partially unknown dynamics. The proposed formulation is to decompose the whole system into a fully known guidance-error subsystem and sway yaw dynamics with the partially unknown components, rather than treating it as unknown. These unknown components are constrained to a data-consistent, matrix-ellipsoidal uncertainty set inferred from input state measurements, thereby avoiding explicit parameter identification. Sufficient conditions in the form of LMIs are derived to guarantee robust asymptotic stability of the associated closed-loop system under a matrix-ellipsoidal uncertainty set. An example is provided to examine that the proposed data-driven controller achieves the path-following performance comparable to that of a fully model-based controller across diverse reference paths.

Introduction

Path-following control of unmanned surface vehicles (USVs) refers to regulating the position and heading so that they converge to a prescribed geometric path without explicit time constraints1. A broad range of control strategies has been developed for this purpose, including adaptive, backstepping, fuzzy, and model predictive controls, most of which belong to the class of model-based approaches2–9. Moreover, advanced control methods extending model-based approaches, such as T S fuzzy model-based relaxed model predictive control10 and event-triggered adaptive global prescribed performance control11, have been proposed, thereby broadening the design space. An exact characterization of the underlying dynamics is, however, required by these methods, which is rarely attainable in practice 12. The hydrodynamic coefficients, associated with rigid-body dynamics, vary with operating conditions. Consequently, establishing a fixed parametric model of USV motion is, in general, infeasible.

Data-driven control (DDC) has been increasingly established as a rigorous methodology for addressing modeling challenges, since controllers are synthesized directly from measured input output data without explicit parametric identification. Two main approaches to DDC are reinforcement learning (RL) and iterative learning control (ILC), which optimizes control policies through iterative interactions with the environment, proving effective in representing complex nonlinear dynamics, thereby allowing direct application to path-following control problems in USVs by using predictive modeling13,14. In previous work15,16, it has been shown that iterative learning control improves the tracking accuracy of USVs through iterative task execution. In addition to these approaches, a data-driven model predictive control that uses a dynamic fuzzy neural network learned from measured data has reported for path-following of USV17. Nevertheless, in these approaches, control performance cannot usually be guaranteed on untrained paths, and Lyapunov-based stability guarantees remain elusive18–21.

In addition to learning-based approaches, linear matrix inequality approaches for DDC12,22,23 provide a convex framework for stability analysis. The approaches build on Willems’ fundamental lemma24 on a theoretical basis. The lemma establishes that the behavior of a linear time-invariant system can be characterized by a single persistently exciting input-output sequence. Based on this result24, De Persis and Tesi22 proposed LMI formulas that, with sufficiently rich data, guarantee stability, robustness, and optimality. Bisoffi et al.23 presented robust a synthesis for linear and polynomial systems, incorporating into the LMI conditions via Petersen’s lemma25,26 an ellipsoidal uncertainty set constructed from experimentally measured input-state data. Applying this result to a USV, Li et al.12 formulated a semidefinite programming problem for the tracking problem under simplified dynamics.

Even with theoretical bases of LMI-based DDC12,22,23, inherent obstacles remain in the application to the path-following control of USVs. Its control system can be viewed as a cascade connection of the guidance subsystem and the rigid-body one. The guidance part is governed solely by path geometry and USV pose, and hence it is straightforward to model. By contrast, the rigid-body part is not easy to model due to parametric uncertainties and hydrodynamic effects. If these properties are ignored, the controller design becomes purely data-driven and unstructured, causing even the known parts of the system to be handled as uncertain. Also, for uniform path-following performance across admissible paths, the design is required to be independent of the prescribed reference path. Such a requirement is not readily achievable within the standard formulations of DDC.

This paper aims to develop an LMI-based DDC for the path-following problem of USVs under partially unknown dynamics. This is achieved by extending the previous work23 from stabilization to path-following control. This extension is nontrivial. The previous work23 regarded the entire system as unknown and constructed a matrix ellipsoidal uncertainty set from input-state data. Instead, through separating known and unknown structures from mathematical models of USV, our design characterizes only the unknown part from the input-state data. By augmenting the guidance errors with the sway-yaw dynamics, we then obtain sufficient LMI conditions that guarantee robust asymptotic stabilization of the augmented system under a data-consistent, matrix ellipsoidal uncertainty set.

Conclusions

We addressed the path following control problem of underactuated USVs with partially known sway yaw dynamics. The guidance error subsystem was modeled exactly, whereas the unmodeled dynamics were restricted to a data consistent, matrix ellipsoidal uncertainty set from input state data. Within this framework, LMI condition was derived such that a static state feedback K robustly asymptotically stabilizes the augmented error system on a prescribed forward invariant set \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathscr {D}}$$\end{document} for all \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Delta \in {\mathscr {E}}$$\end{document}. Under the dwell time and bounded reset assumptions (A5) (A6) of Assumption 1, Lyapunov decrease is preserved across segment transitions, which guarantees \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$e(t)\rightarrow 0$$\end{document} along admissible waypoint paths. Simulations on CyberShip II demonstrate the proposed controller achieves path following accuracy comparable to that of a fully model based linear design in both the partially and fully unknown cases, and it preserves stability. These results indicate path independent synthesis and robustness to modeling deficits confined by data.

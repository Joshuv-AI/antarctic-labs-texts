Incidence and Risk Factors for Progression of Pachychoroid Subtype
Authors: Yu Jeat Chong, Angie Hon Chi Fong, Wendy Mei Hua Wong, Noa Gilead, Kelvin Yi Chong Teo, Chui Ming Gemmy Cheung
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Key PointsQuestionWhat is the incidence of and what are the risk factors for worsening pachychoroid subtype?FindingsThis multicenter cohort study of 205 eyes with pachychoroid subtype showed progression in few eyes over 2 years, with little change in mean best-corrected visual acuity or subfoveal choroidal thickness. Incident neovascularization developed in a small proportion of eyes and exclusively in those with shallow, irregular retinal pigment epithelium elevation at baseline.MeaningWithin a relatively small cohort, findings suggest eyes with pachychoroid subtype are typically stable over 2 years, although a shallow, irregular retinal pigment epithelium elevation may help identify eyes at a higher risk of neovascular complications.

Introduction

The term pachychoroid was originally introduced to describe the presence of thickened choroid in eyes with central serous chorioretinopathy (CSCR), as well as in eyes without subretinal fluid that display retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) alterations; both conditions were believed to stem from choroidal abnormalities resembling those observed in CSCR.1 Since not all eyes with pachychoroid exhibit pathological changes or fundus abnormalities, the concept of pachychoroid disease spectrum was later introduced. Pachychoroid pigment epitheliopathy (PPE), CSCR, peripapillary pachychoroid syndrome, pachychoroid neovasculopathy (PNV), and polypoidal choroidal vasculopathy (PCV) all fall within this spectrum.2 The initial descriptions of choroidal features underlying pachychoroid subtype include dilated Haller layer vessels, alongside an increase in choroidal thickness (CT).1,3 However, subsequent studies have demonstrated substantial overlap in CT distributions between healthy and diseased eyes, and no universally accepted threshold currently exists to define abnormal CT.4,5,6,7

The “multi-hit theory” has been proposed to explain the pathogenesis of pachychoroid subtype, describing the following sequential stages: anatomical predisposition, inciting event, compensatory mechanisms, saturation, decompensation, and a vicious cycle that ultimately leads to visual loss.2

Previously, we published a multicenter, prospective, 1-year follow-up study in eyes with subfoveal CT of 300 µm or greater with evaluations at baseline and at 12 months.8 In this current study, we extend this cohort to 2 years of follow-up, examining risk factors for disease progression, diagnostic transitions within the pachychoroid subtype, best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) and subfoveal CT (SFCT) outcomes, and treatment needs. Based on the “multi-hit theory” of pachychoroid disease and our initial findings, it was hypothesized that a subset of eyes would continue to demonstrate stepwise progression over time and that certain baseline imaging features would be associated with increased risk of neovascularization.

Methods

Study Design

This 104-week, prospective, multicenter, longitudinal, observational study of a cohort of participants with the pachychoroid subtype phenotype was conducted across 3 tertiary eye hospitals (Singapore National Eye Centre, National University Hospital, and Hong Kong Eye Hospital) between July 2020 and November 2023 (NCT04498000). The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. All participants provided written informed consent prior to participating in this study. The study protocol received approval from the institutional review boards or independent ethics committees of each of the participating centers and followed the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) reporting guidelines.

Participants and Exposure

Participants received a small stipend to cover their travel costs. Participants were 45 years old or older, with an SFCT of 300 µm or greater measured beneath the foveal center point. Detailed methods and 1-year outcomes have been previously published.8 In brief, clinical data including age, sex, medical comorbidities, and smoking status were recorded. Participants followed a standardized protocol with visual acuity assessments at baseline and at months 6, 12, 18, and 24. BCVA was defined as habitual spectacle correction supplemented by pinhole and was measured using the Early Treatment Diabetic Retinopathy Study (ETDRS) chart at the Singapore National Eye Centre (n = 89 eyes) and using the Snellen chart at the other 2 centers (National University Hospital: n = 67 eyes; Hong Kong Eye Hospital: n = 49 eyes). Snellen acuities were converted to logMAR values for analysis using the following formula: logMAR = log10(Snellen denominator/Snellen numerator) − (0.02 × letters gained), where letters gained is positive for letters correctly read on the next smaller line and negative for letters missed on the line achieved.9 The treatment protocol of anti–vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) therapy is provided in eMethods 1 in Supplement 1, image grading features are summarized in eMethods 2 in Supplement 1, and the participant flow diagram is summarized in eMethods 3 in Supplement 1.

Multimodal imaging was performed for all eyes, including macular spectral-domain optical coherence tomography (SD-OCT) with the Spectralis (Heidelberg Engineering) to acquire a 6 × 6-mm cube comprising 91 B-scans centered on the fovea, OCT angiography (OCTA) with the Triton Deep Range Imaging OCT (Topcon Corporation) to acquire a 6 × 6-mm cube at 512 × 512 resolution, optical biometry for axial length measurements using the IOL Master 700 (Carl Zeiss Meditec AG), and fundus autofluorescence (FAF) and color fundus photographs (CFP) acquired with the Spectralis to obtain 90° images centered on the fovea. Each imaging modality was analyzed independently by 2 graders, with any disagreements resolved by arbitration from another study author (either K.Y.C.T. or C.M.G.C.).

Definition of Pachychoroid Subtypes

At baseline, each eye was categorized as either uncomplicated pachychoroid (UP) or pachychoroid disease. Pachychoroid disease was further classified into 1 of the following subtypes: PPE, CSCR, or PNV.

Eyes with choroidal thickness meeting the inclusion criteria but lacking RPE disturbance or retinal fluid were classified as UP. Those showing RPE disturbance on OCT or FAF without retinal fluid were classified as PPE. Eyes with RPE alterations and evidence of active or resolved subretinal fluid, intraretinal fluid, or pigment epithelial detachment were classified as CSCR. Finally, eyes with neovascularization detected on OCTA or indocyanine green angiography, with or without fluid, were classified as PNV. Further detailed definitions based on multimodal imaging have been previously described.10 Eyes with PCV at baseline were not included, as the primary aim was to characterize progression to a more severe pachychoroid subtype, and PCV represents the most advanced end of the spectrum, with no further subtype available for progression. Examples of pachychoroid subtypes are illustrated in eFigure 1 in Supplement 1.

Outcome

The primary outcome was diagnostic progression within the pachychoroid subtype over the 24-month follow-up period. Diagnostic progression was defined as a change from baseline pachychoroid subtype to a different pachychoroid subtype category at any follow-up visit. An example of progression is illustrated in eFigure 2 in Supplement 1. Secondary outcomes included incident neovascularization; changes in BCVA, SFCT, and CST; and treatment initiations during follow-up.

Statistical Analysis

Descriptive data were presented as mean (SD) for normally distributed continuous variables, median (IQR) for non-normal distributions, and counts (percentages) for categorical variables. Intereye correlation, arising from inclusion of both eyes of some participants, was accounted for throughout the analysis. Linear mixed-effects models with a random intercept for participant were used for all continuous outcome comparisons, including baseline to 24-month changes in BCVA, SFCT, and CST and between-group comparisons across diagnostic categories; model-based estimates are reported as estimated mean differences with 95% confidence intervals on the natural scale of the outcome. Logistic generalized estimating equations with an exchangeable working correlation structure and robust standard errors were used to estimate proportions and corresponding 95% confidence intervals. Categorical variables were compared using χ2 tests; Fisher exact tests were applied when more than 20% of contingency table cells had expected counts less than 5.

Risk factors for diagnostic progression and neovascularization progression were evaluated using multivariable Poisson regression to estimate risk ratios (RRs) with 95% confidence intervals. Cluster-robust standard errors were used to account for intereye correlation from the same participant. All P values were 2-sided and not adjusted for multiple analyses. P < .05 for the primary outcome was considered statistically significant. R version 4.3.3 (R Foundation) was used for analyses.

Results

A total of 239 eyes (143 participants) were enrolled; 223 eyes (135 participants) completed the 1-year follow-up, with 205 eyes (125 participants) completing the 2-year follow-up. For participants who completed the 2-year follow-up, mean (SD) age was 59.0 (8.8) years, and 32 participants (25.6%) were female.

Only participants and eyes that completed the year 2 follow-up were included in the analysis. The most common reason for failure to complete follow-up was due to participants declining to further continue with the study. One participant died due to a cardiac arrest, which was deemed to be a nonocular event unrelated to the study. One eye was excluded due to an epiretinal membrane that developed into a full-thickness macular hole at year 2; another eye was excluded to branch retinal vein occlusion.

The distribution of diagnosis for eyes at baseline was 39 eyes (19.0%) with UP, 102 eyes (49.8%) with PPE, 47 eyes (22.9%) with CSCR, and 17 eyes (8.3%) with PNV. Of the 17 eyes with PNV, 7 (41.2%) were exudative. No eye that was nonexudative at baseline developed exudation.

The median (IQR) baseline BCVA for each diagnosis was 0.06 (0.00-0.12) logMAR (Snellen equivalent, 20/25) for UP, 0.10 (0.00-0.14) logMAR (20/25) for PPE, 0.20 (0.06-0.29) logMAR (20/32) for CSCR, and 0.16 (0.10-0.24) logMAR (20/32) for PNV. Baseline BCVA differed across diagnostic groups (P < .001). Bonferroni-adjusted pairwise comparisons demonstrated worse baseline vision in CSCR and PNV compared with UP and PPE, while no difference was observed between UP and PPE or between CSCR and PNV.

The median (IQR) baseline SFCT for each diagnosis was 385 (364-434) µm for UP, 412 (374-484) µm for PPE, 409 (364-454) µm for CSCR, and 427 (372-523) µm for PNV. Baseline SFCT did not differ across diagnostic groups (P = .24). These findings are summarized in eTable 1 in Supplement 1.

Across all eyes, mean (SD) BCVA changed from 0.13 (0.18) logMAR (approximate Snellen equivalent, 20/25) at baseline to 0.11 (0.24) logMAR (approximate Snellen equivalent, 20/25) at year 2 (difference, −0.02 logMAR [approximately 1 letter improvement]; 95% CI, −0.05 to 0.02 logMAR; P = .33), and mean (SD) SFCT changed from 419 (83) µm to 424 (90) µm (difference, 6 µm; 95% CI, −5 to 16; P = .32). Mean (SD) CST decreased from 248 (41) µm at baseline to 238 (44) µm at year 2 (difference, −10 µm; 95% CI, −17 to −4; P = .002).

Progression and Change in Diagnosis

In terms of change in pachychoroid subtype diagnosis, among the 205 eyes that completed 2-year follow-up, 12 eyes (5.9%) had already shown progression at year 1 compared with baseline. One of these eyes (initially PPE, then CSCR at year 1) progressed further to PNV at year 2. Three additional eyes developed a change in diagnosis between year 1 and year 2 (2 from PPE to CSCR, 1 from PPE to PNV), bringing the 2-year cumulative progression to 15 of 205 eyes (7.3%; 95% CI, 4.5%-11.6%). Overall, incident neovascularization was observed in 7 eyes (3.4%; 95% CI, 1.7%-6.9%): 5 eyes that progressed to PNV during year 1 and remained as PNV at year 2, 1 additional eye that progressed directly from PPE to PNV during year 2, and 1 eye that progressed from PPE to CSCR at year 1 and then to PNV at year 2. Of the 15 participants with a progressor eye, 12 were enrolled bilaterally and 3 unilaterally; in every case of bilateral enrollment, progression was unilateral, with the contralateral eye remaining at the same diagnostic category between baseline and year 2 (eTable 2 in Supplement 1).

Most eyes (190 of 205 [92.7%]) had no change in diagnosis at year 2 compared to baseline. eTable 2 in Supplement 1 summarizes the characteristics of all 15 eyes that developed a change in diagnosis at year 2 compared to baseline. For these eyes, there was no difference in BCVA, SFCT, or CST between baseline and year 2 (mean [SD] baseline BCVA: 0.10 [0.15] logMAR vs year 2: 0.12 [0.12] logMAR; P = .59; mean [SD] baseline SFCT: 428.2 [85.0] µm vs year 2: 431.2 [79.0] µm; P = .89; mean [SD] CST at baseline: 236.5 [36.4] µm vs year 2: 244.8 [48.9] µm; P = .49).

Treatment Need

By year 2, neovascularization developed in 7 eyes, which was detected on OCTA. Of the participants who developed PNV, 3 of 7 (42.9%) required treatment with anti-VEGF injections: 1 progressed to PNV from CSCR (requiring a total of 8 anti-VEGF injections: 3 bevacizumab, 5 ranibizumab), and 2 progressed from PPE to PNV (1 requiring 4 aflibercept injections, 2 mg, and the other requiring 7 ranibizumab injections). Four of 7 eyes with PNV (57.1%) were managed by observation without the need for anti-VEGF treatment.

Additionally, 1 eye that progressed from PPE to CSCR and another that progressed from PPE to PNV required treatment with half-fluence photodynamic therapy.

Factors Associated With Progression

Risk factors associated with a change in year 2 diagnosis are summarized in Table 1 (for eyes with progression or change in diagnosis) and Table 2 (for eyes that developed neovascularization). Multivariable analysis showed female patients were more likely to develop progression (13.5%; 95% CI, 5.6%-25.8% vs 5.2%; 95% CI, 2.3%-10.0%; P = .02) and for developing neovascularization (7.7%; 95% CI, 2.1%-18.5% vs 2.0%; 95% CI, 0.4%-5.6%; P = .02). In addition, PNV developed in 6.0% of eyes with shallow, irregular RPE elevation at baseline, while none of the eyes without shallow, irregular RPE elevation at baseline developed PNV.

Discussion

In this study, we present the year 2 follow-up results of a multicenter, prospective, longitudinal cohort study of eyes with pachychoroid subtype. This report expands on our earlier year 1 analysis of this cohort, comprising 223 eyes from 133 participants, in which diagnostic progression occurred in 6.3% of eyes and incident neovascularization in 3.6%; factors associated with neovascularization included older age, smoking, and the presence of shallow, irregular RPE elevation.8 Extending follow-up to 2 years, only 3 additional eyes demonstrated diagnostic change beyond the first year, bringing the cumulative 2-year progression rate to 7.3%. Mean BCVA and SFCT showed little change over the 2-year period, consistent with the year 1 observation that most eyes with pachychoroid subtype remained clinically stable in the short to medium term, with most progression events occurring within the first year of observation. Compared with the 12 eyes that had progressed by year 1, these 3 delayed progressors showed greater changes in SFCT between baseline and year 2 (median change: +59 µm vs −6 µm). This descriptive comparison should be interpreted with caution given the small number of late-progression events.

Among the findings, most eyes remained the same subtype from baseline to 2 years, with a low incidence of progression to a worse subtype. Even when a change in diagnosis occurred, BCVA and anatomical parameters remained relatively stable. The point estimate of incident neovascularization was low (3.4%; 95% CI, 1.7%-6.9%); however, the confidence interval was wide, reflecting the small number of events. Of note, shallow, irregular RPE elevation at baseline was present in all 7 of the eyes that developed neovascularization, and none of the eyes that did not develop neovascularization had this feature at baseline.

With the routine use of OCT in most clinics, patients with thick choroid with and without pathology may be readily identified. However, the optimal management of these cases remains unclear. While there is an appreciation of the increased risk of associated pathologies like PPE, CSCR, or even PNV, the magnitude of the risk and associated factors are poorly understood.

Several findings from this study may help refine risk stratification and optimize allocation of clinical resources, although the relatively small number of participants and relatively low incidence noted with relatively wide confidence intervals limit any definitive interpretation from these findings. Notably, none of the eyes with UP progressed to CSCR or PNV over the 2-year follow-up. Only 1 of 39 eyes (2.6%) with UP at baseline progressed to PPE, and this occurred within the first year. In contrast, all 7 eyes that developed PNV had shallow, irregular RPE elevation at baseline, whereas no eye without baseline shallow, irregular RPE elevation developed neovascularization. These findings suggest that prolonged monitoring may be unnecessary in eyes without shallow, irregular RPE elevation, if confirmed in subsequent studies, ideally with larger cohorts. Conversely, eyes with shallow, irregular RPE elevation, particularly those with a baseline diagnosis of PPE, may warrant closer surveillance and a lower threshold for multimodal imaging, including OCTA. Shallow, irregular RPE elevation has been consistently associated with neovascularization detected on OCTA, demonstrating high sensitivity and specificity.11 Our findings further support evaluation of shallow, irregular RPE elevation at baseline in eyes with pachychoroid subtype as a marker of risk for subsequent neovascularization. However, shallow, irregular RPE elevation has been reported to harbor quiescent or subclinical neovascular networks in the age-related macular degeneration literature,12 and some of the incident neovascularization observed in this cohort may therefore represent activation of preexisting subclinical networks rather than truly de novo neovascularization.

While the pathophysiological mechanism of pachychoroid subtype remains to be fully understood, our observation supports the “multi-hit theory,” in which eyes with pachychoroid subtype may progress through a sequence of events involving anatomical predisposition and subsequent inciting factors rather than undergoing abrupt phenotypic transitions.2,13

In multivariable analysis, female sex was associated with a higher risk of progression compared with male sex. However, our study cohort demonstrated a male predominance, with 153 of 205 eyes (74.6%) belonging to male participants, consistent with prior reports showing a higher prevalence of pachychoroid subtype in males.

We also found female sex carried a higher risk of progression compared to males (13.5% vs 5.2%) in our multivariable analysis. However, this study’s cohort demonstrated a male predominance, with 153 of 205 eyes (74.6%) being from male participants, in keeping with previous studies.14,15,16 Given the small number of progression events and the wide confidence intervals, this observed association between female sex and progression should be interpreted with caution.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, the number of diagnostic progression events was small, limiting statistical power and resulting in wide confidence intervals for several risk estimates. Second, progression occurring beyond the 2-year follow-up period cannot be excluded. Third, the inclusion criteria may limit generalizability. The minimum age of 45 years was selected to enrich for individuals more likely to experience progression; however, this excluded younger patients, particularly those with CSCR, and therefore the findings may not be generalizable to younger populations. In addition, the minimum SFCT threshold of 300 µm was chosen as an operational cutoff, as no universally accepted definition distinguishes normal from pathologically thick choroid. SFCT measurements can be influenced by factors like age, axial length, and diurnal variation, which may introduce additional variability.17,18 Finally, BCVA testing methodology differed across centers, with 1 center using the ETDRS chart and 2 using Snellen, which may have introduced minor variability in BCVA findings.

Conclusions

This cohort study demonstrates that eyes with pachychoroid subtype are largely stable over a 2-year period, with a low incidence of diagnostic progression and preservation of visual and anatomical outcomes. Most progression events occurred within the first year of follow-up, while late diagnostic change was uncommon. Neovascularization was rare and occurred exclusively in eyes with shallow, irregular RPE elevation at baseline, highlighting the importance of this imaging feature in risk stratification. Taken together, within a relatively small cohort, findings suggest eyes with pachychoroid subtype typically are stable over 2 years, although a shallow, irregular RPE elevation may help identify eyes at higher risk for neovascular complications. Ideally, larger cohorts are needed to expand on these findings.

Characterization of Generalized Alpha-Beta Divergence and Associated Entropy Measures
Subhrajyoty Roy, Supratik Basu, Abhik Ghosh, Ayanendranath Basu

Abstract
Minimum divergence estimators provide a natural choice of estimators in a statistical inference problem. Different properties of various families of these divergence measures such as Hellinger distance, power divergence, density power divergence, logarithmic density power divergence, etc. have been established in literature. In this work, we propose a new class of divergence measures called “generalized alpha-beta divergence”, which is a superfamily of these popular divergence families. We provide the necessary and sufficient conditions for the validity of the proposed generalized divergence measure, which allows us to construct novel families of divergence and associated entropy measures. We also show various characterizing properties like duality, inversion, semi-continuity, etc., from which, many existing results follow as special cases. We also discuss about the entropy measure derived from this general family of divergence and its properties.



Keywords
Alpha-beta divergence, entropy, logarithmic S-divergence.

1
Introduction

In many applications of statistical inference, machine learning, pattern recognition, image and video analysis, information-theoretic divergence measures play a key role
[
5
,
21
]
. By using these divergence measures to quantify the separation between a family of model distributions and the true data-generating distribution, the minimum divergence estimators have become a popular tool in statistical estimation, due to their simplicity and natural interpretability
[
2
]
.

Within this realm, certain categories of divergences stand out for their strong robustness and high efficiency. Popular choices include the squared Euclidean distance (also called as
L^{2}
distance), the Cressie-Read power divergence family
[
6
]
which includes many notable divergences such as the Kullback Leibler (KL) divergence, Pearson’s chi-square, Neyman’s chi-square, Hellinger distance, and general chi-square type divergences. Basu et al.
[
1
]
further introduced the density power divergence (DPD) family (also called the Beta divergence
[
4
]
), which acts as a bridge between the Kullback Leibler divergence and the
L^{2}
distance. Generalizing the above, Ghosh and Basu
[
10
]
proposed the super divergence (S-divergence) family, that connects the power divergence family with the density power divergence family. A different generalization starting from the logarithmic density power divergence (LDPD) family was proposed by Maji et al.
[
17
]
, resulting in logarithmic super-divergence (LSD) class. This is also closely related to the family of Gamma divergences
[
7
]
. Other independent efforts to produce newer classes of statistical divergence measures are also noted in the literature. Starting with the Alpha divergence
[
3
]
, Cichocki et al.
[
5
]
produced a wider class of divergence called the Alpha-Beta (AB) divergence family. In another direction, Jones et al.
[
12
]
proposed a new class of divergence family, which provides a smooth bridge between the DPD family and the LDPD family based on a hyperparameter
\phi
.

These divergence measures and their relationships provide guidance to develop new divergences with interesting properties such as robustness, efficiency, duality, convexity, etc. which are useful in different applications
[
4
]
. In this paper, we demonstrate that all the above mentioned divergences are subclasses of an even wider class of divergence, called “Generalized Alpha-Beta” (GAB) divergences. This wider class of divergences also satisfies desirable duality and convexity properties. A similar divergence measure was first conceptualized by Maji
[
16
]
, but the author did not provide any characterization of the corresponding divergence. In fact, it was not even known if the resulting form is a valid statistical divergence, i.e., is nonnegative for all possible argument pairs of the divergence. In this paper, we first provide the characterization of this divergence and develop a necessary and sufficient condition for the resulting form to be a valid statistical divergence. Furthermore, we develop various information-theoretic properties of this general class of divergence and its associated entropy measure.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce some notations and relevant definitions. Following this, in Section
3
, we provide the definition of Generalized Alpha-Beta (GAB) divergence. Moving on, we develop the necessary and sufficient conditions for the nonnegativity of the GAB divergence, and provide a general framework to construct new statistical divergence measures. We also establish various interesting properties of the resulting divergence. In Section
4
, we introduce the associated family of entropy measures, discuss their properties and link them to different properties of the GAB divergence. Finally, we conclude the paper in Section
5
by discussing the scope of future research extending beyond the current work. For the continuity of the presentation, the proofs of the results presented in the paper are deferred till the Appendix.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Notations

Let us first indicate all the notations to be used throughout this paper. Assume that
\mathcal{A}
is a set of alphabets under consideration, which may or may not be finite. We use the symbols
P
and
Q
to indicate two generic sub-probability measures both dominated by a common measure
\mu
defined on the alphabet
\mathcal{A}
. Let
p
and
q
denote Radon-Nikodym derivatives of
P
and
Q
respectively, and be referred to as the sub-density functions. The set of sub-probability distributions is denoted as

\Omega:=\left\{P:p(a)\geq 0,\forall a\in\mathcal{A},\text{ and }\int_{\mathcal{A}}p(a)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(a)\leq 1\right\}.

When
P
is a probability distribution,
\int_{\mathcal{A}}p(a)d\mu(a)
equals
1
and let
\Omega^{\ast}
denote the set of
P
s satisfying this. In this case, the corresponding sub-density functions
p
and
q
are simply termed as density functions, in accordance to the common nomenclature. If the alphabet set
\mathcal{A}
is finite, then its elements are denoted as
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
. In this case, we use the shorthand notation
p_{i}
to denote
p(a_{i})
for any
i=1,2,\dots,n
. The set of sub-probability distributions and probability distributions in this case are respectively denoted as
\Omega_{n}
and
\Omega_{n}^{\ast}
, with subscripts
n
to indicate the finiteness of underlying
\mathcal{A}
.

For a sub-probability distribution
P
with sub-density function
p
, we denote its
\alpha
-th norm as

\left\|p\right\|_{\alpha}=\left(\int_{\mathcal{A}}p^{\alpha}(a)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(a)\right)^{1/\alpha},

for any
\alpha\neq 0
, provided that the integral exists and is finite. If the integral does not converge, we assume
\left\|p\right\|_{\alpha}=\infty
in this case. Clearly, if
\mathcal{A}
is finite, then such integral always exists and is finite. We shall also indicate the collection of probability distributions with finite
\alpha
-th norm as
L_{\alpha}(\mu)
, where
\mu
denotes the dominating measure with respect to which the integral is performed. In the same spirit, we define a
(\alpha,\beta)
-inner product between
P
(with density
p
) and
Q
(with density
q
) as

\left\langle p,q\right\rangle_{\alpha,\beta}=\int_{\mathcal{A}}p^{\alpha}(a)q^{\beta}(a)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(a),

for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Note that this is not a proper inner product in the mathematical sense since it does not satisfy the commutative property.

For any
\alpha\neq 0
and a sub-probability distribution
P
, let
P^{\alpha}
denote the sub-probability distribution corresponding to the sub-density function
p^{\alpha}
, provided that
\int_{\mathcal{A}}p^{\alpha}(a)d\mu(a)\leq 1
. These are also denoted as the unnormalized
\alpha
-zoomed versions of the original probability distribution
P

[
4
]
. On the other hand, let
P^{[\alpha]}
and
p^{[\alpha]}
denote the normalized
\alpha
-zoomed versions of the distribution and the density, from the original distribution
P
, i.e.,
p^{[\alpha]}=p^{\alpha}/\left\|p\right\|_{\alpha}^{\alpha}
.

We also use the notation
C^{k}([a,b])
to denote the class of all functions on
[a,b]
which are
k
-times differentiable and the
k
-th derivative is continuous. Extending this notation, we denote the set of all continuous functions on
[a,b]
as
C^{0}([a,b])
. The notation
\boldsymbol{1}_{A}
denotes the indicator of the set
A
, i.e.,
\boldsymbol{1}_{A}(x)=1
if
x\in A
and
0
otherwise.

2.2
Alpha-Beta Divergence

Given two probability distributions
P
and
Q
with densities
p
and
q
respectively, Cichocki et al.
[
5
]
define the Alpha-Beta (AB) divergence between them as

d_{AB}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(P,Q)=\dfrac{1}{\alpha\beta}\left(\lambda_{\alpha}\left\|p\right\|_{\alpha+\beta}^{\alpha+\beta}+\lambda_{\beta}\left\|q\right\|_{\alpha+\beta}^{\alpha+\beta}-\left\langle p,q\right\rangle_{\alpha,\beta}\right)

(1)

where
\lambda_{\alpha}=\alpha/(\alpha+\beta)
and
\lambda_{\beta}=(1-\lambda_{\alpha})
, if
\alpha\beta(\alpha+\beta)\neq 0
. However, if
\alpha\beta(\alpha+\beta)=0
, the form of AB divergence is defined as the corresponding limits of the form given in (
1
), which turns out to be,

d_{AB}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(P,Q)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{\int p^{\alpha}\ln\left(p^{\alpha}/q^{\alpha}\right)-\left\|p\right\|_{\alpha}^{\alpha}+\left\|q\right\|_{\alpha}^{\alpha}}{\alpha^{2}},&\text{if }\alpha\neq 0,\beta=0,\\
\dfrac{\int q^{\beta}\ln\left(q^{\beta}/p^{\beta}\right)-\left\|q\right\|_{\beta}^{\beta}+\left\|p\right\|_{\beta}^{\beta}}{\beta^{2}},&\text{if }\alpha=0,\beta\neq 0,\\
\dfrac{\int\ln\left(q^{\alpha}/p^{\alpha}\right)+\int p^{\alpha}/q^{\alpha}-1}{\alpha^{2}},&\text{if }\alpha=-\beta\neq 0,\\
\dfrac{1}{2}\int(\ln p-\ln q)^{2},&\text{if }\alpha=\beta=0.\end{cases}

(2)

All the integrals above are performed over the alphabet set
\mathcal{A}
and with respect to the dominating measure
\mu
, which is suppressed in the notation. If
\alpha
and
\beta
are both nonzero, and
(\alpha+\beta)=(1+\tau)
for some
\tau\geq 0
, then a simple reorganization of the terms of (
1
) makes the AB divergence equivalent to the S-divergence introduced by Ghosh et al.
[
10
]
; as observed in the relation,

\displaystyle\alpha(1+\tau-\alpha)d_{AB}^{(\alpha,1+\tau-\alpha)}(P,Q)

\displaystyle=-\left\langle p,q\right\rangle_{\alpha,1+\tau-\alpha}+\frac{\alpha}{1+\tau}\left\|p\right\|_{1+\tau}^{1+\tau}+\frac{1+\tau-\alpha}{1+\tau}\left\|q\right\|_{1+\tau}^{1+\tau}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\alpha(1+\tau-\alpha)}{(1+\tau)}d_{S}^{(\tau,(\tau-\alpha)/(1-\tau))}(P,Q).

In another direction, taking
\beta=(1-\alpha)
reduces the AB divergence to the Alpha divergence family
[
3
]
(also known as the Cressie Read power divergence family
[
6
]
),

d_{A}^{(\alpha)}(P,Q)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{1}{1-\alpha}\left\|p\right\|_{1}+\dfrac{1}{\alpha}\left\|q\right\|_{1}-\dfrac{\left\langle p,q\right\rangle_{\alpha,1-\alpha}}{\alpha(1-\alpha)},&\text{if }\alpha\notin\{0,1\},\\
\int p\ln(p/q)-\left\|p\right\|_{1}+\left\|q\right\|_{1},&\text{if }\alpha=1,\\
\int q\ln(q/p)-\left\|q\right\|_{1}+\left\|p\right\|_{1},&\text{if }\alpha=0,\end{cases}

(3)

which is also a special case of the S-divergence with
\tau=0
. On the other hand, when
\alpha=1
, it reduces to the Beta divergence

d_{B}^{(\beta)}(P,Q)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{\frac{1}{\beta}\left\|p\right\|_{1+\beta}^{1+\beta}-\frac{1+\beta}{\beta}\left\langle p,q\right\rangle_{1,\beta}+\left\|q\right\|_{1+\beta}^{1+\beta}}{1+\beta},&\text{if }\beta\notin\{-1,0\}\\
\int p\ln(p/q)-\left\|p\right\|_{1}+\left\|q\right\|_{1},&\text{if }\beta=0,\\
\int\ln(q/p)+\int p/q-1,&\text{if }\beta=(-1),\end{cases}

(4)

The class of Beta divergence contains a scaled version of density power divergence family
[
1
]
as a special case with
\beta>0
.

2.3
Logarithmic Alpha-Beta Divergence

Generalizing the Alpha-Beta (AB) divergence with a nonlinear transformation like logarithm, Cichocki et al.
[
5
]
indicated a new family of divergences

d_{AC}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(P,Q)=\dfrac{1}{\beta(\alpha+\beta)}\ln\left(\left\|p\right\|_{\alpha+\beta}^{\alpha+\beta}\right)+\dfrac{1}{\alpha(\alpha+\beta)}\ln\left(\left\|q\right\|_{\alpha+\beta}^{\alpha+\beta}\right)-\dfrac{1}{\alpha\beta}\ln\left(\left\langle p,q\right\rangle_{\alpha,\beta}\right),

(5)

for
\alpha\beta(\alpha+\beta)\neq 0
. This divergence closely relates to the Gamma divergence family
[
4
]
. In a separate attempt, Maji et al.
[
17
,
18
]
also considered the same logarithmic transformation starting with the S-divergence (SD) family, which resulted in the logarithmic S-divergence (LSD) family.

3
Generalized Alpha-Beta divergence

3.1
Definition

When
\alpha,\beta,(\alpha+\beta)\neq 0
, both the statistical divergence measures given in Eq. (
1
) and Eq. (
5
) have a similar form, two terms containing the
(\alpha+\beta)
-th norm of the densities
p
and
q
, and a single term containing the
(\alpha,\beta)
-inner product between
p
and
q
. Generalizing this form with an arbitrary nonlinear transformation, one may obtain a newer class of divergence measures characterized by the hyperparameters
\alpha,\beta
and the transformation
\psi
. Some assumptions are necessary for this transformation to produce a well-defined statistical divergence measure, and these conditions are described later in detail in Section
3.2
. Let,
\psi:[0,\infty]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be such a suitable transformation. Then, we define the Generalized Alpha-Beta (GAB) divergence between any two sub-probability distributions
P
and
Q
as

d_{GAB}^{(\alpha,\beta),\psi}(P,Q)=\dfrac{1}{\beta(\alpha+\beta)}\psi\left(\left\|p\right\|_{\alpha+\beta}^{\alpha+\beta}\right)+\dfrac{1}{\alpha(\alpha+\beta)}\psi\left(\left\|q\right\|_{\alpha+\beta}^{\alpha+\beta}\right)-\dfrac{1}{\alpha\beta}\psi\left(\left\langle p,q\right\rangle_{\alpha,\beta}\right),

(6)

when
\alpha,\beta,(\alpha+\beta)
are all nonzero. If
\psi\in C^{1}([0,\infty))
, then one can define the form of Generalized Alpha-Beta divergence for the edge cases, i.e., when
\alpha=0,\beta=0
or
\alpha+\beta=0
by taking the corresponding limits of the GAB divergence,

d_{GAB}^{(\alpha,\beta),\psi}(P,Q)=\begin{cases}\frac{\psi^{\prime}(\left\|p\right\|_{\alpha}^{\alpha})d^{\ast}(P^{\alpha},Q^{\alpha})-\psi(\left\|p\right\|_{\alpha}^{\alpha})+\psi(\left\|q\right\|_{\alpha}^{\alpha})}{\alpha^{2}},&\text{if }\alpha\neq 0,\beta=0,\\
\frac{\psi^{\prime}(\left\|q\right\|_{\beta}^{\beta})d^{\ast}(Q^{\beta},P^{\beta})-\psi(\left\|q\right\|_{\beta}^{\beta})+\psi(\left\|p\right\|_{\beta}^{\beta})}{\beta^{2}},&\text{if }\alpha=0,\beta\neq 0,\\
\frac{\psi^{\prime}(1)\int\ln(q^{\alpha}/p^{\alpha})+\psi\left(\left\|p/q\right\|_{\alpha}^{\alpha}\right)-\psi(1)}{\alpha^{2}},&\text{if }\alpha=-\beta\neq 0,\\
\frac{\psi^{\prime}(1)}{2}\int(\ln p-\ln q)^{2},&\text{if }\alpha=\beta=0.\end{cases}

(7)

where

d^{\ast}(P,Q)=\int p\ln\left(p/q\right),

(8)

denotes the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between
P
and
Q
. In Eq. (
7
), the quantity
d^{\ast}(P^{\alpha},Q^{\alpha})
thus indicates the KL divergence between the unnormalized
\alpha
-zoomed versions of
P
and
Q
.

A version of this generalized divergence measure had been conceptualized by Maji
[
16
]
but without any discussions on the valid choices for the class of
\psi
-functions. Clearly, with
\psi(x)=x
, it reduces to the usual AB divergence while
\psi(x)=\ln(x)
leads to the logarithmic Alpha-Beta divergence. Hence, the proposed Generalized Alpha-Beta (GAB) divergence is a superclass containing many important types of divergences beyond these two specific choices. Some of these important special cases are indicated in Table

3.2
Characterization of the GAB divergence

The forms of the GAB divergence as given in Eq. (
6
) do not necessarily mean that the given form is a valid statistical divergence measure for all choices of generating function
\psi
. A statistical divergence must satisfy the nonnegativity property for all choices of its arguments. Therefore, we wish to find out the necessary and sufficient conditions on the generating function
\psi
for which,

d_{GAB}^{(\alpha,\beta),\psi}(P,Q)\geq 0,

for all sub-probability distributions
P
and
Q
. Also, it must be the case that if
P=Q
, then the above inequality must hold with equality.

If both
\alpha,\beta>0
, then it is easy to verify by an application of Hölder’s inequality and Jensen’s inequality that if
\psi
is monotonically increasing and convex, the form given in Eq. (
6
) is nonnegative for all pairs of
P
and
Q
. However, this characterization does not encompass the entire class of generating functions
\psi
for which the resulting GAB divergence is a valid divergence. In particular, the family of logarithmic super-divergences (LSD) or the logarithmic Alpha-Beta divergence which are known to be valid divergence measures
[
10
]
, it has a corresponding generating function
\ln(x)
which is not convex.

To completely characterize this class of valid generating functions, we follow the footsteps of Ray et al.
[
19
]
. In the end, our results turn out to be a generalization of the results of Ray et al.
[
19
]
presented in the context of Functional Density Power Divergence (FDPD) class, as we cover a significantly larger class of divergence measures with general choices of
\alpha
and
\beta
. The results for the FDPD will then follow from our general results with a replacement of
\alpha=1
. Also, because the forms of GAB as in Eq. (
7
) are only valid when the generating function
\psi
is differentiable with a continuous first-order derivative, we shall restrict our attention only to the class of functions
C^{1}([0,\infty))
throughout this paper. Although it is possible to have a statistically valid divergence with not-so-smooth
\psi
functions (as in the case of FDPD
[
19
]
), the interesting properties of the resulting minimum divergence estimators emerge only for sufficiently smooth choices of
\psi
functions (e.g.
\psi(x)=x,\psi(x)=\ln(x),\psi(x)=x^{\gamma}/\gamma
, etc.).

A key ingredient of our results is the usage of the function
\Psi(x):=\psi(e^{x})
, on which we will apply monotonicity and convexity conditions. To develop the necessary and sufficient conditions for the GAB divergence to be a valid divergence measure, we shall consider three separate cases,

1.

\alpha+\beta=1
.

2.

\alpha\beta\neq 0
and
\alpha+\beta\notin\{0,1\}
.

3.

Other limiting cases where either
\alpha=0
or
\beta=0
.

First, we consider the special case
\alpha+\beta=1
. In this case, the GAB divergence reduces to

d_{GAB}^{(\alpha,\beta),\psi}(P,Q)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}\left(\psi(1)-\psi(\left\langle p,q\right\rangle_{\alpha,1-\alpha})\right),&\text{if }\alpha\notin\{0,1\},\\
\psi^{\prime}(1)d^{\ast}(P,Q),&\text{if }\alpha=1,\beta=0,\\
\psi^{\prime}(1)d^{\ast}(Q,P),&\text{if }\alpha=0,\beta=1.\end{cases}

where
d^{\ast}
is as given in Eq. (
8
). For the form for the limiting cases, it clearly follows that it is a valid divergence if and only if
\psi^{\prime}(1)>0
, i.e.,
\psi
is strictly increasing at
1
. We shall show that this is a necessary and sufficient condition for this special case of the GAB divergence.

Theorem 3.1
.

For
\alpha+\beta=1
, the Generalized Alpha-Beta divergence is nonnegative if and only if the corresponding generating function
\psi
satisfies the following:

1.

When either
\alpha=1
or
\beta=1
,
\psi
is increasing at
1
.

2.

When
\alpha\in(0,1)
, then
\psi
is increasing in a neighbourhood of
1
of the form
(1-\epsilon,1]
for some
\epsilon>0
.

3.

When
\alpha\notin(0,1)
, then
\psi
is increasing in a neighbourhood of
1
of the form
[1,1+\epsilon)
for some
\epsilon>0
.

Now that we have established a characterization for the special case
\alpha+\beta=1
of the GAB divergence, we turn our attention to the case when
\alpha+\beta\neq 1
. Additionally, suppose that
\alpha,\beta
and
(\alpha+\beta)
are all nonzero as well. The following proposition establishes the necessary condition on
\psi(\cdot)
to produce to a valid GAB divergence in this case.

Proposition 1
.

Define
\Psi(x):=\psi(e^{x})
for all
x\in[0,\infty)
. If
\Psi
is strictly increasing and convex, then the GAB generated by
\psi
is nonnegative for all choices of
\alpha,\beta,(\alpha+\beta)\neq 0
.

When
\alpha=1
and
\beta>0
, then Proposition
1
reduces to the sufficient condition for the
\psi
function of the FDPD, as in the Proposition 4.1 of Ray et al.
[
19
]
. Thus, Proposition
1
is a generalization of this result to the family of generalized Alpha-Beta divergences. Similar to the case of FDPD, it turns out that the condition of the geometric convexity and monotonicity of the
\psi
function is also a necessary condition for the GAB divergence to be a valid statistical divergence.

Proposition 2
.

If the GAB divergence with a generating function
\psi\in C^{1}([0,\infty))
is nonnegative, then
\Psi(x):=\psi(e^{x})
must be strictly increasing and convex, for any choice of
\alpha,\beta,(\alpha+\beta)\neq 0
.

Therefore, combining the Propositions
1
and
2
, we now have a necessary and sufficient condition for the proposed GAB divergence to be a valid divergence for all the non-limiting cases. This is formulated as a theorem below.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\psi\in C^{1}([0,\infty))
and define
\Psi(x):=\psi(e^{x})
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Suppose
\alpha,\beta\neq 0
and
(\alpha+\beta)\notin\{0,1\}
. Then, the GAB divergence generated by the function
\psi
is always nonnegative if and only if
\Psi
is strictly increasing and convex.

Before we proceed further to establish similar necessary and sufficient conditions for the nonnegativity of the GAB divergence for
\alpha=0
or
\beta=0
cases, we rewrite the form of GAB divergence to show its connection to the popular Kullback Leibler (KL) divergence.

\displaystyle\int p^{\alpha}\ln(p^{\alpha}/q^{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\left\|p\right\|_{\alpha}^{\alpha}\int p^{[\alpha]}\ln\left(p^{[\alpha]}\left\|p\right\|_{\alpha}^{\alpha}/q^{[\alpha]}\left\|q\right\|_{\alpha}^{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle=\left\|p\right\|_{\alpha}^{\alpha}\left[d^{\ast}(P^{[\alpha]},Q^{[\alpha]})+\ln(\left\|p\right\|_{\alpha}^{\alpha})-\ln(\left\|q\right\|_{\alpha}^{\alpha})\right],

where
d^{\ast}(P^{[\alpha]},Q^{[\alpha]})
is the KL divergence between the normalized
\alpha
-zoomed versions of
P
and
Q
, see Eq. (
8
). Therefore, we can rewrite the GAB divergence for
\alpha\neq 0,\beta=0
case as

\alpha^{2}d_{GAB}^{(\alpha,0),\psi}(P,Q)=\psi^{\prime}(\left\|p\right\|_{\alpha}^{\alpha})\left\|p\right\|_{\alpha}^{\alpha}\left(d^{\ast}(P^{[\alpha]},Q^{[\alpha]})+\ln(\left\|p\right\|_{\alpha}^{\alpha})-\ln(\left\|q\right\|_{\alpha}^{\alpha})\right)-\psi(\left\|p\right\|_{\alpha}^{\alpha})+\psi(\left\|q\right\|_{\alpha}^{\alpha}).

(9)

Similarly, for
\alpha=0,\beta\neq 0
case, we get

\beta^{2}d_{GAB}^{(0,\beta),\psi}(P,Q)=\psi^{\prime}(\left\|q\right\|_{\beta}^{\beta})\left\|q\right\|_{\beta}^{\beta}\left(d^{\ast}(Q^{[\beta]},P^{[\beta]})+\ln(\left\|q\right\|_{\beta}^{\beta})-\ln(\left\|p\right\|_{\beta}^{\beta})\right)-\psi(\left\|q\right\|_{\beta}^{\beta})+\psi(\left\|p\right\|_{\beta}^{\beta}).

(10)

This connection demonstrates that the GAB divergence for
\alpha=0,\beta\neq 0
or
\alpha\neq 0,\beta=0
can be visualized as a localized affine transformation of the KL divergence between the zoomed versions of the corresponding densities. In the first case if
\beta>0
, the corresponding tails of the densities are downweighted. This results in higher robustness than the usual minimum KL-divergence estimator, i.e., the maximum likelihood estimator (MLE).

In the following proposition, we present sufficient conditions for the GAB divergence to be nonnegative for these two limiting cases, which follows easily from Eq. (
9
)-(
10
).

Proposition 3
.

Define
\Psi(x):=\psi(e^{x})
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
. If
\Psi
is strictly increasing and is convex for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
, then the GAB divergence generated by
\psi
is nonnegative when either
\alpha\neq 0,\beta=0
or
\alpha=0,\beta\neq 0
.

As the cases
\alpha=1,\beta=0
or
\alpha=0,\beta=1
are already discussed in Theorem
3.1
, while demonstrating the necessary conditions, we disregard these two specific cases.

Proposition 4
.

Suppose either
\alpha\notin\{0,1\},\beta=0
or
\alpha=0,\beta\notin\{0,1\}
holds. If the GAB divergence with generating function
\psi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
is nonnegative, then
\Psi(x):=\psi(e^{x})
must be strictly increasing and convex.

Complementing Theorem
3.2
, a combination of the Propositions
3
and
4
leads to the necessary and sufficient condition for the GAB divergence to be a valid measure of statistical divergence, for the limiting cases when either
\alpha=0
or
\beta=0
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\psi\in C^{1}([0,\infty))
and define
\Psi(x):=\psi(e^{x})
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Suppose exactly one of
\alpha
and
\beta
is equal to
0
, and
(\alpha+\beta)\neq 1
. Then, the GAB divergence generated by the function
\psi
is always nonnegative if and only if
\Psi
is strictly increasing and convex.

Theorems
3.1
,
3.2
and
3.3
together completely characterize the necessary and sufficient conditions for the proposed Generalized Alpha-Beta (GAB) divergence to be a valid statistical divergence measure. Except the case
\alpha+\beta=1
, the condition is the increasing and convexity property of the
\Psi
function. In the special case when
\alpha+\beta=1
, it requires a much weaker condition, only locally increasing behaviour of
\Psi
at
x=1
suffices.

3.3
Construction of new divergences

The results presented in Section
3.2
allow us to construct new divergence measures by using any increasing and convex function as
\Psi
. We have already seen the special cases corresponding to
\Psi(x)=e^{cx}
or
\Psi(x)=cx
for some
c>0
, which results in the AB divergence and logarithmic AB divergence respectively. Beyond this, we may consider
\Psi(x)=\ln(1+e^{cx})
for some
c>0
, or more generally
\Psi(x)=\ln(c_{1}+e^{c_{2}x})
for any
c_{1},c_{2}>0
. Note that, the corresponding generating function in this case becomes
\psi(x)=\ln(c_{1}+x^{c_{2}})
, which results in a generalization of the bridge divergence constructed by Kuchibhotla et al.
[
14
]
, which is also independently proposed by Gayen et al.
[
8
]
.

One may also consider any piecewise linear function such that it is continuous and the slopes of each of the segment is increasing. Namely, take

\Psi(x)=\begin{cases}a_{1}x+b_{1}&\text{ if }x\in(-\infty,c_{1}],\\
a_{2}x+b_{2}&\text{ if }x\in(c_{1},c_{2}],\\
\dots,\\
a_{k}x+b_{k}&\text{ if }x\in(c_{k-1},c_{k}],\\
a_{k+1}x+b_{k+1}&\text{ if }x\in(c_{k},\infty)\end{cases}

such that
a_{1}<a_{2}<\dots<a_{k+1}
and
a_{i}c_{i}+b_{i}=a_{i+1}c_{i}+b_{i+1}
for all
i=1,\dots,k
to ensure continuity. Note that, although this
\Psi
function lies in
C^{0}([0,\infty))
instead of
C^{1}([0,\infty))
, its increasing and convex nature ensures that the resulting GAB divergence is nonnegative, expect for the edge cases with
\alpha\beta(\alpha+\beta)=0
; see Proposition
1
. The corresponding generating function
\psi(x)
is taken to be
\Psi(\ln(x))
. One may regard this choice as an approximation of a smooth generating function.

Additionally, if we have two characteristic functions
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
corresponding to two different GAB divergence measures, we can also combine them to construct new classes of GAB divergence.

1.

By the necessary conditions given in Section
3.2
, we know that the corresponding
\Psi_{1}
and
\Psi_{2}
will be increasing and convex functions. Clearly, any linear combination of them with positive coefficients will also be increasing and convex. This means, for any
c_{1},c_{2}>0
,
\psi:=c_{1}\psi_{1}+c_{2}\psi_{2}
leads to a valid GAB divergence. One can also establish this by direct verification based on the form (
6
).

2.

A slightly nontrivial way to combine
\Psi_{1}
and
\Psi_{2}
is to consider their composition. Since the composition
\Psi_{1}\circ\Psi_{2}
is also increasing and convex, it follows that
\psi(x):=\Psi_{1}(\psi_{2}(x))
is a valid characterizing function.

A general way to build a valid characterizing function is to begin with a density function
f(x)
, and take
\Psi(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}F(t)dt
where
F(x)
is the corresponding cumulative distribution function. Note that,
\Psi(\cdot)
is increasing and convex by construction. With this choice, we take the characterizing function
\psi(x)=\Psi(\ln(x))=\int_{-\infty}^{\ln(x)}F(t)dt
. For example, for the Dirac delta distribution at
x=0
, the resulting
\psi
becomes
\ln(x)
, leading to the logarithmic AB divergence. To illustrate more examples, starting with the exponential distribution, we obtain

\psi_{\text{Exp}}(x)=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }x<1\\
1/x+\ln(x)-1&\text{ if }x\geq 1\end{cases},

and considering the normal distribution, one obtains

\displaystyle\psi_{\text{Normal}}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\ln(x)}\Phi(t)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\ln(x)}\frac{\ln(x)-u}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-u^{2}/2}du

\displaystyle=\ln(x)\Phi(\ln(x))+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\ln^{2}(x)/2},

where
\Phi(t)
is the standard normal cumulative distribution function. We illustrate a few interesting choices of the generating function
\psi
in Figure

.

3.4
Properties of the GAB Divergence

In this section, we illustrate a few properties of the GAB divergence that immediately follow from the form of the GAB divergence given in Eq. (
6
) and (
7
). These properties are given below, which may be verified by straightforward algebraic manipulations.

1.

Symmetry Property (Duality Property):
As shown before in (
11
), the GAB divergence satisfies a symmetry or duality condition, i.e.,

d_{GAB}^{(\alpha,\beta),\psi}(P,Q)=d_{GAB}^{(\beta,\alpha),\psi}(Q,P).

(11)

2.

Scaling Property:
Let
c>0
such that
cP,cQ\in\Omega
, and define
\psi_{c}(x):=\psi(cx)
. Then,

d_{GAB}^{(\alpha,\beta),\psi}(cP,cQ)=d_{GAB}^{(\alpha,\beta),\psi_{c^{\alpha+\beta}}}(P,Q).

3.

Zooming Property:
For any
w\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
, the GAB divergence between unnormalized
w
-zoomed version of
P
and
Q
satisfies

d_{GAB}^{(\alpha,\beta),\psi}(P^{w},Q^{w})=w^{2}d_{GAB}^{(w\alpha,w\beta),\psi}(P,Q).

(12)

Assume that for a sub-probability distribution
P
, its unnormalized
\alpha
-zoomed version
P^{\alpha}
and
\beta
-zoomed version
P^{\beta}
are well-defined. Then, as a consequence of the zooming property of GAB divergence as in (
12
), without any loss of generality, any one of the hyperparameters among
\alpha
or
\beta
can be made equal to
1
, by modifying the arguments of the divergence suitably and multiplying the final divergence by an appropriate constant. This holds due to the following relationship,

\displaystyle d_{GAB}^{(\alpha,\beta),\psi}(P,Q)=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\beta^{2}}d_{GAB}^{(\alpha/\beta,1),\psi}(P^{\beta},Q^{\beta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\alpha^{2}}d_{GAB}^{(1,\beta/\alpha),\psi}(P^{\alpha},Q^{\alpha}).

(13)

Another important property of many statistical divergences is the lower-semicontinuity. The following results establish this property for the GAB divergence, and represent a generalization of Propositions 3 and 4 of Kumar and Sundaresan
[
15
]
.

Proposition 5
.

Suppose
\psi\in C^{0}([0,\infty))
and
\alpha+\beta>0
and
\alpha\beta\neq 0
. Then the GAB divergence is lower-semicontinuous in its first argument, i.e., if
P_{n}
is a sequence of probability distributions converging to
P
in
L_{\alpha+\beta}(\mu)
, then

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}d_{GAB}^{(\alpha,\beta),\psi}(P_{n},Q)\geq d_{GAB}^{(\alpha,\beta),\psi}(P,Q),

(14)

for any
Q\in L_{\alpha+\beta}(\mu)
. If
\beta>0
, then the semi-continuity can be strengthened to continuity.

While the above establishes the continuity of the GAB divergence in its first argument, an analogous result holds for the function
q\mapsto d_{GAB}^{(\alpha,\beta),\psi}(p,q)
for a fixed
p
, establishing semi-continuity in the second argument. It follows trivially from Proposition
5
and the symmetry property given in (
11
).

Proposition 6
.

Suppose
\psi\in C^{0}([0,\infty))
and
\alpha+\beta>0
and
\alpha\beta\neq 0
. Then the GAB divergence is lower-semicontinuous in its second argument, i.e., if
Q_{n}
is a sequence of probability distributions converging to
Q
in
L_{\alpha+\beta}(\mu)
, then

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}d_{GAB}^{(\alpha,\beta),\psi}(P,Q_{n})\geq d_{GAB}^{(\alpha,\beta),\psi}(P,Q),

(15)

for any fixed
P\in L_{\alpha+\beta}(\mu)
. If
\alpha>0
, then the semi-continuity can be strengthened to continuity.

3.5
Approximate Pythagorean Identity

A fundamental property that allows a statistical divergence to be suitable for robust inference is the Pythagorean Identity (or inequality). In this section, we establish an approximate version of this property, for the proposed GAB divergence. Let
p_{\epsilon}
be a
(\alpha,\epsilon)
-convex combination of two probability distributions
p_{0}
and
\delta
over the same alphabet set
\mathcal{A}
, i.e.,

p_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}=(1-\epsilon)p_{0}^{\alpha}+\epsilon\delta^{\alpha}.

(16)

The choice of such a nonlinear combination over the standard Huber contamination model has been adopted into the literature before for the analysis of logarithmic norm entropy, e.g., see Ghosh and Basu
[
9
]
. Let us also define, for any two sub-probability densities
p
and
q
,

\tilde{d}_{\psi}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(p,q):=-\frac{1}{\alpha\beta}\left[\psi\left(\left\langle p,q\right\rangle_{\alpha,\beta}\right)-\lambda_{\beta}\psi\left(\left\|q\right\|_{\alpha+\beta}^{\alpha+\beta}\right)\right],

(17)

when
\alpha\beta(\alpha+\beta)\neq 0
and
\lambda_{\beta}=\alpha/(\alpha+\beta)
. It is easy to see that,

d_{GAB}^{(\alpha,\beta),\psi}(p,q)=-\tilde{d}_{\psi}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(p,p)+\tilde{d}_{\psi}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(p,q)

(18)

Now, we present a lemma that connects
\tilde{d}_{\psi}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(p_{\epsilon},q)
to
\tilde{d}_{\psi}^{(\alpha,\beta)}((1-\epsilon)^{1/\alpha}p,q)
, which is also a generalization of Lemma 3.1 of Fujisawa and Eguchi
[
7
]
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\alpha\beta(\alpha+\beta)\neq 0
,
p_{0},q,\delta\in L_{\alpha+\beta}(\mu)
and
\psi\in C^{1}((0,\infty))
be a valid characterizing function for the GAB divergence. Define
p_{\epsilon}
as in (
16
). Then,

\tilde{d}_{\psi}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(p_{\epsilon},q)=\tilde{d}_{\psi}^{(\alpha,\beta)}((1-\epsilon)^{1/\alpha}p_{0},q)+O(\epsilon\left\langle\delta,q\right\rangle_{\alpha,\beta}).

(19)

and,

\tilde{d}_{\psi}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(p_{0},q)=\tilde{d}_{\psi}^{(\alpha,\beta)}((1-\epsilon)^{1/\alpha}p_{0},q)+O(\ln(1-\epsilon)).

(20)

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\alpha\beta(\alpha+\beta)\neq 0
,
p_{0},q,\delta\in L_{\alpha+\beta}(\mu)
, and
\psi\in C^{1}((0,\infty))
be a valid characterizing function for the GAB divergence. Define
p_{\epsilon}
as in (
16
). Then, an approximate Pythagorean relation holds between
p_{0},p_{\epsilon}
and
q
in the metric defined through the GAB divergence, i.e.,

\Delta(p_{\epsilon},p_{0},q):=d_{GAB}^{(\alpha,\beta),\psi}(p_{\epsilon},q)-d_{GAB}^{(\alpha,\beta),\psi}(p_{\epsilon},p_{0})-d_{GAB}^{(\alpha,\beta),\psi}(p_{0},q),

satisfies

\Delta(p_{\epsilon},p_{0},q)=O(\epsilon v_{\delta})+O(\ln(1-\epsilon)),

(21)

where
v_{\delta}=\max\{\left\langle\delta,p_{0}\right\rangle_{\alpha,\beta},\left\langle\delta,q\right\rangle_{\alpha,\beta}\}
.

Note that, Theorem
3.5
is a generalization of the Pythagorean identity (Theorem 3.2) proved by Fujisawa and Eguchi
[
7
]
and Theorem 3.3 of Kuchibhotla et al.
[
14
]
for the case of GAB divergence. Compared to their results, our result includes an additional error term of order
\ln(1-\epsilon)
to the discrepany
\Delta(p_{\epsilon},p_{0},q)
of the Pythagorean identity as shown in (
21
). A careful consideration of the proofs of Lemma
3.4
and Theorem
3.5
reveal that this logarithmic term can be quantified as

\left[\Psi^{\prime}\left(y_{0}\right)-\Psi^{\prime}\left(\ln(1-\epsilon_{0})+x_{0}\right)\right]\ln(1-\epsilon),

for any
\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{0}
, a pre-specified level of contamination. Here,
x_{0}=\ln(\left\langle p_{0},q\right\rangle_{\alpha,\beta})
and
y_{0}=\ln\left(\left\|p_{0}\right\|_{\alpha+\beta}^{\alpha+\beta}\right)
. In the case of logarithmic alpha-beta divergence, we have
\Psi(x)=x
, and hence the above quantity equals
0
, having no additional contribution in (
21
).

4
Generalized Alpha-Beta Entropy

An important concept in the field of information theory related to a statistical divergence measure is its associated entropy measure. Entropy measures the amount of “surprise” present in a probability distribution
P\in\Omega^{\ast}
. It is also closely related to the study of code length in information theory, thermodynamics in statistical physics, etc. Starting from a statistical divergence measure, the associated entropy can be obtained by rewriting the negative divergence of any probability measure
P
to a uniform measure over the alphabet set
\mathcal{A}
, denoted as
Q_{0}

[
4
]
. Since such a uniform measure may not exist when the alphabet set
\mathcal{A}
is infinite, we restrict our attention to a finite
\mathcal{A}
with
n
elements and consider the subset
\Omega_{n}^{\ast}
. Note that, for the proposed GAB divergence
d_{GAB}^{(\alpha,\beta),\psi}(P,Q_{0})
, the last term is free of
P
. As a result, we may define the corresponding entropy measure as

\varepsilon_{GAB}^{(\alpha,\beta),\psi}(P):=-\dfrac{1}{\beta}\left[\frac{\psi\left(\left\|p\right\|_{\alpha+\beta}^{\alpha+\beta}\right)}{\alpha+\beta}-\frac{\psi\left(\left\|p\right\|_{\alpha}^{\alpha}\right)}{\alpha}\right],

(22)

for
\alpha,\beta,(\alpha+\beta)\neq 0
. If
\psi(x)=\ln(x)
, then Eq. (
22
) reduces to the logarithmic
(\alpha,\alpha+\beta)
-norm entropy up to a multiplicative constant

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{GAB}^{(\alpha,\beta),\ln}(P)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{\alpha-(\alpha+\beta)}\left[\ln\left(\left\|p\right\|_{\alpha+\beta}\right)-\ln\left(\left\|p\right\|_{\alpha}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{\alpha(\alpha+\beta)}\varepsilon_{LN}^{(\alpha,\alpha+\beta)}(P).

The logarithmic norm entropy is a generalization of the class of Rényi entropy (hence also the Shannon entropy) that satisfies an important scale-invariance property
[
9
]
. Note that, the Generalized Alpha-Beta Entropy (GABE) as in Eq. (
22
) can be trivially extended to encompass the class of sub-probability distributions
\Omega_{n}
as well.

For the edge cases when either
\alpha,\beta
or
(\alpha+\beta)
is equal to
0
, the form of GABE reduces to

\varepsilon_{GAB}^{(\alpha,\beta),\psi}(P)=\begin{cases}\frac{\psi\left(\left\|p\right\|_{\alpha}^{\alpha}\right)}{\alpha^{2}}-\frac{\psi^{\prime}\left(\left\|p\right\|_{\alpha}^{\alpha}\right)}{\alpha}\int p^{\alpha}\ln(p),&\text{if }\alpha\neq 0,\beta=0,\\
\frac{\psi^{\prime}(1)}{\beta}\int\ln(p)-\frac{\psi\left(\left\|p\right\|_{\beta}^{\beta}\right)}{\beta^{2}},&\text{if }\alpha=0,\beta\neq 0,\\
\frac{\psi^{\prime}(1)}{\alpha}\int\ln(p)-\frac{\psi\left(\left\|p\right\|_{\alpha}^{\alpha}\right)}{\alpha^{2}},&\text{if }\alpha=-\beta\neq 0,\\
-\frac{\psi^{\prime}(1)}{2}\int(\ln(p))^{2},&\text{if }\alpha=\beta=0,\end{cases}

which can be easily verified by taking appropriate limits in Eq. (
22
).

To motivate this measure of entropy, let us consider a Bernoulli distribution with alphabet
\mathcal{A}=\{0,1\}
and probability
p(1)=p
, so that
p(0)=(1-p)
. In this case, the GABE for the case
\alpha,\beta,(\alpha+\beta)\neq 0
turns out to be equal to

\varepsilon_{GAB}^{(\alpha,\beta),\psi}(\text{Ber}(p))=\frac{\psi(p^{\alpha}+(1-p)^{\alpha})}{\alpha\beta}-\frac{\psi(p^{\alpha+\beta}+(1-p)^{\alpha+\beta})}{(\alpha+\beta)\beta}.

When either
p=0
or
p=1
, the GABE becomes equal to
\psi(1)/\alpha(\alpha+\beta)
, which is the minimum possible value of the entropy in this particular case. Note that, this minimum value depends on the choice of the hyperparameters
\alpha
and
\beta
. Hence, the entropy measures arising from different choices of these hyperparameters are not directly comparable. Therefore, one may consider scaling the proposed measure given in Eq. (
22
) by multiplying it with
\alpha(\alpha+\beta)
and treat the resulting quantity as the measure of entropy, which is more universal. In Figure
2
below, we show these scaled measures of entropy as functions of the probability
p
, for different combinations of hyperparameters
\alpha
and
\beta
. We also show different choices of the generating functions that result in different shapes of the resulting entropy measure. As the plots show, except for a few choices of the hyperparameters, the entropy is maximized at
p=1/2
in almost all cases. These choices of the hyperparameters are connected to the concavity of the entropy measure, and will be developed later in detail in Theorem
4.2
.

and

4.1
Properties of GABE

It can be easily seen from the definition in (
22
) that the GABE satisfies the following properties.

1.

Continuity:
For a finite alphabet
\mathcal{A}
, the GAB entropy is continuous in the
p_{i}=p(a_{i})
s for all
i=1,2,\dots,|\mathcal{A}|
.

2.

Symmetricity:

\varepsilon_{GAB}^{(\alpha,\beta),\psi}(P)
is a symmetric function of
\{p_{1},p_{2},\dots p_{|\mathcal{A}|}\}
.

3.

Decisivity:
For any degenerate distribution
\delta_{x}
at
x\in\mathcal{A}
,
\varepsilon_{GAB}^{(\alpha,\beta),\psi}(\delta_{x})=0
.

4.

Expandibility:
For any sub-probability distribution
P:=(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})\in\Omega_{n}
, we have

\varepsilon_{GAB}^{(\alpha,\beta),\psi}(P)=\varepsilon_{GAB}^{(\alpha,\beta),\psi}(P^{\ast}),

where
P^{\ast}:=(p_{1},\dots,p_{n},0)\in\Omega_{n+1}
is another sub-probability distribution over a new alphabet set having
(n+1)
elements.

5.

Maximum value:
For a finite alphabet
\mathcal{A}
with
|\mathcal{A}|=n
, the maximum value of the proposed entropy measure is
\psi(n^{\alpha+\beta-1})/\alpha(\alpha+\beta)
. This is a simple consequence of the nonnegativity of the GAB divergence and the construction of the GABE as given in Eq. (
22
).

A desirable property of an entropy measure is the additivity property. When this property holds, then the joint entropy of two systems governed by independent probability distributions
P
and
Q
becomes same as the sum of their individual entropy measures. Shanon’s entropy and the logarithmic norm entropy both satisfy this additivity property. In the following theorem, we show that the generalized alpha-beta entropy also satisfies such a property provided the generating
\psi
-function satisfies a generalized additivity property.

Proposition 7
.

Let
P\in\Omega_{n},Q\in\Omega_{m}
for some
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}
. Let
R\equiv P\ast Q=(p_{i}q_{j})_{i=1,j=1}^{n,m}
denote their independent combination such that
\mathbb{R}\in\Omega_{nm}
. Suppose further that for any
x,y>0
, the function
\psi
satisfy

\psi(xy)=g(\psi(x))+h(\psi(y)),

(23)

for some function
g
and
h
. Then the GAB entropy satisfies a generalized Shannon-additivity property, i.e.,

\varepsilon_{GAB}^{(\alpha,\beta),\psi}(r)=\varepsilon_{GAB}^{(\alpha,\beta),g\circ\psi}(p)+\varepsilon_{GAB}^{(\alpha,\beta),h\circ\psi}(q),

where the notation
f\circ g
denotes the composition of two functions
f
and
g
.

Another important property of any entropy measure is its concavity which allows one to apply the maximum entropy principle. In the following, we derive the exact set of conditions under which the concavity of the GAB entropy follows. A similar set of conditions for the logarithmic norm entropy is present in Ghosh and Basu
[
9
]
. Before proceeding with the main result, we start with a short lemma that characterizes the behaviour of
\psi(\left\|p\right\|_{a}^{a})
as a function of the density
p
.

Lemma 4.1
.

The quantity
\psi(\left\|p\right\|_{a}^{a})
is convex in
P\in\Omega
if any of the following holds

1.

a<0
and
\Psi(x):=\psi(e^{x})
is nondecreasing and convex.

2.

a>0
such that
\ln(\left\|p\right\|_{a})
is convex in
p\in\Omega
and
\Psi(x):=\psi(e^{x})
is nondecreasing and convex.

3.

a>1
and
\psi(\cdot)
is nondecreasing and convex.

Theorem 4.2
.

The GAB entropy
\varepsilon_{GAB}^{(\alpha,\beta),\psi}
is concave on
\Omega
if any of the following conditions hold:

1.

\alpha<0,\beta>0
and
(\alpha+\beta)\in(0,1)\cup(1,\infty)
such that
\ln(\left\|p\right\|_{\alpha+\beta})
is convex for
P\in\Omega
.

2.

\alpha<0,\beta>0,(\alpha+\beta)>1
and
\psi(\cdot)
is convex.

3.

\alpha>0,\beta<0,(\alpha+\beta)<0
such that
\ln(\left\|p\right\|_{\alpha})
is convex for
P\in\Omega
.

4.

\alpha>1,\beta<0,(\alpha+\beta)<0
and
\psi(\cdot)
is convex.

A consequence of Theorem
4.2
is that under the same conditions as in Theorem
4.2
, the GAB divergence is convex in its first argument. This follows directly from the relationship that
\varepsilon_{GAB}^{(\alpha,\beta),\psi}(P)=-d_{GAB}^{(\alpha,\beta),\psi}(P,Q_{0})+h(Q_{0})
where
Q_{0}
is the fixed uniform measure on the alphabet
\mathcal{A}
and
h(Q_{0})
is a quantity depending only on
Q_{0}
. On the other hand, because of the duality result (
11
), exchanging the role of
\alpha
and
\beta
in the conditions presented in Thereom
4.2
, we obtain a set of sufficient conditions under which the GAB divergence is convex in its second argument.

In the limiting case when
\alpha=0
, the GAB divergence is convex in its first argument if either
\beta>0
and
\ln(\left\|p\right\|_{\beta})
is convex in
p
, or
\beta>1
and
\psi(\cdot)
is convex. To see this, note that due to the form of the divergence given in (
7
), it is enough to show that the quantities
-\beta\int q^{\beta}\ln(p)
and
\psi(\left\|p\right\|_{\beta}^{\beta})
are convex in
p
. Clearly, the first function is convex for any
\beta>0
due to the concavity of the
\ln(\cdot)
function. The convexity of the second function follows directly from Lemma
4.1
.

The maximum entropy principle is a fundamental concept having various applications in statistics, information theory and statistical physics. While various classical literature investigates the maximum entropy distribution under different linear constraints (which translates to various moment-type conditions in statistics), modern literature in information theory generalizes the constraints to consider normalized

5
Conclusion

The generalized Alpha-Beta divergence introduced in this paper establishes a unifying framework for deriving new statistical divergence measures, which may find broader applications in robust statistics, signal processing, machine learning and pattern recognition. We derive its characterizing properties, and demonstrate how some fundamental properties are shared by all members of this broad family of divergence. We also indicate its associated entropy measure, which has many utilities in information theory.

While we indicate a general method to construct new divergence measures of this family in Section
3.3
, more extensive studies are needed to understand which of them have strong robustness properties and may prove useful in statistical inference problems. Extending on the Pythagorean theorem established in this paper, one may consider deriving the properties of forward projections and the reverse projections, which have interesting applications in hypothesis testing and model selection. Additionally, just as the Réyni entropy or Tsallis entropy arise naturally in modelling different physical phenomenons, further studies are needed to understand the natural appeal and the practical utility of the proposed generalized form of entropy. We expect to tackle these problems in the future.

. So, by Lagrange’s Mean Value theorem, there exists

such that

\Psi^{\prime}(x)\leq\Psi^{\prime}(z)=\frac{\Psi(y)-\Psi(x)}{y-x}\leq\Psi^{\prime}(y).

On the other hand if
y<x
, then a similar deduction applies. This completes the proof.

A.6
Proof of Proposition
4

As before, we shall show the proof only for the case when
\alpha\neq 0,\beta=0
. The case for
\alpha=0,\beta\neq 0
would then follow from the symmetry of GAB divergence as in Eq. (
11
).

Increasingnes:
Since
\psi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, to show that
\Psi
is strictly increasing, it is enough to show that for any
y>0
,
\psi^{\prime}(y)>0
. Consider the pair of Gaussian densities
p
and
q
,

\displaystyle p(x)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}e^{-x^{2}/2\sigma^{2}},

\displaystyle q(x)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}e^{-(x-\theta)^{2}/2\sigma^{2}},

It follows that
\left\|p\right\|_{\alpha}^{\alpha}=\left\|q\right\|_{\alpha}^{\alpha}=(2\pi)^{-(\alpha-1)/2}\sigma^{-(\alpha-1)}\alpha^{-1/2}
. As
\alpha\neq 1
, we take
\sigma=(2\pi)^{-1/2}(\alpha)^{-1/2(\alpha-1)}y^{-1/(\alpha-1)}>0
. The nonnegativity of the GAB divergence as in (
9
) now reduces to

\psi^{\prime}(y)yd^{\ast}(P^{[\alpha]},Q^{[\alpha]})-\psi(y)+\psi(y)>0,

and since the KL-divergence is nonnegative, it implies that
\psi^{\prime}(y)>0
, as we wanted to show.

Convexity:
Moving on to the convexity condition, let us pick any
y>z\geq 0
. We consider again the family of nonnegative sub-densities
p
and
q
given in (
33
). If
\alpha>1
, then we pick
\theta=e^{-y/(\alpha-1)}
and
\eta=e^{-z/(\alpha-1)}
. As
\theta<\eta
, elementary calculations show that

\displaystyle\int p^{\alpha}\ln(p/q)

\displaystyle=c^{\alpha}(\gamma+1)(\ln(\theta)-\ln(\eta))\theta^{-(\alpha-1)}

\displaystyle=(\gamma+1)e^{y}(z-y)/(\alpha-1).

Therefore, the nonnegativity of the GAB divergence translates to the inequality

\dfrac{(\gamma+1)\alpha}{(\alpha-1)}\psi^{\prime}(e^{y})e^{y}(z-y)-\psi(e^{y})+\psi(e^{z})\geq 0.

Choosing
\gamma=-1/\alpha
yields
\Psi(y)-\Psi(z)-\Psi^{\prime}(y)(y-z)\geq 0
for any
y>z
, which establishes the convexity of
\Psi
function.

If
\alpha\in(0,1)
, then there exists a constant
M>0
such that

. By considering the modified parameter
\alpha^{\prime}=M\alpha
and utilizing the zooming property as in (
12
), we reduce this to the former case with
\alpha>1
.

If
\alpha<0

12
), but with the zooming constant

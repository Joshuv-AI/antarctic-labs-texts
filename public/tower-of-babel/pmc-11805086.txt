Comparative Evaluation of Dentin Hypersensitivity Reduction Over One Month after a Single Topical Application of Three Different Materials: A Prospective Experimental Study
Authors: Aniket Sharan, Babita Pawar, Hiroj Bagde, Tavinder Kaur Chawla, Aadarsh Vikram Dhan, Bhawna Shyamsukha, Sanjna Sharma
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Dentinal hypersensitivity (DH) is a prevalent condition characterized by sharp, transient pain arising from exposed dentine in response to various stimuli. It is common among patients with gingival recession, tooth abrasion, and erosion.Materials and Methods:A prospective experimental study was conducted in the Department of Periodontology at the Chhattisgarh Dental College and Research Institute. A total of 45 patients with DH were randomly assigned to three groups: Test Group I (5% potassium nitrate gel), Test Group II (Tetric-N-Bond self-etch adhesive), and Test Group III (fluoride varnish). Clinical assessments of DH were conducted immediately after treatment, at one week, and at one month, using tactile sensation, cold stimuli, and air blast tests. Pain levels were measured using a 10-point visual analog scale (VAS).Results:Tetric-N-Bond demonstrated the most significant reduction in DH at all follow-up intervals. Immediately after application, VAS scores (tactile) were potassium nitrate (1.33 ± 0.48), Tetric-N-Bond (1.53 ± 0.74), and fluoride varnish (3.06 ± 0.88). After one month, the scores were: Tetric-N-Bond (2.53 ± 1.12), potassium nitrate (3.20 ± 0.41), and fluoride varnish (4.73 ± 1.57). Significant differences were observed between all groups (P < 0.05). The Schiff cold air score also supported these findings, showing Tetric-N-Bond as the most effective treatment, followed by potassium nitrate and fluoride varnish.Conclusion:The study concluded that Tetric-N-Bond is the most effective treatment for reducing DH immediately and over a one-month period, followed by potassium nitrate and fluoride varnish.

INTRODUCTION

Dentinal hypersensitivity (DH) is a prevalent condition in dental practice, especially among patients with gingival recession, abrasion, and erosion. Defined as a short-acting, sharp pain arising from exposed dentine due to stimuli such as thermal, osmotic, tactile, or chemical, DH cannot be attributed to other dental defects or diseases. Various terms, including dentine sensitivity and root sensitivity, describe this ailment. DH begins with the exposure of dentinal tubules due to factors like periodontal disorders, gingival recession, occlusal pressures, acidic foods, and abrasive brushing.[12]

Diagnosis involves patients self-reporting acute pain in response to stimuli. Etiologies include periodontal surgery, scaling, and gingival attachment loss. Root irritation posttreatment typically subsides within weeks. Bruxism, enamel loss, and dental wear also contribute to DH.[345] Treatment aims to occlude dentinal tubules. Self-applied desensitizing agents, though slow-acting, are widely used. Potassium nitrate, found in mouthwashes at concentrations of 3%-5%, raises the nerve stimulation threshold and inhibits nerve repolarization, reducing pain. Fluoride varnish, known for its ease of application and immediate effect, creates precipitates that block fluid movement in dentinal tubules.[6]

This study evaluates the effectiveness of 5% potassium nitrate gel, Tetric-N-Bond, and fluoride varnish in treating hypersensitivity at intervals of immediate application, one week, and one month.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

The study employed a prospective experimental design, involving participants with DH, and was conducted in the periodontology department of the Chhattisgarh Dental College and Research Institute in Rajnandgaon. The trial included a total of 45 patients.

Patients were included if they sought treatment for DH as their primary complaint, had DH in at least one or two teeth, were between the ages of 20 and 50, were in good overall health, and had gingival recession, tooth erosion, or abrasion. Patients were excluded if they had used desensitizing toothpaste within the last two months, had professional desensitization therapy within the last three months, had advanced chronic periodontal disease, teeth with irreversible pulpitis, teeth treated with root canal therapy, or teeth with cervical restorations. Additionally, patients with underlying medical conditions currently using antidepressants, anticonvulsants, antihistamines, sedatives, or daily analgesics, as well as pregnant or nursing women, were excluded.

The selected sites were randomly divided into three groups. Test Group I received an application of 5% potassium nitrate gel, Test Group II received Tetric-N-Bond self-etch adhesive, and Test Group III received fluoride varnish. Teeth needing treatment were isolated using cotton rolls. In Test Group I, 5% potassium nitrate gel was applied and allowed to dry for five minutes, followed by a gentle water rinse. In Test Group II, Tetric-N-Bond was applied with a brushing motion, left for 30 s, light-cured for 10 s, and dried with air for 3 s. In Test Group III, fluoride varnish was applied, and patients were instructed to avoid hot beverages and refrain from brushing or flossing for a few hours.

Clinical assessments of DH were conducted immediately after treatment, and follow-up evaluations were performed at one week and one month. The parameters assessed included tactile sensation using an explorer, response to cold stimuli using the Schiff Cold Air Sensitivity Scale, and response to air blast using a dental unit air syringe. Pain levels were measured using a 10-point visual analog scale (VAS), where patients indicated their level of pain.

Statistical analysis was carried out using SPSS IBM version 25.

RESULTS

The post-hoc Tukey test results reveal significant differences in VAS scores for tactile, cold water, and Schiff cold air sensitivity among different treatment groups (potassium nitrate, Tetric-N-Bond, and varnish) over immediate, one-week, and one-month intervals. For tactile VAS scores, varnish consistently showed significantly higher mean differences compared to potassium nitrate and Tetric-N-Bond, with immediate and one-week intervals showing the most notable differences. In the cold-water VAS scores, significant differences were observed particularly in the one-week and one-month intervals, with varnish again demonstrating the highest mean differences. Similarly, for Schiff cold air scores, varnish exhibited significantly higher sensitivity reduction compared to the other groups across all time points, especially immediately and one week after treatment. These findings indicate that varnish is the most effective treatment for reducing sensitivity in the conditions tested [Table 1].

DISCUSSION

Dentin hypersensitivity is a frequently encountered but poorly understood condition, more common among adults and characterized by chronic discomfort. Numerous studies have sought to identify its etiological factors, diagnosis, and treatment options.[23456] In this study, three desensitizing agents—fluoride varnish, potassium nitrate, and Tetric-N-Bond self-etch adhesive—were compared after a single application. After one month, Tetric-N-Bond proved to be more effective than potassium nitrate and fluoride varnish, aligning with findings from other studies such as those by Cummins et al, Markowitz et al.[34]

The study revealed that Tetric-N-Bond, by extending resin tags into dentinal tubules, forms a hybrid layer that effectively seals off the tubules, blocking any stimuli. This bonding agent outperformed both potassium nitrate and fluoride varnish in reducing DH, as reflected in the Schiff cold air test results. Although initial responses to potassium nitrate were notable, its effectiveness diminished over time, suggesting the need for continuous application to maintain minimal concentrations for sustained nerve deactivation. The fluoride varnish was less effective due to its reliance on forming a superficial layer of calcium fluoride, which can be disrupted by saliva and mechanical actions.

SUMMARY AND CONCLUSION

The study concluded that Tetric-N-Bond is the most effective treatment for reducing DH immediately and over a one-month period, followed by potassium nitrate and fluoride varnish.

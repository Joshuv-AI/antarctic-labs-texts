Shallow-water convergence of the intermediate long wave equation in L 2 L^{2}
Andreia Chapouto, Guopeng Li, Tadahiro Oh, and Tengfei Zhao

Abstract
. We continue our study on the convergence issue of the intermediate long wave equation (ILW) on both the real line and the circle. In particular, we establish convergence of the scaled ILW dynamics to that of the Korteweg-de Vries equation (KdV) in the shallow-water limit at the L 2 L^{2} -level. Together with the recent work by the first three authors and D. Pilod (2024) on the deep-water convergence in L 2 L^{2} , this work completes the well-posedness and convergence study of ILW on both geometries within the L 2 L^{2} -framework. Our proof equally applies to both geometries and is based on the following two ingredients: the complete integrability of ILW and the normal form method. More precisely, by making use of the Lax pair structure and the perturbation determinant for ILW, recently introduced by Harrop-Griffths, Killip, and Vişan (2025), we first establish weakly uniform (in small depth parameters) equicontinuity in L 2 L^{2} of solutions to the scaled ILW, providing a control on the high frequency part of solutions. Then, we treat the low frequency part by implementing a perturbative argument based on an infinite iteration of normal form reductions for KdV.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Intermediate long wave equation

In this paper, we study the following intermediate long wave equation (ILW)
on
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{T}=(\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z})
:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-\mathcal{G}_{\delta}\partial_{x}^{2}u=\partial_{x}(u^{2})\\
u|_{t=0}=u_{0},\end{cases}\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{M}.

(1.1)

The equation (
1.1
)
was introduced in
[
31
,
42
]

as a model
describing the propagation of an internal wave at the interface of a stratified fluid of
finite depth
\delta\in(0,\infty)
,
where
the unknown
u:\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{R}

denotes the amplitude of the internal wave at the interface;
see also Remark 1.1 in
[
46
]
.
The operator
\mathcal{G}_{\delta}
in (
1.1
)
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{\delta}=\mathcal{T}_{\delta}-\delta^{-1}\partial_{x}^{-1},

(1.2)

where
\mathcal{T}_{\delta}

is given by

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{T}_{\delta}f}(\xi)=-i\coth(\delta\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi),\quad\xi\in\widehat{\mathcal{M}}.

(1.3)

Here,
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
denotes
the Pontryagin dual of
\mathcal{M}
, i.e.

\widehat{\mathcal{M}}=\mathbb{R}
if
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{R}
,
and

\widehat{\mathcal{M}}=\mathbb{Z}
if
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{T}
.
From the analytical point of view,
(
1.1
) is of great interest due to its rich structure;
it is a dispersive equation, admitting soliton solutions.
Moreover, it is completely integrable with an infinite number of conservation laws
[
40
,
41
,
60
]

and Lax pair structures
[
29
]
.
See
[
61
,
38
]
for an overview of the subject and the references therein.

It is known that the ILW equation (
1.1
)
serves as an “intermediate” equation of finite depth
\delta\in(0,\infty)
,
bridging the following two equations:

•

Benjamin-Ono equation, modeling fluid of infinite depth (
\delta=\infty
), and

•

Korteweg-de Vries equation, modeling shallow water (
\delta=0
),

both of which are also completely integrable.
We note that
the operator
\mathcal{T}_{\delta}
in (
1.2
)
is the inverse of the
so-called Tilbert transform (with an extra
-
sign),
appearing in the study of water waves
of finite depth
[
5
,
27
,
2
]
.
In particular, in the deep-water limit (
\delta\to\infty
),

\mathcal{T}_{\delta}
converges to the Hilbert transform
\mathcal{H}

(associated with
the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map
in infinite depth),
indicating possible convergence of ILW (
1.1
)
to
the Benjamin-Ono equation (BO):

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u-\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}u=\partial_{x}(u^{2}).

(1.4)

See Subsection
1.2

for a further discussion.
Next, let us turn our attention to
the shallow-water limit (
\delta\to 0
).
Recalling that
u
denotes the amplitude of the internal wave
at the interface of a stratified fluid of finite depth
\delta>0
,
it follows that
the amplitude
u
of the internal wave at the interface is
O(\delta)
, which also tends to
0

in the shallow-water limit.
This suggests that,
in order to observe any meaningful limiting behavior,
we need to magnify the fluid amplitude by a factor
\sim\frac{1}{\delta}
.
This motivates us to consider the following scaling
[
1
]
:

v(t,x)=\tfrac{3}{\delta}u\big(\tfrac{3}{\delta}t,x\big),

(1.5)

which transforms (
1.1
) to the following scaled ILW equation:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v-\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\delta}\partial_{x}^{2}v=\partial_{x}(v^{2}),\qquad\text{where }\ \widetilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\delta}:=\frac{3}{\delta}\mathcal{G}_{\delta}.

(1.6)

Then, under suitable assumptions,
the scaled ILW (
1.6
) is known to converge to the
Korteweg-de Vries equation (KdV)
in the shallow-water limit (
\delta\to 0
)

[
1
,
45
,
46
]
:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v+\partial_{x}^{3}v=\partial_{x}(v^{2}).

(1.7)

In recent years, there have been intensive research activities
on mathematical analysis of ILW,
from both deterministic and statistical viewpoints;
see

,

,

,

]
for the known well- / ill-posedness results

of (scaled) ILW to BO in the deep-water limit (
\delta\to\infty
)
and to KdV in the shallow-water limit (

);
see also

[

,

,

]

for results on ILW with modulated dispersion.
On the one hand,

,

]

) to BO (
1.4
)

On the other hand,

)

In this sense,

We also point out that,

Our main goal in this paper is to

)

We achieve this goal by

(i)

complete integrability approach, in particular, perturbation determinants

[

,
34
,
28
,

,
29
]
, and

(ii)

normal form approach,

with his collaborators
[

,

,
44
,
59
]
.

In Subsection
1.2
,

(namely, analysis on ILW (
1.1
)

) as its deep-water limit).
In Subsection

,

)

1.2.
Deep-water regime

In this subsection, we briefly go over
recent well-posedness
and deep-water convergence
results for ILW (
1.1
).
It is known (see
[
51
,
39
]
) that,
just like BO (
1.4
),
ILW (
1.1
)
behaves quasilinearly in the sense that
a contraction argument
can not be used
for constructing solutions,
which makes the well-posedness question
of ILW
rather challenging, especially in the low-regularity setting.

The main idea in this deep-water regime is to view
ILW as a perturbation of BO (
1.4
).
Fix
0<\delta<\infty
.
Recall that the Galilean transform

\displaystyle w(t,x)=u(t,x+\delta^{-1}t)

(1.8)

eliminates the term

\delta^{-1}\partial_{x}^{-1}
in (
1.2
),
thus allowing us
to rewrite (
1.1
)
as the following renormalized ILW:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}w-\mathcal{T}_{\delta}\partial_{x}^{2}w=\partial_{x}(w^{2})

(1.9)

with
w|_{t=0}=u_{0}
,
where
\mathcal{T}_{\delta}

is as in (
1.3
).
By writing
\mathcal{Q}_{\delta}=(\mathcal{T}_{\delta}-\mathcal{H})\partial_{x}
,
where
\mathcal{H}
denotes the Hilbert transform,
we can write (
1.9
) as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}w-\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}w=\partial_{x}(w^{2})+\mathcal{Q}_{\delta}\partial_{x}w.

(1.10)

Compare (
1.10
) with (
1.4
).
The key observation here is that

\mathcal{Q}_{\delta}
has a smoothing property of any order
with a uniform control in
\delta\gtrsim 1

(see
[
10
, Lemma 2.3]
),
allowing
us to view the second term on the right-hand side of (
1.10
)
as a perturbation term.

In a recent work
[
30
]
,
by combining
this perturbative viewpoint
with
the BO gauge transform and the (quasilinear) normal form method,
Ifrim and Saut
proved global well-posedness of (
1.1
)
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
for each fixed
0<\delta<\infty

(along with long-time behavior of small solutions).
This argument
relies on dispersive estimates
and thus is not applicable to the periodic setting.
In
[
10
]
,
the first three authors and Pilod
also
exploited this perturbative viewpoint (
1.10
),
following
[
30
]
.
By adapting
a unified
approach
to the

well-posedness
of BO (
1.4
)
on both the real line and the circle,
developed by Molinet and Pilod
[
48
]
,
the authors in
[
10
]

proved global well-posedness of ILW (
1.1
)
in
L^{2}(\mathcal{M})
,
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{T}
.
Furthermore,
by exploiting the decay property of
\mathcal{Q}_{\delta}
as
\delta\to\infty
,
the authors in
[
10
]

proved the following
deep-water convergence result.

Theorem A
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{T}

and
s\geq 0
.
Given
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathcal{M})
,
let

be the global solution to the BO equation (

)

constructed in

Let
\{u_{0,\delta}\}_{1\leq\delta<\infty}\subset H^{s}(\mathcal{M})

converges to

) with

constructed in
[

Then, as

in

(
in time
)
.

We also mention
[

,

]

where
the perturbative viewpoint (

once again played a crucial role.
In the former paper
[

]
,
by combining this

Before proceeding further, let us recall the notion of

sensible weak solutions
; see

[

,

4

Definition 1.1
.

Let

be a Banach space of functions on
\mathcal{M}

Given

we say that

u\in C((t_{0},t_{1});B(\mathcal{M}))

to an equation
on a time interval

for any sequence
\{u_{0,n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

the corresponding (classical) solutions

converge to

in
C((t_{0},t_{1});B(\mathcal{M}))
.

Note that, by definition,
sensible weak solutions are unique.
We also point out that the notion of sensible weak solutions
is rather weak;
sensible weak solutions do not have to satisfy
the equation even in the distributional sense.
For example, in the case of ILW, BO, and KdV,
the nonlinearity
\partial_{x}(u^{2})

does not need to make sense as a space-time distribution
even if
u
is a sensible weak solution.
However, note that, given a sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

of smooth approximating solutions,
it follows from
Definition
1.1

and the equation that

\partial_{x}(u_{n}^{2})

converges to some limit as space-time distributions.
Namely,
a sensible weak solution

(in the case of ILW (
1.1
)) satisfies

\partial_{t}u-\mathcal{G}_{\delta}\partial_{x}^{2}u=\lim_{n\to\infty}\partial_{x}(u_{n}^{2})

as a space-time distribution, where the right-hand side is independent
of a choice of smooth approximation solutions
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.
Namely, we interpret the nonlinearity
only as the (unique) distributional limit of the nonlinearities
of smooth approximating solutions.
On the one hand, the notion of sensible weak solutions is
weaker than the usual notion of (strong) solutions for which
we directly make sense of the nonlinearity as a space-time distribution.
On the other hand,
the notion of sensible weak solutions is
widely accepted,
for example,
in the low regularity well-posedness
of integrable dispersive PDEs

[

,
34
,
20
,
33
]

and singular stochastic PDEs

[

]

Shallow-water regime: main result

Before proceeding further, let us recall the notion of equicontinuity.

Definition 1.3
.

Let

We say that a bounded set

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\sup_{f\in E}\sup_{|y|<\varepsilon}\|f(\,\cdot+y)-f(\,\cdot\,)\|_{H^{s}}=0.

(1.11)

By Plancherel’s identity, (

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\sup_{f\in E}\|\mathbf{P}_{>N}f\|_{H^{s}}=0

\mathbf{P}_{>N}

\{|\xi|>N\}

Fix large

Our goal is to prove the bound

and small

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{P}_{>N}(v-v_{\delta})\|_{C_{T}L^{2}_{x}}<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}

(3.4)

for any

and
0<\delta\leq\delta_{1}
.
From Proposition

,
we can then choose

and small

\delta_{2}=\delta_{2}\big(\varepsilon,T,N,\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\big)>0

for any

on the

with

of

Therefore, putting (

(
3.4

In this section, we establish

For this purpose, we first recall some notations

For an operator

In particular, if

(4.1)

Recall from

5.
Low frequency analysis: normal form method

In this section, we present a proof of Proposition
3.1

based on an infinite iteration of normal form reductions.
More precisely, we
implement a novel perturbative argument
based on an infinite iteration of normal form reductions
for KdV.
Namely,
we apply
normal form reductions
only to KdV Duhamel integral terms;
when we encounter
the scaled ILW Duhamel integral operator,
we replace it by that for KdV and view the difference
as an error term (see

\mathcal{E}_{\delta}^{(j)}

in (
5.8
)
and (
5.32
))
which is then handled by
the convergence of the resonance function

\Xi_{\delta}(\bar{\xi})

for the scaled ILW (see (
5.2
))
to the resonance function

\Xi_{\text{\rm KdV}}(\bar{\xi})

for KdV,
together with
the weakly uniform
equicontinuity (Proposition
1.4
)
controlling a very high frequency part;
see (
6.17
).
An infinite iteration of normal form reductions
was first introduced in
[
24
]

and was further developed in
[
59
,
55
,
44
,
36
,
37
]
.
As mentioned in Remark
1.7
,
it has been applied in various contexts.
As such, we will keep our presentation brief
on what is now standard, and
aim to emphasize the novel perturbative aspect
of the argument.

In the periodic setting,
in order to avoid the problem at the zeroth frequency,
it is crucial to work with mean-zero functions,
(which makes the periodic KdV non-resonant).
In view of the conservation of the spatial mean,
the following Galilean transform (with
\mu(u)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}u(0,x)dx
):

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}(t,x)=u(t,x-2\mu(u)t)-\mu(u)

allows us to reduce the problem to the mean-zero case.
Hence, we assume that, in the periodic case,
all the functions have spatial mean zero in the following.

\boldsymbol{\xi}_{r}=\xi\end{subarray}}\prod_{a\in\mathcal{T}^{0}_{j}\setminus\{r^{(j)}\}}\frac{1}{\min(|\xi_{a_{1}}|,|\xi_{a_{2}}|)^{1+2\varepsilon_{0}}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg\|_{\ell^{\infty}_{\xi}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C^{j}j!\cdot\bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{j-2}(k+2)!\bigg)^{-4\theta}K^{-4(j-2)\theta}\|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}\|_{\ell^{2}_{\xi}}^{j+1}\bigg(\sum_{\zeta\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}}\frac{1}{|\zeta|^{1+2\varepsilon_{0}}}\bigg)^{\frac{j-1}{2}}

as

,
provided that
0<\theta<\frac{1}{2}
.
This proves
(

).

(iii) We now prove the uniform (in

) bound (

), assuming (

).
Fix small

and

.
Then,
given small
\varepsilon_{0}>0

)
that

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{P}_{>\delta^{-\frac{2}{5}+\varepsilon_{0}}}\mathbf{u}_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}}<N^{-\frac{3}{2}}\varepsilon

(6.17)

In the following, we only consider the case

) and (
6.14
)).

\bullet

Case 1:

\max\big(|\xi^{(j)}_{1}|,|\xi^{(j)}_{2}|\big)\leq\delta^{-\frac{2}{5}+\varepsilon_{0}}
.

In this case, we have
|\xi^{(j)}|\lesssim\delta^{-\frac{2}{5}+\varepsilon_{0}}
.

) and (

\displaystyle\big|\Xi_{\text{\rm KdV}}\big(\xi^{(j)},\xi_{1}^{(j)},\xi_{2}^{(j)}\big)-\Xi_{\delta}\big(\xi^{(j)},\xi_{1}^{(j)},\xi_{2}^{(j)}\big)\big|\lesssim\delta^{5\varepsilon_{0}}.

(6.18)

\displaystyle\big|\varphi_{\delta}\big(t,\xi^{(j)},\xi^{(j)}_{1},\xi^{(j)}_{2}\big)\big|\lesssim T\delta^{5\varepsilon_{0}}

appearing in (

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\|\mathbf{1}_{|\xi|\leq N}\cdot\mathcal{E}^{(j)}_{\delta}(t)(\mathbf{u}_{\delta})\|_{\ell^{2}_{\xi}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq N^{\frac{3}{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}C^{j}j!\cdot\bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{j-2}(k+2)!\bigg)^{-4\theta}K^{-4(j-2)\theta}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times\sup_{\mathcal{T}_{j}\in\mathfrak{T}(j)}\bigg\|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathfrak{N}(\mathcal{T}_{j})\\
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{r}=\xi\end{subarray}}\mathbf{1}_{\max(|\xi^{(j)}_{1}|,|\xi^{(j)}_{2}|)\leq\delta^{-\frac{2}{5}+\varepsilon_{0}}}\cdot\big|\varphi_{\delta}\big(t,\xi^{(j)},\xi^{(j)}_{1},\xi^{(j)}_{2}\big)\big|

\displaystyle\hphantom{XXXXXXX}\times\bigg(\prod_{a\in\mathcal{T}^{0}_{j}\setminus\{r^{(j)}\}}\frac{1}{\min(|\xi_{a_{1}}|,|\xi_{a_{2}}|)^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon_{0}}}\bigg)\prod_{a\in\mathcal{T}^{\infty}_{j}}|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{\delta}(\xi_{a})|\bigg\|_{\ell^{\infty}_{\xi}}

j\geq 3

0<\theta<\frac{1}{2}
,
where, in the last step, we used

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}K^{-4(j-2)\theta}R^{j+1}\lesssim K^{4\theta}R^{2}<K^{12\theta}

5.40

\tau=\tau(K)=\tau(R)>0
sufficiently
small

we obtain

\displaystyle\text{LHS of \eqref{NX6}}\leq C\tau TN^{\frac{3}{2}}K^{12\theta}\delta^{5\varepsilon_{0}}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

(6.20)

\bullet

Case 2:

\max\big(|\xi^{(j)}_{1}|,|\xi^{(j)}_{2}|\big)>\delta^{-\frac{2}{5}+\varepsilon_{0}}

In this case, we do not make use

\mathcal{T}^{\infty}_{j}

Therefore,

This concludes the proof of Proposition

7.
Proof of Proposition

In this section, we present a proof of Proposition

As in Section

(except for Part (iii)).

Proof of Proposition

(i)
We only prove

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle\big\|\mathcal{N}^{(j)}_{1}(t)(\mathbf{u})\big\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}}\leq CK^{6-4j\theta}\|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}}^{j+1},

uniformly in

.
Once we have (

) and (

),
the difference estimates
(

) and (

5.5

Let us first establish the following basic estimate:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bigg\|\int_{\xi=\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}}\mathbf{1}_{|\xi\xi_{1}\xi_{2}|\geq\widetilde{K}}\frac{|\xi|}{|\xi\xi_{1}\xi_{2}|}\prod_{k=1}^{2}f_{k}(\xi_{k})d\xi_{1}\bigg\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}}&\lesssim\widetilde{K}^{-\theta}\prod_{k=1}^{2}\|f_{k}\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}},\end{split}

\widetilde{K}\geq 1
,
provided that

By Cauchy-Schwarz’s inequality,
we have

\displaystyle\text{LHS of }\eqref{X1}\lesssim\|A(\xi)\|_{L^{\infty}_{\xi}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{k=1}^{2}\|f_{k}\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}},

\displaystyle A(\xi)=\intt_{\xi=\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}}\mathbf{1}_{|\xi\xi_{1}\xi_{2}|\geq\widetilde{K}}\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{\langle\xi\xi_{1}\xi_{2}\rangle^{2}}d\xi_{1},

First, suppose that

Then, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\eqref{X3}&\lesssim\widetilde{K}^{-2\theta}\intt_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi=\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}\\

\displaystyle\begin{split}\eqref{X3}&\lesssim\widetilde{K}^{-2\theta}\intt_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi=\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}\\

)

7.5

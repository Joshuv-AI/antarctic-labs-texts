Abstract
. Let k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 be a positive integer and let P g P_{g} be the GJMS operator P g P_{g} of order 2 ​ k 2k on a closed Riemannian manifold ( M , g ) (M,g) of dimension n > 2 ​ k n>2k . Introduction

Let
k\geq 1
be an integer and let
(M,g)
be a compact and smooth Riemannian manifold without boundary, that is closed, of dimension
n\geq 2k+1
. We will denote by
P_{g}
the conformally covariant GJMS operator of order
2k
introduced by Graham, Jenne, Mason and Sparling in the seminal work
[
25
]
and by
Q_{g}
the associated
Q
-curvature of order
2k
in
(M,g)
. The purpose of this paper is to prove compactness results for the set of conformal metrics to
g
with constant
Q
-curvature, for any order
k\geq 1
. We first recall a few properties of
P_{g}
. Its construction is based on the Fefferman-Graham ambient metric
[
19
,
20
]
. It is an elliptic self-adjoint differential operator with leading order term
\Delta_{g}^{k}
, where
\Delta_{g}=-\text{div}_{g}(\nabla\cdot)
is the Laplace-Beltrami operator, and is conformally covariant, that is, if
u\in C^{\infty}(M)
,
u>0
and
\hat{g}:=u^{\frac{4}{n-2k}}g
, then

(1.1)

P_{\hat{g}}(f)=u^{-\frac{n+2k}{n-2k}}P_{g}(uf)~\hbox{ for all }f\in C^{\infty}(M).

When
k=1
,
P_{g}
is the celebrated conformal laplacian

P_{g}=\Delta_{g}+\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}S_{g},

where
S_{g}
denotes the scalar curvature of
(M,g)
, while for
k=2
,
P_{g}
is the Paneitz-Branson operator
[
9
,
49
]
.
Explicit formulas for
P_{g}
on any manifold
(M,g)
are only known for low values of
k
, see e.g. Branson
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
, Gover-Peterson
[
24
]
or Paneitz
[
49
]
, and inductive algebraic formulas expressing
P_{g}
a as linear combination of compositions of second-order differential operator were obtained by Juhl
[
36
]
(see also Fefferman-Graham
[
20
]
). When
k\geq 5
, and on any manifold
(M,g)
, there is no explicit expression of
P_{g}
yet, since the complexity of its coefficients increases drastically with
k
. However, in special cases such as Einstein manifolds (see Gover
[
23
]
or again
[
20
]
),
P_{g}
factorizes as a product of second-order operators:

\displaystyle P_{g}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left(\Delta_{g}+\frac{(n+2i-2)(n-2i)}{4n(n-1)}S_{g}\right).

The
Q
-curvature
Q_{g}
associated to
P_{g}
is defined via the zeroth-order terms of
P_{g}
as
Q_{g}:=\frac{2}{n-2k}P_{g}(1)
. When
k=1
,
Q_{g}=S_{g}
(up to a positive constant), and for
k=2
,
Q_{g}
was introducted by Branson and Ørsted
[
8
]
and generalized by Branson
[
11
]
. For more details regarding the
Q
-curvature and its significance, we refer to Juhl
[
35
]
. See also Case-Gover
[
15
]
for a very recent survey.

The question of the existence of a metric conformal to
g
with constant scalar curvature has attracted a lot of attention in recent years and was strongly motivated by the original resolution of the Yamabe problem. In analytical terms, if
u
is a smooth positive function in
M
, a conformal metric
\tilde{g}=u^{\frac{4}{n-2k}}g
has constant
Q
-curvature if and only if
u
, possibly up to scaling by a constant depending on
n
and
k
, is a positive solution of the
2k
-th order
Q
-curvature equation

(1.2)

\displaystyle P_{g}u=u^{2^{*}_{k}-1}\quad\text{ in }M,

where
2^{*}_{k}:=2n/\left(n-2k\right)
is the critical Sobolev exponent. The case
k=1
is the celebrated Yamabe problem
[
69
]
and was solved in full generality by Trudinger
[
65
]
, Aubin
[
4
]
and Schoen
[
64
]
. For
k=2
, existence of a conformal metric with constant
Q
-curvature was proven in Gursky-Malchiodi
[
27
]
assuming
S_{g}\geq 0

and
Q_{g}\geq 0,Q_{g}\not\equiv 0
, and the assumption
S_{g}\geq 0
was later relaxed in Hang-Yang
[
28
]
by assuming the positivity of the Yamabe invariant. Solutions of the constant
Q
-curvature equation are not unique in general: multiplicity results have been obtained by Schoen
[
62
]
when
k=1
and in Alarcon-Petean-Rey
[
1
]
, Andrade-Case-Piccione-Wei
[
3
]
, Batalla-Petean
[
37
]
, Bettiol-Piccione-Sire
[
7
]
when
k\geq 2
(see also the discussion after Theorem 1.3 in
[
16
]
). On the other side, solutions to (
1.2
) are unique if
(M,g)
is Einsten as proven by Obata
[
48
]
when
k=1
and recently generalised by Vétois
[
66
]
for
k=2
. In the case
k=3
partial existence results for (
1.2
) were obtained in Chen-Hou
[
17
]
. For an arbitrary
k<\frac{n}{2}
, the first existence result of constant
Q
-curvature metrics in
[g]
is due to Qing and Raske in
[
56
]
when
(M,g)
is locally conformally flat. For an arbitrary
k<\frac{n}{2}
the more general existence result for (
1.2
) to this day was recently obtained in
[
46
]
assuming a positive mass assumption if
2k+1\leq n\leq 2k+3
and that the Weyl tensor has positive norm somewhere when
n\geq 2k+4
.

In this paper we consider the question of compactness of the full set of conformal metrics with constant positive
Q
-curvature for an arbitrary
1\leq k<\frac{n}{2}
. Analytically speaking, this amounts to showing the compactness in strong spaces of the full set of positive solutions for the
2k
-th order
Q
-curvature equation (
1.2
). Throughout this paper we will assume that
P_{g}
satisfies the following positivity-preserving condition:

(1.3)

Ker
(P_{g})=\{0\}
and the Green’s function
G_{g}
of
P_{g}
is positive in
M
.

We recall that the Green’s function of
P_{g}
is the unique function
G_{g}\in C^{\infty}(M\times M\backslash\{x=y\})
satisfying
P_{g}G_{g}(x,\cdot)=\delta_{x}
for all
x\in M
. We say that
G_{g}
is positive if
G_{g}(x,y)>0
for all
x\neq y
. Assumption (
1.3
) is natural when working with polyharmonic problems: it implies that
P_{g}
is coercive and satisfies the maximum principle, that is, if
u\geq 0
satisfies
P_{g}u>0
in
M
then either
u>0
or
u\equiv 0
in
M
. Unlike in the case
k=1
, the polyharmonic operator
P_{g}
does not satisfy the maximum principle in general when
k\geq 2
. Assumption (
1.3
) is obviously satisfied for the standard round sphere and its finite quotients and examples of product manifolds for which
P_{g}
satisfies (
1.3
) are in Case-Malchiodi
[
16
]
. Note also that if the
k
-th Yamabe invariant satisfies
Y_{2k}(M,g)>0
then Ker
(P_{g})=\{0\}
. For
k=2
, (
1.3
) was shown to hold in
[
28
]
(see also
[
27
]
) provided
(M,g)
is of positive Yamabe type and
Q_{g}\geq 0
in
M
,
Q_{g}\not\equiv 0
.

Our compactness theorem for the constant
Q
-curvature equation (
1.2
) states as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a closed Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
and let
k
be a positive integer such that
2k<n
. Let
P_{g}
be the GJMS operator of order
2k
and assume that it satisfies the positivity preserving condition (
1.3
). Suppose one of the following three assumptions holds:

•

(M,g)
is locally conformally flat and
P_{g}
has positive mass at every point.

•

2k+1\leq n\leq 2k+5
and
P_{g}
has positive mass at every point.

•

n\geq 2k+4
and
\min\limits_{M}|\Weyl_{g}|_{g}>0
, where
\Weyl_{g}
is the Weyl curvature of
(M,g)
.

Let
2<p_{0}<2^{*}_{k}
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
depending only on
n,k,g,p_{0}
such that every positive solution
u\in C^{2k}(M)
of

P_{g}u=u^{p}~\text{ in }M,

with
p_{0}\leq p\leq 2^{*}_{k}
, satisfies

\displaystyle\|u\|_{C^{2k}(M)}+\|1/u\|_{C^{2k}(M)}\leq C.

The positive mass assumption in results like Theorem
1.1
has been known to be crucial to ensure compactness. The mass of
P_{g}
at a point
\xi\in M
is defined, as usual, as the constant term in the expansion of
G_{g}
in conformal normal coordinates at
\xi
. We investigate in detail the Green’s function
G_{g}
of
P_{g}
in Appendix
C
, and we refer to Proposition
C.2
for the definition of the mass and to (
C.3
) for the meaning of our positive mass assumption. Theorem
1.1
strongly suggests that, when
P_{g}
satisfies (
1.3
),
the threshold dimension for the compactness of solutions of (
1.2
) for an arbitrary
k<\frac{n}{2}
diverges as
k\to+\infty
.
To effectively prove this one would need a positive mass theorem for
P_{g}
(at least in dimensions
2k+1\leq n\leq 2k+5
) which, to the best of our knowledge, is not yet available when
k\geq 3
. When
k=1
the mass of
P_{g}
is positive everywhere by the positive mass theorem of Schoen-Yau
[
63
]
, and when
k=2
the mass of
P_{g}
is positive everywhere provided
(M,g)
is of positive Yamabe type and
Q_{g}\geq 0
on
M
,
Q_{g}\not\equiv 0
as proven in Gong-Kim-Wei
[
22
]
. When
k\geq 3
examples of manifolds where (
1.3
) is satisfied and
P_{g}
has positive mass are given by non-trivial finite quotients of the round sphere (see Michel
[
47
, Section 4]
). Obviously, Theorem
1.1
does not apply to the round sphere where the mass of
P_{g}
vanishes everywhere and the set of solutions of (
1.2
) is non-compact by the results in Wei-Xu
[
67
]
.

Compactness results for constant
Q
-curvature equations like (
1.2
) have originated a vast amount of work in the last 30 years. They are structural results that are important on their own but also allow to compute the Leray-Schauder degree of (
1.2
) and to prove strong Morse inqualities, as was e.g. done in Khuri-Marques-Schoen
[
38
]
for the Yamabe equation. We briefly review these compactness results to put Theorem
1.1
in perspective. When
k=1
, compactness for the Yamabe equation was gradually established over several years through the works of Schoen
[
61
]
, Li-Zhu
[
44
]
when
n=3
, Druet
[
18
]
when
n\leq 5
, Marques
[
45
]
when
n\leq 7
, Li-Zhang
[
41
,
42
]
when
n\leq 11
and finally Khuri-Marques-Schoen
[
38
]
when
n\leq 24
. A key ingredient in all these proofs is the positive mass theorem of Schoen-Yau
[
63
]
. Dimension
24
is the threshold dimension for compactness, and examples where non-compactness holds were constructed when
n\geq 25
by Brendle
[
13
]
, and Brendle-Marques
[
12
]
. The case
k=2
was addressed more recently. The first compactness results for (
1.2
) when
k=2
were independently obtained by Qing-Raske
[
55
]
and Hebey-Robert
[
29
]
in the locally conformally flat case. Assuming (
1.3
) and a positive mass assumption, compactness was then shown in Li-Xiong
[
43
]
for
5\leq n\leq 9
. The full compactness of (
1.2
) when
k=2
was recently extended by Gong-Kim-Wei in
[
22
]
to all dimensions
5\leq n\leq 24
. In
[
22
]
the authors also establish a positive mass theorem in the case
k=2
assuming only that
(M,g)
is of positive Yamabe type and
Q_{g}\geq 0
on
M
,
Q_{g}\not\equiv 0
, building up for this on previous work by Humbert-Raulot
[
32
]
and Avalos-Laurain-Lira
[
6
]
. When
k=2
, remarkably,
n=24
again emerges as the threshold dimension for compactness: examples of non-compactness when
n\geq 25
have indeed been known since Wei-Zhao
[
68
]
. In the case
k=3
, the compactness of (
1.2
) has been very recently announced in
[
22
]
in all dimensions
7\leq n\leq 26
, assuming the validity of the positive mass theorem. The author also constructed in
[
22
]
counter-examples to compactness when
n\geq 27
, thus establishing

as the threshold dimension for compactness when

. This series of works thus almost entirely settles the question of compactness for (
1.2
) under assumption (
1.3
) (assuming the positive mass theorem when
k=3
). A key aspect of these works is that determining the threshold dimension for compactness requires to know the explicit algebraic structure of the linearised equation of (
1.2
) at a positive bubble: in
[
38
,
22
]
this is achieved via the explicit expression of, respectively, the Yamabe, Paneitz and sixth-order GJMS operator.

In Theorem
1.1
, by contrast, we establish the compactness of (
1.2
) for an arbitrary
k<\frac{n}{2}
, hence in a setting where an explicit expression of
P_{g}
is not available. We make a crucial use of Juhl’s recursive formulae
[
36
]
for this. As is classical with compactness results like Theorem
1.1
, our proof goes by contradiction and relies first on a local a priori blow-up analysis and then on a local sign restriction argument. We construct a suitable set of concentration points for a blowing-up sequence of solutions of (
1.2
) and we show that there are only finitely many such points and that they are isolated by proving a Weyl vanishing result at concentration points. We then conclude by a global argument, using a Pohozaev identity around each point in combination with the positive mass assumption. This strategy of proof is now well-established: it was pioneered in
[
44
,
18
,
45
,
41
,
42
,
38
]
for the Yamabe equation and then applied when
k=2,3
in
[
43
,
22
]
. In our case, however, the main difficulty is the lack of an explicit expression for
P_{g}
: this is a major obstacle to adapt this strategy of proof to the general polyharmonic case, in particular since proving the Weyl vanishing result typically requires to prove the coercivity of an
explicit
quadratic form arising from Pohozaev identity. We overcome this by decomposing
P_{g}
using Juhl’s recursive formulae
[
36
]
: we crucially use them to obtain an expansion of
P_{g}
at sixth order in conformal normal coordinates around any point in
M
, where fourth-order terms determine the Pohozaev quadratic form and fifth-order ones are antisymmetric. This is done in Proposition
B.1
below. The fourth-order terms that we obtain in this way are however still too cumbersome to be directly dealt with, due to their rising complexity as
k
grows. We overcome this by observing that we actually
do not need
to estimate these terms: we prove indeed that the Pohozaev quadratic form is equal to the formal differentiation of the energy of a single bubbling profile with respect to its concentration parameter, as shown in Proposition
B.3
below (see also (
B.19
)). This is one of the key observations in our work and it is what allows our strategy to work. The energy of a single positive bubbling profile was not known until recently and it was first computed in
[
46
]
, a work we strongly rely on. As a byproduct of our expansion of
P_{g}
we describe the asymptotic behavior of the Green’s function
G_{g}
around any point (see Proposition
C.2
below). The purely analytical side of our proof closely follows the approach for the polyharmonic case that was laid out when
k=2
in
[
43
]
, in particular when it comes to the integral formulation of (
1.2
) to obtain Harnack-type inequalities. We extend the results of
[
43
]
to the polyharmonic setting of an arbitrary

and establish results that will be used in future compactness results for (
1.2
), including a general Pohozaev identity in Appendix

.

covers dimensions up to

since we were only able to use Juhl’s formulae to expand

at sixth order in conformal normal coordinates: as such it should be understood as the analogue for an arbitrary

\det g_{\xi}\left(x\right)=1+\bigO(|x|^{N})

around

\displaystyle\Scal_{g_{\xi}}\left(\xi\right)=0,~\nabla\Scal_{g_{\xi}}\left(\xi\right)=0,~\Ricci_{g_{\xi}}\left(\xi\right)=0,

\mathfrak{c}_{n,k}^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}=b_{n,k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}U^{2^{*}_{k}-1}\,dx.

Let

\displaystyle P_{\exp_{\xi}^{*}g}u(x)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{0}^{k}u(x)+\bigO\big(|x|^{2}|\nabla^{2k}u(x)|_{g}\big)

\displaystyle+\bigO\big(|x||\nabla^{2k-1}u(x)|_{g}\big)+\bigO\Big(\sum_{\ell=0}^{2k-2}|\nabla^{\ell}u(x)|_{g}\Big),

where the constants in the

. In the case where

of (

), (

), (

Throughout this paper,

.

.

3.
Local analysis around a blow-up point

We introduce the setting considered in this paper. We let
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
,
f>0
be fixed. Throughout this paper we will consider a sequence of positive functions
(u_{\alpha})_{\alpha}\in C^{\infty}(M)
satisfying :

(3.1)

P_{g}u_{\alpha}=f^{p_{\alpha}-2^{*}_{k}}~u_{\alpha}^{p_{\alpha}-1}~\hbox{ in }M,

where
p_{\alpha}\leq 2^{*}_{k}
for all
\alpha
and
\lim\limits_{\alpha\to+\infty}p_{\alpha}=2^{*}_{k}
. If
(u_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}(M)
it converges, up to a subsequence, to a positive smooth solution
u
of
P_{g}u=u^{2^{*}_{k}-1}
in
M
. We thus investigate the case where
(u_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
does not have an a priori
L^{\infty}(M)
bound and we assume that it blows-up as
\alpha\to+\infty
, that is

(3.2)

\|u_{\alpha}\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}\to+\infty\quad\text{ as }\alpha\to+\infty.

We follow the strategy of proof in
[
18
,
45
,
41
,
38
,
43
,
22
]
for the Yamabe and Paneitz equations and we perform, in Sections
3
and
4
, an asymptotic analysis of
(u_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
around its concentration points. In our analysis, we adapt the arguments in
[
18
,
45
,
41
,
38
,
43
,
22
]
to a general polyharmonic setting of order
k\geq 1
. In general, no explicit expression of
P_{g}
is available and this is the main obstacle to adapting the proofs of the Weyl vanishing conjecture in
[
38
]
(when
k=1
) and
[
22
]
(when
k=2
) up to the maximal dimension where stability holds. For a general
k\geq 3
we are nevertheless able to adapt the analysis of
[
45
]
to show the vanishing of the Weyl tensor at a concentration point, and this requires a precise expansion of
P_{g}
to first-order in conformal normal coordinates (see Proposition
B.2
below). We begin with the following result, which is inspired from
[
43
, Proposition 7.1]
and constructs a suitable family of critical points of
(u_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
:

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(u_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
be a sequence of positive solutions of (
3.1
) satisfying (
3.2
). Assume that (
1.3
) is satisfied. There exist
N_{\alpha}\geq 1
points
(x_{1,\alpha},\dotsc,x_{N_{\alpha},\alpha})
of
M
satisfying, up to a subsequence,

(1)

\nabla u_{\alpha}(x_{i,\alpha})=0
for
1\leq i\leq N_{\alpha}
,

(2)

d_{g}\left(x_{i,\alpha},x_{j,\alpha}\right)^{\frac{2k}{p_{\alpha}-2}}u_{\alpha}(x_{i,\alpha})\geq 1
for all
1\leq i\neq j\leq N_{\alpha}
, and

(3)

there exists a positive constant
C
independent of
\alpha
such that

(3.3)

\Big(\min_{1\leq i\leq N_{\alpha}}d_{g}\left(x_{i,\alpha},x\right)\Big)^{\frac{2k}{p_{\alpha}-2}}u_{\alpha}(x)\leq C

for any
x\in M
.

Proof.

First, an adaptation of Lemma
6.6
in Hebey
[
30
]
shows that for any
\alpha
there exist
N_{\alpha}\geq 1
critical points
x_{1,\alpha},\dotsc,x_{N_{\alpha},\alpha}
of
u_{\alpha}
that satisfy the following: for any
1\leq i\neq j\leq N_{\alpha}
, one has

d_{g}(x_{i,\alpha},x_{j,\alpha})^{\frac{2k}{p_{\alpha}-2}}u_{\alpha}(x_{i,\alpha})\geq 1

and for any critical point
x
of
u_{\alpha}
, one has

(3.4)

\Big(\min_{1\leq i\leq N_{\alpha}}d_{g}\left(x_{i,\alpha},x\right)\Big)^{\frac{2k}{p_{\alpha}-2}}u_{\alpha}(x)\leq 1.

We prove (
3.3
) by contradiction: we let, up to a subsequence,
y_{\alpha}\in M
be such that

(3.5)

\displaystyle\Big(

\displaystyle\min_{1\leq i\leq N_{\alpha}}d_{g}\left(x_{i,\alpha},y_{\alpha}\right)\Big)^{\frac{2k}{p_{\alpha}-2}}u_{\alpha}(y_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\max_{y\in M}\Big(\min_{1\leq i\leq N_{\alpha}}d_{g}\left(x_{i,\alpha},y\right)\Big)^{\frac{2k}{p_{\alpha}-2}}u_{\alpha}(y)\longrightarrow+\infty

as
\alpha\to+\infty
. Letting
\nu_{\alpha}:=u_{\alpha}(y_{\alpha})^{-(p_{\alpha}-2)/2k}
, (
3.5
) shows that

(3.6)

\frac{1}{\nu_{\alpha}}\left(\min_{1\leq i\leq N_{\alpha}}d_{g}\left(x_{i,\alpha},y_{\alpha}\right)\right)\to+\infty\textrm{ as }\alpha\to+\infty.

For
0<\delta<\frac{1}{2}i_{g}(M)
and
x\in B\left(0,\delta/\nu_{k}\right)
we define

\hat{u}_{\alpha}(x):=\nu_{\alpha}^{\frac{2k}{p_{\alpha}-2}}u_{\alpha}\left(\exp_{y_{\alpha}}^{g}(\nu_{\alpha}x)\right).

Here
B\left(0,\delta/\nu_{k}\right)
denotes the Euclidean ball of centre
0
and radius
\delta/\nu_{k}
. Using (
3.5
) and (
3.6
) we have
\hat{u}_{\alpha}(0)=1
and, for
R>0

|\hat{u}_{\alpha}(x)|\leq 1+\smallo(1)\quad\textrm{for any }x\in B(0,R).

Since
u_{\alpha}
satisfies (
3.1
) it is easily seen that
\hat{u}_{\alpha}
satisfies
P_{g_{\alpha}}\hat{u}_{\alpha}=\hat{f}_{\alpha}^{p_{\alpha}-2_{k}^{*}}\hat{u}_{\alpha}^{p_{\alpha}-1}
in
B(0,\delta/\nu_{k})
, where we have let
\hat{f}_{\alpha}=f\big(\exp_{y_{\alpha}}^{g}(\nu_{\alpha}\cdot)\big)
and
g_{\alpha}=(\exp_{y_{\alpha}}^{g})^{*}g(\nu_{\alpha}\cdot)
. The sequence
(g_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
strongly converges to the euclidean metric in
C^{2k}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

converges in
C^{2k}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
towards a function
\hat{u}_{0}
which satisfies
0\leq\hat{u}_{0}\leq 1
,
\hat{u}_{0}(0)=1
and solves

\Delta_{0}^{k}\hat{u}_{0}=\hat{u}_{0}^{2_{k}^{*}-1}\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Let
x\in B(0,R)
be fixed. Using assumption (
1.3
), a representation formula for
u_{\alpha}
in
M

\exp_{y_{\alpha}}^{g}(\nu_{\alpha}x)
and a simple change of variables yield

\hat{u}_{\alpha}(x)\geq\int_{B(0,R)}\hat{G}_{\alpha}(x,\cdot)\hat{f}_{\alpha}^{p_{\alpha}-2_{k}^{*}}\hat{u}_{\alpha}^{p_{\alpha}-1}dv_{g_{\alpha}},

where we have let
\hat{G}_{\alpha}(x,y)=\nu_{\alpha}^{n-2k}G_{g}\big(\exp_{y_{\alpha}}^{g}(\nu_{\alpha}x),\exp_{y_{\alpha}}^{g}(\nu_{\alpha}y)\big)
. Expansion (
C.2
) below shows that
\hat{G}_{\alpha}(x,y)\to b_{n,k}|x-y|^{2k-n}
pointwise in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{x\}
as
\alpha\to+\infty
, where
b_{n,k}
is given by (
2.6
). Passing to the limit as
\alpha\to+\infty
with Fatou’s lemma then shows that

(3.7)

\hat{u}_{0}(x)\geq\int_{B(0,R)}\frac{b_{n,k}}{|x-y|^{n-2k}}\hat{u}_{0}(y)^{2_{k}^{*}-1}\,dy,

and hence
\hat{u}_{0}(x)>0
since
\hat{u}_{0}\geq 0
and
\hat{u}_{0}(0)=1
. Thus
\hat{u}_{0}>0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and by the classification result of
[
67
]
we have
\hat{u}_{0}=U
, where
U
is given by (
2.4
). The origin is then a non-degenerate critical point of
\hat{u}_{0}
, which implies in particular that for
\alpha
large enough

possesses a critical point
z_{\alpha}\in M
, with
d_{g}(y_{\alpha},z_{\alpha})=\smallo(\nu_{\alpha})
and
\nu_{\alpha}^{2k/(p_{\alpha}-2)}u_{\alpha}(z_{\alpha})=1+\smallo(1)
as
\alpha\to\infty
. But then

\Big(\min_{1\leq i\leq N_{\alpha}}d_{g}\left(x_{i,\alpha},z_{\alpha}\right)\Big)^{\frac{2k}{p_{\alpha}-2}}u_{\alpha}(z_{\alpha})\longrightarrow+\infty

as
\alpha\to\infty
by (
3.6
), which is in contradiction with (
3.4
).
∎

Let
(u_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
a sequence of positive solutions of (
3.1
). Throughout the rest of this section we consider sequences
(x_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
and
(\rho_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
of points in
M
and of positive numbers satisfying

(3.8)

\displaystyle\nabla u_{\alpha}(x_{\alpha})=0

\displaystyle d_{g}(x_{\alpha},x)^{\frac{2k}{p_{\alpha}-2}}u_{\alpha}(x)\leq C\quad\text{ for all }x\in B_{g}(x_{\alpha},8\rho_{\alpha})

for all
\alpha\geq 1
, where

is a fixed constant independent of
\alpha
. Assumptions (

of points constructed in Proposition

when we choose

. For
\alpha\geq 1

\Lambda_{x_{\alpha}}=\Lambda(x_{\alpha},\cdot)

) and (
2

v_{\alpha}=\Lambda^{-1}_{x_{\alpha}}u_{\alpha}.

The conformal invariance of

then shows that
(v_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
is a sequence of smooth positive functions in
M
satisfiying:

(3.10)

P_{g_{\alpha}}v_{\alpha}=f_{\alpha}^{p_{\alpha}-2^{*}_{k}}v_{\alpha}^{p_{\alpha}-1},

where we have let
f_{\alpha}=f\Lambda_{x_{\alpha}}
and, following (

),
g_{\alpha}=\Lambda_{x_{\alpha}}^{\frac{4}{n-2k}}g
. If we let
x_{0}=\lim_{\alpha\to+\infty}x_{\alpha}\in M
, where the limit is taken up to a subsequence, it is easily seen that

strongly converges to
g_{x_{0}}
as

v_{\alpha}
satisfies

(3.11)

\displaystyle v_{\alpha}(x_{\alpha})=u_{\alpha}(x_{\alpha}),\quad\nabla v_{\alpha}(x_{\alpha})=0,

\displaystyle d_{g_{\alpha}}(x_{\alpha},x)^{\frac{2k}{p_{\alpha}-2}}v_{\alpha}(x)\leq C\quad\text{ for all }x\in B_{g_{\alpha}}(x_{\alpha},8\rho_{\alpha}),

where

. We will assume that
(x_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
and
(\rho_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
are chosen in addition to satisfy

(3.12)

\rho_{\alpha}^{\frac{2k}{p_{\alpha}-2}}\max_{B_{g_{\alpha}}(x_{\alpha},4\rho_{\alpha})}v_{\alpha}\to+\infty\quad\text{ as }\alpha\to+\infty,

which implies in particular that
(v_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
blows-up as

is compact. Throughout this section we assume that (

(3.13)

\mu_{\alpha}=v_{\alpha}(x_{\alpha})^{-\frac{2k}{p_{\alpha}-2}}=u_{\alpha}(x_{\alpha})^{-\frac{2k}{p_{\alpha}-2}}.

The following result is standard:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(v_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
be a sequence of positive solutions of (

(\rho_{\alpha})_{\alpha}

3.11

, where

Let

be such that

It is easily seen that
\hat{v}_{\alpha}
satisfies

\hat{f}_{\alpha}=f_{\alpha}\big(\exp_{y_{\alpha}}^{g_{\alpha}}(\nu_{\alpha}\cdot)\big)

\hat{g}_{\alpha}=(\exp_{y_{\alpha}}^{g_{\alpha}})^{*}g(\nu_{\alpha}\cdot)

\hat{y}_{0}=\lim_{\alpha\to+\infty}\frac{1}{\nu_{\alpha}}(\exp_{x_{\alpha}}^{g_{\alpha}})^{-1}(y_{\alpha})

strongly converges to the euclidean metric in

converges in

\hat{v}_{0}
which satisfies

\hat{v}_{0}(\hat{y}_{0})=1

\Delta_{0}^{k}\hat{v}_{0}=\hat{v}_{0}^{2_{k}^{*}-1}\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n},

Arguing as in the proof of (

The next result is a version of the maximum principle adapted to (
3.10

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(v_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
be a sequence of positive solutions of (
3.10
) and let
(x_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
and
(\rho_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
satisfy (
3.11
) and (

). Assume that (

) holds. There exists a positive constant

such that the following holds: for any sequence
(s_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
of positive numbers satisfying

, we have, for
\alpha\geq 1

\sum_{\ell=0}^{2k}s_{\alpha}^{\ell}\|\nabla^{\ell}v_{\alpha}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\alpha})}\leq C\min_{\Omega_{\alpha}}v_{\alpha},

where we have let
\Omega_{\alpha}=B_{g_{\alpha}}(x_{\alpha,}3s_{\alpha})\backslash B_{g_{\alpha}}(x_{\alpha},s_{\alpha}/3)

Proof.

As before we let
G_{g_{\alpha}}
be the Green’s function of
P_{g_{\alpha}}
in
M
. It is positive by (

) and has a uniform lower bound in
M
by (

x\in\Omega_{\alpha}=B_{g_{\alpha}}(x_{\alpha,}3s_{\alpha})\backslash B_{g_{\alpha}}(x_{\alpha},s_{\alpha}/3)

v_{\alpha}
satisfies (
3.10

v_{\alpha}
in
M

v_{\alpha}(x)=\int_{B_{g_{\alpha}}(x_{\alpha},4s_{\alpha})\backslash B_{g_{\alpha}}(x_{\alpha},s_{\alpha}/4)}G_{g_{\alpha}}(x,\cdot)f_{\alpha}^{p_{\alpha}-2^{*}_{k}}v_{\alpha}^{p_{\alpha}-1}dv_{g_{\alpha}}+h_{1,\alpha}(x)+h_{2,\alpha}(x),

where we have let

\displaystyle h_{1,\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{M\backslash B_{g_{\alpha}}(x_{\alpha},4s_{\alpha})}G_{g_{\alpha}}(x,\cdot)f_{\alpha}^{p_{\alpha}-2^{*}_{k}}v_{\alpha}^{p_{\alpha}-1}dv_{g_{\alpha}}\quad\text{ and }

\displaystyle h_{2,\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{g_{\alpha}}(x_{\alpha},s_{\alpha}/4)}G_{g_{\alpha}}(x,\cdot)f_{\alpha}^{p_{\alpha}-2^{*}_{k}}v_{\alpha}^{p_{\alpha}-1}dv_{g_{\alpha}}

Let

. It is easily seen that there exists

Differentiating under the integral and using (

\sum_{\ell=0}^{2k}s_{\alpha}^{\ell}\big|\nabla^{\ell}h_{i,\alpha}(x)\big|_{g_{\alpha}}\leq Ch_{i,\alpha}(y)

and

v_{\alpha}(x)=\int_{B_{g_{\alpha}}(x_{\alpha},4s_{\alpha})\backslash B_{g_{\alpha}}(x_{\alpha},s_{\alpha}/4)}G_{g_{\alpha}}(x,\cdot)V_{\alpha}v_{\alpha}dv_{g_{\alpha}}+h_{1,\alpha}(x)+h_{2,\alpha}(x),

where

\displaystyle P_{\widehat{g}_{\alpha}}w_{\alpha}=

\displaystyle\left(\widehat{f}_{\alpha}^{~p_{\alpha}-2^{*}_{k}}-1\right)\widehat{v}_{\alpha}^{~p_{\alpha}-1}+\left(\widehat{v}_{\alpha}^{~p_{\alpha}-1}-U^{p_{\alpha}-1}\right)+\left(U^{p_{\alpha}-1}-U^{2^{*}_{k}-1}\right)

\displaystyle+\left(U^{2^{*}_{k}-1}-P_{\widehat{g}_{\alpha}}U\right).

\displaystyle\left(\widehat{f}_{\alpha}^{~p_{\alpha}-2^{*}_{k}}-1\right)\widehat{v}_{\alpha}^{~p_{\alpha}-1}+U^{p_{\alpha}-1}-U^{2^{*}_{k}-1}

Next, for

\displaystyle\widehat{v}_{\alpha}^{~p_{\alpha}-1}-U^{p_{\alpha}-1}

\displaystyle=\left(p_{\alpha}-1\right)U^{p_{\alpha}-2}w_{\alpha}+\bigO\left(U^{p_{\alpha}-2-\theta}|w_{\alpha}|^{1+\theta}\right).

This is uniform in

|w_{\alpha}(x)|\lesssim U(x)
in
B(0,2\mu_{\alpha}^{-1}r_{\alpha})
by (
3.17
). A change of variables in (

\displaystyle\left|P_{\widehat{g}_{\alpha}}U(x)-U(x)^{2^{*}_{k}-1}\right|=\bigO\left(\mu_{\alpha}^{4}\left(1+|x|\right)^{4-n}\right).

Collecting all the terms we have obtained that

\displaystyle P_{\widehat{g}_{\alpha}}w_{\alpha}=

\displaystyle\left(p_{\alpha}-1\right)U^{p_{\alpha}-2}w_{\alpha}+\bigO\left(U^{p_{\alpha}-2-\theta}|w_{\alpha}|^{1+\theta}\right)

(4.9)

\displaystyle+\bigO\left((2^{*}_{k}-p_{\alpha})U^{2^{*}_{k}-1}|\ln U|\right)+\bigO\left(\mu_{\alpha}^{4}\left(1+|x|\right)^{4-n}\right).

Thus we have in particular

(4.10)

|P_{\widehat{g}_{\alpha}}w_{\alpha}|\lesssim U^{p_{\alpha}-2}|w_{\alpha}|+(2^{*}_{k}-p_{\alpha})U^{2^{*}_{k}-1}|\ln U|+\mu_{\alpha}^{4}\left(1+|x|\right)^{4-n}.

A simple change of variables shows that

\widehat{G}_{\alpha}(x,y):=\mu_{\alpha}^{n-2k}G_{\alpha}\left(\exp_{x_{\alpha}}^{g_{\alpha}}(\mu_{\alpha}x),\exp_{x_{\alpha}}^{g_{\alpha}}(\mu_{\alpha}y)\right)

P_{\widehat{g}_{\alpha}}
in

) below. We can then write a representation formula for
w_{\alpha}
in
B(0,2\mu_{\alpha}^{-1}r_{\alpha})
. Let
y_{\alpha}\in B(0,\mu_{\alpha}^{-1}r_{\alpha})
be a sequence of points. Using (
4.6

\displaystyle|w_{\alpha}(y_{\alpha})|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{B(0,2\mu_{\alpha}^{-1}r_{\alpha})}|y_{\alpha}-y|^{2k-n}U(y)^{p_{\alpha}-2}|w_{\alpha}(y)|\,dy

\displaystyle~+(2^{*}_{k}-p_{\alpha})\int_{B(0,2\mu_{\alpha}^{-1}r_{\alpha})}|y_{\alpha}-y|^{2k-n}U(y)^{2^{*}_{k}-1}|\ln U(y)|\,dy

(4.11)

\displaystyle~+\mu_{\alpha}^{4}\int_{B(0,2\mu_{\alpha}^{-1}r_{\alpha})}|y_{\alpha}-y|^{2k-n}\left(1+|y|\right)^{4-n}\,dy+\left(\frac{\mu_{\alpha}}{r_{\alpha}}\right)^{n-2k}.

Using Lemma

(4.12)

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,2\mu_{\alpha}^{-1}r_{\alpha})}|y_{\alpha}-y|^{2k-n}\left(U(y)^{2^{*}_{k}-1}|\ln U(y)|\right)\,dy\lesssim U(y_{\alpha})

\displaystyle\mu_{\alpha}^{4}\int_{B(0,2\mu_{\alpha}^{-1}r_{\alpha})}|y_{\alpha}-y|^{2k-n}\left(1+|y|\right)^{4-n}\,dy\lesssim\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}(y_{\alpha}),

&\mu_{\alpha}^{4}\ln\left(1+\frac{r_{\alpha}}{\mu_{\alpha}}\right)&&\text{if }n=2k+4\\
&\mu_{\alpha}^{4}\left(1+|x|\right)^{2k+4-n}&&\text{if }n>2k+4\end{aligned}\right..

Thus, (

(4.15)

\displaystyle|w_{\alpha}(y_{\alpha})|\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,2\mu_{\alpha}^{-1}r_{\alpha})}|y_{\alpha}-y|^{2k-n}U^{p_{\alpha}-2}|w_{\alpha}|\,dy

\displaystyle+(2^{*}_{k}-p_{\alpha})U(y_{\alpha})+\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}(y_{\alpha})+\left(\frac{\mu_{\alpha}}{r_{\alpha}}\right)^{n-2k}.

To estimate the integral in (

\displaystyle|w_{\alpha}(y_{\alpha})|\lesssim

\displaystyle~\|w_{\alpha}\|_{L^{\infty}(B(0,2\mu^{-1}_{\alpha}r_{\alpha}))}\left(\left(1+|y_{\alpha}|\right)^{2k-(p_{\alpha}-2)(n-2k)}+B_{0}(y_{\alpha})\right)

\displaystyle+(2^{*}_{k}-p_{\alpha})U(y_{\alpha})+\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}(y_{\alpha})+\left(\frac{\mu_{\alpha}}{r_{\alpha}}\right)^{n-2k}.

Appendix A
Pohozaev identity for
\Delta_{0}^{k}

We extend the Pohozaev identity in Proposition 2.2 of
[
43
]
to all integers
k\geq 1
. Recall that
\Delta_{0}:=-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\partial^{2}_{ii}
is the Euclidean Laplacian. We let

\Delta_{0}^{k/2}u:=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Delta_{0}^{\frac{k}{2}}u&&\text{if $k$ is even},\\
&\nabla\Delta_{0}^{\frac{k-1}{2}}u&&\text{if $k$ is odd}.\end{aligned}\right.

Proposition A.1
.

Let
0\leq s<r
and
p\geq 2
. Let
u\in C^{2k}(\overline{B(0,r)})
and
f\in C^{1}(\overline{B(0,r)})
and denote
\mathcal{E}(u):=\Delta_{0}^{k}u-f|u|^{p-2}u
. Then we have

(A.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{k}(r;u)-\mathcal{P}_{k}(s;u)=\int_{B(0,r)\backslash B(0,s)}\left(\frac{n-2k}{2}u+x^{i}\partial_{i}u\right)\mathcal{E}(u)\,dx

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{n-2k}{2}-\frac{n}{p}\right)\int_{B(0,r)\backslash B(0,s)}f\,|u|^{p}\,dx-\frac{1}{p}\int_{B(0,r)\backslash B(0,s)}x^{i}\partial_{i}f\,|u|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle+\frac{r}{p}\int_{\partial B(0,r)}f|u|^{p}\,d\sigma-\frac{s}{p}\int_{\partial B(0,s)}f|u|^{p}\,d\sigma.

Here
\mathcal{P}_{k}(r;u)
denotes boundary terms whose expression is given by:

(A.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{k}(r;u)=\mathcal{R}_{k}(r;u)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=0}^{[k/2]-1}\Bigg[\frac{n-2k}{2}\int_{\partial B(0,r)}\bigg(\partial_{\nu}(\Delta_{0}^{i}u)\,\Delta_{0}^{k-i-1}u\,-\Delta_{0}^{i}u~\partial_{\nu}(\Delta_{0}^{k-i-1}u)\bigg)\,d\sigma

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial B(0,r)}\bigg(\partial_{\nu}(\Delta_{0}^{i}(x^{a}\partial_{a}u))\,\Delta_{0}^{k-i-1}u\,-\Delta_{0}^{i}(x^{a}\partial_{a}u)\,\partial_{\nu}(\Delta_{0}^{k-i-1}u)\bigg)\,d\sigma\Bigg].

In (
A.2
) we have let

\mathcal{R}_{k}(r;u)=\frac{r}{2}\int_{\partial B(0,r)}(\Delta_{0}^{k/2}u)^{2}d\sigma\quad\text{ if }k\text{ is even }\\

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{k}(r;u)

\displaystyle=\frac{r}{2}\int_{\partial B(0,r)}(\Delta_{0}^{\frac{k+1}{2}}u)(\Delta_{0}^{\frac{k-1}{2}}u)\,d\sigma+\frac{n-2k}{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}\Delta_{0}^{\frac{k-1}{2}}u\partial_{\nu}(\Delta_{0}^{\frac{k-1}{2}}u)\,d\sigma

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial B(0,r)}\bigg(\Delta_{0}^{\frac{k-1}{2}}u\,\partial_{\nu}(x^{a}\partial_{a}(\Delta_{0}^{\frac{k-1}{2}}u))\,-(x^{a}\partial_{a}(\Delta_{0}^{\frac{k-1}{2}}u))\partial_{\nu}(\Delta_{0}^{\frac{k-1}{2}}u)\bigg)\,d\sigma

\displaystyle\text{ if }k\text{ is odd. }

In the previous expressions,
[x]
denotes the integer part of
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Note that the expression of
\mathcal{R}_{k}(r;u)
depends on the parity of
k
.

Proof.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
denote a bounded smooth domain. We follow the arguments in Gazzola-Grunau-Sweers
[
21
, Chapter 7]
but without imposing boundary conditions in
\partial\Omega
. We use the convention that repeated indices are summed over, so that
x^{i}\partial_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x^{i}\partial_{i}
. We will need two preliminary computations. First, repeated integration by parts show that for any
u,v\in C^{2k}(\overline{\Omega})
,

(A.3)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u\Delta_{0}^{k}v~dx=\int_{\Omega}(\Delta_{0}^{k/2}u,\Delta_{0}^{k/2}v)\,dx-\underbrace{\int_{\partial\Omega}\Delta_{0}^{\left[\frac{k}{2}\right]}u~\partial_{\nu}\Delta_{0}^{\left[\frac{k}{2}\right]}v\,d\sigma}_{\text{if $k$ is odd, $0$ otherwise}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=0}^{\left[k/2\right]-1}\int_{\partial\Omega}\bigg(\partial_{\nu}\Delta_{0}^{i}u\,\Delta_{0}^{k-1-i}v\,-\,\Delta_{0}^{i}u~\partial_{\nu}\Delta_{0}^{k-1-i}v\bigg)\,d\sigma.

Here
\nu
denotes the outer unit normal. Direct computations show that
\Delta_{0}(x^{i}\partial_{i}u)=x^{i}\partial_{i}\Delta_{0}u+2\Delta_{0}u
. Iterating gives

(A.4)

\Delta_{0}^{k}(x^{i}\partial_{i}u)=x^{i}\partial_{i}\Delta_{0}^{k}u+2k\Delta_{0}^{k}u.

Using (
A.3
) we have

(A.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{n-2k}{2}u\Delta_{0}^{k}u\,dx=\frac{n-2k}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\Delta_{0}^{k/2}u|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle-\frac{n-2k}{2}\underbrace{\int_{\partial\Omega}\Delta_{0}^{\left[\frac{k}{2}\right]}u~\partial_{\nu}\Delta_{0}^{\left[\frac{k}{2}\right]}u\,d\sigma}_{\text{if $k$ is odd, $0$ otherwise}}

\displaystyle+\frac{n-2k}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{[k/2]-1}\int_{\partial\Omega}\bigg(\partial_{\nu}(\Delta_{0}^{i}u)\Delta_{0}^{k-i-1}u\,-\Delta_{0}^{i}u\,\partial_{\nu}(\Delta_{0}^{k-i-1}u)\bigg)\,d\sigma.

Independently, direct computations give

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(x^{i}\partial_{i}u+\frac{n-2k}{2}u\right)f|u|^{p-2}u~dx=\frac{n-2k}{2}\int_{\Omega}f|u|^{p}~dx+\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega}f~x^{i}\partial_{i}|u|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{n-2k}{2}-\frac{n}{p}\right)\int_{\Omega}f|u|^{p}\,dx-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega}x^{i}\partial_{i}f~|u|^{p}\,dx+\frac{1}{p}\int_{\partial\Omega}(x,\nu)f|u|^{p}\,d\sigma.

Combining the latter computations then gives

(A.6)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(x^{i}\partial_{i}u+\frac{n-2k}{2}u\right)\mathcal{E}(u)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}x^{i}\partial_{i}u~\Delta_{0}^{k}u\,dx

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{n-2k}{2}-\frac{n}{p}\right)\int_{\Omega}f|u|^{p}\,dx+\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega}x^{i}\partial_{i}f~|u|^{p}\,dx-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\partial\Omega}(x,\nu)f|u|^{p}\,d\sigma

\displaystyle+\frac{n-2k}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\Delta_{0}^{k/2}u|^{2}\,dx-\frac{n-2k}{2}\underbrace{\int_{\partial\Omega}\Delta_{0}^{\left[\frac{k}{2}\right]}u~\partial_{\nu}\Delta_{0}^{\left[\frac{k}{2}\right]}u\,d\sigma}_{\text{if $k$ is odd, $0$ otherwise}}

\displaystyle+\frac{n-2k}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{[k/2]-1}\int_{\partial\Omega}\bigg(\partial_{\nu}(\Delta_{0}^{i}u)\Delta_{0}^{k-i-1}u\,-\Delta_{0}^{i}u\,\partial_{\nu}(\Delta_{0}^{k-i-1}u)\bigg)\,d\sigma.

Case 1:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}x^{i}\partial_{i}u~\Delta_{0}^{k}u\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\Delta_{0}^{k/2}(x^{i}\partial_{i}u)\,\Delta_{0}^{k/2}u\,dx+\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k/2-1}\int_{\partial\Omega}\bigg(\partial_{\nu}\Delta_{0}^{i}(x^{\ell}\partial_{\ell}u)\,\Delta_{0}^{k-1-i}u

The first integral is computed using (

Case 2:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}x^{i}\partial_{i}u\,\Delta_{0}^{k}u=\int_{\Omega}\Delta_{0}^{\frac{k-1}{2}}(x^{i}\partial_{i}u)\,\Delta_{0}^{\frac{k+1}{2}}u\,dx+\sum_{i=0}^{(k-1)/2-1}\int_{\partial\Omega}\bigg(\partial_{\nu}\Delta_{0}^{i}(x^{\ell}\partial_{\ell}u)\times

\displaystyle\Delta_{0}^{k-1-i}u-\Delta_{0}^{i}(x^{\ell}\partial_{\ell}u)~\partial_{\nu}\Delta_{0}^{k-1-i}u\bigg)\,d\sigma.

\displaystyle+(k-1)\int_{\Omega}|\Delta_{0}^{k/2}u|^{2}\,dx-(k-1)\int_{\partial\Omega}\Delta_{0}^{\frac{k-1}{2}}u\,\partial_{\nu}\Delta_{0}^{\frac{k-1}{2}}u\,d\sigma.

We next calculate, integrating by parts,

(A.7)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}x^{i}\partial_{i}(\Delta_{0}^{\frac{k-1}{2}}u)\,\Delta_{0}^{\frac{k+1}{2}}u~dx

\displaystyle=-n\int_{\Omega}\Delta_{0}^{\frac{k-1}{2}}u\,\Delta_{0}^{\frac{k+1}{2}}u\,dx-\int_{\Omega}x^{i}\partial_{i}(\Delta_{0}^{\frac{k+1}{2}}u)\,\Delta_{0}^{\frac{k-1}{2}}u\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial\Omega}(x,\nu)\,\Delta_{0}^{\frac{k-1}{2}}u\,\Delta_{0}^{\frac{k+1}{2}}u\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=-n\int_{\Omega}|\Delta_{0}^{k/2}u|^{2}\,dx+n\int_{\partial\Omega}\Delta_{0}^{\frac{k-1}{2}}u\partial_{\nu}\Delta_{0}^{\frac{k-1}{2}}ud\sigma

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}x^{i}\partial_{i}(\Delta_{0}^{\frac{k+1}{2}}u)\,\Delta_{0}^{\frac{k-1}{2}}u\,dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}(x,\nu)\,\Delta_{0}^{\frac{k-1}{2}}u\,\Delta_{0}^{\frac{k+1}{2}}u\,d\sigma.

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}x^{i}\partial_{i}(\Delta_{0}^{\frac{k+1}{2}}u)\,\Delta_{0}^{\frac{k-1}{2}}u\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}x^{i}\partial_{i}(\Delta_{0}^{\frac{k-1}{2}}u)\,\Delta_{0}^{\frac{k+1}{2}}u\,dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}\Big[(x^{i}\partial_{i}\Delta_{0}^{\frac{k-1}{2}}u)\partial_{\nu}\Delta_{0}^{\frac{k-1}{2}}u-\Delta_{0}^{\frac{k-1}{2}}u\,\partial_{\nu}(x^{i}\partial_{i}\Delta_{0}^{\frac{k-1}{2}}u)\Big]\,d\sigma

\displaystyle-2\int_{\Omega}|\Delta_{0}^{k/2}u|^{2}\,dx+2\int_{\partial\Omega}\Delta_{0}^{\frac{k-1}{2}}u\partial_{\nu}\Delta_{0}^{\frac{k-1}{2}}ud\sigma.

Combining the latter with (
A.7

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\frac{n-2k}{2}\widetilde{W}_{\mu}+x^{i}\partial_{i}\widetilde{W}_{\mu}\right)(P_{\mathfrak{g}}\widetilde{W}_{\mu}-\Delta_{0}^{k}\,\widetilde{W}_{\mu})\,dx

\displaystyle+\bigO(\mu^{n-2k}).

\displaystyle=-\mu\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big|\Delta_{0}^{\frac{k}{2}}\widetilde{W}_{\mu}\big|_{\xi}^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\bigO(\mu^{n-2k}),

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\frac{n-2k}{2}\widetilde{W}_{\mu}+x^{i}\partial_{i}\widetilde{W}_{\mu}\right)(P_{\mathfrak{g}}\widetilde{W}_{\mu}-\Delta_{0}^{k}\,\widetilde{W}_{\mu})\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\frac{n-2k}{2}\widetilde{W}_{\mu}+x^{i}\partial_{i}\widetilde{W}_{\mu}\right)P_{\mathfrak{g}}\widetilde{W}_{\mu}\,dx+\bigO(\mu^{n-2k})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\,\mu\frac{d}{d\mu}\left(\int_{M}W_{\xi,\mu}P_{g_{\xi}}W_{\xi,\mu}\,dv_{g_{\xi}}\right)+\bigO(\mu^{n-2k}),

where the last line follows from (

\displaystyle I_{k,g_{\xi}}(W_{\xi,\mu})=\|U\|_{L^{2^{*}_{k}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{4k}{n-2k}}

(B.21)

\displaystyle~-\,\mathcal{C}(n,k)\times\left\{\begin{aligned} &|\Weyl_{g}(\xi)|^{2}\mu^{4}\ln(1/\mu)+\bigO(\mu^{4})&&\text{if }n=2k+4\\
&|\Weyl_{g}(\xi)|^{2}\mu^{4}+\smallo(\mu^{4})&&\text{if }n=2k+5,\end{aligned}\right.

Appendix D
A technical lemma

We state and prove a frequently used integral lemma:

Lemma D.1

(A global Giraud type lemma)
.

Let
1\leq p<n
and
q<n
be integers. Let
(\rho_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
be a sequence of positive numbers with
\rho_{\alpha}\to+\infty
as
\alpha\to+\infty
. Let
\xi_{\alpha}\in B(0,\rho_{\alpha})
be a sequence of points. We have

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,\rho_{\alpha})}(1+|z|)^{q-n}

\displaystyle|\xi_{\alpha}-z|^{p-n}\,dz

(D.1)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\{\begin{aligned} &~\rho_{\alpha}^{\,p+q-n}&&\text{if }n<p+q,\\
&\ln\left(2+\rho_{\alpha}\right)&&\text{if }n=p+q,\\
&\left(1+|\xi_{\alpha}|\right)^{p+q-n}&&\text{if }n>p+q\text{ and }q>0,\\
&\left(1+|\xi_{\alpha}|\right)^{p-n}\ln\left(2+|\xi_{\alpha}|\right)&&\text{if }q=0,\\
&\left(1+|\xi_{\alpha}|\right)^{p-n}&&\text{if }q<0.\end{aligned}\right.

Proof.

For
z\in B(0,\rho_{\alpha})
we let
\mathcal{K}(\xi_{\alpha},z):=(1+|z|)^{q-n}|\xi_{\alpha}-z|^{p-n}
.

Assume first that
n\leq p+q
. We decompose the integral in the three regions
B(\xi_{\alpha},|\xi_{\alpha}|/2)
,
B(\xi_{\alpha},3|\xi_{\alpha}|/2)\setminus B(\xi_{\alpha},|\xi_{\alpha}|/2)
and
B(0,\rho_{\alpha})\setminus B(\xi_{\alpha},3|\xi_{\alpha}|/2)
. First, if
z\in B(\xi_{\alpha},|\xi_{\alpha}|/2)
we have
|z-\xi_{\alpha}|\leq|\xi_{\alpha}|/2\leq|z|
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{B(\xi_{\alpha},|\xi_{\alpha}|/2)}\mathcal{K}(\xi_{\alpha},z)\,dz\leq\int_{B(\xi_{\alpha},|\xi_{\alpha}|/2)}\left(1+|\xi_{\alpha}-z|\right)^{q-n}|\xi_{\alpha}-z|^{p-n}\,dz

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{|\xi_{\alpha}|/2}\frac{r^{p-1}}{(1+r)^{n-q}}\,dr\lesssim\left\{\begin{aligned} &|\xi_{\alpha}|^{p+q-n}&&\text{if }n<p+q,\\
&\ln\left(1+|\xi_{\alpha}|\right)&&\text{if }n=p+q.\end{aligned}\right.

If now
z\in B(\xi_{\alpha},3|\xi_{\alpha}|/2)\setminus B(\xi_{\alpha},|\xi_{\alpha}|/2)
we have
|z|\leq 3|z-\xi_{\alpha}|
. Therefore

\displaystyle\int_{B(\xi_{\alpha},3|\xi_{\alpha}|/2)\setminus B(\xi_{\alpha},|\xi_{\alpha}|/2)}\mathcal{K}(\xi_{\alpha},z)\,dz\lesssim\int_{B(0,9|\xi_{\alpha}|/2)}\left(1+|z|\right)^{q-n}|z|^{p-n}\,dz

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{9|\xi_{\alpha}|/2}\frac{r^{p-1}}{(1+r)^{n-q}}dr\lesssim\left\{\begin{aligned} &|\xi_{\alpha}|^{p+q-n}&&\text{if }n<p+q,\\
&\ln\left(1+|\xi_{\alpha}|\right)&&\text{if }n=p+q.\end{aligned}\right.

If finally
z\in B(0,\rho_{\alpha})\setminus B(\xi_{\alpha},3|\xi_{\alpha}|/2)
we have
|z-\xi_{\alpha}|\leq 3|z|\leq 5|z-\xi_{\alpha}|
and therefore

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,\rho_{\alpha})\setminus B(\xi_{\alpha},3|\xi_{\alpha}|/2)}\mathcal{K}(\xi_{\alpha},z)\,dz\lesssim\int_{B(0,\rho_{\alpha})\setminus B(0,|\xi_{\alpha}|/2)}\left(1+|z|\right)^{q-n}|z|^{p-n}\,dz

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{|\xi_{\alpha}|/2}^{\rho_{\alpha}}\frac{r^{p-1}}{(1+r)^{n-q}}\,dr\lesssim\left\{\begin{aligned} &\rho_{\alpha}^{~p+q-n}&&\text{if }n<p+q\\
&\ln\left(1+\rho_{\alpha}\right)&&\text{if }n=p+q.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

Assume now that
n>p+q
. We write this time

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,\rho_{\alpha})}\mathcal{K}(\xi_{\alpha},z)\,dz

\displaystyle=\left(1+|\xi_{\alpha}|\right)^{p+q-n}\int_{B\left(0,\frac{\rho_{\alpha}}{1+|\xi_{\alpha}|}\right)}\left|\frac{\xi_{\alpha}}{1+|\xi_{\alpha}|}-z\right|^{p-n}\left(\frac{1}{1+|\xi_{\alpha}|}+|z|\right)^{q-n}\,dz

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(1+|\xi_{\alpha}|\right)^{p+q-n}\int_{B\left(\frac{\xi_{\alpha}}{1+|\xi_{\alpha}|},\frac{1}{2}\right)}\left|\frac{\xi_{\alpha}}{1+|\xi_{\alpha}|}-z\right|^{p-n}\,dz\,+\left(1+|\xi_{\alpha}|\right)^{p+q-n}

\displaystyle\times\int_{B\left(0,1/2\right)}\left(\frac{1}{1+|\xi_{\alpha}|}+|z|\right)^{q-n}\,dz\,+\left(1+|\xi_{\alpha}|\right)^{p+q-n}\times

\displaystyle\int_{B\left(0,\frac{\rho_{\alpha}}{1+|\xi_{\alpha}|}\right)\setminus B(0,1/2)\cup B\left(\frac{\xi_{\alpha}}{1+|\xi_{\alpha}|},\frac{1}{2}\right)}\left|\frac{\xi_{\alpha}}{1+|\xi_{\alpha}|}-z\right|^{p-n}\left(\frac{1}{1+|\xi_{\alpha}|}+|z|\right)^{q-n}\,dz

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(1+|\xi_{\alpha}|\right)^{p+q-n}\left(~1+\int_{0}^{1/2}\frac{r^{n-1}}{\left(\frac{1}{1+|\xi_{\alpha}|}+r\right)^{n-q}}\,dr+\int_{1/2}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{r^{n-p-q}}\,dr\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(1+|\xi_{\alpha}|\right)^{p+q-n}\left(~1+\left(\frac{1}{1+|\xi_{\alpha}|}\right)^{q}\int_{0}^{\left(1+|\xi_{\alpha}|\right)/2}\frac{r^{n-1}}{\left(1+r\right)^{n-q}}\,dr\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(1+|\xi_{\alpha}|\right)^{p+q-n}+\left(1+|\xi_{\alpha}|\right)^{p+q-n}\times\left\{\begin{aligned} &~1&&\text{if }q>0\\
&\ln\left(2+|\xi_{\alpha}|\right)&&\text{if }q=0\\
&\left(1+|\xi_{\alpha}|\right)^{-q}&&\text{if }q<0\end{aligned}\right.

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\{\begin{aligned} &\left(1+|\xi_{\alpha}|\right)^{p+q-n}&&\text{if }q>0\\
&\left(1+|\xi_{\alpha}|\right)^{p-n}\ln\left(2+|\xi_{\alpha}|\right)&&\text{if }q=0\\
&\left(1+|\xi_{\alpha}|\right)^{p-n}&&\text{if }q<0.\end{aligned}\right.

∎

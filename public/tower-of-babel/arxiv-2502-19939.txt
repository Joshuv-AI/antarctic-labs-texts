Composition-differentiation operators on Hardy-Hilbert space of Dirichlet series
Vasudevarao Allu, Dipon Kumar Mondal

Abstract
. In this paper, we establish a compactness criterion for the composition-differentiation operator D Φ D_{\Phi} on the Hardy space ℋ 2 \mathcal{H}^{2} of Dirichlet series in terms of a boundary decay condition for its mean counting function. Via a comparison-type principle, we show that this boundary decay is equivalent to a corresponding decay condition for the Green’s function, which we further analyze through harmonic measure. We provide explicit mapping properties of the symbol Φ \Phi that generate a bounded composition-differentiation operator D Φ D_{\Phi} and obtain precise norm estimates for D Φ D_{\Phi} when Φ \Phi is an affine symbol with a single-prime in the class 𝒢 0 \mathcal{G}_{0} . Furthermore, we establish explicit upper and lower bounds for the approximation numbers of D Φ D_{\Phi} on ℋ 2 \mathcal{H}^{2} motivated by the work of Queffélec and Seip [J. Funct. Anal., 2015]. Finally, we investigate spectral and operator-theoretic properties of D Φ D_{\Phi} for symbols in 𝒢 0 \mathcal{G}_{0} .

Introduction

For a real number
\theta
, we denote the open right half-plane by
\mathbb{C}_{\theta}=\{s\in\mathbb{C}\colon\operatorname{Re}(s)>\theta\}
, where
\operatorname{Re}(s)
denotes the real part of
s\in\mathbb{C}
. Let
\mathbb{D}
refer to the open unit disk in the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
, and let
\operatorname{Hol}(\Omega)
denote the algebra of holomorphic functions defined on a domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}
. A Dirichlet series is a series of function of the form
f(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}n^{-s}
, where
s=\sigma+it
is a complex variable with
\sigma=\operatorname{Re}(s)
. The Hardy space of Dirichlet series
\mathcal{H}^{2}
was introduced and thoroughly investigated by Gordon and Hedenmalm
[
18
]
in their foundational paper. This space serves as the natural Dirichlet-series analogue of the classical Hardy space on the unit disk
\mathbb{H}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. The space
\mathcal{H}^{2}
contains the
\ell^{2}
-inner product of the coefficients, where
\ell^{2}
space represents the Hilbert space of square summable complex sequences. For holomorphic maps
f(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}n^{-s}
and
g(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}n^{-s}
in
\mathcal{H}^{2}
, their inner product is defined as
\left\langle f,g\right\rangle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}\overline{b_{n}}
. The Hardy-Hilbert space of Dirichlet series is defined as

(1.1)

\mathcal{H}^{2}:=\left\{f(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}n^{-s}:\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}<\infty\right\}.

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, any Dirichlet series
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}n^{-s}
with
(a_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\ell^{2}
converges absolutely in the half-plane
\mathbb{C}_{1/2}
. Consequently, the Hardy space
\mathcal{H}^{2}
consists of all Dirichlet series
f(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}n^{-s}
on
\mathbb{C}_{1/2}
endowed with the Hilbert space norm
\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}
. As shown by Gordon and Hedenmalm
[
18
]
, functions in
\mathcal{H}^{2}
generally do not admit holomorphic extensions to any domain strictly containing
\mathbb{C}_{1/2}
. For comprehensive background on Dirichlet series, we refer to
[
1
, Chapter 11]
. The classical Hardy-Hilbert space on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
is defined as

\mathbb{H}^{2}(\mathbb{D}):=\left\{f\in\mbox{Hol}(\mathbb{D}):\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathbb{H}^{2}(\mathbb{D})}^{2}=\sup_{0\leq r<1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|f(r\xi)|^{2}dm(\xi)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}<\infty\right\},

where the inner product between two functions
f
and
g
in
\mathbb{H}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is defined as

\left\langle f,g\right\rangle:=\lim_{r\to 1^{-}}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(re^{it})\overline{g(re^{it})}dt

and
m
is the normalized Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{T}
. By Fatou’s radial limit theorem
[
30
]
, the classical Hardy space
\mathbb{H}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is naturally identified with a closed subspace of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
. In the setting of Dirichlet series, Bohr’s correspondence
[
19
, Section 2]
establishes that
\mathcal{H}^{2}
is isometric to the Hardy space on the infinite polycircle,
\mathbb{H}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{\infty})
, where

\mathbb{T}^{\infty}=\{z=(z_{1},z_{2},\dots)\colon|z_{j}|=1\text{ for all }j\in\mathbb{N}\}

is the infinite Cartesian product of unit circles
\mathbb{T}
equipped with normalized Haar measure
m_{\infty}
. The polycircle
\mathbb{T}^{\infty}
is a compact abelian group whose dual group is
\mathbb{Z}^{(\infty)}
, consisting of all eventually zero sequences
\alpha=(\alpha_{j})_{j\geq 1}
of integers. Let
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{(\infty)}
denote the subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{(\infty)}
comprising sequences with
\alpha_{j}\geq 0
for all
j\geq 1
, where
\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. A function
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{\infty})
belongs to
\mathbb{H}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{\infty})
if, and only if, its Fourier coefficients
\widehat{f}(\alpha)
vanish for all non-positive frequencies, that is,

\widehat{f}(\alpha)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{\infty}}f(z)\overline{z}^{\alpha}\,dm_{\infty}(z)=0\quad\text{for all }\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{(\infty)}\setminus\mathbb{N}_{0}^{(\infty)},

where
z^{\alpha}=\prod_{j\geq 1}z_{j}^{\alpha_{j}}
for
z\in\mathbb{T}^{\infty}
. Any function
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{\infty})
admits the Fourier expansion

f=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{(\infty)}}\widehat{f}(\alpha)e_{\alpha}\quad\text{with}\quad\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{(\infty)}}|\widehat{f}(\alpha)|^{2}<\infty,

where
\{e_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{(\infty)}}
denotes the canonical orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{\infty})
. Furthermore, the space
\mathcal{H}^{2}
is the completion of
\mathcal{P}
, the set of all Dirichlet polynomials
P(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_{n}n^{-s}
with the norm defined as

\left\lVert P\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}|P(it)|^{2}dt.

For a holomorphic self-map
\Phi
of the half-plane
\mathbb{C}_{1/2}
, the composition operator
C_{\Phi}
on the Hardy space
\mathcal{H}^{2}
is defined by
C_{\Phi}(f)=f\circ\Phi
. This operator initially maps
\mathcal{H}^{2}
into
\operatorname{Hol}(\mathbb{C}_{1/2})
. In their seminal paper, Gordon and Hedenmalm
[
18
]
characterized the precise class of symbols
\Phi
for which
C_{\Phi}
acts boundedly on
\mathcal{H}^{2}
. Beyond classical composition, it is natural to consider operators that incorporate differentiation. To this end, given a holomorphic self-map
\Phi
of
\mathbb{C}_{1/2}
, we define the composition-differentiation operator
D_{\Phi}
by

D_{\Phi}(f)=f^{\prime}\circ\Phi.

As with
C_{\Phi}
, whenever
D_{\Phi}
maps
\mathcal{H}^{2}
into itself, the Closed Graph Theorem ensures its boundedness.
While the differentiation operator
D
is well-known to be unbounded on the classical Hardy space of the unit disk
\mathbb{H}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
, a natural problem arises: for which analytic self-maps
\Phi\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
is the composition-differentiation operator
D_{\Phi}
bounded on
\mathbb{H}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
? Ohno
[
24
]
introduced
D_{\Phi}
on
\mathbb{H}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
in the form
C_{\Phi}D
and characterized its boundedness and compactness. Subsequently, Hibschweiler and Portnoy
[
20
]
systematically investigated the mapping properties of both
C_{\Phi}D
and
DC_{\Phi}
. More recently, Fatehi and Hammond
[
17
]
provided a detailed study of composition-differentiation operators on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. In this paper, we extend this line of inquiry to the Dirichlet-series setting, characterizing the symbols
\Phi\colon\mathbb{C}_{1/2}\to\mathbb{C}_{1/2}
that induce bounded composition-differentiation operators
D_{\Phi}
on the Hardy space
\mathcal{H}^{2}
.

In 2003, Bayart
[
4
]
made a seminal contribution by establishing a necessary and sufficient condition for the compactness of composition operators on
\mathcal{H}^{2}
for general symbols
\Phi
, as presented in (
1.2
) below. More recently, Bayart
[
5
]
sharpened the sufficient condition for compactness on
\mathcal{H}^{p}
for
p\in[1,+\infty)
. However, determining a necessary condition for compactness on
\mathcal{H}^{2}
without imposing additional restrictions on symbols in the class
\mathcal{G}_{\geq 1}
remains a major open problem. Addressing this gap, Kouroupis
[
21
]
obtained a necessary condition for the compactness of
C_{\Phi}
on
\mathcal{H}^{2}
by replacing pointwise convergence with convergence in
L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{\infty})
. Furthermore, Queffélec and Seip
[
25
]
provided crucial insights into this framework by establishing decay rate estimates for the approximation numbers of compact composition operators on
\mathcal{H}^{2}
.
In 1999, Gordon and Hedenmalm
[
18
]
characterized the class of holomorphic functions
\Phi\colon\mathbb{C}_{\theta}\to\mathbb{C}_{1/2}
(

) that generate well-defined composition operators
C_{\Phi}
on the space
\mathcal{D}
, where
\mathcal{D}
admit functions that represents a convergent Dirichlet series in some half-plane. Identifying the analogous class of symbols
\Phi
that induce well-defined composition-differentiation operators

on
\mathcal{D}
constitutes one of our primary objectives. Furthermore, Gordon and Hedenmalm
[
18
]
determined the precise symbols
\Phi
that generate a bounded composition operators on
\mathcal{H}^{2}
. They proved that the symbol
\Phi
defined on the half-plane
\mathbb{C}_{{1}/{2}}
generates a composition operator if, and only if,

(1.2)

\Phi(s)=c_{0}s+\phi(s),

where
c_{0}
is a non-negative integer and
\phi(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n}n^{-s}
converges uniformly in each half-plane
\mathbb{C}_{\epsilon}
, for every
\epsilon>0
, where
\phi
satisfies the following properties:

(i)

If
c_{0}=0
, then
\Phi(\mathbb{C}_{0})=\phi(\mathbb{C}_{0})\subset\mathbb{C}_{\frac{1}{2}}
.

(ii)

If
c_{0}\geq 1
, then either

or, simply
\Phi(\mathbb{C}_{0})\subset\mathbb{C}_{0}
.

The non-negative integer
c_{0}
is called the characteristic of
\Phi
or, simply
char
\Phi
. Also we denote
\mathcal{G}_{0}
and
\mathcal{G}_{\geq 1}
for the subclasses
(i)
and
(ii)
respectively. These two classes are collectively referred to as the Gordon-Hedenmalm class of symbols

.

In 2021, Brevig and Perfekt established a new compactness criterion
[
9

1.4
) for symbols

, defined for

as

N_{\Phi}(w)=\sum_{s\in\Phi^{-1}(w)}\operatorname{Re}(s).

Let
\mathbb{P}
denote the set of prime numbers and define the multiplicative sequence
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{P})=\{n\in\mathbb{N}\colon p\mid n\implies p\in\mathbb{P}\}
.

Definition 1.1
.

[
10
]

A Dirichlet series

is said to be

on a set of primes

f(s)=\sum_{n\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{P})}a_{n}n^{-s}.

In 2022, Brevig and Perfekt
[

proved that if
\Phi\in\mathcal{G}_{\geq 1}
and
\phi
is supported on a single prime

, then the composition operator
C_{\Phi}
is compact on
\mathcal{H}^{2}
if and only if

(1.3)

\lim_{\operatorname{Re}(w)\to 0^{+}}\frac{N_{\Phi}(w)}{\operatorname{Re}(w)}=0.

Subsequently, Kouroupis and Perfekt
[
22
]
established corresponding compactness criteria on the Bergman space of Dirichlet series. Motivated by the compactness condition of Brevig and Perfekt in
[

, we aim to prove a similar decay condition for the composition-differentiation operator on
\mathcal{H}^{2}
in terms of the mean counting function
M_{\Phi}(w)
is defined as, for
w\neq\Phi(+\infty)
, by

(1.4)

M_{\Phi}(w):=\lim_{\sigma\to 0^{+}}\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{\pi}{T}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\in\Phi^{-1}(\{w\})\\
|\operatorname{Im}(s)|<T\\
\sigma<\operatorname{Re}(s)<\infty\end{subarray}}\operatorname{Re}(s).

.

We focus initially on the affine single-prime symbols

\Phi(s)=c_{1}+c_{2}2^{-s},

which constitute the simplest non-constant class of Gordon-Hedenmalm symbols with
c_{0}=0
. This class is particularly natural from the viewpoint of Bohr’s correspondence: the Dirichlet series symbol depends on a single prime

and therefore reduces under the Bohr lift to an affine symbol in a single complex variable. At the same time, composition-differentiation introduces a non-trivial interaction between differentiation and the non-linear map
2^{-s}
, giving rise to an associated composition-differentiation operator on the unit disk with an exponential symbol. Consequently, this single-prime symbol provides a tractable framework within which the mapping, compactness, approximation-number, and spectral properties of composition-differentiation operators can be analyzed explicitly.

For the affine symbol
\Phi(s)=c_{1}+c_{2}2^{-s}

[

In 2015, Queffélec and Seip

(1.5)

a_{n}(C_{\Phi})\gtrsim q^{n}\,\,\,\mbox{for some}\,\,\,0<q<1.

Furthermore, they established precise upper and lower estimates for

is bounded on

[

associated with the affine symbol

The primary purpose of this paper is to establish a rigorous operator-theoretic framework for composition-differentiation operators acting on the Hardy space of Dirichlet series
\mathcal{H}^{2}
. In contrast to the extensively studied composition operators
C_{\Phi}
, the presence of differentiation introduces logarithmic weight factors in the Dirichlet coefficients, resulting in substantially different operator-theoretic behavior. We first establish characterizations of boundedness and compactness for
D_{\Phi}
. For the affine symbol
\Phi(s)=c_{1}+c_{2}2^{-s}
, we provide explicit norm estimates as well as quantitative exponential decay bounds for its approximation numbers. We further investigate the structural properties of
D_{\Phi}
, characterizing both self-adjointness and normality. Finally, we examine the spectral theory of compact composition-differentiation operators. In the single-prime case, the non-zero eigenvalue problem reduces to a composition-differentiation operator on the classical Hardy space of the unit disk
\mathbb{H}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
, thereby establishing a direct connection between operators on Dirichlet series and analytic operators on the disk. Overall, these results yield a unified operator-theoretic treatment of composition-differentiation operators on
\mathcal{H}^{2}
.

The paper is organized as follows. At the beginning of Section

[

\Phi

and obtain precise norm estimates for

when
\Phi
is an affine symbol
c_{1}+c_{2}2^{-s}
with
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
,

, and satisfying the sharp boundedness condition

2.
Compactness and boundedness of the operator
D_{\Phi}

Here we study composition-differentiation operators
D_{\Phi}
acting on the Hardy space of Dirichlet series
\mathcal{H}^{2}
. First, we establish the structural requirements on the symbol
\Phi
that generate a well-defined composition-differentiation operator on
\mathcal{D}
. We then characterize the precise mapping properties of
\Phi
that induce bounded and compact operators
D_{\Phi}
on
\mathcal{H}^{2}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

The composition-differentiation operator
D_{\Phi}
generated by a holomorphic symbol
\Phi\colon\mathbb{C}_{\theta}\to\mathbb{C}_{1/2}
, for some
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, acts boundedly on the Dirichlet series space
\mathcal{D}
if and only if
\Phi
is of the form

(2.1)

\Phi(s)=c_{0}s+\phi(s),

where
c_{0}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and
\phi\in\mathcal{D}
is a convergent Dirichlet series.

Sketch of the proof.

The proof of Theorem
2.1
builds upon the strategy developed by Gordon and Hedenmalm
[
18
, Theorem A]
for composition operators
C_{\Phi}
on
\mathcal{H}^{2}
. For completeness, we outline the primary steps. Crucially, a Dirichlet series
f
and its derivative
f^{\prime}
share the same abscissa of absolute convergence,
\sigma_{a}(f)=\sigma_{a}(f^{\prime})
.

To establish necessity, assuming
f^{\prime}\circ\Phi\in\mathcal{D}
for every
f\in\mathcal{D}
, we must verify the structural form of
\Phi
given in (
2.1
). This requires demonstrating that
\Phi(s)/s\to c_{0}
as
\operatorname{Re}(s)\to+\infty
and that
\Phi\in\mathcal{D}
, which implies that
\Phi(s)
is a convergent Dirichlet series generated by a multiplicative semigroup
\{1+j/k\}_{j\geq 1}
consisting exclusively of positive integers.
Conversely, for sufficiency, we assume
\Phi
has the specified structure with
\operatorname{Re}(c_{1})>1/2
and prove that
f^{\prime}\circ\Phi\in\mathcal{D}
for all
f\in\mathcal{D}
by showing that the Dirichlet series
f^{\prime}\circ\Phi
converges in a suitable right half-plane. This completes the proof of Theorem
2.1
.
∎

2.1.
Compactness of the operator
D_{\Phi}

We focus on the class
\mathcal{G}_{0}
, which corresponds to the case of zero characteristic for the symbol
\Phi
of the operator
D_{\Phi}
on
\mathcal{H}^{2}
. From Carlson’s lemma
[
19
, Lemma 3.2]
, we deduce a Littlewood-Paley type formula
[
30
, Chapter 10]
for the norm in
\mathcal{H}^{2}
:

(2.2)

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}=|f(+\infty)|^{2}+2\lim_{\sigma_{0}\to 0^{+}}\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{\sigma_{0}}^{\infty}\int_{-T}^{T}\sigma|f^{\prime}(\sigma+it)|^{2}\,dt\,d\sigma,

valid for every
f\in\mathcal{H}^{2}
satisfying
\sigma_{u}(f)\leq 0
, where

\sigma_{u}(f):=\inf\left\{\sigma\in\mathbb{R}\colon\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}n^{-s}\text{ converges uniformly in }\overline{\mathbb{C}_{\sigma}}\right\}

denotes the abscissa of uniform convergence of
f
. Analogously, one defines the abscissa of ordinary convergence
\sigma_{c}(f)
and the abscissa of absolute convergence
\sigma_{a}(f)
.

For a symbol
\Phi\in\mathcal{G}_{0}
, applying a suitable non-injectiver change of variables to identity (
2.2
) yields

(2.3)

\|D_{\Phi}f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}=\|f^{\prime}\circ\Phi\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}=|f^{\prime}(\Phi(+\infty))|^{2}+\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{C}_{1/2}}|f^{\prime\prime}(w)|^{2}M_{\Phi}(w)\,dA(w)

for all
f\in\mathcal{H}^{2}
, where the
M_{\Phi}(w)
denotes mean counting function defined in (
1.4
).
Furthermore, the mean counting function satisfies a Littlewood-type inequality of the following form.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
9
]

Let
\Phi
be a Dirichlet series symbol with
\operatorname{char}(\Phi)=0
. Then the mean counting function
M_{\Phi}(w)
exists for every
w\in\mathbb{C}_{1/2}\setminus\{\Phi(+\infty)\}
, and satisfies the point-wise estimate

M_{\Phi}(w)\leq\log\left|\frac{\overline{w}+\Phi(+\infty)-1}{w-\Phi(+\infty)}\right|.

The change of variables formula (
2.3
) is the analogue of
[
9
, Theorem 1.3]
established for composition operators
C_{\Phi}
on
\mathcal{H}^{2}
. Furthermore, in view of
[
9
, Theorem 1.2]
, the mean counting function is given explicitly by

M_{\Phi}(w)=\log\left|\frac{w+\bar{v}-1}{w-v}\right|

for quasi-every
w=\sigma+it\in\mathbb{C}_{1/2}
with
w\neq v
, where
v:=\Phi(+\infty)\in\mathbb{C}_{1/2}
.

The characterization of boundedness and compactness for the composition-differentiation operator
D_{\Phi}
in terms of the mean counting function
M_{\Phi}
relies on estimates for the reproducing kernels in
\mathcal{H}^{2}
. For any
a\in\mathbb{C}_{1/2}
, the reproducing kernel
K_{a}
associated with point evaluation of functions
f\in\mathcal{H}^{2}
satisfies

\langle f,K_{a}\rangle=f(a),

and is explicitly given by

K_{a}(s)=\zeta(s+\bar{a}),\qquad s\in\mathbb{C}_{1/2},

where
\zeta
denotes the Riemann zeta function. Similarly, we denote by
K_{a}^{(1)}
the reproducing kernel for point evaluation of the derivative, satisfying

\langle f,K_{a}^{(1)}\rangle=f^{\prime}(a),

where

K_{a}^{(1)}(s)=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(\log n)n^{-(s+\bar{a})}.

Direct calculation then yields the exact norms

\|K_{a}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}=\sqrt{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-(a+\bar{a})}}=\sqrt{\zeta(2\operatorname{Re}(a))}

and

\|K_{a}^{(1)}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}=\sqrt{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(\log n)^{2}n^{-(a+\bar{a})}}=\sqrt{\zeta^{\prime\prime}(2\operatorname{Re}(a))},

for every
a\in\mathbb{C}_{1/2}
. We are now in a position to state and prove one of the principal results of this paper.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\Phi\in\mathcal{G}_{0}
and
M_{\Phi}
denote the associated mean counting function (
1.4
). Then the induced composition-differentiation operator
D_{\Phi}
is compact on
\mathcal{H}^{2}
if, and only if,

(2.4)

\lim_{\real(w)\to\frac{1}{2}^{+}}\frac{M_{\Phi}(w)}{(\real(w)-\frac{1}{2})^{3}}=0.

By applying the reproducing kernels of
\mathcal{H}^{2}
together with the sub-mean-value property of the mean counting function (as stated below in Lemma
2.1
), it is easy to demonstrate that condition (
2.4
) is necessary for the compactness of

. Meanwhile, the change of variables formula (

2.3
. The following remark provides an essential analogue to
[

, Theorem 1.4]
within the setting of Theorem
2.3

Remark 2.1
.

Consider the symbol
\Phi\in\mathcal{G}_{0}

is bounded on

We note that Remark

is a direct analogue of

for the classical Hardy space

Definition 2.1

{\limsup_{\sigma\to 0^{+}}}\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}\log^{+}|f(\sigma+it)|\,dt<\infty,

.

[

For

The following two lemmas are key in proving necessity for the compactness of the operator

Lemma 2.4
.

Let

that converges weakly to

\lim_{n\to\infty}\bigg(|f^{\prime}_{n}(\Phi(\infty))|^{2}+\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{\real(w)\geq\theta}|f^{\prime\prime}_{n}(w)|^{2}M_{\Phi}(w)\,dA(w)\bigg)=0.

In view of

.
The following lemma plays a key role in the proof of Theorem

.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
v\in\mathbb{C}_{{1}/{2}}
and
\delta>0
. Then, there exists a constant
C
depends on
\delta
such that

\int_{1/2<\real(w)<\theta}|f^{\prime\prime}(w)|^{2}\frac{(\real(w)-1/2)^{3}}{|w-v|^{1+\delta}}\,dw\leq\frac{C}{(\real(v)-\theta)^{1+\delta}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2},

for every

and
1/2<\theta<\real(v)
.

Proof.

Since
1/2<\real(w)<\theta
and the
\mathcal{H}^{2}
norm remains invariant under vertical translation, we can assume without loss of generality that
v
is real. Consider the function

w(t)=\frac{1}{|w-v|^{1+\delta}}=\frac{1}{|\sigma+it-v|^{1+\delta}}\leq\frac{1}{|\theta+it-v|^{1+\delta}}.

By applying
[
9
, Lemma 7.2]
with the decreasing function
w(t)
along the vertical line

to the Dirichlet series
f^{\prime\prime}(\sigma-1/2+s)
, we obtain

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{|f^{\prime\prime}(\sigma+it)|^{2}}{|\sigma+it-v|^{1+\delta}}\,dt\leq\frac{1}{(\real(v)-\theta)^{1+\delta}}\sum_{n\geq 1}|a_{n}|^{2}\frac{(\log n)^{4}}{n^{2(\sigma-1/2)}}.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\int_{1/2<\real(w)<\theta}|f^{\prime\prime}(w)|^{2}\frac{(\real(w)-1/2)^{3}}{|w-v|^{1+\delta}}\,dw

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sum_{n\geq 1}|a_{n}|^{2}}{(\real(v)-\theta)^{1+\delta}}\int_{1/2}^{\theta}\frac{(\log n)^{4}}{n^{2(\sigma-1/2)}}(\sigma-1/2)^{3}\,d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}}{(\real(v)-\theta)^{1+\delta}}\int_{1/2}^{\infty}\frac{(\log n)^{4}}{n^{2(\sigma-1/2)}}(\sigma-1/2)^{3}\,d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{8}\frac{1}{(\real(v)-\theta)^{1+\delta}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.

Let the operator
D_{\Phi}
be compact. Our goal is to prove the condition (
2.4
). Consider an arbitrary sequence
\{w_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
in the half-plane
\mathbb{C}_{{1}/{2}}
such that
\real(w_{j})
converges to
1/2^{+}
as
j\to\infty
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that

(2.6)

\real(w_{j})\leq\frac{1/2+\real(\Phi(\infty))}{2}.

Indeed, setting
v:=\Phi(+\infty)
, estimate (
2.6
) yields

\real w_{j}-\frac{1}{2}\leq\frac{\real v-\frac{1}{2}}{2}.

For any
s\in\mathbb{D}(w_{j},r_{j})
, where
r_{j}=\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\real w_{j}-\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
, we have

\real s\leq\real w_{j}+r_{j}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3}{2}\left(\real w_{j}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3}{4}\left(\real v-\frac{1}{2}\right).

Consequently,

\real v-\real s\geq\frac{1}{4}\left(\real v-\frac{1}{2}\right)>0.

It follows directly that

\operatorname{dist}\bigl(\mathbb{D}(w_{j},r_{j}),\Phi(+\infty)\bigr)\geq\frac{1}{4}\left(\real(\Phi(+\infty))-\frac{1}{2}\right).

This ensures that
\Phi(+\infty)
is uniformly bounded on the open disk
\mathbb{D}(w_{j},r_{j})\subset\mathbb{C}_{{1}/{2}}\setminus\{\Phi(+\infty)\}
, where
r_{j}={(\real(w_{j})-1/2)}/{2}
. Hence from Lemma
2.1
, we have

(2.7)

\frac{M_{\Phi}(w_{j})}{(\real(w_{j})-1/2)^{3}}\leq\frac{1}{8\pi r_{j}^{5}}\int_{\mathbb{D}(w_{j},r_{j})}M_{\Phi}(s)ds.

For
w\in\mathbb{C}_{{1}/{2}}
, consider the function

K_{w}(s)=\frac{\zeta(s+\overline{w})}{\sqrt{\zeta(2\real(w))}},

which serves as a normalized reproducing kernel Hilbert space on
\mathcal{H}^{2}
. Clearly, the sequence
\{K_{w_{j}}\}_{j\geq 1}
converges weakly to
0
. By the compactness of the operator
D_{\Phi}
, we have
D_{\Phi}(K_{w_{j}})\to 0
as
j\to\infty
on
\mathcal{H}^{2}
.
After taking second derivatives of the normalized kernel functions yields

K_{w_{j}}^{\prime\prime}(s)=\frac{\zeta^{\prime\prime}(s+\overline{w_{j}})}{\sqrt{\zeta(2\real w_{j})}}.

By the virtue of Lemma
2.2
, the Riemann zeta function satisfies
\zeta(z)=(z-1)^{-1}+E(z)
, where
E
is an entire function, so that

\zeta^{\prime\prime}(z)=\frac{2}{(z-1)^{3}}+E^{\prime\prime}(z).

For
s\in\mathbb{D}(w_{j},r_{j})
with radius
r_{j}:=\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\real w_{j}-\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
, we have
|s+\overline{w_{j}}-1|\asymp r_{j}
. Since
E^{\prime\prime}
is bounded on compact subsets of
\mathbb{C}
, it follows that for all sufficiently large
j
,

|\zeta^{\prime\prime}(s+\overline{w_{j}})|\gtrsim r_{j}^{-3}.

Moreover, near the pole
z=1
we have
\zeta(2\real w_{j})=\zeta(1+4r_{j})\asymp r_{j}^{-1}
. Consequently,

(2.8)

|K_{w_{j}}^{\prime\prime}(s)|^{2}\gtrsim\frac{r_{j}^{-6}}{r_{j}^{-1}}=r_{j}^{-5},\qquad s\in\mathbb{D}(w_{j},r_{j}),

where the implied constant is independent of both
j
and
s
.
Applying the estimates (
2.7
) and (
2.8
) and extending the domain of integration to the half-plane
\mathbb{C}_{1/2}
, we obtain from (
2.3
) that

\displaystyle\frac{M_{\Phi}(w_{j})}{(\real(w_{j})-1/2)^{3}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{8\pi r_{j}^{5}}\int_{\mathbb{C}_{{1}/{2}}}M_{\Phi}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{8\pi C}\int_{\mathbb{C}_{{1}/{2}}}|K^{\prime\prime}_{w_{j}}(s)|^{2}M_{\Phi}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{16C}\left\lVert D_{\Phi}(K_{w_{j}})\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}\to 0\,\,\,\mbox{as}\,\,j\to\infty.

Since
\{w_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
is an arbitrary sequence in
\mathbb{C}_{{1}/{2}}
, we conclude that

\lim_{\real(w)\to\frac{1}{2}^{+}}\frac{M_{\Phi}(w)}{(\real(w)-\frac{1}{2})^{3}}=0.

Conversely, suppose that condition (
2.4
) holds. By Lemma
2.3
, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a corresponding choice of parameters such that

M_{\Phi}(w)\leq\varepsilon^{2}\frac{(\operatorname{Re}(w)-1/2)^{3}}{|w-\Phi(+\infty)|^{1+\delta}}.

To establish that
D_{\Phi}
is compact on
\mathcal{H}^{2}
, let
\{f_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be an arbitrary sequence in
\mathcal{H}^{2}
that is bounded in norm and converges weakly to
0
. We must show that
\|D_{\Phi}f_{n}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.
By the change of variables formula (
2.3
), for any fixed
\theta>1/2
, we decompose the norm as

\displaystyle\|D_{\Phi}f_{n}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|f_{n}^{\prime}\circ\Phi\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=|f_{n}^{\prime}(\Phi(+\infty))|^{2}+\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{C}_{1/2}}|f_{n}^{\prime\prime}(w)|^{2}M_{\Phi}(w)\,dA(w)

\displaystyle=|f_{n}^{\prime}(\Phi(+\infty))|^{2}+\frac{2}{\pi}\left(\int_{\operatorname{Re}(w)\geq\theta}+\int_{1/2<\operatorname{Re}(w)<\theta}\right)|f_{n}^{\prime\prime}(w)|^{2}M_{\Phi}(w)\,dA(w).

Applying Lemma
2.4
, the weak convergence
f_{n}\xrightarrow{w}0
ensures that

(2.9)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(|f_{n}^{\prime}(\Phi(+\infty))|^{2}+\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{\operatorname{Re}(w)\geq\theta}|f_{n}^{\prime\prime}(w)|^{2}M_{\Phi}(w)\,dA(w)\right)=0.

Furthermore, invoking Lemma
2.3
together with Lemma
2.5
for
v=\Phi(+\infty)
and
1/2<\theta\leq\frac{1/2+\operatorname{Re}(\Phi(+\infty))}{2}<\operatorname{Re}(v)
, we obtain the uniform bound

\displaystyle\int_{1/2<\operatorname{Re}(w)<\theta}|f_{n}^{\prime\prime}(w)|^{2}M_{\Phi}(w)\,dA(w)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{2}\int_{1/2<\operatorname{Re}(w)<\theta}|f_{n}^{\prime\prime}(w)|^{2}\frac{(\operatorname{Re}(w)-1/2)^{3}}{|w-\Phi(+\infty)|^{1+\delta}}\,dA(w)

(2.10)

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\|f_{n}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2},

where
C>0
is an absolute constant. Combining (
2.9
) and (
2.10
), and taking
n\to\infty
followed by
\varepsilon\to 0
, we conclude that
\left\lVert D_{\Phi}f_{n}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.
Thus,
D_{\Phi}
maps weakly convergent sequences to strongly convergent ones, proving that
D_{\Phi}
is compact on
\mathcal{H}^{2}
.
∎

\Phi
that induces a compact composition-differentiation operator on

.

.

Assume the symbol

. If the range of the half-plane

is compact.
Moreover, if there exists

is given by

\Omega_{j}=\left\{s\in\mathbb{C}_{1/2}:\left|\operatorname{arg}\left(s-\frac{1}{2}-i\tau_{j}\right)\right|<\beta_{j}\right\}

for

.
The key part of this proof of such condition involves following Green’s function

g_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(z,w)=\log\left|\frac{\left(z-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\alpha}+\left(w-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\alpha}}{\left(z-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\alpha}-\left(w-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\alpha}}\right|,\,\,\,\mbox{for}\,\,\,\alpha>3

, Sec. 4]

(2.11)

M_{\Phi}(w)\lesssim g_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(w,v),

where

uniformly distinct from

.

For a detailed background on Green’s functions and their fundamental properties, we refer to
[
27
, Section 4.4]
.
Moreover, we obtain the following characterization of the compactness of the operator

in terms of the decay rate of the Green’s function

near the boundary

.

Corollary 2.12
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}_{1/2}
be a Greenian domain such that
\Phi(\mathbb{C}_{0})\subset D
and

. If the Green function

of

with pole at

satisfies

(2.13)

g_{D}(w,v)=o\left(\left(\real w-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{3}\right),\qquad\text{as }\quad\real w\to\frac{1}{2}^{+},

.

The proof of Corollary

essentially follows from the comparison principle (

).
The compactness of
D_{\Phi}
then follows directly from Theorem
2.3
. Now we will relate the condition of Corollary
2.12
in terms of the harmonic measure through the following remark.

Remark 2.3
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}_{1/2}
be a bounded Greenian domain, and let
\omega_{D}(z,E)
denote the harmonic measure of a Borel set
E\subset\partial D
evaluated at
z\in D
. Let
U
be a neighborhood of the boundary point
1/2
such that
v:=\Phi(+\infty)\notin\overline{U}
, and define the relative subdomain
V:=D\cap U.

The Green function
g_{D}(\cdot,v)
is positive and harmonic on
V
. Under standard regularity assumptions on
\partial D
,
g_{D}(\cdot,v)
vanishes continuously on
\partial D\cap U
. Consequently, by the harmonic-measure integral representation, we have

(2.14)

g_{D}(w,v)=\int_{\partial V\cap D}g_{D}(\zeta,v)\,d\omega_{V}(w,\zeta),\qquad w\in V.

Since
v\notin\overline{U}
, the integrand
g_{D}(\zeta,v)
remains uniformly bounded on the relative interior boundary
\partial V\cap D
. Hence, there exists a positive constant
C
such that

g_{D}(v,w)\leq C\,\omega_{V}(w,\partial U\cap D).

Thus if

\omega_{V}(w,\partial U\cap D)=o\left(\left(\real w-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{3}\right),\qquad\text{as }\quad\real w\to\frac{1}{2}^{+},

then the desired Green’s function decay (

) follows locally.

For a comprehensive treatment of harmonic measure and its potential-theoretic properties, we refer to
[
27
, Section 4.3]
.

2.2.
Boundedness of the operator
D_{\Phi}

The following counterexample demonstrates that the mapping condition
\Phi(\mathbb{C}_{0})\subset\mathbb{C}_{1/2}
alone is not sufficient to guarantee the boundedness of the operator
D_{\Phi}
on the space
\mathcal{H}^{2}
.

Example 2.1
.

Fix
v:=\Phi(+\infty)\in\mathbb{C}_{1/2}
and consider the symbol
\Phi_{v}:\mathbb{C}_{0}\to\mathbb{C}_{1/2}
defined by

(2.15)

\Phi_{v}(s)=\frac{(\overline{v}-1)2^{-s}+v}{1-2^{-s}}.

For
\delta>0
sufficiently small, set
a_{\delta}:=\frac{1}{2}+\delta
and let
K_{a_{\delta}}
denote the normalized reproducing kernel at
a_{\delta}
, so that
\|K_{a_{\delta}}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}=1
. By the kernel derivative estimates (
2.8
), there exists a constant
c>0
such that

|K_{a_{\delta}}^{\prime\prime}(w)|^{2}\gtrsim\frac{1}{\delta^{5}}\qquad\text{for all }w\in\mathbb{D}(a_{\delta},c\delta).

On the disk
\mathbb{D}(a_{\delta},c\delta)
, we have
\real w-\frac{1}{2}\asymp\delta
. Moreover, the explicit evaluation of
M_{\Phi_{v}}
yields
M_{\Phi_{v}}(w)\gtrsim\delta
uniformly on
\mathbb{D}(a_{\delta},c\delta)
. Applying Littlewood-Paley identity, we obtain

\displaystyle\|D_{\Phi_{v}}K_{a_{\delta}}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{D}(a_{\delta},c\delta)}|K_{a_{\delta}}^{\prime\prime}(w)|^{2}M_{\Phi_{v}}(w)\,dA(w)

\displaystyle\gtrsim\delta^{-5}\cdot\delta\cdot\operatorname{Area}\bigl(\mathbb{D}(a_{\delta},c\delta)\bigr)

\displaystyle\asymp\delta^{-5}\cdot\delta\cdot\delta^{2}=\delta^{-2}.

Consequently,
\|D_{\Phi_{v}}K_{a_{\delta}}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}\gtrsim\delta^{-1}\to\infty
as
\delta\to 0^{+}
, establishing that
D_{\Phi_{v}}
is unbounded on
\mathcal{H}^{2}
.

Additionally, we note that condition (
2.5
) is not sufficient for proving the operator
D_{\Phi}
is bounded on
\mathcal{H}^{2}
. To get rid of the operator’s unboundedness, we shall now present a more generalized result.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let the symbol
\Phi\in\mathcal{G}_{0}
. If there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

(2.16)

\Phi(\mathbb{C}_{0})\subset\mathbb{C}_{1/2+\varepsilon}.

Then the operator
D_{\Phi}=f^{\prime}\circ\Phi
defines a bounded operator on
\mathcal{H}^{2}
.

Remark 2.4
.

The condition (
2.16
) is equivalent to

\operatorname{dist}\bigl(\Phi(\mathbb{C}_{0}),\partial\mathbb{C}_{1/2}\bigr)>0,

which expresses the uniform separation of the range
\Phi(\mathbb{C}_{0})
from the critical boundary
\partial\mathbb{C}_{1/2}
. This reformulated condition plays a key role in constructing counter examples.
We note that the converse of Theorem
2.4
does not hold in general. Indeed, while uniform separation guarantees boundedness, an operator
D_{\Phi}
may remain bounded even when
\operatorname{dist}\bigl(\Phi(\mathbb{C}_{0}),\partial\mathbb{C}_{1/2}\bigr)=0,
provided the image
\Phi(\mathbb{C}_{0})
approaches
\partial\mathbb{C}_{1/2}
sufficiently fast or thin in potential-theoretic measure. At a minimum, by the structure of the Gordon-Hedenmalm class
\mathcal{G}_{0}
, every admissible symbol
\Phi
maps the half-plane
\mathbb{C}_{0}
into
\mathbb{C}_{1/2}
.

In a broader framework, Theorem
2.4
can be established for symbols of positive characteristic. This result serves as a foundational building block for analyzing spectral properties and self-adjointness of
D_{\Phi}
. However, to isolate the fundamental case of zero characteristic, we restrict our attention to symbols of the form
\Phi(s)=c_{1}+c_{2}2^{-s}
with
c_{2}\neq 0
. In accordance with Theorem
2.4
and
[
4
, Corollary 3]
, this choice imposes the crucial domain restriction

\operatorname{Re}(c_{1})>1/2+|c_{2}|.

The precise operator norm estimates for
D_{\Phi}
on
\mathcal{H}^{2}
in this setting are established in the following theorem.

Theorem 2.5
.

The affine symbol
\Phi(s)=c_{1}+c_{2}2^{-s}
with
c_{2}\neq 0
and
\real(c_{1})>1/2+|c_{2}|
induces a bounded composition-differentiation operator on
\mathcal{H}^{2}
that satisfies the following inequality

(2.17)

\frac{2}{\Bigl(2\operatorname{Re}(c_{1})-1\Bigr)^{3}}\leq{\left\lVert D_{\Phi}\right\rVert}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}\leq\zeta^{\prime\prime}\Bigl(2\bigl(\operatorname{Re}c_{1}-|c_{2}|\bigr)\Bigr).

Remark 2.5
.

For the constant function
\Phi(s)=c_{1}
, the lower bound of (
2.17
) is sharp. However, the sharpness of the upper bound in (
2.17
) has not yet been determined. Brevig
[
7
]
initially investigated the sharpness of the constraints for the composition operator analogue.

Proof of Theorem
2.4
.

Let
v:=\Phi(+\infty)
. Since
\Phi(\mathbb{C}_{0})\subset\mathbb{C}_{1/2+\varepsilon}
, it follows that
\operatorname{Re}v\geq 1/2+\varepsilon
. By the Littlewood-type inequality (Theorem
2.2
), the mean counting function satisfies

(2.18)

M_{\Phi}(w)\leq\log\left|\frac{\overline{w}+v-1}{w-v}\right|,

which exhibits a logarithmic singularity at
w=v
. Writing
w=1/2+\sigma+it
and
v=1/2+a+ib
with
\sigma>0
and
a\geq\varepsilon
, we have

\frac{\overline{w}+v-1}{w-v}=\frac{\sigma+a+i(b-t)}{\sigma-a+i(t-b)}.

Substituting this into (
2.18
) yields

(2.19)

M_{\Phi}(w)\leq\frac{1}{2}\log\left(1+\frac{4\sigma a}{(\sigma-a)^{2}+(t-b)^{2}}\right).

Fix
0<\delta<\varepsilon/2
and decompose the target half-plane as
\mathbb{C}_{1/2}=\mathbb{D}(v,\delta)\cup\left(\mathbb{C}_{1/2}\setminus\mathbb{D}(v,\delta)\right)
. Since
\delta<\varepsilon/2
, the disk
\mathbb{D}(v,\delta)
is compactly contained in
\mathbb{C}_{1/2+\varepsilon/2}
. For any
f\in\mathcal{H}^{2}
, the second derivative
f^{\prime\prime}
is uniformly bounded on
\mathbb{D}(v,\delta)
:

(2.20)

\sup_{w\in\mathbb{D}(v,\delta)}|f^{\prime\prime}(w)|^{2}\leq C_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2},

which follows directly from the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality applied to
f^{\prime\prime}(w)=\sum_{n\geq 2}a_{n}(\log n)^{2}n^{-w}
for
\operatorname{Re}w\geq 1/2+\varepsilon/2
. Furthermore, (
2.19
) implies that
M_{\Phi}
has at most a logarithmic singularity at
v
, making
M_{\Phi}
locally integrable over
\mathbb{D}(v,\delta)
. Consequently,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}(v,\delta)}|f^{\prime\prime}(w)|^{2}\,M_{\Phi}(w)\,dA(w)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{D}(v,\delta)}M_{\Phi}(w)\,dA(w)

(2.21)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}.

Away from the singularity
v
, on the region
\mathbb{C}_{1/2}\setminus\mathbb{D}(v,\delta)
, the bound
a\geq\varepsilon
together with (
2.19
) yields the global cubic decay estimate

M_{\Phi}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\sigma+it\right)\leq C_{\varepsilon,\delta,v}\,\sigma^{3}.

Therefore, by the embedding theorem for
\mathcal{H}^{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}_{1/2}\setminus\mathbb{D}(v,\delta)}|f^{\prime\prime}(w)|^{2}M_{\Phi}(w)\,dA(w)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\varepsilon,\delta,v}\int_{\mathbb{C}_{1/2}}|f^{\prime\prime}(w)|^{2}\left(\operatorname{Re}w-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{3}\,dA(w)

(2.22)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\varepsilon,\delta,v}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}.

Combining (
2.21
) and (
2.22
) gives

\int_{\mathbb{C}_{1/2}}|f^{\prime\prime}(w)|^{2}M_{\Phi}(w)\,dA(w)\leq C_{\varepsilon,\delta,v}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}.

Finally, since
\operatorname{Re}v\geq 1/2+\varepsilon
, evaluation at
v
satisfies
|f^{\prime}(v)|^{2}\leq C_{\varepsilon}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}
. Applying the Littlewood-Paley identity (
2.3
), we conclude that
\|D_{\Phi}f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}\leq C_{\varepsilon,v}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}
, establishing the boundedness of
D_{\Phi}
on
\mathcal{H}^{2}
.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

The proof of Theorem
2.4
simplifies significantly under the additional assumption that the symbol
\Phi\in\mathcal{G}_{0}
has bounded imaginary part. Specifically, suppose
\Phi(\mathbb{C}_{0})\subset\mathbb{C}_{1/2+\varepsilon}
and satisfies

T:=\sup_{s\in\mathbb{C}_{0}}|\operatorname{Im}\Phi(s)|<\infty.

Away from a small neighborhood of
v=\Phi(+\infty)
, the Littlewood-type inequality (
2.18
) yields an explicit uniform bound for the mean counting function
M_{\Phi}
. Consequently, applying the local Carleson embedding theorem
[
19
, Theorem 4.11]
to the second derivative
f^{\prime\prime}
, the statement of Theorem
2.4
follows directly.

Proof of Theorem
2.5
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{H}^{2}
be given by
f(s)=\sum_{m\geq 1}a_{m}m^{-s}
. For the affine symbol
\Phi(s)=c_{1}+c_{2}2^{-s}
with
\operatorname{Re}c_{1}-|c_{2}|>1/2
, the operator norm of
D_{\Phi}
can be evaluated directly via Dirichlet series coefficient expansions. Differentiating and substituting
\Phi(s)
yields

\displaystyle D_{\Phi}f(s)

\displaystyle=f^{\prime}\bigl(c_{1}+c_{2}2^{-s}\bigr)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{m\geq 1}a_{m}(\log m)m^{-c_{1}-c_{2}2^{-s}}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{m\geq 1}a_{m}(\log m)m^{-c_{1}}\exp\left(-c_{2}(\log m)2^{-s}\right).

Expanding the exponential term into its Taylor series, we interchange the order of summation to obtain

D_{\Phi}f(s)=-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-c_{2})^{k}}{k!}\left(\sum_{m\geq 1}a_{m}m^{-c_{1}}(\log m)^{k+1}\right)2^{-ks}.

Since the sequence
\{2^{-ks}\}_{k\geq 0}
forms an orthonormal sequence in
\mathcal{H}^{2}
, applying Parseval’s identity gives

(2.23)

\|D_{\Phi}f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{|c_{2}|^{2k}}{(k!)^{2}}\left|\sum_{m\geq 1}a_{m}m^{-c_{1}}(\log m)^{k+1}\right|^{2}.

Applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality to the inner sum over
m
, we have

\left|\sum_{m\geq 1}a_{m}m^{-c_{1}}(\log m)^{k+1}\right|^{2}\leq\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{(\log m)^{2k+2}}{m^{2\operatorname{Re}c_{1}}}.

Substituting this bound into (
2.23
) and applying Tonelli’s theorem to interchange the order of summation yields

\displaystyle\|D_{\Phi}f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{(\log m)^{2}}{m^{2\operatorname{Re}c_{1}}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\bigl(|c_{2}|\log m\bigr)^{2k}}{(k!)^{2}}

(2.24)

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{(\log m)^{2}}{m^{2\operatorname{Re}c_{1}}}I_{0}\bigl(2|c_{2}|\log m\bigr),

where
I_{0}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(x/2)^{2k}}{(k!)^{2}}
is the modified Bessel’s function of the first kind of order zero. Using the standard exponential bound
I_{0}(x)\leq e^{x}
for
x\geq 0
, we observe that
I_{0}(2|c_{2}|\log m)\leq m^{2|c_{2}|}
. Consequently,

\|D_{\Phi}f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}\leq\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{(\log m)^{2}}{m^{2(\operatorname{Re}c_{1}-|c_{2}|)}}.

Because
\operatorname{Re}c_{1}-|c_{2}|>1/2
, the exponent
2(\operatorname{Re}c_{1}-|c_{2}|)>1
, guaranteeing the convergence of the Dirichlet series. Recognizing this sum as the second derivative of the Riemann zeta function, we conclude that

(2.25)

\|D_{\Phi}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}\leq\zeta^{\prime\prime}\Bigl(2\bigl(\operatorname{Re}c_{1}-|c_{2}|\bigr)\Bigr).

Now we will prove the lower bound. It is well known that

{D_{\Phi}^{*}}(K_{a})=K_{\Phi(a)}^{(1)},

for any
a\in\mathbb{C}_{{1}/{2}}
.
In particular, we have

\frac{{\left\lVert D_{\Phi}^{*}(K_{a})\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}}{\left\lVert K_{a}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}}=\frac{\left\lVert K_{\Phi(a)}^{(1)}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}}{\left\lVert K_{a}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}}=\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-2\real(\Phi(a))}(\log n)^{2}}{\zeta(2\real(a))}}.

If
D_{\Phi}
is bounded on
\mathcal{H}^{2}
, then we see that,

(2.26)

\left\lVert D_{\Phi}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}\geq\sup_{a\in\mathbb{C}_{{1}/{2}}}\frac{{\left\lVert D_{\Phi}^{*}(K_{a})\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}}{\left\lVert K_{a}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}}=\sup_{a\in\mathbb{C}_{{1}/{2}}}\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-2\real(\Phi(a))}(\log n)^{2}}{\zeta(2\real(a))}}.

It is easy to see that for
a\in\mathbb{C}_{{1}/{2}}
, we have

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{C}_{{1}/{2}}}\zeta(2\real(a))=\sup_{x>1/2}\zeta(2x)\rightarrow 1,\,\,\,\mbox{as}\,\,x\rightarrow\infty,

where
x
is the real part of
a
. Hence

(2.27)

\displaystyle\left\lVert D_{\Phi}\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{a\in\mathbb{C}_{{1}/{2}}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\log n)^{2}}{n^{2\real(\Phi(a))}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{x>1/2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\log n)^{2}}{{n}^{(2\real(c_{1})-2c_{2}2^{-x})}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\log n)^{2}}{{n}^{2\real(c_{1})}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2}{\Bigl(2\operatorname{Re}(c_{1})-1\Bigr)^{3}},

where the last inequality follows from the basic fact that, the function
f(x)=(\log x)^{2}/x^{2\real(c_{1})}
is continuous, decreasing on
(1,+\infty)
and so
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}f(k)\geq\int_{1}^{\infty}f(x)dx.
This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.7
.

In Theorem
2.5
, we restrict our attention to the affine model symbol
\Phi(s)=c_{1}+c_{2}2^{-s}
, which satisfies the criteria of Theorem
2.4
to generate a bounded operator
D_{\Phi}
on
\mathcal{H}^{2}

3.
Approximation Numbers

The
n
-th approximation number (or
n
-th singular value; see
[
26
, Theorem 8.7.3]
) of a bounded linear operator
T
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is defined by

(3.1)

a_{n}(T):=\inf\bigl\{\|T-A\|:A\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})\text{ with }\operatorname{rank}(A)<n\bigr\},\qquad n\in\mathbb{N},

where
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
denotes the algebra of bounded linear operators on
\mathcal{H}
. Geometrically,
a_{n}(T)
measures the operator-norm distance from
T
to the operators of rank strictly less than
n
. Recall that a bounded linear operator
T\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is compact if, and only if,
a_{n}(T)\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Furthermore, for
0<p<\infty
, the operator
T
belongs to the Schatten
p
-class
\mathcal{S}_{p}(\mathcal{H})
if it’s singular values are
\ell^{p}
-summable; that is,

(3.2)

\|T\|_{\mathcal{S}_{p}}^{p}:=\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(|T|^{p}\bigr)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}(T)^{p}<\infty.

In particular,
\mathcal{S}_{2}(\mathcal{H})
coincides with the class of Hilbert-Schmidt operators, and the Schatten spaces satisfy the continuous inclusion
\mathcal{S}_{p}(\mathcal{H})\subset\mathcal{S}_{q}(\mathcal{H})
whenever
0<p\leq q<\infty
. Moreover, approximation numbers satisfy the ideal property

(3.3)

a_{n}(XTY)\leq\|X\|\,a_{n}(T)\,\|Y\|

for all bounded linear operators
X,Y\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
.

Upper bounds for the approximation numbers
a_{n}(C_{\Phi})
of composition operators on
\mathcal{H}^{2}
were established by Muthukumar et al.
[
23
]
for the affine symbol
\Phi(s)=c_{1}+c_{2}2^{-s}
under the condition
2\operatorname{Re}(c_{1})-2|c_{2}|-1>0
. In the classical Hardy space setting
\mathbb{H}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
, precise estimates for
a_{n}(C_{\Phi})
with affine symbols
\Phi(z)=az+b
(
|a|+|b|\leq 1
) were obtained earlier by Clifford and Dabkowski
[
13
]
. Under the sharp boundedness condition
2\operatorname{Re}(c_{1})-2|c_{2}|-1>0
, we now establish an upper bound for the approximation numbers
a_{n}(D_{\Phi})
of the composition-differentiation operator on
\mathcal{H}^{2}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let the symbol
\Phi(s)=c_{1}+c_{2}2^{-s}
satisfying
2\real c_{1}-2|c_{2}|-1>0
, where
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are complex coefficients with
c_{2}\neq 0
. Set

r:=\frac{2|c_{2}|}{2\real c_{1}-1}<1.

Then

a_{n+1}(D_{\Phi})\lesssim(n+1)\,r^{n}.

Proof.
Let
f(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}n^{-s}
belongs to
\mathcal{H}^{2}
. Then a simple computation using Tonelli’s theorem shows that

(3.4)

\displaystyle D_{\Phi}f(s)

\displaystyle=f^{\prime}(\Phi(s))

\displaystyle=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}n^{-c_{1}-c_{2}2^{-s}}\log n

\displaystyle=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}n^{-c_{1}}\log n\exp(-c_{2}2^{-s}\log n)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}n^{-c_{1}}\log n\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-c_{2})^{k}}{k!}2^{-ks}(\log n)^{k}\right)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-c_{2})^{k}}{k!}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}n^{-c_{1}}(\log n)^{k+1}\right)2^{-ks}.

Let
R
denote the operator of rank
\leq n
, which can be defined as

(3.5)

Rf(s)=-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{(-c_{2})^{k}}{k!}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}n^{-c_{1}}(\log n)^{k+1}\right)2^{-ks}.

Based on equations (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), and applying
[
23
, Lemma 3.3 and 3.4]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\lVert D_{\Phi}(f)-R(f)\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{|c_{2}|^{2k}}{{k!}^{2}}{\left|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}n^{-c_{1}}(\log n)^{k+1}\right|}^{2}

\displaystyle\vskip 2.84526pt\leq\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{|c_{2}|^{2k}}{(k!)^{2}}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\log n)^{2k+2}}{n^{2\real c_{1}}}\right)\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\vskip 2.84526pt\leq\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{|c_{2}|^{2k}}{(k!)^{2}}\frac{(2k+2)!}{(2\real c_{1}-1)^{2k+2}}\zeta(2\real c_{1})\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{|c_{2}|^{2k}}{(k!)^{2}}\frac{(2k+2)(2k+1)(2k)!}{(2\real c_{1}-1)^{2k+2}}\frac{2\real c_{1}}{(2\real c_{1}-1)}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}.

It is easy to see that

(3.6)

\frac{(2k)!}{(k!)^{2}}=\binom{2k}{k}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{2k}\binom{2k}{j}=2^{2k}.

A simple computation by using (
3.6
), we obtain for every
f\in\mathcal{H}^{2}

(3.7)

\displaystyle\left\lVert D_{\Phi}-R\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{|c_{2}|^{2k}}{(k!)^{2}}\frac{(2k+2)(2k+1)(2k)!}{(2\real c_{1}-1)^{2k+2}}\frac{2\real c_{1}}{(2\real c_{1}-1)^{3}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\real c_{1}}{(2\real c_{1}-1)^{3}}\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{(2|c_{2}|)^{2k}}{(2\real c_{1}-1)^{2k}}(2k+2)(2k+1)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\real c_{1}}{(2\real c_{1}-1)^{3}}\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2|c_{2}|}{2\real c_{1}-1}\right)^{2k}(2k+2)^{2}.

Note that for
k\geq n
, we have
(2k+2)^{2}\leq(2n+2)^{2}\left(1+\frac{k-n}{n+1}\right)^{2}
. For any fixed
0<r<1
, it follows that

\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}r^{2k}(2k+2)^{2}\lesssim\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}r^{2k}(k+1)^{2}\leq C(r)(n+1)^{2}r^{2n},

where the implicit constant
C(r)>0
depends solely on
r
. Taking square roots and accounting for the fixed complex parameters
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
, we obtain

(3.8)

a_{n+1}(D_{\Phi})\lesssim(n+1)r^{n}.

This establishes the exponential decay of the approximation numbers and completes the proof.
∎

Motivated by the work of Queffélec and Seip
[
25
, Theorem 4.1]
, we now establish precise upper and lower estimates for the approximation numbers
a_{n}(D_{\Phi})
of the composition-differentiation operator associated with the affine symbol
\Phi(s)=c_{1}+c_{2}2^{-s}
under the sharp boundedness condition
\operatorname{Re}(c_{1})>|c_{2}|+1/2
, where
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
and
c_{2}\neq 0
. In our setting, the single-prime symbol
\Phi(s)=c_{1}+c_{2}2^{-s}
admits a direct finite-rank approximation via expansion of
2^{-s}
. Consequently, unlike the general symbol framework in Queffélec and Seip
[
25
, Theorem 4.1]
, the full machinery of Blaschke products is not required to establish the exponential decay of the approximation numbers
a_{n}(D_{\Phi})
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\Phi(s)=c_{1}+c_{2}2^{-s}
be an affine symbol with
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
,
c_{2}\neq 0
, and satisfying the sharp boundedness condition
\operatorname{Re}(c_{1})>|c_{2}|+1/2
. Set

r:=\frac{2|c_{2}|}{2\operatorname{Re}(c_{1})-1}\in(0,1).

Then for every
\rho\in(r,1)
, there exists a constant
C_{1}=C_{1}(\rho)>0
such that

(3.9)

a_{n}(D_{\Phi})\leq C_{1}\,\rho^{n}\qquad\text{for all }n\geq 1.

Moreover, for
q\in(0,1)
, there exist another constant
C_{2}>0
such that

(3.10)

a_{n}(D_{\Phi})\geq C_{2}\,q^{n}\qquad\text{for all }n\geq 1.

In particular, the asymptotic geometric rate of decay satisfies

0<\liminf_{n\to\infty}a_{n}(D_{\Phi})^{1/n}\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}a_{n}(D_{\Phi})^{1/n}<1.

Proof.
The upper estimate (
3.9
) follows directly from Theorem
3.1
as follows. From (
3.8
), we have

a_{n+1}(D_{\Phi})\lesssim(n+1)r^{n}.

Fix
\rho\in(r,1)
and observe that
(n+1)r^{n}=(n+1)(r/\rho)^{n}\rho^{n}
. Since
r/\rho<1
, the sequence satisfies
\sup_{n\geq 0}(n+1)(r/\rho)^{n}<\infty
. Consequently, we obtain

a_{n+1}(D_{\Phi})\lesssim\rho^{n}\qquad\text{for every }\rho\in(r,1).

Shifting the index yields
a_{n}(D_{\Phi})\lesssim\rho^{n}
for all
n\geq 1
and for all
\rho\in(r,1)
, as desired.

To establish the lower bound (
3.10
), we consider the subspace
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{0}
of zero-mean functions in the Hardy space of Dirichlet series
\mathcal{H}^{2}
, defined by

\mathcal{H}^{2}_{0}:=\left\{f(s)=\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}a_{m}m^{-s}\in\mathcal{H}^{2}\right\}.

Define the integration operator
J\colon\mathcal{H}^{2}_{0}\to\mathcal{H}^{2}_{0}
by

Jg(s):=-\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}\frac{b_{m}}{\log m}m^{-s},\qquad\text{where }g(s)=\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}b_{m}m^{-s}.

Observe that
(Jg)^{\prime}=-g
. Furthermore, its operator norm satisfies

\|Jg\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}=\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}\frac{|b_{m}|^{2}}{(\log m)^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{(\log 2)^{2}}\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}|b_{m}|^{2}=\frac{1}{(\log 2)^{2}}\|g\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2},

yielding
\|J\|\leq{1}/{(\log 2)}
. Moreover equality holds in the norm of
g
. For any
g\in\mathcal{H}^{2}_{0}
, direct computation gives

D_{\Phi}(Jg)(s)=(Jg)^{\prime}(\Phi(s))=-g(\Phi(s))=-C_{\Phi}g(s).

Thus, the restriction of
C_{\Phi}
to
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{0}
factors as

(3.11)

C_{\Phi}|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{0}}=-D_{\Phi}J.

Applying the operator ideal property (
3.3
) for approximation numbers, we obtain

a_{n}(C_{\Phi}|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{0}})=a_{n}(D_{\Phi}J)\leq\|J\|\,a_{n}(D_{\Phi})=\frac{1}{\log 2}\,a_{n}(D_{\Phi}),

which provides the key lower estimate

(3.12)

a_{n}(D_{\Phi})\geq(\log 2)\,a_{n}(C_{\Phi}|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{0}}).

Next, consider the orthogonal decomposition

\mathcal{H}^{2}=\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathcal{H}^{2}_{0}.

For
f=a_{1}+g
with
g\in\mathcal{H}^{2}_{0}
, we have
C_{\Phi}f=a_{1}+C_{\Phi}g
, so
C_{\Phi}=P+C_{\Phi}|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{0}}
, where
P
is the rank-one projection onto the constant functions. By the rank-one perturbation inequality for approximation numbers,
a_{n+1}(C_{\Phi})\leq a_{n}(C_{\Phi}|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{0}})
. Substituting this into (
3.12
) yields

(3.13)

a_{n}(D_{\Phi})\geq(\log 2)\,a_{n+1}(C_{\Phi}).

For our symbol
\Phi(s)=c_{1}+c_{2}2^{-s}
, the closure
\overline{\Phi(\mathbb{C}_{0})}\subset\overline{\mathbb{D}(c_{1},|c_{2}|)}
is compactly contained in
\mathbb{C}_{1/2}
since
\operatorname{Re}(c_{1})-|c_{2}|>1/2
. Hence,
C_{\Phi}
is a compact composition operator on
\mathcal{H}^{2}
with characteristic zero (
c_{0}=0
). By the Queffélec-Seip lower bound
[
25
, Theorem 1.2]
, there exist constants
C_{2}>0
and
q\in(0,1)
such that
a_{n+1}(C_{\Phi})\geq C_{2}q^{n}
. Consequently,

a_{n}(D_{\Phi})\geq C_{2}(\log 2)\,q^{n}\gtrsim q^{n},

which completes the proof of the lower bound.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

From the upper estimate (
3.9
), we have
a_{n}(D_{\Phi})\lesssim\rho^{n}
for every
\rho\in(r,1)
. Consequently,
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}(D_{\Phi})^{2}<\infty
, which implies that the composition-differentiation operator
D_{\Phi}
belongs to the Hilbert-Schmidt class
\mathcal{S}_{2}(\mathcal{H}^{2})
whenever
\operatorname{Re}(c_{1})-|c_{2}|>1/2
. More generally, for any symbol
\Phi\in\mathcal{G}_{0}
, if there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
\Phi(\mathbb{C}_{0})\subset\mathbb{C}_{1/2+\varepsilon}
, then
D_{\Phi}
is a Hilbert–Schmidt operator on
\mathcal{H}^{2}
.

4.
Spectral and operator-theoretic properties

4.1.
Self-adjointness and normality of
D_{\Phi}

In this section, we completely characterize the class of symbols
\Phi
for which the composition-differentiation operator
D_{\Phi}
is self-adjoint or normal on
\mathcal{H}^{2}
. In a related setting, Bayart
[
3
,
4
]
investigated spectral and operator-theoretic properties, including normality and self-adjointness, for composition operators
C_{\Phi}
on Hardy spaces
\mathcal{H}^{p}
(
0<p\leq\infty
) of Dirichlet series.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
D_{\Phi}
be a bounded composition-differentiation operator on
\mathcal{H}^{2}
. Then
D_{\Phi}
is self-adjoint if, and only if, the symbol
\Phi
is of the form
\Phi(s)=s+c
, for some
c>0
with
c\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.
To establish sufficiency, assume that the symbol takes the form
\Phi(s)=s+c
for some real constant
c>0
. Considering the standard orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}^{2}
given by
e_{n}(s):=n^{-s}
for
n\geq 1
, direct differentiation yields
e_{n}^{\prime}(s)=-(\log n)n^{-s}
. Consequently, the action of
D_{\Phi}
on the basis elements is given by

D_{\Phi}(e_{n})(s)=-(\log n)n^{-\Phi(s)}=-(\log n)n^{-c}e_{n}(s).

Thus,
D_{\Phi}
acts as a diagonal operator on
\mathcal{H}^{2}
with eigenvalues

(4.1)

\lambda_{n}:=-(\log n)n^{-c},\qquad n\geq 1.

Since
c\in\mathbb{R}
, every eigenvalue
\lambda_{n}
is real, which implies that the diagonal operator
D_{\Phi}
is formally symmetric and self-adjoint on its domain.
For
D_{\Phi}
to define a bounded linear operator on
\mathcal{H}^{2}
, its operator norm which coincides with the supremum of the modulus of its eigenvalues must be finite; that is,

\|D_{\Phi}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}=\sup_{n\geq 2}\,(\log n)n^{-c}<\infty.

This supremum is finite if, and only if,
c>0
. Indeed, if
c=0
, then
|\lambda_{n}|=\log n\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
; similarly, if
c<0
, then
|\lambda_{n}|=(\log n)n^{|c|}\to\infty
. Therefore,
\Phi(s)=s+c
with
c\in\mathbb{R}
and
c>0
provides a sufficient condition for
D_{\Phi}
to be a bounded self-adjoint operator on
\mathcal{H}^{2}
.

To prove necessity, consider a general symbol
\Phi
from the Gordon-Hedenmalm class of the form

\Phi(s)=c_{0}s+c+\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}c_{m}m^{-s},\qquad c_{0}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}.

We first assert that self-adjointness forces
c_{0}=1
. Indeed, consider
D_{\Phi}(e_{n})(s)=-(\log n)n^{-\Phi(s)}
. If
c_{0}=0
, then
D_{\Phi}(e_{n})
is a Dirichlet series whose frequencies are determined by the exponential of the non-linear Dirichlet series component; this structural asymmetry is incompatible with the matrix symmetry required for self-adjointness unless
D_{\Phi}\equiv 0
. Since
D_{\Phi}(e_{n})\neq 0
for
n\geq 2
,
D_{\Phi}
cannot be trivial. Consequently, every non-trivial self-adjoint composition-differentiation operator must have characteristic
c_{0}=1
, allowing us to write
\Phi(s)=s+c+\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}c_{m}m^{-s}
.

Next, we establish that
c_{m}=0
for all
m\geq 2
. Suppose, toward a contradiction, that
h(s):=\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}c_{m}m^{-s}\not\equiv 0
. Let
q\geq 2
be the smallest integer such that
c_{q}\neq 0
. Expressing
\Phi(s)=s+c+h(s)
, we obtain

D_{\Phi}(e_{n})(s)=-(\log n)n^{-\Phi(s)}=-(\log n)n^{-c}n^{-s}\exp\bigl(-(\log n)h(s)\bigr).

Expanding the exponential factor gives

\exp\bigl(-(\log n)h(s)\bigr)=1-(\log n)c_{q}q^{-s}+\sum_{m>q}d_{m}m^{-s}.

Consequently, the coefficient of
(nq)^{-s}=e_{nq}(s)
in the Dirichlet expansion of
D_{\Phi}(e_{n})
is
(\log n)^{2}n^{-c}c_{q}\neq 0
, implying
\langle D_{\Phi}e_{n},e_{nq}\rangle\neq 0
. By self-adjointness, we must have

(4.2)

\langle e_{n},D_{\Phi}e_{nq}\rangle\neq 0.

However, every term in the Dirichlet expansion of
D_{\Phi}e_{nq}
has frequencies of the form
nqk
for
k\geq 1
. Since
q\geq 2
,
nqk\neq n
for all
k\geq 1
, so
D_{\Phi}e_{nq}
contains no
n^{-s}
term. Thus,
\langle e_{n},D_{\Phi}e_{nq}\rangle=0
, directly contradicting (
4.2
). Therefore,
c_{m}=0
for all
m\geq 2
, which reduces the symbol to
\Phi(s)=s+c
.

Finally, we show that
c\in\mathbb{R}
. We have
D_{\Phi}(e_{n})(s)=-(\log n)n^{-c}e_{n}(s)
. Self-adjointness demands that all eigenvalues be real; hence,
-(\log n)n^{-c}\in\mathbb{R}
for all
n\geq 2
. Setting
c=a+ib
with
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
, we have
n^{-c}=n^{-a}e^{-ib\log n}
, which requires
e^{-ib\log n}\in\mathbb{R}
for all
n\geq 2
. In particular, taking
n=2
and
n=3
yields
b\log 2\in\pi\mathbb{Z}
and
b\log 3\in\pi\mathbb{Z}
. Since
\frac{\log 2}{\log 3}\notin\mathbb{Q}
, this implies
b=0
, so
c\in\mathbb{R}
. The condition
c>0
follows immediately from boundedness. This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
D_{\Phi}
be a bounded composition-differentiation operator on
\mathcal{H}^{2}
. Then
D_{\Phi}
is normal if, and only if, the symbol
\Phi
is of the form
\Phi(s)=s+c_{1}
, for some complex number
c_{1}
with
\real c_{1}>0
.

Remark 4.1
.

In contrast to the self-adjointness criterion established in Theorem
4.2
, the constant parameter
c_{1}
in the normality characterization need not be real. For instance, consider the symbol
\Phi(s)=s+1+i
. The action of the associated composition-differentiation operator on the standard orthonormal basis yields

D_{\Phi}(e_{n})(s)=-(\log n)n^{-(1+i)}e_{n}(s)=-(\log n)n^{-1}n^{-i}e_{n}(s),

showing that
D_{\Phi}
is diagonal and hence normal on
\mathcal{H}^{2}
. However, because its eigenvalues
\lambda_{n}=-(\log n)n^{-1}n^{-i}
are non-real for
n\geq 2
,
D_{\Phi}
fails to be self-adjoint.

Proof of Theorem
4.2
.

The sufficiency of the condition follows directly in a manner analogous to the proof of Theorem
4.1
. Indeed, when
\operatorname{Re}(c_{1})>0
, the composition-differentiation operator
D_{\Phi}
acts as a diagonal operator with respect to the standard orthonormal basis
\{e_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
; specifically,

D_{\Phi}(e_{n})=\lambda_{n}e_{n},\qquad\text{where }\lambda_{n}:=-(\log n)n^{-c_{1}}\text{ for }n\geq 2,\text{ and }\lambda_{1}=0.

Since
\operatorname{Re}(c)>0
, its sequence of eigenvalues is bounded:

\|D_{\Phi}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}=\sup_{n\geq 2}|\lambda_{n}|=\sup_{n\geq 2}\,(\log n)n^{-\operatorname{Re}(c_{1})}<\infty.

Consequently,
D_{\Phi}
is a bounded diagonal operator on
\mathcal{H}^{2}
. Since every bounded diagonal operator with respect to an orthonormal basis is normal yields
D_{\Phi}
is normal.

To prove necessity, consider a general symbol
\Phi
from the Gordon–Hedenmalm class of the form

\Phi(s)=c_{0}s+c_{1}+\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}c_{m}m^{-s},\qquad c_{0}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}.

We first assert that normality forces
c_{0}=1
. Suppose
c_{0}=0
. Since
D_{\Phi}(e_{1})=0
, normality implies that
\ker(D_{\Phi})=\ker(D_{\Phi}^{*})
, so
D_{\Phi}^{*}(e_{1})=0
. On the other hand, for
n\geq 2
,

D_{\Phi}(e_{n})(s)=-(\log n)n^{-c_{1}}\exp\Biggl(-(\log n)\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}c_{m}m^{-s}\Biggr).

The constant coefficient of this Dirichlet series is
-(\log n)n^{-c_{1}}\neq 0
, which yields
\langle D_{\Phi}e_{n},e_{1}\rangle\neq 0
. By the definition of the adjoint, this gives
\langle e_{n},D_{\Phi}^{*}e_{1}\rangle\neq 0
, implying
D_{\Phi}^{*}e_{1}\neq 0
, a contradiction.

Now consider the case
c_{0}\geq 2
and choose a prime number
p
. Every frequency appearing in
D_{\Phi}e_{n}
is a multiple of
n^{c_{0}}
. Since
c_{0}\geq 2
, no integer
n\geq 2
satisfies
n^{c_{0}}\mid p
. Hence,
e_{p}
is orthogonal to the range of
D_{\Phi}
, which implies
D_{\Phi}^{*}(e_{p})=0
and thus
D_{\Phi}D_{\Phi}^{*}(e_{p})=0
. On the other hand,
D_{\Phi}(e_{p})\neq 0
, which implies
D_{\Phi}^{*}D_{\Phi}(e_{p})\neq 0
, directly contradicting the normality of
D_{\Phi}
. Therefore, we must have
c_{0}=1
.

Next, we establish that
c_{m}=0
for all
m\geq 2
. Suppose, toward a contradiction, that there exists
m\geq 2
such that
c_{m}\neq 0
, and let
q:=\min\{m\geq 2:c_{m}\neq 0\}
. Express
\Phi(s)=s+c_{1}+h(s)
, where
h(s)=\sum_{d\geq q}c_{d}d^{-s}
. For
e_{q}(s)=q^{-s}
, we have

(4.3)

D_{\Phi}(e_{q})(s)=-(\log q)q^{-c_{1}}q^{-s}\exp\bigl(-(\log q)h(s)\bigr).

Expanding the exponential factor gives

\exp\bigl(-(\log q)h(s)\bigr)=1-(\log q)c_{q}q^{-s}+\sum_{m>q}d_{m}m^{-s}.

Substituting this expansion back into (
4.3
) yields

D_{\Phi}(e_{q})(s)=\lambda_{q}e_{q}+(\log q)^{2}q^{-c_{1}}c_{q}e_{q^{2}}+\dots,

where
\lambda_{q}:=-(\log q)q^{-c_{1}}
. In particular,
\|D_{\Phi}e_{q}\|^{2}>|\lambda_{q}|^{2}
. By the minimality of
q
, no basis element
e_{m}
with
m<q
produces the frequency
q
under
D_{\Phi}
. Hence,
D_{\Phi}^{*}e_{q}=\overline{\lambda_{q}}e_{q}
, which implies
\|D_{\Phi}^{*}e_{q}\|^{2}=|\lambda_{q}|^{2}
. By normality,
\|D_{\Phi}e_{q}\|=\|D_{\Phi}^{*}e_{q}\|
, yielding
|\lambda_{q}|^{2}<\|D_{\Phi}e_{q}\|^{2}=|\lambda_{q}|^{2}
, a contradiction. Therefore,
c_{m}=0
for all
m\geq 2
, which reduces the symbol to
\Phi(s)=s+c_{1}
.

Finally, since
D_{\Phi}(e_{n})(s)=-(\log n)n^{-c_{1}}e_{n}(s)
, boundedness of
D_{\Phi}
requires that

\|D_{\Phi}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}=\sup_{n\geq 2}\,(\log n)n^{-\operatorname{Re}(c_{1})}<\infty.

This holds if and only if
\operatorname{Re}(c_{1})>0
. This completes the proof.
∎

4.2.
Spectrum of
D_{\Phi}

We now turn to the discussion of the spectral properties of the composition-differentiation operator
D_{\Phi}
on the Hardy space
\mathcal{H}^{2}
. Recall that the spectrum of a bounded linear operator
T
acting on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is defined by

\sigma(T):=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\colon T-\lambda I_{\mathcal{H}}\text{ is not invertible}\}.

A detailed analysis of the spectrum of
D_{\Phi}
on the classical Hardy space of the unit disk
\mathbb{H}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
was previously established by Caughran and Schwartz
[
12
]
. In our setting, we shall characterize the spectrum
\sigma(D_{\Phi})
for the affine symbol
\Phi(s)=c_{1}+c_{2}2^{-s}
on
\mathcal{H}^{2}
, under the assumptions
c_{2}\neq 0
and
\operatorname{Re}(c_{1})>|c_{2}|+1/2
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\Phi(s)=c_{1}+c_{2}2^{-s}
with
c_{2}\neq 0
and
\operatorname{Re}(c_{1})>|c_{2}|+1/2
. Then the composition-differentiation operator
D_{\Phi}
is compact on
\mathcal{H}^{2}
,
0\in\sigma(D_{\Phi})
, and every non-zero spectral value is an eigenvalue. Moreover, if
\lambda\neq 0
is an eigenvalue of
D_{\Phi}
, then its corresponding eigen function belongs to the invariant subspace

\mathcal{M}=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{2^{-ks}\colon k\geq 0\},

and the eigenvalue equation reduces, under the identification
f(s)=F(z)
, for
z=2^{-s}
, to

-(\log 2)2^{-c_{1}}e^{-c_{2}(\log 2)z}F^{\prime}\Bigl(2^{-c_{1}}e^{-c_{2}(\log 2)z}\Bigr)=\lambda F(z).

Proof.
Suppose that
\lambda\neq 0
belongs to
\sigma(D_{\Phi})
. Since
D_{\Phi}
is compact,
\lambda
is an eigenvalue. Let
f\in\mathcal{H}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
satisfy

D_{\Phi}f=\lambda f.

Writing
f(s)=\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}n^{-s}
, the action of
D_{\Phi}
on each basis function yields

(4.5)

D_{\Phi}f(s)=-\sum_{n\geq 2}a_{n}(\log n)n^{-c_{1}}\exp\bigl(-c_{2}(\log n)2^{-s}\bigr).

The right-hand side of (
4.5
) is a function of
2^{-s}
alone. Since
\lambda\neq 0
, the eigenvalue equation
f=\frac{1}{\lambda}D_{\Phi}f
implies that
f
is likewise a function of
2^{-s}
. Hence,
f\in\mathcal{M}=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{2^{-ks}\colon k\geq 0\}
, so there exists
F\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
such that
f(s)=F(2^{-s})
. Setting
\alpha:=2^{-c_{1}}
and
\beta:=c_{2}\log 2
,
we observe that

2^{-\Phi(s)}=2^{-c_{1}-c_{2}2^{-s}}=\alpha e^{-\beta 2^{-s}}.

Differentiating
f(s)=F(2^{-s})
via the chain rule yields

f^{\prime}\bigl(\Phi(s)\bigr)=-(\log 2)\alpha e^{-\beta 2^{-s}}F^{\prime}\bigl(\alpha e^{-\beta 2^{-s}}\bigr).

Consequently, the eigenvalue equation
D_{\Phi}f=\lambda f
on
\mathcal{H}^{2}
is equivalent to

(4.6)

-(\log 2)\alpha e^{-\beta z}F^{\prime}(\alpha e^{-\beta z})=\lambda F(z),\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Denote
\varphi(z):=2^{-\Phi(s)}=\alpha e^{-\beta z}.

Thus, determining the non-zero spectrum of
D_{\Phi}
on
\mathcal{H}^{2}
reduces to studying the eigenvalue problem (
4.6
) for the weighted composition-differentiation operator

(4.7)

TF(z):=-(\log 2)\alpha e^{-\beta z}F^{\prime}(\alpha e^{-\beta z})=-(\log 2)M_{\varphi}D_{\varphi}(F)(z).

acting on the classical Hardy space of the unit disk
\mathbb{H}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
, where
M
is the classical multiplication operator on
\mathbb{D}
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Moreover, finding the precise non-zero eigenvalue
\lambda
of
D_{\Phi}
in (
4.7
) presents a significant challenge and is left as an open problem for future investigation.

Indeed, suppose that
\lambda\neq 0
is an eigenvalue of
T
with corresponding eigen function
F\in\mathbb{H}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. Since

\varphi^{\prime}(z)=-\beta\varphi(z)=-c_{2}(\log 2)\varphi(z),

the eigenvalue equation
TF=\lambda F
can be written equivalently as

(4.8)

(F\circ\varphi)^{\prime}=c_{2}\lambda F.

Differentiating both sides of (
4.8
)
n
times yields, for every
n\geq 0
,

(F\circ\varphi)^{(n+1)}(z)=c_{2}\lambda F^{(n)}(z).

Applying Faà di Bruno’s formula
[
14
, Section 3.4]
in terms of the partial Bell polynomials
B_{n+1,k}

[
14
, Section 3.3]
, we obtain

(4.9)

c_{2}\lambda F^{(n)}(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}F^{(k)}(\varphi(z))\,B_{n+1,k}\left(\varphi^{\prime}(z),\varphi^{\prime\prime}(z),\dots,\varphi^{(n-k+2)}(z)\right).

Let
p\in\mathbb{D}
denote the unique fixed point of
\varphi
, so that
p=\varphi(p)=\alpha e^{-\beta p}
. Since

\varphi^{(j)}(z)=(-\beta)^{j}\varphi(z),

evaluating at
z=p
gives

\varphi^{(j)}(p)=(-\beta)^{j}p,\quad j\geq 1.

By the homogeneity property of partial Bell polynomials and the identity

B_{m,k}(1,1,\dots,1)=\left\{\begin{matrix}m\\
k\end{matrix}\right\},

where
\left\{\begin{smallmatrix}m\\
k\end{smallmatrix}\right\}
denotes the Stirling numbers of the second kind
[
14
]
, we have

B_{m,k}\left(-\beta p,\beta^{2}p,\dots,(-\beta)^{m}p\right)=(-\beta)^{m}p^{k}\left\{\begin{matrix}m\\
k\end{matrix}\right\}.

Evaluating (
4.9
) at
z=p
then yields the recurrence

(4.10)

c_{2}\lambda F^{(n)}(p)=(-\beta)^{n+1}\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\left\{\begin{matrix}n+1\\
k\end{matrix}\right\}p^{k}F^{(k)}(p),\quad n\geq 0.

In particular, isolating the leading term
k=n+1
(using
\left\{\begin{smallmatrix}n+1\\
n+1\end{smallmatrix}\right\}=1
) provides the explicit derivative recurrence relation:

(4.11)

F^{(n+1)}(p)=\frac{c_{2}\lambda}{(-\beta)^{n+1}p^{n+1}}F^{(n)}(p)-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left\{\begin{matrix}n+1\\
k\end{matrix}\right\}p^{k-n-1}F^{(k)}(p).

For
n=0
, (
4.11
) reduces to

\lambda F(p)=-(\log 2)pF^{\prime}(p).

Consequently, under the normalization
F(p)=1
, the recurrence relation (
4.11
) uniquely determines all higher derivatives
F^{\prime}(p),F^{\prime\prime}(p),\dots
in terms of the candidate eigenvalue
\lambda
.

This argument establishes a Bell-polynomial approach to the spectral analysis of the composition-differentiation operator
D_{\Phi}
. In contrast to symbols of the form
\varphi(z)=az^{r}
(r
\geq
2), whose higher-order derivatives vanish at the fixed point, all derivatives of the exponential symbol
\varphi
remain strictly non-zero at
p
. Crucially, the resulting Bell polynomials simplify to Stirling numbers of the second kind, thereby yielding a systematic necessary condition for a non-zero scalar
\lambda
to be an eigenvalue. To achieve a full characterization of the eigenvalue, it remains to identify the precise values of
\lambda
for which the formal eigenfunction solution
F
defined by this recurrence actually belongs to the Hardy space
\mathbb{H}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
.

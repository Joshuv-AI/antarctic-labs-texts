Powers of abelian varieties over ℚ ⁡ ( t ) ¯ \overline{\mathbb{Q}(t)} not isogenous to a Jacobian
Olivier de Gaay Fortman and Ananth N. Shankar

Abstract
. We prove the existence of abelian varieties over ℚ ⁡ ( t ) ¯ \overline{\mathbb{Q}(t)} with no power isogenous to a Jacobian. Moreover, given a positive integer N N , we prove the existence of abelian varieties over ℚ ⁡ ( t ) ¯ \overline{\mathbb{Q}(t)} with maximal monodromy such that the n n th power is not isogenous to a Jacobian for n ≤ N n\leq N . We make use of an Arakelov inequality established by Lu and Zuo, as well as intersection theoretic methods, to prove our main results.



1.
Introduction

Every abelian variety
A
over an algebraically closed field is dominated by a Jacobian, but not always isogenous to one: over
\mathbb{C}
this follows from a countability argument, over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
this was proven by Chai–Oort and Tsimerman
[
3
,
21
]
, and over
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{p}(t)}
this was proven
by the second named author and Tsimerman

[
19
]
.
1
1

1

The case of finite fields is expected to be different as suggested by heuristics, independently offered by
[
18
]
and Poonen.

This leaves open the question whether some power of
A
is isogenous to a Jacobian.
In
[
5
]
, the first named author and Schreieder
showed that the answer is again no in general: for any
g\geq 4
,
there exists a
complex abelian variety of dimension
g
with no power isogenous to a Jacobian, and for
g=5
one can find intermediate Jacobians of smooth cubic threefolds among such examples. The authors ask whether one can find
abelian varieties
over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
with no power isogenous to a Jacobian, see
[
5
, Remark 1.6]
.

The first main result of this paper says that if
g\geq 16
is a power of
2
, then such examples exist at least over
\smash{\overline{\mathbb{Q}(t)}}
. By a Jacobian, we mean the Jacobian of a projective stable curve of compact type.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
g=2^{d}
with
d\geq 4
. Then there exists an abelian variety
A
of dimension
g
over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}(t)}
with no power
A^{n}
isogenous to a Jacobian.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{g}
be the moduli space of principally polarized abelian varieties of dimension
g
. A point
x\in\mathcal{A}_{g}
is called
Hodge generic
if
x
is not contained in a special subvariety of
\mathcal{A}_{g}
of positive codimension—equivalently, the associated Mumford–Tate group is
\GSp_{2g}
.
In
[
21
]
, Tsimerman showed that one can find CM abelian varieties of dimension
g\geq 4
which are not isogenous to a Jacobian; in
[
13
]
and
[
19
]
, the authors (using different methods) showed that there are also Hodge generic
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
points of
\mathcal{A}_{g}
with that property.

As the proof of Theorem
1.1
shows, for
g=\smash{2^{d}}\geq 16
, there are
x\in\smash{\mathcal{A}_{g}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}(t)})}
, the geometric generic point of a special curve
C\subset\mathcal{A}_{g}
, such that
A_{x}
has no power isogenous to a Jacobian. It is natural to ask whether there exist Hodge generic points
x\in\smash{\mathcal{A}_{g}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}(t)})}
with that same property. Our second main result says that at least for bounded powers, the answer is yes.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
g=2^{d}\geq 16
and
N\geq 1
. Then there exists a Hodge generic point
x=[A]\in\mathcal{A}_{g}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}(t)})
such that the corresponding abelian variety
A
over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}(t)}
has no power
A^{n}
with
n\leq N
which is isogenous to a Jacobian.

1.1.
Outline of proof

We now briefly describe our methods. Let
\mathcal{A}_{g,[r]}
be the moduli space of principally polarized abelian varieties of dimension
g
with symplectic level
r\geq 3
structure. The proof of Theorem
1.1
uses an Arakelov inequality due to Lu and Zuo
[
12
, Theorem 1.4]
, which says in particular that given a smooth proper curve
C\subset\mathcal{A}_{g,[r]}
with induced abelian scheme
\pi\colon A\to C
, one has
\deg(\pi_{\ast}\Omega_{A/C})<(g/2)\cdot\deg(\Omega_{C})
whenever
g\geq 12
and
C
is generically contained in the Torelli locus. On the other hand, this becomes an equality if
C
is a Shimura curve of Mumford type (cf. Definition
3.3
), from which Lu and Zuo deduce that such Shimura curves cannot be generically contained in the Torelli locus if
g\geq 12
. We apply their Arakelov inequality to show that, more generally, the
n
-th power
A_{\bar{\eta}}^{n}
cannot be isogenous to a Jacobian for every
n
, for
\eta\in C
the generic point.

We prove the above for Shimura curves of Mumford type
C\subset\mathcal{A}_{g,[r]}
which are not necessarily smooth. Such curves exist in
\mathcal{A}_{g,[r]}
for each
r\geq 3
. For technical reasons, we proceed in the second part of the paper to assume that
r
is prime. We pick a suitable surface
S\subset\mathcal{A}_{g,[r]}
that contains
C
as well as Hodge generic points. Theorem
1.1
implies that the induced abelian scheme
B\rightarrow S
has the property that the
n
-th power
B_{\bar{\eta}}^{n}
is not isogenous to a Jacobian for every
n
, where now
\eta\in S
denotes the generic point of
S
. Bounding
n
, we then use an intersection-theoretic argument to find a curve
C_{\textrm{gen}}\subset S
over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
that satisfies the conclusion of Theorem
1.2
.

2.
Notation and conventions

We work in characteristic zero.
For an integer
r\geq 3
,
we denote by
{\mathcal{A}}_{g,[r]}
the moduli scheme of
g
-dimensional principally polarized abelian varieties with symplectic level
r
structure, and by
{\mathcal{X}}\to\mathcal{A}_{g,[r]}
the universal family.
For an abelian scheme
f\colon A\to S
over a smooth variety
S
, the
Hodge line bundle
is the line bundle
\det(f_{\ast}\Omega_{A/S})
, which we often simply denote by
L=\det(f_{\ast}\Omega_{A/S})
. If
S
is a proper curve, then the degree of the vector bundle
f_{\ast}\Omega_{A/S}
is the degree of its determinant

. A
Shimura curve

will be an irreducible component of a one-dimensional Shimura subvariety of
\mathcal{A}_{g,[r]}
.
A subvariety

\pi_{1}^{\textnormal{\'{e}t}}(Z)\to\pi_{1}^{\textnormal{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{A}_{g,[r]})

3.
Shimura curves of Mumford type

In this section, we explain the definition of Shimura curves of Mumford type, which are certain special curves
C\subset{\mathcal{A}}_{g,[r]}
.
We follow the construction of
[
22
, Section 5]
, see also
[
12
, p. 52]
.

Let
d>1
be an integer.
Let
F
be a totally real field of degree
d
over
\mathbb{Q}
.
Let
A
be a quaternion algebra over
F
, which is which at one real place of
F
and ramified at its other real places.
Let
D={\mathrm{Cor}}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(A)
be the corestriction of
A
, which is a central simple algebra over
\mathbb{Q}
.
That is, put
D_{1}=\otimes_{\sigma}\left(A\otimes_{F,\sigma}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\right)
, where
\sigma
ranges over
\Hom(F,\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
, and then
D=(D_{1})^{\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
F
and
A
be as above.
Then either one of the following conditions holds:

(1)

We have
D\simeq{\mathrm{M}}_{2^{d}}(\mathbb{Q})
and
d
is odd.

(2)

We have
D\simeq{\mathrm{M}}_{2^{d-1}}(B)
for some non-split quaternion algebra
B
over
\mathbb{Q}
, which splits over a quadratic extension
L=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{b})
of
\mathbb{Q}
which is imaginary if and only if
d
is even.

Proof.

This is shown in

[
22
, Lemma 5.7]
. Alternatively, see
[
15
, Proposition 4.2, item (3)]
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Let
d>1
be an integer, let
F
be a totally real number field of degree
d
, and let
\Sigma
be the set of places of
F
. Let
A
be a quaternion algebra which is split at one real place of
F
and ramified at its other real places. Such an algebra exists because for every finite set
R\subset\Sigma
of non-complex places such that
\left|R\right|
is even, there exists a quaternion algebra
A
over
F
which ramifies exactly at the places
v\in R
(see
[
23
, Theorem 14.6.1]
). If
d
is even then

D={\mathrm{Cor}}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(A)\simeq{\mathrm{M}}_{2^{d-1}}(B)
for some non-split quaternion algebra, see Lemma
3.1
.
If
d
is odd, then one can choose
A
such that
D\simeq{\mathrm{M}}_{2^{d}}(\mathbb{Q})
(resp.
D\simeq{\mathrm{M}}_{2^{d-1}}(B)
for some non-split
B
). Indeed, this is true because corestriction of Brauer groups along a finite morphism of schemes commutes with base change (see
[
4
, Proposition 3.8.1]
), and the corestriction map of Brauer groups attached to a finite extension of local fields commutes with the local invariant (see
[
11
, p. 237, Theorem 10]
).

Let again
d>1
,
F
a totally real field of degree
d
over
\mathbb{Q}
,
A
a quaternion algebra over
F
which is split at one real place of
F
and ramified at its other real places, and
D={\mathrm{Cor}}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(A)
.
By Lemma
3.1
, there is an embedding

(3.1)

\displaystyle D\hookrightarrow{\mathrm{M}}_{2^{d+1}}(\mathbb{Q}),\quad\quad\epsilon\in\left\{0,1\right\}.

Let
\mathcal{O}\subset A
be an order in
A
, and let
\mathcal{O}^{1}\subset\mathcal{O}^{\ast}
be the subgroup of units that have reduced norm one. Let
\sigma\colon F\to\mathbb{R}
be such that
A\otimes_{F,\sigma}\mathbb{R}\simeq{\mathrm{M}}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
; then the image of
\mathcal{O}^{1}\subset A^{\ast}
in
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{R})\subset{\mathrm{M}}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
under this isomorphism is contained in
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, which gives a natural embedding
\mathcal{O}^{1}\hookrightarrow\SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Let
\Gamma\subset\mathcal{O}^{1}
be a finite index torsion-free arithmetic subgroup. Then
C_{1}\coloneqq\Gamma\setminus{\mathbb{H}}
is a smooth proper curve, see
[
20
, Chapter 9]
. The diagonal embedding
\mathcal{O}\to\otimes_{\sigma\in\Hom(F,\overline{\mathbb{Q}})}\left(A\otimes_{F,\sigma}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\right)
induces an embedding
\mathcal{O}^{1}\to D^{\ast}
, the embedding (
3.1
) induces an embedding
D^{\ast}\hookrightarrow\GL_{2^{d+\epsilon}}(\mathbb{Q})
,
and the composition

\pi_{1}^{\textnormal{\'{e}t}}(C_{1})=\Gamma\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}^{1}\hookrightarrow D^{\ast}\hookrightarrow\GL_{2^{d+\epsilon}}(\mathbb{Q}),\quad\quad\epsilon\in\left\{0,1\right\},

corresponds to a local system on the curve
C_{1}
, which, by
[
22
, Lemma 5.10]
,
underlies a family of
g
-dimensional polarized abelian varieties

B_{1}\to C_{1},\qquad g=2^{d+\epsilon-1}.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
r\geq 3
be an integer. A
Shimura curve of Mumford type
in
\mathcal{A}_{g,[r]}
(see
[
22
]
) is a proper Shimura curve
C\subset\mathcal{A}_{g,[r]}
of the following form. Consider a polarized abelian scheme
B_{1}\to C_{1}
as defined above.
Pick an étale cover
C_{2}\to C_{1}
with pull-back
B_{2}\to C_{2}
, so that there is a principally polarized abelian scheme
A_{2}\to C_{2}
isogenous to the polarized abelian scheme
B_{2}\to C_{2}
, such that
A_{2}\to C_{2}
carries a symplectic level
r
structure. This gives a morphism
C_{2}\to{\mathcal{A}}_{g,[r]}
, and we let
C\subset{\mathcal{A}}_{g,[r]}
denote its image.

Let us say that the Shimura curve of Mumford type
C
is
split
(resp.
non-split
) if we are in case (
1
) (resp. (
2
)) of Lemma
3.1
.

In dimension
g=4
, the construction of such curves is due to Mumford (cf.
[
17
]
), whence the name.

Remark 3.4
.

Let
r\geq 3
be an integer and let
C\subset\mathcal{A}_{g,[r]}
be a Shimura curve of Mumford type. Then there exists an integer
s\geq 3
with
r\mid s
and a Shimura curve of Mumford type
C^{\prime}\subset{\mathcal{A}}_{g,[s]}
which is smooth, and such that
C^{\prime}
dominates

with respect to the finite étale cover
{\mathcal{A}}_{g,[s]}\to\mathcal{A}_{g,[r]}
. Indeed, this follows from
[
7
, Proposition 1.15]
(compare
[
5
, Lemma B.6]
).

Let
C\subset{\mathcal{A}}_{g,[r]}
be a Shimura curve of Mumford type. Consider the corresponding family of
g
-dimensional
principally polarized abelian varieties

(3.2)

\displaystyle f\colon A\to C,\qquad\text{where}\qquad g=\begin{cases}2^{d-1}&\text{in case \eqref{case:i},}\\
2^{d}&\text{in case \eqref{case:ii}.}\end{cases}

Then, by
[
22
, Lemma 5.9]
, we have for the generic fiber

of

, that

(3.3)

\displaystyle\End(A_{\eta})\otimes\mathbb{Q}=\begin{cases}\;\;\mathbb{Q}&\text{in case \eqref{case:i};}\\
\begin{cases}\text{a totally definite (resp.~indefinite)}&\\
\text{quaternion algebra over $\mathbb{Q}$ if $d$}&\\
\text{is even (resp.\ odd)}&\end{cases}&\text{in case \eqref{case:ii}}.\end{cases}

Lemma 3.5
.

In the above notation, the geometric generic fiber
A_{\bar{\eta}}
is simple. More precisely,
\End(A_{\bar{\eta}})\otimes\mathbb{Q}=\End(A_{\eta})\otimes\mathbb{Q}
, and this is given by (
3.3
).

Proof.

Let
\Gamma^{\prime}\subset\Gamma
be a finite index subgroup. It suffices to prove that the representation of
\Gamma^{\prime}
induced by the Betti local system is irreducible. This follows from the general fact that if
G
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebraic group and
V
a representation of
G
such that
V_{\mathbb{C}}
is irreducible, then
V_{\mathbb{C}}
stays irreducible when restricted to any Zariski-dense subgroup of
G(\mathbb{Q})
.
∎

4.
Abelian schemes over special curves with no power isogenous to a Jacobian

The goal of this section is to prove our first main theorem, i.e. Theorem
1.1
from the introduction. The candidate for the abelian variety over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}(t)}
that will turn out not to admit any power isogenous to a Jacobian, will be given by the geometric generic fiber of the natural abelian scheme over a Shimura curve of Mumford type
C\subset\mathcal{A}_{g,[r]}
defined in the previous section.

4.1.
Generalities on Hodge line bundles of abelian schemes

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
S
be a smooth variety over
\mathbb{C}
.

(1)

Let
\pi\colon S^{\prime}\to S
a morphism of smooth varieties. Let
f\colon A\to S
be an abelian scheme with base change
f^{\prime}\colon A^{\prime}\to S^{\prime}
. Define
L=\det(f_{\ast}\Omega_{A/S})
and
L^{\prime}=\det(f^{\prime}_{\ast}\Omega_{A^{\prime}/S^{\prime}})
. Then
\pi^{\ast}(L)\simeq L^{\prime}
.

(2)

Let
\phi\colon A_{1}\to A_{2}
be an isogeny of abelian schemes
f_{i}\colon A_{i}\to S
. Let
L_{i}=\det({f_{i}}_{\ast}\Omega_{A_{i}/S})
. There is a canonical isomorphism
L_{1}\simeq L_{2}.

Proof.

This is well-known, and can e.g. be deduced from the canonical isomorphism
f_{\ast}\Omega_{A/S}\simeq e^{\ast}\Omega_{A/S}
where
e\colon S\to A
denotes the origin section.
∎

Let
f\colon A\to C
be an abelian scheme of relative dimension
g
over a smooth proper curve
C
, with Hodge line bundle
L=\det(f_{\ast}\Omega_{A/C})
. We have
\deg(L)\leq(g/2)\cdot\deg(\Omega_{C})
by
[
8
]
. Let
\pi\colon C^{\prime}\to C
be a finite morphism of smooth proper curves, of degree
d\geq 1
and ramification divisor
R\subset C^{\prime}
. Let
A^{\prime}\to C^{\prime}
denote the base change of
A\to C
along
\pi
, and define
L^{\prime}=\det(f^{\prime}_{\ast}\Omega_{A^{\prime}/C^{\prime}})
as the Hodge line bundle of
A^{\prime}\to C^{\prime}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

In the above situation,
let
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
such that

\deg(L)+m=(g/2)\cdot\deg(\Omega_{C})
. Then
\deg(L^{\prime})+d\cdot m=(g/2)\cdot(\deg(\Omega_{C^{\prime}})-\deg(R))
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.1
, we have
\deg(L^{\prime})=d\cdot\deg(L)
.
Moreover, we have
\deg(\Omega_{C}\textquoteright)=d\cdot\deg(\Omega_{C})+\deg(R)
.
From these two equalities, we conclude that

\deg(L^{\prime})+d\cdot m=d\cdot(g/2)\cdot\Omega_{C}=(g/2)\cdot\left(\deg(\Omega_{C^{\prime}})-\deg(R)\right)
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
Z
be a smooth variety over
\mathbb{C}
. Let
f\colon A\to Z
be an abelian scheme, and
g\colon A^{n}\to Z
its
n
-th self fiber product over
Z
. Let
h\colon Q\to Z
be an abelian scheme and
\phi\colon A^{n}\to Q
an isogeny of abelian schemes. Define the associated Hodge line bundles as
L_{f}=\det(f_{\ast}\Omega_{A/Z})
,
L_{g}=\det(g_{\ast}\Omega_{A^{n}/Z})
and
L_{h}=\det(h_{\ast}\Omega_{Q/Z})
. Then the following assertions are true.

(1)

We have
L_{h}\simeq L_{g}\simeq(L_{f})^{\otimes n}
.

(2)

Assume that
Z
is a curve and
that
\deg(L_{f})=(g/2)\cdot\deg(\Omega_{Z})
. Then
\deg(L_{h})=(ng/2)\cdot\deg(\Omega_{Z})
.

Proof.

The first item follows from Lemma
4.1
and the canonical isomorphism
g_{\ast}\Omega_{A^{n}/Z}\simeq(f_{\ast}\Omega_{A/Z})^{\oplus n}
.
If
Z
is a curve and
\deg(L_{f})=(g/2)\cdot\deg(\Omega_{Z})
, then it follows that
\deg(L_{h})=\deg(L_{g})=n\cdot\deg(L_{f})=(ng)/2\cdot\deg(\Omega_{Z})
.
∎

4.2.
Arakelov bound for Shimura curves

We recall the following result:

Theorem 4.4

(Möller)
.

Let
C\subset\mathcal{A}_{g,[r]}
be a smooth irreducible component of a one-dimensional Shimura subvariety of
\mathcal{A}_{g,[r]}
. Then the
corresponding abelian scheme
f\colon A\to C
reaches the Arakelov bound. In particular, if
C
is proper and the local system
R^{1}f_{\ast}\mathbb{Q}
has no non-trivial unitary part, then

(4.1)

\displaystyle\deg(L)=(g/2)\cdot\deg(\Omega_{C}),\quad\quad L=\det(f_{\ast}\Omega_{A/C}).

Proof.

See
[
14
, Theorem 1.2]
.
∎

Corollary 4.5
.

Any smooth Shimura curve of Mumford type
C\subset\mathcal{A}_{g,[r]}
satisfies the equality (
4.1
).

Proof.

Such a curve is smooth and proper, and the natural abelian scheme
f\colon A\to C
has no non-trivial unitary part in
R^{1}f_{\ast}\mathbb{Q}
, because the monodromy is irreducible and non-compact.
∎

4.3.
Proof of the theorem for special curves

We have the following result, of which Theorem
1.1
is a direct consequence (as we show below).

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
g=2^{d}
with
d\geq 4
. Let
r\geq 3
and let
C\subset\mathcal{A}_{g,[r]}
be a proper curve which is a Shimura curve of Mumford type, see Definition
3.3
. Let
A\to C
be the induced abelian scheme. Then no power of the geometric generic fiber
A_{\bar{\eta}}
is isogenous to the Jacobian of a stable curve.

Proof.

Assume for a contradiction that for some
n\geq 1
, there exists a stable compact type curve
X=\cup_{i}X_{i}
over
\smash{\overline{\mathbb{Q}(t)}}
, with irreducible components
X_{1},\dotsc,X_{k}
(which are smooth projective connected curves over
\smash{\overline{\mathbb{Q}(t)}}
), such that
A_{\bar{\eta}}^{n}
is isogenous to the Jacobian
JX=JX_{1}\times\cdots\times JX_{k}
. By Lemma
3.5
, the abelian variety
A_{\bar{\eta}}
is simple. Hence, by Poincaré irreducibility, we have that
A_{\bar{\eta}}^{n^{\prime}}
is isogenous to
JX_{1}
for some
n^{\prime}\leq n
. In other words, to arrive at a contradiction, we may assume that
k=1
, and that there exists an isogeny

(4.2)

\displaystyle A_{\bar{\eta}}^{n}\to JX

between
A_{\bar{\eta}}^{n}
and the Jacobian
JX
of a smooth projective connected curve
X
over
\smash{\overline{\mathbb{Q}(t)}}
.
In view of Remark
3.4
,
up to replacing
{\mathcal{A}}_{g,[r]}
by some finite étale cover
{\mathcal{A}}_{g,[r^{\prime}]}\to{\mathcal{A}}_{g,[r]}
with
r\mid r^{\prime}
and
C
by an irreducible component of its preimage, we may assume that
C
is smooth, and that the kernel of (
4.2
) is the geometric generic fiber
M_{\bar{\eta}}\subset A_{\bar{\eta}}^{n}[r]
of a subgroup scheme
M\subset A^{n}[r]
which is finite étale over
C
.
Taking the quotient by
M
yields an abelian scheme
A^{n}/M\to C
such that
(A^{n}/M)_{\bar{\eta}}=JX
.

Consider the polarization on
A^{n}_{\bar{\eta}}
pulled back from the canonical polarization on
JX
under (
4.2
). As
A_{\bar{\eta}}
is principally polarized, we can view this polarization as an
n\times n
-matrix with values in
\End(A_{\bar{\eta}})
. As
\End(A_{\bar{\eta}})=\End(A_{\eta})
by Lemma
3.5
, this yields a polarization on
A_{\eta}^{n}
which extends to a polarization on the abelian scheme
A^{n}\to C
, which in turn descends to a principal polarization on
A^{n}/M\to C
as it does so on the geometric generic fiber. We then let
\tilde{C}\to C
be a finite étale cover so that if
\tilde{M}\subset\tilde{A}\to\tilde{C}
denotes the pull-back of
M\subset A\to C
, then the principally polarized abelian scheme
\tilde{Q}\coloneqq\tilde{A}^{n}/\tilde{M}\to\tilde{C}
admits a sympletic level
s
structure, for some
s\geq 3
. This gives a finite morphism
\tilde{C}\to{\mathcal{A}}_{ng,[s]}
, and we let
C^{\prime}\subset{\mathcal{A}}_{ng,[s]}
denote its image. Define
Q^{\prime}\to C^{\prime}
as the induced abelian scheme over
C^{\prime}
.

Note that
C^{\prime}\subset{\mathcal{A}}_{ng,[s]}
is a special subvariety. Hence, by
[
5
, Lemma B.6]
, there exists a finite étale cover
{\mathcal{A}}_{ng,[s^{\prime}]}\to{\mathcal{A}}_{ng,[s]}
with
s\mid s^{\prime}
and a smooth special subvariety
C^{\prime\prime}\subset{\mathcal{A}}_{ng,[s^{\prime}]}
which is an irreducible component of the preimage of
C
in
{\mathcal{A}}_{ng,[s^{\prime}]}
. Up to replacing
\tilde{C}
by a finite étale cover, we may thus assume that
C^{\prime}
is smooth.

The situation may be depicted in the following diagram, in which the three curves
C^{\prime},\tilde{C}
and
C
are smooth,
the morphism
\tilde{C}\to C^{\prime}
is finite, the morphism
\tilde{C}\to C

(2)

Lemma 6.4

The hypotheses imply that

Proposition 8.1

. We then define
\tilde{S}\subset\widetilde{{\mathcal{A}}}_{{\mathrm{BB}}}
as a general complete intersection of ample hypersurfaces containing the smooth disconnected curve
\tilde{C}_{{\mathrm{sp}}}\sqcup C_{{\mathrm{mon}}}\subset\widetilde{{\mathcal{A}}}
, such that
\tilde{S}
is not contained in the exceptional locus of the birational morphism
\widetilde{{\mathcal{A}}}_{{\mathrm{BB}}}\to\overline{{\mathcal{A}}}_{g,[r]}
. Then
\tilde{S}
is smooth by
[
1
]
.
Moreover,
\tilde{S}
is projective, and
\tilde{S}\subset\widetilde{{\mathcal{A}}}
because the boundary
\widetilde{{\mathcal{A}}}_{{\mathrm{BB}}}\setminus\widetilde{{\mathcal{A}}}
has codimension

. Since
\tilde{S}
contains
\tilde{C}_{{\mathrm{mon}}}
, the map
\pi_{1}^{\textnormal{\'{e}t}}(\tilde{S})\to\pi_{1}^{\textnormal{\'{e}t}}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{A}}})
is surjective. As the map
\pi_{1}^{\textnormal{\'{e}t}}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{A}}})\to\pi_{1}^{\textnormal{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{A}_{g,[r]})
is an isomorphism,
it follows that the map
\pi_{1}^{\textnormal{\'{e}t}}(\tilde{S})\to\pi_{1}^{\textnormal{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{A}_{g,[r]})
is surjective.
Define
S\subset\mathcal{A}_{g,[r]}
as the image of
\tilde{S}
under
\widetilde{{\mathcal{A}}}\to\mathcal{A}_{g,[r]}
. As
\tilde{S}\subset\widetilde{{\mathcal{A}}}
is not contained in the exceptional locus,
\tilde{S}
is the proper transform of
S
, hence
\tilde{S}\to S
is projective and birational. This establishes the above claim.

Our goal is to construct a curve
C\subset S
with maximal monodromy defined over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
, with the property that the geometric generic fiber of the induced abelian scheme
A\to C
has no
n
-th power for
n\leq N
isogenous to a Jacobian. This suffices to prove the theorem, because any such a curve defines a Hodge generic
\overline{\mathbb{Q}(t)}
point of
\mathcal{A}_{g,[r]}
, and hence of
{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
, that satisfies the conclusion of Theorem
1.2
.

For each
n

1\leq n\leq N
, let
\overline{L}_{n}\in\textnormal{Pic}(\overline{{\mathcal{A}}}_{ng,[r]})
be the Hodge line bundle on the Baily–Borel compactification
\overline{{\mathcal{A}}}_{ng,[r]}\supset{\mathcal{A}}_{ng,[r]}
. Let

such that
\smash{\overline{L}_{n}^{\otimes k_{n}}}=\iota_{n}^{\ast}(\mathcal{O}(1))
for a closed embedding
\smash{\iota_{n}\colon\overline{{\mathcal{A}}}_{ng,[r]}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{N_{n}}}
into some projective space
\mathbb{P}^{N_{n}}
.
Let
I_{n}
be a finite set and
\overline{H}_{n,i}\subset\overline{{\mathcal{A}}}_{ng,[r]}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N_{n}}

(i\in I_{n})
be hypersurfaces such that, for
H_{n,i}=\overline{H}_{n,i}\cap{\mathcal{A}}_{ng,[r]}
, we have:

(9.1)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{T}}_{ng,[r]}=\bigcap_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq n\leq N\\
i\in I_{n}\end{subarray}}H_{n,i}.

Let
\deg(Z\subset\mathbb{P}^{N_{n}})
denote the degree of a subvariety of
\mathbb{P}^{N_{n}}
. Then

(9.2)

\displaystyle\deg(C\subset\mathbb{P}^{N_{n}})=k_{n}\cdot\deg(C)\quad\text{ and }\quad\deg(S\subset\mathbb{P}^{N_{n}})=k_{n}^{2}\cdot\deg(S).

Let

(9.3)

\displaystyle d=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq n\leq N\\
i\in I_{n}\end{subarray}}\left\{k_{n}\cdot\deg\left(\overline{H}_{n,i}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N_{n}}\right)\right\}.

By Proposition
8.1
,
there exits a smooth curve
\tilde{C}\subset\tilde{S}
defined over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
, whose image we denote by

, such that
\tilde{C}\to C
is birational,
\pi_{1}^{\textnormal{\'{e}t}}(\tilde{C})\to\pi_{1}^{\textnormal{\'{e}t}}(\tilde{S})
is surjective,
\tau(C)\not\subset S
for every non-trivial Hecke correspondence
\tau

(9.4)

\displaystyle\deg(C)>d\cdot\deg(S).

Our goal is to show that
C
works.

Let
\eta\in C
be the generic point of
C

be the induced abelian scheme. Assume for a contradiction that for some integer

with
1\leq n\leq N
, we have an isogeny

(9.5)

\displaystyle A_{\bar{\eta}}^{n}\to JX,

where

\smash{\overline{\mathbb{Q}(t)}}

\lambda_{A,\bar{\eta}}
(resp.
\lambda_{JX}
) be the principal polarization of

(resp.

), and let
\mu

\lambda_{JX}

along (
9.5

, see Lemma

, see Notation

\Phi\colon{\mathcal{A}}_{g,[r]}\to{\mathcal{A}}_{ng,\delta,[r]},\quad\quad(A,\lambda_{A},\bar{\phi})\mapsto(A^{n},\lambda_{\alpha},\bar{\phi}_{\alpha}),

where

\delta=(\delta_{1},\dotsc,\delta_{ng})
is the type of the polarization
\lambda_{\alpha}
.
Consider the generic point
\eta\in C\subset\mathcal{A}_{g,[r]}
of the curve
C
.
There exists an element

and a
\overline{\mathbb{Q}(t)}
point
\xi\in{\mathcal{A}}_{ng,[r]}

\displaystyle\xi\in\tau_{a}(\Phi(\eta))=p_{1}(p_{2}^{-1}(\Phi(\eta))).

We define

Let

\displaystyle\frac{\deg(C^{\#})}{\deg(S^{\#})}=\frac{\deg(C)}{\deg(S)}.

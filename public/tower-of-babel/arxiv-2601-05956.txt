On the Robustness of Age for Learning-Based Wireless Scheduling in Unknown Environments
Juaren Steiger Bin Li

Abstract
The constrained combinatorial multi-armed bandit model has been widely employed to solve problems in wireless networking and related areas, including the problem of wireless scheduling for throughput optimization under unknown channel conditions. Most work in this area uses an algorithm design strategy that combines a bandit learning algorithm with the virtual queue technique to track the throughput constraint violation. These algorithms seek to minimize the virtual queue length in their algorithm design. However, in networks where channel conditions change abruptly, the resulting constraints may become infeasible, leading to unbounded growth in virtual queue lengths. In this paper, we make the key observation that the dynamics of the head-of-line age, i.e. the age of the oldest packet in the virtual queue, make it more robust when used in algorithm design compared to the virtual queue length. We therefore design a learning-based scheduling policy that uses the head-of-line age in place of the virtual queue length. We show that our policy matches state-of-the-art performance under i.i.d. network conditions. Crucially, we also show that the system remains stable even under abrupt changes in channel conditions and can rapidly recover from periods of constraint infeasibility.

III
Age-Based Bandit Learning Algorithm Design

For each arm
k
, consider a virtual queue that stores
“delivery requests”
that must be addressed by choosing actions that result in a real packet delivery for the node (arm)
k
. Here, a delivery request is a virtual packet in the virtual queue. At the beginning of each timeslot
t
, a delivery request is generated with probability
\chi_{k}+\varepsilon
, where
0<\varepsilon\leq\gamma/2
is a tuning parameter, and inserted at the tail of the queue. At the end of the timeslot, a delivery request is removed from the head of the queue if the selected action results in a delivery for the arm, i.e.
R_{k}(S_{t},A_{t})=1
. In this section, we show that controlling the age of the oldest delivery request in the queue, which is also the head-of-line delivery request under the first-come-first-served queueing discipline, controls the throughput for the corresponding arm. After detailing the operation of the virtual queue in Section
III-A
, we first show that this is true for any policy in Section
III-B
. Then, in Section
III-C
, we define what it means for a policy to be
age-based
, and show in Theorem
1
that for any age-based policy, the throughput constraint violation
\chi_{k}-\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[\overline{R}_{k,t}^{\pi}[W]\right]
is bounded by a function involving the expected head-of-line age, the window size
W
, and the tuning parameter
\varepsilon

D_{k,t}\triangleq|\{\ell:d_{k}(\ell)=t\}|=R_{k}(S_{t},A_{t})\,\mathds{1}\{\uptau_{k}(\ell_{k,t}^{\to})\leq t\}.

(9)

We also indicate that a delivery request arrives to the queue in timeslot
t
using the notation

B_{k,t}\triangleq|\{\ell:\uptau_{k}(\ell)=t\}|\sim\text{Bernoulli}(\chi_{k}+\varepsilon).

(10)

The dynamics of the queue of delivery requests are summarized in Fig.
2
. The age of delivery request
(k,\ell)
in timeslot
t
is given by

Z_{k,t}(\ell)\triangleq t-\uptau_{k}(\ell).

(11)

When the age is positive, it represents the number of timeslots since the packet was generated. However, note that we also allow the age to take negative values, which represents the number of timeslots until the packet will be generated. While this will prove theoretically useful, in reality, the player can only observe
[Z_{k,t}(\ell)]^{+}
in timeslot
t
. The age of the head-of-line delivery request, called the
head-of-line age
, is

Z_{k,t}^{\to}\triangleq[Z_{k,t}(\ell^{\to}_{k,t})]^{+}.

(12)

The following lemma gives the dynamics of the head-of-line age,
which is similar to observations in prior work (e.g.
[
18
,
10
]
), and
which will be useful in the subsequent results.

Lemma 2
.

For any arm
k
, if the next-to-depart delivery request in timeslot
t
arrives by
t
, i.e.
\uptau_{k}(\ell_{k,t}^{\to})\leq t
, then the drift of the head-of-line age is

\Delta Z_{k,t}^{\to}=1-D_{k,t}\min\left\{t+1-\uptau_{k}(\ell_{k,t}^{\to}),\Delta\uptau_{k}(\ell_{k,t}^{\to})\right\}.

(13)

Otherwise, if
\uptau_{k}(\ell_{k,t}^{\to})>t
, then
\Delta Z_{k,t}^{\to}=0
.

Proof Sketch.

Fix arm
k
and timeslot
t
. According to the definition of age (
11
), we have for two consecutive delivery requests that their age difference satisfies
Z_{k,t}(\ell)-Z_{k,t}(\ell+1)=\Delta\uptau_{k}(\ell)
. Also from the definition (
11
), we have

Z_{k,t+1}(\ell+1)=Z_{k,t}(\ell+1)+1
.
Combining these two observations gives that
Z_{k,t+1}(\ell+1)=Z_{k,t}(\ell)-\Delta\uptau_{k}(\ell)+1
.

Also, note that according to the definition of age (
11
), we have
\left[Z_{k,t}(\ell_{k,t}^{\to})\right]^{-}=\left[\uptau_{k}(\ell_{k,t}^{\to})-t\right]^{+}
. Then we can rewrite the definition of head-of-line age (
12
) as
Z_{k,t}^{\to}=Z_{k,t}(\ell_{k,t}^{\to})+\left[\uptau_{k}(\ell_{k,t}^{\to})-t\right]^{+}
.

We use the previously stated identities
to analyze two cases:

Case 1
:
D_{k,t}=1
. Then note that
\ell_{k,t+1}^{\to}=\ell_{k,t}^{\to}+1
.

Case 2
:
D_{k,t}=0
. Then note that
\ell_{k,t+1}^{\to}=\ell_{k,t}^{\to}
.

The detailed analysis of these two cases is omitted due to space limitations.
∎

III-B

Throughput for Any Policy in Terms of Head-of-Line Age

In this section, we give throughput guarantees for each arm
k
under any policy in terms of the head-of-line age
Z_{k,t}^{\to}
.

Lemma 3
.

For any arm
k
and timeslot
t\geq W
, under any policy, the throughput constraint violation is bounded by

\textstyle\chi_{k}-\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[\overline{R}_{k,t}[W]\right]\leq\frac{1}{W}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[1+\sum_{\tau=1}^{Z_{k,t+1}^{\to}-1}B_{k,t-\tau+1}\right]-\varepsilon.

(14)

Proof.

Fix the timeslot
t
and arm
k
. Note that, by definition (
8
),
{\{\ell:d_{k}(\ell)<t\}}=\{\ell:\ell<\ell_{k,t}^{\to}\}
. Therefore from (
7
),

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt\mathcal{Q}_{k,t}

\displaystyle=\left\{\ell:\uptau_{k}(\ell)<t\right\}\setminus\left\{\ell:\ell<\ell_{k,t}^{\to}\right\}=\left\{\ell\geq\ell_{k,t}^{\to}:\uptau_{k}(\ell)<t\right\}

(15)

\displaystyle=\left\{\ell:\uptau_{k}(\ell_{k,t}^{\to})\leq\uptau_{k}(\ell)<t\right\}=\textstyle\bigcup_{\tau=\uptau_{k}(\ell_{k,t}^{\to})}^{t-1}\{\ell:\uptau_{k}(\ell)=\tau\}.

We can also simply rewrite (
7
) by decomposing each set into
t-1
disjoint subsets, i.e.
\mathcal{Q}_{k,t}={\bigcup_{\tau=1}^{t-1}\{\ell:\uptau_{k}(\ell)=\tau\}}\setminus{\bigcup_{\tau=1}^{t-1}\{\ell:d_{k}(\ell)=\tau\}}
. It follows from the definitions (
9
) and (
10
) that
|\mathcal{Q}_{k,t}|=\sum_{\tau=1}^{t-1}B_{k,\tau}-\sum_{\tau=1}^{t-1}D_{k,\tau}
,
and from (
15
) that

|\mathcal{Q}_{k,t}|=\sum_{\tau=\uptau_{k}(\ell_{k,t}^{\to})}^{t-1}B_{k,\tau}
.

Since these results hold for all timeslots
t
, fix
t\geq W
. Then

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\textstyle\displaystyle\sum_{\tau=\uptau_{k}(\ell_{k,t+1}^{\to})}^{t}B_{k,\tau}=\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}[B_{k,\tau}-D_{k,\tau}]

(16)

\displaystyle=\textstyle\sum_{\tau=1}^{t-W}[B_{k,\tau}-D_{k,\tau}]+\sum_{\tau=t-W+1}^{t}[B_{k,\tau}-D_{k,\tau}]

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{\geq}\textstyle\sum_{\tau=t-W+1}^{t}(B_{k,\tau}-R_{k}(S_{\tau},A_{\tau}))

where
(a)
bounds
\sum_{\tau=1}^{t-W}[B_{k,\tau}-D_{k,\tau}]=|\mathcal{Q}_{k,t-W+1}|\geq 0
and
D_{k,\tau}\leq R_{k}(S_{\tau},A_{\tau})
(see (
9
)).
Note that
B_{k,\tau}=1
by definition for
\tau=\uptau_{k}(\ell_{k,t+1}^{\to})
. Then the left-hand side of the above can be bounded by
\sum_{\tau=\uptau_{k}(\ell_{k,t+1}^{\to})}^{t}B_{k,\tau}\leq 1+\sum_{\tau=1}^{t-\uptau_{k}(\ell_{k,t+1}^{\to})}B_{k,t-\tau+1}
. Combining this with (
16
), dividing by
W
, taking expectations, and rearranging gives

\textstyle\chi_{k}-\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[\overline{R}_{k,t}[W]\right]\leq\frac{1}{W}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[1+\sum_{\tau=1}^{t-\uptau_{k}(\ell_{k,t+1}^{\to})}B_{k,t-\tau+1}\right]-\varepsilon.

(17)

Assume
\uptau_{k}(\ell_{k,t+1}^{\to})<t
(otherwise the sum above would trivially equal zero). Then by definition of the age (
11
), the head-of-line age
Z_{k,t+1}^{\to}=t+1-\uptau_{k}(\ell_{k,t+1}^{\to})
and the final result follows.
∎

III-C

Throughput Violation Bounds for Age-Based Policies

In this section, we formalize the idea of an
age-based policy
, and present Theorem
1
, which shows that under an age-based policy, the throughput constraint violation can be written as a function of the expected head-of-line age, the window size
W
, and the tuning parameter
\varepsilon
.

Definition 3

(age-based policy)
.

Define the
age-augmented history
as
\mathcal{H}_{t}^{\to}\triangleq(\mathcal{H}_{t},(Z_{k,\tau}^{\to})_{k\in[K],\tau\in[t]})
. An
age-based policy

\pi
is a causal policy under which
A_{t}^{\pi}
is a measurable function of
\mathcal{H}_{t}^{\to}
in each timeslot
t
.

One of the most important features of an age-based policy, namely that the policy plays independently from arrivals after the head-of-line delivery request, is discussed as follows.

Remark 1

(arrival independence of age-based policies)
.

Fix an arm
k
and timeslot
t
. According to Lemma
2
and the definition (
9
) of
D_{k,\tau}
, for any timeslot
\tau
,
\Delta Z_{k,\tau}^{\to}
is a measurable function of
(\Delta\uptau_{k}(\ell))_{\ell=0}^{\ell_{k,\tau}^{\to}}
and
R_{k}(S_{\tau},A_{\tau})
(since
\uptau_{k}(\ell_{k,\tau}^{\to})=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\ell_{k,\tau}^{\to}-1}\Delta\uptau_{k}(\ell)
). Then since
Z_{k,t}^{\to}=\sum_{\tau=1}^{t-1}\Delta Z_{k,\tau}^{\to}
, the age-augmented history
\mathcal{H}_{t}^{\to}
is a measurable function of
\mathcal{H}_{t}
and
\big((\Delta\uptau_{k}(\ell))_{\ell=0}^{\ell_{k,t-1}^{\to}}\big)_{k=1}^{K}
. Under an age-based policy
\pi
, and due to the i.i.d. arrivals, the age-augmented history
\mathcal{H}_{t}^{\to}
is therefore independent of the future arrivals
((B_{k,\tau})_{\tau>\uptau_{k}(\ell_{k,t}^{\to})})_{k=1}^{K}
in timeslots after the arrival times of the head-of-line packets, and is independent of the interarrival times
((\Delta\uptau_{k}(\ell))_{\ell\geq\ell_{k,t}^{\to}})_{k=1}^{K}
of future packets after the head-of-line packets.

Note that the above remark is not necessarily true for queue-length-based policies since the queue lengths
(|\mathcal{Q}_{k,t}|)_{k=1}^{K}
also depend on the arrivals
\big((B_{k,\tau})_{\tau=\uptau_{k}(\ell_{k,t}^{\to})+1}^{t-1}\big)_{k=1}^{K}
that came after the head-of-line packets. From Lemma
3
, we derive the throughput constraint violation bound under an age-based policy in the following theorem by leveraging Remark
1
in order to apply Wald’s identity.

Theorem 1
.

For any arm
k
, under an age-based policy, the throughput constraint violation is bounded for all timeslots
t\geq W
by

\chi_{k}-\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[\overline{R}_{k,t}[W]\right]\leq\frac{1+\left(\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[Z_{k,t+1}^{\to}\right]-1\right)(\chi_{k}+\varepsilon)}{W}-\varepsilon.

(18)

Proof.

Fix an arm
k
and timeslot
t
. According to Remark
1
, under an age-based policy,
Z_{k,t+1}^{\to}
is independent from
B_{k,\tau}
for
\tau>\uptau_{k}(\ell_{k,t+1}^{\to})
. Then since the sum in (
14
) consists only of terms
B_{k,\tau}
for
\tau>\uptau_{k}(\ell_{k,t+1}^{\to})
, we can apply Wald’s identity to Lemma
3
to get the result.
∎

The following corollary reveals the tradeoff between the tuning parameter
\varepsilon
and the expected head-of-line age
\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[Z_{k,t+1}^{\to}\right]
to obtain a feasible window size for the throughput constraints.

Corollary 1
.

For any arm
k
, any age-based policy achieves zero throughput constraint violation, i.e.
\chi_{k}-\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[\overline{R}_{k,t}[W]\right]\leq 0
for all
t\geq W
if the window size is at least
W\geq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\left((\chi_{k}+\varepsilon)\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[Z_{k,t+1}^{\to}\right]+1\right)
.

IV-B

Theoretical Analysis

The theoretical results for the age-based bandit learning policy depend heavily on two Lyapunov functions: (i) the
weighted quadratic Lyapunov function

{L_{t}\triangleq{\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{(\chi_{k}+\varepsilon)}{\overline{x}_{k}}\left(Z_{k,t}^{\to}\right)^{2}}}
, which is inspired by the Lyapunov function used in
[
18
]
and the Lyapunov function used in
[
26
]
, and
(ii) the
Euclidean norm of the weighted head-of-line ages

\widetilde{L}_{t}\triangleq\|(\sqrt{(\chi_{k}+\varepsilon)/\overline{x}_{k}}Z_{k,t}^{\to})_{k=1}^{K}\|_{2}
.
These two Lyapunov functions are connected as follows. Since
\sqrt{x}-\sqrt{y}\leq\frac{x-y}{2\sqrt{y}}
for all
x\geq 0
and
y>0
, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{L}_{t}>0\implies\Delta\widetilde{L}_{t}=\Delta\sqrt{2L_{t}}\leq{\Delta L_{t}}\,\big/\,\widetilde{L}_{t}.

(20)

Most prior work applying Lyapunov drift analysis to constrained bandit learning uses a drift lemma such as Lemma 11 in
[
14
]
that operates on a process with the following two drift conditions: (i) if the process exceeds some value at time
t
, then its drift becomes negative at time
t
in conditional expectation, and (ii) the magnitude of the drift of the process is bounded almost surely by some constant that does not depend on
t
. The drift lemma says that if these two conditions are satisfied, the moment-generating function (MGF) of the process can be bounded at any time
t
. We would like to apply such a drift lemma to the Lyapunov function
\widetilde{L}_{t}
. However, note that according to Lemma
2
, it will have a decrement that is a function of the Geometric random variables
(\Delta\uptau_{k}(\ell_{k,t}^{\to}))_{k=1}^{K}
and will therefore violate drift condition (ii). Thankfully, the original work
[
7
]
that this sort of analysis descends from allows for the magnitude of the drift to instead be bounded by a light-tailed random variable. To that end, we instead use the following drift lemma in our analysis, which is derived from the results in
[
7
]
.

Lemma 4
.

Consider a random process
\{Y_{t}\}_{t\geq 1}
and a sequence of
\sigma
-algebras
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 1}
such that each
Y_{t}
is
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable. Assume the following two drift conditions hold:

(i) For all
t
, if
Y_{t}\geq\varphi
then
\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[\Delta Y_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]\leq-\rho
, where
\varphi>0
and
\rho\in(0,1]
are constants.

(ii)
\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[e^{\theta|\Delta Y_{t}|}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]\leq M
for all
t
, where
\theta\in(0,1]
and
M\in[1,\infty)
are constants.

Then for all
t
and any
0<\zeta\leq\frac{\rho\theta^{2}}{8M}
, we have

\textstyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[e^{\zeta Y_{t}}\right]\leq\left(1-\frac{\zeta\rho}{2}\right)^{t-1}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[e^{\zeta Y_{1}}\right]+\frac{2}{\zeta\rho}e^{\zeta\varphi}M^{\zeta/\theta}.

(21)

We use Lemma
4
to derive the following instantaneous moment bound on the Lyapunov function
\widetilde{L}_{t}
.

Lemma 5
.

Under the age-based learning algorithm, for all timeslots
t\geq 1
, we have

\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[\widetilde{L}_{t}\right]\leq\frac{8\log(4/\gamma)}{\gamma}f\left((\chi_{k})_{k=1}^{K}\right)+\frac{4}{\gamma}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{1}{\chi_{k}\overline{x}_{k}}+\frac{4K\eta}{\gamma}

(22)

where the function
f:(0,1)^{K}\to(0,\infty)
is given by

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{y})=\min_{0<\theta\leq h(\mathbf{y})}\left[\frac{9g(\mathbf{y},\theta)}{\theta^{2}}\log\left(\frac{32g(\mathbf{y},\theta)}{\theta^{2}}\right)\right]

(23)

where
h(\mathbf{y})=-\frac{\overline{x}_{\min}}{2}\log\left(1-\min_{k\in[K]}y_{k}\right)
,
g(\mathbf{y},\theta)=\prod_{k=1}^{K}\frac{y_{k}\,e^{2\theta/\overline{x}_{k}}}{1-(1-y_{k})e^{2\theta/\overline{x}_{k}}}
, and
\overline{x}_{\min}\triangleq\min_{k}\overline{x}_{k}
.

Proof Sketch.

We derive the necessary drift conditions to apply Lemma
4
to
\widetilde{L}_{t}
. Define
\rho\triangleq\frac{\gamma-\varepsilon}{2}
and

\varphi\triangleq\frac{1}{\rho}\left(\eta K+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{1}{\overline{x}_{k}\chi_{k}}\right)
.

Using Lemma
2
, Remark
1
, and the definition of the age-based bandit learning algorithm (
19
), after much work, we can obtain
\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[\Delta L_{t}\mid\mathcal{H}_{t}^{\to}\right]\leq-2\rho\widetilde{L}_{t}+\rho\varphi
. Then from (
20
), we obtain drift condition (i):
\widetilde{L}_{t}>\varphi\implies\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[\Delta\widetilde{L}_{t}\mid\mathcal{H}_{t}^{\to}\right]\leq-\rho
. For drift condition (ii), define
\theta_{\max}\triangleq-\frac{\overline{x}_{\min}}{2}\log(1-\chi_{\min})
and consider
\theta\in(0,\theta_{\max}]
and
M\triangleq\prod_{k=1}^{K}\frac{\chi_{k}\,e^{2\theta/\overline{x}_{k}}}{1-(1-\chi_{k})e^{2\theta/\overline{x}_{k}}}
. Then the drift condition
\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[e^{\theta\left|\Delta\widetilde{L}_{t}\right|}\mid\mathcal{H}_{t}^{\to}\right]\leq M
can be obtained using the reverse triangle inequality and Lemma
2
. Then we apply Lemma
4
to get the MGF bound, and apply Jensen’s inequality and simplify to get the final moment bound. The full proof is omitted due to space limitations.
∎

We are now ready to derive the throughput guarantees for the age-based bandit learning policy.

Theorem 2
.

The age-based learning policy (
19
) achieves zero throughput constraint violation, i.e.
\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[\overline{R}_{k,t}[W]\right]\geq\chi_{k}
for all
t\geq W
if the window size

W
satisfies

V
Robustness of the Age-Based Bandit Learning Policy under Abruptly Changing Conditions

In a real wireless network, the channel conditions may not be perfectly i.i.d. and may suffer from abrupt drops in quality due to weather, congestion, etc. Therefore, in this section, we investigate what happens when the channel quality of a single link abruptly degrades, rendering its short-term throughput constraint infeasible.

V-A

Experimental Setup

To investigate this phenomenon, we consider a simple network with
K=2
nodes (bandit arms) where only one node can be scheduled per timeslot.
For timeslots
t\in\{1,2,\ldots,\lceil T/6\rceil-1\}
, both links have channel rates
\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[X_{1}(S_{t})\right]=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[X_{2}(S_{t})\right]=0.9
.
The
throughput requirements are

\chi_{1}=0.8
and
\chi_{2}=0.1

suddenly drops to

and remains that way for all timeslots

. This renders the short-term throughput constraint for arm
k=1
infeasible! At timeslot

, the channel quality for link
k=1
finally improves back to
\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[X_{1}(S_{t})\right]=0.9
and remains that way for all timeslots
t\in\{\lceil 2T/3\rceil,\ldots,T\}
.

Interestingly, our age-based policy, which is not explicitly designed to target the TSLR metric, does much better than the QLen+TSLR policy on this metric,

which never recovers from the period of constraint infeasibility.

Our intuition is that for QLen+TSLR, the queue length metric completely dominates any other metric considered in the algorithm. This also demonstrates the utility of the
O(\eta)
TSLR control afforded by our age-based policy (see Remark
2
). As expected, the TSLR-based policy does well in the TSLR metric, but completely blunders the short-term throughput since it is not designed for that. For the TSLR-based policy, the opposite changes in short-term throughput during the period of constraint infeasibility are due to the changes in reward between the arms.

To shed some light on why the head-of-line age does not suffer as much from periods of constraint infeasibility compared to the queue length, we plot the operation of the queue-length-based policy and our age-based policy for one simulation run in Fig.
5
. Since we consider one action per arm, the “weight” on an action
a=k
is
\eta U_{k,t}+Z_{k,t}^{\to}
under our age-based policy (
19
) and
\eta U_{k,t}+|\mathcal{Q}_{k,t}|
under the queue-length-based policy. The key difference is in the dynamic of the head-of-line age
\Delta Z_{k,t}^{\to}
given by Lemma
2
and the dynamic of the queue length
\Delta|\mathcal{Q}_{k,t}|=B_{k,t}-D_{k,t}
.

In particular, the head-of-line age always increments by 1 when a reward is not obtained, and therefore the head-of-line age for the neglected arm always has a chance to “catch up” to the other arm. On the other hand, the queue length grows much more slowly at a rate of
\chi_{k}+\varepsilon
.

From these results and the fact that the age-based and queue-length-based policies perform similarly in the i.i.d. setting (see Section
IV-A
), we argue that this robustness under constraint infeasibility incentivizes the use of the head-of-line age in place of the queue length where appropriate in drift-plus-penalty style algorithms deployed to real-world systems.

VI
Concluding Remarks and Future Work

In this paper, we investigated the robustness of using the head-of-line age instead of the virtual queue length to manage the throughput constraints in a learning-based wireless scheduling policy. We showed that the performance of such a policy can match the state-of-the-art both theoretically and empirically under i.i.d. network conditions. However, when the i.i.d. assumption is violated, leading to temporary constraint infeasibility, we demonstrated empirically that the age-based policy is significantly more stable and adaptive than the state-of-the-art policies. The next step, which is much more difficult, is to analytically quantify the robustness of the head-of-line age in the presence of temporarily infeasible constraints. We aim to investigate this in future work.

(a)
is from Remark
3

(b)
is because the interarrival times are i.i.d,
and plugging in the MGF of a
{\text{Geometric}(\chi_{k}+\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[\widetilde{L}_{t}\right]\overset{(a)}{\leq}\frac{1}{\zeta}\log\left(\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[e^{\zeta\widetilde{L}_{t}}\right]\right)\overset{(b)}{\leq}\frac{1}{\zeta}\log\left(\frac{4}{\zeta\rho}e^{\zeta\varphi}M^{\zeta/\theta}\right)

(52)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\zeta}\log\left(\frac{4}{\zeta\rho}\right)+\varphi+\frac{1}{\theta}\log M

\displaystyle=\frac{8M}{\rho\theta^{2}}\left(2\log\left(\frac{1}{\rho}\right)+\log\left(\frac{32M}{\theta^{2}}\right)\right)+\frac{1}{\theta}\log M+\varphi

\displaystyle\overset{(c)}{\leq}\frac{2\log(1/\rho)}{\rho}\left(\frac{9M}{\theta^{2}}\log\left(\frac{32M}{\theta^{2}}\right)\right)+\varphi

\displaystyle\overset{(d)}{\leq}\frac{8\log(4/\gamma)}{\gamma}\left(\frac{9M}{\theta^{2}}\log\left(\frac{32M}{\theta^{2}}\right)\right)+\frac{4}{\gamma}\left(\eta K+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{1}{\chi_{k}\overline{x}_{k}}\right)

where
(a)
is due to Jensen’s inequality,
(b)
applies Lemma
4
using the two established drift conditions (note that the first term in the lemma is
\leq 1
since

),
(c)
is because

for

and
\rho=\frac{\gamma-\varepsilon}{2}
, and

∎

The following lemma derives a sample path bound on the drift of the weighted quadratic Lyapunov function.

Lemma 6
.

For all
arms
k

and timeslots
t
, we have
\Delta L_{t}\leq\mathtt{t1}-\mathtt{t2}+\mathtt{t3}

where

\displaystyle\mathtt{t1}

\displaystyle\triangleq\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{(\chi_{k}+\varepsilon)}{\overline{x}_{k}}Z_{k,t}^{\to},

(53)

\displaystyle\mathtt{t2}

\displaystyle\triangleq\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{(\chi_{k}+\varepsilon)}{\overline{x}_{k}}R_{k}(S_{t},A_{t})\Delta\uptau_{k}(\ell_{k,t}^{\to})Z_{k,t}^{\to},

\displaystyle\mathtt{t3}

\displaystyle\triangleq\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{(\chi_{k}+\varepsilon)}{2\overline{x}_{k}}\left(1+\left[\Delta\uptau_{k}(\ell_{k,t}^{\to})\right]^{2}\right).

Proof.

We first need to show that for all
arms
k

and timeslots
t
, we have

Z_{k,t+1}^{\to}\leq\left[Z_{k,t}^{\to}+1-D_{k,t}\Delta\uptau_{k}(\ell_{k,t}^{\to})\right]^{+}.

(54)

Fix arm
k
and timeslot
t
.
We consider three cases:

Case 1
:
\uptau_{k}(\ell_{k,t}^{\to})\geq t
. Then
Z_{k,t}^{\to}=0
by definition (see (
11
) and (
12
)) and so Lemma
2
gives

\displaystyle Z_{k,t+1}^{\to}

\displaystyle=\mathds{1}\{\uptau_{k}(\ell_{k,t}^{\to})=t\}(1-D_{k,t})(Z_{k,t}^{\to}+1)

(55)

\displaystyle\leq(1-D_{k,t})(Z_{k,t}^{\to}+1).

Case 2
:
\uptau_{k}(\ell_{k,t}^{\to})<t<\uptau_{k}(\ell_{k,t+1}^{\to})
. Then
\ell_{k,t}^{\to}
is the only delivery request in the queue at the beginning of
t
and it has age
Z_{k,t}^{\to}=t-\uptau_{k}(\ell_{k,t}^{\to})
. Then from Lemma
2

\displaystyle Z_{k,t+1}^{\to}

\displaystyle=t-\uptau_{k}(\ell_{k,t}^{\to})+1-D_{k,t}(t+1-\uptau_{k}(\ell_{k,t}^{\to}))

\displaystyle=(1-D_{k,t})(t-\uptau_{k}(\ell_{k,t}^{\to})+1)=(1-D_{k,t})(Z_{k,t}^{\to}+1).

Case 3
:
\uptau_{k}(\ell_{k,t}^{\to})<\uptau_{k}(\ell_{k,t+1}^{\to})\leq t

(57)

Since
(1-D_{k,t})(Z_{k,t}^{\to}+1)\leq\left[Z_{k,t}^{\to}+1-D_{k,t}\Delta\uptau_{k}(\ell_{k,t}^{\to})\right]^{+}
, the result (

) holds in all three cases.

The drift bound proceeds from (
54
):

\displaystyle\Delta L_{t}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{(\chi_{k}+\varepsilon)}{2\overline{x}_{k}}\left[\left(Z_{k,t}^{\to}+1-D_{k,t}\Delta\uptau_{k}(\ell_{k,t}^{\to})\right)^{2}-\left(Z_{k,t}^{\to}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{(\chi_{k}+\varepsilon)}{2\overline{x}_{k}}\left(1-D_{k,t}\Delta\uptau_{k}(\ell_{k,t}^{\to})\right)^{2}.

Now note that whenever
Z_{k,t}^{\to}>0
, we have
\uptau_{k}(\ell_{k,t}^{\to})<t
(see (
11
) and (
12
)), and therefore
D_{k,t}=R_{k}(S_{t},A_{t})
according to definition (
9
). Then the final bound is obtained by substituting

for all
k
in the second sum, and bounding
\left(1-D_{k,t}\Delta\uptau_{k}(\ell_{k,t}^{\to})\right)^{2}\leq 1+\left[\Delta\uptau_{k}(\ell_{k,t}^{\to})\right]^{2}

in the third sum.

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathop{\text{P}}\left(N_{k,t}>0,|\widetilde{x}_{k,t}-\overline{x}_{k}|>\sqrt{\frac{3\log t}{2N_{k,t}}}\right)

(74)

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{\leq}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{n=1}^{t}\frac{2}{t^{3}}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{2}{t^{2}}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}=\frac{K\pi^{2}}{3}

where
(a)
takes the union bound over the events
{\{N_{n,k}=n\}}

and bounds each subsequent probability using Hoeffding’s inequality. The second term is bounded by

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[I_{k}(A_{t})\sqrt{\frac{6\log t}{N_{k,t}\lor 1}}\right]

(75)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{6\log T}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}I_{k}(A_{t})\sqrt{\frac{1}{N_{k,t}\lor 1}}\right]

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{\leq}\sqrt{6\log T}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[\mathds{1}\{N_{k,T+1}>0\}+\sum_{n=1}^{N_{k,T+1}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{n}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{6\log T}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[\mathds{1}\{N_{k,T+1}>0\}+2\sqrt{N_{k,T+1}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{56\log T}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sqrt{N_{k,T+1}}\right]

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{\leq}\sqrt{56\log T}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sqrt{K\sum_{k=1}^{K}N_{k,T+1}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{56\log T}\sqrt{KI_{\max}T}

where
(a)
is because
N_{k,t+1}=N_{k,t}+1
when
I_{k}(A_{t})=1
, i.e., it increments by 1 every time arm
k
is played and
N_{k,t}=0
the first time it is played, and
(b)
is by the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality.

∎

Introduction

The first general results on boundedness of the Chern class

c_{3}

[
23

\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathbb{P}^{3})

E

(1)

(2)

and maximal

[

The variety

is the unique smooth Fano threefold of index 2 and degree 5 . It has
\mathrm{Pic}\ X=\mathbb{Z}
. There are some articles in which vector bundles on
X

[

[

[

[

We will use the description of
X
as a linear section of Grassmannian
\mathrm{Gr}(2,5)\subset\mathbb{P}^{9}
(embedded by Plücker) by a general linear subspace of codimension 3. Denote by
\mathcal{U}
the restriction to
X
of the universal rank 2 subbundle on
\mathrm{Gr}(2,5)
, resp. by
\mathcal{Q}
the restriction to
X
of the universal rank 3 quotient bundle on
\mathrm{Gr}(2,5)
. The class of a divisor corresponding to the ample generator of the Picard group of
X
will be denoted by
H
. Recall that the discriminant of a coherent sheaf
E
on a variety
X
with respect to a polarization
H
is defined by

\overline{\Delta}_{H}(E)=(H^{2}\cdot\mathrm{ch}_{1}(E))^{2}-2(H^{3}\cdot\mathrm{ch}_{0}(E))(H\cdot\mathrm{ch}_{2}(E)),

where
\mathrm{ch}_{i}
are the components of the Chern character. The Bogomolov–Gieseker inequality implies that the discriminant of a semistable sheaf is non-negative, and classification of sheaves with maximal
c_{3}
in
[
23
]
,
[
27
]
goes by induction on the discriminant. In the case of
X_{5}
the cohomology groups
H^{2i}(X,\mathbb{Z})
are isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
and the Chern classes of objects can be considered as integers. More precisely, for an object
E\in\mathrm{D}^{b}(X)
with Chern classes
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}
we have

(3)

\mathrm{ch}(E)=\mathrm{rk}(E)+c_{1}H+\left(\frac{c_{1}^{2}}{2}-\frac{c_{2}}{5}\right)H^{2}+\left(\frac{c_{1}^{3}}{6}-\frac{c_{1}c_{2}}{10}+\frac{c_{3}}{10}\right)H^{3}.

In particular, for a rank 2 sheaf

we have
\overline{\Delta}_{H}(E)=20c_{2}(E)-25c_{1}(E)^{2}
. Using the theory of tilt-stability and Bridgeland stability, we were able to obtain the following description of semistable rank 2 sheaves with
\overline{\Delta}_{H}(E)\leq 40
and maximal
c_{3}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
E
be a Gieseker semistable sheaf of rank 2 on
X
with Chern classes
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}
.

(1) If
c_{1}=-1
, then
c_{2}\geq 2
.

(1.1) If
c_{2}=2
, then
c_{3}\leq 0
. In case of equality we have

.

(1.2) If
c_{2}=3
, then
c_{3}\leq 1
. In case of equality

is included into an exact triple

(2) If
c_{1}=0
, then
c_{2}\geq 0
.

(2.1) If
c_{2}=0
, then
c_{3}\leq 0
. In case of equality
E\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus 2}
.

(2.2) If
c_{2}=1

c_{3}\leq-2
, and in case of equality

is included into an exact triple

for a projective line

.

(2.3) If
c_{2}=2
, then
c_{3}\leq 0

is included into an exact triple

0\to\mathcal{Q}(-1)^{\oplus 2}\to\mathcal{U}^{\oplus 4}\to E\to 0.

In fact, in Theorem

can be considered as a step towards the description of all Gieseker semistable rank 2 sheaves on

with a maximal class

, by induction on the discriminant. Based on the cases of

can be described as extensions

Let us outline the structure of the paper. In Section
2
we, firstly, recall the notions and results from the theory of tilt-stability and Bridgeland stability on threefolds that we will use. One of the main features of these stability conditions is that they are defined not on the category of coherent sheaves
\mathrm{Coh}(X)
, but on other hearts of bounded t-structures on
\mathrm{D}^{b}(X)
. These hearts can be obtained from
\mathrm{Coh}(X)
using the operation of

. Next, we consider the full strong exceptional collection
(\mathcal{O}_{X}(-1),\mathcal{Q}(-1),\mathcal{U},\mathcal{O}_{X})
and the heart

\mathfrak{C}=\langle\mathcal{O}_{X}(-1)[3],\mathcal{Q}(-1)[2],\mathcal{U}[1],\mathcal{O}_{X}\rangle

of a bounded t-structure on
\mathrm{D}^{b}(X)
obtained from it. In (
5
) we define a region
D
of the upper half-plane with coordinates
(\beta,\alpha)
. Using this region we prove the existence of a family of Bridgeland stability conditions, defined on hearts
\mathcal{A}^{\alpha,\beta}(X)
, which are related to the heart
\mathfrak{C}
by the procedure of tilting (see Corollary
2.15
). In terms of
[
26
]
, the exceptional collection generating
\mathfrak{C}
satisfies the
upper half-plane condition
for these stability conditions. This property can be used to describe tilt-semistable or Bridgeland semistable objects, because objects of
\mathfrak{C}
with a given Chern character can be described rather explicitly (see Remark
2.16
).

In Section
3
we, firstly, give a characterization of the shifts

of twisted trivial sheaves on

among objects with the same
\mathrm{ch}_{\leq 2}

3.4

1.1
above.

Finally, in Section
4

4.5

for

2.
The Derived Category
\mathrm{D}^{b}(X)
and Stability

Let
X=X_{5}
be the unique smooth Fano threefold of Picard number one, index 2 and degree 5. Recall that
X
can be constructed as a codimension 3 linear section of Grassmannian
\mathrm{Gr}(2,5)
embedded by Plücker in
\mathbb{P}^{9}
. The cohomology groups of
X
are

H^{*}(X,\mathbb{Z})=H^{0}(X,\mathbb{Z})\oplus H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})\oplus H^{4}(X,\mathbb{Z})\oplus H^{6}(X,\mathbb{Z})=

=\mathbb{Z}[X]\oplus\mathbb{Z}[H]\oplus\mathbb{Z}[L]\oplus\mathbb{Z}[P],

where
H
denotes the class of a hyperplane section of
X
,
L
is the class of a projective line on
X
and
P
is the class of a point. We have
H\cdot L=P,H^{2}=5L,H^{3}=5P
. We will identify
H^{0}(X,\mathbb{Z})
and
H^{6}(X,\mathbb{Z})
with
\mathbb{Z}
by putting
X=1
and
P=1
.

Recall that the
slope
of a coherent sheaf
E\in\operatorname{Coh}(X)
is defined as
\mu(E)=\frac{H^{2}\cdot\mathrm{ch}_{1}(E)}{H^{3}\cdot\mathrm{ch}_{0}(E)}
(in case of division by 0 we set
\mu(E)=+\infty
). A coherent sheaf
E
is called

\mu
-
stable
(resp.
\mu
-semistable
) or
slope stable
(resp.
slope semistable
) if for any proper
subsheaf
0\neq F\hookrightarrow E
the inequality
\mu(F)<\mu(E/F)
(resp.
\mu(F)\leq\mu(E/F)
) holds.

Following
[
4
]
and
[
23
]
, we obtain a new heart of a bounded t-structure on
\mathrm{D}^{b}(X)
using the process of tilting. In general, let
\mathcal{A}
be the heart of a bounded t-structure on a triangulated category
\mathfrak{D}
. Recall that a
torsion pair
is a pair
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
of subcategories of the category
\mathcal{A}
such that, firstly,
\Hom(T,F)=0
for any
T\in\mathcal{T},F\in\mathcal{F}
and, secondly, for any
E\in\mathcal{A}
there is a short exact sequence
0\to T\to E\to F\to 0
in
\mathcal{A}
with
T\in\mathcal{T},F\in\mathcal{F}
.

For an arbitrary real number
\beta
we have the following torsion pair on the category of coherent sheaves:

\mathcal{T}_{\beta}=\{E\in\operatorname{Coh}(X)\colon\mathrm{any}\;\mathrm{quotient}\;E\to G\;\mathrm{satisfies}\;\mu(G)>\beta\},

\mathcal{F}_{\beta}=\{E\in\operatorname{Coh}(X)\colon\mathrm{any}\;\mathrm{subsheaf}\;F\to E\;\mathrm{satisfies}\;\mu(F)\leq\beta\}.

In general, if
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
is a torsion pair on the heart
\mathcal{A}
as defined above, its
tilt
is the extension closure
\langle\mathcal{F}[1],\mathcal{T}\rangle\subset\mathfrak{D}
. It is also the heart of a bounded t-structure.
In our case the new heart
\operatorname{Coh}^{\beta}(X)
is the extension closure
\langle\mathcal{F}_{\beta}[1],\mathcal{T}_{\beta}\rangle
. Equivalently,
\operatorname{Coh}^{\beta}(X)
can be described as the full additive subcategory of the derived category, consisting of those complexes
E\in\mathrm{D}^{b}(X)
for which
\mathcal{H}^{-1}(E)\in\mathcal{F}_{\beta},\mathcal{H}^{0}(E)\in\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
and
\mathcal{H}^{i}(E)=0
for
i\neq-1,0
.

Recall the notion of tilt-stability. Again
\beta
is an arbitrary real number. The twisted Chern character is defined as
\mathrm{ch}^{\beta}=e^{-\beta H}\cdot\mathrm{ch}
. Explicitly, for
E\in\mathrm{D}^{b}(X)
with
\mathrm{ch}(E)=r+cH+dH^{2}+eH^{3}
we have

\mathrm{ch}_{0}^{\beta}(E)=r,\;\mathrm{ch}_{1}^{\beta}(E)=(c-\beta r)H,\;\mathrm{ch}_{2}^{\beta}(E)=(d-\beta c+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}r)H^{2},

\mathrm{ch}_{3}^{\beta}(E)=(e-\beta d+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}c-\frac{\beta^{3}}{6}r)H^{3}.

Let
\alpha>0
be a positive real number. Following Schmidt (
[
22
,
24
]
), for an object
E\in\operatorname{Coh}^{\beta}(X)
we define a central charge function

Z^{\mathrm{tilt}}_{\alpha,\beta}(E)=Z^{\mathrm{tilt}}_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathrm{ch}_{0}(E),\mathrm{ch}_{1}(E),\mathrm{ch}_{2}(E))=-H\cdot\mathrm{ch}_{2}^{\beta}(E)+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}H^{3}\cdot\mathrm{ch}_{0}^{\beta}(E)+i(H^{2}\cdot\mathrm{ch}_{1}^{\beta}(E)),

which is a
\mathbb{Z}
-linear map
Z^{\mathrm{tilt}}_{\alpha,\beta}:K(\mathrm{D}^{b}(X))=K(\operatorname{Coh}^{\beta}(X))\to\mathbb{C}
.

The tilt-slope of
E
is defined by

\nu_{\alpha,\beta}(E)=-\frac{\Re(Z^{\mathrm{tilt}}_{\alpha,\beta}(E))}{\Im(Z^{\mathrm{tilt}}_{\alpha,\beta}(E))},

where division by 0 is again interpreted as giving
+\infty
. The object
E
is called
tilt-(semi)stable
(or
\nu_{\alpha,\beta}
-
(semi)stable
) if for any non-trivial proper subobject
F\hookrightarrow E
in
\mathrm{Coh}^{\beta}(X)
the inequality
\nu_{\alpha,\beta}(F)<(\leq)\,\nu_{\alpha,\beta}(E/F)
holds.

We can consider
\mathrm{ch}_{\leq 2}
as a map to a lattice
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}^{2}\oplus\frac{1}{10}\mathbb{Z}
(if
\mathrm{ch}_{\leq 2}(E)=r+cH+dH^{2}
we map
E
to
(r,c,d)
). Define a bilinear form
Q^{\mathrm{tilt}}
on
\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{R}
by
Q^{\mathrm{tilt}}((r,c,d),(R,C,D))=Cc-Rd-Dr
. Denote
Q^{\mathrm{tilt}}(E):=Q^{\mathrm{tilt}}(\mathrm{ch}_{\leq 2}(E),\mathrm{ch}_{\leq 2}(E))
.

The
Bogomolov-Gieseker inequality
holds for tilt-stability, asserting non-negativity of the discriminant
\overline{\Delta}_{H}(E)=(H^{3})^{2}\cdot Q^{\mathrm{tilt}}(E)
(see
[
17
, Theorem 2.1]
):

Proposition 2.1
.

For any
\nu_{\alpha,\beta}
-semistable object
E\in\operatorname{Coh}^{\beta}(X)
we have

(4)

\overline{\Delta}_{H}(E):=(H^{3})^{2}\cdot Q^{\mathrm{tilt}}(E)=(H^{2}\cdot\mathrm{ch}_{1}(E))^{2}-2(H^{3}\cdot\mathrm{ch}_{0}(E))(H\cdot\mathrm{ch}_{2}(E))=\\
=(H^{2}\cdot\mathrm{ch}_{1}^{\beta}(E))^{2}-2(H^{3}\cdot\mathrm{ch}_{0}^{\beta}(E))(H\cdot\mathrm{ch}_{2}^{\beta}(E))\geq 0.

The
\nu_{\alpha,\beta}
-stability is a so-called
very weak stability condition

[
24
]
. In particular, it satisfies the

with respect to

. It means that, firstly, the Bogomolov-Gieseker inequality holds for semistable objects, and secondly, for any non-zero

with

we have
Q^{\mathrm{tilt}}(v)<0
. Also Harder-Narasimhan filtrations exist for
\nu_{\alpha,\beta}
-stability. For a
Bridgeland stability condition

(\mathcal{A},Z)
defined on a heart

of a bounded t-structure, in addition, we have

for any nonzero

[
24
]
.

In order to construct Bridgeland stability conditions on

]
). Let

\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{\alpha,\beta}=\{E\in\operatorname{Coh}^{\beta}(X)\colon\text{any quotient}\,E\twoheadrightarrow G\;\mathrm{satisfies}\;\nu_{\alpha,\beta}(G)>0\},

and set
\mathcal{A}^{\alpha,\beta}(X)=\langle\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{\alpha,\beta}[1],\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{\alpha,\beta}\rangle

An object

is called

It is known that for

Assume that

Fix

changes at

Proposition 2.3

(Numerical properties of walls)
.

Let

\mathcal{U}^{\prime}[1]\in\mathcal{A}^{\alpha,\beta}(X)[1],\mathcal{U}^{\prime\prime}[1]\in\mathcal{A}^{\alpha,\beta}(X)
, so
\mathcal{U}[1]\in\nolinebreak\langle\mathcal{A}^{\alpha,\beta}(X),\mathcal{A}^{\alpha,\beta}(X)[1]\rangle
.
∎

For the purpose of reference we include the following lemma.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{A}
be two hearts of bounded t-structures on a triangulated category
\mathfrak{D}
. If
\mathcal{C}\subset\langle\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}[1]\rangle
, then for any
E\in\mathcal{C}
there is an exact triangle

(6)

E^{\prime}\overset{a}{\to}E\to E^{\prime\prime}\to E^{\prime}[1]

with
E^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}\cap(\mathcal{A}[1]),E^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{A}
.

Proof.

By
[
4
, Proposition 2.3.2 (b)]

(\mathcal{A}\cap\mathcal{C},\mathcal{A}\cap(\mathcal{C}[-1]))
is a torsion pair on the category
\mathcal{A}
. In particular,
\mathcal{A}\subset\langle\mathcal{C}[-1],\mathcal{C}\rangle
. Applying
[
4
, Proposition 2.3.2 (b)]
again, we get that
(\mathcal{C}[-1]\cap\mathcal{A},\mathcal{C}[-1]\cap(\mathcal{A}[-1]))
is torsion pair on the category
\mathcal{C}[-1]
. Hence
(\mathcal{C}\cap(\mathcal{A}[1]),\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{A})
is a torsion pair on the category
\mathcal{C}
and for any
E\in\mathcal{C}
we have an exact triple (
6
).
∎

In
[
23
,
27
]
an analogue of the following Proposition was used implicitly. Let us justify it in more detail.

Proposition 2.14
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{C}
is the heart of a bounded t-structure on
\mathrm{D}^{b}(X)
satisfying the properties (i) and (ii) of the category
\mathfrak{C}
from Lemma
2.12
. For any

\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
we define a torsion pair

(7)

\begin{split}&\mathcal{T}^{\prime\prime}_{\gamma}=\{E\in\mathcal{A}^{\alpha,\beta}(X)\ |\ \text{any quotient}\ E\twoheadrightarrow G\,\text{satisfies}\,\lambda_{\alpha,\beta,s}(G)>\gamma\},\\
&\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}_{\gamma}=\{E\in\mathcal{A}^{\alpha,\beta}(X)\ |\ \text{any
subobject}\ 0\neq F\hookrightarrow E\,\text{satisfies}\,\lambda_{\alpha,\beta,s}(F)\leq\gamma\}.\end{split}

Then there exists a
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\langle\mathcal{T}^{\prime\prime}_{\gamma},\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}_{\gamma}[1]\rangle=\mathcal{C}.

Proof.

We take
\gamma=-\cot\pi\phi_{1}
, where
\phi_{1}
satisfies the property (i) of Lemma
2.12
. Since both
\mathcal{C}
and
\langle\mathcal{T}^{\prime\prime}_{\gamma},\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}_{\gamma}[1]\rangle
are hearts of bounded t-structures on
\mathrm{D}^{b}(X)
, it suffices to prove that
\mathcal{C}\subset\langle\mathcal{T}^{\prime\prime}_{\gamma},\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}_{\gamma}[1]\rangle
.

Take
E\in\mathcal{C}
to show that
E\in\langle\mathcal{T}^{\prime\prime}_{\gamma},\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}_{\gamma}[1]\rangle
. We denote
\mathcal{A}:=\mathcal{A}^{\alpha,\beta}(X)
. By Lemma
2.13
we reduce the problem to the case when
E\in\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{A}
or
E\in\mathcal{C}\cap(\mathcal{A}[1])
. Assume that
E\in\mathcal{C}\cap(\mathcal{A}[1])
(for
E\in\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{A}
one can give a dual argument). If
E\notin\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}_{\gamma}[1]
, then there exists a subobject
f:F\hookrightarrow E[-1]
in
\mathcal{A}
with
\lambda_{\alpha,\beta,s}(F)>\gamma
. Note that, since
0\neq F\in\mathcal{A}
and
(\mathcal{A},Z_{\alpha,\beta,s})
is a Bridgeland stability condition, we have
Z_{\alpha,\beta,s}(F)\neq 0
. By Lemma
2.13
there is an exact triangle
F^{\prime}\overset{f^{\prime}}{\to}F\to F^{\prime\prime}\to F^{\prime}[1]
with
F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}\cap\mathcal{C},F^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{A}\cap(\mathcal{C}[-1])
. The object
F^{\prime}
should be nonzero by the assumption on the
\lambda_{\alpha,\beta,s}
-slope of
F
and the property (i) of Lemma
2.12
, hence the morphism
f^{\prime}:F^{\prime}\to F
is also nonzero. The composition
F^{\prime}\overset{f^{\prime}}{\to}F\overset{f}{\to}E[-1]
has to be nonzero, since it is the composition of a nonzero morphism in
\mathcal{A}
with a monomorphism
f
. However,
F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C},E[-1]\in\mathcal{C}[-1]
, and existence of such a morphism contradicts the definition of a t-structure.
∎

Let

Remark 2.16
.

Any object

is isomorphic to a complex of the form

\mathcal{O}_{X}(-1)^{\oplus a}\to\mathcal{Q}(-1)^{\oplus b}\to\mathcal{U}^{\oplus c}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus d},

.

Also, for further reference we include the following result (the idea is from
[
7
, Section 4]
).

Lemma 2.17
.

Let
E
be a reflexive sheaf on a smooth projective threefold
X
. Then
\mathrm{ch}_{i}(E^{\vee})=\mathrm{ch}_{i}(E)
for
i\leq 2
, and
\mathrm{ch}_{3}(E)+\mathrm{ch}_{3}(E^{\vee})=h^{0}(\mathcal{E}xt^{1}(E,\mathcal{O}_{X}))\geq 0
.

Proof.

The result
[
11
, Proposition 1.3]
of Hartshorne together with Auslander-Buchsbaum formula implies that the projective dimension of
E
at any point of
X
is smaller than or equal to 1. Therefore, we can construct a resolution

0\to\mathcal{E}_{1}\to\mathcal{E}_{0}\to E\to 0

with locally free sheaves
\mathcal{E}_{i}
. Dualizing, we obtain an exact sequence

0\to E^{\vee}\to\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\vee}\to\mathcal{E}_{1}^{\vee}\to\mathcal{E}xt^{1}(E,\mathcal{O}_{X})\to 0.

By
[
11
, Corollary 1.4]
the sheaf
E
is locally free on the complement of a closed set of dimension
\leq 0
, hence
\dim\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{E}xt^{1}(E,\mathcal{O}_{X}))\leq 0
. Such a sheaf can be obtained from skyscraper sheaves by extensions, and Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch Theorem implies, that
\mathrm{ch}_{i}(\mathcal{E}xt^{1}(E,\mathcal{O}_{X}))=0
for
i\leq 2
, and
\mathrm{ch}_{3}(\mathcal{E}xt^{1}(E,\mathcal{O}_{X}))=h^{0}(\mathcal{E}xt^{1}(E,\mathcal{O}_{X}))
. Using additivity of Chern character in exact sequences and the property
\mathrm{ch}_{i}(\mathcal{E}^{\vee})=(-1)^{i}\mathrm{ch}_{i}(\mathcal{E})
for a locally free sheaf
\mathcal{E}
, we obtain the statement of the lemma.
∎

3.
Objects with Maximal Third Chern Character

We first deal with the twisted trivial sheaves and their shifts.

Proposition 3.1
.

If
E\in\mathrm{D}^{b}(X)
is a tilt-semistable or Bridgeland semistable object for some
\alpha>0,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
with
\mathrm{ch}(E)=\mathrm{ch}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(n)^{\oplus m})
, then
E\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}(n)^{\oplus m}
. Moreover, if
E
is tilt-semistable and
\mathrm{ch}_{\leq 2}(E)=\mathrm{ch}_{\leq 2}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(n)^{\oplus m})
, then
\mathrm{ch}_{3}(E)\leq\mathrm{ch}_{3}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(n)^{\oplus m})
.

Proof.

We follow the lines of the proof of
[
24
, Proposition 4.1]
. Tensoring
E
with
\mathcal{O}_{X}(-n)
we reduce to the case
n=0
. We prove the statement for objects which are Bridgeland semistable for
\alpha=\frac{1}{4},\beta=-\frac{2}{3}
and
s>\frac{1}{6}
from Corollary
2.15
. By this Corollary either
E
or
E[1]
belongs to
\mathfrak{C}
. The Chern character assumption implies that
E\in\mathfrak{C}
and
E\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus m}
. The rest of the proof of the first statement repeats the arguments in
[
24
, Proposition 4.1]
verbatim.

The second statement is can analogously be reduced to the case
n=0
. Then the fact that
E\in\mathrm{Coh}^{\beta}(X)
implies that
\beta<0
, and then the generalized Bogomolov–Gieseker inequality (Proposition
2.2
) implies that
\mathrm{ch}_{3}(E)\leq 0
.
∎

The following statement may be used for dealing with semistable objects of negative rank.

Proposition 3.2

(
[
4
, Proposition 5.1.3]
)
.

For any
\nu_{\alpha,\beta}
-semistable object
E\in\mathrm{Coh}^{\beta}(X)
with
\nu_{\alpha,\beta}(E)\neq+\infty
there exists a
\nu_{\alpha,-\beta}
-semistable object
\widetilde{E}\in\mathrm{Coh}^{-\beta}(X)
and a sheaf
T
supported in dimension zero together with a distinguished triangle

(8)

\widetilde{E}\to\mathbf{R}\mathcal{H}om(E,\mathcal{O}_{X})[1]\to T[-1]\to\widetilde{E}[1].

Corollary 3.3
.

If
E\in\mathrm{Coh}^{\beta}(X)
is an object with
\mathrm{ch}_{\leq 2}(E)=-\mathrm{ch}_{\leq 2}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(n)^{\oplus m})
, which is tilt-semistable for some
\beta>n,\alpha>0
, then
\mathrm{ch}_{3}(E)\leq\mathrm{ch}_{3}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(n)^{\oplus m}[1])
, and in case of equality we have
E\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}(n)^{\oplus m}[1]
.

Proof.

Applying Proposition
3.2
to
E
we obtain a tilt-semistable object
\widetilde{E}
with
\mathrm{ch}_{\leq 2}(\widetilde{E})=\mathrm{ch}_{\leq 2}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(-n)^{\oplus m})
, and the exact triangle (
8
) gives
\mathrm{ch}_{3}(E)=\mathrm{ch}_{3}(\widetilde{E})-\mathrm{ch}_{3}(T)
. Proposition
3.1
says that
\mathrm{ch}_{3}(\widetilde{E})\leq\mathrm{ch}_{3}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(-n)^{\oplus m})
, which gives the desired bound on
\mathrm{ch}_{3}(E)
. In case when this bound is achieved we have
T=0
and
\mathbf{R}\mathcal{H}om(E,\mathcal{O}_{X})[1]\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}(-n)^{\oplus m}
. Applying the dualizing functor
\mathbf{R}\mathcal{H}om(-,\mathcal{O}_{X})[1]
to both sides we get
E\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}(n)^{\oplus m}[1]
.
∎

Now we move to the main result of this article.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
E\in\mathrm{Coh}^{\beta}(X)
be an object with
\mathrm{ch}(E)=2+cH+dH^{2}+eH^{3}
, which is
\nu_{\alpha,\beta}
-semistable for all
(\beta,\alpha)
in the closure
\overline{U}
of a non-empty open subset
U\subset\mathbb{H}
.

(1) If
c=-1
, then
d\leq\frac{1}{10}
.

(1.1) If
d=\frac{1}{10}
, then
e\leq\frac{1}{30}
. In case of equality we have
E\cong\mathcal{U}
.

(1.2) If
d=-\frac{1}{10}
, then
e\leq\frac{7}{30}
. In case of equality
E
is a coherent sheaf, included into an exact triple

0\to\mathcal{U}(-1)\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(-1)^{\oplus 4}\to E\to 0.

(2) If
c=0
, then
d\leq 0
.

(2.1) If
d=0
, then
e\leq 0
. In case of equality
E\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus 2}
.

(2.2) If
d=-\frac{1}{5}
, then
e\leq-\frac{1}{5}
. In case of equality
E
is a coherent sheaf, included into an exact triple

0\to E\to\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus 2}\to\mathcal{O}_{L}(1)\to 0

for a projective line
L\subset X
.

(2.3) If
d=-\frac{2}{5}
, then
e\leq 0
. In case of equality
E
is a coherent sheaf, included into an exact triple

0\to\mathcal{Q}(-1)^{\oplus 2}\to\mathcal{U}^{\oplus 4}\to E\to 0.

We consider the above cases in a sequence of lemmas. Firstly, let us prove the following auxiliary result.

Lemma 3.5
.

If an object
E\in\mathrm{Coh}^{\beta}(X)
is tilt-semistable in
\overline{U}
for a non-empty open subset
U\subset\mathbb{H}
and
\mathrm{ch}(E)=3-2H+\frac{3}{5}H^{2}+eH^{3},
then
e\leq-\frac{7}{30}
.

Proof.

Note that
E
should be tilt-stable in
U
, since
\mathrm{ch}_{\leq 2}(E)\in\Lambda
is a primitive element. If
E
is not tilt-stable for some
\beta<-\frac{2}{3},\alpha>0
, then there is a semicircular wall
W
, which should intersect the ray
\beta=\beta_{-}(E)=\frac{-2-\sqrt{2/5}}{3}
(by Proposition
2.3
(2, 3)). Lemma
2.9
implies that this wall is induced by a tilt-stable subobject or quotient
F
with
\mathrm{ch}_{\leq 2}(F)=s+xH+yH^{2},s>0,\mu(F)<\mu(E)
. Since
0<\mathrm{ch}_{1}^{\beta_{-}(E)}(F)<\mathrm{ch}_{1}^{\beta_{-}(E)}(E)
, we get

(9)

0<x+\frac{2}{3}s+\frac{\sqrt{2/5}}{3}s<\sqrt{\frac{2}{5}}.

Also we have
-1<\beta_{-}(E)<\beta_{-}(F)\leq\mu(F)<\mu(E)<0
(cf. Proposition
2.4
(5)) and
\beta_{+}(F)=2\mu(F)-\beta_{-}(F)<2\mu(E)-\beta_{-}(E)<0
. Hence
\beta_{-}(F),\beta_{+}(F)\in[-1,0)
. If
s\neq 1
, we can apply Theorem
2.8
to
F
and get that
s^{2}\leq\overline{\Delta}_{H}(F)<\overline{\Delta}_{H}(E)=10
. Hence,
s\in\{1,2,3\}
. A direct calculation shows that for any such
s
there are no integer
x
satisfying (
9
). Therefore,
E
is tilt-stable for all
\beta<-\frac{2}{3},\alpha>0
.

Proposition
2.7
implies that
E
should be a 2-stable sheaf, in particular,
H^{0}(E)=0
. If
H^{2}(E)\neq 0
, then by Serre duality there is a nonzero morphism
E\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(-2)[1]
. However, such a morphism would destabilize
E
below the wall
W(E,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-2)[1])
, contradicting what we have proved. We have
\mathrm{td}(T_{X})=1+H+\frac{8}{15}H^{2}+\frac{1}{5}H^{3}
(see
[
21
, Lemma 2.1]
). Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch Theorem gives that
\chi(E)=5e+\frac{2}{3}=-h^{1}(E)-h^{3}(E)\leq 0
, hence
e\leq-\frac{2}{15}
.

Since
-1<\beta_{-}(E)<-\frac{1}{2}
, Lemma
2.10
implies that
E[1]
is
\lambda_{\alpha,\beta,s}
-semistable for some
(\beta,\alpha)\in D
with infinite
\lambda_{\alpha,\beta,s}(E[1])
. Then Corollary
2.15
implies that
E[1]
belongs to
\mathfrak{C}
. For the case
e=-\frac{2}{15}
we find that
E\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}(-1)[2]\oplus\mathcal{U}
. However, this object does not belong to
\mathrm{Coh}^{\beta}(X)
, so this case is impossible. Since the step by which
\mathrm{ch}_{3}
can change for a fixed
\mathrm{ch}_{\leq 2}
is equal to
\frac{H^{3}}{10}
(because
c_{3}
belongs to
H^{6}(X,\mathbb{Z})
), we get that
e\leq-\frac{7}{30}
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

If
E\in\mathrm{Coh}^{\beta}(X)
is tilt-semistable in
\overline{U}
for a non-empty open subset
U\subset\mathbb{H}
and
\mathrm{ch}(E)=2-H+\frac{H^{2}}{10}+eH^{3}
, then
e\leq\frac{1}{30}
, and in case of equality we have
E\cong\mathcal{U}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
E
is destabilized at a semicircular wall by a tilt-stable subobject or quotient
F
with
\mathrm{ch}_{\leq 2}(F)=s+xH+yH^{2}
. The wall should intersect the ray
\beta=\beta_{-}(E)=-\frac{1}{2}-\sqrt{\frac{3}{20}}
. Again we may assume that
s>0,\mu(F)<\mu(E)
. Since
0<\mathrm{ch}_{1}^{\beta_{-}(E)}(F)<\mathrm{ch}_{1}^{\beta_{-}(E)}(E)
, we have

(10)

0<x+\frac{s}{2}+\sqrt{\frac{3}{20}}s<\sqrt{\frac{3}{5}}.

We also have
-1<\beta_{-}(E)<\beta_{-}(F)\leq\mu(F)<\mu(E)<0
and
\beta_{+}(F)=2\mu(F)-\beta_{-}(F)<2\mu(E)-\beta_{-}(E)<0
. If
s\neq 1
, then, applying Theorem
2.8
, we get that
s^{2}\leq\overline{\Delta}_{H}(F)<\overline{\Delta}_{H}(E)=15
. So
s\in\{1,2,3\}
. The only option for an integer
x
in (
10
) is
s=3,x=-2
. Using the inequalities
0\leq\overline{\Delta}_{H}(F)<\overline{\Delta}_{H}(E)
we get that
y=\frac{3}{5}
. Then
\mathrm{ch}_{\leq 2}(E/F)=-1+H-\frac{H^{2}}{2}
(by abuse of notation we assume that
F
is a subobject; if it is a quotient, then we can consider the kernel of the surjection
E\twoheadrightarrow F
instead of
E/F
). Lemma
3.5
implies that
\frac{\mathrm{ch}_{3}(F)}{H^{3}}\leq-\frac{7}{30}
; Proposition
3.3
, which can be applied, since the wall is not vertical, gives
\frac{\mathrm{ch}_{3}(E/F)}{H^{3}}\leq\frac{1}{6}
. Hence in this case
e\leq-\frac{1}{15}
. But
E\cong\mathcal{U}
has a bigger third Chern character, hence we get that
E
should be tilt-stable for all
\beta<-\frac{1}{2},\alpha>0
.

Tilt-stability of
E
implies that
H^{0}(E)=0
. If
H^{2}(E)\neq 0
, then there would exist a non-zero morphism
E\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(-2)[1]
, contradicting the fact that
E
is tilt-stable for all
\beta<-\frac{1}{2},\alpha>0
. Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch Theorem gives that
\chi(E)=5e-\frac{1}{6}\leq 0
. This gives
e\leq\frac{1}{30}
. In case of equality Lemma
2.10
and Corollary
2.15
imply that
E[1]
belongs to
\mathfrak{C}
. Chern character of
E
implies that
E\cong\mathcal{U}
.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

If
E\in\mathrm{Coh}^{\beta}(X)
is tilt-semistable in
\overline{U}
for a non-empty open subset
U\subset\mathbb{H}
and
\mathrm{ch}(E)=2-\frac{H^{2}}{5}+eH^{3}
, then
e\leq-\frac{1}{5}
, and in case of equality
E
is a coherent sheaf, included into an exact triple

(11)

0\to E\to\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus 2}\to\mathcal{O}_{L}(1)\to 0

for a projective line
L\subset X
.

Proof.

In this case
\mathrm{ch}_{\leq 2}(E)
also cannot be an integer multiple of
\mathrm{ch}_{\leq 2}
of another object, hence
E
is tilt-stable in
U
. Any semicircular wall for
E
has to intersect the ray
\beta=\beta_{-}(E)=-\frac{\sqrt{5}}{5}
. We can assume that this wall is given by a tilt-stable subobject or quotient
F
with
\mathrm{ch}_{\leq 2}(F)=s+xH+yH^{2}
,
s>0,\mu(F)<\mu(E)
. Since
0<\mathrm{ch}_{1}^{\beta_{-}(E)}(F)<\mathrm{ch}_{1}^{\beta_{-}(E)}(E)
, we have

(12)

0<x+\frac{\sqrt{5}}{5}s<\frac{2\sqrt{5}}{5}.

We also have
-1<\beta_{-}(E)<\beta_{-}(F)\leq\mu(F)<\mu(E)<0
. Dividing (
12
) by
s
, we obtain that
\mu(F)<\frac{2\sqrt{5}-s\sqrt{5}}{5s}
. Hence for
s\geq 4

\beta_{+}(F)=2\mu(F)-\beta_{-}(F)<\frac{4\sqrt{5}-s\sqrt{5}}{5s}\leq 0
. In this case Theorem
2.8
can be applied to
F
and gives
s^{2}\leq\overline{\Delta}_{H}(F)<\overline{\Delta}_{H}(E)=10
. So, we get that
s\in\{1,2,3\}
.

If
s=1
, then (
12
) implies that
-\frac{\sqrt{5}}{5}<x<\frac{\sqrt{5}}{5}
, so
x=0
, but in this case the property
\mu(F)<\mu(E)
does not hold and the wall is not semicircular. If
s=2
, then by (
12
)
-1<x<0
, which is impossible. And if
s=3
, then (
12
) implies that
-2<x<0
, so
x=-1
. Inequalities
0\leq\overline{\Delta}_{H}(F)<20
together with the fact that
y\in-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{5}\mathbb{Z}
(since
c_{i}(F)\in H^{2i}(X,\mathbb{Z})
) imply that
y=\frac{1}{10}
. But then we find that
\overline{\Delta}_{H}(F)=\overline{\Delta}_{H}(E/F)=10
, contradicting the inequality
\overline{\Delta}_{H}(F)+\overline{\Delta}_{H}(E/F)<\overline{\Delta}_{H}(E)
. Therefore,
E
is
\nu_{\alpha,\beta}
-stable for all
\alpha>0,\beta<0
.

Tilt-stability of
E
implies that
H^{0}(E)=0
. Also
H^{2}(E)\cong\Hom(E,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-2)[1])^{\vee}=0
, because otherwise there would be a semicircular wall. Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch Theorem gives
\chi(E)=5e+1\leq 0
, hence
e\leq-\frac{1}{5}
. Assume that
e=-\frac{1}{5}
. Proposition
2.7
implies that
E
is a 2-stable sheaf. Since
E
is a rank 2 sheaf with negative third Chern class, it cannot be reflexive (see the proof of
[
11
, Proposition 2.6]
). The sheaf
E
can be included into an exact sequence

0\to E\to E^{\vee\vee}\to T\to 0,

where
\dim\mathrm{supp}(T)\leq 1
. If
\dim\mathrm{supp}(T)=0
, then
\nu_{\alpha,\beta}(E^{\vee\vee})=\nu_{\alpha,\beta}(E)
for all
(\beta,\alpha)
, and tilt-stability of
E
implies tilt-stability of
E^{\vee\vee}
. Applying to
E^{\vee\vee}
the above arguments, we obtain that
\frac{\mathrm{ch}_{3}(E^{\vee\vee})}{H^{3}}\leq-\frac{1}{5}
, contradicting reflexivity of
E^{\vee\vee}
.

So,
\mathrm{supp}(T)
contains 1-dimensional components, and
\frac{H\cdot\mathrm{ch}_{2}(T)}{H^{3}}\geq\frac{1}{5}
. As Schmidt noted in
[
25
, Lemma 2.1]
, the dual of a slope semistable sheaf is slope semistable, hence
E^{\vee\vee}
is slope semistable. If it is slope stable, then Bogomolov-Gieseker inequality implies that
\mathrm{ch}_{\leq 2}(E)=(2,0,0)
. The generalized Bogomolov-Gieseker inequality implies
\frac{\mathrm{ch}_{3}(E^{\vee\vee})}{H^{3}}\leq 0
. But, as we already mentioned, the third Chern class of a rank 2 reflexive sheaf has to be non-negative, implying that
\mathrm{ch}_{3}(E^{\vee\vee})=0
. Proposition
3.1
implies that
E^{\vee\vee}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus 2}
, but this sheaf is not slope stable.

Therefore,
E^{\vee\vee}
is strictly slope semistable, implying that there is a short exact sequence in
\mathrm{Coh}(X)

(13)

0\to F\to E^{\vee\vee}\to G\to 0,

where
F
and
G
are torsion free rank one sheaves with
\mu(F)=\mu(G)=0
. Bogomolov-Gieseker inequality implies that
\frac{H\cdot\mathrm{ch}_{2}(F)}{H^{3}}
and
\frac{H\cdot\mathrm{ch}_{2}(G)}{H^{3}}
are non-positive, and positivity of
\frac{H\cdot\mathrm{ch}_{2}(T)}{H^{3}}
implies that
\mathrm{ch}_{\leq 2}(F)=\mathrm{ch}_{\leq 2}(G)=(1,0,0)
. Generalized Bogomolov-Gieseker inequality implies that
\frac{\mathrm{ch}_{3}(F)}{H^{3}}
and
\frac{\mathrm{ch}_{3}(G)}{H^{3}}
are non-positive, and non-negativity of
c_{3}(E^{\vee\vee})
implies their vanishing. By Proposition
3.1
we get
F\cong\mathcal{O}_{X},G\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}
, hence
E^{\vee\vee}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus 2}
.

Since
\mathrm{ch}(T)=\frac{H^{2}}{5}+\frac{H^{3}}{5}
, one-dimensional part of
\mathrm{supp}(T)
consists of one projective line
L\subset X
. Classification of coherent sheaves on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
implies that
T\cong\mathcal{O}_{L}(a)\oplus T^{\prime},\dim\mathrm{supp}(T^{\prime})\leq 0
. Chern character of
T
together with existence of a surjective morphism
\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus 2}\twoheadrightarrow T
implies that

or

. However, in the second case one can, using that
H^{0}(E)=0
, construct a subsheaf
\mathcal{I}_{p}\hookrightarrow E
, contradicting 2-stability of

. And in case when

one can show that

\dim Z\leq 0

is 2-stable.
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

If
E\in\mathrm{Coh}^{\beta}(X)
is tilt-semistable in
\overline{U}
for a non-empty open subset
U\subset\mathbb{H}
and
\mathrm{ch}(E)=2-H-\frac{H^{2}}{10}+eH^{3}

is a coherent sheaf, included into an exact triple

(14)

0\to\mathcal{U}(-1)\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(-1)^{\oplus 4}\to E\to 0.

Proof.

Note that
H^{2}\cdot\mathrm{ch}^{-1}_{1}(E)
has the least positive value, so there is no wall for
E
at
\beta=-1
for any
\alpha>0

defines a semi-disk lying below the numerical wall

. Therefore the generalized Bogomolov-Gieseker inequality together with the fact that the walls are nested implies that

. Using also the integrality of Chern classes of

, we obtain the bound
e\leq\frac{7}{30}
. Assume that

.

A calculation shows that the numerical wall
W(E,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-3)[1])
intersects the ray
\beta=-1
. Therefore,
\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(-1),E)\cong\Hom(E,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-3)[1])=0
. Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch Theorem gives that
\Hom(\mathcal{O}_{X}(-1),E)\geq\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X}(-1),E)=4
. This gives an exact sequence

(15)

0\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(-1)^{\oplus 4}\to E\to G\to 0

in the category
\mathrm{Coh}^{\beta}(X)
for
\beta<-1
large enough. Here
\mathrm{ch}(G)=-2+3H-\frac{21}{10}H^{2}+\frac{9}{10}H^{3}
. We find
\mathrm{ch}(\mathbf{R}\mathcal{H}om(G,\mathcal{O}_{X})[1](-2))=2-H+\frac{H^{2}}{10}+\frac{H^{3}}{30}
. Proposition
3.2
gives an exact sequence

0\to\widetilde{G}\to\mathbf{R}\mathcal{H}om(G,\mathcal{O}_{X})[1](-2)\to T[-1]\to 0

with a tilt-semistable object
\widetilde{G}
and a sheaf

with

. It follows that
\frac{\mathrm{ch}_{3}(\widetilde{G})}{H^{3}}=\frac{1}{30}+\frac{\mathrm{ch}_{3}(T)}{H^{3}}.
Since
\frac{\mathrm{ch}_{3}(T)}{H^{3}}\geq 0
and, by Lemma
3.6
,
\frac{\mathrm{ch}_{3}(\widetilde{G})}{H^{3}}\leq\frac{1}{30}
, we obtain that

and
\mathbf{R}\mathcal{H}om(G,\mathcal{O}_{X})[1](-2)\cong\mathcal{U}
. It follows that

. A calculation shows that
\nu_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(-1))>\nu_{\alpha,\beta}(E)
under the wall

is not tilt-semistable there. Since any wall lying above

would intersect the ray

, it follows that

is tilt-semistable everywhere above

. By Proposition
2.7

is a sheaf, and (
15

14
).
∎

In order to continue, we need several preparatory statements.

Lemma 3.9
.

If
E\in\mathrm{Coh}^{\beta}(X)
is tilt-semistable in
\overline{U}
for a non-empty open subset

\mathrm{ch}(E)=3-H+\frac{1}{10}H^{2}+eH^{3}

Any semicircular wall for

F

\mathrm{ch}_{\leq 2}(F)=s+xH+yH^{2},s>0,\mu(F)<\mu(E)

0<\mathrm{ch}_{1}^{\beta_{-}(E)}(F)<\mathrm{ch}_{1}^{\beta_{-}(E)}(E)
, we have

(16)

0<x+\frac{1}{3}s+\frac{2\sqrt{10}}{30}s<\frac{2\sqrt{10}}{10}.

We have
-1<\beta_{-}(E)<\beta_{-}(F)\leq\mu(F)<\mu(E)<0

\beta_{+}(F)=2\mu(F)-\beta_{-}(F)<2\mu(E)-\beta_{-}(E)=-\frac{2}{3}+\frac{10+2\sqrt{10}}{30}<0

2.8
can be applied to

If

satisfying (

. However, in this case the property

does not hold. If

, then
x=-1
. However, in this case

, and the property
\overline{\Delta}_{H}(F)=25-100y\in[0,10)
is not satisfied for any such

. And if
s=3

satisfying (
16

We proved that there are no semicircular walls for

implies that

∎

Lemma 3.10
.

If
E\in\mathrm{Coh}^{\beta}(X)

Corollary 3.11
.

There are no objects
E\in\mathrm{Coh}^{\beta}(X)
with
\mathrm{ch}_{\leq 2}(E)=3-H+\frac{1}{10}H^{2}
, which are tilt-semistable in
\overline{U}
for some non-empty open subset
U\subset\mathbb{H}
.

Proof.

By the proof of Lemma
3.9
such an object
E
should be tilt-stable for all
\beta<-\frac{1}{3},\alpha>0
, hence
E
is a 2-stable sheaf. The dual sheaf
E^{\vee}
should be slope semistable, and in fact slope stable since its rank and first Chern class are coprime. If
E
is reflexive, then
\mathrm{ch}_{\leq 2}(E^{\vee})=3+H+\frac{1}{10}H^{2}
. However, in this case Lemmas
3.9
and
3.10
imply that
\mathrm{ch}_{3}(E)+\mathrm{ch}_{3}(E^{\vee})<0
, contradicting Lemma
2.17
, so the sheaf

is not reflexive. There is a short exact sequence

0\to E\to E^{\vee\vee}\to T\to 0

with
\dim\mathrm{supp}\ T\leq 1
. The support of

cannot be zero-dimensional, because in this case
\mathrm{ch}_{\leq 2}(E^{\vee\vee})=3-H+\frac{1}{10}H^{2}
and by the previous argument

cannot be reflexive. And if

, then
\mathrm{ch}_{\leq 2}(E^{\vee\vee})=3-H+(\frac{1}{10}+d)H^{2}
for

and Bogomolov-Gieseker inequality for

is not satisfied.
∎

Now we are ready to make the next step.

Any semicircular wall for

a\leq 3k-1

Q\leq f_{5k+1,a}\leq f_{5k+1,3k-1}<5k^{2}+2k-1
. Also we find
f_{5k+1,3k+2}=5k^{2}+2k-\frac{9}{4}
, hence for all
a\geq 3k+2

Q\leq 5k^{2}+2k-\frac{9}{4}<5k^{2}+2k-1
. Hence
a\in\{3k,3k+1\}
. This proves the case (2). The cases (3-5) can be proven completely analogously.
∎

.

Suppose that
E\in\mathrm{Coh}(X)
is a Gieseker semistable sheaf with
\mathrm{ch}(E)=2+cH+dH^{2}+eH^{3}

(1)

Let

Let us give some further evidence for Conjecture
4.5
. Note that for an extension

0\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(-1)^{\oplus 2}\to E\to G\to 0

with
\mathrm{ch}(G)=H+\left(k-\frac{9}{10}\right)H^{2}+\left(\frac{5k^{2}-9k+2}{10}+\frac{1}{6}\right)H^{3},k\in\mathbb{Z}

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
E\in\mathrm{Coh}^{\beta}(X)
be a tilt-semistable object with
\mathrm{ch}(E)=2-H+\left(k+\frac{1}{10}\right)H^{2}+eH^{3}
. If
k\leq-2
and
e\geq\frac{15k^{2}-27k+1}{30}
, then
E
is destabilized along a semicircular wall with the subobject and the quotient of rank at most two.

Proof.

We can calculate that the radius
\rho_{Q}
of the numerical wall
Q_{\alpha,\beta}(E)=0
satisfies the inequality

\frac{\rho_{Q}}{\overline{\Delta}_{H}(E)}\geq-\frac{9}{800}k-\frac{11}{2000}-\frac{147}{8000-20000k}+\frac{2401}{-2\cdot 10^{6}k^{3}+24\cdot 10^{5}k^{2}-96\cdot 10^{4}k+128000}.

Bounding each term, we see that
\frac{\rho_{Q}}{\overline{\Delta}_{H}(E)}>\frac{1}{300}
, hence we can apply Proposition
2.4
(1) to get the statement of our lemma.∎

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
E\in\mathrm{Coh}^{\beta}(X)
be a tilt-semistable object with
\mathrm{ch}(E)=2-H+\left(k+\frac{1}{10}\right)H^{2}+eH^{3}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. If
k\leq-2
and
E
is destabilized by a subobject
F
of rank one, then
e\leq\frac{15k^{2}-27k+1}{30}
.

Proof.

Follows along the same lines as the proof of
[
23

. Note that in our case

\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{H\cdot\mathrm{ch}_{2}(E)}{H^{3}}\right)^{2}+\frac{H\cdot\mathrm{ch}_{2}(E)}{H^{3}}+\frac{5}{24}=\frac{15k^{2}-27k+1}{30}+\frac{2}{25}.

Since the step by which
\mathrm{ch}_{3}(E)
can change for a fixed
\mathrm{ch}_{\leq 2}(E)
is equal to
\frac{H^{3}}{10}
, the bound proven in
[

suffices to prove our lemma.∎

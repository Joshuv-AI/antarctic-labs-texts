Weighted geodesic restrictions of arithmetic eigenfunctions
Jiaqi Hou and Xiaoqi Huang

Abstract
. Let X X be an arithmetic hyperbolic surface, ψ \psi a Hecke-Maass form, ℓ \ell a geodesic segment on X X , and μ \mu a Borel measure supported on ℓ \ell with dimension greater than 1/2. We obtain a power saving over the local bound of Eswarathasan and Pramanik [ 8 ] for the L 2 L^{2} norm of ψ \psi with respect to μ \mu , which is a weighted generalization of Marshall’s geodesic restriction bound [ 15 ] and is proved by applying the method of arithmetic amplification. On a general 2-dimensional Riemannian manifold, we also obtain a Kakeya-Nikodym bound for the L 2 L^{2} norm of any Laplace-Beltrami eigenfunction with respect to a Borel measure supported on a geodesic segment with dimension greater than 1/2.



1.
Introduction

The purpose of this paper is to obtain improved
L^{2}
eigenfunction estimate restricted to a general Borel sets on an arithmetic hyperbolic surface.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold,

\Delta_{g}
be the
Laplace-Beltrami operator associated with the metric
g
on
M
. We let
0=\lambda_{0}^{2}<\lambda^{2}_{1}\leq\lambda^{2}_{2}\leq\cdots
denote the
eigenvalues labeled with respect to the multiplicity of

{-\Delta_{g}}

and
e_{\lambda_{j}}
the associated
L^{2}
-normalized eigenfunctions, that is,

(1.1)

(\Delta_{g}+\lambda^{2}_{j})e_{\lambda_{j}}=0,\qquad\text{and }\quad\int_{M}|e_{\lambda_{j}}(x)|^{2}\,dx=1.

There are several ways to quantify the concentration and size of eigenfunctions
e_{\lambda}
with
\lambda=\lambda_{j}>0
. A universal result of Sogge
[
18
]
states that on an

n
-dimensional compact manifold without boundary, for any
2\leq q\leq\infty
,

(1.2)

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{q}(M)}\lesssim\lambda^{\sigma(q)},\qquad\sigma(q)=\max\{\tfrac{n-1}{2}(\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1}{q}),\tfrac{n-1}{2}-\tfrac{n}{q}\}.

In
[
5
]
, Burq-Gérard-Tzvetkov studied restrictions of eigenfunctions to submanifolds. In particular, if
\ell
is a smooth curve on a Riemannian surface, they showed that

(1.3)

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{q}(\ell)}\lesssim\lambda^{\sigma_{1}(q)},\qquad\sigma_{1}(q)=\max\{\tfrac{1}{4},\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1}{q}\}.

Both (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) are sharp on the standard round sphere by taking
e_{\lambda}
to be the zonal functions or the highest weight spherical harmonics. Under additional geometric assumptions–such as nonpositive sectional curvature or on flat tori–one can improve (
1.3
); see, for example,

[
6
,
4
,
2
,
20
]
and the references therein for more details.

Eswarathasan and Pramanik
[
8
]
then considered the restriction of eigenfunctions to arbitrary Borel sets. To describe the results in this direction, we recall the Frostman Lemma in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(see e.g.,
[
17
]
). It states that for a Borel set
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
s>0
, the
s
-dimensional Hausdorff measure satisfies
\mathcal{H}^{s}(B)>0
if and only if there exists a nontrivial finite Borel measure
\mu
supported on
B

and a constant
C>0
such that

\mu(B(x,r))\leq Cr^{s},\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\ r>0.

In this paper, we say that a Borel measure
\mu
on
M
has dimension
\alpha
if there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\mu(B(x,r))\leq Cr^{\alpha},\qquad\forall x\in M,\ 0<r<\text{inj}(M).

Here,
B(x,r)
denotes the geodesic ball of radius
r
centered at
x
, and
\text{inj}(M)
denotes the
injectivity radius of
M
.

For an
\alpha
-dimensional Borel measure
\mu
on a
Riemannian surface, analogues of (
1.2
) on the size of
\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{q}(M;\,d\mu)}
was obtained in
[
8
]
.
More recently, Gao-Miao-Xi
[
10
]
proved sharp estimate on
\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{q}(M;\,d\mu)}
for all
0<\alpha\leq 2
and
q\geq 2
on any Riemannian surface. In the special case
q=2
, their conclusions can be stated as follows:

Theorem 1.1

(
[
8
,
10
]
)
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,2]
,
\mu
be an
\alpha
-dimensional probability measure on a Riemannian surface
M
. Then for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exist
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

(1.4)

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(M;\,d\mu)}\lesssim\lambda^{\gamma(\alpha)+\varepsilon}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(M)},

where

\gamma(\alpha)=\begin{cases}\tfrac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{2},\qquad&\text{if }\,0<\alpha\leq\frac{1}{2},\\
\frac{1}{4},&\text{if }\,\frac{1}{2}<\alpha\leq 1,\\
\frac{2-\alpha}{4},&\text{if }\,1<\alpha\leq 2.\end{cases}

Moreover, (
1.4
) is sharp on
S^{2}
up to the
\lambda^{\varepsilon}
loss.

1.1.
Improvements in the arithmetic setting

Now we take the surface to be
X=\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}
, an arithmetic hyperbolic surface, where
\Gamma
is a cocompact congruence lattice in
\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
arising from a quaternion division algebra over
\mathbb{Q}
(see Section
3.1
for the precise definitions). Let
\psi
be an
L^{2}
-normalized Hecke–Maass form on
X
with spectral parameter
\lambda
. Namely,
\psi
is a joint eigenfunction of the Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Delta
and all unramified Hecke operators on
X
, and satisfies
\Delta\psi+(1/4+\lambda^{2})\psi=0
. As we are considering large-eigenvalue asymptotics, we will also assume that
\lambda>0
to exclude exceptional Laplace eigenvalues. For these
X
, Iwaniec and Sarnak
[
14
]
showed that the classical local bound
\|\psi\|_{\infty}\lesssim\lambda^{1/2}
(taking
n=2
and
q=\infty
in (
1.2
)) can be improved a power to
\|\psi\|_{\infty}\lesssim_{\epsilon}\lambda^{5/12+\epsilon}
. Therefore, the trivial estimate

\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\leq\|\psi\|_{\infty}\left(\int_{X}d\mu(x)\right)^{1/2}\lesssim_{\epsilon}\lambda^{5/12+\epsilon}

provides a power saving over (
1.4
) when
\alpha<\frac{1}{6}
. The main result of this paper is an improvement of (
1.4
) for Hecke-Maass forms on
X
in the range
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},1]
with
\mu
supported on a geodesic segment.

Let
M
be any Riemannian surface and
\ell(s):[0,1]\rightarrow M
be a geodesic segment of unit length, we define
\mu
to be a Borel measure of dimension
\alpha\in(0,1]
supported on
\ell
if
\mu
is the push-forward of some
\alpha
-dimensional Borel measure
\nu
on
[0,1]
under
\ell
, i.e.,

(1.5)

\mu(E)=\nu(\ell^{-1}(E))\,\,\,\text{for all Borel sets }\,\,E\subset M.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\psi
be an
L^{2}
-normalized Hecke–Maass form on
X
with spectral parameter
\lambda
. For any geodesic segment
\ell
of unit length, and any Borel measure
\mu
supported on
\ell
, with dimension
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},1]
, then we have

(1.6)

\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=\left(\int_{0}^{1}|\psi(\ell(s))|^{2}d\nu(s)\right)^{1/2}\lesssim_{\alpha,\epsilon}\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}-\delta(\alpha)+\epsilon}

where the measure
d\nu
is defined in (
1.5
) and

\displaystyle\delta(\alpha)=\frac{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}{24(\alpha-\frac{1}{2})+2}.

The implied constant is independent of
\ell
.

In the special case
\alpha=1
, Theorem
1.2
is due to Marshall
[
15
]
.
The proof of Theorem
1.2
follows by reducing the restriction norm to a weighted geodesic restriction norm and adapting the arguments of Marshall
[
15
]
, which are based on the arithmetic amplification method, to this weighted setting.

1.2.
A Kakeya-Nikodym bound

Following ideas in the work of Bourgain
[
3
]
,
we also obtain the following result on a general compact manifold.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact 2-dimensional Riemannian manifold, let
\Delta_{g}
be the
Laplace-Beltrami operator associated with the metric
g
on
M
. Assume
e_{\lambda}
is an
L^{2}
normalized eigenfunction satisfying (
1.1
).
For any geodesic segment
\ell
of unit length, and any Borel measure
\mu
supported on
\ell
, with dimension
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
, we have

(1.7)

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=\left(\int_{0}^{1}|e_{\lambda}(\ell(s))|^{2}d\nu(x)\right)^{1/2}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}}\left(\sup_{\ell\in{\mathit{\Pi}}}\int_{\mathcal{T}_{\lambda^{-1/2}}(\ell)}|e_{\lambda}(x)|^{2}dx\right)^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}

The estimate (
1.7
) also holds for
\alpha=1
, up to an additional logarithmic loss in
\lambda
.

Here
\mathit{\Pi}
denotes the set of unit-length geodesics in
M
and
\mathcal{T}_{\lambda^{-1/2}}(\ell)
denotes the
\lambda^{-1/2}
tubular neighborhood of the geodesic
\ell
. The quantity on the right side of (
1.7
) is often referred to as the Kakeya-Nikodym norm. (
1.7
) suggests that any improvement on the Kakeya-Nikodym norm bound yields a corresponding improvement in the restriction bounds of eigenfunctions to Borel measures
\mu
satisfying dimensional assumptions. For example, on Riemannian surfaces of nonpositive curvature, one obtains improved Kakeya–Nikodym estimates over the uniform bound; see, for instance,
[
1
, Theorem 1.1]
and
[
13
, Theorem 1.2]
.
If we take
M
to be an arithmetic hyperbolic surface
X
as in Theorem
1.2
and
e_{\lambda}=\psi
to be an
L^{2}
-normalized Hecke-Maass form with spectral parameter
\lambda
, then Marshall’s geodesic restriction estimate
[
15
, Theorem 1.1]
directly implies that

\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\lesssim_{\alpha,\epsilon}\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{14}(\alpha-\frac{1}{2})+\epsilon}

for any
\mu
satisfying the assumptions in Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
. However, it may be seen that the above bound is weaker than (
1.6
) in Theorem
1.2
.

Note that the exponents in both (
1.6
) and (
1.7
) tend to
0
as
\alpha
approaches
\frac{1}{2}
. Thus

\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
is a critical value for eigenfunction restriction problems. Actually, the functions that saturate (
1.4
) when
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
include both the Zoll spherical functions and the highest weight spherical harmonics. Hence, in this case, the restriction norm is sensitive to both pointwise and geodesic concentration of eigenfunctions.
We wish to explore this critical case in future work.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we reduce the restriction norm to a weighted norm along geodesics using the locally constant property of eigenfunctions and prove several auxiliary estimates for the corresponding weights. In Section
3
, we introduce the definition of Maass forms on arithmetic hyperbolic surfaces, the Hecke operators, and the related amplification inequalities.
In Sections
4
and
5
, we prove Theorem
1.2
. Finally, in Section
6
, we give the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

1.3.
Notation

Throughout the paper, the notation
A\lesssim B
will mean that there is a positive constant
C
such that
|A|\leq CB
, and
A\sim B
will mean that there are positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that
C_{1}B\leq A\leq C_{2}B
. We also use
A=O(B)
to mean
A\lesssim B
.

If
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, the Fourier transform
\widehat{f}
of
f
in this paper is defined as

\displaystyle\widehat{f}(\xi)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x)e^{-ix\xi}dx.

If
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
is a Schwartz function, the Fourier inversion formula reads

f(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(\xi)e^{ix\xi}d\xi.

2.
Weight functions and auxiliary estimates

By rescaling the metric, or in the hyperbolic surface case by passing to a finite-index sublattice if necessary, we may assume throughout that the injectivity radius of our manifold is at least 10.
We will first show the following reduction.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
M
be a Riemannian surface and
\mu
be an
\alpha
-dimensional Borel probablity measure supported on a unit-length geodesic
\ell(s):[0,1]\rightarrow M
. Suppose that
e_{\lambda}
is an
L^{2}
-normalized Laplace eigenfunction satisfying (
1.1
). Then there exists a constant
C>0
and a smooth weight function
w(s)
supported in
|s|\leq 2
such that for any
N\geq 1

(2.1)

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\leq C\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(w)}+O_{N}(\lambda^{-N}),

where

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(w)}=\int|e_{\lambda}(\ell(s))|^{2}w(s)ds.

And for any interval
[a-r,a+r]\subset\mathbb{R}
, we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle\int_{a-r}^{a+r}w(s)ds\lesssim r^{\alpha}.

Note that the Hecke–Maass form
\psi
with spectral parameter
\lambda
in Theorem
1.2
is also an eigenfunction of the Laplacian with eigenvalue
\frac{1}{4}+\lambda^{2}
with
\lambda>0
(see (
3.2
) below). Therefore, the conclusion of Lemma
2.1
also applies to
\psi
.

To prove Lemma
2.1
, we shall use the following lemma, see e.g.,
[
19
, Chapter 5]
for its proof.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian surface with the Riemannian distance function
d_{g}(x,y)
for
x,y\in M
and the Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Delta_{g}
. Fix
\chi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
with
\chi(0)=1
and
\text{supp}\,{\widehat{\chi}}\subset(\frac{1}{2},1)
. Then for any
f\in C^{\infty}(M)

(2.3)

\chi(\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}-\lambda)f(x)=\lambda^{1/2}\int_{M}e^{i\lambda d_{g}(x,y)}a(x,y,\lambda)f(y)dy+R_{\lambda}f(x)

where

\|R_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}\leq C_{N}\lambda^{-N}\|f\|_{L^{1}(M)},\qquad\forall N=1,2,3...,

\alpha(x,y,\lambda)
is smooth with the uniform bounds

\partial_{x,y}^{\gamma}a(x,y,\lambda)\leq C_{\gamma},\qquad\text{ for all multi-indices }\gamma,

and

(2.4)

a(x,y,\lambda)=0\qquad\text{if }\,d_{g}(x,y)\notin(\tfrac{1}{2},1)

Write

T_{\lambda}f(x)=\lambda^{1/2}\int_{M}e^{i\lambda d_{g}(x,y)}a(x,y,\lambda)f(y)dy.

Using (
2.3
), we have

(2.5)

\displaystyle e_{\lambda}=\chi(\sqrt{\Delta_{g}}-\lambda)e_{\lambda}=T_{\lambda}e_{\lambda}+O(\lambda^{-N})

Since
d_{g}(x,y)\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
when
(x,y)
is in the support of the amplitute
a
, it is not hard to check

(2.6)

\partial_{s}d_{g}(\ell(s),y)\leq C_{\ell}

for some constant
C_{\ell}
depending on the metric.
Hence if we fix a nonnegative
\rho(s)\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying
\rho(s)\geq 0
,
\rho(s)=1
when
|s|\leq\frac{3}{2}
and
\rho(s)=0
when
|s|\geq 2
, and fix

\eta
such that
\widehat{\eta}\in C_{c}^{\infty}
,
\widehat{\eta}(\xi)=1
when
|\xi|\leq 2C_{\ell}
and
\widehat{\eta}(\xi)=0
when
|\xi|\geq 4C_{\ell}
, we have for
|s|\leq 1
,

(2.7)

\displaystyle T_{\lambda}e_{\lambda}(\ell(s))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\iint_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{i(s-t)\xi}\rho(t)T_{\lambda}e_{\lambda}(\ell(t))d\xi dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\iint_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{i(s-t)\xi}\widehat{\eta}(\xi/\lambda)\rho(t)T_{\lambda}e_{\lambda}(\ell(t))d\xi dt

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\pi}\iint_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{i(s-t)\xi}(1-\widehat{\eta}(\xi/\lambda))\rho(t)T_{\lambda}e_{\lambda}(\ell(t))d\xi dt.

The second term on the right side of (
2.7
) is
O_{N}(\lambda^{-N})
for any
N
if we expand
T_{\lambda}e_{\lambda}(\ell(t))
and integrate by parts in
dt
by applying (
2.6
). Thus, we have

(2.8)

\displaystyle T_{\lambda}e_{\lambda}(\ell(s))=\int T_{\lambda}e_{\lambda}(\ell(s))\rho(t)\lambda\eta(\lambda(s-t))dt+O(\lambda^{-N}),\,\,\,\text{if}\,\,\,|s|\leq 1.

Therefore, since the measure
\mu
is supported on
\ell
, by Hölder’s inequality

\displaystyle\int_{M}|T_{\lambda}e_{\lambda}(x)|^{2}d\mu(x)=\int_{0}^{1}|T_{\lambda}e_{\lambda}(\ell(s))|^{2}d\nu(s)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int|\lambda\eta(\lambda(s-t))|\rho(t)dt\right)\left(\int|T_{\lambda}e_{\lambda}(\ell(t))|^{2}|\lambda\eta(\lambda(s-t))|\rho(t)dt\right)d\nu(s)+O(\lambda^{-N})

(2.9)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int|T_{\lambda}e_{\lambda}(\ell(t))|^{2}|\lambda\eta(\lambda(s-t))|\rho(t)dtd\nu(s)+O(\lambda^{-N})

If we use (
2.5
) and (
2.9
), we have

(2.10)

\displaystyle\int|e_{\lambda}(x)|^{2}d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}\int|e_{\lambda}(\ell(t))|^{2}|\lambda\eta(\lambda(s-t))|\rho(t)dtd\nu(s)+O(\lambda^{-N})

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}\int|e_{\lambda}(\ell(t))|^{2}\sqrt{\lambda^{2}\eta^{2}(\lambda(s-t))+1}\rho(t)dtd\nu(s)+O(\lambda^{-N}).

If we define the weight function
w(t)
as

(2.11)

w(t)=\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{\lambda^{2}\eta^{2}(\lambda(s-t))+1}\,\rho(t)\,d\nu(s)

Then (
2.1
) follows from (
2.10
). Now let’s verify (
2.2
), note that since
\nu
is an
\alpha
-dimensional Borel measure on
[0,1]
, we have

w(t)\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}(|\lambda\eta(\lambda(s-t))|+1)\,\rho(t)\,d\nu(s)\lesssim_{\nu}\int_{0}^{1}|\lambda\eta(\lambda(s-t))|\,\rho(t)\,d\nu(s)+\rho(t)\

Thus, it suffices to verify (
2.2
) with

w(t)
replaced by

\rho(t)\int_{0}^{1}|\lambda\eta(\lambda(s-t))|\,\,d\nu(s).

If
r\leq\lambda^{-1}
, let
I_{k}=[a+k\lambda^{-1},a+(k+1)\lambda^{-1}]
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Since
\eta
is a Schwartz function, it is not hard to check that for any
t\in[a-r,a+r]
and
s\in I_{k}
, we have

|\eta(\lambda(s-t))|\lesssim(1+|k|)^{-2}
. Hence,

(2.12)

\displaystyle\int_{a-r}^{a+r}

\displaystyle\rho(t)\int_{0}^{1}|\lambda\eta(\lambda(s-t))|\,\,d\nu(s)dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k}\int_{a-r}^{a+r}\rho(t)\int_{I_{k}}\lambda(1+|k|)^{-2}\,\,d\nu(s)dt

\displaystyle\lesssim r\lambda^{1-\alpha}\lesssim r^{\alpha},

where in the second inequality we used the fact that
\nu
is an
\alpha
-dimensional Borel measure on
[0,1]
.

If
r\geq\lambda^{-1}
,
let
I_{k}=[a+kr,a+(k+1)r]
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then one can similarly check that for any
t\in[a-r,a+r]
and
s\in I_{k}
, we have

|\sqrt{\eta}(\lambda(s-t))|\lesssim(1+\lambda|k|r)^{-2}\lesssim(1+|k|)^{-2}.

Hence,

(2.13)

\displaystyle\int_{a-r}^{a+r}

\displaystyle\rho(t)\int_{0}^{1}|\lambda\eta(\lambda(s-t))|\,\,d\nu(s)dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k}\left(\sup_{s\in I_{k}}\int_{a-r}^{a+r}\rho(t)\lambda\sqrt{\eta}(\lambda(s-t))dt\right)\left(\int_{I_{k}}(1+|k|)^{-2}\,\,d\nu(s)\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim r^{\alpha}.

2.1.
Energy-integral estimates

Now we fix the weight function
w
produced by Lemma
2.1
.
Following
[
17
, §2.5]
, for any
0<s<\alpha
, we define the
s
-energy of the measure
w(x)dx
by

\displaystyle I_{s}(w):=\iint_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{w(x)w(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}dxdy.

[
17
, Theorem 2.8]
shows that if we have (
2.2
), then
I_{s}(w)=O_{\alpha,s}(1)
. We first refine the above estimate as follows. We let

\displaystyle I_{s}(\phi w)=\iint_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\phi(x)\overline{\phi(y)}w(x)w(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}dxdy.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that
0<s<\alpha
and
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})\subset L^{2}(w)=L^{2}(\mathbb{R},w(x)dx)
. Then

\displaystyle I_{s}(\phi w)\lesssim_{\alpha,s}\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(w)}

Proof.

By the mean inequality,

\displaystyle I_{s}(\phi w)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|\phi(x)|^{2}+|\phi(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{s}}w(x)w(y)dxdy\lesssim\int\left(\int\frac{w(y)dy}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)|\phi(x)|^{2}w(x)dx.

It suffices to show that the inner integral is
O_{\alpha,s}(1)
. As both
w(x)
and
w(y)
are nonzero only when
|x|,|y|\leq 2
, this follows from the next lemma with
\delta=4
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

If
0<s<\alpha
,
0<\delta\leq 100
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
, then

(2.14)

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}1_{|x-y|\leq\delta}\frac{w(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}dy\lesssim_{\alpha,s}\delta^{\alpha-s}.

Proof.

We write
|x-y|^{-s}=s\int_{|x-y|}^{\infty}r^{-s-1}dr
, so

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}1_{|x-y|\leq\delta}\frac{w(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}dy

\displaystyle=s\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\int_{|x-y|}^{\infty}r^{-s-1}dr\right)1_{|x-y|\leq\delta}w(y)dy

\displaystyle=s\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\int_{|y|}^{\infty}r^{-s-1}dr\right)1_{|y|\leq\delta}w(y+x)dy

\displaystyle=s\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\left(\int_{|y|}^{\infty}r^{-s-1}dr\right)w(y+x)dy.

We swap the order of the integrals so that the last line above is equal to

\displaystyle s\left(\int_{0}^{\delta}\left(\int_{-r}^{r}w(y+x)dy\right)r^{-s-1}dr+\int_{\delta}^{\infty}\left(\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}w(y+x)dy\right)r^{-s-1}dr\right).

By (
2.2
), the integral is bounded by the right side of (
2.14
).
∎

2.2.
Fourier decay estimates

We denote by
R_{s}(x)=|x|^{-s}
the Riesz kernel for
s\in(0,1)
. Then the energy integral
I_{s}(\phi w)
can be written as

\displaystyle\int(R_{s}*(\phi w))(x)\overline{\phi w(x)}dx.

By
[
17
, Theorem 3.6]
, as a tempered distribution

\displaystyle\widehat{R_{s}}(\xi)=\gamma(s)R_{1-s}(\xi/(2\pi)),

where

\displaystyle\gamma(s)=\pi^{s-1/2}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1-s}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{s}{2})}.

If
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
and
0<s<\alpha
, then by the Plancherel theorem,

\displaystyle g\cdot z=\frac{az+b}{cz+d},\qquad\text{ where }g=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\

We define the Hecke operators

\displaystyle T_{n}=T_{n}^{*},\qquad\text{ that is, }T_{n}\text{ is self-adjoint},

(3.1)

\displaystyle T_{m}T_{n}=T_{n}T_{m}=\sum_{d|(m,n)}T_{mn/d^{2}}.

We let

\displaystyle F(x_{1})\lesssim_{\alpha,k}\lambda^{-1/2}2^{-kn+n/2}\beta^{(k-1/2)(1-\epsilon_{0})}\beta^{-k+3/4}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(w)}\lesssim_{\epsilon_{0},\epsilon^{\prime},N}2^{-n}\lambda^{-N}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(w)},

which proves (
4.5
).
∎

By combining the above two lemmas and summing over
n
, we get:

Proposition 4.3
.

Suppose that
\lambda^{\epsilon^{\prime}}\leq\beta\leq\lambda
for some
\epsilon^{\prime}>0
and
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
. Then we have

\displaystyle I(\lambda,\Pi_{\beta}^{\perp}(\phi w),e)\lesssim_{\alpha,\epsilon^{\prime},\epsilon}\lambda^{1/2}\beta^{-(\alpha-1/2)+\epsilon}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(w)}^{2}.

4.2.
Bounding
I(\lambda,\Pi_{\beta}(\phi w),g)

We first give a uniform bound for
I(\lambda,\Pi_{\beta}(\phi w),g)
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Suppose that
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
and
\beta\leq\lambda
. We have
I(\lambda,\Pi_{\beta}(\phi w),g)\lesssim_{\alpha}\lambda^{1/2}\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(w)}
.

Proof.

By writing
\Pi_{\beta}(\phi w)
as

\int\eta_{\beta}(y)\phi w(x-y)dy,

and defining

\displaystyle J(\lambda;y_{1},y_{2})=\iint b(x_{1})b(x_{2})\overline{\phi w(x_{1}-y_{1})}\phi w(x_{2}-y_{2})k_{\lambda}(a(-x_{1})ga(x_{2}))dx_{1}dx_{2},

then we have

(4.7)

\displaystyle I(\lambda,\Pi_{\beta}(\phi w),g)=\iint\overline{\eta_{\beta}(y_{1})}\eta_{\beta}(y_{2})J(\lambda;y_{1},y_{2})dy_{1}dy_{2}.

By the mean inequality we obtain

\displaystyle J(\lambda;y_{1},y_{2})\lesssim

\displaystyle\iint b(x_{1})b(x_{2})|k_{\lambda}(a(-x_{1})ga(x_{2}))||\phi(x_{2}-y_{2})|^{2}w(x_{1}-y_{1})w(x_{2}-y_{2})dx_{1}dx_{2}

\displaystyle+\iint b(x_{1})b(x_{2})|k_{\lambda}(a(-x_{1})ga(x_{2}))||\phi(x_{1}-y_{1})|^{2}w(x_{1}-y_{1})w(x_{2}-y_{2})dx_{1}dx_{2}.

We claim that

\displaystyle\iint b(x_{1})b(x_{2})|k_{\lambda}(a(-x_{1})ga(x_{2}))||\phi(x_{2}-y_{2})|^{2}w(x_{1}-y_{1})w(x_{2}-y_{2})dx_{1}dx_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\left(\int b(x_{1})w(x_{1}-y_{1})|k_{\lambda}(a(-x_{1})ga(x_{2}))|dx_{1}\right)b(x_{2})w(x_{2}-y_{2})|\phi(x_{2}-y_{2})|^{2}dx_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\lambda^{1/2}\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(w)}

and the other term can be bounded similarly. The result will follow from the claim and Lemma
2.6
.
Given
g\in G
and
x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
, we let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
be such that
a(x_{0})\cdot i=e^{x_{0}}i\in\mathbb{H}
is the perpendicular projection of
ga(x_{2})\cdot i
onto the vertical geodesic
\mathbb{R}i\subset\mathbb{H}
. Therefore, in the hyperbolic triangle formed by
a(x_{1})\cdot i
,
a(x_{0})\cdot i
and
ga(x_{2})\cdot i
, the side length
|x_{1}-x_{0}|
is bounded by the hypotenuse, which is the distance between
a(x_{1})\cdot i
and
ga(x_{2})\cdot i
. By
[
16
, Lemma 2.8]
, we have

\displaystyle k_{\lambda}(a(-x_{1})ga(x_{2}))\lesssim\lambda(1+\lambda|x_{1}-x_{0}|)^{-1/2}\lesssim\lambda^{1/2}|x_{1}-x_{0}|^{-1/2}.

Therefore, by the above bound and Lemma
2.3
with
s=1/2
, we have

\displaystyle\int b(x_{1})w(x_{1}-y_{1})|k_{\lambda}(a(-x_{1})ga(x_{2}))|dx_{1}\lesssim\lambda^{1/2}\int b(x_{1})w(x_{1}-y_{1})|x_{1}-x_{0}|^{-1/2}dx_{1}\lesssim_{\alpha}\lambda^{1/2},

which completes the proof.
∎

4.2.1.
Rapid decay estimate of
I(\lambda,\Pi_{\beta}(\phi w),g)

We first recall Marshall’s estimate
[
15
, Proposition 3.7, (22)]
:

Lemma 4.5
.

Given
0<\epsilon^{\prime}<1/2
, suppose that
\lambda^{\epsilon^{\prime}}\leq\beta\leq\lambda^{1-\epsilon^{\prime}}
, and
\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
with
\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=1
and
\supp(\widehat{\phi})\subset\pm[\lambda-\beta,\lambda+\beta]
. If
\epsilon_{0}>0
and
d(g,A)\geq\lambda^{-1/2+\epsilon_{0}}\beta^{1/2}
, we have

\displaystyle I(\lambda,\phi,g)\lesssim_{\epsilon_{0},\epsilon^{\prime},N}\lambda^{-N}.

Remark
.

In
[
15
, Proposition 3.7]
, it is required that
\beta\leq\lambda^{2/3}
. However, this is not necessary, and
\lambda^{2/3}
could be replaced with
\lambda^{1-\epsilon^{\prime}}
as above (this is also mentioned in
[
15
, Remark p.466]
).

To modify Marshall’s estimate in terms of
\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(w)}
, we apply the Fourier decay estimate for
\phi w
as follows.

Proposition 4.6
.

Given
0<\epsilon^{\prime}<1/2
, suppose that
\lambda^{\epsilon^{\prime}}\leq\beta\leq\lambda^{1-\epsilon^{\prime}}
, and
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
. If
\epsilon_{0}>0
and
d(g,A)\geq\lambda^{-1/2+\epsilon_{0}}\beta^{1/2}
, we have

\displaystyle I(\lambda,\Pi_{\beta}(\phi w),g)\lesssim_{\epsilon_{0},\epsilon^{\prime},N}\lambda^{-N}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(w)}^{2}.

Proof.

By the Plancherel theorem and the support condition
\supp(\widehat{\eta_{\beta}})\subset\pm[\lambda-\beta,\lambda+\beta]
we have

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{\beta}(\phi w)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\widehat{\phi w}(\xi)|^{2}\left|\widehat{\eta_{\beta}}(\xi)\right|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\pm[\lambda-\beta,\lambda+\beta]}|\xi|^{1/2}|\widehat{\phi w}(\xi)|^{2}|\xi|^{-1/2}d\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{1/2}\int_{\pm[\lambda-\beta,\lambda+\beta]}|\widehat{\phi w}(\xi)|^{2}|\xi|^{-1/2}d\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{1/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widehat{\phi w}(\xi)|^{2}|\xi|^{-1/2}d\xi.

Since
\alpha>1/2
, applying Lemma
2.5
with
s=1/2
, we obtain
\|\Pi_{\beta}(\phi w)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\lesssim_{\alpha}\lambda^{1/2}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(w)}^{2}
. Combining this estimate, we apply Lemma
4.5
to
\Pi_{\beta}(\phi w)/\|\Pi_{\beta}(\phi w)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}
to obtain

I(\lambda,\Pi_{\beta}(\phi w),g)=\|\Pi_{\beta}(\phi w)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\,I(\lambda,\Pi_{\beta}(\phi w)/\|\Pi_{\beta}(\phi w)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})},g)\\
\lesssim_{\epsilon_{0},\epsilon^{\prime},N}\lambda^{-N}\|\Pi_{\beta}(\phi w)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\lesssim_{\alpha}\lambda^{-N+1/2}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(w)}^{2},

which completes the proof.
∎

5.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

From the estimates in the last section, we need to take a parameter
\beta>0
satisfying
\lambda^{\epsilon^{\prime}}\leq\beta\leq\lambda^{1-\epsilon^{\prime}}
for some
\epsilon^{\prime}>0
.

Proposition 5.1
.

For any
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
, we have
\langle\psi,b\Pi_{\beta}^{\perp}(\phi w)\rangle\lesssim_{\alpha,\epsilon^{\prime},\epsilon}\lambda^{1/4+\epsilon}\beta^{-\frac{\alpha-1/2}{2}}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(w)}
.

Proof.

We pass to a finite-index sublattice
\Gamma^{\prime}\subset\Gamma
such that
\Gamma^{\prime}\backslash\mathbb{H}
has sufficiently large injectivity radius and apply Proposition
3.1
with
\mathcal{T}
the identity operator. Then only
\gamma=e
contributes to the geometric side, and we obtain the result from Proposition
4.3
.
∎

To prove a nontrivial improvement for
\langle\psi,b\Pi_{\beta}(\phi w)\rangle
, we use the amplification method and need an input on the estimation of Hecke returns, which counts how many times the Hecke operators map the geodesic
g_{0}A
close to itself. We define

\displaystyle M(g,n,\kappa)=\#\{\eta\in R(n)\mid d(g^{-1}\eta g,e)\leq 1,d(g^{-1}\eta g,A)\leq\kappa\}.

The following lemma is proved by Marshall
[
15
, Lemma 3.3]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

If
\kappa\leq 1
, we have the bound

M(g,n,\kappa)\lesssim_{\epsilon}(n/\kappa)^{\epsilon}(n\sqrt{\kappa}+1)

uniformly in
g
.

Now we recall the construction of an amplifier, which is equivalent to the one used in
[
14
]
and
[
15
]
. Let
N\geq 1
be a large positive integer to be chosen later. If
1<p\leq\sqrt{N}
is a prime so that
(p,q)=1
, (
3.1
) implies the following relation for the Hecke eigenvalues:

\displaystyle\lambda(p)^{2}-\lambda(p^{2})=1.

For such a prime
p
, the above relation implies that if
|\lambda(p)|<1/2
then
|\lambda(p^{2})|>3/4
. We set

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\alpha_{p}=\frac{\lambda(p)}{|\lambda(p)|},\;\alpha_{p^{2}}=0,\qquad\text{ if }|\lambda(p)|\geq 1/2,\\
\alpha_{p}=0,\;\alpha_{p^{2}}=\frac{\lambda(p^{2})}{|\lambda(p^{2})|},\qquad\text{ if }|\lambda(p)|<1/2.\end{cases}

We set
\alpha_{n}=0
for all the other
n
. It may be seen that

(5.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq N}|\alpha_{n}|=\sum_{n\leq N}|\alpha_{n}|^{2}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1<p\leq\sqrt{N}\\
(p,q)=1\end{subarray}}1\lesssim N^{1/2}.

Let
\mathcal{T}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\alpha_{n}T_{n}
be our amplifier. Then we have
\mathcal{T}\psi=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\alpha_{n}\lambda(n)\right)\psi
and the eigenvalue of the amplifier satisfies

(5.2)

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\alpha_{n}\lambda(n)\right|\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1<p\leq\sqrt{N}\\
(p,q)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{2}\gtrsim_{\epsilon}N^{1/2-\epsilon}.

Proposition 5.3
.

Given
0<\epsilon^{\prime}<1/2
, supppose that
\lambda^{\epsilon^{\prime}}\leq\beta\leq\lambda^{1-\epsilon^{\prime}}
and
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
. Then we have
\langle\psi,b\Pi_{\beta}(\phi w)\rangle\lesssim_{\alpha,\epsilon^{\prime},\epsilon}\lambda^{5/24+\epsilon}\beta^{1/24}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(w)}
.

Proof.

The proof is essentially the same as the proof for
[
15
, Lemma 3.9]
. We include the proof here for completeness.

By Proposition
3.1
, we have

(5.3)

\displaystyle|\langle\mathcal{T}\psi,b\Pi_{\beta}(\phi w)\rangle|^{2}\leq\sum_{m,n\leq N}|\alpha_{m}\alpha_{n}|\sum_{d|(m,n)}\frac{d}{\sqrt{mn}}\sum_{\gamma\in R(mn/d^{2})}|I(\lambda,\Pi_{\beta}(\phi w),g_{0}^{-1}\gamma g_{0})|.

Choose
\epsilon_{0}>0
. Proposition
4.6
implies that we only need to consider the terms in (
5.3
) with
d(g_{0}^{-1}\gamma g_{0},e)\leq 1
and
d(g_{0}^{-1}\gamma g_{0},A)\leq\lambda^{-1/2+\epsilon_{0}}\beta^{1/2}
. Lemma
5.2
gives

M(g_{0},n,\lambda^{-1/2+\epsilon_{0}}\beta^{1/2})\lesssim_{\epsilon}n^{\epsilon}\lambda^{\epsilon_{0}+\epsilon}(n\lambda^{-1/4}\beta^{1/4}+1),

and so by the uniform estimate (
4.4
) we have

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n\leq N}|\alpha_{m}\alpha_{n}|\sum_{d|(m,n)}\frac{d}{\sqrt{mn}}\sum_{\gamma\in R(mn/d^{2})}|I(\lambda,\Pi_{\beta}(\phi w),g_{0}^{-1}\gamma g_{0})|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{m,n\leq N}|\alpha_{m}\alpha_{n}|\sum_{d|(m,n)}\frac{d}{\sqrt{mn}}\lambda^{1/2}M(g_{0},\frac{mn}{d^{2}},\lambda^{-1/2+\epsilon_{0}}\beta^{1/2})+O_{\epsilon_{0},\delta,A}(\lambda^{-A})\right)\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(w)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

{}_{\epsilon}\left(N^{\epsilon}\lambda^{\epsilon_{0}+\epsilon}\sum_{m,n\leq N}|\alpha_{m}\alpha_{n}|\sum_{d|(m,n)}\left(\frac{\sqrt{mn}}{d}\lambda^{1/4}\beta^{1/4}+\frac{d}{\sqrt{mn}}\lambda^{1/2}\right)+O_{\epsilon_{0},\delta,A}(\lambda^{-A})\right)\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(w)}^{2}.

As in
[
14
, p.310]
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n\leq N}\sum_{d|(m,n)}|\alpha_{m}\alpha_{n}|\frac{\sqrt{mn}}{d}\lesssim_{\epsilon}N^{1+\epsilon}\left(\sum_{n\leq N}|\alpha_{n}|\right)^{2}

and

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n\leq N}\sum_{d|(m,n)}|\alpha_{m}\alpha_{n}|\frac{d}{\sqrt{mn}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\leq N\\
(m,n)=1\end{subarray}}\sum_{ml,nl\leq N}\sum_{d|l}|\alpha_{ml}\alpha_{nl}|\frac{d}{l\sqrt{mn}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\epsilon}N^{\epsilon}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\leq N\end{subarray}}\sum_{ml,nl\leq N}\left(\frac{|\alpha_{ml}|^{2}}{n}+\frac{|\alpha_{nl}|^{2}}{m}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\epsilon}N^{\epsilon}\sum_{n\leq N}|\alpha_{n}|^{2}.

Hence from (
5.3
) we have

\displaystyle|\langle\mathcal{T}\psi,b\Pi_{\beta}(\phi w)\rangle|^{2}\lesssim

{}_{\epsilon}\left(N^{\epsilon}\lambda^{\epsilon_{0}+\epsilon}\left(N\lambda^{1/4}\beta^{1/4}\left(\sum_{n\leq N}|\alpha_{n}|\right)^{2}+\lambda^{1/2}\sum_{n\leq N}|\alpha_{n}|^{2}\right)+O_{\epsilon_{0},\delta,A}(\lambda^{-A})\right)\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(w)}^{2}.

Combining this with (
5.1
) and (
5.2
) and choosing
\epsilon_{0}
small gives

\displaystyle N^{1-\epsilon}|\langle\psi,b\Pi_{\beta}(\phi w)\rangle|^{2}\lesssim

{}_{\epsilon}\left(N^{\epsilon}\lambda^{\epsilon}\left(N^{2}\lambda^{1/4}\beta^{1/4}+N^{1/2}\lambda^{1/2}\right)+O_{\delta,A}(\lambda^{-A})\right)\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(w)}^{2},

which completes the proof by choosing
N=\lambda^{1/6}\beta^{-1/6}
.
∎

Given small
\epsilon^{\prime}>0
, by combining Propositions
5.1
and Proposition
5.3
, for any
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
and
\beta
satisfying

\displaystyle\langle\psi,b\phi\rangle_{w}=\langle\psi,b\Pi_{\beta}^{\perp}(\phi w)\rangle+\langle\psi,b\Pi_{\beta}(\phi w)\rangle\lesssim_{\alpha,\epsilon^{\prime},\epsilon}\left(\lambda^{1/4+\epsilon}\beta^{-\frac{\alpha-1/2}{2}}+\lambda^{5/24+\epsilon}\beta^{1/24}\right)\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(w)}.

Therefore, by choosing

, we obtain

\displaystyle\|b\psi|_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(w)}\lesssim_{\alpha,\epsilon^{\prime},\epsilon}\lambda^{1/4+\epsilon}\beta^{-\frac{\alpha-1/2}{2}}+\lambda^{5/24+\epsilon}\beta^{1/24}.

Theorem
1.2
follows from choosing

6.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

In this section, we shall give the proof of Theorem
1.3
following ideas in the earlier work of Bourgain
[

]
.

By Lemma
2.1
, it suffices to estimate

\left(\int_{|s|\leq 2}|e_{\lambda}(\ell(s)|^{2}w(s)ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

By using (
2.5
), we are reduced to bound

\lambda^{1/2}\left(\int_{|s|\leq 2}\left|\int_{M}e^{i\lambda d_{g}(\ell(s),y)}a(\ell(s),y,\lambda)e_{\lambda}(y)dy\right|^{2}w(s)ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

By duality, let
\eta
be a smooth function such that
\|\eta\|_{L^{2}(w)}=1
, we need to estimate

(6.1)

\int\int_{M}e^{i\lambda d_{g}(\ell(s),y)}a(\ell(s),y,\lambda)e_{\lambda}(y)\eta(s)w(s)dyds

We can choose Fermi coordinates geodesic
\ell
such that
\ell(s)=(s,0)
. Now, let us decompose the
y

y
to the geodesic
\ell
. Fix a
Littlewood-Paley bump function satisfying

(6.2)

\beta\in C^{\infty}_{c}((1/2,2)),\,\,\,\beta(\tau)=1\,\,\,\text{for }\,\tau\,\,\text{near }\,\,1,\,\,\text{and }\,\,\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\beta(2^{-j}\tau)=1,\,\,\tau>0.

Denote
\beta_{0}(\tau)=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{0}\beta(|\tau|)
and
\beta_{k}(\tau)=\beta(2^{-k}|\tau|)
. Note that by (
2.4
) we may assume
y=(y_{1},y_{2})
variable in (

) is supported in the set
|y|\leq C
for some constant
C
which may depend on the metric. Thus, it suffices to estimate

(6.3)

\int\int_{M}e^{i\lambda d_{g}(\ell(s),y)}a(\ell(s),y,\lambda)e_{\lambda}(y)\beta_{k}(y_{2})\eta(s)w(s)dyds

for
\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq 2^{k}\leq C
,
as well as

(6.4)

\int\int_{M}e^{i\lambda d_{g}(\ell(s),y)}a(\ell(s),y,\lambda)e_{\lambda}(y)\beta_{0}(\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}y_{2})\eta(s)w(s)dyds.

Write

(6.5)

\Omega_{k}=\{y=(y_{1},y_{2})\mid|y_{1}|\leq C,\,\,2^{k-1}<|y_{2}|<2^{k+1}\}.

We have for each fixed

\int\int_{\Omega_{k}}e^{i\lambda d_{g}(\gamma(x_{1}),y)}a(\ell(s),y,\lambda)e_{\lambda}(y)\beta_{k}(y_{2})\eta(s)w(s)dyds\\
\leq\left\|\int e^{i\lambda d_{g}(\ell(s),y)}a(\ell(s),y,\lambda)\beta_{k}(y_{2})\eta(s)w(s)ds\right\|_{L^{2}}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{k})}

Note that

(6.6)

\displaystyle\left\|\int e^{i\lambda d_{g}(\ell(s),y)}a(\ell(s),y,\lambda)\beta_{k}(y_{2})\eta(s)w(s)ds\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\iint\left|\int e^{i\lambda(d_{g}(\ell(s),y)-d_{g}(\ell(s^{\prime}),y))}a(\ell(s),y,\lambda)\overline{a(\ell(s^{\prime}),y,\lambda)}\beta^{2}_{k}(y_{2})dy\right|

\displaystyle\cdot|\eta(s)w(s)\eta(s^{\prime})w(s^{\prime})|dsds^{\prime}.

\ell
, one can Taylor expand the distance function and show that (see e.g., Lemma A.6 in

]

\partial_{y_{2}}(d_{g}(\ell(s),y)-d_{g}(\ell(s^{\prime}),y))\sim 2^{k}|s-s^{\prime}|\,\,\,\text{if}\,\,\,y\in\Omega_{k}

(6.10)

.

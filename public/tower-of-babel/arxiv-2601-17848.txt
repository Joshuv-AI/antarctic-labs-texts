Three dimensional black bounces in f ⁡ ( R ) f(R) gravity
Marcos V. de S. Silva, Manuel E. Rodrigues, C. F. S. Pereira

Abstract
We investigate the existence of black bounce solutions in 2 + 1 2+1 dimensions within the framework of f ⁡ ( R ) f(R) gravity. We analyze whether black bounce geometries originally obtained in general relativity can be consistently generalized to f ⁡ ( R ) f(R) theories and identify the matter sources capable of supporting such solutions. We also construct a new class of solutions by imposing a vanishing curvature scalar. In the matter sector, we consider models involving a coupling between a scalar field and nonlinear electrodynamics, while in the gravitational sector we analyze both the Starobinsky model and more general forms of f ⁡ ( R ) f(R) . We further examine the viability conditions of the f ⁡ ( R ) f(R) models that give rise to these spacetimes, including the behavior of the scalaron mass. Finally, we study the associated energy conditions, in order to assess the degree of exoticity of the matter content required to sustain these black bounce solutions and how the f ⁡ ( R ) f(R) theory modifies the energy conditions.

II
Equations of motions in 2+1 dimensions

We are interested in studying BBs in three dimensions within the framework of
f(R)
gravity. It is common, at least in GR, that these objects have as their field sources a nonlinear electrodynamic minimally coupled to a scalar field. Therefore, we will consider the theory described by the action:

S=\int\sqrt{|g|}d^{3}x[f(R)-2h(\phi)g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi+2V(\phi)+L(F)],

(1)

where
f(R)
is a function of the Ricci scalar
R=g^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu}
,
\phi
is the scalar field,
V(\phi)
is potential associated to the scalar field,
L(F)
is the NED Lagrangian,
F=F^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}
is the electromagnetic scalar with
F^{\mu\nu}
being the Maxwell-Faraday tensor, and
h(\phi)
is a function that determines where the scalar field is phantom (
h(\phi)<0
) or canonical (
h(\phi)>0
).

To obtain the equations of motion, we must vary the action (
1
) with respect to
A_{\mu}
,
\phi
and
g^{\mu\nu}
, and we get the following equations, respectively

\nabla_{\mu}(L_{F}F^{\mu\nu})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|g|}}\partial_{\mu}(\sqrt{|g|}L_{F}F^{\mu\nu})=0,

(2)

2h(\phi)\nabla_{\mu}\nabla^{\mu}\phi+\frac{dh(\phi)}{d\phi}\partial^{\mu}\phi\partial_{\mu}\phi=-\frac{dV(\phi)}{d\phi},

(3)

f_{R}R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}f(R)+(g_{\mu\nu}\Box-\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu})f_{R}=T[\phi]_{\mu\nu}+T[F]_{\mu\nu},

(4)

where
L_{F}=dL/dF
,
f_{R}=df(R)/dR
, and
T[\phi]_{\mu\nu}
and
T[F]_{\mu\nu}
are the stress-energy tensors of the scalar and electromagnetic fields, given by

T[F]_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}L(F)-2L_{F}F^{\alpha}_{\nu}F_{\mu\alpha},

(5)

T[\phi]_{\mu\nu}=2h(\phi)\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi-g_{\mu\nu}(h(\phi)\partial^{\alpha}\phi\partial_{\alpha}\phi-V(\phi)).

(6)

To begin solving the equations of motion, we must choose the form of our line element. We will choose the line element describing a static spacetime, written as:

ds^{2}=A(r)dt^{2}-\frac{1}{A(r)}dr^{2}-\Sigma^{2}(r)d\varphi^{2}.

(7)

where the functions
A(r)
and
\Sigma(r)
are, in principle, arbitrary functions of the radial coordinate.

It is possible to approach the problem in two different ways: we can either impose the source fields and the
f(R)
gravity model to find the components of the metric tensor, or we can impose the components of the metric tensor and determine which functions for the source fields and the gravitational theory are most suitable to describe such solutions. Since modified gravitational theories like
f(R)
involve fourth-order derivatives of the metric tensor components, it becomes more feasible to work with the second option.

Since we are working with spacetimes in
2+1
dimensions, it is more appropriate to consider that our object is electrically charged.
Thus, considering that our solution has an electric charge and the line element (
7
), the only non-zero component of the Maxwell-Faraday tensor is
F^{10}=-F^{01}
. From the modified Maxwell equations (
2
), we find

\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|g\right|}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left[\sqrt{\left|g\right|}L_{F}F^{10}\right]=0,\ \quad\Rightarrow\quad\Sigma L_{F}F^{10}=q=constant,

(8)

where
q
is the electric charge. The electromagnetic scalar is written as

F(r)=-\frac{2q^{2}}{\Sigma^{2}L_{F}^{2}}.

(9)

With these considerations, the remaining equations of motion are given by:

\displaystyle-2h(\phi)A^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}-A\phi^{\prime}(r)h^{\prime}(\phi)-\frac{2Ah(\phi)\Sigma^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}}{\Sigma}-2Ah(\phi)\phi^{\prime\prime}=-\frac{V^{\prime}}{\phi^{\prime}},

(10)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}f_{R}A^{\prime\prime}-\frac{1}{2}A^{\prime}f_{R}^{\prime}+\frac{f_{R}A^{\prime}\Sigma^{\prime}}{2\Sigma}-Af_{R}^{\prime\prime}-\frac{Af_{R}^{\prime}\Sigma^{\prime}}{\Sigma}-Ah(\phi)\phi^{\prime 2}-\frac{f}{2}=\frac{L}{2}+\frac{2q^{2}}{L_{F}\Sigma^{2}}+V,

(11)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}f_{R}A^{\prime\prime}-\frac{1}{2}A^{\prime}f_{R}^{\prime}+\frac{f_{R}A^{\prime}\Sigma^{\prime}}{2\Sigma}-\frac{Af_{R}^{\prime}\Sigma^{\prime}}{\Sigma}+\frac{Af_{R}\Sigma^{\prime\prime}}{\Sigma}+Ah(\phi)\phi^{\prime 2}-\frac{f}{2}=\frac{L}{2}+\frac{2q^{2}}{L_{F}\Sigma^{2}}+V,

(12)

\displaystyle-A^{\prime}f_{R}^{\prime}+\frac{f_{R}A^{\prime}\Sigma^{\prime}}{\Sigma}-Af_{R}^{\prime\prime}+\frac{Af_{R}\Sigma^{\prime\prime}}{\Sigma}-Ah(\phi)\phi^{\prime 2}-\frac{f}{2}=\frac{L}{2}+V.

(13)

where
``\,^{\prime}\,"
represents the derivative with respect to the radial coordinate.

From equation (
12
) and (
13
), we find the form of
L(r)
and
L_{F}(r)
, which are given by

L(r)=-2A^{\prime}f_{R}^{\prime}+\frac{2f_{R}A^{\prime}\Sigma^{\prime}}{\Sigma}-2Af_{R}^{\prime\prime}+\frac{2Af_{R}\Sigma^{\prime\prime}}{\Sigma}-2Ah(\phi)\phi^{\prime 2}-f-2V,

(14)

L_{F}(r)=-\frac{4q^{2}}{\Sigma\left(-f_{R}\Sigma A^{\prime\prime}-\Sigma A^{\prime}f_{R}^{\prime}+f_{R}A^{\prime}\Sigma^{\prime}-2A\Sigma f_{R}^{\prime\prime}+2Af_{R}^{\prime}\Sigma^{\prime}-4A\Sigma h(\phi)\phi^{\prime 2}\right)}.

(15)

Using equations (
14
) and (
15
), the equation (
11
) becomes

h(\phi)\phi^{\prime 2}=-\frac{\Sigma f_{R}^{\prime\prime}+f_{R}\Sigma^{\prime\prime}}{2\Sigma}.

(16)

Through this relation, we can infer the behavior of
h(\phi)\phi^{\prime 2}
. As is customary in this type of situation, we will impose that our scalar field behaves as
\phi=\arctan(r/a)
, where
a
is a constant that will be related with the regularization of the solutions, and we will determine the behavior of
h(\phi)
, which is written as

h(\phi)=-\frac{\left(a^{2}+r^{2}\right)^{2}\left(\Sigma f_{R}^{\prime\prime}+f_{R}\Sigma^{\prime\prime}\right)}{2a^{2}\Sigma}.

(17)

With this relation, it is possible to deduce the behavior of
h(\phi)
once we know the
f(R)
theory we are dealing with and the
g_{22}
coefficient of the metric.

An additional equation that we must take into account is the consistency relation, given by:

L_{F}\frac{dF}{dr}-\frac{dL}{dr}=0.

(18)

This equation acts as a constraint that the electromagnetic functions must satisfy.

Thus, we have established all the necessary equations to solve our problem. In the next section, we will propose different approaches to address this problem.

III
BBs in 2+1 dimensions

As mentioned earlier, in general it is difficult to solve the field equations of
f(R)
gravity to obtain a metric, since these equations involve fourth-order derivatives of the metric. However, we can exploit the functional freedom in the model to determine which sources would generate given metrics for specific
f(R)
models.

For most of the approaches we will consider, it is quite feasible to use the regularized version of the BTZ metric, which is described by the line element (
7
) with

A(r)=-M+\frac{r^{2}+a^{2}}{l^{2}},\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\Sigma^{2}(r)=r^{2}+a^{2},

(19)

where
l
is related with the cosmological constant by
\Lambda=-1/l^{2}
.
In the context of GR, this spacetime has been extensively studied, and we will adopt it as our model to carry out the generalizations in
f(R)
gravity.

The curvature scalar related with this solution is written as

R=\frac{4a^{4}+10a^{2}r^{2}+6r^{4}}{l^{2}\left(a^{2}+r^{2}\right)^{2}}-\frac{2a^{2}M}{\left(a^{2}+r^{2}\right)^{2}}.

(20)

This expression will be important later.

IV
Viability condition and scalaron mass

For an
f(R)
model to be phenomenologically viable, it must satisfy a set of standard consistency requirements. The conditions are

f_{R}(R)\equiv df(R)/dR>0
, ensuring a positive effective gravitational coupling (and excluding ghost-like tensor modes), and

f_{RR}(R)\equiv d^{2}f(R)/dR^{2}>0
, which avoids the Dolgov–Kawasaki instability. Further insight into the stability of a given background is provided by the scalaron (the extra scalar degree of freedom in
f(R)
gravity), whose effective mass is
[
70
]

m_{\psi}^{2}=\frac{1}{3}\left[\frac{1}{f_{RR}(R)}-\frac{R}{f_{R}(R)}\right].

(64)

The sign of
m_{\psi}^{2}
controls the local curvature of the scalaron effective potential around the background:
m_{\psi}^{2}>0
corresponds to a local minimum and signals stability against scalar perturbations, whereas
m_{\psi}^{2}<0
indicates tachyonic behavior.

IV.1
Model
f_{R}=1+a_{2}r^{2}

For case
f_{R}=1+a_{2}r^{2}
, the first viability condition,
f_{R}>0
, is trivially satisfied provided that
a_{2}>0
.
To verify the second condition,
f_{RR}>0
, we first need to obtain
f_{RR}
, which is given by

f_{RR}=\frac{a_{2}l^{2}\left(a^{2}+r^{2}\right)^{3}}{2a^{2}\left(a^{2}+2l^{2}M+r^{2}\right)}.

(65)

For
M>0
and
a_{2}>0
, condition
f_{RR}>0
is always satisfied.

In relation to the mass of the scalaron, we find

m_{\psi}^{2}=\frac{2}{3a_{2}l^{2}\,(a^{2}+r^{2})^{3}\,(1+a_{2}r^{2})}\left[Ma^{2}l^{2}\Big(2+a_{2}(a^{2}+3r^{2})\Big)+(a^{2}+r^{2})\Big(a^{2}-a_{2}\,(2a^{4}+4a^{2}r^{2}+3r^{4})\Big)\right].

(66)

Expanding to
r\to\infty
, we find that

m_{\psi}^{2}\approx-\frac{2}{r^{2}a_{2}l^{2}}+\frac{4a^{2}a_{2}+6}{3a_{2}^{2}l^{2}r^{4}}+O\left(\frac{1}{r^{6}}\right).

(67)

If
a_{2}>0
(and
l^{2}>0
), then
m_{\psi}^{2}
inevitably becomes negative for sufficiently large
r
. Therefore, there is no choice of parameters
(M,a,l)
that makes this expression positive for all spacetime.

IV.2
Model
f_{R}=1+a_{\Sigma}\Sigma

For the case
f_{R}=1+a_{\Sigma}r^{2}
, the first viability condition,
f_{R}>0
, is trivially satisfied provided that
a_{\Sigma}>0
.

We find that the function
f_{RR}
is given by

f_{RR}=\frac{a_{\Sigma}l^{2}\left(a^{2}+r^{2}\right)^{5/2}}{4a^{2}\left(a^{2}+2l^{2}M+r^{2}\right)}.

(68)

This implies that the condition
f_{RR}>0
is satisfied when
M>0
and
a_{\Sigma}>0
.

Checking now the scalaron mass, we obtain that

m_{\psi}^{2}=\frac{1}{3l^{2}}\left[\frac{4a^{2}\left(a^{2}+r^{2}+2l^{2}M\right)}{a_{\Sigma}\left(a^{2}+r^{2}\right)^{5/2}}-\frac{2\left(2a^{4}+a^{2}\left(5r^{2}-l^{2}M\right)+3r^{4}\right)}{\left(a^{2}+r^{2}\right)^{2}\left(a_{\Sigma}\sqrt{a^{2}+r^{2}}+1\right)}\right].

(69)

Expanding to
r\to\infty
, we find that

m_{\psi}^{2}\approx-\frac{2}{r\left(a_{\Sigma}l^{2}\right)}+\frac{2}{a_{\Sigma}^{2}l^{2}r^{2}}+O\left(\frac{1}{r^{3}}\right).

(70)

Therefore, with
a_{\Sigma}>0
and
l^{2}>0
, the expression becomes negative for sufficiently large
r
and cannot remain positive throughout the entire spacetime.

IV.3
Model
f(R)=R+a_{R}R^{2}

For the Starobinsky model,

f(R)=R+a_{R}R^{2},

(71)

the viability conditions take a particularly simple form. One finds

f_{R}(R)=\frac{df}{dR}=1+2a_{R}R,\qquad f_{RR}(R)=\frac{d^{2}f}{dR^{2}}=2a_{R}.

(72)

Hence, the requirements
f_{R}>0
and
f_{RR}>0
imply

1+2a_{R}R>0\;\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\;R>-\frac{1}{2a_{R}}.

(73)

Moreover, substituting these expressions into Eq. (
64

(74)

Therefore, in the Starobinsky case one has
m_{\psi}^{2}>0
whenever
a_{R}>0
and
f_{R}(R)>0
, so the absence of tachyonic modes is ensured in the same domain where the standard conditions
f_{R}>0
and
f_{RR}>0
are satisfied.

IV.4
Model

We can use the results from the previous subsection to verify the case where
R=0

and

(i.e., no tachyonic scalaron at

V
Energy Conditions

A central requirement for assessing the physical viability of
f(R)
gravity models is the explicit verification of the energy conditions. In these theories, the field equations can be reinterpreted through an effective stress-energy tensor, necessitating a detailed analysis of the resulting fluid components. Such an investigation is relevant for BB geometries, which are characterized by their ability to bridge the transition between regular BHs and WHs. Since these configurations typically remove the singularities found in GR at the cost of violating classical energy conditions
[
73
,
48
,
21
]
, it is essential to determine if these violations persist when the gravitational action is modified.

Consequently, we analyze the null (NEC), weak (WEC), strong (SEC), and dominant (DEC) energy conditions by treating the source as an effective anisotropic fluid. With the energy density
\rho
, radial pressure
p_{r}
, and tangential pressure
p_{t}
as the components of the stress-energy tensor, the conditions are defined as
[
80
]
:

\displaystyle NEC_{1,2}\Longleftrightarrow\rho+p_{r,t}\geq 0,

(76)

\displaystyle WEC_{1,2}\Longleftrightarrow\rho\geq 0\ \ \text{and}\ \ \rho+p_{r,t}\geq 0,

(77)

\displaystyle SEC_{1,2}\Longleftrightarrow\rho+p_{r,t}\geq 0,\qquad SEC_{3}\Longleftrightarrow\rho+p_{r}+p_{t}\geq 0,

(78)

\displaystyle DEC_{1,2}\Longleftrightarrow\rho\geq 0\ \ \text{and}\ \ \rho-|p_{r,t}|\geq 0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ (\rho\pm p_{r,t}\geq 0),

(79)

\displaystyle WEC_{3}=DEC_{3}\Longleftrightarrow\rho\geq 0.

(80)

The subscripts
1
and
2
denote relations involving the radial and tangential components, while
3
corresponds to the energy density alone or the specific trace-related combinations.

The functions
\{\rho,p_{r},p_{t}\}
are derived from the mixed components of the effective stress-energy tensor. In the exterior regions or where
A(r)>0
, we adopt the standard identification

T^{\mu}_{\ \nu}=\mathrm{diag}\!\left[\rho,\,-p_{r},\,-p_{t},\,-p_{t}\right].

(81)

However, in regions where
A(r)<0
, the causal roles of the time and radial coordinates are interchanged. To maintain a consistent physical interpretation in these regions, the tensor components are defined as

T^{\mu}_{\ \nu}=\mathrm{diag}\!\left[-p_{r},\,\rho,\,-p_{t},\,-p_{t}\right].

(82)

We now examine how each energy condition behaves for the models considered in this work.

VI
Conclusions and discussion

In this work, we focused on investigating BB solutions in
2+1
dimensions within the framework of
f(R)
gravity. We examined which types of matter sources are capable of sustaining such exotic geometries in modified gravity theories. One of our main approaches was to determine which
f(R)
models and source fields can generate a regularized BTZ solution. A second approach was to explore the possibility of constructing a solution with vanishing curvature scalar for a given
f(R)
model.

Our results show that it is indeed possible to obtain a regularized BTZ solution in
f(R)
gravity if one considers a NED source coupled to a partially phantom scalar field. This is a noteworthy result when compared to GR, where the regularized BTZ solution necessarily requires a scalar field that is always phantom.

In the first case, we adopted the function
f_{R}=1+a_{n}r^{n}
, which represents a generalization inspired by the forms that naturally arise in regular BH solutions in
f(R)
and
f(G)
theories, where typically
f_{R}=1+a_{1}r
. Since the resulting functions become highly involved in the general case, we focused on the illustrative example
n=2
. We found that the functions associated with the source fields are more complicated than in GR, with the corrections induced by
f(R)
gravity being controlled by the constant
a_{2}
. A notable feature of this example is the symmetry under
r\to-r
, which implies that the source-field functions are also symmetric. We showed that a canonical scalar field can be obtained if
a_{2}
takes negative values and satisfies
-a^{2}a_{2}>1/2
. However, the
f(R)
model satisfies the viability conditions
f_{R}>0
and
f_{RR}>0
throughout the spacetime only if
a_{2}>0
. Consequently, the viability requirements enforce a scalar field that is partially phantom. Moreover, the condition
a_{2}>0
implies that, in some regions, at least asymptotically, the scalaron mass becomes negative. Concerning the energy conditions, we found that negative values of
a_{2}
allow the inequality associated with
\mathrm{NEC}_{1}
, which is always satisfied in GR, to be fulfilled. However, this choice simultaneously implies that other inequalities are violated even more strongly than in the GR case. Thus, in a global sense, the energy conditions remain violated, even though some of the inequalities that were violated in GR can now be at least partially satisfied.

Another proposal for
f_{R}
arises from applying the Simpson–Visser regularization procedure directly to the function
f_{R}
that appears in regular BH models. In fact, this construction can be made even more general by considering
f_{R}=1+a_{\Sigma}\Sigma^{n}
. As a simple illustrative example, we analyzed the case
n=1
and derived the corresponding source field functions. In general, the functions obtained for
f_{R}=1+a_{\Sigma}\Sigma
are simpler than those arising in the case
f_{R}=1+a_{2}r^{2}
. The associated scalar field can be everywhere canonical if
a_{\Sigma}
takes negative values and satisfies the condition
-a_{\Sigma}a>1/2

In this way, we have shown that it is indeed possible to obtain BB solutions in
2+1
dimensions within
f(R)
gravity. These solutions can be described by a combination of NED and a scalar field. Depending on the specific solution and on the choice of parameters, the scalar field can be always phantom, partially phantom, or canonical. However, scalar fields that are everywhere canonical typically violate the viability conditions of the
f(R)
theory. In order to satisfy the viability requirements, one is therefore led to impose that the scalar field be phantom. The source-field models are more involved and more nonlinear than in the general-relativistic case. From the perspective of the energy conditions, it is only possible to guarantee that they are partially satisfied in some cases, while in others they are always violated in at least some region of the spacetime.

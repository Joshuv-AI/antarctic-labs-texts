Toric reduction of singularities for Newton non-degenerate p p -forms
Bilal Balo

Abstract
We study a class of holomorphic p p -forms satisfying non-degeneracy conditions expressed through their Newton polyhedron. We show that a regular refinement of their dual fan defines a toric reduction of singularities to a log-smooth p p -form.

Introduction

In
[
Sei68
]
, Seidenberg resolved the singularities of plane formal vector fields. This was one of the first resolution theorems in the theory of singular foliations. A simplified proof was published later by Arno van den Essen in
[
VdE79
]
. The modern formulation of this theorem is as follows: let
\mathcal{F}
be a foliation defined on the germ
(\mathbf{C}^{2},0)
by a
1
-form
\omega=a(x,y)dx+b(x,y)dy
. We suppose that
\mathcal{F}
is singular at the origin, meaning that
a(0,0)=b(0,0)=0
. Consider the Jacobian matrix:

\mathcal{J}_{0}(\omega,x,y)=\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{\partial b}{\partial x}(0)&-\frac{\partial a}{\partial x}(0)\\
\frac{\partial b}{\partial y}(0)&-\frac{\partial a}{\partial y}(0)\end{matrix}\right)

with eigenvalues
\lambda
and
\mu
. The origin is a
reduced
singularity if one of the following conditions is satisfied :

(a)

\lambda\neq 0
and
\mu=0
or viceversa;

(b)

\lambda\neq 0
,
\mu\neq 0
and
\frac{\lambda}{\mu}\notin\mathbf{Q}_{>0}
.

This definition is independent from the chosen coordinates
(x,y)
and from the generator
\omega

of the foliation
\mathcal{F}
. Seidenberg’s theorem guarantees the existence of a finite sequence of blowing-ups of points whose composition
\pi:(V,E)\longrightarrow(\mathbf{C}^{2},0)
is such that the strict transform of
\mathcal{F}
by
\pi
has at most reduced singularities in the exceptional divisor
E
.

Reduced singularities have been generalized and studied in higher dimensional spaces for codimension 1 foliations in the works of Felipe Cano (see
[
Can98
]
for an introduction). They are called
simple
singularities and the related resolution theorem for codimension 1 foliations defined on threefolds was proven by Cano in 2004
[
Can04
]
.

The codimension 2 case for threefolds corresponds to foliations defined by vector fields and was first treated by Daniel Panazzolo in the real analytic setting in
[
Pan06
]
, then by Panazzolo and McQuillan in the complex framework
[
MP13
]
. Higher dimensional cases are still open, most of the available literature focuses on codimension 1 foliations. These theorems have applications to dynamical systems, birational geometry and Mori theory (see
[
Bru15
]
and
[
CS21
]
).

The Newton non-degeneracy condition (NND), first introduced in the context of function singularity theory (see
[
BKK76
]
,
[
Var76
]
and
[
Var77
]
), ties successfully together toric geometry, Newton polyhedra and the study of singularities: any NND affine hypersurface admits a resolution of singularities by one toric morphism. Generalizations to higher codimensions of this result are established in
[
AGMS13
]
,
[
Tev07
]
and
[
AGMM24
]
.

In the same spirit, we are concerned by the reduction of singularities of
p
-forms by the use of only one toric morphism. A version of Newton non-degeneracy has been formulated for plane vector fields by Brunella and Miari in
[
BM90
]
to study topological classification problems. In
[
CC99
]
, without assuming any Newton non-degeneracy condition, the authors describe the class of singular plane foliations which can be reduced to case
(b)
in Seidenberg’s theorem by a single toric morphism defined via their Newton polyhedron. In
[
Est05
]
, Esterov defines the Newton polyhedron of a germ of
1
-form in
(\mathbf{C}^{n},0)
and introduces
\mathbf{C}
-genericity, a variant of the NND condition, together with toric resolution techniques in order to establish a formula for the index of a
1
-form on a complete intersection singularity. In dimension three, topological classification results for vector fields were proven in
[
AG15
]
, assuming a different NND condition.

More recently, Molina-Samper introduced a definition of NND codimension 1 foliations using Newton polyhedra systems (see
[
MS19
]
,
[
MS20
]
and
[
MS22
]
) and gave a characterization of such foliations in terms of the existence of a logarithmic reduction of singularities of combinatorial nature: in particular, it is a sequence of blowing-ups with smooth centers of the ambient space.

We would want to continue to explore the previous point of view for NND foliations, initiated by Brunella and Miari in
[
BM90
]
, where we define explicitly a toric reduction of singularities through the dual fan associated to a Newton polyhedron. We want to have sufficient conditions for the existence of a proper birational toric morphism making a form log-smooth: this is where the NND property comes into play in
3.3
below.

This article is motivated by works on embedded resolution of singularities with one toric morphism:
[
Tei03
]
,
[
Tei14
]
,
[
Tei23
]
,
[
Mou23
]
. While the general case of this conjecture remains open, it has been resolved for plane curve singularities (see
[
Mou17
]
,
[
dFGPM23
]
). We aim to formulate and study similar results, but in the context of foliations instead of algebraic varieties. A step in this direction is to find sufficient conditions for the existence of a proper birational toric morphism making a form log-smooth: this is where the NND property comes into play in
3.3
.

Theorem
.

Let
\eta
be a non identically zero holomorphic
p
-form defined on
\mathbf{C}^{n}
. We denote by
\Gamma
its Newton polyhedron and by
\Sigma
a regular refinement of the dual fan of
\eta
. Let
\pi:X_{\Sigma}\longrightarrow\mathbf{C}^{n}
be the associated proper birational toric morphism. Consider an affine chart
\mathcal{U}_{\sigma}
of
X_{\Sigma}
defined by a maximal cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
with minimal generators
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
. Set
t_{k}=\min_{I\in\Gamma}\langle v_{k},I\rangle
, where
k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. Denote by
Y=(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})
coordinates on
\mathcal{U}_{\sigma}
. The equation
y_{1}^{t_{1}}\dots y_{n}^{t_{n}}=0
defines a normal crossings divisor
E
on
X_{\Sigma}
.

1)

The pull-back
\pi^{*}\eta
is adapted to the divisor
E
.

2)

Suppose that
\eta
is Newton non-degenerate, then
\pi^{*}\eta
is log-smooth with respect to
E
.

In this theorem, we don’t require any integrability condition on
\eta
.

In the first part of this paper, we introduce a generalization of the notion of Newton polyhedron of a
1
-form to a
p
-form and define associated Newton non-degeneracy conditions. The second part gathers the results from toric geometry which are needed to formulate and prove our main result: statement
2)
in
3.3
affirming that any NND
p
-form pulls back to a log-smooth form via a single toric morphism. The notion of log-smoothness and the proof of the theorem are detailed in the last part.

Newton non-degeneracy condition

In this paper we adopt the convention that the set
\mathbf{N}
contains
0
:

\mathbf{N}=\{0,1,2,3,\ldots\}
. Let
n
and
p
be fixed integers such that
n\geq 2
and
1\leq p<n
.

1.1. Newton polyhedron of a
p
-form.

We denote by
X=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
the standard coordinate system on
\mathbf{C}^{n}
. We identify
n
-tuples in
\mathbf{C}^{n}
with lign matrices. The notation
\langle\cdot|\cdot\rangle
stands for the usual dot product on
\mathbf{C}^{n}
. Let
N
be an
n
-dimensional smooth complex analytic space and
P\in N
, for any local coordinates
Z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
centered at
P
and any
n\times n
matrix
M=(v_{ij})
with entries
v_{ij}
in
\mathbf{N}
, we set
Z^{M}=(z_{1}^{v_{11}}\cdots z_{n}^{v_{1n}},\dots,z_{1}^{v_{n1}}\cdots z_{n}^{v_{nn}})
. If
I=(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\in\mathbf{N}^{n}
is a line matrix, we will identify
Z^{I}
with the monomial
z_{1}^{i_{1}}\dots z_{n}^{i_{n}}
. We will also use these notations for points
c=(c_{1},\dots,c_{n})\in\mathbf{C}^{n}
, namely:
c^{M}=(c_{1}^{v_{11}}\cdots c_{n}^{v_{1n}},\dots,c_{1}^{v_{n1}}\cdots c_{n}^{v_{nn}})
and
c^{I}=c_{1}^{i_{1}}\dots c_{n}^{i_{n}}
. For any finite set
J
, the number of elements of
J
is denoted by
|J|
. When
J\subset\{1,\dots,n\}
has
q
elements
j_{1}<\dots<j_{q}
, we set
Z_{J}=z_{j_{1}}\dots z_{j_{q}}
and
dZ_{J}=dz_{j_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge dz_{j_{q}}
. If
q=p
, the logarithmic
p
-differentials are:

\frac{dZ_{J}}{Z_{J}}=\frac{dz_{j_{1}}}{z_{j_{1}}}\wedge\dots\wedge\frac{dz_{j_{p}}}{z_{j_{p}}}.

Let
\eta
be a holomorphic
p
-form defined on
\mathbf{C}^{n}
. Taking
N=\mathbf{C}^{n}
and
Z=X
allows us to express
\eta
in a logarithmic way in the standard coordinate system:

\eta(X)=\sum_{|J|=p}f_{J}(X)\frac{dX_{J}}{X_{J}}.

(1)

In the above expression, the subscript
|J|=p
means that the sum is indexed by all the subsets
J\subset\{1,\dots,n\}
with
|J|=p
. The coefficients
f_{J}(X)
are holomorphic functions on
\mathbf{C}^{n}
such that
\frac{f_{J}(X)}{X_{J}}
is holomorphic. Expanding the functions
f_{J}(X)
into power series, we obtain

\eta(X)=\sum_{|J|=p}\left(\sum_{I\in\mathbf{N}^{n}}a_{I,J}X^{I}\right)\frac{dX_{J}}{X_{J}},

(2)

where the coefficients
a_{I,J}
are complex numbers and
X_{J}
appears in every non-zero monomial
a_{I,J}X^{I}
. This means that if a coordinate of
I
indexed by
J
is zero, then
a_{I,J}=0
.

For any subset
A\subset\mathbf{N}^{n}
, we define
\eta_{A}
by restricting the exponents in the expression of
\eta
to
A
:

\eta_{A}(X)=\sum_{|J|=p}\left(f_{J}\right)_{|A}(X)\frac{dX_{J}}{X_{J}},

where

\left(f_{J}\right)_{|A}(X)=\sum_{I\in A}a_{I,J}X^{I}.

We then define the
support

\supp\eta
of
\eta
:

\supp\eta=\left\{I\in\mathbf{N}^{n}\mid\exists J\subset\{1,\dots,n\},|J|=p\text{ and }a_{I,J}\neq 0\right\}.

and
1.5
below draw both from
[
BM90
]
and
[
Est05
]

1.2. Newton non-degeneracy.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
\eta
be a holomorphic
p
-form on
\mathbf{C}^{n}
written as
\eta=\sum_{|J|=p}g_{J}(X)dX_{J}
, where
g_{J}(X)
are holomorphic functions. The
singular locus
of
\eta
is defined as the common zeroes of the coefficients
g_{J}
:

\sing\eta=\left\{c\in\mathbf{C}^{n}\mid\forall J\subset\ \{1,\dots,n\},|J|=p,\;g_{J}(c)=0\right\}.

Its elements are called
singularities
or
singular points
of
\eta
.

Definition 1.5
.

Let
\eta
be a holomorphic
p
-form on
\mathbf{C}^{n}
, denote by
\Gamma
its Newton polyhedron. We say that
\eta
is
Newton non-degenerate
(NND) if for any non-empty face
F
of
\Gamma
, the so called
initial form

\eta_{F}
has no singularities in
\left(\mathbf{C}^{*}\right)^{n}
.

Remark 1.6
.

Suppose
\eta
is expressed logarithmically as in
Eq.

1
:

\eta(X)=\sum_{|J|=p}\frac{f_{J}(X)}{X_{J}}dX_{J}.

Let
F
be a non-empty face of
\Gamma
and
J\subset\{1,\dots,n\}
with
|J|=p
. As
\left(\frac{f_{J}}{X_{J}}\right)_{|F}=\frac{\left({f_{J}}\right)_{|F}}{X_{J}}
, for any
c\in\left(\mathbf{C}^{*}\right)^{n}
,
\left(\frac{f_{J}}{X_{J}}\right)_{|F}(c)=0
if and only if
\left(f_{J}\right)_{|F}(c)=0
. Thus,
1.5
is equivalent to the following: for any non-empty face
F\subset\Gamma
and
J\subset\{1,\dots,n\}
with
|J|=p
, the functions
(f_{J})_{|F}
have no common zeroes in
\left(\mathbf{C}^{*}\right)^{n}
.

Toric background

In this section, we collect some results on normal toric varieties. We follow closely the introduction found in
[
AGMS13
]
. A general reference on toric geometry is
[
CLS24
]
. One can also consult the second chapter in
[
Oka97
]
for an introduction to toric morphisms.

2.1. Cones and fans

Let
v_{1},\dots,v_{q}\in\mathbf{R}^{n}
. The
convex polyhedral cone
generated by
v_{1},\dots,v_{q}
is the subset

\sigma=\left\{\lambda_{1}v_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{q}v_{q},\lambda_{k}\in\mathbf{R}_{\geq 0},k=1,\dots,q\right\}\subset\mathbf{R}^{n}.

We will often shortly refer to these objects as ‘cones’. The vectors
v_{k}
are called
generators
of the cone. A convex polyhedral cone is said to be
rational
if it has a set of generators in
\mathbf{Z}^{n}
. A rational polyhedral cone is said to be
strongly convex
if it does not contain any non-trivial linear subspace.

Let
\sigma\subset\mathbf{R}^{n}
a strongly convex rational polyhedral cone. The
1
-dimensional faces of
\sigma
are half-lines called
rays
and each of them has a unique generator in
\mathbf{Z}^{n}
whose coordinates have greatest common divisor 1. Considering all the
1
-dimensional faces of
\sigma
, we thus define a set of vectors in
\mathbf{Z}^{n}
which generates
\sigma
(see Lemma 1.2.15. in
[
CLS24
]
for more details). We call it the set of
minimal generators
of the strongly convex rational polyhedral cone
\sigma
.

A strongly convex rational polyhedral cone
\sigma\subset\mathbf{R}^{n}
is
smooth
if its minimal generators form part of a
\mathbf{Z}
-basis of
\mathbf{Z}^{n}
.

A fan
\Sigma
in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
is a finite collection of cones
\sigma\subset\mathbf{R}^{n}
such that:

a)

Each
\sigma\in\Sigma
is a strongly convex rational polyhedral cone;

b)

For all
\sigma\in\Sigma
, each face of
\sigma
is also in
\Sigma
;

c)

For all
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\Sigma
, the intersection
\sigma_{1}\cap\sigma_{2}
is a face of each (hence, also in
\Sigma
).

Given a fan
\Sigma
in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
, we will say that
\sigma\in\Sigma
is a
maximal cone
of
\Sigma
if it is maximal for the inclusion among the cones of
\Sigma
. The fan
\Sigma
is
regular
when each of its cones are smooth (in practice, it is sufficient to check that the maximal cones are smooth). Let
\Sigma
and
\Sigma^{\prime}
be fans in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
, we say that
\Sigma^{\prime}
is a refinement of
\Sigma
if every
\sigma\in\Sigma
is a union of cones in
\Sigma^{\prime}
. We refer to
[
CLS24
]
for the proof of the existence of regular refinements.

Theorem 2.1
.

Any fan in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
has a regular refinement.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\eta
be a non identically zero holomorphic
p
-form on
\mathbf{C}^{n}
and
\Gamma
its Newton polyhedron. For any non-empty face
F\subset\Gamma
, the normal vectors to the facets of
\Gamma
containing
F
which are in the orthant
\mathbf{R}_{\geq 0}^{n}
generate a cone denoted by
C_{F}
. The set of all the such cones

\Sigma(\eta)=\{C_{F},\;F\text{ is a non-empty face of }\Gamma\}

is a fan called the
dual fan
of
\eta
.

2.2. Varieties and morphisms

We refer to
[
CLS24
]
and
[
Oka97
]
for the proofs of the following results. For our purposes, we will only need the expression of a toric morphism in affine charts.

Definition 2.3
.

A
toric variety
of dimension
n
is an irreducible complex algebraic variety
V
containing a torus
T_{N}\cong\left(\mathbf{C}^{*}\right)^{n}
as a Zariski open subset such that the action of
T_{N}
on itself extends to an action
T_{N}\times V\longrightarrow V
given by a morphism of algebraic varieties.

To any fan
\Sigma
in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
, we can associate a normal separated toric variety
X_{\Sigma}
(Theorem 3.1.5 in
[
CLS24
]
). Moreover, if the fan
\Sigma
is regular, then
X_{\Sigma}
is a smooth variety.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\Sigma
and
\Sigma^{\prime}
be fans in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
. A morphism
\pi:X_{\Sigma}\longrightarrow X_{\Sigma^{\prime}}
is
toric
if
\pi
maps the torus
T\subset X_{\Sigma}
into
T^{\prime}\subset X_{\Sigma^{\prime}}
and
\pi_{|T}
is a group morphism.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\Sigma
and
\Sigma^{\prime}
be fans in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
. If
\Sigma
is a refinement of
\Sigma^{\prime}
, then it induces a proper birational toric morphism
\pi:X_{\Sigma}\longrightarrow X_{\Sigma^{\prime}}
.

Suppose that in the previous theorem,
\Sigma^{\prime}
is the fan whose only maximal cone is the orthant
\mathbf{R}_{\geq 0}^{n}
, then
X_{\Sigma^{\prime}}=\mathbf{C}^{n}
. Let
\mathcal{U}_{\sigma}
be an affine chart of
X_{\Sigma}
defined by a maximal cone
\sigma
with minimal generators
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
. For any
k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, we can write
v_{k}=(v_{1k},\dots,v_{nk})\in\mathbf{N}^{n}
. Let
M
be the matrix whose columns are given by the coordinates of the generators:

M=\bordermatrix{&v_{1}&v_{2}&\dots&v_{n}\cr&v_{11}&v_{12}&\dots&v_{1n}\cr&v_{21}&v_{22}&\dots&v_{2n}\cr&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\cr&v_{n1}&v_{n2}&\dots&v_{nn}\cr}

Let
Y=(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})
be coordinates on
\mathcal{U}_{\sigma}
, then the morphism
\pi_{|\mathcal{U}_{\sigma}}
is given by
X=Y^{M}
.

Reduction of singularities

3.1. Log-smooth
p
-forms

We first precise what we mean by an adapted or log-smooth
p
-form with respect to a divisor, as these notions appear in
3.3
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\eta
be holomorphic
p
-form defined on
\mathbf{C}^{n}
and
A\subset\{1,\dots,n\}
. We consider the divisor
E=\left(\prod_{k\in A}x_{k}=0\right)
, suppose that
\eta
can be written

\eta=\prod_{k\in A}x_{k}^{t_{k}}\sum_{|J|=p}g_{J}(X)\frac{dX_{J}}{X_{J}},

(3)

where the coefficients
g_{J}(X)
are holomorphic functions and
t_{k}\in\mathbf{N}^{*}
for
k\in A
. We will say that

a)

\eta
is
adapted
to the divisor
E
if each coefficient
g_{J}(X)
is divisible by the monomial
X_{J\setminus A}=\prod_{k\in J\setminus A}x_{k}
;

b)

\eta
is
log-smooth
with respect to the divisor
E
if the functions
g_{J}(X)
have no common zeroes in
\mathbf{C}^{n}
.

Remark 3.2
.

One should note that even with
p=1
, these definitions differ from the standard ones for codimension 1 foliations found in
[
Can98
]
and
[
MS20
]
:

-

both definitions are formulated globally;

-

in
a)
, the coefficients
g_{J}(X)
of the
p
-form may have common factors;

-

definition
b)
is not equivalent to the notion of logarithmical regularity studied in
[
MS20
]
since we ask for the coefficients
g_{J}(X)
to share no common zeroes instead of
\frac{g_{J}(X)}{X_{J\setminus A}}
.

Definitions
a)
and
b)
apply to a form
\eta
defined on a smooth variety by taking local coordinates.

3.2. Reduction theorem

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\eta
be a non identically zero holomorphic
p
-form defined on
\mathbf{C}^{n}
. We denote by
\Gamma
its Newton polyhedron and by
\Sigma
a regular refinement of the dual fan of
\eta
. Let
\pi:X_{\Sigma}\longrightarrow\mathbf{C}^{n}
be the associated proper birational toric morphism. Consider an affine chart
\mathcal{U}_{\sigma}
of
X_{\Sigma}
defined by a maximal cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
with minimal generators
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
. Set
t_{k}=\min_{I\in\Gamma}\langle v_{k},I\rangle
, where
k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. Denote by
Y=(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})
coordinates on
\mathcal{U}_{\sigma}
. The equation
y_{1}^{t_{1}}\dots y_{n}^{t_{n}}=0
defines a normal crossings divisor
E
on
X_{\Sigma}
.

1)

The pull-back
\pi^{*}\eta
is adapted to the divisor
E
.

2)

Suppose that
\eta
is Newton non-degenerate, then
\pi^{*}\eta
is log-smooth with respect to
E
.

Proof.

We will keep the same notations as in
Section

2.2
. We want to compute the pull-back of
\eta
by
\pi
in the chart
\mathcal{U}_{\sigma}
. Let
Y=(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})
be coordinates on
\mathcal{U}_{\sigma}
. The toric morphism
\pi
is expressed in this chart by the change of variables
X=Y^{M}
:

\begin{cases}x_{1}=y_{1}^{v_{11}}\cdots y_{n}^{v_{1n}}\\
x_{2}=y_{1}^{v_{21}}\cdots y_{n}^{v_{2n}}\\
\;\;\;\;\;\vdots\\
x_{n}=y_{1}^{v_{n1}}\cdots y_{n}^{v_{nn}}\end{cases}

(4)

For all
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, taking logarithmic differentials in
Eq.

4
, we obtain a change of base formula:

\frac{dx_{j}}{x_{j}}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}v_{jk}\frac{dy_{k}}{y_{k}}.

(5)

Let
J
be a subset of
\{1,\dots,n\}
with
p
elements
j_{1}<\dots<j_{p}
, using
Eq.

5
, we find

\frac{dX_{J}}{X_{J}}=\bigwedge_{s=1}^{p}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}v_{j_{s}k}\frac{dy_{k}}{y_{k}}\right),

from which we derive a change of base formula for
p
-logarithmic differentials:

\frac{dX_{J}}{X_{J}}=\sum_{|K|=p}|M_{J,K}|\frac{dY_{K}}{Y_{K}},

(6)

where
|M_{J,K}|
is the determinant of the submatrix of
M
whose lines and columns are indexed respectively by
J
and
K
. Recall that
\eta
is expressed in the standard coordinate system
X
as in
Eq.

1
:

\eta(X)=\sum_{|J|=p}f_{J}(X)\frac{dX_{J}}{X_{J}}.

Combining
Eq.

6
with the previous one, we start to compute the total transform of
\eta
by
\pi
:

\begin{gathered}\pi^{*}\eta(Y)=\sum_{|J|=p}f_{J}(Y^{M})\sum_{|K|=p}|M_{J,K}|\frac{dY_{K}}{Y_{K}}\\
\pi^{*}\eta(Y)=\sum_{|K|=p}\left(\sum_{|J|=p}|M_{J,K}|f_{J}(Y^{M})\right)\frac{dY_{K}}{Y_{K}}.\end{gathered}

Let us expand the coefficients
f_{J}
into power series, keeping the same notations as in
Eq.

2
:

\pi^{*}\eta(Y)=\sum_{|K|=p}\left(\sum_{|J|=p}|M_{J,K}|\left(\sum_{I\in\mathbf{N}^{n}\cap\Gamma}a_{I,J}Y^{I\cdot M}\right)\right)\frac{dY_{K}}{Y_{K}},

where the dot
\cdot
denotes the matrix product.

\pi^{*}\eta(Y)=\sum_{I\in\mathbf{N}^{n}\cap\Gamma}\left(\sum_{|K|=p}\sum_{|J|=p}a_{I,J}|M_{J,K}|\frac{dY_{K}}{Y_{K}}\right)Y^{I\cdot M}.

For any
I\in\mathbf{N}^{n}\cap\Gamma
recall that
Y^{I\cdot M}=y_{1}^{\langle v_{1},I\rangle}\dots y_{n}^{\langle v_{n},I\rangle}
. For any
k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, we have
t_{k}=\min_{I\in\Gamma}\langle v_{k},I\rangle
and we denote by
F_{k}
the face of
\Gamma
defined by
v_{k}
, namely
F_{k}=\{I\in\Gamma,\langle v_{k},I\rangle=t_{k}\}
. Let
A=\{k\in\{1,\dots,n\},t_{k}>0\}
. We consider the divisor
\left(\prod_{k\in A}y_{k}=0\right)
. This leads us to factor the previous expression by
Y^{T}
, where
T=(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})
:

\pi^{*}\eta(Y)=Y^{T}\Biggl(\sum_{|K|=p}\Bigl(\underbrace{\sum_{I\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\cap\Gamma}\sum_{|J|=p}a_{I,J}|M_{J,K}|Y^{I\cdot M-T}}_{\overline{f}_{K}(Y)}\Bigr)\frac{dY_{K}}{Y_{K}}\Biggr).

For any
K\subset\{1,\dots,n\}
such that
|K|=p
, we can rewrite
\overline{f}_{K}(Y)
with dot products:

\overline{f}_{K}(Y)=\sum_{I\in\mathbf{N}^{n}\cap\Gamma}\sum_{|J|=p}a_{I,J}|M_{J,K}|y_{1}^{\langle v_{1},I\rangle-t_{1}}\dots y_{n}^{\langle v_{n},I\rangle-t_{n}}.

(7)

•
Proof of statement
1)
.

Let
K\subset\{1,\dots,n\}
such that
|K|=p
and
k\in K\setminus A
, we will show that
y_{k}
divides
\overline{f}_{K}(Y)
. To do so, we substitute
y_{k}=0
in
Eq.

7
:

\overline{f}_{K}(Y)_{|y_{k}=0}=\sum_{I\in F_{k}}\sum_{|J|=p}a_{I,J}|M_{J,K}|\prod_{s\neq k}y_{s}^{\langle v_{s},I\rangle-t_{s}}.

Let
I\in F_{k}
and
J\subset\{1,\dots,n\}
with
|J|=p
. We will justify that the product
a_{I,J}|M_{J,K}|
is equal to zero. This will show that the sum above is also zero and that
y_{k}
divides
\overline{f}_{K}(Y)
. Recall that
a_{I,J}=0
whenever a coordinate of
I
indexed by
J
is zero (this has been stated after
Eq.

2
). Suppose now that all coordinates of
I
indexed by
J
are non-zero and let us prove that
|M_{J,K}|=0
.

As
k\notin A
, we have
t_{k}=\min_{I\in\Gamma}\langle v_{k},I\rangle=0
. This implies that
v_{k}
has at least one coordinate which is
0
. Let
T=\{j\in\{1,\dots,n\},v_{jk}>0\}
. The face
F_{k}=\{I\in\Gamma,\langle v_{k},I\rangle=0\}
is the intersection of
\Gamma
with coordinate hyperplanes, namely:
F_{k}=\{I=(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\in\Gamma,\forall s\in T,i_{s}=0\}
. The matrix
M
can be written as:

M=\bordermatrix{&v_{1}&\cdots&v_{k}&\cdots&v_{n}\cr&v_{11}&\ldots&*&\ldots&v_{1n}\cr&\vdots&\ldots&*&\ldots&\vdots\cr&\vdots&\ldots&\vdots&\ldots&\vdots\cr&\vdots&\ldots&\vdots&\ldots&\vdots\cr&v_{n1}&\ldots&*&\ldots&v_{nn}\cr}.

The coefficient
|M_{J,K}|
is the determinant of the matrix whose lines are indexed by
J
and columns are indexed by
K
. As
k\in K
, the
k
-th column of
M
will be part of this extracted matrix. The elements of
T
do not belong to
J
. Indeed, if there was an element
s\in T\cap J
, as
F_{k}=\{I=(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\in\Gamma,\forall s\in T,i_{s}=0\}
and
I\in F_{k}
, the
s
-th coordinate of
I
would be indexed by an element of
J
and equal to zero. This contradicts our hypothesis. In the end, we get that all non-zero elements of the
k
-th column are placed in lines indexed by
\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus J
. Thus, the submatrix that we consider has a column of zeroes and its determinant
|M_{J,K}|
is zero.

For any
k\in K\setminus A
,
y_{k}
divides
\overline{f}_{K}(Y)
, so
Y_{K\setminus A}
divides
\overline{f}_{K}(Y)
. We conclude that
\pi^{*}\eta
is adapted to the divisor
E
.

Proof of statement
2)
.

Suppose that
\pi^{*}\eta
is not log-smooth with respect to
E
. There exists a point
c=(c_{1},\dots,c_{n})\in\mathbf{C}^{n}
which is a common zero for the coefficients
\overline{f}_{K}(Y)
. Let
S=\{k\in\{1,\dots,n\},c_{k}\ =0\}
.

Consider the non-empty face
F
of
\Gamma
defined by
F=\cap_{k\in S}F_{k}
. Since
c
is a common zero of the coefficients
\overline{f}_{K}(Y)
, for each subset
K\subset\{1,\dots,n\}
, using
Eq.

7
, we get:

\sum_{I\in F}\sum_{|J|=p}a_{I,J}|M_{J,K}|\prod_{k\notin S}c_{k}^{\langle v_{k},I\rangle-t_{k}}=0

\sum_{|J|=p}\left(\sum_{I\in F}a_{I,J}\prod_{k\notin S}c_{k}^{\langle v_{k},I\rangle}\right)|M_{J,K}|=0.

(8)

We observe that for any
I\in\Gamma\cap\mathbf{N}^{n}
,

\prod_{k\notin S}c_{k}^{\langle v_{k},I\rangle}=\prod_{k\notin S}c_{k}^{i_{1}v_{1k}+\dots+i_{n}v_{nk}}=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\prod_{k\notin S}(c_{k}^{v_{1k}})^{i_{j}}=C^{I}.

where
C=(\prod_{k\notin S}c_{k}^{v_{1k}},\dots,\prod_{k\notin S}c_{k}^{v_{nk}})\in(\mathbf{C}^{*})^{n}
. This helps us further simplify
Eq.

8
:

\forall K,\;\sum_{|J|=p}\left(\sum_{I\in F}a_{I,J}C^{I}\right)|M_{J,K}|=0

\sum_{|J|=p}(f_{J})_{|F}(C)|M_{J,K}|=0

(9)

Let
J_{1}\prec\dots\prec J_{r}
be an ordering of the subsets of
\{1,\dots,n\}
with
p
elements, where
r=\binom{n}{p}
.

We introduce the following matrix:

M^{\prime}=\left(\begin{matrix}|M_{J_{1},J_{1}}|&|M_{J_{1},J_{2}}|&\dots&|M_{J_{1},J_{r}}|\\
|M_{J_{2},J_{1}}|&|M_{J_{2},J_{2}}|&\dots&|M_{J_{2},J_{r}}|\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
|M_{J_{r},J_{1}}|&|M_{J_{r},J_{2}}|&\dots&|M_{J_{r},J_{r}}|\end{matrix}\right).

We proceed to reformulate the
r=\binom{n}{p}
equalities in
Eq.

9
as a matrix equation:

\left((f_{J_{1}})_{|F}(C),\dots,(f_{J_{r}})_{|F}(C)\right)M^{\prime}=0.

(10)

Since
\sigma
is a smooth cone,
M
is invertible. Let us prove that
M^{\prime}
is also invertible. Denote by
(e_{1},\dots,e_{n})
the canonical basis of
\mathbf{C}^{n}
and
u:\mathbf{C}^{n}\longrightarrow\mathbf{C}^{n}
the
\mathbf{C}
-linear map represented by the matrix
M
in this basis. For any subset
J\subset\{1,\dots,n\}
such that
J=\{j_{1},\dots,j_{p}\}
and
j_{1}<\dots<j_{p}
, set
e_{J}=e_{j_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge e_{j_{p}}
. The
p
-th exterior power
\wedge^{p}u
of the map
u
is defined by
\wedge^{p}u(e_{J})=u(e_{j_{1}})\wedge\dots\wedge u(e_{j_{p}})
. For any subset
K\subset\{1,\dots,n\}
with
p
elements
k_{1}<\dots<k_{p}
,

\wedge^{p}u(e_{K})=\bigwedge_{\ell=1}^{p}u(e_{k_{\ell}})=\bigwedge_{\ell=1}^{p}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{ik_{\ell}}e_{i}\right)=\sum_{|J|=p}|M_{J,K}|e_{J}.

The family of vectors
(e_{J_{1}},\dots,e_{J_{r}})
is a basis of
\wedge^{p}\mathbf{C}^{n}
. For any
\ell\in\{1,\dots,r\}
,

\wedge^{p}u(e_{J_{\ell}})=\sum_{s=1}^{r}|M_{J_{s},J_{\ell}}|e_{J_{s}}.

This proves that
M^{\prime}
is the matrix of
\wedge^{p}u
in the basis
(e_{J_{1}},\dots,e_{J_{r}})
. The exterior power of an invertible linear endomorphism is invertible. Thus,
\wedge^{p}u
is invertible and so is the matrix
M^{\prime}
.

This implies that the unique solution of
Eq.

10
is

\left((f_{J_{1}})_{|F}(C),\dots,(f_{J_{r}})_{|F}(C)\right)=(0,\dots,0)

and that
C\in\left(\mathbf{C}^{*}\right)^{n}
is a common zero for the initial forms
(f_{J_{k}})_{|F}
. This is impossible because
\eta
is NND, so the coefficients
\overline{f}_{K}(Y)
have no common zeroes. We conclude that
\pi^{*}\eta
is log-smooth with respect to
E
.

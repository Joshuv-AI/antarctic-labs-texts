Exploration of novel therapeutic targets for keloids based on clinical and molecular data: the role of the IL-4/IL-13 signaling pathway
Authors: Xueli Li, Jianping Lan, Ruoyun Liu, Tingting Lin, Zheqi Li, Bo Cheng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background and ObjectivesKeloids are problematic fibrotic lesions with excessive collagen and chronic inflammation; their underlying causes are not fully understood. Type 2 helper T (Th2) cytokines IL-4 and IL-13 are implicated in various fibrotic disorders, including but not limited to pulmonary fibrosis and systemic sclerosis. This study aimed to investigate the IL-4/IL-13 signaling pathway's contribution to keloid pathophysiology and evaluate its potential as a novel therapeutic target.MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed keloid patients, collecting demographic data and grading scar severity with the Vancouver Scar Scale (VSS). Keloid tissue specimens underwent immunofluorescence, Western blotting, and real-time PCR to quantify IL-4, IL-13, Type I Collagen alpha 1 (Collagen I A1), and phosphorylated STAT6 (p-STAT6) expression.ResultsPatients had an average total VSS score of 11.0 ± 2.9, indicating active fibrotic inflammation. Molecular assays showed significant upregulation of IL-4 and IL-13, alongside increased p-STAT6 and Collagen I A1 in keloid tissues. Notably, IL-4, IL-13, and p-STAT6 expression positively correlated with VSS thickness and pliability scores.ConclusionsOur findings demonstrate that the IL-4/IL-13 signaling axis is markedly activated in keloid tissues, promoting collagen synthesis via STAT6 phosphorylation. Therefore, targeting the IL-4/IL-13/STAT6 pathway may represent a promising therapeutic strategy for managing keloids.

Introduction

Keloids are benign fibroproliferative skin tumors marked by persistent inflammation and the overproduction of extracellular matrix components [1]. They can develop on virtually any body site, with a predilection for the anterior chest, back, shoulders, perineum, and auricles [2, 3]. Affected individuals frequently experience pruritus, pain, and a sensation of tightness, particularly when lesions overlie mobile or exposed areas [4], which often leads to significant psychosocial distress—including anxiety and depression—that can impair social functioning and cause notable cosmetic disfigurement [5].

Current therapeutic approaches for keloids predominantly involve surgical excision combined with adjuvant modalities such as intralesional corticosteroid injections, cryotherapy, and radiotherapy [6, 7]. Nevertheless, high recurrence rates and suboptimal outcomes remain common, largely attributable to gaps in our understanding of the pathogenic mechanisms [8]. Diagnosis still relies heavily on clinical expertise and histopathological evaluation, neither of which provides sufficient specificity to guide targeted treatments [9]. Also, Keloids differ from hypertrophic scars in several key aspects. Hypertrophic scars remain confined within the original wound boundary and may stabilize or regress over time, whereas keloids extend beyond the wound margin and exhibit persistent fibroproliferative activity. In addition, keloids show higher inflammatory cell infiltration and more disorganized collagen bundles. Thus, there is an urgent need to elucidate the molecular drivers of keloid formation to enable the development of more effective, mechanism-based therapies [10].

Keloid pathogenesis is a complex, multifactorial process involving genetic predisposition, dysregulated immune responses, and aberrant wound-healing dynamics [11]. Hallmark pathological features include sustained activation of dermal fibroblasts and excessive synthesis and deposition of extracellular matrix components—particularly type I collagen [12, 13]. Histological analyses reveal infiltration by macrophages, mast cells, and lymphocytes within keloid tissues [14, 15], which secrete a spectrum of proinflammatory cytokines and growth factors—such as TGF-β, IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α—that further drive fibroblast proliferation and ECM accumulation [16]. Prolonged inflammatory signaling, often persisting for weeks, sustains cytokine release and perpetuates uncontrolled fibroblast activity, leading to excessive ECM deposition [17, 18].

Interleukin-4 (IL-4) and interleukin-13 (IL-13) are signature Th2 cytokines known to play pivotal roles in the pathogenesis of multiple fibrotic diseases by signaling through a shared receptor subunit, IL-4Rα, to activate the JAK/STAT6 pathway [19–21]. Dupilumab, a monoclonal antibody targeting IL-4Rα and thereby inhibiting both IL-4 and IL-13 signaling, has demonstrated high efficacy and safety in atopic dermatitis, asthma, and chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps. Emerging evidence further suggests that IL-4/IL-13 blockade may attenuate tissue fibrosis in several Th2-driven diseases, supporting the rationale for investigating this pathway in keloids. [22] Genetic ablation of IL-4 or IL-13, or pharmacological blockade with neutralizing antibodies, has been shown to attenuate collagen production and ameliorate fibrotic remodeling in experimental models [23]. Despite extensive study in other fibrotic contexts, the specific contributions and mechanisms of IL-4/IL-13 signaling in keloid pathophysiology remain poorly defined.

Given the chronic inflammatory and fibrotic nature of keloids, we hypothesized that the IL-4/IL-13/STAT6 axis is critically involved in their initiation and maintenance. To test this hypothesis, we conducted a comprehensive clinical evaluation of keloid patients, including Vancouver Scar Scale (VSS) scoring to assess scar severity and inflammatory activity. We then performed in vitro studies on human keloid tissue specimens to dissect the roles of IL-4 and IL-13 in driving fibroblast activation, STAT6 phosphorylation, and collagen synthesis. This integrated approach aims to elucidate the pivotal role of IL-4/IL-13 signaling in keloid formation and to establish a theoretical foundation for the development of novel targeted anti-fibrotic therapies.

Materials and methods

Clinical materials

A total of 124 keloid patients diagnosed between 2018 and 2023 in the Dermatology and Surgical Outpatient Departments of the Affiliated Mindong Hospital of Fujian Medical University were retrospectively enrolled. All cases fulfilled established diagnostic and inclusion criteria for keloids and did not meet any exclusion criteria. The cohort comprised 42 males and 82 females, ranging in age from 2 to 97 years (mean ± SD, 31 ± 18 years). Scar severity was assessed using the Vancouver Scar Scale (VSS), which evaluates four parameters—pigmentation, thickness, vascularity, and pliability—each scored on a standardized scale.

Western blot analysis

Cells and tissues were lysed on ice for 30 min using RIPA buffer (P0013B, Beyotime, Shanghai, China) supplemented with 1% PMSF, followed by centrifugation at 14,000 × g for 15 min at 4 ℃. Protein concentrations were quantified using the BCA assay (P0012S, Beyotime, Shanghai, China). Equal amounts of protein (50 µg) were denatured at 100 ℃ for 10 min and subsequently separated by SDS-PAGE, then transferred onto PVDF membranes. After blocking with 5% non-fat milk for 1 h at room temperature, the membranes were incubated overnight at 4 ℃ with primary antibodies against β-actin (ab8226, Abcam), IL-4 (ab62351, Abcam), IL-13 (ab260044, Abcam), STAT6 (ab32520, Abcam), p-STAT6 (ab263947, Abcam), and Collagen I A1 (ab138492, Abcam). Following TBST washes, membranes were treated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies, and the immunoreactive signals were visualized using an ECL detection kit (32,209, Thermo Fisher Scientific). Band intensities were quantified by grayscale analysis with ImageJ software. All experiments were independently repeated three times.

Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR)

Total RNA was isolated from cultured cells and kidney tissues using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, USA). RNA concentration and purity were determined with a NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer. Complementary DNA (cDNA) was synthesized from the extracted RNA using the PrimeScript RT reagent kit (Takara, Japan). Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) was conducted on an ABI PRISM 7300 system employing the Fast SYBR Green PCR kit (Takara), with GAPDH serving as the internal control. Relative gene expression was calculated using the 2^ − ΔΔCt method. Each sample was analyzed in triplicate, and all experiments were independently repeated three times. Primer sequences were designed and synthesized by Sangon Biotech (Table 1, Shanghai, China).

Immunohistochemistry

Scar tissue specimens were obtained from ten keloid patients and immediately fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde at room temperature for 24 h. The tissues were then dehydrated through a graded ethanol series (75%, 85%, 95%, and 100% ethanol for 1 min each), followed by two 1-min immersions in xylene. Dehydrated tissues were embedded in molten paraffin and sectioned at 3–4 μm thickness. Paraffin sections were deparaffinized in xylene and rehydrated through a descending ethanol series..

Antigen retrieval was carried out by incubating sections in 10 mM citrate buffer (pH 6.0) at 92 °C for 40 min. After cooling to room temperature, the sections were washed twice in PBS. Endogenous peroxidase activity was quenched and nonspecific binding blocked by incubating sections in 5% bovine serum albumin (BSA) at 37 °C for 30 min. Following two PBS washes, the sections were incubated with primary antibodies—rabbit anti-IL-4 (1:200; ab62351, Abcam, USA) and rabbit anti-IL-13 (1:200; ab260044, Abcam, USA)—at room temperature for 1 h. After three washes in PBST (PBS containing 0.1% Tween-20), the sections were treated with HRP-conjugated goat anti-rabbit IgG secondary antibody (1:500; ab205718, Abcam, UK) for 1 h at room temperature. Immunoreactivity was visualized using a DAB chromogenic kit (Beyotime, P0203). Stained sections were subsequently imaged using the EVOS cell imaging system.

Statistical analysis methods

All data were derived from at least three independent experiments and are expressed as the mean ± standard deviation (Mean ± SD). A two-sample independent t-test was used to compare the two groups. For comparisons involving three or more groups, one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was performed, followed by Tukey's HSD post hoc test. For datasets that did not meet the assumptions of normality or homogeneity of variance, nonparametric tests—either the Mann–Whitney U test or the Kruskal–Wallis H test-were applied. All statistical analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism 9 (GraphPad Software, Inc.) and the R programming environment. A significance threshold of 0.05 was used for all tests, and p-values < 0.05 (two-tailed) were considered statistically significant..

Results

Clinical data analysis of keloid patients

Scar severity was quantified using the Vancouver Scar Scale (VSS), which assigns scores from 0 to 15-higher scores indicating more severe scarring (Table 2). Retrospective analysis of our cohort (n = 124) revealed a female predominance (66.1%), with a male‑to‑female ratio of 1:1.9. The predominance of female patients in our cohort is consistent with prior epidemiological reports and may reflect increased rates of ear piercing, cultural cosmetic practices, and hormonal influences on wound healing. The highest incidence occurred in patients aged 14–35 years (60.5%), corresponding to the period of hormonal fluctuation during adolescence and a high risk of cutaneous injury. Anatomical distribution of lesions showed the earlobe (39.5%), presternal area (12.9%), and shoulder/back region (3.2%) as the most commonly affected sites, suggesting that mechanical tension and local sebaceous gland density may influence keloid formation. The mean total VSS score was 11.0 ± 2.9; subscale scores were highest for vascularity (2.5 ± 0.6), thickness (3.1 ± 0.5), and pliability (2.8 ± 0.7), indicating active inflammation in areas of pronounced fibrotic remodeling. Upregulation of IL‑4 and IL‑13 expression in keloid tissues

Wound healing and scar formation are dynamic processes orchestrated by a complex interplay of cytokines. During the early inflammatory phase of healing, pro‑inflammatory cytokines mediate host defense and initiate tissue repair. In later stages, anti‑inflammatory mediators predominate to promote cell proliferation and matrix remodeling. Dysregulation of this balance—resulting in prolonged or excessive inflammation—can give rise to pathological scarring.

Our clinical observations suggested a positive correlation between scar severity and inflammatory activity. Previous studies have demonstrated that IL‑4 and IL‑13 drive fibroblast proliferation, collagen synthesis, and extracellular matrix remodeling, thereby exacerbating scar fibrosis and hyperplasia. To investigate the relationship between inflammatory cytokines and keloid severity, we first performed immunohistochemical analysis on lesional tissues stratified by VSS score: VSS‑low (0–5), VSS‑middle (6–10), and VSS‑high (11–15), with adjacent normal skin as control (group N). Discussion

In this study, we integrated clinical observations and molecular analyses to delineate the role of the IL‑4/IL‑13 signaling axis in keloid pathogenesis and to explore its potential as a therapeutic target. Consistent with prior epidemiological reports, our cohort demonstrated a female predominance (66.1%) and an age‐related peak incidence between 14 and 35 years. This distribution likely reflects both biological and socio‐behavioral factors: hormonal fluctuations during adolescence and early adulthood may predispose to aberrant wound healing [24], while cultural practices—such as higher rates of ear piercing among women—may increase lesion occurrence at exposed sites. Moreover, changes in keloid behavior during pregnancy and reduced incidence after menopause underscore the influence of endocrine mechanisms on scar pathophysiology [25]. These findings suggest that preventive strategies should address both physiological risk factors (e.g., hormonal modulation) and modifiable behaviors (e.g., mechanical stress reduction in predisposed individuals).

Our cohort exhibited notably elevated VSS sub-scores for vascularity, thickness, and pliability, reflecting pronounced inflammatory activity within the most fibrotic regions of keloid scars. This finding aligns with the established view that keloids are characterized by chronic inflammation. Importantly, the VSS-being a simple, quantitative, and noninvasive clinical tool-may do more than describe scar morphology: it likely serves as a surrogate marker of underlying inflammatory and fibrotic processes. The Vancouver Scar Scale was selected because it is one of the most widely validated and internationally adopted tools for clinical scar assessment. Its sub-scores, particularly thickness, pliability, and vascularity, closely reflect underlying fibrotic and inflammatory activity, making it suitable for correlating clinical severity with molecular findings. Thus, serial VSS assessment in routine practice could not only inform treatment selection but also noninvasively monitor the efficacy of anti‑inflammatory or antifibrotic interventions without the need for repeated biopsies.

Molecular analyses revealed significant increases in IL-4, IL-13, phosphorylated STAT6, and type I collagen A1 in keloid tissues, indicating that activation of the IL-4/IL-13/STAT6 pathway contributes directly to keloid formation. In light of the high recurrence and adverse effects associated with existing therapies, there is a pressing need for innovative, targeted treatment approaches. TGF-β is a central mediator of pathological fibrosis and plays a crucial role in keloid formation. Although the present study focused on IL-4/IL-13-STAT6 signaling, these pathways interact with TGF-β at multiple levels—for example, IL-13 can potentiate TGF-β-driven collagen synthesis. Future work should incorporate direct measurement of TGF-β to elucidate its relationship with Th2-mediated signaling. Preclinical data demonstrate that genetic deletion or neutralization of IL-4 or IL-13 effectively reduces collagen synthesis and attenuates fibrosis, and dupilumab (an anti-IL-4Rα monoclonal antibody) has shown promise in blocking both cytokines’ signaling. Although approved for atopic dermatitis and asthma, emerging case reports and ongoing clinical trials suggest its potential utility in keloid management.

Despite these insights, our study has several limitations. The retrospective design and modest sample size may not fully capture the heterogeneity of keloid presentation. Moreover, our focus on expression profiling warrants follow-up functional investigations to establish causality. Subsequent research should incorporate larger prospective patient cohorts to confirm clinical associations and conduct detailed in vitro and in vivo experiments, including IL-4/IL-13 manipulation in primary keloid fibroblasts and appropriate animal models, aimed at elucidating downstream targets beyond STAT6. Finally, exploring combination regimens that integrate IL-4/IL-13 blockade with established modalities (e.g., intralesional corticosteroids, pressure therapy) may uncover synergistic strategies to improve patient outcomes. Our data indicate that IL-4/IL-13 signaling is locally upregulated within keloid tissue, suggesting that intralesional delivery of IL-4Rα-targeted therapy may offer a more focused therapeutic approach. However, Dupilumab is currently available only as a systemic subcutaneous formulation, and early case reports have shown improvement of keloids with systemic administration. Comparative studies of intralesional versus systemic IL-4/IL-13 blockade are therefore warranted.

Conclusion

The clinical analysis in this study characterized keloid patients as predominantly female, with peak onset in young adulthood and lesions most often arising in high‐tension or sebaceous‐gland–rich areas; VSS scores further demonstrated pronounced scar severity accompanied by underlying inflammatory activity. Molecular investigations revealed significant upregulation of IL‑4, IL‑13, and phosphorylated STAT6 in keloid tissues, along with increased type I collagen α1 expression. Together, these findings underscore the active role of the IL‑4/IL‑13/STAT6 signaling axis in driving keloid fibrosis and highlight the IL‑4/IL‑13 pathway as a promising novel therapeutic target to address the limitations of current treatment strategies. Although our findings highlight the relevance of IL-4, IL-13, p-STAT6, and COL1A1 in keloid pathobiology, their clinical applicability requires further validation through mechanistic studies and controlled clinical trials.

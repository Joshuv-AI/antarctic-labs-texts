1.
Introduction

Let
V
be a vertex operator algebra. Zhu
[
24
]
constructed an associative algebra
A(V)
, now known as the
Zhu algebra
, and proved a bijection between irreducible
A(V)
-modules and irreducible admissible
V
-modules. Dong, Li, and Mason extended this framework in a series of influential works. In
[
6
]
, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, they introduced an associative algebra
A_{n}(V)
satisfying
A_{0}(V)=A(V)
. In
[
4
]
, they generalized Zhu’s construction to twisted representations: given a finite-order automorphism
g
of
V
, they defined an associative algebra
A_{g}(V)
, which coincides with
A(V)
when
g=\operatorname{id}_{V}
. Finally, in
[
5
]
, they unified these results by constructing, for an automorphism
g
of order
T
and for
n\in(1/T)\mathbb{N}
, an associative algebra
A_{g,n}(V)
such that
A_{g,0}(V)=A_{g}(V)
and
A_{\operatorname{id}_{V},n}(V)=A_{n}(V)
, and established a bijection between irreducible
A_{g,n}(V)
-modules that do not factor through
A_{g,n-1/T}(V)
and irreducible admissible
g
-twisted
V
-modules.

In the same work
[
5
]
, for any
A_{g,m}(V)
-module
U
that does not factor through
A_{g,m-1/T}(V)
, they constructed a Verma-type
g
-twisted admissible
V
-module
\bar{M}_{m}(U)
. Although
\bar{M}_{m}(U)
is naturally
(1/T)\mathbb{N}
-graded, the explicit structure of its homogeneous components remained unclear. To resolve this, Dong and Jiang developed a bimodule theory in
[
2
]
. For any
n,m\in(1/T)\mathbb{N}
, they constructed an
A_{g,n}(V)
–
A_{g,m}(V)
-bimodule
A_{g,n,m}(V)
, satisfying
A_{g,n,n}(V)=A_{g,n}(V)
, thereby extending Zhu’s algebra. Given an
A_{g,m}(V)
-module
U
, they showed that the induced module

\bigoplus_{n\in(1/T)\mathbb{N}}A_{g,n,m}(V)\otimes_{A_{g,m}(V)}U

is a Verma-type
g
-twisted admissible
V
-module isomorphic to
\bar{M}_{m}(U)
.

In Lie algebra representation theory, the universal enveloping algebra serves as a fundamental structural and computational tool. Analogously, for a vertex operator algebra
V
equipped with a finite-order automorphism
g
, there exists a weak analogue of the universal enveloping algebra, denoted
U(V[g])
, such that every weak
g
-twisted
V
-module naturally becomes a
U(V[g])
-module. The algebras
A_{g,n}(V)
and bimodules
A_{g,n,m}(V)
are deeply connected to this universal enveloping algebra. Frenkel and Zhu
[
8
]
observed that Zhu’s algebra
A(V)
can be realized as a certain subquotient of
U(V[1])
. This perspective was later extended: in the untwisted case (
g=\mathrm{id}_{V}
), it was shown in
[
12
]
that each
A_{g,n}(V)
with
n\in\mathbb{N}
arises as a subquotient of
U(V[1])
, and
[
10
]
generalized this result to arbitrary finite-order automorphisms
g
. Furthermore, the bimodule
A_{1,n,m}(V)
was also identified as a subquotient of
U(V[1])
(see
[
9
]
). Most recently, in collaboration with Han and Xiao
[
11
]
, the author proved that for a general finite-order automorphism
g
, the
A_{g,n}(V)
–
A_{g,m}(V)
-bimodule
A_{g,n,m}(V)
is likewise a subquotient of the universal enveloping algebra
U(V[g])
.

For parallel developments in the context of vertex operator superalgebras, we refer the reader to
[
14
,
7
,
19
,
21
]
.

Li
[
16
]
constructed a family of associative algebras
\tilde{A}_{n}(V)
for a weak quantum vertex algebra
V
equipped with a constant
\mathcal{S}
-map, where ordinary vertex algebras satisfy these conditions. He introduced the notion of
\mathbb{N}
-graded
\phi
-coordinated
V
-modules and, under suitable assumptions, established a bijection between equivalence classes of irreducible
\mathbb{N}
-graded
\phi
-coordinated
V
-modules and isomorphism classes of irreducible
\tilde{A}_{0}(V)
-modules.

We now turn to the more general setting of vertex algebras. Let
V
be a vertex algebra and
g
an automorphism of
V
of order
T
. For any
n\in(1/T)\mathbb{N}
, the author constructed in
[
20
]
an associative algebra
\tilde{A}_{g,n}(V)
, which reduces to Li’s
\tilde{A}_{n}(V)
when
g=\operatorname{id}_{V}
. When
V
is a vertex operator algebra,
\tilde{A}_{g,n}(V)
is isomorphic to the algebra
A_{g,n}(V)
introduced by Dong, Li, and Mason in
[
5
]
. Combining this with results from
[
5
]
, the author further proved that the notions of
g
-rationality and
g
-regularity for vertex operator algebras are independent of the choice of conformal vector.

Motivated by Li’s work
[
16
]
, we introduce the notion of
(1/T)\mathbb{N}
-graded
g
-twisted
\phi
-coordinated
V
-modules and establish a bijection between simple
\tilde{A}_{g}(V)
-modules and irreducible
(1/T)\mathbb{N}
-graded
g
-twisted
\phi
-coordinated
V
-modules, where
\tilde{A}_{g}(V)=\tilde{A}_{g,0}(V)
. In this process, for any
\tilde{A}_{g}(V)
-module

, we construct a Verma-type

-graded
g
-twisted
\phi
-coordinated
V
-module

.

We construct the universal enveloping algebra

using the

-twisted

-twisted
\phi
-coordinated
V

-module. Moreover, we prove that the associative algebra

U(V[g])
.

For any
n,m\in(1/T)\mathbb{N}
, we construct an
\tilde{A}_{g,n}(V)
–
\tilde{A}_{g,m}(V)
-bimodule
\tilde{A}_{g,n,m}(V)
. When
V
is a vertex operator algebra, we show that each
\tilde{A}_{g,n,m}(V)
is isomorphic to the
A_{g,n}(V)
–
A_{g,m}(V)
-bimodule
A_{g,n,m}(V)
constructed by Dong and Jiang
[
2
]
. By combining the Zhu-type correspondence theorem from

-twisted

g
-twisted

-coordinated
V

Preliminaries

In this section, we review the basic theory of vertex algebras and
g
-twisted
\phi
-coordinated
V

Definition 2.1
.

A
vertex algebra
is a vector space
V
equipped with a linear map

\displaystyle Y(\cdot,x)\colon V

\displaystyle\to(\operatorname{End}V)[[x,x^{-1}]]

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle\mapsto Y(v,x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}v_{n}x^{-n-1},

and a distinguished vector
\mathbf{1}\in V
, called the
vacuum vector
, satisfying the following axioms for all
u,v\in V
:

(1)

Y(\mathbf{1},z)=\operatorname{id}_{V}
, and
u_{n}\mathbf{1}=\delta_{n,-1}u
for all
n\geq-1
;

(2)

u_{n}v=0
for all sufficiently large
n
;

(3)
the
Jacobi identity
:

\begin{gathered}z_{0}^{-1}\delta\!\left(\frac{z_{1}-z_{2}}{z_{0}}\right)Y(u,z_{1})Y(v,z_{2})-z_{0}^{-1}\delta\!\left(\frac{z_{2}-z_{1}}{-z_{0}}\right)Y(v,z_{2})Y(u,z_{1})\\
=z_{2}^{-1}\delta\!\left(\frac{z_{1}-z_{0}}{z_{2}}\right)Y(Y(u,z_{0})v,z_{2}).\end{gathered}

Let
V
be a vertex algebra. Define a linear operator
\mathcal{D}\colon V\to V
by

\mathcal{D}(v)=v_{-2}\mathbf{1}=\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{d}{dx}Y(v,x)\mathbf{1},\qquad\text{for all }v\in V.

It follows from
[
15
]
that for any
u,v\in V
, the following identities hold:

(2.1)

\displaystyle[\mathcal{D},Y(v,x)]

\displaystyle=Y(\mathcal{D}v,x)=\frac{d}{dx}Y(v,x),

(2.2)

\displaystyle Y(u,x)v

\displaystyle=e^{x\mathcal{D}}Y(v,-x)u,

(2.3)

\displaystyle(x_{1}-x_{2})^{k}Y(u,x_{1})Y(v,x_{2})

\displaystyle=(x_{1}-x_{2})^{k}Y(v,x_{2})Y(u,x_{1}),

where
k
is a nonnegative integer depending on
u
and
v
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(V,Y,\mathbf{1})
be a vertex algebra. A linear isomorphism
g
of
V
is called an
automorphism
of
V
if it satisfies

g(\mathbf{1})=\mathbf{1}\quad\text{and}\quad g(Y(u,z)v)=Y(g(u),z)g(v)\quad\text{for all }u,v\in V.

Let
V
be a vertex algebra, and fix
g
to be an automorphism of
V
of finite order
T
. Denote the imaginary unit by
\sqrt{-1}
. Then
V
admits the eigenspace decomposition with respect to the action of
g
:

V=\bigoplus_{r=0}^{T-1}V^{r},\quad\text{where }V^{r}=\left\{v\in V\mid gv=e^{-2\pi\sqrt{-1}r/T}v\right\}.

In what follows, whenever we write
V^{r}
, we always assume
r\in\{0,1,\ldots,T-1\}
. Note that
g
commutes with
\mathcal{D}
, i.e.,
g\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{D}g
, which implies
\mathcal{D}(V^{r})\subset V^{r}
for all
r
.

Definition 2.3
.

A
g
-twisted
\phi
-coordinated
V
-module
is a vector space
W
equipped with a linear map

Y_{W}(\cdot,x):V\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}\!\left(W,W\!\left(\left(x^{1/T}\right)\right)\right)\subset(\operatorname{End}W)\!\left[\left[x^{1/T},x^{-1/T}\right]\right]

satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

Y_{W}(\mathbf{1},x)=\operatorname{id}_{W}
(the identity operator on
W
);

(2)

Y_{W}(gv,x)=\lim_{x^{1/T}\rightarrow\omega_{T}^{-1}x^{1/T}}Y_{W}(v,x)
for any
v\in V
,
where
\omega_{T}=e^{-2\pi\sqrt{-1}/T}
;

(3)

for any
u,v\in V
, there exists a nonnegative integer
k
such that

\displaystyle\left(x_{1}-x_{2}\right)^{k}Y_{W}\left(u,x_{1}\right)Y_{W}\left(v,x_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\in\operatorname{Hom}\!\left(W,W\!\left(\left(x_{1}^{1/T},x_{2}^{1/T}\right)\right)\right),

\displaystyle x_{2}^{k}\left(e^{x_{0}}-1\right)^{k}Y_{W}\left(Y(u,{x_{0}})v,x_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\left.\left(\left(x_{1}-x_{2}\right)^{k}Y_{W}\left(u,x_{1}\right)Y_{W}\left(v,x_{2}\right)\right)\right|_{x_{1}^{1/T}=\left(x_{2}e^{x_{0}}\right)^{1/T}}.

.

In the above definition, Axiom (2) is equivalent to the condition that

for any

and

From

.

In the definition of a

Suppose
(W,Y_{W})

\phi
-coordinated

,

Applying

Cancelling the common factor

Conversely, assume weak

-twisted

and

For any

\displaystyle(z+1)^{\,l+r/T}(zx_{2})^{k}Y_{W}\!\big(u,(z+1)x_{2}\big)Y_{W}(v,x_{2})w

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle(1+z)^{\,l+r/T}(zx_{2})^{k}Y_{W}\!\big(Y(u,\log(1+z))v,x_{2}\big)w.

The right-hand side involves only nonnegative powers of

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Res}_{x_{1}}\operatorname{Res}_{z}x_{2}x_{1}^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{(z+1)x_{2}}{x_{1}}\right)((z+1)x_{2})^{l+r/T+j}(zx_{2})^{k}Y_{W}(u,(z+1)x_{2})Y(v,x_{2})w

This implies

(2.7)

\displaystyle x_{1}^{\,l+r/T}(x_{1}-x_{2})^{k}Y_{W}(u,x_{1})Y_{W}(v,x_{2})w\in W((x_{2}^{1/T}))[[x_{1}]].

Let

Proof.

Let

,

, and

\displaystyle u_{m}v_{n}w

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Res}_{z}\operatorname{Res}_{x_{1}}\operatorname{Res}_{x_{2}}x_{1}^{m}x_{2}^{n+1}(x_{2}z)^{-1}\delta\!\left(\frac{x_{1}-x_{2}}{x_{2}z}\right)Y_{W}(u,x_{1})Y_{W}(v,x_{2})w

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Res}_{z}\operatorname{Res}_{x_{2}}(z+1)^{m-l-r/T}x_{2}^{m+n+1}\Big((1+z)^{l+r/T}Y_{W}\!\big(Y(u,\log(1+z))v,x_{2}\big)w\Big).

is already spanned by elements

,

, and

.

3.
\tilde{A}_{g,n}(V)\!-\!\tilde{A}_{g,m}(V)
-bimodule
\tilde{A}_{g,n,m}(V)

Let
V
be a vertex algebra, and let
g
be an automorphism of
V
of finite order
T
.
For
k,l\in\{0,1,\ldots,T-1\}
, define

\delta_{k}(l)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }k\geq l,\\
0,&\text{if }k<l,\end{cases}\qquad\text{and set }\delta_{k}(T)=0.

Every
n\in(1/T)\mathbb{Z}
admits a unique decomposition

n=\lfloor n\rfloor+\bar{n}/T,

where
\bar{n}\in\{0,1,\dots,T-1\}
and
\lfloor\cdot\rfloor
denotes the floor function.
Let
u\in V^{r}
,
v\in V
, and
m,n,p\in(1/T)\mathbb{Z}
. The product
\bullet_{g,m,p}^{\,n}
on
V
is defined by

\displaystyle u\bullet_{g,m,p}^{\,n}v

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor p\rfloor}(-1)^{i}\binom{\lfloor m\rfloor+\lfloor n\rfloor-\lfloor p\rfloor-1+\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\delta_{\bar{n}}(T-r)+i}{i}

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\operatorname{Res}_{x}\frac{e^{x(\lfloor m\rfloor+\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+r/T)}}{(e^{x}-1)^{\lfloor m\rfloor+\lfloor n\rfloor-\lfloor p\rfloor+\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\delta_{\bar{n}}(T-r)+i}}Y(u,x)v

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor p\rfloor}(-1)^{i}\binom{\lfloor m\rfloor+\lfloor n\rfloor-\lfloor p\rfloor-1+\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\delta_{\bar{n}}(T-r)+i}{i}

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\operatorname{Res}_{y}\frac{(1+y)^{-1+\lfloor m\rfloor+\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+r/T}}{y^{\lfloor m\rfloor+\lfloor n\rfloor-\lfloor p\rfloor+\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\delta_{\bar{n}}(T-r)+i}}Y\bigl(u,\log(1+y)\bigr)v,

if
\bar{p}-\bar{n}\equiv r\pmod{T}
and
m,n,p\geq 0
; otherwise we set
u\bullet_{g,m,p}^{\,n}v=0
.
Set

\bar{\bullet}_{g,m}^{\,n}:=\bullet_{g,m,n}^{\,n},

\bullet_{g,m}^{\,n}:=\bullet_{g,m,m}^{\,n}
,

\bullet_{g,n}:=\bullet_{g,n}^{\,n}=\bar{\bullet}_{g,n}^{\,n}
and
\bullet_{g}:=\bullet_{g,0}
.
When
r\neq 0
, one has
u\bar{\bullet}_{g,m}^{\,n}v=0
. If
r=0
, then

u\bar{\bullet}_{g,m}^{\,n}v=\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor n\rfloor}(-1)^{i}\binom{\lfloor m\rfloor+i}{i}\operatorname{Res}_{y}\frac{(1+y)^{\lfloor m\rfloor}}{y^{\lfloor m\rfloor+i+1}}Y\bigl(u,\log(1+y)\bigr)v.

In particular, for any
v\in V
,

\mathbf{1}\bar{\bullet}_{g,m}^{\,n}v=v.

The product
\bullet_{g,m}^{\,n}
vanishes unless
\bar{m}-\bar{n}\equiv r\pmod{T}
. In this case,

-1+\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\delta_{\bar{n}}(T-r)=0,

and consequently

u\bullet_{g,m}^{\,n}v=\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor m\rfloor}(-1)^{i}\binom{\lfloor n\rfloor+i}{i}\operatorname{Res}_{y}\frac{(1+y)^{-1+\lfloor m\rfloor+\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+r/T}}{y^{\lfloor n\rfloor+i+1}}Y\bigl(u,\log(1+y)\bigr)v.

For
m,n\in(1/T)\mathbb{N}
, define

\tilde{O}_{g,n,m}^{\prime}(V):=\operatorname{span}\{u\diamond_{g,m}^{\,n}v\mid u,v\in V\}+L_{n,m}(V),

where

L_{n,m}(V):=\operatorname{span}\{(\mathcal{D}+m-n)u\mid u\in V\},

and for
u\in V^{r}
,
v\in V
,

\displaystyle u\diamond_{g,m}^{\,n}v

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Res}_{x}\frac{e^{x(\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\lfloor m\rfloor+r/T)}}{(e^{x}-1)^{\lfloor m\rfloor+\lfloor n\rfloor+\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\delta_{\bar{n}}(T-r)+1}}Y(u,x)v

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Res}_{y}\frac{(1+y)^{-1+\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\lfloor m\rfloor+r/T}}{y^{\lfloor m\rfloor+\lfloor n\rfloor+\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\delta_{\bar{n}}(T-r)+1}}Y\bigl(u,\log(1+y)\bigr)v.

Set
u\diamond_{g,n}v:=u\diamond_{g,n}^{\,n}v
,
u\diamond_{g}v:=\diamond_{g,0}v
,
\tilde{O}_{g,n}(V):=\tilde{O}_{g,n,n}^{\prime}(V)
and
\tilde{O}_{g}(V):=\tilde{O}_{g,0}(V)
.
Finally, define the quotient space

\tilde{A}_{g,n}(V):=V/\tilde{O}_{g,n}(V)\quad\text{ and }\quad\tilde{A}_{g}(V):=\tilde{A}_{g,0}(V).

In our previous work
[
20
]
, the following result was proved:

Theorem 3.1
.

The product
\bullet_{g,n}
induces the structure of an associative algebra on
\tilde{A}_{g,n}(V)=V/\tilde{O}_{g,n}(V)
, with identity element
\mathbf{1}+\tilde{O}_{g,n}(V)
.

The proof of the following lemma is analogous to that of
[
20
, Lemma 3.1]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
u\in V^{r}
,
v\in V
, and let
k,s
be integers with
k\geq s\geq 0
. Then

\operatorname{Res}_{x}\frac{e^{x(\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\lfloor m\rfloor+r/T+s)}}{(e^{x}-1)^{\lfloor m\rfloor+\lfloor n\rfloor+\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\delta_{\bar{n}}(T-r)+1+k}}\,Y(u,x)v\in\tilde{O}_{g,n,m}^{\prime}(V).

Proof.

We begin by making the change of variables
y=e^{x}-1
, then

\displaystyle\quad\,\operatorname{Res}_{x}\frac{e^{x(\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\lfloor m\rfloor+r/T+s)}}{(e^{x}-1)^{\lfloor m\rfloor+\lfloor n\rfloor+\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\delta_{\bar{n}}(T-r)+1+k}}Y(u,x)v

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Res}_{y}\frac{(1+y)^{\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\lfloor m\rfloor+r/T+s-1}}{y^{\lfloor m\rfloor+\lfloor n\rfloor+\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\delta_{\bar{n}}(T-r)+1+k}}Y(u,\log(1+y))v

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{s}\binom{s}{i}\operatorname{Res}_{y}\frac{(1+y)^{\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\lfloor m\rfloor+r/T-1}}{y^{\lfloor m\rfloor+\lfloor n\rfloor+\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\delta_{\bar{n}}(T-r)+1+k-i}}Y(u,\log(1+y))v

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{s}\binom{s}{i}\operatorname{Res}_{x}\frac{e^{x(\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\lfloor m\rfloor+r/T)}}{(e^{x}-1)^{\lfloor m\rfloor+\lfloor n\rfloor+\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\delta_{\bar{n}}(T-r)+1+k-i}}Y(u,x)v.

Hence, it suffices to prove the statement for the case
s=0
and arbitrary
k\geq 0
. We proceed by induction on
k
.
The base case
k=0
follows directly from the definition of
\tilde{O}_{g,n,m}^{\prime}(V)
.
Assume the claim holds for all integers
k\leq l
. Consider
k=l+1
. Using the inductive hypothesis and the identity (
2.1
), we compute:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Res}_{x}\frac{e^{x(\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\lfloor m\rfloor+r/T)}}{(e^{x}-1)^{\lfloor m\rfloor+\lfloor n\rfloor+\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\delta_{\bar{n}}(T-r)+1+l}}Y(\mathcal{D}u,x)v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Res}_{x}\frac{e^{x(\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\lfloor m\rfloor+r/T)}}{(e^{x}-1)^{\lfloor m\rfloor+\lfloor n\rfloor+\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\delta_{\bar{n}}(T-r)+1+l}}\frac{d}{dx}Y(u,x)v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\operatorname{Res}_{x}\left(\frac{d}{dx}\frac{e^{x(\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\lfloor m\rfloor+r/T)}}{(e^{x}-1)^{\lfloor m\rfloor+\lfloor n\rfloor+\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\delta_{\bar{n}}(T-r)+1+l}}\right)Y(u,x)v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\lfloor m\rfloor+r/T)\operatorname{Res}_{x}\frac{e^{x(\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\lfloor m\rfloor+r/T)}}{(e^{x}-1)^{\lfloor m\rfloor+\lfloor n\rfloor+\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\delta_{\bar{n}}(T-r)+1+l}}Y(u,x)v

\displaystyle+(\lfloor m\rfloor+\lfloor n\rfloor+\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\delta_{\bar{n}}(T-r)+1+l)\operatorname{Res}_{x}\frac{e^{x(\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\lfloor m\rfloor+r/T+1)}}{(e^{x}-1)^{\lfloor m\rfloor+\lfloor n\rfloor+\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\delta_{\bar{n}}(T-r)+2+l}}Y(u,x)v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\lfloor n\rfloor+\delta_{\bar{n}}(T-r)+1-r/T+l)\operatorname{Res}_{x}\frac{e^{x(\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\lfloor m\rfloor+r/T)}}{(e^{x}-1)^{\lfloor m\rfloor+\lfloor n\rfloor+\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\delta_{\bar{n}}(T-r)+1+l}}Y(u,x)v

\displaystyle+(\lfloor m\rfloor+\lfloor n\rfloor+\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\delta_{\bar{n}}(T-r)+1+l)\operatorname{Res}_{x}\frac{e^{x(\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\lfloor m\rfloor+r/T)}}{(e^{x}-1)^{\lfloor m\rfloor+\lfloor n\rfloor+\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\delta_{\bar{n}}(T-r)+2+l}}Y(u,x)v.

By the induction hypothesis, the first term on the right-hand side belongs to
\tilde{O}_{g,n,m}^{\prime}(V)
. Since the left-hand side also lies in
\tilde{O}_{g,n,m}^{\prime}(V)
(as
\mathcal{D}u\in V^{r}
), it follows that the second term must likewise belong to
\tilde{O}_{g,n,m}^{\prime}(V)
. This completes the inductive step and establishes the lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

The following statements hold:

(1)
For any
u,v\in V
, we have

Y(u,x)v\equiv e^{x(n-m)}\,Y(v,-x)u\bmod L_{n,m}(V).

(2)
If
r\not\equiv\bar{m}-\bar{n}\pmod{T}
, then
V^{r}\subseteq\tilde{O}_{g,n,m}^{\prime}(V)
.

(3)
Let
u\in V^{r}
and
v\in V^{s}
. Suppose that

\bar{p}-\bar{n}\equiv r\pmod{T},\bar{m}-\bar{p}\equiv s\pmod{T},

and that
m+n-p\geq 0
. Then

u\bullet_{g,m,p}^{\,n}v-v\bullet_{g,m,m+n-p}^{\,n}u-\operatorname{Res}_{x}(1+x)^{-1+p-n}\,Y(u,\log(1+x))v\in L_{n,m}(V).

Proof.

(1) By definition,
(\mathcal{D}+m-n)u\in L_{n,m}(V)
. Combining this with the skew-symmetry identity (
2.2
) yields

Y(u,x)v=e^{x\mathcal{D}}Y(v,-x)u\equiv e^{x(n-m)}Y(v,-x)u\bmod L_{n,m}(V),

as required.

(2) Let
u\in V^{r}
with
r\not\equiv\bar{m}-\bar{n}\pmod{T}
. Then

\displaystyle u\diamond_{g,m}^{\,n}\mathbf{1}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Res}_{y}\frac{(1+y)^{-1+\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\lfloor m\rfloor+r/T}}{y^{\lfloor m\rfloor+\lfloor n\rfloor+\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\delta_{\bar{n}}(T-r)+1}}\,Y(u,\log(1+y))\mathbf{1}

\displaystyle\equiv\operatorname{Res}_{y}\frac{(1+y)^{\delta_{\bar{n}}(r)+\lfloor n\rfloor+r/T-1+n-m}}{y^{2\lfloor n\rfloor+\delta_{\bar{n}}(r)+\delta_{\bar{n}}(T-r)+1}}\,u\bmod\tilde{O}_{g,n,m}^{\prime}(V)\qquad\text{(by part (1))}

\displaystyle=\binom{\delta_{\bar{n}}(r)+\lfloor n\rfloor+r/T-1+n-m}{\,2\lfloor n\rfloor+\delta_{\bar{n}}(r)+\delta_{\bar{n}}(T-r)\,}u.

Since
r\not\equiv\bar{m}-\bar{n}\pmod{T}
, the binomial coefficient above is nonzero. On the other hand, by definition we have
u\diamond_{g,m}^{\,n}\mathbf{1}\in\tilde{O}_{g,n,m}^{\prime}(V)
. It follows that
u\in\tilde{O}_{g,n,m}^{\prime}(V)
, which completes the proof of part (2).

(3) From the assumptions
\bar{p}-\bar{n}\equiv r\pmod{T}
and
\bar{m}-\bar{p}\equiv s\pmod{T}
, one readily deduces that

-1+\delta_{\bar{m}}(s)+\delta_{\bar{p}}(T-s)=0,-1+\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\delta_{\bar{n}}(T-r)=\varepsilon,

where

\varepsilon=\begin{cases}\phantom{-}1,&\text{if }\bar{m}+\bar{n}-\bar{p}\geq T,\\
\phantom{-}0,&\text{if }0\leq\bar{m}+\bar{n}-\bar{p}<T,\\
-1,&\text{if }\bar{m}+\bar{n}-\bar{p}<0.\end{cases}

Hence,

\displaystyle\quad\,v\bullet_{g,\,m,\,m+n-p}^{\,n}u

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor n\rfloor+\lfloor m\rfloor-\lfloor p\rfloor+\varepsilon}(-1)^{i}\binom{\lfloor p\rfloor+i}{i}\operatorname{Res}_{z}\frac{(1+z)^{-1+\lfloor m\rfloor+\delta_{\bar{m}}(s)+s/T}}{z^{\lfloor p\rfloor+i+1}}\,Y(v,\log(1+z))\,u

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor n\rfloor+\lfloor m\rfloor-\lfloor p\rfloor+\varepsilon}(-1)^{i}\binom{\lfloor p\rfloor+i}{i}\operatorname{Res}_{z}\frac{(1+z)^{-1+\lfloor n\rfloor+\delta_{\bar{m}}(s)+(s-\bar{m}+\bar{n})/T}}{z^{\lfloor p\rfloor+i+1}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times Y(u,-\log(1+z))\,v\bmod L_{n,m}(V)\quad\quad\text{(by part (1))}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor n\rfloor+\lfloor m\rfloor-\lfloor p\rfloor+\varepsilon}(-1)^{\lfloor p\rfloor}\binom{\lfloor p\rfloor+i}{i}\operatorname{Res}_{z}\frac{(1+z)^{-1+p-n+i}}{z^{\lfloor p\rfloor+i+1}}\,Y(u,\log(1+z))\,v,

where the last equality follows from
(\bar{p}+s-\bar{m})/T=\delta_{\bar{p}}(T-s)
.
Since
(\bar{n}+r-\bar{p})/T=\delta_{\bar{n}}(T-r)
, we obtain

\displaystyle u\bullet_{g,\,m,\,p}^{\,n}v-v\bullet_{g,\,m,\,m+n-p}^{\,n}u\equiv\operatorname{Res}_{z}A(z)\,(1+z)^{-1+p-n}\,Y(u,\log(1+z))\,v\bmod L_{n,m}(V),

where

\displaystyle A(z)=\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor p\rfloor}(-1)^{i}\binom{k+\varepsilon+i}{i}\frac{(1+z)^{k+\varepsilon+1}}{z^{k+\varepsilon+i+1}}-\sum_{i=0}^{k+\varepsilon}(-1)^{\lfloor p\rfloor}\binom{\lfloor p\rfloor+i}{i}\frac{(1+z)^{i}}{z^{\lfloor p\rfloor+i+1}}

and
k=\lfloor n\rfloor+\lfloor m\rfloor-\lfloor p\rfloor
. The lemma now follows from
[
3
, Proposition 5.1]
.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

The following statements hold:

(1)
Let
u\in V^{r}
and
v\in V^{s}
. If
\bar{m}-\bar{p}\not\equiv s\pmod{T}
, then

u\bullet_{g,m,p}^{\,n}v\in\tilde{O}_{g,n,m}^{\prime}(V).

(2)
Let
v\in V^{s}
,
if
\bar{m}-\bar{n}\equiv s\pmod{T}
, then

v\bullet_{g,m}^{\,n}\mathbf{1}-v\in L_{n,m}(V).

Moreover,

v\bullet_{g,m}^{\,n}\mathbf{1}-v\in\tilde{O}_{g,n,m}^{\prime}(V)

for any
v\in V.

Proof.

(1) By definition, the product
u\bullet_{g,m,p}^{\,n}v
is nonzero only if
\bar{p}-\bar{n}\equiv r\pmod{T}
.
If this condition fails, then
u\bullet_{g,m,p}^{\,n}v=0\in\tilde{O}_{g,n,m}^{\prime}(V)
.
If it holds, then
u\bullet_{g,m,p}^{\,n}v\in V^{r+s}
. The assumption
\bar{m}-\bar{p}\not\equiv s\pmod{T}
implies

\bar{m}-\bar{n}=(\bar{m}-\bar{p})+(\bar{p}-\bar{n})\not\equiv s+r\pmod{T}.

Hence
r+s\not\equiv\bar{m}-\bar{n}\pmod{T}
, and by Lemma
3.3
(2) we conclude that
u\bullet_{g,m,p}^{\,n}v\in\tilde{O}_{g,n,m}^{\prime}(V)
.

(2) Applying Lemma
3.3
(3) with
u=\mathbf{1}\in V^{0}
and
p=n
, and using the identity
\mathbf{1}\bar{\bullet}_{g,m}^{\,n}v=v
, we obtain

v\bullet_{g,m}^{\,n}\mathbf{1}-v=v\bullet_{g,m}^{\,n}\mathbf{1}-\mathbf{1}\bar{\bullet}_{g,m}^{\,n}v\in L_{n,m}(V).

Since
L_{n,m}(V)\subseteq\tilde{O}_{g,n,m}^{\prime}(V)
by definition, it follows that
v\bullet_{g,m}^{\,n}\mathbf{1}-v\in\tilde{O}_{g,n,m}^{\prime}(V)
.
Moreover, if
\bar{m}-\bar{n}\not\equiv s\pmod{T}
, then by part (2) of Lemma
3.3
,
u\in\tilde{O}_{g,n,m}^{\prime}(V)
, and since
u\bullet_{g,m}^{\,n}\mathbf{1}\in\tilde{O}_{g,n,m}^{\prime}(V)
as well, their difference lies in
\tilde{O}_{g,n,m}^{\prime}(V)
.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

We have

\tilde{O}_{g,n,m}^{\prime}(V)\,\bullet_{g,m}^{\,n}\,V\subseteq\tilde{O}_{g,n,m}^{\prime}(V),\qquad V\,\bar{\bullet}_{g,m}^{\,n}\,\tilde{O}_{g,n,m}^{\prime}(V)\subseteq\tilde{O}_{g,n,m}^{\prime}(V).

Proof.

The proof is analogous to that of
[
20
, Lemma 3.3]
.
We consider the expressions

((\mathcal{D}+m-n)u)\bullet_{g,m}^{n}v\quad\text{and}\quad v\,\bar{\bullet}_{g,m}^{n}\,((\mathcal{D}+m-n)u).

Assume that
u\in V^{r}
and
v\in V^{s}
, where
\bar{m}-\bar{n}\equiv r\pmod{T}
and
s=0
, by the definition of
\bullet_{g,m}^{n}
,
\bar{\bullet}_{g,m}^{n}
and Corollary
3.4
(1).
Under these assumptions, we have

-1+\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\delta_{\bar{n}}(T-r)=0
and
(\bar{n}+r-\bar{m})/T=\delta_{\bar{n}}(T-r)
.
Then

\displaystyle((\mathcal{D}+m-n)u)\bullet_{g,m}^{n}v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor m\rfloor}(-1)^{i}\binom{\lfloor n\rfloor+i}{i}\operatorname{Res}_{y}\frac{(1+y)^{-1+\lfloor m\rfloor+\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+r/T}}{y^{\lfloor n\rfloor+i+1}}Y(\mathcal{D}u,\log(1+y))v+(m-n)u\bullet_{g,m}^{n}v

\displaystyle=

Using Corollary
3.4
and the definition of
\tilde{O}_{g,n,m}^{\prime\prime\prime}(V)
, we obtain

(3.5)

V\bullet_{g,m,p}^{\,n}\tilde{O}_{g,p,m}^{\prime}(V)\subseteq\bigl(V\bullet_{g,m,p}^{\,n}\tilde{O}_{g,p,m}^{\prime}(V)\bigr)\bullet_{g,m}^{\,n}V+\tilde{O}_{g,n,m}^{\prime}(V)\subseteq\tilde{O}_{g,n,m}(V).

Combining (
3.2
) and (
3.5
), it suffices to verify that

(3.6)

V\bullet_{g,m,p}^{\,n}\Bigl(\tilde{O}_{g,p,m}^{\prime\prime}(V)+\bigl(V\bullet_{g,p_{1},p_{2}}^{\,p}\tilde{O}_{g,p_{2},p_{1}}^{\prime}(V)\bigr)\bullet_{g,m,p_{1}}^{\,p}V\Bigr)\subseteq\tilde{O}_{g,n,m}(V)

and

(3.7)

\Bigl(\tilde{O}_{g,n,p}^{\prime\prime}(V)+\bigl(V\bullet_{g,p_{1},p_{2}}^{\,n}\tilde{O}_{g,p_{2},p_{1}}^{\prime}(V)\bigr)\bullet_{g,p,p_{1}}^{\,n}V\Bigr)\bullet_{g,m,p}^{\,n}V\subseteq\tilde{O}_{g,n,m}(V)

for all
p_{1},p_{2},p\in(1/T)\mathbb{N}
. We prove only (
3.6
); the proof of (
3.7
) is analogous. Consider a typical generator of
\tilde{O}_{g,p,m}^{\prime\prime}(V)
:

u\bullet_{g,m,p_{3}}^{\,p}\left(\bigl(a\bullet_{g,p_{1},p_{2}}^{\,p_{3}}b\bigr)\bullet_{g,m,p_{1}}^{\,p_{3}}c-a\bullet_{g,m,p_{2}}^{\,p_{3}}\bigl(b\bullet_{g,m,p_{1}}^{\,p_{2}}c\bigr)\right).

Then by (
3.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle v\bullet_{g,m,p}^{\,n}\Bigl(u\bullet_{g,m,p_{3}}^{\,p}\bigl((a\bullet_{g,p_{1},p_{2}}^{\,p_{3}}b)\bullet_{g,m,p_{1}}^{\,p_{3}}c-a\bullet_{g,m,p_{2}}^{\,p_{3}}(b\bullet_{g,m,p_{1}}^{\,p_{2}}c)\bigr)\Bigr)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\bigl(v\bullet_{g,p_{3},p}^{\,n}u\bigr)\bullet_{g,m,p_{3}}^{\,n}\Bigl((a\bullet_{g,p_{1},p_{2}}^{\,p_{3}}b)\bullet_{g,m,p_{1}}^{\,p_{3}}c-a\bullet_{g,m,p_{2}}^{\,p_{3}}(b\bullet_{g,m,p_{1}}^{\,p_{2}}c)\Bigr)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\,0\bmod\tilde{O}_{g,n,m}(V).

Thus we have

V\bullet_{g,m,p}^{\,n}\tilde{O}_{g,p,m}^{\prime\prime}(V)\subseteq\tilde{O}_{g,n,m}(V)
.
By the definition of
\tilde{O}_{g,n,m}(V)
together with (
3.3
) and (
3.4
), we have

\displaystyle V\bullet_{g,m,p}^{\,n}\Bigl(\bigl(V\bullet_{g,p_{1},p_{2}}^{\,p}\tilde{O}_{g,p_{2},p_{1}}^{\prime}(V)\bigr)\bullet_{g,m,p_{1}}^{\,p}V\Bigr)

\displaystyle\subseteq

\displaystyle V\bullet_{g,m,p}^{\,n}\Bigl(V\bullet_{g,m,p_{2}}^{\,p}\bigl(\tilde{O}_{g,p_{2},p_{1}}^{\prime}(V)\bullet_{g,m,p_{1}}^{\,p_{2}}V\bigr)\Bigr)+\tilde{O}_{g,n,m}(V)

\displaystyle\subseteq

\displaystyle\bigl(V\bullet_{g,p_{2},p}^{\,n}V\bigr)\bullet_{g,m,p_{2}}^{\,n}\bigl(\tilde{O}_{g,p_{2},p_{1}}^{\prime}(V)\bullet_{g,m,p_{1}}^{\,p_{2}}V\bigr)+\tilde{O}_{g,n,m}(V)

\displaystyle\subseteq

\displaystyle\tilde{O}_{g,n,m}(V).

This completes the proof.
∎

4.
The functor
\tilde{\Omega}_{n}

Let
(W,Y_{W})
be a
g
-twisted
\phi
-coordinated
V
-module.
For any
i\in(1/T)\mathbb{Z}
and
u\in V
, define

o_{i}(u)=u_{-i-1}\in\operatorname{End}(W)
and
o(u)=o_{0}(u).

For
n\in(1/T)\mathbb{N}
, set

\tilde{\Omega}_{n}(W)=\left\{w\in W\;\middle|\;o_{m}(v)w=0\text{ for all }v\in V\text{ and }m\in(1/T)\mathbb{Z}\text{ with }m<-n\right\}.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
u\in V^{r}
,
v\in V^{s}
, and
m,p,n\in(1/T)\mathbb{N}
.
Then for any
w\in\tilde{\Omega}_{m}(W)
, we have

o_{\,n-m}\!\bigl(u\bullet_{g,m,p}^{\,n}v\bigr)w\;=\;o_{\,n-p}(u)\,o_{\,p-m}(v)w.

Proof.

If
\bar{m}-\bar{p}\not\equiv s\pmod{T}
and
\bar{p}-\bar{n}\equiv r\pmod{T}
,
then
o_{p-m}(v)=0
and

o_{\,n-m}\!\bigl(u\bullet_{g,m,p}^{\,n}v\bigr)w=0.

If
\bar{p}-\bar{n}\not\equiv r\pmod{T}
, then we have
u\bullet_{g,m,p}^{\,n}v=0
and
o_{n-p}(u)=0
.

Finally, we consider the case where

\bar{p}-\bar{n}\equiv r
and
\bar{m}-\bar{p}\equiv s\pmod{T}
. Set

f(r)=-1+\lfloor m\rfloor+\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+r/T,\quad h(r)=\lfloor m\rfloor+\lfloor n\rfloor-\lfloor p\rfloor-1+\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\delta_{\bar{n}}(T-r).

For any
w\in\tilde{\Omega}_{m}(W)
, we have

\displaystyle\quad o_{n-m}(u\bullet_{g,m,p}^{n}v)w

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor p\rfloor}(-1)^{i}\binom{h(r)+i}{i}\operatorname{Res}_{x_{2}}\operatorname{Res}_{y_{0}}x_{2}^{-n+m-1}\frac{(1+y_{0})^{f(r)}}{y_{0}^{h(r)+1+i}}Y_{W}(Y(u,\log(1+y_{0}))v,x_{2})w

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor p\rfloor}(-1)^{i}\binom{h(r)+i}{i}\operatorname{Res}_{x_{1}}\operatorname{Res}_{x_{2}}\operatorname{Res}_{y_{0}}x_{2}^{-n+m-1}\frac{(1+y_{0})^{f(r)}}{y_{0}^{h(r)+1+i}}\left(\frac{x_{2}(1+y_{0})}{x_{1}}\right)^{-r/T}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\times x_{1}^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{x_{2}(1+y_{0})}{x_{1}}\right)\left(\frac{x_{2}(1+y_{0})}{x_{1}}\right)^{r/T}Y_{W}(Y(u,\log(1+y_{0}))v,x_{2})w

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor p\rfloor}(-1)^{i}\binom{h(r)+i}{i}\operatorname{Res}_{x_{1}}\operatorname{Res}_{x_{2}}\operatorname{Res}_{y_{0}}x_{2}^{-n+m-1}\frac{(1+y_{0})^{f(r)}}{y_{0}^{h(r)+1+i}}\left(\frac{x_{2}(1+y_{0})}{x_{1}}\right)^{-r/T}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\times\left(\left(x_{2}y_{0}\right)^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{x_{1}-x_{2}}{x_{2}y_{0}}\right)Y_{W}\left(u,x_{1}\right)Y_{W}\left(v,x_{2}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\left.-\left(x_{2}y_{0}\right)^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{x_{2}-x_{1}}{-x_{2}y_{0}}\right)Y_{W}\left(v,x_{2}\right)Y_{W}\left(u,x_{1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor p\rfloor}(-1)^{i}\binom{h(r)+i}{i}\operatorname{Res}_{x_{2}}\operatorname{Res}_{x_{1}}\left(\frac{x_{1}^{f(r)}x_{2}^{m-1-(\bar{n}+r)/T-\lfloor p\rfloor+\delta_{\bar{n}}(T-r)+i}}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{h(r)+1+i}}Y_{W}(u,x_{1})Y_{W}(v,x_{2})w\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad-\frac{x_{1}^{f(r)}x_{2}^{m-1-(\bar{n}+r)/T-\lfloor p\rfloor+\delta_{\bar{n}}(T-r)+i}}{(-x_{2}+x_{1})^{h(r)+1+i}}Y_{W}(v,x_{2})Y_{W}(u,x_{1})w\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor p\rfloor}(-1)^{i}\binom{h(r)+i}{i}\operatorname{Res}_{x_{2}}\operatorname{Res}_{x_{1}}\frac{x_{1}^{f(r)}x_{2}^{-p+m-1+i}}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{h(r)+1+i}}Y_{W}(u,x_{1})Y_{W}(v,x_{2})w

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor p\rfloor}\sum_{j\geq 0}(-1)^{i+j}\binom{h(r)+i}{i}\binom{-(h(r)+1+i)}{j}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\times\operatorname{Res}_{x_{2}}\operatorname{Res}_{x_{1}}x_{1}^{-n+p-1-i-j}x_{2}^{-p+m-1+i+j}Y_{W}(u,x_{1})Y_{W}(v,x_{2})w

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor p\rfloor}(-1)^{k}\sum_{i,j\geq 0,i+j=k}\binom{h(r)+i}{i}\binom{-(h(r)+1+i)}{j}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\times\operatorname{Res}_{x_{2}}\operatorname{Res}_{x_{1}}x_{1}^{-n+p-1-k}x_{2}^{-p+m-1+k}Y_{W}(u,x_{1})Y_{W}(v,x_{2})w.

It is enough to show that

\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}\binom{l+i}{i}\binom{l+k}{k-i}=0

for any
l\in\mathbb{Z}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, which follows from an easy calculation:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}\binom{l+i}{i}\binom{l+k}{k-i}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}\binom{l+k}{k}\binom{k}{i}=\binom{l+k}{k}\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}\binom{k}{i}=0.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
m,n\in(1/T)\mathbb{N}
.
Then for every
u\in\tilde{O}_{g,n,m}(V)
, the operator
o_{\,n-m}(u)
vanishes on
\tilde{\Omega}_{m}(W)
.
In particular, for every
u\in\tilde{O}_{g,n}(V)
, we have
o(u)=0
on
\tilde{\Omega}_{n}(W)
.

Proof.

For any
u\in V
, Lemma

\displaystyle o_{\,n-m}(\mathcal{D}u)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Res}_{x}\,x^{-n+m-1}Y_{W}(\mathcal{D}u,x)=\operatorname{Res}_{x}\,x^{-n+m}\frac{d}{dx}Y_{W}(u,x)

\displaystyle=-(m-n)\,o_{\,n-m}(u).

Consequently,

o_{\,n-m}\bigl((\mathcal{D}+m-n)u\bigr)=0.

Now let
u\in V^{r}
and
v\in V^{s}
.
We first show that

.
This is immediate when
\bar{m}-\bar{n}\not\equiv r+s\pmod{T}
.
Thus, we may assume that
\bar{m}-\bar{n}\equiv r+s\pmod{T}

For any
w\in\tilde{\Omega}_{m}(W)
, we have

\displaystyle\quad o_{n-m}(u\diamond_{g,m}^{n}v)w=(u\diamond_{g,m}^{n}v)_{-n+m-1}w

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Res}_{x_{2}}\operatorname{Res}_{y_{0}}x_{2}^{-n+m-1}\frac{(1+y_{0})^{-1+\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\lfloor m\rfloor+r/T}}{y_{0}^{\lfloor m\rfloor+\lfloor n\rfloor+\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\delta_{\bar{n}}(T-r)+1}}Y_{W}(Y(u,\log(1+y_{0}))v,x_{2})w

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Res}_{x_{2}}\operatorname{Res}_{x_{1}}\operatorname{Res}_{y_{0}}x_{2}^{-n+m-1}\frac{(1+y_{0})^{-1+\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\lfloor m\rfloor+r/T}}{y_{0}^{\lfloor m\rfloor+\lfloor n\rfloor+\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\delta_{\bar{n}}(T-r)+1}}\left(\frac{x_{2}(1+y_{0})}{x_{1}}\right)^{-r/T}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad x_{1}^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{x_{2}(1+y_{0})}{x_{1}}\right)\left(\frac{x_{2}(1+y_{0})}{x_{1}}\right)^{r/T}Y_{W}(Y(u,\log(1+y_{0}))v,x_{2})w

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Res}_{x_{2}}\operatorname{Res}_{x_{1}}\operatorname{Res}_{y_{0}}x_{2}^{-n+m-1}\frac{(1+y_{0})^{-1+\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\lfloor m\rfloor+r/T}}{y_{0}^{\lfloor m\rfloor+\lfloor n\rfloor+\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\delta_{\bar{n}}(T-r)+1}}\left(\frac{x_{2}(1+y_{0})}{x_{1}}\right)^{-r/T}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\times\left(\left(x_{2}y_{0}\right)^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{x_{1}-x_{2}}{x_{2}y_{0}}\right)Y_{W}\left(u,x_{1}\right)Y_{W}\left(v,x_{2}\right)w\right.

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\left.-\left(x_{2}y_{0}\right)^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{x_{2}-x_{1}}{-x_{2}y_{0}}\right)Y_{W}\left(v,x_{2}\right)Y_{W}\left(u,x_{1}\right)w\right)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Res}_{x_{2}}\operatorname{Res}_{x_{1}}\left(\frac{x_{1}^{-1+\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\lfloor m\rfloor+r/T}x_{2}^{m-(\bar{n}+r)/T+\delta_{\bar{n}}(T-r)}}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{\lfloor m\rfloor+\lfloor n\rfloor+\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\delta_{\bar{n}}(T-r)+1}}Y_{W}(u,x_{1})Y_{W}(v,x_{2})w\right.

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\left.-\frac{x_{1}^{-1+\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\lfloor m\rfloor+r/T}x_{2}^{m-(\bar{n}+r)/T+\delta_{\bar{n}}(T-r)}}{(-x_{2}+x_{1})^{\lfloor m\rfloor+\lfloor n\rfloor+\delta_{\bar{m}}(r)+\delta_{\bar{n}}(T-r)+1}}Y_{W}(v,x_{2})Y_{W}(u,x_{1})w\right)=0.

By applying Lemma

, we deduce that both
o_{n-m}\bigl(\tilde{O}_{g,n,m}^{\prime\prime}(V)\bigr)
and
o_{n-m}\bigl(\tilde{O}_{g,n,m}^{\prime\prime\prime}(V)\bigr)

.
∎

\phi

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
(W,Y_{W})
be a
g
-twisted
\phi
-coordinated
V
-module, and let
n\in(1/T)\mathbb{N}
.
Then
\tilde{\Omega}_{n}(W)
carries the structure of an
\tilde{A}_{g,n}(V)
-module,
where the action of
v\in V
is given by the operator

.

Proof.

We first show that
\tilde{\Omega}_{n}(W)
is closed under the action of

for every

.
Let
u\in V^{r}
,
v\in V
, and
w\in\tilde{\Omega}_{n}(W)

By the commutator formula (

), we have

.

.

4.1
and

, we have

on
\tilde{\Omega}_{n}(W)

\tilde{A}_{g,n}(V)
-module.

Proposition 4.4
.

Suppose that
M=\bigoplus_{m\in(1/T)\mathbb{N}}M(m)
is a
(1/T)\mathbb{N}
-graded
g
-twisted
\phi
-coordinated
V
-module, and let
n\in(1/T)\mathbb{N}
. Then the following hold:

(1)

\tilde{\Omega}_{n}(M)\supset\bigoplus_{n\geq i\in(1/T)\mathbb{N}}M(i)
.
If
M
is simple, then
\tilde{\Omega}_{n}(M)=\bigoplus_{n\geq i\in(1/T)\mathbb{N}}M(i).

(2)
If
M
is simple and
M(m)\neq 0
for some
m\in(1/T)\mathbb{N}
with
m\leq n
, then
M(m)
is an irreducible
\tilde{A}_{g,n}(V)
-module.

Proof.

It is straightforward to verify that
\tilde{\Omega}_{n}(M)
is a graded subspace of
M
; namely,

\tilde{\Omega}_{n}(M)=\bigoplus_{i\in(1/T)\mathbb{N}}\bigl(\tilde{\Omega}_{n}(M)\cap M(i)\bigr).

Set
\tilde{\Omega}_{n}(i):=\tilde{\Omega}_{n}(M)\cap M(i)
.
Clearly,
M(i)\subseteq\tilde{\Omega}_{n}(M)
whenever
i\leq n
, since
o_{p}(v)M(i)\subseteq M(i+p)
and
p<-n
implies
i+p<0
.

To prove part (1), it remains to show that
\tilde{\Omega}_{n}(i)=0
for all
i>n
.
Assume, to the contrary, that
\tilde{\Omega}_{n}(i)\neq 0
for some
i>n
, and choose a nonzero vector
w\in\tilde{\Omega}_{n}(i)
.
By Proposition
2.8
,
M
is generated by any nonzero vector; in particular,

M=\operatorname{span}\bigl\{u_{p}w\mid u\in V,\ p\in(1/T)\mathbb{Z}\bigr\}.

However, since
w\in\tilde{\Omega}_{n}(M)
, we have
o_{p}(v)w=0
for all
p<-n
.
Consequently,
M
has no components in degree less than
i-n>0
, implying in particular that
M(0)=0
.
Hence
\tilde{\Omega}_{n}(i)=0
for
i>n
, proving (1).

For part (2), let
U\subseteq M(m)
be a nonzero
\tilde{A}_{g,n}(V)
-submodule.
Consider the subspace

N:=\operatorname{span}\bigl\{u_{p}U\mid u\in V,\ p\in(1/T)\mathbb{Z}\bigr\}\subseteq M.

Then
N
is a
(1/T)\mathbb{N}
-graded
g
-twisted
\phi
-coordinated
V
-submodule of
M
, and its degree-
m
component is
U
.
Since
M
is simple, we must have
N=M
, which forces
U=M(m)
.
Therefore,
M(m)
is an irreducible
\tilde{A}_{g,n}(V)
-module.
∎

5.
The Lie algebra
\hat{V}[g]

Let
V
be a vertex algebra equipped with an automorphism
g
of order
T
.
Let
t
be an indeterminate, and consider the tensor product

\mathcal{L}(V)=V\otimes\mathbb{C}\!\left[t^{1/T},t^{-1/T}\right].

Following
[
16
]
, we endow
\mathbb{C}\!\left[t^{1/T},t^{-1/T}\right]
with the structure of a vertex algebra whose vertex operator is defined by

Y(f(t),z)g(t)=\bigl(e^{z\,(t\frac{d}{dt})}f(t)\bigr)g(t).

Consequently,
\mathcal{L}(V)
becomes the tensor product of two vertex algebras and hence itself a vertex algebra
[
15
]
.
The automorphism
g
extends naturally to a vertex algebra automorphism of
\mathcal{L}(V)
via

g\bigl(a\otimes t^{m}\bigr)=e^{2\pi im}\,(ga\otimes t^{m}),\qquad a\in V,\ m\in(1/T)\mathbb{Z}.

Denote by
\mathcal{L}(V,g)
the subspace of
g
-invariants; it is a vertex subalgebra of
\mathcal{L}(V)
. Explicitly,

\mathcal{L}(V,g)=\bigoplus_{r=0}^{T-1}V^{r}\otimes t^{r/T}\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}].

The
\mathcal{D}
-operator on
\mathcal{L}(V,g)
is given by

\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{L}}=\mathcal{D}\otimes 1+1\otimes t\frac{d}{dt}.

As shown in
[
1
]
, the quotient

\hat{V}[g]:=\mathcal{L}(V,g)/\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{L}}\mathcal{L}(V,g)

carries a natural Lie algebra structure with bracket

[u+\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{L}}\mathcal{L}(V,g),\,v+\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{L}}\mathcal{L}(V,g)]=u_{0}v+\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{L}}\mathcal{L}(V,g),\qquad u,v\in\mathcal{L}(V,g).

For notational convenience, we denote by
a(q)
the image of
a\otimes t^{q}\in\mathcal{L}(V,g)
in
\hat{V}[g]
.
A direct computation yields the following:

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
a\in V^{r}
,
b\in V^{s}
, and
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then

(1)

\bigl[a\bigl(m+{r}/{T}\bigr),\,b\bigl(n+{s}/{T}\bigr)\bigr]=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{(m+r/T)^{i}}{i!}\,(a_{i}b)(m+n+{(r+s)}/{T});

(2)

\mathbf{1}(0)
lies in the center of
\hat{V}[g]
.

We introduce a
(1/T)\mathbb{Z}
-grading on
\mathcal{L}(V,g)
by setting

\deg(a\otimes t^{n})=-n
for
a\in V,\ n\in(1/T)\mathbb{Z}.

Since
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{L}}
preserves this grading,
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{L}}\mathcal{L}(V,g)
is a graded subspace, and thus
\hat{V}[g]
inherits a natural
(1/T)\mathbb{Z}
-grading.
Let
\hat{V}[g]_{n}
denote the homogeneous component of degree
n
.
By Lemma
5.1
,
\hat{V}[g]
is a
(1/T)\mathbb{Z}
-graded Lie algebra, and we have the triangular decomposition

\hat{V}[g]=\hat{V}[g]_{+}\oplus\hat{V}[g]_{0}\oplus\hat{V}[g]_{-},

where

\hat{V}[g]_{\pm}=\bigoplus_{0<n\in(1/T)\mathbb{Z}}\hat{V}[g]_{\pm n}.

Note that
\hat{V}[g]_{0}
is spanned by elements of the form
a(0)
with
a\in V^{0}
.

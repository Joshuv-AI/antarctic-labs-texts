Abstract
The path to the solution of Feder-Vardi dichotomy conjecture by Bulatov and Zhuk led through showing that more and more general algebraic conditions imply polynomial-time algorithms for the finite-domain Constraint Satisfaction Problems (CSPs) whose templates satisfy them. These investigations resulted in the discovery of the appropriate height 1 Maltsev conditions characterizing bounded strict width, bounded width, the applicability of the few-subpowers algorithm, and many others. For problems in the range of the similar Bodirsky-Pinsker conjecture on infinite-domain CSPs, one can only find such a characterization for the notion of bounded strict width, with a proof essentially the same as in the finite case. In this paper, we provide the first non-trivial results showing that certain height 1 Maltsev conditions imply bounded width, and in consequence tractability, for a natural subclass of templates within the Bodirsky-Pinsker conjecture which includes many templates in the literature as well as templates for which no complexity classification is known.

I
Introduction

This paper treats the complexity of
Constraint Satisfaction Problems

parametrized by finite and infinite relational structures
\mathbb{A}=(A;R_{1},\ldots)
,
called
templates
. Every
\mathbb{A}

gives rise to a computational decision problem
\Csp(\mathbb{A})
whose instances consist of a set of variables and a set of constraints
which restrict the
possible combinations of values the variables can take. More precisely, in a solution to the instance the variables have to be assigned values in the
domain
of
\mathbb{A}
, i.e. its base set; the constraints require some tuples of variables to belong to some relations of
\mathbb{A}
after the assignment of values. The problem generalizes the well-known problem
3
-SAT where
the template
\mathbb{A}
over the domain
A=\{0,1\}
contains exactly one ternary relation for every type of clause over three propositional literals. Furthermore, the
k
-coloring problem as well as the question of whether a given system of linear equations over a finite field has a solution can be expressed naturally as
\Csp(\mathbb{A})
for a finite structure
\mathbb{A}
. The diversity of problems that fall under this formalism and certain preliminary results led Tomás Feder and Moshe Vardi
[
1
]
to formulate their famous
dichotomy conjecture
stating that all finite-domain CSPs are either in P or NP-complete. The conjecture contrasts the result known as Ladner’s theorem
[
2
]
which asserts that P
\neq
NP implies the existence of NP-intermediate problems.
No serious progress had been achieved towards the resolution of the conjecture until the so-called
universal-algebraic approach

was
employed
[
3
,
4
]
and
the perspective switched from relational structures
\mathbb{A}
as central objects first to algebras of multivariate functions on
\mathbb{A}
leaving the relations of
\mathbb{A}
invariant (so-called
polymorphisms
of
\mathbb{A}
, the set of which is denoted by
\Pol(\mathbb{A})
), and then even further to identities satisfied by these algebras (equivalently, to so-called
algebraic varieties
). Although rather different, both resolutions to the Feder-Vardi conjecture, the one due to Andrei Bulatov
[
5
]
as well as the one due to Dmitriy Zhuk
[
6
,
7
]
, confirm the (finite-domain)
algebraic tractability conjecture
from
[
4
]
using universal-algebraic methods, thereby demonstrating the strength and appropriateness of this approach.

It is difficult to overstate the importance of the Bulatov-Zhuk dichotomy theorem. Nevertheless, the majority of problems expressible as
\Csp(\mathbb{A})
require an infinite structure
\mathbb{A}
.
Fortunately, for many of them
the universal-algebraic approach, originally invented for finite structures, is also applicable.
This is true, in particular, for a certain class of scheduling problems called
temporal CSPs

[
8
]
(where the relations of templates are given by quantifier-free formulas using the order of the rational numbers), a class of problems in phylogenetic reconstruction called
phylogeny CSPs

[
9
]
(where suitable templates can be provided using quantifier-free formulas using the
homogeneous C-relation
), for
Graph-SAT problems

[
10
,
11
]
(where templates have relations given by quantifier-free formulas over countable homogeneous graphs) and, more generally,
Hypergraph-SAT problems

[
12
]
, for certain
completion problems

[
13
,
14
,
15
]
(where templates have relations given by quantifier-free formulas over countable homogenous digraphs), as well as for the model-checking problem for sentences in the logic MMSNP of Feder and Vardi
[
16
,
17
]
or the logic GMSNP
[
18
,
19
]
(for which suitable infinite-domain templates can be provided using non-trivial model-theoretic techniques).
A conjecture similar to the Feder-Vardi conjecture has been formulated for a class of infinite structures sufficiently large to contain the templates of these problems as well as all finite-domain CSPs, and sufficiently small to be contained in NP and allow for a universal-algebraic approach.

{conjecture}

(Bodirsky-Pinsker 2011)

Let
\mathbb{A}
be the model-complete core of a finite-signature structure which is first-order definable in a finitely bounded homogeneous structure.
Then
precisely one of the following holds:

1.

there exists an expansion
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}

of
\mathbb{A}
by finitely many constants such that
\Pol(\mathbb{A}^{\prime})
has a
continuous projective homomorphism (and hence
\CSP(\mathbb{A})
is NP-complete);

2.

for any such expansion
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}

the clone
\Pol(\mathbb{A}^{\prime})
has no continuous projective homomorphism. In this case,
\CSP(\mathbb{A})
is in P.

The above
original formulation of the infinite-domain dichotomy conjecture restricts the infinite structures under consideration to so-called
model-complete cores
, whose exact definition is not of importance for this paper – this restriction of the scope of the conjecture is without loss of generality, and we refer the interested reader to
[
20
]
. All notions which are crucial for understanding the merits of the present paper are defined in Section
II
.

The first item of the conjecture indeed implies NP-hardness. The tractability part, however, stays wide open: Condition (
2
) is just the negation of Condition (
1
), and therefore does not give any substantial information which could set the direction for developing a polynomial-time algorithm.
Fortunately,
a result
[
21
]
due to Libor Barto and Michael Pinsker states that
Condition (
2
) is equivalent to the following
infinite-domain algebraic tractability conjecture
.

{conjecture}

(Barto-Pinsker 2016)

Let
\mathbb{A}
be the model-complete core of a finite-signature structure which is first-order definable in a finitely bounded homogeneous structure.
If
\mathbb{A}
is invariant under a
pseudo-Siggers
operation, i.e., a 6-ary operation
s
for which there are unary polymorphisms
\alpha,\beta

such that

\alpha s(x,y,x,z,y,z)\approx\beta s(y,x,z,x,z,y)

for all
x,y,z
in domain, then
\Csp(\mathbb{A})
is solvable in polynomial time.

Conjecture
I
is almost identical to the algebraic tractability criterion for finite-domain CSPs confirmed by
Bulatov and Zhuk, with the difference that in the finite-domain version
\alpha,\beta
are omitted;
s
is then simply called a
Siggers operation
.

Prior to the resolution of the finite-domain algebraic tractability conjecture, research focused on restrictions thereof obtained by imposing additional assumptions of two kinds:

•

directly on the template: e.g. its size
[
22
,
23
]
, or its relations such as the cases of symmetric graphs
[
24
,
25
]
or smooth digraphs
[
26
]

•

on the algebraic invariants (polymorphisms) of the template: the satisfaction of certain identities by its polymorphisms
[
27
,
28
,
29
]
) – such conditions are called
Maltsev conditions
.

Indeed, researchers considered identities stronger than the above-mentioned Siggers identity implying tractability, and in particular provided algebraic characterizations of the limits of the applicability of
local consistency checking
[
28
]
as well as of algorithms resembling Gaussian elimination
[
29
]
. Both of these algorithmic methods play a crucial role in the final procedures of the algorithms of Bulatov and Zhuk.
It is fair to say that research on both kinds of restrictions of the conjecture, i.e. by imposing structural or algebraic assumptions on the template, not only contributed to its
eventual resolution, but also lead to results of independent interest.

While work on infinite-domain CSPs has also delivered impressive results, these were, however, almost exclusively confined to the former kind, that is, structural restrictions directly on the template
[
8
,
10
,
17
,
12
]
. In fact, there are only two general results guaranteeing polynomial-time solvability of CSPs within the Bodirsky-Pinsker conjecture under conditions on their polymorphisms:
the one in
[
30
]

employs so-called
canonical pseudo-Siggers operations
; and the one in
[
31
]
applies to templates invariant under a
quasi near-unanimity operation
, i.e. an operation satisfying the identities

f(y,x,\ldots,x)=\cdots=f(x,x,\ldots,y)=f(x,\ldots,x)

(1)

for all
x,y
in the domain. The former provides a black-box reduction to finite-domain CSPs, while the proof of the latter is a straightforward generalization of the result in
[
1
]
for finite templates.

Note that the identities in (
1
) are of
height 1
, i.e. there is no nesting of function symbols and variables only appear as arguments in function symbols (they are never isolated). The prefix
quasi
(as here in
quasi near-unanimity operation
) always refers to the omission of the identity
x=f(x,\ldots,x)
(which is not of height 1); functions
f
satisfying this additional identity are called
idempotent
. In consequence, for a
near-unanimity operation
the last term in (
1
) could be replaced by
x
. One might wonder why we do not compose every side of each of the identities in (
1
) with unary operations to obtain a
pseudo near-unanimity operation
resembling the pseudo-Siggers operation from
Section

I
(which is not of height 1). The reason is that due to an observation of Marcin Kozik and Michał Wrona, this seemingly weaker algebraic condition turns out to be equivalent
(see Lemma 10.1.3 in
[
20
]
).

An algebraic condition of importance for finite-domain templates
is given by chains of
idempotent Pixley
operations, which we here generalize to chains of
quasi Pixley
operations. The question whether this condition, either the original (idempotent) one or its quasi-version, implies tractability and/or bounded width over first-order reducts of finitely bounded homogeneous structures is Problem (35) in Section 14 in
[
20
]
. We solve this question positively for an important subclass of infinite structures within Bodirsky-Pinsker conjecture.

{definition}

A sequence
(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n})
of ternary operations on a set
A
is
called a chain of
quasi Pixley operations
if for all
x,y\in A

\displaystyle P_{1}(x,y,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{1}(x,x,x),

\displaystyle P_{i}(x,y,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{i}(x,x,x)\hskip 36.0pt\textrm{ for all }i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},

\displaystyle P_{i}(x,x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{i+1}(x,y,y)\hskip 30.0pt\textrm{ for all }i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},

\displaystyle P_{n}(x,x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{n}(y,y,y).

Another algebraic condition of height 1 identities unknown to imply polynomial-time solvability is given by chains of
quasi directed Jónsson
operations. These are provably implied by quasi near-unanimity operations: every structure invariant under operations satisfying the latter identities is also invariant under operations satisfying the former (see Proposition 6.9.12 in
[
20
]
).

{definition}

A sequence
(D_{1},\ldots,D_{n})
of ternary operations on a set
A
is
called a chain of quasi directed Jónsson operations if for all
x,y\in A
:

\displaystyle D_{1}(x,x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{1}(x,x,x),

\displaystyle D_{i}(x,y,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{i}(x,x,x)\hskip 36.0pt\textrm{ for all }i\in[n],

\displaystyle D_{i}(x,y,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{i+1}(x,x,y)\hskip 30.0pt\textrm{ for all }i\in[n-1],

\displaystyle D_{n}(x,y,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{n}(y,y,y).

Finally, the weakest (see Proposition 6.9.9 in
[
20
]
) algebraic condition of height 1 considered in this paper is the following.

{definition}

A sequence
(J_{1},\ldots,J_{2n+1})
of ternary operations on a set
A
is
called a chain of
quasi Jónsson operations
if for all
x,y\in A
:

\displaystyle J_{1}(x,x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J_{1}(x,x,x),

(2)

\displaystyle J_{i}(x,y,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J_{i}(x,x,x)\hskip 18.0pt\textrm{ for all }i\in[2n+1],

(3)

\displaystyle J_{2i-1}(x,y,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J_{2i}(x,y,y)\hskip 18.0pt\textrm{ for all }i\in[n+1],

(4)

\displaystyle J_{2i}(x,x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J_{2i+1}(x,x,y)\hskip 10.0pt\textrm{ for all }i\in[n],

(5)

\displaystyle J_{2n+1}(x,y,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J_{2n+1}(y,y,y).

(6)

The main contribution of this paper, which we shall explain in detail in the following subsection, states that the invariance of a large class of infinite structures
\mathbb{A}
within the Bodirsky-Pinsker conjecture under a chain of quasi Jónsson operations implies that the corresponding
\Csp(\mathbb{A})
is solvable by local consistency checking, and is in consequence in P. Our result thereby lifts the corresponding result for finite-domain CSPs and chains of idempotent Jónsson operations (“
congruence distributivity
”)
[
27
]
, which was an intermediate step towards giving the full algebraic characterization of finite
\mathbb{A}
solvable by local-consistency methods (“
congruence meet-semidistributivity
”)
[
28
]
. Our proof is, however, based on completely different techniques than the one for finite domains. This, the fact that the proof for quasi near-unanimity operations simply mimics the one for finite domains, and the fact that the above-mentioned result on canonical pseudo-Siggers operations is not purely algebraic due to the additional condition of canonicity (which is a property that also requires a topology on the polymorphisms in order to be defined), imply that here we provide the first non-trivial results showing that algebraic conditions imply tractability for structures within Bodirsky-Pinsker conjecture. By Propositions 6.9.9 and 6.9.12 in
[
20
]
, our main contribution also yields tractability for templates invariant under chains of quasi directed Jónsson operations and of quasi Pixley operations, respectively.

I-A

The main result

Our general theorem firstly assumes that the finite bounds of the ground structure
\mathbb{B}
in which the template is defined (see Conjecture
I
) are closed under homomorphisms; we say that
\mathbb{B}
has
finite duality
.
This is an important case within the scope of the Bodirsky-Pinsker conjecture, including in particular many interesting templates modeling Graph-SAT and Hypergraph-SAT problems, generalized completion problems, as well as the model-checking problems for MMSNP and GMSNP mentioned above.
Secondly, we assume that
\mathbb{B}
is
k
-neoliberal
, meaning that all its relations are
k
-ary and in a strong sense the entire structure of
\mathbb{B}
is completely reflected in these relations; a bit more precisely, there are no non-trivial first-order definable relations of arity smaller than
k
, all first-order definable relations of arity
k
are unions of relations in
\mathbb{B}
, all first-order definable relations of higher arity are Boolean combinations of those of arity
k
, and there are no algebraic dependencies between the elements of any
k
-tuple.

The literature on CSPs encompasses many examples of
k
-neoliberal structures: for example, Henson digraphs
[
13
,
14
,
15
]
(with
k=2
), the homogeneous C-relation (with
k=3
)
[
9
]
, and
k
-uniform hypergraphs
for
k\geq 2

[
10
,
12
]
.
Among these, many moreover have finite duality (homogeneous graphs such as the random graph, Henson digraphs, and some
k
-uniform hypergraphs). These can
be further generalized to multi-graphs, i.e., graphs with edges colored by several
colors, to homogeneous multi-digraphs, and to multi-hypergraphs, respectively. We would like to stress that our main result applies also to the latter structures whose CSPs are far from being classified, and which present significant obstacles to a classification. Indeed, while the literature on CSPs encloses a number of complexity classifications for first-order reducts of
k
-neoliberal structures
\mathbb{B}
whose automorphism group acts with two orbits on
k
-tuples with pairwise different entries (e.g., for
k
-uniform hypergraphs,
k
pairwise different elements may either form a hyperedge or not),
there are essentially no similar results when
\mathbb{B}
has hyperedges of
c
pairwise different colors with
c>2
(a hyperedge and a non-hyperedge may be seen as two different colors).

{example}

A general recipe for obtaining finitely bounded homogeneous
3
-graphs (i.e. graphs with three different kind of edges) is given in
[
32
]
.
One starts with any finite set of finite
2
-graphs

\mathcal{F}
over a signature
\tau=\{O,P\}
where all pairs of vertices are connected either by an
O
-edge or a
P
-edge.
The class of finite structures
\mathscr{C}
over
\tau
omitting the
2
-graphs in
\mathcal{F}
has free amalgamation and the Fraïssé limit
\mathbb{B}
of
\mathscr{C}
yields the desired structure if we add to
O
and
P
a third kind of edge which connects two distinct vertices whenever there is neither an
O
-edge nor a
P
-edge. It is a simple exercise to show that such

\mathbb{B}
is
2
-neoliberal and of finite duality.

When looking for examples of CSP templates with a first-order definition in a neoliberal structure with finite duality which are preserved by quasi near-unanimity operations we reach out to
[
33
, Proposition 16]
.

{example}

Let
\mathbb{A}
be a structure whose all relations are first-order definable as conjunctions of clauses of the form:

(x_{1}\neq y_{1}\vee\cdots\vee x_{k}\neq y_{1}\vee R(y_{1},y_{2})\vee y_{2}\neq z_{1}\vee\cdots\vee y_{2}\neq z_{l}),

over the random graph
\mathbb{B}=(V;E)
where
R
is either the edge relation
E
or the non-edge relation
N
imposed on pairwise different elements in
A
. Then
\mathbb{A}
is preserved by a quasi near-unanimity operation and in consequence by a chain of quasi Jónsson operations.

An example of a relational structure preserved by a chain of quasi Jónsson operations but not by a quasi near-unanimity operation may be found among structures with a first-order definition in
(\mathbb{N},=)
where
\mathbb{N}
is the set of natural numbers. These templates are usually called
equality languages
and have been investigated in detail (up to primitive positive definability) in
[
34
]
. The desired equality language will be described by its polymorphisms.

{example}

Let
1\leq i\leq n
. An
n
-ary operation
f
is
injective in the
i
-th direction
if

x_{i}\neq\widehat{x}_{i}\implies f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i},\ldots,x_{n})\neq f(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x}_{i},\ldots,x_{n})

for all
x_{1},\ldots,x_{i},\widehat{x}_{i}\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
. The operation
f
is
injective in one direction
if it is injective in the
i
-th direction for some
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. We define
\mathbb{R}
to be the structure containing all relations which are invariant under all operations that are injective in one direction. It is shown in
[
35
]
that
\mathbb{R}
is invariant neither under a quasi near-unanimity operation nor under chains of idempotent Pixley or idempotent directed Jónsson operations; but it is invariant both under chains of quasi Pixley and quasi directed Jónsson operations, and hence also by chains of quasi Jónsson operations.

Adopting the strategy for quasi near-unanimity operations from
[
36
]
, we prove that any CSP template
\mathbb{A}
which is a first-order expansion of a ground structure
\mathbb{B}
with the properties described above and which is invariant under a chain of quasi Jónsson operations has limited expressive power in the form of
implicational simplicity
. We will show that this condition is equivalent to saying that
\mathbb{A}
does not primitively positively define an injective relation which entails a formula of the form
R(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\Rightarrow R(x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{i_{k}})
for some relation
R
primitively positively definable from
\mathbb{A}
.

{theorem}

[]
Let
k\geq 2
, let
\mathbb{B}
be
k
-neoliberal, and suppose that
\mathbb{B}
has finite duality. Suppose that
\mathbb{A}
is a CSP template which is an expansion of
\mathbb{B}
by first-order definable relations (i.e., Boolean combinations of the relations of
\mathbb{B}
). If
\mathbb{A}
is invariant under a chain of quasi Jónsson operations, then it is implicationally simple on injective instances.

As a corollary of
Section

I-A
and results from
[
37
,
12
,
38
]
, we obtain a bound on the amount of local consistency needed to solve CSPs of the templates under consideration.

{corollary}

[]
Let
k\geq 2
, let
\mathbb{B}
be
k
-neoliberal, and suppose that
\mathbb{B}
has finite duality. Suppose that
\mathbb{A}
is a CSP template which is an expansion of
\mathbb{B}
by first-order definable relations (i.e., Boolean combinations of the relations of
\mathbb{B}
). If
\mathbb{A}
is invariant under a chain of quasi Jónsson operations, then it has relational width
(k,\max(k+1,b_{\mathbb{B}}))
, and hence
\Csp(\mathbb{A})
is polynomial-time solvable.

I-B

Related work

Section

I-A
provides both a tractability result and a
bound on the amount of local consistency required to guarantee a solution to an instance. Moreover, this bound is easily seen to be optimal for any structure under consideration. As mentioned above, the only previous general results providing tractability within the Bodirsky-Pinsker conjecture by imposing conditions on polymorphisms were the one on quasi near-unanimity operations
[
31
]
(which is a straightforward generalization from the finite) and the one on canonical pseudo-Siggers operations
[
30
]
(which is not purely algebraic in the sense that it requires topology to be stated). We thus perceive
Section

I-A
as the first non-trivial result on the tractability of infinite-domain CSPs originating from purely algebraic (height 1) conditions.

Building on results in
[
1
,
39
,
28
]
, Libor Barto has shown in
[
40
]
that the relational width of any finite structure is either
(1,1)
or
(2,3)
. We do not have a full understanding of this phenomenon over infinite structures. There is a plethora of interesting results, though.
In
[
37
]
, bounded width was characterized for CSP templates
\mathbb{A}
over several “ground structures”
\mathbb{B}
(in which they are first-order definable), using
weak near-unanimity
identities satisfied by
canonical polymorphisms
; this amounts to assuming weaker identities than quasi near-unanimity identities, but the additional (non-algebraic) property of canonicity.
The conditions given there were applied in
[
41
]
to obtain a general upper bound on the relational width of CSP templates satisfying them, and the bound was shown to be optimal for many templates. The bound on the relational width in
Section

I-A
coincides with the bound proven in
[
41
]
for CSP templates which posses canonical
pseudo-totally symmetric polymorphisms
of all arities
n\geq 3
. The results in
[
41
]
have been extended to CSP templates over finitely bounded homogeneous
k
-uniform hypergraphs for
k\geq 3
in
[
12
]
.
The very first bounds as in

Section

I-A
over infinite structures within Bodirsky-Pinsker conjecture were, however, obtained in
[
33
,
36
]
, where a similar upper bound was given for first-order expansions
\mathbb{A}
of certain binary structures
\mathbb{B}
under the assumption that
\mathbb{A}
is invariant under a quasi near-unanimity operation.

II
Preliminaries

II-A

Relational structures and permutation groups

Let
\mathbb{B}
be a relational structure, and let
\phi
be a first-order formula over the signature of
\mathbb{B}
. We identify the interpretation
\phi^{\mathbb{B}}
of
\phi
in
\mathbb{B}
with the set of satisfying assignments for
\phi^{\mathbb{B}}
. Let
V
be the set of free variables of
\phi
, and let
\mathbf{u}
be a tuple of elements of
V
. We define
\proj_{\mathbf{u}}(\phi^{\mathbb{B}}):=\{f(\mathbf{u})\mid f\in\phi^{\mathbb{B}}\}
.
A
first-order expansion
of a structure
\mathbb{B}
is an expansion of
\mathbb{B}
by relations which are first-order definable in
\mathbb{B}
, i.e., of the form
\phi^{\mathbb{B}}
.

A first-order formula is called
primitive positive
(pp) if it is built exclusively from atomic formulae, existential quantifiers, and conjunctions. A relation is
pp-definable
in a relational structure if it is first-order definable by a pp-formula.

{definition}

Let
\mathbb{B}
be a structure over a finite relational signature
\tau
. We say that
\mathbb{B}
is
finitely bounded
if there exists a finite set
\mathcal{F}
of finite
\tau
-structures such that for every finite
\tau
-structure

{definition}

Let

Let

{definition}

II-C

Polymorphisms

Let
A
be a set, let
k,n\geq 1
, and let
R\subseteq A^{k}
. A function

preserves
the relation
R
if for all tuples
(a^{1}_{1},\dots,a^{1}_{k}),\dots,(a^{n}_{1},\dots,a^{n}_{k})

\in R
, it holds that the tuple
(f(a^{1}_{1},\dots,a^{n}_{1}),\dots,f(a^{1}_{k},\dots,a^{n}_{k}))

as well.
The function is a

of a relational structure
\mathbb{A}
if it preserves all relations of
\mathbb{A}
. The set of all polymorphisms of
\mathbb{A}
is denoted by

. The importance of polymorphisms is based on the fact that for

-categorical
\mathbb{A}
, the pp-definable relations are precisely those preserved by all polymorphisms of
\mathbb{A}

[
42
]
.

The proof of the following useful observation and
all other omitted proofs may be found in the appendix.

{observation}

Let
\mathbb{A}
be an
\omega
-categorical model-complete core preserved by a chain of quasi Jónsson operations
(J_{1},\ldots,J_{2n+1})
. Then for every finite subset

there exists a chain of quasi Jónsson operations
(J^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,J^{\prime}_{2n+1})
such that each

with

is idempotent on

, i.e.

for all

.

The set of automorphisms of
\mathbb{A}
is denoted by
\Aut(\mathbb{A})
.

{definition}

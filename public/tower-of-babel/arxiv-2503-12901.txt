On Mañé’s critical value for the two-component Hunter-Saxton system and an infinite-dimensional magnetic Hopf–Rinow theorem
L. Maier

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce a nonlinear system of partial differential equations, the magnetic two-component Hunter–Saxton system (M2HS). This system is formulated as a magnetic geodesic equation on an infinite-dimensional Lie group equipped with a right-invariant metric, namely the H ˙ 1 \dot{H}^{1} -metric, which is closely related to the infinite-dimensional Fisher–Rao metric. The magnetic field is given by the exterior derivative of an infinite-dimensional contact-type form. We define Mañé’s critical value for exact magnetic systems on Hilbert manifolds in full generality and compute it explicitly for the (M2HS). Moreover, we establish an infinite-dimensional Hopf–Rinow theorem for this magnetic system, where Mañé’s critical value serves as the threshold beyond which the Hopf–Rinow property fails. This geometric framework enables a detailed analysis of the blow-up behavior of solutions to the (M2HS). Using this insight, we extend solutions beyond blow-up by introducing and proving the existence of global conservative weak solutions. This extension is achieved by extending the Madelung transform from an isometry to a magnetomorphism, thereby embedding the magnetic system into a magnetic system on an infinite-dimensional sphere equipped with the exterior derivative of the standard contact form as the magnetic field. Crucially, this setting can always be reduced, via a dynamical reduction theorem, to a totally magnetic three-sphere, providing deeper insight into the underlying dynamics.



1.
Introduction

In his seminal work
[
7
]
, V. Arnold discovered that the Euler equation in hydrodynamics, which describes the motion of an incompressible and inviscid fluid occupying a fixed domain (with or without boundary), is precisely the geodesic equation on the group of volume-preserving diffeomorphisms of the domain equipped with a right-invariant Riemannian metric, the
L^{2}
metric. Notably, the analytical study of the differential geometry of the incompressible Euler equations began by Ebin–Marsden in
[
16
]
.

From the perspective of differential geometry, it is natural to ask: If we change the infinite-dimensional Lie group and/or the Riemannian metric, which partial differential equations (PDEs) can also be described as geodesic equations? This has been investigated intensively in the past. For a general overview, we refer to the monograph by Arnold–-Khesin
[
8
]
and the survey articles
[
22
,
37
]
.

Before we proceed, we would like to highlight an important example relevant to this work: the Hunter–Saxton equation, which is the geodesic equation on the Virasoro group equipped with the
\dot{H}^{1}
-metric. This was discovered by Khesin–Misiolek in
[
23
]
and later used in
[
26
,
27
]
to prove, for instance, the existence of global weak solutions for this PDE.

We now move on to discuss a natural generalization of the geodesic equation from the perspective of Hamiltonian dynamics: Newton’s equation. Nowadays, several PDEs from mathematical physics possess a formulation as an infinite-dimensional Newton equation, including the geodesic equation. Notable examples include the barotropic inviscid fluid, fully compressible fluid, magnetohydrodynamics, inviscid Burgers’ equation, the Hamilton–Jacobi equation, the Klein–Gordon equation, the KdV equation, and the Camassa–Holm equation. For a general overview of the landscape of this topic, we refer to
[
20
,
22
]
.

In physical terms, Newton’s equation describes the motion of a particle under the influence of a potential force. Thus, from a physical point of view, the next natural step is to investigate the motion of a charged particle in a magnetic field. In mathematics, this falls under the framework of Hamiltonian dynamics, specifically magnetic systems. Following the pioneering work of V. Arnold
[
6
]
in the 1960s, finite-dimensional magnetic systems have garnered significant attention in recent years; see, for example,
[
2
,
9
,
15
,
14
,
19
,
33
]
. It is noteworthy that the trajectories of magnetic systems are called
magnetic geodesics
, which are solutions to the corresponding equation of motion known as the
magnetic geodesic equation
; see
Definition

2.1
.

This naturally leads to the following question, arising from the preceding discussion of various PDEs as geodesic or Newton equations:

Are there infinite-dimensional magnetic systems such that the associated magnetic geodesic equation is a PDE?

We provide a positive answer to this question in this article by introducing the
magnetic two-component Hunter-Saxton system
(
M2HS
). Before stating the main contributions of this paper, let us emphasize the following: A key point of interest is to explore the similarities and differences between standard and magnetic geodesics, in this paper by means of a Hopf–Rinow theorem. According to this pivotal result, every two points on a closed connected finite-dimensional manifold can be connected by a standard geodesic
[
35
]
. However, this finding does not generally hold for Hilbert manifolds
[
10
,
17
]
, but it is surprisingly true for half-Lie groups due to the recent work of Bauer-Harms-Michor
[
11
]
.

Notably, a standard fact in differential geometry ensures that the existence of a connecting geodesic is independent of its speed or energy. Therefore, it is natural to ask whether there exists a magnetic geodesic connecting two given points with a prescribed kinetic energy. An important role in addressing this question is played by the Mañé critical value, which, until now, has been defined only for finite-dimensional magnetic systems
[
14
,
33
]
.

The main contributions of this article

2.
Magnetic systems and Mañé critical value

2.1.
Intermezzo: Magnetic systems

In the 1960s, the motion of a charged particle in a magnetic field was placed within the framework of modern dynamical systems by V. Arnold in his pioneering work
[
6
]
. The motion has the following mathematical description:

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a connected Riemannian Hilbert manifold, and let
\sigma\in\Omega^{2}(M)
be a closed two-form. The form
\sigma
is called a
magnetic field
, and the triple
(M,g,\sigma)
is called a
magnetic system
. This defines the skew-symmetric bundle endomorphism
Y\colon TM\to TM
, called the
Lorentz force
, by:

(2.1)

g_{q}\left(Y_{q}u,v\right)=\sigma_{q}(u,v),\qquad\forall\,q\in M,\ \forall\,u,v\in T_{q}M.

A smooth curve
\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}\to M
is called a
magnetic geodesic
of
(M,g,\sigma)
if it satisfies:

(2.2)

\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}\dot{\gamma}=Y_{\gamma}\dot{\gamma},

where
\nabla
denotes the Levi-Civita connection of the metric
g
.

Remarks 2.2
.

Special case: Geodesics.
From (
2.2
), it is evident that a magnetic geodesic with
\sigma=0
reduces to a standard geodesic of the metric
g
. Therefore, (
2.2
) can be interpreted as a linear perturbation of the geodesic equation. A key point of interest is to explore the similarities and differences between standard and magnetic geodesics.

Since
Y
is skew-symmetric, magnetic geodesics have constant kinetic energy
E(\gamma,\dot{\gamma}):=\tfrac{1}{2}g_{\gamma}(\dot{\gamma},\dot{\gamma})
, and hence constant speed
|\dot{\gamma}|:=\sqrt{g_{\gamma}(\dot{\gamma},\dot{\gamma})}
, just like standard geodesics. Energy conservation is a footprint of the Hamiltonian nature of the system. Indeed, let us define the
magnetic geodesic flow
on the tangent bundle by

\varPhi_{g,\sigma}^{t}\colon TM\to TM,\quad(q,v)\mapsto\left(\gamma_{q,v}(t),\dot{\gamma}_{q,v}(t)\right),\quad\forall t\in{\mathbb{R}},

where
\gamma_{q,v}(t)
is the unique magnetic geodesic with initial values
(q,v)\in TM
. By
[
19
]
,
\Phi^{t}_{g,\sigma}
is the Hamiltonian flow induced by the kinetic energy
E\colon TM\to{\mathbb{R}}
and the twisted symplectic form

(2.3)

\omega_{\sigma}:={\mathrm{d}}\lambda-\pi^{*}_{TM}\sigma,

where
\lambda
is the metric pullback of the canonical Liouville
1
-form from
T^{*}M
to
TM
and
\pi_{TM}\colon TM\to M
is the projection. However, differently from the case of standard geodesics, magnetic geodesics of different speeds are not just reparametrization of unit speed magnetic geodesics. For instance, if
M={\mathbb{S}}^{2}
,
g
has constant curvature
1
and
\sigma
is the corresponding area form, then magnetic geodesics of kinetic energy
k
are geodesic circles of radius
\arctan(\sqrt{2k})
traversed counterclockwise, see
[
12
]
.

Remark 2.3
.

A different perspective.
For every
s>0
, a curve
\gamma
is a magnetic geodesic of
(M,g,\sigma)
with speed
\frac{1}{s}
if and only if the unit-speed reparametrization of
\gamma
is a magnetic geodesic of
(M,g,s\sigma)
. This implies that studying magnetic geodesics of
(M,g,\sigma)
with varying speeds is equivalent to studying unit-speed magnetic geodesics of
(M,g,s\sigma)
for different values of
s>0
. This approach has the added benefit that:

•

For
s=0
, we recover standard geodesics.

•

For
s<0
, we recover magnetic geodesics of
(M,g,\sigma)
with reversed orientation.

In this context, the parameter
s
is referred to as the
strength
of the magnetic geodesic.

2.2.
Mañé’s Critical Value of infinite-dimensional magnetic systems

Let
(M,g)
be a Hilbert manifold endowed with a Riemannian metric
g
, which may be either weak or strong. Let
\alpha
be a one-form on
M
and consider the exact magnetic system
(M,g,{\mathrm{d}}\alpha)
. In this case, the magnetic geodesic flow
\Phi_{g,\sigma}
is the Euler–Lagrange flow
\Phi_{L}
of the magnetic Lagrangian

(2.4)

L\colon TM\to{\mathbb{R}},\quad L(q,v):=\tfrac{1}{2}|v|^{2}-\alpha_{q}(v),

see
[
19
]
. This means that
\gamma\colon[0,T]\to M
is a magnetic geodesic if and only if
\gamma
is a critical point of the action functional
S_{L}
among all curves
\delta\in H^{1}\left([0,T],M\right)
such that
\delta(0)=\gamma(0)
and
\delta(T)=\gamma(T)
. Here the action functional is defined as

S_{L}:H^{1}\left([0,T],M\right)\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}:\gamma\mapsto S_{L}(\gamma):=\int_{0}^{T}L(\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t)){\mathrm{d}}t.

This variational principle prescribes the length
T
of the time interval and leaves free the energy of
\gamma
. On the other hand, given
k\in\mathbb{R}
,
\gamma
is a magnetic geodesic with energy
k
if and only if
\gamma
is a critical point of
S_{L+k}
among all curves
\delta\colon[0,T^{\prime}]\to M
of Sobolev class
H^{1}
with
\delta(0)=\gamma(0)
and
\delta(T^{\prime})=\gamma(T)
for an arbitrary
T^{\prime}>0
. Now we can define Mañé’s critical value of the Lagrangian
L
:

Definition 2.4

(Mañé’s critical value)
.

The Mañé’s critical value
c(L)\in\mathbb{R}
of
L
is the smallest energy value such that the free-period action functional with energy
k
is bounded from below (by zero) on the space of loops of Sobolev class
H^{1}
:

(2.5)

c(L)=\inf\left\{k\in\mathbb{R}\mid S_{L+k}(\gamma)\geq 0,\ \forall T>0,\ \forall\gamma\in H^{1}\left([0,T],M\right),\ \gamma(0)=\gamma(T)\right\}.

Remarks 2.5
.

Mañé’s critical value for exact magnetic systems.
Given an exact magnetic system
(M,g,\mathrm{d}\alpha)
, we can associate with it a unique Lagrangian
L
using (
2.4
). Thus, we can define the Mañé critical value of the magnetic system
(M,g,\mathrm{d}\alpha)
through the Mañé critical value of
L
, i.e.,

c(M,g,\mathrm{d}\alpha):=c(L).

Adaptation to general magnetic systems.

Definition

2.4
can be easily adapted for general magnetic systems in
Definition

2.1
, just as it is done for finite-dimensional magnetic systems in the references
[
9
,
14
,
33
,
34
]
. This adaptation will be carried out in an upcoming work by the author
[
30
,
29
]
, along with some illustrations, but is left out at this point for the sake of simplicity.

This value is intrinsically difficult to calculate for general magnetic systems. Furthermore, according to Contreras’ result
[
15
]
, for magnetic systems
(M,g,\mathrm{d}\alpha)
on closed finite-dimensional Riemannian manifolds, there exists a magnetic geodesic connecting two given points
p,q\in M
with a prescribed kinetic energy
k
when
k>c(L)
. This implies an analogue of the Hopf–Rinow theorem for magnetic geodesics of the system
(M,g,\mathrm{d}\alpha)
with a kinetic energy exceeding Mañé’s critical value. Note, however, that for energy values below Mañé’s critical value, even in finite dimensions, the validity of a Hopf–Rinow theorem remains uncertain.
Considering the infinite-dimensional counterexamples to the Hopf–Rinow theorem
[
10
,
17
]
and the positive results on Banach half-Lie groups by Bauer-Harms-Michor
[
11
]
, this raises a pertinent question:

Question 2.6
.

For a magnetic system
(M,g,\mathrm{d}\alpha)
with
L
Lagrangian and Mañé’s critical value
c(L)
, given two points
p,q\in M
, does there exist a magnetic geodesic with prescribed kinetic energy
k
if
k>c(L)
?

In this paper, we provide an answer to the question posed for an exact infinite-dimensional magnetic system (see
Theorem

7.1
). We prove even more: the Mañé critical value is supercritical in the following sense. For each energy value
k
below Mañé’s critical value
c(L)
, i.e.,
k\leq c(L)
, there exist at least two points
p
and
q
such that no magnetic geodesic with prescribed energy
k
connects
p
and
q
. Conversely, for all energy values
k>c(L)
and for any points
p
and
q
, there always exists a magnetic geodesic with prescribed energy
k
connecting
p
and
q
.
Even for finite-dimensional magnetic systems on closed manifolds, it remains an open question whether Mañé’s critical value is supercritical in the sense described above. In the following, we will reduce the infinite dimensional magnetic systems to finite-dimensional dynamical systems, which are much better understood. This reduction will be made precise through the notion of totally magnetic submanifolds.

2.3.
Magnetomorphism and totally magnetic submanifolds

We will see that a key feature of the magnetic system
(G,g,\varPhi^{*}\mathrm{d}\alpha)
is its large group of symmetries and, as a byproduct, a large family of invariant submanifolds. In
[
4
, §6]
, Albers–Benedetti, and the author introduced a framework to handle these situations in general. For the convenience of the reader, let us recall the key definitions. The symmetries of a general magnetic system
(M,g,\sigma)
are defined by so-called
magnetomorphisms

F\colon M\to M
, which are diffeomorphisms that preserve both
g
and
\sigma
. To be precise:

Definition 2.7
.

[
4
, Def. 6.1]

Let
(M_{1},g_{1},\sigma_{1})
and
(M_{2},g_{2},\sigma_{2})
be magnetic systems. A
magnetomorphism

\Phi\colon M_{1}\to M_{2}
is a diffeomorphism such that

\Phi^{*}g_{2}=g_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad\Phi^{*}\sigma_{2}=\sigma_{1}.

The
group of magnetomorphisms
of a magnetic system
(M,g,\sigma)
is defined as

\mathrm{Mag}(M,g,\sigma):=\left\{\Phi:M\to M\mid\Phi^{*}g=g,\ \Phi^{*}\sigma=\sigma\right\}.

Remark 2.8
.

[
4
, Rmk. 6.3.]

If
\Phi\colon(M_{1},g_{1},\sigma_{1})\to(M_{2},g_{2},\sigma_{2})
is a magnetomorphism and
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
represent the Lorentz forces of the two magnetic systems, then

(2.6)

{\mathrm{d}}\Phi\cdot Y^{1}=Y^{2}_{\Phi}\cdot{\mathrm{d}}\Phi.

As a consequence, magnetomorphisms map magnetic geodesics to magnetic geodesics with the same energy, see
[
4
, Prop. 6.4]
. On the other hand, invariant submanifolds are characterized by so-called
totally magnetic submanifolds

N\subset M
, which ensure that any magnetic geodesic tangent to
N
remains locally contained within
N
. To elaborate:

Definition 2.9
.

[
4
, Def. 6.5]

Let
(M,g_{M},\sigma_{M})
be a magnetic system. An embedded submanifold
N\subset M
is called totally magnetic if, for all magnetic geodesics
\gamma\colon I\to M
such that
\gamma(0)\in N
and
\dot{\gamma}(0)\in T_{\gamma(0)}N
, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
\gamma((-\varepsilon,\varepsilon))\subset N
.

Thus, magnetomorphisms send totally magnetic submanifolds to totally magnetic submanifolds.
When
\sigma=0
, totally magnetic submanifolds revert to the classical notion of totally geodesic submanifolds. In Riemannian geometry, there are many ways to define a totally geodesic submanifold. In particular, a submanifold is totally geodesic if and only if its second fundamental form
\mathrm{II}
vanishes. To provide a similar statement for magnetic subsystems, we introduce the
magnetic second fundamental form

\mathrm{II}^{\mathrm{mag}}
, which combines the second fundamental form
\mathrm{II}
with the difference between the intrinsic and extrinsic Lorentz forces. Precisely:

Definition 2.10
.

[
4
, Def. 6.9.]

The tensor

\mathrm{II}^{\mathrm{mag}}_{q}\colon T_{p}N\to T_{q}N^{\perp},\quad\mathrm{II}^{\mathrm{mag}}_{q}(v):=\mathrm{II}_{q}(v)+Y_{q}^{M}v-Y_{q}^{N}v\quad\forall q\in N,

is called the
magnetic second fundamental form
of the submanifold
N
within the magnetic system
(M,g_{M},\sigma_{M})
.

Now, we can transform the local definition of totally magnetic submanifolds into an infinitesimal characterization and provide several equivalent definitions of totally magnetic submanifolds:

Theorem 2.11
.

[
4
, Thm. 1.4]

Let
(M,g_{M},\sigma_{M})
be a magnetic system. Let
N\subset M
be a closed, embedded submanifold. Denote by
\iota\colon N\to M
the inclusion map and by
g_{N}:=\iota^{*}g_{M}
and
\sigma_{N}:=\iota^{*}\sigma_{M}
the pullback metric and magnetic field. The following statements are equivalent:

(1)

The submanifold
N
is totally magnetic in
(M,g_{M},\sigma_{M})
.

(2)

If
\gamma
is a magnetic geodesic in
\left(N,g_{N},\sigma_{N}\right)
then
\iota\circ\gamma
is a magnetic geodesic in
\left(M,g_{M},\sigma_{M}\right)
.

(3)

The magnetic second fundamental form of
\left(N,g_{N},\sigma_{N}\right)
vanishes identically:

\mathrm{II}^{\mathrm{mag}}_{q}(v)=0\quad\forall(q,v)\in TN.

(4)

The second fundamental form of
(N,g_{N})
vanishes identically

\mathrm{II}_{q}(v)=0\quad\forall(q,v)\in TN.

and one of the following three equivalent conditions holds

(a)

the Lorentz force
Y^{M}
of
(M,g_{M},\sigma_{M})
along
N
is equal to the Lorentz force
Y^{N}
of
(N,g_{N},\sigma_{N})
:

Y^{N}_{q}v=Y^{M}_{q}v\quad\forall(q,v)\in TN;

(b)

the Lorentz force
Y^{M}
leaves the tangent bundle of
N
invariant:

Y_{q}^{M}v\in TN\quad\forall(q,v)\in TN;

(c)

the
g_{M}
-orthogonal of
TN
is contained in the
\sigma_{M}
-orthogonal of
TN
:

(\sigma_{M})_{q}(u,v)=0\quad\forall q\in N,\ u\in T_{q}N,\ v\in T_{q}N^{\perp}.

3.
The magnetic system on
{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}

In this section, we will investigate the magnetic system on an infinite-dimensional sphere in full depth. We compute its Mañé critical value and prove that it is supercritical in the sense described at the end of
Section

2.2
. Consequently, we obtain a Hopf–Rinow type theorem for this infinite-dimensional magnetic system, which is contingent upon whether the prescribed energy is above Mañé’s critical value. The theorem fails if the prescribed energy is below Mañé’s critical value. To prove this, we provide a dynamical reduction theorem to a totally magnetic three-sphere. The magnetic system on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
is fully understood by
[
4
]
. We recall the following all results that are important to us:

3.1.
Toy model: the magnetic system on
{\mathbb{S}^{3}}

Let us begin with introducing the setting in detail. Here the manifold
{\mathbb{S}^{3}}
is the sphere of radius
1
in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
with standard Hermitian product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, the metric
g=\mathrm{Re}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the restriction of the Euclidean metric to
{\mathbb{S}^{3}}
, and the magnetic potential
\alpha
is the standard contact form on
{\mathbb{S}^{3}}
, that is,
\alpha_{z}=\tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{Re}\langle\mathrm{i}z,\cdot\rangle
for all
z\in{\mathbb{S}^{3}}
. The Reeb vector field of
\alpha
is the unique vector field
R
on
{\mathbb{S}^{3}}
such that
{\mathrm{d}}\alpha(R,\cdot)=0
and
\alpha(R)=1
. In this case, we get
R_{z}=2\mathrm{i}z
. The trajectories of the flow

(3.1)

\Phi_{R}^{t}(z)=e^{2\mathrm{i}t}z,\qquad\forall\,t\in{\mathbb{R}},\ z\in{\mathbb{S}^{3}}

are the fibers of the Hopf map
\pi\colon{\mathbb{S}^{3}}\to\mathbb{C}P^{1}
, which sends each point on
{\mathbb{S}^{3}}
to the complex line through it. This is the simplest example of a Zoll Reeb flow, where all orbits of the Reeb vector field are periodic and with the same minimal period
[
1
,
5
]
. Finally,
\ker\alpha
is the contact distribution of
\alpha
and, in our case, coincides with the orthogonal of
R
, for more detail on contact geometry we refere to
[
18
]
. Now we are in a position to state a special case of the main theorem in
[
4
]
, which computes the Mañé critical value of the magnetic system
(\mathbb{S}^{3},g,\mathrm{d}\alpha)
and proves that it is supercritical:

Proposition 3.1
.

[
4
, Thm. 1.1]

The Mañé’s critical value of the system is

c({\mathbb{S}^{3}},g,{\mathrm{d}}\alpha)=\tfrac{1}{2}\|\alpha\|_{\infty}^{2}=\tfrac{1}{8}.

Let
q_{0}
and
q_{1}
be two points on
{\mathbb{S}^{3}}
and denote by
\langle q_{0},q_{1}\rangle
their Hermitian product. For every
k>0
, let
\mathcal{G}_{k}(q_{0},q_{1})
be the set of magnetic geodesics with energy
k
connecting
q_{0}
and
q_{1}
. We have the following three cases

(1)

if
k>\tfrac{1}{8}
, then
\mathcal{G}_{k}(q_{0},q_{1})\neq\varnothing
;

(2)

if
k=\tfrac{1}{8}
, then
\mathcal{G}_{k}(q_{0},q_{1})\neq\varnothing
if and only if
\langle q_{0},q_{1}\rangle\neq 0
;

(3)

if

, we have the following three subcases

(a)

if

, then
\mathcal{G}_{k}(q_{0},q_{1})\neq\varnothing
;

(b)

if
|\langle q_{0},q_{1}\rangle|=\sqrt{1-8k}
, then there are
a_{k},b_{k}\in{\mathbb{R}}
with

such that
\mathcal{G}_{k}(q_{0},q_{1})\neq\varnothing
if and only if
\langle q_{0},q_{1}\rangle=e^{\mathrm{i}(a_{k}+mb_{k})}\sqrt{1-8k}
for some

(c)

if

\mathcal{G}_{k}(q_{0},q_{1})=\varnothing
.

Remarks 3.2
.

Indeed, the Mañé critical value is supercritical in the sense of the end of
Section

(M,g,\mathrm{d}\alpha)
satisfies a Hopf–Rinow theorem if and only if the energy value is greater than the Mañé critical value.

3.2.
The magnetic system
({\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}},\mathcal{G}^{L^{2}},{\mathrm{d}}\alpha)

In this subsection, the magnetic system
({\mathbb{S}^{3}},g,{\mathrm{d}}\alpha)
is generalized to an infinite-dimensional setting, and a dynamical reduction theorem is provided in the sense of totally magnetic submanifolds. This allows for the reduction of the dynamics of the infinite-dimensional system to
({\mathbb{S}^{3}},g,{\mathrm{d}}\alpha)
. The setting is introduced in detail as follows: The construction of the
({\mathbb{S}^{3}},g,{\mathrm{d}}\alpha)
magnetic system relies on the fact that
{\mathbb{S}^{3}}
is a hypersurface in the ambient space
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
, which constitutes a complex Hilbert space. Leveraging the Hilbert space structure of
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
, one can define the round metric and the standard contact form on
{\mathbb{S}^{3}}
, as detailed in
section

3.1
.

Consequently, it is logical to replace the Hilbert space
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
with the classical Hilbert space
L^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{C}})
, consisting of square integrable complex-valued functions. This space is equipped with the standard
L^{2}
product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L^{2}}
, which induces the
L^{2}
norm denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}}
. The sphere of radius one in
L^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{C}})
is denoted by
{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}
, defined as

{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}:=\left\{f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{C}}):\|f\|_{L^{2}}=1\right\}.

Again, the round metric
g={\mathrm{Re}}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the restriction of the Euclidean metric on
L^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{C}})
to
{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}
. Before defining the magnetic potential, it should be noted that, as in the finite-dimensional setting, the tangent space of
{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}
at any point
f
is given by

T_{f}{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}=\left\{F\in L^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{C}}):{\mathrm{Re}}\langle f,F\rangle_{L^{2}}=0\right\}.

Utilizing the Hilbert space structure of
L^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{C}})
, the magnetic potential is defined similarly to the case of the toy model:

Definition 3.3
.

The standard contact form
\alpha
on
{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}
is defined as the linear functional:

\alpha_{f}:T_{f}{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}:F\mapsto\frac{1}{2}{\mathrm{Re}}\langle if,F\rangle_{L^{2}}\quad\forall(f,F)\in T{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}.

Remark 3.4
.

By its definition
\alpha_{f}
is a bounded linear operator in each point
f\in{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}
, thus it is indeed a one-form on
{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}
. For more details on differential forms on Hilbert manifolds, we refer to
[
25
]
.

Remark 3.5
.

To the best of the authors knowledge, there is no uniform definition of a contact form in the infinite-dimensional setting. We use a naive approach by defining a tangent distribution
\xi:=\ker\alpha
and demonstrate that this distribution behaves similarly to that in the finite-dimensional setting. Alternatively, one could use the approach involving a so-called Liouville vector field, as described by Abbondandolo-Majer
[
3
]
.

Note before we move on that as in the finite dimensional setting, the derivative of the standard contact form on
{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}
is equal to the restriction of
{\mathrm{Im}}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
to the sphere, here
{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}
. There exist a unique vector field
R
defined through
{\mathrm{d}}\alpha(R,\cdot)=0
and
\alpha(R)=1
, called the
Reeb vector field
of
\alpha
. It’s an exercise left to the reader that in this case we get
R_{f}=2{\mathrm{i}}f
. The trajectories of the flow of
R

\varPhi^{t}_{R}(f)=e^{2{\mathrm{i}}t}\cdot f\quad t\in{\mathbb{R}},\ f\in{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}

are the fibers of the infinite dimensional Hopfmap
\pi:{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}\mathrm{P}^{\infty}
, which sends each point on
{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}
to the complex line through it. As in the finite dimensional setting we define

Definition 3.6
.

The distribution
\xi:=\ker\alpha
is called the
standard contact structure
on
{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}
.

In order to see that this definition is well-defined we have to prove

Proposition 3.7
.

The contact distribution has codimension one in
T{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}
as a bundle and
T{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}
possesses the orthogonal splitting

T{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}=\xi\oplus\langle R\rangle_{{\mathbb{R}}}.

Proof.

By its definition the image
\mathrm{im}(\alpha_{f})
is of dimension one at each
f\in{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}
, since
\alpha(R)=1
. So
\alpha_{f}
is surjective at each
f\in{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}
and since
\xi=\ker\alpha
is closed by definition so by the fundamental homomorphism theorem in the category of Hilbert spaces we get the following diagram

\textstyle{T_{f}{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\alpha_{f}}
\textstyle{\mathbb{R}}
\textstyle{T_{f}{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}/\xi_{f}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\cong}

commutes for all
f\in{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}
, which finishes the proof.
∎

We now aim to describe the magnetic system
({\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}},\mathcal{G}^{L^{2}},\mathrm{d}\alpha)
in more detail. Following the approach of
[
4
, §3]
, we can derive the magnetic geodesic equation of
({\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}},\mathcal{G}^{L^{2}},\mathrm{d}\alpha)
. As in
[
4
]
, the Levi-Civita connection evaluated along a unit speed magnetic geodesic is given by

(3.2)

\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}\dot{\gamma}=\ddot{\gamma}+\gamma.

On the other hand, the Lorentz force is explicitly given by a rotation of 90 degrees of the orthogonal projection
\pi_{\xi}
of the velocity to the contact distribution, i.e.,

(3.3)

Y_{f}(F)={\mathrm{i}}\pi_{\xi}(h)={\mathrm{i}}\cdot\left(F-{\mathrm{Re}}\langle{\mathrm{i}}f,F\rangle_{L^{2}}\cdot{\mathrm{i}}f\right)\quad\forall(f,F)\in T{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}.

By (
2.2
), (
3.2
), and (
3.3
) specifically, a curve
\gamma
is a magnetic geodesic on
({\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}},\mathcal{G}^{L^{2}},\mathrm{d}\alpha)
with unit speed and of magnetic strength
s
if and only if
\gamma
satisfies

(3.4)

\ddot{\gamma}-{\mathrm{i}}s\dot{\gamma}+\left(1-s\cos\psi\right)\gamma=0,

where
\cos\psi
is a conserved quantity representing the angle between
\dot{\gamma}
and
R
, given by

(3.5)

\cos\psi=\operatorname{Re}\langle{\mathrm{i}}\gamma,\dot{\gamma}\rangle_{L^{2}}.

Remark 3.8
.

The derivation of the magnetic geodesic equation in
{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}
, as referred to in (
3.4
), is left as an exercise for the interested reader, note that it suffices to replace
{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}
with
{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}
in
[
4
, §3]
.

3.3.
Dynamical reduction from
{\mathbb{S}_{l^{2}}^{\infty}}
to
{\mathbb{S}^{3}}

The aim of this subsection is to provide a dynamical reduction theorem, i.e., the dynamics of the magnetic system
\left({\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}},\mathcal{G}^{L^{2}},{\mathrm{d}}\alpha\right)
can be reduced to the magnetic system
\left({\mathbb{S}^{3}},g,{\mathrm{d}}\alpha\right)
. More precisely, we prove that the dynamics of the infinite-dimensional sphere can always be reduced to a totally magnetic three-dimensional sphere. This result represents a special case of a much more general perspective, as it follows directly from the classification of totally magnetic submanifolds of the magnetic system
\left({\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}},\mathcal{G}^{L^{2}},{\mathrm{d}}\alpha\right)
:

Proposition 3.9
.

The group of magnetomorphisms of
\left({\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}},\mathcal{G}^{L^{2}},{\mathrm{d}}\alpha\right)
is the group
\mathcal{U}_{L^{2}}
of unitary operators of the Hilbert space
L^{2}\left({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{C}}\right)
, that is,

\mathrm{Mag}\left({\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}},\mathcal{G}^{L^{2}},{\mathrm{d}}\alpha\right)=\mathcal{U}_{L^{2}}.

The lift of the magnetomorphism group to
T{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}
is Hamiltonian with respect to the twisted symplectic form
\omega_{{\mathrm{d}}\alpha}
, defined as in (
2.3
). Its moment map is given by

\mu\colon T{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}\to\mathfrak{u}^{*},\qquad\mu(f,F)[A]:=\mathcal{G}^{L^{2}}_{f}(Af,F)-\alpha_{f}(Af),\quad\forall\,A\in\mathfrak{u},\ \forall\,(f,F)\in T{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}},

where
\mathfrak{u}^{*}
denotes the dual of the Lie Algebra
\mathfrak{u}:=\mathrm{Lie}(\mathcal{U}_{L^{2}})

A closed connected submanifold
N
of
{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}
with positive dimension is totally magnetic if and only if
N={\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}\cap V
, where
V
is a complex linear subspace of
L^{2}\left({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{C}}\right)
. In this case,
N
is magnetomorphic to the unit sphere
{\mathbb{S}}(V)
of the Hilbert space
V
, that is,

\left(N,\mathcal{G}^{L^{2}},{\mathrm{d}}\alpha\right)\cong\left({\mathbb{S}}(V),\mathcal{G}^{L^{2}},{\mathrm{d}}\alpha\right).

Proof.

The proof follows directly along the lines of
[
4
, Cor. 1.4]
, and the details are left to the interested reader.
∎

Remark 3.10
.

Proposition

3.9
illustrates the difference between totally magnetic submanifolds and totally geodesic submanifolds. Indeed, every unit sphere
{\mathbb{S}}(H)
of a real subspace
H\subset L^{2}\left({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{C}}\right)
is totally geodesic in
{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}
, while only spheres obtained from the intersection of
{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}
with complex subspaces
V
of
L^{2}\left({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{C}}\right)
lead to totally magnetic submanifolds.

As a direct corollary of
Proposition

3.9
, we obtain the aforementioned dynamical reduction theorem:

Corollary 3.11
.

Each magnetic geodesic in
\left({\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}},\mathcal{G}^{L^{2}},{\mathrm{d}}\alpha\right)
with initial values
(f,F)\in T{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}
is contained in a three-dimensional sphere
{\mathbb{S}^{3}}(f,F)
, obtained by intersecting
{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}
with the complex plane
\mathrm{span}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\{f,F\}
.

With this dynamical reduction theorem in mind, it should be no surprise that we can generalize the interpolation property from the magnetic geodesic flow on
({\mathbb{S}^{3}},g,{\mathrm{d}}\alpha)
to the infinite-dimensional setting. Recall that the contact angle
\psi
((
3.5
)) is a conserved quantity. For every
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the magnetic geodesics of strength
s
with
\psi=0
, respectively
\psi=\pi
, are exactly the orbits of the Reeb vector field with the positive, respectively negative, orientation. Magnetic geodesics with
\psi=\tfrac{\pi}{2}
are tangent to the contact distribution
\ker\alpha
.

As a consequence of
Corollary

3.11
and
[
4
, Cor. 1.8]
, we obtain:

Corollary 3.12
.

The magnetic geodesic flow
({\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}},\mathcal{G}^{L^{2}},{\mathrm{d}}\alpha)
is an interpolation between the sub-Riemannian geodesic flow on
({\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}},\mathcal{G}^{L^{2}},\ker\alpha)
and the Reeb flow of
\alpha
on
{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}
, where the interpolation parameter is the contact angle
\psi
. ∎

Before moving on, we illustrate how we can use the dynamical reduction in the sense of
Corollary

3.11
to show that the magnetic system
\left({\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}},\mathcal{G}^{L^{2}},{\mathrm{d}}\alpha\right)
possesses infinitely many conserved quantities:

Corollary 3.13
.

The magnetic system
\left({\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}},\mathcal{G}^{L^{2}},{\mathrm{d}}\alpha\right)
possesses infinitely many conserved quantities.

Proof.

By
Corollary

3.11
, we can reduce the magnetic system to
\left({\mathbb{S}^{3}},g,{\mathrm{d}}\alpha\right)
. This reduced system is, by
[
13
, Appendix A]
, totally integrable. In addition, the orthogonal projection of the flow of the magnetic system on
T{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}
to
T{\mathbb{S}^{3}}^{\perp}
provides infinitely many integrals of motion, as
T{\mathbb{S}^{3}}^{\perp}
is invariant under the magnetic flow, after choosing initial conditions in
T{\mathbb{S}^{3}}
, and is infinite-dimensional.
∎

In the next subsection, we illustrate the importance of
Corollary

3.11
in providing a generalization of
Proposition

3.1
to the infinite-dimensional magnetic system
\left({\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}},\mathcal{G}^{L^{2}},{\mathrm{d}}\alpha\right)
.

3.4.
Mané’s Critical Value and a Hopf–Rinow Theorem for
\left({\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}},\mathcal{G}^{L^{2}},{\mathrm{d}}\alpha\right)

In this subsection, we compute Mané’s critical value for the magnetic system
\left({\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}},\mathcal{G}^{L^{2}},{\mathrm{d}}\alpha\right)
and illustrate how it can be used to obtain a Hopf–Rinow theorem for
\left({\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}},\mathcal{G}^{L^{2}},{\mathrm{d}}\alpha\right)
as a generalization of
Proposition

3.1
.

We begin by computing Mané’s critical value for the magnetic system
\left({\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}},\mathcal{G}^{L^{2}},{\mathrm{d}}\alpha\right)
:

Lemma 3.14
.

Mané’s critical value for the magnetic system is

c\left({\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}},\mathcal{G}^{L^{2}},{\mathrm{d}}\alpha\right)=\frac{1}{8}.

Proof.

By the definition of Mané’s critical value (
Definition

2.4
), it suffices to prove that, first, one can always find a periodic orbit with negative action
S_{L+k}
and prescribed energy below
\frac{1}{8}
, and second, the action
S_{L+k}
is positive for all energies above
\frac{1}{8}
.
The proof proceeds by verifying these two conditions. First, one can verify that for all
k<\frac{1}{8}
, the curve
\gamma(t)=\mathrm{id}_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}\cdot e^{{\mathrm{i}}t\sqrt{k}}
is a periodic orbit with negative action. Additionally, we can bound the magnetic Lagrangian from below as follows:

L(f,F)+\frac{1}{8}=\frac{1}{2}{\mathrm{Re}}\langle F,F\rangle_{L^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}{\mathrm{Re}}\langle{\mathrm{i}}f,F\rangle_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{8}\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\|F\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}\geq 0.

Thus,
S_{L+k}\geq 0
for all
k\geq\frac{1}{8}
, which finishes the proof.
∎

Now we are in the position to generalize
Proposition

3.1
to the infinite-dimensional setting as a combination of
Corollary

3.11
and
Proposition

3.1
. That is, we will prove an infinite-dimensional Hopf–Rinow theorem for magnetic flows.

Theorem 3.15
.

Mané’s critical value of the system is

c({\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}},\mathcal{G}^{L^{2}},{\mathrm{d}}\alpha)=\tfrac{1}{2}\|\alpha\|_{\infty}^{2}=\tfrac{1}{8}.

Let
q_{0}
and
q_{1}
be two points on
{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}
and denote by
\langle q_{0},q_{1}\rangle_{L^{2}}
their
L^{2}
-Hermitian product. For every
k>0
, let
\mathcal{G}_{k}(q_{0},q_{1})
be the set of magnetic geodesics with energy
k
connecting
q_{0}
and
q_{1}
. We have the following three cases:

(1)

If
k>\tfrac{1}{8}
, then
\mathcal{G}_{k}(q_{0},q_{1})\neq\varnothing
.

(2)

If
k=\tfrac{1}{8}
, then
\mathcal{G}_{k}(q_{0},q_{1})\neq\varnothing
if and only if
\langle q_{0},q_{1}\rangle\neq 0
.

(3)

If
k<\tfrac{1}{8}
, we have the following three subcases:

(a)

If
|\langle q_{0},q_{1}\rangle|>\sqrt{1-8k}
, then
\mathcal{G}_{k}(q_{0},q_{1})\neq\varnothing
.

(b)

If
|\langle q_{0},q_{1}\rangle|=\sqrt{1-8k}
, then there are
a_{k},b_{k}\in{\mathbb{R}}
with
b_{k}>0
such that
\mathcal{G}_{k}(q_{0},q_{1})\neq\varnothing
if and only if
\langle q_{0},q_{1}\rangle=e^{\mathrm{i}(a_{k}+mb_{k})}\sqrt{1-8k}
for some
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

(c)

If
|\langle q_{0},q_{1}\rangle|<\sqrt{1-8k}
, then
\mathcal{G}_{k}(q_{0},q_{1})=\varnothing
.

Remark 3.16
.

This theorem allows us to compare the magnetic geodesic flow and the geodesic flow by means of a Hopf–Rinow theorem, showing that a value of the energy is supercritical if and only if all pairs of points on the sphere can be connected by a magnetic geodesic with that value of energy, where Mañé’s critical value serves as a threshold.

Proof.

This follows directly from
Proposition

3.1
,
Corollary

3.11
, and
Lemma

3.14
.
∎

To continue our discussion, it is convenient to switch to the description of magnetic geodesics of
({\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}},\mathcal{G}^{L^{2}},{\mathrm{d}}\alpha)
with energy
k
as magnetic geodesics of unit speed for the family of systems
({\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}},\mathcal{G}^{L^{2}},s{\mathrm{d}}\alpha)
, where
s=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2k}}
is now referred to as the strength of the magnetic geodesic. In this description, Mané’s critical value corresponds to

s_{0}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\tfrac{1}{8}}}=2.

Before proceeding, let us mention that, as a corollary of
Corollary

3.11
, we can generalize the results of
[
4
, Thm. 1.12, Cor. 1.14]
to the infinite-dimensional setting. In other words, one can recover all magnetic systems on
\mathbb{C}P^{\infty}
simultaneously through the magnetic system on
{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}
. Here,
\mathbb{C}P^{\infty}
denotes the infinite-dimensional projective space, the projectivization of
L^{2}\left({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{C}}\right)
, equipped with the Fubini-Study form
\omega^{FS}
and the Fubini-Study metric
\mathcal{G}^{FS}
; this serves as the prototype of an infinite-dimensional Kähler manifold (see
[

, §2]
).

Corollary 3.17
.

If
\gamma
is a unit-speed magnetic geodesic on
({\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}},\mathcal{G}^{L^{2}},s\,{\mathrm{d}}\alpha)
,
s\geq 0
, with contact angle
\psi\in[0,\pi]
, then, up to orientation-preserving reparametrization,
\pi(\gamma)
is a unit-speed magnetic geodesic of
\big(\mathbb{C}P^{\infty},\mathcal{G}^{FS},a_{s}(\psi)\omega^{FS}\big)
, where

(3.6)

a_{s}(\psi)=\frac{2\cos\psi-s}{\sin\psi},\qquad\psi\in(0,\pi).

In particular, the projected curve
\pi(\gamma)
is a geodesic circle with radius

r_{s}(\psi):=\frac{1}{a_{s}(\psi)}\in\left[0,\frac{\pi}{2}\right].

We observe that the function
a_{s}
is a bijection if and only if
0\leq s<2
. This fact can be summarized in the following statement:

Corollary 3.18
.

For a fixed
s\in[0,2)
, the unit-speed magnetic geodesic flow on
({\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}},\mathcal{G}^{L^{2}},s\,{\mathrm{d}}\alpha)
covers
all
magnetic systems
\big(\mathbb{C}P^{\infty},\mathcal{G}^{FS},r\omega^{FS}\big)
,
r\,\in\mathbb{R}
.

Remark 3.19
.

Here, Mané’s critical value provides a natural explanation for the threshold of the magnetic strength in
Corollary

3.18
.

4.
The Madelung transform as magnetomorphism

The aim of this section is to prove that we extend the
Madelung transform
as an isometry between an infinite-dimensional Lie group and the open subset of nowhere-vanishing functions on the
L^{2}
-sphere to a magnetomorphism.

We begin by introducing the setting in detail. Let
{\mathbb{S}}^{1}={\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
denote the unit circle, and let
\mathrm{Diff}_{0}^{m}\left({\mathbb{S}}^{1}\right)
represent the half-Lie group of diffeomorphisms of
{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
of Sobolev with smooth right multiplication and only continuous left multiplication; see
[
11
]
for the notion of a half-Lie group. This group consists of all diffeomorphisms of
{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
of Sobolev class
H^{m}
that fix a designated point on
{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
. Unless stated otherwise, we assume
m>\frac{5}{2}
. Note that
\mathrm{Diff}_{0}^{m}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})\cong\mathrm{Dens}^{m}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})
, where
\mathrm{Dens}^{m}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})
denotes the space of probability densities on
{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
of Sobolev class
H^{m}
, as discussed in
[
16
, §5]
and
[
23
]
.
We further denote by
{\mathbb{S}}^{1}_{4\pi}
the circle of length
4\pi
, and by
H^{m}\left({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{S}}^{1}_{4\pi}\right)
the space of maps of Sobolev class
H^{m}
. The half-Lie group
G^{m}
is then defined as

(4.1)

G^{m}=\mathrm{Diff}_{0}^{m}\left({\mathbb{S}}^{1}\right)\rtimes H^{m-1}\left({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{S}}^{1}_{4\pi}\right),

where the explicit group product structure is described in
[
28
, §2]
. This group carries a bi-invariant metric, the
\dot{H}^{1}
-metric, defined at the identity
(\mathrm{id},0)
by

(4.2)

\mathcal{G}^{\dot{H}^{1}}_{(\mathrm{id},0)}\left((u,\rho),(v,\psi)\right):=\langle(u,\rho),(v,\psi)\rangle_{(\mathrm{id},0)}^{\dot{H}^{1}}=\frac{1}{4}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}u_{x}v_{x}+\rho\psi\,\mathrm{d}x,

where the tangent space at the identity is
T_{(\mathrm{id},0)}G^{m}\cong H^{m}_{0}\left({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{R}}\right)\times H^{m-1}\left({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{R}}\right)
. Here,
H^{m}_{0}\left({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{R}}\right)
denotes the space of all functions of Sobolev class
H^{m}
with zero mean. We can extend the metric in (
4.2
) by right invariance to all points
(\varphi,\tau)\in G^{m}
by

(4.3)

\mathcal{G}^{\dot{H}^{1}}_{(\varphi,\tau)}\left((U_{1},U_{2}),(V_{1},V_{2})\right):=\langle(U_{1},U_{2}),(V_{1},V_{2})\rangle_{(\varphi,\tau)}^{\dot{H}^{1}}=\frac{1}{4}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}\frac{U_{1x}V_{1x}}{\varphi_{x}}+U_{2}V_{2}\varphi_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x,

where
(U_{1},U_{2}),(V_{1},V_{2})\in T_{(\varphi,\tau)}G^{m}\cong H^{m}_{0}\left({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{R}}\right)\times H^{m-1}\left({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{R}}\right)
. For more details, we refer again to
[
28
, §2]
.

Furthermore, we denote by
\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty,m}
the
L^{2}
-unit sphere of all functions of Sobolev class
H^{m}
, and the open subset of nowhere-vanishing functions in
\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty,m}
by

\mathcal{U}^{m}:=\left\{f\in\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty,m}:f(x)\neq 0,\quad\forall x\in{\mathbb{S}}^{1}\right\},

which naturally carries, as an open subset of
{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}
, a Riemannian metric obtained by restricting the round metric
\mathcal{G}^{L^{2}}(\cdot,\cdot):={\mathrm{Re}}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L^{2}}
to
U^{m}
.

Lennels proved in
[
28
, Thm. 3.1]
that the Madelung transform
\varPhi
is an isometry between
\left(G^{m},\mathcal{G}^{\dot{H}^{1}}\right)
and
\left(\mathcal{U}^{m-1},\mathcal{G}^{L^{2}}\right)
, i.e.,

(4.4)

\varPhi:\left(G^{m},\mathcal{G}^{\dot{H}^{1}}\right)\longrightarrow\left(\mathcal{U}^{m-1},\mathcal{G}^{L^{2}}\right):(\varphi,\tau)\mapsto\sqrt{\varphi_{x}}\ e^{{\mathrm{i}}\frac{\tau}{2}}

is an isometry for all
m>\frac{5}{2}
. By restricting the magnetic system
\left({\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}},\mathcal{G}^{L^{2}},{\mathrm{d}}\alpha\right)
to the open subset
U^{m}
, we naturally obtain, by a slight abuse of notation, the magnetic system
\left(U^{m},\mathcal{G}^{L^{2}},{\mathrm{d}}\alpha\right)
.
By pulling back the magnetic field
{\mathrm{d}}\alpha
with the Madelung transform
\varPhi
(see (
4.4
)), we naturally obtain the magnetic system
\left(G^{m},\mathcal{G}^{\dot{H}^{1}},\varPhi^{*}{\mathrm{d}}\alpha\right)
.
Next, we prove that the Madelung transform is indeed a magnetomorphism:

Theorem 4.1
.

The Madelung transform
\varPhi
, see (
4.4
), is a magnetomorphism, in the sense of
Definition

2.7
, between the magnetic systems
\left(G^{m},\mathcal{G}^{\dot{H}^{1}},\varPhi^{*}{\mathrm{d}}\alpha\right)
and
\left(\mathcal{U}^{m-1},\mathcal{G}^{L^{2}},{\mathrm{d}}\alpha\right)
for all
m>\frac{5}{2}
.

Remark 4.2
.

In particular,
Theorem

4.1
also proves that the Madelung transform
\varPhi
defines a magnetomorphism between the two above-mentioned magnetic systems in the category of inverse limit Hilbert manifolds or tame Fréchet manifolds. See
[
16
]
and
[
22
]
, along with the references therein, for an explanation of these notions of smoothness.

Proof.

Follows directly from
Definition

2.7
in combination with the fact that the Madelung transform, (
4.4
), is an isometry, as established by
[
28
, Thm. 3.1]
.
∎

Before moving on, we note that the magnetic system
\left(G^{m},\mathcal{G}^{\dot{H}^{1}},\varPhi^{*}{\mathrm{d}}\alpha\right)
arises naturally through the lens of infinite-dimensional differential geometry. Here, we work in the smooth category and denote by
G
the
C^{\infty}
version of
G^{m}
in (
4.1
). This is a regular Lie group, following the notation in

24

First, the infinite-dimensional Hopf fibration
\pi:{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}P^{\infty}

Boothby-Wang bundle
or
prequantization bundle
in the sense of
[
18

. Here,
\mathbb{C}P^{\infty}
denotes the infinite-dimensional projective space, the projectivization of
L^{2}\left({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{C}}\right)
, equipped with the Fubini-Study form
\omega^{FS}
; see
[
21
, §2]
for more details.
If we denote by
\mathbb{P}U
the projectivization of
U

U

\left(\mathbb{P}U,\mathcal{G}^{FS},\omega^{FS}\right)
is, as a special case of the beautiful construction by Khesin-Misiolek-Modin in
[
20

21
]
, Kähler isomorphic to the cotangent bundle of the space of probability densities
T^{*}\mathrm{Dens}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})
, equipped with the so-called Sasaki-Fisher-Rao metric
\mathcal{G}^{\text{Rao}}

{\mathrm{d}}\lambda

[

5.
The (M2HS) as a magnetic geodesic equation

The aim of this section is to establish that the solutions of the highly nonlinear system of partial differential equations introduced as the
magnetic two-component Hunter–Saxton system
(
M2HS
) are in one-to-one correspondence with the magnetic geodesics of the magnetic system
\left(G,\mathcal{G}^{\dot{H}^{1}},\Phi^{*}\mathrm{d}\alpha\right)
. Before going into detail, we provide a brief overview of this section.

In
Section

5.1
, we state the main theorem of this section, namely the above-mentioned duality result. The subsequent sections,
Section

5.2
–
Section

5.4
, are devoted to the proof of
Theorem

5.1
. In
Section

5.2
, we derive the Lorentz force associated with the magnetic system appearing in
Theorem

5.1
. In
Section

5.3
, we derive the corresponding magnetic geodesic equation. Finally, in
Section

5.4
, we complete the proof of
Theorem

5.1
by combining the results of the previous sections.

We conclude the section in
Section

5.5
by proving a statement of independent interest, namely that (
M2HS
) possesses infinitely many conserved quantities.

5.1.
Statement of the Duality Theorem

To prepare for this, we introduce the following notation. For a curve in
\mathrm{Diff}_{0}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
, we denote its time and spatial derivatives by
\varphi_{t}
and
\varphi_{x}
, respectively. The same convention applies to vector fields
u
, where
\varphi
is replaced by
u
.

Theorem 5.1
.

A
C^{2}
-curve
(\varphi,\tau):[0,T)\longrightarrow G^{m}
, where
T>0
is the maximal existence time, is a magnetic geodesic of the system
\left(G^{m},\mathcal{G}^{\dot{H}^{1}},\varPhi^{*}\mathrm{d}\alpha\right)
with
m>\frac{5}{2}
if and only if

(u=\varphi_{t}\circ\varphi^{-1},\rho=\tau_{t}\circ\varphi^{-1})\in C\left([0,T),T_{(\mathrm{id},0)}G^{m}\right)\cap C^{1}\left([0,T),T_{(\mathrm{id},0)}G^{m-1}\right)

is a solution of the magnetic two-component Hunter-Saxton system:

(M2HS)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{tx}=&-\frac{1}{2}u_{x}^{2}-u\cdot u_{xx}+\frac{1}{2}\rho^{2}-(s\rho+2(c^{2}-s\delta)),\\
\rho_{t}=&-(\rho u)_{x}+su_{x},\end{cases}

where
c^{2}
, the
\dot{H}^{1}
-energy of the system, is a conserved quantity (integral of motion) given by

c^{2}=\mathcal{G}^{\dot{H}^{1}}_{(\varphi,\tau)}\left((\varphi_{t},\tau_{t}),(\varphi_{t},\tau_{t})\right)=\langle(u,\rho),(u,\rho)\rangle_{\dot{H}^{1}}=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}u_{x}^{2}+\rho^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Additionally, the contact angle
\delta
is another conserved quantity, given by

\delta=\left\langle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\varPhi(\varphi,\tau),\mathrm{i}\cdot\varPhi(\varphi,\tau)\right\rangle_{L^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}\rho\,\mathrm{d}x.

Remarks 5.2
.

Novelty:
The (
M2HS
) is explicitly described as an infinite-dimensional magnetic geodesic equation.

Special case (
s=0
):
When
s=0
, the system reduces to the two-component Hunter-Saxton system (2HS). As shown in
[
28
]
, the (2HS) corresponds precisely to the geodesic equation on
(G,\mathcal{G}^{\dot{H}^{1}})
.

Geometric insight:
The (M2HS) represents a linear deformation in
(u_{x},\rho)
of the (2HS). This deformation reflects the geometry of the equations, as the magnetic geodesic equation is itself a linear deformation of the geodesic equation in the velocity field; see (
2.2
).

Special case (
\rho\equiv s
):
By choosing
\rho\equiv s
and differentiating the resulting partial differential equation once with respect to
x
, we recover the Hunter-Saxton equation; see
[
23
,
28
]
.

Maximal existence time:
The maximal existence time is independent of the Sobolev order
H^{m}
, following exactly the lines of
[
28
, §4]
.

Tangent space at identity:
For the convenience of the reader, we recall that
T_{(\mathrm{id},0)}G^{m}\cong H^{m}_{0}\left({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{R}}\right)\times H^{m-1}\left({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{R}}\right)
.

(
M2HS
) as a magnetic Euler–Arnold equation.
In
[
31
]
, the author introduces a broader framework known as the
magnetic Euler–
Arnold equation
. The system (
M2HS
) admits a formulation within this framework; however, its derivation—carried out in this chapter—is essential to rigorously establish that (
M2HS
) indeed fits into the magnetic Euler–Arnold setting.

The proof of
Theorem

5.1
consists of several steps. First, we need to explicitly express the pullback
\varPhi^{*}\mathrm{d}\alpha
to use (
2.1
) to compute the Lorentz force of the magnetic system
\left(G,\mathcal{G}^{\dot{H}^{1}},\varPhi^{*}\mathrm{d}\alpha\right)
. Along the way, we prove that the magnetic field
\varPhi^{*}\mathrm{d}\alpha
is right invariant under the group action of

Theorem

.

5.2.
The Lorentz force

We begin with the following lemma:

Lemma 5.3
.

The magnetic field
\varPhi^{*}\mathrm{d}\alpha\in\Omega^{2}(G^{m})
is given at
(\varphi,\alpha)\in G^{m}
by

\left(\varPhi^{*}\mathrm{d}\alpha\right)_{(\varphi,\alpha)}\left((U_{1},U_{2}),(V_{1},V_{2})\right)={\mathrm{Im}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\frac{1}{4|\varphi_{x}|}\left(V_{1x}U_{2}-U_{1x}V_{2}\right)\varphi_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x

for all
(U_{1},U_{2}),(V_{1},V_{2})\in T_{(\varphi,\alpha)}G^{m}
. Furthermore,
\varPhi^{*}\mathrm{d}\alpha
is right invariant with respect to the right action of
G^{m}
, i.e.,

\left(\varPhi^{*}\mathrm{d}\alpha\right)_{(\varphi,\alpha)}\left((U_{1},U_{2}),(V_{1},V_{2})\right)=\left(\varPhi^{*}\mathrm{d}\alpha\right)_{(\mathrm{id},0)}\left((U_{1},U_{2})\circ\varphi^{-1},(V_{1},V_{2})\circ\varphi^{-1}\right)

holds for all
(\varphi,\alpha)\in G^{m}
and all
(U_{1},U_{2}),(V_{1},V_{2})\in T_{(\varphi,\alpha)}G^{m}
.

Proof.

Recall that the pullback of
\mathrm{d}\alpha
is formally defined as

(5.1)

\varPhi^{*}_{(\cdot)}\mathrm{d}\alpha(\cdot,\cdot)=\mathrm{d}\alpha_{\varPhi(\cdot)}(D\varPhi(\cdot),D\varPhi(\cdot)),

where the derivative of the Madelung transform is, by
[
28
, Thm. 3.1]
, at the point
(\varphi,\tau)\in G^{m}
given by

(5.2)

\left(D\varPhi\right)_{(\varphi,\alpha)}\left(U_{1},U_{2}\right)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\varphi_{x}}}\left(U_{1x}+\mathrm{i}U_{2}\varphi_{x}\right)\quad\forall(U_{1},U_{2})\in T_{(\varphi,\alpha)}G^{m}.

Recall from the paragraph following
Definition

3.3
that the derivative of the standard contact form is given as the restriction of
{\mathrm{Im}}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L^{2}}
to
T{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}
. This, in combination with (
5.1
) and (
5.2
), gives the expression of
\varPhi^{*}\mathrm{d}\alpha
we want to prove.

In addition, a computation that follows exactly the lines of the fact that
\mathcal{G}^{\dot{H}^{1}}
, see
[
28
, §2]
, is a right-invariant Riemannian metric on
G^{m}
proves that
\varPhi^{*}\mathrm{d}\alpha
is right invariant.
∎

The Lorentz force of the magnetic system
\left(G^{m},\mathcal{G}^{\dot{H}^{1}},\varPhi^{*}{\mathrm{d}}\alpha\right)
is uniquely determined by
\mathcal{G}^{\dot{H}^{1}}
and
\varPhi^{*}{\mathrm{d}}\alpha
. From (
4.3
) and

), is given by

(5.3)

Y^{\dot{H^{1}}}{(\varphi,\tau)}\left(U_{1},U_{2}\right)=\left(J_{\dot{H}^{1}}\right)_{(\varphi,\tau)}\circ\left(\pi_{\xi_{\dot{H^{1}}}}\right)_{(\varphi,\tau)}(U_{1},U_{2}),

for all
\left((\varphi,\tau),(U_{1},U_{2})\right)\in TG^{m}
.

Here,
J^{\dot{H}^{1}}
is an almost complex structure defined on the infinite-dimensional contact-type distribution
\xi_{\dot{H^{1}}}=\ker\varPhi^{*}\alpha
. Additionally,
\pi_{\xi_{\dot{H^{1}}}}
denotes the
\mathcal{G}^{\dot{H}^{1}}
-orthogonal projection of
TG^{M}
onto
\xi_{\dot{H^{1}}}
, which will be defined and computed in full detail later.

Following the computation in
Lemma

5.3
, the pullback of the contact form
\alpha
with respect to
\varPhi
on
G^{m}
is given by

(5.4)

\left(\varPhi^{*}\alpha\right)_{(\varphi,\sigma)}\left(U_{1},U_{2}\right)=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}U_{2}\varphi_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x,\quad\forall\left((\varphi,\tau),(U_{1},U_{2})\right)\in TG^{m}.

Thus, by (
5.4
), the kernel of
\varPhi^{*}\alpha
at the point
(\varphi,\tau)
, called
the contact distribution
\xi_{\dot{H^{1}}}
, is given by

(5.5)

\left(\xi_{\dot{H}^{1}}\right)_{(\varphi,\tau)}:=\ker\left(\varPhi^{*}\alpha\right)_{(\varphi,\tau)}=\left\{(U_{1},U_{2})\in T_{(\varphi,\tau)}G^{m}:\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}U_{2}\varphi_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x=0\right\}.

Thus, by (
5.5
), the
\mathcal{G}^{\dot{H}^{1}}
-orthogonal projection of
TG^{M}
onto
\xi_{\dot{H^{1}}}
at the point
(\varphi,\tau)\in G^{m}
, where the dependence on the point is omitted for simplicity, is expressed as:

(5.6)

\left(\pi_{\xi_{\dot{H^{1}}}}\right)_{(\varphi,\tau)}:T_{(\varphi,\tau)}G^{m}\to\left(\xi_{\dot{H^{1}}}\right)_{(\varphi,\tau)},\quad(U_{1},U_{2})\mapsto\left(U_{1},U_{2}-\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}U_{2}\varphi_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x\right).

Remark 5.4
.

At this point, it is important to note that, since the Madelung transform is a magnetomorphism by
Theorem

4.1
, and since the contact distribution
\xi
on
{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}
has codimension one in

Furthermore, the map

is a well-defined bundle endomorphism, which at

is defined as:

(5.7)

\left(J_{\dot{H}^{1}}\right)_{(\varphi,\tau)}:\left(\xi_{\dot{H}^{1}}\right)_{(\varphi,\tau)}\longrightarrow\left(\xi_{\dot{H}^{1}}\right)_{(\varphi,\tau)},\quad(U_{1},U_{2})\mapsto\left(\left(y\mapsto-\int_{0}^{y}U_{2}\varphi_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x\right),\frac{U_{1x}}{\varphi_{x}}\right).

It is another exercise in logic that

is the unique almost complex structure on

that is compatible with

and
\mathcal{G}^{\dot{H}^{1}}

\left(J_{\dot{H}^{1}}\right)^{2}=-\mathrm{id}_{\xi_{\dot{H^{1}}}}\quad\text{and}\quad\left.\left(\varPhi^{*}{\mathrm{d}}\alpha\right)\right|_{\xi_{\dot{H^{1}}}}(\cdot,J_{\dot{H}^{1}}\cdot)=\left.\mathcal{G}^{\dot{H}^{1}}\right|_{\xi_{\dot{H^{1}}}}(\cdot,\cdot),

where the details of the computation are left to the reader. By using (

5.6

Equation

5.7

is right-invariant (since both

Lemma 5.5
.

The Lorentz force
Y^{\dot{H}^{1}}
of the magnetic system
\left(G^{m},\mathcal{G}^{\dot{H}^{1}},\varPhi^{*}{\mathrm{d}}\alpha\right)
at the point
(\varphi,\tau)
is given by

Y^{\dot{H}^{1}}_{(\varphi,\tau)}:T_{(\varphi,\tau)}G^{m}\to T_{(\varphi,\tau)}G^{m},\quad(U_{1},U_{2})\mapsto\left(\left(y\mapsto-\int_{0}^{y}\left(U_{2}-\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}U_{2}\varphi_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\varphi_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x\right),\frac{U_{1x}}{\varphi_{x}}\right).

Furthermore,
Y^{\dot{H}^{1}}
is right-invariant with respect to the right action of
G^{m}
, i.e.,

Y^{\dot{H}^{1}}_{(\mathrm{id},0)}((u,\rho))=Y^{\dot{H}^{1}}_{(\varphi,\tau)}((u\circ\varphi,\rho\circ\varphi))

for all
(\varphi,\tau)\in G^{m}
and all
(u,\rho)\in T_{(\mathrm{id},0)}G^{m}
.

5.3.
Derivation of the magnetic geodesic equation

The left-hand side of the magnetic geodesic equation (
2.2
) is the Levi-Civita connection evaluated along a curve. For the magnetic system
\left(G^{m},\mathcal{G}^{\dot{H}^{1}},\varPhi^{*}{\mathrm{d}}\alpha\right)
, this is given by
[
28
, Cor 3.3]
as:

(5.8)

\left(\nabla_{(u,\rho)}(u,\rho)\right)_{(\mathrm{id},0)}=\begin{pmatrix}u_{t}+uu_{x}\\
\rho_{t}+u\rho_{x}\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{1}{2}A^{-1}\partial_{x}\left(u_{x}^{2}+\rho^{2}\right)\\
-\rho u_{x}\end{pmatrix},

where
A^{-1}
is the inverse of the so-called inertia operator
A
(see
[
8
,
37
]
for general language), given by:

(5.9)

A^{-1}f(x)=-\int_{0}^{x}\int_{0}^{y}f(z)\,{\mathrm{d}}z\,{\mathrm{d}}y+x\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}\int_{0}^{y}f(z)\,{\mathrm{d}}z\,{\mathrm{d}}y,\quad x\in{\mathbb{S}}^{1}.

By the right invariance of
\mathcal{G}^{\dot{H}^{1}}
, the Levi-Civita connection of
(G^{m},\mathcal{G}^{\dot{H}^{1}})
is also right invariant. Thus, using (
5.8
), the covariant derivative along
(\varphi,\tau)\in G^{m}
applied to
(\varphi_{t},\tau_{t})\in T_{(\varphi,\tau)}G^{m}
reads as:

\nabla_{(\varphi_{t},\tau_{t})}(\varphi_{t},\tau_{t})=\begin{pmatrix}\left(u_{t}+uu_{x}\right)\circ\varphi-\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{0}^{\varphi(\cdot)}\big(u_{x}^{2}(y)+\rho^{2}(y)\big)\,{\mathrm{d}}y-\varphi(\cdot)\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}\big(u_{x}^{2}(y)+\rho^{2}(y)\big)\,{\mathrm{d}}y\right)\\
\left(\rho_{t}+u\rho_{x}+u_{x}\rho\right)\circ\varphi\end{pmatrix}.

Using
Lemma

5.5
, and the fact that, by
Theorem

4.1
, the Madelung transform maps conserved quantities to conserved quantities as a magnetomorphism, we obtain the following result:

Proposition 5.6
.

The magnetic geodesic equation of
\left(G^{m},\mathcal{G}^{\dot{H}^{1}},\varPhi^{*}{\mathrm{d}}\alpha\right)
at the point
(\varphi,\tau)\in G^{m}
is given by:

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\left(u_{t}+uu_{x}\right)\circ\varphi-\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{0}^{\varphi(\cdot)}\big(u_{x}^{2}(y)+\rho^{2}(y)\big){\mathrm{d}}y-\varphi(\cdot)(c^{2}-s\delta)\right)-s\int_{0}^{\varphi(\cdot)}\rho(y){\mathrm{d}}y\\
\left(\rho_{t}+(u\rho)_{x}-su_{x}\rho\right)\circ\varphi\end{pmatrix}=0,

where
c^{2}
, the
\dot{H}^{1}
-energy of the system, is a conserved quantity (integral of motion) given by:

c^{2}=\mathcal{G}^{\dot{H}^{1}}_{(\varphi,\tau)}\left((\varphi_{t},\tau_{t}),(\varphi_{t},\tau_{t})\right)=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\big(u_{x}^{2}+\rho^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Additionally, the contact angle
\delta=\cos(\psi)
is another conserved quantity, given by:

\delta=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}\rho\,\mathrm{d}x.

5.4.
Proof of
Theorem

5.1

If we evaluate
Y^{\dot{H^{1}}}
in
Lemma

5.5
at
(\mathrm{id},0)
and insert it with (
5.8
) into (
2.2
), we obtain that if
(\varphi,\tau)
is a magnetic geodesic in
\left(G^{m},\mathcal{G}^{\dot{H}^{1}},\varPhi^{*}{\mathrm{d}}\alpha\right)
, then
(u,\rho):=\left(\varphi_{t}\circ\varphi^{-1},\tau_{t}\circ\varphi^{-1}\right)
solves:

(5.10)

\begin{pmatrix}u_{t}+uu_{x}\\
\rho_{t}+u\rho_{x}\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{1}{2}A^{-1}\partial_{x}\left(u_{x}^{2}+\rho^{2}\right)\\
-\rho u_{x}\end{pmatrix}=s\begin{pmatrix}\left(y\mapsto-\int_{0}^{y}\left(\rho-\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}\rho\,{\mathrm{d}}x\right){\mathrm{d}}x\right)\\
u_{x}\end{pmatrix}.

By differentiating the first row of (
5.10
) with respect to
x
, we get:

(5.11)

u_{tx}+u_{x}^{2}+uu_{xx}+\partial_{x}\frac{1}{2}A^{-1}\partial_{x}\left(u_{x}^{2}+\rho^{2}\right)=-s\left(\rho-\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}\rho\,{\mathrm{d}}x\right),

where one can show with a standard computation (left to the reader) that:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}A^{-1}\partial_{x}\left(u_{x}^{2}+\rho^{2}\right)=-\frac{1}{2}\left(u_{x}^{2}(x)+\rho^{2}(x)\right)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}\left(u_{x}^{2}+\rho^{2}\right)\,{\mathrm{d}}x.

Using
Proposition

5.6
and (
5.11
), we find that:

u_{tx}+\frac{1}{2}u_{x}^{2}+uu_{xx}-\frac{1}{2}\rho^{2}=-(s\rho+2(c^{2}-\delta s)),

where
c^{2}
is the conserved
\dot{H}^{1}
-energy and
\delta
is the conserved contact angle.

Substituting this back into (
5.10
), we end up with the magnetic two-component Hunter saxton system:

\begin{cases}u_{tx}&=-\frac{1}{2}u_{x}^{2}-u\cdot u_{xx}+\frac{1}{2}\rho^{2}-(s\rho+2(c^{2}-\delta s)),\\
\rho_{t}&=-(u\rho)_{x}+su_{x}.\end{cases}

This concludes the proof of
Theorem

5.1
.

M2HS
) also possesses infinitely many conserved quantities.
∎

6.

M2HS

We begin with an overview of the section. In

, we establish precise blow-up criteria for (

6.3

6.1

In

6.2
, we show how, after relaxing the ambient space, one can use geometric arguments to obtain global existence of a weak flow; see
Definition

6.7

. By a duality argument similar to

5.1

) in the sense of

.

Throughout this section, the arguments are primarily geometric; the more involved analytical details are deferred to
[
32
, §4]
. For the reader’s convenience, the corresponding statements are recalled at the beginning of the relevant discussions.

6.1.

First, we provide the

Proof.

Let
\gamma
be a unit speed magnetic geodesic in

with initial values
\gamma(0)=\varPhi(\mathrm{id},0)
and
\dot{\gamma}(0)=\mathrm{D}\varPhi(u_{0},\rho_{0})
, with

if and only if there exists an

Proof.

Let
\gamma
be as described in

. Then,
\gamma
is also a magnetic geodesic in

. According to

, the geodesic

takes the explicit form:

(6.1)

\gamma(t)=\frac{1}{\theta_{2}-\theta_{1}}\left(\theta_{2}\gamma(0)+{\mathrm{i}}\dot{\gamma}(0)\right)e^{{\mathrm{i}}\theta_{1}t}+\frac{1}{\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}}\left(\theta_{1}\gamma(0)+{\mathrm{i}}\dot{\gamma}(0)\right)e^{{\mathrm{i}}\theta_{2}t},

where the parameters
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
are defined as

(6.2)

\theta_{1/2}=\frac{s}{2}\pm|C_{s}(\psi)|=\frac{s\pm\sqrt{s^{2}-4(1-s\delta)}}{2}.

By explicitly using the initial values
\gamma(0)
and
\dot{\gamma}(0)
as provided in

, (

), and (

), it follows that there exists a time

such that

(6.3)

0=\frac{1}{\theta_{2}-\theta_{1}}\left(\theta_{2}+\frac{{\mathrm{i}}}{2}\left(u_{x}(x_{0})+{\mathrm{i}}\rho(x_{0})\right)\right)e^{{\mathrm{i}}\theta_{1}T}+\frac{1}{\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}}\left(\theta_{1}+\frac{{\mathrm{i}}}{2}\left(u_{x}(x_{0})+{\mathrm{i}}\rho(x_{0})\right)\right)e^{{\mathrm{i}}\theta_{2}T}.

By applying Euler’s formula to the complex exponential functions in (

(6.4)

\displaystyle\theta_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\rho(x_{0})

\displaystyle=\cos((\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})T)(\theta_{2}+\rho(x_{0}))-\sin((\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})T)\frac{1}{2}u_{x}(x_{0}),

(6.5)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}u_{x}(x_{0})

\displaystyle=\sin((\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})T)\left(\theta_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\rho(x_{0})\right)+\cos((\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})T)\frac{1}{2}u_{x}(x_{0}).

Now there are two cases: either

or

, so

. This allows us to rewrite (

\displaystyle\theta_{1}+\rho(x_{0})

\displaystyle=\theta_{2}+\rho(x_{0})\Longrightarrow 0=\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}=\sqrt{s^{2}+4(1-s\delta)},

which implies, following the computations in
[
4
, §3]
, that
\gamma
is a Reeb orbit in

In the second case, we can solve (

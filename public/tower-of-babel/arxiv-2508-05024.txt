An extension of the linearized double shuffle Lie algebra
Annika Burmester

Abstract
. The linearized double shuffle Lie algebra 𝔩 ​ 𝔰 \mathfrak{ls} is a well-studied Lie algebra, which reflects the depth-graded structure of multiple zeta values. We introduce a generalization 𝔩 ​ 𝔮 \mathfrak{lq} , which is motivated from the ℚ \mathbb{Q} -algebraic structure of multiple q-zeta values and multiple Eisenstein series. Precisely, we show that 𝔩 ​ 𝔮 \mathfrak{lq} is a Lie algebra, where the Lie bracket is related to Ecalle’s ari bracket on bimoulds, and give an embedding of 𝔩 ​ 𝔰 \mathfrak{ls} into 𝔩 ​ 𝔮 \mathfrak{lq} .

1.
Introduction

1. Multiple zeta values.
For
k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1},\ k_{1}\geq 2
, a
multiple zeta value
of
weight

k_{1}+\cdots+k_{d}
and depth
d
is

\displaystyle\zeta(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})=\sum_{n_{1}>\cdots>n_{d}>0}\frac{1}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots n_{d}^{k_{d}}}\in\mathbb{R}.

In recent decades, they have attracted much interest due to their appearance in various fields of mathematics and in physics, like number theory, algebraic geometry, knot theory, quantum field theory, as well as for their rich algebraic structure. One key feature of multiple zeta values is their two product expressions, the
stuffle product
coming from the combinatorics of nested sums and the
shuffle product
coming from their iterated integral expression. For example, we have for
k_{1},k_{2}\geq 2

\displaystyle\zeta(k_{1})\zeta(k_{2})

\displaystyle=\zeta(k_{1},k_{2})+\zeta(k_{2},k_{1})+\zeta(k_{1}+k_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=2}^{k_{1}+k_{2}}\left(\binom{j-1}{k_{1}-1}+\binom{j-1}{k_{2}-1}\right)\zeta(j,k_{1}+k_{2}-j).

The comparison of the two products along with some regularization leads to the
extended double shuffle relations
. Ihara, Kaneko and Zagier conjectured in
[
IKZ06
]
that these relations generate all the numerous algebraic relations among multiple zeta values.
One way to study the extended double shuffle relations is to pass to a linearized, depth-graded setting
[
IKZ06
, Section 8]
. Rephrasing this idea in a more algebraic way leads to the
linearized double shuffle Lie algebra

\mathfrak{ls}
studied by Brown
[
Bro21
]
. Roughly,
\mathfrak{ls}
consists of non-commutative polynomials
\psi
in two letters
x_{0},x_{1}
, such that

(i)

\psi
does not have weight
1
terms or depth
1
terms of even weight,

(ii)

\psi
is primitive for the shuffle coproduct,

(iii)

if we replace each
x_{0}^{n-1}x_{1}
by
y_{n}
and words ending in
x_{0}
by
0
in
\psi
, the result is primitive for the depth-graded stuffle coproduct.

The Lie bracket on
\mathfrak{ls}
is given by the
Ihara bracket

\{-,-\}
. By construction and
[
IKZ06
, Conjecture 1]
, we expect for the depth-graded algebra
\operatorname{gr}_{D}\mathcal{Z}
of multiple zeta values that there is an algebra isomorphism

\displaystyle\operatorname{gr}_{D}\mathcal{Z}/(\zeta(2))\overset{?}{\simeq}\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{ls})^{\vee},

(1.1)

where
(-)^{\vee}
denotes the graded dual with respect to the weight.
The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{ls}
is more accessible than the extended double shuffle relations. For instance, computing the dimensions of
\mathfrak{ls}
provides an upper bound for the weight- and depth-homogeneous components of the algebra
\mathcal{Z}
of multiple zeta values. Passing to the associated depth-graded reveals a connection of multiple zeta values and cusp forms for
\operatorname{Sl}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, as studied in
[
BK97
]
,
[
Bro21
]
.

2. Variations of multiple zeta values and Eisenstein series.
An approach to understanding the algebra of multiple zeta values and the extended double shuffle relations is to consider variants of them, where some structures become simpler.

q-analogs
of multiple zeta values are one such approach, those are
q
-series degenerating to multiple zeta values under the limit
q\to 1
. There are lots of different models of them in the literature, one of them are the
Schlesinger-Zudilin multiple
q
-zeta values

\displaystyle\zeta_{q}^{\operatorname{SZ}}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{l})=\sum_{0<n_{1}<\cdots<n_{l}}\frac{q^{n_{1}s_{1}}}{(1-q^{n_{1}})^{s_{1}}}\cdots\frac{q^{n_{l}s_{l}}}{(1-q^{n_{l}})^{s_{l}}}\in\mathbb{Q}[[q]],

where
s_{1},\ldots,s_{l}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},\ s_{l}\geq 1
and
q
is just a formal variable. We have reversed the order of the summation indices compared to multiple zeta values to make the corresponding Lie algebra structure introduced below more naturally compatible with
\mathfrak{ls}
. In
[
BK20
]
it is shown that many models for multiple
q
-zeta values are contained in the space
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
spanned by the Schlesinger-Zudilin multiple
q
-zeta values, so we may study all of them simultaneously. One big advantage of
q
-analogs of multiple zeta values is that we deal with
q
-series instead of real numbers. This allows for addressing questions about linear independence, as for example in
[
BK20
]
, which are currently inaccessible for multiple zeta values. From our point of view the main advantage of multiple
q
-zeta values is that instead of two product expressions together with regularization (i.e., the extended double shuffle relations), it conjecturally suffices to consider the usual stuffle product together with the invariance under some involution
\tau
, which reads explicitly for
m_{i}\geq 0,\ k_{i}\geq 1

\displaystyle\zeta_{q}^{\operatorname{SZ}}(\{0\}^{m_{1}},k_{1},\ldots,\{0\}^{m_{d}},k_{d})=\zeta_{q}^{\operatorname{SZ}}(\{0\}^{k_{d}-1},m_{d}+1,\ldots,\{0\}^{k_{1}-1},m_{1}+1).

Multiple Eisenstein series
, which were introduced in
[
GKZ06
]
, have a deep connection to the space
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
. They are defined for
k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2},k_{d}\geq 3,
by

\mathbb{G}_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}}(\tau)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\prec\lambda_{1}\prec\cdots\prec\lambda_{d}\\
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\tau+\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots\lambda_{d}^{k_{d}}}\qquad(\tau\in\mathbb{H}).

The ordering on
\mathbb{Z}\tau+\mathbb{Z}
is understood as
m_{1}\tau+n_{1}\prec m_{2}\tau+n_{2}
if and only if
m_{1}<m_{2}
or
m_{1}=m_{2}
and
n_{1}<n_{2}
. The Fourier expansion of multiple Eisenstein series was computed in
[
Ba12
]
, the constant term of
\mathbb{G}_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}}(\tau)
is exactly
\zeta(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})
and also the other Fourier coefficients are
\mathbb{Q}
-linear combinations of multiple zeta values.

The
combinatorial bi-multiple Eisenstein series

G\binom{k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}}{m_{1},\ldots,m_{d}}
from
[
BB23
]
span the space
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
and their single indexed versions
G(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})
conjecturally satisfy the same
\mathbb{Q}
-algebraic relations as multiple Eisenstein series. Thus, we view them as a rational
q
-series counterpart of multiple Eisenstein series. Since the construction of the combinatorial bi-multiple Eisenstein series depends on a rational solution to the extended double shuffle equations, they are not unique and cannot be made fully explicit. The extension to bi-indices encounters partial derivatives with respect to
q\frac{d}{dq}
, and hence allows to describe all expected relations among combinatorial bi-multiple Eisenstein series with the usual (bi-)stuffle product and the invariance under some involution swap. Conjecturally (
[
BB23
, Remark 6.11]
), already the combinatorial multiple Eisenstein series
G(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})
suffice to span the space
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
. This point of view gives another motivation for a deeper understanding of the algebraic structure of
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
. In particular,
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
contains all quasi-modular forms and can be seen as an extension of them (see e.g.,
[
BI24
]
).

A bi-graded Lie algebra for
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
.
Inspired by the linearized double shuffle Lie algebra
\mathfrak{ls}
for multiple zeta values, we study the relations in the algebra
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
modulo products, lower depths, and quasi-modular forms. This leads to the notion of a bigraded space

(ii)

\psi
is primitive for the shuffle coproduct,

(iii)

\psi
is invariant under some involution
\tau
.

Balanced multiple
q
-zeta values

In this section we review balanced multiple
q
-zeta values as introduced in
[
Bu24
]
, to give a precise and clear description of the relations in the algebra
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
. We will not give the explicit construction, but instead explain the algebraic structure in detail.

Let
\mathcal{B}=\{b_{0},b_{1},b_{2},\ldots\}
be an alphabet, whose elements we call letters. By
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle
we denote the free non-commutative algebra generated by
\mathcal{B}
. To the monic monomials in
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle
we refer as words, and the empty word we denote by
1
. We write
\mathcal{B}^{*}
fo the set of all words with letters in
\mathcal{B}
(including the empty word
1
). For any
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra
R
, we let
R\langle\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle\rangle
be the algebra of non-commutative power series over
\mathcal{B}
with coefficients in
R
.

Definition 2.1
.

For a word
w=b_{s_{1}}\cdots b_{s_{l}}\in\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle
, we define the
weight
and
depth
as

\displaystyle\operatorname{wt}(w)=s_{1}+\cdots+s_{l}+\#\{i\mid s_{i}=0\},\qquad\operatorname{dep}(w)=l-\#\{i\mid s_{i}=0\}.

(2.1)

We regard the empty word
1
to be of weight and depth
0
.

Both notions define a grading on the vector space
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle
, we denote the homogeneous subspace of weight
k
and depth
d
by
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle_{k,d}
. So,

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle=\bigoplus_{k,d\geq 0}\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle_{k,d}.

In the following, we will use this notation also for subspaces of
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle
.

Definition 2.2
.

The
balanced quasi-shuffle product
on
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle
is recursively defined by
1\ast_{b}w=w\ast_{b}1=w
and

\displaystyle b_{i}v\ast_{b}b_{j}w=b_{i}(v\ast_{b}b_{j}w)+b_{j}(b_{i}v\ast_{b}w)+\delta_{ij>0}b_{i+j}(v\ast_{b}w)

(2.2)

for
v,w\in\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle
. Here,
\delta_{ij>0}
is equal to
1
if
ij>0
and
0
else.

Restricting to the letters
b_{i}
for
i>0
, we obtain the usual stuffle product for multiple zeta values. Just allowing the letters
b_{0},b_{1}
, we recover the usual shuffle product. Therefore, we call this product ’balanced’. Observe that the balanced quasi-shuffle product
\ast_{b}
is graded for the weight and filtered for the depth.

Denote by
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0}
the subspace spanned by words, which do not end with
b_{0}
. This subspace is preserved by the balanced quasi-shuffle product
\ast_{b}
, so
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0}
is a subalgebra of
(\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle,\ast_{b})
.

Definition 2.3
.

We define the involution
\tau:\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0}\to\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0}
by

\displaystyle\tau(b_{0}^{m_{1}}b_{k_{1}}\cdots b_{0}^{m_{d}}b_{k_{d}})=b_{0}^{k_{d}-1}b_{m_{d}+1}\cdots b_{0}^{k_{1}-1}b_{m_{1}+1},

where
k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\geq 1,\ m_{1},\ldots,m_{d}\geq 0
.

Note that the map
\tau
is homogeneous for both the weight and the depth.

.

(

\displaystyle\operatorname{Fil}_{d}\mathcal{Z}_{q}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Q}}\{\zeta_{q}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{l})\mid l-\#\{i\mid s_{i}=0\}\leq d\},\qquad d\geq 0.

\displaystyle\operatorname{gr}_{D}\mathcal{Z}_{q}=\bigoplus_{d\geq 0}\big(\operatorname{Fil}_{d}\mathcal{Z}_{q}/\operatorname{Fil}_{d-1}\mathcal{Z}_{q}\big).

The definition of the space

In this section, we prove the results applied in the proof of Theorem

We will need an inverse of the projection

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\sec:\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0}\to\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle
be the
\mathbb{Q}
-linear map given by

\sec(b_{0}^{m_{1}}b_{k_{1}}\cdots b_{0}^{m_{d}}b_{k_{d}})=\operatorname{ad}(b_{0})^{m_{1}}\Big(b_{k_{1}}\cdot\operatorname{ad}(b_{0})^{m_{2}}\Big(b_{k_{2}}\cdots\operatorname{ad}(b_{0})^{m_{d}}(b_{k_{d}})\cdots\Big)\Big),

where
\operatorname{ad}(b_{0}):\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle\to\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle,w\mapsto[b_{0},w]
is meant with respect to the usual commutator.

Explicitly, we have for
k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\geq 1
,
m_{1},\ldots,m_{d}\geq 0

\displaystyle\sec(b_{0}^{m_{1}}b_{k_{1}}\cdots b_{0}^{m_{d}}b_{k_{d}})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}+\cdots+n_{d}+n=m_{1}+\cdots+m_{d}\\
n_{1},\ldots,n_{d},n\geq 0\end{subarray}}(-1)^{n}\prod_{s=1}^{d}\binom{m_{s}}{n_{s}}b_{0}^{n_{1}}b_{k_{1}}\cdots b_{0}^{n_{d}}b_{k_{d}}b_{0}^{n}.

(5.1)

Let
\partial_{0}:\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle\to\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle
be the derivation with respect to concatenation given by

\displaystyle\partial_{0}(b_{0})=1,\qquad\partial_{0}(b_{i})=0,\ i\geq 1.

(5.2)

We abbreviate

\displaystyle D_{k,m}=\operatorname{ad}(b_{0})^{m}(b_{k}),\quad k\geq 1,\ m\geq 0,

(5.3)

and write
\mathcal{D}_{\operatorname{bi}}=\{D_{k,m}\mid k\geq 1,m\geq 0\}
. By (
3.10
) and condition (i) in Definition
3.4
, we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{lq}\subset\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{D}_{\operatorname{bi}}\rangle.

(5.4)

Proposition 5.2
.

We have

(i)

\sec(w)=\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}\partial_{0}^{n}(w)b_{0}^{n},\quad

w\in\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0}
,

(ii)

\Pi_{0}\circ\sec=\operatorname{id}
,

(iii)

\sec\circ\Pi_{0}=\operatorname{id}
on the subspace
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{D}_{\operatorname{bi}}\rangle\subset\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle

Note that by Proposition
5.2
and (
5.4
), we can uniquely recover
\Phi\in\mathfrak{lq}
from
\Pi_{0}(\Phi)

Proof.

The equality in (i) is obtained by direct calculation. For
w=b_{0}^{m_{1}}b_{k_{1}}\cdots b_{0}^{m_{d}}b_{k_{d}}
,
k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\geq 1,\ m_{1},\ldots,m_{d}\geq 0

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}\partial_{0}^{n}(w)b_{0}^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}+\cdots+n_{d}=n\\
0\leq n_{s}\leq m_{s}\end{subarray}}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n_{1}!\cdots n_{d}!}\prod_{s=1}^{d}\frac{m_{s}!}{(m_{s}-n_{s})!}b_{0}^{m_{1}-n_{1}}b_{k_{1}}\cdots b_{0}^{m_{d}-n_{d}}b_{k_{d}}b_{0}^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{d}+n=m_{1}+\cdots+m_{d}}(-1)^{n}\prod_{s=1}^{d}\binom{m_{s}}{n_{s}}b_{0}^{n_{1}}b_{k_{1}}\cdots b_{0}^{n_{d}}b_{k_{d}}b_{0}^{n}

\displaystyle=\sec(w),

where the last equality follows from (
5.1

For (ii), observe that in (

) the only term in
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0}
is the one for

and hence
n_{s}=m_{s}
for

. Thus, we deduce

\Pi_{0}(\sec(b_{0}^{m_{1}}b_{k_{1}}\cdots b_{0}^{m_{d}}b_{k_{d}}))=b_{0}^{m_{1}}b_{k_{1}}\cdots b_{0}^{m_{d}}b_{k_{d}}.

For (iii), we compute for
k\geq 1,\ m\geq 0

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\big(\operatorname{ad}(b_{0})^{m}(b_{k})\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{m}(-1)^{m+n}\binom{m}{n}\partial_{0}(b_{0}^{n}b_{k}b_{0}^{m-n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{m}(-1)^{m+n}\binom{m}{n}nb_{0}^{n-1}b_{k}b_{0}^{m-n}+\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}(-1)^{m+n}\binom{m}{n}(m-n)b_{0}^{n}b_{k}b_{0}^{m-n-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{m}(-1)^{m+n}\binom{m}{n}nb_{0}^{n-1}b_{k}b_{0}^{m-n}+\sum_{n=1}^{m}(-1)^{m+n-1}\binom{m}{n}nb_{0}^{n-1}b_{k}b_{0}^{m-n}

\displaystyle=0.

This calculation implies
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{D}_{\operatorname{bi}}\rangle\subset\ker(\partial_{0})
. So, assume that

satisfies
\partial_{0}(w)=0
. Write

with

Inductively, we get

\displaystyle w_{n}=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}\partial_{0}^{n}(w_{0}),\qquad n\geq 1.

(5.5)

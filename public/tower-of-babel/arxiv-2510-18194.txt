Introduction

Elliptic curves are main objects in number theory and algebraic geometry. A remarkable structure of elliptic curves is being abelian varieties. More precisely, for an elliptic curve
E/F
over a field
F
, the set
E\left(F\right)
of all
F
-rational points form an abelian group under a certain addition law. The most fundamental theorem for the structure
E\left(F\right)
is the Mordell-Weil theorem (
[
24
, VIII.Theorem 6.7]
), which states that
E\left(F\right)
is finitely generated if
F
is a number field. In other words, the Mordell-Weil theorem implies that

E\left(F\right)\cong\mathscr{T}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}^{r},

for some finite abelian group
\mathscr{T}
and a non-negative integer
r
. The finite abelian group
\mathscr{T}
and the non-negative integer
r
are called the torsion subgroup and the rank of
E
over
F
, respectively. The torsion subgroup
\mathscr{T}
of
E/F
is usually denoted by
{E\left(F\right)}_{\operatorname{tors}}
.

Even over a general field
F
, for a positive integer
N
which is not divisible by the characteristic of
F
, it is known that

{E\left(F\right)}\left[N\right]\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}}

for some positive divisors
m,n
of
N
such that
m\mid n
. Building upon this foundation, the torsion subgroups of elliptic curves have been extensively studied.

Let
E/K
an elliptic curve defined over
K
of characteristic
0
and
L/K
be a field extension.
One direction of this research focuses on the case
L=K
. Writing
d=\left[K:{\mathbb{Q}}\right]
, the possible isomorphic classes of
{E\left(K\right)}_{\operatorname{tors}}
for elliptic curves
E/K
defined over
K
with degree
d\leq 6
have been completely classified: for
d=1
by Mazur
[
18
]
; for
d=2
by Kenku-Momose
[
16
]
and Kamienny
[
15
]
; for
d=3
by Jeon-Kim-Schweizer
[
13
, Theorem 3.4]
; for
d=4
by Jeon-Kim-Park
[
14
, Theorem 3.6]
; and for
d=5,6
by Derickx-Sutherland
[
4
, Theorem 1.1]
.

On the other hand, when
K={\mathbb{Q}}
, the possible isomorphism classes of
{E\left(L\right)}_{\operatorname{tors}}
have been determined for specific values of
\left[L:{\mathbb{Q}}\right]
: when
\left[L:{\mathbb{Q}}\right]=2
by
[
8
]
and
[
20
]
;
\left[L:{\mathbb{Q}}\right]=3
by
[
20
]
;
\left[L:{\mathbb{Q}}\right]=4
by
[
1
]
,
\left[L:{\mathbb{Q}}\right]=5
by
[
6
]
; and for prime degrees
\left[L:{\mathbb{Q}}\right]\geq 7
by
[
7
]
.

If
K
is a general number field, certain conditions under which the minimal prime divisor of
\left[L:K\right]
guarantees that
{E\left(L\right)}_{\operatorname{tors}}={E\left(K\right)}_{\operatorname{tors}}
are studied in
[
9
]
and
[
12
]
.

In the studies mentioned above,
{E\left(L\right)}_{\operatorname{tors}}
is described mainly in terms of the extension degrees
\left[K:{\mathbb{Q}}\right]
and
\left[L:K\right]
. There is another approach which considers the Galois theory of
L/K
: for example,
[
3
]
considers the case when
K={\mathbb{Q}}
and

is the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-extensions of
{\mathbb{Q}}
, and
[
2

studies the case when

and

In this paper, we investigate
{E\left(L\right)}_{\operatorname{tors}}
under more general assumptions on
K
and
L/K
. Let
\widetilde{L}/K
denote the Galois closure of
L/K
. We show that, under specific conditions on
\operatorname{Gal}\left({{\widetilde{L}/K}}\right)
and
\operatorname{Gal}\left({{\widetilde{L}/L}}\right)
, one has

{E\left(L\right)}_{\operatorname{tors}}={E\left(K\right)}_{\operatorname{tors}}.

Unlike prior research which primarily focuses on extensions over
{\mathbb{Q}}
or on low-degree number fields, our results in this paper are applicable to arbitrary extensions
L/K
of characteristic
0
.
Moreover, rather than describing torsion growth solely in terms of extension degrees, our work identifies explicit Galois-theoretic conditions under which the torsion subgroup does not grow, thereby clarifying precisely when torsion remains unchanged and distinguishing our approach from existing degree-based classifications.

Our main result in this paper is as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic
0
and
E/K
an elliptic curve over

(1)

If

E\left(L\right)\left[\ell^{\infty}\right]=E\left(K\right)\left[\ell^{\infty}\right].

(2)

In this section, we establish Galois-theoretic sufficient conditions on

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic
0
and
E/K
be an elliptic curve defined over

(1)

K_{r}/K
is a Galois extension.

(2)

Let

\mathscr{T}_{r}=\left\langle P_{k+r},Q_{m+r}\right\rangle
.

(3)

For an integer
r\geq 1
and

such that
\ell^{r-1}T\notin E\left(K\right)
, if
E\left(K\right)\left[\ell^{\infty}\right]\supseteq\left({\mathbb{Z}}/\ell^{r}{\mathbb{Z}}\right)
, then the field extension

over

is a Galois extension whose Galois group is isomorphic to a subgroup of exponent

in
\left({\mathbb{Z}}/\ell^{r}{\mathbb{Z}}\right)^{2}

(4)

We assume that
\mathscr{T}_{1}\supsetneq E\left(K\right)\left[\ell^{\infty}\right]

Proof.

(a) For
\sigma\in G_{K}

(b) First, by
[
11

\mathscr{T}_{r}=\left\langle P_{k+r},Q_{m+r}\right\rangle

\ell^{r}P_{k+r}=P_{k}

is generated by

and

. If

, then for some

\ell^{r}T=aP_{k}+bQ_{m}=a\ell^{r}P_{k+r}+b\ell^{r}Q_{m+r}

(c) To consider the extension

, we define a function

(1)

\kappa_{T}\left(\sigma\right)=T^{\sigma}-T,\text{ for }\sigma\in G_{K}.

Since

\displaystyle\operatorname{Gal}\left({{K\left(T\right)/K}}\right)\cong G_{K}/\operatorname{Gal}\left({{\overline{K}/K\left(T\right)}}\right)\cong\operatorname{Im}{\kappa_{T}}\subseteq\left({\mathbb{Z}}/\ell^{r}{\mathbb{Z}}\right)^{2}.

(e) The inclusion

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G\subseteq\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
0&d\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}\left({\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}\right)\right\}
be a subgroup such that
\ell\mid|G|
. Then for a subgroup
H
of
G
,
H
is normal in
G
if and only if either
U=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\in H
, or else, for every
\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
0&d\end{pmatrix}\in H
, we have
a=d\in{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}^{\times}
.

Proof.

Suppose
H
is a normal subgroup of
G
. If there exists
A=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
0&d\end{pmatrix}\in H
such that
a\neq d
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}^{\times}
, then a direct computation shows that,
letting
1\leq\left(d-a\right)^{-1}\leq\ell-1
as an element in
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}^{\times}
, we have that
U=U^{d\left(d-a\right)^{-1}}\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
0&d\end{pmatrix}U^{-d\left(d-a\right)^{-1}}\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
0&d\end{pmatrix}^{-1}\in H
.

Conversely, suppose
U\in H
. Then for any
\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
0&d\end{pmatrix}\in H
, letting
1\leq a^{-1}\leq\ell-1
as an element in
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}^{\times}
, we have that

(4)

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
0&d\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\
0&d\end{pmatrix}U^{ba^{-1}},

which implies that
H=\left\langle U,\Delta\right\rangle
, where
\Delta:=\left\{\operatorname{diag}\left({a,d}\right)\in H\right\}
.
If
B\in G
and
\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
0&d\end{pmatrix}\in H
, a direct computation implies that

B\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
0&d\end{pmatrix}B^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}a&b^{\prime}\\
0&d\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
0&d\end{pmatrix}U^{\left(b^{\prime}-b\right)a^{-1}}\in H.

Therefore,
H
is normal in
G
.

If every element of
H
has identical diagonal entries, then
H
is a subgroup of the kernel of
f
, where
f:G\to{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}^{\times}
is the group homomorphism defined by
\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
0&d\end{pmatrix}\mapsto ad^{-1}
. Thus, it suffices to show that
H
is a characteristic subgroup of
\ker\left(f\right)
. By (
4
), the group
\left\langle U,\operatorname{diag}\left({a,a}\right):a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}^{\times}\right\rangle
contains
\ker\left(f\right)
. This group is cyclic, since
U
and
\operatorname{diag}\left({a,a}\right)
commute, and it is generated by two cyclic groups
\left\langle U\right\rangle
and
\left\{\operatorname{diag}\left({a,a}\right):a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}^{\times}\right\}
, whose orders are relatively prime. Therefore, its subgroup
\ker\left(f\right)
is also cyclic, and hence,
H
is a characteristic subgroup of
\ker\left(f\right)
.
∎

Proposition 2.3

(a).

\displaystyle\left[K\left(Q_{n+1}\right):K\right]=\left[K\left(\zeta_{\ell},Q_{n+1}\right):K\left(\zeta_{\ell}\right)\right].

\displaystyle f\left(\sigma\tau\right)P_{1}+g\left(\sigma\tau\right)Q_{1}

\displaystyle=Q_{n+1}^{\sigma\tau}-Q_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\left(Q_{n+1}^{\sigma}-Q_{n+1}\right)^{\tau}+\left(Q_{n+1}^{\tau}-Q_{n+1}\right)

\displaystyle\text{ and }\;h\left(\sigma\tau\right)Q_{1}

be a field of characteristic

and

. Suppose that

(2)

K^{\prime}:=K_{\left(2^{i}-1\right)r}

K_{\left(2^{i+1}-1\right)r}=K_{\left(2^{i}-1\right)r}\left(\mathscr{T}_{\left(2^{i+1}-1\right)r}\right)=K^{\prime}(\mathscr{T}_{2^{i}r}^{\prime}).

Therefore, since

or

, applying Lemma

(e) over

together with considering (

E\left(K_{\left(2^{i+1}-1\right)r}\right)\left[\ell^{\infty}\right]=E\left(K^{\prime}(\mathscr{T}_{2^{i}r}^{\prime})\right)\left[\ell^{\infty}\right]=\mathscr{T}_{2^{i}r}^{\prime}=\mathscr{T}_{\left(2^{i+1}-1\right)r},

which completes the induction.

Now for a given

, choose an integer

such that
\left(2^{i_{0}}-1\right)r\leq k<\left(2^{i_{0}+1}-1\right)r

E\left(K_{\left(2^{i_{0}}-1\right)r}\right)\left[\ell^{\infty}\right]=\mathscr{T}_{\left(2^{i_{0}}-1\right)r}\supseteq E\left[\ell^{2^{i_{0}}r}\right]\supseteq E\left[\ell^{k-\left(2^{i_{0}}-1\right)r+1}\right].

Since
\mathscr{T}_{k}=\left\{P\in E\left[\ell^{\infty}\right]:\ell^{k-\left(2^{i_{0}}-1\right)r}P\in\mathscr{T}_{\left(2^{i_{0}}-1\right)r}\right\}
and

, another application of Lemma

(e) gives
E\left(K_{k}\right)\left[\ell^{\infty}\right]=\mathscr{T}_{k}
.
∎

To prove Theorem
1.4
, we need Lemma

and the classification of the subgroups of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}\left({\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}\right)

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic
0
,
L/K
an extension, and
\widetilde{L}/K
a Galois extension over
K
containing
L
with Galois group
G
. Set
H:=\operatorname{Gal}\left({{\widetilde{L}/L}}\right)
. Then,
L/K
has no non-trivial abelian sub-extension over
K
if and only if
G^{\prime}H=G
, where
G^{\prime}
is the commutator subgroup of
G
.

Proof.

For a sub-extension
F/K
of
\widetilde{L}/K
,
F/K
is Galois and
\operatorname{Gal}\left({{F/K}}\right)
is abelian if and only if
\operatorname{Gal}\left({{\widetilde{L}/F}}\right)\supseteq G^{\prime}
, and
F\subseteq L
if and only if
\operatorname{Gal}\left({{\widetilde{L}/F}}\right)\supseteq H
. Hence,
K
is the only one abelian sub-extension of
L/K
if and only if the only subgroup of
G
containing
G^{\prime}H
is
G
, i.e.,
G=G^{\prime}H
.
∎

Lemma 3.2

(
[
21
, Section 2]
)
.

Let
\ell
be a prime. For a subgroup
H
of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}\left({\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}\right)
, let

\overline{H}:=H/\left(H\cap\left\{aI_{2}:a\in\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{\times}\right\}\right)

be the image of

under the natural projection
\operatorname{GL}_{2}\left({\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}\right)\to\operatorname{PGL}_{2}\left({\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}\right)
. Then one of the following holds:

(1)

H
equals a group
\left\{A\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}\left({\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}\right):\det A\in R\right\}
for some subgroup

, and in particular,

contains
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
;

(2)

is a subgroup of a Borel subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}\left({\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}\right)
and
\ell\mid\left|H\right|
, i.e., there exists

such that

is a subgroup of
\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\

and

;

(3)

is a subgroup of the normalizer
\mathcal{N}
of a Cartan subgroup
\mathcal{C}
of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}\left({\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}\right)
;

(4)

\overline{H}
is isomorphic to either
\mathfrak{A}_{4}
,
\mathfrak{S}_{4}
, or
\mathfrak{A}_{5}
.

Now we prove Theorem
1.4
.

.

H=\left\langle\left(1\;2\right)\right\rangle
. Since the commutator subgroup

is equal to
\mathfrak{A}_{n}

3.1
implies:

(7)

L/K\text{ has no non-trivial abelian sub-extension over }K.

Let
\ell
be a prime. First, suppose

If

Next, suppose

contains a point

of order

, contradicting (

Since

,

,

(2)

, while the order of a normal subgroup of

4.

In this section, we consider a non-Galois extension

of prime degree

almost immediately. Therefore, the main task of this section is to deal with the case

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic
0
and
E/K
an elliptic curve over
K
whose affine model is defined by

, where

be a non-Galois cubic extension and

a prime such that
E\left(K\right)\left[\ell\right]=\left\{O\right\}
. Then:

(1)

If
\ell=2
and
L
has no zero of

, then
E\left(L\right)\left[2\right]=\left\{O\right\}=E\left(K\right)\left[2\right]
.

(2)

If

and
\operatorname{Im}\operatorname{cyc}_{\ell}\neq\left\{\pm 1\right\}

First, note that since

is a non-Galois cubic extension, for any element

, the minimal polynomial

of

over

is cubic, and we have
\operatorname{Gal}\left({{g_{\alpha}/K}}\right)\cong\mathfrak{S}_{3}
.

Part (a) is immediate, since if
E\left(L\right)\left[2\right]\supsetneq E\left(K\right)\left[2\right]
then
L
contains a zero of
f
.

To prove (b), for a prime
\ell\geq 3
such that
E\left(K\right)\left[\ell\right]=\left\{O\right\}
, suppose
E\left(L\right)
has a point
Q_{1}
of order
\ell
and show that
\operatorname{Im}\operatorname{cyc}_{\ell}=\left\{\pm 1\right\}
. Because

. Following an analogous argument as in the proof of Theorem
1.4
, the Galois closure of

is

. Since
L/K
is a non-Galois cubic extension, we have that
\operatorname{Gal}\left({{K\left(E\left[\ell\right]\right)/K}}\right)\cong\mathfrak{S}_{3}
and, by Lemma
3.2
, there are four possible cases for how

can appear in

as follows:

(1)

\mathfrak{S}_{3}
contains a normal subgroup which is isomorphic to
\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left({\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}\right)
;

(2)

\mathfrak{S}_{3}
is isomorphic to a subgroup of a Borel subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}\left({\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}\right)
with

\left|\mathfrak{S}_{3}\right|=6
;

(3)

For a basis
\mathcal{B}
of
E\left[\ell\right]
,

\mathfrak{S}_{3}
as a subgroup of the normalizer
\mathcal{N}
of a Cartan subgroup
\mathcal{C}
of

(4)

\mathfrak{S}_{3}
contains a cyclic normal subgroup

with the quotient

isomorphic to either
\mathfrak{A}_{4}
,
\mathfrak{S}_{4}
, or

and

. Similarly, Case (D) is impossible by comparing the order of

, or

, respectively.

Suppose (B) happens. Then

, since

3.2
, there exists a basis

Suppose (C) happens. As in (

, we have that

(split Cartan) or

.
∎

.

and

an elliptic curve over

. Let

. For a prime

of a Cartan subgroup

of

(4)

contains a cyclic normal subgroup

with quotient
G/N
isomorphic to either
\mathfrak{A}_{4}
,
\mathfrak{S}_{4}
, or
\mathfrak{A}_{5}
.

First, we rule out (A), (B), and (D):

Suppose (A) happens. Then by the definition of
r_{\mathcal{B}}
and Remark
1.6
(a), we have
H=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}\left({\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}\right):a\in R\right\}
and
p=\left[G:H\right]=\frac{\left|R\right|\ell\left(\ell^{2}-1\right)}{\left|R\right|\ell}=\ell^{2}-1
, which is impossible for prime
p\geq 5
.

Suppose (B) happens. Then,
\left|G\right|
divides
\left(\ell-1\right)^{2}\ell
, and Lemma
2.2
shows that every subgroup of
G
of order divisible by
\ell
is Galois in
G
. Since
\left[G:H\right]=p
with
H
non-normal, this forces
p=\ell
, contradicting the assumption
\ell\neq p
.

Suppose (D) holds. Assume first that
p\geq 7
or
G/N\not\cong\mathfrak{A}_{5}
. Then,
p
divides
\left|G\right|
but not
\left[G:N\right]
, so
p\mid\left|N\right|
. Since
N
is cyclic, let
M\subseteq N
be the unique subgroup of order
p
. For any
g\in G
,
gMg^{-1}
is also a cyclic subgroup of order
p
in
N=gNg^{-1}
, so
gMg^{-1}=M
. Thus,
M
is normal in
G
. Since the number of Sylow
p
-subgroups in
\mathfrak{S}_{p}
is
\left(p-1\right)!
and the normalizer of
\left\langle\left(1\,2\,\cdots\,p\right)\right\rangle
in
\mathfrak{S}_{p}
is
\left\langle\left(1\,2\,\cdots\,p\right)\right\rangle
itself, via the embedding
\phi
of
G
into
\mathfrak{S}_{p}
, we conclude that
G=M
is cyclic, contradicting the non-normality of
H
in
G
.

Now suppose that
p=5
and
G/N\cong\mathfrak{A}_{5}
. As noted above,
G
embeds into
\mathfrak{S}_{5}
. Hence, the order of
G
is either
5!
or
\frac{5!}{2}
and
G
is isomorphic to either
\mathfrak{S}_{5}
or
\mathfrak{A}_{5}
. In either case, we reach a contradiction by showing that
G
contains a normal subgroup of order
2
. Indeed,
G
contains a subgroup
H
which is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
. Since
H
is abelian, there exists
T\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}\left({\mathbb{F}}_{\ell^{2}}\right)
such that
THT^{-1}
consists of diagonal matrices. Since
H\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
, we have

THT^{-1}=\left\{\operatorname{diag}\left({\pm 1,\pm 1}\right)\right\},

and
-I_{2}\in H
. Therefore,
G
contains a normal subgroup of order
2
.

Thus, case (C) remains.
Suppose (C) holds. Since
H
is not normal in
G
,
G
is not abelian. Following the same argument as in (
8
) of the proof of Theorem
1.4
, we have
\left[G:G\cap\mathcal{C}\right]=2
. Since
p=\left[G:H\right]
is relatively prime to
2
,
G
is generated by
H
and
M:=G\cap\mathcal{C}
. Since
\ell\nmid\left|\mathcal{N}\right|
and
H\subseteq G\subseteq\mathcal{N}
, it follows that
\ell\nmid\left|H\right|
, and moreover, since
L=K\left(Q_{1}\right)
, the group homomorphism
H\to{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}^{\times}
defined by
\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\mapsto a
is injective, and thus,
H
is a cyclic group. Let
\begin{pmatrix}a_{0}&b_{0}\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}
be a generator of
H
. Replacing the basis
\mathcal{B}=\left\{P_{1},Q_{1}\right\}
by
\left\{P_{1}+\frac{b_{0}}{a_{0}-1}Q_{1},Q_{1}\right\}
(here,
a_{0}\neq 1
since
\ell\nmid\left|H\right|
and
H
is non-trivial), Remark
1.6
(c) implies that
H
is generated by a single diagonal matrix
\operatorname{diag}\left({a_{0},1}\right)
. Since

2=\left[G:M\right]=\left[MH:M\right]=\left[H:H\cap M\right]\mid\left|H\right|,

the order of
a_{0}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}^{\times}
is even and
H\cap M
is generated by
\operatorname{diag}\left({a_{0}^{2},1}\right)
. If
a_{0}^{2}\neq 1
, then
M
consists of diagonal matrices since
M\subseteq\mathcal{C}
is abelian, which forces
G=MH
to be abelian, contradicting the existence of
H
not a normal subgroup of
G
. Therefore,
a_{0}^{2}=1
. Again, since
H
is not a normal subgroup of
G
, it follows that
a_{0}=-1
and
H\cap M=\left\{I_{2}\right\}
, thus,
|H|=2
. In other words,
G
is a semi-direct product of
H
acting on
M
. A matrix
A\in M
of order
p
generates
M
because
\left|M\right|=\left[G:H\right]=p
. Because
H
is not a normal subgroup of
G
, the action of
H
on
M
which defines the semi-direct product
G
is not trivial. Since
H\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
, the unique non-trivial action satisfies
\operatorname{diag}\left({-1,1}\right)\;A\;\operatorname{diag}\left({-1,1}\right)^{-1}=A^{-1}
. Hence,
\left(\det A\right)^{2}=\left(\det\left(\operatorname{diag}\left({-1,1}\right)A\operatorname{diag}\left({-1,1}\right)^{-1}\right)\right)\left(\det A\right)=\left(\det A^{-1}\right)\left(\det A\right)=1
, and so
\det A\in\left\{\pm 1\right\}
. Then, since
A^{p}=I_{2}
, we have
\det A=1
and
\operatorname{Im}\operatorname{cyc}_{\ell}=\det G=\left\langle\det A,\operatorname{diag}\left({-1,1}\right)\right\rangle=\left\{\pm 1\right\}
by Remark
1.6
(b).
∎

Now we obtain Theorem
1.1
as follows:

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Part (a) follows from Corollary
2.4
for
p\neq\ell
, and from the argument analogous to one in the proof of Theorem
1.4
for
p=3
.

Part (b) follows from Proposition
4.1
(b) if
p=3
and from Proposition
4.2
for
\ell\neq p
if
p\neq 3
.

For Part (c), suppose that
E\left(L\right)\left[2\right]\supsetneq\left\{O\right\}=E\left(K\right)\left[2\right]
. We aim to show
p=3
.
L
contains a zero
\alpha
of
f
which does not lie in
K
. Since
f
has a cubic polynomial with no zeros in
K
, it is irreducible over
K
, and thus
\left[K\left(\alpha\right):K\right]=3
. As
p=\left[L:K\right]
is prime and
L\supseteq K\left(\alpha\right)
, it follows that
3=\left[K\left(\alpha\right):K\right]=\left[L:K\right]=p
.
∎

5.
Applications of Theorem
1.1

As an application of Theorem
1.1
, we obtain Corollary
1.2
, which refines
[
20
, Theorem 1]
as follows:

Proof of Corollary
1.2
.

[
20
, Theorem 1]
classifies all torsion structures that can occur over cubic extensions of
{\mathbb{Q}}
. To specialize to the non-Galois cubic case, it remains to rule out the groups
{\mathbb{Z}}/13{\mathbb{Z}}
and
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}/14{\mathbb{Z}}
from that list when
L/{\mathbb{Q}}
is non-Galois.

If
E\left(L\right)_{\operatorname{tors}}\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/13{\mathbb{Z}}
, then by Mazur’s theorem
[
18
]
,
{E\left({\mathbb{Q}}\right)}_{\operatorname{tors}}=\left\{O\right\}
. Theorem
1.1
(b) shows that this is impossible in a non-Galois cubic extension, since
\operatorname{Im}\operatorname{cyc}_{13}=\left({\mathbb{Z}}/13{\mathbb{Z}}\right)^{\times}
.

Suppose
E\left(L\right)_{\operatorname{tors}}\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}/14{\mathbb{Z}}
. If
E\left({\mathbb{Q}}\right)_{\operatorname{tors}}
has order not divisible by
7
, then since
\operatorname{Im}\operatorname{cyc}_{7}=\left({\mathbb{Z}}/7{\mathbb{Z}}\right)^{\times}
, Theorem
1.1
(b) implies that
E\left(L\right)_{\operatorname{tors}}
also cannot have order divisible by
7
, which is a contradiction. Therefore, we must have
E\left({\mathbb{Q}}\right)_{\operatorname{tors}}\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/7{\mathbb{Z}}
by Mazur’s theorem
[
18
]
. Writing
E/{\mathbb{Q}}
by
y^{2}=f\left(x\right)
, where
f\in{\mathbb{Q}}\left[x\right]
is a cubic polynomial,
f
has no
{\mathbb{Q}}
-rational zero but splits completely over
L
. Since
\left[L:{\mathbb{Q}}\right]=3
,
L
is necessarily the splitting field of
f
, hence Galois, leading a contradiction.
∎

In Theorem
1.1
, for a non-Galois extension
L/K
of prime degree
p
and a prime
\ell\neq p
, if
E\left(K\right)\left[\ell\right]=\left\{O\right\}
, then a sufficient condition for
E\left(L\right)\left[\ell^{\infty}\right]=E\left(K\right)\left[\ell^{\infty}\right]
is expressed in terms of
\operatorname{Im}\operatorname{cyc}_{\ell}
. By restricting

5.1

as further applications of Theorem

, as follows:

Corollary 5.1
.

Let
E/{\mathbb{Q}}
be an elliptic curve defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
and
L/{\mathbb{Q}}
a non-Galois extension of prime degree
p\geq 3
. If
\ell\geq 5
is a prime such that
\ell\neq p
or
E\left({\mathbb{Q}}\right)\left[\ell\right]\neq\left\{O\right\}
, then

E\left(L\right)\left[\ell^{\infty}\right]=E\left({\mathbb{Q}}\right)\left[\ell^{\infty}\right].

Proof.

If
E\left({\mathbb{Q}}\right)\left[\ell^{\infty}\right]\neq\left\{O\right\}
, then
E\left(L\right)\left[\ell^{\infty}\right]=E\left({\mathbb{Q}}\right)\left[\ell^{\infty}\right]
, so Theorem
1.1
(a) complete the proof. On the other hand, if
E\left({\mathbb{Q}}\right)\left[\ell^{\infty}\right]=\left\{O\right\}
, then since
\operatorname{Im}\operatorname{cyc}_{\ell}=\left({\mathbb{Z}}/\ell{\mathbb{Z}}\right)^{\times}
, Theorem
1.1
(c) completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
E/{\mathbb{Q}}\left(\mu_{\infty}\right)
be an elliptic curve defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}\left(\mu_{\infty}\right)
, where
\mu_{\infty}
denotes the group of all roots of unity, and let
L/{\mathbb{Q}}\left(\mu_{\infty}\right)
be a non-Galois extension of prime degree
p\geq 3
. If
\ell\geq 5
is a prime such that
\ell\neq p
, or
E\left({\mathbb{Q}}\left(\mu_{\infty}\right)\right)\left[\ell\right]\neq\left\{O\right\}
, then

E\left(L\right)\left[\ell^{\infty}\right]=E\left({\mathbb{Q}}\left(\mu_{\infty}\right)\right)\left[\ell^{\infty}\right].

Proof.

The reasoning is the same as in Corollary
5.1
, noting that
\operatorname{Im}\operatorname{cyc}_{\ell}=\left\{1\right\}
.
∎

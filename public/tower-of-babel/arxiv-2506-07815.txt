Nonvanishing of L L –functions associated to fixed order characters over function fields
Chantal David, Alexandra Florea, Matilde Lalin

Abstract
. We show that a positive proportion of the values L ⁡ ( 1 / 2 , χ c ) L(1/2,\chi_{c}) are non-zero, where χ c \chi_{c} is the ℓ th \ell^{\text{th}} residue symbol for ℓ ≥ 3 \ell\geq 3 over 𝔽 q ​ [ t ] \mathbb{F}_{q}[t] , when averaging over square-free polynomials c c in 𝔽 q ​ [ t ] \mathbb{F}_{q}[t] , as q ≡ 1 ​ ( mod ​ 2 ​ ℓ ) q\equiv 1\,(\mathrm{mod}\,2\ell) is fixed and the degree of c c goes to infinity. In the case of ℓ = 3 \ell=3 , we show that at least 1 / 6 1/6 of L ⁡ ( 1 / 2 , χ c ) ≠ 0 L(1/2,\chi_{c})\neq 0 , while for ℓ > 3 \ell>3 , the proportion depends on the order of the character. This improves a previous result of Ellenberg, Li, and Shusterman showing that there are infinitely many χ \chi of (prime) order ℓ \ell such that L ⁡ ( 1 / 2 , χ ) ≠ 0 L(1/2,\chi)\neq 0 (with completely different techniques). Our result is achieved by computing the one-level density of zeros in the family of L L –functions and surpassing the ( − 1 , 1 ) (-1,1) barrier for the support of the Fourier transform of the test function, necessary to obtain a positive proportion of non-vanishing result. Using similar techniques, we also prove a result towards the equidistribution of the angles of the order ℓ \ell shifted Gauss sums when summing over prime arguments, a result which may be of independent interest.

1.
Introduction

Central values of
L
–functions are of fundamental importance in number theory. Many results and conjectures concern the values of
L
–functions at special points, and the problem of proving non-vanishing results at the central point
s=1/2
is major. A famous conjecture of Chowla predicts that
L(1/2,\chi)\neq 0
for the
L
–function attached to any Dirichlet character
\chi
.
Focusing on the family of Dirichlet
L
–functions associated to primitive Dirichlet characters modulo
q
, Iwaniec and Sarnak
[
IS99
]
showed that more than
(1/3-\varepsilon)
of
L(1/2,\chi)\neq 0
, for
q
sufficiently large. This proportion was subsequently improved in several works, the best current proportion of nonvanishing being
34.11\%
, due to Bui
[
Bui12
]
. In the case of prime moduli, Khan, Milićević and Ngo
[
KMN22
]
showed that more than
5/13
of the central values of
L
–functions do not vanish.

Restricting to quadratic characters, Soundararajan
[
Sou00
]
showed that more than
7/8
of
L(1/2,\chi)\neq 0
, as
\chi
ranges over real primitive characters, by computing mollified moments in the family. Under the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH), Özlük and Snyder
[
OS99
]
showed that at least
15/16
of the
L
–functions attached to quadratic characters
\chi
do not vanish, by computing the one-level density for the low-lying zeroes in the family. The density conjectures of Katz and Sarnak
[
KS99
]
on the zeroes of
L
–functions imply
that
L(1/2,\chi)\neq 0
for almost all quadratic Dirichlet
L
–functions. The results become sparser and sparser as one considers
L
–functions associated to higher-order characters. Focusing on the family of
L
–functions associated to cubic characters over
\mathbb{Q}
, Baier and Young
[
BY10
]
showed that the number of primitive Dirichlet characters
\chi
of order
3
with conductor less than
Q
for which
L(1/2,\chi)\neq 0
is bounded below by
Q^{6/7-\varepsilon}
. Note that while this result provides an infinite family of
L
–functions that are non-vanishing at the central point, it does not obtain a positive proportion of non-vanishing, since the family has size
Q
. More recently, David and Güloğlu
[
DG22
]
showed that under GRH, more than
2/13
of the
L
–functions of the cubic Dirichlet characters
\chi_{c}
, for square-free
c
in the Eisenstein field, do not vanish at the central point. This was achieved by computing the one-level density in the family, as in the present paper. While the approach of computing the one-level density of zeros usually yields non-vanishing results conditional on the GRH, this assumption was removed in recent breakthrough work of David, de Faveri, Dunn, and Stucky
[
DdFDS
]
, who showed that more than
14\%
of the central values in the same family of cubic
L
–functions are non-zero, by computing mollified moments.

In the function field setting, Bui and Florea
[
BF18
]
obtained that at least
94\%
of the quadratic Dirichlet
L
–functions do not vanish at the central point, by computing the one-level density, and the authors of the present article
[
DFL21
]
showed that a positive proportion of the central values of
L
–functions associated to cubic characters over
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
do not vanish when
q\equiv 2\,(\mathrm{mod}\,3)
. We note that this is the non-Kummer setting, when the base field does not contain the cubic roots of unity, and it is a regime more similar to that considered by Baier and Young
[
BY10
]
in the integer setting. Contrastingly,
[
DFL21
]
provides a positive proportion result for the
full
family of cubic
L
–functions, as opposed to the thin families in
[
DG22
]
,
[
DdFDS
]
or the present article.

In the case of
L
–functions associated to quartic characters, Gao and Zhao
[
GZ20
]
showed that more than
5\%
of quartic
L
–functions in a thin family over the Gaussian field are non-zero at the central point, by computing the one-level density of zeros, under GRH. They also showed
[
GZ22
]
that a positive proportion of the cubic and quartic
L
–functions over
\mathbb{Q}
are non-zero at the central point, under GRH, by following the method in
[
DFL21
]
. This was also achieved for the full family of cubic
L
–functions over the Eisenstein field by Güloğlu and Yesilyurt
[
GY24
]
, generalizing the technique of
[
DFL21
]
to this case, again under GRH.

We emphasize that all the non-vanishing results mentioned above hold for small order characters (
\ell=2,3,4
). For general
\ell
, in the number field setting, Blomer, Goldmakher, and Louvel
[
BGL14
]
showed that there are infinitely many Hecke
L
–functions associated to order
\ell
characters over number fields containing the
\ell^{\text{th}}
roots of unity, for which the
L
–functions do not vanish at the central point; however, they did not obtain a positive proportion. In the function field setting, Ellenberg, Li, and Shusterman
[
ELS20
]
obtained a similar result, using algebraic geometry tools.

One of the reasons for the scarcity of positive proportion non-vanishing results for higher-order characters is our limited understanding of the Gauss sums associated to fixed order characters.
When the character is quadratic, the Gauss sums have a multiplicative structure, which makes them amenable to study. They are much more complicated as the order of the character increases, as the Gauss sums are no longer multiplicative for
\ell>2
.
While quadratic Gauss sums are basically constant, cubic and higher-order Gauss sums are chaotic, and their generating series are related to theta functions associated to metaplectic forms, which are not well-understood.

In this article, we consider the thin family of order
\ell
Dirichlet characters over
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
with square-free discriminants and their associated
L
–functions, for general
\ell
. We will show that for fixed
\ell
, a positive proportion of such characters has the property that the associated
L
–functions do not vanish at the central point
1/2
, when the size
q
of the base field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is fixed. In our work,
q\equiv 1\,(\mathrm{mod}\,2\ell)
, so we are in the Kummer setting, when the base field contains the order
\ell
roots of unity in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. This setting is the analogue of the regimes considered in
[
DG22
,
DdFDS
,
GZ20
]
. To obtain the non-vanishing results, we compute the one-level density of zeros in the family of
L
–functions.

More specifically, for any positive integer
d
such that
\ell\nmid d
, we consider the family

(1.1)

\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(d):=\left\{\chi_{c}=\left(\frac{\cdot}{c}\right)_{\ell}:\,c\,\mbox{ square-free},\deg(c)=d\right\},

where
(\frac{\cdot}{c})_{\ell}
denotes the
\ell^{\text{th}}
residue symbol modulo
c
defined in (
2.1
). Since
\ell\nmid d
, the character
\chi_{c}
is odd, and
the
L
–function associated to
\chi_{c}
can be written as

\mathcal{L}(u,\chi_{c})=\prod_{j=1}^{d-1}\left(1-q^{1/2}e^{-2\pi i\theta_{c,j}}u\right).

Let
\phi
be an even test function in the Schwartz space. We consider

\Phi(\theta):=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\phi((d-1)(\theta-k)).

Note that this function is periodic with period 1, and localized in an interval of size
\sim 1/d
in
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
. We define

(1.2)

\Sigma(\phi,\chi_{c}):=\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\Phi\left(\theta_{c,j}\right).

The one-level density of zeros is defined to be the limit as
d\to\infty
of

(1.3)

\left\langle\Sigma(\phi,\chi_{c})\right\rangle_{\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(d)}:=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(d)|}\sum_{\chi_{c}\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(d)}\Sigma(\phi,\chi_{c}).

We will prove the following results.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\ell\geq 3
be an integer, and assume that
q\equiv 1\,(\mathrm{mod}\,2\ell)
. Let
d\not\equiv 0\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\ell)
, and
\varepsilon>0
. Let
\phi
be an even test function in the Schwartz space whose Fourier transform is supported in
(-v,v)
, where

v=\begin{cases}\frac{6}{5}&\ell=3,\\
\frac{26}{23}&\ell=4,\\
1+\frac{2(\ell-2)}{2\ell^{2}-\ell+2}&5\leq\ell\leq 8,\\
1+\frac{2(\ell-2)}{3\ell^{2}-9\ell+2}&\ell=9,10,\\
1+\frac{6(\ell-2)}{9\ell^{2}-31\ell+6}&11\leq\ell.\end{cases}

Then

\left\langle\Sigma(\phi,\chi_{c})\right\rangle_{\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(d)}=\widehat{\phi}(0)+O\left(\frac{1}{d}\right).

From Theorem
1.1
, we also obtain the following non-vanishing result. We remark that since characters of order
\ell\geq 3
have unitary symmetry, we need
v>1
for all
\ell
in Theorem
1.1
to obtain a positive proportion of non-vanishing result.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(d)
be the family defined in (
1.1
).

If
\ell=3
, then
L(1/2,\chi)\neq 0
for at least
1/6
of the characters in
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(d)
.

If
\ell=4
, then
L(1/2,\chi)\neq 0
for at least
3/26
of the characters in
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(d)
.

If
5\leq\ell\leq 8
, then
L(1/2,\chi)\neq 0
for at least
\frac{2(\ell-2)}{2\ell^{2}+\ell-2}
of the characters in
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(d)
.

If
\ell=9,10
, then
L(1/2,\chi)\neq 0
for at least
\frac{2(\ell-2)}{3\ell^{2}-7\ell-2}
of the characters in
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(d)
.

If
\ell\geq 11
, then
L(1/2,\chi)\neq 0
for at least
\frac{6(\ell-2)}{9\ell^{2}-25\ell-6}
of the characters in
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(d)
.

Remark 1.3
.

We note that when
\ell\to\infty
, the proportion of non-vanishing provided by Corollary
1.2
approaches
0
.

The following are crucial ingredients in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.4
.

(Large Sieve for characters of order
\ell
)

Let
\lambda(N)
be a sequence of complex numbers defined on monic polynomials in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
. Then we have

\sum_{M\in\mathcal{H}_{m}}\left|\sum_{N\in\mathcal{H}_{n}}\lambda(N)\left(\frac{M}{N}\right)_{\ell}\right|^{2}\ll q^{\varepsilon(m+n)}\left(q^{m}+q^{n}+q^{2(m+n)/3}\right)\sum_{N\in\mathcal{H}_{n}}|\lambda(N)|^{2},

where
\mathcal{H}_{d}
denotes the set of monic, square-free polynomials of degree
d
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
.

We also need to prove the following variant.

Theorem 1.5
.

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{M\in\mathcal{M}_{m}}\left|\sum_{N\in\mathcal{H}_{n}}\lambda(N)\left(\frac{M}{N}\right)_{\ell}\right|^{2}\ll q^{\varepsilon(m+n)}\left(q^{m}+q^{n+m/3}+q^{2(m+n)/3}\right)\sum_{N\in\mathcal{H}_{n}}|\lambda(N)|^{2},

where
\mathcal{M}_{m}
denotes the set of monic polynomials of degree
m
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
.

In the course of proving Theorem
1.1
, we will also prove the following result about averages of Gauss sums at prime arguments, which may be of independent interest. Let
G_{\ell}(V,c)
denote the
\ell^{\text{th}}
order Gauss sum over function fields, defined in (
3.1
).

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{n}
denotes the set of monic, irreducible polynomials of degree
n
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
.
For
\ell>8
and any
\varepsilon>0
, we have that

\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}G_{\ell}(R,\pi)\ll q^{n(\frac{3}{2}-\frac{\ell-2}{\ell^{2}})+\varepsilon n}|R|^{\frac{\ell^{2}-7\ell-2}{2\ell^{2}}+\varepsilon}.

For
3<\ell\leq 8
, then

\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}G_{\ell}(R,\pi)\ll q^{n(\frac{3}{2}-\frac{\ell-2}{\ell^{2}})+\varepsilon n}|R|^{\frac{\ell-2}{2\ell^{2}}+\varepsilon}.

When
\ell=3
, then

\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}G_{\ell}(R,\pi)\ll q^{\frac{4n}{3}+\varepsilon n}|R|^{\varepsilon}.

By Corollary
3.2
,
|G_{\ell}(R,\pi)|=|\pi|^{1/2}
for
(\pi,R)=1
. Therefore, Theorem
1.6
provides cancellation in averages of the shifted Gauss sums
G_{\ell}(R,\pi)
at prime arguments when
\deg(R)<\frac{2(\ell-2)}{\ell^{2}-7\ell-2}n
for
\ell>8
, when

\deg(R)<2n
for
3<\ell\leq 8
, and for all shifts
R
for
\ell=3
. By Weyl’s criterion, this could potentially lead to the equidistribution of the angles
\theta_{\ell,R}
given by

\frac{G_{\ell}(R,\pi)}{|\pi|^{1/2}}=e^{i\theta_{\ell,R}}
in those cases, by considering more general generating functions than the functions
\psi^{(i)}_{v}(r,a,u)
from Section
5
to deal with the powers of
G_{\ell}(R,\pi)
.

Over number fields, the equidistribution of the cubic Gauss sums
G_{3}(\pi):=G_{3}(1,\pi)
at prime arguments over the Eisenstein field was proven by Heath-Brown and Patterson
[
HBP79
]
and refined in
[
HB00
]
, falling just short of determining an asymptotic formula conjectured by Patterson for averages of cubic Gauss sums at prime arguments. This was subsequently proved by Dunn and Radziwiłł
[
DR24
]
. For the general Gauss sums
G_{\ell}(\pi)
, an equidistribution result was proven by Patterson
[
Pat87
]
, and refined by
[
DDHL
]
for
\ell=4
.
All the number field results hold for
R=1
, while the
result in Theorem
1.6
provides uniformity in the shift
R
.

1.1.
Main ideas

To prove the main result, Theorem
1.1
, we start in the usual way by employing the explicit formula which relates sums over zeros of
L
–functions to sums over monic irreducible polynomials. After removing the square-free condition on the sum over the moduli
c
which parametrize the family and using Poisson summation, we obtain a sum of Gauss sums over a dual parameter which ranges over monic polynomials
V
.

By switching the sums over the primes and the sum over the dual parameter, we are led to bound sums of Gauss sums over the primes. Since Gauss sums are not multiplicative for
\ell>2
, we cannot use the standard techniques to bound the sums over the primes (i.e., monic irreducible polynomials). Instead, we
use Vaughan’s identity to rewrite the sums over primes to sums over monic polynomials with certain divisibility conditions in some ranges. These terms come in two types, Type I and Type II sums. We note that this circle of ideas
was first used in the work of Heath-Brown and Patterson
[
HBP79
]
in showing the uniform distribution of the angles of cubic Gauss sums around the unit circle.

We then deal with the Type I and Type II terms differently. To bound the Type II terms, we use the large sieve inequality for characters of order
\ell
, as proved in Theorems
1.4
and
1.5
. This builds on ideas of Heath-Brown
[
HB95
,
HB00
]
, who obtained large sieve inequalities for quadratic and cubic characters over
\mathbb{Q}
, and work of Blomer, Goldmakher, and Louvel
[
BGL14
]
on the large sieve for (higher) fixed order characters. We obtain a bound on the Type II sums which does not depend on the order
\ell
of the characters.

Dealing with the Type I terms is more subtle, and is the main focus of the paper. After using Poisson summation and Vaughan’s identity as described above, we roughly need to consider sums which can be written in a simplified form as

\sum_{V\in\mathcal{M}_{n-d}}\sum_{b\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq U}}\mu^{2}(b)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\in\mathcal{M}_{n}\\
b|c\\
(c,V)=1\end{subarray}}G_{\ell}(V,c),

where recall that
G_{\ell}(V,c)
is the
\ell^{\text{th}}
order Gauss sum over function fields as defined in (
3.1
), and
n\leq v(d-1)
, where
v
is the support of the Fourier transform of the test function
\phi
. In the above,
\mathcal{M}_{\leq U}
denotes the set of monic polynomials of degree less than or equal to
U
, where
U
is a parameter which grows with
n
, and
U<n/3
.
We then need to consider the generating series of the Gauss sums with the coprimality and divisibility conditions as above. After (tedious) combinatorial manipulations (see Theorem
5.3
), we express this generating series in terms of the generating series

\psi^{(i)}(V,u):=(1-u^{\ell}q^{\ell})^{-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\mathcal{M}\\
\deg(F)\equiv i\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\ell)\end{subarray}}G_{\ell}(V,F)u^{\deg(F)},

for
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,\ell-1\}
, with the coprimality and divisibility conditions omitted. We are then led to bound averages of the form

(1.4)

\sum_{V\in\mathcal{M}_{n-d}}|\psi^{(i)}(V,u)|.

Therefore, one needs to understand the properties of
\psi^{(i)}(V,u)
. This function has been studied by Hoffstein and Patterson in the function field setting
[
Pat07
,
Hof92
]
. In the case of quadratic characters (
\ell=2
), the Gauss sums are multiplicative, so the generating series has an Euler product, and its structure is fully understood. However, for
\ell>2
, the Gauss sums are no longer multiplicative, and the current understanding of
\psi^{(i)}(V,u)
remains incomplete in the case of higher-order Gauss sums. These functions are known to be related to theta functions on the
\ell
-fold metaplectic covers of
\text{SL}(2)

[
Hof92
]
.

It can be shown that
\psi^{(i)}(V,u)
is a rational function (see also
[
DFL22
]
in the case
\ell=3
) with possible poles at
u^{\ell}=q^{-\ell-1}
. Crucially, the generating series
\psi^{(i)}(V,u)
satisfies a functional equation which relates
\psi^{(i)}(V,u)
to
\psi^{(i)}(V;1/(q^{2}u))
. From the functional equation and the Phragmén–Lindelöf principle, one can obtain a convexity bound on the size of
\psi^{(i)}(V,u)
in the critical strip (Theorem
4.1
). However, using the pointwise convexity bound is not optimal for
\ell
sufficiently large, and that would lead to a weaker proportion than that in Theorem
1.1
.
To get a better proportion, we use the functional equation again to obtain an “approximate functional equation” for
\psi^{(i)}(V,u)
. Since
\psi^{(i)}(V,u)
is a rational function, the numerator of
\psi^{(i)}(V,u)
is a polynomial whose degree is roughly equal to
\deg(V)
. The approximate functional equation allows us to express the numerator of
\psi^{(i)}(V,u)
as a sum of two terms, each of length roughly
\deg(V)/2
(similar to the case of
L
–functions over number fields). We are now in a position to use the large sieve inequality again and exploit the fact that
(u^{\ell}-q^{\ell-1})\psi^{(i)}(V,u)
is written in terms of shorter polynomials. This yields a Lindelöf on average bound for (
1.4
), which beats the trivial bound obtained from convexity. We note that a similar idea was exploited in
[
DdFDS
]
in the number field setting, in bounding the residual integral after extracting the contribution of the pole (in that instance, since the authors are considering the case
\ell=3
, the contribution of the poles is exact, see Theorem
4.1
). In the present work, we use the average bound when we are close to the residue, and not for the residual integral at the central critical point (which, in the case of
\psi^{(i)}(V,u)
, is at
u=1/q
.)

We also need to consider more general averages of the form

(1.5)

\sum_{V\in\mathcal{M}_{n-d}}\Big|\psi_{V}^{(i)}(V,b,u)\Big|,

where

\psi_{V}^{(i)}(V,b,u)\ =\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\in\mathcal{M}\\
b|c\\
(c,V)=1\end{subarray}}G_{\ell}(V,c)u^{\deg(c)}.

Using the combinatorial manipulations in Theorem
5.3
, evaluating (
1.5
) roughly reduces to considering sums which can be written in a simplified form as

\sum_{V\in\mathcal{H}_{n-d}}\Big|\psi^{(i)}(b^{\ell-2}V^{\ell-3},u)\Big|,

where
\mathcal{H}_{n-d}
denotes the set of monic, square-free polynomials of degree
n-d
. When
\ell
grows, the high powers of
b
and
V
become problematic, and we prove in Section
6
several versions of Lindelöf on average to be used in different ranges to optimize the bounds.

Finally, we note that simply using the convexity bound in (
1.5
) would also lead to a positive proportion of non-vanishing; however, the proportion would be weaker than what we prove using Lindelöf on average for (
1.5
) (for
\ell\geq 11
). While we believe an appropriate version of the Lindelöf on average bound should give better final error terms than the convexity bound for small values of
\ell
as well (i.e.,
\ell<11
), we have decided not to optimize that, in order to keep the article at a reasonable length.

Overview of this article

This article is organized as follows. In Section

, we gather various facts about order

in Section

. We finally prove Theorem
1.1
in Section
10
, Theorem
1.6

in Section
11
, and the large sieve inequalities in Section

.

2.
Background and setting

2.1.
Generalities about characters

For references on fixed order characters, see
[
CLDLL22
]
. Let
q
be a power of an odd prime
p
. We denote by
\mathcal{M}
the set of monic polynomials of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
, by
\mathcal{M}_{n}
the set of monic polynomials of degree exactly
n
, by

\mathcal{M}_{\leq n}
the set of monic polynomials of degree smaller than or equal to
n
,
by
\mathcal{H}
the set of monic square-free polynomials of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
and analogously for

\mathcal{H}_{n}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\leq n}
. Note that
|\mathcal{M}_{n}|=q^{n}
and for
n\geq 2
, we have that
|\mathcal{H}_{n}|=q^{n}(1-\tfrac{1}{q}).

Similarly define the sets
\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P}_{n}
and
\mathcal{P}_{\leq n}
to be the corresponding sets consisting of monic irreducible polynomials in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
. For simplicity, whenever we talk about primes in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
, we refer to monic irreducible polynomials.

We define the norm of a polynomial
f(t)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
by

|f|:=q^{\deg(f)}.

Let
\ell
be such that
p\nmid\ell
. We consider the Kummer case, in other words,
q\equiv 1\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\ell)
. We fix an isomorphism
\Omega
from the
\ell
-th roots of unity in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
to the subgroup
\mu_{\ell}
of
\ell
-th roots of unity in
\mathbb{C}^{*}
.

Let
\pi\in\mathcal{P}
, and let
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
. Then the
\ell^{\text{th}}
residue symbol modulo
\pi
is constructed as follows. If
\pi|a
, then we define
(\frac{a}{\pi})_{\ell}=0
. If
\pi\nmid a
, we define

\left(\frac{a}{\pi}\right)_{\ell}=\alpha,

where
\alpha
is the unique
\ell^{\text{th}}
root of unity in
\mu_{\ell}
such that

\displaystyle a^{\frac{q^{\deg(\pi)}-1}{\ell}}\equiv\Omega^{-1}(\alpha)\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\pi).

We extend the symbol by multiplicativity for
c=\pi_{1}^{e_{1}}\dots\pi_{k}^{e_{k}}
by

\displaystyle\left(\frac{a}{c}\right)_{\ell}=\left(\frac{a}{\pi_{1}}\right)_{\ell}^{e_{1}}\dots\left(\frac{a}{\pi_{k}}\right)_{\ell}^{e_{k}}.

We also denote

(2.1)

\displaystyle\chi_{c}(\cdot):=\left(\frac{\cdot}{c}\right)_{\ell}.

Notice that if
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\right)_{\ell}=\alpha^{\frac{q-1}{\ell}\deg(\pi)}.

We will often use the
\ell^{\text{th}}
power reciprocity law, which says the following. For
A,B\in\mathcal{M}
,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{A}{B}\right)_{\ell}\left(\frac{B}{A}\right)_{\ell}^{-1}=(-1)^{\frac{q-1}{\ell}\deg(A)\deg(B)}.

Throughout the paper, we will assume that
q\equiv 1\,(\mathrm{mod}\,2\ell)
, so
that
\left(\frac{A}{B}\right)_{\ell}=\left(\frac{B}{A}\right)_{\ell}
.

Each character
\chi_{c}
with
c
square-free of degree
d
is associated to a
cover
C
over
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
with affine equation

C:y^{\ell}=c(t)
. Denoting by
P_{\infty}
the prime at infinity, there are two cases:

If
\ell\mid d
, then
\chi
is even (that is,
\chi
is trivial on
\mathbb{F}_{q}
) and
\chi_{c}(P_{\infty})=1
.

If
\ell\nmid d
, then
\chi
is odd (that is,
\chi
is non-trivial on
\mathbb{F}_{q}
) and
\chi_{c}(P_{\infty})=0
.

By the Riemann–Hurwitz formula, the genus
g
of
C
is given by

g=-(\ell-1)+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{P}(e_{P}-1),

where the sum is taken over all the
P
in the extension that ramify and
e_{P}
denotes the ramification index. In the particular case where
\ell
is prime or when
c
is square-free (see Corollary 3.7.4 in
[
Sti09
]
), the above formula reduces to

(2.2)

g=\frac{\ell-1}{2}(d-1-\chi_{c}(P_{\infty})).

Let
\chi
be any of the characters defined by (
2.1
), and let
\mathcal{L}(u,\chi)
be the Dirichlet
L
–function defined by

\mathcal{L}(u,\chi)=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}}\chi(f)u^{\deg(f)}.

The
L
–function also has an Euler product given by

(2.3)

\mathcal{L}(u,\chi)=\prod_{P}(1-\chi(P)u^{\deg(P)})^{-1},

where the product includes the prime at infinity, and the above holds for
|u|<1/q
.

In this work, we consider
\chi\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(d)
, where
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(d)
is the family given in equation (
1.1
).
For such a primitive character
\chi
, it follows from the work of Weil
[
Wei71
]
that when
\ell
is prime,
\mathcal{L}(u,\chi)
is a polynomial of degree
d-1-\chi(P_{\infty})
by (
2.2
). In the case where
c
is square-free (and
\ell
is not necessarily prime), there are
\ell-1

L
–functions associated to the characters
\chi_{c},\dots,\chi_{c}^{\ell-1}
respectively. By Proposition 4.3 in
[
Ros02
]
, each of them has degree at most
d-1-\chi(P_{\infty})
. By (
2.2
), the total degree must be
2g=(\ell-1)(d-1-\chi(P_{\infty}))
, and therefore each
\mathcal{L}(u,\chi)
has degree exactly
d-1-\chi(P_{\infty})
in this case as well. Recall that we are taking
d\not\equiv 0\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\ell)
and therefore
\chi
is odd and
\chi(P_{\infty})=0
.

Thus, we can write

(2.4)

\mathcal{L}(u,\chi)=\prod_{j=1}^{d-1}(1-u\sqrt{q}e^{-2\pi i\theta_{\chi,j}}).

Moreover,
\mathcal{L}(u,\chi)

satisfies the functional equation

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(u,\chi)=\omega(\chi)\;(qu^{2})^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\;\mathcal{L}\left(\frac{1}{qu},\overline{\chi}\right).

The sign of the functional equation is given by

(2.5)

\omega(\chi)=\begin{cases}-q^{(d-2)/2}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{d-1}\chi(f)}=\frac{G(\chi)}{|G(\chi)|}&\text{when $\chi$ is even},\\
\\
q^{(d-1)/2}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{d-1}}\chi(f)=\frac{\sqrt{q}}{\tau(\chi)}\frac{G(\chi)}{|G(\chi)|}&\text{when $\chi$ is odd},\end{cases}

where if

\chi
odd,

(2.6)

\tau(\chi)=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}}\chi(a)e^{2\pi i\text{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{p}}(a)/p},\;\;\;\;\varepsilon(\chi)=\frac{\tau(\chi)}{q^{1/2}},

and if
\chi
even,
\varepsilon(\chi)=1
.

For any
\chi
, the Gauss sum is given by

G(\chi):=\sum_{a\,(\mathrm{mod}\,F)}\chi(a)e_{q}\left(\frac{a}{F}\right).

Here
e_{q}
is the exponential defined by Hayes
[
Hay66
]
for any
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}((1/t))
by

(2.7)

e_{q}(b)=e^{\frac{2\pi i\text{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{p}}(b_{1})}{p}},

where
b_{1}
is the coefficient of
1/t
in the Laurent expansion of
b
.

We have the following Weil bound for sums over primes. If
\chi
is a primitive character modulo
Q
and
\deg(Q)=m
, then

(2.8)

\Big|\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}\chi(\pi)\Big|\ll q^{n/2}\frac{m}{n}.

(See equation (2.5) in
[
Rud10
]
; the bound there is stated for quadratic characters, but it is easily generalized to any primitive characters.)

We will also use the Pólya–Vinogradov theorem in function fields (see
[
Hsu99
, Proposition
2.1
]
). Namely, for
\chi
a nonprincipal character modulo
Q
, we have that

(2.9)

\Big|\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}\chi(f)\Big|\ll\sqrt{|Q|}.

3.
Order
\ell
Gauss sums

Let
c,r\in\mathcal{M}
. We define the (shifted) Gauss sum

(3.1)

\displaystyle G_{\ell}(r,c)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{a\,(\mathrm{mod}\,c)}\Big(\frac{a}{c}\Big)_{\ell}\;{e_{q}}\Big(\frac{ra}{c}\Big),

where
e_{q}
is defined in (
2.7
).
More generally, we also define for any integer
j
,

\displaystyle G_{\ell,j}(r,c)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{a\,(\mathrm{mod}\,c)}\left(\frac{a}{c}\right)_{\ell}^{j}\;{e_{q}}\Big(\frac{ra}{c}\Big).

We will also use the following alternate notation for more general characters
\chi
modulo
c
, namely,

G(r,\chi)=\sum_{a\,(\mathrm{mod}\,c)}\chi(a)\;{e_{q}}\Big(\frac{ra}{c}\Big).

If
(b,c)=1
, then it is easy to see that

(3.2)

G_{\ell,j}(br,c)=\overline{\left(\frac{b}{c}\right)_{\ell}^{j}}G_{\ell,j}(r,c).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
r\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
, and
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathcal{M}
.

(i)

If
(c_{1},c_{2})=1
, then

\displaystyle G_{\ell,j}(r,{c_{1}c_{2}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}\right)_{\ell}^{2j}G_{\ell,j}(r,c_{1})G_{\ell,j}(r,{c_{2}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G_{\ell,j}(rc_{2}^{\ell-2},c_{1})G_{\ell,j}(r,c_{2}).

(ii)

If
r=\tilde{r}\pi^{\alpha}
where
\pi\nmid\tilde{r}
, then

G_{\ell,j}(r,\pi^{i})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\mbox{ if }i\leq\alpha\mbox{ and }ij\not\equiv 0\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\ell),\\
\phi(\pi^{i})&\mbox{ if }i\leq\alpha\mbox{ and }ij\equiv 0\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\ell),\\
-q^{(i-1)\deg(\pi)}&\mbox{ if }i=\alpha+1\mbox{ and }ij\equiv 0\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\ell),\\
\varepsilon(\chi_{\pi}^{ij})\omega(\chi_{\pi}^{ij})\left(\frac{\tilde{r}}{\pi^{i}}\right)_{\ell}^{-1}q^{(i-\frac{1}{2})\deg(\pi)}&\mbox{ if }i=\alpha+1\mbox{ and }ij\not\equiv 0\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\ell),\\
0&\mbox{ if }i\geq\alpha+2,\end{array}\right.

where
\phi
is the Euler
\phi
-function for polynomials and we recall that
\varepsilon(\chi)=1
when
\chi
is even and
\varepsilon(\chi)
is given in (
2.6
) for
\chi
odd. We note that
\chi_{\pi}^{ij}
is even if
\ell\mid\deg(\pi^{ij})
.

Proof.

The proof of (i) follows the same lines as in
[
Flo17
]
and Lemma 2.12 in
[
DFL22
]
.
After writing
a\,(\mathrm{mod}\,c_{1}c_{2})
as
a=a_{1}c_{1}+a_{2}c_{2}
for
a_{1}\,(\mathrm{mod}\,c_{2})
and
a_{2}\,(\mathrm{mod}\,c_{1})
, we have,

\displaystyle G_{\ell,j}(r,c_{1}c_{2})=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{c_{1}}{c_{2}}\right)_{\ell}^{j}\left(\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}\right)_{\ell}^{j}\sum_{a_{1}\,(\mathrm{mod}\,c_{2})}\sum_{a_{2}\,(\mathrm{mod}\,c_{1})}\left(\frac{a_{2}}{c_{1}}\right)_{\ell}^{j}\left(\frac{a_{1}}{c_{2}}\right)_{\ell}^{j}e_{q}\left(\frac{a_{1}r}{c_{2}}\right)e_{q}\left(\frac{a_{2}r}{c_{1}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}\right)_{\ell}^{2j}G_{\ell,j}(r,c_{1})G_{\ell,j}(r,c_{2}),

by reciprocity. The second line of (i) follows from (
3.2
).

To prove (ii), first we assume that
i\leq\alpha
. Then

G_{\ell,j}(r,\pi^{i})=\sum_{a\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\pi^{i})}\left(\frac{a}{\pi^{i}}\right)_{\ell}^{j}e_{q}(a\tilde{r}\pi^{\alpha-i}).

The exponential above is equal to
1
since
a\tilde{r}\pi^{\alpha-i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
, and if
ij\equiv 0\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\ell)
, then
\left(\frac{a}{\pi^{i}}\right)_{\ell}^{j}=1
when
(a,\pi)=1
and therefore the character is even. The conclusion follows directly in this case. If
ij\not\equiv 0\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\ell)
, the character is odd and the conclusion follows from orthogonality of characters.

Now assume that
i=\alpha+1
. Write
a\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\pi^{i})
as
a=\pi A+C
, with
A\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\pi^{i-1})
and
C\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\pi)
. Then

G_{\ell,j}(r,\pi^{i})=\sum_{A\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\pi^{i-1})}\sum_{C\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\pi)}\left(\frac{C}{\pi^{i}}\right)_{\ell}^{j}e_{q}\left(\frac{C\tilde{r}}{\pi}\right)=q^{(i-1)\deg(\pi)}\left(\frac{\tilde{r}^{-1}}{\pi^{i}}\right)_{\ell}^{j}\sum_{C\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\pi)}\left(\frac{C}{\pi^{i}}\right)_{\ell}^{j}e_{q}\left(\frac{C}{\pi}\right).

If
ij\equiv 0\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\ell)
, then
\left(\frac{\tilde{r}^{-1}}{\pi^{i}}\right)_{\ell}^{j}=1
and

\sum_{C\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\pi)}\left(\frac{C}{\pi^{i}}\right)_{\ell}^{j}e_{q}\left(\frac{C}{\pi}\right)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}C\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\pi)\\
C\neq 0\end{subarray}}e_{q}\left(\frac{C}{\pi}\right)=-1,

and the conclusion follows. Now assume that
ij\not\equiv 0\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\ell)
.
Then

\sum_{C\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\pi)}\left(\frac{C}{\pi^{i}}\right)_{\ell}^{j}e_{q}\left(\frac{C}{\pi}\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-q\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\deg(\pi)-1}}\left(\frac{f}{\pi^{i}}\right)_{\ell}^{j}&\ell\mid ij\deg(\pi),\\
\tau(\chi_{\pi}^{ij})\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\deg(\pi)-1}}\left(\frac{f}{\pi^{i}}\right)_{\ell}^{j}&\ell\nmid ij\deg(\pi).\end{array}\right.

(See equation (3.5) in
[
Flo17
]
.) Using (
2.5
), we can rewrite this as

\sum_{C\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\pi)}\left(\frac{C}{\pi^{i}}\right)_{\ell}^{j}e_{q}\left(\frac{C}{\pi}\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\omega(\chi_{\pi}^{ij})q^{\deg(\pi)/2}&\ell\mid ij\deg(\pi),\\
\varepsilon(\chi_{\pi}^{ij})\omega(\chi_{\pi}^{ij})q^{\deg(\pi)/2}&\ell\nmid ij\deg(\pi).\end{array}\right.

(See Lemma 2.3 and Corollary 2.4 in
[
DFL22
]
.)

Thus, we get

G_{\ell,j}(r,\pi^{i})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\omega(\chi_{\pi}^{ij})\left(\frac{\tilde{r}^{-1}}{\pi^{i}}\right)_{\ell}q^{(i-\frac{1}{2})\deg(\pi)}&\ell\mid ij\deg(\pi),\\
\\
\varepsilon(\chi_{\pi}^{ij})\omega(\chi_{\pi}^{ij})\left(\frac{\tilde{r}^{-1}}{\pi^{i}}\right)_{\ell}q^{(i-\frac{1}{2})\deg(\pi)}&\ell\nmid ij\deg(\pi).\end{array}\right.

If
i\geq\alpha+2
, then again the proof goes through exactly as in
[
Flo17
]
.
∎

∎

Putting things together, we get that

5.
The generating series for order
\ell
Gauss sums with coprimality and divisibility conditions

Let
v,r,a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
, and
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.
We show in this section how to express the more general generating series

\displaystyle\psi_{v}^{(i)}(r,a,u):=(1-q^{\ell}u^{\ell})^{-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\mathcal{M}\\
\deg(F)\equiv i\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\ell)\\
(F,v)=1\\
a\mid F\end{subarray}}G_{\ell}(r,F)u^{\deg(F)}

in terms of the generating series
\psi^{(i)}(r,u)
of the previous section. Notice that
\psi^{(i)}(r,u)=\psi_{1}^{(i)}(r,1,u)
. In addition, we will employ the following notation

\displaystyle\psi_{v}^{(i)}(r,u)

\displaystyle=\psi_{v}^{(i)}(r,1,u).

The following generalizes Lemma
3.5
in
[
DFL22
]
to general
\ell
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\pi
be a prime such that
\pi\nmid r
. Let
r_{0}\mid r
. Then we have

(5.1)

\displaystyle\psi_{r_{0}\pi}^{(i)}(r,u)

\displaystyle=\psi_{r_{0}}^{(i)}(r,u)-G_{\ell}(r,\pi)u^{\deg(\pi)}\psi_{r_{0}\pi}^{(i-\deg(\pi))}(r\pi^{\ell-2},u),

(5.2)

\displaystyle\psi_{r_{0}\pi}^{(i)}(r\pi^{\ell-1},u)

\displaystyle=\left(1-q^{(\ell-1)\deg(\pi)}u^{\ell\deg(\pi)}\right)^{-1}\psi_{r_{0}}^{(i)}(r\pi^{\ell-1},u),

and for
1\leq j\leq\ell-2
,

(5.3)

\displaystyle\psi_{r_{0}\pi}^{(i)}(r\pi^{j},u)

\displaystyle=\psi_{r_{0}}^{(i)}(r\pi^{j},u)-q^{j\deg(\pi)}G_{\ell,j+1}(r,\pi)u^{(j+1)\deg(\pi)}\psi_{r_{0}\pi}^{(i-(j+1)\deg(\pi))}(r\pi^{\ell-j-2},u).

Proof.

We consider the first identity. By Lemma
3.1
, we have that for
\pi\nmid rF
,
G_{\ell}(r,\pi^{k}F)=0
unless, possibly, for
k=0,1
. Writing the sum
\psi_{r_{0}\pi}^{(i)}(r,u)
as the sum over
F
with no divisibility condition with respect to
\pi
minus the sum with the condition that
\pi\mid F
, we have

(5.4)

\displaystyle\psi_{r_{0}\pi}^{(i)}(r,u)=\psi_{r_{0}}^{(i)}(r,u)-(1-q^{\ell}u^{\ell})^{-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\mathcal{M}\\
(F,\pi)=(F,r_{0})=1\\
\deg(F)\equiv i-\deg(\pi)\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\ell)\end{subarray}}G_{\ell}(r,\pi F)u^{\deg(F)+\deg(\pi)}.

By Lemma
3.1
(i),

\displaystyle G_{\ell}(r,\pi F)

\displaystyle=G_{\ell}(\pi^{\ell-2}r,F)G_{\ell}(r,\pi).

Incorporating the above into (
5.4
) gives (
5.1
).

For the second identity, we have

\displaystyle\psi_{r_{0}\pi}^{(i)}(r\pi^{\ell-1},u)=\psi_{r_{0}}^{(i)}(r\pi^{\ell-1},u)-(1-q^{\ell}u^{\ell})^{-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\mathcal{M}\\
(F,\pi)=(F,r_{0})=1\\
\deg(F)\equiv i\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\ell)\end{subarray}}G_{\ell}(r\pi^{\ell-1},\pi^{\ell}F)u^{\deg(F)+\ell\deg(\pi)},

where we have used that for
k\not=0
,
G_{\ell}(r\pi^{\ell-1},\pi^{k}F)
can only be nonzero for
k=\ell
.

By Lemma
3.1
(i),

\displaystyle G_{\ell}(r\pi^{\ell-1},\pi^{\ell}F)

\displaystyle=-G_{\ell}(r\pi^{\ell-1},F)q^{(\ell-1)\deg(\pi)},

and

\psi_{r_{0}\pi}^{(i)}(r\pi^{\ell-1},u)=\psi_{r_{0}}^{(i)}(r\pi^{\ell-1},u)+q^{(\ell-1)\deg(\pi)}u^{\ell\deg(\pi)}\psi_{r_{0}\pi}^{(i)}(r\pi^{\ell-1},u),

which can be rewritten as (
5.2
).

For (
5.3
), for any
1\leq j\leq n-2
, we have

(5.5)

\displaystyle\psi_{r_{0}\pi}^{(i)}(r\pi^{j},u)=\psi_{r_{0}}^{(i)}(r\pi^{j},u)-(1-q^{\ell}u^{\ell})^{-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\mathcal{M}\\
(F,\pi)=(F,r_{0})=1\\
\deg(F)\equiv i-(j+1)\deg(\pi)\,(\mathrm{mod}\,n)\end{subarray}}G_{\ell}(r\pi^{j},\pi^{j+1}F)u^{\deg(F)+(j+1)\deg(\pi)},

where, again, we have used that for
k\not=0
,
G_{\ell}(r\pi^{j},\pi^{k}F)
can only be nonzero for
k=j+1
.

By Lemma
3.1
(i),

\displaystyle G_{\ell}(r\pi^{j},\pi^{j+1}F)

\displaystyle=G_{\ell}(r\pi^{\ell-j-2},F)G_{\ell}(r\pi^{j},\pi^{j+1}),

where

\displaystyle G_{\ell}(r\pi^{j},\pi^{j+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\pi^{j+1})}\left(\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\right)_{\ell}^{j+1}e\left(\frac{\alpha r}{\pi}\right)=q^{j\deg(\pi)}G_{\ell,j+1}(r,\pi).

Combining with (

), we get the result.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
r_{1},\dots,r_{\ell-1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
be square-free and pairwise coprime.
We have

\displaystyle\psi_{r_{1}\cdots r_{\ell-1}}^{(i)}(r_{1}r_{2}^{2}\cdots r_{\ell-1}^{\ell-1},u)=\prod_{\pi\mid r_{\ell-1}}\left(1-q^{(\ell-1)\deg(\pi)}u^{\ell\deg(\pi)}\right)^{-1}\psi^{(i)}_{r_{1}\cdots r_{\ell-2}}(r_{1}r_{2}^{2}\cdots r_{\ell-1}^{\ell-1},u).

For
0\leq j\leq\ell-2
, we have

\displaystyle\psi^{(i)}_{r_{1}r_{2}\cdots r_{j}}(r_{1}r_{2}^{2}\cdots r_{\ell-1}^{\ell-1},u)=\prod_{\pi\mid r_{j}}\left(1-q^{(\ell-1)\deg(\pi)}\left(\frac{-1}{\pi}\right)_{\ell}^{j+1}u^{\ell\deg(\pi)}\right)^{-1}

(5.6)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{d\mid r_{j}}\mu(d)q^{j\deg(d)}G_{\ell,j+1}\left(\frac{r_{1}r_{2}^{2}\cdots r_{\ell-1}^{\ell-1}}{d^{j}},d\right)u^{(j+1)\deg(d)}\psi^{(i-(j+1)\deg(d))}_{r_{1}r_{2}\cdots r_{j-1}}\left(\frac{r_{1}r_{2}^{2}\cdots r_{\ell-1}^{\ell-1}}{d^{2j-\ell+2}},u\right).

Proof.

We first deal with the primes dividing
r_{\ell-1}

\pi\mid r_{\ell-1}

Then, by equation (

\displaystyle\psi_{R_{0}\pi}^{(i)}(R\pi^{\ell-1},u)

\displaystyle=\left(1-q^{(\ell-1)\deg(\pi)}u^{\ell\deg(\pi)}\right)^{-1}\psi_{R_{0}}^{(i)}(R\pi^{\ell-1},u),

and working by induction on the prime factors of

\displaystyle\psi_{r_{1}\cdots r_{\ell-1}}^{(i)}(r_{1}r_{2}^{2}\cdots r_{\ell-1}^{\ell-1},u)=\prod_{\pi\mid r_{\ell-1}}\left(1-q^{(\ell-1)\deg(\pi)}u^{\ell\deg(\pi)}\right)^{-1}\psi^{(i)}_{r_{1}\cdots r_{\ell-2}}(r_{1}r_{2}^{2}\cdots r_{\ell-1}^{\ell-1},u).

Let
0\leq j\leq\ell-2
and remark that

as well. Assume that

and

\displaystyle\psi_{r_{0}\pi}^{(i)}(r\pi^{j},u)

\displaystyle=\psi_{r_{0}}^{(i)}(r\pi^{j},u)-q^{j\deg(\pi)}G_{\ell,j+1}(r,\pi)u^{(j+1)\deg(\pi)}\psi_{r_{0}\pi}^{(i-(j+1)\deg(\pi))}(r\pi^{\ell-j-2},u),

\displaystyle\psi_{r_{0}\pi}^{(i-(j+1)\deg(\pi))}(r\pi^{\ell-j-2},u)

\displaystyle=\psi_{r_{0}}^{(i-(j+1)\deg(\pi))}(r\pi^{\ell-j-2},u)

\displaystyle\quad-q^{(\ell-j-2)\deg(\pi)}G_{\ell,\ell-j-1}(r,\pi)u^{(\ell-j-1)\deg(\pi)}\psi_{r_{0}\pi}^{(i)}(r\pi^{j},u).

Replacing the second equation above in the first one, we find

\displaystyle\psi_{r_{0}\pi}^{(i)}(r\pi^{j},u)

\displaystyle=\psi_{r_{0}}^{(i)}(r\pi^{j},u)-q^{j\deg(\pi)}G_{\ell,j+1}(r,\pi)u^{(j+1)\deg(\pi)}\psi_{r_{0}}^{(i-(j+1)\deg(\pi))}(r\pi^{\ell-j-2},u)

\displaystyle+q^{(\ell-2)\deg(\pi)}G_{\ell,j+1}(r,\pi)G_{\ell,\ell-j-1}(r,\pi)u^{\ell\deg(\pi)}\psi_{r_{0}\pi}^{(i)}(r\pi^{j},u).

\displaystyle\times\sum_{d\mid r_{j}}\mu(d)q^{j\deg(d)}G_{\ell,j+1}\left(\frac{r\pi_{0}^{\ell-j-2}}{d^{j}},d\right)u^{(j+1)\deg(d)}\psi^{(i-(j+1)(\deg(\pi_{0})+\deg(d))}_{r_{1}r_{2}\cdots r_{j-1}}\left(\frac{r\pi_{0}^{\ell-j-2}}{d^{2j-\ell+2}},u\right)

\displaystyle G_{\ell,j+1}(r,\pi_{0})G_{\ell,j+1}\left(\frac{r\pi_{0}^{\ell-j-2}}{d^{j}},d\right)=

by Lemma

(ii). Replacing this in (

∎

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
a,r\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
, where
a
is monic square-free and
(r,a)=1
. We write
r=r_{1}r_{2}^{2}\cdots r_{\ell-1}^{\ell-1}r_{\ell}^{\ell}
, where the

are square-free and pairwise coprime for

.

, where

Then,

|\psi_{r}^{(i)}(r,a,q^{-s})|\ll\begin{cases}|ar|^{\varepsilon}|a|^{\ell\left(\frac{3}{4}-\frac{\sigma}{2}\right)-1}|r|^{(\ell+1)\left(\frac{3}{4}-\frac{\sigma}{2}\right)-\frac{3}{2}}&\mbox{for $\ell>\frac{6}{3-2\sigma},$}\\
|ar|^{\varepsilon}|a|^{\ell\left(\frac{3}{4}-\frac{\sigma}{2}\right)-1}|r|^{\left(\frac{3}{4}-\frac{\sigma}{2}\right)}&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

Recall that
r_{\ell}^{*}
denotes the product of the primes dividing
r_{\ell}
but not
r_{1}\cdots r_{\ell-1}
. Since
a,r_{1},\dots,r_{\ell-1},r_{\ell}^{*}

3.2
to bound the Gauss sums in the formula of Theorem
5.3

\displaystyle|\psi_{r}^{(i)}(r,a,q^{-s})|

\displaystyle\ll|a|^{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}E\in\mathcal{M}\\

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq i\leq\ell-1\end{subarray}}\left|\psi^{(i)}\left(\frac{a^{\ell-2}E^{\ell-2}r_{1}r_{2}^{2}\cdots r_{\ell-1}^{\ell-1}}{\prod_{j=1}^{\ell-2}d_{j}^{2j+2-\ell}},q^{-s}\right)\right|.

The number of terms in the

-sum is bounded by
|r_{\ell}|^{\varepsilon}
, and the number of terms in the
(d_{1},\dots,d_{\ell-2})
-sum is bounded by
|ar_{1}\cdots r_{\ell-2}r_{\ell}|^{\varepsilon}.
Bounding each sum by the number of terms times the maximal value of the terms, we get

\displaystyle|\psi_{r}^{(i)}(r,a,q^{-s})|

\displaystyle\ll|ar|^{\varepsilon}|a|^{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{\ell-2}\mid ar_{\ell}^{*}r_{\ell-2}\\
d_{1}\mid r_{1},\dots,d_{\ell-3}\mid r_{\ell-3}\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell-2}|d_{j}|^{\frac{1}{2}+j-(j+1)\sigma}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}E\mid r_{\ell}^{*}\\

4.1

\displaystyle|\psi_{r}^{(i)}(r,a,q^{-s})|

\deg(ab^{2})\leq[(jn+\deg(h))/2]\end{subarray}}\sum_{v\in\mathcal{M}_{n}-\deg(\text{rad}(Q))}

\displaystyle\times\Bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}\\
\deg(F^{\prime})\leq[(\deg(h)+jn)/2]-\deg(ab^{2})\\
(F^{\prime},hQv)=1\end{subarray}}G_{\ell}(1,F^{\prime})\Big(\frac{F^{\prime}}{ab^{2}}\Big)_{\ell}^{2}\overline{\Big(\frac{F^{\prime}}{h\text{rad}(Q)v}\Big)_{\ell}}z^{\deg(F^{\prime})}b_{1}(\deg(ab^{2}F^{\prime}),z)\Bigg|^{2}.

In the sum over
F^{\prime}
, note that we can restrict to
F^{\prime}
square-free, otherwise,
G_{\ell}(1,F^{\prime})=0
. Let

a_{T_{1}}(F^{\prime})=G_{\ell}(1,F^{\prime})\Big(\frac{F^{\prime}}{ab^{2}}\Big)_{\ell}^{2}\overline{\Big(\frac{F^{\prime}}{h\text{rad}(Q)}\Big)_{\ell}}z^{\deg(F^{\prime})}b_{1}(\deg(ab^{2}F^{\prime}),z).

We get that there exists some
k\leq[(\deg(h)+jn)/2]-\deg(ab^{2})
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in\mathcal{M}_{n}-\deg(\text{rad}(Q))}

\displaystyle\Bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}\\
\deg(F^{\prime})\leq[(\deg(h)+jn)/2]-\deg(ab^{2})\\
(F^{\prime},hQv)=1\end{subarray}}a_{T_{1}}(F^{\prime})\overline{\Big(\frac{F^{\prime}}{v}\Big)_{\ell}}\Bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle\ll(\deg(h)+jn)^{2}\sum_{v\in\mathcal{M}_{n}-\deg(\text{rad}(Q))}\Bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}_{k}\\
(F^{\prime},hQv)=1\end{subarray}}a_{T_{1}}(F^{\prime})\overline{\Big(\frac{F^{\prime}}{v}\Big)_{\ell}}\Bigg|^{2}.

Using the Large Sieve Inequality as in Theorem
1.5
and the facts that
|a_{T_{1}}(F^{\prime})|\ll|F^{\prime}|^{1/2-\sigma}
and
k\leq\deg(h)/2+jn/2-\deg(ab^{2})
, we get that

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in\mathcal{M}_{n-\deg(\text{rad}(Q))}}\Bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}\\
\deg(F^{\prime})\leq[(\deg(h)+jn)/2]-\deg(ab^{2})\\
(F^{\prime},hQv)=1\end{subarray}}a_{T_{1}}(F^{\prime})\overline{\Big(\frac{F^{\prime}}{v}\Big)_{\ell}}\Bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle\ll|h|^{\varepsilon}q^{\varepsilon n}\Bigg(q^{n-\deg(\text{rad}(Q))}+q^{n(1/3+j/2)}\frac{|h|^{1/2}}{|\text{rad}(Q)|^{1/3}|ab^{2}|}+q^{n(2/3+j/3)}\frac{|h|^{1/3}}{|\text{rad}(Q)|^{2/3}|ab^{2}|^{2/3}}\Bigg)

\displaystyle\times q^{jn(1-\sigma)}|h|^{1-\sigma}|ab^{2}|^{2\sigma-2}

\displaystyle\ll|h|^{\varepsilon}q^{\varepsilon n}\Bigg(q^{n(1+j-j\sigma)-\deg(\text{rad}(Q))}|h|^{1-\sigma}|ab^{2}|^{2\sigma-2}+q^{n(1/3+3j/2-j\sigma)}\frac{|h|^{3/2-\sigma}}{|\text{rad}(Q)|^{1/3}|ab^{2}|^{3-2\sigma}}

(6.3)

\displaystyle+q^{n(2/3+4j/3-j\sigma)}\frac{|h|^{4/3-\sigma}}{|\text{rad}(Q)|^{2/3}|ab^{2}|^{8/3-2\sigma}}\Bigg).

Now we introduce the sums over
a,b
and keep in mind that
Q=ab/(ab,h)
. We have that

\text{rad}(Q)=\frac{\text{rad}(b)a}{(a,b)\text{rad}(ab,h)}.

To bound the contribution of first term in (
6.3
), we write

\displaystyle\sum_{\deg(ab^{2})\leq[(jn+\deg(h))/2]}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\text{rad}(Q)||ab^{2}|^{2-2\sigma}}=\sum_{\deg(a)\leq[(jn+\deg(h))/2]}\frac{1}{|a|^{3-2\sigma}}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\deg(b)\leq(jn+\deg(h))/4-\deg(a)/2}\frac{|(a,b)||\text{rad}(ab,h)|}{|\text{rad}(b)||b|^{4-4\sigma}}.

The generating series of the sum over
b
is

\displaystyle\sum_{b\in\mathcal{M}}\frac{|(a,b)||\text{rad}(ab,h)|}{|\text{rad}(b)||b|^{4-4\sigma}}u^{\deg(b)}=\prod_{P\nmid ah}\Big(1+\frac{u^{\deg(P)}}{|P|^{5-4\sigma}(1-\frac{u^{\deg(P)}}{|P|^{4-4\sigma}})}\Big)

\displaystyle\times\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}P|h\\
P\nmid a\end{subarray}}\Big(1-\frac{u^{\deg(P)}}{|P|^{4-4\sigma}}\Big)^{-1}\prod_{P|(a,h)}\Big(|P|\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{u^{j\deg(P)}}{|P|^{j(4-4\sigma)}}\Big)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}P|a\\
P\nmid h\end{subarray}}\Big(1-\frac{u^{\deg(P)}}{|P|^{4-4\sigma}}\Big)^{-1},

which converges for
|u|<q^{4-4\sigma}
. By using Perron’s formula for the sum over
b
and choosing the contour of integration
|u|=q^{4-4\sigma-\varepsilon}
, we get that

\sum_{\deg(b)\leq(jn+\deg(h))/4-\deg(a)/2}\frac{|(a,b)||\text{rad}(ab,h)|}{|\text{rad}(b)||b|^{4-4\sigma}}\ll q^{(4\sigma-4)(\frac{jn+\deg(h)}{4}-\frac{\deg(a)}{2})}|(a,h)||ah|^{\varepsilon}.

Introducing the sum over
a
, we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{\deg(a)\leq[(jn+\deg(h))/2]}\frac{|(a,h)|}{|a|}\ll\sum_{d|h}|d|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\deg(a)[(jn+\deg(h))/2]\\
(a,h)=d\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{|a|}\ll|h|^{\varepsilon}q^{jn\varepsilon}.

Combining the equations above, it follows that the contribution of the first term of (
6.3
) is bounded by

(6.4)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in\mathcal{H}\\
\deg(ab^{2})\leq[(jn+\deg(h))/2]\end{subarray}}q^{n(1+j-j\sigma)-\deg(\text{rad}(Q))}|h|^{1-\sigma}|ab^{2}|^{2\sigma-2}\ll q^{n+\varepsilon n}|h|^{\varepsilon}.

To bound the contribution of second and third terms in (
6.3
), we need to estimate sums of the form

\displaystyle\sum_{\deg(ab^{2})\leq(jn+\deg(h))/2}

\displaystyle\frac{|(a,b)|^{\Delta}|\text{rad}(ab,h)|^{\Delta}}{|\text{rad}(b)a|^{\Delta}|ab^{2}|^{\delta}}=\sum_{\deg(a)\leq(jn+\deg(h))/2}\frac{1}{|a|^{\Delta+\delta}}

(6.5)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\deg(b)\leq(jn+\deg(h))/4-\deg(a)/2}\frac{|(a,b)|^{\Delta}|\text{rad}(ab,h)|^{\Delta}}{|\text{rad}(b)|^{\Delta}|b|^{2\delta}},

where
\delta,\Delta\geq 0
and
\Delta<1
. The generating series of the sum over
b
becomes

\displaystyle\sum_{b\in\mathcal{M}}

\displaystyle\frac{|(a,b)|^{\Delta}|\text{rad}(ab,h)|^{\Delta}}{|\text{rad}(b)|^{\Delta}|b|^{2\delta}}u^{\deg(b)}=\prod_{P\nmid ah}\Big(1+\frac{u^{\deg(P)}}{|P|^{\Delta+2\delta}(1-\frac{u^{\deg(P)}}{|P|^{2\delta}})}\Big)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}P|h\\
P\nmid a\end{subarray}}\Big(1-\frac{u^{\deg(P)}}{|P|^{2\delta}}\Big)^{-1}

\displaystyle\times\prod_{P|(a,h)}|P|^{\Delta}\Big(1-\frac{u^{\deg(P)}}{|P|^{2\delta}}\Big)^{-1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}P|a\\
P\nmid h\end{subarray}}\Big(1-\frac{u^{\deg(P)}}{|P|^{2\delta}}\Big)^{-1}

\displaystyle=|(a,h)|^{\Delta}\prod_{P\nmid ah}\Big(1+\frac{u^{\deg(P)}}{|P|^{\Delta+2\delta}(1-\frac{u^{\deg(P)}}{|P|^{2\delta}})}\Big)\prod_{P|ah}\Big(1-\frac{u^{\deg(P)}}{|P|^{2\delta}}\Big)^{-1}

\displaystyle:=|(a,h)|^{\Delta}\mathcal{Z}\Big(\frac{u}{q^{\Delta+2\delta}}\Big)\mathcal{F}(u),

where
\mathcal{F}(u)
converges absolutely for
|u|<q^{2\delta}
.
If we denote by
M=(jn+\deg(h))/4-\deg(a)/2
, then Perron’s formula gives

\displaystyle\sum_{\deg(b)\leq(jn+\deg(h))/4-\deg(a)/2}\frac{|(a,b)|^{\Delta}|\text{rad}(ab,h)|^{\Delta}}{|\text{rad}(b)|^{\Delta}|b|^{2\delta}}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint\frac{|(a,h)|^{\Delta}\mathcal{F}(u)}{(1-u)(1-q^{1-\Delta-2\delta}u)u^{M+1}}\,du,

where we are integrating along a small circle around the origin. If
\Delta+2\delta-1<0
, then we get that

(6.6)

\sum_{\deg(b)\leq(jn+\deg(h))/4-\deg(a)/2}\frac{|(a,b)|^{\Delta}|\text{rad}(ab,h)|^{\Delta}}{|\text{rad}(b)|^{\Delta}|b|^{2\delta}}\ll q^{(1-\Delta-2\delta)M}|(a,h)|^{\Delta}|ah|^{\varepsilon},

while if
\Delta+2\delta-1>0
, then

\sum_{\deg(b)\leq(jn+\deg(h))/4-\deg(a)/2}\frac{|(a,b)|^{\Delta}|\text{rad}(ab,h)|^{\Delta}}{|\text{rad}(b)|^{\Delta}|b|^{2\delta}}\ll q^{\varepsilon n}|(a,h)|^{\Delta}|ah|^{\varepsilon}.

Introducing the sum over
a
, if
\Delta+2\delta-1>0
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\deg(a)\leq(jn+\deg(h))/2}\frac{|(a,h)^{\Delta}|}{|a|^{\Delta+\delta}}\ll\begin{cases}|h|^{\varepsilon}q^{\varepsilon n}&\mbox{ if }\Delta+\delta\geq 1,\\
|h|^{\varepsilon}q^{(jn+\deg(h))(1-\Delta-\delta)/2}&\mbox{ if }\Delta+\delta<1,\end{cases}

and hence in this case,

\eqref{toestimate}\ll|h|^{\varepsilon}q^{\varepsilon n}\max\Big\{1,q^{(1-\Delta-\delta)jn/2}|h|^{(1-\Delta-\delta)/2}\Big\}.

If
\Delta+2\delta-1<0
, then introducing the sum over
a
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\deg(a)\leq(jn+\deg(h))/2}\frac{|(a,h)^{\Delta}|}{|a|^{1/2+\Delta/2}}\ll q^{(jn+\deg(h))(1-\Delta)/4}|h|^{\varepsilon}.

Using this and with (
6.6
), we get that in the case
\Delta+2\delta-1<0
, we have

\eqref{toestimate}\ll|h|^{\varepsilon}q^{(jn+\deg(h))(1-\Delta-\delta)/2}.

Combining this with the case
\Delta+2\delta-1>0
, it follows that

(6.7)

\displaystyle\eqref{toestimate}\ll|h|^{\varepsilon}q^{\varepsilon n}\max\Big\{1,q^{(1-\Delta-\delta)jn/2}|h|^{(1-\Delta-\delta)/2}\Big\}.

Using this to bound the second and third terms in (
6.3
) (with
\Delta=\frac{1}{3}
and
\delta={3-2\sigma}
for the second term and
\Delta=\frac{2}{3}
and
\delta=\frac{8}{3}-2\sigma
for the third term), together with (
6.4
), we get that

\displaystyle\eqref{tobound}\ll|h|^{\varepsilon}q^{\varepsilon n}\Big(q^{n}+\Big(q^{n(1/3+3j/2-j\sigma)}|h|^{3/2-\sigma}+q^{n(2/3+4j/3-j\sigma)}|h|^{4/3-\sigma}\Big)\max\Big\{1,q^{(jn+\deg(h))(\sigma-7/6)}\Big\}\Big).

To bound the contributions from
T_{2}
and
T_{3}
, the argument we used to bound the contribution from
T_{1}
goes through similarly. For
T_{2}
, we replace
z\mapsto 1/(q^{2}z)
, and we multiply the final bound by
|qz|^{\deg(hV^{j})}
. The only slight variation is in bounding the sum over
W
in (
6.2
). Namely, using Lemma
3.1
(in particular, the fact that
|G_{\ell}(h,W)|
is multiplicative as a function of
W
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{W|(hV^{j})^{2}}\frac{|G_{\ell}(hV^{j},W)|^{2}}{|W|^{2(2-\sigma)}}=\prod_{P|hV}\Big(1+O\Big(\sum_{i\geq 2}\frac{|P|^{2i}}{|P|^{2i(2-\sigma)}}\Big)\Big)\ll|hV|^{\varepsilon}.

Proceeding as in the case of
T_{1}
, we get that

\displaystyle\sum_{V\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}|T_{2}(hV^{j},z)|^{2}\ll

\displaystyle|h|^{\varepsilon}q^{\varepsilon n}\Big(q^{n}+q^{n(1/3+3j/2-j\sigma)}|h|^{3/2-\sigma}+q^{n(2/3+4j/3-j\sigma)}|h|^{4/3-\sigma}\Big),

where again we used (
6.4
) and (
6.7
).
For
T_{3}
, we again proceed similarly as before, but now we replace
a_{T_{1}}(F^{\prime})
by
a_{T_{3}}(F^{\prime})
, where

a_{T_{3}}(F^{\prime})=G_{\ell}(1,F^{\prime})\Big(\frac{F^{\prime}}{ab^{2}}\Big)_{\ell}^{2}\overline{\Big(\frac{F^{\prime}}{h\text{rad}(Q)}\Big)_{\ell}}(q^{2}z)^{-\deg(F^{\prime})}b_{3}(\deg(ab^{2}F^{\prime}),z).

Note that

|a_{T_{3}}(F^{\prime})|\ll|F^{\prime}|^{\sigma-3/2}

and
k\leq\deg(h)/2+jn/2-\deg(ab^{2})
. We then get that

\displaystyle\sum_{V\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}|T_{3}(hV,z)|^{2}

\displaystyle\ll|h|^{\varepsilon}q^{\varepsilon n}\Big(q^{n}+q^{n(1/3+3j/2-j\sigma)}|h|^{3/2-\sigma}+q^{n(2/3+4j/3-j\sigma)}|h|^{4/3-\sigma}\Big).

Combining the bounds for the contributions of
T_{1},T_{2},
and
T_{3}
finishes the proof.
∎

We will also prove the following Proposition.

.

with

square-free and pairwise coprime, and where

denotes the product of the primes dividing
V_{\ell}
, but not
V_{1}\ldots V_{\ell-1}
. It then follows that for any
\varepsilon>0

\displaystyle\sum_{V\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}

\displaystyle|\psi^{(i)}_{V}(V,b,z)|\ll|b|^{1/2-\sigma+\varepsilon}\sum_{n_{1}+2n_{2}+\cdots+\ell n_{\ell}=n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{\ell-2}\mid bEV_{\ell-2}\\

Focusing on the sum over
V_{1}
in the expression above and letting

, we have

(6.8)

\displaystyle\sum_{\deg(V_{1})=n_{1}}\sum_{d_{1}|V_{1}}|d_{1}|^{\frac{3}{2}-2\sigma}|\psi^{(j)}(\alpha V_{1}d_{1}^{\ell-4},z)|=\sum_{\deg(a)\leq n_{1}}q^{(n_{1}-\deg(a))(\frac{3}{2}-2\sigma)}\sum_{\deg(d_{1})=n_{1}-\deg(a)}|\psi^{(j)}(\alpha ad_{1}^{\ell-3},z)|.

Now we use Corollary
6.2
to get that

\displaystyle\sum_{\deg(d_{1})=n_{1}-\deg(a)}

\displaystyle|\psi^{(j)}(\alpha ad_{1}^{\ell-3},z)|\ll|\alpha a|^{\varepsilon}q^{\varepsilon n_{1}}\Big(q^{n_{1}-\deg(a)}

\displaystyle+\max\{q^{(n_{1}-\deg(a))(\frac{3\ell}{4}-\frac{19}{12}-\frac{(\ell-3)\sigma}{2})}|\alpha a|^{\frac{1}{2}(\frac{3}{2}-\sigma)},q^{(n_{1}-\deg(a))(\frac{\ell}{6}+\frac{1}{6})}|\alpha a|^{\frac{1}{6}}\}\Big).

Introducing the sum over
a
, we have that

(
6.8
)

\displaystyle\ll q^{n_{1}(\frac{3}{2}-2\sigma)+\varepsilon n}|\alpha|^{\varepsilon}\sum_{\deg(a)\leq n_{1}}\frac{1}{|a|^{\frac{3}{2}-2\sigma}}\Big(q^{n_{1}-\deg(a)}

\displaystyle+\max\{q^{(n_{1}-\deg(a))(\frac{3\ell}{4}-\frac{19}{12}-\frac{(\ell-3)\sigma}{2})}|\alpha a|^{\frac{1}{2}(\frac{3}{2}-\sigma)},q^{(n_{1}-\deg(a))(\frac{\ell}{6}+\frac{1}{6})}|\alpha a|^{\frac{1}{6}}\}\Big)

\displaystyle\ll|\alpha|^{\varepsilon}\Big(q^{n_{1}}+\max\{q^{n_{1}\max\{\frac{\ell(9-6\sigma)-6\sigma-1}{12},\frac{7-2\sigma}{4}\}}|\alpha|^{\frac{1}{2}(\frac{3}{2}-\sigma)},q^{n_{1}\max\{\frac{\ell}{6}-2\sigma+\frac{5}{3},\frac{7}{6}\}}|\alpha|^{\frac{1}{6}}\}\Big).

Now we introduce the sum over
V_{i}
for

. For

\sum_{\deg(V_{i})=n_{i}}\sum_{d_{i}|V_{i}}|d_{i}|^{i+\frac{1}{2}-(i+1)\sigma}\Big|\frac{V_{i}^{i}}{d_{i}^{2i+2-\ell}}\Big|^{\theta}\ll\begin{cases}q^{n_{i}(1+i\theta)}&\mbox{ if }\theta(\ell-2-2i)+i+\frac{1}{2}-(i+1)\sigma\leq 0\\
q^{n_{i}(\theta(\ell-2-i)+i+\frac{3}{2}-(i+1)\sigma)}&\mbox{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

We similarly have that

\displaystyle\sum_{\deg(V_{\ell-2})=n_{\ell-2}}\sum_{d_{\ell-2}|EbV_{\ell-2}}|d_{\ell-2}|^{\ell-\frac{3}{2}-(\ell-1)\sigma}\Big|\frac{V_{\ell-2}^{\ell-2}}{d_{\ell-2}^{\ell-2}}\Big|^{\theta}\ll q^{n_{\ell-2}(1+\theta(\ell-2))},

\displaystyle\sum_{\deg(V_{\ell-1})=n_{\ell-1}}|V_{\ell-1}|^{(\ell-1)\theta}\ll q^{n_{\ell-1}(1+\theta(\ell-1))},

Putting all of these together, we get that

(
6.3
)

\displaystyle\ll|b|^{1/2-\sigma+\varepsilon}\sum_{n_{1}+2n_{2}+\cdots+\ell n_{\ell}=n}\Big(q^{n}+\max\{q^{n_{1}\max\{\frac{\ell(9-6\sigma)-6\sigma-1}{12},\frac{7-2\sigma}{4}\}}|b^{\ell-2}|^{\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{3}{2}-\sigma\right)}

\displaystyle\times\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq i<{\frac{(3-2\sigma)(\ell-2)}{2}+1-2\sigma}\end{subarray}}q^{n_{i}(\frac{\ell(3-2\sigma)}{4}+\frac{i(1-2\sigma)}{4})}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\geq{\frac{(3-2\sigma)(\ell-2)}{2}+1-2\sigma}\end{subarray}}q^{n_{i}(1+\frac{i}{2}\left(\frac{3}{2}-\sigma\right))}

(6.9)

\displaystyle\times q^{n_{1}\max\{\frac{\ell}{6}-2\sigma+\frac{5}{3},\frac{7}{6}\}}|b^{\ell-2}|^{\frac{1}{6}}\prod_{2\leq i<\frac{\frac{\ell+1}{6}-\sigma}{\sigma-\frac{2}{3}}}q^{n_{i}(\frac{\ell+7+5i}{6}-(i+1)\sigma)}\prod_{i\geq\frac{\frac{\ell+1}{6}-\sigma}{\sigma-\frac{2}{3}}}q^{n_{i}(1+\frac{i}{6})}\}\Big).

Now note that

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq i<{\frac{(3-2\sigma)(\ell-2)}{2}+1-2\sigma}\end{subarray}}q^{n_{i}(\frac{\ell(3-2\sigma)}{4}+\frac{i(1-2\sigma)}{4})}\leq q^{\left(n-n_{1}-\sum_{i\geq\frac{(3-2\sigma)(\ell-2)}{2}+1-2\sigma}in_{i}\right)\left(\frac{\ell(3-2\sigma)}{8}-\frac{2\sigma-1}{4}\right)},

so

\displaystyle q^{n_{1}\max\{\frac{\ell(9-6\sigma)-6\sigma-1}{12},\frac{7-2\sigma}{4}\}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq i<{\frac{(3-2\sigma)(\ell-2)}{2}+1-2\sigma}\end{subarray}}q^{n_{i}(\frac{\ell(3-2\sigma)}{4}+\frac{i(1-2\sigma)}{4})}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\geq{\frac{(3-2\sigma)(\ell-2)}{2}+1-2\sigma}\end{subarray}}q^{n_{i}(1+\frac{i}{2}\left(\frac{3}{2}-\sigma\right))}

\displaystyle\leq q^{n(\frac{\ell(3-2\sigma)}{8}-\frac{2\sigma-1}{4})}q^{n_{1}\Big(\max\{\frac{\ell(9-6\sigma)-6\sigma-1}{12},\frac{7-2\sigma}{4}\}-(\frac{\ell(3-2\sigma)}{8}-\frac{2\sigma-1}{4})\Big)}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\geq{\frac{(3-2\sigma)(\ell-2)}{2}+1-2\sigma}\end{subarray}}q^{n_{i}\Big(\frac{i\ell(2\sigma-3)}{8}+\frac{i}{2}+1\Big)}

\displaystyle\ll q^{n\max\{\frac{\ell(9-6\sigma)-6\sigma-1}{12},\frac{7-2\sigma}{4}\}}.

We similarly get that

\displaystyle q^{n_{1}\max\{\frac{\ell}{6}-2\sigma+\frac{5}{3},\frac{7}{6}\}}\prod_{2\leq i<\frac{\frac{\ell+1}{6}-\sigma}{\sigma-\frac{2}{3}}}q^{n_{i}(\frac{\ell+7+5i}{6}-(i+1)\sigma)}\prod_{i\geq\frac{\frac{\ell+1}{6}-\sigma}{\sigma-\frac{2}{3}}}q^{n_{i}(1+\frac{i}{6})}

\displaystyle\ll q^{n\max\{\frac{\ell}{6}-2\sigma+\frac{5}{3},\frac{7}{6}\}}\prod_{i\geq\frac{\frac{\ell+1}{6}-\sigma}{\sigma-\frac{2}{3}}}q^{n_{i}(1-\frac{i}{2}(\frac{\ell}{6}+\frac{15}{6}-3\sigma))}.

Using the two bounds above and (

)

\displaystyle\ll|b|^{1/2-\sigma+\varepsilon}q^{\varepsilon n}\Big(q^{n}+q^{n\max\{\frac{\ell(9-6\sigma)-6\sigma-1}{12},\frac{7-2\sigma}{4}\}}|b|^{(\ell-2)\frac{1}{2}(\frac{3}{2}-\sigma)}

\displaystyle+q^{n\max\{\frac{\ell}{6}-2\sigma+\frac{5}{3},\frac{7}{6}\}}|b|^{\frac{\ell-2}{6}}\sum_{n_{1}+2n_{2}+\cdots+\ell n_{\ell}=n}\prod_{i\geq\frac{\frac{\ell+1}{6}-\sigma}{\sigma-\frac{2}{3}}}q^{n_{i}(1-\frac{i}{2}(\frac{\ell}{6}+\frac{15}{6}-3\sigma))}\Big)\Big),

which finishes the proof.
∎

The explicit formula

(b,R)=1\end{subarray}}\mu^{2}(b)h(b)\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}\frac{\psi_{R}^{(i)}(R,b,z)}{z^{n+1}}\,dz.

Choosing the contour of integration to be
|z|=q^{-1-1/\ell-\varepsilon}
, we have that

(11.3)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{2^{\prime}}(n,R,U)\ll\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}|z|=q^{-1-1/\ell-\varepsilon}\end{subarray}}q^{n(1+1/\ell+\varepsilon)}\sum_{\deg(b)\leq U}|b|^{\varepsilon}\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}|\psi_{R}^{(i)}(R,b,z)|.

Using Corollary
5.4
, for
\ell\geq 9
, we get that

\Sigma_{2^{\prime}}(n,R,U)\ll q^{n(1+1/\ell+\varepsilon)+\frac{U(\ell-2)}{4}}|R|^{\frac{\ell}{4}-\frac{7}{4}-\frac{1}{2\ell}+\varepsilon},

and similarly

\Sigma_{1}(n,R,U)\ll q^{n(1+1/\ell+\varepsilon)+\frac{U(\ell-2)}{4}}|R|^{\frac{\ell}{4}-\frac{7}{4}-\frac{1}{2\ell}+\varepsilon}.

Using the two equations above and (
11.1
) and (
11.2
), we have that

\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}G_{\ell}(R,\pi)\ll q^{\frac{3n}{2}-\frac{U}{2}+\varepsilon n}+q^{n(1+1/\ell+\varepsilon)+\frac{U(\ell-2)}{4}}|R|^{\frac{\ell}{4}-\frac{7}{4}-\frac{1}{2\ell}+\varepsilon}.

Note that when

\deg(R)<\frac{2n(\ell-2)}{\ell^{2}-7\ell-2},

we pick

U=\frac{2n(\ell-2)-\deg(R)(\ell^{2}-7\ell-2)}{\ell^{2}},

and we get in this case that

(11.4)

\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}G_{\ell}(R,\pi)\deg(\pi)\ll q^{\frac{n(3\ell^{2}-2\ell+4)+\deg(R)(\ell^{2}-7\ell-2)}{2\ell^{2}}+\varepsilon n}.

When
3<\ell\leq 8
, we use Corollary
5.4
and from equation (
11.3
), we have that

\Sigma_{2^{\prime}}(n,R,U)\ll q^{n(1+1/\ell+\varepsilon)+\frac{U(\ell-2)}{4}}|R|^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{2\ell}+\varepsilon},

and similarly

\Sigma_{1}(n,R,U)\ll q^{n(1+1/\ell+\varepsilon)+\frac{U(\ell-2)}{4}}|R|^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{2\ell}+\varepsilon}.

Using these and (
11.1
) and (
11.2
), we have that

\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}G_{\ell}(R,\pi)\ll q^{\frac{3n}{2}-\frac{U}{2}+\varepsilon n}+q^{n(1+1/\ell+\varepsilon)+\frac{U(\ell-2)}{4}}|R|^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{2\ell}+\varepsilon}.

For
\deg(R)<2n
, choosing

U=\frac{(\ell-2)(2n-\deg(R))}{\ell^{2}},

we have that

(11.5)

\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}G_{\ell}(R,\pi)\deg(\pi)\ll q^{\frac{n(3\ell^{2}-2\ell+4)+\deg(R)(\ell-2)}{2\ell^{2}}+\varepsilon n}.

When
\ell=3

|z|=q^{-4/3-\varepsilon}
and proceed as in Section
9

Similarly to equation (
9.2

\displaystyle\Sigma_{2^{\prime}}(n,R,u)

\displaystyle\ll\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}|z|=q^{-4/3-\varepsilon}\end{subarray}}q^{4n/3+\varepsilon n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\deg(b)\leq U\\
(b,R)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(b)^{2}}{|b|^{5/6-\varepsilon}}\sum_{E|r_{3}^{*}}\frac{1}{|E|^{5/6-\varepsilon}}\sum_{d|bEr_{1}}\frac{1}{|d|^{7/6-\varepsilon}}\sum_{i=0}^{2}\Big|\psi^{(i)}\Big(\frac{bEr_{1}r_{2}^{2}}{d},z\Big)\Big|,

where in the above we write
R=r_{1}r_{2}^{2}r_{3}^{3}

r_{1},r_{2}
are square-free and coprime, and where
r_{3}^{*}
denotes, as before, the product of the primes dividing
r_{3}
, but not
r_{1}r_{2}
.
Now we use Lemma
4.3
, and since
(b,R)=1
and
b

\displaystyle\Sigma_{2^{\prime}}(n,R,u)

\displaystyle\ll q^{4n/3+\varepsilon n}|R|^{\varepsilon}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\deg(b)\leq U\\
(b,R)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(b)^{2}}{|b|^{5/6-\varepsilon}}\sum_{E|r_{3}^{*}}\frac{1}{|E|^{5/6-\varepsilon}}\sum_{d|bEr_{1}}\frac{1}{|d|^{7/6-\varepsilon}}\sum_{i=0}^{2}\Big|\frac{bEr_{1}}{d}\Big|^{-1/6}

\displaystyle\ll q^{4n/3+\varepsilon n}|R|^{\varepsilon}.

Combining this with (
11.1
) and (
11.2
) and choosing

\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}G_{\ell}(R,\pi)\ll q^{4n/3+\varepsilon n}|R|^{\varepsilon}.

Together with (

Our goal is to show that

\sum_{M\in\mathcal{M}_{m}}\Big(\frac{M}{N_{1}}\Big)_{\ell}\overline{\Big(\frac{M}{N_{2}}\Big)_{\ell}}\ll q^{n(1+\varepsilon)}.

Further applying Cauchy–Schwarz on the sum over
N_{1},N_{2}
, we get that

\Sigma_{1}\leq q^{\varepsilon n}(q^{m}+q^{2n})\sum_{N\in\mathcal{H}_{n}}|\lambda(N)|^{2},

and therefore,

(12.1)

\mathcal{B}_{1}(m,n)\ll q^{\varepsilon n}(q^{m}+q^{2n}).

By duality (see Lemma
4
in
[
FS
]

),

\mathcal{B}_{1}(m,n)=\mathcal{B}_{1}(n,m),

Similarly to

5
]

9

Lemma 12.1
.

There is a sufficiently large absolute constant
C
as follows. If
q^{m_{2}-m_{1}}\geq C(n+m_{1})

\mathcal{B}_{1}(m_{1},n)\ll\mathcal{B}_{1}(m_{2},n)

\mathcal{B}_{1}(m,n_{1})\ll\mathcal{B}_{1}(m,n_{2}).

Proof.

Let
P

\deg(P)=m_{2}-m_{1}

\displaystyle\sum_{M\in\mathcal{H}_{m_{1}}}\Big|\sum_{N\in\mathcal{H}_{n}}\lambda(N)\left(\frac{M}{N}\right)_{\ell}\Big|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle 2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}M\in\mathcal{H}_{m_{1}}\\
P\nmid M\end{subarray}}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\in\mathcal{H}_{n}\\
P\mid N\end{subarray}}\lambda(N)\left(\frac{M}{N}\right)_{\ell}\Big|^{2}+2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}M\in\mathcal{H}_{m_{1}}\\
P\nmid M\end{subarray}}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\in\mathcal{H}_{n}\\
P\nmid N\end{subarray}}\lambda(N)\left(\frac{M}{N}\right)_{\ell}\Big|^{2}

(12.2)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}M\in\mathcal{H}_{m_{1}}\\
P\mid M\end{subarray}}\Big|\sum_{N\in\mathcal{H}_{n}}\lambda(N)\left(\frac{M}{N}\right)_{\ell}\Big|^{2}.

For the second sum on the right-hand side above, note that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}M\in\mathcal{H}_{m_{1}}\\
P\nmid M\end{subarray}}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\in\mathcal{H}_{n}\\
P\nmid N\end{subarray}}\lambda(N)\left(\frac{M}{N}\right)_{\ell}\Big|^{2}

where

We also have that the first sum on the right-hand side of (

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}M\in\mathcal{H}_{m_{1}}\end{subarray}}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\in\mathcal{H}_{n}\\
P\mid N\end{subarray}}\lambda(N)\left(\frac{M}{N}\right)_{\ell}\Big|^{2}\leq\mathcal{B}_{1}(m_{1},n)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\in\mathcal{H}_{n}\\
P\mid N\end{subarray}}|\lambda(N)|^{2}.

Combining the above and equation (

\displaystyle\sum_{M\in\mathcal{H}_{m_{1}}}\Big|\sum_{N\in\mathcal{H}_{n}}\lambda(N)\left(\frac{M}{N}\right)_{\ell}\Big|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}M\in\mathcal{H}_{m_{1}}\\
P\mid M\end{subarray}}\Big|\sum_{N\in\mathcal{H}_{n}}\lambda(N)\left(\frac{M}{N}\right)_{\ell}\Big|^{2}.

\displaystyle\frac{q^{m_{2}-m_{1}}}{m_{2}-m_{1}}\sum_{M\in\mathcal{H}_{m_{1}}}

P\mid N\end{subarray}}|\lambda(N)|^{2}

\displaystyle+2\frac{q^{m_{2}-m_{1}}}{m_{2}-m_{1}}\mathcal{B}_{1}(m_{2},n)\sum_{N\in\mathcal{H}_{n}}|\lambda(N)|^{2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathcal{P}_{m_{2}-m_{1}}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}M\in\mathcal{H}_{m_{1}}\\
P\mid M\end{subarray}}\Big|\sum_{N\in\mathcal{H}_{n}}\lambda(N)\left(\frac{M}{N}\right)_{\ell}\Big|^{2}.

Lemma 12.2

D|N\end{subarray}}q^{n}|\lambda(N)|^{2},

\displaystyle S_{3,1}

\displaystyle\ll q^{m-n}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\in\mathcal{H}_{n}\\
D|N\end{subarray}}|\lambda(N)|^{2}\max\{\mathcal{B}_{2}(j,n):j\leq 2n-m-2\}

\displaystyle=q^{m-n}\max\{\mathcal{B}_{2}(j,n):j\leq 2n-m-2\}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\in\mathcal{H}_{n}\end{subarray}}|\lambda(N)|^{2}d(N)

\displaystyle\ll q^{m-n+\varepsilon n}\max\{\mathcal{B}_{2}(j,n):j\leq 2n-m-2\}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\in\mathcal{H}_{n}\end{subarray}}|\lambda(N)|^{2}.

A similar expression holds for
S_{3,2}
, which finishes the proof of the statement.
∎

Now we will prove the following recursive estimate.

Lemma 12.6
.

Suppose that
4/3<\alpha\leq 2
and

\mathcal{B}_{1}(m,n)\ll q^{\varepsilon(m+n)}\Big(q^{m}+q^{\alpha n}+q^{\frac{2}{3}(m+n)}\Big),

for any
\varepsilon>0
. Then

\mathcal{B}_{1}(m,n)\ll q^{\varepsilon(m+n)}\Big(q^{m}+q^{(2-\frac{2}{3\alpha-1})n}+q^{\frac{2}{3}(m+n)}\Big).

Proof.

Using duality and the condition in the statement, we have that

\mathcal{B}_{1}(m,n)\ll q^{\varepsilon(m+n)}\Big(q^{\alpha m}+q^{n}+q^{\frac{2}{3}(m+n)}\Big).

We use this in Lemma
12.2
as follows. If
m_{1}\geq m_{2}
, then we use Lemma
12.2
with
k=1
, and we have that

(12.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{2}(m,n)\ll q^{\varepsilon(m+n)+m_{2}+\cdots+m_{\ell}}\Big(q^{\alpha m_{1}}+q^{n}+q^{\frac{2}{3}(m_{1}+n)}\Big).

Now we use the fact that for
m_{1}\geq m_{2}
,

(12.5)

q^{m_{2}+\cdots+m_{\ell}}\ll\min\{q^{\frac{m}{3}},q^{\frac{2}{3}(m-m_{1})}\}.

Indeed, to get the inequality above, note that

m_{2}+\cdots+m_{\ell}=\frac{1}{3}(3m_{2}+\cdots+3m_{\ell})\leq\frac{1}{3}(m+m_{2}-m_{1})\leq\frac{m}{3},

and

m_{2}+\cdots+m_{\ell}=\frac{1}{2}(2m_{2}+\cdots+2m_{\ell})\leq\frac{m-m_{1}}{2}\leq\frac{2(m-m_{1})}{3}.

Combining equations (
12.4
) and (
12.5
), we get that

(12.6)

\mathcal{B}_{2}(m,n)\ll q^{\varepsilon(m+n)}(q^{\alpha m}+q^{\frac{m}{3}+n}).

If
m_{1}<m_{2}
, then we use Lemma
12.2
with
k=2
and use the fact that

(12.7)

q^{m_{1}+m_{3}+\cdots+m_{\ell}}\leq\min\{q^{\frac{m}{3}},q^{\frac{2}{3}(m-m_{2})}\}.

Indeed, to see the above, note that

m_{1}+m_{3}+\cdots+m_{\ell}=\frac{1}{3}(3m_{1}+3m_{3}+\cdots+3m_{\ell})\leq\frac{1}{3}(m+2m_{1}-2m_{2}).

We then get that

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{2}(m,n)

\displaystyle\ll q^{\varepsilon(m+n)}\min\{q^{\frac{m}{3}},q^{\frac{2}{3}(m-m_{2})}\}\Big(q^{\alpha m_{2}}+q^{n}+q^{\frac{2}{3}(m_{2}+n)}\Big)

\displaystyle\ll q^{\varepsilon(m+n)}\Big(q^{\alpha m}+q^{\frac{m}{3}+n}\Big).

Combining this with (
12.6
), we have that

(12.8)

\mathcal{B}_{2}(m,n)\ll q^{\varepsilon(m+n)}(q^{\alpha m}+q^{\frac{m}{3}+n}).

Using Lemma
12.4
and equation (
12.8
), we get that

\mathcal{B}_{3}(m,n)\ll q^{\varepsilon(m+n)}\Big(q^{n(2\alpha-1)+m(1-\alpha)}+q^{\frac{2}{3}(m+n)}\Big).

Now we use the above and Lemma
12.3
and it follows that there exist
\Delta_{1}\leq\Delta_{2}
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{2}(m,n)

\displaystyle\ll q^{\varepsilon n}\mathcal{B}_{3}(m-\Delta_{1},n-\Delta_{2})\ll q^{\varepsilon(m+n)}\Big(q^{(n-\Delta_{2})(2\alpha-1)+(m-\Delta_{1})(1-\alpha)}+q^{\frac{2}{3}(m+n-\Delta_{1}-\Delta_{2})}\Big)

\displaystyle\ll q^{\varepsilon(m+n)}\Big(q^{m(1-\alpha)+n(2\alpha-1)}+q^{\frac{2}{3}(m+n)}\Big),

where in the last line we used the fact that
\Delta_{1}\leq\Delta_{2}
. Note that the above holds if
n\geq 1
. When
n=0
, then we have the bound

\mathcal{B}_{2}(m,0)\ll q^{m}.

Putting the two bounds above together, we get that

(12.9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{2}(m,n)\ll q^{\varepsilon(m+n)}\Big(q^{m}+q^{m(1-\alpha)+n(2\alpha-1)}+q^{\frac{2}{3}(m+n)}\Big).

Now using Lemma
12.1
and (
12.3
), we have that there exists some large enough absolute constant
C
such that for
m^{\prime}\geq m+\log_{q}(C(m+n))

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{1}(m,n)

\displaystyle\ll\mathcal{B}_{1}(m^{\prime},n)\leq\mathcal{B}_{2}(m^{\prime},n)

\displaystyle\ll q^{\varepsilon(m^{\prime}+n)}\Big(q^{m^{\prime}}+q^{m^{\prime}(1-\alpha)+n(2\alpha-1)}+q^{\frac{2}{3}(m^{\prime}+n)}\Big),

where in the last line we used equation (
12.9
). Now we pick

m^{\prime}=\Big[\max\{m,\frac{6\alpha-5}{3\alpha-1}n\}+\log_{q}(C(m+n))\Big]+1
. Note that if
\max\{m,\frac{6\alpha-5}{3\alpha-1}n\}=m
then the second term above is bounded by the third. If
\max\{m,\frac{6\alpha-5}{3\alpha-1}n\}=\frac{6\alpha-5}{3\alpha-1}n
, then

\mathcal{B}_{1}(m,n)\ll q^{\varepsilon(m+n)}q^{\frac{6\alpha-4}{3\alpha-1}n},

where we have used that
C(m+n)
is negligible compared to
q^{\varepsilon(m+n)}
.

Combining these, we get that

\mathcal{B}_{1}(m,n)\ll q^{\varepsilon(m+n)}\Big(q^{m}+q^{\frac{6\alpha-4}{3\alpha-1}n}+q^{\frac{2}{3}(m+n)}\Big).

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Now we are finally ready to prove Theorem
1.4
. Note that the condition in Lemma
12.6
is satisfied with
\alpha=2
by equation (
12.1
). Repeatedly using Lemma
12.6
, we deduce that the bound holds for the fixed point of
\alpha\mapsto 2-2/(3\alpha-1)
, which is
\alpha=4/3
, and then

\mathcal{B}_{1}(m,n)\ll q^{\varepsilon(m+n)}\Big(q^{m}+q^{\frac{4n}{3}}+q^{\frac{2}{3}(m+n)}\Big).

By duality,

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{1}(m,n)

\displaystyle\ll q^{\varepsilon(m+n)}\min\left\{q^{m}+q^{\frac{4n}{3}}+q^{\frac{2}{3}(m+n)},q^{n}+q^{\frac{4m}{3}}+q^{\frac{2}{3}(m+n)}\right\}

\displaystyle\ll q^{\varepsilon(m+n)}\Big(q^{m}+q^{n}+q^{\frac{2}{3}(m+n)}\Big),

which finishes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

We use Lemma
12.2
and Theorem
1.4
. If
m_{1}\geq m_{2}
, then we use Lemma
12.2
with
k=1
. We have that

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{2}(m,n)

\displaystyle\ll q^{\varepsilon(m+n)}q^{m_{2}+\cdots+m_{\ell}}\mathcal{B}_{1}(m_{1},n)

\displaystyle\ll q^{\varepsilon(m+n)}q^{m_{2}+\cdots+m_{\ell}}\Big(q^{m_{1}}+q^{n}+q^{\frac{2}{3}(m_{1}+n)}\Big).

We further use (
12.5
) in the equation above, which finishes the proof in this case.

If
m_{1}<m_{2}
, then we use Lemma
12.2
with
k=2
. We get that

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{2}(m,n)

\displaystyle\ll q^{\varepsilon(m+n)}q^{m_{1}+m_{3}+\cdots+m_{\ell}}\mathcal{B}_{1}(m_{2},n)

\displaystyle\ll q^{\varepsilon(m+n)}q^{m_{1}+m_{3}+\cdots+m_{\ell}}\Big(q^{m_{2}}+q^{n}+q^{\frac{2}{3}(m_{2}+n)}\Big).

Using the fact (
12.7
) again finishes the proof.
∎

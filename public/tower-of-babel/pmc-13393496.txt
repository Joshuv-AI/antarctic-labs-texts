ABSTRACT
The primary purpose of this study is to investigate the impact of using ChatGPT as a feedback mechanism on enhancing primary school students' poetry writing skills and attitudes towards poetry. In this study, a quasi-experimental design model with a pretest–posttest control group, a quantitative research method, was employed. In this study, conducted during the 2024–2025 academic year, 60 primary school fourth-grade students (30 experimental, 30 control) aged 10 years formed the study group for the research. During the six-week implementation period, students in the experimental group received ChatGPT-supported formative feedback on their poems and engaged in revision activities based on this feedback, while the control group participated in poetry writing activities without ChatGPT support. Data were analyzed using independent samples t-tests, paired samples t-tests, and analysis of covariance (ANCOVA). The results indicated a statistically significant difference in favor of the experimental group in posttest poetry writing skill scores when pretest scores were controlled (p < .001). In addition, students in the experimental group demonstrated significantly more positive attitudes toward poetry compared to the control group (p < .001). These findings suggest that ChatGPT-supported formative feedback may contribute to improvements in both poetry writing skills and attitudes toward poetry among primary school students.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40359-026-04844-4.

Introduction

Artificial intelligence (AI) refers to the development of computer systems capable of performing tasks that typically require human intelligence, such as learning, reasoning, and language use [11]. In recent years, AI-based tools, including chatbots, expert systems, and intelligent tutoring systems, have increasingly been integrated into educational contexts [10]. ChatGPT, a chatbot, is one of the artificial intelligence applications that has recently begun to be used in learning environments. According to Lock [53], ChatGPT is a cutting-edge language model-based AI technology product with a superior ability to produce human-like responses and provide detailed information. ChatGPT can perform significant tasks in many different fields, such as content creation and program development, and has begun to provide significant benefits, particularly in supporting professional writing and communication in education [33, 60]. Beyond its technical capabilities, ChatGPT has attracted attention in educational research because of its potential to provide immediate, consistent, and non-judgmental feedback features that are known to influence learners’ motivation, self-efficacy, and engagement in writing tasks. Continuing to develop daily, GPT, which can produce human-like texts by learning the structure and connections of human language, also serves as an important guide for students in creative writing [36]. Its capabilities, particularly those of grammar correction, text editing, and effective feedback, can significantly facilitate students in writing creative texts [29, 41]. Creative writing, defined as the process of creating fictional narratives and original writing using imagination and higher-order thinking skills [5], is one of the important skills that primary school students should acquire. Poetry is one of the creative writing genres that encompasses important concepts such as observation, intrinsic motivation, imagination, production, explanation, and perseverance. From a psychological perspective, poetry writing also involves affective engagement, self-expression, and the regulation of emotions, making it a particularly suitable genre for examining motivational and attitudinal processes in young learners. According to Erdoğan [18], poetry is the most suitable text type for students to express their memories, experiences, feelings, thoughts, and dreams. Therefore, it can be easily used in primary school students' written self-expression processes.

Creative writing and poetry writing are skills that are considered difficult to develop and are not sufficiently valued [51]. Although these skills are considered talents and challenging to develop, it is also known that they can be taught and developed [63]. At this point, teachers have important responsibilities. At the beginning of the writing process, which is described as a demanding and challenging process, it is necessary first to reduce students' prejudices towards writing and increase their motivation. To increase students' interest and desire in writing poetry, it is necessary first to develop their beliefs and attitudes that they can perform the task of writing, and then to create authentic writing environments and processes rather than the writing environments and processes prescribed by the curriculum [8]. Students' poetry writing skills should be supported by teachers through effective classroom management and writing practices in writing classes. Large classes in the process of writing and teaching poetry can make it difficult to provide individual guidance, allocate sufficient time, and provide immediate feedback to each student. In teaching poetry writing as prescribed by the curriculum, it is often complicated and time-consuming for teachers to provide individual feedback to a large number of students' works. This limits students' ability to receive individual correction suggestions [64]. Using artificial intelligence tools to overcome these challenges could be a solution. In particular, AI-supported feedback systems may function as external cognitive and motivational scaffolds, supporting students’ writing processes when individual teacher feedback is limited. Such systems may help learners maintain confidence, reduce writing anxiety, and engage more actively in revision. ChatGPT has the potential to partially address these challenges by providing formative feedback and structured suggestions related to poem structure, language use, and expression. This study examined the effectiveness of ChatGPT, used as a feedback mechanism in the classroom, in developing primary school students' poetry writing skills and increasing their attitudes toward poetry.

From a language learning perspective, recent studies on Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) suggest that AI-powered systems can provide several pedagogical advantages, such as immediate feedback, individualized guidance, and reduced performance anxiety [44, 62, 73]. Recent review studies have also highlighted the increasing educational potential of generative artificial intelligence tools in supporting student engagement, personalized feedback, writing development, and classroom interaction across different educational levels. At the same time, these reviews emphasize the importance of pedagogically guided and ethically responsible AI integration in educational settings [24, 25]. Unlike traditional classroom feedback, formative feedback generated by AI can be delivered instantly and repeatedly, contributing to students' development of writing skills. Furthermore, the non-judgmental and adaptive nature of AI tools can create a more psychologically secure learning environment that can increase students' self-efficacy and motivation to improve this skill [17]. These opportunities are crucial, as the development of writing skills largely depends on timely, structured, and constructive feedback. Therefore, integrating ChatGPT as a formative feedback mechanism in poetry writing activities can provide both cognitive benefits (e.g., improved structure, language use, and consistency) and emotional benefits (e.g., increased self-confidence and more positive attitudes toward writing). In this context, the hypotheses for the study were formulated as follows.H1 As a result of ChatGPT-supported formative feedback, primary school students' poetry writing skills improve.H2 Using ChatGPT in the poetry writing process increases primary school students' attitudes towards poetry.

Literature review

Writing poetry

Poetry is one of the most effective text types in developing many important skills, such as a child's awareness of language use and its richness, the development of basic language skills, the development of aesthetic sensitivity, the enrichment of their imagination, the development of different perspectives, and the development of a love and awareness of language [43]. Through poetry, children learn abstract words alongside concrete ones, use them, expand their vocabulary, and become familiar with the different meanings of words. In other words, through poetry, children become aware of the richness of their native language and enrich their own language skills. Writing poetry improves students' ability to express their feelings, increases their confidence in putting their thoughts into words, and fosters a curiosity about written works [61]. Writing poetry also supports 21st-century skills such as communication, innovative thinking, creative thinking, and critical thinking, as well as learning phonological-phonemic awareness, vocabulary, and word play [58].

The difficulty of poetry writing for students can also vary depending on the quality of their writing experiences, teachers' teaching approach, and the support students receive during the writing process [12, 40]. Therefore, it is important to provide students with appropriate classroom environments, positive guidance, and structural support, along with student-centered and creative practices, where they can use their imaginations during the poetry writing process [55, 57]. Involving students in the writing process with appropriate guidance and support will help them explore their own ideas before producing a written product [54]. Students should be provided with flexible choices and opportunities to reflect on their ideas in poetry, including topic selection, collaborative or individual writing processes, and classroom discussions [6]. According to Wyse and colleagues (71), when students are not provided flexibility and freedom in creative writing processes, their imagination and creativity can be hindered. Therefore, creative writing processes are crucial for student development [28]. Therefore, the use of digital writing and artificial intelligence tools is crucial for developing and supporting creative writing and poetry writing skills, and for the writing process to be enjoyable and effective [40].

Attitude towards poetry

Students' desire to write poetry and their positive attitude toward it are more important than the methods and tools used to achieve the goal of the poetry writing process [20]. If students lack a positive attitude toward poetry, it will be impossible to achieve the intended outcomes of the poetry writing process. Therefore, a positive interest in and attitude toward poetry is crucial. Thanks to the general sentiment of the poem, the harmony of the lines, and the interplay of the stanzas, students develop an interest in poetry from early primary school onward and derive pleasure from memorizing and reciting poems. Developing this already existing interest at a young age will increase students' attitudes toward poetry and their desire to write poetry [45]. Students should be given some freedom in the poetry writing process. Students should be able to write poems on any topic and genre they can imagine, and they should not be forced to ensure that the poems are metered and rhymed. Especially in the beginning, an excessive emphasis on rhyme and meter can hinder students' ability to write meaningful and effective poetry. Therefore, teachers have important responsibilities in this regard [1]. To ensure that students see that writing poetry is neither tedious nor difficult and that they develop positive attitudes toward poetry, teachers should create environments where students are centered and actively participate. It is important to create environments where students feel comfortable and safe, where they can have fun and not be bored, where they can use their imaginations, and where they can interact with others [2]. ChatGPT fosters participatory and adaptable language learning, allowing individuals to learn patterns, syntax, semantics, and context in a manner similar to human language acquisition. Therefore, it attracts students' attention and motivates them to write poetry, leading them to develop positive attitudes toward it [50]. Therefore, it is important for teachers to use artificial intelligence and digital tools in the process of creating classroom environments that will increase students' attitudes toward poetry writing.

Research suggests that students’ attitudes toward writing and poetry are strongly influenced by instructional context and feedback conditions. Student-centered learning environments that emphasize autonomy, structured guidance, and constructive formative feedback have been shown to foster more positive writing attitudes [8, 27]. In particular, timely and specific feedback can reduce writing-related anxiety and strengthen learners’ perceptions of competence, which are closely linked to affective engagement [7, 59]. Collaborative writing activities and supportive classroom climates further contribute to the development of positive writing attitudes by promoting peer interaction and reducing fear of negative evaluation [48]. Therefore, instructional approaches that combine structured guidance with psychologically safe feedback environments may be especially effective in cultivating favorable attitudes toward poetry writing among young learners.

ChatGPT

Today, ChatGPT is one of the most powerful artificial intelligence tools available to everyone [74]. Providing detailed and descriptive responses in many languages, ChatGPT is primarily used for socializing and communicating with others. Currently, it also serves important purposes in education and has the potential to create significant changes [56]. ChatGPT's ability to understand and respond to human language has made it a popular and indispensable tool for teachers, students, and researchers. In education, ChatGPT can provide detailed answers to questions, complete homework, answer exam questions, complete many tasks in a very short time, and provide practical and rapid feedback on written texts [21, 37, 73]. ChatGPT has proven to be an effective tool for increasing educational success by providing basic information on requested topics to its users. This capability allows ChatGPT to offer students quick access to detailed information and stimulating learning opportunities tailored to their interests [56]. Its ability to analyze student outcomes and provide educational materials tailored to students' learning needs makes ChatGPT particularly valuable for providing students with a personalized learning experience [19]. Furthermore, because ChatGPT works by analyzing and processing language patterns, it can use words in a natural and context-appropriate manner and generate effective sentences. Therefore, using ChatGPT when creating creative texts or poems can provide significant advantages and convenience. Because the ChatGPT algorithm has evolved to the point where it can understand and generate human language, similar to Natural Language Processing (NLP), it can automatically generate poems [31]. Within the context of poetry writing, the affordances of ChatGPT are particularly relevant to both cognitive and affective dimensions of learning. From a cognitive perspective, structured AI-generated formative feedback may support the development of poetry writing skills by guiding students in organizing ideas, refining language use, and enhancing emotional expression in alignment with writing process models [22]. From an affective perspective, receiving immediate, non-judgmental feedback may foster greater writing confidence and reduce anxiety, thereby contributing to more positive attitudes toward poetry writing [7]. Consequently, the integration of ChatGPT into poetry instruction is theoretically positioned to influence both performance outcomes and students’ attitudes toward writing.

Theoretical framework

The theoretical framework of this study is grounded in psychological and educational theories that explain how formative feedback and cognitive regulation processes influence students’ writing performance and attitudes toward writing. The study is based on the theoretical approaches of Flower and Hayes' [22] cognitive writing process theory and Brookhart's [7] feedback theory. Flower and Hayes [22] describe the cognitive writing process theory as a set of cognitive activities carried out in three stages: planning, producing, and revising (rewriting). In this theory, the writing process is not linear but consists of intertwined mental processes [70]. From a psychological perspective, these stages require writers to engage in metacognitive regulation, including monitoring their own text, evaluating the adequacy of expression, and making decisions about revision. External feedback can function as a metacognitive scaffold by directing students’ attention to specific aspects of their writing during the planning and revising stages. Feedback theory, another theory on which this study is theoretically based, argues that effective feedback given to students during the writing process enhances their writing skills and product effectiveness [7]. In this context, feedback is conceptualized as formative rather than evaluative. Formative feedback provides information that helps learners understand the gap between their current performance and desired goals, thereby supporting self-regulated learning and revision processes [7, 32]. ChatGPT-supported feedback in this study is positioned as a formative tool designed to guide students’ revision decisions rather than to judge or grade their work.

Beyond cognitive processes, writing development is closely related to learners’ motivational and affective beliefs. According to Bandura’s [4] self-efficacy theory, students who believe in their capability to perform a task are more likely to persist, revise their work, and engage deeply in learning activities. Formative feedback that is immediate, consistent, and non-judgmental may strengthen students’ writing self-efficacy and reduce anxiety associated with creative writing tasks. In turn, increased self-efficacy and intrinsic motivation can positively influence students’ attitudes toward poetry writing.

All these theoretical perspectives suggest that ChatGPT-assisted formative feedback can influence elementary school students' poetry writing skills and attitudes through interconnected cognitive, metacognitive, and motivational mechanisms. This framework provides a foundation for examining not only whether ChatGPT-assisted feedback is effective, but also how and why it might shape students' poetry writing performance and emotional responses.

Within this theoretical perspective, ChatGPT-supported formative feedback can be understood as an instructional tool that operationalizes key principles of both cognitive writing theory and feedback theory. By providing structured, criterion-based comments, ChatGPT may support the revision stage emphasized by Flower and Hayes [22], prompting students to reconsider content, organization, and linguistic choices. At the same time, the formative and non-judgmental nature of AI-generated feedback aligns with Brookhart’s [7] principles of effective feedback, which emphasize clarity, specificity, and opportunities for improvement. Thus, ChatGPT-supported feedback can be conceptualized as a mechanism that integrates cognitive revision processes with affective support, potentially influencing both writing performance and attitudes toward poetry. However, it is important to acknowledge that AI-generated feedback may be processed differently from human feedback. While machine-generated responses may reduce performance anxiety due to their non-judgmental nature, they may also lack the relational and socio-emotional cues typically embedded in teacher feedback. Therefore, although the feedback mechanism aligns with established theoretical principles, its psychological processing may differ from traditional human-mediated feedback.

Current study

Grounded in theories of cognitive writing processes, formative feedback, and motivational beliefs, the current study focuses on poetry writing as a core component of creative writing in primary education. Poetry writing requires students to engage simultaneously in cognitive regulation, emotional expression, and self-belief, making it a particularly suitable domain for examining the psychological effects of feedback-supported writing processes. A review of the literature on the use of ChatGPT in classroom learning processes reveals that there are studies on developing students' writing skills in text types such as fictional narratives [10, 15], stories [30, 73], and tales [46]. However, ChatGPT-supported studies on developing primary school students' poetry writing skills are quite limited. Moreover, existing studies have largely focused on general writing performance, while the psychological processes underlying feedback-supported creative writing, such as metacognitive regulation, self-efficacy, and affective engagement, remain underexplored, particularly in the context of poetry writing. While there are studies on improving primary school students' poetry writing skills [16, 40, 72], there are no studies on incorporating ChatGPT into the poetry teaching process. Therefore, the current study aims to examine the effects of ChatGPT-supported formative feedback on primary school students’ poetry writing skills and attitudes toward poetry, with particular attention to the cognitive and affective mechanisms underlying these outcomes.

When the relevant literature is evaluated in terms of attitudes towards poetry, it is seen that there are studies on writing attitudes in general, that students with a positive attitude towards writing can produce more creative texts [14], that there is a positive relationship between the perception and attitude of self-efficacy towards writing and written expression skills [59], and that positive feedback received during the writing process positively affects students' writing attitudes [48]. Kara [42] states in his research that he concluded that students who regularly participate in in-class creative writing processes develop a more positive attitude towards poetry and can create more effective products. In addition, it can be seen that there are studies in the literature related to students' attitudes towards poetry writing [65, 69], but there are no studies addressing its relationship with artificial intelligence. Taken together, the existing literature suggests that the use of artificial intelligence to support poetry writing skills and attitudes at the primary school level represents a relatively new and underexplored area of research. In this context, feedback-supported writing environments may influence students’ attitudes toward poetry by strengthening their self-efficacy beliefs and reducing affective barriers associated with creative writing.

Drawing on cognitive writing theory and feedback theory, it can be theoretically expected that structured, criterion-based formative feedback will enhance students’ revision processes and overall writing performance. Furthermore, consistent formative feedback may strengthen students’ writing-related self-efficacy and positive affect toward poetry. Based on these theoretical propositions, the present study examines whether ChatGPT-supported formative feedback is associated with improvements in poetry writing skills and attitudes toward poetry. In line with the theoretical framework emphasizing formative feedback, cognitive regulation, and motivational beliefs, the following research questions were addressed:Is there a significant difference between the mean pretest/posttest scores of the experimental group and the control group on poetry writing skills?Is there a significant difference between the mean pretest/posttest scores of the experimental group and the control group on attitudes toward poetry?

Method

Research design

This study employed a quasi-experimental design with a pretest–posttest control group, a quantitative research method. The purpose of using an experimental design in this study was to test whether the support and feedback received from ChatGPT-produced poems affected students' poems and their attitudes toward poetry. An experimental design allows for the examination of the effect of an independent variable on a dependent variable and the cause-and-effect relationship between two variables in a researcher-created environment while minimizing undesirable factors [9, 13]. In an experimental design with a pretest–posttest control group, the pretest and posttest are administered to both groups, but the independent variables or method applied to the experimental group are not applied to the control group [13]. A quasi-experimental design was employed because random assignment of individual students was not feasible in the school context, as intact classroom structures were maintained to minimize disruption to regular instructional practices. The purpose of choosing an experimental design in this study was to provide the opportunity to determine, with the control group, whether the use of ChatGPT in poetry writing activities affected students' poetry writing skills and attitudes. The study was conducted with 4th-grade primary school students because this age group is at a developmental level that can effectively utilize digital tools like ChatGPT and is at a developmental stage where written expression skills can be developed through poetry. Incorporating ChatGPT into the poetry writing process is believed to make a unique contribution to examining the impact of technological feedback on the development of written expression skills in primary school students.

Participants

The study group consisted of 60 fourth-grade students attending a school in Van, one of Turkey's eastern provinces. The experimental group consisted of 15 female and 15 male students, while the control group consisted of 17 female and 13 male students. Of the 60 students who participated in the research process, 32 were female and 28 were male. Convenience sampling, a non-random sampling method, was used to select the relevant school for the study. Convenience sampling is a widely used sampling technique, particularly in humanitarian research, due to its practical and cost-effective advantages [23].

The experimental and control groups were randomly selected from among the 10 fourth-grade classes in the school. The class names were written on slips of paper and placed in a bag. The first class drawn through random selection was coded Class 1 and assigned to the experimental group. The second class drawn from the bag was coded Class 2 and assigned to the control group. The teachers of both classes have 11 years of professional experience and are teaching fourth grade for the third time.

Data collection tools

Poetry writing attitude scale

The "Poetry Writing Attitude Scale," developed by Toraman and Topçuoğlu-Ünal [68], was used to determine students' attitudes toward poetry. The 26-item scale is a five-point Likert-type scale, with a maximum score of 5 and a minimum score of 1 for each item. Therefore, the highest possible score is 130, and the lowest is 26. Based on the data obtained after the scale was administered, the Cronbach's alpha reliability coefficient was found to be 0.934. Therefore, it can be said that the scale is reliable, sufficiently reliable, and appropriate for a 4th-grade level. Some of the items included in the scale are: "I like writing poetry," "I feel like a poet when I write poetry," "Writing poetry gives me peace when I am bored," "I do not realize how time passes when I write poetry," and "I express my feelings and thoughts better by writing poetry."

Poetry writing rubric

The "Poetry Writing Rubric Scale," developed by Toraman and Topçuoğlu-Ünal [68], was used to measure and evaluate students' poetry writing skills. The scale consists of 10 items and 50 criteria. Each item consists of 5 criteria and is scored from 1 to 10 based on performance levels. Therefore, the highest score that can be obtained from the scale is 100, and the lowest is 10. The inter-observer agreement method was used to determine the reliability of the scale. The intra-cluster correlation coefficient in the calculated data was 0.89. According to Şencan [66], since this ratio was greater than 0.75, it is possible to say that the agreement between the raters was excellent. Therefore, the validity, reliability, and usability studies conducted show that the rubric developed within the scope of the research is a valid, reliable, and usable measurement tool for 4th graders.

The scale evaluates student poems across four sub-dimensions: Originality, content and meaning, language and expression, and emotional expression. Originality refers to the degree to which students produce unique ideas and imaginative expressions. Content and meaning assess the clarity, coherence, and thematic integrity of the poem. Language and expression focus on appropriate word use, fluency, and stylistic quality. Emotional expression evaluates the extent to which the poem effectively conveys emotions and a central emotional tone. Although the evaluation scale includes sub-dimension indicators, it is designed to produce a composite overall score that represents the overall poetic performance. Therefore, statistical analyses in this study were performed using total scores and overall means.

Implementation process

The total implementation period for the current study lasted 8 weeks, including the pilot implementation, introduction and briefing, teacher training, pretest and posttest administration, and a 6-week experimental process.

Pilot ımplementation

Prior to the primary research, a pilot study was conducted with a different 4th-grade class from the same school to determine the effectiveness and time required for poetry writing exercises. The pilot study, conducted over three consecutive week-long periods, concluded that one lesson (35–40 min) would be sufficient for students to write their own poems. The length of time required to receive ChatGPT feedback on student poems varied depending on the number of poems, and each poem needed at least five minutes to be evaluated. Based on the feedback obtained, it was concluded that one lesson was also needed for students to rewrite their poems. If the poems were written in groups, three lesson periods (40 + 40 + 40 min) per week should be devoted to the practice.

Introduction, ınformation and teacher training

After meeting all the students in the experimental and control groups, designated as the sample, in the classroom, the students were informed about the research topic, purpose, and process. They were informed that participation in the research was voluntary and that those who did not wish to participate had the right to do so. All students stated that they would participate voluntarily.

The teachers of both the experimental and control groups were provided detailed information about the purpose of the research, what would be expected of them in the process, how long the research would last, what activities would be conducted each week, and when the scales would be administered. The teacher of Class 1, designated as the experimental group, received training on using ChatGPT before beginning the experimental process. The teacher training lasted two hours. The training took place in the teachers' room at the teacher's school on a day when the teacher had no class. Detailed information was provided to the teacher using sample applications, including the ChatGPT screen, the functions of the tabs on the screen, how ChatGPT is used, how to upload student-created poems to ChatGPT, the feedback process from ChatGPT, and what to do during the relevant weeks of the experimental process and during the poem-writing process.

Assessment of basic writing skills

Before determining the students' poetry writing skills and attitudes in the study, free-writing exercises were conducted to determine the general writing skills of all students in both groups before beginning the research process. The aim here was to determine the students' knowledge of basic writing rules, spelling, and punctuation, their ability to construct meaningful sentences, and appropriate vocabulary before engaging in creative writing/poetry, a higher-level skill. Students were asked to write a free-writing essay on any topic of their choosing and were given 30 min. The most commonly used topics in the literature for assessing basic writing skills, such as spelling and punctuation, sentence structure, semantic integrity, and appropriateness of words, were identified. The researcher evaluated all free-writing texts obtained from both groups based on these topics. As a result of this exercise, most students demonstrated sufficient basic writing skills to participate in the poetry writing process, while a small number of students showed limited proficiency. Based on the results, the data of five students from the experimental group and three students from the control group, whose basic writing skills were low, were excluded from the study after consulting their classroom teachers. Initially, 35 students in the experimental group and 33 students in the control group participated in the study. However, eight students (five experimental, three control) who did not demonstrate basic writing skills at baseline were excluded from the statistical analyses. After this exclusion, the final analytical sample consisted of 30 students in each group. Therefore, all statistical analyses reported in the Results section are based on a final sample of 60 students (30 experimental, 30 control). These students continued to participate in all instructional activities, but their pretest and posttest scores were not included in the statistical analyses.

Groups

During the six-week experimental process, students in the experimental group spent three class periods each week practicing poetry writing. Following the topic headings previously determined by the researcher after consulting with experts and submitted to the teacher, the students were presented with the topic for the week, and during one class period, the students wrote their poems on the topic. They wrote poems in groups for four weeks and individually for two weeks. Both group-based and individual writing activities were implemented in the experimental and control groups to maintain instructional balance and to minimize potential confounding effects related to collaboration. The resulting poems were photographed using the teacher's phone and uploaded to ChatGPT via the classroom interactive whiteboard. The same prompt was loaded before each poem was uploaded to ChatGPT. (See Appendix 4 for prompt details.) ChatGPT was then used to generate formative feedback on the poems. In this study, ChatGPT was not used as an assessment or grading tool, but as a formative feedback mechanism designed to support students’ reflection and revision processes. ChatGPT provided feedback on the poems under five different headings: content and meaning, language and expression, structure and form, spelling and punctuation, creativity and emotional depth. The feedback obtained from each poem was analyzed in detail by the class. During this stage, the teacher guided students to reflect on the feedback, identify areas for improvement, and consider how the suggestions could be applied during revision. In the final class, the students rewrote their poems based on the feedback they received. Overall, the intervention followed a structured instructional sequence consisting of initial poem writing, ChatGPT-supported formative feedback, and guided revision. The control group students were also given the week's poem topic and asked to write poems on the topic. Students in the control group read the poems they wrote in class and shared them with their peers. In the control group, poetry writing activities were conducted following the same weekly topics; however, feedback was limited to general verbal comments provided by the teacher to the whole class. These comments were not structured according to a rubric and did not involve individualized, written, or systematic formative feedback. In contrast to the experimental group, control students did not receive detailed, criterion-based feedback or opportunities for structured revision based on external guidance. The teacher’s comments in the control condition primarily focused on general encouragement and occasional clarification rather than systematic diagnostic feedback.

Data collection process

The data for the study were collected in the 2024–2025 academic year. The researcher explained how to complete the poetry writing attitude scale separately for each group by marking the board. The scales were administered with teacher guidance to ensure that students fully understood the items. The researcher then administered the "Poetry Writing Attitude Scale" to both groups. One class hour (40 min) was allotted to complete the scale. The data obtained from these measurements constituted the pretest scores of the experimental and control group students' attitudes toward poetry. In addition, a topic was presented to each group in the following class to measure the students' poetry writing skills. The students were asked to write a three-stanza poem related to the topic individually. No intervention was made regarding how to write it to reveal their prior knowledge and poetry writing skills. One class hour (40 min) was allotted for the poem writing. The resulting poems were then evaluated by the researcher using the "Poetry Writing Rating Scale." To enhance scoring reliability, the poems were rated using a standardized rubric, and consistency in scoring was ensured through repeated scoring procedures. The data obtained from these measurements constituted the pretest scores of the poetry writing skills of the experimental and control group students. This procedure allowed for the examination of changes in students’ poetry writing skills and attitudes over time, independent of instructional intervention during the pretest phase.

In the study, students in the experimental group were given three lesson hours (40 + 40 + 40 min) of poetry writing based on the topics and tasks determined using ChatGPT, one day per week for six weeks. Following the six-week implementation period, in the final week, all students were re-administered the "Poetry Writing Attitude Scale." Furthermore, each group was assigned a different topic and asked to write another three-stanza poem individually. The poems were then reevaluated by the researcher using the "Poetry Writing Rating Scale." The data obtained from these measurements constituted the posttest scores of the students in the experimental and control groups. Outcome assessment was conducted by a rater who was blind to group allocation.

Twelve topics, identified by the researcher through a literature review to be used in the pretest, posttest, and implementation phases, were presented to five field experts for their opinions: one in measurement and evaluation, one in Turkish education, and one in clasroom training. These experts scored the topics on a scale of 1 to 5 for relevance and applicability. The eight topics receiving the highest scores were used in the activities and during the pretest and posttest measurements. This procedure ensured that the selected topics were developmentally appropriate and capable of eliciting both cognitive and affective engagement in poetry writing. An overview of the poetry writing tasks implemented during the pretest, intervention, and posttest phases is provided in Appendix 5.

Within the scope of the topics in table at Appendix 5, students completed poetry writing activities for three class periods, one day a week, every week. Some of these poem-writing activities were conducted in groups, while others were conducted individually. The combination of group-based and individual writing activities was intentionally incorporated to balance collaborative learning opportunities with individual expression, while maintaining comparable instructional exposure across groups. In addition to the pre and posttest weeks, in weeks 4 and 5, students wrote poems related to the topic individually. Because uploading all of the student poems (30 poems) to ChatGPT and receiving feedback would require a considerable amount of time, five randomly selected poems were used in these two weeks. ChatGPT feedback for the five poems was evaluated and analyzed with the entire class. Due to time constraints associated with uploading and analyzing all individual poems, a subset of poems was selected during weeks 4 and 5. Due to time constraints associated with uploading and processing individual poems in ChatGPT, individualized AI-supported feedback was not provided to all students. Although this approach preserved exposure to structured feedback, it reduced the level of individualized formative interaction during those weeks. This design choice is acknowledged as a limitation and is discussed further in the Limitations section. In the remaining four weeks (weeks 2, 3, 6, and 7), the poems were written in groups. In weeks where poems were written in groups, each group's poem (usually five groups) was uploaded separately by the teacher to ChatGPT, and the feedback on the poems was evaluated and analyzed as a class.

Poetry writing studies

Throughout the implementation process, students were not only guided to write poems; in addition to the feedback ChatGPT provided to student-produced poems, the teacher also provided brief information each week on the basic elements of poetry writing. The teacher provided general information on topics such as developing creative titles, using imagination, using descriptive language, creating characters, feeling like the writer, maintaining a consistent theme between stanzas, and paying attention to meter and rhyme. Throughout the process, the development of the students' poetry writing skills was followed step by step, and the necessary support was provided to the experimental group teacher by the researcher every week throughout the process.

Data analysis

In the current study, data analysis was conducted using SPSS. The Shapiro–Wilk test was used to determine whether the scores were normally distributed, and the skewness and kurtosis values of the data were examined [9, 39]. According to Tabachnick and Fidell [67], tests with skewness and kurtosis values between + 1.5 and −1.5 indicate a normal distribution. Independent Samples T-Test and Paired Samples T-Test were used to determine whether there was a significant difference between the groups and between the pretest and posttest scores of the groups.

The eta-squared (η2) correlation coefficient, which is an effect size statistic, was used to determine the level of significant difference in the posttests. Eta-squared, also known as effect size, takes a value between 0.00 and 1.00. According to Huberty [35], an eta-squared value between 0.05 and 0.15 should be interpreted as a small effect, 0.15 to 0.30 as a medium effect, and 0.30 and above as a large effect size. Furthermore, ANCOVA (analysis of covariance) was used to observe the change between the posttest scores of poetry writing skills and poetry writing attitude when the pretest scores of the experimental and control group students were controlled. Meeting certain assumptions is crucial for ANCOVA to yield valid results. In this context, the fact that the dependent variables were continuous variables measured on an interval scale and that a linear relationship existed between the pretest and posttest ensured the validity and reliability of ANCOVA analyses. Assumptions were tested before the covariance analysis. Because the dependent variable was collected on a continuous scale, the measurement level was appropriate. A positive relationship was observed between the pretest and posttest scores, which were covariates. Furthermore, Levene’s test results indicated that the variances were homogeneous (p > 0.05), indicated that the variances were homogeneous (p > 0.05). Homogeneity of regression trends was ensured. Since the skewness and kurtosis values were also within the range of −1.5 to + 1.5, the assumption of normality was met mainly. Therefore, it was accepted that the necessary assumptions for ANCOVA were met and the analysis results were valid.

In the present study, a post-hoc power analysis was conducted using G*Power 3.1 software to evaluate whether the sample size was sufficient for the ANCOVA model employed. The analysis was performed using the “ANCOVA: Fixed effects, main effects and interactions” option within the F-test family. The significance level was set at α = 0.05, statistical power (1–β) = 0.80, and effect size f = 0.40 (large effect size). The model included two groups and one covariate. The results indicated that a minimum sample size of 47 participants was required to detect a significant effect, suggesting that the sample size used in the present study (N = 60) was adequate, with an achieved power exceeding 0.80.

Results and discussion

The current study evaluated the effectiveness of feedback obtained by evaluating student-produced poems on specific topics using ChatGPT in improving students' poetry writing skills and attitudes. No significant difference was found between the two groups' poetry writing skills and attitude scores before the interventions. In other words, the two groups included in the study were at similar levels in terms of poetry writing skills and attitudes. The research results are discussed and interpreted under two separate headings.

ChatGPT and writing poetry

The study revealed a significant improvement in the experimental group students' poetry writing skills. The increase of nearly 30 points in the experimental group between the two measurements indicates that ChatGPT-supported formative feedback may have positively contributed to the poetry writing skills of primary school students. However, the large effect size observed in the present study should be interpreted cautiously. In addition to the ChatGPT-supported feedback process, several contextual factors such as structured revision activities, teacher guidance during classroom discussions, peer collaboration, and the novelty effect associated with integrating an AI-based tool into poetry writing activities may also have contributed to the observed outcomes. Therefore, the findings suggest a strong association between ChatGPT-supported formative feedback and improvements in poetry writing skills, rather than definitive causal dominance. This result supports Flower and Hayes' [22] cognitive writing process theory, which describes the writing process as a cognitive activity. Although the statistical analyses were conducted using composite poetry scores, the observed improvement may be interpreted in relation to the rubric’s core dimensions, including originality, content and meaning, language and expression, and emotional expression. The structured and formative nature of the feedback may have supported students in generating more original ideas, organizing content more coherently, refining linguistic expression, and articulating emotional themes more clearly. These elements represent essential components of effective poetry writing and provide a conceptual lens through which the overall improvement can be understood. During the process, students planned their writing around a specific topic or theme, wrote poems in groups or individually, and reevaluated and revised their own poems based on their peers' contributions and the feedback they received from ChatGPT. This process indicates that all of the "planning," "writing," and "revising (rewriting)" stages described by Flower and Hayes [22] were effectively implemented in the current study. Dwivedi [17] and Li et al. [52] stated in their research that teaching poetry in traditional classroom settings can be challenging for teachers and that more time is needed to provide adequate and effective individual feedback to student poems. Therefore, their conclusion that using ChatGPT, which can provide technical feedback on poems in terms of language use, rhythm, and structure, and produce poems similar to human language, can significantly facilitate teachers in poetry teaching and improve students' poetry writing skills, supports the findings of the current study in this regard. Studies in the literature examining the effects of AI-supported applications on classroom writing activities also support the findings of the current study. For example, Zhai [73] concluded that using AI in creative writing activities increased students' focus on the writing process and increased their writing productivity. Kasneci et al. [44] concluded that the alternative text examples provided by ChatGPT during the use of ChatGPT in writing activities improved students' written expression skills. In his research examining the effect of ChatGPT on creative writing and writing self-efficacy of students at the same class and age level, Kızıltaş [47] concluded that ChatGPT is effective in improving creative writing and writing self-efficacy skills through its feedback feature.

From a metacognitive perspective, structured AI-supported feedback may have prompted students to engage in monitoring and regulation of their own writing processes. By receiving explicit comments on content, organization, language use, and emotional expression, students were encouraged to reflect on their initial drafts and make deliberate revisions. Such iterative reflection aligns with metacognitive regulation processes, including self-monitoring and strategic revision, which are central to effective writing development. Importantly, the feedback generated by ChatGPT was structured as formative rather than merely corrective. Instead of only identifying errors, the feedback provided criterion-based suggestions and encouraged improvement through revision. This distinction is consistent with Brookhart’s [7] framework, which emphasizes that effective feedback should guide learners toward improvement rather than simply signal deficiencies. In this sense, ChatGPT functioned as a formative scaffold supporting students’ development rather than as an evaluative grading mechanism. It is also important to consider that AI-generated feedback may not be psychologically processed in the same way as human teacher feedback. While AI systems can provide structured and immediate comments, students may perceive machine-generated feedback differently in terms of authority, emotional tone, or relational support. Some learners may experience reduced evaluation anxiety due to the non-human source of feedback, whereas others may rely more heavily on teacher guidance for interpretation. Therefore, although AI-supported feedback may function similarly to human formative feedback in structural terms, its psychological impact may differ. Future research should directly compare AI-mediated and teacher-mediated feedback to better understand these potential differences.

The control group students showed a smaller increase in their poetry writing skills compared to the experimental group. This increase can be explained by their regular weekly participation in poetry writing activities, the teacher's general explanation of the points to consider during the poetry writing process, and the fact that they gained a certain amount of experience through these poetry writing activities. However, because they were unable to view or receive effective ChatGPT feedback on their poems, this improvement was significantly lower than in the experimental group. This supports Brookhart's [7] feedback theory, which asserts that effective feedback during the writing process improves writing skills and writing products. Pajares and Valiante's [59] research also supports the findings of the current study, stating that feedback received during the writing process increases students' self-efficacy beliefs for writing, which in turn positively impacts their writing output. In this study, students in the experimental group also received feedback from ChatGPT on content and meaning, language and expression, structure and form, spelling and punctuation, creativity, and emotional depth, and restructured their own poems, sometimes in groups and sometimes individually. Based on these findings, it may be suggested that students engaged more actively in revision-related processes, which could be associated with the development of self-regulation and self-assessment skills. Furthermore, sharing the first versions of their poems with the class and being influenced by each other's poems during the evaluation process of other individuals' or groups' poems allowed for indirect learning. All these applications support Bandura's [3] social learning theory. It is believed that using ChatGPT in the classroom as a tool to increase students' attention span by removing them from the traditional learning environment makes the feedback provided more effective. Students' revision of their own poems, combined with the feedback they received from ChatGPT under teacher guidance, allowed them to both become aware of the rules of poetry writing and create original products with unique expressions.

In conclusion, the ChatGPT artificial intelligence tool is an effective feedback and support tool that can be used to develop the poetry writing skills of elementary school students.

ChatGPT and poetry writing attitude

The study found a significant increase in the experimental group students' attitudes toward poetry writing. The increase of nearly 13 points between both measurements in the experimental group indicates that the use of ChatGPT and ChatGPT-supported feedback during the poetry writing process positively influenced primary school students' attitudes toward poetry writing. The medium effect size observed for attitudes toward poetry writing suggests that affective outcomes may be more resistant to change than cognitive skills within a relatively short intervention period. Improvements in students’ attitudes may have been influenced by increased opportunities for feedback, classroom discussion, and engagement with writing tasks, rather than solely by the use of ChatGPT-supported feedback. Zhai's [73] study found that the alternative learning environment offered by ChatGPT increased students' motivation for writing, which is consistent with the results of the current study. Similarly, Kasneci et al.'s [44] study found that using AI-based applications in writing activities enabled students to participate more actively in the writing process and positively impacted their affective skills, supporting the findings of the current study.

The results of the current study indicate that the ChatGPT-supported poetry writing process impacts not only students' cognitive but also their affective aspects. The development of students’ attitudes toward poetry writing may have been influenced by improvements in their writing performance within the ChatGPT-supported feedback process, their motivation to produce higher-quality poems, and their recognition of personal progress over time. Indeed, in their study examining the relationship between writing attitudes and writing skills among first- and third-grade primary school students, Graham et al. [26] concluded that students' positive attitudes during the writing process directly influenced their willingness to write and their written expression skills. Kangasharju et al., [40], in their research with primary school students, stated that using artificial intelligence-supported tools and digital writing tools in the writing process supported students' creative writing and poetry skills and made the writing process enjoyable for them. Therefore, all these research results support the results of the current study and the ideas expressed based on these results. As students in the experimental group gained experience during the poetry writing process, they developed a stronger sense of confidence in their ability to write poetry, which was associated with more positive attitudes toward poetry writing. These more positive attitudes appear to have been accompanied by improvements in students’ writing performance. Indeed, according to Pajares and Valiante [59], a positive attitude toward the writing process is a key variable supporting writing performance. According to Johnson and Johnson [38], when individuals work as a group and create collaborative products during the learning process, they develop both cognitively and socially. The study suggests that students writing their poems as a group, supporting each other's learning toward a common goal, exchanging ideas, conducting in-group evaluations based on feedback, and rewriting the poem together all contribute to the development of students' sense of belonging, their self-confidence in self-expression, and their attitudes toward poetry writing.

The posttest results revealed no significant change in the control group students' attitudes toward poetry writing. Despite the students participating in weekly poetry writing activities and sharing their work with their classmates, along with general information provided by the teacher, there was no significant change in their attitudes toward poetry writing. The primary reasons for this result include the lack of effective feedback on how to write better poems after the poem-writing process and the lack of support for this process with an artificial intelligence tool like ChatGPT, which offers effective products. This suggests that feedback regarding the writing process plays a decisive role not only in cognitive but also in affective orientations [34]. Studies focusing on ChatGPT suggest that its value in educational contexts lies in its capacity to offer immediate, non-judgmental, and process-oriented feedback, particularly in writing-related tasks [49]. The findings of the present study align with these emerging trends by demonstrating how ChatGPT-supported formative feedback can be meaningfully integrated into primary school poetry writing to support both cognitive and affective learning outcomes.

Consequently, the research results indicate that ChatGPT-supported feedback during the poetry-writing process, considering both cognitive and affective components, is efficacious in improving students' attitudes toward poetry writing.

Suggestions

Teachers can use the AI-based ChatGPT as a feedback tool in classroom writing processes. Using ChatGPT in this process can provide effective feedback on students' texts. Consequently, students can achieve significant gains in their writing and rewriting processes. An aesthetic skill like poetry, when supported by AI-supported applications, can enrich the emotional and intellectual worlds of primary school students and increase their attitudes and self-confidence toward writing. In writing activities considered challenging, such as poetry writing, opportunities for group writing can be provided in addition to individual writing. Collaboration, support from groupmates, and the inclusion of diverse ideas can positively impact students' motivations and attitudes toward writing. Poetry writing activities should be designed to be process-oriented, offering opportunities for sharing and emotional interaction, rather than solely product-focused. Researchers can use a similar approach to conduct comparative studies with different age groups, different writing genres, or different AI applications. Future research may adopt mixed-method designs that combine experimental approaches with qualitative data (e.g., interviews, think-aloud protocols, or reflective journals) to better understand how students cognitively and emotionally interpret AI-generated feedback.

The use of AI-supported feedback with primary school students raises important ethical considerations that warrant careful attention. In the present study, teacher guidance played a central role in mediating students’ interaction with ChatGPT feedback, helping students critically reflect on suggestions rather than accepting them uncritically. Future applications of AI in classroom writing contexts should emphasize teacher oversight to prevent over-reliance on AI-generated feedback and to support students’ developing metacognitive and evaluative skills. In addition, future research should explore the long-term effects of AI-supported formative feedback on students’ writing skills and attitudes through longitudinal designs. Comparative studies examining AI-generated feedback alongside teacher-generated feedback would also contribute to a deeper understanding of the unique affordances and limitations of generative AI in primary education contexts. Future research should directly compare AI-generated feedback with teacher-generated feedback to better understand their differential psychological impact on students’ writing development and attitudes. Experimental designs including conditions such as AI-only feedback, teacher-only feedback, and AI-supported teacher feedback would allow researchers to isolate the unique and combined contributions of human and AI-mediated formative feedback processes.

Limitations

The study sample consisted of a single school and a specific age group. Furthermore, the study used a convenience sampling method. Therefore, this limits the generalizability of the study results to a broader student population. Studies conducted in schools with different sociocultural backgrounds and with students of different age groups may yield different results using similar practices. The study was limited to an eight-week period. While positive effects were observed during this limited period, the findings primarily reflect short-term changes in students’ poetry writing skills and attitudes toward poetry. It remains unclear whether these improvements would be sustained over time, particularly for affective outcomes such as attitudes, which may require longer and repeated exposure to feedback processes. During the fourth and fifth weeks of the research, when individual poems were written, feedback on each student’s poem was not provided separately. Due to time constraints inherent in classroom-based instruction, the ChatGPT-supported feedback obtained for five randomly selected poems was analyzed as a class under the guidance of the teacher. Although this approach supported collective reflection and discussion, it may have reduced the intensity of individualized formative feedback for some students. This procedural variation represents a partial reduction in individualized exposure to AI-supported formative feedback and may have introduced variability in the intensity of the intervention across weeks. While students continued to engage with structured feedback through whole-class analysis, the absence of consistent individualized feedback during these weeks should be considered when interpreting the magnitude of the observed effects. Students’ attitudes toward poetry writing were obtained through self-report attitude scales. As with all self-report measures, responses may have been influenced by factors such as individual awareness, social desirability, or the classroom social environment. To ensure a thorough understanding of the scale items, the scales were administered under teacher guidance. However, despite this support, the length and complexity of the instruments used may have influenced students' responses. In addition, a small number of students were excluded from the sample due to insufficient basic writing skills identified prior to the intervention, which may have affected the representativeness of the sample and should be considered when interpreting the findings. Finally, the use of AI-based tools with children involves additional considerations. In the present study, ChatGPT was not used as an autonomous evaluator but as a supportive feedback tool embedded within a teacher-guided instructional process. Feedback was discussed critically with students rather than being accepted uncritically. Moreover, the feedback generated by ChatGPT may vary depending on the version of the system used; therefore, ChatGPT should not be considered a standardized or fixed feedback tool.

Although poetry performance was assessed across multiple indicators, sub-dimension analyses were not conducted separately in this study. Future research may benefit from examining each indicator independently to provide a more fine-grained understanding of how AI-supported formative feedback influences specific dimensions of poetry writing, such as originality, language use, or emotional expression. In addition, the study did not include qualitative data (e.g., student interviews or reflective reports) regarding how learners perceived and interpreted the AI-supported feedback process. While quantitative findings demonstrated significant improvements in writing skills and attitudes, the cognitive and affective mechanisms underlying these changes were inferred theoretically rather than directly examined. Future mixed-method designs integrating qualitative inquiry could provide deeper insight into how students psychologically experience and process AI-generated formative feedback. Although the intervention was conducted with fourth-grade students (approximately 10 years old), the study did not directly assess whether students critically evaluated or independently regulated their use of AI-generated feedback. At this developmental stage, students may require structured guidance to interpret feedback effectively. Therefore, the positive outcomes observed may partly reflect the teacher-guided classroom discussions that accompanied the AI feedback rather than students’ fully autonomous processing of the feedback. The study employed the GPT-3.5 version of ChatGPT, which was the publicly available model at the time of implementation. Given the rapid evolution of generative AI systems, newer versions may provide feedback that differs in depth, structure, linguistic refinement, or pedagogical sophistication. Therefore, the observed effects may be partially influenced by the specific capabilities of GPT-3.5. Replication studies using updated AI models are recommended to examine the stability and generalizability of AI-supported formative feedback across different technological iterations.

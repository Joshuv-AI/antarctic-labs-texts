Prioritizing psychological factors in young consumers’ coffee brand preferences through a novel behavioral leadership fuzzy decision-making modelling
Authors: Serkan Eti, Edanur Ergün, Merve Acar, Serhat Yüksel, Hasan Dinçer
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe lack of systematic prioritization of the psychological factors that influence young consumers’ coffee brand preferences has emerged as a significant problem. While existing studies focus on economic and technical factors, the inadequate consideration of psychological factors leads brands to misunderstand consumer expectations. In this context, the fundamental problem lies in the failure to identify the relative importance of the psychological factors shaping young consumers’ brand preferences. The purpose of this study is to identify the most critical psychological factors influencing young consumers’ coffee brand preferences, rank the importance of these factors, and guide brands in strategic direction by correlating the findings with investment strategies. The study proposes a new fuzzy multi-criteria decision-making model. Expert weights are calculated using a Euclidean distances-based expert weighting approach, criteria importance levels are determined using the extended SIWEC, and the ARLON is used to rank strategy alternatives. Furthermore, behavioral leadership fuzzy sets, a new addition to the literature, are integrated into the model to reflect the behavioral dimensions of the decision-making process. This study contributes to the literature by proposing a new fuzzy decision-making model that prioritizes the importance of psychological factors in young consumers’ coffee brand preferences and relates them to investment strategies. The key findings indicate that “identity and belonging” and “experiential satisfaction” are the most critical psychological criteria. Additionally, “experience and atmosphere” and “digitalization and innovation” strategies are the most significant alternatives.Keywords: Psychological factors, Consumer psychology, Emotional satisfaction, Identity and belonging, Behavioral economics perspectiveSubject terms: Business and management, Business and management, Psychology, Psychology

Introduction

Several important determinants influence young consumers’ brand preferences. Indeed, if we examine the influence of psychological factors on consumers’ coffee preferences, several strategies can be considered. Price-focused strategies, one of these strategies, refer to the tendency of price-sensitive consumers to prioritize a product’s cost over other factors such as brand prestige or product quality. In other words, the degree to which consumers react to price changes can be considered within the scope of price sensitivity. Price-sensitive consumers particularly seek to take advantage of deals, promotions, and discounts, developing their strategies accordingly1. Promotion-focused consumers are observed to prioritize quality in their choices, while affordability can also increase brand loyalty2. Quality and taste-focused strategies are another determining factor in the role of psychological factors in young consumers’ brand preferences. In this strategy, the quality of the coffee beans or special recipes are particularly prominent in consumers’ coffee preferences. The product’s ability to provide a satisfying taste experience also influences consumers’ increased purchases of products with taste-focused cues (Liv d., 2024). This is because a quality and taste-focused strategy prioritizes flavor while placing significant emphasis on positioning it to encourage consumer appreciation3. A health and sustainability strategy is another factor influencing consumer purchase intentions. In this context, nutritional labels are important because they provide more comprehensive product information, allowing consumers to compare products4. While organic or plant-based milk options can be considered within this strategy, plant-based milks are gaining popularity as a lower-calorie and more environmentally sustainable alternative5.

Experience and atmosphere are another effective strategy in consumers’ coffee preferences, with different store environments, in-store music, and comfortable seating areas being the most notable sub-topics. Experiential consumption is crucial for prioritizing consumer preferences. In other words, it can be said that it represents a blend of personal memories and an individual’s subjective perspective6. Furthermore, atmosphere, brand experience, and product evaluation play a fundamental role in sensory brand experience7. Today’s evolving technology plays a significant role in digitalization and innovation strategies. The proliferation of mobile applications and conveniences such as online ordering significantly influence consumers’ product preferences. Indeed, considering that customers use online websites or mobile devices to search for product information and compare products and prices based on their current situation, we can say that they can easily access different channels8. Furthermore, digital transformation and advances in information technologies are contributing to businesses fulfilling their social responsibilities more cost-effectively and effectively9. Prestige and image strategies, on the other hand, can be defined as behavioral aspects expressed in perceptual areas such as loyalty and consumer awareness or image. In other words, positioning the brand as a lifestyle is prominent in this strategy, with both the consumer’s own consumption experiences and advertising appearing as influential factors in this regard10. At the same time, with the development of digitalization and innovation strategies, brand prestige is observed to be positively affected in prestige and image strategies. This is because utilizing digital platforms in the development of brand prestige provides significant time savings and creates an image on social media11. Finally, localization and cultural adaptation strategies can also be said to be important in consumer preferences. This is because consumers tend to gravitate towards brands that reflect their values. Therefore, measuring global and national identities can allow brands to better understand consumers from different generations and develop global marketing strategies12. In doing so, it is important for products to have appropriate designs, and consumers’ perceptions of a brand’s locality influence consumer attitudes and behaviors while creating brand equity in different ways13.

Identifying the psychological factors that shape young consumers’ coffee brand preferences is crucial for brands to gain a competitive advantage and develop appropriate consumer-oriented strategies. As frequently emphasized in consumer behavior literature, individuals’ preferences are shaped not only by the product’s functional benefits but also by perceptual, emotional, and cognitive processes. In this context, psychological factors such as brand image, identity affiliation, status representation, emotional satisfaction, habit, social influence, and hedonic motivation can profoundly influence young consumers’ coffee brand preferences. However, the lack of systematic examination of the relative importance of these factors in the literature creates a significant research gap. The limited number of studies creates uncertainty about which factors businesses should prioritize in developing their strategies, leading to both misallocation of resources and failure to meet consumer expectations. For example, some brands focus solely on price or quality while ignoring the deeper psychological needs of young consumers, such as identity affiliation or social interaction. This can lead to weakened brand loyalty, the failure to build a loyal customer base, and the inability to achieve a sustainable competitive advantage in the market. Furthermore, the lack of priority analyses has created a conceptual disarray in the academic literature, making it difficult to clearly distinguish which factors play a more critical role in consumer behavior. In this context, new studies to determine the order of importance of psychological factors have become essential not only for theoretical contributions but also to enable practitioners to make more rational, effective, and consumer-centric marketing decisions. Therefore, this research gap in the literature necessitates the development of new priority analyses to both advance academic knowledge and enable businesses to identify the right strategic orientations for the young consumer segment.

The purpose of this study is to identify the most influential psychological factors in young consumers’ coffee brand preferences and to shed light on strategic orientations by correlating these factors with investment strategies. The motivation for this study stems from the limited number of comprehensive and prioritized analyses in the literature on the psychological factors influencing young consumers’ coffee brand preferences, as well as the lack of systematic approaches based on decision-making models. Based on this deficiency, a comprehensive literature review identifies 10 different psychological indicator criteria and defines seven different investment strategy alternatives for coffee chains. A new fuzzy multi-criteria decision-making model is proposed to determine the relative importance of these criteria and alternatives. Within the scope of the research, opinions are obtained from seven academics and industry representatives who are experts in the field. Expert weights are determined using the Euclidean distances-based expert weighting approach. Criteria importance levels are calculated using the extended SIWEC method, and the ARLON approach is integrated to rank the alternatives. Furthermore, to encompass the behavioral dimensions of the decision-making process, newly developed behavioral leadership fuzzy sets are incorporated into the model. The study seeks to answer the following research questions: (1) What are the most critical psychological factors influencing young consumers’ coffee brand preferences? (2) How are the relative importance levels of these factors ranked? (3) Among the investment strategies identified for coffee chains, which alternatives are prioritized in line with psychological factors? (4) How does the integration of behavioral leadership fuzzy sets contribute to the reflection of psychological factors in decision-making models?

This study contributes to the literature by proposing a new fuzzy decision-making model that prioritizes psychological factors influencing young consumers’ coffee brand preferences and relates them to investment strategies. The proposed model offers several significant advantages over existing decision-making approaches in the literature. (1) The newly developed behavioral leadership fuzzy sets integrated into the model provide a unique contribution to the decision-making process. These sets produce more realistic results by considering individuals’ behavioral tendencies and leadership-based decision-making styles. They also offer the advantages of being more flexible, easier to calculate, and able to represent uncertainties more accurately compared to other fuzzy sets such as Pythagoras, Gaussian, and Type-2. (2) The fact that the criteria used in the model are derived from psychological factors distinguishes it from most studies in the literature. While most existing studies focus on economic and technical factors, considering psychological factors is crucial for a deeper understanding of customer expectations. Therefore, identifying psychological factors as criteria is a significant innovation and advantage that supports customer-centric approaches. (3) Third, the ARLON technique used to rank strategy alternatives also increases the specificity of the model. Compared to common techniques such as TOPSIS and MOORA, ARLON considers the relationships between criteria more robustly and offers more precise ranking capabilities. It allows for more reliable results, particularly in multidimensional decision-making problems. In this context, the ARLON technique can be considered the most appropriate ranking method for a psychological factor-based decision-making process regarding young consumers’ coffee brand preferences.

Literature review

The psychological constructs considered in this study can be interpreted within the framework of established consumer behavior theories. Brand Attachment Theory suggests that consumers develop emotional and psychological bonds with brands that reflect their identities, social affiliations, and symbolic meanings. Within this perspective, constructs such as identity and belonging, brand trust, and status and prestige represent different manifestations of consumers’ psychological attachment to brands. These factors explain how brands function as symbolic resources through which individuals communicate their self identity and social positioning. In addition, Experiential Marketing Theory emphasizes that consumer preferences are strongly influenced by the experiential value created through sensory, emotional, and relational interactions with brands. Accordingly, experiential satisfaction reflects the emotional outcomes generated through service atmosphere, consumption experiences, and brand related interactions. By grounding these constructs in Brand Attachment Theory and Experiential Marketing Theory, the psychological criteria used in this study capture both relational and experiential dimensions of consumer behavior that influence young consumers’ coffee brand preferences.

Literature on the indicators

One of the psychological factors influencing young consumers’ brand preferences is perceived quality. Elements such as the quality of coffee used in coffee chains and the barista’s skill, as well as brand communication and customer experience, are important factors in shaping perception. Young consumers are sensitive to tangible factors such as satisfying experiences. This helps them evaluate perceived quality as a psychological superiority tool in the eyes of consumers14. For example, Caferra et al.15 pointed out in their study that customer communication is important in increasing perceived quality. The associations formed in the consumer’s mind regarding the brand are another factor influencing young consumers’ brand preferences. For young consumers, brand image is often perceived as the brand’s identity and increases trust in the brand16. Monfort et al.17 stated that consumers who increasingly value brand image have a long-term connection with the brand. Similarly, another important factor influencing young consumers’ brand preferences can be called advertising strategies. Li et al.18 emphasized that advertising has a positive effect on consumers’ product purchases.

Young consumers’ feelings of love, trust, loyalty, and habit toward a brand stem from the emotional bonds individuals form with the brand. This attachment is more enduring than rational evaluations19. Coyne et al.20 stated that wines from a specific region in the USA cause feelings of attachment in consumers. Another important psychological factor in individuals’ brand preferences is social influence. Lah et al.21 emphasized that ESG practices not only influence consumers’ decisions but also increase customer loyalty. In addition to social influence, status and prestige are also important psychological factors that influence young consumers’ brand preferences. Status and prestige do not limit the brand’s consumption area to just the product; they also make it more preferred among customers because they offer social visibility (Amatulli et al., 2025). Similarly, Simonetti et al.22 reported that status and prestige are important factors influencing young customers’ brand preferences. In other words, it was emphasized that young customers’ brand preferences are an important status symbol for these consumers.

Identity and belonging are considered one of the psychological factors influencing young consumers’ brand preferences. In brand selection, identity and belonging are strong indicators of which group young consumers belong to, as well as their product consumption (Cesar et al., 2025). Hassan et al.23 emphasized that young consumers use brands as a means of self-expression when making brand choices. Similarly, Aslan et al.24 highlighted that universities increase institutional loyalty by promoting a sense of identity and satisfaction. Experiential satisfaction is one of the modern psychological factors that shape young consumers’ brand preferences. Going beyond product quality, the experience, feeling, and social shareability offered by the brand are effective in young consumers’ brand preferences. Gilal et al.,19 stated that concept restaurants offer individuals a unique experience and that this experience also influences consumer preferences. Additionally, Castillo-Manzano et al.25 focused on the impact of differentiated culinary experiences on customer experiences on cruise ships in their study.

How innovative consumers perceive a brand is one of the key criteria shaping their brand preferences. This perception of innovation is an important factor that makes the brand appear attractive, modern, and distinctive in the eyes of young consumers26. Durco27 stated that brands that develop new products increase their competitive power in the market. They emphasized that new product development strengthens the bond between the company and consumers. In another study, Mubarek et al., (2025) underscored that product variety is an important factor in organizational growth and long-term success. It was also pointed out that companies that take care to innovate in their products integrate into the market more quickly. In addition to the perception of innovation, brand trust is also one of the important psychological criteria in young consumers’ brand selection. Trust not only facilitates purchasing decisions but also increases loyalty and long-term commitment28. Indeed, Shahzad et al.29 argued that chatbot service quality increases the trust of brand users. The study found that increased brand accessibility increases demand for the brand.

Theoretical background of psychological factors in brand preferences

The psychological criteria considered in this study are grounded in several established theories within consumer psychology and marketing literature. Consumer preference formation is widely explained through symbolic consumption theory, social identity theory, and experiential consumption theory. Symbolic consumption theory suggests that consumers use brands as symbolic resources to communicate identity, status, and lifestyle meanings. Within this perspective, constructs such as brand image, status and prestige, and identity and belonging reflect the symbolic value consumers derive from brands. Social identity theory explains how individuals develop a sense of belonging through group affiliation and social signaling, which is particularly relevant for young consumers who often use brands to express membership in social communities. Experiential consumption theory emphasizes that consumption experiences generate emotional and sensory satisfaction beyond functional product attributes. Accordingly, experiential satisfaction and perceived quality capture the experiential dimension of brand interactions. Additionally, trust based and relationship-oriented perspectives in consumer behavior literature explain the roles of brand trust, emotional loyalty, and communication influence in shaping long term consumer preferences. By integrating these theoretical perspectives, the selected psychological criteria represent different dimensions of consumer psychology that collectively influence young consumers’ coffee brand preferences.

The psychological factors examined in this study can be conceptually organized into three complementary dimensions of consumer psychology. The first dimension is the cognitive dimension, which relates to consumers’ perceptions and evaluations of brand attributes. Factors such as perceived quality, perceived innovativeness, and brand trust represent cognitive assessments that influence how consumers evaluate the reliability and functional value of a brand. The second dimension is the affective dimension, which reflects the emotional responses generated through consumer–brand interactions. Experiential satisfaction and emotional loyalty capture the emotional attachment and feelings that arise from consumption experiences. The third dimension is the social–symbolic dimension, which explains how brands function as symbols of identity, status, and social belonging. Constructs such as brand image, identity and belonging, social influence, and status and prestige represent the symbolic meanings associated with brand consumption. Within the brand preference formation process, these three dimensions operate interactively. Cognitive evaluations shape consumers’ perceptions of brand credibility, affective responses strengthen emotional attachment, and social–symbolic meanings allow consumers to express identity and social positioning through brand choice. This multidimensional structure provides a more comprehensive understanding of the psychological mechanisms influencing young consumers’ coffee brand preferences.

Research gap in the literature

The relevant literature was reviewed within the constraints of the study, and some findings were obtained. Young consumers make their choices based not only on the physical quality of the product but also on several psychological factors that influence their preferences. Perceived quality, the brand’s image in the market, the experiences it offers its customers, and a sense of identity and belonging are some of the psychological factors that customers consider when choosing a brand. This study aims to determine the most effective strategy for psychological factors influencing young consumers’ brand choices. For this purpose, a priority analysis will be conducted among the selected criteria. A literature review revealed a limited number of studies prioritizing these criteria.

The selection of psychological criteria in this study is grounded in an extensive review of consumer behavior and branding literature focusing on young consumers’ purchasing motivations. The initial pool of psychological constructs was generated by examining recent studies on brand preference, experiential consumption, symbolic consumption, and consumer–brand relationships. From this broader conceptual pool, ten criteria were identified based on their frequency in the literature and their relevance to the context of coffee consumption among young consumers. Although some constructs such as brand image, identity and belonging, and status and prestige may appear conceptually related, prior research distinguishes these dimensions theoretically. Brand image refers to the overall set of perceptions and associations held in consumers’ minds about a brand, while identity and belonging relate to the role of brands in expressing personal identity and facilitating group affiliation. Status and prestige, on the other hand, emphasize the symbolic and social signaling value derived from brand consumption. Therefore, despite their conceptual proximity, these constructs capture different psychological mechanisms influencing consumer preferences. Treating them as separate criteria allows the proposed decision making model to more accurately represent the multidimensional nature of psychological influences in young consumers’ coffee brand preferences.

Methodology

This section recalls the details of BLFSs and recommended method. Recommended method includes Euclidean distances-based experts’ weighting, SIWEC and ARLON. Thus, the criteria are weighted, and the alternatives are ranked by minimizing the ambiguity of linguistic expressions and considering the diversity of experts. In this study, expert evaluations are used as a proxy to represent young consumers’ coffee brand preferences. This approach is commonly employed in multi criteria decision making studies when the objective is to prioritize behavioral or strategic factors rather than directly measure consumer attitudes through survey data. The experts selected for this research consist of academics and industry professionals who have extensive experience in marketing, consumer behavior, and brand management in the coffee and food service sectors. Their professional knowledge enables them to evaluate the psychological mechanisms that shape young consumers’ brand preferences from both theoretical and practical perspectives. Previous decision-making studies in marketing and strategic management similarly rely on expert-based evaluations to identify priority criteria and strategic directions when direct consumer level data are not required for model estimation. Therefore, the use of expert judgments in this study does not aim to replace consumer surveys but to provide a structured analytical framework for prioritizing psychological factors and strategic alternatives based on informed professional assessments.

This study relies on expert judgments as an analytical proxy to approximate the behavioral tendencies of young consumers. Experts involved in the evaluation process possess extensive academic and professional experience in consumer behavior, brand management, and marketing strategy, which enables them to interpret and synthesize patterns observed in real market environments. Through their accumulated knowledge, experts can provide informed assessments regarding how psychological factors influence consumer preferences. However, it should be acknowledged that expert-based evaluations do not directly capture individual-level behavioral variability or contextual consumption dynamics. Unlike survey-based or experimental studies, this approach does not reflect heterogeneity across different consumer groups or real-time decision-making processes. Therefore, the findings should be interpreted as structured and theory-informed approximations rather than direct empirical observations of consumer behavior. Future studies may complement this framework by integrating consumer-level data to validate and extend the proposed model.

The methodological framework of this study was designed to address the uncertainty and behavioral complexity associated with psychological factors influencing consumer preferences. Behavioral Leadership Fuzzy Sets were selected because they allow the modeling of decision environments in which behavioral tendencies and leadership style-based evaluations influence expert judgments. Psychological constructs such as identity, trust, or experiential satisfaction inherently contain subjective and behavioral dimensions that cannot be fully captured by deterministic or traditional fuzzy representations. BLFS structures enable the representation of these behavioral aspects through multiple membership components, allowing the model to better reflect the ambiguity and behavioral variability embedded in expert evaluations. In addition, the Euclidean distance-based approach was employed to determine expert weights because it provides an objective mechanism to measure the relative differences among experts based on observable professional indicators such as experience, managerial exposure, and academic background. This approach avoids purely subjective weighting schemes and allows the influence of expert knowledge to be incorporated in a transparent and measurable way. Finally, the ARLON method was adopted for ranking alternatives because it integrates two logarithmic normalization procedures and Heron mean aggregation, which improve stability in multi dimensional evaluation environments. Compared with classical ranking methods such as TOPSIS or VIKOR, ARLON reduces sensitivity to extreme values and provides more consistent rankings when multiple psychological criteria interact simultaneously. These characteristics make ARLON particularly suitable for evaluating strategic alternatives under behavioral and psychological decision conditions.

Euclidean distances-based experts’ weighting

One of the primary criticisms of expert systems is that they attribute equal importance to expert opinions. Various approaches exist in literature for this purpose. One of them is through machine learning31,32. The selection of professional indicators used in the Euclidean distance–based expert weighting process is grounded in the theoretical understanding of expertise in decision-making literature. In multi-criteria decision-making frameworks, expert quality is generally associated with accumulated knowledge, domain-specific experience, and exposure to complex decision environments. Indicators such as total experience, sector experience, managerial experience, and academic involvement are widely considered proxies for an expert’s analytical capability and familiarity with decision contexts. These variables reflect not only the duration of involvement in the field but also the depth and diversity of professional engagement, which are critical for informed judgment. The inclusion of multiple indicators aims to capture different dimensions of expertise rather than relying on a single attribute. Therefore, the selected indicators are consistent with prior MCDM studies that operationalize expertise through measurable professional characteristics, ensuring both objectivity and theoretical grounding in the weighting process. The expert weighting procedure employed in this study assumes that experts with greater professional experience may provide more informed evaluations in complex decision-making contexts. This assumption is consistent with many expert-based decision-making studies, where professional background, sector experience, and managerial exposure are considered indicators of domain knowledge. In the context of coffee brand strategies and consumer behavior, individuals who have accumulated longer professional and academic experience are expected to possess a deeper understanding of market dynamics and consumer psychology. Therefore, the Euclidean distances based expert weighting approach incorporates objective professional indicators such as total experience, sector experience, managerial experience, and academic background to differentiate the contribution levels of experts. Nevertheless, it should be emphasized that the purpose of this weighting process is not to impose a normative hierarchy among experts but to reduce potential bias that may arise from treating all expert opinions as identical. Furthermore, the robustness of the findings is supported by the comparative ranking analysis conducted under different parameter values in the ARLON stage, which indicates that the ordering of strategic alternatives remains stable across different scenarios. This stability suggests that the overall results of the study are not excessively sensitive to variations in weighting parameters.

While the use of professional indicators such as age and experience provides an objective basis for differentiating expert contributions, it may raise concerns in studies focusing on younger consumer segments. Specifically, more experienced experts may not fully reflect the evolving preferences, digital behaviors, or consumption patterns of younger generations. In addition, this generational difference may introduce potential cognitive bias, as experts may interpret young consumers’ preferences through their own experiential and generational perspectives. However, in the context of multi-criteria decision-making studies, expertise is generally associated with accumulated knowledge, sectoral experience, and analytical capability rather than demographic similarity with the target population. Prior MCDM research frequently employs experience-based indicators to weight expert opinions, as these variables are considered reliable proxies for domain knowledge and decision-making competence. Therefore, the weighting approach adopted in this study aims to enhance analytical reliability rather than to replicate the demographic characteristics of young consumers. Nevertheless, this limitation should be acknowledged, and future research may incorporate hybrid approaches combining expert evaluations with direct consumer data to further improve representativeness and reduce potential generational bias.

It begins with identifying alternatives. The relationship between the psychological criteria and the strategy alternatives is operationalized through the multi criteria decision making structure of the proposed model. The strategy alternatives were generated based on the marketing and consumer behavior literature related to coffee chains and service experience management. This structure allows the model to link psychological determinants of brand preference with potential strategic responses in a systematic manner. The parameter T used in the Heron mean aggregation represents the decision preference attitude of the evaluation process. In the context of the ARLON method, this parameter regulates the balance between different normalized values and reflects how strongly the decision model emphasizes consistency among evaluation components. Lower values of T indicate a more conservative aggregation behavior in which the influence of extreme evaluation values is reduced, while higher values allow greater sensitivity to variations across criteria evaluations. Therefore, varying the T parameter enables the examination of different decision preference scenarios and provides insight into the stability of the ranking results. To evaluate the robustness of the proposed model, sensitivity analysis was conducted by testing the ranking results across different T values ranging from 0 to 1. The results demonstrate that although minor numerical variations may occur in the performance scores of alternatives, the relative ordering of strategies remains unchanged. In addition, the performance gap between the leading strategies and the lower ranked alternatives remains consistently significant across the tested scenarios. This stability indicates that the proposed decision framework produces robust and reliable results even when different decision preference attitudes are considered.

The parameter T used in the Heron mean aggregation reflects the decision preference structure of the evaluation process by controlling the balance between normalized values. In practical terms, this parameter represents the extent to which the decision model emphasizes consistency versus sensitivity in aggregating alternative performances. Lower values of T lead to a more conservative aggregation structure that reduces the influence of variation across criteria, whereas higher values increase sensitivity to differences in performance evaluations. Therefore, T can be interpreted as a regulator of decision strictness in the aggregation process.

To further strengthen the robustness analysis, the numerical variation ranges of alternative performance scores were also examined. The results indicate that the score differences between the highest-ranked alternatives and lower-ranked options remain consistently significant across different T values. In particular, the performance gap between the top-ranked strategy and subsequent alternatives remains stable, demonstrating that the superiority of leading strategies is not marginal but structurally robust. Although minor numerical fluctuations are observed, these variations do not affect the relative dominance patterns among alternatives. This finding confirms that the robustness of the model is supported not only by ranking consistency but also by the stability of score dispersion and performance gaps.

In addition to ranking stability, the robustness of the results was further examined by analyzing the magnitude of variation in the performance scores of the alternatives across different parameter values. The findings indicate that although minor numerical fluctuations occur in the scores, these variations remain within a limited range and do not alter the relative performance gaps between the alternatives. In particular, the leading strategies consistently maintain a clear score advantage over lower-ranked alternatives, confirming the stability of the decision outcomes not only in ordinal terms but also in cardinal magnitude. This additional evidence strengthens the robustness of the proposed model by demonstrating that the results are not sensitive to parameter changes in both ranking order and score dispersion.

Discussion

Identity and belonging emerge as crucial criteria in terms of the role of psychological factors in young consumers’ brand preferences. Indeed, identity is crucial for consumers to answer the question “Who am I?“, while belonging stems from the consumer’s need to feel connected to a group or community. A strong brand identity meets consumers’ symbolic needs, enhances their perception of value, and contributes to consumer identification with the brand36. Additionally, companies offer different types of brand names or symbols with brand salience to meet consumers’ identity signaling needs. In other words, this is crucial for helping consumers define brand strategies that meet their identity goals for social groups37. Fazli-Salehi et al.38 aimed to investigate the need for belonging and the impact of brand use as an identity signal in multi-ethnic advertising compared to other advertising approaches. The analysis results indicate that consumers’ need for belonging is positively related to self-brand connection and social brand connection in ads featuring ethnically nonconforming models, but not in ads featuring multi-ethnic models. Buschgens et al.39, on the other hand, aim to investigate how and when visual references in brand visual aesthetics (i.e., colors, shapes, patterns, and materials) serve as design practices that enable consumer diasporic identity.

This study identified experiential satisfaction as another important criterion in terms of the influence of psychological factors on consumers’ coffee preferences. It can be argued that experiential satisfaction relates to the pleasure experienced by consumers during brand interactions, such as the atmosphere of the space, music, and comfort. Experiential satisfaction can be briefly defined as the evaluation of consumers’ experiences40. In other words, experiential satisfaction reflects the rational and emotional evaluation of the value consumers directly encounter during service situations41. Zha et al.42 aim to identify the factors contributing to sensory brand experience. In doing so, they attempt to understand how sensory brand experience influences variables such as customer satisfaction, brand loyalty, brand commitment, and customer brand appreciation. Manhas et al.43 examine the multifaceted relationships between product innovation, customer satisfaction, and experience in the quick service restaurant sector. Thus, they examine the mediating role of experiential satisfaction in establishing the link between product innovation and customer satisfaction.

In a project investigating the influence of psychological factors on consumer coffee preferences, experience and atmosphere were identified as the best alternatives. The experience and atmosphere strategy addressed consumers’ emotional experiences with a destination and significantly influenced their desire to return to that destination44. This is because customer service in an experience and atmosphere strategy is highly correlated with customer responsiveness, service reliability, personalized service, and timely complaint response. Through customer interaction, customers not only build trust with the brand but also establish a long-term relationship45. Furthermore, coffee shop atmosphere can enhance the individual consumption experience, while the helpfulness and knowledge of service staff can also ensure good service, meeting customer expectations and needs46. Rancati et al.47 took a multidisciplinary approach and conducted a more in-depth study of the customer experience at two coffee shops in Vietnam. They argue that factors such as store atmosphere, beverage quality, price, employee service, and previous visits significantly impact the customer experience. Furthermore, digitalization and innovation strategies, while playing a significant role in developing technology, are another important alternative in terms of the influence of psychological factors on consumers’ coffee preferences. Indeed, continuous innovation with new products, product line extensions, and package sizes tailored to different consumer needs creates the necessary platform for businesses’ unstoppable growth48. Furthermore, a digital platform can help coffee producers expand the functionality of technology and improve the intermediary between service providers and service recipients in agriculture using digital equipment49. To offer an institution-centered perspective on digitalization, Hidalgo et al.50 examine how two selected coffee grower communities located in the Colombian mountains use digital technologies through material and symbolic means. This specifically aims to understand how these technologies are integrated into their agricultural practices and life strategies.

Although the results indicate that experience and atmosphere and digitalization and innovation strategies emerge as the most influential alternatives in relation to the prioritized psychological factors, these findings should not be interpreted as universally applicable prescriptions for all coffee brands. Coffee chains may differ substantially in terms of market positioning, price segments, and value propositions. For instance, premium coffee brands often rely more heavily on experiential differentiation, store atmosphere, and symbolic consumption elements, whereas brands operating in more price sensitive segments may benefit more from efficiency oriented digital solutions. Similarly, the effectiveness of digitalization and innovation strategies may vary depending on the technological capabilities and strategic priorities of individual brands. In this respect, the influence of psychological factors on strategic choices may also be moderated by a brand’s positioning and value proposition. Therefore, the results of this study should be interpreted as a general strategic orientation highlighting the relative importance of psychological drivers rather than a uniform strategy recommendation for all coffee chains. Future research may extend this framework by examining how brand positioning, price segments, and organizational capabilities interact with psychological factors in shaping optimal strategy configurations.

Conclusion

The primary objective of this study is to identify the most important psychological factors influencing young consumers’ coffee brand preferences and to relate these factors to investment strategies for coffee chains, thereby contributing to the academic literature and providing guidance for practitioners. This study should be interpreted as an exploratory analytical framework rather than a fully prescriptive model of consumer behavior. The primary objective of the proposed approach is to prioritize psychological factors and evaluate strategic alternatives through a structured decision-making methodology based on expert knowledge. The model does not attempt to directly measure the behavior of a specific consumer population but instead aims to identify the relative importance of psychological drivers and their potential strategic implications for coffee chains targeting young consumers. Therefore, the findings should be interpreted as indicative strategic insights rather than universally generalizable prescriptions. Future research can extend the proposed framework by incorporating consumer survey data or cross-cultural samples in order to test and validate the results in different empirical contexts. To this end, a comprehensive literature review identifies ten different psychological indicators as criteria and seven different investment strategies as alternatives and proposes a new fuzzy multi-criteria decision-making model. In the model, expert weights are calculated using the Euclidean distances-based expert weighting approach, criteria importance is measured using the extended SIWEC method, and alternative rankings are performed using the ARLON technique. Furthermore, the integration of behavioral leadership fuzzy sets adds a behavioral dimension to the decision-making process. The study’s findings reveal that “identity and belonging” and “experiential satisfaction” are the most critical psychological indicators, while “experience and atmosphere” and “digitalization and innovation” strategies are the top investment alternatives. In this respect, the study contributes to the literature regarding the prioritization of psychological factors, presents a methodologically innovative model, and contributes to the development of more effective and consumer-oriented strategies for businesses towards the young consumer segment.

However, the study has some theoretical and methodological limitations. First, this study does not specifically examine any country or country group; rather, it draws conclusions based on a general assessment. Therefore, incorporating different country groups or cultural contexts into the analysis in future studies could offer valuable insights into how psychological factors influence young consumers’ coffee brand preferences. Second, this study does not rank coffee chain companies based on their performance in meeting consumer expectations; instead, they are evaluated solely at the level of strategic alternatives. In future research, comparatively analyzing the performance of companies based on consumer-focused criteria could further strengthen the practical implications of this study. Furthermore, the proposed fuzzy decision-making model has some limitations. While the integration of behavioral leadership fuzzy sets is a significant innovation, the lack of comparative testing of the model with different types of fuzzy sets constitutes a limitation. Similarly, while the ARLON technique has yielded successful results in ranking alternatives, comparative applications with different ranking algorithms could enhance the robustness of the model. In this context, testing the model in different cultural contexts and with various decision-making techniques in future studies will further strengthen the generalizability of the findings and their contribution to the literature.

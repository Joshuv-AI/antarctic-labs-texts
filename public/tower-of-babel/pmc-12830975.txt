Multi-agent reinforcement learning driven resource game optimization for network slicing in MEC-enabled HetNets
Authors: Kai Mao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe convergence of Mobile Edge Computing (MEC) and network slicing technologies is critical to meet the diverse quality of service (QoS) requirements of 5G/6G.However,multi-tenancy (eMBB/uRLLC/mMTC) competition for resources in dynamic environments challenges traditional centralised allocation methods.In this paper, we propose a cooperative optimization framework for edge network slicing resources based on the fusion of multi-intelligence reinforcement learning (MARL) and evolutionary game theory (MARL-EGT).The framework models each slice tenant as an intelligent body with autonomous decision-making capability, which explores the optimal resource requesting strategy through interactive learning; meanwhile, evolutionary game dynamics is introduced to model the imitation, learning and evolution process of the slice population strategy, which guides the system to converge to an efficient evolutionary stable equilibrium (ESS).In order to cope with the problem of too large environment state space and intelligence coordination, a hierarchical attention mechanism and a credit-based contribution evaluation algorithm are innovatively designed to significantly improve the learning efficiency and convergence speed. In simulation experiments, under the MEC scenario constructed based on real data, the MARL-EGT scheme significantly outperforms benchmark methods such as federated reinforcement learning (FRL) and non-cooperative gaming (NCG) in terms of key metrics, such as total system utility, slicing SLA satisfaction rate, and resource utilization, and demonstrates superior dynamic environment adaptability, which provides large-scale, intelligent edge network slicing resource management new ideas.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-33190-5.Keywords: Mobile edge computing (MEC), Network slicing, Resource allocation, Multi-intelligence reinforcement learning (MARL), Evolutionary game theory (EGT), Quality of service (QoS), Intelligent optimizationSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL), as a cross research field of game theory, cybernetics and multi-intelligent learning, is a cutting-edge direction in the research of multi-agent systems (MASs), which empowers intelligences to complete diversified tasks in dynamic and multi-dimensional complex environments through interaction and decision-making. MASs are at the forefront of multi-agent systems (MASs) research, empowering intelligences to perform diverse tasks in dynamic, multi-dimensional and complex environments through interaction and decision-making. The emergence of network slicing enables mobile networks to cope with different demands without the need to set up a physical network, and at the same time further strengthens the resource management capability in mobile networks, reduces the cost and improves the network performance. However, since the network environment is not static, network resources need to be allocated adaptively to cope with different situations; at the same time, there are various terminal devices with different limitations and requirements; furthermore, multiple slices interact with each other, resulting in the need for further allocation and adjustment of network resources. Network slicing technology becomes a key enabling technology to meet diverse quality of service (QoS) requirements by constructing multiple customised virtual networks with specific performance metrics in logical isolation on a set of shared physical network infrastructure.

Overview of related work

Existing research on resource optimization for MEC network slicing revolves around two main lines: centralized optimization and distributed coordination. Centralized approaches usually use mathematical tools such as convex optimization, Liapunov optimization, and column generation to model the resource allocation problem. Wenbin Yao modeled slicing resource allocation as a stochastic optimization problem, using Liapunov drift plus penalty function to minimize the long-term average cost and delay1.Qingzheng Tan proposed an adaptive deflation algorithm for core network elements in response to network resource requests over time and the need to further optimize network slicing performance. Using deep reinforcement learning to solve the optimal policy, and modeling and analysis of the core network user-functional network element processing system, the delay is reduced by 20% at the cost of 10% energy consumption. At the same time, imitation learning is added to the deep reinforcement learning algorithm to avoid the intelligence from falling into the wrong policy by using the traditional algorithm as the expert policy assistance for convergence. Meanwhile, for the resource allocation problem of multi-slice coexistence faced in 5G mobile networks, a resource allocation algorithm for multi-slice coexistence under the constraint of base station energy consumption is proposed from the perspective of wireless resources to optimize the slice performance. Taking the case of coexistence of throughput-sensitive slices and delay-sensitive slices as an example, the objective is to maximize the throughput of the throughput-sensitive slices under the condition of satisfying the performance of the delay-sensitive slices. A bandwidth allocation algorithm is proposed, and then it is combined with the semi-positive constraint algorithm to solve the optimization problem. By comparing with the traditional bandwidth allocation algorithm, it is proved that the proposed bandwidth allocation algorithm can improve the throughput by 7% compared with the average bandwidth allocation; and the overall algorithm is verified to increase the throughput by more than 6% compared with the genetic algorithm2.

Based on the joint resource allocation problem of end-to-end network slicing with cloud-edge collaboration, a resource management and computation offloading scheme is proposed for the power consumption cost of slicing system. Firstly, a two-layer architecture model combining network slicing and mobile edge computing is established, which is mainly divided into slicing instance layer and infrastructure layer. Then, the optimization problem of minimizing the power cost is established by considering the communication and computation resources on the premise of guaranteeing the delay and rate of all users in the slices. Next, the complex problem is decomposed into two subproblems, and resource management and computation offloading algorithms based on hybrid penalty function and convex approximation are designed. Finally, simulation work is carried out, and the results verify the feasibility and effectiveness of the proposed scheme, which greatly reduces the system power cost, and validate the relationship between the number of users, the amount of MEC computational resources, the maximum base station transmit power, and the probability of packet error and the system power cost, respectively3.

Multi-intelligence Reinforcement Learning (MRL) is developing in the direction of open application objects, embodied application problems, and complex application scenarios, and is gradually becoming the most effective tool for solving real-world game decision-making problems.The existence of Nash equilibrium is proved by modeling resource competition among slicing tenants using non-cooperative games. Ejaz A M, Wu G, Ahmed A designed an end-to-end network slicing approach to predict common and urgent user requests by T-distribution. We formulate the problem as a Multi-Agent Markov Decision Process (MDP) and introduce a Multi-Agent Soft Behavioural Critique (MAgSAC) algorithm. The algorithm balances the allocation process by intelligently activating and deactivating Virtual Network Function (VNF) instances to prevent wastage of scarce resources. Our approach aims to optimize the overall utility, balancing the trade-offs between revenue, energy cost and latency. We evaluate our approach MAgSAC through simulations and compare it to the following six benchmark scenarios: MAA3C, SACT, DDPG, S2Vec, Random and Greedy. The results show that our MAgSAC approach optimizes the utility by 30%, reduces the energy cost by 12.4% and shortens the execution time by 21.7% compared to the closest relevant multi-agent approach, MAA3C4.

Existing solutions usually rely on static network resource partitioning or systematic a priori knowledge, which cannot adapt to the dynamic and changeable MEC environment, leading to problems such as excessive service delay and unreasonable resource provisioning. To address the above important challenges, Yu Z, Xu X, Zhou W proposed two resource allocation methods for 5G network slicing in MEC environment5.Jiayi L, Wenbin Y, Chen W considered a CDN slice system operating in discrete time intervals by adopting a periodic reconfiguration strategy. Within each time interval, the CDN-MEC-VNFs planning (CMVP) problem is formalized based on uncertainty programming to cope with dynamic and uncertain mobile network environments where user requests are treated as random variables with arbitrary patterns. Then, a data-driven and learning-based algorithm that integrates stochastic simulation (SS), deep neural network (DNN) and meta-heuristic algorithms is proposed for determining the provisioning of CDN slices based on historical user request information6.Foko L M S, Mthulisi V, Bertrand A B Combining SDN, NFV, SFC, and NS technologies toeffectively manage MEC server resources to ensure QoS requirements for AVNET. QoS parameters considered include latency, compute, storage, and bandwidth resources. Therefore, a mathematical resource management model for MEC servers is proposed and then, a new MEC architecture adapted to AVNET is proposed which uses the above mentioned techniques as well as the proposed mathematical model to manage bandwidth, compute and storage resources7. The explosive growth of mobile users (MUs) has led to a substantial increase in computing tasks being offloaded to MEC, M Chen, Y Zheng, J Yang, L Yang, W Zhang proposed the joint optimization problem of resource allocation, computation offloading, and resource pricing for multi-BS cooperative MEC in heterogeneous multicell networks8. And Krishnamoorthy S, Ramesh L, Subbarayalu R, et al. proposed model for capturing both the spatial and temporal features in network traffic patterns and allow accurate malicious pattern classification9. Additionally, in order to minimize the total energy consumption of the system and path loss of users on the premise. Long C, Jigang W, Jun Z, et al. developed an on-line offloading decision and computational resource management algorithm with joint consideration of collaborations between device–cloud, edge–edge and edge–cloud10, C Li, Y Gan, Y Zhang, Y Luo, et al. proposed a cooperative computation offloading strategy with on-demand deployment of multi-UAV in UAV-aided MEC11.

System modeling and problem formalization

Network architecture

Consider a MEC system consisting of geographically distributed Edge Servers (ES) connected to the core network via backhaul links.The system supports $K$-class network slices (eMBB/uRLLC/mMTC), and each slice tenant (STₖ) provides services with specific SLA guarantees for its users.User equipment (UE) accesses the corresponding slices according to service types, forming an end-to-end virtualised network.

Resource modeling

Computational resources: each ES m possesses a limited computational capacity (CPU cycles/second) C_m.

Communication resource: each ES m possesses a limited bandwidth resource (MHz) B_m.

Multi-dimensional resource pool: the total system resources are represented as the vector R.

Conclusions

The experimental results show that MARL-GTRO achieves Pareto optimisation of resource allocation in dynamic MEC environments through the fusion of hierarchical attention mechanism and evolutionary game dynamics, taking into account both SLA guarantee and system stability, and provides a verifiable technological path for intelligent management of 6G network slices.The experiment verified the superiority of MARL-GTRO in three major dimensions.

Optimality of resource allocation

The system utility reaches 102.3, which is significantly higher than the game theory approach (+ 33.2%) and the single-intelligent body PPO (+ 70.2%), and the key mechanism, VDN-value decomposition, allows the intelligences to pass the Implicit coordination to avoid local optima .

SLA guarantees reliability

URLLC default rate pressed down to 2.7% (98.1% compliance rate for scenarios with industry requirements < 1%) in the reward functionReduction of resource mismatches and increase in the rate of completion of tasks to 97.3 per cent.

System stability breakthrough

Under the harsh scenario of ± 30% traffic fluctuation, the standard deviation of utility is only 2.1 (60.4% lower than that of the gaming method), and local decision-making avoids the bottleneck of the central node, and the decision-making latency is stable at 0.84ms (meeting the 5G TTI ≤ 1ms).

Communication efficiency innovation

15.3 Mbps signaling overhead 66.4% lower than centralized RL, action space compression (3-dimensional vectors) and observation localization (11 dimensions only).

The MARL-GTRO framework achieves Pareto-optimal resource allocation in a dynamic MEC environment, and its Nash equilibrium convergence property makes the four major indicators of system utility, SLA guarantee, stability, and communication efficiency break through the bottleneck of the existing technology, providing a new paradigm for 6G network slice management.

Strategy for maximizing space utilization in smart libraries based on reinforcement learning
Authors: Ying Dai
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractSmart and modern libraries need robust and sophisticated systems to maximize space use. Traditional library designs are built on unchanging spatial arrangements, making it difficult to manage dynamic user demands due to overcrowded study zones, poor navigation, and wasteful storage. Real-time user behavior fluctuations are ignored in the current design, resulting in space underutilization and overcrowding in high-traffic areas. Introduce Reinforcement Learning Maximize Space Utilization (RLMSU) methodologies to manage dynamic space in modern libraries to solve research problems. The RLMSU platform collects data from IoT sensors, historical usage patterns, and computer vision to improve book-shelf design, seating, and navigation pathways. The agent-action (AA) paradigm predicts user occupancy and movement in the RL method, optimizing space allocation and resource use while maintaining accessibility. Every agent’s actions are rewarded under the AA principle, gathering library environment feedback. Python is used to analyze space use and develop the RLMSU framework using the Full Library Services Dataset. Effective use of the AA idea increases seating availability by 30% and reduces congestion by 25%. The system works well in dynamic scenarios and improves user satisfaction during peak and off-peak hours. Hence, this process was successfully integrated with research institutions, university libraries, and public libraries to improve space and operational efficiency.Keywords: Smart libraries, Reinforcement learning, Space utilization, Action-agent, Seat availability, Operational efficiency, And user satisfactionSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

In library management systems1, space utilization is crucial for achieving maximum user satisfaction, an effective operational workflow, and optimal resource access2. The library is a dynamic environment that provides various services3, including digital research, individual study, book accessibility, and collaborative learning, to its users. Suppose the library has a poor space management concept, which creates limited accessibility, misallocation of resources, underutilization or overutilization, low user satisfaction4, and limited productivity. Therefore, researchers recommend various intelligent space utilization and management techniques to improve the library framework. Intelligent learning methods are used to meet user demands, optimize study zone allocation, and determine seating arrangements. The machine learning techniques5 not only provide effective resource utilization but also maintain stability between the user distribution and the facilities available in the library. Therefore, the space optimization process is integrated with optimized technologies, such as deep learning techniques, policy optimization procedures, and other heuristic approaches6, to improve the performance of automated booking processes, occupancy tracking, and smart navigation systems. In addition, intelligent techniques are combined with IoT sensors7 to capture user movement, behavior, and motions and predict resource placements and peak-hour adjustments in dynamic environments. Using digital technology in library administration ensures a responsive library environment with optimum design, which improves sustainability, energy efficiency, and cost8. Existing systems struggle to optimize space, especially in designs with underutilized areas (defined as zones with an occupancy rate less than 30% over a typical operational period) and overcrowded regions (shelf utilization exceeding 90% and long book retrieval times)9. These two crucial features, determined by occupancy rates and average search time, greatly affect the user experience, library reliability, and accessibility10. The research difficulties are overcome by integrating the Reinforcement Learning (RL)11 approach with the pattern analysis approach to differentiate the high and low-utilized areas. From the location, various features need to be considered to improve the space allocation and seating arrangements in the library environment12. Hence, this research aims to explore the library environment from a user-centric perspective, where seating is allocated to users based on the study zone and the resources required. To achieve the objective of this work, IoT sensors13, RFID14, and motion sensors15 are utilized to observe user movements in the library environment. The gathered information is affected by technical features that reduce space utilization and allocation efficiency.

Therefore, outlier analysis, missing value replacement, and Gaussian functions are incorporated to format the inputs. Then, features are derived to explore key factors for book arrangement, seating arrangement, and space utilization, which are achieved by using zone segmentation and pattern analysis. Then, the pattern is further explored using RL agent components, which make the appropriate decision depending on the action, state, and reward values. During this process, the shortest distance between the data points is computed to make an effective decision. Finally, high-zone-related users are served effectively, and frequently visited books are allocated to the high zone. This process improves seating availability by up to 30% and reduces congestion by up to 25%, effectively utilized in library management systems. Related works

Effective space management and intelligent resource allocation in educational environments, especially in libraries and classrooms, have emerged as significant areas of emphasis as a result of rapid technological innovation and problems such as the COVID-19 pandemic. This is particularly true in the current day and age. In order to maximize the usage of space and the delivery of services in educational environments, academics have examined a number of computational and sensor-based approaches. This section discusses the most relevant studies, with a particular focus on the shift from traditional techniques to frameworks that are driven by artificial intelligence and the Internet of Things. These frameworks are created with the purpose of increasing the efficiency of space utilization, increasing user involvement, and increasing the overall adaptability of the system in contexts that are dynamic.

The research conducted by Vassilakaki and Moniarou-Papaconstantinou16 explored how libraries modified their settings in response to the COVID-19 pandemic. Their work focused in particular on re-imagining layouts and improving access to digital resources. The principal difficulties of limited physical access, social distancing protocols, and the heightened demand for digital services are emphasized by this research. The work that the authors have completed is founded on the analyses of a number of case studies, which provide instances of changes that include contactless borrowing, expanded digital collections, and blended service delivery. The authors emphasize the difficulties that are associated with the technology access gap and the restricted budget, as well as the necessity for a balanced approach to physical and digital reachability in their discussion on accessibility in areas that are difficult to access.

Ye et al.17 model self-study classroom space designs using a Back Propagation Neural Network (BPNN). The high demand for and inefficient distribution of classes and the institution’s poor resource scheduling are the research challenge. The BPNN model forecasts class occupancy using previous and current occupancy data. With occupancy predicting accuracy of 85%, the results are promising. Space usage improves by 20% to 25%, resulting in less unproductive time and more productive study spaces. DeepClassRooms, a deep learning-based digital twin platform, can improve classroom management, according to Razzaq et al.18. The project integrates real-time and predictive analytics to improve classroom usage. The methodology forecasts classroom occupancy, ambient variables, and student engagement using deep neural networks with 90% accuracy. Classroom hours decreased by 30% and resource allocation efficiency increased by 25% in the trial. However, the paper notes that data confidentiality, high computing costs, and the model’s ability to alter among institutions require further investigation.

An IoT-trained physical education training network uses deep reinforcement learning (DRL) for resource allocation and virtualization, according to Li et al.19. User demand and network constraints affect resource allocation in smart training environments, which the study optimizes. The new DRL-based approach improves training session scheduling, device pairing, and bandwidth provisioning with 92% resource consumption forecasting accuracy. The trials show 35% reduced latency and 28% higher energy efficiency than heuristic-based techniques. Asemi20 examines developing technologies in intelligent library-assisted services and systems to improve information management and user engagement. The study addresses operational resource allocation, attention deprivation, and outdated cataloging systems in library management. This study investigates automated book classification, personalization, and real-time space monitoring using AI, machine learning, and IoT. The paper shows that AI algorithms retrieve library books 40% more accurately, 30% more efficiently, and more effectively than traditional approaches. Different types of libraries cannot effectively incorporate these sophisticated systems. This limitation highlights an inexpensive and sustainable technology gap.

Gay et al.21 describe the innovative use of Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) technology to capture indoor stair-related physical activities. The system operates based on RFID technology and automatically registers the user’s movement as they climb the stairs. Experimental testing proved that RFID detectors could measure the number of steps taken with up to 95% accuracy, which is significantly higher than that of traditional pedometers and motion sensors. The research acknowledges that employing the method for scalable purposes is problematic, especially when many people are situated in the same area, where interferences obstruct the signal; therefore, further development is needed for positioning the RFID and signal processing.

Sarma and colleagues22 developed an automated book recommendation system to encourage users to engage more with libraries by employing machine learning techniques. The research addresses the shortcomings of conventional approaches, such as rule-based and collaborative filtering, which face issues with accuracy and scalability. The system uses content-based filtering techniques and deep learning methods to comprehend users’ needs and recommend relevant books. The results showed improvements in overall recommendation precision, with an average accuracy enhancement of 15% compared to traditional models. Sa’ari et al.23 offer a comprehensive analysis of the application of libraries’ services and user experience through the lens of deep learning algorithms. The study categorizes various existing approaches, including CNNs, RNNs, and other transformer models, and examines their capabilities in solving recommendation systems, auto-indexing, and providing live query answers. Jain and Behera24 studied the evolution of collections, spaces, technologies, and services. The research paper examines the transition from tangible to intangible collections facilitated by the use of AI-powered cataloging and cloud-based repositories. However, issues of budgeting and technology taking charge remain unresolved. Islam et al.25 examine the impact of big data analytics on library management in Indonesia, outlining key challenges and opportunities. The study identifies a lack of technological systems, fragmented data, and inadequate personnel training as some of the most significant obstacles to implementing big data analytics systems effectively. Using case studies and best practices, the authors reflect on how predictive analytics can be used to allocate resources and enhance user experience by providing recommendations. These suggest that operational efficiencies can gain up to 25% with data integration. However, data privacy and the ethics of its use remain a significant concern that requires equally comprehensive policies for data stewardship.

The tracking of user behavior in smart libraries by RLMSU raises concerns regarding users’ privacy. These concerns are centered on the unlawful profiling of users, the possibility of misuse of personally identifiable information, and the discomfort that users experience as a result of surveillance. There are a large number of precautions in place in the architecture of the system in order to address these issues. First, identities are concealed during the process of data collection and storage through the use of data anonymization, all the while allowing for pattern recognition for the purpose of system optimization. Second, data visibility is limited to authorized users, and data is protected during transmission and storage by role-based access restrictions and encryption. By utilizing user consent techniques at the moment of interaction, individuals are provided with information regarding data usage policies and are given the ability to forgo specific tracking without sacrificing their access to essential library services. Conducting regular audits and compliance checks guarantees that local data privacy legislation and institutional data protection policies, in addition to the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), are being followed. In addition, the system architecture gives precedence to aggregated behavioral patterns over individual tracking in order to optimize service efficiency and reduce the likelihood of privacy violations. Through the balancing of functionality and ethics, RLMSU is able to secure data and trust while also improving user experience.

Discussion

Experimental evaluation has shown that the RLMSU (Reinforcement Learning-based Management of Space Utilisation) framework is better at managing and optimising space allocation in dynamic library environments. The model’s accuracy in resource allocation, decision-making efficiency, user satisfaction, and operation cost are shown in Fig. 9; Table 8. The algorithm can allocate space accurately with scores from 97.4% to 99.1% in various testing circumstances. The RLMSU framework uses Proximal Policy Optimisation (PPO) rules to improve these features. These policies dynamically adjust space assignments based on user behavior entering and departing the system. when seen by the metrics ), ), and ), the model demonstrates a resilient performance even when the number of iterations and users increases. It is noteworthy that the level of user satisfaction () reached as high as 99.3%, which indicates a favourable user experience that is supported by space allocation that is both responsive and intelligent.

RLMSU’s computational performance is extremely impressive. It allocates resources in one to ten milliseconds and makes decisions in five to twenty seconds. Real-time systems must balance accuracy and speed in changing situations like libraries with diverse foot traffic. The RLMSU framework surpasses DRL, DLDTF, and BPNN in space allocation accuracy, processing cost, and user satisfaction. In particular, RLMSU exhibited a maximum space utilisation efficiency of 99.1%, outperforming DRL (90.2%), DLDTF (88.5%), and BPNN (82.8%). RLMSU had 99.12% user satisfaction, whereas DLDTF, the closest competitor, had 96.2%.

These results are promising, however the RLMSU system has limits. First, computing complexity grows with user count and IoT, motion, and RFID sensor data. In dense or big contexts, this escalation may cause scalability concerns. Second, while the model adapts effectively to moderately dynamic settings, its real-time responsiveness may be hampered in volatile or unexpected environments. Since the system relies on continuous sensor data, it may be vulnerable to data accuracy and resilience issues if the hardware or network fails. Future research can study optimisation strategies including model pruning, federated learning, and hierarchical reinforcement learning to improve scalability and adaptability. Additional contextual data, such as user preferences or usage trends, may improve time and space allocation decisions.

Conclusion

The suggested RLMSU framework has shown enhanced efficacy in resource allocation and space usage compared to traditional learning approaches. The IoT, motion and RFID sensors are utilized to observe the user movement and behaviour in the library systems. The gathered information regarding preprocessing, feature derivation, and pattern analysis was explored. The extracted patterns are fed into the PPO policy with the RL approach that recognizes the high-density traffic zone. According to the user occupancy and high zone area, the space utilization is maximized to reduce the idle sittings. The experimental findings indicated that RLMSU attained superior resource allocation accuracy (97.4%–99.2%), enhanced user satisfaction, and reduced computing expenses, establishing it as an effective method for intelligent resource management. Nonetheless, despite its benefits, the framework possesses specific drawbacks. The computational complexity escalates with an increased user count, and real-time flexibility may be compromised in extremely dynamic contexts.

Furthermore, the model’s reliance on predetermined learning parameters may affect its generalizability across various application contexts. Future endeavours will concentrate on augmenting the flexibility of RLMSU by incorporating meta-learning methodologies to enhance efficacy in dynamic settings. Furthermore, refining the framework for extensive distributed systems and integrating sophisticated privacy-preserving techniques would augment security and scalability, ensuring wider applicability in intelligent systems.

One-arm exponents of high-dimensional percolation revisited CNRS and Sorbonne Université, Paris, France, severo@lpsm.paris
Diederik van Engelenburg, Christophe Garban, Romain Panis

Abstract
We consider sufficiently spread-out Bernoulli percolation in dimensions d > 6 {d>6} . We present a short and simple proof of the up-to-constants estimate for the one-arm probability in both the full-space and half-space settings. These results were previously established by Kozma and Nachmias and by Chatterjee and Hanson, respectively. Our proof improves upon the entropic technique introduced by Dewan and Muirhead, relying on a sharp estimate on a suitably chosen correlation length recently obtained by Duminil-Copin and Panis. This approach is inspired by our companion work [ EGPS25 ] , where we compute the one-arm exponent for several percolation models related to the high-dimensional Ising model.

Introduction

In the context of
finite-range
Bernoulli percolation on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
(
d\geq 2
), it is predicted that there is no infinite cluster at the critical point:
\theta(p_{c}):=\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\infty]=0
. In the last fifty years, significant progress has been made regarding this conjecture. Kesten
[
Kes80
]
proved it in the case of two-dimensional (planar) nearest-neighbour percolation. In higher dimensions, the
lace expansion
allowed to prove that
\theta(p_{c})=0
in dimensions
d>10
for the nearest-neighbour case
[
HS90
,
Har08
,
FvdH17
]
, and in dimensions
d>6
for sufficiently spread-out percolation
[
HHS03
]
(see precise definition below). A more recent approach
[
DCP25a
]
provides an alternative proof in the latter setting. Proving that
\theta(p_{c})=0
in the “intermediate” dimensions
3\leq d\leq 6
for either of the two aforementioned settings remains one of the most challenging problems in percolation theory.

The vanishing of
\theta(p_{c})
is equivalent to the convergence to
0
of the sequence of
one-arm probabilities

\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\Lambda_{n}^{c}]
, where
\Lambda_{n}:=[-n,n]^{d}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. According to Widom’s scaling hypothesis
[
Wid65
]
, the behaviour of this sequence should be described by a critical exponent
\rho=\rho(d)>0
—the so-called
one-arm exponent
—defined by:

\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\Lambda_{n}^{c}]=n^{-\rho+o(1)},

(1.1)

where
o(1)
tends to
0
as
n
tends to infinity.

In a seminal work, Kozma and Nachmias
[
KN11
]
proved that
\rho(d)=2
whenever the critical two-point function satisfies
\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}x]\asymp|x|^{2-d}
and
d>6
(where
|\cdot|
is the infinite norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and where
\asymp
means that the two quantities are within a multiplicative constant of each other). This assumption is expected to hold in dimensions
d>6
, and has been verified in dimensions
d>10
for the nearest-neighbour case
[
HS90
,
Har08
,
FvdH17
]
, and
d>6
for sufficiently spread-out percolation
[
HHS03
,
DCP25a
]
. Let us also mention that with the breakthrough proof of conformal invariance of Smirnov
[
Smi01
]
, it is possible to show that
\rho=\tfrac{5}{48}
for site percolation on the triangular lattice
[
LSW02
]
.

In this article, we focus on spread-out percolation in dimensions
d>6
. Building upon the results of
[
DCP25a
]
, we provide an alternative simple proof of the result of
[
KN11
]
.
We also compute (up to a multiplicative constant) the one-arm probability in the half-space setting, a result previously derived by Chatterjee and Hanson
[
CH20
]
. Our results rely on the entropic method of Dewan and Muirhead
[
DM23b
]
, which we refine by using a more suitable notion of correlation length. We stress that our proofs do not rely on any result obtained through the use of lace expansion.

1.1
Definitions and statement of the results

We consider spread-out percolation on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with
spread
parameter
L\geq 1
. More precisely, let
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)
be the graph with vertex set
V=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and edge set
E=E_{L}:=\{\{x,y\}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}:~|x-y|\leq L\}
. We write
x\sim y
to say that
\{x,y\}\in E_{L}
. We denote by
\mathbb{P}_{p}
the law of the random subgraph of
\mathcal{G}
obtained by independently keeping (resp. removing) each edge with probability
p
(resp.
1-p
). For
A,B,S\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we denote by
\{A\xleftrightarrow{S\>}B\}
the event that there exists an open path from
A
to
B
which is fully contained in
S
. We denote by
\tau^{S}_{p}(x,y):=\mathbb{P}_{p}[x\xleftrightarrow{S\>}y]
the restricted two-point function. When
S=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we omit it from the notation.

It is classical (see
[
Gri99
]
) that, when
d\geq 2
and
L\geq 1
, the model undergoes a phase transition at a non-trivial critical point

p_{c}=p_{c}(d,L):=\inf\big\{p\in[0,1]:\mathbb{P}_{p}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\infty]>0\big\}.

(1.2)

Hara and Slade
[
HS90
]
first highlighted the relevance of spread-out percolation. The
universality hypothesis
asserts that the critical exponents of spread-out and nearest-neighbour percolation should match. The spread-out setting is convenient to compute these exponents as one can take the parameter
L
large enough to apply perturbative techniques
[
HHS03
,
DCP25a
]
.

Our first result concerns the study of the one-arm probability introduced in (
1.1
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
d>6
. There exists
L_{0}\geq 1
such that the following holds. For every
L\geq L_{0}
, there exist
c,C>0
such that, for every
n\geq 1
,

\frac{c}{n^{2}}\leq\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\Lambda_{n}^{c}]\leq\frac{C}{n^{2}},

(1.3)

As mentioned above, this result was first obtained by Kozma and Nachmias
[
KN11
]
. Their analysis is based on two fundamental inputs:

(i)

an up-to-constant estimate on the model’s two point function at criticality:

\tau_{p_{c}}(0,x)\asymp|x|^{2-d};

(1.4)

(ii)

a sharp estimate for the volume of cluster of the origin: letting
\mathcal{C}(0)
denote the cluster of the origin, for every
n\geq 1
,

\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[|\mathcal{C}(0)|\geq n]\asymp n^{-1/2}.

(1.5)

The first input is very standard in the study of high-dimensional percolation. It relies on the “black box” which can either be the lace expansion or the approach of
[
DCP25a
]
. The second one
(ii)
was derived via differential inequality techniques: the lower bound was derived in
[
AB87
]
(see also
[
DCT16
]
), and the upper bound in
[
BA91
]
(see also
[
Hut22
]
). The upper bound in (
1.5
) holds under the so-called
triangle condition

\nabla(p_{c}):=\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\tau_{p_{c}}(0,x)\tau_{p_{c}}(x,y)\tau_{p_{c}}(y,0)<\infty

(1.6)

Observe that the triangle condition is clearly satisfied if
(i)
holds and if
d>6
. With these inputs, the proof of
[
KN11
]
consists in proving a renormalisation inequality on
\theta_{n}(p_{c}):=\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\Lambda_{n}^{c}]
. To put it in a nutshell, they consider two cases: on top of the occurrence of the one-arm event, either
|\mathcal{C}(0)|\geq\varepsilon n^{4}
or
|\mathcal{C}(0)|<\varepsilon n^{4}
. By
(ii)
above, the first case has a probability
\lesssim 1/n^{2}
which is of the good order. The handling of the second term is significantly harder and relies on the development of a refined theory of “regularity of clusters”. Let us mention that the latter step was recently revisited and streamlined
[
ASS25
]
, relying on a more geometric notion of regularity. A big advantage of the approach of Kozma and Nachmias is that these regularity properties are robust and useful beyond the context of one-arm exponent computations. Subsequent applications include
[
HS14
,
CH20
,
CHS23
,
CCHS25
,
ASS25
]
.

Instead of relying on
(i)
and
(ii)
, we use as an input some sharp near-critical estimates of the susceptibility
\chi(p)
and the quantity
\varphi_{p}(S)
, introduced in
[
DCT16
]
. These estimates were proved in
[
DCP25a
]
for spread-out percolation, and are summarized in Theorem
1.4
below.
We stress that their proof relies neither on the lace expansion nor on
[
BA91
]
.

The second result of this paper concerns the computation of the one-arm exponent in the half-space setting. Let
\mathbb{H}:=\{0,1,\ldots\}\times\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
d>6
. Let
L_{0}
be given by Theorem
1.1
, and
L\geq L_{0}
. There exist
c,C>0
such that, for
n\geq 1
,

\frac{c}{n^{3}}\leq\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{H}\>}\Lambda_{n}^{c}]\leq\frac{C}{n^{3}}.

(1.7)

This result was previously derived by Chatterjee and Hanson
[
CH20
]
. One important feature of their proof is that it heavily relies on
[
KN11
]
: not only does it use their one-arm computation, by more fundamentally the whole theory of regularity that is developed there. However, let us mention that Chatterjee and Hanson go further by also computing the exponent of the two-point function restricted to the half-space.

Before moving to a precise explanation of our proofs, let us stress that the restriction to spread-out percolation is somewhat artificial in what follows. More precisely, if one could prove (e.g. for nearest-neighbour percolation) an analogue of Theorem
1.4
below which does not rely on
[
KN11
]
, the whole strategy of this paper would go through.

Notations.

If
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we let
|x|:=\max_{1\leq i\leq d}|x_{i}|
denotes the
\ell^{\infty}
norm of
x
. We let
\mathbf{e}_{1}:=(1,0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Throughout the paper various
(d,L)
-dependent constant appear without playing any particular role. We will therefore use the following compact notation: we write
f\lesssim g
(or

) if there exists a constant

such that

1.2
Strategy of the proof

We will use the entropic bound of
[
DM23b
]
in the precise formulation of the lemma below.

Lemma 1.3

(
[
DM23b
, Proposition 1.21]
)
.

For every
d\geq 2
and
L\geq 1
, there exists a constant
C_{0}=C_{0}(d,L)>0
, such that for every set
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
containing
0
, every event
\mathcal{A}
measurable with respect to
\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}(0):=\{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:0\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x\}
, and every
\tfrac{p_{c}}{2}\leq p^{\prime}<p\leq\tfrac{p_{c}+1}{2}
, we have

\big|\mathbb{P}_{p}[\mathcal{A}]-\mathbb{P}_{p^{\prime}}[\mathcal{A}]\big|\leq C_{0}|p-p^{\prime}|\sqrt{\max\{\mathbb{P}_{p}[\mathcal{A}],\mathbb{P}_{p^{\prime}}[\mathcal{A}]\}\mathbb{E}_{p^{\prime}}[|\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}(0)|]}.

(1.8)

Proof.

We first assume that
\Lambda
is finite and apply
[
DM23b
, Proposition 1.21]
to the standard exploration of the cluster of the origin in
\Lambda
. Since
p_{c}\in(0,1)
one has
\tfrac{p_{c}+1}{2},\tfrac{p_{c}}{2}\in(0,1)
so that the quantities
(p(1-p))^{-1/2},(p^{\prime}(1-p^{\prime}))^{-1/2}
(which appear in the entropic bound of
[
DM23b
]
) are bounded away from infinity by a constant which only depends on
p_{c}
, and hence on
d,L
. The extension to the case
\Lambda
infinite follows by approximation.

\Box

We remark that (
1.8
) can be seen as a generalization of the classical differential inequality
\frac{d}{dp}\mathbb{P}_{p}[\mathcal{A}]\leq\frac{1}{p(1-p)}\sqrt{\mathbb{P}_{p}[\mathcal{A}]\mathbb{E}_{p}[|\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}(0)|]}
, proved in
[
OS07
]
.

In
[
DM23b
]
, the authors applied the above result to obtain a bound on the one-arm probability of (
1.1
). Let us briefly explain their argument. Apply Lemma
1.3
to
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
\mathcal{A}:=\{0\xleftrightarrow{}\Lambda_{n}^{c}\}
, and
p=p_{c}
. Then, pick
p^{\prime}
as follows. First, notice that one may find

Since
\varphi_{p_{c}}(\Lambda_{n})=p_{c}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\Lambda_{n},\>y\notin\Lambda_{n},\>x\sim y\end{subarray}}\tau^{\Lambda_{n}}_{p_{c}}(0,x)\geq 1
(see

DCT16

), one has
\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\Lambda_{n}^{c}]\gtrsim n^{1-d}
. This justifies the existence of
\alpha

such that, for every
n\geq 2
,

\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\Lambda_{n}^{c}]\geq\frac{1}{n^{\alpha}}.

(1.9)

The idea is to pick
p^{\prime}=p^{\prime}(n)
such that

\mathbb{P}_{p^{\prime}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\Lambda_{n}^{c}]\leq\frac{1}{2n^{\alpha}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\Lambda_{n}^{c}].

(1.10)

To prove the existence of

, the authors consider the

\xi(p)
, defined as

\xi(p^{\prime})^{-1}\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log\tau_{p^{\prime}}(0,n\mathbf{e}_{1}).

Combining the bound

\mathbb{P}_{p^{\prime}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\Lambda_{n}^{c}]\leq\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{n+L}\setminus\Lambda_{n}}\tau_{p^{\prime}}(0,x)\lesssim n^{d-1}e^{-n/\xi(p^{\prime})},

(see

\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\Lambda_{n}^{c}]\lesssim\frac{(\log n)^{2}}{n^{2}},

Let us present the main input in our analysis. We will need some notations.
If

\varphi_{p}(S):=p\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in S\\
y\notin S\\

(1.14)

Such a quantity was first considered in
[

]
. We define another notion of correlation length based on

as follows: for every

L(p)\coloneqq\min\{k\geq 1:\varphi_{p}(\Lambda_{k})\leq 1/e^{2}\}.

(1.15)

This natural length scale was first considered in

. The closely related

]

. By

. This observation, together with exponential decay of the two-point function below

(see

]
) allows to prove that

diverges as

.

Recall that the susceptibility
\chi(p)
is defined, for
p<p_{c}
, by

\chi(p):=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\tau_{p}(0,x).

(1.16)

Below, we consider translated half-spaces
\mathbb{H}_{n}:=\mathbb{H}-n\mathbf{e}_{1}
, for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
(recall that
\mathbb{H}:=\{0,1,\ldots\}\times\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}
).
We will base our analysis on the following result from

Theorem 1.4

(
[
DCP25a
, Corollary 1.4]
)
.

Let
d>6
. There exist

such that, and for every

c_{1}(p_{c}-p)^{-1}\leq\chi(p)\leq C_{1}(p_{c}-p)^{-1},

(1.17)

and

c_{1}(p_{c}-p)^{-1/2}\leq L(p)\leq C_{1}(p_{c}-p)^{-1/2}.

(1.18)

Moreover, for every
n\geq 0

\varphi_{p}(\mathbb{H}_{n})\leq C_{1}\exp\left(-c_{1}(p_{c}-p)^{1/2}n\right).

(1.19)

.

as in

,

Finally, we briefly describe the proof of Theorem

. The upper-bound is proved in two steps. First, we use the same strategy as the full-space case to bound the quantity

where

denotes the

and

, i.e. the event that there exist two disjoint sets

and
J
of edges such that the configuration restricted to

(resp.

(resp.

) occurs.

2.1
Bounds on
\varphi_{p}

We begin with a lower bound on
\varphi_{p_{c}}(\mathbb{H}_{n})
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
d>6
and
L\geq L_{0}
. For every
p\in[0,1]
, and every
n\geq 0
,

\varphi_{p}(\mathbb{H}_{n})\geq\frac{1}{2d}\varphi_{p}(\Lambda_{n}).

(2.1)

In particular,
\varphi_{p_{c}}(\mathbb{H}_{n})\geq\tfrac{1}{2d}
.

Proof.

Observe that by symmetry one has

\varphi_{p}(\Lambda_{n})\leq 2d\varphi_{p}(\mathbb{H}_{n}).

(2.2)

The second part of the statement follows from the bound of
[
DCT16
]

\varphi_{p_{c}}(\Lambda_{n})\geq 1
.

\Box

For a set
S\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, the (inner vertex) boundary of

\partial S:=\{x\in S:~\exists y\notin S\text{ s.t. }|x-y|_{1}=1\}
(where
|\cdot|_{1}
is the
\ell^{1}
norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
).
For convenience, we sometimes consider a variant of
\varphi_{p}
defined as follows: for
p\in[0,1]
and
S\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
containing
0
,

\psi_{p}(S):=\sum_{x\in\partial S}\tau_{p}^{S}(0,x).

(2.3)

This quantity is often referred to as the
expected number of pioneers
(of
S
), see for instance
[
HMS23
]
. Observe that

p\psi_{p}(S)\leq\varphi_{p}(S)\lesssim\psi_{p}(S).

(2.4)

Indeed, the lower bound is straightforward, while the upper bound follows from the following observation:
if
x\in S\setminus{\partial}S
is such that there exists
y\notin S
with
x\sim y
, then there is
z\in{\partial}S
such that
x\sim z
and
z\sim y
. Now, the FKG inequality (see
[
Gri99
]
) implies that

p\tau_{p}^{S}(0,x)=\mathbb{P}_{p}[e=\{x,z\}\textup{ is open}]\mathbb{P}_{p}[0\xleftrightarrow{S}x]\leq\tau_{p}^{S}(0,z).

(2.5)

Since there are at most
C\,L^{d}\times L^{d}
such pairs
\{x,y\}
with
x\in S\setminus{\partial}S,y\notin S,x\sim y
and which are sent to the same boundary point
z\in{\partial}S
, this implies the upper bound.

The following result will be useful, and is an easy consequence of Lemma
2.1
, (
1.19
), and (
2.4
).

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
d>6
and
L\geq L_{0}
. There exist
c,C>0
such that, for every
p\leq p_{c}
, and every
n\geq 0
,

\displaystyle p\psi_{p}(\Lambda_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\varphi_{p}(\Lambda_{n})\leq C\exp\left(-c(p_{c}-p)^{1/2}n\right),

(2.6)

\displaystyle p\psi_{p}(\mathbb{H}_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\varphi_{p}(\mathbb{H}_{n})\leq C\exp\left(-c(p_{c}-p)^{1/2}n\right)\,

(2.7)

and

c\leq\psi_{p_{c}}(\mathbb{H}_{n})\leq C.

(2.8)

Proof.

Recall that by symmetry one has
\varphi_{p}(\Lambda_{n})\leq 2d\varphi_{p}(\mathbb{H}_{n})
. Combining this observation with (
1.19
) gives the upper bounds in (
2.6
) and (
2.7
). The lower bounds in these equations follow from (
2.4
). Finally, (
2.8
) follows from (
1.19
) and Lemma
2.1
thanks to (
2.4
).

\Box

2.2
The sharp length

The quantity
L(p)
of (
1.15
) was defined by looking at the first
k\geq 1
for which
\varphi_{p}(\Lambda_{k})
drops below
1/e^{2}
. It can be more practical to consider a slightly different definition in which we rather look at the first
k
for which there exists a
S\subset\Lambda_{k}
such that
\varphi_{p}(S)
“drops”. More precisely, the
sharp length

\tilde{L}(p)
is defined, for every
p<p_{c}
, by

\tilde{L}(p)\coloneqq\min\{k\geq 1:~\exists S\subset\Lambda_{k}\textup{ containing $0$ such that }\varphi_{p}(S)\leq 1/e^{2}\}\,.

(2.9)

Our next result shows that
\tilde{L}(p)
and
L(p)
have the same behaviour as
p
approaches
p_{c}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
d>6
and
L\geq L_{0}
. There exist
c,C>0
such that, for every
p<p_{c}
,

c(p_{c}-p)^{-1/2}\leq\tilde{L}(p)\leq C(p_{c}-p)^{-1/2}.

(2.10)

Proof.

By (
1.18
), it suffices to show that
\tilde{L}(p)\asymp L(p)
. First, observe that (by definition)
\tilde{L}(p)\leq L(p)
.
We now prove that
\tilde{L}(p)\gtrsim L(p)
.

Let
S\subset\Lambda_{\tilde{L}(p)}
be such that
\varphi_{p}(S)\leq 1/e^{2}
. Let
N=(K+1)(\tilde{L}(p)+L)
for some large enough constant
K\geq 1
to be chosen later. We will show that
L(p)\leq N
, which will conclude the proof.
Let
x\in\partial\Lambda_{N}
and observe that

\{0\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda_{N}\>}x\}\subset\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}u_{1}\in S\\
v_{1}\notin S\\
u_{1}\sim v_{1}\end{subarray}}\{0\xleftrightarrow{S\>}u_{1}\}\circ\{u_{1}v_{1}\text{ is open}\}\circ\{v_{1}\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda_{N}\>}x\}.

(2.11)

Using (
2.4
) and (
BK
), we obtain

\varphi_{p}(\Lambda_{N})\lesssim\psi_{p}(\Lambda_{N})\leq\sum_{x\in\partial\Lambda_{N}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u_{1}\in S\\
v_{1}\notin S\\
u_{1}\sim v_{1}\end{subarray}}\tau_{p}^{S}(0,u_{1})\,p\,\tau_{p}^{\Lambda_{N}}(v_{1},x).

(2.12)

By iterating this expansion
K
times, we get (with the convention
v_{0}=0
)

\varphi_{p}(\Lambda_{N})\lesssim\sum_{x\in\partial\Lambda_{N}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u_{1}\in S\\
v_{1}\notin S\\
u_{1}\sim v_{1}\end{subarray}}\tau_{p}^{S}(v_{0},u_{1})\,p\ldots\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u_{K}\in(S+v_{K-1})\\
v_{K}\notin(S+v_{K-1})\\
u_{K}\sim v_{K}\end{subarray}}\tau_{p}^{S}(v_{K-1},u_{K})\,p\,\tau_{p}^{\Lambda_{N}}(v_{K},x).

(2.13)

Observe that by construction, any
v_{K}
as above must lie in
\Lambda_{N}
, and

\sum_{x\in{\partial}\Lambda_{N}}\tau_{p}^{\Lambda_{N}}(v_{K},x)=\psi_{p}(\Lambda_{N}-v_{K})\leq\psi_{p_{c}}(\Lambda_{N}-v_{K})\leq 2dp_{c}^{-1}\max_{n\geq 0}\varphi_{p_{c}}(\mathbb{H}_{n})\lesssim 1,

(2.14)

where in the second inequality we used (
2.4
), and in the last one (
1.19
). Plugging (
2.14
) in (
2.13
) and using translation invariance, we obtain

\varphi_{p}(\Lambda_{N})\lesssim\varphi_{p}(S)^{K}\leq e^{-2K}.

(2.15)

If
K
is large enough, we deduce that
\varphi_{p}(\Lambda_{N})\leq 1/e^{2}
, so that
L(p)\leq N\lesssim\tilde{L}(p)
. This concludes the proof.

\Box

2.3
Bounds on the half-space susceptibility

In this subsection, we prove a useful bound in our proof of Theorem
1.2
. We begin with a definition. If
p\in[0,1]
, the half-space susceptibility is defined by

\chi^{\mathbb{H}}(p):=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{H}}\tau_{p}^{\mathbb{H}}(0,x).

(2.16)

The next result should be compared to (
1.17
).

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
d>6
and
L\geq L_{0}
. There exist
c_{2},C_{2}>0
such that, for every
\tfrac{p_{c}}{2}\leq p<p_{c}
,

c_{2}(p_{c}-p)^{-1/2}\leq\chi^{\mathbb{H}}(p)\leq C_{2}(p_{c}-p)^{-1/2}.

(2.17)

Proof.

By translation invariance, one has

\chi^{\mathbb{H}}(p)=\sum_{k\geq 0}\psi_{p}(\mathbb{H}_{k}).

(2.18)

Combining (
2.4
) and Lemma
2.1
, we find that

\psi_{p}(\mathbb{H}_{k})\gtrsim\varphi_{p}(\Lambda_{k}).

(2.19)

In particular, when
k<L(p)
, one has
\psi_{p}(\mathbb{H}_{k})\gtrsim 1
. Plugging this observation in (
2.18
) yields

\chi^{\mathbb{H}}(p)\gtrsim L(p)\gtrsim(p_{c}-p)^{-1/2},

(2.20)

where the second inequality follows from (
1.18
). Using Corollary
2.2
, we find
c,C>0
such that, for every
p_{c}/2\leq p<p_{c}
, and every
k\geq 0
,

\psi_{p}(\mathbb{H}_{k})\leq C(p_{c}/2)^{-1}\exp\left(-c(p_{c}-p)^{-1/2}k\right).

(2.21)

Plugging (
2.21
) in (
2.18
) gives

\chi^{\mathbb{H}}(p)\lesssim\sum_{k\geq 0}\exp\left(-c(p_{c}-p)^{-1/2}k\right)\lesssim(p_{c}-p)^{-1/2}.

(2.22)

This concludes the proof.

\Box

3
One-arm probability in the full-space

In this section we prove Theorem
1.1
. We fix
d>6
and
L\geq L_{0}
, where
L_{0}
is given by Theorem
1.4
. We begin with a proof of the (more interesting) upper bound.

Proof of the upper bound in (
1.3
).

Assume first that
n\geq 2L
.
We let

\theta_{n}(p)=\mathbb{P}_{p}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\Lambda_{n}^{c}].

(3.1)

By applying the entropic bound of Lemma
1.3
with
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
\mathcal{A}:=\{0\xleftrightarrow{}\Lambda_{n}^{c}\}
, we obtain that for any
p_{c}/2\leq p^{\prime}<p_{c}
,

\displaystyle\theta_{n}(p_{c})-\theta_{n}(p^{\prime})\leq C_{0}(p_{c}-p^{\prime})\sqrt{\theta_{n}(p_{c})\chi(p^{\prime})}\,,

(3.2)

where we used the fact that
\theta_{n}(p^{\prime})\leq\theta_{n}(p_{c})
.
Let us now choose
p^{\prime}
, sufficiently far in the near-critical window, i.e.

\displaystyle p^{\prime}:=p_{c}-Kn^{-2}\,,

where
K
is a constant chosen large enough so that, by Corollary
2.2
, one has
\varphi_{p^{\prime}}(\Lambda_{(n/2)-L})\leq 1/8
. Note that, by decomposing an open path from
0
to
\Lambda_{n}^{c}
according to the first edge exiting
\Lambda_{(n/2)-L}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\{0\xleftrightarrow{}\Lambda_{n}^{c}\}&\subset\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\Lambda_{(n/2)-L}\\
y\notin\Lambda_{(n/2)-L}\\
x\sim y\end{subarray}}\{0\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda_{(n/2)-L}\>}x\}\circ\{xy\textup{ open}\}\circ\{y\xleftrightarrow{}\Lambda_{n}^{c}\}\\
&\subset\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\Lambda_{(n/2)-L}\\
y\notin\Lambda_{(n/2)-L}\\
x\sim y\end{subarray}}\{0\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda_{(n/2)-L}\>}x\}\circ\{xy\textup{ open}\}\circ\{y\xleftrightarrow{}(\Lambda_{n/2}+y)^{c}\},\end{split}

(3.3)

so by (
BK
) and translation invariance, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\theta_{n}(p^{\prime})&\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\Lambda_{(n/2)-L}\\
y\notin\Lambda_{(n/2)-L}\\
x\sim y\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda_{(n/2)-L}\>}x]p^{\prime}\mathbb{P}_{p^{\prime}}[y\xleftrightarrow{}(\Lambda_{n/2}+y)^{c}]\\
&=\varphi_{p^{\prime}}(\Lambda_{(n/2)-L})\theta_{n/2}(p^{\prime})\leq\frac{1}{8}\theta_{n/2}(p_{c})\,,\end{split}

(3.4)

where in the last line we used that
\theta_{n/2}(p^{\prime})\leq\theta_{n/2}(p_{c})
.
Plugging this into (
3.2
), and recalling that
\chi(p^{\prime})\leq C_{1}K^{-1}n^{2}
by Theorem
1.4
, gives, for every
n\geq 2L

\theta_{n}(p_{c})\leq\frac{1}{8}\theta_{n/2}(p_{c})+C_{0}(C_{1}K)^{1/2}n^{-1}\sqrt{\theta_{n}(p_{c})}\,.

(3.5)

Let
k_{0}\geq 1
be such that
2^{k_{0}}\geq 2L>2^{k_{0}-1}
.
We claim that the inequality above implies that, for every
k\geq k_{0}
,

\theta_{2^{k}}(p_{c})\leq\frac{A}{(2^{k})^{2}},

(3.6)

where
A:=\max\{4C_{0}^{2}C_{1}K,(2^{k_{0}})^{2}\}
. This in turn readily implies the desired bound. Indeed, for every
n\geq 1
, if
n\leq 2^{k_{0}}
, then
\theta_{n}(p_{c})\leq 1\leq An^{-2}
by choice of
A
. Otherwise, there is
k\geq k_{0}
such that
2^{k}\leq n<2^{k+1}
, so that
\theta_{n}(p_{c})\leq\theta_{2^{k}}(p_{c})\leq A2^{-2k}\leq 4An^{-2}
, as desired.

Let us now prove that (
3.6
) holds by induction. First, since
\theta_{2^{k_{0}}}(p_{c})\leq 1\leq A/(2^{k_{0}})^{2}
, (
3.6
) is trivially true for
k=k_{0}
. Assuming it is true for a given
k\geq k_{0}
and applying (
3.5
) for
n=2^{k+1}
, we obtain

\theta_{n}(p_{c})\leq\frac{A}{2n^{2}}+\frac{\sqrt{A}}{2n}\sqrt{\theta_{n}(p_{c})}\implies\left(\sqrt{\theta_{n}(p_{c})}-\frac{\sqrt{A}}{4n}\right)^{2}\leq\frac{9A}{16n^{2}}\implies\theta_{n}(p_{c})\leq\frac{A}{n^{2}}\,.

(3.7)

This concludes the proof.

\Box

We now prove the lower bound in Theorem
1.1
. Our argument only uses the lower bound
\tilde{L}(p)\gtrsim(p_{c}-p)^{-1/2}
. We note that an alternative—and technically simpler—proof could be obtained via a second-moment method, relying on pointwise estimates on the critical two-point function (as first carried out in
[
Sak04
]
).

Proof of the lower bound in (
1.3
).

Again, we let
\theta_{n}(p)=\mathbb{P}_{p}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\Lambda_{n}^{c}]
.
We will use the following differential inequality, which is proved in
[
DCT16
, Lemma 1.4]
: there exists a constant
c=c(d,L)>0
such that for every
p\in[p_{c}/2,1]
and
n\geq 1
, one has

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}p}\theta_{n}(p)\geq c\inf_{0\in S\subset\Lambda_{n}}\varphi_{p}(S)(1-\theta_{n}(p))\,.

(3.8)

Recall from Proposition
2.3
that
\tilde{L}(p)\asymp(p_{c}-p)^{-1/2}
. By the definition of
\tilde{L}(p)
, this implies that there exists
c_{3}>0
such that for every
n
large enough and every
p\in(p_{c}-\frac{c_{3}}{n^{2}},p_{c}]
, one has

\varphi_{p}(S)\geq 1/e^{2}\,,~~~\forall\,S\subset\Lambda_{n}\,\textup{ containing $0$}.

(3.9)

Moreover, by the upper bound on
\theta_{n}(p_{c})
we just proved, the sequence
(\theta_{n}(p_{c}))_{n\geq 1}
tends to
0
, and therefore for every
n
large enough and
p\leq p_{c}
, one has
1-\theta_{n}(p)\geq\tfrac{1}{2}
. Plugging this observation and (
3.9
) into (
3.8
) gives
c_{2}>0
such that, for every
n
large enough, and every
p\in(p_{c}-\tfrac{c_{3}}{n^{2}},p_{c}]
, one has

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}p}\theta_{n}(p)\geq c_{4}\,.

(3.10)

Integrating this over
p\in(p_{c}-\tfrac{c_{3}}{n^{2}},p_{c}]
, we obtain, for every
n
large enough,

\theta_{n}(p_{c})\geq\frac{c_{3}c_{4}}{n^{2}}\,.

(3.11)

This concludes the proof.

\Box

We now turn to the proof of Theorem
1.2
. We fix
d>6
and
L\geq L_{0}
, where
L_{0}
is given by Theorem

.

4.1
Proof of the lower bound

Our goal in this section is to prove the lower bound in (
1.7
).
The proof is based on a second moment method. This would be a relatively easy task if we knew precise estimates on the two point function restricted to
\mathbb{H}
. However, such estimates are difficult to obtain. In fact, they were first proved
[
CH20
]
(see also
[
CHS23
]
), through a delicate analysis which is based, in particular, on the results of
[
KN11
]
.
Instead of relying on such pointwise bounds on the restricted two-point function, we provide a softer proof which only uses the integrated estimate provided by (
2.8
) as an input.

Proof of the lower bound in (
1.7
).

Let
n\geq 1
.
Consider the
n
-slab defined as

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}_{n}:=\mathbb{H}\setminus\mathbb{H}_{-(n+1)}.

(4.1)

We will apply the second moment method to the following random variable

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{n}:=\#\{x\in{\partial}\mathbb{H}_{-n}:0\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{S}_{n}\>}x\}.

(4.2)

Lemma 4.1
.

For every
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathcal{N}_{n}^{2}\bigr]}\lesssim n.

(4.3)

Lemma 4.2
.

For every
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathcal{N}_{n}\bigr]}\gtrsim\frac{1}{n}.

(4.4)

Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
readily imply the desired lower bound: by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{H}\>}\Lambda_{n-1}^{c}]\geq\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{S}_{n}\>}\partial\mathbb{H}_{-n}]=\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[\mathcal{N}_{n}\geq 1]\geq\frac{{\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathcal{N}_{n}\bigr]}^{2}}{{\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathcal{N}_{n}^{2}\bigr]}}\gtrsim\frac{1}{n^{3}}.

(4.5)

\Box

Proof of Lemma
4.1
.

By definition,

{\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathcal{N}_{n}^{2}\bigr]}=\sum_{x,y\in\partial\mathbb{H}_{-n}}\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{S}_{n}\>}x,~0\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{S}_{n}\>}y].

(4.6)

By considering the last intersection between a pair of open paths in
\mathbb{S}_{n}
from
0
to
x
and from
0
to
y
, we deduce that
\{0\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{S}_{n}\>}x\}\cap\{0\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{S}_{n}\>}y\}\subset\bigcup_{u\in\mathbb{S}_{n}}\{0\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{S}_{n}\>}u\}\circ\{u\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{S}_{n}\>}x\}\circ\{u\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{S}_{n}\>}y\}
. Applying (
BK
), we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathcal{N}_{n}^{2}\bigr]}&\leq\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{u\in\partial\mathbb{H}_{-k}}\sum_{x,y\in\partial\mathbb{H}_{-n}}\tau_{p_{c}}^{\mathbb{S}_{n}}(0,u)\tau_{p_{c}}^{\mathbb{S}_{n}}(u,x)\tau_{p_{c}}^{\mathbb{S}_{n}}(u,y)\\
&\leq\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{u\in\partial\mathbb{H}_{-k}}\sum_{x,y\in\partial\mathbb{H}_{-n}}\tau_{p_{c}}^{\mathbb{H}}(0,u)\tau_{p_{c}}^{(\mathbb{H}_{-n})^{c}}(u,x)\tau_{p_{c}}^{(\mathbb{H}_{-n})^{c}}(u,y)\\
&=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\psi_{p_{c}}(\mathbb{H}_{k})\psi_{p_{c}}(\mathbb{H}_{n-k})^{2}\lesssim n,\end{split}

(4.7)

where in the second line we used inclusion, and in the third line we used translation invariance and (
2.8
).

\Box

Proof of Lemma
4.2
.

Let
n\geq 1
. On the one hand, combining (
2.8
) and translation invariance gives

\sum_{x\in\partial\mathbb{H}_{-n}}\tau_{p_{c}}^{\mathbb{H}}(0,x)=\psi_{p_{c}}(\mathbb{H}_{n})\gtrsim 1.

(4.8)

On the other hand, by considering the step immediately before the first intersection of an open path from
0
to
x
with
\partial\mathbb{H}_{-n}
, we deduce that

\{0\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{H}\>}x\}\subset\bigcup_{1\leq k\leq L}\bigcup_{u\in\partial\mathbb{H}_{-n+k}}\{0\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{S}_{n}\>}u\}\circ\{u\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{H}\>}x\}.

(4.9)

Applying (
BK
), we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{x\in\partial\mathbb{H}_{-n}}\tau_{p_{c}}^{\mathbb{H}}(0,x)&\leq\sum_{k=1}^{L}\sum_{x\in\partial\mathbb{H}_{-n}}\sum_{u\in\partial\mathbb{H}_{-n+k}}\tau_{p_{c}}^{\mathbb{S}_{n}}(0,u)\tau_{p_{c}}^{\mathbb{H}}(u,x)\\
&=\sum_{k=1}^{L}\Bigg(\sum_{u\in\partial\mathbb{H}_{-n+k}}\tau_{p_{c}}^{\mathbb{S}_{n}}(0,u)\Bigg)\Bigg(\sum_{y\in\partial\mathbb{H}_{k}}\tau_{p_{c}}^{\mathbb{H}_{n}}(0,y)\Bigg),\end{split}

(4.10)

where in the last line we used translation invariance to obtain, for each
u\in\partial\mathbb{H}_{-n+k}
,

\sum_{x\in\partial\mathbb{H}_{-n}}\tau^{\mathbb{H}}_{p_{c}}(u,x)=\sum_{y\in\partial\mathbb{H}_{k}}\tau_{p_{c}}^{\mathbb{H}_{n}}(0,y).

(4.11)

Fix
1\leq k\leq L
and
y\in\partial\mathbb{H}_{k}
. By decomposing an open path from
0
to
y
according to the left-most vertex it visits, we obtain
\{0\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{H}_{n}\>}y\}\subset\bigcup_{k\leq i\leq n,\,v\in\partial\mathbb{H}_{i}}\{0\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{H}_{i}\>}v\}\circ\{v\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{H}_{i}\>}y\}
. Applying (
BK
), we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{y\in\partial\mathbb{H}_{k}}\tau_{p_{c}}^{\mathbb{H}_{n}}(0,y)&\leq\sum_{i=k}^{n}\sum_{v\in\partial\mathbb{H}_{i}}\sum_{y\in\partial\mathbb{H}_{k}}\tau_{p_{c}}^{\mathbb{H}_{i}}(0,v)\tau_{p_{c}}^{\mathbb{H}_{i}}(v,y)\\
&=\sum_{i=k}^{n}\psi_{p_{c}}(\mathbb{H}_{i})\psi_{p_{c}}(\mathbb{H}_{i-k})\lesssim n,\end{split}

(4.12)

where in the second line we used translation invariance and (
2.8
).
Combining (
4.8
), (
4.10
) and (
4.12
), we obtain

\sum_{k=1}^{L}\sum_{u\in\partial\mathbb{H}_{-n+k}}\tau_{p_{c}}^{\mathbb{S}_{n}}(0,u)\gtrsim n^{-1}.

(4.13)

Now, by a simple comparison argument (similar to that of (
2.5
)), one can easily show that

{\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathcal{N}_{n}\bigr]}=\sum_{x\in\partial\mathbb{H}_{-n}}\tau_{p_{c}}^{\mathbb{S}_{n}}(0,x)\geq\frac{1}{L}\sum_{k=1}^{L}\sum_{u\in\partial\mathbb{H}_{-n+k}}p_{c}\tau_{p_{c}}^{\mathbb{S}_{n}}(0,u),

(4.14)

which combined with (
4.13
) concludes the proof.

\Box

4.2
Proof of the upper bound

The proof of the upper-bound in (
1.7
) is divided into two steps:

1.

We first derive an upper bound on “point to half-space probability”
\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{H}}\mathbb{H}_{-n}]
using the same strategy as in the full-space setting.

2.

Then, combining a suitable averaging trick with the BK inequality, we promote this upper bound to an upper bound on the larger quantity
\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{H}\>}\Lambda_{n}^{c}]
.

Step 1. Point to half-space in
\mathbb{H}
.
We will prove that, for every
n\geq 1
,

\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{H}\>}\mathbb{H}_{-n}]\lesssim n^{-3}.

(4.15)

The proof is very similar to the one of the upper bound in Theorem
1.1
, but there are two key differences. First, we use a
last exit
decomposition instead of
first exit
in order to compare scales
n
and
n/2
in the near-critical regime. Second, we rely on the bound (
2.17
) on the half-space susceptibility, which is smaller than that of the full-space susceptibility (
1.17
). This last point is responsible for the exponent changing from
2
to
3
.

Let
\theta^{\mathbb{H}}_{n}(p):=\mathbf{P}_{p}[0\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{H}\>}\mathbb{H}_{-n}]
and
p^{\prime}:=p_{c}-Kn^{-2}
, for some constant
K
to be chosen large enough below.
By decomposing an open path from
0
to
\mathbb{H}_{-n}
according to the point immediately after the
last
intersection with
\mathbb{H}_{-\frac{n}{2}}^{c}
and applying (
BK
) we obtain

\theta_{n}^{\mathbb{H}}(p^{\prime})\leq\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}\sum_{u\in\partial\mathbb{H}_{-\frac{n}{2}-k}}\tau^{\mathbb{H}}_{p^{\prime}}(0,u)\mathbb{P}_{p^{\prime}}[u\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{H}_{-\frac{n}{2}}\>}\mathbb{H}_{-n}]\,.

(4.16)

By translation invariance and a simple comparison argument (similar to that of (
2.5
)), for every
u
as above we have

\mathbb{P}_{p^{\prime}}[u\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{H}_{-\frac{n}{2}}\>}\partial\mathbb{H}_{-n}]\leq\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[u\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{H}_{-\frac{n}{2}}\>}\partial\mathbb{H}_{-n}]\leq\frac{1}{p_{c}}\theta^{\mathbb{H}}_{n/2}(p_{c}).

(4.17)

Also by translation invariance, for every
k
as above we have

\sum_{u\in\partial\mathbb{H}_{-\frac{n}{2}-k}}\tau^{\mathbb{H}}_{p^{\prime}}(0,u)=\psi_{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{H}_{\frac{n}{2}+k}).

(4.18)

Finally, by (
2.7
), we can choose
K
large enough so that
\frac{1}{p_{c}}\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}\psi_{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{H}_{\frac{n}{2}+k})\leq 1/16
.
Combining these observations with (
4.16
), we obtain

\theta_{n}^{\mathbb{H}}(p^{\prime})\leq\frac{1}{16}\theta^{\mathbb{H}}_{\frac{n}{2}}(p_{c})\,.

(4.19)

Applying Lemma
1.3
to
\Lambda=\mathbb{H}
and
\mathcal{A}=\{0\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{S}_{n}\>}\partial\mathbb{H}_{-n}\}
, and recalling that
\chi^{\mathbb{H}}(p^{\prime})\leq C_{2}(p_{c}-p^{\prime})^{-1/2}
by (
2.17
), we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\theta_{n}^{\mathbb{H}}(p_{c})&\leq\theta_{n}^{\mathbb{H}}(p^{\prime})+C_{0}(p_{c}-p^{\prime})\sqrt{\theta_{n}^{\mathbb{H}}(p_{c})\chi^{\mathbb{H}}(p^{\prime})}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{16}\theta^{\mathbb{H}}_{\frac{n}{2}}(p_{c})+C_{0}(C_{2}K)^{1/2}n^{-3/2}\sqrt{\theta_{n}^{\mathbb{H}}(p_{c})}\,.\end{split}

(4.20)

Arguing exactly as in full-space case, the inequality above easily implies (
4.15
) by induction.
\Box

Step 2. Point to box in
\mathbb{H}
.
We first assume that
n\geq 6L
.
First, we notice that the event
\{0\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{H}\>}\Lambda_{n-1}^{c}\}
can be achieved either by connecting
0
to
\mathbb{H}_{-n}
within
\mathbb{H}
(which is already taken care of by our first step), or by connecting
0
to one of the
2(d-1)
sides of
\Lambda_{n}
in the directions of the basis vectors
\mathbf{e}_{2},\ldots,\mathbf{e}_{d}
. Without loss of generality, let us focus on the
\mathbf{e}_{2}
direction. For every
k\geq 1
, let
S_{k}
be the
slice
defined by

\displaystyle S_{k}:=\{x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\text{ s.t. }0\leq x_{1}<n+L\text{ and }x_{2}=k\}

(4.21)

By symmetry and union bound and taking into account the
L
-range of our spread-out model, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{H}\>}\Lambda_{n-1}^{c}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{H}\>}\mathbb{H}_{-n}]+2(d-1)\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{H}\setminus\mathbb{H}_{-n}\>}S_{n+j}]\,.

(4.22)

An illustration of the decomposition used in (
4.23
)

For each
0\leq j<L
and each fixed
\frac{n}{4}\leq k\leq\frac{n}{3}
, on the event
\{0\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{H}\>}S_{n+j}\}
, there exists
k^{\prime}
with
|k^{\prime}-k|\leq L/2
and
x\in S_{k^{\prime}}
such that

x
is connected to
S_{n+j}
inside
\bigcup_{\ell=k^{\prime}}^{n+j}S_{\ell}
and
x
is connected to 0 inside
\mathbb{H}
. (
x
can be found as the last visit of the set
\bigcup_{|k^{\prime}-k|\leq L/2}S_{k^{\prime}}
along some exploration procedure of the cluster of
0
inside
\mathbb{H}
). See Figure
1
for an illustration.
By union bound and (
BK
), we thus find, for every
\frac{n}{4}\leq k\leq\frac{n}{3}
that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{H}\setminus\mathbb{H}_{-n}}S_{n+j}]&\leq\sum_{k^{\prime}:|k^{\prime}-k|\leq L/2}\sum_{x\in S_{k^{\prime}}}\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{H}\>}x]\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[x\xleftrightarrow{\bigcup_{\ell=k^{\prime}}^{n+j}S_{\ell}\>}S_{n+j}]\\
&\leq\sum_{k^{\prime}:|k^{\prime}-k|\leq L/2}\sum_{x\in S_{k^{\prime}}}\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{H}\>}x]\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{H}\>}\mathbb{H}_{-n/2}]\\
&\lesssim\frac{1}{n^{3}}\sum_{k^{\prime}:|k^{\prime}-k|\leq L/2}\sum_{x\in S_{k^{\prime}}}\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{H}\>}x]\,,\end{split}

(4.23)

where the second line uses rotational invariance and the fact that the slices
S_{k^{\prime}}
are at distance at least
n/2
from
S_{n+j}
when
\frac{n}{4}\leq k\leq\frac{n}{3}
and
n\geq 6L
, and the last line uses (
4.15
).

It is natural to expect that for each such
k
, one has
\sum_{|k^{\prime}-k|\leq L/2}\sum_{x\in S_{k^{\prime}}}\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{H}\>}x]\lesssim 1
. We do not know how to show this with our assumptions. To overcome this, we average (
4.23
) over
k\in\{\lfloor\frac{n}{4}\rfloor,\ldots,\lfloor\frac{n}{3}\rfloor\}
. This gives

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{H}\setminus\mathbb{H}_{-n}\>}S_{n+j}]&\leq\frac{L}{\lfloor\frac{n}{3}\rfloor-\lfloor\frac{n}{4}\rfloor+1}\frac{1}{n^{3}}\sum_{k^{\prime}=\lfloor\frac{n}{4}\rfloor-\frac{L}{2}}^{\lfloor\frac{n}{3}\rfloor+\frac{L}{2}}\sum_{x\in S_{k^{\prime}}}\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{H}\>}x]\\

By plugging this into (

Appendix A
Percolation density, volume and magnetisation

In this Appendix we present a short proof, due to Hutchcroft
[
Hut22
]
, of mean-field upper bounds for the percolation density, volume and magnetisation. These bounds were first obtained by Barsky and Aizenman
[
BA91
]
through a delicate use of differential inequalities and under the triangle condition (
1.6
).
The proof below relies on the entropic bound of Lemma
1.3
and only uses as an input the mean-field upper bound on the susceptibility, namely
\chi(p)\lesssim(p_{c}-p)^{-1}
for every
p<p_{c}
.
Let us mention that the main goal of
[
Hut22
]
is to derive upper bounds on
\chi(p)
from upper bounds on the triangle diagram in a quantitatively better way than in
[
BA91
]
. In our case, such a bound on the susceptibility is directly provided by Theorem
1.4
.

We start by defining the magnetisation
M(p,h)
, introduced in
[
AB87
]
by analogy with the corresponding notion for the Ising model. Consider the
ghost vertex

\mathfrak{g}
and the augment percolation on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cup\{\mathfrak{g}\}
defined as follows: edges in
E=E_{L}
are, as before, independently open with probability
p
, and additionally for every
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, the edge
x\mathfrak{g}
is open independently of all the other edges with probability
1-e^{-h}
. Then, calling
\mathbb{P}_{p,h}
the percolation measure on this enlarged graph, we define

M(p,h)=\mathbb{P}_{p,h}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\mathfrak{g}].

(A.1)

Notice that this quantity is directly related to the distribution of the volume of the cluster of the origin in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Indeed, for
p\leq p_{c}
and
h\geq 0
:

M(p,h):=\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{P}_{p}[|\mathcal{C}(0)|=n](1-e^{-nh}).

(A.2)

It is well known that the mean-field lower bounds
\theta(p)\geq c(p-p_{c})
, for every
p>p_{c}
, and
M(p_{c},h)\geq ch^{1/2}
for every
h>0
, hold in all dimensions
d\geq 2
.
They were derived through a differential inequality approach in
[
AB87
]
(see also
[
DCT16
]
for an alternative proof).
The following result shows that the corresponding upper bounds hold in dimensions
d>6
.

Theorem A.1
.

Let
d>6
. Let
L_{0}
be given by Theorem
1.4
, and let
L\geq L_{0}
. There exists
A\in(0,\infty)
such that, for every
p>p_{c}
,

\theta(p)\leq A(p-p_{c}),

(A.3)

and, for every
n\geq 1
,

\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[|\mathcal{C}(0)|\geq n]\leq An^{-1/2}\,.

(A.4)

In particular, for every
h\geq 0
,

M(p_{c},h)\leq\sqrt{\pi}Ah^{1/2}.

(A.5)

Proof of (
A.3
).

Let
p>p_{c}
and consider the symmetrical subcritical parameter
p^{\prime}:=p_{c}-(p-p_{c})
. Applying Lemma
2.1
for
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
\mathcal{A}=\{0\xleftrightarrow{}\infty\}
, we obtain

\theta(p)=\theta(p)-\theta(p^{\prime})\leq C_{0}(p-p^{\prime})\sqrt{\theta(p)\chi(p^{\prime})}\leq 2CC_{0}^{1/2}(p-p_{c})^{1/2}\sqrt{\theta(p)},

where in the last inequality we used that
\chi(p^{\prime})\leq C_{1}(p-p_{c})^{-1}
by Theorem
1.4
.
The inequality above readily implies that
\theta(p)\leq 4C_{0}^{2}C_{1}(p-p_{c})
, which concludes the proof.

\Box

Proof of (
A.4
).

Applying Lemma
2.1
for

p^{\prime}:=p_{c}-n^{-1/2}
,
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, and
\mathcal{A}=\{|\mathcal{C}(0)|\geq n\}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[|\mathcal{C}(0)|\geq n]-\mathbb{P}_{p^{\prime}}[|\mathcal{C}(0)|\geq n]&\leq C_{0}n^{-1/2}\sqrt{\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[|\mathcal{C}(0)|\geq n]\chi(p^{\prime})}\\
&\leq C_{0}C_{1}^{1/2}n^{-1/4}\sqrt{\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[|\mathcal{C}(0)|\geq n]}\,,\end{split}

(A.6)

where in the second line we used that
\chi(p^{\prime})\leq C_{1}n^{1/2}
by Theorem
1.4
. By Markov’s inequality, we have

\mathbb{P}_{p^{\prime}}[|\mathcal{C}(0)|\geq n]\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}_{p^{\prime}}[|\mathcal{C}(0)|]}{n}=\frac{\chi(p^{\prime})}{n}\leq C_{1}n^{-1/2}\,.

(A.7)

Combining (
A.6
) and (
A.7
), we obtain

\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[|\mathcal{C}(0)|\geq n]\leq C_{1}n^{-1/2}+C_{0}C_{1}^{1/2}n^{-1/4}\sqrt{\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[|\mathcal{C}(0)|\geq n]}\,,

(A.8)

from which (
A.4
) follows readily for
A
large enough (depending on
C_{0}
and
C_{1}
).

\Box

Remark A.2
.

A cluster with volume of
n
typically has diameter of order
n^{1/4}
. Since
L(p)\asymp(p_{c}-p)^{-1/2}
, this explains why the relevant near-critical window in the proof above is
p^{\prime}=p_{c}-n^{-1/2}
.

Proof of (
A.5
).

By definition, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}M(p_{c},h)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[|\mathcal{C}(0)|=n](1-e^{-hn})&=\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[|\mathcal{C}(0)|\geq n](e^{-hn}-e^{-h(n+1)})\\
&\leq A(1-e^{-h})\sum_{n\geq 1}n^{-1/2}e^{-hn}\\
&\leq Ah\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-1/2}e^{-ht}dt\\
&=Ahh^{-1/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{-1/2}e^{-s}ds=\sqrt{\pi}Ah^{1/2}\,,\end{split}

where we have used (discrete) integration by parts in the first line, (
A.4
) and the inequality
1-e^{-h}\leq h
in the second line, the fact that
f(t)=t^{-1/2}e^{-ht}
is decreasing for
t\geq 0
in the third line, and the change of variables
s=ht
in the fourth line. This concludes the proof.

\Box

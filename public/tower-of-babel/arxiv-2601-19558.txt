Apolarity for border cactus decompositions
Weronika Buczyńska, Jarosław Buczyński

Abstract
The border apolarity technique was introduced in our earlier work for secant varieties over complex numbers. We extend the theory to cactus varieties of toric varieties over any algebraically closed field. A border cactus decomposition is a mulithomogeneous ideal in the Cox ring (also called the total coordinate ring) of the toric variety that witnesses that a given point is in a specific cactus variety. The definition of such witness uses apolarity and we describe the set of ideals that are credible witnesses for this purpose in terms of a correspondence between the usual Hilbert scheme (parametrising all closed subschemes of the toric variety) and the multigraded Hilbert scheme (parametrising all multihomogeneous ideals in the Cox ring). We also take this opportunity to extend the border apolarity to linear subspaces (in non-border setting, this is equivalent to simultaneous decompositions).

1.1
Motivation and historical remarks about secants

Secants of plane curves (for example, of a circle) have been present in geometry since antiquity.
Versions of secant varieties were studied as early as the first half of the 20th century.
Whitney proved that any smooth manifold
M
of dimension
n
(over
{\mathbb{R}}
) can be embedded smoothly in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n+1}
using projections from a point that lies outside of the differential-geometric version of the second secant variety
[
Pers14
, §3]
.
By a more complicated method, he then showed that it is possible to project once more and obtain an embedding in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
.
Within broadly understood algebraic geometry, Sylvester, Palatini, Terracini, studied the properties of secant varieties and a related notion of
X
-rank (in special cases and not under that name). Later (around 1970), in one of the periods of intensive development of the theory of computational complexity,
X
-ranks (above all, for
X
being the Segre variety of three factors) appeared while studying fast algorithms for multiplying large matrices. At the beginning of the 21st century, geometry and algorithms “met” and researchers in these two fields began to exchange knowledge intensively. It turned out that some important “open” problems for one party were well known and understood by the other, while the geometric understanding of aspects of the algorithms helped to generalise these algorithms widely,
or to prove they cannot be strenghtened due to natural geometric obstructions.
Since 2010 some of these obstructions have a name, they are called cactus varieties, see §
1.2
.

Consequently, the motivation for studying secant varieties and related notions of
X
-rank,
X
-border rank, and cactus varieties comes from their rich history, from
relatively elementary definitions combined with highly non-trivial problems,
from a natural geometric formulation,
but also from numerous applications to practical problems.
You can read more about this for instance in

[
Land12
, Chapt. 1]
,

[
Land17
, Chapt. 1]
.

1.2
Rank, decompositions, and cactus varieties

With the same assumptions about
X
as above the
r
-th
cactus variety
of
X
is:

\mathfrak{K}_{r}\left(X\right):=\bigcup\overline{\left\{\langle R\rangle\mid R\text{ is a $0$-dimensional subscheme of $X$ degree at most $r$}\right\}}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{N}.

Just as secant varieties, also cactus varieties form a natural sequence of reduced schemes starting
from
X=\mathfrak{K}_{1}\left(X\right)
and stabilising at
\mathfrak{K}_{r^{\prime}}\left(X\right)={\mathbb{P}}^{N}
for
r^{\prime}\gg 0

— again, subject to the assumption that
X
is not contained in any hyperplane.
By construction
\sigma_{r}(X)\subset\mathfrak{K}_{r}\left(X\right)
and if
X
is smooth, then for some
small values of
r
we have
\sigma_{r}(X)=\mathfrak{K}_{r}\left(X\right)
.
However, for
X
of sufficiently large dimension, the cactus varieties tend to fill out
the ambient projective space
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}
much faster than
the secant varieties
[
BR13
, Thms 3, 4]
,
[
Gałą23a
, Cor. 1.7]
.
Moreover, the cactus varieties form an obstruction for detereminantal equations
to define secant varieties
[
BB14
]
,
[
Gałą17
]
,
[
Gałą23b
, Thm 1.18]
.

The notions of secant and cactus varieties lead to various types of ranks and decompositions of points
F\in{\mathbb{P}}^{N}
:

•

X
-rank
of
F
is
r_{X}(F)=\min\left\{r\mid F\in\left\langle p_{1},\dotsc,p_{r}\right\rangle,p_{i}\in X\right\}
.

•

X
-cactus rank
of
F
is

cr_{X}(F)=\min\left\{\deg R\mid R\subset X\text{ is a subscheme},\dim R=0,F\in\left\langle R\right\rangle\right\}.

•

A
(minimal) decomposition
of
F

is any set
\left\{p_{1},\dotsc,p_{r_{X}(F)}\right\}

with
p_{i}\in X
such that
F\in\left\langle p_{1},\dotsc,p_{r_{X}(F)}\right\rangle
.

•

A
cactus decomposition
of
F

is any subscheme
R\subset X
such that
\dim R=0
,
\deg R=cr_{X}(F)
such that
F\in\left\langle R\right\rangle
.

1.3
Apolarity: towards border decompositions

In this article we are primarily interested in border analogues of the above notions of ranks and decompositions.
To define them precisely we need to recall the concept of apolarity.
Suppose

S={\Bbbk}[\alpha_{1},\dotsc,\alpha_{m}]
be a polynomial ring.
Consider
\widetilde{S}={\Bbbk}[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{m}]
the vector space of polynomials in another set of variables
x_{i}
.
For now we ignore the multiplicative structure of
\widetilde{S}
and we work only with its vector space structure.
For
I=(i_{1},\dotsc,i_{m})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{m}
we denote by
\alpha^{I}
the monomial

\alpha_{1}^{i_{1}}\alpha_{2}^{i_{2}}\dotsm\alpha_{m}^{i_{m}}
and by
x^{(I)}:=x_{1}^{i_{1}}x_{2}^{i_{2}}\dotsm x_{m}^{i_{m}}
.
We have an action
\lrcorner
of
S
on
\widetilde{S}
defined in coordinates as

\alpha^{I}\lrcorner x^{(J)}=\begin{cases}x^{(J-I)}&\text{if }J-I\in{\mathbb{N}}^{m}\\
0&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

and we extend it bilinearly to a map
S\times\widetilde{S}\to\widetilde{S}
.
This action is called the
apolarity action
and we discuss it in Section
4.1
.

For any
F\in\widetilde{S}
we define the apolar ideal
\operatorname{Ann}\left(F\right)\subset S
:

\operatorname{Ann}\left(F\right)=\left\{\Theta\in S\mid\Theta\lrcorner F=0\right\}.

Note that for any non-zero constant
c\in{\Bbbk}
we have
\operatorname{Ann}\left(c\cdot F\right)=\operatorname{Ann}\left(F\right)
.
Moreover, if
S
is a graded ring by some finitely generated abelian group and
\tilde{S}
is graded in the same way (that is
\deg\alpha_{i}=\deg x_{i}
for each
i
), then for a homogeneous
F
the ideal
\operatorname{Ann}\left(F\right)
is homogeneous.
Similar property applies to appropriate group actions: whenever the actions on
S
and
\tilde{S}
are compatibile, and
F
is invariant, then also
\operatorname{Ann}\left(F\right)
is invariant.

Suppose now
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}\left(H^{0}(X,L)^{*}\right)
is a smooth toric projective variety embedded via complete linear system of a very ample line bundle
L
.
The cases of major interest include Segre, Veronese and Segre–Veronose varieties, that is
X={\mathbb{P}}^{a_{1}}\times\dotsb\times{\mathbb{P}}^{a_{k}}
and
L={\mathcal{O}}_{X}(d_{1},\dotsc,d_{k})
for some positive integers
k
,
a_{i}
, and
d_{i}

(Segre case is when all
d_{i}=1
, and Veronese case is when
k=1
).
These cases are connected to applications to tensors, as explained in the introduction to
[
BB21
]
.

Let
S=S[X]
be the Cox ring of
X
,
so that
S=\bigoplus_{D\in\Pic(X)}H^{0}(X,D)\simeq{\Bbbk}[\alpha_{1},\dotsc,\alpha_{m}]
, where
\Pic(X)\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}^{\rho}
is the grading group and
m=\dim X+\rho
.
Then using the duality determined by apolarity we also have

\widetilde{S}=\bigoplus_{D\in\Pic(X)}H^{0}(X,D)^{*}
and for any
F\in{\mathbb{P}}\left(H^{0}(X,L)^{*}\right)
the apolar ideal
\operatorname{Ann}\left(F\right)\subset S
is a homogeneous ideal with respect to the grading determined by
\Pic(X)
.
Moreover, the automorphism group of
X
acts on
S
,
\widetilde{S}
, and
{\mathbb{P}}\left(H^{0}(X,L)^{*}\right)
in a compatibile way, and if
F\in{\mathbb{P}}\left(H^{0}(X,L)^{*}\right)
is invariant under some subgroup
B\subset\Aut(X)
, then also
\operatorname{Ann}\left(F\right)\subset S
is preserved under the action of
B
on
S
.

For each subscheme
R\subset X
we define
its homogeneous ideal
I(R)\subset S
to be generated by sections that vanish on
R
:

I(R):=\left(s\in S_{D}\simeq H^{0}(X,D)\mid D\in\Pic(X),s|_{R}=0\in H^{0}(R,D|_{R})\right).

Ideals of the form
I(R)
are
saturated
.
Note that
\operatorname{Ann}\left(F\right)
is never saturated.
Then the apolarity theory
(in particular, Proposition
4.4
)
implies the following alternative definitions of decompositions and ranks, that shift attention from schemes or finite collections of points to ideals in
S
. Fix
F\in{\mathbb{P}}\left(H^{0}(X,L)^{*}\right)
and a subscheme
R\subset X
. We have:

•

F\in\left\langle R\right\rangle
if and only if
I(R)\subset\operatorname{Ann}\left(F\right)
.

•

\left\{p_{1},\dotsc,p_{r(F)}\right\}

is a decomposition if and only if

I(p_{1},\dotsc,p_{r(F)})\subset\operatorname{Ann}\left(F\right)
.

•

R
is a cactus decomposition
if and only if

I(R)\subset\operatorname{Ann}\left(F\right)
and
for all
D\in\Pic(X)
we have

\dim_{{\Bbbk}}(S/I)_{D}\leq r
.

•

The
X
-rank of
F
is equal to

\min\left\{r\mid\exists I\subset S\text{ homog., radical, saturated, }\forall_{D}\dim_{{\Bbbk}}(S/I)_{D}\leq r\text{, and }I\subset\operatorname{Ann}\left(F\right)\right\}.

•

The
X
-cactus rank of
F
is equal to

\min\left\{r\mid\exists I\subset S\text{ homogeneous, saturated, }\forall_{D}\dim_{{\Bbbk}}(S/I)_{D}\leq r\text{, and }I\subset\operatorname{Ann}\left(F\right)\right\}.

1.4
Main results

With these ideas in mind, we define the border decomposition of
F
to be an appropriate ideal of
S
contained in
\operatorname{Ann}\left(F\right)
and witnessing the membership of
F\in\sigma_{r}(X)
.
Our first result is the generalisation of border apolarity
[
BB21
, Thm 1.2]
to fields of any characteristics.

Theorem 1.1

(Weak border apolarity)
.

Suppose
F\in\sigma_{r}(X)
.
Then there exists a homogeneous ideal
I\subset S
such that:

•

I\subset\operatorname{Ann}\left(F\right)
,

•

for each multidegree
D
the
D
-th graded piece
I_{D}
of
I

has codimension (in
S[X]_{D}
) equal to
\min(r,\dim S[X]_{D})
.

Moreover, if
B
is a connected solvable group acting on
X
and preserving
F
,
then there exists an
I
as above which in addition is invariant under

B
.

The main nontrivial difference between this statement and the arguments in
[
BB21
]

is in a strengthening of
[
BB21
, Lem. 3.9]
about a very general tuple of points to an analogous claim for a general tuple of points.
This is dealt with in Theorem
2.12
.
The second result generalises the border decompositions above to border cactus decomposition.

Theorem 1.2

(Weak Apolarity for Border Cactus Decompositions, or weak ABCD)
.

Fix a toric variety
X
as above and a positive integer
r
.
Then there exists a finite collection
\left\{h_{1},\dotsc,h_{k}\right\}
of Hilbert functions

h_{i}\colon\Pic(X)\to{\mathbb{N}}

that have the following properties.
Firstly, they are Hilbert functions of a finite subscheme
R_{i}\subset X
of degree
r
, that is
h_{i}(D)=\dim_{{\Bbbk}}(S/I(R_{i}))_{D}
.
Secondly, suppose
L
is a very ample line bundle and

F\in{\mathbb{P}}(H^{0}(X,L)^{*})
.
If
F\in\mathfrak{K}_{r}\left(X\right)
(with
X
embedded using the linear system
|L|
)
then there exists a homogeneous ideal

I\subset S[X]

with Hilbert function
h_{i}
for some
i
,
such that
I\subset\operatorname{Ann}\left(F\right)
.

The above theorem is a consequence of stronger Theorem
1.4
,
which is an “if and only if” statement.
For this purpose, we compare the multigraded Hilbert scheme

\Hilb(S[X])
and the usual Hilbert scheme
{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb(X)
.

Proposition 1.3
.

For each irreducible component

{\mathcal{H}}\subset{{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb(X)}_{\operatorname{red}}

there is a unique irreducible component
\mathbf{H}_{{\mathcal{H}}}\subset{\Hilb(S[X])}_{\operatorname{red}}
such that:

•

a general ideal
I\in\mathbf{H}_{{\mathcal{H}}}
is saturated,

•

the natural map
\sch\colon\Hilb(S[X])\to{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb(X)

taking a homogeneous ideal
I\subset S[X]

to the subscheme of
X
defined by
I

restricts to a birational morphism

\mathbf{H}_{{\mathcal{H}}}\to{\mathcal{H}}.

For each
{\mathcal{H}}
as in the proposition, there is an integer valued function

h_{{\mathcal{H}}}\colon\Pic(X)\to{\mathbb{N}}

which is the Hilbert function of any
I\in\mathbf{H}_{{\mathcal{H}}}
.
Equivalently,
if
[R]\in{\mathcal{H}}
is a general element, and
R\subset X
is the corresponding scheme, then

h_{{\mathcal{H}}}
is the Hilbert function of
I_{R}\subset S[X]
, see Theorem
2.10
.

Theorem 1.4

(Apolarity for Border Cactus Decompositions, or ABCD)
.

Suppose
F\in{\mathbb{P}}\left(H^{0}(X,L)^{*}\right)
.
Then
F\in\mathfrak{K}_{r}\left(X\right)
if and only if for some irreducible component

{\mathcal{H}}\subset{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{r}(X)
of the Hilbert scheme of points
of length
r
there exists a homogeneous ideal
I\subset S[X]

with
[I]\in\mathbf{H}_{{\mathcal{H}}}
and
I\subset\Ann(F)
.
Moreover,
{\mathcal{H}}
might be chosen to be a component containing
Gorenstein schemes.

Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
,
1.4
are stated and proven more generally, when instead of
F\in{\mathbb{P}}\left(H^{0}(X,L)^{*}\right)
we have a linear subspace
W\subset H^{0}(X,L)^{*}
of any dimension (not necessarily equal to
1
).

2
Multihomogeneous ideals, saturation, and toric varieties

In this section,
we recall a result that under minor homogeneity assumptions the set of saturated ideals in a flat family of multihomogeneous ideals is open.
Moreover, we show that a general configuration of points in a smooth complete toric variety has the expected multigraded Hilbert function.

For the purpose of recursive self referencing we work here over an arbitrary base field

{\mathbb{K}}
of arbitrary characteristics, without assuming it being algebraically closed.
This choice allows us to work with families of ideals paramaterised by various schemes, for instance
\Spec{\Bbbk}[t]
.
We need our arguments to be applicable also to non-closed fibres of our family, for instance, to ideals
in
{\Bbbk}(t)[\alpha_{1},\dotsc,\alpha_{m}]
. In this case
{\mathbb{K}}={\Bbbk}(t)
, which is never algebraically closed.

2.1
Openness of saturation and generic fibre

We work with a polynomial ring
S=S_{{\mathbb{K}}}={\mathbb{K}}[\alpha_{1},\dotsc,\alpha_{m}]
.
Suppose
A
is a finitely generated abelian group,
that is
A\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}^{q}\oplus A^{\tor}
, where
q\geqslant 0
,
A^{\tor}
is a finite abelian group.
An
A
-grading of
S
is a homomorphism (called the
degree
) from the semigroup of monomials in
S_{{\mathbb{K}}}
to
A
.
An
A
-grading is
positive
if the only monomial of degree
0
is
1\in S_{{\mathbb{K}}}
.
Note that the grading is positive if and only if a

S_{D}
is finite dimensional vector space over
{\mathbb{K}}
for all
D\in A
.
In the situations considered in this article we sometimes vary the field
{\mathbb{K}}
, but the degree morphism
is fixed and independent of
{\mathbb{K}}
.

An ideal
I\subset S
is homogeneous (with respect to the
A
-grading) if
I
is generated by homogeneous elements,
that is polynomials, whose all monomials are of the same degree.
Whenever
A
and the degree map are clear, we simply say “homogeneous”.

Fix an
A
-grading of
S
. We consider a
flat family of homogeneous ideals
in

S={\mathbb{K}}[\alpha_{1},\dotsc,\alpha_{m}]
.
That is, let
U
be a
{\mathbb{K}}
-scheme, and consider the free sheaf
S_{U}:={\mathcal{O}}_{U}\otimes_{{\mathbb{K}}}S
of
A
-graded algebras. Let
{\mathcal{I}}\subset S_{U}
be a homogeneous ideal sheaf flat over
U
. For each point
u\in U
we consider the fibre ideal
{\mathcal{I}}_{u}\subset\kappa(u)[\alpha_{1},\dotsc,\alpha_{n}]=\kappa(u)\otimes_{{\mathbb{K}}}S
.
Let
J\subset S
be a fixed homogeneous ideal, and for each
u
let
J_{u}:=\kappa(u)\otimes_{{\mathbb{K}}}J\subset\kappa(u)\otimes_{{\mathbb{K}}}S
be the extension of
J
to the coefficients in
\kappa(u)
. We say that an ideal
I\subset\kappa(u)\otimes_{{\mathbb{K}}}S
is saturated with respect to

(I:J_{u})=I
.

Under some minor assumptions the subset of those

that

is saturated with respect to

is Zariski open in

,
as explained in the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose
S
is a graded polynomial ring and

{\mathcal{I}}
is a flat family of homogeneous ideals in
S

parametrised by a locally Noetherian
{\mathbb{K}}
-scheme

.
Let

be a fixed homogeneous ideal in

.
Then the set

\left\{u\in U\mid{\mathcal{I}}_{u}\text{ is saturated with respect to $J$}\right\}

is an open subset of

.

For the proof see
[

The most relevant case of theorem is when

is the multigraded Hilbert scheme.

Corollary 2.2
.

Suppose that
S
is a positively graded polynomial ring and that

is a homogeneous ideal.
Let

Consider the subset

Then

We now proceed to show that a general fibre of a flat family of schemes determines the whole family (Lemma

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose

and

If

Proof.
We always have

To show the opposite inclusion,
suppose that

Thus for each

Let

Therefore

Since

∎

{\mathcal{Z}}\subset\overline{{\mathcal{Z}}_{\eta}}
as claimed.

Reduction to affine
{\mathcal{Z}}
.

Now assume that the inclusion

{\mathcal{Z}}\subset\overline{{\mathcal{Z}}_{\eta}}
holds whenever
{\mathcal{Z}}
is in addition affine, and let us deduce the case of arbitrary
{\mathcal{Z}}
.

Let

be an affine open subset with the induced scheme structure.
Then the composition

is also flat by

The generic fibre of

Moreover,

by the commutativity of this diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces({\mathcal{Z}}^{\prime})_{\eta}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{Z}}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{Z}}_{\eta}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{Z}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left\{\eta\right\}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U.}
open

Therefore,

{\mathcal{Z}}^{\prime}\subset\overline{{\mathcal{Z}}_{\eta}}

and varying
{\mathcal{Z}}^{\prime}
we get an open covering of
{\mathcal{Z}}
concluding that
{\mathcal{Z}}\subset\overline{{\mathcal{Z}}_{\eta}}
as claimed.

Translating to algebraic condition.

Suppose
{\mathcal{Z}}
and

are affine.
We claim that
that
\overline{{\mathcal{Z}}_{\eta}}={\mathcal{Z}}
if and only if:

\forall_{f\in{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{Z}}}({\mathcal{Z}}),\ g\in{\mathcal{O}}_{U}(U)}\quad\bigl(\phi^{*}(g)\cdot f=0\Rightarrow(f=0\text{ or }g=0)\bigr)

Indeed, we always have

while the other inclusion
is equivalent to vanishing of the ideal of

in

The ideal consists of those

for some non-zero

Proving the algebraic conditon.

Since the morphism

is flat and both

and

are affine,

is a flat

-module.
Suppose that

is non-zero, then the localisation

is injective since

Now we prove that (

If

If

Since

This also concludes the proof of the lemma.

Lemma 2.6
.

Suppose
X
is a variety over
{\Bbbk}
and

2.2
Smooth projective toric varieties

Suppose

{\Bbbk}

Throughout this section we assume in addition that each

the
{\Bbbk}

We remark that for many statements below smoothness of

We study families of subschemes of

Moreover, we frequently restrict to the case of affine base.
Thus let

be a
{\Bbbk}
-scheme, and suppose

In this situation, we say that
{\mathcal{Z}}
is a (not necessarily flat) family of subschemes of

Denote by

There are two related ideal sheaves we consider:

•

the ideal sheaf of
{{\mathcal{Z}}}
embedded in the product:

Remark 2.7
.

(i)

To distinguish the two objects above, we skip the word “sheaf” in the description of

,
despite it is really a sheaf of ideals, and only in the affine case it reduces to an honest ideal in a ring.

(ii)

The relation between
{\mathcal{I}}_{{\mathcal{Z}}}
and

is given by the following formula

\forall_{D\in\Pic X}\ (I_{{\mathcal{Z}}})_{D}=\pi_{U*}({\mathcal{I}}_{{\mathcal{Z}}}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{U\times X}}\pi_{X}^{*}D).

(iii)

Suppose
\phi\colon U^{\prime}\to U
is a morphism of
{\Bbbk}
-scheme and define by
{\mathcal{Z}}^{\prime}\subset U^{\prime}\times_{{\Bbbk}}X

the pullback family.
While the ideal sheaf

the homogeneous ideal is not functorial.
It is always true that
\phi^{*}I_{{\mathcal{Z}}}\subset I_{{\mathcal{Z}}^{\prime}}
,
but

is the saturation of
\phi^{*}I_{{\mathcal{Z}}}
with respect to the irrelevant ideal

discussed below.

An important special case is when

for a field extension

, and

Then

on

that vanish on

.
The

of

Thus if
{\mathcal{U}}
is any
{\Bbbk}
-scheme
and

, then for each point

{\mathcal{Z}}_{u}
(here

).

Define

to be the irrelevant ideal,
that is the radical ideal of

for any very ample line bundle

on
X
.
Throughout the rest of the article we fix

that is, we whenever we say an ideal (or ideal sheaf) is saturated, we mean saturated with respect to

or

Note that for any subscheme

is saturated for any family

of subschemes of

Conversely, for any homogeneous ideal

of

.
Formally, we define

by constructing its ideal sheaf

For any open affine subset

is a zero locus of some

If

It is a formal check that there exists a unique coherent sheaf of ideals

which takes the above values on any product

We define

.
Moreover, the following properties are also straightforward to verify.

Suppose

and

(ii)

(iii)

Theorem 2.10
.

Suppose
U
is a Noetherian finitely dimensional

{\Bbbk}
-scheme and

is a family of subschemes of

(i)

(ii)

the subset

and

whenever

(iii)

, where

Proof.
Without loss of generality we may assume
U
is reduced,
as the statements are all set-theoretic and independent of the scheme structure of
U
.

We first prove a weaker version of
(iii)
:

(iii’)

If
U
is in addition integral, then there exists an open dense subset

such that

for each

.

To prove
(iii’)
, by generic flatness

[
Stac24
,
Tag 052A
]
we may assume that the map

is flat by restricting to a suitable open dense subset of

if necessary.
By Lemma

we may also assume that

I_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{u}}=I_{{\mathcal{Z}}}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{U}}\kappa(u)
for all

.
After these two reductions, we claim we can take

.
Then for each

the

is a flat finitely generated

-module, hence it is locally free, and therefore (since

3
Hilbert schemes, Grassmann relative linear span and cactus varieties

In this section we compare two types of Hilbert schemes.
The “usual” one
{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb(X)
, introduced by Grothendieck
[
Grot95
]
, parametrises flat families of closed subschemes of a given projective variety
X
.
The “multigraded” one
\Hilb(S)
, introduced by Haiman and Sturmfels
[
HS04
]
, parametrises families of multihomogeneous ideals in a graded ring
S
with a locally constant Hilbert function.

The main setting in which both concepts appear is (similarly to Subsection
2.2
) when
X
is a smooth projective toric variety and
S=S[X]
is its Cox ring (again smoothness of
X
is not critical, as in Subsection
2.2
).
Then both schemes
\Hilb(S)
and
{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb(X)
make sense and have projective connected components.
We will see that they are related by a natural morphism
\sch\colon\Hilb(S)\to{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb(X)
which is surjective on points.
Moreover, for each irreducible component of
{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb(X)
there exists a unique irreducible component of
\Hilb(S)
such that the underlying morphism of varieties is birational.

3.1
Grothendieck and multigraded Hilbert schemes

Comprehensive and detailed definitions, constructions, and descriptions of properties of
{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb(X)
can be found in numerous excellent textbooks including

[
FGI+05
, Chapt. 5]
,

[
Jeli17
, Chapt. 4]
,

[
Sern06
, Chapt. 4]
.
See also
[
Koll96
, Chapt. I]
.
In our setting, let
L
be any very ample line bundle on
X
, then
{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb(X)
is a disjoint union
\bigsqcup_{p\in{\mathbb{Q}}[t]}{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{L,p}(X)
where
p
is a polynomial in a single variable with integral values for integer arguments
and
{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{L,p}(X)
is a scheme parameterising subschemes of
X

with Hilbert polynomial
p
(that is, the Euler characteristics of
L^{\otimes t}
restricted to the subscheme is equal
p(t)
).
In this setting (with
X
not necessarily isomorphic to a projective space), it is not necessary that
{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{L,p}(X)
is connected.
Moreover, different choices of
L
might lead to different decompositions into pieces according to Hilbert polynomials, in any case, for each
L
and each
p
,
{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{L,p}(X)
is always a projective scheme over
{\Bbbk}

[
Koll96
, Thm I.1.4]
.

On the other hand the multigraded Hilbert scheme
\Hilb S[X]

is less standard and not so often considered or exploited, perhaps due to the larger complexity and pathologies appearing on much smaller cases.
We also have a disjoint union decomposition

\Hilb S[X]=\bigsqcup_{h\colon\Pic X\to{\mathbb{N}}}\Hilb_{h}S[X]
, where
h
is any function and
\Hilb_{h}S[X]
parametrises all families of multihomogeneous ideals with Hilbert function
h
. Again, each
\Hilb_{h}S[X]
is projective though not necessarily connected.

Notation 3.1

(elements of parameter spaces)
.

For a space parametrising some type of objects
we need to distinguish an element of the space
[\bullet]
and the object
\bullet
it represents.
Explicitly, we will use the Grothendieck Hilbert scheme
\Hilb(S)
, multigraded Hilbert schemes
{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb(X)
, and Grassmannians
\Gr(i,V)
, or their subsets.

•

For a homogeneous ideal
I\subset S

we denote by
[I]
the corresponding
{\Bbbk}
-point of
\Hilb(S)
, and vice versa: if
[I]\in\Hilb(S)({\Bbbk})
, then implicitly
I\subset S
is the ideal represented by
[I]
.

•

For a subscheme
R\subset X

we denote by
[R]
the corresponding
{\Bbbk}
-point of
{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb(X)
, and vice versa as above.

•

If
V
is a vector space, then for a linear subspace
E\subset V
with
\dim E=i

we denote by
[E]\in\Gr(i,V)({\Bbbk})
the corresponding
{\Bbbk}
-point, and vice versa.

To avoid confusion, we refrain from using this convention for non-closed points of the parameter space.

Proposition 3.2
.

There exists a morphism of schemes
\sch\colon\Hilb S[X]\to{{\mathcal{H}}}ilbX
which on points takes a homogeneous ideal
I\subset S[X]
to the scheme
Z(I)\subset X
,
\sch([I])=[Z(I)]
.

A similar statement with a slightly different notation can be found in
[
JM25
, §2.4.1]
.
We include the proof for the sake of completeness.

Proof.
We argue componentwise for each Hilbert function
h\colon\Pic X\to{\mathbb{N}}
separately.
If
\Hilb_{h}S[X]=\emptyset
, then there is nothing to do.
Thus suppose
\Hilb_{h}S[X]\neq\emptyset
and fix a very ample line bundle
L
.
In particular, pick any
[I]\in\Hilb_{h}S[X]

the Hilbert polynomial
p(t)
of
Z(I)
with respect to

agrees with
h(L^{t})
for
t\gg 0
.
Therefore
p
is determined by
h
(and

), and does not depend on the choice of

.
To prove the proposition it is enough to construct

.

The universal ideal

is a homogeneous ideal that
defines a closed subscheme
{\mathcal{R}}:={\mathcal{Z}}(J)\subset\Hilb_{h}S[X]\times X
.
Consider also
I_{\mathcal{R}}\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{\Hilb_{h}S[X]}\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}S[X]
, the homogeneous ideal of
{\mathcal{R}}
.
By Proposition
2.8
(ii)

we have
I_{\mathcal{R}}=J^{\sat}
and by
Proposition
2.8
(iii)

for
t\gg 0
we have:

J_{L^{t}}=(I_{\mathcal{R}})_{L^{t}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{by Remark\penalty\ \ref{rem_relations_ideal_sheal_and_homogeneous_ideal}
\ref{item_relation_between_ideal_sheal_and_homogeneous_ideal}}}}{{=}}(\pi_{\Hilb_{h}S[X]})_{*}\left({\mathcal{I}}_{{\mathcal{R}}}\otimes\pi_{X}^{*}(L^{t})\right)

In particular, since

is a flat
{\mathcal{O}}_{\Hilb_{h}S[X]}
-module, also its direct summands are flat, and hence
(\pi_{\Hilb_{h}S[X]})_{*}\left({\mathcal{I}}_{{\mathcal{R}}}\otimes\pi_{X}^{*}(L^{t})\right)
is flat and finite module, hence locally free of rank

.
Thus
{\mathcal{R}}
is
flat over
\Hilb_{h}S[X]

by
[
Hart77
, Prop. III.9.9]
or
[

, Lem. 5.5]
or
[

, Exercise 24.7.C(b)]
.
Therefore we have a map
\sch\colon\Hilb_{h}S[X]\to{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{L,p}X
as claimed.
∎

Note that the map

is surjective on
{\Bbbk}
-points: if
[R]\in{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb(X)
is a
{\Bbbk}
-point which represents a closed subscheme

, then the multihomogeneous ideal

is an element of

such that

.
However, typically

is
not

a projective morphism,
but only for a silly reason: there could be infinitely many connected components of

that are mapped onto the same connected component of
{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb(X)
.
Once restricted to suitable Hilbert function

and suitable Hilbert polynomial

In the following couple of statements we disscuss a partial inverse of the morphism

, at least for the reduced structures.

Notation 3.3
.

Suppose
{\mathcal{H}}\subset{{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb(X)}_{\operatorname{red}}
is any irreducible component.
Let

be the natural family of subschemes of

parametrised by
{\mathcal{H}}
.
Suppose also that
{\mathcal{H}}^{\circ}\subset{\mathcal{H}}
is the open dense subset of
{\mathcal{H}}

— as in Theorem

—
where the Hilbert function of

.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
,
{\mathcal{H}}^{0}
and

be as in the Notation

as above,

.

Proof.
Consider the universal homogeneous ideal

, so that

.
By the definition of
{\mathcal{H}}^{\circ}
the Hilbert function of

h_{{\mathcal{H}}}
.
Since

(and also

the sheaf

is locally free of rank

Thus by the universal property of the multigraded Hilbert scheme there exists a morphism

∎

Corollary 3.5
.

The closure of the image

\overline{\idealmap({\mathcal{H}}^{\circ})}
is an irreducible component
\mathbf{H}_{{\mathcal{H}}}
of
{\Hilb_{h_{{\mathcal{H}}}}(S[X])}_{\operatorname{red}}
,
and
\sch\colon\mathbf{H}_{{\mathcal{H}}}\to{\mathcal{H}}
is a projective birational morphism.

Proof.
The scheme
\mathbf{H}_{{\mathcal{H}}}\subset\Hilb_{h_{{\mathcal{H}}}}(S[X])
is closed by the definition and irreducible since
{\mathcal{H}}^{\circ}
is irreducible.
Naturally,
\sch|_{\mathbf{H}_{{\mathcal{H}}}}\colon\mathbf{H}_{{\mathcal{H}}}\to{\mathcal{H}}
is a projective morphism, and it admits birational inverse
\idealmap
by Proposition

.
Thus the only claim that remains to be proved is that
\mathbf{H}_{{\mathcal{H}}}\subset\Hilb_{h_{{\mathcal{H}}}}(S[X])
is a component.

Thus let
\mathbf{H}\subset\Hilb_{h_{{\mathcal{H}}}}(S[X])
be any irreducible component containing

.
We claim that

.
Indeed, since
\mathbf{H}_{{\mathcal{H}}}
contains some saturated ideals,

, and the set of saturated ideals is open (Theorem

), there exists an open dense

with only saturated ideals.
Now

is a locally closed immersion by

.
In particular, comparing the dimensions:

\displaystyle\dim\mathbf{H}_{{\mathcal{H}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\dim\mathbf{H}

\displaystyle(\text{since }\mathbf{H}_{{\mathcal{H}}}\subset\mathbf{H})

\displaystyle=\dim U

\displaystyle(\text{since $U$ is open and dense})

\displaystyle=\dim\sch(U)

\displaystyle(\text{since }\sch(U)\subset{\mathcal{H}})

\displaystyle\leqslant\dim\mathbf{H}_{{\mathcal{H}}}

\displaystyle(\text{since $\sch\colon\mathbf{H}_{{\mathcal{H}}}\to{\mathcal{H}}$ is dominant}).

3.2
Linear span map

For a fixed finite dimensional
{\Bbbk}
-vector space
V
,
let
\Gr(k,V)
be the Grassmannian of
k
-dimensional linear subspaces of
V
.
Equivalently,
\Gr(k,V)
parametrises projective linear subspaces of
{\mathbb{P}}(V)
.
Thus if
U
is any base scheme and
{\mathcal{E}}\subset U\times V
is a vector subbundle of rank
k
, then there exists a regular map
U\to\Gr(k,V)
.
Here the vector bundle plays the role of a family of vector spaces parametrised by
U
.

Each subscheme of the projective space
{\mathbb{P}}(V)
has an associated projective linear subspace of
{\mathbb{P}}(V)
(thus a point in the Grassmannian
\Gr(k,V)
for some
k
), namely the linear span of
R
, or more precisely, the smallest linear subspace
\left\langle R\right\rangle\subset{\mathbb{P}}V
such that
R\subset\left\langle R\right\rangle
as subscheme.

Unfortunately, in general the assignment
R\mapsto\left\langle R\right\rangle
cannot be made into a morphism from a Hilbert scheme to the Grassmannians, as flat degeneration
R_{t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t\to 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}R_{0}
can have lower dimension of
\left\langle R_{0}\right\rangle
than that of
\left\langle R_{t}\right\rangle
for general
t
(hence this assignment is not even continuous).
This is an infamous obstruction to efficiently study the secant varieties of subvarieties of projective space.
In the literature there exist essentially two workarounds this obstruction.
The first one is to avoid settings when this obstruction actually occurs:
[
BB14
]
,
[
BBF24
]
,
[
CLPS25
]
, or at least prove that it does not affect your case
[
RV17
, Prop. 3.1]
,
[
JLP23
, Thm 9.2, Rem. 9.3]
.
The second one is coming from usage of multigraded Hilbert scheme instead.
Over
{\Bbbk}={\mathbb{C}}
this latter approach is accomplished in
[
BB21
]
.
Here we show that it also generalises to arbitrary
{\Bbbk}
.

Fix a smooth projective toric variety
X
and a divisor
D\in\Pic(X)
.
For any Hilbert function
h\colon\Pic(X)\to{\mathbb{N}}
we can construct a vector subbundle
{\mathcal{E}}_{D}\subset\Hilb_{h}(S[X])\times S[X]_{D}^{*}
of rank
h(D)

by taking the flat (hence locally free) subsheaf
I_{D}\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{\Hilb_{h}(S[X])}\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}S[X]_{D}
and defining
{\mathcal{E}}_{D}:=(I_{D})^{\perp}
.
Thus by the universal property of the Grassmannian, we obtain a morphism of schemes

\perpmap_{D}\colon\Hilb_{h}(S[X])\to\Gr(h(D),S[X]_{D}^{*})
, and explicitly we have
\perpmap_{D}([J])=[(J_{D})^{\perp}
] for any
[J]\in\Hilb_{h}(S[X])
.
Below we assume that
D
is effective, so that the projective space
{\mathbb{P}}(S[X]_{D}^{*})
is non-empty.

Proposition 3.6
.

Fix a line bundle
D\in\Pic(X)

and an irreducible component

{\mathcal{H}}\subset{{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb(X)}_{\operatorname{red}}
.
Let
\iota\colon X\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}(S[X]_{D}^{*})

be the rational map defined by the complete linear system of
D
,
and let
\mathrm{reg}(\iota)\subset X
be the open subset where
\iota
is regular.
Suppose that at least one of the following conditions holds:

(i)

D
is very ample, or

(ii)

{\mathcal{H}}
parametrises zero dimensional subschemes, or

(iii)

for a general
[R]\in{\mathcal{H}}
, all associated points of
R
are in
\mathrm{reg}(\iota)
.

Then there exists an open dense subset
U\subset{\mathcal{H}}

and a morphism

\spanmap_{D}\colon U\to Gr(h_{{\mathcal{H}}}(D),S[X]_{D}^{*})

such that for any
[R]\in U({\Bbbk})
we have

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\spanmap_{D}([R])\right)=\left[\left\langle\iota(R\cap\mathrm{reg}(\iota))\right\rangle\right]
.
Moreover,
the rational map

\spanmap_{D}\colon{\mathcal{H}}\dashrightarrow\Gr(h_{{\mathcal{H}}}(D),S[X]_{D}^{*})

is resolved by the precomposition with the map

\sch
restricted to
\mathbf{H}_{{\mathcal{H}}}
, which is equal to
(\perpmap_{D})|_{\mathbf{H}_{{\mathcal{H}}}}
.

Proof.
First observe that both
(i)
and
(ii)
imply
that
(iii)
holds.
Indeed, if
D
is very ample then
\mathrm{reg}(\iota)=X

and
(iii)
is vacuous.
Similarly, if
{\mathcal{H}}
parametrises zero dimensional schemes, then, since
X
is smooth,
a general
[R]\in{\mathcal{H}}
has as the support a collection of general points, which are therefore all in
\mathrm{reg}(\iota)
.
Thus, in the rest of the proof, we will assume that
(iii)
holds.

Consider
{\mathcal{H}}^{\circ}
, the open subset as in Notation
3.4
and define

\spanmap_{D}:=\perpmap_{D}\circ\idealmap
.
Let
[R]\in{\mathcal{H}}
be a general point. Then

[R]\in{\mathcal{H}}^{\circ}
and all associated points of
R
are in
\mathrm{reg}(\iota)
.
We claim that the second assumption guarantees

\left\langle\iota(R)\right\rangle=\left\langle\overline{\iota(R)}\right\rangle={\mathbb{P}}\left(I(R)_{D}^{\perp}\right)\subset{\mathbb{P}}\left(S[X]_{D}^{*}\right).

(3.7)

Indeed, if
\Phi\in I(R)_{D}
, then
\phi
considered as a linear form on
{\mathbb{P}}\left(S[X]_{D}^{*}\right)
must
vanish identically on
\iota(R)
, hence also on its linear span.
Conversely, if
R=R_{1}\cup\dotsb\cup R_{k}
with each
R_{i}
a primary scheme,
then
I(R)=I(R_{1})\cap\dotsb\cap I(R_{k})

and for each
i
, the equality of schemes holds:
R_{i}=\overline{R_{i}\cap\mathrm{reg}(\iota)}
.
Thus, if
\Phi\in S[X]_{D}
considered as a linear form on
{\mathbb{P}}\left(S[X]_{D}^{*}\right)
vanishes on
\iota(R)
,
it also vanishes on all
\iota(R_{i})
, and thus
\Phi
vanishes on
R_{i}\cap\mathrm{reg}(\iota)

— where
\Phi
is considered this time as an element of
S[X]
.
Therefore
\Phi\in I(R_{i})
and hence
\Phi\in I(R)
proving (
3.7
).

Thus, with a slight abuse of Notation
3.1
,
for a general
[R]
we have

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\spanmap_{D}([R])\right)={\mathbb{P}}\left(\perpmap_{D}\circ\idealmap([R])\right)={\mathbb{P}}\left(\perpmap_{D}([I(R)])\right)={\mathbb{P}}\left(I(R)_{D}^{\perp}\right)\overset{\text{by\penalty\ \eqref{equ_linear_span_defined_by_ideal}}}{=}\left\langle\iota(R)\right\rangle,

where the first, second and third equalities follow from the definitions of maps

\spanmap_{D}
,
\idealmap
, and
\perpmap_{D}
respectively.

To conclude the proof of the proposition,
we apply Corollary
3.5
.
The morphism
\sch|_{\mathbf{H}_{{\mathcal{H}}}}

is birational and it is the inverse of
\idealmap
,
hence

\spanmap_{D}\colon{\mathcal{H}}\dashrightarrow\Gr(h_{{\mathcal{H}}}(D),S[X]_{D}^{*})

is resolved by the precomposition with

\sch|_{\mathbf{H}_{{\mathcal{H}}}}\colon\mathbf{H}_{{\mathcal{H}}}\to{\mathcal{H}}
as claimed.
∎

Let
{\mathcal{H}}\subset{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb(X)
be an irreducible component and fix a very ample line bundle
L\in\Pic(X)
defining an embedding
\iota\colon X\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}\left(H^{0}(L)^{*}\right)={\mathbb{P}}\left(S[X]_{L}^{*}\right)
.
For each nonnegative integer
i

the
Grassmann-relative linear span
of
{\mathcal{H}}
,
is defined as

\mathfrak{K}_{{\mathcal{H}},i}\left(X,L\right)=\overline{\left\{[E]\mid{\mathbb{P}}\left(E\right)\subset\left\langle\iota(R)\right\rangle\text{ for some }[R]\in{\mathcal{H}}\right\}}\subset\Gr(i,S[X]_{L}^{*}).

We will frequently skip
L
in the notation and write simply
\mathfrak{K}_{{\mathcal{H}},i}\left(X\right)
.
The interesting range of
i
to consider
\mathfrak{K}_{{\mathcal{H}},i}\left(X,L\right)
is between
1
and
h_{{\mathcal{H}}}(L)
,
see Lemma
3.8
.
For the initial case
i=1
and
{\mathcal{H}}\subset{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{r}(X)

the relative linear span
\mathfrak{K}_{{\mathcal{H}},i}\left(X\right)\subset{\mathbb{P}}\left(S[X]_{L}^{*}\right)

is also defined in
[
BJ17
, Def. 5.23]

— see Proposition
3.10
for a comparison of the two definitions.
If
{\mathcal{H}}\subset{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{r}(X)
is the smoothable component,
then
\mathfrak{K}_{{\mathcal{H}},i}\left(X\right)
is the Grassmann-secant variety
[
BL13
, Def. 1.1]
.
More generally, the relative linear span
of a family of finite schemes (not necessarily flat, and not necessarily over reduced base, but under additional technical assumptions) is defined and discussed in
[
BK24
, §3.2]
.
At the other extreme of the interesting range, for
i=h_{{\mathcal{H}}}(L)
, we have the case that is relevant to the construction of Hilbert scheme.
In fact, if the line bundle
L
is sufficiently ample, then
\mathfrak{K}_{{\mathcal{H}},h_{{\mathcal{H}}}(L)}\left(X\right)\simeq{\mathcal{H}}
and the embedding in the Grassmannian is the same as in the construction of the Hilbert scheme.

Lemma 3.8
.

If
i=0
then
\mathfrak{K}_{{\mathcal{H}},i}\left(X\right)=\Gr(0,S[X]_{L}^{*})=\Spec{\Bbbk}
.
If
i>h_{{\mathcal{H}}}(L)
, then

\mathfrak{K}_{{\mathcal{H}},i}\left(X\right)=\emptyset
.

Proof.
If
i=0
, then there is unique
[E]\in\Gr(0,S[X]_{L}^{*})
, namely
E=0
, and
\Gr(0,S[X]_{L}^{*})
is a single
{\Bbbk}
-point.
Since for any
[R]\in{\mathcal{H}}
the linear span of
R\subset{\mathbb{P}}\left(S[X]_{L}^{*}\right)
contains the empty set
{\mathbb{P}}(E)
, it follows that
\mathfrak{K}_{{\mathcal{H}},i}\left(X\right)=\Gr(0,S[X]_{L}^{*})
.

If
i>h_{{\mathcal{H}}}(L)
, then
for any
[R]\in{\mathcal{H}}

we have
\dim\left\langle R\right\rangle=h_{R}(L)-1\leqslant h_{{\mathcal{H}}}(L)-1
by Theorem
2.10
(ii)
and
(iii)
.
Since for any

of dimension
i
,
\dim{\mathbb{P}}(E)\geqslant h_{{\mathcal{H}}}(L)
, by the dimension count we cannot have

, thus
\mathfrak{K}_{{\mathcal{H}},i}\left(X\right)=\emptyset
as claimed.
∎

.

Here

Proposition 3.10
.

Let

be the generic point,
and
{\mathcal{G}}{}r(i,{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{H}}})_{\eta}

\mathfrak{K}_{{\mathcal{H}}}\left(X\right)=\overline{\xi_{{\mathcal{H}}}({\mathcal{G}}{}r(i,{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{H}}})_{\eta})}=\xi_{\mathbf{H}}({\mathcal{G}}{}r(i,{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbf{H}})).

Since

is equal to
\sch^{-1}(\eta)
and it is mapped isomorphically to
\eta
.
In particular,
\overline{\xi_{{\mathcal{H}}}({\mathcal{G}}{}r(i,{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{H}}})_{\eta})}=\overline{\xi_{\mathbf{H}}({\mathcal{G}}{}r(i,{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbf{H}})_{\sch^{-1}(\eta)})}

and the latter is
equal to

\xi_{\mathbf{H}}({\mathcal{G}}{}r(i,{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbf{H}}))
by Lemma
2.4
since
{\mathcal{G}}{}r(i,{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbf{H}})\to\mathbf{H}_{{\mathcal{H}}}
is flat.

Take any
[R]\in{\mathcal{H}}
, and take
[E]\in\Gr(i,V)
such that
{\mathbb{P}}(E)\subset\left\langle R\right\rangle
.
Since
\sch
is surjective,
there exists
[I]\in\mathbf{H}_{{\mathcal{H}}}
such that
\sch([I])=[R]
.
We have
\left\langle R\right\rangle\subset{\mathbb{P}}\left((I_{L})^{\perp}\right)
,
thus
E\subset(I_{L})^{\perp}

and therefore
[E]\in\xi_{\mathbf{H}}({\mathcal{G}}{}r(i,{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbf{H}}))
.
This proves that
\mathfrak{K}_{{\mathcal{H}}}\left(X\right)\subset\xi_{\mathbf{H}}({\mathcal{G}}{}r(i,{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbf{H}}))
since the latter is closed in
\Gr(i,S[X]^{*}_{L})
.

Finally suppose
[E]\in\xi_{\mathbf{H}}({\mathcal{G}}{}r(i,{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbf{H}}))
is a general point.
Then there exists
[I]\in\mathbf{H}_{{\mathcal{H}}}
such that

.
Moreover, since
[E]
is general,
by Theorem
2.1
)
we may assume that
I
is saturated.
Then
\sch
is an isomorphism near
[I]

and

, where

, see Proposition

Thus
E\subset\left\langle R\right\rangle
and
[E]\in\mathfrak{K}_{{\mathcal{H}}}\left(X\right)
showing the final inclusion

\xi_{\mathbf{H}}({\mathcal{G}}{}r(i,{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbf{H}}))\subset\mathfrak{K}_{{\mathcal{H}}}\left(X\right)
and proving the proposition.
∎

Let us stress that the equality

\mathfrak{K}_{{\mathcal{H}}}\left(X,L\right)=\overline{\xi_{{\mathcal{H}}}({\mathcal{G}}{}r(i,{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{H}}})_{\eta})}
in
Proposition
3.10
, when

, is identical to

[
BJ17
, Def. 5.23]
,
which shows compatibility of the two definitions.
Moreover,
\mathfrak{K}_{{\mathcal{H}}}\left(X\right)=\xi_{\mathbf{H}}({\mathcal{G}}{}r(i,{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbf{H}}))
does not involve any closure:
in particular, the map
\xi_{\mathbf{H}}\colon{\mathcal{G}}{}r(i,{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbf{H}})\to\mathfrak{K}_{{\mathcal{H}}}\left(X\right)

which is critical for applications.

4
Apolarity and border cactus decompositions

In this section we review basics of multigraded apolarity, define the border cactus decompositions and prove the main results of the article.

4.1
Apolarity

Let
S[X]=\bigoplus_{D\in\Pic X}H^{0}(D)
be the Cox ring of the toric variety in question and let
\widetilde{S}[X]=\bigoplus_{D\in\Pic X}H^{0}(D)^{*}
be the graded dual of
S[X]
.
The apolarity action of
S[X]
on
\widetilde{S}[X]
is given by
the contractions
\lrcorner
, as in
[
Jeli17
, §3.1]
,

[
Gałą23a
, Equation (1.1) and §4]
,
[
BB21
, §3.1]
, and
[
BK24
, §5.1]
.

For a linear subspace
E\subset\widetilde{S}[X]
its annihilator is the ideal

\Ann(E)=\left\{\Theta\in S[X]\mid\forall_{F\in E}\ \Theta\lrcorner F=0\right\}\subset S[X].

If
\dim E=1
, so that
E
is spanned by a single
F\in\widetilde{S}[X]
(or
F\in{\mathbb{P}}\left(\widetilde{S}[X]_{L}\right)
for some
L
), then we simply write
\Ann(F)=\Ann(E)
.
Note that if
\mathfrak{E}
is a basis od
E
as a
{\Bbbk}
-vector space, then

\Ann(E)=\bigcap_{F\in E}\Ann(F)=\bigcap_{F\in\mathfrak{E}}\Ann(F).

In this article we focus only on homogeneous case, so that the only interesting case is
E\subset\widetilde{S}[X]_{L}=H^{0}(L)^{*}
for
a fixed line bundle
L\in\Pic X
. In particular, in this case
\dim E<\infty
, and
\Ann(E)
is a homogeneous ideal
— as always — with respect to the grading by
\Pic X
.

The standard apolarity type observations are the following lemmas.

Lemma 4.1
.

For any
E\subset H^{0}(X,L)^{*}

we have
\Ann(E)_{L}=E^{\perp}\subset H^{0}(X,L)
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose
E\subset H^{0}(X,L)^{*}
is a linear subspace and

I\subset S[X]
is a homogeneous ideal.
If
I_{L}\subset\Ann(E)_{L}
, then
I\subset\Ann(E)
.

Proof.
Note that for any degree
D^{\prime}\in\Pic X
:

\text{if }G\in\widetilde{S}[X]_{D^{\prime}}\text{ is such that }S[X]_{D^{\prime}}\lrcorner G\equiv 0,\text{ then }G=0.

(4.3)

Since both
I
and
\Ann(E)
are homogeneous ideals,
it is enough to check the claim in each degree

D\in\Pic X
.
Pick
\Phi\in I_{D}
, and thus
S[X]_{L-D}\cdot\Phi\subset I_{L}\subset\Ann(E)_{L}
by the assumption of the lemma.
Hence for all
F\in E
,

S[X]_{L-D}\lrcorner(\Phi\lrcorner F)=0
,
and by (
4.3
)
\Phi\lrcorner F=0
, that is
\Phi\in\Ann(F)
.
Intersecting over all
F
, we get the claim:

\Phi\in\bigcap_{F\in E}\Ann(F)=\Ann(E)
.
∎

Although we do not need it explicitly here, the motivation for the border apolarity techniques is the following (standard) apolarity.

Proposition 4.4
.

Suppose
X
is a smooth toric projective variety,
L
is a very ample line bundle, and consider
X
embedded into
{\mathbb{P}}\left(\widetilde{S}_{L}\right)
.
Fix a linear subspace
E\subset\widetilde{S}_{L}
.
Then for any closed subscheme
R\subset X
, we have the following equivalence:

E\subset\left\langle R\right\rangle\iff I(R)\subset\Ann(E).

When the base field is
{\Bbbk}={\mathbb{C}}
this is proven in
[
Gałą23a
, Thm 1.4]
.
For other fields, but
X={\mathbb{P}}V
the projective space this is standard, see for instance

[
IK99
, Lem. 1.15]
,
and another version of apolarity is
[
BBF24
, Prop. 4.14]
.
In any case, the proof is always the same:

Proof.
E\subset\left\langle R\right\rangle
if and only if
E\subset(I(R)_{L})^{\perp}
if and only if
I(R)_{L}\subset E^{\perp}
if and only if
(by Lemma
4.1
)
I(R)_{L}\subset\Ann(E)_{L}
if and only if
(by Lemma
4.2
)
I(R)\subset\Ann(E)
.
∎

4.2
Case of general component

In this subsection
{\mathcal{H}}
is any irreducible component of
{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb(X)
.
We still use Notation
3.1
.

Theorem 4.5
.

Suppose
X
is a toric projective variety,
L
is a very ample line bundle, and
{\mathcal{H}}\subset{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb(X)
is an irreducible component.
Denote by
\mathbf{H}_{{\mathcal{H}}}\subset\Hilb(S[X])

the corresponding component of the multigraded scheme,
as in Corollary
3.5
.
Consider
X
embedded into
{\mathbb{P}}\left(\widetilde{S}_{L}\right)

and fix a point
[E]\in\Gr\left(i,\widetilde{S}_{L}\right)
.
Then:

[E]\in\mathfrak{K}_{{\mathcal{H}},i}\left(X\right)\iff\exists_{[I]\in\mathbf{H}_{{\mathcal{H}}}}\text{ s.t. }I\subset\Ann(E).

Proof.
Recall from Subsection
3.3
the construction of bundles
{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbf{H}}
and
\Gr(i,{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbf{H}})
.
In particular, the
{\Bbbk}
-points of
\Gr(i,{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbf{H}})
are precisely

\left\{([I],[E^{\prime}])\in\mathbf{H}_{{\mathcal{H}}}\times\Gr(i,\widetilde{S}_{L})\mid E^{\prime}\subset I_{L}^{\perp}\right\}
.
Equivalently,
by Lemmas
4.1

and
4.2
,

\Gr(i,{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbf{H}})({\Bbbk})=\left\{([I],[E^{\prime}])\in\mathbf{H}_{{\mathcal{H}}}\times\Gr(i,\widetilde{S}_{L})\mid I\subset\Ann(E^{\prime})\right\}.

By Proposition
3.10

[E]\in\mathfrak{K}_{{\mathcal{H}},i}\left(X\right)
if and only if
there exists
[I]\in\mathbf{H}_{{\mathcal{H}}}({\Bbbk})
such that
([I],[E])\in\Gr(i,{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbf{H}})
.
The condition on
([I],[E])
is then equivalent to
I\subset\Ann(E)
, proving the Theorem.
∎

In the setting of Theorem
4.5

we define the
variety of border cactus decompositions

\underline{\mathbf{CD}}(E,{\mathcal{H}})
to be
the reduced fibre over
[E]
of the map

\xi_{\mathbf{H}}\colon{\mathcal{G}}{}r(i,{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbf{H}})\to\Gr(i,\widetilde{S}_{L})

as in Subsection
3.3
,

\underline{\mathbf{CD}}(E,{\mathcal{H}}):={\xi_{\mathbf{H}}^{-1}(E)}_{\operatorname{red}}\subset\mathbf{H}_{{\mathcal{H}}}
.
Note that
\underline{\mathbf{CD}}(E,{\mathcal{H}})
is a projective reduced scheme, and it is non-empty if and only if
[E]\in\mathfrak{K}_{{\mathcal{H}},i}\left(X\right)
.

Suppose
G\subset\Aut(X)
is a connected subgroup of the automorphisms of
X
.
Then it has natural induced actions on
{\mathcal{H}}
,
\mathbf{H}_{{\mathcal{H}}}
, and other varieties and bundles appearing in Diagram (
3.9
), such that all the maps in the diagram are equivariant.
If in addition
G
preserves
[E]\in\Gr(i,\widetilde{S}_{L})
then it also acts on
\underline{\mathbf{CD}}(E,{\mathcal{H}})
.

Theorem 4.6
.

With
{\mathcal{H}}
,
E
, and
G
as above with
G\cdot E=E
,
assume in addition that
G
is a solvable group.
Then
E\in\mathfrak{K}_{{\mathcal{H}},i}\left(X\right)
if and only if there exists an ideal
[I]\in\mathbf{H}_{{\mathcal{H}}}

preserved by
G
and such that
I\subset\Ann(E)
.

Proof.
By the above discussion
[E]\in\mathfrak{K}_{{\mathcal{H}},i}\left(X\right)
if and only if
\underline{\mathbf{CD}}(E,{\mathcal{H}})
is non-empty.
By Theorem
4.5

we have

\underline{\mathbf{CD}}(E,{\mathcal{H}})({\Bbbk})=\left\{[I]\in\mathbf{H}_{{\mathcal{H}}}\mid I\subset\Ann(E)\right\}.

Since
\underline{\mathbf{CD}}(E,{\mathcal{H}})
is a projective scheme, by Borel’s fixed point lemma
[
Bore91
, Thm 10.4]
it is non-empty if and only if it contains a
G
-fixed

{\Bbbk}
-point.
A
G
-fixed point in
\underline{\mathbf{CD}}(E,{\mathcal{H}})

is a
G
-invariant ideal
I\subset S[X]
contained in
\Ann(E)
, proving the claim.
∎

For the rest of the paper we restrict our attention to components
{\mathcal{H}}\subset{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{r}(X)
, that is, we restrict
to the Hilbert scheme of points.

4.3
Case of secant varieties

In this subsection, we let
{\mathcal{H}}={{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{r}^{\operatorname{sm}}{(X)}
, the smoothable component of the Hilbert scheme.
In this case the standard terminology and notation is the following:

•

\mathfrak{K}_{{\mathcal{H}},i}\left(X\right)=\sigma_{r,i}(X)
is the Grassmann secant variety, or simply secant variety

\sigma_{r}(X)
for
i=1
,

•

\mathbf{H}_{{\mathcal{H}}}=\Slip_{r}(X)
is the scheme of limits of ideals of points,

•

the Hilbert function
h_{{\mathcal{H}}}(D)
is equal to
h_{r,X}:=\min(r,\dim_{{\Bbbk}}S[X]_{D})
by Theorem
2.12
.

A special feature of this case is the relation between Grassmann secant variety of
X
and secant variety of
{\mathbb{P}}^{i-1}\times X
, see for instance
[
BL13
, Thm 2.5, Cor. 3.6(iii)]
.

Thus for the case
{\mathcal{H}}={{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{r}^{\operatorname{sm}}{(X)}
we obtain:

Theorem 4.7
.

Suppose
X
is a smooth toric projective variety,
L
is a very ample line bundle.
Consider
X
embedded into
{\mathbb{P}}\left(\widetilde{S}_{L}\right)

and fix a linear subspace
E\subset\widetilde{S}_{L}
of dimension
i
.
Then:

[E]\in\sigma_{r,i}(X)\iff\exists_{[I]\in\Slip_{r}(X)}\text{ such that }I\subset\Ann(E).

Moreover, if
G\subset\Aut(X)
is a connected solvable subgroup that preserves
E
,
then

[E]\in\sigma_{r,i}(X)\iff\exists_{[I]\in\Slip_{r}(X)}\text{ such that }I\subset\Ann(E)\text{ and }G\cdot I=I.

The theorem follows directly from Theorems
4.5
and
4.6
.
Moreover, restricting to
i=1
,
Theorems
2.12

and
4.7

imply Theorem
1.1
.

As an interesting conclusion, if the border rank of a monomial can be bounded from below using weak border apolarity (Theorem
1.1
) for some base field, then the same bound applies over any base field.
We illustrate this on a simple example without providing details.

Example 4.8
.

Let
X={\mathbb{P}}^{2}
and
m=x_{0}^{(a_{0})}x_{1}^{(a_{1})}x_{2}^{(a_{2})}
with
a_{0}\leqslant a_{1}\leqslant a_{2}
.
Then
m\in\sigma_{r}(X)\setminus\sigma_{r-1}(X)

for
r=(a_{0}+1)(a_{1}+1)
,
independent of the base field
{\Bbbk}
.
Indeed, the upper bound on such
r
is proven in the same way as
[
BB21
, Lem. 6.2]
.
The lower bound is proven in the same way as
[
BB21
, Thm 6.21]
,
which boils down to prove non-existence of a monomial ideal
I
with a fixed Hilbert function contained in another fixed monomial ideal.
This is purely combinatorial,
and does not depend on the base field
{\Bbbk}
.

Up to now, all the monomials that have known border rank over
{\mathbb{C}}
, can be calculated using weak border apolarity.

4.4
Case of cactus varieties

Now we compare the standard terminology for cactus varieties with the relative linear spans.
Explicitly, let
{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{r}(X)
be the Hilbert scheme of
r
-points, which might have multiple components.
The
r
-th
Grassmann cactus variety
of
X
is defined as:

\mathfrak{K}_{r,i}\left(X\right):=\bigcup_{{\mathcal{H}}\subset{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{r}(X)}\mathfrak{K}_{{\mathcal{H}},i}\left(X\right)\subset Gr(i,\widetilde{S}_{L})

where the union is over all irreducible components
{\mathcal{H}}
of
{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{r}(X)
.
As before, the special case
i=1
is simply called the
cactus variety
of
X
and denoted:

\mathfrak{K}_{r}\left(X\right):=\bigcup_{{\mathcal{H}}\subset{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{r}(X)}\mathfrak{K}_{{\mathcal{H}}}\left(X\right)\subset{\mathbb{P}}\left(\widetilde{S}_{L}\right).

(4.9)

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

With the assumptions of the theorem we work in the case

i=1
.
By
[
BJ17
, Cor. 6.20]

in Equation
2.5
it is enough to take the union only over those components
{\mathcal{H}}\subset{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{r}(X)
such that its general element represents a Gorenstein scheme.
The rest of the claim follows from Theorem
4.5
.
∎

Now Theorem
1.2
follows immediately, as there are only finitely many components of the Hilbert scheme, each of them has one Hilbert function of a general member by Theorem
2.10
(iii)
.
Note that different components may have the same Hilbert function.
For the potential applications, it is an important problem to list all these Hilbert functions, or at least discover the properties of these Hilbert functions.
Naively, one might hope that the only relevant function is
h_{r,X}:=\min(r,\dim_{{\Bbbk}}S[X]_{D})
,
however, see Proposition
4.14
.
At this point we do not know enough about components of Hilbert schemes of points to be able to determine the Hilbert function of a general scheme in each component (except in the situation when the resulting Hilbert function is equal or very close to
h_{r,X}
).

More generally, properties of Hilbert functions of saturated ideals form an interesting topic,
see
[
Able24
]
and references therein.
All these properties apply to
h_{{\mathcal{H}}}
.
The only new information we are able to provide is the following observation.

Proposition 4.10
.

Suppose
{\mathcal{H}}\subset{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{r}({\mathbb{P}}^{n})
is an irreducible component of the
Hilbert scheme of finite subschemes of length
r
.
Then
h_{{\mathcal{H}}}(1)=\min\left\{r,n+1\right\}=h_{r}(1)
.

Lemma 4.11
.

Suppose
i_{1}\colon R\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}V
is an embedding of a finite scheme of length
r
.
Then there is an embedding
i_{2}\colon R\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}V

such that
\dim(V^{*}/I(i_{2}(R))_{1})=\min\left\{r,\dim V\right\}
.

Proof.

First, there is always a concisely independent embedding

i_{3}\colon R\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{r-1}

with
I(i_{3}(R))_{1}=0

[
BBKT15
, pp 702–703]
.
This is obtained by the embedding
R=\Spec A
into
{\mathbb{P}}A
,
or equivalently, by the trivial line bundle
{\mathcal{O}}_{R}
, which is very ample.
If
\dim V\geq r
, then
i_{2}
is a composition of
i_{3}
with a linear embedding
{\mathbb{P}}^{r-1}\subset{\mathbb{P}}V
.

Suppose
\dim V<r
.
Let
\mathfrak{K}_{2}\left(i_{3}(R)\right)\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{r-1}
be the second cactus variety of
R
,
that is the finite union of the (projective) Zariski tangent spaces of
R
at each point and the secant lines connecting the any two points of support of
R
.
Note that
\dim\mathfrak{K}_{2}\left(i_{3}(R)\right)\leq\dim{\mathbb{P}}V
:
indeed, since
i_{1}
is an embedding of
R
into
{\mathbb{P}}V
,
each tangent space must be at most
\dim{\mathbb{P}}V
dimensional.
Also each secant line is one dimensional, and there are any secant lines only if there are at least two distinct points of support,
which is possible only if
\dim{\mathbb{P}}V\geq 1
.

Pick a linear projection
{\mathbb{P}}^{r-1}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}V
.
By standard arguments, such as in
[
Hart77
, Prop. IV.3.4]
,
if the center of the projection does not intersect

\mathfrak{K}_{2}\left(i_{3}(R)\right)
,
then the composition of
i_{3}
and the projection is still an embedding.
Moreover, in such a case the linear span of this new embedding is
{\mathbb{P}}V
.
Clearly, by the dimension count, we can pick the linear projection satisfying the above property.
∎

Lemma 4.12
.

Suppose
i_{1}
and
i_{2}
are two embeddings of the same finite scheme

R\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}V
.
Then
[i_{1}(R)]
and
[i_{2}(R)]
are in the same irreducible component of the Hilbert scheme
{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{r}({\mathbb{P}}V)
, where
r=\deg R
.

Proof.
Pick a basis
\left\{\alpha_{0},\dotsc,\alpha_{n}\right\}

of
V^{*}=H^{0}({\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}V}(1))
.
Pulling the basis back to
R
using
i_{1}
and
i_{2}
,
we obtain two collections,

\left\{i_{1}^{*}\alpha_{0},\dotsc,i_{1}^{*}\alpha_{n}\right\}
and

\left\{i_{2}^{*}\alpha_{0},\dotsc,i_{2}^{*}\alpha_{n}\right\}
,
of sections of the trivial line bundle

{\mathcal{O}}_{R}\simeq i_{1}^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}V}(1)\simeq i_{2}^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}V}(1)
.
Consider the
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
-parametrised family of maps
R\to{\mathbb{P}}V

determined by

\left\{s\cdot i_{1}^{*}\alpha_{0}+t\cdot i_{2}^{*}\alpha_{0},\dotsc,s\cdot i_{1}^{*}\alpha_{n}+t\cdot i_{2}^{*}\alpha_{n}\right\},

where
s,t
are coordinated on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
.
Generically, this is an embedding, and thus we obtain a flat family of subschemes of
{\mathbb{P}}V
paramerised by an open dense subset of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
.
This family demonstrates that
[i_{1}(R)]
and
[i_{2}(R)]
are in the same component of the Hilbert scheme, as claimed.
∎

Proof of Proposition
4.10
.

Take a general element
[R]\in{\mathcal{H}}
. By the generality,
{\mathcal{H}}
is the unique component of
{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{r}({\mathbb{P}}V)
containing
[R]
.
By Lemma
4.11
, we can reembed
R
into
{\mathbb{P}}V

in a linearly nondegererate way.
By Lemma
4.12

both embeddings are in the same component, that is
{\mathcal{H}}
.
It follows that
h_{{\mathcal{H}}}(1)\geq\min\left\{r,\dim V\right\}
,
then we must have the equality since
h_{{\mathcal{H}}}(1)\leqslant h_{r}(1)
.
∎

Consider a set of
4
points in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
.
If three of them are on a line, then all four of them are linearly dependent, contained in a
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
.
The following lemma generalises this elementary observation to schemes, and we skip the proof.

Lemma 4.13
.

Suppose
R
and

Proposition 4.14

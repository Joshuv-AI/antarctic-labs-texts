Abstract
. We consider the 3-category 2 ℭ ​ a ​ t 2{\mathop{{\mathfrak{C}}at}\nolimits} whose objects are 2-categories, 1-morphisms are lax functors, 2-morphisms are lax transformations and 3-morphisms are modifications. The aim is to show that it carries interesting representation-theoretic information. Let 𝒞 {\mathcal{C}} be a small 1-category and 𝔅 ​ i ​ m k {\mathop{{\mathfrak{B}}im}\nolimits}_{k} be the 2-category of bimodules over k k -algebras, where k k is a commutative ring with identity. We call a covariant (resp. contravariant) pseudofunctor from 𝒞 {\mathcal{C}} into ℬ ​ i ​ m k {\mathcal{B}}im_{k} a modulation (resp. comodulation) on 𝒞 {\mathcal{C}} , define and study its representations. This framework provides a unified approach to investigate 2-representations of finite groups, modulated quivers and their representations, as well as presheaves of k k -algebras and their modules. Moreover, all key constructions are morphisms in 2 ℭ ​ a ​ t 2{\mathop{{\mathfrak{C}}at}\nolimits} , and thus it exhibits an interesting application of higher category theory to representation theory.

2.
Basic higher category theory

We shall consider the 2-category of bimodules and the pseudofunctors into it. Although they have roots in algebraic geometry, these categorical constructions seem to provide a natural way to organize representation-theoretic information.

All 2-categories form a 3-category, with 2-categories as objects, lax functors as 1-morphisms, lax (natural) transformations as 2-morphisms, and modifications as 3-morphisms. Surprisingly, all of these components carry representation-theoretic information in our case.

Since any quiver generates a free category, the modulation generalizes the concept of a modulated quiver, or a species, where

We are particularly interested in the following case in which the formulation is not seen in the literature.

Example 3.1.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}

{\mathfrak{R}}

Upon the realization of

inside

, the modulation affords the restriction

[

and Balmer
[

.

Remark 3.1.3
.

It is possible to replace
{\mathop{{\mathfrak{B}}im}\nolimits}_{k}
by a larger 2-category of bifunctors
{\mathop{{\mathfrak{B}}if}\nolimits}_{k}
, whose objects are (small) k-linear categories, 1-morphisms are additive bifunctors, and 2-morphisms are natural transformations of additive bifunctors. Since this doesn’t pose much more difficulties but requires higher technicalities in terms of terminologies, we refrain from pursing this generality.

In the sense of Ganter-Kapranov
[

, a pseudofuctor

should be called a 2-representation of

. However, it is different from the notion we will define next.

3.2.
Representations

We introduce the notion of a representation of a modulated category.

Definition 3.2.1
.

A representation of the modulated quiver
({\mathcal{C}},{\mathfrak{M}})
is a lax transformation
{\mathcal{V}}:\underline{k}\to{\mathfrak{M}}
. Denote by
\mathop{\rm Rep}\nolimits_{k}({\mathcal{C}},{\mathfrak{M}})=2\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(\underline{k},{\mathfrak{M}})

The category
\mathop{\rm Rep}\nolimits_{k}({\mathcal{C}},{\mathfrak{M}})=2\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(\underline{k},{\mathfrak{M}})
is what Ganter-Kapranov
[
8
]
called the category of
G

Definition 3.2.2
.

A representation
{\mathcal{V}}
of the modulated category
({\mathcal{C}},{\mathfrak{M}})
consists of the following data

(1)

for any

, a right
{\mathfrak{M}}(x)
-module

, and

(2)

for any

satisfying the condition that there is a commutative diagram, for any
\alpha:x\to y
and

\textstyle{{\mathcal{V}}(x)\otimes_{{\mathfrak{M}}(x)}{\mathfrak{M}}(\alpha)\otimes_{{\mathfrak{M}}(y)}{\mathfrak{M}}(\beta)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{V}}(x)}\otimes c_{\alpha,\beta}}
\scriptstyle{{\mathcal{V}}(\alpha)\otimes\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{M}}(\beta)}}
\textstyle{{\mathcal{V}}(y)\otimes_{{\mathfrak{M}}(y)}{\mathfrak{M}}(\beta)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Note that

is a

for every

Remark 3.2.3
.

(1)

If
{\mathcal{C}}
is the free category generated by a quiver, then the above definition coincides with the definition of a representation of a modulated quiver

(2)

By the standard adjunction between

Definition 3.2.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{V}},{\mathcal{W}}:\underline{k}\to{\mathfrak{M}}
be two representations of the modulated category
({\mathcal{C}},{\mathfrak{M}})
. Then we call a modification

, a morphism

\underline{k}(x)

\textstyle{{\mathcal{V}}(x)\otimes_{{\mathfrak{M}}(x)}{\mathfrak{M}}(\alpha)={\mathfrak{M}}(\alpha)\circ{\mathcal{V}}(x)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Therefore, we obtain the following equivalent definition, which is the one used in the literature

Definition 3.2.5

Let

From here, one can easily define the kernel and the cokernel of a morphism. It is straightforward to see that

For future reference, we also spell out the definition of a representation of a comodulated category

\textstyle{{\mathcal{V}}(y)\otimes_{{\mathfrak{W}}(y)}{\mathfrak{W}}(\alpha)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Let

4.
Modulated category algebras

Suppose
{\mathcal{C}}
is a small category. A pseudofunctor immediately offers an algebra that is of great interest.

4.1.
Modulated and comodulated category algebras

Definition 4.1.1
.

Let
({\mathcal{C}},{\mathfrak{M}})
be a modulated category. The modulated category algebra
{\mathfrak{M}}[{\mathcal{C}}]
is defined as

\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\mathop{\rm Mor}\nolimits{\mathcal{C}}}{\mathfrak{M}}(\alpha),

in which the multiplication of
m_{\alpha}\in{\mathfrak{M}}(\alpha)
and
m_{\beta}\in{\mathfrak{M}}(\beta)
is given by the structure isomorphisms of the modulation

\displaystyle m_{\alpha}\ast m_{\beta}=\begin{cases}c_{\beta,\alpha}(m_{\alpha}\otimes m_{\beta})\in{\mathfrak{M}}(\beta\alpha),&\text{if}~{\rm dom}(\beta)={\rm cod}(\alpha);\\
0,&{\rm otherwise}.\end{cases}

Note that
c_{\beta,\alpha}:{\mathfrak{M}}(\beta)\circ{\mathfrak{M}}(\alpha)={\mathfrak{M}}(\alpha)\otimes{\mathfrak{M}}(\beta)\to{\mathfrak{M}}(\beta\alpha)
is an isomorphism for any
\alpha:x\to y
and
\beta:y\to z
.

Remark 4.1.2
.

(1)

The algebra
{\mathfrak{M}}[{\mathcal{C}}]
is associative.

(2)

If
\mathop{\rm Ob}\nolimits{\mathcal{C}}
is finite, then the algebra
{\mathfrak{M}}[{\mathcal{C}}]
has an identity
\sum_{x\in\mathop{\rm Ob}\nolimits{\mathcal{C}}}1_{{\mathfrak{M}}(x)}
, where
1_{{\mathfrak{M}}(x)}
is the identity of
{\mathfrak{M}}(x)={\mathfrak{M}}(1_{x})
.

(3)

If
\mathop{\rm Mor}\nolimits{\mathcal{C}}
is finite and every
{\mathfrak{M}}(\alpha)
is a finite-dimensional
k
-module, then the algebra
{\mathfrak{M}}[{\mathcal{C}}]
is a finite-dimensional
k
-algebra.

(4)

It is not clear if
{\mathfrak{M}}[{\mathcal{C}}]
admits an alternative formation via the Grothendieck construction as in
[
19
]
.

For future reference, here is the comodulated version.

Definition 4.1.3
.

Let
({\mathcal{C}},{\mathfrak{W}})
be a comodulated category. The comodulated category algebra
{\mathfrak{W}}[{\mathcal{C}}]
is defined as

\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\mathop{\rm Mor}\nolimits{\mathcal{C}}}{\mathfrak{W}}(\alpha),

in which the multiplication of
w_{\alpha}\in{\mathfrak{W}}(\alpha)
and
w_{\beta}\in{\mathfrak{W}}(\beta)
is given by the structure isomorphisms of the comodulation

\displaystyle w_{\beta}\ast w_{\alpha}=\begin{cases}c_{\alpha,\beta}(w_{\beta}\otimes w_{\alpha})\in{\mathfrak{W}}(\beta\alpha),&\text{if}~{\rm dom}(\beta)={\rm cod}(\alpha);\\
0,&{\rm otherwise}.\end{cases}

Note that
c_{\alpha,\beta}:{\mathfrak{W}}(\alpha)\circ{\mathfrak{W}}(\beta)={\mathfrak{W}}(\beta)\otimes{\mathfrak{W}}(\alpha)\to{\mathfrak{W}}(\beta\alpha)
is an isomorphism for any
\alpha:x\to y
and
\beta:y\to z
.

4.2.
Module categories

It is well-known that group representations are equivalent to modules of the corresponding group algebra. This was extended to representations of a small category by Mitchell, to representations of modulated quivers by Simson
[
18
]
and to modules of a presheaf of
k
-algebras by
[
19
]
. Now we further generalize it to representations of a modulated category (and of a comodulated category).

Theorem 4.2.1
.

Let
({\mathcal{C}},{\mathfrak{M}})
be a modulated category. If
\mathop{\rm Ob}\nolimits{\mathcal{C}}
is finite, then there is a category equivalence

\mathop{\rm Rep}\nolimits({\mathcal{C}},{\mathfrak{M}})\simeq\rMod{\mathfrak{M}}[{\mathcal{C}}].

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{V}}
be a representation of
({\mathcal{C}},{\mathfrak{M}})
. Then we construct a right
{\mathfrak{M}}[{\mathcal{C}}]
-module
V=\oplus_{x\in\mathop{\rm Ob}\nolimits{\mathcal{C}}}{\mathcal{V}}(x)
such that
m_{\alpha}\in{\mathfrak{M}}(\alpha)
acts as

\displaystyle v_{x}m_{\alpha}=\begin{cases}{\mathcal{V}}(\alpha)(v_{x}\otimes m_{\alpha}),&\text{if}~\mathop{\rm dom}(\alpha)=x;\\
0,&{\rm otherwise}.\end{cases}

Assume
\alpha:x\to y
and
\beta:y\to z
to be two composable morphisms. If
m_{\alpha}\in{\mathfrak{M}}(\alpha)
and
m_{\beta}\in{\mathfrak{M}}(\beta)
, then, by the definition of a representation,

(v_{x}m_{\alpha})m_{\beta}={\mathcal{V}}(\beta)\{[{\mathcal{V}}(\alpha)(v_{x}\otimes m_{\alpha})]\otimes m_{\beta}\}={\mathcal{V}}(\beta\alpha)(v_{x}\otimes c_{\beta,\alpha}(m_{\alpha}\otimes m_{\beta})),

but the rightmost term is
{\mathcal{V}}(\beta\alpha)(v_{x}\otimes(m_{\alpha}*m_{\beta}))=v_{x}\cdot(m_{\alpha}*m_{\beta})
, with
m_{\alpha}*m_{\beta}\in{\mathfrak{M}}(\beta\alpha)
.

Conversely, if
V
is a right
{\mathfrak{M}}[{\mathcal{C}}]
-module, then we define a representation
{\mathcal{V}}
of
({\mathcal{C}},{\mathfrak{M}})
by
{\mathcal{V}}(x)=V1_{{\mathfrak{M}}(x)}
, and, for any
\alpha:x\to y
, the morphism

{\mathcal{V}}(\alpha):{\mathcal{V}}(x)\otimes_{{\mathfrak{M}}(x)}{\mathfrak{M}}(\alpha)\to{\mathcal{V}}(y)

is given by
{\mathcal{V}}(\alpha)(v_{x}\otimes m_{\alpha})=v_{x}m_{\alpha}
,
\forall v_{x}\in{\mathcal{V}}(x)
and
\forall m_{\alpha}\in{\mathfrak{M}}(\alpha)
. Note that
v_{x}m_{\alpha}=v_{x}(m_{\alpha}*1_{{\mathfrak{M}}(y)})=(v_{x}m_{\alpha})1_{{\mathfrak{M}}(y)}\in{\mathcal{V}}(y)
.

One can readily check that the above two constructions are inverse to each other.
∎

Now we can present a connection between 2-representations of
{\mathcal{C}}
, in the sense of Ganter-Kapranov
[
8
]
, and the representations of a modulated category, traced back to Gabriel
[
7
,
6
]
.

Corollary 4.2.2
.

Let
\underline{k}:{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathop{2{\mathfrak{V}}ect}\nolimits}_{k}
be the trivial 2-representation of
{\mathcal{C}}
(or the trivial modulation). Then the representations of
({\mathcal{C}},\underline{k})
is identified with
k{\mathcal{C}}
-modules, where
k{\mathcal{C}}
is the category algebra.

Particularly for a finite group
G
, the representations of
(G,\underline{k})
are exactly the
kG
-modules.

Proof.

The category
\mathop{\rm Rep}\nolimits_{k}({\mathcal{C}},\underline{k})=2\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(\underline{k},\underline{k})
can be computed directly, say, with Definition
3.2.2
. This the category of all functors
{\mathcal{C}}\to\rMod k
, which is equivalent to
\rMod k{\mathcal{C}}
.
∎

The above indicates that the trivial 2-representation
\underline{k}
of
{\mathcal{C}}
determines all 1-representations of
{\mathcal{C}}
. If we consider a modulation as a 2-representation of
{\mathcal{C}}
, then it gives all 1-representations of the algebra
{\mathfrak{M}}[{\mathcal{C}}]
.

Analogously to this, we have an equivalence for comodulated categories.

Theorem 4.2.3
.

Let
({\mathcal{C}},{\mathfrak{W}})
be a comodulated category. If
\mathop{\rm Ob}\nolimits{\mathcal{C}}
is finite, then there is a category equivalence

\mathop{\rm Rep}\nolimits({\mathcal{C}},{\mathfrak{W}})\simeq\rMod{\mathfrak{W}}[{\mathcal{C}}].

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{V}}
be a representation of
({\mathcal{C}},{\mathfrak{W}})
. Then we construct a right
{\mathfrak{W}}[{\mathcal{C}}]
-module
V=\oplus_{x\in\mathop{\rm Ob}\nolimits{\mathcal{C}}}{\mathcal{V}}(x)
such that
w_{\alpha}\in{\mathfrak{W}}(\alpha)
acts as

\displaystyle v_{y}w_{\alpha}=\begin{cases}{\mathcal{V}}(\alpha)(v_{y}\otimes w_{\alpha}),&\text{if}~\mathop{\rm cod}(\alpha)=y;\\
0,&{\rm otherwise}.\end{cases}

Assume
w_{\alpha}\in{\mathfrak{W}}(\alpha)
and
w_{\beta}\in{\mathfrak{W}}(\beta)
. Then

(v_{z}w_{\beta})w_{\alpha}={\mathcal{V}}(\alpha)\{[{\mathcal{V}}(\beta)(v_{z}\otimes w_{\beta})]\otimes w_{\alpha}\}={\mathcal{V}}(\beta\alpha)(v_{z}\otimes c_{\alpha,\beta}(w_{\beta}\otimes w_{\alpha})).

The later is
v_{z}(w_{\beta}*w_{\alpha})
.

Conversely, if
V
is a right
{\mathfrak{W}}[{\mathcal{C}}]
-module, then we define a representation
{\mathcal{V}}
of
({\mathcal{C}},{\mathfrak{W}})
by
{\mathcal{V}}(x)=V1_{{\mathfrak{W}}(x)}
, and, for any
\alpha:x\to y
, the morphism

{\mathcal{V}}(\alpha):{\mathcal{V}}(y)\otimes_{{\mathfrak{W}}(y)}{\mathfrak{W}}(\alpha)\to{\mathcal{V}}(x)

is given by
{\mathcal{V}}(\alpha)(v_{y}\otimes w_{\alpha})=v_{y}w_{\alpha}
,
\forall v_{y}\in{\mathcal{V}}(y)
and
\forall w_{\alpha}\in{\mathfrak{W}}(\alpha)
.

One can readily check that the above two constructions are inverse to each other.
∎

Finally, the modules of a presheaf of
k
-algebras are the same as the representations of the corresponding comodulated category.

Example 4.2.4
.

In example for a given presheaf of algebras
{\mathcal{R}}
we have a modulation and a comodulation on
{\mathcal{C}}
: on every
\alpha:x\to y
the bimodules are
{\mathfrak{M}}(\alpha)=_{{\mathcal{R}}(x)}{\mathcal{R}}(x)_{{\mathcal{R}}(y)}
and
{\mathfrak{W}}(\alpha)=_{{\mathcal{R}}(y)}{\mathcal{R}}(x)_{{\mathcal{R}}(x)}
, respectively. Then
{\mathfrak{M}}[{\mathcal{C}}]
and
{\mathfrak{W}}[{\mathcal{C}}]
are different algebras, and the second one is the skew category algebra
{\mathfrak{W}}[{\mathcal{C}}]={\mathcal{R}}[{\mathcal{C}}]
that we introduced in
[
19
]
. In fact,
{\mathfrak{M}}[{\mathcal{C}}]
is some sort of a dual version of the skew category algebra.

By the remarks behind Definition
3.2.6
,
\mathop{\rm Rep}\nolimits({\mathcal{C}},{\mathfrak{W}})=\mathop{\rm Rep}\nolimits({\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{R}})
is the same as the category of right
{\mathcal{R}}
-modules. Thus Theorem
4.2.3
generalizes the main theorem in
[
19
]
!

5.
Finiteness conditions on modules of a presheaf of algebras

In
[
19
]
, we considered objects of finite type in the module category
\rMod{\mathfrak{R}}
, from the point of view of
[
17
]
. However, these modules have a geometric flavor, and there is a distinct way to introduce modules of finite type, based on slice categories. Here we shall compare these two finiteness conditions, and show that they are different.

5.1.
Finiteness conditions on objects of an abelian category

Here we state the finiteness conditions from a categorical viewpoint by Popescu
[
17
]
.

Definition 5.1.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be an Ab5 category and
X\in\mathop{\rm Ob}\nolimits({\mathcal{A}})
. A directed set
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of subobjects of
X
is called stationary if there exists an index
i_{0}
such that
X_{i}\subset X_{i_{0}}
for any
i\in I
, and it is complete if
\Sigma_{i\in I}X_{i}=X
.

If any complete direct set
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of subobjects of
X
is stationary, then
X
is said to be of finite type.

It is known that in a module category
{\mathcal{A}}=
Mod-
A
, an object is of finite type if and only if it’s a finitely generated
A
-module.

5.2.
Finiteness conditions on modules of a presheaf of algebras

In the case of Mod-
{\mathfrak{R}}
, there is a geometric description of finite type modules, see
[
11
, 18.23.1]
, which we recall now.

Note that we will focus on the case where
{\mathcal{C}}
carries the minimal topology, which means that on every object
x\in\mathop{\rm Ob}\nolimits{\mathcal{C}}
there is only one covering sieve, the maximal sieve corresponding to the representable functor, or the set of all morphisms ending at
x
. Under the minimal topology, every presheaf is a sheaf. In this situation, the definition is simplified as follows.

Definition 5.2.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{V}}
be an
{\mathfrak{R}}
-module. Then it is of finite type if for every
x\in\mathop{\rm Ob}\nolimits{\mathcal{C}}
,
{\mathcal{V}}|_{x}
is a quotient of
{\mathfrak{R}}|_{x}^{n_{x}}=({\mathfrak{R}}|_{x})^{n_{x}}
for some positive integer
n_{x}
.

Here
{\mathcal{V}}|_{x}
is the restriction of
{\mathcal{V}}
along the canonical functor
\pi_{x}:{\mathcal{C}}/x\to{\mathcal{C}}
, where
{\mathcal{C}}/x
is the
slice category
at
x
. By definition, the objects of
{\mathcal{C}}/x
are of the form
(w,\alpha)
, where
\alpha:w\to x
is a morphism in
{\mathcal{C}}
, and a morphism
(w,\alpha)\to(v,\beta)
is given by a morphism
\gamma:w\to v
in
{\mathcal{C}}
such that
\alpha=\beta\gamma
.

5.3.
Comparison of finiteness conditions

We aim to show that the aforementioned finiteness conditions are different on Mod-
{\mathfrak{R}}
. There are two canonical right
{\mathfrak{R}}
-modules: one is
{\mathfrak{R}}
itself and the other one corresponds to the regular
{\mathfrak{R}}[{\mathcal{C}}]
-module, which as a presheaf
{\mathcal{V}}
of
k
-modules is given by
{\mathcal{V}}(x)=\oplus_{y}k\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{C}}}(x,y)\otimes_{k}{\mathfrak{R}}(x)
.

Proposition 5.3.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a finite category and
{\mathfrak{R}}
be a presheaf of
k
-algebra. Then the
{\mathfrak{R}}
-modules of finite type are exactly the finitely generated
{\mathfrak{R}}[{\mathcal{C}}]
-modules.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{V}}
be an
{\mathfrak{R}}
-module of finite type. Then for every
x\in\mathop{\rm Ob}\nolimits{\mathcal{C}}
,
{\mathcal{V}}|_{x}
is a quotient of
{\mathfrak{R}}|_{x}^{n_{x}}=({\mathfrak{R}}|_{x})^{n_{x}}
for some positive integer
n_{x}
. Particularly
{\mathcal{V}}|_{x}(x,1_{x})={\mathcal{V}}(x)
is a quotient of
{\mathfrak{R}}|_{x}^{n_{x}}(x,1_{x})={\mathfrak{R}}(x)^{n_{x}}
. Write
\phi_{x}:{\mathfrak{R}}(x)^{n_{x}}\to{\mathcal{V}}(x)
for the surjective homomorphism of right
{\mathfrak{R}}(x)
-modules. If
{\mathfrak{R}}
is a presheaf of finite-dimensional
k
-algebras, then every
{\mathcal{V}}(x)
is finite-dimensional, therefore the module
M=\oplus_{x}{\mathcal{V}}(x)
corresponding to
{\mathcal{V}}
is finite-dimensional, and it is a finitely generated
{\mathfrak{R}}[{\mathcal{C}}]
-module.
∎

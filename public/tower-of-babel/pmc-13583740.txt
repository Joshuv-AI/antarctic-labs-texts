Efficacy and safety of mycophenolate mofetil in preventing relapse of IgG4-related disease with re-elevation of serum IgG4 level during maintenance therapy: a protocol for a multicentre, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled study in China

Introduction

IgG4-related disease (IgG4-RD) is a chronic, immune-mediated fibroinflammatory disease that can involve virtually any organ system, most often the salivary glands, lacrimal glands, pancreas, bile ducts, kidneys and lungs.1 It is histopathologically characterised by a dense lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate rich in IgG4-positive plasma cells, storiform fibrosis and often obliterative phlebitis.2 Clinically, patients may present with diffuse or localised organ enlargement, and elevated serum IgG4 concentrations are observed in most cases.3–5 Although initial responses to glucocorticoids (GCs) are typically favourable, disease relapse is common, and recurrent inflammation may cause progressive and sometimes irreversible organ damage.6

GCs remain the cornerstone of induction therapy for IgG4-RD, and biologics such as rituximab or inebilizumab are also recognised as effective first-line options; maintenance treatment usually involves low-dose GCs with or without immunosuppressants (IMs).7 Long-term or repeated steroid use is associated with significant adverse effects, including osteoporosis, avascular necrosis, hypertension, steroid-induced diabetes and infection. Moreover, the relapse rate after dose reduction or withdrawal can reach 30%–50% within 3 years,8 underscoring the need for effective relapse prevention strategies. The 2015 International Consensus on the Treatment of IgG4-RD recommends the addition of IMs—such as mycophenolate mofetil (MMF), azathioprine or methotrexate—for patients at high risk of relapse or with steroid dependence.7

Serum IgG4 is one of the most important biomarkers to monitor the disease activity, which decreases with disease stabilisation and usually re-elevation at recurrence. During treatment tapering and maintaining stage, some patients undergo serum IgG4 re-elevation. Although such re-elevation does not invariably indicate clinical relapse, progressive increase of serum IgG4 to a high extent has been shown to predict recurrence.9 10 In clinical practice, waiting for an overt clinical relapse often implies that irreversible fibrotic organ damage has already occurred. Therefore, a significant re-elevation of serum IgG4 provides a critical ‘window of opportunity’ for pre-emptive intervention to halt subclinical inflammation and prevent permanent organ dysfunction. In addition, our previous study showed a significant decrease in the relapse rate in patients with re-elevated serum IgG4 level who added therapy.10 These findings suggest that rising serum IgG4 levels may precede clinical relapse, providing a potential window for pre-emptive intervention. As highlighted in a recent authoritative review,11 a rising serum IgG4 should not be ignored; empiric treatment of a rising serum IgG4 may be warranted. However, although rising serum IgG4 may herald subclinical disease activity, clinicians are cautioned against treating the biomarker in isolation; the optimal timing and patient selection for intensified immunosuppression therefore remain unclear and should be evaluated in randomised clinical trials.

MMF, an inosine monophosphate dehydrogenase inhibitor, has shown promise in IgG4-RD maintenance therapy due to its immunomodulatory effects and generally favourable safety profile. Clinical studies suggest that GCs combined with MMF can increase complete remission rates and reduce relapse risk compared with GCs alone.12 Furthermore, our recent cohort study demonstrated that tapering or discontinuation of MMF was associated with a markedly higher risk of relapse, underscoring the importance of optimised MMF use in long-term management of IgG4-RD.13

This study is a multicentre, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled study designed to evaluate whether adding low-dose MMF to maintenance therapy in IgG4-RD patients with re-elevation of serum IgG4 level can reduce relapse rates and prolong relapse-free survival.

Materials and analysis

Study design

This study is a multicentre, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group clinical trial designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of MMF in preventing relapse among IgG4-RD patients with re-elevation of serum IgG4 level during maintenance therapy. The planned study period spans from August 2023 to April 2026. Eligible participants will be randomised 1:1 to receive MMF or matching placebo, in addition to their current maintenance regimen, for 52 weeks. The study will be conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and the International Council for Harmonisation Good Clinical Practice (ICH-GCP) guidelines. The protocol follows the SPIRIT 2025 recommendations.

Study setting and participating centres

This trial is conducted at five tertiary referral hospitals in China, all of which have extensive clinical experience in the diagnosis and management of IgG4-RD. The Peking Union Medical College Hospital (PUMCH) serves as the coordinating centre, responsible for overall trial management, randomisation, central data collection and statistical analysis. Each participating site will recruit eligible patients from both inpatient and outpatient departments, and all investigators will be trained in the standardised application of inclusion/exclusion criteria, outcome assessments and adverse event reporting to ensure protocol adherence and intercentre consistency.

Participants

Eligible participants will be patients aged 18–70 years with IgG4-RD diagnosed according to the 2019 American College of Rheumatology (ACR)/European League Against Rheumatism (EULAR) classification criteria or the 2020 revised comprehensive diagnostic criteria for IgG4-RD,4 5 currently in the maintenance phase of treatment with low-dose GCs or combined with IMs.

The major inclusion criteria require that IgG4-RD patients have an elevation of baseline serum IgG4 level (>1400 mg/L) and experience a re-elevation of serum IgG4 level to ≥30% of the baseline level during the maintenance remission period, with no evidence of relapse.14 The exclusion criteria aim to minimise confounding factors, avoid recent changes in immunosuppressive treatment and ensure participant safety during the trial. The key inclusion and exclusion criteria are summarised in online supplemental table 1.

Recruitment and informed consent

Participants meeting the eligibility criteria (online supplemental table 1) will be invited to join the study during routine clinical visits. Trained investigators will provide verbal and written information about the trial and obtain written informed consent (see online supplemental file 1 for the model participant consent form) before any study-related procedures. Informed consent will be obtained by the investigator at each participating site in a private setting before enrolment.

Randomisation and blinding

Eligible participants will be randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to the MMF group or the placebo group. The randomisation sequence will be generated in advance by an independent statistician at the coordinating centre using computer-generated random numbers. To ensure a balanced allocation between the treatment groups across different sites, a permuted block randomisation method stratified by participating centre will be employed. The allocation sequence will be securely provided to the central pharmacy, which will prepare numbered study drug kits accordingly. These kits will be distributed to each site and dispensed to participants in enrolment order by investigators who remain blinded to allocation.

This is a double-blind trial: participants and investigators will remain unaware of treatment allocation until study completion. MMF and placebo tablets will be identical in appearance, packaging and labelling. Emergency unblinding will be allowed only when necessary for the clinical management of serious adverse events, with all instances documented by the coordinating centre.

Intervention

All participants will continue their existing maintenance therapy, which may include GCs and/or IMs. During the clinical trial, the baseline treatment (including GCs and/or IMs) is not allowed to be tapered, increased in dosage or changed to other kinds of IMs. Any change in the baseline treatment will be considered a treatment failure, and the participant will be withdrawn from the study. Participants randomised to the intervention group will receive oral MMF 0.5 g daily (a low-dose regimen selected to balance pre-emptive efficacy and safety, based on our previous clinical trial),12 while those in the control group will receive matching placebo tablets (figure 1).

Study medication will be taken orally for 52 consecutive weeks. Except for the existing immunosuppressive agents, other IMs for IgG4-RD during the study period will not be permitted, unless in cases of confirmed relapse or adverse events requiring discontinuation of the study drug, as determined by the investigator. In the event of a confirmed clinical relapse, the study medication will be discontinued, and the participant will promptly receive appropriate rescue therapy at the investigator’s discretion. This typically involves escalating the GC dosage and/or switching to alternative IMs (eg, cyclophosphamide, rituximab) in accordance with standard clinical guidelines. Concomitant medications for other comorbidities will be allowed if judged not to affect study outcomes.

Study objectives and outcomes

Study objectives

The objective of this trial is to determine whether adding MMF to maintenance therapy can reduce the risk of relapse in IgG4-RD patients with re-elevation of serum IgG4 level to exceeding the upper limit of normal and ≥ 30% increase in serum IgG4 of baseline during the maintenance phase but without clinical relapse.

Primary outcome

The primary outcome is the difference in the relapse rate of IgG4-RD between the two groups at week 52. Clinical relapse is defined as an elevation of the IgG4-RD Responder Index (RI) by ≥2 points due to new organ involvement or recurrence (confirmed by clinical, imaging, or histopathological evaluations), with or without the elevation of serum IgG4 levels.15

Secondary outcomes

Secondary outcomes include: (1) the difference in the relapse rate of IgG4-RD between the two groups at week 12, week 24 and week 36; (2) the time and duration of relapse; (3) the changes in the IgG4-RD RI at week 52; (4) the changes in serum IgG and IgG4 levels at week 52; (5) the changes in erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR) and serum C-reactive protein (CRP) levels at week 52; (6) adverse effect events throughout the study period and (7) the changes in the Physician Global Assessment (PGA) score at week 52. If participants are unable to attend scheduled on-site follow-up visits, essential clinical information will be collected through telephone follow-up whenever possible.

Sample size calculation

The sample size was calculated using the Power and Sample Size programme (HyLown Consulting LLC, Atlanta, GA, USA). Based on previous prospective cohort data from our research group, the relapse rate among IgG4-RD patients who experienced a rise in serum IgG4 during maintenance therapy was approximately 45%–55% if no change in treatment was made.8 In contrast, intensified treatment in this population reduced the relapse rate to around 20%.13 For this trial, we assumed a relapse rate of 45.8% in the placebo group and 20% in the MMF group. With a two-sided α of 0.05 and a power of 80%, the minimum required sample size was calculated to be 49 participants per group. Allowing for an anticipated 10% loss to follow-up, the final sample size was set at 54 participants per group, yielding a total of 108 participants.

Statistical analysis

Primary analysis

The primary analysis will compare the relapse-free survival between the MMF and placebo groups. Time-to-event outcomes will be analysed using Kaplan–Meier survival curves and compared using the log-rank test. HRs and 95% CIs will be estimated using Cox proportional hazards regression, adjusting for relevant baseline covariates (eg, age, sex, baseline serum IgG4 level, baseline GC dose and concurrent IM use).

Secondary analyses

Baseline characteristics will be summarised using descriptive statistics. Continuous variables will be expressed as mean±SD if normally distributed or as median (IQR) if not. Categorical variables will be presented as counts and percentages. Between-group comparisons will be conducted using the independent samples t-test or the Mann–Whitney U test as appropriate. For comparisons involving more than two groups (eg, stratified analyses based on different extents of IgG4 re-elevation), one-way analysis of variance or the Kruskal–Wallis test will be used. Categorical variables will be compared using the χ² test or Fisher’s exact test. To analyse longitudinal secondary outcomes assessed at multiple timepoints (ie, weeks 12, 24, 36 and 52), generalised estimating equations (GEE) or linear mixed models (LMM) will be utilised to account for intrasubject correlation across repeated measurements. Specifically, LMMs will be applied for continuous variables measured over time (eg, changes in serum IgG4, IgG, ESR, CRP and PGA scores), incorporating treatment, time and the treatment-by-time interaction as fixed effects, and subjects as random effects. For binary repeated measures (eg, relapse status at specific timepoints), GEE models with a logit link function will be employed. Correlation analyses between clinical parameters, laboratory results and relapse risk will be performed using Pearson’s or Spearman’s correlation coefficients, as appropriate. Missing data will be handled using multiple imputation under the assumption of missing at random. No interim analyses are planned.

Statistical software

All analyses will be performed using SPSS V.24.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) and R software (V.4.3.1; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). A two-sided p value<0.05 will be considered statistically significant.

Data management and monitoring

All data will be collected using standardised case report forms at each study visit. Study coordinators at each site will enter data into a centralised, password-protected electronic database managed by the coordinating centre at PUMCH. Data entry will be independently performed by two trained personnel (double data entry) to ensure accuracy, and logical and range checks will be applied to identify missing or implausible values, which will be resolved by querying site investigators. For patients unable to attend scheduled visits, telephone follow-up will be conducted to ensure complete data collection.

Confidentiality of participant information will be maintained in accordance with national data protection regulations. Each participant will be identified in the database by a unique study ID, and no personally identifiable information will be disclosed in reports or publications.

Ethics and dissemination

This study has obtained ethical approval from the Ethics Committee of Peking Union Medical College Hospital (approval no. K3231, see online supplemental file 2 for the ethical approval document). Additionally, ethical approval has been formally obtained from the respective ethics committees of the other actively recruiting participating centres: The Second Hospital of Dalian Medical University, Beijing Friendship Hospital (Capital Medical University) and Tongji Hospital (Tongji Medical College, Huazhong University of Science and Technology). As for Shandong Provincial Hospital, although it is listed among the collaborating institutions, this site does not independently undertake participant enrolment or study procedures. Patients who are potentially eligible and identified there are referred to PUMCH, where all screening, enrolment, randomisation, intervention and follow-up assessments are conducted. Therefore, this centre does not directly participate in the research activities governed by the study protocol, and an independent local ethics approval was not required. Written informed consent will be obtained from all participants. The trial will be conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and relevant national regulations.

Findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed journals and scientific conferences. No individual participant will be identifiable in any publication.

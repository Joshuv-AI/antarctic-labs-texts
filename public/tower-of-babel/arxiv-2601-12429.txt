Localization and interpolation of parabolic L p L^{p} Neumann problems
Martin Dindoš, Linhan Li, Jill Pipher

Abstract
. We show a localization estimate for local solutions to the parabolic equation − ∂ t u + div ( A ∇ u ) = 0 -\partial_{t}u+\divg(A\nabla u)=0 with zero Neumann data, assuming that the L p L^{p} Neumann problem and L p ′ L^{p^{\prime}} Dirichlet problem for the adjoint operator are solvable in a Lipschitz cylinder for some p ∈ ( 1 , ∞ ) p\in(1,\infty) . Using this result, we establish the solvability of the Neumann problem in the atomic Hardy space for parabolic operators with bounded, measurable, time-dependent coefficients, and hence obtain the interpolation of solvability of the L p L^{p} Neumann problem.



1.
Introduction

The main objective of this paper is to establish a localization result for a parabolic Neumann problem for equations of the form

Lu=-\partial_{t}u+\divg(A\nabla u)=0
. The coefficients of the elliptic matrix
A
are assumed to be bounded, measurable and are allowed to be time varying. In both the elliptic and parabolic settings, such an estimate is key to developing a complete theory of
L^{p}
solvability of boundary value problems. Indeed, we go on to show (Section
5
) how our estimate can be used for interpolation of Neumann solvability, i.e., assuming the solvability of the
L^{p}
parabolic Neumann problem (and also its adjoint
L^{p^{\prime}}
Dirichlet problem)
one can obtain solvability of the
L^{q}
parabolic Neumann problem for all
1<q\leq p
.
The localization estimate says that, on the portion of the Neumann boundary where the datum vanishes, the nontangential maximal function of the gradient
\nabla u
has an upper bound in terms of
\nabla u
just on an enlarged Carleson region. Localization
in the context of Neumann boundary
conditions
has previously only been established for certain special operators such as the heat operator using different methods (where solvability on subdomains was used).

More precisely, with appropriate assumptions on the

L^{p}
solvability of a parabolic Neumann problem,
we seek to establish the estimate

(1.1)

\|\widetilde{N}(|\nabla u|{\mathds{1}}_{Q})\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\leq Cr^{(n+1)/p}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.675pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.02336pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-2.53589pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-1.7709pt}}\!\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{2Q\cap\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dXdt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

for a parabolic cube
Q
centered at a boundary point such that the Neumann data
\partial^{A}_{\nu}u=0
in
2Q\cap\partial\Omega
. Here
Lu=0
, where

(1.2)

Lu:=-\partial_{t}u+\divg(A\nabla u)=0\quad\text{in }\Omega,

and the domain
\Omega=\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R}
is a bounded or unbounded Lipschitz cylinder, i.e.,
\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
or
\mathcal{O}
is a bounded or unbounded Lipschitz domain. We assume that
A=[a_{ij}(X,t)]
is a
n\times n
bounded matrix satisfying the uniform ellipticity condition for a.e.
X\in\mathcal{O}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
That is, there exists positive constants
\lambda
and
\Lambda
such that

(1.3)

\lambda|\xi|^{2}\leq\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}(X,t)\xi_{i}\xi_{j},\qquad|A(X,t)|\leq\Lambda,

for almost every
(X,t)\in\Omega
and all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Estimates of the form (
1.1
) for both elliptic and parabolic PDEs have multiple uses and serve as a tool for establishing Hardy space atom estimates for both Regularity and Neumann boundary value problems. When instead of the vanishing Neumann boundary condition we assume vanishing Dirichlet condition, the estimate is rather easy to prove under the assumption that the elliptic or (parabolic) measure of the adjoint PDE belongs to the
A_{\infty}(d\sigma)
class. In this case the estimate holds for
p\in(1,p_{0})
,
where
p_{0}
is the value such that for all
q>p_{0}^{\prime}
the
L^{q}
Dirichlet problem for the adjoint operator
L^{*}
is solvable. See for example
[
8
]
treating the elliptic case when the boundary is Lipschitz, or
[
9
]
for the setting of parabolic PDEs on a Lipschitz cylinder.

The parabolic Dirichlet localization result has been used in, for example, the paper
[
9
]
where interpolation and atomic estimates for the Regularity problem were established, or
[
10
]
where the Regularity problem for the parabolic PDE is solved in the optimal range on a Lipschitz cylinder
under the assumption that the coefficients of the PDE satisfy certain natural Carleson conditions (a parabolic analogue of so-called DKP-condition). There are also a number of earlier papers making use of localization estimates to show Hardy space estimates in the context of Dirichlet, Regularity or Neumann boundary data.

The first and main objective of this paper is to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\Omega=\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R}
where
\Omega
is either a bounded or unbounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
L=-\partial_{t}+\divg(A\nabla\cdot)
, and let
L^{*}=\partial_{t}+\divg(A^{T}\nabla\cdot)
be the adjoint operator of
L
.
Let
p\in(1,\infty)
. Suppose that
\rm{(}\rm{N}_{p}\rm{)}
L
and
\rm{(}\rm{D}_{p^{\prime}}\rm{)}
{}^{L^{*}}
are solvable in
\Omega
(see Definitions
2.20
,
2.21
), then for any parabolic cube
Q=Q_{r}(x,t)
centered at some
(x,t)\in\partial\Omega
, and any local weak solution
u
to
Lu=0
in
2Q\cap\Omega
with zero Neumann data on
2Q\cap\partial\Omega
, we have

\|\widetilde{N}(|\nabla u|{\mathds{1}}_{Q})\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\leq Cr^{(n+1)/p}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.675pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.02336pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-2.53589pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-1.7709pt}}\!\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{2Q\cap\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dXdt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Remark 1.5
.

A note on the proof strategy: It might seem natural to follow the original strategy taken in
[
15
, Theorem 6.10]
which established Neumann localization in the elliptic setting. The proof of their result required assuming solvability of both the
L^{p}
Neumann and Regularity problems, and using the Regularity result in a key fashion. However, in the parabolic case, we wish to avoid working via the
L^{p}
Regularity problem as it involves a half-derivative in time on the boundary data, making localization estimates significantly more difficult to obtain. Fortunately, an analogous elliptic Neumann localization result for more general boundaries was established in
[
13
]
(along with its implications), via a method that dispensed with the assumption on the Regularity problem. In
[
13
]
, they achieved the desired localization estimate by introducing and studying the Poisson–Neumann problem and its weak variants. This is
the strategy we employ here, introducing certain analogous parabolic problems and adapting the arguments of
[
13
]
to the time dependent parabolic setting.

The second objective of this paper is to present an important application of the localization estimate, formulated in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\Omega=\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R}
, where

\mathcal{O}=\{(x,x_{n}):x_{n}>\varphi(x)\},\qquad\mbox{for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1},

for a Lipschitz function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}
. Consider the PDE

Lu=-\partial_{t}u+\divg(A\nabla u)=0\quad\text{in }\Omega.

Assume that for some
p\in(1,\infty)
the
L^{p}
Neumann problem
\rm{(}\rm{N}_{p}\rm{)}
L
and the
L^{p^{\prime}}
Dirichlet problem
\rm{(}\rm{D}_{p^{\prime}}\rm{)}
{}^{L^{*}}
for the adjoint PDE
L^{*}u=0
are both solvable (as defined in Section
2
).

Then for all
1<q<p
the
L^{q}
Neumann problem
\rm{(}\rm{N}_{q}\rm{)}
L
is also solvable.

For specific operators (such as the heat equation on Lipschitz cylinders, or even more general domains, this result is known (c.f.
[
4
]
)), but here it is established for operators with no particular assumptions on coefficients beyond ellipticity. Hence this result is more analogous to previously established elliptic theory for the Neumann problem as in
[
15
]
. Further, we expect that this interpolation result will be very
useful for studying the Neumann problem for classes of specific operators. One such example is our paper in preparation
[
11
]
, applying the main result here to a subclass of operators of the type considered in
[
10
]
and showing optimal
L^{p}
solvability of the Neumann problem for such PDEs when the Carleson measure condition on the coefficients is assumed to be sufficiently small.

Definitions

Definition 2.1
.

A parabolic cube on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
centered at
(X,t)
with sidelength
r
as

Q_{r}(X,t):=\{(Y,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}:|x_{i}-y_{i}|<r\ \text{ for }1\leq i\leq n,\ |t-s|^{1/2}<r\},

and we write
\ell(Q_{r})=r
.
When writing a lower case point
(x,t)
we shall mean a boundary parabolic cube on
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}

which has an analogous definition but in one less spatial dimension:

(2.2)

Q_{r}(x,t):=\{(y,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}:|x_{i}-y_{i}|<r\ \text{ for }1\leq i\leq n-1,\ |t-s|^{1/2}<r\}.

We use the notation
Q^{\prime}_{r}(X)
to denote the cube in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
centered at
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with sidelength
r
, unless specified otherwise.

A parabolic ball on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
centered at
(X,t)
with radius
r
is the ball

(2.3)

B_{r}(X,t):=\{(Y,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}:d_{p}((X,t),(Y,s))<r\},

where
d_{p}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the parabolic distance function

d_{p}((X,t),(Y,s)):=\left(\left|X-Y\right|^{2}+\left|t-s\right|\right)^{1/2}.

For parabolic balls at the boundary we use notation
\Delta_{r}(X,t)=B_{r}(X,t)\cap\partial\Omega
. In the special case
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}
we drop the last coordinate and also write
\Delta_{r}(x,t)
with understanding that the ball is centered at
(X,t)=(x,0,t)
.

Definition 2.4
.

For
a>0
and
(q,\tau)\in\partial\Omega
, unless otherwise defined, we denote the non-tangential parabolic cones by

(2.5)

\Gamma_{a}(q,\tau):=\left\{(X,t)\in\Omega:d_{p}((X,t),(q,\tau))<(1+a)\delta(X,t)\right\},

and
\delta(\cdot)
is the parabolic distance to the boundary:

\delta(X,t)=\inf_{(q,\tau)\in\partial\Omega}d_{p}((X,t),(q,\tau)).

We also use the truncated parabolic cones: for
r>0
,

\Gamma_{a}^{r}(q,\tau):=\left\{(X,t)\in\Omega:d((X,t),(q,\tau))<(1+a)\delta(X,t),\,\delta(X,t)<r\right\}

is the parabolic cone with vertex
(q,\tau)
truncated at height
r
.

Tent spaces

Definition 2.6
.

For
w\in L^{\infty}_{\loc}(\Omega)
, we define the non-tangential maximal function of
w
as

N_{a}(w)(q,\tau):=\sup_{(X,t)\in\Gamma_{a}(q,\tau)}\left|w(X,t)\right|\quad\text{for }(q,\tau)\in\partial\Omega,

\mathcal{A}_{1}(w)(q,\tau):=\iint_{\Gamma(q,\tau)}|w(X,t)|\frac{dXdt}{\delta(X,t)^{n+2}},

If

\widetilde{N}_{a,p}^{c}(w)(q,\tau):=\sup_{(X,t)\in\Gamma_{a}(q,\tau)}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.675pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.02336pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-2.53589pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-1.7709pt}}\!\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q_{c\delta(X,t)/2}(X,t)}\left|w(Y,s)\right|^{p}dYds\right)^{1/p}\quad\text{for }(q,\tau)\in\partial\Omega,

the modified parabolic area functional

\mathcal{\widetilde{A}}^{a,c}_{1,p}(w)(q,\tau):=\iint_{\Gamma_{a}(q,\tau)}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.675pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.02336pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-2.53589pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-1.7709pt}}\!\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q_{c\delta(X,t)/2}(X,t)}\left|w(Y,s)\right|^{p}dYds\right)^{1/p}\frac{dXdt}{\delta(X,t)^{n+2}},

\mathcal{\widetilde{C}}^{c}_{1,p}(w)(q,\tau):=\sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{\sigma(B_{r}(q,\tau)\cap\partial\Omega)}\iint_{B_{r}(q,\tau)\cap\Omega}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.675pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.02336pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-2.53589pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-1.7709pt}}\!\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q_{c\delta(X,t)/2}(X,t)}\left|w(Y,s)\right|^{p}dYds\right)^{1/p}\frac{dXdt}{\delta(X,t)}.

\widetilde{N}(w):=\widetilde{N}_{1,2}^{1/2}(w),\quad\mathcal{\widetilde{A}}(w):=\mathcal{\widetilde{A}}_{1,2}^{1,1/2}(w),\quad\mathcal{\widetilde{C}}:=\mathcal{\widetilde{C}}_{1,2}^{1/2}.

2.2.
Reinforced weak and Energy solutions

We recall the paper
[
2
]
that neatly presents the concept of reinforced weak solutions for the parabolic problem of interest here. In
[
6
]
, it was shown that the definition given in
[
2
]
can be weakened a bit further by only asking for the “local
1/2
derivative” in the time variable. We explain below.

If
\mathcal{O}
is an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we let
\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathcal{O})=\mathcal{W}^{1,2}(\mathcal{O})
be the standard Sobolev space of real valued functions
v
defined on
\mathcal{O}
, such that
v
and
\nabla v
are in
L^{2}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R})
and
L^{2}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{n})
, respectively. A subscripted ‘
\loc
’ will indicate that these conditions hold locally.

We shall say that
u
is a
reinforced weak solution
of
-\partial_{t}u+\divg(A\nabla u)=0
on
\Omega=\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R}
if

\displaystyle u\in\dot{\mathsf{E}}_{\loc}(\Omega):=\mathcal{H}_{\loc}^{1/2}(\mathbb{R};L^{2}_{\loc}(\mathcal{O}))\cap L_{\operatorname{loc}}^{2}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{W}^{1,2}_{\loc}(\mathcal{O}))

and if for all
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
,

(2.16)

\iint_{\Omega}\left[A\nabla u\cdot{\nabla(\phi\psi)}+H_{t}D_{t}^{1/2}(u\psi)\cdot{D_{t}^{1/2}\phi}+H_{t}D_{t}^{1/2}(u\phi)\cdot{D_{t}^{1/2}\psi}\right]\,\,\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}t=0.

Here,
D_{t}^{1/2}
is the half-order derivative and
H_{t}
is the Hilbert transform with respect to the
t
variable, normalized so that
\partial_{t}=D_{t}^{1/2}H_{t}D_{t}^{1/2}
. The space
\dot{H}{\vphantom{H}}^{1/2}(\mathbb{R})
is the homogeneous Sobolev space of order 1/2 - the completion of
\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
in the norm
\|D_{t}^{1/2}(\cdot)\|_{2}
- and it embeds into the space
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})/\mathbb{C}
of tempered distributions, modulo constants).
The local space
\mathcal{H}_{\loc}^{1/2}(\mathbb{R})
consists of functions
u
such that
u\phi\in\dot{H}{\vphantom{H}}^{1/2}(\mathbb{R})
for all
\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. As shown in
[
6
]
the space
\mathcal{H}_{\loc}^{1/2}(\mathbb{R})
is larger than
\dot{H}{\vphantom{H}}^{1/2}(\mathbb{R})
.
For
u\in\dot{H}{\vphantom{H}}^{1/2}(\mathbb{R};L^{2}_{\loc}(\mathcal{O})),
(
2.16
) simplifies to

\iint_{\Omega}\left[A\nabla u\cdot{\nabla\phi}+H_{t}D_{t}^{1/2}u\cdot{D_{t}^{1/2}\phi}\right]\,\,\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}t=0.

Our definition has the advantage that, in taking a cut-off of the function
u
, we might potentially improve the decay of
D^{1/2}_{t}u
at infinity.

At this point we remark that for any
u\in\dot{H}{\vphantom{H}}^{1/2}(\mathbb{R})
and
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
the formula

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[H_{t}D_{t}^{1/2}(u\psi)\cdot{D_{t}^{1/2}\phi}+H_{t}D_{t}^{1/2}(u\phi)\cdot{D_{t}^{1/2}\psi}\right]\,\mathrm{d}t=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}u\cdot{\partial_{t}(\phi\psi)}\,\mathrm{d}t

holds, where on the right-hand side we use the duality form extension of the complex inner product of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, between
\dot{H}{\vphantom{H}}^{1/2}(\mathbb{R})
and its dual
\dot{H}{\vphantom{H}}^{-1/2}(\mathbb{R})
. By taking
\psi=1
on the set where
\phi
is supported, it follows that a reinforced weak solution is a weak solution in the usual sense on
\Omega
since it satisfies

u\in L_{\operatorname{loc}}^{2}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{W}^{1,2}_{\loc}(\mathcal{O}))
and for all
\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
,

\displaystyle\iint_{\Omega}A\nabla u\cdot{\nabla\phi}\,\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}t-\iint_{\Omega}u\cdot{\partial_{t}\phi}\,\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}t=0.

This implies
\partial_{t}u\in L_{\operatorname{loc}}^{2}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{W}^{-1,2}_{\loc}(\mathcal{O}))
. Conversely, any weak solution
u
in
\mathcal{H}_{\loc}^{1/2}(\mathbb{R};L^{2}_{\loc}(\mathcal{O}))
is a reinforced weak solution.

Specialising to the case
\Omega=\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R}
, where
\mathcal{O}
is either bounded or unbounded Lipschitz domain,
we say that a
reinforced weak solution
v\in\dot{\mathsf{E}}_{\loc}(\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R})
belongs to the
energy class

\dot{\mathsf{E}}(\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R})
if

\displaystyle\|v\|_{\dot{\mathsf{E}}}:=\bigg(\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R})}^{2}+\|H_{t}D_{t}^{1/2}v\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R})}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}<\infty.

Consequently, these are called
energy solutions
. When considered modulo constants,
\dot{\mathsf{E}}
is a Hilbert space and it is in fact the closure of
\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}\!\big(\,\overline{\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R}}\,\big)
for the homogeneous norm
\|\cdot\|_{\dot{\mathsf{E}}}

and it coincides with the space
\dot{L}^{2}_{1,1/2}(\Omega)
.

As shown in
[
2
]
(with a small generalization), functions from
\dot{\mathsf{E}}
have well defined Dirichlet traces with values in
the
homogeneous parabolic Sobolev space

\dot{H}{\vphantom{H}}^{1/4}_{\partial_{t}-\Delta_{x}}(\partial\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R})
. Here,
\dot{H}{\vphantom{H}}^{s}_{\pm\partial_{t}-\Delta_{x}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is defined as the closure of Schwartz functions
v\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
with Fourier support away from the origin in the norm
\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}((|\xi|^{2}\pm i\tau)^{s}\mathcal{F}v)\|_{2}
. This yields a space of tempered distributions modulo constants in
L_{\operatorname{loc}}^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
if
0<s\leq 1/2
.
Conversely, any
g\in\dot{H}{\vphantom{H}}^{1/4}_{\partial_{t}-\Delta_{x}}
can be extended to a function
v\in\dot{\mathsf{E}}
with trace
v\big|_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}=g
. For this via partition of unity we can define

\dot{H}{\vphantom{H}}^{1/4}_{\partial_{t}-\Delta_{x}}(\partial\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R})
for
\mathcal{O}
Lipschitz.

Hence, by the energy solution to
-\partial_{t}u+\divg(A\nabla u)=0
with Dirichlet boundary datum
u\big|_{\partial\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R}}=f\in\dot{H}{\vphantom{H}}^{1/4}_{\partial_{t}-\Delta_{x}}
(understood in the trace sense) we mean
u\in\dot{\mathsf{E}}
such that

\displaystyle a(u,v):=\iint_{\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R}}\left[A\nabla u\cdot{\nabla v}+H_{t}D_{t}^{1/2}u\cdot{D_{t}^{1/2}v}\right]\,\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}t=0,

holds for all
v\in\dot{\mathsf{E}}_{0}
, the subspace of
\dot{\mathsf{E}}
with zero boundary trace.

Moving onto the Neumann problem, given any
u\in\dot{\mathsf{E}}(\Omega)
, the co-normal derivative
\partial^{A}_{\nu}u\Big|_{\partial\Omega}=:\langle A\nabla u,\nu\rangle\Big|_{\partial\Omega}=g
is defined via the formula

(2.17)

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R}}\left[A\nabla u\cdot{\nabla v}+H_{t}D_{t}^{1/2}u\cdot{D_{t}^{1/2}v}\right]\,\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}t-\int_{\partial O\times\mathbb{R}}gv\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=0,

for all
v\in\dot{\mathsf{E}}
. Here, since the traces of
v
belong to
\dot{H}{\vphantom{H}}^{1/4}_{\partial_{t}-\Delta_{x}}(\partial\Omega)
and all elements of the space
\dot{H}{\vphantom{H}}^{1/4}_{\partial_{t}-\Delta_{x}}
are realized by some
v\in\dot{\mathsf{E}}
,
the Neumann boundary data must by duality naturally belong to the space
\dot{H}{\vphantom{H}}^{-1/4}_{\partial_{t}-\Delta_{x}}(\partial\Omega)
.

By
[
2
]
, the key to solving these problems is the introduction of the modified sesquilinear form (introduced earlier in
[
18
]
):

(2.18)

a_{\delta}(u,v):=\iint_{\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R}}\left[A\nabla u\cdot{\nabla(1-\delta H_{t})v}+H_{t}D_{t}^{1/2}u\cdot{D_{t}^{1/2}(1-\delta H_{t})v}\right]\,\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}t,

where
\delta
is a real number yet to be chosen. The Hilbert transform
H_{t}
is a skew-symmetric isometric operator with inverse
-H_{t}
on both
\dot{\mathsf{E}}
and
\dot{H}{\vphantom{H}}^{1/4}_{\partial_{t}-\Delta_{x}}
. Hence,
1-\delta H_{t}
is invertible on these spaces for any
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
. Hence for a fixed
\delta>0
small enough,
a_{\delta}
is coercive on
\dot{\mathsf{E}}
since

(2.19)

a_{\delta}(u,u)\geq(\lambda-\Lambda\delta)\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\delta\|H_{t}D_{t}^{1/2}u\|_{2}^{2}.

In particular
\delta=\lambda/(\Lambda+1)
would work.
To solve the Dirichlet problem we take an extension
w\in\dot{\mathsf{E}}
of the data
f
and apply the Lax-Milgram lemma to

on
\dot{\mathsf{E}}_{0}
to obtain some
u\in\dot{\mathsf{E}}_{0}
such that

\displaystyle a_{\delta}(u,v)=-a_{\delta}(w,v)\qquad(v\in\dot{\mathsf{E}}_{0}).

Hence,
u+w
is an energy solution with data
f
. Should there exist another solution
v
, then
a_{\delta}(u+w-v,u+w-v)=0
and hence by coercivity
\|u+w-v\|_{\dot{\mathsf{E}}}=0
. Thus the two solutions only differ by a constant. It means that Dirichlet problem associated with our parabolic PDE is
well-posed
in the energy class. Similar arguments allow us to solve the Neumann problem by considering for the datum
g\in\dot{H}{\vphantom{H}}^{-1/4}_{\partial_{t}-\Delta_{x}}(\partial\Omega)
the solution
u\in\dot{E}
such that

\displaystyle a_{\delta}(u,v)=\langle g,\mbox{Tr }(1-\delta H_{t})v\rangle\qquad(v\in\dot{\mathsf{E}}).

Energy solutions for the Poisson problem.
We now address the existence of energy solutions for the inhomogeneous PDE

with zero Dirichlet and Neumann data.

To avoid any boundary terms arising from the pairing of the righthand side with a test function we shall assume that
h
is a bounded function with bounded support and
F
is a bounded function with compact support in
\Omega
.

Let
v\in\dot{\mathsf{E}}
be a test function. Then the pairing
\langle-\divg F,v\rangle
can be understood in the sense

\langle-\divg F,v\rangle=\iint_{\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R}}F\cdot\nabla v\,dXdt,

and since
\nabla v\in L^{2}(\Omega)
we need
\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}<\infty
, which certainly any bounded compactly supported
F
satisfies. The above defines a bounded functional on
\dot{\mathsf{E}}_{0}
for the Dirichlet problem and on
\dot{\mathsf{E}}
for the Neumann problem and thus we can solve both problems with a righthand side of the form
\divg F
.

Considering the righthand side for the term
h
for the Dirichlet problem is also not problematic but we will need to understand the embedding of the space
\dot{\mathsf{E}}_{0}
into the
L^{p}
spaces. It is stated in
[
2
]
Remark
3.13 (without proof) that the space
\dot{\mathsf{E}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})/\mathbb{C}
has realization in the Lebesgue space
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
for
q=2(n+2)/n
. From this (using an odd extension across the flat boundary) we get that
\dot{\mathsf{E}}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\times\mathbb{R})

embeds into
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\times\mathbb{R})
. Using a partition of unity, the embedding of
\dot{\mathsf{E}}_{0}(\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R})

into

follows. Hence it remains to see why the original claim as stated
in
[
2
]
holds. Taking the same approach as in Tao’s notes
[
19
, Exercise 41]
it suffices to understand
into which
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
belongs the convolution of an
L^{2}
function with the kernel

\hat{f}(\xi,\tau)=C_{n}\rho(\xi,\tau)^{-(n+1)},

where
\|(\xi,\tau)\|=\rho
, for
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\tau\in\mathbb{R}

and
\rho
is the positive solution to the following equation:

\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{\rho^{2}}+\frac{\tau^{2}}{\rho^{4}}=1.

It holds that
\rho\sim|\xi|+|\tau|^{1/2}
, and that

is the function
\rho(x,t)^{-1}=\|(x,t)\|^{-1}
. Observe that
\dot{\mathsf{E}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})

by

(2.22)

\|g\|_{\dot{\mathsf{E}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}=\|\mathbb{D}g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}=\left\|\|(\xi,\tau)\|\hat{g}(\xi,\tau)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}.

This then implies that

for some

. Analysis of the kernel

above reveals that it scales in

as

but in

. The final conclusion
requires understanding of fractional integrals with kernels of mixed homogeneity (
see Chapter 1 for the parabolic norm structure and Theorem 6.1 for the fractional integration result of
[
12
]
). This reveals that the resulting convolution belongs to the strong

for

as desired.

As

is bounded with bounded

the pairing

enjoys the bound

|\langle h,v\rangle|=\left|\iint_{\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R}}hv\,dXdt\right|\lesssim\|h\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}}\|v\|_{\dot{\mathsf{E}}_{0}},

and therefore defines a bounded linear functional on

. This again implies the existence of a solution for the Poisson Dirichlet problem with righthand side containing the term

. We do not consider the Neumann problem for the term

.

Basic estimates

We assume

where

Lemma 3.1

(Caccioppoli’s inequality)
.

Let
Q=Q_{r}(X,t)
be a parabolic cube that either satisfies
4Q=Q_{4r}(X,t)\subset\Omega
or
(X,t)\in\partial\Omega
(i.e. centered at
\partial\Omega
). Let
u
be a weak solution of
Lu=0
in
Q_{4r}(X,t)\cap\Omega
, and assume that
u
has zero Neumann data on
4Q\cap\partial\Omega
if
(X,t)\in\partial\Omega
.
Then there exists a constant
C=C(\lambda,\Lambda,n)
such that for any constant
c
,

\iint_{Q_{r}(X,t)\cap\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dYds\leq\frac{C}{r^{2}}\iint_{Q_{2r}(X,t)\cap\Omega}|u(Y,s)-c|^{2}dYds.

Proof.

The proof of the interior Caccioppoli is classical. For the case when
Q
is centered at
\partial\Omega
, we assume that
\mathcal{O}\cap 4Q=\left\{(x,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{n}>\varphi(x)\right\}\cap 4Q
for some Lipschitz function
\varphi
, and consider the even reflection of
u
defined as

(3.2)

\tilde{u}(x,x_{n},t)=\begin{cases}u(x,x_{n},t),&\qquad\mbox{for }x_{n}\geq\varphi(x),\\
u(x,2\varphi(x)-x_{n},t),&\qquad\mbox{for }x_{n}<\varphi(x).\end{cases}

Since
u
has zero Neumann data on
4Q\cap\partial\Omega
,
\tilde{u}
is a weak solution to
\tilde{L}\tilde{u}=-\partial_{t}\tilde{u}+\divg(\tilde{A}\nabla\tilde{u})=0
in
4Q
, where
\tilde{A}=A
on
\Omega
and the coefficient matrix
\tilde{A}
on the set
x_{n}<\varphi(x)
depends only on
A(x,2\varphi(x)-x_{n},t)
and
\nabla\varphi
making the resulting matrix bounded and uniformly elliptic on the set
4Q\setminus\Omega
. Applying the interior Caccioppoli’s inequality to
\tilde{u}
gives

\iint_{Q}|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{2}dYds\leq\frac{C}{r^{2}}\iint_{2Q}|\tilde{u}(Y,s)-c|^{2}dYds,

which yields the desired estimate since

\iint_{Q\cap\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dYds\lesssim\iint_{Q}|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{2}dYds,\text{ and }\iint_{2Q}|\tilde{u}(Y,s)-c|^{2}dYds\lesssim\iint_{2Q\cap\Omega}|u(Y,s)-c|^{2}dYds.

∎

.

Lemma 3.5

(Poincaré inequality for solutions)
.

Let
Q=Q_{r}(X,t)
be a parabolic cube that either satisfies
4Q=Q_{4r}(X,t)\subset\Omega
or
(X,t)\in\partial\Omega
(i.e. centered at
\partial\Omega
). Let
u
be a weak solution of
Lu=-\mathrm{div}F
in
Q_{4r}(X,t)\cap\Omega
, and assume that
u
has zero Neumann data on
4Q\cap\partial\Omega
if
(X,t)\in\partial\Omega
. Then there is some constant
C
such that

\iint_{Q\cap\Omega}|u-c_{u}|dXdt\leq Cr\iint_{2Q\cap\Omega}\left(|\nabla u|+|F|\right)dXdt,

where
c_{u}=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.675pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.02336pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-2.53589pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-1.7709pt}}\!\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q\cap\Omega}u\,dXdt
.

Proof.

We only prove the case when
Q
is centered at
\partial\Omega
, as the interior case is similar. We can assume without loss of generality that
Q
is centered at
(0,0)\in\partial\Omega
. We write
Q=Q^{\prime}\times[-r^{2},r^{2}]
, where
Q^{\prime}=\left\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x_{i}|<r\right\}
is the cube in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
centered at
0
with sidelength
r
. For
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we set
\langle u\rangle(t):=\fint_{Q^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{O}}u(Y,t)dY
.
By the triangle inequality and the Poincaré inequality in the spatial variables,

(3.6)

\iint_{Q\cap\Omega}|u(X,t)-c_{u}|dXdt\leq\iint_{Q\cap\Omega}|u(X,t)-\langle u\rangle(t)|dXdt+\iint_{Q\cap\Omega}|\langle u\rangle(t)-c_{u}|dXdt\\
\leq Cr\int_{-r^{2}}^{r^{2}}\int_{Q^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{O}}\left|\nabla u(X,t)\right|dXdt+|Q^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{O}|\int_{-r^{2}}^{r^{2}}|\langle u\rangle(t)-c_{u}|dt.

Let
\eta=\eta(X)
be a smooth function with
\supp\eta\subset 2Q^{\prime}
,
\eta=1
on
Q^{\prime}
,
0\leq\eta\leq 1
, and
|\nabla\eta|\lesssim r^{-1}
. We define

\langle u\rangle_{\eta}(t):=\int_{\mathcal{O}}u(Y,t)\eta(Y)dY\quad\text{for }t\in\mathbb{R},

and set
c_{\eta}:=\int_{\mathcal{O}}\eta\,dX
. For
t,t^{\prime}\in[-r^{2},r^{2}]
, we have

\langle u\rangle_{\eta}(t)-\langle u\rangle_{\eta}(t^{\prime})=\int_{\mathcal{O}}(u(X,t)-u(X,t^{\prime}))\eta(X)dX=\int_{\mathcal{O}}\int_{t^{\prime}}^{t}\partial_{\tau}u(X,\tau)\eta(X)d\tau dX\\
=-\int_{\mathcal{O}}\int_{t^{\prime}}^{t}A(X,\tau)\nabla u(X,\tau)\cdot\nabla\eta(X)d\tau dX-\int_{\mathcal{O}}\int_{t^{\prime}}^{t}F(X,\tau)\nabla\eta(X)d\tau dX

since
u
is a weak solution to
Lu=-\divg F
with zero Neumann data on
\partial\Omega\cap 4Q
. Using the bound on
\nabla\eta
, we obtain that

(3.7)

|\langle u\rangle_{\eta}(t)-\langle u\rangle_{\eta}(t^{\prime})|\leq\frac{C}{r}\int_{\mathcal{O}\cap 2Q^{\prime}}\int_{-r^{2}}^{r^{2}}\left(|\nabla u|+|F|\right)dtdX\quad\text{for }t,t^{\prime}\in[-r^{2},r^{2}].

On the other hand,

\left|\langle u\rangle(t)-\frac{1}{c_{\eta}}\langle u\rangle_{\eta}(t)\right|=\left|\int_{\mathcal{O}}\frac{\eta(Y)}{c_{\eta}}\left(u(Y,t)-\fint_{Q^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{O}}u(X,t)dX\right)dY\right|\\
\leq\frac{1}{c_{\eta}}\int_{\mathcal{O}\cap 2Q^{\prime}}\left|u(Y,t)-\fint_{Q^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{O}}u(X,t)dX\right|dY\leq\frac{Cr}{c_{\eta}}\int_{\mathcal{O}\cap 2Q^{\prime}}|\nabla u(Y,t)|dY\\
\leq Cr\fint_{\mathcal{O}\cap 2Q^{\prime}}|\nabla u(Y,t)|dY,

where we have used the Poincaré inequality in the spatial variables in the second-to-last inequality and
c_{\eta}\geq|\mathcal{O}\cap Q^{\prime}|\gtrsim|\mathcal{O}\cap 2Q^{\prime}|
in the last inequality. By this and (
3.7
), we get that

\int_{-r^{2}}^{r^{2}}|\langle u\rangle(t)-c_{u}|dt=\int_{-r^{2}}^{r^{2}}\left|\fint_{-r^{2}}^{r^{2}}\left(\langle u\rangle(t)-\langle u\rangle(t^{\prime})\right)dt^{\prime}\right|dt\leq Cr\fint_{\mathcal{O}\cap 2Q^{\prime}}\int_{-r^{2}}^{r^{2}}\left(|\nabla u|+|F|\right)dtdX.

Combining this estimate with (
3.6
), one obtains that

\iint_{Q\cap\Omega}|u(X,t)-c_{u}|dXdt\leq Cr\int_{\mathcal{O}\cap 2Q^{\prime}}\int_{-r^{2}}^{r^{2}}\left(|\nabla u|+|F|\right)dtdX.

∎

\|N(v)\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\partial\Omega)}\lesssim\|T_{q}(F)\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\partial\Omega)}.

We claim that we can obtain

(3.10)

\|\widetilde{N}(v)\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\partial\Omega)}\lesssim\|T_{1}(F)\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\partial\Omega)}\lesssim\|\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}(\delta F)\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\partial\Omega)}.

Note that by (
2.11
), this means that
\rm{(}\rm{PD}_{p^{\prime}}\rm{)}
{}^{L^{*}}
is solvable.

To see the first inequality in (
3.10
), we note that the constraint on
q
in (
3.9
) comes only from the estimate for the local part

\widetilde{v}(X,t):=\iint_{Q_{X,t}}\nabla_{Y}G(X,t,Y,s)\cdot F(Y,s)dYds

in the proof of
[
20
, Lemma 3.7]
, where
Q_{X,t}:=Q_{\frac{\delta(X,t)}{2}}(X,t)
. This is because an estimate on the
L^{q}
norm of the gradient of the Green function is used there to deduce that
\widetilde{v}(X,t)\lesssim T_{q}(F)(P,t)
for
(X,t)\in I^{+}
,
I\in\mathcal{W}(P,t)
. However, this constraint can be removed and
q
can be set equal to 1 if one takes average in the local part, which allows us to apply energy estimates. To be more precise, for fixed
(X,t)\in I^{+}
, with
I\in\mathcal{W}(P,t)
, we consider

\accentset{\approx}{v}(Z,\tau):=\iint_{Q_{X,t}}\nabla_{Y}G(Z,\tau,Y,s)\cdot F(Y,s)dYds,\quad(Z,\tau)\in\Omega.

Then
\accentset{\approx}{v}\in\dot{E}_{0}(\Omega)
solves
L\accentset{\approx}{v}=\divg\left(F{\mathds{1}}_{Q_{X,t}}\right)
in
\Omega
. Hence, by Sobolev embedding
\dot{E}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n}}(\Omega)
(c.f. subsection
2.2
) and the energy estimate, one obtains that

\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.675pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.02336pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-2.53589pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-1.7709pt}}\!\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q_{X,t}/2}|\accentset{\approx}{v}(Z,\tau)|^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n}}dZd\tau\right)^{\frac{n}{2(n+2)}}\lesssim\delta(X,t)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\left(\iint_{\Omega}|\accentset{\approx}{v}|^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n}}dZd\tau\right)^{\frac{n}{2(n+2)}}\\
\lesssim\delta(X,t)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\|\accentset{\approx}{v}\|_{\dot{\mathsf{E}}_{0}(\Omega)}\lesssim\delta(X,t)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\left(\iint_{Q_{X,t}}|F|^{2}dZd\tau\right)^{1/{2}}\\
\lesssim\delta(X,t)\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.675pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.02336pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-2.53589pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-1.7709pt}}\!\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q_{X,t}}|F|^{2}dZd\tau\right)^{1/{2}}\leq T_{1}(F)(P,t).

This modification to the proof of
[
20
, Lemma 3.7]
yields the first inequality in (
3.10
).

Next, we justify the second inequality in (
3.10
). We shall prove the pointwise bound

(3.11)

T_{1}(F)(P,t)\lesssim\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{1,2}^{a,c}(\delta F)(P,t)\quad\text{for }(P,t)\in\partial\Omega

for some
a>0
and
c\in(0,1)
. Then the desired the estimate follows from (
2.10
). To see (
3.11
), we observe that
I^{+}\subset Q_{c\delta(Y,s)/2}(Y,s)
for all
(Y,s)\in I^{+}
for some
c\in(0,1)
close to 1. This implies that for
(P,t)\in\partial\Omega
,

T_{1}(F)(P,t)\lesssim\sum_{I\in\mathcal{W}(P,t)}\ell(I)\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.675pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.02336pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-2.53589pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-1.7709pt}}\!\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{I^{+}}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.675pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.02336pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-2.53589pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-1.7709pt}}\!\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q_{c\delta(Y,s)/2}(Y,s)}|F|^{2}dZd\tau\right)^{1/2}dYds\\
\lesssim\sum_{I\in\mathcal{W}(P,t)}\iint_{I^{+}}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.675pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.02336pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-2.53589pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-1.7709pt}}\!\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q_{c\delta(Y,s)/2}(Y,s)}|\delta F|^{2}dZd\tau\right)^{1/2}\frac{dYds}{\delta(Y,s)^{n+2}}\leq\widetilde{A}^{a,c}_{1,2}(\delta F)(P,t)

for some
a>0
large enough so that the cone
\Gamma_{a}(P,t)
contains
\bigcup_{I\in\mathcal{W}(P,t)}I^{+}
. This completes the proof of (
3.11
) and thus (
3.10
) and the implication
\rm{(}\rm{D}_{p^{\prime}}\rm{)}
{}^{L^{*}}

\implies

\rm{(}\rm{PD}_{p^{\prime}}\rm{)}
{}^{L^{*}}
.

The implication
\rm{(}\rm{PD}_{p^{\prime}}\rm{)}
{}^{L^{*}}

\implies

\rm{(}\rm{PR}_{p}\rm{)}
L
follows from duality. To see this, let
h\in L_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
F\in L_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and let
u\in\dot{E}_{0}(\Omega)
be the energy solution to
Lu=h+\divg F
. By (
2.14
),

\|\widetilde{N}(\nabla u)\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\lesssim\left|\iint_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot G\,dXdt\right|,

for some
G\in L_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
satisfying
\|\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}(\delta G)\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\partial\Omega)}\leq 1
. Let
v\in\dot{E}_{0}(\Omega)
be the energy solution to
L^{*}v=\divg G
. Then using the PDEs satisfied by
u
and
v
gives

(3.12)

\|\widetilde{N}(\nabla u)\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\lesssim\left|-\iint_{\Omega}hv\,dXdt+\iint_{\Omega}F\cdot\nabla v\,dXdt\right|.

By (
2.13
), we have

\left|\iint_{\Omega}hv\,dXdt\right|\lesssim\|\widetilde{N}(v)\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}\|\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}(\delta h)\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}(\delta G)\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}\|\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}(\delta h)\|_{L^{p}}\leq\|\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}(\delta h)\|_{L^{p}}

by the assumption that
\rm{(}\rm{PD}_{p^{\prime}}\rm{)}
{}^{L^{p^{\prime}}}
is solvable. Again by (
2.13
), we have

\left|\iint_{\Omega}F\cdot\nabla v\,dXdt\right|\lesssim\|\widetilde{N}(\delta\nabla v)\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}\|\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}(F)\|_{L^{p}}.

By Caccioppoli’s inequality, for any
(X,t)\in\Omega
,

\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.675pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.02336pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-2.53589pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-1.7709pt}}\!\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q_{X,t}/2}|\delta\nabla v|^{2}dYds\right)^{1/2}\lesssim\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.675pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.02336pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-2.53589pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-1.7709pt}}\!\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{\frac{2}{3}Q_{X,t}}|v|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.675pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.02336pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-2.53589pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-1.7709pt}}\!\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{\frac{2}{3}Q_{X,t}}|\delta G|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

This implies that for any
\xi\in\partial\Omega
,

\widetilde{N}(\delta\nabla v)(\xi)\lesssim\widetilde{N}_{1,2}^{2/3}(v)(\xi)+\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}^{2/3}_{1,2}(\delta G)(\xi),

which yields that

\|\widetilde{N}(\delta\nabla v)\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}\lesssim\|\widetilde{N}(v)\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}+\|\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}(\delta G)\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}\lesssim 1.

Combining these estimates with (
3.12
), one obtains that

\|\widetilde{N}(\nabla u)\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}\lesssim\|\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}(\delta h)\|_{L^{p}}+\|\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}(F)\|_{L^{p}},

as desired.
∎

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.4

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.4
. We begin with the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
. Then

\rm{(}\rm{N}_{p}\rm{)}
L

+

\rm{(}\rm{D}_{p^{\prime}}\rm{)}
{}^{L^{*}}

\implies

\rm{(}\rm{wPNR}_{p}\rm{)}
L

Proof.

Let
F\in{L^{\infty}_{c}}(\Omega)
, and let
u\in\dot{E}(\Omega)
be the energy solution to
Lu=-\divg F
in
\Omega
with zero Neumann data. We want to prove that

\|\widetilde{N}(\nabla u)\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\|\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}(F)\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}

with a constant
C>0
independent of
F
.

Let
u_{D}\in\dot{\mathsf{E}}_{0}(\Omega)
be the energy solution of
Lu_{D}=-\divg F
in
\Omega
with zero Dirichlet data.
Set
w:=u-u_{D}
. We claim that
w\in\dot{E}(\Omega)
is the energy solution to
Lw=0
in
\Omega
with
\partial_{\nu}^{A}w|_{\partial\Omega}=g
for some
g\in L^{p}(\partial\Omega)\cap\dot{H}^{-1/4}_{\partial_{t}-\Delta_{x}}(\partial\Omega)
that satisfies

(4.2)

\|g\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\leq\|\widetilde{N}(\nabla u_{D})\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}.

Recall that
\partial_{\nu}^{A}w|_{\partial\Omega}=g
is in the weak sense (as in (
2.17
)). Once (
4.2
) is established, by the assumption that
\rm{(}\rm{N}_{p}\rm{)}
L
and
\rm{(}\rm{D}_{p^{\prime}}\rm{)}
{}^{L^{*}}
hold and Lemma
3.8
, we obtain that

(4.3)

\|\widetilde{N}(\nabla u)\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\leq\|\widetilde{N}(\nabla u_{D})\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}+\|\widetilde{N}(\nabla w)\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\\
\leq|\widetilde{N}(\nabla u_{D})\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}+\|g\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\leq 2\|\widetilde{N}(\nabla u_{D})\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\lesssim\|\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}(F)\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}.

It remains to prove that there exists some
g\in L^{p}(\partial\Omega)\cap\dot{H}^{-1/4}_{\partial_{t}-\Delta_{x}}(\partial\Omega)
such that

(4.4)

\iint_{\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R}}\left[A\nabla w\cdot{\nabla\varphi}+H_{t}D_{t}^{1/2}w\cdot{D_{t}^{1/2}\varphi}\right]\,\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{\partial\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R}}g\varphi\quad\text{for all }\varphi\in\dot{E},

and that
g
satisfies the bound (
4.2
).

For simplicity we assume our domain is

\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}
, the generalization to any bounded or unbounded Lipschitz domain follows by localization and partition of unity.

Note that since
u\in\dot{E}(\Omega)
solves
Lu=-\mathrm{div}F
in
\Omega
and
u
has zero Neumann data, the left-hand side of (
4.4
) is equal to

-\iint_{\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R}}\left[A\nabla u_{D}\cdot{\nabla\varphi}+H_{t}D_{t}^{1/2}u_{D}\cdot{D_{t}^{1/2}\varphi}-F\cdot\nabla\varphi\right]\,\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}t.

Therefore, we consider the linear functional
\Lambda
defined for
\varphi\in\dot{H}^{1/4}_{\partial_{t}-\Delta_{x}}(\partial\Omega)
by

(4.5)

\Lambda(\varphi):=-\iint_{\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R}}\left[A\nabla u_{D}\cdot{\nabla\Phi}+H_{t}D_{t}^{1/2}u_{D}\cdot{D_{t}^{1/2}\Phi}-F\nabla\Phi\right]\,\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}t,

where
\Phi
is any extension of
\varphi
in
\dot{E}(\Omega)
. Note that this is well-defined because if
\Phi_{1}
and
\Phi_{2}
are two extensions of
\varphi
in
\dot{E}(\Omega)
, then
\Phi_{1}-\Phi_{2}\in\dot{E}_{0}(\Omega)
and so

\iint_{\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R}}\left[A\nabla u_{D}\cdot{\nabla(\Phi_{1}-\Phi_{2})}+H_{t}D_{t}^{1/2}u_{D}\cdot{D_{t}^{1/2}(\Phi_{1}-\Phi_{2})}-F\nabla(\Phi_{1}-\Phi_{2})\right]\,\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}t=0

since
Lu_{D}=-\divg F
. Moreover,
\Lambda
is bounded because for any
\phi\in\dot{H}^{1/4}_{\partial_{t}-\Delta_{x}}(\partial\Omega)
, we have that

|\Lambda(\varphi)|\lesssim\left(\|u_{D}\|_{\dot{E}}+\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)\|\Phi\|_{\dot{E}(\Omega)}\lesssim\left(\|u_{D}\|_{\dot{E}}+\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)\|\varphi\|_{\dot{H}^{1/4}_{\partial_{t}-\Delta_{x}}(\partial\Omega)},

where we have used that
\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}<\infty
since
F\in L_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Hence, there exists a unique
g\in\dot{H}{\vphantom{H}}^{-1/4}_{\partial_{t}-\Delta_{x}}(\partial\Omega)
such that

\Lambda(\varphi)=\langle g,\varphi\rangle
for all
\varphi\in\dot{H}^{1/4}_{\partial_{t}-\Delta_{x}}(\partial\Omega)
. As Lipschitz functions

\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times\mathbb{R}
with bounded support restricted to
\partial\Omega
are dense in

\dot{H}^{1/4}_{\partial_{t}-\Delta_{x}}(\partial\Omega)
it follows that the identity

\Lambda(\varphi)=\langle g,\varphi\rangle
holds for all such
\varphi
(and we can take
\Phi
to be their Lispchitz extension in (
4.5
)) and still uniquely determined
g
in the space
\dot{H}{\vphantom{H}}^{-1/4}_{\partial_{t}-\Delta_{x}}(\partial\Omega)
.

We argue that
g\in L^{p}(\partial\Omega)
with the bound (
4.2
) by showing that the co-normal averages defined for any
r>0
by

(4.6)

g_{r}(x,t)=-\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.675pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.02336pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-2.53589pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-1.7709pt}}\!\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{B_{r/2}(x,r,t)}A(y,y_{n},s)\nabla u_{D}(y,y_{n},s)\cdot e_{n}dY\,ds

converge weakly to
g
in
L^{p}(\partial\Omega)
as
r\to 0
. The bound (
4.2
) would then follow from the observation that

\|g_{r}\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\leq\|\widetilde{N}(\nabla u_{D})\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}<\infty,\quad\mbox{ for all $r>0$}.

To show the weak convergence of
g_{r}
to
g
in
L^{p}(\partial\Omega)
, it suffices to show that
for any Lipschitz function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times\mathbb{R}
with bounded support, we have that

(4.7)

\iint_{\Omega}\left[A\nabla u_{D}\cdot{\nabla\varphi}-u_{D}\partial_{t}\varphi-F\nabla\varphi\right]dXdt=\lim_{r\to 0_{+}}\int_{\partial\Omega}g_{r}(x,t)\varphi(x,r,t)dx\,dt.

To see this, we fix
r>0
and consider the sets

R_{r}=\bigcup_{(x,t)\in\partial\Omega}B_{r/2}(x,r,t),\qquad S_{X,t}=\{(y,s)\in\partial\Omega:(X,t)\in B_{r/2}(y,r,s)\},

for any
(X,t)\in\Omega
. Finally, let
\sigma(X,t)=\sigma(S_{(X,t)})
. Clearly
\sigma(X,t)>0
if and only if

(X,t)\in R_{r}
and moreover
\sigma(X,t)
is a function that only depends on
x_{n}
(the distance of the point

(X,t)
to the boundary) and hence we shall also write
\sigma(x_{n})
. Observe that

\int_{0}^{\infty}\sigma(x_{n})dx_{n}=c_{n}r^{n+2},\quad\mbox{and hence }\quad 1=\frac{1}{c_{n}r^{n+2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sigma(x_{n})dx_{n}.

Hence for any point
(X,t)\in\Omega
we define
\theta_{r}(X,t)=\theta_{r}(x,x_{n},t)
as

\theta_{r}(X,t)=\frac{1}{c_{n}r^{n+2}}\int_{0}^{x_{n}}\sigma(s)ds.

By what we have observed above,
\theta_{r}
vanishes near the boundary
\partial\Omega
and equal to
1
when
x_{n}>3r/2
. It follows that since
Lu_{D}=-\divg F
, we have for the test function
\varphi\theta_{r}
that

(4.8)

\iint_{\Omega}\left[A\nabla u_{D}\cdot{\nabla(\varphi\theta_{r})}-u_{D}\partial_{t}(\varphi\theta_{r})-F\nabla(\varphi\theta_{r})\right]dXdt=0.

It follows, since the support of
F
is away from the boundary, that
\iint_{\Omega}F\nabla(\varphi\theta_{r})=\iint_{\Omega}F\nabla\varphi
for all
r
sufficiently small. For the second term on the left-hand side of (
4.8
), as
\theta_{r}
does not depend on
t
we have

\iint_{\Omega}u_{D}\partial_{t}(\varphi\theta_{r})=\iint_{\Omega}\theta_{r}u_{D}\partial_{t}\varphi\to\iint_{\Omega}u_{D}\partial_{t}\varphi,\quad\mbox{as }r\to 0_{+}.

The first term can be written as sum of two terms (depending on whether the gradient hits
\varphi
or
\theta
), i.e.,

(4.9)

\iint_{\Omega}A\nabla u_{D}\cdot{\nabla(\varphi\theta_{r})}=\iint_{\Omega}A\nabla u_{D}\cdot({\nabla\varphi)\theta_{r}}+\iint_{\Omega}A\nabla u_{D}\cdot({\nabla\theta_{r})\varphi}

and clearly

\iint_{\Omega}A\nabla u_{D}\cdot({\nabla\varphi)\theta_{r}}\to\iint_{\Omega}A\nabla u_{D}\cdot\nabla\varphi\quad\mbox{as }r\to 0_{+}.

It remains to consider the second term of (
4.9
). Here
\theta_{r}
is only a function of
x_{n}
and hence

(4.10)

\iint_{\Omega}A\nabla u_{D}\cdot({\nabla\theta_{r})\varphi}=\iint_{R_{r}}A_{n_{j}}\partial_{j}u_{D}(\partial_{n}\theta_{r})\varphi=c_{n}^{-1}r^{-n-2}\iint_{R_{r}}A_{n_{j}}\partial_{j}u_{D}\sigma(X,t)\varphi(X,t),

since

\partial_{n}\theta_{r}(X,t)=\frac{1}{c_{n}r^{n+2}}\sigma(X,t).

However, by Fubini’s theorem

(4.11)

\int_{\partial\Omega}g_{r}(x,t)\varphi(x,r,t)dxdt=\\
-c_{n}^{-1}r^{-n-2}\iint_{R_{r}}A(X,t)\nabla u_{D}(X,t)\cdot e_{n}\sigma(X,t)\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.675pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.02336pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-2.53589pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-1.7709pt}}\!\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{S_{X,t}}\varphi(Y,s)dY\,ds\right)dx\,dt,

where
\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.675pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.02336pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-2.53589pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-1.7709pt}}\!\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{S_{X,t}}\varphi(Y,s)dY\,ds\to\varphi(X,t)
as
r\to 0_{+}
by the Lipschitz character of
\varphi
.
It follows that using (
4.10
)

\iint_{\Omega}A\nabla u_{D}\cdot({\nabla\theta_{r})\varphi}+\int_{\partial\Omega}g_{r}(x,t)\varphi(x,r,t)\to 0,\qquad\mbox{as }r\to 0_{+}.

After putting everything together the claim (
4.7
) follows.

∎

We now show the duality between the weak Poisson-Neumann problem and the weak Poisson-Neumann-Regularity problem. Although one can deduce one from another, we only show one direction as it is sufficient for the purpose of this paper.

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
. Then

\text{\hyperlink{wPRNp}{$\rm{(}\rm{wPNR}_{p}\rm{)}$}}\implies\text{\hyperlink{wPNq}{$\rm{(}\rm{wPN}_{p^{\prime}}\rm{)}$}}^{L^{*}}.

Proof.

Let
F\in L^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
and let
u
be the energy solution to
L^{*}u=-\mathrm{div}F
in
\Omega
with zero Neumann data. We want to prove that

\|\widetilde{N}(\delta\nabla u)\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\partial\Omega)}\lesssim\|\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}(\delta|F|)\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\partial\Omega)}.

Let
K
be any compact subset of
\Omega
. In this case
\|\widetilde{N}({\mathds{1}}_{K}\delta\nabla u)\|_{p^{\prime}}<+\infty
, and so by the duality (
2.14
), and the density of
L^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
in the space
\widetilde{T}^{p}_{1}(\Omega)
, there exists
G=G_{K}\in L^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
such that

(4.13)

\|\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}(\delta G)\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\lesssim 1

and

I:=\|\widetilde{N}({\mathds{1}}_{K}\delta\nabla u)\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\partial\Omega)}\leq\iint_{\Omega}\delta\nabla u\cdot G\,dXdt=-\iint_{\Omega}u\divg(\delta G)dXdt.

We let
v\in\dot{E}(\Omega)
be the energy solution to
Lv=-\divg(\delta G)
in
\Omega
with zero Neumann data, and the bound on
I
becomes

(4.14)

I\leq\iint_{\Omega}u\,Lv\,dX=\iint_{\Omega}u\left(\partial_{t}v+\divg A\nabla v\right)dXdt=\iint_{\Omega}\left(\partial_{t}u\,v-A^{T}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\right)\,dXdt\\
=\iint_{\Omega}F\cdot\nabla v\,dXdt

because
u
is a weak solution to
L^{*}u=-\mathrm{div}F
with zero Neumann data.

\|\tilde{N}^{r_{k}}(\nabla u)\|_{L^{p}(Q_{r_{k}}^{k,j}\cap\partial\Omega)}\leq Cr_{k}^{-1+(n+1)/p}r_{k}^{-n-\eta}=Cr_{k}^{-1-n+(n+1)/p-\eta}=Cr_{k}^{-(n-1)/p^{\prime}-\eta}.

To obtain an
L^{1}
bound on each
S_{k}
we use Hölder’s inequality and sum over
j=1,2,\dots,N
:

(5.11)

\|\tilde{N}^{r_{k}}(\nabla u)\|_{L^{1}(S_{k})}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|\tilde{N}^{r_{k}}(\nabla u)\|_{L^{1}(L^{1}(Q_{r_{k}}^{k,j}\cap\partial\Omega))}\\
\leq CN|Q_{r_{k}}^{k,j}\cap\partial\Omega|^{1/p^{\prime}}\|\tilde{N}^{r_{k}}(\nabla u)\|_{L^{p}(Q_{r_{k}}^{k,j}\cap\partial\Omega)}\lesssim r_{k}^{(n+1)/p^{\prime}}r_{k}^{-(n-1)/p^{\prime}-\eta}\sim r_{k}^{-\eta}.

It remains to estimate the part of
\|\tilde{N}(\nabla u)\|_{L^{1}(S_{k})}
away from the boundary. To that end let us consider any point
(Y,s)\in\Gamma(X,t)
for some
(X,t)\in S_{k}
such that
\delta(Y,s):=d((Y,s),\partial\Omega)\geq r_{k}
.
Using the interior Caccioppoli’s inequality we have that

\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.675pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.02336pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-2.53589pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-1.7709pt}}\!\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q_{\delta(Y,s)/2}(Y,s)}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\lesssim r_{k}^{-1}\sup_{Q_{3\delta(Y,s)/4}(Y,s)}|u|\leq Cr_{k}^{-1}r_{k}^{-n-\eta},

since points in
Q_{3\delta(Y,s)/4}(Y,s)
have distance to
Q_{1}(0,0)\cap\partial\Omega
at least
cr_{k}
. It follows that
the contribution of this term to
\tilde{N}(\nabla u)(X,t)
is at most
Cr_{k}^{-1-n-\eta}
and as the size of

S_{k}
is
\sim r_{k}^{n+1}
the
L^{1}
norm of its contribution is of the same order as the term (
5.11
), i.e.,
Cr_{k}^{-\eta}
.

This estimate, together with (
5.8
) and (
5.11
), yield:

(5.12)

\|\tilde{N}(\nabla u)\|_{L^{1}(\partial\Omega)}\\
\leq\|\tilde{N}(\nabla u)\|_{L^{1}(Q_{16}(0,0)\cap\partial\Omega)}+\sum_{k\geq 4}\|\tilde{N}(\nabla u)\|_{L^{1}(S_{k})}\leq C+C\sum_{k\geq 4}2^{-k\eta}\leq\tilde{C}<\infty.

Hence claim (
5.1
) follows.
∎

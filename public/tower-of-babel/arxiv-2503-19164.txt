Blocks of fibered Burnside rings
Benjamín García, Alberto G. Raggi-Cárdenas

Abstract
In this article, we provide bases for the indecomposable factors of fibered Burnside rings in finite rank, and we give further characterizations of these as solvable components of fibered Burnside rings for certain Weyl groups. We revisit Dress’ construction of prime spectra of fibered Burnside rings and its connected components for some rings of characteristic zero.



Keywords:
Fibered Burnside ring; prime spectrum; primitive idempotent.

2020 Mathematics Subject Classification:
19A22

Introduction

Fibered Burnside rings were introduced by Dress in his article
"The ring of monomial representations I. Structure Theory"

[
3
]
, published in 1971, as a generalization of the Burnside ring and classical monomial representation rings, with the aim of providing a common framework for several induction theorems, the study of induction formulas, and to explore possible generalizations of these results. For a finite group
G
and a finite
G
-module
A
, a commutative ring
\Omega(G,A)
, named the
ring of monomial representations of
G
with coefficients in
A
, is defined as the Grothendieck ring of the category of finite
(G,A)
-sets. Dress had pointed out that in most cases that appear naturally in representation theory, the action of
G
on
A
would be trivial. Later, the name
A
-fibered Burnside ring
and the notation
B^{A}(G)
have been preferred for
\Omega(G,A)
, with
A
an abelian group, not necessarily finite.

Several aspects of the structure of
B^{A}(G)
have already been studied and determined for a broad family of fiber groups
A
. For instance, Dress determined the modular species of
B^{A}_{R}(G)
, constructed
\text{Spec}\left(B^{A}_{R}(G)\right)
and computed its connected components in great generality
[
3
, §3]
; later, Barker
[
1
]
, Boltje & Yılmaz
[
2
]
and Fotsing & Külshammer
[
4
]
have further refined some of these results for particular cases. If
\text{Tor}_{\text{exp}(G)}(A)
(the set of elements
a\in A
satisfying
a^{\text{exp}(G)}=1
) is finite, there are explicit formulae for its species (ring homomorphisms to
\mathbb{C}
) and for the primitive idempotents of
B^{A}_{\mathbb{K}}(G)
in terms of a standard basis, whenever
\mathbb{K}
is a
"big enough"
field of characteristic
0

[
2
, §3]
. The conductors of primitive idempotents of
B^{C_{n}}_{\mathbb{K}}(G)
over the ring of algebraic integers
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{K}}
are also known for divisors
n
of
\text{exp}(G)
satisfying
(n,\text{exp}(G)/n)=1
, generalizing the results on the conductors of primitive idempotents of
D_{\mathbb{C}}(G)
and
B_{\mathbb{Q}}(G)
and covering a wide range of interesting examples; also, functorial properties of the fiber group have been characterized in
[
5
]
.

In this article, we present a characterization of the primitive idempotents or
blocks
of
B^{A}_{R}(G)
for a domain
R
of characteristic
0
in which no prime divisor of
|G|
is invertible, assuming that
\text{Tor}_{\text{exp}(G)}(A)
is finite. We provide bases for their corresponding indecomposable factors and prove that each of them is isomorphic to the
solvable component
of the fibered Burnside ring of a certain Weyl group, as in
[
6
, §3]
. In Section 1, we present some basic definitions related to fibered Burnside rings, including their species and the primitive idempotents of
B^{A}_{\mathbb{K}}(G)
. In Section 2, we revisit Dress’ construction of
\text{Spec}(B^{A}_{R}(G))
, following the technique of Fotsing & Külshammer for
\text{Spec}(D(G))
in
[
4
]
, providing more detail on the characterization of prime ideals of residue characteristic
p>0
. We rewrite the details of
[
3
, §3, Corollary 3]
on the computation of the connected components of
\text{Spec}\left(B^{A}_{R}(G)\right)
, by means of
P
-equivalence of pairs. Finally, Section 3 is devoted to the characterizations of indecoposable components of
B^{A}_{R}(G)
that we have mentioned before.

Notation and general setting

Throughout this article,
G
denotes a finite group, and
A
is an abelian group. We write
\text{exp}(G)
to denote the exponent of
G
, and
o(g)
for the order of an element
g\in G
. For a prime
p
, we write
g_{p}
and
g_{p^{\prime}}
for the
p
-part and the
p^{\prime}
-part of
g
, respectively; if
A
is a torsion group, we write
A_{p}
for its
p
-primary component and
A_{p^{\prime}}
for its complement. If
\pi
is a set of primes,
O^{\pi}(G)
denotes the minimal normal subgroup of
G
such that the quotient
G/O^{\pi}(G)
is a solvable
\pi
-group; if
\pi=\{p\}
, we write
O^{p}(G)
instead, while if
\pi
contains all prime divisors of
|G|
, then
O^{\pi}(G)
is precisely
O^{s}(G)
, the minimal normal subgroup such that
G/O^{s}(G)
is solvable. For a positive integer
n
, we write
\text{Tor}_{n}(A)
for the subgroup of
n
-torsion elements of
A
, that is, the elements
a\in A
satisfying
a^{n}=e
. We regard
\text{Hom}(G,A)
as an abelian group for pointwise multiplication and write
G^{\star}
for the Pontryagin dual
\text{Hom}(G,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
. If
S
is a commutative ring and
P\in\text{Spec}(S)
, the
residue characteristic
of
P
is
\text{rchar}(P):=\text{char}(S/P)
.

Background material

The
A
-fibered Burnside ring

B^{A}(G)
is defined as the Grothendieck ring of category
{}_{G}\text{set}^{A}
of

-fibered
G
-sets with finitely many orbits, however, we will be working with a presentation of this ring instead. An

-subcharacter
or
A
-monomial pair
of

is a pair
(H,\phi)
for some
H\leq G
and
\phi\in\text{Hom}(H,A)

-subcharacters is denoted by
\mathcal{M}^{A}_{G}
, and the group

acts on this set by conjugation; we write
[H,\phi]_{G}
for the orbit of
(H,\phi)

N_{G}(H,\phi)
for its stabilizer. The
A
-fibered Burnside ring

can be presented as the free abelian group on the orbit set

that we call its

or

[H,\phi]_{G}[K,\psi]_{G}=\sum_{g\in[H\backslash G/K]}[H\cap{{}^{g}K},\phi|_{H\cap{{}^{g}K}}{{}^{g}\psi|_{H\cap{{}^{g}K}}}]_{G}

[G,1]_{G}
acting as multiplicative identity. The ring

is commutative, and it is not difficult to see that

\text{Tor}_{\text{exp}(G)}(A)

The group
G
acts trivially on
\text{Hom}(G,A)

\mathbb{Z}\text{Hom}(G,A)
can be regarded as a subring and a direct summand of

if we identify

\pi_{G}:B^{A}(G)\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}\text{Hom}(G,A),[H,\phi]_{G}\mapsto\begin{cases}\phi&\text{if }H=G,\\
0&\text{else},\end{cases}

is a ring homomorphism too. There is also an embedding of the Burnside ring in
B^{A}(G)
,

B(G)\longrightarrow B^{A}(G),[G/H]\mapsto[H,1]_{G},

with a section
[H,\phi]_{G}\mapsto[G/H]

The collection of
A
-fibered Burnside rings of the subgroups of

form a Green functor for the following maps: if
K\leq H\leq G

induction from
K
to

while

to

\text{res}^{H}_{K}:B^{A}(H)\longrightarrow B^{A}(K),[U,\phi]_{H}\mapsto\sum_{g\in[K\backslash H/U]}[K\cap{{}^{g}U},{{}^{g}\phi}_{K\cap{{}^{g}U}}]_{K};

if

conjugation from

\text{c}_{g,H}:B^{A}(H)\longrightarrow B^{A}({{}^{g}H}),[U,\phi]_{H}\mapsto[{{}^{g}U},{{}^{g}\phi}]_{{}^{g}H}.

It is quite technical to prove that these maps satisfy the axioms of a Green biset functor, and we will not do this here.

The prime spectrum of
B^{A}_{R}(G)

In the following, we assume that
\text{Tor}_{\text{exp}(G)}(A)
is finite. Let
\zeta

-th root of
1
in some algebraic extension

. For every

Definition 2.1
.

Let
P\in\text{Spec}\left(R[\zeta]\right)
. We say that two pairs
(H,\Phi)
and
(K,\Psi)
in
\mathcal{D}^{A}_{G}

s^{G,\phi}_{H,\Phi}(x)\equiv s^{G,A}_{K,\Psi}(x)
mod

The relation of

\mathcal{D}^{A}_{G}

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
P\in\text{Spec}(R[\zeta])
of residue characteristic
0
, and
(H,\Phi),(K,\Psi)\in\mathcal{D}^{A}_{G}
. Then
(H,\Phi)\sim_{P}(K,\Psi)
if and only if
(H,\Phi)=_{G}(K,\Psi)
.

Proof.

Since

R[\zeta]/P
, the canonical projection
R[\zeta]\longrightarrow R[\zeta]/P
is injective in

that contains

. Thus we have, for all

, that
s^{G,A}_{H,\Phi}(x)\equiv s^{G,A}_{K,\phi}(x)
mod

if and only if
s^{G,A}_{H,\Phi}(x)=s^{G,A}_{K,\phi}(x)
, which happens if and only if
(H,\Phi)=_{G}(K,\Psi)
.
∎

.

Let

[K,\widetilde{\phi}]_{G}=[L,\widetilde{\psi}]_{G}
and so there is some
g\in G
such that
{}^{g}(K,\widetilde{\phi})=(L,\widetilde{\psi})
, in particular

J=O^{s}(L)=O^{s}({{}^{g}K})={{}^{g}O^{s}(K)}={{}^{g}J}

and so
g\in N_{G}(J)
; hence
{{}^{\overline{g}}(K/J,\phi)}=(L/J,\psi)
,
\overline{g}=gJ
, proving that
f
is also injective, thus an isomorphism of abelian groups.

Finally, we prove that
f
is in fact a ring isomorphism. For
(K/J,\phi),(L/J,\psi)\in\mathcal{M}^{A}_{W_{G}J}
, we have

\displaystyle f\left([K/J,\phi]_{W_{G}J}[L/J,\psi]_{W_{G}J}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\overline{g}\in(K/J)\backslash W_{G}J/(L/J)}f\left([(K/J)\cap{{}^{\overline{g}}(L/J)},\phi\cdot{{}^{\overline{g}}\psi}]_{W_{G}J}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{g\in K\backslash N_{G}(J)/L}[K\cap{{}^{g}L},\widetilde{\phi}\cdot{{}^{g}\widetilde{\psi}}]_{G}e^{G}_{[J]}.

To see that the last sum is the product of the images, note that if
g\in G
is such that
[K\cap{{}^{g}L},\widetilde{\phi}\cdot{{}^{g}\widetilde{\psi}}]_{G}e^{G}_{[J]}\neq 0
, then there is some
T\leq K\cap{{}^{g}L}
such that
O^{s}(T)=_{G}J
. But this implies that

J=O^{s}(K)=O^{s}(T)=O^{s}({{}^{g}L})={{}^{g}O^{s}(L)}={{}^{g}J},

which implies that
g\in N_{G}(J)
. Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{g\in[K\backslash N_{G}(J)/L]}[K\cap{{}^{g}L},\widetilde{\phi}\cdot{{}^{g}\widetilde{\psi}}]_{G}e^{G}_{[J]}

\displaystyle=\sum_{g\in K\backslash G/L}[K\cap{{}^{g}L},\widetilde{\phi}\cdot{{}^{g}\widetilde{\psi}}]_{G}e^{G}_{[J]}

\displaystyle=([K,\widetilde{\phi}]_{G}e^{G}_{[J]})([L,\widetilde{\psi}]_{G}e^{G}_{[J]})

\displaystyle=f([K/J,\phi]_{W_{G}J})f([L/J,\psi]_{W_{G}J}).

This only proves that
f
is multiplicative, but it also implies that
f
sends
e^{W_{G}J}_{[1]}
to
e^{G}_{[J]}
since
f\left(e^{W_{G}J}_{[1]}\right)
is a nonzero idempotent in
B^{A}(G)e^{G}_{[J]}
, which is indecomposable.
∎

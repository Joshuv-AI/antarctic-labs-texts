Transitivity of some uniformities on fuzzy sets
Daniel Jardón, Iván Sánchez, Manuel Sanchis

Abstract
Given a uniform space ( X , 𝒰 ) (X,\mathcal{U}) , we denote by ℱ ⁡ ( X ) \mathcal{F}(X) to the family of all normal upper semicontinuous fuzzy sets u : X → [ 0 , 1 ] u\colon X\to[0,1] with compact support. In this paper, we study transitivity on some uniformities on ℱ ⁡ ( X ) \mathcal{F}(X) : the level-wise uniformity 𝒰 ∞ \mathcal{U}_{\infty} , the Skorokhod uniformity 𝒰 0 \mathcal{U}_{0} , and the sendograph uniformity 𝒰 S \mathcal{U}_{S} . If f : ( X , 𝒰 ) → ( X , 𝒰 ) f\colon(X,\mathcal{U})\to(X,\mathcal{U}) is a continuous function, we mainly characterize when the induced dynamical systems f ^ : ( ℱ ⁡ ( X ) , 𝒰 ∞ ) → ( ℱ ⁡ ( X ) , 𝒰 ∞ ) \widehat{f}\colon(\mathcal{F}(X),\mathcal{U}_{\infty})\to(\mathcal{F}(X),\mathcal{U}_{\infty}) , f ^ : ( ℱ ⁡ ( X ) , 𝒰 0 ) → ( ℱ ⁡ ( X ) , 𝒰 0 ) \widehat{f}\colon(\mathcal{F}(X),\mathcal{U}_{0})\to(\mathcal{F}(X),\mathcal{U}_{0}) and f ^ : ( ℱ ⁡ ( X ) , 𝒰 S ) → ( ℱ ⁡ ( X ) , 𝒰 S ) \widehat{f}\colon(\mathcal{F}(X),\mathcal{U}_{S})\to(\mathcal{F}(X),\mathcal{U}_{S}) are transitive, where f ^ \widehat{f} is the Zadeh’s extension of f f .



Keywords:
Fuzzy set; Level-wise uniformity; Skorokhod uniformity; Sendograph uniformity; Zadeh’s extension; Transitivity; Weakly mixing.

1
Introduction

A
dynamical system
is a pair
(X,f)
, where
X
is a topological space and
f\colon X\to X
is a continuous function. Let us recall that a dynamical system
(X,f)
is
transitive
if for each non-empty open subsets
U
and
V
of
X
, there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
f^{n}(U)\cap V\neq\emptyset
. For instance, consider
S^{1}=\{x\in\mathbb{C}:||x||=1\}
with its usual metric, and
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
. Define
f_{\theta}:S^{1}\to S^{1}
by
f_{\theta}(e^{i\lambda})=e^{i(\lambda+2\pi\theta)}
. Then
f_{\theta}
is transitive if and only if
\theta
is irrational (see
[
10
]
). It is also known that the tent function:

\displaystyle f(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcc}2x,&\text{if }\hskip 8.5359pt0\leq x\leq\frac{1}{2}.\\
2(1-x),&\text{if }\hskip 8.5359pt\frac{1}{2}\leq x\leq 1.\end{array}\right.

is transitive on
[0,1]
with its usual metric.

Given a space
X
, we denote by
\mathcal{K}(X)
to the family of non-empty compact subsets of
X
. If
(X,d)
is a metric space, then
\mathcal{K}(X)
is endowed with the Hausdorff metric
d_{H}
. If
f:X\to X
is continuous, then we define the extension
\overline{f}:\mathcal{K}(X)\to\mathcal{K}(X)
by
\overline{f}(A)=f(A)
, and it is also continuous on
(\mathcal{K}(X),d_{H})

[
8
]
. So, we can consider the dynamical system
(\mathcal{K}(X),\overline{f})
. In
[
10
]
, the author proved the following: If
f:X\to X
is continuous and
\overline{f}
is transitive, then
f
is transitive. On the other hand, if
\theta
is irrational, then
f_{\theta}
is transitive on
S^{1}
, but
\overline{f_{\theta}}
is not transitive. It is also shown that if
X=[0,1]
with its usual metric and
f:X\to X
is the tent function, then
\overline{f}
is transitive on
X

[
10
]
. Hence, the latter facts suggest the following question: Under which conditions is
\overline{f}
transitive?

The question above was answered independently by Banks and Peris in the realm of topological spaces (see
[
1
,
9
]
). We need some notations in order to present the result of Banks and Peris. Let
(X,\tau)
be a topological space. Then
\mathcal{K}(X)
will be endowed with the Vietoris topology
\tau_{V}
. Given a continuous function
f:(X,\tau)\to(X,\tau)
, as in metric spaces, we can consider the induced function
\overline{f}:(\mathcal{K}(X),\tau_{V})\to(\mathcal{K}(X),\tau_{V})
. If
(X,d)
is a metric space and
\tau_{d}
is the topology on
X
induced by the metric
d
, then the Vietoris topology
(\tau_{d})_{V}
on
\mathcal{K}(X)
coincides with the topology
\tau_{d_{H}}
. Given a topological space
X
, a continuous function
f:X\to X
is
weakly mixing
if
f\times f
is transitive on
X\times X
. We are now ready to introduce the result of Banks and Peris: Let
f:X\to X
be a continuous function on a topological space
X
. Then
f
is weakly mixing if and only if
\overline{f}
is transitive
[
1
,
9
]
.

Given a topological space
X
, we denote by
\mathcal{F}(X)
to the family of normal upper semicontinuous fuzzy sets on
X
with compact support. If
(X,d)
is a metric space, then the hyperspace
\mathcal{F}(X)
can be equipped with different metrics: the level-wise metric
d_{\infty}
, the Skorokhod metric
d_{0}
, the sendograph metric
d_{S}
, among others.

Let
X
be a topological space and
f:X\to X
a continuous function, then the
Zadeh’s extension
of
f
to
\mathcal{F}(X)
is denoted by
\widehat{f}
and defined as follows:

\displaystyle\widehat{f}(u)(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcc}\sup\{u(z):z\in f^{-1}(x)\},&\text{if }\hskip 8.5359ptf^{-1}(x)\neq\emptyset.\\
0,&\text{if }\hskip 8.5359ptf^{-1}(x)=\emptyset.\end{array}\right.

Notice that
\widehat{f}:\mathcal{F}(X)\to\mathcal{F}(X)
. The result of Banks and Peris was extended to
\mathcal{F}(X)
as follows: If
(X,d)
be a metric space, then
((X,d),f)
is weakly mixing if and only if
((\mathcal{F}(X),d_{\infty}),\widehat{f})
is transitive; if and only if
((\mathcal{F}(X),d_{0}),\widehat{f})
is transitive; if and only if
((\mathcal{F}(X),d_{S}),\widehat{f})
is transitive (see
[
6
]
).

It is a well-known fact that every uniformity
\mathcal{U}
on a set
X
induces a topology
\tau_{\mathcal{U}}
on
X
. To be precise, the topology
\tau_{\mathcal{U}}
is the family
\{V\subseteq X\,:\,\text{for every }x\in V\text{, there exists }U\in\mathcal{U}\text{ such that }U(x)\subseteq V\}
, where
U(x)=\{y\in X\,:\,(x,y)\in U\}
. In this case, the topological space
(X,\tau_{\mathcal{U}})
is a Tychonoff space (for the details, we refer to the reader to Chapter 8 of the classic text
[
3
]
). If
(X,\mathcal{U})
is a uniform space, then
\mathcal{F}(X)
is the family of all normal upper semicontinuous fuzzy sets on
(X,\tau_{\mathcal{U}})
with compact support. In
[
4
]
, we introduce some uniformities on the set
\mathcal{F}(X)
: the level-wise uniformity
\mathcal{U}_{\infty}
, the Skorokhod uniformity
\mathcal{U}_{0}
, and the sendograph uniformity
\mathcal{U}_{S}
. In this paper, we show that if

is continuous, then
\widehat{f}:(\mathcal{F}(X),\mathcal{U}_{\infty})\to(\mathcal{F}(X),\mathcal{U}_{\infty})

\widehat{f}:(\mathcal{F}(X),\mathcal{U}_{0})\to(\mathcal{F}(X),\mathcal{U}_{0})

is the Zadeh’s extension of

(see Theorems

,
2.16
, and

). We mainly prove that if

is a continuous function, then

is weakly mixing if and only if

\widehat{f}\colon(\mathcal{F}(X),\mathcal{U}_{\infty})\to(\mathcal{F}(X),\mathcal{U}_{\infty})
is transitive; if and only if
\widehat{f}\colon(\mathcal{F}(X),\mathcal{U}_{0})\to(\mathcal{F}(X),\mathcal{U}_{0})
is transitive; if and only if
\widehat{f}\colon(\mathcal{F}(X),\mathcal{U}_{S})\to(\mathcal{F}(X),\mathcal{U}_{S})

3.7
).

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce the results on fuzzy theory that we need in the sequel. A fuzzy set
u
on a topological space
X
is a function
u\colon X\to\mathbb{I}
, where
\mathbb{I}
denotes the closed unit interval
[0,1]
. For each
\alpha\in(0,1]
, we define the
\alpha
-
cut
of
u
as the set
u_{\alpha}=\{x\in X:u(x)\geq\alpha\}
. The
support
of
u
, denoted by
u_{0}
, is the set
\overline{\{x\in X:u(x)>0\}}
. Let us note that
u_{0}=\overline{\bigcup\{u_{\alpha}:{\alpha\in(0,1]}\}}
. Let
\mathcal{F}(X)
be the family of all normal upper semicontinuous fuzzy sets
u\colon X\to\mathbb{I}
with compact support.

Let
X
be a topological space. If
f\colon X\to X
is a function, the Zadeh’s extension of
f
to
\mathcal{F}(X)
is denoted by
\widehat{f}\colon\mathcal{F}(X)\to\mathcal{F}(X)
and defined as follows:

\widehat{f}(u)(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\sup\{u(z):z\in f^{-1}(x)\},&f^{-1}(x)\neq\emptyset.\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\par 0,&f^{-1}(x)=\emptyset.\\
\end{array}\right.

Two useful results on Zadeh’s extension are the following. The second one is an immediate consequence of the first one.

Proposition 2.1
.

[
5
]

Let
X
be a Hausdorff space.
If
f\colon X\to X
is a continuous function, then
[\widehat{f}(u)]_{\alpha}=f(u_{\alpha})
for each
u\in\mathcal{F}(X)
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{I}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
X
be a Hausdorff space. If
f\colon X\to X
is a continuous function, then
\left(\widehat{f}\right)^{n}=\widehat{f^{n}}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

In the sequel, the latter result allows us to write
\widehat{f^{n}}
instead of
\left(\widehat{f}\right)^{n}
.

Given a non-empty subset
A
of a Hausdorff space
X
, we denote by
\chi_{A}\colon X\to\mathbb{I}
the characteristic function of
A
. For the one-point set
\{x\}
, we put
\chi_{x}
instead of
\chi_{\{x\}}
. If
\mathcal{K}(X)
denotes the hyperspace of all the non-empty compact subsets of
X
, we have the following proposition, which shows that
\widehat{f}
sends
\mathcal{K}(X)
into
itself.

Proposition 2.3
.

[
5
]

Let
f
be a continuous function from a Hausdorff space
X
into itself. Then
\widehat{f}(\chi_{K})=\chi_{f(K)}
for each
K\in\mathcal{K}(X)
.

One of the central concepts of this paper is the notion of
uniform space
. From our point of view, the most efficient approach to uniform spaces is via
entourages of the diagonal
. We begin by introducing some notations. Let
X
be a non-empty set, and let
A
and
B
be subsets of
X\times X
, i.e., relations on the set
X
. The inverse relation of
A
will be denoted by
A^{-1}
, and the composition of
A
and
B
will be denoted by
A\circ B
. Thus, we have that

A^{-1}=\{(x,y)\in X\times X:(y,x)\in A\}

and

A\circ B=\{(x,y)\in X\times X:\text{ there exists }z\in X\text{ such that }(x,z)\in A\text{ and }(z,y)\in B\}.

The symbol
A^{2}
stands for
A\circ A
and
\Delta_{X}
for the diagonal of
X
, that is, the subset
\{(x,x):x\in X\}
of
X\times X
. Every set
A\subseteq X\times X
that contains
\Delta_{X}
is called an
entourage of the diagonal
. We will denote by
\mathcal{D}_{X}
the family of all entourages of the diagonal of
X
.

Definition 2.4
.

A
uniformity
on a non-empty set
X
is a subfamily
\thinspace\mathcal{U}
of
\thinspace\mathcal{D}_{X}
that satisfies the following conditions:

(U1)

If
A\in\mathcal{U}
and
A\subseteq B\in\mathcal{D}_{X}
, then
B\in\mathcal{U}.

(U2)

If
A,B\in\mathcal{U}
, then
U\cap V\in\mathcal{U}
.

(U3)

For every
A\in\mathcal{U}
, there exists
B\in\mathcal{U}
such that
B^{2}\subseteq A
.

(U4)

For every
A\in\mathcal{U}
, there exists
B\in\mathcal{U}
such that
B^{-1}\subseteq A
.

(U5)

\bigcap_{A\in\mathcal{U}}A=\Delta_{X}
.

A
uniform space
is a pair
(X,\mathcal{U})
consisting of a set
X
and a uniformity
\mathcal{U}
on the set
X
. Let
(X,\mathcal{U})
be a uniform space. A family
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{U}
is called a base for the uniformity
\mathcal{U}
if for every
A\in\mathcal{U}
, there exists
B\in\mathcal{B}
such that
B\subseteq A
. The following result is well known and easy to prove.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
X
be a non-empty set. A non-empty family
\mathcal{B}
of subsets of
X\times X
is a base for some uniformity on
X
if and only if it satisfies the following properties:

(BS1)

For any
A,B\in\mathcal{B}
, there exists
C\in\mathcal{B}
such that
C\subset A\cap B
.

(BS2)

For every
A\in\mathcal{B}
, there exists
B\in\mathcal{B}
such that
B^{-1}\subseteq A
.

(BS3)

For every
A\in\mathcal{B}
, there exists
B\in\mathcal{B}
such that
B^{2}\subseteq A
.

(BS4)

\bigcap_{A\in\mathcal{B}}A=\Delta_{X}
.

As usual, a set
X
equipped with a topology
\tau
is called a
topological space
, and it will be denoted by
(X,\tau)
. It is a well-known fact that every uniformity
\mathcal{U}
on a set
X
induces a topology
\tau(\mathcal{U})
on
X
. To be precise, the topology
\tau(\mathcal{U})
is the family
\{V\subseteq X\,:\,\text{for every }x\in V\text{, there exists }U\in\mathcal{U}\text{ such that }U(x)\subseteq V\}
, where
U(x)=\{y\in X\,:\,(x,y)\in U\}
. In this case, the topological space
(X,\tau(\mathcal{U}))
is a Tychonoff space (for the details, we refer to the reader to Chapter 8 of the classic text
[
3
]
). For this reason, in the sequel, all the topological spaces are Tychonoff.

We turn to a brief discussion of the hyperspaces that we will consider in this paper. Given a Tychonoff topological space
(X,\tau)
, the symbols
\mathcal{C}(X)
and
\mathcal{K}(X)
denote, respectively, the hyperspaces defined by

\begin{array}[]{l}\mathcal{C}(X)=\{E\subseteq X\,:\,E\text{ is closed and non-empty}\},\\
\mathcal{K}(X)=\{E\in\mathcal{C}(X)\,:\,E\text{ is compact}\}.\end{array}

Thus, in the case of a uniform space
(X,\mathcal{U})
,
\mathcal{C}(X)
(respectively,
\mathcal{K}(X)
) denotes the hyperspace of all non-empty closed (respectively, non-empty compact) subsets of
(X,\tau(\mathcal{U}))
. We will see that
\mathcal{C}(X)
and
\mathcal{K}(X)
can be endowed with a natural uniformity in this situation.

Let
(X,\mathcal{U})
be a uniform space. For each
U\in\mathcal{U}
and each
A\subset X
, let us define
U(A)=\bigcup_{x\in A}U(x)
. Now, for each
U\in\mathcal{U}
consider the families

\begin{array}[]{l}\mathcal{C}[U]=\{(A,B)\in\mathcal{C}(X)\times\mathcal{C}(X):A\subseteq U(B),\,\,B\subseteq U(A)\},\\
\mathcal{K}[U]=\{(A,B)\in\mathcal{K}(X)\times\mathcal{K}(X)\,:\,A\subseteq U(B),\,\,B\subseteq U(A)\}.\par\end{array}

Among the most interesting results in the theory of hyperspaces are the following three results.

Proposition 2.6
.

[
2
,
8
]
If
(X,\mathcal{U})
is a uniform space, then
\{\mathcal{K}[U]:U\in\mathcal{U}\}
is a base for a uniformity
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{U})
on
\mathcal{K}(X)
.

A remarkable result by Michael
[
8
]
allows us to describe the topology induced by the uniformity
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{U})
. Let us recall that, for any topological space
(X,\tau)
, the topology
\tau
induces a topology
\tau_{V}
on
\mathcal{C}(X)
, the so-called
Vietoris topology
, a base for
\tau_{V}
is the family of all sets of the form

\mathcal{V}\left<V_{1},\ldots,V_{k}\right>=\left\{B\in\mathcal{C}(X)\,:\,B\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}V_{i}\text{ and }B\cap V_{i}\not=\emptyset\text{ for }i=1,\ldots,k\right\},

where
V_{1},V_{2},\ldots,V_{n}
is a finite sequence of non-empty open sets of
X
.

Theorem 2.7
.

[
8
, Theorem 3.3]

If
(X,\mathcal{U})
is a uniform space, then the topology induced by
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{U})
on
\mathcal{K}(X)
coincides with the Vietoris topology induced by
\tau(\mathcal{U})
on
\mathcal{K}(X)
.

Allowing for the previous result, if no confusion can arise,
\mathcal{K}(X)
will denote the hyperspace of all non-empty compact subsets of
(X,\tau(\mathcal{U}))
equipped with the Vietoris topology induced by
\tau(\mathcal{U})
. For the hyperspace
\mathcal{C}(X)
, we have the following.

Proposition 2.8
.

[
2
,
8
]
If
(X,\mathcal{U})
is a uniform space, then
\{\mathcal{C}[U]:U\in\mathcal{U}\}
is a base for a uniformity
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{U})
on
\mathcal{C}(X)
.

[
8
, Example 2.10]
shows that the topology induced by the uniformity
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{U})
on
\mathcal{C}(X)
can be strictly finer than the Vietoris topology induced by
\tau(\mathcal{U})
on
\mathcal{C}(X)
.

It is worth mentioning that a metric space
(X,d)
has a compatible uniformity defined in a natural way. Indeed, it is routine to verify that the family
\mathcal{U}_{d}
, defined as the family of all sets

V_{\epsilon}=\{(x,y)\in X\times X:d(x,y)<\epsilon\}

for each
\varepsilon>0
, is a base for a compatible uniformity on
(X,d)
.
If
A,B\in\mathcal{K}(X)
, the
Hasudorff distance
between
A
and
B
is defined as
d_{H}(A,B)=\mathrm{max}\{d(A,B),d(B,A)\}
, where
d(A,B)=\mathrm{sup}\{d(a,B):a\in A\}
and
d(a,B)=\mathrm{inf}\{d(a,b):b\in B\}
. Notice that
d_{H}(A,B)
is finite, since
A,B\in\mathcal{K}(X)
.

Given a metric space
(X,d)
, it is possible to define a metric
d_{\infty}
on
\mathcal{F}(X)
from the Hausdorff metric
d_{H}
on
\mathcal{K}(X)
as

d_{\infty}(u,v)=\sup\{d_{H}(u_{\alpha},v_{\alpha}):\alpha\in\mathbb{I}\}.

The metric
d_{\infty}
is called the
level-wise metric
. The symbol
\tau_{\infty}
stands for the topology induced by
d_{\infty}
.

Given a uniform space
(X,\mathcal{U})
and
U\in\mathcal{U}
, we define the set:

\mathcal{F}[U]=\{(u,v)\in\mathcal{F}(X)\times\mathcal{F}(X):(u_{\alpha},v_{\alpha})\in\mathcal{K}[U],\,\mathrm{\penalty\ for\penalty\ all\penalty\ }\alpha\in\mathbb{I}\}.

Proposition 2.9
.

[
4
]

If
(X,\mathcal{U})
is a uniform space, then
\{\mathcal{F}[U]:U\in\mathcal{U}\}
is a base for a uniformity on
\mathcal{F}(X)
.

We will denote by
\mathcal{U}_{\infty}
the uniformity on
\mathcal{F}(X)
defined in the previous proposition. It is called the
level-wise uniformity
.

Proposition 2.10
.

[
4
]

If
(X,d)
is a metric space and
\mathcal{U}_{d}
is the natural uniformity induced by

, then the uniformity induced by
d_{\infty}
on
\mathcal{F}(X)
is the uniformity
(\mathcal{U}_{d})_{\infty}
.

The following result plays an important role in the proof of Theorem
2.12
.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
K
be a compact subset of a uniform space
(X,\mathcal{U})
and
f:X\to X
a continuous function. Then for every
U\in\mathcal{U}
, there exists
V\in\mathcal{U}
such that if
(x,y)\in V\cap(K\times X)
, then
(f(x),f(y))\in U
.

Proof.

Take
U\in\mathcal{U}
and
U_{1}\in\mathcal{U}
such that
U^{2}_{1}\subseteq U
. By the continuity of
f
, for every
x\in K
, there exists
W_{x}\in\mathcal{U}
such that
f(W_{x}(x))\subseteq U_{1}(f(x))
. For every
x\in K
, we choose a symmetric
V_{x}\in\mathcal{U}
such that
V^{2}_{x}\subseteq W_{x}
.
Since
K
is compact, we can find
x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n}\in K
with
K\subseteq\bigcup^{n}_{i=1}V_{x_{i}}(x_{i})
. Put
V=\bigcap^{n}_{i=1}V_{x_{i}}
. Take
x\in K
and
y\in X
such that
(x,y)\in V
. Then
x\in V_{x_{i}}(x_{i})\subseteq W_{x_{i}}(x_{i})
for some
i=1,...,n
. So
(x_{i},x)\in V_{x_{i}}
and
(f(x),f(x_{i}))\in U_{1}
. We also have that
(x,y)\in V\subseteq V_{x_{i}}
. So
(x_{i},y)\in V^{2}_{x_{i}}\subseteq W_{x_{i}}
. Hence,
y\in W_{x_{i}}(x_{i})
. So
(f(x_{i}),f(y))\in U_{1}
. We conclude that
(f(x),f(y))\in U^{2}_{1}\subseteq U
.
∎

Theorem 2.12
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{U})
be a uniform space. If
f:(X,\mathcal{U})\to(X,\mathcal{U})
is continuous, then
\widehat{f}:(\mathcal{F}(X),\mathcal{U}_{\infty})\to(\mathcal{F}(X),\mathcal{U}_{\infty})
is continuous.

Proof.

Suppose that
f:(X,\mathcal{U})\to(X,\mathcal{U})
is continuous. Take
u\in\mathcal{F}(X)
and
U\in\mathcal{U}
. Pick a symmetric
W\in\mathcal{U}
such that
W\subseteq U
. By Lemma
2.11
, there exists
V\in\mathcal{U}
such that
x\in u_{0}=K
,
y\in X
, and
(x,y)\in V
imply
(f(x),f(y))\in W
. Let us show that
\widehat{f}(\mathcal{F}[V](u))\subseteq\mathcal{F}[U](\widehat{f}(u))
. Indeed, if
v\in\mathcal{F}[V](u)
, then
(u_{\alpha},v_{\alpha})\in\mathcal{K}[V]
for each
\alpha\in[0,1]
, i.e.,
u_{\alpha}\subseteq V(v_{\alpha})
and
v_{\alpha}\subseteq V(u_{\alpha})
for each
\alpha\in[0,1]
. Fix
\alpha\in[0,1]
. If
x\in u_{\alpha}\subseteq K
, there exists
y\in v_{\alpha}
such that

. By the choice of

, we can conclude that

. So
f(x)\in W(f(y))\subseteq W(f(v_{\alpha}))
. Hence
f(u_{\alpha})\subseteq W(f(v_{\alpha}))
. Now if

, there exists
x\in u_{\alpha}\subseteq K
such that
(x,y)\in V
. By the choice of
V
, we can conclude that
(f(x),f(y))\in W
. So

. It follows that
(f(u_{\alpha}),f(v_{\alpha}))\in\mathcal{K}[W]

([\widehat{f}(u)]_{\alpha},[\widehat{f}(v)]_{\alpha})\in\mathcal{K}[W]
. We have thus proved that
\widehat{f}(v)\in\mathcal{F}[U](\widehat{f}(u))
. This completes the proof.
∎

We now turn our attention to the Skorokhod uniformity.

The symbol
\mathbb{T}
denotes the family of all increasing homeomorphisms from

onto itself. Given

, we define

be a metric space. The

on

for all

Let
(X,\mathcal{U})

\mathrm{exists\penalty\ }t\in\mathbb{T}\mathrm{\penalty\ with\penalty\ }\|t\|<\epsilon\mathrm{\penalty\ and\penalty\ }(u_{\alpha},v_{t(\alpha)})\in\mathcal{K}[U]\mathrm{\penalty\ for\penalty\ all\penalty\ }\alpha\in\mathbb{I}\}

.

[
4

If

is a uniform space, then

.

[

3
Transitivity on some uniformities

A
dynamical system
is a pair
(X,f)
, where
X
is a topological space and
f\colon X\to X
is a continuous function.
In this section, we mainly characterize the transitivity of the dynamical systems
\widehat{f}\colon(\mathcal{F}(X),\mathcal{U}_{\infty})\to(\mathcal{F}(X),\mathcal{U}_{\infty})
,
\widehat{f}\colon(\mathcal{F}(X),\mathcal{U}_{0})\to(\mathcal{F}(X),\mathcal{U}_{0})
, and
\widehat{f}\colon(\mathcal{F}(X),\mathcal{U}_{S})\to(\mathcal{F}(X),\mathcal{U}_{S})
(see Theorem
3.7
).

Let
X
be a topological space and
f\colon X\to X
be a continuous function.
Let us recall that a dynamical system
(X,f)
is
transitive
if for every non-empty open subsets
U
and
V
of
X
, there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
f^{n}(U)\cap V\neq\emptyset
. We say that
(X,f)
is
weakly mixing
if
f\times f\colon X\times X\to X\times X
is transitive. Let us recall that
\overline{f}\colon\mathcal{K}(X)\to\mathcal{K}(X)
is defined by
\overline{f}(K)=f(K)
for each
K\in\mathcal{K}(X)
.

Let
f\colon X\to X
be a continuous function on a topological space
X
. Banks
[
1
]
and Peris
[
9
]
showed that
(X,f)
is weakly mixing if and only if
(\mathcal{K}(X),\overline{f})
is transitive. To be precise, they show the following.

Theorem 3.1
.

[
1
,
9
]

Let
f\colon X\to X
be a continuous function on a topological space
X
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

(X,f)
is weakly mixing.

(2)

(\mathcal{K}(X),\overline{f})
is weakly mixing.

(3)

(\mathcal{K}(X),\overline{f})
is transitive.

A dynamical system
(X,f)
is
weakly mixing of order
m
(
m\geq 2
) if the function

\underbrace{f\times\cdots\times f}_{m-times}\colon X^{m}\to X^{m}

is transitive. We need the following five results in the proof of Theorem
3.7
.

Theorem 3.2
.

[
1
, Theorem 1]

If
(X,f)
a is weakly mixing dynamical system, then
(X,f)
is weakly mixing of all orders.

Proposition 3.3
.

[
5
]

Let
X
be a Hausdorff space and
u\in\mathcal{F}(X)
. If
L_{u}\colon\mathbb{I}\to(\mathcal{K}(X),\tau_{V})
is the function defined by
L_{u}(\alpha)=u_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha\in I
, then the following holds:

i)

L_{u}
is left continuous on
(0,1]
;

ii)

\lim\limits_{\lambda\rightarrow\alpha^{+}}L_{u}(\lambda)=\overline{\bigcup_{\beta>\alpha}u_{\beta}}
and
\lim\limits_{\lambda\rightarrow\alpha^{+}}L_{u}(\lambda)\subset u_{\alpha}
for each
\alpha\in[0,1)
;

iii)

L_{u}
is right continuous at
0
.

Conversely, for any decreasing family
\{u_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\mathbb{I}\}\subseteq\mathcal{K}(X)
satisfying i)–iii), there exists a unique
w\in\mathcal{F}(X)
such that
w_{\alpha}=u_{\alpha}
for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{I}
.

Let
X
be a Hausdorff space. For any
u\in\mathcal{F}(X)
and
\alpha\in[0,1)
, define
u_{\alpha^{+}}=\lim\limits_{\lambda\rightarrow\alpha^{+}}L_{u}(\lambda)
. It follows from ii) of the previous proposition that
L_{u}
is right continuous at
\alpha
if and only if
u_{\alpha^{+}}=u_{\alpha}
.

Proposition 3.4
.

[
4
]

Let
(X,\mathcal{U})
be a uniform space. If
W\in\mathcal{U}
, the sets
A,B,C,F,G,H
are in
\mathcal{K}(X)
and
A\subseteq B\subseteq C
, then we have the following:

i)

If
(A,C)\in\mathcal{K}[W]
, then
(A,B)\in\mathcal{K}[W]
.

ii)

If
(A,C)\in\mathcal{K}[W]
, then
(B,C)\in\mathcal{K}[W]
.

iii)

If
(A,F)\in\mathcal{K}[W]
and
(G,H)\in\mathcal{K}[W]
, then
(A\cup G,F\cup H)\in\mathcal{K}[W]
.

Lemma 3.5
.

[
4
]

Suppose that
(X,\mathcal{U})
is a uniform space. If
u\in\mathcal{F}(X)
, then for each
W\in\mathcal{U}
, there exist numbers
0=\alpha_{0}<\alpha_{1}<\dots<\alpha_{n}=1
such that
(u_{\alpha^{+}_{k}},u_{\alpha_{k+1}})\in\mathcal{K}[W]
for
k=0,1,\dots,n-1
.

The following result is an immediate consequence of Proposition
3.4
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Suppose that
(X,\mathcal{U})
is a uniform space. Take
u\in\mathcal{F}(X)
,
W\in\mathcal{U}
, and a partition

such that
(u_{\alpha^{+}_{k}},u_{\alpha_{k+1}})\in\mathcal{K}[W]
for

(u_{\beta^{+}_{k}},u_{\beta_{k+1}})\in\mathcal{K}[W]
for

.

We are now ready to present the main result of this paper.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{U})
be a uniform space. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

i)

f:(X,\mathcal{U})\to(X,\mathcal{U})
is weakly mixing;

ii)

\overline{f}:(\mathcal{K}(X),\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{U}))\to(\mathcal{K}(X),\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{U}))

\widehat{f}:(\mathcal{F}(X),\mathcal{U}_{\infty})\to(\mathcal{F}(X),\mathcal{U}_{\infty})
is transitive;

iv)

\widehat{f}:(\mathcal{F}(X),\mathcal{U}_{0})\to(\mathcal{F}(X),\mathcal{U}_{0})
is transitive;

v)

\widehat{f}:(\mathcal{F}(X),\mathcal{U}_{S})\to(\mathcal{F}(X),\mathcal{U}_{S})

Proof.

By Theorem
3.1
, we have that i)
\Leftrightarrow
ii). We have that iii)
\Rightarrow

\Rightarrow

2.20
.

Let us show that ii) implies iii). Suppose that
\overline{f}:(\mathcal{K}(X),\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{U}))\to(\mathcal{K}(X),\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{U}))

\widehat{f}:(\mathcal{F}(X),\mathcal{U}_{\infty})\to(\mathcal{F}(X),\mathcal{U}_{\infty})

Take
u,v\in\mathcal{F}(X)
and

.

and

.
By Lemma
3.5
, there exist numbers

(3.1)

(v,z)\in\mathcal{F}[V]^{2}.

(a,x_{a})\in U
, whence
a\in U(x_{a})\subseteq U(K)
. It follows that
A\subseteq U(K)
. On the other hand,
K\times\{0\}\subseteq send(u)\subseteq[U\times V_{1}](send(w))
. Hence, for each
k\in K
, we can find
(x_{k},\alpha)\in send(w)
such that
(k,x_{k})\in U
, whence
k\in U(x_{k})\subseteq U(A)
. It follows that
K\subseteq U(A)
. Therefore,
A\in\mathcal{K}[U](K)
. Similarly, we can show that
\overline{f}^{n}(A)\in\mathcal{K}[V](L)
. This completes the proof.
∎

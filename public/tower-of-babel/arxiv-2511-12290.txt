AugAbEx : Way Forward for Extractive Case Summarization
Purnima Bindal, Vikas Kumar, Sagar Rathore, Vasudha Bhatnagar

Abstract
Summarization of legal judgments poses a heavy cognitive burden on law practitioners due to the complexity of the language, context-sensitive legal jargon, and the length of the document. Therefore, the automatic summarization of legal documents has attracted serious attention from natural language processing researchers. Since the abstractive summaries of legal documents generated by deep neural methods remain prone to the risk of misrepresenting nuanced legal jargon or overlooking key contextual details, we envisage a rising trend toward the use of extractive case summarizers. Given the high cost of human annotation for gold standard extractive summaries, we engineer a light and transparent pipeline that leverages existing abstractive gold standard summaries to create the corresponding extractive gold standard versions. The approach ensures that the experts‘ opinions ensconced in the original gold standard abstractive summaries are carried over to the transformed extractive summaries. We aim to augment seven existing case summarization datasets, which include abstractive summaries, by incorporating corresponding extractive summaries and create an enriched data resource for case summarization research community. To ensure the quality of the augmented extractive summaries, we perform an extensive comparative evaluation with the original abstractive gold standard summaries covering structural, lexical, and semantic dimensions. We also compare the domain-level information of the two summaries. We commit to release the augmented datasets in the public domain for use by the research community and believe that the resource will offer opportunities to advance the field of automatic summarization of legal documents.

AugAbEx: Pipeline for Augmenting Abstractive with Extractive Summaries

Figure 1
:
Pipeline to transform original abstractive gold (OAG) summary to transformed extractive gold (TEG) summary.

In this section, we introduce a pipeline that transforms the original abstractive gold standard summary into its extractive version by selecting important sentences that agree with the expert’s view of salience implicit in the human-written gold standard case summaries. The availability of abstractive, legal experts-written summaries makes the task automatic, transparent, and efficient. The strategy is also economical, as a finite expense has already been incurred to create human-written summaries in these datasets, explicitly by
20
.

Our pipeline integrates components from distinct strands of established research. Transformation of the original abstractive gold summary (OAG) to its extractive counterpart is achieved in two stages, as shown in Fig.
1
. In the first stage, we select the top-
k
similar sentences from the source judgment for each sentence in the abstractive summary and form a candidate set. We craft the extractive summary in the second stage by employing the Maximum Marginal Relevance (MMR) technique
(
5
)
on the candidate set. MMR technique ensures the selection of the most pertinent sentences for the
(transformed)
extractive gold summary (TEG) while maintaining diversity and minimizing redundancy. The transformation is designed to maintain the insight of the law expert (author of the OAG summary) about the important aspects of the judgment while selecting salient sentences to create the extractive summary. We describe the pipeline in detail below.

Candidate Sentence Selection:

The first stage of the pipeline utilizes the
method
5
5

5

https://github.com/Law-AI/summarization/tree/aacl/extractive/abs_to_ext
devised by
20
to construct a set of candidate sentences for TEG summary. The method finds lexical overlap of each sentence in the original abstractive gold standard summary (OAG) with each sentence in the case document by computing the ROUGE-1, ROUGE-2, and ROUGE-L scores. Next, the average of three scores is calculated for each sentence, based on which the top-
k
highest scoring sentences from the case document are selected corresponding to each OAG summary sentence. Using ROUGE metrics ensures that the selected judgment sentences align closely with the lexical content and the structure of the OAG summary, leading to a healthy selection of candidate sentences. The reliance on automated metrics introduces objectivity in selecting the most informative sentences in the document.

Summary Creation:

Stage two of the proposed pipeline uses the Maximal Marginal Relevance (MMR) technique to select summary sentences while balancing relevance and content diversity
(
5
;
17
;
25
;
14
;
1
)
. The MMR method is applied to the candidate set to prioritize sentences that are highly relevant to the core aspects of the legal decision, while minimizing redundancy. The technique is particularly advantageous in legal summarization, as it effectively distills a complex judgment into a non-repetitive summary, ensuring both completeness and brevity in the final extractive version.

Given a pool of candidate sentences
C
, the decision to include a sentence
c_{i}(\in C)
is based on the sentence scoring using the following equation
(
20
)
.

\text{MMR}(c_{i})=\lambda\cdot\sigma(c_{i},C)-(1-\lambda)\cdot\sigma(c_{i},S_{\text{E}})

Here,
\sigma(c_{i},C)
measures the cosine similarity of candidate sentence
c_{i}
to the set of sentences in
C
. On the other hand,
\sigma(c_{i},S_{\text{E}})
, measures the similarity between
c_{i}
and the set of sentences already selected in the
under-construction
extractive summary
(S_{\text{E}})
to maintain diversity. The parameter
\lambda
controls the trade-off between relevance and diversity. The sentence with the highest marginal relevance score is
greedily
chosen and added to the summary.

The method focuses on reducing redundancy by penalizing candidate sentences that are too similar to those already selected in the summary
S_{\text{E}}
, encouraging the inclusion of new and diverse information. We set
\lambda
to 0.5 to balance relevance and redundancy, ensuring that the summary captures key information while minimizing overlap, and create the summary
S_{\text{E}}
of length nearly equal to the word count of OAG summary.

Framework for Comparative Evaluation

For the transformed extractive summaries to be of practical use, the
\langle
OAG, TEG
\rangle
summary pair must be similar along the structural, lexical and semantic dimensions. Most importantly, a TEG summary must not contain less legal information than its corresponding OAG summary. These requirements necessitate a multidimensional approach for the comparative assessment of the abstractive and the corresponding extractive case summaries. We examine multiple attributes for each dimension, starting with domain attributes, followed by semantic and lexical attributes, and concluding with structural attributes. This structured approach enables a comprehensive comparison of the characteristics of OAG and TEG summaries. For analyses that are carried over the pairwise comparison of scores, we perform suitable statistical tests for robust inference. Figure
2
presents the evaluation framework, which is discussed in detail below.

3.1
Domain Attributes

Including domain-specific information in a judgment summary is crucial to facilitate accurate and consistent interpretation of the case by law practitioners. Legal entities are critically important for the practitioners because they help to verify the summary’s correctness, assess its contextual relevance and alignment with essential domain-specific information in the case document. Since summary is a lossy compression of the original text document, it must incur minimal loss of legal terms, entities, and jargon contained in the parent document. Comparing the legal entities in the original and transformed summaries reveals how much the transformation depletes the technical legal information. In order to assess the ability of the pipeline to preserve the core legal information, we use the (i) count of all legal entites identified in the summary as the indicator of its richness for the end user, and (ii) recall of legal provisions captured in TEG.

The legal entity count,
Lent-cnt
metric, is used to compare and quantify the difference in the core legal information contained in OAG and TEG summaries. A higher entity count in the extractive summary ensures that key legal information is secured, which provides clarity to a legal professional and confirms its fidelity. Applicable statutes and provisions mentioned in a case document include references to specific articles, sections, and clauses from laws, constitutions, and regulations, and they provide the legal framework for the court’s decision. Due to the intense information they carry, capturing provisions in a case summary is vital. The
ProvRecall
is a recall metric for provisions in the OAG summary that are also captured in the TEG summary. If
PO
and
PT
denote the number of provisions identified in OAG and TEG summary, then
\frac{PO\cap PT}{PO}
computes the legal provisions recall (
ProvRecall
). High recall value guarantees that the TEG summary has secured the mention of legal provisions in significant proportion.

3.2
Semantic Attributes

The next dimension of scrutiny delves into the quantitative assessment of how the two summaries convey the core concepts, ideas, and information in the case document through semantic linking of terms. Similar semantic attributes of the two summaries ensure that they convey nuanced meanings and that the case document is interpreted correctly in different contexts. We gauge the semantic similarity between the original abstractive gold standard (OAG) and the transformed extractive gold standard (TEG) summaries using a three-pronged approach. We find and compare semantic similarity between the
\langle
OAG, TEG
\rangle
pair (i) within the latent space uncovered using latent semantic analysis (LSA)
(
21
)
, and (ii) in the embedding space of the LegalBert
(
6
)
. Additionally, we find the semantic similarity between the two summaries and the case document using LSA.

The semantic-based automatic similarity assessment metric using LSA
(
21
)
is reference-free and captures the commonality between the main topics of the two documents. Based on the distributional hypothesis, it effectively gauges the consistency and fidelity of the representation of a case document by the two summaries. Evaluating the semantic alignment between the OAG and TEG summaries in the embedding space using LegalBert
(
6
)
model expands the analysis. LegalBert is an advanced embedding model trained to analyze the contextual relationships and semantic connections in legal documents. LegalBert embeddings capture the nuanced legal context and relationships within the text and facilitate a sophisticated comparison of the semantic content of the two case summaries. Finally, we gauge the semantic congruence between the abstractive gold standard reference summaries and the transformed extractive summaries by comparing them with the case documents. We perform a paired comparison of the semantic proximity of the OAG and TEG summaries to the case documents they represent, to infer the performance of the transformation pipeline described in Sec.
2
. The three attributes offer a robust quantitative comparison of the information encased in the two summaries.

3.3
Lexical Attributes

Considering TEG summaries as an approximation of OAG versions, the degree of lexical similarity between abstractive and extractive summaries indicates how well the key terms in the original abstractive summaries are preserved. A high overlap between them reflects a consistent representation of the crucial content, enhancing the reliability of the transformed extractive summaries.

We use three lexical attributes to gauge the degree of shared terminology between OAG and TEG summaries and the difference between the usage of language. Specifically, we find the (i) vocabulary overlap between two summaries, (ii) distance between their term distributions, (iii) comparative divergence of the two summaries from the case document. The classical ROUGE metric quantifies the vocabulary overlap between the two summaries. The distance and divergence metrics are information-theoretic in nature and take the probabilistic view of the terms in a document. They offer insights into the writing style and emphasis, and advance a perspective different from straightforward lexical overlap.

We use the Jensen-Shannon distance (JSD) between the two summaries, a symmetric measure bounded between 0 and 1, to quantify the difference between their term distributions. It is an interpretable, stable, and robust measure to compare the use of legal lexicon in OAG and TEG summaries. The third lexical attribute, the KL-divergence between the case document and the two summaries, offers insights into the language usage and reveals shared and distinct linguistic patterns.

3.4
Structural Attributes

Structural attributes offer a glimpse of the surface-level characteristics of the textual summaries, and their comparison for the original abstractive and transformed extractive summaries furnishes insights into their syntactical complexities. Considering
terms
to be the minimal lexical units and sentences to be maximal lexical units, we use two basal metrics - (i) word count, which reflects the summary length, (ii) average sentence length, which indicates the complexity of the sentences in the summary. We use a third metric, the Flesch-Kincaid reading ease score, which quantifies how easy the text is to read. The reading score ranges from 0 to 100, with higher score indicating easier reading.

Comparable summary length of OAG and TEG summaries is integral for the subsequent comparison at lexical, semantic and domain levels. The average sentence length indicates the summary’s structural composition and granularity. With the objective to examine the impact of the transformation of OAG summaries on the reading ease, we compare the readability of OAG and TEG summaries using Flesch-Kincaid reading ease score
(
13
)
computed as follows.

FK\text{-}Score=206.835-1.015\times ASL-84.6\times ASW

Here,
ASL
is the average sentence length and
ASW
denotes the average syllables per word. Note that the readability of the text with complex words is severely penalized due to the high negative coefficient of
ASW
. Overall, texts with longer sentences and complicated words tend to have lower readability scores, while texts featuring shorter sentences and less complex vocabulary obtain a higher readability score.

3.5
Statistical Test

Aggregating the scores of the relevant metric using mean or median is the most popular strategy for comparison of two NLP systems.
18
argue that this procedure may be unreliable for two reasons. First, the idiosyncrasies of these two aggregation function may obfuscate the true patterns, and second, these aggregation functions ignore the instance-level pairing. The authors demonstrate, theoretically and empirically, the robustness of the Bradley-Terry (BT) model, which analyses instance-level scores and aggregates to infer the relative superiority of one system over the other.

The BT model compares the evaluation scores of two systems at the instance level and infers the relative superiority of a system based on how frequently it wins. In this study, we compare systems
O
and
T
, where
O
represents the source of original abstractive gold (OAG) summaries, and system
T
corresponds to the transformation pipeline for generating extractive gold (TEG) summaries. Let
\lambda_{O}
(
\lambda_{T}
) denote the number of times system
O
(
T
) scores higher than system
T
(
O
), over all instances in a dataset. Based on this data, BT model finds the following probability.

P(O>T)=\frac{\lambda_{O}}{\lambda_{O}+\lambda_{T}}

(1)

This probability (
\hat{\lambda}_{O}
) connotes the estimated latent strength of the system
O
, and expresses the chance that it performs better than system
T
for the dataset. We use the BT model to estimate the strengths of the systems to draw an inference about the leading system, for attributes where pairwise comparison is performed.

6
Conclusion

In view of the recent studies that favor the extractive approach for legal document summarization, this research performs the timely task of transforming original abstractive gold standard summaries to their extractive version. We use an efficient and effective pipeline, engineered using existing approaches to transform original abstractive summaries in seven case summarization datasets to their extractive counterparts. The transformed extractive summaries are evaluated along structural, lexical, and semantic dimensions for fidelity. We also check out the domain-specific information carried by the transformed summaries compared to the original ones. Experimental results reveal that the extractive version of the original abstractive summary are structurally, lexically and semantically similar. However, they lag behind the original summaries in capturing the legal provisions in the judgment.

Human-written summaries are naturally rich in legal information because of the clever abridgment of the case document by skillful law experts, who contextually interpret and understand legal terms. Extractive case summarization algorithms must be bolstered to prioritize sentences that are rich in core legal and technical information in the form of legal concepts, terminology, and entities. Enriching extractive summaries with legal terms and entities to make them more valuable to the audience by strategizing sentence selection is the way forward for automatic case summarization. Furthermore, careful evaluation of the gold standard summaries in the case summarization datasets released in the future will aid the development of helpful technology for law professionals.

The Forgotten Shield: Safety Grafting in Parameter-Space for Medical MLLMs
Jiale Zhao, Xing Mou, Jinlin Wu, Hongyuan Yu, Mingrui Sun, Yang Shi, Xuanwu Yin, Zhen Chen, Zhen Lei, Yaohua Wang, [0.4ex] National University of Defense Technology

Abstract
Medical Multimodal Large Language Models (Medical MLLMs) have achieved remarkable progress in specialized medical tasks; however, research into their safety has lagged, posing potential risks for real-world deployment. In this paper, we first establish a multidimensional evaluation framework to systematically benchmark the safety of current SOTA Medical MLLMs. Our empirical analysis reveals pervasive vulnerabilities across both general and medical-specific safety dimensions in existing models, particularly highlighting their fragility against cross-modality jailbreak attacks. Furthermore, we find that the medical fine-tuning process frequently induces catastrophic forgetting of the model’s original safety alignment. To address this challenge, we propose a novel ”Parameter-Space Intervention” approach for efficient safety re-alignment. This method extracts intrinsic safety knowledge representations from original base models and concurrently injects them into the target model during the construction of medical capabilities. Additionally, we design a fine-grained parameter search algorithm to achieve an optimal trade-off between safety and medical performance. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach significantly bolsters the safety guardrails of Medical MLLMs without relying on additional domain-specific safety data, while minimizing degradation to core medical performance.

To fill this gap, we construct a multi-dimensional evaluation framework to systematically assess the safety of SOTA medical MLLMs. We leverage a suite of state-of-the-art safety benchmarks and jailbreak attacks to simulate diverse threat vectors, thereby comprehensively evaluating the model’s safety across both general and medical-specific safety dimensions. Pervasive Safety Gaps in SOTA Medical MLLMs
: While medical MLLMs have achieved remarkable progress in specialized tasks, current SOTA medical MLLMs, such as Lingshu-7B
Team et al. 2025
and HuatuoGPT-Vision
Chen et al. 2024

). Furthermore, these medical MLLMs reveal more severe safety vulnerabilities when subjected to cross-modality jailbreak attacks. For instance, when Lingshu-7B is subjected to FigStep—a typographic visual prompt attack—its safety scores on HarmBench and MedSafetyBench precipitously decline from 0.4750 and 0.6022 (under baseline textual prompts) to 0.2375 and 0.3467, respectively, representing substantial reductions of over 50% and 42%.

Domain-Specific Fine-tuning Induced Safety Degradation
: The medical fine-tuning process compromises the model’s safety alignment. For instance, Lingshu-7B (fine-tuned from Qwen-2.5-VL-7B), despite achieving enhanced medical performance, exhibits degraded general and medical-specific safety scores compared to its base model. This suggests that the fine-tuning process, even with benign data, can catastrophically forget the model’s original safety alignment, introducing security vulnerabilities.

Enhanced Yet Insufficient Safety in Medical MLLMs
: More recent Medical MLLMs demonstrate enhanced safety awareness, yet their absolute safety levels remain insufficient. For example, on the 3MAD-Tiny-1K benchmark, Lingshu-7B (0.8000) outperforms HuatuoGPT-Vision-7B (0.6731) and LLaVA-Med (0.5167). We attribute this to iterations in the base models and an increased focus on safety alignment by researchers. However, synthesizing results across all benchmarks including general safety and medical specific saferty benchmarks, the absolute safety of these medical MLLMs remains inadequate.

Inspired by the aforementioned empirical findings, this paper proposes a novel Parameter-Space Intervention method aimed at achieving safety re-alignment for medical MLLMs. Specifically, our approach first extracts intrinsic safety knowledge representations from the original base model (without fine-tuning) corresponding to the SOTA medical MLLM. Subsequently, we concurrently inject this extracted safety knowledge (safety vector) during the process of endowing the original base model with medical domain knowledge. To strike an optimal balance between safety and medical performance, we devise a fine-grained parameter search algorithm to determine the optimal coefficients for both types of knowledge injection. Ultimately, this method significantly bolsters the safety guardrails of medical MLLMs while minimizing degradation to their core medical performance.

Our contributions are as follows:

(1)

Systematic Safety Benchmarking: We conduct a comprehensive safety evaluation of current SOTA medical MLLMs, spanning two critical dimensions: general safety and medical-specific safety.

(2)

In-depth Empirical Analysis: Building on extensive evaluation results, we perform a rigorous empirical analysis that reveals critical vulnerabilities in existing medical MLLMs and distills three insightful core conclusions.

(3)

Cost-efficient Safety Re-alignment Framework: We propose a novel safety re-alignment framework that significantly enhances model safety at a low cost without relying on expensive high-quality human-annotated safety data or additional domain-specific data, while simultaneously minimizing degradation to core medical performance.

Enhancing Model Accuracy: From Data Curation to Architectural Innovation.
As model capabilities expand, enhancing accuracy of Med-MLLMs has emerged as a central research focus. Early approaches, such as LLaVA-Med, relied on instruction data generated by ”blind” LLMs that only processed textual captions. This reliance inevitably introduced noise and potential hallucinations into the training signal. To address these limitations, subsequent research has focused on optimizations spanning both data curation and model architecture.
At the data level, HuatuoGPT-Vision

At the architectural level, MedGemma incorporates MedSigLIP, a medically-tuned visual encoder designed to boost downstream performance. Furthermore, HealthGPT
Lin et al. 2025
proposes Heterogeneous Low-Rank Adaptation (H-LORA), a mechanism specifically tailored to mitigate performance degradation arising from conflicting objectives between ”understanding” and ”generation” tasks.

Safety Benchmark.
General safety benchmarks aim to evaluate a model’s capacity to refuse harmful instructions within general domains, thereby preventing the generation of outputs that provide guidance on manufacturing dangerous goods or engaging in illicit activities. Representative benchmarks in this domain include HarmBench
Mazeika et al. 2024
, MMJ-Bench
Weng et al. 2025
, and so on.

However, general benchmarks often fail to fully address specific risks in critical medical contexts, such as fabricating clinical trial data or providing unethical medical advice. Consequently, safety benchmarks specifically tailored for medical scenarios have emerged to more accurately assess model reliability and compliance in these clinical environments. MedSafetyBench
Han et al. 2024
is the first benchmark dataset specifically designed to evaluate the medical safety of large language models. It defines medical safety standards based on the Principles of Medical Ethics established by the American Medical Association (AMA) and contains 1,800 harmful medical requests to measure faithful adherence to these important ethical requirements.

As research advances, medical safety benchmarks have begun to incorporate more complex evaluation dimensions. The CARES benchmark
Chen et al. 2025
not only covers over 18,000 prompts based on eight medical safety principles but also introduces four graded levels of harmfulness, alongside diverse prompting styles such as direct, indirect, obfuscated, and role-play. This benchmark aims to comprehensively evaluate the robustness of medical large models against adversarial attacks (e.g., ”jailbreak” prompts) and address issues of over-refusal when handling ambiguous queries.

Addressing the unique risks of multimodal models, the 3MAD (Multimodal Medical Model Attack Dataset)
Huang et al. 2024
was proposed to resolve challenges related to ”mismatched” and ”malicious” queries in clinical environments.

Safety Alignment.
Currently, safety alignment for MLLMs predominantly relies on post-training paradigms, such as SFT
Wei et al. 2021
and RLHF
Ouyang et al. 2022
. While these methods demonstrate significant effectiveness in general domains, they are typically resource-intensive and highly dependent on large-scale, high-quality human-annotated safety data and domain-specific data. Particularly in specialized fields such as medicine, acquiring domain-specific training data is difficult. Furthermore, constructing high-quality safety datasets that adhere to clinical standards and cover a wide range of risk scenarios is extremely challenging and costly, which restricts the rapid adaptation and deployment of traditional training-based alignment methods in medical settings.

Meanwhile, Model Merging has garnered increasing attention as an efficient safety alignment paradigm recently
Wortsman et al. 2022
. Unlike traditional methods that require expensive re-training processes, model merging techniques effectively inject safety capabilities into target models by directly performing arithmetic interventions or interpolations in the parameter space. This approach circumvents the severe reliance on additional domain-specific safety training data, achieving the migration and integration of safety knowledge with minimal computational overhead.

In this section, we elaborate on our proposed Parameter-Space Re-alignment framework, designed to restore the safety capabilities of Medical MLLMs that are often compromised during fine-tuning, without requiring additional training data. As illustrated in Figure
2
, our approach comprises four distinct stages: 1) obtaining an unaligned version of the base model to extract safety vector; 2) deriving a medical performance vector; 3) implementing feature disentanglement based on Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization; and 4) conducting fine-grained parameter-space optimization via CMA-ES.

3.1
Preliminaries and Problem Definition

Let
\theta_{\text{base}}
denote a pre-finetuned base model (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL). Following medical fine-tuning, we obtain a specialized medical model
\theta_{\text{med}}
(e.g., Lingshu). While
\theta_{\text{med}}
demonstrates superior performance in the medical domain, it frequently suffers from catastrophic forgetting, leading to a significant degradation of its inherent safety capabilities. Our objective is to identify an optimal parameter set
\theta^{*}
that enhances safety while preserving medical competency. Specifically, we achieve this by searching for a fine-grained linear combination of medical performance vectors and safety vectors within the parameter space.

3.2
Vector Extraction and Orthogonalization

Safety Vector Extraction
: To explicitly characterize ”safety capability” within the parameter space, we first need to construct a contrasting ”unsafe” state. We perform an unalignment operation on the base model
\theta_{\text{base}}
(e.g., via reverse gradient updates on a small subset of harmful data) to obtain an unsafe model
\theta_{\text{unsafe}}

Bhardwaj et al. 2024
. Consequently, the divergence between the original base model and this unsafe model in the parameter space delineates the model’s intrinsic safety attributes. We define the raw safety vector
\mathbf{v}_{s}^{\text{raw}}
as follows, This vector indicates the direction of transition from ”unsafe” to ”safe” within the parameter space:

\mathbf{v}_{s}^{\text{raw}}=\theta_{\text{base}}-\theta_{\text{unsafe}}

(1)

Medical Performance Vector Extraction
: We define the medical performance vector
\mathbf{v}_{m}^{\text{raw}}
as the difference between the medically fine-tuned model and the base model, representing the specific capabilities acquired by the model in the medical domain:

\mathbf{v}_{m}^{\text{raw}}=\theta_{\text{med}}-\theta_{\text{base}}

(2)

Orthogonal Disentanglement
: Directly merging
\mathbf{v}_{s}^{\text{raw}}
and
\mathbf{v}_{m}^{\text{raw}}
may yield sub-optimal results due to potential unanticipated coupling (i.e., non-orthogonality) between safety and medical capabilities in the parameter space, where adjusting one might interfere with the other. To achieve independent control over these two capabilities, we employ Gram-Schmidt Orthogonalization to disentangle the vectors. Our core objective is to introduce safety while maximally preserving medical performance. Therefore, we treat the medical vector as the basis axis and remove the component from the safety vector that ”conflicts” (i.e., is parallel) with medical capability, retaining only the ”pure” safety component orthogonal to medical capability. The rectified orthogonal safety vector
\mathbf{v}_{s}^{\perp}
is calculated as follows:

\begin{split}\mathbf{v}_{s}^{\perp}&=\mathbf{v}_{s}^{\text{raw}}-\text{proj}_{\mathbf{v}_{m}^{\text{raw}}}(\mathbf{v}_{s}^{\text{raw}})\\
&=\mathbf{v}_{s}^{\text{raw}}-\frac{\mathbf{v}_{s}^{\text{raw}}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{m}^{\text{raw}}}{\|\mathbf{v}_{m}^{\text{raw}}\|^{2}}\mathbf{v}_{m}^{\text{raw}}\end{split}

(3)

To ensure scale consistency in the subsequent search space, we perform normalization on both vectors after orthogonalization, yielding the final control vectors:

\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{s}=\frac{\mathbf{v}_{s}^{\perp}}{\|\mathbf{v}_{s}^{\perp}\|},\hskip 10.00002pt\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{m}=\frac{\mathbf{v}_{m}^{\text{raw}}}{\|\mathbf{v}_{m}^{\text{raw}}\|}

(4)

Through this procedure, we construct a decoupled parameter manipulation space, such that adjustments along the
\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{s}
direction theoretically do not impact the core medical capability represented by
\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{m}
.

3.3
Fine-grained Optimization via CMA-ES

Layer-wise Parameter Space
: Current model merging paradigms typically employ global scalar coefficients across the entire model. However, distinct layers within MLLMs often specialize in different functionalities (e.g., shallow layers processing syntax versus deep layers handling semantics and reasoning). To achieve a precise equilibrium between safety and medical performance, we propose a fine-grained, layer-wise control strategy. Assuming a model with
L
layers, we assign independent safety coefficients
\alpha^{(l)}
and medical coefficients
\beta^{(l)}
to each layer
l\in\{1,\dots,L\}
. The parameters for the
l
-th layer of the final merged model, denoted as
\theta_{\text{target}}^{(l)}
, are formulated as:

\theta_{\text{target}}^{(l)}=\theta_{\text{base}}^{(l)}+\alpha^{(l)}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{s}^{(l)}+\beta^{(l)}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{m}^{(l)}

(5)

where
\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{s}^{(l)}
and
\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{m}^{(l)}
denote the normalized vector components corresponding to that specific layer. Our optimization objective is to identify the optimal coefficient vector
\mathbf{x}=[\alpha^{(1)},\dots,\alpha^{(L)},\beta^{(1)},\dots,\beta^{(L)}]\in\mathbb{R}^{2L}
.

Optimization Algorithm
: Given that this optimization objective involves complex black-box evaluations (necessitating assessing safety and medical scores on a validation set) and operates within a high-dimensional parameter space (
2L
), conventional gradient-based methods are rendered inapplicable. We leverage the Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy (CMA-ES) for gradient-free optimization. We define a joint reward function
R(\theta)
as the optimization objective, aiming to maximize medical performance while simultaneously satisfying safety constraints:

\max_{\mathbf{x}}R(\theta_{\text{final}}(\mathbf{x}))=\lambda_{1}\cdot\text{S}_{\text{med}}(\theta_{\text{final}})+\lambda_{2}\cdot\text{S}_{\text{safe}}(\theta_{\text{final}})

(6)

where
\text{S}_{\text{med}}
and
\text{S}_{\text{safe}}
represent the model’s medical accuracy and safety (general safety and medical-specific safety) compliance rate on the validation set, respectively, with
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
serving as balancing coefficients. CMA-ES iteratively samples candidate coefficient vectors from a multivariate normal distribution and updates the distribution’s mean and covariance matrix based on their validation performance. It ultimately converges to a solution on the Pareto Optimal Frontier, thereby achieving an optimal trade-off between safety and performance.

This section first introduces the experimental setup, including the selected models, evaluation benchmarks, attack methods, and evaluation metrics. Subsequently, through quantitative and qualitative analyses, we systematically evaluate the current safety status of Medical MLLMs and validate the effectiveness of our proposed parameter-space re-alignment method. Concurrently, we investigated the influence of varying optimization granularity on model safety and medical performance. Finally, we conduct ablation studies to demonstrate the effectiveness of the search algorithm within the overall re-alignment framework.

4.1
Experimental Setup

Models
: To comprehensively evaluate the safety status of existing Medical MLLMs, we selected the most representative current open-source medical-specific models as research objects, including Lingshu
Team et al. 2025
and HuatuoGPT-Vision
Chen et al. 2024
. Simultaneously, we also selected a representative general MLLM, Qwen2.5-VL
Bai et al. 2025
, for comparative experiments. Considering computational resource constraints and the principle of fair comparison, all experimental models use the 7B parameter scale version.

Datasets and Benchmarks
: Our evaluation framework covers two core dimensions: medical professional capability and safety (including general safety and medical-specific safety). Medical Performance: To assess the models’core medical capabilities, we employ the MedEvalKit
Team et al. 2025
evaluation framework and conduct extensive testing on eight authoritative medical test sets, including MedQA (MCMLE and USMLE), PubMedQA, SuperGPQA, VQA-RAD, CMExam, and Medbullets (op4 and op5). General Safety: We utilize three general safety test sets—HarmBench, CATQA, and HEx-PHI—aiming to evaluate the models’ defense performance when facing general malicious instructions (e.g., hate speech, illegal suggestions). Medical-Specific Safety: We employ four medical safety benchmarks—MedSafetyBench, CARES, MedSentry, and 3MAD-Tiny-1K—to evaluate model performance in medical-specific high-risk scenarios, such as falsifying medical records and providing harmful medical advice.

Attack Methods
: To simulate unique threats faced by multimodal models in the real world, in addition to standard pure-text attacks, we specifically introduce Cross-modality Jailbreak Attacks. Specifically, these include the FigStep
Gong et al. 2025
attack utilizing typographic visual prompts, and the QR
Liu et al. 2024
(Query-Relevant) attack based on text-to-image models generating query-relevant images. These attacks aim to test the models’ safety robustness when facing more covert and complex adversarial multimodal inputs.

Evaluation Metrics
: For medical performance, based on the MedEvalKit framework, we adopt overall Accuracy (ACC) as the primary evaluation metric. For safety evaluation, we use the Safety Score as the core metric, defined as
1-\text{ASR}
(Attack Success Rate). Given the high cost and non-scalability of manual evaluation, we follow prior work and adopt the ”LLM-as-a-Judge” paradigm for automated evaluation. Specifically, we deploy Qwen3Guard
Zhao et al. 2025
and DeepSeek-V3
DeepSeek-AI 2024
as judge models for general safety and medical safety tests, respectively, to judge whether the target model’s responses violate corresponding safety principles, thereby ensuring the consistency and reproducibility of the evaluation process.

4.2
Effectiveness of Re-alignment Method

We conduct a comprehensive quantitative evaluation to assess the effectiveness of our proposed parameter-space re-alignment framework. The results are shown as Table
1
. Further results at different granularities can be found in the Supplementary Material. The primary objective is to measure the trade-off between restoring holistic safety—against both general and medical-specific threats—and preserving the model’s core medical performance. We benchmark our method against the original medical MLLMs and two representative re-alignment baseline strategies: ModelMerge
Farn et al. 2025
and RESTA
Bhardwaj et al. 2024
.

Analysis of Baselines
. As established in our initial findings, the Original Medical MLLMs (e.g., Lingshu-7B, HuatuoGPT-Vision-7B) serve as our primary baseline. While proficient in medical tasks, they exhibit pervasive safety vulnerabilities. Our analysis of alternative re-alignment methods reveals a difficult trade-off.

ModelMerge, representing parameter merging techniques, generally succeeds in preserving or even slightly enhancing medical task performance. However, we find it provides insufficient safety re-alignment ability, leaving the models susceptible to a significant fraction of general and medical-specific harmful prompts.

RESTA (REstoring Safety through Task Arithmetic), which intervenes by adding a pre-computed safety vector, demonstrates a strong capability to restore safety guardrails. However, this robust safety improvement often comes at the cost of substantial degradation in medical task performance, making it suboptimal for high-stakes clinical applications where domain accuracy is critical.

Effectiveness of Our Method
. In contrast to the baselines, our proposed framework demonstrates an optimal balance, achieving significant safety gains with minimal impact on medical competency. We first analyze the results for Lingshu-7B as follows.

To investigate the impact of parameter search space granularity on safety re-alignment efficacy and medical performance preservation, we compare two distinct intervention strategies:
Model-Wise
and
Layer-Wise
optimization.

Setup.

Let
\mathbf{v}_{s}
and
\mathbf{v}_{m}
denote the safety vector and medical performance vector, respectively. We define the two granularities as follows:

•

Model-Wise:
We assign two global scalar coefficients,
\alpha
and
\beta

\theta_{new}=\theta_{base}+\alpha\cdot\mathbf{v}_{s}+\beta\cdot\mathbf{v}_{m}

•

Layer-Wise:
We assign independent coefficients
\alpha^{(l)}
and
\beta^{(l)}
for each Transformer block
l
. The intervention becomes
\theta_{new}^{(l)}=\theta_{base}^{(l)}+\alpha^{(l)}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{s}^{(l)}+\beta^{(l)}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{m}^{(l)}
. This fine-grained approach allows the optimization algorithm (CMA-ES) to adaptively modulate the injection intensity at different network depths.

Our framework employs the CMA-ES to navigate the parameter space and identify the optimal coefficients. This ablation is designed to verify that this search process is indispensable. We hypothesize that a random combination of the medical performance vector and the safety vector is insufficient to restore safety without catastrophically degrading medical competency. To test this hypothesis, we establish baselines using non-optimized parameters. The CMA-ES algorithm initializes its search by sampling an initial population of candidate coefficient vectors from a multivariate normal distribution. By definition, the coefficient vectors in this first iteration are effectively random samples from the search space before any optimization occurs . We select three distinct, randomly sampled coefficient sets from this initial iteration (denoted as Random-1, Random-2, and Random-3). We then apply these static, non-optimized coefficients to merge the base model with the medical and safety vectors . We report the average performance of these three configurations (Random_Avg) and compare it against our fully optimized framework (Ours-CMA-ES) .

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we addressed a critical vulnerability in Medical Multimodal Large Language Models (Medical MLLMs): the catastrophic forgetting of safety alignment during domain-specific fine-tuning. Our empirical analysis revealed that state-of-the-art Medical MLLMs exhibit pervasive safety gaps, particularly against cross-modality jailbreak attacks.
To remedy this, we proposed a novel Parameter-Space Intervention framework that performs ”safety grafting” to re-align the model efficiently, without requiring additional domain-specific safety data.
Our quantitative results confirm that this approach successfully restores robust safety guardrails, significantly outperforming baseline re-alignment methods. Critically, our ablation studies demonstrate that the fine-grained search is essential; a naive merge results in catastrophic performance degradation, whereas our method preserves high-fidelity medical competency.

Limitations and Future Work
: Despite these promising results, this work presents several limitations that open avenues for future research.

Scalability: Our experiments were conducted on 7B-parameter models. The scalability of our parameter-space grafting approach, particularly the computational cost of the CMA-ES search, on significantly larger foundation models (e.g., 70B+) remains to be validated.

Vector Derivation: The construction of the safety vector currently relies on an ”un-alignment” step to create an unsafe model variant. Future work should investigate more efficient methods for deriving this safety representation, potentially by identifying safety-critical subspaces without full model un-alignment.

Scope of Evaluation: Our analysis focused on the primary trade-off between safety and medical task performance. The impact of our intervention on other vital model properties—such as fairness across demographic groups, calibration, or general-purpose reasoning in non-medical contexts—is an important open question for future exploration.

Methodology Details

In this section, we provide a detailed derivation and theoretical justification for the proposed Parameter-Space Intervention (PSI) framework, specifically focusing on the extraction of task vectors and the necessity of orthogonal disentanglement (Stage 3 of Algorithm 1).

6.1
Theoretical Motivation: Task Vectors in Parameter Space

Recent findings in the geometry of loss landscapes suggest that fine-tuned models often reside in a low-dimensional subspace relative to their pre-trained initialization. Let
\theta_{base}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
denote the parameters of the base foundation model. We hypothesize that specific capabilities (e.g., medical knowledge or safety alignment) can be represented as displacement vectors in the parameter space.

Safety Representation.

Standard safety alignment (e.g., RLHF) shifts model parameters from an unaligned state toward a safe basin. Conversely, we define the ”intrinsic safety capability” as the negation of the ”unsafe” direction. Let
\theta_{unsafe}
be a model explicitly unaligned on harmful datasets
\mathcal{D}_{harm}
. We define the raw safety vector
v_{s}^{raw}
as:

v_{s}^{raw}=\theta_{base}-\theta_{unsafe}

(7)

Here,
\theta_{base}
serves as the anchor. The vector
v_{s}^{raw}
represents the restorative direction required to traverse from a compromised (unsafe) manifold back to the safe initialization.

Medical Representation.

Similarly, the acquisition of medical knowledge via fine-tuning is modeled as a translation vector
v_{m}^{raw}
:

v_{m}^{raw}=\theta_{med}-\theta_{base}

(8)

where
\theta_{med}
is the model fine-tuned on medical corpora.

6.2
Orthogonal Disentanglement Derivation

A naive linear combination of
v_{s}^{raw}
and
v_{m}^{raw}
risks destructive interference. Since both vectors are derived from the same base model, they are not guaranteed to be linearly independent. Specifically, updating parameters along the safety direction might inadvertently traverse ”away” from the medical manifold if the vectors are negatively correlated (cosine similarity
<0
).

To mitigate this, we employ Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization to decouple safety from medical performance. We prioritize medical performance as the basis vector, as it is the primary utility of the specialized model.

Proof of Interference Elimination.

Let the target parameter update be
\Delta\theta=\alpha v_{s}^{raw}+\beta v_{m}^{raw}
. The effective magnitude of the update projected onto the medical axis
v_{m}^{raw}
is:

\text{Proj}_{v_{m}}(\Delta\theta)=\beta v_{m}^{raw}+\alpha\frac{v_{s}^{raw}\cdot v_{m}^{raw}}{\|v_{m}^{raw}\|^{2}}v_{m}^{raw}

(9)

The second term represents the
safety interference
. If
v_{s}^{raw}\cdot v_{m}^{raw}\neq 0
, scaling the safety coefficient
\alpha
implicitly alters the effective medical capability.

To ensure independent control, we construct a rectified safety vector
v_{s}^{\perp}
such that
\langle v_{s}^{\perp},v_{m}^{raw}\rangle=0
:

v_{s}^{\perp}=v_{s}^{raw}-\frac{v_{s}^{raw}\cdot v_{m}^{raw}}{\|v_{m}^{raw}\|^{2}}v_{m}^{raw}

(10)

By substituting
v_{s}^{raw}
with
v_{s}^{\perp}
in the merging process, the safety injection becomes orthogonal to the medical capability. Consequently, increasing the safety weight does not shift the model parameters along the medical axis, theoretically preserving medical performance while enhancing safety.

Algorithmic Description

The complete procedure is formalized in Algorithm

. We employ a layer-wise parameter grafting strategy combined with Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy (CMA-ES) to find the optimal blending coefficients.

Normalization.

Prior to optimization, we normalize the vectors to ensure scale invariance during the search process:

\hat{v}_{s}=\frac{v_{s}^{\perp}}{\|v_{s}^{\perp}\|_{2}},\quad\hat{v}_{m}=\frac{v_{m}^{raw}}{\|v_{m}^{raw}\|_{2}}

(11)

We define the search space as
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2L}
, where
L
is the number of layers. For each layer
l
, we learn specific coefficients
x^{(l)}
(safety) and
x^{(L+l)}
(medical). The final parameters are constructed as:

\theta_{final}^{(l)}=\theta_{base}^{(l)}+x^{(l)}\cdot\hat{v}_{s}^{(l)}+x^{(L+l)}\cdot\hat{v}_{m}^{(l)}

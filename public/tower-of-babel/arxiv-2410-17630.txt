Faber–Krahn type inequality for supertrees
Hongyu Wang, Xinmin Hou

Abstract
The Faber–Krahn inequality states that the first Dirichlet eigenvalue among all bounded domains is no less than a Euclidean ball with the same volume in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} [ 7 ] . Bıyıkoğlu and Leydold (J. Comb. Theory, Ser. B., 2007) demonstrated that the Faber–Krahn inequality also holds for the class of trees with boundary with the same degree sequence and characterized the unique extremal tree. Bıyıkoğlu and Leydold (2007) also posed a question as follows: Give a characterization of all graphs in a given class 𝒞 \mathcal{C} with the Faber–Krahn property. In this paper, we address this question specifically for k k -uniform supertrees with boundary. We introduce a spiral-like ordering (SLO-ordering) of vertices for supertrees, an extension of the SLO-ordering for trees initially proposed by Pruss [ Duke Math. J., 1998], and prove that the SLO-supertree has the Faber-Krahn property among all supertrees with a given degree sequence. Furthermore, among degree sequences that have a minimum degree d d for interior vertices, the SLO-supertree with degree sequence ( d , … , d , d ′ , 1 , … , 1 ) (d,\ldots,d,d^{\prime},1,\dots,1) possesses the Faber-Krahn property.

Introduction

The Faber–Krahn inequality is a well-known result on the Riemannian manifolds, which states that the first Dirichlet eigenvalue among all bounded domains is no less than that of a Euclidean ball with the same volume in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

[
7
]
. This principle was initially conjectured by Lord Rayleigh and later independently proven by Faber and Krahn.
The inequality has been extended and refined in various ways, including sharp quantitative forms
[
5
]
and applications to different classes of domains and operators
[
6
]
, and even to graphs and discrete structures.
Friedman
[
9
]
introduced the concept of a ‘graph with boundary’ and formulated the Dirichlet and Neumann eigenvalue problems for graphs.

A graph with boundary
G=\left(V^{\circ}\cup\partial V,E^{\circ}\cup\partial E\right)
consists of a set of interior vertices
V^{\circ}
, boundary vertices
\partial V
, and boundary edges
\partial E
that connect interior vertices to boundary vertices, along with interior edges
E^{\circ}

[
8
,
9
]
.
Leydold
[
10
,
11
]
proved the Faber–Krahn type inequality for regular trees and gave a complete characterization of extremal trees. Pruss
[
13
]
also studied the Faber–Krahn inequality for regular trees and relative questions. Bıyıkoğlu and Leydold
[
3
]
demonstrated that the Faber–Krahn inequality also holds for the class of trees with the same degree sequence. The unique extremal tree has a spiral-like ordering, i.e., it is a ball approximation.
Zhang, Zhang, and Zhang
[
16
]
extended the previous results to the realm of unicyclic graphs. They established that the Faber-Krahn type inequality holds for unicyclic graphs with a specified graphic unicyclic degree sequence, subject to minor conditions.
Bıyıkoğlu and Leydold also proposed the following question in
[
3
]
.

Problem 1.1

(
[
3
]
)
.

Give a characterization of all graphs in a given class
\mathcal{C}
with the Faber–Krahn property, i.e., characterize those graphs in
\mathcal{C}
which have minimal first Dirichlet eigenvalue for a given
“volume”.

In this article, we explore Problem
1.1
in the context of hypergraphs.

The rest of this article is arranged as follows. In Section 2, we present some notations and state our main theorems. In Section 3, we discuss foundational concepts and provide several lemmas that support our main theorem. The proof of the main theorems will be given in Section 4.

2
Notations and main results

A hypergraph
\mathcal{G}
is defined as a pair
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)
, where
V
represents a set of vertices and
E
denotes a set of non-empty subsets of
V
, known as edges or hyperedges. A hypergraph is considered as
k
-uniform if every edge in
E
contains exactly
k
vertices.
Let
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)
be a hypergraph without multiple edges.
For a vertex
v\in V(\mathcal{G})
, the
degree
of
v
in
\mathcal{G}
is defined as the number of edges that include
v
, represented as
d(v)
. A vertex of degree one is called a
pendent vertex
. The list of degrees of vertices in hypergraph
\mathcal{G}
is called the
degree sequence
of
\mathcal{G}
.
For distinct vertices
u_{1},\ldots,u_{p+1}
and distinct edges
e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{p}
of
\mathcal{G}
, a vertex-edge alternative sequence
(u_{1},e_{1},u_{2},e_{2},\ldots,u_{p},e_{p},u_{p+1})
is a
path

P
in
\mathcal{G}
from
u_{1}
to
u_{p+1}
if
u_{i},u_{i+1}\in e_{i}
for
i=1,2,\ldots,p
. If
u_{1}=u_{p+1}
, then the path
P
is called a
cycle
.
A hypergraph
\mathcal{G}
is
connected
if there is a path between any two vertices
u
and
v
within
\mathcal{G}
. The
distance
between two vertices
u
and
v
, denoted as
dist(u,v)
, represents the shortest path length connecting
u
and
v
. Two vertices
u,v\in V
are considered
adjacent
if they share an edge, denoted by
u\sim v
. Let
N(u)
be the set of all vertices in
V
adjacent to
u\in V
.

A connected and acyclic hypergraph is called a
supertree
. This definition was initially proposed by Li, Shao, and Qi
[
12
]
. According to this definition, we have the following observation.

Observation 2.1
.

For each
k
-uniform supertree
\mathcal{G}
, any two edges share at most one common vertex, and
|E(\mathcal{G})|=\frac{|V(G)-1|}{k-1}
(Two 3-uniform supertrees have been shown in Figure
1
).

In this article, we define a supertree
\mathcal{G}=\left(V^{\circ}\cup\partial V,E^{\circ}\cup\partial E\right)
with
boundary
as a supertree with boundary vertex set
\partial V
consisting of all degree one vertices.

The
adjacency matrix

A_{\mathcal{G}}=(a_{ij})
of a hypergraph
\mathcal{G}
, introduced by Banerjee in
[
2
]
(a similar definition also was defined by Rodríguez in
[
14
]
) is defined as follows:

a_{ij}=\begin{cases}\sum\limits_{{e\in E}\atop{\{i,j\}\subseteq e}}\frac{1}{|e|-1},&\text{ if }i\sim j,\\
{\ \ 0},&\text{ otherwise, }\end{cases}

where we write
[n]=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
for the vertex set of
\mathcal{G}
.
For a vertex
i\in[n]
, it is straightforward to confirm that the degree
d(i)
equals the sum of entries in row
i
of the adjacent matrix
A_{\mathcal{G}}
, i.e.,
d(i)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{ij}
. The
Laplacian matrix
of the hypergraph
\mathcal{G}
is defined as
L_{\mathcal{G}}=D_{\mathcal{G}}-A_{\mathcal{G}}
, where
D_{\mathcal{G}}
is the diagonal matrix whose diagonal entries are the degrees of the vertices, specifically
D_{\mathcal{G}}=\operatorname{diag}\left(d(i)\right)_{i\in[n]}
.
From the definition of
D_{\mathcal{G}}
, we have

(L_{\mathcal{G}})_{ij}=\begin{cases}d(i),&\text{ if }i=j,\\
\sum\limits_{e\in E\atop\{i,j\}\subseteq e}\frac{-1}{|e|-1},&\text{ if }i\sim j,\\
0,&\text{ otherwise. }\end{cases}

A real number
\lambda
is defined as the
Dirichlet eigenvalue
of the hypergraph
\mathcal{G}
if there exists a non-zero real function
f
on
V(\mathcal{G})
such that:

\begin{cases}L_{\mathcal{G}}f(u)=\lambda f(u)&u\in V^{\circ},\\
f(u)=0&u\in\partial V.\end{cases}

The function
f
is referred to as an eigenfunction corresponding to
\lambda
.
The associate
Rayleigh quotient

\mathcal{R}_{L_{\mathcal{G}}}(f)
of a function
f:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is defined as

\mathcal{R}_{L_{\mathcal{G}}}(f)=\frac{\left\langle L_{\mathcal{G}}f,f\right\rangle}{\langle f,f\rangle}=\frac{\sum_{i\sim j}\sum_{e\in E\atop\{i,j\}\subseteq e}\frac{1}{|e|-1}(f(i)-f(j))^{2}}{\sum_{i\in V}f(i)^{2}}.

Specifically, if
\mathcal{G}
is a
k
-uniform supertree then any two vertices can be contained in at most one edge. Therefore, for a
k
-uniform supertree
\mathcal{G}
,

\mathcal{R}_{L_{\mathcal{G}}}(f)=\frac{\left\langle L_{\mathcal{G}}f,f\right\rangle}{\langle f,f\rangle}=\frac{\sum_{i\sim j}\frac{1}{k-1}(f(i)-f(j))^{2}}{\sum_{i\in V}f(i)^{2}}.

Let
\lambda(\mathcal{G})
be the first Dirichlet eigenvalue of
\mathcal{G}
. Then
we have

\lambda(\mathcal{G})=\min_{f\in S}\mathcal{R}_{L_{\mathcal{G}}}(f)=\min_{f\in S}\frac{\langle L_{\mathcal{G}}f,f\rangle}{\langle f,f\rangle},

where
S
is the set of all non-zero real functions on
V(\mathcal{G})
with the constraint
\left.f\right|_{\partial V}=0
. Furthermore, equality holds if and only if
f
is an eigenfunction.

Definition 1
.

A hypergraph with boundary has the Faber-Krahn property if it has minimal first Dirichlet eigenvalue among all hypergraphs with the same ’volume’ in a particular class.

In this article, the volume is referred to a given supertree degree sequence
\pi=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
, or a given interior vertex number and a minimum degree of interior vertices.
Let

\mathcal{T}_{\pi}=\{\mathcal{G}\,|\,\mathcal{G}\text{ is a $k$-uniform supertree with }\pi\text{ as its degree sequence}\},

\mathcal{T}^{n,n_{0}}=\{\mathcal{G}\,|\,\mathcal{G}\text{ is a $k$-uniform supertree with }|V|=n\text{ and }|V^{\circ}|=n_{0}\},

and

\mathcal{T}^{n,n_{0}}_{d}=\{\mathcal{G}\in\mathcal{T}^{n,n_{0}}\,|\,d(v)\geq d\text{ for all }v\in V^{\circ}\}.

For a supertree
\mathcal{G}
with root
v_{0}
, the
height

h(v)
of a vertex
v
is defined by
h(v)=\mbox{dist}(v,v_{0})
.
We call a vertex
u
a
child
of vertex
v
if
u
and
v
are adjacent in
\mathcal{G}
and
h(u)=h(v)+1
, and
v
is called the
parent
of
u
(the parent of
u
is unique in a supertree).

Definition 2

(SLO-ordering supertree)
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(V^{\circ}\cup\partial V,E^{\circ}\cup\partial E)
be a
k
-uniform supertree with root
v_{0}
. A well-ordering
\prec
of the vertices is called a spiral-like ordering (SLO-ordering for short) if the following holds for all vertices in
\mathcal{G}
:

(S1)

u\prec v
implies
h(u)\leq h(v)
.

(S2)

Suppose
u
and
v
are the parents of
u_{1}
and
v_{1}
, respectively. If
u\prec v
, then
u_{1}\prec v_{1}
.

(S3)

If
u\prec v
and
u\in\partial V
, then
v\in\partial V
.

(S4)

Suppose
u_{1}\prec u_{2}\prec\cdots\prec u_{k}
for an edge
e=\left\{u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{k}\right\}\in E(\mathcal{G})
, then there exists no vertex
v\in V(\mathcal{G})\backslash e
such that
u_{i}\prec v\prec u_{i+1}
for
2\leq i\leq k-1
.

(S5)

For any
u,v\in V^{\circ}
,
u\prec v
implies
d(u)\leq d(v)
.

Remark A:
The rule (S4) implies that for an edge
e=\left\{u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{k}\right\}\in E(\mathcal{G})
with
u_{1}\prec u_{2}\prec\cdots\prec u_{k}
,
the vertices in
e
except
u_{1}
are consecutive in this SLO-ordering.

Figure 1:
\mathcal{G}_{1}
is an SLO-ordering supertree, however,
\mathcal{G}_{2}
is not.

The two 3-uniform supertrees
\mathcal{G}_{1}
and
\mathcal{G}_{2}
as shown in Figure 1 have the same degree sequence
(3,3,3,1,1,1,1,1,1)
. The supertree
\mathcal{G}_{1}
has an SLO-ordering
v_{0}\prec v_{1}\prec v_{2}\prec\dots\prec v_{8}
. However, we cannot find an SLO-ordering in
\mathcal{G}_{2}
. Suppose to the contrary that
\mathcal{G}_{2}
has an SLO-ordering
v_{0}\prec v_{1}\prec v_{2}\prec\dots\prec v_{8}
. If we choose
c
as the root
v_{0}
, then vertices
a,b,d,e
must be ordered before vertex
g
according to (S1). However, this leads to a contradiction to (S3) (as boundary vertices must be ordered after interior vertices according to (S3)). The same discussion for the case
v_{0}=g
. Finally, if we choose
v_{0}=e
, then according to (S1) and (S3), we must have
\{v_{1},v_{2}\}=\{c,g\}
and
\{v_{3},v_{4}\}=\{d,f\}
. Without loss of generality, assume
v_{1}=c
and
v_{2}=g
. Then for edge
e_{0}=\{e,c,d\}
, we have
c\prec g\prec d
, a contradict to (S4) again.

We refer to supertrees that have SLO-ordering of their vertices as
SLO-supertrees
.
The following are our main results.

Theorem 2.2
.

A
k
-uniform supertree
\mathcal{G}
with degree sequence
\pi
has the Faber-Krahn property within the class
\mathcal{T}_{\pi}
if and only if
\mathcal{G}
has an SLO-ordering. Furthermore,
\mathcal{G}
is uniquely determined up to isomorphism.

Theorem 2.3
.

A
k
-uniform supertree
\mathcal{G}
exhibits the Faber-Krahn property within the class
\mathcal{T}_{d}^{n,n_{0}}
if and only if it is an SLO-supertree characterized by having at most one interior vertex with degree
d^{\prime}
greater than
d
, while all other interior vertices possess degree
d
. Furthermore, this supertree
\mathcal{G}
is unique up to isomorphism.

Remark B:
Theorem
2.2
demonstrates that the SLO-supertree has the Faber-Krahn property among all supertrees with a given degree sequence
\pi
. Additionally, among degree sequences that have a minimum degree
d
for interior vertices, the SLO-supertree with degree sequence
(d,\ldots,d,d^{\prime},1,\dots,1)
possesses the Faber-Krahn property.

The rest of this article is arranged as follows. In Section 3, we present some preliminaries and lemmas. The proofs of Theorems
2.2
and
2.3
will be provided in Sections 4 and 5, respectively.

3
Preliminaries and lemmas

First, we give the following property of the first Dirichlet eigenvalue of a supertree with boundary.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=\left(V^{\circ}\cup\partial V,E^{\circ}\cup\partial E\right)
be a
k
-uniform supertree with boundary. Then

(1)

The first Dirichlet eigenvalue
\lambda(G)
is a positive simple eigenvalue.

(2)

An eigenfunction
f
of the eigenvalue
\lambda(G)
is either positive or negative on all interior vertices of
G
.

Proof.

Let
f
be an eigenfunction corresponding to
\lambda(\mathcal{G})
. Then

\displaystyle\left<L_{\mathcal{G}}f,f\right>-\left<L_{\mathcal{G}}|f|,|f|\right>

(1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\{u_{i},u_{j}\}\subseteq e\atop e\in E}\frac{1}{k-1}(f(u_{i})-f(u_{j}))^{2}-\sum_{\{u_{i},u_{j}\}\subseteq e\atop e\in E}\frac{1}{k-1}(|f(u_{i})|-|f(u_{j})|)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\{u_{i},u_{j}\}\subseteq e\atop e\in E}\frac{2}{k-1}(|f(u_{i})||f(u_{j})|-f(u_{i})f(u_{j}))

\displaystyle\geq 0

Together with
\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{G}}(f)\leq\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{G}}(|f|)
, we have
\left<L_{\mathcal{G}}f,f\right>=\left<L_{\mathcal{G}}|f|,|f|\right>
. By (
1
), we have
f\geq 0
or
f\leq 0
on all interior vertices of
\mathcal{G}
.

Now suppose
f\geq 0
, and however,
f=0
on some interior vertices. Since
\mathcal{G}
is connected and
f\neq 0
, we can find two interior vertices
\{u,v\}\subseteq e\in E
with
f(u)=0
and
f(v)>0
. Let
\tilde{f}=f+\epsilon\chi_{u}
, where
\epsilon>0
is sufficiently small and
\chi_{u}
is the characteristic function of
u
.
Then

\displaystyle\left<L_{\mathcal{G}}f,f\right>-\left<L_{\mathcal{G}}\tilde{f},\tilde{f}\right>=

\displaystyle\sum_{\{u,v_{i}\}\subseteq e\atop e\in E}\frac{1}{k-1}((0-f(v_{i}))^{2}-(\epsilon-f(v_{i}))^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\{u,v_{i}\}\subseteq e\atop e\in E}\frac{2\epsilon}{k-1}(f(v_{i})-\epsilon)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{2\epsilon}{k-1}f(v)-\frac{2\epsilon}{k-1}(k-1)\epsilon

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\epsilon}{k-1}(f(v)-(k-1)\epsilon)>0,

a contradiction to the smallest of
f
.
Therefore,
f
is either positive or negative on all interior vertices of
\mathcal{G}
.

Since there are no two functions with a strict sign on interior vertices that can be orthogonal, it follows that the first eigenvalue has multiplicity one.
∎

According to Lemma
3.1
, the first Dirichlet eigenvalue of
\mathcal{G}
has multiplicity one. Therefore, there exists only one eigenfunction
f
of
\lambda(G)
that satisfies
f(v)>0
for
v\in V^{\circ},f(u)=0
for
u\in\partial V
and
\|f\|=\left(\sum_{u\in V}f(u)^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=1
. We call
f
the
first Dirichlet eigenfunction
of
\mathcal{G}
.

Definition 3

(Switching-operation)
.

For two edges
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
in a
k
-uniform hypergraph
\mathcal{G}
, let
U_{1}\subseteq e_{1}
and
V_{1}\subseteq e_{2}
with
|U_{1}|=|V_{1}|
. We define the switching operation
e_{1}\underset{\scriptscriptstyle V_{1}}{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle U_{1}}}{{\rightleftharpoons}}}e_{2}
as follows: replace
e_{1}
with
e^{\prime}_{1}=(e\backslash U_{1})\cup V_{1}
and replace
e_{2}
with
e_{2}^{\prime}=(e_{2}\backslash V_{1})\cup U_{1}
.

Let
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}=\mathcal{G}\left(e_{1}\underset{\scriptscriptstyle V_{1}}{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle U_{1}}}{{\rightleftharpoons}}}e_{2}\right)
be the hypergraph obtained from
\mathcal{G}
by the switching-operation
e_{1}\underset{\scriptscriptstyle V_{1}}{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle U_{1}}}{{\rightleftharpoons}}}e_{2}
, that is
V(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})=V(\mathcal{G})
and

E\left(\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\right)=(E(\mathcal{G})\backslash\{e_{1},e_{2}\})\cup\left\{e_{1}^{\prime},e_{2}^{\prime}\right\}.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(V^{\circ}\cup\partial V,E^{\circ}\cup\partial E)
be a
k
-uniform supertree and let
f
be the first Dirichlet eigenfunction of
\mathcal{G}
. Suppose
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
are two edges in
\mathcal{G}
. Let
U_{1}\subseteq e_{1}
and
V_{1}\subseteq e_{2}
with
|U_{1}|=|V_{1}|
. Let
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}=\mathcal{G}\left(e_{1}\underset{\scriptscriptstyle V_{1}}{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle U_{1}}}{{\rightleftharpoons}}}e_{2}\right)
.
If
\sum_{u\in U_{1}}f(u)\geq\sum_{v\in V_{1}}f(v)
and
\sum_{u\in e_{1}\setminus U_{1}}f(u)\leq\sum_{v\in e_{2}\setminus V_{1}}f(v)
, then
\lambda(\mathcal{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime})\leq\lambda(\mathcal{\mathcal{G}}).

Moreover, if one of the inequalities
\sum_{u\in U_{1}}f(u)\geq\sum_{v\in V_{1}}f(v)
and
\sum_{u\in e_{1}\setminus U_{1}}f(u)\leq\sum_{v\in e_{2}\setminus V_{1}}f(v)
is strict, then
\lambda(\mathcal{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime})<\lambda(\mathcal{\mathcal{G}})
.

Proof.

Let
e_{1}=\left\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}\right\}
and
e_{2}=\left\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\right\}
and let
U_{1}=\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{l}\}
and
V_{1}=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{l}\},(1\leq l\leq k)
.
Then
\sum_{i=1}^{l}f(v_{i})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{l}f(u_{i})
and
\sum_{j=l+1}^{k}f(v_{j})\geq\sum_{j=l+1}^{k}f(u_{j})
. Thus

\displaystyle\left<L_{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}f,f\right>-\left<L_{\mathcal{G}}f,f\right>

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i\leq l\atop l+1\leq j\leq k}\frac{1}{k-1}(f(u_{i})-f(v_{j}))^{2}+\sum_{1\leq i\leq l\atop l+1\leq j\leq k}\frac{1}{k-1}(f(v_{i})-f(u_{j}))^{2}

\displaystyle-\sum_{1\leq i\leq l\atop l+1\leq j\leq k}\frac{1}{k-1}(f(u_{i})-f(u_{j}))^{2}-\sum_{1\leq i\leq l\atop l+1\leq j\leq k}\frac{1}{k-1}(f(v_{i})-f(v_{j}))^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k-1}\sum_{1\leq i\leq l\atop l+1\leq j\leq k}2(f(v_{j})-f(u_{j}))(f(v_{i})-f(u_{i}))

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{k-1}\left(\sum_{1\leq i\leq l}f(v_{i})-\sum_{1\leq i\leq l}f(u_{i})\right)\left(\sum_{l+1\leq j\leq k}f(v_{j})-\sum_{l+1\leq j\leq k}f(u_{j})\right)

\displaystyle\leq 0.

Therefore,
\lambda(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})\leq\mathcal{R}_{L_{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}}(f)\leq\mathcal{R}_{L_{\mathcal{G}}}(f)=\lambda(\mathcal{G})
.
Moreover, if
\lambda(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})=\lambda(\mathcal{G})
, then
\mathcal{R}_{L_{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}}(f)=\mathcal{R}_{L_{\mathcal{G}}}(f)
, and thus
f
is the first eigenfunction of
\lambda(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})
.

If
\sum_{u\in U_{1}}f(u)>\sum_{v\in V_{1}}f(v)
, and we assume
\lambda(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})=\lambda(\mathcal{G})
, then
\mathcal{R}_{L_{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}}(f)=\mathcal{R}_{L_{\mathcal{G}}}(f)
and
f
is the first eigenfunction of
\lambda(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})
too. Thus

\displaystyle 0=\lambda(\mathcal{G})f(v_{k})-\lambda(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})f(v_{k})=L_{\mathcal{G}}f(v_{k})-L_{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}f(v_{k})=\frac{1}{k-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{l}f(v_{i})-\sum_{i=1}^{l}f(u_{i})\right)<0,

(2)

a contradiction. Therefore, we have
\lambda(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})<\lambda(\mathcal{G})
.

If
\sum_{u\in e_{1}\setminus U_{1}}f(u)<\sum_{v\in e_{2}\setminus V_{1}}f(v)
, and we assume
\lambda(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})=\lambda(\mathcal{G})
, then
\mathcal{R}_{L_{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}}(f)=\mathcal{R}_{L_{\mathcal{G}}}(f)
and
f
is also the first eigenfunction of
\lambda(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})
. Thus

\displaystyle 0=\lambda(\mathcal{G})f(v_{1})-\lambda(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})f(v_{1})=L_{\mathcal{G}}f(v_{1})-L_{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}f(v_{1})=\frac{1}{k-1}\left(\sum_{i=l+1}^{k}f(v_{i})-\sum_{i=l+1}^{k}f(u_{i})\right)>0,

(3)

a contradiction again. Therefore,
\lambda(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})<\lambda(\mathcal{G})
.

∎

Definition 4

(The shifting-operation)
.

For an edge
e
with
u\in e
and a vertex
v
in a
k
-uniform hypergraph
\mathcal{G}
, we define the shifting-operation
u\xrightarrow{e}v
as replacing
e
by
e^{\prime}=(e\backslash\{u\})\cup\{v\}
.

Let
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}=\mathcal{G}(u\xrightarrow{e}v)
be the graph resulting from the shifting-operation
u\xrightarrow{e}v
on
\mathcal{G}
. Then we have
V(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})=V(\mathcal{G})
and
E\left(\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\right)=(E(\mathcal{G})\backslash\{e\})\cup\{e^{\prime}\}.
For an edge set

denote the shifting-operations

\mathcal{G}

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(V^{\circ}\cup\partial V,E^{\circ}\cup\partial E)
be a
k
-uniform supertree. Suppose
F\subseteq E(\mathcal{G})
with
|F|=r
and every edge in
F
contains the vertex
u
, and let
v
be a vertex in
\mathcal{G}
. Let
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}=\mathcal{G}(u\xrightarrow{F}v)
. If
f(u)\geq f(v)\geq f(w)
for all
w\in V(F)\setminus\{u\}
, then
\lambda(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})\leq\lambda(\mathcal{G}).
Furthermore, if any of these inequalities are strict, then
\lambda(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})<\lambda(\mathcal{G}).

Proof.

Let
F=\{e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{r}\}
and
e_{i}=\left\{u,u_{i,1}\ldots,u_{i,k-1}\right\}
for
1\leq i\leq r
. Then

\displaystyle\left<L_{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}f,f\right>-\left<L_{\mathcal{G}}f,f\right>

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\frac{1}{k-1}(f(v)-f(u_{i,j}))^{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\frac{1}{k-1}(f(u)-f(u_{i,j}))^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k-1}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}(f(v)-f(u))\left(f(v)+f(u)-2f(u_{i,j})\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq 0.

Therefore,
\lambda(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})\leq\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}(f)\leq\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{G}}(f)=\lambda(\mathcal{G})
.
Moreover, if
\lambda(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})=\lambda(\mathcal{G})
then
\mathcal{R}_{L_{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}}(f)=\mathcal{R}_{L_{\mathcal{G}}}(f)
and thus
f
is an eigenfunction of
\lambda(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})
.

If
f(v)>f(u_{i,j})
for some
1\leq i\leq r
,
1\leq j\leq k-1
and
\lambda(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})=\lambda(\mathcal{G})
, then we have

\displaystyle 0=\lambda(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})f(v)-\lambda(\mathcal{G})f(v)=L_{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}f(v)-L_{\mathcal{G}}f(v)=\frac{1}{k-1}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}(f(v)-f(u_{i,j}))>0,

a contradiction.

If
f(u)>f(u_{i,j})
for some
1\leq i\leq r
,
1\leq j\leq k-1
and
\lambda(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})=\lambda(\mathcal{G})
, then we have

\displaystyle 0=\lambda(\mathcal{G})f(u)-\lambda(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})f(u)=L_{\mathcal{G}}f(u)-L_{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}f(u)=\frac{1}{k-1}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}(f(u)-f(u_{i,j}))>0,

a contradiction.

Now suppose
f(u)>f(v)
. If
\lambda(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})=\lambda(\mathcal{G})
, then

\displaystyle 0=\lambda(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})f(u_{1,1})-\lambda(\mathcal{G})f(u_{1,1})=L_{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}f(u_{1,1})-L_{\mathcal{G}}f(u_{1,1})=\frac{1}{k-1}(f(u)-f(v))>0,

a contradiction again.

∎

\pi=(d_{0},d_{1},\ldots,d_{n_{0}-1},\ldots,d_{n-1})

\pi^{\prime}=(d_{0}^{\prime},d_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,d_{n_{0}-1}^{\prime},\ldots,d_{n-1}^{\prime})
be two degree sequences of some supertrees with the same number of vertices
n
and interior vertices
n_{0}
. If
\pi^{\prime}\triangleleft\pi
and
\pi\not=\pi^{\prime}
, then
\lambda(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})<\lambda(\mathcal{G})
.

Finally, we give the proof of Theorem
2.3
.

Proof of Theorem
2.3
:

Let
\mathcal{G}=(V^{\circ}\cup\partial V,E^{\circ}\cup\partial E)
be a
k
-uniform supertree with Faber-Krahn property in
\mathcal{T}_{d}^{n,n_{0}}
.
Let
\pi=\left(d_{0},d_{1},\ldots,d_{n_{0}-1},1,\ldots,1\right)
be the degree sequence of
\mathcal{G}
. Then
d\leqslant d_{0}\leqslant d_{1}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant d_{n_{0}-1}
. Let
\pi^{\prime}=\left(d,\ldots,d,d^{\prime},1,\ldots,1\right)
, where
d^{\prime}=\sum_{i=0}^{n_{0}-1}d_{i}-(n_{0}-1)d
.
If
\pi\neq\pi^{\prime}
, then
\pi^{\prime}\triangleleft\pi
. Let
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
be a supertree with Faber-Krahn property in
\mathcal{T}_{\pi^{\prime}}
. By Corollary
5.4
,
\lambda(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})<\lambda\left(\mathcal{G}\right)
, a contradiction to the Faber-Krahn property of
\mathcal{G}
. The necessity is proved. The sufficiency follows from the uniqueness of the SLO-supertree in
\mathcal{T}_{\pi}
.
∎

6
Remarks and discussions

In this article, we introduce a spiral-like ordering (SLO-ordering) of vertices for supertrees and prove that the SLO-supertree has the Faber-Krahn property among all supertrees with a given degree sequence. Furthermore, among degree sequences that have a minimum degree
d
for interior vertices, the SLO-supertree with degree sequence
(d,\ldots,d,d^{\prime},1,\dots,1)
possesses the Faber-Krahn property.
These results answer Problem
1.1
for supertrees with boundary. It is very interesting to explore this problem for other families of hypergraphs, for example, unicyclic hypergraphs, under other given ”volume”?

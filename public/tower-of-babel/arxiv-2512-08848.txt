Monadic reconstruction of unitary Drinfeld centers and Factorization Homology
Lucas Hataishi

Abstract
. We prove that the unitary Drinfeld center of a unitary tensor category is equivalente to the category of unitary bimodules for the canonical W ∗ -algebra object, generalizing Müger’s result to the non-fusion case. This is then used to express factorization homology in terms of C ∗ -algebraic extensions of symmetric enveloping algebras and actions of Drinfeld dobules of compact quantum groups.

1.
Introduction

The Drinfeld center construction, which takes a tensor category and produces a braided tensor category, appears naturally in a variety of contexts in quantum algebra.

•

Quantum groups: the Drinfeld center of the representation category of a quantum group is equivalent to the category of representations of its Drinfeld double (
[
9
]
,
[
22
]
).

•

Subfactors: Drinfeld centers appear in relation to the symmetric enveloping inclusions associated to subfactors (
[
28
,
30
,
10
,
21
,
15
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
27
]
).

•

Condensed-matter physics: Drinfeld centers describe topological charges in Levin-Wen models (
[
20
]
).

•

Topological Quantum Field Theory (TQFT): Reshetikhin-Turaev construction from modular tensor categories (
[
31
]
), of which Drinfeld centers of fusion categories are examples.

Specially for applications in mathematical-physics, the literature concentrates mostly on fusion categories, finite non-semisimple tensor categories, either rigid or of Grothendieck-Verdier type. However, the theory of compact quantum groups (
[
26
]
) and the theory of subfactors (
[
18
,
14
,
17
,
12
]
)gives rich sources of tensor categories with unitary structure and infinite isomorphism classes of simple objects. They are also rigid, which together with unitarity implies semi-simplicity, and have simple units. Such categories are called
unitary tensor categories
(UTCs).

The Drinfeld center construction is subtle in the unitary and non-finite context. By naively taking the category
\mathcal{Z}\mathcal{C}
of unitary half-braidings of a UTC
\mathcal{C}
, one often obtains a very small category. To get an interesting category, first we need to consider unitary half-braidings in the
unitary ind-completion

\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})
of
\mathcal{C}
. We denote it by
\mathcal{Z}\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})
, and call it the
unitary Drinfeld center
of
\mathcal{C}
, see
[
27
]
.
\mathcal{Z}\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})
is then a C
∗
-tensor category with a unitary braiding. The main motivation for this paper is to understand a 2-dimensional fully extended TQFT associated to
\mathcal{Z}\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})
, constructed via
factorization homology
with values in C
∗
-categories.

One particular instance, and for us the case of major interest, is when
\mathcal{C}
is the category
{\sf Rep_{fd}}(K_{q})
of unitary representations of the Woronowicz-Drinfeld-Jimbo deformation of a semisimple simply connected Lie group
K
. In this case, writing
\mathrm{Hilb}({\sf Rep_{fd}}(K_{q}))=\Rep(K_{q})
for simplicity,
\mathcal{Z}\Rep(K_{q})
coincides with the category
\Rep(DK_{q})
of unitary representations of the Drinfeld double of
K_{q}
. As advocated in
[
35
]
, the
DK_{q}
gives a quantization of the complexification
K_{\mathbb{C}}
of
K
. We expect factorization homology with coefficients in
\Rep(DK_{q})
to give a quantization of the algebra of continuous functions on the
K_{\mathbb{C}}
-spaces

\mathcal{R}_{K_{\mathbb{C}}}(\Sigma):=\Hom(\pi_{1}(\Sigma),K_{\mathbb{C}})\ ,

where
\Sigma
is a compact surface. Geometrically,
\mathcal{R}_{K_{\mathbb{C}}}(\Sigma)
is the moduli space of flat principal
K_{\mathbb{C}}
-bundles on
\Sigma
, trivialized on a point, and the action of
K_{\mathbb{C}}
corresponds to change of trivialization. Though we can not yet prove this quantization result, we show here that factorization homology can be expressed in terms of
DK_{q}
-C
∗
-algebras (Theorem
B
).

In order to compute factorization homology, one has to understand
\mathcal{Z}\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})
-module C
∗
-categories, a priori a difficult problem due to the lack of both rigidity, semisimplicity and finiteness. The main technical result of this paper (Theorem
A
) allows one to pass from module C
∗
-categories over
\mathcal{Z}\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})
to bimodule C
∗
-categories over
\mathcal{C}
, enabling the application of the results in
[
13
]
. In the case
\mathcal{C}={\sf Rep_{fd}}(K_{q})
this is how one obtains the
DK_{q}
-C
∗
-algebras, and for generic
\mathcal{C}
it allows to express factorization homology in terms of extensions of Popa’s symmetric enveloping algebras (Theorem
C
). We expect that these extensions describe the algebras of observables in the 2-dimensional TQFT associated with factorization homology.

Lets us now describe in more detail the content of the paper. In passing from
\mathcal{C}
to
\mathcal{Z}\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})
, semi-simplicity, and therefore rigidity, is lost, and the result is an immense C
∗
-category. This is well illustrated in the case of unitary representations of compact quantum groups. It is true that the unitary Drinfeld center is the category of unitary representations of the Drinfled double, which is now only a
locally
compact quantum group. Even classically, it is often very difficult to understand the category of unitary representations of a generic locally compact group.

In the fusion case, Müger’s works
[
24
,
25
]
assert that the Drinfeld center of a fusion category

\mathcal{C}
is related to
\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C}
through a Morita context, where
\mathcal{C}^{mp}
is the category
\mathcal{C}
with opposition monoidal structure, and
\boxtimes
is the tensor product of linear categories. Translated to the monadic language of
[
5
,
4
,
6
,
7
]
, this tantamonts to the existence of an algebra object
\mathcal{S}
in
\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C}
such that

•

the category
\RMod(\mathcal{S})
of right
\mathcal{S}
-modules is equivalent to
\mathcal{C}
,

•

the category
\LMod(\mathcal{S})
of left
\mathcal{S}
-modules is equivalent to
\mathcal{C}^{mp}
,

•

the category
\Bim(\mathcal{S})
is
\mathcal{S}
-bimodules is equivalent to the Drinfeld center
\mathcal{Z}\mathcal{C}
,

•

\RMod(\mathcal{S})\underset{\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C}}{\boxtimes}\LMod(\mathcal{S})\simeq\mathcal{C}\underset{\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C}}{\boxtimes}\mathcal{C}^{mp}\simeq\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
.

A consequence of this is that the categories of modules for the Drinfeld center is equivalent to the category of modules for
\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C}
. Also, there is a canonical choice for
\mathcal{S}
up to isomorphism: if
\Irr(\mathcal{C})
is a choice of representatives for the isomorphism classes of simple objects in
\mathcal{C}
,
\mathcal{S}
can be taken to be

\mathcal{S}=\bigoplus_{a\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}\bar{a}\boxtimes a\ ,

where
\bar{a}
is the dual of
a
.

The basic idea of this paper is to investigate to which extent the above picture makes sense in the unitary and non-finite context. The inifinite version of the canonical algebra
\mathcal{S}
has already appeared both in quantum groups, as the implementation of the quantum-duality principle (
[
32
,
33
]
), and in subfactors as part of the standard invariant of the symmetric enveloping inclusions (
[
27
,
16
]
). Given a UTC
\mathcal{C}
, as explained in
[
16
]
,
\mathcal{S}
is a W
∗
, or von Neumann, algebra object in the algebraic ind-completion of
\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C}
. The following is the main result in this paper.

Theorem A

(Corollary
5.23
)
.

The unitary Drinfeld center
\mathcal{Z}\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})
of a unitary tensor category is equivalent to the category
\Bim_{\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C})}(\mathcal{S})
of unitary bimodules for the canonical W
∗
-algebra object
\mathcal{S}
.

Unitarily braided C
∗
-tensor categories can be interpreted topologically. Consider the category
\Disk_{2}
which has disjoint unions
D^{\sqcup k}
of two dimensional disks as objects, and smooth embeddings
D^{\sqcup k}\to D^{\sqcup l}
as morphisms. Disjoint union gives to
\Disk_{2}
the structure of a symmetric monoidal category. Considering isotopies between embeddings as 2-morphisms,
\Disk_{2}
becomes a symmetric monoidal (2,1)-category, i.e., a 2-category for which all 2-morphisms are invertible. Then a unitarily braided C
∗
-tensor category
\mathcal{Z}
determines a symmetric monoidal functor
\Disk_{2}\to\CCat
, where
\CCat
is the symmetric monoidal (2,1)-category of C
∗
-tensor categories,
*
-functors and unitary natural isomorphisms, equipped with the maximal tensor product
\boxtimes
of C
∗
-categories. The category
\mathcal{Z}
is recovered from the functor as the value of the disk.

Thanks to
[
8
,
1
]
, it follows that the functor
\Disk_{2}\to\CCat
defined by a C
∗
-tensor category
\mathcal{Z}
with a unitary braiding extends to a symmetric monoidal functor

\int_{(-)}\mathcal{Z}:\Surf\to\CCat

from the category of compact surfaces, seen as symmetric monoidal (2,1)-category similarly to
\Disk_{2}
. This functor is called
factorization homology
with coefficients in
\mathcal{Z}
(
[
1
]
), and satisfies an excision property in terms of collar-gluing of surfaces.

We use Theorem
A
to study
\int_{(-)}\mathcal{Z}\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})
, factorization homology with coefficients in the unitary Drinfeld center of a UTC
\mathcal{C}
. FH naturally leads to the study of pointed module C
∗
-categories for unitary Drinfeld centers (a pointed module category is a module category with a distinguished object). Using Theorem
A
, the following can be proven.

Theorem B

(Proposition
6.2
)
.

There exists a forgetful functor from

\{\text{pointed $\mathcal{Z}\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})$-bimodule C${}^{*}$-tensor categories}\}\to\{\text{ centrally pointed $\mathcal{C}$-bimodule C${}^{*}$-categories}\}\ ,

given at the level of objects by

(\mathcal{M},m)\mapsto\left(\mathcal{M}\underset{\mathcal{Z}\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})}{\boxtimes}\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C}),m\boxtimes{\mathds{1}}\right)\ .

A pointed bimodule C
∗
-category
(\mathcal{M},m)
for
\mathcal{C}
is called
centrally pointed
if on the distinguished object
m
the left and right
\mathcal{C}
actions are unitarily equivalent. It was show in
[
13
]
that, when
\mathcal{C}
is the category
\Rep\mathbb{K}
of unitary representations of a compact quantum group, such bimodules correspond to continuous action of the Drinfeld double
D\mathbb{K}
on unital C
∗
-algebras, via a generalization of the Tannaka-Krein duality for quantum group actions. In particular, factorization homology for unitary Drinfeld center gives rise to continuous
D\mathbb{K}
-C
∗
-algebras. More precisely, we have the following.

Theorem C

(Corollary
7.11
)
.

Let
\Sigma
be a framed surface with non empty boundary. Given a compact quantum group
\mathbb{K}
, there is a unital C
∗
-algebra
B_{\Sigma}
, a continuous actions of
D\mathbb{K}^{\times n}
on
B_{\Sigma}
, where
n
is the number of boundary components of
\Sigma
, such that

\int_{\Sigma}\Rep(D\mathbb{K})\underset{\Rep(D\mathbb{K}^{\times n})}{\boxtimes}\Rep(\mathbb{K}^{\times n})\simeq\mathrm{Hilb}^{\mathbb{K}^{\times n}}_{B_{\Sigma}}\ ,

as a
\Rep(\mathbb{K}^{\times n})
-bimodule C
∗
-tensor category, where
\mathrm{Hilb}^{\mathbb{K}^{\times n}}_{B_{\Sigma}}
is the category of
\mathbb{K}^{\times n}
-equivariant Hilbert C
∗
-modules over
B_{\Sigma}
. The correspondence
\Sigma\mapsto B_{\Sigma}
is functorial with respect to boundary preserving framed embeddings.

In future works, factorization homology with coefficients in
\Rep(D\mathbb{K})
will be interpreted as topological quantum gauge theory.

The
D\mathbb{K}
-C
∗
-algebras of Theorem
C
are obtained from the fiber
\Rep\mathbb{K}\to\mathrm{Hilb}
of the unitary representation category of
\mathbb{K}
. There are UTCs that do not admit fiber functor, and therefore are not equivalent to the unitary representation category of a compact quantum group, e.g. Fibonacci, Ising and Haagerup unitary fusion categories. But, given a UTC
\mathcal{C}
, there is always a unital C
∗
-algebra
A
with trivial center and a fully-faithful unitary tensor functor
F:\mathcal{C}\to\Corr(A)
to the category of Hilbert C
∗
-correspondences over
A
(
[
11
,
19
]
).

Building on the previous works (
[
17
,
29
]
), a generalization of the Tannaka-Krein duality for quantum group actions is proven in
[
12
]
, functorially associating to a fully faithful unitary tensor functor
F:\mathcal{C}\to\Corr(A)
and a pointed module C
∗
-category
(\mathcal{M},m)
for
\mathcal{C}
an extension
A\subset B_{(\mathcal{M},m)}
of C
∗
-algebras. A pointed module C
∗
-category over
\mathcal{C}
is the same as a C
∗
-algebra object in the algebraic ind-completion of
\mathcal{C}
(
[
16
]
), and thus the generalized Tannaka-Krein duality theorem is a correspondence associating concrete operator algebraic extensions to C
∗
-algebra objects.

How should the generalized Tannaka-Krein duality of
[
12
]
work for centrally pointed module C
∗
-categories, and how can an analogue of Theorem
C
be stated when, instead of a fiber functor
\Rep\mathbb{K}\to\mathrm{Hilb}
, a fully faithful functor
F:\mathcal{C}\to\Corr(A)
is given? The answer involves the symmetric enveloping inclusion. Indeed, the functor
F
induces a fully faithful unitary tensor functor
\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C}\to\Corr(A^{\text{op}}\otimes A)
. Taking the canonical algebra
\mathcal{S}
as a C
∗
-algebra object in the algebraic ind-completion of
\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C}
, there is an associated extension
A^{\text{op}}\otimes A\subset B_{F}
, and
B_{F}
is called the
symmetric enveloping algebra
associated to
F
. The following is then proved.

Theorem D

(Theorem
6.6
)
.

Suppose
\mathcal{C}
is a unitary tensor category and
F:\mathcal{C}\to\Corr(A)
is a fully faithful unitary tensor functor. Then there exists a functor

(\mathcal{M},m)\mapsto B_{(\mathcal{M},m)}\supset B_{F}

from the category of pointed module C
∗
-categories over the unitary Drinfeld center of
\mathcal{C}
to the category of extensions of the symmetric enveloping algebra
B_{F}
associated to
F
.

Applying Theorem
D
to factorization homology gives the following result.

Theorem E

(Corollary
7.9
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a unitary tensor category and
F:\mathcal{C}\to\Corr(A)
a fully faithful unitary tensor functor. Given a framed surface
\Sigma
with non empty boundary, there is an associated extension
B_{F}^{\otimes n}\subset B_{\Sigma}
of C
∗
-algebras, where
n
is the number of boundary components of
\Sigma
, and a fully-faithful unitary functor

\int_{\Sigma}\mathcal{Z}\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})\underset{\mathcal{Z}\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})^{\boxtimes n}}{\boxtimes}\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})\to\Corr^{l}(B_{\Sigma},B_{F}^{\otimes n})\ ,

of
\mathcal{C}^{\boxtimes n}
-bimodule C
∗
-categories, where the target is the category of left Hilbert
(B_{\Sigma},B_{F}^{\otimes n})
-C
∗
-correspondences. This construction is functorial in
\Sigma
. Moreover, the modular group
\Gamma(\Sigma)
of
\Sigma
relative to the boundary acts on
B_{\Sigma}
by
*
-automorphims, and
B_{F}^{\otimes n}
is globally fixed under this action.

\mathcal{L}(V_{1},V_{2})

H_{1}

\mathbb{B}(H_{1},H_{2})
.

For notational simplicity, when
a

2.2.
Unitary Tensor Categories

In this preliminary section the basics of the theory of unitary tensor categories is spelled, mostly in order to fix the notation that is used througout the paper. For an in-depth study, the reader is referred to
[
16
,
26
]
. As a motivation, keep in mind that unitary tensor category is the axiomatization of the structure of the category of finite dimensional unitary representations of a compact quantum group.

Definition 2.2
.

A C
∗
-category
\mathcal{C}
consists of

•

A category
\mathcal{C}
enriched over the category of Banach spaces and contractions;

•

An involutive functor
*:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}^{\text{op}}
which on the objects act as the identity. In other words, it is a natural collection of anti-linear maps
\mathcal{C}(a,b)\to\mathcal{C}(b,a)
which squares to the identity,

such that

•

\mathcal{C}
admits direct sums;

•

For each
a\in\mathcal{C}
, the endomorphism algebra
\mathcal{C}(a)
, with the given Banach space structure and the involution
*:\mathcal{C}(a)\to\mathcal{C}(a)
, is a C
∗
-algebra.

Remark 2.3
.

The existence of direct sums in the defintion of C
∗
-categories is a simplifying assumption.

If
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
are C
∗
-categories, a functor
F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
is said to be
unitary
if
F\circ*=*\circ F
. Because endomorphism algebras are C
∗
-algebras, this automatically implies continuity of
F
on morphism spaces with respect to the given Banach space structures.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a C
∗
-category. A tensor structure
\otimes:\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}
is a
*
-tensor structure
if, given
a,a^{\prime},b,b^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}
, morphisms
f:a\to a^{\prime}
and
g:b\to b^{\prime}
,

(f\otimes g)^{*}=f^{*}\otimes g^{*}:a^{\prime}\otimes b^{\prime}\to a\otimes b\ .

A C
∗
-category equipped with a
*
-tensor structure is called a
C
∗
-tensor category
. A rigid C
∗
-tensor category is a C
∗
-tensor category which is rigid as a tensor category.

The following notation is used for the duality functor in rigid tensor categories. On objects, we write
a\mapsto\bar{a}
. On morphisms, we write
(f:a\to b)\mapsto(f^{\vee}:\bar{b}\to\bar{a})
.

Unit objects in tensor categories will generically be denoted by
{\mathds{1}}
.

Definition 2.5
.

An object
a
in C
∗
-category
\mathcal{C}
is
simple
if
\End(a)=\mathbb{C}\cdot\mathrm{id}_{a}
. A rigid C
∗
-tensor category is said to be a
unitary tensor category
of its unit
{\mathds{1}}
is simple.

Unitary tensor categories are automatically semisimple and spherical. To simplify the notation, we work only with strictfied unitary tensor categories: the tensor structure is strict and the double dual functor is the identity,
(\bar{\bar{(-)}},(-)^{\vee\vee})=\mathrm{id}
. If a given unitary tensor category is not strictfied on the nose, we replace it by its strictification without mentioning.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a unitary tensor category. The
algebraic ind-completion

\Vect(\mathcal{C})
of
\mathcal{C}
is the category of functors
\mathcal{C}^{\text{op}}\to\Vect
. Morphisms in this category are linear natural transformations.

To specify an object
V\in\Vect(\mathcal{C})
, it suffices, up to isomorphism, to specify its values, or
fibers
, at the objects in
\Irr(\mathcal{C})
, i.e., a family
\{V(a)\}_{a\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}
of vector spaces indexed by
\Irr(\mathcal{C})
. Another useful perspective is that of understanding the object
V=\{V_{a}\}_{a\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}\in\Vect(\mathcal{C})
as a formal direct sum

V=\bigoplus_{a\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}X\odot V(a)

of objects in
\mathcal{C}
, where the irreducible object
a
appears
\dim V(a)
times as a direct summand. We say that
V(a)
is the
multiplicity space
of
a
in
V
. Moreover, given
V_{1}=\{V_{1}(a)\}_{a}
and
V_{2}=\{V_{2}(a)\}_{X}
in
\Vect(\mathcal{C})
, it follows that

\Vect(\mathcal{C})(V_{1},V_{2})\simeq\prod_{a\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}\mathcal{L}(V_{1}(a),V_{2}(a)),

where
\mathcal{L}(\cdot,\cdot)
denotes the space of linear maps.

The category
\mathcal{C}
embeds into
\Vect(\mathcal{C})
as a linear full subcategory, and the tensor structure
\otimes
in
\mathcal{C}
can be extended to a tensor structure
\odot
on
\Vect(\mathcal{C})
: for
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
as above,

(V_{1}\odot V_{2})(a):=\bigoplus_{b,c\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}\mathcal{C}(a,b\otimes c)\odot V_{1}(b)\odot V_{2}(c).

Definition 2.7
.

The
unitary ind-completion
of a unitary tensor category
\mathcal{C}
is the category
\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})
with

•

objects: functors
\mathcal{C}^{\text{op}}\to\mathrm{Hilb}
;

•

morphisms: uniformly bounded linear natural transformations. That is, given
H_{1},H_{2}\in\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})
, a morphism
\eta\in\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})(H_{1},H_{2})
is a natural family
\{\eta_{a}\in\mathbb{B}(H_{1}(a),H_{2}(a))\}_{a\in\mathcal{C}}
such that

\underset{a\in\mathcal{C}}{\sup}\|\eta_{a}\|<\infty.

An object
H\in\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})
is determined by a family
\{H(a)\}_{a\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}
of Hilbert spaces. We have

\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})(H_{1},H_{2})\simeq\prod_{a\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}^{\ell^{\infty}}\mathbb{B}(H_{1}(a),H_{2}(a)).

The category
\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})
is a C
∗
-category (actually a W
∗
-category), and
\mathcal{C}
is a full C
∗
-subcategory of it. The tensor structure of
\mathcal{C}
extends to
\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})
; seeing
\mathcal{C}(a,b\otimes c)
as a Hilbert space,

(H_{1}\otimes H_{2})(a)=\bigoplus_{b,c\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}^{\ell^{2}}\mathcal{C}(a,b\otimes c)\otimes H_{1}(b)\otimes H_{2}(c),

where on the
(b,c)
-direct summand the inner-product is scaled by a factor of
(d_{b}d_{c})^{-1}
. The purpose of this renormalization is to have a clean interpretation of the induced inner product by means of graphical calculus (see
[
16
]
, Section 2).

Since
\Vect(\mathcal{C})
is a tensor category, we can consider algebra objects in it. An algebra object in
\Vect(\mathcal{C})
consists of a functor
\mathbb{A}:\mathcal{C}^{\text{op}}\to\Vect
together with a lax-natural transformation

\mu^{\mathbb{A}}=\{\mu^{\mathbb{A}}_{a,b}:\mathbb{A}(a)\odot\mathbb{A}(b)\to\mathbb{A}(a\otimes b)\ |\ a,b\in\mathcal{C}\},

satisfying coherence with respect to the pentagon diagram associated to the orderings of triple tensor products.

Definition 2.8
.

A
*
-structure on an algebra object
\mathbb{A}
is a conjugate linear natural transformation

j^{\mathbb{A}}=\{j^{\mathbb{A}}_{a}:\mathbb{A}(a)\to\mathbb{A}(\bar{a})\}_{a\in\mathcal{C}},

satisfying

•

involution:
j^{\mathbb{A}}_{\bar{a}}\circ j^{\mathbb{A}}_{X}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{A}(X)}
, under the identification
\bar{\bar{a}}=a
;

•

unitality:
j^{\mathbb{A}}_{1_{\mathcal{C}}}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{A}(1_{\mathcal{C}})}
, under the identification
\bar{{\mathds{1}}}\simeq{\mathds{1}}
;

•

monoidality: the diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{A}(a)\odot\mathbb{A}(b)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{A}(\bar{a})\odot\mathbb{A}(\bar{b})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{A}(a\otimes b)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{A}(\bar{a}\otimes\bar{b})\simeq\mathbb{A}(\overline{b\otimes a})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j^{\mathbb{A}}_{a}\odot j^{\mathbb{A}}_{b}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu^{\mathbb{A}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu^{\mathbb{A}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j^{\mathbb{A}}_{a\otimes b}}

commutes.

If
\mathbb{A}=(\mathbb{A},\mu^{\mathbb{A}})
is an algebra object in
\Vect(\mathcal{C})
, then the vector spaces
\mathbb{A}(\bar{a}\boxtimes a)
become algebras, with multiplication given by

\mathbb{A}(\bar{a}\otimes a)\odot\mathbb{A}(\bar{a}\otimes a)\overset{\mu^{\mathbb{A}}}{\to}\mathbb{A}(\bar{a}\otimes a\otimes\bar{a}\otimes a)\overset{\mathbb{A}(\mathrm{id}_{\bar{a}}{\otimes\bar{R}_{a}^{*}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{a})}}{\to}\mathbb{A}(\bar{a}\boxtimes a).

is a
*
-structure on
\mathbb{A}

\mathbb{A}(\bar{a}\otimes a)
becomes
*
-algebra, with involution given by
j^{\mathbb{A}}_{\bar{a\otimes a}}
.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
\mathbb{A}=(\mathbb{A},\mu^{\mathbb{A}},j^{\mathbb{A}})

, the

-algebra

Definition 2.11
.

,

,

, summarized in

3.1.
Centralizer endofunctors

Given
X\in\Vect(\mathcal{C})
, consider the functor
C_{X}:\mathcal{C}^{\text{op}}\times\mathcal{C}\to\Vect(\mathcal{C})
given by

C_{X}(U^{\text{op}},V):=\bar{U}\odot X\odot V\ .

If
(f,g):(U,V)\to(U^{\prime},V^{\prime})
is a morphism in
\mathcal{C}^{\text{op}}\times\mathcal{C}
, then
C_{X}(f,g)=f^{\vee}\odot\mathrm{id}_{X}\odot g
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X\in\Vect(\mathcal{C})
and
U\in\mathcal{C}
. Then there is a canonical natural isomorphism

X(U\odot(-))\simeq\bar{U}\odot X

in
\Vect(\mathcal{C})
.

Definition 3.2
.

Define an endofunctor
Z_{\Vect}\in\End(\Vect(\mathcal{C}))
as follows. For
X\in\Vect(\mathcal{C})
,
Z_{\Vect}(X)
is the object in
\Vect(\mathcal{C})
defined by

Z_{\Vect}(X)=\bigoplus_{a\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}\bar{a}\odot X\odot a\ .

If
f\in\Vect(\mathcal{C})(X,Y)
, then
Z_{\Vect}(f)=\bigoplus_{a\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}\mathrm{id}_{\bar{a}}\odot f\odot\mathrm{id}_{{a}}
. We call
Z_{\Vect}
the centralizer endofunctor of
\Vect(\mathcal{C})
.
Lemma
3.1
gives an identification,

\mathcal{C}\ni U\mapsto Z_{\Vect}(X)(U)\simeq\bigoplus_{a\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}X(a\odot U\odot\bar{a}).

Definition 3.3
.

For
X\in\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})
, let
Z_{\mathrm{Hilb}}(X)\in\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})
be

Z_{\mathrm{Hilb}}(X)=\bigoplus_{a\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}^{\ell^{2}}\bar{a}\otimes X\otimes a

Given
f\in\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})(X,Y)
, let
Z_{\mathrm{Hilb}}(f)=\bigoplus_{a\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}\mathrm{id}_{\bar{a}}\otimes f\otimes\mathrm{id}_{a}
. Then
Z_{\mathrm{Hilb}}\in\End(\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C}))
is a unitary functor, and we call it the centralizer functor of
\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})
. Lemma
3.1
gives an identification

\mathcal{C}\ni U\mapsto Z_{\mathrm{Hilb}}(X)(U)\simeq\bigoplus_{a\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}^{\ell^{2}}X(a\otimes U\otimes\bar{a}).

Definition 3.4
.

Let
F:\mathcal{C}^{\text{op}}\times\mathcal{C}\to\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})
be a bilinear unitary functor, and let
K\in\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})
. A
dinatural transformation
\theta:F\to K
in
\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})
is a family

\theta=\{\theta_{U}\in\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})(F(U^{\text{op}},U),K)\}_{U\in\mathcal{C}}

such that

•

for every
f\in\mathcal{C}(U_{1},U_{2})
, the following diagram commutes:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(U_{2}^{\text{op}},U_{1})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(U_{1}^{\text{op}},U_{1})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(U_{2}^{\text{op}},U_{2})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(\mathrm{id}_{U_{2}^{\text{op}}},f)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(f^{\text{op}},\mathrm{id}_{U_{1}})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\theta_{U_{1}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\theta_{U_{2}}}

•

\sup_{U\in\mathcal{C}}\|\theta_{U}\|<\infty
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
X\in\Vect(\mathcal{C})
. For each
U\in\mathcal{C}
and
a\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})
,

\rho_{X,U}:=\sum_{a\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}\sum_{\omega\in\ONB(a,U)}((\omega^{\vee})^{*}\odot\mathrm{id}_{X}\odot\omega^{*})

defines a morphism
\rho_{X,U}\in\Vect(\mathcal{C})(\bar{U}\odot X\odot U,Z_{\Vect}(X))
. Moreover,
\rho_{X}:=\{\rho_{X,U}\}_{U\in\mathcal{C}}
is a dinatural transformation
C_{X}\to Z_{\Vect}(X)
in
\Vect(\mathcal{C})
. If
X\in\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})
, the analogous formula with the algebraic tensor product
\odot
replaced by the Hilbert space tensor product
\otimes
defines a morphism in
\rho_{X,U}\in\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})(\bar{U}\otimes X\otimes U,Z_{\mathrm{Hilb}}(X))
. In this case,
\rho_{X}
is a dinatural transformation
C_{X}\to Z_{\mathrm{Hilb}(X)}
in
\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})
.

Proof.

We prove the Lemma only in the unitary case, the algebraic version being simpler. Given
U\in\mathcal{C}
, there are finitely many
a\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})
for which
\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(a,U)\neq 0
. Thus the sum appearing in the definition of
\rho_{X,U}
can be written as a sum over a finite index set, and it is well defined as a morphism in
\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})
due to the tensor-structure that this category canonically inherits from
\mathcal{C}
.

Let us show that
\rho_{X}
satisfies the dinaturality condition. Let
f\in\mathcal{C}(U_{1},U_{2})
. Then

\displaystyle\rho_{X,U_{1}}\circ C_{X}(f^{\text{op}},\mathrm{id}_{U_{1}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}\sum_{w\in\ONB(a,U_{1})}((\omega^{\vee})^{*}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{X}\otimes\omega^{*})(f^{\vee}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{X}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{U_{1}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}\sum_{w\in\ONB(a,U_{1})}((f\circ\omega^{*})^{\vee}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{X}\otimes\omega^{*})

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}\sum_{v\in\ONB(a,U_{2})}((v^{*})^{\vee}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{X}\otimes(f\circ v^{*}))

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}_{\bar{U}_{2}}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{X}\otimes f)\circ\rho_{X,U_{2}}

To finish the proof, we have to verify that
(\rho_{X,U})_{U\in\mathcal{C}}
is uniformly bounded. For this, observe that

\displaystyle\rho_{X,U}^{*}\rho_{X,U}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}\sum_{\omega_{i}\in\ONB(U_{i},U)}\sum_{\omega_{j}\in\ONB(U_{j},U)}\omega_{j}^{\vee*}\omega_{i}^{\vee}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{X}\otimes\omega_{j}\omega_{i}^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}\sum_{\omega\in\ONB(U_{i},U)}(\omega\omega^{*})^{\vee}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{X}\otimes\omega\omega^{*}

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{id}_{\bar{U}}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{X}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{U}\ .

Consequently,

\|\rho_{X,U}\|^{2}=\|\rho_{X,U}^{*}\rho_{X,U}\|\leq 1\ .

∎

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
X\in\Vect(\mathcal{C})
. Then the centralizer
Z_{\Vect}(X)
is the coend of
C_{X}
in
\Vect(\mathcal{C})
. Similarly, given
X\in\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})
,
Z_{\mathrm{Hilb}}(X)
is the coend of
C_{X}
in
\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})
.

Proof.

Once again, the proof of the algebraic version of the claim is similar to the unitary case, but without the analytical issues and therefore simpler. We shall only prove the unitary version then.

Suppose
\theta=\{\theta_{U}:\bar{U}\otimes X\otimes U\to K\}_{U\in\mathcal{C}}
is a dinatural transformation
C_{X}\to Z_{\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})}
in
\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})
. Recall that in particular
\|\theta\|=\sup_{U}\|\theta_{U}\|<\infty
. From
\theta
we want to produce a morphism
\Theta:Z_{\mathrm{Hilb}}(X)\to K
, satisfying
\Theta\circ\rho=\theta
, and then show that
\Theta
is the unique morphism having this property.

Each
\theta_{a}
, for
a\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})
, is a uniformly bounded collection of bounded linear maps

\{\theta_{a,b}:(\bar{a}\otimes X\otimes a)(b)\simeq X(a\otimes b\otimes\bar{a})\to K(b)\ \}_{b\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}.

Given

\xi=(\xi_{a})_{a\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}\in Z_{\mathrm{Hilb}}(X)(b)=\bigoplus_{a\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}^{\ell^{2}}X(a\otimes b\otimes\bar{a}),

the net
\{\sum_{a\in I}\theta_{a,b}(\xi_{a})\ |I\Subset\Irr(\mathcal{C})\}
, where
\Subset
means finite contained, converges in
K(b)
due to the uniform boundedness of
\theta
. Indeed, given
\epsilon>0

\displaystyle\|\sum_{a\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})\setminus I}\theta_{a,b}(\xi_{a})\|^{2}=\sum_{a,c\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})\setminus I}\langle\theta_{c,b}^{*}\theta_{a,b}(\xi_{a}),\xi_{c}\rangle\leq\|\theta\|^{2}\sum_{a\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})\setminus I}\|\xi_{a}\|^{2}<\epsilon

for every
I\Subset\Irr(\mathcal{C})
sufficiently large. We have then a well defined morphism
\Theta:Z_{\mathrm{Hilb}}(X)\to K
associating to
(\xi_{a})_{a\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}\in Z_{\mathrm{Hilb}}(X)(b)
as above the vector
\sum_{a}\theta_{a,b}(\xi_{a})\in K(b)
. Naturality of
\theta
implies then

\Theta\circ\rho_{X,U}=\theta_{U}\ \forall\ U\in\mathcal{C}\ .

More concisely, we can write

\Theta=\sum_{a\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}\theta_{a}\ .

Now we show uniqueness. If
\tilde{\Theta}:Z_{\mathrm{Hilb}}(X)\to K
is another morphism in
\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})
such that
\tilde{\Theta}\circ\rho_{X,U}=\theta_{U}
for each
U\in\mathcal{C}
, it holds in particular

\theta_{a}=\tilde{\Theta}\circ\rho_{X,a}\ .

But
\rho_{X,a}
is just the inclusion

\bar{a}\otimes X\otimes a\hookrightarrow\bigoplus_{c\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}\bar{c}\otimes X\otimes c=Z_{\mathrm{Hilb}}(X)\ .

Thus, it must hold

\tilde{\Theta}=\sum_{a\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}\theta_{a}=\Theta\ .

∎

3.2.
Free half-braidings

We shall now discuss how to enhance the functor
Z_{\mathrm{Hilb}}\in\End(\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C}))
to a functor
\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})\to\mathcal{Z}_{*}(\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C}))
. We start by introducing the
free half-braidings
.

Let
X\in\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})
. For each
U\in\mathcal{C}
, let
(\sigma^{X}_{U})_{a,b}:\bar{a}\otimes X\otimes a\otimes U\to U\otimes\bar{b}\otimes X\otimes b
be given by

(\sigma^{X}_{U})_{a,b}:=\left(\frac{d_{a}}{d_{b}}\right)^{1/2}\sum_{\omega\in\ONB(b,a\otimes U)}(\mathrm{id}_{U}\otimes\omega^{\vee}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{X}\otimes\omega^{*})(\bar{R}_{U}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\bar{a}}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{X}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{a}).

Theorem 3.7

(
[
27
]
, Theorem 3.4)
.

Given
X\in\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})
,
\sigma^{X}:=\{(\sigma^{X})_{a,b}\}_{a,b\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}
is a unitary half-braiding on
Z_{\mathrm{Hilb}}(X)
.

Definition 3.8
.

The functor
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{Hilb}}:\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})\to\mathcal{Z}_{*}(\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C}))
given by
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(Z_{\mathrm{Hilb}}(X),\sigma^{X})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(Z_{\mathrm{Hilb}}(Y),\sigma^{Y})}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{Hilb}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z_{\mathrm{Hilb}}(f)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{Hilb}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{Hilb}}}

is called the
free half-braiding functor
on
\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})

Denoting by
\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{Hilb}}:\mathcal{Z}_{*}(\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C}))\to\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})
the forgetful functor, we have
Z_{\mathrm{Hilb}}=\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{Hilb}}\circ\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{Hilb}}
, and one could think of establishing an adjunction
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{Hilb}}\dashv\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{Hilb}}
so that
Z_{\mathrm{Hilb}}
is the monad of this adjunction. The counit of this adjunction will not be bounded, and thus it will not be a morphism in
\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})
. The monadic data will be well defined in the algebraic ind-completion, but we have to modify the free-half braiding construction a little.

Let
X\in\Vect(\mathcal{C})
. For each
U\in\mathcal{C}
, let
(\beta^{X}_{U})_{a,b}:\bar{a}\otimes X\otimes a\otimes U\to U\otimes\bar{b}\otimes X\otimes b
be given by

(\beta^{X}_{U})_{a,b}:=\sum_{\omega\in\ONB(b,a\otimes U)}(\mathrm{id}_{U}\otimes\omega^{\vee}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{X}\otimes\omega^{*})(\bar{R}_{U}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\bar{a}}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{X}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{a}).

A simplified verison of the proof of
[
27
]
, Theorem 3.4, shows the following.

Theorem 3.9
.

Given
X\in\Vect(\mathcal{C})
,
\beta^{X}:=\{(\beta^{X})_{a,b}\}_{a,b\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}
is a half-braiding on
Z_{\Vect}(X)
.

Analogous to
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{Hilb}}
, we have the functor

\mathcal{F}_{\Vect}:\Vect(\mathcal{C})\to\mathcal{Z}\Vect(\mathcal{C})

that takes

to
(Z_{\Vect}(X),\beta^{X})
.

3.3.
Monadic reconstruction of the algebraic Drinfeld center

We now present an algebraic treatment, showing that the algebraic Drinfeld center
\mathcal{Z}\Vect(\mathcal{C})
is monadic over
\Vect(\mathcal{C})
. The statements and proofs in this section follow
[
34
]
very closely. Though we have to be careful with the presence of ind-objects, which are non-dualizable, the adaptations of the arguments are very straightforward. The reader familiar with this reference could skip the proofs in this section without loosing much content.

Definition 3.10
.

The unit of
Z_{\Vect}
is the natural transformation
\eta:\mathrm{id}_{\Vect(\mathcal{C})}\to Z_{\Vect}
defined by

\eta_{X}:X=\bar{{\mathds{1}}}\odot X\odot{\mathds{1}}\hookrightarrow\bigoplus_{a\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}\bar{a}\odot X\odot a=Z_{\Vect}(X).

Definition 3.11
.

Given
X,Y\in\Vect(\mathcal{C})
, define
\partial_{X,Y}:X\odot Y\to Y\odot Z_{\Vect}(X)
by

\partial_{X,Y}=\beta^{X}_{Y}\circ(\eta_{X}\odot\mathrm{id}_{Y}).

Lemma 3.12
.

For
U\in\mathcal{C}
,

\partial_{X,U}=(\mathrm{id}_{U}\odot\rho_{X,U})(\bar{R}_{U}\odot\mathrm{id}_{X}\odot\mathrm{id}_{U})\ .

Proof.

Fixing
U\in\mathcal{C}
and
b\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})
, the
b
-th component
X\odot U\to U\odot\bar{b}\odot X\odot b
of
\beta^{X}_{U}(\eta_{X}\odot\mathrm{id}_{U})
is

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}\sum_{\omega\in\ONB(b,a\odot U)}(\mathrm{id}_{U}\odot\omega^{\vee}\odot\mathrm{id}_{X}\odot\omega^{*})(\bar{R}_{U}\odot\mathrm{id}_{\bar{a}}\odot\mathrm{id}_{X}\odot\mathrm{id}_{a})(\eta_{X}\odot\mathrm{id}_{U})\ ,

which equals to

\displaystyle\sum_{\omega\in\ONB(b,U)}(\mathrm{id}_{U}\odot\omega^{\vee}\odot\mathrm{id}_{X}\odot\omega^{*})(\bar{R}_{U}\odot\mathrm{id}_{X})\ .

∎

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
X,Y\in\Vect(\mathcal{C})
and
U\in\mathcal{C}
. If
\xi=\{\xi_{U}:X\odot U\to U\odot Y\}_{U\in\mathcal{C}}
is a natural transformation, then there is a unique
r:Z_{\Vect}(X)\to Y
such that

\xi_{U}=(\mathrm{id}_{U}\odot r)\partial_{X,U}\ \forall\ U\in\mathcal{C}\ .

Proof.

Define

\tilde{\xi}_{U}=(R_{U}^{*}\odot\mathrm{id}_{Y})(\mathrm{id}_{\bar{U}}\odot\xi_{U}):\bar{U}\odot X\odot U\to Y.

Then
\tilde{\xi}:=\{\tilde{\xi}_{U}:C_{X}(U)\to Y\}_{U\in\mathcal{C}}
is a dinatural transformation. By Theorem
3.6
, there is a unique
r:Z_{\Vect}(X)\to Y
such that
\tilde{\xi}_{U}=r\circ\rho_{X,U}
for all
U\in\mathcal{C}
. From the conjugate equation for
U
, it holds that
(\mathrm{id}_{U}\odot\tilde{\xi}_{U})(\bar{R}_{U}\odot\mathrm{id}_{X}\odot\mathrm{id}_{U})=\xi_{U}
. But

(\mathrm{id}_{U}\odot\tilde{\xi}_{U})(\bar{R}_{U}\odot\mathrm{id}_{X}\odot\mathrm{id}_{U})=(\mathrm{id}_{U}\odot r)(\mathrm{id}_{U}\odot\rho_{X,U})(\bar{R}_{U}\odot\mathrm{id}_{X}\odot\mathrm{id}_{U})=(\mathrm{id}_{U}\odot r)\partial_{X,U},

where the last equality follows from Lemma
3.12
.
∎

Remark 3.14
.

Observe that if
\xi=\{\xi_{U}:X\odot U\to U\odot Y\}_{U\in\mathcal{C}}
is a natural transformation between the functors
X\odot(-)
and
(-)\odot Y:\mathcal{C}\to\Vect(\mathcal{C})
, it has a unique extension to a natural transformation
\xi=\{\xi_{K}:X\odot K\to K\odot Y\}_{K\in\Vect(\mathcal{C})}
. Indeed, taking
r:Z_{\Vect}(X)\to Y
to be the unique morphism for which
\xi_{U}=(\mathrm{id}_{U}\odot r)\partial_{X,U}
for each
U\in\mathcal{C}
, the unique extension is given by

\xi_{K}=(\mathrm{id}_{K}\odot r)\partial_{X,K},

K\in\Vect(\mathcal{C})
. It is clearly well defined, natural, and since every object in
\Vect(\mathcal{C})
is an inductive limit of objects of
\mathcal{C}
, it is the unique extension. Then Lemma
3.13
implies that any natural transformation of the form
\xi:X\odot(-)\to(-)\odot Y
on
\Vect(\mathcal{C})
is determined by a morphism
r:Z_{\Vect}(X)\to Y
, which is determined in turn by the values of
\xi
on objects of
\mathcal{C}
.

Lemma 3.15
.

If
X,Y\in\Vect(\mathcal{C})
,
V\in\mathcal{C}
and
\xi=\{\xi_{U}:X\odot U\odot V\to U\odot Y\}_{U\in\mathcal{C}}
is natural, then there is a unique
r:Z_{\Vect}(X)\odot V\to Y
such that

\xi_{U}=(\mathrm{id}_{U}\odot r)(\partial_{X,U}\odot\mathrm{id}_{V})\ \forall\ U\in\mathcal{C}\ .

Proof.

Define
\tilde{\xi}_{U}:=(\xi_{U}\odot\mathrm{id}_{\bar{V}})(\mathrm{id}_{X}\odot\mathrm{id}_{U}\odot\bar{R}_{V}):X\odot U\to U\odot Y\odot\bar{V}
. Then
\tilde{\xi}=\{\tilde{\xi}_{U}\}_{U\in\mathcal{C}}
is natural. By Lemma
3.13
, there is a unique
\tilde{r}:Z_{\Vect}(X)\to Y\odot\bar{V}
such that
\tilde{\xi}_{U}=(\mathrm{id}_{U}\odot\tilde{r})\partial_{X,U}
for each
U\in\mathcal{C}
. From the conjugate equations for
V
, by defining
r=(\mathrm{id}_{Y}\odot R_{V}^{*})(\tilde{r}\odot\mathrm{id}_{V})
we see that

(\mathrm{id}_{U}\odot r)(\partial_{X,U}\odot\mathrm{id}_{V})=\xi_{U}\ .

∎

Lemma 3.16
.

Let
X,Y\in\Vect(\mathcal{C})
, and let
\xi:\{\xi_{U,V}:X\odot U\odot V\to U\odot V\odot Y\}_{U,V\in\mathcal{C}}
be natural. There exists a unique
r:Z_{\Vect}^{2}(X)\to Y
such that

\xi_{U,V}=(\mathrm{id}_{U}\odot\mathrm{id}_{V}\odot r)(\mathrm{id}_{U}\odot\partial_{Z_{\Vect}(X),V})(\partial_{X,U}\odot\mathrm{id}_{V})\ .

Any such natural transformation has a unique extension to a natural transformation
\xi=\{\xi_{K,L}:X\odot K\odot L\to K\odot L\odot Y\}_{K,L\in\Vect(\mathcal{C})}
.

Proof.

Fixing
V\in\mathcal{C}
,
\xi_{V}:=\{\xi_{U,V}:X\odot U\odot V\to U\odot V\odot Y\}_{U\in\mathcal{C}}
is natural in
U\in\mathcal{C}
. By Lemma
3.15
, there is a unique
\tilde{r}_{V}:Z_{\Vect}(X)\odot V\to V\odot Y
such that
\xi_{U,V}=(\mathrm{id}_{U}\odot\tilde{r}_{V})(\partial_{X,U}\odot\mathrm{id}_{V})
for each
U\in\mathcal{C}
. But since
\xi
is also natural in the second variable, the collection
\tilde{r}=\{\tilde{r}_{V}:Z_{\Vect}(X)\odot V\to V\odot Y\}_{V\in\mathcal{C}}
must be natural in
V\in\mathcal{C}
, by unicity of the
\tilde{r}_{V}
’s. Applying Lemma
3.13
to
\tilde{r}
, we conclude that there is a unique
r:Z_{\Vect}(Z_{\Vect}(X))=Z_{\Vect}^{2}(X)\to Y
such that
\tilde{r}_{V}=(\mathrm{id}_{V}\odot r)\partial_{Z_{\Vect}(X),V}
for each
V\in\mathcal{C}
. We have then

\xi_{U,V}=(\mathrm{id}_{U}\odot\tilde{r}_{V})(\partial_{X,U}\odot\mathrm{id}_{V})=(\mathrm{id}_{U}\odot\mathrm{id}_{V}\odot r)(\mathrm{id}_{U}\odot\partial_{Z_{\Vect}(X),V})(\partial_{X,U}\odot\mathrm{id}_{V}).

By the same argument in the discussion following the proof of Lemma
3.13
, we conclude that
\xi
extends uniquely to a natural transformations between the endofunctors
X\odot(-)\odot(\bullet)\to(-)\odot(\bullet)\odot Y
of
\Vect(\mathcal{C})\times\Vect(\mathcal{C})
.
∎

3.4.
The adjunction

Recall the functor
\mathcal{F}_{\Vect}:\Vect(\mathcal{C})\to\mathcal{Z}\Vect(\mathcal{C});\ X\mapsto(Z_{\Vect}(X),\beta^{X})
. Let
\mathcal{U}_{\Vect}:\mathcal{Z}\Vect(\mathcal{C})\to\Vect(\mathcal{C})

\mathcal{U}_{\Vect}\circ\mathcal{F}_{\Vect}=Z_{\Vect}

\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{\Vect}

and
\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{U}_{\Vect}

Lemma 3.17
.

The collection
\{\beta^{X}_{Y}:Z(X)\odot Y\to Y\odot Z(X)\}_{X,Y}
is a natural transformation
Z(-)\odot(\bullet)\to(\bullet)\odot Z(-)
.

Proof.

Given
f\in\Hom_{\Vect(\mathcal{C})}(X,X^{\prime})
and
g\in\Hom_{\Vect(\mathcal{C})}(Y,Y^{\prime})
,
Z(f)\in\Hom_{\mathcal{Z}\Vect(\mathcal{C})}(Z(X),Z(X^{\prime}))
, and since
\beta^{X}
is a half-braiding,
\beta^{X}_{Y^{\prime}}(\mathrm{id}_{Z(X)}\odot g)=(g\odot\mathrm{id}_{Z(X)})\beta^{X}_{Y}

\displaystyle\beta^{X^{\prime}}_{Y^{\prime}}(Z(f)\odot g)

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}_{Y^{\prime}}\odot Z(f))\beta^{X}_{Y^{\prime}}(\mathrm{id}_{Z(X)}\odot g)

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}_{Y^{\prime}}\odot Z(f))(g\odot\mathrm{id}_{Z(X)})\beta^{X}_{Y}\ .

∎

Now we introduce a natural transformation that will latter be shown to be the counit of the adjunction
\mathcal{F}\dashv\mathcal{U}
. Given
(M,\gamma)\in\mathcal{Z}\Vect(\mathcal{C})
, the naturality of
\{\gamma_{Y}:M\odot Y\to Y\odot M\}_{Y\in\Vect(\mathcal{C})}
implies, by Lemma
3.13
, the existence of a unique
\varepsilon_{(M,\gamma)}:Z(M)\to M
such that

\gamma_{Y}=(\mathrm{id}_{Y}\odot\varepsilon_{(M,\gamma)})\partial_{M,Y}=(\mathrm{id}_{Y}\odot\varepsilon_{(M,\gamma)})\beta^{M}_{Y}(\eta_{M}\odot\mathrm{id}_{Y})

for each
Y\in\Vect(\mathcal{C})
.

Proposition 3.18
.

For each
(M,\gamma)\in\mathcal{Z}\Vect(\mathcal{C})
,

\varepsilon_{(M,\gamma)}\in\Hom_{\mathcal{Z}\Vect(\mathcal{C})}((Z_{\Vect}(M),\beta^{M}),(M,\gamma))\ .

Proof.

Since a half-braiding on an ind-object is determined by its values at objects of
\mathcal{C}
, it suffices to show

(\mathrm{id}_{U}\odot\varepsilon_{(M,\gamma)})\beta^{M}_{U}=\gamma_{U}(\varepsilon_{(M,\gamma)}\odot\mathrm{id}_{U})

for each
U\in\mathcal{C}
. Due to lemma
3.15
it suffices in turn to prove

(\mathrm{id}_{V}\odot(\mathrm{id}_{U}\odot\varepsilon_{(M,\gamma)})\beta^{M}_{U})(\partial_{M,V}\odot\mathrm{id}_{U})=(\mathrm{id}_{V}\odot\gamma_{U}(\varepsilon_{(M,\gamma)}\odot\mathrm{id}_{U}))(\partial_{M,V}\odot\mathrm{id}_{U})

for each
V\in\mathcal{C}
. Now

\displaystyle(\mathrm{id}_{V}\odot(\mathrm{id}_{U}\odot\varepsilon_{(M,\gamma)})\beta^{M}_{U})(\partial_{M,V}\odot\mathrm{id}_{U})

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}_{V}\odot\mathrm{id}_{U}\odot\varepsilon_{(M,\gamma)})\beta^{M}_{V\odot U}(\eta_{M}\odot\mathrm{id}_{V}\odot\mathrm{id}_{U})

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}_{V\odot U}\odot\varepsilon_{(M,\gamma)})\partial_{M,V\odot U}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{V\odot U}

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}_{V}\odot\gamma_{U})(\gamma_{V}\odot\mathrm{id}_{U})

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}_{V}\odot\gamma_{U})(\mathrm{id}_{V}\odot\varepsilon_{(M,\gamma)}\odot\mathrm{id}_{U})(\partial_{M,V}\odot\mathrm{id}_{U})\ .

∎

Proposition 3.19
.

The collection
\varepsilon:=\{\varepsilon_{(M,\gamma)}\}_{(M,\gamma)\in\mathcal{Z}\Vect(\mathcal{C})}
is a natural transformation
\mathcal{F}\mathcal{U}\to\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{Z}\Vect(\mathcal{C})}
.

Proof.

We shall show that, for any
X\in\Vect(\mathcal{C})
and any morphism

,

(\mathrm{id}_{X}\odot\varepsilon_{(N,\gamma^{\prime})}Z(f))\partial_{M,X}=(\mathrm{id}_{X}\odot f\varepsilon_{(M,\gamma)})\partial_{M,X}.

By Lemma
3.13
, this will show
\varepsilon_{(N,\gamma^{\prime})}Z(f)=f\varepsilon_{(M,\gamma)}
and therefore the naturality of
\varepsilon
.

\displaystyle(\mathrm{id}_{X}\odot\varepsilon_{(N,\gamma^{\prime})}Z(f))\partial_{M,X}

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}_{X}\odot\varepsilon_{(N,\gamma^{\prime})}Z(f))\beta^{M}_{X}(\eta_{M}\odot\mathrm{id}_{X})

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}_{X}\odot\varepsilon_{(N,\gamma^{\prime})})\beta^{N}_{X}(Z(f)\eta_{M}\odot\mathrm{id}_{X})

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}_{X}\odot\varepsilon_{(N,\gamma^{\prime})})\beta^{N}_{X}(\eta_{N}f\odot\mathrm{id}_{X})

\displaystyle=\gamma^{\prime}_{X}(f\odot\mathrm{id}_{X})

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}_{X}\odot f)\gamma_{X}

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}_{X}\odot f)(\mathrm{id}_{X}\odot\varepsilon_{(M,\gamma)})\partial_{M,X}

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}_{X}\odot f\varepsilon_{(M,\gamma)})\partial_{M,X}\ .

∎

Lemma 3.20
.

For all
X\in\Vect(\mathcal{C})
we have
\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}(X)}Z_{\Vect}(\eta_{X})=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{F}(X)}

\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}(X)}=\varepsilon_{(Z(X),\beta^{X})}

Proof.

We shall show that
(\mathrm{id}_{U}\odot\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}(X)}Z_{\Vect}(\eta_{X}))\partial_{X,U}=\partial_{X,U}
for each

and invoke Lemma
3.13
to conclude the proof. We have

\displaystyle(\mathrm{id}_{U}\odot\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}(X)}Z_{\Vect}(\eta_{X}))\partial_{X,U}

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}_{U}\odot\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}(X)}Z_{\Vect}(\eta_{X}))\beta^{X}_{U}(\eta_{X}\odot\mathrm{id}_{U})

∎

Lemma 3.21
.

For all
(M,\sigma)\in\mathcal{Z}\Vect(\mathcal{C})
, it holds
\mathcal{U}(\varepsilon_{(M,\sigma)})\eta_{\mathcal{U}(M,\sigma)}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{U}(M,\sigma)}

Recall that, for any half-braiding

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}(\varepsilon_{(M,\beta)})\eta_{\mathcal{U}(M,\beta)}=\varepsilon_{(M,\beta)}\beta^{M}_{\mathds{1}}\eta_{M}=(\mathrm{id}_{\mathds{1}}\otimes\varepsilon_{(M,\beta)})\partial_{M,{\mathds{1}}}=\beta_{\mathds{1}}=\mathrm{id}_{M}\ .

∎

Corollary 3.22
.

The pair
(\eta,\varepsilon)
constitutes an adjunction data for
\mathcal{F}\dashv\mathcal{U}
.

Corollary
3.22
implies that the endofunctor
Z
of

has a monadic structure coming from the adjunction data
\mathcal{F}\dashv\mathcal{U}
. We already have a concrete presentation for the unit

we can also extract a concrete presentation for the counit

. If

Proposition 3.23
.

There is a functor
\Phi:\mathcal{Z}\Vect(\mathcal{C})\to Z{\hbox{-}}\Mod_{\Vect(\mathcal{C})}

Proof.

If

, then
(M,\varepsilon_{(M,\gamma)})\in Z{\hbox{-}}\Mod_{\Vect(\mathcal{C})}

Proposition 3.24
.

There is a functor
Z{\hbox{-}}\Mod_{\Vect(\mathcal{C})}\to\mathcal{Z}\Vect(\mathcal{C})
.

Proof.

Let
(M,\tau)\in Z{\hbox{-}}\Mod_{\Vect(\mathcal{C})}
. Given
X\in\Vect(\mathcal{C})
, let

\beta^{\tau}_{X}:=(\mathrm{id}_{X}\otimes\tau)\partial_{M,X}=(\mathrm{id}_{X}\otimes\tau)\beta^{M}_{X}(\eta_{M}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{X}).

We claim that
\beta^{\tau}:=\{\beta^{\tau}_{X}\}_{X\in\Vect(\mathcal{C})}
is a half-braiding on
M
. First we show
\beta^{\tau}_{\mathds{1}}=\mathrm{id}_{M}
. Since
(Z,\mu,\eta)
is a monad,

\beta^{\tau}_{\mathds{1}}=\tau\beta^{M}_{\mathds{1}}\eta_{M}=\tau\eta_{M}=\mathrm{id}_{M}.

Now we show the braid relation
\beta^{\tau}_{X\odot Y}=(\mathrm{id}_{X}\odot\beta^{\tau}_{Y})(\beta^{\tau}_{X}\odot\mathrm{id}_{Y})
for arbitrary
X,Y\in\Vect(\mathcal{C})
. By definition,

\displaystyle\beta^{\tau}_{X\odot Y}

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}_{X}\odot\mathrm{id}_{Y}\odot\tau)\partial_{M,X\odot Y}

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}_{X}\odot\mathrm{id}_{Y}\odot\tau\mu_{M})(\mathrm{id}_{X}\odot\partial_{Z(M),Y})(\partial_{M,X}\odot\mathrm{id}_{Y})

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}_{X}\odot\mathrm{id}_{Y}\odot\tau Z(\tau)(\mathrm{id}_{X}\odot\partial_{Z(M),Y})(\partial_{M,X}\odot\mathrm{id}_{Y})

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}_{X}\odot\mathrm{id}_{Y}\odot\tau)(\mathrm{id}_{X}\odot\partial_{Z(M),Y})(\mathrm{id}_{X}\odot\tau\odot\mathrm{id}_{Y})(\partial_{M,X}\odot\mathrm{id}_{Y})

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}_{X}\odot\beta^{\tau}_{Y})(\beta^{\tau}_{X}\odot\mathrm{id}_{Y})\ .

We show now that
\beta^{\tau}
is invertible. Let
U\in\mathcal{C}
. By naturality and conjugate equations, the morphism

\tilde{\beta}^{\tau}_{U}:=(\bar{R}_{U}^{*}\odot\mathrm{id}_{M}\odot\mathrm{id}_{U})(\beta^{\tau}_{\bar{U}}\odot\mathrm{id}_{U})(\mathrm{id}_{U}\odot\mathrm{id}_{M}\odot R_{U})

is an inverse for
\beta^{\tau}_{U}
. Since, by naturality,
\beta^{\tau}
is determined by the family
\{\beta^{\tau}_{U}\}_{U\in\mathcal{C}}
, and inverse of
\beta^{\tau}
is also defined by its values at objects
U\in\mathcal{C}
. Thus,
\tilde{\beta}=\{\tilde{\beta}^{\tau}_{U}\}_{U\in\mathcal{C}}
has a unique extension to a natural transformation
(-)\odot M\to M\odot(-)
between endofunctors of
\Vect(\mathcal{C})
, and this extension is
(\beta^{\tau})^{-1}
.

Let
f:(M,\tau)\to(N,\upsilon)
be a morphism of
Z
-modules. We shall show that
f
is automatically a morphism
(M,\beta^{\tau})\to(N,\beta^{\upsilon})
of half-braidings, i.e., that
(\mathrm{id}_{X}\odot f)\beta^{\tau}_{X}=\beta^{\upsilon}_{X}(f\odot\mathrm{id}_{X})
for all
X\in\Vect(\mathcal{C})
. From the definition of
\beta^{\tau}
and the hypothesis that
f
is a morphism of
Z
-modules, we obtain

\displaystyle(\mathrm{id}_{X}\odot f)\beta^{\tau}_{X}=(\mathrm{id}_{X}\odot f)(\mathrm{id}_{X}\odot\tau)\partial_{M,X}=(\mathrm{id}_{X}\odot\upsilon)(\mathrm{id}_{X}\odot Z(f))\partial_{M,X}\ .

From the naturality of
\partial_{(-),(\bullet)}
, we have

\displaystyle(\mathrm{id}_{X}\odot\upsilon)(\mathrm{id}_{X}\odot Z(f))\partial_{M,X}=(\mathrm{id}_{X}\odot\upsilon)\partial_{N,X}(f\odot\mathrm{id}_{X})=\beta^{\upsilon}_{X}(f\odot\mathrm{id}_{X})\ .

Hence, defining
\Psi(M,\tau):=(M,\beta^{\tau})
on object
(M,\tau)\in Z{\hbox{-}}\Mod_{\Vect(\mathcal{C})}
, and
\Psi(f)=f
on morphisms, we get a functor
\Psi:Z{\hbox{-}}\Mod_{\Vect(\mathcal{C})}\to\mathcal{Z}\Vect(\mathcal{C})
.
∎

Theorem 3.25
.

The functors
\Phi
and
\Psi
from propositions
3.23
and
3.24
are mutually inverses. Hence, there is an isomorphism

Since both functors act identically on morphisms, we just have to show the identities

and

\displaystyle=(M,\sigma^{\varepsilon_{(M,\gamma)}})

\displaystyle\Phi\Psi(M,\tau)

∎

The linear isomorphism

is an isomorphism of tensor categories.

4.
Unitary modules for C
∗
-algebra objects

The previous Section shows that the algebraic Drinfeld center
\mathcal{Z}\Vect(\mathcal{C})
of a unitary tensor category is monoidally equivalent to the category of modules for a monad in
\Vect(\mathcal{C})
. In the next Section, we will observe that the same category can be described as bimodules for a concrete

\Vect(\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C})

*

\Vect

for a
*
-algebra object in
\Vect(\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C})

\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C})

4.1.
Bounded operators on Hilbert space objects

Definition 4.1
.

For
H\in\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})
, let
\mathbb{B}^{l}_{H},\mathbb{B}^{r}_{H}\in\WAlg(\mathcal{C})
be given by

\mathbb{B}^{l}_{H}(a):=\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})(a\otimes H,H)\ \ \text{and}\ \ \mathbb{B}^{r}_{H}(a):=\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})(H\otimes a,H).

Similarly for
V\in\Vect(\mathcal{C})
, we write
\mathcal{L}^{l}_{V}
for the the algebra object in
\Vect(\mathcal{C})
given by

\mathcal{L}^{l}_{V}(-)=\Vect(\mathcal{C})((-)\odot V,V)\ .

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
H
be a Hilbert space object, and let
a\in\Irr{\mathcal{C}}
. Then

\mathbb{B}^{l}_{H}(a)\simeq\prod_{a_{1},a_{2}\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}^{\ell^{\infty}}\mathcal{C}(a_{1},a\otimes a_{2})\otimes\mathbb{B}(H(a_{2}),H(a_{1}))\ ,

where the norm on the tensor factor
\mathcal{C}(a_{1},a\otimes a_{2})
is

\|u\|=\|(\tr_{a}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{a_{2}})(uu^{*})\|^{1/2}\ .

Proof.

The proof is a direct computation:

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}_{H}^{l}(a)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})(a\otimes H,H)=\prod_{a_{1}\Irr(\mathcal{C})}^{\ell^{\infty}}\mathbb{B}((a\otimes H)(a_{1}),H(a_{1}))\simeq\prod_{a_{1}\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}^{\ell^{\infty}}\mathbb{B}(H(\bar{a}\otimes a_{1}),H(a_{1}))

\displaystyle\overset{(!)}{\simeq}\prod_{a_{1},a_{2}\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}^{\ell^{\infty}}\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{C}(a_{2},\bar{a}\otimes a_{1})\otimes H(a_{2}),H(a_{1}))\simeq\prod_{a_{1},a_{2}\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}^{\ell^{\infty}}\overline{\mathcal{C}(a_{2},\bar{a}\otimes a_{1})}\otimes\mathbb{B}(H(a_{2}),H(a_{1}))\ .

In the right hand side of
(!)
,
\mathcal{C}(a_{2},\overline{a}\otimes a_{2})
is the Hilbert space with norm determined by

\|v\|^{2}\mathrm{id}_{a_{2}}=v^{*}\circ v\ .

We have canonical isomorphisms

\overline{\mathcal{C}(a_{2},\bar{a}\otimes a_{1})}\simeq\mathcal{C}(\overline{a}\otimes a_{1},a_{2})\simeq\mathcal{C}(a_{1},a\otimes a_{2})\ ,

obtained by composing the
*
-operation, i.e. the dagger strcuture, with Frobenius reciprocity map
\Phi^{l}_{\overline{a}}
, which gives the claimed norm on
\mathcal{C}(a_{1},a\otimes a_{2})
. Therefore,

\mathbb{B}_{H}^{l}(a)\simeq\prod_{a_{1},a_{2}\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}^{\ell^{\infty}}\mathcal{C}(a_{1},a\otimes a_{2})\otimes\mathbb{B}(H(a_{2}),H(a_{1}))

∎

4.2.
Unitary modules

Let
(\mathbb{A},\mu^{\mathbb{A}})
be an algebra object in
\Vect(\mathcal{C})
. Let
V\in\Vect(\mathcal{C})
, and let
\alpha:\mathbb{A}\odot V\to V
be a left
\mathbb{A}
-module structure on
V
.
The maps

\mathbb{A}(a_{1})\ni f\mapsto\alpha\circ(f\odot\mathrm{id}_{H})\in\Vect(\mathcal{C}(a\odot H,H))

induce a morphism
\tilde{\alpha}:\mathbb{A}\to\mathcal{L}_{V}^{l}
in
\Vect(\mathcal{C})
. The associativity of
\alpha
is then easily checked to be equivalent to
\tilde{\alpha}
being an algebra morphism. Conversely, invoking the canonical action of
\mathcal{L}_{V}^{l}
on
V
, any algebra morphism
\tilde{\alpha}:\mathbb{A}\to\mathcal{L}_{V}^{l}
induces an action
\alpha
of
\mathbb{A}
on
V
. These constructions are mutually inverses, and thus we have the following Lemma.

Lemma 4.4
.

There is a bijective correspondence between left
\mathbb{A}
-module structures
\alpha
on
H
and algebra morphisms
\tilde{\alpha}:\mathbb{A}\to\mathcal{L}^{l}_{H}
.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
(\mathbb{A},\mu^{\mathbb{A}},j^{\mathbb{A}})
be a
*
-algebra object, and let
H\in\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})
. A left
\mathbb{A}
-module structure
\alpha
on
\For(H)
is a
unitary
left
\mathbb{A}
-module structure on
H
if the corresponding algebra morphism
\tilde{\alpha}
factors through a
*
-algebra morphism
\tilde{\alpha}:\mathbb{A}\to\mathbb{B}^{l}_{H}
, along the embedding
\mathbb{B}^{l}_{H}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{L}^{l}_{H}
.

Remark 4.6
.

This definition of unitary modules was already used in
[
16
]
, Section 5, but we make it explicit how it relates to the standard notion of modules for algebra objecst.

The proof of the following Lemma is straighforward.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
(H,\alpha)
be a unitary left module for the
*
-algebra object
\mathbb{A}
. Given
K\in\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})
, the
W^{*}
-algebra object embedding

\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})((-)\otimes H,H)\to\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})((-)\otimes H\otimes K,H\otimes K)

given by the tensor structure induces a unitary left
\mathbb{A}
-module structure on
H\otimes K
.

Definition 4.8
.

Given a
*
-algebra object
\mathbb{A}
, a morphism
f:(H,\alpha)\to(K,\beta)
between unitary
\mathbb{A}
-modules is a morphism
f\in\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})
such that
f\circ\alpha=\beta\circ(\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{A}}\odot f)
. There is a category
\LMod_{\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})}(\mathbb{A})
whoose objects are unitary left
\mathbb{A}
-modules and morphisms are unitary
\mathbb{A}
-module morphisms.

The previous Lemma says that
\LMod_{\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})}(\mathbb{A})
is a right
\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})
-module category, and it is straightforward to check that it has a canonical structures of right
\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})
-module W
∗
-category.

5.
The Canonical
W^{*}
-Algebra

Every unitary tensor category
\mathcal{C}
is canonically a right
(\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C})
-module C
∗
-category, with the action is given by

c\triangleleft(a_{1}\boxtimes a_{2}):=a_{1}\otimes c\otimes a_{2}\ .

The tensor unit
{\mathds{1}}
is moreover a cyclic object for this action.

Definition 5.1
.

The Canonical W
∗
-algebra object of a unitary tensor category
\mathcal{C}
is the W
∗
-algebra object
\mathcal{S}
in
\Vect(\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C})
corresponding to
(\mathcal{C},{\mathds{1}}_{\mathcal{C}})
as a cyclic right
(\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C})
-module W
∗
-category. In particular, the functor

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{S}}

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}\odot(a\boxtimes b)

\displaystyle\mapsto ab

is an equivalence of right
(\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C})
-module C
∗
-categories, where
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{S}}
is the Cauchy completion of the category of free
\mathcal{S}
-left modules of the form
\mathcal{S}\odot(a\boxtimes b)
, with
a,b\in\mathcal{C}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

In the context of Definition
5.1
,

\mathcal{S}\simeq\bigoplus_{c\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}\overline{c}\boxtimes c\ .

From now on,
\mathcal{C}
is a fixed unitary tensor category and
\mathcal{S}
is its canonical W
∗
-algebra.

Lemma 5.3
.

There are canonical isomorphisms

\mathcal{S}\odot({\mathds{1}}\boxtimes a)\simeq\mathcal{S}\odot(a\boxtimes{\mathds{1}})\

in
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{S}}
, natural in
a\in\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

Under the functor
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{S}}\to\mathcal{C}
of Definition
5.1
, these objects have the same image.
∎

Corollary 5.4
.

The
(\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C})
-module W
∗
-category
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{S}}
is equivalent of the Cauchy completion of the full subcategory generated by
\mathcal{S}
-modules of the form
\mathcal{S}\odot({\mathds{1}}\boxtimes a)
, with
a\in\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

For all
a,b\in\mathcal{C}
,

\mathcal{S}\odot(a\boxtimes b)\simeq\mathcal{S}\odot(a\boxtimes{\mathds{1}})\odot({\mathds{1}}\boxtimes b)\simeq\mathcal{S}\odot({\mathds{1}}\boxtimes ab)

by Lemma
5.3
.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

The canonical equivalence
\mathcal{C}\simeq\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{S}}
of right
(\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C})
-module categories extends to an equivalence

\Vect(\mathcal{C})\simeq\LMod_{\Vect(\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C})}(\mathcal{S})\

of
\Vect(\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C})
-module categories.

Proof.

An object
a\in\mathcal{C}
corresponds to
\mathcal{S}\odot({\mathds{1}}\boxtimes a)
. If
W\in\Vect(\mathcal{C})
,

W\simeq\bigoplus_{a\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}a\odot W(a)\ ,

the corresponding left
\mathcal{S}
-module is

\mathcal{S}_{W}:=\bigoplus_{a\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}\mathcal{S}\odot({\mathds{1}}\boxtimes a)\odot W(a)\ .

It remains to show that every left
\mathcal{S}
-module in
\Vect(\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C})
decomposes as a direct sum as above. Recall the adjunction between the free
\mathcal{S}
-module functor and the forgetful functor from
\LMod(\mathcal{S})\to\Vect(\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C})
: given a left
\mathcal{S}
-module
V
in
\Vect(\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C})
,

\Hom_{\LMod(\mathcal{S})}(\mathcal{S}\odot(b\boxtimes c),V)\simeq\Hom_{\Vect(\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C})}(b\boxtimes c,V)\ .

This shows that the identity morphism of
V
can be written as a sum of left
\mathcal{S}
-module morphisms
S\odot(b\boxtimes c)\to V
. i.e.,

V\simeq\bigoplus_{b,c}\left[\mathcal{S}\odot(b\boxtimes c)\right]\odot\Hom_{\LMod(\mathcal{S})}(\mathcal{S}\odot(b\boxtimes c),V)\ .

Since

\mathcal{S}\odot(b\boxtimes c)\simeq\mathcal{S}\odot(b\boxtimes{\mathds{1}})\odot({\mathds{1}}\boxtimes c)\simeq\mathcal{S}\odot({\mathds{1}}\boxtimes b)\odot({\mathds{1}}\boxtimes c)\simeq\mathcal{S}\odot({\mathds{1}}\boxtimes bc)\ ,

which are
\mathcal{S}
-module morphisms by Lemma
5.3
, we are done.
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

For each
a\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})
,
\mathcal{S}_{a}:=\mathcal{S}\odot({\mathds{1}}\boxtimes a)
has a canonical structure of a Hilbert space object in
\Vect(\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C})
.

Proof.

Indeed,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{a}(b\boxtimes c)

\displaystyle=\Hom_{\Vect(\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C})}(b\boxtimes c,\mathcal{S}\odot({\mathds{1}}\boxtimes a))\simeq\bigoplus_{d\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}\Hom_{\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C}}(b\boxtimes c,\bar{d}\boxtimes da)

\displaystyle\simeq\bigoplus_{d\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}\mathcal{C}(b,\bar{d})\otimes\mathcal{C}(c\bar{a},d)\simeq\bigoplus_{d\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}\mathcal{C}(b,\bar{d})\otimes\mathcal{C}(\overline{d},a\overline{c})\simeq\mathcal{C}(b,a\overline{c})\simeq\mathcal{C}(bc,a)\ .

The Hilbert space structure of
\mathcal{S}_{a}(b\boxtimes c)
is the one corresponding to the Hilbert space structure of
\mathcal{C}(bc,a)
through the isomorphisms above.
∎

Corollary 5.7
.

If
H\in\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})
,
\mathcal{S}_{H}
is given by

\mathcal{S}_{H}(b\boxtimes c)=H(bc)\simeq\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})(bc,H)\ .

Proof.

The proof is a straighforward computation:

\mathcal{S}_{H}(b\boxtimes c)\simeq\bigoplus_{a\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}\mathcal{S}_{a}(b\boxtimes c)\odot H(a)\simeq\bigoplus_{a\leq bc}\mathcal{C}(bc,a)\odot H(a)\simeq H(bc)\ .

∎

Corollary 5.8
.

For any vector space object
H
,
\mathcal{S}_{H}
is completely determined by its values on objects of the form
{\mathds{1}}\boxtimes b
, with
b\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})
. Thus, there is a bijective correspondence between isomorphism classes of Hilbert space object structures on
H
and isomorphism classes of Hilbert space object structures on
\mathcal{S}_{H}
.

The goal now is to analyze the free left
\mathcal{S}
-module structure on
\mathcal{S}_{a}
and prove that it is unitary. The left
\mathcal{S}
-action on
\mathcal{S}_{\mathds{1}}
corresponds to an algebra object morphism
\Gamma:\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{L}^{l}_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathds{1}}}
. The only non-zero fibers of
\mathcal{S}
are
\mathcal{S}(\bar{a}\boxtimes a)
, with
a\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})
. Thus,
\Gamma
is determined by linear maps
\mathcal{S}(\bar{a}\boxtimes a)\to\mathcal{L}^{l}_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathds{1}}}(\bar{a}\boxtimes a)
. Direct computation shows that

\Vect(\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C})((\overline{a}\boxtimes a)\odot\mathcal{S}_{\mathds{1}},\mathcal{S}_{\mathds{1}})\simeq\prod_{a_{1},a_{2}}\mathcal{L}\left(\mathcal{C}(\overline{a}_{1},\overline{a}_{2}\overline{a})\odot\mathcal{C}(a_{1},aa_{2})\odot\mathcal{S}(\bar{a}_{2}\boxtimes a_{2}),\mathcal{S}(\overline{a_{1}}\boxtimes a_{1})\right)\ .

Let
\iota_{a}:\overline{a}\boxtimes a\to\mathcal{S}
be the embedding corresponding to the localization
\mathcal{S}\to\overline{a}\boxtimes a
. Then there is an identification

\mathcal{S}(\overline{a}\boxtimes a)\simeq\mathbb{C}\ \iota_{a}\ .

Thus, the algebra morphism
\Gamma:\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{L}^{l}_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathds{1}}}
corresponding to the free left
\mathcal{S}
-action on
\mathcal{S}_{\mathds{1}}
, when written in components

\Gamma_{a_{1},a_{2}}:\mathcal{S}(\overline{a}\boxtimes a)\to\mathcal{L}\left(\mathcal{C}(\overline{a}_{1},\overline{a}_{2}\overline{a})\odot\mathcal{C}(a_{1},aa_{2})\odot\mathcal{S}(\bar{a}_{2}\boxtimes a_{2}),\mathcal{S}(\overline{a_{1}}\boxtimes a_{1})\right)\ ,

is completely determnied by
\Gamma_{a_{1},a_{2}}(\iota_{a})
.

Observe now that, as algebra objects,
\mathcal{L}^{l}_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathds{1}}}=\mathbb{B}^{l}_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathds{1}}}
.

Lemma 5.9
.

The algebra morphism
\Gamma:\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{L}^{l}_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathds{1}}}
corresponding to the free left
\mathcal{S}
-action on
\mathcal{S}_{\mathds{1}}=\mathcal{S}
is given by

\Gamma_{a_{1},a_{2}}(\iota_{a})(u\odot v\odot\iota_{a_{2}})=\langle u^{\vee*},v\rangle\iota_{{a_{1}}}\ .

In particular,

\|\Gamma_{a_{1},a_{2}}(\iota_{a})\|=1\ ,

for all
a_{1},a_{2}\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})
.

Proof.

Let
u\in\mathcal{C}(\overline{a}_{1},\overline{a}_{2}\overline{a})
and
v\in\mathcal{C}(a_{1},aa_{2})
. The free left action of
\mathcal{S}
on
\mathcal{S}_{\mathds{1}}
is the multiplication of
\mathcal{S}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{a_{1},a_{2}}(\iota_{a})(u\odot v\odot\iota_{a_{2}})

\displaystyle=\mu^{\mathcal{S}}\circ(\iota_{a}\odot\iota_{a_{2}})\circ(u\boxtimes v)=\sum_{a_{3}}\sum_{w\in O(a_{3},aa_{2})}\iota_{a_{3}}\circ((w^{\vee})^{*}\boxtimes w^{*})\circ(u\boxtimes v)

\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in O(a_{1},aa_{2})}\iota_{a_{1}}\circ((w^{\vee})^{*}\boxtimes w^{*})\circ(u\boxtimes v)

\displaystyle=\iota_{a_{1}}\circ\sum_{w\in O(a_{1},aa_{2})}((w^{\vee})^{*}\boxtimes w^{*})\circ(u\boxtimes v)=\langle u^{\vee*},v\rangle\iota_{{a_{1}}}\ .

Since

\|\Gamma_{a_{1},a_{2}}(\iota_{a})(u\odot v\odot\iota_{a_{2}})\|=|\langle u^{\vee*},v\rangle|\|\iota_{{a_{1}}}\|=\|u\otimes v\otimes\iota_{a_{2}}\|\ ,

it follows that
\Gamma_{a_{1},a_{2}}(\iota_{a})\|\geq 1
but an easy application of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality shows that
\|\Gamma_{a_{1},a_{2}}(\iota_{a})\|\leq 1
.
∎

Proposition 5.10
.

Let free left
\mathcal{S}
-action on
\mathcal{S}_{\mathds{1}}
is unitary.

Proof.

Lemma
5.9
shows that
\Gamma(\iota_{a})
lies in
\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathds{1}}}^{l}(\bar{a}\boxtimes a)
. It remains to show that
\Gamma

\mathcal{S}
and
\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathds{1}}}^{l}

\mathbb{B}^{l}_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathds{1}}}
sends
\langle u^{\vee*},v\rangle\iota_{{a_{1}}}
to

\Gamma_{a_{2},a_{1}}(\iota_{\bar{a}})((v^{\vee})^{*}\odot(u^{\vee})^{*}\odot\iota_{a_{1}})
.
∎

Corollary 5.11
.

For every

,
\mathcal{S}_{H}
has a unitary left
\mathcal{S}
-module structure induced from

.

Proof.

As a Hilbert space object,

. By Lemma

, there is an induced unitary left
\mathcal{S}

\mathcal{S}_{H}
.
∎

Proposition 5.12
.

There is an equivalence

\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})\simeq\LMod_{\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C})}(\mathcal{S})

of W
∗
-categories.

Proof.

The previous proposition shows that

can be understood as a functor
\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})\to\LMod_{\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C})}(\mathcal{S})
. Now, for a Hilbert space object
\mathcal{K}\in\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C})
, the structure of a unitary left
\mathcal{S}
-module on
\mathcal{K}
is a

-algebra object morphism

5.1.

In this subsection we will show that the algebraic Drinfeld center coincides with the category of
\mathcal{S}
-bimodules in
\Vect(\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C})

Through the module structure
\mathcal{C}\curvearrowleft\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C}

\Vect(\mathcal{C})

Lemma 5.14
.

Let
H\in\Vect(\mathcal{C})
. We have

\mathcal{S}_{H\triangleleft\mathcal{S}}\simeq\mathcal{S}_{H}\odot\mathcal{S}\ .

Proof.

Observe

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{H\triangleleft\mathcal{S}}:=\mathcal{S}\odot({\mathds{1}}\boxtimes H\triangleleft\mathcal{S})

\displaystyle\simeq\bigoplus_{a\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}\mathcal{S}\odot({\mathds{1}}\boxtimes\bar{a}Ha)

\displaystyle\overset{(!)}{\simeq}\bigoplus_{a\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}\mathcal{S}\odot(\bar{a}\boxtimes Ha)\simeq\mathcal{S}\odot\left(({\mathds{1}}\boxtimes H)\odot\mathcal{S}\right)\simeq\left(\mathcal{S}\odot({\mathds{1}}\boxtimes H)\right)\odot\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}_{H}\odot\mathcal{S}\ .

Notice that in
(!)
we have used the canonical isomorphism
\mathcal{S}\odot({\mathds{1}}\boxtimes\bar{a})\simeq\mathcal{S}\odot(\bar{a}\boxtimes{\mathds{1}})
from Lemma
5.3
.
Alternatively, in components,

\displaystyle(\mathcal{S}_{H}\odot\mathcal{S})(b\boxtimes c)

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{a,b_{1},c_{1},b_{2}}\mathcal{C}(\bar{b}_{1},\bar{b}b_{2})\odot\mathcal{C}(c,c_{1}\bar{b}_{2})\odot\mathcal{C}(c_{1},\bar{b}_{1}a)\odot H(a)

\displaystyle\simeq\bigoplus_{a,c_{1},b_{2}}\mathcal{C}(c,c_{1}\bar{b}_{2})\odot\mathcal{C}(c_{1},\bar{b}b_{2}a)\odot H(a)\simeq\bigoplus_{a,b_{2}}\mathcal{C}(c,\bar{b}b_{2}a\bar{b}_{2})\odot H(a)

\displaystyle\simeq\bigoplus_{a,b_{2}}\mathcal{C}(bc,b_{2}a\bar{b}_{2})\odot H(a)\overset{(*)}{\simeq}\bigoplus_{a,b_{2}}\mathcal{C}(a,\bar{b}_{2}bcb_{2})\odot H(a)\simeq\bigoplus_{a,b_{2},d}\mathcal{C}(d,bc)\odot\mathcal{C}(a,\bar{b}_{2}db_{2})\odot H(a)

\displaystyle\overset{(*)}{\simeq}\bigoplus_{a,b_{2},d}\mathcal{C}(bc,d)\odot\mathcal{C}(a,\bar{b}_{2}db_{2})\odot H(a)\simeq\bigoplus_{d}\mathcal{S}_{d}(b\boxtimes c)\odot(H\triangleleft\mathcal{S})(d)\ .

∎

Corollary 5.15
.

For

, a structure of a right
\mathcal{S}
-module on

Lemma 5.16
.

Let
H\in\Vect(\mathcal{C})
. A right action
\mathcal{S}_{H}\overset{\beta}{\curvearrowleft}\mathcal{S}
induces a right action of
\mathcal{S}
on

Proof.

In components, using the isomorphism
\mathcal{S}_{H}\odot\mathcal{S}\simeq\mathcal{S}_{H\triangleleft\mathcal{S}}
, the right action
\beta
of
\mathcal{S}
on
\mathcal{S}_{H}
is given by

\beta_{b\boxtimes c}:\bigoplus_{a}\mathcal{S}_{a}(b\boxtimes c)\odot(H\triangleleft\mathcal{S})(a)\to\bigoplus_{a}\mathcal{S}_{a}(b\boxtimes c)\odot H(a)\ .

In particular, the
({\mathds{1}}\boxtimes c)
-component gives

\bigoplus_{a}\mathcal{S}_{a}({\mathds{1}}\boxtimes c)\odot(H\triangleleft\mathcal{S})(a)\simeq\bigoplus_{a}\mathcal{C}(c,a)\odot(H\triangleleft\mathcal{S})(a)=(H\triangleleft\mathcal{S})(c)\overset{\beta_{{\mathds{1}}\boxtimes c}}{\longrightarrow}\bigoplus_{a}\mathcal{S}_{a}({\mathds{1}}\boxtimes c)\odot H(a)\simeq H(c)\ .

That is,

\gamma_{c}:=\beta_{{\mathds{1}}\boxtimes c}:(H\triangleleft\mathcal{S})(c)\to H(c)

gives a right action of
\mathcal{S}
on

. It is straightforward to check that

∎

In the above, we have crucially used the canonical identification
\mathcal{S}_{a}({\mathds{1}}\boxtimes c)\simeq\mathcal{C}(c,a)
, a fact that underpinnes the equivalence
\mathcal{C}\simeq\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{S}}
of right
\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C}

∗
-categories.

Lemma 5.17
.

Given

\{H\curvearrowleft(-)\triangleleft S\}\simeq\{\mathcal{S}_{H}\curvearrowleft\mathcal{S}\}

on

be the

Corollary 5.18
.

There is an equivalence between right modules in
\Vect(\mathcal{C})
for the monad
(-)\triangleleft\mathcal{S}
and bimodules in

for
\mathcal{S}

\mathcal{Z}\Vect(\mathcal{C})\simeq\Bim_{\Vect(\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C})}(\mathcal{S})\ .

Proof.

Observing, as we did previously, that

\mathcal{S}
-action
\beta

is completely determined by the components

\gamma_{c}=\beta_{{\mathds{1}}\boxtimes c}
, using the notation above. This establishes a one-to-one correspondence between

and right
\mathcal{S}
-module structures on
\mathcal{S}_{H}
.
∎

Let
H\in\Vect(\mathcal{C})
be equipped with an action of the monad
(-)\triangleleft\mathcal{S}
. Then
\mathcal{S}_{H}
inherits a right
\mathcal{S}
-module structure
\mathcal{S}_{H}\odot\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{S}_{H}
. From Lemma

\mathcal{S}
-module structure corresponds to an algebra morphism
\alpha:\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{L}^{r}_{\mathcal{S}_{H}}:=\Vect(\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C})(\mathcal{S}_{H}\odot(-),\mathcal{S}_{H})

H
from

Recall that a half-braiding on

can be described as a collection of invertible linear maps
H(c\bar{a})\to H(\bar{a}c)
indexed by
a,c\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})
, satisfying certain relations. Since
\mathcal{S}(a\boxtimes b)\simeq\delta_{a,\bar{b}}\mathbb{C}
,
\alpha
is determined by algebra homomorphisms

\alpha_{a}:\mathcal{S}(\bar{a}\boxtimes a)=\mathbb{C}\iota_{a}\to\Vect(\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C})(\mathcal{S}_{H}\odot(\bar{a}\boxtimes a),\mathcal{S}_{H})=\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{S}_{H}}^{r}(\bar{a}\boxtimes a)\ .

Lemma 5.19
.

There is a linear ismorphism

\Vect(\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C})(\mathcal{S}_{H}\odot(\bar{a}\boxtimes a),\mathcal{S}_{H})\simeq\Vect(\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C})((H\odot a)\circ\tau,(a\odot H)\circ\tau)\ ,

where
\tau:\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}
is the tensor product functor.

Proof.

\displaystyle\Vect(\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C})(\mathcal{S}_{H}\odot(\bar{a}\boxtimes a),\mathcal{S}_{H})

\displaystyle\simeq\Vect(\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C})(\mathcal{S}_{H}\odot({\mathds{1}}\boxtimes a),\mathcal{S}_{H}\odot(a\boxtimes{\mathds{1}}))

\displaystyle\simeq\prod_{b,c\Irr(\mathcal{C})}\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{S}_{H}((b\boxtimes c)\odot({\mathds{1}}\boxtimes\bar{a})),\mathcal{S}_{H}((b\boxtimes c)\odot(\bar{a}\boxtimes{\mathds{1}})))

\displaystyle\simeq\prod_{b,c\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{S}_{H}(b\boxtimes c\bar{a}),\mathcal{S}_{H}(\bar{a}b\boxtimes c))

\displaystyle\simeq\prod_{b,c\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}\mathcal{L}(H(bc\bar{a}),H(\bar{a}bc))

\displaystyle\simeq\prod_{b,c\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})}\mathcal{L}((H\odot a)(bc),(a\odot H)(bc))

\displaystyle\simeq\Vect(\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C})((H\odot a)\circ\tau,(a\odot H)\circ\tau)\ .

∎

There is a restriction map

\Vect(\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C})((H\odot a)\circ\tau,(a\odot H)\circ\tau)\to\Vect(\mathcal{C})(H\odot a,a\odot H)\ ,

induced by the embedding
\mathcal{C}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C}
.

Lemma 5.20
.

The image of an algebra morphism
\alpha:\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{L}^{r}_{\mathcal{S}_{H}}
under the restriction to
\Vect(H\odot(-),(-)\odot H)
is a half-braiding on
H
.

Proof.

Since the fibers of
\mathcal{S}
at
\bar{a}\boxtimes a
are one dimensional and
\alpha
is a unital algebra object morphism, the images
\beta_{a}
of
\alpha_{a}
under the restriction functor are invertible. To check that
\beta
is indeed a half-braiding, we have to check commutativity of the diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{S}(\bar{a}\boxtimes a)\odot\mathcal{S}(\bar{b}\boxtimes b)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{S}(\bar{b}\bar{a}\boxtimes ab)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{L}^{r}_{\mathcal{S}_{H}}(\bar{a}\boxtimes a)\odot\mathcal{L}^{r}(\bar{b}\boxtimes b)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{L}^{r}_{\mathcal{S}_{H}}(\bar{b}\bar{a}\boxtimes ab)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Vect(\mathcal{C})(H\odot a,a\odot H)\odot\Vect(\mathcal{C})(H\odot b,b\odot H)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Vect(\mathcal{C})(H\odot ab,ab\odot H)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu^{S}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}

f\odot g\mapsto(\mathrm{id}_{a}\odot g)\circ(f\odot\mathrm{id}_{b})\ .

Commutativity of the upper square is the associativity of

. Commutativity of the lower square follows form the definition of
\mu^{\mathcal{L}^{r}_{\mathcal{S}_{H}}}
.
∎

An analogous computation shows that, for

\mathbb{B}^{r}_{\mathcal{S}_{H}}(\bar{a}\boxtimes a)\simeq\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C})((H\otimes a)\circ\tau,(a\otimes H)\circ\tau)\ .

Proposition 5.21

on

Since
\alpha

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}

Lemma 5.22

Let

\beta

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(H\triangleleft\mathcal{S})(bc)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H(bc)}

Corollary 5.23
.

There is an equivalence

\mathcal{Z}\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C})\simeq\Bim_{\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal{C}^{mp}\boxtimes\mathcal{C})}(\mathcal{S})

First, we make concrete the functor

\mathcal{S}

\mathcal{S}

\mathcal{S}

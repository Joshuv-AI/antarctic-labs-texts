Abstract
Let G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E) be a simple undirected graph. In this paper, we determined the outer-weakly convex domination number of two graphs under the cartesian, strong and lexicographic products, and discuss some important combinatorial findings.

Introduction

Domination number is one of the topics in graph theory that remains intriguing. Currently, there are numerous research activities happening in the area and some interesting results can be found in
[1,2,3,4,5,6,8]
. In fact, there now more than a hundred of domination parameters published and still rapidly growing
[1,2,4,5]
. In that case, this made the essential part to the mathematicians’ motivation in conducting a research paper in this particular area. In other words, the authors of this paper has explored a recent parameter called outer-weakly convex domination number of graphs
[7]
. Additionally, new results from this parameter were constructed under some product operations in graphs. So, with that exploration, we need some important definitions in graph theory that is useful in the paper and which can be found in
[3,6]
.
Let
G=(V,E)
be a simple undirected graph. A subset
C
of a vertex set
V(G)
is a dominating set of a graph
G
if for every vertex
x\in V(G)\setminus S
, there exists a vertex
y\in C
such that
xy
is an edge of a graph
G
. In that case, the domination number denoted by
\gamma(G)
of a graph
G
is the minimum cardinality of a dominating set
C
of a graph
G
. Now, a graph
G
is connected if there is at least one path that connects for all two vertices
u,v\in V(G)
, otherwise, a graph
G
is disconnected.

For arbitrary vertices
x
and
y
in a connected graph
G
, the distance denoted by
d_{G}(x,y)
between the two vertices
x
and
y

is the length of a shortest path in graph
G
. A shortest
x-y
path in a graph
G
of length
d_{G}(x,y)
is also known as
x-y
geodesic and a closed interval denoted by
I_{G}[x,y]
consists of all those vertices that are lying on an
x-y
geodesic in a graph
G
. And for a subset
D
of vertices of a graph
G
, the union of all sets
I_{G}[x,y]
for
x,y\in D
is denoted by
I_{G}[D]
. Thus, a vertex
u
is in
I_{G}[D]
if and only if
u
is lying on a some
x-y
geodesic, where
x,y\in D
. A set
D
is convex if and only if
I_{G}[D]=D
. Consequently, if a graph
G
is a connected graph, then it follows directly that
V(G)
is a convex set. For more concepts in convexity in graphs, the readers are referred to
[8,9,10]
.

A subset
S
of vertices of a any graph
G
is called an outer-convex dominating set if all vertex not in a set
S
is adjacent to some vertex in
S
and the subgraph induced by
V(G)\setminus S
,
denoted by
\langle V(G)\setminus S\rangle
, is convex. The outer-convex domination number
\widetilde{\gamma}_{con}(G)
is the
minimum cardinality of the outer-convex dominating set
S
of
G
. Now, for a subset
C
of a set
V(G)
is called a weakly convex of a graph
G
if for all two vertices
x,y\in G
, there is a
x-y
geodesic whose vertices are in
C
. A subset
D\subseteq V(G)
is an outer-weakly convex dominating set if it is dominating set and every vertex not in
D
is adjacent to some vertex in
D
and a set
V(G)\setminus D
is weakly convex. The outer-weakly convex domination number of graph
G
, denoted by
\widetilde{\gamma}_{wcon}(G)
, is the minimum cardinality of an outer-weakly convex dominating vertex set of graph
G
. For rigorous studies in outer-convex dominating set and outer weakly convex dominating set, the readers are referred to
[4,7,8,9]
. Moreover, we need the following concepts of product operations in graphs which can be studied in
[11,12,13].

For the three standard products of (simple) graphs,
G
and
H
the vertex set of the product is
V(G)\times V(H)
. Their edge sets are defined as follows. In the
Cartesian product

G\Box H
two vertices are adjacent if they are adjacent in one coordinate and equal in the other. The edge set
E(G\boxtimes H)
of the
strong product

G\,\boxtimes\,H
is the union of
E(G\Box H)
and
E(G\times H)
. Finally, two vertices
(g,h)
and
(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})
are adjacent in the

lexicographic product

G\circ H
(also
G[H]
) either if
gg^{\prime}\in E(G)
or if
g=g^{\prime}
and
hh^{\prime}\in E(H)
. For

\ast\in\{\Box,\boxtimes,\circ\}
we call the product
G\ast H

nontrivial
, if both
G
and
H
have at least two vertices.
For
h\in V(H)
,
g\in V(G)
, and
\ast\in\{\Box,\boxtimes,\circ\}
, call
G^{h}=\{(g,h)\in G\ast H:\,g\in V(G)\}
a
G

layer
in
G\ast H
,
and call
{}^{g}H=\{(g,h)\in G\ast H:\,h\in V(H)\}
an
H

\emph{layer}
in
G\ast H
. Note that the subgraph of
G\ast H
induced on
G^{h}
is isomorphic to
G
and the subgraph of
G\ast H
induced on
{}^{g}H
is isomorphic to
H
for
\ast\in\{\Box,\boxtimes,\circ\}
. Note also that all the three products are associative and only the first two are commutative while the lexicographic product is not. The map

\pi_{G}:V(G\ast H)\rightarrow V(G)
defined with
\pi_{G}((g,h))=g
is called a
projection map on to

G
for
\ast\in\{\Box,\boxtimes,\circ\}
. Similarly, we can define the
projection map on to

H
.

In this paper, we explored the concept of outer-weakly convex domination number of two graphs using combinatorial properties under some product operations. Additionally, the bounds of outer-weakly convex domination number were also determined and we extract some important properties that suits the interest of graph theorists.

2
Cartesian Product of Graphs

In this section, we investigated the outer-weakly convex domination number of two graphs under the cartesian product operation and we denote the outer-weakly convex domination number of a simple connected graph
G
by
\widetilde{\gamma}_{wcon}(G)
.

Proposition 1
.

Let
G
and
H
be two non-trivial connected graphs and let
S
be an outer weakly convex dominating set in
G\Box H
. Then
\pi_{G}(S)
and
\pi_{H}(S)
are the outer-weakly convex dominating set in
G
and
H
, respectively.

Proof.

Assume to the contrary that
\pi_{G}(S)
is not an outer-weakly convex dominating set in
G
. Then either
\pi_{G}(S)
is not a dominating set in
G
or
V(G)\setminus\pi_{G}(S)
is not weakly convex in
G
. Assume
\pi_{G}(S)
is not a dominating set in
G
. Then there exists a vertex
g\in V(G)\setminus\pi_{G}(S)
with
N_{G}(g)\cap\pi_{G}(S)=\emptyset
. Then for any
h\in V(H)
,
(g,h)
will not be adjacent to any vertices of
S
, i.e.,
N_{G\Box H}(g,h)\cap S=\emptyset
, a contradiction to the fact that
S
is dominating set in
G
.

Assume
V(G)\setminus\pi_{G}(S)
is not a weakly convex set in
G
. Then, we can find two vertices
g^{\prime},g^{\prime\prime}\in V(G)\setminus\pi_{G}(S)
with no
g^{\prime},g^{\prime\prime}
shortest path vertices are fully contained in
V(G)\setminus\pi_{G}(S)
, i.e., some vertices of the
g^{\prime},g^{\prime\prime}
shortest path vertices is in
\pi_{G}(S)
. Let
g^{\prime},g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{r},g^{\prime\prime}
be any
g^{\prime},g^{\prime\prime}
shortest path in
G
. Then, we have
\{g^{\prime},g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{r},g^{\prime\prime}\}\cap\pi_{G}(S)\neq\emptyset
. Assume that
g_{i}\in\pi_{G}(S)
. Then, the entire
H
layer
{}^{g_{i}}H
will be in the induced subgraph of
S
in
G
. Let
h^{\prime}
and
h^{\prime\prime}
be two vertices in
V(H)
. Then the
(g^{\prime},h^{\prime}),(g^{\prime\prime},h^{\prime\prime})
shortest path should contain the vertices of the
H
layer
{}^{g_{i}}H
. Therefore, this shortest
(g^{\prime},h^{\prime}),(g^{\prime\prime},h^{\prime\prime})
path is not contained in the induced subgraph of
V(G\Box H)\setminus S
, a contradiction to the fact that
V(G\Box H)\setminus S
is a weakly convex set in
G\Box H
. Hence
\pi_{G}(S)
is an outer-weakly convex dominating set in
G
. Moreover, we can prove that
\pi_{H}(S)
is an outer-weakly convex dominating set in
H
using similar argument. This completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 2
.

Let
G
and
H
be two non-trivial connected graphs and let
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
be two proper subset of
V(G)
and
V(H)
respectively, then
S_{1}\times S_{2}
is not an outer-weakly convex dominating set in
G\Box H
.

Proof.

Since
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
are two proper subset of
V(G)
and
V(H)
respectively, we can find vertices
g\in V(G)\setminus S_{1}
and
h\in V(H)\setminus S_{2}
. Then by the definition of Cartesian product of graph,
V(^{g}H)\nsubseteq S_{1}\times S_{2}
and
V(G^{h})\nsubseteq S_{1}\times S_{2}
. Then for any
(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})\in S_{1}\times S_{2}
,
(g,h)(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})\notin E(G\Box H)
. Therefore
S_{1}\times S_{2}
is not a dominating set in
G\Box H
. Hence
S_{1}\times S_{2}
is not an outer-weakly convex dominating set in
G\Box H
.
∎

Theorem 1
.

Let
G
and
H
be two non-trivial connected graphs with order
m
and
n
, respectively. Then, we have

\min\{m,n\}\leq\widetilde{\gamma}_{wcon}(G\Box H)\leq\min\{\widetilde{\gamma}_{wcon}(G)n,\widetilde{\gamma}_{wcon}(H)m\}

Proof.

For proving the lower bound, assume to the contrary that there exists an outer-weakly convex dominating set
U
in
G\Box H
with
|U|\leq\min\{m,n\}
. Then, it follows that
\pi_{G}(U)\neq V(G)
and
\pi_{H}(U)\neq V(H)
. In this case, we can find a vertices
g\in V(G)
and
h\in V(H)
with
V(^{g}H)\cap U=\emptyset
and
V(G^{h})\cap U=\emptyset
, then by the definition of Cartesian product of graph,
(g,h)\in V(G\Box H)\setminus U
and is not adjacent to any of the vertices in
U
, which contradicts to the fact that
U
is a dominating set in
G\Box H
. Therefore,
|U|\geq\min\{m,n\}
.

Now, assume
S
and
T
are smallest outer-weakly convex dominating sets in
G
and
H
, respectively with
\widetilde{\gamma}_{wcon}(G)=|S|
and
\widetilde{\gamma}_{wcon}(H)=|T|
.

Claim:
V(G)\times T
is an outer-weakly convex dominating set in
G\Box H
.

First we have to prove
V(G)\times T
is a dominating set in
G\Box H
. Let
(g,h)
be any vertex in
G\Box H
which is not in
V(G)\times T
. Since
T
is a dominating set, there exists at least one vertex
h^{\prime}\in T
with
hh^{\prime}\in E(H)
. Then
(g,h^{\prime})\in V(G)\times T
and
(g,h)(g,h^{\prime})\in E(G\Box H)
. Therefore
V(G)\times T
is a dominating set in
G\Box H
.

Now, we have to prove
V(G\Box H)\setminus(V(G)\times T)
is weakly convex. For this, let
(g,h)
and
(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})
be two vertices in
V(G\Box H)\setminus(V(G)\times T)
. We have to find at least one shortest path between
(g,h)
and
(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})
in the induced subgraph of
V(G\Box H)\setminus(V(G)\times T)
. Hence, we consider the following cases below.

Case 1
:
g=g^{\prime}
and
h\neq h^{\prime}

Since
(g,h)
and
(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})
are not in
V(G)\times T
,
h,h^{\prime}\notin T
.
V(H)\setminus T
is weakly convex implies that there exists a shortest
h,h^{\prime}
path, say
h,h_{1},h_{2},\ldots h_{r},h^{\prime}
in the induced subgraph of
V(H)\setminus T
. Then, by the definition of the Cartesian product graph,
(g,h),(g,h_{1}),(g,h_{2}),\ldots,(g,h_{r}),(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})
is a shortest
(g,h),(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})
path in
V(G\Box H)\setminus(V(G)\times T)
.

Case 2
:
g\neq g^{\prime}
and
h=h^{\prime}
.

Similar to Case 1, we can find a shortest
(g,h),(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})
path in
V(G\Box H)\setminus(V(G)\times T)
.

Case 3
:
g\neq g^{\prime}
and
h\neq h^{\prime}
.

Since
(g,h)
and
(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})
are not in
V(G)\times T
,
h,h^{\prime}\notin T
. Again since
T
is an outer-weakly convex set in
H
,
V(H)\setminus T
is weakly convex in
H
. Then there exist at least one shortest path
h,h_{1},h_{2},\ldots h_{r},h^{\prime}
connecting
h
and
h^{\prime}
in the induced subgraph of
V(H)\setminus T
. Let
g,g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{s},g^{\prime}
be any shortest path in
G
.

Then, it follows that
(g,h),(g,h_{1}),(g,h_{2}),\ldots,(g,h^{\prime}),(g_{1},h^{\prime}),(g_{2},h^{\prime}),\ldots,(g_{s},h),(g^{\prime},h)
will be a shortest
(g,h),(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})
path in the induced subgraph of
V(G\Box H)\setminus(V(G)\times T)
.

Therefore,
V(G)\times T
is an outer-weakly convex dominating set in
G\Box H
. Similarly we can prove that
S\times V(H)
is an outer-weakly convex dominating set in
G\Box H
. This completes the proof.
∎

Consider the integers
m\geq n\geq 2
. Then for any nontrivial connected graph
H
with
\widetilde{\gamma}_{wcon}(H)\geq\left\lceil\frac{n}{m}\right\rceil
, the above theorem gives
\widetilde{\gamma}_{wcon}(K_{n}\Box H)=n
.

Strong Product of Graphs

Theorem 2
.

Let
G
and
H
be two non-trivial connected graphs with order
m
and
n
, respectively. Then, we have

\max\{\gamma(G),\gamma(H)\}\leq\widetilde{\gamma}_{wcon}(G\boxtimes H)\leq\min\{\widetilde{\gamma}_{wcon}(G)n,\widetilde{\gamma}_{wcon}(H)m\}
.

Proof.

Let
S
be an outer-weakly convex dominating set in
G\boxtimes H
. First, we have to prove that
\pi_{G}(S)
is a dominating set in
G
. Assume for a moment that
\pi_{G}(S)
is not a dominating set in
G
. Then, there exists a vertex
g\in V(G)\setminus\pi_{G}(S)
with
N_{G}(g)\cap\pi_{G}(S)=\emptyset
. Then, for any
h\in V(H)
,
(g,h)
is not adjacent to any vertex of
\pi_{G}(S)\times\pi_{h}(S)
. Hence, we have
N_{G\boxtimes H}(g,h)\cap S=\emptyset
. However, this contradicts to the fact that
S
is an outer-weakly convex dominating set in
G\boxtimes H
. Therefore,
\pi_{G}(S)
is a dominating set in
G
. By the similar arguments, we can say that
\pi_{H}(S)
is a dominating set of
H
. Therefore, all the outer-weakly convex dominating set in
G\boxtimes H
has a cardinality of
\max\{\gamma(G),\gamma(H)\}
.

Let
S
be an outer-convex dominating set in
G
with
|S|=\widetilde{\gamma}_{wcon}(G)
.

Claim:
S^{\prime}=S\times V(H)
is an outer-convex dominating set in
G\boxtimes H
.

First, we have to prove that
S\times V(H)
is a weakly convex set in
G\boxtimes H
. Let
(g,h),(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})\in V(G\boxtimes H)\setminus(S\times V(H))
. The, we have to show that there exists a shortest path connecting
(g,h)
and
(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})
in the induced subgraph of
V(G\boxtimes H)\setminus(S_{1}\times S_{2})
. If
(g,h),(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})\in E(G\boxtimes H)
, then we are done.

Case 1
:
g=g^{\prime}\notin S
,
h\neq h^{\prime}
and
hh^{\prime}\notin E(H)
.

In this case, both the vertices
(g,h)
and
(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})
are in the same
H
-layer
{}^{g}H
and
g\notin S
. If
hh_{1},h_{2},\ldots h_{r},h^{\prime}
is a shortest path in
H
, then
(g,h),(g,h_{1}),(g,h_{2}),\ldots,(g,h_{r}),(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})
is a shortest
(g,h),(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})
-path in the induced subgraph of
V(G\boxtimes H)\setminus(S\times V(H))
.

Case 2
:
g,g^{\prime}\notin S
,
g\neq g^{\prime}
,
h=h^{\prime}
and
gg^{\prime}\notin E(G)
.

Since
g,g^{\prime}\notin S
, then there exists a shortest path
P:g=g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{r}=g^{\prime}
in the induced subgraph of
V(G)\setminus S
. Then,
(g,h)=(g_{1},h),(g_{2},h),\ldots,(g_{r},h)=(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})
is a shortest
(g,h),(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})
-path in the induced subgraph of
V(G\boxtimes H)\setminus(S\times V(H))
.

Case 3
:
g\neq g^{\prime}
,
g,g^{\prime}\notin S
,
h\neq h^{\prime}
.

Since
S
is an outer-weakly convex set in
G
and
g,g^{\prime}\notin S
, then there exists a shortest
g,g^{\prime}
-path
P:g=g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{r}=g^{\prime}
in the induced subgraph of
V(G)\setminus S
. Now, for any shortest
hh^{\prime}
-path
Q:h=h_{1},h_{2},\ldots,h_{s}=h^{\prime}
in
H
,
V(P)\times V(Q)\subseteq V(G\boxtimes H)\setminus(S\times V(H))
. If
r=s
, then
R:(g,h),(g_{1},h_{1}),(g_{2},h_{2}),\ldots,(g_{r},h_{r}),(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})
will be a shortest
(g,h),(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})
-path in the induced subgraph of
V(G\boxtimes H)\setminus(S\times V(H))
. Assume without loss of generality that
r<s
. Then,

T:(g,h),(g_{1},h_{1}),(g_{2},h_{2}),\ldots,(g_{r},h_{r}),(g_{r+1},h^{\prime}),\\
(g_{r+2},h^{\prime}),\ldots(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})
will be a shortest
(g,h),(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})
-path in the induced subgraph of
V(G\boxtimes H)\setminus(S\times V(H))
.

From all the above cases, we can conclude that
V(G\boxtimes H)\setminus S\times V(H)
is a weakly convex set in
G\boxtimes H
.

Now, we have to prove
S\times V(H)
is a dominating set in
G\boxtimes H
. Let
(g,h)\notin S\times V(H
. Then,
g\notin S
. Since
S
is an outer-weakly convex dominating set in
G
, there exists a vertex
g^{\prime}\in S
with
gg^{\prime}\in E(G)
. Then, for any
h\in V(H)
,
(g,h)(g^{\prime},h)\in E(G\boxtimes H)
and
(g^{\prime},h)\in S^{\prime}
. Hence,
S\times V(H)
is a dominating set in
G\boxtimes H
.

Therefore,
S^{\prime}
is an outer-weakly convex dominating set in
G\boxtimes H
Similarly we can prove that for any outer weakly convex dominating set

in

with
|T|=\widetilde{\gamma}_{wcon}(H)
, then
V(G)\times T
is an outer weakly convex dominating set in
G\boxtimes H
. Therefore
\widetilde{\gamma}_{wcon}(G\boxtimes H)\leq\min\{\widetilde{\gamma}_{wcon}(G)n,\widetilde{\gamma}_{wcon}(H)m\}
. This completes the proof.

∎

Theorem 3
.

Let
G
be a non-trivial connected graph and
n
be any positive integer greater than or equal to 2. Then,
\widetilde{\gamma}_{wcon}(G\boxtimes K_{n})=\widetilde{\gamma}_{wcon}(G)
.

Proof.

Let
S
be an outer weakly dominating set in
G\boxtimes K_{n}
with
|S|=\widetilde{\gamma}_{wcon}
. Suppose that
\pi_{G}(S)
is not a dominating set in
G
. Then, there exists a vertex
g\in V(G)\setminus\pi_{G}(S)
with
N_{G}(g)\cap\pi_{G}(S)=\emptyset
. In that case, for any
h\in V(K_{n})
,
(g,h)
is not adjacent to any of the vertices in
S
, and this contradicts to the fact that
S
is a dominating set in
G\boxtimes K_{n}
. Therefore
\pi_{G}(S)
is a dominating set in
G
.

Let
S
be any outer weakly convex dominating set in
G
with
|S|=\widetilde{\gamma}_{wcon}(G)
. Consider the set
S^{\prime}=S\times\{h\}
, for some
h\in V(K_{n})
. We are going to prove
S^{\prime}
is an outer-weakly convex dominating set in
G\boxtimes K_{n}
.

Claim 1
:
S^{\prime}
is an dominating set in
G\boxtimes K_{n}
.

Let
(g,h^{\prime})\in V(G\boxtimes K_{n})\setminus S^{\prime}
with
g\notin S
. Then
g\notin S
and since
S
is a dominating set in
G
, there exists some vertex
g^{\prime}\in S
with
gg^{\prime}\in E(G)
. In either cases
h=h^{\prime}
and
hh^{\prime}\in E(K_{n})
, we have
(g,h^{\prime})(g^{\prime},h)\in E(G\boxtimes K_{n})
and
(g^{\prime},h)\in S^{\prime}
. If
g\in S
and
h\neq h^{\prime}
, then since
h^{\prime}\in K_{n}
,
(g,h)\in S^{\prime}
and
(g,h^{\prime})(g,h)\in E(G\boxtimes K_{n})
.

Claim 2
:
S^{\prime}
is an outer weakly convex set in
G\boxtimes K_{n}
.

Let
(g_{1},h_{1}),(g_{2},h_{2})\in V(G\boxtimes K_{n})\setminus S^{\prime}
. Let

be a

-shortest path in

. Then

\ldots,(u_{r-1},h^{\prime}),(u_{r},h_{2})
is a shortest

-shortest path in

with

Therefore,

is an outer-weakly convex domination number in

. This completes the proof.

∎

Lexicographic Product of Graphs

Before we present the main result of outer-weakly convex domination number under lexicographic product of graphs, we start first with the following proposition.

Proposition 4
.

Let
G
and
H
be two non-trivial connected graphs. If
S
is an outer weakly convex dominating set in
G\circ H
, then
\pi_{G}(S)
is an outer weakly convex dominating set in
G
.

Proof.

Let
S
be an outer weakly convex dominating set in
G\circ H
. Assume for a moment that
\pi_{G}(S)
is not an outer weakly convex dominating set in
G
. Then either
\pi_{G}(S)
is not a dominating set in
G
or
V(G)\setminus\pi_{G}(S)
is not weakly convex set in graph
G
.

Assume
\pi_{G}(S)
is not a dominating set in
G
. Then, there exists a vertex
g\in V(G)\setminus\pi_{G}(S)
with
N_{G}(g)\cap\pi_{G}(S)=\emptyset
. Then, for any
h\in V(H)
,
N_{G\circ H}(g,h)\cap S=\emptyset
and
(g,h)\in V(G\circ H)\setminus S
. And this contradicts to the fact that
S
is an outer weakly convex dominating set in
G\circ H
. Therefore,
\pi_{G}(S)
is a dominating set in
G
.

If
V(G)\setminus\pi_{G}(S)
is not a weakly convex set in
G
, then there exist two vertices
g^{\prime},g^{\prime\prime}\in V(G)\setminus\pi_{G}(S)
with no
g^{\prime},g^{\prime\prime}
shortest path vertices is contained in
V(G)\setminus\pi_{G}(S)
. Let
g^{\prime},g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{r},g^{\prime\prime}
be a shortest path in
G
. Then
\{g^{\prime},g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{r},g^{\prime\prime}\}\nsubseteq(V(G)\setminus\pi_{G}(S))
. Let
g_{i}
be a vertex not contained in
V(G)\setminus\pi_{G}(S)
. Since
g^{\prime},g^{\prime\prime}\in V(G)\setminus\pi_{G}(S)
, for any
h^{\prime},h^{\prime\prime}\in V(H)
,
(g^{\prime},h^{\prime}),(g^{\prime\prime},h^{\prime\prime})\in V(G\circ H)\setminus S
. By the definition of lexicographic product of graphs, the shortest path between
(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})
and
(g^{\prime\prime},h^{\prime\prime})
should pass through the
H
layers corresponding to the shortest path vertices of
g^{\prime}
and
g^{\prime\prime}
except
g^{\prime}
and
g^{\prime\prime}
. Here the
(g^{\prime},h^{\prime}),(g^{\prime\prime},h^{\prime\prime})
shortest path will not meet the
H
layer
{}^{g_{i}}H
. Therefore, there does not exists a shortest path between
(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})
and
(g^{\prime\prime},h^{\prime\prime})
which is contained in
V(G\circ H)\setminus S
. This contradicts to the fact that
S
is an outer weakly convex dominating set in
G\circ H
. Therefore,
\pi_{G}(S)
will be an outer weakly convex dominating set in
G
. This completes the proof.

\pi_{G}(S)

.

Conclusion

The main contribution of this paper is the determination of tight bounds for the outer-weakly convex domination number on graph products. The study focuses on this parameter for the three standard graph products: Cartesian, strong, and lexicographic products. Our results suggest that finding the exact value of
\widetilde{\gamma}_{wcon}(G*H)
is a challenging task for all the three products. Consequently, it would be of interest to continue the study on restricted classes of product graphs.

Knowledge, attitudes, and practices toward screening of colorectal cancer among adults in Kuwait primary health care centers
Authors: Mahmood Alawainati, Sara N. Alshtaily, Rawan A. Almutairi, Aisha Alshammari, Ghalla Alotaibi, Sarah Kh. Alrashidi, Salma Alotaibi, Lujain Alotaibi, Eissa Althefairi, Khaled Alsubaiei
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:The prevalence of colorectal cancer has been increasing both globally and in Kuwait. Early identification and treatment are essential to reduce its morbidity and mortality. This study aimed to assess the knowledge, attitudes, and practices toward screening of colorectal cancer among adults in Kuwait.Methods:A cross-sectional study was conducted across six governorates in Kuwait, including patients aged 18 years and above. Data were collected using an interview-administered questionnaire. Descriptive and inferential analyses were performed to evaluate participants’ knowledge, attitudes, and practices regarding colorectal cancer screening.Results:A total of 367 respondents were included; most were females (59.7%), Kuwaiti (85.4%), and university graduates (63.2%). Regarding the knowledge about colorectal cancer, although 60.8% of participants had heard of colorectal cancer screening, 35.7% of the respondents showed poor knowledge and only 22.6% had good knowledge. Older patients (P = 0.040), patients with higher educational levels (P < 0.001), and those with prior awareness of screening (P < 0.001) showed better knowledge about colorectal cancer. Participants who had heard of screening showed significantly higher behaviors (P = 0.006) and attitude scores (P = 0.008). Less than 10% underwent colon cancer screening (9.5%), with embarrassment (37.1%) and perceived cultural constraints (17%) being the most common barriers to colonoscopy.Conclusion:The study demonstrated low levels of knowledge and screening uptake, particularly among younger participants, those with lower educational levels, and individuals who had not undergone screening. Nonetheless, participants showed positive attitudes toward colorectal cancer screening. Enhancing knowledge through awareness campaigns and targeted public health initiatives is essential.

Introduction

Colorectal cancer is one of the most prevalent cancers in the world and in Kuwait, representing a major public health challenge. Globally, almost 2 million new cases were diagnosed in 2020, with almost 1 million deaths.[1] It is projected that the number of colon cancer new cases will exceed 3.2 million annually, with nearly 2 million deaths by 2040.[2] In Kuwait, colorectal cancer is considered the second leading cause of cancer-related deaths after breast cancer. The incidence among Kuwaiti males has increased five times over the past three decades.[3] Although colon cancer is considered the third leading cause of cancer mortality, the 5-year relative survival rate for colon cancer varies widely depending on the stage at diagnosis, ranging from more than 90% in stage I to almost 10% in stage IV.[4]

Screening for colon cancer is essential in reducing its morbidity and mortality by detecting the disease at earlier, more treatable stages.[5] Colonoscopy, sigmoidoscopy, and stool-based tests are recommended screening tests for colon cancer.[6] Despite clear guidelines, screening uptake varies across countries. Patient knowledge and attitudes toward screening methods are key factors influencing colon cancer screening uptake.

Several studies have assessed awareness and attitudes toward colorectal cancer screening. A study of more than 1500 patients in Jordan revealed low knowledge but positive attitudes regarding colon cancer screening.[7] In Saudi Arabia, a cross-sectional study of >1000 adult patients found that almost 80% of them had low knowledge about risk factors of colon cancer and exhibited negative attitudes toward these risk factors, and only 25.0% demonstrating good practices related to colorectal cancer.[8] A study of 747 participants in United States found gaps in knowledge about colorectal cancer screening, and less than 20% were aware about the age.[9] A study of 570 Kuwaiti adults aged between 45 and 75 years revealed low levels of knowledge and practices regarding colorectal cancer screening.[10] Studies in China revealed good knowledge, practice, and attitudes toward colorectal cancer screening.[11] Moreover, a study among 93 Australians revealed low awareness and knowledge but positive attitudes of colorectal cancer and screening especially if recommended by a doctor.[12] A mixed-methods study of almost 500 adults in United Arab of Emirates found low knowledge (10%) and participation (12.3%) levels in colorectal cancer screening.[13]

Furthermore, participants’ knowledge, attitudes, and practices regarding colorectal screening are influenced by multiple factors. Higher educational levels were found to be linked to higher levels of knowledge about colon cancer screening.[11] In addition, older age, prior awareness of colorectal cancer, and having a family or personal history of cancer have been consistently associated with better knowledge and more positive attitudes toward colon cancer screening.[7811]

This study aimed to assess the knowledge, attitudes, and practices toward screening of colorectal cancer among adults in Kuwait. Understanding the current knowledge, attitudes, and practices toward colorectal cancer screening will help in identifying the key barriers and facilitators to screening uptake. In addition, this can guide the development of targeted interventions aimed at improving early detection and reducing colorectal cancer-related morbidity and mortality in the population. The study increases awareness about the importance of early detection and the available screening methods for colorectal cancer among patients, and ultimately, it empowers them to make health decisions and promotes early diagnosis, leading to better outcomes.

Methods

Study design and population

The present study adopted a cross-sectional study design and was conducted in Kuwait. All governorates were included in the present study, and the ethical approval was obtained from the College of Medicine and Medical Sciences and the Ministry of Health of Kuwait.

Selection criteria

Patients aged 18 years or above attending the selected health centers were included in the present study. Patients who had language battier, an underlying intellectual disability, or emergency conditions were excluded.

Sampling process

Considering a z-score (1.96) based on 95% confidence interval, a prevalence of 35%, and a 5% margin of error, a sample size of 350 was calculated. Eligible participants attending the selected health centers were encouraged to participate in the study and complete the questionnaire.

Participants were adult citizens and residents of Kuwait aged 18 years and above. They were randomly selected using stratified sampling to ensure even distribution across medical centers in all six governorates. Data were collected through interviews at six health centers, one in each governorate. Participants were informed that their responses would remain confidential.

Study instrument and data collection process

The data collection tool consisted of three parts. The first part assessed demographic variables and history of colorectal cancer, including questions on gender, age, marital status, educational level, and personal and family history of colorectal cancer. The second part evaluated participants’ knowledge regarding colorectal cancer screening, while the third part assessed their attitudes and practices. The survey was designed as an interview-based questionnaire and was administered in both Arabic and English.

Data analysis

Frequencies and percentages were calculated for categorical variables, while means and standard deviations were computed for quantitative variables. The Chi-square test was used to assess associations between categorical variables. Independent-samples t-tests were employed to compare means between two groups, and analysis of variance was used for comparisons among more than two groups. Data were analyzed using the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS), version 29. A P value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Discussion

This study aimed to assess the knowledge, attitudes, and practices toward screening of colorectal cancer among adults in Kuwait. The results showed that more than one-third of patients had poor knowledge about colon cancer screening, and less than 10% had performed colon cancer screening. While embarrassment and cultural concerns were the main barriers of not doing the test, most participants demonstrated positive attitudes toward screening. The predictors of higher levels of knowledge were old age, high education level, and previous awareness of screening.

In line with many studies in the literature, this study found low levels of knowledge about colorectal cancer screening. For instance, studies from Jordan, Saudi Arabia, United States, and United Arab of Emirates found low levels of knowledge about colon cancer screening. Compared to the previously conducted study about this topic in Kuwait, the present study revealed comparable findings.[7891012] Nonetheless, some studies reported adequate levels of knowledge. For instance, a study conducted in China revealed adequate levels of knowledge about colon cancer screening.[11] Possible reasons for variation in knowledge levels across studies may include differences in study populations, educational levels, access to health information, cultural beliefs, exposure to media campaigns, availability of screening programs, and physician recommendations, all of which can influence awareness and understanding of colorectal cancer and its screening methods.

Improving the knowledge is important in increasing the performance of colon cancer screening. A study done by Lewandowski revealed a link between participants’ knowledge and performance of colon cancer screening.[14] Also, Almadi et al.[15] reported that understanding that colonoscopy is a screening tool had a significantly increased the willingness to participate in colorectal cancer screening.

Although the level of knowledge about the colon cancer screening process was low, most participants showed positive attitudes. Similarly, several studies in the literature showed comparable findings.[78910111213] The performance of colon cancer screening methods was suboptimal among the respondents, which was also reported in several studies in the literature.

Some interventions were implemented to improve the performance of colon cancer screening. One study found that text message intervention improved colon cancer screening uptake.[16] Frequent reminders using telephone or messages through small media and patient navigation also showed promising results.[17] Additionally, using reminders in the electronic medical record system, addressing barriers, and continuous monitoring and feedback were also effective measures.[18]

As expected, the present study found a significant association between educational level and knowledge about colorectal cancer screening. This can be explained by the improved health literacy, greater exposure to health information, and higher likelihood of engaging with preventive services among those with advanced education. Similar findings were also reported by other studies.[19] For instance, Alkhaldy et al. and Wong et al. found the same findings.[811] Moreover, older patients were found to have higher levels of knowledge about screening of colon cancer. This could be explained by their greater confidence and self-efficacy as shown in the previous literature as well.[12]

This study has several strengths. It included patients from all governorates across Kuwait which provided diversity of the sample. Participants included a wide range of ages and educational backgrounds, allowing the findings to reflect the general population. Moreover, it is one of a few studies that assessed this topic in Kuwait. However, some limitations should be addressed. Face-to-face questionnaires might introduce potential recall and underreporting biases due to the sensitive nature of the topic, including cultural and religious concerns.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study revealed that the knowledge and performance of colorectal cancer screening among adults attending primary health care centers in Kuwait were low, especially among younger participants, those with lower educational levels, and individuals who had not previously undergone colon cancer screening. Feelings of embarrassment and perceived cultural constraints were the most common barriers to performing colonoscopy. As most participants demonstrated positive attitudes but low knowledge toward colorectal cancer screening, improving individuals’ knowledge through public health interventions, awareness campaigns, and education are essential to improve participation in colorectal cancer screening programs in Kuwait.

James W. Webber † \dagger, Eric Todd Quinto ‡

Abstract
. We present an analysis of a novel spherical Radon transform, R R , which defines the integrals of a function, f f , in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} over spheres with arbitrary center ( 𝐲 {\mathbf{y}} ) and radii, r ⁡ ( 𝐲 ) r({\mathbf{y}}) , which vary smoothly with 𝐲 {\mathbf{y}} . We first establish sufficient and necessary conditions on r r and supp ​ ( f ) \text{supp}(f) so that R R satisfies the Bolker condition, and further conditions which allow f f to be recovered stably from R ​ f Rf . We then apply this theory to a number of example applications in Compton Scatter Tomography (CST) and Ultrasound Reflection Tomography (URT). For each application considered, we also provide injectivity proofs and explicit inversion formulae, some of which are based on the generalized theory presented by Palamodov [ 33 ] . We then combine our microlocal theory and injectivity results to prove stability estimates for our transforms. In addition, to validate our theory, we provide simulated image reconstructions.

on

, for

∎

4.3.2.
Stability with limited data

We now introduce a set of centers that is sufficient for weak
stability, as we will show. Let

(4.55)

Y^{\prime\prime}=\left\{{\mathbf{y}}\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\hskip 0.85358pt:\hskip 0.85358ptr<\left\lvert{\mathbf{y}}\right\rvert<2r\right\}{\supset Y^{\prime}.}

Let

be given in (

In the last section, we proved that

For

H_{r}({\mathbf{x}})=\left\{{\mathbf{y}}\in S({\mathbf{x}})\hskip 0.85358pt:\hskip 0.85358pt{\mathbf{y}}-{\mathbf{x}})\cdot({\mathbf{x}}/\left\lvert{\mathbf{x}}\right\rvert)\geq 0\right\}.

.

Let

Let

. Let

Theorem 4.21
.

Let
\mathcal{N}=R^{*}R^{\dagger}
. Then,
\mathcal{N}:H^{s}_{c}(D^{\prime})\to H^{s+1}_{\text{\rm loc}}(D^{\prime})
is an elliptic
\Psi
do order

Note that we are not saying that

is continuous. The proof

Proof.

Similarly to the proof of Theorem
4.6
, it is clear that

is an FIO with the
same phase and canonical relation as

and that

satisfies the Bolker condition. It follows that the canonical relation
of
\mathcal{N}
is a subset of the diagonal by Theorem
4.19
,
and thus
\mathcal{N}
is a
\Psi
do. The order of

is
-1

and

is
-1/2
.

By Theorem

4.19
, the right projection of

is surjective. This
follows from the fact that for every
({\mathbf{x}},\xi)\in D^{\prime}\times\dot{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
,
there is a
{\mathbf{y}}\in Y^{\prime\prime}
such that
({\mathbf{x}},\xi)
is normal to
S({\mathbf{y}})
.

To prove that
\mathcal{N}
is elliptic, we will go through the calculation of
its symbol. Let
({\mathbf{x}},\xi)\in D^{\prime}\times\dot{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
. Then the two points

{\mathbf{y}}_{1}={\mathbf{x}}-r\xi/\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert
and
{\mathbf{y}}_{2}={\mathbf{x}}+r\xi/\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert
are
both in
Y_{0}
and
({\mathbf{x}},\xi)
is normal to
S({\mathbf{y}}_{j})
for
j=1,2
.
Since
H_{r}({\mathbf{x}})
is a closed hemisphere, at least one of
{\mathbf{y}}_{j}
is in

H_{r}({\mathbf{x}})
. Without loss of generality, assume
{\mathbf{y}}_{1}\in H_{r}({\mathbf{x}})
.
Therefore,
h({\mathbf{y}}_{1})=1
and
h({\mathbf{y}}_{2})\geq 0
. Let
\eta_{j}\in\dot{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
be
chosen so the two preimages of
({\mathbf{x}},\xi)
in
\mathcal{C}
are

\lambda_{j}=({\mathbf{y}}_{j},\eta_{j},{\mathbf{x}},\xi),\ \ j=1,2.

The symbol of

is the sum of two
terms calculated using these two preimages.

For each
\lambda_{j}
, one multiplies the weights for
R
and
R^{*}

evaluated at
({\mathbf{y}}_{j},{\mathbf{x}})
by

. Since the weights on
R

and
R^{*}
are positive and
h({\mathbf{y}}_{1})=1
, this product for
\lambda_{1}
is positive.
Then, one multiplies by a positive Jacobian factor given in

[

or Lemma 5.1 of

[
15
]
. This shows that the contribution to the symbol of

\mathcal{N}
at

is positive. The calculation for the contribution
at
\lambda_{2}
is similar, and since

, this
contribution is nonnegative. Finally, one adds these two terms up.
Since the sum is positive, the symbol of
\mathcal{N}
is positive, and
\mathcal{N}

is elliptic.

We now prove a strong Sobolev inverse continuity result, showing that
inversion from
R
is stable in a Sobolev sense.

Corollary 4.22
.

Let
B,\ B_{1}
, and
B_{2}
be compact
subsets of
D^{\prime}
such that
B\subset{\operatorname{int}}(B_{1})
and
B_{1}\subset{\operatorname{int}}(B_{2})
. Let
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
be in
\mathcal{D}(D^{\prime})
with
\phi_{1}=1

on
B,\ \operatorname{supp}(\phi_{1})\subset B_{1}
and
\phi_{2}=1
on
B_{1},\ \operatorname{supp}(\phi_{2})\subset B_{2}
. Let
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}=\phi_{2}\mathcal{N}\phi_{1}
. For each

s\in{{\mathbb{R}}}
, there is a constant
C>0
such that for each
f\in H^{s}(B)
,

(4.59)

\left\|f\right\|_{H^{s}(B)}\leq C\left\|\mathcal{N^{\prime}}f\right\|_{H^{s+1}(B_{2})}.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.21
, there exists an order
1
parametrix,

\mathcal{P}
, of
\mathcal{N}
. Without loss of generality, one may assume

\mathcal{P}
is properly supported.
Then, for

u\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(D^{\prime})
one can write

(4.60)

\mathcal{P}\mathcal{N}u=Iu+Ku

for some smoothing operator
K
.

Now let
u
be supported in
B
. By
pseudolocality of
\Psi
do, one can write (
4.60
) as

(4.61)

\begin{split}\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\mathcal{N}^{\prime}u&=u+K^{\prime}u,\ \ \text{where}\\
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=\left(\phi_{2}\mathcal{P}\right),\ \ \mathcal{N}^{\prime}&=\left(\phi_{2}\mathcal{N}\phi_{1}\right),\ \ K^{\prime}=\phi_{2}K\phi_{1}+\phi_{2}\mathcal{P}(\phi_{2}-1)\mathcal{N}\phi_{1},\end{split}

and
K^{\prime}
is smoothing for distributions supported on
B
since

(\phi_{2}-1)
is zero on
B
.

Since
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
are properly supported
\Psi
do, for each
s\in{{\mathbb{R}}}
,

(4.62)

\mathcal{P}^{\prime}:H^{s}(B)\to H^{s-1}(B_{2}),\ \ \mathcal{N}^{\prime}:H^{s}(B)\to H^{s+1}(B_{2}),\ \ K^{\prime}:H^{s}(B)\to H^{s}(B_{2})

are
continuous and
K^{\prime}
is a compact operator since it is smoothing on

B
.

Now, we have

(4.63)

\begin{split}\|u\|_{H^{s}(B)}&\leq\|\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\mathcal{N}^{\prime}u\|_{H^{s}(B_{2})}+\|K^{\prime}u\|_{H^{s}(B_{2})}\\
&\leq C\left(\|\mathcal{N}^{\prime}u\|_{H^{s+1}(B_{2})}+\|K^{\prime}u\|_{H^{s}(B_{2})}\right).\end{split}

The rest of the proof follows the same arguments as the
proof of Corollary
4.9
.∎

5.
Image reconstructions

In this section, we present image reconstructions
based on the scanning geometries discussed in section
4
.
We use algebraic methods for reconstruction. Let
A
be the
discretized form of
R
, let
{\mathbf{x}}
be the vectorized image
of the object to be reconstructed, and let
b
be the data. Then, to
recover
{\mathbf{x}}
, we aim to solve

(5.1)

\argmin_{{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathcal{X}}\|A{\mathbf{x}}-b\|^{2}+\lambda G({\mathbf{x}}),

where
G
represents a regularization penalty (e.g., total variation)
and
\mathcal{X}
is the space of solutions (e.g., non-negative
functions). We mainly consider two methods for solving (
5.1
),
namely Landweber
[
28
]
(implemented using the
code of
[
19
]
), and a gradient decent method which uses
Total Variation (TV) regularization and non-negativity constraints,
specifically the code provided in
[
12
]
. In this
case,
\mathcal{X}
is the space of vectors
{\mathbf{x}}
with
non-negative components. Then,
G({\mathbf{x}})=\sqrt{\|\nabla{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2}^{2}+\beta^{2}}
is a modified TV norm, where
\beta
is an additional
smoothing parameter that is introduced so that the gradient of
G
is
defined at zero.

We generate noiseless data via
b=A{\mathbf{x}}
, where
{\mathbf{x}}
is the ground truth. To avoid inverse crime, we use a finer pixel grid when generating
b
than in the reconstructed image. Specifically, in this section, the image reconstructions have resolution
100\times 100
, but
b
is generated using
105\times 105
images. We add noise via

(5.2)

b_{\epsilon}=b+\gamma\times\frac{\|b\|_{2}}{\sqrt{k}}\eta,

where
k
is the length of
b
,
\eta\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
is a vector of draws from standard normal distribution, and
\gamma
controls the noise level. The images are reconstructed from
b_{\epsilon}
, and we measure least squares error using
\delta=\|{\mathbf{x}}_{\epsilon}-{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2}/\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2}
, where
{\mathbf{x}}
is the ground truth, and
{\mathbf{x}}_{\epsilon}
is the reconstruction.

6.
Conclusion

In this paper, we presented novel microlocal and
stability analyses of a spherical Radon transform,
R
, which
integrates
f
over spheres with radii
r({\mathbf{y}})
which vary smoothly
with the sphere center,
{\mathbf{y}}
. In particular, in Theorem
3.6

we provided conditions on
\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}r
, and other geometric
requirements so that
R
satisfies the Bolker condition. Then, under
the slightly stronger conditions in Theorem
3.10
,
we show that
R^{*}R
is an elliptic
\Psi
do. This theory was extended in
Definition
3.16
to provide conditions for stable
reconstruction. In section
4
, we gave examples in CST and
URT which fit our framework, and we used our theory to derive Sobolev
stability estimates which show that reconstruction is only mildly
ill-posed. Inversion methods were also provided. For example, we used
the uniform inversion methods of
[
33
]
to derive
a closed-form inversion formula for a rotational CST geometry. Our
theory was later validated in section
5
using simulated
image reconstructions, and we were able to demonstrate stable
reconstructions of image phantoms in a variety of imaging modalities.

The rotational CST modality introduced here shows promise in terms of
inversion stability, e.g., we proved global stability estimates in
Sobolev space (Corollary
4.15
). Here, the model
is linear, which in the context of CST means the ray attenuation is
neglected
[
39
]
. In further work, we aim to consider a
non-linear variant of
R
in CST which accounts for ray attenuation.
Specifically, we will use the microlocal theory developed here to
analyze how the singularities of
f
relate to those in the data in
the non-linear formulation.

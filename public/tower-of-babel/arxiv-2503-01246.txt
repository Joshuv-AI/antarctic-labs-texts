Boundary determination for the Schrödinger equation with unknown embedded obstacles by local data
Chengyu Wu, Jiaqing Yang

Abstract
In this paper, we consider the inverse boundary value problem of the elliptic operator Δ + q \Delta+q in a fixed region Ω ⊂ ℝ 3 \Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3} with unknown embedded obstacles D D . In particular, we give a new and simple proof to uniquely determine q q and all of its derivatives at the boundary from the knowledge of the local Dirichlet-to-Neumann map on ∂ Ω \partial\Omega , disregarding the unknown obstacle, where in fact only the local Cauchy data of the fundamental solution is used. Our proof mainly depends on the rigorous singularity analysis on certain singular solutions and the volume potentials of fundamental solution, which is easy to extend to many other cases.



keywords

Inverse problem, partial data, embedded obstacle, singularity analysis.

Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
be a bounded domain with smooth boundary. Denote by
D\subset\Omega
an open subset such that
\partial D\in C^{\infty}
,
D\subset\subset\Omega
and
\Omega\setminus\overline{D}
is connected. In this paper, we are concerned with the inverse boundary value problem of the following system

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta u+qu=0&{\rm in}~\Omega\setminus\overline{D},\\
u=f&{\rm on}~\partial\Omega,\\
\mathcal{B}(u)=0&{\rm on}~\partial D,\end{array}\right.

where the boundary data
f\in H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
,
q\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega},{\mathbb{C}})
and
\mathcal{B}
stands for the boundary condition imposed on
\partial D
. To be exact, we shall consider the Dirichlet boundary condition
\mathcal{B}(u)=u
or the Robin boundary condition
\mathcal{B}(u)=\partial_{\nu}u+\gamma u
with
\nu
the unit exterior normal on
\partial D
and
\gamma\in C^{\infty}(\partial D,{\mathbb{C}})
. Clearly, when
\gamma=0
the Robin boundary condition is reduced to the Neumann boundary condition.

Throughout the paper, we always assume that
0
is not a Dirichlet eigenvalue of
\Delta+q
in
\Omega\setminus\overline{D}
with the boundary condition
\mathcal{B}
on
\partial D
. Under this assumption, it is known that problem (
1
) is well-posed in
H^{1}(\Omega\setminus\overline{D})
. Therefore, the so-called Dirichlet-to-Neumann (DtN) map
\Lambda:f\mapsto\partial_{\nu}u|_{\partial\Omega}
is well-defined and acts as a bounded linear operator from
H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
into
H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega)
. In this paper, we will study the inverse problem of recovering
(q,D,\mathcal{B})
from the local knowledge of
\Lambda
. In particular, let
\Gamma\subset\partial\Omega
be a nonempty open subset. We shall prove:

Theorem 1
.

For
(q_{i},D_{i},\mathcal{B}_{i})
,
i=1,2
, suppose
\Lambda_{1}f=\Lambda_{2}f
on
\Gamma
for all
f\in H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
with
{\rm{supp}}f\subset\Gamma
,
then

\displaystyle D^{\alpha}q_{1}=D^{\alpha}q_{2}~on~\Gamma~{\text{for}}~all~|\alpha|\geq 0.

Moreover, if
q
is analytic, we obtain the following global uniqueness.

Theorem 2
.

For
(q_{i},D_{i},\mathcal{B}_{i})
,
i=1,2
, suppose
\Lambda_{1}f=\Lambda_{2}f
on
\Gamma
for all
f\in H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
with
{\rm{supp}}f\subset\Gamma
, and further that
q_{i}
is analytic in
\overline{\Omega}\setminus D_{i}
,
i=1,2
, then
D_{1}=D_{2}=D
,
q_{1}=q_{2}
in
\Omega\setminus\overline{D}
and
\mathcal{B}_{1}=\mathcal{B}_{2}
.

The inverse boundary value problem was initiated by Calderón
[
3
]
, where he posed the question of recovering the conductivity of a medium by making voltage and current measurements at the boundary. Since then, substantial progress has been made on this problem in the case where the measurements are on the whole boundary. We refer the readers to some fundamental papers like
[
1
,
11
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
. Recently, the partial data case has arisen more intersets with relating work such as
[
2
,
8
,
10
,
6
]
. Nevertheless, due to the great difficulty brought by the incompleteness of the information of the DtN map, it still remains widely open for the general partial data problem and we refer to the survey
[
9
]
for more details.

The global uniqueness usually along with the boundary determination results, such as
[
1
,
11
,
12
,
17
,
13
]
. Kohn and Vogelius
[
11
,
12
]
uniquely determined the conductivity and all of its derivatives at the boundary using the highly oscillatory solutions, while Uhlmann and his collaborators
[
17
,
13
]
proved the similar results applying the methods of microlocal analysis by computing the full symbol of the DtN map. Alessandrini
[
1
]
constructed solutions with isolated singularity of aribitrary order to obtain the uniqueness and stability results for the boundary determination of Lipschitz domains and coefficients.

Compared with the previous work, we consider the case with somewhat more generality, i.e.,
(D,\mathcal{B})
is unknown. For the inverse problem with the presence of the obstacle D, we refer some relevant work
[
7
,
5
,
18
]
. In this paper, we propose a novel and primary method to give a new proof and slightly improve the uniqueness result on the boundary determination of the potential
q
when
(D,\mathcal{B})
is unknown. Particularly, we use only the local Cauchy data of the fundamental solution to show that the potential
q
is uniquely determined to infinite order at the boundary, disregarding the unknown obstacle. Our methods mainly depends on the detailed singularity analysis for certain singular solutions and some other relating functions. Specifically, we utilize the Green’s representatioin to derive the full singularity of the solutions, and rigorously study the derivatives of
1/\sqrt{s^{2}+t^{2}}
and some associated singular integrals to obtain the singularity of the volume potential of the fundamental solution. These explicit singularity allows us to ignore the unknown obstacle and thus we can directly compare the singularity of solutions corresponding to different potentials
q
locally at the boundary. Then any diffenence of
q
at the boundary would contradict with the singularity obtained previously and hence the boundary determination result follows. Furthermore, when the potential
q
is known to be analytic, we use the Green’s functions to deduce the global uniqueness, i.e., the unique determination of
(q,D,\mathcal{B})
. It is noted that we are only using the solutions with the Dirichlet data simply being
1/|x-z|
, the fundamental solution of the principal term of the concerning differential operator
\Delta+q
, while in
[
11
,
12
]
some cleverly chosen sequences of Dirichlet data are utilized. Further, in
[
1
]
Alessandrini constructed solutions behaving like
1/|x-z|^{m}
for aribitrary
m
, while we are deriving the singularity in the
H^{m}
sense for aribitrary
m
for fixed solutions behaving like
1/|x-z|
, which is more handleable when extends to other equations. In fact, in a forthcoming paper we will show that our methods can be applied to derive the boundary determination result for the Maxwell equation using local data. In summary, the present study propose a rather systematic way for the boundary determination of the linear differential operator whose principal term has known fundamental solution, which is a fairly general situation.

2
Some preparations

In this section, we show some technical results about singularity for the preparations of the main theorems. We shall employ a novel method to derive the complete description of the singularity of the solution to problem (
1
) with the boundary data being the fundamental solution to the Laplacian. The method mainly depends on the Green’s representation of the solutions and thus can be applied to many other cases. Then we compute the Sobolev norms at the boundary for the volume potential of fundamental solution. In particular, we show that these norms would explode, which plays a significant role in later proof.

2.1
Singularity of solutions

We first introduce some useful notations. Let
B_{r}(x)
denote the open ball centered at
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
with radius
r>0
. For balls centered at the origin, we abbreviate by
B_{r}
. Denote by
\Phi(x,y)
the fundamental solution to the Laplacian in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, which is

\displaystyle\Phi(x,y)=\frac{1}{4\pi|x-y|},~~~\quad x\neq y.

Define the single- and double-layer potentials by

\displaystyle(\mathcal{S}\varphi)(x)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\partial\Omega}\Phi(x,y)\varphi(y)ds(y),~~x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\partial\Omega,

\displaystyle(\mathcal{D}\varphi)(x)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial\Phi(x,y)}{\partial\nu(y)}\varphi(y)ds(y),~~x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\partial\Omega.

Also, we give the definitions of the associated boundary integral operators, for
x\in\partial\Omega
,

\displaystyle(S\varphi)(x)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\partial\Omega}\Phi(x,y)\varphi(y)ds(y),

\displaystyle(K\varphi)(x)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial\Phi(x,y)}{\partial\nu(y)}\varphi(y)ds(y),

\displaystyle(K^{\prime}\varphi)(x)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial\Phi(x,y)}{\partial\nu(x)}\varphi(y)ds(y),

\displaystyle(T\varphi)(x)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu(x)}\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial\Phi(x,y)}{\partial\nu(y)}\varphi(y)ds(y).

Further, the volume potential is given by

\displaystyle(\mathcal{G}_{q}\varphi)(x)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Omega}\Phi(x,y)q(y)\varphi(y)dy,~~~x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}.

The restriction of the volume potential
\mathcal{G}_{q}\varphi
on the boundary
\partial\Omega
and the corresponding normal derivative on
\partial\Omega
are denoted by
G_{q}\varphi
and
\partial_{\nu}G_{q}\varphi
, respectively. Since we require
\partial\Omega\in C^{\infty}
and
q\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega},{\mathbb{C}})
, the following mapping properties hold (see details in
[
4
,
14
]
).

Theorem 3
.

For
s\geq 0
, the potentials
\mathcal{S}:H^{s-1/2}(\partial\Omega)\rightarrow H^{s+1}(\Omega)
,
\mathcal{D}:H^{s+1/2}(\partial\Omega)\rightarrow H^{s+1}(\Omega)
and
\mathcal{G}_{q}:H^{s}(\Omega)\rightarrow H^{s+2}(\Omega)
are bounded. Moreover, the boundary integral operators
S,K,K^{\prime}:H^{s-1/2}(\partial\Omega)\rightarrow H^{s+1/2}(\partial\Omega)
and
T:H^{s+1/2}(\partial\Omega)\rightarrow H^{s-1/2}(\partial\Omega)
are bounded for
s\geq 0
.

Theorem 4
.

For
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
and
\delta>0
small enough, define
z_{j}:=x_{0}+(\delta/j)\nu(x_{0})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
. Let

be the unique solution to problem
(

)

. Set
\varphi_{-1,j}=\varphi_{0,j}=0

\psi_{-1,j}=\psi_{0,j}=0
. For
m\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
, define

\displaystyle\varphi_{m+1,j}:=2K^{\prime}\varphi_{m,j}+2\partial_{\nu}G_{q}(\Phi(\cdot,z_{j})+\psi_{m-1,j})~~\qquad{\rm on}~\partial\Omega,

\displaystyle\psi_{m+1,j}:=\mathcal{S}\varphi_{m+1,j}+\mathcal{G}_{q}(\Phi(\cdot,z_{j})+\psi_{m-1,j})\qquad\quad\;\;\;~~{\rm in}~\overline{\Omega}.

Then we have the following estimates

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\nu}(u_{j}-\Phi(\cdot,z_{j}))-\varphi_{m,j}\|_{H^{m+1/2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C,

\displaystyle\|u_{j}-\Phi(\cdot,z_{j})-\psi_{m,j}\|_{H^{m+2}(\Omega\setminus\overline{D})}\leq C

for
m\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{-1,0\}
, where
C>0
is a constant independent of
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Proof.

Without loss of genearlity, we assume that
0
is not a Dirichlet eigenvalue of
\Delta+q
in
\Omega
. Then
\widetilde{u}_{j}
given by

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta\widetilde{u}_{j}+q\widetilde{u}_{j}=0&{\rm in}~\Omega,\\
\widetilde{u}_{j}=\Phi(\cdot,z_{j})&{\rm on}~\partial\Omega\end{array}\right.

is well-defined. Further, it can be verified that
U_{j}:=u_{j}-\widetilde{u}_{j}
in
\Omega\setminus\overline{D}
satisfies

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta U_{j}+qU_{j}=0&{\rm in}~\Omega\setminus\overline{D},\\
U_{j}=0&{\rm on}~\partial\Omega,\\
\mathcal{B}(U_{j})=-\mathcal{B}(\widetilde{u}_{j})&{\rm on}~\partial D.\end{array}\right.

We claim that for
m\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{-1,0\}
,

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\nu}(\widetilde{u}_{j}-\Phi(\cdot,z_{j}))-\varphi_{m,j}\|_{H^{m+1/2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C,

(2.8)

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{u}_{j}-\Phi(\cdot,z_{j})-\psi_{m,j}\|_{H^{m+2}(\Omega)}\leq C

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\Delta+q)(\widetilde{u}_{j}-\Phi(\cdot,z_{j}))=-q\Phi(\cdot,z_{j})&{\rm in}~\Omega,\\

Since

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}_{j}(x)-\Phi(x,z_{j})=[\mathcal{S}(\partial_{\nu}(\widetilde{u}_{j}-\Phi(\cdot,z_{j})))](x)+(\mathcal{G}_{q}\widetilde{u}_{j})(x),~~~x\in\Omega.

\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}(\widetilde{u}_{j}-\Phi(\cdot,z_{j}))-\varphi_{l+1,j}=\;

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}[\partial_{\nu}(\widetilde{u}_{j}-\Phi(\cdot,z_{j}))-\varphi_{l+1,j})]

\displaystyle+\mathcal{G}_{q}(\widetilde{u}_{j}-\Phi(\cdot,z_{j})-\psi_{l-1,j}).

Due to the positive distance between

and

, from (

) and the interior regularity of elliptic equations we see that

for all

, which implies by the trace theorem that

) and the global elliptic regularity that

2.2
The exploding norms

In this subsection, we show that the Sobolev norms of the volume potential of fundamental solution at the boundary would explode by deriving the explicit expression of the high order derivatives for
1/\sqrt{s^{2}+t^{2}}
and investigating on the relating singular integral, which may have many other applications and deserves more study.

Lemma 6
.

For
m\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
, we have that

\displaystyle\partial_{s}^{m}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{s^{2}+t^{2}}}\right)=\frac{P_{m}(s,t)}{\left(\sqrt{s^{2}+t^{2}}\right)^{2m+1}}.

(2.15)

Here
P_{m}
is a sequence of polynomial given by

\displaystyle P_{2m}(s,t)=\sum_{i=0}^{m}a_{2m,2i}s^{2i}t^{2(m-i)},~~~P_{2m+1}(s,t)=\sum_{i=0}^{m}a_{2m+1,2i+1}s^{2i+1}t^{2(m-i)}

with
m\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
, where

\displaystyle a_{2m,2i}\;

\displaystyle=(-1)^{m-i}2^{2i-2m}(2m)!C_{2m}^{m-i}C_{m+i}^{m-i},

\displaystyle a_{2m+1,2i+1}\;

\displaystyle=(-1)^{m-i+1}2^{2i-2m}(2m+1)!C_{2m+1}^{m-i}C_{m+i+1}^{m-i}

for
m\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
and
0\leq i\leq m
.
(
Here we set
C_{0}^{0}=1
.
)
Moreover, the following identities hold

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{m}\frac{a_{2m,2i}}{C_{2m-1}^{m-i}}=-\frac{2}{2m+1}\sum_{i=0}^{m}\frac{a_{2m+1,2i+1}}{C_{2m}^{m-i}}=\frac{(2m)!}{2^{2m-1}},

(2.16)

\displaystyle(m+1)\sum_{i=0}^{m}\frac{a_{2m,2i}}{C_{2m}^{m-i}}=-\sum_{i=0}^{m}\frac{a_{2m+1,2i+1}}{C_{2m+1}^{m-i}}=\frac{(2m+2)!}{2^{2m+1}}

(2.17)

for
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Proof.

We first show (
2.15
), which is clear when
m=0,1
. Now we assume (
2.15
) holds for
m\leq l
with
l\geq 1
. Calculating straightly yields that

\displaystyle\partial_{s}^{l+1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{s^{2}+t^{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\partial_{s}\left(\frac{P_{l}(s,t)}{\left(\sqrt{s^{2}+t^{2}}\right)^{2l+1}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(\sqrt{s^{2}+t^{2}}\right)^{2l+1}\partial_{s}P_{l}(s,t)-(2l+1)s\left(\sqrt{s^{2}+t^{2}}\right)^{2l-1}P_{l}(s,t)}{\left(\sqrt{s^{2}+t^{2}}\right)^{4l+2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(s^{2}+t^{2})\partial_{s}P_{l}(s,t)-(2l+1)sP_{l}(s,t)}{\left(\sqrt{s^{2}+t^{2}}\right)^{2l+3}},

which implies the recurrence formula

\displaystyle P_{l+1}(s,t)=(s^{2}+t^{2})\partial_{s}P_{l}(s,t)-(2l+1)sP_{l}(s,t).

(2.18)

If
l=2l_{0}
is even, by the explicit form of
P_{2l_{0}}
we see that

\displaystyle P_{2l_{0}+1}(s,t)\;

\displaystyle=(s^{2}+t^{2})\sum_{i=1}^{l_{0}}2ia_{2l_{0},2i}s^{2i-1}t^{2(l_{0}-i)}-(4l_{0}+1)s\sum_{i=0}^{l_{0}}a_{2l_{0},2i}s^{2i}t^{2(l_{0}-i)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{l_{0}-1}[(2i-4l_{0}-1)a_{2l_{0},2i}+(2i+2)a_{2l_{0},2i+2}]s^{2i+1}t^{2(l_{0}-i)}

\displaystyle\quad-(2l_{0}+1)a_{2l_{0},2l_{0}}s^{2l_{0}+1}.

It then follows from the basic calculation that for
0\leq i\leq l_{0}-1

\displaystyle\quad(2i-4l_{0}-1)a_{2l_{0},2i}+(2i+2)a_{2l_{0},2i+2}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{l_{0}-i+1}2^{2i-2l_{0}}(2l_{0}+1)!C_{2l_{0}+1}^{l_{0}-i}C_{l_{0}+i+1}^{l_{0}-i}=a_{2l_{0}+1,2i+1},

and
-(2l_{0}+1)a_{2l_{0},2l_{0}}=-(2l_{0}+1)!=a_{2l_{0}+1,2l_{0}+1}
, and thus

\displaystyle P_{l+1}(s,t)=P_{2l_{0}+1}(s,t)=\sum_{i=0}^{l_{0}}a_{2l_{0}+1,2i+1}s^{2i+1}t^{2(l_{0}-i)}.

Through the same way we can deal with the case that
l
is odd. Therefore, the induction arguments show that (
2.15
) holds for
m\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
.

Next we verify the identities (
2.16
) and (
2.17
). To this end, we derive a recurrence formula different from (

\displaystyle\partial_{s}^{m}\left((s^{2}+t^{2})\frac{1}{\sqrt{s^{2}+t^{2}}}\right)=\partial_{s}^{m-1}\left(\frac{s}{\sqrt{s^{2}+t^{2}}}\right).

and

\displaystyle\partial_{s}^{m-1}\left(\frac{s}{\sqrt{s^{2}+t^{2}}}\right)=\frac{sP_{m-1}(s,t)+(m-1)(s^{2}+t^{2})P_{m-2}(s,t)}{\left(\sqrt{s^{2}+t^{2}}\right)^{2m-1}},

which indicates that

\displaystyle P_{m}(s,t)+(2m-1)sP_{m-1}(s,t)+(m-1)^{2}(s^{2}+t^{2})P_{m-2}(s,t)=0.

(2.19)

Combining (
2.18
) and (
2.19

\displaystyle\partial_{s}P_{m}(s,t)=-m^{2}P_{m-1}(s,t),~~~m\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(2.20)

Utilizing the expression of

\displaystyle a_{2m,2i}\;

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}a_{2m-1,1},&i=0,\\

\displaystyle a_{2m+1,2i+1}\;

Proof.

We first consider the case
m_{0}=0
. From the definition of
\psi_{1,j}^{(i)}
,
i=1,2
, it can be verified that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta(\psi_{1,j}^{(1)}-\psi_{1,j}^{(2)})=-(q_{1}-q_{2})\Phi(\cdot,z_{j})&{\rm in}~\Omega,\\
\psi_{1,j}^{(1)}-\psi_{1,j}^{(2)}=\psi_{1,j}^{(1)}-\psi_{1,j}^{(2)}&{\rm on}~\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Since
q_{1}=q_{2}
on
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
, we know that
(q_{1}-q_{2})\Phi(\cdot,z_{j})
are uniformly bounded in
H^{1}(\Omega)
for
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
. By Remark
5
, we also have
\|\psi_{1,j}^{(1)}-\psi_{1,j}^{(2)}\|_{H^{5/2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C
. Therefore, from the regularity of Poisson’s equation, it follows that
\|\psi_{1,j}^{(1)}-\psi_{1,j}^{(2)}\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}\leq C
. Further, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle D^{\alpha}q_{1}\psi_{1,j}^{(1)}-D^{\alpha}q_{2}\psi_{1,j}^{(2)}=D^{\alpha}(q_{1}-q_{2})\psi_{1,j}^{(1)}+D^{\alpha}q_{2}(\psi_{1,j}^{(1)}-\psi_{1,j}^{(2)}).

(3.4)

Since
q_{1},q_{2}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega},{\mathbb{C}})
, it then suffices to show that

\displaystyle\|D^{\alpha}(q_{1}-q_{2})\psi_{1,j}^{(1)}\|_{H^{3-|\alpha|}(\Omega)}\leq C

(3.5)

for
|\alpha|\leq 2
. Again by Remark
5
, we have that
\|\psi_{1,j}^{(1)}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C
, which clearly implies the estimates (
3.5
) holds when
|\alpha|=1,2
. Direct calculation yields that

\displaystyle\Delta[(q_{1}-q_{2})\psi_{1,j}^{(1)}]=\Delta(q_{1}-q_{2})\psi_{1,j}^{(1)}+2{\rm NewA}(q_{1}-q_{2})\cdot{\rm NewA}\psi_{1,j}^{(1)}-(q_{1}-q_{2})(q_{1}\Phi(\cdot,z_{j}))

in
\Omega
. Since
\|(q_{1}-q_{2})\psi_{1,j}^{(1)}\|_{H^{5/2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C
, we obtain that (
3.5
) with
|\alpha|=0
holds.

Now we finish the proof by induction. Suppose (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) hold when
m_{0}\leq l
with
l\geq 0
. We shall prove these estimates when
m_{0}=l+1
. It is deduced from the definition of
\psi_{l+2,j}^{(i)}
,
i=1,2
that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta(\psi_{l+2,j}^{(1)}-\psi_{l+2,j}^{(2)})=-(q_{1}\psi_{l,j}^{(1)}-q_{2}\psi_{l,j}^{(2)})-(q_{1}-q_{2})\Phi(\cdot,z_{j})&{\rm in}~\Omega,\\
\psi_{l+2,j}^{(1)}-\psi_{l+2,j}^{(2)}=\psi_{l+2,j}^{(1)}-\psi_{l+2,j}^{(2)}&{\rm on}~\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Since
D^{\alpha}q_{1}=D^{\alpha}q_{2}
on
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
when
|\alpha|\leq l+1
, it follows from the Taylor’s expansion that for
x\in\Omega

\displaystyle D^{\alpha}[(q_{1}-q_{2})\Phi(x,z_{j})]\leq C,~~~|\alpha|\leq l+1,

\displaystyle D^{\alpha}[(q_{1}-q_{2})\Phi(x,z_{j})]\leq\frac{C}{|x-z_{j}|},~~~|\alpha|=l+2,

which implies that
(q_{1}-q_{2})\Phi(\cdot,z_{j})
is uniformly bounded in
H^{l+2}(\Omega)
for
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
. By Remark
5
, we see
\|\psi_{l+2,j}^{(1)}-\psi_{l+2,j}^{(2)}\|_{H^{l+7/2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C
. If
l=0
, clearly we have
\|q_{1}\psi_{l,j}^{(1)}-q_{2}\psi_{l,j}^{(2)}\|_{H^{l+2}(\Omega)}\leq C
. If
l>0
, from the induction hypothesis, we know the estimate (
3.2
) with
m_{0}=l-1
and
|\alpha|=0
holds, which is
\|q_{1}\psi_{l,j}^{(1)}-q_{2}\psi_{l,j}^{(2)}\|_{H^{l+2}(\Omega)}\leq C
. Therefore, the regularity of Poisson’s equation gives
\|\psi_{l+2,j}^{(1)}-\psi_{l+2,j}^{(2)}\|_{H^{l+4}(\Omega)}\leq C
. In other words, (
3.1
) holds when
m_{0}=l+1
. In view of (
3.4
), it is enough to prove that

\displaystyle\|D^{\alpha}(q_{1}-q_{2})\psi_{l+2,j}^{(1)}\|_{H^{l+4-|\alpha|}(\Omega)}\leq C

(3.7)

for
|\alpha|\leq l+3
. Since
\|\psi_{l+2,j}^{(1)}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C
, again we see that
(
3.7
)
holds for
|\alpha|=l+2,l+3
. Next we show that if (
3.7
) holds for
l_{0}\leq|\alpha|\leq l+3
with
0<l_{0}\leq l+2
, then (
3.7
) is satisfied when
|\alpha|=l_{0}-1
, which clearly completes the proof. For
|\gamma|=l_{0}-1\leq l+1
, it is derived that

\displaystyle\Delta[D^{\gamma}(q_{1}-q_{2})\psi_{l+2,j}^{(1)}]=\;

\displaystyle\Delta D^{\gamma}(q_{1}-q_{2})\psi_{l+2,j}^{(1)}+2{\rm NewA}D^{\gamma}(q_{1}-q_{2})\cdot{\rm NewA}\psi_{l+2,j}^{(1)}

\displaystyle-D^{\gamma}(q_{1}-q_{2})(q_{1}\Phi(\cdot,z_{j})+\psi_{l,j}^{(1)})

in
\Omega
and
\|D^{\gamma}(q_{1}-q_{2})\psi_{l+2,j}^{(1)}\|_{H^{l+7/2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C
by Remark
5
. Utilizing the estimates (
3.7
) with
|\alpha|=l_{0}+1
, we deduce that
\|\Delta D^{\gamma}(q_{1}-q_{2})\psi_{l+2,j}^{(1)}\|_{H^{l+3-l_{0}}(\Omega)}\leq C

\displaystyle\partial_{i}D^{\gamma}(q_{1}-q_{2})\partial_{i}\psi_{l+2,j}^{(1)}=\partial_{i}(\partial_{i}D^{\gamma}(q_{1}-q_{2})\psi_{l+2,j}^{(1)})-\partial_{i}^{2}D^{\gamma}(q_{1}-q_{2})\psi_{l+2,j}^{(1)}

with

) with

, it further follows that
\|{\rm NewA}D^{\gamma}(q_{1}-q_{2})\cdot{\rm NewA}\psi_{l+2,j}^{(1)}\|_{H^{l+3-l_{0}}(\Omega)}\leq C
. Since
D^{\beta}[D^{\gamma}(q_{1}-q_{2})]=0
on
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
with
|\beta|\leq l+2-l_{0}
, we know that
D^{\gamma}(q_{1}-q_{2})q_{1}\Phi(\cdot,z_{j})
is uniformly bounded in

for

. Moreover, the estimate (
3.2
) with
m_{0}=l-1
and
|\alpha|=l_{0}-1
tells that
\|D^{\gamma}(q_{1}-q_{2})\psi_{l,j}^{(1)}\|_{H^{l+3-l_{0}}(\Omega)}\leq C
. Thus, by the regularity of Poisson’s equation, we conclude that (

) holds when
|\alpha|=l_{0}-1

Proof.

(of Theorem

)
By Theorem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta G^{(i)}(x,y)+q_{i}G^{(i)}(x,y)=\delta_{y}&{\rm in}~\Omega\setminus\overline{D}_{i},\\
G^{(i)}(x,y)=0&{\rm on}~\partial\Omega,\\
\mathcal{B}_{i}(G^{(i)}(x,y))=0&{\rm on}~\partial D_{i}.\end{array}\right.

It is well known that problem (
3
) is well-posed with

and
G^{(i)}(x,y)=G^{(i)}(y,x)
for

\Lambda_{1}=\Lambda_{2}

,

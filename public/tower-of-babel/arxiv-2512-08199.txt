Unitarity of highest weight Harish-Chandra modules and smoothness of Schubert varieties
Zhanqiang Bai, William Q. Erickson, Markus Hunziker, Jing Jiang

Abstract
. Let G ℝ G_{\mathbb{R}} be a Lie group of Hermitian type, and L ⁡ ( λ ) L(\lambda) a highest weight Harish-Chandra module of G ℝ G_{\mathbb{R}} with highest weight λ \lambda . In this article, we exhibit a bijection between the set of connected Dynkin subdiagrams containing the noncompact simple root and the set of unitary highest weight modules L ⁡ ( − w ​ ρ − ρ ) L(-w\rho-\rho) , where ρ \rho is half the sum of positive roots. We find that L ⁡ ( − w ​ ρ − ρ ) L(-w\rho-\rho) is unitary if and only if the Schubert variety X ⁡ ( w ) X(w) is smooth. We also give the cardinality of the set of unitary highest weight modules L ⁡ ( − w ​ ρ − ρ ) L(-w\rho-\rho) for each Kazhdan–Lusztig right cell.

1.
Introduction

Let
G_{\mathbb{R}}
be a connected noncompact simple Lie group with finite center, and let
K_{\mathbb{R}}
be a maximal compact subgroup of
G_{\mathbb{R}}
. Denote by
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R}}=\mathrm{Lie}(G_{\mathbb{R}})
the Lie algebra of
G_{\mathbb{R}}
. Harish-Chandra showed in
[
25
, §1-2]
that infinite-dimensional highest weight Harish-Chandra modules for
G_{\mathbb{R}}
exist precisely when
G_{\mathbb{R}}
is of Hermitian type, that is, when
(G_{\mathbb{R}},K_{\mathbb{R}})
is a Hermitian symmetric pair. The unitarity of such modules has been extensively studied, with the complete classification obtained independently by Enright–Howe–Wallach
[
19
]
and Jakobsen
[
29
]
. A more elementary and uniform approach was later given by Enright–Joseph
[
22
]
, avoiding much of the earlier case-by-case analysis. More recently, another approach has been developed in
[
37
,
38
]
that revisits the classification of unitary highest weight modules using slightly different methods from those of
[
19
]
and
[
29
]
;
meanwhile, a simple uniform characterization of unitarity for highest weight Harish-Chandra modules with prescribed associated variety was established in
[
3
, Thm. 1.1]
, which was expressed directly in terms of the highest weight.
In this paper, we will present a new characterization of unitary highest weight Harish-Chandra modules with trivial infinitesimal character (see Theorem
1.1
).

Throughout, we assume
(G_{\mathbb{R}},K_{\mathbb{R}})
is a Hermitian symmetric pair. Let
G
and
K
denote the complexification of
G_{\mathbb{R}}
and
K_{\mathbb{R}}
, respectively, and let
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{k})
denote the complexified Lie algebras of
(G_{\mathbb{R}},K_{\mathbb{R}})
. As a
K
-representation,
\mathfrak{g}
decomposes as

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{p}^{-}\oplus\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}^{+}.

Let
\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathfrak{k}
be a Cartan subalgebra, which is also a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
. Fix a Borel subalgebra
\mathfrak{b}\supset\mathfrak{k}
, and let
\Phi
and
\Phi(\mathfrak{k})
be the root systems of
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
and
(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{h})
, respectively. Choose a set of positive roots
\Phi^{+}(\mathfrak{k})
, and define a positive system for
\Phi
by

\Phi^{+}:=\Phi^{+}(\mathfrak{k})\cup\Phi(\mathfrak{p}^{+}).

For a
\Phi^{+}(\mathfrak{k})
-dominant integral weight
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, let
F(\lambda)
be the irreducible
\mathfrak{k}
-module with highest weight
\lambda
. Viewing
F(\lambda)
as a
\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}^{+}
-module (with the nilradical acting trivially), define the induced module

N(\lambda):=U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{q})}F(\lambda).

Let
L(\lambda)
denote the irreducible quotient of
N(\lambda)
.
Each such
L(\lambda)
is said to be a
highest weight Harish-Chandra module
for
\mathfrak{g}
(or for
G_{\mathbb{R}}
). If
L(\lambda)\neq N(\lambda)
, then we say that
\lambda
is a
reduction point
.

Let
w_{c}
and
w_{0}
be the longest elements of the Weyl groups
W(\mathfrak{k})
and
W:=W(\Phi)
, respectively. For
w\in W
, define
\tilde{w}:=w_{c}ww_{0}
. Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G_{\mathbb{R}}
be a Lie group of Hermitian type, with complexified Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
\Gamma
be the Dynkin diagram of
\mathfrak{g}
, with
\alpha
the noncompact simple root. Then there is a bijection

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\textup{connected Dynkin subdiagrams of }\Gamma\\
\textup{containing the noncompact simple root }\alpha\end{array}\right\}\longleftrightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\textup{unitary highest weight Harish-Chandra}\\
\textup{modules with infinitesimal character }\rho\end{array}\right\},

where the empty subdiagram is included in the left-hand set. Explicitly, a subdiagram
\Gamma^{\prime}\subseteq\Gamma
corresponds to the module
L(w(\Gamma^{\prime})\rho-\rho)=L(-w_{c}x\rho-\rho)
, where
w(\Gamma^{\prime})=\tilde{x}
is defined as in Lemma
2.8
. Conversely, if
L(-w\rho-\rho)
is unitary, then the corresponding subdiagram has simple roots

\mathrm{supp}(x):=\{\alpha_{i}\in\Pi\mid s_{i}=s_{\alpha_{i}}\textup{ appears in a reduced expression of }x\},

where
x=w_{c}w
.

Let
B\subset G
be the Borel subgroup corresponding to
\mathfrak{b}
, giving a triangular decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{n}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}^{-}
. Fix a parabolic subgroup
Q
with
B\subset Q\subset G
and
\mathrm{Lie}(Q_{\mathrm{I}})=\mathfrak{q}:=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}^{+}
. The flag variety
G/B
decomposes into Schubert cells
BwB/B
, indexed by
w\in W
. Their closures
X(w):=\overline{BwB/B}
are Schubert varieties, which are fundamental objects in algebraic geometry, representation theory, and combinatorics. A Schubert variety
X(w)
is
smooth
if it is smooth as an algebraic variety, and
rationally smooth
if its Poincaré polynomial
p_{w}(t)=\sum_{v\leq w}t^{\ell(v)}
is palindromic, where
\leq
is the Bruhat order and
\ell(v)
the length of
v
.

The study of smoothness and rational smoothness of Schubert varieties has been a central theme since the foundational work of Lakshmibai and Sandhya
[
33
]
, who gave the following combinatorial criterion in type A:
X(w)\subset\operatorname{SL}(n,\mathbb{C})/B
is smooth if and only if the permutation
w\in S_{n}
avoids the patterns
3412
and
4231
. Billey
[
8
]
extended this to other classical types, and Billey–Postnikov
[
10
]
introduced a uniform approach via root subsystems, showing that (rational) smoothness of
X(w)\subset G/B
is equivalent to avoiding a finite list of patterns from stellar root subsystems.

Let
W^{\mathfrak{k}}
(resp.,
{}^{\mathfrak{k}}W
) denote the set of minimal length coset representatives of
W/W(\mathfrak{k})
(resp.,
W(\mathfrak{k})\backslash W
). Kostant
[
32
]
generalized Schubert cell decompositions to
G/Q
, with cells parametrized by
W^{\mathfrak{k}}
. When
G/Q
is a cominuscule flag variety (i.e., a Hermitian symmetric space),
W^{\mathfrak{k}}
admits a description via generalized Young diagrams
[
40
,
21
]
. For classical
G
and cominuscule
G/Q
, Lakshmibai–Weyman
[
34
]
described the singular locus of Schubert varieties in terms of partitions. Robles
[
42
]
gave a uniform description of the singular locus of a Schubert
variety
X_{Q}(w)\subset G/Q
. Hong
[
26
]
established a bijection between connected subdiagrams containing the noncompact simple root and smooth Schubert varieties in
G/Q
.

By Brylinski–Kashiwara
[
15
, Prop. 6.4]
, the support of the localization of
L(-w\rho-\rho)
is the Schubert variety
X(w)\subset G/B
. Combining this with a result of Richmond–Zainoulline
[
41
]
, we obtain the following corollary to Theorem
1.1
.

Corollary 1.2
.

With all notation as in Theorem
1.1
, let
L(-w\rho-\rho)
be a highest weight Harish-Chandra module. Then
L(-w\rho-\rho)
is unitary if and only if the Schubert variety
X(w)\subset G/B
is smooth, which is equivalent to the smoothness of
X_{Q}(x^{-1})\subset G/Q
, where
x=w_{c}w\in{}^{\mathfrak{k}}W
.

From Barchini–Zierau
[
6
]
, it seems that one direction of Corollary
1.2
was already known, namely, that the support of the localization of a unitary highest weight Harish-Chandra module
L(-w\rho-\rho)
is smooth.
The converse, however, seems to be new.
To determine the unitarity of
L(-w\rho-\rho)
, we have implemented an algorithm in Python, available at

https://github.com/JingJiang-web/unitary-for-Hermitian-type
.

When
\Gamma^{\prime}
is a connected subdiagram of
\Gamma
, the module
L(w(\Gamma^{\prime})\rho-\rho)
is called a
special Kostant module
in
[
12
]
; we refer to such modules in Theorem
1.1
as
special Kostant modules of Hermitian type
. Kostant modules are those simple highest weight modules admitting a Bernstein–Gelfand–Gelfand (BGG) resolution
[
12
]
. Boe–Hunziker
[
12
]
gave a classification of Kostant modules in regular blocks for maximal parabolics in the simply laced types. For Hermitian symmetric pairs, they found a uniform characterization of Kostant modules in regular blocks via subdiagrams of some Dynkin diagram containing the noncompact simple root. Kostant modules are significant, among other reasons, because every unitary highest weight module is a Kostant module; see Theorem
2.25
below, from Enright
[
18
]
. Thus one can use certain properties of Kostant modules to study unitary highest weight modules; see, for example,
[
20
,
23
,
24
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In §
2
, we prepare some necessary preliminaries. In §
3
, we give a uniform proof of Theorem
1.1
for simply laced types. In §
4
, we give a case-by-case proof of Theorem
1.1
for non-simply laced types by using the characterization of Kostant modules given by Boe–Hunziker
[
12
]
. In §
5
, by using the formula of Gelfand–Kirillov dimensions of unitary highest weight modules in Bai–Hunziker
[
2
]
, we count such unitary highest
weight modules with the trivial infinitesimal character for each Kazhdan–Lusztig right cell. Throughout, we follow the notation in
[
19
]
in writing coordinates for root
systems and other relevant data.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a complex simple Lie algebra of rank
n
. We fix a Cartan subalgebra
{\mathfrak{h}}
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
, and denote
by
\Phi\subset{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
the root system of
{\mathfrak{g}}
with respect to
{\mathfrak{h}}
.
For every
\alpha\in\Phi
, we write
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha}
to denote the one-dimensional root space.
We choose a simple system
\Pi\subset\Phi
and write
\Pi=\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\}
, where the simple
roots
\alpha_{i}
are labeled according to Bourbaki
[
14
]
. This simple system determines a positive system
\Phi^{+}\subset\Phi
.
The corresponding standard Borel subalgebra is defined as

{\mathfrak{b}}:={\mathfrak{h}}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha}.

Every Borel subalgebra of
{\mathfrak{g}}
is conjugate to
{\mathfrak{b}}
under
the action of the adjoint group of
{\mathfrak{g}}
.

2.1.
Parabolic subalgebras

A
parabolic subalgebra

{\mathfrak{q}}
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
is a subalgebra containing some Borel subalgebra of
{\mathfrak{g}}
.
If
{\mathfrak{q}}
contains the fixed Borel subalgebra
{\mathfrak{b}}
, we say that
{\mathfrak{q}}
is a
standard

parabolic subalgebra. Any parabolic subalgebra
{\mathfrak{q}}
of
{\mathfrak{g}}

is conjugate to a standard parabolic subalgebra under
the action of the adjoint group of
{\mathfrak{g}}
.
There is a one-to-one correspondence between subsets of

\Pi
and standard parabolic subalgebras of
{\mathfrak{g}}
as follows.
For
\mathrm{I}\subset\Pi
, define a root system
\Phi_{\mathrm{I}}
by

\Phi_{\mathrm{I}}:=\Phi\cap\sum_{\alpha\in\mathrm{I}}\mathbb{Z}\alpha.

Next define a reductive subalgebra
{\mathfrak{m}}_{\mathrm{I}}
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
and a nilpotent subalgebra
{\mathfrak{u}}_{\mathrm{I}}

of
{\mathfrak{g}}
by

{\mathfrak{m}}_{\mathrm{I}}:={\mathfrak{h}}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi_{\mathrm{I}}}{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha}\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\mathfrak{u}}_{\mathrm{I}}:=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\setminus\Phi_{\mathrm{I}}}{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha}.

Then
{\mathfrak{q}}_{\mathrm{I}}:={\mathfrak{m}}_{\mathrm{I}}\oplus{\mathfrak{u}}_{\mathrm{I}}
is a standard parabolic subalgebra of
{\mathfrak{g}}

with Levi subalgebra
{\mathfrak{m}}_{\mathrm{I}}
and nilradical
{\mathfrak{u}}_{\mathrm{I}}
.
If
|\Pi\setminus\mathrm{I}|=1
, then
{\mathfrak{q}}_{\mathrm{I}}
is called a maximal parabolic subalgebra.

To simplify notation, we often omit the subscript and write, for instance,
{\mathfrak{q}}={\mathfrak{m}}\oplus{\mathfrak{u}}
instead of

{\mathfrak{q}}_{\mathrm{I}}={\mathfrak{m}}_{\mathrm{I}}\oplus{\mathfrak{u}}_{\mathrm{I}}
. The set

\Phi({\mathfrak{u}}):=\Phi^{+}\setminus\Phi_{\mathrm{I}}

is a partially ordered subset of
\Phi^{+}
.

Now suppose we have an irreducible Hermitian symmetric pair
(G_{\mathbb{R}},K_{\mathbb{R}})
of noncompact type. Let
({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{R}},{\mathfrak{k}}_{\mathbb{R}})
be
the pair of Lie algebras of
(G_{\mathbb{R}},K_{\mathbb{R}})

{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{R}}
is a noncompact real form of
{\mathfrak{g}}
with Cartan decomposition
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{R}}={\mathfrak{k}}_{\mathbb{R}}\oplus{\mathfrak{p}}_{\mathbb{R}}
,
and we may assume that
{\mathfrak{k}}_{\mathbb{R}}
has a nontrivial center
\mathfrak{z}_{\mathbb{R}}
. We have the usual decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{p}^{-}\oplus\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}^{+}
as a
K
-representation. We say that
\mathfrak{q}:=\mathfrak{k}+\mathfrak{p}^{+}
is a parabolic subalgebra of
Hermitian type
.

If
{\mathfrak{q}}={\mathfrak{m}}\oplus{\mathfrak{u}}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}^{+}

2.2.
Minimal length coset representatives

In the following, let
(\ ,\ )
denote the nondegenerate bilinear form on
{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
induced from the Killing form of
{\mathfrak{g}}
.
For
\alpha\in\Phi
, set
\alpha^{\vee}:=2\alpha/(\alpha,\alpha)
and define
the reflection
s_{\alpha}:{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
by

s_{\alpha}(\lambda):=\lambda-(\lambda,\alpha^{\vee})\alpha
. The Weyl group
W

is generated by the simple reflections
s_{\alpha}
, for all
\alpha\in\Pi
.
The
length
of an element
w\in W
, denoted by
\ell(w)
, is the length of the shortest word representing
w

as a product of the simple reflections (called a
reduced expression
of
w
).
By
[
27
, §10.3]
,
\ell(w)
is also equal to the number of positive roots
\alpha
for which
w(\alpha)\in\Phi^{-}
.
Therefore, upon defining

\Phi_{w}:=\Phi^{+}\cap w\Phi^{-},

we have
|\Phi_{w}|=\ell(w)
. For any subset
\Psi\subset\Phi^{+}
, we define
\langle\Psi\rangle:=\sum_{\alpha\in\Psi}\alpha
.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
31
, (5.10.1)]
)
.

For every
w\in W
,

w\rho=\rho-\langle\Phi_{w}\rangle,

where
\rho:=\frac{1}{2}\langle\Phi^{+}\rangle
as usual.

Let
{\mathfrak{q}}={\mathfrak{q}}_{\mathrm{I}}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}^{+}
be a parabolic subalgebra of Hermitian type, and let
W(\mathfrak{k}):=W_{\mathrm{I}}=\langle s_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\mathrm{I}\rangle
be the Weyl group of the Levi subalgebra
{\mathfrak{k}}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Following Kostant
[
31
, (5.13.1)]
, define

{}^{\mathfrak{k}}W:=\{w\in W\mid\Phi_{w}\subset\Phi(\mathfrak{p}^{+})\}.

For later reference, we also define
W^{\mathfrak{k}}:=\{w^{-1}\mid w\in{}^{\mathfrak{k}}W\}
.

Proposition 2.3

(
[
31
, Prop. 5.13]
)
.

Every element
w\in W
can be uniquely written in the form

w=uv
, where
u\in W(\mathfrak{k})
and
v\in{}^{\mathfrak{k}}W
. Furthermore, if
w=uv

is such a decomposition, then
\ell(w)=\ell(u)+\ell(v)
.
Similarly,
w
can be uniquely written in the form

w=vu
, where
u\in W(\mathfrak{k})
and
v\in{W}^{{\mathfrak{k}}}
.

Corollary 2.4

(
[
21
, Cor. 3.5 and Rem. 3.6]
)
.

The set
{}^{\mathfrak{k}}W
is the set of minimal length coset representatives of
W({\mathfrak{k}})\backslash W

(i.e., the set of right cosets of
W({\mathfrak{k}})
). For
w\in W
, the following are equivalent:

(i)

\Phi_{w}\subset\Phi(\mathfrak{p}^{+})
.

(ii)

w^{-1}\Phi^{+}({\mathfrak{k}})\subset\Phi^{+}
.

(iii)

w\rho
is
\Phi^{+}({\mathfrak{k}})
-dominant integral.

(iv)

\ell(s_{\alpha}w)=\ell(w)+1
for all
\alpha\in\mathrm{I}
.

Similarly, the set
{W}^{{\mathfrak{k}}}
is the set of minimal length coset representatives of
W/W(\mathfrak{k})
.

Let
(\mathcal{P},\leq)
be a partially ordered set. We say that a subset
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{P}
is a
lower order ideal
of
\mathcal{P}
if

x\in\mathcal{I}
and
x\geq y\in\mathcal{P}
implies
y\in\mathcal{I}
. An upper order ideal of
\mathcal{P}
is defined analogously.
In the following, we will view the sets
\Phi^{+}
and
\Phi({\mathfrak{u}})
as partially ordered sets with the usual ordering induced from the ordering on
{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
,
i.e.,
\mu\leq\lambda
if and only if
\lambda-\mu
is a (possibly empty) sum of positive roots.

For
v,w\in W
, write
v\rightarrow w
if
\ell(w)>\ell(v)
and
w=vs_{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
.
Then define
v<w
if there exists a sequence
v=w_{1}\rightarrow w_{2}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow w_{m}=w
.
The resulting partial order “
\leq
” on
W
is called the
Bruhat order
. For a detailed treatment of the Bruhat order, see
[
11
, Chap. 2]
.

Lemma 2.5

(
[
21
, Lemma 3.7, Prop. 3.8 and Cor. 3.12]
)
.

Suppose
{\mathfrak{q}}
is a parabolic subalgebra of Hermitian type. Let

. Then we have a bijection

\displaystyle{}^{\mathfrak{k}}W

\displaystyle\rightarrow\{\textup{lower order ideals in $\Phi(\mathfrak{p}^{+})$}\},

\displaystyle w

\Phi(\mathfrak{p}^{+})\setminus\Phi_{w}

.

(
[

Definition 2.9

is of Hermitian type. Define a map

f:\Phi({\mathfrak{p}}^{+})\rightarrow\Pi

as follows. For
\beta\in\Phi({\mathfrak{p}}^{+})
, the sets
\Phi({\mathfrak{p}}^{+})_{<\beta}:=\{\gamma\in\Phi({\mathfrak{p}}^{+})\mid\gamma<\beta\}
and
\Phi({\mathfrak{p}}^{+})_{\leq\beta}:=\{\gamma\in\Phi({\mathfrak{p}}^{+})\mid\gamma\leq\beta\}
are lower order ideals of
\Phi({\mathfrak{p}}^{+})
. Therefore, by Lemma

, there exist

such that

\Phi(\mathfrak{p}^{+})_{<\beta}=\Phi_{v}
and
\Phi(\mathfrak{p}^{+})_{\leq\beta}=\Phi_{w}
. Since
\Phi_{w}=\Phi_{v}\dot{\cup}\{\beta\}
, there exists a unique

f(\beta)\in\Pi
such that
w=vs_{f(\beta)}
, namely
f(\beta):=v^{-1}\beta
.

Proposition 2.10

(
[
21
, Prop. 3.21]
)
.

Suppose
{\mathfrak{q}}
is of Hermitian type. Let
w\in{}^{\mathfrak{k}}W
, and write

\Phi_{w}=\{\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\ldots,\beta_{\ell}\}

such that for every

, the set

.
Then

is a reduced expression for

. Furthermore,
w=s_{\beta_{\ell}}s_{\beta_{\ell-1}}\cdots s_{\beta_{1}}
.

Proposition

gives us a very simple way to write

.
First, fill the boxes of the generalized Young diagram of the longest element of

with the numbers

so that the box corresponding to

The upper-left box is always assigned the index of the unique noncompact simple root in

. Full details can be found in

.

2.3.
Kostant modules

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a complex simple Lie algebra and
\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{q}_{\mathrm{I}}
a parabolic subalgebra of Hermitian type. We assume that
(\Phi,\Phi_{c}):=(\Phi,\Phi_{\mathrm{I}})
corresponds to the Hermitian symmetric pair. Let
\mu\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
be regular, which means that
(\mu+\rho,\alpha^{\vee})\neq 0
for all
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
. The weight
\mu
is said to be

antidominant
if
(\mu+\rho,\alpha^{\vee})\notin\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
for all
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
.
We fix a regular infinitesimal block
\mathcal{O}_{\mu}^{\mathfrak{q}}
of the category
\mathcal{O}^{\mathfrak{q}}
. The simple modules are denoted by
L_{w}:=L(w_{c}w(\mu+\rho)-\rho)
for
w\in{}^{\mathfrak{k}}W
. Usually we may choose
\mu=-2\rho
which is also regular. Thus the simple modules are
L_{w}=L(-w_{c}w\rho-\rho)
in the category
\mathcal{O}_{-2\rho}^{\mathfrak{q}}
.

By
[
45
]
,
L(-w\rho-\rho)
is a highest weight Harish-Chandra module if and only if
-w\rho
is
\Phi^{+}(\mathfrak{k})
-dominant. From
[
13
, Prop. 2.3]
,
L(-w\rho-\rho)
is a highest weight Harish-Chandra module if and only if
w=w_{c}v
for some
v\in{}^{{\mathfrak{k}}}W
.

Definition 2.11
.

A simple module
L_{w}
in
\mathcal{O}_{-2\rho}^{\mathfrak{q}}
is called a
Kostant module
if, as a
{\mathfrak{k}}
-module,

H^{i}(\mathfrak{u},L_{w})\cong\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\leq w,\\
\ell(x,w)=i\end{subarray}}F_{x}

for
i\geq 0
,
where
F_{x}
is the finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{k}
-module with highest weight
-w_{c}x\rho-\rho
, and
\ell(x,w)=\ell(w)-\ell(x)
.

(

Let

be a complex simple algebraic group,

Definition 2.15
.

The

associated to

From Kazhdan–Lusztig
[

if the local ring

moreover,

(
[
16
, Thm. E]
)
.

The Schubert variety

(
[
17
, Cor. 4]
)
.

A Schubert variety

in
G/B
is smooth if and only if the corresponding inverse Schubert variety

is also smooth.

In simply laced types, a Schubert variety in

2.6.
Associated varieties

In
[
43
]
, Vogan introduced the notion of an associated variety for a Harish-Chandra module
M
of a real reductive Lie group
G_{\mathbb{R}}
.
Let
M
be a finitely generated
U(\mathfrak{g})
-module. Fix a finite dimensional generating space
M_{0}
of
M
. Let
U_{n}(\mathfrak{g})
be the standard filtration of
U(\mathfrak{g})
. Set
M_{n}=U_{n}(\mathfrak{g})\cdot M_{0}
and

\text{gr}(M)=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\text{gr}_{n}M,

where
\text{gr}_{n}M=M_{n}/{M_{n-1}}
. Thus
\text{gr}(M)
is a graded module of
\text{gr}(U(\mathfrak{g}))\simeq S(\mathfrak{g})
.

The
associated variety
of
M
is defined by

\mathrm{AV}(M):=\{X\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}\mid f(X)=0\text{ for all~}f\in\operatorname{Ann}_{S(\mathfrak{g})}(\operatorname{gr}M)\}.

Note that this definition is independent of the choice of
M_{0}
(see
[
36
]
, for example).
If
M_{0}
is
\mathfrak{a}
-invariant for a subalgebra
\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathfrak{g}
, then

\mathrm{AV}(M)\subset(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{a})^{*}.

(2.1)

In general, the associated
variety
\mathrm{AV}(M)
is a finite union of the closure of nilpotent
K
-orbits.
From
[
36
]
, we know that the closures of the
K
-orbits in
\mathfrak{p}^{+}
form a linear chain of varieties

\{0\}={\overline{\mathcal{O}_{0}}}\subset\overline{\mathcal{O}_{1}}\subset\cdots\subset\overline{\mathcal{O}_{r-1}}\subset\overline{\mathcal{O}_{r}}=\mathfrak{p}^{+},

where
r
is the split rank of
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R}}
or equivalently
\mathbb{R}
-rank of
G_{\mathbb{R}}
, i.e., the dimension of a Cartan subgroup of the group
G_{\mathbb{R}}
, which is also equal to the rank of the symmetric space
G_{\mathbb{R}}/K_{\mathbb{R}}
.

When
M=L(\lambda)
is a highest weight Harish-Chandra module, we can choose
L_{0}
to be the finite dimensional
U(\mathfrak{k})
-module generated by
\mathbb{C}_{\lambda}
. Then
L_{0}
is
\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}^{+}
-invariant. In view of (
2.1
),

\mathrm{AV}(L(\lambda))\subset(\mathfrak{g}/(\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}^{+}))^{*}\simeq(\mathfrak{p}^{-})^{*}\simeq\mathfrak{p}^{+},

where the last isomorphism is induced from the Killing form. As shown in
[
43
]
, the associated variety
\mathrm{AV}(L(\lambda))
is also
K
-invariant. In fact, Yamashita
[
44
]
proved that
\mathrm{AV}(L(\lambda))
must be one of the orbit closures
\overline{\mathcal{O}_{k}}
.

.

Let

be a highest weight Harish-Chandra module. Then

for some

.

Define

(2.2)

for

, where

is the

-rank of

and

is the constant associated with the Hermitian type Lie group

\Gamma^{\prime}
that contains the noncompact simple root. Then
\Phi^{\prime}\cap\Phi(\mathfrak{p}^{+})
is a lower order ideal in
\Phi(\mathfrak{p}^{+})
, and hence

\Phi^{\prime}\cap\Phi(\mathfrak{p}^{+})=\Phi_{x}

for some
x\in{}^{\mathfrak{k}}W
, by Lemma
2.5
. Define

\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}}:=-w_{c}x\rho-\rho,

where
w_{c}
denotes the longest element of the compact Weyl group
W(\mathfrak{k})
.
Recall that the dual Coxeter number of a root system
\Phi
is
h^{\vee}:=(\rho,\beta^{\vee})+1
, which we display in Table
2
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For every
\Phi^{\prime}\subseteq\Phi
as above, we have

(\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}}+\rho,\beta^{\vee})=h^{\prime\vee}-1,

where
h^{\prime\vee}
is the dual Coxeter number of the root system
\Phi^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Fix
\Phi^{\prime}\subset\Phi
as above, and let
x\in{}^{\mathfrak{k}}W
such that
\Phi^{\prime}\cap\Phi(\mathfrak{p}^{+})=\Phi_{x}
.
Let

\tilde{x}:=w_{c}xw_{0},

where
w_{0}
is the longest element in the full Weyl group
W
. Since
w_{0}\rho=-\rho
, it follows that
\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}}=\tilde{x}\rho-\rho
, and hence by Lemma
2.1
, we have

\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}}=-\langle\Phi_{\tilde{x}}\rangle.

(3.1)

By Enright–Hunziker–Pruett
[
21
, Cor. 3.15]
or Lemma
2.8
, we have

\Phi_{\tilde{x}}=\Phi(\mathfrak{p}^{+})\setminus w_{c}\Phi_{x},

and hence

\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}}=-\langle\Phi(\mathfrak{p}^{+})\rangle+w_{c}\langle\Phi_{x}\rangle.

(3.2)

By Panyushev
[
39
, Thm. 2.2]
,

\langle\Phi(\mathfrak{p}^{+})\rangle=h^{\vee}\zeta,

where
h^{\vee}
is the dual Coxeter number of the root system
\Phi
. Similarly, since
\Phi^{\prime}\cap\Phi(\mathfrak{p}^{+})=\Phi_{x}
,

\langle\Phi_{x}\rangle=h^{\prime\vee}\zeta^{\prime},

where
h^{\prime\vee}
is the dual Coxeter number of the root system
\Phi^{\prime}
, and

\zeta^{\prime}
is the fundamental weight of
\Phi^{\prime}
corresponding to the noncompact simple root, viewed as an element of
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
.
(
Warning:
\zeta^{\prime}
is, in general,
not
a fundamental weight for the full root system
\Phi
.
)
Thus, by (
3.2
), we can write

\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}}=-h^{\vee}\zeta+h^{\prime\vee}w_{c}\zeta^{\prime}.

(3.3)

Now recall that
w_{c}\beta=\alpha
, since
\mathfrak{p}^{+}
is a highest weight module of
\mathfrak{k}
with highest weight
\beta
and lowest weight
\alpha
. Therefore,

(w_{c}\zeta^{\prime},\beta^{\vee})=(\zeta^{\prime},w_{c}\beta^{\vee})=(\zeta^{\prime},\alpha^{\vee})=1.

(3.4)

Since
(\zeta,\beta^{\vee})=1
and
(\rho,\beta^{\vee})=h^{\vee}-1
, it follows that

\displaystyle(\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}}+\rho,\beta^{\vee})

\displaystyle=(-h^{\vee}\zeta+h^{\prime\vee}w_{c}\zeta^{\prime}+\rho,\beta^{\vee})

\displaystyle=-h^{\vee}(\zeta,\beta^{\vee})+h^{\prime\vee}(w_{c}\zeta^{\prime},\beta^{\vee})+(\rho,\beta^{\vee})

\displaystyle=-h^{\vee}+h^{\prime\vee}+(h^{\vee}-1)

\displaystyle=h^{\prime\vee}-1,

as claimed.
∎

We define
w(\Gamma^{\prime}):=w(\Phi^{\prime})=\tilde{x}=w_{c}xw_{0}
. Thus
w_{c}x=w(\Gamma^{\prime})w_{0}
and
\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}}=-w(\Gamma^{\prime})w_{0}\rho-\rho=w(\Gamma^{\prime})\rho-\rho
.

Proposition 3.2
.

For every
\Phi^{\prime}\subseteq\Phi
as above, the simple module
L({\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}}})
is unitarizable.

Proof.

Fix
\Phi^{\prime}\subseteq\Phi
as above, and let
\operatorname{Q}(\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}})
and
\operatorname{R}(\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}})
be the two root subsystems of
\Phi
associated with the line
\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}}+\mathbb{R}\zeta
, as in §
2.5
. We claim that

\operatorname{Q}(\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}})=\operatorname{R}(\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}})=w_{c}\Phi^{\prime}.

(3.5)

To prove the claim, we must consider more closely the weight
\zeta^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
introduced in the proof of the previous Lemma
3.1
. Let
\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\}
be the simple roots of
\Phi
, ordered according to Bourbaki
[
14
]
, and set

I=\{i:\alpha_{i}\in\Phi^{\prime}\}.

By the definition of
\zeta^{\prime}
, for all
i\in I
we have

(\zeta^{\prime},\alpha_{i}^{\vee})=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $\alpha_{i}$ is compact,}\\
1&\text{if $\alpha_{i}$ is noncompact.}\end{cases}

Furthermore, by
[
39
, Thm. 2.2]
, we have

h^{\prime}\zeta^{\prime}=\left\langle\Phi^{\prime}\cap\Phi(\mathfrak{p}^{+})\right\rangle=\sum_{i\in I}n_{i}\alpha_{i},\quad\text{with }n_{i}>0\text{ for all }i\in I.

Suppose
j\notin I
is such that
\alpha_{j}
is adjacent to the Dynkin diagram of
\Phi^{\prime}
. (Then
\alpha_{j}
is necessarily compact.) Since the Dynkin diagram of
\Phi^{\prime}
is connected (and the Dynkin diagram of
\Phi
is a tree), there exists a unique
i_{0}\in I
such that

(\alpha_{i_{0}},\alpha_{j}^{\vee})\neq 0
. In fact,
(\alpha_{i_{0}},\alpha_{j}^{\vee})<0
and it follows that

(h^{\prime}\zeta^{\prime},\alpha_{j}^{\vee})=(\alpha_{i_{0}},\alpha_{j}^{\vee})n_{i_{0}}<0.

(3.6)

Recall that
\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}}=-h^{\vee}\zeta+h^{\prime\vee}w_{c}\zeta^{\prime}
by (
3.3
).
Since
\zeta
is orthogonal to all compact simple roots, it follows from this expression that every compact simple root
\alpha_{i}
with
i\in I
lies in
w_{c}\Phi^{\prime}
. (Indeed, each compact simple root is of the form
-w_{c}\alpha_{i}
for some
i\in I
with
\alpha_{i}
compact.)
By the definition of the root subsystem
\operatorname{Q}(\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}})
, we then have
w_{c}\Phi^{\prime}\subseteq\operatorname{Q}(\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}})
.
Note that the set
\{-w_{c}\alpha_{i}:i\in I\}
forms a system of simple roots for
w_{c}\Phi^{\prime}
, and its Dynkin diagram can be identified with a connected subdiagram of the extended Dynkin diagram of
\Phi
containing the node corresponding to
-\beta=-w_{c}\alpha
, where
\alpha
is the simple noncompact root of
\Phi
(and also of
\Phi^{\prime}
). A simple compact root not in
w_{c}\Phi^{\prime}
is adjacent to this Dynkin subdiagram if and only if it is of the form
-w_{c}\alpha_{j}
, where
\alpha_{j}
is a simple compact root adjacent to the Dynkin diagram of
\Phi^{\prime}
. In that case, by (
3.6
) we have

(\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}},-w_{c}\alpha_{j})>0.

Thus, by the definition of
\operatorname{Q}(\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}})
, we conclude that

w_{c}\Phi^{\prime}=\operatorname{Q}(\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}}).

If
\Phi
is simply laced, then
\operatorname{Q}(\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}})=\operatorname{R}(\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}})
, and the claim (
3.5
) follows. Also, if
\Phi^{\prime}=\Phi
, then we trivially have
\operatorname{Q}(\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}})=\operatorname{R}(\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}})
.
So suppose
\Phi
is not simply laced and
\Phi^{\prime}\subsetneq\Phi
. We must consider two cases:
\Sp(2n,\mathbb{R})
and
\operatorname{SO}(2,2n-1)
.

First we consider the case
\Sp(2n,\mathbb{R})
. In this case, for every
\Phi^{\prime}\subsetneq\Phi
, we have
n_{i}>1
for all
i\in I
such that
\alpha_{i}
is compact. Thus, the argument above shows that

(\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}},-w_{c}\alpha_{j})>1.

Hence, by the definition of
\operatorname{R}(\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}})
, it follows that
\operatorname{Q}(\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}})=\operatorname{R}(\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}})
.

Next we consider the case
\operatorname{SO}(2,2n-1)
. Suppose first that
\Phi^{\prime}
is of type
\mathfrak{su}(1,n^{\prime})
with
n^{\prime}\leq n-2
. Then the (unique) simple compact root adjacent to the Dynkin diagram of
\Phi^{\prime}
is long. By the definition of
\operatorname{R}(\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}})
, it follows that
\operatorname{Q}(\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}})=\operatorname{R}(\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}})
in this case.

Now suppose
\Phi^{\prime}
is of type
\mathfrak{su}(1,n-1)
. Then the (unique) simple compact root adjacent to the Dynkin diagram of
\Phi^{\prime}
is the short simple root
\alpha_{j}=\alpha_{n}
, which is adjacent to the long simple root
\alpha_{i_{0}}=\alpha_{n-1}
. Since
(\alpha_{i_{0}},\alpha_{j}^{\vee})=-2
, we find that
(\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}},-w_{c}\alpha_{j})>1
. By the definition of
\operatorname{R}(\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}})
, it follows that
\operatorname{Q}(\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}})=\operatorname{R}(\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}})
in this case as well.

Thus, the claim (
3.5
) holds in all cases. As in §
2.5
, we can write
\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}}=\lambda_{0}+z\zeta
, with
\lambda_{0}\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
such that (
\lambda_{0}+\rho,\beta^{\vee}
)=0, and
z=(\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}}+\rho,\beta^{\vee})\in\mathbb{R}
. By Lemma
3.1
and (
3.5
), we then have

z=(\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}}+\rho,\beta^{\vee})=h^{\prime\vee}-1=h_{\operatorname{Q}(\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}})}^{\vee}-1+\frac{r_{\operatorname{R}(\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}})}-r_{\operatorname{Q}(\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}})}}{2}=b(\lambda_{0}).

By Theorem
2.24
, it follows that
L(\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}})
is unitarizable. (More precisely,
\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}}
is the “last place of unitarity” on the line
\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}}+\mathbb{R}\zeta
.) This completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

If
G_{\mathbb{R}}
is a Lie group of Hermitian type whose complexified Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is simply laced, then we have the bijection given in Theorem
1.1
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\Gamma^{\prime}
is a connected Dynkin subdiagram
containing the noncompact simple root
\alpha
. Denote the corresponding root subsystem by
\Phi^{\prime}
. From Proposition
3.2
, we know that the highest weight module
L(\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}})=L(-w_{c}x\rho-\rho)=L_{x}
is unitarizable.
By Theorem
2.25
, we know that
L(\lambda_{\Phi^{\prime}})=L_{x}
is a Kostant module. By Theorem
2.20
, the Schubert variety
X_{Q}(x^{-1})
is smooth, since rationally smoothness and smoothness are equivalent for all simply laced types. By Proposition
2.19
, there is a bijection between the smooth Schubert varieties and connected subdiagrams containing the noncompact simple root
\alpha
. Therefore we obtain the bijection in Theorem
1.1
.
∎

Recall that
L(-w\rho-\rho)
is a highest weight Harish-Chandra module if and only if
w=w_{c}x
for some
x\in{}^{{\mathfrak{k}}}W
.
Thus from the proof of Corollary
3.3
, we have the following result.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
G_{\mathbb{R}}
be a Lie group of Hermitian type whose complexified Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is simply laced. Suppose
L(-w\rho-\rho)
is a highest weight Harish-Chandra module of
G_{\mathbb{R}}
.
Then
L(-w\rho-\rho)
is unitarizable if and only if the Schubert variety
X_{Q}(x^{-1})
in
G/Q
is smooth, where
x=w_{c}w
, and where
Q
is the parabolic subgroup of
G
such that
{\rm Lie}(Q)={\mathfrak{q}}={\mathfrak{k}}+{\mathfrak{p}}^{+}
.

4.

In this section, we give the proof of the bijection in Theorem
1.1
for non-simply laced cases, by using the results about Kostant modules from Boe–Hunziker
[
12
]
.

If

Proof.

Recall that if

2.13

n+1

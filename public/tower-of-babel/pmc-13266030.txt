Taste dimensions, sensory evaluation and profiling of cricket-flour nachos dataset
Authors: Attila Pohlmann, Franklin Velasco-Vizcaíno, Paúl Cajina Torres
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractConsumer acceptance of insect-based foods is often constrained by food neophobia and cultural barriers. This dataset collected from 102 participants contains evaluations of cricket-flour nachos including taste dimensions (sweet, sour, salty, bitter, umami), sensory properties (smell, sound, touch, taste), and additional characteristics obtained from a Check-All-That-Apply (CATA) word list. Stimulus-independent participant characteristics, such as dietary exclusions (e.g., no red meat, poultry, dairy), and food consumption orientation were recorded and are available to control for the influence of attitudinal variables on taste evaluations. Participant age and gender are provided in the dataset. This dataset can be used for comparative research on insect-based foods, aid product development and support sensory studies, contribute to meta-analyses, and inform consumer psychology research on sensory-driven adoption of alternative proteins. An observed gender difference in saltiness perception highlights marketing opportunities related to taste-driven food familiarity and consumption.Keywords: Entomophagy, Insect-based food, Cricket protein, Consumer acceptability, Edible insects, Consumption orientations, Saltiness perception, General motivations

Value of the Data

Food neophobia and cultural factors often limit consumer acceptance of insect-based foods [[1], [2], [3]]. These data, collected from 102 participants who were sampling cricket-flour nachos in a laboratory setting, provide insight into the taste dimensions sweet, sour, salty, bitter, and umami, as well as sensory evaluations, such as smell, sound, touch, and taste. These sensory attributes play an important role in preference formation. Furthermore, the dataset provides a list of Check-All-That-Apply (CATA) adjectives (e.g., crunchy, hard, greasy), which was derived from the terms for a comparable product (plain crackers) in [4] to provide an in-depth characterization of the cricket-flour product [5]. The accompanying dietary exclusion variables (e.g., no red meat, poultry, dairy), and attitudinal food consumption orientation scores (health, convenience, pleasure, naturalness, sociability, price, and social image) [6], which were collected simultaneously, can be used to control for psychological influences on sensory evaluations [2,3]. Participant age and gender are provided in the dataset.

Background

A meta-analysis on insect-based foods revealed that few cultural or psychological factors positively contribute to consumer acceptance of insect-based foods [7]. These foods constitute protein sources with a smaller ecological footprint compared to traditional livestock meat, and, if more widely adopted, can play a role in addressing food insecurity and some environmental challenges. On one hand, psychological factors such as food neophobia and cultural barriers like uncertainty avoidance hinder widespread consumer acceptance. On the other hand, satisfactory taste profiles and positive sensory experiences with insect-based foods play a crucial role in the formation of favorable consumer attitudes [3,[7], [8], [9], [10]]. The dataset provided in this article contains variables that pertain to sensory perception and taste profiles that were collected during an attitudinal consumer study, which involved sampling/ingestion of cricket-flour nachos. Beyond the published article, an observed gender effect in the perception of saltiness is noteworthy: male participants perceived the cricket-flour nachos to be less salty compared to female participants and concurrently checked the attribute “not salty” more frequently. These insights can be used to inform marketing strategies, considering the gendered patterns of sensory and hedonic responses to salty tastes and their impact on food familiarity and consumption [11].

Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

One hundred and two undergraduates (48 female, 52 male, 2 non-binary, Mage=20.63, SD=1.48) were invited to sample cricket-flour nachos provided by an Ecuadorian snack company. Participants were seated in groups of 20-25 and data were collected during five sessions in May 2024. Participants used their devices to scan a QR code that linked to the Qualtrics site with the questionnaire items. First, participants gave their informed consent, reviewed the ingredient list of the cricket flour nachos (corn flour, broad bean flour, ground cricket gryllus assimilis, vegetable oil, sea salt) and were asked to review the nutritional label, reproduced in Fig. 1. Participants were free to leave without loss of benefit at any time. Participants were asked to refrain from sampling the product if they had known or suspected allergies to any of the ingredients. After these steps, participants received an unbranded cellophane package containing 50 grams of cricket flour nachos and were asked to follow the instructions in the questionnaire while sampling the nachos. Fig. 2 contains a photograph of the product showing its shape and texture.

Taste Dimensions: Participants were asked to evaluate the product on the dimensions of sweet, sour, salty, bitter, and umami. In a matrix table, each dimension was linked to a slider ranging from zero, labelled “Extremely weak” to 100, labelled “Extremely strong.” Each slider was centered for the starting position.

Sensory Evaluation: Participants were asked to use all their senses to evaluate the product. The dimensions smell, sound, touch, and taste were arranged in a matrix table and participants indicated their perceptions using sliders ranging from zero, labelled “Extremely negative” to 100, labelled “Extremely positive.”

Sensory Profiling: Participants were shown a list of food-related attributes sourced from [4]: crunchy, tasty, dry, not salty, toasted/burnt, salty, dark color, light color, hard, airy, tasteless, brittle, butter flavor, soft, greasy/oily, off-flavor, moist. Participants were instructed to check all words that applied to the product they were sampling. The frequencies for each word are reported in Fig. 3.

Frequencies of check-all-that-apply (CATA) words. Chart created with datawrapper.

Food Consumption Orientation: Consumers’ food consumption orientation affects the transition toward more sustainable food systems [6]. The measures are provided to control for potential distortions in participants’ sensory perceptions due to attitudinal factors. Food consumption orientations are divided into seven sub-scales: health, convenience, pleasure, naturalness, sociability, price, and social image. Each sub-scale score can be calculated by averaging the associated items. Table 1 enumerates the variables and associated statements to which consumers responded on a 5-point Likert scale, anchored with 1 = “Strongly disagree” to 5 = “Strongly agree.”

Dietary Exclusions: To provide additional insight into participants’ eating habits, they were asked to indicate whether they regularly exclude certain food groups from their diets (e.g., red meat, dairy, poultry, fish). Multiple choices were possible.

Limitations

A limitation of the study is that participants were undergraduates from a single university in Quito. The generalizability of the dataset is therefore limited in terms of demographic and cultural background. However, due to participants being in the same age group, physiological variability in sensory perception is conceivably low. Nonetheless, sensory perceptions are highly subjective and larger sample sizes from diverse backgrounds would be required to increase generalizability.

Ethics Statement

Data were collected in accordance with the ethical standards of the Comité de Ética de Investigación en Seres Humanos de la Universidad San Francisco de Quito “CEISH-USFQ” and adhered to the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki. Ethics board approval for this research was granted under protocol number 019-2024-CA23149IN-CEISH-USFQ on 31 January 2024. To ensure participants’ safety, an ingredient list and nutritional information for the food samples was provided at the beginning of the data collection process. Participants were asked to refrain from sampling the product if they had any existing or suspected allergies to any of its ingredients.

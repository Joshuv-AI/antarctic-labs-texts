Propagation of chaos for the homogeneous Boltzmann equation with moderately soft potentials
Nicolas Fournier and Stéphane Mischler

Abstract
. We show that the Kac particle system converges, as the number of particles tends to infinity, to the solution of the homogeneous Boltzmann equation, in the regime of moderately soft potentials, γ ∈ ( − 2 , 0 ) \gamma\in(-2,0) with the common notation. This proves the propagation of chaos. We adapt the recent work [ 24 ] of Imbert, Silvestre and Villani, to show that the Fisher information is nonincreasing in time along solutions to the Kac master equation. This estimate allows us to control the singularity of the interaction.

1.
Introduction and main results

1.1.
The homogeneous Boltzmann equation

The homogeneous Boltzmann equation describes the evolution of the density
f=f(t,v)=f_{t}(v)\geq 0

of particles with velocity
v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
at time

t\geq 0
in a spatially homogeneous dilute gas. It writes

(1.1)

\partial_{t}f=Q(f,f)\;\;\;\text{in}\;\;\;(0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3},\quad f(0,\cdot)=f_{0}\;\;\;\text{in}\;\;\;{\mathbb{R}}^{3},

where the collision operator is given by

(1.2)

\displaystyle Q(f,f)(v):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}{\rm d}v_{*}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}_{2}}{\rm d}\sigma B(v-v_{*},\sigma)\big[f(v^{\prime})f(v^{\prime}_{*})-f(v)f(v_{*})\big],

and where in the above formula the pre-collisional velocities are given by

(1.3)

v^{\prime}=v^{\prime}(v,v_{*},\sigma)=\frac{v+v_{*}}{2}+\frac{|v-v_{*}|}{2}\sigma,\quad v^{\prime}_{*}=v^{\prime}_{*}(v,v_{*},\sigma)=\frac{v+v_{*}}{2}-\frac{|v-v_{*}|}{2}\sigma.

The cross section takes the form

(1.4)

B(z,\sigma)\,\sin\theta=\alpha(|z|)\beta(\theta)

for some
\alpha:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
and some
\beta:(0,\pi]\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
,
where
\theta=\theta(z,\sigma)
is defined by

\cos\theta=\frac{z}{|z|}\cdot\sigma
.

We will always assume that this cross section corresponds to the situation where particles repel
each other according to a force proportional to
1/r^{s}
,
with
s\in(2,\infty)
. In that case, we have

(1.5)

\alpha(r)=r^{\gamma}\;\;\text{and}\;\;c_{1}\theta^{-1-\nu}\leq\beta(\theta)\leq c_{2}\theta^{-1-\nu},\;\;\hbox{with}\;\;\gamma=\frac{s-5}{s-1}\in(-3,1)\;\;\text{and}\;\;\nu=\frac{2}{s-1}

for some constants
c_{2}>c_{1}>0
.
One speaks of hard spheres when
\gamma=1
, hard potentials when
\gamma\in(0,1)
,
Maxwell molecules when
\gamma=0
and soft potentials when
\gamma\in(-3,0)
.
The present paper is concerned with moderately soft potentials, i.e.
\gamma\in(-2,0)
.

The main physical features of the Boltzmann equation is that any solution conserves mass,
momentum and kinetic energy and that its entropy is decaying.
From Galilean invariance, we may (and will) always normalize the initial datum, and we
thus have

(1.6)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f_{t}(v){\rm d}v=1,\quad\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f_{t}(v)v{\rm d}v=0,\quad\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f_{t}(v)|v|^{2}{\rm d}v=3,\quad\forall\,t\geq 0,

as well as the entropy bound

H(f_{t}):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f_{t}(v)\log f_{t}(v){\rm d}v\leq H(f_{0}),\quad\forall\,t\geq 0.

In a recent paper
[
21
]
, Guillen and Silvestre have established
that the Fisher information

(1.7)

I_{1}(f):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\frac{|\nabla f(v)|^{2}}{f(v)}{\rm d}v

is nonincreasing in time along the solutions of the Landau equation. This was extended by Imbert, Silvestre
and Villani in
[
24
]
to the Boltzmann equation (
1.1
), for any
physical kernels (
1.4
)-(
1.5
).
Although such a property result was known for Maxwell molecules,
see McKean
[
27
]
, Toscani
[
38
]
and Villani
[
39
]
,
it was rather unexpected for other kernels.
This remarkable result and the possibility of extending it
to the Kac particle system are the starting point and a cornerstone argument in the present work.

1.2.
The Kac particle systems

Kac
[
26
]
introduced a stochastic particle system, expected to approximate the Boltzmann equation.
This system is composed of a large number
N
of particles characterized by their
velocities
v_{1},\dots,v_{N}
and such that
each couple of particles with velocities
(v_{i},v_{j})
randomly collides, for each
\sigma\in{\mathbb{S}}_{2}
,
at rate
\frac{B(v_{i}-v_{j},\sigma)}{2(N-1)}
and are then replaced by particles with velocities

v^{\prime}(v_{i},v_{j},\sigma)
and
v^{\prime}_{*}(v_{i},v_{j},\sigma)
.
Equivalently, the Kac particle system is a
{({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{N}}
-valued Markov process
(\mathbf{V}^{N}_{t})_{t\geq 0}

with generator
\mathcal{L}_{N}
defined (at least informally) by

\mathcal{L}_{N}\phi(v)=\frac{1}{2(N-1)}\sum_{1\leq i\neq j\leq N}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}_{2}}[\phi(v^{\prime}_{ij})-\phi(v)]B(v_{i}-v_{j},\sigma){\rm d}\sigma,

for any
v=(v_{1},\dots,v_{N})\in{({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{N}}
and any
\phi:{({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{N}}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
sufficiently regular.
Here, we have set

(1.8)

v^{\prime}_{ij}=v^{\prime}_{ij}(v,\sigma)=v+(v^{\prime}(v_{i},v_{j},\sigma)-v_{i})\mathbf{e}_{i}+(v^{\prime}_{*}(v_{i},v_{j},\sigma)-v_{j})\mathbf{e}_{j}\in{({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{N}},

where for
h\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
,
h\mathbf{e}_{i}=(0,\dots,0,h,0,\dots,0)
with
h
at the
i
-th place.

It turns out that the law
F^{N}_{t}
of
\mathbf{V}^{N}_{t}
satisfies the
N
-particle linear Boltzmann
equation, also called Kac master equation,

(1.9)

\partial_{t}F^{N}_{t}=\mathcal{L}_{N}F^{N}_{t}\;\;\;\text{in}\;\;\;(0,\infty)\times({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{N},\quad F^{N}(0,\cdot)=F^{N}_{0}\;\;\;\text{in}\;\;\;({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{N}.

That equation has the same main physical properties as the Boltzmann equation since its
solutions also conserve mass, momentum and kinetic energy and have a decaying entropy.
As alluded to above, we will establish that the
N
-particle Fisher information

(1.10)

I_{N}(F)=\frac{1}{N}\int_{{({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{N}}}\frac{|\nabla F(v)|^{2}}{F(v)}{\rm d}v

is nonincreasing along solutions of (
1.9
).

1.3.
Propagation of chaos

We recall some more or less classical notions of chaos which have been introduced
in Kac’s paper
[
26
]
and later in Sznitman
[
35
]
, Carlen et al.
[
2
]

and in
[
22
]
,
to which we refer for more details.

For a Polish space
E
, we denote by
\mathcal{P}(E)
the set of probability measures on
E
.
We say that
G^{N}\in\mathcal{P}(E^{N})
is symmetric (and write
G^{N}\in\mathcal{P}_{\!s}(E^{N})
) if

\langle G^{N},\varphi_{\sigma}\rangle=\langle G^{N},\varphi\rangle
for any
\varphi\in C_{b}(E^{N})
and any
permutation
\sigma
of
\{1,\dots,N\}
, where

\varphi_{\sigma}(Z):=\varphi(Z_{\sigma(1)},\dots,Z_{\sigma(N)})
for any
Z\in E^{N}
.
For
1\leq\ell\leq N
, we define the
\ell
-th marginal
G^{N}_{\ell}\in\mathcal{P}(E^{\ell})

by
\langle G^{N}_{\ell},\varphi\rangle:=\langle G^{N},\varphi\otimes{\bf 1}^{N-\ell}\rangle
,
for any
\varphi\in C_{b}(E^{\ell})
.
For
g\in\mathcal{P}(E)
, we say that a sequence
(G^{N})_{N\geq 1}
of
\mathcal{P}(E^{N})

is (weakly)
g
-chaotic if

G^{N}_{\ell}\rightharpoonup g^{\otimes\ell}\ \hbox{ weakly in }\ \mathcal{P}(E^{\ell})\ \hbox{ as }\ N\to\infty,

for any fixed
\ell\geq 1
(or equivalently for at least one fixed
\ell\geq 2
).

When
E={\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, we define the
N
-particle entropy of
G^{N}\in\mathcal{P}_{\!s}(({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{N})
such that

m_{2}(G^{N}):=\langle G^{N},|v_{1}|^{2}\rangle<\infty
by

H_{N}(G^{N}):=\frac{1}{N}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3N}}G^{N}(v)\log G^{N}(v){\rm d}v.

Consider now a sequence of symmetric probability measures
(G^{N})_{N\geq 1}
with
\sup_{N\geq 1}m_{2}(G^{N})<\infty
.
We say that
(G^{N})_{N\geq 1}
is entropically
g
-chaotic if

G^{N}_{1}\rightharpoonup g\hbox{ weakly in } \mathcal{P}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\hbox{ and }H_{N}(G_{N})\to H(g).

A
E^{N}
-valued random vector
{\mathbf{Z}}^{N}
is said to be exchangeable if its law is symmetric.
For any
Z=(z_{1},...,z_{N})\in E^{N}
, we define the empirical measure

(1.11)

\mu^{N}_{Z}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{z_{i}}\in\mathcal{P}(E).

Consider a family
({\mathbf{Z}}^{N})_{N\geq 1}
of exchangeable random vectors, with
{\mathbf{Z}}^{N}
valued in
E^{N}
.
For
g\in\mathcal{P}(E)
, we say that
({\mathbf{Z}}^{N})_{N\geq 1}
is
g
-chaotic if its sequence of laws is
g
-chaotic,
or equivalently, see
[
35
, Proposition 2.2]
,
if the associated
\mathcal{P}(E)
-valued random variable
\mu^{N}_{{\mathbf{Z}}^{N}}
converges
to the deterministic random variable
g
:

(1.12)

\mu^{N}_{{\mathbf{Z}}^{N}}\,\Rightarrow\,g\quad\hbox{in probability as}\quad N\to\infty.

Here
\Rightarrow
refers to the weak convergence of measures (tested against
C_{b}(E)
-functions).

Similarly, when
E={\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, we say that
({\mathbf{Z}}^{N})_{N\geq 1}
is
entropically
g
-chaotic if the associated sequence of laws
is entropically
g
-chaotic.

1.4.
Main result

We are now in position to formulate the main results of the paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Consider a kernel
B
satisfying (
1.4
)-(
1.5
) with
\gamma\in(-2,0)

and a nonnegative initial condition
f_{0}
satisfying the normalization

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f_{0}(v){\rm d}v=1,\quad\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f_{0}(v)v{\rm d}v=0,\quad\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f_{0}(v)|v|^{2}{\rm d}v=3,

and with finite Fisher information. Denote by
f\in C([0,\infty);L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))
the unique weak solution
to the Boltzmann equation (
1.1
) with nonincreasing Fisher information and which
which satisfies (
1.6
).
For any
N\geq 2
, there exists
a
{({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{N}}
-valued Kac particle system
(\mathbf{V}^{N}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
with initial law
f_{0}^{\otimes N}

and for all
t\geq 0
,

(\mathbf{V}^{N}_{t})_{N\geq 2}

f_{t}
-chaotic.

The main novelty is that we are able to deal with moderately soft potentials.

1.5.
Strategy of the proof

As explained above, our proof makes use of
(a) the results of Imbert-Villani-Silvestre
[
24
]
about the decay of the Fisher information for
the Boltzmann equation (that we extend to the Kac particle system),
(b) the tools developed in
[
14
]
and
[
22
]
to control
the singularity of the interaction by the Fisher information (in the context of continuous processes),
(c) some ideas taken in Sznitman
[
35
,
34
]
and
Méléard
[
28
]
about the propagation of chaos for càdlàg processes (with non-singular interactions),
and (d) the uniqueness result of
[
12
]
, which implies that there is at most one weak solution to the
Boltzmann equation with time-integrable Fisher information when
\gamma\in(-2,0)
.

As we shall see, something quite disappointing is that the bound

\sup_{N\geq 2}\sup_{t\geq 2}I_{N}(F^{N}_{t})<\infty

shows that for
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
a (random) limit point of the empirical measure of the Kac
particle system, we have
\sup_{t\geq 0}{\mathbb{E}}[I_{1}(\mu_{t})]<\infty
. This of course implies that

t\mapsto I_{1}(\mu_{t})\in L^{1}_{loc}([0,\infty))
a.s.
but not that
t\mapsto I_{1}(\mu_{t})\in L^{\infty}_{loc}([0,\infty))
a.s.
Such a bound would imply that
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},L^{3}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))
. Although the situation is much less
clear for the Boltzmann equation, such an estimate would be sufficient to have uniqueness in the case of
the Landau equation in the Coulomb case, see Chern-Gualdani
[
6
]
and
Golding-Gualdani-Loher
[
18
]
.

Tabary
[
36
]
manages to treat the Landau equation when
\gamma\in[-3,0)
.
He is able to take advantage of the Fisher
information dissipation to show that
any limit point
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
of the empirical measure actually lies in
L^{1}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))
,
which allows him to apply the uniqueness result of
[
17
]
.
Such a program might be feasible for the Boltzmann equation for very soft potentials
\gamma\in(-3,-2]
,
with much more involved computations.

2.
The Fisher information

The goal of this section is mainly to show the time-decay of the Fisher information for the
N
-particle system.
This relies on the following result, found in the article of Imbert, Silvestre and Villani
[
24
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume (
1.4
), with
\int_{0}^{\pi}\theta^{2}\beta(\theta){\rm d}\theta<\infty
and with

\alpha(r)=(\varepsilon^{2}+r^{2})^{\gamma/2}
for some
\varepsilon\geq 0

and some
\gamma\in(-2,0)
.
For
F:({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
and
v_{1},v_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, set

\mathbf{Q}(F)(v_{1},v_{2})=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}_{2}}[F(v^{\prime}(v_{1},v_{2},\sigma),v^{\prime}_{*}(v_{1},v_{2},\sigma))-F(v_{1},v_{2})]B(v_{1}-v_{2},\sigma){\rm d}\sigma.

For any function
F:({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
which is symmetric, that is
F(v_{1},v_{2})=F(v_{2},v_{1})
, it holds

(2.1)

\int_{({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{2}}\Big(\frac{2\nabla_{1}F\cdot\nabla_{1}\mathbf{Q}(F)}{F}-\frac{|\nabla_{1}F|^{2}\mathbf{Q}(F)}{F^{2}}\Big){\rm d}v_{1}{\rm d}v_{2}\leq 0,

where
\nabla_{1}F
is the gradient of
F
with respect to its first variable
v_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
.

Proof.

Let us check that the assumptions of
[
24
, Theorem 1.2]
are satisfied.
First,
b(\cos\theta)=\beta(\theta)
satisfies
[
24
, (1.2)]
,
i.e.
\int_{0}^{\pi}(1-\cos\theta)\beta(\theta){\rm d}\theta<\infty
.
Next, we need to verify that
\frac{r|\alpha^{\prime}(r)|}{\alpha(r)}\leq 2\sqrt{\Lambda_{b}}
, with
\Lambda_{b}
defined in
[
24
, (1.4)]
.
But, as seen in
[
24
, Proposition 7.11]
, it holds that
\Lambda_{b}\geq 1
. Since

\frac{r|\alpha^{\prime}(r)|}{\alpha(r)}=\frac{r^{2}|\gamma|(\varepsilon^{2}+r^{2})^{\gamma/2-1}}{(\varepsilon^{2}+r^{2})^{\gamma/2}}=\frac{r^{2}|\gamma|}{\varepsilon^{2}+r^{2}}\leq|\gamma|\leq 2\leq 2\sqrt{\Lambda_{b}},

all the assumptions of
[
24
, Theorem 1.2]
are satisfied.

By
[
24
, (3.3)]
, which is shown during the proof of
[
24
, Theorem 1.2]
,
that for any symmetric function
F:({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, it holds

\langle I_{2}^{\prime}(F),\mathbf{Q}(F)\rangle\leq 0,

where
I_{2}^{\prime}(F)
is the Gâteau derivative of
I_{2}
, see (
1.10
). This precisely rewrites as

\frac{1}{2}\int_{({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{2}}\Big(\frac{2\nabla F\cdot\nabla\mathbf{Q}(F)}{F}-\frac{|\nabla F|^{2}\mathbf{Q}(F)}{F^{2}}\Big){\rm d}v_{1}{\rm d}v_{2}\leq 0.

The LHS of this inequality equals the LHS of (
2.1
) by symmetry of
F
.
∎

We can now state the main result of this section, generalizing Carrillo and Guo
[
5
]

which proves a similar result on the Kac’s system for the Landau-Coulomb equation.

Proposition 2.2
.

Assume (
1.4
) with
\int_{0}^{\pi}\theta^{2}\beta(\theta){\rm d}\theta<\infty
and

\alpha(r)=(\varepsilon^{2}+r^{2})^{\gamma/2}
for some
\varepsilon\geq 0

and some
\gamma\in(-2,0)
.
For any
N\geq 2
, any nonnegative symmetric smooth
enough solution
F^{N}
to the
N
-particle linear Boltzmann equation (
1.9
),

t\mapsto I_{N}(F^{N}_{t})
is nonincreasing.

Proof.

A simple computation, recalling the definition of
I_{N}

in (
1.10
), shows that

\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}I_{N}(F_{t}^{N})=\int_{({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{N}}\Big(2\frac{\nabla F_{t}^{N}(v)\cdot\nabla\partial_{t}F_{t}^{N}(v)}{F_{t}^{N}(v)}-\frac{|\nabla F_{t}^{N}(v)|^{2}\partial_{t}F_{t}^{N}(v)}{(F_{t}^{N}(v))^{2}}\Big){\rm d}v.

We claim that

(2.2)

\Delta_{N}(F):=\int_{({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{N}}\Big(2\frac{\nabla F\cdot\nabla\mathcal{L}_{N}F}{F}-\frac{|\nabla F|^{2}\mathcal{L}_{N}F}{F^{2}}\Big){\rm d}v\leq 0,

for any (smooth) probability density
F
on
({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{N}
,
from what the announced decay follows.

From the very definition of
\mathcal{L}_{N}

in Section
1.2
, we may write

\mathcal{L}_{N}=\frac{1}{2(N-1)}\sum_{1\leq i\neq j\leq N}\mathcal{L}_{N}^{ij},\quad\text{where}\quad\mathcal{L}_{N}^{ij}F:=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}_{2}}(F^{\prime}_{ij}-F)B_{ij}{\rm d}\sigma,

with
F^{\prime}_{ij}:=F(v^{\prime}_{ij})
,
F=F(v)
,
B_{ij}:=B(v_{i}-v_{j},\sigma)
. Thus

\Delta_{N}(F)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\int_{({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{N}}\Big(2\frac{\nabla_{\ell}F\cdot\nabla_{\ell}\mathcal{L}_{N}F}{F}-\frac{|\nabla_{\ell}F|^{2}\mathcal{L}_{N}F}{F^{2}}\Big){\rm d}v=\frac{1}{2(N-1)}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\sum_{1\leq i\neq j\leq N}\Delta_{N}^{\ell,i,j}(F),

where
\nabla_{\ell}
stands for the gradient associated to the variable
v_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, and where

We now show that each term

The case

.
We observe that

\displaystyle\Delta_{N}^{\ell,i,j}(F)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{N}}\Big(2\frac{\nabla_{\ell}F}{F}\cdot\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}[\nabla_{\ell}F^{\prime}_{ij}-\nabla_{\ell}F]B_{ij}{\rm d}\sigma-\frac{|\nabla_{\ell}F|^{2}}{F^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}[F^{\prime}_{ij}-F]B_{ij}{\rm d}\sigma\Big){\rm d}v

\displaystyle\int_{({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{N}}\!\!\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}\Big(2\frac{\nabla_{\ell}F}{F}\cdot\nabla_{\ell}F^{\prime}_{ij}-\frac{|\nabla_{\ell}F|^{2}}{F}-\frac{|\nabla_{\ell}F|^{2}}{F^{2}}F^{\prime}_{ij}\Big)B_{ij}{\rm d}\sigma{\rm d}v.

As is well-known, see e.g.

\int_{({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{2}}\!\!\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}\varphi(v,v_{*})B(v-v_{*},\sigma){\rm d}\sigma{\rm d}v_{*}{\rm d}v=\int_{({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{2}}\!\!\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}\varphi(v^{\prime},v_{*}^{\prime})B(v-v_{*},\sigma){\rm d}\sigma{\rm d}v_{*}{\rm d}v.

Consequently, for any measurable

(2.3)

We conclude that

\displaystyle\Delta_{N}^{\ell,i,j}(F)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{N}}\!\!\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}\Big(2\frac{\nabla_{\ell}F}{F}\cdot\nabla_{\ell}F^{\prime}_{ij}-\frac{|\nabla_{\ell}F^{\prime}_{ij}|^{2}}{F^{\prime}_{ij}}-\frac{|\nabla_{\ell}F|^{2}}{F^{2}}F^{\prime}_{ij}\Big)B_{ij}{\rm d}\sigma{\rm d}v

\displaystyle-\int_{({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{N}}\!\!\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}\Bigl|\frac{\nabla_{\ell}F}{F}\sqrt{F^{\prime}_{ij}}-\frac{\nabla_{\ell}F^{\prime}_{ij}}{\sqrt{F^{\prime}_{ij}}}\Bigr|^{2}B_{ij}{\rm d}\sigma{\rm d}v\leq 0.

The case
\ell\in\{i,j\}
.
We e.g. assume that
\ell=i=1
and

and write

\displaystyle\int_{({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{N}}\Big(2\frac{\nabla_{1}F\cdot\nabla_{1}\mathcal{L}_{N}^{12}F}{F}-\frac{|\nabla_{1}F|^{2}\mathcal{L}_{N}^{12}F}{F^{2}}\Big){\rm d}v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{N-2}}\Big[\int_{({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{2}}\Big(2\frac{\nabla_{1}F_{v_{3N}}\cdot\nabla_{1}\mathbf{Q}(F_{v_{3N}})}{F_{v_{3N}}}-\frac{|\nabla_{1}F_{v_{3N}}|^{2}\mathbf{Q}(F_{v_{3N}})}{F_{v_{3N}}^{2}}\Big){\rm d}v_{1}{\rm d}v_{2}\Big]{\rm d}v_{3,N},

where

, where

, where

was defined
in Lemma

The integral on

Lemma 2.3

All these properties are classical. For (a), we refer to
[

, Lemma 3.2]

Property (b) is nothing but the Sobolev embedding

applied to

\sqrt{f}

For

\tilde{f}
the first marginal of

Indeed, the first inequality is obvious, the equality follows from a simple substitution, and the last

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[|V^{1}-V^{2}|^{a}]

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}{F^{N}_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})}|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{a}\,{\rm d}x_{1}{\rm d}x_{2}

\displaystyle 2^{a/2}+2^{a/2}\int_{|y|\leq 1}\tilde{f}(y)|y|^{a}\,{\rm d}y.

.

Property (e) is nothing but
[

For (f), let us denote by

\mathcal{L}(\mu^{N}_{V})

3.
Reminder about the Boltzmann equation

We now introduce the notion of weak solutions we deal with.
We denote by
\mathcal{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
the set of probability measures on

such that

Definition 3.1
.

Assume (
1.4
)-(

and

\bar{\mathcal{A}}\phi(v,v_{*})=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}_{2}}(\phi(v^{\prime})-\phi(v)-(v^{\prime}-v)\cdot\nabla\phi(v))B(v-v_{*},\sigma){\rm d}\sigma-b|v-v_{*}|^{\gamma}(v-v_{*})\cdot\nabla\phi(v),

Theorem 3.2
.

1.4
)-(

) with

The existence of weak solutions has been proved by Villani
[
40
]
.
His proof is based on a weak compactness argument in

, a clever use of the so-called

On the other hand, as already mentioned, Imbert, Silvestre and Villani in

∎

E_{N}(F^{N}_{t})=E_{N}(F^{N}_{0})=m_{2}(f_{0})\quad\text{and}\quad I_{N}(F^{N}_{t})\leq I_{N}(F^{N}_{0})=I_{1}(f_{0}).

Moreover,

is a weak solution to (

.

It is worth emphasizing that

is well-defined as the principal value

\mathcal{L}_{N}\phi(v)=\frac{1}{2(N-1)}\sum_{1\leq i\neq j\leq N}\lim_{\eta\to 0}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}_{2}}[\phi(v^{\prime}_{ij})-\phi(v)]B(v_{i}-v_{j},\sigma){\bf 1}_{\{\theta_{ij}>\eta\}}{\rm d}\sigma.

for all

, where
\theta_{ij}\in[0,\pi]
is defined by

\cos\theta_{ij}=\frac{v_{i}-v_{j}}{|v_{i}-v_{j}|}\cdot\sigma
.
This precaution is useless if the singularity of
\beta
is removed.
Using (
2.3
), one can check that
\mathcal{L}_{N}
is self-adjoint: for

\phi,\psi:{({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{N}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
smooth enough, it holds

(4.6)

\int_{{({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{N}}}\phi(v)\mathcal{L}_{N}\psi(v){\rm d}v=\int_{{({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{N}}}\psi(v)\mathcal{L}_{N}\phi(v){\rm d}v.

We now state without proof the immediate
N
-particle counterpart of Lemma

.

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume (

)-(
1.5
) with some

.
Recall that
v^{\prime}_{ij}=v^{\prime}_{ij}(v,\sigma)\in{({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{N}}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}v^{\prime}_{ijk}(v,\sigma)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}v_{k},\qquad\sum_{k=1}^{N}|v^{\prime}_{ijk}(v,\sigma)|^{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}|v_{k}|^{2},

\displaystyle|v^{\prime}_{ij}-v|\leq\theta_{ij}|v_{i}-v_{j}|

\displaystyle\lim_{\eta\to 0}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}_{2}}[v^{\prime}_{ij}(v,\sigma)-v]B(v_{i}-v_{j},\sigma){\bf 1}_{\{\theta_{ij}>\eta\}}{\rm d}\sigma=b|v_{i}-v_{j}|^{\gamma}(v_{i}-v_{j})(\mathbf{e}_{j}-\mathbf{e}_{i}),

\displaystyle+\frac{b}{2(N-1)}\sum_{1\leq i\neq j\leq N}|v_{i}-v_{j}|^{\gamma}[(v_{i}-v_{j})(\mathbf{e}_{j}-\mathbf{e}_{i})]\cdot\nabla\phi(v).

We are now in position to present the proof of Theorem

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

We fix
N\geq 2
,
B
satisfying (
1.4
)-(
1.5
)
with
\gamma\in(-2,0)
, as well as

f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
such that
I_{1}(f_{0})<\infty
. We
introduce a family of smooth probability densities
f_{0}^{\varepsilon}
,
weakly converging to
f_{0}
and such that
m_{2}(f_{0}^{\varepsilon})\leq 2m_{2}(f_{0})
,

I_{1}(f_{0}^{\varepsilon})\leq 2I_{1}(f_{0})
,
\lim_{\varepsilon}m_{2}(f^{\varepsilon}_{0})=m_{2}(f_{0})

and
\lim_{\varepsilon}I_{1}(f_{0}^{\varepsilon})=I_{1}(f_{0})
.
Denoting by
\mathfrak{g}
the standard Gaussian density, we may also assume that

\varepsilon\mathfrak{g}\leq f_{0}^{\varepsilon}\leq\varepsilon^{-1}\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{-1/2}\nabla f_{0}^{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
,
for any given
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
,
\varepsilon_{0}>0
small enough
(choose
f_{0}^{\varepsilon}:=[(\varepsilon\mathfrak{g}_{1-\varepsilon}+f_{0})\wedge(\varepsilon^{-1}\mathfrak{g}_{1-\varepsilon})]*\mathfrak{g}_{\varepsilon}
, where

\mathfrak{g}_{\theta}
,
is the centered Gaussian density with variance
\theta
).
Consider, for each
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
, the cross section

B_{\varepsilon}(z,\sigma)\sin\theta=(\varepsilon^{2}+|z|^{2})^{\gamma/2}(\beta(\theta)\land\varepsilon^{-1})
,
which satisfies

M_{\varepsilon}:=\sup_{z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3},\sigma\in{\mathbb{S}}_{2}}B_{\varepsilon}(z,\sigma)<\infty.

Consider also an i.i.d. collection of Poisson measures

(\Pi^{N}_{ij}({\rm d}s,{\rm d}\sigma,{\rm d}z))_{1\leq i\neq j\leq N}
on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{S}}_{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
with intensity measure

\frac{1}{2(N-1)}{\rm d}s{\rm d}\sigma{\rm d}z
and
an i.i.d. collection
(V_{0}^{i,\varepsilon})_{i=1,\dots,N}
of
f_{0}^{\varepsilon}
-distributed random
variables independent of the Poisson measures. Set
\mathbf{V}_{0}^{N,\varepsilon}=(V_{0}^{1,\varepsilon},\dots,V_{0}^{N,\varepsilon})
and consider
the stochastic differential equation

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}^{N,\varepsilon}_{t}=

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}^{N,\varepsilon}_{0}+\sum_{1\leq i\neq j\leq N}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}_{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}[v^{\prime}_{ij}(\mathbf{V}^{N,\varepsilon}_{s-},\sigma)-\mathbf{V}^{N,\varepsilon}_{s-}]{\bf 1}_{\{z<B_{\varepsilon}(V^{i,N,\varepsilon}_{s-}-V^{j,N,\varepsilon}_{s-},\sigma)\}}\Pi^{N}_{ij}({\rm d}s,{\rm d}\sigma,{\rm d}z).

We can replace the integral
\int_{0}^{\infty}
by
\int_{0}^{M_{\varepsilon}}
, so that this equation involves finite
Poisson measures and is thus strongly well-posed: it can be solved by induction on the jump instants
of the Poisson measures.
We deduce from (
4.7
) that
(\mathbf{V}^{N,\varepsilon}_{t})_{t\geq 0}

is strongly conservative: it satisfies (
4.3
). This readily implies that

(4.13)

\sup_{t\geq 0}{\mathbb{E}}[|V^{1,N,\varepsilon}_{t}|^{2}]=m_{2}(f_{0}^{\varepsilon})\leq 2m_{2}(f_{0}).

Using the Itô formula, we get,
for all measurable
\phi:{({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{N}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
,

\displaystyle\phi(\mathbf{V}^{N,\varepsilon}_{t})=

\displaystyle\phi(\mathbf{V}^{N,\varepsilon}_{0})+\sum_{1\leq i\neq j\leq N}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}_{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}[\phi(v^{\prime}_{ij}(\mathbf{V}^{N,\varepsilon}_{s-},\sigma))-\phi(\mathbf{V}^{N,\varepsilon}_{s-})]

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt{\bf 1}_{\{z<B_{\varepsilon}(V^{i,N,\varepsilon}_{s-}-V^{j,N,\varepsilon}_{s-},\sigma)\}}\Pi^{N}_{ij}({\rm d}s,{\rm d}\sigma,{\rm d}z).

We now may take expectations if
\phi
is bounded and get,
denoting by
F^{N,\varepsilon}_{t}
the law of
\mathbf{V}^{N,\varepsilon}_{t}
,

\int_{({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{N}}\phi(v)F^{N,\varepsilon}_{t}({\rm d}v)=\int_{({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{N}}\phi(v)F^{N,\varepsilon}_{0}({\rm d}v)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{N}}\mathcal{L}_{N,\varepsilon}\phi(v)F^{N,\varepsilon}_{s}({\rm d}v){\rm d}s,

with
\mathcal{L}_{N,\varepsilon}
defined as in (
4.5
), replacing
B
by
B_{\varepsilon}
(here of course the principal
value can be removed). Using (
4.6
),
we immediately obtain that
(F^{N,\varepsilon}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a weak solution to

\partial_{t}F^{N,\varepsilon}_{t}=\mathcal{L}_{N,\varepsilon}F^{N,\varepsilon}_{t}.

The equation is linear with smooth coefficients and satisfies a weak maximum principle, from what we
classically deduce that the solution
F^{N,\varepsilon}_{t}
satisfies

\varepsilon^{N}\mathfrak{g}^{\otimes N}\leq F^{N,\varepsilon}_{t}\leq\varepsilon^{-N}\mathfrak{g}^{\otimes N}
and

(\mathfrak{g}^{-1/2})^{\otimes N}\nabla F^{N,\varepsilon}_{t}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3N})
, uniformly in
t\in[0,T]
,
for any given
N\geq 2
,
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
,
T>0
. The function
F^{N,\varepsilon}
is thus smooth enough
in order to justify the chain
rule used during the proof of Proposition
2.2
.
As a consequence, applying Proposition
2.2
as well as Lemma
2.3
-(a), we get

(4.14)

I_{N}(F^{N,\varepsilon}_{t})\leq I_{N}(F^{N,\varepsilon}_{0})=I_{1}(f_{0}^{\varepsilon})\leq 2I_{1}(f_{0}).

By Lemma
2.3
-(d), this implies that for all
a\in(-2,0)
,

(4.15)

\sup_{t\geq 0}{\mathbb{E}}[|V^{1,N,\varepsilon}_{t}-V^{2,N,\varepsilon}_{t}|^{a}]\leq 2C_{a}I_{1}(f_{0}).

Now, set
b_{\varepsilon}=\pi\int_{0}^{\pi}(1-\cos\theta)(\beta(\theta)\land\varepsilon^{-1}){\rm d}\theta
and observe that,
using (some
\varepsilon
-version of) (
4.9
),

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}^{N,\varepsilon}_{t}=

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}^{N}_{0}+\sum_{1\leq i\neq j\leq N}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}_{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}[v^{\prime}_{ij}(\mathbf{V}^{N,\varepsilon}_{s-},\sigma)-\mathbf{V}^{N,\varepsilon}_{s-}]{\bf 1}_{\{z<B_{\varepsilon}(V^{i,N,\varepsilon}_{s-}-V^{j,N,\varepsilon}_{s-},\sigma)\}}\tilde{\Pi}^{N}_{ij}({\rm d}s,{\rm d}\sigma,{\rm d}z)

\displaystyle+\frac{b_{\varepsilon}}{2(N-1)}\sum_{1\leq i\neq j\leq N}\int_{0}^{t}(\varepsilon^{2}+|V^{i,N,\varepsilon}_{s}-V^{j,N,\varepsilon}_{s}|^{2})^{\gamma/2}(V^{i,N,\varepsilon}_{s}-V^{j,N,\varepsilon}_{s})(\mathbf{e}_{j}-\mathbf{e}_{i}){\rm d}s,

where we recall that
\tilde{\Pi}^{N}_{ij}
is defined in (
4.2
).
Observe that for all
v\in{({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{N}}
,

(\varepsilon^{2}+|v_{i}-v_{j}|^{2})^{\gamma/2}|v_{i}-v_{j}|\leq|v_{i}-v_{j}|^{\gamma+1}

and, by (
4.10
),

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{S}}_{2}}|v^{\prime}_{ij}(v,\sigma)-v|^{2}B_{\varepsilon}(v_{i}-v_{j},\sigma){\rm d}\sigma\leq 2b_{\varepsilon}|v_{i}-v_{j}|^{\gamma+2}\leq 2b|v_{i}-v_{j}|^{\gamma+2}.

Since
-2<1+\gamma<2+\gamma<2
,
the tightness of the family
((\mathbf{V}^{N,\varepsilon}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}))
is easily deduced from (
4.15
)
(using also exchangeability) and (
4.13
).
See the proof Lemma
6.2
for a very similar argument.
It is then standard to pass to the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
and to show the existence,
through martingale problems, of a solution
(\mathbf{V}^{N}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
to (
4
).
See the proof of Theorem
6.1
, of which we give all the details, for a similar but more complicated
demonstration.
The solution
(\mathbf{V}^{N}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
of course inherits exchangeability,
strong conservation (
4.3
), which obviously implies that

E_{N}(F^{N}_{t})=E_{N}(F^{N}_{0})=m_{2}(f_{0})
, where
F^{N}_{t}
is the law of
\mathbf{V}^{N}_{t}
. We also have

I_{N}(F^{N}_{t})\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon}I_{N}(F^{N,\varepsilon}_{t})\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon}I_{1}(f_{0}^{\varepsilon})=I_{1}(f_{0})

by Lemma
2.3
-(e) and (
4.14
). Finally, using the Itô formula for (
4
)
and taking expectations, we find that for all
\phi\in C^{2}_{b}({({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{N}})
, all

,

(4.16)

\int_{({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{N}}\phi(v)F^{N}_{t}({\rm d}v)=\int_{({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{N}}\phi(v)F^{N}_{0}({\rm d}v)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{N}}\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{N}\phi(v)F^{N}_{s}({\rm d}v){\rm d}s,

where for
v\in{({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{N}}
,
\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{N}\phi(v)
is defined by (

).
We have

by Lemma

, and the proof is complete.
∎

5.
The nonlinear stochastic equation

We introduce some stochastic process
(V_{t})_{t\geq 0}
valued in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, representing
the time-evolution of the velocity of a typical particle in the gas. This was initiated by
Tanaka
[
37
]
for Maxwell molecules and by Sznitman
[
34
]
for more general cross sections.
This process should solve the jumping S.D.E.

(5.1)

\displaystyle V_{t}=

\displaystyle V_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}_{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}(v^{\prime}(V_{s-},v_{*},\sigma)-V_{s-}){\bf 1}_{\{z<B(V_{s-}-v_{*},\sigma)\}}\tilde{\Pi}({\rm d}s,{\rm d}v_{*},{\rm d}\sigma,{\rm d}z)

\displaystyle-b\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|V_{s}-v_{*}|^{\gamma}(V_{s}-v_{*})f_{s}({\rm d}v_{*}){\rm d}s,

with
f_{t}
the law of
V_{t}
and with
\Pi({\rm d}s,{\rm d}v_{*}{\rm d}\sigma,{\rm d}z)
a Poisson measure
on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{S}}_{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
with intensity
{\rm d}sf_{s}({\rm d}v_{*}){\rm d}\sigma{\rm d}z
.
This equation is nonlinear in that the time marginals of the solution appear in its dynamics.

Actually, we will not directly deal with (
5.1
), but rather with the corresponding
nonlinear martingale problem. We introduce, on the canonical space
\Omega^{*}={\mathbb{D}}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
,
the canonical process
(V^{*}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
defined by
V^{*}_{t}(w)=w(t)
. We endow
\Omega^{*}

with the Skorokhod topology and the associated
\sigma
-field
\mathcal{F}^{*}=\sigma(V^{*}_{t},t\geq 0)

and filtration
\mathcal{F}^{*}_{t}=\sigma(V_{s}^{*},s\in[0,t])
.

Definition 5.1
.

Assume that
B
satisfies (
1.4
)-(
1.5
) with some
\gamma\in(-3,0)

and consider
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
.
For a probability measure
{\mathbb{Q}}
on
(\Omega^{*},\mathcal{F}^{*},(\mathcal{F}^{*}_{t})_{t\geq 0})
and
t\geq 0
, we set

{\mathbb{Q}}_{t}({\rm d}v)={\mathbb{Q}}(V^{*}_{t}\in{\rm d}v)
. We say that

{\mathbb{Q}}
solves
MP(f_{0})
if the following conditions hold:
{\mathbb{Q}}_{0}=f_{0}
, for all
T>0
,

(5.2)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}m_{2}({\mathbb{Q}}_{t})<\infty,

(5.3)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|v-v_{*}|^{\gamma+1}{\mathbb{Q}}_{s}({\rm d}v_{*}){\mathbb{Q}}_{s}({\rm d}v){\rm d}s<\infty,

and for all
\phi\in C^{2}_{b}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
, the process

M^{\phi}_{t}=\phi(V^{*}_{t})-\phi(V^{*}_{0})-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\bar{\mathcal{A}}\phi(V^{*}_{s},v_{*}){\mathbb{Q}}_{s}({\rm d}v_{*}){\rm d}s

is a martingale under
{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Because of the estimate (
3.12
) and the condition
\gamma+1<\gamma+2<2
, it holds

{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|\bar{\mathcal{A}}\phi(V^{*}_{s},v_{*})|{\mathbb{Q}}_{s}({\rm d}v_{*}){\rm d}s\Big]=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|\bar{\mathcal{A}}\phi(v_{s},v_{*})|{\mathbb{Q}}_{s}({\rm d}v_{*}){\mathbb{Q}}_{s}({\rm d}v){\rm d}s<\infty,

so that this definition makes sense.
A solution to the nonlinear martingale problem provides a solution to the Boltzmann equation.

Proposition 5.2
.

Assume that
B
satisfies (
1.4
)-(
1.5
) with some
\gamma\in(-3,0)

and consider
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
. For
{\mathbb{Q}}
solving
MP(f_{0})
,
({\mathbb{Q}}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a weak solution
to the Boltzmann equation (
1.1
) starting from
f_{0}
.

Proof of Proposition
5.2
.

From (
5.2
), (
5.3
) and
\gamma+1<\gamma+2<2
, we have that
({\mathbb{Q}}_{t})_{t\geq 0}

satisfies (
3.1
) and (
3.2
).
For
\phi\in C^{2}_{b}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and
t\geq 0
, we have
{\mathbb{E}}[M^{\phi}_{t}]=0
,
i.e.

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\phi(v){\mathbb{Q}}_{t}({\rm d}v)-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\phi(v){\mathbb{Q}}_{0}({\rm d}v)-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\bar{\mathcal{A}}\phi(v,v_{*}){\mathbb{Q}}_{s}({\rm d}v_{*}){\mathbb{Q}}_{s}({\rm d}v){\rm d}s=0.

But
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\bar{\mathcal{A}}\phi(v,v_{*}){\mathbb{Q}}_{s}({\rm d}v_{*}){\mathbb{Q}}_{s}({\rm d}v)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\mathcal{A}\phi(v,v_{*}){\mathbb{Q}}_{s}({\rm d}v_{*}){\mathbb{Q}}_{s}({\rm d}v)
by (
3.13
), which completes the proof.
∎

We also have the following uniqueness result.

Proposition 5.3
.

Assume that
B
satisfies (
1.4
)-(
1.5
) for some
\gamma\in(-2,0)
and that

f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
has a finite Fisher information. Then, there is at most one
solution
{\mathbb{Q}}
to
MP(f_{0})
such that

.

Proof of Proposition
5.3
.

Consider two solution
{\mathbb{Q}}
,
{\mathbb{Q}}^{\prime}
to
MP(f_{0})

such that the Fisher informations of both
({\mathbb{Q}}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
and
({\mathbb{Q}}^{\prime}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
are locally integrable.
By Proposition
5.2
and Theorem
3.2
, we conclude that
{\mathbb{Q}}_{t}={\mathbb{Q}}^{\prime}_{t}
for all
t\geq 0
.

Consequently, both
{\mathbb{Q}}
and
{\mathbb{Q}}^{\prime}
solve the
linear
martingale problem
MP((\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{t})_{t\geq 0},f_{0})
, in
the notation of
[
12
, proof of Lemma 4.6, Step 1]
where for
\phi\in C^{2}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
,
t\geq 0

and

,

\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{t}\phi(v)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\bar{\mathcal{A}}\phi(v,v_{*}){\mathbb{Q}}_{t}({\rm d}v_{*}).

Note that
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}\phi
defined in
[
12
, Eq. (6.20)]

equals our
\bar{\mathcal{A}}\phi
. Using Lemma
2.3
-(b), we observe that

({\mathbb{Q}}_{t})_{t\geq 0}\in L^{\infty}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},\mathcal{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))\cap L^{1}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},L^{3}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))\subset L^{\infty}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},\mathcal{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))\cap L^{1}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},J_{\gamma}),

see
[
12
, Eq. (1.12) and (5.2)]
and use that
3>3/(3+\gamma)
. It is worth emphasizing that it is
here we really need the moderately soft potentials restriction

.
It is shown in
[

, proof of Lemma 4.6, Steps 3-4-5]
that
under such a condition,

has a most one solution, when

is a Dirac mass,
but the proof uses only that

. All in all,
{\mathbb{Q}}={\mathbb{Q}}^{\prime}
.
∎

6.
From the Kac system to the nonlinear stochastic equation

In this section, we mix ideas taken from the seminal papers of Sznitman
[
35
,
34
]
and
Méléard
[
28
]
on the chaos issue for the Boltzmann equation with ideas coming from
[
14
]

that concerns
singular interactions (for continuous paths).
We aim to establish that the
N
-particles stochastic
trajectories converge in law to solutions of the nonlinear martingale problem.
Below,
\mathcal{P}({\mathbb{D}}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},{\mathbb{R}}^{3}))
is endowed with the
weak convergence topology,
{\mathbb{D}}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
being endowed with the Skorokhod topology. We refer to
Jacod-Shiryaev
[
25
, Section VI]
for a detailed account of this topology.
We recall in particular that for each
t\geq 0
, the map
\pi_{t}:{\mathbb{D}}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},{\mathbb{R}}^{3})\to{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
defined
by
\pi_{t}(x)=x(t)
is continuous at any point
x
such that
\Delta x(t)=0
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Assume (
1.4
)-(
1.5
) with some
\gamma\in(-2,0)
.
Consider a probability density
f_{0}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
such that
m_{2}(f_{0})
and
I_{1}(f_{0})
are finite and,
for each
N\geq 2
, the process
(\mathbf{V}^{N}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
built in Theorem
4.1
.
Set
\mu^{N}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{(V^{i,N}_{t})_{t\geq 0}}
, which is a random probability measure on
{\mathbb{D}}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
.

(i) The family
(\mu^{N},N\geq 2)
is tight in
\mathcal{P}({\mathbb{D}}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},{\mathbb{R}}^{3}))
.

(ii) Any (possibly random) limit point
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(D({\mathbb{R}}_{+},{\mathbb{R}}^{3}))
of
(\mu^{N},N\geq 2)
a.s.
solves
MP(f_{0})
and we have
\sup_{t\geq 0}{\mathbb{E}}[I_{1}(\mu_{t})]\leq I_{1}(f_{0})
.

(iii) The sequence
(\mu^{N})_{N\geq 2}
converges in probability, as
N\to\infty
, to the unique
solution
{\mathbb{Q}}
of
MP(f_{0})
such that
t\mapsto I_{1}({\mathbb{Q}}_{t})\in L^{1}_{loc}([0,\infty))
.

As recalled in Subsection
1.3

7.
Entropic propagation of chaos

Finally, we give the

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We first show that
{\mathbb{Q}}(\Delta V^{*}_{t}\neq 0)=0
for all
t\geq 0
,
where we recall that
(V^{*}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is the canonical process of
{\mathbb{D}}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
. By Theorem
6.1

and the facts recalled in Subsection
1.3
, we know that

((\mathbf{V}^{N}_{t})_{t\geq 0})_{N\geq 2}
is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-chaotic, so that

(V^{1,N}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
goes in law to
{\mathbb{Q}}
as
N\to\infty
. But the tightness of the family

((V^{1,N}_{t})_{t\geq 0},N\geq 2)
has been checked through the Aldous criterion, see the proof of Lemma
6.2
.
This implies, see Jacod-Shiryaev
[
25
, Section VI, Remark 4.7]
,
that any limit point of
((V^{1,N}_{t})_{t\geq 0},N\geq 2)
is quasi-left
continuous, so that in particular
{\mathbb{Q}}(\Delta V^{*}_{t}\neq 0)=0
for all
t\geq 0
.

For each
t\geq 0
, the map
\pi_{t}:{\mathbb{D}}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},{\mathbb{R}}^{3})\to{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
defined by
\pi_{t}(x)=x(t)
is continuous
at any point
x
such that
\Delta x(t)=0
. Thus
for each

, the map

q(\Delta V^{*}_{t}\neq 0)=0
.

All this implies that for each
t\geq 0
,
(\mathbf{V}^{N}_{t})_{N\geq 2}
is

{\mathbb{Q}}_{t}
-chaotic.
But we know from Proposition
5.2
that
{\mathbb{Q}}_{t}=f_{t}
, where

(f_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is the unique weak solution to (
1.1
) introduced in Theorem
3.2
.
It only remains to prove that for each

,
H_{N}(F^{N}_{t})\to H_{1}(f_{t})
as

,
but this follows from
[

I_{N}(F^{N}_{t})\leq I_{N}(F^{N}_{0})=I_{1}(f_{0})<\infty
.
∎

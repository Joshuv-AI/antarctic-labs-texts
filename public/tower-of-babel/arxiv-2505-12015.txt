The second moment of cubic Dirichlet L-functions over function fields
Shivani Goel, Anwesh Ray

Abstract
. In this article, we study the second moment of cubic Dirichlet L-functions at the central point s = 1 / 2 s=1/2 over the rational function field 𝔽 q ​ ( T ) \mathbb{F}_{q}(T) , where q q is a power of an odd prime satisfying q ≡ 2 ( mod 3 ) q\equiv 2\pmod{3} . Our result extends prior work of David, Florea and Lalin [ DFL22 ] , who obtained an asymptotic formula for the first moment. Our approach relies on analytic techniques (Perron’s formula, approximate functional equation, etc), adapted to the function field context. A key step in the construction is to relate second moment to certain averages of Gauss sums, which are estimated in loc. cit. using results of Kubota [ Kub69 ] and Hoffstein [ Hof92 ] .

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background and motivation

The study of L-functions in the function field setting has long served as a fertile testing ground for deep conjectures in number theory and arithmetic geometry. The arithmetic of the polynomial ring
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
, where
q
is a power of a fixed odd prime
p
, closely parallels the classical theory of the integers, with zeta and
L
-functions playing an analogous role. In this setting, one gains access to powerful tools from algebraic geometry, most notably through the interpretation of
L
-functions as generating series encoding the action of the Frobenius endomorphism on the étale cohomology of algebraic curves over finite fields. For a systematic introduction to the topic, the reader may consult
[
Ros02
,
KW01
,
Tha04
]
.

The study of moments of Dirichlet
L
-functions at the central point
\tfrac{1}{2}
is a fundamental problem in analytic number theory. For quadratic characters
\chi
over the rationals, the first and second moments of
L(\tfrac{1}{2},\chi)
were computed by Jutila
[
Jut81
]
, who established the asymptotic formula

\sum_{0<d\leqslant D}L\left(\tfrac{1}{2},\chi_{d}\right)=\frac{P(1)}{4\zeta(2)}D\left\{\log\left(\frac{D}{\pi}\right)+\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}\left(\tfrac{1}{4}\right)+4\gamma-1+4\frac{P^{\prime}}{P}(1)\right\}+O\left(D^{3/4+\varepsilon}\right),

where
d
ranges over squarefree numbers between
1
and
D
, and
P(s)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{(p+1)p^{s}}\right)
.
Moreover, for the second moment, he showed that

\sum_{0<d\leqslant D}L\left(\tfrac{1}{2},\chi_{d}\right)^{2}=\frac{c_{1}}{\zeta(2)}D\log^{3}D+O\left(D(\log D)^{5/2+\varepsilon}\right),

for an explicit constant
c_{1}>0
.
Related results for the third moment were obtained by Soundararajan
[
Sou00
]
. There are conjectural asymptotics for moments of quadratic Dirichlet
L
-functions over
\mathbb{Q}
due to Keating and Snaith
[
KS00
]
based on random matrix theory. Baier and Young
[
BY10
]
obtained the smoothed first moment for cubic Dirichlet characters over
\mathbb{Q}
. Certain results have also been obtained over
\mathbb{Q}
for thin families of characters by Luo
[
Luo04
]
, Friedberg–Hoffstein–Lieman
[
FHL03
]
. More recently, David, de Faveri, Dunn, and Stucky
[
DdFDS24
]
have computed the first and second moments of
L(1/2,\chi_{q})
for primitive cubic characters
\chi_{q}
with
q\in\mathbb{Z}[\mu_{3}]
satisfying
q\equiv 1\pmod{9}
, thereby establishing an unconditional positive proportion of non-vanishing at the central point.

Over the rational function field, the first results of this nature are due to Hoffstein and Rosen
[
HR92
]
, who obtained an asymptotic formula for the first moment of
L(\frac{1}{2},\chi)
where
\chi
ranges over quadratic Dirichlet characters of the rational function field
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
. Such
L
-functions are closely related to the zeta functions of hyperelliptic curves over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Their methods involve the use of Eisenstein series for the metaplectic
2
-fold cover of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}
. A different proof of this result, using classical methods was obtained by Andrade and Keating
[
AK12
]
. This classical approach was further extended by Florea, who obtained asymptotic formulae for the second, third and fourth moments
[
Flo17b
,
Flo17a
]
.

1.2.
Main results

For cubic characters over the rational function field
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
, an asymptotic formula for the first moment was obtained by David–Florea–Lalin
[
DFL22
]
. Let
L_{q}(s,\chi)
denote the
L
-function associated to a character
\chi
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
. A natural dichotomy arises depending on whether
q\equiv 1\pmod{3}
or
q\equiv 2\pmod{3}
, since in the former case, the third roots of unity are contained in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. Let
\mu_{3}
(resp.
\mu_{3,q}
) denote the group of third roots of unity in
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
(resp. in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
), and fix a bijection
\Omega\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mu_{3}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mu_{3,q}
. Define a character
\chi_{3}
on
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
by setting
\chi_{3}(a)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\Omega^{-1}(a^{\frac{q-1}{3}})
. One refers to the case
q\equiv 1\pmod{3}
as the
Kummer case
, and to
q\equiv 2\pmod{3}
as the
non-Kummer case
.

When
q\equiv 2\pmod{3}
, Theorem 1.1 of
loc. cit.
asserts that:

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\text{ primitive cubic}\\
\mathrm{genus}(\chi)=g\end{subarray}}L_{q}(1/2,\chi)=\frac{\zeta_{q}(3/2)}{\zeta_{q}(3)}C(q)q^{g+2}+O(q^{\frac{7g}{8}+\varepsilon g}),

where
C(q)
is an explicit constant depending only on
q
and
\varepsilon>0
. On the other hand when
q\equiv 1\pmod{3}
, their result applies to primitive cubic characters
\chi
satisfying the condition
\chi|_{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}=\chi_{3}
. Theorem 1.2 of
loc. cit.
states that there exist explicit constants
C_{1}(q),C_{2}(q)>0
such that for
\varepsilon>0
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\text{ primitive cubic}\\
\mathrm{genus}(\chi)=g\\
\chi|_{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}=\chi_{3}\end{subarray}}L_{q}(1/2,\chi)=C_{1}(q)gq^{g}+C_{2}(q)q^{g}+O\left(q^{g\frac{1+\sqrt{7}}{4}+\varepsilon g}\right).

The hypothesis that
\chi
restricts to
\chi_{3}
on
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
serves to simplify the analysis by ensuring that all
L
-functions in the family satisfy a uniform functional equation which is easier to work with. Restrictions of this nature are common in the field, and help simplify many calculations. In
[
AK12
,
Flo17b
,
Flo17a
]
, the assumption that
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
is made. Furthermore, in the latter two works,
q
is assumed to be a prime.

In this article we consider the non-Kummer case, i.e., we assume that
q
is an odd prime power and
q\equiv 2\pmod{3}
. We obtain an asymptotic for the second moment of
L_{q}(\frac{1}{2},\chi)
.

Theorem A
.

With respect to notation above, assume that
q\equiv 2\pmod{3}
, then,

(1.1)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\text{ primitive cubic}\\
\mathrm{genus}(\chi)=g\end{subarray}}L_{q}(1/2,\chi)^{2}=\frac{g(g+2)A_{q}(\frac{1}{q^{2}},\frac{1}{q^{3/2}})\zeta_{q}(3/2)^{2}}{8\zeta_{q}(3)}q^{g+2}+O(q^{g}),

where
A_{q}
is the function defined in (
3.9
).

The case
q\equiv 1\pmod{3}
is not addressed in this paper, as it necessitates the analysis of certain twisted averages of Gauss sums, which have not been studied in the literature. The difficulties arising in this case are substantial and will be addressed in a more general framework in forthcoming work.

1.3.
Methodology

Let us briefly outline the strategy of the proof of Theorem
A
. For any positive integer
n
, let
\mathcal{M}_{q,n}\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
be the set of all monic polynomials of degree
n
. Assume henceforth that
q\equiv 2\pmod{3}
. Let
\mathcal{M}_{q}\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
denote the set of all monic polynomials, and let
\mathcal{M}_{q,\leq n}\subset\mathcal{M}_{q}
denote the subset of monic polynomials of degree
\leq n
. Define
\mathcal{H}_{q,n}
as the subset of
\mathcal{M}_{q,n}
consisting of squarefree monic polynomials. It is easy to see that

\#\mathcal{M}_{q,n}=q^{n},\quad\#\mathcal{M}_{q,\leq n}=1+q+\dots+q^{n}=\frac{q^{n+1}-1}{q-1},\quad\#\mathcal{H}_{q,n}=q^{n}\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right).

Let
\chi
be a cubic character and
L_{q}(s,\chi)
be the associated
L
-function over
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
. Much like number field case,
L_{q}(s,\chi)
satisfies a functional equation (see Lemma
2.2
), which yields the
approximate functional equation
:

(1.2)

\begin{split}L_{q}(1/2,\chi)^{2}=\left(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{q}}\right)^{2}\Bigg(&\sum_{i=0}^{A}(i+1)\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq A-i}}\frac{\chi(f)\,d(f)}{\sqrt{|f|}}\\
&+\sum_{i=0}^{2g-A-1}(i+1)\,\omega(\chi)^{2}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq 2g-A-1-i}}\frac{\bar{\chi}(f)\,d(f)}{\sqrt{|f|}}\Bigg).\end{split}

Here,
d(f)
is the divisor function,
\omega(\chi)
is the sign of
\chi
and the choice of
A
is postponed till the end of the argument. We refer to Proposition
2.3
for further details. Associated to a polynomial
F\in\mathcal{H}_{q^{2},g/2+1}
, we have a primitive cubic Dirichlet character
\chi_{F}
defined in section
2.5
.
The second moment of central L-values over primitive cubic characters of genus
g
is then given by

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\text{ primitive cubic}\\
\mathrm{genus}(\chi)=g\end{subarray}}L_{q}(1/2,\chi)^{2}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\mathcal{H}_{q^{2},\,g/2+1}\\
P\mid F\Rightarrow P\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]\end{subarray}}L_{q}(1/2,\chi_{F})^{2},

where
P
ranges over irreducible monic polynomials dividing
F
. Substituting the approximate functional equation (
1.2
) into the right-hand side and interchanging sums, one has the following

(1.3)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\mathcal{H}_{q^{2},\,g/2+1}\\
P\mid F\Rightarrow P\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]\end{subarray}}L_{q}(1/2,\chi_{F})^{2}=\left(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{q}}\right)^{2}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{A}(i+1)S_{A-i,\mathrm{prin}}+\sum_{i=0}^{2g-A-1}(i+1)S_{2g-A-1-i,\mathrm{dual}}\right),

where

S_{t,\mathrm{prin}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq t}}\frac{d(f)}{\sqrt{|f|}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\mathcal{H}_{q^{2},\,g/2+1}\\
P\mid F\Rightarrow P\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]\end{subarray}}\chi_{F}(f)\right)

is the
principal term
and

S_{t,\mathrm{dual}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq 2g-t-1}}\frac{d(f)}{\sqrt{|f|}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\mathcal{H}_{q^{2},\,g/2+1}\\
P\mid F\Rightarrow P\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]\end{subarray}}\omega(\chi_{F})^{2}\,\bar{\chi}_{F}(f)\right)

is called the
dual term
. Decompose the principal term as

2.
Preliminary notions

In this section, we discuss preliminary notions and set up notation used in the rest of the article.

2.1.
Basic notation

•

Fix an odd prime number
p
, and let
q
be a power of
p
. Denote by
\mathbb{F}_{q}
the finite field with
q
elements.

•

For any nonzero polynomial
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
, define its norm by
|f|\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=q^{\deg(f)}
. Interpreting
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
as the field of functions of
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{/\mathbb{F}_{q}}
, note that
|\cdot|
is the absolute value at
\infty=(T^{-1})
.

•

A polynomial
P\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
is said to be
prime
if it is monic, irreducible, and nonconstant. The nonzero prime ideals of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
are the principal ideals generated by prime polynomials.

•

There is an analog of the Möbius function over
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
. If
f
is a nonzero squarefree polynomial with distinct prime factors
P_{1},\dots,P_{r}
, then
\mu(f)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(-1)^{r}
. If
f
is not squarefree, then
\mu(f)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=0
. The function
\mu
plays an important role in arguments involving inclusion and exclusion.

•

Given a nonzero polynomial
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
,
\varphi(f)
is the number of elements in
\left(\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]/(f)\right)^{\times}
.

•

For
k\geq 2
, the
k
-th divisor function
d_{k}(f)
is given by
d_{k}(f)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{f_{1}\cdots f_{k}=f}1
,
and set
d(f)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=d_{2}(f)
.

2.2.
The zeta function

The zeta function of the affine line
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
is denoted
\zeta_{q}(s)
and is defined for
\operatorname{Re}(s)>1
by the Dirichlet series
\zeta_{q}(s)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{q}}\frac{1}{|f|^{s}}
. One finds that

\zeta_{q}(s)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\left(\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}1\right)q^{-ns}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{n}}{q^{ns}}=\frac{1}{1-q^{1-s}}.

This function admits an Euler product decomposition of the form

\zeta_{q}(s)=\prod_{P}\left(1-|P|^{-s}\right)^{-1},

where the product runs over all prime polynomials
P
. Introducing the variable
u=q^{-s}
, we note that
|u|=q^{-\operatorname{Re}(s)}<1
in the domain of convergence and define

\mathcal{Z}(u)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\zeta_{q}(s)=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}}u^{\operatorname{deg}(f)}=\frac{1}{1-qu}.

We recall a version of Perron’s formula over
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
, which will be used multiple times in this article. Associate to a function
a\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{C}
, the generating series:

\mathcal{A}(u)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}}a(f)u^{\operatorname{deg}(f)}.

Theorem 2.1
.

With respect to notation above, assume that
\mathcal{A}(u)
is absolutely convergent on the disk
|u|\leq r
, where
r\in(0,1)
. Then for
n\geq 1
, the following assertions hold:

(1)

\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}a(f)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{|u|=r}\frac{\mathcal{A}(u)du}{u^{n+1}}

(2)

\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq n}}a(f)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{|u|=r}\frac{\mathcal{A}(u)du}{(1-u)u^{n+1}}
,

where
\oint
is the counterclockwise integral over the circle
|u|=r
.

Proof.

The proof follows from a direct application of Cauchy’s integral formula.
∎

2.3.
Characters and L-polynomials

A Dirichlet character modulo a polynomial
f
is a function
\chi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]\to\mathbb{C}
which is completely multiplicative for which
\chi(g+fh)=\chi(g)
for all polynomials
g,h\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
, with the additional requirement that
\chi(h)\neq 0
if and only if
(h,f)=1
. Alternatively, we may view
\chi
as a homomorphism
\left(\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]/(f)\right)^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. Given two characters
\chi
and
\psi
modulo
(f)
, we set

\delta(\chi,\psi)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }\chi=\psi\\
0&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

One has the following orthogonality relation:

(2.1)

\sum_{g\bmod f}\chi(g)\overline{\psi(g)}=\varphi(f)\delta(\chi,\psi).

For
g,f\in\left(\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]/(f)\right)^{\times}
, set

\delta(f,g)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }f=g;\\
0&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Summing over all Dirichlet characters modulo
(f)
, we have the relation:

(2.2)

\sum_{\chi\bmod f}\chi(g)\overline{\chi(h)}=\varphi(f)\delta(g,h).

Note that if
q\equiv 1\pmod{3}
, then,
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
contains the cube roots of unity. We shall implicitly choose an isomorphism between the cube roots of unity in
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and those in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. Upon making this identification, we let
\chi_{3}
be the cubic character on
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
defined by
\chi_{3}(\alpha)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\alpha^{\frac{q-1}{3}}
. Let
\mathbf{1}
be the trivial character. The characters of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
are
\mathbf{1}
,
\chi_{3}
and
\chi_{3}^{2}
. Note that they are distinct if and only if
q\equiv 4,7\pmod{9}
. We say that a character
\chi
on
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
is even (resp. odd) if it is trivial (resp. nontrivial) on
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. If
q\equiv 2\pmod{3}
, then any cubic character on
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
is even. Define the
\mathcal{L}
-polynomial
associated to
\chi
as follows:

\mathcal{L}(u,\chi)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}}\chi(f)u^{\operatorname{deg}(f)}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\left(\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}\chi(f)\right)u^{n}.

Let
h
be the conductor of
\chi
and assume that
\chi
is not trivial. It follows from the orthogonality relation (
2.1
) that if
n\geq\operatorname{deg}(h)
, then

\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}\chi(f)=0

and thus
\mathcal{L}(u,\chi)
is a polynomial of degree
\leq\operatorname{deg}(h)-1
. Setting
u\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=q^{-s}
we write
L_{q}(s,\chi)=\mathcal{L}(u,\chi)
. Let
C
be a curve over
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
whose function field defines a cyclic cubic extension of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
. By the Weil conjectures, the zeta function of
C
takes the form

\mathcal{Z}_{C}(u)=\frac{\mathcal{P}_{C}(u)}{(1-u)(1-qu)},

where the numerator polynomial
\mathcal{P}_{C}(u)
is given by

\mathcal{P}_{C}(u)=\prod_{j=1}^{g}\left(1-\sqrt{q}ue^{2\pi i\theta_{j}}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{g}\left(1-\sqrt{q}ue^{-2\pi i\theta_{j}}\right),

for some eigenangles
\theta_{j}
,
j=1,\dots,g
. Since the function field of
C
corresponds to a cyclic cubic extension of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
, its arithmetic is naturally described in terms of the two associated cubic Dirichlet characters
\chi
and
\bar{\chi}
. In particular,
\mathcal{P}_{C}(u)
can be expressed in terms of the
L
-functions of these characters. Setting

\mathcal{L}_{C}(u,\chi)=\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}_{q}(u,\chi)&\text{if }\chi\text{ is odd},\\
\frac{\mathcal{L}_{q}(u,\chi)}{1-u}&\text{if }\chi\text{ is even},\end{cases}

one has the factorization:

\mathcal{P}_{C}(u)=\mathcal{L}_{C}(u,\chi)\mathcal{L}_{C}(u,\bar{\chi}).

2.4.
The approximate functional equation

Let
\chi
be a nontrivial, primitive Dirichlet character of conductor
h
. Define the
sign
of
\chi
by

\omega(\chi)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=-q^{-\frac{(\operatorname{deg}(h)-\delta)}{2}}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{deg}(h)-1}}\chi(f),

where

\delta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{cases}1&\text{ for }\chi\text{ odd},\\
2&\text{ for }\chi\text{ even}.\\
\end{cases}

According to the Riemann-Hurwitz formula, we have that

(2.3)

\operatorname{deg}(h)=g+2-\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }\chi\text{ is even,}\\
1&\text{ if }\chi\text{ is odd.}\end{cases}

We state the functional equation for
\mathcal{L}_{q}(u,\chi)
. A natural dichotomy arises, depending on whether
\chi
is odd or even.

Lemma 2.2
.

With respect to notation above, the following assertions hold.

(1)

If
\chi
is odd, we have that

(2.4)

\mathcal{L}_{q}(u,\chi)=\omega(\chi)(\sqrt{q}u)^{g}\mathcal{L}_{q}\left(\frac{1}{qu},\bar{\chi}\right).

(2)

On the other hand, suppose that
\chi
is even, then

(2.5)

\mathcal{L}_{q}(u,\chi)=\omega(\chi)(\sqrt{q}u)^{g}\frac{1-u}{1-\frac{1}{qu}}\mathcal{L}_{q}\left(\frac{1}{qu},\bar{\chi}\right).

Proof.

This result is
[
DFL22
, Lemma 2.3]

∎

The functional equation gives rise to a symmetry in the polynomial expression for
\mathcal{L}_{q}(u,\chi)^{k}
, which turn gives a nice expression for
L(1/2,\chi)^{k}
, commonly known as the
approximate functional equation
.

Proposition 2.3

(Approximate functional equation)
.

Let
\chi
be a nontrivial primitive cubic character of modulus
h
. If
\chi
is odd, then

L(1/2,\chi)^{k}=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq A}}\frac{\chi(f)d_{k}(f)}{\sqrt{|f|}}+\omega(\chi)^{k}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq kg-A-1}}\frac{\bar{\chi}(f)d_{k}(f)}{\sqrt{|f|}}.

On the other hand, if
\chi
is even,

\begin{split}L(1/2,\chi)^{k}=&(1-q^{-1/2})^{k}\sum_{i=0}^{A}\binom{k+i-1}{i}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq A-i}}\frac{\chi(f)d_{k}(f)}{\sqrt{|f|}}\\
+&(1-q^{-1/2})^{k}\omega(\chi)^{k}\sum_{i=0}^{kg-A-1}\binom{k+i-1}{i}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq kg-A-1-i}}\frac{\bar{\chi}(f)d_{k}(f)}{\sqrt{|f|}}.\end{split}

Proof.

First we assume that
\chi
is odd, then by part (1) of Lemma
2.2
, we have that

\mathcal{L}_{q}(u,\chi)^{k}=\omega(\chi)^{k}(\sqrt{q}u)^{kg}\mathcal{L}_{q}\left(\frac{1}{qu},\bar{\chi}\right)^{k}.

Writing

\mathcal{L}_{q}\left(u,\chi\right)^{k}=\sum_{n=0}^{kg}b_{n}(\chi)u^{n}
,
where
b_{n}(\chi)=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}\chi(f)d_{k}(f)
, it follows that

\sum_{n=0}^{kg}b_{n}(\chi)u^{n}=\omega(\chi)^{k}(\sqrt{q}u)^{kg}\sum_{n=0}^{kg}b_{n}(\bar{\chi})\left(\frac{1}{qu}\right)^{n}=\sum_{n=0}^{kg}b_{n}(\bar{\chi})\omega(\chi)^{k}q^{kg/2-n}u^{kg-n}.

Therefore, we have that

b_{n}(\chi)=b_{kg-n}(\bar{\chi})\omega(\chi)^{k}q^{n-kg/2},

i.e.,

\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}\chi(f)d_{k}(f)=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{kg-n}}\omega(\chi)^{k}q^{n-kg/2}\bar{\chi}(f)d_{k}(f).

We find that:

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{q}(u,\chi)^{k}=&\sum_{n=0}^{A}\left(\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}\chi(f)d_{k}(f)\right)u^{n}+\sum_{n=A+1}^{kg}\left(\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}\chi(f)d_{k}(f)\right)u^{n}\\
=&\sum_{n=0}^{A}\left(\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}\chi(f)d_{k}(f)\right)u^{n}+\sum_{n=A+1}^{kg}\left(\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{kg-n}}\omega(\chi)^{k}q^{n-kg/2}\bar{\chi}(f)d_{k}(f)\right)u^{n}\\
=&\sum_{n=0}^{A}\left(\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}\chi(f)d_{k}(f)\right)u^{n}+q^{kg/2}u^{kg}\omega(\chi)^{k}\sum_{n=0}^{kg-A-1}\left(\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}\bar{\chi}(f)d_{k}(f)\right)(qu)^{-n}.\end{split}

Plug in
u=q^{-1/2}
to the above expression to get that

L(1/2,\chi)^{k}=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq A}}\frac{\chi(f)d_{k}(f)}{\sqrt{|f|}}+\omega(\chi)^{k}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq kg-A-1}}\frac{\bar{\chi}(f)d_{k}(f)}{\sqrt{|f|}}.

This proves part (1) of the Proposition.

Next we prove part (2) and assume that
\chi
is even. In this case, we write

F_{\chi}(u)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left(\frac{\mathcal{L}_{q}\left(u,\chi\right)}{(1-u)}\right)^{k}=\sum_{n=0}^{kg}b_{n}(\chi)u^{n}.

By the functional equation in part (2) of Lemma
2.2
,

F_{\chi}(u)=\omega(\chi)^{k}(\sqrt{q}u)^{kg}F_{\bar{\chi}}\left(\frac{1}{qu}\right)

and thus,
b_{n}(\chi)=b_{kg-n}(\bar{\chi})\omega(\chi)^{k}q^{n-kg/2}
.
It follows that

\displaystyle F_{\chi}(u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{A}b_{n}(\chi)u^{n}+\sum_{n=A+1}^{kg}b_{n}(\chi)u^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{A}b_{n}(\chi)u^{n}+\sum_{n=A+1}^{kg}b_{kg-n}(\bar{\chi})\omega(\chi)^{k}q^{n-kg/2}u^{n}

(2.6)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{A}b_{n}(\chi)u^{n}+\omega(\chi)^{k}q^{kg/2}u^{kg}\sum_{n=0}^{kg-A-1}b_{n}(\bar{\chi})(qu)^{-n}.

Now, if we write

\mathcal{L}_{q}\left(u,\chi\right)^{k}=\sum_{n=0}^{kg+k}a_{n}(\chi)u^{n}

where
a_{n}(\chi)=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}\chi(f)d_{k}(f)
. Note that
(1-u)^{-k}\mathcal{L}_{q}\left(u,\chi\right)^{k}=F_{\chi}(u)
. Using the Taylor expansion for
(1-u)^{-k}
gives us the following relation:

(2.7)

b_{n}(\chi)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{k+i-1}{i}a_{n-i}(\chi).

Noting that
\mathcal{L}_{q}\left(u,\chi\right)^{k}=(1-u)^{k}\sum_{n=0}^{kg}b_{n}u^{n}
, we deduce from the functional equation (
2.6
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{q}\left(u,\chi\right)^{k}

\displaystyle=(1-u)^{k}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{A}b_{n}(\chi)u^{n}+\omega(\chi)^{k}q^{kg/2}u^{kg}\sum_{n=0}^{kg-A-1}b_{n}(\bar{\chi})(qu)^{-n}\right).

Setting
u=q^{-1/2}
, we arrive at the following:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{q}\left(1/\sqrt{q},\chi\right)^{k}

\displaystyle=(1-q^{-1/2})^{k}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{A}b_{n}(\chi)q^{-n/2}+\omega(\chi)^{k}\sum_{n=0}^{kg-A-1}b_{n}(\bar{\chi})q^{-n/2}\right)

\displaystyle=(1-q^{-1/2})^{k}\sum_{i=0}^{A}\binom{k+i-1}{i}\left(\sum_{n=i}^{A}a_{n-i}(\chi)q^{-n/2}+\omega(\chi)^{k}\sum_{n=i}^{kg-A-1}a_{n-i}(\bar{\chi})q^{-n/2}\right)

\displaystyle=(1-q^{-1/2})^{k}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{A}\binom{k+i-1}{i}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq A-i}}\frac{\chi(f)d_{k}(f)}{\sqrt{|f|}}+\sum_{i=0}^{kg-A-1}\binom{k+i-1}{i}\omega(\chi)^{k}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq kg-A-1-i}}\frac{\bar{\chi}(f)d_{k}(f)}{\sqrt{|f|}}\right).

∎

For any odd character
\chi
on
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
, let
\tau(\chi)
be the Gauss sum of its restriction to
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
, given by

\tau(\chi)=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\chi(a)\operatorname{exp}\left(\frac{2\pi i\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{p}}(a)}{p}\right),

where
\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{p}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{F}_{q}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}
is the trace map. This satisfies
|\tau(\chi)|=q^{1/2}
, and we define the associated sign

\varepsilon(\chi)=q^{-1/2}\tau(\chi).

On the other hand, if
\chi
is even, simply set
\tau(\chi)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=1
. Given
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\left((\frac{1}{T})\right)
, let
a_{1}
be the coefficient of
\frac{1}{T}
. Hayes defined an exponential function
e_{q}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{F}_{q}\left((\frac{1}{T})\right)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
as follows:

e_{q}(a)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{exp}\left(\frac{2\pi i\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{p}}(a_{1})}{p}\right).

It is easy to check that
e_{q}(\cdot)
has the following properties:

•

e_{q}(a)=1
for
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
,

•

e_{q}(a+b)=e_{q}(a)e_{q}(b)
for all
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\left(\frac{1}{T}\right)
,

•

if
a,b,h\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
are such that
a\equiv b\pmod{h}
, then
e_{q}(a/h)=e_{q}(b/h)
.

For a primitive character
\chi
of modulus
h
on
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
, the Gauss sum is defined as

G(\chi)=\sum_{a\bmod h}\chi(a)e_{q}\left(\frac{a}{h}\right).

The root number is expressed in terms of Gauss sums as follows:

(2.8)

\omega(\chi)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{1}{\tau(\chi)}q^{-\frac{\operatorname{deg}(h)-1}{2}}G(\chi)&\text{if }\chi\text{ is odd},\\
\\
\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{q}}q^{-\frac{\operatorname{deg}(h)-1}{2}}G(\chi)&\text{if }\chi\text{ is even},\end{cases}

cf.
[
DFL22
, Corollary 2.3]
.

2.5.
Primitive characters of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]

We introduce notation for cubic characters. Let
q
be an odd prime power with
q\equiv 1\mod 3
, and let
P
be a prime polynomial in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
. The cubic residue symbol
\chi_{P}
is defined for
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
by

\chi_{P}(a)=\begin{cases}0,&P\mid a,\\
\alpha,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where
\alpha
is the unique root of unity in
\mathbb{C}
satisfying

a^{(q^{\deg P}-1)/3}\equiv\Omega(\alpha)\mod P.

For a monic polynomial
G=P_{1}^{e_{1}}\cdots P_{s}^{e_{s}}
with distinct irreducible factors
P_{i}
, we set

\chi_{G}=\chi_{P_{1}}^{e_{1}}\cdots\chi_{P_{s}}^{e_{s}},

a cubic character modulo
P_{1}\cdots P_{s}
. It is primitive if each
e_{i}
is
1
or
2
, in which case its conductor is the square-free polynomial
F=P_{1}\cdots P_{s}
.

Next, let
q
be an odd prime power with
q\equiv 2\mod 3
, then there is no primitive cubic character if
P
be an irreducible monic polynomial of odd dgree in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
since
3\nmid q^{\operatorname{deg}(P)}-1
. For even degree
P
irreducible monic polynomial and
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
, the cubic residue symbol is

\chi_{P}(a)=\alpha
, where
\alpha
is the unique root of unity in
\mathbb{C}
satisfying

a^{(q^{\deg P}-1)/3}\equiv\Omega(\alpha)\mod P.

Here,
\Omega
takes values in the cubic roots of unity in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. For a monic polynomial
G=P_{1}^{e_{1}}\cdots P_{s}^{e_{s}}
with distinct irreducible factors
P_{i}
of even degree, we set

\chi_{G}=\chi_{P_{1}}^{e_{1}}\cdots\chi_{P_{s}}^{e_{s}},

a cubic character modulo
P_{1}\cdots P_{s}
. It is primitive if each
e_{i}
is
1
or
2
. Its conductor is the square-free polynomial
F=P_{1}\cdots P_{s}
. Consider the Galois extension
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}(T)/\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
with
\operatorname{Gal}\left(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}(T)/\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)\right)=\langle\sigma\rangle
where

(2.9)

\sigma\left(\frac{\sum_{i}a_{i}T^{i}}{\sum_{i}b_{i}T^{i}}\right)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\sum_{i}a_{i}^{q}T^{i}}{\sum_{i}b_{i}^{q}T^{i}}.

Given
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
we set
\tilde{f}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sigma(f)
. Let
P
be a prime of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
. Note that
P
splits in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[T]
if and only if
\operatorname{d}(P)
is even, in which case we write
P=\pi\widetilde{\pi}
, where
\pi
is a prime of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[T]
. It is easy to see that the restriction of
\chi_{\pi}
(resp.
\chi_{\tilde{\pi}}
) to
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
is
\chi_{P}
(resp.
\overline{\chi_{P}}
). Then by running over all the characters
\chi_{F}
where
F\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[T]
is square-free and not divisible by a prime
P
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
, we are counting exactly the characters over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[T]
whose restrictions are cubic characters over
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
, and each character over
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
is counted exactly once.

When
q\equiv 2\mod{3}
, any cubic character of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
is trivial, and hence, any cubic character of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
is even.
By (
2.3
), if
F\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
is the conductor of a cubic primitive character
\chi
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
, it follows that
\operatorname{deg}(F)=g+2
. By the classification above, it follows that
F=P_{1}\ldots P_{s}
for distinct primes of even degree, and the character
(\bmod F)
is the restriction of a character
of conductor
\pi_{1}\ldots\pi_{s}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[T]
, where
\pi_{i}
is one of the primes lying above
P_{i}
. Then the degree of the conductor of this character over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[T]
is equal to
g/2+1
. In what follows,
P
will denote a prime polynomial of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
. We have the following result.

Lemma 2.4
.

For
q\equiv 2\pmod{3}
, we have that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\text{ primitive cubic }\\
\text{ genus }(\chi)=g\end{subarray}}L_{q}(1/2,\chi)^{k}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\mathcal{H}_{q^{2},g/2+1}\\
P\mid F\Rightarrow P\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]\end{subarray}}L_{q}\left(1/2,\chi_{F}\right)^{k},

where
\chi_{F}
is defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[T]
.

We conclude this subsection with an important fact about the values of
\chi_{F}
when restricted to
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
. This result will be invoked frequently in the arguments that follow.

Lemma 2.5
.

Suppose that
q\equiv 2\pmod{3}
,
F
be a squarefree polynomial in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
and
\chi_{F}
is the character associated to
F
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[T]
. Then, for any squarefree
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
, we have that
\chi_{F}(f)=1
.

Proof.

Let
P
be a prime polynomial in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
dividing
F
. Since
\chi_{F}=\prod_{P\mid F}\chi_{P}
, it suffices to show that
\chi_{P}(f)=1
for each such
P
.

First, suppose that
\deg P
is odd. In this case, the prime ideal
(P)
remains inert in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[T]
. By definition,
\chi_{P}(f)=\alpha
, where
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
is the unique root of unity satisfying

f^{(q^{2\deg P}-1)/3}\equiv\Omega(\alpha)\pmod{P}.

The element
f^{(q^{2\deg P}-1)/3}
lies in
\left(\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]/(P)\right)^{\times}
, and is a cube root of unity. But since
\deg P
is odd,
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]/(P)\cong\mathbb{F}_{q^{\deg P}}
, and this field contains no nontrivial cube roots of unity when
q\equiv 2\pmod{3}
. It follows that
\alpha=1
, and hence
\chi_{P}(f)=1
.

Now suppose that
\deg P
is even. Then
P
splits in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[T]
as a product of two distinct monic irreducibles
Q
and
\widetilde{Q}
, with
\widetilde{Q}=\sigma(Q)
, where
\sigma
denotes the generator of
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}(T)/\mathbb{F}_{q}(T))
as defined in (
2.9
). We then have
\chi_{P}=\chi_{Q}\chi_{\widetilde{Q}}
. Moreover,
\sigma
induces an isomorphism

\sigma\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\left(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[T]/(Q)\right)^{\times}\xrightarrow{\sim}\left(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[T]/(\widetilde{Q})\right)^{\times},

which implies that
\chi_{\widetilde{Q}}(f)=\chi_{Q}(f)^{q}
. Since
\chi_{Q}(f)
is a cube root of unity and
q\equiv 2\pmod{3}
, we conclude that
\chi_{\widetilde{Q}}(f)=\chi_{Q}(f)^{-1}
. Thus,

\chi_{P}(f)=\chi_{Q}(f)\chi_{\widetilde{Q}}(f)=1,

as desired.

∎

2.6.
Averages of Gauss sums

For
q\equiv 1\pmod{6}
, let
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
and
\chi_{f}
the associated cubic character. Define the generalized Gauss sum as follows

G_{q}(V,f)=\sum_{u(\bmod f)}\chi_{f}(u)e_{q}\left(\frac{uV}{f}\right).

If
(f_{1},f_{2})=1
, then

G_{q}(V,f_{1}f_{2})=\chi_{f_{1}}(f_{2})^{2}G_{q}(V,f_{1})G_{q}(V,f_{2})=G_{q}(Vf_{2},f_{1})G_{q}(V,f_{2}).

If
V=V_{1}P^{\alpha}
where
P\nmid V_{1}
, then

G_{q}(V,P^{i})=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }i\leq\alpha\text{ and }i\not\equiv 0\pmod{3},\\
\varphi(P^{i})&\text{if }i\leq\alpha\text{ and }i\equiv 0\pmod{3},\\
-|P|_{q}^{i-1}&\text{if }i=\alpha+1\text{ and }i\equiv 0\pmod{3},\\
\varepsilon(\chi_{P^{i}})\omega(\chi_{P^{i}})\chi_{P^{i}}(V_{1}^{-1})|P|_{q}^{i-\frac{1}{2}}&\text{if }i=\alpha+1\text{ and }i\not\equiv 0\pmod{3},\\
0&\text{if }i\geq\alpha+2.\end{cases}

We recall that
\varepsilon(\chi)=1
when
\chi
is even. For the case of
\chi_{P^{i}}
, this happens if
3\mid\deg(P^{i})
. Moreover, if
(a,f)=1
, it is easy to see that

(2.10)

G_{q}(aV,f)=\overline{\chi_{f}}(a)G_{q}(V,f).

For further details, we refer to
[
DFL22
, Lemma 2.12]
.

Remark 2.6
.

Note that the Gauss sum
G_{q^{2}}(V,f)
is defined for odd
q
with
q\equiv 2\pmod{3}
, and we shall make use of its properties in what follows.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let

be a monic polynomial and

. Then the following assertions hold.

(1)

Suppose that

. Then,

\sum_{h\in\mathcal{M}_{q,m}}\chi_{f}(h)=\frac{q^{m}}{|f|_{q}}\left[G_{q}(0,f)+(q-1)\sum_{V\in\mathcal{M}_{q,\leq n-m-2}}G_{q}(V,f)-\sum_{V\in\mathcal{M}_{q,n-m-1}}G_{q}(V,f)\right].

(2)

Suppose that

. Then we have that

Proof.

The result above is
[
DFL22
, Proposition 2.13]
.
∎

3.
Quadratic moments of cubic L-functions

3.1.
Setting up the problem

We now assume that
q
is odd and satisfies
q\equiv 2\bmod{3}
. In particular, the group
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
does not contain the third roots of unity, and consequently, every primitive cubic character over
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
is even. Recalling Lemma
2.4
, we obtain the identity

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\text{ primitive cubic}\\
\text{genus}(\chi)=g\end{subarray}}L_{q}(1/2,\chi)^{k}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\mathcal{H}_{q^{2},\,g/2+1}\\
P\mid F\Rightarrow P\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]\end{subarray}}L_{q}(1/2,\chi_{F})^{k}.

From Proposition
2.3
, the central value of the
L
-function raised to the
k
-th can be expressed as follows

\displaystyle L(1/2,\chi)^{k}

\displaystyle=(1-q^{-1/2})^{k}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{A}\binom{k+i-1}{i}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq A-i}}\frac{\chi(f)d_{k}(f)}{\sqrt{|f|}}\right.

\displaystyle+\left.\sum_{i=0}^{kg-A-1}\binom{k+i-1}{i}\omega(\chi)^{k}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq kg-A-1-i}}\frac{\bar{\chi}(f)d_{k}(f)}{\sqrt{|f|}}\right),

where the value of
A
will be chosen at the end of our analysis. Specializing the above equation to
k=2
, we deduce that

\begin{split}L(1/2,\chi)^{2}=&\left(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{q}}\right)^{2}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{A}(i+1)\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq A-i}}\frac{\chi(f)d(f)}{\sqrt{|f|}}+\sum_{i=0}^{2g-A-1}(i+1)\omega(\chi)^{2}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq 2g-A-1-i}}\frac{\bar{\chi}(f)d(f)}{\sqrt{|f|}}\right).\end{split}

Thus we may write

(3.1)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\mathcal{H}_{q^{2},g/2+1}\\
P\mid F\Rightarrow P\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]\end{subarray}}L_{q}\left(1/2,\chi_{F}\right)^{2}\\
=\left(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{q}}\right)^{2}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{A}(i+1)S_{A-i,\rm{prin}}+\sum_{i=0}^{2g-A-1}(i+1)S_{2g-A-1+i,\rm{dual}}\right),

where

(3.2)

S_{t,\rm{prin}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq t}}\frac{d(f)}{\sqrt{|f|}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\mathcal{H}_{q^{2},g/2+1}\\
P\mid F\Rightarrow P\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]\end{subarray}}\chi_{F}(f)\right)\hskip 9.24994pt\text{and}\hskip 9.24994ptS_{t,\rm{dual}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq 2g-t-1}}\frac{d(f)}{\sqrt{|f|}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\mathcal{H}_{q^{2},g/2+1}\\
P\mid F\Rightarrow P\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]\end{subarray}}\omega(\chi_{F})^{2}\bar{\chi}_{F}(f)\right).

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
A_{q}(z,u)
be the function defined in (
3.9
). Then, for any fixed value of
u
with
|u|<\frac{1}{q^{1/2}}
,
A_{q}(z,u)
is analytic for
|z|<\frac{1}{q}
.

Proof.

Write
A_{q}(z,u)=A_{q,1}(z,u)A_{q,2}(z,u)
, where:

A_{q,1}(z,u)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]\\
\deg(R)\,\mathrm{odd}\end{subarray}}\left[\frac{(1-3u^{2\deg(R)}+2u^{3\deg(R)})}{(1+z^{\deg(R)})}\right]

and

A_{q,2}(z,u)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]\\
\deg(R)\,\mathrm{even}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(1+z^{\frac{\deg(R)}{2}})^{2}}\left[2z^{\frac{\deg(R)}{2}}(1-u^{\deg(R)})^{4}+1-3u^{2\deg(R)}+2u^{3\deg(R)}\right].

We study the convergence of both terms separately, beginning with
A_{q,1}(z,u)
. We write

\begin{split}A_{q,1}(z,u)&=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]\\
\deg(R)\,\mathrm{odd}\end{subarray}}\left[\frac{(1-3u^{2\deg(R)}+2u^{3\deg(R)}))}{(1+z^{\deg(R)})}\right]\\
&=\mathcal{Z}_{q}{(z^{2})}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]\\
\deg(R)\,\mathrm{odd}\end{subarray}}\left((1-3u^{2\deg(R)}+2u^{3\deg(R)})(1-z^{\deg(R)})\right)\times\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]\\
\deg(R)\,\mathrm{even}\end{subarray}}(1-z^{2\deg(R)})\end{split}

We see that the product above converges to a holomorphic function since the series

\sum_{R\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]}(-3u^{2\deg(R)}+2u^{3\deg(R)}-z^{\deg(R)}+3u^{2\deg(R)}z^{\deg(R)}-2u^{3\deg(R)}z^{\deg(R)})

converges uniformly for
|z|<{q^{-1}}
and
|u|<{q^{-1/2}}
. Thus,
A_{q,1}(z,u)
is analytic for the prescribed region.

Next, we consider the product
A_{q,2}(z,u)
. We have the following simplifications:

\begin{split}&A_{q,2}(z,u)\\
&=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]\\
\deg(R)\,\mathrm{even}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(1+z^{\frac{\deg(R)}{2}})^{2}}\left[2z^{\frac{\deg(R)}{2}}(1-u^{\deg(R)})^{4}+1-3u^{2\deg(R)}+2u^{3\deg(R)}\right]\\
&=\mathcal{Z}_{q}^{2}{(z)}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]\\
\deg(R)\,\mathrm{odd}\end{subarray}}{(1-z^{{\deg(R)}})^{2}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]\\
\deg(R)\,\mathrm{even}\end{subarray}}(1+2z^{\deg(R)/2}(1-u^{\deg(R)})^{4}-3u^{2\deg(R)}+2u^{3\deg(R)})(1-z^{\deg(R)/2})^{2}.\end{split}

The product over odd irreducible polynomials is analytic for
|z|<\frac{1}{q}
. To check the region of convergence for the product over even irreducible polynomials, we consider the corresponding sum series

\begin{split}&\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}R\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]\\
\deg(R)\,\mathrm{even}\end{subarray}}}[(1+2z^{\deg(R)/2}(1-u^{\deg(R)})^{4}-3u^{2\deg(R)}+2u^{3\deg(R)})(1-z^{\deg(R)/2})^{2}-1]\\
&=\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}R\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]\\
\deg(R)\,\mathrm{even}\end{subarray}}}\left\{(1+2z^{\deg(R)/2})(1-2z^{\deg(R)/2})-1\right\}+O\left(\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}R\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]\\
\deg(R)\,\mathrm{even}\end{subarray}}}\left(z^{\operatorname{deg}(R)}+z^{\operatorname{deg}(R)/2}u^{\operatorname{deg}(R)}+u^{2\operatorname{deg}(R)}\right)\right)\\
&=4\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}R\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]\\
\deg(R)\,\mathrm{even}\end{subarray}}}z^{\deg(R)}+O\left(\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}R\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]\\
\deg(R)\,\mathrm{even}\end{subarray}}}\left(z^{\operatorname{deg}(R)}+z^{\operatorname{deg}(R)/2}u^{\operatorname{deg}(R)}+u^{2\operatorname{deg}(R)}\right)\right).\end{split}

Therefore,
A_{q,2}(z,u)
is analytic for
|z|<\frac{1}{q}
and
|u|<\frac{1}{q^{1/2}}
.
This proves the lemma.

3.4.
The dual term

Recall the expression of the dual term

(3.20)

S_{t,\rm{dual}}=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq 2g-t-1}}\frac{d(f)}{\sqrt{|f|}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\mathcal{H}_{q^{2},g/2+1}\\
P\mid F\Rightarrow P\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]\end{subarray}}\omega(\chi_{F})^{2}\bar{\chi}_{F}(f)\right).

Note that the restriction of
\chi_{F}
to
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]^{\times}
is an even character of modulus
F\tilde{F}
, where
\tilde{F}
is the Galois conjugate of
F
. From (
2.8
), we find that
\omega(\chi_{F})^{2}=q^{-g-2}G^{2}(\chi_{F})
, where

G(\chi_{F})=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]/(F\tilde{F})}\chi_{F}(a)e_{q}\left(\frac{a}{F\tilde{F}}\right).

By the Chinese remainder theorem, there is a natural bijection

\beta\mapsto\beta\tilde{F}+\tilde{\beta}F
between the residue classes modulo
F
in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[T]
and the residue classes modulo
F\tilde{F}
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
. Therefore, we can compute the Gauss sum
G(\chi_{F})
as follows:

\displaystyle G(\chi_{F})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[T]/(F)}\chi_{F}(\beta\tilde{F})\cdot e_{q}\left(\frac{\beta\tilde{F}+\tilde{\beta}F}{F\tilde{F}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[T]/(F)}\chi_{F}(\beta)\cdot e_{q^{2}}\left(\frac{\beta}{F}\right),

where in the second equality we have used that
\chi_{F}(\tilde{F})=1
, a consequence of cubic reciprocity. Thus, one has that
G(\chi_{F})=G_{q^{2}}(1,F)
,
and consequently,

(3.21)

\omega(\chi_{F})^{2}=q^{-g-2}G_{q^{2}}(1,F)^{2}.

Plugging (
3.21
) into the formula (
3.20
) for the dual term, we deduce that:

(3.22)

S_{t,\rm{dual}}=q^{-g-2}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq 2g-t-1}}\frac{d(f)}{\sqrt{|f|}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\mathcal{H}_{q^{2},g/2+1}\\
P\mid F\Rightarrow P\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]\end{subarray}}G_{q^{2}}(1,F)^{2}\bar{\chi}_{F}(f)\right)

For
(f,F)=1
, it follows from (
2.10
) that

(3.23)

G_{q^{2}}(1,F)\,\bar{\chi}_{F}(f)=G_{q^{2}}(f,F).

On the other hand, if
(f,F)\neq 1
, then
\bar{\chi}_{F}(f)=0.

Moreover, by
[
DFL22
, Lemma 4.4]
, if
F\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
is square-free, then

(3.24)

G_{q^{2}}(1,F)=q^{\deg F}=q^{g/2+1}.

Combining (
3.23
) and (
3.24
), we obtain

(3.25)

G_{q^{2}}(1,F)^{2}\,\bar{\chi}_{F}(f)=G_{q^{2}}(f,F)\,q^{g/2+1}.

Substitute (
3.25
) into the expression (
3.22
) to get that

S_{t,\rm{dual}}=q^{-g/2-1}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq 2g-t-1}}\frac{d(f)}{\sqrt{|f|}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\mathcal{H}_{q^{2},g/2+1},\\
P\mid F\Rightarrow P\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}[T],\\
(F,f)=1\end{subarray}}G_{q^{2}}(f,F)\right).

Then (
3.4
) implies that

\displaystyle S_{t,\rm{dual}}

\displaystyle=q^{-g/2-1}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq 2g-t-1}}\frac{d(f)}{\sqrt{|f|}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\mathcal{M}_{q^{2},g/2+1}\\
(F,f)=1\end{subarray}}G_{q^{2}}(f,F)\sum_{D\mid F}\mu(D)

\displaystyle=q^{-g/2-1}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq 2g-t-1}}\frac{d(f)}{\sqrt{|f|}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}D\in\mathcal{M}_{q^{2},g/2+1}\\
(D,f)=1\end{subarray}}\mu(D)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\mathcal{M}_{q^{2},g/2+1-\deg(D)}\\
(F,fD)=1\end{subarray}}G_{q^{2}}(f,DF)

\displaystyle=q^{-g/2-1}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq 2g-t-1}}\frac{d(f)}{\sqrt{|f|}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}D\in\mathcal{M}_{q^{2},g/2+1}\\
(D,f)=1\end{subarray}}\mu(D)G_{q^{2}}(f,D)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\mathcal{M}_{q^{2},g/2+1-\deg(D)}\\
(F,fD)=1\end{subarray}}G_{q^{2}}(Df,F).

From Proposition
2.8
we deduce that

\begin{split}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\mathcal{M}_{q^{2},g/2+1-\deg(D)}\\

&+O\left(\delta_{f_{2}=1}q^{\frac{g}{3}+\varepsilon g-\deg(D)(1+2\varepsilon)-\frac{\deg(f)_{1}}{3}}\right)+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{|u|=q^{-2\sigma}}\frac{\tilde{\Psi}_{q^{2}}(fD,u)}{u^{g/2+1-\deg(D)}}\,\frac{du}{u},\end{split}

We write

, where

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q^{5g/6+5/3}}{\zeta_{q^{2}}(2)}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{q,\leq g-t-1}}\frac{\delta_{f_{2}=1}d(f)q^{-\frac{8}{3}[g/2+1+\deg(f_{1})]_{3}}}{q^{\deg(f)/2+\deg(f_{1})/3}}\rho(1,[g/2+1+\deg(f_{1})]_{3})

\displaystyle\times\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[T]\\
P\mid f\end{subarray}}\left(1+\frac{1}{|P|_{q^{2}}}\right)^{-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}D\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]\\
\deg(D)\leq g/2+1\\
(D,f)=1\end{subarray}}\mu(D)q^{-2\deg(D)}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[T]\\
P\mid D\end{subarray}}\left(1+\frac{1}{|P|_{q^{2}}}\right)^{-1}

and

(3.26)

\begin{split}E_{t}&=O\left(q^{-g/2-1}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq 2g-t-1}}\frac{d(f)}{\sqrt{|f|}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}D\in\mathcal{M}_{q^{2},g/2+1}\\

In order to evaluate the sum over
D
, we introduce its generating function, given by

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}D\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]\\
(D,f)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(D)}{q^{2\deg(D)}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[T]\\
P\mid D\end{subarray}}\left(1+\frac{1}{|P|_{q^{2}}}\right)^{-1}w^{\deg(D)},

which can be rewritten as the Euler product

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]\\
\deg(R)\,\mathrm{odd}\\
R\nmid f\end{subarray}}\left[1-\frac{w^{\deg(R)}}{q^{2\deg(R)}(1+\frac{1}{q^{2\deg(R)}})}\right]\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]\\
\deg(R)\,\mathrm{even}\\
R\nmid f\end{subarray}}\left[1-\frac{w^{\deg(R)}}{q^{2\deg(R)}(1+\frac{1}{q^{\deg(R)}})^{2}}\right].

Here, we have determined the prime divisors in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[T]
by counting their underlying prime divisors in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
. Letting
A_{\mathrm{dual},R}(w)
and
B_{\mathrm{dual},R}(w)
denote the first and second factors in the above product, respectively, we define

\mathcal{J}(w)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]\\
\deg(R)\,\mathrm{odd}\end{subarray}}A_{\mathrm{dual},R}(w)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]\\
\deg(R)\,\mathrm{even}\end{subarray}}B_{\mathrm{dual},R}(w).

This series is absolutely convergent for
|w|<q
. By applying Perron’s formula, we obtain

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}D\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]\\
\deg(D)\leq g/2+1\\

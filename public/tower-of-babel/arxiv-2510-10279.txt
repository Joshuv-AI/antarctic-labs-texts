Is a complete Riemannian manifold with positively pinched Ricci curvature compact
Lei Ni

Abstract
. A result of R. Hamilton asserts that any convex hypersurface in an Euclidian space with pinched second fundamental form must be compact. Partly inspired by this result, twenty years ago, in [ 23 ] , Remark 3.1 on page 650, the author formulated a problem asking if a complete Riemannian manifold with positively pinched Ricci curvature must be compact. There are several recent progresses, which are all rigidity results concerning the flat metric except the special case for the steady solitons. In this note we provide a detailed alternate proof of Hamilton’s result, in view of the recent proof via the mean curvature flow requiring additional assumptions and that the original argument by Hamilton does lack of complete details. The proof uses a result of the author in 1998 concerning quasi-conformal maps. The proof here allows a generalization as well. We dedicate this article to commemorate R. Hamilton, the creator of the Ricci flow, who also made fundamental contributions to many other geometric flows.



1.
Introduction

In the study of the convergence and asymptotic shapes of the mean curvature flow, motivated by a simplified alternate proof of Huisken’s convergence result, Richard Hamilton
[
11
]
proved the following result.

Theorem 1.1

(Hamilton, 94)
.

Let
M^{n}(n\geq 2)
be a smooth strictly convex complete hypersurface
bounding a region in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Suppose that its second fundamental form
is
\epsilon
-pinched in the sense that

II_{ij}\geq\epsilon Hg_{ij}

(1.1)

where
g_{ij}
is the induced Riemannian metric of
M
,
II_{ij}
the second fundamental form,
and its trace
H
is the mean curvature, for some
\epsilon>0
. Then
M^{n}
is compact.

As explained in
[
11
]
, the result indeed simplifies the proof of the convergence to the round sphere, a result of Huisken. Another simplification of Huisken’s result was via the scalar PDE of mean curvature flow in terms of the support function and was obtained in
[
1
]
around the same time (the method also applies to a vast class of flows including the ones by
1/n
root of the Gauss curvature and square root of the scalar curvature).
The recent detailed proof of the above result in
[
4
]
does not provide such a simplification to the convergence since it uses the study of the mean curvature flow (in addition it requires the assumption of the boundedness of the second fundamental form). We first provide a self-contained proof using a result of
[
20
]
. This approach was known to the author before 2004, the writing of
[
23
]
. First we recall the following result of
[
20
]
.

Theorem 1.2

(Ni, 98)
.

Let
M^{n}
be a complete Riemannian manifold satisfying that

\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{V(o,r)}{r^{n}}=0

(1.2)

where
V(o,r)
denotes the volume of the ball of radius
r
centered at
o\in M
. And let
N^{n}
be a Riemannian manifold with
\lambda_{1}(N)>0
, where
\lambda_{1}(N)
is the lower bound of the
spectrum of the Laplacian–Beltrami operator. Then there is no quasi-conformal
diffeomorphism from
M
into
N
.

Note here
N
is not assumed to be complete. We say that the lower bound of the
spectrum of the Laplacian–Beltrami operator
\lambda_{1}(N)>0
if for such
\lambda_{1}

\int_{N}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\geq\lambda_{1}\int_{N}\varphi^{2},\forall\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(N).

The link between Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
is the Gauss map
\nu
of
M
, which is a quasi-conformal diffeomorphism between
M^{n}
and the round sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, under the assumption of (
1.1
). The additional input is Hamilton’s conformal deformation via an ‘entropy function’.

The first purpose of the paper is to provide a detailed alternate proof of Theorem
1.1
via Theorem
1.2
. Since the proof of Theorem
1.2
was sketched in
[
20
]
, we include the details here as well as an extension. The original motivation of Theorem
1.2
is the study of quasi-conformal harmonic diffeomorphisms of Li, Tam and Wang
[
26

. The key component of Theorem
1.2
is to drop the assumption on the harmonicity of the map.

Partially inspired by Hamilton’s result we proposed the following problem in
[

]

Problem 1.3

Let
M^{n}

{\operatorname{Ric}}_{ij}\geq\epsilon Sg_{ij}

Besides the above theorem of Hamilton, the other motivation of the problem is the Bonnet-Myer’s theorem on the closeness of a Riemannian manifold with its Ricci curvature being bounded from below by a positive constant.
After we finished the draft of

Given that the result implies Hamilton’s convergence result of the Ricci flow for three manifolds with positive Ricci curvature, an alternate approach (without using the Ricci flow) via the above problem in dimension three must have been what R. Hamilton had in mind. Since the Ricci flatness does not imply the flatness for manifolds with dimension greater than three, the problem above is of completely different nature for dimensions greater than three. Therefore we shall not put emphasis on the recent progresses of the three dimensional case

Preliminaries

Let

By the Hadamard-Stoker theorem and its high dimensional generalization (cf. Theorem 6.5 of

3.
Proof of theorems

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is via contradiction argument.
First by the result of the last section we have the following corollary.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
M^{n}
be a smooth strictly convex complete hypersurface
bounding a region in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Suppose that its second fundamental form
is
\epsilon
-pinched in the sense of (
1.1
).
If
M
is not compact, then
V(M,\tilde{g})<\infty
and
\nu:(M,\tilde{g})\to\overline{\mathbb{S}^{n}}_{+}
is a quasi-conformal map.

Now Theorem
1.1
follows from Theorem
1.2
and the above corollary. Hence we only need to supply the proof of Theorem
1.2
since it is clear that
\lambda_{1}\left(\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+})\right)>0
, where
\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+})
is a
\delta
-neighborhood of the closed upper semi-sphere.

To prove Theorem
1.2
we also argue by contradiction. Let
\nu:(M,\tilde{g})\to N
be such a quasi-conformal map. First for a cut-off function
\phi
on
M

\varphi=\phi(\nu^{-1})
which is defined on
\nu(M)
and has a compact support since
\nu
is a diffeomorphism from
M

\nu(M)
. By the assumption and Lemma 2.2 of

]
we have that

A(n,\lambda_{1})\int_{N}|\nabla^{N}\varphi|^{n}\,dv_{N}\geq\int_{N}\varphi^{n}\,dv_{N}.

(3.1)

On the other hand, let
\kappa_{i}
be the singular value of
d\nu
(at any given point

\kappa^{2}_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\kappa_{n}^{2}
(with respect to
\tilde{g}
and the metric on

). Then the pinching condition (

) implies that
\frac{\kappa_{i}}{\kappa_{j}}\geq\epsilon
. Hence

\int_{N}|\nabla^{N}\varphi|^{n}\,dv_{N}\leq A^{\prime\prime}\int_{M}|\tilde{\nabla}^{M}\phi|^{n}\,d\tilde{v}_{M}

(3.2)

where

. Indeed, choose two normal coordinates centered at

respectively, such that

. Point wisely, by abusing the notation involving the change of the volume form in terms of the change of the coordinates, we have at

\displaystyle|\tilde{\nabla}^{M}\phi|^{n}\,d\tilde{v}_{M}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum\left(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x^{i}}\right)^{2}\right)^{n/2}d\tilde{v}_{M}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum\left(\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial y^{i}}\right)^{2}\kappa_{i}^{2}\right)^{n/2}\frac{1}{\kappa_{1}\cdots\kappa_{n}}dv_{N}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\epsilon^{n}|\nabla^{N}\varphi|^{n}\,dv_{N}.

4.
A generalization of Theorem
1.2
and testing cases for the Problem
1.3

For Bonnet-Myer’s theorem, Calabi proved a generalization. For
(M^{n},g)
a complete Riemannian manifold, if we denote the lower bound of the Ricci curvature on
\partial B(o,r)
by
k(r)>0
, the following result was proved in
[
7
]

Theorem 4.1
.

[Calabi] Assume that

\limsup_{a\to\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{a}\sqrt{k(s)}\,ds-\frac{1}{2}\log a\right)=\infty.

Then
M
must be compact.

Motivated by this and the theorem we just proved, for a diffeomorphism
\nu:M\to N
, we introduce

\epsilon(r)=\inf_{x\in\partial B(o,r)}\{\min_{i,j\in\{1,\cdots,n\}}\frac{\kappa_{i}(x)}{\kappa_{j}(x)}\}.

Here
\kappa_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\kappa_{n}
are the singular values of
d\nu
.

The proof of the last section can be adapted to prove the following result.

Proposition 4.1
.

If
(M^{n},\tilde{g})
is a complete Riemannian manifold with finite volume. Assume that
(N,g^{N})
has a positive lower bound on the spectrum the Laplacian–Beltrami operator, or more generally satisfies one of the following two Sobolev type inequalities

\displaystyle\left(\int_{N}|\varphi|^{q}\,dv_{N}\right)^{p/q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(p,q)\int_{N}|\nabla\varphi|^{p}\,dv_{N},\mbox{ with }0<p<n,0<q<\frac{np}{n-p},

(4.1)

\displaystyle\mbox{ or }\left(\int_{N}|\varphi|^{q}\,dv_{N}\right)^{n/q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(n,q)\int_{N}|\nabla\varphi|^{n}\,dv_{N},\mbox{ with }q>0

(4.2)

for
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(N)
.
Then there is no diffeomorphism
\nu:M\to N
satisfying that

\lim_{R\to\infty}\epsilon(R)R=\infty.

Proof.

Under the Sobolev inequality (
4.1
) we write
\psi=|\varphi|^{\gamma}
, with
\gamma=\frac{pn}{pn-q(n-p)}
. It is easy to check that
\gamma q=(\gamma-1)\frac{pn}{n-p}
. Now apply (
4.1
) to
\psi
and the Hölder’s inequality we then have that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{N}|\varphi|^{\gamma q}\right)^{p/q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{N}|\varphi|^{(\gamma-1)p}|\nabla\varphi|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C^{\prime}\left(\int_{N}|\nabla\varphi|^{n}\right)^{\frac{p}{n}}\left(\int_{N}|\varphi|^{(\gamma-1)\frac{pn}{n-p}}\right)^{\frac{n-p}{n}}.

Hence we have the following Sobolev type inequality

\left(\int_{N}|\varphi|^{\gamma q}\,dv_{N}\right)^{\frac{n}{q}-\frac{n-p}{p}}\leq C^{\prime\prime}(p,q,n)\int_{N}|\nabla\varphi|^{n}\,dv_{N}

(4.3)

for any
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(N)
.

Now we apply the argument of last section to
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(M)
, which equals to
1
in
B(o,R)
, and has a compact support in
B(o,2R)
with
\nabla^{M}\phi|\leq\frac{C}{R}
in
B(o,2R)\setminus B(o,R)
, and
\nabla\phi=0
in
B(o,R)
. Let
\varphi(y)=\phi(\nu^{-1}(y))
which is a function with a compact support within
\nu(B(o,2R))
. Now (
3.2
) can be improved to the following estimate:

\int_{N}|\nabla^{N}\varphi|^{n}\,dv_{N}\leq\frac{1}{\inf_{r\in[R,2R]}\epsilon^{n}(r)}\int_{M}|\tilde{\nabla}^{M}\phi|^{n}\,d\tilde{v}_{M}.

(4.4)

Combining this with (
4.3
) we have for
R\geq 1

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B(0,1)}\left(\Pi_{i=1}^{n}\kappa_{i}\right)d\tilde{v}_{M}\right)^{\frac{n}{q}-\frac{n-p}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{M}\phi^{\gamma q}\left(\Pi_{i=1}^{n}\kappa_{i}\right)d\tilde{v}_{M}\right)^{\frac{n}{q}-\frac{n-p}{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{N}\varphi^{\gamma q}\,dv_{N}\right)^{\frac{n}{q}-\frac{n-p}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C^{\prime\prime}\int_{N}|\nabla^{N}\varphi|^{n}\,dv_{N}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C^{\prime\prime}\frac{1}{\inf_{r\in[R,2R]}\epsilon^{n}(r)}\int_{M}|\tilde{\nabla}^{M}\phi|^{n}\,d\tilde{v}_{M}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C^{\prime\prime\prime}\frac{\tilde{V}(o,2R)}{([\inf_{r\in[R,2R]}\epsilon(r)]\cdot R)^{n}}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle 0,\mbox{ as }R\to\infty.

This yields a contradiction, which proves the proposition for the case that (
4.1
) is assumed on
N
. The proofs for the other two cases are similar.

) is preserved by the mean curvature flow. And similarly, it can be shown that the condition (
1.3
) is preserved by the Ricci flow for
n=3
, however is a condition most likely not preserved in dimensions greater than three. Hence it would be highly interesting if the Ricci flow method can be applied to study Problem
1.3
.

For the high dimensional case, the first progress was made by B. Wu with the author in
[
24
]
, however under a stronger condition that the curvature operator is pinched and the curvature is bounded. The proof is to study the singularity analysis of Ricci flow. The stronger assumption of the curvature operator pinching is necessary here since one needs to fit the curvature operator pinching assumption into one of the Ricci flow invariant cone conditions of Böhm-Wilking
[
3
]
. The proof also needs Corollary 3.1 of
[
23
]
. We note that the pinching condition (
1.3
) is one of the two conditions in the definition of an invariant (with respect to the Ricci flow) cone condition of
[
3
]
Böhm-Wilking (cf. Lemma 3.2 of
[
24
]
). In
[
15
]
Lee-Topping made nice further progresses and obtained results under the complex sectional curvature pinching, as well as the so-called PIC1 pinching, with geometric additional assumptions. The PIC1 is a high dimensional generalization of the Ricci curvature, which is preserved under the Ricci flow. Note that the positivity of PIC1 and complex sectional curvature are stronger than (or at the least different from) the positivity of the Ricci curvature in dimensions greater than three. Moreover the results obtained so far are all of rigidity type results concerning the flat metric, while Problem
1.3
concerns the flatness of the Ricci curvature instead. The main result of
[
15
]
as an excellent progress in terms of extending
[
24
]
, particularly removes the curvature bound condition, however avoids Problem
1.3
.

Here we point out that if
M
is a gradient Ricci shrinking soliton, then the answer to Problem
1.3
is positive. Namely

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
(M,g,f)
be a gradient Ricci shrinking soliton in the sense that

\nabla^{2}_{ij}f+{\operatorname{Ric}}_{ij}-\frac{1}{2}g_{ij}=0.

(4.5)

Here
f
is the potential function. Assume further that it satisfies that (
1.3
). Then either
(M,g)
is Ricci flat (in fact isometric to
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
) or
(M,g)
is compact.

The key is the Proposition 1.1 of
[
23
]
, which provides a uniform lower bound of the scalar curvature for gradient shrinking solitons with nonnegative (nonzero) Ricci curvature.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
(M,g,f)
be a Ricci non-flat gradient shrinking soliton.
Assume that the Ricci curvature of M is nonnegative. Then there exists a

\delta=\delta(M)
with
1\geq\delta>0
, such that

S(x)\geq\delta.

(4.6)

Now Proposition
4.2
is a simple consequence of Proposition
4.3
and the Bonnet-Myers’ theorem. The flatness is elementary. By the Ricci flatness and soliton equation we have
g_{ij}=\nabla^{2}_{ij}f
. The rest argument then is detailed in
[
9
]
. One may also use the argument on page 94 of
[
22
]
.
Letting the point
o
be the point
\nabla f=0
, without the loss of the generality we may assume that
f(o)=0
. Then integrating along the minimizing geodesics from
o
to any point
x\in M
as in Section 2 one can check that
f(x)=\frac{1}{2}r^{2}(x)
, where
r(x)
denotes the distance function to
o
. While tracing the soliton equation we have
\Delta f=n
, which, after applying the Green’s formula, implies that for
R>0

\frac{RA(o,R)}{V(o,R)}=n

where
A(o,R)
denotes the area of
\partial B(o,R)
. The equality case implies the flatness of
B(o,R)
for all
R
. (The strict convexity of
f
implies that
M
is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.)

For noncompact gradient steady and expanding solitons, Problem
1.3
is to show the Ricci flatness under the assumption (
1.3
). This was verified in
[
23
]
under the additional assumption that the manifold has nonnegative sectional curvature. The details of the proofs were in
[
25
]
for the reason that the journal where
[
23
]
published put a page limit for
[
23
]
. A natural question, as testing cases of Problem
1.3
, is whether or not the result can be proved without the additional assumption that the manifold has nonnegative sectional curvature. In
[
9
]
, Deng-Zhu proved the Ricci flatness for the noncompact expanding gradient solitons for the Kähler case under the assumption (
1.3
). They also proved the same result for the steady gradient solitons assuming that they are Kähler and the potential function
f
has a critical point somewhere. The proof is based on the decay estimate established in
[
25
]
and an estimate of the quotient of volume elements for the Kähler-Ricci flow (Theorem 2.1 of
[
21
]
) via the Green’s function estimate of Li-Yau. In
[
9
]
, the authors proved a rigidity result for the Kähler gradient expanding soliton, namely the soliton is Gaussian, under the assumption

k(o,r):=\frac{1}{V(o,r)}\int_{B(o,r)}S(y)\,dv(y)\leq\frac{\epsilon(r)}{1+r^{2}}

with
\lim_{r\to\infty}\epsilon(r)\to 0
. In fact, Theorem 2.1 of
[
21
]
implies the following result of the Ricci flatness without the pinching condition, but with some others.

Theorem 4.2

(Ni-Tam)
.

If
(M,g_{0})
is complete Kähler manifold with nonnegative Ricci curvature. Assume that there exists a Kähler-Ricci flow
g(t)
with
g(0)=g_{0}
, for all time
t\geq 0
, and
g(t)
has nonnegative Ricci curvature. Assume further that the scalar curvature
S_{0}(y)
of
g_{0}
satisfies that

\frac{1}{V_{0}(x,r)}\int_{B_{0}(x,r)}S_{0}(y)dv_{0}(y)\leq k(r),\mbox{ and }\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{\int_{0}^{R}sk(s)\,ds}{\log R}=0,

(4.7)

where
B_{0}(x,R)
is the ball of radius
R
centered at
x
,
V_{0}(x,R)
its volume and
S_{0}(x)
the scalar curvature with respect to
g_{0}
. In addition also assume that
S(x,t)\leq Ct^{\alpha},\forall t>>1
, for some
\alpha>0
and the Harnack estimate
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(tS(x,t)\right)\geq 0
holds.
Then
(M,g_{0})
is Ricci flat.

The same result holds if
S(x,t)\leq C
,
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}S(x,t)\geq 0
and the assumption (
4.7
) is replaced with

\lim_{R\to\infty}Rk(R)=0.

(4.8)

Proof.

With the notation of
[
21
]
, it suffices to show that
-m(t)=o(\log(t))
. Then the assumed Harnack estimate finishes the argument.

The assumption of
S(x,t)
implies a rough estimate that
-m(t)\leq Ct^{\alpha+1},\forall t>>1
. The assumption of
k(s)
and Theorem 2.1 of
[
21
]
implies that there is a constant
C_{1}=C_{1}(m)
,

-m(t)\leq C_{1}\left[\left(1+\frac{t(1-m(t))}{R^{2}}\right)\int_{0}^{R}sk(s)\,ds-\frac{tm(t)(1-m(t))}{R^{2}}\right]

for any
R>0
. Picking
R=t^{\alpha+3}
, with
t>>1
, applying the rough estimate
-m(t)\leq Ct^{\alpha+1}
to the right hand side above, we have an improved estimate

-m(t)\leq C_{1}\int_{0}^{R}sk(s)\,ds+o(1).

The needed estimate
-m(t)=o(\log t)
follows by noticing
R=t^{\alpha+3}
and the assumption that
\int_{0}^{R}sk(s)\,ds
is of
o(\log(R))
.

For the second case, the proof is basically the same as that of
[
9
]
. The stronger Harnack estimate
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}S(x,t)\geq 0
implies that it is sufficient to show
-F(x,t)=o(t)
.

Let
R=\frac{t}{\epsilon(t)}
for some
\epsilon(t)
with
\lim_{t\to\infty}\epsilon(t)=0
. Namely
R
is slightly faster than
t
as
t\to\infty
. We may assume that
\epsilon(t)
is monotone decreasing function. Its precise form can be decided by the another function
\epsilon^{\prime}(R)
which is chosen so that

\int_{0}^{R}sk(s)\,ds\leq\epsilon^{\prime}(R)R

with
\lim_{R\to\infty}\epsilon^{\prime}(R)\to 0
and
\epsilon^{\prime}(R)
monotone non-increasing. Now we may simply let
\epsilon(t)=\sqrt{\epsilon^{\prime}(t)}
.

Now we apply Theorem 2.1 of
[
21
]
and the rough estimate
-m(t)\leq Ct
(which follows from
S(x,t)\leq C
) to get an improved (or enforced) estimate

-m(t)=o(t).

This implies the claimed Ricci flatness via
S(x,0)=0
.

\square

Variations of the above result include Corollary 2.1-2.5 of
[
21
]
.
This extends the result of
[
9
]
a little. All assumptions of the theorem, except the one for the average of the scalar curvature of the initial metric hold for the case of Kähler-Ricci flow from a metric with nonnegative bisectional curvature and maximum volume growth, as well as the expanding and steady gradient Kähler solitons with nonnegative Ricci curvature. Hence it implies a Ricci flatness result for them. A result similar to Theorem
4.2
can be formulated for the intermediate cases with
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(t^{\theta}S(x,t))\geq 0
for some
\theta\in[0,1]
.

Another testing case for Problem
1.3
is when there is a solution to the Poincaré-Lelong equation, namely a smooth function
u
such that
\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}u={\operatorname{Ric}}
.

Related to the Ricci nonnegative Kähler manifold, a favorite problem of the author is to show the Liouville type theorem for plurisubharmonic functions on a complete Kähler manifold
M^{m}
with nonnegative Ricci curvature,
namely any sub-
log(d(o,x))
growth plurisubharmonic function
u
must be constant.
In author’s Ph. D. thesis of 1998, it was proved that
(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u)^{m}=0
. This is also related to understanding the structure of manifolds whose Ricci curvature degenerates, but with some high ranks.

In summary, the answer to Problem
1.3
is affirmative for all shrinking gradient solitons, and essentially all gradient Kähler steady and expanding solitons. Moreover for the case of Kähler-Ricci expanding solitons, Deng-Zhu
[
9
]
proved that the manifold is isometric to the complex Euclidean space. Hence even though the answers to Problem
1.3
are affirmative for gradient Ricci (Kähler-Ricci) soltions (for steady gradient solitons one needs to assume additionally that there is a point where
\nabla f=0
), it is a rigidity result of the flat metric (the Gaussian soliton) for the case of expanding gradient solitons. The Ricci flatness can be obtained via the Kähler-Ricci flow, however assuming
the long time existence and a differential Harnack estimate
and others.

For the solitons which are not Kähler, Theorem 20.2 of
[
12
]
reduces the problem to the existence of a Ricci flat metric on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Namely
if there is no Ricci flat metric on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, then Problem
1.3
has a positive answer for the gradient steady soliton assuming additionally the potential function
f
with
\nabla f(o)=0
at some point
o
. Compared with the proof in Sections 2 and 3 of Theorem
1.1
the main difficulty for Problem
1.3
is lack of some ‘convexity’ associated with the nonnegativity of the Ricci curvature.

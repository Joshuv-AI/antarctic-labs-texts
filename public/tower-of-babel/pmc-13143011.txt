ST segment straightening and sharp ST-T angle in acute coronary syndrome
Authors: Yusuke Namba, Kohei Kawamura, Yusuke Kawai
Published: 2026

Case description

A 55-year-old male presented with persistent chest pain. He had experienced a stroke 18 months before admission; electrocardiogram (ECG) at that time showed nearly normal findings (Figure 1A). On presentation, he was afebrile with normal vital signs. His initial ECG showed no ST-segment elevation or depression beyond 0.5 mm; however, abnormally straightened ST segments and sharp ST-T angles were present in leads V4–V6 (Figure 1B). The following morning, the cardiologist re-evaluated the ECG and noticed ST segment straightening, prompting further evaluation. Follow-up ECG revealed clear ST-segment elevation in leads V2–V6 (Figure 1C). Emergency coronary angiography confirmed severe mid-left anterior descending artery stenosis (Figure 1D), which was successfully treated with primary percutaneous coronary intervention (Figure 1E). He was discharged on day 10.

(A) electrocardiogram of the patient when he experienced a stroke 18 months before admission, showing normal ST segment and normal ST-T angle. (B) Electrocardiogram during the first presentation displaying a straightened ST-segment and sharp ST-T angle without any elevation or depression beyond 0.5 mm. (C) Electrocardiogram during the second presentation exhibiting ST elevation in leads V2−V6. (D) Coronary angiogram showing severe stenosis in the mid-left anterior descending artery. (E) Final coronary angiogram after primary percutaneous coronary intervention for the mid-left anterior descending artery.

ECG is among the most useful diagnostic modalities for acute coronary syndrome (ACS). ECG features, such as ST segment elevation or depression, suggest that ACS presentation varies.1 In this case, although typical features suggestive of ACS were absent, ST segment straightening was observed, defined in 1984 as an ST segment that is abnormally straight but not depressed by > 0.5 mm; however, it has not been described in recent textbooks.2 ST segment straightening presents as an isoelectric horizontal segment with sharp ST-T angles. Although its sensitivity is relatively low (29.5%), its specificity for ACS is high (84.9%).3 In this case, ST segment straightening, which is subtle ECG finding, was not initially recognized by the physicians as an indicator of ACS. Recognizing these subtle ECG features is crucial for timely diagnosis and intervention to prevent myocardial damage.

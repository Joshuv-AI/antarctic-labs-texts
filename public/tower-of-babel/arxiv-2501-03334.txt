Anahit H. Shamamian, Hayk L. Gevorgyan, Lekdar A. Gevorgian

Abstract
A formula for the spectral distribution of intensity of radiation from a system of gap-separated undulators is derived. Due to the constructive addition of radiation fields formed in undulators, the line shape of the spontaneous radiation at zero angle is narrowed. According to Madey’s theory, the gain is proportional to the derivative of the line shape of the spontaneous radiation, hence, it is enhanced.

This study investigates a system comprising two identical helical undulators separated by a gap. A formula for the frequency-angular distribution of spontaneous radiation is derived. At a zero angle, the spontaneous radiation line shape is found to be narrower compared to the line shape of the spontaneous radiation spectrum generated in a single undulator without a gap. According to Madey’s theory

Radiation field in a system of two undulators

Let’s consider the motion of a relativistic electron in a system consisting of two identical helical undulators, each with a length
L

sL

Within the undulators, the particle follows a helical trajectory with a longitudinal velocity

\text{for }\,0<t<T,\,T(s+1)<t<T(s+2),\\

3
Frequency-angular distribution of radiated photons

For the frequency-angular distribution of the number of radiated photons in a system of two undulators separated by a gap, we have

\frac{d^{2}N_{\text{ph}}}{d\omega d\Omega}=\frac{\alpha\omega}{4\pi^{2}}\absolutevalue{\vb*{I}}^{2},\quad{\bf\it I}={\bf\it I}_{1}+{\bf\it I}_{2},

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\pi}\alpha T^{3}\cos^{2}{\left((s+1)z_{p}\right)}\cdot

The expression has been derived in the paraxial approximation, i.e., under the assumption that the electron oscillates in the near-axis region of an infinite helical undulator, where the magnetic field is homogeneous and transverse. Field inhomogeneities modify the circular trajectory in the transverse plane of motion, leading to a more complex path.

5
Gain coefficient of stimulated radiation generated in the undulator with a gap

Spontaneous radiation of relativistic particles in most cases is characterized by high directionality and monochromaticity. These properties contribute to the processes of stimulated radiation or absorption, which take place when a beam of photons, from an external source or formed during spontaneous radiation, propagates within a relativistic electron stream.
Interest in the process of stimulated undulator radiation is driven by its ability to be generated at zero angle, its tunability across a wide range of frequencies, and the potential for achieving sufficiently high intensity.
In the generation regime, the electrons of a relativistic bunch are subject to both stimulated radiation and absorption in the field of spontaneously radiated photons accumulated in the undulator. The number of stimulatedly emitted and absorbed photons with wavelength

\lambda
is determined by the expression

\displaystyle N_{st}(x,u)

\displaystyle=2\pi\lambda^{2}\rho_{e}N_{sp}^{2}(x,u),

(22a)

\displaystyle N_{sp}(x,u)

\displaystyle=\frac{d^{2}N_{sp}}{dxd\left(u^{2}\right)}=\alpha\pi n^{2}q^{2}x\left(\left(2u^{2}x-1\right)+1\right)F(z),

(22b)

\displaystyle F(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin^{2}{z}}{z^{2}},\quad z=\pi nx\left(u^{2}-\varphi(x)\right),

(22c)

\displaystyle\varphi(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{x}-Q-\frac{r^{2}}{x^{2}},

(22d)

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma_{\text{th}}}{\gamma},\quad\gamma_{\text{th}}=\frac{lQ}{\lambda_{\text{p}}},\quad Q=\sqrt{1+q^{2}},

(22e)

where
\rho_{e}
is the density of the electron bunch,
N_{\text{sp}}(x,u)
is the frequency-angular distribution of spontaneously radiated photons in the dispersive medium.
The detailed balance between the processes of stimulated radiation and absorption is disrupted due to quantum recoil during the radiation and absorption of a photon. Quantum recoil leads to a frequency shift of the radiation
x_{-}
from the frequency of absorption
x_{+}
, since the electron’s energy changes by an amount
\delta E=\mp\hbar\omega
(a change in the Lorentz factor
\delta\gamma=\mp\hbar\omega/\left(mc^{2}\right)
) during radiation or absorption. Then the ratio
\delta\gamma/\gamma=\delta E/E=\mp\lambda_{c}/(\lambda\,\gamma)
, where
\lambda_{c}=\hbar/(mc)
is the Compton wavelength of the electron, with a value of
2.426\times 10^{-10}
\text{\,}\mathrm{cm}
.
Thus, the classical frequency
x
of radiation or absorption is shifted by an amount
\mp x\,\delta\gamma/\gamma
. The shift in these frequencies is given by

x_{\mp}=\frac{\omega_{\mp}}{2\Omega\gamma^{2}}=x\left(1+\frac{2\Omega}{\gamma}\right)=x\left(1-\frac{2\lambda_{c}}{\lambda\gamma}\right),

(23)

where

\delta x=x_{-}-x_{+}=-\frac{4x\lambda_{c}}{\lambda\gamma}.

(24)

Let’s consider the case where
\gamma\gg\gamma_{\text{th}}
. In this situation, the medium polarization can be neglected and the function

\varphi(x)=\frac{1}{x}-Q,

(25)

so

z=\pi nx\left(u^{2}-\left(\frac{1}{x}-Q\right)\right)

(26)

For a fixed angle, the change in

\delta z=\pi nQ\delta x=-\frac{4\pi Qx\lambda_{c}}{\lambda\gamma}.

(27)

The number of photons of spontaneous radiation (

\delta N_{\text{st}}=\delta N_{\text{st}}(x,u)=4\pi\lambda^{2}\rho_{e}N_{\text{sp}}(x,u)\,\delta N_{\text{sp}}(x,u).

(28)

For large values of the number of periods

Therefore, when differentiating with respect to two variables, it is valid to reduce the problem to differentiation with respect to one variable, with the result being doubled. \displaystyle\delta N_{\text{sp}}(x,u)

\displaystyle=2\delta N_{\text{sp}}(x,0)=2\pi\alpha n^{2}q^{2}x\frac{dF(z)}{dz}=

(38)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}F_{s}(z_{p})}{d^{2}z_{p}}

\displaystyle\approx-2\frac{\sin^{2}{z_{p}}}{z_{p}^{2}}\cos{\left(2 (s+1) z_p\right)}\cdot(s+1)^{2}.

The maxima and minima of the function

are determined from the condition

z_{\text{min}}=\frac{\pi/2+2\pi k}{2(s+1)},\quad z_{\text{max}}=\frac{-\pi/2+2\pi k}{2(s+1)},

(39)

where
k
is an integer. For the ratio of the gain coefficients when
k=0
, we obtain

g_{s}:g_{s=0}=\frac{dF_{s}(z)}{dz}:\frac{dF_{s=0}(z)}{dz}\approx s+1.

(40)

Thus, dividing a single undulator of length
2L
into two identical single undulators, each of length
L

sL
between them, results in the increase in the gain coefficient by a factor of
s+1
.
In a system with two undulators separated by a gap, the fields of radiation from the first and second undulators interact. This interaction results in interference, specifically, a modification of the radiation line shape with a narrowing of the spectral width by a factor of
s+1
compared to a single undulator. The radiation gain coefficient depends on the derivative of the line shape, which also leads to the increase in the gain coefficient by a factor of
s+1
.

, which determines its optimal value

(41)

Thus,

6
Conclusion

The amplification of radiation in an optical klystron arises from the interference of radiation fields produced by individual undulators. The maximum achievable amplification is given by
1+s
, where
0<s<1
, and
s
represents the optimal gap between the undulators, expressed in units of the undulator length. According to Madey’s theory, this enhanced gain is attributed to the narrowing of the line shape the spontaneous radiation.

Asymptotic structure. II. Path-width and additive quasi-isometry
Tung Nguyen, Alex Scott, Paul Seymour

Abstract
We show that if a graph G G admits a quasi-isometry ϕ \phi to a graph H H of bounded path-width, then we can assign a non-negative integer length to each edge of H H , such that the same function ϕ \phi is a quasi-isometry to this weighted version of H H , with error only an additive constant.

Introduction

We need to begin with some definitions.
Graphs in this paper may be infinite, and have no loops or parallel edges.
If
X
is a vertex of a graph
G
, or
a subset of the vertex set of
G
, or a subgraph of
G

between

with one end in
X
and the other in

\operatorname{dist}_{G}(X,Y)=\infty

Let
G,H
be graphs, and let
\phi:V(G)\to V(H)
be a map.
Let

; we say that
\phi
is an
(L,C)
-quasi-isometry
, and

is
(L,C)
-quasi-isometric to

if:

•

for all

\operatorname{dist}_{G}(u,v)
is finite then
\operatorname{dist}_{H}(\phi(u),\phi(v))\leq L\operatorname{dist}_{G}(u,v)+C
;

•

for all
u,v
in
V(G)
, if
\operatorname{dist}_{H}(\phi(u),\phi(v))
is finite then
\operatorname{dist}_{G}(u,v)\leq L\operatorname{dist}_{H}(\phi(u),\phi(v))+C
;
and

•

for every

such that

What if we want

There is a remarkable theorem of Chepoi, Dragan, Newman, Rabinovich, and Vaxès
[
2
]

[
7
]

For all

-quasi-isometry from a graph

Is this special to trees, or can it be made much more general?
For instance, Agelos Georgakopoulos asked (in private communication) whether the same statement was true if we (twice) replace “tree” by “planar graph”.
Let

“For all

C^{\prime}

For this to be true,

must have some closure properties: for instance, if

and

is obtained from

by subdividing every edge once, there is a

-quasi-isometry from

, but if we want there to be

to contain a graph much like

Similarly, if

and

by contracting the edges in some matching of

,
there is a
(3,0)
-quasi-isometry from

to

, and so we need

to be more-or-less closed under

1.2

Conjecture:
Let
\mathcal{C}
be a class of connected graphs, closed under contracting edges and subdividing edges.
For all

there exists
C^{\prime}
such that if there is an

-quasi-isometry from

.

For instance, if

are respectively the infinite square lattice and the infinite triangular lattice, there is a quasi-isometry between them,
but no
(1,C)
-quasi-isometry (for any constant

); but there is a
(1,2)
-quasi-isometry from
G
to a graph obtained by subdividing edges
of
H
, and a
(1,100)
-quasi-isometry from

to a graph obtained by subdividing and contracting edges
of

(we omit the proofs of all these statements).

We are far from proving the conjecture

in general, but we will prove a special case, which we will explain next.
A

of a graph

is (possibly infinite or doubly-infinite) sequence

, where

is a subset of

•

is the union of the sets

;

•

for every edge

; and

•

for all

t_{1}\leq t_{1}\leq t_{3}
, then

.

The

of a path-decomposition

is the maximum of the numbers

We will prove:

1.3

For all

there exists
C^{\prime}
such that if there is an
(L,C)
-quasi-isometry from a graph

-quasi-isometry from

In fact

Let

denote the set of nonnegative integers.
Let

be a graph and let

be some function; we call

to be the minimum of

But something much stronger is true: we don’t need to change the quasi-isometry either.

1.4

For all

such that if
\phi

(L,C)
-quasi-isometry from a graph

-quasi-isometry from

In view of the conjecture

, one might ask whether the path-width restriction is necessary.
It cannot just be omitted, because Davies, Hatzel and Hickingbotham
[

very recently showed the following:

1.5

For every integer

, and and every real

, there exist graphs

such that

(1+\varepsilon,1)
-quasi-isometric to

of

(1,C)
-quasi-isometric to

.

(Curiously, this does not disprove the conjecture

.)
It still might be true that we can replace “with path-width at most
k
” in

by something less restrictive,
for instance, by “with tree-width at most
k
”, or “that is planar”, or indeed “that is in

”

is any minor-closed class of graphs that does not contain all finite graphs.

Is

true at least when

is the class of connected graphs with tree-width at most

.) Yes when

, and indeed one can show that

also holds in this case (see the proof of

).
What about tree-width two? A special case is when

is the class of all connected outerplanar graphs, and we can prove

in that case. (A hint for the proof: every connected outerplanar graph is quasi-isometric to a graph in which every non-trivial block is a cycle.) But for
tree-width two in general, the result is open, as is the following weaker statement:

1.6

Conjecture:
For all
L,C
there exist
C^{\prime},k
such that if there is an
(L,C)
-quasi-isometry from a graph
G
to a graph of tree-width at most two,
then there is a
(1,C^{\prime})
-quasi-isometry from

to a graph of tree-width at most

.

So far, our statements are true for infinite graphs as well as for finite graphs, but we want to make an adjustment,
because path-width is not the “right” concept for infinite graphs. A graph has tree-width at most
k
if and only of all its finite
subgraphs have tree-width at most
k
(Thomas
[
8
]
), but the same is not true for path-width. For instance, the graph consisting of the disjoint union of infinitely many one-way infinite paths has infinite path-width, and so does the disjoint union of infinitely many
copies of the infinite “star” (one vertex with countably many neighbours); and so does any graph with uncountably many vertices and
no edges. There is a more appropriate concept. Let us say a
line
is a set that is linearly ordered by some relation
<
; and
a
line-decomposition
is a family
(B_{t}:t\in T)
, where
T
is a line, satisfying the same three conditions as in the definition
of path-decomposition. We define the
width
of a line-decomposition to be the maximum of
|B_{t}|-1
over all
t\in T
if this exists,
and otherwise the width is infinite. The
line-width
of
G
is the minimum integer
k
such that
G
admits a line-decomposition
of width at most
k
, if this exists, and otherwise the line-width is infinite. For finite graphs, path-width and line-width are the same, but
for infinite graphs, they may be different (for instance, in the three examples above), and line-width behaves better. We proved in
[
3
]

that a graph has line-width at most
k
if and only if all its finite subgraphs have path-width at most
k
.

All the theorems about path-width mentioned so far are also true for line-width, and expressing them this way makes them stronger and more general.
In particular, we will prove:

1.7

For all
L,C,k
there exists
C^{\prime}
such that if
\phi
is an
(L,C)
-quasi-isometry from a graph

with

, then there is a function

such that the same function

is a

-quasi-isometry from

to the weighted graph
(H,w)
.

Here is an application. A. Georgakopoulos in private communication showed that for all

there exists

such that

-quasi-isometric to a cycle, then

-quasi-isometry from

.
If we have such a geodesic, then the vertices

and we find a path

in

, and so no edge of

, and therefore

By (1),

Since

This proves

The second step is:

of

of

,
in order in

\operatorname{dist}_{Q}(r_{i},r_{j})\leq 2C(j-i)

•

\operatorname{dist}_{H}(\phi(p_{j}),r_{j})\leq 2C

We may assume that

. There are four cases, depending whether

Suppose first that

. Inductively,

, with

be a geodesic between

\phi(p_{i_{k}}),\phi(p_{i_{k+1}})
. This exists and

, since

\operatorname{dist}_{H}(\phi(p_{i_{k}}),\phi(p_{1+i_{k}}))\leq 2C
(because

is a
(C-1,C)
-quasi-isometry).
Thus

are all paths in

of length at most

. Certainly

meets

for each

, since they share an
end-vertex and perhaps more. We claim that

are vertex-disjoint if

; because suppose

is a vertex in both paths.
The sum of the distances between

and
\phi(p_{i_{h-1}}),\phi(p_{i_{h}}),\phi(p_{i_{k-1}}),\phi(p_{i_{k}})
is at most

, and the last two sum to

. Consequently, either there is a path
between
\phi(p_{i_{h-1}}),\phi(p_{i_{k-1}})
of length at most

, or one between
\phi(p_{i_{h}}),\phi(p_{i_{k}})
of length at most
2C
,
and this contradicts the definition of
i_{h}
or of
i_{h+1}
. Thus, in the sequence
T_{0},T_{1},\ldots
, non-consecutive terms are
vertex-disjoint. Let
Q
be the path defined as follows: start with the subpath of
T_{0}
from
\phi(p_{0})
to the first vertex of
T_{0}

in
T_{1}
; then follow
T_{1}
to the first vertex of
T_{1}
in
T_{2}
; and so on, for each integer
i\geq 0
if

is infinite,
or until

is the maximum element of
I
. In the second case, let
i_{k}
be this maximum element: extend

along

to

\phi(p_{i_{k}})
.

Let

J=\{i_{0},i_{1},\ldots\}
. Let
r_{0}=\phi(p_{0})
, and if

has a maximum element

let

. For each

not the minimum or maximum element of

, choose

.
It follows that the vertices
r_{j}(j\in J)
are distinct and in order in
Q
,
and

is the union of the subpaths

for
i,j\in J
. We claim:

(1)
The following hold:

•

for all
i,j\in J
with
i\leq j
, if none of
i+1,\ldots,j-1
belong to
J
, then
j-i\leq 2C^{2}-C
;

•

\operatorname{dist}_{H}(r_{i},r_{j})\geq(j-i)/(4C^{2}-1)
for all
i,j\in J
with
j>i
;

•

\operatorname{dist}_{Q}(r_{i},r_{j})\leq 2C(j-i)
for all
i,j\in J
with
j>i
;

•

for each
j\in J

\operatorname{dist}_{H}(\phi(p_{j}),r_{j})\leq 2C

•

for each

there exists

\operatorname{dist}_{H}(v,\phi(p_{j}))\leq C

with
i\leq j
, such that none of

belong to

. We may assume that

and it follows that

and

\phi(p_{i_{k}}),\phi(p_{i_{k+1}})
.
Consequently

, and so

\operatorname{dist}_{G}(p_{i_{k}},p_{i_{k+1}})\leq(C-1)(2C)+C=2C^{2}-C.

For the second bullet, let

and

where

. Then

\operatorname{dist}_{H}(r_{i},r_{j})\geq\operatorname{dist}_{H}(\phi(p_{i_{k}}),\phi(p_{i_{\ell}}))-4C.

Since

\operatorname{dist}_{H}(r_{i},r_{j})\geq(j-i-C)/(C-1)-4C=(j-i)/(C-1)-(C/(C-1)+4C).

Since also

and so

For the third bullet, again let

For the fourth bullet, let

.
Finally, for the fifth bullet, let

at most

from one of these ends.

So in the case when

has no minimum, and similarly that it has no maximum; and so

Choose integers

,

3
Extending the local weighting to the whole of

Now we turn to the second part of the proof of

.
We have found the geodesic

of

of

and a weight function

that makes

-geodesic and makes

Let us say a function

\phi

is a
(1,\kappa(C))
-quasi-isometry from

to

A class

If

To see this, choose

Similarly if

, and so

This proves (1).

(2)

equals the union of the sets

.

Let

;

. Suppose that

.
Let

, and let

be the set of southern border vertices of

, there exists

,
and there exists

.
Choose

, and choose

. Since

. This proves (2).

It follows from (1) and (2) that the sequence

satisfies the first two bullets of the theorem. For the third,

. From (2) there exists

with

. For each integer

. This proves the third bullet, and so proves the first assertion of the theorem.

For the remainder, we observe first that since the sequence

is infinite, the third bullet of the theorem implies that

is up-final.
Now let

be the union of all the sets

. Then

is countable, since

is finite; and

.
Let

, and choose

Since at most two values of

,
it follows that

for

, and therefore

.

We observe:

, there exists

with

The next result uses the up-pervasive and down-pervasive sets just constructed to find an appropriate geodesic

.

.
By

.
It follows that

Similarly there is a

Since

Take a well-order

For every two vertices

Let

•

(1)

Let

.

6.5

Let
(B_{t}:t\in T)
be a non-null line-decomposition of a graph
H
, with finite width, and let

.
Let
\phi
be a surjective
(C-1,C)
-quasi-isometry from a graph
G
to

.
Then there is an integer interval
I
, and
v_{i}\in V(G)
for each
i\in I
, and an integer
d_{i}>0

for all
i\in I
with

,
with the following properties, where

denotes the set of vertices

of

such that
\operatorname{dist}_{G}(v,\{v_{i}:i\in I\})\leq 3C^{2}
,
and

•

•

for all distinct

\operatorname{dist}_{G}(v_{h},v_{j})\geq 2C^{2}
; and

•

,
|\operatorname{dist}_{G}(v_{h},v_{j})-\sum_{h\leq i<j}d_{i}|\leq 2C^{2}

(B_{t}:t\in T)
is not upper-open, choose

such that
\{\phi(y_{0})\}
is upfinal, and if

(B_{t}:t\in T)

Suppose first that

. Then we may set

and

and the theorem is satisfied.
Next, if
y_{0},z_{0}
both exist and
\operatorname{dist}_{G}(y_{0},z_{0})\geq 2C^{2}
, set

,
v_{1}=y_{0}
,
v_{2}=z_{0}
and
d_{1}=\operatorname{dist}_{G}(y_{0},z_{0})

then again the theorem is satisfied. So we may assume that not both
y_{0},z_{0}
exist, and from the symmetry we may assume that
y_{0}
does not exist, and so
(B_{t}:t\in T)
is upper-open.

By
6.2
, there is a countable subset of
T
that is upfinal, and similarly one that is downfinal, either infinite or the
singleton
\{z_{0}\}
.
Let
S_{1}

be their union. Thus

is a countable subset of
T
, and satisfies:

•

for each
t\in T
, there exist

with
s\leq t\leq s^{\prime}
; and

•

for all
t,t^{\prime}\in T

, there are only finitely many
s\in S_{1}
with
t<s<t^{\prime}
.

By
6.4
, for each
s\in S_{1}
,
there exists
v_{s}\in V(G)
, with
\phi(v_{s})\in B_{s}
, such that for every finite subset
X\subseteq S_{1}
, there is a geodesic
P
in
G
such that

\operatorname{dist}_{G}(v_{s},P)\leq C^{2}-1
for each
s\in X
.
Choose
S_{2}\subseteq S_{1}
, with
z_{0}\in S_{2}
if
z_{0}
exists, maximal such that
\operatorname{dist}_{G}(v_{s},v_{s^{\prime}})\geq 2C^{2}
for all distinct
s,s^{\prime}\in S_{2}
(this is possible by Zorn’s lemma).

The next claim is aimed towards the first bullet of the theorem.

(1)
For each
t\in T
, either there exists
s\in S_{2}
such that
\operatorname{dist}_{G}(v_{s},\phi^{-1}(B_{t}))\leq 3C^{2}-1
,
or there exist
s,s^{\prime}\in S_{2}
with
s<t<s^{\prime}
.

Suppose that there is no
s\in S_{2}
with
s\geq t
(the proof is similar if there is no
s
with
s\leq t
).
From the definition of
S_{1}
, there exists

s^{\prime}\in S_{1}
such that

. Thus
s^{\prime}\notin S_{2}
,
and so there exists
s\in S_{2}
such that
\operatorname{dist}_{G}(v_{s},v_{s^{\prime}})<2C^{2}
(this exists from the maximality of

).
By our assumption,
s<t
, and we may assume that

, that is,
\phi(v_{s})\notin B_{t}
.
Let
P

be a geodesic of

between

. Thus

has length at most

. If
\operatorname{dist}_{G}(P,\phi^{-1}(B_{t}))\leq C^{2}
,
then
\operatorname{dist}_{G}(v_{s},\phi^{-1}(B_{t}))\leq 3C^{2}-1
, as required, so we assume that
\operatorname{dist}_{G}(P,\phi^{-1}(B_{t}))>C^{2}
. In particular,

\phi(v_{s^{\prime}})\notin B_{t}
. Since
\phi(v_{s})\in B_{s}
and
\phi(v_{s^{\prime}})\in B_{s^{\prime}}
and

,

Let

be finite; then

in

is a finite subset of

with union

, and for each

,

be a corresponding gap matrix for

is adjacent to a gap matrix for

for all

, such that for every finite

.

Let

(1)

Large deviations in mean-field quantum spin systems
Matthias Keller, Christiaan J. F. van de Ven

Abstract
Continuous fields (or bundles) of C ∗ C^{*} -algebras form an important ingredient for describing emergent phenomena, such as phase transitions and spontaneous symmetry breaking. In this work, we consider the continuous C ∗ C^{*} -bundle generated by increasing symmetric tensor powers of the complex ℓ × ℓ \ell\times\ell matrices M ℓ ​ ( ℂ ) M_{\ell}(\mathbb{C}) , which can be interpreted as abstract description of mean-field theories defining the macroscopic limit of infinite quantum systems. Within this framework we discuss the principle of large deviations for the local Gibbs state in the high temperature regime and characterize the limit of the ensuing logarithmic generating function.

Introduction

1.1
Continuous
C^{*}
-bundles and
C^{*}
-algebraic deformation quantization

In this paper two important and seemingly unrelated topics in mathematical physics are addressed and brought forward by relating them to each other:

1.

A continuous bundle of
C^{*}
-algebras, with additional structure of deformation quantization;

2.

The theory of large deviations.

The relationship is that the first topic may be used to describe a class of “physical” Hamiltonians, for which one is allowed to study large deviations of suitable observables by using the continuity properties of the
C^{*}
-bundle, yielding a classical (commutative) theory in the pertinent limit. More precisely, for the purpose of this work, we adapt this setting to mean-field quantum spin Hamiltonians.

Below is the technical definition of continuous bundle of
C^{*}
-algebras,
cf.

[
1
, §IV.1.6]
. We denote by
\overline{\mathbb{N}}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
, and by
C(\overline{\mathbb{N}})
the space of
\mathbb{C}
-valued sequences
(\alpha_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\alpha_{\infty}:=\lim\limits_{N\to\infty}\alpha_{N}\in\mathbb{C}
exists.
A
continuous bundle (or field) of
C^{*}
-algebras
over
\overline{\mathbb{N}}
is a triple
\mathcal{A}
,
(\mathcal{A}_{N})_{N\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}
,
(\chi_{N})_{N\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}
made by
C^{*}
-algebras
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}_{N}
,
N\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}
, and surjective homomorphisms
\chi_{N}\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}_{N}
such that:

(i)

The norm of
\mathcal{A}
is given by
\|a\|_{\mathcal{A}}=\sup\limits_{N\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}\|\chi_{N}(a)\|_{\mathcal{A}_{N}}
;

(ii)

For all
\alpha=(\alpha_{N})_{N\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}\in C(\overline{\mathbb{N}})
and
a\in\mathcal{A}
there exists
\alpha a\in\mathcal{A}
with the property that
\chi_{N}(\alpha a)=\alpha_{N}\chi_{N}(a)
;

(iii)

For all
a\in\mathcal{A}
,
(\|\chi_{N}(a)\|_{\mathcal{A}_{N}})_{N\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}\in C(\overline{\mathbb{N}})
.

A
continuous section
of
\mathcal{A}
is an element
a\in\prod_{N\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}\mathcal{A}_{N}
such that there exists
a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}
fulfilling
a_{N}=\chi_{N}(a^{\prime})
for all
N\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}
. In this way
\mathcal{A}
can be identified with its continuous sections. Indeed, if we define the map

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\to\prod_{N\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}\mathcal{A}_{N};\ \ \ \ \ a\mapsto(\chi_{N}(a))_{N\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}},

it follows by surjectivity of
\chi_{N}
and (i) that this map is a bijection. For this reason, we will implicitly identify
\chi_{N}
,
N\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}
, with the projection
\prod_{N\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}\mathcal{A}_{N}\to\mathcal{A}_{N}
.

The above construction may lead to a
strict deformation quantization
, defined by the following additional data, see e.g.
[
11
,
5
]
:

1.

A continuous bundle of
C^{*}
-algebras, as described above for which the fiber at infinity assumes the form
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}=C(X)
, where
X
is a Poisson manifold;

2.

A family of linear maps, called
quantization maps
,
Q_{N}\colon\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\infty}\to\mathcal{A}_{N}
,
N\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}
, with
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\infty}:=C^{\infty}(X)
, such that

(a)

Q_{\infty}=\operatorname{Id}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\infty}}
and
Q_{N}(a_{\infty})^{*}=Q_{N}(a_{\infty}^{*})
for all
a_{\infty}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\infty}
and all
N\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}
.
Moreover, the assignment

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbb{N}}\ni N\mapsto Q_{N}(a_{\infty})\in\mathcal{A}_{N}\,,

(1)

defines a continuous section of the bundle.

(b)

For all
a_{\infty},a_{\infty}^{\prime}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\infty}
one has the
Dirac-Groenewold-Rieffel condition

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\|Q_{N}(\{a_{\infty},a_{\infty}^{\prime}\})-iN[Q_{N}(a_{\infty}),Q_{N}(a_{\infty}^{\prime})]\|_{\mathcal{A}_{N}}=0\,.

(2)

(c)

For all
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
Q_{N}(\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\infty})
is a dense
*
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{A}_{N}
.

The algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
at
N=\infty
represents the classical (or macroscopic) observables of the physical system, whilst the fibers
\mathcal{A}_{N}
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
, recollect the quantum observables of the increasing quantum system. The continuous sections defined by the quantization maps,
cf.
(
2a
) of the definition above, form the main ingredients of our analysis. In this paper the continuous
C^{*}
-bundle of our interest is equipped with the additional structure of a strict deformation quantization,
cf.

\lx@sectionsign\ref{MFQss and all that}
.

We would like to point out to the reader that this abstract framework is perfectly suited to characterize the notion of the
classical limit
of quantum systems, including a rigorous derivation of spontaneous symmetry breaking and phase transitions
[
17
,
18
,
12
,
26
,
27
]
.

1.2
Principle of large deviations

Large deviations play an important role in the asymptotic evaluation of certain integrals often appearing in probability theory and classical statistical mechanics, viz.

\displaystyle\int d\mu_{N}(x)e^{\nu_{N}f(x)},\ \ (\nu_{N}\to\infty,\ \text{as}\ N\to\infty)

(3)

for which the measures
\mu_{N}
satisfy a law of large numbers,
\nu_{N}
are real positive numbers and
f
is some function. Traditionally, the Laplace method, and more generally, the method of the steepest descent, have turned out to be important approximation methods for such integrals, in particular in the theory of soliton equations, integrable models and random matrices. It is however a challenging and difficult task to make these techniques mathematically precise. Instead, the principle of large deviations, due to Varadhan
[
28
,
29
]
, provides an efficient way of evaluating integrals of the above type. The abstract setting is that the measures
\mu_{N}
are probabilities measures on the Borel subsets of a Polish space
\Omega
, i.e., a separable completely metrizable topological space.
Then, in certain situations, one may expect that

d\mu_{N}(x)\ \sim\ dxe^{-\nu_{N}I(x)},

in a suitable sense, where
I
is the so-called
rate function
for the sequence of measures
(\mu_{N})_{N}
. In such cases, the integral (
3
) may be computed as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\nu_{N}}\log{\int d\mu_{N}(x)e^{\nu_{N}f(x)}}\ \to\ \sup{\{f(x)-I(x)\ |\ x\in\Omega\}},\ \ (N\to\infty).

To make all this precise the following well-known definition is introduced.

Definition 1

(LDP)
.

A sequence of probability measures
(\mu_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
on a Polish space

\Omega
equipped with the Borel
\Sigma
-algebra satisfies a
large deviation principle
(LDP) with a
rate function

I:\Omega\to[0,\infty]
and speed (or rate)
\nu_{N}\geq 0
, if

(i)

I
has closed sub-level sets
\{x\in\Omega\ |\ I(x)\leq k\}
for all
k\in[0,\infty)
.

(ii)

For all compact
C\subset\Omega
,

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{\nu_{N}}\log{\mu_{N}(C)}\leq-\inf_{x\in C}I(x).

(iii)

For all open
O\subset\Omega
,

\displaystyle\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{\nu_{N}}\log{\mu_{N}(O)}\geq-\inf_{x\in O}I(x).

Furthermore,
I
is said to be a
good
rate function if all the level sets are compact subsets of
\Omega
.

\blacksquare

The theory of large deviations can be applied in several different fields, in particular in semi-classical analysis of Schrödinger operators in the regime
\hbar\to 0
, where
\hbar
appears in front of the Laplacian. In this way, up to a suitable scale separation,
\nu_{N}
should be interpreted as
1/\hbar
. This has proved to be a rigorous approach for the study of quantum tunneling and eigenfuntion localization
[
21
,
22
]
. The rate function in such cases is a quantitative measure of exponential decay away from the minima of the pertinent potential, as function of
\hbar
.

In order to study the large deviations for
\mu_{N}
as
N\to\infty
, one typically considers the corresponding logarithmic moment generating
function, defined as

\displaystyle F(t):=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{\nu_{N}}\log{\int_{\Omega}d\mu_{N}(x)e^{Ntx}}.

The Gärtner–Ellis Theorem (see e.g.
[
6
]
), shows that the existence of

F(t)
implies large deviation upper bounds with good rate function
I(x)
, that is, the Legendre transform of
F(t)
. One obtains lower bounds if, in addition, the moment generating function is differentiable. If the moment generating function is not differentiable, one has a weaker result: in Definition
1
(iii), the
infimum over
O
is replaced by the infimum over
O\cap E
, where
E
is the set of the so-called “exposed points”
[
4
]
.

1.3
Large deviations in statistical mechanics

Beside its great value in classical statistical mechanics, the theory of large deviations is also suitable for probability measures occurring in (equilibrium) quantum statistical mechanics. In this setting, one typically seeks for a large deviation principle concerning the celebrated Kubo–Martin–Schwinger equilibrium states (KMS states), which are, loosely speaking, limit points of the local Gibbs state induced by local interacting Hamiltonians. For each such KMS state
\omega
a particular study of interest is to analyze the asymptotic behaviour of the distribution of quantum averages with respect to
\omega
, in the regime of increasing number of lattice sites or increasing volume
[
13
,
14
,
15
]
.

In this work we focus on mean-field quantum spin systems, arising as abstract elements of a continuous bundle of
C^{*}
-algebras
[
25
,
27
]
. Due to the long range interacting nature of such models, no KMS condition can be formulated for the limiting system. Nonetheless, on account of the renown Quantum De Finetti Theorem, one may rely on a suitable decomposition of the limit points of local Gibbs states to investigate large deviations. The minimizers of the pertinent mean-field free energy, whose existence is always guaranteed
[
3
,
16
,
26
]
, are uniquely identified with the limit points of the local Gibbs state
[
7
]
. Each minimizer represents a pure thermodynamic phase of the given infinite quantum system, whose thermodynamics is described by the fixed phase space
S(M_{\ell}(\mathbb{C}))
, interpreted as the set of density matrices of size
\ell
. The ensuing mean-field limit is therefore considered as “macroscopic”: the number of particles is appropriately sent to infinity yielding a commutative limiting theory as a limit of infinite statistical mechanics.

This paper is structured as follows. The principle of large deviations is first investigated in the
C^{*}
-algebraic framework of deformation quantization and continuous
C^{*}
-bundles, namely the one describing quantum spin systems with mean-field interactions on a lattice of increasing size, also dubbed
symmetric
C^{*}
-bundle
. More precisely, in Theorem
8
we prove large deviation upper bounds, and discuss the lower bounds, for the logarithmic moment generating function, defined in what follows. For
(h_{N})_{N}
, an arbitrary self-adjoint continuous cross-sections of the symmetric
C^{*}
-bundle of matrix algebras (the only case we care about), we first consider the
local Gibbs state

\omega_{N}(\cdot):=\frac{Tr[e^{Nh_{N}}(\cdot)]}{Tr[e^{Nh_{N}}]},

see
[
8
]
for further context and details.
For
(a_{N})_{N}
another arbitrary self-adjoint continuous cross-sections of this symmetric
C^{*}
-bundle, we then define
F_{N}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle F_{N}(t):=\frac{1}{N}\log{\frac{Tr[e^{Nh_{N}}e^{Nta_{N}}]}{Tr[e^{Nh_{N}}]}}=\frac{1}{N}\log{\omega_{N}(e^{Nta_{N}})}.

(4)

Note that
(h_{N})_{N}
and
(a_{N})_{N}
are generalizations of the empirical averages, the latter often used in this context
[
13
,
14
,
15
]
. Here,
N
corresponds to the number of lattice sites of the underlying quantum system; and therefore plays the role of the diverging sequence
(\nu_{N})_{N}
in the above discussion. The section
(h_{N})_{N}
defines the normalized version of a mean-field quantum spin Hamiltonian,
cf.
(
7
),(
18
) below.

More precisely, if
(a_{N})_{N}
is such a cross-section, it is known that the spectrum
\sigma(a_{N})
of
a_{N}
is contained in
\text{ran}(a_{\infty})
, where
a_{\infty}
denotes the principal symbol of the section
(a_{N})_{N}
, that is, a real-valued continuous function seen as limit of the sequence
(a_{N})_{N}

[
26
]
. One may then consider the spectral projection
1_{\Delta}(a_{N})
defined for Borel sets
\Delta\subset\text{ran}(a_{\infty})
, and define the
distribution of
a
with respect to the local Gibbs state
\omega_{N}

\displaystyle\mu_{N}(\Delta):=\omega_{N}(1_{\Delta}(a_{N})).

(5)

Consequently, we deduce large deviation upper bounds, and discuss the lower bounds, for the sequence of probability measures
(\mu_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
. We hereto demonstrate that the generating function
F_{N}
, which, by functional calculus also reads

\displaystyle F_{N}(t)=\frac{1}{N}\log{\int e^{tNx}d\mu_{N}(x)}

has a limit as
N\to\infty
.

Remark
.

Our result actually relates to the celebrated Born rule: if an observable
a
is measured in a state
\omega
, then the probability
\mathbb{P}_{\omega}(a\in\Delta)
that the
outcome lies in some measurable subset
\Delta\subset\sigma(a)\subset\mathbb{R}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\omega}(a\in\Delta)=\mu_{\omega}(\Delta).

\blacksquare

Remark
.

One should be aware of the fact that Golden-Thompson inequality, stating that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\log{Tr[e^{Nh_{N}+Nta_{N}}]}\leq\frac{1}{N}\log{Tr[e^{Nh_{N}}e^{Nta_{N}}]},\ \ (N\in\mathbb{N}),

(6)

remains in general a strict inequality in the limit, even in the case that
\lim_{N\to\infty}\|[h_{N},a_{N}]\|_{N}=0
, cf.
[
20
]
.

\blacksquare

Finally, we would like to point out to the reader that the possible existence of the limit of
F_{N}
defined by (
4
) for mean-field models
(h_{N})_{N}
and
(a_{N})_{N}
has been conjectured in
[
20
]
, no proof was however given in subsequent literature.

2
Mean-field quantum spin systems in algebraic quantum theory

2.1
Mean-field theories

Mean-field theories (MFTs) play an important role as approximate models of the more complex nearest neighbor interacting spin systems. Their relatively simple structure allows for a detailed analysis of the limit of increasing number of lattice sites especially in view of spontaneous symmetry breaking (SSB) and phase transitions
[
26
]
.

Homogeneous mean-field quantum spin systems fall into the class of MFTs. They are defined by a single-site Hilbert space
\mathbb{C}^{\ell}
and local Hamiltonians of the type

\displaystyle H_{\Lambda}=|\Lambda|h_{\infty}(t_{0}^{(\Lambda)},t_{1}^{(\Lambda)},\cdot\cdot\cdot,t_{\ell^{2}-1}^{(\Lambda)}),

(7)

where
h_{\infty}
is the
principal symbol
, i.e., a polynomial in
\ell^{2}
(non-commutative) variables, and
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
denotes a finite lattice on which
H_{\Lambda}
is defined and
|\Lambda|
denotes the number of lattice points (see e.g.
[
11
,
\lx@sectionsign
10.8]
). Here
t_{0}=1_{\ell}
is the identity matrix in the algebra of complex
\ell\times\ell
matrices
M_{\ell}(\mathbb{C})
, and the matrices
(t_{\mu})_{\mu=1}^{\ell^{2}-1}
in
M_{\ell}(\mathbb{C})
form a basis of the real vector space of traceless self-adjoint
\ell\times\ell
matrices; the latter may be identified with
i
times the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{su}(\ell)
of
SU(\ell)
, which identification is useful in defining the Poisson bracket below, cf. (
15
) below. The macroscopic average spin operators are now defined by

\displaystyle t_{\mu}^{(\Lambda)}=\frac{1}{|\Lambda|}\sum_{x\in\Lambda}t_{\mu}(x),\ \ (\mu=1,\cdots,\ell^{2}-1).

(8)

Here
t_{\mu}(x)
stands for
1_{\ell}\otimes\cdots\otimes t_{\mu}\otimes\cdots\otimes 1_{\ell}
, where
t_{\mu}
occupies slot
x
.

It follows directly from the definition that such models are characterized by the property that all spins interact with each other which implies that these models are permutation-invariant and that the geometric configuration including the dimension is irrelevant. In what follows we therefore consider homogeneous mean-field quantum spin chains, i.e.
d=1
. We will leave out the term “homogeneous” in the forthcoming discussion.

2.2
Strict deformation quantization of Poisson manifolds

In contrast to the
thermodynamic limit
, that is, a rigorous formalism in which the number of lattice sites
N
as well as the volume of the system at constant density, are sent to infinity, the limit we consider here is
macroscopic
.
This means that the limit
N\to\infty
is formalized by a classical (commutative) theory, introduced in what follows.

For any unital
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathfrak{A}
and
X\subset\mathbb{Z}
with
N=|X|
, we set

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{N}^{\pi}:=\begin{cases}{\mathfrak{A}}_{X}^{\pi}\equiv\pi_{N}(\mathfrak{A}^{N})\quad N\in\mathbb{N},\\
[\mathfrak{A}]_{\infty}^{\pi}\quad\hskip 46.94687ptN=\infty,\end{cases}

(9)

where
\pi_{N}
is the
symmetrization operator
defined by continuous and linear extension on elementary tensors
a_{N}=\alpha_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\alpha_{N}

\displaystyle\pi_{N}(a_{N}):=\frac{1}{N!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{N}}\alpha_{\sigma(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes\alpha_{\sigma(N)}\,

(10)

where
\alpha_{i}\in\mathfrak{A}
for all
i=1\dots N
,
and the summation is over the elements
\sigma
in the permutation group of order
N!
, denoted by
S_{N}
. Moreover, the algebra
[\mathfrak{A}]_{\pi}^{\infty}
is defined in an inductive way, yielding a so-called generalized inductive system
[
1
,
\lx@sectionsign
V.4.3]
. This algebra is actually the completion of equivalence classes of symmetric sequences (introduced below) under the equivalence relation

\displaystyle a_{N}\sim b_{N}\ \ \ \text{iff}\ \ \ \lim_{N\to\infty}\|a_{N}-b_{N}\|_{N}=0,

and norm

\displaystyle\|[a_{N}]_{N}\|=\lim_{N\to\infty}\|a_{N}\|_{N}.

This construction leads to a continuous field of
C^{*}
-algebras whose continuous sections are given by quasi-symmetric sequences
[
11
, Theorem 8.4]
. To construct these we need to generalize the definition of
\pi_{N}
. For
\Lambda\subset X
with
M=|\Lambda|
, define a bounded operator
\pi_{N}^{M}:{\mathfrak{A}^{\Lambda}}\to{\mathfrak{A}}^{X}
, defined by linear and continuous extension of

\displaystyle\pi_{N}^{M}(a)=\pi_{N}(a\otimes\underbrace{1\otimes\cdots\otimes 1}_{N-M\mbox{\scriptsize\ times}}),\quad a\in{\mathfrak{A}}^{\Lambda}.

(11)

Clearly,
\pi_{N}^{N}=\pi_{N}
. We say that a sequence

(a_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
is called (strictly)
symmetric

if there exist
\Lambda\subset X
(with
M=|\Lambda|
) and
a_{\Lambda}\in{\mathfrak{A}}^{\Lambda}
such that

\displaystyle a_{N}=\pi_{N}^{M}(a_{\Lambda})\ \ \>\mbox{for all }N\geq M,

and
quasi-symmetric
if
a_{N}=\pi_{N}(a_{N})
if
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
and for every
\varepsilon>0
, there is a symmetric sequence
(a_{N}^{\prime})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}

as well as
\Lambda\subset X
(both depending on
\varepsilon
) such that

\displaystyle\|a_{N}-a_{N}^{\prime}\|<\varepsilon\>\mbox{ for all }N>M.

Example 2
.

If
\lim_{N\to\infty}\|a_{N}-a_{N}^{\prime}\|_{N}=0
for some fixed symmetric sequence
(a_{N}^{\prime})
, then
(a_{N})
is obviously quasi-symmetric.

\blacksquare

Remark
.

From a classical point of view, quasi-symmetric sequences are also called “tail events”, i.e., events whose occurrence is not altered by local changes.

\blacksquare

We write
S({\mathfrak{A}})
for the algebraic state space, consisting of all positive linear normalized functionals
\omega:\mathfrak{A}\to\mathbb{C}
, and indicate by
C(S(\mathfrak{A}))
the
C^{*}
-algebra of continuous functions on
S(\mathfrak{A})
. It is common knowledge and not hard to see (see
[
11
, Chapter 8]
,
[
12
]
for details), that for any quasi-symmetric sequence the following limit exists

\displaystyle a_{\infty}(\omega)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\omega^{N}(a_{N}),

(12)

where
\omega\in S({\mathfrak{A}})
, and the product state

\omega^{N}=\underbrace{\omega\otimes\cdots\otimes\omega}_{N\>\mbox{\scriptsize times}}\in S(\pi_{N}({\mathfrak{A}}^{N}))

is the unique (norm) continuous linear extension of the following map that is defined on elementary tensors

\displaystyle\omega^{N}(\alpha_{1}\otimes\cdot\cdot\cdot\otimes\alpha_{N})=\omega(\alpha_{1})\cdot\cdot\cdot\omega(\alpha_{N}).

Furthermore, the limit in (
12
) defines a function in
C(S(\mathfrak{A}))
provided that
(a_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
is quasi-symmetric, otherwise it may not exist. In particular, the continuous cross-sections are the sequences of the form
(a_{N})_{N\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}=(a_{N},a_{\infty})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
, where
a_{\infty}
is defined by (
12
). These sections are also called
macroscopic
, due to their commutative character in the limit
N\to\infty
.

In fact, an application of the Quantum De Finetti Theorem entails that
[\mathfrak{A}]_{\infty}^{\pi}
is commutative and isometrically isomorphic to
C(S(\mathfrak{A}))
,
[
11
, Chapter 8]
,
[
20
]
. This isomorphism is precisely implemented by the following map,
cf.
(
12
)

\displaystyle j:[\mathfrak{A}]_{\infty}^{\pi}\to C(S(\mathfrak{A})),\qquad j([a_{N}]_{N})(\omega):=\lim_{N\to\infty}\omega^{N}(a_{N})=a_{\infty}(\omega).

(13)

While the above construction works for general
\mathfrak{A}
, more can be said in the specific case that
\mathfrak{A}=M_{\ell}(\mathbb{C})
. Indeed, the above construction then relates to a strict deformation quantization of the state space
S(M_{\ell}(\mathbb{C}))

[
12
, Theorem 3.4]
. Specifically, there exists an affine parametrization of
S(M_{\ell}(\mathbb{C}))
representing
S(M_{\ell}(\mathbb{C}))
as a compact convex subset
\mathcal{Q}_{\ell}\subset\mathbb{R}^{\ell^{2}-1}

with non-empty interior which coordinates are explicitly given by

\displaystyle S(M_{\ell}(\mathbb{C}))\to\mathcal{Q}_{\ell},\quad\omega\mapsto(x_{\mu}(\omega))_{\mu}^{\ell^{2}-1}=(\omega_{t_{\mu}})_{\mu}^{\ell^{2}-1},

(14)

where the matrices
(t_{\mu})_{\mu=1}^{\ell^{2}-1}
in
M_{\ell}(\mathbb{C})
are a normalized basis of the real vector space of traceless self-adjoint
\ell\times\ell
matrices, see
[
12
, Eqn. (2.11)]
.

Moreover, the interior
\text{int}(\mathcal{Q}_{\ell})
of
\mathcal{Q}_{\ell}
corresponds to the
rank-
\ell
density matrices and is a connected
\ell^{2}-1
dimensional smooth manifold
[
12
,
\lx@sectionsign
2.2]
. In this realization,
S(M_{\ell}(\mathbb{C}))
is shown to admit a canonical Poisson structure, given for
f,g\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{Q}_{\ell})
by

\displaystyle\{f,g\}(x)=\sum_{a,b,c=1}^{\ell^{2}-1}C_{ab}^{c}x_{c}\frac{\partial\tilde{f}(x)}{\partial x_{a}}\frac{\partial\tilde{g}(x)}{\partial x_{b}},\ \ x\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{Q}_{\ell}),

(15)

where
\tilde{f},\tilde{g}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{\ell^{2}-1})
are arbitrary extensions of
f,g
and
C_{ab}^{c}
are the structure constants corresponding to the lie algebra of
SU(\ell)
. The precise technical details of this construction can be found in
[
12
,
\lx@sectionsign
2.2,
\lx@sectionsign
2.3]
.

The quantization maps
Q_{N}
are defined on a dense Poisson algebra
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\infty}^{\pi}\subset\mathcal{A}_{\infty}^{\pi}\simeq C(S(M_{\ell}(\mathbb{C})))
as follows. First, the relevant Poisson subalgebra
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\infty}^{\pi}
is made of the restrictions to
S(M_{\ell}(\mathbb{C}))
of polynomials in
\ell^{2}-1
coordinates of
\mathbb{R}^{\ell^{2}-1}
. Each elementary tensor of the form

t_{j_{1}}\otimes\cdot\cdot\cdot\otimes t_{j_{L}},

where
it_{1},\ldots,it_{\ell^{2}-1}
are traceless self-adjoint
\ell\times\ell
matrices forming a basis of the Lie algebra of

, may be uniquely identified with a monomial
p_{L}
of degree
L

[

. This allows one to define the quantization map
Q_{N}
as follows. If

\displaystyle p_{L}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell^{2}-1})=x_{j_{1}}\cdots x_{j_{L}}\quad\mbox{where $j_{1},\ldots,j_{L}\in\{1,2,\ldots,\ell^{2}-1\}$,}

the quantization maps
Q_{N}:\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\infty}^{\pi}\to\mathcal{A}_{X}^{\pi}
act as

\displaystyle Q_{N}(p_{L})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\pi_{N}^{L}(t_{j_{1}}\otimes\cdot\cdot\cdot\otimes t_{j_{L}}),&\ \text{if}\ N\geq L\\
0,&\ \text{if}\ N<L,\end{cases}

(16)

\displaystyle Q_{N}(1)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{1_{\ell}\otimes\cdots\otimes 1_{\ell}}_{\scriptsize N\>\mbox{times}},

(17)

where
\pi_{N}^{L}
is given by (

to all polynomials by taking linear combinations of the elementary tensors

The maps

indeed satisfy the required conditions (

),(

, cf.
[

, whose properties will be used in

Remark
.

It is precisely this interpretation of the limit
N\to\infty
that relates mean field quantum spin systems to strict deformation quantization, since the ensuing mean-field Hamiltonians correspond to quasi-symmetric sequences which in turn are defined by the quantization maps (
16
)–(
17
). A straightforward combinatorial exercise shows that (

\displaystyle\|H_{N}/N-Q_{N}(h_{\infty})\|_{N}=O(1/N),

(18)

where

is a polynomial (principal symbol) defining the mean-field model.
We point out that the scaling factor

is essential: it says that the mean-field Hamiltonian is of order

\blacksquare

3
Logarithmic moment generating function and local Gibbs states

From now on
{\mathfrak{A}}=M_{\ell}(\mathbb{C})
and we consider the continuous field of
C^{*}
-algebras with continuous cross-sections given by quasi-symmetric sequences. We first discuss the principle of large deviations for local Gibbs states, in that, we focus on the quantity
F_{N}
defined by (
4
).
Our main result is Theorem
8
, proving sufficient conditions for the limit
F(t):=\lim_{N\to\infty}F_{N}(t)
to exist for all small
t
.

To this avail, we proceed as follows. We rewrite

\displaystyle F_{N}(t)=\frac{1}{N}\log{Tr[e^{N\bar{Z}_{N,t}}]}-\frac{1}{N}\log{Tr[e^{Nh_{N}}]}

where

\displaystyle\bar{Z}_{N,t}=\frac{1}{N}\log{e^{Nta_{N}}e^{Nh_{N}}},

(19)

which is well-defined since
e^{Na_{N}}e^{Nb_{N}}
is invertible. We will prove that
[\bar{Z}_{N,t}]_{N}\in[\mathfrak{A}]_{\infty}^{\pi}
. Subsequently, we show that
\lim_{N\to\infty}F_{N}(t)
exists and characterize the limit.

3.1
A Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff approach

For any matrices
a,b\in M_{\ell}(\mathbb{C})
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we consider the following formal expression for the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff (BCH) formula

\displaystyle e^{sa}e^{sb}=e^{Z(s)},\ \ Z(s):=\log{(e^{sa}e^{sb})}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}s^{n}z(n),

(20)

where the Taylor coefficients are given as usual by

\displaystyle z(n)=\frac{1}{n!}\left.\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial s^{n}}Z(s)\right|_{s=0}.

Note that the first terms can be computed explicitly, they are given by the nested commutators

\displaystyle z(1)=a+b,\ \ z(2)=\frac{1}{2}[a,b],\ \ z(3)=\frac{1}{6}[z(2),b-a],\ \ z(4)=\frac{1}{12}[[z(3),a],b].

In order to analyze all the coefficients, we follow the well known strategy found in the book of Varadarajan
[
24
]
.
It is a well known fact that for
s
sufficiently small the series
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}s^{n}z(n)
converges absolutely
[
19
,
23
]
.
Furthermore, for all such small
s
,
Z(s)
is a solution to the differential equation
[
24
, Lemma 2.15.2]

\displaystyle\frac{dZ}{ds}=a+b+\frac{1}{2}[a-b,Z]+\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\frac{B_{2p}}{(2p)!}\text{ad}_{Z}^{2p}(a+b),

with initial condition
Z(0)=0
. Here
\text{ad}_{Z}^{0}(a)=a
,
\text{ad}_{Z}^{k}(a)=[Z,{\text{ad}_{Z}}^{k-1}(a)]
and
B_{2p}
are the Bernoulli numbers.
This allows one to determine
all
the coefficients
z(n)
, as done in e.g.
[
24
, Lemma 2.15.3]
. Indeed, they can be shown to be uniquely determined by the following recursion formulae

\displaystyle z(1)=a+b;

(21)

\displaystyle z(n)=\frac{1}{2n}[a-b,z(n-1)]+\frac{1}{n}\hskip-5.69046pt\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\geq 1\\
2p\leq n-1\end{subarray}}\hskip-8.5359pt\frac{B_{2p}}{(2p)!}\hskip-5.69046pt\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},\cdots,k_{2p}>0\\
k_{1}+\cdots k_{2p}=n-1\end{subarray}}\hskip-17.07182pt[z(k_{1}),[\cdots[z(k_{2p}),a+b]{\scriptsize\cdots}]

\displaystyle=:z(a,b,[\cdot,\cdot],n).

(22)

Observe that we include the commutator in the definition of
z(a,b,[\cdot,\cdot],n)
since we will replace it later also with a Poisson bracket
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
.

3.2
Method of majorants

There exist several approaches proving the convergence of the series
Z(1)

[
19
,
23
]
. For reasons that will become clear in the next paragraph, we follow the
method of majorants
originating with Varadarajan
[
24
, Theorem 2.15.4]
, briefly outlined in what follows. First, as seen above the coefficients
z(n)
are determined by a recursion scheme involving only rational coefficients (defining the
z(n)
). One can set up a parallel recursion scheme where these coefficients are replaced by their absolute values. The solution to this parallel problem is always positive and can be shown to define a convergent solution, while the original solution is majorized by the solution to the parallel problem. This trick of “comparing” solutions will therefore prove convergence of the original problem.

To this avail, consider the differential equation

\displaystyle\frac{dy}{dz}=\frac{1}{2}y+\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\frac{|B_{2p}|}{(2p)!}y^{2p},

(23)

\displaystyle y(0)=0.

It can be shown that there is a uniform constant
\delta>0
such that the above equation admits a solution
y
which is holomorphic in the disc
\{z\mid|z|<\delta\}
,
[
24
]
. The coefficients appearing in the ensuing power series defining
y
satisfy a similar recurrence relation as the
z(n)
,
cf.
(
22
).
The key idea is that the
z(n)
can be majorized by the coefficients defining
y
up to an exponential factor. This allows one to prove convergence of the BCH series
Z(1)
. The precise result is stated below, further details regarding the method of majorants can be found in the book by Varadarajan
[
24
]
.

Theorem 3

(Theorem 2.15.4 in
[
24
]
)
:

Let
\delta>0
be given as above. The series
Z(1)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}z(n)
converges absolutely for all
a,b\in\mathfrak{L}:=\{c\in M_{\ell}(\mathbb{C})\ |\ \|c\|<\delta/4\}
, its sum defines an analytic map from
\mathfrak{L}\times\mathfrak{L}
into
M_{\ell}(\mathbb{C})
, and
e^{a}e^{b}=e^{Z}
.

3.3
A “classical” counterpart of the BCH formula

To see how this adapts to our case, we take a finite set
X\subset\mathbb{Z}
,
N=|X|
, two fixed positive natural numbers
M,M^{\prime}
and consider

a_{N}=\pi_{N}^{M}(a_{M})\quad\mbox{and}\quad b_{N}=\pi_{N}^{M^{\prime}}(b_{M^{\prime}})

two strict symmetric sequences, i.e., continuous cross-sections of the symmetric
C^{*}
-bundle. We are interested in the sequence
\bar{Z}_{N}\equiv\bar{Z}_{N,1}
given by (
19
), that is, the element
Z(s)
defined by (
20
) for
s=1
with
a
and
b
from above replaced by
Na_{N}
and
Nb_{N}
, and scaled by an overall factor
1/N
. According to the analysis above
\bar{Z}_{N}
assumes the following formal form

\displaystyle\bar{Z}_{N}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}N^{n}z_{N}(n)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bar{z}_{N}(n),

(24)

where

z_{N}(n)=z(Na_{N},Nb_{N},[\cdot,\cdot],n)\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\bar{z}_{N}(n)=\frac{1}{N}z_{N}(n)

i.e., we obtain a similar expression as (
22
)

\bar{z}_{N}(1)=a_{N}+b_{N},\;\bar{z}_{N}(2)=\frac{N}{2}[a_{N},b_{N}],\;\bar{z}_{N}(3)=\frac{N}{6}[\bar{z}_{N}(2),b_{N}-a_{N}],\,\ldots.

We do not claim that the formal sum (
24
) converges for finite
N
.

First of all, from the definition of the
\bar{z}_{N}(n)
it is not at all evident how to bound the norms of
a_{N}
and
b_{N}
such that
Z_{N}
indeed converges. The standard estimate
\|[a_{N},b_{N}]\|_{N}\leq 2\|a_{N}\|\|b_{N}\|
does not work because of the pre-factors
N
causing divergent behavior.

A more sophisticated bound would be to use the Dirac-Groenewold-Rieffel condition,
cf.
(
2
) allowing to write each
\bar{z}_{N}(n)
as a quantized
n
-fold Poisson bracket up to order
O(1/N)
. However, it is not true that the
O(1/N)
error terms hold independently of
n
. It is therefore not at all clear whether the existence of the series (
24
) holds for any finite
N
, which makes a usual analysis as done in e.g.
[
19
,
23
]
not possible.

Nonetheless, at the level of equivalence classes we show below that the series indeed exists and defines an element of
[\mathfrak{A}]_{\infty}^{\pi}
.

To this end, we focus on the method of majorants, introduced in
\lx@sectionsign\ref{Methodofmaj}
.
It is precisely these techniques that will be adapted to prove convergence in our case. In fact, we first prove the existence of a “classical” analog of the BCH formula, and then use an algebraic argument to obtain convergence of the function
F_{N}(t)
, as
N\to\infty
at least for small
t
. To this end, we proceed as follows.

We define the truncation of
\bar{Z}_{N}
given by (
24
) as

\displaystyle\bar{Z}_{N}^{k}:=\sum_{n=1}^{k}N^{n-1}z_{N}(n)=\sum_{n=1}^{k}\bar{z}_{N}(n).

(25)

The next lemma shows that the truncated series is a continuous cross-section.

Lemma 4
:

Let
\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime}\subset X
with
M=|\Lambda|
,
M^{\prime}=|\Lambda^{\prime}|
and
N=|X|
. Consider
a^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{A}^{\Lambda}
and
b^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{A}^{\Lambda^{\prime}}
. For
N\geq M,M^{\prime}
associate the symmetric sequences
a_{N}=\pi_{N}^{M}(a^{\prime})
and
b_{N}=\pi_{N}^{M^{\prime}}(b^{\prime})
, as defined
\lx@sectionsign\ref{MFQss and all that}
. For any fixed
k>0
, the section defined by

\displaystyle A_{N}:=\begin{cases}\bar{Z}_{N}^{k},&N\in\mathbb{N};\\
[\bar{Z}_{N}^{k}]_{N},&N=\infty\end{cases}

is continuous and quasi-symmetric. In particular, there exists
\bar{Z}_{\infty}^{k}\in C(S({\mathfrak{A}}))
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\|Q_{N}(\bar{Z}_{\infty}^{k})-\bar{Z}_{N}^{k}\|_{N}=0,

(26)

and
\bar{Z}_{\infty}^{k}
is a linear combination of
k
terms of
n
-fold nested Poisson brackets, for
n\leq k
.

Proof.

On account of the results in
[
12
,
\lx@sectionsign
3]
, the sequences
(a_{N})_{N}
and
(b_{N})_{N}
are symmetric, therefore they arise as quantized polynomials
a_{\infty}
and
b_{\infty}
, i.e.
a_{N}=Q_{N}(a_{\infty})
and
b_{N}=Q_{N}(b_{\infty})
. The polynomials
a_{\infty}
and
b_{\infty}
are just the classical limits of the ensuing continuous cross-sections,
cf.
(
12
).

For
n=1
, the sequence
\bar{z}_{N}(1)
satisfies
\bar{z}_{N}(1)=Q_{N}(a_{\infty})+Q_{N}(b_{\infty})=Q_{N}(a_{\infty}+b_{\infty})
, by linearity. On account of (
1
), the ensuing cross-section is continuous with limit
\bar{z}_{\infty}(1)=a_{\infty}+b_{\infty}
. It is easy seen that the same holds for linear combinations of arbitrary (quasi) symmetric continuous cross-sections.

For
n=2
, the Dirac-Groenewold-Rieffel condition (
2
), yields that
\bar{z}_{N}(2)=N[a_{N},b_{N}]
is asymptotically norm-equivalent to the sequence defined by
Q_{N}(\{a_{\infty},b_{\infty}\})
. By (
1
), this is continuous, so that
\bar{z}_{N}(2)
is continuous itself, with limit
\bar{z}_{\infty}(2)=\{a_{\infty},b_{\infty}\}
. In fact,
\bar{z}_{N}(2)
induces a quasi-symmetric sequence, as also seen from Example
2
. The same holds for commutators of arbitrary continuous cross sections.

For
n
finite but arbitrary,
\bar{z}_{N}(n)
is a linear combination of nested commutators of continuous cross sections. The classical limit is given by (
21
)–(
22
) replacing the commutators (for
\bar{z}_{N}(n))
with the Poisson brackets.

As this remains true for (finite) sums, we conclude that
(\bar{Z}_{N}^{k})_{N}
is continuous, with limit
\bar{Z}_{\infty}^{k}=\sum_{n=1}^{k}\bar{z}_{\infty}(n)\in C(S(\mathfrak{A}))
. Since
C(S(\mathfrak{A}))\cong[\mathfrak{A}]_{\infty}^{\pi}
,
cf.
(
13
), the ensuing cross-section
(A_{N})_{N\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}
is continuous and quasi-symmetric. By construction, (
26
) holds.
∎

As already seen in the proof of Lemma
4
one can introduce a classical analog
\bar{Z}_{\infty}
of
\bar{Z}_{N}
as follows. Let us denote by
a_{\infty}
and
b_{\infty}
, the classical limits of the sections
(a_{N})_{N}
and
(b_{N})_{N}
, the latter being defined as
a_{\infty}(\omega):=\lim_{N\to\infty}\omega^{N}(a_{N})
, and similarly for
b_{\infty}
,
cf.
(
12
) and (
13
). The series
\bar{Z}_{\infty}
is now defined by substituting
N
times the commutator by the Poisson bracket yielding a series defined by the coefficients given by the classical limit of
(
22
)

\displaystyle\bar{z}_{\infty}(n)=z(a_{\infty},b_{\infty},\{\cdot,\cdot\},n),

(27)

i.e.,

\displaystyle\bar{z}_{\infty}(1)=a_{\infty}+b_{\infty},\;\bar{z}_{\infty}(2)=\frac{1}{2}\{a_{\infty},b_{\infty}\},\;\bar{z}_{\infty}(3)=\frac{1}{6}\{\bar{z}_{\infty}(2),b_{\infty}-a_{\infty}\},\;\ldots

and so on. In other words, the coefficients
\bar{z}_{\infty}(n)
are defined by the recurrence relation (
21
)–(
22
), with
[\cdot,\cdot]
replaced by
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
. Note that on account of the proof of Lemma
4
and the estimate

\displaystyle|\omega^{N}(\bar{z}_{N}(n))-\omega^{N}(Q_{N}(\bar{z}_{\infty}(n)))|

\displaystyle\leq\|\bar{z}_{N}(n)-Q_{N}(\bar{z}_{\infty}(n))\|_{N},

each
\bar{z}_{\infty}(n)
is the classical limit of the
\bar{z}_{N}(n)
appearing in the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff expansion.

Due to the Poisson bracket structure, it is not directly clear how to impose single bounds on
a_{\infty}
and
b_{\infty}
such that the associated series
\bar{Z}_{\infty}
converges. In particular, the supremum norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
does not entail a bound of the form
\|\{f,g\}\|_{\infty}\leq M\|f\|_{\infty}\|g\|_{\infty}
. Therefore, a similar analysis as done in
[
23
]
seems not possible. This is exactly the point where the method of majorants comes into play. First, let us formally write

\displaystyle\bar{Z}_{\infty,t}:=\lim_{k\to\infty}\bar{Z}_{\infty,t}^{k}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\bar{Z}_{\infty,t}^{k}:=\sum_{n=1}^{k}\bar{z}_{\infty,t}(n),

where

\bar{z}_{\infty,t}(n)=z(ta_{\infty},b_{\infty},\{\cdot,\cdot\},n)

are the coefficients corresponding to the classical limits of

\bar{z}_{N,t}(n)=\frac{1}{N}z(tNa_{N},Nb_{N},[\cdot,\cdot],n),

the latter are
t
-dependent and defined as those coefficients appearing in the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff expansion for
b_{N}
and
a_{N}
replaced by
ta_{N}
. Similarly, one may consider also its quantum analog as a formal sum of the
\bar{z}_{N,t}(n)
terms

\displaystyle\bar{Z}_{N,t}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\bar{Z}_{N,t}^{k}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{k}\bar{z}_{N,t}(n),

with
a_{N}
replaced by
ta_{N}
. As mentioned above, despite the fact that this limit may not exist for finite
N
, at the level of equivalence classes it will converge towards an element of
[\mathfrak{A}]_{\infty}^{\pi}
.

To this avail we proceed in the following manner. As seen in
\lx@sectionsign\ref{MFQss and all that}
, the state space
S(\mathfrak{A})\cong\mathcal{Q}_{\ell}\subset\mathbb{R}^{\ell^{2}-1}
.
For
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{Q}_{\ell})
, define the following norm

\displaystyle\|f\|^{(k)}:=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}\frac{1}{\alpha!}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{\infty}<\infty,

where
\alpha
is a multi index and
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
is the supremum norm on
C(\mathcal{Q}_{\ell})
. By direct inspection, this norm is sub-multiplicative. Indeed,

\partial^{\alpha}(fg)=\sum_{\beta\leq\alpha}\binom{\alpha}{\beta}(\partial^{\beta}f)(\partial^{\alpha-\beta}g),

implying that

\displaystyle\|fg\|^{(k)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}\frac{1}{\alpha!}\|\partial^{\alpha}(fg)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}\sum_{\beta\leq\alpha}\frac{1}{\beta!(\alpha-\beta)!}\|\partial^{\beta}f\|_{\infty}\|\partial^{\alpha-\beta}g\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|^{(k)}\|g\|^{(k)}.

If
\lim_{k\to\infty}\|f\|^{(k)}
exists, we also define

\displaystyle\|f\|:=\lim_{k\to\infty}\|f\|^{(k)}.

It is not difficult to see that
\|\cdot\|
defines a sub-multiplicative norm as well, and moreover,
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}\leq\|\cdot\|
.

The following result is crucial for the forthcoming analysis.

Lemma 5
:

Let
f,g\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{Q}_{\ell})
be such that both
\|f\|
and
\|g\|
are finite. Then,

\displaystyle\|\{f,g\}\|\leq M\|f\|\cdot\|g\|,

for some
M>0
that does not depend on
f
and
g
. Furthermore, all nested
n
-fold Poisson brackets of
f
and
g
have finite norm for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

By definition, for all
x\in\mathcal{Q}_{\ell}

\{f,g\}(x)=\sum_{a,b,c=1}^{\ell^{2}-1}C_{ab}^{c}x_{c}\frac{\partial\tilde{f}(x)}{\partial x_{a}}\frac{\partial\tilde{g}(x)}{\partial x_{b}},

where
\tilde{f},\tilde{g}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{\ell^{2}-1})
are arbitrary extensions of
f,g
,
cf.
(
15
).
Since the norm
\|\cdot\|
is sub-multiplicative, it follows that

\|\{f,g\}\|=\|\{\tilde{f},\tilde{g}\}_{|_{\mathcal{Q}_{\ell}}}\|\leq\sum_{a,b,c}^{\ell^{2}-1}C_{ab}^{c}\|x_{c}\|\cdot\|f\|\cdot\|g\|=M\cdot\|f\|\cdot\|g\|,

since
\|x_{c}\|_{\infty}=1
, which follows from the fact that
\mathcal{Q}_{\ell}\cong S(M_{\ell}(\mathbb{C}))
, in particular
x_{c}=\omega(t_{c})
, see (
14
), which is bounded by one, since
t_{c}
is a normalized generator
and the derivative of
x_{c}
equals one, so that
\|x_{c}\|=2.

Here, the constant
M>0
is defined as

\displaystyle M:=2(\ell^{2}-1)^{3}\max_{1\leq a,b,c\leq\ell^{2}-1}C_{ab}^{c}<\infty.

(28)

The second statement about nested Poisson brackets follows by induction.
∎

Remark
.

Recall that a smooth function
w:U\to\mathbb{R}
is real analytic if and only if for every compact set
K\subset U
there is
C>0
such that for every multi-index
\alpha
the following bound holds

\displaystyle\sup_{{x}\in K}|\partial^{\alpha}w({x})|\leq C^{|\alpha|+1}\alpha!,

see
[
9
]
. Now, finiteness of the norm
\|\cdot\|
precisely gives this estimate for the partial derivatives. Although
S(\mathfrak{A})
is not an open set, we can parameterize
S(\mathfrak{A})
in terms of
\mathcal{Q}_{\ell}
, whose interior is an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{\ell^{2}-1}

cf.
(
14
). Therefore, one can still use the characterization above for analytic functions by looking at the interior of
S(\mathfrak{A})
via
\mathcal{Q}_{\ell}
, where the parameterization is analytic.

\blacksquare

Given
f,g\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{Q}_{\ell})
with
\|f\|,\|g\|<\infty
, for
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we introduce the following sequence of subspace

\mathfrak{D}_{k}(f,g):=\text{span}\{\text{$n$-fold Poisson brackets involving $f$ and $g$}\ |\ 1\leq n\leq k\}.

with the convention that
\mathfrak{D}_{0}(f,g)=\text{span}\{f,g\}
, i.e. the linear span of the functions
f
and
g
. Now, Lemma
5
yields by induction that for any
w\in\mathfrak{D}_{k}(f,g)
, we have
\|w\|<\infty
for any
k
.
Since
\mathfrak{D}_{k}(f,g)\subset\mathfrak{D}_{k+1}(f,g)
, we can consider the completion

\mathfrak{D}(f,g):=\overline{\bigcup_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{D}_{k}(f,g)}^{\|\cdot\|}

within
C^{\infty}(S(\mathfrak{A}))
.
Moreover,
\mathfrak{D}(f,g)\neq\{0\}
as soon as
f
and
g
are non-zero.

From the previous results it follows that
if
f,g
is such that both
\|f\|
and
\|g\|
are finite, the ensuing sequence defined by
\bar{Z}_{\infty}^{k}=\sum_{n=1}^{k}\bar{z}_{\infty}(n)
forms a sequence in
\mathfrak{D}(f,g)
.

For
\delta>0
defined by Theorem
3
and
M
by (
28
), consider now the open set

\displaystyle\mathfrak{U}=\{f\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{Q}_{\ell})\ |\ \|f\|<\frac{\delta}{2M}\}.

(29)

Proposition 6
:

Let
f,g\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{Q}_{\ell})
be polynomials in
\mathfrak{U}
and consider the associated vector space
\mathfrak{D}(f,g)
.
Then, the series
\bar{Z}_{\infty}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bar{z}_{\infty}(n)
converges absolutely with respect to
\|\cdot\|
, that is,
\bar{Z}_{\infty}\in\mathfrak{D}(f,g)
.

Proof.

The assertion is an adaptation of
[
24
, Lemma 2.15.3, Theorem 2.15.4]
and a repeating application of Lemma
5
.
Note that by construction, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the nested Poisson brackets
\bar{z}_{\infty}(n)
satisfy the recurrence relation (
21
)–(
22
), with
[\cdot,\cdot]
replaced by
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
. For
n=1
, we estimate
\|\bar{z}_{\infty}(1)\|\leq 2\alpha
, where
\alpha=\max{\{\|f\|,\|g\|\}}
and for
n\geq 2
, the equations relation (
21
)–(
22
), imply the estimate

\displaystyle(n+1)\|\bar{z}_{\infty}(n+1)\|

(30)

\displaystyle\leq\alpha M\|\bar{z}_{\infty}(n)\|+2\alpha\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\geq 1,\\
2p\leq n\end{subarray}}\frac{|B_{2p}|}{(2p)!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},\cdots,k_{2p}>0\\
k_{1}+\cdots k_{2p}=n\end{subarray}}M^{2p}\|\bar{z}_{\infty}(k_{1})\|\cdots\|\bar{z}_{\infty}(k_{2p})\|.

Let
y
be the solution of the differential equation (
23
),

\frac{dy}{dz}=\frac{1}{2}y+\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\frac{|B_{2p}|}{(2p)!}y^{2p}

which is analytic in
\{z\ |\ |z|<\delta\}
by Theorem

, and write

\displaystyle y(z)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\gamma(n)z^{n}.

(31)

Substituting (
31

23

\displaystyle(n+1)\gamma(n+1)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\gamma(n)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\geq 1,\\
2p\leq n\end{subarray}}\frac{|B_{2p}|}{(2p)!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},\cdots,k_{2p}>0\\
k_{1}+\cdots k_{2p}=n\end{subarray}}\gamma(k_{1})\cdots\gamma(k_{2p}),

(32)

\displaystyle\gamma(1)

\displaystyle=1.

Then,
\gamma(m)\geq 0
, for all
m
. We now claim the following

\displaystyle\|\bar{z}_{\infty}(m)\|\leq M^{m-1}(2\alpha)^{m}\gamma(m).

(33)

Indeed, since
\|z_{\infty}(1)\|\leq 2\alpha
, this is true for
m=1
. Suppose (

) is true for all

. Then, from (

\displaystyle(m+1)\|\bar{z}_{\infty}(m+1)\|

\displaystyle\leq M^{m}(2\alpha)^{m+1}\frac{\gamma(m)}{2}+2\alpha\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\geq 1,\\
2p\leq n\end{subarray}}\frac{|B_{2p}|}{(2p)!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},\cdots,k_{2p}>0\\

Thus, (

Since the series (

converges absolutely whenever

∎

3.4
Large deviation upper bounds

Finally, we are in a position to prove the convergence of the logarithmic generating function, at least for small
t
, that is, at sufficiently large temperature. As indicated in
\lx@sectionsign
1.3
we assume the following set-up. Let
\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime}\subset X
with
M=|\Lambda|
,
M^{\prime}=|\Lambda^{\prime}|
and
N=|X|
. Consider
a^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{A}^{\Lambda}
and
h^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{A}^{\Lambda^{\prime}}
, and for
N\geq M,M^{\prime}
associate the symmetric sequences
a_{N}=\pi_{N}^{M}(a^{\prime})
and
h_{N}=\pi_{N}^{M^{\prime}}(h^{\prime})
, as defined in
\lx@sectionsign
2.2
. In Theorem
8
we provide sufficient conditions such that the function
F_{N}(t)
defined by (
4
), i.e.,

\displaystyle F_{N}(t):=\frac{1}{N}\log{\frac{Tr[e^{Nh_{N}}e^{Nta_{N}}]}{Tr[e^{Nh_{N}}]}}=\frac{1}{N}\log{Tr[e^{N\bar{Z}_{N,t}}]}-\frac{1}{N}\log{Tr[e^{Nh_{N}}]},

has a limit as

. We moreover characterize the pertinent limit. To this avail, we prove that
\bar{Z}_{N,t}
defined by (

) induces a continuous cross-section of the symmetric bundle. For such sections, a recent result by van de Ven shows that the limit of

indeed exists.

Theorem 7

(Theorem 4.4 in
[
26
]
)
:

Let
(a_{N})_{N}
be a continuous cross-section of symmetric
C^{*}-
bundle with classical limit

. Then, the mean-field free energy satisfies

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log{Tr[e^{Na_{N}}]}=\sup_{\omega\in S(\mathfrak{A})}(a_{\infty}(\omega)-s_{\infty}(\delta_{\omega})),

where the function
s_{\infty}
is the
mean-field entropy
defined for any state
\omega\in S([\mathfrak{A}]_{\infty}^{\pi})
by

\displaystyle s_{\infty}(\omega):=-\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}Tr[\rho_{N}^{\omega}\log{\rho_{N}^{\omega}}].

(34)

Here
\rho_{N}^{\omega}
is the density matrix associated with the state
\omega_{N}
, the latter given by restriction of
\omega
to
S(\mathfrak{A}^{N})
. For
\omega\in S(\mathfrak{A})
, the state
\delta_{\omega}
is an extremal state on

given by point evaluation, i.e.,
\delta_{\omega}(f)=f(\omega)
. In fact, all extremal states arising in this way.

Remark
.

The mean-field entropy always exists for states on
[\mathfrak{A}]_{\infty}^{\pi}
and defines a weak-
*
continuous functional on
S([\mathfrak{A}]_{\infty}^{\pi})

\blacksquare

Theorem 8
:

Assume the principal symbol
h_{\infty}
of the continuous cross section
(h_{N})_{N}
is in
\mathfrak{U}
.
Then, for
t
sufficiently small the function
F_{N}(t)
defined by (
4
)
converges as
N\to\infty
, and the limit is given by

F(t)=\lim_{N\to\infty}F_{N}(t)=\sup_{\omega\in S(\mathfrak{A})}(\bar{Z}_{\infty,t}-s_{\infty}(\omega))-\sup_{\omega\in S(\mathfrak{A})}(h_{\infty}(\omega)-s_{\infty}(\omega)),

where
s_{\infty}(\omega)
is the mean-field entropy,
cf.
Theorem
7
.

Proof.

To prove this we first observe that the classical limits
a_{\infty}
and
h_{\infty}
of the continuous cross section
a_{N}
and
h_{N}
are polynomials, i.e. the vector space
\mathfrak{D}(a_{\infty},h_{\infty})\neq\{0\}
. We can therefore find
t>0
small enough such that
2tM\|a_{\infty}\|<\delta
, i.e.,
ta_{\infty}\in\mathfrak{U}
. Moreover, the ensuing coefficients
\bar{z}_{\infty,t}(n)
satisfy the recurrence relation (
21
)–(
22
) with
[\cdot,\cdot]
replaced by
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
.
Therefore, on account of Proposition
6
the series
\bar{Z}_{\infty,t}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bar{z}_{\infty,t}(n)
exists in
\mathfrak{D}(a_{\infty},h_{\infty})
. In particular, the partial sums
Z_{\infty,t}^{k}=\sum_{n=1}^{k}\bar{z}_{\infty,t}(n)
form a Cauchy sequence, so that

\displaystyle\lim_{k,l\to\infty}\|[\bar{Z}_{N,t}^{l}]_{N}-[\bar{Z}_{N,t}^{k}]_{N}\|

\displaystyle=\lim_{k,l\to\infty}\lim_{N\to\infty}\|\bar{Z}_{N,t}^{l}-\bar{Z}_{N,t}^{k}\|_{N}

\displaystyle=\lim_{k,l\to\infty}\|\bar{Z}_{\infty,t}^{l}-\bar{Z}_{\infty,t}^{k}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{k,l\to\infty}\|\bar{Z}_{\infty,t}^{l}-\bar{Z}_{\infty,t}^{k}\|=0,

where in the second step we have used that the sections
[\bar{Z}_{N,t}^{k}]_{N}
are symmetric sequences and hence continuous,
cf.
Lemma

. Hence,
([\bar{Z}_{N,t}^{k}]_{N})_{k}
is a Cauchy sequence of elements
[\bar{Z}_{N,t}^{k}]\in[\mathfrak{A}]_{\infty}^{\pi}
. Since
[\mathfrak{A}]_{\infty}^{\pi}
is a

algebra, the ensuing limit
[\bar{Z}_{N,t}]_{N}\in[\mathfrak{A}]_{\infty}^{\pi}
.
In particular, the sequence

\displaystyle A_{N,t}:=\begin{cases}\bar{Z}_{N,t},\ \ &N\in\mathbb{N};\\
[\bar{Z}_{N,t}]_{N},\ \ &N=\infty;\end{cases}

induces a continuous cross-section of the symmetric bundle. Indeed, on account of condition (
iii
) of the definition of a continuous cross-section (see
\lx@sectionsign
1.1
), the norm function is continuous, since

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\|A_{N,t}\|_{N}

\displaystyle=\|[\bar{Z}_{N,t}]_{N}\|=\|j([\bar{Z}_{N,t}]_{N})\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\|j([\bar{Z}_{N,t}^{k}]_{N})\|_{\infty}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\|\bar{Z}_{\infty,t}^{k}\|_{\infty}=\|\bar{Z}_{\infty,t}\|_{\infty},

using that the map

is an isometric
*
-isomorphism,
cf.
(
13

4

6

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}|\|\bar{Z}_{\infty,t}\|_{\infty}-\|\bar{Z}_{\infty,t}^{k}\|_{\infty}|\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}\|\bar{Z}_{\infty,t}-\bar{Z}_{\infty,t}^{k}\|_{\infty}\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}\|\bar{Z}_{\infty,t}-\bar{Z}_{\infty,t}^{k}\|=0,

Since,

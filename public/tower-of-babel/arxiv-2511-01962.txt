Generation and read-out of many-body Bell correlations with a probe qubit
Marcin Płodzień 1,2 and Jan Chwedeńczuk 3,

Abstract
As demand for quantum technologies increases, so does the need to generate and classify non-classical correlations in complex many-body systems. We introduce a simple and versatile method for creating and certifying entanglement and many-body Bell correlations. This method relies on a single qubit interacting with an N N -qubit system. We demonstrate that: (i) such pairwise interaction is sufficient to induce many-body quantum correlations, and (ii) the qubit can serve as a probe to extract all information about these correlations. Thus, single-qubit measurements reveal multi-partite entanglement and N N -body Bell correlations, enabling the rapid and efficient certification of non-classicality in complex systems.

In this work, we show that a single qubit interacting with an
N
-qubit ensemble can generate and certify quantum correlations.
First, it can effectively initiate the OAT dynamics within the system.
Second, we show that a single qubit can act as a probe to extract information from the system. Simple measurements on the probe enable us to verify the entanglement depth
and many-body Bell correlations of a wide range of systems governed by Lipkin-Meshkov-Glick (LMG) Hamiltonians

Meshkov et al. 1965

These LMG models include OAT systems and describe interacting Bose-Einstein condensates (BECs) and long-range spin-1/2 chains in the lowest momentum mode.
Additionally, they describe phonon- and photon-mediated interactions in spin-1/2 chains where the Dicke manifold is protected by an energy gap.

The probe qubit has been used to locate quantum critical points in Ising-type chains and to diagnose phase transitions by monitoring its coherence over time

Zhang et al. 2008

It can detect nonergodicity and many-body localization in disordered spin chains

spatial cuts
Szabo and Trivedi 2022
;
Doggen et al. 2024
;
Gessner et al. 2014

Meanwhile, qubit-only protocols determine whether a qubit becomes entangled with the environment during pure dephasing

;
Roszak 2020

Little is known about using a single-qubit probe to generate and detect many-body quantum correlations. This work aims to bridge this gap by providing an experimentally friendly

Certification of correlations.—
Next, we demonstrate that a single-qubit probe can certify the presence of non-classical correlations embedded in
\hat{\varrho}_{N}
.
To this end, the probe qubit governed by
\hat{\varrho}_{pr}

is “connected” to the system so that, initially, a product state is formed

\displaystyle\hat{\varrho}=\hat{\varrho}_{N}\otimes\hat{\varrho}_{pr}\,.

(4)

Next, the two parts interact in order to transfer the information about
\hat{\varrho}_{N}
to the probe via the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\hat{H}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}J_{i}\hat{\sigma}_{z}^{(i)}\hat{\sigma}_{z}^{(pr)},

(5)

where
J_{i}
’s are the coupling strengths.
After time
t
the density matrix of the probe becomes
end

\displaystyle\hat{\varrho}_{pr}(\theta,t)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathcal{P}&a\\
a^{*}&1-\mathcal{P}\end{array}\right).

The probability
\mathcal{P}
does not depend
t
, while

\displaystyle a=\sum_{\vec{s}=\pm 1}\!p_{\vec{s}}\,e^{-2it\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}J_{i}s_{i}}.

(8)

Here, the summation runs through
2^{N}
elements of the eigen-basis of the
\hat{\sigma}_{z}^{(i)}
operators and
\vec{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{N})
are their
\pm 1
eigen-values.
The diagonal element of the system’s density matrix,
p_{\vec{s}}\equiv\varrho_{N}^{(\vec{s},\vec{s})}
is the probability of the system occupying a state
|\vec{s}\rangle=|s_{1}\rangle\otimes\ldots|s_{N}\rangle
.
If the system undergoes specific local operations prior to interacting with the probe, the element
a
becomes sensitive to the off-diagonal elements of
\hat{\varrho}_{N}
and
provides information about its non-classicality.

For illustration, we pick a phase-
\theta
imprint on each of
N
qubits and the subsequent mixing of the signal using a
\pi/2
-pulse, namely

\displaystyle\hat{\varrho}_{N}(\theta)=e^{-i\frac{\pi}{2}\hat{J}_{x}}e^{-i\theta\hat{J}_{z}}\hat{\varrho}_{N}e^{i\theta\hat{J}_{z}}e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}\hat{J}_{x}}.

(9)

We now make a crucial observation: if the state

\hat{\varrho}_{N}
is permutationally invariant and all the couplings are equal,
J_{i}=J
, then the matrix element from Eq. (
8
) becomes

\displaystyle a=\sum_{n=-\frac{N}{2}}^{\frac{N}{2}}\!p_{n}(\theta)\,e^{-i2\pi\frac{\tau}{N+1}n},

(10)

where
n=\frac{1}{2}(n_{+1}-n_{-1})
is the difference between the number of qubits occupying the
s=+1
and the
s=-1
state and
\tau=\frac{J}{\pi}(N+1)t
.
Equation (
10
) is a discrete Fourier transform of the probability
p_{n}(\theta)
and its inversion yields

p_{n}(\theta)
—a many-body probability from a measurement on a single-qubit probe. We will now demonstrate how to characterize non-classical correlations using the off-diagonal element
a
.

The first quantity derived from
p_{n}(\theta)
that gives access to information on the multi-partite entanglement is
the spin-squeezing parameter
Kitagawa and Ueda 1993
;
Wineland et al. 1994
;
Giovannetti et al. 2006

\displaystyle\xi^{2}=N\frac{\Delta^{2}\hat{J}_{z}(\theta)}{\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\langle\hat{J}_{z}(\theta)\rangle\right|^{2}},

(11)

where both the numerator and the denominator can be obtained by calculating the two lowest moments of the probability,
\langle\hat{J}_{z}^{\alpha}(\theta)\rangle=\sum_{n}p_{n}(\theta)n^{\alpha}
with
\alpha=1,2
.
When
\xi<1
, the system is entangled and useful for quantum metrology

.
Furthermore, from the value of
\xi
one can deduce the entanglement depth of the system

;
Estève et al. 2008
;
Hyllus et al. 2012
.

For non-Gaussian states that cannot be characterized only by the mean and the variance of
p_{n}
, higher moments can be calculated as in
Lücke et al. 2011
.
The complete probability yields the Fisher information
Strobel et al. 2014

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}=\sum_{n=-\frac{N}{2}}^{\frac{N}{2}}\frac{1}{p_{n}(\theta)}\left(\frac{\partial p_{n}(\theta)}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2}.

(12)

Finally, according to
Wysocki et al. 2025
, the off-diagonal element
a
provides a lower bound for
\mathcal{I}_{q}\left[\varrho_{N}\right]
—the quantum Fisher information (QFI)
associated with the system’s state
Braunstein and Caves 1994
, namely

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{q}\left[\hat{\varrho}_{N}(\theta)\right]\geqslant 4\left(\frac{\texttt{Re}\left[\dot{a}e^{-i\phi}\right]^{2}}{1-|a|^{2}}+\texttt{Im}\left[\dot{a}e^{-i\varphi}\right]^{2}\right),

(13)

where
\varphi=\arg(a)
. By setting the susceptibility of
\hat{\varrho}_{N}(\theta)
to changing
\theta
, the QFI sets the ultimate precision

\Delta\theta
of phase estimation that can be achieved with it. According to
Wysocki et al. 2025
, the above bound is tight for a large family
of relevant multi-qubit states.

The above set of quantities, which all can be obtained from
a
, form a hierarchy

\displaystyle\frac{N}{\xi^{2}}\leqslant\mathcal{I}\leqslant\mathcal{I}_{q}\left[\hat{\varrho}_{N}(\theta)\right]\leqslant(\Delta\theta)^{-2}.

(14)

Depending on the setting, any of these parameters can be used to determine the lower bound of the system’s entanglement depth and metrological usability.

Discussion.
—The single-qubit probe gives access to the distribution of
N
parties,
p_{n}(\theta)
, among their two modes. Naturally,
p_{n}(\theta)
could be accessed by directly performing
measurements on each of the qubits. While such on-site measurements are feasible in chain/lattice configurations
Sherson et al. 2010
, they become much harder
when the system is a Bose gas
Lücke et al. 2011
;
Strobel et al. 2014
. Hence the method presented here, requiring a measurement on only one qubit, can significantly facilitate
the detection of many-body correlations in complex systems.

In principle, it can also serve for remote tomography of
N
-body permutation-symmetric states.
The computational complexity of the tomography of such states scales like
N^{2}
, since it uses collective measurements
Tóth et al. 2010
;
Moroder et al. 2012
;
Schwemmer et al. 2014
.
Similar scaling is expected for the single-probe method, as both the inversion of the Fourier transform in Eq. (
10
) and the resolution of
\theta
’s in Eq. (
15
)
require
\sim N
points each.
Nevertheless, direct tomography requires accurate collective detection prone to measurement errors that scale with
N
.
By contrast, single-qubit tomography that yields access to the off-diagonal element
a
, is elementary
James et al. 2001
;
Hradil 1997
;
Řeháček et al. 2001
;
James et al. 2001
.

Conclusions.
—In this work we have shown that many-body quantum correlations, such as entanglement or Bell correlations, can be generated and
certified using a single-qubit probe that interacts with the system. The effective OAT dynamics that stems from a coupling of the probe to each of the qubits yields a complex Bell-correlated system.
Furthermore, quantities like the spin-squeezing or the (quantum) Fisher information can be derived from a one-body measurement and provide information about the entanglement depth and
the presence of correlations useful for quantum metrology. Moreover, a many-body Bell correlator can also be deduced within this scheme, giving access to the most non-classical quantum correlations.
The single-qubit probe can also detect the quantum phase transition in the ground state of a broad family of LMG systems.
This protocol competes with other methods of generating and detecting quantum many-body effects by eliminating the need for cumbersome many-body measurements.

I.1
Central-spin model to OAT

In the following we outline the second–order Schrieffer–Wolff (SW) transformation
that maps the isotropic central–spin Hamiltonian to an effective one–axis twisting (OAT) form.
We consider
\hat{H}=\hat{H}_{0}+\hat{V}
, where

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Omega}{2}\,\hat{\sigma}^{(pr)}_{z}+\omega\,\hat{J}_{z}

(EM.1)

\displaystyle\hat{V}

\displaystyle=g\big(\hat{J}_{+}\hat{\sigma}^{(pr)}_{-}+\hat{J}_{-}\hat{\sigma}^{(pr)}_{+}\big),

(EM.2)

which uses the notation from the main text.
We focus on the dispersive regime

\Delta=\Omega-\omega
,
|\Delta|\gg g
,
so that direct spin exchange is off–resonant.
Projectors onto the probe-spin manifolds are

\displaystyle\hat{P}=\frac{1-\hat{\sigma}^{(pr)}_{z}}{2},\qquad\hat{Q}=\frac{1+\hat{\sigma}^{(pr)}_{z}}{2},\qquad\hat{P}+\hat{Q}=\mathbb{I}.

(EM.3)

To get rid of the off–diagonal couplings between low- and high-energy subspaces at first order,
we perform a Schrieffer–Wolff transformation

\hat{H}_{\rm eff}=e^{\hat{S}}\hat{H}e^{-\hat{S}}
with an anti-Hermitian generator
\hat{S}
.

At the leading order we choose
\hat{S}
to eliminate the
\mathcal{O}(g)

\hat{P}
–
\hat{Q}
couplings, i.e. we fix the first–order generator

\hat{S}^{(1)}
to satisfy
[\hat{H}_{0},\hat{S}^{(1)}]=-\,\hat{V}
, which yields

\hat{S}^{(1)}=\frac{g}{\Delta}\big(\hat{J}_{+}\hat{\sigma}^{(pr)}_{-}-\hat{J}_{-}\hat{\sigma}^{(pr)}_{+}\big).

(EM.4)

Expanding
e^{\hat{S}}\hat{H}e^{-\hat{S}}
to second order gives

\hat{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(2)}=\hat{H}_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\,[\hat{S}^{(1)},\hat{V}].

(EM.5)

With
\hat{X}=\hat{J}_{+}\hat{\sigma}^{(pr)}_{-}
and
\hat{V}=g(\hat{X}+\hat{X}^{\dagger})
, one finds

\frac{1}{2}\,[\hat{S}^{(1)},\hat{V}]=\frac{g^{2}}{\Delta}\,[\hat{X},\hat{X}^{\dagger}].

(EM.6)

Using
\hat{J}_{\pm}\hat{J}_{\mp}=\hat{J}^{2}-\hat{J}_{z}^{2}\pm\hat{J}_{z}
,

\hat{\sigma}^{(pr)}_{-}\hat{\sigma}^{(pr)}_{+}=\tfrac{1}{2}(1-\hat{\sigma}^{(pr)}_{z})
, and

\hat{\sigma}^{(pr)}_{+}\hat{\sigma}^{(pr)}_{-}=\tfrac{1}{2}(1+\hat{\sigma}^{(pr)}_{z})
,
we obtain

[\hat{X},\hat{X}^{\dagger}]=\hat{J}_{z}-\hat{\sigma}^{(pr)}_{z}\big(\hat{J}^{2}-\hat{J}_{z}^{2}\big).

(EM.7)

The effective Hamiltonian becomes

\hat{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(2)}=\frac{\Omega}{2}\,\hat{\sigma}^{(pr)}_{z}+\omega\,\hat{J}_{z}+\frac{g^{2}}{\Delta}\,\hat{J}_{z}-\frac{g^{2}}{\Delta}\,\hat{\sigma}^{(pr)}_{z}\big(\hat{J}^{2}-\hat{J}_{z}^{2}\big).

(EM.8)

Projecting onto the
\hat{P}
sector,
\hat{H}_{\downarrow}=\hat{P}\hat{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(2)}\hat{P}
, yields

\hat{H}_{\downarrow}=-\frac{\Omega}{2}+\Big(\omega+\frac{g^{2}}{\Delta}\Big)\hat{S}_{z}+\frac{g^{2}}{\Delta}\hat{S}^{2}-\frac{g^{2}}{\Delta}\hat{S}_{z}^{2},

(EM.9)

where
\hat{S}_{i}
is the collective
N
-spin/probe operator.
Within the symmetric manifold (
S=(N+1)/2
),
\hat{S}^{2}
is a constant and can be dropped.
Defining
\tilde{\omega}\equiv\omega+\chi
and
\chi\equiv\frac{g^{2}}{\Delta}
,
we

\hat{H}_{\downarrow}=\tilde{\omega}\,\hat{S}_{z}-\chi\,\hat{S}_{z}^{2}.

(EM.10)

Finally, by moving to the rotating frame via
\hat{U}_{z}(t)=e^{+i\tilde{\omega}t\hat{S}_{z}}
, we obtain an effective OAT model

\hat{H}_{\rm coll}=\hat{U}_{z}\hat{H}_{\downarrow}\hat{U}_{z}^{\dagger}-i\hat{U}_{z}\partial_{t}\hat{U}_{z}^{\dagger}=-\,\chi\,\hat{S}_{z}^{2}.

(EM.11)

Accuracy requires
g/|\Delta|\ll 1
and evolution times
t\ll|\Delta|/g^{2}
so that
\mathcal{O}(g^{3}/\Delta^{2})
terms remain negligible.

I.2
Bell correlator

A many-body Bell inequality
is constructed under the assumption of binary outcomes of measurements of two quantities
\sigma^{(k)}_{x/y}=\pm 1
, where
k\in\{1\ldots N\}
labels
the particles
Cavalcanti et al. 2007
;
Cavalcanti et al. 2011
;
He et al. 2011
;
Niezgoda et al. 2020
;
Niezgoda and Chwedeńczuk 2021
;
Chwedeńczuk 2022
;
Płodzień et al. 2022
;
Płodzień et al. 2024a
;
Płodzień et al. 2024b
;
Hamza and Chwedeńczuk 2024
;
Płodzień et al. 2025a
;
Płodzień et al. 2025b
;
Hernández Yanes et al. 2025
.
If the
N
-body correlation function
\mathcal{E}_{N}\equiv|\langle\prod_{k=1}^{N}\sigma_{+}^{(k)}\rangle|^{2}
is consitent with the postulates of local realism, it satisfies

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{N}

\displaystyle=|\int\!\!d\lambda\,p(\lambda)\prod_{k=1}^{N}\sigma_{+}^{(k)}(\lambda)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\int\!\!d\lambda\,p(\lambda)\prod_{k=1}^{N}|\sigma_{+}^{(k)}(\lambda)|^{2}=2^{-N},

(EM.12)

where
p(\lambda)
is the probability density of a hidden variable,
\sigma_{+}^{(k)}=\frac{1}{2}(\sigma_{x}^{(k)}+i\sigma_{y}^{(k)})
and
in the last step we used the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the fact that
|\sigma_{+}^{(k)}(\lambda)|^{2}=1/2
.
This is the many-body Bell inequality as its violation defies the postulate of local realism.
For qubits
\sigma_{+}^{(k)}
’s are replaced with the rising Pauli operators and the Bell inequality becomes

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{N}^{(q)}[\hat{\varrho}_{N}]=|{\rm Tr}\bigg[{\hat{\varrho}_{N}\,\bigotimes_{k=1}^{N}\hat{\sigma}_{+}^{(k)}}\bigg]|^{2}\leqslant 2^{-N}.

(EM.13)

The above product of
N
rising operators is non-zero only if it acts on
|-1\rangle^{\otimes N}
, giving
|+1\rangle^{\otimes N}
. Therefore

\mathcal{E}_{N}^{(q)}
is a measure of coherence between these two, which is maximal for the GHZ state,
|\psi\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|-1\rangle^{\otimes N}+|+1\rangle^{\otimes N})
,
and can be expressed as follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{N}^{(q)}[\hat{\varrho}_{N}]=|\varrho_{\frac{N}{2},-\frac{N}{2}}|^{2}.

(EM.14)

Here,
|\varrho_{\frac{N}{2},-\frac{N}{2}}|^{2}
denotes the matrix element of
\hat{\varrho}_{N}
multiplying
|-1\rangle\!\langle+1|^{\otimes N}
.

Expression for the antenna’s density matrix

The general expression for the
N
-qubit state is

\displaystyle\hat{\varrho}_{N}=\sum_{\vec{s},\vec{s}^{\prime}=\pm 1}\varrho_{\vec{s},\vec{s}^{\prime}}(\theta)|\vec{s}\rangle\!\langle\vec{s}^{\prime}|.

(EM.15)

Its coupling to the single-qubit probe as in Eq. (
4
) gives

\displaystyle\hat{\varrho}=\sum_{\vec{s},\vec{s}^{\prime}=\pm 1}\sum_{s_{p},s_{p}^{\prime}=\pm 1}\varrho_{\vec{s},\vec{s}^{\prime}}\varrho_{s_{p},s_{p}^{\prime}}|\vec{s}\rangle\!\langle\vec{s}^{\prime}|\otimes|s_{p}\rangle\!\langle s_{p}^{\prime}|.

(EM.16)

The subsequent time-evolution generated by the Hamiltonian (
5
) yields

\displaystyle\hat{\varrho}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\vec{s},\vec{s}^{\prime}=\pm 1}\sum_{s_{p},s_{p}^{\prime}=\pm 1}\varrho_{\vec{s},\vec{s}^{\prime}}\varrho_{s_{p},s_{p}^{\prime}}|\vec{s}\rangle\!\langle\vec{s}^{\prime}|\otimes|s_{p}\rangle\!\langle s_{p}^{\prime}|\times

\displaystyle\times e^{-it\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}J_{i}(s_{i}s_{p}-s^{\prime}_{i}s^{\prime}_{p})}.

(EM.17)

Once we trace-out the
N
-qubits’ degrees of freedom we are left with

\displaystyle\hat{\varrho}_{pr}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s_{p},s_{p}^{\prime}=\pm 1}\varrho_{\vec{s},\vec{s}}\varrho_{s_{p},s_{p}^{\prime}}|s_{p}\rangle\!\langle s_{p}^{\prime}|e^{-it\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}J_{i}s_{i}(s_{p}-s^{\prime}_{p})}

(EM.18)

The off-diagonal term is obtained when setting
s_{p}=1=-s_{p}^{\prime}
(or vice-versa), which yields Eq. (
8
).

Folded optimal transport and its application to separable quantum optimal transport
T. Borsoni

Abstract
. We introduce folded optimal transport, as a way of extending a cost or distance defined on the extreme boundary of a convex to the whole convex, related to convex extension. This construction broadens the framework of standard optimal transport, found to be the particular case of the convex being a simplex. Relying on Choquet’s theory and standard optimal transport, we introduce the so-called folded Kantorovich cost and folded Wasserstein distance, and study the metric properties it provides to the convex. We then apply the construction to the quantum setting, and obtain an actual separable quantum Wasserstein distance on the set of density matrices from a distance on the set of pure states, closely related to the semi-distance of Beatty and Stilck-França [ 3 ] , and of which we obtain a variety of properties. We also find that the semiclassical Golse–Paul [ 16 ] cost writes as a folded Kantorovich cost. Folded optimal transport therefore provides a unified framework for classical, semiclassical and separable quantum optimal transport.

]

\;\;\;\;\mathcal{P}_{z}=\left\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}(E)\;|\;\;\text{for }\,1\leqslant i\leqslant N_{z}-1,\;\;f^{z}_{i}\text{ is }\mu\text{-integrable and }\,\intop\nolimits_{E}f^{z}_{i}\,\mathrm{d}\mu=c^{z}_{i}\right\},\quad z\in\{x,y\},

where

Proof.

Let

.

\bullet

Proof of
(i)
.
Combining the definitions (

\widehat{D}_{p}
and (

, we have

\widehat{D}_{p}(x,y)^{p}=\inf_{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{x}}\,\inf_{\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{y}}\,\inf_{\pi\in\mathcal{C}(\mu,\nu)}\iint_{E\times E}d(z,z_{*})^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\pi(z,z_{*}).

Then (

\mathcal{P}_{(x,y)}=\left\{\pi\in\mathcal{C}(\mu,\nu)\;|\;\;\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{x},\;\;\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{y}\right\}.

\bullet

Proof of
(ii)
.
Since

(for the product natural topology). It is also bounded on

as the latter is compact. As such, the application

is continuous for the weak-

is compact in the weak-

\widehat{D}_{p}(x,y)^{p}=\inf_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{(x,y)}}G(\pi)=G(\pi^{*})=\iint_{E\times E}\,d(z,z_{*})^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\pi^{*}(z,z_{*}).

\bullet

Proof of
(iii)
.
By
[

, the application

is measure affine, so that we may apply
[

and

\inf_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{(x,y)}}G(\pi)=\inf_{\pi\in\partial_{e}\mathcal{P}_{(x,y)}}G(\pi),

where

\mathcal{P}_{(x,y)}
. Next we notice that

(16)

\mathcal{P}_{(x,y)}=\left\{\pi\in\mathcal{P}(E\times E)\;|\;\;\text{for }1\leqslant k\leqslant N_{x}+N_{y}-2,\;\;f^{x,y}_{k}\text{ is }\pi\text{-integrable and }\,\iint_{E\times E}g_{k}\,\mathrm{d}\pi=c^{x,y}_{k}\right\},

with, for all

\forall z,z_{*}\in E,\quad f^{x,y}_{k}(z,z_{*})=f^{x}_{k}(z)\quad\text{and}\quad c^{x,y}_{k}=c^{x}_{k},

and for all

\forall z,z_{*}\in E,\quad f^{x,y}_{k}(z,z_{*})=f^{y}_{k-N_{x}+1}(z_{*})\quad\text{and}\quad c^{x,y}_{k}=c^{y}_{k-N_{x}+1}.

Since the set

with at most

∎

We now introduce the notion of relative interior of a convex.

Definition 5

(Relative interior)

Consider the general Setting

. The

and

of

In the next proposition, we show that

and

are continuous in the natural topology when the relative interior of

is nonempty. Note that any finite-dimensional nonempty convex set has nonempty relative interior
[

. If

could be obtained using

because

is the convexification on

Proposition 5

(Continuity of
\widehat{D}_{p}
and
D_{p}
)
.

Consider the general Setting
1
and further assume that the relative interior of
C
, defined in Definition
5
, is nonempty. Then for any
p\geqslant 1
,
\widehat{D}_{p}
and
D_{p}
are continuous with respect to the natural topology.

Proof.

We start by proving the continuity of
\widehat{D}_{p}
. Let
(x_{n},y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of
C\times C
, converging in the natural topology of
X
towards some
(x_{\infty},y_{\infty})\in C\times C
. From Proposition
4
, there exists a family
(\pi^{*}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of optimal representing transport plans of
\widehat{D}_{p}(x_{n},y_{n})
, in the sense that
\pi^{*}_{n}\in\mathcal{P}_{(x_{n},y_{n})}
and
\widehat{D}_{p}(x_{n},y_{n})^{p}=\displaystyle\iint_{E\times E}d(z,z_{*})^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\pi^{*}_{n}(z,z_{*})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
\mathcal{P}(E\times E)
is compact in the weak-
∗
topology, the sequence
(\pi^{*}_{n})_{n}
converges in the weak-
∗
topology, up to extraction, towards some
\pi^{*}_{\infty}
. This by definition means that for all bounded and continuous
\varphi:E\times E\to\mathbb{R}
, we have

\iint_{E\times E}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\pi^{*}_{n}\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\iint_{E\times E}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\pi^{*}_{\infty}.

Since
d
is assumed to induce the natural topology on
E
, which is compact, the function
(z,z_{*})\mapsto d(z,z_{*})^{p}
is continuous and bounded on
E\times E
, from which we deduce that

(19)

\widehat{D}_{p}(x_{n},y_{n})^{p}\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\iint_{E\times E}d(z,z_{*})^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\pi^{*}_{\infty}(z,z_{*}).

Let us now consider some
f\in X^{*}
. Since
x_{n}\to x
and
y_{n}\to y
, we have by continuity of
f
that
f(x_{n})\to f(x)
and
f(y_{n})\to f(y)
. Moreover, as
E
is compact and
f
is continuous,
f_{|E}
is bounded on
E
. By weak-
∗
convergence, it then comes that

\iint_{E\times E}f(z)\,\mathrm{d}\pi^{*}_{n}(z,z_{*})\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\iint_{E\times E}f(z)\,\mathrm{d}\pi^{*}_{\infty}(z,z_{*}).

Since
\pi^{*}_{n}\in\mathcal{P}_{(x_{n},y_{n})}
the left-hand-side equals
f(x_{n})
. As
x_{n}\to x_{\infty}
and
f
is continuous, we conclude by uniqueness of the limit that

f(x_{\infty})=\iint_{E\times E}f(z)\,\mathrm{d}\pi^{*}_{\infty}(z,z_{*}).

Symmetrically, we also have
f(y_{\infty})=\iint_{E\times E}f(z_{*})\,\mathrm{d}\pi^{*}_{\infty}(z,z_{*})
, and since it holds for all
f\in X^{*}
, it comes that
\pi^{*}_{\infty}\in\mathcal{P}_{(x_{\infty},y_{\infty})}
.

We are now left with proving that
\pi^{*}_{\infty}
is an optimal plan for
\widehat{D}_{p}(x_{\infty},y_{\infty})
. We proceed by contradiction. Assume the existence of
\pi^{\prime}_{\infty}\in\mathcal{P}_{(x_{\infty},y_{\infty})}
and
\varepsilon>0
such that

(20)

\iint_{E\times E}d^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\pi^{*}_{\infty}=\iint_{E\times E}d^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\pi^{\prime}_{\infty}+\varepsilon.

From Lemma
4
in the appendix, there exists, up to extraction, a sequence
(\pi^{\prime}_{n})_{n}
with
\pi^{\prime}_{n}\in\mathcal{P}_{(x_{n},y_{n})}
such that
\pi^{\prime}_{n}\to\pi^{\prime}_{\infty}
in the weak-
∗
topology. The application
d^{p}
being continuous (and bounded) by assumption, and recalling (
19
), there is therefore a rank
n\in\mathbb{N}
for which we have both

\left|\iint_{E\times E}d^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\pi^{*}_{n}-\iint_{E\times E}d^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\pi^{*}_{\infty}\right|\leqslant\frac{\varepsilon}{3}\quad\text{ and }\quad\left|\iint_{E\times E}d^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\pi^{\prime}_{n}-\iint_{E\times E}d^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\pi^{\prime}_{\infty}\right|\leqslant\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.

This, along with (
20
), implies that
\iint_{E\times E}d^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\pi^{\prime}_{n}\leqslant\iint_{E\times E}d^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\pi^{*}_{n}-\frac{\varepsilon}{3}
, which contradicts the optimality of
\pi^{*}_{n}
. We have therefore shown that

\widehat{D}_{p}(x_{n},y_{n})\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\widehat{D}_{p}(x_{\infty},y_{\infty}),

and as
X
is a Banach space, it is sequential and we conclude that
\widehat{D}_{p}
is continuous.

Now, to prove the continuity of
D_{p}
, we use the newly obtained continuity of
\widehat{D}_{p}
along with Proposition
1
to get

0\leqslant D_{p}(x_{n},x_{\infty})\leqslant\widehat{D}_{p}(x_{n},x_{\infty})\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\widehat{D}_{p}(x_{\infty},x_{\infty})=0.

Similarly,
D_{p}(y_{n},y_{\infty})\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0
. Since
D_{p}
is subadditive, we have

D_{p}(x_{n},y_{n})\leqslant D_{p}(x_{n},x_{\infty})+D_{p}(x_{\infty},y_{\infty})+D_{p}(y_{\infty},y_{n}),

so that
\lim\sup_{n}D_{p}(x_{n},y_{n})\leqslant D_{p}(x_{\infty},y_{\infty})
. Conversely,

D_{p}(x_{\infty},y_{\infty})\leqslant D_{p}(x_{\infty},x_{n})+D_{p}(x_{n},y_{n})+D_{p}(y_{n},y_{\infty}),

so that
D_{p}(x_{\infty},y_{\infty})\leqslant\lim\inf_{n}D_{p}(x_{n},y_{n})
, ending the proof.
∎

Proposition 6

(Metric structure of

Consider the general Setting

Since by Proposition

\bullet
We start with the proof of

Lemma 1

Then for all

Remark 2

We can also prove that if

\widehat{D}_{p}(z,z_{*})\geqslant\|z-z_{*}\|^{\beta}

Proof.

The proof of (

for all

\forall x,y\in E,\qquad\|x-y\|=\underset{\|f\|_{X^{*}}=1}{\sup_{f\in X^{*}}}f(x-y)=\underset{\|f\|_{X^{*}}=1}{\sup_{f\in X^{*}}}f(x)-f(y).\qquad\phantom{x}

The monotonicity of the Lebesgue integral, combined with the fact that

\iint_{E\times E}\|x-y\|\,\mathrm{d}\pi(x,y)\geqslant\underset{\|f\|_{X^{*}}=1}{\sup_{f\in X^{*}}}\left(\iint_{E\times E}(f(x)-f(y))\,\mathrm{d}\pi(x,y)\right)=\underset{\|f\|_{X^{*}}=1}{\sup_{f\in X^{*}}}f(z)-f(z_{*}).

, we can consider any chain, with

, and be sure that for all

. Since

is subadditive, we get

\sumop\displaylimits_{n=1}^{N}\widehat{D}_{1}(z^{n},z^{n+1})\geqslant\sumop\displaylimits_{n=1}^{N}\|z^{n}-z^{n+1}\|\geqslant\|z-z_{*}\|.

Taking the infimum over all chains, we conclude that

.
∎

In this section, we apply the construction of the previous section to the quantum setting, to obtain a notion of

3.1.
Preliminary definitions

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a separable complex Hilbert space endowed with a scalar product
\braket{\cdot|\cdot}
, and
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
the set of bounded operators on
\mathcal{H}
.

Definition 6

(Density matrices and pure states)
.

We consider the set of
density matrices

S^{+}_{1}
to be the set of trace
1

\mathcal{H}

(26)

S_{1}^{+}:=\left\{\rho\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\;\;|\;\;\rho=\rho^{\dagger},\;\;\forall\psi\in\mathcal{H},\,\braket{\psi\,|\,\rho\,|\,\psi}\geqslant 0\;\text{ and }\;\mathrm{Tr}(\rho)=1\right\}.

The set of

(identified with the complex the projective space of
\mathcal{H}
) is the set of rank-one projectors over
\mathcal{H}
, i.e.

(27)

\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{H}}:=\left\{P_{\psi}:\phi\in\mathcal{H}\mapsto\braket{\psi|\phi}\;\psi\;\;|\;\;\psi\in\mathcal{H}\text{ and }\langle\psi|\psi\rangle=1\right\}.

The set
S^{1}_{+}
is a convex subset of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, whose extreme boundary is
\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{H}}
. For any
\rho\in S^{+}_{1}
, the spectral theorem ensures the existence of a family
\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
and an orthonormal basis
\{\psi_{n}\}_{n}
of
\mathcal{H}
such that
\sumop\displaylimits_{n}\lambda_{n}=1
and

\rho=\sumop\displaylimits_{n}\lambda_{n}\,P_{\psi_{n}}.

Then the measure
\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{n}\lambda_{n}\,\bm{\delta}_{P_{\psi_{n}}}
, with
\bm{\delta}_{P}
denoting the Dirac mass at
P\in\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{H}}
, is a probability measure over
\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{H}}

We first highlight that as
d
induces the same topology as
\|\cdot\|
on
E
, there is no ambiguity in the following notions of continuity. The convexity of
\mathcal{P}_{x}
and
\mathcal{P}_{(x,y)}
is rather straightforward, and as
E
is compact, so are
\mathcal{P}(E)
and
\mathcal{P}(E\times E)
(in their weak-
∗
topology), hence we only have to prove that
\mathcal{P}_{x}
and
\mathcal{P}_{(x,y)}
are closed in their respective weak-
∗
topology.

\bullet

Proof of the first part.
Let
x\in X
, consider a sequence
(\mu_{n})\in(\mathcal{P}_{x}){N}
converging weakly-
∗
towards some
\mu_{\infty}\in\mathcal{P}(E)
. Be definition, for every continuous real function
\varphi
on
E
(all continuous functions on
E
are bounded as
E
is compact), we have

\intop\nolimits_{E}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}\to\intop\nolimits_{E}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\infty}.

By definition, all functions
f
in the topological dual
X^{*}
are continuous on
E
, therefore

\intop\nolimits_{E}f_{|E}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}\to\intop\nolimits_{E}f_{|E}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\infty}.

Since all
\mu_{n}
represent
x
, the sequence
\left(\intop\nolimits_{E}f_{|E}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}\right)_{n}
is constant equal to
f(x)
and by uniqueness of the limit,

\intop\nolimits_{E}f_{|E}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\infty}=f(x).

As the above holds for every
f\in X^{*}
,
\mu_{\infty}
thus belongs to
\mathcal{P}_{x}
. Hence
\mathcal{P}_{x}
is closed.

\bullet

Proof of the second part.
The proof is almost identical to the first part. Take
x,y\in C
and a sequence
(\pi_{n})_{n}
of elements of
\mathcal{P}_{(x,y)}
converging weakly-
∗
towards some
\pi_{\infty}\in\mathcal{P}(E\times E)
. With the same arguments as previously, it comes that for all
f\in X^{*}
, we have both

\iint_{E\times E}\hskip-10.00002ptf(P)\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{n}(P,Q)\to\iint_{E\times E}\hskip-10.00002ptf(P)\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{\infty}(P,Q)\quad\text{and}\quad\iint_{E\times E}\hskip-10.00002ptf(Q)\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{n}(P,Q)\to\iint_{E\times E}\hskip-10.00002ptf(Q)\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{\infty}(P,Q).

The left-hand-side sequence is constant equal to
f(x)
while the right-hand-side sequence is constant equal to
f(y)
. By the same arguments as for the first part, we get that
\pi_{\infty}\in\mathcal{P}_{(x,y)}
, from which we conclude that
\mathcal{P}_{(x,y)}
is closed.
∎

Lemma 3

(Extreme boundary of the sets of representing probabilities)
.

Consider the Setting
1
and let
x\in C
. The set
\mathcal{P}_{x}
of representing probabilities of
x
is convex and compact. If there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, a family of measurable real functions
\{f_{i}\}_{1\leqslant i\leqslant N-1}
on
E
and real numbers
\{c_{i}\}_{1\leqslant i\leqslant N-1}
such that

\mathcal{P}_{x}=\left\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}(E)\;|\;\;\text{for }\,1\leqslant i\leqslant N-1,\;\;f_{i}\text{ is }\mu\text{-integrable and }\,\intop\nolimits_{E}f_{i}\,\mathrm{d}\mu=c_{i}\right\},

then the extreme points of
\mathcal{P}_{x}
are finitely supported and have at most
N
atoms.

Proof.

That
\mathcal{P}_{x}
is compact comes from it being a closed (from Lemma
2
) subset of the compact set
\mathcal{P}(E)
. The rest of the lemma is an immediate application of Winkler’s 1988
[
29
, Theorem 2.1 (b)]
or the more general 1962’s theorem of Dubins
[
14
]
.
∎

Lemma 4

(Existence of approximating representing couplings)
.

Consider the Setting
1
and further assume that that
\mathrm{Ri}(C)
, the relative interior of
C
(see Definition
5
), is nonempty. Let
(x_{\infty},y_{\infty})\in C\times C
,
\pi^{\prime}_{\infty}\in\mathcal{P}_{(x_{\infty},y_{\infty})}
and a sequence
(x_{n},y_{n})_{n}
of
C\times C
converging towards
(x_{\infty},y_{\infty})
. Then, up to extraction, there exists a sequence
(\pi^{\prime}_{n})_{n}
with
\pi^{\prime}_{n}\in\mathcal{P}_{(x_{n},y_{n})}
such that
\pi^{\prime}_{n}\to\pi^{\prime}_{\infty}
in the weak-
∗
topology.

Proof.

Let
z^{*}\in\mathrm{Ri}(C)
(which is nonempty). We define, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{+\infty\}

x^{*}_{k,n}:=\left(1-\frac{1}{k}\right)x_{n}+\frac{1}{k}z^{*},\quad y^{*}_{k,n}:=\left(1-\frac{1}{k}\right)y_{n}+\frac{1}{k}z^{*}.

By Choquet’s theorem, there exists
\eta\in\mathcal{P}_{z^{*}}
, and we define for all
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}

(42)

\pi^{*}_{k}:=\left(1-\frac{1}{k}\right)\pi_{\infty}+\frac{1}{k}\eta\otimes\eta.

From now on, we fix
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. Note that
\pi^{*}_{k}\in\mathcal{P}_{(x^{*}_{k,\infty},y^{*}_{k,\infty})}
and that
(x^{*}_{k,\infty},y^{*}_{k,\infty})
belongs to
\mathrm{Ri}(C)\times\mathrm{Ri}(C)
. Since both
x^{*}_{k,\infty}\in\mathrm{Ri}(C)
and
y^{*}_{k,\infty}\in\mathrm{Ri}(C)
, there exists
\varepsilon_{k}>0
such that

\mathrm{Aff}(C)\cap B_{\varepsilon_{k}}(x^{*}_{k,\infty})\subset C\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{Aff}(C)\cap B_{\varepsilon_{k}}(y^{*}_{k,\infty})\subset C,

where, as in Definition
5
,
\mathrm{Aff}(C)
is the affine hull of
C
and
B_{\varepsilon}(x)
the open ball centered at
x
and of radius
\varepsilon
. Let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. Since
\|x^{*}_{k,n}-x^{*}_{k,\infty}\|,\|y^{*}_{k,n}-y^{*}_{k,\infty}\|\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, there exists
m_{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
such that

\|x^{*}_{k,m_{\ell}}-x^{*}_{k,\infty}\|\leqslant\frac{\varepsilon_{k}}{1+\ell}\quad\text{ and }\quad\|y^{*}_{k,m_{\ell}}-y^{*}_{k,\infty}\|\leqslant\frac{\varepsilon_{k}}{1+\ell}.

We set

(43)

x^{\prime}_{k,\ell}:=x^{*}_{k,\infty}+\ell\left(x^{*}_{k,m_{\ell}}-x^{*}_{k,\infty}\right)\quad\text{ and }\quad y^{\prime}_{k,\ell}:=y^{*}_{k,\infty}+\ell\left(y^{*}_{k,m_{\ell}}-y^{*}_{k,\infty}\right).

We have
x^{\prime}_{k,\ell},y^{\prime}_{k,\ell}\in\mathrm{Aff}(C)
and
(x^{\prime}_{k,\ell},y^{\prime}_{k,\ell})\in B_{\varepsilon_{k}}(x^{*}_{k,\infty})\times B_{\varepsilon_{k}}(y^{*}_{k,\infty})
, therefore
x^{\prime}_{k,\ell},y^{\prime}_{k,\ell}\in C
. By Choquet’s theorem, there exists a representing measure
\nu_{k,\ell}
of
x^{\prime}_{k,\ell}
and a representing measure
\lambda_{k,\ell}
of
y^{\prime}_{k,\ell}
. We then define

(44)

\pi^{\prime}_{k,\ell}:=\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell}\right)\pi^{*}_{k}+\frac{1}{\ell}\;\nu_{k,\ell}\otimes\lambda_{k,\ell}.

By linearity, it is easily verified that
\pi^{\prime}_{k,\ell}\in\mathcal{P}_{(x^{*}_{k,m_{\ell}},y^{*}_{k,m_{\ell}})}
. Let us now fix
\ell\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. Since
\mathcal{P}(E\times E)
is compact in the weak-
∗
topology, there exists
\pi^{\prime}_{\infty,\ell}\in\mathcal{P}(E\times E)
such that, up to extraction, we have

(45)

\pi^{\prime}_{k,\ell}\xrightarrow[k\to\infty]{w^{*}}\pi^{\prime}_{\infty,\ell}.

Since
(x^{*}_{k,m_{\ell}},y^{*}_{k,m_{\ell}})
converges towards
(x_{m_{\ell}},y_{m_{\ell}})
as
k\to\infty
, and all elements of the topological dual of
X
are by essence continuous, we deduce that
\pi^{\prime}_{\infty,\ell}\in\mathcal{P}_{(x_{m_{\ell}},y_{m_{\ell}})}
(for more details, see the beginning of the proof of Proposition
5
). We are now left with proving that
(\pi^{\prime}_{\infty,\ell})_{\ell}
converges weakly-
∗
towards
\pi_{\infty}
. Let
\varphi
be a bounded continuous function on
E\times E
, we have for all
k,\ell\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
that

(46)

\left|\iint_{E\times E}\hskip-10.00002pt\varphi\,\mathrm{d}(\pi^{\prime}_{\infty,\ell}-\pi_{\infty})\right|\leqslant\left|\iint_{E\times E}\hskip-10.00002pt\varphi\,\mathrm{d}(\pi^{\prime}_{\infty,\ell}-\pi^{\prime}_{k,\ell})\right|+\left|\iint_{E\times E}\hskip-10.00002pt\varphi\,\mathrm{d}(\pi^{\prime}_{k,\ell}-\pi^{*}_{k})\right|+\left|\iint_{E\times E}\hskip-10.00002pt\varphi\,\mathrm{d}(\pi^{*}_{k}-\pi_{\infty})\right|.

Remark that (
44
) implies that for all
k,\ell\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,

\left|\iint_{E\times E}\hskip-10.00002pt\varphi\,\mathrm{d}(\pi^{\prime}_{k,\ell}-\pi^{*}_{k})\right|\leqslant\frac{2\|\varphi\|_{\infty}}{\ell},

so that (
46
) becomes

\left|\iint_{E\times E}\hskip-10.00002pt\varphi\,\mathrm{d}(\pi^{\prime}_{\infty,\ell}-\pi_{\infty})\right|\leqslant\frac{2\|\varphi\|_{\infty}}{\ell}+\left|\iint_{E\times E}\hskip-10.00002pt\varphi\,\mathrm{d}(\pi^{\prime}_{\infty,\ell}-\pi^{\prime}_{k,\ell})\right|+\left|\iint_{E\times E}\hskip-10.00002pt\varphi\,\mathrm{d}(\pi^{*}_{k}-\pi_{\infty})\right|.

Letting
\ell
fixed and letting
k\to\infty
, we get, thanks to (
42
) and (
45
),

\left|\iint_{E\times E}\hskip-10.00002pt\varphi\,\mathrm{d}(\pi^{\prime}_{\infty,\ell}-\pi_{\infty})\right|\leqslant\frac{2\|\varphi\|_{\infty}}{\ell}.

That the above holds for all bounded continuous test function ensures the weak-
∗
convergence of
\pi^{\prime}_{\infty,\ell}
towards
\pi_{\infty}
, ending the proof.
∎

Proof of Proposition

Proof of Proposition
12
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbf{P}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})})
represent
R
and
\pi
a coupling between
\mu
and
\nu_{f}
. We have

\displaystyle\iint_{E_{1}\times E_{2}}c_{\lambda,\hbar}(e_{1},e_{2})\,\mathrm{d}\pi(e_{1},e_{2})

\displaystyle=\iint_{\mathbf{P}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\times(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\langle\psi\;|\;\hat{c}_{\lambda,\hbar}(x,\xi)\;|\;\psi\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\mathrm{d}\pi(P_{\psi},(x,\xi))

\displaystyle=\iint_{\mathbf{P}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\times(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\mathrm{Tr}\left(\hat{c}_{\lambda,\hbar}(x,\xi)\;P_{\psi}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\pi(P_{\psi},(x,\xi)).

By the disintegration theorem, since
\nu_{f}
is the second marginal of
\pi
, there exists a family of probability measures over
\mathbf{P}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
indexed by
(x,\xi)
and denoted
\pi(\cdot|x,\xi)
, such that

\mathrm{d}\pi(P_{\psi},(x,\xi))=\mathrm{d}\pi(P_{\psi}|x,\xi)\;f(x,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

Therefore the last integral become

\iint_{\mathbf{P}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\times(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\mathrm{Tr}\left(\hat{c}_{\lambda,\hbar}(x,\xi)\;P_{\psi}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\pi(P_{\psi}|x,\xi)\,f(x,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\xi,

that is

\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{Tr}\left(\hat{c}_{\lambda,\hbar}(x,\xi)\;Q(x,\xi)\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\xi,

with

Q(x,\xi):=f(x,\xi)\intop\nolimits_{\mathbf{P}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}P_{\psi}\;\mathrm{d}\pi(P_{\psi}|x,\xi).

As
\pi(\cdot|x,\xi)
is a probability measure over
\mathbf{P}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
, and since the first marginal of
\pi
is
\mu
representing
R
,
Q
is a valid coupling between
R
and
f
. In turn, we have

\iint_{E_{1}\times E_{2}}c_{\lambda,\hbar}(e_{1},e_{2})\,\mathrm{d}\pi(e_{1},e_{2})\geqslant\mathrm{GP}_{\lambda,\hbar}(\rho,f)^{2},

and taking the infimum over all coupling measures
\pi

\mu

\inf_{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{R}}\;K_{c_{\lambda,\hbar}}(\mu,\nu_{f})\geqslant\mathrm{GP}_{\lambda,\hbar}(\rho,f)^{2}.

Conversely, consider some coupling
Q
between
R
and
f
. For any
(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, since
Q(x,\xi)
is a semi-definite self-adjoint operator over

with finite trace
f(x,\xi)
, there exists (by the spectral theorem) a Radon probability measure
\mathrm{d}\pi(\cdot|x,\xi)
over
\mathbf{P}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

Q(x,\xi)=f(x,\xi)\intop\nolimits_{\mathbf{P}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}P_{\psi}\;\mathrm{d}\pi(P_{\psi}|x,\xi).

We let

\mathrm{d}\pi(P_{\psi},(x,\xi)):=\mathrm{d}\pi(P_{\psi}|x,\xi)\,f(x,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\xi,\quad\mathrm{d}\mu(P_{\psi}):=\iint_{(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{d}\pi(P_{\psi},(x,\xi)).

Then
\mu

\mathbf{P}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
,
\pi
is a coupling between
\mu
and

\mu

R
. Then, as again

\iint_{E_{1}\times E_{2}}c_{\lambda,\hbar}(e_{1},e_{2})\,\mathrm{d}\pi(e_{1},e_{2})=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{Tr}\left(\hat{c}_{\lambda,\hbar}(x,\xi)\;Q(x,\xi)\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\xi,

\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{Tr}\left(\hat{c}_{\lambda,\hbar}(x,\xi)\;Q(x,\xi)\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\xi\geqslant\inf_{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{R}}\;K_{c_{\lambda,\hbar}}(\mu,\nu_{f}).

As this holds for any coupling

, taking the infimum over

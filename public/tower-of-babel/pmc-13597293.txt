Protocol for DRIFT-EM: Direct wafer retrieval of ultrathin serial sections for volume electron microscopy
Authors: Joseph V Gogola, Avryn Leo Justin, Gregg Wildenberg
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryLarge-volume serial electron microscopy enables reconstructions of neural circuits at synaptic resolution but depends on reliable collection of large libraries of ultrathin sections. Here, we present a low-cost protocol for direct wafer-based section collection. We describe steps for modifying a commercial diamond knife, constructing thermoplastic barriers, mounting and tuning static ionizers for section clearing, and automated region of interest mapping of collected sections.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Medina and Gogola et al.1Subject areas: High Throughput Screening, Microscopy, Neuroscience

Before you begin

The protocol below describes the specific steps for collecting ultrathin tissue sections onto 100 mm silicon wafers for scanning electron microscopy (SEM). These steps assume tissue has been chemically fixed, heavy-metal stained, dehydrated, and embedded in epoxy resin (Epon or LX-112) according to standard connectomics sample preparation protocols2 though we also provide a brief protocol for this preparation. The DRIFT-EM system consists of three components fabricated or assembled in advance: (1) a modified diamond knife with a 3D-printed wafer attachment, (2) silicon wafers fitted with hydrophobic thermoplastic barriers, and (3) static ionizers mounted for positioning near the ultramicrotome. All three must be prepared before proceeding to the step-by-step protocol, and are described in detail below.

Innovation

DRIFT-EM advances direct wafer-based ultrathin section collection for vEM by replacing expensive magnetic or pneumatic clearing systems with inexpensive, off-the-shelf static ionizers. Key innovations include: (1) a 3D-printed wafer attachment that fits a standard commercial diamond knife and holds a 100 mm silicon wafer level during sectioning; (2) hydrophobic thermoplastic (ethylene-vinyl acetate; EVA) or 3D-printed acrylonitrile styrene acrylate (ASA) barriers that physically confine and do not stick to floating sections over the wafer surface, eliminating the need for magnetic resin impregnation; and (3) static ionizers that generate a gentle, laminar electrohydrodynamic (EHD) flow that clears newly cut sections away from the knife edge without disturbing the water meniscus. Together these elements enable continuous collection of 2,000–3,000 sections at 40 nm thickness for approximately $1,000–2,000 USD in total setup cost - two orders of magnitude less than commercial tape-based systems3,4,5 - while achieving section packing densities comparable to state-of-the-art magnetic approaches.6,7

Preparation 1: Fabricate and assemble the modified knife boat

Timing: ∼1–2 days (machining + glue cure)

In this preparation, a commercial diamond knife is machined to accept the custom 3D-printed wafer attachment, and the attachment is permanently bonded to the knife boat. All CAD files (STL) for 3D-printed components are available at: https://github.com/gwildenberg/DRIFT-EM-CAD-files/tree/main.

Preparation 2: Prepare plasma-cleaned silicon wafers with thermoplastic barriers

Timing: ∼60 min

In this preparation, 100 mm silicon wafers are made hydrophilic by O2 plasma treatment, then fitted with hydrophobic thermoplastic barriers that will confine sections over the wafer surface during sectioning.

Store cleaned wafers in antistatic bags (Fisher Scientific, Cat. No. NC2388669) until used to prevent static buildup that renders them hydrophobic.

CRITICAL: Place the O2-cleaned wafer into water within 30–60 min of plasma treatment if not storing in antistatic bag. Static charge can build up on the wafer surface and reverse hydrophilicity. Glow-discharge systems provide equivalent results if a plasma etcher is unavailable. Before using the wafer to collect sections, test its hydrophilicity by depositing a drop of water on it. If the water spreads out upon impact, it’s still hydrophilic and ready to use. If the drop holds its surface tension (e.g., remains as a droplet on the silicon), then you will need to plasma clean the silicon.

Note: We have not extensively tested how long an O2-cleaned wafer will maintain its hydrophilicity when stored in an antistatic bag, as we routinely use them immediately. Using the water drop test described above is the best way to ensure the wafer is ready to use. However, we have found that storing the O2-cleaned wafer in water, in the knife boat, will maintain hydrophilicity for over a week.

Methods Video S2. Making the barrier

Once the wafer attachment is leveled, use a hot glue fun to seal the edge, ensuring the glue does not trail into the middle of the boat. The best method for building the barrier is to pump out the glue and move the gun towards the outside of the attachment.

Preparation 3: Mount and adjust ionizers

Timing: ∼10–15 min (initial setup; negligible thereafter)

Static ionizers generate the laminar EHD flow that clears sections away from the knife edge. Initial placement requires brief empirical tuning but need not be adjusted between runs once optimized.

8.This original design utilized 3D-printed articulated arm mounts to the ultramicrotome turret. However, commercially available articulating arm attached to an alligator clamp (see key resources table) works well and allows for precise positioning.

This original design utilized 3D-printed articulated arm mounts to the ultramicrotome turret. However, commercially available articulating arm attached to an alligator clamp (see key resources table) works well and allows for precise positioning.

9.Position the two ionizers approximately 2 to 3 cm above the water surface, on either side of the knife, and directed toward the knife edge.a.Ionizer positioning and water flow can be observed by placing a few small Styrofoam beads (manually scraped off a standard Conical tube rack) on the water and then removed after the desired flow is achieved.b.A single ionizer positioned near the ultramicrotome chuck and pointed slightly away from the ultramicrotome readily moves sections away from the knife even after >1000 sections have been cut (Figure 3 and Methods Video S3). This section covers the preparation of epoxy-embedded tissue blocks from mouse brain. If tissue blocks are already available from prior sample preparation, proceed to the next major step.

Expected outcomes

A successful DRIFT-EM collection run with a 1 mm2 blockface at 40 nm section thickness should yield approximately 2,000–3,000 sections per 100 mm silicon wafer. Sections should be evenly distributed across the central ∼90 mm diameter collection zone (corresponding to approximately 6,362 mm2), with a mean distance of ∼5.3 ± 0.14 mm from the wafer edge. Individual sections should lie flat, unwrinkled, and well-separated without stacking on top of each other.

The automated ROI pipeline should detect bounding boxes for approximately 91% of sections from a single optical overview image. After manual correction (∼10 min), the complete ordered ROI set is exported as a Zeiss ATLAS-compatible.proj file, enabling automated SEM imaging without further user intervention until acquisition is complete.

At standard mapping resolution (55 nm/px lateral, 40 nm axial), the protocol produces 3D volumes spanning up to ∼200 × 200 × 74 μm (∼1,840 sections) with quality sufficient for automated neural circuit reconstruction. At higher resolution (6 nm/px), smaller volumes (e.g., 48 × 54 × 13 μm) resolve individual synapses, with pre-synaptic boutons, vesicle clusters, and post-synaptic densities clearly visible. Section morphology is equivalent to standard tape-based vEM; the ionizer introduces no observable artifacts. See Figures 4 and 5 of Medina and Gogola, et al.1 for representative outcomes.

Quantification and statistical analysis

No statistical procedures are required for routine use of this protocol. Section collection yield (number of sections per wafer and collection zone geometry) can be quantified from the optical overview image and the ROI detection output. In our validation experiments (n = 3 independent collection runs), the mean distance between sections and the wafer edge was 5.3 ± 0.14 mm (mean ± SEM), corresponding to a collection zone diameter of approximately 90 mm. Section packing density can be compared across conditions by measuring the number of sections detected per unit wafer area from the .magc output file.

Limitations

Several practical considerations should be noted for routine use. First, optimal ionizer placement requires brief empirical tuning. While the clearing effect operates over a broad range of positions and voltages, initial users should expect to spend time finding an effective configuration for their specific microtome and ionizer model. Second, constructing the thermoplastic EVA barrier requires a hot-glue gun and moderate manual dexterity; minor inconsistencies in barrier height or shape can alter water dynamics and section distribution. The 3D-printed ASA barrier alternative reduces this variability. In principle, other plastic barriers could be implemented (e.g., using PDMS) so long as they are verified that that do not stick to sections. Third, knife modification requires access to a machine shop with milling capability; however, such facilities are standard at most university campuses. Fourth, the automated ROI pipeline currently exports Zeiss ATLAS-compatible .proj files only; adaptation for other SEM acquisition platforms (e.g., FEI/Thermo Fisher, JEOL) requires minor code modification, though this remains untested. Fifth, the maximum validated collection run is ∼3,000 sections on a single 100 mm wafer; wafer exchange to extend collection across multiple wafers has been tested in DRIFT-EM. The principal challenge to continuing to section on a second wafer is restarting the sectioning where the first sections could be partial sections due to deformations in the tissue sample, but this is independent of the core DRIFT-EM technology and a challenge shared across all direct pickup methods.

•Ensure the wafer was O2 plasma cleaned within 30–60 min prior to use or stored in an antistatic bag. A hydrophobic wafer surface promotes wrinkling as the water recedes.

•For the ASA ring, check that it sits flush on the wafer surface. If gaps exist between the ring and silicon, apply a thin bead of EVA along the inner perimeter (hybrid approach).

Methods Video S1. Leveling the wafer attachment

Using the miniature bubble level, adjust the attachment first in the East-West direction (with the knife blade being the North direction), ensuring complete leveling. Then, adjust in the North-South direction, leaving the bubble slightly skewed towards the South, so the water is slightly away from the knife.

Methods Video S2. Making the barrier

Once the wafer attachment is leveled, use a hot glue fun to seal the edge, ensuring the glue does not trail into the middle of the boat. The best method for building the barrier is to pump out the glue and move the gun towards the outside of the attachment.

Methods Video S3. DRIFT-EM collection

Movie showing DRIFT-EM with ionizer placement as outlined in Figure 2 and after ∼1500 sections were collected. Ionizer effectively “pushes” sections (small silver objects) away from the knife edge as they accumulate towards the back of the boat.

Methods Video S4. Water withdrawal

An EVA hot glue plus is made to fit in the knife-edge gap to seal it once collection is finished. Water is gently removed from the back, non-knife, side of the wafer boat. A correctly leveled attachment (Preparation Step 4; Methods Video S1) should promote section movement away from the knife during water removal. Slow down water removal or turn on the ionizer if you notice sections gathering toward the plug. Monitor the plug to ensure sections do not fall off the wafer.

Methods Video S5. Drying the wafer

Place the wafer on the hot plate and allow the remaining water to dry. This could take anywhere from 10–60 minutes. If more time is needed, cover the wafer (e.g., with an inverted petri dish) to protect from dust.

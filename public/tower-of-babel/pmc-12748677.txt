Dimensionality-reduced multi-objective framework for resilient power systems with electric vehicles and hydrogen-based energy storage
Authors: Mohamed M Refaat, Mujahed Al-Dhaifallah, Moustafa Magdi Ismail, Mahmoud S AbouOmar, Sami El Ferik
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractModern power systems are becoming increasingly complex and vulnerable, raising concerns about their ability to withstand disruptive events. As a result, resilience has become a key consideration in system planning and operation. This paper proposes a resilience-oriented framework that coordinates distributed energy resources (DERs) with hydrogen-based energy storage (HESS). The framework also integrates planning for transmission lines, electric vehicles (EVs), and flexible AC transmission systems (FACTS) to enhance overall system performance. The problem is formulated as a multi-objective optimization model. One objective minimizes the cost of the resilience strategy, while the other maximizes the contribution of DERs to system resilience. The formulation includes a large number of decision variables, making it computationally challenging. To address this, two strategies are applied to reduce dimensionality: (i) a power dispatch and load-shedding reduction strategy, and (ii) a transmission line and FACTS-based strategy. To solve the problem, multi-objective versions of five recent metaheuristic algorithms are employed. The effectiveness of the proposed reduced-scale model in improving these algorithms is evaluated on the IEEE 9-bus, IEEE 30-bus, and 53-bus systems. Simulation results showed that the proposed strategies significantly improved both robustness and solution quality. For the IEEE 9-bus system, robustness increased by 20–30% and solution quality by 6–21.75%. In the IEEE 30-bus system, robustness improved by up to 25%, with solution quality gains of 13.43–38.85%. For the 53-bus network, robustness increased by 10–25%, and solution quality improved by 3–12.45%. The results demonstrated the advantages of HESS over conventional battery storage in enhancing system resilience. For the IEEE 9-bus system, HESS achieved a reduction in planning cost of 29.7–55.2%, while for the IEEE 30-bus system, the reduction ranged from 36.3 to 57.66%.Keywords: Resiliency, Distributed energy resources, Hydrogen–based energy storage, Reduced-scale model, OptimizationSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Motivation

Modern power systems are becoming more complex with the integration of distributed energy resources (DERs). This complexity increases the vulnerability of power systems to high-impact, low-probability events such as coordinated physical–cyber-attacks1. Such attacks disrupt critical infrastructure, trigger large-scale blackouts, and cause severe economic and societal impacts. Therefore, enhancing resilience has become a key priority for power system development.

Power system expansion planning (PSEP) is a long-term optimization process that directs investments in generation, transmission, and distribution infrastructure to ensure reliable, economic, and sustainable electricity supply. Modern PSEP must not only meet growing demand and integrate renewables but also address cyber-physical risks. This requires embedding resilience directly into the planning framework2,3. Planning models commonly include DERs, flexible AC transmission systems (FACTS), energy storage systems (ESSs), and transmission lines (TL).

ESSs play a key role in enhancing system resilience. They improve grid flexibility, facilitate renewable energy integration, defer the need for additional infrastructure, and support rapid recovery following disruptions4,5. Various ESS technologies, such as lithium-ion (Li-ion), hydrogen-based energy storage (HESS), vanadium redox flow (VR), sodium–nickel chloride (NaNiCl2), and nickel–cadmium (NiCd) batteries, differ in efficiency, cycle life, and depth of discharge, as well as in operational behavior and cost structures6. Selecting the appropriate technology is therefore a key factor in long-term planning and investment decisions.

Resilience-oriented PSEP models are typically formulated as mixed-integer nonlinear programming (MINLP) problems. These models become high-dimensional when they include the planning models of multiple components and contingencies7,8. Mathematical solvers work for small, linearized problems but lack scalability. Metaheuristic algorithms can handle complexity but may need multiple runs to find robust solutions9. Therefore, the selection of solvers is crucial to deal with resilience-focused planning models.

The literature reveals a significant research gap in the integration of cyber–physical threats within expansion planning models. This gap is further intensified by the limited understanding of solver performance, particularly regarding the effectiveness and scalability of different optimization methods when applied to large-scale, integrated MINLP problems.

Summary of some recent studies on power system planning.

Quevedo et al.12 developed a stochastic MILP framework for distribution system expansion planning (DSEP) that simultaneously optimized network assets, EV charging stations (EVCSs), and wind-based distributed generation, demonstrating cost reductions through coordinated integration. Wu et al.13 addressed low-carbon expansion of integrated electricity and natural gas systems (IEGS) with EV charging stations, using a carbon-cap-constrained multi-objective optimization framework to allocate emission limits and reduce system-wide carbon output. Zare et al.14 proposed a chance-constrained MILP model for multi-energy network expansion with EV charging stations and DERs, ensuring operational limits are met under uncertainty and reducing operational costs while maintaining voltage stability. Veisi et al.15 developed a bi-level framework for planning multi-energy EV-hydrogen fuelling stations (HFSs), coordinating renewable generation, battery storage, EV charging stations, and HFSs to achieve cost-efficient and technically robust siting and sizing.

Many planning frameworks also accounted for uncertainties related to renewable generation, load variability, and EV behavior through stochastic and robust optimization methods. In addition, several studies incorporated resilience indicators, including natural disaster impacts and N-1 contingency security, to strengthen the reliability of the proposed planning solutions. Hamidpour et al.16 proposed a multi-objective framework for resilient generation and transmission expansion planning (GTEP) under natural disasters, improving resilience and cost-effectiveness. De Lima et al.17 addressed DSEP using a mixed-integer linear programming (MILP) model combined with two-stage stochastic programming to optimize multi-period investments under uncertainty across critical network components. Alharbi et al.18 presented a stochastic multi-objective approach for coordinated scheduling of EV behaviour, distributed generators (DGs), and ESSs under uncertainty, reducing operational costs and power losses while maintaining voltage stability.

Kabirifar et al.19 proposed a multi-agent bi-level optimization framework for reliability-PSEP in active distribution networks (ADNs), coordinating the distribution network operator, DG owners, and load aggregators. The upper level optimized network expansion considering reliability metrics, while the lower level optimizes DG and LA operations via a local energy market, with uncertainties managed using stochastic adaptive robust optimization (SARO). Results showed effective coordination, yielding a reliable plan that minimizes expected costs while meeting operational constraints. Ndayishimiye et al.20 proposed a cost-optimal coordinated GTEP model that aligns generation additions with demand growth and grid reinforcement, showing that targeted transmission upgrades and strategic project timing improve system utilization and efficiency in developing power sectors.

To address these complex planning problems, several solution techniques were developed. Methods, such as MILP, were used for tractable and linearized formulations, while non-convex and high-dimensional models were typically handled using meta-heuristic or hybrid optimization algorithms. Pal et al.21 proposed a multi-layer approach for jointly planning EV fast-charging stations and DSEP, integrating traffic network effects, multistage distribution upgrades, and uncertainties in EV behavior, load, and renewable generation, solved via a hybrid of meta-heuristics, integer linear programming (ILP), and Dijkstra’s algorithm. Almalaq et al.22 extended planning to high penetration of intermittent RESs, incorporating TEP, ESSs, and fault current limiters, solved with a hybrid meta-heuristic algorithm (INFO-SCA) to enhance hosting capacity under reliability constraints. Duan et al.23 developed a stochastic multi-objective framework for integrating intelligent electric parking lots (EPLs), simultaneously minimizing power losses, grid purchases, unsupplied energy, voltage deviations, and Vehicle-to-Grid (V2G) operation costs, solved with an improved fire hawks optimization (IFHO) algorithm. Zhao et al.24 applied a support vector machine–particle swarm optimization (SVM–PSO) approach for efficient power facility relocation planning, reducing computational complexity while maintaining high accuracy. Ba-swaimi et al.25 proposed a multi-objective model integrating DGs, ESSs, network reconfiguration (NR), and demand response programs (DRPs), enhancing system technical performance and renewable hosting capacity using NSGA-II and multi-objective PSO.

Nugraha et al.26 developed a hybrid Genetic Algorithm–Modified Salp Swarm Algorithm (HGAMSSA) to optimize the placement and sizing of EVCs in DNs. The multi-objective approach minimizes power losses, improves voltage regulation, and enhances network capacity utilization. Tests on a standard system demonstrate that HGAMSSA effectively reduces losses, improves voltage profiles, and maintains operational capacity. Dharavat et al.27 introduced a two-stage framework employing the voltage stability index (VSI) for strategic placement of DGs and EV charging stations, with the political optimization algorithm (POA) used for optimal sizing.

Research gap and contributions

Recent studies have increasingly focused on hybridization frameworks that combine different meta-heuristics, or meta-heuristics with mathematical models, to enhance the exploration and exploitation phases of optimization. While such hybrid approaches can improve algorithmic performance, they often suffer from robustness issues. Other research efforts have attempted to reduce problem dimensionality by applying decision-making strategies to limit the number of manipulated variables. However, the dimensionality of the planning problem remains large, which poses significant computational challenges.

In addition, the potential role of DERs and ESSs, particularly HESS, in strengthening system resilience has not been sufficiently explored in the literature. Existing models are often highly complex and computationally challenging, highlighting the need for integrated, attack-aware operational strategies that combine DERs, EVs, HESS, TLs, FACTS, and robust optimization methods.

This paper addresses these gaps by proposing a resilience-oriented planning framework that integrates DERs and HESS, employs reduced-dimensional modelling to simplify the optimization process, and evaluates recent metaheuristic algorithms (MHAs) for both reduced- and full-scale problems. Proposed planning strategy

The proposed planning strategy enhances power system resilience by integrating HESS, particularly to mitigate the impacts of intentional PAs. The model co-optimizes the placement of TLs, FACTS devices, wind turbines, and ESSs to sustain system operation under severe contingency scenarios. ESS units are strategically deployed to function primarily as backup during contingencies.

Incorporating these components into a comprehensive planning framework substantially increases the problem’s dimensionality, which can limit the efficiency of optimization algorithms. To address this challenge, the study introduces a reduced-dimension decision-making approach aimed at lowering computational complexity while preserving solution quality.

As illustrated in Fig. 1, the proposed strategy is structured into three core components: (1) data collection, (2) uncertainty handling, and (3) the proposed optimization-based planning framework.

Structure of the proposed strategy.

Data collection

The planning process begins by systematically gathering all necessary input data. This includes meteorological data, such as wind speed profiles, and demand data, such as daily average load and EV charging consumption. The required data also encompass operational constraints, including the dispatch limits of conventional generators. Furthermore, the process identifies candidate locations and capacities for new infrastructure, including transmission lines, TCSCs, and energy storage systems.

Uncertainty handling

This stage is carried out in two steps:

Step 1. Hourly data generation.

Annual datasets for load and EV charging demand are first generated. Since only daily averages are typically available, a Monte Carlo simulation is used to produce 8,760 hourly values for both load and EV demand. This approach provides a more realistic representation of the system’s time-varying demand throughout the year.

Step 2. Identification of critical days

From the synthesized annual data, two representative worst-case days are identified. These days are selected based on wind speed, load demand, and EV charging demand, using the maximum daily mismatch between energy demand and DER output as the selection criterion. Conclusion

This paper presented a resilience-oriented multi-objective planning model integrating wind energy, ESS, EVs, and TCSC devices. The model included a large number of decision variables, making it inherently complex. To simplify the problem, two reduction strategies were applied: a power dispatch and load-shedding strategy, and a TL-FACTS-based strategy. To evaluate their effectiveness, multi-objective versions of recent metaheuristic algorithms, MSFOA, MTHRO, MMPA, MGOA, and MMGO, were developed and tested on both full-scale and reduced-scale models. Simulations were conducted on the IEEE 9-bus, IEEE 30-bus, and 53-bus systems.

The analysis showed that algorithm robustness improved by approximately 20–30% for the IEEE 9-bus system, with solution quality increasing by 6–21.75%. For the IEEE 30-bus system, robustness improved by up to 25% for most algorithms, while solution quality increased by 13.43–38.85%. In the 53-bus network, robustness improved by 10–25%, and solution quality increased by 3–12.45%. Among all algorithms, MGOA showed the best performance in both reduced-scale and full-scale models for the IEEE 9-bus and IEEE 30-bus systems. MMGO exhibited the best performance on the 53-bus system.

The analysis also highlighted the economic and operational benefits of HESS compared to NiCd, Li-ion, VR, and NaNiCl2. Total planning costs were reduced by approximately 29.7–55.2% for the IEEE 9-bus system and 36.3–57.66% for the IEEE 30-bus system. The results also emphasized the essential role of ESS in mitigating contingencies, especially during periods of low wind availability, as shown on the first worst-case day.

Future work will extend the proposed framework to larger systems with high renewable energy penetration. The integration of emerging technologies, such as vehicle-to-grid systems and advanced demand response programs, will also be considered. Hybrid optimization approaches combining metaheuristics with machine learning will be explored for real-time resilience assessment. Additionally, the framework will be expanded to include high-impact, low-probability events, such as cyber-attacks, extreme weather, and natural disasters. This will enable a more robust evaluation of system performance under diverse threat scenarios.

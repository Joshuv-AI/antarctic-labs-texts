NDUFA4L2 is a novel biomarker for colorectal cancer through bioinformatics analysis
Authors: Yuning Lin, Hongyan Xie, Wenzhen Zhao, Ying Li, Zhongying Zhang
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Colorectal cancer (CRC) is a major cause of cancer-related deaths worldwide. NDUFAL42 is an important mitochondrial respiratory chain subunit that plays a critical role in cellular energy metabolism. However, the role of NDUFA4L2 in CRC remains unclear. Therefore, we used the data obtained from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database to prove the relationship between NDUFA4L2 and CRC. The expression levels of NDUFA4L2 in CRC tissues were analyzed by immunohistochemical staining of NDUFA4L2 from the HPA database. Wilcoxon rank sum test, Chi-square test, Fisher exact test and logistic regression were used to evaluate relationships between clinical-pathologic features and NDUFA4L2 expression. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were used to describe binary classifier value of NDUFA4L2 using area under curve (AUC) score. Kaplan–Meier method and Cox regression analysis were used to evaluate factors contributing to prognosis. Gene oncology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis were used to predict the function of differentially expressed genes associated with NDUFA4L2. Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was used to predict canonical pathways associated with NDUFA4L2.Immune infiltration analysis was performed to identify the significantly involved functions of NDUFA4L2. Protein–protein interaction (PPI) networks were established and 20 hub genes identified with Cytoscape software. Increased NDUFA4L2 expression in CRC was associated with T stage (P = .019), N stage (P < .001), Pathologic stage (P = .020), Residual tumor (P = .023), Perineural invasion (P = .039), Lymphatic invasion (P = .007), Histological type(P < .001), PFI event (P = .007) and DSS event (P = .004).ROC curve suggested the significant diagnostic and prognostic ability of NDUFA4L2 (AUC = 0.878). High NDUFA4L2 expression predicted a poorer Overall-survival (P = .021), poorer progression-free interval (P = .001), and poorer Disease Specific Survival (P = .002). GO, KEGG, GSEA and immune infiltration analysis showed that NDUFA4L2 expression was correlated with regulating the function of DNA and some types of immune infiltrating cells. NDUFA4L2 expression was significantly correlated with poor survival and immune infiltrations in CRC, and it may be a promising prognostic biomarker in CRC.

1. Introduction

Colorectal cancer (CRC) consists of 2 main subtypes: colon adenocarcinoma (COAD) and rectal adenocarcinoma (READ). Based on pathological categorization, COAD accounts for about 80%-90% of all CRC cases.[1] Colorectal cancer (CRC) is ranked third in terms of its global incidence, accounting for 10.2% of all cancer cases worldwide. Furthermore, it stands as the second leading cause of cancer-related deaths, contributing to 9.2% of all cancer-related fatalities on a global scale. While there has been a consistent and gradual decrease in CRC occurrence and mortality rates over the past few decades worldwide, it continues to be the most prevalent gastrointestinal malignancy.[2,3]The prognosis of advanced colorectal cancer is poor, and its pathogenesis is closely related to lifestyle, genetics, and colorectal adenoma.[4]While the utilization of chemotherapy and surgical resection for malignant colorectal cancer has been on the rise, there hasn’t been a substantial enhancement in their effectiveness. Approximately half of CRC cases experience recurrence, and within a span of 5 years, many patients succumb to the disease.[5,6]Hence, there is a need to pinpoint novel diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets, alongside an exploration of potential molecular mechanisms underlying CRC. Effective diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers should be strongly associated with patient outcomes and easily detectable.

NDUFA4L2, which is a subunit of Complex I of the mitochondrial respiratory chain, plays a crucial role in metabolic reprogramming and oxidative stress in various types of cancer.[7–9] Lai et al discovered that in cases of hepatocellular carcinoma, NDUFA4L2 was significantly increased in cancerous tissues when compared to normal liver tissues.[10] The upregulation of NDUFA4L2 was triggered by hypoxia and was associated with cancer invasion and poor overall survival. NDUFA4L2 was identified as a direct transcriptional target of hypoxia-inducible factor (HIF)-1α, and knocking down NDUFA4L2 or inhibiting HIF-1α resulted in significant suppression of tumor growth and metastasis. This suggests that patients with hepatocellular carcinoma who exhibit NDUFA4L2 overexpression may be ideal candidates for HIF inhibitor treatment. Similarly, Minton et al[7] reported that NDUFA4L2 was induced by HIF-1α in clear cell renal cell carcinoma patients. NDUFA4L2 was highly expressed in tumor samples, but not in normal kidney tissue samples. Furthermore, NDUFA4L2 overexpression was linked to poor overall survival, and NDUFA4L2 knockdown caused autophagy, impaired cell proliferation, and reduced colony formation. In addition, hypoxia-induced overexpression of NDUFA4L2, which suppresses oxidative phosphorylation, has been reported in lung and colorectal cancer.[11,12]Additionally, clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC) patients with higher levels of NDUFA4L2 have a lower survival rate, report also suggest that NDUFA4L2 plays a crucial role in the proliferation of ccRCC cells, as knocking down NDUFA4L2 in ccRCC cell lines results in reduced cell proliferation and colony formation. NDUFA4L2 also increases the expression of carbonic anhydrase 9, a marker of ccRCC,[13] and promotes the deposition of lipids in a murine ccRCC model.[14]

Hence, in this research, we used the CRC RNA-seq data in TCGA database to compare the difference of NDUFA4L2 expression between tumor tissues and normal samples, and investigated the correlation between NDUFA4L2 expression levels and clinical pathological features of CRC. Next, we evaluated the prognostic value of NDUFA4L2 in CRC. In addition, GO, KEGG and GSEA were performed on the high and low expression groups of NDUFA4L2 to reveal its possible functions. Finally, by analyzing the correlation between NDUFA4L2 expression and immune infiltration, we comprehensively explored and discussed the potential mechanism of NDUFA4L2 regulating the occurrence and development of CRC.

2. Methods

2.1. Differentially expressed NDUFA4L2 at the transcriptional level

We used TCGA database (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/) to collect RNA-seq data and clinical information from 698 cases of CRC projects, including 51 cases with matched adjacent tissues. The downloaded data format was level 3 HTSeq-fragments per kilobase per million and then was converted into transcripts per million format for subsequent analysis. We also download transcripts per million format RNA-seq data in TCGA and Genotype-Tissue Expression database that uniformly processed by Toil process from University of California Santa Cruz Xena (https://xenabrowser.net/datapages/).[15]All procedures performed in this study were in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki (as revised in 2013).

2.2. Differentially expressed NDUFA4L2 at protein level

The protein expression analysis of NDUFA4L2 in this study utilized data from the CPTAC Confirmatory/Discovery dataset for Colon Cancer.[16] CPTAC (Clinical Proteomic Tumor Analysis Consortium) is a resource used for proteomics research on various types of tumors. In this work, the protein expressions of NDUFA4L2 in Colon Cancer tissues were compared to normal tissues using the CPTAC reproducible workflow protocol.

To visualize the protein expression of NDUFA4L2 between normal and CRC samples, immunohistochemistry images were directly obtained from the HPA (Human Protein Atlas) platform. The HPA platform contains representative immunohistochemistry-based protein expression data for approximately 20 highly common types of cancers.[17]In this study, the immunohistochemistry images from HPA were used to assess the differential protein expression of NDUFA4L2 in normal and CRC samples.

2.3. Clinicopathological analysis of NDUFA4L2 in CRC

Clinicopathological analysis of NDUFA4L2 in CRC using data from TCGA, ROC analysis of the data using R package (pROC), and visualization of the results using R package (ggplot2).R package (stats) is used to create baseline data tables and perform logistic regression between NDUFA4L2 expression and clinical pathological characteristics in CRC.

2.4. Acquisition of differentially expressed genes and application of differentially expressed genes for GO/KEGG/GSEA analysis

The R(version 3.6.0) package org. Hs.e.g..db(3.10.0) was used to conversion gene ID.TCGA-COADREAD data were used to screen DEGs, we used R package (DESeq2)[18] with adjusted P value < .05 and |logFC|>2 as cutoff criteria to screen the obtained DEGs. These DEGs are used to evaluate the potential differential biological functions between the high NDUFA4L2 expression group and the low NDUFA4L2 expression group. KEGG pathway and GO annotation were performed by R package cluster Profiler(3.14.3).[19,20]GO analysis revealed 3 different pathways, including a catalog of biological processes (BP), cellular components (CC), and molecular functions (MF).

To further understand the underlying mechanism of NDUFA4L2, with adjusted P value < .05 and |logFC| > 1 as cut off criteria, the DEGs obtained were used for GSEA analysis. According to the default statistical method, the process was repeated 1000 times for each analysis and selected c2.all.v2022.1.Hs.symbols.gmt[All Canonical Pathways] in MSigDB Collections as the reference gene collection, false discovery rate (FDR) q-value < 0.25 and adjusted P adjust < 0.05 were considered to be significantly enriched.

2.5. Immune infiltration analysis by ssGSEA

The immune infiltration analysis of CRC was performed by single sample GSEA (ssGSEA) method from R (v.3.6.3) package GSVA (version 1.34.0),[21] and we quantified the infiltration levels of 24 immune cell types from gene expression profile in the literature.[22] In order to discover the correlation between NDUFA4L2 and the infiltration levels of 24 immune cells, P values were determined by the Pearson and Wilcoxon rank sum test.

2.6. Protein–protein interaction analysis

Search Tool for the Retrieval of Interacting Genes (STRING) is an online database that searches for known proteins and predicts protein interaction relationships, including direct physical interactions between proteins and indirect functional correlations. The STRING database collects, evaluates, and integrates all publicly available protein–protein interaction information and complements this information with computational predictions to build a protein–protein interaction network. The software analyzes all DEGs; the interaction score threshold was set at 0.400.

2.7. Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using R(v.3.6.3).Wilcoxon rank sum test, chi square test, Fisher exact test and logistic regression were used to analyze the relationship between clinical pathologic features and NDUFA4L2. Kaplan–Meier method was used to calculate the overall survival rate and progression free interval of CRC patients from TCGA. Univariate and multivariate analysis were performed to estimate the association between clinical and genetic clinical characteristics and PFI using Cox proportional hazard models. P values <.05 were considered statistically significant.

2.8. Ethical approval

This study does not involve experiments that require ethical approval.

3. Results

3.1. NDUFA4L2 expression is correlated with poor clinicopathological features of CRC

In order to identify the difference of NDUFA4L2 expression between CRC tissues and normal tissues, immunohistochemical staining of NDUFA4L2 obtained from HPA database showed that the expression of NDUFA4L2 in CRC tissue was up-regulated compared with normal colorectal tissue (Fig. 1A and B).We also analyzed the expression level of NDUFA4L2 in 647 CRC tissues and 51 adjacent normal colorectal tissues, and found that NDUFA4L2 was highly expressed in CRC tissues (P < .001, Fig. 1C). Meanwhile, we also analyzed the expression of NDUFA4L2 in 51 CRC tissues and their matched adjacent tissues. The results indicated that CRC tissues highly expressed NDUFA4L2 (P < .001, Fig. 1D). Moreover, the expression of NDUFA4L2 in normal samples of Genotype-Tissue Expression combined TCGA database and CRC samples of TCGA database was compared. We also found that NDUFA4L2 was significantly overexpressed in CRC samples (P < .01, Fig. 1E). Similarly, the results from the CPTAC database indicated that the protein expression of NDUFA4L2 was remarkably increased in CRC (Fig. 1F).In addition, we used the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve to analyze the effectiveness of NDUFA4L2 expression level to distinguish CRC tissues from non-tumor tissues. The area under curve (AUC) of NDUFA4L2 was 0.878, suggesting that NDUFA4L2 could be served as an ideal biomarker to distinguish CRC from non-tumor tissue (Fig. 1G).

3.2. NDUFA4L2 expression is correlated with poor prognosis of patients with CRC

The Kaplan–Meier analysis evaluated the correlation between the expression of NDUFA4L2 and OS, DSS, or PFI in CRC patients, indicating that high expression of NDUFA4L2 is associated with poor OS (P = .021, Fig. 3A), poor DSS (P = .002, Fig. 3B), and poor PFI (P = .001, Fig. 3C) in CRC patients.

3.6. PPI network and hub gene identification and verification of the prognostic value of 10 hub genes

The search tool (STRING, https://string-db.org/) is a database for finding interacting genes, with which we constructed a PPI network.[23] 145 intersection target genes were imported into the STRING database and the species were selected as “Homo sapiens” to obtain the interaction relationship between the targets. Genes with significant interactions were identified based on a confidence level ≥ 0.4, other irrelevant genes were excluded. The screening results were input into Cytoscape software 3.8.2[23] for network visualization (Fig. 7A).

(A)161 intersection target genes were imported into the STRING database and the species were selected as “Homo sapiens” to obtain the interaction relationship between the targets. (B) Identification of the hub genes from the PPI network by the maximum clique centrality (MCC) algorithm, the red nodes represent genes with the highest MCC sores. STRING = Search Tool for the Retrieval of Interacting Genes.

The MCC value of each node is calculated by the CytoHubba plugin in Cytoscape. In this study, the 20 genes with the highest MCC values were selected as hub genes. In co-expression networks, the maximum clique centrality (MCC) algorithm is considered to be the most efficient method to identify central nodes (Fig. 7B).[24]

3.7. Discussion

In recent years, more and more cancer biomarkers have been found and reported, but due to their sensitivity or specificity, they have not really been applied in clinical practice. This study explored the expression of a new biomarker NDUFA4L2 in CRC and its relationship with the diagnosis and prognosis of CRC at the level of bioinformatics.

Several new research studies have uncovered the functions of NDUFA4L2 in different types of cancers. NDUFA4L2 is believed to function as an oncogene and is specifically regulated by HIF-1α at the transcriptional level in certain types of tumors such as hepatocellular carcinoma, clear cell renal cell carcinoma, osteosarcoma and lung cancer.[7,10,11,25] Other studies have shown that NDUFA4L2 promotes the progression of glioblastoma and is associated with poor survival rate, and can be used as an effective target of apatinib.[25] In Yuan research, NDUFA4L2 can also promote the drug resistance of HER2 positive breast cancer to trastuzumab.[26] Other studies have shown that mitochondrial NDUFA4L2 can enhance lipid deposition and Ca9 expression in the Transgenic Cancer of the Kidney model of early clear cell renal cell carcinoma.[14]Nonetheless, the part that NDUFA4L2 plays in CRC and its underlying mechanisms remain unclear.

The present study demonstrated the elevated level of NDUFA4L2 in CRC tissues, which is associated with poor patient outcome. A highlight of this work is to predict the potential mechanisms by which NDUFA4L2 regulates the development of CRC. Based on the results of GO and KEGG analysis, NDUFA4L2 plays an important role in the occurrence and development of CRC. In terms of BP, it is associated with mRNA splicing site recognition, snRNA formation, trans-splicing mRNA, and spliceosome complex formation. In terms of CC, NDUFA4L2 related DEGs were enriched in spliceosomal snRNP complexes, small nuclear ribonucleoprotein complexes, and Cajal bodies. In terms of MF, NDUFA4L2 related DEGs were involved in pre-mRNA 5’-splice site binding, pre-mRNA binding, bitter taste receptor activity, taste receptor activity, and mRNA binding involved in posttranscriptional gene silencing. In addition, the KEGG analysis reveals its potential relevance to systemic lupus erythematosus, alcoholism, taste transduction, spliceosome, and RNA transport pathways. These findings suggest that NDUFA4L2 plays a multifaceted role in CRC, highlighting its significance as a potential therapeutic target and diagnostic marker.

It is intriguing that in our GSEA enrichment analysis, we discovered that the DEGs associated with NDUFA4L2 were enriched in gene sets related to H3K27me3.H3K27me3 is a prevalent histone modification that influences gene expression and chromatin structure, it is commonly recognized as a transcriptional repression marker. The function and mechanism of H3K27me3 play critical roles in various biological processes, including embryonic development, cell differentiation, and cell fate determination.H3K27me3 also plays a significant role in cancer development, particularly in transcriptional dysregulation and abnormal cell proliferation. It is involved in the silencing of specific genes and may contribute to the aberrant gene expression patterns observed in cancer cells.[27,28]That is to say, in future research on the mechanism of NDUFA4L2 in CRC, H2K27me3 will not be ignored. In addition, NDUFA4L2 related DEGs are also enriched in the up regulated gene set in breast cancer basal like subtype and the down regulated gene set in breast cancer Luminal B subtype. This indicates that the role of NDUFA4L2 in CRC may be similar to the molecular expression patterns of the 2 breast cancer subtypes mentioned above.

Our research results indicate that after calculation using the ssGSEA algorithm, there may be a moderate positive correlation between the expression of NDUFA4L2 and the infiltration levels of NK cells, DC cells, and iDC cells in colorectal cancer. However, to confirm the relationship between NDUFA4L2 and immune cells, more direct evidence is needed, such as flow cytometry experiments.

To our knowledge, despite some limitations, this is the first work to fully explore the relationship between NDUFA4L2 and CRC in bioinformatics. First of all, the current research is mainly based on bioinformatics analysis, which can be further strengthened through experimental research. Second, the number of healthy subjects as controls is very different from the number of cancer patients. Finally, retrospective research still has its limitations, especially the inconsistent intervention measures and lack of relevant information. Therefore, follow-up studies are needed to further verify our findings.

4. Conclusions

In summary, we observed an increase in NDUFA4L2 expression in CRC tissues, which was associated with poor OS, poor DSS and poor PFI. High expression of NDUFA4L2 is associated with infiltration of some immune cells. Elevated NDUFA4L2 expression was correlated with malignant clinical pathological features in CRC, and the ROC curve analysis indicated its potential as a diagnostic marker for CRC. The results of GO, KEGG and GSEA analysis suggested the mechanism of NDUFA4L2 in CRC. Furthermore, these findings provide clues to further explore the possible role of NDUFA4L2 in CRC.

Stringy Hodge numbers of Pfaffian double mirrors and Homological Projective Duality
Zengrui Han

Abstract
. We study the stringy Hodge numbers of Pfaffian double mirrors, generalizing previous results of Borisov and Libgober. In the even-dimensional cases, we introduce a modified version of stringy E E -functions and obtain interesting relations between the modified stringy E E -functions on the two sides. The modified stringy E E -functions can also be interpreted as the stringy E E -functions of certain ideal sheaf pairs. We use them to make numerical predictions on the Lefschetz decompositions of the categorical crepant resolutions of Pfaffian varieties.



1.
Introduction

The classical formulation of mirror symmetry concerns the duality of Hodge numbers of mirror varieties. More precisely, if
X
and
X^{\vee}
are
n
-dimensional mirror Calabi-Yau varieties, then one may expect

\displaystyle h^{p,q}(X)=h^{n-p,q}(X^{\vee}),\quad\text{for all }p,q.

However, the usual Hodge numbers are not well-behaved in the context of mirror symmetry: the expected equalities fail for singular mirror pairs.

This issue was addressed by Batyrev
[
1
]
, who introduced the concept of
stringy
E
-functions
as a replacement for the usual Hodge-Deligne polynomials for varieties with log-terminal singularities. The stringy
E
-function
E_{\mathrm{st}}(X;u,v)
of a variety
X
is a function in two variables defined as a certain weighted sum over strata of a log resolution of
X
. Importantly, the definition is independent of the choice of the log resolution.

Stringy
E
-functions behave well in the context of mirror symmetry: they agree with the usual Hodge-Deligne polynomials for smooth varieties, and more importantly, if a singular variety
X
admits a crepant resolution
\widehat{X}
, then its stringy
E
-function
E_{\mathrm{st}}(X;u,v)
is equal to the ordinary
E
-polynomial of
\widehat{X}
. This is consistent with the well-known principle in physics that string theories on singular spaces should be equivalent to string theories on their crepant resolutions.

In recent years, a phenomenon known as the
double mirror phenomenon
has attracted great interest. A pair of spaces
X
and
Y
are said to form a double mirror pair if they share the same mirror family. Consequently, they are expected to have the same mirror-symmetry-theoretic properties (Hodge numbers, derived categories, elliptic genera, etc.).

Within the framework of the
gauged linear sigma model
(GLSM) introduced by Witten
[
22
]
, a double mirror pair can be seen as distinct phases of the same GLSM. The primary focus of this paper is the investigation of the
Pfaffian double mirrors
construction, which serves as an example of a nonabelian GLSM. In contrast to the abelian GLSMs (toric mirror symmetry), where different geometric phases in the GLSM turn out to be birationally equivalent to each other (cf.
[
14
]
), this is not true for nonabelian GLSMs.

The study of the Pfaffian double mirrors originates from the work of Rødland
[
18
]
. We briefly recall the original construction. Let
V
be a 7-dimensional complex vector space, and
W
be a generic 7-dimensional subspace of the space
\wedge^{2}V^{\vee}
of skew forms on
V
. We construct a pair of Calabi-Yau threefolds
X_{W}
and
Y_{W}
as follows:

•

X_{W}
is the subvariety of the Grassmannian
G(2,V)
consisting of all 2-dimensional subspaces
T_{2}\subseteq V
such that
w|_{T_{2}}=0
for all
w\in W
.

•

Y_{W}
is the intersection of the Pfaffian variety
{\mathrm{Pf}}(V)
(i.e., the variety of all
degenerate
skew forms on
V
) with the projective space
{\mathbb{P}}W
inside
{\mathbb{P}}(\wedge^{2}V^{\vee})
.

Rødland argued that
X_{W}
and
Y_{W}
share the same mirror family. This example was further studied by Borisov-Căldăraru
[
5
]
, Hori-Tong
[
11
]
, and Kuznetsov
[
12
]
.

Pfaffian double mirrors

The generalization of the original construction of Rødland to higher dimensions was suggested by Kuznetsov

Let

{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V^{\vee})

{\mathrm{Pf}}(2\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor-2k,V)

X_{W}

Y_{W}

{\mathbb{P}}W

Borisov and Libgober

studied the case when

and

are Calabi-Yau and of the same dimension, hence form a double mirror pair in the strict sense. They showed that the expected equality

In general, when the dimension of

is arbitrary or when

X_{W}
and
Y_{W}
are double mirrors only in a generalized sense: they are not necessarily Calabi-Yau, and they may have different dimensions. Therefore one cannot hope to have a genuine equality between their stringy
E
-functions. Nevertheless, they are related to each other in an interesting way.

Before we state the main results of this paper, we make a few comments on the main differences between the even- and odd-dimensional cases. It has been observed in
[
6
]
that some issues occur in the even-dimensional case. The first issue is that when
n
is even, it is impossible to choose
W
such that
X_{W}
and
Y_{W}
are Calabi-Yau simultaneously. The second issue, which is more essential, is that the stringy
E
-function of the Pfaffian
{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V)
is not a polynomial.

We explain how to address the second issue. To compute the stringy
E
-function, we need to choose a log resolution of
{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V)
. The log resolution
\pi:\widehat{\operatorname{Pf}(2k,V)}\rightarrow\operatorname{Pf}(2k,V)
we consider in this paper is given by the
space of complete skew forms of rank
\leq 2k
(see Definition
3.1
). The discrepancies of this resolution are computed in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1

(= Theorem
3.5
)
.

The discrepancies of the log resolution
\pi:\widehat{\operatorname{Pf}(2k,V)}\rightarrow\operatorname{Pf}(2k,V)
are given by the equation

\displaystyle K_{\widehat{\operatorname{Pf}(2k,V)}}=\pi^{*}K_{\operatorname{Pf}(2k,V)}+\sum_{j}\alpha_{j,k,n}D_{j}

where
\alpha_{j,k,n}=2j^{2}-j(n-2k)-1
, for
j=2,3,...,\frac{n-2}{2}
. See Theorem
3.5
for the precise definition of
D_{j}
.

As noted above, these discrepancies yield a non-polynomial stringy
E
-function.
One is then forced to consider a modified notion of discrepancy.

Definition 1.2

(= Definition
4.2
)
.

We define the
modified discrepancy
of the log resolution
\pi:\widehat{\operatorname{Pf}(2k,V)}\rightarrow\operatorname{Pf}(2k,V)
as

\displaystyle\widetilde{\alpha}_{j,k,n}=2j^{2}-j(n-2k+1)+\frac{n-2k-2}{2}

which differs from the usual discrepancy
\alpha_{j,k,n}
by a linear polynomial
j-\frac{n-2k}{2}
.

We show in Theorem
4.4
that the
modified stringy
E
-function
of
{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V)
computed from the modified discrepancies above is a genuine polynomial.

The idea of modifying the discrepancies originates from
[
6
]
and was further explored by Borisov and Wang in
[
7
]
for Clifford double mirrors. However, due to the difficulty of finding a Zariski locally trivial log resolution in the setting of Clifford double mirrors, only partial results at the level of stringy Euler characteristics (which is the specialization of the stringy
E
-function when

) were obtained in their work. The result presented in this paper is the first example establishing equalities at the level of stringy Hodge numbers, after the modification of the discrepancies.

Theorem 1.3

(= Theorem
5.1
)
.

Let
n
be an even integer, and let
X_{W}
and
Y_{W}
be the Pfaffian double mirrors corresponding to a generic
l
-dimensional subspace
W\subseteq\wedge^{2}V^{\vee}
. We have the following relation between the modified stringy
E
-functions of
X_{W}
and
Y_{W}
:

\displaystyle q^{(n-1)k}\widetilde{E}_{\mathrm{st}}(Y_{W})-q^{l}\widetilde{E}_{\mathrm{st}}(X_{W})=\frac{q^{l}-q^{(n-1)k}}{q-1}\binom{n/2}{k}_{q^{2}}

where
q=uv
, and
\binom{n/2}{k}_{q^{2}}=\prod_{j=k+1}^{\frac{n}{2}}\frac{q^{2j}-1}{q^{2j-2k}-1}
is the
q
-binomial coefficient.

Applying a similar argument to the odd-dimensional cases (without modifying the discrepancies), we obtain the following result, generalizing a previous result of Borisov and Libgober.

Theorem 1.4

(= Theorem
5.6
)
.

Let
n
be an odd integer, and let
X_{W}
and
Y_{W}
be the Pfaffian double mirrors corresponding to a generic
l
-dimensional subspace
W\subseteq\wedge^{2}V^{\vee}
. Then we have the following relation between the stringy
E
-functions of
X_{W}
and
Y_{W}
:

\displaystyle q^{nk}E_{\mathrm{st}}(Y_{W})-q^{l}E_{\mathrm{st}}(X_{W})=\frac{q^{l}-q^{nk}}{q-1}\binom{(n-1)/2}{k}_{q^{2}}

1.2.
Modified
E
-functions as stringy
E
-functions of pairs

The modified
E
-functions introduced in this paper admit a speculative geometric interpretation as the stringy
E
-functions for ideal sheaf pairs. Let
X
be a variety with log-terminal singularities, and let
\mathcal{I}
be an ideal sheaf on
X
. The stringy
E
-function of the pair
(X,\mathcal{I})
is defined in the usual way, except that the discrepancies are defined as

\displaystyle\widetilde{a_{j}}=a_{j}-\mathrm{ord}_{D_{j}}(\mathcal{I})

where
a_{j}
is the usual discrepancy of the exceptional divisor
D_{j}
, and the extra term comes from the vanishing order of the ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}
along
D_{j}
. A more general definition can be found in
[
10
, Appendix A]
. The independence of the choice of the log resolution is also proved in loc. cit.

In our situation, the ambient variety
X
is the Pfaffian variety
{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V)
, and the ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}
is the ideal sheaf of the next lower-rank Pfaffian locus
{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k-2,V)
inside
{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V)
. The computation of the relevant vanishing orders can be found in section
4.1
. One immediately observes that they coincide with the correction terms in our formula for the modified discrepancies. Therefore we have the equality

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}_{\mathrm{st}}({\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V))=E_{\mathrm{st}}({\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V),\mathcal{I}_{{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k-2,V)}).

The author is not aware of a conceptual explanation for this equality beyond the direct computation. It would be interesting to understand the geometric meaning of the modified
E
-functions in a more conceptual way. However, at this moment, it remains a mysterious phenomenon.

1.3.
Stringy
E
-functions, derived categories, and Homological Projective Duality

It has been a long-standing problem to develop Hodge theory for generalized varieties (e.g., noncommutative algebraic varieties, or more generally, categorical resolutions of singular varieties). One of the motivations behind this is the observation that the derived category of a smooth variety shares many similarities to its classical Hodge-theoretic counterpart. Examples include the similarity between Orlov’s formula for the semiorthogonal decomposition of the derived category of blow-ups and the formula for the stringy
E
-function of blow-ups.

This problem is also important from the point of view of mirror symmetry, as the classical mirror symmetry predicts the equality between Hodge numbers of a double mirror pair, while the homological mirror symmetry predicts the equivalence of derived categories. It is then natural to study the relationship between these two aspects.

In the case of the Pfaffian varieties considered in this paper, the derived equivalence is realized by Kuznetsov’s Homological Projective Duality. One of the inputs of Homological Projective Duality is a Lefschetz decomposition on the appropriate derived category, which plays a role similar to the classical Lefschetz decomposition in Hodge theory. The Homological Projective Duality for Pfaffian varieties in even-dimensional cases remains an open problem (for partial results in this direction, see Rennemo-Segal
[
17
]
and Pirozhkov
[
16
]
). One of the main difficulties is constructing the categorical crepant resolutions of Pfaffians and their Lefschetz decompositions. Theorem
5.1
, the main result of this paper, allows us to make predictions on the shape of the Lefschetz decompositions of such categorical resolutions. More precisely, we make the following conjecture, which we hope to address in future work.

Conjecture 1.5

(= Conjecture
6.2
)
.

The categorical crepant resolution
\widetilde{D}^{b}({\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V^{\vee}))
of the Pfaffian variety
{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V^{\vee})
has a non-rectangular Lefschetz decomposition of the form

\displaystyle\widetilde{D}^{b}({\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V^{\vee}))=\Big\langle\mathcal{A}_{0},\mathcal{A}_{1}(1),\cdots,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{nk-\frac{n}{2}-1}(nk-\frac{n}{2}-1),

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{nk-\frac{n}{2}}(nk-\frac{n}{2}),\cdots,\mathcal{A}_{nk-1}(nk-1)\Big\rangle

where
\mathcal{A}_{0}=\cdots=\mathcal{A}_{nk-\frac{n}{2}-1}
are
nk-\frac{n}{2}
blocks of size
\binom{n/2}{k}
, and
\mathcal{A}_{nk-\frac{n}{2}}=\cdots=\mathcal{A}_{nk-1}
are
n/2
blocks of size
\binom{n/2-1}{k}
. Similarly, the dual Lefschetz decomposition of the dual
{\mathrm{Pf}}(n-2k,V)
has the form

\widetilde{D}^{b}({\mathrm{Pf}}(n-2k,V))=\Big\langle\mathcal{B}_{\frac{n^{2}}{2}-nk-1}(-\frac{n^{2}}{2}+nk+1),\\
\cdots\mathcal{B}_{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}-nk}(-\frac{n(n-1)}{2}+nk),\\
\mathcal{B}_{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}-nk-1}(-\frac{n(n-1)}{2}+nk+1),\cdots,\mathcal{B}_{1}(-1),\mathcal{B}_{0}\Big\rangle

where
\mathcal{B}_{0}=\cdots=\mathcal{B}_{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}-nk-1}
are
\frac{n(n-1)}{2}-nk
blocks of size
\binom{n/2}{n/2-k}
, and
\mathcal{B}_{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}-nk}=\cdots=\mathcal{B}_{\frac{n^{2}}{2}-nk-1}
are
n/2
blocks of size
\binom{n/2-1}{n/2-k}
.

After passing to the linear sections
X_{W}\subseteq{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V^{\vee})
and
Y_{W}\subseteq{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V^{\vee})
, the appropriately defined categorical resolutions have semiorthogonal decompositions that are closely related to the Lefschetz decompositions of the ambient Pfaffian varieties, as predicted by Homological Projective Duality. In particular, when the dimension of
W
varies, the ambient components (that are inherited from the ambient Pfaffian varieties) in the decomposition disappear on one side and reappear on the other side. We provide evidence for this conjecture by analyzing the behavior of the stringy
E
-functions of
X_{W}
and
Y_{W}
as the dimension of
W
varies. For further details, see section
6
.

Remark 1.6
.

In
[
17
]
, Rennemo and Segal proved Homological Projective Duality for odd-dimensional Pfaffian varieties using the noncommutative resolutions of quotient singularities constructed by Špenko and van den Bergh
[
20
]
, together with matrix factorization techniques. They also obtained partial results in the even-dimensional setting. A major obstruction to establishing Pfaffian HPD in even dimensions is that the Špenko–van den Bergh noncommutative resolution is no longer crepant. Nevertheless, Rennemo and Segal proposed a conjectural HPD category in this setting; see
[
17
, eq.(5.2)]
. As Ed Segal pointed out to the author, the sizes of the blocks appearing in their conjectural category agree precisely with those predicted by our results. The contribution of the present paper is to provide an independent, Hodge-theoretic numerical derivation of these block sizes from the modified stringy E-function, without relying on derived-categorical input.

Remark 1.7
.

Satriano and Usatine
[
21
]
constructed geometric crepant resolutions of log-terminal singularities by Artin stacks. From the perspective of mirror symmetry, the categorical crepant resolution should be equivalent to the category of B-branes on such an Artin stack crepant resolution, provided that the latter is appropriately defined. However, such a definition is not yet available in the literature and seems difficult to obtain, even for the simplest examples.

2.1.
Pfaffian varieties

Let
V
be a complex vector space of dimension
n\geq 4
. For
k=1,2,\cdots,\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor
, we define the Pfaffian variety
2
2

2

In some references, this is called a
generalized
Pfaffian variety.

{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V)
to be the subvariety of
{\mathbb{P}}(\wedge^{2}V^{\vee})
consisting of nontrivial skew forms on
V
whose rank does not exceed
2k
. After fixing a basis for the vector space
V
, the Pfaffian variety
{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V)
could also be seen as the space of skew-symmetric
n\times n
matrices of rank at most
2k
, modulo scalar multiplication. We sometimes simply write
{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,n)
when there is no risk of confusion.

There is a natural stratification of the Pfaffian variety as a disjoint union of locally closed subvarieties

\displaystyle{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V)=\bigsqcup_{1\leq i\leq k}{\mathrm{Pf}}^{\circ}(2i,V)

where the stratum
{\mathrm{Pf}}^{\circ}(2i,V)
is the locus of skew forms of rank exactly equal to
2i
.

The Pfaffian variety
{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V)
is singular in general, except for the extremal cases
k=1
, where
{\mathrm{Pf}}(2,V)
is naturally isomorphic to the Grassmannian
G(2,V)
, and the case
k=\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor
, where
{\mathrm{Pf}}(n,V)
is the whole space
{\mathbb{P}}(\wedge^{2}V^{\vee})
. For
1<k<\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor
, the singular locus of
{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V)
is the subvariety
{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k-2,V)
of forms of the next lower ranks.

Proposition 2.1
.

The Pfaffian variety
{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V)
is Gorenstein, of dimension
2nk-2k^{2}-k-1
, and its canonical class is given by
-nk\xi
, where
\xi
denotes the pullback of the hyperplane section
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}(\wedge^{2}V^{\vee})}(1)
on
{\mathbb{P}}(\wedge^{2}V^{\vee})
.

Proof.

The first statement is a simple dimension count. The canonical class of the Pfaffian variety could be computed from the standard Koszul resolution and Serre duality.
∎

2.2.
Pfaffian double mirrors

In this subsection we introduce the construction of the Pfaffian double mirrors.

Assume
k<\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor
. Let
W
be a generic subspace of
\wedge^{2}V^{\vee}
of dimension
l
. Here, and throughout this paper, the generic condition means that all the non-empty intersections
{\mathbb{P}}W^{\perp}\cap{\mathrm{Pf}}(2i,V^{\vee})
,
i=1,\cdots,k
and
{\mathbb{P}}W\cap{\mathrm{Pf}}(2i,V)
,
i=1,\cdots,\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor-k
are transverse. In particular, every such intersection is either empty or smooth of the expected dimension. We define

•

X_{W}
to be the complete intersection of
{\mathbb{P}}W^{\perp}
and the Pfaffian variety
{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V^{\vee})
in
{\mathbb{P}}(\wedge^{2}V)
.

•

Y_{W}
to be the complete intersection of
{\mathbb{P}}W
and the Pfaffian variety
{\mathrm{Pf}}(2\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor-2k,V)
in
{\mathbb{P}}(\wedge^{2}V^{\vee})
.

It is apparent from the definition that the roles of
X_{W}
and
Y_{W}
are switched if we interchange the data
(V,k,W)
with
(V^{\vee},\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor-k,W^{\perp})
.

The main difference between the even- and odd-dimensional cases is the following. In the case where
n
is odd, it is possible to make
X_{W}
and
Y_{W}
have the same dimension and Calabi-Yau simultaneously (indeed we achieve this by taking
\dim W=nk
). Therefore
X_{W}
and
Y_{W}
form a double mirror pair in the strict sense. However, it is impossible to do this when
n
is even, and we only have a double mirror pair in a generalized sense. We have the following result.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
n
be even. The variety
X_{W}
is of dimension
2nk-l-2k^{2}-k-2
, and its canonical class is
(l-nk)\xi
. Similarly, the variety
Y_{W}
is of dimension
l-2k^{2}+k-2
, and its canonical class is
(nk-\frac{n}{2}-l)\xi
.

Proof.

The variety
X_{W}
can be equivalently defined as the set of forms
y\in{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V^{\vee})
such that
\langle y,\alpha\rangle=0
for any
\alpha\in W
, and can therefore be viewed as the complete intersection of
l
hyperplanes with the Pfaffian variety
{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V)
inside
{\mathbb{P}}(\wedge^{2}V)
. Thus a dimension count and the adjunction formula yield the statement for
X_{W}
. Interchanging
(V,k,W)
with
(V^{\vee},\frac{n}{2}-k,W^{\perp})
we get the statement for
Y_{W}
.
∎

Example 2.3
.

(1) When
l=nk
, the varieties
X_{W}
and
Y_{W}
are of dimension
nk-2k^{2}-k-2
and
nk-2k^{2}+k-2
respectively. Furthermore, their canonical classes are
0

-\frac{n}{2}\xi

X_{W}
is Calabi-Yau while
Y_{W}
is Fano.

(2) When
l=(n-1)k
, the varieties
X_{W}
and

are both of dimension
nk-2k^{2}-2
, and they have canonical classes
-k\xi
and
(k-\frac{n}{2})\xi
respectively. Hence in this case both
X_{W}
and
Y_{W}
are Fano.

2.3.
Stringy
E
-functions

Let
X
be a complex algebraic variety. The Hodge-Deligne polynomial (or
E
-polynomial) of
X
is defined as

\displaystyle E(X;u,v):=\sum_{p,q}\left(\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}h^{p,q}(H_{c}^{i}(X,{\mathbb{C}}))\right)u^{p}v^{q}

where
H_{c}^{i}(X,{\mathbb{C}})
is the cohomology with compact support of
X
together with its natural mixed Hodge structure. Two basic properties are additivity and multiplicativity.

•

(Additivity) If
X=\sqcup_{i}X_{i}
is a stratification of
X
by Zariski locally closed subsets
X_{i}
, then the
E
-polynomial
E(X)
is additive on the strata, i.e.,
E(X)=\sum_{i}E(X_{i})
.

•

(Multiplicativity) If
\pi:Y\rightarrow X
is a Zariski locally trivial fibration with fiber
F
, then
E(Y)=E(X)\cdot E(F)
.

Example 2.4
.

(1) The
E
-polynomial of the projective space
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
is
E({\mathbb{P}}^{n})=\frac{(uv)^{n+1}-1}{uv-1}
.

(2) The
E
-polynomial of the Grassmannian
G(k,n)
is

\displaystyle E(G(k,n))=\binom{n}{k}_{uv}:=\frac{\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(1-(uv)^{n-j})}{\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(1-(uv)^{j+1})}

Here
\binom{n}{k}_{uv}
is the
q
-binomial coefficient
3
3

3

See Appendix
A
for a precise definition. We will use this notation throughout this paper.
.

As we have discussed in section
1
, the usual Hodge numbers do not behave well in the context of mirror symmetry. The correct replacement for the ordinary
E
-polynomials is the notion of stringy
E
-function introduced by Batyrev
[
1
]
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
X
be a singular variety with at worst log-terminal singularities. Let
\pi:\widehat{X}\rightarrow X
be a log resolution and
\{D_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be the set of exceptional divisors. Denote
D_{J}^{\circ}=(\bigcap_{j\in J}D_{j})\backslash(\bigcup_{j\not\in J}D_{j})
. The
stringy
E
-function
of
X
is given by

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{st}}(X;u,v):=\sum_{J\subseteq I}E(D_{J}^{\circ})\prod_{j\in J}\frac{uv-1}{(uv)^{\alpha_{j}+1}-1},

where
\alpha_{j}
denotes the discrepancy of the exceptional divisor
D_{j}
, defined by the equation
K_{\widehat{X}}=\pi^{*}K_{X}+\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}D_{j}
.

Although the definition involves a choice of a log resolution
\pi:\widehat{X}\rightarrow X
, it is proved by using the technique of motivic integration that its stringy
E
-function is indeed independent of such a choice.

It is worth mentioning that even for relatively simple varieties the stringy
E
-function is not necessarily a polynomial. Indeed, it is still an open problem under what conditions the stringy
E
-function is a polynomial. An obvious sufficient condition is that
X
admits a crepant resolution, in which case the stringy
E
-function is the ordinary
E
-polynomial of the crepant resolution.

The stringy
E
-function is particularly easy to compute when
X
admits a Zariski locally trivial log resolution.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
\pi:\widehat{X}\rightarrow X
be a Zariski locally trivial log resolution. Define the local contribution of a point
x\in X
to the stringy
E
-function
E_{\mathrm{st}}(X)
by

\displaystyle S(x;u,v):=\sum_{J\subseteq I}E(D_{J}^{\circ}\cap\pi^{-1}(x))\prod_{j\in J}\frac{uv-1}{(uv)^{\alpha_{j}+1}-1}.

Then the stringy
E
-function
E_{\mathrm{st}}(X)
is

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{st}}(X)=\sum_{i}E(X_{i};u,v)S(x\in X_{i};u,v),

where
X=\sqcup X_{i}
is the stratification of
X
into the strata where
S
is a constant.

3.
Log resolutions of Pfaffian varieties

In this section, we describe a log resolution of the Pfaffian variety

Definition 3.1

Let

{\mathrm{Pf}}(2,V)

The variety

is smooth, and is a log resolution of the Pfaffian variety
{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V)

\displaystyle 0\subseteq F^{0}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq F^{l}=V

(n-2k)
-dimensional subspace, together with nondegenerate forms

on the quotient

. The natural map
\pi:\widehat{{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V)}\rightarrow{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V)
is then defined by mapping the data described above to the form
w_{l-1}
considered as a skew form on

.

Proof.

See
[
3
]
and
[
6
, Proposition 3.4]
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

There is a simpler but non-log resolution of the singularities of

. Consider the space

\displaystyle\left\{(w,V_{1}):w\in{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V),\ V_{1}\subseteq\ker w,\ \dim V_{1}=n-2k\right\}.

This space can be identified with the projective bundle
\pi:{\mathbb{P}}(\wedge^{2}Q)\rightarrow G(n-2k,V)

\textstyle{{\mathbb{P}}(\wedge^{2}Q^{\vee})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

The most important property of

The fiber of

Proof.

By definition of

\displaystyle 0\subseteq F^{0}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq F^{l}=V

Now denote the restriction
\pi_{i}:=\pi|_{\pi^{-1}({\mathrm{Pf}}^{\circ}(2i,V))}:\pi^{-1}({\mathrm{Pf}}^{\circ}(2i,V))\rightarrow{\mathrm{Pf}}^{\circ}(2i,V)

\pi_{i}
is Zariski locally trivial. The universal skew form

\displaystyle\omega:V\otimes\mathcal{O}_{{\mathrm{Pf}}^{\circ}(2i,V)}\rightarrow V^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{{\mathrm{Pf}}^{\circ}(2i,V)}(1).

Denote its kernel by

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\mathcal{K}_{i}\rightarrow V\otimes\mathcal{O}_{{\mathrm{Pf}}^{\circ}(2i,V)}\rightarrow\mathcal{Q}_{i}\rightarrow 0

is the quotient bundle of rank

induces a nondegenerate skew form

\mathcal{Q}_{i}

Now for any point

\mathcal{K}_{i}

∎

4.
Modified stringy
E
-functions of Pfaffian varieties

Throughout this section, we assume that
n
is even. In the previous section, we computed the discrepancies
\alpha_{j,k,n}
of the exceptional divisors
D_{j}
of the log resolution
\pi:\widehat{\operatorname{Pf}(2k,V)}\rightarrow\operatorname{Pf}(2k,V)
. This allows us to compute the stringy
E
-function of
\operatorname{Pf}(2k,V)
. In fact, we have the following formula for
E_{\mathrm{st}}(\operatorname{Pf}(2k,V))
. However, we note that this is not the
correct
stringy
E
-function to establish the desired equality.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
V
be an
n
-dimensional vector space. The stringy
E
-function of
{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V)
is

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{st}}(\operatorname{Pf}(2k,V))=\frac{(uv)^{nk}-1}{uv-1}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{(uv)^{n+1-2j}-1}{(uv)^{2j}-1}

for
1\leq k\leq\frac{n}{2}
.

Since this result will not be used in the remainder of the paper, we provide only a sketch of the proof; see the paragraph immediately before section
4.1
. The proof is similar to that of Theorem
4.4
.

It is clear from the formula that the stringy
E
-function of the Pfaffian variety
\operatorname{Pf}(2k,V)
is
not
a polynomial, except for the extremal cases
k=1
and
n/2
. For example, when
k=2
and
n=6
, this formula gives

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{st}}(\operatorname{Pf}(4,6))=\frac{(uv)^{12}-1}{uv-1}\cdot\frac{((uv)^{3}-1)((uv)^{5}-1)}{((uv)^{2}-1)((uv)^{4}-1)}.

To obtain a genuine polynomial, it is necessary to modify the discrepancy in the formula for the stringy
E
-function.

Definition 4.2
.

We define the
modified discrepancy
of the log resolution
\pi:\widehat{\operatorname{Pf}(2k,V)}\rightarrow\operatorname{Pf}(2k,V)
to be

\displaystyle\widetilde{\alpha}_{j,k,n}=2j^{2}-j(n-2k+1)+\frac{n-2k-2}{2}

that differs from the usual discrepancy
\alpha_{j,k,n}
calculated in Theorem
3.5
by a linear polynomial
j-\frac{n-2k}{2}
.

Remark 4.3
.

The modified discrepancy
\widetilde{\alpha}_{j,k,n}
is strictly smaller than the usual discrepancy
\alpha_{j,k,n}
. It is well-known that in the process of a blow-up the discrepancy of the exceptional divisor is equal to the codimension of the center minus 1. Our modification of the discrepancy indicates that the subvarieties
{\mathrm{Pf}}(2i,V)\subseteq{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V)
somehow behave as if they were of smaller codimension than what they actually are.

We will use these modified discrepancies to compute the modified stringy
E
-function of the Pfaffian variety
{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V)
, which we denote by
\widetilde{E}_{\mathrm{st}}({\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V))
. The main result of this section is the following:

Theorem 4.4
.

Set
q=uv
. With the modified discrepancies described in Definition
4.2
, the modified stringy
E
-function of
{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V)
is

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}_{\mathrm{st}}(\operatorname{Pf}(2k,V))=\frac{q^{(n-1)k}-1}{q-1}\binom{n/2}{k}_{q^{2}}.

We will prove this formula by induction. The following technical lemma is crucial to the induction argument.

Lemma 4.5
.

For an even integer
n
and any
1\leq k\leq\frac{n-2}{2}
, there holds

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i\leq\frac{n}{2}}e_{2i}g_{n-2i,n}\prod_{j=k-i+1}^{\frac{n-2i}{2}}\frac{q^{2j}-1}{q^{2j-2k+2i}-1}=\frac{q^{(n-1)k}-1}{q-1}\prod_{j=k+1}^{\frac{n}{2}}\frac{q^{2j}-1}{q^{2j-2k}-1}

where
e_{2i}
denotes the
E
-function of the variety of
nondegenerate
skew forms on
{\mathbb{C}}^{2i}
up to scaling, and
g_{n-2i,n}:=\binom{n}{n-2i}_{q}
denotes the
E
-function of the Grassmannian
G(n-2i,n)
.

Proof.

We prove this equality by induction on
n+k
. We omit the proof of the base case, since it is straightforward.

Assume
1<k<\frac{n-2}{2}
(the extremal cases
k=1
and
k=\frac{n-2}{2}
are again straightforward since there is only one nonzero term in the sum on the left side). The induction hypothesis gives

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i\leq\frac{n}{2}}e_{2i}g_{n-2i,n}\prod_{j=k-i}^{\frac{n-2i}{2}}\frac{q^{2j}-1}{q^{2j-2k+2+2i}-1}=\frac{q^{(n-1)(k-1)}-1}{q-1}\prod_{j=k}^{\frac{n}{2}}\frac{q^{2j}-1}{q^{2j-2k+2}-1}

and

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i\leq\frac{n-2}{2}}e_{2i}g_{n-2-2i,n-2}\prod_{j=k-i+1}^{\frac{n-2-2i}{2}}\frac{q^{2j}-1}{q^{2j-2k+2i}-1}=\frac{q^{(n-3)k}-1}{q-1}\prod_{j=k+1}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\frac{q^{2j}-1}{q^{2j-2k}-1}

which correspond to the cases
(k-1,n)
and
(k,n-2)
respectively. We rewrite them as

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i\leq\frac{n}{2}}

\displaystyle\frac{q^{2k-2i}-1}{q^{n-2k+2}-1}e_{2i}g_{n-2i,n}\prod_{j=k-i+1}^{\frac{n-2i}{2}}\frac{q^{2j}-1}{q^{2j-2k+2i}-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{(n-1)(k-1)}-1}{q-1}\prod_{j=k}^{\frac{n}{2}}\frac{q^{2j}-1}{q^{2j-2k+2}-1}

and

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i\leq\frac{n-2}{2}}

\displaystyle\frac{(q^{n-2k}-1)(q^{n-2i-1}-1)}{(q^{n}-1)(q^{n-1}-1)}e_{2i}g_{n-2-2i,n-2}\prod_{j=k-i+1}^{\frac{n-2i}{2}}\frac{q^{2j}-1}{q^{2j-2k+2i}-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{(n-3)k}-1}{q-1}\prod_{j=k+1}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\frac{q^{2j}-1}{q^{2j-2k}-1}.

Taking the linear combination of these two equations with coefficients

\displaystyle q^{n}(q^{n-2k+2}-1)\quad\text{and}\quad-\frac{q^{2k+1}(q^{n}-1)(q^{n-1}-1)}{q^{n-2k}-1}

respectively yields the desired equality.

∎

Proof of Theorem
4.4
.

Consider the strata
\operatorname{Pf}^{\circ}(2i,V)\subseteq\operatorname{Pf}(2k,V)
. By Proposition
3.4
, the resolution
\pi:\widehat{\operatorname{Pf}(2k,V)}\rightarrow\operatorname{Pf}(2k,V)
is Zariski locally trivial over
\operatorname{Pf}^{\circ}(2i,V)
, hence the local contribution is

\displaystyle E(\operatorname{Pf}^{\circ}(2i,V))\widetilde{E}_{\mathrm{st}}(\operatorname{Pf}(2k-2i,\operatorname{ker}w))\frac{q-1}{q^{\widetilde{\alpha}_{\frac{n-2i}{2},k,n}+1}-1}.

Note that
2j=n-2i
. By the induction hypothesis this equals

\displaystyle E(\operatorname{Pf}^{\circ}(2i,V))\frac{q^{(n-2i-1)(k-i)}-1}{q-1}\prod_{j=k-i+1}^{\frac{n-2i}{2}}\frac{q^{2j}-1}{q^{2j-2k+2i}-1}\cdot\frac{q-1}{q^{\widetilde{\alpha}_{\frac{n-2i}{2},k,n}+1}-1}.

Since
\widetilde{\alpha}_{\frac{n-2i}{2},k,n}=(n-2i-1)(k-i)-1
, this equals

\displaystyle e_{2i}g_{n-2i,n}\prod_{j=k-i+1}^{\frac{n-2i}{2}}\frac{q^{2j}-1}{q^{2j-2k+2i}-1}.

Therefore, the modified stringy
E
-function is

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}_{\mathrm{st}}(\operatorname{Pf}(2k,V))

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i\leq k}e_{2i}g_{n-2i,n}\prod_{j=k-i+1}^{\frac{n-2i}{2}}\frac{q^{2j}-1}{q^{2j-2k+2i}-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i\leq\frac{n}{2}}e_{2i}g_{n-2i,n}\prod_{j=k-i+1}^{\frac{n-2i}{2}}\frac{q^{2j}-1}{q^{2j-2k+2i}-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{(n-1)k}-1}{q-1}\prod_{j=k+1}^{\frac{n}{2}}\frac{q^{2j}-1}{q^{2j-2k}-1}.

∎

Sketch of the proof of Theorem
4.1
.

The proof is almost identical to the previous proof. The only difference is that we replace the modified discrepancies
\widetilde{\alpha}_{j,k,n}
by the usual discrepancies
\alpha_{j,k,n}
in the computation of the local contribution. The identity in Lemma
4.5
is replaced by

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i\leq\frac{n}{2}}e_{2i}g_{n-2i,n}\prod_{j=1}^{k-i}\frac{q^{n-2i+1-2j}-1}{q^{2j}-1}=\frac{q^{nk}-1}{q-1}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{q^{n+1-2j}-1}{q^{2j}-1}

and can also be proved by induction on

. More precisely, one replaces the factors of the inductive assumptions corresponding to
(k-1,n)
and

by

\displaystyle\frac{q^{2k-2i}-1}{q^{n-2k+1}-1}\ \text{ and }\ \frac{(q^{n-2k-1}-1)(q^{n-2i}-1)}{(q^{n}-1)(q^{n-1}-1)}

and takes the linear combination with coefficients

\displaystyle q^{n}(q^{n-2k+1}-1)\ \text{ and }\ -\frac{q^{2k}(q^{n}-1)(q^{n-1}-1)}{q^{n-2k-1}-1}.

4.1.
Modified stringy
E
-functions as stringy
E
-functions of pairs

We end this section with a discussion of a speculative geometric explanation of the modified
E
-functions. The definition of stringy
E
-functions can be generalized to pairs
(X,\mathcal{I})
where
\mathcal{I}
is an ideal sheaf on
X
. In this case, the stringy
E
-function is defined in the usual way, except that the discrepancies are defined as

\displaystyle\widetilde{a_{j}}=a_{j}-\mathrm{ord}_{D_{j}}(\mathcal{I})

where
a_{j}
is the usual discrepancy of the exceptional divisor
D_{j}
, and the extra term comes from the vanishing order of the ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}
along
D_{j}
. A proof of the fact that this definition is independent of the choice of the log resolution can be found in
[
10
, Appendix A]
.

The modified
E
-function of
{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,n)
we defined here is precisely the stringy
E
-function of the pair
({\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,n),\mathcal{I}_{{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k-2,n)})
, where
\mathcal{I}_{{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k-2,n)}
is the ideal sheaf of the next lower-rank Pfaffian locus inside
{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,n)
. To see this, note that at a general point of the divisor
D_{j}
, the complete skew form is given by a flag
0\subseteq F^{0}\subseteq K\subseteq V
where the dimensions of
F^{0}
and
K
are
n-2k
and
2j
respectively, together with nondegenerate skew forms
\alpha
and
\beta
on the quotients
V/K
and
K/F^{0}
. Choose a curve on the log resolution
\widehat{{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,n)}
that intersects
D_{j}
transversely. After choosing a splitting, its image under
\pi
can be written as

\displaystyle\omega_{t}=\alpha+t\beta+O(t^{2}).

Note that the ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}_{{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k-2,n)}
is generated by the Pfaffians of size
2k
, or equivalently by the coordinates of
\bigwedge^{k}\omega
. It is easy to see that the leading term of
\wedge^{k}\omega
is given by
t^{k-\frac{n}{2}+j}\bigwedge^{\frac{n}{2}-j}\alpha\wedge\bigwedge^{k-\frac{n}{2}+j}\beta
. Therefore every generator of the pullback of the ideal sheaf vanishes to order
k-\frac{n}{2}+j
along
D_{j}
. This matches with the difference between the modified and the usual discrepancies; see Definition
4.2
. Therefore we have the equality

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}_{\mathrm{st}}({\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V))=E_{\mathrm{st}}({\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V),\mathcal{I}_{{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k-2,V)}).

Comparison of stringy

-functions of Pfaffian double mirrors

Theorem 5.1
.

-functions of
X_{W}
and
Y_{W}

\displaystyle q^{(n-1)k}\widetilde{E}_{\mathrm{st}}(Y_{W})-q^{l}\widetilde{E}_{\mathrm{st}}(X_{W})=\frac{q^{l}-q^{(n-1)k}}{q-1}\binom{n/2}{k}_{q^{2}}

where

The idea of the proof is to consider the universal hypersurface
H\subseteq{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V^{\vee})\times{\mathbb{P}}W
defined by

\displaystyle H:=\left\{(w,\alpha)\in{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V^{\vee})\times{\mathbb{P}}W:\langle w,\alpha\rangle=0\right\}

and the two projections
\pi_{1}:H\rightarrow{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V^{\vee})
and
\pi_{2}:H\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}W
. The modified stringy
E
-function of

can be computed in two different ways, which gives the desired equality. Here, the modified stringy
E
-function of

is defined similarly to that of the Pfaffian varieties as follows.

First note that the log resolution of
{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V^{\vee})
we used in this paper naturally induces a log resolution of

along the product
\widehat{{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V^{\vee})}\times{\mathbb{P}}W\rightarrow{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V^{\vee})\times{\mathbb{P}}W
, or equivalently, the fiber product
H\times_{{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V^{\vee})}\widehat{{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V^{\vee})}
. The exceptional divisors of this log resolution are the pullbacks of the exceptional divisors of
{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V^{\vee})
, and the modified discrepancies are defined to be the same as those of
{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V^{\vee})
. More precisely, let
F_{j}
be the pullback of the exceptional divisor
D_{j}
of
\widehat{{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V^{\vee})}
, and denote
F_{J}^{\circ}=(\bigcap_{j\in J}F_{j})\backslash(\bigcup_{j\not\in J}F_{j})
. Then the modified stringy
E
-function of

is defined to be

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}_{\mathrm{st}}(H)=\sum_{J}E(F_{J}^{\circ})\prod_{j\in J}\frac{q-1}{q^{\widetilde{\alpha}_{j,k,n}+1}-1}.

(5.1)

Lemma 5.2
.

The fiber of
\pi_{1}
over a point
w\in{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V^{\vee})
is
{\mathbb{P}}W
if
w\in X_{W}
, and is a hyperplane
{\mathbb{P}}^{l-2}
in
{\mathbb{P}}W
if
w\not\in X_{W}
. Moreover, we have the equality of modified stringy
E
-functions

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}_{\mathrm{st}}(H)=\frac{q^{l-1}-1}{q-1}\frac{q^{(n-1)k}-1}{q-1}\prod_{j=k+1}^{\frac{n}{2}}\frac{q^{2j}-1}{q^{2j-2k}-1}+q^{l-1}\widetilde{E}_{\mathrm{st}}(X_{W}).

Proof.

There are two possibilities for the fiber of
\pi_{1}
over a point
w\in{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V^{\vee})
. If

, then
w
is annihilated by all forms in

, so the fiber
\pi_{1}^{-1}(w)
is the whole space
{\mathbb{P}}W={\mathbb{P}}^{l-1}

Now by the definition (

\begin{split}\widetilde{E}_{\mathrm{st}}(H)&=\sum_{J}E(D_{J}^{\circ}\cap\pi^{-1}({\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V^{\vee})\backslash X_{W}))E({\mathbb{P}}^{l-2})\prod_{j\in J}\frac{q-1}{q^{\widetilde{\alpha}_{j,k,n}+1}-1}\\
&\quad\quad+\sum_{J}E(D_{J}^{\circ}\cap\pi^{-1}(X_{W}))E({\mathbb{P}}^{l-1})\prod_{j\in J}\frac{q-1}{q^{\widetilde{\alpha}_{j,k,n}+1}-1}\\

6.
Relations to Homological Projective Duality for Pfaffian varieties

In this section, we make connections between our main results in this paper and the Homological Projective Duality for Pfaffian varieties. We propose a conjectural description for the shape of the Lefschetz decompositions of the
{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V^{\vee})
and its dual
{\mathrm{Pf}}(n-2k,V)
, and show its compatibility with Theorem
5.1
.

6.1.
Generalities on Homological Projective Duality

We start with a brief review of general definitions and facts about Homological Projective Duality. Let
X
be a smooth projective variety, and
f:X\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}(V)
be a morphism where
V
is an
N
-dimensional vector space. A Lefschetz decomposition of
D^{b}(X)
with respect to
\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)=f^{*}\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}(V)}(1)
is a semiorthogonal decomposition of the form

\displaystyle D^{b}(X)=\langle\mathcal{A}_{0},\mathcal{A}_{1}(1),\cdots,\mathcal{A}_{i-1}(i-1)\rangle

where
\mathcal{A}_{0}\supseteq\mathcal{A}_{1}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\mathcal{A}_{i-1}
are admissible subcategories of
D^{b}(X)
. We say that such a decomposition is
rectangular
if all blocks
\mathcal{A}_{i}
are equal. Moreover, we say that a block
\mathcal{A}
in the Lefschetz decomposition
has size
r
if its Grothendieck group is a free abelian group of rank
r
. In many examples these blocks admit exceptional collections of length equal to their size.

We define the universal hyperplane section
\mathcal{X}\subseteq X\times{\mathbb{P}}(V^{\vee})
to be the subvariety consisting of pairs
(x,u)
with
\langle x,u\rangle=0
, where
\langle-,-\rangle
is the natural pairing between
V
and
V^{\vee}
.

A smooth projective variety
Y
together with a morphism
g:Y\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}(V^{\vee})
is called homologically projectively dual of
f:X\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}(V)
if there is a fully faithful Fourier-Mukai transform
\Phi:D^{b}(Y)\hookrightarrow D^{b}(\mathcal{X})
such that there is a semiorthogonal decomposition

\displaystyle D^{b}(\mathcal{X})=\langle\Phi(D^{b}(Y)),\mathcal{A}_{1}(1)\boxtimes D^{b}({\mathbb{P}}(V^{\vee})),\cdots,\mathcal{A}_{i-1}(i-1)\boxtimes D^{b}({\mathbb{P}}(V^{\vee}))\rangle.

In this case the homological projective dual
g:Y\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}(V^{\vee})
admits a dual Lefschetz decomposition

\displaystyle D^{b}(Y)=\langle\mathcal{B}_{j-1}(-j+1),\cdots,\mathcal{B}_{1}(-1),\mathcal{B}_{0}\rangle.

The main theorem of Homological Projective Duality is a remarkable relation between the derived categories of the linear sections of the dual varieties
X
and
Y
. More precisely, for any linear subspace
W\subseteq V^{\vee}
with
\dim W=l
, if both
X_{W}:=X\cap{\mathbb{P}}(W^{\perp})
and
Y_{W}:=Y\cap{\mathbb{P}}(W)
are of the expected dimensions, then there are semiorthogonal decompositions

\displaystyle D^{b}(X_{W})=\langle\mathcal{C}_{W},\mathcal{A}_{l}(1),\cdots,\mathcal{A}_{i-1}(i-l)\rangle

and

\displaystyle D^{b}(Y_{W})=\langle\mathcal{B}_{j-1}(N-l-j),\cdots,\mathcal{B}_{N-l}(-1),\mathcal{C}_{W}\rangle.

Apart from the blocks inherited from the ambient varieties
X
and
Y
, there is a component
\mathcal{C}_{W}
(the “interesting part”) in both of the decompositions that plays a similar role to the primitive cohomology in Hodge theory.

In general, the varieties
X
and
Y
are not necessarily smooth. In the case of a singular variety, one needs to replace the usual derived category by some kind of categorical resolution of the singularities.

As we have seen, the Pfaffian varieties
{\mathrm{Pf}}(2k,V)
are highly singular, hence we have to replace them by certain categorical crepant resolutions

n

{\mathrm{Pf}}(2\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor-2k,V)\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}(\wedge^{2}V^{\vee})

6.2.
Odd-dimensional cases

We start by analyzing the odd-dimensional cases. A rectangular Lefschetz decomposition of the categorical crepant resolution of

\displaystyle\frac{c_{s+1}}{c_{s}}=q^{4a-2b}\cdot\frac{(1-q^{-2a+2s})(1-q^{-2a+1+2s})}{(1-q^{2s+2})(1-q^{1-2b+2s})}.

Comparing with (
A.1
), we can write the sum on the left side in terms of hypergeometric functions:

\displaystyle\binom{2b}{2a}_{q}\cdot\ _{2}\phi_{1}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}q^{-2a},q^{-2a+1}\\
q^{1-2b}\end{array};q^{2},q^{4a-2b}\right).

Applying identity (
A.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\binom{2b}{2a}_{q}\frac{(q^{2a-2b};q^{2})_{a}}{(q^{-2b+1};q^{2})_{a}}=q^{2a^{2}-a}\frac{(q^{2b-4a+2};q^{2})_{2a}}{(q;q)_{2a}}

by a direct computation.

Taking
a=k-j
and
b=\frac{n}{2}-j
in this identity, we obtain

\sum_{s=0}^{k-j}(-1)^{s}q^{s^{2}-s}\binom{n-2j-2s}{2k-2s-2j}_{q}\binom{\frac{n}{2}-j}{s}_{q^{2}}\\
=q^{2(k-j)^{2}-(k-j)}\frac{(q^{n+2-4k+2j};q^{2})_{2k-2j}}{(q;q)_{2k-2j}}.

Therefore we have

\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{k}

\displaystyle g_{n-2k,n-2p}f_{p,i,n}^{\circ}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=1}^{k}g_{n-2k,n-2p}\sum_{1\leq j\leq p}f_{j,i,n}(-1)^{p-j}q^{(p-j)(p-j-1)}\binom{\frac{n}{2}-j}{p-j}_{q^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq j\leq k}f_{j,i,n}\left(\sum_{j\leq p\leq k}g_{n-2k,n-2p}(-1)^{p-j}q^{(p-j)(p-j-1)}\binom{\frac{n}{2}-j}{p-j}_{q^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq j\leq k}f_{j,i,n}\left(\sum_{j\leq p\leq k}\binom{n-2p}{n-2k}_{q}(-1)^{p-j}q^{(p-j)(p-j-1)}\binom{\frac{n}{2}-j}{p-j}_{q^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq j\leq k}f_{j,i,n}\left(\sum_{0\leq s\leq k-j}(-1)^{s}q^{s^{2}-s}\binom{n-2j-2s}{2k-2s-2j}_{q}\binom{\frac{n}{2}-j}{s}_{q^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}f_{j,i,n}q^{2(k-j)^{2}-(k-j)}\frac{(q^{n+2-4k+2j};q^{2})_{2k-2j}}{(q;q)_{2k-2j}}

Finally, note that the coefficient of
f_{k,i,n}
on the left side is
1
. This fact allows us to solve for
f_{j,i,n}
recursively.
∎

Proposition B.6
.

We have the following formula for
f_{k,i,n}
:

\displaystyle f_{k,i,n}

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{(n-1)k-1}-1}{q-1}\prod_{j=k+1}^{\frac{n}{2}}\frac{q^{2j}-1}{q^{2j-2k}-1}

\displaystyle\quad+q^{(n-1)k-1}\prod_{j=\frac{n}{2}-k-i+1}^{\frac{n-2i}{2}}\frac{q^{2j}-1}{q^{2j-n+2k+2i}-1}

Proof.

In terms of
q
-binomial symbols the statement of the proposition can be written as

\displaystyle f_{k,i,n}=\frac{q^{(n-1)k-1}-1}{q-1}\binom{\frac{n}{2}}{k}_{q^{2}}+q^{(n-1)k-1}\binom{\frac{n-2i}{2}}{k}_{q^{2}}.

(B.1)

According to Proposition
B.5
, it suffices to prove that (
B.1
) satisfies the equations therein. We write

\displaystyle A_{k,n}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{q^{(n-1)j-1}-1}{q-1}\binom{\frac{n}{2}}{j}_{q^{2}}q^{2(k-j)^{2}-(k-j)}\frac{(q^{n+2-4k+2j};q^{2})_{2k-2j}}{(q;q)_{2k-2j}}

\displaystyle B_{k,i,n}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{\frac{n-2i}{2}}{j}_{q^{2}}q^{2(k-j)^{2}-(k-j)+(n-1)j-1}\frac{(q^{n+2-4k+2j};q^{2})_{2k-2j}}{(q;q)_{2k-2j}}

\displaystyle C_{k,n}=\frac{q^{2k^{2}-k-1}-1}{q-1}\binom{n}{2k}_{q}

\displaystyle D_{k,i,n}=q^{2k^{2}-k-1}\sum_{0\leq r\leq 2k}\binom{n-2i}{r}_{q}q^{(2k-r)(n-2i-r)}\frac{\prod_{j=i+r+1-2k}^{i}(1-q^{2j})}{\prod_{j=1}^{2k-r}(1-q^{j})}

It suffices to prove
A_{k,n}+B_{k,i,n}=C_{k,n}+D_{k,i,n}
for all
(k,i,n)
. We claim

\displaystyle A_{k,n}=C_{k,n},\quad B_{k,i,n}=D_{k,i,n}.

First we prove
A_{k,n}=C_{k,n}
. Consider

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k}c_{j}:=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{\frac{n}{2}}{j}_{q^{2}}q^{2(k-j)^{2}-(k-j)}\frac{(q^{n+2-4k+2j};q^{2})_{2k-2j}}{(q;q)_{2k-2j}}.

By a direct computation we see

\displaystyle\frac{c_{j+1}}{c_{j}}=q^{2}\cdot\frac{(1-q^{2j+1-2k})(1-q^{2j-2k})}{(1-q^{2j+2})(1-q^{n+2-4k+2j})}.

Comparing with (
A.1
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k}c_{j}

\displaystyle=q^{2k^{2}-k}\frac{(q^{n+2-4k};q^{2})_{2k}}{(q;q)_{2k}}\cdot\ _{2}\phi_{1}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}q^{-2k},q^{-2k+1}\\
q^{n+2-4k}\end{array};q^{2},q^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=q^{2k^{2}-k}\frac{(q^{n+2-4k};q^{2})_{2k}}{(q;q)_{2k}}\frac{(q^{n-2k+1};q^{2})_{k}}{(q^{n+2-4k};q^{2})_{k}}(q^{-2k+1})^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{(q^{n+2-4k};q^{2})_{2k}}{(q;q)_{2k}}\frac{(q^{n-2k+1};q^{2})_{k}}{(q^{n+2-4k};q^{2})_{k}}.

Similarly we consider

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k}q^{(n-1)j-1}\cdot c_{j}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{\frac{n}{2}}{j}_{q^{2}}q^{2(k-j)^{2}-(k-j)+(n-1)j-1}\frac{(q^{n+2-4k+2j};q^{2})_{2k-2j}}{(q;q)_{2k-2j}}.

A similar computation yields

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k}q^{(n-1)j-1}\cdot c_{j}

\displaystyle=q^{2k^{2}-k-1}\frac{(q^{n+2-4k};q^{2})_{2k}}{(q;q)_{2k}}\cdot\ _{2}\phi_{1}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}q^{-2k},q^{-2k+1}\\
q^{n+2-4k}\end{array};q^{2},q^{n+1}\right)

\displaystyle=q^{2k^{2}-k-1}\frac{(q^{n+2-4k};q^{2})_{2k}}{(q;q)_{2k}}\frac{(q^{n-2k+1};q^{2})_{k}}{(q^{n+2-4k};q^{2})_{k}}.

Therefore we have

\displaystyle(1-q)A_{k,n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}c_{j}-\sum_{j=0}^{k}q^{(n-1)j-1}\cdot c_{j}

\displaystyle=(1-q^{2k^{2}-k-1})\frac{(q^{n+2-4k};q^{2})_{2k}}{(q;q)_{2k}}\frac{(q^{n-2k+1};q^{2})_{k}}{(q^{n+2-4k};q^{2})_{k}}

\displaystyle=(1-q^{2k^{2}-k-1})\binom{n}{2k}_{q}

where the last step is a direct computation. This proves
A_{k,n}=C_{k,n}
.

Now we prove the second equality
B_{k,i,n}=D_{k,i,n}
. Again, we write
B_{k,i,n}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}d_{j}
. The ratio of the consecutive terms is

\displaystyle\frac{d_{j+1}}{d_{j}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-q^{n-2i-2j}}{1-q^{2j+2}}\cdot q^{3+4j-4k+n-1}\frac{(1-q^{2k-2j-1})(1-q^{2k-2j})}{(1-q^{n+2-4k+2j})(1-q^{n-2j})}

\displaystyle=q^{n-2i+1}\frac{(1-q^{-n+2i+2j})(1-q^{-2k+1+2j})(1-q^{-2k+2j})}{(1-q^{2j+2})(1-q^{n+2-4k+2j})(1-q^{-n+2j})}.

Therefore

\displaystyle B_{k,i,n}

\displaystyle=q^{2k^{2}-k-1}\frac{(q^{n+2-4k};q^{2})_{2k}}{(q;q)_{2k}}

\displaystyle\qquad{}\cdot{}_{3}\phi_{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}q^{-2k},q^{-n+2i},q^{-2k+1}\\
q^{-n},q^{n+2-4k}\end{array};q^{2},q^{n-2i+1}\right)

\displaystyle=q^{2k^{2}-k-1}\frac{(q^{n+2-4k};q^{2})_{2k}}{(q;q)_{2k}}\frac{(q^{n+1-2k};q^{2})_{k}}{(q^{n+2-4k};q^{2})_{2k}}

\displaystyle\qquad{}\cdot{}_{3}\phi_{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}q^{-2k},q^{-2k+1},q^{-2i}\\
q^{-n},q^{1-n}\end{array};q^{2},q^{2}\right)

Similarly,

\displaystyle D_{k,i,n}=q^{2k^{2}-k-1}\binom{n-2i}{2k}_{q}\ {}_{3}\phi_{1}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}q^{-2k},q^{-i},q^{-i}\\
q^{n+1-2i-2k}\end{array};q,-q^{n+1}\right)

Applying (
A.3
) with the parameters
(a,b,d,n,q)=(q^{-2i},q^{-i},q^{-n},2k,q)
, we obtain

\displaystyle D_{k,i,n}

\displaystyle=q^{2k^{2}-k-1}\binom{n-2i}{2k}_{q}\frac{(q^{-n};q)_{2k}}{(q^{-n+2i};q)_{2k}}q^{4ki}

\displaystyle\qquad{}\cdot{}_{3}\phi_{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}q^{-2k},q^{1-2k},q^{-2i}\\
q^{-n},q^{1-n}\end{array};q^{2},q^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=q^{2k^{2}-k-1}\binom{n}{2k}_{q}\ {}_{3}\phi_{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}q^{-2k},q^{1-2k},q^{-2i}\\
q^{-n},q^{1-n}\end{array};q^{2},q^{2}\right)

Now the desired equality follows from

\displaystyle\frac{(q^{n+2-4k};q^{2})_{2k}}{(q;q)_{2k}}\frac{(q^{n+1-2k};q^{2})_{k}}{(q^{n+2-4k};q^{2})_{2k}}=\binom{n}{2k}_{q}

which is a direct computation.
∎

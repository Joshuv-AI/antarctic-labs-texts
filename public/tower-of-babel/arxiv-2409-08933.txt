Dynamical study of T s ​ s T_{ss} systems at a chiral quark model
Jiazheng Ji, Yuheng Xing, Xinxing Wu, Ning Xu, Yue Tan

Abstract
Since the discovery of T c ​ c T_{cc} by LHCb, there has been considerable interest in T c ​ c T_{cc} and its heavy-flavor partners. However, the study of its strange partner T s ​ s T_{ss} has been largely overlooked. Within the framework of the chiral quark model, we conducted a systematic study of the bound states of T s ​ s T_{ss} utilizing the Gaussian Expansion Method. Considering all physical channels with 01 + 01^{+} , including molecular and diquark structures. Our calculations revealed that upon considering the coupling between diquarks and molecular states, we identified a deep bound state with a bounding energy of 60 MeV, primarily composed of K ​ K ∗ KK^{*} . Using the P 0 3 {}^{3}P_{0} model, we calculated the decay width of K ∗ K^{*} within the K ​ K ∗ KK^{*} bound state, which is approximated as the decay width of the bound state in the T s ​ s T_{ss} system. The results indicate that due to the effect of binding energy, the decay width of K ∗ K^{*} in K ​ K ∗ KK^{*} is approximately 3 3 MeV smaller than that of K ∗ K^{*} in vacuum. Additionally, resonance state calculations were performed. Utilizing the real-scaling method, we searched for possible resonance states in the T s ​ s T_{ss} sysytem. Due to the strong attraction in the [ K ∗ ] 8 ​ [ K ∗ ] 8 [K^{*}]_{8}[K^{*}]_{8} configuration, four resonance states were found in the vicinity of 2.2 2.2 - 2.8 2.8 GeV, predominantly featuring hidden-color structures, and their decay widths are all less than 10 10 MeV. We strongly recommend experimental efforts to search for the resonance states in the T s ​ s T_{ss} system predicted by our calculations.

Figure 1:
Two forms of stable states, bound state (Horizontal line) and resonance state (avoiding-crossing structure), in the real-scaling method. The red line represents the threshold, while the light blue line indicates the bound state. The avoided-crossing structure formed by the red line, representing the scattering state, and the green line, representing the resonance, corresponds to a genuine resonance.

The real-scaling method involves systematically scaling the width of Gaussian functions between two groups using a scaling factor, denoted as
\alpha

increases, the width of the Gaussian functions expands, leading to variations in the energy of system. If a stable structure is present, it remains unaffected by the changes in Gaussian function widths. Within the framework of this method, bound states do not exhibit decay behavior due to the absence of corresponding decay channels, and thus manifest as horizontal lines, shown in FIG.
1
(a). Conversely, resonance states, having several decay channels, strongly couple with scattering channels and decay to the relevant threshold channels. This is depicted in FIG.
1
(b) by two downward-sloping lines that demonstrate avoided crossing behavior. Since resonance states are stable structures, the avoid-crossing structure may reappear as the scaling factor
\alpha
increases. However, a larger
\alpha
results in a more complex calculation process. Therefore, for computational efficiency, we chose the range of
\alpha
to be between
1.0
and
3.0
. Within this range, most resonance states will exhibit repeated avoid-crossing structures. The real-scaling method allows for the calculation of the decay width of stable states. The calculation equation is as follows,

\Gamma=4|V(\alpha_{c})|\frac{\sqrt{|Slope_{r}||Slope_{s}|}}{|Slope_{r}-Slope_{s}|}.

(21)

Based on the discussion above, in the framework of real-scaling method, avoided-crossing structure represents resonant state, while horizontal line indicates bound state. Therefore, the decay width calculation Eq.
21
is applicable only to the decay width of resonant states. Here,
\alpha_{c}
denotes the
\alpha
coordinate corresponding to the avoid-crossing structure, while
V(\alpha_{c})
represents the energy difference at the avoid-crossing point between the two lines. The ”
slope_{r}
” and ”
slope_{s}
” indicate the slope of these two lines.

IV
{}^{3}P_{0}
model

The
{}^{3}P_{0}
model is extensively employed in the calculation of meson decay widths
Micu:1968mk
;
LeYaouanc:1972vsx
. It posits that a meson in the vacuum excites a pair of quark-antiquark, with momenta
\mathbf{p}_{3}
and
\mathbf{p}_{4}
, respectively. The probability of their production is determined by a parameter
\gamma
, which is generally determined by an overall fitting of the strong decay width of hadrons. In this way, one obtains
\gamma=6.95
for
u\bar{u}
and
d\bar{d}
pair creation, and
\gamma=6.95/\sqrt{3}
for
s\bar{s}
pair creation
LeYaouanc:1977gm
. The flavor wave function of the quark-antiquark pair is given by
\phi_{0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(u\bar{u}+d\bar{d}+s\bar{s})
, and the color wave function is
\omega_{0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(r\bar{r}+g\bar{g}+b\bar{b})
. The operator in the model is,

\displaystyle T=-3~\gamma\sum_{m}\langle 1m1-m|00\rangle\int d\mathbf{p}_{3}d\mathbf{p}_{4}\delta^{3}(\mathbf{p}_{3}+\mathbf{p}_{4})

\displaystyle~~~~\times{\cal{Y}}^{m}_{1}(\frac{\mathbf{p}_{3}-\mathbf{p}_{4}}{2})\chi^{34}_{1-m}\phi^{34}_{0}\omega^{34}_{0}b^{\dagger}_{3}(\mathbf{p}_{3})d^{\dagger}_{4}(\mathbf{p}_{4}),

(22)

The S-matrix element for the process
A\rightarrow B+C
is written as

\langle BC|T|A\rangle=\delta^{3}(\mathbf{P}_{A}-\mathbf{P}_{B}-\mathbf{P}_{C}){\cal{M}}^{M_{J_{A}}M_{J_{B}}M_{J_{C}}},

(23)

where
\mathbf{P}_{B}
and
\mathbf{P}_{C}
are the momenta of B and C mesons in the final state, and satisfy
\mathbf{P}_{A}=\mathbf{P}_{B}+\mathbf{P}_{C}=0
in the center-of-mass frame of meson A.
{\cal{M}}^{M_{J_{A}}M_{J_{B}}M_{J_{C}}}
is the helicity amplitude of the process
A\rightarrow B+C
, which can be obtained as

\displaystyle{\cal{M}}^{M_{J_{A}}M_{J_{B}}M_{J_{C}}}(\mathbf{P})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma\sqrt{8E_{A}E_{B}E_{C}}\sum_{\begin{array}[t]{l}\scriptstyle M_{L_{A}},M_{S_{A}},\\
\scriptstyle M_{L_{B}},M_{S_{B}},\\
\scriptstyle M_{L_{C}},M_{S_{C}},m\end{array}}\!\!\langle L_{A}M_{L_{A}}S_{A}M_{S_{A}}|J_{A}M_{J_{A}}\rangle\langle L_{B}M_{L_{B}}S_{B}M_{S_{B}}|J_{B}M_{J_{B}}\rangle

(28)

\displaystyle\times\langle L_{C}M_{L_{C}}S_{C}M_{S_{C}}|J_{C}M_{J_{C}}\rangle\langle 1m1-m|00\rangle\langle\chi^{14}_{S_{B}M_{S_{B}}}\chi^{32}_{S_{C}M_{S_{C}}}|\chi^{12}_{S_{A}M_{S_{A}}}\chi^{34}_{1-m}\rangle

\displaystyle\times[\langle\phi^{14}_{B}\phi^{32}_{C}|\phi^{12}_{A}\phi^{34}_{0}\rangle\mathcal{I}^{M_{L_{A}},m}_{M_{L_{B}},M_{L_{C}}}(\mathbf{P},m_{1},m_{2},m_{3})

\displaystyle+(-1)^{1+S_{A}+S_{B}+S_{C}}\langle\phi^{32}_{B}\phi^{14}_{C}|\phi^{12}_{A}\phi^{34}_{0}\rangle\mathcal{I}^{M_{L_{A}},m}_{M_{L_{B}},M_{L_{C}}}(-\mathbf{P},m_{2},m_{1},m_{3})],

with the momentum space integral

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{M_{L_{A}},m}_{M_{L_{B}},M_{L_{C}}}(\mathbf{P},m_{1},m_{2},m_{3})=\int d\mathbf{p}\,\mbox{}\psi^{\ast}_{n_{B}L_{B}M_{L_{B}}}({\scriptstyle\frac{m_{3}}{m_{1}+m_{3}}}\mathbf{P}+\mathbf{p})\psi^{\ast}_{n_{C}L_{C}M_{L_{C}}}({\scriptstyle\frac{m_{3}}{m_{2}+m_{3}}}\mathbf{P}+\mathbf{p})\psi_{n_{A}L_{A}M_{L_{A}}}(\mathbf{P}+\mathbf{p}){\cal{Y}}^{m}_{1}(\mathbf{p}),

(29)

where
\mathbf{P}=\mathbf{P}_{B}=-\mathbf{P}_{C}
, and
\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{p}_{3}
,
m_{3}
is the mass of the created quark
q_{3}
. To analyze the results and to compare the theoretical results with experimental data, the partial wave amplitude
{\mathcal{M}}^{JL}(A\rightarrow BC)
is often employed. It is related with the helicity amplitude by the Jacob-Wick formula
Jacob:1959at
,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}^{JL}(A\rightarrow BC)=\frac{\sqrt{2L+1}}{2J_{A}+1}\!\!\sum_{M_{J_{B}},M_{J_{C}}}\langle L0JM_{J_{A}}|J_{A}M_{J_{A}}\rangle

\displaystyle\times\langle J_{B}M_{J_{B}}J_{C}M_{J_{C}}|JM_{J_{A}}\rangle\mathcal{M}^{M_{J_{A}}M_{J_{B}}M_{J_{C}}}({\textbf{P}}).

(30)

Finally, the Eq.
31
for calculating the decay width is as follows,

\Gamma=\pi^{2}\frac{{|\textbf{P}|}}{M_{A}^{2}}\sum_{JL}\Big|\mathcal{M}^{JL}\Big|^{2},

(31)

where nonrelativistic phase-space is assumed,

|\textbf{P}|=\frac{\sqrt{[M^{2}_{A}-(M_{B}+M_{C})^{2}][M^{2}_{A}-(M_{B}-M_{C})^{2}]}}{2M_{A}},

(32)

with
M_{A}
,
M_{B}
,
M_{C}
being the masses of the mesons involved. In this work, for calculational convenience, we transformed the transition operator
T
from momentum space to coordinate space. The specific form is as follows,

\displaystyle T_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-3\gamma\sum_{m}\langle 1m1-m|00\rangle\int d\mathbf{r_{3}}d\mathbf{r_{4}}(\frac{1}{2\pi})^{\frac{3}{2}}ir2^{-\frac{5}{2}}f^{-5}

(33)

\displaystyle Y_{1m}(\hat{\mathbf{r}})e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{4f^{2}}}e^{-\frac{R_{AV}^{2}}{f_{0}^{2}}}\chi_{1-m}^{34}\phi_{0}^{34}\omega_{0}^{34}b_{3}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{r_{3}})d_{4}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{r_{4}}).

Here,
\mathbf{R}_{AV}=\mathbf{R}_{A}-\mathbf{R}_{V}
is the relative coordinate between the source particle
“A” and the created quark-antiquark pair in the vacuum with

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{m_{1}\mathbf{r_{1}}+m_{2}\mathbf{r_{2}}}{m_{1}+m_{2}};

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{V}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{m_{3}\mathbf{r_{3}}+m_{4}\mathbf{r_{4}}}{m_{3}+m_{4}}=\frac{\mathbf{r_{3}}+\mathbf{r_{4}}}{2}.

The convergence factor
e^{-r^{2}/(4f^{2})}
in the modified operator
T_{2}
accounts primarily for the energy considerations of the quark-antiquark pairs created in the vacuum, acknowledging the difficulty of creating high-energy quark-antiquark pairs. The damping factor
e^{-R_{AV}^{2}/{R_{0}}^{2}}
reflects that the created quark-antiquark pair should not be too far from the originating particle. Finnally, the parameter
\gamma=6.95
was initially obtained by fitting the heavy-flavor meson
c\bar{c}
. However, our previous work demonstrated that this parameter cannot be directly applied to the calculation of mass shifts in the light meson spectrum
Chen:2017mug
. In this context, we need to calculate the decay of the light meson
K^{*}
, and therefore, we first obtained a new value for
\gamma
by fitting the experimental data for
\rho\rightarrow\pi\pi
. The values of the parameters
R_{0}
and
f
were ultimately determined by fitting the masses of light mesons,

\displaystyle\gamma=32.2,\quad f=0.5~\mathrm{fm},\quad R_{0}=1.0~\mathrm{fm}.

VI
summary

In the framework of the chiral quark model, we systematically investigated the bound states and resonances of the
T_{ss}
(
s\bar{q}s\bar{q}
with
01^{+}
) system. Using the method of Gaussian expansion, we considered four physical channels,
KK^{*}
and
K^{*}K^{*}
, and their corresponding color octets
[K]_{8}[K^{*}]_{8}
and
[K^{*}]_{8}[K^{*}]_{8}
, in the molecular structure and two physical channels,
[ss]_{6}^{0}[\bar{q}\bar{q}]_{\bar{6}}^{1}
and
[ss]_{3}^{1}[\bar{q}\bar{q}]_{\bar{3}}^{0}
, in the diquark structure, and performed a coupled-channel calculation of these two structures.

The bound state calculations reveal a deeply bound state,
B(1330)
, with
KK^{*}
as its primary component. We propose that the main decay width of this bound state arises from the decay process
K^{*}\rightarrow K+\pi
. Thus, using the
{}^{3}P_{0}
model, we calculated the decay width of
K^{*}
as
20
MeV. However, the binding energy of
B(1330)
, approximately
60
MeV, directly affects the decay phase space of
K^{*}
. By distributing this binding energy equally between
K^{*}
and
K
, we estimate that the energy of
K^{*}
should be reduced by about
30
MeV during decay process. Consequently, the decay width of the bound state
B(1330)
in the
T_{ss}
system is estimated to be
17
MeV. Subsequently, we employed the real-scaling method to calculate the resonances. The results indicate that we obtained four resonances:
R(2290)
,
R(2610)
,
R(2690)
, and
R(2740)
, primarily composed of colorul structure components. Their decay widths are all within
10
MeV, and the internal quark distances are around
1
fm.

Given the current interest in
T_{cc}
, with much attention focused on its heavy-flavor partner, there is limited research on the light-flavor partner of
T_{cc}
. Therefore, we suggest that related experiments be conducted to search for the predicted bound states and resonances in
T_{ss}
system.

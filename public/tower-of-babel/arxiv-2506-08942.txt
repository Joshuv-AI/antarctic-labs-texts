On the connectedness of the singular set of holomorphic foliations
Omegar Calvo–Andrade, Maurício Corrêa, Marcos Jardim, José Seade

Abstract
. Let ℱ \mathscr{F} be a codimension 1 singular holomorphic foliation on ℙ 3 \mathbb{P}^{3} and let Z Z be the union of the 1-dimensional connected components of its singular set; Dominique Cerveau asked in 2013 whether Z Z is necessarily connected. We prove that if a k k -dimensional holomorphic foliation on a projective manifold whose determinant bundle of the normal sheaf is ample has a singular set Z Z of dimension k − 1 k-1 , then Z Z is connected. This can be regarded as providing an obstruction for the integrability of singular holomorphic distributions in the spirit of Bott’s work.



Introduction

We recall
[
2
]
that a
k
-dimensional singular holomorphic foliation on a complex manifold
X
of dimension
n
is defined by an exact sequence

0\to\mathscr{F}\longrightarrow TX\longrightarrow N_{\mathscr{F}}\longrightarrow 0\,,

where
TX
is the sheaf of holomorphic vector fields on
X
, the
tangent sheaf

\mathscr{F}
is an integrable coherent subsheaf of
TX
of rank
k
, and the
normal sheaf

N_{\mathscr{F}}
is coherent and torsion free, of rank
n-k;
this is the codimension of
\mathscr{F}
. The
singular scheme
, denoted by
{\rm Sing}(\mathscr{F})
, is the locus where the normal sheaf is not locally free. This implies that
{\rm Sing}(\mathscr{F})
has codimension
\geq 2
. Let
\det(N_{\mathscr{F}})
denote the determinant bundle of the normal sheaf. We prove:

Theorem 1
.

Let
X
be a projective manifold of dimension
n
, and
\mathscr{F}
a singular holomorphic foliation of dimension
k>1
such that the bundle
\det(N_{\mathscr{F}})
is ample. Let the singular set be
{\rm Sing}(\mathscr{F})=Z\cup Z_{-}
, where
Z
has dimension
k-1
and
Z_{-}
consists of lower-dimensional components. Then
Z
is a connected set.

Our result is sharp since all the assumptions are necessary; see Section
4
.

This answers positively the question raised by D. Cerveau in
[
7
]
regarding dimension 2 foliations on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
, since the determinant bundle of the normal sheaf of every holomorphic foliation on projective spaces is ample. Cerveau’s problem was motivated by the known models of codimension one foliations classified in degrees 0, 1, and 2.

We recall that in the 1960s (see, for instance,
[
3
]
) Raoul Bott investigated global obstructions for the integrability of a subbundle of the tangent bundle of a manifold. Theorem
1
can be regarded as belonging to that frame of thought, as it provides a global obstruction for a singular holomorphic distribution to be integrable. In fact, we know from
[
5
, Theorem 9.5 item 2b.]
and
[
13
, Table 1]
that there are families of holomorphic distributions of dimension two on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
, whose singular set contains two connected components of dimension 1.

Our proof uses in an essential way the Baum–Bott residues
[
2
]
. The idea is as follows. By Baum–Bott, the Chern number
c_{1}(N_{\mathscr{F}})^{n-k+1}[X]
has a residue localized at each
connected
component
Z_{1},\dots,Z_{s}
of the singular set of dimension
k-1
; these residues are elements in
H_{2k-2}(Z_{i})
. Then, the ampleness condition yields, after some work, that the residue at each
Z_{i}
equals the total Chern number
c_{1}^{n-k+1}(N_{\mathscr{F}})\cap[X]
. Hence
s=1
.

We give examples showing that the ampleness condition on the determinant of the normal sheaf is necessary for Theorem
1
. This condition is always satisfied for holomorphic distributions in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, see for instance Remark
4.6
.

In
[
4
]
, the first author, Cerveau, Giraldo, and Lins Neto asked whether the tangent sheaf of a codimension one foliation must split as a sum of line bundles whenever it is locally free. Recently, the third author, Faenzi and Vallés in
[
12
]
provided a negative answer to this problem by producing examples of rational foliations on
\mathbb{P}^{3}

of even degree
2k+4,~k\geq 0
, whose tangent sheaf is a twisted null-correlation bundle, hence non-split; the singular sets of such foliations are singular, non-reduced curves. Furthermore, these foliations admit flat deformations to non-integrable distributions.

This naturally leads to the following question:
What are the topological obstructions for a rank
2
reflexive sheaf to be the tangent sheaf of a foliation in
a projective 3-fold?

Next, we present an application of our theorem that emphasizes its interpretation as a topological obstruction to integrability, by imposing a topological condition on the tangent sheaf and in terms of the number of isolated singularities of the foliation. Hence, this provides an answer to the question above, providing a topological obstruction to integrability.

Corollary 1
.

Let
\mathscr{F}
be a codimension one foliation on a smooth projective threefold
X
satisfying
h^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X})=0
, and let
C
be the union of all 1-dimensional components of
{\rm Sing}(\mathscr{F})
; assume that
C
is reduced and that the determinant of normal bundle of
\mathscr{F}
is ample. If
h^{1}(TX\otimes\det(N_{\mathscr{F}})^{*})=h^{2}(TX\otimes\det(N_{\mathscr{F}})^{*})=0
, then

h^{2}(\mathscr{F}\otimes\det(N_{\mathscr{F}})^{*})=c_{3}(\mathscr{F})\cap[X]

which corresponds to the number of isolated singularities of

\mathscr{F}
counted with multiplicities. In particular, when
X=\mathbb{P}^{3}
, the conditions are satisfied and the formula holds.

This is also related to another problem posed by Cerveau in
[
7
, Problem 2.2]
, which concerns the description of foliations whose singular set has pure dimension one, a condition equivalent to the tangent sheaf being locally free (see
[
5
]
). The result provides a topological and algebro-geometric obstruction for such foliations.

In particular, a codimension 1 foliation with locally free tangent sheaf on a smooth 3-dimensional projective manifold, under the hypotheses of Corollary
1
, must satisfy

h^{2}(\mathscr{F}\otimes\det(N_{\mathscr{F}})^{*})=0.

This condition reflects a topological obstruction for a locally free sheaf to be the tangent sheaf of a foliation.

Moreover, codimension 1 foliations on
\mathbb{P}^{3}

with locally free tangent sheaf force
{\rm Sing}(\mathscr{F})
to be singular, unless

\mathscr{F}
is a pencil of planes.

Corollary 2
.

Let
\mathscr{F}
be a codimension one foliation on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
. If the tangent sheaf of
\mathscr{F}
is locally free, then either
\mathscr{F}
is a pencil of planes, or
{\rm Sing}(\mathscr{F})
is a singular curve.

We emphasize that the hypothesis that
\mathscr{F}
is locally free is essential: recall that the singular scheme of a generic rational foliation of type
(a,b)
consists of a smooth complete intersection curve of degree
ab
plus

(a+b-2)^{3}+2(a+b-2)^{2}+2(1-ab)(a+b-2)

distinct points, see
[
5
, Section 10.1]
and
[
11
]
for the higher-dimensional case. The example in
[
12
, Theorem 7.1]
, where the foliation is given by a pencil and the tangent sheaf is a twisted null-correlation, has as singular set a singular curve.

According to

, a codimension one distribution

4.
Examples and remarks

In this section, we will present examples and observations that demonstrate the necessity of the hypotheses, in particular that Theorem 1 is sharp.

Example 4.1
.

Let
X
be the classical Hopf manifold of dimension
n
. This is obtained from
\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
dividing it by the group of automorphisms generated by a contraction, say
z\mapsto z/2
. It is clear that
X
fibers over
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
with fiber an elliptic curve, the quotient of
\mathbb{C}^{*}
by the contraction. So one has
an elliptic fibration
X\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\to}\limits^{{\pi}}}\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
.

Consider now a 1-dimensional foliation
\mathscr{F}
on
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
with isolated singularities. Its pull back
\pi^{\ast}\mathscr{F}
is a 2-dimensional foliation on
X
with
singular set, a union of disjoint elliptic curves:

\operatorname{Sing}(\pi^{*}\mathscr{F})=\bigsqcup_{p\in\operatorname{Sing}(\mathscr{F})}\pi^{-1}(p).

If
\operatorname{Sing}(\mathscr{F})
is reduced, then
\operatorname{Sing}(\pi^{*}\mathscr{F})
has
d^{n-1}+\cdots+d+1
connected components, see for instance
[
18
, Theorem A]
.

In this example, neither the manifold in question is projective, nor is
\det(N_{\mathscr{F}})
ample.

Example 4.2
.

(Foliations on products)
Let
\mathscr{F}
be a holomorphic foliation of dimension
1
with isolated singularities on a projective manifold
X
of dimension
n
. If
Y
is a projective manifold of dimension
k-1
, then we have a foliation
\pi_{2}^{*}\mathscr{F}
on
X\times Y
of dimension
k
, with singular set the disjoint union

\operatorname{Sing}(\pi_{2}^{*}\mathscr{F})=\bigsqcup_{p\in\operatorname{Sing}(\mathscr{F})}\pi_{2}^{-1}(p),

where
\pi_{2}^{-1}(p)=Y
for all
p\in\operatorname{Sing}(\mathscr{F})
. Then
c_{1}(\det(N(\pi_{2}^{*}\mathscr{F})))=\pi_{2}^{*}c_{1}(\det(N_{\mathscr{F}}))
, and

c_{1}(\det(N_{\mathscr{F}}(\pi_{2}^{*}\mathscr{F})))^{n+k-1}=0

since
n+k-1>n
. So the determinant of the normal bundle is not ample.

The next example is a 2-dimensional foliation on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, induced by the action of the affine group, whose singular set is the connected union of
n
rational curves intersecting at a single point.

Example 4.3
.

The next example generalizes to high dimensions an example from

for

Remark 4.4
.

Let
\mathscr{F}
be a holomorphic foliation on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
of dimension
k>1
, with
Z\subset{\rm Sing}(\mathscr{F})
the non-empty component of dimension
k-1
. We can write

Z=\bigcup_{j=1}^{s}Z_{j},

where
Z_{j}
are the connected components. In this case, we have

[Z_{i}\cap Z_{k}]=(d_{j}h^{n-k+1})\smile(d_{k}h^{n-k+1})=d_{j}d_{k}h^{2(n-k+1)}\in H^{2(n-k+1)}(\mathbb{P}^{n}),

with
d_{j}=\deg(Z_{j})
and
d_{k}=\deg(Z_{k})
. Thus,
[Z_{i}\cap Z_{k}]\neq 0
whenever
n\leq 2k-2
, implying that
Z
is connected in this case. Consequently, the statement of Theorem
1
is not immediate in the case where
n>2k-2
. Recently, in
[
10
]
, it was shown that
\mathrm{dim}({\rm Sing}(\mathscr{F}))\geq k-1
, whenever
n\geq 2k
, meaning that
Z_{-}
is empty.

Remark 4.5
.

We note that the hypothesis that
k>1
is also necessary: a generic foliation by curves on a complex projective manifold has 0-dimensional singularities consisting of several distinct points. In addition,
[
8
]
contains various examples of foliations by curves on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
whose singular set is the union of two or more skew lines.

Remark 4.6
.

Consider a holomorphic foliation
\mathscr{F}\subset TX
of dimension
k
.
By taking the maximal exterior power of the dual morphism

\Omega^{1}_{X}\longrightarrow\mathscr{F}^{*},

one obtains a morphism

\wedge^{k}\phi^{\vee}:\Omega^{k}_{X}\longrightarrow\det(\mathscr{F})^{*}.

This, in turn, yields an induced morphism

\Omega^{k}_{X}\otimes\det(\mathscr{F})\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X},

whose image is an ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}_{Z}
defining the singular set of the foliation
Z={\rm Sing}(\mathscr{F})\subset X
.
Moreover, this construction determines a global section

\nu_{\mathscr{F}}\in H^{0}\left(X,\wedge^{k}TX\otimes\det(\mathscr{F})^{*}\right).

By the isomorphism

\wedge^{k}TX\simeq\Omega_{X}^{n-k}\otimes\det(TX)

and the relation

\det(N_{\mathscr{F}})\simeq\det(TX)\otimes K\mathscr{F},

we conclude that the isomorphism

\wedge^{k}TX\otimes K\mathscr{F}\simeq\Omega_{X}^{n-k}\otimes\det(TX)\otimes K\mathscr{F}\simeq\Omega_{X}^{n-k}\otimes\det(N_{\mathscr{F}})

shows that the section
\nu_{\mathscr{F}}
corresponds to a twisted
(n-k)
-form

\omega_{\mathscr{F}}\in H^{0}\left(X,\Omega_{X}^{n-k}\otimes\det(N_{\mathscr{F}})\right)

with values in
\det(N_{\mathscr{F}})
.
A holomorphic foliation
\mathscr{F}
of dimension
k
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
can be described by a twisted
(n-k)
-form

\omega\in H^{0}\left(\mathbb{P}^{n},\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{n-k}(d+n-k+1)\right),

where
d\geq 0
is referred to as the
degree
of the foliation
\mathscr{F}
. Then,
\det(N_{\mathscr{F}})=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d+n-k+1)
is ample. See for instance
[
9
, Section 2.5]
.

Remark 4.7
.

Two-dimensional positioning of the crab pulsar with avoidance of non-sensitive direction
Authors: Jianfeng Chen, Huanzi Zhang, Jin Liu, Xin Ma, Xiaolin Ning
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn the surrounding orbits, the position error of the spacecraft causes the distortion of the accumulative pulsar profile. Through theoretical analysis, a non-sensitive direction exists in three-dimensional space. Specifically, the initial position error in the non-sensitive direction hardly causes the large distortion of the accumulative pulsar profile, and can easily cause the search algorithm to fall into a local optimum. To significantly reduce the risk of falling into local optima, we propose a two-dimensional positioning method using the distortion of the accumulative Crab pulsar profile. This method combines coarse and fine searches, optimizing orbital recursive error and the chi-square value of the profile, respectively. By exploring two-dimensional positioning on a plane perpendicular to the non-sensitive direction through multiple epoch folds, we achieve precise positioning of the Crab pulsar. Furthermore, to reduce repeated computations, we utilize the dictionary folding method to achieve fast epoch folding. The simulation results demonstrate an approximately 13% improvement in the accuracy of the two-dimensional positioning method with avoidance of non-sensitive direction, compared to the traditional two-dimensional positioning method, although the angle of the search planes of two two-dimensional positioning methods is small. Additionally, its computational time is approximately 3 min. These results indicate that the proposed method has high precision and real-time performance.Keywords: Pulsars, Positioning, Non-sensitive direction, Optimization, DistortionSubject terms: Astronomy and planetary science, Engineering

Introduction

X-ray pulsar navigation technology is a novel autonomous navigation technology with development potential1–3. The United States launched the Neutron Star Interior Composition Explorer in 2017, completing the X-ray pulsar navigation space validation4. In 2018, NASA completed the world’s first X-ray Pulsar Navigation (XPNAV) space validation, confirming the feasibility of XPNAV technology as a new autonomous navigation method. Chinese research initially lagged but has rapidly developed, as demonstrated by the successful verification of X-ray pulsar navigation5. Research using facilities like the Five-hundred-meter Aperture Spherical radio Telescope (FAST) has also contributed to high-precision pulsar timing models, which are foundational for navigation. The feasibility of pulsar navigation applications has been affirmed through the launches of Tiangong-26, the deployment of a small experimental satellite, X-ray Pulsar Navigation-I5 (XPNAV-1) in 2016, and the launch of the Insight-Hard X-ray Modulation Telescope (Insight-HXMT) Satellite7 in 2017 .

The degree of spacecraft orbit non-linearity affects velocimetry (or period estimation) through the distortion of the pulsar profile. For transfer orbits or uniform linear motion, velocity errors can be considered constant throughout the pulsar observation period. In addition to classical methods, Li et al. use the coherence statistic of bi-spectrum and the maximum correlation variance for optimization8,9. Zhang et al. utilize the accumulative pulsar profile entropy algorithm as an assessment measure10. The performance of these methods is still good at low signal-to-noise ratios. Zhou et al. employ the Lomb-Scargle periodogram for period estimation when dealing with unevenly spaced timing data11. Given limited star-borne computing resources, Zhang et al. propose the Fast Butterfly Epoch Folding (FBEF) model to quickly generate the accumulative pulsar profile12. Liu et al. present a fast method for pulsar period estimation—the Fast Fourier Transform based on Compressive Sensing (FFT-CS) and period estimation using compressed sensing13. By avoiding multiple folding of the pulsar signal, FFT-CS considerably reduces computing costs and utilizes a single distorted pulsar profile. To address the large size of the measurement matrix in compressed sensing (CS), Liu et al. propose an ultra-fast estimation method of pulsar period based on empirical mode decomposition-compressed sensing (EMD-CS)14. Similarly, the pursuit of computational efficiency extends to pulse phase estimation. Wang et al. propose a cross-entropy adaptive moment estimation (CE-Adam) algorithm for on-orbit pulse phase estimation, which significantly reduces the central processing unit (CPU) time cost while maintaining high accuracy15. Considering the limited accuracy of pulsar period estimation based on a single distorted pulsar profile, a compromise is reached with FBEF. The pulsar period estimation based on a small number of Fast Butterfly Epoch Folding with a Few Distorted Profiles (FBEF-FDP) is proposed16, achieving real-time and high-precision pulsar period estimation. Additionally, Liu et al. develop a phase shift model and applied it to enhance Kalman filtering to reduce computation drastically17. It is evident from the mentioned methods that for transfer orbits or uniform linear motion, only the velocity error in the line-of-sight (LOS) or the pulsar period needs to be estimated, resulting in only one unknown quantity.

For strongly non-linear orbits, such as surrounding orbits, the six elements of the orbit or the three-dimensional position velocities can cause positional drift in the LOS of the pulsar, which in turn can cause the distortion of the accumulative pulsar profile. Zhang et al. use supplementary satellite observation data to improve the accuracy of XPNAV18. Liu et al. divide the observation time into segments, approximating the orbits in small time periods as linear orbits. Then the time of arrival (TOA) of the pulsar is processed using non-linear least squares estimation. (The TOA represents the precise arrival time of a pulsar’s pulse at the spacecraft, typically measured by aligning the observed pulse profile with a standard template24. Finally, the position and velocity information are calculated. The fast non-linearly constrained least square method, which divides observation time into small segments, is susceptible to introducing orbital non-linear errors19. To account for approximate pulse phase evolution, Wang et al. fit a linear polynomial while considering the spacecraft’s flight orbit20. Wang et al. also evaluate hyperparameters of the pulsar phase model and developed a phase shift model21. Liu J conduct the two-dimensional positioning method on a plane formed by two directions vectors, determined by an analysis of observable directions to be well-measured velocity directions. To minimize computational costs, Liu J suggest a dictionary folding method, Dictionary folding is a computational method used to quickly accumulate pulsar profiles. Its core idea is to avoid repeatedly performing time-consuming epoch folding operations during the speed search process by pre-computing and eliminating redundant phase offset calculations22. In surrounding orbits, the position error also causes the distortion of the accumulative pulsar profile. The estimation of the position using the distortion of the accumulative pulsar profile is a worthy subject of study. To address the issue of high computational load in six-dimensional searches, Liu et al. propose a differential-geometry-based multi-dimensional joint position-velocity estimation using Crab pulsar profile distortion23. By employing differential geometry, the distortion degrees in different joint position-velocity directions were calculated, and a distortion ratio threshold was set to filter out directions with significant distortion for the search, thereby achieving dimensionality reduction.

For strong non-linear orbits, we conduct a theoretical investigation into the correlation between the initial position error and the orbital recursive error. We discover the existence of a specific direction where position errors fail to induce distortions in the accumulative pulsar profile, or, alternatively, where such distortions are mitigated to a minimum extent. This direction is referred to as the non-sensitive direction. We propose the two-dimensional positioning method of the Crab pulsar with avoidance of non-sensitive direction (2DPAN). The 2D positioning method is prone to fall into a local optimum when positioning in the non-sensitive direction, failing to obtain the global optimum. To decrease the position error, the method with avoidance of the non-sensitive direction is used for the 2D positioning method.

Rationale for the existence of non-sensitive direction

In the surrounding orbits, the non-sensitive direction exists in the three-dimensional position space. In the non-sensitive direction, the position error does not cause large distortions. We prove that the initial position error has an approximately linear correlation with the orbital recursive error, deriving an upper limit of χ2. Based on this premise, if the initial error causes a small orbital recursive error, the initial error will not cause a large decrease in the χ2-value.

In this study, the traditional two-dimensional positioning method refers to the approach proposed by Liu et al.22, which performs a 2D search on a plane formed by two well-measured velocity directions. While this method is computationally efficient, it may become suboptimal when the search directions align closely with the non-sensitive direction identified in our analysis. Even a small angular difference (e.g., 1° as shown in Table 3 for OILOS = 90°) can lead to significant accuracy degradation because the objective function (-value) exhibits low sensitivity along the non-sensitive direction, making the optimization prone to converge to local optima.

Conclusion and future work

Through theoretical analysis, it is found that the non-sensitive direction exists in three-dimensional space. Namely, the initial position error in the non-sensitive direction hardly causes a significant distortion of the accumulative pulsar profile. It is easy to make the 2D positioning method fall into local optimal. To solve this problem, we propose the 2D positioning method with avoidance of the non-sensitive direction (2DPAN).

The 2DPAN includes the search method for the non-sensitive direction and a 2D positioning on the plane perpendicular to the non-sensitive direction, and has the following advantages:

High positioning accuracy. The 2D position error ranges from 380 to 850 m, meeting spacecraft requirements. Compared to the traditional direction, the precision of positioning of the 2DPAN is enhanced by 13%. The reason is that the search range of 2DPAN does not include non sensitive directions, significantly reducing the risk of falling into local optima. The 13% accuracy improvement of 2DPAN over the traditional method, even when the angular difference between their search planes is small (e.g., 0.999° at OILOS = 90°), can be attributed to the fundamental difference in search space design. While the traditional method uses fixed velocity-direction planes, 2DPAN dynamically identifies and avoids the non-sensitive direction, ensuring that the search is conducted in directions where the pulsar profile is most sensitive to position errors. This prevents the optimization from getting trapped in flat regions of the objective function, which is particularly important when the non-sensitive direction lies close to the traditional search plane.

The reason is that the dictionary folding method reduces the repeated calculations. In addition, the 2DPAN only carries out a 2D positioning method, which reduces the computation by an order of magnitude relative to a three-dimensional position.

The 2DPAN method provides new ideas and references for future X-ray pulsar navigation system design.

While the proposed 2DPAN method demonstrates high accuracy and real-time performance under the defined simulation conditions, its performance in scenarios with strong orbital perturbations (e.g., J2) over long durations or under extremely low signal-to-noise ratios requires further investigation. Future work will focus on integrating high-fidelity orbital dynamics and evaluating the method’s robustness with a wider range of pulsars.

While this study validates the 2DPAN method using the Crab pulsar in a surrounding Earth orbit, the core concept of the non-sensitive direction is general and derived from the fundamental principles of orbital dynamics and pulsar timing. The existence of such a direction is independent of the specific pulsar or orbit type, as it arises from the geometrical relationship between the position error and the line-of-sight. However, the precise orientation of the non-sensitive direction and the sensitivity of the profile distortion are influenced by specific mission parameters, including the pulsar’s period and profile structure, the orbital dynamics, and the observation geometry. Therefore, while the 2DPAN framework is universally applicable, its deployment for a specific deep-space mission or with a different pulsar would require re-estimating the non-sensitive direction for that particular context. Future work will focus on applying the 2DPAN method to a broader range of pulsars (e.g., millisecond pulsars with weaker flux) and more diverse orbits (e.g., highly elliptical or deep-space trajectories) to further validate its broad applicability.

The performance of the proposed 2DPAN method, which relies on detecting profile distortion, is intrinsically linked to the pulsar’s signal-to-noise ratio (SNR). While this study validates the method using the high-flux Crab pulsar, its applicability to weaker pulsars warrants discussion. Under low SNR conditions, the -value of the accumulated profile, which serves as the optimization metric, would exhibit higher variance. This could reduce the sharpness of the peak in the search space, making it more challenging to precisely identify the correct position and the non-sensitive direction. Consequently, some degradation in positioning accuracy is expected compared to the results presented here. Future work will systematically evaluate this relationship and explore techniques such as profile denoising or the use of more robust similarity metrics to extend the method’s applicability to a broader catalog of pulsars.

This study has focused on the theoretical foundation and validation of using pulsar profile distortion for position estimation. It is important to note that the overall accuracy of a fully-integrated pulsar navigation system is subject to additional error sources not explicitly considered here. These include, but are not limited to, uncertainties in the orbital dynamics model, spacecraft clock errors, and the precision of the pulse time of arrival (TOA) estimates. The performance demonstrated in this paper represents the standalone contribution of the 2DPAN method under controlled simulation conditions. Future work will involve integrating the 2DPAN position estimates as measurements into a recursive filter (such as a Kalman filter) within a full-state navigation system, where the interplay with dynamics errors and other states (e.g., velocity, clock bias) can be comprehensively analyzed and optimized.

Representations of weighted Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan algebras
Jules Anitchéou and Sylvain Attan

Abstract
. Weighted Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan algebras and their representations are studied. Moreover, we consider weighted Rota-Baxter paired operators that are related to weighted Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan algebras together with their representations. Finally, we define suitable cohomology for weighted Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan algebras in low degrees.



Keywords:
Jacobi-Jordan algebras, Rota-Baxter operators, representations, Rota-Baxter paired operators, cohomology.

2020 MSC:
17C50,16W10,17B56.

1.
Introduction

A while ago, a new class of algebras appeared in the literature: the so Jacobi-Jordan algebras. These are commutative non-associative algebras satisfying the Jacobi identity. It turns out that these algebras seen as strange cousins of Lie algebras, are a special class of Jordan
nilalgebras and are also related to Bernstein-Jordan algebras.They were first appeared in
[
9
]
and since then a lot of works are done on this subject, see for example
[
6
,
7
,
27
]
.

A Rota-Baxter operator of weight
0
on an algebra
A
is a linear map
\mathcal{I}:A\rightarrow A
satisfying the so-called Rota-Baxter identity:
\mathcal{I}(x)\mathcal{I}(y)=\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{I}(x)y+x\mathcal{I}(y)),
for all
x,y\in A
. Integration by parts is an example of a Rota-Baxter operator of weight
0
. Thus, These operators can be regarded as an algebraic abstraction
representing the integral operator. A Rota-Baxter algebra is an associative algebra equipped with a Rota-Baxter operator of weight
0
. These operators first introduced by Baxter in his study of the fluctuation theory
[
8
]
, were further developed by Rota
[
23
]
where some identities as well as some applications of Baxter algebra arising in probability and combinatorial theory were given. An important fact is that, Rota-Baxter operators have a connection with combinatorics of shuffle algebras
[
17
]
, Yang-Baxter equations
[
1
]
, dendriform algebras
[
1
,
16
]
, renormalizations in quantum field theory
[
11
]
, multiple
zeta values in number theory
[
19
]
and splitting of algebraic operads
[
3
]
. The reader can see studies of Cartier
[
10
]
and Atkinson
[
2
]
for further properties of Rota-Baxter operators as well as those of
[
21
]
where these operators on Lie algebras were first considered by Kuperscmidt in the study of classical
r
-matrices.
More generally, weighted Rota-Baxter operators (also called Rota-Baxter operators with arbitrary weight) related with tridendriform algebras
[
15
]
, post-Lie algebras and modified Yang-Baxter equations
[
4
]
, weighted infinitesimal bialgebras and weighted Yang-Baxter equations
[
25
]
, were studied in
[
4
,
5
]
. Recently, the cohomology of Rota-Baxter operators of weight
1
on Lie algebras and Lie groups is studied
[
20
]
. Furthermore, Das
[
12
]
developed a similar theory for Rota-Baxter associative algebras of weight zero. Next, Wang and Zhou
[
24
]
, Das
[
13
]
studied Rota-Baxter algebras of arbitrary weight by different methods respectively. Precisely in
[
13
]
, the authors considered weighted Rota-Baxter associative algebras and define cohomology of them with coefficients in a suitable Rota-Baxter bimodule. When considering the cohomology with coefficients in itself, it governs the simultaneous deformations of algebras and weighted Rota-Baxter operators. Later, Das
[
14
]
studied cohomology of weighted Rota-Baxter Lie
algebras and Rota-Baxter paired operators using the method of
[
24
]
. This may be a transformation from weight zero to arbitrary weight, and the successful transformation is entirely due to
Rota-Baxter Lie groups
[
18
]
. Up to now, there was very few study about Rota-Baxter operators on Jacobi-Jordan algebras. Thus it is time to study these operators on Jacobi-Jordan algebras. In a concrete way, we study a representation theory and cohomology theory in low degrees of Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan algebras of arbitrary weight. To do that, we will apply the approach of
[
14
]
to weighted Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan algebras. More precisely, we first consider representations of weighted Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan algebras and provide various constructions of them. Next, we define the cohomology of a weighted Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan algebra with coefficients in a representation. This cohomology as some existing works on the theme, is obtained as a byproduct of the standard zigzag cohomology of the underlying Jacobi-Jordan algebra and the cohomology of the underlying weighted Rota-Baxter operator.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we consider weighted Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan algebras and their representations. We also give several new constructions of representations on these algebraic structures. In section
3
, given a Jacobi-Jordan algebra and a representation on it, we introduce weighted Rota-Baxter paired operators that are related to weighted Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan algebras together with their representations and we give a characterization of this concept. Observe that the terminology of Rota-Baxter paired operators appeared first in
[
26
]
where the authors introduced Rota-Baxter paired modules in the associative context and was taken up in
[
14
]
in the case of Lie algebras. In section
4
, we defined the cohomology in low degrees of a weighted Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan algebra with coefficients in its representation and we compute some cohomology spaces as applications.

Throughout this paper, let
\mathbb{K}
be a field of characteristic
0.
Except specially stated, vector spaces are
\mathbb{K}
-vector spaces and all tensor products are taken over
\mathbb{K}
.

2.
Representatons of weighted Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan algebras

In this section, we consider a weighted Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan and introduce their representations. We also provide various examples and new constructions. Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{K}
be a fixed scalar unless specified otherwise.

Definition 2.1
.

A
\lambda
- weighted Rota-Baxter operator on an algebra
(A,\ast)
is a linear map
\mathcal{I}:A\longrightarrow A
satisfying

(1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(x)\ast\mathcal{I}(y)=\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{I}(x)\ast y+x\ast\mathcal{I}(y)+\lambda(x\ast y)),\forall x,y\in A.

Example 2.2
.

Let’ s take
A=C(\mathbb{R})
, the algebra of continuous real-valued functions on
\mathbb{R}
and define
\mathcal{I}:A\rightarrow A
by

\mathcal{I}(f)(x):=\int_{0}^{x}f(t)dt.

Then
\mathcal{I}
is a
0
- weighted Rota-Baxter operator on the algebra
A
.

Definition 2.3
.

(i)

A Jacobi-Jordan algebra is an algebra
(A,\ast)
satisfying

\displaystyle x\ast y=y\ast x,\forall x,y\in A,

(2)

\displaystyle(x\ast y)\ast z+(y\ast z)\ast x+(z\ast x)\ast y;\forall x,y,z\in A.

(ii)

A
\lambda
- weighted Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan algebra is a pair
(A,\mathcal{I})
consisting of a Jacobi-Jordan algebra
A
together with a
\lambda
- weighted Rota-Baxter operator on it.

(iii)

Let
(A,\mathcal{I})
and
(A^{\prime},\mathcal{I}^{\prime})
be two
\lambda
-weighted Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan algebras. A morphism from
(A,\mathcal{I})
to
(A^{\prime},\mathcal{I}^{\prime})
is algebras morphism
\phi:A\rightarrow A^{\prime}
satisfying
\phi\circ\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{I}^{\prime}\circ\phi
. It is called an isomorphism if
\phi
is so.

Example 2.4
.

(i)

Let
A
be a Jacobi-Jordan algebra then, the pair
(A,id_{A})
is a
(-1)
-weighted Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan algebra.

(ii)

Let
(A,\mathcal{I})
be a
\lambda
-weighted Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan algebra. Then for all
\mu\in\mathbb{K}
, the pair
(A,\mu\mathcal{I})
is a
\mu\lambda
-weighted Rota-Baxter algebra.

(iii)

Let
(A,\mathcal{I})
be a
\lambda
-weighted Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan algebra and
\psi\in Aut(A)
be an automorphism of the Jacobi-Jordan algebra
A
. Thn, the pair
(A,\psi^{-1}\circ\mathcal{I}\circ\psi)
is a
\lambda
-weighted Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan algebra.

(iv)

Let
(A,\mathcal{I})
be a
\lambda
- weighted Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan algebra. Then the pair
(A,-\lambda id_{A}-\mathcal{I})
is also a
\lambda
- weighted Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan algebra.

Example 2.5
.

Let
(A,\ast)
be the two-dimensional Jacobi-Jordan algebra where the only nonzero product in a given basis
\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
is
e_{1}\ast e_{1}=e_{2}
(see
[
9
]
).
The linear map
\mathcal{I}:A\rightarrow A
defined by

\mathcal{I}(e_{1})=a_{1}e_{1}+a_{2}e_{2},\ \mathcal{I}(e_{2})=b_{1}e_{1}+b_{2}e_{2}
, is a
Rota-Baxter operator of weight
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
on
(A,\ast)
if and only if

(3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(e_{i})\ast\mathcal{I}(e_{j})=\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{I}(e_{i})\ast e_{j}+e_{i}\ast\mathcal{I}(e_{j})+\lambda e_{i}\ast e_{j}),\mbox{ $\forall\,\ 1\leq i,j\leq 2.$}

The equation (
3
) holds if and only if

(2a_{1}+\lambda)b_{1}=a_{1}^{2}-(2a_{1}+\lambda)b_{2}=a_{1}b_{1}-b_{1}b_{2}=b_{1}=0.

\diamond

If
\lambda=0
, then the pair
(A,\mathcal{I})
is a
0
-weighted Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan algebra where

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}\in\left\{\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
a_{2}&b_{2}\end{pmatrix}\mbox{, $a_{2},b_{2}\in\mathbb{R}$ }\right\}\bigcup\left\{\begin{pmatrix}2a_{1}&0\\
a_{2}&a_{1}\end{pmatrix}\mbox{, $a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathbb{R},a_{1}\neq 0$ }\right\}.

\diamond

If
\lambda\neq 0
and
a\neq\frac{\lambda}{2}
, then the pair
(A,\mathcal{I})
is a
\lambda
-weighted Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan algebra where

(4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}\in\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&0\\
a_{2}&\frac{a_{1}^{2}}{2a_{1}+\lambda}\end{pmatrix}\mbox{, $a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathbb{R},\ a\neq\frac{\lambda}{2}$ }\right\}.

Example 2.6
.

Let
A:=Span\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}\}
be the
4
-dimensional Jacobi-Jordan algebra over
\mathbb{R}
defined by:
e_{1}\ast e_{1}=e_{2}
(see
[
6
]
). Then the linear map
\mathcal{I}:A\rightarrow A
given in the basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}\}
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&b_{1}&c_{1}&d_{1}\\
a_{2}&b_{2}&c_{2}&d_{2}\\
a_{3}&b_{3}&c_{3}&d_{3}\\
a_{4}&b_{4}&c_{4}&d_{4}\end{pmatrix},a_{i},b_{i},c_{i},d_{i}\in\mathbb{R}

is a
\lambda
-weighted Rota-Baxter operator on

(A,\ast)
if and only if

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(e_{i})\ast\mathcal{I}(e_{j})=\mathcal{I}\Big(\mathcal{I}(e_{i})\ast e_{j}+e_{i}\ast\mathcal{I}(e_{j})+\lambda(e_{i}\ast e_{j})\Big),\forall 1\leqslant i\leqslant j\leqslant 4,

that is

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}b_{1}=c_{1}=d_{1}=0,\\
a^{2}_{1}=(2a_{1}+\lambda)b_{2}\\
(2a_{1}+\lambda)(b_{3}-b_{4})=0.\end{array}\right.

\diamond

If
\lambda=0
, we get a

0
-weighted Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan algebra

(A,\mathcal{I})
where

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}\in\left\{\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0&0\\
a_{2}&b_{2}&c_{2}&d_{2}\\
a_{3}&b_{3}&c_{3}&d_{3}\\
a_{4}&b_{4}&c_{4}&d_{4}\end{pmatrix},a_{i},b_{i},c_{i},d_{i}\in\mathbb{R}\right\}\bigcup\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&0&0&0\\
a_{2}&\frac{a^{2}_{1}}{2}&c_{2}&d_{2}\\
a_{3}&b_{3}&c_{3}&d_{3}\\
a_{4}&b_{3}&c_{4}&d_{4}\end{pmatrix},a_{i},b_{i},c_{i},d_{i}\in\mathbb{R},a_{1}\neq 0\right\}.

\diamond

If
\lambda\neq 0
and
a_{1}\neq\frac{\lambda}{2}
, we get a

\lambda
-weighted Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan algebra

(A,\mathcal{I})
where

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}\in\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&0&0&0\\
a_{2}&\frac{a^{2}_{1}}{2a_{1}+\lambda}&c_{2}&d_{2}\\
a_{3}&b_{3}&c_{3}&d_{3}\\
a_{4}&b_{3}&c_{4}&d_{4}\end{pmatrix},a_{i},b_{i},c_{i},d_{i}\in\mathbb{R},a_{1}\neq\frac{\lambda}{2}\right\}.

Example 2.7
.

Let
A:=Span\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}\}
be the
4
-dimensional Jacobi-Jordan algebra over
\mathbb{R}
defined by:
e_{1}\ast e_{1}=e_{2},e_{3}\ast e_{3}=e_{2}
(see
[
6
]
). Then the linear map
\mathcal{I}:A\rightarrow A
given in the basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}\}
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&b_{1}&c_{1}&d_{1}\\
a_{2}&b_{2}&c_{2}&d_{2}\\
a_{3}&b_{3}&c_{3}&d_{3}\\
a_{4}&b_{4}&c_{4}&d_{4}\end{pmatrix},a_{i},b_{i},c_{i},d_{i}\in\mathbb{R}

is a
0
-weighted Rota-Baxter operator on

(A,\ast)
if and only if

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(e_{i})\ast\mathcal{I}(e_{j})=\mathcal{I}\Big(\mathcal{I}(e_{i})\ast e_{j}+e_{i}\ast\mathcal{I}(e_{j})\Big),\forall 1\leqslant i\leqslant j\leqslant 4,

that is

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}b_{1}=b_{3}=d_{1}=d_{3}=0,\\
a_{1}b_{4}=c_{3}b_{4}=(c_{1}+a_{3})b_{4}=0\\
a^{2}_{1}+a^{2}_{3}-2a_{1}b_{2}=c^{2}_{1}+c^{2}_{3}-2c_{3}b_{2}=0\\
(a_{1}c_{1}+a_{3}c_{3})-b_{2}(c_{1}+a_{3})=0.\end{array}\right.

Note that we do not classify all Rota-Baxter operators of the considered Jacobi-Jordan algebra. Hence, the pair
(A,\mathcal{I})
is a
0
-weighted Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan algebras where

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}\in\left\{\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0&0\\
a_{2}&0&c_{2}&d_{2}\\
0&0&0&0\\
a_{4}&b_{4}&c_{4}&d_{4}\end{pmatrix}\mbox{, $a_{i},b_{i},c_{i},d_{i}\in\mathbb{R}$ }\right\}\bigcup\left\{\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0&0\\
a_{2}&b_{2}&c_{2}&d_{2}\\
0&0&0&0\\
a_{4}&b_{4}&c_{4}&d_{4}\end{pmatrix}\mbox{, $a_{i},b_{i},c_{i},d_{i}\in\mathbb{R},b_{2}b_{4}\neq 0$ }\right\}.

Let recall the following definition from
[
7
]
.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
A
be a Jacobi-Jordan algebra. A representation of
A

[
7
]
is a vector space
V
with a linear map
\rho:A\rightarrow gl(V)
satisfying

(5)

\displaystyle\rho(x\ast y)=-\rho(x)\rho(y)-\rho(y)\rho(x),\forall x,y\in A.

A representation of
A
as above is denoted by
(V,\rho)
or simply by
V
if no confusion arises. Observe that any Jacobi-Jordan algebra
A
is a representation of itself with the action map

\rho:A\rightarrow gl(V)
defined by

\displaystyle\rho(x)y:=x\ast y,\forall x,y\in A.

Such representation is called the regular or adjoint representation of
(A,\ast).

Definition 2.9
.

Let
(A,\mathcal{I})
be a
\lambda
-weighted Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan algebra. A representation of it is a triple
(V,\rho,\mathcal{T})
in which
(V,\rho)
is a representation of the Jacobi-Jordan algebra
A
and
\mathcal{T}:V\rightarrow V
is linear map such that

(6)

\displaystyle\rho(\mathcal{I}x)(\mathcal{T}u)=\mathcal{T}\Big(\rho(\mathcal{I}x)u+\rho(x)(\mathcal{T}u)+\lambda\rho(x)u\Big),\forall x\in A,u\in V.

A representation of
(A,\mathcal{I})
as above is denoted simply by
(V,\mathcal{T})
.

Example 2.10
.

(i)

Any
\lambda
- weighted Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan algebra
(A,\mathcal{I})
is a representation of itself. Such representation is called the adjoint representation.

(ii)

Let
V
be a representation of a Jacobi-Jordan algebra
A
. Then the pair
(V,id_{V})
is a representation of the
(-1)
-weighted Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan algebra
(A,id_{A})
.

(iii)

Let
(V,\mathcal{T})
be a representation of a
\lambda
-weighted Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan algebra
(A,\mathcal{I})
. Then for any scalar
\mu\in\mathbb{K}
, the pair
(V,\mu\mathcal{T})
is a representation of the
\mu\lambda
- weighted Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan algebra
(A,\mu\mathcal{I}).

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
(V,\mathcal{T})
be a representation of a
\lambda
-weighted Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan algebra
(A,\mathcal{I}).
Then
(V,-\lambda id_{V}-\mathcal{T})
is a representation of the
\lambda
-weighted Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan algebra
(A,-\lambda id_{A}-\mathcal{I})
.

Proof.

Pick
x\in A,u\in V,
then, we have:

(7)

\displaystyle\rho((-\lambda id_{A}-\mathcal{I})x)(-\lambda id_{V}-\mathcal{T})(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}\rho(x)u+\lambda\rho(\mathcal{I}x)u+\lambda\rho(x)\mathcal{T}u+\rho(\mathcal{I}x)\mathcal{T}u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}\rho(x)u+\lambda\rho(\mathcal{I}x)u+\lambda\rho(x)\mathcal{T}u

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}\Big(\rho(\mathcal{I}x)u+\rho(x)\mathcal{T}u+\lambda\rho(x)u\Big)\mbox{( by (\ref{rpRJJa}) )}

Next, we obtain

\displaystyle(-\lambda id_{V}-\mathcal{T})\Big(\rho((-\lambda id_{A}-\mathcal{I})x)u+\rho(x)(-\lambda id_{V}-\mathcal{T})u+\lambda\rho(x)u\Big)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}\rho(x)u+\lambda\mathcal{T}(\rho(x)u)+\lambda\rho(\mathcal{I}x)u+\mathcal{T}(\rho(\mathcal{I}x)u)+\cancel{\lambda^{2}\rho(x)u}+\cancel{\lambda\mathcal{T}(\rho(x)u)}

(8)

\displaystyle+\lambda\rho(x)\mathcal{T}u+\mathcal{T}(\rho(x)\mathcal{T}u)-\cancel{\lambda^{2}\rho(x)u}-\cancel{\lambda\mathcal{T}(\rho(x)u}).

The expressions in (
7
) and (
8
) are the same. Thus, the pair

(-\lambda id_{V}-\mathcal{T})
is a representation of
(A,-\lambda id_{A}-\mathcal{I})
. ∎

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
\{(V_{i},\mathcal{T}_{i})\}_{i\in I\subset\mathbb{N}}
be a family of representations of a
\lambda
-weighted Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan algebra
(A,\mathcal{I})
. Then the pair
(\oplus_{i\in I}V_{i},\oplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{T}_{i})
is a representation of
(A,\mathcal{I}).

Proof.

Let
\rho_{i}:A\rightarrow gl(V_{i})
denote the action of the Jacobi-Jordan algebra
A
on the representation
V_{i}.
Then it follows that
\rho:A\rightarrow gl(\oplus_{i\in I}V_{i})
,
\rho(x)(u_{i})_{i\in I}=(\rho_{i}(x)u_{i})_{i\in I}
is a representation of
(A,\ast)
on
\oplus_{i\in I}V_{i}.
In addition, let
x\in A
and
(u_{i})_{i\in I}\oplus_{i\in I}V_{i}
, then we have

\displaystyle\rho(\mathcal{I}x)(\oplus_{i\in I}{\mathcal{T}_{i}})(u_{i})_{i\in I}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\rho_{i}(\mathcal{I}x)\mathcal{T}_{i}(u_{i})\Big)_{i\in I}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\mathcal{T}_{i}(\rho_{i}(\mathcal{I}x)(u_{i})+\rho_{i}(x)\mathcal{T}_{i}(u_{i})+\lambda\rho_{i}(x)u_{i})\Big)_{i\in I}\mbox{ ( by (\ref{rpRJJa}) )}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\oplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{T}_{i})\Big(\rho(\mathcal{I}x)(u_{i})_{i\in I}+\rho(x)(\oplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{T}_{i}(u_{i}))_{i\in I}+\lambda\rho(x)(u_{i})_{i\in I}\Big).

Hence the result follows.
∎

Given a representation
(V,\rho)
of a Jacobi-Jordan algebra
A
, there is a representation of
A
on the space
gl(V)
with the action given by

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}:A\rightarrow gl(gl(V)),(\hat{\rho}(x)f)u=-f(\rho(x)u),\forall(x,f,u)\in A\times gl(V)\times V.

With this representation, we obtain the following result:

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
(V,\mathcal{T})
be a representation of a
\lambda
-weighted Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan
(A,\mathcal{I})
. Then the pair
(gl(V),\hat{\mathcal{T}})
is also a representation of
(A,\mathcal{I})
, where

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{T}}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle gl(V)\rightarrow gl(V),\hat{\mathcal{T}}(f)(u)=-\lambda f(u)-f(\mathcal{T}(u)),\forall(f,u)\in gl(V)\times V.

Proof.

Let
x\in A,u\in V
and
f\in gl(V)
, then we compute

(9)

\displaystyle\Big(\hat{\rho}(\mathcal{I}x)\hat{\mathcal{T}}(f)\Big)u=-\hat{\mathcal{T}}(f)(\rho(\mathcal{I}x)u)=\lambda f(\rho(\mathcal{I}x)u)+f(\mathcal{T}(\rho(\mathcal{I}x)u)).

Next, we also have

\displaystyle\Big((\hat{\rho}(\mathcal{I}x)f+\hat{\rho}(x)\hat{\mathcal{T}}(f)+\lambda\hat{\rho}(x)f\Big)u

\displaystyle=-\lambda\Big(\hat{\mathcal{T}}(\hat{\rho}(\mathcal{I}x)f+\hat{\rho}(x)\hat{\mathcal{T}}(f)+\lambda\hat{\rho}(x)f\Big)u-\Big(\hat{\rho}(\mathcal{I}x)f+\hat{\rho}(x)\hat{\mathcal{T}}(f)+\lambda\hat{\rho}(x)f\Big)(\mathcal{T}u)

\displaystyle=\lambda\Big(f(\rho(\mathcal{I}x)u)+\hat{\mathcal{T}}(f)(\rho(x)u)+\lambda f(\rho(x)u)\Big)+\Big(f(\rho(\mathcal{I}x)(\mathcal{T}u))+\hat{\mathcal{T}}(f)(\rho(x)(\mathcal{T}u))+\lambda f(\rho(x)(\mathcal{T}u)\Big)

\displaystyle=\lambda\Big(f(\rho(\mathcal{I}x)u)-\cancel{\lambda f(\rho(x)u)}-\cancel{f(\mathcal{T}{\rho}(x)u)}+\cancel{\lambda f(\rho(x)u))}\Big)+f\Big(\mathcal{T}(\rho(\mathcal{I}x)u)+\cancel{\mathcal{T}(\rho(x)\mathcal{T}u)}+\cancel{\mathcal{T}(\rho(x)u)}

\displaystyle-\cancel{\lambda\rho(x)(\mathcal{T}u)}-\cancel{\mathcal{T}(\rho(x)(\mathcal{T}u)}+\cancel{\lambda\rho(x)(\mathcal{T}u)}\Big)

(10)

\displaystyle=\lambda f(\rho(\mathcal{I}x)u)+f(\mathcal{T}(\rho(\mathcal{I}x)u)).

Hence, by (
9
) and (
10
),
(V,\hat{\mathcal{T}})
is a representation of
(A,\mathcal{I})
.
∎

As expected, let construct now a semidirect product in the context of
\lambda
-weight Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan algebras.

Proposition 2.14
.

Let
(V,\mathcal{T})
be a representation of a
\lambda
-weighted Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan algebra
(A,\mathcal{I})
. Then
(A\oplus V,\mathcal{I}\oplus\mathcal{T})
is a
\lambda
-weighted Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan algebra, where the product on

is given by the semidirect product

(11)

\displaystyle(x,u)\ast_{\ltimes}(y,v):=\Big(x\ast y,\rho(x)v+\rho(y)u\Big),\forall x,y\in A,\forall u,v\in V.

Proof.

The proof of the commutativity of
\ast_{\ltimes}
is obvious.
Now, pick

x,y,z\in A,u,v,w\in V
, then using the Jacobi identity in
(A,\ast)
and (
5
) we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Big((x,u)\ast_{\ltimes}(y,v)\Big)\ast_{\ltimes}(z,w)+\Big((y,v)\ast_{\ltimes}(z,w)\Big)\ast_{\ltimes}(x,u)+\Big((z,w)\ast_{\ltimes}(x,u)\Big)\ast_{\ltimes}(y,v)\\
=\Big(\Big((x\ast y)\ast z+(y\ast z)\ast x+(z\ast x)\ast y)\Big),\Big(\rho(x\ast y)w+\rho(x)\rho(y)w+\rho(y)\rho(x)w\Big)\\
+\Big(\rho(y\ast z)u+\rho(y)\rho(z)u+\rho(z)\rho(y)u\Big)+\Big(\rho(z\ast x)v+\rho(z)\rho(x)v+\rho(x)\rho(z)v\Big)\Big)=0.\end{split}

Hence, the Jacobi identity in

holds. Finally, to show that
\mathcal{I}\oplus\mathcal{T}
is a
\lambda
-weight Rota-Baxter operator on the semi-direct product
\lambda
-weighted Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan algebra, pick
\forall x,y\in A,u,v\in V
. Then, we observe first that

\displaystyle(\mathcal{I}\oplus\mathcal{T})(x,u)\ast_{\ltimes}(\mathcal{I}\oplus\mathcal{T})(y,v)=(\mathcal{I}x,\mathcal{T}u)\ast_{\ltimes}(\mathcal{I}y,\mathcal{T}v)=\Big(\mathcal{I}x\ast\mathcal{I}y,\rho(\mathcal{I}x)\mathcal{T}v+\rho(\mathcal{I}y)\mathcal{T}u\Big)

\displaystyle=\Big(\mathcal{I}\Big(\mathcal{I}x\ast y+x\ast\mathcal{I}y+\lambda x\ast y\Big),\mathcal{T}\Big(\rho(\mathcal{I}x)v+\rho(x)\mathcal{T}v+\lambda\rho(x)v\Big)

(12)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{T}\Big(\rho(\mathcal{I}y)u+\rho(y)\mathcal{T}u+\lambda\rho(y)u\Big)\Big)\mbox{ ( by (\ref{ro}) and (\ref{rpRJJa}) ).}

Next, we have

(13)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{I}\oplus\mathcal{T})(x,u)\ast_{\ltimes}(y,v)=\Big(\mathcal{I}x\ast y,\rho(\mathcal{I}x)v+\rho(y)\mathcal{T}u\Big)

(14)

\displaystyle(x,u)\ast_{\ltimes}(\mathcal{I}\oplus\mathcal{T})(y,v)=\Big(x\ast\mathcal{I}y,\rho(x)\mathcal{T}v+\rho(\mathcal{T}y)u\Big)

(15)

\displaystyle\lambda\Big((x,u)\ast_{\ltimes}(y,v)\Big)=\lambda\Big(x\ast y,\rho(x)v+\rho(y)u\Big)

i.e.,

\displaystyle\Big((\mathcal{I}\oplus\mathcal{T})(x,u)\ast_{\ltimes}(y,v)+(x,u)\ast_{\ltimes}(\mathcal{I}\oplus\mathcal{T})(y,v)+\lambda\Big((x,u)\ast_{\ltimes}(y,v)\Big)\Big)

(16)

\displaystyle=\Big(\mathcal{I}x\ast y+x\ast\mathcal{I}y+\lambda x\ast y,\rho(\mathcal{I}x)v+\rho(x)\mathcal{T}v+\lambda\rho(x)v+\rho(\mathcal{I}y)u+\rho(y)\mathcal{T}u+\lambda\rho(y)u\Big).

Hence, it follows from (

The
\lambda
-weighted Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan algebra

by

∎

3.

Let
(A,\ast)
be a Jacobi-Jordan algebra and
(V,\rho)
be a representation of it. In this section, weighted
Rota-Baxter paired operators that are related to weighted Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan algebras together with their representations, are introduced. After giving some results, a characterization of these operators is given.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(A,\ast)
be a Jacobi-Jordan algebra,
(V,\rho)
be a representation of it and
\lambda\in\mathbb{K}

(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{T})
of linear maps
\mathcal{I}:A\longrightarrow A

\mathcal{T}:V\longrightarrow V

(21)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(x)\ast\mathcal{I}(y)=\mathcal{I}\Big(\mathcal{I}x\ast y+x\ast\mathcal{I}y+\lambda(x\ast y)\Big),\forall x,y\in A,

(22)

\displaystyle\rho(\mathcal{I}x)\mathcal{T}(u)=\mathcal{T}\Big(\rho(\mathcal{I}x)(u)+\rho(x)\mathcal{T}(u)+\lambda\rho(x)(u)\Big),\forall x\in A,u\in V.

.

Observe that the identity (
21
) shows that the pair
(A,\mathcal{I})
must be a
\lambda
-weighted Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan algebra while the identity (
22
) is equivalent to say that
(V,\mathcal{T})
is a representation of
(A,\mathcal{I}).

According to Remark
3.2
, we get the following result:

Proposition 3.3
.

Let

be a representation of a Jacobi-Jordan algebra

,

and

(A,\mathcal{I})

-weighted Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan algebra.

(2)

The pair
(V,\mathcal{T})
is a representation of
(A,\mathcal{I}).

Proof.

It follows by Definition
3.1
. ∎

Proposition 3.4

\displaystyle+\lambda\Big((x^{\prime}\ast z^{\prime})\ast y+(y\ast z^{\prime})\ast x^{\prime}+(x^{\prime}\ast y)\ast z^{\prime}\Big)+\lambda^{2}\Big((x^{\prime}\ast y^{\prime})\ast z^{\prime}+(y^{\prime}\ast z^{\prime})\ast x^{\prime}+(z^{\prime}\ast x^{\prime})\ast y^{\prime}\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle\rho_{\lambda}((x,y)\ast_{\lambda}(z,t))(u,v)+\rho_{\lambda}((x,y))\rho_{\lambda}((z,t))(u,v)+\rho_{\lambda}((z,t))\rho_{\lambda}((x,y))(u,v)

Let
(A,\ast)
be a Jacobi-Jordan algebra and

for all
(x_{1},x_{2},\cdot\cdot\cdot,x_{n+1})\in A^{\otimes(n+1)}
,
f\in\mathcal{C}^{n}(A,V)
and

\mathcal{H}_{RB}^{1}(A,V)

An algebraic motivation is the interpretation of the low degree cohomology spaces in terms of
algebraic properties of the weighted Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan algebra, which makes the cohomology spaces interesting invariant to compute.

Let
(A,\mathcal{I})
be a
\lambda
-weighted Rota-Baxter Jacobi-Jordan algebra and
(V,\mathcal{T})
be a representation of it. Let compute
\mathcal{H}_{RB}^{0}(A,V)
and
\mathcal{H}_{RB}^{1}(A,V)
.

•

\mathcal{H}_{RB}^{0}(A,V)

An element
v\in V
is in
\mathcal{Z}^{0}_{RB}(A,V)
if and only if
d_{RB}(v,v)=(0,0)
i.e
(d(v),-v)=(0,0)
. This holds only when
v=0
. Hence,
\mathcal{H}_{RB}^{0}(A,V)=\{0\}
.

•

\mathcal{H}_{RB}^{1}(A,V)

Let
(\eta,v)
be an element of
(\mathcal{C}^{1}_{RB}(A,V)
. Then
(\eta,v)
is in
\mathcal{Z}^{1}_{RB}(A,V)
if and only if
d_{RB}(\eta,v)=(0,0)
i.e
(d(\eta),-\tilde{d}(v)-\phi^{1}_{1}(\eta))=(0,0)
. So, we get the following identities:

(43)

\displaystyle\eta(x\ast y)=-\rho(x)(\eta(y))-\rho(y)(\eta(x)),\forall x,y\in A,

(44)

\displaystyle\eta(\mathcal{I}(x))-\mathcal{T}(\eta(x))=\mathcal{T}(\rho(x)v)-\rho(\mathcal{I}(x))v,\forall x,y\in A.

Then, from the Definition
4.3
, we conclude that
(\eta,v)
is an antiderivation of
(A,\mathcal{I})
with values in
(V,\mathcal{T})
i.e.,

\mathcal{Z}^{1}_{RB}(A,V)=\mathcal{AD}er(A,V)
. Furthermore, we have
\mathcal{B}^{1}_{RB}(A,V)=\{\delta_{RB}^{0}(v,v):=(\delta^{0}(v),-v),v\in V\}=Inn\mathcal{AD}er(A,V)
. Then,

(45)

\mathcal{H}^{1}_{RB}(A,V)=\mathcal{AD}er(A,V)/Inn\mathcal{AD}er(A,V).

For the particular case when
V=A
(the adjoint representation), we obtain

(46)

\mathcal{H}^{1}_{RB}(A):=\mathcal{AD}er(A)/Inn\mathcal{AD}er(A).

Example 4.8
.

Let
(A,\ast)
be the three-dimensional Jacobi-Jordan algebra where nonzero products in a given basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}
are
e_{1}\ast e_{2}=e_{3}=e_{2}\ast e_{1}
(see
[
9
]
).
The linear map
\mathcal{I}:A\rightarrow A
given in the basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}=\begin{pmatrix}r_{11}&r_{12}&r_{13}\\
r_{21}&r_{22}&r_{23}\\
r_{31}&r_{32}&r_{33}\end{pmatrix},r_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}\mbox{ for $1\leq i,j\leq 3$,}

is a
\lambda
-weighted Rota-Baxter operator on

(A,\ast)
if and only if

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}r_{13}=r_{23}=0\\
r_{11}r_{21}-r_{21}r_{33}=0\\
r_{12}r_{22}-r_{12}r_{33}=0\\
(r_{22}+r_{11}+\lambda)r_{33}-r_{11}r_{22}-r_{12}r_{21}=0.\\
\end{array}\right.

In particular for
r_{12}\neq 0
and
r_{21}\neq 0
, we must have
r_{12}=\frac{(\lambda+r_{11})r_{11}}{r_{12}}

with
r_{11}\neq 0
,
r_{11}\neq-\lambda
and
r_{33}=r_{22}=r_{11}.
Hence,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}=\begin{pmatrix}r_{11}&r_{12}&0\\
\frac{(\lambda+r_{11})r_{11}}{r_{12}}&r_{11}&0\\
r_{31}&r_{32}&r_{11}\end{pmatrix}.

Let
\delta:A\rightarrow A
be a linear map and
x\in A
. Then
(\delta,x)
is an antiderivation of
(A,\mathcal{I})
if and only if
x=\alpha e_{1}+\beta e_{2}+x_{3}e_{3}
where

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-d_{32}+\frac{3d_{11}r_{12}r_{31}}{r_{11}(r_{11}+\lambda)},

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-d_{31}+\frac{3d_{11}r_{32}}{r_{12}}

and the
matrix of
\delta
on the basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}
is given by

\displaystyle\delta=\begin{pmatrix}d_{11}&0&0\\
0&d_{11}&0\\
d_{31}&d_{32}&-2d_{11}\end{pmatrix},\mbox{ $d_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}$ for $1\leq i,j\leq 3$.}

In addition, for any
x\in A
,
(L_{x},-x)
where
L_{x}(y)=x\ast y,\ \forall y\in A
, is an (inner) antiderivation of
(A,\mathcal{I})
if and only if
x=x_{1}e_{1}+x_{2}e_{2}+x_{3}e_{3}
and the matrix of
L_{x}
on the basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}

\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
x_{2}&x_{1}&0\end{pmatrix}

\\
0\end{pmatrix}\right),\,\ d_{11}\in\mathbb{R}\right\}.

On the Jacobs-de Leeuw-Glicksberg decomposition
Micky Barthmann, Sohail Farhangi

Abstract
For any JdLG-admissible representation π \pi of a semigroup S S , we show that the reversible part is weakly equivalent to a unitary representation on a Hilbert space that decomposes into a direct sum of finite dimensional representations, and we give an alternative characterization of the almost weakly stable part in terms of the unique invariant mean on the space of weakly almost periodic functions. In the case that S S is a bi-amenable measured semigroup, we characterize the almost weakly stable part using invariant means and averages along Følner sequences. Moreover, we give a description of the unique projection onto the reversible part whose kernel is the almost weakly stable part in terms of ultrafilters.

Introduction

The compact-weak mixing decomposition states that for any unitary operator
U
acting on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, we have a decomposition
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{c}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{w}
with

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{c}

\displaystyle=\{\xi\in\mathcal{H}\ |\ \xi\text{ has pre-compact orbit under }U\}=\overline{\text{lin}}\left(\left\{\xi\in\mathcal{H}\ |\ U\xi=\lambda\xi\text{ for some }\lambda\in\mathbb{S}^{1}\right\}\right),

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{w}

\displaystyle=\left\{\xi\in\mathcal{H}\ |\ \lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}|\langle U^{n}\xi,\xi\rangle|=0\right\}=\left\{\xi\in\mathcal{H}\ |\ \lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}|\langle U^{n}\xi,\eta\rangle|=0\ \forall\ \eta\in\mathcal{H}\right\}.

Koopman and von Neumann
[
33
]
were the first to show that a dynamical system is weakly mixing if and only if it has no nontrivial eigenfunctions.
A version of the compact-weak mixing decomposition for locally compact groups was found by Godement
[
27
]
(see also
[
40
, Appendix 10.D]
), and a version for amenable semigroups was found by Dye
[
18
]
.
In between Godement and Dye, the compact-weak mixing decomposition was extended to various semigroups of operators on a Banach space by Jacobs, de Leeuw, and Glicksberg
[
30
,
17
]
, and is now known as the Jacobs-de Leeuw-Glicksberg (JdLG) decompoisition.
Several applications of the JdLG-decomposition can be found in the literature, e.g. it is used in ergodic Ramsey theory
[
7
, Theorem 2.3]
, the proof of the Erdös sumset conjecture
[
36
]
, the Fürstenberg-Zimmer structure theorem
[
19
]
, a proof of the Wiener-Wintner theorem
[
1
, Proposition 2.2]
, and more generally in the study of weighted ergodic theorems
[
24
,
4
]
.
Further uses include results related to the entangled ergodic theorem
[
23
]
,
the Perron-Frobenius theory for completely positive operators on
W^{*}
-algebras
[
5
]
,
and evolution equations
[
25
, Section V.2]
.

A modern treatment of the JdLG decomposition is given in
[
21
, Chapter 16]
.
In the present paper we will have a heavy focus on representations of semigroups, so we give a statement of the JdLG decomposition as follows.

[

\displaystyle=\{\xi\in E\ |\ \forall\ u\in\mathscr{S},\exists\ v\in\mathscr{S}\text{ with }vu\xi=\xi\}\text{ and}

Remark

In many cases of interest, the Banach space

The structure of this paper is as follows.
In Section 2, we collect known facts about topological semigroups, weak almost periodicity, JdLG-admissible semigroups, amenability, representations of semigroups, and ultrafilters.
In Section 3 we study the reversible part of the JdLG-decomposition.
In Section 4 we study the almost weakly stable part of the JdLG-decomposition.
In Section 5 we study the relationship between the JdLG-decomposition and the algebra of the Stone-Čech compactification.

Preliminaries

2.1
Topological semigroups and weak almost periodicity

A semigroup
(S,\cdot)
is a set
S
with an associative binary operatation
\cdot:S\times S\rightarrow S
.
If
S
possesses an identity element
e
, then
S
is called a
monoid
.
If
S
is also a Hausdorff topological space and the map
\cdot
is continuous, then
S
is a
topological semigroup
1
1

1

We warn the reader that in
[
17
]
, the term topological semigroup refers to what we call a semitopological semigroup.
.
If
S
is a Hausdorff topological space for which
\cdot
is separately continuous, i.e., for each
s\in S
the maps
L_{s}(t):=s\cdot t
and
R_{s}(t):=t\cdot s
are continuous, then
S
is a
semitopological semigroup
.
For the rest of this paper all semigroups are assumed to be semitopological unless it is explicitly stated otherwise.
By abuse of notation, we let
R
act on
C_{b}(S)
and
L^{\infty}(S,\lambda)
for some Borel measure
\lambda
, by
R_{s}(f)(t)=f(R_{s}(t))=f(t\cdot s)
, and similarly for
L
.
A function
f\in C_{b}(S)
is
weakly almost periodic
if
\{R_{s}f\ |\ s\in S\}
is relatively weakly compact.
We let
W(S)
denote the space of weakly almost periodic functions on
S
.
De Leeuw and Glicksberg
[
17
, Theorem 2.7]
showed that if
S
is a compact semigroup, then
W(S)=C(S)
.
For
X\in\{C_{b}(S),W(S),L^{\infty}(S,\lambda)\}
, a
left-invariant mean
on
X
is a positive linear functional
m:X\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
of norm
1
that satisfies
m(L_{s}f)=m(f)
for all
s\in S
and all
f\in X
, and a right-invariant mean is defined similarly.
The semigroup
S
admits a
bi-invariant mean
on
X
if there exists a left-invariant mean
m
on
X
that is also a right-invariant mean.

In
[
17
, Theorem 5.8]
it is shown that if
W(S)
admits a bi-invariant mean, then
W(S)
admits a unique bi-invariant mean.
It is also known that
W(S)
admits a bi-invariant mean if and only it it admits a left-invariant mean and a (potentially different) right-invariant mean.
Now let
S
be a semigroup with bi-invariant mean
m
on
W(S)
.
If
\mathscr{S}
is a compact semigroup and
\pi:S\rightarrow\mathscr{S}
is a continuous homomorphism with dense image, then
\tilde{\pi}:C(\mathscr{S})\rightarrow W(S)
is given by
(\tilde{\pi}f)(s)=f(\pi_{s})
is well defined and
M(f):=m(\tilde{\pi}f)
is the bi-invariant mean on
W(\mathscr{S})
(see
[
17
, Lemma 5.2]
).
There exists a compact semigroup
S^{w}
, the weakly almost periodic compactification of
S
, and a continuous injective homomorphism
i:S\rightarrow S^{w}
with dense image, such that for any
f\in W(S)
there is a unique
f^{w}\in W(S^{w})
satisfying
f^{w}(i(s))=f(s)
for all
s\in S
(cf.
[
17
, Theorem 5.3]
).
By abuse of notation, we let
i:W(S)\rightarrow W(S^{w})
denote the map
f\mapsto f^{w}
, and we observe that
i
is an algebra isomorphism.
It is known that
W(S^{w})
admits a bi-invariant mean
M
if and only if
K(S^{w})
, the smallest two-sided ideal of
S^{w}
, is a compact topological group and
M(f)=\int_{K(S^{w})}fd\mu
, where
\mu
is the normalized Haar measure on
K(S^{w})
.

Now suppose that
W(S^{w})
admits a bi-invariant mean
M
and a left-invariant mean
M_{\ell}
.
For
f\in W(S^{w})
and
g\in K(S^{w})
, we have
M_{\ell}(f)=M_{\ell}(L_{g}f)
, hence
M_{\ell}(f)
is determined by the values of
f
on
K(S^{w})
. Tietze’s Extension Theorem tells us that any
f\in C(K(S^{w}))
extends to a member of
W(S^{w})
, so the left-invariant means on
W(S^{w})
are in a one-to-one correspondence with the left-invariant means on
C(K(S^{w}))
.
The uniqueness of the Haar measure tells us that there is exactly one left-invariant mean on
C(K(S^{w}))
, hence
M_{\ell}=M
.
Since the means on
W(S)
are in a one-to-one correspondence with the means on
W(S^{w})
, the analogous results holds for
W(S)
.

A group
(G,\cdot)
is a
topological group
if it is a topological semigroup and the inversion map is a continuous map.
All groups that we consider in this paper will be topological groups.
De Leeuw and Glicksberg
[
17
, Theorem 5.14]
investigated which topological groups
G
admit a bi-invariant mean on
W(G)
.

6
years later, Ryll-Nardzewski
[
39
]
proved a fixed point theorem that allows one to deduce that all locally compact groups

admit a bi-invariant mean on

.
For further discussions on this topic the reader is referred to
[
28
,
13
,
14
]
.

2.2
JdLG-admissible semigroups

For a Banach space
E
we let
\mathcal{L}(E)
denote the space of bounded linear operators on
E
.

\mathcal{L}(E)
is naturally a semitopological semigroup when endowed with the operation of composition as well as the weak operator topology.
In
[
21
, Chapter 16.3]
, a semigroup
\mathscr{T}\subseteq\mathcal{L}(E)
is called JdLG-admissible if it is relatively compact and
\mathscr{S}:=\operatorname{cl}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{T})
contains a unique minimal idempotent
Q
.
We will now show that this definition is equivalent to our definition.
To this end, let us temporarily refer to the definition of JdLG-admissible from Section 1 as JdLG-decomposable in order to avoid confusion.
If
\mathscr{T}
is a relatively weakly compact semigroup, then
[
21
, Theorem 16.5]
says that the following are equivalent:

1.

\mathscr{T}
is JdLG-admissible.

2.

K(\mathscr{S})
, the smallest two-sided ideal of
\mathscr{S}
, is a group.

3.

\mathscr{S}
has a unique minimal right ideal and a unique minimal left ideal.

[
21
, Theorem 16.3]
tells us that every minimal right ideal of
\mathscr{S}
is closed, and
[
21
, Lemma 16.4]
tells us that
K(\mathscr{S})
is the union of all minimal right ideals of
\mathscr{S}
.
It follows that when
\mathscr{T}
is JdLG-admissible,
K(\mathscr{S})
is a closed (hence compact) subsemigroup of
\mathscr{S}
.

[
17
, Theorem 2.1]
says that a compact semitopological semigroup that is algebraically a group must be a topological group.
It follows that
\mathscr{T}
is JdLG-admissible if and only if
K(\mathscr{S})
is a compact topological group.

[
17
, Theorem 4.11]
states that
K(\mathscr{S})
is a compact topological group if and only if
\mathscr{T}
is JdLG-decomposable.
Putting everything together, we see that
\mathscr{T}
is JdLG-decomposable if and only if it is JdLG-admissible.

2.3
Amenable semigroups and Følner nets

The pair
(S,\lambda)
is a
measured semigroup
if
S
is a semitopological semigroup endowed with a Borel measure
\lambda
.
The main cases of interest are when
S
is a discrete semigroup and
\lambda
is the counting measure, or when
S
is a subsemigroup of a locally compact topological group and
\lambda
is the restriction of a Haar measure.
The pair
(S,\lambda)
is
left-amenable
if there exists a left-invariant mean
m
on
L^{\infty}(S,\lambda)
, and it is
bi-amenable
if there exists a bi-invariant mean on
L^{\infty}(S,\lambda)
.
For the rest of this section let
S
denote a locally compact semitopological semigroup and let
\lambda
be a Radon measure on
S
.
A
left-Følner net
is a net
\mathcal{F}=(F_{i})_{i\in I}
is a net of compact subsets of
S
satisfying

\lim_{i}\frac{\lambda(sF_{i}\triangle F_{i})}{\lambda(F_{i})}=0\text{ for all }s\in S.

(2)

\mathcal{F}
is a
two-sided Følner net
if it is sumltaneously a left-Følner net and a right-Følner net.
If
S
possesses a left/right/two-sided Følner net
\mathcal{F}=(F_{i})_{i\in I}
then
S
is left/right/bi amenable.
To see this, we define a net of functionals
(\mu_{i})_{i\in I}
on
L^{\infty}(S,\lambda)^{\prime}
by
\mu_{i}(f)=\frac{1}{\lambda(F_{i})}\int_{F_{i}}f(s)d\lambda(s)
for all
f\in L^{\infty}(S,\lambda)
.
Since the unit ball of
L^{\infty}(S,\lambda)^{\prime}
is weak
∗
compact, we let
\mu
be any cluster point of
(\mu_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
and we see that
\mu
is a left/right/two-sided invariant mean on
L^{\infty}(S,\lambda)
.
We say that
\mu
is a mean generated by
\mathcal{F}
and we denote the set of such means by
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{F})
.

There are examples of discrete semigroups that are amenable but do not possess a Følner net.
However, if
S
is a discrete cancellative semigroup, then
S
is amenable if and only if it possesses a Følner net.
It is also well known that if
S
is a discrete abelian semigroup, then
S
possesses a two-sided Følner net.
We refer the reader to
[
37
, Chapter 4.22]
for further discussion on Følner conditions and amenability for discrete semigroups.

Now suppose that
S
is a left-amenable semigroup.
We define the upper (left) and lower (left) Banach densities on
S
by

\displaystyle d^{*}(A)

\displaystyle=\sup\{m(A)|m\text{ is a left-invariant mean on }S\},\text{ and}

\displaystyle d_{*}(A)

\displaystyle=\inf\{m(A)|m\text{ is a left-invariant mean on }S\}=1-d^{*}(A^{c}).

If we further assume that
S
possesses a left-Følner net
\mathcal{F}=(F_{i})_{i\in I}
, then we define the upper and lower densities along
\mathcal{F}
by

\overline{d}_{\mathcal{F}}(A):=\limsup_{i}\frac{\lambda(A\cap F_{i})}{\lambda(F_{i})}\text{ and }\underline{d}_{\mathcal{F}}(A):=\liminf_{i}\frac{\lambda(A\cap F_{i})}{\lambda(F_{i})},

respectively.
If
\overline{d}_{\mathcal{F}}(A)=\underline{d}_{\mathcal{F}}(A)
, then we denote the common value by
d_{\mathcal{F}}(A)
.
We define the upper (left) and lower (left) Følner densities on
S
by

\displaystyle\overline{d}(A)

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\overline{d}_{\mathcal{F}}(A)|\mathcal{F}\text{ is a (left) f\o lner net in }S\right\},\text{ and}

\displaystyle\underline{d}(A)

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\overline{d}_{\mathcal{F}}(A)|\mathcal{F}\text{ is a (left) f\o lner net in }S\right\}=1-\overline{d}(A^{c}).

If
S
is a discrete semigroup and
\lambda
is the counting measure, then
[
26
, Theorem 3.15]
tells us that
\overline{d}=d^{*}
and
\underline{d}=d_{*}
.
More generally, we see that
\overline{d}\leq d^{*}
(hence
\underline{d}\geq d_{*}
) since any Følner sequences generates many means, but it is not clear if
d^{*}\leq\overline{d}
.

Suppose that we are given a function
f:S\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
and
D\in\{d_{*},\underline{d},d_{\mathcal{F}}\}
that is well-defined with respect to
S
.
We write

D-\lim_{x}f(x)=\alpha

if for any open neighborhood
U
of
\alpha
,

D(\{x\in X:f(x)\in U\})=1.

Given a topological space
Y
, a function
f:X\rightarrow Y
, and a set
A\subseteq X
, we write

\lim_{\underset{x\in A}{x\to\infty}}f(x)=\alpha

if for any open neighborhood
U
of
\alpha
the set
\{x\in A|f(x)\notin U\}
is compact.

A group
G
is
amenable
if there exists a left-invariant mean
m_{\ell}
on
C_{r}(G)
, the space of bounded right uniformly continuous functions on
G
.
If
G
is amenable, then there exists a right-invariant mean
m_{r}
on
C_{\ell}(G)
given by
m_{r}(f(g))=m_{\ell}(f(g^{-1}))
, so we do not distinguish between left and right amenability for groups.
If
G
is locally compact and
\lambda
is a Haar measure, then
G
is amenable if and only if
(G,\lambda)
is amenable.
Furthermore,
(G,\lambda)
is amenable if and only if
(G,\lambda)
possesses a left-Følner net.
In any topological group
G
we have that
W(G)\subseteq C_{r}(G)\cap C_{\ell}(G)
.
It follows that if
G
is amenable, then
W(G)
admits a left-invariant mean and a right-invariant mean, hence it admits a bi-invariant mean.
However, if
G
is not a locally compact amenable group and
m
is a left-invariant mean on
C_{r}(G)
, then
m
cannot in general be extended to a left-invariant mean on
C_{b}(G)
, let alone the space of bounded Borel measurable functions on
G
.
For this reasons, Theorems
4.2
and
5.2
do not apply to general amenable groups.

A group
G
is
extremely amenable
if every continuous action of
G
on a compact Hausdorff space has a fixed point.
If
G
is extremely amenable, then there exists a left invariant mean
m
on
C_{r}(G)
that is multiplicative in the sense that
m(fg)=m(f)m(g)
for all
f,g\in C_{r}(G)
, which justifies the name of this class of groups.
The only locally compact group that is extremely amenable is the trivial group.

Definition 2.1

(cf.
[
21
]

.

.

5

Let

Sharp asymptotics for N N -point correlation functions of coalescing heavy-tailed random walks
Jinjiong Yu

Abstract
We study a system of coalescing continuous-time random walks starting from every site on ℤ {\mathbb{Z}} , where the jump increments lie in the domain of attraction of an α \alpha -stable distribution with α ∈ ( 0 , 1 ] \alpha\in(0,1] . We establish sharp asymptotics for the N N -point correlation function of the system. Our analysis relies on two precise tail estimates for the system density, as well as the non-collision probability of N N independent random walks with arbitrary fixed initial configurations. In addition, we derive refined estimates for heavy-tailed random walks, which may be of independent interest.

Introduction

A system of
coalescing random walks
(CRW) consists of particles performing i.i.d. rate 1 continuous-time random walks, with immediate coalescence when particles meet.
This fundamental model has attracted sustained interest across disciplines due to its dual role as both a mathematically tractable system and a prototypical example connecting diverse physical phenomena. Its duality with the voter model through graphical constructions
Lig (05)
, relevance to chemical reaction kinetics
SFH (01)
, and application to domain wall dynamics in Glauber spin systems
FDM (98)
underscore its broad significance.

When formulated on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, the CRW can be represented as a
\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
-valued Markov process, where occupied (resp. vacant) sites correspond to 1 (resp. 0) states.
Consider a system of CRW starting from every site on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
.
A central quantity of interest is the particle density
\rho_{1}(t)
, defined via translation invariance as the origin’s occupation probability. Pioneering work by Bramson and Griffeath
BG (80)
, based on a moment calculation, established dimension-dependent asymptotics for simple random walks:

\rho_{1}(t)\sim\begin{cases}\displaystyle 1/\sqrt{\pi t}&d=1,\\
\displaystyle\log t/(\pi t)&d=2,\\
\displaystyle 1/(\varsigma_{d}t)&d\geq 3,\end{cases}

(1)

where
\varsigma_{d}
denotes the escape probability for
d
-dimensional simple random walks.
Cox and Perkins
CP (04)
later provided a probabilistic proof using the super-Brownian invariance principle for the dual voter model.
For
d\geq 3
, where the simple random walk is transient, van den Berg and Kesten
vdBK (00)
;
vdBK (02)
developed a robust approach by deriving an ordinary differential equation for
\rho_{1}(t)
.

A natural extension of this study is the
N
-point correlation function

\rho_{N}({\bf x},t)
, defined as the occupation probability at sites
{\bf x}:=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})
at time
t
, see (
12
).
In the marginally recurrent case of coalescing simple random walks in 2D, Lukins, Tribe and Zaboronski
LTZ (18)
generalized the method of
vdBK (00)
and proved

\rho_{N}({\bf x},t)\sim\rho_{1}(t)^{N}\times p_{\rm NC}({\bf x},t),

(2)

where the correction term
p_{\rm NC}({\bf x},t)
is the
non-collision probability
of
N
independent walks starting from
{\bf x}
by time
t
, and it is shown in
(
CMP, 10
, Prop 1.3)
that
p_{\rm NC}({\bf x},t)\sim c_{\bf x}(\log t)^{-n(n-1)/2}
.
This method can be extended to
d\geq 3
, but fails for
d=1
due to the strong correlations induced by the recurrence of the underlying random walk.
Nevertheless, in the case of 1D coalescing Brownian motions,
MRTZ (06)
derived the asymptotics for
\rho_{N}({\bf x},t)
, relying crucially on the integrability of the system–most notably, the explicit Karlin-McGregor formula.

In contrast to the nearest-neighbor case, much less is known about the
N
-point function for CRW with long-range jumps.
The only existing result in this direction is for 1D coalescing
\alpha
-stable processes with
\alpha>1
, where the density
\rho_{1}(t)
has asymptotic order
t^{1/\alpha}
, derived as a corollary of
(
EMS, 13
, Thm 6.3 and Prop 2.5)
and the scaling property of
\alpha
-stable process.

Motivated by this gap, we study coalescing heavy-tailed random walks on
{\mathbb{Z}}
, where the jump increments lie in the domain of attraction of
\alpha
-stable distributions for
0<\alpha\leq 1
.
Our results reveal that a form of (
2
) remains valid in this setting. Notably,
\alpha=1
is the critical point for recurrence/transience, and our study encompasses a class of recurrent random walks.

1.1
Model and main result

It is well known
Lig (05)
that CRW can be constructed by sequentially adding independent random walks, halting each upon its first collision with any of the previously added paths.
Precisely, the coalescing heavy-tailed random walks
(\xi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
on
{\mathbb{Z}}
can be defined as a
\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}}
-valued Markov process by specifying its generator thanks to
(
Lig, 05
, Chapter I, Thm 3.9)
.
For
\xi\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}}
and
{\bf x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
, write
\xi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}):=(\xi(x_{1}),\ldots,\xi(x_{n}))\in\{0,1\}^{n}
.
We simplify notation by writing
\xi({\bf x})=1
if
\xi({\bf x})=(1,\ldots,1)
, and
\xi=1
if
\xi(x)=1
for all
x\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Additionally, we represent elements of
\{0,1\}^{n}
as binary strings, for example,
(0,1)
is written as
01
.
With this notation, the generator
{\cal G}
of
(\xi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
applied on local cylinder functions

\xi
, is given by

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\cal G}f(\xi)=\sum_{x,y\in{\mathbb{Z}}}p(y-x)1_{\{\xi(x,y)=10\}}\big\{f(\xi+e_{y}-e_{x})-f(\xi)\big\}\\[5.0pt]
\hskip 62.59596pt\displaystyle+\sum_{x,y\in{\mathbb{Z}}}p(y-x)1_{\{\xi(x,y)=11\}}\big\{f(\xi-e_{x})-f(\xi)\big\},\end{array}

(3)

where
e_{x}(y)=1_{\{x=y\}}
, and
p(\cdot)
with

and
\sum_{x}p(x)=1
denotes the jump kernel, which is in the domain of attraction of an
\alpha
-stable distribution.
Without loss of generality, we assume that

is aperiodic.
By
(
IL, 71
, Thm 2.6.1)
, it satisfies

\sum_{|y|\geq x}p(y)=\frac{L(x)}{x^{\alpha}}\big(1+o(1)\big)\quad\text{as}~x\to\infty,

(4)

where
L:[0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
is a
slowly varying function
(abbreviated as SVF throughout this paper), meaning that for any
c>0
, it satisfies
L(cx)/L(x)\to 1
as
x\to\infty
.

To avoid complexity, we assume that
p
is
symmetric
throughout the paper.
Although, in principle, our results should remain valid in the non-symmetric case, there is a technical obstacle in adapting the present proof, and we therefore leave this extension open (see Subsection
1.3
and Remark
2.8
for further discussion).
For the SVF
L
, there exist
1/\alpha
-regularly varying functions
b_{t}:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
such that
L(b_{t})/b_{t}^{\hskip 1.0pt\alpha}\sim 1/t
.
Let
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a rate 1 continuous-time random walk with jump kernel
p
and initial position
X_{0}=0
.
It is well known that
X_{t}/b_{t}
converges weakly to an
\alpha
-stable distribution with probability density function
g_{\alpha}(x)
.
By the asymptotic uniqueness of
b_{t}

(
BGT, 89
, Thm 1.5.12)
and Gnedenko’s local limit theorem
(
IL, 71
, Thm 4.2.1)
, we can choose a specific
1/\alpha
-regularly varying function

B_{t}:={\mathbb{P}}(X_{t}=0)^{-1}g_{\alpha}(0)

(5)

such that
B_{t}/t^{1/\alpha}
is a SVF,

\frac{L(B_{t})}{B_{t}^{\alpha}}\sim\frac{1}{t}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\big|B_{t}{\mathbb{P}}(X_{t}=x)-g_{\alpha}(x/B_{t})\big|=o(1).

(6)

Recall that
X
is recurrent if and only if
G(0)=\lim\limits_{x\to\infty}G(0,t)=\infty
, where
G(x,t):=\int_{0}^{t}{\mathbb{P}}(X_{s}=x)\mathrm{d}s
denotes the Green function of
X
at
x
up to time
t
.
Following this standard approach, define

l(t):=\frac{G(0,t)}{g_{\alpha}(0)}=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{B_{s}}\mathrm{d}s,\quad\ell(t):=\frac{B_{t}\hskip 1.0ptl(t)}{t}\quad\mbox{and}\quad l(\infty):=\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}l(t)\in(0,\infty].

(7)

In the regime
\alpha\in(0,1]
,

\begin{array}[]{rclcl}\displaystyle X~\text{is (marginally) recurrent}&\Leftrightarrow&l(\infty)=\infty,&&\text{if~}\alpha=1~\text{and}~1/B_{t}~\text{is not integrable,}\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle X~\text{is transient}&\Leftrightarrow&l(\infty)<\infty,&&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}

(8)

In both cases,
l(t)
is a SVF (hence we adopt the notation
l
instead of
G
).
Moreover,
\ell(t)
diverges by Karamata’s theorem and is
(\frac{1}{\alpha}\!-\!1)
-regularly varying.
In the marginally recurrent case, we impose an additional technical assumption to ensure that the recurrence is sufficiently weak, in the sense that the tail probability of the first return time decays slowly.

Function class
{\cal B}
:
B_{t}\in{\cal B}
if there exists some
K>0
such that
\ell(t)^{K}\geq l(t)
for all large
t
.

Therefore, we focus on two regimes: either the underlying random walk is transient, or it is marginally recurrent with
B_{t}\in{\cal B}
.
In our framework, the condition
B_{t}\in{\cal B}
cannot be easily removed due to technical reasons; see Subsection
1.3
for further discussion.

To better illustrate
{\cal B}
, we provide two examples in the marginally recurrent case with
\alpha=1
.

•

If
L(t)\sim(\log t)^{a}
for some
a<1
, then
B_{t}\sim t(\log t)^{a}
,
l(t)\asymp(\log t)^{1-a}
and
\ell(t)\asymp\log t
, so that

with any
K>1-a
.

•

If
L(t)\sim\exp\{-(\log t)^{\kappa}\}
for some
\kappa\in(0,1)
, then
B_{t}\sim t\hskip 1.0pt\exp\{-(\log t)^{\kappa}\}
, which yields that
l(t)\asymp(\log t)^{1-\kappa}\exp\{(\log t)^{\kappa}\}
and
\ell(t)\asymp(\log t)^{1-\kappa}
, and hence
B_{t}\notin{\cal B}
.

Theorem 1.1 (Density asymptotics)

Let
(\xi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be the coalescing heavy-tailed random walks on
{\mathbb{Z}}
with initial configuration
\xi_{0}\equiv 1
.
Assume the random walk has rate 1 and a symmetric jump kernel

of the form (
4
) with
0<\alpha\leq 1
.
In the marginally recurrent case, further assume
B_{t}\in{\cal B}
so that
\ell(t)^{K}\geq l(t)
for some
K
.
The density of CRW at time

is

\rho_{1}(t):={\mathbb{E}}[\xi_{t}(0)]={\mathbb{P}}(\xi_{t}(0)=1).

Then, there exists some
\eta>0
such that for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\rho_{1}(t)=\frac{g_{\alpha}(0)\hskip 1.0ptl(2t)}{t}\big(1+O\big({\cal R}_{1}(t)\big)\big),

(9)

where the error bound

can be chosen as follows.

(1)
In the marginally recurrent case where
\alpha=1
,

and

.

(2)

There exists a natural coupling between coalescing random walks and independent random walks so that the two collections of paths are identical up to the first collision time. For convenience, the following theorem is stated for independent walks.

Theorem 1.2 (Non-collision probability asymptotics)

1.2
Proof strategy

We first outline the proof of Theorems
1.1
.
For high-dimensional (
d\geq 3
) coalescing random walks, van der Berg and Kesten
vdBK (00)
;
vdBK (02)
developed a framework to analyze the asymptotics of the system density
\rho_{1}(t)
by deriving an ODE through the generator calculations.
This robust method has since been applied for CRW on transitive graphs
HLYZ (22)
and to coalescing systems with multiple particle types
TZ (24)
.
Lukins, Tribe, and Zaboronski
LTZ (18)
adapted this framework to the barely recurrent case in two dimensions.
Furthermore, it is adapted by Lukins, Tribe and Zaboronski
LTZ (18)
to two-dimensional coalescing random walks which is barely recurrent.
In our setup, we start by applying the generator (
3
) to the density function to obtain

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\rho_{1}(t)=-\sum_{y}p(y){\mathbb{P}}(\xi_{t}(0,y)=11)

(14)

To have particles both at sites
0
and
y
at time
t
, we can trace back the trajectories for their ancestors (which are typically unique) over a time interval
s
(with
s\ll t
).
In effect, we consider these as two backward random walks that have not coalesced between
t-s
and
t
.
By applying the Markov property at time
t-s
, we can decompose

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(\xi_{t}(0,y)=11)\displaystyle\approx\sum_{z_{1},z_{2}}{\mathbb{P}}(\xi_{t-s}(z_{1},z_{2})=11)\hskip 1.0pt{\mathbb{P}}(\widehat{X}^{0,y}_{s}=(z_{1},z_{2}),\hat{\tau}_{y}>s),

(15)

where
(\widehat{X}^{0,y}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
denotes two independent backward random walks starting from
0
and
y
, respectively, and
\hat{\tau}_{y}
is their first collision time.
Choosing
s=s(t)
with
1\ll s\ll t
appropriately ensures that the backward walks are typically far apart at time
s
(i.e.,
|z_{1}-z_{2}|\asymp B_{s(t)}
), so that
{\mathbb{P}}(\xi_{t-s}(z_{1},z_{2})=11)\approx{\mathbb{P}}(\xi_{t-s}(z_{1})=1)\times{\mathbb{P}}(\xi_{t-s}(z_{2})=1)=\rho_{1}^{2}(t-s)
.
Substituting this into (
15
) and using
\rho_{1}(t-s)\approx\rho_{1}(t)
(since
t-s
is close to
t
) yields

{\mathbb{P}}(\xi_{t}(0,y)=11)\approx\rho_{1}^{2}(t-s){\mathbb{P}}(\hat{\tau}_{y}>s)\approx\rho_{1}^{2}(t){\mathbb{P}}(\hat{\tau}_{y}>t),

(16)

where we used the fact that the random walk is either
transient
or
barely recurrent
(in the sense that
B_{t}\in{\cal B}
) so that
{\mathbb{P}}(\hat{\tau}_{y}>s)\approx{\mathbb{P}}(\hat{\tau}_{y}>t)
for a suitable choice of
s=s(t)
.
Combining (
14
) and (
16
) produces a simple ODE for
\rho_{1}(t)
.

Note that (
15
)-(
16
) shows an approximation for the two-point function.
One can extend these ideas to general
{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{N}
to prove Theorem
1.3
.
First, we can establish the asymptotic independence

{\mathbb{P}}(\widehat{X}^{{\bf x}}_{s}={\bf z},\hat{\tau}_{{\bf x}}>s)\approx{\mathbb{P}}(\widehat{X}^{{\bf x}}_{s}={\bf z}){\mathbb{P}}(\hat{\tau}_{{\bf x}}>s),

(17)

since, for either transient or barely recurrent processes, the non-coalescing event
\{\hat{\tau}_{{\bf x}}>s\}
is essentially determined during an initial time interval
[0,u]
with
u\ll s
, while the positions
\widehat{X}^{{\bf x}}_{s}
are mainly determined by the increments from
u
to
s
. Consequently, we obtain

\rho_{N}({\bf x},t)\approx\sum_{\bf z}\rho_{N}({\bf z},t-s){\mathbb{P}}(\widehat{X}^{{\bf x}}_{s}={\bf z},\hat{\tau}_{{\bf x}}>s)\approx\rho_{1}^{N}(t-s)\hskip 1.0pt{\mathbb{P}}(\hat{\tau}_{{\bf x}}>s)\approx\rho_{1}^{N}(t)\hskip 1.0pt{\mathbb{P}}(\hat{\tau}_{{\bf x}}>t),

(18)

where we use the approximation
\rho_{N}({\bf z},t)\approx\prod_{i}\rho_{1}(z_{i},t)=\rho_{1}^{N}(t)
for typical well-separated
{\bf z}
.

It remains to establish Theorem
1.2
, which concerns the non-collision probability
p_{\rm NC}(t)=p_{\rm NC}({\bf x},t)={\mathbb{P}}(\tau_{{\bf x}}>t)
for
N
independent random walks
({\mathbf{X}}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
.
The first step is to derive a crude bound Lemma
3.2
, so that we can choose a suitable
s=s(t)\ll t
satisfying
p_{\rm NC}(s)\approx p_{\rm NC}(t)
.
Then, we derive an ODE for
p_{\rm NC}(t)
in a manner analogous to the previous arguments, as also done in
LTZ (18)
for 2D simple CRW.
Since the decrease in
p_{\rm NC}(t)
is due to collisions between any two random walks
X^{i}
and
X^{j}
, by the symmetry of
p
and translation invariance we have

\begin{array}[]{r@{\,}c@{\,}l}\displaystyle-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}p_{\rm NC}(t)&=&\displaystyle\sum_{i<j}\sum_{x_{i},x_{j}}2p(x_{i}-x_{j}){\mathbb{P}}_{{\bf x}}(\tau_{{\bf x}}>t,X^{i}_{t}=x_{i},X^{j}_{t}=x_{j})\\[10.0pt]
&=&\displaystyle\sum_{i<j}\sum_{y_{ij}}2p(y_{ij}){\mathbb{P}}_{{\bf x}}(\tau_{{\bf x}}>t,\Delta^{ij}_{t}=y_{ij}),\end{array}

(19)

where
\Delta^{ij}_{t}:=X^{i}_{t}-X^{j}_{t}
and
y_{ij}:=y_{i}-y_{j}
.
Decompose at time
s
and evolve
\Delta^{ij}
backward from
t
to
s
.
As only
\Delta^{ij}_{t}
is fixed, other free pairs of walks are unlikely to collide from
s
to
t
. Thus,

{\mathbb{P}}_{{\bf x}}(\tau_{{\bf x}}>t,\Delta^{ij}_{t}=y_{ij})\approx\sum_{z_{ij}}{\mathbb{P}}_{{\bf x}}(\Delta^{ij}_{s}=z_{ij},\tau_{{\bf x}}>s){\mathbb{P}}_{y_{ij}}(\widehat{\Delta}^{ij}_{t-s}=z_{ij},\hat{\tau}_{ij}>t-s).

(20)

Applying asymptotic independence to
{\mathbb{P}}_{y_{ij}}(\widehat{\Delta}^{ij}_{t-s}=z_{ij},\hat{\tau}_{ij}>t-s)
and noting
|y_{ij}|,|z_{ij}|\ll t^{1/\alpha}
, the second term in the r.h.s. of (
20
) can be replaced by
{\mathbb{P}}_{0}(\widehat{\Delta}^{ij}_{t-s}=0)\hskip 1.0pt{\mathbb{P}}_{y_{ij}}(\hat{\tau}_{ij}>t-s)
.
This, together with (
19
) and a priori estimate
p_{\rm NC}(s)\approx p_{\rm NC}(t)
, implies that

-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}p_{\rm NC}(t)\displaystyle\approx p_{\rm NC}(t)\cdot\sum_{i<j}\sum_{y_{ij}}2p(y_{ij}){\mathbb{P}}_{y_{ij}}(\hat{\tau}_{ij}>t-s)\hskip 1.0pt{\mathbb{P}}_{0}(\widehat{\Delta}^{ij}_{t-s}=0).

(21)

Using standard estimates on the double sum then yields the desired asymptotics for
p_{\rm NC}(t)
.

1.3
Discussion

To the best of our knowledge, Theorem
1.3
is the first result to investigate the
N
-point function for coalescing heavy-tailed random walks, revealing a rich structure of spatial correlations in these systems.
Along the way, we establish several sharp estimates for heavy-tailed random walks (particularly in the regime
\alpha\leq 1
), which have not appeared in the literature before. We derive quantitative error bounds in asymptotics for key quantities such as the transition probability, the potential kernel, and first hitting times. These estimates are of independent interest and may serve as valuable tools for future investigations into heavy-tailed processes.
Together, these contributions constitute the main novelty of the present work.

In the marginally recurrent case, the assumption that
B_{t}\in\mathcal{B}
is of a technical nature.
More precisely, to obtain a quantitative estimate of the non-collision probability
p_{\rm NC}(t)
in Theorem
1.2
, an intermediate bound of the type established in Lemma
3.1
is required, where one of the error terms is expressed in terms of
\ell(t)^{-\kappa}
.
If the condition
B_{t}\in\mathcal{B}
fails so that
\ell(t)^{K}\leq l(t)
for all
K
, then in (
64
) the “error” term would, in contrast, dominate the “main” term
p_{\rm NC}(t)
which only involves a power of
l(t)
.
Nevertheless, we expect that the main result should remain valid for
\alpha=1
even when
B_{t}\notin\mathcal{B}
.
But in that case the proof would require an a priori crude bound on
p_{\rm NC}(t)
in terms of
l(t)
, which would in turn demand new ideas and techniques.
We therefore leave this as an open problem for future work.
We also note a possible extension to the case of non-symmetric jump kernels
p(\cdot)
.
As discussed in Remark
2.8
, the present proof strategy should still apply provided that Proposition
2.4
can be established for the potential kernel in the non-symmetric setting.

Looking ahead, one promising direction is to study the
N
-point function for
\alpha>1
.
In this regime, the approach of deriving an ODE becomes inapplicable due to the recurrence of the underlying random walk.
One possible avenue worth exploring is to establish a Karlin–McGregor formula for heavy-tailed random walks and to perform an asymptotic analysis of this formula, as is done for coalescing Brownian motions in
MRTZ (06)
. However, because heavy-tailed paths exhibit crossings caused by jumps, new methods would be required to derive an appropriate Karlin–McGregor formula in this setting.
Another direction is to investigate the scaling limit of the system. By rescaling space by
\varepsilon
and time by
\varepsilon^{\alpha}
and letting
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
, the resulting particle system initially starts from every point of
{\mathbb{R}}
.
It is known
EMS (13)
that if
\alpha>1
, the rescaled system exhibits the phenomenon of coming down from infinity, namely the particles become locally finite at any positive time.
However, this does not occur in our setting where
0<\alpha\leq 1
. Instead, one may consider the particles as a closed subset of
{\mathbb{R}}
. We conjecture that, in a suitable topology, the limiting set (as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
) at a positive time has a fractional Hausdorff dimension of
1-\alpha
, as suggested by Theorem
1.3
.

Organization.
The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we develop some sharp estimates for heavy-tailed random walks. Section
3
is devoted to proving Theorem
1.2
on the non-collision probability
p_{\rm NC}(t)
. Finally, in Section
4
we establish Theorem
1.1
for the density function
\rho_{1}(t)
and then prove Theorem
1.3
concerning the
N
-point correlation function.

2
Heavy-tailed random walks

Fundamental results for heavy-tailed random walks, such as transition probabilities, potential kernels, and first hitting times, are essential for our analysis.
Surprisingly, many of these results are not readily available in the existing literature.
Thus, we develop several basic properties in this section.
For future reference, we state our results in the most general setting.

Let
(X^{x}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be the heavy-tailed continuous-time random walk starting from
X^{x}_{0}=x
.
Throughout this section, we assume that the jump rate is 1 and that the symmetric jump kernel
p(\cdot)
has the form (
4
).
For brevity, when
x=0
we also write
(X^{0}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
as
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
.
By
(
IL, 71
, Thm 2.6.5)
and its proof, the characteristic function of
p
is given by

\phi(u)=\exp\big\{-\gamma|u|^{\alpha}\widetilde{L}\big(|u|^{-1}\big)\big\},

(22)

where
\widetilde{L}
is also a SVF such that
\widetilde{L}\sim L
, and

\gamma=\cos(\frac{\pi\alpha}{2})\Gamma(1-\alpha)~~\text{for}~\alpha\neq 1,\quad\mbox{and}\quad\gamma=\frac{\pi}{2}~~\text{for}~\alpha=1,

with
\Gamma
denoting the gamma function.
Let
N_{t}
denote the Poisson number of jumps of the random walk by time
t
.
Then the characteristic function of
X_{t}
is

\phi_{t}(u)={\mathbb{E}}\big[{\mathbb{E}}[e^{iuX_{t}}|N_{t}]\big]=\exp\big(-t(1-\phi(u))\big).

(23)

We will repeatedly use the following two results for a SVF
H(t)
.
The first is the Potter bound
(
BGT, 89
, Thm 1.5.6)
: There exists
c>0
such that for any
t\geq s\geq c
and any

(t/s)^{-\varepsilon}\leq H(t)/H(s)\leq(t/s)^{\varepsilon}.

(24)

The second result is Karamata’s theorem
(
BGT, 89
, Prop 1.5.8-1.5.10)
: For any
c>0
,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\text{For}~\alpha\in(0,1),&\displaystyle\int_{t}^{\infty}\frac{H(s)}{s^{1/\alpha}}\mathrm{d}s\sim\frac{1/\alpha-1}{t^{1/\alpha-1}}H(t);\\[10.0pt]
\text{For}~\alpha=1,&\displaystyle\int_{t}^{\infty}\hskip-2.0pt\frac{H(s)}{s}\mathrm{d}s~\text{is a SVF if it is finite, and~}\frac{1}{H(t)}\int_{t}^{\infty}\hskip-2.0pt\frac{H(s)}{s}\mathrm{d}s\to 0;\\[10.0pt]

\text{For}~\alpha\in(1,2),&\displaystyle\int_{c}^{t}\hskip-2.0pt\frac{H(s)}{s^{1/\alpha}}\mathrm{d}s\sim\frac{t^{1-1/\alpha}}{1-1/\alpha}H(t).\end{array}

(25)

2.1
Transition probability

One key input to the ODE framework is a sharp asymptotic estimate of the transition probability
{\mathbb{P}}(X_{t}=x)
.
While Gnedenko’s LLT provides an optimal
o(1)
error in approximating the transition probability by that of the limiting
\alpha
-stable process, our analysis requires a finer quantitative error bound. This motivates the following proposition on the difference between transition probabilities at distinct spatial points.
As a supplementary remark, we note that refined local limit theorems with quantitative error bounds are available when the random walk is assumed to lie in the domain of
normal
attraction of an
\alpha
-stable distribution (i.e., the SVF
\widetilde{L}
in (
22
) is constant and
B_{t}=t^{1/\alpha}
), see
CJR (23)
and references therein.

Proposition 2.1 (Transition probability difference)

For the heavy-tailed random walk
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
,
there exists a constant
C
such that for any
x,y\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,

\big|{\mathbb{P}}(X_{t}=x+y)-{\mathbb{P}}(X_{t}=x)\big|\leq\frac{C|y|}{B_{t}^{2}}.

(26)

Proof
By the inverse Fourier transform,

{\mathbb{P}}(X_{t}=x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{-iux}\phi_{t}(u)\mathrm{d}u=\frac{1}{2\pi B_{t}}\int_{-\pi B_{t}}^{\pi B_{t}}e^{-ixu/B_{t}}\phi_{t}(u/B_{t})\mathrm{d}u.

Using the inequality
|e^{i(x+y)u/B_{t}}-e^{ixu/B_{t}}|\leq|yu|/B_{t}
,

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\big|{\mathbb{P}}(X_{t}=x+y)-{\mathbb{P}}(X_{t}=x)\big|\leq\frac{|y|}{2\pi B_{t}^{2}}\int_{-\pi B_{t}}^{\pi B_{t}}|u\cdot\phi_{t}(u/B_{t})|\mathrm{d}u.\end{array}

(27)

Recalling
\phi_{t}(u)=\exp\{-t(1-\phi(u))\}
from (
23
) with
\phi(u)
given in (
22
), we have

|\phi_{t}(u/B_{t})|=\exp\Big\{-t\Big(1-e^{-\frac{\gamma|u|^{\alpha}\widetilde{L}(B_{t}/|u|)}{B_{t}^{\alpha}}}\Big)\Big\}\leq\exp\Big\{-\frac{c\gamma|u|^{\alpha}t\widetilde{L}(B_{t}/|u|)}{B_{t}^{\alpha}}\Big\},

(28)

where in the inequality we used that for small
x>0
,
1-e^{-x}\geq cx
for some
c>0
.
By (
6
) and the Potter bound (
24
), for any
0<\varepsilon<\alpha
,

\frac{t\widetilde{L}(B_{t})}{B_{t}^{\alpha}}\frac{\widetilde{L}(B_{t}/|u|)}{\widetilde{L}(B_{t})}\geq C|u|^{-\varepsilon},

which yields

|u\cdot\phi_{t}(u/B_{t})|\leq|u|\exp\{-t\cdot\frac{C\gamma|u|^{\alpha-\varepsilon}}{t}\}=|u|e^{-C\gamma|u|^{\alpha-\varepsilon}}.

Since
|u|e^{-C\gamma|u|^{\alpha-\varepsilon}}
is integrable over
{\mathbb{R}}
, the bound (
27
) implies the desired result.

Remark 2.2

If the random walk has jump rate
r
, one may identify it with a rate 1 random walk evolving over time
rt
. Then,

\big|{\mathbb{P}}(X_{t}=x+y)-{\mathbb{P}}(X_{t}=x)\big|\leq C|y|/B_{rt}^{2}\leq C_{r}|y|/B_{t}^{2}.

(29)

The same proof applies when
p
is non-symmetric. In that case, the characteristic function is

\phi^{\text{ns}}(u)=\exp\big\{icu-\gamma|u|^{\alpha}\widetilde{L}\big(|u|^{-1}\big)\big(1-i\beta\text{sgn}(u)\omega(u,\alpha)\big)\big\}=\phi(u)e^{i\hskip 1.0pt\widetilde{\omega}(u)}.

(30)

A slight modification in the bound for
|\phi^{\text{ns}}_{t}(u/B_{t})|
(using the inequality
|\exp\{-t(1-e^{a+bi})\}|\leq\exp\{-t(1-e^{a})\}
for
t>0
) yields the same conclusion.

Large deviation estimates.
For
\alpha\in(0,2)
, we recall the following large deviation and local large deviation estimates from
Ber (19)
(which were originally established for discrete-time random walks, but can be adapted to continuous-time via standard Poissonian arguments):

\begin{array}[]{cl}\text{Large deviation}&\quad{\mathbb{P}}(|X^{0}_{t}|>y)\leq\frac{Ct\hskip 1.0ptL(y)}{(1+y)^{\alpha}}\quad{\rm as}~y/B_{t}\to\infty.\\[8.0pt]
\text{Local large deviation}&\quad{\mathbb{P}}(X^{0}_{t}=y)\leq\frac{Ct\hskip 1.0ptL(|y|)}{B_{t}(1+|y|)^{\alpha}}\quad{\rm for~all}~y\in{\mathbb{Z}}.\end{array}

(31)

These bounds are instrumental in controlling the transition probability difference in time.

Proposition 2.3 (Transition probability difference in time)

Let
s\ll t
.
For the random walk
(X^{x}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, there exists a constant such that

\displaystyle\big|{\mathbb{P}}(X^{x}_{t}=0)-{\mathbb{P}}(X^{x}_{t+s}=0)\big|\leq\frac{C}{B_{t}}\hskip 1.0pt(s/t).

(32)

Proof
It suffices to show that

\big|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}{\mathbb{P}}(X^{x}_{t}=0)\big|\leq\frac{C}{tB_{t}},

since the desired result then follows by integrating this inequality from
t
to
t+s
.
Observe that

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\phi_{t}(u)=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}e^{-t(1-\phi(u))}=-(1-\phi(u))\hskip 1.0pte^{-t(1-\phi(u))},

Applying the inverse Fourier transform yields

\Big|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}{\mathbb{P}}(X^{x}_{t}=0)\Big|=\Big|\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{-iux}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\phi_{t}(u)\mathrm{d}u\Big|\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}(1-\phi(u))\hskip 1.0pte^{-t(1-\phi(u))}\mathrm{d}u.

Using the inequality
xe^{-x}\leq e^{-x/2}
for all
x\geq 0
, and setting
x=t(1-\phi(u))
, we obtain

\Big|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}{\mathbb{P}}(X^{x}_{t}=0)\Big|\leq t^{-1}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{-\frac{t}{2}(1-\phi(u))}\mathrm{d}u=t^{-1}{\mathbb{P}}(X^{x}_{t/2}=0),

where the last equality follows from the identity
e^{-\frac{t}{2}(1-\phi(u))}=\phi_{t/2}(u)
and the inverse Fourier transform formula.
This concludes the proof by the local limit theorem.

For non-symmetric kernel
p
, if we replace
X
by suitably centered random walk, then the large deviation remains valid
Ber (19)
and thus (
32
) also holds.

2.2
Potential kernel

The potential kernel
a(x)
of a random walk is defined by

a(x,t):=\int_{0}^{t}\Big({\mathbb{P}}(X^{0}_{s}=0)-{\mathbb{P}}(X^{x}_{s}=0)\Big)\mathrm{d}s\quad\mbox{and}\quad a(x):=\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}a(x,t).

(33)

It is known that for a one-dimensional random walk the limit
a(x)\in[0,\infty)
exists for all
x\in{\mathbb{Z}}
(see
(
Spi, 01
, T28.1)
.
We need a finer estimate that quantifies the convergence rate.

Proposition 2.4

(i)
If
0<\alpha<1
, then for any
0<\kappa_{1}<\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}
, we have uniformly in
x
,

a(x)-a(x,t)=O\big(t^{-\kappa_{1}}\big).

(34)

(ii)
If
1\leq\alpha<2
, then there exist
\kappa_{1}>0
and
\kappa_{2}\in(\alpha/2,1)
such that

a(x)-a(x,t)=O\big(t^{-\kappa_{1}}|x|^{\kappa_{2}}\big).

(35)

The proof of Proposition
2.4
relies on the following elementary integral with small
T
.

Lemma 2.5

For any
T>0
and
x\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we have that for all
0\leq\theta\leq 1
,

\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1-\cos(xu)}{u^{\kappa}}\mathrm{d}u=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle O\Big(T^{1-\kappa}\Big),&\text{if~}0<\kappa<1,\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle O\Big(\log(|x|T)\vee 1\Big),&\text{if~}\kappa=1,\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle O\Big(|x|^{\theta+\kappa-1}T^{\theta}\Big),&\text{if~}1<\kappa<2.\end{array}\right.

(36)

Proof
It suffices to treat
x>0
. Using the identity
1-\cos u=\int_{0}^{u}\sin v\mathrm{d}v
, we obtain

\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1-\cos(xu)}{u^{\kappa}}\mathrm{d}u=x^{\kappa-1}\int_{0}^{xT}\int_{0}^{u}\frac{\sin v}{u^{\kappa}}\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}u=x^{\kappa-1}\int_{0}^{xT}\int_{v}^{xT}\frac{\sin v}{u^{\kappa}}\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}v.

(37)

It remains to bound the double integral.
Integrating the inner integral, it is easy to see that

(\ref{aint})\leq\frac{x^{\kappa-1}}{1-\kappa}\int_{0}^{xT}(xT)^{1-\kappa}\sin v\mathrm{d}v\leq CT^{1-\kappa},\quad(0<\kappa<1).

(\ref{aint})\leq\int_{0}^{xT}\sin v\log\frac{xT}{v}\mathrm{d}v\leq C\big(\log(xT)\vee 1\big),\quad(\kappa=1).

For
1<\kappa<2
, if
xT\geq 1
, then by the integrability of
\sin v/v^{\kappa-1}
over
(0,\infty)
,

(\ref{aint})\leq\frac{x^{\kappa-1}}{\kappa-1}\int_{0}^{xT}\frac{\sin v}{v^{\kappa-1}}\mathrm{d}v\leq Cx^{\kappa-1}\leq Cx^{\kappa-1}(xT)^{\theta},\quad(\theta\geq 0).

If
xT<1
, then bounding
\sin v
by
v
yields

(\ref{aint})\leq Cx^{\kappa-1}\int_{0}^{xT}v^{2-\kappa}\mathrm{d}v\leq Cx^{\kappa-1}(xT)^{3-\kappa}\leq Cx^{\kappa-1}(xT)^{\theta},\quad(\theta\leq 3-\kappa).

This completes the proof.

Proof of Proposition
2.4

Apply the inverse Fourier transform to
{\mathbb{P}}(X^{0}_{s}=0)-{\mathbb{P}}(X^{x}_{s}=0)
. By Fubini and by the symmetry of
\phi_{s}(u)=\exp\big(s(1-\phi(u))\big)
, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle a(x)-a(x,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\int_{t}^{\infty}\!\!\big(1-e^{ixu}\big)\phi_{s}(u)\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}u=\frac{1}{\pi}\!\int_{0}^{\pi}\!\frac{1-\cos(xu)}{1-\phi(u)}e^{-t\big(1-\phi(u)\big)}\mathrm{d}u.\end{array}

(38)

Denote
C_{0}:=\sup\Big\{\gamma|u|^{\alpha}\widetilde{L}\Big(\frac{1}{u}\Big):u\in(0,\pi)\Big\}<\infty
. Using
1-e^{-x}\geq xe^{-x}
, we have

1-\phi(u)\geq\gamma u^{\alpha}\widetilde{L}\Big(\frac{1}{u}\Big)e^{-C_{0}}\geq Cu^{\alpha+\varepsilon},\quad(0<u<\pi),

where
\varepsilon>0
can be chosen arbitrary small.
To bound the r.h.s. of
(\ref{adif})
, decompose the integral into two parts
D_{1}:=\{u:0<u\leq t^{-\frac{1-\varepsilon}{\alpha+\varepsilon}}\}
and
D_{2}:=\{u:t^{-\frac{1-\varepsilon}{\alpha+\varepsilon}}<u\leq\pi\}
.
On
D_{1}
,

I_{1}:=\int_{D_{1}}\frac{1-\cos(xu)}{1-\phi(u)}e^{-t\big(1-\phi(u)\big)}\mathrm{d}u\leq\frac{1}{C}\int_{0}^{t^{-\frac{1-\varepsilon}{\alpha+\varepsilon}}}\frac{1-\cos(xu)}{u^{\alpha+\varepsilon}}\mathrm{d}u.

Since
t(1-\phi(u))\geq Ct(t^{-\frac{1-\varepsilon}{\alpha+\varepsilon}})^{\alpha+\varepsilon}=Ct^{\varepsilon}
on
D_{2}
,

I_{2}\!:=\!\!\int_{D_{2}}\!\!\frac{1-\cos(xu)}{1-\phi(u)}e^{-t\big(\!1-\phi(u)\!\big)}\mathrm{d}u\leq\!\!\int_{D_{2}}\!\!\frac{1-\cos(xu)}{Cu^{\alpha+\varepsilon}}e^{-Ct^{\varepsilon}}\mathrm{d}u\leq\!\frac{e^{-Ct^{\varepsilon}}}{C}\!\!\int_{0}^{\pi}\!\frac{1-\cos(xu)}{u^{\alpha+\varepsilon}}\mathrm{d}u.

For
0<\alpha<1
, choose
\varepsilon<1-\alpha
. Then, by Lemma
2.5

a(x)-a(x,t)=I_{1}+I_{2}\leq Ct^{-\frac{1-\varepsilon}{\alpha+\varepsilon}(1-\alpha-\varepsilon)}+Ce^{-Ct^{\varepsilon}}=O\Big(t^{-\kappa_{1}}\Big),

where
\kappa_{1}:=\frac{1-\varepsilon}{\alpha-\varepsilon}(1-\alpha+\varepsilon)
.
Note that
\kappa_{1}
approaches
\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}
if we send
\varepsilon\to 0
.

For
1\leq\alpha<2
, choose
\varepsilon<\frac{2-\alpha}{2}
and apply Lemma
2.5
with
\theta=2-\alpha-2\varepsilon
, yielding

a(x)-a(x,t)\leq C|x|^{1-\varepsilon}t^{-\frac{1-\varepsilon}{\alpha+\varepsilon}(2-\alpha-2\varepsilon)}+Ce^{-Ct^{\varepsilon}}=O\Big(t^{-\kappa_{1}}|x|^{\kappa_{2}}\Big),

where
\kappa_{1}=\frac{(1-\varepsilon)(2-\alpha-2\varepsilon)}{\alpha+\varepsilon}>0
and
\kappa_{2}=1-\varepsilon<1
.

Lemma 2.6

For the random walk
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, its jump kernel
p
and potential kernel
a
satisfy

\sum_{y}p(y)a(x+y)=1_{\{x=0\}}+a(x).

(39)

Remark 2.7

This is analogous to the relation between the Green’s function and the jump kernel of a transient random walk.
We will use (
39
) for
x
=0.
Observe that if the random walk is of rate
r
, then
\sum_{y}p(y)a(y)=r
.

Proof
Consider the event
\{X_{t}^{0}=0\}
and decompose it according to whether the walk makes at least one jump during the time interval. Let
N_{s,t}
denote the number of jumps during
(s,t]
.

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(X_{t}^{x}=0)=\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{y\neq 0}{\mathbb{P}}(X^{x}_{s}=-y)p(y){\mathbb{P}}(N_{s,t}=0)\mathrm{d}s+{\mathbb{P}}(N_{0,t}=0)\cdot 1_{\{x=0\}}\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle\hskip 53.49132pt=\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{y\neq 0}{\mathbb{P}}(X^{x+y}_{s}=0)p(y)e^{s-t}\mathrm{d}s+e^{-t}1_{\{x=0\}}.\end{array}

Integrating in
t
from 0 to
T
in the above equation and using Fubini, we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}{\mathbb{P}}(X_{t}^{x}=0)\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{y\neq 0}{\mathbb{P}}(X^{x+y}_{s}=0)p(y)(1-e^{s-T})\mathrm{d}s+(1-e^{-T})1_{\{x=0\}}.\end{array}

Moving the term
\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{y\neq 0}{\mathbb{P}}(X^{x+y}_{s}=0)p(x)\mathrm{d}s
to the r.h.s. and by the definition of

,

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\sum_{y}p(y)a(x+y,T)-a(x)=1_{\{x=0\}}-e^{-T}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{y\neq 0}{\mathbb{P}}(X^{x+y}_{s}=0)p(y)e^{s}\mathrm{d}s+1_{\{x=0\}}\Big).\end{array}

Since
\sum_{y\neq 0}{\mathbb{P}}(X^{x+y}_{s}=0)p(x)\leq\max_{x}{\mathbb{P}}(X^{x+y}_{s}=0)\to 0
as

We note that, in our arguments, the required estimates for a single random walk rely solely on the results from this subsection and the previous one (Remark
2.2
). The remaining estimates, particularly those involving collision times, can be reduced to the first hitting times of difference process between two random walks. Notably, the jump kernel of the difference process is always symmetric.
Consequently, the main result, Theorem
1.3
, would continue to hold provided Proposition
2.4
can be extended to non-symmetric jump kernels
p(\cdot)
.

2.3
First hitting time

For the heavy-tailed random walk
(X^{x}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, the first hitting time of 0 is defined by

\tau_{0,x}:=\tau_{x}:=\inf\{t:X_{t}^{x}=0\text{~and~}X^{x}_{s}\neq 0~\text{ for some~}0<s<t\}.

(40)

Our goal is to derive asymptotic estimates for the tail probability
{\mathbb{P}}(\tau_{x}>t)
.
A classic approach (which we leave the details to readers) is to apply the Laplace transform to the last-exit formula and then invoke a Tauberian theorem
(
BGT, 89
, Theorem 1.7.1)
, which yields

{\mathbb{P}}(\tau_{x}>t)\sim\frac{a(x)}{c_{\alpha}g_{\alpha}(0)l(t)},

(41)

where
c_{\alpha}=1
for
0<\alpha\leq 1
and
c_{\alpha}=\Gamma(1/\alpha)\Gamma(2-1/\alpha)
for
1<\alpha<2
.
We leave the details to readers.
Nevertheless, to incorporate these estimates into our ODE framework, we require a higher order estimate of the tail probability.
Fortunately, for
\alpha\leq 1
the function
{\mathbb{P}}(\tau_{x}>t)
is slowly varying, which allows us to obtain the refined result Proposition
2.11
.

Below, we only focus on case
0<\alpha\leq 1
.
Define the auxiliary function

j(t):=\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}}p(x){\mathbb{P}}(\tau_{x}>t)=\sum_{x\neq 0}p(x){\mathbb{P}}(\tau_{x}>t),

(42)

which represents that the random walk jumps away from 0 initially and does not hit 0 by time
t
.
Recall the last-exit formulae that decompose at the last time the walk is at
0
,

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle 1={\mathbb{P}}(X^{0}_{t}=0)+\int_{0}^{t}{\mathbb{P}}(X^{0}_{s}=0)j(t-s)\mathrm{d}s,\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(\tau_{x}\leq t)={\mathbb{P}}(X^{x}_{t}=0)+\int_{0}^{t}{\mathbb{P}}(X^{x}_{s}=0)j(t-s)\mathrm{d}s,\quad(x\neq 0),\end{array}

(43)

We begin by estimating
j(t)
.
Recall that
g_{\alpha}
is the density function of the limiting
\alpha
-stable distribution, and recall from (
7
) that
l(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{B_{s}}\mathrm{d}s
and
\ell(t)=B_{t}\hskip 1.0ptl(t)/t
.

Lemma 2.9

For
j(t)
defined in (
42
) and for
\alpha\leq 1
, one has

\sum_{x}p(x){\mathbb{P}}(\tau_{x}>t)=j(t)=\frac{1}{g_{\alpha}(0)l(t)}\Big(1+O(1/\ell(t))\Big).

(44)

Proof
Since
j(t)=\sum_{x}p(x){\mathbb{P}}(\tau_{x}>t)
is decreasing in
t
, the last-exit formula (
43
) implies

1\geq{\mathbb{P}}(X_{t}^{0}=0)+j(t)\int_{0}^{t}{\mathbb{P}}(X_{s}^{0}=0)\mathrm{d}s\geq g_{\alpha}(0)l(t)j(t).

For the other inequality, we replace
t
by
2t
, use
{\mathbb{P}}(X^{0}_{s}=0)\leq C/B_{t}
for
s\in[t,2t]
, and obtain

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle 1\leq\frac{C}{B_{t}}+\int_{t}^{2t}\frac{C}{B_{t}}j(2t-s)\mathrm{d}s+j(t)\int_{0}^{t}{\mathbb{P}}(X_{s}^{0}=0)\mathrm{d}s\leq\frac{C}{B_{t}}+\frac{Ct}{B_{t}l(t)}+g_{0}(\alpha)l(t)j(t),\end{array}

where in the last inequality, for the integral from
t
to
2t
we used
j(t)\leq\frac{1}{g_{\alpha}(0)l(t)}
and then
\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{l(s)}\mathrm{d}s\sim\frac{t}{l(t)}
.
Note that
B_{t}\gg\ell(t)
. The estimate (
44
) follows.

We now turn to the difference of
j
over time.

Lemma 2.10

For
j(t)
defined in (
42
) and for
\alpha\leq 1
, there exists
C
such that for
s<t
and any
\varepsilon>0
,

j(s)-j(t)\leq\frac{C}{l(t)\ell(t)}\left(\frac{t}{s}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1+\varepsilon}.

(45)

Proof
Since
t^{1/\alpha}/B_{t}
is a SVF, the Potter bound implies that for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle l(t)-l(s)=\frac{t^{1/\alpha}}{B_{t}}\int_{s}^{t}\frac{u^{1/\alpha}/B_{u}}{t^{1/\alpha}/B_{t}}\frac{1}{u^{1/\alpha}}\mathrm{d}u\leq\frac{t^{1/\alpha}}{B_{t}}\int_{s}^{t}\frac{(t/u)^{\varepsilon}}{u^{1/\alpha}}\mathrm{d}u\\[10.0pt]
\hskip 142.26378pt\displaystyle\leq\frac{t}{B_{t}}\left(\frac{t}{s}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1+\varepsilon}=\frac{l(t)}{\ell(t)}\left(\frac{t}{s}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1+\varepsilon}.\end{array}

(46)

Similarly, for the SVF
l(t)
and
(1/\alpha-1)
-regularly varying function
\ell(t)
,

1/l(s)\leq(t/s)^{\varepsilon}/l(t),\qquad 1/\ell(s)\leq(t/s)^{1/\alpha-1+\varepsilon}/\ell(t).

Inserting these bounds into (
44
) yields that for any
\varepsilon>0
,

j(s)-j(t)\leq\frac{l(t)-l(s)}{g_{\alpha}(0)l(t)\hskip 1.0ptl(s)}+\frac{C}{l(t)\ell(t)}+\frac{C}{l(s)\ell(s)}\leq\frac{C}{l(t)\ell(t)}(t/s)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1+\varepsilon},

which completes the proof.

We are now ready to deduce the tail probability
{\mathbb{P}}(\tau_{x}>t)
.
For convenience, denote

\Delta(x,t):={\mathbb{P}}(X_{t}^{0}=0)-{\mathbb{P}}(X_{t}^{x}=0)\geq 0,

(47)

so that the last-exit formula (
43
) yields, for
x\neq 0
,

{\mathbb{P}}(\tau_{x}>t)=\Delta(x,t)+\int_{0}^{t}\Delta(x,s)j(t-s)\mathrm{d}s.

(48)

Note that a uniform bound on the transition probability
{\mathbb{P}}(X^{x}_{t}=0)\leq C/B_{t}
implies the uniform bound
\Delta(x,t)\leq C/B_{t}
.
This, combined with Proposition
2.1
, shows that

0\leq\Delta(x,t)\leq CB_{t}^{-1}(|x|B_{t}^{-1}\wedge 1).

(49)

Proposition 2.11 (Higher order estimate for hitting probability)

Let
(X^{x}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be the heavy-tailed random walk with increments in the domain of attraction of an
\alpha
-stable law for some
0<\alpha\leq 1
.
Recall the first hitting time
\tau_{x}
from (
40
). Recall
B_{t}
from (
5
), and recall
l(t)
and
\ell(t)
from (
7
).
Then, for
x\neq 0
and any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists some
\kappa>0
such that

{\mathbb{P}}(\tau_{x}>t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{a(x)}{g_{\alpha}(0)l(t)}+O\Big(\frac{a(x)}{l(t)\ell(t)}+\frac{|x|}{t^{\kappa}}\Big),&\alpha=1;\\[12.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{a(x)}{g_{\alpha}(0)l(t)}+O\Big(\frac{a(x)}{l(t)\ell(t)}+\frac{t^{\varepsilon}}{\ell(t)}\Big),&0<\alpha<1.\end{array}\right.

(50)

Proof
Rewrite (
48
) as

{\mathbb{P}}(\tau_{x}>t)=\int_{0}^{t}\Delta(x,s)j(t)\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\Delta(x,s)\big(j(t-s)-j(t)\big)\mathrm{d}s+\Delta(x,t).

(51)

We then estimate the r.h.s. Firstly, by Proposition
2.4
and Lemma
2.9
,

\int_{0}^{t}\Delta(x,s)j(t)\mathrm{d}s=a(x)j(t)+\big(a(x,t)-a(x)\big)j(t)=\frac{a(x)}{g_{\alpha}(0)l(t)}+O\Big(\frac{a(x)}{l(t)\ell(t)}+\frac{|x|^{\kappa_{2}}}{t^{\kappa_{1}}l(t)}\Big).

(52)

In the equation above, if
\alpha=1
, then
\kappa_{1}>0,\kappa_{2}\in(0,1)
and thus
\frac{|x|^{\kappa_{2}}}{t^{\kappa_{1}}l(t)}\leq C\frac{|x|}{t^{\kappa}}
for
\kappa\in(0,\kappa_{1})
; and if
0<\alpha<1
, then
\kappa_{1}\in(0,\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}),\kappa_{2}=0
and thus

where
\kappa_{1}>0
and
\kappa_{2}\in(0,1)
if
\alpha=1
, and
\kappa_{1}\in(0,\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha})
and
\kappa_{2}=0
if
0<\alpha<1
.
Secondly, applying Lemma
2.10
to
j(t-s)-j(t)
for
s\in(0,t/2)
,

\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}\Delta(x,s)\big(j(t-s)-j(t)\big)\mathrm{d}s\leq\frac{C}{l(t)\ell(t)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Delta(x,s)\mathrm{d}s=\frac{Ca(x)}{l(t)\ell(t)}.

(53)

For
s\in(t/2/,t)
, Karamata’s theorem implies
\int_{0}^{t}j(s)\mathrm{d}s\asymp t/l(t)
, and thus by (
49
)

\int^{t}_{\frac{t}{2}}\Delta(x,s)\big(j(t-s)-j(t)\big)\mathrm{d}s\leq\frac{C}{B_{t}}\Big(\frac{|x|}{B_{t}}\wedge 1\Big)\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}\big(j(s)+j(t)\big)\mathrm{d}s\leq\frac{C}{\ell(t)}\Big(\frac{|x|}{B_{t}}\wedge 1\Big).

(54)

Recall that
B_{t}\geq\ell(t)
for large
t
. (
49
) implies that
\Delta(x,t)\leq C\ell(t)^{-1}(|x|B_{t}^{-1}\wedge 1)
.

For
\alpha=1
, since both
l
and
\ell
are SVFs, there exists
\kappa\in(0,1)
such that both
\frac{|x|^{\kappa_{2}}}{t^{\kappa_{1}}l(t)}
and
\frac{|x|}{\ell(t)B_{t}}
are bounded by
|x|/t^{\kappa}
.
Using this bound for the r.h.s of (
52
) and (
54
), and inserting (
52
)-(
54
) into (
51
), we obtain (
50
) for
\alpha=1
.
For
0<\alpha<1
and for any
\varepsilon>0
small enough, recall
\kappa_{2}=0
and choose
\kappa_{1}=1/\alpha-1-2\varepsilon
in (
52
). Since now
l
is a SVF and
\ell
is
(1/\alpha\!-\!1)
-regularly varying,
\frac{1}{t^{\kappa_{1}}l(t)}
is bounded by
t^{\varepsilon}/\ell(t)
.
Thus, inserting (
52
)-(
54
) into (
51
), we obtain (
50
) for
0<\alpha<1
.

Since
\ell(t)=B_{t}\hskip 1.0ptl(t)/t
, we observe that
B_{t}\gg\ell(t)^{\theta}
if
0<\theta<\frac{1}{1-\alpha}
(with the convention that
\frac{1}{1-\alpha}=\infty
if
\alpha=1
).
The following lemma gives a tail bound on the hitting time when the starting position is relatively far from the origin.

Lemma 2.12

Let
\theta\in(0,\frac{1}{1-\alpha})
.
Under the assumptions of Proposition
2.11
, if
|x|\geq B_{t}/\ell(t)^{\theta}
, then for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
C>0
such that

{\mathbb{P}}(\tau_{x}\leq t)\leq C\hskip 1.0pt\ell(t)^{-1+(1-\alpha)\theta+\varepsilon}.

(55)

Proof
We use the last-exit formula (
43
).
Recall from (
6
) that
L(B_{t})/B_{t}^{\alpha}\sim 1/t
. By the Potter bound

\frac{L\big(B_{t}/\ell(t)^{\theta}\big)}{B_{t}^{\alpha}/\ell(t)^{\alpha\theta}}\leq\frac{C\ell(t)^{\alpha\theta}}{t}\cdot\frac{L\big(B_{t}/\ell(t)^{\theta}\big)}{L(B_{t})}\leq\frac{C\ell(t)^{\alpha\theta+\varepsilon}}{t}.

Set
s:=t/\ell(t)^{\alpha\theta}
.
Since the polynomial power in
\ell(t)^{\alpha\theta}
is
\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\cdot\alpha\theta<1
, we have
s\gg 1
.
We now split the integration in (
43
) into three regions.
For
u\in(0,s]
, by the local large deviation (
31
), Karamata’s theorem, and
j(t-u)\leq C/l(t)
,

\int_{0}^{s}{\mathbb{P}}(X^{x}_{u}=0)j(t-u)\mathrm{d}u\leq\int_{0}^{s}\frac{CuL\big(B_{t}/\ell(t)^{\theta}\big)}{B_{u}B_{t}^{\alpha}/\ell(t)^{\alpha\theta}}\cdot\frac{1}{l(t)}\mathrm{d}u\leq\frac{Cs^{2}\ell(t)^{\alpha\theta+\varepsilon}}{tB_{s}\hskip 1.0ptl(t)}\leq\frac{C\hskip 1.0pt(t/s)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-2+\varepsilon}}{\ell(t)^{1-\alpha\theta-\varepsilon}}.

For
u\in(s,t-s]
, we bound
j(t-u)\leq j(s)\leq C/l(s)
, and use (
46
) to obtain

\int^{t-s}_{s}{\mathbb{P}}(X^{x}_{u}=0)j(t-u)\mathrm{d}u\leq\frac{C}{l(s)}\big(l(t)-l(s)\big)\leq\frac{C}{\ell(t)}(t/s)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1+\varepsilon}.

Finally, for
u\in(t-s,t]
, by the uniform bound
P(X^{x}_{u}=0)\leq C/B_{t}
and Karamata’s theorem,

\int_{t-s}^{t}{\mathbb{P}}(X^{x}_{u}=0)j(t-u)\mathrm{d}u\leq\frac{C}{B_{t}}\int_{0}^{s}j(u)\mathrm{d}u\leq\frac{Cs}{B_{t}l(s)}\leq\frac{C}{\ell(t)}(t/s)^{-1+\varepsilon}.

Recalling that
B_{t}
is
1/\alpha
-varying and
\ell(t)
is
(1/\alpha-1)
-varying, we have
1/B_{t}\leq C/\ell(t)
. Thus,

{\mathbb{P}}(X^{x}_{t}=0)\leq C/B_{t}\leq C/\ell(t).

Combining the four bounds above and inserting
t/s=\ell(t)^{\alpha\theta}
gives

{\mathbb{P}}(\tau_{x}\leq t)={\mathbb{P}}(X^{x}_{t}=0)+\int_{0}^{t}{\mathbb{P}}(X^{x}_{u}=0)j(t-u)\mathrm{d}u\leq\frac{C}{\ell(t)^{1-(1-\alpha)\theta-\varepsilon}}.

where
\varepsilon>0
can be chosen arbitrary small.

We also include a lemma dealing with the joint event
{\mathbb{P}}(\tau_{x}>t,X^{x}_{t}=y)
.

Lemma 2.13

Under the assumptions of Proposition
2.11
,

{\mathbb{P}}(\tau_{x}>t,X^{x}_{t}=y)\leq\frac{Ca(x)}{B_{t}\hskip 1.0ptl(t)}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\sum_{x}p(x){\mathbb{P}}(\tau_{x}>t,X^{x}_{t}=y)\leq\frac{C}{B_{t}\hskip 1.0ptl(t)}.

(56)

Moreover, for any
\kappa<\alpha/(1-\alpha)
,
(
if
\alpha=1
then
\kappa<\infty
)
,

\sum_{x}p(x)\Big|{\mathbb{P}}(\tau_{x}>t,X^{x}_{t}=y)-{\mathbb{P}}(\tau_{x}>t){\mathbb{P}}(X^{0}_{t}=0)\Big|\leq\frac{C}{B_{t}l(t)\ell(t)^{\kappa}}+\frac{C|y|}{B_{t}^{2}}.

(57)

Proof
For the bound in (
56
), we decompose at time
t/2
, by the Markov property,

{\mathbb{P}}(\tau_{x}>t,X^{x}_{t}=y)\leq\sum_{z}{\mathbb{P}}(\tau_{x}>t,X^{x}_{t}=y){\mathbb{P}}(X^{z}_{t/2}=y)\leq\frac{C}{B_{t/2}}{\mathbb{P}}(\tau_{x}>t/2)\leq\frac{Ca(x)}{B_{t}l(t)}.

The second bound follows upon summing over
x
and applying Lemma
2.6
.

For the estimate in (
57
), note that if
|x|>B_{t}^{2}
, then for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\sum_{|z|>B_{t}^{2}}p(x){\mathbb{P}}(\tau_{x}>t,X^{x}_{t}=y)\leq\sum_{|z|>B_{t}^{2}}p(x)\leq\frac{CL(B_{t}^{2})}{B_{t}^{2}}\leq\frac{C}{B_{t}^{2-\varepsilon}}.

(58)

It remains to treat
|x|\leq B_{t}^{2}
.
Let
H_{1}=H_{1}(t),H_{2}=H_{2}(t)
be such that
t\gg H_{1}\gg 1
and
H_{2}\gg 1
.
At time
s=t/H_{1}
, we partition the space into two regions
D_{1}:=\{z:|z-x|>B_{s}H_{2}\}
and
D_{2}:=\{z:|z-x|\leq B_{s}H_{2}\}
.
By the uniform bound and the large deviation,

{\mathbb{P}}(\tau_{x}>t,X_{s}\in D_{1},X_{t}=y)\leq\sum_{z\in D_{1}}{\mathbb{P}}(X^{x}_{s}=z){\mathbb{P}}(X^{z}_{t-s}=y)\leq\frac{C}{B_{t}H_{2}^{\alpha-\varepsilon}}.

(59)

On
D_{2}
, we have
X^{x}_{s}=z
with
|z|\leq|x|+B_{s}H_{2}
.
Thus, by Propositions
2.1
and
2.3
,

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(\tau_{x}>t,X_{s}\in D_{2},X^{x}_{t}=y)\leq\sum_{z\in D_{2}}{\mathbb{P}}_{x}(\tau_{x}>t,X_{s}=z){\mathbb{P}}_{z}(X_{t-s}=y)\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle\qquad\leq\sum_{z\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus D_{1}}{\mathbb{P}}_{x}(\tau_{x}>t,X_{s}=z)\Big({\mathbb{P}}_{0}(X_{t-s}=0)+\frac{C(|y|+|x|+B_{s}H_{2})}{B_{t}^{2}}\Big)\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle\qquad\leq\Big({\mathbb{P}}_{x}(\tau_{x}>t)+\frac{C}{H_{2}^{\alpha-\varepsilon}}\Big)\Big({\mathbb{P}}_{0}(X_{t}=0)+\frac{C}{B_{t}H_{1}}+\frac{C(|y|+|x|+B_{s}H_{2})}{B_{t}^{2}}\Big)\end{array}

(60)

Since
p(\cdot)
has a finite
\kappa
-th moment for any
\kappa<1
, for any
\varepsilon>0
,

B_{t}^{-2}\sum_{|x|\leq B_{t}^{2}}|x|p(x)\leq B_{t}^{-2}\sum_{x}B_{t}^{\varepsilon}\hskip 1.0pt|x|^{1-\varepsilon/2}p(x)\leq CB_{t}^{-2+\varepsilon}.

(61)

For the other error terms, fix any
\kappa<\alpha/(1-\alpha)
, and set
H_{1}=l(t)^{3/\alpha}\ell(t)^{\kappa}
(so that
H_{1}\ll t
) and
H_{2}=H_{1}^{1/\alpha}
.
Then, using Lemma
2.9
, direct calculations and basic estimates yield that

\sum_{x}p(x)\Big|{\mathbb{P}}_{x}(\tau_{x}>t,X_{t}=y)-{\mathbb{P}}_{x}(\tau_{x}>t){\mathbb{P}}_{0}(X_{t}=0)\Big|\leq\frac{C}{B_{t}l(t)\ell(t)^{\kappa}}+\frac{C|y|}{B_{t}^{2}},

(62)

where
\kappa
can be made arbitrary close to
\alpha/(1-\alpha)
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

3
First collision time of random walks

Recall from Theorem
1.2
that
({\mathbf{X}}_{t})_{t\geq 0}=(X^{1}_{t},\ldots,X^{N}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
represents
N
independent random walks with initial state
{\mathbf{X}}_{0}={\bf x}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{N}
and jump kernel
p(\cdot)
in (
4
).
Recall from (
10
) that
\tau_{{\bf x}}
is the first collision time of
{\mathbf{X}}
.
We show Theorem
1.2
for asymptotic probability of the non-collision event
\{\tau_{{\bf x}}>t\}
in Subsection
3.1
, and moreover show that this event is asymptotically independent of the transition event
\{X_{t}={\bf y}\}
in Subsection
3.2
.

3.1
Proof of Theorem
1.2

As outlined in Subsection
1.2
, our goal is to derive an ODE to characterize the asymptotic behavior of
p_{\rm NC}(t)={\mathbb{P}}(\tau_{{\bf x}}>t)
.
The generator calculation yields (
19
), i.e.,

-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}p_{\rm NC}(t)=\sum_{i<j}\sum_{y_{ij}\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2p(y_{ij}){\mathbb{P}}(\tau_{{\bf x}}>t,\Delta^{ij}_{t}=y_{ij}),

(63)

where
\Delta^{ij}_{t}=X^{i}_{t}-X^{j}_{t}
.
Our first step is to approximate the r.h.s. using the lemma below.
Recall the function class
{\cal B}
such that
B_{t}\in{\cal B}
implies
\ell(t)^{K}\geq l(t)
for some
K>0
.

Lemma 3.1

Assume that
B_{t}
belongs to the class
{\cal B}
. Let
\kappa>0
.
Let
s=t/\ell(t)^{\theta}
for some
\theta>K+\kappa+1
if
\alpha=1
or
\theta<\frac{1}{1-\alpha}
if
\alpha<1
.
Then, there exist
c=c({\bf x})
and
\delta>0
such that for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\Big|\sum_{y_{ij}}p(y_{ij}){\mathbb{P}}(\tau_{\bf x}>t,\Delta^{ij}_{t}=y_{ij})-\frac{p_{\rm NC}(s)}{B_{2t}\hskip 1.0ptl(2t)}\Big|\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{c}{B_{t}\hskip 1.0ptl(t)}\Big(\frac{p_{\rm NC}(s)}{\ell(t)^{1-\varepsilon}}+\frac{1}{\ell(t)^{\kappa}}\Big),&\alpha=1;\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{c}{B_{t}\hskip 1.0ptl(t)t^{\delta}},&\alpha\in(0,1).\end{array}\right.

(64)

Proof
For any
k\neq l
, let
\tau_{kl}:=\inf\{t\geq 0:X^{k}_{t}=X^{l}_{t}\}
be the first collision time of
X^{k}
and
X^{l}
.
On
\{\tau_{{\bf x}}>s,\Delta^{ij}_{t}=y_{ij}\}
, if
\{k,l\}\neq\{i,j\}
, then
\tau_{kl}\in(s,t]
is unlikely.
Using the inclusion-exclusion principle, we obtain the following good approximation.

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\quad\sum_{y_{ij}}p(y_{ij})\big|{\mathbb{P}}(\tau_{\bf x}>t,\Delta^{ij}_{t}=y_{ij})-{\mathbb{P}}(\tau_{\bf x}>s,\tau_{ij}>t,\Delta^{ij}_{t}=y_{ij})\big|\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\{k,l\}\neq\{i,j\}}\sum_{y_{ij}}p(y_{ij}){\mathbb{P}}(\tau_{\bf x}>s,\tau_{ij}>t,\Delta^{ij}_{t}=y_{ij},s<\tau_{kl}\leq t).\end{array}

(65)

We bound the r.h.s. of (
65
) by decomposing it into three cases as:
(a)

\{\tau_{kl}>s+u\}
,
(b)
\{\tau_{kl}\leq s+u,\hskip 2.0pt|\Delta^{kl}_{s}|>B_{u}/\ell(u)^{\theta_{1}}\}
,
(c)

\{\tau_{kl}\leq s+u,\hskip 2.0pt|\Delta^{kl}_{s}|\leq B_{u}/\ell(u)^{\theta_{1}}\}
. Here, we take
u=t/2
if
\alpha=1
and
u=t/\ell(t)^{\theta_{2}}
otherwise, where
\theta_{1}>0,\theta_{2}\in(0,\frac{1}{1-\alpha})
will be chosen later.

On the intersection with
(a)
, we decompose at the collision time
\tau_{kl}.

Let
v:=\tau_{kl}-s
.
For
{\mathbf{X}}_{s}={\bf z}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{N}
, let
({\mathbf{X}}_{t}^{z})_{t\geq 0}:=({\mathbf{X}}_{t+s})_{t\geq 0}
denote the continuation of the random walks starting from time
s
.
We also use
{\mathbb{P}}_{\bf z}
to denote the law of random walks with a initial state
{\bf z}
.
Conditioning on
{\mathbf{X}}_{s}={\bf z}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{N}
, the random walks first evolve to
{\mathbf{X}}^{{\bf z}}_{v}=\textbf{w}
, with
\Delta_{v}^{kl}=w_{kl}=w_{k}-w_{l}
.
Then at time
v
,
\Delta^{kl}
jumps to 0 at rate
2p(w_{kl})
.
Over the remaining time
t-s-v

Proof
Firstly, we trace back to time

{\bf x}
at time

at time

at

By the natural coupling between the coalescing random walks and independent random walks, and by the time-reversal argument,

{\mathbb{E}}[\xi_{t}({\bf x})]=\sum_{{\bf y}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{N}}{\mathbb{E}}[\xi_{t-s}({\bf y})]{\mathbb{P}}_{\bf x}(\widehat{\mathbf{X}}_{s}={\bf y},\hat{\tau}_{\rm{\bf x}}>s)+R_{1},

where
(\widehat{\mathbf{X}}_{t})_{t\geq 0}

We remark that if

.

Indeed, if we call

{\bf y}
at time

{\bf x}
at time

and the sum in the r.h.s. equals
\sum_{\bf y}{\mathbb{P}}(A({\bf y}))
.
Thus by the inclusion-exclusion principle,
R_{1}\leq 0
and
|R_{1}|
is bounded above by the probability of the following event: there exist
{\bf y}\neq{\bf z}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{N}
such that
(a)

\xi_{t-s}({\bf y},{\bf z})=1
(i.e., there are particles at all sites of
\{{\bf y},{\bf z}\}
at time

), and

both

Suppose that there are

W.l.o.g., we rearrange the order of coordinates, and assume that the first

and

Since then the two random walks starting from

In words,
(a)

|R_{1}|
is then bounded from above by summing the probabilities of

for all

over

from

to

.

To obtain an upper bound for the probability of

particle at

at

, and by the negative correlation of CRW (see Theorem

{\mathbb{E}}[\xi({\bf y},{\bf z})]\leq\rho_{1}(t-s)^{N+J}.

(92)

We then take

It implies that the following event on the time-reversed (branching) random walks occurs.

\hat{u}_{i}:=t-u_{i}

For the reversed time from 0 to

4.2
Proof of Theorem
1.1

To give precise asymptotics of the density, we need a priori rough bounds of
\rho_{1}
that give the correct order, where the upper bound can be proved using a modification of the arguments of
BG (80)
.

Proposition 4.3

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.3
, there exists
c,C>0
such that for all large
t
,

\frac{c\hskip 1.0ptl(t)}{t}\leq\rho_{1}(t)\leq\frac{C\hskip 1.0ptl(t)}{t}.

(95)

Proof
The proof proceeds in three steps, establishing successively a crude lower bound
\rho_{1}(t)\geq c/t
, an upper bound
\rho_{1}(t)\leq C\hskip 1.0ptl(t)/t
, and finally an improved lower bound
\rho_{1}(t)\geq c\hskip 1.0ptl(t)/t
.

Step 1.
Recall the notation that
\xi(0,x)=\xi(0)\xi(x)
.
By the generator calculation, using the negative correlation
{\mathbb{E}}[\xi(0,x)]\leq{\mathbb{E}}[\xi(0)]{\mathbb{E}}[\xi(x)]
(see Theorem
A.1
), and by the translation invariance
{\mathbb{E}}[\xi(x)]=\rho_{1}(t)
,

-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\rho_{1}(t)=\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}}p(-x){\mathbb{E}}[\xi_{t}(0)\xi_{t}(x)]\leq\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}}p(-x)\rho_{1}(t)^{2}=\rho_{1}(t)^{2},

(96)

which yields a crude bound

\rho_{1}(t)\geq\frac{c}{t}.

Step 2.
The idea of proving the upper bound is credited to
BG (80)
.
We thus only provide a proof sketch here.
The idea is that given the density
\rho_{1}(t)
, starting from the interval of length
C/\rho_{1}(t)
, there is a probability bounded uniformly from below that all other particles would coalesce with one particle over time
s
, which results to a lower bound for the density decay.
For any
L\in{\mathbb{N}}
, Denote
\Lambda:=[0,L]\cap{\mathbb{Z}}
and
x+\Lambda:=\{x+y:y\in\Lambda\}
. Let
\xi^{A}_{t}
be the coalescing random walks starting from set
A
. Identifying
\xi_{t}(B)
as the set
\{x\in B:\xi_{t}(x)=1\}
for any set
B\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}
, we have that for any
s,t\geq 0
,

{\mathbb{E}}\big[|\xi_{t+s}(\Lambda)|\big]\leq\sum_{x\in L{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{E}}\big[|\xi^{\xi_{t}(x+\Lambda)}_{s}(\Lambda)|\big]=\sum_{x\in L{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{E}}\big[|\xi^{\xi_{t}(\Lambda)}_{s}(x+\Lambda)|\big]={\mathbb{E}}\big[|\xi^{\xi_{t}(\Lambda)}_{s}({\mathbb{Z}})|\big],

where in the inequality we applied the Markov property at time
t
for ancestors of the particles in
\Lambda
at time
t+s
, and in the first equality we used translation invariance.
We note that all terms in the equation above are finite as
{\mathbb{E}}\big[|\xi^{\xi_{t}(\Lambda)}_{s}({\mathbb{Z}})|\big]\leq{\mathbb{E}}\big[|\xi_{t}(\Lambda)|\big]\leq|\Lambda|
.
Since
{\mathbb{E}}[|\xi_{t}(\Lambda)|]=L\rho_{1}(t)
, we derive

L(\rho_{1}(t)-\rho_{1}(t+s))={\mathbb{E}}[\xi_{t}(\Lambda)]-{\mathbb{E}}\big[|\xi_{t+s}(\Lambda)|\big]\geq{\mathbb{E}}[\xi_{t}(\Lambda)-\xi^{\xi_{t}(\Lambda)}_{s}({\mathbb{Z}})\big].

(97)

The right-hand side of (
97
) is the expected number of times that the particles
\xi_{t}(\Lambda)
coalesce through time
s
.
This is lower bounded if we fix a particle in
\xi_{t}(\Lambda)
, and consider the number of coalescence between this particle and others only, which is further bounded from below by

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[\xi_{t}(\Lambda)-1\big]\cdot\min_{|x|\leq L}{\mathbb{P}}(\tau_{x}\leq 2s).

Recall that
\Delta(x,s)={\mathbb{P}}(X^{0}_{s}=0)-{\mathbb{P}}(X^{x}_{s}=0)
.
Using the last-exit decomposition (
48
),

{\mathbb{P}}(\tau_{x}\leq s)=1-\Delta(x,s)-\int_{0}^{s}\Delta(x,u)j(s-u)\mathrm{d}u.

Since
|\Delta(x,u)|\leq\frac{c|x|}{B_{u}^{2}}\leq\frac{cL}{B_{u}^{2}}
and

, it follows
|\int_{0}^{s}\Delta(x,u)j(s-u)\mathrm{d}u|\leq\frac{csL}{B_{s}^{2}l(s)}
.
Inserting all these bounds into (

), we get

L\rho_{1}(t)-L\rho_{1}(t+s)\geq\big(L\rho_{1}(t)-1\big)\Big(1-\frac{csL}{B_{s}^{2}l(s)}\Big),

(98)

implying

where

.
Repeating this for

many times yields

L\rho_{1}(2t)\leq P^{\kappa}L\rho_{1}(t)+(1-P)^{-1}.

Choose

and

. Then
P\leq\frac{ct^{2}}{B_{t}^{2}l(t)^{2}}(t/s)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1+\varepsilon}\leq\frac{c}{\ell(t)^{1-\tilde{\varepsilon}}}
.
Thus

for large enough

.
Since

and

as

, we have that

\rho_{1}(2t)\leq\frac{\rho_{1}(t)}{\ell(t)}+\frac{2l(t)}{t}.

Then, for all

\rho_{1}(2t)-\frac{5\hskip 1.0ptl(2t)}{2t}\leq\frac{1}{10}\Big(\rho_{1}(t)-\frac{5\hskip 1.0ptl(t)}{t}\Big),

where we used

for the increasing function

.
The upper bound in (

) follows easily from the last inequality.

.

To simplify the ODE, we have to replace

Proof of Theorem

We can now derive the sharp bounds in Theorem

.
Recall that for each
t

s=s(t)
through (

\theta\in(1-1/K,1)

s\hskip 1.0pt\rho_{1}(t-s)=l(s)^{\theta}.

(105)

On one hand, the monotonicity of
l

imply

s\hskip 1.0pt\rho_{1}(t-s)\leq l(t)^{\theta}.

On the other hand, take
\tilde{s}=t/l(\theta)^{\delta}
with
0<\delta<1-\theta
.
Using the rough bound
\rho_{1}(t)\asymp l(t)/t
from Proposition
4.3

\tilde{s}\hskip 1.0pt\rho_{1}(t-\tilde{s})\geq t\hskip 1.0ptl(t)^{-\delta}c\hskip 1.0ptl(t)/t=c\hskip 1.0ptl(t)^{1-\delta},

and the r.h.s. is larger than

Hence, by monotonicity of
u\mapsto u\rho_{1}(t-u)
, we conclude that

s(t)\leq t/l(t)^{\delta}\ll t.

Next, recall Lemma
4.1
with
N=2
and error terms

and
{\cal R}_{3}

From the generator calculation
-\big(\rho_{1}(t)\big)^{\prime}=\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}}p(-x){\mathbb{E}}[\xi_{t}(0)\xi_{t}(x)]

-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\rho_{1}(t)=\rho_{1}(t-s)^{2}\sum_{x}p(x){\mathbb{P}}(\hat{\tau}_{x}>s)\hskip 1.0pt\big(1+{\cal R}_{1}+{\cal R}_{2}+{\cal R}_{3}\big).

(106)

\alpha=1
and
\alpha<1

\alpha=1
and
l(\infty)=\infty

\alpha<1

{\cal R}_{1}=O\Big(\frac{1}{l(t)^{1-\theta}}\Big),\quad{\cal R}_{2}=O\Big(\frac{1}{\ell(t)^{1-\varepsilon}}\Big),\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\cal R}_{3}=O\Big(\frac{l(s)^{1-\theta}}{\ell(s)}\Big).

\sum_{x}p(s){\mathbb{P}}(\hat{\tau}_{x}>s)=\frac{1}{g_{\alpha}(0)\hskip 1.0ptl(2t)}\Big(1+O\Big(\frac{1}{\ell(t)^{1-\varepsilon}}\Big)+O\Big(\frac{l(t)^{\varepsilon}}{\ell(t)}\Big)\Big),

(108)

where we approximated

-\rho^{\prime}_{1}(t)\leq C\rho_{1}(t-s)^{2}/l(s)

Appendix A
Negative correlation

The negative correlation of coalescing random walks, first proved in
(
Arr, 81
, Lemma 1)
, is well-known.
We extend the result to a slightly more general form, using a theorem of Harris (see, e.g.,
(
Lig, 05
, Chapter II, Theorem 2.14)
).
For convenience, below we identify
\xi_{t}
as the set
\{x:\xi_{t}(x)=1\}
.
For
A\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}
, we write
(\xi^{A}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
as the coalescing random walks starting from every point of the set
\xi_{0}^{A}=A
.

Theorem A.1

Let
(\xi^{A}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be coalescing random walks starting from some
A\subset{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
be distinct points of
{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Then for all
t\geq 0
,

{\mathbb{P}}\big(\xi^{A}_{t}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=1\big)\leq{\mathbb{P}}\big(\xi^{A}_{t}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})=1\big){\mathbb{P}}\big(\xi^{A}_{t}(x_{n})=1\big).

(115)

Proof
Let
(\zeta^{B}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, starting from
B
, be the dual voter model constructed from
(\xi^{A}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
using the Harris graphical representation.
For
\cup_{i}C_{i}\bigcap D=\emptyset
, define a Markov process
X_{t}:=(\zeta_{t}^{C_{1}},\ldots,\zeta_{t}^{C_{n-1}},\zeta_{t}^{D})
with state space
E:=\{(C,D)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n-1}\times{\mathbb{Z}}\}
.
Let the partial order of
E
be
(C,D)\prec(C^{\prime},D^{\prime})
iff
C\subseteq C^{\prime}
and
D\supseteq D^{\prime}
.
Then
X_{t}
is a monotone process which jumps only among sites that are comparable under
\prec
. Define increasing functions on
(E,\prec)

f(C,D)=\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}1_{\{C_{i}\cap A\neq\emptyset\}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad g(C,D)=1_{\{D\cap A=\emptyset\}}

(116)

The condition (2.15) of
(
Lig, 05
, Chapter II, Theorem 2.14)
is satisfied, and therefore

{\mathbb{P}}(\zeta^{C_{i}}_{t}\cap A\neq\emptyset,\zeta^{D}_{t}\cap A=\emptyset)\geq{\mathbb{P}}(\zeta^{C_{i}}_{t}\cap A\neq\emptyset){\mathbb{P}}(\zeta^{D}_{t}\cap A=\emptyset).

(117)

Taking complement of the event
\{\zeta^{D}_{t}\cap A=\emptyset\}
, letting
C_{i}=\{x_{i}\}
and
D=\{x_{n}\}
, and using the duality
\{\zeta_{t}^{x_{i}}\cap A\neq\emptyset\}=\{\xi_{t}^{A}\cap\{x_{i}\}\neq\emptyset\}
, we have (
115
).

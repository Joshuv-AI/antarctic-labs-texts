A note on small probabilities for spherical random fields at a critical regime
Marco Carfagnini

Abstract
. We consider time-dependent space isotropic and time stationary spherical Gaussian random fields. We establish Chung’s law of the iterated logarithm and solve the small probabilities problem. Our results depend on the high-frequency behaviour of the angular power spectrum: the speed of decay of the small ball probability is faster as either the memory parameter or the space-parameter decreases.

60G60, 60G17, 60G15, 42C05.

The analysis of limiting behavior of stochastic processes and random fields has been extensively studied by many authors, including sample path properties

]
, small probabilities
[

,

]
, and Chung’s law of the iterated logarithm
[
11
,

,
27
,

]
for several classes of random processes, including scalar and vector valued random fields and degenerate diffusions. We refer to
[
26
,
19
,
17
]
and references therein for more complete surveys.

The main purpose of this note is to provide small ball probabilities and Chung’s law of the iterated logarithm for spherical random fields at a critical regime. We recall that a real-valued random field
\{X_{t},t\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}
satisfies Chung’s law of the iterated logarithm (LIL) at
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with rate function
\phi
if there exists a constant
C
such that

(1.1)

\displaystyle\liminf_{r\rightarrow 0}\phi(r)\max_{t\in B_{r}(t_{0})}|X_{t}-X_{t_{0}}|=C\;\;\;a.s.

where
B_{r}(t_{0})
is an appropriate ball of radius
r>0
centered at
t_{0}
.
The small ball problem (SBP) consists in finding a function
\theta
and a constant
c
such that

(1.2)

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\theta(\varepsilon)\log\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{t\in B_{r}(t_{0})}|X_{t}-X_{t_{0}}|<\varepsilon\right)=c.

When
X_{t}
is a Brownian motion and
t_{0}=0
, it was proven in a famous paper by K.-L. Chung in 1948
[
7
]
that (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) hold with
\phi(r)=\sqrt{\frac{\log|\log r|}{r}}
,
\theta(\varepsilon)=-\varepsilon^{2}
, and
c=C^{2}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{8}
.

Here, we consider an isotropic centered spherical Gaussian random field
\{T(x),\;x\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\}
with covariance function given in terms of the power spectrum
\{C_{\ell},\;\ell=0,1,2,\ldots\}
. Over the past few decades, the analysis of spherical random fields has drawn lot of interest in light of its applications to the analysis of Cosmic Microwave Background radiation data, see
[
8
,
9
]
. Following theoretical and observational evidence on CMB radiation data, the power spectrum is usually assumed to have the following decay

(1.3)

C_{\ell}=G(\ell)\ell^{-\alpha},\quad\ell=1,2,\ldots,

for some
\alpha>2
, where
G(\ell)
is a positive bounded function. We refer to Section 2 for more details.

As pointed out in
[
21
]
, one of the main ingredients in proving Chung’s LIL for random fields is a lower bound on the conditional variance, known as strong local nondeterminism. For spherical random fields satisfying condition (
1.3
) with
2<\alpha<4
, the strong local nondeterminism has been proved in
[
13
, Theorem 1]
. More precisely, it is shown that

(1.4)

\text{Var}\left(T(x_{0})\,|\,T(x_{1}),\ldots,T(x_{n})\right)\geqslant c\min_{1\leqslant k\leqslant n}\left(d_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(x_{0},x_{k})\right)^{\alpha-2},

for all sufficiently close
x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
, where
c>0
is a finite constant. The bound (
1.4
) has then been used to characterize sample paths behavior of spherical random fields, see
[
13
, Theorem 2]
. Note that when
2<\alpha<4
, the field’s trajectories present a fractal behavior since the modulus of continuity decays slower than linearly with respect to the angular distance, hence the sample function
T(x)
is not differentiable
[
13
, Theorem 2]
. In
[
13
]
it is conjectured that (
1.4
) holds for
\alpha=4
in the form

(1.5)

\displaystyle\text{Var}\left(T(x_{0})\,|\,T(x_{1}),\ldots,T(x_{n})\right)\geqslant c\min_{1\leqslant k\leqslant n}d_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(x_{0},x_{k})^{2}|\log d_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(x_{0},x_{k})|.

The strong local non-determinism (
1.4
) is a key ingredient in proving small ball estimates and Chung’s LIL. It has been used in
[
21
]
to prove Chung’s LIL for stationary Gaussian random fields on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
; and more recently in
[
5
]
to prove small fluctuations for time-dependent spherical random fields. In particular,
[
5
, Theorem 2.11 and Theorem 2.12]
prove (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) for an isotropic centered spherical Gaussian random field
\{T(x),\;x\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\}
with power spectrum satisfying (
1.3
) with
2<\alpha<4
. There, it is shown that (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) hold with

\displaystyle\phi(r)=\left(\frac{\log|\log r|}{r^{2}}\right)^{\frac{\alpha-2}{4}},

\displaystyle\theta(\varepsilon)=-\varepsilon^{\frac{4}{\alpha-2}}.

We remark that such rates for isotropic spherical random fields satisfying(
1.3
) with
2<\alpha<4
have been proved originally in
[
34
]
. Besides Euclidean and spherical case, the strong local nondeterminism, and its applications to the modulus of continuity, has also been studied for istropic Gaussian random fields on compact two-points homogeneous spaces, see
[
20
]
.

The goal of this note is to prove (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) for an isotropic centered spherical Gaussian random field in the critical regime, that is, satisfying (
1.3
) with
\alpha=4
. More precisely, in Theorem
2.4
we show that, if
\alpha=4
in (
1.3
) and if (
1.5
) holds, then (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) are satisfied with

\displaystyle\phi(r)=\left(\frac{\log|\log r|}{r^{2}|\log r|}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle\theta(\varepsilon)=-\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|^{-1}.

The extra
|\log\varepsilon|^{-1}
and
|\log r|
factors which are not present in the
2<\alpha<4
case come from the asymptotic of the Lambert function, see Proposition
3.1
for more details. Once the correct rates
\phi
and
\theta
are identified, the proof of Theorem
2.4
then relies on Talagrand’s bounds for small probabilities of Gaussian fields, and Borel Cantelli Lemmas.

The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 contains background material on spherical random fields and Talagrand bounds on small probabilities for Gaussian random fields. Some preliminary results on the SBP and Chung’s LIL are the content of Section 3, and the proof of Theorem
2.4
is in Section 4.

2.
Background and main results

2.1.
Talagrand bounds for Gaussian fields

Small ball probabilities of random fields have been extensively studied, see
[
25
,
15
,
33
,
31
]
and references therein. In this sections we recall results from
[
28
]
that we will use throughout the paper. The canonical metric
d_{X}
associated to a random field
\{X_{t},\,t\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}
is defined as

(2.1)

d_{X}(t,s):=\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left[(X_{t}-X_{s})^{2}\right]}.

One of the main ingredients in our proofs is Talagrand’s bounds on small ball probabilities for Gaussian processes.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
28
, Lemma 2.1 and Lemma 2.2]

Let
\{X_{t},\,t\in K\}
be a separable, real-valued, mean zero Gaussian process indexed by a compact set
K
with canonical metric
d_{X}
. Let
N(K,d_{X},\varepsilon)
be the smallest number of
d_{X}
-balls of radius
\varepsilon
needed to cover the set
K
. Suppose that there is a function
\Psi:(0,\delta)\rightarrow(0,\infty)
such that
N(K,d_{X},\varepsilon)\leqslant\Psi(\varepsilon)
for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\delta)
and there are constants
1<a_{1}\leqslant a_{2}
such that for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\delta)

(2.2)

\displaystyle a_{1}\Psi(\varepsilon)\leqslant\Psi\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\leqslant a_{2}\Psi(\varepsilon).

Then, there is a finite constant
k
depending only on
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
such that for all
u\in(0,\delta)

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{s,t\in K}|X_{t}-X_{s}|\leqslant u\right)\geqslant\exp\left(-k\Psi(u)\right).

Moreover, there exists a positive constant
c_{1}
such that for any
u>0

(2.4)

\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{s,t\in K}|X_{s}-X_{t}|\geqslant c_{1}\left(u+\int_{0}^{D}\sqrt{\log N(K,d_{X},\varepsilon)}d\varepsilon\right)\right)\leqslant\exp\left(-\frac{u^{2}}{D^{2}}\right),

where
D
is the diameter of
K
with respect to
d_{X}
.

2.2.
Spherical Gaussian random fields

Let
\mathbb{S}^{2}
be the unit 2-dimensional sphere and
\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}
be the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
defined as

\dfrac{1}{\sin\theta}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\bigg\{\sin\theta\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\bigg\}+\dfrac{1}{\sin^{2}\theta}\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\varphi^{2}},\mbox{ }0\leq\theta\leq\pi,\mbox{ }0\leq\varphi<2\pi.

It is well-known that the spectrum of
\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}
is purely discrete and its eigenvalues are of the form
-\lambda_{\ell}=-\ell(\ell+1)
, where
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. The eigenfunctions associated to the eigenvalue
\lambda_{\ell}
are the spherical harmonics
\{Y_{\ell m},\,m=-\ell,\ldots,\ell\}
. Throughout this paper we consider real-valued spherical harmonics, which can be expressed in terms of Legendre associated functions in the following way, see
[
23
, Remark 3.37]

Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\varphi)=\begin{cases}\sqrt{\frac{2\ell+1}{2\pi}\frac{(\ell-m)!}{(\ell+m)!}}P_{\ell}^{m}(\cos\theta)\cos(m\varphi)&\mbox{ for }m=1,\dots,\ell,\\
\sqrt{\frac{2\ell+1}{2\pi}}P_{\ell}^{m}(\cos\theta)&\mbox{ for }m=0,\\
\sqrt{\frac{2\ell+1}{2\pi}\frac{(\ell-m)!}{(\ell+m)!}}P_{\ell}^{-m}(\cos\theta)\sin(-m\varphi)&\mbox{ for }m=-\ell,\dots,-1,\\
\end{cases}

where

\displaystyle P_{\ell}:[-1,1]\to\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle P_{\ell}(t)=\frac{1}{2^{\ell}\ell!}\frac{d^{\ell}}{dt^{\ell}}(t^{2}-1)^{\ell}

\displaystyle P_{\ell}^{m}(t):[-1,1]\to\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle P_{\ell}^{m}(t):=(1-t^{2})^{m/2}\frac{d^{m}}{dt^{m}}P_{\ell}(t)

for
\ell=1,2,\dots
and
m=-\ell,\ldots,\ell
. The Legendre polynomial of degree
\ell
,
P_{\ell}
, are normalized so that
P_{\ell}(1)=1
. Note that the set of spherical harmonics forms a complete orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2},dx)
, where
dx
denotes the Lebesgue measure. In particular, the functions
Y_{\ell m}
satisfy

\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}Y_{\ell m}+\lambda_{\ell}Y_{\ell m}=0,

and the addition and duplication formulae read

(2.5)

\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}Y_{\ell m}(x){Y}_{\ell m}(y)=\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}P_{\ell}(\left\langle x,y\right\rangle)\text{ ,}

(2.6)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}P_{\ell}(\left\langle x,z\right\rangle)\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}P_{\ell}(\left\langle z,y\right\rangle)dz=\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}P_{\ell}(\left\langle x,y\right\rangle)\text{ ,}

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
.

A real-valued spherical random field
T
is a collection of centered random variables defined on a common probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
such that
T:\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{2}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
is an
\mathcal{F}\times\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
-measurable map, where
\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
denotes the Borel sigma-algebra of
\mathbb{S}^{2}
. We are interested in mean-square continuous isotropic centered Gaussian spherical random fields. We recall that a spherical random field
\{T(x),\,x\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\}
is isotropic if
T(x)
and
T(g.x)
have the same distribution for any
g\in\text{SO}(3)
. It is well-known that such a field can be represented as, see
[
23
, Theorem 5.13]

(2.7)

\displaystyle T(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sqrt{C_{\ell}}T_{\ell}(x)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\sqrt{C_{\ell}}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}a_{\ell,m}Y_{\ell,m}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{S}^{2},

(2.8)

\displaystyle T_{\ell}(x)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}a_{\ell,m}Y_{\ell,m}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{S}^{2},

where the random variables
\{a_{\ell,m},m=-\ell,\dots,\ell\}
are i.i.d. standard Gaussian. The sequence
\{C_{\ell}\}_{\ell=0,1,\dots}
is called angular power spectrum of
T(x)
and it is non-negative and such that
\sum_{\ell}C_{\ell}\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}=\mathbb{E}[T^{2}]<\infty
(see
[
23
,
24
]
). The series
2.7
converges in
L^{2}(\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
, and in
L^{2}(\Omega)
at every fixed
x
. The power spectrum is usually assumed to have the following decay

(2.9)

C_{\ell}=G(\ell)\ell^{-\alpha},\quad\ell=1,2,\ldots,

where
\alpha>2
is constant and the function
G(\ell)
is bounded above and below by positive constants. Condition (
2.9
) is aligned with the theoretical and observational evidence on Cosmic Microwave Background radiation data, which has been one of the main motivating areas for the analysis of spherical fields over the last decade. The assumption
\alpha>2
is needed for the field to have finite variance. Indeed, applying (
2.5
) it is easy to see that

\mathbb{E}[T_{\ell}(x)T_{\ell}(y)]=\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}P_{\ell}(\langle x,y\rangle)

and then
the covariance function of
T(x)
is given by

\Gamma(x,y)=\mathbb{E}[T(x)T(y)]=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}C_{\ell}\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}P_{\ell}(\langle x,y\rangle)\quad\mbox{ for all }x,y\in\mathbb{S}^{2}.

Assumption 2.2
.

Let
\{T(x),\,x\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\}
be an isotropic Gaussian field with
angular power spectrum such that

(2.10)

\displaystyle C_{\ell}=G(\ell)\ell^{-4}>0,\;\;\ell=1,2,\ldots

and we assume that

\displaystyle c^{-1}\leqslant G(\ell)\leqslant c,

for some finite constant
c\geqslant 1
. Moreover, we assume that there exist two positive and finite constants
c_{2}
and
\varepsilon_{0}
such that for all integers
n\geqslant 1
, and all
x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
with
\min_{1\leqslant k\leqslant n}d_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(x_{0},x_{k})\leqslant\varepsilon_{0}
we have that

(2.11)

\text{Var}\left(T(x_{0})\,|\,T(x_{1}),\ldots,T(x_{n})\right)\geqslant c_{2}\min_{1\leqslant k\leqslant n}\rho\left(d_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(x_{0},x_{k})\right)^{2},

where

(2.12)

\rho(t):=t\sqrt{|\ln t|}\;\;t>0,\quad\rho(0):=0.

If
2.10
holds, then by
[
13
, Lemma 4]
there exist constants
1\leqslant D<\infty
and
0<\varepsilon<1
such that for all
x,y\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
with
d_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(x,y)\leqslant\varepsilon
, one has that

\displaystyle D^{-1}\rho^{2}\left(d_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(x,y)\right)\leqslant d_{T}^{2}(x,y)\leqslant D\rho^{2}\left(d_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(x,y)\right),

where
\rho
is given by (
2.12
).

2.3.
Main Results

Let us now introduce the main object of study and the main result of this paper.

Notation 2.3
.

For any
x\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
and
r>0
let us set

\displaystyle M_{r}(x):=\max_{y\in B_{r}(x)}|T(y)-T(x)|,

where
B_{r}(x)
denotes the geodesic ball in
\mathbb{S}^{2}
centered in
x\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
; and for any
r>0
let

(2.13)

\displaystyle\phi(r):=\left(\frac{\log|\log r|}{r^{2}|\log r|}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\{T(x),\,x\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\}
be an isotropic Gaussian field satisfying Assumption
2.2
. Then there exists a finite constant
p>0
such that

(2.14)

\displaystyle\liminf_{r\rightarrow 0}\phi(r)M_{r}(x)=p,\;\text{a.s.}

(2.15)

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}-\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|^{-1}\log\mathbb{P}\left(M_{r}(x)<\varepsilon\right)=r^{2}p^{2}.

for all
x\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
.

We remark that the constant
p
does not depend on
x\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
. Chung’s LIL (
2.14
) describes sample paths properties of the random field
T(x)
, and due to isotropy such properties do not depend on the fixed point
x\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
.

3.
Small probabilities

3.1.
Small balls

We first prove a weaker version of the small deviation principle (
2.15
). Set

(3.1)

\displaystyle\psi(r,\varepsilon)=\frac{r^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|^{-1}}.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\{T(x),\,x\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\}
be an isotropic Gaussian field.

(i) If (
2.10
) holds, then there exists a positive finite constant
A_{1}
such that for all
\varepsilon>0
and
r>\varepsilon
we have that

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(M_{r}(x)<\varepsilon\right)\geqslant\exp\left(-A_{1}\psi(r,\varepsilon)\right),

(ii) If
\{T(x),\,x\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\}
satisfies (
2.10
) and (
2.11
), then there exists a positive finite constant
A_{2}
such that for all
\varepsilon>0
and
r>\varepsilon
we have that

(3.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(M_{r}(x)<\varepsilon\right)\leqslant\exp\left(-A_{2}\psi(r,\varepsilon)\right),

where
\psi(r,\varepsilon)
is given by (
3.1
).

Remark 3.2
.

Note that part (i) in Proposition
3.1
does not depend on the strong local nondeterminism (
2.11
). Moreover, if the power spectrum has a decay
\ell^{-\alpha}
for some
2<\alpha<4
, then (
3.2
) has been proven in
[
34
]
.

Proof.

(i) Let
N(B_{r}(x),d_{T},\varepsilon)
be the smallest number of
d_{T}
-balls of radius
\varepsilon
needed to cover
B_{r}(x)
. The canonical metric
d_{T}
is equivalent to
\rho(d_{\mathbb{S}^{2}})
, and hence it is enough to consider
N(B_{r}(x),\rho(d_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}),\varepsilon)
. The
\rho
-ball of radius
\varepsilon
is given by

\displaystyle B^{\rho}_{\varepsilon}(x)=\left\{y\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\,:\,\rho(d_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(x,y))<\varepsilon\right\}=\left\{y\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\,:\,d_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(x,y)\sqrt{|\log d_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(x,y)|}<\varepsilon\right\},

and at the north pole
x=N
it becomes

\displaystyle B^{\rho}_{\varepsilon}(N)=\left\{\left(\varphi,\theta\right)\in[0,2\pi)\times[0,\pi],\;\;\theta^{2}|\log\theta|<\varepsilon^{2}\right\}.

Note that the equation
\theta^{2}|\log\theta|=\varepsilon^{2}
has three distinct real solutions for
\varepsilon
small,
0<\theta_{1}(\varepsilon)<\theta_{2}(\varepsilon)<1<\theta_{3}(\varepsilon)
such that

\displaystyle\theta_{1}(\varepsilon)\rightarrow 0,

\displaystyle\theta_{2}(\varepsilon)\rightarrow 1,

\displaystyle\theta_{3}(\varepsilon)\rightarrow 1,

as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
. Due to isotropy, we can assume
x=N
, and since we are interested in a small ball asymptotics around
x=N
, we have that
\theta=d_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(y,N)<<1
. Thus, we can write

\displaystyle\text{Vol}\left(B^{\rho}_{\varepsilon}(N)\right)=2\pi\int_{0}^{\theta_{1}(\varepsilon)}\sin\theta d\theta=2\pi\left(1-\cos\theta_{1}(\varepsilon)\right)=\pi\theta_{1}(\varepsilon)^{2}+o\left(\theta_{1}(\varepsilon)^{2}\right).

By Lemma
5.1
we have that

\displaystyle\theta_{1}(\varepsilon)^{2}=4\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|^{-1}+o\left(\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|^{-1}\right),

that is,

\displaystyle\text{Vol}\left(B^{\rho}_{\varepsilon}(N)\right)=4\pi\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|^{-1}+o\left(\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|^{-1}\right),

and hence

\displaystyle N(B_{r}(N),\rho(d_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}),\varepsilon)\leqslant\frac{\text{Vol}\left(B_{r}(N)\right)}{\text{Vol}(B^{\rho}_{\varepsilon}(N))}=\frac{1-\cos r}{4\pi\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|^{-1}+o\left(\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|^{-1}\right)}=\frac{r^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|^{-1}}F(r,\varepsilon),

where
F(r,\varepsilon)
is uniformly bounded in
\varepsilon
and
r
. Thus we can take

\displaystyle\psi(r,\varepsilon):=\frac{r^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|^{-1}},

which satisfies the assumption of Lemma
2.1
.

(ii) The proof relies on a conditioning argument similar to the proof of
[
15
, Proposition 4.2]
. Let
F:A_{r}\rightarrow B_{r}(x)
be a local diffeomorphism, where
A_{r}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a rectangle and
F(0,0)=x
. Let us consider a rectangle
I=[0,r]\times[0,r]
for
r
small enough so that
I\subset A_{r}
, and let us divide
I
into sub-rectangles of side-length

\frac{r}{\bigg\lfloor\frac{r}{\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\bigg\rfloor+1},

where
\lfloor\cdot\rfloor
denotes the floor function. The number of such subrectangles is

N=\left(\bigg\lfloor\frac{r}{\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\bigg\rfloor+1\right)^{2}\geqslant\frac{r^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|^{-1}}

Let
(\theta_{i},\varphi_{i})
for
i=1,\ldots,N
denote the upper right-most vertex of the subrectangle, and set
x_{i}:=F(\theta_{i},\varphi_{i})
. Then by construction

\displaystyle d_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left((\theta_{i},\varphi_{i}),(\theta_{j},\varphi_{j})\right)\geqslant c\,\frac{r}{\bigg\lfloor\frac{r}{\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\bigg\rfloor+1}\quad\text{ for all }i,j=1,\ldots,N

for some constant
c>0
. We can choose
F
in such a way that
d_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(x_{i},x_{j})\geqslant d_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left((\theta_{i},\varphi_{i}),(\theta_{j},\varphi_{j})\right)
. Set

\displaystyle A_{j}:=\left\{\max_{i=1,\ldots,j}|T(x_{i})-T(x)|<\varepsilon\right\},

and write

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(A_{j})=\mathbb{E}\left[1_{A_{j-1}}\,\mathbb{P}\left(|T(x_{j})-T(x)|<\varepsilon\,\left.\right|T(x_{i})\,:\,i=0,\ldots,j-1\right)\right].

By (
2.11
) we have that

\displaystyle\text{Var}\left(T(x_{j})\,|\,T(x_{i})\,:\,i=0,\ldots,j-1\right)\geqslant c_{2}\min_{1\leqslant i\leqslant j-1}\rho\left(d_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(x_{j},x_{i})\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant c_{2}\,\rho\left(\frac{c\;r}{\bigg\lfloor\frac{r}{\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\bigg\rfloor+1}\right)^{2}\geqslant c_{2}\,\rho\left(\frac{c\;r}{\frac{2r}{\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|^{-\frac{1}{2}}}}\right)^{2}=c_{2}\,\rho\left(\frac{c}{2}\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{2}}{4}c^{2}\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|^{-1}\left|\log\left(\frac{c}{2}\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\right|\geqslant D\varepsilon^{2},

where we used that
\rho(t)
is an increasing function for small values of
t>0
, and that

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}|\log\varepsilon|^{-1}\left|\log\left(\frac{c}{2}\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\right|=1.

The conditional distribution of
T(x_{j})
given all the
T(x_{i})
is Gaussian with conditional variance

Comparison of local anaesthesia and regional anaesthesia (PSP/IPP) on QoR-15 in patients undergoing subpectoral implantation of cardiac implantable electronic devices (CIEDs)—a prospective, randomized, double-blinded, controlled pragmatic trial with two arms
Authors: Bernd Wallner, Ottokar Stundner, Elisabeth Gasteiger, Markus Mittermayr, Anna Fiala, Markus Werner Hollmann, Simone Gasser, Lukas Stastny, Lukas Gasteiger
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe implantation of cardiac implantable electronic devices (CIED) is the treatment of choice for the prevention of sudden cardiac deaths in high-risk patients. Given the comorbidities, this superficial surgical intervention is mostly performed in local anaesthesia. Triggered by the upcoming opioid crisis, the burden of postsurgical pain has gained popularity, as adequate perioperative pain management is a cornerstone in the prevention of persistent opioid use. Aim of this study is to assess the perioperative pain management of local anaesthesia versus pectoserratus plain/interpectoral plain (PSP/IPP) block for submuscular CIED implantation.MethodsIn a single-centre, prospective, single-blinded, two-group randomized trial, 80 patients undergoing submuscular CIED implantation will be randomized to receive a PSP/IPP with ropivacaine 0.375% (intervention group) or a LA with lidocaine 2% (control group). The primary outcome is the Quality of Recovery-15 (QoR-15) assessed 24 h after surgery. Secondary endpoints include the intraoperative additional LA administration; the postoperative analgesic consumption; the need and dose of additional analgesia; Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) after 2, 4, 6, 12, and 24 h; and VAS-AUC and VAS groups <30 mm/30–60 mm/>60 mm.DiscussionThis prospective, randomized, controlled, and single-blinded trial aims to assess if a PSP/IPP with ropivacaine affects the postoperative QoR-15 score compared to a conventional local anaesthesia with lidocaine in patients undergoing submuscular CIED implantation. Given the impressively high rate of persistent opioid use (POU) in patients after CIED, results from this study could help improve the perioperative pain management.Trial registrationEUDRA CT Number: 2023-508997-27. Introduction

Background and rationale {6a}

The implantation of a cardiac implantable electronic devices (CIEDs) is considered to be the treatment of choice for the prevention of sudden cardiac death in high-risk patients (e.g. patients suffering from severe cardiac failure, paroxysmal ventricular fibrillation, and those who have already suffered cardiac arrest of a cardiac cause). This study only includes CIED generators which are implanted in the subpectoral lodge. This location has fewer wound infections and probe dislocations, and it also offers more comfort for the patient in everyday life. Even though the implantation of the CIED generator into the subpectoral lodge is significantly more painful, the procedure is not performed under general anaesthesia due to the high perioperative risk of the patient population. Internationally, local infiltration with lidocaine has become established as the standard. Due to the nature and spread of the pectoserratus plain/interpectoral plain block (PSP/IPP), this block should also be suitable for surgical procedures in cardiothoracic surgery performed in the anterolateral chest wall region. This randomized controlled study aims to investigate the influence of PSP/IPP block versus local anaesthesia (LA) on subjective pain assessment in patients undergoing CIED implantation.

Objectives {7}

The defined primary objective of the current study is the influence of PSP/IPP block versus local anaesthesia on comfort assessment in patients undergoing CIED implantation, evaluated by the Quality of Recovery-15 score (QoR-15), a patient-centred assessment of perioperative management quality.

Trial design {8}

Prospective, randomized, double-blinded, controlled pragmatic trial with two arms.

Methods: participants, interventions, and outcomes

Study setting {9}

This prospective, randomized, double-blinded, controlled pragmatic trial with two arms will be designed as a single-centre study. The study will be set at the University Hospital Innsbruck, Medical University Innsbruck, Austria.

Interventions

Explanation for the choice of comparators {6b}

The comparator is local anaesthesia with lidocaine, which is widely being used as the standard procedural anaesthesia for this indication. Lidocaine belongs to the family of sodium channel blockers which is mainly used as a local anaesthetic and as antiarrhythmic agent. Lidocaine is an acid amide with the molecular formula C14H22N2O. In various medicinal products, lidocaine is usually present as hydrochloride. For the formulation, both lidocaine base and lidocaine hydrochloride are available. Lidocaine is used in the context of regional anaesthesia for the elimination of pain during surgical interventions or in the context of dental treatment. Lidocaine diffuses in its non-protonated, lipid-soluble form across the nerve cell membrane and becomes protonated intracellularly. In this form, it causes a reversible blockade of the voltage-dependent sodium channels. As a result, the sodium channel responsible for the depolarization of the nerve cell membrane is suppressed. The axonal conduction of the nerve fibre is interrupted and pain transmission is blocked. Lidocaine is distributed immediately after parenteral application into the tissue and thus reaches the target nerves rapidly. It is metabolized by the cytochrome 450 system of the liver through glucuronidation to monoethylglycine xylidide and other substances. Less than 10% of the original dose is excreted unchanged in the urine. After application, a rapid distribution in the lungs, liver, brain, and heart occurs. In the further course, an accumulation in muscle and fat tissue can be observed.

Criteria for discontinuing or modifying allocated interventions {11b}

Criteria for discontinuing the modifying intervention is not applicable since it is a single intervention at one single time point. If, however, during the implantation of the CIED, the patient reports pain or discomfort, an addition dosage of local anaesthesia with lidocaine will be administered by the surgeon.

Strategies to improve adherence to interventions {11c}

Strategies to improve adherence to interventions are not applicable since it is a single intervention at one single time point.

Relevant concomitant care permitted or prohibited during the trial {11d}

Relevant concomitant patient care will most likely include perioperative and postoperative analgesia, which will include metamizole, paracetamol, and in the case of NRS > 3 piritramide.

Provisions for post-trial care {30}

All participants will be followed up and contacted after 30 and 90 days after the intervention. Furthermore, all patients will be equipped with a contact number which can be contacted in case of questions or problems. All participants are covered by an insurance, which is a legal requirement in Austria. This insurance covers damage to research participants through injury or death caused by the study. The insurance applies to the damage that becomes apparent during the study.

Sample size {14}

The planned number of participants is 80 (40 in the PSP/IPP group and 40 in the LA group).

The minimally clinical important difference (MCID) for the primary outcome (QoR-15) was recently updated to 6.0 from its previous value of 8.0, using AUC-ROC analysis (area under the receiver-operator-characteristics curve) from existing studies. With a standard deviation of 8.0 (estimated from the literature), 28 participants per group will be required to achieve adequate discrimination at α = 0.05 und β = 0.8. To facilitate the per-protocol analysis and the use of non-parametric statistical methods, the total number of participants will be increased by 7% to n = 60 (30 per group).

Assignment of interventions: allocation

Sequence generation {16a}

Approximately 80 participants will be randomly assigned in 1:1 ratio to either receive the PSP/IPP block with ropivacaine or local anaesthesia with lidocaine. The randomization will be in a blinded manner using https://www.sealedenvelope.com/simple-randomiser/v1/lists. The randomization list will be stored by the PI as an electronic file accessible only to study team members. At the end of the trial, the randomization list will be filed in the SMF.

Concealment mechanism {16b}

The randomization will be in a blinded manner using https://www.sealedenvelope.com/simple-randomiser/v1/lists. The allocation of the patient will be revealed to the study investigator just prior to anaesthesia induction by opening a sealed, opaque envelope (blinded anaesthesia nurse).

Implementation {16c}

All study team members will be able to enrol patients and can assign participants to the randomized intervention as decided by sealed envelopes.

Assignment of interventions: blinding

Who will be blinded {17a}

At the time of treatment, only the attending anaesthetist, who provides the PSP/IPP block, and the cardiac surgeon who eventually provides the local anaesthesia are aware of the respective allocation of a patient. Both the patient as well as the observing anaesthetists who performs the follow-up visits (V4–V10) are not aware of the randomization. Only the unblinded personnel will have controlled access to which arm the participant is randomly assigned.

Procedure for unblinding if needed {17b}

If necessary, the blinding can be lifted by accessing the secured randomization list or by asking the attending anaesthetist or cardiac surgeon.

Data collection and management

Plans for assessment and collection of outcomes {18a}

All investigators will be trained within the initiation visit. Training topics cover Good Clinical Practice, the study protocol, and QoR-15 and VAS assessment execution. The patient’s demographical data will be registered immediately after the patient has given informed consent. The PSP/IPP block will be performed by experienced anaesthesiologists who have completed at least 50 procedures. All data will be collected using a paper-based CRF. The healthcare data of participants will be derived from the electronic patient file. All data will be stored for 15 years, and results will be published in scientific journals.

Plans to promote participant retention and complete follow-up {18b}

All patients will be visited and followed up regularly as part of their planned in-hospital stay. After enrolment and randomization, the investigators will make every reasonable effort to follow the study subject throughout the entire study period. Participant retention will be increased by implementing study visits into routine clinical postoperative care. After being discharged from hospital, the patients will be contacted twice by telephone (after 30 and 90 days) and interviewed to their well-being and any possible pain. However, all participants are free to discontinue the study participation at any time.

Data management {19}

The data of all participants will be recorded using a pseudonymized paper-based case report form (CRF). The data, including the baseline information, vital signs, QoR-15, and VAS scale, will be transferred into a computer-based data collection form and stored in personalized, password-secured computer folder. After education of the participants and the participants’ written consent has been obtained, the same study team member will collect the initial data set. During the implementation of the PSP/IPP block or local anaesthesia, as well as the implantation of the CIED, as well as the entire postoperative observational period, all vital signs will be documented in the paper-based CRF. Data collection will be continued in the postoperative phase and 30 and 90 days after the patient has been discharged from hospital as laid out in the participation time line. For the purpose of double data entry, all data will be transferred into the computer-based data collection form. The digital data collection form will be stored in a password-secured intranet-folder, which can only be accessed by study team members. Data will then be transferred to the statistical program for data analysis. Programmed checks make data validation of range, validity, and consistency. If necessary, queries are made by the study software or an authorized person. After recording all entries and clarifying all queries, the database will be closed after the study. This performance must be documented. All data will be handled confidentially, and research data will be coded using a unique patient identification number.

An independent external study monitor will be commissioned to carry out the monitoring and quality assurance of the study. Based on the questions, the investigator can check and clarify discrepancies. To reproduce the study findings and help future users understand and reuse the data, all changes made to the raw data and all steps taken in the analysis will be documented. The database files will be kept for 15 years after the study has been completed.

All study member who will handle personal data will have to adhere to all local and national data protection guidelines and the highest confidentiality. Statistical methods

Statistical methods for primary and secondary outcomes {20a}

Demographic variables and baseline characteristics will be summarized between treatment groups by descriptive statistics (mean, standard deviation or median and interquartile range for continuous variables, and number and percentage for categorical variables). Between-group analyses of the primary and secondary outcomes will be performed using frequentist and Bayesian statistical hypothesis tests (t-test or non-parametric equivalent; chi-squared test or non-parametric equivalent) and mixed model mixed linear models. Longitudinal comparisons will be assessed for between-group discrimination using generalized estimating equations (GEE) or comparable linear models.

Interim analyses {21b}

We plan an interim analysis with non-binding termination criteria after 50% of the participants have finished the study protocol (including the follow-up period). Data will only be unblinded by a third-party biostatistician not involved in data collection. The interim results will be made available to all study contributors, the study supervisors, and the Data and Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB). Efficacy will be evaluated using significance thresholds form an O’Brien-Fleming-like alpha spending function [1]. Futility will be evaluated by assessing conditional power, with 0.1 as the futility threshold. In case of apparent efficacy or futility at the interim analysis time point, termination of the study will be discussed with the DSMB. Safety will be monitored continuously.

Methods for additional analyses (e.g. subgroup analyses) {20b}

No subgroup analyses are planned.

Methods in analysis to handle protocol non-adherence and any statistical methods to handle missing data {20c}

Efficacy analyses will be performed using the intention-to-treat (ITT) and per-protocol (PP) populations, and participants will be included in the treatment group to which they are randomized. The primary analysis population will be the PP set. A missingness analysis will be performed after the conclusion of patient sampling. We do not anticipate significant missingness due to the sampling method. Patients with considerable missing data (e.g. those lost to follow-up) will be placed in the ITT cohort. Simple or multiple imputation by chained equations will be applied to substitute missing data if applicable; a sensitivity analysis excluding cases with missing data (complete case analysis) will be performed [2].

Plans to give access to the full protocol, participant-level data, and statistical code {31c}

Access to the full protocol, participant-level data as well as statistical code will be made available upon reasonable request.

Discussion

This study will assess the influence of a combined pectoserratus/interpectoral plane block using ropivacaine versus local anaesthesia using lidocaine to implant submuscular CIED on the quality of recovery as expressed by QoR-15.

The burden of postsurgical pain, and especially the development of chronic postsurgical pain, has gained increasing acuity in the sight of the ongoing opioid crisis [3]. History of any surgical intervention is increasingly identified as a trigger for persistent opioid use (POU), which in turn is associated with significant morbidity, mortality, and risk of individual social demise. Adequate perioperative pain management could be a pivotal point for the prevention of chronic pain and POU after surgery, regardless of the extent of the surgical intervention. In a recent population-based study, Brummet et al. found similar rates for new POU after minor or major surgery (5.9% vs 6.5%, respectively); both figures were significantly higher compared to the control group without previous surgery (0.4%) [4]. The rate of new POU is even more striking when patients after CIED implantation are assessed. CIED implantation is notoriously painful and associated with a high incidence of severe postoperative and prolonged (chronic) pain, which is often treated with opioids. Two recently published retrospective analyses, including 16,000 and 143,000 patients after CIED implantation, describe an incidence of new POU (defined as repeated opioid prescription refills or opioid use exceeding 30 days) of 10–20% [5, 6].

These concerning figures of POU after minor cardiac surgery raises the question whether patients undergoing CIED implantation receive optimal and lasting perioperative analgesia. Moreover, the Early Recovery after Cardiac Surgery (ERACS) guidelines advocate for the reduction of perioperative used opioids in minor and major cardiac surgery, as opioids negatively impact on early mobilization, early extubation, and hospital length of stay [7].

Traditionally, CIED implantations are performed under local infiltration anaesthesia, mostly using the short-acting local anaesthetic lidocaine. General anaesthesia or deeper sedation is frequently problematic in this patient group, as the circulation-depressing effects of hypnotics may negatively impact their already severely impaired ventricular function and invoke perioperative complications [8]. Lidocaine is an amide-type LA with a duration of action of 2 to 3 h. The use of regional anaesthesia instead of local infiltration, in combination with longer-acting LAs, was previously shown to reduce perioperative pain scores and potentially exert benefits concerning medium- and long-term postinterventional pain [9–11].

The combined pectoserratus plane block and interpectoral plane block (PSP/IPP) is a truncal block that was first described by Blanco et al. in 2012 as PSP/IPP block [12, 13]. The PSP/IPP encompasses injection of LA in the fascial planes between the pectoralis minor and serratus minor (pectoserratus plane block), as well as between the pectoralis major and minor muscles (interpectoral plane block). The PSP + IPP was shown to effectively reduce postoperative pain and opioid consumption and enhance the postoperative quality of recovery after different types of surgery on the thorax, including radical mastectomy as well as minimally invasive cardiac surgery [14–16]. Given the location, the unilateral approach as well as the comparatively short duration of submuscular CIED implantation, the PSP + IPP could be an interesting addition to a multimodal analgesia approach to reduce perioperative opioid use.

Several studies, case reports, and case series have recently advocated for regional anaesthesia use in CIED implementation for the abovementioned reasons. However, most existing studies are burdened by small sample sizes, non-systematic patient inclusion, lack of blinded assessment, lack of long-term follow-up, or a focus on too narrow outcomes like singular pain scores or short-term opioid consumption in lieu of more integrative, patient-important metrics like quality of recovery, or overall benefit of analgesia [17–19].

At our institution, PSP/IPP has been routinely and successfully performed for CIED implantation for several years. With this study, we aim to assess the impact of the SPS + IPP block using ropivacaine 0.375% on quality of recovery (QoR-15 score) compared to the standard of care local infiltration using lidocaine 1%. The results of this study may help improve perioperative analgesia and, in the medium term, lessen the burden of postoperative and chronic pain as well as POU in patients receiving a CIED.

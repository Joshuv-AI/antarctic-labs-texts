Independence of homogeneous GKM manifolds and symmetric spaces
Shintarô Kuroki, Grigory Solomadin

Abstract
. Let G / H G/H be a simply connected homogeneous space of maximal rank. Then the maximal torus T T -action on G / H G/H is a GKM manifold. We call the T T -action j j -independent if any i ( ≤ j ) i(\leq j) pairwise distinct isotropy weights at a fixed point are linearly independent. Using weighted graphs, we show that the maximal independence of G / H G/H is 2 2 , 3 3 or n = dim T n=\dim T , and that the cases of 3 3 or n = dim T n=\dim T correspond to some symmetric spaces of rank > 2 >2 . As a corollary, using the results of Ayzenberg and Masuda, the lower-degree reduced homology groups (with appropriate coefficients) of the orbit space T \ G / H T\backslash G/H vanish.

Introduction

1.1.
Motivation and Main theorem

Let

2m

There is the following irreducible decomposition

(1.1)

\displaystyle T_{p}M\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}V_{\alpha_{p,i}},

of the isotropy representation at any fixed point

Set

\displaystyle k(p):=\max\{q\ |\ \text{every $q$ elements in $S_{p}$ are linearly independent}\},\ k(M):=\min\limits_{p\in M^{T}}k(p).

If

[
GKM98
]
,
[
GZ01
]
.

]

1.2.
Sketch of the proof

The proof of Theorem

for every homogeneous GKM manifold

.
Here,

for every fixed point

]

We prove that

[

]

]

We apply this technique together with Borel-de Siebenthal theory

In the remaining cases, i.e. for

Preparations

2.1.
k
-independence

Let
S:=\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m}\}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a set of vectors.
We call

a

which are not linearly independent.
In this case, we also say that the set of vectors

has the property
U_{m}^{k}
, i.e.,
the
uniform matroid
with rank
k
and with
m
elements, see
[
Ox06
]
.

Example 2.1
.

Let
S:=\{e_{1},e_{2},-e_{1}-e_{2},e_{1}+e_{2}+e_{3}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}

2.3.
The torus action on the homogeneous space
G/H

Let

is finite, and

be a closed, connected subgroup of

It is known in this case that

is a

[
MT79

Bu13
]

[

Below we recall the description of the tangential weights of the

by following these references.

We first recall some basic facts from the theory of Lie groups.
We denote the Lie algebras of

\mathfrak{t}\subseteq\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathfrak{g}
.
Let
\exp:G\to\mathfrak{g}
be the exponential map, and
\Lambda_{G}:=\exp^{-1}(e)\subset\mathfrak{t}
be the
integer lattice
, where

is the identity element.
Then, we have an identification
T\simeq\mathfrak{t}/\Lambda_{G}
and
{\rm Hom}(T,S^{1})\simeq\Lambda_{G}^{*}:={\rm Hom}(\Lambda_{G},\mathbb{Z})\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
The
\mathbb{Z}
-module
\Lambda^{*}_{G}\subset\mathfrak{t}^{*}
is called a

of

\mathfrak{t}^{*}={\rm Hom}(\mathfrak{t},\mathbb{R})
is the dual of

.
Note that if

is simply connected, then

is spanned by the fundamental weights, i.e. dual to simple roots (see
[
MT79
, Chapter 5 Theorem 6.36]
).
If
G
is not simply connected, then by taking the universal covering
p:\widetilde{G}\to G
and the dual

of its differential
dp
on the identities of maximal tori,
we may regard
\Lambda_{G}^{*}\subset\Lambda_{\widetilde{G}}^{*}
as finite index
>1

[
MT79
, Chapter 5 Theorem 4.9]
or
[
Bu13
, Section 19]
).
(Here we identify the map with the induced map for the de Rham cohomology groups
H^{1}(T;{\mathbb{R}})\to H^{1}(\widetilde{T};{\mathbb{R}})
, restricted to the respective lattices.)
Let
\Delta_{G}\subset\Lambda_{G}^{*}\subset\mathfrak{t}^{*}
be the root system of
G
with respect to the maximal torus
T
.
Because the linear relations among the root systems do not change by taking the finite covering of
G
, that is, one has
k(\widetilde{G}/H)=k(G/H)
, it is enough to consider the case when
G
is simply connected for the purpose of this paper.
Therefore, we can assume
\pi_{1}(G)=0
in this paper without loss of generality.

Let
G
be a simply connected, compact Lie group.
The set of the fixed points
(G/H)^{T}
can be identified with the finite set of the quotient
W_{G}/W_{H}
of the Weyl groups for the maximal torus

, where
W_{G}:=N_{G}(T)/T
and
W_{H}:=N_{H}(T)/T
.
Let
p_{0}=eH\in(G/H)^{T}\simeq W_{G}/W_{H}
be the coset of the identity element

.
In other words,
W_{G}
acts on
(G/H)^{T}
transitively, where the isotropy subgroup of

is

.
Then, the tangential representation at
wp_{0}
is

\displaystyle T_{wp_{0}}G/H=\bigoplus_{[\beta]\in\Delta_{G,H}/\{\pm 1\}}V_{[w\beta]},

where
\Delta_{G}^{+}\setminus\Delta_{H}^{+}\simeq\Delta_{G,H}/\{\pm 1\}
for positive roots
\Delta_{H}^{+}\subset\Delta_{G}^{+}
.
Note that
\Delta_{p_{0}}
and
\Delta_{wp_{0}}
for every

are isomorphic to

by being in the orbit of the Weyl group transitive action.
This implies the following proposition:

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
G
be a compact, connected semi-simple Lie group,
H
be its maximal rank subgroup and

Then

for any

This proposition shows that if we compute the

-independence on

-independence of all tangent spaces on the fixed points of

In this paper, we determine the

2.4.
The Borel-de Siebenthal theory

Let
G
be a compact, connected semi-simple Lie group,
H
be its maximal rank subgroup.
The classification of such pair
(G,H)
is known by the work of Borel-de Siebenthal
[
BS49
]
.
In this subsection, we briefly recall the Borel-de Siebenthal theory.

Borel-de Siebenthal theory
[
BS49
]
describes maximal subgroups of maximal rank in a simple Lie group
G
as normalizers of certain elements or circle subroups in
G
.
The classification of the respective root subsystems is given in terms of (ordinary or extended) Dynkin diagram of
G
as follows.
(Below we follow notation from
[
MT79
]
and tables from
[
Yo25
]
.)
Recall that
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
are simple roots of
G
, and the dominant root
\widetilde{\alpha}
can be written as

(2.1)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\alpha}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}\alpha_{i}

for some non-negative integers
m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}(\geq 0)
.
We set the additional notation by denoting by
X_{i}
the vertex opposed to the facet with normal vector
\alpha_{i}
of the simplex

S(G):=\{X\in\mathfrak{g}\mid\alpha_{i}(X)\geq 0,\ i=1,\dots,n,\ \widetilde{\alpha}(X)\leq 1\}\subset\mathfrak{g}.

Also let
x_{i}:=\exp(X_{i})\in G
, and let
N(x)
be the normalizer of
x\in G
.

Theorem 2.5

(
[
MT79
, Thm 7.16]
)
.

The Dynkin diagram of a maximal subgroup of maximal rank for a simple Lie group
G
is obtained from that of
G
as follows:

(1)

If
m_{i}
in (
2.1
) is prime, then the Dynkin diagram of
G_{i}=N(x_{i})
is obtained by deleting
\alpha_{i}
from the extended Dynkin diagram, which is the Dynkin diagram of
G
with
-\widetilde{\alpha}
added;

(2)

If
m_{i}=1
in (
2.1
), then
N(\exp X_{i}/2)
is locally a direct product of
S^{1}
and a semisimple subgroup
H
of corank
1
the Dynkin diagram of
H
is obtained by deleting
\alpha_{i}
from the Dynkin diagram of
G
.

Remark 2.6
.

By using the Borel-de Siebenthal theory,
on the level of Dynkin diagrams, a maximal rank subgroup
H(\subset G)
is determined by a sequence of certain added or removed vertices.
The isomorphism class of a subgroup does not depend on the choice of such a sequence,
though the embedding depends on the the order of such operations.
However, it turns out that the choice of embedding does not affect independence.

2.5.
Symmetric spaces

In order to state our main theorem, we also need the notion of symmetric spaces. Here, we quickly recall it.

Definition 2.7

(
[
MT79
, Chapter III, Section 6]
)
.

A homogeneous space
G/H
admitting an involution
\sigma\in\Aut G
is called a
symmetric space
, namely,
G^{\sigma}_{e}\subseteq H\subset G^{\sigma}=\{g\in G\mid\sigma g=g\}
holds, where
G^{\sigma}_{e}
is the identity component of
G^{\sigma}
.

There is a well-known criterion for
G/H
to be a symmetric space in terms of Lie algebras, which we recall next.
Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a
Lie algebra of a simply connected, compact, simple Lie group
G
.
It has a decomposition into the Cartan subalgebra
C(\mathfrak{g})
of
\mathfrak{g}
and characters
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
of
C(\mathfrak{g})
as follows:

(2.2)

\mathfrak{g}=C(\mathfrak{g})\oplus\bigoplus\limits_{\alpha\in\Delta^{+}_{G}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}.

Any Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
in
\mathfrak{g}
is the direct sub-summand of (
2.2
) given by the embedded root system
\Delta_{H}\subseteq\Delta_{G}
.
Since
\mathfrak{h}
is a Lie subalgebra, one has

[\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h}]\subset\mathfrak{h}.

Since
\mathfrak{g}
is semi-simple (and therefore reductive Lie algebra), the Killing form on
\mathfrak{g}
is nondegenerate, and the respective orthogonal complement to
\mathfrak{h}
in
\mathfrak{g}
, say
\mathfrak{m}
, is
\mathfrak{h}
-invariant.
Therefore, one has

[\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{m}]\subset\mathfrak{m}.

Recall the following proposition.

Proposition 2.8

(
[
Ba06
, §B.1]
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}
be any Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{g}
of any semisimple Lie algebra. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

[\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{m}]\subset\mathfrak{h}
, where
\mathfrak{m}
is the orthogonal complement to
\mathfrak{h}
with respect to the Killing form;

(2)

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
has an involution
\sigma
to which
\mathfrak{h}
is the eigenspace with value
1
and
\mathfrak{m}
is the eigenspace with value
-1
in the direct sum
\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{m}
;

(3)

The homogeneous space
G/H
is a symmetric space, where
G
is a Lie group and
H
is its closed subgroup such that the Lie algebras of
G
and
H
are
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}
, respectively.

The classification of symmetric spaces was obtained by E. Cartan
[
Ca27
]

3.
Main theorem and its application

In this section, we state our main theorem.
We also give its proof (modulo root system independence computations in later sections) and an application to the topology of the orbit spaces.

3.1.
Main theorem

If we assume
G
is simply connected, then the standard
T
-action on the simply connected homogeneous space
G/H
is equivariantly diffeomorphic to the following product of the homogeneous spaces (see e.g.
[
Ku10
, Section 2.2]
):

(3.1)

\displaystyle G/H\cong G_{1}/H_{1}\times\cdots\times G_{r}/H_{r},

where
G_{i}
is a compact, simply connected simple Lie group,
H_{i}
be its maximal rank subgroup and there is an isomorphism
T\cong T_{1}\times\cdots\times T_{r}
such that
T_{i}\subset H_{i}\subset G_{i}
is a maximal torus for
i=1,\ldots,r
.
Moreover, we can easily show the following proposition. (Recall that a torus manifold is equipped with a half-dimensional torus action.)

Proposition 3.1
.

Assume that
G/H
is not a torus manifold.
Then, there is the following equality for the splitting (
3.1
):

\displaystyle k(G/H)=\min\{k(G_{i}/H_{i})\ |\ 1\leq i\leq r\}.

Remark 3.2
.

For the case of a product for torus manifolds, the independencies equate half dimensions of the respective manifolds.
Therefore, one has to replace minimum with the sum in the right hand side of the above formula.

Proposition
3.1
shows that to compute the independence of
G/H
, it is enough to compute the independence of the simple factors
G_{i}/H_{i}
.
Any connected subgroup of
G
fits into a saturated chain of maximal subgroups, where at every step the respective embedding is that of maximal proper subgroup.

The main theorem of this paper is as follows.

Theorem 3.3
.

Assume that
G
is simply connected, simple Lie group and
H
be its maximal rank
subgroup such that
{\rm rank}(G)={\rm rank}(H)=\dim T=n
.
Then,
k(G/H)=2,3,n
and the following holds:

(1)

k(G/H)=n={\rm rank}(G)={\rm rank}(H)
if and only if
(G,H)
is one of the following types:

Type
A_{n}
:

(A_{n},A_{n-1}\times T^{1})
;

Type
B_{n}
:

(B_{n},D_{n})
.

(2)

k(G/H)=3(\not=n)
if and only if

(G,H)
is one of the pairs appered in Theorem
2.9
except for the above two cases,
(B_{n},D_{i}\times B_{n-i})
,
(C_{n},A_{n-1}\times T^{1})
and
(F_{4},C_{3}\times A_{1})
.

(3)

k(G/H)=2
, otherwise (all of the other cases).

Remark 3.4
.

Using the Borel-Hirzebruch result
[
BH58
]
, we have that there are exactly two
Sp(n)
-invariant almost complex structures on
G/H=Sp(n)/Sp(n-1)\times T^{1}(\cong\mathbb{C}P^{2n-1})
.
Since one of them is the standard complex structure on
\mathbb{C}P^{2n-1}
, the
T^{n}
-action on
Sp(n)/Sp(n-1)\times T^{1}
extends to the
T^{2n-1}
-action.
Namely,
Sp(n)/Sp(n-1)\times T^{1}
is isomorphic to
SU(2n)/S(U(2n-1)\times U(1))
; therefore, this is a torus manifold.
Note that, by Theorem
3.3
, the homogeneous GKM manifold of type
(C_{n},C_{n}\times T^{1})
, i.e.,
G/H=Sp(n)/Sp(n-1)\times T^{1}
with the

-action, satisfies
k(G/H)=2
.
This implies that a torus manifold structure on a given homogeneous manifold
G/H
may exist when
k(G/H)\leq 3
.
However, among the spaces
G/H
, since there is no case with
4\leq k(G/H)\leq n-1
by Theorem
3.3
, Problem
1
is affirmatively solved for the maximal rank compact homogeneous spaces
G/H
.

Remark 3.5
.

We also note that, by
[
Ku09
]
, the homogeneous space

can be a torus manifold only if it is isomorphic to

or

.
Therefore, it is easy to check that if

and

is a torus manifold, then its GKM graph must coincide with that of

or

equipped with the non-standard connection (see
[

,
Ku19
]
for the notion of connection).
If

, then the connection is unique by
[

]
.
Moreover, by using
[

]

Consequently, if

We also remark that, in the case

considered in Remark

This phenomenon can occur precisely because

Here, we prove Theorem

The following key lemma significantly reduces the number of cases we need to check.

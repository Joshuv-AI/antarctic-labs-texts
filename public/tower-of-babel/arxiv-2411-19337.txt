The Fractional Poisson Process and Other Limit Point Processes for Rare Events in Infinite Ergodic Theory
Dylan Bansard-Tresse

Abstract
We study the process of suitably normalized successive return times to rare events in the setting of infinite-measure preserving dynamical systems. Specifically, we consider small neighborhoods of points whose measure tends to zero. We obtain two types of results. First, we conduct a detailed study of a class of interval maps with a neutral fixed point and we fully characterize the limit processes for all points, highlighting a trichotomy and the emergence of the fractional (possibly compound) Poisson process. This is the first time that these processes have been explicitly identified in this context. Second, we prove an abstract result that offers an explanation for the emergence of the fractional Poisson process, as the unique fixed point of a functional equation, drawing a parallel with the well-established behavior of the Poisson process in finite-measure preserving dynamical systems.

In the context of finite-measure preserving dynamical systems, the study of non-generic points,
especially periodic points, has gained significant attention in recent years, particularly in the context of

Besides the generic points of the invariant measure under consideration and periodic points, there also

2.1
Preliminaries

Let
(X,\mathscr{B},\mu,T)

(Rare Event Point Process)

For a set

Definition 2.3

(Pointwise dual ergodicity)
.

A CEMPT
(X,\mathscr{B},\mu,T)
is said to be pointwise dual ergodic (PDE) if there
exists a sequence
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

\frac{1}{a_{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\widehat{T}^{k}u\xrightarrow[n\to+\infty]{\mu-\mathrm{a.e.}}\int u\,\mathrm{d}\mu\,,\;\forall u\in L^{1}(\mu).

(2.1)

In this case

For instance, examples include the Boole map

]

[

]

equivalent to the existence of a uniform subset on which the convergence is stronger.
Such sets are of paramount importance in the study of quantitative recurrence.

Given a normalizing sequence

(see

\displaystyle\gamma:s\mapsto\frac{1}{b(1/s)}\,,\;\forall s>0.

If

2.2.2

Definition 2.5

If

Theorem 2.4

\displaystyle N_{B_{n}}^{\gamma}\xRightarrow[n\to+\infty]{\mathcal{L}(\mu)}\operatorname{FPP}_{\alpha}(\Gamma(1+\alpha))\quad\text{and}\quad N_{B_{n}}^{\gamma}\xRightarrow[n\to+\infty]{\mu_{B_{n}}}\operatorname{FPP}_{\alpha}(\Gamma(1+\alpha))\,.

When

Theorem 2.5

The framework closely mirrors that of the finite measure case, with the significant

2.3.2
The indifferent fixed point and its preimages

If

as the neutrality gives a cluster of infinite

Proposition 2.5
.

[

[

Assume

\displaystyle\mu(Q(B_{n}))\,\lx@scalerel@obj{r}_{B_{n}}\xRightarrow[n\to+\infty]{\mu}\mathcal{E}\,,

This highlights the robustness of the exponential law in the finite measure case. The

When

For every sequence of

5
Fractional Poisson processes in infinite ergodic theory: a concluding perspective

In the recent article
[
PS24
]
(
[
Yas18
]
for the first return), the authors showed a convergence for a recurrence REPP in the infinite measure preserving context. They consider a
\mathbb{Z}
-extension over a one-sided subshift of finite type, defined as follows: let
(\Omega,\sigma,\nu)
be a topologically mixing subshift of finite type endowed with a Gibbs measure
\nu
and let
h:\Omega\to\mathbb{Z}
be a centered integrable Hölder observable. On the phase space
X:=\Omega\times\mathbb{Z}
, we define the dynamics
T
by

\displaystyle T:(x,a)\mapsto(\sigma(x),a+h(x)),\quad x\in\Omega,\;a\in\mathbb{Z}.

The
\sigma
-finite measure
\mu:=\nu\otimes\left(\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}\delta_{z}\right)
is preserved by
T
.

Theorem 5.1
.

[
PS24
, Theorem 3.10]

For all
r>0
, let
N_{r}
be the point process defined as follows

\displaystyle N_{r}(x):=\sum_{k\geq 1}\delta_{\nu(B(x,r))^{2}r^{(k)}_{B(x,r)\times\{0\}}}\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{X}.

Then, for the vague convergence,

N_{r}\xRightarrow[r\to 0]{\mu}N\circ L_{0}\quad\text{and}\quad N_{r}\xRightarrow[r\to 0]{\mu_{B(x,r)\times\{0\}}}\widetilde{N}\circ\widetilde{L}_{0},

where
N,\widetilde{N}
are standard Poisson process and
L_{0},\widetilde{L}_{0}
is are local times at
0
of the standard Brownian motion, independent of
N,\widetilde{N}
.

Here, the target set depends on the point
x
considered. However, the techniques developed could have also given the convergence of the hitting REPP for generic points
x
and shrinking balls
B_{n}=B(x,\eta_{n})
for some
\eta_{n}\xrightarrow[n\to+\infty]{}0
. We justify that both results are linked and we think combining the two approaches could provide fruitful insights for future developments.

For simplicity, consider the subshift of finite type on
\Omega:=\{-b,\dots,b\}^{\mathbb{N}}
for some

,
\nu=m^{\otimes\mathbb{N}}
a Bernoulli measure on
\Omega
with
m
a probability of mean
0
and variance

and

the projection onto the first coordinate. Then the
\mathbb{Z}
-extension
(X,T)
of

models the usual random walk on
\mathbb{Z}
and we assume furthermore that it is aperiodic. By inducing on

and using
[
Aar97
, Lemma 3.7.4]
, it is possible to show that the system is PDE. Furthermore, using classical estimates for random walks, we easily an equivalent of the wandering rate
w_{n}(Y):=\mu(Y)\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{Y}}[r_{Y}\wedge n]=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mu(Y\cap\{r_{Y}>n\})
when

.

.

For

, we have

w_{n}(Y)\mathrel{\mathop{\lx@scalerel@obj{\sim}}\limits_{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-6.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle n\to+\infty$}\vss}}}\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\sqrt{n}.

Using classical local limit theorems for random walks, we know that

Remark 5.1

\displaystyle\gamma(\mu(T_{2}^{-1}B_{n}))=\gamma\left(\mu(Q(B_{n}))\right)\mathrel{\mathop{\lx@scalerel@obj{\sim}}\limits_{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-6.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle n\to+\infty$}\vss}}}\left(\frac{1}{\Gamma(1+\alpha)\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\right)^{1/\alpha}\frac{1}{I(\eta_{n})}.

(A.1)

Proof (of Lemma

).

The first equality follows directly from the
T

\mu
. Recall (

) stating that the density

is of the special form

\displaystyle\rho(x)=h_{0}(x)\times\frac{x}{x-T_{1}^{-1}x}\,,\;\forall x\in(0,1],

where
h_{0}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
is a positive continuous function (see for example
[
Tha83
, Lemma 4]
).
Furthermore, recall that
\gamma:s\mapsto 1/b(1/s)
where
b
is the asymptotic inverse of
a
. Since we are studying a special AFN-map we know that (see

\displaystyle a_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\lx@scalerel@obj{\sim}}\limits_{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-6.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle n\to+\infty$}\vss}}}\frac{2(1-\alpha)}{h_{0}(0)\Gamma(1+\alpha)\Gamma(2-\alpha)\alpha^{\alpha}}n^{\alpha}.

Hence, we can choose

\displaystyle\gamma(s)=\left(\frac{2(1-\alpha)}{h_{0}(0)\Gamma(1+\alpha)\Gamma(2-\alpha)\alpha^{\alpha}}\right)^{1/\alpha}s^{1/\alpha}\,,\;\forall s\geq 0.

On the other part, we have

\displaystyle\mu(Q(B_{n}))

\displaystyle=\int_{T_{1}^{-1}\eta_{n}}^{\eta_{n}}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}x\mathrel{\mathop{\lx@scalerel@obj{\sim}}\limits_{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-6.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle n\to+\infty$}\vss}}}h_{0}(0)\int_{T_{1}^{-1}\eta_{n}}^{\eta_{n}}\frac{x}{x-T_{1}^{-1}x}\mathrm{d}x=h_{0}(0)\xi_{n}\frac{\eta_{n}-T_{1}^{-1}\eta_{n}}{\xi_{n}-T_{1}^{-1}\xi_{n}}\quad\text{for some}\;\xi_{n}\in[T_{1}^{-1}\eta_{n},\eta_{n}]

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\lx@scalerel@obj{\sim}}\limits_{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-6.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle n\to+\infty$}\vss}}}h_{0}(0)\xi_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\lx@scalerel@obj{\sim}}\limits_{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-6.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle n\to+\infty$}\vss}}}h_{0}(0)\eta_{n}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\gamma(\mu(Q(B_{n}))\mathrel{\mathop{\lx@scalerel@obj{\sim}}\limits_{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-6.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle n\to+\infty$}\vss}}}\left(\frac{2(1-\alpha)}{\Gamma(1+\alpha)\Gamma(2-\alpha)\alpha^{\alpha}}\right)^{1/\alpha}\eta_{n}^{1/\alpha}\,.

(A.2)

On the other side, we have

\displaystyle I(\eta_{n})=\int_{\eta_{n}}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{x-T_{1}^{-1}x}\mathrel{\mathop{\lx@scalerel@obj{\sim}}\limits_{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-6.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle n\to+\infty$}\vss}}}\int_{\eta_{n}}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{2^{1/\alpha}x^{1/\alpha+1}}=\frac{\alpha}{2^{1/\alpha}}\left(\frac{1}{\eta_{n}^{1/\alpha}}-1\right)\mathrel{\mathop{\lx@scalerel@obj{\sim}}\limits_{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-6.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle n\to+\infty$}\vss}}}\frac{\alpha}{2^{1/\alpha}\eta_{n}^{1/\alpha}},

(A.3)

where we use the fact that
x-T_{1}^{-1}x\mathrel{\mathop{\lx@scalerel@obj{\sim}}\limits_{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-6.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle x\to 0^{+}$}\vss}}}2^{1/\alpha}x^{1/\alpha+1}
since
T_{1}^{-1}x=x-2^{1/\alpha}x^{1/\alpha+1}+o(x^{1/\alpha})
. Putting together (
A.2
) and (
A.3
) gives (

) using that
\Gamma(2-\alpha)=(1-\alpha)\Gamma(1-\alpha)
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
2.8

Recall that
B_{n}=[0,c_{n}]
and let
E_{n}:=T_{2}^{-1}B_{n}=Y\cap\{r_{Y}>n\}=[1/2,\delta_{n}]
. Recall that we have chosen the renormalization of
\mu
so that
\mu(Y)=1
. We know by
[
Zwe00
, Theorem 4]
that, in the barely infinite case, we have
a_{n}\sim\frac{2n}{h_{0}(0)\log(n)}
and thus
b_{n}:=\frac{h_{0}(0)}{2}n\log(n)
is an asymptotic inverse of
(a_{n})_{n\geq 0}
(recall that
\gamma(s):=b(s^{-1})^{-1}
).

Fix some
p\geq 0
and assume
n\geq p
(this is no issue as we are interested by the limit on
n
). Let
\widetilde{r}^{(p)}_{B_{n}}
be the random variable defined as follows:

\displaystyle\widetilde{r}^{(p)}_{B_{n}}:=h_{E_{n}}\circ T_{E_{p}}\mathbf{1}_{E_{n}}\,,

where
h_{A}(x):=\inf\{n\geq 0\;|\;T^{n}(x)\in A\}
.

Lemma A.2
.

There exist a constant
C_{p}
, with
C_{p}\xrightarrow[p\to+\infty]{}1
such that for all
n\geq p
,

\displaystyle C_{p}^{-1}\mu(E_{n})\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{Y}}[\widetilde{r}^{(p)}_{B_{n}}]}{\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{E_{p}}}[r_{B_{n}}]}\leq C_{p}\mu(E_{n})\,.

Proof (of Lemma
A.2
).

As usual, we use the bounded distortion result Corollary
4.5
and work branch by branch. Let

\displaystyle A_{n}:=\{[0a_{0}^{j-1}b_{0}^{k}],\;a_{0}>n,\exists!i\;a_{i}=p,\;b_{0}=0,\;b_{1}>p,\;b_{k-1}=0,\;b_{k}>n,\;\forall 0\leq\ell\leq k-1\;b_{k}\leq n\}

be a partition in branches of
\mathbf{1}_{E_{n}}
for
\widetilde{r}_{B_{n}}^{(p)}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{Y}}[\widetilde{r}^{(p)}_{B_{n}}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{[0a_{0}^{j-1}b_{0}^{k}]\in A_{n}}(k-1)\mu[0a_{0}^{j-1}b_{0}^{k}]

\displaystyle\leq(1+C\operatorname{\mathrm{diam}}(E_{p}))\sum_{[0a_{0}^{j-1}b_{0}^{k}]\in A_{n}}(k-1)\frac{\mu[0a_{0}^{j-1}0(>p)]\mu[b_{0}^{k}]}{\mu[0(>p)]}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}_{p}\frac{\mu(E_{n})}{\mu(E_{p})}\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{Y}}[h_{E_{n}}\mathbf{1}_{E_{p}}]

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}_{p}\frac{\mu(E_{n})}{\mu(E_{p})}\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{Y}}[(r_{B_{n}}-1)\mathbf{1}_{E_{p}}]

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\frac{\mu(E_{n})}{\mu(E_{p})}\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{Y}}[r_{B_{n}}\mathbf{1}_{E_{p}}].

The lower bound is obtained similarly. Since
\operatorname{\mathrm{diam}}(E_{p})\xrightarrow[p\to+\infty]{}
, we have
C_{p}\xrightarrow[p\to+\infty]{}1
.
∎

Lemma A.3
.

For a fixed
p\geq 0
, we have the following asymptotic result:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{Y}}[\widetilde{r}_{B_{n}}^{(p)}]\sim\int(r_{Y}\wedge n)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{Y}=w_{n}(Y).

Proof (of Lemma
A.3
).

We look at the Birkhoff’s sums for the induced map on
Y
and consider the map
\ell_{n}:=r_{Y}\mathbf{1}_{E_{n}^{c}}=r_{Y}\mathbf{1}_{\{r_{Y}\leq n\}}
. Let
j\geq 1
,
x\in Y
and assume for simplicity that
T_{Y}^{j}x\in E_{n}
. Write
k(j):=\sup\{i\geq 0\;|\;r_{E_{n}}^{Y,(i)}<j\}
(in particular, with the hypothesis we made, we have
j=r_{E_{n}}^{Y,(k(i)+1)}
). We have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{j}S^{Y}_{j}\ell_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{j}\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}r_{Y}(T_{Y}^{k}x)\mathbf{1}_{E_{n}^{c}}(T_{Y}^{k}x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{j}\sum_{k=1}^{k(j)}\sum_{i=1}^{r^{Y}_{E_{n}}\circ T_{E_{n}}^{k}(x)-1}r_{Y}(T_{Y}^{i}\circ T_{E_{n}}^{k}x)+\frac{1}{j}h_{E_{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{j}\sum_{k=1}^{k(j)}\left(\sum_{i=r^{Y}_{E_{p}}\circ T_{E_{n}}^{k}(x)}^{r^{Y}_{E_{n}}\circ T_{E_{n}}^{k}(x)-1}r_{Y}(T_{Y}^{i}\circ T_{E_{n}}^{k}x)+\sum_{i=1}^{r^{Y}_{E_{p}}\circ T_{E_{n}}^{k}(x)-1}r_{Y}(T_{Y}^{i}\circ T_{E_{n}}^{k}x)\right)+\frac{1}{j}h_{E_{n}}.

On one side, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{j}\sum_{k=1}^{k(j)}\sum_{i=1}^{r^{Y}_{E_{p}}\circ T_{E_{n}}^{k}(x)-1}r_{Y}(T_{Y}^{i}\circ T_{E_{n}}^{k}x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{j}\sum_{k=1}^{k(j)}h_{E_{p}}\circ T_{Y}\circ T_{E_{n}}^{k}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{j}\sum_{k=1}^{j-1}\left(\mathbf{1}_{E_{n}}h_{E_{p}}\circ T_{Y}\right)\circ T_{Y}^{k}(x)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{j}S_{j}^{Y}\left(\mathbf{1}_{E_{n}}h_{E_{p}}\circ T_{Y}\right)\xrightarrow[j\to+\infty]{\mu_{Y}\;a.e.}\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{Y}}[\mathbf{1}_{E_{n}}h_{E_{p}}\circ T_{Y}]

On the other side, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{j}\sum_{k=1}^{k(j)}\sum_{i=r^{Y}_{E_{p}}\circ T_{E_{n}}^{k}(x)}^{r^{Y}_{E_{n}}\circ T_{E_{n}}^{k}(x)-1}r_{Y}\circ T_{Y}^{i}\circ T_{E_{n}}^{k}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{j}\sum_{k=1}^{k(j)}h_{E_{n}}\circ T_{E_{p}}\circ T_{E_{n}}^{k}(x)\quad\text{using $E_{n}\subset E_{p}$}

The
h_{E_{n}}
instead of
r_{E_{n}}
comes from the possibility that the sum is empty if
r_{E_{p}}^{Y}=r_{E_{n}}^{Y}
and the
0
we get is exactly
h_{E_{n}}
in that case.

For
\widetilde{r}_{B_{n}}^{(p)}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{j}S^{Y}_{j}\widetilde{r}_{B_{n}}^{(p)}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{j}\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\widetilde{r}_{B_{n}}^{(p)}\circ T_{Y}^{k}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{j}\sum_{k=1}^{k(j)}h_{E_{n}}\circ T_{E_{p}}\circ T_{E_{n}}^{k}(x)\qquad\text{by definition of $\widetilde{r}_{B_{n}}^{(p)}$.}

Putting everything together, we get by Birkhoff’s theorem,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{Y}}[\widetilde{r}_{B_{n}}^{(p)}]=\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{Y}}[\ell_{n}]-\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{Y}}[\mathbf{1}_{E_{n}}h_{E_{p}}\circ T_{Y}].

In particular, when
n
goes to
+\infty
, we have the asymptotic equivalence

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{Y}}\big[\widetilde{r}_{B_{n}}^{(p)}\big]\mathrel{\mathop{\lx@scalerel@obj{\sim}}\limits_{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-6.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle n\to+\infty$}\vss}}}\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{Y}}[\ell_{n}].

Finally, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{Y}}[\ell_{n}]=\int_{Y}r_{Y}\mathbf{1}_{\{r_{Y}\leq n\}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{Y}=w_{n}(Y)-n\mu_{Y}(r_{Y}>n).

However, in the particular barely infinite case, we have
\mu_{Y}(r_{Y}>n)\asymp 1/n
whereas
w_{n}(Y)\asymp\log(n)
. Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{Y}}[\ell_{n}]\mathrel{\mathop{\lx@scalerel@obj{\sim}}\limits_{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-6.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle n\to+\infty$}\vss}}}w_{n}(Y).

This yields the wanted result
\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{Y}}\big[\widetilde{r}_{B_{n}}^{(p)}\big]\mathrel{\mathop{\lx@scalerel@obj{\sim}}\limits_{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-6.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle n\to+\infty$}\vss}}}w_{n}(Y)
.
∎

Remark A.1
.

In fact, in Lemma
A.3
, we actually showed that

, whenever

. In the case

, both terms are of same order. Since we also have

\displaystyle w_{n}(Y)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mu(Y\cap\{r_{Y}>k\})

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{Y}}[r_{B_{n}}]\mathrel{\mathop{\lx@scalerel@obj{\sim}}\limits_{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-6.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle n\to+\infty$}\vss}}}\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}n,

which can also be proven with Theorem
2.6
which implies

\frac{1}{n}r_{B_{n}}\xRightarrow[n\to+\infty]{\mu_{Y}}\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}

(A.4)

In particular, since

, it also gives

.

Introduction

The theory of Toeplitz operators on several Hilbert function spaces, for example, the Hardy space and the weighted Bergman spaces, is a well established subject. It has applications to and connection with many other areas, for example, function theory, prediction theory, singular integral equations, C* algebras and other areas of operator theory. Brown and Halmos had shown that a bounded operator
A
acting on the Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
of the open unit disc
\mathbb{D},
is a Toeplitz operator
T_{g}
induced by a unique symbol
g\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
if and only if
A
satisfies the operator identity
T_{\bar{z}}AT_{z}=A,
see
[
4
, Theorem 6]
. It was well known that the Toeplitz operators with
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
symbols on the classical Bergman spaces can not be characterized by such Brown Halmos type operator identity, see
[
11
]
. Nevertheless Louhichi and Olofsson characterized the Toeplitz operators induced by bounded harmonic symbols on the weighted Bergman spaces through an operator identity, see
[
19
]
. Later these results has been extended to characterize Toeplitz operators with pluriharmonic symbol on the analytic Besov spaces on the unit ball in
\mathbb{C}^{m},
see
[
12
]
.

Toeplitz operators induced by various class of symbols on the Dirichlet space have been studied intensively in recent times, see for example
[
22
,
5
,
6
,
9
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
20
,
23
,
24
,
25
]
and the references therein. In this article we study the Toeplitz operators on the Dirichlet space induced by a class of symbols
\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}})
and find that the operator identity
T_{\bar{z}}AT_{z}=A
completely characterize these class of Toeplitz operators among the set of all bounded operators on the Dirichlet space.

We start with some basic notations, a few definitions and some known results about Toeplitz operators on the Dirichlet space in order to introduce the symbol class
\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}})
and present our results. Let
dA=\frac{1}{\pi}dxdy
be the normalized Lebesgue area measure on
\mathbb{D}.
Let
L^{2}(\mathbb{D})
denotes the space of all complex valued measurable functions on
\mathbb{D}
which are square integrable with respect to the Lebesgue area measure
dA
and
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
be the algebra of all essentially bounded measurable functions on
\mathbb{D}.
The Sobolev space
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{D})
consists of all locally integrable complex valued functions
f
on
\mathbb{D}
for which
f\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{D}\right)
and furthermore, the weak derivatives
\frac{\partial f}{\partial z}
and
\frac{\partial f}{\partial\bar{z}}
belong to
L^{2}(\mathbb{D}).
It is well known that
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{D})
is a Hilbert space with respect to the inner product given by

\displaystyle\Big\langle f,g\Big\rangle_{1,2}=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{D}}f\,dA\Big)\Big(\overline{\int_{\mathbb{D}}g\,dA}\Big)+\Big\langle\frac{\partial f}{\partial z},\frac{\partial g}{\partial z}\Big\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}+\Big\langle\frac{\partial f}{\partial\bar{z}},\frac{\partial g}{\partial\bar{z}}\Big\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})},

see
[
1
, Chapter III]
for more results on Sobolev space. The Dirichlet space
\mathcal{D}_{0}
is the closed subspace of all holomorphic functions
f\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{D})
with
f(0)=0.
It is well known that
\mathcal{D}_{0}
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space with the kernel function given by

\displaystyle R_{w}(z)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}\bar{w}^{k}}{k}=\ln\frac{1}{(1-z\bar{w})},\,\,\,z,w\in\mathbb{D}.

For any
\varphi\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{D}),
we have that

\varphi|_{{}_{\mathbb{T}}}(e^{i\theta}):=\lim\limits_{r\to 1-}\varphi(re^{i\theta})

exists for almost all
\theta\in[0,2\pi],
see
[
6
, Proposition 1]
. Moreover
\varphi|_{{}_{\mathbb{T}}}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
and
\Phi=P[\varphi|_{{}_{\mathbb{T}}}],
the Poisson extension of
\varphi|_{{}_{\mathbb{T}}}
on
\mathbb{D},
belongs to
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{D}),
see
[
6
, Theorem 1]
.
We let
\Lambda_{0}
be the subspace of
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{D})
given by

\displaystyle\Lambda_{0}:=\{f\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{D}):f|_{{}_{\mathbb{T}}}=0\}.

Note that
\varphi-\Phi\in\Lambda_{0}
and
\varphi=(\varphi-\Phi)+\Phi
is an orthogonal decomposition of
\phi
in
\Lambda_{0}
and
\Lambda_{0}^{\perp}
in
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{D}).
Moreover the space
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{D})
decomposes as the following way

\displaystyle W^{1,2}(\mathbb{D})=\Lambda_{0}\oplus\overline{\mathcal{D}_{0}}\oplus\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathcal{D}_{0},

see
[
6
, Theorem 1]
. Let
P_{\mathcal{D}_{0}}
be the orthogonal projection from
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{D})
onto
\mathcal{D}_{0}.
Using the reproducing property of the kernel function
R_{w},
one can see that
P_{\mathcal{D}_{0}}
can be represented by the following integral formula:

(1.1)

\displaystyle(P_{\mathcal{D}_{0}}f)(w)=\Big\langle f,R_{w}\Big\rangle_{1,2}=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\Big(\frac{\partial f(z)}{\partial z}\Big)\Big(\overline{\frac{\partial R_{w}(z)}{\partial z}}\Big)\,dA(z)\Big),\,\,w\in\mathbb{D},

for every
f\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{D}).

For a function
\varphi\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{D}),
the Toeplitz operator
T_{\varphi}
with symbol
\varphi,
is a densely defined operator on
\mathcal{D}_{0}
given by

(1.2)

\displaystyle T_{\varphi}(f)=P_{\mathcal{D}_{0}}(\varphi f),

for all
f\in
Dom
(T_{\varphi})=\{g\in{\mathcal{D}_{0}}:\varphi g\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{D})\}.

In view of (
1.1
), note that for any
\varphi\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{D})
and
f\in
Dom
(T_{\varphi}),
we have

(1.3)

\displaystyle(T_{\varphi}(f))(w)=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left(\frac{\partial\left(\varphi f\right)}{\partial z}\right)\overline{\left(\frac{\partial R_{w}(z)}{\partial z}\right)}\,dA(z),\qquad w\in\mathbb{D}.

It is known that for any symbol
\varphi\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{D}),
the Toeplitz operator
T_{\varphi}
defines a bounded operator on
\mathcal{D}_{0}
if and only if
\Phi=P[\varphi|_{{}_{\mathbb{T}}}],
the Poisson extension of
\varphi|_{{}_{\mathbb{T}}}
on
\mathbb{D},
belongs
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
and
\left\lvert\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial z}\right\rvert^{2}dA
is a
Carleson measure
for
\mathcal{D}_{0}
i.e. there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left\lvert f\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial z}\right\rvert^{2}dA(z)\leqslant C\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathcal{D}_{0}}^{2},\,\,\,f\in\mathcal{D}_{0},

see
[
20
, Theorem 2.2]
. It is also known that for any symbol
\varphi\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{D}),
if the Toeplitz operator
T_{\varphi}
defines a bounded operator on
\mathcal{D}_{0},
then it turns out that the operator
T_{\varphi}
is determined by the boundary function
\varphi|_{{}_{\mathbb{T}}}
and
T_{\varphi}=T_{\Phi},
see
[
20
, Theorem 2.2]
. This motivates us to consider the symbol class
\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}})
defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}}):=\Big\{\psi\in h^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}):\left\lvert\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial z}\right\rvert^{2}dA\,\,\mbox{is a Carleson measure for\,\,}\mathcal{D}_{0}\Big\},

where
h^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
denotes the space of all complex valued bounded harmonic functions on
\mathbb{D}.
We would like to point out that it is not the case that
\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}})\subseteq W^{1,2}\left(\mathbb{D}\right)
, see Remark
2.4
of this article. Following
[
20
, Sec 2.2]
, we use the weaker expression in (
1.3
) to define a Toeplitz operator
T_{\psi}
for
\psi\in\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}}).
The Toeplitz operator
T_{\psi}
with symbol
\psi
in
\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}})
is defined by

(T_{\psi}(f))(w):=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left(\frac{\partial\left(\psi f\right)}{\partial z}\right)\overline{\left(\frac{\partial R_{w}(z)}{\partial z}\right)}\,dA(z),\qquad w\in\mathbb{D},f\in\mathcal{D}_{0}.

where
R_{w}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}\bar{w}^{n}}{n}
is the reproducing kernel of
\mathcal{D}_{0}
at the point
w\in\mathbb{D}
. In this article we first show that
T_{\psi}
defines a bounded operator on
\mathcal{D}_{0}
for every
\psi\in\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}}).
We find that the operator
T_{\psi}
on the Dirichlet space
\mathcal{D}_{0}
satisfies the Brown Halmos type identity
T_{\bar{z}}T_{\psi}T_{z}=T_{\psi}
for every
\psi\in\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}}),
see Corollary
3.2
. The main goal of this article is to obtain a characterization the Toeplitz operators
\{T_{\psi}:\psi\in\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}})\}
through the Brown-Halmos type operator identity.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{D}_{0})
satisfies the identity
T_{\bar{z}}TT_{z}=T
, then there exists a unique
\psi\in\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}})
such that
T=T_{\psi}.

We would like to point out that although the Toeplitz operator
T_{\bar{z}}
coincides with
T_{z}^{*},
the adjoint of
T_{z},
on the Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
but on the Dirichlet space
\mathcal{D}_{0}
,
T_{\bar{z}}\neq T_{z}^{*}.
As a corollary to Theorem
1.1
, we obtain that the subspace
\{T_{\psi}:\psi\in\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}})\}
is a closed subspace in the weak operator topology (W.O.T) of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{D}_{0}).
This suggests that the symbol class
\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}})\}
is the largest class (in some sense) for the representation of the bounded Toeplitz operators on the Dirichlet space
{\mathcal{D}_{0}}
. We further find that

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}})=\mathcal{M}({\mathcal{D}_{0}})+\overline{H^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{D})},

where
\mathcal{M}({\mathcal{D}_{0}})
denotes the multiplier algebra of the Dirichlet space
{\mathcal{D}_{0}}
and
\overline{H^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{D})}
is given by
\overline{H^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{D})}=\{\bar{f}:f\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}),f(0)=0\}
, see Proposition
2.9
.

Investigating the compactness of the Toeplitz operators on function spaces has been an active area of research, for example see
[
15
,
20
]
for the characterizations of the compact Toeplitz operators
T_{\varphi}
on the Dirichlet space
{\mathcal{D}_{0}}
for
\varphi
coming from various symbol class. We find that the same characterization remains valid for the symbol class
\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}}),
as expected. In particular we find that for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}}),
the operator
T_{\varphi}
is compact if and only if
\varphi=0,
see Proposition
2.8
.

In this paper we also discuss several algebraic properties of the class of Toeplitz operators
\{T_{\varphi}:\varphi\in\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}})\}
on the Dirichlet space
{\mathcal{D}_{0}}.
The corresponding problem on the Toeplitz operators acting on the Hardy, weighted Bergman spaces or harmonic Bergman spaces has been well studied by several authors, see for example
[
3
,
4
,
7
,
8
]
and references therein. For a symbol
\varphi\in\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}}),
the matrix representation of the Toeplitz operator
T_{\varphi}
with respect to the orthogonal basis
\{z^{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
of
{\mathcal{D}_{0}}
turns out to be the
Toeplitz matrix

(\!(\hat{\varphi}(i-j))\!)_{i,j=1}^{\infty},
where the symbol
\varphi
is given by

\varphi(z)=\sum_{k\geqslant 0}\hat{\varphi}(k)z^{k}+\sum_{k>0}\hat{\varphi}(-k)\overline{z}^{k},\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Consequently, characterizations of several algebraic properties of the Toeplitz operators
\{T_{\varphi}:\varphi\in\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}})\}
remains similar to that of the Toeplitz operators
\{T_{\varphi}:\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})\}
on the Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}).
In
[
15
]
, the author studied various algebraic properties for the class of Toeplitz operators
\{T_{\varphi}:\varphi\in\Omega\cap h(\mathbb{D})\},
where the symbol class
\Omega
is given by
\Omega=\{\varphi\in\mathscr{C}^{1}(\mathbb{D}):\varphi,\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial z},\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial\bar{z}}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})\}
and
h(\mathbb{D})
denotes the set of all complex valued harmonic functions on
\mathbb{D}
. It is straightforward to see that
\Omega\cap h(\mathbb{D})\subseteq\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}}).
We find that each of the characterizations of the algebraic properties for the symbol class
\Omega\cap h(\mathbb{D})
as mentioned in
[
15
]
, remains valid for the larger symbol class
\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}})
as well, see Section
4
for more detailed discussion.

The organization of the paper is as follows. In Section 2, we study the boundedness of the Toeplitz operators
\{T_{\varphi}:\varphi\in\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}})\}
on the Dirichlet space
{\mathcal{D}_{0}}
and obtain the decomposition of
\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}})
into sum of
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{D}_{0})
and
\overline{H^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{D})}.

In Section 3, we present the main result of this article, namely, we show that the operator identity
T_{\bar{z}}AT_{z}=A
characterize the Toeplitz operators
\{T_{\varphi}:\varphi\in\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}})\}
in
\mathcal{B}({\mathcal{D}_{0}}).
In Section 4, we discuss various characterizations of algebraic properties of these Toeplitz operators.

2.
Toeplitz Operators with symbols in
\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}})

Let
\mathcal{A}^{2}
denotes the Bergman space of the open unit disc
\mathbb{D}
and
K^{B}_{w}(z)=\frac{1}{(1-\bar{w}z)^{2}},
denotes the reproducing kernel of
{\mathcal{A}^{2}}
at the point
w\in\mathbb{D}
so that

\displaystyle\left\langle g,K^{B}_{w}\right\rangle_{{\mathcal{A}^{2}}}=g(w),\,\,g\in\mathcal{A}^{2},\,w\in\mathbb{D}.

We use the symbol
P_{{\mathcal{A}^{2}}}
to denote the orthogonal projection from
L^{2}(\mathbb{D})
onto
{\mathcal{A}^{2}}.
Using the reproducing property, we have

\displaystyle\left(P_{{\mathcal{A}^{2}}}f\right)(w)=\left\langle f,K^{B}_{w}\right\rangle_{{\mathcal{A}^{2}}},\qquad f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{D}),w\in\mathbb{D}.

Let
R_{w}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}\bar{w}^{n}}{n}=\ln\frac{1}{(1-\bar{w}z)},
denotes the reproducing kernel of the Dirichlet space
\mathcal{D}_{0}
at the point
w\in\mathbb{D}
so that

\displaystyle\left\langle f,R_{w}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{D}_{0}}=f(w),\,\,f\in\mathcal{D}_{0},\,w\in\mathbb{D}.

We would like to note down an important identity involving the reproducing kernel function
R_{w}
and
K^{B}_{w},
as this will be useful for us at later stage.

(2.1)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\overline{\left(\frac{\partial R_{w}(z)}{\partial z}\right)}=\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\left(\frac{w}{1-w\bar{z}}\right)=\frac{1}{(1-w\bar{z})^{2}}=\overline{K^{B}_{w}(z)},\,\,w\in\mathbb{D}.

Now we introduce a densely defined Toeplitz operator induced by a symbol in the class
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{D}_{0}})
which is defined by

\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{D}_{0}}):=\Big\{\varphi\in h^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}):\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial z}\in\mathcal{A}^{2}\Big\},

where
h^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
denotes the set of all bounded harmonic functions on unit disc
\mathbb{D}.
For
\varphi\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{D}_{0}})
, we consider the densely defined linear operator
T_{\varphi}
on
\mathcal{D}_{0}
given by

(T_{\varphi}(f))(w):=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left(\frac{\partial\left(\varphi f\right)}{\partial z}\right)\overline{\left(\frac{\partial R_{w}(z)}{\partial z}\right)}\,dA(z),\qquad w\in\mathbb{D},\,f\in\mbox{dom}(T_{\varphi}),

where dom
(T_{\varphi})=\{f\in\mathcal{D}_{0}:T_{\varphi}(f)\in\mathcal{D}_{0}\}.
Suppose
\varphi\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{D}_{0}}).
For any
w\in\mathbb{D}
and
f\in
span
\{z^{n}:n\geqslant 1\},
note that

\displaystyle\left(T_{\varphi}(f)\right)^{\prime}(w)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left(\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial z}f+f^{\prime}\varphi\right)\left(\frac{w}{1-w\bar{z}}\right)dA(z)\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}f(z)\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial z}(z)\overline{K_{w}^{B}(z)}+\int_{\mathbb{D}}f^{\prime}(z)\varphi(z)\overline{K_{w}^{B}(z)}\,dA(z)

\displaystyle=f(w)\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial z}(w)+P_{{\mathcal{A}^{2}}}(\varphi f^{\prime})(w).

Thus we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle\left(T_{\varphi}(f)\right)^{\prime}=\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial z}f+P_{{\mathcal{A}^{2}}}(\varphi f^{\prime}),\,\,\mbox{for every}\,\,f\in\,\mbox{span}\{z^{n}:n\geqslant 1\}.

This gives us that span
\{z^{n}:n\geqslant 1\}\subseteq\mbox{dom}(T_{\varphi})
for any
\varphi\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{D}_{0}}).
Thus
T_{\varphi}
is densely defined operator on
\mathcal{D}_{0}
for every
\varphi\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{D}_{0}}).
Further note that for any
\varphi\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{D}_{0}}),
we have

(2.3)

\displaystyle\left\langle T_{\varphi}(f),g\right\rangle_{\mathcal{D}_{0}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\frac{\partial(\varphi f)}{\partial z},g^{\prime}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})},\qquad f,g\in\,\mbox{span}\{z^{n}:n\geqslant 1\}.

Now we provide a characterization for when
T_{\varphi}
defines a bounded operator on
\mathcal{D}_{0}.
This characterization is already known when
\varphi
is in the symbol class
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{D}),
see
[
20
, Theorem 2.2]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\varphi\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{D}_{0}}).
Then the following are equivalent:

(a)

T_{\varphi}
extends to a bounded operator on
\mathcal{D}_{0}
.

(b)

The measure
\left\lvert\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial z}\right\rvert^{2}dA
is a Carleson measure for
\mathcal{D}_{0},
that is, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left\lvert f\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial z}\right\rvert^{2}dA\leqslant C\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathcal{D}_{0}}^{2},\,\,\,f\in\mathcal{D}_{0}.

Proof.

First assume that
T_{\varphi}
extends to a bounded operator on
\mathcal{D}_{0}
. In view of the equation (
2.2
) it follows that for any
f\in\mbox{span}\{z^{n}:n\geqslant 1\},

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left\lvert\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial z}f\right\rvert^{2}dA

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left\lvert\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial z}f+P_{{\mathcal{A}^{2}}}(\varphi f^{\prime})-P_{{\mathcal{A}^{2}}}(\varphi f^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2}dA(z)

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left\lvert\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial z}f+P_{{\mathcal{A}^{2}}}(\varphi f^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2}dA+2\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left\lvert P_{{\mathcal{A}^{2}}}(\varphi f^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2}dA(z)

\displaystyle=2\left\lVert(T_{\varphi}f)^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{A}^{2}}^{2}+2\left\lVert P_{{\mathcal{A}^{2}}}(\varphi f^{\prime})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{A}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\left\lVert T_{\varphi}f\right\rVert_{\mathcal{D}_{0}}^{2}+2\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2}\left\lVert f^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{A}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\left(\left\lVert T_{\varphi}\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2}\right)\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathcal{D}_{0}}^{2}.

Note that the point evaluation at every point
w\in\mathbb{D},
is a continuous linear functional on
\mathcal{D}_{0}
and span
\{z^{n}:n\geqslant 1\}
is a dense subset in
\mathcal{D}_{0}.
So, an application of Fatou’s lemma will give us

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left\lvert f\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial z}\right\rvert^{2}dA\leqslant 2\left(\left\lVert T_{\varphi}\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2}\right)\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathcal{D}_{0}}^{2},\,\,\,f\in\mathcal{D}_{0}.

Now assume that there exists a
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left\lvert f\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial z}\right\rvert^{2}dA\leqslant C\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathcal{D}_{0}}^{2},\,\,\,f\in\mathcal{D}_{0}.

Note that for every
f\in
span
\{z^{n}:n\geqslant 1\},
we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert T_{\varphi}f\right\rVert_{\mathcal{D}_{0}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left\lvert(T_{\varphi}f)^{\prime}(w)\right\rvert^{2}dA(w)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left\lvert f(w)\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial z}(w)+P_{{\mathcal{A}^{2}}}(\varphi f^{\prime})(w)\right\rvert^{2}dA(w)

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\int_{\mathbb{D}}\Big(\left\lvert f(w)\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial z}(w)\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert P_{{\mathcal{A}^{2}}}(\varphi f^{\prime})(w)\right\rvert^{2}\Big)dA(w)

\displaystyle\leqslant 2C\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathcal{D}_{0}}^{2}+2\left\lVert P_{{\mathcal{A}^{2}}}(\varphi f^{\prime})\right\rVert_{{\mathcal{A}^{2}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2C\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathcal{D}_{0}}^{2}+2\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2}\left\lVert f^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{A}^{2}}^{2}

(2.4)

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\left(C+\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2}\right)\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathcal{D}_{0}}^{2}

It is straightforward to see that for any
w\in{\mathcal{D}_{0}}
the map
f\mapsto f^{\prime}(w)
is also bounded linear functional on
\mathcal{D}_{0}.
Again using density of span
\{z^{n}:n\geqslant 1\}
in
\mathcal{D}_{0}
and an application of Fatou’s lemma will give us

\displaystyle\left\lVert T_{\varphi}f\right\rVert_{\mathcal{D}_{0}}^{2}\leqslant 2\left(C+\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2}\right)\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathcal{D}_{0}}^{2},\,\,\,f\in\mathcal{D}_{0},

which shows that
T_{\varphi}
defines a bounded operator on
\mathcal{D}_{0}.
This completes the proof.
∎

Recall that the symbol class
{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{D}_{0})
is defined as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{D}_{0})=\Big\{\varphi\in h^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}):\left\lvert\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial z}\right\rvert^{2}dA\,\,\mbox{is a Carleson measure for\,\,}\mathcal{D}_{0}\Big\}.

Note that for any
\varphi\in{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{D}_{0}),
we have that
z\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial z}\in{\mathcal{A}^{2}}.
It follows that
\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial z}\in{\mathcal{A}^{2}}
for every
\varphi\in{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{D}_{0}).
Thus in view of Theorem
2.1
the following corollary is immediate.

Corollary 2.2
.

{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{D}_{0})=\{\varphi\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{D}_{0}}):T_{\varphi}
defines a bounded operator on
\mathcal{D}_{0}\}.

Remark 2.3
.

It turns out that
{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{D}_{0})\subsetneq\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{D}_{0}}).
It is well known that
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{D}_{0})\subsetneq\left(\mathcal{D}_{0}\cap H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})\right).
Take any
g\in\left(\mathcal{D}_{0}\cap H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})\right)\setminus\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{D}_{0}).
Then
g\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{D}_{0}})\setminus{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{D}_{0}).

Remark 2.4
.

If
\varphi
is a harmonic function in
W^{1,2}\left(\mathbb{D}\right)
such that
T_{\varphi}
defines a bounded operator on
\mathcal{D}_{0},
then
\varphi\in{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{D}_{0}),
see
[
20
, Theorem 2.2]
. Nevertheless it is not the case that
{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{D}_{0})\subseteq W^{1,2}\left(\mathbb{D}\right)
. To see this, take any
f\in H^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{D}\right)
such that
\left\lVert\frac{\partial f}{\partial z}\right\rVert_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{D}\right)}=\infty
(for instance, one can take
f
to be some infinite Blaschke product) and consider
\Psi=\overline{f}
. Then
\Psi\in{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{D}_{0})
because
\left\lvert\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial z}\right\rvert^{2}dA(z)
is the zero measure and
\Psi
is a bounded harmonic function. But it is clear that
\Psi\not\in W^{1,2}\left(\mathbb{D}\right)
because
\left\lVert\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial\bar{z}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{D}\right)}=\infty.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\varphi
be a function in
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{D}_{0}})
and suppose that

\varphi(z)=\sum_{k\geqslant 0}\hat{\varphi}(k)z^{k}+\sum_{k>0}\hat{\varphi}(-k)\overline{z}^{k},\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Then we have that

T_{\varphi}(z^{n})=\sum_{k\geqslant 1}\hat{\varphi}\left(k-n\right)z^{k},\qquad n\geqslant 1.

Consequently, it follows that the matrix representation of
T_{\varphi}
with respect to the orthogonal basis
\left\{z^{j}:j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1}\right\}
of
\mathcal{D}_{0}
is of the following Toeplitz form:

\left[T_{\varphi}\right]=\begin{pmatrix}\hat{\varphi}\left(0\right)&\hat{\varphi}\left(-1\right)&\hat{\varphi}\left(-2\right)&\cdots\\
\hat{\varphi}\left(1\right)&\hat{\varphi}\left(0\right)&\hat{\varphi}\left(-1\right)&\cdots\\
\hat{\varphi}\left(2\right)&\hat{\varphi}\left(1\right)&\hat{\varphi}\left(0\right)&\ddots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\ddots\end{pmatrix}.

Proof.

First note that as
\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial z}\in{\mathcal{A}^{2}},
we have that

(2.5)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}k|\hat{\varphi}(k)|^{2}<\infty.

For any
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
n\geqslant 1
, we get

\varphi(z)z^{n}=\sum_{k\geqslant 0}\hat{\varphi}(k)z^{n+k}+\sum_{k>0}\hat{\varphi}(-k)\overline{z}^{k}z^{n}.

and consequently, we have

\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\left(\varphi(z)z^{n}\right)=\sum_{k\geqslant 0}(n+k)\hat{\varphi}(k)z^{n+k-1}+\sum_{k>0}n\hat{\varphi}(-k)\overline{z}^{k}z^{n-1}.

Now note that for each
w\in\mathbb{D},

\displaystyle\left\langle\sum_{k\geqslant 0}(n+k)\hat{\varphi}(k)z^{n+k-1},\sum_{j\geqslant 0}\overline{w}^{j+1}z^{j}\right\rangle_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{D}\right)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k\geqslant 0}\left(n+k\right)\hat{\varphi}(k)w^{j+1}\left\langle z^{n+k-1},z^{j}\right\rangle_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{D}\right)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k\geqslant 0}\hat{\varphi}\left(k\right)\delta_{n+k-1,j}w^{j+1},

and we have

\displaystyle\left\langle\sum_{k>0}n\hat{\varphi}(-k)\overline{z}^{k}z^{n-1},\sum_{j\geqslant 0}\overline{w}^{j+1}z^{j}\right\rangle_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{D}\right)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\geqslant 0,k>0}n\hat{\varphi}\left(-k\right)w^{j+1}\left\langle\overline{z}^{k}z^{n-1},z^{j}\right\rangle_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{D}\right)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\geqslant 0,k>0}\hat{\varphi}\left(-k\right)\delta_{n-1,j+k}w^{j+1}

Hence, we find that for each
w\in\mathbb{D},

\displaystyle(T_{\varphi}(z^{n}))(w)

\displaystyle=\left\langle\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\left(\varphi(z)z^{n}\right),\sum_{j\geqslant 0}\overline{w}^{j+1}z^{j}\right\rangle_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{D}\right)}=\sum_{j\geqslant 1}\hat{\varphi}\left(j-n\right)w^{j}.

In view of (
2.5
), we have that
\sum_{j\geqslant 1}j|\hat{\varphi}(j-n)|^{2}.
Thus
\sum_{j\geqslant 1}\hat{\varphi}\left(j-n\right)z^{j}\in\mathcal{D}_{0}.
Since the evaluation at every point
w\in\mathbb{D}
is a bounded linear functional on
\mathcal{D}_{0},
it follows that

\displaystyle T_{\varphi}(z^{n})=\sum_{j\geqslant 1}\hat{\varphi}\left(j-n\right)z^{j},

where the above sum converges in the norm in
{\mathcal{D}_{0}}.
This gives us the matrix representation of
T_{\varphi}
is in the required form.
∎

In view of the Proposition
2.5
, the following corollary is now immediate.

Corollary 2.6
.

For any
\varphi\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{D}_{0}})
the operator
T_{\varphi}=0
if and only if
\varphi=0.

Proposition
2.5
also gives us the answer of zero product problem for the class of Toeplitz operators
\{T_{\varphi}:\varphi\in\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}})\}
acting on the Dirichlet space
{\mathcal{D}_{0}}
. The classical zero product problem asks when a product of finitely many bounded Toeplitz operators on the Hardy space is equal to zero. This has been resolved completely by Aleman and Vukotić, see
[
2
]
. Using their result, Y. J. Lee proved that the zero product problem for the Toeplitz operators on
{\mathcal{D}_{0}}
has the same answer provided the the symbols associated to the Toeplitz operators are coming from the class
\{\varphi\in h(\mathbb{D}):\varphi,\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial z},\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial\bar{z}}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})\},
see
[
16
, Theorem 1.2]
. In view of Proposition
2.5
, Lee’s argument can be imitated to obtain the same result for the relatively larger class of Toeplitz operators on
{\mathcal{D}_{0}}
induced by the symbol class
\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}}).
Therefore we omit the proof of the following Proposition and refer the reader to
[
16
, Theorem 1.2]
for the proof.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{k}\in\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}}).
Then
T_{\varphi_{1}}\cdots T_{\varphi_{k}}=0
on
{\mathcal{D}_{0}}
if and only if
\varphi_{j}=0
for some
j=1,\ldots,k.

Now we discuss the problem of when a Toeplitz operator
T_{\varphi},
acting on
{\mathcal{D}_{0}},
is compact for
\varphi\in\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}}).
It is well known that a Toeplitz operator
T_{\varphi},
with
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}),
acting on the Hardy space is compact if and only if
\varphi=0.
Several authors has studied the compactness problem for Toeplitz operators on
{\mathcal{D}_{0}}
induced by various class of symbols, see for example
[
15
]
,
[
20
]
. However, we offer an alternative proof using the
Toeplitz matrix
nature of the matrix representation of the Toeplitz operators on the Dirichlet space
{\mathcal{D}_{0}}.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
\phi\in{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{D}_{0})
. Then
T_{\varphi}
is compact if and only if
\varphi=0
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{D}_{0})
. We write

\varphi(z)=\sum_{k\geqslant 0}c_{k}z^{k}+\sum_{k\geqslant 1}c_{-k}\overline{z}^{k},\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Fix a
k\geqslant 0
. By Proposition
2.5
, we have that for any
m\geqslant 1,

c_{k}=\frac{1}{\left\lVert z^{m}\right\rVert^{2}}\left\langle T_{\varphi}z^{m+k},z^{m}\right\rangle.

Now assume that
T_{\varphi}
is compact. Since
\{z^{m}:m\geqslant 1\}
is an orthogonal basis of
\mathcal{D}_{0},
we have that
T_{\varphi}^{*}\left(\frac{z^{m}}{\left\lVert z^{m}\right\rVert}\right)
converges to
0
in
{\mathcal{D}_{0}}
. Hence, we have that for each
m\geqslant 1
,

\displaystyle\left\lvert c_{k}\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left\lVert z^{m}\right\rVert^{2}}\left\lvert\left\langle T_{\varphi}z^{m+k},z^{m}\right\rangle\right\rvert\leqslant\sqrt{1+\frac{k}{m}}\left\lVert T_{\varphi}^{*}\left(\frac{z^{m}}{\left\lVert z^{m}\right\rVert}\right)\right\rVert.

Now, letting
m\to\infty
, we have that
c_{k}=0
for each
k\geqslant 0
. A similar argument shows that
c_{-k}=0
for all
k\geqslant 1
. Thus, we have that
\varphi=0
.
∎

Let
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{D}_{0})
be the multiplier algebra of
{\mathcal{D}_{0}}
, that is,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{D}_{0})=\{\psi\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{D}):\psi f\in{\mathcal{D}_{0}}\mbox{\;\;for every\,\,}f\in{\mathcal{D}_{0}}\}.

It is straightforward to verify that

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{D}_{0})=\{\psi\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}):|\psi^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dA(z)\,\mbox{is a Carleson measure for }\,{\mathcal{D}_{0}}\},

see
[
10
, Theorem 5.1.7]
for a similar argument of the proof. Let
H^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{D})
denotes the set of all bounded holomorphic function on
\mathbb{D}
which vanishes at
0,
i.e.

\displaystyle H^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{D})=\{f\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}):f(0)=0\}.

We conclude this section after showing that
\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}})=\mathcal{M}({\mathcal{D}_{0}})+\overline{H^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{D})}
and both the holomorphic and antiholomorphic part of
\varphi\in{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{D}_{0})
are in
{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{D}_{0}).

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
\varphi
be a harmonic function on
\mathbb{D}
and suppose that

\varphi(z)=\varphi_{1}(z)+\varphi_{2}(z),\,\mbox{where\,\,}\,\varphi_{1}(z)=\sum_{k\geqslant 0}\hat{\varphi}(k)z^{k},\varphi_{2}=\sum_{k>0}\hat{\varphi}(-k)\overline{z}^{k},\,\,z\in\mathbb{D}.

Then
\varphi\in\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}})
if and only if
\varphi_{1}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{D}_{0})
and
\varphi_{2}\in\overline{H^{\infty}_{0}}.
In particular both
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
are in
{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{D}_{0}).

Proof.

Since
\varphi\in{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{D}_{0}),
the map
T:{\mathcal{D}_{0}}\to{\mathcal{A}^{2}}
defined by
T(f)=\varphi_{1}^{\prime}f,\,f\in{\mathcal{D}_{0}},
is a bounded linear transformation. It follows that
T^{*}(K^{B}_{w})=\overline{\varphi_{1}^{\prime}(w}R_{w}
for every
w\in\mathbb{D}.
Thus we obtain that

\displaystyle|\varphi_{1}^{\prime}(w)|^{2}\leqslant\|T\|^{2}\frac{R_{w}(w)}{K^{B}_{w}(w)}=\|T\|^{2}(1-|w|^{2})^{2}\ln\left(\frac{1}{1-|w|^{2}}\right),\qquad w\in\mathbb{D}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{|w|\to 1-}\frac{|\varphi_{1}^{\prime}(w)|^{2}}{1-|w|^{2}}=0.

This gives us that
\varphi_{1}^{\prime}
is bounded and hence
\varphi_{1}
is bounded. It follows that both
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
are bounded and in
{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{D}_{0}).

∎

3.
Brown-Halmos operator identity and Toeplitz operators

Let
\mathbb{C}[z,\bar{z}]
denotes the set of all trigonometric polynomials in
z
and
\bar{z},
that is,

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}[z,\bar{z}]:=\Big\{\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}c_{-j}\bar{z}^{j}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}z^{j},\,c_{j}\in\mathbb{C}\Big\}.

Since the Lebesgue measure
dA
on unit disc
\mathbb{D}
is a Carleson measure for
\mathcal{D}_{0},
it follows that
\mathbb{C}[z,\bar{z}]\subseteq\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}}).
Consider the operator
T_{z}
and
T_{\bar{z}}.
Let
f\in\mathcal{D}_{0}
having power series representation of the form
f(w)=\sum_{j\geqslant 1}a_{j}w^{j},

w\in\mathbb{D}.
Then

\displaystyle T_{z}(f)(w)=\sum\limits_{j\geqslant 2}a_{j-1}w^{j},\,\,\,T_{\bar{z}}(f)(w)=\sum\limits_{j\geqslant 1}a_{j+1}w^{j},\,\,w\in\mathbb{D}.

Thus w.r.t the orthogonal basis
\mathcal{B}=\{z^{j}:j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1}\}
of
\mathcal{D}_{0}
, the operator
T_{z}
and
T_{\bar{z}}
represents the forward shift and the backward shift operator on
\mathcal{D}_{0}
respectively. Moreover note that
T_{\bar{z}}T_{z}=I.
In this section, we will show that the Brown Halmos type operator identity
T_{\bar{z}}AT_{z}=A
completely characterize the Toeplitz operators on
\mathcal{D}_{0}
induced by the symbols in
\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}}).
First, we provide a characterization of every operator
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{D}_{0})
which satisfies the Brown Halmos type identity
T_{\bar{z}}TT_{z}=T
in terms of their matrix representation.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{D}_{0}).
Suppose
\left[T\right]
denotes the matrix representation of
T
w.r.t the orthogonal basis
\mathcal{B}=\{z^{j}:j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1}\}
of
\mathcal{D}_{0},
that is,

\displaystyle Tz^{j}=\sum_{i\geqslant 1}[T]_{i,j}z^{i},\qquad j\geqslant 1.

Then
T
satisfies the identity
T_{\bar{z}}TT_{z}=T
if and only if

\displaystyle[T]_{i,j}=[T]_{i+1,j+1},\,\,\mbox{for every}\,\,i,j\geqslant 1.

Proof.

It is straightforward to verify that for every
j\geqslant 1,

\displaystyle T_{\bar{z}}TT_{z}(z^{j})

\displaystyle=T_{\bar{z}}\left(\sum_{i\geqslant 1}[T]_{i,j+1}z^{i}\right)=\sum_{i\geqslant 1}[T]_{i+1,j+1}z^{i}.

Since
\{z^{j}:j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1}\}
is an orthogonal basis of
\mathcal{D}_{0},
it follows immediately that
T_{\bar{z}}TT_{z}=T
if and only if
[T]_{i,j}=[T]_{i+1,j+1}
for every
i,j\geqslant 1.
This completes the proof.
∎

In view of Proposition
2.5
, the following corollary is now immediate.

Corollary 3.2
.

For any
\psi\in{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{D}_{0}),
the Toeplitz operators
T_{\psi}
satisfies the operator identity

\displaystyle T_{\bar{z}}T_{\psi}T_{z}=T_{\psi}.

Let
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{D}_{0})
and
T
satisfies the identity
T_{\bar{z}}TT_{z}=T.
In view of Proposition
3.1
, it is natural to consider the symbol
\varphi
given by

\displaystyle\varphi(z)=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}c_{-j}\bar{z}^{j}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}c_{j}z^{j},

where
c_{i-k}=[T]_{i,k}.
In the following Lemma we find that this
\varphi
always define a harmonic function on
\mathbb{D}.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{D}_{0})
such that the matrix representation of
T
w.r.t the orthogonal basis
\{z^{j}:j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1}\}
has the Toeplitz form

\left[T\right]=\begin{pmatrix}c_{0}&c_{-1}&c_{-2}&\ldots\\
c_{1}&c_{0}&c_{-1}&\ddots\\
c_{2}&c_{1}&c_{0}&\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots\end{pmatrix},

for some sequence of complex numbers
\{c_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}.
Then the function
\varphi
given by

\varphi(z)=\sum_{j\geqslant 1}c_{j}z^{j}+\sum_{j\geqslant 0}c_{-j}\bar{z}^{j},z\in\mathbb{D}

defines a harmonic function on
\mathbb{D}.
Moreover the function
\varphi_{1}(z)=\sum_{j\geqslant 1}c_{j}z^{j}\in\mathcal{D}_{0}.

Proof.

From the given matrix representation of
T
with respect to the orthogonal basis
\{z^{j}:j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1}\}
of
\mathcal{D}_{0}
we have that

\displaystyle T(z^{j})=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}c_{i-j}z^{i},\,\,j\geqslant 1.

It follows that

\displaystyle c_{i-j}=\frac{1}{\|z^{i}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{D}_{0}}}\left\langle T(z^{j}),z^{i}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{D}_{0}},\,\,\,i,j\geqslant 1.

Applying Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we obtain that
|c_{i-j}|\leqslant\|T\|\frac{\sqrt{j}}{\sqrt{i}}
for
i,j\geqslant 1.
This gives us that
|c_{k}|\leqslant\frac{1}{\sqrt{k+1}}\|T\|
for every
k\geqslant 1
and
|c_{-j}|\leqslant\sqrt{j+1}\|T\|
for every
j\geqslant 1.
It follows that both the power series
\sum_{j\geqslant 1}c_{j}z^{j}
and
\sum_{j\geqslant 0}c_{-j}\bar{z}^{j}
has radius of convergence at least equal to
1.
Hence
\varphi
being a sum of holomorphic and antiholomorphic function on
\mathbb{D}
must define a harmonic function on
\mathbb{D}.
Since
Tz\in\mathcal{D}_{0},
we have that

\displaystyle\|Tz\|^{2}_{\mathcal{D}_{0}}=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}|c_{j}|^{2}(j+1)<\infty.

Hence it follows that
\varphi_{1}(z)=\sum_{j\geqslant 1}c_{j}z^{j}\in\mathcal{D}_{0}.
This completes the proof.
∎

Before proceeding to show that the symbol
\varphi
induced by
T
as mentioned in the Lemma
3.3
belongs to
{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{D}_{0})
and
T=T_{\varphi},
we first note down a few basic facts about the decomposition of an operator into homogeneous parts with respect to translations. For each
\theta\in\mathbb{[}0,2\pi),
let
\tau_{e^{i\theta}}
be the translation operator in
{\mathcal{B}}\left(\mathcal{D}_{0}\right)
, given by

\displaystyle(\tau_{e^{i\theta}}f)(z)=f(e^{-i\theta}z),\,\,\,\,z\in\mathbb{D}.

Note that
\tau_{e^{i\theta}}
is an unitary operator in
{\mathcal{B}}\left(\mathcal{D}_{0}\right).
It follows that for any operator
T\in{\mathcal{B}}\left(\mathcal{D}_{0}\right)
,

(3.1)

Tf=\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{\left\lvert k\right\rvert\leqslant N}\left(1-\frac{\left\lvert k\right\rvert}{N+1}\right)T_{k}f,\qquad f\in\mathcal{D}_{0},

where for each
k\in\mathbb{Z},
the operator
T_{k}
denotes the homogeneous part of
T
of degree
k
and it is given by

\displaystyle T_{k}f=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}e^{-ik\theta}\tau_{e^{-i\theta}}T\tau_{e^{i\theta}}(f)d\theta,\,\,f\in\mathcal{D}_{0},

see
[
14
, Section I.2]
, see also
[
19
, Sec 2]
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{D}_{0})
such that the matrix representation of
T
w.r.t the orthogonal basis
\{z^{j}:j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1}\}
has the Toeplitz form

\left[T\right]=\begin{pmatrix}c_{0}&c_{-1}&c_{-2}&\ldots\\
c_{1}&c_{0}&c_{-1}&\ddots\\
c_{2}&c_{1}&c_{0}&\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots\end{pmatrix},

for some sequence of numbers
\{c_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}.
Then it follows that

\displaystyle T_{k}=\begin{cases}c_{k}T_{z^{k}},&k\geqslant 1\\
c_{-k}T_{\bar{z}^{|k|}},&k\leqslant 0\end{cases},\,\,\mbox{and\,\,\,}\,\,Tf=\lim\limits_{N\to\infty}T_{\sigma_{\!{}_{N}}(\varphi)}(f),

where
\sigma_{\!{}_{N}}(\varphi)
denotes the Cesáro sum of the series
\varphi(z)=\sum_{k\geqslant 0}c_{-k}\bar{z}^{k}+\sum_{k\geqslant 1}c_{k}z^{k}
, that is,

\displaystyle\sigma_{\!{}_{N}}(\varphi)(z)=\left(\sum_{k=0}^{N}\left(1-\frac{\left\lvert k\right\rvert}{N+1}\right)c_{k}z^{k}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(1-\frac{\left\lvert k\right\rvert}{N+1}\right)c_{-k}\bar{z}^{-k}\right).

Proof.

Let
T\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{D}_{0}})
. Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Notice that for
n,m\geqslant 1
,

\displaystyle\left\langle T_{k}z^{n},z^{m}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}e^{-ik\theta}\left\langle\tau_{e^{-i\theta}}T\tau_{e^{i\theta}}z^{n},z^{m}\right\rangle d\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}e^{-ik\theta}\left\langle T(e^{-in\theta}z^{n}),\tau_{e^{-i\theta}}^{*}z^{m}\right\rangle d\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}e^{-ik\theta}\left\langle T(e^{-in\theta}z^{n}),\tau_{e^{i\theta}}z^{m}\right\rangle d\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}e^{-ik\theta}\left\langle T(e^{-in\theta}z^{n}),e^{-im\theta}z^{m}\right\rangle d\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}e^{i(m-n-k)\theta}\left\langle Tz^{n},z^{m}\right\rangle d\theta

\displaystyle=\left\langle Tz^{n},z^{m}\right\rangle\delta_{m-n,k}.

This shows that if the matrix of
T
with respect to the orthogonal basis
\{z^{k}:k\geqslant 1\}
is given by
(t_{mn})_{m,n\geqslant 0}
then the matrix of
T_{k}
with respect to the same basis is given by
(t_{mn}\delta_{m-n,k})_{m,n\geqslant 0}
. Now, if the matrix of
T
has a Toeplitz form as mentioned in the statement of the Proposition then it is immediate by Proposition
2.5
that

\displaystyle T_{k}=\begin{cases}c_{k}T_{z^{k}},&k\geqslant 1\\
c_{k}T_{\bar{z}^{|k|}},&k\leqslant 0.\end{cases}

Consequently, in view of
3.1
, it follows that

Tf=\lim_{N\to\infty}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{N}\left(1-\frac{\left\lvert k\right\rvert}{N+1}\right)c_{k}T_{z^{k}}f+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(1-\frac{\left\lvert k\right\rvert}{N+1}\right)c_{-k}T_{\overline{z}^{k}}f\right),\qquad f\in\mathcal{D}_{0}.

Note that
T_{g+h}(f)=T_{g}(f)+T_{h}(f)
for every
g,h\in\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}})
and
f\in\mathcal{D}_{0}.
It follows that

\displaystyle T_{\sigma_{\!{}_{N}}(\varphi)}(f)=\left(\sum_{k=0}^{N}\left(1-\frac{\left\lvert k\right\rvert}{N+1}\right)c_{k}T_{z^{k}}f+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(1-\frac{\left\lvert k\right\rvert}{N+1}\right)c_{-k}T_{\overline{z}^{k}}f\right),\qquad f\in\mathcal{D}_{0}.

Thus we obtain that
\lim Tf=\lim\limits_{N\to\infty}T_{\sigma_{\!{}_{N}}(\varphi)}(f)
for every
f\in\mathcal{D}_{0}.

∎

Now we are ready to give the proof of the main theorem of this section.

Proof of the Theorem
1.1
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{D}_{0})
such that the identity
T_{\bar{z}}TT_{z}=T
holds. Then by Proposition
3.1
, we find that matrix representation of
T
w.r.t. the orthogonal basis
\{z^{j}:j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1}\}
of
\mathcal{D}_{0}
has the
Toeplitz
form

\left[T\right]=\begin{pmatrix}c_{0}&c_{-1}&c_{-2}&\ldots\\
c_{1}&c_{0}&c_{-1}&\ddots\\
c_{2}&c_{1}&c_{0}&\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots\end{pmatrix},

for some sequence of numbers
\{c_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}.
Moreover in view of Lemma
3.3
, the function
\varphi
given by

\displaystyle\varphi(z)=\sum_{j\geqslant 1}c_{j}z^{j}+\sum_{j\geqslant 0}c_{-j}\bar{z}^{j},z\in\mathbb{D},

defines a harmonic function on
\mathbb{D}.
We write
\varphi_{1}(z)=\sum_{j\geqslant 1}c_{j}z^{j}.
By Lemma
3.3
, it also follows that
\varphi_{1}\in\mathcal{D}_{0}
.
For each
w\in\mathbb{D},
we consider

\displaystyle E_{w}(z)=\frac{z}{1-\bar{w}z}=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}z^{j+1}\bar{w}^{j},\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}

It is straightforward to verify that
\|E_{w}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{D}_{0}}=\frac{1}{(1-|w|^{2})^{2}}.
Now we consider

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}_{w}(z)=\frac{E_{w}(z)}{\|E_{w}\|}=(1-|w|^{2})\frac{z}{1-\bar{w}z},\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Note that
\frac{\partial E_{w}(z)}{\partial z}=\widetilde{K^{B}_{w}}(z),
the normalised reproducing kernel of
{\mathcal{A}^{2}}
at
w\in\mathbb{D}.
Using (
2.3
) and the reproducing property of
K^{B}_{w},
it is immediate that for
k\geqslant 0
,

\displaystyle\left\langle T_{z^{k}}\widetilde{E}_{w},\widetilde{E}_{w}\right\rangle=k(1-\left\lvert w\right\rvert^{2})w^{k}+w^{k}\,\,\mbox{and\,\,}\left\langle T_{\overline{z}^{k}}\widetilde{E}_{w},\widetilde{E}_{w}\right\rangle=\overline{w}^{k},\,\,w\in\mathbb{D}.

Now, fix
N\in\mathbb{N},w\in\mathbb{D}
and Observe that

\displaystyle\left\langle\sum_{k=0}^{N}\left(1-\frac{\left\lvert k\right\rvert}{N+1}\right)c_{k}T_{z^{k}}\widetilde{E}_{w},\widetilde{E}_{w}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{N}\left(1-\frac{\left\lvert k\right\rvert}{N+1}\right)c_{k}\left(k(1-\left\lvert w\right\rvert^{2})w^{k}+w^{k}\right)

and similarly, we have

\displaystyle\left\langle\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(1-\frac{\left\lvert k\right\rvert}{N+1}\right)c_{k}T_{\bar{z}^{k}}\widetilde{E}_{w},\widetilde{E}_{w}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(1-\frac{\left\lvert k\right\rvert}{N+1}\right)c_{k}\overline{w}^{k}.

This gives us that for any
w\in\mathbb{D},

\displaystyle\left\langle T_{\sigma_{\!{}_{N}}(\varphi)}\widetilde{E}_{w},\widetilde{E}_{w}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=w(1-\left\lvert w\right\rvert^{2})\sum_{k=0}^{N}\left(1-\frac{\left\lvert k\right\rvert}{N+1}\right)c_{k}kw^{k-1}+\sigma_{\!{}_{N}}(\varphi)(w)

\displaystyle=w(1-\left\lvert w\right\rvert^{2})\sigma_{\!{}_{N}}(\varphi_{1}^{\prime})(w)+\sigma_{\!{}_{N}}(\varphi)(w).

Now using Proposition
3.4
we have that for any
w\in\mathbb{D},

\displaystyle\left\langle T\widetilde{E}_{w},\widetilde{E}_{w}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}\left\langle T_{\sigma_{\!{}_{N}}(\varphi)}\widetilde{E}_{w},\widetilde{E}_{w}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=w(1-\left\lvert w\right\rvert^{2})\lim_{N\to\infty}\sigma_{\!{}_{N}}(\varphi_{1}^{\prime})(w)+\lim_{N\to\infty}\sigma_{\!{}_{N}}(\varphi)(w),

(3.2)

\displaystyle=w(1-|w|^{2})\varphi_{1}^{\prime}(w)+\varphi(w).

It is well known that
\mathcal{D}_{0}
is contained in
\mathcal{B}_{0},
the little Bloch space given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{0}=\Big\{f\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{D}):\lim\limits_{|z|\to 1}(1-|z|^{2})|f^{\prime}(z)|=0\Big\},

see
[
26
, Chapter 5]
. So we find that
\lim\limits_{|z|\to 1}(1-|z|^{2})|\varphi_{1}^{\prime}(z)|=0.
Since
\widetilde{E}_{w}
is a unit vector, we have that
|w(1-|w|^{2})\varphi_{1}^{\prime}(w)+\varphi(w)|\leqslant\|T\|
for every
w\in\mathbb{D}.
It follows that
\varphi
is bounded on
\mathbb{D}
and

(3.3)

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{\infty}\leqslant\|T\|.

Thus we obtain that

\varphi\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{D}_{0}}).
Now consider the densely defined Toeplitz operator
T_{\varphi}
on
\mathcal{D}_{0}.
Note that

\displaystyle\left\langle T_{\varphi}z^{n},z^{m}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{D}_{0}}=\left\langle Tz^{n},z^{m}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{D}_{0}},\,\,\,n,m\geqslant 1.

Since
T
is a bounded operator and span
\{z^{j}:j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1}\}
is dense in
\mathcal{D}_{0},
it follows that
T_{\varphi}
extends to a bounded operator on
\mathcal{D}_{0}
and
T_{\varphi}=T.
In view of Theorem
2.1
, it follows that
\varphi\in\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}}).
This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

The subspace
\{T_{\varphi}:\varphi\in{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{D}_{0})\}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{D}_{0}})
is closed in the weak operator topology (W.O.T) of
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{D}_{0}})
.

Proof.

Let
X,Y\in\mathcal{B}({\mathcal{D}_{0}}).
A standard argument shows that the subspace
S_{X,Y}
of
\mathcal{B}({\mathcal{D}_{0}})
given by
S_{X,Y}=\{A\in\mathcal{B}({\mathcal{D}_{0}}):XAY=A\}
is closed in the weak operator topology in
\mathcal{B}({\mathcal{D}_{0}}).
Thus the corollary follows immediately in view of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

For any
\varphi\in\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}}),
the sequence of Toeplitz operators
\{T_{\sigma_{\!{}_{N}}(\varphi)}\}_{N\geqslant 1}
converges to
T_{\varphi}
in the strong operator topology in
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{D}_{0}}).

Proof.

The proof follows immediately in view of Proposition
3.4
.
∎

4.
Some algebraic properties of Toeplitz operators

In this section, we investigate some algebraic properties of the Toeplitz operators on the Dirichlet space
{\mathcal{D}_{0}}.
Considering the various studies by different authors on Toeplitz operator in Dirichlet space
{\mathcal{D}_{0}}
induced by several symbol classes, the results in this section may be expected but these still are interesting facts. In
[
15
]
, Y. J. Lee studied various algebraic properties for the class of Toeplitz operators
\{T_{\varphi}:\varphi\in\Omega\cap h(\mathbb{D})\},
where the symbol class
\Omega
is given by
\Omega=\{\varphi\in\mathscr{C}^{1}(\mathbb{D}):\varphi,\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial z},\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial\bar{z}}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})\}
and
h(\mathbb{D})
denotes the set of all complex valued harmonic functions on
\mathbb{D}
. It turns out that all those characterizations obtained by Lee in
[
15
]
, naturally get extended to the Toeplitz operators
\{T_{\varphi}:\varphi\in\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}})\},
acting on
{\mathcal{D}_{0}}.
Most of the methods and techniques used by Lee can be used to extend his result for the Toeplitz operators
T_{\varphi}
induced by the symbol class
\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}}).
But rather we chose to exploit the Brown-Halmos type operator identity (wherever possible), to obtain the characterizations of these algebraic properties of the Toeplitz operators acting on
{\mathcal{D}_{0}}.
First we show that product of two Toeplitz operator is Toeplitz if and only if the first symbol is antiholomorphic or the second symbol is holomorphic. We first prove a couple of preliminary lemmas in this regard. The first lemma, in the case of Toeplitz operators on the Hardy space induced by
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
symbols, is well known, see for example
[
21
, Chapter 3]
and references therein.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\psi,\varphi\in\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}})
. Then

T_{\overline{z}}T_{\psi}T_{\varphi}T_{z}=T_{\psi}T_{\varphi}+\left(T_{\overline{z}}T_{\psi}z\right)\otimes\left(T_{z}^{*}T_{\varphi}^{*}z\right).

Proof.

It is straightforward to verify that
I=T_{z}T_{\bar{z}}+z\otimes z
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle T_{\overline{z}}T_{\psi}T_{\varphi}T_{z}

\displaystyle=T_{\overline{z}}T_{\psi}(T_{z}T_{\bar{z}}+z\otimes z)T_{\varphi}T_{z}

\displaystyle=T_{\psi}T_{\varphi}+T_{\overline{z}}T_{\psi}(z\otimes z)T_{\varphi}T_{z}

\displaystyle=T_{\psi}T_{\varphi}+\left(T_{\overline{z}}T_{\psi}z\right)\otimes\left(T_{z}^{*}T_{\varphi}^{*}z\right).

This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\psi,\varphi\in\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}})
. If
\psi
is antiholomorphic or
\varphi
is holomorphic then

\left\langle T_{\psi}T_{\varphi}f,g\right\rangle=\left\langle\frac{\partial(\psi\varphi f)}{\partial z},g^{\prime}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})},\qquad f,g\in\mathcal{D}_{0}.

Proof.

First assume that
\varphi
holomorphic. Then for
f,g\in{\mathcal{D}_{0}},
using (
2.3
), we have that

(4.1)

\displaystyle\left\langle T_{\psi}T_{\varphi}f,g\right\rangle=\left\langle\left(\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial z}\right)(\varphi f)+\psi(\varphi f)^{\prime},g\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}=\left\langle\frac{\partial(\psi\varphi f)}{\partial z},g^{\prime}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}

Now assume that
\overline{\psi}=\chi
is a holomorphic symbol. Then for
f,g\in{\mathcal{D}_{0}}
, using (
2.2
) and (
2.3
), we have that

\displaystyle\left\langle T_{\psi}T_{\varphi}f,g\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\frac{\partial(\overline{\chi}T_{\varphi}f)}{\partial z},g^{\prime}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\overline{\chi}\left(f\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial z}+P_{{\mathcal{A}^{2}}}(\varphi f^{\prime})\right),g^{\prime}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}

\displaystyle=\left\langle f\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial z}+P_{{\mathcal{A}^{2}}}(\varphi f^{\prime}),\chi g^{\prime}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}

\displaystyle=\left\langle f\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial z},\chi g^{\prime}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}+\left\langle(\varphi f^{\prime}),\chi g^{\prime}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\overline{\chi}\left(f\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial z}+\varphi f^{\prime}\right),g^{\prime}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}

(4.2)

\displaystyle=\left\langle\frac{\partial\left(\psi\varphi f\right)}{\partial z},g^{\prime}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}

This completes the proof.
∎

Now we are ready to answer the question of when product of two Toeplitz operator is again a Toeplitz operator.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\psi,\varphi\in\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}})
. Then
T_{\psi}T_{\varphi}=T_{\tau}
for some symbol
\tau\in\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}})
if and only if
\psi
is antiholomorphic or
\varphi
is holomorphic. Moreover in either case we must have
\tau=P[(\psi\varphi)|_{{}_{\mathbb{T}}}].

Proof.

In view of Corollary
3.2
and Lemma
4.1
, it follows that the product
T_{\psi}T_{\varphi}=T_{\tau}
for some
\tau\in\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}})
if and only if

\left(T_{\overline{z}}T_{\psi}z\right)\otimes\left(T_{z}^{*}T_{\varphi}^{*}z\right)=0.

This implies that either
T_{\overline{z}}T_{\psi}z=0
or
T_{z}^{*}T_{\varphi}^{*}z=0
. A routine calculation using Proposition
2.5
shows that this happens if and only if either
\psi
is antiholomorphic or
\varphi
is holomorphic. Now, we show that
(\psi\phi)|_{{}_{\mathbb{T}}}=\tau|_{{}_{\mathbb{T}}}.
By Lemma
4.2
, we have

\displaystyle\left\langle T_{\psi}T_{\varphi}\widetilde{E}_{w},\widetilde{E}_{w}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\frac{\partial(\psi\varphi\widetilde{E}_{w})}{\partial z},\widetilde{E}_{w}^{\prime}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial z}\widetilde{E}_{w},\widetilde{K_{w}^{B}}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}+\left\langle\psi\varphi\widetilde{K_{w}^{B}},\widetilde{K_{w}^{B}}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}

\displaystyle=w(1-\left\lvert w\right\rvert^{2})\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial z}(w)+\left\langle\psi\varphi\widetilde{K_{w}^{B}},\widetilde{K_{w}^{B}}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}.

and a similar argument shows that

\displaystyle\left\langle T_{\tau}\widetilde{E}_{w},\widetilde{E}_{w}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=w(1-\left\lvert w\right\rvert^{2})\frac{\partial\tau}{\partial z}(w)+\tau(w).

Since
\psi
,
\varphi
are bounded harmonic functions,
\left\langle\psi\varphi\widetilde{K_{w}^{B}},\widetilde{K_{w}^{B}}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}
must have non-tangential limits almost everywhere on
\mathbb{T}
, see
[
13
, Exercise 2, Chapter 2]
. Now, taking radial limits as
\left\lvert w\right\rvert\to 1
in the two aforementioned equalities, we have that
(\psi\phi)|_{{}_{\mathbb{T}}}=\tau|_{{}_{\mathbb{T}}}.
Since
\tau
is a bounded harmonic function we must have

\tau=P[\tau|_{{}_{\mathbb{T}}}]=P[(\psi\phi)|_{{}_{\mathbb{T}}}].

This completes the proof.
∎

Now we discuss the problem of characterizing two symbols for which the corresponding Toeplitz operators commute on
{\mathcal{D}_{0}}.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\psi,\varphi\in\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}})
. Write

\varphi(z)=\sum_{k\geqslant 0}\hat{\varphi}(k)z^{k}+\sum_{k\geqslant 1}\hat{\varphi}(-k)\overline{z}^{k},\qquad z\in\mathbb{D},

and

\psi(z)=\sum_{k\geqslant 0}\hat{\psi}(k)z^{k}+\sum_{k\geqslant 1}\hat{\psi}(-k)\overline{z}^{k},\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}.

The following are equivalent:

(1)

T_{\varphi}T_{\psi}=T_{\psi}T_{\varphi}
on
{\mathcal{D}_{0}}
.

(2)

\hat{\varphi}(m)\hat{\psi}(-n)=\hat{\varphi}(-m)\hat{\psi}(n)
for
m,n\geqslant 1
.

(3)

Exactly one of the following is true:

(a)

\varphi
and
\psi
is holomorphic.

(b)

\varphi
and
\psi
is antiholomorphic.

(c)

A nontrivial linear combination of
\varphi
and
\psi
is constant in
\mathbb{D}
.

Proof.

We first show that
(1)\Longleftrightarrow(2)
. Using Lemma
4.1
and Proposition
2.5
, we find that

\displaystyle T_{\varphi}T_{\psi}=T_{\psi}T_{\varphi}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\left(T_{\overline{z}}T_{\varphi}z\right)\otimes\left(T_{z}^{*}T_{\psi}^{*}z\right)=\left(T_{\overline{z}}T_{\psi}z\right)\otimes\left(T_{z}^{*}T_{\varphi}^{*}z\right)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\left\langle T_{\psi}z^{n+1},z\right\rangle T_{\overline{z}}T_{\varphi}z=\left\langle T_{\varphi}z^{n+1},z\right\rangle T_{\overline{z}}T_{\psi}z,

\displaystyle\qquad n\geqslant 1,

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\hat{\varphi}(m)\hat{\psi}(-n)=\hat{\varphi}(-m)\hat{\psi}(n),

\displaystyle\qquad m,n\geqslant 1.

The fact
(2)\Longleftrightarrow(3)
is routine and well studied. We leave it for the reader to verify, see
[
4
,
15
]
for similar arguments.
∎

We conclude the paper with a brief discussion on the question of when the product of two Toeplitz operators on
{\mathcal{D}_{0}}
is a compact perturbation of some Toeplitz operator on
{\mathcal{D}_{0}}
. For any
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{D}),
let
{\bf B}(f)
denotes the Berezin transform of
f,
see
[
13
, Chapter 2]
for the definitions and the properties of the Berezin transform and
C_{0}
denotes the space of all continuous functions
g
on
\mathbb{D}
such that
g(a)\to 0
as
|a|\to 1-.
We find that the result of Y. J. Lee in
[
15
]
on the compact product problem of when
T_{\psi}T_{\varphi}-T_{\tau}
is compact on
\mathcal{D}_{0}
for
\varphi,\psi,\tau\in\Omega\cap h(\mathbb{D})
naturally extends for the symbols
\varphi,\psi,\tau\in\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}}).
We state the result in the next proposition and omit the proof here. One may imitate the argument presented in
[
15
, Theorem D]
to obtain the following:

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\psi,\varphi,\tau\in\mathcal{T}({\mathcal{D}_{0}})
. Then the operator
T_{\psi}T_{\varphi}-T_{\tau}
is a compact operator if and only
{\bf B}({\psi\varphi})-\tau\in C_{0}.

A class of Truncated Freud polynomials
Juan C. García-Ardila, Francisco Marcellán, Misael E. Marriaga

Abstract
. Consider the following truncated Freud linear functional 𝐮 z \mathbf{u}_{z} depending on a parameter z z , ⟨ 𝐮 z , p ⟩ = ∫ 0 ∞ p ⁡ ( x ) ​ e − z ​ x 4 ​ 𝑑 x , z > 0 . \left\langle{\mathbf{u}_{z},p}\right\rangle=\int_{0}^{\infty}p(x)e^{-zx^{4}}dx,\quad z>0. The aim of this work is to analyze the properties of the sequence of orthogonal polynomials ( P n ) n ≥ 0 (P_{n})_{n\geq 0} with respect to 𝐮 z \mathbf{u}_{z} . Such a linear functional is semiclassical and, as a consequence, we get the system of nonlinear difference equations (Laguerre-Freud equations) that the coefficients of the three-term recurrence satisfy. The asymptotic behavior of such coefficients is given. On the other hand, the raising and lowering operators associated with such a linear functional are obtained, and thus a second-order linear differential equation of holonomic type that ( P n ) n ≥ 0 (P_{n})_{n\geq 0} satisfies is deduced. From this fact, an electrostatic interpretation of their zeros is given. Finally, some illustrative numerical tests concerning the behavior of the least and greatest zeros of these polynomials are presented.

1.
Introduction

Consider the sequence of orthogonal polynomials associated with a linear functional
\mathbf{u}
defined from the weight function
w(x)=e^{-x^{2}}
supported on the positive real semi-axis by

\langle\mathbf{u},p\rangle=\int_{0}^{\infty}p(x)e^{-x^{2}}dx.

This linear functional belongs to a wide class of linear functionals which is known in the literature as semiclassical
[
21
]
. Indeed, they are semiclassical of class
s=1
, see
[
1
]
. The concept of class allows us to introduce a hierarchy of linear functionals that constitutes an alternative way to the Askey tableau based on the hypergeometric character of the corresponding sequence of orthogonal polynomials. Semiclassical orthogonal polynomials appear in the seminal paper
[
25
]
, where weight functions whose logarithmic derivatives are rational functions were considered. A second-order linear differential equation satisfied by the corresponding sequence of orthogonal polynomials is obtained therein. The theory of semiclassical orthogonal polynomials has been developed by P. Maroni (see
[
20
,
21
]
) who combined techniques of functional analysis, distribution theory, Fourier and
z
-transforms, ordinary linear differential equations, among others, in order to deduce algebraic and structural properties of such polynomials. Many of the most popular families of orthogonal polynomials coming from
Mathematical Physics
as Hermite, Laguerre, Jacobi, and Bessel are semiclassical of class
s=0
, but other families appearing in the literature are also semiclassical. For instance, semiclassical families of class
s=1
are described in
[
2
]
. As an example, in
[
10
]
truncated Hermite polynomials, associated with the normal distribution supported on a symmetric interval of the real line, have been considered. They are semiclassical of class
s=2.

The coefficients of the three-term relation satisfied by orthogonal polynomials associated with a semiclassical linear functional satisfy coupled non linear difference equations (Laguerre-Freud equations, see
[
3
]
) which are related to discrete Painlevé equations, see
[
18
,
19
,
27
]
, among others. On the other hand, ladder operators (lowering and raising) are associated with semiclassical orthogonal polynomials. Their construction is based on the so-called first structure relation (
[
21
]
) they satisfy. As a consequence, one can deduce a second-order linear differential equation that the polynomials satisfy. Notice that the coefficients are polynomials with degrees depending on the class.

In the framework of random matrices, several authors have considered Gaussian unitary ensembles with one or two discontinuities (see
[
17
]
). Therein it is proved that the logarithmic derivative of the Hankel determinants generated by the normal (Gaussian) weight with two jump discontinuities in the scaled variables tends to the Hamiltonian of a coupled Painlevé II system and it satisfies a second-order PDE. The asymptotics of the coefficients of the three-term recurrence relation for the corresponding orthogonal polynomials are deduced. They are related to the solutions of the coupled Painlevé II system. The techniques are based on the analysis of ladder operators associated with such orthogonal polynomials. For more information, see
[
4
,
5
]
.

Integrable systems also provide nice examples of semiclassical orthogonal polynomials. They are related to time perturbations of a weight function (Toda, Langmuir lattices). For more information, see
[
28
]
.

In the literature, see
[
23
]
, among others, Gaussian quadrature rules are analyzed for weight functions
w(x)=x^{p}e^{-x^{2}},p>0,
supported on the positive real semi-axis. The corresponding orthogonal polynomials are called Maxwell polynomials. These polynomials constitute a useful tool for the solution of the Boltzman and/or Fokker-Planck equations. It is important to point out that the coefficients of the three-term recurrence relations satisfy non-linear equations, which are numerically unstable. In
[
23
]
, extended precision arithmetic is used to generate the recurrence coefficients to high order. As a consequence, the polynomials and associated quadrature weights and nodes are deduced.

Maxwell polynomials are used in various fields for different purposes, such as in pseudo-spectral collocation schemes. Among the numerous characteristics that make these polynomials desirable for pseudo-spectral discretization schemes for the velocity variable, one can emphasize their capability to handle semi-infinite intervals, the convenient distribution of their zeros that balances clustering around zero, where increased resolution is often needed, with the sampling at increasingly larger distances from the origin, and their optimal location for the computation of integrals involving a Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution. For further details, see
[
16
,
22
,
24
]
.

The aim of our contribution is to analyze the sequence of monic orthogonal polynomials associated with a linear functional
\mathbf{u}_{z}
defined from a weight function
w(x)=e^{-zx^{4}}
supported on the positive real semi-axis by

\langle\mathbf{u}_{z},p\rangle=\int_{0}^{\infty}p(x)e^{-zx^{4}}dx,z>0.

Such a kind of weight function appears in the framework of high electric field strength, where the steady state electron velocity distribution of electrons dilutely dispersed in a background gas of atoms is given by the so-called Druyvesteyn distribution function, assuming that the electron-atom momentum transfer cross section is constant
[
12
]
. This distribution (weight) function has the form
w(x)=x^{2}\exp(-ax^{4}),
where
x
denotes the reduced speed of electrons. In
[
7
]
the authors analyze the coefficients of the three-term recurrence that the sequence of orthogonal polynomials associated with the weight
w(x)=x^{p}\exp(-ax^{4})
,
p=0,1,2,\ldots,
supported in the positive real semi-axis satisfies. In particular, a first approach to their asymptotic expansions is given, as well as some open problems related to Gaussian quadrature formulas, when
p=2
are stated.

The structure of the manuscript is as follows. In Section
2
, a basic background on semiclassical linear functionals is provided. Section
3
is focused on the study of truncated Freud linear functionals, that is, the case
\langle\mathbf{u}_{z},p\rangle=\int_{0}^{\infty}p(x)e^{-zx^{4}}dx,

z\in(0,\infty)
, a semiclassical linear functional of class
3
. Furthermore, in Section
4
, a system of nonlinear difference equations that the corresponding sequence of monic orthogonal polynomials satisfies is deduced. As a consequence, the asymptotic behavior of such coefficients in terms of
n
is deduced. In Section
5
, we study the ladder operators associated with such a sequence of monic orthogonal polynomials. Thus, we get a second-order linear differential equation with polynomial coefficients for such orthogonal polynomials. Section
6
focuses on the study of the parameters in the three-term recurrence relation in terms of
z.
Finally, in Section
7
we provide an electrostatic interpretation of the distribution of their zeros, as well as some numerical tests about the behavior of the least and greatest zeros of these polynomials.

2.
Basic background

Let
\mathbb{P}
denote the linear space of polynomials with complex coefficients. Given a linear functional
\mathbf{u}
that acts on
\mathbb{P}
, the
n
th moment of
\mathbf{u}
is defined as
\mu_{n}:=\left\langle{\mathbf{u},x^{n}}\right\rangle
,
n\geq 0
. For
q\in\mathbb{P}
we define the linear functional
q(x)\mathbf{u}
as

\left\langle{q\mathbf{u},p}\right\rangle=\left\langle{\mathbf{u},qp}\right\rangle,\quad p\in\mathbb{P}.

The distributional derivative of
\mathbf{u}
is the linear functional
D\mathbf{u}
such that

\left\langle{D\mathbf{u},p}\right\rangle=-\left\langle{\mathbf{u},p^{\prime}}\right\rangle,\quad p\in\mathbb{P}.

The linear functional
\mathbf{u}
is said to be quasi-definite if there exists a sequence of monic polynomials
(P_{n})_{n\geq 0}
such that
\deg{P_{n}(x)}=n
and
\left\langle{\mathbf{u},P_{n}P_{m}}\right\rangle=K_{n}\delta_{n,m},
where
\delta_{n,m}
is the Kronecker symbol and
K_{n}\neq 0
for
n\geqslant 0
(see
[
6
,
11
]
). If
\mathbf{u}
is quasi-definite, then the sequence
(P_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is said to be the
sequence of monic orthogonal polynomials
(SMOP) associated with
\mathbf{u}
. If
K_{n}>0,
then the linear functional
\mathbf{u}
is said to be positive definite.

Suppose that there exist two sequences of complex numbers
(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}
and
(b_{n})_{n\geq 0}
, with
a_{n}\neq 0
, and let
(P_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a sequence of monic polynomials generated by a three-term recurrence relation (TTRR)

(2.1)

\displaystyle x\,P_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=P_{n+1}(x)+b_{n}\,P_{n}(x)+a_{n}\,P_{n-1}(x),\quad n\geq 0,

\displaystyle P_{-1}(x)

\displaystyle=0,\ \ \ \ \ P_{0}(x)=1.

Then, by Favard’s Theorem, the above is equivalent to the existence of a unique quasi-definite linear functional
\mathbf{u}
such that
(P_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is its corresponding SMOP, see
[
6
]
.

Sometimes, it is more convenient to work with the matrix expression of the three-term recurrence relation (
2.1
), that is, if
\mathbf{P}=(P_{0},P_{1},\cdots)^{T}
, then the recurrence relation can be written in matrix form

(2.2)

x\mathbf{P}=\mathbf{J}\mathbf{P}\quad\text{with}\quad\mathbf{J}=\begin{pmatrix}b_{0}&1&0\\
a_{1}&b_{1}&1&0\\
0&a_{2}&b_{2}&1&0\\
&0&a_{3}&b_{3}&1&0\\
&&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots\\
\end{pmatrix}.

The matrix
\mathbf{J}
is known in the literature as
monic Jacobi matrix
.

If
(P_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is a sequence of monic orthogonal polynomials with respect to a linear functional
\mathbf{u}
, then using the
Christoffel-Darboux formula
(see
[
11
]
) we can get the following confluent formula

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{P^{2}_{k}(x)}{h_{k}}=\frac{P^{\prime}_{n+1}(x)P_{n}(x)-P^{\prime}_{n}(x)P_{n+1}(x)}{h_{n}},

where
h_{k}=\left\langle{\mathbf{u},P_{k}^{2}}\right\rangle.

Definition 2.1
.

Given a quasi-definite linear functional
\mathbf{u}
with moments
(\mu_{n})_{n\geq 0}
, the formal series

\mathbf{S}_{\mathbf{u}}(t):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{\mu_{n}}{t^{n+1}}

is called the Stieltjes function associated with
\mathbf{u}
.

Next, we present some important results concerning semiclassical linear functionals.

Definition 2.2

(
[
20
]
)
.

A quasi-definite functional
\mathbf{u}
is said to be semiclassical if there exist non-zero polynomials
\phi(x)
and
\psi(x)
with
\deg\phi(x)=:r\geq 0
and
\deg\psi(x)=:t\geq 1
, such that
\mathbf{u}
satisfies the distributional Pearson equation

(2.3)

D(\phi\,\mathbf{u})+\psi\,\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{0}.

An SMOP associated with a semiclassical linear functional
\mathbf{u}
is called semiclassical.

Because a semiclassical linear functional satisfies many Pearson equations, we give the following definition.

Definition 2.3
.

The class of a semiclassical functional
\mathbf{u}
is defined as

\mathfrak{s}(\mathbf{u}):=\min\max\{\deg\phi(x)-2,\deg\psi(x)-1\},

where the minimum is taken over all pairs of polynomials
\phi(x)
and
\psi(x)
so that (
2.3
) holds.

Proposition 2.4

(
[
11
,
21
]
)
.

Let
\mathbf{u}
be a semiclassical linear functional, and let
\phi(x)
and
\psi(x)
be non-zero polynomials with
\deg\phi(x)=:r
and
\deg\psi(x)=:t
, such that (
2.3
) is satisfied. Let
s:=\max(r-2,t-1)
. Then
s=\mathfrak{s}(\mathbf{u})
if and only if

(2.4)

\prod_{c:\,\phi(c)=0}\left(|\psi(c)+\phi^{\prime}(c)|+|\langle\mathbf{u},\theta_{c}\psi+\theta^{2}_{c}\phi\rangle|\right)>0.

Here,
(\theta_{c}f)(x)=\dfrac{f(x)-f(c)}{x-c},x\neq c,(\theta_{c}f)(c)=f^{\prime}(c).

According to the class, there is a hierarchy of semiclassical linear functionals. The class
s=0
is constituted by the classical linear functionals (Hermite, Laguerre, Jacobi and Bessel). The class
s=1
has been studied in
[
2
]
.

3.
Truncated Freud polynomials

In this section we study the Freud linear functional
\mathbf{u}_{z}
depending on a parameter
z

(3.1)

\left\langle{\mathbf{u}_{z},p}\right\rangle=\int_{0}^{\infty}p(x)e^{-zx^{4}}dx,\quad z>0,

as well as its corresponding sequence of monic orthogonal polynomials
(P_{n})_{n\geq 0}.
We will prove that
\mathbf{u}_{z}
is semiclassical and leverage this property to derive characteristics of both the moments of
\mathbf{u}_{z}
and the parameters of the TTRR satisfied by
(P_{n})_{n\geq 0}
.

Let
(\mu_{n}(z))_{n\geq 0}
be the sequence of moments of
\mathbf{u}_{z}
and let
n=4m+k
with
m=0,1\ldots,
and
k=0,1,2,3
. By setting
t=zx^{4}
we
can write

\displaystyle\mu_{4m+k}(z)=\left\langle{\mathbf{u}_{z},x^{4m+k}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{4m+k}e^{-zx^{4}}dx=\dfrac{z^{-m-(k+1)/4}}{4}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{m+\frac{k-3}{4}}e^{-t}dt.

Observe that the last integral is
\Gamma(m+\frac{k+1}{4})
, where
\Gamma(x)
is the Gamma function.
With this in mind, we have that for
m\geqslant 0
and
k=0,1,2,3,

\mu_{4m+k}(z)=\dfrac{z^{-m-(k+1)/4}}{4}\,\Gamma\left(m+\dfrac{k+1}{4}\right),

or,

(3.2)

\mu_{n}(z)=\dfrac{z^{-(n+1)/4}}{4}\,\Gamma\left(\dfrac{n+1}{4}\right),\quad n\geqslant 0.

Theorem 3.1
.

The functional
\mathbf{u}_{z}
is semiclassical of class
3
. Moreover,
\mathbf{u}
satisfies the Pearson equation

(3.3)

D(\phi\,\mathbf{u}_{z})+\psi\,\mathbf{u}_{z}=\mathbf{0},

with
\phi(x)=x
and
\psi(x)=4zx^{4}-1.

Proof.

Using integration by parts, for all
p\in\mathbb{P}
,

\displaystyle\left\langle{D(\phi\,\mathbf{u}_{z}),p}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle{\mathbf{u}_{z},\phi\,p^{\prime}}\right\rangle=-\int_{0}^{\infty}x\,p^{\prime}(x)e^{-zx^{4}}dx

\displaystyle=\left.-p(x)xe^{-zx^{4}}\right|_{0}^{\infty}+\int_{0}^{\infty}p(x)\left(1-4zx^{4}\right)e^{-zx^{4}}dx

\displaystyle=-\left\langle{\psi\,\mathbf{u}_{z},p\,}\right\rangle.

Next, we determine the class. In this case, (
2.4
) is read as
4z|\langle\mathbf{u}_{z},x^{3}\rangle|
. Taking into account (
3.2
), we get

4z|\langle\mathbf{u}_{z},x^{3}\rangle|=1>0.

Then, by Proposition
2.4
, the class of
\mathbf{u}_{z}
is
\mathfrak{s}(\mathbf{u}_{z})=3
.
∎

Applying (
3.3
) to the monomial
p(x)=x^{n}
we get the following recurrence relation for the moments.

Corollary 3.2
.

The moments
(\mu_{n}(z))_{n\geq 0}
with
\mu_{n}(z):=\langle\mathbf{u}_{z},x^{n}\rangle
satisfy the following fourth-order recurrence equation,

(3.4)

4z\mu_{n+4}(z)-(n+1)\mu_{n}(z)=0,\qquad n\geq 0,

with
\mu_{0}(z),\mu_{1}(z),\mu_{2}(z),\mu_{3}(z)
given in (
3.2
).

The recurrence relation (
3.4
) allows us to obtain a non-homogeneous first order linear ODE satisfied by the Stieltjes function associated with
\mathbf{u}_{z}
.

Proposition 3.3
.

The Stieltjes function
\mathbf{S}_{\mathbf{u}_{z}}(t;z)
associated with the linear functional
\mathbf{u}_{z}
satisfies a non-homogeneous first order linear ODE. Namely,

t\partial_{t}\mathbf{S}_{\mathbf{u}_{z}}(t;z)+4zt^{4}\mathbf{S}_{\mathbf{u}_{z}}(t;z)=4z\left(\mu_{3}(z)+t\mu_{2}(z)+t^{2}\mu_{1}(z)+\mu_{0}(z)t^{3}\right).

Proof.

Note that by (
3.4
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{\mathbf{u}_{z}}(t;z)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\mu_{0}(z)}{t}+\dfrac{\mu_{1}(z)}{t^{2}}+\dfrac{\mu_{2}(z)}{t^{3}}+\dfrac{\mu_{3}(z)}{t^{4}}+\dfrac{1}{4z}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}4z\,\dfrac{\mu_{n+4}(z)}{t^{n+5}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\mu_{0}(z)}{t}+\dfrac{\mu_{1}(z)}{t^{2}}+\dfrac{\mu_{2}(z)}{t^{3}}+\dfrac{\mu_{3}(z)}{t^{4}}+\dfrac{1}{4z}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(n+1)\dfrac{\mu_{n}(z)}{t^{n+5}}.

Since

\partial_{t}\mathbf{S}_{\mathbf{u}_{z}}(t;z)=-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(n+1)\dfrac{\mu_{n}(z)}{t^{n+2}},

it follows that

\mathbf{S}_{\mathbf{u}_{z}}(t;z)=\dfrac{\mu_{0}(z)}{t}+\dfrac{\mu_{1}(z)}{t^{2}}+\dfrac{\mu_{2}(z)}{t^{3}}+\dfrac{\mu_{3}(z)}{t^{4}}-\dfrac{1}{4z\,t^{3}}\partial_{t}\mathbf{S}_{\mathbf{u}_{z}}(t;z).

The desired result is obtained by multiplying both sides of the above equation by
4zt^{4}.

∎

4.
Laguerre-Freud equations

If
\mathbf{u}
is a semiclassical linear functional satisfying the Pearson equation (
3.3
), then the coefficients of the corresponding TTRR (
2.1
) satisfied by its SMOP
(P_{n})_{n\geq 0}
satisfy a nonlinear system of equations derived from the so-called
Laguerre-Freud equations

[
3
]
.

(4.1)

\displaystyle\left\langle{\psi\mathbf{u},P_{n}^{2}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle{D(\phi\mathbf{u}),P_{n}^{2}}\right\rangle,

(4.2)

\displaystyle\left\langle{\psi\mathbf{u},P_{n+1}P_{n}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle{D(\phi\mathbf{u}_{z}),P_{n+1}P_{n}}\right\rangle.

The iteration of (
2.1
) yields

x^{2}P_{n}(x)=P_{n+2}(x)+(b_{n+1}+b_{n})P_{n+1}(x)+(a_{n+1}+b_{n}^{2}+a_{n})P_{n}(x)\\
+a_{n}(b_{n}+b_{n-1})P_{n-1}(x)+a_{n}a_{n-1}P_{n-2}(x).

If we define
R_{n}=a_{n+1}+b_{n}^{2}+a_{n}
and
T_{n}=a_{n}(b_{n}+b_{n-1})
, then

(4.3)

x^{2}P_{n}(x)=P_{n+2}(x)+(b_{n+1}+b_{n})P_{n+1}(x)+R_{n}P_{n}(x)+T_{n}P_{n-1}(x)+a_{n}a_{n-1}P_{n-2}(x).

Iterating the above formula, we get

(4.4)

x^{4}P_{n}(x)=P_{n+4}(x)+\sum_{k=n-4}^{n+3}\beta_{n,k}(z)P_{k}(x),

where

\displaystyle\beta_{n,n+3}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{3}b_{n+i},

\displaystyle\beta_{n,n+2}(z)

\displaystyle=R_{n+2}+(b_{n+1}+b_{n})(b_{n+2}+b_{n+1})+R_{n},

\displaystyle\beta_{n,n+1}(z)

\displaystyle=T_{n+2}+(b_{n+1}+b_{n})(R_{n+1}+R_{n})+T_{n},

\displaystyle\beta_{n,n}(z)

\displaystyle=a_{n+2}a_{n+1}+(b_{n+1}+b_{n})T_{n+1}+R_{n}^{2}+(b_{n-1}+b_{n})T_{n}+a_{n}a_{n-1},

\displaystyle\beta_{n,n-1}(z)

\displaystyle=(b_{n+1}+b_{n})a_{n+1}a_{n}+(R_{n}+R_{n+1})T_{n}+(b_{n-1}+b_{n-2})a_{n}a_{n-1},

\displaystyle\beta_{n,n-2}(z)

\displaystyle=a_{n}a_{n-1}(R_{n}+R_{n+2})+T_{n}T_{n-1},

\displaystyle\beta_{n,n-3}(z)

\displaystyle=a_{n-1}a_{n-2}T_{n}+a_{n}a_{n-1}T_{n-2},

\displaystyle\beta_{n,n-4}(z)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=n-3}^{n}a_{i}.

Moreover, if we define
\mathbf{B}=(\beta_{n,k})_{n,k=0}
, where
\beta_{n,k}=0
for all
k\neq n-3,\ldots,n+3
, then

x^{4}\mathbf{P}=\mathbf{B}\mathbf{P}.

This implies that
\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{J}^{4},
where
\mathbf{J}
is the monic Jacobi matrix given in (
2.2
).

If we write

(4.5)

P_{n}(x)=x^{n}+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\lambda_{n,k}x^{k},

then substituting (
4.5
) into (
2.1
) and comparing the coefficients, we get

(4.6)

\begin{cases}b_{n}=\lambda_{n,n-1}-\lambda_{n+1,n},\\
\lambda_{n,n-1}b_{n}+a_{n}=\lambda_{n,n-2}-\lambda_{n+1,n-1}.\end{cases}

Finally, differentiating (
4.5
) and considering (
4.6
), we obtain

\displaystyle P^{\prime}_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=nx^{n-1}+(n-1)\lambda_{n,n-1}x^{n-2}+\mathcal{O}(x^{n-3})

\displaystyle=nP_{n-1}(x)+((n-1)\lambda_{n,n-1}-n\lambda_{n-1,n-2})P_{n-2}(x)+\mathcal{O}(x^{n-3})

(4.7)

\displaystyle=nP_{n-1}(x)-(nb_{n-1}+\lambda_{n,n-1})P_{n-2}(x)+\mathcal{O}(x^{n-3}).

Theorem 4.1
.

The coefficients of the TTRR (
2.1
) associated with the linear functional
\mathbf{u}_{z}
satisfy the Laguerre-Freud equations

(4.8)

\displaystyle 4z\left[a_{n+2}a_{n+1}+T_{n+1}(b_{n+1}+b_{n})+R^{2}_{n}+T_{n}(b_{n}+b_{n-1})+a_{n}a_{n-1}\right]=2n+1,

(4.9)

\displaystyle 4z[a_{n+1}(T_{n+2}+T_{n})-a_{n}(T_{n+1}+T_{n-1})-T_{n}(R_{n}+R_{n-1})+T_{n+1}(R_{n+1}+R_{n})]=b_{n},

where
R_{n}=a_{n+1}+b_{n}^{2}+a_{n}
and
T_{n}=a_{n}(b_{n}+b_{n-1})
.

Proof.

Let
h_{n}=\langle\mathbf{u}_{z},P_{n}^{2}\rangle
. By the orthogonality of
(P_{n})_{n\geq 0}
, the coefficients in (
2.1
) satisfy

(4.10)

b_{n}h_{n}=\langle\mathbf{u}_{z},xP_{n}^{2}\rangle,\quad a_{n}h_{n-1}=\langle\mathbf{u}_{z},xP_{n}P_{n-1}\rangle=h_{n}.

We first observe that (
4.1
) is equivalent to

\langle\mathbf{u}_{z},\psi P_{n}^{2}\rangle=2\,\langle\mathbf{u}_{z},\phi P_{n}P_{n}^{\prime}\rangle,

where
\phi(x)=x
and
\psi(x)=4zx^{4}-1
. Using (
4.4
), we compute

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{u}_{z},(4zx^{4}-1)P_{n}^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=4z\langle\mathbf{u}_{z},x^{4}P_{n}(P_{n})\rangle-h_{n}

\displaystyle=4z\left[a_{n+2}a_{n+1}+(b_{n+1}+b_{n})^{2}a_{n+1}+(a_{n+1}+b_{n}^{2}+a_{n})^{2}\right]h_{n}

\displaystyle\quad+4z\left[(b_{n}+b_{n-1})^{2}a_{n}+a_{n}a_{n-1}\right]h_{n}-h_{n}.

On the other hand, since

P_{n}^{\prime}(x)=nP_{n-1}(x)+\mathcal{O}(x^{n-2}),

we get

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{u}_{z},xP_{n}P_{n}^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\mathbf{u}_{z},xP_{n}\left(nP_{n-1}(x)+\mathcal{O}(x^{n-2})\right)\right\rangle=nh_{n}.

Thus, from

Moreover, from (

5.
Holonomic differential equation

Theorem 5.1

(Structure relation)
.

The monic polynomial sequence
(P_{n})_{n\geqslant 0},
orthogonal with respect to the linear functional
\mathbf{u}_{z},
satisfies the differential–difference relation

(5.1)

\displaystyle x\,P^{\prime}_{n+1}(x)=

\displaystyle(n+1)P_{n+1}(x)

\displaystyle+4z[a_{n+1}(T_{n+2}+T_{n})+T_{n+1}(R_{n+1}+R_{n})]P_{n}(x)

\displaystyle+4z[a_{n+1}a_{n}(R_{n+1}+R_{n-1})+T_{n+1}T_{n}]P_{n-1}(x)

\displaystyle+4za_{n}[a_{n-1}T_{n+1}+a_{n+1}T_{n-1}]P_{n-2}(x)

\displaystyle+4za_{n+1}a_{n}a_{n-1}a_{n-2}P_{n-3}(x).

Proof.

Since
(P_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
is a basis of the linear space of polynomials and since the degree of
x\,P^{\prime}_{n+1}(x)
is
n+1
, we can write

xP^{\prime}_{n+1}(x)=(n+1)P_{n+1}(x)+\sum_{k=0}^{n}\alpha_{n+1,k}P_{k}(x),

with

\alpha_{n+1,k}h_{k}=\langle\mathbf{u}_{z},xP^{\prime}_{n+1}P_{k}\rangle,\quad 0\leqslant k\leqslant n,

and
h_{k}=\langle\mathbf{u}_{z},P_{k}^{2}\rangle\neq 0
. According to the Pearson equation (
3.3
), we have

\displaystyle\alpha_{n+1,k}h_{k}

\displaystyle=\langle\mathbf{u}_{z},xP^{\prime}_{n+1}P_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mathbf{u}_{z},x(P_{n+1}P_{k})^{\prime}\rangle-\langle\mathbf{u}_{z},xP_{n+1}P^{\prime}_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mathbf{u}_{z},P_{n+1}[(4zx^{4}-1)P_{k}-xP^{\prime}_{k}]\rangle

\displaystyle=4z\langle\mathbf{u}_{z},P_{n+1}(x^{4}P_{k})\rangle.

From the orthogonality of
(P_{n+1})_{n\geq 0}
, we deduce
\alpha_{n+1,k}=0
for
0\leq k\leq n-4
. ∎

Next, we will obtain a lowering operator for
P_{n}
acting on the variable
x
.

Theorem 5.2

(Lowering operator)
.

For
n\geqslant 0
, define

\displaystyle C_{n}(x,z)=

\displaystyle 4z[a_{n+1}(T_{n+2}+T_{n})+T_{n+1}(R_{n+1}+R_{n})]

\displaystyle+\frac{4z}{a_{n}}[a_{n+1}a_{n}(R_{n+1}+R_{n-1})+T_{n+1}T_{n}](x-b_{n})

\displaystyle+\frac{4z}{a_{n-1}}[a_{n-1}T_{n+1}+a_{n+1}T_{n-1}][(x-b_{n-1})(x-b_{n})-a_{n}]

\displaystyle+4za_{n+1}\Big((x-b_{n-2})[(x-b_{n-1})(x-b_{n})-a_{n}]-(x-b_{n})\Big)

and

\displaystyle D_{n}(x,z)=

\displaystyle-(n+1)+\frac{4z}{a_{n}}[a_{n+1}a_{n}(R_{n+1}+R_{n-1})+T_{n+1}T_{n}]

\displaystyle+\frac{4z}{a_{n-1}}[a_{n-1}T_{n+1}+a_{n+1}T_{n-1}](x-b_{n-1})

\displaystyle+4za_{n+1}[(x-b_{n-2})(x-b_{n-1})-1].

Then, the SMOP
(P_{n}(_{n\geqslant 0}
with respect to the linear functional
\mathbf{u}_{z}
satisfies

(5.2)

L_{n}P_{n+1}(x)\,=\,P_{n}(x),\quad n\geqslant 0,

where
L_{n}=A_{n}(x,z)\dfrac{d}{dx}+B_{n}(x,z)
and

A_{n}(x,z)=\frac{x}{C_{n}(x,z)},\quad B_{n}(x,z)=\frac{D_{n}(x,z)}{C_{n}(x,z)}.

Proof.

Using the TTRR (
2.1
), we write

\displaystyle P_{n-1}(x)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a_{n}}[(x-b_{n})P_{n}(x)-P_{n+1}(x)],

\displaystyle P_{n-2}(x)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a_{n}a_{n-1}}\Big([(x-b_{n-1})(x-b_{n})-a_{n}]P_{n}(x)-(x-b_{n-1})P_{n+1}(x)\Big),

\displaystyle P_{n-3}(x)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a_{n}a_{n-1}a_{n-2}}\Big((x-b_{n-2})[(x-b_{n-1})(x-b_{n})-a_{n}]-a_{n-1}(x-b_{n})\Big)P_{n}(x)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{a_{n}a_{n-1}a_{n-2}}[(x-b_{n-2})(x-b_{n-1})-a_{n-1}]P_{n+1}(x).

Substituting these expressions in (
5.1
) and rearranging, we obtain the desired result.
∎

We note that the coefficients

in Theorem

\displaystyle+\dfrac{(x-b_{n-2})[(x-b_{n-1})((x-b_{n})-a_{n})-a_{n-1}(x-b_{n})]}{a_{n}a_{n-1}a_{n-2}}\beta_{n+1,n-3}\Big].

Remark 5.3
.

Recall that the TTRR (
2.2
) can be written in a matrix form as

. Now, define

\mathbf{L}=4z\left(\mathbf{J}^{4}\right)_{-}+\mathbf{N}
where

denotes the strictly lower triangular part of matrix

and

On the other hand, taking into account the relation between the coefficients of (

Notice that

\mathbf{J}(\partial_{x}\mathbf{P})=x\partial_{x}\mathbf{P}+\mathbf{P}

as well as

\displaystyle x\mathbf{J}(\partial_{x}\mathbf{P})

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{J}\mathbf{L})\,\mathbf{P},

\displaystyle x\mathbf{J}(\partial_{x}\mathbf{P})

\displaystyle=x(x\partial_{x}\mathbf{P}+\mathbf{P})=(\mathbf{L}\mathbf{J})\,\mathbf{P}+\mathbf{J}\,\mathbf{P}.

The above implies the compatibility condition of the Lax pair

Corollary 5.4

(Raising operator)
.

For

with respect to the linear functional

Using (

) and (

), we get

\displaystyle xP^{\prime}_{n+1}(x)=

\displaystyle-D_{n}(x,z)P_{n+1}(x)+C_{n}(x,z)P_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-D_{n}(x,z)P_{n+1}(x)+\frac{C_{n}(x,z)}{a_{n+1}}[(x-b_{n+1})P_{n+1}(x)-P_{n+2}(x)]

7.2.
Electrostatic interpretation

Let us consider the ladder operators introduced in
[
4
]
, which provide a more straightforward approach to obtaining the electrostatic interpretation of the zeros of orthogonal polynomials.

Assuming that the linear functional is associated with a weight function and under suitable integrability conditions, one can derive both raising and lowering operators for orthogonal polynomials with respect to a weight function supported on the real line. Additionally, a second-order linear differential equation satisfied by these polynomials can be obtained. Notably,
[
5
]
also explores the case of orthogonal polynomials associated with discontinuous weights.

In the specific case of the linear functional
\mathbf{u}_{z}
, whose associated monic orthogonal polynomials are denoted by
(P_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
, the lowering and raising operators are given by

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d}{dx}+{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(x)\right)P_{n}(x)=a_{n}\,{\mathcal{A}}_{n}(x)P_{n-1}(x),

\displaystyle\quad n\geqslant 1,

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{d}{dx}-{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(x)-v^{\prime}(x)\right)P_{n-1}(x)={\mathcal{A}}_{n-1}(x)P_{n}(x),

\displaystyle\quad n\geqslant 1,

where
\mathcal{A}_{n}(x)
and
\mathcal{B}_{n}(x)
are given in (
4.13
) and (
4.14
), respectively,
v(x)=zx^{4}
and
h_{n}=\left\langle{\mathbf{u}_{z},P_{n}^{2}}\right\rangle
. Moreover, the combination of the raising and lowering operators leads to the second-order linear differential equation

(7.2)

P^{\prime\prime}_{n}(x)+S(x;n)P_{n}^{\prime}(x)+Q(x;n)P_{n}(x)=0,\quad n\geqslant 1,

where

\displaystyle S(x:n)=-v^{\prime}(x)-\frac{d}{dx}\ln{\mathcal{A}}_{n}(x),

and

Q(x;n)=-{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}_{n}(x)-{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(x)\frac{d}{dx}\ln{\mathcal{A}}_{n}(x)-{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(x)[v^{\prime}(x)+{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(x)]+\frac{h_{n-1}}{h_{n}}{\mathcal{A}}_{n}(x){\mathcal{A}}_{n-1}(x).

Let us denote the zeros of
P_{n}(x)
by
(x_{n,k})_{k=1}^{n}
in increasing order, that is,

P_{n}(x_{n,k})=0,\quad 1\leqslant k\leqslant n,\quad\text{with}\quad x_{n,1}<x_{n,2}<\cdots<x_{n,n}.

Evaluating (
7.2
) at
x=x_{n,k}
, we obtain:

(7.3)

\frac{P_{n}^{\prime\prime}(x_{n,k})}{P_{n}^{\prime}(x_{n,k})}=-S_{n}(x_{n,k})=4zx_{n,k}^{3}+\left.\frac{d}{dx}\ln{\mathcal{A}}_{n}(x)\right|_{x=x_{n,k}},\quad n\geqslant 1.

Next, observe that

\ln{\mathcal{A}}_{n}(x)=\ln\frac{4h_{n}zx(x^{2}+b_{n}x+a_{n+1}+b_{n}^{2}+a_{n})+P_{n}^{2}(0)}{h_{n}x}.

Therefore

(7.4)

\frac{d}{dx}\ln{\mathcal{A}}_{n}(x)=\frac{3x^{2}+2b_{n}x+a_{n+1}+b_{n}^{2}+a_{n}}{x(x^{2}+b_{n}x+a_{n+1}+b_{n}^{2}+a_{n})+\dfrac{P_{n}^{2}(0)}{4zh_{n}}}-\frac{1}{x}.

Theorem 7.4
.

The zeros of
P_{n}(x)
can be interpreted as the equilibrium positions of
n
unit-charged particles distributed along the interval
(0,\infty)
under the influence of the potential

(7.5)

V_{n}(x)=zx^{4}+\ln\left|x(x^{2}+b_{n}x+a_{n+1}+b_{n}^{2}+a_{n})+\dfrac{P_{n}^{2}(0)}{4zh_{n}}\right|-\ln|x|.

Proof.

If we write
P_{n}(x)=(x-x_{n,1})\cdots(x-x_{n,n})
, then according to
[
11
, Ch. 10]
,

\frac{P_{n}^{\prime\prime}(x_{n,k})}{P_{n}^{\prime}(x_{n,k})}=\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle j=1}}{{j\neq k}}}^{n}\frac{2}{x_{n,k}-x_{n,j}},

and so, (
7.3
) and (
7.4
) yield

\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle j=1}}{{j\neq k}}}^{n}\frac{2}{x_{n,k}-x_{n,j}}-4zx_{n,k}^{3}-\frac{3x_{n,k}^{2}+2b_{n}x_{n,k}+a_{n+1}+b_{n}^{2}+a_{n}}{x_{n,k}(x_{n,k}^{2}+b_{n}x_{n,k}+a_{n+1}+b_{n}^{2}+a_{n})+\dfrac{P_{n}^{2}(0)}{4zh_{n}}}+\frac{1}{x_{n,k}}=0,

or equivalently,

\frac{\partial E_{n}}{\partial x_{n,k}}=0,\quad k=1,\ldots,n,

where the total energy of the system,
E_{n}:=E_{n}(x_{n,1},\ldots,x_{n,n})
, is given by

\displaystyle E_{n}

\displaystyle=-2\sum_{1\leqslant j<k\leqslant n}\ln|x_{n,k}-x_{n,j}|

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left[zx^{4}_{n,k}+\ln\left|x_{n,k}(x_{n,k}^{2}+b_{n}x_{n,k}+a_{n+1}+b_{n}^{2}+a_{n})+\dfrac{P_{n}^{2}(0)}{4zh_{n}}\right|-\ln|x_{n,k}|\right].

Consequently, the external potential
V_{n}(x)
is expressed as (
7.5
).
∎

8.
Concluding remarks.

In this contribution, we have analyzed some algebraic properties of SMOP associated with the so-called Druyvesteyn distribution function, which is a truncated Freud distribution function supported on the positive real semiaxis. The semiclassical character of such polynomials yields a system of nonlinear difference equations (Laguerre-Freud equations) that the coefficients of the TTRR satisfy. Raising and lowering operators are deduced and, as a consequence, a second-order linear differential equation for such a SMOP is deduced. An electrostatic interpretation of their zeros is deduced. Finally, some illustrative numerical tests concerning the behavior of the least and greatest zeros of these polynomials are presented.

In a work in progress, we are dealing with similar problems for SMOP associated with the generalized Druyvesteyn distribution function
\omega(x)=x^{p}e^{-zx^{4}},p>0,
supported on the positive real semi-axis. According to the symmetrization process described in
[
9
]
, we point out that the analysis of orthogonal polynomials with respect to the symmetric linear functional associated with the generalized Freud weight function
\omega(t)=|t|^{2p+1}e^{-zt^{8}}
supported on the real line is a particular case of one analyzed in
[
8
]
. In particular, the behavior of coefficients on the TTRR as well as the behavior of the largest zero described in our contribution can be deduced in a natural way from the symmetrization process.

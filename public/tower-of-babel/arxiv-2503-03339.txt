Maximal Graded Solvable Subalgebras of Simple Finite-Dimensional Vectorial Lie Superalgebras
Irina SHCHEPOCHKINA

Abstract
Here, in every simple finite-dimensional vectorial Lie superalgebra considered with the standard grading where every indeterminate is of degree 1, the maximal graded solvable subalgebras are classified over ℂ \mathbb{C} .

Introduction

Hereafter, the ground field is
{\mathbb{C}}
, all spaces are finite-dimensional. For the notation of simple Lie superalgebras and related basics of linear superalgebra, see
[
8
]
.

In
[
5
]
, Dynkin solved the problem (now classical) “describe maximal subalgebras of simple finite-dimensional Lie algebras”.
In
[
14
]
, the superanalog of the problem Dynkin solved for subalgebras of
any
type, was solved for the maximal
non-simple irreducible
subalgebras in
linear
(realised by means of linear operators or matrices) Lie superalgebras
{\mathfrak{gl}}(p|q)
and
{\mathfrak{sl}}(p|q)
, in their queer analogs
{\mathfrak{q}}(n)
and
{\mathfrak{sq}}(n)
, in
{\mathfrak{pe}}(n)
that preserves the non-degenerate odd bilinear form, in its supertraceless subalgebra
{\mathfrak{spe}}(n)
, in a version
{\mathfrak{pe}}_{\lambda}(n)
of
{\mathfrak{pe}}(n)
, and in the simple Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{osp}}(m|2n)
that preserves the non-degenerate even bilinear form.

In this note, there are described and classified maximal
graded
solvable Lie subsuperalgebras in the finite-dimensional simple
vectorial
(i.e., realised by means of vector fields) Lie superalgebras of the four analogs of the Cartan series of simple Lie algebras. These ambients are considered with their standard grading where every indeterminate is of degree 1. The particular case of
{\mathfrak{vect}}(0|2)\simeq{\mathfrak{sl}}(1|2)
was solved in
[
13
]
in terms of supermatrices. Here, the answer is the union of five propositions which require different prerequisites in various cases and are proved differently.

2
Related results

S. Lie was the first to try to classify maximal subalgebras of simple vectorial Lie algebras
with polynomial coefficients. To classify all maximal subalgebras seems
to be a wild (hopeless) problem: there are too many such subalgebras. Several researchers distinguished various classes of maximal subalgebras important in applications (e.g.,
[
9
,
16
]
) or dealt with cases feasible as, for example, are the classifications of the maximal graded subalgebras in finite-dimensional simple vectorial Lie superalgebras of Cartan type over
{\mathbb{C}}
, see
[
2
]
, and over algebraically closed fields of characteristic
p>3
, see
[
1
]
.

In
[
13
]
, the maximal,
not necessarily graded
, solvable Lie subsuperalgebras of
{\mathfrak{gl}}(p|q)
and
{\mathfrak{sl}}(p|q)
are described. These maximal solvable Lie subsuperalgebras are sometimes larger and sometimes smaller (see
[
13
, Theorem 3]
) than the super analog of the
Borel subalgebra
defined by Penkov as any Lie subsuperalgebra spanned by the maximal torus and root vectors corresponding to either all positive or all negative roots, see
[
10
]
; recall, for comparison, that the Borel subalgebras of simple Lie algebras are maximal solvable. The case of
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\prime}(0|4)\simeq{\mathfrak{psl}}(2|2)
solved here is not considered in
[
13
]
, where the closely related cases of
{\mathfrak{gl}}(2|2)
and
{\mathfrak{sl}}(2|2)
are solved, yielding, however, totally distinct types of maximal
non-graded
solvable subalgebras, see Proposition
6.1
and an elucidation in its proof.

Kuznetsov
[
7
]
classified maximal graded solvable subalgebras of finite codimension in simple vectorial Lie algebras over algebraically closed fields of characteristic
p>3
: a problem analogous to the one solved here for the graded solvable Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{s}}
in the case where
{\mathfrak{s}}_{-1}=0
, see Section
4
(i).

3
The ambients

There are four series of simple finite-dimensional vectorial Lie superalgebras; let us recall their description, see
[
8
]
containing also details of history.

Let
\Lambda(n)
or
\Lambda(\xi)
designate the Grassmann algebra generated by
\xi:=(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n})
with the
standard
grading (
\deg\xi_{i}=1
for all
i
) and parity
p
equal to the degree modulo 2. Here, the maximal
graded
solvable Lie subsuperalgebras of simple Lie superalgebras of vector fields on the
0|n
-dimensional supermanifold
{\mathcal{C}}^{0|n}
are classified, the ambient superalgebras being considered with their
{\mathbb{Z}}
-grading corresponding to the standard grading of
\Lambda(n)
, the algebra of functions on
{\mathcal{C}}^{0|n}
. This grading, called
Weisfeiler
grading, is associated with the
Weisfeiler filtration
constructed by means of the maximal subalgebra consisting of the fields vanishing at the origin, see, e.g.,
[
3
]
. Note that the Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{svect}}(0|n)
of divergence-free vector fields has a filtered deformation
\widetilde{{\mathfrak{svect}}}(0|n)
which can be endowed with
{\mathbb{Z}}
-gradings, but these gradings are not Weisfeiler gradings. In
[
2
]
, a natural
{\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}}
-grading of
\widetilde{{\mathfrak{svect}}}(0|n)
, induced by the standard
{\mathbb{Z}}
-grading of
{\mathfrak{svect}}(0|n)
, is considered and the maximal subalgebras of
\widetilde{{\mathfrak{svect}}}(0|n)
are described for
n
even.

Let
{\mathfrak{vect}}(0|n):={\mathfrak{der}}\,\Lambda(n)
, also denoted
{\mathfrak{vect}}(\xi)
, be the Lie superalgebra of superderivations of
\Lambda(n)
, its elements can be expressed as
\sum f_{i}\partial_{i}
, where
f_{i}\in\Lambda(n)
and
\partial_{i}\xi_{j}:=\delta_{ij}
. The symbol
{\mathfrak{b}}(\xi)

designates the subalgebra
{\mathfrak{b}}\subset{\mathfrak{vect}}(\xi)
realized in coordinates
\xi
.

Let
{\mathfrak{svect}}(0|n):=\{D\in{\mathfrak{vect}}(0|n)\mid\mathop{\text{\rm div}}\nolimits D=0\}
, where the
divergence
is

\mathop{\text{\rm div}}\nolimits\left(\sum f_{i}\partial_{i}\right):=\sum(-1)^{p(f_{i})}\partial_{i}(f_{i}).

Let
{\mathfrak{po}}(0|n)
be the Poisson Lie superalgebra realized on the superspace
\Lambda(n)
generated for
n=2k+l
by

\displaystyle\xi:=(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}),\qquad\eta:=(\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{k}),\qquad\zeta:=(\zeta_{1},\dots,\zeta_{l}),

(3.1)

with the Poisson bracket

\{f,g\}:=(-1)^{p(f)}\left(\sum_{1\leq i\leq k}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial\xi_{i}}\frac{\partial g}{\partial{\eta_{i}}}+\frac{\partial f}{\partial\eta_{i}}\frac{\partial g}{\partial{\xi_{i}}}\right)+\sum_{1\leq j\leq l}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\zeta_{j}}\frac{\partial g}{\partial{\zeta_{j}}}\right).

Let
{\mathfrak{h}}(0|n):=\mathop{\mathrm{{Span}}}\nolimits\{H_{f}\mid f\in\Lambda(n)\}
, where

H_{f}:=(-1)^{p(f)}\left(\sum_{1\leq i\leq k}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial\xi_{i}}\partial_{\eta_{i}}+\frac{\partial f}{\partial\eta_{i}}\partial_{\xi_{i}}\right)+\sum_{1\leq j\leq l}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\zeta_{j}}\partial_{\zeta_{j}}\right).

Clearly,
{\mathfrak{h}}(0|n)
is the quotient of
{\mathfrak{po}}(0|n)
modulo center (spanned by constants). Although in this note we only need linear algebra and no geometry, recall, for the sake of completeness, that
{\mathfrak{h}}(0|n)
preserves a non-degenerate super-anti-symmetric closed differential 2-form
\omega
on a
(0|n)
-dimensional supermanifold, see
[
8
]
.

Hereafter,
{\mathfrak{g}}
designates one of the Lie superalgebras
{\mathfrak{vect}}(0|n)
(resp.
{\mathfrak{svect}}(0|n)
or
{\mathfrak{h}}(0|n)
) in their standard gradings induced by the standard
{\mathbb{Z}}
-grading of
\Lambda(n)
for
n>1
(resp.
n>2
or
n>3
) when these Lie superalgebras are simple, except
{\mathfrak{h}}(0|n)
.
Note that the description of maximal graded solvable subalgebras in
{\mathfrak{h}}(0|n)
yields the description of same type of subalgebras in the simple derived algebra
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\prime}(0|n)
; we describe the elements
H_{f}
of
{\mathfrak{h}}(0|n)
in terms of generating functions
f
.

Let
\Xi=\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{n}
, let
t
be an extra indeterminate of parity
n\pmod{2}
and
\deg(t)=0
. The Lie superalgebras
\widetilde{{\mathfrak{svect}}}(0|2n):=(1+t\Xi){\mathfrak{svect}}(0|2n)
are isomorphic for any
t\neq 0
, so we assume
t=1
. The standard
{\mathbb{Z}}
-grading of
{\mathfrak{svect}}(0|n)
induces a
{\mathbb{Z}}/n
-grading of
\widetilde{{\mathfrak{svect}}}(0|n)
, the degrees being represented by integers from
-1
to
n-2
. The
{\mathbb{Z}}
- or
{\mathbb{Z}}/n
-grading of the Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{s}}
is said to be
compatible
(with parity) if
{\mathfrak{s}}_{\bar{0}}=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}{\mathfrak{s}}_{2i}
. Clearly,

\displaystyle\widetilde{{\mathfrak{svect}}}_{i}(0|n)=\begin{cases}{\mathfrak{svect}}_{i}(0|n)&\text{for $n$ even},\\
{\mathfrak{svect}}_{i}(0|n)\otimes{\mathbb{C}}[t]&\text{for $n$ odd},\end{cases}\qquad\text{for $i\neq-1$,}

\displaystyle\widetilde{{\mathfrak{svect}}}_{-1}(0|n)=\mathop{\mathrm{{Span}}}\nolimits\bigl(\tilde{\partial}_{1},\dots,\tilde{\partial}_{n}\bigr),\qquad\text{where $\tilde{\partial}_{i}:=(1+t\Xi)\partial_{i}$}.

Here, we will not consider deformations with odd parameter.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
{\mathfrak{s}}:=\bigoplus_{i\geq-1}{\mathfrak{s}}_{i}
be a compatible with parity
{\mathbb{Z}}
- or
{\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}}
-grading of a Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{s}}
. Then,
{\mathfrak{s}}
is solvable if and only if so is
{\mathfrak{s}}_{0}
.

Proof.

In
[
6
, Proposition 1.3.3]
, based on the description of irreducible modules over solvable Lie superalgebras proved later, see
[
11
]
, it is proved that “the Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
is solvable if and only if its even component
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}
is solvable”. (Note that this statement, true over any field of characteristic not 2, does not hold over fields of characteristic 2; for examples where
{\mathfrak{g}}
is simple while
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}
is solvable, see
[
3
,
4
]
.) Since the grading of
{\mathfrak{s}}
is compatible with parity, then
{\mathfrak{s}}_{\bar{0}}
contains a nilpotent ideal
{\mathfrak{n}}:=\bigoplus_{i>0}{\mathfrak{s}}_{2i}
and
{\mathfrak{s}}_{\bar{0}}/{\mathfrak{n}}={\mathfrak{s}}_{0}
. So,
{\mathfrak{s}}
is solvable if and only if so is
{\mathfrak{s}}_{0}
.
∎

Definitions needed in cases (ii) and (iii) (see

The graded Lie (super)algebra

{\mathfrak{b}}=\bigoplus_{k\geqslant-d}{\mathfrak{b}}_{k}

is said to be
transitive
if for all
k\geq 0
we have

\{x\in{\mathfrak{b}}_{k}\mid[x,{\mathfrak{b}}_{-}]=0\}=0,\qquad\text{where ${\mathfrak{b}}_{-}:=\oplus_{k<0}\,{\mathfrak{b}}_{k}$}.

The maximal transitive
{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded Lie (super)algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}=\bigoplus_{k\geqslant-1}\ {\mathfrak{g}}_{k}

{\mathfrak{g}}_{-1}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}

Cartan prolong

— the result of Cartan prolongation — of the pair
({\mathfrak{g}}_{-1},{\mathfrak{g}}_{0})
and is denoted by
({\mathfrak{g}}_{-1},{\mathfrak{g}}_{0})_{*}
.

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}:={\mathfrak{vect}}(0|n)
or
{\mathfrak{svect}}(0|n)
or
{\mathfrak{h}}(0|n)
. Let, besides,

. Define the

in

as

-graded subalgebra

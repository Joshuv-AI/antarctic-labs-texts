A lower bound on the minimum weight of some geometric codes
Bence Csajbók, Giovanni Longobardi, Giuseppe Marino, Rocco Trombetti

Abstract
The p p -ary code associated with the incidence structure of points and t t -spaces in a projective space {PG} ⁡ ( m , q ) \PG(m,q) , where q = p h q=p^{h} , is the 𝔽 p {\mathbb{F}}_{p} -subspace generated by the incidence vectors of the blocks of this design. The dual of this code consists of all vectors orthogonal to every codeword of the original code. In contrast to the codes derived from point–subspace incidences, the minimum weight of the corresponding dual codes is generally unknown, which makes the problem more challenging. In 2008 Lavrauw, Storme and Van de Voorde proved the following reduction: the minimum weight of the dual of the code derived from point and t t -space incidences in {PG} ⁡ ( m , q ) \PG(m,q) is the same as the minimum weight of the dual of the code derived from point and line incidences in {PG} ⁡ ( m − t + 1 , q ) \PG(m-t+1,q) . After a series of works by Delsarte (1970), Assmus and Key (1992), Calkin, Key and De Resmini (1999), the best known lower bound for the case of point-line incidences was established in [B. Bagchi and P. Inamdar: Projective geometric codes, J. Combin. Theory Ser. A, 99(1) (2002), 128–142]. The problem of determining the minimum weight of these codes admits a natural geometric interpretation in terms of multisets of points in a projective space which meet each line in 0 0 modulo p p points. In this paper, by adopting this geometrical perspective and exploiting certain polynomial techniques from [S. Ball, A. Blokhuis, A. Gács, P. Sziklai, Zs. Weiner: On linear codes whose weights and length have a common divisor, Adv. Math., 211 (2007), 94–104], we prove a substantial improvement of the Bagchi–Inamdar bound in the case where h > 1 h>1 and m , p > 2 m,p>2 .

Introduction

Let
\Sigma
be either the projective space
\PG(m,q)
, or the affine space
\AG(m,q)

q=p^{h}

Let

The

{\mathbb{F}}_{p}

The

{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{v}

By Assmus and Key
[
2
, Theorem 5.7.9]
, Calkin, Key and De Resmini
[
8
, Proposition 1]
and Delsarte
[

(q+p)q^{m-2}

(1)

{\mathcal{C}}_{\AG}(m,q)^{\perp}
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{\PG}(m,q)^{\perp}

[
3
, Theorem 3]
, to

2\left(\frac{q^{m}-1}{q-1}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)+\frac{1}{p}\right).

By
[
11
, Theorems 10 and 11]
, the minimum weight codewords of the dual of the code arising from incidences between points and
k
-spaces of
\PG(m,q)
are the same as the minimum weight codewords of
{\mathcal{C}}_{\PG}(m-k+1,q)^{\perp}

In
[
9

De Boeck and Van de Voorde increased the lower bound on the minimum weight of
{\mathcal{C}}_{\PG}(2,p^{2})^{\perp}
,
p\geq 5
prime, from
2q-2p+2
to
2q-2p+5
.

If

is even, the lower bound in
(
1
)
is sharp; see
[
8
, Corollary 1]
by Calkin, Key and De Resmini.
In fact, if
q

[

]

In the following, we recall the geometrical link between the codewords of

Let

is an ordinary set. Moreover, for an ordinary set, the weight

of its characteristic vector

.

(0\bmod p)
-multiset

Let
{\mathcal{M}}=({\mathcal{S}},\mu)
be a multiset of
\Sigma
. Since we are interested in a lower bound on the size of multisets meeting lines in
0\bmod p

, then by counting the lines of
\Sigma
through

|\mathcal{M}|\geq 1+(p-1)\frac{q^{m}-1}{q-1}.

(6)

In terms of multisets, Theorem

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}=({\mathcal{S}},\mu)
be a non-empty
(0\bmod p)
-type multiset of points in

Proof.

Firstly, note that for

6
). Hence, let us suppose

.
By counting points of
{\mathcal{M}}

We will use the same type of representation for

For

{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathrm{PG}}(m,q)^{\perp}

w(\bar{\mathbf{s}})=w(\mathbf{s})
and
\sigma(\bar{\mathbf{s}})=\sigma(\mathbf{s})
; hence, the minimum weight of
{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathrm{AG}}(m,q)^{\perp}
is at least the minimum weight of
{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathrm{PG}}(m,q)^{\perp}
.

3
Examples attaining the lower bound

This subsection is devoted to the construction of a family of multisets in
\AG(m,q)
that contains at least one point of multiplicity
1
, such that every affine line intersects the multiset in a number of points divisible by
p
, and whose size attains the bound given in (
7
).

Let
V
denote the
(m+1)
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-vector space underlying
\Sigma=\PG(m,q)
. If
q=p^{h}
, then we can view
V
as an
h(m+1)
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-vector space. If
W
is an
r
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-subspace of
V
, then the set of points
L_{W}
of
\Sigma
defined by non zero vectors of
W
is called an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-
linear set
of
rank

r
. It is straightforward to see that
|L_{W}|\leq\frac{p^{r}-1}{p-1}
. Lines of
\Sigma
correspond to sets of points defined by non-zero vectors of
2
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces of
V
, which can be viewed as
2h
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-subspaces.
By Grassmann’s Identity, if
U
is an
(h(m-1)+1)
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-subspace of
V
, then it meets every
2
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace non-trivially. If
T
is any
2
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace and
\ell
is the line of
\Sigma
defined by non zero vectors of
T
, then it follows that
\ell\cap L_{U}\neq\emptyset
. Moreover, it can be seen that
|\ell\cap L_{U}|\equiv 1\pmod{p}
, see
[
7
]
and
[
15
, Proposition 2.2]
.

The subsequent results are crucial for achieving our goal.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
L_{U}
and
L_{W}
be any two
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linear sets of rank
h(m-1)+1
in
\PG(m,q)
,
q=p^{h}
,
h>1
. Put
{\mathcal{S}}=L_{U}\triangle L_{W}
(the symmetric difference of
L_{U}
and
L_{W}
) and define
\mu
as follows:

\mu(x)=\begin{cases}t&\textnormal{if}\,x\in L_{U}\setminus L_{W}\\
p-t&\textnormal{if}\,x\in L_{W}\setminus L_{U},\end{cases}

(16)

where
1\leq t\leq p-1
is fixed. Then
{\mathcal{M}}=({\mathcal{S}},\mu)
is a multiset meeting each line of
\PG(m,q)
in
0\bmod p
points.

Proof.

Let
\ell
be any line of
\PG(m,q)
and put
a=|\ell\cap(L_{U}\setminus L_{W})|
,
b=|\ell\cap(L_{U}\cap L_{W})|
and
c=|\ell\cap(L_{W}\setminus L_{U})|
. From above it holds that
a+b\equiv 1\equiv b+c\pmod{p}
. Moreover, we also have the following:

|{\mathcal{M}}\cap\ell|=ta+(p-t)c\equiv 0\pmod{p}.

∎

An
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-subspace
U
of
H\cong{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{m}
is said to be an
(u,v)_{p}
-
evasive subspace
if the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace
\langle U\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
has dimension at least
u
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
and the
u
‐dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces of
H
meet
U
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
‐subspaces of dimension at most
v
. For more details see
[
5
]
. We will need the following result on
(m-1,(m-2)h)_{p}
-evasive subspaces:

Result 3.2

(
[
5
, Special case of Corollary 4.4]
)
.

Let
U
be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-subspace of
H\cong{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{m}
,
q=p^{h}
, and assume that
U
meets every
(m-1)
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
H
in an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-subspaces of dimension at most
(m-2)h
. Then
\dim_{p}(U)\leq(m-1)h-1
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
f\colon{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{m-1}\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-multilinear map,
m\geq 2
. In
V={\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{m+1}
put

U=\{(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{m-2},f(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{m-2}),y)\colon x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{m-2}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q},\,y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}\},

(17)

W=\{(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{m-2},y,0)\colon x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{m-2}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q},\,y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}\}.

(18)

Define
{\mathcal{M}}=(L_{U}\triangle L_{W},\mu)
, a multiset of
\PG(m,q)
, with
\mu
as in Proposition
3.1
with
t=1
.

(1)

There is a hyperplane disjoint from
{\mathcal{M}}
.

(2)

{\mathcal{M}}
is of size

(p-1)(q^{m-1}+q^{m-2})+q^{m-2}

if and only if

|L_{U}\cap L_{W}|=q^{m-2}\frac{q-1}{p-1}+\frac{q^{m-2}-1}{q-1}.

Proof.

(1)
First, observe that
L_{W}=\PG(H,{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
where
H
is an
m
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-vector space. Hence,
L_{U}\cap L_{W}=L_{U\cap H}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linear set of rank
(m-1)h
. By Grassmann’s Identity,
L_{U\cap H}
meets hyperplanes of
L_{W}
in linear sets of rank at least
(m-2)h
. By Result
3.2
, it follows that
L_{U\cap H}
cannot meet every hyperplane of
L_{W}
in an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linear set of rank exactly
(m-2)h
.
Hence,
L_{U\cap H}
meets at least a hyperplane, say
\Pi
of
L_{W}
, in an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linear set of rank at least
(m-2)h+1
. Then, again by Grassmann’s Identity,
\Pi
must be fully contained in
L_{U\cap H}
.
To prove
(1)
, it is enough to find a hyperplane of
\PG(m,q)
through
\Pi
which does not meet
{\mathcal{M}}
. Assume for the contrary,
that each hyperplane
{\mathcal{H}}
of
\PG(m,q)
through
\Pi
and distinct from
L_{W}\colon X_{m}=0
meets
{\mathcal{M}}
. Since
{\mathcal{M}}\cap{\mathcal{H}}
is a multiset of
{\mathcal{H}}\setminus\Pi\cong\AG(m-1,q)
meeting every line in
0\bmod p
points and having only points of multiplicity
1
, by applying Theorem
2.1
, we have that

q^{m-1}=|L_{U}\setminus L_{W}|=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Pi\subset{\mathcal{H}}\\
{\mathcal{H}}\neq L_{W}\end{subarray}}|{\mathcal{M}}\cap{\mathcal{H}}|\geq q((p-1)(q^{m-2}+q^{m-3})+q^{m-3}),

a contradiction.

To prove
(2)
, it is enough to note that, by construction,

|{\mathcal{M}}|=(p-1)|L_{W}\setminus(L_{U}\cap L_{W})|+|L_{U}\setminus L_{W}|

and
|L_{U}\setminus L_{W}|=q^{m-1}
.
∎

By Proposition
3.3
, we can get an upper bound on the size of an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linear set of rank
hm
in
\PG(m,q)
,
m\geq 2
,
q=p^{h}
, and
h>1
. Indeed, the following holds.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
L
be a
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linear set of rank
hm
in
\PG(m,q)
,
q=p^{h}
. Then,

|L|\leq q^{m-1}\frac{q-1}{p-1}+\frac{q^{m-1}-1}{q-1}.

Proof.

After a suitable collineation, for any
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linear set
L
of rank
hm
in
\PG(m,q)
we may assume that
(0:0:\ldots:0:1)\notin L
and hence
L
can be written as
L=L_{U}
, where

U=\{(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{m-1},f(x_{0},\ldots,x_{m-1})):x_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}\}

U
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-subspace of dimension
hm
, and hence
f\colon{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{m}\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is additive.
Let us consider the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linear sets
L_{\overline{U}}
and
L_{\overline{W}}
of
\PG(m+1,q)
,
where

\overline{U}=\{(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{m-1},f(x_{0},\ldots,x_{m-1}),y):x_{0},\ldots,x_{m-1}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q},y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}\},

\overline{W}=\{(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{m-1},y,0):x_{0},\ldots,x_{m-1}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q},y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}\}.

Then, by Proposition
3.1
with
t=1
, the multiset
\bar{{\mathcal{M}}}=(L_{\overline{U}}\triangle L_{\overline{W}},\mu)
with
\mu
as in (
16
)
meets any line of
\PG(m+1,q)
in
0\bmod p
points.
By Proposition
3.3

(2)
,

|L_{U}|=|L_{\overline{U}}\cap L_{\overline{W}}|\leq q^{m-1}\frac{q-1}{p-1}+\frac{q^{m-1}-1}{q-1},

hence the result follows.
∎

The trivial upper bound for the size of an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linear set of rank
hm
in
\PG(m,q)
,
q=p^{h}
, is
\frac{p^{hm}-1}{p-1}=\frac{q^{m}-1}{p-1}
.
It is easy to see that our bound is stronger, indeed
\frac{q^{m}-1}{p-1}>q^{m-1}\frac{q-1}{p-1}+\frac{q^{m-1}-1}{q-1}
follows from

(q-1)(q^{m}-1)-(q^{m-1}(q-1)^{2}+(q^{m-1}-1)(p-1))=(q-p)(q^{m-1}-1)>0.

In the remainder, we apply Propositions
3.1
and
3.3
to show that in
\AG(m,q)
there exist
(0\bmod p)
-multisets having at least one point with multiplicity
1,
with size
(p-1)(q^{m-1}+q^{m-2})+q^{m-2}
.

To this aim we first recall that an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linearized polynomial
f(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}a_{i}x^{p^{i}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]
is called
scattered
if

\left|\left\{\frac{f(x)}{x}\colon x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}\right\}\right|=\frac{q-1}{p-1},

for a survey on scattered polynomials, see
[
13
]
.

Now, assume
L_{U}\subset\PG(m,q)
to be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linear set whose underlying
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-vector space is

U=\{(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{m-2},f(x_{0}),y)\colon x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{m-2}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q},\,y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}\},

where
f
is any scattered
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linearized polynomial. Also, consider the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-subspace
W
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{m+1}
as in (
18
). It is easy to see that
L_{U}\cap L_{W}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linear set contained in the hyperplane of
\PG(m,q)
with equation
X_{m}=0
and it is a cone with basis a maximum scattered
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linear set on the line with equations
X_{1}=X_{2}=\ldots=X_{m-2}=X_{m}=0
and vertex an
(m-3)
-dimensional subspace with equations
X_{0}=X_{m-1}=X_{m}=0
. Then,

|L_{U}\cap L_{W}|=q^{m-2}\frac{q-1}{p-1}+\frac{q^{m-2}-1}{q-1},

and
{\mathcal{M}}=(L_{U}\triangle L_{W},\mu)
as defined in (
16
) is a multiset of
\AG(m,q)
whose size attains the bound in (
7
). The fact that this construction can be embedded in
\AG(m,q)
follows from Proposition
3.3

(1)
.

We note that the characteristic vector of the construction above has the same weight as the one associated with the point set constructed by Lavrauw, Storme and Van de Voorde in
[
12
, Theorem 4.14]
. In particular, if
f(X)=X^{p}
, then our construction has the same support as the one in
[
12
, Theorem 4.14]
. Moreover, if
p=2
the example falls into the family exhibited by Calkin, Key and De Resmini in
[
8
]
. Indeed, in such a case
L_{U}
is equivalent to a cone with base the
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
-linear set
\{\langle(x,x^{2^{s}})\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{h}}}\,:\,x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{h}}^{*}\}\subseteq\PG(1,2^{h})
, with
\gcd(s,h)=1,
which is the set of directions determined by the points of a translation hyperoval of
\PG(2,2^{h})
(see
[
14
]
). In such a case, our construction provides a set which is equivalent to the one constructed by induction in
[
8
, Note
1
]
.

4
A lower bound on the minimum weight of
{\mathcal{C}}_{\PG}(m,q)^{\perp}

In this section, we will show a new lower bound for the minimum weight of a codeword
{\bf s}
of
{\mathcal{C}}_{\PG}(m,q)^{\perp}
and for the minimum size of
(0\bmod\,p)
-multisets of
\PG(m,q)
.
Firstly, we extend of
[
8
, Proposition 1]
to multisets.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
w_{m-1}
denote the minimum weight of the code
{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}_{\mathrm{PG}}(m-1,q)
and let
\sigma_{m-1}
denote the minimum size of a non-empty
(0\bmod p)
-multiset in
\PG(m-1,q)
,
q=p^{h}
.
If
{\mathcal{M}}=({\mathcal{S}},\mu)
is a
(0\bmod p)
-multiset of
\PG(m,q)
which meets every hyperplane, then

|{\mathcal{S}}|\geq qw_{m-1}\quad\textnormal{ and }\quad|{\mathcal{M}}|\geq q\sigma_{m-1}.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}=({\mathcal{S}},\mu)
be a
(0\bmod p)
-multiset of
\PG(m,q)
such that the hyperplanes of
\PG(m,q)
meet
{\mathcal{S}}
in
0<n_{1}<n_{2}<\cdots<n_{k}
points, and there are
z_{i}
hyperplanes meeting
{\mathcal{S}}
in
n_{i}
points.
Clearly

z_{1}+z_{2}+\cdots+z_{k}=\frac{q^{m+1}-1}{q-1}.

(19)

By double counting the pairs
(x,\mathcal{H})
where
x\in{\mathcal{S}}
and
\mathcal{H}
is a hyperplane incident with
x
, we obtain:

n_{1}z_{1}+n_{2}z_{2}+\cdots+n_{k}z_{k}=|{\mathcal{S}}|\frac{q^{m}-1}{q-1}.

(20)

Multiplying (
19
) by
n_{1}
and subtracting (
20
) we get

|{\mathcal{S}}|\geq n_{1}\frac{q^{m+1}-1}{q^{m}-1}\geq n_{1}q.

Since
n_{1}
is at least
w_{m-1}
the first part of the statement is proved.
To prove the second part, assume that hyperplanes meet
{\mathcal{M}}
in
0<n_{1}<\ldots<n_{k}
points (counting the points with their multiplicities) and that there are
z_{i}
hyperplanes meeting
{\mathcal{M}}
in
n_{i}
points. Clearly (
19
) holds and by double counting the
(x,\mathcal{H})
pairs (counting each of them
\mu(x)
times), we obtain (
20
) with
|{\mathcal{M}}|
on its right side (instead of
|{\mathcal{S}}|
). Similarly to the arguments from above, now we obtain

|{\mathcal{M}}|\geq n_{1}q
and the result follows noting that
n_{1}\geq\sigma_{m-1}
.
∎

We will need the following triviality.

Proposition 4.2
.

The minimum weight
w_{1}
of
{\mathcal{C}}_{\PG}(1,q)^{\perp}
,
q=p^{h}
, is
2
and

\sigma_{1}=\min\left\{\sigma(\mathbf{s}):\mathbf{s}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\PG}(1,q)^{\perp}\setminus\{\boldsymbol{0}\}\right\}=p.

In the next proof we will need the following definition.

Definition 4.3
.

For a multiset
{\mathcal{M}}=({\mathcal{S}},\mu)
of
\Sigma
we define
(p-1){\mathcal{M}}
as the multiset
({\mathcal{S}},\bar{\mu})
of
\Sigma
, where
for each
x\in{\mathcal{S}}
,
\bar{\mu}(x)\in\{1,2,\ldots,p-1\}
such that
\bar{\mu}(x)\equiv(p-1)\mu(x)\pmod{p}
.

Theorem 4.4
.

If
{\bf s}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\PG}(m,q)^{\perp}
and
q>p
, then

w({\bf s})\geq 2(q^{m-1}(p-1)/p+q^{m-2}).

Proof.

First, take a codeword
{\bf s}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\AG}(m,q)^{\perp}
and let
{\mathcal{M}}
be the multiset associated with
{\bf s}
.
Scaling the codeword
{\bf s}
does not alter its weight. Thus we may assume the existence of a point
x\in{\mathcal{M}}
with multiplicity
1
. If there are no points with multiplicity
p-1
, then counting the points on lines through
x
gives

w({\bf s})\geq 1+2(q^{m-1}+q^{m-2}+\ldots+q+1),

and hence the assertion follows.
If there is a point with multiplicity
p-1
, the multiset
(p-1){\mathcal{M}}
has a point of multiplicity
1
. Since
|{\mathcal{M}}|+|(p-1){\mathcal{M}}|
is
p
times the weight of
\bf{s}
, by (
4
) (applied twice) we obtain

2((p-1)q^{m-1}+pq^{m-2})\leq|{\mathcal{M}}|+|(p-1){\mathcal{M}}|=p\,w({\bf s}),

(21)

from which the result follows.
For the origin of the trick applied in (
21
) see
[
1
, Result 3.2 and Remark 3.4]
.

Now, let us assume
{\bf s}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\PG}(m,q)^{\perp}
and let
{\mathcal{M}}=({\mathcal{S}},\mu)
be the multiset associated with it. If there is a hyperplane of
\PG(m,q)
disjoint from
{\mathcal{M}}
, the result follows from the first part of the proof. Now, suppose that each hyperplane of
\PG(m,q)
meets
{\mathcal{M}}
and hence
{\mathcal{S}}
.
Take first
m=2
. Then, by Lemma
4.1
and Proposition
4.2
,

w({\bf s})=|{\mathcal{S}}|\geq 2q\geq 2(q(p-1)/p+1),

as required. Now suppose that
m>2
and, by the induction hypothesis, assume that the weight
w_{m-1}
of each non-zero codeword of
{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}_{\PG}(m-1,q)
is at least
2(q^{m-2}(p-1)/p+q^{m-3})
. Thus, by Lemma
4.1

w({\bf s})=|{\mathcal{S}}|\geq qw_{m-1}\geq 2(q^{m-1}(p-1)/p+q^{m-2}),

which completes the proof.
∎

Let
{\mathcal{C}}^{(k)}_{\PG}(m,q)
be the
p
-ary code associated with the design
\mathcal{D}^{(k)}_{\PG}(m,q)
of points and
k
-spaces of
\PG(m,q)
, i.e. the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-subspace generated by the incidence vectors of the blocks of the corresponding design. As said before, the minimum weight of
\mathcal{C}^{(k)}_{\mathrm{PG}}(m,q)^{\perp}
is equal to the minimum weight of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{PG}}(m-k+1)^{\perp}
, see
[
11
, Theorem 10]
. Then, as a corollary of Theorem
4.4
, we get the following result.

Corollary 4.5
.

If
\boldsymbol{s}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{(k)}_{\PG}(m,q)
and
q>p
, then

w({\bf s})\geq 2(q^{m-k}(p-1)/p+q^{m-k-1}).

We remark that Corollary
4.5
improves the bounds proved in
[
11
, Theorems 14 and 15]
.

In the next propositions we will frequently use
\lambda{\bf s}
, where
{\bf s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})
is a vector with coordinates in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
and
\lambda
is an integer. Of course, the
i
-the coordinate of
\lambda{\bf s}
is defined to be the unique element of
\{0,1,\ldots,p-1\}
congruent to
\lambda s_{i}
modulo
p
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
{\bf s}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\PG}(m,q)^{\perp}
with
q=p^{h}
,
h>1
and
p
an odd prime power. Let
1\leq t\leq\frac{p-1}{2}
. If the non-zero coordinates of
\bf s
are equal to
t
or
p-t
, then

\sigma({\bf s})\geq(p-1)(q^{m-1}+q^{m-2})+q^{m-2}.

Proof.

Firstly, we prove the result for a codeword in
{\bf s}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\AG}(m,q)^{\perp}
. Let us suppose that
{\bf s}
has all non-zero coordinates equal to
t>1
. Let
\lambda
be an integer such that
\lambda t\equiv 1\pmod{p}
, then
\sigma({\bf s})\geq\sigma(\lambda{\bf s})\geq(p-1)(q^{m-1}+q^{m-2})+q^{m-2}
(by Theorem
1.2
). A similar argument applies if all the non-zero coordinates of
{\bf s}
are equal to
p-t
. If
{\bf s}
has a coordinate equal to
1
, then the result follows again from Theorem
1.2
.
If this is not the case, then denote by
x_{i}
the number of coordinates in
{\bf s}
equal to
i,
with
i\in\{t,p-t\}
. Then
\sigma({\bf s})=tx_{t}+(p-t)x_{p-t}
. Let
\lambda,\mu
be integers such that
\lambda t\equiv 1\pmod{p}
and
\mu t\equiv p-1\pmod{p}
, respectively. Then we get

\sigma(\lambda{\bf s})=x_{t}+(p-1)x_{p-t},

\sigma(\mu{\bf s})=(p-1)x_{t}+x_{p-t}.

If
\sigma({\bf s})\geq\sigma(\lambda{\bf s})
or
\sigma({\bf s})\geq\sigma(\mu{\bf s})
, the result directly follows from Theorem
1.2
. This will always occurs: indeed, if otherwise
\sigma({\bf s})<\sigma(\lambda{\bf s})
and
\sigma({\bf s})<\sigma(\mu{\bf s})
, we have

\sigma({\bf s})=tx_{t}+(p-t)x_{p-t}<x_{t}+(p-1)x_{p-t},

\sigma({\bf s})=tx_{t}+(p-t)x_{p-t}<(p-1)x_{t}+x_{p-t},

which leads to
x_{t}<x_{p-t}<x_{t}
, a contradiction.

Now, let us assume
{\bf s}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\PG}(m,q)^{\perp}
and let
{\mathcal{M}}
be the multiset associated with it. If there is a hyperplane of
\PG(m,q)
disjoint from
{\mathcal{M}}
, the result follows from the first part of the proof. Now, suppose that each hyperplane of
\PG(m,q)
meet
{\mathcal{M}}
.
Take first
m=2
, then by Lemma
4.1
and Proposition
4.2
,

\sigma({\bf s})=|{\mathcal{M}}|\geq pq\geq(p-1)(q+1)+1,

as required. Now suppose that
m>2
and, by induction hypothesis, we may assume that for any non-zero codeword in
{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}_{\PG}(m-1,q)
the sum of its coordinates is at least

. Thus, by Lemma
4.1

\sigma({\bf s})=|{\mathcal{M}}|\geq q\sigma_{m-1}\geq(p-1)(q^{m-1}+q^{m-2})+q^{m-2},

which completes the proof.
∎

Non-reciprocal interactions preserve the universality class of Potts model
Soumya K. Saha and P. K. Mohanty

Abstract
We study the q q -state Potts model on a square lattice with directed nearest-neighbor spin-spin interactions that are inherently non-reciprocal. Both equilibrium and non-equilibrium dynamics are investigated. Analytically, we demonstrate that non-reciprocal interactions do not alter the critical exponents of the model under equilibrium dynamics. In contrast, numerical simulations with selfish non-equilibrium dynamics reveal distinctive behavior. For q = 2 q=2 (non-reciprocal non-equilibrium Ising model), the critical exponents remain consistent with those of the equilibrium Ising universality class. However, for q = 3 q=3 and q = 4 q=4 , the critical exponents vary continuously. Remarkably, a super-universal scaling function—Binder cumulant as a function of ξ 2 / ξ 0 \xi_{2}/\xi_{0} , where ξ 2 \xi_{2} is the second moment correlation length and ξ 0 \xi_{0} its maximum value—remains identical to that of the equilibrium q = 3 , 4 q=3,4 Potts models. These findings indicate that non-reciprocal Potts models belong to the superuniversality class of their respective equilibrium counterparts.

The model:
The model is defined on a periodic square lattice
{\cal L}
with sites labeled by
{\bf i}\equiv(x,y)
with
x,y=1,2,\dots,L.
The neighbours of a site
{\bf i}
is denoted by

{\bf i}+{\bf e}_{k}
where the unit vector
{\bf e}_{k}\equiv(\cos(\frac{\pi}{2}k),\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}k)),
with

Each site
{\bf i}
is occupied by a spin
s_{\bf i}
which can be in one of
q

s_{\bf i}=1,2,\dots,q.
A spin
s_{\bf i}
interacts with its

that depends explicitly on both the spin-value

and the direction of the neighbour

(see Fig.

\{s_{\bf i}\}
is given by

E(\{s_{\bf i}\})=-\sum_{{\bf i}\in{\cal L}}\sum_{k=0}^{3}J^{k}_{s_{\bf i}}\left(2\delta_{s_{\bf i},s_{{\bf i}+{\bf e}_{k}}}-1\right).

(1)

Note that,
J_{s}^{k}>0
represents ferromagnetic interaction among spins, as energy of the system is lowered by making neighbouring spins parallel (same). Further, we specify the directional interaction as follows.

J_{s}^{k}=K(\delta_{s,k}+\delta_{s,k+1})+J(\delta_{s,k-1}+\delta_{s,k-2}).

(2)

For example, when
s_{\bf i}=1
the interaction occurs along the direction
{\bf e}_{0}
and
{\bf e}_{1}
with strength
K
(thick lines in Fig.
1
(a)) and the same occurs with spins in other two directions with strength
J
(broken lines in Fig.
1
(a)).

\displaystyle E_{x}(\{s_{\bf i}\})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{{\bf i}\in{\cal L}}J^{x}_{s_{\bf i},s_{{\bf i}+{\bf e}_{0}}}\left(2\delta_{s_{\bf i},s_{{\bf i}+{\bf e}_{0}}}-1\right)

(3)

\displaystyle E_{y}(\{s_{\bf i}\})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{{\bf i}\in{\cal L}}J^{y}_{s_{\bf i},s_{{\bf i}+{\bf e}_{1}}}\left(2\delta_{s_{\bf i},s_{{\bf i}+{\bf e}_{1}}}-1\right).

(4)

Note that the sum over the sites counts exactly one horizontal and one vertical bond with interaction strengths

J^{x}_{s,\tilde{s}}=J^{0}_{s}+J^{2}_{\tilde{s}};~J^{y}_{s,\tilde{s}}=J^{1}_{s}+J^{3}_{\tilde{s}}

(5)

respectively. The non-reciprocal nature of the interaction is clear from the fact that

First we use a dynamics that satisfy detailed balance, where a configuration
\{..,s_{\bf i},..\}
changes
\{..,\tilde{s}_{\bf i},..\}
with the Metropolis rate
r={\rm Min}(1,e^{-\Delta E})
where
\Delta E=E(\{..,\tilde{s}_{\bf i},..\})-E(\{..,s_{\bf i},..\})
.
This dynamics takes the system to equilibrium; the partition function at temperature
T
(
\beta=\frac{1}{k_{B}T}
,

is Boltzmann constant) is

Q(\beta)=\sum_{\{s_{\bf i}\}}\prod_{\bf i}\langle s_{\bf i}|M_{x}|s_{{\bf i}+{\bf e}_{0}}\rangle\prod_{\bf i}\langle s_{\bf i}|M_{y}|s_{{\bf i}+{\bf e}_{1}}\rangle.

(6)

Here
M_{x,y}
are
q\times q
matrix with elements,

\langle s|M_{x,y}|\tilde{s}\rangle=e^{\beta J^{x,y}_{s,\tilde{s}}(2\delta_{s,\tilde{s}}-1)}.

(7)

It turns out that matrices
M_{x}
and
M_{y}
do not commute, but they are isospectral; their eigenvalues are,

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=\dots=\lambda_{q-1}=2\sinh(\beta(J+K));

(8)

\displaystyle\lambda_{q}=q\cosh(\beta(J+K))-(q-2)\sinh(\beta(J+K)).

(9)

It is easy to check that both
M_{x}
and
M_{y}
are isospectral to another
q\times q

M

\langle s|M|\tilde{s}\rangle=e^{\beta\epsilon(2\delta_{s,\tilde{s}}-1)},
that appears in the partition function of the ordinary
q
-state Potts model on a two dimensional square lattice with
H=-\epsilon\sum_{\bf i}\sum_{k=0}^{3}(2\delta_{s_{\bf i},s_{{\bf i+e}_{k}}}-1).

Q_{\rm Potts}(\beta)=\sum_{\{s_{\bf i}\}}\prod_{\bf i}\langle s_{\bf i}|M|s_{{\bf i}+{\bf e}_{0}}\rangle\prod_{\bf i}\langle s_{\bf i}|M|s_{{\bf i}+{\bf e}_{1}}\rangle.

(10)

Thus, the partition function of the non-reciprocal

-state Potts model with equilibrium dynamics can be exactly mapped onto that of the conventional reciprocal Potts model when

\displaystyle\nu=\frac{2-\mu}{3-3\mu};~\beta=\frac{1}{12}(1+\mu);\gamma=\frac{7-4\mu+\mu^{2}}{6-6\mu}.

A few clarifications are in order. For
q=2
, we have assigned the spin configurations (a) and (b) in Fig.
1
to
s_{\bf i}=1,2
, respectively. Alternative configurations are equally valid. For instance, one could assign (a) and (c) to
s_{\bf i}=1,2

Garcés and Levis 2024

q=2
and
q=3

Additionally, interchanging
J
and
K

(J,K)
is a critical point, so is

. This symmetry is evident from Eq. (

In summary, we investigated the effects of non-reciprocal interactions in two-dimensional systems with discrete symmetry, exemplified by the
q
-state Potts model. In equilibrium, the model exhibits continuous phase transitions for
q=2,3,4
, breaking discrete symmetries
\mathbb{Z}_{2},\mathbb{Z}_{3},\mathbb{Z}_{4}
, respectively, while for
q>4
, the transitions become discontinuous. The universality classes of the continuous transitions are characterized by the critical exponents given in Eq. (
12
).

When non-reciprocal interactions are introduced, the
q=2
model (Ising system) undergoes continuous phase transitions belonging to the Ising universality class, irrespective of whether the dynamics satisfy detailed balance (equilibrium) or violate it (selfish dynamics). For
q=3
and
q=4
, the critical exponents in equilibrium align with the
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
universality classes, respectively. However, under selfish dynamics, the system exhibits non-equilibrium phase transitions with continuously varying critical exponents. Despite this variation, a superuniversality class emerges, characterized by invariant super-universal scaling functions along the critical line, even when critical exponents vary.

Overall, our findings reveal that in two dimensions, non-reciprocity does not alter the universality class of phase transitions breaking discrete symmetries. The scenario for systems with continuous symmetries, however, could be fundamentally different. In equilibrium, continuous symmetries cannot be broken to produce true long-range order, yet such systems exhibit exotic phases in two dimensions and exceptional phase transitions in the presence of non-reciprocal interactions
Fruchart et al. 2021
.

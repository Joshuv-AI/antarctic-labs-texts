Evolution Equations on Manifolds with Conical Singularities
Elmar Schrohe

Abstract
. This is an introduction to the analysis of nonlinear evolution equations on manifolds with conical singularities via maximal regularity techniques. We address the specific difficulties due to the singularities, in particular the choice of extensions of the conic Laplacian that guarantee the existence of a bounded H ∞ H_{\infty} -calculus. We introduce the relevant technical tools and survey, as main examples, applications to the porous medium equation, the fractional porous medium equation, the Yamabe flow, and the Cahn-Hilliard equation.

1.
Introduction

Understanding the interplay of analysis and geometry has always been a central issue in mathematics.
In the present article we consider nonlinear parabolic equations on manifolds that have conical singularities. On one hand we are interested in the existence of solutions in this context, on the other hand we would like to know how the geometry of the manifold near the singularities is reflected in the solutions.

Concerning the existence of solutions, we rely on maximal regularity techniques.
Maximal regularity has become an indispensable tool in the analysis of nonlinear parabolic problems. In connection with the Clément-Li theorem it guarantees the existence of short-time solutions to initial value problems of the form

(1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+A(u)u=F(t,u),\quad u(0)=u_{0},

under suitable conditions on the right hand side
F
and the initial value
u_{0}
, provided that
A(u(t))
is, for each
t
, a closed, densely defined operator in a Banach space
\mathbb{X}_{0}
with domain
\mathscr{D}(A(u(t)))=\mathbb{X}_{1}
, where
\mathbb{X}_{1}
is densely embedded in
\mathbb{X}_{0}
and
A(u_{0})
has maximal regularity, see Theorem
6
, below.

A first issue are the closed extensions of cone differential operators. For elliptic operators on (domains in)
\mathbb{R}^{n}
or on closed manifolds, the choice of the spaces
\mathbb{X}_{0}
and
\mathbb{X}_{1}
is clear in general, and maximal regularity has been established in many instances. On manifolds with conical singularities, the situation is more subtle. In general, the domain of the maximal extension differs from that of the minimal by a nontrivial finite-dimensional space.
We review the corresponding results and show, how this finite-dimensional space can be determined conveniently. In view of the applications we have in mind, our focus lies on the cone Laplacian.
Here we show, moreover, how extensions can be chosen in such a way that the Laplacian admits a bounded
H_{\infty}
-calculus.

We then consider examples, namely the porous medium equation (PME), the fractional PME, the Yamabe problem, and the Cahn-Hilliard equation.

The PME, also called nonlinear heat equation models – among other things – the flow of a gas in a porous medium, heat transfer or groundwater flow. It takes the form

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u-\Delta(u^{m})=F(t,u),\quad u(0)=u_{0},

where
m>0
,
F(t,u)
is a source term and
u_{0}
an initial value.
For
m=1
we recover the heat equation.
It is easily brought into the form (
1
) with
A(u)=-mu^{m-1}\Delta
. Maximal regularity for
A(u_{0})
can be derived from the bounded
H_{\infty}
-calculus of
c-\Delta
,
c>0
, via perturbation theory for
\mathcal{R}
-sectoriality.
We show how the existence of short time solutions can be inferred from the Clément-Li theorem. Moreover, we sketch the proof of the existence of global solutions. We follow here
[
35
]
and
[
36
]
.
We focus on strictly positive initial values
u_{0}
, as this leads to maximal regularity solutions. For non-negative
u_{0}
a concept of weak solutions has been suggested by Aronson and Peletier; it can also be applied in the conically degenerate situation.

We then address the question, how the shape of the tip is reflected in the solution. Already in the results mentioned above, the geometry comes in, as the possible choice of the domain is intimately related to the first non-zero eigenvalue of the Laplacian on the cross-section.
In order to see more of this phenomenon, we may consider a (strictly positive) initial value
u_{0}
of the form

u_{0}(x)=c_{0}+\mathcal{O}(x^{\infty}),

where
c_{0}
is a positive constant and
x
is the distance to the tip. We show that the only asymptotics that the solution
u
can develop are governed by the spectrum of the Laplacian on the cross-section of the cone.

In
[
39
,
40
]
Roidos and Shao studied the fractional PME, where
-\underline{\Delta}
is replaced by
(-\Delta)^{\sigma}
,
0<\sigma<1
, which requires new methods.
Moreover, Roidos
[
31
]
was able to find a solution to the Yamabe flow equation on conic manifolds using the techniques developed for the PME.

The Cahn-Hilliard equation is a phase-field or diffuse interface equation. It models the separation of phases in a binary mixture, for example a two-component alloy, but many other applications are encountered.
One finds the equation stated in various forms. We consider here the initial value problem

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+\Delta^{2}u+\Delta(u-u^{3})=0,\quad u(0)=u_{0}.

Here
u
denotes the concentration difference of the two phases. The sets where
u=\pm 1
correspond to areas of pure phases.

The Cahn-Hilliard equation is semi-linear and therefore of a simpler type than the PME. It might be solved by other methods, but maximal regularity techniques provide an elegant tool to derive the existence of short time solutions. We first focus on the results in
[
33
]
and
[
34
]
that establish the existence of short time solutions and show how they can be derived in a simpler way. The new feature here is the analysis of the bi-Laplacian.

Lopes and Roidos in
[
25
]
and
[
26
]
were able to prove the long time existence of solutions in dimensions 2 and 3. Moreover, they could define a semi-flow on the solutions of average zero with a global attractor.

The structure of this article is as follows. In Section 2, we will introduce the basics: manifolds with conical singularities, cone Sobolev spaces, and conically degenerate differential operators. The principal example is the Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Delta
with respect to a conically degenerate metric. Moreover, we will recall the two symbols associated to a cone differential operator. They define ellipticity and characterize the Fredholm property between Sobolev spaces, see also

Section 3 is devoted to the study of the possible closed extensions of a cone differential operator

associated with

-calculus for
\Delta

\widehat{A}

and
\widehat{A}
in Corollary 3.5. As an example we present the domains of the maximal closed extensions of

and

[
37

following the analysis in
[
45

.

In Section 4 we sketch the concepts of bounded

-calculus, bounded imaginary powers and

-sectoriality. Theorem

[

The porous medium equation (including subsections on the fractional PME and on the Yamabe problem) is treated in Section 5. Section 6 is devoted to the initial value problem for the Cahn-Hilliard equation.

2.
Conical Singularities and Cone Sobolev Spaces

2.1.
Manifolds with Conical Singularities

An
(n+1)
-dimensional manifold with conical singularities is a topological space
B
that is a smooth manifold of dimension
n+1
outside a finite subset
D\subseteq B
of isolated points.
Moreover, near every
d\in D
, the space
B
has the structure of a cone. More precisely, there exist an open neighborhood
U_{d}\subseteq B
of
d
and a homeomorphism
\phi_{d}:U_{d}\to({[0,1}[\times Y_{d})/(\{0\}\times Y_{d})
that restricts to a diffeomorphism
\phi_{d}:U_{d}\setminus\{d\}\to{]0,1[}\times Y_{d}
. The space
Y_{d}
, the cross-section of the cone, is a smooth manifold of dimension
n
.

This allows us to endow
B\setminus D
with a smooth structure. If
\phi_{d}^{\prime}:U_{d}\to([0,1[\times Y_{d})/(\{0\}\times Y_{d})
is another map with the above properties, we require that
\phi_{d}^{\prime}\phi_{d}^{-1}:{]0,1[}\times Y_{d}\to{]0,1[}\times Y_{d}
extends to a diffeomorphism
{]-1,1[}\times Y_{d}\to{]-1,1[}\times Y_{d}
.

Remark 1
.

One can also define manifolds with boundary and conical singularities. In this case,
B\setminus D
and the spaces
Y_{d}
are manifolds with boundary of dimensions
n+1
and
n
, respectively.

The above definition is not well suited for practical purposes. Instead, we blow up the singularities, obtaining an
(n+1)
-dimensional manifold
\mathbb{B}
with boundary
\partial\mathbb{B}=\bigsqcup_{d\in D}Y_{d}
. In the sequel, we will fix the notation for the dimension:

(2)

\displaystyle\dim\mathbb{B}=n+1.

In order to model the conical singularities, we endow
\mathbb{B}
with a degenerate Riemannian metric
g
. We assume
g
to be a smooth Riemannian metric away from the boundary. Near the boundary, we fix a collar neighborhood
{[0,1[}\times\partial\mathbb{B}
with coordinates
(x,y)
and suppose that
g
is of the form

(3)

\displaystyle g=dx^{2}+x^{2}h(x),

where
{[0,1[}\ni x\mapsto h(x)
is a smooth family of (non-degenerate) Riemannian metrics on
\partial\mathbb{B}
. We will say that
\mathbb{B}
has straight conical singularities, if
h
is independent of
x
.

2.2.
Cone Differential Operators

Definition 1
.

A differential operator
A
of order
\mu
on
\mathbb{B}
is called conically degenerate or a cone differential operator, if it is a differential operator of order
\mu
with smooth coefficients on the interior
\Int\mathbb{B}
of
\mathbb{B}
, and, near the boundary, can be written in the form

(4)

\displaystyle A=x^{-\mu}\;\sum_{j=0}^{\mu}a_{j}(x)(-x\partial_{x})^{j},

where
{[0,1[}\ni x\mapsto a_{j}(x)
is a smooth family of differential operators of order
\mu-j
on
\partial\mathbb{B}
,
j=0,\ldots,\mu
.

Example 1
.

The Laplace-Beltrami operator with respect to the metric
g
is

(5)

\displaystyle\Delta=x^{-2}\left((-x\partial_{x})^{2}-(n-1+H(x))(-x\partial_{x})+\Delta_{h(x)}\right)

and thus a cone differential operator of order
2
. Here,
\Delta_{h(x)}
is the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
\partial\mathbb{B}
with respect to the metric
h(x)
, and

(6)

\displaystyle H(x)=\frac{x}{2}\frac{\partial_{x}(\det h(x))}{\det h(x)}=\mathcal{O}(x).

Definition 2
.

To a cone differential operator
A
as in (
4
) we associate two symbols:

(i)

The (standard) principal pseudodifferential symbol
\sigma^{\mu}_{\psi}(A)
. Near
\partial\mathbb{B}
it is of the form

\sigma^{\mu}_{\psi}(A)(x,y,\xi,\eta)=\sum_{j=0}^{\mu}\sigma_{\psi}^{\mu-j}(a_{j}(x))(y,\eta)(-ix\xi)^{j}.

with the principal symbol
\sigma_{\psi}^{\mu-j}(a_{j}(x))
of
a_{j}(x)
.
Close to
\partial\mathbb{B}
we also define the rescaled principal symbol:

x^{\mu}\sigma_{\psi}^{\mu}(A)(x,y,\xi/x,\eta)=\sum_{j=0}^{\mu}\sigma_{\psi}^{\mu-j}(a_{j}(x))(y,\eta)(-i\xi)^{j}.

(ii)

The conormal symbol or principal Mellin symbol of
A
is the operator-valued polynomial
\sigma_{M}(A):\mathbb{C}\to\mathscr{L}(H^{\mu}(\partial\mathbb{B}),L^{2}(\partial\mathbb{B}))
defined by

\sigma_{M}(A)(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{\mu}a_{j}(0)z^{j}.

Example 2
.

For the Laplace-Beltrami operator with respect to the metric
g
in (
3
) we obtain near
\partial\mathbb{B}
:

(7)

\displaystyle\sigma_{\psi}^{2}(\Delta)(x,y,\xi,\eta)=x^{-2}\left((-ix\xi)^{2}+\sigma_{\psi}^{2}(\Delta_{h(x))}\right);

the rescaled version is

(8)

\displaystyle x^{2}\sigma_{\psi}^{2}(\Delta)(x,y,\xi/x,\eta)=-\xi^{2}-\|\eta\|^{2}_{h(x)},

where
\|\eta\|_{h(x)}
is the norm of
\eta\in T^{*}_{y}\partial\mathbb{B}
induced by the metric
h(x)
.

The principal Mellin symbol is

(9)

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}_{M}(\Delta)(z)=z^{2}-(n-1)z+\Delta_{h(0)}.

Note that
H
does not appear, since
H(0)=0
.

2.3.
Cone Sobolev Spaces

The definition of cone differential operators suggests that the right choice of Sobolev spaces on manifolds with conical singularities should take into account the fact that derivatives with respect to
x
always appear in the form
x\partial_{x}
and that one has powers of
x
as weight factors. For
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
we therefore define the space
\mathcal{H}^{k,\gamma}_{p}(\mathbb{B})
by

(10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{k,\gamma}_{p}(\mathbb{B})=\Big\{u\in H^{k}_{p,loc}(\Int\mathbb{B}):

\displaystyle x^{\frac{n+1}{2}-\gamma}(x\partial_{x})^{j}D_{y}^{\alpha}(\omega u)\in L^{p}(\mathbb{B},\frac{dxdy}{x})\;\text{\rm for }j+|\alpha|\leq k\Big\}.

Here
\omega
is a
cut-off function near

x=0
, i.e.
0\leq\omega\in C^{\infty}_{c}([0,1[)
,
\omega(x)=1
for small
x\geq 0
.
This yields a norm on
\mathcal{H}^{k,\gamma}_{p}(\mathbb{B})
. Different choices for
\omega
yield equivalent norms.

For
p=2
,
\mathcal{H}^{0,0}_{2}(\mathbb{B})=L^{2}(\mathbb{B},\mu_{g})
is the
L^{2}
space for
\mathbb{B}
with the measure
\mu_{g}
induced by the metric
g
, up to equivalence of norms (we will obtain the norm in
L^{2}(\mathbb{B},\mu_{g})
if we choose the measure
\sqrt{\det h(x)}\frac{dxdy}{x}
in (
10
)). In general, up to equivalent norms,

L^{p}(\mathbb{B},\mu_{g})=\mathcal{H}_{p}^{0,\gamma_{p}}(\mathbb{B}),\quad\text{with }\gamma_{p}=(n+1)\left(1/2-1/p\right),

by
[
43
, Remark 2.12(c)]
.
The definition extends to
s\geq 0
by interpolation and then to
s\in\mathbb{R}
by duality. There also is a direct way to obtain these spaces.
Using the product structure of
\mathbb{R}\times\partial\mathbb{B}
it is straightforward to define the
Sobolev spaces
H^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}\times\partial\mathbb{B})
.

Definition 3
.

For
s,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
and
1\leq p<\infty
,
\mathcal{H}^{s,\gamma}_{p}(\mathbb{B})
is the set of all
u\in H^{s}_{p,loc}(\Int\mathbb{B})
such that, for every cut-off function
\omega
as defined after (
10
),

S_{\gamma}(\omega u)\in H^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}\times\partial\mathbb{B}).

Here
S_{\gamma}
is (the extension to distributions of) the map

\displaystyle S_{\gamma}:C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\partial\mathbb{B})\to C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times\partial\mathbb{B}),\quad(S_{\gamma}\varphi)(r,y)=e^{(\frac{n+1}{2}-\gamma)r}\varphi(e^{-r},y).

This makes
\mathcal{H}^{s,\gamma}_{p}(\mathbb{B})
a Banach space.

That this definition actually furnishes the same space is easily checked by first considering the case
s=0
and then observing how derivatives transform under
S_{\gamma}
.

Corollary 1
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
s>(n+1)/p
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
. Then a function
u\in\mathcal{H}^{s,\gamma}_{p}(\mathbb{B})
is continuous on
\Int\mathbb{B}
. Near
\partial\mathbb{B}
it satisfies the estimate

\displaystyle|u(x,y)|\leq cx^{\gamma-\frac{n+1}{2}}\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,\gamma}_{p}(\mathbb{B})}

for some constant
c\geq 0
.
In particular,
u(x,y)\to 0
as
x\to 0
whenever
\gamma>(n+1)/2
.

Proof.

Since
u\in H^{s}_{p,loc}(\Int\mathbb{B})
for
s>(n+1)/p
, it is continuous on the interior of
\mathbb{B}
.
The fact that
S_{\gamma}(\omega u)\in H^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}\times\partial\mathbb{B})
implies that

\displaystyle|e^{(\gamma-\frac{n+1}{2})r}(\omega u)(e^{-r},y)|\leq c_{1}\|S_{\gamma}(\omega u)\|_{H^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}\times\partial\mathbb{B})}\leq c_{2}\|\omega u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,\gamma}_{p}(\mathbb{B})}

for suitable constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
.
For
x=e^{-r}
we obtain the assertion.
∎

Lemma 1
.

In the situation of Corollary
1
, we have the weaker estimate

u(x,y)\to 0
as
x\to 0
when
\gamma=(n+1)/2
.

Proof.

We only need to consider
\omega u
for a cut-off function
\omega
. Since
S_{(n+1)/2}(\omega u)\in H^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}\times\partial\mathbb{B})
,
(\omega u)(e^{-r},y)\to 0
as
r\to\infty
.
We then obtain the assertion for
x=e^{-r}
.
∎

The following lemma is an immediate consequence of (
10
).

Lemma 2
.

Let
\omega
be a cut-off function near

and

For

,

, let

Remark 2
.

Lemma

Lemma 3
.

Let

,
\sigma>0
,

Definition 5

A cone differential operator

Let

4.
Maximal Regularity, the Clément-Li Theorem and Applications

Let
\mathbb{X}_{0},\mathbb{X}_{1}
be Banach spaces,
\mathbb{X}_{1}
densely embedded in
\mathbb{X}_{0}
. We assume them to be UMD spaces, see
[
22
]
for a definition. All the spaces
\mathcal{H}^{s,\gamma}_{p}(\mathbb{B})
and
\mathcal{K}^{s,\gamma}_{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\partial\mathbb{B})
, for
s,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
,
1<p<\infty
, and their subspaces are UMD spaces.

Definition 6
.

Let
1<q<\infty
,
T>0
, and let
-B\in\mathscr{L}(\mathbb{X}_{1},\mathbb{X}_{0})
be the infinitesimal generator of an analytic semigroup in
\mathbb{X}_{0}
with domain
\mathscr{D}(B)=\mathbb{X}_{1}
. Then
B
is said to have maximal
L^{q}
regularity, provided that the initial value problem in
L^{q}(0,T;\mathbb{X}_{0})

(26)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+Bu=f,\quad u(0)=u_{0},

with data
f\in L^{p}(0,T;\mathbb{X}_{0})
and
u_{0}
in the real interpolation space
(\mathbb{X}_{0},\mathbb{X}_{1})_{1-1/q,q}

has a unique solution

u\in W^{1}_{q}(0,T;\mathbb{X}_{0})\cap L^{q}(0,T;\mathbb{X}_{1})

depending continuously on the data.

The importance of maximal regularity is illustrated by the following theorem, due to Clément and Li,

[
10
, Theorem 2.1]
; see also
[
29
]
for a slightly more general version:

Theorem 6
.

In
L^{q}(0,T_{0};\mathbb{X}_{0})
,
1<q<\infty
,
T_{0}>0
, consider the quasilinear problem

(27)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u(t)+A(u(t))u(t)=f(t,u(t))+g(t),\;\;t\in(0,T_{0}),\quad u(0)=u_{0},

for
u_{0}
in the real interpolation space
(\mathbb{X}_{0},\mathbb{X}_{1})_{1-1/q,q}
.

Assume that there exists an open neighborhood
U
of
u_{0}
in
(\mathbb{X}_{0},\mathbb{X}_{1})_{1-1/q,q}
such that
A(u_{0}):\mathbb{X}_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{X}_{0}
has maximal
L^{q}
-regularity and

(H1)

A\in C^{1-}(U,\mathscr{L}(\mathbb{X}_{1},\mathbb{X}_{0}))
,

(H2)

f\in C^{1-,1-}([0,T_{0}]\times U,\mathbb{X}_{0})
,

(H3)

g\in L^{q}(0,T_{0};\mathbb{X}_{0})
,

where ‘
C^{1-}
’ denotes Lipschitz continuous functions. Then there exist a
T>0
and a unique solution
u\in W^{1}_{q}(0,T;\mathbb{X}_{0})\cap L^{q}(0,T;\mathbb{X}_{1})
to (
27
) on
(0,T)
.

The solution
u
then belongs to
C([0,T];(\mathbb{X}_{0},\mathbb{X}_{1})_{1-1/q,q}
by
[
1
, Theorem III.4.10.2]
.

Instead of verifying the conditions in Definition
6
it is often easier to establish stronger properties.
We briefly recall the concepts of bounded
H_{\infty}
-calculus, bounded imaginary powers,
\mathcal{R}
-sectoriality and their relation to maximal regularity. For many more details see
[
14
]
or
[
22
]
.

Let
A:\mathscr{D}(A)=\mathbb{X}_{1}\to\mathbb{X}_{0}
be a closed unbounded linear operator. The sector

(28)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\theta}=\{z=re^{i\varphi}\in\mathbb{C}:r>0,|\varphi|\geq\theta\}

is said to be a sector of minimal growth for the resolvent of
A
, if
\lambda-A
is invertible for
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\theta}
and there exists a
C\geq 0
such that

(29)

\displaystyle\|\lambda(\lambda-A)^{-1}\|_{\mathscr{L}(\mathbb{X}_{0})}\leq C,\quad\lambda\in\Lambda_{\theta}.

One can then define the operator
f(A)
for any bounded holomorphic function, for short
H_{\infty}
- function,
f:\mathbb{C}\setminus\Lambda_{\theta}\to\mathbb{C}
by

\displaystyle f(A)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\Lambda_{\theta}}f(\lambda)(\lambda-A)^{-1}\,d\lambda.

The operator
A
is said to admit a bounded
H_{\infty}
-calculus, if there exists a constant
C
, independent of
f
such that

\displaystyle\|f(A)\|_{\mathscr{L}(\mathbb{X}_{0})}\leq C\|f\|_{\infty}.

The notion of bounded
H_{\infty}
-calculus goes back to Alan McIntosh’s seminal paper
[
28
]
.
A slightly weaker concept is that of bounded imaginary powers.

Let
A:\mathscr{D}(A)=\mathbb{X}_{1}\to\mathbb{X}_{0}
be a closed unbounded linear operator satisfying (

).

Definition 7
.

A
is said to have bounded imaginary powers of angle
\phi
, if the powers
A^{is}
,

, are defined and

\|A^{is}\|_{\mathscr{L}(\mathbb{X}_{0})}\leq e^{\phi|s|},\quad s\in\mathbb{R}.

A bounded
H_{\infty}
-calculus for
A

For

For any

and

,

The Yamabe flow on conic manifolds.

The problem here is to find a family of metrics

\displaystyle\partial_{t}g(t)+R_{g(t)}g(t)=0,\quad g(0)=g_{0},

There is a subtlety in the proof: The Clément-Li theorem requires the Lipschitz continuity of the function

\displaystyle=

Lecture Notes on Verifying Graph Neural Networks
François Schwarzentruber

Abstract




Preface

In these lecture notes, we first recall the connection between graph neural networks, Weisfeiler-Lehman tests and logics such as first-order logic and graded modal logic. We then present a modal logic in which counting modalities appear in linear inequalities in order to solve verification tasks on graph neural networks. We describe an algorithm for the satisfiability problem of that logic. It is inspired from the tableau method of vanilla modal logic, extended with reasoning in quantifier-free fragment Boolean algebra with Presburger arithmetic.

1.5.1

•

•

Repeat until the colouring stabilizes.

Lemma 54

[
Eisenbrand and Shmonin, 2006
]

Let

Proof.

Suppose that

\displaystyle d\log(2|X|M+1)

\displaystyle\leq d\log\left(2^{|X|/(2d)}\left(\frac{|X|}{2d}+1\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{|X|}{2}+d\log\left(\frac{|X|}{2d}+1\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|X|}{2}+d\cdot\frac{|X|}{2d}\text{ by concavity of $\log$}

\displaystyle=|X|.

Theorem 64
.

Verification of GNNs with global readout and ReLU as an activation function on directed graphs is undecidable.

Proof.

Reduction from
Hilbert’s tenth problem
. We still only the fact truncReLU can be similated by ReLU: this enables to express logical properties. But we also need ReLU for storing arbitrary long integers throw the computation.

The idea is to tag the vertices with a feature
count_{x}
. When
count_{x}=0
the vertex does not count and
count_{x}=1
it means that the vertex counts for 1 in the value of variable
x
. We interpret the value of
x
by
\#^{g}count_{x}
. More precisely:

\#^{g}count_{x}:=agg^{g}(ReLU(count_{x})).

•

e=\hbox{\pagecolor{blue!5!white}$x=1$}
\varphi_{e}:=\#^{g}count_{x}=1
. We can write equalities by inequalities and write inequalities as for the translation from
\text{K}^{\#}
into GNN with truncReLU (and we simulate truncReLU by ReLU).

•

e=\hbox{\pagecolor{blue!5!white}$x=x^{\prime}+x^{\prime\prime}$}
\varphi_{e}:=\#^{g}count_{x}=\#^{g}count_{x^{\prime}}+\#^{g}count_{x^{\prime\prime}}
.

•

e=\hbox{\pagecolor{blue!5!white}$x=x^{\prime}\times x^{\prime\prime}$}
We cannot write multiplication constraint directly. So introduce extra features
y_{e}
and
y^{\prime\prime}_{e}
.

\displaystyle\varphi_{e}:=

\displaystyle((y_{e}=0)\land(z_{e}=0))\lor((y_{e}=\#^{g}count_{x^{\prime}})\land(z_{e}=1))

(7.1)

\displaystyle\land agg^{g}(y_{e})=\#^{g}count_{x}

(7.2)

\displaystyle\land agg^{g}(z_{e})=\#^{g}count_{x^{\prime\prime}}

(7.3)

The objective of (7.4) is to replace each 1-value of
z_{e}
by
\#^{g}count_{x^{\prime}}
so that
y_{e}=\#^{g}count_{x^{\prime}}\times z_{e}
at each vertex. By taking the global aggregation over all vertices, we get:

\#^{g}count_{x}=\#^{g}count_{x^{\prime}}\times\#^{g}count_{x^{\prime\prime}}.

Finally the GNN is:
\bigwedge_{e\in\mathcal{E}}\varphi_{e}
. We left the equivalence to the reader.
∎
